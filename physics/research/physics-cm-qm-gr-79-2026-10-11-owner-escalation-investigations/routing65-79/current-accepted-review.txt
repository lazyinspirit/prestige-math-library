## Definition

This item fixes the unit and coordinate readings of the explicitly defined
Lorentzian objects, smooth-curve proper time and four-velocity of
[[def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects]], the tensor fields of
[[def-gr-smooth-covariant-two-tensor-field]], and the constructed connection,
curvature, Ricci and scalar fields of [[def-gr-metric-selected-connection]].
It states conventions, not a physical law or a measurement.

**(a) Constants and tensor slot.** Adopt $c>0$ with SI dimension
$\mathrm m\,\mathrm s^{-1}$, $G_N>0$ with dimension
$\mathrm m^3\,\mathrm{kg}^{-1}\,\mathrm s^{-2}$, and a signed parameter
$\Lambda$ with dimension $\mathrm m^{-2}$. No numerical value is asserted.
Define $\kappa:=8\pi G_N/c^4$, of dimension
$\mathrm m^{-1}\,\mathrm{kg}^{-1}\,\mathrm s^2$.
For a smooth symmetric covariant two-tensor $T$ adopt the convention that its
components $T_{AB}=T(e_A,e_B)$ in an ordered $g$-orthonormal basis are energy
per volume, $\mathrm J\,\mathrm m^{-3}=\mathrm{kg}\,\mathrm m^{-1}\mathrm s^{-2}$.
This is a tensor slot and unit convention; interpreting a particular $T$ as
matter is a subsequent definition. Therefore $\kappa T_{AB}$ has dimension
$\mathrm m^{-2}$. No positivity, conservation or matter realization is assumed.

**(b) Coordinates and dimensions.** In a length-coordinate chart,
$x^0=ct$ and all four coordinates have units $\mathrm m$; $g_{ab}$ are
dimensionless and the quadratic line element $ds^2=g_{ab}dx^adx^b$ has units
$\mathrm m^2$. The Christoffel symbols defined by
$\nabla^g_{\partial_b}\partial_c=\Gamma^a{}_{bc}\partial_a$
([[def-christoffel-symbols-of-an-affine-connection]]) have units
$\mathrm m^{-1}$: the constructed formula differentiates dimensionless
$g_{ab}$ once with respect to a length coordinate. Curvature components have
units $\mathrm m^{-2}$ since the curvature operator differentiates the
connection once more and includes products of two connection coefficients;
so do $\operatorname{Ric}_{ab}$ and scalar curvature $S$ in these coordinates.
In a time-coordinate chart $x^0=t$ has units $\mathrm s$, and $g_{00}$ has
units $\mathrm m^2\,\mathrm s^{-2}$; mixed, spatial, angular or other chart
components have the dimensions obtained by the coordinate Jacobians. A single
component has no universal dimension in a general chart. Orthonormal-frame
readings use the normalized geometric basis, with its length normalization.

**(c) Curves and invariant readings.** For the smooth future timelike curve
and positive scaling parameter of [[def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects]] (e),
read $c$ as in (a) and
$\tau(s)=\tau_0+c^{-1}\int_{s_0}^{s}\sqrt{-g(\gamma',\gamma')}\,dr$
as a proper-time parameter in seconds. Its four-velocity $U=d\gamma/d\tau$
has length-coordinate and normalized orthonormal components in
$\mathrm m\,\mathrm s^{-1}$; in a time-coordinate chart $U^0=dt/d\tau$
is dimensionless and other component dimensions follow the coordinates.
Lorentzian length $c\,\Delta\tau$ is in metres. The tensors $R^g$,
$\operatorname{Ric}$ and the scalar $S$ have the orthonormal readings
$\mathrm m^{-2}$, whereas the lowered curvature components in a mixed chart
have the units required by the metric and coordinate slots.

**(d) Future observer readings and SI restoration.** Let $n$ be a future
unit timelike vector, $g(n,n)=-1$ and $g(n,N)<0$. Define
$\rho_n:=T(n,n)$, $j_n:=-\sharp(T(n,\cdot))$ and its spatial part
$\phi_n:=j_n-\rho_n n$; indeed $g(j_n,n)=-\rho_n$, so
$g(\phi_n,n)=0$. In a $g$-orthonormal basis with $e_0=n$, set
$\phi^i=-T(e_0,e_i)=T^{0i}$ for $i=1,2,3$. The **SI energy flux** vector
and **SI momentum density** covector are, respectively,

$$q_n:=c\phi_n,\qquad p_n:=\phi_n^{\flat}/c,\qquad q_n^i=-cT_{0i},\qquad (p_n)_i=-T_{0i}/c.$$

Thus $\rho_n$ and each $\phi^i$ have units $\mathrm J\,\mathrm m^{-3}$;
$q_n^i$ has units $\mathrm W\,\mathrm m^{-2}$ and $(p_n)_i$ has units
$\mathrm{kg}\,\mathrm m^{-2}\mathrm s^{-1}$. Stress components $T_{ij}$ have
units $\mathrm J\,\mathrm m^{-3}=\mathrm{Pa}$. Tensor components retain the
common energy-density units; the factors of $c$ convert their mixed readings
to different SI quantities. Future-directedness and the signs here are part
of the convention, not positivity assumptions on $T$.

**(e) Natural units.** Setting $c=1$, or additionally $G_N=1$, abbreviates
these dimensionful conventions. Restore the factors displayed above before
comparison with SI quantities; in particular the same spatial tensor component
corresponds to energy flux multiplied by $c$ and momentum density divided by
$c$. In a chart whose coordinates $x^0,x^1,x^2,x^3$ are all lengths and whose
metric components are $\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$, a nonzero null tangent
vector $v=(v^0,\mathbf v)$, null as in clause (b) of
[[def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects]], satisfies
$\|\mathbf v\|_2:=\sqrt{(v^1)^2+(v^2)^2+(v^3)^2}=|v^0|$. Since $v\ne0$, this
implies $v^0\ne0$, so $\|\mathbf v\|_2/|v^0|=1$. This is a coordinate identity
for the null condition, not a postulate about light propagation, and it does
not change the physical constant $c$.

## Postulate

Adopt as physical assumptions of the classical gravitational framework, in
addition to [[post-gr-spacetime-and-classical-domain]], the following statements
about the matter content of a model.

**(i) Matter content is model input.** A model of the framework specifies its
nongravitational fields or matter and their explicit equations of motion and
couplings. The geometric data $(M,g,N)$ of
[[def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects]] and covariance of the model's equations
under relabelling of events do not select those fields, equations or couplings.
Here covariance means that, on overlapping smooth charts, the component
transformations specified for the model's fields carry solutions of the
equations in one chart to solutions of the same equations in the other.
Different matter specifications over the same geometry are different models of
the framework, and no dynamical selection principle is asserted.

**(ii) Smooth matter and its stress-energy tensor.** The matter of a model on
its matter domain is represented by smooth fields on an open domain
$U\subseteq M$, and the model's stress-energy tensor is the smooth symmetric
covariant $2$-tensor field $T$ on $U$ of
[[def-gr-stress-energy-tensor-field]], with the unit reading recorded there.
The relation of $T$ to the matter fields is part of the model: a model derived
from an action defines $T$ as the Hilbert stress-energy tensor of that action,
while a model that prescribes $T$ directly is a separate source model.

**(iii) Consistency with the field law.** Where a model prescribes $T$
independently of matter fields, the prescription is admissible only if it meets
the consistency conditions imposed by the gravitational field law adopted for
the model, stated where that law is stated. If a model specifies a real-valued
action functional on its admissible metric and matter configurations, the
action, its dependence on those fields, its stated symmetries, and the matter
equations obtained from it are part of the model. Any conservation identity
used for $T$ when those equations hold must be established from that model's
action, symmetries, equations, and regularity hypotheses; it is not assumed
for an arbitrary action or arbitrary smooth symmetric $T$.

**(iv) Energy conditions are optional additional assumptions.** A model may
adopt any of the pointwise inequalities of [[def-gr-energy-conditions]] as
further assumptions on its stress-energy tensor. The general framework
postulates impose none automatically. A particular matter model may imply one
or more of them; any such implication must be established from that model's
equations and hypotheses. Each model records which conditions, if any, it
adopts, and a model that adopts none is not thereby excluded.

**(v) Scope and exclusions.** Idealizations such as unmodeled self-force,
finite-size and tidal corrections, dispersion, dissipation, and quantum effects
are outside the framework unless the model's explicit equations include them;
matter concentrated in a distributional or lower-dimensional source, and
matter configurations whose fields fail to be smooth on the model domain, are
outside the stated smooth branch and require additional mathematics. The
adoption of a matter model is not a claim that it describes nature.

## Status, scope and limitations

- **Adopted, not deduced.** Clauses (i)-(v) are model assumptions. They
  constrain what counts as a model of the framework; they imply no theorem
  about matter and no measured property of any substance. Clause (iii)
  leaves any conservation equation to the item stating the field law; that
  item must define the derivative used and state the matter equations and
  regularity hypotheses under which the equation is asserted.

- **Line of responsibility.** The geometric objects, the inverse metric, the
  causal classification and the metric-selected connection class are supplied
  by the mathematical items of this page; the framework postulates adopt them
  physically. Empirical comparisons involving matter require a fully specified
  model and reported observations with apparatus, calibration, sampling and
  uncertainty qualifications, and would test the combined model rather than
  these assumptions individually.

## Statement

Let $(M,g,N)$ be a time-oriented smooth Lorentzian four-manifold with
metric-selected connection $\nabla$
([[def-gr-metric-selected-connection]],
[[thm-gr-nondegenerate-levi-civita-and-tensor-derivative]]), let $a<b$, and
let $\gamma:[a,b]\to M$ be a $C^2$ future timelike curve whose image is
contained in the domain $U$ of one smooth chart $(U,x)$
([[def-gr-timelike-worldline-and-proper-time]]). Suppose $\gamma$ is
parametrized so that

$$g_{\gamma(t)}\bigl(\gamma'(t),\gamma'(t)\bigr)=-c^2 \qquad (t\in[a,b]),$$

that is, by proper time up to a choice of origin
([[lem-gr-proper-time-reparametrization-and-four-velocity]]). Let
$H:(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times[a,b]\to U$ be a map that is $C^2$ in both
variables, with $H(0,t)=\gamma(t)$ for all $t$, with
$H(s,a)=\gamma(a)$ and $H(s,b)=\gamma(b)$ for all $s$, and such that each
curve $t\mapsto H(s,t)$ is future timelike; write
$T(s,t):=\partial_tH(s,t)$, $J(t):=\partial_sH(s,t)|_{s=0}$, so that
$J(a)=J(b)=0$, and

$$L(s):=\int_a^b\sqrt{-g_{H(s,t)}\bigl(T(s,t),T(s,t)\bigr)}\,dt .$$

Then:

1. $L$ is differentiable at $0$ and
   $$L'(0)=-\int_a^b\frac{g_{\gamma(t)}\bigl(D_tJ(t),\gamma'(t)\bigr)}{\sqrt{-g_{\gamma(t)}(\gamma'(t),\gamma'(t))}}\,dt =-\frac1c\int_a^bg_{\gamma(t)}\bigl(D_tJ(t),\gamma'(t)\bigr)\,dt .$$
2. Equivalently,
   $L'(0)=\frac1c\int_a^bg_{\gamma(t)}\bigl(J(t),D_t\gamma'(t)\bigr)\,dt$.
3. $L'(0)=0$ for **every** such variation $H$ if and only if
   $D_t\gamma'=0$ on $[a,b]$. Consequently, within this class, stationarity
   of the length functional is exactly the geodesic equation in the given
   proper-time parameter.

The chart hypothesis is used to realize variations of prescribed compactly
supported variation fields by coordinate displacement; the formula of claim 1
and the implication "geodesic $\Rightarrow$ stationary" are local and do not
require it beyond the smooth parametrization.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A time-oriented smooth Lorentzian four-manifold $(M,g,N)$ with metric-selected connection $\nabla$, an interval $[a,b]$ with $a<b$, a $C^2$ future timelike curve $\gamma$ in a chart domain $U$ with $g(\gamma',\gamma')=-c^2$, and a $C^2$ variation $H$ as in the statement.

[L1] The metric pairing identity along a curve holds: $\frac{d}{dt}g(V,W)=g(D_tV,W)+g(V,D_tW)$ for smooth sections along a smooth curve ([[lem-gr-parallel-transport-and-geodesic-speed]], clause 1).

[L2] In a chart, $D_tV^a=\partial_tV^a+\sum_{b,c}\Gamma^a{}_{bc}\dot\gamma^bV^c$ for a field $V$ along $\gamma$ with tangent $\dot\gamma$, and $\Gamma^a{}_{bc}=\Gamma^a{}_{cb}$ ([[def-covariant-derivative-along-a-curve]], [[def-christoffel-symbols-of-an-affine-connection]], [[def-gr-metric-selected-connection]], [[lem-gr-connection-conditions-in-chart-components]]).

[L3] For a $C^2$ two-parameter family the mixed derivatives commute as ordinary partial derivatives in a chart, so $D_sT=D_tJ$ ([[thm-clairaut-schwarz-mixed-partials]], [[def-covariant-derivative-along-a-curve]]).

[L4] If $F$ and $H$ are continuous on $[-\varepsilon_0,\varepsilon_0]\times[a,b]$ and $\partial_sF(s,t)=H(s,t)$ for $|s|<\varepsilon_0$, then the compact-rectangle differentiation theorem applies to the Darboux/Riemann integrals of the continuous slices and gives that $s\mapsto\int_a^bF(s,t)\,dt$ is differentiable at $0$ with derivative $\int_a^bH(0,t)\,dt$ ([[thm-differentiation-under-the-integral-sign-on-a-compact-rectangle]], [[def-darboux-integral]]).

[L5] If $G$ is $C^1$ on $[a,b]$, then $\int_a^bG'=G(b)-G(a)$ ([[thm-ftc-second-part]]). If a continuous function is strictly positive at a point of an open interval, then its integral over a sufficiently small closed subinterval is strictly positive ([[thm-monotonicity-of-the-integral]], [[def-continuity-real]], [[def-darboux-integral]]).

[L6] For $0<r<R$ in $\mathbb R$ there is a smooth $\rho:\mathbb R\to[0,1]$ with $\rho=1$ on $[t_0-r,t_0+r]$ and support in $(t_0-R,t_0+R)$ ([[lem-smooth-bump-between-concentric-euclidean-balls]]).

[L7] In a chart, a nondegenerate metric identifies every nonzero tangent vector $A$ with a nonzero covector $g(A,\cdot)$ ([[def-gr-inverse-metric-and-musical-isomorphisms]]). Since the chart coordinate vectors form a basis, at least one coordinate vector $\partial_j$ has $g(\partial_j,A)\neq0$; choosing its sign makes this pairing positive. The coordinate vector field $\partial_j$ has constant components in that chart and hence gives a smooth section along $\gamma$ ([[def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 (Differentiability of $L$.) The integrand $\rho(s,t):=\sqrt{-g_{H(s,t)}(T(s,t),T(s,t))}$ is strictly positive and continuous on $(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times[a,b]$ (indeed $C^1$): $H$ and $T=\partial_tH$ are $C^1$ in both variables, the metric components are smooth, and the square root is smooth on $(0,\infty)$. Hence [L4] applies and $L'(0)=\int_a^b\partial_s\rho(0,t)\,dt$. [L4, algebra]

2.1 (Derivative of the integrand.) By [L1] applied to $V=W=T$ with the derivative in the $s$-direction (the pairing identity holds for the two-parameter family by the same component computation as for one parameter), $\partial_s\rho(0,t)=-\frac{g(D_sT,T)}{\sqrt{-g(T,T)}}\bigl|_{s=0}$. [step 1.1, L1, algebra]

2.2 (Commutator.) On the chart $U$, writing $H^a=H^a(s,t)$ and using [L2], $D_sT^a-D_tJ^a=\sum_{b,c}\Gamma^a{}_{bc}(J^bT^c-T^bJ^c)=0$ since $\partial_s\partial_tH^a=\partial_t\partial_sH^a$ by [L3] and $\Gamma$ is symmetric in its lower indices. Hence $D_sT=D_tJ$ at $s=0$, where $J=\partial_sH$, and $T=\gamma'$. [step 1.1, L2, L3, algebra]

3.1 (First variation formula.) Substituting steps 2.1 and 2.2 into step 1.1, and using $g(\gamma',\gamma')=-c^2$, gives the formula of claim 1. [step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 (Integration by parts.) By [L1], $\frac{d}{dt}g(J,\gamma')=g(D_tJ,\gamma')+g(J,D_t\gamma')$ and both terms are continuous, so the derivative of the $C^1$ function $t\mapsto g(J,\gamma')$ is integrable; [L5] gives $\int_a^b\bigl(g(D_tJ,\gamma')+g(J,D_t\gamma')\bigr)dt=g(J,\gamma')|_a^b=0$ because $J(a)=J(b)=0$. Multiplying the first variation formula by $c$ and using this identity gives claim 2, $L'(0)=\frac1c\int_a^bg(J,D_t\gamma')dt$. [step 3.1, L1, L5, algebra]

5.1 ($\Rightarrow$ direction of claim 3.) If $D_t\gamma'=0$ on $[a,b]$, the integrand of claim 2 vanishes identically for the variation at hand, so $L'(0)=0$; since $H$ was arbitrary, every such variation is stationary. [step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 (Converse.) If $D_t\gamma'$ is nonzero at an endpoint, continuity makes it nonzero at an interior point as well; hence, if $D_t\gamma'$ is not identically zero on $[a,b]$, choose $t_0\in(a,b)$ with $A(t_0):=D_t\gamma'(t_0)\neq0$. The field $A$ is continuous because $\gamma$ is $C^2$ and the Christoffel symbols are smooth. By [L7], choose a chart coordinate vector field with sign $Z=\pm\partial_j$ along $\gamma$ so $g(Z(t_0),A(t_0))>0$; by continuity, this pairing is positive on a neighborhood of $t_0$. Choose a closed interval $[u,v]\subseteq(a,b)$ with $t_0$ in its interior on which the pairing stays positive. Then choose $0<r<R$ with $[t_0-r,t_0+r]\subseteq[u,v]$ and $(t_0-R,t_0+R)\subseteq(u,v)$, and let $\varphi\ge0$ be the bump of [L6] equal to $1$ on $[t_0-r,t_0+r]$ with support in $(t_0-R,t_0+R)$. Put $J(t):=\varphi(t)Z(t)$; its chart components are smooth and it vanishes at $a,b$. Define $H(s,t):=x^{-1}\bigl(x(\gamma(t))+s\,J^{\mathrm{ch}}(t)\bigr)$ on a sufficiently small $|s|$, where $J^{\mathrm{ch}}$ denotes the chart components of $J$. This is a $C^2$ variation, and [L7] ensures $g(J,D_t\gamma')>0$ wherever $\varphi>0$. [step 5.1, L6, L7, construct]

7.1 (Admissibility of the constructed variation.) $H$ fixes the endpoints because $J(a)=J(b)=0$; it is $C^2$; and for all sufficiently small $|s|$ its fibers are future timelike and lie in $U$: the perturbation $sJ^{\mathrm{ch}}$ vanishes outside $[u,v]$, so on the compact set $[u,v]$ the image stays in a fixed compact subset of the chart domain for small $|s|$, and the family of velocities $T(s,t)$ depends continuously on $(s,t)$ with $T(0,t)=\gamma'(t)$ future timelike, so by compactness of $[a,b]$ the timelike condition persists for small $|s|$. [step 6.1, L1, algebra]

8.1 (Nonstationarity.) For this variation, claim 2 and the choice of $J$ give $L'(0)=\frac1c\int_a^b\varphi(t)\,g_{\gamma(t)}(Z(t),D_t\gamma'(t))\,dt>0$: the pairing is positive wherever $\varphi>0$, while $\varphi$ is nonnegative and is $1$ on the nondegenerate interval $[t_0-r,t_0+r]$, so the integrand is continuous, nonnegative and strictly positive on that interval; hence the integral is strictly positive by [L5]. Therefore a curve with $D_t\gamma'\neq0$ admits a variation with $L'(0)\neq0$, and stationarity for every variation forces $D_t\gamma'=0$, proving claim 3. [step 7.1, step 4.1, L5, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

- **Scope.** The variation class is restricted to curves and variations contained in one chart domain with fixed endpoints and timelike fibers; the result asserts nothing about null curves, about maximality (which is the content of [[thm-gr-lorentz-gauss-lemma-and-normal-maximum]]), or about second variations. The chart hypothesis only serves the realization of variations in steps 6.1-7.1; the formula of claim 1 is local and holds for any $C^2$ variation of a timelike curve.

- **Physical reading.** The lemma is mathematics: it identifies the stationary curves of the length functional with geodesics. The physical statement that ideal clocks measure proper time and that freely falling bodies follow geodesics is adopted separately in [[post-gr-ideal-clocks-and-free-fall]] and is not asserted here.

## Statement

Let $(M,g,N)$ be a time-oriented smooth Lorentzian four-manifold with
metric-selected connection $\nabla$
([[def-gr-metric-selected-connection]],
[[thm-gr-nondegenerate-levi-civita-and-tensor-derivative]]), and let $p\in M$.
Let $W_p\subseteq T_pM$ be a star-shaped open neighborhood of $0$ on which
$\exp_p$ is a diffeomorphism onto an open set $U_p:=\exp_p(W_p)$, as supplied
by [[thm-gr-local-geodesics-and-exponential-map]] and
[[thm-gr-point-normal-and-exponential-coordinate-charts]]; identify
$T_v(T_pM)$ with $T_pM$ for every $v$.

**(a) Gauss lemma.** For every $v\in W_p$ and every $w\in T_pM$,

$$g_{\exp_pv}\bigl(d(\exp_p)_v(v),\,d(\exp_p)_v(w)\bigr)=g_p(v,w).$$

**(b) Radial maximum.** Let $\alpha:[0,1]\to U_p$ be a piecewise-$C^1$ future
timelike curve with $\alpha(0)=p$, with nonzero future timelike one-sided
tangents, set $q:=\alpha(1)$ and $v_q:=\exp_p^{-1}(q)$. Then

$$L_g(\alpha)\le\sqrt{-g_p(v_q,v_q)},$$

and equality holds if and only if there are $v_0\in W_p$ and a piecewise-$C^1$
strictly increasing surjection $\rho:[0,1]\to[0,\rho(1)]$ with
$v_q=\rho(1)v_0$ and $\alpha(t)=\exp_p(\rho(t)v_0)$ for all $t$; that is,
$\alpha$ is a monotone reparametrization of the radial geodesic segment from
$p$ to $q$. Dividing by $c$, the proper-time increment of $\alpha$ is at most
$\frac1c\sqrt{-g_p(v_q,v_q)}$, which is the proper-time increment of that
radial geodesic.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A time-oriented smooth Lorentzian four-manifold $(M,g,N)$ with metric-selected connection $\nabla$, a point $p$, a star-shaped domain $W_p$ on which $\exp_p$ is a diffeomorphism, and the curves and vectors named in the claims.

[L1] For $v\in W_p$ the curve $t\mapsto\exp_p(tv)$ is the geodesic $\gamma_{p,v}$ on some open interval containing $[0,1]$, and its tangent has constant norm $g_p(v,v)$; radial geodesics are defined by the initial-value problem of [[thm-gr-local-geodesics-and-exponential-map]] ([[thm-gr-local-geodesics-and-exponential-map]], [[lem-gr-parallel-transport-and-geodesic-speed]], clauses 1 and 3).

[L2] If $V,W$ are smooth sections of $\gamma^*TM$ along a smooth curve, then $\frac{d}{dt}g(V,W)=g(D_tV,W)+g(V,D_tW)$; and for a $C^2$ two-parameter family $\Phi(s,r)$ one has $D_s\partial_r\Phi=D_r\partial_s\Phi$, because in a chart both sides equal $\partial_s\partial_r\Phi^a$ plus the same symmetric $\Gamma$-contraction ([[lem-gr-parallel-transport-and-geodesic-speed]], clause 1, [[def-covariant-derivative-along-a-curve]], [[def-christoffel-symbols-of-an-affine-connection]], [[def-gr-metric-selected-connection]], [[thm-clairaut-schwarz-mixed-partials]]).

[L3] If $n$ is an observer and $w$ is nonzero future causal at the same point, then $-g(n,w)>0$. If $w$ is future unit timelike, then $-g(n,w)\ge1$, with equality exactly when $w=n$. Consequently, for any future timelike $X$, putting $s:=\sqrt{-g(X,X)}$ and $O:=X/s$ gives an observer $O$ and $-g(n,X)=s[-g(n,O)]\ge s$, with equality exactly when $O=n$ ([[lem-gr-observer-pairing-with-future-causal-vectors]], claims 1 and 2, [[def-gr-observer-and-rest-space]]).

[L4] The timelike set at a point has exactly two connected components, so a continuous curve of timelike vectors that starts future-directed and never becomes null stays future-directed; the closure of the future timelike component is the future causal cone, and a nonzero limit of future timelike vectors is future causal ([[lem-timelike-cone-has-two-convex-components]], [[def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects]], [[def-causal-classification-of-lorentz-vectors]]).

[L5] $R(t)=\sqrt{-g_p(v(t),v(t))}$ has derivative $R'=-g_p(v,v')/R$ on intervals where $R>0$, since the square root is differentiable on $(0,\infty)$; the fundamental theorem of calculus applies to $C^1$ functions on closed intervals ([[def-rational-power]], [[thm-real-power-continuity-and-derivatives]], [[thm-ftc-second-part]]).

[L6] A $C^1$ curve in $U_p$ lifted by $\exp_p^{-1}$ is piecewise $C^1$, $\alpha=\exp_p\circ v$, and $\alpha'=d(\exp_p)_v(v')$; for the inverse map $d(\exp_p^{-1})_p=\operatorname{id}$ under the canonical identification, so for the lift of a curve starting at $p$ one has $v(t)=t\alpha'(0)+o(t)$ as $t\downarrow0$ ([[thm-gr-local-geodesics-and-exponential-map]], [[def-differential-of-a-smooth-map]], [[thm-gr-point-normal-and-exponential-coordinate-charts]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 (Two-parameter family.) Let $r\mapsto v(r)$ be a smooth curve in $W_p$ and put $F(s,r):=\exp_p(sv(r))$ for $s\in[0,1]$, so that $F(0,r)=p$ and $F(1,r)=\exp_p(v(r))$. Set $T:=\partial_sF$, $J:=\partial_rF$. For each fixed $r$, $s\mapsto F(s,r)$ is the radial geodesic with tangent $v(r)$ at $s=0$; hence $D_sT=0$ and $g(T,T)=g_p(v(r),v(r))$ for all $s$ by [L1]. By [L2], $D_sJ=D_rT$. [L1, L2, given]

1.2 (The lift of a curve.) Let $\alpha$ be as in (b) and define $v(t):=\exp_p^{-1}(\alpha(t))$ for $t\in(0,1]$, $v(0):=0$; by [L6], $v$ is piecewise $C^1$ with $\alpha=\exp_p\circ v$ and $\alpha'=d(\exp_p)_v(v')$, and $v(t)=t\alpha'(0)+o(t)$. Write $Q(t):=g_p(v(t),v(t))$ and $R(t):=\sqrt{-Q(t)}$ on $\{Q<0\}$. [L5, L6, construct]

1.3 (First possible exit from the future timelike cone.) Let $T^*:=\sup\{t\in[0,1]:v(s)\text{ is future timelike for every }s\in(0,t]\}$; this is positive by [L6] and continuity, because $v(t)=t\alpha'(0)+o(t)$ and $\alpha'(0)$ is future timelike. The vector $v(t)$ is future timelike for every $0<t<T^*$. If $T^*<1$, openness of the future timelike cone rules out $v(T^*)$ being timelike, since that would extend the defining interval past $T^*$. If $T^*=1$ and $v(1)$ is future timelike, the desired cone assertion already holds at the endpoint. In every remaining case, continuity and closure of the future timelike cone imply that $v(T^*)$ is either a nonzero future causal vector or zero; after excluding the timelike case it is either future null (case A) or zero (case B). With signature $(-,+,+,+)$, $Q(T^*)=g_p(v(T^*),v(T^*))\le0$, with equality in case A; the case B value is also $0$. [L4, L6, algebra]

2.1 (Gauss computation.) By [L2] and step 1.1, $$\partial_sg(T,J)=g(D_sT,J)+g(T,D_sJ)=g(T,D_rT)=\tfrac12\partial_rg(T,T)=g_p(v(r),v'(r)),$$ using symmetry of $g$ in the middle term and step 1.1 in the last. Since $J(0,r)=\partial_r\exp_p(0)=0$, integrating the constant-in-$s$ right-hand side from $s=0$ to $s=1$ gives $g_{\exp_p(v(r))}\bigl(d(\exp_p)_{v(r)}(v(r)),d(\exp_p)_{v(r)}(v'(r))\bigr)=g_p(v(r),v'(r))$. Every pair $(v,w)$ with $v\in W_p$ is of the form $(v(0),v'(0))$ for the smooth family $r\mapsto v+rw$, so the identity holds for all such pairs; this proves (a). [step 1.1, L2, algebra]

3.1 (Case A is impossible.) In case A, $Q<0$ on $(0,T^*)$ and $Q(T^*)=0$, so its one-sided left derivative satisfies $Q'(T^{*-})\ge0$; if $T^*$ is a corner of the piecewise curve, use the left tangent and left derivative. On the smooth pieces the Gauss lemma of step 2.1 gives $Q'(t)=2g_p(v,v')=2g_{\alpha(t)}(T(t),\alpha'(t))$, where $T(t)=d(\exp_p)_{v(t)}(v(t))$ is the radial tangent. For each $t<T^*$, the radial geodesic $s\mapsto\exp_p(sv(t))$ has constant timelike speed by [L1]; its tangent begins in the future timelike component and stays there by continuity and the two-component classification [L4]. Thus $T(t)$ is future timelike. Since $v(T^*)$ is a nonzero future null vector, $d(\exp_p)_{v(T^*)}$ is invertible and $T(T^*)=\lim_{t\uparrow T^*}T(t)$ is nonzero, null by [L1], and future-directed by [L4]. Let $s:=\sqrt{-g(\alpha'(T^{*-}),\alpha'(T^{*-}))}>0$ and $O:=\alpha'(T^{*-})/s$; then $O$ is an observer. By [L3] applied to observer $O$ and future null vector $T(T^*)$, $-g(O,T(T^*))>0$. Therefore $Q'(T^{*-})=2g(T(T^*),\alpha'(T^{*-}))=2s\,g(T(T^*),O)<0$, contradicting $Q'(T^{*-})\ge0$. The argument also applies when $T^*=1$, using the left endpoint tangent of the final piece. [step 1.3, step 2.1, L1, L3, L4, algebra]

3.2 (Case B is impossible.) In case B, $v(t)$ is future timelike for $t<T^*$ and $v(T^*)=0$, so $R>0$ on $(0,T^*)$ and $R(t)\to0$ as $t\uparrow T^*$. On each smooth piece, by [L5] and the Gauss lemma, $R'=-g_p(v,v')/R=-g_{\alpha}(\widehat T,\alpha')$, where $\widehat T:=T/R$ is future unit timelike by [L1] and the future-directedness of $v$. Put $s:=\sqrt{-g(\alpha',\alpha')}>0$ and $O:=\alpha'/s$, a future unit timelike observer. Applying claim 2 of [L3] to observers $\widehat T,O$ gives $-g(\widehat T,O)\ge1$, hence $R'=-g(\widehat T,\alpha')\ge s>0$. Thus $R$ is strictly increasing on each smooth piece of $(0,T^*)$ and, by continuity across the finitely many corners, $R(t)>R(\delta)$ whenever $0<\delta<t<T^*$. Taking $t\uparrow T^*$ yields $R(T^*)\ge R(\delta)>0$, contradicting $R(T^*)=0$. This argument also applies when $T^*=1$. [step 1.3, step 2.1, L1, L3, L5, algebra]

4.1 (Length inequality.) By steps 1.2, 1.3, 3.1 and 3.2, $v(t)$ is future timelike for every $t\in(0,1]$, so $R>0$ on $(0,1]$. On each smooth piece put $s:=\sqrt{-g(\alpha',\alpha')}>0$, $O:=\alpha'/s$, and $\widehat T:=T/R$; both $O$ and $\widehat T$ are observers. Claim 2 of [L3] gives $-g(\widehat T,O)\ge1$, hence $R'=-g(\widehat T,\alpha')\ge s$. Integrating over the smooth pieces from $\delta$ to $1$ and adding gives $R(1)-R(\delta)\ge\int_\delta^1s(t)\,dt$. Letting $\delta\downarrow0$, continuity of $v$ gives $R(\delta)\to0$, and the integral over $(0,\delta)$ tends to $0$ because $s$ is bounded on the finitely many pieces with finite one-sided limits. Therefore $L_g(\alpha)\le R(1)=\sqrt{-g_p(v_q,v_q)}$. Dividing by $c$ and using [[lem-gr-proper-time-reparametrization-and-four-velocity]] gives the proper-time statement. [step 3.2, L3, L5, algebra]

5.1 (Equality case.) If equality holds in step 4.1, the continuous nonnegative function $R'-s$ on each smooth open piece has zero integral over $(0,1)$, so it vanishes identically there. Consequently $\alpha(t)=\exp_p(\rho(t)v_0)$, which is the equality form in (b). Conversely, for any equality-form curve from the Statement, $\alpha'=\rho'\,d(\exp_p)_{\rho v_0}(v_0)$ has norm $|\rho'|\sqrt{-g_p(v_0,v_0)}$; its length is $\rho(1)\sqrt{-g_p(v_0,v_0)}=\sqrt{-g_p(v_q,v_q)}$, so equality holds. This proves (b) and completes the proof. [step 4.1, L3, L5, L6, algebra] ∎

## Proof

3.1 (No mass or structure parameter.) If $W$ is timelike, then either $W$ is future timelike or $-W$ is future timelike; in the second case $R(W,J)W=R(-W,J)(-W)=0$ as well. ## Example

Let $M=\mathbb R^4$ carry the identity chart, the flat metric
$g=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$, the time orientation $N=\partial_0$ and the
chart orientation, with metric-selected connection $\nabla$ and volume form
$\mathrm{vol}_g$ ([[def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects]],
[[def-oriented-smooth-manifold-and-oriented-chart]],
[[def-gr-metric-volume-form-and-current-flux]]). Let

$$J:=x^1\partial_2$$

be the smooth vector field of [[def-smooth-vector-field-as-a-tangent-bundle-section]].
Then:

1. $\nabla_aJ^a=\partial_2x^1=0$: the current is divergence-free.
2. The current $3$-form is
   $\iota_J\mathrm{vol}_g=x^1\,dx^0\wedge dx^1\wedge dx^3$, and
   $d(\iota_J\mathrm{vol}_g)=0$ identically, consistently with clause (a) of
   [[thm-gr-density-divergence-and-finite-chain-flux]].
3. The pointwise flux density of $J$ through a non-null face with adapted unit
   normal $\nu^{*}$ is $\varepsilon g(J,\nu^{*})\sqrt{|\det(g(v_i,v_j))|}$; for
   the coordinate directions this is
   $$0\ \text{for }\nu^{*}=\pm\partial_0,\pm\partial_1,\qquad x^1\ \text{for }\nu^{*}=\partial_2,\qquad -x^1\ \text{for }\nu^{*}=-\partial_2 .$$
   Thus the divergence-free current has nonzero flux density through the faces
   normal to $\partial_2$: vanishing divergence does not mean vanishing flux
   through every face, and the flux balance
   $\Phi_J(\Sigma_2)-\Phi_J(\Sigma_1)=-\Phi_J(B)$ of clause (b) of
   [[thm-gr-density-divergence-and-finite-chain-flux]] controls the side
   boundary genuinely.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The flat Lorentzian four-manifold $(\mathbb R^4,g)$ with identity chart, $g=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$, metric-selected connection $\nabla$, volume form $\mathrm{vol}_g$ and the vector field $J=x^1\partial_2$.

[L1] $\mathrm{vol}_g=dx^0\wedge dx^1\wedge dx^2\wedge dx^3$, $w=1$, and $\Gamma\equiv0$ on the identity chart ([[def-gr-metric-volume-form-and-current-flux]], [[lem-gr-metric-divergence-and-density-formula]], [[thm-gr-nondegenerate-levi-civita-and-tensor-derivative]]).

[L2] $\iota_{\partial_2}\mathrm{vol}_g=(-1)^2dx^0\wedge dx^1\wedge dx^3$ and $\iota_J\mathrm{vol}_g$ is obtained by multiplying the $\partial_2$-insertion by the component $J^2=x^1$ ([[def-interior-product-of-a-form-by-a-vector-field]], [[def-wedge-product-of-differential-forms]]).

[L3] $d(\iota_J\mathrm{vol}_g)=(\nabla_aJ^a)\mathrm{vol}_g$ ([[thm-gr-density-divergence-and-finite-chain-flux]] clause (a)).

[L4] On a non-null face with velocity basis $(v_1,v_2,v_3)$ and adapted unit normal $\nu^{*}$, $\varepsilon=g(\nu^{*},\nu^{*})$ and the pullback of $\iota_J\mathrm{vol}_g$ on $(v_1,v_2,v_3)$ is $\varepsilon g(J,\nu^{*})\sqrt{|\det(g(v_i,v_j))|}$ ([[thm-gr-density-divergence-and-finite-chain-flux]] clause (c), [[lem-gr-metric-volume-form-patching-and-face-normal]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct; compute the divergence, the current form and its derivative, then evaluate the flux densities on the coordinate normals.

1.1 (Divergence.) By [L1], $\nabla_aJ^a=\partial_aJ^a=\partial_0 0+\partial_1 0+\partial_2x^1+\partial_3 0=0$. [L1, algebra]

2.1 (Current form.) By [L2] and $J^2=x^1$, $\iota_J\mathrm{vol}_g=x^1dx^0\wedge dx^1\wedge dx^3$. Its exterior derivative is $\partial_1x^1\,dx^1\wedge dx^0\wedge dx^1\wedge dx^3=0$, because $dx^1$ occurs twice; consistently, [L3] and step 1.1 give $d(\iota_J\mathrm{vol}_g)=0$. [step 1.1, L2, L3, algebra]

3.1 (Flux densities.) At a point of a face with adapted unit normal $\nu^{*}=\partial_2$ (so $\varepsilon=g(\partial_2,\partial_2)=1$ and $\sqrt{|\det(g(v_i,v_j))|}=1$ for a coordinate face), [L4] gives the flux density $g(J,\partial_2)=g(x^1\partial_2,\partial_2)=x^1$; for $\nu^{*}=-\partial_2$ it is $-x^1$. For $\nu^{*}=\pm\partial_1$, $g(J,\pm\partial_1)=0$; for $\nu^{*}=\pm\partial_0$, $g(J,\pm\partial_0)=0$. This is the displayed list of values. Since $x^1\neq0$ on the open set $\{x^1\neq0\}$, the flux density through the faces normal to $\partial_2$ is nonzero there although the divergence of $J$ vanishes identically, which completes the verification. [step 2.1, L4, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

- **Reading.** The example isolates the pointwise flux density: a divergence-free current can have nonzero density through faces normal to $\partial_2$. The flux-balance theorem concerns integrals over oriented chains and states exactly how the slice fluxes differ by the side flux; whether a whole side-chain flux vanishes depends on that chain and its orientation.

## Example

Let $M=\mathbb R^4$ carry the identity chart, the flat metric
$g=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$ and the chart orientation, with
metric-selected connection $\nabla$ ([[def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects]],
[[def-gr-metric-selected-connection]],
[[def-oriented-smooth-manifold-and-oriented-chart]]). Let $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$
be a smooth function, let

$$\varphi(x):=f(x^0+x^1),$$

and let $(\varphi,V,K)$ be the scalar-field matter model with $V\equiv0$ and
$K>0$ ([[def-gr-scalar-field-matter-model]]). Then:

1. $\partial^c\varphi\,\partial_c\varphi=0$: the metric dual of $d\varphi$ is
   null wherever $d\varphi\ne0$ and is zero wherever $d\varphi=0$.
2. $\Box_g\varphi=\nabla^a\partial_a\varphi=0$: $\varphi$ satisfies the scalar
   equation of motion, and its stress-energy tensor is
   $T_{ab}=K\,\partial_a\varphi\,\partial_b\varphi$.
3. $\nabla^aT_{ab}=0$: the tensor is divergence-free, and $T(v,v)=K(\partial_a\varphi\,v^a)^2\ge0$
   for every $v\in T_pM$, with equality exactly on the kernel $\partial_a\varphi\,v^a=0$; this is a hyperplane where $d\varphi\ne0$ and the full tangent space where $d\varphi=0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The flat Lorentzian four-manifold $(\mathbb R^4,g)$ with identity chart and $g=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$; the smooth function $\varphi=f(x^0+x^1)$; and the scalar-field matter model $(\varphi,V,K)$ with $V\equiv0$, $K>0$.

[L1] For the flat metric in the identity chart, the inverse-metric components are $\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$ and $\Box_g\varphi=g^{ab}\partial_a\partial_b\varphi=-\partial_0^2\varphi+\partial_1^2\varphi+\partial_2^2\varphi+\partial_3^2\varphi$ ([[def-gr-inverse-metric-and-musical-isomorphisms]], [[thm-gr-nondegenerate-levi-civita-and-tensor-derivative]]).

[L2] The scalar-field model has equation of motion $\Box_g\varphi-V'(\varphi)=0$ and candidate tensor $T_{ab}=K[\partial_a\varphi\partial_b\varphi-g_{ab}(\tfrac12\partial^c\varphi\partial_c\varphi+V(\varphi))]$; it satisfies $\nabla^aT_{ab}=K(\Box_g\varphi-V'(\varphi))\partial_b\varphi$ ([[def-gr-scalar-field-matter-model]], [[prop-gr-scalar-field-stress-energy-conservation]] clause (c)).

[L3] For a smooth function of $u=x^0+x^1$, the chain rule gives $\partial_0\varphi=\partial_1\varphi=f'(u)$ and $\partial_2\varphi=\partial_3\varphi=0$ ([[def-c-r-and-smooth-maps-between-smooth-manifolds]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct; apply the chain rule and evaluate the flat-space expressions.

1.1 (Null or zero gradient.) By [L3], $\partial_a\varphi=(f'(u),f'(u),0,0)$ in the identity chart. By [L1], $\partial^a\varphi\partial_a\varphi=g^{ab}\partial_a\varphi\partial_b\varphi=-(f')^2+(f')^2+0+0=0$. Thus the metric dual of $d\varphi$ is null where $f'(u)\ne0$ and is the zero vector where $f'(u)=0$. [L1, L3, algebra]

2.1 (Equation of motion.) Again by [L3], $\partial_0^2\varphi=f''(u)$ and $\partial_1^2\varphi=f''(u)$, while $\partial_2^2\varphi=\partial_3^2\varphi=0$; hence $\Box_g\varphi=-f''+f''=0$. Since $V\equiv0$, the scalar equation $\Box_g\varphi=V'(\varphi)=0$ holds. Using step 1.1 in the tensor formula of [L2], the metric bracket vanishes and $T_{ab}=K\partial_a\varphi\partial_b\varphi$. [step 1.1, L1, L2, L3, algebra]

3.1 (Conservation and positivity.) By the conservation identity of [L2] and step 2.1, $\nabla^aT_{ab}=K\cdot0\cdot\partial_b\varphi=0$. For any $v\in T_pM$, $T(v,v)=K\partial_a\varphi\partial_b\varphi\,v^av^b=K(\partial_a\varphi\,v^a)^2\ge0$ because $K>0$ and the square is nonnegative; equality holds exactly when the linear functional $\partial_a\varphi\,v^a$ vanishes, i.e. on its kernel $\{v:\partial_a\varphi\,v^a=0\}$, a hyperplane at nonzero gradient and the whole tangent space at zero gradient, containing the gradient direction itself. This completes the verification. [step 2.1, L2, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

- **Reading.** The example is the flat-space plane-wave branch of the scalar model: the kinetic bracket vanishes identically on a null gradient, so the stress-energy tensor is a nonnegative tensor of rank at most one, with rank one wherever the gradient is nonzero. It illustrates the conservation identity of the proposition without any physical claim about the model's realization.

## Definition

Let $(M,g,N)$ be a time-oriented smooth Lorentzian four-manifold
([[def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects]]), let $U\subseteq M$ be a nonempty open
subset carrying the restricted smooth structure of
[[prop-an-open-subset-of-a-smooth-manifold-has-a-canonical-restricted-smooth-structure]],
choose an orientation on $U$ and its metric volume form (independent of the
time orientation), let $T$ be a smooth symmetric covariant $2$-tensor field on $U$
([[def-gr-smooth-covariant-two-tensor-field]]) — in an application this is the
stress-energy tensor field of the model's matter sector
([[def-gr-stress-energy-tensor-field]]) — and let $\xi$ be a Killing field of
$(M,g)$ on $U$ ([[def-gr-killing-field]],
[[thm-gr-killing-and-on-shell-matter-identities]] clause (a)) that is
everywhere timelike and future-directed,

$$g_p(\xi_p,\xi_p)<0,\qquad g_p(\xi_p,N_p)<0\qquad\text{for every }p\in U .$$

Put $X:=\sqrt{-g(\xi,\xi)}>0$ on $U$ and $n:=\xi/X$, an observer congruence on
$U$: $g(n,n)=-1$ and $g(n,N)=g(\xi,N)/X<0$
([[def-gr-observer-congruence-and-adapted-charts]]).

**(a) Killing current.** The **Killing current** of the pair $(T,\xi)$ is the
smooth vector field on $U$

$$J_\xi{}^a:=T^{ab}\xi_b .$$

Since $\xi=Xn$ and $J^a=-T^{ab}n_b$, one has
$T^{ab}\xi_b=-XJ^a$, where $J=-T(\cdot,n)$ is the measured
energy-momentum current of [[def-gr-observer-energy-momentum-current]]; no
conservation of $J_\xi$ is asserted by this definition.

**(b) Killing charge of a three-chain.** For every finite smooth singular
$3$-chain $\Sigma$ of $M$ contained in $U$, the **Killing charge** of $\Sigma$
with respect to $\xi$ is the real number

$$Q_\xi(\Sigma):=-\Phi_{J_\xi}(\Sigma),$$

where $\Phi_{J_\xi}$ is the flux of the current $J_\xi$ through
$3$-chains on the oriented open submanifold $U$ in the sense of [[def-gr-metric-volume-form-and-current-flux]]
clause (b).

When $\xi$ is normalized with a length-valued flow parameter in the SI
length-coordinate convention, this charge is read as a **Killing energy**.

**(c) Relation to the measured energy current.** Since $J_\xi=-XJ$,

$$Q_\xi(\Sigma)=\Phi_{XJ}(\Sigma),$$

the flux of the redshift-weighted measured energy current $XJ$. For a
$3$-simplex $\sigma$ that is non-null with velocity basis $(v_1,v_2,v_3)$ and
adapted unit normal $\nu^{*}$ (so that $(\nu^{*},v_1,v_2,v_3)$ is positively
oriented and $\varepsilon:=g(\nu^{*},\nu^{*})\in\{-1,1\}$), the pullback of
$\iota_{XJ}\mathrm{vol}_g$ is
$\varepsilon\,g(XJ,\nu^{*})\sqrt{|\det(g(v_i,v_j))|}\,dt^1\wedge dt^2\wedge dt^3$
([[thm-gr-density-divergence-and-finite-chain-flux]] clause (c)); when
$\nu^{*}=-n$ and the $v_i$ span $n^{\perp}$ this coefficient equals
$-X\mu\sqrt{|\det(g(v_i,v_j))|}$, so up to the orientation sign carried by the
adapted normal, $Q_\xi(\Sigma)$ is the integral of the redshift-weighted
measured energy density $X\mu$ over $\Sigma$. The factor $X$ carries the
normalization and units of $\xi$.

**(d) Reading and scope.** $Q_\xi(\Sigma)$ is the charge that the model
assigns to the chain $\Sigma$ under the symmetry $\xi$, in the framework
of the postulates [[post-gr-spacetime-and-classical-domain]],
[[post-gr-field-and-matter-assumptions]] with the assigned tensor and symmetry; it does not require the field law
for its definition. Under the unit conventions of
[[def-gr-si-unit-and-coordinate-conventions]], if $T$ is read in
$\mathrm J\,\mathrm m^{-3}$ and the flow parameter of $\xi$ is measured in
metres, then $X$ is dimensionless and $Q_\xi(\Sigma)$ is read in $\mathrm J$.
More generally, if that parameter has unit $\mathcal S$, then in a
length-coordinate chart $X$ has units $\mathrm m/\mathcal S$ and $Q_\xi$ has
units $\mathrm J\,\mathrm m/\mathcal S$; converting it to an energy requires
an explicitly specified normalization factor. The value depends on the model,
on $\xi$ (replacing $\xi$ by a dimensionless constant multiple $\lambda\xi$
with $\lambda>0$ multiplies $X$, $J_\xi$ and $Q_\xi$ by $\lambda$), and on the
chain $\Sigma$. The definition asserts no conservation, no positivity, no
finiteness for unbounded chains and no measured value. If additionally
$\nabla^aT_{ab}=0$ on $U$, the Killing
current lemma gives conservation up to the side flux. The field law
([[post-gr-einstein-field-law]] or [[post-gr-classical-gravitational-action]])
is one way to supply this divergence condition; it is not necessary for
the mathematical conservation implication. If no
timelike Killing field exists on $U$, $Q_\xi$ is not defined by this item.

## Facts & Assumptions

[L2] $\xi=\partial_0$ has constant components in the identity chart, so it is Killing, $g(\xi,\xi)=-1$, $g(\xi,N)=-1$ and $X=1$; hence $n=\xi$ is a smooth observer congruence ([[def-gr-killing-field]], [[thm-gr-killing-and-on-shell-matter-identities]] clause (a), [[def-gr-observer-congruence-and-adapted-charts]], [[def-causal-classification-of-lorentz-vectors]]).

[L3] $(\rho,\partial_0)$ is a dust model with tensor $T^{ab}=\rho\,u^au^b$; the dust system holds for constant $\rho$ and constant $u$, and it is equivalent to $\nabla^aT_{ab}=0$ ([[def-gr-dust-matter-model]], [[prop-gr-dust-and-perfect-fluid-conservation-equations]] clause (a)).

[L4] The Killing current and Killing energy are $J_\xi{}^a=T^{ab}\xi_b$ and $Q_\xi(\Sigma)=-\Phi_{J_\xi}(\Sigma)$; if $\xi$ is timelike with $X=\sqrt{-g(\xi,\xi)}$ and $n=\xi/X$, then $J_\xi=-XJ$ with $J$ the measured current ([[def-gr-killing-energy-charge]] clauses (a)-(c), [[def-gr-observer-energy-momentum-current]]).

[L5] If $T$ is divergence-free and $\xi$ is timelike Killing on the oriented domain, $J_\xi=-XJ$ is divergence-free by [[lem-gr-killing-congruence-kinematics-and-energy-current]] clause (d). Finite-chain Stokes gives the Killing energy balance $Q_\xi(\Sigma_2)-Q_\xi(\Sigma_1)=-Q_\xi(B)$ for every finite smooth singular $4$-chain with $\partial C=\Sigma_2-\Sigma_1+B$ ([[thm-gr-density-divergence-and-finite-chain-flux]] clause (b)).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct; check the Killing and dust hypotheses, compute the Killing current and its three-form in the identity chart, evaluate the flux on slice chains, and apply the divergence-free Killing-current lemma and finite-chain Stokes.

1.1 (Hypotheses.) By [L2], $\xi$ is a Killing field that is everywhere timelike and future-directed, with $X=1$ and $n=\xi$; by [L3] the constant dust $(\rho,\partial_0)$ satisfies the dust system and $\nabla^aT_{ab}=0$. Hence the Killing-current construction applies and the conservation and symmetry hypotheses of [L5] are met; no gravitational field equation is used. [L2, L3, given]

2.1 (The Killing current and its three-form.) By [L1], $\xi_b=\eta_{b0}=-\delta^0{}_b$, so $J_\xi{}^a=T^{ab}\xi_b=\rho\,u^au^b\xi_b=\rho\,\partial_0^a(u^0\xi_0)=\rho\,\partial_0^a(1\cdot(-1))=-\rho\,\partial_0^a$. This proves clause 2. [step 1.1, L1, L4, algebra]

3.1 (Killing energy on slice chains.) Let $\sigma$ be a smooth singular $3$-simplex of $M$ whose chart velocity vectors at every parameter point are $\sigma_*\partial_i=\partial_i$ for $i=1,2,3$ (so that its image lies in a constant-$x^0$ slice and it is parametrized by the slice coordinates). Then $\sigma^*(dx^1\wedge dx^2\wedge dx^3)=dt^1\wedge dt^2\wedge dt^3$, so by the definition of the flux through a simplex as the integral of the pulled-back current form, $\Phi_{J_\xi}(\sigma)=-\rho\int_{\Delta^3}dt^1\wedge dt^2\wedge dt^3$. By additivity of the flux over finite chains the same formula holds for every finite smooth singular $3$-chain $\Sigma$ with the stated velocity vectors, and $Q_\xi(\Sigma)=-\Phi_{J_\xi}(\Sigma)=\rho\int_\Sigma dx^1\wedge dx^2\wedge dx^3$ by [L4]. This proves clause 3. [step 2.1, L1, L4, algebra]

4.1 (Conservation and side flux.) By [L5] applied to the present Killing field, $Q_\xi(\Sigma_2)-Q_\xi(\Sigma_1)=-Q_\xi(B)$ for every finite smooth singular $4$-chain $C$ with $\partial C=\Sigma_2-\Sigma_1+B$. If the velocity vectors of every simplex of $B$ span a hyperplane containing $\partial_0$, then the four arguments $J_\xi,v_1,v_2,v_3$ of $\mathrm{vol}_g$ are linearly dependent — $J_\xi$ is a constant multiple of $\partial_0$ — so $\iota_{J_\xi}\mathrm{vol}_g$ vanishes on them and $\Phi_{J_\xi}(B)=0$, whence $Q_\xi(B)=0$ and $Q_\xi(\Sigma_2)=Q_\xi(\Sigma_1)$. ## Statement

Adopt one of the two presentations of the framework's field law,
[[post-gr-einstein-field-law]] or
[[post-gr-classical-gravitational-action]], for a model with geometric data
and matter sector satisfying [[post-gr-spacetime-and-classical-domain]] and
[[post-gr-field-and-matter-assumptions]]; the two presentations define one
field law by [[pthm-gr-adopted-action-and-einstein-equations]]. Let $(M,g,N)$
be the model's smooth time-oriented Lorentzian four-manifold with an
orientation, let $U\subseteq M$ be the matter domain with the induced orientation, let $T$ be the model's
smooth symmetric stress-energy tensor field on $U$
([[def-gr-stress-energy-tensor-field]]), and let $n$ be a smooth observer
congruence on $U$, read as the field of directions of ideal-clock observers of
the model ([[post-gr-ideal-clocks-and-free-fall]]). Let $\mu$, $J$, $\Phi$, $S$
be the measured fields of [[def-gr-observer-energy-momentum-current]], with the
physical reading and units of [[def-gr-measured-energy-density-flux-and-stress]]: $\Phi$ is the normalized spatial tensor current and its SI measured
energy-flux vector is $q=c\Phi$,
and let $a$, $\theta$, $D$ be the kinematic fields of
[[def-gr-congruence-acceleration-and-expansion]].

**(a) On-shell conservation.** The adopted field law implies

$$\nabla^aT_{ab}=0\qquad\text{on }U .$$

**(b) Local energy balance.** On $U$,

$$D\mu+\mu\theta+\nabla_a\Phi^a=\nabla_aJ^a=-T^{ab}\nabla_an_b .$$

Thus the energy density measured by the congruence, its normalized spatial
current $\Phi$ (SI energy flux $q=c\Phi$) and its expansion obey a local balance whose source is the congruence term
$-T^{ab}\nabla_an_b$; the measured energy current is conserved on an open set
exactly where that source vanishes there.

**(c) Finite-chain bookkeeping.** For every finite smooth singular $4$-chain
$C$ of $M$ contained in $U$ with induced boundary
$\partial C=\Sigma_2-\Sigma_1+B$,

$$\Phi_J(\Sigma_2)-\Phi_J(\Sigma_1)=-\Phi_J(B)+\int_C\bigl(-T^{ab}\nabla_an_b\bigr)\mathrm{vol}_g .$$

In particular, if the source term vanishes on an open set containing the image
of $C$ and the side flux $\Phi_J(B)$ vanishes, then
$\Phi_J(\Sigma_2)=\Phi_J(\Sigma_1)$: equal measured energy needs a vanishing
side flux, not merely on-shell conservation.

**(d) Stationary models and the Killing charge.** Suppose in addition that
$U$ carries a Killing field $\xi$ of $(M,g)$ that is everywhere timelike and
future-directed, with $X:=\sqrt{-g(\xi,\xi)}>0$ and $n=\xi/X$, and let
$Q_\xi$ be the Killing charge of
[[def-gr-killing-energy-charge]]. Then:

1. $\nabla_a(XJ^a)=0$ and $\nabla_a(T^{ab}\xi_b)=0$ on $U$.
2. For every finite smooth singular $4$-chain $C\subseteq U$ with
   $\partial C=\Sigma_2-\Sigma_1+B$,

$$Q_\xi(\Sigma_2)-Q_\xi(\Sigma_1)=-Q_\xi(B);$$

   in particular, if $Q_\xi(B)=0$ then $Q_\xi(\Sigma_2)=Q_\xi(\Sigma_1)$: the
   model's Killing charge assigned to homologous slices is conserved up to the
   side flux, and is conserved when that flux vanishes. With a length-normalized
   $\xi$, this charge is the model's energy in joules.
3. In this stationary case the measured energy current itself satisfies
   $\nabla_aJ^a=-\Phi^aa_a$ with $a=\nabla\ln X$, so
   $\nabla_aJ^a=0$ on $U$ if and only if $\Phi^aa_a=0$ there.

**(e) Qualification.** The theorem asserts no existence of a timelike Killing
field, no finiteness or convergence for chains outside the finite smooth
singular class, no asymptotic or quasilocal mass, no positivity of $\mu$, and
no measured value. Its conclusions are conditional on the adopted field law,
the stated smoothness and the stated chains; the theorem uses no empirical
premise, and any later use of a numerical energy must carry its own
measurement, calibration and uncertainty qualifications.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The adopted field law in one of its two presentations; a model with smooth $(M,g,N)$, matter domain $U$, smooth symmetric stress-energy tensor $T$; a smooth observer congruence $n$ on $U$; the measured fields $\mu$, $J$, $\Phi$, $S$ of [[def-gr-observer-energy-momentum-current]] and the kinematic fields $a$, $\theta$, $D$ of [[def-gr-congruence-acceleration-and-expansion]]. In (d) additionally the timelike future-directed Killing field $\xi$ on $U$ with $X=\sqrt{-g(\xi,\xi)}$ and $n=\xi/X$, and the Killing energy $Q_\xi$ of [[def-gr-killing-energy-charge]].

[L1] The field equation holds on the matter domain $U$ in the direct presentation, and in the action presentation its equation form is that of [[pthm-gr-adopted-action-and-einstein-equations]] (for prescribed tensors the equation is adopted directly). Restrict the metric, time orientation and connection to the open manifold $U$ and apply [[pthm-gr-einstein-consistency-and-energy-conditions]] clause (a) with ambient manifold $U$: it yields $\nabla^aT_{ab}=0$ on $U$, without an extension of $T$ beyond $U$.

Consequently, for every smooth $\varphi$, every $k$ and every $\alpha$, $\int \chi_kD^\alpha\varphi\,d\mu=(-1)^{|\alpha|}(ik)^\alpha\int\chi_k\varphi\,d\mu$: induct on $|\alpha|$, moving one variable derivative from $\varphi$ onto $\chi_k$ by the one-variable identity and using $\partial_j\chi_k=ik_j\chi_k$. [L2, L3, algebra]

1.2 (The symbol bounds.) For $k\in\mathbb Z^3$ put $q_k:=\sum_{|\alpha|\le s}|k^\alpha|^2$. Each term is at most $(1+|k|^2)^s$, and there are at most $(s+1)^3$ terms, so $q_k\le(s+1)^3(1+|k|^2)^s$. Conversely, the multinomial expansion of $(1+k_1^2+k_2^2+k_3^2)^s$ is $\sum_{|\alpha|\le s}c_\alpha |k^\alpha|^2$, where $c_\alpha=s!/((s-|\alpha|)!\alpha!)$. These nonnegative integer coefficients sum to $4^s$ by setting all four variables to $1$, so every $c_\alpha\le4^s$. It follows that $(1+|k|^2)^s\le4^s q_k$. This includes $s=0$, where both sides are $1$. Hence $4^{-s}(1+|k|^2)^s\le q_k\le(s+1)^3(1+|k|^2)^s$. [L4, algebra]

2.1 (The differentiation identity (a).) If $u\in H^s$ and $|\alpha|\le s$, then $D^\alpha u\in H^{s-|\alpha|}$: a weak derivative of $D^\alpha u$ of order $|\beta|\le s-|\alpha|$ is the weak derivative $D^{\alpha+\beta}u$, because the defining test identity for the iterated derivative follows by applying the two defining identities in succession and noting that $D^\beta\varphi$ is again a smooth test function. Testing the defining identity for $D^\alpha u$ against $\varphi=\chi_{-k}$ gives $\widehat{D^\alpha u}(k)=(-1)^{|\alpha|}\int u\,D^\alpha\chi_{-k}\,d\mu=(-1)^{|\alpha|}(-ik)^\alpha\int u\,\chi_{-k}\,d\mu=(ik)^\alpha\widehat u(k)$, where the middle equality uses step 1.1 for the smooth test function $D^\alpha\chi_{-k}=(-ik)^\alpha\chi_{-k}$ and the last uses $(-1)^{|\alpha|}(-i)^\alpha=i^\alpha$. [step 1.1, L2, algebra]

2.2 (The norm bounds for $u\in H^s$.) By Parseval [L1] applied to the $L^2$ classes $D^\alpha u$, $\|D^\alpha u\|_2^2=\sum_k|k^\alpha|^2|\widehat u(k)|^2$, so the definition of the $H^s$ norm gives $\|u\|_{H^s}^2=\sum_kq_k|\widehat u(k)|^2$. Combining with step 1.2, $\|u\|_{H^s}^2\le(s+1)^3N_s(u)^2$ and $N_s(u)^2\le4^s\|u\|_{H^s}^2$, which are the right-hand inequality and the converse inequality of (b) whenever $u\in H^s$. [step 1.2, L1, algebra]

3.1 (Converse direction of (b): the weighted series produce weak derivatives.) Let $u\in L^2$ with $N_s(u)<\infty$. For each $|\alpha|\le s$ the sequence $k\mapsto(ik)^\alpha\widehat u(k)$ is square-summable because $|k^\alpha|^2\le(1+|k|^2)^{|\alpha|}\le(1+|k|^2)^s$; by the surjectivity of the coefficient isometry [L1] there is a unique class $w_\alpha\in L^2$ with $\widehat{w_\alpha}(k)=(ik)^\alpha\widehat u(k)$. For a smooth real test $\varphi$, Parseval's pairing identity [L1] gives $\int u\,D^\alpha\varphi\,d\mu=\sum_k\widehat u(k)\overline{\widehat{D^\alpha\varphi}(k)}=\sum_k\widehat u(k)\overline{(ik)^\alpha\widehat\varphi(k)}=\sum_k(-1)^{|\alpha|}(ik)^\alpha\widehat u(k)\overline{\widehat\varphi(k)}=(-1)^{|\alpha|}\int w_\alpha\varphi\,d\mu$, where the second equality is step 2.1 applied to the smooth $\varphi$ and the third uses $\overline{(ik)^\alpha}=(-i)^\alpha k^\alpha=(-1)^{|\alpha|}(ik)^\alpha$. Hence $D^\alpha u=w_\alpha$ weakly for every $|\alpha|\le s$, so $u\in H^s$. [step 2.1, L1, L2, algebra]

4.1 (Conclusion.) By step 3.1 and step 2.2, for $u$ with $N_s(u)<\infty$ the class lies in $H^s$ and satisfies $4^{-s}N_s(u)^2\le\|u\|_{H^s}^2\le(s+1)^3N_s(u)^2$, so (b) holds with $c_s=4^{-s}$ and $C_s=(s+1)^3$, while (a) was proved in step 2.1. [step 3.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, construct, discharge-construct] ∎

## Definition

Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$
([[def-countable-choice]]). Fix an integer $m\ge1$, an open set
$O\subseteq\mathbb R^m$ with the subspace topology and a nondegenerate open
interval $I\subseteq\mathbb R$ containing $0$. All functions on
$\mathbb T^3_{2\pi}$ are those of [[def-gr-periodic-torus-and-sobolev-spaces]],
and smoothness on $I\times\mathbb T^3_{2\pi}\times O$ means smoothness of the
representative in the sense of clause (e) of that item.

**(a) Coefficients.** A **periodic symmetric hyperbolic coefficient family** on
$O$ consists of smooth maps
$$A^\mu:I\times\mathbb T^3_{2\pi}\times O\to\operatorname{Sym}_m(\mathbb R)\quad(\mu=0,1,2,3),\qquad B:I\times\mathbb T^3_{2\pi}\times O\to\mathbb R^m,$$
such that $A^0(t,x,u)$ is positive definite for every
$(t,x,u)\in I\times\mathbb T^3_{2\pi}\times O$; here
$\operatorname{Sym}_m(\mathbb R)$ is the real vector space of symmetric
$m\times m$ matrices, identified with symmetric endomorphisms of $\mathbb R^m$
through the standard coordinate inner product, and positive definiteness means
$z^TA^0(t,x,u)z>0$ for every nonzero $z\in\mathbb R^m$
([[def-ck-and-multi-index-notation-in-several-variables]]).

**(b) The system.** The associated **symmetric hyperbolic system** is
$$A^0(t,x,u)\,\partial_tu+\sum_{i=1}^3A^i(t,x,u)\,\partial_iu+B(t,x,u)=0 .\qquad(\star)$$

**(c) Values and norms.** For each integer $s\ge0$, an $H^s$ class
$u\in H^s(\mathbb T^3_{2\pi};\mathbb R^m)$ **takes values in a set
$E\subseteq\mathbb R^m$** when it has a continuous representative whose values
lie in $E$. Such a representative is unique, since continuous functions equal
almost everywhere on the torus agree everywhere. A map $u:J\to H^s$ with
$J\subseteq I$ takes values in $E$ when every class $u(t)$ does. For compact
$K\subseteq O$ and $R\ge0$ put
$$\mathcal S_s(K,R):=\bigl\{u_0\in H^s(\mathbb T^3_{2\pi};\mathbb R^m):u_0\text{ takes values in }K,\ \|u_0\|_{H^s}\le R\bigr\}.$$
When an item fixes $s$, it may abbreviate this class by $\mathcal S(K,R)$.

**(d) Classical solutions.** Let $s\ge2$ be an integer and let $J\subseteq I$ be
an interval. A **classical solution of $(\star)$ on
$J\times\mathbb T^3_{2\pi}$** is a $C^1$ map
$u:J\times\mathbb T^3_{2\pi}\to\mathbb R^m$ with $u(t,x)\in O$ for all
$(t,x)$ and $(\star)$ satisfied at every point, such that
$u\in L^\infty(J;H^s)$ and $\partial_tu\in L^\infty(J;H^{s-1})$.

**(e) Sobolev solutions.** Let $s\ge5$ be an integer and let $J\subseteq I$ be
an open interval. A map
$u:J\to H^s(\mathbb T^3_{2\pi};\mathbb R^m)$ is a **Sobolev solution of
$(\star)$ on $J$** when

1. $u\in L^\infty(J;H^s)\cap C^0(J;H^{s-1})$ and the distributional time
   derivative $\partial_tu$ lies in $L^\infty(J;H^{s-1})$;
2. $u$ takes values in $O$ using the continuous representatives from (c);
3. for almost every $t\in J$ the identity $(\star)$ holds in
   $L^2(\mathbb T^3_{2\pi};\mathbb R^m)$, with coefficients evaluated using
   the continuous representative of $u(t)$. This condition requires each
   displayed product to define an $L^2$ class; it does not assert a global
   coefficient-composition bound depending only on $\|u(t)\|_{H^s}$.

**(f) Initial data.** For an integer $s\ge5$, **initial data for $(\star)$ at
time $0$** are a class $u_0\in H^s(\mathbb T^3_{2\pi};\mathbb R^m)$ whose values
lie in a compact subset of $O$; a solution **has initial value $u_0$** when
$u(t)\to u_0$ in $H^{s-1}$ as $t\to0$ within $J$.

The definition introduces the system and its solution classes only; existence,
uniqueness, continuous dependence and smooth persistence are asserted and
proved in [[thm-gr-periodic-symmetric-hyperbolic-existence]],
[[thm-gr-periodic-symmetric-hyperbolic-uniqueness-stability]] and
[[thm-gr-periodic-symmetric-hyperbolic-smooth-persistence]].

## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$
([[def-countable-choice]]). Let $m\ge1$, $N\ge0$, and let $P_N$ be the Galerkin
projection and $X_N$ the Galerkin space of [[def-gr-galerkin-fourier-projection]]
on $\mathbb T^3_{2\pi}$. Then:

**(a)** $P_N$ is linear on $L^2(\mathbb T^3_{2\pi};\mathbb R^m)$, self-adjoint
($\langle P_Nu,v\rangle_2=\langle u,P_Nv\rangle_2$ for all $u,v\in L^2$) and
idempotent ($P_N^2=P_N$), with range $X_N$ and kernel the classes with vanishing
coefficients on $\Lambda_N$;

**(b)** for every integer $s\ge0$, every multi-index $\alpha$ with
$|\alpha|\le s$ and every $u\in H^s$, $P_ND^\alpha u=D^\alpha P_Nu$;

**(c)** for every integer $r\ge0$ and every $u\in H^r$,
$\|P_Nu\|_{H^r}\le\|u\|_{H^r}$;

**(d)** for every integer $r\ge0$ and every $u\in H^r$, $P_Nu\to u$ in $H^r$ as
$N\to\infty$;

**(e)** for every integer $s\ge1$, every $u\in H^s$ and every $N\ge0$,
$$\|u-P_Nu\|_{H^{s-1}}\le C_s\,(1+N^2)^{-1/2}\,\|u\|_{H^s}$$
with $C_s$ depending only on $s$.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** Countable Choice, $m\ge1$, $N\ge0$, and the torus, characters, Fourier coefficients, projection $P_N$ and spaces $H^r$ of [[def-gr-periodic-torus-and-sobolev-spaces]] and [[def-gr-galerkin-fourier-projection]].

[L1] Orthonormal basis and pairing: for $f,g\in L^2$ the coefficient map is a surjective isometry and $\langle f,g\rangle_2=\sum_k\widehat f(k)\cdot\overline{\widehat g(k)}$ with absolute convergence; the characters are real-paired through the reality condition $\widehat u(-k)=\overline{\widehat u(k)}$ for real-valued $u$.

[L2] Differentiation and norm equivalence: for $u\in H^r$ and $|\alpha|\le r$, $\widehat{D^\alpha u}(k)=(ik)^\alpha\widehat u(k)$, and $\|u\|_{H^r}^2=\sum_kq_k^r|\widehat u(k)|^2$ with $q_k^r:=\sum_{|\alpha|\le r}|k^\alpha|^2$ satisfying $4^{-r}(1+|k|^2)^r\le q^r_k\le(r+1)^3(1+|k|^2)^r$ ([[lem-gr-periodic-fourier-differentiation-and-norm-equivalence]]).

[L3] $\Lambda_N$ is finite and symmetric under $k\mapsto-k$ ([[def-gr-galerkin-fourier-projection]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 (Algebraic properties (a).) Linearity is immediate since coefficients are linear in $u$. For the pairing, [L1] and the finiteness of $\Lambda_N$ give $\langle P_Nu,v\rangle_2=\sum_{k\in\Lambda_N}\widehat u(k)\cdot\overline{\widehat v(k)}$ and $\langle u,P_Nv\rangle_2=\sum_{l\in\Lambda_N}\widehat u(l)\cdot\overline{\widehat v(l)}$ after reindexing $l\mapsto-l$ and using [L3]; the two sums are equal. Since the coefficients in $\Lambda_N$ are unchanged, $P_N^2=P_N$; the kernel and range descriptions are read off from the coefficient isometry. [L1, L3, algebra]

1.2 (Derivative commutation (b).) For $|\alpha|\le s$ and $u\in H^s$, both $D^\alpha u\in H^{s-|\alpha|}\subseteq L^2$ and $P_Nu\in X_N$ are defined; by [L2] the Fourier coefficient of $P_ND^\alpha u$ at $k$ is $(ik)^\alpha\widehat u(k)$ for $k\in\Lambda_N$ and $0$ otherwise, and that of $D^\alpha P_Nu$ is $(ik)^\alpha\widehat u(k)$ for $k\in\Lambda_N$ and $0$ otherwise, since $P_Nu$ is a finite trigonometric polynomial whose derivatives have the stated coefficients. Equality of coefficients gives (b). [L1, L2, algebra]

1.3 (Contraction (c) and convergence (d).) For $u\in H^r$, [L2] and the definition of $P_N$ give $\|P_Nu\|_{H^r}^2=\sum_{k\in\Lambda_N}q^r_k|\widehat u(k)|^2\le\sum_kq^r_k|\widehat u(k)|^2=\|u\|_{H^r}^2$, which is (c). Similarly $\|u-P_Nu\|_{H^r}^2=\sum_{k\notin\Lambda_N}q^r_k|\widehat u(k)|^2$ is the tail of the convergent series $\sum_kq^r_k|\widehat u(k)|^2$, hence tends to $0$ as $N\to\infty$ with $\Lambda_N$ increasing to $\mathbb Z^3$; this is (d). [L2, L3, algebra]

2.1 (Tail bound (e).) For $|k|>N$ and $|\alpha|\le s-1$, $|k^\alpha|^2\le(1+|k|^2)^{s-1}$, so $q^{s-1}_k\le(s+1)^3(1+|k|^2)^{s-1}\le(s+1)^32(1+N^2)^{-1}(1+|k|^2)^s$ because $(1+|k|^2)\ge1+N^2$; hence $\|u-P_Nu\|_{H^{s-1}}^2\le2(s+1)^3(1+N^2)^{-1}\sum_k(1+|k|^2)^s|\widehat u(k)|^2\le C_s^2(1+N^2)^{-1}\|u\|_{H^s}^2$ by the norm equivalence [L2]. Taking square roots gives (e). [L2, algebra] ∎

- **Use.** Clause (b) is used to differentiate the Galerkin equation in [[lem-gr-galerkin-energy-inequality]] and [[lem-gr-galerkin-time-derivative-bound]]; (c) makes $P_N$ an $L^2$-contraction in the weak-form argument of [[lem-gr-galerkin-limit-is-classical-solution]]; (e) is the compactness estimate of [[lem-gr-galerkin-compactness]].

## Definition

Let $(M,g,N)$ be a time-oriented smooth Lorentzian four-manifold with
signature $(-+++)$ and metric-selected connection $\nabla$. Fix mathematical
parameters $c>0$, $\kappa>0$ and $\Lambda\in\mathbb R$. On an open set
$U_0\subseteq M$, prescribe a smooth function $\rho\ge0$ and a smooth
future-directed unit timelike field $u$, with $g(u,u)=-1$. Set
$\varepsilon:=\rho c^2$ and $T^{ab}:=\varepsilon u^au^b$. On the open positive
subset $\{\rho>0\}$, the tuple $(\varepsilon,u)$, rather than $(\rho,u)$, is
exactly the dust model of [[def-gr-dust-matter-model]]: that supplier's density
is the coefficient of $u\otimes u$. Here $\rho$ denotes its mass-normalized
parameter and $\varepsilon$ its energy-normalized coefficient. On zero-density
regions the smooth unit field is prescribed separately; the geodesic equation
is imposed there as well and is not deduced from conservation of the zero
tensor. This is the smooth geodesic-dust extension, without a free boundary
or a division by density. Make the following
**reduction conventions**, all local to an open set $\mathcal U\subseteq U_0$:

**(a) Signature flip.** Put $\gamma:=-g$, a smooth metric of signature
$(+---)$ on $\mathcal U$, with inverse $\gamma^{-1}=-g^{-1}$; all indices in the
reduction are raised and lowered with $\gamma$
([[def-gr-inverse-metric-and-musical-isomorphisms]]). Put
$$U:=u,\qquad r:=\rho c^2 .$$
Then $\gamma(U,U)=1$. Whenever the mathematical model equation
$G(g)+\Lambda g=\kappa T$ holds, it reads
$G(\gamma)-\Lambda\gamma=\kappa\,r\,U^\flat_\gamma\otimes U^\flat_\gamma$ with
the same constant $\kappa$ and the Einstein tensor of $\gamma$
([[def-gr-curvature-tensor-and-derived-tensors]]). Indeed the constant metric
flip leaves the connection and mixed curvature unchanged, so Ricci is unchanged,
the scalar changes sign and $G(\gamma)=G(g)$; both lower factors in $T$
change sign together. The original metric is recovered by $g=-\gamma$.
Physical identification of these mathematical parameters is made by the
consuming physical model, not assumed by this definition.

**(b) Orthonormal frame.** Choose a smooth orthonormal frame
$(e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3)$ on $\mathcal U$ with $e_0=U$ and
$\gamma(e_i,e_j)=\eta_{ij}:=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)_{ij}$
([[def-gr-local-frame-and-tetrad-fields]], [[def-gr-observer-congruence-and-adapted-charts]]).
Its connection coefficients are defined by
$$\nabla_ie_j=\Gamma_i{}^k{}_j\,e_k,\qquad \Gamma_{ijk}:=\gamma_{kl}\Gamma_i{}^l{}_j=-\Gamma_{ikj},$$
and the frame components of $U$ are $U^i=\delta^i_0$.
The **spatial projection** onto the $\gamma$-orthogonal complement of $U$ and the tensor
$$h_{ij}:=\gamma_{ij}-U_iU_j,\qquad l_{ij}:=h_{ij}-U_iU_j$$
coincide with $\gamma$-orthogonal projection onto $\operatorname{span}(e_1,e_2,e_3)$
when restricted to spatial pairs.

**(c) Kinematics.** With $\nabla$ the metric connection of $\gamma$
([[def-gr-metric-selected-connection]],
[[thm-gr-nondegenerate-levi-civita-and-tensor-derivative]]) put
$$\chi_{ij}:=h_i{}^k\nabla_kU_j,\qquad \chi:=h^{ij}\chi_{ij},\qquad \chi^{ij}:=h^{ik}h^{jl}\chi_{kl},$$
and let $D$ denote fully projected covariant differentiation: for a spatial
tensor field $T$, $(DT)_{i\ldots}:=(h_i{}^j\cdots)\nabla_j(T_\ldots)$, so
$D_ih_{jk}=0$ and $D_i\epsilon_{jkl}=0$. Let $\epsilon_{ijkl}$ be the
$\gamma$-volume tensor with $\epsilon_{0123}=1$ and set
$\epsilon_{ijk}:=\epsilon_{0ijk}$ (so $\epsilon_{123}=1$).

**(d) Comoving-parallel gauge.** Assume the coordinates
$(x^0=s,x^1,x^2,x^3)$ on $\mathcal U$ are **comoving** ($e_0=\partial_s$ and $x^\alpha$
constant along the flow lines of $e_0$, $s$ a parameter on them) and
**parallel** ($\Gamma_0{}^i{}_j=0$, i.e. the spatial frame is parallelly
transported along $U$). This gauge is admissible for tilted configurations: the
initial surface $s=0$ is the given spacelike surface and need not be orthogonal
to $U$.

**(e) Unknowns.** The unknowns of the reduction are the coefficients
$$u_{\mathrm{red}}:=\bigl(e_a{}^\mu,\ \Gamma_a{}^i{}_j,\ E_{ij},\ B_{ij},\ r\bigr),\qquad a=1,2,3,$$
where $E$ and $B$ are the electric and magnetic parts of the Weyl curvature
defined in [[def-gr-dust-electric-magnetic-weyl-variables-and-schouten]]; the transport,
evolution and subsidiary equations they satisfy are stated and derived in
[[def-gr-dust-electric-magnetic-weyl-variables-and-schouten]],
[[lem-gr-dust-reduced-evolution-equations]],
[[lem-gr-dust-symmetric-hyperbolic-reduction]] and
[[thm-gr-dust-subsidiary-and-residual-vanishing]].

During the independent reduced evolution, the frame and the coefficients
$\Gamma_a{}^i{}_j$ are tentative smooth unknowns. The coefficients define a
metric-compatible connection by $\widehat\nabla_{e_a}e_j=\Gamma_a{}^i{}_j e_i$
and the tensor product rule; impose the same coefficient antisymmetry and
parallel gauge, but do not assume its torsion vanishes or that it is the
metric-selected connection. In reduction calculations $\nabla$ denotes this
tentative connection. The residual theorem establishes that it equals the
metric-selected connection for constrained solutions. The frame is required
to remain a basis, so it defines the tentative metric by its constant
orthonormal components.

## Statement

$\Sigma\subseteq M$ be a spacelike embedded hypersurface with induced metric

## Facts & Assumptions

[L1] On the slice, $g_{0i}=0$ makes its tangential derivatives zero. The Christoffel formula gives $g(\nabla_{\partial_i}\partial_s,\partial_j)=\tfrac12(\partial_i g_{sj}+\partial_s g_{ij}-\partial_j g_{is})=\tfrac12\partial_sg_{ij}=K_{ij}$. Together with $g_{00}=-1$ and the chosen time orientation, this identifies $\partial_s$ as the future unit normal and gives the stated second fundamental form ([[lem-gr-spacelike-hypersurface-normal-and-second-fundamental-form]], [[def-christoffel-symbols-of-an-affine-connection]]).

[L2] Christoffel symbols on $\Sigma$ in the coordinate frame $(\partial_s,\partial_i)$: $g^{00}=-1$, $g^{0i}=0$ and $g^{ij}=h^{ij}$ there, so $\Gamma^0{}_{00}=-\tfrac12\partial_sg_{00}=\operatorname{tr}_hK$, $\Gamma^0{}_{0i}=\tfrac12\partial_ig_{00}=0$, $\Gamma^0{}_{ij}=\tfrac12\partial_sg_{ij}=K_{ij}$, $\Gamma^i{}_{00}=0$ and $\Gamma^i{}_{jk}=\Gamma^h{}^i{}_{jk}$ on $\Sigma$ ([[def-christoffel-symbols-of-an-affine-connection]], [[def-gr-metric-selected-connection]]).

[L3] Background Christoffel symbols: $\bar g=-ds^2+h_0$ with $\partial_sh_0=0$ gives $\bar\Gamma^0{}_{00}=0$, $\bar\Gamma^0{}_{0i}=0$, $\bar\Gamma^0{}_{ij}=0$, $\bar\Gamma^i{}_{00}=0$ and $\bar\Gamma^i{}_{jk}=\Gamma^h{}^i{}_{jk}$ on $\Sigma$ (because $h_0|_\Sigma=h$ and the tangential derivatives of $g_{jk}$ and $h_{0,jk}$ agree on $\Sigma$) ([[def-christoffel-symbols-of-an-affine-connection]], [[def-c-r-and-smooth-maps-between-smooth-manifolds]]).

[L4] The gauge vector is $H^a=g^{bc}(\Gamma^a{}_{bc}-\bar\Gamma^a{}_{bc})$ ([[def-gr-harmonic-gauge-and-reduced-equations]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 (Existence of the metric germ and its slice geometry (a).) Prescribe a smooth metric on a product neighborhood whose value and $s$-derivative on $\Sigma$ are the displayed jet and whose higher jets are chosen arbitrarily smooth; such a metric germ exists because a smooth function and its first normal derivative can be prescribed on a hypersurface and extended smoothly. On $\Sigma$, $g_{00}=-1$, $g_{0i}=0$, $g_{ij}=h_{ij}$, so $g$ restricted to $T\Sigma$ is $h$ and $\partial_s$ is a unit timelike vector orthogonal to $\Sigma$; by [L1] it is the future unit normal (with the orientation of the statement) and the second fundamental form is $\tfrac12\partial_sg_{ij}=K_{ij}$. This is (a). [L1, algebra]

1.2 (Computation of $H^0$.) Insert the Christoffel components [L2], [L3] into [L4]: $H^0=g^{00}(\Gamma^0{}_{00}-\bar\Gamma^0{}_{00})+g^{ij}(\Gamma^0{}_{ij}-\bar\Gamma^0{}_{ij})+2g^{0i}(\Gamma^0{}_{0i}-\bar\Gamma^0{}_{0i})=-(\operatorname{tr}_hK-0)+h^{ij}(K_{ij}-0)+0=-\operatorname{tr}_hK+\operatorname{tr}_hK=0$ on $\Sigma$. [L2, L3, L4, algebra]

2.1 (Computation of $H^i$.) Similarly $H^i=g^{00}(\Gamma^i{}_{00}-\bar\Gamma^i{}_{00})+g^{jk}(\Gamma^i{}_{jk}-\bar\Gamma^i{}_{jk})+2g^{0j}(\Gamma^i{}_{0j}-\bar\Gamma^i{}_{0j})=-(0-0)+h^{jk}(\Gamma^h{}^i{}_{jk}-\Gamma^h{}^i{}_{jk})+2\cdot0=0$ on $\Sigma$, using that the tangential Christoffel symbols of $g$ on $\Sigma$ agree with those of $h$ and hence with those of the background because $h_0|_\Sigma=h$ and tangential derivatives agree. This is (b). [step 1.2, L2, L3, L4, algebra] ∎

## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$
([[def-countable-choice]]). Let $s\ge5$ be an integer, let $A^\mu,B$ be a periodic symmetric
hyperbolic coefficient family on the open set $O\subseteq\mathbb R^m$, let
$K\subseteq\operatorname{int}K'\subseteq O$ be compact, let $R\ge0$, and let
$T>0$ and $C$ be as in [[thm-gr-periodic-symmetric-hyperbolic-existence]] for
the data class $\mathcal S(K,R)$. Let $u_0\in C^\infty(\mathbb T^3_{2\pi};\mathbb R^m)$
take values in $K$ and satisfy $\|u_0\|_{H^s}\le R$. Then the solution $u\in L^\infty((-T,T);H^s)\cap C^0H^{s-1}$
of that theorem with $u(0)=u_0$ satisfies $u\in L^\infty((-T,T);H^q)$ and
$\partial_tu\in L^\infty((-T,T);H^{q-1})$ for every integer $q\ge s$, with bounds
depending only on $q$, $T$, $R$, the coefficient family, $K'$ and the $H^j$ bounds
of $u_0$ for $j\le q$; consequently $u\in C^\infty((-T,T)\times\mathbb T^3_{2\pi})$
and solves $(\star)$ classically.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice; integer $s\ge5$; coefficient family; compact $K\subseteq\operatorname{int}K'$; smooth datum $u_0\in\mathcal S(K,R)$; and the original common interval $(-T,T)$ from the existence construction.

[L1] That construction gives a cofinal Galerkin family with values in a fixed compact $Q\subseteq K'$ and uniform $H^s$ and $H^{s-1}$ time derivative bounds, converging along a subsequence to $u$ ([[thm-gr-periodic-symmetric-hyperbolic-existence]]).

[L2] Composition, homogeneous commutators and the lower-norm-controlled high refinement are proved in [[lem-gr-smooth-composition-and-commutator-estimates]]; matrix positivity gives energy equivalence ([[lem-gr-positive-definite-symmetric-matrix-bounds]]).

[L3] Projections contract in every $H^m$, and finite Fourier-sum limits preserve uniform higher bounds for the convergent solution and its derivative subsequence ([[lem-gr-galerkin-projection-properties]], [[lem-gr-galerkin-limit-is-classical-solution]]).

[L4] Gronwall controls both time directions when the absolute energy derivative is bounded ([[thm-gronwall-integral-inequality]]).

[L5] The Galerkin equation and its exact weighted energy identity are valid for smooth finite-dimensional solutions, and the lower-order time-derivative bound is uniform in $N$ ([[lem-gr-galerkin-ode-local-solution]], [[lem-gr-galerkin-time-derivative-bound]], [[lem-gr-galerkin-energy-inequality]]).

[L6] Sobolev embedding and uniqueness are established locally ([[lem-gr-periodic-sobolev-embedding]], [[thm-gr-periodic-symmetric-hyperbolic-uniqueness-stability]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** induction.

1.1 (Projected time derivative at one higher order.) Fix $n\ge6$ and a smooth Galerkin field $z$ with $\|z\|_{H^{n-1}}\le\rho$ and values in $Q$. Set $g=\partial_tz$. The lower-order time derivative lemma [L5], used at base order $n-1$, gives $\|g\|_{H^{n-2}}\le C_\rho$. For $|\alpha|\le n-1$ differentiate $P_N(A^0(z)g)=-P_N(A^i(z)\partial_i z+B(z))$ and pair with $D^\alpha g$. Coercivity gives $a\|D^\alpha g\|_2\le\|D^\alpha(A^i\partial_i z+B)\|_2+\|[D^\alpha,A^0]g\|_2$. In the source, coefficient derivatives of order at most $n-3$ are $L^\infty$ bounded by $\rho$, and the remaining derivative of $\partial_i z$ has order at most $n-1$, hence $L^2$ bound $\|z\|_{H^n}$. For coefficient derivatives of order $n-2$ or $n-1$, at most one derivative lands on $\partial_i z$, which is $L^\infty$ bounded by $H^{n-1}$ since $n\ge6$; coefficient derivatives have bounded $L^2$ norms. The commutator has coefficient derivatives at most $n-3$ times $L^2$ derivatives of $g$ of order at most $n-2$, or coefficient derivatives of order at least $n-2$ times at most one $L^\infty$ derivative of $g$, controlled by $H^{n-2}$. Thus $\|g\|_{H^{n-1}}\le C_\rho(1+\|z\|_{H^n})$. [L2, L5, algebra]

1.2 (Base approximation bounds.) Let $\rho_s$ be the uniform $H^s$ bound for the Galerkin family of [L1]. These approximations exist on the original $(-T,T)$ independently of their higher norms and have values in $Q$. At every higher order $m$, their initial norms are at most $\|u_0\|_{H^m}$ by projection contraction. [L1, L3, base]


1.3 (Induction hypothesis.) Fix $M\ge s$ and assume the same cofinal Galerkin family has a uniform $H^M$ bound $\rho_M$ on $(-T,T)$. [ih]

2.1 (Higher energy controlled by the lower norm.) Define $E_n=\sum_{|\alpha|\le n}\int(D^\alpha z)^{\mathsf T}A^0(z)D^\alpha z$. The energy identity of [L5] applies at order $n$: its derivation uses only smoothness, symmetry, projection commutation and integration by parts. The errors are commutators with $g$ and $\partial_i z$, the $H^n$ composition of $B$, and quadratic terms with $\dot A^0$ and $\partial_i A^i$. By [L2](e), step 1.1 and the lower $H^{n-2}$ bounds, each commutator and the $B$ term has $L^2$ norm at most $C_\rho(1+\|z\|_{H^n})$. Total coefficient derivatives have bounded $L^\infty$ norms by the lower bounds for $z,g$. Pairing with $D^\alpha z$, summing and using $2xy\le x^2+y^2$ gives $|E_n'|\le C_\rho(1+E_n)$ and $a\|z\|_{H^n}^2\le E_n\le b\|z\|_{H^n}^2$, uniformly in $N$. This establishes the high-order refinement before applying induction to the limit. [step 1.1, L2, L5, algebra]

3.1 (Gain one derivative.) Apply step 2.1 with $n=M+1\ge6$ and $\rho=\rho_M$. The approximations are smooth ODE solutions, so their order-$(M+1)$ energies exist before any higher-regularity assertion about the limit. Step 2.1 gives $|E_{M+1}'|\le C_M(1+E_{M+1})$ uniformly in $N$, with $E_{M+1}(0)\le b\|u_0\|_{H^{M+1}}^2$. Gronwall gives $1+E_{M+1}(t)\le(1+b\|u_0\|_{H^{M+1}}^2)e^{C_MT}$. Energy equivalence yields a uniform $H^{M+1}$ bound $\rho_{M+1}$ on the original whole interval. Step 1.1 also bounds $\partial_tu_N$ in $H^M$. This proves the step from $M$ to $M+1$. [step 1.3, step 1.1, step 2.1, L3, L4]

4.1 (Higher bounds for the same solution.) Induction supplies these approximation bounds at every order. Finite Fourier-sum limits along the original convergent solution subsequence and its convergent time-derivative subsequence give $u\in L^\infty H^m$ and $\partial_tu\in L^\infty H^{m-1}$ at every order, with the recursively obtained bounds. The time $T$ is unchanged. Uniqueness [L6] identifies any alternative construction with this $u$. [step 1.2, step 3.1, L3, L6, discharge-induction]

5.1 (Joint smoothness.) At every integer $q\ge0$, the uniform $H^q$ derivative bound and coefficientwise fundamental theorem give $\|u(t)-u(t')\|_{H^q}\le C_q|t-t'|$: apply the bound first to finite Fourier sums and take their supremum. Thus $u$ is continuous into every $H^q$. The PDE writes $\partial_tu=-(A^0(t,x,u))^{-1}(A^i(t,x,u)\partial_i u+B(t,x,u))$; smooth composition and products, using one extra spatial order, make this right side continuous in every $H^q$. Integrating the coefficientwise identity gives $u\in C^1((-T,T);H^q)$. Repeated differentiation of this smooth coefficient formula in time, with the chain and Leibniz rules and arbitrarily high spatial norms, inductively gives $u\in C^k((-T,T);H^q)$ for all $k,q$. Sobolev embedding makes all mixed spacetime derivatives continuous, proving joint smoothness. [step 4.1, L2, L3, L6, discharge-induction, algebra] ∎
