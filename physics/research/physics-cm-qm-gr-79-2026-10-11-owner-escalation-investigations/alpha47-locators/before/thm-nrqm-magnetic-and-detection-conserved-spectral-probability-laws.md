## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and the Axiom of Countable Choice
([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-countable-choice]]). Let $\mathcal H$ be a
nonzero complex Hilbert space ([[def-hilbert-space]]), let $K$ be a
self-adjoint operator on $\mathcal H$ with spectral projection-valued measure
$E=E_K$ on the Borel subsets of $\mathbb R$
([[thm-spectral-theorem-for-unbounded-self-adjoint-operators]],
[[def-projection-valued-measure]]), and put
$U(t):=e^{-itK/\hbar}=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-it\lambda/\hbar}\,dE(\lambda)$ for
$\hbar>0$, the strongly continuous unitary group generated by $-iK/\hbar$
([[lem-self-adjoint-operator-generates-a-strongly-continuous-unitary-group]],
[[def-unbounded-integral-against-a-pvm]]).

(a) If a bounded operator $B\in\mathcal B(\mathcal H)$ satisfies
$BE(C)=E(C)B$ for every Borel set $C\subseteq\mathbb R$, then
$BU(t)=U(t)B$ for every $t\in\mathbb R$.

(a') If a bounded self-adjoint operator $S$ satisfies $SK\subseteq KS$ — that
is $S(Kx)=K(Sx)$ for every $x\in D(K)$ — then $SU(t)=U(t)S$ for every $t$.

(b) Let $F$ be a projection-valued measure on a measurable space
$(Y,\Sigma)$ with $F(S)E(C)=E(C)F(S)$ for all $S\in\Sigma$ and all Borel
$C\subseteq\mathbb R$. Then:

(i) $F(S)U(t)=U(t)F(S)$ for all $t$ and $S$;

(ii) for every density operator $\rho$ on $\mathcal H$ — a self-adjoint
positive trace-class operator with $\operatorname{tr}\rho=1$
([[def-trace-class-operator]],
[[lem-nrqm-composites-density-and-scalar-spectral-probability]] clause (a)) —
the numbers $\operatorname{tr}(\rho F(S))$ are independent of $t$:
$$\operatorname{tr}\bigl(U(t)\rho U(t)^*F(S)\bigr)=\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\rho F(S)\bigr) \qquad(t\in\mathbb R,\ S\in\Sigma);$$

(iii) for every unit vector $\psi\in\mathcal H$ and every $S$,
$\|F(S)U(t)\psi\|^2=\|F(S)\psi\|^2$ for all $t$;

(c) applying (b) with $F=E$: for every density operator $\rho$ the energy law
$\operatorname{tr}(\rho E(C))$ is independent of $t$, for every unit $\psi$
the law $\|E(C)U(t)\psi\|^2=\|E(C)\psi\|^2$ is independent of $t$, and if
$\psi\in D(K)$ then both existing scalar moments are conserved:
$$\int_{\mathbb R}\lambda^k\,dE_{K,U(t)\psi}(\lambda) =\int_{\mathbb R}\lambda^k\,dE_{K,\psi}(\lambda),\qquad k=1,2,$$
with the integrals finite at $t=0$ and equal to $\langle K\psi,\psi\rangle$
and $\|K\psi\|^2$ respectively; moments that are infinite at $t=0$ remain
outside the assertion;

(d) if $K\psi=\lambda\psi$ for some $\lambda\in\mathbb R$ and unit
$\psi\in D(K)$, then $U(t)\psi=e^{-it\lambda/\hbar}\psi$ for every $t$, and
every $S\mapsto\|F(S)\psi\|^2$ of (b) agrees with
$S\mapsto\|F(S)U(t)\psi\|^2$.

No equilibration, relaxation or convergence of the law of an arbitrary state
is asserted, and no moment beyond those finite at $t=0$ is claimed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** a nonzero complex Hilbert space $\mathcal H$; a self-adjoint $K$ with spectral PVM $E$; $U(t)=e^{-itK/\hbar}$; in (b) a PVM $F$ commuting with $E$ and a density operator $\rho$.

[F1] For bounded Borel $h:\mathbb R\to\mathbb C$ the bounded integral $\Phi_E(h)=\int h\,dE$ satisfies $\|\Phi_E(h)x\|^2=\int|h|^2dE_x$, $\Phi_E(h_1h_2)=\Phi_E(h_1)\Phi_E(h_2)$, and $\Phi_E(h)$ is a norm limit of integrals of simple functions: there are simple Borel functions $s_n$ with $\|h-s_n\|_\infty\to0$ and $\|\Phi_E(h)-\int s_n\,dE\|\to0$ ([[thm-bounded-borel-pvm-integral]], [[thm-pvm-integral-is-a-star-homomorphism]]).

[F2] $U(t)=\Phi_E(f_t)$ with $f_t(\lambda)=e^{-it\lambda/\hbar}$, bounded with $|f_t|=1$; each $U(t)$ is unitary and $U(t)D(K)=D(K)$ ([[lem-self-adjoint-operator-generates-a-strongly-continuous-unitary-group]], [[def-unbounded-integral-against-a-pvm]]).

[F3] For a self-adjoint operator $T$ with PVM $E_T$ and $\psi\in D(T)$: $\|(T-\mu)\psi\|^2=\int(\lambda-\mu)^2dE_{T,\psi}(\lambda)$, $\int\lambda\,dE_{T,\psi}=\langle T\psi,\psi\rangle$ and $\int\lambda^2dE_{T,\psi}=\|T\psi\|^2$ ([[lem-unbounded-pvm-integral-is-well-defined-and-closed]], [[lem-nrqm-composites-density-and-scalar-spectral-probability]] clause (c)).

[F4] Trace cyclicity: $\operatorname{tr}(BT)=\operatorname{tr}(TB)$ for trace-class $T$ and bounded $B$; the trace-class operators form a two-sided operator ideal, so $U\rho U^*$ is trace class for a density operator $\rho$ ([[lem-nrqm-composites-nuclear-bounded-cyclicity]] clause (b), [[lem-nrqm-composites-rank-one-operator-calculus]]).

[F5] Measures on $\mathbb R$ are determined by their values on Borel sets; a positive measure with $\mu(\mathbb R\setminus\{\lambda\})=0$ is the point mass at $\lambda$ ([[lem-scalar-and-complex-measures-from-a-pvm]]).

## Proof

1.1 [Commutation passes to bounded Borel functions] Assume $B$ commutes with every $E(C)$, hence with every simple integral $\int s\,dE=\sum_kc_kE(B_k)$. Choose simple $s_n\to f_t$ uniformly by [F1]; then $\int s_n\,dE\to U(t)$ in operator norm, and $\|B\int s_n\,dE-\int s_n\,dE\,B\|\le\|B\|\,\|\int s_n\,dE-U(t)\|+\|\int s_n\,dE-U(t)\|\,\|B\|\to0$, while each commutator is zero; hence $BU(t)=U(t)B$. This is (a). [given, F1, F2, algebra]

1.2 [Resolvent commutation and the group] Assume $SK\subseteq KS$ for a bounded self-adjoint $S$. For $z\in\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb R$ the resolvent $R(z)=(K-z)^{-1}$ is a bounded operator with range $D(K)$, and $S(K-z)=(K-z)S$ on $D(K)$ implies $SR(z)=R(z)S$. For $t\in\mathbb R$ and $n\ge1$ put $W_n(t):=\bigl(1+\frac{it}{\hbar n}K\bigr)^{-1}$, which is defined and bounded because it equals $-\frac{i\hbar n}{t}\bigl(K-\frac{i\hbar n}{t}\bigr)^{-1}$ for $t\ne0$ (and $W_n(0)=I$), so $SW_n(t)=W_n(t)S$. By the spectral theorem, $\bigl(1+\frac{it\lambda}{\hbar n}\bigr)^{-n}\to e^{-it\lambda/\hbar}$ boundedly for every $\lambda$, so $W_n(t)^n\to U(t)$ strongly by dominated convergence in the spectral measures [F1]; since every $W_n(t)^n$ commutes with $S$, the strong limit $U(t)$ does, by $\|(SU(t)-U(t)S)x\|\le\|S\|\,\|(W_n^n-U(t))x\|+\|(W_n^n-U(t))Sx\|$. This is (a'). [given, F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 [Commuting sharp observable] Under the hypotheses of (b), each $F(S)$ is bounded and commutes with every $E(C)$, so step 1.1 with $B=F(S)$ gives $F(S)U(t)=U(t)F(S)$ for all $t,S$. This is (b)(i). [given, step 1.1]

3.1 [Invariant law of a density operator] Let $\rho$ be a density operator. Then $U(t)\rho U(t)^*$ is trace class by [F4], and by trace cyclicity twice, $\operatorname{tr}(U(t)\rho U(t)^*F(S))=\operatorname{tr}(\rho U(t)^*F(S)U(t))$. By step 2.1, $U(t)^*F(S)U(t)=U(t)^*U(t)F(S)=F(S)$; substituting gives $\operatorname{tr}(U(t)\rho U(t)^*F(S))=\operatorname{tr}(\rho F(S))$, independent of $t$. This is (b)(ii). [given, step 2.1, F4, algebra]

3.2 [Invariant law of a pure state] For unit $\psi$, $\|F(S)U(t)\psi\|^2=\|U(t)F(S)\psi\|^2$ by step 2.1, and this equals $\|F(S)\psi\|^2$ because $U(t)$ is unitary. This is (b)(iii). [given, step 2.1, F2, algebra]

4.1 [Energy law] Applying steps 3.1 and 3.2 with $F=E$ gives $\operatorname{tr}(U(t)\rho U(t)^*E(C))=\operatorname{tr}(\rho E(C))$ and $\|E(C)U(t)\psi\|^2=\|E(C)\psi\|^2$ for every Borel $C$. Moreover $E_{K,U(t)\psi}(C)=\|E(C)U(t)\psi\|^2=\|E(C)\psi\|^2=E_{K,\psi}(C)$ for every $C$, so the two scalar measures agree by [F5]. [given, step 3.1, step 3.2, F5]

5.1 [Moment conservation] Let $\psi\in D(K)$ and $u_0:=\psi$; by [F2], $U(t)\psi\in D(K)$ for every $t$. Since $E_{K,U(t)\psi}=E_{K,\psi}$ by step 4.1, the two integrals $\int\lambda^k\,dE_{K,U(t)\psi}$ and $\int\lambda^k\,dE_{K,\psi}$ coincide for $k=1,2$ (extended real values, both finite at $t=0$ by [F3]); by [F3] the time-zero values are $\langle K\psi,\psi\rangle$ and $\|K\psi\|^2$. If a moment is infinite at $t=0$, no equality of finite quantities is claimed. This proves (c). [given, step 4.1, F2, F3]

6.1 [Stationary eigenrays] Let $K\psi=\lambda\psi$ with $\|\psi\|=1$. By [F3], $\|(K-\lambda)\psi\|^2=\int(\mu-\lambda)^2dE_{K,\psi}(\mu)=0$, so the finite positive measure $E_{K,\psi}$ assigns mass zero to $\mathbb R\setminus\{\lambda\}$ and is the point mass at $\lambda$ by [F5]. Then $\|U(t)\psi-e^{-it\lambda/\hbar}\psi\|^2=\int|e^{-it\mu/\hbar}-e^{-it\lambda/\hbar}|^2dE_{K,\psi}(\mu)=0$ by [F1], so $U(t)\psi=e^{-it\lambda/\hbar}\psi$. The last assertion of (d) is step 3.2 with this $\psi$. [given, step 3.2, F1, F3, F5, algebra] ∎
