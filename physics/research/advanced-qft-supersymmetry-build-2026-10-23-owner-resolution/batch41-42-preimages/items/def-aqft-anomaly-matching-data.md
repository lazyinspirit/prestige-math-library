## Definition

Use natural units $c=\hbar=1$ and flat oriented Minkowski spacetime $M=\mathbb R^{1,3}$ with metric $\eta=\operatorname{diag}(-,+,+,+)$ and its standard spin structure. Fix Dirac matrices by $\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\}=2\eta^{\mu\nu}I$ and $\gamma_5=i\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$; set $P_+=(I+\gamma_5)/2$ and $P_-=I-P_+$. The global symmetry group is the fixed global form $H=SU(3)\times U(1)_V$, acting on a finite multiset $S$ of massless positive-chirality Weyl fields. Each field is a Grassmann-odd smooth compactly supported section of $\operatorname{im}(P_+)\otimes V_i$, where $V_i$ carries a specified finite-dimensional unitary representation $R_i$ of $H$. The chiral kinetic operator is $\mathcal D_{+,i}=P_-i\gamma^\mu D_{\mu,i}P_+$ from positive to negative chirality; its regulated determinant phase is a formal perturbative input. The matter field has mass dimension $3/2$; the background potentials have mass dimension one, their curvature two-forms dimension two, and all $U(1)_V$ charges and trace coefficients are dimensionless. The backgrounds are global one-forms on the trivial bundles over $M$, vary smoothly, and have compact support; the global symmetry is not integrated over as a dynamical gauge redundancy.

For each species, the background covariant derivative is $D_{\mu,i}=\partial_\mu+\rho_{i*}(A_\mu,iB_\mu)$ in its representation, where $A$ is an anti-Hermitian $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ connection, $B$ is a real $U(1)_V$ connection, and $\rho_{i*}:\mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus i\mathbb R\to\mathfrak u(V_i)$ is the derived Lie-algebra representation. Set $F_A=dA+A\wedge A$ and $f=dB$, and write $A_i:=\rho_{i*}(A,iB)$ and $F_i:=dA_i+A_i\wedge A_i$; thus $D_{\mu,i}=\partial_\mu+(A_i)_\mu$. For $E=(\mathbf3,+1)$, $A_E=A+iB I_3$ and $F_E=F_A+ifI_3$; for $E^*=(\bar{\mathbf3},-1)$, $A_{E^*}=-A^T-iB I_3$ and $F_{E^*}=-F_A^T-ifI_3$. Local transformations are $A\mapsto g^{-1}Ag+g^{-1}dg$ and $B\mapsto B+d\alpha$ for compactly supported $g: M\to SU(3)$ and $\alpha:M\to\mathbb R$; the $U(1)$ group element is $e^{i\alpha}$. Let $c$ be the ghost-number-one odd $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ ghost and $\eta$ the ghost-number-one odd real $U(1)_V$ ghost. The finite-jet BRST differential for $\mathfrak h=\mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus i\mathbb R$ is $sA=dc+[A,c]$, $sc=-[c,c]/2$, $sB=d\eta$, $s\eta=0$, and $s\psi_i=-\rho_{i*}(c,i\eta)\psi_i$; it acts on jets by commuting with total derivatives. The $U(1)_V$ factor is central, so these are the direct-sum Lie-algebra BRST formulas.

The anomaly-matching data include one common Pauli–Villars subtraction prescription for the chiral triangle graphs, with opposite-statistics regulator fields and regulator masses taken to the large-mass limit, together with the consistent effective-action variation convention and compact-support boundary conditions. The local anomaly complex is the finite-jet background subalgebra generated by $A,B$ and their BRST ghosts, with no matter-field jets, so its ghost-number-zero four-forms are precisely the admitted local counterterms. We quotient by all such finite-jet local counterterms and by compactly supported total derivatives; no background-gauge-invariance condition is imposed on an individual counterterm. In particular, for real dimensionless $\lambda$, the four-form $C_4=\lambda B\wedge K_3(A)$ is admitted, where $K_3(A):=\operatorname{tr}(A\wedge dA+\tfrac23A\wedge A\wedge A)$. As an explicit source-backed one-loop input, after the regulator masses are taken to infinity this selected prescription assigns to each positive-chirality species $R_i$ the consistent local anomaly obtained by descent from $\mathcal I_6(R_i,A_i)$ of the preceding cubic-polynomial definition. The fixed linear descent map is normalized by $\mathcal I_6=dI^{(0)}_5$, $sI^{(0)}_5+d_HI^{(1)}_4=0$, and $sI^{(1)}_4+d_HI^{(2)}_3=0$, with $\delta\Gamma=\sum_{i\in S}\int_M I^{(1)}_4(R_i,A_i)$. This determinant law is an adopted regulator input in the data, not a locally derived theorem about every regulator. The total polynomial is $\mathcal I_6^{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_{i\in S}\mathcal I_6(R_i,A_i)$, and its local anomaly class is interpreted using the preceding local ’t Hooft-anomaly definition. No large-transformation test, torsion calculation, mixed gravitational term, dynamical gauge quantization, or continuum-existence result is included.
