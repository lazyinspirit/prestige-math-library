## Statement

Work in $\mathrm{ZF}+\mathrm{AC}$. Let $T$ be a compact torus acting smoothly on a compact oriented smooth $n$-manifold $M$ without boundary; use the standard Hausdorff, second-countable manifold convention, and assume the action preserves the orientation. Write $F=M^T=\coprod_{j=1}^{s}F_j$ for its fixed locus, with $s<\infty$, and $i_j:F_j\hookrightarrow M$ for each connected component. Give each normal bundle $\nu_j$ a $T$-equivariant orientation and orient $F_j$ by $o(TM|_{F_j})=o(TF_j)\wedge o(\nu_j)$. Use real Borel equivariant cohomology, $\mathcal R=H^*(BT;\mathbb R)$, the multiplicative set $S$ generated by nonzero character classes, and the degree-shifting fiber-integration convention of the preceding definition. For every homogeneous $\alpha\in H_T^k(M;\mathbb R)$,

$$\int_M\alpha=\sum_j\int_{F_j}\frac{i_j^*\alpha}{e_T(\nu_j)}\quad\text{in }S^{-1}H^{k-n}(BT;\mathbb R).$$

If the fixed locus is isolated, use the ambient orientation on each normal space $\nu_p=T_pM$ and the positive orientation on each point $p$. Necessarily $n=2m$. Choose a $T$-invariant complex structure on each $T_pM$ and let $\chi_{p,1},\ldots,\chi_{p,m}$ be its nonzero character weights. Let $\epsilon_p=+1$ when its complex orientation agrees with the orientation of $M$ at $p$ and $-1$ otherwise. Then the same formula specializes to

$$\int_M\alpha=\sum_{p\in M^T}\frac{\epsilon_p\,i_p^*\alpha}{\prod_{a=1}^{m}u_{\chi_{p,a}}}\quad\text{in }S^{-1}\mathcal R.$$

This is a finite-dimensional formula for compact manifolds; it does not assert a formula for arbitrary noncompact instanton spaces or infinite-dimensional field spaces.

## Proof

1.1 Localize the restriction map $i^*:H_T^*(M;\mathbb R)\to H_T^*(F;\mathbb R)$ at $S$. By [L2] it is an isomorphism in this compact smooth finite-type case. Let $\beta=i^*\alpha=(\beta_j)_j$ under $H_T^*(F)=\bigoplus_jH_T^*(F_j)$. [given, A1, L2]

2.1 By [F3], each $e_T(\nu_j)$ is invertible after localization. Form $\gamma=\sum_j i_{j*}^T(\beta_j/e_T(\nu_j))\in S^{-1}H_T^*(M;\mathbb R)$; the sum is finite because $F$ has finitely many components. [given, A1, F3, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 All maps and identities in [F2] are $\mathcal R$-linear and extend $S^{-1}\mathcal R$-linearly to the localized modules. For a component $F_\ell$, pull back $\gamma$. The $j=\ell$ term is $e_T(\nu_\ell)\,\beta_\ell/e_T(\nu_\ell)=\beta_\ell$ by the localized self-intersection identity; each $j\ne\ell$ term restricts to zero by the localized disjoint-support identity. Hence $i^*\gamma=\beta=i^*\alpha$. Since the localized restriction map in step 1.1 is injective, $\gamma=\alpha$. [given, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The $\mathcal R$-linear pushforwards in [F1] and the identity $\int_M i_{j*}^T(\delta)=\int_{F_j}\delta$ in [F2] extend $S^{-1}\mathcal R$-linearly, so they apply to $\delta=\beta_j/e_T(\nu_j)$. Applying $\int_M$ to step 3.1 gives $\int_M\alpha=\sum_j\int_Mi_{j*}^T(\beta_j/e_T(\nu_j))=\sum_j\int_{F_j}\beta_j/e_T(\nu_j)$, which is the fixed-component formula in the localized coefficient ring. If $F=\varnothing$, [L2] says $S^{-1}H_T^*(M)=0$, so $\int_M\alpha$ localizes to zero; because $\mathcal R$ is a domain and every element of $S$ is nonzero, $\mathcal R\hookrightarrow S^{-1}\mathcal R$ is injective and the empty sum is indeed zero. If a normal rank is zero, its Euler denominator is $1$ by [F3], so that term is the ordinary integral over that fixed component. [given, A1, F1, F2, F3, L2, step 3.1]

5.1 If each fixed component is a point $p$, then $\nu_p=T_pM$ has no trivial weight and hence $n=2m$. With the stated ambient normal and positive point orientations, its complex character-line decomposition has Euler class $e_T(T_pM)=\epsilon_p\prod_{a=1}^m u_{\chi_{p,a}}$ by [F1]. Substituting into step 4.1 and using $\epsilon_p^{-1}=\epsilon_p$ yields the isolated-fixed-point formula. If $n=0$, the product is empty and the same calculation reads as the sum of the oriented point evaluations. [given, A1, F1, F3, step 4.1] ∎
