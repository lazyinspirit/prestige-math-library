## Statement

For $n=2$ or $3$, let $X=\mathbb P^n_{\mathbb C}$ and let $X^{an}$ be its complex analytic space. Define analytification by $\mathcal F^{an}=\mathcal F\otimes_{\mathcal O_X}\mathcal O_{X^{an}}$. It is an exact tensor-compatible equivalence from algebraic coherent sheaves (locally finitely presented $\mathcal O_X$-modules) to holomorphic coherent sheaves. It identifies algebraic and holomorphic morphisms, preserves cohomology and the derived morphism groups $\operatorname{Ext}^q$, and commutes with restriction to the fixed hyperplane $L_\infty=\{z_0=0\}$. For an algebraic coherent sheaf $\mathcal E$ flat over a finite-type complex parameter scheme $S$, let $p:X\times S\to S$ be projection. Analytification commutes with the algebraic family structure and fiber restrictions. Whenever the algebraic and analytic higher direct images $R^qp_*\mathcal E$ are locally free and the natural maps from their residue-field fibers to $H^q(X_s,\mathcal E_s)$ are isomorphisms, the comparison identifies those direct images and base-change maps. No assertion is made that an arbitrary holomorphic family over a nonproper algebraic base algebraizes.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $n\in\{2,3\}$, the algebraic projective space $X=\mathbb P^n_{\mathbb C}$, and its analytic space $X^{an}$.

[A1] For this conditional derivation, assume the projective analytic comparison package: local algebraic-to-holomorphic local-ring maps are flat and form flat pairs; the analytic structure sheaf is coherent, coherent sheaves admit arbitrarily long finite free resolutions after shrinking, and coherent analytic sheaves are acyclic on sufficiently small coordinate polydiscs; coherent analytic cohomology on compact projective space is finite-dimensional; sufficiently positive projective twists have vanishing higher cohomology and are generated by finitely many global sections as in Serre's §16; and on each $\mathbb P^r$ for $0\le r\le3$, analytic $H^0(\mathcal O)=\mathbb C$ and $H^q(\mathcal O)=0$ for $q>0$, with the natural scalar-section identification. Projective space itself is not Stein: Cartan A/B are not asserted for arbitrary coherent sheaves there. These are the analytic inputs in Serre's proof, not consequences of the five listed algebraic suppliers. No current local carrier for this package was found.

[L2] The family clause retains exactly its stated locally-free and fiber-base-change hypotheses. Its comparison morphism and compatibility are constructed below rather than assumed as an additional relative-GAGA package.

[F1] Full AC is available through the finite-resolution and projective-cohomology suppliers. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F2] Every coherent algebraic sheaf on projective space has a finite resolution by finite sums of twisting line bundles. [[lem-coherent-sheaf-finite-twisted-locally-free-resolution-projective-space]]

[F3] The algebraic cohomology of each twisting sheaf on $\mathbb P^n$ is finite-dimensional and has the standard projective-space values; sufficiently positive twists of a coherent sheaf have no higher cohomology. [[thm-cohomology-projective-space-twisting-sheaves]], [[thm-serre-vanishing]]

[F4] In a morphism of long exact sequences, if the four neighboring vertical maps are isomorphisms, then the middle vertical map is an isomorphism. [[thm-five-lemma-for-a-morphism-of-long-exact-sequences]]

[F5] Under AC, ringed-space module sheaves have enough injectives; their injective resolutions are flasque, flasque restrictions are cohomologically acyclic, and the acyclic-resolution theorem applies under DC, which follows from AC. Cohomology sheaves are sheafifications of presheaf cohomology. [[lem-ringed-space-module-sheaves-enough-injectives]], [[thm-flasque-sheaves-acyclic]], [[thm-acyclic-resolution-theorem-for-right-derived-functors]], [[lem-image-sheaf-is-sheafification-presheaf-image]], [[thm-sheafification-universal-property]], [[def-higher-direct-image-sheaf]], [[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]]

[F6] Pullback of sheaf cohomology classes along a continuous map and a compatible sheaf morphism is natural and respects compositions. [[lem-cohomology-functoriality-sheaf-and-space]]

## Proof

**Given:** The statement and [A1] for the conditional proof below.

1.1 Flatness of the local analytic-ring extension in [A1] makes analytification exact: tensoring an exact coherent stalk sequence with the holomorphic local ring preserves its kernels and cokernels. It also gives $(\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal G)^{an}\cong\mathcal F^{an}\otimes\mathcal G^{an}$ by associativity of stalkwise tensor product. Restriction to $L_\infty$ is the same stalkwise tensor construction along its local defining ideal, so analytification commutes with that restriction. [A1, algebra]

2.1 For $\mathcal O_X$, both zeroth cohomology groups are $\mathbb C$ and higher algebraic cohomology vanishes by [F3]; higher analytic cohomology vanishes by the projective structure-sheaf clause of [A1]. For every integer $m$, the hyperplane sequence $0\to\mathcal O_X(m-1)\to\mathcal O_X(m)\to\mathcal O_{L}(m)\to0$ is exact on both sides by step 1.1. Induction on dimension for $L\cong\mathbb P^{n-1}$, followed by the long exact sequences and [F4], propagates the algebraic-to-analytic cohomology isomorphism from $m=0$ in both directions to every integer $m$. This proves comparison for every twisting line bundle. [A1, F3, F4, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Resolve a coherent algebraic $\mathcal F$ by the finite line-bundle resolution of [F2]. AC is retained through this finite-resolution supplier [F1]. By step 1.1 its analytification remains exact. Apply the cohomology comparison from step 2.1 to every term. Starting at the leftmost nonzero resolution term and using the long exact cohomology sequences, induction on the finite resolution length proves $H^q(X,\mathcal F)\cong H^q(X^{an},\mathcal F^{an})$ for every $q$. Thus the analytification functor is fully cohomology-compatible on coherent objects. [A1, F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, induction]

4.1 For coherent algebraic $\mathcal F,\mathcal G$, the sheaf $\mathcal H om(\mathcal F,\mathcal G)$ is coherent. Flatness in [A1] identifies its analytification with $\mathcal H om(\mathcal F^{an},\mathcal G^{an})$ stalkwise. Step 3.1 in degree zero therefore identifies algebraic and holomorphic morphisms. A finite locally free resolution of $\mathcal F$ computes $\operatorname{Ext}^q(\mathcal F,\mathcal G)$ by the hypercohomology of its Hom complex; exact analytification and termwise cohomology comparison identify the two finite Ext groups. [A1, F2, step 1.1, step 3.1]

5.1 To see essential surjectivity, let $\mathcal M$ be holomorphic coherent. The uniform positive-twist generation assertion included in [A1] supplies one integer $a$ and finitely many global generators of $\mathcal M(a)$, hence a surjection $\mathcal O(-a)^r\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal M$. Its kernel is coherent. Applying the same assertion to the kernel gives a surjection from a finite sum of twisting sheaves; composing it with the kernel inclusion yields a finite presentation by twisting sheaves. The matrix entries are holomorphic sections of line bundles; step 2.1 identifies each such section with an algebraic section, so the presentation matrix is algebraic. Its algebraic cokernel $\mathcal F$ analytifies to $\mathcal M$ by exactness in step 1.1. If two algebraic sheaves analytify to isomorphic holomorphic sheaves, step 4.1 algebraizes that isomorphism and its inverse; their composites remain identities by faithfulness. This proves essential surjectivity and uniqueness up to algebraic isomorphism. [A1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 To complete the family comparison after the absolute equivalence of step 5.1, first establish the local-section formula: for any morphism of ringed spaces $f:Y\to B$ and module sheaf $M$, take the module-injective resolution from [F5]. Each term is flasque, its restriction to $f^{-1}U$ is flasque for every open $U\subset B$, and hence its sections there compute $H^q(f^{-1}U,M)$ by the acyclic-resolution theorem. The cohomology sheaf of $f_*I^\bullet$ is the sheafification of its presheaf cohomology: kernels are computed on opens, image sheaves are the sheafifications of presheaf images, and the assertion follows on stalks by exactness of filtered colimits. Thus $R^qf_*M=(U\mapsto H^q(f^{-1}U,M))^\#$, compatibly with restrictions and scalar multiplication. This argument covers analytic ringed spaces as well as schemes. [F1, F5, step 5.1, construct]

7.1 Write $Y=X\times S$ and let $\eta_Y:Y^{an}\to Y$, $\eta_S:S^{an}\to S$ be analytification maps. For every algebraic open $U\subset S$, [F6] gives $H^q(p^{-1}U,\mathcal E)\to H^q((p^{an})^{-1}\eta_S^{-1}U,\mathcal E^{an})$. Composing with the presheaf-to-sheaf map of step 6.1, then sheafifying on $S$, gives the $\mathcal O_S$-linear map $R^qp_*\mathcal E\to\eta_{S*}R^qp^{an}_*\mathcal E^{an}$. Pull back and tensor with $\mathcal O_{S^{an}}$ to obtain $c_q:(R^qp_*\mathcal E)^{an}\to R^qp^{an}_*\mathcal E^{an}$; explicitly $a\otimes[\xi]$ maps to $a$ times the analytically pulled-back germ of $\xi$. Tensor balancing proves well-definedness. This constructs an actual comparison morphism without assuming its invertibility or a relative-GAGA theorem. [F5, F6, step 6.1, construct]

8.1 At $t\in S^{an}$ over the closed complex point $s\in S$, tensor associativity gives $(M^{an})(t)=M(s)$ for every algebraic module sheaf $M$, and $\mathcal E^{an}|_{X^{an}\times\{t\}}=(\mathcal E_s)^{an}$. These are underived restrictions, valid also for singular or nonreduced bases. The square with top map $c_q(t)$, vertical maps the assumed algebraic and analytic fiber-base-change maps, and bottom map $H^q(X,\mathcal E_s)\to H^q(X^{an},(\mathcal E_s)^{an})$ commutes: represent a stalk by a class on $p^{-1}U$ using step 6.1; both paths pull back that class along the same map from the analytic fiber, with identical tensor restriction of coefficients, so [F6] identifies them. The bottom map is an isomorphism by step 3.1, and both vertical maps are isomorphisms by the Statement's explicit hypotheses. Hence $c_q(t)$ is an isomorphism. Both sheaves compared by $c_q$ are finite locally free by those same hypotheses. Locally their ranks agree, and its matrix has determinant with nonzero residue at every $t$, hence a unit; the adjugate gives a local inverse. Therefore $c_q$ is an isomorphism. [A1, F6, step 3.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 For an algebraic finite-type base map $S'\to S$, apply the same class-pullback construction to the pulled-back family. Tensor associativity identifies its analytification with the analytic pullback family. The square of the two comparison morphisms and the algebraic and analytic base-change maps commutes, since all maps pull back the same local cohomology classes and [F6] respects compositions. Whenever both families satisfy the Statement's locally-free and fiber-base-change hypotheses, step 8.1 makes their comparison morphisms invertible, so it identifies their base-change maps. This proves the complete stated family range, including the closed-fiber maps, without asserting arbitrary analytic-family algebraization. Full AC is inherited through [F1] and the resolution construction [F5]. The unresolved absolute analytic package [A1] remains necessary to step 3.1; nothing here closes that separate gap. [A1, F1, F5, F6, step 3.1, step 7.1, step 8.1, algebra] ∎
