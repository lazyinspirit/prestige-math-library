## Supplier arguments

**Complete reducibility and Reynolds operator.** Steps 1.1–2.1 obtain complex Lie charts from smoothness and the Jacobian/holomorphic interfaces, and show the subgroup generated by exponentials is Zariski dense: its closure has full tangent dimension, and a smooth connected algebraic group is irreducible. Thus Lie-invariant subspaces are group-invariant by their algebraic stabilizers. In steps 2.2–3.1 the finite set of radical weights is fixed by connected conjugation, so `[g,rad(g)]` is strictly upper triangular. The closure generated by its polynomial exponential curves is connected, normal and unipotent in the exact fixed-vector definition; reductivity kills it. Levi then gives the central radical plus a semisimple summand.

Step 5.1 does not assume that Jordan components already lie in the group Lie algebra: exponential-polynomial independence puts the exponential of the nilpotent component in the central Zariski closure. A nonzero component would give a closed normal additive unipotent subgroup. Hence the centre acts semisimply in the faithful embedding. Step 6.1 uses characters of the closed diagonal subgroup and the coaction to split arbitrary rational modules into its weights; Weyl splits each semisimple Lie-module weight space. Density lifts complements to the connected group. Step 7.1 justifies reductivity of the identity component by finite conjugates and successive fixed vectors, then averages a projection over the finite component group. Steps 8.1–9.1 supply arbitrary rational-module semisimplicity, the canonical trivial-isotypic projection, naturality, exactness on invariants, invariant scalar linearity and the recorded converses. The sum of nontrivial simple summands is the unique stable complement because equivariant maps from nontrivial simples to trivial modules vanish.

Clause (iv), step 9.2, is explicitly conditional on a **given compact Zariski-dense subgroup**. Haar averaging, density and uniqueness give that same projection; the finite-dimensional convex-hull argument is valid. Compact existence is neither claimed nor used to establish clauses (i)–(iii). Consequently the unread compact-existence theorem is not a missing bridge here. No defect was found.
