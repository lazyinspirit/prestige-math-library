## Conventions and elementary cone facts

All spaces and homotopies are based. Spheres have their usual finite CW structures with a chosen zero-cell basepoint. We use compactly generated based products/quotients; for these finite compact CW spaces the displayed ordinary quotients give the same spaces. The wedge is the union identifying the basepoints.

For a based map $f:S^q\to S^p$, let

$$
C_f=S^p\cup_f D^{q+1}
$$

mean the ordinary cell attachment, oriented by the supplied characteristic disk. It has cells in dimensions $0,p,q+1$. We also use the reduced cone model $S^p\cup_f CS^q$. The ordinary cone has a cone vertex and a basepoint track joining it to the target basepoint. With the standard cone CW structure that track is a contractible subcomplex. Collapsing it gives the reduced cone and is a homotopy equivalence relative the target basepoint by the imported CW-collapse lemma. Thus the models have identical cohomology and operations, respecting the target inclusion and relative top-cell generator.

Reduced suspension commutes with reduced mapping cones:

$$
\Sigma(C_f^{\mathrm{red}})\cong C_{\Sigma f}^{\mathrm{red}}.
$$

For completeness, both sides are the quotient of the disjoint union of $I\times S^p$ and $I\times S^q\times I$. They impose the same identifications: collapse the suspension ends and all basepoint tracks, collapse each cone top, and identify each cone base by $(s,x,0)\sim(s,f(x))$. The identity of these quotient relations induces continuous inverse maps. Interval products preserve these quotient identifications by the library's quotient-homotopy/interval-exponential argument. After the preceding ordinary-to-reduced collapses, this gives natural homotopy equivalences $C_{\Sigma f}\simeq\Sigma C_f$.

Mapping cones preserve a specified homotopy of attaching maps, with degree $+1$ on the relative top cell. Explicitly, if $H(x,s)$ is a based homotopy from $f_0$ to $f_1$, define $T:C_{f_0}\to C_{f_1}$ to be the identity on the target. On the cone parameter $t$, with $t=0$ the base and $t=1$ the apex, put $T([x,t])=H(x,2t)$ for $t\le1/2$, and $T([x,t])=[x,2t-1]$ for $t\ge1/2$. The two formulas agree at $t=1/2$, because $H(x,1)=f_1(x)$. Reverse $H$ for the inverse. The composites insert $H$ followed by its reverse in a collar. Contract that pair of paths by replacing its triangular path parameter $\min(2s,2-2s)$ by $(1-u)\min(2s,2-2s)$, then remove the constant collar by a linear reparametrization. These are based homotopies relative the target. On the quotient top sphere, $T$ collapses a collar and radially stretches the remaining cone; it has degree $+1$. This proves the homotopy invariance of the oriented Hopf coefficient used below without presuming a bare mapping-cone functor on the homotopy category.

## 1. The Hopf map generates $\pi_3(S^2)$ and has invariant one

Identify $S^2$ with $\mathbb{CP}^1$ and define

$$
h:S^3\subset\mathbb C^2\longrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^1,\qquad h(z_0,z_1)=[z_0:z_1].
$$

Take basepoints $(1,0)$ and $[1:0]$. The identification with the geometric sphere can be made by

$$
[z_0:z_1]\longmapsto\frac{(2\operatorname{Re}(z_0\overline z_1),\,2\operatorname{Im}(z_0\overline z_1),\,|z_0|^2-|z_1|^2)}{|z_0|^2+|z_1|^2}.
$$

It is well defined, continuous, and bijective: the two affine projective charts give the usual stereographic inverse formulas. Compactness and Hausdorffness then make it a homeomorphism. All group identifications below can instead remain in the projective model.

On $U_0=\{z_0\ne0\}$, with affine coordinate $w=z_1/z_0$, a section of $h$ is $(1,w)/\sqrt{1+|w|^2}$. On $U_1$ use $(w,1)/\sqrt{1+|w|^2}$. Every vector over a projective point is uniquely the section times a common scalar $\lambda\in S^1$. The scalar is $z_i/|z_i|$ in the corresponding chart. These formulas prove local triviality with fibre $S^1$.

For an explicit finite numeration set $a_i([z])=|z_i|^2/(|z_0|^2+|z_1|^2)$ and $b_i=\max(0,a_i-1/4)$, and put $\rho_i=b_i/(b_0+b_1)$. Since $a_0+a_1=1$, at least one $a_i\ge1/2$, so the denominator is positive. Each closed support lies in $\{a_i\ge1/4\}\subset U_i$. Thus the bundle is numerable with exactly the closed-support condition required by the imported theorem. It is a Hurewicz, hence Serre, fibration under AC.

The real-line covering of $S^1$ is also Hurewicz by finite local-strip lifting. Its total space is contractible and its fibre $\mathbb Z$ is discrete. A based map from a connected positive-dimensional sphere into a discrete space is constant, so the covering LES gives $\pi_j(S^1)=0$ for $j\ge2$. Consequently the Hopf LES contains

$$
0=\pi_3(S^1)\longrightarrow\pi_3(S^3)\xrightarrow{h_*}\pi_3(S^2)\longrightarrow\pi_2(S^1)=0.
$$

Hence $h_*$ is an isomorphism. Degree gives $\pi_3(S^3)=\mathbb Z[\operatorname{id}]$, so

$$
\pi_3(S^2)=\mathbb Z[h].
$$

Now identify $C_h$ with $\mathbb{CP}^2$. Its characteristic map is

$$
D^4=\{w\in\mathbb C^2:\|w\|\le1\}\longrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^2,\qquad w\longmapsto[w_0:w_1:\sqrt{1-\|w\|^2}].
$$

On the boundary this is precisely $h$ into the last-coordinate-zero projective line. On the interior it is a homeomorphism onto the complement of that line: an affine coordinate $v\in\mathbb C^2$ has inverse $w=v/\sqrt{1+\|v\|^2}$. The resulting continuous bijection from the compact attachment quotient to Hausdorff projective space is a homeomorphism. The integral cohomology supplier gives a generator $u\in H^2(C_h;\mathbb Z)$ with $u^2$ generating $H^4(C_h;\mathbb Z)$. Orient the top cell so that this coefficient is $+1$.

For any $f:S^3\to S^2$, let $x_f\in H^2(C_f;\mathbb Z)$ restrict to the same oriented sphere generator, and let $y_f\in H^4(C_f;\mathbb Z)$ be dual to the oriented top cell. Define $H(f)\in\mathbb Z$ by $x_f^2=H(f)y_f$. Cellular homology and the UCT show both groups are $\mathbb Z$ with these generators. The explicit cone maps above show this integer is invariant under based homotopy of $f$. We have $H(h)=1$.

For a based degree-$m$ map $d_m:S^3\to S^3$, the map of cones $C_{h\circ d_m}\to C_h$ is the identity on $S^2$ and the cone on $d_m$ on the attached cone. It sends the relative top class by degree $m$: after collapsing the target sphere the quotient map is $\Sigma d_m$, whose degree is $m$ by naturality of the homology suspension isomorphism. Therefore pullback sends $x_h$ to $x_{h\circ d_m}$ and $y_h$ to $m y_{h\circ d_m}$. Pull back $x_h^2=y_h$ to obtain

$$
H(h\circ d_m)=m.
$$

Every element of $\pi_3(S^2)$ is represented by such a composite, because $h_*$ is an isomorphism and degree classifies $d_m$. Thus this Hopf coefficient is an isomorphism on $\pi_3(S^2)$, with $H([h])=1$. In particular an attachment with Hopf coefficient $2$ represents $2[h]$.

## 2. A folded product attachment is $2h$ and suspends to zero

Let $P=S^2\times S^2$ and $W=(S^2\times\{*\})\cup(\{*\}\times S^2)=S^2\vee S^2$. Using $S^2=D^2/\partial D^2$, the product characteristic map $D^2\times D^2\to P$ carries its boundary into $W$ and gives

$$
P=W\cup_\alpha D^4,\qquad \alpha:S^3=\partial(D^2\times D^2)\longrightarrow W.
$$

A radial homeomorphism between $D^4$ and the convex product disk identifies their boundaries; orient it to preserve the product orientation. Choose the sphere basepoint at a corner mapping to $(*,*)$. Let $\nabla:W\to S^2$ restrict to the identity on each summand, and set $\beta=\nabla\circ\alpha$.

There is a cellular map $Q:P\to C_\beta$ equal to $\nabla$ on $W$ and the same oriented characteristic disk on the top cell. It exists because the boundary equality is exactly $\beta=\nabla\alpha$. Let $A,B\in H^2(P;\mathbb Z)$ be the two projection pullbacks. The actual Künneth ring statement gives $A^2=B^2=0$ and $AB=BA$ generating degree four. The map $Q$ pulls $x_\beta$ back to $A+B$, since its restriction to both sphere summands has degree one. It pulls $y_\beta$ back to $AB$ because its relative top-cell degree is one. Naturality of cup product therefore gives

$$
H(\beta)AB=Q^*(x_\beta^2)=(A+B)^2=2AB.
$$

Since $H^4(P;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[AB]$, $H(\beta)=2$, so $[\beta]=2[h]$ by §1.

It remains to prove $\Sigma\beta$ is null, rather than simply asserting that suspension kills a Whitehead product. Write $i:W\hookrightarrow P$. On reduced suspensions define

$$
r:\Sigma P\longrightarrow\Sigma S^2\vee\Sigma S^2
$$

by $r([p,t])=[\operatorname{pr}_1p,2t]$ in the first summand for $t\le1/2$, and $r([p,t])=[\operatorname{pr}_2p,2t-1]$ in the second for $t\ge1/2$. Both values at $t=1/2$ are the reduced-suspension basepoint. The endpoint and based-track relations are respected, so quotient descent and pasting make $r$ continuous and based.

Under $\Sigma W=\Sigma S^2\vee\Sigma S^2$, the composite $r\Sigma i$ restricts on the first summand to the height reparametrization $t\mapsto\min(2t,1)$ and on the second to $t\mapsto\max(2t-1,0)$. Each is based homotopic to $t\mapsto t$ by their linear interpolation. The two homotopies agree at the wedge basepoint; hence $r\Sigma i\simeq\operatorname{id}_{\Sigma W}$.

The map $i\alpha:S^3\to P$ extends across the characteristic disk, and is based nullhomotopic. One explicit nullhomotopy restricts the disk map to a linear contraction of the disk toward its chosen boundary basepoint; that point remains fixed. Suspension preserves this based nullhomotopy. Apply $r$ and use $r\Sigma i\simeq\operatorname{id}$ to conclude $\Sigma\alpha\simeq *$. Postcompose with $\Sigma\nabla$:

$$
\Sigma\beta\simeq *,\qquad 2[\eta_3]=2[\Sigma h]=[\Sigma\beta]=0.
$$

The last equality uses that suspension is a homomorphism on the relevant homotopy groups, as included in the actual Freudenthal supplier. No claim about its endpoint kernel having exactly one generator has been assumed.

## 3. The Hopf cones have nonzero $Sq^2$ after every suspension

Reduction modulo two of §1's projective calculation gives $H^*(C_h;\mathbb F_2)=\mathbb F_2[c]/(c^3)$ and $Sq^2 c=c^2\ne0$ by the top-square property. Thus

$$
Sq^2:H^2(C_h;\mathbb F_2)\longrightarrow H^4(C_h;\mathbb F_2)
$$

is an isomorphism. The precise cone/suspension equivalence at the beginning and the library's actual suspension compatibility of squares imply, for every $n\ge2$,

$$
Sq^2:H^n(C_{\eta_n};\mathbb F_2)\longrightarrow H^{n+2}(C_{\eta_n};\mathbb F_2)
$$

is an isomorphism. The cohomology groups involved are each one-dimensional. This uses the suspension of a degree-two square operation, not the assertion that a positive-degree cup product survives suspension. Positive-degree cup products on suspensions vanish; Steenrod squares nevertheless commute with suspension.

## 4. The explicit three-cell contradiction proves $\eta_3\eta_4\ne0$

More generally fix $n\ge2$, put $f=\eta_n:S^{n+1}\to S^n$ and $g=\eta_{n+1}:S^{n+2}\to S^{n+1}$, and suppose $f\circ g$ is based nullhomotopic. Let $K:CS^{n+2}\to S^n$ be a cone extension of this composite, and let $\chi:CS^{n+1}\to C_f$ be the attached-cell characteristic map, with restriction $f$ on its cone base.

Decompose $S^{n+3}$ into two cones on $S^{n+2}$. Define

$$
k:S^{n+3}\longrightarrow C_f
$$

on one cone by $\chi\circ Cg$ and on the other by $K$, followed by the inclusion $S^n\hookrightarrow C_f$. On their common equator both values are $f\circ g$, so the two maps paste continuously. The basepoint can be taken on that equator, where all the maps agree with the target basepoint. Form

$$
X=C_f\cup_k D^{n+4}.
$$

It has cells only in dimensions $0,n,n+2,n+4$. Its mod-two cohomology in the three positive occupied degrees is one-dimensional, and is zero in degree $n+1$. There are no adjacent positive cell dimensions, so all relevant cellular differentials are zero; the cellular comparison and UCT give these exact groups.

Let $j:C_f\hookrightarrow X$ be the inclusion. Its pullback is an isomorphism in degrees $n$ and $n+2$, either from the displayed cell complexes or from the relative cohomology LES since $(X,C_f)$ has only an $(n+4)$-cell. Write $x,y,z$ for the respective nonzero classes in $H^n(X;\mathbb F_2)$, $H^{n+2}(X;\mathbb F_2)$ and $H^{n+4}(X;\mathbb F_2)$. Naturality and §3 give

$$
Sq^2x=y.
$$

Now collapse the bottom $S^n$. The quotient $C_f/S^n$ is the sphere obtained from its characteristic $(n+2)$-disk by collapsing the boundary, hence $S^{n+2}$. On the cone carrying $K$ the quotient of $k$ is constant. On the other it is the cone on $g$ followed by collapse of the cone base. Collapsing the constant hemisphere in the source and then radially reparametrizing the remaining cone identifies this attaching map, up to the canonical based sphere equivalence, with $\Sigma g$. Therefore

$$
X/S^n\simeq S^{n+2}\cup_{\Sigma g}D^{n+4}=C_{\Sigma g}=C_{\eta_{n+2}}.
$$

This is the actual quotient attaching map, not a deduction from an unproved cofiber homotopy exact sequence. The collapse $q:X\to X/S^n$ induces isomorphisms on cohomology in degrees $n+2$ and $n+4$: in the pair LES both adjacent positive cohomology groups of $S^n$ are zero. Equivalently, it preserves the two upper cellular generators. Apply naturality and §3 again, now in the quotient, to get

$$
Sq^2y=z.
$$

It follows that $Sq^2Sq^2x=z\ne0$. But $H^{n+1}(X;\mathbb F_2)=0$, so $Sq^1x=0$, and the actual imported Adem relation gives

$$
Sq^2Sq^2x=Sq^3Sq^1x=0.
$$

This contradiction proves $[\eta_n\circ\eta_{n+1}]\ne0$ for every $n\ge2$. Taking $n=3$ gives the promised nonzero $a\in\pi_5(S^3)$. In particular $\eta_3$ itself cannot be nullhomotopic, since a composite with a nullhomotopic outer based map is nullhomotopic.

## 5. Finish both minimal numerical inputs

Apply Freudenthal to $S^2$, which is $1$-connected, with $n=2$ and $i=3=2n-1$. It gives a **surjective homomorphism**

$$
E:\pi_3(S^2)=\mathbb Z[h]\longrightarrow\pi_4(S^3).
$$

Thus $\pi_4(S^3)$ is cyclic, generated by $\eta_3$. Section 2 proves $2\eta_3=0$, and §4 proves $\eta_3\ne0$. A cyclic group generated by a nonzero element annihilated by two has precisely two elements. Hence

$$
\pi_4(S^3)=\{0,\eta_3\}\cong\mathbb Z/2.
$$

Because suspension is a homomorphism, $\eta_4=E\eta_3$ also satisfies $2\eta_4=0$. Postcomposition with $\eta_3$ induces a homomorphism $\pi_5(S^4)\to\pi_5(S^3)$ by ordinary homotopy-group functoriality. Consequently

$$
2a=(\eta_3)_*(2\eta_4)=0.
$$

Since $a\ne0$ by §4, it has exact order two and determines an injection $\mathbb Z/2\hookrightarrow\pi_5(S^3)$. This is all the final gauge-group argument needs; an assertion that this subgroup exhausts $\pi_5(S^3)$ would require an additional upper-bound argument, which has not been used or claimed.

## Consequence for the full Singer consumer

$$

$$
\pi_5(SU(3))\longrightarrow\pi_5(S^5)=\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\pi_4(SU(2))=\mathbb Z/2.
$$

The kernel of the last homomorphism contains $2\mathbb Z$, so exactness forces the first map to have nonzero image. It is unnecessary to show the last map is surjective. For $N\ge4$, the bundles $SU(N-1)\to SU(N)\to S^{2N-1}$ stabilize $\pi_5$ because $\pi_5$ and $\pi_6$ of those spheres vanish below their dimension. This supplies nonzero $\pi_5(SU(N))$ for all $N\ge3$.
