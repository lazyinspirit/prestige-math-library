   Constraints on Effective Field Theory operators from

                   The CMS Collaboration




                             Abstract


   A search for new physics in electroweak multiboson production is performed through
    a statistical combination of ten CMS measurements using proton-proton collision data
     collected at √s = 13 TeV during 2016–2018, corresponding to an integrated luminos-
     ity of 138 fb−1. The combination encompasses six vector boson scattering channels:
    same-sign WW production in fully leptonic and ℓτh final states, opposite-sign WW,
   WZ, and two VBS channels with a hadronically decaying vector boson (WV and ZV);
    two vector boson fusion channels (Wjj and Zjj), inclusive W+W−diboson produc-
     tion, and triboson VVV (no γ) production. Within the standard model effective field
    theory framework, potential contributions from physics beyond the standard model
     are parameterized in terms of six CP-even bosonic dimension-six operators of the
    Warsaw basis: OW, OHW, OHWB, OH□, OHB, and OHD. Their effects are introduced
    through event-by-event reweighting of leading-order Monte Carlo simulations, en-
    abling a consistent propagation of EFT effects to detector level with a consistent treat-
    ment of systematic uncertainties. Constraints on the associated Wilson coefficients are
     extracted using a binned profile likelihood fit, in scenarios ranging from individual
     coefficients to the fully profiled six-dimensional fit. No significant deviations from
    the standard model are observed. The combination improves substantially upon any
    individual analysis and constitutes the first detector-level combination of direct EFT
    measurements targeting bosonic dimension-six operators at the LHC.

1  Introduction

The standard model effective field theory (SMEFT) [1, 2] is a theoretical framework that extends
the standard model (SM) of particle physics by systematically incorporating the effects of new
physics at a mass scale Λ that is large in comparison to the electroweak (EW) scale and beyond
the reach of current experiments. Predictions for experimental observables are then provided
in terms of a systematic expansion of the SM Lagrangian in inverse powers of Λ, obtained
by including higher-dimensional operators O(d)i    , with dimension d > 4, that respect the SM
symmetries but introduce interactions that do not exist in the original theory. The effect of the
SMEFT operators is gauged by a dimensionless parameter called Wilson coefficient c(d)i  and
tamed by inverse powers of the new energy scale Λ−(d−4):

                                                                    c(d)i                          LSMEFT = LSM + ∑         O(d)i    .                               (1)                                            Λd−4                                d>4∑i

Comparing these predictions with observations provides constraints on the Wilson coefficients,
offering insight into BSM physics. The effective field theory interpretations of experimental
results have been explored by both ATLAS and CMS [3–6]. Assuming baryon and lepton num-
ber conservation, only operators of even dimension are allowed. This study is restricted to the
leading term in the tower expansion, namely dimension-6 operators.

This study presents a statistical combination of ten CMS analyses, interpreted in the frame-
work of SMEFT, to constrain the Wilson coefficients of six purely bosonic dimension-six op-
erators. The operators are defined in the Warsaw basis [2], which provides a complete and
non-redundant operator set.  Their definitions are listed in Table 1. The SMEFT effects are
incorporated through event-by-event reweighting of leading-order SM matrix elements, con-
sistently propagating both normalization and kinematic modifications to detector level in each
analysis. SMEFT-induced propagator corrections, which arise from operator contributions to
the total decay widths of the W, Z, and H bosons and are largest near the boson mass peaks,
are not included in the simulation. These corrections are most significant for operators enter-
ing δΓW and δΓZ through fermionic decays; for the purely bosonic operators considered here,
their impact on the extracted constraints has been verified to be negligible [7]. The combina-
tion includes the latest CMS measurements of EW production of one, two, and three vector
bosons in proton-proton collisions at the LHC. The input analyses probe complementary, non-
overlapping regions of phase space and are interpreted using a common SMEFT implementa-
tion and flavor-symmetry assumptions. The SMEFT effects are assumed to modify only EW
interactions among gauge bosons and fermions, providing a coherent probe of possible new
physics in the EW symmetry-breaking sector. The analyses included in the combination are
listed in Table 2.

The six Wilson coefficients considered in this combination correspond to the complete set of CP-
even, purely bosonic dimension-six operators of the Warsaw basis that contribute at tree level
to the EW diagrams entering the vector-boson-scattering (VBS), vector-boson-fusion (VBF), di-
boson, and triboson final states studied here. OW modifies the triple and quartic EW gauge-
boson self-couplings; OHW, OHB, and OHWB modify the HVV vertex, with OHWB additionally
inducing a momentum-dependent WWγ/WWZ coupling; and OH□and OHD enter through
corrections to the Higgs kinetic term and the EW input parameters. Together, these six oper-
ators span the complete set of tree-level modifications accessible to the EW gauge and Higgs
sectors probed by the final states considered in this combination.

SMEFTSIM implementation used in this combination, its effect after EW symmetry breaking
can therefore be reabsorbed into a redefinition of the EW input parameters. As a result, it
rescales the predicted cross sections without altering the shape of the kinematic distributions,
and acts effectively as a normalization operator. The remaining operators – OW, OHW, OHB, and
OHWB – all contain explicit gauge field-strength tensors (Wiµν and/or Bµν), which introduce new
derivative couplings among gauge bosons, or between the Higgs boson and gauge bosons, that
are absent in the SM Lagrangian and cannot be reabsorbed in the same way; in particular, OHWB
modifies the WWγ and WWZ vertices with a momentum-dependent structure equivalent to
a shift of the anomalous coupling ∆κγ. OH□similarly cannot be reabsorbed into the input
parameters, modifying instead the HVV vertex. Since these couplings are absent at tree level
in the SM, their contribution to the amplitude grows with the energy of the hard-scattering
process, distorting the shape of the kinematic distributions used in the fit; visible shape effects
are indeed observed for OH□in the SSWW and WZ channels, and for OHWB in the diboson
W+W−and VBF channels.

In this note, we consider the detector-level results of the latest CMS data analyses on the EW
production of one, two, and three vector bosons at the LHC, and statistically combine them.
This study focuses on processes that are non-overlapping in terms of the covered portions
of the physics phase space, using a consistent definition of the SMEFT operators based on
their defining basis and the flavor symmetries imposed upon them. The SMEFT effects under
investigation are assumed to modify only the EW couplings among bosons and fermions, to
capture BSM effects in the EW symmetry breaking across different key EW processes. The list
of the individual analyses considered in this note is reported in Table 2.

In the remainder of this note, Section 2 describes the CMS detector, Section 3 presents the inputs
to the combination, Section 4 illustrates the methods for simulating events. In Section 5 and
Section 6 respectively we discuss the statistical procedure employed to measure the Wilson
coefficients and modeling of systematic uncertainties. The SMEFT interpretation results are
given in Section 8, and we conclude with a summary in Section 9.

2  The CMS detector

The central feature of the CMS apparatus is a superconducting solenoid of 6 m internal diam-
eter, providing a magnetic field of 3.8 T. Within this magnetic field volume are a silicon pixel
and silicon strip tracker, a lead tungstate crystal electromagnetic calorimeter (ECAL), and a
brass-and-scintillator hadron calorimeter (HCAL), each composed of a barrel and two endcap
sections. Forward calorimeters extend the coverage in pseudorapidity η provided by the bar-
rel and endcap detectors. Muons are detected in gas-ionization chambers embedded in the
steel flux-return yoke outside the solenoid. A more detailed description of the CMS detector,
together with a definition of the coordinate system used and the relevant kinematic variables,
can be found in Ref. [8].

The silicon tracker used in 2016 measured charged particles within the range |η| < 2.5. For
nonisolated particles of transverse momentum pT in the range 1 < pT < 10 GeV and |η| <
1.4, the track resolutions were typically 1.5% in pT and 25–90 (45–150) µm in the transverse
(longitudinal) impact parameter. For isolated particles with pT = 100 GeV emitted at |η| <
1.4, the resolutions are approximately 2.8% in pT, and in impact parameter 10 µm (transverse)
and 30 µm (longitudinal) [9]. At the start of 2017, a new pixel detector was installed [10]; the
upgraded tracker measured particles up to |η| < 3.0 with typical resolutions of 1.5% in pT and
20–75 µm in the transverse impact parameter for nonisolated particles of 1 < pT < 10 GeV [11].

Events of interest are selected using a two-tiered trigger system [12]. The first level, composed
of custom hardware processors, uses information from the calorimeters and muon detectors to
select events at a rate of around 100 kHz. The second level, known as the high-level trigger,
consists of a farm of processors running a version of the full event reconstruction software,
optimized for fast processing that reduces the event rate to a few kHz before data storage.


3  Input analyses

For this study we consider the results of ten analyses, as outlined in Table 2. Six vector bo-
son scattering (VBS) channels are included, four of them with a fully leptonic final state: the
scattering of two W bosons with same electric charge (SSWW) with electrons or muons in
the final state (e, µ), or with one hadronically decaying τ lepton (τh); the scattering of two W
bosons with opposite electric charge (OSWW); and the scattering of a WZ pair. Two further VBS
analyses are included in which one of the two vector bosons decays hadronically (V →qq):
WV →ℓ±νjj and ZV →ℓ+ℓ−jj, where V = Z, W. The EW production of a vector boson Vjj
through vector boson fusion (VBF), where V = W, Z, is also considered. One diboson process
W+W−, targeting the electron-muon final state to suppress the Drell–Yan background, with
leptonic decay of both W bosons (W+W−→e±µ∓νν), and one triboson process (VVV/γ),
targeting the production of three massive gauge bosons VVV with V = W, Z and excluding
final states with a photon, complete the picture. The triboson analysis includes multiple final
states from 0 up to 2 charged leptons. All analyses considered in the combination are based on
pp collisions data collected between 2016 and 2018 with the CMS detector, corresponding to an
integrated luminosity of 138 fb−1. The VBS SSWW(τh) and VVV/γ analyses are the only ones
targeting final states that include a hadronically decaying τ lepton. Details about the genera-
tion of the SM and SMEFT terms are described in Section 4. Representative SM diagrams for
the EW production mechanism and final state of each analysis are shown in Fig. 1, with the
shaded blob denoting the EW production amplitude, which receives contributions from multi-
ple diagrams; the dimension-six operators of Table 1 can affect both this production amplitude
and the subsequent decay vertices of the vector bosons.

4  Signal and background samples

Monte Carlo (MC) simulation is used throughout the combination to design the event selec-
tion, evaluate signal efficiencies, and estimate background contributions. The VBS, VBF, di-
boson, and triboson SMEFT signal samples are all generated at leading order (LO) with MAD-
GRAPH5 aMC@NLO v2.6.5 [22]; the generation syntax used by each analysis is reported in Table
5 in the appendix. Further details on the signal simulation can be found in the documentation
of each individual analysis. The SMEFT simulation is performed at LO using the SMEFTSIM
package [23, 24]. For each analysis considered in this combination, a single set of events is gen-
erated with all six CP-even operators of interest simultaneously active at unit coupling. The
MADGRAPH5 aMC@NLO reweighting functionality is then used to assign each event a weight
corresponding to a given new physics hypothesis, from which the full SMEFT parametriza-
tion is recovered: the SM hypothesis, each operator individually set to ci = ±1, and each pair
of operators simultaneously set to ci = cj = 1. The closure of this reweighting procedure has
been validated, on a per-analysis basis, against the direct generation of the linear and quadratic
amplitude components implemented in SMEFTSIM.

SMEFTSIM is used exclusively to generate the SMEFT contributions to the EW signal pro-
cesses; all background processes are instead simulated using their SM-only matrix elements, as
described below.

The strategies to estimate the background contributions vary across input channels and include
both data-driven estimations and MC simulation techniques. Backgrounds that are reducible or
poorly simulated, such as non-prompt leptons, charge-misidentification electrons, and Vector
Boson + Jets processes, are constrained through dedicated control regions (CRs) in data. Most
irreducible backgrounds are instead modeled using MC simulation, as described in the follow-
ing lines. In the VBS channels, the QCD-induced backgrounds and EW-QCD interference are
simulated at leading order (LO) with MADGRAPH5 aMC@NLO v2.6.5 and using MADSPIN [25]
to simulate the decay of the vector bosons. In the case of OSWW, quark- and gluon-induced
WW backgrounds are instead simulated using POWHEG v2 [26–28]. W/Z+jets, Wγ, QCD-
induced diboson, and VBF production are simulated with MADGRAPH5 aMC@NLO at LO,
while triboson processes are simulated at next-to-leading order (NLO). Zγ, Wg and Zg are gen-
erated at LO or NLO depending on the input channel. Resonant diboson EW or QCD produc-
tion is generated through MADGRAPH5 aMC@NLO (LO) and PYTHIA [29]. Processes involving
top quarks are generated mainly with POWHEG at NLO and MADGRAPH5 aMC@NLO at NLO,
depending on the input analysis. W+Jets and Z+Jets are estimated through data-driven meth-
ods in some analyses, namely VBS-ZV, VBS-WV, VBF-Z, while in the others they are generated
via MADGRAPH5 aMC@NLO at LO with MLM jet matching [30]. The same matching strategy is
used also for QCD multijet and diboson events. Higgs boson production processes, in particu-
lar gluon-gluon fusion, VBF, and WH production, are generated through POWHEG or JHUGEN
[31] at NLO, with reweighting to next-to-NLO [32], where applicable.

The NNPDF3.1 next-to-NLO [33] parton distribution functions (PDFs) are used in the simula-
tion of the background and signal samples.

The generators used for signal and background processes are interfaced with the PYTHIA pro-
gram, in versions 8.306 or 8.230 depending on the analysis, with the CP5 tune [34], to model
parton showering, hadronization and underlying events.

Additional collisions in the same or adjacent bunch crossings (pileup) are taken into account
by superimposing simulated minimum bias interactions onto the hard-scattering process, with
a multiplicity distribution matching the one that is observed in the data. Simulated events

5  Statistical procedure

The statistical interpretation of the data follows the methodology shared by the ATLAS and
CMS Collaborations [37, 38]. The results were derived using the CMS statistical analysis tool,

likelihood ratio q is used as a test statistic in order to measure the Wilson coefficients ⃗c and
their associated confidence intervals. By denoting ⃗x the measurements and ⃗θ the nuisance
parameters modeling various source of uncertainties the test statistics is defined as:

and q < 2.3, q < 6.18 for two-dimensional ones.

6  Systematic uncertainties

Systematic uncertainties from both experimental and theoretical sources are taken into account
in the combination, implemented as nuisance parameters in the in the binned maximum like-
lihood fit to the dimension-6 Wilson coefficients. All uncertainties affecting the SM EW com-
ponent of the signal in a specific analysis are also considered on the SMEFT prediction and
fully correlated across all bins and channels. Theoretical uncertainties are treated as correlated
across analyses and between signal and background samples wherever appropriate. Experi-
mental uncertainties are also correlated across analyses when justified. The adopted correla-
tion scheme has been tested for robustness, with the results showing only limited sensitivity
to variations in the correlation assumptions. The systematic uncertainties are globally treated

                                                                          9


as either normalization-only or shape-affecting, depending on their impact on the predicted
yields and distributions.

The integrated luminosity of the 2016, 2017, and 2018 datasets is measured with uncertainties
of 1.2, 2.3, and 2.5%, respectively, resulting in an overall uncertainty of 1.6% [42–44].

Reconstruction and identification efficiencies of electrons and muons, alongside with their mo-
mentum scale and resolution uncertainties, are evaluated using dedicated studies [45]. Sim-
ilarly, statistical uncertainties related to the τ lepton identification, energy scale and resolu-
tion are accounted for [46]. Depending on the analysis, specific uncertainties are computed to
account for differences between measurement and signal regions in the modeling of the non-
prompt lepton background or the charge misidentification electrons background, which are
data-driven estimation techniques [13].

Trigger efficiency uncertainties are derived from measurements based on control samples in
data and propagated to the predicted yields.

Jet energy scale and resolution uncertainties are assessed through the variation of the jet mo-
menta within their ±1σ uncertainties, which in turn affects jet multiplicities and pmissT    [47].
For small- and large-radius jets, additional uncertainties in the jet mass scale and resolution
are considered, particularly in channels targeting boosted topologies. Multiplicative factors
are applied to all simulated samples tocorrect the discrepancies in jet pileup identification be-
tween data and simulation. These corrections affect only analyses with jets of pT < 50 GeV and
are uncorrelated through years, correlated across processes. Efficiencies for b tagging are ad-
justed using corrections based on control samples in data [45]. Pileup modeling uncertainties
are estimated by varying the total inelastic pp cross section by 4.6% [48].

Theoretical uncertainties are propagated by varying renormalization and factorization scales
by a factor of two, excluding the two extreme and opposite variations, and by evaluating un-
certainties from PDFs and the strong coupling αS following the PDF4LHC prescription [49].
Parton shower modeling uncertainties are derived by varying initial- and final-state radiation
weights in the simulated samples.


7  Combined fit inputs and uncertainty correlations

Table 3 summarizes the signal regions (SRs) and input distributions entering the combined
maximum-likelihood fit. Several analyses further split their SRs into finer categories in order
to maximize sensitivity to the SMEFT signal.

In the VBS-OSWW analysis, the two SRs are separated by the Zeppenfeld variable [50] of the
dilepton system, Zℓℓ= 0.5 Zℓ1 + Zℓ2  , with Zℓ= ηℓ−0.5  ηj1 + ηj2   , as defined in Ref. [15],
and are fit in the dijet invariant mass mjj. The VBS-SSWW(e, µ) and VBS-WZ analyses use a
single SR each, fit in a two-dimensional distribution of mjj and mℓℓ, or of mjj and the transverse
mass mWZT   of the diboson system for VBS-WZ, flattened into a one-dimensional array of bins.
The VBS-SSWW(τh) analysis uses a single SR fit in an SMEFT-sensitive DNN score. The VBF-
W and VBF-Z analyses are fit in the azimuthal separation ∆ϕjj between the two tagging jets,
uniformly binned for VBF-W and further split into windows of DNN score, flattened into a
single array, for VBF-Z. The VBS-WV SRs are split by the value of a DNN discriminant trained
to separate the EW signal from the backgrounds, and are fit in the diboson invariant mass mWV;
the VBS-ZV SRs are instead split according to the number of b-tagged jets (b-veto or b-tag), and
are fit in mZV.

The VVV/γ analysis defines the largest number of SRs, split according to the number of charged
leptons, the number of hadronically decaying τ leptons (τh), and the number of large-radius,
V-tagged jets (VTJ). Categories with two charged leptons are further split by the leptons’ rel-
ative electric charge – same-sign (SS) or opposite-sign (OS) – by their flavor – opposite-flavor
(OF) or same-flavor (SF) – and, for same-flavor pairs, by whether the dilepton invariant mass
lies close to the Z boson mass (Z) or away from it (noZ). Most SRs are fit in the scalar sum of
the transverse momenta of the reconstructed objects, ST. In the categories with one hadron-
ically decaying τh, a boosted decision tree (BDT) trained to separate the SMEFT signal from
the background is used instead of ST as the discriminating variable, with the working point
separating the low- and high-BDT-score regions ranging from 0.95 to 0.98 depending on the
data-taking year; the high-BDT, low-ST region is excluded from the fit due to its limited event
yield. Finally, the diboson W+W−analysis (WW) merges the 0- and 1-jet SRs, fit in the dilepton
invariant mass mℓℓ.

The combined fit spans 163 signal and control regions, corresponding to 912 bins of the discrim-
inating distributions used in the likelihood, with 3.39 million events observed in total. System-
atic uncertainties are implemented through 562 independent nuisance parameters, profiled in
the fit; several background normalizations are additionally left free to float and constrained
directly by data rather than assigned an external uncertainty.

The correlation pattern of the systematic uncertainties is assigned according to their physical
origin rather than by region. Approximately 30% of the nuisance parameters are shared be-
tween two or more regions and are thus treated as fully correlated wherever they appear; the
remaining 70% affect a single region and are left uncorrelated with the rest of the combination.
The correlated component is dominated by sources common to the detector or to the theoretical
modeling: jet energy scale and resolution, b-tagging efficiency, pileup modeling, and lepton re-
construction/identification efficiencies are correlated across all regions and years in which the
same calibration applies, as are parton distribution function and QCD renormalization and
factorization scale uncertainties for shared background processes. The integrated luminosity
uncertainty follows the standard LHC Run 2 decomposition into a component uncorrelated be-
tween years, a component correlated only between the last two years of data-taking, and a com-
ponent fully correlated across the entire dataset. Conversely, uncertainties on backgrounds es-
timated with region-specific data-driven techniques, such as nonprompt-lepton contributions,
are treated as fully uncorrelated between regions, since they arise from measurements specific
to each selection. Statistical uncertainties of the MC simulations are treated independently in
every bin of every region and are never correlated.

The SMEFT signal, is not assigned its own independent set of systematic uncertainties. Instead,
every nuisance parameter that affects the SM component of a signal process is applied identi-
cally to all of its associated SMEFT interference and quadratic components, so that the whole
SMEFT parameterization of a given process responds coherently to a systematic shift, exactly
as if it were a single template. Since the linear, quadratic, and mixed terms are obtained ana-
lytically from a common set of simulated samples rather than being independently generated,
no separate MC statistical uncertainty is assigned to them: bin-by-bin statistical uncertainties
are propagated only for the SM signal template and the background templates, not for the
SMEFT-dependent pieces individually.

In the combined fit, the most constrained coefficient is cW, which modifies both the triple and
quartic gauge couplings; the strongest constraints on cW originate from the triboson, diboson
WW, and VBF channels, where momentum-enhanced effects in the boson transverse momenta
increase the sensitivity. The coefficient cHD enters only through corrections to the EW input
parameters, rescaling the overall cross sections without affecting the kinematic distributions;
it is therefore predominantly constrained by VBF and diboson processes thanks to the higher
cross-section. The coefficient cHWB modifies the WWγ and WWZ vertices with a momentum-
dependent structure and is similarly best constrained by the VBF and diboson WW channels,
which are directly sensitive to the resulting shape distortions. The coefficients cH□and cHW
modify the HVV couplings and are mainly constrained by the VBS-WV channel, by VBS-
SSWW, where a t-channel Higgs boson exchange contributes, and by the triboson channel.
The coefficient cHB affects only Higgs couplings to the Z boson and the photon, and is there-
fore unconstrained by the diboson and VBF channels, being instead set mainly by the VBS and
triboson channels, which probe off-shell Higgs contributions.

to data while fixing all the others to zero. The corresponding expected results, obtained under
the SM-only hypothesis, are also shown for reference.

A summary of the expected and observed limits are shown in Fig. 2, together with the fractional
contribution of each analysis to the constraint on each Wilson coefficient and with the limits on
the energy scale Λ. The observed 68% and 95% CL intervals are reported in Table 4.

For most operators, the one-dimensional sensitivity is dominated by the quadratic SMEFT
term, consistent with what is observed in the individual VBS analyses. The exceptions are
cHWB and cHD, for which the linear term dominates, driven by the VBF channels.
The ranking of the input channels’ fractional contribution f jα , shown in the central panel of Fig.
2, also shifts between the linear-only and the linear+quadratic fits. For cW and cHWB, the linear
fit is dominated by the VBF-W and VBF-Z channels, while in the linear+quadratic fit the lead-
ing contribution shifts to the diboson WW channel, with the triboson channel also becoming
relevant for cW. For cHD, the VBF channels dominate both fits, with VBF-W alone account-
ing for most of the linear+quadratic constraint. The VBS-WV channel dominates both fits for
cHW, although the linear fit receives a sizeable additional contribution from the VBS-OSWW
and VBS-SSWW(e, µ) channels that becomes negligible in the linear+quadratic fit. For cHB,
the linear constraint is spread fairly evenly across most channels, whereas the linear+quadratic
constraint is almost entirely set by the VBS-WV channel. For cH□, the VBS-OSWW channel
dominates the linear fit, while in the linear+quadratic fit the constraint is shared between the
OSWW and VBS-WV channels.

All of the operators are found to be compatible with the SM expectation of zero, within one
standard deviation. As observed already with the expected results, the tightest constraint is set
on cW, particularly constrained by the diboson WW, triboson, VBF-W, and VBS-WV analyses.
The coefficients cHD and cHWB are predominantly constrained by the VBF and diboson chan-
nels, while cH□and cHW are primarily constrained by the VBS-WV channel. The coefficient cHB
is mainly constrained by the VBS and triboson channels.

9  Summary

A search for physics beyond the standard model is performed in the framework of the stan-
dard model effective field theory (SMEFT) through a statistical combination of ten CMS mea-
surements of electroweak (EW) processes. The combination includes vector boson scattering
(VBS), vector boson fusion (VBF), diboson, and triboson production, probing final states with
one, two, and three EW gauge bosons. The analyses use the full Run 2 proton-proton collision
dataset recorded by the CMS experiment at the CERN LHC at √s = 13TeV, corresponding to an
integrated luminosity of 138 fb−1. For all measurements, the SMEFT parametrization is prop-
agated directly to detector level through event-by-event reweighting, allowing a consistent

A  Diboson W+W−

The diboson W+W−analysis targets leptonic W boson decays, W+W−→ℓ+ℓ−νℓνℓwith ℓ=
e, µ. The background composition depends strongly on the lepton-flavour final state: same-
flavour events (ee, µµ) are dominated by Drell–Yan production, while different-flavour events
(eµ) are dominated by top-quark processes. To maximize sensitivity to SMEFT effects, only
the different-flavour (eµ) category is considered, further split by jet multiplicity (Njets = 0, 1).
The analysis uses the full 2016–2018 CMS dataset, corresponding to an integrated luminosity
of 138 fb−1.

Events are preselected using single-lepton or dilepton triggers, requiring at least one isolated
electron or muon, or two isolated leptons (electrons and/or muons), respectively.

Leptons are further selected offline using tight MVA-based identification [51–53] and isolation
criteria, together with track-based constraints consistent with the primary vertex.

Jets are reconstructed with the anti-kT [54, 55] algorithm using a distance parameter of R = 0.4,
and are required to satisfy pjT > 30 GeV and |ηj| < 4.7. The pmissT    is computed with the PUPPI
algorithm [56, 57]. Jets from b quark fragmentation are identified among jets with pjT > 30 GeV
and |ηj| < 2.5, using the medium working point of the DeepJet algorithm [58, 59].

The leading and subleading leptons are required to have opposite charge and flavour, with
pT > 25 and 20 GeV, respectively. Events with an additional lepton satisfying loose identifica-
tion and pT > 10 GeV are rejected to suppress other diboson backgrounds. pmissT  > 20 GeV is
required to account for the undetected neutrinos. Contributions from nonprompt leptons are
suppressed by requiring pℓℓT > 30 GeV and mℓℓ> 20 GeV.
Two regions are defined: a signal region, enriched in electroweak W+W−production by veto-
ing b-tagged jets with pT > 20 GeV and requiring mW+W−T   > 60 GeV and mW2T > 30 GeV (the
transverse mass built from the subleading-pT lepton); and a tt-enriched control region, used to
constrain its normalization from data, requiring mℓℓ> 50 GeV, mW2T > 30 GeV, and at least one
b-tagged jet.
All SM backgrounds and the W+W−signal are simulated, except for nonprompt leptons,
which mainly arise from W+jets events where a jet is misidentified as the second lepton. These
are estimated with a data-driven fake-rate method [20]: misidentification and prompt rates,
measured in multijet and Drell–Yan control samples and parametrized in lepton pT and η,
define a transfer factor applied to a loose-lepton data sample to predict the nonprompt con-
tribution passing the tight selection. The quark-induced W+W−production is simulated with
POWHEG v2.0 at next-to-NLO accuracy in QCD using the MINNLOPS method [60, 61], with
higher-order EW corrections computed at NLO with MATRIX v2.1.0 [62, 63]; a shape-only cor-
rection in pWWT    is applied to the MINNLOPS sample. The non-resonant gluon-induced W+W−
production, treated as part of the signal, is modelled with MCFM v7.0 [64]. The dominant top-
induced backgrounds (tt, tW, and t-channel single top quark) are simulated with POWHEG v2.0
at NLO in QCD, with top quark decays modelled by MADSPIN; the s-channel single top con-
tribution is generated with MADGRAPH5 aMC@NLO v2.6.5 at NLO in QCD. Drell–Yan produc-
tion enters the different-flavour region mainly via residual Z →τ+τ−decays and is generated
at NLO in QCD with MADGRAPH5 aMC@NLO with up to two additional partons. Remain-
ing minor backgrounds (diboson and triboson EW production, and Higgs-mediated resonant
W+W−production) are generated with either MADGRAPH5 aMC@NLO or POWHEG.

B  VBF analyses

The VBF-W and VBF-Z analyses target the electroweak production of a single massive vector
boson in association with two jets, where the boson decays leptonically. These processes pro-
vide complementary sensitivity to the bosonic SMEFT operators considered in this combina-
tion, in particular to cHD, cHWB, and cW. Their characteristic topology consists of two energetic
jets with large invariant mass and large pseudorapidity separation, together with a centrally
produced massive vector boson.

Events are reconstructed using the CMS particle-flow algorithm [36].  Electrons and muons
are required to satisfy identification and isolation criteria optimized for high-purity prompt-
lepton selection [51, 52]. Jets are reconstructed with the anti-kT algorithm and are corrected for
detector response, pileup contamination, and residual data-to-simulation differences [47, 57].
Missing transverse momentum [65] is used in the VBF-W analysis to reconstruct the leptonic
W boson decay, while the VBF-Z analysis uses a pair of opposite electric charge, same-flavour
leptons compatible with the Z boson mass. Events containing additional leptons or b-tagged
jets [45, 58, 59] are rejected in order to suppress diboson and top-quark backgrounds.

The VBF-W analysis selects events with exactly one isolated electron or muon, significant miss-
ing transverse momentum, and at least two jets. The two leading jets are required to satisfy
VBF-like requirements on their invariant mass and pseudorapidity separation. Additional se-
lections on the lepton kinematics, the transverse mass of the W boson, and the event topology
are applied to reduce the dominant W+jets and top-quark backgrounds. A Deep Neural Net-
work (DNN) is used to enhance the separation between the electroweak VBF-W signal and the
dominant backgrounds. The signal-enriched region is defined by requiring the DNN output
to be larger than 0.5, and the azimuthal separation between the two tagging jets, ∆ϕjj, is used
as the final discriminating observable in the fit. Dedicated control regions enriched in W+jets
and top-quark events are included in the likelihood to constrain the corresponding background
normalizations in data.

The VBF-Z analysis selects events with two isolated, oppositely charged electrons or muons
and at least two jets with high pseudorapidity separation and high invariant mass. A DNN is
trained to separate the SM signal from the background, which is dominated by DY+jets produc-
tion. The ∆ϕjj distribution is found to be the observable most sensitive to SMEFT contributions
relative to the SM VBF signal. To maximize sensitivity to the SMEFT signal while suppressing
the SM background, the ∆ϕjj distribution is therefore fit within windows of the DNN score.

The selected signal and control regions are combined in a simultaneous binned maximum-
likelihood fit. The observables entering the fit are chosen to maximise the discrimination be-
tween the electroweak signal and the dominant backgrounds while retaining sensitivity to pos-
sible SMEFT-induced deviations from the Standard Model. Figure 6 shows the post-fit distri-
butions of the final discriminants in the signal regions of the VBF-W and VBF-Z analyses.

The dominant systematic uncertainties arise from the modelling of the V+jets background,
renormalisation and factorisation scale variations, PDFs, jet energy scale and resolution. These
uncertainties are propagated to the final discriminant distributions and included in the likeli-
hood fit as nuisance parameters.

The VBF channels are particularly important in the combined SMEFT interpretation because
of their large signal yields and their direct sensitivity to modifications of electroweak input-
parameter relations. As a result, they provide some of the strongest constraints on cHD and
cHWB, while also contributing to the sensitivity to cW through momentum-enhanced effects in
the VBF phase space.

C  VBS-SSWW(e, µ), WZ, and OSWW analyses

The VBS-SSWW(e, µ), -WZ, and -OSWW analyses target the production of a pair of massive
vector bosons in association with two jets. Only leptonic decays of the Z and W to e and µ
and the associated neutrinos are considered. They provide sentisivity to a wide range of the
SMEFT operators considered, namely cW, cHW, cHWB, cHD and cH□for SSWW and WZ and cW,
cHW, cHWB, cHD, cH□and cHB for OSWW. Such studies are SMEFT reinterpretations of Run-
2 CMS results [13] and [15]. The analysis strategy here employed follows closely that of the
public results, with few notable exceptions. In particular, the SMEFT reinterpretations employ
a tighter lepton ID strategy based on a multivariate analysis, which grants better discrimination
against fake leptons.

Similarly to the VBF analyses described in section B, the VBS analyses use most up-to-date re-
construction for 2016–2018, which provides improved and consistent detector calibrations and
object reconstruction across the data-taking period. Events are reconstructed using the CMS
particle-flow algorithm [36]. Electrons and muons are required to satisfy identification and iso-
lation criteria optimized for high-purity prompt-lepton selection [51, 52]. Jets are reconstructed
with the anti-kT algorithm and are corrected for detector response, pileup contamination, and
residual data-to-simulation differences [47, 57]. Missing transverse momentum [65] is used in
to reconstruct the leptonic W boson decay. Events containing additional leptons or b-tagged
jets [45, 58, 59] are rejected in order to suppress top-quark backgrounds in the SSWW and WZ
analyses. A veto on τ particles is employed in the SSWW analysis[46].

To isolate the signal-enriched phase space, the analyses employ a set of kinematic selections
on the final state objects. All the three analyses require the presence of at least two jets, with
high invariant mass (mjj) and high pseudorapidity separation, and the presence of missing
transverse energy due to the presence of neutrinos from the W boson. Furthermore, the VBS
SSWW signal region is designed to select a pair of same-sign leptons (e or µ), while OSWW
requires two opposite electric charge, opposite flavour leptons (e and µ). To increase sensitivity,
the 2018 and 2017 OSWW signal region is further divided into two subcategories based on the
dilepton system centrality with respect to the tagging jets. For 2016 data, due to the lower
statistics, events are considered into a single category due to the lack of statistics. The VBS WZ
analysis, on the other hand, requires three leptons, two of which must be of opposite electric
charge and same flavour.

Backgrounds are estimated using a combination of simulation and data-driven techniques. The
main background contribution for SSWW stems from nonprompt leptons, which are estimated
through data-driven techniques. For WZ, the main contributions come from QCD-induced WZ
production, estimated through MC simulation, and from the tV + q background, constrained
in a dedicated control region. The main background to OSWW arises from top-quark pair pro-
duction, where both top quarks decay leptonically. This background is normalized using a
dedicated control region. The same strategy is used to constrain also the DY process, which
represents a subdominant background contribution. All other background processes are esti-
mated through MC simulations.

The statistical analysis is performed using a binned maximum likelihood fit to the distribution
of meaningful kinematic variables, and systematic uncertainties are incorporated as nuisance
parameters. In particular, for SSWW a bidimensional combination of the invariant mass of the
two leading jets (mjj) and the invariant mass of the dilepton system (mℓℓ) is used. For WZ, the
transverse mass of the WZ system is used, combined with mjj. For OSWW, the invariant mass
of the di-jet system mjj is used. Such variables are displayed in Figure 7.
