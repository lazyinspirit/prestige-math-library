## Statement

Let $(X,\mathcal A)$ be a ringed space whose structure sheaf $\mathcal A$ is coherent in the sense that every finite tuple of its local sections has a locally finite-type relation sheaf. For all finite integers $p,q\ge0$ and every $\mathcal A$-module morphism $\Phi:\mathcal A^{\oplus p}\to\mathcal A^{\oplus q}$, the kernel sheaf $\ker\Phi$ is locally of finite type.

## Statement references

[Wehler, §4.2, Theorem 4.28 proof, target-rank induction, printed pp. 138–140/PDF pp. 142–144](https://www.math.lmu.de/~wehler/20190530_Stein_Manifolds_script.pdf)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A ringed space $(X,\mathcal A)$ with coherent structure sheaf and finite integers $p,q\ge0$.

[F1] [[def-qftx-analytic-coherent-module]]: coherence of $\mathcal A$ means that every finite tuple of sections of $\mathcal A$ has a locally finite-type relation sheaf.

[F2] [[def-kernel-cokernel-image-sheaves]]: the kernel sheaf of a module morphism is the subsheaf whose sections are objectwise kernels.

[F3] [[thm-exactness-of-sheaves-stalkwise]]: equality and exactness of the relevant sheaf maps can be checked on every stalk.

## Proof

1.1 Induct on the target rank $q$. If $q=0$, then $\ker\Phi=\mathcal A^{\oplus p}$, generated by its $p$ standard sections (and equal to zero if $p=0$). If $q=1$, the images of the $p$ standard sections are a finite tuple in $\mathcal A$, and [F1] says its relation sheaf, which is $\ker\Phi$ by [F2], is locally of finite type. [F1, F2, given, construct]

2.1 Assume $q\ge1$ and the result for target rank $q$. Let $\Phi:\mathcal A^{\oplus p}\to\mathcal A^{\oplus(q+1)}$, and let $\pi:\mathcal A^{\oplus(q+1)}\to\mathcal A^{\oplus q}$ drop the first coordinate. By the induction hypothesis, around any fixed $x\in X$ the sheaf $\ker(\pi\Phi)$ has finitely many generating sections $s_1,\ldots,s_r$ whose germs generate every stalk on one neighborhood $V$ of $x$. [step 1.1, given, construct]

3.1 Since $\Phi(s_i)$ lies in $\ker\pi=\mathcal A\oplus0^{\oplus q}$, write $\Phi(s_i)=(h_i,0,\ldots,0)$ with $h_i\in\mathcal A(V)$. By [F1], after shrinking $V$ the relation sheaf of $(h_1,\ldots,h_r)$ is generated on $V$ by finitely many sections $t^{(1)},\ldots,t^{(m)}\in\mathcal A(V)^{\oplus r}$. For each $\ell$, define $\sigma_\ell=\sum_{i=1}^r t_i^{(\ell)}s_i$. Then $\Phi(\sigma_\ell)=0$ because $\sum_i t_i^{(\ell)}h_i=0$. [F1, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 For any $y\in V$ and any germ $v\in(\ker\Phi)_y$, step 2.1 gives $v=\sum_i c_i(s_i)_y$ for some germs $c_i\in\mathcal A_y$. The first coordinate of $\Phi_y(v)=0$ says $\sum_i c_i(h_i)_y=0$, so $(c_i)_i$ lies in the relation stalk generated by the $t^{(\ell)}_y$. Hence $v$ is an $\mathcal A_y$-linear combination of $(\sigma_\ell)_y$. Thus these finitely many sections generate every kernel stalk on $V$; [F2] and [F3] identify the resulting subsheaf with $\ker\Phi$, proving local finite type. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The target-rank induction is the sheaf version of reducing a map into $\mathcal A^{\oplus(q+1)}$ to its projection into $\mathcal A^{\oplus q}$ and one scalar relation map. The proof above uses stalks only to verify the generated kernel; it does not use a diagram as a proof.

The projection used in the induction is recorded by the commutative diagram

```tikzcd
\mathcal A^{\oplus p} \arrow[r,"\Phi"] \arrow[dr,"\pi\circ\Phi"'] & \mathcal A^{\oplus(q+1)} \arrow[d,"\pi"] \\
& \mathcal A^{\oplus q}
```

Here the diagonal map is the target-rank-$q$ map whose kernel is generated by the induction hypothesis; the vertical map drops the first coordinate. The diagram fixes the map directions and adds no premise to the proof.
