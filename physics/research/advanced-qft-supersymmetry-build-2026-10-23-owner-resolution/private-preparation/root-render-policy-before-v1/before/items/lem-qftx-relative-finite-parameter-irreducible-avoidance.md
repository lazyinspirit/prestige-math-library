## Statement

Assume ZF together with the Axiom of Choice. Let $M$ be $S^3$ or $S^4$ with its compact, connected, oriented, boundaryless smooth structure, let $N\ge2$, and let $P\to M$ be a smooth principal right $SU(N)$-bundle with a smooth reference connection $A_0$. Fix a basepoint $m\in M$ and a frame of $P_m$. Equip $\mathcal A_\infty=A_0+\Omega^1(M;\operatorname{ad}P)$ with the Fréchet topology generated by suprema of all derivatives of section coefficients in a fixed finite bundle atlas. Let $\mathcal A_\infty^*$ consist of connections whose based holonomy action on $\mathbb C^N$ has no nonzero proper invariant complex subspace. Let $K$ be the geometric realization of a finite simplicial complex, let $L$ be the realization of a subcomplex, and let $f:K\to\mathcal A_\infty$ be continuous with $f(L)\subseteq\mathcal A_\infty^*$. Then there is a continuous homotopy $H:K\times[0,1]\to\mathcal A_\infty$, fixed on $L$ and on an open neighborhood of $L$, such that $H(\cdot,1)$ maps all of $K$ into $\mathcal A_\infty^*$. For every finite list of these $C^\infty$ section seminorms $p_1,\ldots,p_m$ and positive tolerances $\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_m$, the homotopy can be chosen with $\sup_{(s,t)\in K\times[0,1]}p_j(H(s,t)-f(s))<\epsilon_j$ for every $j$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** ZF and the Axiom of Choice; the fixed bundle and affine connection space in the statement; a finite polyhedron $K$; a subpolyhedron $L$; and a continuous map $f$ whose restriction to $L$ is irreducible.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice means every family of nonempty sets has a choice function ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). In particular it implies countable choice, the weaker set-theoretic assumption used by the finite-dimensional approximation, transversality and Sard results below ([[def-countable-choice]]).

[F1] The batch-56 configuration definition identifies $\mathcal A_\infty$ with $A_0+\Omega^1(M;\operatorname{ad}P)$, uses the finite-atlas $C^\infty$ section topology, and defines irreducibility by the based holonomy action on $\mathbb C^N$. Its complete definition was inspected and its current Step 1 record is ready; the proof uses exactly that interface ([[def-qftx-smooth-sobolev-gauge-configuration]]).

[F2] The finite-holonomy lemma states that $\mathcal A_\infty^*$ is open and supplies finite based loops witnessing each irreducible connection. Its complete authored proof and exact batch-56 inputs were inspected; the configuration and stabilizer suppliers now have current ready Step 1 records ([[lem-qftx-irreducible-finite-holonomy-witness]]).

[F3] The incidence manifold of $r$ holonomies preserving a $k$-plane has codimension $2r k(N-k)$ in $SU(N)^r\times\operatorname{Gr}_{\mathbb C}(k,N)$; the dimension deficit after including the plane variable is $2(r-1)k(N-k)$ ([[lem-qftx-holonomy-incidence-codimension]]).

[F4] A jointly smooth matrix ODE solution depends smoothly on its finite-dimensional initial-state and parameter data on a common compact time interval ([[thm-smooth-dependence-of-ode-solutions-on-parameters]]).

[F5] For a smooth map $\mathcal F:X\times S\to Y$ transverse to an embedded submanifold $Z\subseteq Y$, the parameters whose slices $\mathcal F_s$ fail to be transverse to $Z$ form a null subset of $S$; this theorem assumes countable choice ([[thm-parametric-transversality]]).

[F6] Finite families of Euclidean smooth bumps, normalized by their positive sum, give smooth convex parameter weights and cutoffs separating disjoint compact subsets. The proof constructs them by the explicit scalar bump formula; it makes no appeal to a smooth-approximation theorem with a Fréchet target.

[F7] If a smooth map between finite-dimensional smooth manifolds has a regular value with nonempty fiber, its fiber is an embedded submanifold whose codimension is the target dimension ([[thm-a-regular-level-set-is-an-embedded-submanifold]]).

[F8] Under countable choice, critical values of a smooth map between finite-dimensional smooth manifolds form a null set ([[thm-morse-sard-for-smooth-manifolds]]).

[F9] A null subset of a positive-dimensional smooth parameter manifold has dense complement, and a finite union of null sets is null ([[prop-a-null-set-has-dense-complement-in-a-positive-dimensional-manifold]], [[prop-countable-unions-and-subsets-of-manifold-null-sets-are-null]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** global finite-dimensional parameter smoothing and holonomy-incidence avoidance.

1.1 If $K=\varnothing$ or $f(K)\subseteq\mathcal A_\infty^*$, the constant homotopy suffices. Otherwise realize the given finite simplicial complex by its barycentric coordinates as a compact subset of $\mathbb R^q$, taking $q\ge1$. Let $U=f^{-1}(\mathcal A_\infty^*)$, an open neighborhood of $L$ by [F2]. Choose nested relatively open neighborhoods $U_0,U_1,U_2$ of $L$ in $K$ with $\overline U_0\subset U_1$, $\overline U_1\subset U_2$, and $\overline U_2\subset U$; if $L=\varnothing$ take them empty. The compact family $f(\overline U_2)$ is uniformly stable under a sufficiently small $C^1$ perturbation: each member has the finite-loop positive margin in [F2], and a finite subcover gives one positive tolerance. Include this $C^1$ norm among the finitely many seminorms in which all forthcoming approximations are to be small. [A1, F1, F2, given, cases, construct]

2.1 Fix a small positive tolerance $\delta$ in each of those seminorms. Uniform continuity on compact $K$ gives a radius $\rho>0$ so that $f(s_i)-f(s)$ is smaller than $\delta$ in every selected seminorm when $s,s_i\in K$ and $|s-s_i|<2\rho$. Choose finitely many $s_i\in K$ with the balls $B_\rho(s_i)$ covering $K$. Let $\psi_i$ be nonnegative smooth ambient bumps supported in $B_{2\rho}(s_i)$ and positive on $B_\rho(s_i)$, constructed by translating and rescaling $\exp(-1/(1-|x|^2))$ on the unit ball and extending by zero. On the open neighborhood $V=\{\sum_i\psi_i>0\}$ of $K$, put $\lambda_i=\psi_i/\sum_j\psi_j$ and $g(s)=A_0+\sum_i\lambda_i(s)(f(s_i)-A_0)$. This is an ambient smooth finite-affine family of smooth connections. For $s\in K$, every contributing sample is within $2\rho$ of $s$, so $p(g(s)-f(s))<\delta$ for each selected seminorm $p$. This construction smooths the parameter dependence directly and simultaneously on the whole polyhedron; it changes no face independently of its cofaces. [F1, F6, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Choose a smooth ambient cutoff $0\le\theta\le1$ which is zero on a neighborhood of $\overline U_0$ and one on a neighborhood of the disjoint compact set $K\setminus U_1$. Such a cutoff follows from finitely many ambient bumps separating the two compact sets. Define on $K$ the continuous family $h(s)=f(s)+\theta(s)(g(s)-f(s))$. The straight interpolation from $f$ to $h$ is fixed near $L$ and is $\delta$-small in every selected seminorm. By the uniform $C^1$ margin in step 1.1, choosing $\delta$ small ensures it is irreducible on $\overline U_2$. Its closed reducible-parameter set $R=\{s\in K:h(s)\notin\mathcal A_\infty^*\}$ is compact and contained in $K\setminus U_2$, hence in the ambient open region where $\theta=1$ and $h=g$ on $K$. If $R$ is empty the first interpolation already proves the result. Otherwise choose an ambient open $W$ with compact closure in $V\cap\{\theta=1\}$, containing $R$ and avoiding a neighborhood of $L$, and a smooth cutoff $0\le\chi\le1$ with compact support $T\subset W$, positive on $R$. All subsequent holonomy calculations use the ambient family $g$ on $W$; on the actual parameter subset $K\cap W$ this is exactly $h$. [F1, F2, F6, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 Choose $r\ge2$ so that $2(r-1)(N-1)>q$, where $q$ is the ambient parameter dimension in step 1.1. In $M\setminus\{m\}\cong\mathbb R^d$ choose $r$ disjoint closed coordinate balls. Their exterior is path connected for $d=3$ or $4$: a polygonal path can be detoured along the connected boundary spheres of the finitely many balls, and a ray outside a large ball reaches $m$. For each ball join its boundary to $m$ through that exterior, enter the ball, run once around a small embedded circle, and return along the entering path. These are based piecewise-smooth loops $\ell_i$. Choose a closed arc on each small circle away from the entering point; it has a tube inside that ball meeting its own loop only on that arc and meeting no other loop. Thus each loop has a private control tube. The entering and returning stems are outside every private tube. [given, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 Let $U_i(s,t)$ be parallel transport for $g(s)$ from the chosen base frame to a point at parameter $t$ of $\ell_i$. It is smooth in $s\in W$ and in $t$ on each segment by [F4], applied on finitely many common intervals along the compact loop. In tube coordinates $(t,z)$ around its private arc, choose a bump $\rho_i(t)$ of integral one supported away from the arc ends, a transverse bump $c(z)$ with $c(0)=1$, and a real basis $X_a$ of $\mathfrak{su}(N)$. In a fixed smooth frame on the tube define the adjoint-valued form $b_{i,a}(s)=\rho_i(t)c(z)U_i(s,t)X_aU_i(s,t)^{-1}\,dt$, extending its coefficients constantly in the transverse coordinates before multiplying by $c$ and extending by zero outside the tube. It is a genuine smooth bundle-valued one-form depending smoothly on the ambient parameter. Along the loop its contraction is exactly the displayed transported Lie-algebra element. [F1, F4, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 For transport $U'=-B(t)U$ and a perturbation $b(t)$, differentiate the ODE to obtain $(U^{-1}\delta U)'=-U^{-1}bU$ and hence $U(1)^{-1}\delta U(1)=-\int U(t)^{-1}b(t)U(t)\,dt$. The control form in step 5.1 makes this last integral $-X_a$ for loop $i$. The other loop holonomies do not vary, since their images miss its tube. Therefore, for $D=r(N^2-1)$ parameters $w=(w_{i,a})$, the derivative at $w=0$ of $\Psi(s,w)=(\operatorname{Hol}_{\ell_i}(g(s)+\sum_{j,a}w_{j,a}b_{j,a}(s)))_{i=1}^r$ is an isomorphism in the $w$ variable onto $T SU(N)^r$, for every $s\in W$. Smooth ODE dependence and compactness of $T$ give a number $a>0$ on which this derivative remains an isomorphism uniformly for $s\in T$ and $|w|<a$. [F4, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Choose a ball $B\subset\mathbb R^D$ about zero of radius less than $a$ and put $\Phi(s,v)=\Psi(s,\chi(s)v)$ for $s\in W$. Where $\chi(s)>0$, its parameter derivative is $\chi(s)D_w\Psi(s,\chi(s)v)$, still invertible by step 6.1. For each $1\le k<N$, the map $\mathcal F_k:((W_+\times\operatorname{Gr}_{\mathbb C}(k,N))\times B)\to SU(N)^r\times\operatorname{Gr}_{\mathbb C}(k,N)$, $((s,Q),v)\mapsto(\Phi(s,v),Q)$, with $W_+=\{s\in W:\chi(s)>0\}$, is a submersion and thus transverse to $I_{k,r}$ of [F3]. Notice that the smooth source is the ambient open set $W_+$, not an individual simplex or a nonsmooth polyhedron. [F3, F4, step 3.1, step 6.1, construct]

8.1 By [F5], outside a null subset of $B$ the fixed-$v$ map from $W_+\times\operatorname{Gr}_{\mathbb C}(k,N)$ is transverse to $I_{k,r}$. Its source dimension is $q+2k(N-k)$, while that incidence manifold has codimension $2r k(N-k)$ by [F3]. Since $k(N-k)\ge N-1$ and $2(r-1)(N-1)>q$, the codimension is strictly larger than the source dimension. A transverse slice can therefore have no incidence preimage: a putative preimage would require a surjection from a smaller-dimensional tangent space to the normal space. [A1, F3, F5, step 4.1, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 Equivalently, [F7] applied in local incidence charts makes $Z_k=\mathcal F_k^{-1}(I_{k,r})$ a smooth manifold of dimension $q+D-2(r-1)k(N-k)<D$. Every point of its projection to $B$ is critical, so [F8] makes its image null. There are finitely many $k$, and [F9] makes the union of these images and the exceptional sets in step 8.1 null with dense complement. Thus arbitrarily small $v\in B$ avoid every incidence condition throughout $W_+$. This also directly verifies the transversality dimension argument by Sard. [A1, F3, F7, F8, F9, step 7.1, step 8.1, algebra]

10.1 Define on all of $K$ the final family $h_v(s)=h(s)+\chi(s)\sum_{j,a}v_{j,a}b_{j,a}(s)$, with the added term zero off $W$. The compact support $T$ and its spatial smoothness make this term continuous in every section seminorm, and its value uniformly small in any specified finite list by taking $v$ sufficiently small. If $\chi(s)>0$, then $s\in W$ and $h(s)=g(s)$, so $h_v(s)$ has the holonomy tuple $\Phi(s,v)$, which has no common invariant proper subspace by step 9.1. Therefore $h_v(s)$ is irreducible. If $\chi(s)=0$, then $s\notin R$ since $\chi$ is positive on $R$, so $h_v(s)=h(s)$ was already irreducible. The straight perturbation homotopy from $h$ to $h_v$ is fixed on an open neighborhood of $L$. Concatenate it with the interpolation in step 3.1. Choose $\delta$ and then $v$ within half of each requested seminorm tolerance; the triangle inequality gives the asserted bounds relative to the original $f$ for the entire concatenated homotopy. All formulas are global on $K$, so faces and cofaces agree automatically. [F1, F2, F9, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 9.1, construct, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The parameter transversality and direct Sard arguments are finite-dimensional. The proof uses holonomy on the standard representation and never assumes $\operatorname{Hol}_A=SU(N)$ for $N>2$.
