## Statement

Assume AC. Let $T=(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)^r$ act smoothly on a compact finite-dimensional Hausdorff second-countable smooth manifold $M$ without boundary, and let $F=M^T$. For $k\in\{\mathbb Q,\mathbb R\}$, define $H_T^*(X;k)=H^*(ET\times_T X;k)$ using the product of the right Milnor circle models, let $A_T(k)=H^*(BT;k)$, and let $\mathscr S_T(k)$ be the multiplicative set generated by the first Chern classes of all nontrivial smooth characters $T\to S^1$. Then restriction along $i:F\hookrightarrow M$ induces a graded-ring isomorphism
$$
i^*:\mathscr S_T(k)^{-1}H_T^*(M;k)\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\mathscr S_T(k)^{-1}H_T^*(F;k).
$$
The conclusion concerns compact finite-dimensional smooth $T$-manifolds and ordinary Borel singular cohomology; it makes no noncompact or compact-support assertion.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the compact smooth $T$-action, its fixed set $F$, and $k\in\{\mathbb Q,\mathbb R\}$.

[A1] Full AC includes the choice strength required by the product-torus coefficient, slice, and Thom suppliers. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] $F$ is a compact embedded submanifold with an invariant tubular disk, and its normal bundle has a componentwise $T$-invariant complex orientation with only nontrivial weights. [[lem-qftx-compact-torus-fixed-locus-normal-weights]] [[lem-qftx-compact-torus-equivariant-slice-neighborhoods]]

[F2] The Borel cohomology, coefficient ring, character multiplicative set, and module localization use the product Milnor model. [[def-qftx-character-localization]]

[F3] The localized cohomology of $M\setminus F$ is zero; localization preserves exact sequences of modules. [[lem-qftx-nonfixed-locus-cohomology-is-torsion-after-localization]] [[lem-qftx-module-localization-preserves-exactness]]

[F4] The localized Euler class of the Borel normal bundle over each component of $F$ is a unit. [[lem-qftx-fixed-normal-euler-class-is-unit-after-character-localization]]

[F5] The singular cohomology of a pair has its natural long exact sequence, excision holds for a subspace whose closure lies in the interior of the excised member, and homotopic maps induce the same cohomology map. [[thm-long-exact-sequence-of-a-pair-in-singular-cohomology]] [[thm-excision-for-singular-cohomology]] [[lem-qftx-singular-cohomology-homotopy-invariance]]

[F6] For an invariant pair $Z\subseteq Y$, write $H_T^*(Y,Z;k)=H^*(ET\times_TY,ET\times_TZ;k)$. The fixed-locus Borel base $BT\times F$ is paracompact Hausdorff of CW type; a locally trivial vector bundle there is numerable under AC by a subordinate partition of unity. The Thom theorem uses the closed disk/sphere pair specified by the metric-vector-bundle definition; an oriented numerable vector bundle over this base has a normalized Thom class and Thom isomorphism. The Euler class is its zero-section pullback and is natural under pullback. [[lem-qftx-borel-base-is-paracompact-of-cw-type]] [[thm-subordinate-partitions-of-unity-exist]] [[def-partition-of-unity-subordinate-to-a-cover]] [[def-disk-sphere-and-thom-space-of-a-metric-vector-bundle]] [[thm-thom-isomorphism-for-oriented-vector-bundles]] [[def-r-oriented-vector-bundle-and-orientation-local-system]] [[def-euler-class-by-zero-section-pullback-of-the-thom-class]] [[thm-naturality-orientation-sign-and-whitney-product-for-euler-classes]]

## Proof

**Given:** The data in the statement.

1.1 For any invariant pair $Z\subseteq Y$, use the relative Borel notation of [F6]. Put $X_T=ET\times_T M$ and $A_T=ET\times_T(M\setminus F)$. The fixed set is closed by [F1], so $M\setminus F$ is invariant and open; its Borel construction is open because the quotient map by the diagonal $T$-action is open. The pair long exact sequence [F5] has segment
$$
H_T^{q-1}(M\setminus F;k)\to H_T^q(M,M\setminus F;k)\to H_T^q(M;k)\to H_T^q(M\setminus F;k).
$$
By [F3], after localization both outside terms vanish in every degree, and the sequence remains exact. Hence the relative-to-absolute map
$$
\mathscr S_T(k)^{-1}H_T^*(M,M\setminus F;k)\longrightarrow\mathscr S_T(k)^{-1}H_T^*(M;k)
$$
is an isomorphism. [F3, F5, given, algebra]

2.1 Let $U\subset M$ be the invariant open tubular disk of radius $\epsilon$ around $F$ from [F1], and choose $0<\rho<\epsilon$. The Borel opens $ET\times_TU$ and $A_T$ cover $X_T$. Their intersection is $ET\times_T(U\setminus F)$, so excision [F5] identifies
$$
H_T^*(M,M\setminus F;k)\cong H^*(ET\times_TU,ET\times_T(U\setminus F);k).
$$
The equivariant tubular diffeomorphism identifies this pair with the Borel open disk bundle pair $(D(\nu_{F,T}),D(\nu_{F,T})\setminus F_T)$, where $F_T=BT\times F$. Let $S_\rho(\nu_{F,T})$ be the sphere bundle of radius $\rho$, which lies inside the open disk. On the punctured disk the radial formula $v\mapsto ((1-t)\lVert v\rVert+t\rho)v/\lVert v\rVert$, for $0\leq t\leq1$, stays inside the disk because both endpoint radii are less than $\epsilon$; it gives a continuous equivariant deformation retraction onto $S_\rho(\nu_{F,T})$. Thus the inclusion of pairs $(D(\nu_{F,T}),S_\rho(\nu_{F,T}))\to(D(\nu_{F,T}),D(\nu_{F,T})\setminus F_T)$ induces a cohomology isomorphism: in the long exact pair sequences the absolute disk terms agree and the sphere-to-punctured-disk terms are isomorphisms by [F5]. Let $\overline D_\rho(\nu_{F,T})$ be the closed subdisk bundle of radius $\rho$. The inclusion of pairs $(\overline D_\rho,S_\rho)\hookrightarrow(D(\nu_{F,T}),S_\rho)$ is a homotopy equivalence: the equivariant radial clamp $R_t(0)=0$, $R_t(v)=((1-t)\lVert v\rVert+t\min\{\lVert v\rVert,\rho\})v/\lVert v\rVert$ for $v\ne0$, stays inside the open disk and fixes $\overline D_\rho$, including $S_\rho$. Rescaling identifies $(\overline D_\rho,S_\rho)$ with the unit closed disk/sphere pair in the definition of the Thom isomorphism. Hence [F6] identifies these relative groups componentwise with $H_T^{*-c_j}(F_j;k)$, where $F_j$ ranges over the finitely many components and $c_j=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb R}\nu_{F_j}$. Composing this identification with relative-to-absolute defines the Gysin map $i_*^T$. Pulling its tubular Thom representative back to the zero section gives
$$
i^*i_*^T(\beta)=e_T(\nu_F)\smile\beta
$$
on every component, by the zero-section definition of the Euler class and naturality in [F6]. [A1, F1, F5, F6, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Let $\gamma\in\mathscr S_T(k)^{-1}H_T^n(F;k)$. The finite component decomposition writes $\gamma=(\gamma_j)$. By [F4], $e_j=e_T(\nu_{F_j})$ has a localized inverse on every component. Define $\beta_j=e_j^{-1}\gamma_j$, which has degree $n-c_j$; the componentwise Thom isomorphism in step 2.1 combines the $\beta_j$ into a relative class, and its Gysin image satisfies $i^*i_*^T(\beta)=\gamma$. Therefore $i^*$ is surjective after localization. [F4, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Suppose $\alpha\in\mathscr S_T(k)^{-1}H_T^n(M;k)$ satisfies $i^*\alpha=0$. Step 1.1 writes $\alpha=i_*^T(\beta)$ for a relative class $\beta=(\beta_j)$. Step 2.1 gives $0=i^*\alpha=(e_j\beta_j)_j$; since every $e_j$ is a unit by [F4], each $\beta_j=0$, so $\alpha=0$. This proves injectivity. If $F=\varnothing$, step 1.1 and [F3] give zero localized cohomology on both sides. If $T$ is trivial, then $F=M$, $\mathscr S_T(k)=\{1\}$, the normal bundle has rank zero, and restriction is the identity. Empty $M$ is included in these cases. Hence the map in the Statement is an isomorphism. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1] ∎
