The Measure Theory pages develop measurable spaces, sigma-algebras, measures, and integration. They prove generation and extension results, construct Lebesgue measure, and treat measurable functions, simple approximation, convergence theorems, product measures, and Fubini–Tonelli. Further topics include signed and complex measures, Radon–Nikodym and Lebesgue decomposition, Lebesgue–Stieltjes measures, Radon representation, Hausdorff measure, differentiation and maximal functions, and the relation between Lebesgue and Riemann integration. The collection also develops Lp spaces and duality, modes of convergence, measure-preserving systems, recurrence, mixing, and the ergodic theorems of von Neumann and Birkhoff. It also treats nonmeasurable sets, choice assumptions, and the cardinalities of sigma-algebras.

Real Analysis supplies the main completeness and integration foundations, alongside results from Topology, Set Theory, Linear Algebra, Abstract Algebra, and Complex Analysis. Partial Differential Equations, Fourier Analysis, Probability, Functional Analysis, Complex Analysis, Mathematical Statistics, and Representation Theory build on the collection's integration, convergence, and Lp results in their own arguments, including work on operators, random laws, and ergodic theory.
