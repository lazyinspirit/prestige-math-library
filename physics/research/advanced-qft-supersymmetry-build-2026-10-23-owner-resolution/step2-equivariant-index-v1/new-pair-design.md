## Sign and support conventions

The generic complex Koszul symbol uses exterior powers of W and exterior multiplication by the tautological vector plus its adjoint. Its even-to-odd map on a complex line is z. This matches the existing root Bott supplier's sign, whereas exterior powers of W-dual with contraction plus adjoint would give bar(z).

For the doubled real normal bundle, the complex structure is J(v,w)=(-w,v). The metric extends complex-bilinearly to an equivariant isomorphism between the complexified real normal and its complex dual. Therefore its exterior-power zero-section class can still be written lambda-minus-one of the complexified normal dual. No such complex-linear identification is claimed for an arbitrary Hermitian complex bundle.

A Koszul symbol over a noncompact base is only supported properly over that base. It becomes compactly supported after multiplication by a compact-support symbol. Extension from a tubular neighborhood and the corresponding self-intersection identity require proof and are not definitions of acceptance.

The RR proof owner is fixing cotangent orientation explicitly. With dtheta=sum dxi wedge dx it has no additional (-1)^dim F; using Gilkey's dx wedge dxi orientation moves that sign into the formula. The analytic comparison must use the identical Bott and normal convention.
