## Statement

Assume ZF together with the Axiom of Choice. Let $M$ be $S^3$ or $S^4$ with its compact, connected, oriented, boundaryless smooth structure, let $N\ge2$, and let $P\to M$ be a smooth principal right $SU(N)$-bundle with a smooth reference connection $A_0$. Fix a basepoint $m\in M$ and a frame of $P_m$. Equip $\mathcal A_\infty=A_0+\Omega^1(M;\operatorname{ad}P)$ with the Fréchet topology generated by suprema of all derivatives of section coefficients in a fixed finite bundle atlas. Let $\mathcal A_\infty^*$ consist of connections whose based holonomy action on $\mathbb C^N$ has no nonzero proper invariant complex subspace. Let $K$ be the geometric realization of a finite simplicial complex, let $L$ be the realization of a subcomplex, and let $f:K\to\mathcal A_\infty$ be continuous with $f(L)\subseteq\mathcal A_\infty^*$. Then there is a continuous homotopy $H:K\times[0,1]\to\mathcal A_\infty$, fixed on $L$ and on an open neighborhood of $L$, such that $H(\cdot,1)$ maps all of $K$ into $\mathcal A_\infty^*$. For every finite list of these $C^\infty$ section seminorms $p_1,\ldots,p_m$ and positive tolerances $\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_m$, the homotopy can be chosen with $\sup_{(s,t)\in K\times[0,1]}p_j(H(s,t)-f(s))<\epsilon_j$ for every $j$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** ZF and the Axiom of Choice; the fixed bundle and affine connection space in the statement; a finite polyhedron $K$; a subpolyhedron $L$; and a continuous map $f$ whose restriction to $L$ is irreducible.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice means every family of nonempty sets has a choice function ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). In particular it implies countable choice, the weaker set-theoretic assumption used by the finite-dimensional approximation, transversality and Sard results below ([[def-countable-choice]]).

[F1] The batch-56 configuration definition identifies $\mathcal A_\infty$ with $A_0+\Omega^1(M;\operatorname{ad}P)$, uses the finite-atlas $C^\infty$ section topology, and defines irreducibility by the based holonomy action on $\mathbb C^N$. Its complete definition was inspected and its current Step 1 record is ready; the proof uses exactly that interface ([[def-qftx-smooth-sobolev-gauge-configuration]]).

[F2] The finite-holonomy lemma states that $\mathcal A_\infty^*$ is open and supplies finite based loops witnessing each irreducible connection. Its complete authored proof and exact batch-56 inputs were inspected; the configuration and stabilizer suppliers now have current ready Step 1 records ([[lem-qftx-irreducible-finite-holonomy-witness]]).

[F3] The incidence manifold of $r$ holonomies preserving a $k$-plane has codimension $2r k(N-k)$ in $SU(N)^r\times\operatorname{Gr}_{\mathbb C}(k,N)$; the dimension deficit after including the plane variable is $2(r-1)k(N-k)$ ([[lem-qftx-holonomy-incidence-codimension]]).

[F4] A jointly smooth matrix ODE solution depends smoothly on its finite-dimensional initial-state and parameter data on a common compact time interval ([[thm-smooth-dependence-of-ode-solutions-on-parameters]]).

[F5] For a smooth map $\mathcal F:X\times S\to Y$ transverse to an embedded submanifold $Z\subseteq Y$, the parameters whose slices $\mathcal F_s$ fail to be transverse to $Z$ form a null subset of $S$; this theorem assumes countable choice ([[thm-parametric-transversality]]).

[F6] Every continuous map between finite-dimensional smooth manifolds is homotopic to a smooth map under countable choice ([[cor-every-continuous-map-between-smooth-manifolds-is-homotopic-to-a-smooth-map]]). Its target is finite-dimensional; this fact is used only for the finite-dimensional coefficient map, never for a map into the Fréchet space $\mathcal A_\infty$.

[F7] If a smooth map between finite-dimensional smooth manifolds has a regular value with nonempty fiber, its fiber is an embedded submanifold whose codimension is the target dimension ([[thm-a-regular-level-set-is-an-embedded-submanifold]]).

[F8] Under countable choice, critical values of a smooth map between finite-dimensional smooth manifolds form a null set ([[thm-morse-sard-for-smooth-manifolds]]).

[F9] A null subset of a positive-dimensional smooth parameter manifold has dense complement, and a finite union of null sets is null ([[prop-a-null-set-has-dense-complement-in-a-positive-dimensional-manifold]], [[prop-countable-unions-and-subsets-of-manifold-null-sets-are-null]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** finite-parameter avoidance of the finite holonomy incidence manifolds.

1.1 If $K=\varnothing$ or $f(K)\subseteq\mathcal A_\infty^*$, the constant homotopy works. Otherwise [F2] and continuity make $f^{-1}(\mathcal A_\infty^*)$ an open neighborhood of $L$. Since $K$ is a compact metrizable polyhedron, choose an open neighborhood $V$ of $L$ whose closure is contained in this preimage; after subdivision, keep a smaller subcomplex neighborhood of $L$ fixed. Work through the remaining simplices in increasing dimension, so at each positive-dimensional simplex its boundary has already been mapped into $\mathcal A_\infty^*$. A zero-cell is itself a constant smooth parameter family and needs no boundary collar. [F1, F2, given, cases, construct]

2.1 For a positive-dimensional simplex $\sigma$ being treated, compactness of $\partial\sigma$ and openness in [F2] give a boundary collar $C$ whose current image is irreducible. Choose nested compact cores $Q\subset\operatorname{int}Q'\subset\operatorname{int}\sigma$ with $\sigma\setminus Q\subset C$, and subdivide finely enough that the current map varies by less than any prescribed finite list of seminorm tolerances on every simplex. Cover $Q'$ by finitely many small coordinate balls $U_i$ and choose $s_i\in U_i$ and sample connections $A_i=f_\sigma(s_i)$; uniform continuity and small ball diameters ensure $p_j(A_i-f_\sigma(s))<\delta$ whenever $s\in U_i$. A continuous partition $\lambda_i$ subordinate to this cover is obtained by normalizing the distance-to-complement functions. To smooth its finite-dimensional coefficient map with controlled error, cover $Q'$ by smaller balls $W_a$ on which every $\lambda_i$ oscillates by less than $\eta$, choose sample points $s_a\in W_a$, and choose smooth nonnegative bumps $\psi_a$ supported in $W_a$ and positive on a covering by still smaller balls. Such bumps are obtained in coordinates by scaling and translating $b(x)=\exp(-1/(1-\lVert x\rVert^2))$ for $\lVert x\rVert<1$, extended by zero outside the unit ball. Normalize the bumps by their positive sum. Then $\widetilde\lambda_i(s)=\sum_a\psi_a(s)\lambda_i(s_a)$ is smooth, has nonnegative entries summing to one, and differs from $\lambda_i(s)$ by less than $\eta$; whenever $\widetilde\lambda_i(s)>0$, some $s_a$ lies within the chosen small radius of $s$ and has $\lambda_i(s_a)>0$, so $s_a\in U_i$ and uniform continuity makes $A_i$ close to $f_\sigma(s)$ as well. Thus every index with positive coefficient in either $\lambda$ or $\widetilde\lambda$ uses a sample with $p_j(A_i-f_\sigma(s))<\delta$, and the convex coefficient homotopy has the same bound by the seminorm triangle inequality. This is a controlled finite-dimensional smoothing instance of [F6]; its target is the affine hyperplane $\sum_i c_i=1$, never the Fréchet space of connections. Consequently $g(s)=\sum_i\widetilde\lambda_i(s)A_i$ is smooth and finite-affine on a neighborhood of $Q'$, and it and the coefficient homotopy are uniformly small in all prescribed seminorms. [A1, F1, F2, F6, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Choose a smooth cutoff $\theta$ equal to one on $Q$ and supported in $\operatorname{int}Q'$, and replace the current map $f_\sigma$ on $\sigma$ by $h(s)=f_\sigma(s)+\theta(s)(g(s)-f_\sigma(s))$. On the transition region $Q'\setminus Q\subset C$, a finite subcover of the compact good collar and openness in [F2] give one $C^1$ tolerance that keeps every straight interpolation irreducible. Outside $Q'$ the map is unchanged and irreducible; on $Q$ it equals the smooth map $g$. Its closed reducible set is therefore contained in $\operatorname{int}Q$: the boundary of $Q$ lies in the good collar, and compactness plus openness give a neighborhood of $\partial Q$ on which the interpolation remains irreducible. Thus $h$ is smooth on a neighborhood of its reducible set. Applying the cutoff to the straight coefficient homotopy from $\lambda$ to $\widetilde\lambda$, then concatenating with the cutoff interpolation to $g$, gives a small homotopy fixed on a boundary collar. [F2, F6, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 Let $R$ be the closed reducible-parameter set of the map just obtained. It is compact, contained in the smooth interior region, and disjoint from the good collar. Choose a smooth cutoff $\chi$ with $0\le\chi\le1$, supported in that smooth region and positive on $R$. Put $q=\dim K$, and choose $r\ge2$ with $2(r-1)(N-1)>q$. Fix the basepoint $m$ and identify $M\setminus\{m\}$ with $\mathbb R^d$, where $d=3$ or $4$. Choose $r$ disjoint closed coordinate balls in this chart. The complement of their interiors is path connected: polygonal paths in $\mathbb R^d$ can be detoured along the connected boundary spheres of the balls, and a ray outside a large ball reaches the point at infinity $m$. Use a common stem from $m$ to the complement and, for each ball, a separate out-and-back path to a small loop $\gamma_i$ inside it. Arrange a private control arc for $\gamma_i$ inside its ball. The resulting based piecewise-smooth loops $\ell_i$ have control arcs whose small tubular neighborhoods meet no other loop. [given, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 Along the private control arc of $\ell_i$, let $U_i(s,t)$ denote parallel transport for the smooth family $g(s)$ from the basepoint to parameter $t$. Choose a smooth bump $\rho_i$ supported in the arc with integral one and a real basis $X_1,\ldots,X_{N^2-1}$ of $\mathfrak{su}(N)$. In a smooth trivialization of its tube, choose an $\operatorname{ad}P$-valued one-form $b_{i,a}(s)$ whose contraction with the path tangent is $\rho_i(t)U_i(s,t)X_aU_i(s,t)^{-1}$, and extend it by a spatial bump. For the transport equation $U'=-B(t)U$, variation gives
$$U(1)^{-1}\left.\frac{\partial U_\varepsilon(1)}{\partial\varepsilon}\right|_{0}=-\int_0^1U(t)^{-1}b(t)U(t)\,dt.$$
The displayed choice makes this integral $-X_a$, so the variation of the $i$th holonomy spans its full left-translated tangent space. No other loop holonomy varies, since the other loops miss this support tube. Therefore the combined derivative in the $r(N^2-1)$ real perturbation parameters is an isomorphism onto $T SU(N)^r$. [F4, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 The ODE dependence theorem [F4] makes the holonomy tuple smooth in $s$ and the perturbation parameters on each smooth loop segment, and finite composition gives smoothness for each complete loop. On the compact support of $\chi$, the unscaled parameter derivative at zero is everywhere an isomorphism by step 5.1. Continuity and compactness give a ball $B\subset\mathbb R^{r(N^2-1)}$ about zero on which, for each $s$ with $\chi(s)>0$, the holonomy tuple
$$\Phi(s,v)=\left(\operatorname{Hol}_{\ell_i}\left(g(s)+\chi(s)\sum_{j,a}v_{j,a}b_{j,a}(s)\right)\right)_{i=1}^r$$
has surjective parameter derivative: factor the derivative as $\chi(s)$ times the derivative for the unscaled perturbation, whose rank remains full for all sufficiently small $v$ uniformly on the compact support. For each $1\le k<N$, the map $((s,P),v)\mapsto(\Phi(s,v),P)$ from $(U_+\times\operatorname{Gr}_{\mathbb C}(k,N))\times B$ to $SU(N)^r\times\operatorname{Gr}_{\mathbb C}(k,N)$, with $U_+=\{s:\chi(s)>0\}$, is consequently a submersion and is transverse to $I_{k,r}$. [F3, F4, step 5.1, construct]

7.1 For every $1\le k<N$, one has $k(N-k)\ge N-1$. The choice of $r$ in step 4.1 therefore gives
$$2r k(N-k)>q+2k(N-k)\ge \dim(U_+\times\operatorname{Gr}_{\mathbb C}(k,N)).$$
By [A1, F5], outside a null subset of $B$ the slice map is transverse to $I_{k,r}$. Such a slice cannot meet $I_{k,r}$: at a putative preimage its derivative would have to surject onto a normal space of dimension $2r k(N-k)$ from a domain of strictly smaller dimension. [A1, F3, F5, given, step 4.1, step 6.1, algebra]

7.2 The incidence-preimage dimension gives an independent Sard check. Near each point of $I_{k,r}$, an embedded-submanifold chart expresses it as the common zero set of $c=2r k(N-k)$ coordinates whose differential is onto. Composing those coordinates with the submersion in step 6.1 is still a submersion, so zero is a regular value; [F7] gives $W_k=\mathcal F_k^{-1}(I_{k,r})$ as a smooth submanifold of codimension $c$. Its dimension is at most $q+r(N^2-1)-2(r-1)k(N-k)<r(N^2-1)=\dim B$. [F3, F7, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 Every point of the smooth projection $W_k\to B$ is critical because $\dim W_k<\dim B$, so [A1, F8] makes its image null; any parameter with a slice meeting $I_{k,r}$ lies in that image. There are finitely many $k$, so [F9] makes the union of these incidence images and the exceptional sets from step 7.1 null. Since $B$ has positive dimension, its complement is dense and supplies arbitrarily small parameters outside that union. [A1, F8, F9, step 7.1, step 7.2, algebra]

9.1 On the compact support of $\chi$, each smooth family $b_{j,a}(s)$ has bounded value in every one of the finitely many specified section seminorms. Choose such a parameter $v$ small enough that $\sup_s p_\ell(\chi(s)\sum_{j,a}v_{j,a}b_{j,a}(s))<\epsilon_\ell$ after assigning its share of the total homotopy tolerance. If $\chi(s)>0$, the tuple of loop holonomies has no common proper nonzero invariant complex subspace, for otherwise it would lie in some $I_{k,r}$; hence the perturbed connection is irreducible. If $\chi(s)=0$, then $s\notin R$ because $\chi$ is positive on $R$, so its connection was already irreducible. Thus the endpoint lies in $\mathcal A_\infty^*$. The straight perturbation homotopy is fixed on the good collar. [F1, step 4.1, step 8.1, construct]

10.1 The perturbations are supported in simplex interiors, so they agree with the previously treated faces. Finite induction over the simplices glues the homotopies to one on $K$, fixed on $L$ and on an open neighborhood of $L$. Each stage can be chosen within any assigned finite seminorm tolerances; divide each requested bound among the finitely many stages to keep the concatenated homotopy inside the original specified $C^\infty$ neighborhood of $f$. This proves the assertion. [given, step 1.1, step 9.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The parameter transversality and direct Sard arguments are finite-dimensional. The proof uses holonomy on the standard representation and never assumes $\operatorname{Hol}_A=SU(N)$ for $N>2$.
