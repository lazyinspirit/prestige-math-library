## Definition

Give $\mathbb R^4$ its Euclidean topology, whose basic open sets are the
Euclidean open balls, and write a point as an ordered pair $(x,y)$ with
$x,y\in\mathbb R^2$. The **ordered configuration space** is the subspace
$$F_2(\mathbb R^2):=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^2:x\ne y\}$$
with the subspace topology. The transposition acts by $(x,y)\mapsto(y,x)$.
The **unordered configuration space of two distinct planar points** is
$$C_2(\mathbb R^2):=F_2(\mathbb R^2)/\bigl((x,y)\sim(y,x)\bigr)$$
with the quotient topology of the canonical projection
$q:F_2(\mathbb R^2)\to C_2(\mathbb R^2)$. We write its points as unordered
sets $\{x,y\}$; equality in this set means equality up to transposition.
The diagonal $x=y$ is excluded, so collisions are not points of this space.

The topology on each finite Cartesian coordinate space used below is its
Euclidean topology; on a finite product it is equivalently the topology with
basis the products of open sets in the factors.

claim about a physical particle species, exchange statistics, or a realized
holonomy.

## Example

On $\mathbb C^2$, define the Pauli matrices
$$
\sigma_x=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad \sigma_y=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{pmatrix},\quad \sigma_z=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The six effects $F_{j,\pm}=(I\pm\sigma_j)/6$, with $j\in\{x,y,z\}$, form an informationally complete POVM. For every qubit density matrix $\rho=(I+r_x\sigma_x+r_y\sigma_y+r_z\sigma_z)/2$, the exact outcome probabilities satisfy $r_j=3(p_{j,+}-p_{j,-})$ and the canonical linear-inversion procedure returns $\rho$. A valid finite frequency vector can instead yield a nonpositive output.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The six displayed matrices and a density matrix $\rho$ on $\mathbb C^2$.

[A1] The Pauli matrices and effects are the finite matrices and operators in the Example.

[A2] $\rho$ is Hermitian, positive semidefinite, and has trace one.

[F1] [[thm-nrqmx-tomography-identifiability-stability]]: for a spanning finite POVM, exact probabilities reconstruct $\rho$, and the linear-inversion output is $\sum_af_aD_a$ for its canonical dual.

[F2] [[lem-nrqmx-frame-pseudoinverse-reconstruction]]: for a positive semidefinite Gram matrix with spectral projections $P_\lambda$ at positive eigenvalues, $G^+=\sum_{\lambda>0}\lambda^{-1}P_\lambda$.

[F3] [[def-nrqmx-finite-hermitian-frame-and-gram-pseudoinverse]]: $G_{ab}=\operatorname{Tr}(F_aF_b)$ and $D_a=\sum_b(G^+)_{ba}F_b$.

## Verification


1.1 Direct multiplication gives $\sigma_j^2=I$, $\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_j)=0$, $\sigma_x\sigma_y=i\sigma_z$, $\sigma_y\sigma_z=i\sigma_x$, and $\sigma_z\sigma_x=i\sigma_y$; reversing the order negates each mixed product. Thus $\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_j\sigma_k)=2\delta_{jk}$. Since a Hermitian $2\times2$ matrix has two real diagonal entries and one complex off-diagonal entry, $\dim_{\mathbb R}\mathsf H_2=4$; the four nonzero orthogonal matrices $I,\sigma_x,\sigma_y,\sigma_z$ form a basis. Every trace-one Hermitian $\rho$ therefore has the stated form with $r_j=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\sigma_j)\in\mathbb R$. Positivity [A2] restricts $|r|\le1$, since $(r_x\sigma_x+r_y\sigma_y+r_z\sigma_z)^2=|r|^2I$ and the eigenvalues of $\rho$ are $(1\pm|r|)/2$. [A1, A2, algebra]

2.1 Each $\sigma_j$ is Hermitian with eigenvalues $1,-1$ by step 1.1, so $F_{j,\pm}$ has eigenvalues $1/3,0$ and is positive. For each $j$, $F_{j,+}+F_{j,-}=I/3$; summing over $x,y,z$ gives $I$. Also $F_{j,+}-F_{j,-}=\sigma_j/3$, so each $\sigma_j$ lies in the real span of the effects. Since $I$ is their sum, the basis in step 1.1 shows that the effects span $\mathsf H_2$. [A1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 In the coordinate order $(x+,x-,y+,y-,z+,z-)$, direct multiplication and step 1.1 give $G_{(j,s),(k,t)}=\frac1{36}\operatorname{Tr}((I+s\sigma_j)(I+t\sigma_k))=\frac1{18}(1+st\,\delta_{jk})$ for $s,t\in\{+1,-1\}$. Thus the diagonal entries are $1/9$, opposite outcomes on one axis have entry $0$, and distinct axes have entry $1/18$. Let $u=(1,1,1,1,1,1)$ and $v_j=e_{j+}-e_{j-}$. Then $Gu=u/3$, $Gv_j=v_j/9$, and $G$ vanishes on vectors $(s_x,s_x,s_y,s_y,s_z,s_z)$ with $s_x+s_y+s_z=0$. These mutually orthogonal subspaces have dimensions $1,3,2$ and span $\mathbb R^6$. [A1, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 By [F2] and the eigenspaces in step 3.1, $G^+=3P_u+9\sum_jP_{v_j}$, where $P_u=uu^\mathsf T/6$ and $P_{v_j}=v_jv_j^\mathsf T/2$. The $e_{j+}$ column of $G^+$ is $u/2+9v_j/2$. Applying the canonical-dual formula [F3], using $\sum_{k,\pm}F_{k,\pm}=I$ and $F_{j,+}-F_{j,-}=\sigma_j/3$, gives $D_{j,+}=\frac12I+\frac32\sigma_j$; similarly $D_{j,-}=\frac12I-\frac32\sigma_j$. The probabilities are $p_{j,\pm}=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho F_{j,\pm})=(1\pm r_j)/6$, so $\sum_{j,\pm}p_{j,\pm}D_{j,\pm}=\frac12I+\frac12\sum_jr_j\sigma_j=\rho$, in agreement with [F1]. [A1, A2, F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 The frequency vector with $f_{x,+}=1$ and all other entries zero is nonnegative and sums to one. Its output is $D_{x,+}=\frac12I+\frac32\sigma_x$, whose eigenvalues are $2$ and $-1$. It is Hermitian but not positive, so the linear-inversion procedure does not preserve positivity for every frequency vector. [A1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎

## Statement

Fix a positive real action scale $\hbar>0$ and a real squeeze parameter
$s\ne0$. For each finite $N\ge1$, apply independently to $N$ vacuum modes
the one-mode dilation $[U_sf](x)=e^{-s/2}f(e^{-s}x)$. The
resulting finite-mode unitary exists, its mean total number is
$$N\sinh^2(s),$$
and the magnitude of its overlap with the $N$-mode vacuum is
$$|\langle\Omega_N,U_s^{\otimes N}\Omega_N\rangle| =(\cosh s)^{-N/2}\longrightarrow0.$$
These exact finite-$N$ calculations exhibit quantities that are not uniform
in mode number. They do not prove an infinite-mode non-implementability
theorem and do not assign a limiting density operator or measurement.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A positive action scale $\hbar>0$, a nonzero real squeeze
parameter $s$, and a finite integer $N\ge1$.

[A1] The action scale is positive, $\hbar>0$, the squeeze is fixed with
$s\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}$, and the mode count is a finite integer
$N\ge1$.

[F1] For $u=\cosh s$, $v=\sinh s$, the finite-mode Bogoliubov theorem
supplies an implementer on every fixed finite product of $N$ modes
([[thm-nrqmx-finite-mode-bogoliubov]]).

[F2] The finite-mode Bogoliubov theorem supplies a unitary on every fixed
finite number $N$ of modes; it asserts nothing about an infinite collection
([[thm-nrqmx-finite-mode-bogoliubov]]).

[F3] In Schrödinger coordinates, $Q=\sqrt\hbar x$,
$P=-i\sqrt\hbar\partial_x$, and
$a=(Q+iP)/\sqrt{2\hbar}$; the vacuum is the normalized ground Hermite
function ([[def-nrqmx-finite-mode-symplectic-data]],
[[lem-nrqmx-finite-mode-fock-schr-identification]]).

[F4] For $A>0$ and $B\in\mathbb R$,
$$\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-Ax^2+iBx}\,dx=\sqrt{\frac\pi A}e^{-B^2/(4A)}$$
by scaling the imported $2\pi$-frequency Gaussian transform; finite-interval
integration by parts and exponential domination justify the Gaussian second
moment ([[lem-euclidean-gaussian-fourier-transform-with-two-pi-normalization]],
[[lem-complex-integration-by-parts-on-intervals-and-decaying-lines]],
[[thm-exponential-beats-every-polynomial]]).

[F5] In every finite dimension $n$, Lebesgue substitution applies to the
$C^1$ dilation $\mathbf x\mapsto e^{-s}\mathbf x$ and every $L^1$ integrand;
in particular this covers $|f|^2$ in one mode, $x^2|U_sg_0|^2$, and the
$N$-coordinate product dilation ([[cor-c-one-change-of-variables-for-l-one-functions]]).

## Proof

1.1 Apply [F5] to $x\mapsto e^{-s}x$ and $|f|^2$ to get
$$\int_{\mathbb R}|U_sf(x)|^2dx =\int_{\mathbb R}|f(y)|^2dy,$$
so $U_s$ is unitary. The chain rule for $Q=\sqrt\hbar x$ and
$P=-i\sqrt\hbar\partial_x$ gives
$U_s^*aU_s=\cosh(s)a+\sinh(s)a^*$. The scalar pair obeys
$|u|^2-|v|^2=1$ and $uv=vu$. For the $N$-mode product take
$U_N=uI_N$ and $V_N=vI_N$; the finite Bogoliubov result [F1] supplies an
implementer for these coefficients. Write
$\psi_s(x)=U_sg_0(x)=e^{-s/2}\pi^{-1/4}e^{-e^{-2s}x^2/2}$. Then
$$a\psi_s=\frac{1-e^{-2s}}{\sqrt2}x\psi_s.$$
By [F4] with $A=1,B=0$, $\int e^{-y^2}dy=\sqrt\pi$. On $[-R,R]$,
integration by parts gives
$$\int_{-R}^R y^2e^{-y^2}dy=\left[-\frac y2e^{-y^2}\right]_{-R}^R+\frac12\int_{-R}^Re^{-y^2}dy.$$
The boundary term tends to zero by [F4], so
$\int y^2|g_0(y)|^2dy=1/2$. The substitution $y=e^{-s}x$ in [F5] gives
$$\int_{\mathbb R}x^2|\psi_s(x)|^2dx=e^{2s}\int_{\mathbb R}y^2|g_0(y)|^2dy=e^{2s}/2.$$
Consequently
$$\|a\psi_s\|^2=\frac{(1-e^{-2s})^2}{2}\frac{e^{2s}}2=\sinh^2(s).$$
By [F3], the $N$-mode Fock–Schrödinger unitary sends its vacuum
$\Omega_N$ to $g_0^{\otimes N}$. On $L^2(\mathbb R^N)$ define
$$[U_s^{(N)}f](x_1,\ldots,x_N)=e^{-Ns/2} f(e^{-s}x_1,\ldots,e^{-s}x_N).$$
The $C^1$ change-of-variables formula [F5] in $\mathbb R^N$ gives
$\|U_s^{(N)}f\|_2=\|f\|_2$, so this is unitary. It is the
product of the one-mode dilations, and the coordinatewise chain rule gives
$U_s^{(N)*}a_jU_s^{(N)}=u a_j+v a_j^*$ for each $j$. The finite Bogoliubov
statement [F1] also supplies an implementer for this coefficient pair. Put
$\Psi_N=(U_s\Omega)^{\otimes N}$ under the same identification; its $j$th
number expectation is the one-mode value $\|a\psi_s\|^2=\sinh^2(s)$ already
computed above. The number operator is the sum of the $N$ mode number
operators, so linearity gives
$$\langle\Psi_N,\mathcal N_N\Psi_N\rangle=N\sinh^2(s).$$
The product unitary exists because it is the tensor product of finitely many
copies of this unitary. The same coefficient pair is covered by the finite Bogoliubov
statement [F2]; no infinite product is taken. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5,
algebra]

2.1 In Schrödinger coordinates, $\Omega=g_0$ and
$U_sg_0(x)=e^{-s/2}\pi^{-1/4}e^{-e^{-2s}x^2/2}$. Set
$A=(1+e^{-2s})/2>0$. By [F4] with $B=0$,
$$\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-Ax^2}dx=\sqrt{\pi/A}=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{1+e^{-2s}}}.$$
Hence
$$\langle g_0,U_sg_0\rangle=\pi^{-1/2}e^{-s/2}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{1+e^{-2s}}}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{e^s+e^{-s}}}=\frac1{\sqrt{\cosh s}}.$$
Since $U_s$ and $g_0$ are real and positive here, this is also the overlap
magnitude; the formula holds for every real $s$. Product inner products
factor over the $N$ modes, yielding
$$|\langle\Omega_N,\Psi_N\rangle|=(\cosh s)^{-N/2}.$$
For $s\ne0$, $\cosh s>1$, so this magnitude tends to zero as $N\to\infty$.
Together with step 1.1 this proves only the stated finite-$N$ divergence and
overlap limit. Finite-dimensional implementation does not itself provide an
infinite-mode implementer, and this example does not prove that none exists.
[A1, F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, algebra] ∎

## Statement

Assume Countable Choice and Dependent Choice, $m,\hbar>0$, and real $q,B$
with $qB\ne0$. On
$L^2(\mathbb R^2;\mathbb C)$ close the nonnegative form
$$q_B[u]=\frac1{2m}\left(\|-i\hbar\partial_xu\|_2^2+\|(-i\hbar\partial_y-qBx)u\|_2^2\right),\qquad u\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^2;\mathbb C).$$
Put $\omega_c=|qB|/m$,
$E_n=\hbar\omega_c(n+\tfrac12)$, and let $(\psi_n)_{n\ge0}$ be the
complete normalized eigenfunctions of the one-dimensional oscillator with
mass $m$, frequency $\omega_c$, and action constant $\hbar$ in its exact
self-adjoint oscillator realization. For
Schwartz $u$ define
$\mathcal F_yu(x,k)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-iky}u(x,y)\,dy$,
and use its unitary $L^2$ extension. Put $x_0(k)=\hbar k/(qB)$ and
$(Uu)(\xi,k)=\mathcal F_yu(\xi+x_0(k),k)$.
Then $U$ is unitary from $L^2(\mathbb R^2_{x,y})$ onto
$L^2(\mathbb R^2_{\xi,k})$. Writing
$$Uu(\xi,k)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty f_n(k)\psi_n(\xi),\qquad f_n\in L^2(\mathbb R_k),$$
the form closure domain and value are exactly
$$D(\overline q_B)=\left\{u:\sum_{n=0}^\infty E_n\|f_n\|_{L^2(k)}^2<\infty\right\},\qquad \overline q_B[u]=\sum_{n=0}^\infty E_n\|f_n\|_{L^2(k)}^2.$$
Thus the form is the diagonal oscillator form with an unrestricted $L^2(k)$
degeneracy factor; it asserts no finite-area state count.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice and Dependent Choice, $m,\hbar>0$, $qB\ne0$,
the form $q_B$, and the oscillator eigenfamily $(\psi_n)$ with energies
$E_n$.

[F1] The magnetic form on $C_c^\infty$ is the form of
[[def-nrqmx-magnetic-form-data]] with $d=2$, $A=(0,Bx)$ and $V=0$.

[F2] The $2\pi$-frequency Fourier transform on $L^1$ has kernel
$e^{-2\pi i y\xi}$ ([[def-fourier-transform-on-l-one-of-rn]]), and its
extension to $L^2$ is unitary ([[thm-plancherel]]). The wave-number convention
in the Statement is the unitary rescaling
$\mathcal F_y g(k)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\widehat g(k/(2\pi))$; for Schwartz $g$ it
satisfies $\mathcal F_y(-i\partial_y g)=k\mathcal F_y g$ by integration by
parts, and Fourier transform preserves Schwartz space
([[cor-fourier-transform-is-a-topological-automorphism-of-schwartz-space]]).
The Euclidean $L^2$ product identification and Fubini apply
([[lem-nrqm-composites-euclidean-l2-product-unitary]],
[[thm-fubini-theorem-for-l-one-on-sigma-finite-product-spaces]]); the
integration-by-parts identity used for the derivative multiplier is
[[thm-integration-by-parts-for-absolutely-continuous-functions]].

[F3] For $\omega_c>0$, let $H_{\mathrm{osc}}$ be the exact self-adjoint
oscillator realization in
[[thm-nrqm-oscillator-oscillator-scaling-and-exact-realization]]. It has
complete orthonormal basis $(\psi_n)$, eigenvalues $E_n$, and domain
$$D(H_{\mathrm{osc}})=\left\{g:\sum_{n=0}^\infty E_n^2|\langle g,\psi_n\rangle|^2<\infty\right\}.$$
On its polynomial-Gaussian core it has differential expression
$L=-\hbar^2\partial_\xi^2/(2m)+m\omega_c^2\xi^2/2$; in particular,
$L\psi_n=E_n\psi_n$ on that core.

[F4] Translation of one Lebesgue variable preserves its integral, and
integration by parts holds for smooth compactly supported functions
([[cor-c-one-change-of-variables-for-l-one-functions]],
[[thm-integration-by-parts-for-absolutely-continuous-functions]]).

[F5] Smooth compactly supported cutoffs equal to one on expanding balls and
with first derivatives bounded by $C/R$ are obtained by rescaling the
standard smooth step function ([[def-the-standard-smooth-step-function]]).

[F6] Tonelli's theorem permits integration of the nonnegative oscillator
energy series term by term over $(\xi,k)$ with Lebesgue measure
([[thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces]]).

[F7] For every finite $p$ and dimension, compactly supported smooth
functions are dense in $L^p(\mathbb R^d)$; in particular this holds for
$L^2(\mathbb R_k)$ ([[cor-compactly-supported-smooth-functions-are-dense-in-wkp-of-rn]]).

[F8] The weighted sequence space in the Statement is complete: it is
$L^2$ on $\mathbb N_0\times\mathbb R_k$ for the sigma-finite measure
$(n,k)\mapsto E_n\,d\#(n)\,dk$, and $L^2$ of any measure space is
Hilbert under Countable Choice
([[lem-l-two-with-the-integral-pairing-is-a-hilbert-space]]).

[F9] On any measure space, measurable complex-valued functions converging almost everywhere and bounded in modulus by a single nonnegative measurable $L^1$ function converge in integral (indeed in $L^1$). [[thm-dominated-convergence]]

[A1] Countable Choice is assumed by the unitary Fourier, product-space, and
oscillator suppliers; Dependent Choice is an exact hypothesis of the
absolutely-continuous integration-by-parts supplier
([[def-countable-choice]], [[def-dependent-choice]],
[[thm-integration-by-parts-for-absolutely-continuous-functions]]).

## Proof

1.1 On the dense tensor core $a(x)b(y)$ with $a,b\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$,
$\mathcal F_y$ is the identity on the first factor and the unitary Fourier
transform on the second, by [F2] and the Euclidean $L^2$ product identification
in [F2]. It extends uniquely to a unitary on $L^2(\mathbb R^2)$. For each
$k$, translation $x=\xi+x_0(k)$ preserves the $x$-integral by [F4]; Fubini
then shows that $Wg(\xi,k)=g(\xi+x_0(k),k)$ is an isometry with inverse
$W^{-1}h(x,k)=h(x-x_0(k),k)$. Hence $U=W\mathcal F_y$ is unitary. [given, A1, F2, F4, construct]

2.1 Let $u\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^2)$ and write
$F(\xi,k)=Uu(\xi,k)$. For each fixed $k$, $F(\cdot,k)$ is smooth and
compactly supported in $\xi$. Plancherel in $y$, the derivative identity
in [F2], and the substitution $x=\xi+x_0(k)$ give
$$q_B[u]=\int_{\mathbb R^2}\left(\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}|\partial_\xi F|^2+\frac{(qB)^2}{2m}\xi^2|F|^2\right)d\xi\,dk.$$
For a compactly supported smooth $g(\xi)$, put
$Lg=-\hbar^2g''/(2m)+m\omega_c^2\xi^2g/2\in L^2$. Integrating by parts
twice on a finite interval containing the support of $g$ gives
$\langle Lg,\psi_n\rangle=E_n\langle g,\psi_n\rangle$, since
$L\psi_n=E_n\psi_n$ and the boundary terms vanish. Completeness and Parseval
therefore give
$$\sum_{n=0}^\infty E_n^2|\langle g,\psi_n\rangle|^2=\|Lg\|_2^2<\infty.$$
The exact domain characterization in [F3] now implies
$g\in D(H_{\mathrm{osc}})$, and its diagonal action gives
$H_{\mathrm{osc}}g=Lg$. Hence
$$\langle H_{\mathrm{osc}}g,g\rangle=\sum_{n=0}^\infty E_n|\langle g,\psi_n\rangle|^2.$$
Integration by parts also identifies the left side with
$$\frac1{2m}\left(\hbar^2\|g'\|_2^2+(qB)^2\|\xi g\|_2^2\right).$$
Apply this identity to $F(\cdot,k)$ for almost every $k$. Tonelli [F6]
permits integrating the nonnegative series, so the displayed core form is
the coefficient form on every transformed core vector. [given, A1, F2, F3, F4, F6, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For $N<\infty$ and $f_0,\ldots,f_N\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R_k)$,
$F_N(\xi,k)=\sum_{n=0}^Nf_n(k)\psi_n(\xi)$ has inverse
$$U^{-1}F_N(x,y)=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{n=0}^N\int_{\mathbb R}e^{iky}f_n(k)\psi_n\!\left(x-\frac{\hbar k}{qB}\right)dk.$$
Write $v:=U^{-1}F_N$. The integrands have compact $k$-support and are Schwartz in $x$ uniformly
there by the Hermite form in [F3]; differentiating under the integral and
integrating by parts in $k$ show rapid decay in $y$, so the inverse is
Schwartz. Define
$$\chi_R(x,y)=\prod_{s\in\{x,y\}}\sigma(2-s/R)\sigma(2+s/R),$$
using [F5]. These cutoffs are one on $[-R,R]^2$, vanish outside
$[-2R,2R]^2$, and have $|\nabla\chi_R|\le C/R$. For each covariant
derivative,
$$\Pi_j^A(\chi_Rv)=\chi_R\Pi_j^Av-i\hbar(\partial_j\chi_R)v.$$
Take integer $R\ge1$. The step-function bounds in [F5] give $0\le\chi_R\le1$, and for every fixed $(x,y)$, $\chi_R(x,y)=1$ once $R\ge\max\{|x|,|y|\}$, so $\chi_R\to1$ pointwise. Since $v$ is Schwartz and $A=(0,Bx)$ is linear, $\Pi_1^Av=-i\hbar\partial_xv$ and $\Pi_2^Av=-i\hbar\partial_yv-qBxv$ are Schwartz and belong to $L^2(\mathbb R^2)$. For each $w\in\{v,\Pi_1^Av,\Pi_2^Av\}$, the functions $|(\chi_R-1)w|^2$ are measurable, tend pointwise to zero, and are bounded by the single measurable $L^1$ majorant $|w|^2$. Applying [F9] on the Lebesgue measure space $\mathbb R^2$ gives $\|(\chi_R-1)w\|_2\to0$. The derivative-cutoff term separately satisfies $\|\hbar(\partial_j\chi_R)v\|_2\le C\hbar\|v\|_2/R\to0$. Thus the displayed product identity and the triangle inequality give $\|\Pi_j^A(\chi_Rv-v)\|_2\to0$ for both $j$, as well as $\|\chi_Rv-v\|_2\to0$.
The form identity of step 2.1 also holds for Schwartz functions: the Fourier and shear maps preserve Schwartz regularity, and for each Schwartz $g(\xi)$ one has $Lg\in L^2$; its integration-by-parts pairings with the polynomial-Gaussian $\psi_n$ have vanishing boundary terms at both infinities. The same Parseval calculation and exact diagonal operator-domain criterion in [F3] therefore give $g\in D(H_{\mathrm{osc}})$ and the same oscillator energy identity. All resulting nonnegative energies can again be integrated by [F6]. Apply this extended identity to the Schwartz difference $\chi_Rv-v$. Its weighted coefficient norm squared is exactly $\frac1{2m}\sum_{j=1}^2\|\Pi_j^A(\chi_Rv-v)\|_2^2$, which tends to zero by the preceding bounds. Since $\chi_Rv\in C_c^\infty$, each $F_N$ therefore lies in the weighted-norm closure of the transformed original core, without assuming closability in advance. Finite coefficient families are dense
in the weighted target: truncate its convergent nonnegative series, then
approximate the remaining finitely many $L^2(k)$ coefficients by
$C_c^\infty(k)$ using [F7]. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F9, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Let $\mathcal K_E$ be the coefficient space
$\{(f_n):\sum_nE_n\|f_n\|_2^2<\infty\}$ with squared norm
$\sum_nE_n\|f_n\|_2^2$, and let
$J:\mathcal K_E\hookrightarrow L^2(\mathbb N_0\times\mathbb R,d\#\,dk)$
be the identity inclusion. Step 2.1 maps the transformed core isometrically
into $\mathcal K_E$. Since $E_n\ge E_0=\hbar\omega_c/2>0$, $J$ is
continuous and injective, with
$\|Jf\|_2^2\le E_0^{-1}\sum_nE_n\|f_n\|_2^2$. The coefficient map is
unitary on the underlying $L^2$ space by [F2] and [F3]. Thus
$q_B[u]\ge E_0\|u\|_2^2$ on the core. For every $\varepsilon>0$,
$$q_B[u]\le q_B[u]+\varepsilon\|u\|_2^2\le(1+\varepsilon/E_0)q_B[u],$$
so each shifted form norm is equivalent to $q_B[u]^{1/2}$. Suppose a core
sequence is Cauchy in $q_B[u]^{1/2}$ and tends to zero in $L^2$. Its
weighted coefficient image converges in $\mathcal K_E$ to
some $f$; unweighted $L^2$ convergence and continuity of $J$ give $Jf=0$.
Injectivity of $J$ gives $f=0$, so $q_B[u]\to0$ along that sequence.
The equivalence above then gives convergence to zero in the defining shifted
form norm, proving closability of the core form. By [F8] the
target is complete, so the isometric image of the form completion is
closed there. Step 3.1 places a dense subspace of that target in the image;
hence the image is the whole weighted space and the form closure is exactly
the stated one. [F1, F2, F3, F8, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎

## Statement

Let $M$ be a finite-dimensional smooth manifold, let $r\ge1$, and let $P:M\to M_r(\mathbb C)$ be a $C^2$ orthogonal projector of constant rank $m$, with $1\le m\le r$. Write $E_P=\{(x,v):P(x)v=v\}$ and give it the projected connection $\nabla s=P\,ds$. For $x\in M$ and $X,Y\in T_xM$, define the curvature two-form with values in $M_r(\mathbb C)$ by

$$F_x(X,Y):=P(x)\bigl(dP_x(X)dP_x(Y)-dP_x(Y)dP_x(X)\bigr)P(x).$$

Let $U\subseteq M$ be open and let $V:U\to\mathbb C^{r\times m}$ be a $C^2$ orthonormal frame, meaning $V(x)^*V(x)=I_m$ and $V(x)V(x)^*=P(x)$ for all $x\in U$. Put $A=V^*dV$ and $w=v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge v_m$, where $v_1,\ldots,v_m$ are the columns of $V$. The induced projected connection on $\det E_P$ has local connection one-form $a=\operatorname{tr}A$ in the unit frame $w$, and its scalar curvature is

$$da=\operatorname{tr}(V^*FV)=\operatorname{tr}F.$$

Thus the curvature of the determinant line is the trace of the original bundle curvature, with the same orientation and sign convention.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The smooth projector and the supplied local orthonormal frame in the Statement.

[F1] [[def-nrqmx-determinant-line-projector]] identifies $\det E_P$ with the range of $Q=\bigwedge^mP$.

[F2] [[lem-nrqmx-determinant-line-projector-well-defined]] proves that $Q$ is a smooth rank-one orthogonal projector.

[F3] [[thm-nrqmx-berry-projector-curvature]] gives the frame connection $A=V^*dV$ and curvature $F_{\mathrm{fr}}=dA+A\wedge A=V^*FV$.

## Proof

**Given:** [F1]–[F3].

1.1 Because the columns of $V$ are orthonormal, $w=v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge v_m$ has unit norm and spans the determinant line. Differentiating by the product rule gives $dw=\sum_j v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dv_j\wedge\cdots\wedge v_m$. The projected connection applies $P$ to each differentiated factor. Since $P\,dv_j=\sum_a v_a A_{aj}$, each term with $a\ne j$ vanishes from a repeated wedge factor, while the $a=j$ term is $A_{jj}w$. Summing gives $Qdw=(\operatorname{tr}A)w$, so the determinant connection form is $a=\operatorname{tr}A$. [F1, F2, given, algebra]

2.1 A line connection with scalar one-form $a$ has curvature $da+a\wedge a=da$, since a scalar one-form wedges with itself to zero. By [F3], $\operatorname{tr}F=\operatorname{tr}(dA)+\operatorname{tr}(A\wedge A)$. In coordinates, $\operatorname{tr}(A\wedge A)=\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}\wedge A_{ji}=0$: diagonal terms vanish and each off-diagonal pair cancels because one-forms anticommute. Therefore $da=d\operatorname{tr}A=\operatorname{tr}F_{\mathrm{fr}}=\operatorname{tr}(V^*FV)$. Finally, since $PFP=F$ and $VV^*=P$, cyclicity of finite-dimensional trace gives $\operatorname{tr}(V^*FV)=\operatorname{tr}(FVV^*)=\operatorname{tr}(FP)=\operatorname{tr}F$. [F3, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
