## Statement refuted

A bounded complex of graded type-A Soergel bimodules whose alternating class in the split Grothendieck ring is zero must be contractible in the homotopy category.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $n\ge2$, $R=\mathbb Q[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$, and the type-A Soergel category with unit bimodule $R$.

[F1] [[def-qftx-categorified-line-model]]: $R$ is the unit object of $\mathrm{SBim}_n$, and bounded cochain complexes are considered in $K^b(\mathrm{SBim}_n)$.

[F2] [[def-split-grothendieck-rings-of-type-a-soergel-categories]]: the split Grothendieck group identifies isomorphic objects and has $[X\oplus Y]=[X]+[Y]$; the unit bimodule $R$ is a nonzero object.

[F3] [[lem-euler-class-of-a-rouquier-complex-is-homotopy-invariant-and-multiplicative]]: the Euler class of a bounded complex is $\chi(C)=\sum_m(-1)^m[C^m]$.

[F4] [[def-complex-homotopy-and-contractibility-in-an-additive-category]]: a contracting homotopy satisfies $1_{C^m}=d^{m-1}h^m+h^{m+1}d^m$ in every cochain degree $m$.

[A1] Take $C^0=C^1=R$, $d^0=0$, with all other terms and differentials zero; use the unshifted grading on both copies of $R$. Cohomology means $H^m(C)=\ker(d^m)/\operatorname{im}(d^{m-1})$.

## Counterexample

1.1 The complex $C$ is bounded and its two nonzero terms are objects of $\mathrm{SBim}_n$. Its Euler class is $\chi(C)=[R]-[R]=0$ by [F2] and [F3]. [A1, F1, F2, F3, algebra]

1.2 Since every differential is zero, $H^0(C)=R$ and $H^1(C)=R$. Each is nonzero because $1\in\mathbb Q[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ is nonzero. [A1, F1, algebra]

2.1 If $C$ were contractible, the equation of [F4] in degree $0$ would give $1_R=d^{-1}h^0+h^1d^0=0$, contradicting $1_R\ne0$. Thus $C$ is not contractible although its Euler class vanishes, refuting the Statement. [A1, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2] ∎

## Statement

Let $I,J$ be finite nonempty sets, let $P,Q$ be probability vectors on $I\times J$, and assume $Q(x,y)>0$ for every $(x,y)$. For probability vectors $p,q$ on a finite set with $q$ strictly positive, define
$$D(p\|q)=\sum_{z:p(z)>0}p(z)\ln\frac{p(z)}{q(z)},$$
with zero-mass terms omitted. Let $P_X,Q_X$ be the first-coordinate marginals. For $x$ with $P_X(x)>0$, let $P_{Y|x},Q_{Y|x}$ be the conditional probability vectors; terms weighted by $P_X(x)=0$ are omitted. Then
$$D(P\|Q)=D(P_X\|Q_X)+\sum_{x:P_X(x)>0}P_X(x)D(P_{Y|x}\|Q_{Y|x}).$$
For every subset $C\subseteq I\times J$, grouping the atoms into $C$ and its complement cannot increase relative entropy:
$$D(P\|Q)\ge d(P(C)\|Q(C)),\qquad d(a\|b)=a\ln\frac ab+(1-a)\ln\frac{1-a}{1-b},$$
with zero-weight terms omitted. For the empty or full event, the binary divergence is $0$, and the grouping bound reduces to $D(P\|Q)\ge0$, with equality exactly when $P=Q$. For a nonempty proper event $C$, equality holds exactly when, on each of $C$ and $C^c$ having positive $P$-mass, the conditional probability vectors of $P$ and $Q$ agree.

## Statement references

[[lem-it-finite-classical-kl-nonnegative-equality]]

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Finite nonempty sets $I,J$, probability vectors $P,Q$ on $I\times J$, strict positivity of $Q$, and the natural logarithm convention.

[F1] [[lem-it-finite-classical-kl-nonnegative-equality]]: for finite probability vectors with positive reference vector, relative entropy as defined above is nonnegative and vanishes exactly when the probability vectors coincide; its proof derives the scalar inequality $-\ln u\ge1-u$ from the logarithm derivative.

[F2] [[def-natural-logarithm]] and [[thm-natural-logarithm-laws]]: $\ln(ab)=\ln a+\ln b$ for $a,b>0$.

## Proof

1.1 Write $p_x=P_X(x)$ and $q_x=Q_X(x)$. Strict positivity of $Q$ gives $q_x>0$ and $Q_{Y|x}(y)>0$ for each $x,y$. If $P(x,y)>0$, then $p_x>0$ and
$$\ln\frac{P(x,y)}{Q(x,y)}=\ln\frac{p_x}{q_x}+\ln\frac{P_{Y|x}(y)}{Q_{Y|x}(y)}$$
by [F2]. Multiply by $P(x,y)$ and sum over positive joint atoms. For each $x$ with $p_x>0$, summing the first term over $y$ gives $p_x\ln(p_x/q_x)$, and summing the second gives $p_xD(P_{Y|x}\|Q_{Y|x})$. Atoms with $p_x=0$ contribute no terms. This is exactly the claimed chain rule. [given, F2, algebra]

2.1 If $C$ is empty or all of $I\times J$, then $P(C)=Q(C)$ is respectively $0$ or $1$, so the binary divergence is $0$ under the stated zero-weight convention. The grouping bound is then $D(P\|Q)\ge0$, with equality exactly when $P=Q$ by [F1]. For a nonempty proper $C$, strict positivity gives $0<Q(C)<1$. Put $a=P(C)$ and $b=Q(C)$. When $a>0$, the conditional laws on $C$ are $P_C(z)=P(z)/a$ and $Q_C(z)=Q(z)/b$; the latter is strictly positive on $C$. The logarithmic identity $\ln(P(z)/Q(z))=\ln(a/b)+\ln(P_C(z)/Q_C(z))$ for each $z\in C$ with $P(z)>0$ yields
$$\sum_{z\in C:P(z)>0}P(z)\ln\frac{P(z)}{Q(z)}=a\ln\frac ab+aD(P_C\|Q_C).$$
If $a=0$, both the sum and the weighted conditional term are zero, so the same identity holds with that term omitted. Applying the identical calculation to $C^c$ and adding gives $D(P\|Q)=d(a\|b)+aD(P_C\|Q_C)+(1-a)D(P_{C^c}\|Q_{C^c})$, with zero-weight conditional terms omitted. Both weighted conditional divergences are nonnegative by [F1], which proves the grouping inequality, including $a=0$ or $1$. Equality holds precisely when every conditional divergence with positive weight vanishes; by the equality clause of [F1], this is equivalent to agreement of the conditional probability vectors on each positive-$P$-mass block. Zero-$P$-mass blocks impose no conditional-law condition. [given, F1, F2, algebra, cases] ∎

## Statement

Let $\Gamma$ be an at most countable set (finite or in bijection with $\mathbb N$) and let $\sim$ be a reflexive symmetric relation on $\Gamma$ ($\gamma\sim\gamma$ and $\gamma\sim\eta$ implies $\eta\sim\gamma$). Let $V\subseteq\Gamma$ be finite, and fix maps $w:V\to\mathbb C$ and $a:V\to[0,\infty)$ and a radius $r>0$. Call $X\subseteq V$ compatible when no two distinct elements of $X$ are related by $\sim$. Suppose there is $\varepsilon\in(0,1]$ such that

$$\sum_{\substack{\eta\in V\\\eta\sim\gamma}}r|w_\eta|e^{a_\eta}\leq(1-\varepsilon)a_\gamma\qquad(\gamma\in V).$$

For $t\in\mathbb C$, define $Z_V(t):=\sum_{\substack{X\subseteq V\\\text{and }X\text{ is compatible}}}\prod_{\gamma\in X}(tw_\gamma)$. Define the connected series

$$L_V(t):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^n}{n!}\sum_{\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_n\in V}U_n(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_n)\prod_{i=1}^nw_{\gamma_i}.$$

Then $L_V$ converges absolutely and uniformly on every closed subdisc strictly inside $|t|<r$, may be differentiated there term by term, and satisfies $Z_V(t)=\exp(L_V(t))$ for $|t|<r$. Thus $Z_V$ has no zero in this disc. Here an analytic logarithm of $Z_V$ on the disc means a holomorphic function $L$ with $\exp(L(t))=Z_V(t)$ there; the logarithm $L_V$ is normalized by $L_V(0)=0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite polymer model, weights, radius, and strict majorant in the Statement.

[F1] [[def-csm-frontier-abstract-polymer]]: for finite $V$, $Z_V$ sums products of activities over compatible subsets, and $U_n$ is a real-valued sum of connected graph products with edge weight $-\mathbf 1_{\{\gamma_i\sim\gamma_j\}}\in\{-1,0\}$.

[F2] [[lem-csm-frontier-polymer-tree-majorant]]: if $\sum_{\eta\sim\gamma}|z_\eta|e^{a_\eta}\leq a_\gamma$, then the rooted connected-cluster sum with root $\gamma$ is at most $e^{a_\gamma}$.

[F3] [[def-complex-exponential]] and [[thm-complex-exponential-addition-and-real-extension]]: the complex exponential is defined by its everywhere convergent power series and satisfies $\exp(u+v)=\exp(u)\exp(v)$.

[F4] [[thm-termwise-differentiation-of-complex-power-series]]: a complex power series differentiates term by term at every point strictly inside its radius of convergence.

[F5] [[def-complex-conjugate-real-imaginary-part-and-modulus]] and [[lem-complex-conjugation-and-modulus-laws]]: for $z=x+iy$, $|z|=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$; complex modulus is nonnegative, multiplicative and subadditive. The equality of complex modulus on real embeddings with real absolute value is supplied by [[lem-complex-modulus-of-real-embedding-equals-real-absolute-value]], a draft mathematical item with an AI-generated, complete local proof and a format precheck only (no audited or verified proof stamp); repeated subadditivity gives the finite-sum triangle inequality.

[F6] [[def-sum-over-a-finite-index-set]]: real and natural sums and products over finite sets are defined by any enumeration, independently of the enumeration; finite complex sums and products use the same enumeration-independent recursion specified in [[def-csm-frontier-abstract-polymer]].

[A1] For every $\gamma\in V$, $\sum_{\eta\in V,\ \eta\sim\gamma}r|w_\eta|e^{a_\eta}\leq(1-\varepsilon)a_\gamma$.

## Proof

**Given:** The definitions [F1], the rooted estimate [F2], and [A1].

1.1 Write $f_{ij}=-\mathbf 1_{\{\gamma_i\sim\gamma_j\}}$. Reflexivity makes $\prod_{i<j}(1+f_{ij})$ zero when a tuple repeats a polymer, and otherwise this product is one exactly for a compatible subset. Hence $Z_V(t)=\sum_{n\geq0}\frac{t^n}{n!}\sum_{\vec\gamma\in V^n}(\prod_iw_{\gamma_i})\prod_{i<j}(1+f_{ij})$ as a formal series. Expanding the pair product gives all simple graphs. A set partition of $[n]$ is a finite family of nonempty pairwise-disjoint blocks whose union is $[n]$; write $\Pi_n$ for the set of such partitions. This set is finite since it is contained in $\mathcal P(\mathcal P([n]))$. The connected components of each finite graph give its unique partition into connected graph blocks. Each graph's edge weight factors over these blocks. The coefficient of $t^n$ in $\exp(L_V(t))$ is $\frac1{n!}\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_n}\sum_{\vec\gamma\in V^n}(\prod_{B\in\pi}U_{|B|}(\vec\gamma_B))(\prod_{i=1}^nw_{\gamma_i})$: for ordered block sizes $m_1,\ldots,m_k$, assigning the $n$ labels to those blocks contributes $n!/(m_1!\cdots m_k!)$, and the exponential's factor $1/k!$ removes the ordering of the $k$ blocks. These factors leave exactly the connected-component expansion of the all-graph coefficient. Thus $Z_V$ and $\exp(L_V)$ have identical formal coefficients. [F1, F6, algebra]

1.2 Let $d_n$ be the coefficient of $t^n$ in $L_V$, and apply [F2] to the finite polymer set $V$ with activities $r w_\eta$. Since $r>0$, [F5] gives $|r w_\eta|=r|w_\eta|$, so [A1] implies the rooted criterion. For each fixed root $\gamma_1$, the $n$th nonnegative term of that rooted sum is at most $e^{a_{\gamma_1}}$. The finite-sum triangle inequality, multiplicativity in [F5], and agreement of the complex modulus with real absolute value give $r^n|d_n|\leq\frac1{n!}\sum_{\gamma_1\in V}r|w_{\gamma_1}|\sum_{\gamma_2,\ldots,\gamma_n\in V}|U_n(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_n)|\prod_{j=2}^n|r w_{\gamma_j}|\leq\frac1n\sum_{\gamma\in V}r|w_\gamma|e^{a_\gamma}$. Thus on $|t|\leq s<r$, the series is dominated by a constant times $\sum_{n\geq1}(s/r)^n/n$, and its derivative series by a constant times $\sum_{n\geq1}(s/r)^{n-1}$. Both converge uniformly on every such closed subdisc. [A1, F2, F5, F6, algebra]

2.1 By [F4], $L_V$ differentiates term by term throughout $|t|<r$. For each fixed $t$ with $|t|<r$, step 1.2 gives $\sum_{n\geq1}|d_n||t|^n<\infty$, so the total absolute sum after expanding $\exp(L_V(t))$ is at most $\sum_{k\geq0}(\sum_{n\geq1}|d_n||t|^n)^k/k!<\infty$. The products may therefore be regrouped by total degree; their coefficients are the formal coefficients identified in step 1.1, so $Z_V(t)=\exp(L_V(t))$ on the disc. By [F3], $\exp(L_V(t))\exp(-L_V(t))=1$, so $Z_V(t)$ is nonzero and $L_V$ is its analytic logarithm with $L_V(0)=0$. [F3, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2] ∎

## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice. Let $d\ge1$ and let $\rho_0:\mathbb T^d\to\mathbb R$ be measurable with $0\le\rho_0\le1$ almost everywhere, using normalized product Haar measure $dx$. For $k\in\mathbb Z^d$ define $e_k(x)=\exp(2\pi i k\cdot x)$ and $\widehat f(k)=\int_{\mathbb T^d}f(x)e_{-k}(x)\,dx$. For $t>0$ set

$$\rho_t(x):=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^d}e^{-4\pi^2|k|^2t}\widehat\rho_0(k)e_k(x),$$

and at $t=0$ set $\rho_t=\rho_0$ as an almost-everywhere class. For smooth periodic $G$, define

$$P_tG(x):=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^d}e^{-4\pi^2|k|^2t}\widehat G(k)e_k(x)\quad(t>0),\qquad P_0G:=G.$$

Then on every strip $[\epsilon,T]\times\mathbb T^d$ with $0<\epsilon<T<\infty$, the series for $\rho_t$ and all its space and time derivatives converge absolutely and uniformly; in particular $\rho_t$ is real and smooth for each $t>0$ and $\partial_t\rho_t=\Delta\rho_t$. For every finite $T$ and every smooth $G$, $P_tG$ is smooth on $[0,T]\times\mathbb T^d$, satisfies $\partial_tP_tG=\Delta P_tG$, has uniformly bounded derivatives of every fixed order there, and $P_tG\to G$ uniformly as $t\downarrow0$. Moreover

$$\int_{\mathbb T^d}\rho_tG\,dx=\int_{\mathbb T^d}\rho_0P_tG\,dx,$$

so $(\rho_t)_{0\le t\le T}$ is the unique weak heat solution with datum $\rho_0$ in the stated weak formulation. It preserves $0\le\rho_t\le1$ almost everywhere and the mass $\int\rho_t\,dx=\int\rho_0\,dx$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Countable Choice, $d\ge1$, a real measurable $\rho_0$ with $0\le\rho_0\le1$ almost everywhere on the normalized torus $\mathbb T^d$, and smooth periodic test functions.

[A1] Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$. Full Choice is not needed for this heat-flow lemma; [[def-countable-choice]] states that every countable family of nonempty sets has a choice function.

[F1] [[def-fourier-coefficients-and-trigonometric-polynomials]]: on $\mathbb T^d$ the characters are $e_k(x)=\exp(2\pi i k\cdot x)$, the normalized measure has total mass one, and $\widehat f(k)=\int f e_{-k}\,dx$ for $f\in L^1$.

[F2] [[thm-fourier-basis-and-parseval-on-the-n-torus]]: under $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, the characters form a complete orthonormal basis of $L^2(\mathbb T^d)$; smooth functions equal their Fourier sums in $L^2$ and Parseval holds.

[F3] [[def-csm-frontier-ssep-weak-heat-solution]]: a weak heat solution has continuous pairings with smooth tests and satisfies the displayed integral identity for every such test.

## Proof

1.1 Since $0\le\rho_0\le1$ and $dx(\mathbb T^d)=1$, $\rho_0\in L^1$ and $|\widehat\rho_0(k)|\le1$ for every $k$. For any smooth $G$ and integer $m\ge1$, periodic integration by parts gives $(1+4\pi^2|k|^2)^m\widehat G(k)=\widehat{(1-\Delta)^mG}(k)$, hence $|\widehat G(k)|\le\|(1-\Delta)^mG\|_{L^1}(1+4\pi^2|k|^2)^{-m}$. If $q\ge0$ is fixed, choose $m>(d+q)/2$; grouping lattice points by $r=\|k\|_\infty$ gives at most $(2r+1)^d-(2r-1)^d\le C_dr^{d-1}$ points in shell $r$. The shell contribution to $\sum_k(1+|k|)^q|\widehat G(k)|$ is at most $C r^{d-1+q-2m}$. Grouping shells into dyadic blocks $2^j\le r<2^{j+1}$ bounds the $j$th block by $C'2^{j(d+q-2m)}$, a convergent geometric series since $2m>d+q$. Thus $\sum_k(1+|k|)^q|\widehat G(k)|<\infty$. [A1, F1, construct, algebra]

2.1 For each $t>0$ and each fixed derivative order, multiplication of the coefficients by any corresponding polynomial in $k$ still leaves an absolutely summable sequence, because $|\widehat\rho_0(k)|\le1$ and the Gaussian factor $e^{-4\pi^2|k|^2t}$ dominates every inverse power on lattice shells. Therefore the heat series and all fixed-order differentiated series converge uniformly on $\mathbb T^d$ for $t\ge\varepsilon>0$. Pairing the $k$ and $-k$ terms shows $\rho_t$ is real. Termwise differentiation is valid on every such compact time interval; both $\partial_t$ and $\Delta$ multiply the $k$th term by $-4\pi^2|k|^2$, so $\partial_t\rho_t=\Delta\rho_t$. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 The decay estimate in step 1.1 makes the Fourier series for $G$ and for each fixed space or time derivative of $P_tG$ uniformly absolutely convergent even at $t=0$. By [F2], the series at $t=0$ equals $G$ in $L^2$; both are continuous, so equality almost everywhere implies equality everywhere. Uniform convergence of the differentiated series proves smoothness and uniform derivative bounds on $[0,T]\times\mathbb T^d$. Also $\|P_tG-G\|_\infty\le\sum_k|\widehat G(k)|\,|e^{-4\pi^2|k|^2t}-1|\to0$ by splitting into a finite set of modes and an absolutely summable tail. Termwise differentiation gives $\partial_tP_tG=\Delta P_tG$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Absolute convergence permits termwise integration against $\rho_0\in L^1$. Reindexing $k\mapsto-k$ and using the even multiplier $e^{-4\pi^2|k|^2t}$ gives $\int\rho_tG=\sum_k e^{-4\pi^2|k|^2t}\widehat\rho_0(k)\widehat G(-k)=\int\rho_0P_tG$. By step 2.2 this pairing tends to $\int\rho_0G$ as $t\downarrow0$. Differentiating the pairing for $t>0$ and using $\Delta P_tG=P_t\Delta G$ yields $\frac{d}{dt}\int\rho_tG=\int\rho_t\Delta G$; integration from $0$ proves the weak identity in [F3]. Thus the constructed family is a weak heat solution. [F1, F3, step 2.2, algebra]

3.2 Parseval and the heat coefficients give $\|\rho_t-\rho_0\|_2^2=\sum_k|\widehat\rho_0(k)|^2|e^{-4\pi^2|k|^2t}-1|^2\to0$ as $t\downarrow0$: choose a finite set whose tail in the convergent series $\sum_k|\widehat\rho_0(k)|^2$ is small, then take the limit on that finite set. For $\epsilon>0$ put $a_\epsilon(z)=\frac12(1-z/\sqrt{z^2+\epsilon^2})$ and $\Phi_\epsilon(z)=\int_0^z\int_0^r a_\epsilon(q)\,dq\,dr$. Then $\Phi_\epsilon$ is smooth and convex, $\Phi_\epsilon(0)=\Phi_\epsilon'(0)=0$, and $0\le\Phi_\epsilon(z)\le z^2/2$. On any interval $[a,b]\subset(0,T]$, the heat equation, chain rule, and periodic integration by parts give $\int\Phi_\epsilon(\rho_b)-\int\Phi_\epsilon(\rho_a)=-\int_a^b\int\Phi_\epsilon''(\rho_s)\sum_{i=1}^d|\partial_{x_i}\rho_s|^2\,dx\,ds\le0$. For each real $z$, the defining double integral tends to $\frac12(z^-)^2$, where $z^-:=\max\{-z,0\}$; dominated convergence on the bounded range of $\rho$ over $[a,b]\times\mathbb T^d$ gives the same monotonicity for $\|\rho_t^-\|_2^2$. Letting $a\downarrow0$, the $L^2$ continuity just proved and $\rho_0\ge0$ imply $\rho_t^-=0$. The assumed datum already gives the required bound when $T=0$. [A1, F1, F2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 For the upper bound put $b_\epsilon(z)=\frac12(1+(z-1)/\sqrt{(z-1)^2+\epsilon^2})$ and $\Psi_\epsilon(z)=\int_1^z\int_1^r b_\epsilon(q)\,dq\,dr$. This is smooth and convex with $\Psi_\epsilon(1)=\Psi_\epsilon'(1)=0$ and $0\le\Psi_\epsilon(z)\le(z-1)^2/2$. The same integration by parts gives $\int\Psi_\epsilon(\rho_b)\le\int\Psi_\epsilon(\rho_a)$ for $0<a<b$, and $\Psi_\epsilon(z)\to\frac12((z-1)^+)^2$, where $r^+:=\max\{r,0\}$. Dominated convergence and $L^2$ continuity at zero, together with $\rho_0\le1$, give $(\rho_t-1)^+=0$. The zero Fourier coefficient is unchanged by the heat multiplier, so $\int\rho_t=\widehat\rho_0(0)=\int\rho_0$. The assumed datum already gives the bound when $T=0$. [A1, F1, F2, step 3.2, algebra]

5.1 Let $(u_t)$ be any weak heat solution in the sense of [F3]. Testing its weak identity with $e_{-k}$ shows $g_k(t):=\widehat u_t(k)$ is continuous and satisfies $g_k(t)=\widehat\rho_0(k)-4\pi^2|k|^2\int_0^t g_k(s)\,ds$. Hence $g_k$ is differentiable and solves $g_k'=-4\pi^2|k|^2g_k$, so $g_k(t)=e^{-4\pi^2|k|^2t}\widehat\rho_0(k)$, the coefficient of the constructed $\rho_t$. For each $t$, both functions are in $L^2$; equality of all Fourier coefficients and completeness in [F2] imply $u_t=\rho_t$ almost everywhere. Thus the weak heat solution is unique. [F1, F2, F3, step 3.1, algebra] ∎

## Statement

Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$. For every $\epsilon>0$, $c,\lambda,L>0$, and smooth real initial pair $(f_{+,0},f_{-,0})$ on $\mathbb T_L$, the periodic system

$$\partial_t f_+^\epsilon+\frac c\epsilon\partial_x f_+^\epsilon=\frac\lambda{\epsilon^2}(f_-^\epsilon-f_+^\epsilon),\qquad \partial_t f_-^\epsilon-\frac c\epsilon\partial_x f_-^\epsilon=\frac\lambda{\epsilon^2}(f_+^\epsilon-f_-^\epsilon),\qquad f_\pm^\epsilon(\cdot,0)=f_{\pm,0}$$

has a unique global smooth solution $(f_+^\epsilon,f_-^\epsilon)$. Its total mass $M^\epsilon(t)$ is constant. If both initial densities are nonnegative, then both evolved densities are nonnegative. For any two smooth real initial pairs $F_0,G_0$, their solutions satisfy

$$\|F^\epsilon(t)-G^\epsilon(t)\|_{2,L}^2\le \|F_0-G_0\|_{2,L}^2\qquad(t\ge0),$$

where $\|(g_+,g_-)\|_{2,L}^2:=L^{-1}\int_0^L(|g_+|^2+|g_-|^2)\,dx$. In particular, for one solution,

$$\frac{d}{dt}\|F^\epsilon(t)\|_{2,L}^2=-\frac{2\lambda}{\epsilon^2}\|f_+^\epsilon(t)-f_-^\epsilon(t)\|_{2,L}^2\le0.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, $\epsilon,c,\lambda>0$, a period $L>0$, and the smooth real initial data and Cauchy problem in the Statement.

[A1] Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice, $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ ([[def-countable-choice]]): every countable family of nonempty sets has a choice function.

[F1] [[def-csm-frontier-two-velocity-scaling]]: $\mathbb T_L=\mathbb R/(L\mathbb Z)$, $dm_L=dx/L$, $\kappa_n=2\pi n/L$, and the two equations have transport speeds $\pm c/\epsilon$ and switching rate $\lambda/\epsilon^2$ in each direction.

[F2] [[thm-fourier-basis-and-parseval-on-the-n-torus]]: under $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, the characters $y\mapsto e^{2\pi iny}$ form an orthonormal basis of complex $L^2(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z,dy)$ and Parseval's identity holds. The change of variable $y=x/L$ preserves normalized measure and carries these characters to $e_n(x)$, so the same coefficient expansion and Parseval identity hold on $\mathbb T_L$ with $dm_L$.

[F3] [[thm-ftc-second-part]]: for a differentiable function $G$ on a closed interval with integrable derivative, $\int_a^b G'(x)\,dx=G(b)-G(a)$. For periodic $C^1$ functions on $[0,L]$, the right side is zero.

[F4] [[lem-linear-matrix-odes-have-unique-global-solutions-on-a-given-interval]]: for every compact interval $I$, continuous real matrix field $C$ on $I$, and initial time $t_0\in I$, the real linear matrix IVP $Y'=C(t)Y$ has a unique solution on all of $I$.

[F5] [[lem-exponential-series-of-a-bounded-operator]]: on a real or complex Banach space, a bounded operator $A$ has an operator-norm convergent exponential series $E(t)=\sum_{m\ge0}t^mA^m/m!$; $E'(t)=AE(t)$ and $\|E(t)-I-tA\|\le\tfrac12t^2\|A\|^2e^{|t|\|A\|}$.

[F6] [[thm-euclidean-space-complete]]: for every integer $n\ge1$, $\mathbb R^n$ with its Euclidean metric is complete; in particular $\mathbb R^4$ with the Euclidean norm is a Banach space.

[F7] [[thm-cauchy-schwarz-and-the-euclidean-norm]]: on $\mathbb R^4$, $|x\cdot y|\le\|x\|_2\|y\|_2$ and $\|\cdot\|_2$ is the Euclidean norm.

[F8] [[def-euclidean-inner-product]]: on $\mathbb R^4$, $x\cdot y=\sum_{j=1}^4x_jy_j$ and $\|x\|_2=(x\cdot x)^{1/2}$.

[F9] [[def-operator-norm]]: for a bounded linear map $A$ on a normed space, $\|A\|=\sup_{\|x\|\le1}\|Ax\|$ and $\|Ax\|\le\|A\|\|x\|$.

## Proof

**Given:** [A1], [F1]–[F3], and the initial data of the Statement.

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 For $n\in\mathbb Z$, put $U_n=(\widehat f_+^\epsilon(n),\widehat f_-^\epsilon(n))^{\mathsf T}$, $v=c/\epsilon$, and $\alpha=\lambda/\epsilon^2$. Integrating by parts in the real and imaginary parts separately, using [F3], gives $U_n'=M_nU_n$, with $M_n=\begin{pmatrix}-i\kappa_n v-\alpha&\alpha\\ \alpha&i\kappa_n v-\alpha\end{pmatrix}$. Realify $z\in\mathbb C^2$ by $\mathcal Rz=(\operatorname{Re}z_1,\operatorname{Re}z_2,\operatorname{Im}z_1,\operatorname{Im}z_2)\in\mathbb R^4$, and for $M=P+iQ$ put $\mathcal RM=\begin{pmatrix}P&-Q\\Q&P\end{pmatrix}$. Direct coordinate multiplication gives $\mathcal R(Mz)=(\mathcal RM)\mathcal Rz$ and $|\mathcal Rz|_2^2=|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2$. By [F6], $\mathbb R^4$ is Banach; [F7] applied rowwise bounds $\mathcal RM_n$ as an operator, and [F8]–[F9] specify the Euclidean and operator norms. Hence [F5] makes $t\mapsto e^{t\mathcal RM_n}\mathcal RU_n(0)$ a solution of the realified constant-matrix IVP; [F4] gives uniqueness on each compact interval $[0,T]$, so these compatible solutions define the unique global complex Fourier mode solution after pulling back by $\mathcal R$. Since real initial data satisfy $U_{-n}(0)=\overline{U_n(0)}$ and $M_{-n}=\overline{M_n}$, uniqueness gives $U_{-n}(t)=\overline{U_n(t)}$, so the reconstructed Fourier series is real. The skew-Hermitian transport part contributes zero to the derivative of $|U_n|^2$, and direct multiplication of the collision matrix gives $(|U_n|^2)'=-2\alpha|\widehat f_+^\epsilon(n)-\widehat f_-^\epsilon(n)|^2\le0$. For every integer $r\ge0$ and every integer $q>r+1$, repeated integration by parts gives $|\widehat f_{\pm,0}(n)|\le |\kappa_n|^{-q}\|\partial_x^q f_{\pm,0}\|_{L^1(dm_L)}$ for $n\ne0$; grouping $2^j\le |n|<2^{j+1}$ shows $\sum_n(1+|\kappa_n|)^r|U_n(0)|<\infty$. The mode bound preserves these summable tails, and $M_n$ grows at most linearly in $|n|$, so the Fourier series and every space-time derivative converge uniformly on compact time intervals. They define a global smooth solution of the Cauchy problem. Any other smooth solution has the same mode ODEs and initial coefficients; [A1] and [F2] make the solutions equal as $L^2(\mathbb T_L,dm_L;\mathbb C^2)$ classes, hence almost everywhere. Both solutions are smooth and therefore continuous in $x$. If their difference were nonzero at some $x_0$, continuity would give a nonempty open arc on which its norm is bounded below by a positive constant; every nonempty open arc has positive normalized Lebesgue measure $dm_L$, contradicting equality almost everywhere. Thus they agree pointwise on $\mathbb T_L$. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, given, algebra]

1.2 Put $F_0=(f_{+,0},f_{-,0})$. For $h\ge0$, free transport $T_h(g_+,g_-):=(g_+(\cdot-vh),g_-(\cdot+vh))$ preserves nonnegativity and pair norm. It preserves each component mass because a change of variable turns its integral into an integral of a smooth periodic function over another full period, which is unchanged by [F3]. The pointwise collision operator $C_h$ is the matrix $\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1+e^{-2\alpha h}&1-e^{-2\alpha h}\\1-e^{-2\alpha h}&1+e^{-2\alpha h}\end{pmatrix}$. Its entries are nonnegative and its column sums are one. The orthonormal vectors $(1,1)/\sqrt2$ and $(1,-1)/\sqrt2$ have eigenvalues $1$ and $e^{-2\alpha h}\in[0,1]$, so $C_h$ preserves nonnegativity and total mass and contracts the pair norm pointwise, hence in pair $L^2$. Thus the alternating products $(T_{t/m}C_{t/m})^mF_0$ are nonnegative when $F_0$ is and are $L^2$ contractions. [F1, F3, algebra]

2.1 Fix a Fourier mode $k\in\mathbb Z$ and let $A_k=\operatorname{diag}(-i\kappa_kv,i\kappa_kv)$ and $B=\alpha\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}$. Under the realification of step 1.1, their generators are real $4\times4$ matrices $\widetilde A_k=\mathcal R(A_k)$ and $\widetilde B=\mathcal R(B)$. Transport on this mode is $e^{\widetilde A_kh}$, and collision is $e^{\widetilde Bh}$ because the eigenvectors and eigenvalues in step 1.2 diagonalize $B$. By [F6], $\mathbb R^4$ with its Euclidean norm is Banach; applying [F7] rowwise bounds every real matrix as an operator, and [F8]–[F9] give the Euclidean and operator norms used in [F5]. For $X\in\{\widetilde A_k,\widetilde B,\widetilde A_k+\widetilde B\}$, [F5] gives $e^{Xh}=I+hX+R_X(h)$ with $\|R_X(h)\|\le K_Xh^2$ on $0\le h\le t$, where $K_X:=\tfrac12\|X\|^2e^{t\|X\|}$. Multiplying the first two expansions and subtracting the third shows $\|e^{\widetilde A_kh}e^{\widetilde Bh}-e^{(\widetilde A_k+\widetilde B)h}\|\le C_{k,\epsilon,t}h^2$, where one may take $C_{k,\epsilon,t}=\|\widetilde A_k\|\|\widetilde B\|+K_{\widetilde A_k}+K_{\widetilde B}+K_{\widetilde A_k+\widetilde B}+t(\|\widetilde A_k\|K_{\widetilde B}+\|\widetilde B\|K_{\widetilde A_k})+t^2K_{\widetilde A_k}K_{\widetilde B}$. All factors are contractions: the split factors by step 1.2, and the exact mode evolution by the energy identity in step 1.1. Telescoping the $m$-fold products with $h=t/m$ bounds the error by $mC_{k,\epsilon,t}(t/m)^2=C_{k,\epsilon,t}t^2/m$, so the split evolution converges to the exact solution in each fixed mode. [F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra]

2.2 The zero mode in the equations of [F1] satisfies $(\widehat f_+(0)+\widehat f_-(0))'=0$, since the switching terms cancel and the transport frequency is zero. As $M^\epsilon(t)=L(\widehat f_+^\epsilon(0,t)+\widehat f_-^\epsilon(0,t))$, this proves total-mass conservation. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For a finite set of modes, step 2.1 gives convergence of the split evolution to the exact solution. Let $P_{>K}$ be the Fourier projection onto $|k|>K$, applied componentwise. Its initial pair tail tends to zero by [A1] and [F2]. Every split product preserves modes and contracts this tail by step 1.2. The exact evolution also preserves modes; applying the mode energy identity in step 1.1 and summing by Parseval [F2] shows it contracts the same tail. Hence the split products converge in pair $L^2$ to the solution from step 1.1. [A1, F2, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The nonnegative cone is closed in real $L^2$: if classes $g_m$ have nonnegative representatives almost everywhere and $g_m\to g$, then for representatives of $g$ and $g_m$ the inequality $g^-\le|g-g_m|$ holds almost everywhere, so $\|g^-\|_2\le\|g-g_m\|_2\to0$. Applying this componentwise, the split products are nonnegative by step 1.2 and converge by step 3.1, so each component of the exact solution is nonnegative almost everywhere. Fix $t\ge0$. Step 1.1 gives smooth, hence continuous, functions $x\mapsto f_\pm^\epsilon(x,t)$. If either component were negative at some $x_0$, continuity would give a nonempty open arc on which it is bounded above by a negative constant. Such an arc has positive $dm_L$ measure, contradicting nonnegativity almost everywhere. Thus both components are nonnegative pointwise for every $t\ge0$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Under [A1], apply the mode identity in step 1.1 to the difference of two solutions; summing over $n$ by Parseval [F2] gives the stated contraction. Step 1.1 gives uniform convergence of the solution and time-derivative Fourier series, so termwise differentiation of Parseval's sum is valid. Applying the same sum to one solution gives $\frac{d}{dt}\|F^\epsilon(t)\|_{2,L}^2=-2\alpha\|f_+^\epsilon(t)-f_-^\epsilon(t)\|_{2,L}^2$, which is the displayed dissipation identity because $\alpha=\lambda/\epsilon^2$. [A1, F2, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
