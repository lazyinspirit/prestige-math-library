## Statement

Let $m>0$ and $q_e,q_m\in\mathbb R$ be length parameters, put $Q^2=q_e^2+q_m^2$, and assume $0\le Q^2<m^2$. Use the asymptotically flat dyonic Reissner–Nordström metric with $M=m$ and $f(r)=1-2m/r+Q^2/r^2$ on the exterior $r>r_+:=m+\sqrt{m^2-Q^2}$. Here $r_-$ denotes the other algebraic horizon root $m-\sqrt{m^2-Q^2}$; when $Q^2>0$, $r_\pm$ are the local null Killing horizons of the selected RN model, and when $Q^2=0$, $r_+=2m$ is the larger root. An equatorial circular null geodesic means a smooth nonconstant affinely parametrized geodesic $\gamma(s)=(x^0(s),r_c,\pi/2,\phi(s))$ with $g(\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma)=0$ and nonzero angular momentum $\ell=r_c^2\dot\phi$. Its effective radial function is $h(r)=f(r)/r^2$. The outer circular null radius is

$$r_{\mathrm{ph}+}=\frac{3m+\sqrt{9m^2-8Q^2}}{2}.$$

It lies strictly above $r_+$ and is a strict local maximum of $h$. Along an equatorial geodesic, define the conserved quantities $E:=f(r)\dot x^0$ and $\ell:=r^2\dot\phi$. At fixed $E,\ell$, the null radial geodesic equation is $\ddot r=-\tfrac12\ell^2h'(r)$. Its radial linearization at $r=r_{\mathrm{ph}+}$, defined by writing $r=r_{\mathrm{ph}+}+\epsilon\rho(s)$ and retaining the terms of first order in $\epsilon$, is $\ddot\rho-\nu^2\rho=0$, where $\nu^2:=-\tfrac12\ell^2h''(r_{\mathrm{ph}+})>0$; it has the growing mode $\rho(s)=e^{\nu s}$. The other algebraic stationary radius is $r_{\mathrm{ph}-}=(3m-\sqrt{9m^2-8Q^2})/2$; this proposition assigns it no exterior-orbit interpretation. Coordinates $x^0,r,m,q_e,q_m$ have length units, angular coordinates are dimensionless, and the affine parameter has arbitrary normalization. This is a null-ray calculation for the exact RN metric; it proves no wave-decay or Morawetz estimate.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The dyonic RN metric with $m>0$, $0\le Q^2<m^2$, and a smooth equatorial affinely parametrized null geodesic in the static exterior with nonzero angular momentum.

[F1] [[def-emg-xs-rn-data]]: for $Q^2=q_e^2+q_m^2$ and $f(r)=1-2m/r+Q^2/r^2$, the static metric is $g=-f(dx^0)^2+f^{-1}dr^2+r^2\gamma_{S^2}$ on each open set where $f\ne0$.

[F2] [[ex-the-round-metric-on-the-sphere-as-an-induced-metric]]: in spherical coordinates the unit round metric is $\gamma_{S^2}=d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2$.

[F3] [[prop-emg-xs-rn-horizon-data]]: when $Q^2>0$ and $m^2\ge Q^2$, the positive roots are $r_\pm=m\pm\sqrt{m^2-Q^2}$ and are local null constant-radius Killing horizons. Its theorem's $Q^2>0$ hypothesis is used only for this horizon interpretation; the $Q^2=0$ root is handled directly below.

[F4] [[def-geodesic-of-an-affine-connection]]: an affinely parametrized geodesic satisfies $D_s\dot\gamma=0$ on its interval.

[F5] [[thm-gr-nondegenerate-levi-civita-and-tensor-derivative]]: assuming $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, the metric-selected connection of a smooth nondegenerate metric has coordinate coefficients $\Gamma^a{}_{bc}=\tfrac12g^{ad}(\partial_bg_{dc}+\partial_cg_{bd}-\partial_dg_{bc})$.

[F6] [[def-emg-explicit-power-regimes]]: for $a>0$, $\sqrt a$ is the unique positive real $s$ with $s^2=a$.

[F7] [[def-countable-choice]]: $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ asserts that every sequence of nonempty sets has a choice function. This is the assumption explicitly used by [F5].

[A1] $m>0$, $q_e,q_m\in\mathbb R$, and $0\le Q^2=q_e^2+q_m^2<m^2$.

[A2] Adopt $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ as required by [F5]. No further choice principle is used.

## Proof

1.1 Let $x=\sqrt{m^2-Q^2}$. By [F6] and [A1], $0<x\le m$, so $r_-=m-x\ge0$ and $r_+=m+x>0$. Factoring gives $f(r)=(r-r_+)(r-r_-)/r^2>0$ on $r>r_+$. From [F1]–[F2] the equatorial metric components are $g_{00}=-f$, $g_{rr}=f^{-1}$, $g_{\theta\theta}=r^2$, and $g_{\phi\phi}=r^2\sin^2\theta$. Substitution in [F5] gives $\Gamma^0{}_{0r}=f'/(2f)$, $\Gamma^\phi{}_{r\phi}=1/r$, $\Gamma^r{}_{00}=ff'/2$, $\Gamma^r{}_{rr}=-f'/(2f)$, $\Gamma^r{}_{\phi\phi}=-fr\sin^2\theta$, $\Gamma^\theta{}_{r\theta}=1/r$, and $\Gamma^\theta{}_{\phi\phi}=-\sin\theta\cos\theta$. The $x^0$ and $\phi$ geodesic equations of [F4] are therefore $\ddot x^0+(f'/f)\dot r\dot x^0=0$ and $\ddot\phi+2(\dot r/r)\dot\phi=0$. The $\theta$ equation is $\ddot\theta+2(\dot r/r)\dot\theta-\sin\theta\cos\theta\dot\phi^2=0$, so $\theta=\pi/2$, $\dot\theta=0$ is invariant. Differentiating the $x^0$ and $\phi$ equations shows that $E:=f\dot x^0$ and $\ell:=r^2\dot\phi$ are constant along the geodesic. [given, F1, F2, F4, F5, F6, F7, A1, A2, algebra]

2.1 The null condition is $-f(\dot x^0)^2+f^{-1}\dot r^2+r^2\dot\phi^2=0$. Multiplying by $f>0$ and using the constants from 1.1 gives $\dot r^2+\ell^2h(r)=E^2$, where $h=f/r^2$. [given, F1, A1, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 For a circular orbit $r=r_c$, the radial geodesic equation from 1.1 has $\dot r=\ddot r=0$ and reads $\tfrac12ff'(\dot x^0)^2-fr_c(\dot\phi)^2=0$. The null equation and $\ell\ne0$ imply $r_cf'(r_c)-2f(r_c)=0$, equivalently $h'(r_c)=0$. Direct differentiation gives $h'(r)=-2(r^2-3mr+2Q^2)/r^5$, so its algebraic roots are $r_{\mathrm{ph}\pm}=(3m\pm\sqrt{9m^2-8Q^2})/2$. They are real because $9m^2-8Q^2=m^2+8(m^2-Q^2)>0$. [given, F1, F4, F5, A1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Put $x=\sqrt{m^2-Q^2}$, so $0<x\le m$. Then $\sqrt{9m^2-8Q^2}=\sqrt{m^2+8x^2}>x$, and
$$r_{\mathrm{ph}+}-r_+=\frac{m+\sqrt{m^2+8x^2}-2x}{2}=\frac{(m-x)+(\sqrt{m^2+8x^2}-x)}2>0.$$
For $Q^2>0$, [F3] identifies $r_+$ as the outer local horizon. For $Q^2=0$, [F1] gives $f=1-2m/r$, whose larger root is $r_+=2m$; the same inequality applies. Thus $f(r_{\mathrm{ph}+})>0$ and the outer root lies in the static exterior. [given, F1, F3, F6, A1, step 2.2, algebra]

3.2 At the outer root, $r^2-3mr+2Q^2=0$ and $2r_{\mathrm{ph}+}-3m=\sqrt{9m^2-8Q^2}$, so $h''(r_{\mathrm{ph}+})=-2\sqrt{9m^2-8Q^2}/r_{\mathrm{ph}+}^5<0$. Hence $h$ has a strict local maximum there. [given, A1, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 Since $f(r_{\mathrm{ph}+})>0$, [F6] defines $\sqrt{h(r_{\mathrm{ph}+})}$. Choose $\ell>0$ and $E=\ell\sqrt{h(r_{\mathrm{ph}+})}$. The explicit curve $x^0(s)=Es/f(r_{\mathrm{ph}+})$, $r(s)=r_{\mathrm{ph}+}$, $\theta(s)=\pi/2$, and $\phi(s)=\ell s/r_{\mathrm{ph}+}^2$ is null and satisfies the $x^0,\phi,\theta$ geodesic equations from 1.1. Its radial geodesic equation is exactly the circular condition in 2.2, so it is an affinely parametrized circular null geodesic. For a general equatorial null geodesic, the radial geodesic equation from 1.1 and the constants in 1.1 give
$$\ddot r=-\frac{E^2f'(r)}{2f(r)}+\frac{f'(r)}{2f(r)}\dot r^2+\frac{f(r)\ell^2}{r^3}.$$
Using the null first integral 2.1, $E^2=\dot r^2+\ell^2f(r)/r^2$, cancels the two $\dot r^2$ terms and yields $\ddot r=-\frac{\ell^2}{2}h'(r)$. Thus the first radial variation $\rho$ at the outer circular orbit, with $E,\ell$ fixed, satisfies $\ddot\rho+\tfrac12\ell^2h''(r_{\mathrm{ph}+})\rho=0$, equivalently $\ddot\rho-\nu^2\rho=0$, where $\nu^2=-\tfrac12\ell^2h''(r_{\mathrm{ph}+})>0$ by 3.2. This equation has the growing solution $\rho(s)=e^{\nu s}$, as asserted. [given, F1, F4, F5, F6, A1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 3.2, algebra, construct] ∎

## Statement refuted

Every admitted scattering mode on a nonzero charged subextremal electric
Reissner--Nordström background is amplified, regardless of the test scalar's
charge.

## Statement references

[[def-emg-fx-charged-radial-scattering]]

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The charged radial scattering model on a fixed subextremal electric
RN exterior. We construct an admitted solution for the neutral spherical mode;
no scattering-solution existence is assumed.

[F1] [[lem-emg-fx-charged-radial-equation]]: for a smooth separated solution,
$u''+[(\omega-eQ/r)^2-f(\ell(\ell+1)/r^2+f'/r)]u=0$, with derivatives in
$r_*$.

[F2] [[prop-emg-fx-superradiant-wronskian]]: every admitted scattering
solution satisfies $|R|^2=1-(\omega-eQ/r_+)|T|^2/\omega$.

[F3] [[def-emg-fx-charged-radial-scattering]]: $x=r_*$ is a smooth
coordinate from the exterior onto $\mathbb R$ with $dr/dx=f$; the separated
field is $A_{\mathrm{in}}e^{-i\omega t}Y u/r$. For $e=0$,
$\Theta=\omega x$ and $\omega_H=\omega$, and admissibility requires
$$u=e^{-i\omega x}+Re^{i\omega x}+o(1),\quad
u'=-i\omega e^{-i\omega x}+i\omega Re^{i\omega x}+o(1)\quad(x\to+\infty),$$
$$u=Te^{-i\omega x}+o(1),\quad
u'=-i\omega Te^{-i\omega x}+o(1)\quad(x\to-\infty).$$

[A1] Choose any $M>0$, $0<|Q|<M$, and $\omega>0$, and set $e=0$ and
$\ell=0$.

[A2] For the $\ell=0$ angular mode choose
$Y(\Omega)=(4\pi)^{-1/2}$ on the unit round sphere. It is smooth, satisfies
$\Delta_{\gamma_{S^2}}Y=0$. Since
$d\omega_{S^2}=\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi$,
$$\int_{S^2}Y^2\,d\omega_{S^2}
=(4\pi)^{-1}\int_0^{2\pi}\!\int_0^\pi\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi=1.$$

## Counterexample

1.1 Write $x=r_*$ and use the choices in [A1]--[A2]. By [F3], $x$ ranges over
all of $\mathbb R$ and $dr/dx=f$. The charged radial
equation [F1] becomes
$$u''(x)+\bigl(\omega^2-V(x)\bigr)u(x)=0,\qquad
V(x):=\frac{f(r(x))f'(r(x))}{r(x)}.$$
The definition gives $dr/dx=f$ and $f(r)=(r-r_+)(r-r_-)/r^2$. Since
$r_+>M>r_-$ and $r_+r_-=Q^2$, we have $f(r)>0$ and $Mr>Mr_+>Q^2$
for $r>r_+$, so $f'(r)=2(Mr-Q^2)/r^3>0$ and $V>0$. Thus $V$ is smooth
and real. Moreover,
$$\int_{\mathbb R}|V(x)|\,dx=\int_{r_+}^{\infty}\frac{f'(r)}r\,dr
=\int_{r_+}^{\infty}\frac{2(Mr-Q^2)}{r^4}\,dr<\infty.$$
[given, F1, A1, A2, algebra]

1.2 Define $u_-$ by the Volterra equation
$$u_-(x)=e^{-i\omega x}+\frac1\omega\int_{-\infty}^{x}
\sin\!\bigl(\omega(x-y)\bigr)V(y)u_-(y)\,dy.$$
Iteration gives a uniformly convergent Neumann series: the absolute value of
its $n$th iterated term is at most
$\|V\|_{L^1(\mathbb R)}^n/(\omega^n n!)$, using $|\sin z|\le1$ for real $z$
and the ordered integration region. The sum is bounded on $\mathbb R$; differentiating
the integral equation shows it is a smooth solution of the equation in step
1.1. The tail integrals tend to zero as $x\to-\infty$, and differentiation
under the integral gives
$$u_-(x)=e^{-i\omega x}+o(1),\qquad
u_-'(x)=-i\omega e^{-i\omega x}+o(1)\quad(x\to-\infty).$$
[given, step 1.1, algebra, construct]

2.1 Since $V u_-\in L^1(\mathbb R)$, expanding the sine in step 1.2 into
exponentials and replacing the upper integration limit by $+\infty$ gives
$$u_-(x)=a e^{-i\omega x}+b e^{i\omega x}+o(1),\qquad
u_-'(x)=-i\omega a e^{-i\omega x}+i\omega b e^{i\omega x}+o(1)$$
as $x\to+\infty$, where
$$a=1-\frac1{2i\omega}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{i\omega y}V(y)u_-(y)\,dy,
\qquad b=\frac1{2i\omega}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-i\omega y}V(y)u_-(y)\,dy.$$
The same tail estimate after differentiating the integral equation proves the
displayed derivative asymptotic. [given, step 1.2, algebra]

2.2 For $j=\operatorname{Im}(\overline{u_-}u_-')$, the real-coefficient
equation in step 1.1 gives $j'=0$. The horizon asymptotics in step 1.2 yield
$j=-\omega$, while step 2.1 yields $j=\omega(|b|^2-|a|^2)$ at infinity;
the two oscillatory cross terms have real sum and contribute no imaginary part.
Hence $|a|^2=|b|^2+1$, so $a\ne0$. Put $u=u_-/a$, $R=b/a$, and $T=1/a$.
Then $u$ has the asymptotics in [F3], with unit incoming amplitude and only
the ingoing branch at the horizon. The separated-wave substitution in the
proof of [F1] is reversible here: $Y$ is a nonzero constant, $A_{\mathrm{in}}$
and $fr$ are nonzero, and each displayed step is an equality. Therefore
$\psi=A_{\mathrm{in}}e^{-i\omega t}Y u/r$ solves $\Box_{g,A}\psi=0$ and is
an admitted scattering solution for any fixed nonzero allowed
$A_{\mathrm{in}}$. [given, F1, F3, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra]

3.1 Apply [F2] to this constructed solution. Since $e=0$ and $T=1/a\ne0$,
$$|R|^2=1-|T|^2=1-|a|^{-2}<1.$$
Thus this nonzero-charge background admits a neutral mode whose reflected
radial flux is less than the incident flux, contradicting the universal
amplification statement. [given, F2, A1, step 2.2, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The construction proves existence for the neutral $\ell=0$ mode under the
stated subextremal real-frequency assumptions. It does not assert existence
for every charged mode or for other boundary-value problems.

## Statement

Assume Countable Choice and Dependent Choice, as required by the supplied
causal-curve and push-up results. Let $(M,g,N)$ be a smooth time-oriented
Lorentzian four-manifold. Let $S$ be a smooth embedded two-manifold without
boundary whose induced metric $q:=g|_{TS}$ is positive definite, and let
$p\in S$. Choose an open neighborhood $U\subseteq S$ of $p$ and a smooth
future null normal field $\ell$ along $U$, so
$g(\ell_q,Y)=0$ for every $q\in U$ and $Y\in T_qS$.
Let $\gamma:[0,L_1]\to M$ be smooth up to its endpoints, with
$$\gamma(0)=p,\qquad \dot\gamma(0)=\ell_p,\qquad g(\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma)=0,\qquad g(\dot\gamma,N)<0,\qquad D_\lambda\dot\gamma=0.$$
Here $D_\lambda$ is covariant differentiation along the curve. Put
$\chi_\ell(X,Y):=g(\nabla_X\ell,Y)$ as in
[[def-gr-fx-null-screen-geometry]], and set $B_0:T_pS\to T_pS$ by
$q(B_0X,Y)=\chi_\ell(X,Y)$ for all $X,Y\in T_pS$.
The screen along $\gamma$ is
$$\mathscr S_\lambda:=\dot\gamma(\lambda)^\perp/\mathbb R\dot\gamma(\lambda),$$
with its positive metric and screen derivative $\mathcal D_\lambda$ from
[[def-gr-fx-null-screen-geometry]]. Define
$$\mathcal R_\lambda[X]:=[R(X,\dot\gamma)\dot\gamma].$$
A **normal screen Jacobi field** is a smooth section $J$ of $\mathscr S$ that
satisfies $\mathcal D_\lambda^2J+\mathcal R_\lambda J=0$ and the initial
conditions $J(0)=[X]$, $\mathcal D_\lambda J(0)=B_0X$ for some
$X\in T_pS$. A **focal instant** $\lambda_0\in(0,L_1]$ is a parameter at
which such a field with $X\ne0$ has $J(\lambda_0)=0$.

Put $I^+(S):=\bigcup_{p\in S}I^+(p)$,
$J^+(S):=\bigcup_{p\in S}J^+(p)$, and
$E^+(S):=J^+(S)\setminus I^+(S)$, using the causal-reachability conventions
of [[def-gr-causal-curves-and-reachability]].

If $\lambda_0$ is a focal instant, there is $\varepsilon>0$ such that
$$\gamma(\lambda)\in I^+(S)\qquad (\lambda_0<\lambda<\lambda_0+\varepsilon).$$
Consequently, no point on this portion of the generator belongs to
$E^+(S)$. The conclusion is local along the generator and does not assert
geodesic incompleteness or curvature blow-up. In an SI interpretation take
$x^0=ct$ and $\lambda$ in metres; the proof itself is independent of units.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The spacelike initial surface, future null geodesic normal to it,
the focal screen Jacobi field, and Countable Choice and Dependent Choice.

[F1] On the positive screen $\ell^\perp/\mathbb R\ell$, the null second
fundamental form is symmetric and its endomorphism is
$B_0=q^{-1}\chi_\ell$ ([[def-gr-fx-null-screen-geometry]]).

[F2] A geodesic variation field satisfies the Jacobi equation
$D_\ell^2J=R(\ell,J)\ell$; its initial value and derivative determine it
uniquely ([[thm-gr-jacobi-equation-and-geodesic-deviation]]).

[F3] The symmetric screen index form with the $+\chi_\ell$ initial boundary
term equals the energy second variation for variations starting on $S$ and
ending at $\gamma(L)$. The proof realizes an admissible orthogonal screen lift
$V$ by a family with actual variation field $\partial_sF_s|_{s=0}=V$
([[lem-gr-fx-null-screen-index-form]], proof step 3.1).

[F4] Every point has arbitrarily small strictly convex normal neighborhoods;
in one such neighborhood, causal and timelike reachability are characterized
by future causal and future timelike position vectors, respectively
([[thm-gr-convex-normal-neighborhoods]],
[[thm-gr-normal-neighborhood-causal-cone]]).

[F5] A future causal curve containing a future timelike subcurve has
chronologically related endpoints, under Countable Choice and Dependent
Choice ([[lem-gr-push-up-rule]] clause (c),
[[def-countable-choice]], [[def-dependent-choice]]).

[F6] $q\in I^+(p)$ means a future timelike curve joins $p$ to $q$;
$I^+(S)$, $J^+(S)$ and $E^+(S)$ have the unions and set difference in the
Statement ([[def-gr-causal-curves-and-reachability]]).

[F7] The exponential map is smooth on a neighborhood of the zero section,
and its differential there is invertible, giving the local exponential
charts used to realize and adjust variations
([[thm-gr-local-geodesics-and-exponential-map]]).

[F8] Solutions of a smooth local ordinary differential equation exist on a
common short interval and depend smoothly on the initial data and parameters
([[thm-smooth-dependence-of-ode-solutions-on-parameters]]).

[F9] A smooth geodesic on an interval with an included finite endpoint extends
to an open interval about that endpoint by the local geodesic initial-value
theorem; its proof extends the endpoint position-velocity data using the
geodesic ODE and uniqueness. The geodesic evaluation depends smoothly on
initial data, so a smooth normal field on a neighborhood of $p$ generates a
smooth geodesic variation on a common interval about any compact segment
([[thm-gr-local-geodesics-and-exponential-map]], clauses (a)-(b)). The
Jacobi initial-value theorem gives uniqueness and continuation of the screen
Jacobi field along this extension
([[thm-gr-jacobi-equation-and-geodesic-deviation]], clause (c)).

[F10] A smooth map with invertible differential has a smooth local inverse
([[thm-smooth-inverse-function-theorem-on-manifolds]]).

[F11] A continuous real-valued function on a closed bounded interval takes
every value between its endpoint values
([[thm-intermediate-value]]).

## Proof

**Given:** [F1]–[F11] and the hypotheses in the Statement.

1.1 By [F9], the geodesic extends to an open interval containing $[0,\lambda_0]$.
Choose a smooth curve $\eta(s)\subset U$ with $\eta(0)=p$ and $\eta'(0)=X$.
The geodesics with initial data $(\eta(s),\ell(\eta(s)))$ form a smooth
variation on a common interval containing $[0,\lambda_0]$. Its variation
field $Z$ satisfies $Z(0)=X$ and
$D_\lambda Z(0)=D_X\ell$ by torsion-freeness. For each $Y\in T_pS$,
$\bar q([D_X\ell],[Y])=g(D_X\ell,Y)=\chi_\ell(X,Y)=q(B_0X,Y)$, so its
screen initial derivative is $B_0X$. Uniqueness in [F2] identifies its screen
field with the focal field in the Statement, so that field extends as well.
Choose a parallel
orthonormal frame of the positive screen along this extended geodesic segment
and write $\mathcal R[X]:=[R(X,\dot\gamma)\dot\gamma]$. In this frame the
focal field satisfies $J''+\mathcal RJ=0$. In the parallel frame the pair
$(J,J')$ solves a first-order linear system with continuous coefficient
matrix. On the compact interval the matrix norm is bounded by some $M$.
Choose adjacent subintervals of length $h$ with $hM<1$ (any $h>0$ if $M=0).
For zero initial data at one endpoint, the integral equation bounds the
solution's supremum on such a subinterval by $hM$ times the same supremum,
so the solution vanishes there. A finite subdivision in each direction proves
uniqueness on $[0,\lambda_0]$. Thus zero value and screen derivative at
$\lambda_0$ would force $J$ to vanish identically, contrary to
$J(0)=[X]\ne0$; hence $J'(\lambda_0)\ne0$. Since $J(0)=[X]$ and
$J'(0)=B_0X$,
$\langle J'(0),X\rangle=\chi_\ell(X,X)$. [F1, F2, F3, F9, construct, algebra]

2.1 Fix any $L\in(\lambda_0,\lambda_0+\delta)$, where $\delta>0$ is small
enough that the extension in step 1.1 is defined through $\lambda_0+\delta$.
On $[0,L]$ let
$Y=J$ up to $\lambda_0$ and $Y=0$ after $\lambda_0$. This continuous
piecewise-smooth screen field has $Y(0)=X$ and $Y(L)=0$. Let $I$ be the
screen index form supplied by [F3]. Its displayed formula extends
continuously to piecewise-smooth $H^1$ fields: in a parallel frame the
curvature coefficients are bounded on $[0,L]$, the integral terms are
continuous in $H^1$, and the endpoint trace is continuous because
$$|V(0)|\le L^{-1/2}\|V\|_{L^2}+L^{1/2}\|D_\ell V\|_{L^2},$$
obtained by integrating $V(0)=V(t)-\int_0^tD_\ell V\,d\lambda$ over
$0\le t\le L$. Thus $I(Y,Y)$ is defined. Integrating the Jacobi equation on
$[0,\lambda_0]$ gives
$$I(Y,Y)=\langle J'(\lambda_0),J(\lambda_0)\rangle- \langle J'(0),X\rangle+\chi_\ell(X,X)=0,$$
since the screen value at $\lambda_0$ is zero and
$\langle J'(0),X\rangle=\chi_\ell(X,X)$. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Choose a smooth screen field $W$ supported in a small interval about
$\lambda_0$ contained in $(0,L)$, zero at both endpoints, and with
$W(\lambda_0)=-J'(\lambda_0)$. Integration by parts on each side of
$\lambda_0$ yields
$$I(Y,W)=\langle J'(\lambda_0),W(\lambda_0)\rangle =-|J'(\lambda_0)|^2<0.$$
Therefore $I(Y+\delta W,Y+\delta W)<0$ for sufficiently small $\delta>0$.
In the parallel frame, smooth the single derivative corner of $Y$ on
$[\lambda_0-\epsilon,\lambda_0+\epsilon]$ by a cubic interpolant matching
the values and first derivatives at the two endpoints, leaving the field
unchanged near $0$ and $L$. Since $Y$ is continuous and piecewise smooth,
the interpolant and its derivative stay bounded as $\epsilon\downarrow0$;
the $H^1$ error tends to zero because its support has length $2\epsilon$.
Adding $\delta W$ preserves smoothness, so continuity of $I$ gives a smooth
field $V$ with $V(0)=X\in T_pS$, $V(L)=0$ and $I(V,V)<0$. [F1, F2, F3,
step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 By [F3], realize the orthogonal lift $V$ itself by a smooth curve
variation $F_s$ with $F_s(0)\in S$, $F_s(L)=\gamma(L)$ and
$\partial_sF_s|_{s=0}=V$; the construction in the proof of [F3] preserves
this first variation when it corrects the initial endpoint. Its energy
$E(s):=\frac12\int_0^L g(\partial_\lambda F_s,\partial_\lambda F_s)\,d\lambda$
satisfies $E''(0)=I(V,V)<0$. The first-variation formula gives $E'(0)=0$,
and $E(0)=0$ because $\gamma$ is null. Pointwise,
$$\left.\partial_s g(\partial_\lambda F_s,\partial_\lambda F_s)\right|_{s=0}=2g(D_\lambda V,\dot\gamma)=0,$$
since $V$ is orthogonal to $\dot\gamma$, $D_\lambda\dot\gamma=0$, and the
connection is metric-compatible. Smoothness on the compact interval now gives
a uniform Taylor expansion
$$g(\partial_\lambda F_s,\partial_\lambda F_s)=s^2h(\lambda)+O(s^3),\qquad \int_0^L h(\lambda)\,d\lambda=I(V,V)<0,$$
where $h(\lambda):=\frac12\left.\partial_s^2g(\partial_\lambda F_s,\partial_\lambda F_s)\right|_{s=0}$. [F1, F3, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Choose a convex normal neighborhood $C$ of $\gamma(\lambda_0)$ and
$0<\rho<\lambda_0$ small enough that
$\gamma((\lambda_0-\rho,\lambda_0+\rho))\subset C$. For the fixed $L$,
choose $w\in C_c^\infty((0,L))$ with $w\ge0$, support in
$(\lambda_0-\rho,\lambda_0+\rho)\cap(0,L)$, $w>0$ on an interval about
$\lambda_0$, and $\int_0^Lw=-I(V,V)$. This is possible since $I(V,V)<0$.
Along $\gamma$, put $T=N/\sqrt{-g(N,N)}$,
$\alpha=-g(\dot\gamma,T)>0$, and
$$n:=\frac{1}{\alpha}T-\frac{1}{2\alpha^2}\dot\gamma.$$
Then $n$ is smooth, future null, and $g(\dot\gamma,n)=-1$. Since this
pairing is constant and $D_\lambda\dot\gamma=0$, metric compatibility gives
$g(\dot\gamma,D_\lambda n)=0$. Extend $n$ smoothly to a field $n_s(\lambda)$
along the variation $F_s$, with $n_0=n$, using local trivializations over a
finite cover of the compact parameter interval. The explicit exponential-chart
construction in the proof of [F3] extends $F_s$ smoothly a little past both
endpoints (the endpoint constraints are imposed only at $0$ and $L$); together
with [F9], this lets the speed equation below be posed on an open parameter
neighborhood of $[0,L]$.
For small $s$ and bounded $u$, define in the local exponential charts
$$\Psi(s,\lambda,u):=\exp_{F_s(\lambda)}\!\bigl(s^2u n_s(\lambda)\bigr).$$
The maps are defined on all of $[0,L]$ for sufficiently small $s$, by [F7]
and compactness. For $v\in\mathbb R$, set
$$Q(s,\lambda,u,v):=g_{\Psi(s,\lambda,u)}\bigl(\partial_\lambda\Psi+v\partial_u\Psi,\partial_\lambda\Psi+v\partial_u\Psi\bigr),$$
where $\partial_\lambda\Psi$ holds $u$ fixed. If $a$ is a differentiable
function of $\lambda$, the squared speed of $\Psi(s,\lambda,a(\lambda))$ is
$Q(s,\lambda,a,a')$. By step 4.1, $Q(0,\lambda,u,v)=0$ and
$\partial_sQ(0,\lambda,u,v)=0$. Thus $\widehat Q:=Q/s^2$ for $s\ne0$ extends
smoothly to $s=0$ by Taylor's integral remainder. The term in $Q$ independent
of $u,v$ is $s^2h+O(s^3)$ by step 4.1. The exponential differential at its
zero vector is the identity [F7], so $\partial_u\Psi=s^2n+O(s^3)$; the
$u$-dependent second-order cross term is
$2s^2u\,g(\dot\gamma,D_\lambda n)=0$, while the $v$-dependent one is
$2s^2v\,g(\dot\gamma,n)=-2s^2v$. Therefore
$$\widehat Q(0,\lambda,u,v)=h(\lambda)-2v,\qquad \partial_v\widehat Q(0,\lambda,u,v)=-2.$$
For a scalar parameter $\zeta>0$, apply [F10] to the smooth map
$$\mathcal G(s,\lambda,u,\zeta,v):=(s,\lambda,u,\zeta,\widehat Q(s,\lambda,u,v)+\zeta w(\lambda)).$$
At every $s=0$ solution point $v=(h(\lambda)+\zeta w(\lambda))/2$, its
differential is block triangular with last diagonal entry $-2$, hence
invertible. The local inverse supplied by [F10] therefore gives a smooth
local root function $v=\Phi(s,\lambda,u,\zeta)$. At $s=0$,
$$\Phi(0,\lambda,u,\zeta)=\frac{h(\lambda)+\zeta w(\lambda)}2.$$
Fix $0<\eta<1$. For each $\zeta\in[1-\eta,1+\eta]$, the scalar initial-value
problem
$$a_{s,\zeta}'(\lambda)=\Phi(s,\lambda,a_{s,\zeta}(\lambda),\zeta),\qquad a_{s,\zeta}(0)=0$$
has, for $s$ sufficiently small, a solution on $[0,L]$ depending continuously
on $(s,\zeta)$. Indeed at $s=0$ its explicit solution is
$$a_{0,\zeta}(\lambda)=\frac12\int_0^\lambda(h(t)+\zeta w(t))\,dt;$$
its graphs for $\zeta\in[1-\eta,1+\eta]$ form a compact set. The local root
functions above agree near this graph by their local uniqueness and therefore
patch to one smooth $\Phi$ on a neighborhood of it. The local ODE existence
and smooth-dependence theorem [F8] applies on a finite subdivision covering
that set; compactness gives one common small $|s|$ for all
$\zeta\in[1-\eta,1+\eta]$, with continuous dependence of $a_{s,\zeta}(L)$ on
$(s,\zeta)$. At the endpoint,
$$a_{0,\zeta}(L)=\frac12\left(I(V,V)+\zeta\int_0^Lw\right)=\frac{I(V,V)}2(1-\zeta).$$
Because $I(V,V)<0$, these endpoint values have opposite signs at
$\zeta=1-\eta$ and $\zeta=1+\eta$. Continuity in $s$ preserves those signs
for all sufficiently small positive $s$. For each such $s$, the continuous
function $\zeta\mapsto a_{s,\zeta}(L)$ therefore has a zero
$\zeta_s\in(1-\eta,1+\eta)$ by [F11].
Set $\widetilde F_s(\lambda):=\Psi(s,\lambda,a_{s,\zeta_s}(\lambda))$.
The defining equation gives its squared speed exactly
$$g(\partial_\lambda\widetilde F_s,\partial_\lambda\widetilde F_s)=-s^2\zeta_s w(\lambda).$$
Also $a_{s,\zeta_s}(0)=a_{s,\zeta_s}(L)=0$, so
$\widetilde F_s(0)=F_s(0)\in S$ and $\widetilde F_s(L)=F_s(L)=\gamma(L)$.
The solutions and their first derivatives stay bounded on $[0,L]$ as
$s\to0$, so $\widetilde F_s$ converges in $C^1$ to $\gamma$. On the compact
interval, $g(\dot\gamma,N)<0$ has a uniform negative bound, so the corrected
velocity remains future-directed and nonzero for small $s$; the displayed
norm makes the whole curve future causal and makes it strictly timelike where
$w>0$. On a closed subinterval where $w>0$, its image lies in $C$ for small
$s$. The local cone result [F4] identifies the endpoints of that subcurve as
chronologically related (and supplies the radial timelike replacement).
Thus the endpoint-fixed path from $S$ to $\gamma(L)$ is future causal with a
timelike subcurve. [F1, F3, F4, F7, F8, F9, F10, F11, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct,
algebra]

6.1 The push-up rule [F5] applied to the corrected path in step 5.1 gives
$F_s(0)\ll\gamma(L)$. Since $F_s(0)\in S$, this means
$\gamma(L)\in I^+(S)$ by [F6]. [F5, F6, step 5.1]

7.1 By [F9], the geodesic and its normal variation extend through some
$\lambda_0+\delta_0$, with $\delta_0>0$. Choose $C$ and $\rho$ as in
step 5.1 and put $\varepsilon:=\min\{\delta_0,\rho\}$. For every
$L\in(\lambda_0,\lambda_0+\varepsilon)$ the construction applies, with its
bump and variation allowed to depend on $L$. Hence
$\gamma(\lambda)\in I^+(S)$ for every
$\lambda\in(\lambda_0,\lambda_0+\varepsilon)$. Since
$E^+(S)=J^+(S)\setminus I^+(S)$, no such point lies in $E^+(S)$. This is a
causal-boundary conclusion and contains no completeness or curvature-divergence
assertion. [F6, F9, step 6.1] ∎

## Remarks

The screen index form has a positive boundary contribution $+\chi_\ell$ for
the convention $\chi_\ell(X,Y)=g(\nabla_X\ell,Y)$. Reversing that sign would
invalidate the Jacobi boundary cancellation in step 2.1. The proof is local
and uses only a short interval after the focal instant.

## Statement

Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$. Let $s>0$ and let $g_{\mathrm{ext}}$ be the
regular advanced-chart extension of the Schwarzschild exterior, with
$f(r)=1-s/r$ and stationary field $T=\partial_v$. Give its domain
$M_{\mathrm{ext}}=\mathbb R_v\times(0,\infty)_r\times S^2$ the product
orientation determined by $dv\wedge dr\wedge\omega_{S^2}$, where
$\omega_{S^2}$ is the positive area form of the unit round sphere in its
outward orientation; write $d\Omega$ for its associated positive area measure.
In this orientation,
$\mathrm{vol}_{g_{\mathrm{ext}}}=r^2\,dv\wedge dr\wedge\omega_{S^2}$. Let $C$ be a compact finite
smooth singular four-chain in the exterior closure $r\geq s$, with oriented
boundary
$\partial C=\Sigma_2-\Sigma_1+\mathcal H^++B_{\mathrm{out}}$, where
$\Sigma_1,\Sigma_2$ are spacelike faces with future unit normals $n_1,n_2$,
$\mathcal H^+$ is a future-horizon face, and $B_{\mathrm{out}}$ is the
remaining signed outer boundary. Let $\psi$ be smooth on a neighborhood of
$C$ and solve $\Box_{g_{\mathrm{ext}}}\psi=0$. Define
$j_T^a:=-Q^{a}{}_{b}[\psi]T^b$. Orient each spacelike face $\Sigma_j$ so
that its adapted unit normal is the future unit normal $n_j$, and define

$$E_j:=\Phi_{j_T}(\Sigma_j)=\int_{\Sigma_j}Q_{ab}[\psi]n_j^aT^b\,d\mu_{\Sigma_j}.$$

Define $F_{\mathrm{out}}$ as the signed flux of
$j_T$ through $B_{\mathrm{out}}$ and
$F_H:=\int_{\mathcal H^+}(T\psi)^2\,d\mu_H$, where
$d\mu_H=s^2\,dv\,d\Omega$ is the positive horizon measure in the advanced
chart. Then $E_1,E_2,F_H\geq0$ and

$$E_2+F_H+F_{\mathrm{out}}=E_1.$$

The signed outer flux need not be nonnegative. On the exterior static slices
$x^0=\text{constant}$, $n=f^{-1/2}T$ and
$Q(n,T)=\sqrt f\,Q(n,n)$; this stationary slice energy degenerates as
$r\downarrow s$, where $T$ becomes null in the regular extension.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The chain, boundary decomposition, smooth wave, and Schwarzschild
extension in the Statement.

[F1] [[def-gr-fx-scalar-wave-stress]]: $J_a^T=Q_{ab}T^b$ and the energy-current vector is $j_T^a=-g^{ab}J_b^T$.

[F2] [[prop-gr-fx-wave-multiplier-identity]]: on a solution of $\Box_g\psi=0$, a Killing multiplier $T$ gives $\nabla^aJ_a^T=0$.

[F3] [[def-gr-killing-field]]: $T$ is Killing when $\mathcal L_Tg=0$.

[F4] [[def-gr-schwarzschild-exterior-model]] and [[lem-gr-schwarzschild-metric-properties]]: in the exterior $T=\partial_0$ is future timelike, $g(T,T)=-f$, and the metric coefficients are independent of $x^0$.

[F5] [[lem-gr-schwarzschild-extension-chart]]: the advanced extension has $g_{\mathrm{ext}}=-f\,dv^2+2\,dv\,dr+r^2d\Omega^2$, the exterior is isometric to $r>s$, and $r=s$ is a future null hypersurface generated by $T=\partial_v$.

[F6] [[lem-gr-fx-scalar-dominant-energy]]: for a future unit timelike $n$ and future causal $X$, $Q(n,X)\geq0$.

[F7] [[def-gr-metric-volume-form-and-current-flux]] and [[thm-gr-density-divergence-and-finite-chain-flux]]: for a smooth vector field $j$, $d(\iota_j\mathrm{vol}_g)=(\nabla_a j^a)\mathrm{vol}_g$ and $\Phi_j(\partial C)=\int_C(\nabla_a j^a)\mathrm{vol}_g$.

[F8] [[thm-gr-density-divergence-and-finite-chain-flux]] clause (c): its face formula gives flux density $\varepsilon g(j,\nu^*)\sqrt{|\det(g(v_i,v_j))|}$ for the adapted unit normal $\nu^*$; on a spacelike face oriented with future unit normal $n$, this is $-g(j,n)\,d\mu_\Sigma$ for the induced positive volume measure.

[A1] $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ is the only choice principle used in the smooth Schwarzschild extension and tangent-bundle conventions ([[def-countable-choice]]).

## Proof

1.1 In the advanced coordinates all coefficients of $g_{\mathrm{ext}}$ are independent of $v$, so the coordinate formula for $\mathcal L_{\partial_v}g_{\mathrm{ext}}$ gives $\mathcal L_Tg_{\mathrm{ext}}=0$ and $T$ is Killing by [F3]. The multiplier identity [F2] therefore gives $\nabla^aJ_a^T=0$ on $C$. Since $j_T^a=-g^{ab}J_b^T$, its divergence also vanishes. [given, A1, F2, F3, F5, algebra]

1.2 The Statement orients each spacelike face so its adapted unit normal is the future unit normal $n_j$. By [F8] its current flux density is $-g(j_T,n_j)\,d\mu_{\Sigma_j}$. The definition $j_T^a=-g^{ab}Q_{bc}T^c$ gives $g(j_T,n_j)=-Q_{ab}n_j^aT^b$, so integration yields $\Phi_{j_T}(\Sigma_j)=\int_{\Sigma_j}Q_{ab}n_j^aT^b\,d\mu_{\Sigma_j}=E_j$. [F1, F8, algebra]

1.3 The isometric exterior has $T$ future timelike for $r>s$, and [F5] makes $T$ future null on $\mathcal H^+$. Thus $T$ is future causal throughout the stated closure. Applying [F6] to each spacelike face gives $E_1,E_2\geq0$. [given, F4, F5, F6]

2.1 On $\mathcal H^+$, the inverse of the $(v,r)$ metric block has $g^{rv}=1$ and $g^{rr}=f=0$. Hence $j_T^r=-g^{rb}Q_{bv}=-Q_{vv}=-(T\psi)^2$, since $g_{vv}=0$ there. With
$\mathrm{vol}_{g_{\mathrm{ext}}}=r^2\,dv\wedge dr\wedge\omega_{S^2}$ and the outward boundary orientation at the inner face $r=s$, the pullback of $\iota_{j_T}\mathrm{vol}_{g_{\mathrm{ext}}}$ is $(T\psi)^2s^2\,dv\wedge\omega_{S^2}$. Therefore this face flux is exactly $F_H\geq0$. [F1, F5, F7, step 1.3, algebra]

3.1 By [F7] and step 1.1, $\Phi_{j_T}(\partial C)=0$. The stated boundary decomposition and additivity of flux give $\Phi_{j_T}(\Sigma_2)-\Phi_{j_T}(\Sigma_1)+F_H+F_{\mathrm{out}}=0$. Using step 1.2 to identify the two face fluxes with $E_2$ and $E_1$ gives the asserted balance. [given, F7, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 On the static exterior slices $g(T,T)=-f$ and $n=f^{-1/2}T$, so $T=\sqrt f\,n$ and bilinearity gives $Q(n,T)=\sqrt f\,Q(n,n)$. By [F6] with $X=n$, $Q(n,n)\geq0$, while the coefficient $\sqrt f$ tends to zero as $r\downarrow s$. In the regular extension $g(T,T)=-f$ vanishes at $r=s$ by [F5], so $T$ becomes null there and the static Killing slice density loses its transverse coercivity. [F4, F5, F6, step 1.3, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The calculation proves conservation with the stated side flux and the
degeneracy of the stationary Schwarzschild energy at the horizon. A redshift
estimate remains deferred: its proof route requires a multiplier vector field
that is timelike across the horizon and has positive deformation tensor near
the nonextremal horizon. A Morawetz estimate also remains deferred: its proof
route requires a radial multiplier whose bulk term degenerates at the photon
sphere $r=3s/2$ (here $s=2M$), together with a controlled far-region
$r$-weighted estimate. This finite-region stationary balance proves none of
those estimates. The item does not supply a Kerr scalar-decay theorem or any
gravitational perturbation stability result.

## Statement

Let $a_i(t)=A_i(t/t_0)^{p_i}$ on a nonempty open interval $I\subseteq(0,\infty)$, where $A_i>0$ are lengths in metres, $t_0>0$ is a time in seconds, and $p_i\in\mathbb R$ are dimensionless exponents. These $p_i$ are called Kasner exponents for this power-law branch; define their sums $P:=\sum_{i=1}^3p_i$ and $Q:=\sum_{i=1}^3p_i^2$. For a nonstatic datum, meaning that not all $p_i$ vanish, the geometric vacuum equation $\operatorname{Ric}=0$ in the $C^2$ curvature definition of this page holds exactly when $P=Q=1$. If all $p_i=0$, then the full curvature tensor vanishes, so the metric is flat in the curvature sense. These are exact statements about the specified finite-dimensional metrics; no singularity or generic cosmological conclusion follows.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A diagonal Bianchi I datum, the power-law scale factors in the Statement, and the mathematical vacuum condition $\operatorname{Ric}=0$ where invoked. The inherited foundational context assumes $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ only.

[A1] The Bianchi I product and quotient construction carries $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ and no stronger choice principle ([[def-gr-fx-bianchi-i-data]]).

[F1] The scale factors are positive $C^2$ lengths on $I\subseteq(0,\infty)$, and the definitions of $H_i$, $H$ and $V$ are those of the diagonal Bianchi I datum ([[def-gr-fx-bianchi-i-data]]).

[F2] The local Ricci components are $\operatorname{Ric}_{00}=-\sum_i(\dot H_i+H_i^2)$ and $\operatorname{Ric}_{ii}=a_i^2(\dot H_i+H_iH_\Sigma)/c^2$, with zero off-diagonal components; the only potentially nonzero curvature components are the two displayed families ([[lem-gr-fx-bianchi-i-curvature-components]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct substitution into the local curvature components.

1.1 Work in the inherited $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ context [A1]; the calculation itself uses only finite sums and differentiation. Put $P=\sum_i p_i$ and $Q=\sum_i p_i^2$. The power laws give $H_i=p_i/t$, $\dot H_i=-p_i/t^2$, and $H_\Sigma=P/t$, so [F2] yields $\operatorname{Ric}_{00}=(P-Q)/t^2$ and $\operatorname{Ric}_{ii}=a_i^2p_i(P-1)/(c^2t^2)$. [given, A1, F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 Suppose not all $p_i$ vanish and $\operatorname{Ric}=0$. Choose an index $k$ with $p_k\ne0$. Since $a_k,c,t$ are positive, the equation $\operatorname{Ric}_{kk}=0$ from step 1.1 implies $P=1$. The equation $\operatorname{Ric}_{00}=0$ then implies $Q=P=1$. [step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Conversely, if $P=Q=1$, step 1.1 makes every diagonal Ricci component zero, and [F2] makes every off-diagonal component zero; hence $\operatorname{Ric}=0$. [step 1.1, F2, algebra]

3.1 If $p_1=p_2=p_3=0$, then every $H_i$ and $\dot H_i$ is zero. Both curvature-component families in [F2] therefore vanish, and every other component is zero by that lemma, so the full curvature tensor vanishes. [given, F1, F2, algebra] ∎
