## Statement

Take the finite-chain data of the current definition on $\mathcal H_L$ and identify $\mathcal H:=\mathcal H_L$. Let $H=H^*$ have energy unit joules, let $\hbar>0$ have unit joule-seconds, and let $\rho$ be a positive-definite trace-one matrix satisfying $[\rho,H]=0$. Define $\omega(A):=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho A)$ and $\alpha_t(A):=e^{iHt/\hbar}Ae^{-iHt/\hbar}$. Let $J_L$ be the total lattice current of the finite-volume current definition and center it with respect to this same $\rho$, setting $J:=J_L-\omega(J_L)I$. With no identification of $H$ and $H_L$, the estimate below is an abstract bound for this observable under $\alpha_t$. Its lattice-transport specialization further takes $H=H_L$; then the Gibbs density $\rho_{\beta,L}$ in that definition is stationary and is an admissible choice of $\rho$. More generally, $\rho_{\beta,L}$ can be used with another $H$ exactly when it also satisfies $[\rho_{\beta,L},H]=0$. Then $J=J^*$, as established by the local continuity result. For $m\ge1$, let $Q_1,\ldots,Q_m$ be linearly independent conserved matrices, $[H,Q_a]=0$. Put
$$G_{ab}:=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho Q_a^*Q_b),\qquad v_a:=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho Q_a^*J).$$
Then $G$ is positive definite, the finite Cesàro limit exists, and
$$\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\int_0^T\operatorname{Re}\,\omega\!\left(\alpha_t(J)J\right)\,dt\;\ge\;v^*G^{-1}v\;\ge0.$$
This is a finite-dimensional persistent-correlation bound. It does not identify a thermodynamic Drude weight or assert that either volume/time order of limits exists.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite-dimensional Hilbert space, self-adjoint $H$, positive-definite stationary density $\rho$, centered self-adjoint current $J$, conserved matrices $Q_a$, and $\hbar>0$ in the Statement.

[A1] Faithfulness and stationarity: $\rho>0$, $\operatorname{Tr}\rho=1$, and $[\rho,H]=0$.

[F1] [[def-qsmx-lattice-current-and-limits]]: the lattice total current is $J_L=\sum_{(j,j+1)} j_{j,j+1}$, with $j_{j,j+1}=(i/\hbar)[h_{j,j+1},q_{j+1}]$; the same definition specifies the connected finite-volume correlation convention.

[F2] [[lem-qsmx-lattice-current-continuity]]: each such bond current is self-adjoint and obeys the local continuity equation under the stated bond-locality and charge-conservation hypotheses.

[F3] [[thm-qsm-dt-cesaro-dephasing]]: for finite-dimensional unitary evolution generated by self-adjoint $H$, Cesàro averages of every density matrix converge in trace norm to its equal-energy block-diagonal part $\sum_E P_E\rho P_E$; blocks inside degenerate eigenspaces remain.

[F4] [[lem-qsm-matrix-state-calculus]]: finite Hermitian matrices have orthogonal spectral projections, and the finite trace is cyclic.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** orthogonal projection onto the conserved subspace.

**Given:** [A1], [F1]–[F4], a centered self-adjoint $J$, and linearly independent $Q_a$ with $[H,Q_a]=0$.

1.1 The lattice-current definition fixes $J_L=\sum_jj_{j,j+1}$, and the local-current lemma gives $J_L=J_L^*$. Subtracting its real mean $\omega(J_L)$ preserves self-adjointness, so $J$ is a matrix observable in $\mathcal B(\mathcal H)$. [given, F1, F2, algebra]

1.2 On $\mathcal B(\mathcal H)$ define $(A,B)_\rho:=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho A^*B)$, linear in its second argument. It is positive definite: if $(A,A)_\rho=0$, then $\rho\ge cI$ for some $c>0$ in finite dimension, so $0=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho A^*A)\ge c\operatorname{Tr}(A^*A)$ and hence $A=0$. The invariance $[\rho,H]=0$ gives $(\alpha_t(A),\alpha_t(B))_\rho=(A,B)_\rho$ by cyclicity of the finite trace, so $\alpha_t$ is unitary on this finite-dimensional Hilbert space of matrices. [given, A1, F4, algebra]

2.1 Write $H=\sum_E E P_E$ over its distinct eigenvalues. Then $\alpha_t(J)=\sum_{E,F}e^{it(E-F)/\hbar}P_EJP_F$. For $E\ne F$,
$$\left|\frac1T\int_0^T e^{it(E-F)/\hbar}\,dt\right|\le\frac{2\hbar}{T|E-F|}\longrightarrow0,$$
while for $E=F$ the coefficient is $1$. The sum is finite, so $M_TJ:=T^{-1}\int_0^T\alpha_t(J)\,dt$ converges in matrix norm to $PJ:=\sum_E P_EJP_E$. This block map is the orthogonal projection onto $\mathcal N:=\{A:[H,A]=0\}$ in $(\cdot,\cdot)_\rho$: $PJ\in\mathcal N$, and each off-diagonal block of $J-PJ$ has zero weighted trace pairing with a block-diagonal matrix because $\rho$ is block diagonal. It is the same energy-block projection as the density-matrix dephasing in [F3]; [F3] states the density-matrix version, while the explicit finite expansion here proves the observable version used. [given, A1, F3, F4, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Since $J=J^*$, $(\alpha_t(J),J)_\rho=\omega(\alpha_t(J)J)$. Norm convergence in step 2.1 therefore gives
$$\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\int_0^T\operatorname{Re}\,\omega(\alpha_t(J)J)\,dt=\operatorname{Re}(PJ,J)_\rho=\|PJ\|_\rho^2,$$
because $J-PJ$ is orthogonal to $\mathcal N$ and $PJ\in\mathcal N$. [step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Set $S:=\operatorname{span}\{Q_1,\ldots,Q_m\}\subseteq\mathcal N$, and let $P_S$ be its orthogonal projection. Then $P_SJ=P_SPJ$, so $\|PJ\|_\rho^2\ge\|P_SJ\|_\rho^2$. The Gram matrix $G$ is positive definite by linear independence and the positive-definite inner product. Writing $P_SJ=\sum_a c_aQ_a$, orthogonality gives $Gc=v$, so $c=G^{-1}v$ and $\|P_SJ\|_\rho^2=c^*Gc=v^*G^{-1}v$. Together with step 3.1 this proves the bound. [given, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 3.1, algebra] ∎

## Definition

Let $O$ be a nonempty connected open subset of a smooth time-oriented Lorentzian four-manifold $(M,g)$ of signature $(-,+,+,+)$, with its Levi-Civita connection $\nabla$ and length-coordinate convention $x^0=ct$. Let $\beta$ be a smooth future-directed timelike Killing vector field on $O$, with components in $\mathrm{J}^{-1}$, and fix $k_B>0$ in $\mathrm{J\,K}^{-1}$. On $O$ define

$$T:=\frac{1}{k_B\sqrt{-g(\beta,\beta)}},\qquad u:=k_BT\,\beta,\qquad \varpi_{ab}:=-\frac12(\nabla_a\beta_b-\nabla_b\beta_a).$$

Thus $T$ is in kelvins, $g(u,u)=-1$, and the covariant two-form $\varpi$ has units $\mathrm{J}^{-1}\mathrm{m}^{-1}$. For a smooth chemical-potential scalar $\mu:O\to\mathbb R$ in joules, set $\zeta:=\mu/(k_BT)$ and $\alpha:=-\zeta$, both dimensionless. These fields are defined only on the region where $\beta$ is future timelike; no thermal state or material realization is part of this definition.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The smooth time-oriented Lorentzian domain, its Levi-Civita connection, a smooth future-timelike Killing field $\beta$, a smooth scalar $\mu$, and $k_B>0$ as specified in the Definition.

[F1] [[def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects]]: $M$ is a smooth time-oriented four-manifold, $g$ is a smooth Lorentzian metric of signature $(-,+,+,+)$, timelike means $g(v,v)<0$, and future direction is fixed by the time orientation.

[F2] [[def-gr-killing-field]]: a smooth vector field is Killing when $\mathcal L_\beta g=0$.

[F3] [[def-tgr-stationary-thermal-carriers]]: the metric-selected connection is Levi-Civita; the length-coordinate convention is $x^0=ct$, vector $u$ is unit timelike, $\beta$ has inverse-joule units, and $\mu$ is in joules.

## Proof

1.1 The scalar $s:=-g(\beta,\beta)$ is smooth and strictly positive on $O$. Hence $s^{-1/2}$ is smooth and positive, so $T=(k_B\sqrt{s})^{-1}$ is a smooth positive kelvin-valued scalar and $u=\beta/\sqrt{s}$ is a smooth future-directed vector field. Direct contraction gives $g(u,u)=g(\beta,\beta)/s=-1$. [given, F1, F3, algebra]

2.1 Lowering the smooth vector field $\beta$ with $g$ gives a smooth one-form, and its covariant derivative is a smooth covariant two-tensor. Antisymmetrizing this tensor gives the smooth two-form $\varpi$; the inverse-length derivative of the inverse-joule components of $\beta$ gives its stated units. Since $\mu$ and $k_BT>0$ are smooth, $\zeta$ and $\alpha=-\zeta$ are defined and dimensionless on all of $O$. The only foundational assumption in this construction is the inherited $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ in [F4]; no selection or stronger choice principle is used. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, algebra] ∎

## Definition

Fix an integer $n\geq1$ and a real loop parameter $\delta$. Define $[0]_\delta=0$, $[1]_\delta=1$, and $[r+1]_\delta=\delta[r]_\delta-[r-1]_\delta$ for $r\geq1$. Assume $[k]_\delta\neq0$ for $2\leq k\leq n$; this list is empty when $n=1$. The abstract generator algebra $P_n(\mathbb C,\delta)$ is the unital complex algebra with generators $u_1,\ldots,u_{n-1}$ and relations $u_i^2=\delta u_i$, $u_i u_{i\pm1}u_i=u_i$, and $u_i u_j=u_j u_i$ when $|i-j|>1$. The diagram algebra $\mathrm{TL}_n(\mathbb C,\delta)$ has basis the noncrossing pairings of $n$ ordered bottom and $n$ ordered top points; its product stacks the left factor above the right, deletes each closed loop, and multiplies by $\delta$ per loop. Its elementary diagram $d_i$ has a cap joining top points $i,i+1$, a cup joining bottom points $i,i+1$, and vertical strands elsewhere. The isomorphism $\Phi_n:P_n(\mathbb C,\delta)\to\mathrm{TL}_n(\mathbb C,\delta)$ sends $u_i$ to $d_i$.

For every $1\leq i\leq n-2$, the adjacent relations in the presentation mean both $u_i u_{i+1}u_i=u_i$ and $u_{i+1}u_i u_{i+1}=u_{i+1}$. For matching the imported recursion's notation, write $U_i:=d_i$.

Set $p_1=1_1$. For $1\leq r<n$, put $Q_r=p_r\otimes1_1$ by adjoining one rightmost vertical strand and define

$$p_{r+1}:=Q_r-\frac{[r]_\delta}{[r+1]_\delta}Q_r d_r Q_r.$$

The denominator hypotheses make this recursion defined. For each $1\leq r\leq n$, let $\widehat p_r:=\Phi_r^{-1}(p_r)$. The cited Jones-Wenzl result gives $p_n^2=p_n$ under these unit-denominator hypotheses. For $1\leq r\leq n$, let $\iota_{r,n}:P_r(\mathbb C,\delta)\to P_n(\mathbb C,\delta)$ send each generator $u_i$ to the same-named generator, and let $\jmath_{r,n}:\mathrm{TL}_r(\mathbb C,\delta)\to\mathrm{TL}_n(\mathbb C,\delta)$ append $n-r$ rightmost vertical strands. The maps commute with $\Phi$: on each generator, both composites send $u_i$ to $d_i$ with those added strands, and both send the unit to the identity diagram. Since generators and unit generate $P_r$, the composites agree on every element.

Let $H$ be a nonzero finite-dimensional complex inner-product space whose inner product is linear in its first argument and conjugate-linear in its second. A unital complex-algebra representation is a complex-linear map $\pi:P_n(\mathbb C,\delta)\to\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(H)$ such that $\pi(xy)=\pi(x)\pi(y)$ for all $x,y\in P_n(\mathbb C,\delta)$ and $\pi(1)=I_H$. Here $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(H)$ is the algebra of complex-linear endomorphisms, with composition as multiplication and $I_H$ as unit. Require each generator image to be self-adjoint, meaning $\langle\pi(u_i)x,y\rangle=\langle x,\pi(u_i)y\rangle$ for all $x,y\in H$. Define $P:=\pi(\widehat p_n)$, define $\operatorname{rank}P:=\dim_{\mathbb C}\operatorname{im}P$, and restrict to data with $\operatorname{rank}P>0$. The algebraic representation as a physical boundary state space is an additional model identification and is not part of this definition.

## Tower-map convention

For $r<n$, the presentation tower map $\iota_{r,n}$ is well-defined because the defining relations are unchanged and it preserves the unit. For basis diagrams $E,D\in\mathrm{TL}_r$, appending $n-r$ rightmost vertical strands before stacking gives the same matching as stacking $E$ above $D$ first and then appending those strands; the appended strands create no closed loops. Hence $\jmath_{r,n}(E\circ D)=\jmath_{r,n}(E)\circ\jmath_{r,n}(D)$, and $\jmath_{r,n}(1_r)=1_n$. Bilinearity then makes $\jmath_{r,n}$ a unital algebra homomorphism. The two unital algebra homomorphisms $\Phi_n\circ\iota_{r,n}$ and $\jmath_{r,n}\circ\Phi_r$ agree on every generator and the unit, so they agree on all of $P_r$; this identity transfers the unit-denominator recursion on diagrams to the presentation used by $\pi$.
