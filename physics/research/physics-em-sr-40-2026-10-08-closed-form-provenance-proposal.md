Fix one inertial affine chart, identifying U with an open subset of R^4, and write alpha=sum_i a_i dx^i. Closedness for a C^1 form means partial_i a_j=partial_j a_i. All integration below is ordinary integration along finitely piecewise-C^1 paths.
(1) Local C^1 primitive, supplied in full rather than invoking the smooth-only imported Poincare theorem. On any convex ball B contained in U, fix b in B and set phi(x)=integral_0^1 sum_i a_i(b+t(x-b))(x_i-b_i) dt. For x in B and sufficiently small h, the radial segments for x+h e_j lie in a compact subset of B. The parameter derivative of the integrand is continuous on that compact set. The one-variable FTC expresses its difference quotient as the average of that derivative along x+u h e_j; uniform continuity makes these averages converge uniformly in t. Hence taking the integral of the quotient gives partial_j phi(x)=integral_0^1 [a_j(b+t(x-b))+t sum_i (x_i-b_i) partial_j a_i(b+t(x-b))]dt. Closedness and the chain rule identify the integrand with d/dt[t a_j(b+t(x-b))], so FTC gives partial_j phi=a_j. These partial derivatives are continuous, hence phi is C^1; since all a_j are C^1, phi is C^2. Chain rule and FTC on each path piece give integral_gamma alpha=phi(gamma(1))-phi(gamma(0)) for every piecewise-C^1 path contained in B. This is the C^1 radial proof of Lebl Thm 9.3.6, written explicitly here.
(2) Compact small-cell construction. Given a continuous based null-homotopy H:[0,1]^2 -> U of a piecewise-C^1 loop gamma at p, compactness of K=H([0,1]^2) gives finitely many balls B(b_l,r_l) contained in U whose concentric half-radius balls cover K. To obtain this finite family, cover K by all pairs of such balls centered at points of K; use compactness, then finitely many witnesses. Set r=min_l r_l>0. Uniform continuity of H gives delta>0 such that domain distances less than delta imply image distances less than r/2. Choose a finite rectangular mesh with cell diameter less than delta. For each cell, its lower-left image vertex belongs to a half-radius ball; the entire cell image is therefore contained in the corresponding full-radius ball. Only finitely many ball assignments are made. Refine the bottom mesh by the finitely many breakpoints of gamma if desired; this preserves the small-cell property.
(3) Replace each oriented mesh edge by the affine straight segment between its two image vertices. Each cell's four segments lie in its assigned convex ball, so its boundary integral is zero by (1). Every shared edge is exactly the same segment traversed oppositely by the adjacent cell; therefore finite summation cancels all interior edges. The top and vertical boundary segments are constant, since the homotopy is based. Each bottom loop arc and its replacement chord lie in the ball assigned to the corresponding bottom cell, so (1) makes their integrals equal. Thus integral_gamma alpha=0. No differentiability of H or smoothing theorem is used. The argument needs only continuous based null-homotopies, which the simply-connected definition supplies at every basepoint.
(4) For any two piecewise-C^1 paths from p to q, concatenate the first with the reversed second. It is a loop with zero integral by (3); finite concatenation and reversal give equal integrals. Piecewise-C^1 paths between points actually exist: take any continuous path, cover its compact image by finitely many half-radius balls inside U, apply uniform continuity and a finite subdivision as in (2), and replace the successive arcs by chords lying in the full balls. The resulting polygonal path stays in U and has the same endpoints.
(5) Fix p0 in the nonempty U. For each x let F(x) be the unique real value of the integral over a piecewise-C^1 path from p0 to x; existence follows from (4), and path independence makes the value unique. This defines F by a unique-value relation, without choosing a path simultaneously for every x. For any ball B around x contained in U, (1) and path concatenation give F(y)=F(x)+phi(y)-phi(x) for y in B. Therefore F is C^2 and dF=alpha on U. If alpha is smooth, repeated differentiation of partial_j F=a_j makes F smooth. Loop-integral vanishing and path independence were proved in (3)-(4). No AC or countable-choice principle is used. All existential path/homotopy choices concern a single object at a time, all mesh/ball choices are finite, and the global F uses uniqueness rather than a family of selected representatives.
