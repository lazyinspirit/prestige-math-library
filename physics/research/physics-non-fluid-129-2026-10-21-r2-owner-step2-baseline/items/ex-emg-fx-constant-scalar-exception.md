## Example

Let $m>0$, let $c\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}$, and on the fixed extremal RN background take the constant neutral spherical test scalar $\psi(v,r)=c$. Then $\Box_g\psi=0$, $\phi(v,r)=cr$, and its horizon charge is $H=c\ne0$, while $\phi(v,m)=cm$ does not tend to zero and $\partial_r^2\phi(v,m)=0$ for all $v$. Hence nonzero horizon charge alone does not satisfy the extra decay hypothesis of the conditional horizon-growth proposition and does not force its linear-growth conclusion.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $m>0$, $c\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}$, the fixed extremal RN metric, and $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ for the inherited metric-connection background.

[F1] [[def-emg-fx-extremal-test-scalar]]: an admissible scalar is real, spherical, $C^3$, and solves $\Box_g\psi=0$ on the fixed metric; $\phi=r\psi$ and the horizon is $r=m$ in the regular ingoing chart.

[F2] [[thm-emg-fx-aretakis-spherical-charge]]: for such a solution, $H=\partial_r\phi(v,m)$ is constant in $v$.

[F3] [[prop-emg-fx-conditional-horizon-growth]]: the derivative-growth conclusion additionally requires $H\ne0$ and $\phi(v,m)\to0$.

[A1] [[def-countable-choice]]: the metric-connection suppliers inherit only $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$; the explicit constant solution uses no choice.

## Verification

1.1 The constant function $\psi(v,r)=c$ is real, spherical, and $C^3$. All its first coordinate derivatives vanish, so its scalar Hessian and metric trace vanish; hence $\Box_g\psi=0$ and it is admissible by [F1]. The definition $\phi=r\psi$ gives $\phi(v,r)=cr$. [given, F1, A1, algebra]

2.1 Differentiating $\phi=cr$ gives $\partial_r\phi=c$ and $\partial_r^2\phi=0$. Thus [F2] identifies its constant horizon charge as $H=c\ne0$, and its horizon trace is $\phi(v,m)=cm$. [step 1.1, F2, algebra]

3.1 Since $c\ne0$ and $m>0$, $cm\ne0$, so the horizon trace is constant nonzero and does not tend to zero. The extra hypothesis of [F3] therefore fails, while step 2.1 shows the second transverse derivative vanishes identically. The conditional proposition remains consistent with this example. [given, F3, step 2.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The constant solution checks why nonzero charge alone is insufficient for the conditional growth conclusion. It makes no assertion about decay for solutions generated by global initial data.
