## Definition

Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite simple graph, so $V$ is finite and $E$ is a set of two-element subsets of $V$. Fix an integer $Q\geq2$, write $[Q]=\{1,\ldots,Q\}$, and assign to each edge $e\in E$ a finite activity $v_e\in[0,\infty)$. A spin configuration is a function $\sigma:V\to[Q]$. Its unnormalized Potts weight and partition function are
$$w_P(\sigma):=\prod_{e=\{u_e,w_e\}\in E}\bigl(1+v_e\mathbf1_{\{\sigma(u_e)=\sigma(w_e)\}}\bigr),\qquad Z_P(Q,\mathbf v):=\sum_{\sigma:V\to[Q]}w_P(\sigma).$$
For $A\subseteq E$, let $G_A=(V,A)$, and let $k_G(A)$ be its number of connected components, counting isolated vertices; equivalently, these are the equivalence classes generated by the endpoint pairs of edges in $A$. Put $v_A:=\prod_{e\in A}v_e$, with empty product $1$. The unnormalized Fortuin–Kasteleyn (FK) weight and partition function are
$$w_{FK}(A):=Q^{k_G(A)}v_A,\qquad Z_{FK}(Q,\mathbf v):=\sum_{A\subseteq E}w_{FK}(A).$$
Both normalizers are finite and positive: the spin sum is finite with every term at least $1$, while the empty-edge FK term is $Q^{|V|}>0$. These are finite mathematical weights; no physical units or empirical status are assigned.
