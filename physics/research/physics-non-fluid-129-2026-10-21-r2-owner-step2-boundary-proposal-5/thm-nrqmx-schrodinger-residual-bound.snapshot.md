## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and Countable Choice. Let $H\ne\{0\}$ be a complex Hilbert space, let $T=T^*$ be self-adjoint on $H$, and fix $\hbar>0$. Let $I\subseteq\mathbb R$ be an open interval, let $t_0\in I$, and let $v\in C^1(I;H)$ satisfy $v(s)\in D(T)$ for every $s\in I$ and $s\mapsto Tv(s)$ continuous. Define $r(s)=i\hbar v'(s)-Tv(s)$ and $U_T(t,t_0)=e^{-i(t-t_0)T/\hbar}$ by the Borel functional calculus. Then for every $t\in I$,

$$\|v(t)-U_T(t,t_0)v(t_0)\|\le\frac1\hbar\int_{\min(t,t_0)}^{\max(t,t_0)}\|r(s)\|\,ds.$$

The integral is the ordinary integral of the continuous real-valued function $s\mapsto\|r(s)\|$; the bound is conditional on the stated operator-domain regularity and makes no assertion about an asymptotic approximation outside this residual estimate.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Choice and Countable Choice; a nonzero complex Hilbert space $H$; a self-adjoint $T$; $\hbar>0$; an open interval $I$; $t_0,t\in I$; and the path $v$ with its domain and continuity properties from the Statement.

[A1] Choice supplies the unbounded spectral theorem, and Countable Choice is required by the Banach-valued Bochner integral theorem used below ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-countable-choice]]).

[F1] A self-adjoint $T$ determines the strongly continuous unitary group $s\mapsto e^{isT}$ by the Borel functional calculus; it preserves $D(T)$ and has derivative $iTe^{isT}x$ for $x\in D(T)$ ([[thm-stone-one-parameter-unitary-groups]], [[lem-self-adjoint-operator-generates-a-strongly-continuous-unitary-group]]).

[F2] The spectral theorem supplies the PVM representation of $T$, and the Borel functional calculus defines products and the exponential functions of $T$ on their exact domains ([[thm-spectral-theorem-for-unbounded-self-adjoint-operators]], [[thm-unbounded-borel-functional-calculus]]).

[F3] For a Banach space-valued continuously differentiable curve on a compact interval, the Bochner fundamental theorem gives $w(b)-w(a)=\int_a^b w'(s)\,ds$; this integral is defined by the Bochner-integrable-function construction ([[def-bochner-integrable-function]], [[lem-fundamental-theorem-of-calculus-for-banach-valued-continuous-curves]]).

[F4] The Bochner integral satisfies $\|\int_a^b f(s)\,ds\|\le\int_a^b\|f(s)\|\,ds$ for integrable $f$ ([[lem-bochner-integral-norm-inequality]]).

## Proof

1.1 Fix $s$ in the compact segment joining $t_0$ to $t$, put $W(\tau)=e^{i\tau T}$ and $U(s)=W((s-t_0)/\hbar)$, and define $w(s)=U(s)v(s)$. For $h$ with $s+h$ in the interval, the group law gives $\frac{w(s+h)-w(s)}h=U(s+h)\frac{v(s+h)-v(s)}h+U(s)\frac{W(h/\hbar)-I}{h}v(s)$. By [F1], $U$ is strongly continuous and unitary, the first term tends to $U(s)v'(s)$, and since $v(s)\in D(T)$ the second tends to $\frac{i}{\hbar}U(s)Tv(s)$ by the derivative of $W$ at zero and the scalar chain rule. Thus $w$ is differentiable and $w'(s)=U(s)v'(s)+\frac{i}{\hbar}U(s)Tv(s)=-\frac{i}{\hbar}U(s)r(s)$, after substituting $i\hbar v'=Tv+r$. The derivative is continuous because $v'$, $Tv$, and hence $r$ are continuous and $U$ is strongly continuous. [A1, F1, F2, given, algebra]

2.1 If $t\ge t_0$, apply [F3] on $[t_0,t]$; if $t<t_0$, apply it on $[t,t_0]$ and negate the resulting identity. In either case $w(t)-w(t_0)=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\int_{t_0}^{t}U(s)r(s)\,ds$, using step 1.1. Then [F4] gives $\|w(t)-w(t_0)\|\le\frac1\hbar\int_{\min(t,t_0)}^{\max(t,t_0)}\|U(s)r(s)\|\,ds=\frac1\hbar\int_{\min(t,t_0)}^{\max(t,t_0)}\|r(s)\|\,ds$; the equality uses unitarity of $U(s)$. Since $w(t_0)=v(t_0)$ and $w(t)=U_T(t,t_0)^*v(t)$, unitarity also gives $\|w(t)-w(t_0)\|=\|v(t)-U_T(t,t_0)v(t_0)\|$. This is the required estimate, including $t=t_0$ and either time orientation. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The estimate is an exact bound in terms of the actual residual $r(s)$ and the stated operator-domain regularity. Writing down an asymptotic ansatz or a discrete time step does not bound this continuum residual; no WKB or discretization error claim is made here.
