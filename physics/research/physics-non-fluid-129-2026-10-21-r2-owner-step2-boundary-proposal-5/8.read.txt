## Statement

Let $T=\partial_t$ be the stationary field and let $\Phi$ be the sphere
rotation field, extended to $\mathcal M_{\mathrm K}$. Both are Killing fields.
In a smooth ingoing extension across $r=r_+$, the hypersurface $H^+=\{r=r_+\}$
is null and its null normal direction is generated by
$\chi=T+\Omega_H\Phi$, where
$\Omega_H=a/(r_+^2+a^2)$. In the ingoing product coordinates $(v,r,p)$, the smooth field
$$\overline N:=\partial_v-\frac{2Mr}{\Sigma}\partial_r$$
is timelike on the extension and selects a time orientation agreeing with the
chosen exterior time orientation. On $H^+$ one has $g(\chi,\overline N)=-1$,
so $\chi$ is its future null generator. On the exterior polar chart, $T$ is spacelike
exactly for

$$r_+<r<r_{\mathrm s}(\theta),\qquad r_{\mathrm s}(\theta):=M+\sqrt{M^2-a^2\cos^2\theta}.$$

At the rotation axis $r_{\mathrm s}=r_+$, so the open ergoregion has zero
radial width there.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $M>0$, $|a|<M$, and the Kerr exterior metric on
$r>r_+$.

[F1] [[def-gr-fx-kerr-exterior]]: the Boyer-Lindquist metric coefficients are independent of $t$ and $\phi$, with $g_{tt}=-(1-2Mr/\Sigma)$, $g_{t\phi}=-2Mar\sin^2\theta/\Sigma$, and $g_{\phi\phi}=\sin^2\theta[(r^2+a^2)^2-a^2\Delta\sin^2\theta]/\Sigma$.

[F2] [[lem-gr-fx-kerr-exterior-properties]]: the exterior is a smooth time-oriented Lorentzian four-manifold with $\Sigma,\Delta>0$ and chosen future timelike field $N=-\sharp_g(dt)$.

[F3] [[def-gr-killing-field]]: a smooth vector field $X$ is Killing when $\mathcal L_Xg=0$.

[F4] [[prop-coordinate-formula-for-the-lie-derivative-of-a-covariant-tensor]]: for a covariant $2$-tensor, $(\mathcal L_Xg)_{ab}=X^c\partial_cg_{ab}+g_{cb}\partial_aX^c+g_{ac}\partial_bX^c$.

[F5] [[def-gr-fx-null-screen-geometry]]: a null hypersurface has a null normal field tangent to it; its null generators are the integral curves of that field.

[F6] [[cor-primitives-of-a-continuous-function]]: each continuous function on an interval has a differentiable primitive whose derivative is that function.

[F7] [[thm-real-power-continuity-and-derivatives]]: $x\mapsto x^\alpha$ is differentiable on $(0,\infty)$ with derivative $\alpha x^{\alpha-1}$ for each real $\alpha$.

[F8] [[thm-ck-euclidean-maps-closed-under-algebra-and-composition]]: finite sums, products, and compositions of smooth Euclidean coordinate functions are smooth.

[F9] [[def-gr-inverse-metric-and-musical-isomorphisms]]: in a chart, $\sharp_g(dx^a)=g^{ab}\partial_b$ and $(g^{ab})$ is the inverse matrix of $(g_{ab})$.

[F10] [[thm-sine-and-cosine-derivatives]]: $(\sin x)'=\cos x$ and $(\cos x)'=-\sin x$ on $\mathbb R$.

[A1] $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ is the only choice principle in the smooth tangent-bundle and time-orientation conventions of [F2] and [F5] ([[def-countable-choice]]).

## Proof


1.1 The coefficients in [F1] have no $t$ or $\phi$ dependence. For $T=\partial_t$ and $\Phi=\partial_\phi$, the coordinate components are constant, so every term in [F4] vanishes; hence $\mathcal L_Tg=\mathcal L_\Phi g=0$ and both fields are Killing by [F3]. [F1, F3, F4, algebra]

2.1 Choose $0<\varepsilon<r_+/2$ and use the extension manifold $\overline{\mathcal M}:=\mathbb R_v\times(r_+-\varepsilon,\infty)\times S^2$ with its product smooth structure. Let $\bar\pi:\overline{\mathcal M}\to S^2$ be the sphere projection, $\bar\gamma:=\bar\pi^*\gamma_{S^2}$, $\eta:=\bar\pi^*(\gamma_{S^2}(\xi,\cdot))$, and $q:=\bar\gamma(\xi,\xi)=|\xi|_{\bar\gamma}^2$. These are global smooth tensors or functions; on a polar chart of the new sphere factor, $\eta=\sin^2\theta\,d\widetilde\phi$ and $q=\sin^2\theta$. Fix $r_0>r_+$ and define $f(r)=\int_{r_0}^r (s^2+a^2)/\Delta(s)\,ds$ and $h(r)=\int_{r_0}^r a/\Delta(s)\,ds$ on $(r_+,\infty)$. Their integrands are smooth because $\Delta>0$: repeated use of [F7] gives all derivatives of $\Delta^{-1}$, and [F8] handles the polynomial products. [F6] gives $f,h\in C^1$, and successive differentiation of $f'=(r^2+a^2)/\Delta$ and $h'=a/\Delta$ shows that both primitives are smooth. Set $v=t+f(r)$ and, on the sphere factor, replace each point $(x,y,z)$ by $R_{h(r)}(x,y,z):=(x\cos h-y\sin h,x\sin h+y\cos h,z)$. Repeated application of [F10] makes this rotation smoothly dependent on $r$, and its inverse is $R_{-h(r)}$. Thus the exterior map is a smooth diffeomorphism, with coordinate Jacobian determinant $1$ on every polar chart; there $\widetilde\phi=\phi+h(r)$ and the pullback of the original $\xi^\flat$ is $\eta-aq\Delta^{-1}dr$. Substitution in [F1], using $\Sigma^2+a^2q(\Sigma+2Mr)=(r^2+a^2)^2-a^2\Delta q$, gives the global ingoing expression
$$g=-\frac{\Delta-a^2q}{\Sigma}\,dv\otimes dv+dv\otimes dr+dr\otimes dv+\Sigma\bar\gamma+\frac{a^2(\Sigma+2Mr)}{\Sigma}\eta\otimes\eta-\frac{2aMr}{\Sigma}(dv\otimes\eta+\eta\otimes dv)-a(dr\otimes\eta+\eta\otimes dr).$$
For every $r>r_+-\varepsilon>r_+/2>0$, one has $\Sigma>0$ and $\Delta=A-2Mr<A$, where $A:=r^2+a^2$. If $\Delta\ge0$, then $A^2-a^2\Delta q\ge A^2-a^2\Delta\ge A^2-a^2A=Ar^2>0$; if $\Delta<0$, then $A^2-a^2\Delta q\ge A^2>0$. Consequently the angular restriction in an off-axis polar chart, $\Sigma\,d\theta^2+\frac{q(A^2-a^2\Delta q)}{\Sigma}\,d\widetilde\phi^2$, is positive definite. The determinant of the full metric matrix there is $-\Sigma^2q<0$; its Schur complement in the $(v,r)$ directions is symmetric with negative determinant, hence has one negative and one positive eigenvalue. The full metric therefore has signature $(-,+,+,+)$ off the axis. At an axis point $\eta=0$ and $\Sigma=A$; the $(v,r)$ block has matrix $\begin{pmatrix}-\Delta/\Sigma&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ of determinant $-1$, and the sphere block is $\Sigma\gamma_{S^2}$ on the tangent space, so the extension has the same Lorentzian signature there. Every coefficient in the global expression is smooth across $r=r_+$ because $\Sigma>0$ and no coefficient contains $\Delta$ in a denominator. It is a smooth Lorentzian extension across $r=r_+$. In this chart $g^{-1}(dr,dr)=\Delta/\Sigma$, so $dr(\sharp_gdr)=g^{-1}(dr,dr)=0$ on $H^+$ by [F9]; the normal is null and tangent there. [A1, F1, F2, F6, F7, F8, F9, F10, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Put $A=r^2+a^2$. In the ingoing chart the inverse metric has $g^{vr}=A/\Sigma$, $g^{\widetilde\phi r}=a/\Sigma$, and $g^{rr}=\Delta/\Sigma$. Thus on $H^+$, $\sharp_g(dr)=(A/\Sigma)\partial_v+(a/\Sigma)\partial_{\widetilde\phi}$ by [F9]. The stationary and axial fields remain $\partial_v$ and $\partial_{\widetilde\phi}$, so $\sharp_g(dr)=(A/\Sigma)(T+(a/A)\Phi)$. It follows that the null normal direction is $\chi=T+\Omega_H\Phi$ with $\Omega_H=a/(r_+^2+a^2)$. Equivalently, $\Delta(r_+)=0$ gives $2Mr_+=r_+^2+a^2$, and direct substitution yields $g(\chi,\chi)|_{r_+}=0$. The time orientation also extends explicitly. Write $R:=\partial_r$ in the ingoing product coordinates and set $\overline N:=T-(2Mr/\Sigma)R$. Step 2.1 gives $g(T,R)=1$, $g(R,R)=0$, and $g(T,T)=-(\Delta-a^2q)/\Sigma$. Since $\Sigma=r^2+a^2(1-q)$,
$$g(\overline N,\overline N)=-\frac{\Delta-a^2q+4Mr}{\Sigma}=-\frac{\Sigma+2Mr}{\Sigma}<0$$
on the entire extension, where $r>r_+-\varepsilon>0$. Both product coordinate fields and $\Sigma$ are smooth globally, including the rotation axis, so this is a smooth nowhere-zero timelike field there. On the exterior overlap, $t=v-f(r)$ and $f'=(r^2+a^2)/\Delta$ from step 2.1 imply
$$dt(\overline N)=1+\frac{2Mr(r^2+a^2)}{\Sigma\Delta}>0.$$
Thus $g(N,\overline N)=-dt(\overline N)<0$ by [F2], so the two timelike fields select the same future cone on the overlap. Finally put $A_+:=r_+^2+a^2$ and $\Sigma_+:=r_+^2+a^2(1-q)$. On $H^+$,
$$g(\chi,R)=1-a\Omega_Hq=\frac{\Sigma_+}{A_+}>0.$$
Since $\chi$ is normal to $H^+$ and $T$ is tangent, $g(\chi,T)=0$ there; using $2Mr_+=A_+$ gives
$$g(\chi,\overline N)=-\frac{A_+}{\Sigma_+}\frac{\Sigma_+}{A_+}=-1<0.$$
Therefore the nonzero null generator $\chi$ is future-directed in this proved compatible time orientation, without an extra orientation hypothesis. [A1, F1, F2, F5, F9, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 From [F1], $g(T,T)=-(1-2Mr/\Sigma)$. It is positive precisely when $r^2-2Mr+a^2\cos^2\theta<0$. The two roots of this quadratic in $r$ are $M\pm\sqrt{M^2-a^2\cos^2\theta}$; because $|a|<M$, they are real, and the upper root is $r_{\mathrm s}(\theta)$. The lower root is at most $r_-$, since $\cos^2\theta\le1$, and $r_-<r_+$; restricting the interval between the roots to the exterior therefore gives exactly $r_+<r<r_{\mathrm s}(\theta)$. At $\theta=0,\pi$ the upper root equals $r_+$. If $a\ne0$ and $0<\theta<\pi$, then $a^2\cos^2\theta<a^2$, so the upper root exceeds $r_+$; if $a=0$, the upper root equals $r_+$ for every $\theta$ and the ergoregion is empty. This proves the stated condition in all allowed cases. [F1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The Boyer-Lindquist coordinates are singular at $r=r_+$; the proposition uses
the explicit regular ingoing extension above. It asserts no uniqueness of the
Kerr solution among stationary vacuum metrics.
