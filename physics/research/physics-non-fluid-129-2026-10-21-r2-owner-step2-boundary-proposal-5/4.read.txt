## Statement

Let $A_1,A_2:\Xi\to\mathbb C$ be jointly measurable classical phasor amplitudes
at one fixed observation point for a single selected scalar field component,
on one probability space, each in V m$^{-1}$, with
$\mathbb E(|A_1|^2+|A_2|^2)<\infty$. Define
$I_j:=\mathbb E[|A_j|^2]$ and
$\Gamma_{12}:=\mathbb E[A_1\overline{A_2}]$. Adopt the ideal scalar
two-beam model with a phase multiplier ranging over the full unit circle
$\mathbb T:=\{\zeta\in\mathbb C:|\zeta|=1\}$. For each $\zeta\in\mathbb T$,
set $A_\zeta:=A_2+\zeta A_1$ and $I(\zeta):=\mathbb E[|A_\zeta|^2]$.
The quantities $I_j$ and $I(\zeta)$
are mean-square field coordinates in (V m$^{-1}$)$^2$, not calibrated
irradiances. Set
$I_{\max}:=\max_{\zeta\in\mathbb T}I(\zeta)$ and
$I_{\min}:=\min_{\zeta\in\mathbb T}I(\zeta)$. When $I_1+I_2>0$, define the
model visibility by
$$V:=\frac{I_{\max}-I_{\min}}{I_{\max}+I_{\min}}.$$
Then
$$I(\zeta)=I_1+I_2+2\operatorname{Re}(\zeta\Gamma_{12}),\qquad V=\frac{2|\Gamma_{12}|}{I_1+I_2}\le1.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The common probability space, square-integrable beam amplitudes
$A_1,A_2$, a full controllable phase sweep, and the ideal mean-square detector
model stated above.

[F1] [[def-emx-coherency-matrix]]: applying its second-moment construction to
$W=(A_1,A_2)^{\mathsf T}$ gives $\Gamma_{11}=I_1$,
$\Gamma_{22}=I_2$, and $\Gamma_{12}=\mathbb E[A_1\overline{A_2}]$. Here the
coordinates are distinct beam channels, not polarization components.

[F2] [[pthm-emx-stokes-positivity]]: every such second-moment matrix is
positive semidefinite, so its entries obey
$I_1I_2\ge|\Gamma_{12}|^2$.

[F3] [[cor-expectation-linearity-monotonicity-and-modulus-bound]]: expectation
of the integrable cross terms is linear and commutes with complex conjugation
under the componentwise complex-integral convention.

[F4] [[def-complex-conjugate-real-imaginary-part-and-modulus]]: write a complex
number as $a+ib$, take its conjugate as $a-ib$, and define its modulus by
$\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$.

[A1] The ideal detector output is the ensemble mean of the squared modulus of
the coherently summed phasor; no finite-sample or noisy detector response is
assumed.

## Proof

1.1 Expanding the squared modulus in [A1] gives
$|A_\zeta|^2=|A_2|^2+|A_1|^2+
\zeta A_1\overline{A_2}+\overline\zeta A_2\overline{A_1}$.
Taking expectations, using [F1] and [F3], yields
$I(\zeta)=I_1+I_2+2\operatorname{Re}(\zeta\Gamma_{12})$.
[given, F1, F3, A1, algebra]

2.1 Put $S=I_1+I_2$ and $c=\Gamma_{12}$. Write
$\zeta=a+ib$ and $c=u+iv$. Since $\zeta\in\mathbb T$,
$a^2+b^2=1$. By [F4], $|c|^2=u^2+v^2$ and
$\operatorname{Re}(\zeta c)=au-bv$. Therefore
$$|c|^2-\bigl(\operatorname{Re}(\zeta c)\bigr)^2=(a^2+b^2)(u^2+v^2)-(au-bv)^2=(av+bu)^2\ge0,$$
so each $\operatorname{Re}(\zeta c)$ lies in $[-|c|,|c|]$. If $c\ne0$,
set $r:=|c|=\sqrt{u^2+v^2}>0$ and $\zeta_0:=(u-iv)/r$. Then
$|\zeta_0|^2=(u^2+v^2)/r^2=1$, so $\zeta_0\in\mathbb T$, and direct
multiplication gives $\zeta_0c=r$ while $(-\zeta_0)c=-r$. Both endpoints
are therefore attained. If $c=0$, the cross term is identically zero. Hence
$I_{\max}=S+2|c|$ and $I_{\min}=S-2|c|$.
[step 1.1, given, F4, algebra]

3.1 By [F2],
$S^2-4|c|^2=(I_1-I_2)^2+4(I_1I_2-|c|^2)\ge0$; since $S\ge0$, this gives
$2|c|\le S$. Under the given $S>0$ condition,
$I_{\max}+I_{\min}=2S>0$, so substitution of step 2.1 into the visibility
definition gives $V=2|c|/S\le1$.
[given, step 2.1, F2, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The displayed $V$ is a phase-extremized ensemble-model contrast. A finite set
of detector readings need not attain it; estimating visibility from data
requires separate sampling, calibration, uncertainty, and detector-response
assumptions. This theorem supplies none of those empirical inputs.
