## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice, let $\hbar>0$, and use the transverse Hilbert space, helicity projectors, and dense domain $\mathcal D$ defined above. Let $e_1,e_2,e_3$ be the standard coordinate basis of $\mathbb R^3$. Let $SO(3)=\{R\in M_3(\mathbb R):R^TR=I,\det R=1\}$ with the topology inherited from the Frobenius matrix norm $\|A\|_F=(\sum_{i,j=1}^3|A_{ij}|^2)^{1/2}$. For $R\in SO(3)$ define $U(R)f(\mathbf k)=R f(R^{-1}\mathbf k)$. Then $U$ is a strongly continuous unitary representation on $\mathcal H_T^\nu$, and $P_\sigma(\mathbf k/|\mathbf k|)\,U(R)=U(R)\,P_\sigma(\mathbf k/|\mathbf k|)$ for $\sigma=\pm1$. For a unit axis $\mathbf a$, put $R_{\mathbf a}(t)=\exp(t[\mathbf a]_\times)$, where $[\mathbf a]_\times v=\mathbf a\times v$, and define $(S_{\mathbf a})_{ij}=-i\sum_{\ell=1}^3a_\ell\epsilon_{\ell ij}$, $L_{\mathbf a}=-i\hbar\,(\mathbf a\times\mathbf k)\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf k}$, and $S_{\mathbf a}^{\mathrm{op}}=\hbar S_{\mathbf a}$. The unique self-adjoint Stone generator $J_{\mathbf a}$ specified by $U(R_{\mathbf a}(t))=\exp(-itJ_{\mathbf a}/\hbar)$ acts on $\mathcal D$ as $J_{\mathbf a}=L_{\mathbf a}+S_{\mathbf a}^{\mathrm{op}}$. This total generator preserves transversality, while its orbital and spin terms generally do not separately preserve the transverse space. The stated self-adjoint operators are the Stone generators; the differential expression is their restriction to $\mathcal D$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice; $\hbar>0$; the weighted transverse Hilbert space and dense domain $\mathcal D$; the standard Euclidean dot and cross products; and the matrix topology on $SO(3)$.

[A1] Assume the Axiom of Choice. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] $\mathrm{AC}$ implies $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, so the countable-choice hypothesis of the transverse-space definition is available. [[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]] [[def-countable-choice]]

[F2] The weighted transverse space is Hilbert, $\mathcal D$ is dense, the multiplier $Wf=f/\sqrt{2|\mathbf k|}$ is unitary to the Lebesgue transverse space, orthogonal maps preserve $d\nu$, and every radial annulus has finite $\nu$-measure. [[def-qftx-photon-helicity-bundle]]

[F3] A strongly continuous one-parameter unitary group on a complex Hilbert space has a unique self-adjoint Stone generator: $V(t)=e^{itT}$ and $\frac{d}{dt}V(t)x|_{t=0}=iTx$ exactly for $x\in D(T)$. This theorem assumes $\mathrm{AC}$. [[thm-stone-one-parameter-unitary-groups]]

[F4] Closed and bounded subsets of finite-dimensional Euclidean space are compact, and continuous maps from compact metric spaces to metric spaces are uniformly continuous. [[thm-heine-borel-rn]] [[thm-heine-cantor-metric]]

[F5] There is a smooth compactly supported bump that equals $1$ on a smaller ball inside a prescribed larger ball. [[lem-smooth-bump-between-concentric-euclidean-balls]]

[F6] A continuous real-valued function on a nonempty compact metric space attains its minimum and maximum. [[thm-extreme-value-metric]]

## Proof

**Given:** [A1], [F1]–[F6], $\hbar>0$, $R\in SO(3)$, and $f\in\mathcal H_T^\nu$.

1.1 By [F1], the Axiom of Choice [A1] supplies the Countable Choice hypothesis of [F2]. For $\mathbf k\in M$,
$$\mathbf k\cdot U(R)f(\mathbf k)=\mathbf k\cdot Rf(R^{-1}\mathbf k)=(R^{-1}\mathbf k)\cdot f(R^{-1}\mathbf k)=0,$$
so $U(R)$ preserves transversality. Since $R$ preserves $d\nu$ by [F2] and preserves the Hermitian norm on $\mathbb C^3$, change of variables gives $\|U(R)f\|_\nu=\|f\|_\nu$. The same formula with $R^{-1}$ is an inverse, so $U(R)$ is unitary. [A1, F1, F2, given, algebra]

1.2 On the Lebesgue transverse space write $U_0(R)u(\mathbf k)=Ru(R^{-1}\mathbf k)$. Since $|R^{-1}\mathbf k|=|\mathbf k|$, the multiplier $W$ satisfies
$$W(U(R)f)(\mathbf k)=\frac{Rf(R^{-1}\mathbf k)}{\sqrt{2|\mathbf k|}}=R\frac{f(R^{-1}\mathbf k)}{\sqrt{2|R^{-1}\mathbf k|}}=U_0(R)(Wf)(\mathbf k).$$
Thus the weighted and Lebesgue rotation actions are unitarily intertwined. [F2, given, algebra]

1.3 For $R\in SO(3)$, $(R\mathbf n)\times(Rv)=R(\mathbf n\times v)$: pairing both sides with any real $w$ reduces the equality to invariance of the oriented scalar triple product under $R$. Hence $P(R\mathbf n)=RP(\mathbf n)R^{-1}$, $h(R\mathbf n)=Rh(\mathbf n)R^{-1}$, and $P_\sigma(R\mathbf n)=RP_\sigma(\mathbf n)R^{-1}$. Substituting $\mathbf n=R^{-1}\mathbf k/|R^{-1}\mathbf k|$ proves that the multiplication projector $P_\sigma$ commutes with $U(R)$. [F2, given, algebra]

2.1 For $R_1,R_2\in SO(3)$, $R_1R_2$ is again orthogonal with determinant $1$, and $R^{-1}=R^T$. Hence
$$U(R_1)U(R_2)f(\mathbf k)=R_1R_2f(R_2^{-1}R_1^{-1}\mathbf k)=U(R_1R_2)f(\mathbf k),$$
and $U(I)=I$, so $U$ is a unitary representation. [given, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 First let $f\in\mathcal D$ be nonzero. Its compact support is a compact subset of $M$, so [F6] applied to $\mathbf k\mapsto|\mathbf k|$ gives $0<r_0\le|\mathbf k|\le r_1<\infty$ on the support. Thus it lies in a radial annulus $K=\{r_0\le|\mathbf k|\le r_1\}$. The set $SO(3)\times K$ is closed and bounded in a finite-dimensional Euclidean space: the equations defining $SO(3)$ are closed and its matrix entries have absolute value at most $1$. Hence it is compact by [F4]. The map $(R,\mathbf k)\mapsto Rf(R^{-1}\mathbf k)$ is continuous there, so [F4] makes it uniformly continuous. Rotations preserve $K$ and $\nu(K)<\infty$ by [F2]; therefore $R\mapsto U(R)f$ is continuous in $L^2(d\nu)$. For $f=0$ this continuity is immediate. For general $f\in\mathcal H_T^\nu$, choose $g\in\mathcal D$ with $\|f-g\|_\nu<\varepsilon$ by [F2]. Unitarity gives
$$\|U(R)f-U(R_0)f\|_\nu\le2\varepsilon+\|U(R)g-U(R_0)g\|_\nu,$$
so continuity on $\mathcal D$ extends to all $f$ as $R\to R_0$. [F2, F4, F6, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For a unit axis $\mathbf a$ and $f\in\mathcal D$, differentiating $R_{\mathbf a}(t)f(R_{\mathbf a}(-t)\mathbf k)$ at $t=0$ gives $(\mathbf a\times f)(\mathbf k)-(\mathbf a\times\mathbf k)\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf k}f(\mathbf k)$. Because $-iS_{\mathbf a}v=\mathbf a\times v$, this equals $-\frac{i}{\hbar}(L_{\mathbf a}+S_{\mathbf a}^{\mathrm{op}})f$. The support stays in a fixed annulus for $t$ near $0$; the composite is smooth on a compact time interval times that annulus, so [F4] makes its $t$-derivative uniformly continuous. The integral form of the difference quotient gives uniform convergence in $\mathbf k$, hence convergence in $L^2(d\nu)$ because the annulus has finite measure. Thus every $f\in\mathcal D$ lies in the domain of the Stone generator and the differential formula holds there. [given, F2, F4, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 If $f\in\mathcal D$, then $(\mathbf a\times\mathbf k)\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf k}(\mathbf k\cdot f)=0$; expanding this derivative and using $\mathbf k\cdot f=0$ gives $\mathbf k\cdot L_{\mathbf a}f=i\hbar(\mathbf k\times f)\cdot\mathbf a$. The matrix definition of $S_{\mathbf a}$ gives $\mathbf k\cdot S_{\mathbf a}^{\mathrm{op}}f=-i\hbar(\mathbf k\times f)\cdot\mathbf a$. Their sum is zero, so $J_{\mathbf a}$ preserves transversality on $\mathcal D$. [given, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 The separate terms need not preserve transversality. For $\mathbf a=e_3$, choose by [F5] a smooth bump $\rho$ equal to $1$ on $B_{1/8}(0)$ and supported in $B_{1/4}(0)$, and translate it to $\chi(\mathbf k)=\rho(\mathbf k-e_1)$. Its support misses the $e_3$ axis. Put $f(\mathbf k)=\chi(\mathbf k)(e_3\times\mathbf k)$. Then $f\in\mathcal D$, while $(\mathbf k\times f)\cdot e_3=\chi(\mathbf k)(k_1^2+k_2^2)$ is nonzero near $e_1$. By step 4.1, both $\mathbf k\cdot L_{e_3}f$ and $\mathbf k\cdot S_{e_3}^{\mathrm{op}}f$ are nonzero there, with opposite signs. [F2, F5, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Put $A=[\mathbf a]_\times$, so $A^T=-A$. The norm-convergent power series for the matrix exponential gives continuity in $t$, $R_{\mathbf a}(s+t)=R_{\mathbf a}(s)R_{\mathbf a}(t)$, and $R_{\mathbf a}(t)^T=R_{\mathbf a}(-t)$; hence $R_{\mathbf a}(t)$ is orthogonal. Its determinant is continuous, takes values in $\{+1,-1\}$, and equals $1$ at $t=0$, so it is $1$ for all $t$ and $R_{\mathbf a}(t)\in SO(3)$. Therefore $t\mapsto U(R_{\mathbf a}(t))$ is a strongly continuous one-parameter unitary group by steps 1.1, 2.1, and 2.2. Applying Stone's theorem [F3] gives its unique self-adjoint generator $T_{\mathbf a}$ with $U(R_{\mathbf a}(t))=e^{itT_{\mathbf a}}$. Set $J_{\mathbf a}=-\hbar T_{\mathbf a}$; it is self-adjoint and satisfies $U(R_{\mathbf a}(t))=e^{-itJ_{\mathbf a}/\hbar}$. The derivative characterization in [F3] and step 3.1 give $J_{\mathbf a}=L_{\mathbf a}+S_{\mathbf a}^{\mathrm{op}}$ on $\mathcal D$. This uses Stone's self-adjoint generator and makes no essential-self-adjointness claim about the restricted differential expression. [A1, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

For each $R\in SO(3)$, the unitary $W$ from the preceding measure-isometry lemma intertwines the weighted and Lebesgue realizations of this rotation action:

```tikzcd
\begin{tikzcd}
\mathcal H_T^\nu \arrow[r,"W"] \arrow[d,"U_\nu(R)"'] & \mathcal H_T^0 \arrow[d,"U_0(R)"]\\
\mathcal H_T^\nu \arrow[r,"W"] & \mathcal H_T^0
\end{tikzcd}
```

The vertical arrows are the actions $f(\mathbf k)\mapsto Rf(R^{-1}\mathbf k)$ in the indicated Hilbert spaces. The diagram records the identity $WU_\nu(R)=U_0(R)W$, proved by direct substitution in the measure-isometry lemma; no step of the proof above relies on the diagram.
