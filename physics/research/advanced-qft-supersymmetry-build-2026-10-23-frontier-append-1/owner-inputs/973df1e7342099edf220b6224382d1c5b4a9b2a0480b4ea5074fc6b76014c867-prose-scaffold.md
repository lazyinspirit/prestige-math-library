## Gauge geometry and a conditional Yang–Mills calculation

Let `M` be one of the stated smooth four-manifolds and let `pi:P -> M` be a
smooth principal right `G`-bundle. A principal connection is a `g`-valued
one-form `A` on `P` satisfying `R_g^*A=Ad_{g^{-1}}A` for every `g in G` and
`A(xi_P)=xi` for each fundamental vertical vector `xi_P`. Its curvature is
the horizontal, equivariant two-form

$$F_A = dA + \tfrac12[A\wedge A].$$

In a local section, `A` and `F_A` are `g`-valued forms on the base and the same
formula holds. The bracket on forms combines wedge product with the Lie bracket.
The Bianchi identity `d_A F_A = 0`, where `d_A = d + [A,\cdot]`, follows from
the graded Jacobi identity and the definition of curvature; it is not an
independent physical postulate.

For a local gauge map `u:M -> G`, choose the convention

$$A^u = u^{-1}Au + u^{-1}du, \qquad F_{A^u}=u^{-1}F_Au.$$

The curvature identity follows by substituting `A^u` into `dA + A\wedge A`
and using `d(u^{-1})=-u^{-1}(du)u^{-1}`; the terms containing `du` cancel,
leaving conjugation of `F_A`. The exact component proof, patch compatibility,
and regularity hypotheses are assigned to the named local lemma in the
inventory. This transformation law makes an invariant quadratic form of
`F_A` gauge invariant; it does not define a quantum functional measure.

On a Lorentzian four-manifold, fix a positive-definite `Ad`-invariant inner
product `⟨·,·⟩` on the compact Lie algebra, with its trace normalization
specified, a positive dimensionless coupling `g_YM` in the four-dimensional
normalization, and the metric Hodge operator. This choice fixes the sign of
the gauge kinetic energy. The adopted classical action is

$$S_{YM}[A] = -\frac{1}{2g_{YM}^2}\int_M \langle F_A\wedge *F_A\rangle.$$

Its variation is taken through smooth compactly supported connection
variations `a`. Since `\delta F_A=d_A a`, invariance of the inner product gives

$$d\,\langle a\wedge *F_A\rangle
=\langle d_Aa\wedge *F_A\rangle-\langle a\wedge d_A*F_A\rangle.$$

The same positive inner product is used in the pairing below. The boundary
term vanishes for compactly supported `a`, so covariant integration by parts yields

$$\delta S_{YM}[A](a) = -\frac{1}{g_{YM}^2}\int_M\langle a\wedge d_A *F_A\rangle.$$

Thus stationary points under all such `a` satisfy `d_A *F_A=0`. This is a
conditional Euler–Lagrange deduction from the specified action, field space,
inner product, and boundary convention. The action itself is an adopted model
postulate; it is not deduced from the principal-bundle definitions or from
experimental evidence. The proof must check form degrees, signs, trace
normalization, and the no-boundary-term condition locally.

For a representation `R:G->GL(V_R)` and a closed piecewise smooth curve `C`,
the Wilson loop is the trace in `V_R` of parallel transport around `C`:

$$W_R(C;A)=\operatorname{tr}_{V_R}\,\mathcal P\exp\!\left(-\oint_C R_*(A)\right).$$

The sign in the exponential follows the chosen parallel-transport convention.
Here `\mathcal P\exp` means the endpoint of the matrix ODE for parallel
transport along a parameterization of `C`, with later parameters ordered to
the left. The exact definition states the curve's regularity and
base-point/conjugacy conventions. An 't Hooft line is not merely
another Wilson representation: it is specified by a magnetic singularity or
bundle/transition-function condition on a small linking two-sphere. The
allowed electric/magnetic charge lattice depends on the global form of `G` and
the chosen spectrum of line operators.

## Formal gauge fixing, BRST, and perturbative renormalization

The BRST section begins only after the gauge action and field transformations
are fixed. A BRST differential is an odd degree-one derivation `s` on a
specified graded algebra of gauge fields, ghosts, antighosts, and auxiliary
fields. On a local non-Abelian potential and ghost `c`, a conventionally
normalized example is

$$sA=d_Ac, \qquad sc=-\tfrac12[c,c].$$

Then `s^2 A = d_A(sc)+[sA,c] = 0` by the graded Jacobi identity and the chosen
sign convention; likewise `s^2 c=0`. This is a finite algebraic identity on
the declared jet or field algebra. It does not prove that gauge fixing is
globally possible, that a Faddeev–Popov determinant is nonzero, that Gribov
copies are absent, or that a BRST charge acts on a positive physical Hilbert
space. The current reusable QFT BRST item is Abelian and cannot discharge this
non-Abelian obligation.

For a local gauge condition `G(A)=0`, the Faddeev–Popov operator is the
derivative of `G(A^u)` at the identity along a gauge parameter. A local slice
theorem requires a specified Sobolev or smooth function space, gauge group,
base connection, stabilizer, and invertibility condition. Ghost determinants
and path-integral manipulations remain formal until the regulator, measure,
domain, and determinant are constructed. The `qftx-causal-renormalization`,
`qftx-renormalization-hopf`, and `qftx-bv-brst` pages supply their exact scalar,
combinatorial, and Abelian interfaces only; non-Abelian Slavnov–Taylor
identities, gauge-compatible renormalization, and anomaly cancellation remain
separate local proof contracts.

The perturbative sector represents amplitudes as formal power series in the
couplings or loop-counting parameter. Equality means coefficientwise equality;
it does not mean convergence. A finite-lattice or finite-mode model is exact
at its declared regulator and finite volume. A continuum, infinite-volume,
cutoff-removal, or scattering statement requires its own limit theorem and
cannot be inferred from a finite regulator or a formal Ward identity.

## Effective field theory and a bounded remainder interface

An EFT datum includes a microscopic or ultraviolet model, retained light
fields, integrated heavy fields, regulator, matching scale, cutoff `Lambda`,
and the domain of external energies `E`. These roles are distinct: the
renormalization scale `mu`, heavy mass `M`, and EFT cutoff `Lambda` need not be
equal. A Wilsonian action is defined only after specifying the high-mode split
and a well-defined integration or a regulator where that operation is an
actual finite integral. A one-particle-irreducible effective action and a
renormalized time-ordered product are different constructions.

In four dimensions, an operator of engineering dimension `Delta_i` may be
written with the convention `C_i(mu) O_i / Lambda^(Delta_i-4)` when the
coefficient `C_i` is dimensionless. The local operator space fixes fields,
representations, derivatives, statistics, tensor contractions, symmetries,
and coefficient ring. Its basis quotient separately records algebraic and
Fierz relations, total derivatives under compact support or boundary
conditions, equations of motion at the stated order, and allowed invertible
field redefinitions. An equation-of-motion relation between actions does not
automatically identify off-shell Green functions or include the quantum
Jacobian.

The present exact heavy-field supplier gives a useful bounded example. For a
positive self-adjoint heavy operator with source spectral support in
`[0,E^2]`, truncating the inverse expansion after order `N` has error at most
`M^-2(E^2/M^2)^(N+1)`, under the supplier's exact positivity, spectral, and
normalization hypotheses and with `E<M`. This supplies a concrete local
remainder, not a generic interacting Wilsonian theorem. In a new conditional
example, if a scalar amplitude is holomorphic on an open neighborhood of the
closed disk `|z|<=r` and bounded by `K` on its boundary, write
`a_k=f^{(k)}(0)/k!` for its Taylor coefficients. Cauchy's coefficient
estimate gives for `|z|<r`

$$\left|f(z)-\sum_{k=0}^{N}a_kz^k\right|\leq K\frac{(|z|/r)^{N+1}}{1-|z|/r}.$$

The estimate is a mathematical consequence of holomorphy and the stated
bound; applying it to a physical amplitude requires proving those hypotheses
on the chosen amplitude domain, including its thresholds, poles, cuts, and
infrared regulator. It is not a numerical uncertainty estimate for every EFT.

Matching equates a stated set of renormalized low-energy amplitudes or Green
functions in two theories using the same infrared regulator and compatible
schemes. Running evolves the coefficient vector inside one EFT; matching
changes the retained field content at a threshold. A scale-independent
prediction through a fixed truncation is proved only after the coefficient
running, threshold maps, matrix elements, and omitted-order errors are all
specified. Scalar formal RG examples and Gaussian shell composition do not
establish a coupled SMEFT anomalous-dimension matrix or a generic continuum
flow.

Forward-limit positivity begins with a gapped scalar channel whose
real-analytic, crossing-symmetric, unitary amplitude has the declared
polynomial bound and finite forward limit. The local proof must derive the
subtracted dispersion relation, control the contour at infinity, subtract
poles, invoke the optical-theorem sign under its exact normalization, and
identify the low-energy coefficient. Massless gauge bosons and gravitons add
infrared poles/cuts and need further hypotheses. No universal sign for every
Wilson coefficient is asserted.

## Supersymmetry algebra and the BPS positivity argument

A Lie superalgebra is a `Z/2`-graded vector space with a graded-antisymmetric
bracket satisfying graded Jacobi. Its representation on a field space must
state the grading, operator domains, and whether brackets close off shell or
modulo equations of motion and gauge transformations. A supersymmetry
transformation is a specified odd derivation on the selected multiplet; a
super-Poincare representation is a representation of the fixed spacetime
superalgebra on a Hilbert space with a positive inner product and self-adjoint
translation generators.

$QE(B)\psi=E(B)Q\psi$ for every Borel $B$ and $\psi\in D(Q)$, and the

For four-dimensional `N=2`, take supercharges `Q^I_alpha` and their Hilbert
adjoints `bar Q_{dot(beta) J}`, with `I,J=1,2`, spinor indices
`alpha,dot(beta)=1,2`, `epsilon_12=1`, `sigma^mu=(I,vec(sigma))`, and
`eta=diag(-,+,+,+)`. The matrices obey
`sigma^mu bar(sigma)^nu+sigma^nu bar(sigma)^mu=-2 eta^{mu nu} I`. For the
scalar central-charge matrix `Z^{IJ}=-Z^{JI}`, fix the convention

$$\{Q^I_\alpha,\bar Q_{\dot\beta J}\}
=-2\sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot\beta}P_\mu\delta^I{}_J,\qquad
\{Q^I_\alpha,Q^J_\beta\}
=2\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}Z^{IJ}.$$

The supercharges have a conjugate-linear adjoint on a common invariant dense
domain in a positive Hilbert-space representation. Restrict to a massive
one-particle representation with fixed mass `M>0` and fixed central charge
acting as the scalar `Z`; use its rest-frame fiber `P^mu=(M,0,0,0)`, so
`P_0=-M` and the first anticommutator is positive. This is a statement on
that invariant mass/charge subspace, not on arbitrary wave-packet vectors in
the full QFT Hilbert space. For `N=2`, rotate `Z^{12}=Z`. Choose a phase `phi` with
`e^{-i phi}Z=|Z|` and form
`A=Q^1_1-e^{i phi}(Q^2_2)^dagger`. The algebra gives
`{A,A^dagger}=4(M-|Z|)I`. For every vector `psi` in the common domain,
`<psi|{A,A^dagger}|psi>=||A psi||^2+||A^dagger psi||^2>=0`, hence
`M>=|Z|`. Equality forces the corresponding supercharges to annihilate the
state and yields a shortened representation. The local proof must verify the
signs, phase choice, common domain, and anticommutator expansion. This
argument does not establish that a soliton exists, that `Z` is topological in
a selected model, or that a protected index counts such states. Those are
separate local derivations.

Superspace is a graded coordinate algebra over a specified spacetime patch,
with even coordinates `x^mu` and odd Grassmann coordinates `theta^alpha` and
`bar(theta)^dot_alpha`. Superfields are functions in that algebra with a stated
reality and representation condition. Chiral constraints, vector multiplets,
auxiliary fields, invariant actions, and Noether supercharges must be defined
and checked for the selected `N`, dimension, signature, gauge group, and
boundary conditions. A source's perturbative Wilsonian nonrenormalization
statement is imported only with its regulator and holomorphy hypotheses; it
does not imply nonperturbative exactness or a theorem about an arbitrary 1PI
functional.

## Equivariant localization: what the theorem proves

For a compact Lie group `K` acting smoothly on a manifold `X`, let `EK` be a
contractible free `K`-space and `BK=EK/K`. The Borel construction is
`EK x_K X=(EK x X)/K`, and its cohomology is the Borel equivariant cohomology
`H_K^*(X;R)`. The Cartan complex is
`(S(k^*) tensor Omega^*(X))^K` with total grading in which linear
coordinates on `k` have degree two. For polynomial `K`-equivariant forms
`alpha`, define `d_K alpha = d alpha - i_{X_xi} alpha`, where `xi` is the
formal Lie-algebra variable and `X_xi` the induced vector field. On invariant
forms, `d_K^2 alpha = -L_{X_xi}alpha = 0`; the final equality is exactly the
invariance condition. This algebraic complex is not yet a localization
formula.

The finite-dimensional localization theorem requires its own exact
hypotheses: compact oriented `X`, a compact torus `T` action, an
equivariantly closed class, a coefficient field `k` equal to `Q` or `R`, and
localization of `H_T^*(pt;k)` by the nonzero characters of `T`. Each fixed component must
be a smooth submanifold with an oriented normal bundle whose equivariant Euler
class is invertible in that localized ring; the integration map and degree
conventions are specified. Under those hypotheses the integral over `X`
equals the sum of fixed-component integrals divided by those Euler classes.
The local proof obligation is the actual Borel/Atiyah–Bott or
Berline–Vergne argument, including orientation and denominator conventions;
the theorem is not supplied by the definition `d_K^2=0`.

For a finite-dimensional supersymmetric integral, a separate deformation
argument is available under explicit analytic conditions. Let `Q` be an odd
graded derivation on the integration algebra, `Q^2=0`, and let the action `S`
and observable `O` be even and `Q`-closed. Let `V` be odd, so `QV` is even.
Assume the integration functional is `Q`-invariant (`int Q eta=0`) on the
chosen cycle and there are no boundary contributions. For every compact
interval `K` of `t`, require an integrable function `g_K` that bounds both
`|O e^{-S-t QV}|` and the absolute value of its `t`-derivative for every
`t in K`; dominated convergence then justifies differentiation under the
integral. For the deformation `S_t=S+t QV`,

$$\frac{d}{dt}\int O e^{-S_t}=-\int Q(V O e^{-S_t})=0.$$

The graded Leibniz rule and `QS=QO=0` make the derivative a `Q`-exact term,
which vanishes by the assumed Ward identity. This proves deformation
independence for that integral. A saddle-point or
fixed-point formula additionally needs coercivity/positivity, control of
zero-modes, determinant regularization, and a justified limiting argument.
The identity does not establish existence of an infinite-dimensional path
integral. Sphere partition functions, superconformal indices, and lens-space
defect indices therefore remain formal or regulator-specific until their
measure, contour, one-loop determinants, instanton terms, and limits are
defined and proved.

## BPS sectors, generalized symmetries, and defects

A `p`-form global symmetry in `d` dimensions is represented by topological
operators supported on closed codimension-`p+1` submanifolds, with a specified
fusion/action on `p`-dimensional charged operators. For a discrete Abelian
symmetry group `A`, write its topological generators as `U_a` for `a in A`;
a charged operator transforms by a character `chi:A->U(1)`,
`U_a O U_a^{-1}=chi(a)O`. For continuous higher-form symmetries, the
background is a higher-degree gauge field with its transformation law and
flux quantization stated. Ordinary `0`-form symmetries may be non-Abelian and
act on fields through representations; their representation labels are not
the group elements labeling symmetry transformations. In four-dimensional
pure `SU(N)` Yang–Mills, the
center `Z_N` acts on Wilson lines by their center character, provided there is
no dynamical matter in a center-charged representation. Adding such matter
breaks that one-form symmetry. This elementary example depends on the global
form and matter content and cannot be universalized to every gauge theory.

A defect is a specified modification of local fields, boundary conditions,
or operator insertions supported on a submanifold of stated codimension. A
topological defect has correlation functions invariant under deformations
that avoid insertions, under the assumptions and Ward identities of the
chosen model. An invertible defect has an inverse under fusion; a
non-invertible defect instead has fusion data without an inverse, often a
direct-sum decomposition. Physical junctions, associators, charge actions,
and defect anomalies are part of the interface, not consequences of a
mathematical braid or categorical action alone.

For a soliton, specify the field space, finite-energy boundary condition,
topological charge, energy functional, and central-charge normalization. In
the static adjoint-Higgs model in the Prasad–Sommerfield limit, choose the
action convention

$$E=\frac{1}{2g^2}\int_{\mathbb R^3}(|B|^2+|D\Phi|^2)\,d^3x.$$

Define `B_i=\tfrac12\epsilon_{ijk}F_{jk}` and
`m=\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{S_R^2}\langle\Phi,B_i\rangle n_i\,dS_i`.
Assume smooth fields, `D_iB_i=0`, `D_i\Phi\to0`, asymptotic magnetic field
in the unbroken algebra, and sufficient falloff for the surface limit and
integration by parts. Put `T=g^{-2}m`. Gauge invariance of the inner product
and the Bianchi identity give
`\partial_i\langle\Phi,B_i\rangle=\langle D_i\Phi,B_i\rangle`.
Expanding the two squares on a finite ball and passing to the declared limit
yields

$$E=\frac{1}{2g^2}\int_{\mathbb R^3}|B-D\Phi|^2\,d^3x+T,$$

$$E=\frac{1}{2g^2}\int_{\mathbb R^3}|B+D\Phi|^2\,d^3x-T.$$

Thus `E>=|T|`; for `T>0`, equality requires `B=D\Phi`, and for `T<0`,
equality requires `B=-D\Phi` (the zero-charge equality case is separate).
Flux quantization requires a further derivation from the chosen global gauge
group, unbroken subgroup, transition function, and matter charge lattice. The
square completion establishes neither such quantization nor existence of a
smooth solution. This conditional classical energy bound is not an
experimental observation or a quantum particle state. The mathematical
Hall/BPS and categorical line interfaces are reused only after their exact
input data are provided; a physical protected-index dictionary is additional
data and proof.

## Anomalies and inflow

An anomaly is a failure of a specified quantum generating functional or
partition function to be invariant under a symmetry after an explicit
regularization and counterterm convention. A local anomaly is represented by
a local functional of background fields and symmetry parameters. Its
Wess–Zumino consistency condition is the cocycle identity induced by the
commutator of two symmetry variations. For an invariant polynomial
`P_{2n+2}(F)`, on each local trivializing patch (or after choosing stated
extension data), a descent chain has the form

$$P_{2n+2}(F)=d I_{2n+1}^{(0)},\qquad
\delta_\epsilon I_{2n+1}^{(0)}=d I_{2n}^{(1)}(\epsilon,A),$$

so the boundary variation is represented by the integral of
`I_{2n}^{(1)}`. This local equation does not assert that a globally defined
characteristic form is exact on the base: patching of local Chern–Simons
forms and any differential-cohomology data are separate obligations. The
coefficient, trace normalization, gauge representation,
spin structure, allowed counterterms, and global form of the gauge group
must be given. Chern–Weil theory supplies real characteristic forms under its
mathematical assumptions; it does not by itself fix an integral quantum
charge normalization or a fermion determinant phase.

A global anomaly is a nontrivial phase of the determinant or partition
function around a loop in background/gauge-field space and may remain when
the local anomaly polynomial vanishes. Anomaly inflow is a cancellation
statement for a specified bulk-and-boundary system: the bulk variation must
equal the negative of the boundary variation, with the extension, quantized
coefficient, boundary fields, and admissible gauge transformations stated.
Not every anomaly is represented by one ordinary local polynomial, and a
formal descent identity does not construct a global determinant line or
physical boundary theory. The local Abelian/scalar anomaly-cocycle supplier
does not establish general non-Abelian, global, mixed, or supersymmetric
anomaly cancellation.

## Experiments and empirical constraints

An `experiment` record describes a source-reported observation, not a
deduction from the EFT or supersymmetry model it tests. Each future item names
the primary report, question, preparation, accelerator/detector or other
apparatus, operational observable, selection, trigger, calibration,
procedure, dataset, sampling model, likelihood/test, uncertainty, systematic
effects, correlations, and source limitations. Numerical estimates are
reproduced only at the source's scope; a null result excludes only its stated
parameterization and assumptions.

For a binned search with observed counts `n_i`, a signal parameter `theta`,
and nuisance parameters `eta`, one possible stated sampling model is

$$L(\theta,\eta)=\prod_i \operatorname{Pois}
(n_i\mid s_i(\theta,\eta)+b_i(\eta))\,L_{aux}(\eta).$$

This formula assumes conditionally independent Poisson bins and an auxiliary
likelihood `L_aux` whose calibration and correlations are part of the report.
It is a model definition, not a claim that a named experiment used this exact
likelihood. A profile statistic requires its exact maximization domain and
test construction; asymptotic thresholds, `CL_s` interpretation, coverage,
and nuisance constraints need the report's own justification and validation.
No events, counts, limits, or nuisance values are invented by this scaffold.

The existing Fan electron-moment experiment is reusable with its stated
quantum-jump procedure, eleven weighted determinations, systematic
corrections, reported one-sigma uncertainty, and explicit note that raw
records/covariances were not reconstructed. It is not a new SUSY search and
does not itself prove a QED prediction. The specialist EFT note also records
two directly read combinations and two source-limited candidates: the ATLAS
global SMEFT fit (48 coefficient directions), the CMS global fit (64
individual coefficients and 43 combinations), the inaccessible-full-PAS CMS
multiboson report (no numerical limits may be copied until retrieved), and
the ATLAS tau/squark/gluino search (model-specific exclusions, including a
local excess that was not a significant overall deviation). Those exact
conditions and reading extents travel with the experiment contracts. A
profile-likelihood p-value is conditional on its signal/background and
nuisance model; it is not the probability that a framework is false.
