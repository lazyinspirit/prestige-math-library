## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice, as used by the variational, Sobolev and compact-spectral suppliers and by the Fredholm alternative ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $O\subset\mathbb R^3$ be a bounded open set with $C^\infty$ boundary ([[def-bounded-c-k-domain-and-boundary-charts]]) whose exterior $D=\mathbb R^3\setminus\overline O$ is connected, let $k>0$, and let $R>0$ with $\overline O\subset B_R(0)$. Write $A:=B_R(0)\setminus\overline O$ and let $n$ be the outward unit normal field of $A$, so that on $\partial O$ the field $n$ points into $O$; put $\kappa:=\operatorname{div}n|_{\partial O}$ and, for a field $E$ on $\partial O$, $E_n:=E\cdot n|_{\partial O}$.

For a smooth $\mathbb C$-valued tangential field $e_T$ on $\partial O$ put
$$\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}e_T:=\operatorname{div}\widetilde e_T-\partial_n(\widetilde e_T\cdot n)\quad\text{on }\partial O,$$
where $\widetilde e_T$ is any smooth extension of $e_T$ to a neighbourhood of $\partial O$; the expression is independent of the extension and equals the surface divergence of $e_T$ with respect to the Euclidean surface measure of $\partial O$. Write $\Delta$ for the Euclidean Laplacian ([[def-laplacian-of-a-c2-function]]), acting componentwise, and for a vector field $f=(f_1,f_2,f_3)$ on $S_R$ write $T_Rf:=(T_Rf_1,T_Rf_2,T_Rf_3)$ with $T_R$ the scalar Dirichlet-to-Neumann map of [[lem-em-sphere-outgoing-dirichlet-to-neumann-map]].

Then there is exactly one field $E\in C^\infty(\overline D;\mathbb C^3)$ with
$$(\Delta+k^2)E=0\ \text{on }D,\qquad \operatorname{div}E=0\ \text{on }D,\qquad E_{\text{tangential}}=e_T\ \text{on }\partial O,$$
which is outgoing on the sphere $S_R$ in the modal sense of that lemma: its trace $f:=E|_{S_R}$ lies in $H^{1/2}(S_R;\mathbb C^3)$ and the normal derivative of $E$ on $S_R$ equals $T_Rf$ componentwise in the weak trace sense of the sphere DtN lemma. With these data the normal boundary values of $E$ on $\partial O$ satisfy the closure relation
$$\partial_nE_n+(\operatorname{div}n)E_n=-\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}e_T$$
as an identity of $C^\infty$ functions on $\partial O$. No scalar Dirichlet condition is imposed on the three Cartesian components of $E$, and no decay rate, asymptotic expansion or pointwise Sommerfeld formula beyond the stated modal outgoing class is assumed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]); a bounded $C^\infty$ obstacle $O$ with connected exterior $D$; $k>0$; $R>0$ with $\overline O\subset B_R(0)$; the truncated annulus $A=B_R(0)\setminus\overline O$ with outward unit normal $n$ and $\kappa=\operatorname{div}n|_{\partial O}$; a smooth $\mathbb C$-valued tangential field $e_T$ on $\partial O$; the conventions of the fields $E_n$, $\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}e_T$, $T_Rf$ explained in the Statement, and the notation $H^m$, $H^m_{\mathrm{loc}}$ of [[def-sobolev-space-wkp-and-its-norm]].

[L1] Sphere Dirichlet-to-Neumann lemma: for $k,R>0$ the map $T_Rf=\sum_{l,m}t_lf_{lm}Y^R_{lm}$ is bounded $H^{1/2}(S_R)\to H^{-1/2}(S_R)$ with $\operatorname{Re}\langle T_Rf,f\rangle\le0$ and $\operatorname{Im}\langle T_Rf,f\rangle=\sum_{l,m}(\operatorname{Im}t_l)|f_{lm}|^2>0$ for $f\ne0$; for every $f\in H^{1/2}(S_R)$ the outgoing series $u(rn)=\sum_{l,m}f_{lm}h_l(kr)/h_l(kR)\,R^{-1}Y_{lm}(n)$, $r\ge R$, is the unique outgoing finite-local-energy exterior solution with trace $f$ on $S_R$ and normal derivative $T_Rf$ in the weak trace sense; it is smooth away from the sphere, and for smooth $f$ it satisfies the ordinary Sommerfeld condition ([[lem-em-sphere-outgoing-dirichlet-to-neumann-map]]).

[L2] Exterior scalar uniqueness: if $O$ has bounded $C^\infty$ boundary, $D$ is connected, $k>0$, $u\in C^\infty(\overline D)$ solves $(\Delta+k^2)u=0$ on $D$, vanishes on $\partial O$ and is outgoing in the class of [L1], then $u=0$ on $D$ ([[lem-em-exterior-outgoing-scalar-uniqueness]]).

[L3] Weak prescribed-trace Dirichlet existence: for a bounded $C^1$ domain $\Omega$, a uniformly elliptic measurable coefficient $\varepsilon$, $\rho\in L^2(\Omega)$ and $g\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)$, there is exactly one $u\in H^1(\Omega)$ with trace $Tu=g$ solving $\int_\Omega\nabla v\cdot\varepsilon\nabla u=\int_\Omega\rho v$ for all $v\in H_0^1(\Omega)$, with $\|u\|_{H^1}\le C(\|\rho\|_{L^2}+\|g\|_{H^{1/2}})$ ([[lem-em-prescribed-fractional-dirichlet-existence]]).

[L4] Smooth-domain elliptic regularity: on a bounded $C^\infty$ domain with smooth uniformly positive $\varepsilon$ and smooth $\rho$ on the closure, the weak solution with smooth Dirichlet data $g\in C^\infty(\partial\Omega)$ is $C^\infty$ on the closure and satisfies the classical equation and boundary values ([[lem-em-smooth-domain-elliptic-regularity]]).

[L5] Bounded-potential unique continuation: for nonempty open connected $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^3$, $V\in L^\infty_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ and $\psi\in H^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ with $\Delta\psi=V\psi$ distributionally, vanishing of $\psi$ on a nonempty open subset of $\Omega$ forces $\psi=0$ on $\Omega$ ([[thm-bounded-potential-weak-unique-continuation-in-three-dimensions]]).

[L6] Interior $H^2$ regularity: if $u\in H^1(\Omega)$ is a local weak solution of $-\Delta u=f$ with $f\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$, then $u\in H^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ ([[thm-interior-h-two-regularity-for-divergence-form-equations]]).

[L7] Interior $H^{k+2}$ regularity: if $u\in H^1(\Omega)$ is a local weak solution of $-\Delta u=f$ with $f\in H^k_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$, $k\ge0$, then $u\in H^{k+2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ ([[thm-interior-h-k-plus-two-elliptic-regularity]]).

[L8] Riesz representation: every bounded linear functional on a real or complex Hilbert space is represented by a unique vector ([[thm-riesz-representation-for-hilbert-space]]).

[L9] Fredholm alternative: for a compact operator $K$ on a Banach space, $I-K$ is injective if and only if it is surjective, and in that case it is boundedly invertible ([[thm-fredholm-alternative-for-identity-minus-compact]]).

[L10] Trace operator: on a bounded $C^1$ domain the trace $T:W^{1,p}(\Omega)\to L^p(\partial\Omega)$ is bounded and equals classical restriction on continuous Sobolev functions ([[thm-lp-trace-operator-on-a-bounded-c-one-domain]]).

[L11] Sobolev-Gauss-Green formula: for a bounded $C^1$ domain $\Omega$, $u,v\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)$ and every $i$, $\int_\Omega u\,\partial_iv=-\int_\Omega(\partial_iu)v+\int_{\partial\Omega}(Tu)(Tv)\nu_i\,dS$ ([[thm-sobolev-gauss-green-formula-on-c-one-domains]]).

[L12] Half-space strip trace estimate: for $u$ smooth with compact support in the closed half-space and $g=u(\cdot,0)$ one has $h|g(x')|^2\le 2(\int_0^h|u|^2dt+h^2\int_0^h|\partial_nu|^2dt)$ for a.e. $x'$ and every $h>0$ ([[thm-trace-estimate-on-the-half-space]]).

[L13] Extension theorem for bounded smooth domains: every bounded $C^\infty$ domain is a $W^{k,2}$-extension domain ([[thm-extension-theorem-for-bounded-smooth-domains]]).

[L14] Subcritical Sobolev compactness: for a bounded extension domain $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ and $p=2<n$, every sequence bounded in $W^{1,2}(\Omega)$ has a subsequence converging in $L^2(\Omega)$ ([[cor-bounded-sobolev-sequences-have-strongly-convergent-subsequences]]).

[L15] Sequential compact-embedding criterion: under Countable and Dependent Choice, $X\Subset Y$ holds if and only if every bounded sequence in $X$ has a subsequence converging in $Y$ ([[def-compactly-embedded-normed-spaces]]).

[L16] In ZF the Axiom of Choice implies the Axiom of Dependent Choice and the Axiom of Countable Choice ([[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]]).

[L17] Difference-quotient product identity: $\delta_h^i(\eta^2\delta_h^iu)=\eta^2\delta_h^i\delta_h^iu+(\delta_h^i\eta^2)(\tau_{-he_i}\delta_h^iu)$ on the common domain, with $\|\delta_h^iu\|_{L^p}\le\|\partial_iu\|_{L^p}$ on the enlarged set ([[lem-cutoff-difference-quotient-commutator-estimate]]).

[L18] Higher-order Sobolev embedding: on a bounded extension domain, $W^{m,2}$ membership with $m>j+\tfrac32$ yields a $C^j$ representative on the closure, compatibly for different $j$ ([[thm-higher-order-sobolev-embedding]]).

[L19] $L^2$ Cauchy-Schwarz: $\int|fg|\,d\mu\le\|f\|_2\|g\|_2$, and $2ab\le\delta a^2+\delta^{-1}b^2$ for $a,b\ge0$, $\delta>0$ ([[cor-cauchy-schwarz-inequality-for-l-two]]).

[L20] Clairaut-Schwarz: mixed partial derivatives of a $C^2$ function commute ([[thm-clairaut-schwarz-mixed-partials]]).

**Choice accounting.** The Axiom of Choice is assumed in the Statement. It supplies the Fredholm alternative [L9], the tensor/frame and Sobolev suppliers' own choice assumptions, and through [L16] the Countable and Dependent Choice used by the sequential compactness criterion [L15] and by the trace and difference-quotient interfaces of [L10], [L12] and [L17]. No other choice principle is used, and no choice is made in the explicit finite constructions below.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** The data and conventions of the Given paragraph above; in particular the truncated annulus $A=B_R(0)\setminus\overline O$, its outward unit normal $n$, the coefficient $\kappa=\operatorname{div}n$ on $\partial O$, and the smooth tangential field $e_T$ on $\partial O$.

1.1 **The test space and the variational form.** Define $X:=\{v\in H^1(A;\mathbb C^3):v_{\text{tangential}}=0\text{ on }\partial O\}$, the tangential part being taken with respect to the trace of $v$ on the smooth boundary component $\partial O$; by the boundedness of the trace operator [L10] applied componentwise, $X$ is a closed subspace of the complex Hilbert space $H^1(A;\mathbb C^3)$, hence complete. Define the sesquilinear form $a(E,v):=\int_A(\nabla E:\overline{\nabla v}-k^2E\cdot\overline v)\,dx+\int_{\partial O}\kappa\,E_n\overline{v_n}\,dS-\langle T_RE,v\rangle_{S_R}$, where $\nabla E:\overline{\nabla v}:=\sum_{i,m}\partial_iE_m\overline{\partial_iv_m}$ and $\langle T_RE,v\rangle_{S_R}:=\sum_{m=1}^3\langle T_RE_m,v_m\rangle$ uses the scalar DtN pairing of [L1] on the components. Then $|a(E,v)|\le C\|E\|_{H^1(A)}\|v\|_{H^1(A)}$: the first two terms are bounded by Cauchy-Schwarz [L19]; the boundary term is bounded by $\|\kappa\|_\infty\|E\|_{L^2(\partial O)}\|v\|_{L^2(\partial O)}\le C'\|E\|_{H^1}\|v\|_{H^1}$ by [L10]; and the last by the boundedness of $T_R:H^{1/2}(S_R)\to H^{-1/2}(S_R)$ [L1] combined with trace continuity for $H^1(A)$ functions on the smooth sphere $S_R$. [given, L1, L10, L19]

1.2 **Collar trace inequality on the inner boundary.** For every $\delta>0$ there is $C_\delta$ such that every $u\in H^1(A;\mathbb C^3)$ satisfies $\|u\|_{L^2(\partial O)}^2\le\delta\|\nabla u\|_{L^2(A)}^2+C_\delta\|u\|_{L^2(A)}^2$. Indeed, cover $\partial O$ by finitely many flattening charts of the smooth boundary ([[def-bounded-c-k-domain-and-boundary-charts]]) and let $\chi$ be a smooth cutoff, equal to $1$ near $\partial O$ and supported in the resulting collar. On the collar, in normal coordinates $x=\Phi(y,t)$ with $t$ proportional to the distance to $\partial O$, the strip estimate [L12] with $p=2$, divided by $h$ and integrated over $y$ with the bounded chart Jacobians, gives $\|u\chi\|_{L^2(\partial O)}^2\le C(h^{-1}\|u\chi\|_{L^2(A)}^2+h\|\nabla(u\chi)\|_{L^2(A)}^2)$ for every smooth $u$; choosing $h$ so small that $2Ch\le\delta$, expanding $\nabla(u\chi)=\chi\nabla u+u\nabla\chi$ and absorbing the cutoff terms into $C_\delta\|u\|_{L^2(A)}^2$ yields the claim for smooth $u$, and both sides are continuous in the $H^1(A)$ norm (boundary term by [L10]), while products of global $C_c^\infty$ approximations with a fixed cutoff are dense in the $H^1$ classes supported in the collar by [[cor-compactly-supported-smooth-functions-are-dense-in-wkp-of-rn]] and [L13]; the inequality therefore extends to all $u\in H^1(A;\mathbb C^3)$. [L10, L12, L13]

1.3 **Smooth componentwise lift of the tangential datum.** For $j=1,2,3$ let $(e_T)_j\in C^\infty(\partial O)$ be the Cartesian components of $e_T$ and extend them by zero to $\partial A=\partial O\cup S_R$, obtaining smooth functions $g_j\in C^\infty(\partial A)$. By [L3] with $\varepsilon=I$, $\rho=0$ there is a unique $G_j\in H^1(A)$ with trace $g_j$ and $\int_A\nabla\overline v\cdot\nabla G_j=0$ for all $v\in H_0^1(A)$; by [L4] with $\rho=0$ and the smooth Dirichlet data $g_j$, each $G_j$ is $C^\infty$ on $\overline A$, is harmonic on $A$, and $G_j|_{\partial A}=g_j$. Hence $G:=(G_1,G_2,G_3)$ is $C^\infty$ on $\overline A$, satisfies $\Delta G=0$ componentwise, $G|_{\partial O}=e_T$ and $G|_{S_R}=0$, and is tangential on $\partial O$. [L3, L4]

2.1 **Coercivity of the shifted form.** With $\lambda:=\|\kappa\|_\infty(1+C_1)+k^2+1$, where $C_1$ is the constant of step 1.2 for $\delta:=\tfrac12(\|\kappa\|_\infty+1)^{-1}$, one has $\operatorname{Re}(a(E,E)+\lambda\|E\|_{L^2(A)}^2)\ge c\|E\|_{H^1(A)}^2$ for every $E\in X$, with $c=\tfrac12$. Indeed $\operatorname{Re}a(E,E)=\|\nabla E\|_2^2-k^2\|E\|_2^2+\int_{\partial O}\kappa|E_n|^2-\operatorname{Re}\langle T_RE,E\rangle$; the last term is nonnegative by $\operatorname{Re}\langle T_Rf,f\rangle\le0$ of [L1] applied componentwise to $f=E|_{S_R}$; and the boundary term obeys $|\int_{\partial O}\kappa|E_n|^2|\le\|\kappa\|_\infty\|E\|_{L^2(\partial O)}^2\le\tfrac12\|\nabla E\|_2^2+\|\kappa\|_\infty C_1\|E\|_2^2$ by step 1.2 and [L19]. [L1, step 1.2, L19, algebra]

3.1 **The shifted form is boundedly invertible.** By the Riesz representation theorem [L8], applied to the bounded conjugate-linear functional $v\mapsto a(E,v)+\lambda(E,v)_{L^2}$ on the complete space $X$, the shifted form is represented by a bounded linear operator $S:X\to X$ with $\operatorname{Re}\langle Sx,x\rangle\ge c\|x\|^2$ and $\|S\|\le M$ for constants $c,M>0$ (step 2.1 and step 1.1). Choose $0<t<2c/M^2$ and put $q:=\sqrt{1-2tc+t^2M^2}<1$. For $x,y\in X$, $\|(I-tS)(x-y)\|^2\le q^2\|x-y\|^2$ by expanding the square and using [L19]; hence for every $f\in X$ the iteration $x_{n+1}:=(I-tS)x_n+tf$ is a strict contraction of the complete space $X$, so it has a unique fixed point $x$, and $Sx=f$. If $Sx=0$ then $c\|x\|^2\le\operatorname{Re}\langle Sx,x\rangle=0$, so $x=0$; thus $S$ is boundedly invertible with $\|S^{-1}\|\le c^{-1}$. [L8, L19, step 1.1, step 2.1, choose, algebra]

3.2 **The kernel of the form is trivial.** Let $E\in X$ satisfy $a(E,v)=0$ for every $v\in X$. Taking $v=E$ and imaginary parts in step 2.1 gives $0=\operatorname{Im}a(E,E)=-\operatorname{Im}\langle T_RE,E\rangle$, where $f=E|_{S_R}$; by [L1] the imaginary pairing equals $\sum_m\sum_{l,m'}(\operatorname{Im}t_l)|(f_m)_{lm'}|^2$ with $\operatorname{Im}t_l>0$, so every coefficient vanishes, $f=0$ on $S_R$, and consequently $T_Rf=0$. For every $v\in X$ supported away from $\partial O$ the identity $a(E,v)=0$ has no inner-boundary contribution and reads $\int_A(\nabla E:\overline{\nabla v}-k^2E\cdot\overline v)\,dx=\langle T_RE,v\rangle_{S_R}=\langle T_Rf,v|_{S_R}\rangle=0$; in particular every $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(D)$, whose restriction to $A$ is an admissible test vanishing near $\partial O$, satisfies $\int_A\nabla E:\overline{\nabla\varphi}\,dx=k^2\int_AE\cdot\overline\varphi\,dx$, and taking $v$ compactly supported in the interior of $A$ gives $-\Delta E=k^2E$ weakly on $A$. Define $\widetilde E:=E$ on $A$ and $\widetilde E:=0$ on $\{|x|>R\}$; these two open sets cover $D$, so $\widetilde E$ is defined on all of $D$, and its only interface is the sphere $S_R$, across which the full trace of the field is $f=0$. For $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(D)$ the Sobolev-Gauss-Green formula [L11] on the bounded $C^1$ domain $A$, applied componentwise with $u=E_m\in W^{1,2}(A)$ and $v=\varphi|_A$, gives $\int_AE_m\overline{\partial_i\varphi}\,dx=-\int_A(\partial_iE_m)\overline\varphi\,dx+\int_{\partial A}(TE_m)(T\varphi)\nu_i\,dS$; the boundary term over $\partial O$ vanishes because $\varphi$ is compactly supported in $D$, and the term over $S_R$ vanishes because $TE_m=f_m=0$ there; hence $\int_D\widetilde E_m\overline{\partial_i\varphi}\,dx=-\int_A(\partial_iE_m)\overline\varphi\,dx$, so the distributional gradient of $\widetilde E$ is the field $(\nabla E)\mathbf 1_A\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(D)$, $\widetilde E\in H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(D)$, and the weak identity above gives $\int_D\nabla\widetilde E:\overline{\nabla\varphi}\,dx=k^2\int_D\widetilde E\cdot\overline\varphi\,dx$. By [L6] each real and imaginary part of each component of $\widetilde E$ lies in $H^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(D)$ and satisfies $\Delta w=-k^2w$ distributionally; $D$ is nonempty, open and connected and $\widetilde E=0$ on the nonempty open set $D\cap\{|x|>R\}$, so [L5] gives $\widetilde E=0$ on $D$, in particular $E=0$ on $A$. [L1, L5, L6, L11, step 2.1]

4.1 **Compactness of the $L^2$ pairing and the Fredholm reduction.** Let $J:X\to X^*$ be the $L^2$ pairing $\langle Jx,v\rangle:=(x,v)_{L^2(A)}$. The inclusion $X\hookrightarrow H^1(A;\mathbb C^3)$ is continuous and $A$ is a bounded $W^{1,2}$-extension domain [L13], so by [L14] with $p=2<n=3$ every bounded sequence in $H^1(A)$ has an $L^2$-convergent subsequence; by [L16] the Countable and Dependent Choice assumptions of the sequential criterion [L15] are available, so the inclusion $H^1(A)\hookrightarrow L^2(A)$ is compact, and so is its restriction $X\hookrightarrow L^2(A)$, hence also the composition $J$ of that restriction with the bounded pairing $L^2(A)\to X^*$. Therefore $K:=S^{-1}J:X\to X$ is compact, and the operator $A_{\mathrm{op}}:=S-\lambda J$ represented by $a$ satisfies $S^{-1}A_{\mathrm{op}}=I-\lambda K$; by the Fredholm alternative [L9] the operator $S^{-1}A_{\mathrm{op}}$ is boundedly invertible as soon as its kernel is trivial, and its kernel consists exactly of the $E\in X$ with $a(E,v)=0$ for all $v\in X$. [L9, L13, L14, L15, L16, step 3.1]

5.1 **Bounded invertibility of $a$.** By step 3.2 the kernel of $S^{-1}A_{\mathrm{op}}=I-\lambda K$ is trivial, so the Fredholm alternative [L9] makes it boundedly invertible; hence $A_{\mathrm{op}}=S(I-\lambda K)$ and the form $a$ are boundedly invertible: for every bounded conjugate-linear functional $F$ on $X$ there is exactly one $E\in X$ with $a(E,v)=F(v)$ for all $v\in X$, and $\|E\|_{H^1(A)}\le C\|F\|_{X^*}$. [L9, step 4.1, step 3.2]

6.1 **The weak solution with prescribed tangential trace.** Define on $X$ the functional $F(v):=-a(G,v)-\int_{\partial O}(\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}e_T)\overline{v_n}\,dS$. Both summands are bounded in $v$: the first by step 1.1, the second because $\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}e_T\in C^\infty(\partial O)$ and $v\mapsto v_n|_{\partial O}$ is trace-continuous into $L^2(\partial O)$ by [L10], applied in the boundary charts. By step 5.1 there is exactly one $u\in X$ with $a(u,v)=F(v)$ for all $v\in X$; set $E:=G+u\in H^1(A;\mathbb C^3)$. Then $E_{\text{tangential}}=e_T$ on $\partial O$ because $u\in X$ and $G$ is tangential there, and testing the defining identity with $v$ compactly supported in the interior of $A$ gives $-\Delta E=k^2E$ weakly on $A$. Moreover, for every $v\in X$ supported away from $\partial O$ one has $a(E,v)=a(G,v)+a(u,v)=-\int_{\partial O}(\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}e_T)\overline{v_n}\,dS=0$, so the same integration-by-parts computation as in step 3.2 shows that the normal trace of $E$ on $S_R$ equals $T_Rf$ in the weak trace sense, where $f=E|_{S_R}=u|_{S_R}\in H^{1/2}(S_R;\mathbb C^3)$. [L10, step 5.1, step 1.3, algebra]

7.1 **Gluing across the artificial sphere and smoothness on the open set $D$.** Let $E_{\mathrm{out}}$ be the outgoing series of [L1] built from the trace $f=E|_{S_R}$, defined on $\{|x|>R\}$; by [L1] it is a finite-local-energy solution there with normal derivative $T_Rf$, and it is smooth away from the sphere. Define $\widetilde E:=E$ on $A$ and $\widetilde E:=E_{\mathrm{out}}$ on $\{|x|>R\}$. The two pieces have the same trace $f$ on $S_R$; applying the Sobolev-Gauss-Green formula [L11] on $A$ and on the exterior annulus $B_M\setminus\overline B_R$ (with the outward normals $+n$ and $-n$ on $S_R$) and adding, the two interface pairings cancel, so $\widetilde E\in H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(D)$ with weak gradient the two-sided gradient. For every test $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(D)$ the weak equations on both sides then give $\int_D\nabla\widetilde E:\overline{\nabla\varphi}=k^2\int_D\widetilde E\cdot\overline\varphi$: on $A$ this is the weak identity of step 6.1, on the exterior it is the weak Helmholtz equation satisfied by the outgoing series, and the interface terms have cancelled. Since $k^2\widetilde E\in H^k_{\mathrm{loc}}(D)$ for every $k$ as soon as $\widetilde E\in H^k_{\mathrm{loc}}(D)$, repeated application of the interior gain-two estimate [L7] starting from $H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ gives $\widetilde E\in H^m_{\mathrm{loc}}(D)$ for every $m$, hence a $C^\infty(D)$ representative satisfying the classical Helmholtz equation on $D$. [L1, L7, L11, step 6.1]

7.2 **Flattening near $\partial O$ and the local mixed system.** Fix a boundary point and a flattening chart of $\partial O$ with coordinates $(y,s)$, $y\in B\subseteq\mathbb R^2$, $s<0$ inside $A$; shrink it and let $\eta$ be a cutoff equal to $1$ on a smaller half-box and supported in the larger one. Choose a smooth orthonormal frame $Q=(\tau_1,\tau_2,n)$ with $n$ the outward normal of $A$ extended along the normal lines (so that $\partial_s=\partial_n$ in the collar), and write $E=QU$ on the chart. Transporting the form of step 1.1 and localizing by $\eta$, the weak identity $a(E,v)=-\int_{\partial O}(\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}e_T)\overline{v_n}\,dS$ of step 6.1 becomes, for every admissible localized test $V$ (frame components of a field in $X$, compactly supported in the larger half-box, with $V_1=V_2=0$ on $B$), $\sum_m\int a^{ij}\partial_jU_m\overline{\partial_iV_m}\,dyds+\int_B\beta\,U_3\overline{V_3}\,dy=\int f\cdot\overline V\,dyds+\sum_i\int G_i\cdot\overline{\partial_iV}\,dyds+\int_B h\,\overline{V_3}\,dy$, where the principal matrix $a^{ij}$ is smooth, uniformly positive and scalar in the component index $m$ (the frame satisfies $Q^{\mathsf T}Q=I$, so the principal part of $\nabla E:\overline{\nabla V}$ keeps the identity in $m$, multiplied by the chart Jacobian); $\beta=\kappa J_B$ is smooth and possibly signed ($J_B$ the boundary Jacobian); $f=-k^2JU+(\text{smooth})U+(\text{smooth})\partial U\in L^2$; $G_i=(\text{smooth})U\in H^1$; and $h=-(\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}e_T)\circ\psi\,J_B\in C_c^\infty(B)$, with $\psi:B\to\partial O$ the graph parametrization. Every localized $H^1$ triple $V$ with $V_1=V_2=0$ on $B$ and compact support in the chart pulls back to an element of $X$: reflect $V_1,V_2$ oddly and $V_3$ evenly across $s=0$ (these reflections are $H^1$ because the odd components have zero trace and the even one matches), mollify with a kernel even in $s$ to keep the parities and the exact zero traces, multiply by an even cutoff, pull back through the smooth frame and chart, and extend by zero. Hence the displayed identity holds for all such tests, in particular for tangential difference-quotient tests. [L10, L11, step 6.1]

8.1 **Tangential second derivatives and recovery of the normal derivatives.** Let $k\in\{1,2\}$ be a tangential direction, put $W:=\delta_h^kU=(U(y+he_k,s)-U(y,s))/h$ and test the system of step 7.2 with $V:=-\delta_{-h}^k(\eta^2W)$ on a nested pair of half-boxes; this test has $V_1=V_2=0$ on $B$ and compact support, so it is admissible. The discrete integration by parts of [L17] and the shifted product rule give a principal term bounded below by $\alpha\|\eta\nabla W\|_{L^2}^2$ with $\alpha>0$ the ellipticity constant of $a^{ij}$, while all remaining terms involve $\delta_h a$, $\nabla\eta$, $W$, $\nabla U$, $f$ or the transferred quotients of $G$, and are bounded by $\epsilon\|\eta\nabla W\|_2^2+C_\epsilon(\|U\|_{H^1}^2+\|f\|_{L^2}^2+\|G\|_{H^1}^2)$ using [L17], the increment estimate $\|\delta_h^kU\|_{L^2}\le\|\partial_kU\|_{L^2}$, and [L19]. On $B$ the Robin term gives $\int_B\beta(y+he_k)\eta^2|W_3|^2+\int_B(\delta_h^k\beta)U_3\eta^2\overline{W_3}$; the trace estimate $\|Tz\|_{L^2(B)}^2\le\epsilon\|\nabla z\|_{L^2}^2+C_\epsilon\|z\|_{L^2}^2$ of step 1.2 (with $B$ the flattened $\partial O$) absorbs the first term for either sign of $\beta$, and bounded $\delta_h\beta$, the trace bound for $U_3$ and [L19] bound the second; the smooth datum $h$ contributes $\int_B(\delta_h^kh)\overline{W_3}$, bounded by $\|h\|_{H^1(B)}\|T(\eta^2W_3)\|_{L^2(B)}$ and absorbed in the same way. Choosing the finitely many $\epsilon$ small gives $\|\eta\nabla\delta_h^kU\|_{L^2}\le C(\|U\|_{H^1}+\|f\|_{L^2}+\|G\|_{H^1}+\|h\|_{H^1(B)})$ uniformly in $h$, hence every tangential second derivative of $U$ lies in $L^2$ after the limit argument in which the bounded functional $\psi\mapsto\lim_h\int(\partial_iU_m)\delta_{-h}^k(\eta^2\psi)$ is represented in $L^2$ by [L8]. From the interior equation $-\partial_i(a^{ij}\partial_jU_m)=f_m-\partial_iG_{i,m}$ one then recovers $a^{33}\partial_{ss}U_m=\partial_iG_{i,m}-f_m-\sum_{(i,j)\ne(3,3)}a^{ij}\partial_{ij}U_m-\sum_{i,j}(\partial_ia^{ij})\partial_jU_m$, whose right side is now $L^2$ and whose coefficient satisfies $a^{33}\ge\alpha>0$; so all second derivatives of $U$ lie in $L^2$ on the smaller half-box, and a finite cover of the compact boundary gives $E\in H^2$ up to $\partial O$. [L8, L19, step 1.2, step 7.2, algebra]

9.1 **Higher-order bootstrap and the classical boundary conclusion.** Suppose $U\in H^{r+1}$ on the larger half-box for some $r\ge1$; the data of the local system then satisfy $f\in H^r$, $G\in H^{r+1}$ and $h\in H^r(B)$ because all coefficients are smooth. For a tangential multi-index $\alpha$ of order $r$ put $Z:=D_y^\alpha U$; differentiating the system of step 7.2 by $D_y^\alpha$ keeps the principal matrix $a^{ij}$ and the Robin coefficient $\beta$, and replaces the data by $f_\alpha\in L^2$, $G_\alpha\in H^1$ and $h_\alpha\in H^1(B)$ built from $D_y^\alpha f$, $D_y^\alpha G$ and the finitely many commutator terms $D_y^\gamma(\text{coefficient})\,D_y^{\alpha-\gamma}U$ with $0<\gamma\le\alpha$, all known by the induction hypothesis; the tangential traces of $Z$ are again zero in the first two components and free in the third, and localized tests for $Z$ are admissible by the gluing argument of step 7.2. Applying step 8.1 to $Z$ gains two further tangential or mixed derivatives, and differentiating the normal-recovery equation of step 8.1 and inducting on the number of normal derivatives recovers the remaining ones; nested half-boxes and a finite boundary cover give $U\in H^m$ on each smaller patch for every $m$, with constants depending on the data, $\kappa$, the chart and the fixed nested covers. By the Sobolev embedding [L18] on the flat half-box (a bounded $C^\infty$ domain, hence an extension domain by [L13]) each $H^m$ class with $m>j+\tfrac32$ has a $C^j$ representative on the closed patch, compatible for different $j$; hence $E$ has a $C^\infty$ representative up to $\partial O$, and the classical integration by parts in the local frame (equivalently [L11] with classical traces) turns $a(E,v)=0$ for admissible tests into the boundary identities $\partial_nE_n+\kappa E_n=-\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}e_T$ and $E_{\text{tangential}}=e_T$ on $\partial O$. [L11, L13, L18, step 7.2, step 8.1, algebra]

10.1 **The divergence constraint.** Let $q:=\operatorname{div}E$ on $D$. By step 9.1 and step 7.1, $E\in C^\infty(\overline D;\mathbb C^3)$ once the two-sided solution is matched at $S_R$; the mixed partials commute [L20], so $\Delta q=\operatorname{div}\Delta E=-k^2\operatorname{div}E=-k^2q$ on $D$. On $\partial O$ the exact boundary identity $\operatorname{div}E=\partial_nE_n+(\operatorname{div}n)E_n+\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}E_{\text{tangential}}$ holds in the classical sense with the chosen normal extension, and its first two terms sum to $-\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}e_T$ by step 9.1 while the third equals $\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}e_T$; hence $q=0$ on $\partial O$. Finally $q$ is outgoing in the class of [L1]: on $\{|x|>R\}$ the field $E$ equals the outgoing series of [L1] by the uniqueness clause of that lemma, and differentiating the series componentwise in the spherical-harmonic expansion and using the spherical-Bessel recurrences $h_l'=h_{l-1}-(l+1)h_l/z$ and $h_l/z=(h_{l-1}+h_{l+1})/(2l+1)$ (which follow from the finite polynomial expression for $h_l$) expresses every radial factor of $q$ as a finite combination of outgoing factors $h_{l'}(kr)$, $l'\in\{l-1,l,l+1\}$; so $q$ has no incoming modal component and satisfies $\partial_rq=T_R(q|_{S_R})$ on $S_R$ in the class of [L1]. The exterior scalar uniqueness lemma [L2] applies to $q$ on the connected $D$ and gives $q=0$ on $D$, that is, the divergence constraint. [L1, L2, L20, step 7.1, step 9.1, algebra]

11.1 **Uniqueness of the classical problem.** Let $E_1,E_2\in C^\infty(\overline D;\mathbb C^3)$ both satisfy the conditions of the Statement. Their difference $W:=E_1-E_2$ is $C^\infty$ on $\overline D$, solves $(\Delta+k^2)W=0$ componentwise on $D$, has $\operatorname{div}W=0$ and $W_{\text{tangential}}=0$ on $\partial O$, and is outgoing on $S_R$. Then $W\in X$ with $W|_{S_R}\in H^{1/2}$, and the integration by parts of step 3.2 and step 6.1 shows that $a(W,v)=0$ for every $v\in X$: the volume terms reproduce the Helmholtz equation, the inner boundary term cancels against $\operatorname{div}W=0$ on $\partial O$ through the exact identity of step 10.1, and the outer term cancels because $\partial_rW=T_R(W|_{S_R})$ by outgoingness. By step 3.2, $W=0$ on $D$. [L1, step 3.2, step 6.1, step 10.1, algebra]

12.1 **Assembly and choice accounting.** Existence is step 6.1 together with the regularity and gluing of steps 7.1, 8.1 and 9.1: the constructed $E=G+u$ has the prescribed tangential trace, satisfies $(\Delta+k^2)E=0$ on $D$, is $C^\infty$ on $\overline D$ and outgoing on $S_R$ in the stated weak trace sense, and satisfies the closure relation of step 9.1; its divergence vanishes by step 10.1. Uniqueness is step 11.1. The only choice principle used is the Axiom of Choice assumed in the Statement: it supplies the Fredholm alternative, and through [L16] the Countable and Dependent Choice invoked by the compactness, trace and difference-quotient interfaces [L15], [L10], [L12], [L17]; the lift, the coercive inversion and the boundary bootstrap make only finitely many explicit selections. This proves all assertions. [given, L16, step 6.1, step 9.1, step 10.1, step 11.1, algebra] ∎
