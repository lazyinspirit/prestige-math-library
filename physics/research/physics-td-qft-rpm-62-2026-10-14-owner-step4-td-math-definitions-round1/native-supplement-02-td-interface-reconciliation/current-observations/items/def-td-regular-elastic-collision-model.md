## Definition

Fix a particle mass $m_p>0$ in kilograms and velocity space $V=\mathbb R^3$ with components in $\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. The elastic collision set is
$$Q_{\rm el}=\{(v,w,v',w')\in V^4:v+w=v'+w',\ |v|^2+|w|^2=|v'|^2+|w'|^2\}.$$
Thus each allowed event conserves momentum $m_p(v+w)$ and kinetic energy $m_p(|v|^2+|w|^2)/2$. Equip $Q_{\rm el}$ with the Borel sets generated by its relative topology.

Collision-rate data consist of a nonnegative, countably additive, $\sigma$-finite Borel measure $\nu$ on these Borel sets. It is invariant under exchanging $v$ with $w$, exchanging $v'$ with $w'$, and exchanging the incoming pair $(v,w)$ with the outgoing pair $(v',w')$: for each of these maps $S$ and Borel set $E$, $\nu(S^{-1}E)=\nu(E)$. This is the three-dimensional physical restriction of the collision set and exchange conventions in [[def-tdmath-elastic-collision-kernel]], with $\sigma$-finiteness stated explicitly for the product integrals used by the entropy theorem. For physical applications of that weak identity, at a fixed position and time let $f:V\to(0,\infty)$ be the one-particle number density per spatial volume and velocity volume: $f(v)\,d^3v$ is number per spatial volume in the velocity element. Particle number is dimensionless, $d^3v$ has units $\mathrm{m^3\,s^{-3}}$, and $f$ has units $\mathrm{s^3\,m^{-6}}$. A collision term $Q_\nu(f)$, when a representative satisfying the absolutely convergent weak identity exists, has units $\mathrm{s^2\,m^{-6}}$, namely $f$ per second. Test functions $\varphi:V\to\mathbb R$ are measurable and have one common unit $U_\varphi$ for their values (dimensionless for the tests $1$ and $\log(f/f_{\rm ref})$, where $f_{\rm ref}>0$ has the units of $f$). Assign the measure $\nu$ itself units $\mathrm{m^9\,s^{-7}}$: its numerical values are taken in these SI units, not as a density relative to an unstated measure on $Q_{\rm el}$. Thus $\int_V\varphi Q_\nu(f)\,d^3v$ and the collision integral of $f^2$ times the test bracket both have units $U_\varphi\,\mathrm{m^{-3}\,s^{-1}}$. The magnitude and distribution of $\nu$ remain model inputs; dimensional consistency fixes neither a numerical collision rate nor existence of $Q_\nu(f)$. The measure is a declared model input; no differential cross section or microscopic limit is inferred from this Definition.
