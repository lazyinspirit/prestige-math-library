## Definition

Assume AC. Fix an integer dimension $d\geq1$, use the Euclidean norm $|\cdot|_2$ from [[def-euclidean-inner-product]], define $|x|_\infty=\max_j|x_j|$, and use the numerical coordinate conventions in [[def-tdmath-poisson-configuration-reference]]. A finite configuration is a finite subset $\omega\subset\mathbb R^d$ with no repeated points; its number of points is $N(\omega)$. A pair potential is an even Borel function, measurable for the Borel sigma-algebra generated by open sets ([[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]), $\phi:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\}$, where even means $\phi(-x)=\phi(x)$. Define
$$H_\phi(\omega)=\sum_{\{x,y\}\subset\omega}\phi(x-y),\qquad H_\phi(\varnothing)=0.$$
The sum is over unordered two-point subsets, so every term is evaluated at a nonzero separation; if any term is $+\infty$, the sum is $+\infty$.

The potential is stable if some $B\geq0$ satisfies $H_\phi(\omega)\geq-BN(\omega)$ for every finite configuration. It is superstable in the Georgii splitting convention if $\phi=\phi_s+\phi_r$, where $\phi_s$ is stable and $\phi_r\geq0$ everywhere and, for some $r_0,\delta>0$, $\phi_r(x)\geq\delta$ whenever $|x|_\infty\leq r_0$. It is lower regular if a nonnegative nonincreasing $\psi:[0,\infty)\to[0,+\infty]$ satisfies
$$\phi(x)\geq-\psi(|x|_\infty),\qquad \int_0^\infty\psi(r)r^{d-1}\,dr<\infty.$$
It is regular if it is lower regular and, for some $R<\infty$, $\phi(x)\leq\psi(|x|_\infty)$ whenever $|x|_\infty\geq R$. These are the conventions used for the interacting results on this page. A genuine divergent positive core is the separate additional condition that some nonincreasing $\chi:(0,r_1]\to(0,\infty)$ satisfies $\phi(x)\geq\chi(|x|_\infty)$ for $0<|x|_\infty\leq r_1$ and $\int_0^{r_1}\chi(r)r^{d-1}dr=+\infty$; it is not implied by superstability.

For $L>0$ put $\Lambda_L=[0,L)^d$, $V_L=L^d$, and let $H_{L,\mathrm{free}}$ be $H_\phi$ for configurations restricted to $\Lambda_L$. For the periodic-image construction assume in addition that $\phi$ is regular in the convention just defined. For sufficiently large periodic cubes, periodize $\omega\subset\Lambda_L$ by $\omega^{\mathrm{per}}=\{x+Lk:x\in\omega,\ k\in\mathbb Z^d\}$ and set
$$H_{L,\mathrm{per}}(\omega)=\frac12\sum_{x\in\omega}\sum_{y\in\omega^{\mathrm{per}},\ y\ne x}\phi(y-x),$$
interpreting the value as $+\infty$ if a positive infinite term occurs. Under this regularity assumption, the far image sum is absolutely convergent for all sufficiently large $L$ by [[lem-tdmath-integrable-tail-diagonal-gap]]; this is the image convention used below. With fixed numerical length and energy units, the canonical positional partition is
$$Z(L,N;\beta)=\frac1{N!}\int_{\Lambda_L^N}e^{-\beta H_\phi(q_1,\ldots,q_N)}dq_1\cdots dq_N,\quad Z(L,0;\beta)=1,$$
where the tuple notation means $H_\phi(q_1,\ldots,q_N):=H_\phi(\{q_1,\ldots,q_N\})$ when the coordinates are distinct, and $H_\phi(q_1,\ldots,q_N):=+\infty$ on collision diagonals. The integrand is therefore zero on collisions; these diagonals have product Lebesgue measure zero by the no-collision construction in [[def-tdmath-poisson-configuration-reference]], so this convention leaves all partition integrals unchanged. For $N=0$ the empty tuple has energy zero. Here $\beta>0$, and the grand partition is $\Xi(L;a,\beta)=\sum_{N\geq0}a^NZ(L,N;\beta)$ for numerical activity $a>0$. The normalized unit-intensity Poisson version is $Z_Q(L;z,\beta)=E_{Q_{\Lambda_L}}[z^{N_{\Lambda_L}}e^{-\beta H_{L,\mathrm{free}}}]$; since the Poisson density contributes $e^{-V_L}$, $\Xi(L;z,\beta)=e^{V_L}Z_Q(L;z,\beta)$. A physical pressure identification is not part of this mathematical definition.

For a probability law $P$ on the configuration space of [[def-tdmath-poisson-configuration-reference]], stationarity means $P\circ T_x^{-1}=P$ for every $x\in\mathbb R^d$. When $P$ is stationary and $E_PN_{[0,1)^d}<\infty$ (finite intensity), write $\rho(P)=E_PN_{[0,1)^d}$. Use the finite-region relative entropy $D$ defined in [[def-tdmath-finite-region-relative-entropy]]. If $\phi$ is stable with constant $B$, then $H_{L,\mathrm{free}}\geq-BN_{\Lambda_L}$, so the negative part of $H_{L,\mathrm{free}}$ is $P$-integrable for every such $P$. Whenever the limit exists, write
$$\mathcal E(P)=\lim_{L\to\infty}V_L^{-1}E_PH_{L,\mathrm{free}},\qquad I(P)=\lim_{L\to\infty}V_L^{-1}D(P_{\Lambda_L}\Vert Q_{\Lambda_L}).$$
In the variational results below, $\Phi(P)$ denotes this same positional energy density: $\Phi(P):=\mathcal E(P)$. The cited results establish existence under their stated hypotheses; this definition does not assume either limit exists for every stationary finite-intensity law.
