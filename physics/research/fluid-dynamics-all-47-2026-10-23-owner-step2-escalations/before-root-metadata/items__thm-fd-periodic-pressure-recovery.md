## Statement

Let $Q_d=\mathbb R^d/(2\pi\mathbb Z)^d$ have normalized measure $d\mu=(2\pi)^{-d}dx$. For $\alpha=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_d)\in\mathbb N_0^d$, write $|\alpha|=\sum_j\alpha_j$ and $\partial^\alpha=\partial_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots\partial_d^{\alpha_d}$. Give $C^\infty(Q_d;\mathbb R)$ the Fr\'echet topology generated by $p_m(\phi)=\max_{|\alpha|\le m}\|\partial^\alpha\phi\|_\infty$, for $m\in\mathbb N_0$, where the derivatives are those of the smooth periodic lift to $\mathbb R^d$. A periodic distribution is a continuous real-linear functional on this test space, extended complex-linearly to complex-valued tests. For such a distribution $S$, define $\widehat S(k)=\langle S,e^{-ik\cdot x}\rangle$ for $e_k(x)=e^{ik\cdot x}$ and define its mean to be $\widehat S(0)=\langle S,1\rangle$. For an integrable function $g$, the pairing is $\langle g,\phi\rangle=\int_{Q_d}g\phi\,d\mu$. Define distributional derivatives by $\langle\partial_jS,\phi\rangle=-\langle S,\partial_j\phi\rangle$. For a smooth scalar test, $\nabla\phi=(\partial_1\phi,\ldots,\partial_d\phi)$ and $\Delta\phi=\sum_j\partial_j^2\phi$; for a smooth vector field $v$, $\operatorname{div}v=\sum_j\partial_jv_j$, $(v\otimes v)_{ij}=v_iv_j$, and $(v\cdot\nabla)v_i=\sum_jv_j\partial_jv_i$. In particular, $\operatorname{div}u=0$ distributionally means $-\int_{Q_d}u\cdot\nabla\phi\,d\mu=0$ for every real test function $\phi$. Equality of periodic distributions means equality on every such test function. Assume Countable Choice. Let $d\in\{1,2,3\}$, let $s>d/2+1$, and let $u\in H^s(Q_d;\mathbb R^d)$ satisfy $\operatorname{div}u=0$ in this sense. Then $u\otimes u\in H^s$, the mean-zero scalar $\pi=Q(u\otimes u)$ belongs to $H^s$, and in $H^{s-1}$,
$$P(u\cdot\nabla u)=u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla\pi.$$
Among mean-zero periodic distributions $\widetilde\pi$ for which this identity holds as an equality of periodic distributions, this pressure is unique. The formula is a torus pressure recovery result and asserts no wall pressure regularity.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The hypotheses and conventions stated in the formulation.

[F1] [[def-countable-choice]]: countable families of nonempty sets admit a choice function.

[F2] [[def-fd-periodic-fourier-sobolev-spaces]]: $H^s$ is the completion in $\sum_k w_k^{2s}|\widehat q(k)|^2$ with $w_k=(1+|k|^2)^{1/2}$ on the normalized periodic torus; for $s\ge0$ each element has a unique $L^2$ representative.

[F3] [[lem-fd-periodic-fourier-embedding-products]]: when $\sigma>d/2$, multiplication is continuous $H^\sigma\times H^\sigma\to H^\sigma$, and when $\sigma>1+d/2$, $H^\sigma$ embeds in $C^1$.

[F4] [[def-fd-periodic-leray-pressure-maps]]: $P$ and $Q$ are the stated bounded Fourier multipliers and $\nabla QF=-(I-P)\operatorname{div}F$.

[F5] [[thm-fourier-basis-and-parseval-on-the-n-torus]]: under Countable Choice, the characters $e^{2\pi i k\cdot y}$ form a complete orthonormal basis of $L^2(\mathbb R^d/\mathbb Z^d)$, so Fourier synthesis recovers each $L^2$ function from its coefficients; the change of variables $x=2\pi y$ gives the same completeness for $e^{ik\cdot x}$ on $Q_d$ with its normalized measure.

## Proof

**Given:** Countable Choice, $d\in\{1,2,3\}$, $s>d/2+1$, and divergence-free $u\in H^s$.

1.1 Since $s>d/2$, [F3] gives $u\otimes u\in H^s$. The bounded map $Q$ in [F4] therefore gives a mean-zero $\pi=Q(u\otimes u)\in H^s$. The coefficientwise derivative convention in [F4] gives $\partial_j u\in H^{s-1}$ because $|k_j|\le w_k$; also $u\in H^{s-1}$ since $w_k\ge1$. Since $s-1>d/2$, [F3] places each product $u_j\partial_j u_i$ and hence $u\cdot\nabla u$ in $H^{s-1}$. The embedding in [F3] gives a $C^1$ representative of $u$, since $s>1+d/2$. [given, F2, F3, F4]

2.1 The $C^1$ representative from step 1.1 obeys the ordinary product rule. With the distributional derivative convention in the Statement, this is also the periodic distributional identity
$\operatorname{div}(u\otimes u)_i=\sum_j\partial_j(u_i u_j)=u\cdot\nabla u_i+u_i\operatorname{div}u=u\cdot\nabla u_i$.
Applying the multiplier identity in [F4] yields $\nabla\pi=-(I-P)(u\cdot\nabla u)$, hence the asserted decomposition. [given, F3, F4, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 If another mean-zero periodic distribution $\widetilde\pi$ gives the same decomposition, then $\nabla S=0$ for $S=\widetilde\pi-\pi$. The derivative convention implies $\widehat{\partial_jS}(k)=ik_j\widehat S(k)$, so for each $k\ne0$ the vector equation $ik\widehat S(k)=0$ gives $\widehat S(k)=0$; the zero mode also vanishes because $S$ has mean zero. To see that this forces $S=0$ as a distribution, fix $\phi\in C^\infty(Q_d)$. For every integer $N\ge1$, periodic integration by parts gives $w_k^{2N}|\widehat\phi(k)|\le\|(1-\Delta)^N\phi\|_{L^1(d\mu)}$. Choosing $N$ arbitrarily large, the lattice shell bound $\#\{k\in\mathbb Z^d:n\le|k|<n+1\}\le C_d(n+1)^{d-1}$ follows by placing disjoint unit cubes at those lattice points: their union lies in an annulus of fixed enlarged width, whose volume is $O_d((n+1)^{d-1})$; the $n=0$ shell is finite. This bound shows that the Fourier series of $\phi$ and each of its differentiated series converge absolutely and uniformly. Its sum is therefore a smooth function with the same Fourier coefficients as $\phi$; by [F5] the two functions agree in $L^2$, hence everywhere by continuity. Thus finite Fourier sums converge to $\phi$ in every seminorm $p_m$. The distribution $S$ vanishes on every character and hence on every finite Fourier sum; its continuity gives $\langle S,\phi\rangle=0$. Since $\phi$ was arbitrary, $S=0$, proving uniqueness among all mean-zero periodic distributions. [given, F1, F2, F5, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
