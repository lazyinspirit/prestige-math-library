## Remark

As checked on 2026-10-23, the Clay Mathematics Institute lists the three-dimensional Navier–Stokes existence-and-smoothness problem among its Active problems. In its announcement dated 2026-09-11, CMI said it contemplated an announcement that the problem had apparently been settled and that the evaluation process was deliberately unhurried. This remark records CMI's public status only: it does not assess or accept the announced proof, and this batch's two-dimensional periodic theorem makes no claim about three-dimensional Navier–Stokes or unrestricted three-dimensional Euler regularity.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** This is a dated report of the public status displayed by the Clay Mathematics Institute, checked on 2026-10-23.

[F1] The CMI's current [Millennium Prize Problems page](https://www.claymath.org/Millennium%20Problems/) lists “Navier–Stokes Equation” under “Active problems.”

[F2] In a notice dated 2026-09-11, CMI [describes](https://www.claymath.org/news/navier-stokes-announcement/) its response to an announcement that the Navier–Stokes problem has “apparently been settled” and says the evaluation process is “deliberately unhurried.”

## Proof

1.1 The checked CMI list places Navier–Stokes Equation under its Active problems heading. This supports the first dated status sentence and states only the institution's public classification. [given, F1]

2.1 The CMI notice is dated 2026-09-11 and describes an announced result as apparently settling the problem while saying evaluation is deliberately unhurried. Its qualification reports an announcement under evaluation; it does not certify the proof. The remark preserves that qualification and limits the batch's result to two-dimensional periodic Navier–Stokes. [given, F2, algebra] ∎
