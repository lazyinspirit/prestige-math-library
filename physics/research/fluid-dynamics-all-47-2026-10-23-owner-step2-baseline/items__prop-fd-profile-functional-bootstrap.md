## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $H=L^2([0,1],\mathcal B,\lambda;\mathbb R)$, where $\mathcal B$ is generated by intervals with rational endpoints and $\lambda$ is Lebesgue measure restricted to $\mathcal B$. Let $(S,\mathcal S,\nu)$ be a probability space, let $(\delta_t)_{t\in\mathbb Z}$ be independent identically distributed $S$-valued innovations with law $\nu$, and let $f:S^{\mathbb N_0}\to H$ be measurable from the product sigma-algebra to the Borel sigma-algebra of $H$. Set $X_t=f(\delta_t,\delta_{t-1},\ldots)$ and assume its $H$-valued expectation is zero and $\mathbb E\|X_0\|_H^2<\infty$. For each $t\in\mathbb Z$, $m\ge1$, and $k\le t-m$, let $\delta'_{t,m,k}$ have law $\nu$, with these copies mutually independent and independent of the original innovations, and set $X_{t,m}=f(\delta_t,\ldots,\delta_{t-m+1},\delta'_{t,m,t-m},\delta'_{t,m,t-m-1},\ldots)$. Assume $\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\delta_m<\infty$, where $\delta_m=(\mathbb E\|X_0-X_{0,m}\|_H^2)^{1/2}$. For each $n\ge1$, let the integer block length $b_n$ satisfy $b_n\to\infty$ and $b_n/\sqrt n\to0$; for all sufficiently large $n$, apply moving-block resampling with block length $b_n$ to $X_1,\ldots,X_n$, truncating the last block to exactly $n$ elements. Write $\bar X_n^*=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^nX_j^*$, and let $\mathbb E^*$ and $\operatorname{Cov}^*$ be expectation and covariance over resampling with the observations fixed. Let $G$ be the centered $H$-valued Gaussian limit with covariance operator $\Gamma$ supplied by the Hilbert-valued sample-mean CLT for this process. For any bounded real-linear $L:H\to\mathbb R$, set $\|L\|=\sup_{\|u\|_H\le1}|L(u)|$, let $h_L\in H$ be the unique vector satisfying $L(u)=\langle u,h_L\rangle_H$ for every $u\in H$, and define $L^*r=rh_L$. Identify the scalar operator $L\Gamma L^*:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ with its value at $1$. Then
$$\mathcal L^*\!\left(\sqrt n\,L(\bar X_n^*-\mathbb E^*\bar X_n^*)\right)\Rightarrow\mathcal L(LG)\quad\text{in probability},$$
and, with $C_n=n\operatorname{Cov}^*(\bar X_n^*)$,
$$\left|\operatorname{Var}^*\!\left(\sqrt n\,L(\bar X_n^*-\mathbb E^*\bar X_n^*)\right)-L\Gamma L^*\right|\le\|L\|^2\|C_n-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\longrightarrow0\quad\text{in probability}.$$
Here $L\Gamma L^*$ is the variance of the limiting scalar Gaussian. This conclusion concerns bounded linear profile observations under the stated Bernoulli-shift and block-length hypotheses; it makes no claim for nonlinear statistics or for finite-replicate quantile accuracy.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The statement assumptions, including the Axiom of Choice and the exact block-length regime.

[A1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: every family of nonempty sets has a choice function. This directly supplies the assumption of the moving-block theorem and implies Countable Choice for the Riesz and basis suppliers.

[F1] [[def-fd-fluid-profile-bootstrap-interface]]: $H$ is real $L^2([0,1])$; the data are centered causal Bernoulli-shift profiles with the independent-tail replacement errors; MBB uses uniform overlapping starts, preserves within-block order, truncates to length $n$, and is centered by its actual conditional expectation.

[F2] [[thm-moving-block-bootstrap-clt-for-dependent-data]]: under AC, centered causal $L^2$-$m$-approximable $H$-valued data and $b_n\to\infty,\ b_n/\sqrt n\to0$, the conditional law of $\sqrt n(\bar X_n^*-\mathbb E^*\bar X_n^*)$ converges weakly in probability to $\mathcal L(G)$, and $\|n\operatorname{Cov}^*(\bar X_n^*)-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\to0$ in probability.

[F3] [[thm-hilbert-valued-clt-for-l2-m-approximable-bernoulli-shifts]]: the sampling-law limit $G$ is centered Gaussian in $H$, with covariance $\Gamma$; the result is a sample-sum CLT, not a time-indexed functional CLT.

[F4] [[def-conditional-weak-convergence-in-probability]]: convergence in conditional weak topology in probability means convergence in probability in a metric that metrizes weak convergence. [[def-weak-convergence-of-borel-probability-measures]] characterizes weak convergence by bounded continuous test functions.

[F5] [[thm-riesz-representation-for-hilbert-space]]: under Countable Choice, every bounded real-linear functional on a real Hilbert space has a unique representing vector $h_L$, with $\|h_L\|_H=\|L\|$; thus $L^*r=rh_L$ and $\|L^*\|=\|L\|$.

[F6] [[def-l2-m-approximable-causal-bernoulli-shift]] supplies separability of $H$ under Countable Choice, and [[thm-separable-hilbert-space-has-a-countable-orthonormal-basis]] turns a fixed countable dense sequence into a finite or countable orthonormal basis.

[F7] [[def-hilbert-schmidt-operator]] defines the Hilbert-Schmidt norm relative to a supplied Hilbert basis; [[thm-hilbert-schmidt-norm-is-basis-independent]] makes its unqualified value independent of that basis; and [[thm-hilbert-schmidt-operators-form-a-two-sided-ideal]] gives $\|ATB\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le\|A\|\,\|T\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\,\|B\|$ for bounded $A,B$. On $\mathbb R$, the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of a scalar operator is its absolute value.

## Proof

**Given:** The assumptions and supplier facts above.

1.1 By [A1], applying Choice to a family indexed by $\mathbb N$ supplies Countable Choice. Then [F1] makes $H$ a complete real Hilbert space, and [F6] makes it separable; hence $H$ is separable complete. The real line has the same property, so [F4]'s conditional weak-law topology applies to both. Let $\mu_n^*=\mathcal L^*(\sqrt n(\bar X_n^*-\mathbb E^*\bar X_n^*))$ and $\mu=\mathcal L(G)$. For every bounded continuous $\varphi:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$, $\varphi\circ L$ is bounded and continuous on $H$. The bounded-continuous-test definition [F4] therefore gives $L_\#\eta_j\Rightarrow L_\#\eta$ whenever $\eta_j\Rightarrow\eta$ on $H$, so $L_\#$ is continuous between the weak probability-law topologies. For any weak-law neighborhood $V$ of $L_\#\mu$, continuity supplies a neighborhood $U$ of $\mu$ with $L_\#U\subseteq V$. Since [F2] gives $\mu_n^*\Rightarrow\mu$ in probability, $\mathbb P(\mu_n^*\in U)\to1$, and thus $\mathbb P(L_\#\mu_n^*\in V)\to1$. For fixed $n$, resampling has finitely many block-start tuples, so conditional expectation is a finite weighted sum and commutes with $L$; hence $L_\#\mu_n^*$ is the displayed conditional scalar law and $L_\#\mu=\mathcal L(LG)$. [A1, F1, F2, F4, F6, given, algebra]

2.1 Apply [A1] to countably indexed families to obtain Countable Choice. Then [F5] gives the Riesz representative, while [F1] and [F6] give separability of $H$ and a countable orthonormal basis; use $\{1\}$ as the basis of $\mathbb R$. Put $T_n=C_n-\Gamma$ and $Z_n^*=\sqrt n(\bar X_n^*-\mathbb E^*\bar X_n^*)$. The Riesz identity gives $\operatorname{Var}^*(L(Z_n^*))=\mathbb E^*\langle Z_n^*,h_L\rangle_H^2=\langle C_nh_L,h_L\rangle_H=(LC_nL^*)(1)$. Apply [F7] to $A=L$, $T=T_n$, and $B=L^*$: $|LT_nL^*(1)|=\|LT_nL^*\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le\|L\|\,\|T_n\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\,\|L^*\|=\|L\|^2\|T_n\|_{\mathrm{HS}}$. By [F2] this bound tends to zero in probability. The covariance of $LG$ is $L\Gamma L^*$ by [F3], proving the variance conclusion, including the zero-variance case. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F5, F6, F7, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
