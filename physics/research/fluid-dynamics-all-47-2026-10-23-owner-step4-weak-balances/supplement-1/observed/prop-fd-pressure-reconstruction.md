## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $d\ge2$, $L>0$, $\nu>0$, and $u_0\in H$ for the
periodic solenoidal Fourier spaces on $\mathbb T_L^d$. Let $u$ be any global
unforced periodic Leray–Hopf solution with data $(\nu,u_0)$ supplied by Global
periodic weak viscous existence under these hypotheses. Put
$U=(0,\infty)\times\mathbb T_L^d$, and let $\mathcal D_{\rm per}(U)$ be the
space of smooth complex-valued functions on $U$, represented by smooth
$L$-periodic lifts in space, that are compactly supported in time. For tests
supported in a fixed compact interval $K\subset(0,\infty)$, use the seminorms
$$p_{K,m}(\phi)=\max_{j+|\alpha|\le m}\sup_{(t,x)\in K\times\mathbb T_L^d}|\partial_t^j\partial_x^\alpha\phi(t,x)|\qquad(m\in\mathbb N_0);$$
equip $\mathcal D_{\rm per}(U)$ with the locally convex inductive-limit
topology over these fixed-time-support spaces, generated by all seminorms whose
restriction to every fixed-time-support space is continuous. A spacetime distribution means a
continuous complex-linear functional on this test space; all pairings are
bilinear, and derivatives act by transposing the corresponding test derivative
with a minus sign. Then there is a unique mean-zero-in-space
distribution $\pi$ on $(0,\infty)\times\mathbb T_L^d$ such that
$\partial_tu+\operatorname{div}(u\otimes u)+\nabla\pi=\nu\Delta u$
as a spacetime distribution, normalized by requiring its zero spatial Fourier
coefficient to vanish. Specifically, for $k\in\mathbb Z^d$, its $k$th spatial
Fourier coefficient is the time distribution defined by
$\langle\widehat\pi(k),\psi\rangle=\langle\pi,\psi(t)e^{-i\kappa_k\cdot x}\rangle$
for $\psi\in C_c^\infty((0,\infty);\mathbb C)$, with the spacetime
distribution pairing taken using $dt\,L^{-d}dx$ and extended complex-linearly;
normalization means $\widehat\pi(0)=0$. No
function-space or pointwise regularity for $\pi$ is
asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice, $d\ge2$, $L>0$, $\nu>0$, $u_0\in H$, the global weak velocity supplied under those data, the Fourier convention of [[def-fd-periodic-solenoidal-spaces]], and the distribution and derivative conventions stated by [[def-distribution]] and [[def-distributional-derivative]].

[F1] [[thm-fd-periodic-global-weak-existence]]: under Countable Choice and the stated data, $u\in L^\infty_{\rm loc}H\cap L^2_{\rm loc}V$ and satisfies the unforced periodic solenoidal weak identity.

[F2] [[def-fd-periodic-solenoidal-spaces]]: $P_k=I-\kappa_k\kappa_k^{\mathsf T}/|\kappa_k|^2$ for $k\ne0$, and $P_0=I$.

[F3] [[def-distribution]] and [[def-distributional-derivative]]: a distribution is a continuous linear functional on smooth compactly supported tests; derivatives transfer to tests with a minus sign.

[F4] [[def-regular-distribution-from-a-locally-integrable-function]]: a locally integrable field defines a distribution by integration against tests.

## Proof

1.1 Write $T=u\otimes u$. Since $u\in L^\infty_{\rm loc}(0,\infty;L^2_x)$, it and $T$ are locally integrable on $U$; on each compact time interval this follows from finite spatial measure and $\|u(t)\otimes u(t)\|_{L^1_x}=\|u(t)\|_{L^2_x}^2$. For any fixed compact time support $K$, the regular pairings of $u$ and $T$ are bounded by their local $L^1$ norms times $p_{K,0}$, so their absolute-value seminorms restrict continuously to every fixed-support test space and belong to the family defining the test topology. They are spacetime distributions. Their distributional derivatives therefore have the declared transpose meaning. The map $a\mapsto a\otimes a$ is continuous from $L^2_x$ to $L^1_x$, since $\|a\otimes a-b\otimes b\|_1\le(\|a\|_2+\|b\|_2)\|a-b\|_2$; hence the Fourier coefficients $\widehat T(k,t)$ are measurable and bounded on finite time intervals. For $k\ne0$ set
$$\widehat\pi(k,t)=-\sum_{a,b=1}^d\frac{\kappa_{k,a}\kappa_{k,b}}{|\kappa_k|^2}\widehat T_{ab}(k,t),\qquad \widehat\pi(0,t)=0.$$
The coefficients are bounded in $k$ by $C_d\|u(t)\|_2^2$. For a smooth spacetime test $\phi$, repeated spatial integration by parts makes $\widehat\phi(k,t)$ decay faster than any power of $|k|$, uniformly with each time derivative on its compact support. Therefore $\langle\pi,\phi\rangle=\int\sum_k\widehat\pi(k,t)\widehat\phi(-k,t)dt$ converges absolutely and is bounded by a constant times one finite-order seminorm $p_{K,m}$. Its absolute-value seminorm belongs to the family defining the test topology, so it defines a spacetime distribution with zero spatial mean. [given, F1, F3, F4]

2.1 At each nonzero frequency, the Fourier multiplier identity gives $i\kappa_k\widehat\pi(k)=- (I-P_k)\,i\sum_b\kappa_{k,b}\widehat T_{\cdot b}(k)$; the zero-frequency identity is also valid because both sides vanish. The weak identity in [F1] is exactly $\partial_tu+P\operatorname{div}T-\nu\Delta u=0$ after testing against solenoidal fields. Adding the displayed gradient identity yields $\partial_tu+\operatorname{div}T+\nabla\pi-\nu\Delta u=0$ for every smooth periodic vector test, which is the claimed equation. [given, F1, F2, F3, step 1.1]

3.1 If $\pi_1,\pi_2$ are two such mean-zero distributions, their difference $Q$ has zero spatial gradient. Its $k$th spatial Fourier coefficient, a distribution in time, therefore vanishes for every $k\ne0$ since $i\kappa_k\ne0$; its zero coefficient vanishes by the mean-zero normalization. For any compactly time-supported smooth periodic test $\phi(t,x)$, repeated integration by parts in $x$ makes its spatial Fourier coefficients and all their time derivatives decay faster than every power of $|k|$, uniformly on the compact time support. Thus its rectangular Fourier partial sums converge to $\phi$ in the smooth test-function topology. Continuity of $Q$ and vanishing of all its spatial Fourier coefficients give $\langle Q,\phi\rangle=0$. Hence $Q=0$ as a spacetime distribution, proving uniqueness. [given, F2, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
