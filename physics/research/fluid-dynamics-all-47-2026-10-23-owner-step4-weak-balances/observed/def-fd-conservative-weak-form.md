## Definition

Adopt the Axiom of Countable Choice, meaning that every sequence of nonempty sets admits a choice function. For $n\ge1$, use the complete Euclidean Lebesgue measure space $(\mathbb R^n,\mathcal L_n,\lambda_n)$, where $\mathcal L_n=\mathcal L(\mathbb R^n)$ is the Caratheodory-measurable family for Lebesgue outer measure and $\lambda_n$ is that outer measure restricted to $\mathcal L_n$. The sigma-algebra and complete-measure assertions are the Lebesgue measure theorem under this axiom. For a Lebesgue-measurable set $E\subseteq\mathbb R^n$, use the trace sigma-algebra $\mathcal L_E:=\{E\cap B:B\in\mathcal L_n\}$ and the measure $\lambda_E(A):=\lambda_n(A)$ for $A\in\mathcal L_E$; its countable additivity is inherited from $\lambda_n$. A map $f:E\to\mathbb R^k$ is measurable when $f^{-1}(B)\in\mathcal L_E$ for every Borel set $B\subseteq\mathbb R^k$, where the Borel sigma-algebra is generated by the open sets. For matrix-valued maps use their finite coordinate arrays. A property holds almost everywhere on $E$ when its exceptional set is contained in a measurable $\lambda_E$-null set. Nonnegative integrals are suprema of nonnegative simple minorants; signed real integrals are the difference of the positive- and negative-part integrals when those parts are finite. Finite-dimensional integrals are taken componentwise.

Let $T>0$, let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^3$ be open, and let $m\ge1$ be an integer. Put $D:=\Omega\times[0,T)\subseteq\mathbb R^4$ in spatial-first coordinates $(x,t)$. Both $D$ and $\Omega$ are Borel and hence Lebesgue measurable. Spatial integrals use $\lambda_\Omega$ from $\lambda_3$, and space-time integrals use $\lambda_D$ from $\lambda_4$. For a finite coordinate array $v$, set $\|v\|_1:=\sum_i|v_i|$, summing all entries for a matrix. A measurable field $f:E\to\mathbb R^k$ is locally integrable when $\int_K\|f\|_1\,d\lambda_E<\infty$ for every compact $K\subseteq E$. For $E=D$ the compact sets are taken in the relative topology, including those meeting $t=0$. Fields on $D$ are identified up to $\lambda_D$-almost-everywhere equality, and initial fields on $\Omega$ up to $\lambda_\Omega$-almost-everywhere equality. The slice $\Omega\times\{0\}$ is contained in a Lebesgue-null coordinate hyperplane in $\mathbb R^4$; values of a space-time representative on that slice do not determine the separately prescribed initial field.

In the displayed weak identities, $\int_0^T\int_\Omega f\,dx\,dt$ denotes the joint integral of the zero extension of $f$ from $D$ with respect to $\lambda_4$. For each test, its compact support bounds the test and its first derivatives, so every component of the tested products is measurable and is bounded in absolute value by a fixed multiple of the norm of a locally integrable field on that compact set. Thus all tested products and their finite sums have finite signed integrals. Euclidean Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R$ is the completed product of $\lambda_3$ and $\lambda_1$; completed-product Fubini therefore also gives the displayed iterated notation, with section integrals taken for almost every time and set to zero on a measurable exceptional null set. No integrability of every individual time slice is required. The integral is unchanged by the stated almost-everywhere identifications. The same conventions apply to the scalar inequality and to the initial integrals on $\Omega$.

Let $U: \Omega\times[0,T)\to\mathbb R^m$, $F: \Omega\times[0,T)\to\mathbb R^{m\times3}$, and $S: \Omega\times[0,T)\to\mathbb R^m$ be such fields, and let $U_0:\Omega\to\mathbb R^m$ be locally integrable. For each row $a$, define $(\operatorname{div}_x F)_a=\sum_{j=1}^3\partial_{x_j}F_{aj}$ in the distributional sense, and define $F:\nabla_x\phi=\sum_{a=1}^m\sum_{j=1}^3F_{aj}\partial_{x_j}\phi_a$. The equation $\partial_tU+\operatorname{div}_xF=S$ with distributional initial datum $U_0$ means that for every $\phi\in C_c^1(\Omega\times[0,T);\mathbb R^m)$, continuously differentiable up to $t=0$ and supported away from $\partial\Omega$ and $t=T$,

$$\int_0^T\!\int_\Omega\bigl(U\cdot\partial_t\phi+F:\nabla_x\phi+S\cdot\phi\bigr)\,dx\,dt+\int_\Omega U_0(x)\cdot\phi(x,0)\,dx=0.$$

No boundary condition on $\partial\Omega$ is encoded because the test fields are supported away from it. This initial identity specifies the initial datum distributionally; it does not assert $U(\cdot,t)\to U_0$ in $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ or any other stronger trace topology. Such a trace must be assumed or proved separately. The fields are abstract mathematical variables; no physical unit system or balance postulate is imposed.

For locally integrable scalar fields $H,R:D\to\mathbb R$ and $H_0:\Omega\to\mathbb R$, and a locally integrable flux $Q: \Omega\times[0,T)\to\mathbb R^3$, the inequality $\partial_tH+\operatorname{div}_x Q\ge R$ with initial datum $H_0$ means that for every nonnegative $\psi\in C_c^1(\Omega\times[0,T))$ with the same support convention,

$$\int_0^T\!\int_\Omega\bigl(H\,\partial_t\psi+Q\cdot\nabla_x\psi+R\psi\bigr)\,dx\,dt+\int_\Omega H_0(x)\psi(x,0)\,dx\le0.$$
