This page develops conditional mathematical results for mixtures, finite-site shells, continuous ideal gases, regular elastic collision models, a specified Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, Hamiltonian volume dynamics, discrete quantum dephasing, and compact regular energy shells. Each result states the regularity, integrability, choice, measure, trace-class, or dynamical assumptions it uses.

For mixtures, compactness of the generator space and continuity of the energy give attainment and a finite-support optimizer; noncompact attainment needs additional coercivity or closedness hypotheses. The finite-site microcanonical result uses the exact width condition $\delta_N\ge q\sum_j|e_j|$ and $\delta_N/N\to0$. The continuous gas calculation uses the declared classical measure $dq\,dp/(N!h^{dN})$ and a normalized-Gaussian preparation on the exact kinetic-energy sphere; it does not establish equivalence for finite-width shells or interacting particles.

The collision entropy inequality is conditional on the symmetric weak collision identity and the stated regular solution class. The identity is a model premise; it is not derived here from reversible many-particle mechanics. Hamiltonian volume preservation gives recurrence only almost everywhere in finite invariant measure. It does not imply ergodicity, and the time-average conclusion requires ergodicity of the particular sampled map and an $L^1$ observable.

The Ornstein–Uhlenbeck result is a mathematical Brownian model under the stated filtration hypothesis. A physical Einstein relation or measured noise spectrum is not asserted. Quantum Cesàro dephasing is restricted to the batch 11 trace-class Gibbs eigenbasis interface and a discrete spectrum with finite multiplicities; its proof remains conditional on that current draft supplier. The shell measure $dA_E/|\nabla H|$ applies only to compact regular energy levels and a complete invariant Hamiltonian map on the specified band. Singular and noncompact levels require separate arguments.
