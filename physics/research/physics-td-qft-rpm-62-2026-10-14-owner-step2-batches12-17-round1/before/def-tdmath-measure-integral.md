## Definition

A measure space is a triple $(X,\Sigma,\mu)$ where $\Sigma$ contains $\varnothing,X$ and is closed under complements and countable unions (a $\sigma$-algebra) of subsets of $X$ and $\mu:\Sigma\to[0,\infty]$ is countably additive with $\mu(\varnothing)=0$. A measurable function $f:X\to[-\infty,\infty]$ has measurable inverse images of Borel sets, where the Borel sets of the extended real line are the sigma-algebra generated by its open sets. A nonnegative simple function has the form $s=\sum_{j=1}^m c_j\mathbf1_{A_j}$ with finitely many disjoint $A_j\in\Sigma$ and finite $c_j\ge0$. Define $c\mu(A)=+\infty$ if $c>0$ and $\mu(A)=+\infty$, define $0\cdot\infty:=0$, and define the finite sum of these extended nonnegative terms to be $+\infty$ if any term is $+\infty$ and otherwise the real finite sum of [[def-finite-sum]]. Then $\int s\,d\mu:=\sum_j c_j\mu(A_j)$; using canonical level sets $\{s=c\}$ makes this value representation-independent. For measurable $f\ge0$, define $\int f\,d\mu:=\sup\{\int s\,d\mu: s\text{ is simple and }0\le s\le f\}$, using [[def-complete-ordered-field]] for a bounded set of finite values and setting the supremum to $+\infty$ when the values are unbounded or one is infinite. A real measurable $f$ is integrable when $\int|f|\,d\mu<\infty$, and then $\int f:=\int f^+-\int f^-$, where $f^+=\max(f,0)$ and $f^-:=\max(-f,0)$. Complex integrability and integration are defined by integrable real and imaginary parts.

## Scope

The integral may equal $+\infty$ for a nonnegative function. For an integrable signed or complex function it is finite. The measure $\mu$ is the reference measure: changing it can change an integral and any entropy defined from a density relative to it.
