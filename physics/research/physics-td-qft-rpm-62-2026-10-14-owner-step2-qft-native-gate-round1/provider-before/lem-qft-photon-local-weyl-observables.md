## Statement

Assume Countable Choice and use the Minkowski coordinates and separation condition of [[lem-qft-photon-local-field-strength]], so $\eta(x-y,x-y)=|\mathbf x-\mathbf y|^2-(x^0-y^0)^2$. A spacetime region $O$ means an open subset of $\mathbb R^4$ in its standard topology. A real antisymmetric test $h=(h^{ab})$ is supported in $O$ when each component $h^{ab}\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^4;\mathbb R)$ has support contained in $O$. Two open regions $O_1,O_2$ are spacelike separated when $\eta(x-y,x-y)>0$ for every $x\in O_1$ and $y\in O_2$. Let $g_h\in\mathcal H_T$ be the one-particle vector for such a test from [[lem-qft-photon-local-field-strength]]. The space $\mathcal H_T$ is separable: it is the range of the bounded projection $P_T$ on the separable space $L^2(\mathbb R^3;\mathbb C^3)$. Define
$$W_O(h)=W_{\rm Segal}(\sqrt2\,g_h),$$
where $W_{\rm Segal}$ is the Weyl unitary of [[lem-qft-segal-weyl-domain]]. It is bounded and unitary, and its one-parameter group has the self-adjoint field-strength closure as generator. For every open region $O\subseteq\mathbb R^4$, let $\mathcal A(O)$ be the norm-closed unital $*$-algebra generated by $W_O(h)$ for real antisymmetric tests supported in $O$; equivalently its bounded real and imaginary parts are included as observables. If $O_1,O_2$ are spacelike separated by the stated Minkowski condition, then $\mathcal A(O_1)$ and $\mathcal A(O_2)$ commute.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice; the local field-strength test vectors; the bosonic CCR; the countable-density result; and the exact Segal/Weyl-domain supplier.

[A1] Assume Countable Choice. [[def-countable-choice]]

[F1] A compact real antisymmetric test defines $F(h)=a(g_h)+a^\dagger(g_h)$, and spacelike-separated field-strength tests commute. [[lem-qft-photon-local-field-strength]]

[F2] The bosonic CCR on finite-particle vectors are $[a(u),a^\dagger(v)]=\langle u,v\rangle_2I$. [[lem-qft-fock-ccr-car]]

[F3] Under Countable Choice, finite rational linear combinations of indicators of rational boxes form a countable dense subset of real $L^2(\mathbb R^3)$. [[thm-rational-box-step-functions-form-a-countable-dense-subset-of-l-p-of-rn]]

[F4] For separable $H$, the Segal field is essentially self-adjoint on $\mathcal D_{\rm fin}$, its closure is the generator of $W_{\rm Segal}(h)$, and
$$W_{\rm Segal}(u)W_{\rm Segal}(v)=e^{-i\,\operatorname{Im}\langle u,v\rangle_2/2}W_{\rm Segal}(u+v).$$
[[lem-qft-segal-weyl-domain]]

## Proof

**Given:** [A1], [F1]–[F4], and the second-variable-linear Fock pairing.

1.1 Let $D$ be the real countable dense family in [F3]. Then $D+iD$ is countable and dense in complex scalar $L^2$, because the squared norm of a complex error is the sum of the squared real- and imaginary-part errors. Its finite triple product is countable and dense in $L^2(\mathbb R^3;\mathbb C^3)$. If $(v_n)$ enumerates that dense set, boundedness of $P_T$ makes $(P_Tv_n)$ dense in $\operatorname{Ran}P_T=\mathcal H_T$: for $u=P_Tu$, any $v_n\to u$ implies $P_Tv_n\to u$. Thus the separability hypothesis of [F4] holds, and the Segal supplier gives a bounded unitary $W_{\rm Segal}(u)$ for every $u\in\mathcal H_T$. [A1, F3, F4, given]

2.1 From [F1]–[F2], on $\mathcal D_{\rm fin}$,
$$[F(h),F(k)]=[a(g_h)+a^\dagger(g_h),a(g_k)+a^\dagger(g_k)] =(\langle g_h,g_k\rangle_2-\langle g_k,g_h\rangle_2)I =2i\,\operatorname{Im}\langle g_h,g_k\rangle_2\,I.$$
For spacelike-separated supports the left side vanishes by [F1], hence $\operatorname{Im}\langle g_h,g_k\rangle_2=0$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 The supplier's one-parameter group generated by the closed Segal field gives $W_{\rm Segal}(\sqrt2g_h)$ with generator $\sqrt2\,\Phi(g_h)=\overline{a(g_h)+a^\dagger(g_h)}$, which is the closure of $F(h)$ from [F1]. Thus $W_O(h)$ is the bounded Weyl observable associated to this field strength. It is unitary, and its real and imaginary parts are bounded self-adjoint operators with norm at most one. [F1, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 The Weyl relation [F4] gives
$$W_O(h)W_O(k)=e^{-i\,\operatorname{Im}\langle\sqrt2g_h,\sqrt2g_k\rangle_2/2}W_{\rm Segal}(\sqrt2g_h+\sqrt2g_k).$$
By step 2.1 the phase is one for spacelike-separated tests, so $W_O(h)$ and $W_O(k)$ commute. Products, adjoints, and norm limits preserve commutation; therefore the norm-closed algebras of two spacelike-separated regions commute. Since $W_{\rm Segal}(0)=I$, these algebras are unital. [F4, step 2.1, given] ∎
