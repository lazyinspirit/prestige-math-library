## Proposed statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let `Omega` be a bounded `C∞` domain in `R3`, in the graph sense of `def-bounded-c-k-domain-and-boundary-charts`; connectedness is unnecessary. Write `H^m(Omega)=W^{m,2}(Omega;R)` with the multi-index integral norm and `H0^1(Omega)` for the `H1` closure of `Cc∞(Omega)`. For every real `f in L2(Omega)` there is a unique `u in H0^1(Omega) ∩ H2(Omega)` satisfying

$$\int_\Omega(\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+uv)=\int_\Omega fv\quad(v\in H_0^1(\Omega)).$$

It satisfies `(1−Delta)u=f` distributionally and almost everywhere as an `L2` identity, and

$$\|u\|_{H^1(\Omega)}\le\|f\|_{L^2(\Omega)},\qquad \|u\|_{H^2(\Omega)}\le C_\Omega\|f\|_{L^2(\Omega)}.$$

Moreover `D={phi in C∞(closure Omega): phi|boundary Omega=0}` is dense in `H2(Omega) ∩ H0^1(Omega)` in the `H2` norm: every such `w` has `phi_j in D` with `phi_j→w` in `H2`. Here smoothness on the closure means restriction of a smooth function in a neighbourhood of the closure, equivalently the smooth chart representatives used below. In particular, `grad phi_j→grad w` in the graph norm of `H(curl) ∩ H(div)`, with zero curl and `div grad w=Delta w`; every `grad phi_j` is smooth and PEC since its tangential derivatives vanish at the boundary. Thus `grad w` belongs to the cavity's explicitly defined completion `X`. This is a membership conclusion for that completion, without an assertion equating it with every maximal trace realisation.

No smoothness of `f`, no classical boundary value for general `u`, and no general nonsmooth-domain, Neumann, interface, or variable-coefficient claim is included. Zero boundary condition means membership in `H0^1`; the published trace theorem identifies its trace as zero.

## Complete scaffold strategy

1. **Variational existence and its sharp H1 bound.** B1 supplies completeness and the Hilbert structure of `H0^1`. Equip it with the full quadratic `H1` norm and set `a(w,v)=integral(grad w·grad v+wv)`. Cauchy–Schwarz on the four-component `L2` tuple makes this a bounded symmetric form, and `a(v,v)=||v||H1²`, so the coercivity constant is exactly one. The functional `ell(v)=integral fv` has norm at most `||f||2` because `||v||2≤||v||H1`. Apply B2's explicit minimizing-sequence solver, with its declared Countable Choice covered by AC, to obtain the unique weak `H0^1` solution and `||u||H1≤||f||2`. Testing against compactly supported smooth functions gives the distributional equation. No Lax–Milgram result or unproved minimizer theorem is substituted. None of B1's numerical Poincaré constants is used.

2. **Charts and admissible tangential tests.** Cover the compact closure by finitely many interior patches and smooth graph patches. Shrink each patch twice, with compact support away from its artificial sides, and choose cutoffs equal to one on the inner patches. A graph flattening followed, if needed, by reversal of the last coordinate sends the domain side to `t>0`; smooth charts and inverse bounds through order two give bounded local `H1` and `H2` changes of variables. With `y=Phi(x)`, `J=|det D(Phi^{-1})|`, `U=u∘Phi^{-1}`, the local weak equation is

$$\int A_{ij}\partial_jU\partial_iV+cUV=\int FV,\qquad A=J(D\Phi)(D\Phi)^{\mathsf T},\quad c=J,\quad F=J(f\circ\Phi^{-1}).$$

All coefficients and first derivatives are bounded on the chosen patch, `A` is symmetric and uniformly positive, and `||F||2≤C||f||2` locally. The change-of-variables formula and weak chain rule justify these identities. A localized flattened `U` lies in `H0^1` of the half-space: take the sequence of `Cc∞(Omega)` functions defining the original `H0^1` membership, multiply by the patch cutoff, and pull back. These are smooth compactly supported functions on the open half-space and converge in `H1`; their supports need not have a uniform distance from its boundary. Hence tangential translations and smooth cutoffs preserve this closure. For `k=1,2`, `w_h=delta_h^k U` and `V=−delta_{−h}^k(eta² w_h)` are admissible tests, pulled back and extended by zero outside the chart. Use a larger preliminary localization equal to one on the quotient neighbourhood, so its cutoff never changes the original equation on the test support. Interior patches allow all three quotient directions. No normal quotient crosses the boundary.

3. **The L2-data estimate, with no differentiated datum.** Put `S=F−cU`, only in `L2`. Discrete integration by parts and the quotient product rule give the principal term

$$\int\eta^2 A_{ij}(y+he_k)\partial_jw_h\partial_iw_h\ge\alpha\|\eta\nabla w_h\|_2^2.$$

The exact remaining principal terms are `integral eta²(delta_h Aij)∂jU ∂iw_h` and `integral [Aij(y+h)∂jw_h+(delta_h Aij)∂jU](∂i eta²) w_h`. Thus their absolute sum is at most

$$C\bigl(\|U\|_{H^1}+\|w_h\|_2\bigr)\|\eta\nabla w_h\|_2+C\|U\|_{H^1}\|w_h\|_2.$$

Here `||delta_h A||infinity≤||∂k A||infinity`, and the published directional increment estimate yields `||w_h||2≤C||U||H1` on the required enlarged quotient neighbourhood. The undifferentiated right side obeys

$$\left|\int S\,\delta_{-h}^k(\eta^2w_h)\right|\le C\|S\|_2\bigl(\|\eta\nabla w_h\|_2+\|w_h\|_2\bigr),$$

because the same increment bound applies to `eta²w_h` in that direction. Cauchy–Schwarz and Young's inequality absorb half the principal gradient square and give, uniformly for all sufficiently small nonzero `h`,

$$\|\eta\nabla\delta_h^kU\|_2\le C(\|F\|_2+\|U\|_{H^1}).$$

This estimates the actual `L2` datum, not its difference quotient; no `delta_h F` or derivative of `f` is present. The published tangential-test lemma supplies the exact commutator identity, but this explicit estimate is still part of the new proof.

4. **Weak mixed derivatives and corrected normal recovery.** On a smaller patch where `eta=1`, for every compactly supported smooth test `psi`, discrete integration by parts gives `integral delta_h^k(∂jU) psi = −integral ∂jU delta_{−h}^k psi`, whose limit is `−integral ∂jU ∂k psi`. The uniform bound in step 3 makes this distribution a bounded functional in the `L2` norm on tests. Extend it continuously to the closed `L2` subspace generated by those tests, which is Hilbert by `L2` completeness; Riesz gives an `L2` representing function with the same bound. This represents `∂k∂j U` distributionally. No weakly convergent subsequence, reflexivity theorem, or asserted density of tests in the whole `L2` space is required. Thus boundary patches have every second derivative with at least one tangential index; weak derivatives commute by testing smooth functions. The divergence-form equation gives the missing normal derivative with the signs

$$A_{33}\partial_{33}U=cU-F-\sum_{(i,j)\ne(3,3)}A_{ij}\partial_{ij}U-\sum_{i,j}(\partial_iA_{ij})\partial_jU.$$

Every term on the right is in `L2` and `A33≥alpha>0`, so division yields `∂33U in L2` and its bound. This follows distributionally before making the almost-everywhere identification. Interior patches already have all second derivatives. Return through the bounded `H2` chart maps, sum the finitely many inner-patch estimates, and obtain `||u||H2≤C(||f||2+||u||H1)≤C_Omega||f||2`. The equation now holds in `L2`. Uniqueness in `H0^1∩H2` is already uniqueness in the larger variational space.

5. **Zero-trace H2 density: prerequisites and flat gluing.** This step applies to every `w in H2∩H0^1`, not just the resolvent. Use a finite smooth ambient partition with one interior part and boundary parts compactly contained in graph charts. Multiplication and flattening preserve `H2`; a boundary part gives a compactly supported `W in H2(H)` with flat trace zero by chart compatibility and bounded trace continuity (or by the `H0^1` approximating sequence from step 2). Its support is separated from the chart's artificial edges. To justify the reflection, first obtain unrestricted smooth `H2` approximants of `W` on the half-space from the published `k=2,p=2` half-space extension, multiplication by a fixed compact cutoff equal to one on its support, and whole-space mollification. They converge in `H2`, so traces of the functions and their first derivatives converge in `L2` by the published `H1` flat trace bound. For any `zeta in Cc∞(R2)` and tangential `k`, their classical boundary integration by parts gives `integral T(∂kW) zeta = −integral TW ∂k zeta = 0`. Hence `T(∂kW)=0` as a distribution and almost everywhere; uniqueness of regular distributions follows directly from mollification/test pairings. This proves the needed tangential trace identity, rather than assuming it.

Define the odd reflection `W_o(y,t)=W(y,t)` for `t>0`, `−W(y,−t)` for `t<0`. A piecewise `H1` function whose two traces agree is globally `H1`: prove this by testing each half against a compact smooth ambient test, using smooth half-space approximation, one-variable integration and Fubini; the sole interface contribution is the difference of its two traces and hence cancels. Apply this gluing calculation first to `W_o` (both traces zero), then to its first derivatives. The tangential derivatives are odd reflections of `∂kW`, with both traces zero by the preceding argument. The normal derivative is the even reflection of `∂tW`, with identical traces on both sides; its trace need not vanish. Thus `W_o in H2(R3)`, and each derivative has the indicated reflected `L2` class; its `H2` norm is `sqrt(2)||W||H2`. No delta layer occurs. Even reflection of `W`, or zero extension in `H2`, would generally fail and is not used.

6. **Mollification, localization, and the actual cavity norm.** Choose a real compactly supported mass-one mollifier invariant under `t→−t`, for example a radial one, and convolve `W_o`. The mollifications converge in `H2(R3)` because weak derivatives commute with convolution and the `L2` approximate identity converges for each of the finitely many derivatives. They remain odd, so vanish exactly at `t=0`. Multiply by a fixed smooth cutoff in the flattened ambient chart equal to one near the support of `W_o`, with support separated from the artificial edges. This preserves boundary vanishing and convergence. For all sufficiently small radii the cutoff may also be chosen even in `t`, although only zero values at `t=0` are needed after multiplication. Pull back through the smooth chart and extend by zero outside its artificial support to get globally smooth functions on a neighbourhood of the closure with zero physical boundary value. Approximate the interior part by compactly supported smooth functions using legitimate compact-support zero extension and mollification, with radii smaller than its distance from the physical boundary. Add the finitely many parts, choosing radii tending to zero (AC covers sequence choices), to obtain `phi_j→w` in `H2` with `phi_j|boundary=0`. Each lies in `H0^1` by the published zero-trace kernel theorem. Finally `curl grad phi_j=0`, `div grad phi_j=Delta phi_j`, and `n×grad phi_j=0`, the last identity following by differentiating the identically zero smooth boundary restriction. Therefore

$$\|\nabla\phi_j-\nabla w\|_2+\|\operatorname{curl}(\nabla\phi_j-\nabla w)\|_2+\|\operatorname{div}(\nabla\phi_j-\nabla w)\|_2\longrightarrow0.$$

The curls vanish distributionally by commutation of weak derivatives, which is a direct test-function calculation. This is exactly convergence in G3's graph norm, giving `grad w in X` without a maximal-trace-density assumption.

## Exact G3 use and qualifications carried forward
