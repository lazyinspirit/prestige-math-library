## Sobol interface

The actual Luna rejection is genuine. The original prose restricts the returned ratios to a positive empirical denominator but then supplies an unqualified derivative-based covariance matrix. The cited algorithm returns that matrix only in the positive-denominator branch. The ratio map's derivative is undefined at a zero denominator.

Every denominator row is a nonnegative half squared difference. Thus the empirical denominator is nonnegative, and the two branches exhaust its possibilities. When it is zero, the procedure returns `zero-empirical-variance`, no normalized indices, and no plug-in covariance matrix or MCSE. When it is positive, the ratio map is smooth on an open half-space containing the observed means, so its derivative and the finite formal plug-in matrix are defined. These are the exact branches of the imported algorithm; the proposal does not discard failed samples, resample, condition a population claim on success, or replace failure with a finite ratio.

The full imported ratio-CLT proof and Statement were read. Its finite row covariance and delta-method/MCSE conclusion require a finite product input law, IID rows, the stated independent vectors within each row, AC, fourth-integrable output and positive population variance. The current remark retains the distinction between its L2 numerator identities and the stronger L4 asymptotic interface. A finite formal sample covariance under L2 alone is not a valid asymptotic MCSE assertion. The independent-coordinate model is not generalized to dependent coordinates. The expected scalar-observable budget still gives no error bound for normalized Sobol ratios.

The theorem defines an extension of the ratio vector on the zero-status event only for asymptotic statements; any fixed finite extension of the MCSE matrix there has the same scaled covariance convergence because the failure probability tends to zero. This convention does not make the finite-sample algorithm return a value on failure. The proposal explicitly distinguishes the two.

The Bernoulli witness proves that positive population variance does not rule out finite-sample failure. With two IID rows, each denominator is positive with probability one half, so their count `K` is Binomial(2, 1/2). The denominator mean is `K/4`, hence it is zero with probability one quarter. Independent numerator draws give expected ratio `1/K` conditional on a positive count. The conditional probabilities of counts one and two are two thirds and one third, so the expected returned ratio is `5/6`, while the population first-order index is one. The exact enumeration of all 4096 equally likely 12-bit draws, including unused X-prime bits, independently confirms failure probability `1/4` and conditional expectation `5/6`; it is arithmetic evidence, not measured data.

The proposal makes the zero branch explicit in the Remark, Facts F4, and final proof step, records the witness failure probability, and states the asymptotic-only extension convention. It retains all finite-sample branches and all original assumptions. It makes no empirical certainty or model-validation claim.
