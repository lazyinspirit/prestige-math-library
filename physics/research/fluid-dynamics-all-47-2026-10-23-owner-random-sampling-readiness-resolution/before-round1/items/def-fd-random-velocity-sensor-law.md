## Definition

Let $d\ge1$ be an integer and let $(D,\mathcal D,\lambda)$ be a finite measure space with $0<\lambda(D)<\infty$, interpreted as a spatial region with volume measure $\lambda$. Let $H:=L^2(D,\lambda;\mathbb R^d)$ consist of almost-everywhere equivalence classes of measurable vector fields $v$ with $\int_D|v(x)|^2\,d\lambda(x)<\infty$, where $|\cdot|$ is the Euclidean norm. Give $H$ the norm $\|v\|_H:=(\int_D|v(x)|^2\,d\lambda(x))^{1/2}$ and its norm topology; this is the finite direct sum of the scalar $L^2$ norms. Write $\mathcal B(H)$ for the sigma-algebra generated by the norm-open subsets of $H$. A finite-second-moment random velocity law is a probability measure $\mu$ on $(H,\mathcal B(H))$ such that
$$\int_H \|v\|_H^2\,d\mu(v)<\infty.$$
For a sensor weight $\psi\in H$, define the ideal linear sensor and its centered observable by
$$S_\psi(v):=\int_D v(x)\cdot\psi(x)\,d\lambda(x),\qquad
f_\psi(v):=S_\psi(v)-\int_H S_\psi(w)\,d\mu(w).$$
The sensor reads a weighted spatial average; it does not represent a point evaluation. When $v$ is assigned SI velocity units of metres per second and $\lambda$ is volume in $\mathrm m^d$, assigning $\psi$ units $\mathrm m^{-d}$ makes $S_\psi(v)$ a velocity. Other weight units multiply the sensor units by the corresponding factor. The centered observable is dimensionally the same as the sensor reading.
