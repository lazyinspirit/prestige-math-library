## Statement

Let $(\mathcal Z,\Sigma)$ be a measurable space and let $\mu$ be a probability measure on it, meaning a countably additive measure on $\Sigma$ with $\mu(\mathcal Z)=1$. Let $X$ be a real normed vector space and give it the norm-Borel $\sigma$-algebra generated by its norm-open sets. Let $S,\widetilde S:\mathcal Z\to X$ be measurable maps. Let $J:X\to\mathbb R$ be $L$-Lipschitz, meaning $|J(x)-J(y)|\le L\|x-y\|_X$ for all $x,y\in X$, with $L\ge0$. Suppose

$$\int_{\mathcal Z}\bigl(\|S\|_X^2+\|\widetilde S\|_X^2\bigr)\,d\mu<\infty,\qquad \int_{\mathcal Z}\|S-\widetilde S\|_X^2\,d\mu\le\varepsilon^2$$

for some finite $\varepsilon\ge0$. For a measurable scalar function $h$, write $\|h\|_{L^2(\mu)}=(\int_{\mathcal Z}|h|^2\,d\mu)^{1/2}$. Let $g:\mathcal Z\to[0,\infty)$ be measurable with $\int g\,d\mu=1$ and $g\le C$ $\mu$-almost everywhere for a finite $C\ge1$, and define the target probability law by $\nu(E)=\int_Eg\,d\mu$ for $E\in\Sigma$, with target expectation $\mathbb E_\nu[H]=\int Hg\,d\mu$ whenever the integral exists. Then the measurable scalar observables $J\circ S$ and $J\circ\widetilde S$ are $\nu$-integrable and

$$\left|\mathbb E_\nu[J(S)]-\mathbb E_\nu[J(\widetilde S)]\right|\le L\sqrt C\,\varepsilon.$$

The estimate assumes a population mean-square error under $\mu$ and a bounded target density $g$; a finite test sample by itself establishes neither assumption. The state space may represent fluid fields when that measurable state model is supplied. For a physical application, both observables must use the same declared scalar unit; this mathematical proposition assigns no physical units.

## Statement references

[Sheldon Axler, *Measure, Integration & Real Analysis*, §7.9](https://measure.axler.net/MIRA.pdf).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The measurable space, normed state space, maps, Lipschitz observable, probability measure, population moment bounds, target density, and constant $C$ stated above.

[A1] $S,\widetilde S$ are measurable and have the finite second moments displayed in the Statement; $J$ is $L$-Lipschitz.

[A2] $g\ge0$, $\int g\,d\mu=1$, and $g\le C$ $\mu$-almost everywhere; target expectations use the stated density integral.

[F1] [[def-fd-random-fluid-observable]]: a supplied measurable random state may take values in a specified measurable state or path space, and a real observable is its measurable scalar composition; physical outputs compared as a bias must use the same declared scalar unit. The Statement supplies a normed state space, measurable maps, Lipschitz $J$, and the needed moment assumptions, and assigns no units.

[F2] [[thm-holder-inequality-for-integrals]]: for measurable square-integrable real functions $a,b$, $\int|ab|\,d\mu\le\|a\|_{L^2(\mu)}\|b\|_{L^2(\mu)}$.

## Proof

**Given:** The hypotheses in [A1]–[A2] and the supplier interfaces in [F1]–[F2].

1.1 The Lipschitz map $J$ is continuous, so the measurable state maps make $J\circ S$ and $J\circ\widetilde S$ measurable scalar observables as in [F1]. By [A2], $\int g^2d\mu\le C\int gd\mu=C$, hence $\|g\|_{L^2(\mu)}\le\sqrt C$. For every $z\in\mathcal Z$, $|J(S(z))|\le|J(0)|+L\|S(z)\|_X$; [F2] and [A1] therefore give $\int |J(S)|g\,d\mu\le |J(0)|+L\|\|S\|_X\|_{L^2(\mu)}\|g\|_{L^2(\mu)}<\infty$. The same estimate with $\widetilde S$ proves both target expectations are finite. [A1, A2, F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 The target-density definition and Lipschitz condition give
$$\left|\mathbb E_\nu[J(S)]-\mathbb E_\nu[J(\widetilde S)]\right|=\left|\int (J(S)-J(\widetilde S))g\,d\mu\right|\le L\int\|S-\widetilde S\|_Xg\,d\mu.$$
The integral is well-defined by step 1.1 and $g\ge0$. [A1, A2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Apply [F2] to $\|S-\widetilde S\|_X$ and $g$, then use the mean-square bound in [A1] and $\|g\|_2\le\sqrt C$ from step 1.1. This bounds the last integral in step 2.1 by $\varepsilon\sqrt C$. [A1, A2, F2, step 1.1]

4.1 Multiplying the step 3.1 bound by $L$ in step 2.1 proves the claimed inequality. [A1, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
