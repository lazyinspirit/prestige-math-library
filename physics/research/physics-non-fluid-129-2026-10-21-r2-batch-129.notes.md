## Formulation and dependency audit

The open problem is conditional on an actually existing selected solution; it does not infer solution existence, uniqueness, stability, or empirical applicability from the semiclassical equation. The model data include the free scalar equation and couplings, globally hyperbolic domain, fixed-coupling equation, state, causal prescription, scale and finite covariant counterterms. A Hadamard state is stated as a positive normalized state with a bisolution and smooth difference from the fixed local singular kernel. The horizon is a specified smooth segment; if called an event horizon, its conformal completion and future-null-infinity definition are included. Cuts are compact smooth spacelike cross-sections transverse to a future affine null generator, and their strict order is defined by the affine cut parameter on each common generator.

At each finite regulator the entropy terms have units J K⁻¹ and follow the finite-regulator entropy Definition. The fixed scale, local terms, and finite normalization are held to the same covariant prescription on both cuts and across compared sequences. The target is the finite limit of the *difference*; it does not require separate limits of the two endpoint entropies. The admissible regulator class must be nonempty, include multiple constructions, and use the same selected solution/state and scheme, so independence is not vacuous. “Nonperturbative in the scalar field” excludes scalar-amplitude/state-correlation truncation while retaining the adopted effective semiclassical regime. The open problem does not invoke the optional area-calibration branch of the regulated-entropy Definition and does not add an ACω assumption; the Definition's transitive graph includes its optional calibration dependencies, which are not used here.

The finite GSL theorem is not strengthened: it assumes finite nested factors, faithful reference states, and the adopted finite balance, and its limit clause assumes both endpoint sequences converge finitely in one scheme. It does not provide continuum convergence, regulator independence, semiclassical backreaction, or existence of the selected collapse solution. The finite balance supplier is referenced only to state which identity is not assumed.

The remark distinguishes research-status reports from theorem premises and limits each source statement to its actual scope. Wall's proof retains horizon-algebra, semiclassical, renormalized-entropy, and limit hypotheses; crossed-product results retain their stationary/Killing-horizon and algebraic assumptions; Jia–Qi–Gao's result is first-order with leading local area-law identification; island and JT calculations retain replica/saddle and ensemble assumptions. Kirklin's 2025 theorem is a particularly close comparison and is recorded explicitly: it proves an exact modified inequality for von Neumann entropies of gauge-invariant algebras outside idealized dynamical quantum-reference-frame cuts in perturbative gravity, conditioned on cut ordering and including the later-cut free energy. In its UV-cutoff semiclassical limit the paper obtains only an approximate leading-order reduction to the usual area-plus-outside entropy, up to a state-independent constant; ordinary monotonicity requires the later-cut free energy to be nonnegative under extra cut conditions. Its Type II algebra entropy and idealized relational-cut model are not identified with the specific local fixed-covariant-scheme finite-regulator sequence in this open problem. The remark does not claim exhaustive literature coverage, that no other proof exists, that universal claims are false, or that any result establishes generic evaporation unitarity.
