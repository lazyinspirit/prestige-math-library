

## Remark

For the mathematical terms, let $(M,g,N)$ be a time-oriented smooth Lorentzian four-manifold and $\iota:\Sigma_0\hookrightarrow M$ a spacelike embedded three-manifold. Vacuum initial data mean $(\Sigma_0,h_0,K_0)$, where $h_0=\iota^*g$ and $K_0(V,W)=g(\nabla_Vn,W)$ for tangent vectors $V,W$ and the unique future-directed unit normal $n$; the data satisfy the matter-free, zero-cosmological-constant specialization of the initial-data constraints. Here the vacuum Einstein equation means $\operatorname{Ric}(g)=0$ with the curvature convention defining its Ricci tensor. A Cauchy hypersurface is a spacelike embedded hypersurface met exactly once, in parameter value, by every inextendible causal curve. A future globally hyperbolic development is a time-oriented Lorentzian solution with an embedding of $\Sigma_0$ as a Cauchy hypersurface inducing $h_0$ and $K_0$. The local Kerr definition uses geometric parameters $M>0$ and $|a|<M$ for the subextremal exterior; when a physical interpretation is adopted, $M=G_Nm_{\mathrm{phys}}/c^2$ and $a=J_{\mathrm{phys}}/(m_{\mathrm{phys}}c)$, so $a$ is the specific-angular-momentum length parameter. The paper's notation $\operatorname{Kerr}(a_0,m_0)$ corresponds to $(a,M)=(a_0,m_0)$, and its smallness condition $|a_0|/m_0\ll1$ is dimensionless.

In this report, “asymptotically flat” refers to the source's own initial-data class, not to an unspecified universal falloff condition. Definition 3.4.2 takes an initial-data layer $L_0(a_0,m_0)$ in the future of asymptotically flat data on a spacelike $\Sigma_0$, with source parameters $m_0$ and $a_0$, and defines $(\epsilon_0,k)$-admissibility using its initial-layer norm $I_k$ from §3.3.6. The precise Theorem 3.4.3 assumes $|a_0|/m_0$ sufficiently small, $k_{\rm large}$ sufficiently large, $\epsilon_0$ sufficiently small, and in particular $I_{k_{\rm large}+10}\le\epsilon_0^2$. The authors define $\mathscr I^+$ as their future null infinity; Definition 3.4.5 uses “admissible future null complete” for a future development of such a layer with complete $\mathscr I^+$ and the other listed geometric conditions.

The arXiv v1 abstract says that the authors prove full unconditional nonlinear stability of this small-angular-momentum Kerr family in asymptotically flat solutions of the vacuum Einstein equations. Its simplified Theorem 1.2.1 states that the future globally hyperbolic development of a general initial-data set in that source class, sufficiently close in a suitable topology to $\operatorname{Kerr}(a_0,m_0)$ initial data and with sufficiently small $|a_0|/m_0$, has complete future null infinity and converges in the source's set $J^{-1}(\mathscr I^+)$ to a nearby $\operatorname{Kerr}(a_\infty,m_\infty)$, with final parameters close to the initial ones. The precise convergence and parameter-closeness conclusions are stated in the authors' norms, including estimate (3.4.8).

This is a report of the authors' stated result and its source-defined scope, not a local proof or independent verification of the full series. The v1 abstract records that three companion papers had been released and the remaining papers were expected; §1.2.1 identifies estimates assigned to forthcoming papers. The 2008 review's discussion of then-known global constructions is historical only. This remark makes no current-status claim about whether the full subextremal or extremal Kerr stability problem is open or solved.
