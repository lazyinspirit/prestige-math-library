## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $d\ge1$, $N\ge3$, $T\ge0$, and let $(\eta_t)_{0\le t\le T}$ be the SSEP from the diffusive-model definition. Let $H:[0,T]\times\mathbb T^d\to\mathbb R$ be smooth up to the time endpoints, meaning all time and space derivatives used below extend continuously to $[0,T]\times\mathbb T^d$. Define

$$F_s^N(\eta):=N^{-d}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_N}\eta_xH(s,x/N),\qquad 0\le s\le T.$$

Then

$$M_t^N:=F_t^N(\eta_t)-F_0^N(\eta_0)-\int_0^t\langle\pi_s^N,\partial_sH(s,\cdot)+\Delta_NH(s,\cdot)\rangle\,ds$$

is a square-integrable martingale, and

$$A_t^N:=N^{2-2d}\int_0^t\sum_{i=1}^d\sum_{x\in\Lambda_N}(\eta_{x+e_i}(s)-\eta_x(s))^2\bigl(H(s,(x+e_i)/N)-H(s,x/N)\bigr)^2\,ds$$

is a continuous predictable increasing process with $(M_t^N)^2-A_t^N$ a martingale. In particular,

$$A_T^N\le T N^{-d}\sum_{i=1}^d\sup_{0\le s\le T}\|\partial_iH(s,\cdot)\|_\infty^2.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, the finite SSEP rate matrix and its uniformized realization, a smooth time-dependent periodic test function $H$, and a finite horizon $T$.

[A1] Assume the Axiom of Choice, meaning every family of nonempty sets has a choice function, as required by the finite-chain realization and martingale suppliers ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F1] [[def-csm-frontier-ssep-diffusive-model]]: the state space is finite, each allowed bond swap has rate $N^2$, and $F_s^N(\eta)=\langle\pi^N(\eta),H(s,\cdot)\rangle$.

[F2] [[lem-csm-frontier-finite-generator-chain-existence]]: for a finite rate matrix, uniformization has a finite dominating event rate and conditional transition matrices $e^{hL}$.

[F3] [[lem-csm-frontier-finite-chain-martingale]]: a fixed bounded state function has a generator martingale with compensated squared-jump rate $\Gamma_f(i)=\sum_{j\ne i}L_{ij}(f(j)-f(i))^2$.

[F4] [[def-progressively-measurable-and-predictable-process]]: the predictable sigma-algebra is generated by rectangles $(a,b]\times B$ with $B\in\mathcal F_a$ and time-zero sets; a continuous adapted process is predictable by its left-step approximations.

## Proof

1.1 For fixed $s$, reindexing the bond sum as in the model generator gives $L_NF_s^N(\eta)=\langle\pi^N(\eta),\Delta_NH(s,\cdot)\rangle$; also $\partial_sF_s^N(\eta)=\langle\pi^N(\eta),\partial_sH(s,\cdot)\rangle$. Hence the compensating drift in the Statement is exactly $(\partial_sF_s^N+L_NF_s^N)(\eta_s)$. [F1, algebra]

2.1 Fix $N$ and a state $i=\eta_s$. By [F2], on an interval of length $h$, two or more potential uniformization events have probability $O_N(h^2)$, one actual transition $i\to j$ has probability $hL_N(i,j)+O_N(h^2)$, and no state change has probability $1-h\lambda_i+O_N(h^2)$, with $\lambda_i=\sum_{j\ne i}L_N(i,j)$. Taylor expansion is uniform over the finite state set and $s\in[0,T]$. If no state change occurs, then $F_{s+h}^N(i)-F_s^N(i)=h\partial_sF_s^N(i)+O_N(h^2)$ and the compensating integral is $h(\partial_sF_s^N+L_NF_s^N)(i)+O_N(h^2)$, so the increment of $M^N$ is $-hL_NF_s^N(i)+O_N(h^2)$. A single transition $i\to j$ gives increment $F_s^N(j)-F_s^N(i)+O_N(h)$; summing its conditional mean over $j$ gives $hL_NF_s^N(i)+O_N(h^2)$, which cancels the no-transition term. Paths with at least two potential events contribute $O_N(h^2)$ because $M^N$ has bounded increments for fixed $N,T$. Therefore $|\mathbb E[M_{s+h}^N-M_s^N\mid\mathcal F_s]|\le C_Nh^2$. In the conditional second moment, no transition contributes $O_N(h^2)$, one transition contributes $h\sum_{j\ne i}L_N(i,j)(F_s^N(j)-F_s^N(i))^2+O_N(h^2)=h\Gamma_{F_s^N}(i)+O_N(h^2)$, and two or more potential events contribute $O_N(h^2)$. [A1, F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 If $T=0$, the only time is $0$ and $M_0^N=0$. For $T>0$, fix $s<t$ and partition $[s,t]$ into $n$ equal intervals of length $h=(t-s)/n$. Applying the conditional-mean estimate from step 2.1 on every subinterval, then the tower property, bounds $|\mathbb E[M_t^N-M_s^N\mid\mathcal F_s]|$ by $nC_Nh^2\to0$. Thus $M^N$ is an all-pairs martingale. For fixed $N$ its paths are bounded on $[0,T]$ because the state space is finite, $H$ and its time derivative are bounded, and the compensating integrand is bounded; hence it is square-integrable. [A1, F1, F2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Set $\gamma_s(\eta)=\Gamma_{F_s^N}(\eta)$ and $A_t^N=\int_0^t\gamma_s(\eta_s)ds$. If $T=0$, both processes are zero. For $T>0$, the second-moment expansion in step 2.1 and continuity of $\gamma_s(i)$ in $s$ imply $\mathbb E[A_{s+h}^N-A_s^N\mid\mathcal F_s]=h\gamma_s(\eta_s)+O_N(h^2)$: without a state change the integrand differs from $\gamma_s(\eta_s)$ by $O_N(h)$, and a state change has probability $O_N(h)$ and changes the bounded integrand over an interval of length $h$. Hence $\mathbb E[(M_{s+h}^N-M_s^N)^2-(A_{s+h}^N-A_s^N)\mid\mathcal F_s]=O_N(h^2)$. For fixed $s<t$, partition $[s,t]$ into $n$ equal intervals and write $\Delta_jM=M_{t_{j+1}}^N-M_{t_j}^N$, $\Delta_jA=A_{t_{j+1}}^N-A_{t_j}^N$. Since $M^N$ is a martingale, $\mathbb E[M_{t_j}^N\Delta_jM\mid\mathcal F_s]=0$ by the tower property. Expanding the square, conditioning each remainder $\Delta_jM^2-\Delta_jA$ first on $\mathcal F_{t_j}$, and using the bound just proved gives an absolute conditional expectation at most $nC_Nh^2\to0$. Therefore $(M_t^N)^2-A_t^N$ is an all-pairs martingale. [A1, F1, F2, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 The integrand $\gamma_s(\eta_s)$ is nonnegative, so $A^N$ is increasing; it is continuous and adapted. If $T=0$, the zero process is predictable. Otherwise, for each nested deterministic partition, the process equal to $A^N$ at the left endpoint on each interval $(a,b]$ and zero at time zero is predictable by [F4]; these processes converge pointwise to $A^N$ by continuity. Thus $A^N$ is predictable. At a bond $\{x,x+e_i\}$, the test-function jump is $N^{-d}(\eta_{x+e_i}-\eta_x)(H(s,x/N)-H(s,(x+e_i)/N))$. Multiplying its square by the rate $N^2$ and summing over all bonds yields the displayed formula for $\gamma_s(\eta_s)$. The fundamental theorem of calculus gives $|H(s,(x+e_i)/N)-H(s,x/N)|\le N^{-1}\|\partial_iH(s,\cdot)\|_\infty$; using $(\eta_{x+e_i}-\eta_x)^2\le1$ and $N^d$ bonds per coordinate gives the stated bound for $A_T^N$. [F1, F2, F4, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
