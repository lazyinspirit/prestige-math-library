# Local mathematical prerequisite development for the QM design

This is research prose, not authored library items, a proof certification, or Step-1 evidence. Proposed IDs below are reservations for design discussion only. All assertions in this file are mathematical: no physical premise, experiment, unit identification, or Born postulate is a dependency. Work in ZFC (the published unbounded spectral calculus assumes AC); restricted finite constructions require no choice. The published library uses the pairing `(u,v)=sum u_j conjugate(v_j)`, linear in its first argument. Physics bra notation is translated by `⟨v|u⟩=(u,v)`.

## M1 — Hilbert tensor products (proposed def-qm-hilbert-tensor-product, lem-qm-hilbert-tensor-construction)

Let H,K be complex Hilbert spaces. Form the algebraic tensor product over C, the vector space generated by symbols u⊗v modulo bilinearity. On finite sums put `(sum_i u_i⊗v_i,sum_j a_j⊗b_j)=sum_ij (u_i,a_j)_H (v_i,b_j)_K`. This descends through the bilinear relations, since each relation pairs to zero with every elementary tensor. For any finite sum choose orthonormal bases e_a,f_b of the finite-dimensional spans of its factors. The tensor has coordinates c_ab and its squared length is sum_ab |c_ab|². Product coordinate functionals `(u,e_a)(v,f_b)` are bilinear, hence descend to linear functionals on the algebraic tensor product; consequently a nonzero coordinate gives a nonzero tensor and zero coordinates imply the tensor is zero. Thus the form is positive definite, independently of representation. Complete it using published `thm-completion-of-an-inner-product-space-is-hilbert`. Denote the result H⊗̂K.

If supplied orthonormal systems e_a,f_b have dense spans, the products are orthonormal and have dense span in the completion: approximate each u,v separately by finite basis sums and use `||u⊗v-u'⊗v'||≤||u-u'|| ||v||+||u'|| ||v-v'||`. Finite sums are dense by construction. Bounded S:H→H', T:K→K' extend to S⊗T with norm at most ||S||||T||. To check the estimate, expand the second factors in an orthonormal basis of their finite span: `z=sum_b u_b⊗f_b`, whence `||(S⊗I)z||²=sum_b ||Su_b||²≤||S||²||z||²`; applying the analogous estimate for I⊗T gives the assertion. Continuous extension to the completion is unique. Products, adjoints and the interchange `(S⊗I)(I⊗T)=S⊗T` follow on dense elementary tensors and therefore everywhere. This argument does not prove a rule for arbitrary unbounded tensor operators or their sums.

Dependencies: published algebraic tensor construction/product-basis theorem; finite orthonormal bases; Cauchy–Schwarz; completion and bounded extension. Local argument closes the Hilbert completion step. The L²-product-space identification additionally requires sigma-finite product measures and density of rectangle simple functions; leave its full supplier proof open rather than reuse an unqualified arbitrary-measure textbook assertion.

## M2 — Density operators and spectral probabilities (proposed def-qm-density-operator, lem-qm-density-spectral-probability)

On nonzero separable H, a density operator is a positive self-adjoint trace-class operator ρ with tr ρ=1. Published positive compact spectral and trace theorems give `ρ=sum_n w_n R_{u_n}`, `w_n≥0`, `sum w_n=1`, with orthonormal u_n for its positive eigenvalues and `R_u x=(x,u)u`. A rank-one R_u for unit u has trace 1. Every probability-weighted sum of such rank-one operators converges in trace norm: its tail trace norm is at most the tail weight, by the trace-class ideal and completeness. The limit is positive since quadratic forms converge, and its trace is 1 by trace continuity. Distinct decompositions may represent the same ρ.

For a PVM E on a measurable space define `μρ(B)=tr(ρE(B))`. This equals `sum_n w_n(E(B)u_n,u_n)=sum_n w_n||E(B)u_n||²`. Justification: multiply the trace-norm spectral expansion by the bounded projection and use trace continuity. Every term is nonnegative; total mass is 1; countable additivity follows by nonnegative double-series interchange and strong additivity of E. Hence μρ is a probability measure, without a physical interpretation being presumed. For pure ρ=R_u it is ||E(B)u||². For general self-adjoint A, define moments from this scalar law, not by automatically multiplying unbounded A with ρ. A finite first absolute moment is sufficient for the scalar mean; a finite second moment for the variance. For a pure u∈D(A), `(Au,u)=integral a dμu` and `||Au||²=integral a² dμu`. The first mean can also exist on the larger form domain D(|A|^(1/2)).

Dependencies: published PVM, unbounded spectral theorem, positive trace-class spectral/trace/ideal results, monotone convergence or Tonelli. Closed as a local mathematical argument, subject to later independent review.

## M3 — Finite partial trace and Schmidt decomposition (proposed def-qm-finite-partial-trace, lem-qm-finite-reduced-state, thm-qm-finite-schmidt)

For H=C^m, K=C^n with orthonormal bases e_i,f_j and a matrix R on H⊗K, define `(Tr_K R)_{ik}=sum_j R_{ij,kj}`. Then `tr((Tr_K R)A)=tr(R(A⊗I))` for every matrix A, by expanding finite sums. This identity uniquely determines Tr_K R, since taking matrix units extracts each entry, and proves basis independence. For positive R and x∈H, `(Tr_K R x,x)=sum_j (R(x⊗f_j),x⊗f_j)≥0`; its trace is tr R. Thus partial trace takes density matrices to density matrices. For R=R_Ψ, Ψ=sum_ij c_ij e_i⊗f_j, the reduced matrix is CC*.

Finite matrix singular-value decomposition (or diagonalize CC* and explicitly construct the partner vectors for positive eigenvalues) gives `Ψ=sum_r s_r u_r⊗v_r`, s_r>0, orthonormal factors, sum_r s_r²=||Ψ||². More explicitly, the coefficients of v_r are obtained by contracting Ψ with u_r and dividing by s_r; the eigenvector identities of CC* make their pairings δ_rs. This proves the Schmidt formula and rank-one criterion: a nonzero Ψ is a product iff C has rank one, equivalently iff exactly one Schmidt coefficient is nonzero. For unit Ψ, reduced-state purity tr ρ_H²=sum s_r⁴ is 1 iff Ψ is a product. This equivalence concerns pure bipartite states, not mixed-state separability.

Finite nonselective local maps of the form `R↦sum_a (I⊗M_a)R(I⊗M_a*)`, with sum_a M_a*M_a=I, leave Tr_K R unchanged: test against A and use finite trace cyclicity and the completeness identity. This is a mathematical no-signalling lemma for the specified instrument. Selective conditioning need not preserve the reduced state.

Dependencies: M1; finite spectral theorem; finite trace cyclicity. Infinite-dimensional partial trace, infinite Schmidt convergence and general completely positive instruments remain separate open contracts; finite conclusions cannot silently supply them.

## M4 — Finite effects and instruments (proposed def-qm-finite-effect-and-instrument, lem-qm-instrument-normalization)

For H=C^d, an effect is a positive Hermitian matrix F with 0≤F≤I. A finite POVM is a list (F_a) with sum F_a=I. Then `p_a=tr(ρF_a)≥0` and sum p_a=1, by diagonalizing ρ and summing `(F_a u_n,u_n)`. An instrument specified by matrices M_{a,r} has outcome effect F_a=sum_r M_{a,r}*M_{a,r} and unnormalized update I_a(ρ)=sum_r M_{a,r}ρM_{a,r}*. If sum_ar M_{a,r}*M_{a,r}=I, positivity and finite trace cyclicity show tr I_a(ρ)=p_a and normalized update I_a(ρ)/p_a is a density matrix when p_a>0. Effects alone do not determine an update. The single-projector case I_a(ρ)=P_aρP_a is an ideal Lüders instrument, not every apparatus.

Dependencies: finite Hermitian spectral theorem, trace cyclicity, positivity; M2 terminology. Closed finite mathematics. Complete positivity and dilation in infinite dimensions remain open if later required.

## M5 — Variance and uncertainty without illegal operator products (proposed lem-qm-weak-robertson-bound)

Let A,B be self-adjoint and u a unit vector in D(A)∩D(B). Set a=(Au,u), b=(Bu,u), x=(A-a)u, y=(B-b)u. The real means exist and ΔA=||x||, ΔB=||y||. Cauchy–Schwarz gives `ΔA ΔB≥|(x,y)|≥|Im(x,y)|`. If u additionally belongs to D(AB)∩D(BA), then `([A,B]u,u)=(Bu,Au)-(Au,Bu)=-2i Im(Au,Bu)` in the first-linear convention, so `ΔA ΔB≥|( [A,B]u,u)|/2`. Means subtract only real terms and do not change the imaginary part. Without the product-domain hypothesis state only the weak pairing inequality. In particular, a Schwartz u on R satisfies the position–momentum commutator `QP-PQ=i c I` with P=−ic∂, c>0, by the product rule, yielding ΔQΔP≥c/2. Extension to maximal domains requires a separate boundary/domain argument.

Dependencies: Cauchy–Schwarz, dense adjoint/self-adjoint definitions, weak/Schwartz differentiation. Closed conditional argument. No energy–time inequality follows by declaring time an observable.

## M6 — Free and bounded-potential Hamiltonian operators (proposed lem-qm-real-multiplier-self-adjoint, thm-qm-free-plus-bounded-potential)

For a real measurable finite-valued a on R^d, multiplication M_a on `D(M_a)={f∈L²:af∈L²}` is self-adjoint. Density follows by truncating f on {|a|≤n}. It is symmetric by integral pairing. If g∈D(M_a*), a vector h represents `(M_a f,g)=(f,h)` for all f in that domain. Test on bounded-support sets where |a| and |g| and |h| are bounded (or use all L² tests supported on {|a|≤n}); these tests show h=ag a.e. on {|a|≤n}. Their union has full measure, hence ag=h∈L² and g∈D(M_a). Conversely each such g is in the adjoint domain. Thus domains and actions agree.

Use the published unitary Fourier transform `F f(ξ)=integral exp(−2πi x·ξ)f(x)dx`. For constants c>0 and b real, set `H0=F^{-1}M_{c|ξ|²+b}F`, domain `{f:|ξ|² Ff∈L²}`. Unit conjugation preserves self-adjointness by the defining adjoint identity. The published Sobolev Fourier theorem identifies this domain as W^{2,2}(R^d) (equivalent norms, not identical norms). On Schwartz functions H0=−c/(4π²)Δ+b. For real V∈L∞, M_V is bounded symmetric with relative H0-bound zero, so published Kato–Rellich gives H=H0+M_V self-adjoint on exactly W^{2,2}. Stone supplies U(t)=exp(−itH), with differentiability exactly for initial data in D(H).

Dependencies: L² definitions, Plancherel, Fourier weak-derivative characterization, dense unbounded adjoint, Kato–Rellich, Stone. Closed for the displayed domain and bounded real potential. Coulomb V∝1/|x|, oscillator V∝|x|² and delta interaction are outside the bounded-potential proof; their model-specific self-adjointness cannot be asserted from this lemma.

## M7 — Finite CHSH and coherence accounting (proposed lem-qm-chsh-classical-bound, lem-qm-finite-fringe-fluctuation)

On any probability space let A0,A1,B0,B1 take values ±1. Pointwise `A0(B0+B1)+A1(B0−B1)` is ±2, because precisely one parenthesis is zero and the other ±2. Integrating gives `|E[A0B0]+E[A0B1]+E[A1B0]−E[A1B1]|≤2`. Applying this to an experimental model requires an explicitly common setting-independent distribution and stated factorization/local-response assumptions; the mathematical lemma contains no physics.

For N independent categorical samples with bin probabilities p_j, a specified sequence j_1,...,j_N has probability product_k p_{j_k}. If all selected probabilities are positive, the sequence has positive probability. Therefore any declared fringe-detection criterion which rejects at least one such sequence has a nonzero failure probability under that model. No universal numerical failure rate follows. For a bin indicator the published binomial mean/variance theorem gives E frequency=p, Var frequency=p(1−p)/N, and Chebyshev gives its declared finite-sample bound. Multinomial count likelihood is `N!/(prod n_j!) prod p_j^{n_j}` for nonnegative integers n_j summing to N: the number of orderings is the multinomial coefficient, each ordering has the same product probability. An inference item must state the sampling and detector model; no theorem turns this likelihood into an observed measurement.

Dependencies: published probability, independence, binomial/expectation/variance, finite combinatorics. Closed algebraic/conditional arguments; advanced finite-data Bell statistics remain open.

## M8 — Finite permutation sectors (proposed def-qm-finite-symmetric-antisymmetric-sector)

On the n-fold Hilbert tensor power of H let U_π permute factors with `U_π(u_1⊗...⊗u_n)=u_{π^{-1}(1)}⊗...⊗u_{π^{-1}(n)}`. The tensor norm construction makes each U_π unitary and U_πU_σ=U_{πσ}. Define `P_+=1/n! sum_π U_π`, `P_-=1/n! sum_π sign(π)U_π`. Group reindexing gives P_±²=P_±, inverse reindexing gives P_±*=P_±; thus they are orthogonal projections with ranges satisfying U_πu=u or sign(π)u respectively. For u⊗u, antisymmetrization in two factors is zero, as swapping leaves it unchanged. Choosing these sectors for particular species is a physical postulate, not a theorem of this construction; a spin–statistics theorem needs relativistic field hypotheses not present here.

Dependencies: M1, finite permutation group/sign and finite sums. Closed construction. General Fock space operators require separate domains.
