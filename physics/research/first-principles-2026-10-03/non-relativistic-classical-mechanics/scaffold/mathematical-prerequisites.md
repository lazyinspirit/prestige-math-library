## M1 — Affine time, Euclidean slices, curves and Galilean transformations

Let V be a real four-dimensional vector space, let τ:V→R be a surjective linear map, and let g be a positive definite inner product on K=ker τ. Let A be an affine space modeled on V: a free transitive V-action lets one subtract any two points of A to obtain a unique vector. Choose an origin o∈A, u∈V with τ(u)=1, and an oriented g-orthonormal basis e1,e2,e3 of K. Each event uniquely equals o+tu+Σxᵢeᵢ. The resulting bijection A→R×R³ is an adapted inertial chart. A simultaneous slice τ(a−b)=0 inherits g-distance; there is no g-norm of a general temporal displacement.

For an interval I⊂R, a time-parametrized C² curve is γ:I→A with τ(γ(t)−o)=t. Its derivative lies in τ⁻¹(1) and its second derivative lies in K. Relative to a chart, γ(t)=(t,x(t)), velocity is x′, acceleration x″. A second adapted chart differs by t′=t+b, x′=Rx+vt+a, where R∈SO(3)={R∈R³ˣ³:RᵀR=I,det R=1}, a,v∈R³ and b∈R. Different orientation conventions may enlarge SO(3) to O(3), but oriented angular momentum then transforms axially. Differentiation gives dx′/dt′=R dx/dt+v and d²x′/dt′²=R d²x/dt². Thus zero acceleration is preserved. This proves a kinematic transformation statement, not existence of inertial frames in nature. The ten parameters are dimensions of a Lie group, not ten independent linear maps.

An arbitrary moving chart is x=R(t)y+c(t), R(t)∈SO(3), R,c∈C². Differentiate RᵀR=I: Ω=RᵀR′ is skew. Every skew 3×3 matrix has unique ω with Ωz=ω×z (read off its three off-diagonal entries). Therefore Rᵀx″=y″+2ω×y′+ω′×y+ω×(ω×y)+Rᵀc″. These are purely coordinate identities. Rigid motion preserves distances since |Rz|=|z|.

## M2 — Mass-metric splitting and finite balance identities

For positive numbers mᵢ and twice differentiable xᵢ:I→R³, put M=Σmᵢ, X=M⁻¹Σmᵢxᵢ, ρᵢ=xᵢ−X. Then Σmᵢρᵢ=Σmᵢρᵢ′=0. Expanding squares gives Σmᵢ|xᵢ′|²=M|X′|²+Σmᵢ|ρᵢ′|², as the cross term is 2X′·Σmᵢρᵢ′=0. For N=2, r=x1−x2 and μ=m1m2/M give Σmᵢ|ρᵢ′|²=μ|r′|² by ρ1=m2r/M, ρ2=−m1r/M.

If differentiable pᵢ=mᵢxᵢ′ satisfy pᵢ′=fᵢ+Σj≠i fᵢⱼ with fᵢⱼ=−fⱼᵢ, finite pair summation gives P′=Σfᵢ, P=Σpᵢ. For J=Σxᵢ×pᵢ, the term Σxᵢ′×pᵢ vanishes and J′=Σxᵢ×fᵢ+Σi<j(xᵢ−xⱼ)×fᵢⱼ. Pairwise centrality makes the latter zero; it is sufficient, not necessary (different pair torques can cancel). Finally for C¹ U(t,x) and f=−∇xU, K=Σmᵢ|xᵢ′|²/2 satisfies (K+U)′=∂tU by chain rule. An additional force d gives (K+U)′=∂tU+Σdᵢ·xᵢ′. These statements hold for curves satisfying the stated identities; they do not assert that forces of nature have this form.

## M3 — Finite-interval first variation with time dependence

Let U⊂Rⁿ be open, a<b, ℒ∈C²([a,b]×U×Rⁿ), where C² means an extension to an open neighborhood, and q∈C²([a,b],U). For η∈C² with η(a)=η(b)=0, compactness of q([a,b]) permits qε=q+εη to remain in U for small ε. Define S(q)=∫aᵇℒ(t,q,q′)dt. The compact-rectangle differentiation theorem gives δS(q)[η]=∫(ℒq·η+ℒv·η′)dt: the parameter derivative is continuous on a compact ε,t rectangle. Integration by parts gives δS=∫(ℒq−dℒv/dt)·η dt. If a continuous component Eᵢ of this coefficient is nonzero at t0∈(a,b), it has strict sign on a smaller interval. A nonnegative C∞ bump ηᵢ supported there with positive integral, other components zero, makes δS nonzero. Such a bump can be explicitly constructed from exp(−1/(1−s²)) for |s|<1 and zero otherwise; repeated differentiation has polynomial/rational factors dominated by the exponential at the endpoints. Hence stationarity for all η implies E=0; E=0 implies stationarity. This establishes the time-dependent chart version. Stationary does not mean minimum.

For a smooth manifold Q, the result holds chartwise for compactly supported variations; the global autonomous published theorem is separately mapped. Global assembly of arbitrary variation fields and C² coordinate invariance must use the published tangent bundle/chart interfaces rather than an unspoken embedding.

## M4 — Regular, hyperregular, and mechanical Legendre maps

Define p=ℒv(t,q,v). At fixed t the derivative of (q,v)↦(q,p) has block matrix [I 0; ℒvq ℒvv]. Its determinant is det ℒvv, so a nonsingular velocity Hessian gives a local C¹ inverse by the exact Euclidean IFT. Regular means nonsingular at every point; hyperregular means a global diffeomorphism of each tangent fibre and bundle, with inverse smooth jointly in t. These are different: ℒ(v)=eᵛ has ℒvv>0 but p=eᵛ misses p≤0. Singular ℒ=0 has no inverse.

On any inverse branch v(t,q,p), H=p·v−ℒ gives dH=−ℒt dt−ℒq·dq+v·dp because the dv terms cancel. Thus Hpi=vᵢ, Hqi=−ℒqi, Ht=−ℒt. The equations q′=Hp,p′=−Hq are equivalent on that branch to dℒv/dt=ℒq; global equivalence requires hyperregularity. For ℒ=½vᵀG(t,q)v+A(t,q)·v−U(t,q), with smooth symmetric positive definite G, p=Gv+A and inverse v=G⁻¹(p−A) exist globally in each fibre, and H=½(p−A)ᵀG⁻¹(p−A)+U. Smoothness of G⁻¹ follows from cofactors/det G≠0. A is a mathematical covector field here, not an electromagnetic potential. Generalized momentum is not necessarily mass times coordinate velocity. Along an Euler–Lagrange solution E=v·ℒv−ℒ satisfies E′=−ℒt by differentiation and cancellation. Autonomous ℒ gives conserved E.

## M5 — Point symmetry and boundary-term Noether identity

Let ξ(q) be a C¹ vector field on U and B(t,q) a C¹ function. Suppose ℒq·ξ+ℒv·Dξ(q)v=Bt+Bq·v identically. This is the infinitesimal condition that a time-independent coordinate symmetry changes ℒ by a total time derivative. Along a C² Euler–Lagrange curve, define C=ℒv·ξ−B. Direct differentiation gives C′=(dℒv/dt)·ξ+ℒv·Dξ q′−Bt−Bq·q′=0. Thus C is conserved. The condition is an explicit equation; a symmetry of kinematic spacetime does not guarantee a symmetry of a chosen potential. Time translation is separately handled by M4; general transformations of both t and q require a stronger Noether contract below. Adding dF(t,q)/dt to ℒ changes S by F(b,q(b))−F(a,q(a)), hence fixed-endpoint stationarity is unchanged. Multiplying ℒ by a nonzero constant preserves its stationary curves; zero does not.

## M6 — Regular holonomic constraints and ideal reactions

A functional annihilating the kernel of a surjection is a unique transpose multiple; therefore Mq″=f+DGᵀλ. Differentiating G(q)=0 twice gives DG q″+D²G[q′,q′]=0. Substitution gives (DG M⁻¹ DGᵀ)λ=−DG M⁻¹ f−D²G[q′,q′]. This k×k matrix is positive definite: for z≠0, DGᵀz≠0 by rank, so zᵀDG M⁻¹ DGᵀz>0. ## M7 — Quadratic modes and energy stability

For symmetric positive definite M and symmetric K≥0, use the reviewed finite real spectral theorem to diagonalize M, construct M¹ᐟ² by positive square roots of its eigenvalues, and diagonalize S=M⁻¹ᐟ²KM⁻¹ᐟ² with orthogonal O. Set q=M⁻¹ᐟ²Oz; then Mq″+Kq=0 becomes zᵢ″+λᵢzᵢ=0 with λᵢ≥0. For λᵢ>0, zᵢ=aᵢcos(√λᵢt)+bᵢsin(√λᵢt); for λᵢ=0, zᵢ=aᵢ+bᵢt. Verification by differentiation and uniqueness from Picard–Lindelöf proves these are all solutions. Negative λ would give real exponential growth; a zero mode is not an oscillator. Small-oscillation linearization about an equilibrium uses Taylor remainder and is an approximation; this exact quadratic calculation alone gives no long-time error bound.

For a smooth autonomous mechanical energy E(q,v)=½vᵀM(q)v+U(q), suppose q0 is a strict isolated local minimum of U and M positive definite near q0. Then (q0,0) is Lyapunov stable: in a sufficiently small closed coordinate ball in (q,v), E−E0>0 on its boundary, compactness gives a positive minimum δ, and initial data sufficiently near the equilibrium have E−E0<δ by continuity. Energy conservation forbids a first crossing of the boundary. For each smaller prescribed neighborhood use its boundary similarly. The trajectory is defined for all forward times while trapped in a compact set inside the ODE domain by the continuation lemma M10. This is stability, not asymptotic attraction; exact energy conservation prevents convergence from higher-energy data. A degenerate strict minimum is allowed. A saddle Hessian suggests linear instability but requires a nonlinear theorem before a global instability claim.

## M8 — Rigid-body tensor and rotating coordinates

Let ν be a finite nonnegative measure on a bounded Borel subset of R³ with finite second moment, total mass m>0, and ∫ρdν=0. A rigid placement is x(t,ρ)=c(t)+R(t)ρ with C² c,R and R∈SO(3). Expanding ∫|c′+R′ρ|²/2 dν and using zero first moment gives K=½m|c′|²+½ωᵀIω with R′=RΩ, Ωz=ω×z. The angular momentum relative to c is R Iω because ρ×(ω×ρ)=(|ρ|²Id−ρρᵀ)ω, a coordinate expansion identity. Differentiating RIω gives body equation Iω′+ω×Iω=τbody whenever spatial angular-momentum rate equals the stated torque. Spectral diagonalization supplies principal axes. ## M9 — Central-force reduction away from collisions

## M10 — Compact continuation of a locally Lipschitz ODE

Let D⊂R×Rⁿ be open and F continuous, locally state-Lipschitz as in the published definition. A solution x:[t0,b)→Rⁿ, b<∞, whose graph near b lies in a compact K⊂D extends past b. F is bounded on K, so |x(t)−x(s)|≤M|t−s| by the integral equation. Completeness of Rⁿ gives x(t)→xb as t↑b. Compactness implies (b,xb)∈K⊂D. Picard–Lindelöf at (b,xb) supplies a new solution. The old integral equation extends continuously to b, and uniqueness joins the two solutions. Consequently finite-time breakdown requires escape from every compact subset of D; merely saying “force is finite” at each point is insufficient. Autonomous solutions trapped in a compact subset of their state domain have no finite forward endpoint.
