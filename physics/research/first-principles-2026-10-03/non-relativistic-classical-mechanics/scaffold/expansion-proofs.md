## D1 — Explicit stability, dynamics and continuum definitions

For F:D⊂Rⁿ→Rⁿ locally Lipschitz, an equilibrium z* satisfies F(z*)=0. It is Lyapunov stable if for each ε>0 sufficiently small there is δ>0 such that every solution with |z(0)−z*|<δ exists for all t≥0 and |z(t)−z*|<ε for every t≥0. Asymptotic stability additionally requires convergence to z* for all data in some neighborhood. Orbital stability of an invariant set S uses dist(z(t),S) instead of |z(t)−z*|, with a stated norm and a compact neighborhood for continuation. Linear stability means bounded evolution of every solution of y'=DF(z*)y for t≥0. These distinct definitions are the ones used in M7/E9/E16. A hyperbolic equilibrium has no spectrum on the imaginary axis; a hyperbolic fixed point of a diffeomorphism has no derivative spectrum on the unit circle. A limit cycle is an isolated periodic orbit, with attraction a separate property. A stroboscopic map for a P-periodic vector field is ΦP,0 on a domain where the full period exists; its iterates require corresponding forward existence. A homoclinic trajectory tends to the same equilibrium/periodic orbit at both time infinities. Calling stable and unstable sets differentiable manifolds requires a stable-manifold theorem; E12 does not assert it.

For a constrained configuration Q of dimension n, the mass metric is a smooth positive definite symmetric bilinear form Gq on TqQ; in a particle embedding X its matrix is Xqᵀ M Xq, positive definite precisely when Xq is injective. Its quadratic kinetic energy is Gq(v,v)/2. A material region is a bounded open B⊂R³ with declared C¹ boundary; a motion χ and its current field domain are E13's diffeomorphic family. Density is mass per volume, defined here as a nonnegative integrable function ρ whose integral is mass; positivity/smoothness are separately assumed in E13. Traction t(n) is force per area on an oriented surface with unit normal n; σ is the matrix giving t(n)=σn after E13's conditional contact argument. Stress positivity is not asserted: −p Id is compressive when p>0. Force per volume ρb differs from b, which is acceleration. Elasticity/fluid laws are explicit constitutive postulates in E14, not mathematics or empirical reports. The reduced potential V_eff in a regular heavy-top chart is E9's displayed function, with a,b,C,A fixed and sinθ≠0.

## E1 — Basic geometry, curves, differential forms and local primitives

A countable base for Q and finitely many Euclidean rational boxes over each chart gives second countability of its bundles (use countable choice to choose the countable chart refinement when required). This fills the curve-identification use absent from the former audit.

SO(3) is the subset of 3×3 real matrices R with RᵀR=Id and det R=1. The map R↦RᵀR takes values in symmetric matrices; at an orthogonal R its derivative H↦RᵀH+HᵀR is surjective, since H=RS/2 maps to any symmetric S. ## E2 — Quantified ODE tools

On a closed cylinder inside an open time-state domain let |F|≤B and |F(t,x)−F(t,y)|≤L|x−y|. ## E3 — Oscillators, forcing and elementary examples

For m>0,c≥0,k>0 the equation m x''+c x'+kx=0 has characteristic roots λ±=(−c±√(c²−4mk))/(2m). For distinct real roots its solution is Ae^{λ+t}+Be^{λ−t}; for a double root λ the solution is (A+Bt)e^{λt}; for complex roots −γ±iω it is e^{−γt}(A cos ωt+B sin ωt), γ=c/(2m), ω=√(k/m−γ²). Differentiation verifies every formula, and the two initial-value vectors are independent, giving all initial data; uniqueness E2 proves exhaustiveness. The k=0 case instead gives x=A+Be^{−ct/m} if c>0 and x=A+Bt if c=0. If k<0 one root is positive, an explicit unstable model. Phase space is R² with coordinates (x,x'); the phase curve solves (x',v')=(v,−(cv+kx)/m). Equilibrium is a zero of this vector field.

For forcing f(t), let g solve the homogeneous equation with g(0)=0,g'(0)=1/m. Then x=x_h+∫₀ᵗg(t−s)f(s)ds solves the forced equation: two differentiations produce f(t)/m and the homogeneous kernel terms cancel. This applies to continuous f on compact intervals, including each decoupled mode of M7. For f=f₀ cos Ωt, the complex particular amplitude is f₀/(k−mΩ²+icΩ). If the denominator is nonzero its modulus is |f₀|/√((k−mΩ²)²+c²Ω²); the real part supplies the phase. For c>0 all homogeneous modes decay when k>0, so the response is asymptotically periodic and bounded. The maximum amplitude occurs at Ω²=k/m−c²/(2m²) when this is positive, otherwise at Ω=0; differentiating the squared denominator proves this. For c=0 and Ω=√(k/m)>0 the particular solution is f₀t sin Ωt/(2mΩ), which is unbounded for f₀≠0. No finite resonant amplitude is asserted.

A uniform-force model m x''=mg gives x=x₀+v₀t+gt²/2 by integrating twice. Linear drag m x''=mg−c x' with c>0 gives v=mg/c+(v₀−mg/c)e^{−ct/m}, and x=x₀+(mg/c)t+(m/c)(v₀−mg/c)(1−e^{−ct/m}); these are adopted models, not measured laws. A continuous non-Lipschitz equation x'=√|x| with x(0)=0 has x=0 until any a≥0 and x=(t−a)²/4 after a, verifying nonuniqueness. ## E5 — Nonholonomic distinction with an actual counterexample

In R³ with coordinates (x,y,z), impose z'=x y'. Its allowed virtual vectors obey δz=xδy. The vector fields A=∂x and B=∂y+x∂z span this distribution. If it were tangent to two-dimensional regular level surfaces, brackets of tangent fields would also be tangent: for a defining function G, AG=BG=0 implies [A,B]G=0 by direct subtraction. But [A,B]=∂z is not in their span (its y and x components force both coefficients zero). Hence it is nonintegrable, without invoking an unproved Frobenius converse.

For L=(x'²+y'²+z'²)/2, d'Alembert gives x''=0,y''=−λx,z''=λ and differentiated constraint λ=x'y'/(1+x²). Vakonomic stationary action for L+η(z'−xy') instead gives x''=−η y', y''=η' x+η x', z''=−η' with the same velocity constraint. A d'Alembert trajectory from (x,y,z)=(0,0,0) and (x',y',z')=(1,1,0) has x=t and y'=1/√(1+t²)>0, because y''=−t y'/(1+t²), with z'=t y'. If it were also vakonomic, x''=0 forces η=0 throughout that interval, which would give y''=0, contrary to the explicit y'' for t>0. Thus the laws differ even with the same constraint and initial data. Rolling/contact mode changes still require distinct contact models and are not proved by this example.

## E7 — Symplectic calculus, canonical maps and Hamilton–Jacobi

On coordinates z=(q,p) in R²ⁿ, λ=Σpᵢdqᵢ and ω=−dλ=Σdqᵢ∧dpᵢ. Contraction with X=(a,b) is Σ(aᵢdpᵢ−bᵢdqᵢ); it is zero only for a=b=0. Thus ω is closed and nondegenerate, and ιXHω=dH gives XH=(Hp,−Hq). Set J=[0 Id;−Id 0], so XH=J∇H, and {f,g}=∇fᵀJ∇g. Bilinearity/antisymmetry follow from Jᵀ=−J; the product rule gives the derivation rule. For Jacobi, expand {f,{g,h}} as ΣJij Jkl f_i(g_kj h_l+g_k h_lj). In the cyclic sum every term containing a Hessian, e.g. g_kj, has an opposite term from another cyclic summand after swapping its symmetric indices and using antisymmetry of J. They cancel in pairs. For C³ functions this proves Jacobi with no missing geometric supplier.

For H C², the flow derivative Y obeys E2 with A=J Hess H. Since AᵀJ+JA=0, (YᵀJY)'=0 and YᵀJY=J. Thus the local flow is symplectic. ## E9 — Rigid nonlinear spin stability and heavy-top reduction

Write body angular momentum l=Iω with distinct positive principal moments I₁<I₂<I₃. Torque-free Euler gives l'=l×I⁻¹l. Thus C=|l|² and E=Σlᵢ²/(2Iᵢ) are constant by dot products. For rotation l₀=L e₁, L≠0, D=C/(2I₁)−E=Σi>1(1/I₁−1/Iᵢ)lᵢ²/2≥c(l₂²+l₃²), c>0. Small initial deviations make D and C−L² small; invariance bounds transverse components, and l₁²=C−l₂²−l₃² stays close to L². It cannot change sign while bounded away from zero, so l stays close to l₀ for every forward time, by compact continuation on its constant-C sphere. The same proof for e₃ uses E−C/(2I₃). This is Lyapunov stability in body momentum, corresponding to orientation stability modulo the spin phase, not closeness of absolute orientations forever.

Intermediate rotation l₀=L e₂ is unstable without invoking a general nonlinear instability theorem. On the invariant levels C=L²,E=L²/(2I₂), solve (1/I₁−1/I₂)l₁²=(1/I₂−1/I₃)l₃², so l₃=a l₁ with a>0. It therefore escapes a fixed neighborhood of l₀. Distinctness is essential; repeated moments have a different equilibrium set.

Linearizing Iω'=−ω×Iω at spin ω=Ωe₃ gives δ₁'=Ω(I₂−I₃)δ₂/I₁, δ₂'=Ω(I₃−I₁)δ₁/I₂ and δ₃'=0. Thus δ₁''=Ω²(I₂−I₃)(I₃−I₁)δ₁/(I₁I₂). Cyclic permutation gives the other axes. ## E10 — Central scattering and repulsive Coulomb law

Let U∈C²((0,∞)) vanish for r≥R, fix E>0 and j>0, and define V=U+j²/(2μr²). Suppose a simple outer turning point r*>0 satisfies V(r*)=E,V'(r*)<0 and V(r)<E for every r>r*. Radial energy gives r'=±√(2(E−V)/μ). Its time integral is finite at r* because E−V is comparable to r−r*; acceleration at r* is −V'/μ>0, so the unique smooth orbit turns there. The integral to infinity diverges (speed tends to √(2E/μ)), hence the incoming and outgoing branches exist for all infinite times, and beyond R motion is exactly free. Asymptotic velocity vectors v± therefore exist, |v±|=√(2E/μ). The impact parameter is the perpendicular distance of the incoming free line from the force centre; j=μ b|v−|. Polar angle advances by

Δ=∫r*^∞ j dr/[r²√(2μ(E−V(r)))].
