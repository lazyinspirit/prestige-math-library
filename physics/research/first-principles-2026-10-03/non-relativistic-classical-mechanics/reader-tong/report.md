## 1. Adopted foundational framework and explicit objects

A suitable starting formulation is finite-dimensional, nonrelativistic Newtonian point-particle mechanics on Galilean spacetime. Define an affine four-dimensional spacetime E with translation vector space W, a nonzero absolute-time covector τ:W→R, and a positive definite Euclidean inner product h on the three-dimensional spatial subspace ker τ. Fix time units and an orientation on ker τ. An inertial coordinate frame is the affine chart determined by a spacetime event o, a constant vector u∈W with τ(u)=1, and a positively oriented h-orthonormal basis (e1,e2,e3) of ker τ: an event has unique coordinates o+t u+Σxᵃeₐ. These charts are related by constant spatial rotations, space/time translations and constant boosts. This is an explicit geometric frame, not just a set of informal observer words. Tong gives the operational inertial straight-line criterion and Galilean transformations at §1.2 p3 eqs(1.2)–(1.4), but not this affine construction.

A particle worldline is γ:I→E, γ(t)=o+t u+Σrᵃ(t)eₐ for an interval I⊂R, with r∈C²(I,R³), so τ(γdot)=1. No Lorentzian causal condition is assumed. Velocity/acceleration are rdot/rddot in this frame. For N labelled particles with fixed masses mᵢ>0, configuration space Q is an open submanifold of (R³)^N, excluding collision/source singularities when required; configuration trajectory q:I→Q is C². Positions alone do not specify a state: instantaneous Newtonian state is (q,v)∈TQ. For smooth holonomic constraints of full rank, replace Q locally by the constrained submanifold; time-dependent constraints require a configuration family over time, not a single fixed spatial subset.

Newtonian primitives adopted here: spacetime's absolute time/spatial metric/inertial class, particle labels, positive constant inertial masses, applied force laws Fᵢ:I×TQ→R³, and mᵢrddotᵢ=Fᵢ in inertial charts. Force laws and model constants (spring stiffness, gravitational coupling, friction coefficient, prescribed fields) remain adopted physical inputs; second law does not derive them. Free motion/inertial existence is a postulate. Pair reciprocity Fᵢⱼ=−Fⱼᵢ and centrality Fᵢⱼ∥(rᵢ−rⱼ) are additional assumptions for the corresponding closed particle model, not consequences of second law and not universal mechanical laws for subsystems coupled to fields. Tong explicitly contrasts weak and strong third law at §1.3 pp6–8 and points to field momentum.

SI conventions recommended: spatial coordinates m, time s, mass kg; velocity m s⁻¹; acceleration m s⁻²; force N=kg m s⁻². Angles are dimensionless radians; distinguish angular rates rad s⁻¹ from cycle frequency Hz, with ω=2πf. Tong sometimes suppresses 2π in astronomical examples and switches EM conventions; do not inherit that ambiguity.

Under r′=O r+c+b t, v′=O v+b and a′=O a (time translated independently), p′=O p+m b; kinetic energy changes accordingly. Potential transformation and rotational invariance are properties of the force model, not merely the existence of a coordinate rotation. Total symmetry statements must specify whether fields, boundaries and apparatus transform with the system. Angular momentum as an axial vector uses the chosen orientation; proper SO(3) convention avoids reflection ambiguity.

Prescribed electromagnetic potentials are a coupling model, not derived from mechanics: smooth φ:Ω×I→R and A:Ω×I→R³ give E=−∇φ−∂tA, B=∇×A; L=m|v|²/2+e A·v−eφ yields Lorentz force. ## 3. Exact equivalence and theorem obligations

**Newtonian ↔ Lagrangian.** For constant positive masses, q∈Q⊂R^(3N), V∈C²(Q×I), and forces −∂qV, L=Σm|v|²/2−V gives Euler–Lagrange equations precisely m qddot=−∂qV. Conservation of energy additionally needs ∂tV=0. Not all force laws have this form; Lorentz coupling is velocity dependent but variational, while dissipative generalized forces ordinarily require an explicit Lagrange–d'Alembert forcing term. This equivalence does not cover arbitrary friction or open variable-mass systems. Tong §§1.2–2.1 pp2–12, §2.5.7 pp36–37.

**Stationary-action theorem.** On fixed [a,b], L∈C²(TQ×[a,b]), q∈C², choose C¹ variations qε with the same q(a),q(b) and admissible tangent variation η vanishing at endpoints. Differentiation under the finite integral, integration by parts and the fundamental lemma give DS[q](η)=0 for every η iff Euler–Lagrange residual is zero. Work in charts or a suitable manifold path setup. This is stationarity, not existence, uniqueness, global minimization, or a boundary-value solution for arbitrary endpoints. Tangency is needed for constrained variations. Tong §2.1 pp10–12 eqs(2.3)–(2.8), with the saddle caveat p12.

**Coordinate covariance.** A C² time-dependent local diffeomorphism x=x(q,t) transforms velocity by xdot=Dqx qdot+∂tx. The transformed L is its tangent/time pullback; Euler–Lagrange residual transforms by Jacobian. Nonzero Jacobian is local invertibility, not a global chart. Rotating-frame inertial terms arise from this pullback, not an assertion that inertial Cartesian F=m a keeps its form without them. Tong §2.2 pp13–17 eqs(2.10)–(2.26). Hyperbolic coordinates and Euler/spherical angles have singular points and do not globally parameterize configuration spaces.

**Holonomic constraints.** f:U×I→Rᵏ must be C² with rank Dxf=k along the allowed locus. Local parametrization x=X(q,t) exists with correct time dependence. Reaction forces must annihilate admissible instantaneous virtual displacement: R·DqX η=0 for all η, equivalently reactions lie in span{dxfα} under rank hypothesis. Then reduced variational equations match constrained Newtonian equations; multipliers recover reactions. Merely imposing f=0 does not imply ideal reactions or frictionless motion. If f depends on time, reactions can do actual work despite zero virtual work. Multiplier L has singular velocity Hessian; do not directly apply unrestricted Legendre inversion to it. Nonholonomic rolling requires separately specified admissible velocity distribution and d'Alembert rule; substituting nonintegrable velocity constraints into action gives a different variational problem. Tong §2.3 pp17–22, especially eqs(2.29)–(2.38).

**Lagrangian ↔ Hamiltonian.** Define fibre derivative FL:TQ×I→T*Q×I, (q,v,t)↦(q,∂vL,t). Invertible velocity Hessian ∂²vL gives a local inverse; a global diffeomorphism (hyperregularity) is needed for globally equivalent Hamiltonian dynamics on all T*Q. Natural positive definite mass metrics give fibrewise invertibility. Define ω=Σdqᵢ∧dpᵢ and i_XHω=dH, giving qdot=∂pH, pdot=−∂qH; phase trajectory z:I→T*Q is C¹. Poisson bracket convention {f,g}=Σ(∂qf∂pg−∂pf∂qg); fdot={f,H}+∂tf. A Hamiltonian-first model adopts (phase manifold, symplectic form,H) and its evolution postulate; it need not have an everywhere invertible Lagrangian representation. Tong §§4.1 pp80–88,4.3 pp93–95.

Hamilton phase action ∫(p·qdot−H)dt treats q and p as independent off shell, qdot as path derivative; only q endpoint variations must vanish in the standard proof, p endpoints are free. Adding dF(q,p,t)/dt preserves the same variational problem only with compatible fixed boundary data. Tong §4.1.5 pp87–88 is explicit about this boundary distinction.

**Local ODE existence and uniqueness.** Continuous time dependence and local Lipschitz state dependence of the first-order vector field give local well-posedness. Smooth forces/H guarantee it on regular domains; extend maximally until leaving domain, singularity or escape. Finite force at each point alone gives neither uniqueness nor all-time continuation. Time-dependent flows are two-time evolution maps Φ(t,s), so a point in unextended phase space at unspecified time does not determine future evolution; projected curves from different initial times can cross. Tong pp2 and81 overstate these points.

**Noether.** For a C² L and smooth one-parameter point-transformation family preserving L at fixed time, along Euler–Lagrange solutions J=pᵢξᵢ is constant; ξ=∂sQ|s=0. Allowing δL=dB/dt gives J=p·ξ−B. For time transformations use the extended charge p·ξ−Hτ−B. Symmetry must hold on the relevant configurations/velocities, not just one trajectory. Translation/rotation/time invariance require corresponding model invariance. A fixed-time canonical symmetry generated by time-independent G gives G conserved iff {G,H}=0; time-dependent G needs ∂tG as well. Tong §2.4 pp23–26 proves strict fixed-time version; §4.4.2 p104 gives autonomous generator version. Do not encode its rhetorical 'all conservation laws' as an unrestricted proved converse.

**Liouville volume.** For H∈C² and the smooth Hamiltonian flow defined over the interval, divergence XH=0; Jacobian determinant satisfies Jacobi's determinant ODE and remains 1. This supplies the finite-time step missing from the infinitesimal argument §§4.2 pp88–91. General symplectic volume ωⁿ/n! is invariant even for explicitly time-dependent H. Density transport needs a nonnegative integrable density and the appropriate smooth or weak transport assumptions; normalize probability to 1 or number density to N, not both.

**Recurrence.** An invertible measure-preserving map on an invariant finite-measure space, with positive-measure D, gives overlap of some forward iterates, hence a returning point. Standard almost-everywhere recurrent-point version needs a separate proof; Tong §4.2.3 pp92–93 only proves its weaker neighborhood statement. Positivity of kinetic/potential energies alone does not make accessible volume finite; free unconfined coordinates already defeat it. The theorem does not certify a particular gas microstate, give practical return times, or refute a statistical second law.

**Integrability/action-angle.** The locally applicable Liouville–Arnold theorem needs n functionally independent C² commuting integrals on a 2n-dimensional symplectic manifold, a regular common level set, compact connected level component, and suitable neighborhood conditions; it yields an invariant n-torus and local action-angle coordinates around that torus. Singular levels/separatrices, noncompact components and global fibration monodromy need separate treatment. Tong §4.5.2 pp107–108 cites Arnold without proving it and omits these hypotheses. One-degree autonomous regular bound motion admits action I=(2π)⁻¹∮p dq and dI/dE=T/(2π); simple turning points and finite period are essential, not mere boundedness. Endpoint differentiation at §4.5.3 pp109–110 is intuitive; at simple turning points endpoint slivers scale like |δE|^(3/2), not generally O(δE²). The first derivative result survives appropriate justification.

**Adiabatic invariance.** Define a small parameter, e.g. λ(t)=Λ(εt), uniform regular periodic orbits and periods bounded away from infinity, sufficient smoothness and a specified time interval/error target; exclude separatrix crossings. Tong §4.6 pp113–118 argues by cycle averaging and explicitly admits informality in footnote6 p115. It does not supply a uniform error bound for all slow drives or arbitrary long times. Hannay angle §§4.6.3 pp118–121 requires smooth parameter-dependent regular cycles, consistent averaging, local angle gauges and a closed parameter loop; delta-function manipulations are formal and need a distributional/smooth-family supplier.

**Hamilton–Jacobi.** A C² local S(q,t) solving ∂tS+H(q,dqS,t)=0 yields trajectories on its Lagrangian graph through qdot=∂pH; differentiation gives pdot=−∂qH. A classical endpoint action as a single smooth function requires a selected locally smooth family of extremals, absence of relevant caustics and a nonsingular endpoint map; multiple extremals make branches, not a unique universal action. A complete integral/canonical transformation additionally needs a nondegenerate mixed Hessian. Tong §4.7 pp121–126 supplies calculations but not these global/regularity conditions.

## 4. Rigid bodies, deformable bodies, continuum and omitted scope

For a nondegenerate labelled rigid body with fixed material coordinates aᵢ and mass data, configuration xᵢ=c+A aᵢ with c∈R³ and A∈SO(3) gives Q≅SE(3) (as a manifold R³×SO(3)), six degrees of freedom; a body fixed at a point has Q≅SO(3). Collinear bodies or indistinguishable symmetries have stabilizers and require quotient/reduced configurations. Adopt C² c(t),A(t); choose the orientation and basis/coordinate convention explicitly. Tong §3.1 pp46–50 uses basis rows ea=Rab ẽb and body skew ω=Rdot R⁻¹, so don't transpose into the active-vector convention without explanation. The usual active rotation matrix obeys its corresponding spatial/body angular-velocity conventions. Euler's equations Iωdot+ω×(Iω)=N in body principal components follow from torque balance with the rotating-basis term; not by pretending body components are inertial components. §§3.2–3.3 pp50–55.

For continuum rigid mass distributions define a nonnegative finite mass measure μ with finite second moments on material coordinates. M=∫dμ; inertia ∫(|a|²Id−a⊗a)dμ. Density ρ requires dμ=ρ d³a, ρ kg m⁻³; rods/discs use line/surface measures and kg m⁻¹/kg m⁻², not volume density. This precisely supports Tong's replacements of sums by density integrals (§3 pp46,51–53), without claiming a PDE continuum theory.

Free-top stability §§3.4.3 pp57–58 is linearized at a nonzero principal-axis spin, with distinct positive moments. Extreme-axis stability needs a nonlinear energy-momentum argument if a nonlinear Lyapunov claim is desired; intermediate-axis exponential growth gives a linear instability. Poinsot construction §§3.4.4 pp58–61 presumes nondegenerate invariants; separatrix/equilibrium exceptional curves are not periodic. Heavy symmetric top §§3.6 pp67–74 is fixed-pivot, axisymmetric inertia, uniform external gravity, frictionless rigid idealization; fixed spin/conserved cyclic momenta determine reduced motion. Sleeping-top critical equality and nonlinear perturbation claims require separate handling. Dissipation invalidates free Euler invariants, and real Earth/Toutatis anecdotes are not exact rigid realizations.

Deformable reorientation §§3.7 pp74–79 factors translations and uses shape quotient Q/SO(3), local orientation representatives and zero angular momentum. A smooth quotient/local section requires a free proper group action on a regular region; symmetric configurations have stabilizers. Instantaneous inertia must be invertible. The reconstructed connection/closed-loop holonomy explains reorientation without external torque. Connection matrices depend on representative choice; loop matrices transform by conjugation and physically interpreted orientation includes the representative change. Arbitrary global section is not established. The small-loop curvature expansion is asserted after algebra, not proved in full detail. Infinite-dimensional body spaces are mentioned without topology, differential calculus, constitutive law or PDE well-posedness.

The point-vortex model §§4.3.3 pp98–100 is a special finite-dimensional Hamiltonian reduction on distinct planar positions, with nonzero strengths γᵢ, Poisson factors 1/γᵢ and adopted first-order dynamics. It is not a derivation of Euler/Navier–Stokes. A logarithm needs a reference length when dimensions are retained, and circulation/prefactor conventions need fresh definition. Numerical/chaos remarks do not imply every four-vortex initial condition is chaotic. Tong does not develop elasticity, fluid continuum balance laws, stress/strain, nonholonomic mechanics, collision/impact/contact laws, open systems, Dirac constrained Hamiltonian mechanics, rigorous nonlinear chaos or quantitative statistical inference.

## 5. Source errors, informal steps and evidence limitations

This is an authoritative teaching source, not a uniformly theorem-complete or typographically flawless text. Do not stamp all its statements as audited mathematics. Specific directly read issues worth isolating:

- §1.2 p2: general ODEs supposedly determine r(t) 'for all t' while F is finite; local Lipschitz and continuation conditions are missing. The variable-mass rocket aside confuses closed momentum balance with open-system momentum flux.
- §1.3.1 p7 eq(1.19): each central pair is sufficient for summed internal torque cancellation; cancellation of the sum does not imply every pair is central. The preceding displayed sum eq(1.18) also changes Fij to Fji without matching the earlier index convention.
- §2.1 p11: a unique 'true path' for fixed endpoints is unsupported; stationarity is shown. §2.3 p18 eq(2.30) suppresses t in a discussion admitting time-dependent constraints; eq(2.35) p19 visibly misses m before yddot. §2.3.3 p22 permits αL for α∈R, but α=0 destroys equations, so require α≠0 and f=f(q,t) for the ordinary total derivative equivalence.
- §2.6 p41 drops a minus sign in the inner-product line after (2.129); conclusion of reality is unaffected. p43's mode-3 frequency comparison contradicts its own positive-mass formula: sqrt((k/m)(1+2m/M)) exceeds sqrt(k/m), including M>2m.
- §3.2 p51 says principal moments positive but proves nonnegative and immediately gives a rod zero eigenvalue. §3.4.4 p59 'always closed/periodic' ignores separatrices. Six rigid-body degrees p46 needs nondegeneracy. Astronomical satellite/location and period anecdotes are secondary prose, not validated empirical records here.
- §4.1.1 p82 eq(4.9) sign should be ∂yg=−∂yf. p83's convex-conjugate geometry correctly uses a maximum of ux−f for convex f, but its assertion that the Legendre transform can only be applied to convex functions is too restrictive for local mechanical Legendre inversion: a nonsingular velocity Hessian is the relevant local equivalence requirement, with a global inverse required for global equivalence.
- §4.2.2 pp91–92: ρ(H) stationary needs autonomous H and normalization/boundary assumptions; exp(−H/kT) alone is not normalized, nor normalizable on unconstrained spatial R³ for a free particle. Bohr–van Leeuwen discussion needs equilibrium canonical ensemble assumptions and spin exclusion; its rhetorical 'no magnetism in classical physics' is too broad.
- §4.3.1 p96 Runge–Lenz example fixes the potential coupling to 1 without dimensional explanation; the dot-product orbit formula drops m relative to A definition. SO(4) hidden symmetry needs the negative-energy rescaling regime. §4.3.3 p99 visually shows an extra R² in the orbit sine/cosine after ω has already been defined as (γ1+γ2)/R², and a momentum expression mixing m v with eB y/(m c); derive again with stated units. Strong-field reduction needs an actual reduced bracket/constraint model, not dropping terms in a canonical momentum identity.
- §§4.4.1 p103: displayed infinitesimal symplectic conditions are incomplete unless the other symmetry conditions are included. A symplectic vector field is locally Hamiltonian; global generator existence requires exactness/topological conditions, and a general time-varying family is generated by a parameter-dependent G, not automatically one fixed G.
- §4.5.2 p108 independence, regularity/compactness missing from cited integrability theorem. §4.5.4 p112 identifies Kepler action angle θϕ with spatial polar ϕ, but ϕdot=L/(mr²) varies on noncircular orbits while action angles have constant rates. It also calls closed orbits unique to 1/r, ignoring isotropic harmonic central potential. Sign/orientation of angular action and circular/zero-action singular limits need restrictions.
- §4.6.2 p117 eq(4.162), visually confirmed ∂iB≪R, is dimensionally invalid; a meaningful slow-gradient condition is R|∇B|/|B|≪1 on B≠0. Conservation of magnetic moment/action is approximate in this discussion, not an exact prohibition of drift across level sets. The p118 magnetic-mirror idealization must use a divergence-free spatial field with transverse structure; Bz increasing in z alone cannot be a globally consistent ∇·B=0 field.
- §4.7 p124 says singularity of W0 occurs when V=0; turning-point degeneracy is tied to E−V and caustics, not the arbitrary choice of potential zero. Terminology for principal/characteristic action differs among books; define the actual function rather than trusting names.
- §4.8 p126 eq(4.198), visually confirmed +iℏ∂q, conflicts with [q,p]=+iℏ; standard representation needs −iℏ∂q. p129 eq(4.204) compares ℏ|Wqq| with |Wq| in different dimensions; a standard phase-gradient condition involves ℏ|Wqq|≪|Wq|² plus amplitude control away from nodes/turning points. Formal path measure, oscillatory Gaussian integrals, endpoint delta normalization and limit exchanges pp129–131 do not constitute a rigorous path-integral construction.
- §4.8.2 p132 final summand of (4.214) visibly repeats h instead of the derivative of g. More deeply, the sum of independent 3D determinant brackets for n>1 does not automatically define a Nambu–Poisson tensor satisfying the fundamental identity; this extension requires a separate audit. It is unsuitable as a proved foundational supplier from these notes alone.

Tong's Earth-wobble account §3.4.2 p57 distinguishes approximately 300-day rigid prediction from reported 427/435-day observations and attributes discrepancy to deformability; it reports no apparatus, sampling, uncertainty, raw data or primary retrieval in this reading. The equinox discussion §§3.6.4 pp72–74 uses a deliberately rough historical mnemonic, not a controlled measurement. Plate tossing/tennis racket suggestions are demonstration proposals, not retrieved experiment reports. These can motivate future sourcing but cannot be authored as new measured outcomes or exact tests. No experiments prove Newton's postulates. A discrepancy tests the adopted dynamics together with body model, preparation, apparatus and inference. For stochastic/uncertain evidence, rare samples are not automatic contradictions, and no invented error bars or certainty belong downstream.
