# Classical mechanics source report: SICM, second edition

Sussman and Wisdom's *Structure and Interpretation of Classical Mechanics* is a complete, authoritative MIT textbook with unusually precise functional notation, extensive coordinate derivations and substantial coverage of Hamiltonian geometry, dynamics and perturbation. It is a strong source for explicit constructions and worked models. It is not a self-contained proof source for every geometric, analytic or chaos theorem it discusses: several are explicitly informal, summarized, assumed or computationally demonstrated. This distinction must survive harvesting.

## Explicit mathematical formulation before physical use

The following are recommendations for rigorous library formulation, informed by SICM §§1.1–1.3, 1.6.1, 3.1, 5.1–5.3 and 6.2, not claims that the source already supplies all manifold proofs.

Choose a classical spacetime model explicitly: an affine four-dimensional manifold E with an absolute-time affine map τ:E→R, and a Euclidean inner product on the three-dimensional vector space ker(dτ) of simultaneous displacement vectors. A classical inertial reference frame means an affine coordinate isomorphism E→R×R³ compatible with τ and the spatial metric; transformations between allowed oriented inertial frames have t'=t+t₀ and x'=Rx+ut+a with constant R∈SO(3), velocity u and displacement a. Fix units for t,x before comparing coordinates. A rotating or accelerating frame uses a stated smooth time-dependent origin b(t) and orthonormal basis R(t), x=b(t)+R(t)x'; it is not an inertial frame. This is a formulation choice supplementing SICM's rotating-coordinate examples, not a relativistic spacetime assumption.

A point-particle worldline is a C² curve w:I→E, I⊂R an interval, with τ(w(t))=t; its frame-coordinate spatial path is x:I→R³. For N distinguishable particles, a configuration is an ordered tuple of simultaneous spatial positions. The unconstrained configuration manifold is Q=(R³)^N, or an explicitly chosen open collision-free subset. A regular holonomic constraint c:I×Q→R^r with spatial derivative rank r yields each Q_t=c(t,·)^{-1}(0) as a smooth embedded manifold of dimension 3N−r. Time-dependent Q_t requires a time-dependent configuration bundle or a specified trivialization; do not write a single fixed Q without explaining the identification. A general configuration manifold Q is assumed finite-dimensional, smooth, Hausdorff and second countable. A chart χ:U→V⊂R^n is a diffeomorphism, not a globally unique tuple label.

A tangent vector v∈T_qQ can be defined as the derivation f↦(d/ds)f(γ(s))|₀ for a smooth curve γ through q, or an equivalent curve-jet definition. The tangent bundle TQ is the disjoint union of these spaces with its induced smooth structure. A covector p∈T_q*Q is a real linear functional on T_qQ; T*Q is the cotangent bundle. Position components q^i are chart values; velocity components v^i belong to a tangent vector; momentum components p_i belong to a covector. For q=F(q'), v=DF(q')v' and p'=DF(q')*p; hence p(v)=p'(v'). Time-dependent transformations add ∂_tF to coordinate velocity. SICM's up/down tuples make these distinctions operational but do not replace global bundle definitions.

A configuration trajectory is a C² curve q:I→Q satisfying the stated dynamical equations; an arbitrary comparison curve is only a path. Its tangent lift is (q(t),q̇(t))∈TQ. For a regular Lagrangian it yields a phase trajectory z:I→T*Q by the fiber derivative. In the unconstrained Hamiltonian formulation the phase space is the symplectic manifold (T*Q,ω), not merely R^{2n}; a local chart labels it by (q^i,p_i). Extended state space I×T*Q is odd-dimensional and not this symplectic phase space. The extended symplectic phase space is T*(R×Q), with coordinates (t,q,p_t,p), dimension 2n+2.

Let π:T*Q→Q. Define the tautological one-form θ at (q,p) by θ_{(q,p)}(ξ)=p(Tπ(ξ)); set ω=−dθ. In cotangent coordinates θ=Σp_i dq^i and ω=Σdq^i∧dp_i. A symplectic form is a closed, nondegenerate two-form. For H:I×T*Q→R, define X_{H_t} by ι_{X_{H_t}}ω=dH_t; its coordinates are (∂H/∂p_i,−∂H/∂q^i). A Hamiltonian trajectory solves ż=X_{H_t}(z). For C² H, the vector field is C¹ and admits local unique solutions with differentiable dependence; the flow Φ_{t,s}:D_{t,s}→D_{s,t} is a diffeomorphism only on its existence domain. Completeness/global-in-time evolution needs a separate proof.

## Variational hypotheses and physical premises

A first-order Lagrangian is a C² function L:I×TQ→R with physical dimension energy. On C² paths in a chart, S[q]=∫_a^b L(t,q,q̇)dt. Fix endpoints, use C¹ compactly supported or endpoint-zero variations η, and keep nearby paths inside the chart/domain. A C² L on a neighborhood of the compact lifted path justifies first differentiation under the integral; integrate by parts and use the fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations. Stationarity means dS[q+εη]/dε|₀=0 for every allowed η. This is equivalent to d/dt(∂_vL)=∂_qL under these hypotheses. Neither minimum, unique boundary-value solution nor global existence follows. For higher derivative Lagrangians, specify jet order, stronger path regularity and fixed endpoint jets; SICM Exercise1.10 is a prompt, not a supplied general theorem.

The principle that a specified mechanical system follows these stationary paths is an adopted physical postulate/model law. Alternatively adopt Newtonian point-particle dynamics m_αẍ_α=−∇_{xα}V and deduce the T−V variational formulation for that class (§1.6). Positive constant particle masses, inertial Euclidean coordinates and a C² position-dependent potential are physical model premises. “Every conceivable classical system has L=T−V” is not established; dissipation and velocity-dependent interactions need separate formulations. Stationarity-to-Euler–Lagrange is pure mathematics once L and admissible curves are given; its interpretation as realizable mechanical motion is conditional physics.

For ideal rigid constraints, SICM §1.6.2 accepts Newtonian constraint forces along interparticle rods with equal/opposite forces. This idealization and zero virtual work are model assumptions. Real stiffness-to-rigidity convergence is not proved (endnote65). Multiple constraints require constant rank and invertibility of the constrained mass matrix/multiplier system. “Enough equations” alone does not establish well-posedness. Positional elimination is local on regular constraints; redundant distances can be dependent, and singular frameworks are explicitly excluded (endnote67).

Define a nonholonomic constraint distribution D_q⊂T_qQ as a smooth constant-rank subbundle, for example ker A(q), and distinguish an affine time-dependent velocity constraint A(t,q)v+b(t,q)=0. Integrable means the distribution is locally tangent to submanifolds, with Frobenius hypotheses stated; general velocity constraints require more than testing whether one written equation is an exact total derivative. D'Alembert ideal reaction means its covector annihilates the permitted virtual-displacement subspace. The resulting Lagrange–d'Alembert equations do not generally equal equations obtained by adding λψ to an unconstrained variational action. SICM §1.10.3 explicitly makes this distinction. Vakonomic stationarity and mechanical rolling require distinct postulates; neither set of equations obtains empirical correctness from algebra alone.

Adding dG(t,q)/dt changes S by endpoint G and leaves fixed-endpoint stationarity invariant (§1.6.4). It changes canonical momentum by d_qG and Hamiltonian by −∂_tG with momentum substitution (§5.4.5). Require a globally defined G for a global gauge claim; closed forms need not be exact on nontrivial topology. This is also why canonical momentum is formulation-relative rather than universally m times velocity.

## Regular and singular Legendre maps

The fiber derivative is FL_t:TQ→T*Q, FL_t(q,v)=(q,d_vL_t(q,v)). Regular means its velocity Hessian W_{ij}=∂²L/∂v^i∂v^j is nonsingular everywhere in the stated domain; the inverse function theorem gives local diffeomorphism. Hyperregular means FL_t is a global diffeomorphism. The local Hamiltonian H(t,q,p)=p(v)−L(t,q,v), v=V(t,q,p), and Lagrangian/Hamiltonian path equivalence require an actual smooth inverse branch. Regularity is weaker than hyperregularity; it neither forces convexity nor licenses replacing the mechanics transform by unrestricted Legendre–Fenchel duality. A positive-definite kinetic metric with a smooth linear velocity term gives a global affine fiber inverse on full fibers; restricting the domain can still remove global surjectivity.

SICM §3.1.1 derives the inverse relations conditionally and its code computes only affine/quadratic velocity dependence using an invertible Hessian. General symbolic inversion is expressly unavailable. Its treatment is not a Dirac–Bergmann theory of singular Lagrangians. A singular FL can have constrained image, nonunique velocities, compatibility equations or gauge freedom. Example: L=½(v¹)² on Q=R² has p₂=0 and no inverse v²; changing genuine charts cannot fix this rank defect. The action-angle oscillator H=ωI (§5.2/Exercise5.3) has singular momentum Hessian and no ordinary inverse Lagrangian in those canonical variables despite its valid Hamiltonian dynamics. Coordinate singularity at Euler-angle sinθ=0 is a different phenomenon.

## Canonical maps, flow and conserved quantities

For a time-independent C:U'→U that is a diffeomorphism and C*ω=ω, H'=H∘C gives T C·X_{H'}=X_H∘C. Conversely preservation for arbitrary smooth H gives the symplectic condition. Intertwining one specific Hamiltonian's vector field, SICM Eq5.20's initial system-relative definition, is weaker: for H=H'=0 any diffeomorphism intertwines zero vector fields. Do not equate that test alone with symplectic geometry. In coordinates the universal condition is DC J DC^T=J with stated J and column conventions.

A smooth family C_t of symplectomorphisms needs a Hamiltonian correction K when q,p=C_t(q',p'); the time-variation vector field must be Hamiltonian for a global correction. Locally it exists on suitable contractible charts; on a general symplectic manifold a closed contraction one-form can fail to be exact. Point cotangent lifts give SICM's explicit correction −p(∂_tF). Generating functions specify the graph of a symplectomorphism in mixed coordinates; the chosen projection must be invertible, for F₁ typically det ∂²F₁/(∂q∂q')≠0. A generator is local unless exactness/topology is established. Symplectic is not globally exact: Exercise5.10's shifted action yields action-integral difference 2πa on a full angle loop. Oriented area sums must count multiplicity as pullback two-form integrals, not naive area of overlapping projected sets.

Hamiltonian flow preserves ω by differentiating Φ_{t,s}*ω and using dω=0, d²H=0 (or SICM §6.2's variational calculation). It therefore preserves ω^n/n!, the Liouville volume. Preserve flow domains and integration hypotheses. A stroboscopic return map needs existence for the whole forcing period. An autonomous fixed-energy section needs a regular energy level, transverse section, smooth finite return time and a chosen crossing branch; it preserves the induced symplectic form, represented by dq∧dp in SICM's canonical sections (§6.2.1).

For smooth functions F,G define {F,G}=Σ(F_q G_p−F_p G_q). Along solutions dF/dt=∂_tF+{F,H}. A conserved quantity is a function for which this derivative vanishes on every solution in the specified domain. H is conserved if ∂_tH=0; it is physical energy T+V only under the given autonomous natural-Lagrangian/constraint assumptions. If L has cyclic q^i then p_i is conserved. For an exact differentiable one-parameter point symmetry, momentum applied to its infinitesimal generator is conserved by the Euler–Lagrange equations (§1.8.5); quasi-invariance up to a boundary term needs the corrected charge. Linear and angular momentum require translation/rotation symmetry of the model, not merely suggestive names. Independent conserved quantities reduce dimension only at regular common level sets.

Liouville integrability on a 2n-dimensional symplectic manifold requires n independent first integrals in involution; compact connected regular common levels yield invariant tori and local action-angle coordinates under the appropriate theorem. SICM §4.4's independence-only wording is insufficient. Global action-angle coordinates can fail at separatrices, singular fibers and through topology. KAM requires a precise version with regularity, nondegeneracy/twist, perturbation-smallness norms and Diophantine frequency bounds; SICM explicitly does not prove it (§4.6).

## Stability, perturbation and chaos boundaries

An equilibrium z_e satisfies X_H(z_e)=0. Lyapunov stability means for each neighborhood U there exists a neighborhood V such that every forward solution starting in V remains in U while defined; specify completeness when asserting all t≥0. Linear stability concerns bounded solutions of the variational linear equation; it does not automatically imply nonlinear stability. Hyperbolic means spectrum avoids the imaginary axis for flows or unit circle for maps. Purely imaginary/elliptic eigenvalues require semisimplicity for bounded linear motion; repeated/parabolic cases need Jordan analysis, excluded by SICM's simple-eigenvalue assumptions. Positive definite constrained energy minima can establish nonlinear stability with explicit compactness/local bounds.

A smooth return map f has a fixed/periodic point; its stable set W^s(x) consists of points with f^k(y)→x as k→∞, with unstable set defined using f^{-k}. Calling these manifolds needs the stable manifold theorem and hyperbolicity. Homoclinic means an intersection of the stable/unstable sets of the same fixed point; transverse intersections support specific chaotic invariant sets under a separate theorem. SICM §4.3's tangle narrative and plots do not supply a universal proof that all perturbations force a crossing or that the resulting region has positive-area chaos.

A Lyapunov growth rate is a limit, when it exists, of t^{-1}log||DΦ_t(z)u|| relative to a chosen norm and admissible trajectory. Use dimensionless scaled coordinates before summing position and momentum squares. Almost-everywhere exponents require a multiplicative ergodic theorem with invariant measure/integrability assumptions. Unbounded coordinate changes can alter exponents; general claims of coordinate invariance need uniform equivalence of norms/derivatives. Positive exponent alone is not a universal definition/proof of chaos (a hyperbolic equilibrium has it). Specify sensitivity, topological mixing/transitivity or positive entropy if that is the claim. Finite-time numerical divergence and a visually area-filling section are suggestive computational examples, not theorem or experimental evidence.

SICM §§6.3–6.5 uses exact Hamiltonian Lie flows and formal series. A smooth flow need not be analytic; a convergent Taylor expansion is additional. Formal identities hold coefficientwise where defined; interpreting exponentials of unbounded operators needs domains and convergence. A finite Lie-series truncation need not be symplectic or exactly invertible (§7.2). Splitting compositions of exact subflows are symplectic, but error order and long-term shadow energy require separate estimates. Near-identity perturbation must include bounds on generator derivatives, remainder, domain and time interval. Fourier coefficient decay needs a uniform analytic strip/norm, and small denominators k·ω require quantified nonresonance. At resonance retain resonant terms and prove validity of the normal-form approximation. An integer angle change on a torus requires a unimodular matrix for global invertibility; mere real linear independence gives only local coordinates. Resonance overlap is a heuristic transition estimate (§7.4.3), not a general theorem guaranteeing chaos.

## Quantities and dimensions relative to formulation

Use SI or explicitly defined nondimensional scales. In Newtonian particle mechanics time, spatial geometry and positive mass parameters are adopted; force law/potential parameters are physical inputs. Velocity and acceleration are derivatives of the worldline; linear momentum mv and kinetic energy mv²/2 are derived in this model. Potential energy is an adopted scalar model function up to a gauge, not derived from geometry alone. In a Lagrangian-first formulation L and stationary-action postulate are inputs; generalized momentum d_vL and energy p(v)−L are derived. In a Hamiltonian-first formulation the cotangent/symplectic state structure and H are inputs; canonical p is part of the state and velocity is derived from ∂_pH. These statuses are formulation-relative.

Reinstate scales x=x₀X, p=p₀P, t=t₀τ and energy scale before physical interpretation; adding x² and x³ with coefficient1 otherwise fails dimensional consistency. Every trigonometric/exponential argument must be dimensionless.

## Empirical evidence boundary

SICM gives observations/motivation about planets, Mercury radar, Hyperion tumbling, a measured top and galaxy velocity dispersions, but does not furnish a complete experimental apparatus/data/calibration/uncertainty/sampling record for these. Its plots largely report numerical model integrations. Do not harvest those as standalone `experiment` items or report their numerical parameters as new measurements. Retrieve the primary experimental report before empirical authoring. A future experiment item must separate apparatus/preparation, observed dataset, measurement uncertainties and specified model comparison. A qualitative textbook observation stays qualitative. Numerical agreement does not prove the physical law, and a simulation is not an observation of nature.

The first-principles chain therefore uses postulates for dynamical laws and idealizations; mathematical items for smooth-manifold/variation/ODE/symplectic deductions; physical-theorems for model predictions; thought experiments for explicitly idealized setups with complete conditional deductions; examples/remarks for unproved numerical explorations. Empirical support links are separate from logical prerequisites. All uncertainties and applicability assumptions must propagate to physical consumers.
