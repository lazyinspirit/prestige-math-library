## Framework and explicit objects needed before exposition

The following are proposed rigorous local formulations to make the source usable; they are not claims that Likharev supplied all these definitions or that their mathematical prerequisites were audited in the repository.

Start with a Newtonian spacetime affine structure with absolute time map τ:M→R and oriented Euclidean three-dimensional spatial fibres. A convenient chosen trivialization identifies M with R×E, where E is oriented Euclidean affine3-space and differences of spatial points lie in its Euclidean vector space V. This geometric structure is adopted physical background, not a consequence of mathematical axioms. An inertial Cartesian frame is an adapted affine coordinate map whose transformation to another admitted inertial frame is t'=t+b, x'=Rx+ut+a with constant R∈SO(3), u,a∈R³ and b∈R. Declare origin, orientation, unit scales and time convention. This is more precise than the source's observational platform/clock definition (§1.1), and must not import Lorentzian causal structure.

For N constant positive-mass point particles, take an open configuration domain Q⊂E^N, excluding any collision/singular loci demanded by the chosen force model. A trajectory is x:I→Q of class C² on an interval I⊂R. Define v=dx/dt∈T_xQ and a=d²x/dt² via the adopted flat structure. A prescribed force law is a map F:U×I→(V*)^N or, after Euclidean identification, V^N, where U⊂TQ. State continuity and local Lipschitz regularity in the state if invoking uniqueness of solutions; velocity-dependent laws are allowed only when explicitly declared. An initial state is (x0,v0)∈TQ at t0. The first-order vector field (v,M^−1F) lives on TQ; existence may be local and cease at collision or escape. Naming Newton's equation does not establish global solutions.

In Likharev's actual adopted presentation (§1.2), mass and isolated momentum conservation are postulated, then force is defined along motion by p'=F, and pair-force superposition is additionally assumed. In an alternative Newton-first presentation, prescribed interaction force is input to m x''=F. These are different choices of primitives; declare which is used. A force defined after observing motion does not by itself predict another trajectory; predictive use needs an independently specified interaction model.

For a general smooth configuration manifold Q, generalized velocity is a tangent vector in TQ, not a separate arbitrary coordinate without a space. For a moving holonomic embedding ρ:Q×I→E^N, with each ρ_t a sufficiently smooth full-rank immersion/embedding, physical particle velocity is D_qρ·qdot+∂tρ. Ideal constraint reaction is a covector annihilating D_qρ(T_qQ) at fixed t. This ideality is an adopted restriction (frictionless reaction), not automatic for every constraint. A rank-defined constraint f(x,t)=0 gives smooth configuration fibres only under an appropriate constant-rank/regular-value theorem. Inequalities/contact and nonintegrable velocity distributions require separate unilateral/impact/nonholonomic models. Standard EL substitution does not automatically handle them.

Let L:TQ×I→R be C² with energy units; in coordinates specify all derivatives and conventions. L=T−U for coordinate-only U is the source's natural-mechanical branch (§2.1), not the definition of all possible Lagrangians. The fibre derivative FL(q,v,t)=(q,∂L/∂v,t) maps TQ to T*Q; regular velocity Hessian gives only local Legendre inversion, whereas global hyperregularity must be separately established for a globally defined equivalent H:T*Q×I→R. Canonical momentum is a covector with coordinate-dependent components. Phase space in the Hamiltonian framework is T*Q with its canonical symplectic form, not merely any collection of coordinates called a plane. Energy surfaces, where dH≠0, are codimension-one submanifolds; singular energy levels require separate handling. Likharev does not develop this manifold/symplectic structure.

For fixed-endpoint action, choose a stated path space, for example C² curves q:[a,b]→Q and a smooth family qε with qε(a),qε(b) fixed. Define S[q]=∫a^b L(q,qdot,t)dt. Prove first variation under declared differentiability and the fundamental lemma, giving stationary paths iff EL equations in the chosen regularity class. Do not call stationary paths minimizers without second-variation/coercivity/time-interval hypotheses. L and L+dG(q,t)/dt can give the same fixed-endpoint variation, so uniqueness of a Lagrangian is false in general.

Work along a C¹ trajectory is W=∫a^b F(x(t),v(t),t)·v(t)dt under integrability assumptions. Kinetic energy T=Σm_i|v_i|²/2 is defined from adopted masses and Euclidean metric; work-energy is then conditional calculus from Newton's equation. For a time-independent potential U:Q→R with sufficient regularity and F=−dU, work is minus potential difference. A locally curl-free force is not necessarily globally exact; state topology/exactness. The source's Gibbs-like U−∫Fext·dr (§1.4) needs this global path-independence qualification.

## Model postulates and conditional results

Adopt separately: Newtonian spacetime; inertial-frame existence; constant masses; inertial momentum law/equation; chosen force addition/pair decomposition; relevant reciprocity/centrality; interaction laws; ideal constraints; rigid/continuum approximations; constitutive relations; boundary conditions. Source §1.3 already makes centrality explicit for angular conservation; preserve it. Newton's equal-and-opposite force law alone does not cancel internal torque unless the pair force is central or a broader angular-momentum model is given. Velocity-dependent electromagnetic forces can require field momentum; do not silently impose particle-only third law on a coupled model.

Then conditional physical theorems may establish work-energy, conserved momentum/torque, EL dynamics under ideal holonomic constraints, cyclic momenta, H time evolution, natural-system H=E, two-body reduction and Kepler conics. Empirical material constants and Newtonian gravity remain model assumptions, with evidence links separate from prerequisite proof edges. A purely mathematical ODE or variational result uses only explicit mathematical assumptions and remains mathematical despite physical motivation.

Continuum mechanics does not follow from point-particle Newton equations without additional limiting/locality and constitutive assumptions. Define reference body B⊂R³, deformation χ:B×I→R³ with regularity and orientation-preserving invertibility where used, current density, material/Eulerian maps, boundaries and normals. Linear elasticity uses χ(X,t)=X+u(X,t), small ∇u, strain symgrad u and isotropic Hooke σ=2μ strain+(K−2μ/3)tr(strain)Id. Momentum balance is ρu_tt=divσ+f only in the declared linear/material approximation. Finite rotation and finite strain require other strain measures. Fluid variables v:Ω×I→R³, ρ:Ω×I→(0,∞), P:Ω×I→R with declared smoothness generate material trajectories by χ_t=v(χ,t). Mass balance, momentum balance, equation of state/energy closure, boundary assumptions and initial data are distinct. Smooth derivations do not license discontinuities, shocks, point loads or turbulence singularities without weak formulations.

Evidence in this volume is mainly secondary qualitative motivation, numerical examples, approximate material tables and historical reports. It does not supply original apparatus records, sampling models, uncertainties or data needed for experiment items. No new measured value or uncertainty is certified here. Numerical predictions of an adopted model are not observations of nature. Finite tests support or constrain specified models and auxiliary assumptions; they do not prove Newton's laws, constitutive laws or a framework.

## Exact proof gaps and demonstrable source issues

These findings are about the retrieved version; they are not exhaustive, and no repository item has been edited.

1. **Definition rigor is explicitly missing.** §1.1 says it uses less rigorous consensus notions. Frames, trajectories, force models, time dependence and regularity must be supplied as above. This is not merely a formatting gap.
2. **Variational/Hamiltonian closure.** §2.1 needs independent full-rank coordinates and ideal reaction hypothesis; §10.1 needs regular/hyperregular Legendre transform. Noether is external (§2.4 footnote16), Jacobi identity is asserted readily verifiable (§10.1), action-angle canonicality explicitly unproved (§10.2 footnote14). Supply proofs/prerequisites before harvesting general claims.
3. **Stationarity versus minimum.** Eqs10.48–10.51 establish first-order stationarity, not general minimality. The later repeated minimum/eikonal language (§10.3) needs restriction. PDF246/Ch10p10 visually gives λ=(π/(2ω0))² in qa=A(1−λt²); endpoint condition instead requires λ=(2ω0/π)², with units s^−2.
4. **Adiabatic invariance.** §10.2 explicitly approximates a nonperiodic motion by period averaging; footnote12 calls the critical step conjectural and cites stricter treatments. Do not turn J≈const into exact conservation under arbitrary slow changes. State a small parameter, finite interval/error bound, nonzero frequency and distance from separatrix/turning degeneracy. PDF248/Ch10p12 calls J an integer quantum number: J has action units, so J/ℏ with appropriate semiclassical phase corrections, not J itself, is the dimensionless candidate. Quantum heuristics cannot serve as a classical proof.
5. **Linearized stability.** §§3.2,5.6 omit precise Lyapunov/asymptotic/orbital definitions and theorem hypotheses. Positive-definite energy can prove nonlinear stability of a strict minimum; hyperbolic linearization supplies other cases. Pure imaginary/zero eigenvalues are inconclusive in general. Ch3p3 footnote2 equates κ=0 with inflection; U(q)=q⁴ at0 is a degenerate minimum instead.
6. **Normal-mode scope.** §6.2 Eq6.16 is not the most general linear Lagrangian: mixed velocity-coordinate gyroscopic terms can occur. The strictly negative characteristic-root conclusion needs M>0 and K>0; semidefinite stiffness introduces zero modes, and generalized gyroscopic stability has different analysis. Supply the symmetric generalized eigenproblem proof under exact hypotheses.
7. **Attenuation correction.** §6.6 Eqs6.85–6.88 uses low-frequency v for complex-k decay even when k is not acoustic. From undamped Ω(k)²=4κef sin²(kd/2)/m and damped relation Ω(k)²=ω²+2iωδ, put k=k0+iκi at fixedω. First order gives 2ωvg κi=2ωδ, hence κi=δ/vg, where vg=dΩ/dk at k0. Only in acoustic limit is vg≈v. Require weak damping away from band edge, and do not present frequency-independent decay generally.
8. **False elastic-moduli independence.** §7.4, PDF168/Ch7p14, infers from Δ(div u)=0 that prescribed displacement boundaries make full deformation independent of moduli. Taking divergence loses information. Explicit counterexample: on unit ball with f=0 and boundary u=ε(x²,0,0), let μ>0, Lamé parameter λL=K−2μ/3, and c=−(λL+2μ)/(λL+4μ). Set u=ε(x²+c(r²−1),0,0). Then Δu=ε(2+6c,0,0) and grad divu=ε(2+2c,0,0), so μΔu+(λL+μ)grad divu=0. On r=1 all choices have identical boundary displacement; μ=1,λL=0 gives c=−1/2, while μ=1,λL=1 gives c=−3/5. Both K>0,μ>0; smallε keeps linear strain small. The displayed solutions differ inside. Young's common scale may cancel, but Poisson dependence does not generally disappear.
9. **Laminar is not irrotational.** §8.5, PDF209/Ch8p17 labels Couette laminar flow vorticity-free. Its own next-page solution v=(0,0,v0y/d) has curlv=(v0/d,0,0), nonzero for v0≠0. Preserve the solution, correct terminology. Separately, PDF182/Ch7p28 gives transverse impedance denominator ∂qx/∂z; the force/velocity definition and units require ∂qx/∂t.
10. **Potential flow and Kelvin.** §8.4's global curl-free/potential and far-field statements need topology/initial circulation, smoothness, conservative forcing and barotropic/incompressible closure. A uniform velocity at infinity around a cylinder permits additional circulating harmonic velocity in the multiply connected exterior; far-field uniformity alone does not rule it out. Streamlines equal particle trajectories only for steady velocity fields. Kelvin is referenced externally, not proved here.
11. **Chaos theorem rigor.** §9.2's non-crossing argument is not a proof of a correctly stated Poincaré–Bendixson theorem; uniqueness needs hypotheses and compact ω-limit assumptions. §9.3 Eq9.17's fixed factor2 per reflection is not a derivative-cocycle proof for arbitrary nearby billiard trajectories. Irrational rectangular billiards are dense in the allowed region, not literally a trajectory visiting every point. Lyapunov limit existence, typicality and invariant measure remain unsupplied. No rigorous universal chaos/turbulence equivalence is established.
12. **Secondary empirical details.** Elastic/fluid tables, turbulence thresholds, equivalence-principle and Gravity Probe B summaries require original source reads before experiment/precision claims. Definitions of Re and chosen length/speed can change numerical thresholds; linear instability threshold and observed transition are not interchangeable. Surface-wave, Love-wave, and general finite-domain spectral claims need exact geometry and boundary hypotheses.
