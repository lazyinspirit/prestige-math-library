## Adopted physical starting points and distinctions

Choose a microscopic classical Maxwell–Lorentz formulation in rationalized SI. The primitive model fields are E and B; source charge is a signed real parameter or density. Prescribe sources first, then treat matter dynamics in separately stated coupled models. Adopt four Maxwell equations and Lorentz force as physical postulates/empirical laws, not mathematical theorems about nature (§1.2 pp.4–7). Charge/current operational meaning is flux and charge per volume (§1.1 pp.2–4). Local conservation can be an independent source-compatibility requirement before solving and a conditional consequence of Maxwell afterward; avoid a circular dependency in exposition.

Maxwell equations do not alone supply the dynamics or constitution of matter. Newtonian m r''=q(E+r'×B) is an additional mechanical approximation; relativistic d(m gamma v)/dt=q(E+v×B) needs special-relativistic mechanics (§5.4.1 pp.115–116). Particle mass m enters as a parameter. The field self-energy discussion (§2.3.1 pp.27–29) illustrates divergence and a heuristic relativistic energy contribution; it does not generally derive measured mass, an electron radius, or a complete stable charged-particle model. Spin, charge quantization, microscopic magnetic moments, photon dictionary, Thomas–Fermi/Lindhard/Friedel analysis introduce quantum assumptions and belong in qualified context or a separately coupled framework, not hidden classical prerequisites.

Vacuum Maxwell is a continuum idealization. No magnetic-charge source is included, a framework scope assumption; the author's discussion of non-observation and possible monopoles (§3.2.1 pp.47–48) supplies no exhaustive empirical exclusion. Line/surface/point charges are idealized distributions. Electrostatics assumes time-independent pinned charge in a specified frame, J=0, and a selected B=0 solution (p.8); magnetostatics assumes steady divergence-free currents and optionally ρ=0,E=0 (p.41). These are restricted models, not universal implications of charge/current names.

Ohm J=σE is explicitly an extra input valid only in suitable materials (§4.1.3 pp.74–75). Linear dielectric, magnetic, Drude and damped-oscillator laws are further constitutive models (§§7.1–7.6). Retarded/no-incoming solution selection is additional to time-symmetric Maxwell (§6.1.2 p.142). The retarded Green function cannot be called the unique general solution without fixing initial/boundary/radiation conditions and accounting for homogeneous solutions.

## Required explicit definitions and regularity

For a smooth chapter baseline, take an open spatial region Ω⊂R³, interval I⊂R, oriented Euclidean coordinates, and E,B:Ω×I→R³ of class C¹; ρ:Ω×I→R and J:Ω×I→R³ continuous. Require C² fields or appropriate weak derivatives where mixed derivative identities and wave equations are used. Give units below. All derivatives are spatial at fixed t unless stated; density/flux integrals use Euclidean volume/area measures and declared orientations. Specify a fixed bounded V with piecewise smooth boundary closure in Ω, and a compact oriented piecewise smooth surface S with oriented boundary C when applying Gauss/Stokes. Time differentiation through integrals requires sufficient continuous derivatives or dominated convergence. These hypotheses are recommended local rigor additions, not claims that Tong states them in full.

A particle trajectory r:I→Ω should be at least C² for force dynamics; q∈R is fixed. Evaluate external fields at (r(t),t), with self-field excluded. The relativistic version requires adopted Minkowski spacetime (R⁴,η=diag(1,-1,-1,-1), time orientation), an inertial frame defined as an affine orthonormal coordinate system with future time axis, and a future-directed timelike C² worldline X:J→R⁴ satisfying η(X',X')>0. Proper-time parameter τ satisfies η(dX/dτ,dX/dτ)=c². Define the Lorentz group via ΛᵀηΛ=η and specify proper orthochronous transformations when time/order/orientation matter. A coordinate frame is not an observer congruence or arbitrary tetrad. Tong gives coordinates and matrices (§5.1 pp.95–101); exact geometric suppliers remain necessary.

Singular sources do not satisfy that smooth baseline on all Ω. Either exclude their support/worldline and give flux or interface data, or define distributions on spacetime test functions and Maxwell distributionally. For a moving point source use ρ=qδ³(x-r(t)), J=q r'(t)δ³(x-r(t)) (§§1.1,6.4 pp.2–3,157–158). The delta is a distribution, not a real-valued smooth density. E² and self-force may fail to be integrable/defined at the worldline; distributional Maxwell does not authorize multiplying singular distributions. Piecewise C¹ fields with traces across a smooth stationary surface require jump formulas, not asserted C¹ regularity across it.

Static scalar potential ϕ:Ω→R has E=-∇ϕ; global existence requires vanishing periods, e.g. suitable simply connected domain, not curl-free alone (§2.2 p.17). Vector potential A needs an appropriate closed-form exactness condition, e.g. contractible Ω, not divergence-free alone (§3.2 p.46). On spacetime potentials E=-∇ϕ-∂tA, B=∇×A require at least C² potentials. Gauge equivalence (ϕ,A)↦(ϕ-∂tχ,A+∇χ) with sufficiently smooth χ preserves fields (§5.3.1 pp.105–106). Gauge fixing requires Poisson/wave solvability under actual data; it is not universally automatic.

For retarded particle fields restrict to events away from the worldline, speed |r'|<c, and a history on which t'=t-|x-r(t')|/c has a solution. The derivative of t'+|x-r(t')|/c is 1-n·v/c>0, giving uniqueness when a root exists; existence needs history/domain conditions. Additional trajectory smoothness supplies differentiated fields. These conditions make precise Tong's retarded-time Jacobian (§6.4.1 pp.158–159).

## Equations, units and constants

Smooth vacuum/microscopic Maxwell: div E=ρ/ε₀, div B=0, curl E=-∂tB, curl B=μ₀J+μ₀ε₀∂tE (§1.2.1 p.6). For fixed V and S the integral forms are ∮∂V E·n dA=Q(V)/ε₀, ∮∂V B·n dA=0, ∮C E·dl=-d/dt∫S B·n dA, ∮C B·dl=μ₀∫S J·n dA+μ₀ε₀ d/dt∫S E·n dA. Gauss/Stokes plus differentiation hypotheses establish differential-to-integral implication; converse requires validity for all suitable small test regions/surfaces and regularity, not one chosen Gaussian surface. For moving loops with velocity u, emf=∮C(t)(E+u×B)·dl=-d/dt∫S(t)B·n dA (§4.1.1 pp.69–71); include transport theorem and no flux singularities crossed. Induction alone does not specify current without constitutive/circuit dynamics.

Continuity ∂tρ+div J=0 follows from Maxwell at C² regularity or distributionally by commuting distributional derivatives. Global conservation additionally requires zero boundary flux or sufficiently decaying currents (§1.1.1 p.4). Static Coulomb/Biot–Savart formulae require appropriate localized integrable sources and decay/boundary choices (§2.2.3 pp.20–23; §3.2.3 pp.49–52). A nondecaying infinite line has logarithmic potential, so infinity-normalization is unavailable.

Modern SI fixes c=299792458 m s⁻¹ and elementary charge e=1.602176634×10⁻¹⁹ C exactly; μ₀ is experimentally determined and ε₀=1/(μ₀c²) follows the adopted convention. Do not copy exact μ₀=4π×10⁻⁷ from pp.6/83 as current SI. Tong's p.64 footnote explicitly notes the post-2019 change; do not repeat its informal precision estimate as a current uncertainty measurement. ℏ, temperature and Boltzmann constant are auxiliary to quantum/statistical sections.

Matter: define an actual averaging operation/kernel and scale separation before P,M (§7.1.1 p.174 explicitly leaves this informal). With stationary material boundaries and smooth bulk data, ρ_b=-div P and J_b=∂tP+curl M; surface densities follow from traces. Then div D=ρ_f, curl H=J_f+∂tD, div B=0,curl E=-∂tB (§7.3 pp.183–184). Constitutive closure is essential: D=εE,B=μH only for local isotropic linear nondispersive materials in declared regimes. Tensor response, nonlinear response, hysteresis, temporal convolution and spatial nonlocality require separate definitions. Frequency-dependent ε acts on Fourier amplitudes, not time-dependent fields by instantaneous multiplication. Scalar susceptibilities are dimensionless; conductivity has S m⁻¹. Avoid symbol collisions between charge density/resistivity and surface charge/conductivity.

## Energy, momentum, gauge and relativity

Construct u_em=(ε₀|E|²+|B|²/μ₀)/2, S=E×B/μ₀. Maxwell gives ∂tu_em+div S=-J·E (§4.4 pp.92–94; §5.6 pp.127–131). Mechanical work law identifies J·E as energy transferred to matter. Integrated total conservation needs mechanical accounting and boundary flux conditions. Finite energy requires integrability; point-charge self-energy diverges. Do not silently add kinetic energy of prescribed sources and claim an isolated closed system. Complex harmonic notation must be converted to real fields before quadratic quantities; time-average phasor formula needs conjugation and factor 1/2.

Momentum g=ε₀E×B=S/c². With conventional mechanical Maxwell stress τ=ε₀(E⊗E-|E|²1/2)+(B⊗B-|B|²1/2)/μ₀, ∂tg=div τ-(ρE+J×B). Tong uses σ=-τ as momentum flux (§5.6.1 pp.128–129). Angular-momentum density x×g and flux depend on symmetry and boundary control. Derivations are conditional identities; coupling to matter establishes total conservation. The vacuum stress tensor cannot be transferred without change to dispersive matter with stored material energy and momentum.

The covariant formulation (§§5.3–5.5 pp.105–127) needs explicit tensors/metric/index conventions. F=dA, Maxwell and Lorentz force are Lorentz covariant; E,B components mix by frame. The action is a chosen functional with fixed/compactly supported variations and integrations by parts; least action means stationary, not necessarily minimum. Gauge invariance with sources uses conserved current and boundary restrictions. A constant theta term changes classical bulk equations only through boundaries under declared global assumptions. No blanket uniqueness of Maxwell from aesthetics or Lorentz covariance: Tong p.114 acknowledges alternative nonlinear/higher derivative equations.

## Evidence and source defects requiring attention

Historical accounts pp.39–40,65–66,77–79,83–84 discuss Coulomb, Oersted, Faraday and wave-speed comparisons. They are secondary qualitative accounts, not complete authoritative measurement reports containing apparatus calibration, sample datasets and uncertainties. Electron g-factor, pulsar numbers, polarizability and Friedel photo are contextual numerical examples. No new experiment item should invent counts, error bars, independence or significance from these notes. Retrieve the actual experimental publication for source-backed experiment authoring; inference tests the chosen model plus preparation/apparatus auxiliaries. Classical waves superpose and interfere (§4.3.1 pp.86–87); fringes alone cannot disprove classical EM. Finite observations support/constrain model applicability, not prove Maxwell laws true.

Specific hazards found in reading:

- §§2.1/3 introduction pp.8/41 asserts unique fields from fixed sources without immediately stating boundary/decay conditions. Add them; homogeneous solutions otherwise remain.
- §2.4.2 pp.34–35 labels a uniqueness theorem without existence/Neumann compatibility; pure Neumann yields only potential modulo constant. Its proof does not establish existence. p.34 also writes exterior E=4πσ n in SI prose, inconsistent with p.30 E=σ n/ε₀; extraction alone checked for this last typo, no visual verification claimed.
- §6.1 p.135: visually confirmed gauge-fixing sign defect. For A'=A+∂χ and f=∂·A one needs □χ=-f, not □χ=f. Solve the actual PDE with data, rather than cite universal solvability.
- §6.3.1 p.155: visually confirmed incident Poynting magnitude c E₀²/μ₀ is wrong for E₀ an electric amplitude; it should be E₀²/(μ₀c). Printed final Thomson cross-section is the standard expression; rederive it using corrected flux. The section's quoted numerical electron cross-section should not be copied without check.
- §7.7.2 p.216: visually confirmed normalization shift. D(k)=ε_abs(k)E(k) is followed by ϕ_ext=ε(k)ϕ and ε=1-ρ_ind/(ε₀k²ϕ), using relative dimensionless ε without announcing conversion. Define ε_r=ε_abs/ε₀ explicitly.
- p.223 extracted Thomas–Fermi check loses the negative sign of ρ_ind/ϕ even though preceding ∂f/∂E=-δ gives it and following dielectric expression requires it; not visually verified here. Quantum tail needs independent rederivation rather than unchecked transcription.
- Kramers–Kronig pp.202–204 needs specified analytic continuation, decay and boundary-value existence; causality alone without growth hypotheses is insufficient. p.204 does state a decay assumption but final prose drops it. Group velocity is a narrow-packet approximation, not a general signal velocity.
