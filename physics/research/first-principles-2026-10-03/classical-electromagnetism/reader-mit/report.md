## Mathematical definitions to put before any consuming claim

Use the following precise proposed library formulation; these are recommendations from the reading, not claims that the source supplies all analytic hypotheses.

* Spacetime: affine four-space M with translation space R^4 and constant Lorentz bilinear form η=diag(-1,1,1,1), chosen orientation/time orientation. An inertial frame is an affine coordinate chart x=(ct,x1,x2,x3) whose basis is η-orthonormal, time basis future-directed. Related frames use proper orthochronous Lorentz matrices plus translations. A spatial region Ω is an open subset of R^3; I is an open time interval; U=I×Ω is the frame's spacetime region. Non-inertial coordinate changes are smooth diffeomorphisms; a chart is not an observer congruence.
* Electric and magnetic fields are maps E,B:Ω×I→R^3. For pointwise Maxwell, require C^1 jointly; for wave-equation manipulations C^2. E is polar and B axial under orientation-reversing spatial coordinates. Charge density ρ:Ω×I→R and current density J:Ω×I→R^3 are volume densities with charge Q(V,t)=∫Vρd^3x and oriented flux ∫SJ·n dS. For classical continuity require appropriate C^1 regularity and bounded piecewise-C^1 integration regions. These definitions alone do not postulate conservation.
* Singular sources: a charge q at prescribed spatial curve z:I→R^3 is ρ=qδ_z(t), J=q z'(t)δ_z(t), understood as distributions via test functions. More invariantly, for a future timelike C^2 curve X:K→M with parameter τ satisfying η(X',X')=-c^2, the four-current is q c∫K X'(τ)δ4(x-X(τ))dτ in x0=ct coordinates. Require absence of endpoints within U for distributional conservation; any endpoint requires additional source terms. Do not multiply its singular self-field by this current without a separate regularization/coupled-particle model.
* The electromagnetic tensor F is an antisymmetric rank-two tensor field with F0i=Ei/c in the source's upper-index convention and Fij=εijkBk. Its dual is defined using the oriented Lorentz volume form and η. Four-current is j=(cρ,J). The source's metric sign/index conventions matter: do not change signs by mixing tensor components from another text.
* Potential: scalar φ:U→R and spatial vector A:U→R^3, combined into a four-potential a=(φ/c,A), with B=curl A and E=-grad φ-∂tA. Require C^2 for commuting derivatives. Gauge equivalence is (φ,A)~(φ-∂tχ,A+gradχ) for χ∈C^2(U). Global existence is a separate topology question; locally dF=0 gives a potential on a contractible neighborhood, not on every Ω.
* Mechanics added to EM: q is a charge parameter and m>0 a rest-mass parameter. Momentum p=mX' and four-force f=dp/dτ. Coordinate-time three-force is F3=d(γmv)/dt, γ=(1-|v|^2/c^2)^(-1/2). Consequently f=γ(F3·v/c,F3), correcting Binney Eq.(1.45). A Lorentz-force model with a prescribed external field is an adopted coupling; classical EM does not derive m.
* Action is a real-valued functional on specified field/curve spaces, S[a]=(1/c)∫U[-F^{μν}F_{μν}/(4μ0)+jμaμ]d^4x with sign conventions checked against the metric. Stationarity means dS[a+s h]/ds at s=0 vanishes for every smooth compactly supported variation h; it is not necessarily a minimum. A conservation current is a vector field j whose distributional divergence vanishes. A stress-energy tensor is a rank-two tensor whose components encode energy density, momentum density and their fluxes in a specified frame; derive its formula and divergence, rather than assigning physical interpretations by name alone.
* Surface sources, if later supplied: for an oriented C^2 interface Σ, surface charge σ is a scalar density (C/m²) and surface current K is a tangent vector density (A/m). Fields must possess one-sided traces; discontinuities are represented by distributions supported on Σ. In vacuum at a stationary interface the jump relations are n·[E]=σ/ε0, n×[B]=μ0K, n·[B]=0, n×[E]=0. Derive under stated weak Maxwell hypotheses, with [field]=plus-minus. Moving interfaces require additional terms. Binney does not prove this interface theory.

SI conventions: charge q in C, ρ in C/m³, J in A/m²; E in V/m, B in T; φ in V and A in T m; force in N, mass in kg, energy in J. ε0 has F/m, μ0 has N/A², c has m/s, with ε0μ0c²=1 in the chosen vacuum normalization. c's modern SI numerical value is exact; μ0's is experimentally determined, not exactly 4π×10^-7. Binney's 2007 course is not a current metrology source. Adopt positive constants, document the formulation-local primitive/derived choice, and obtain authoritative modern SI references before numeric authoring. Field energy u=(ε0|E|²+|B|²/μ0)/2 (J/m³) and flux S=E×B/μ0 (W/m²) are derived conditional quantities; finite-energy/integrability hypotheses must travel with integrated identities.

## Physical assumptions and mathematical obligations

Vacuum Maxwell plus charge coupling can be adopted directly as postulates, or derived conditionally from the explicitly **adopted** Maxwell action and stationarity. Lorentz covariance, locality, quadratic dependence and a limited ansatz motivate the action in §3.6; they do not prove the action uniquely describes nature or rule out higher derivatives/nonlinear terms. The alternative action organization avoids treating an action-to-Euler-Lagrange deduction as empirical proof.

Maxwell pointwise equations need C^1 fields; deriving the wave equation and continuity through commuting derivatives needs C^2 or distributional derivatives. Stokes/divergence laws require oriented surfaces, boundaries and suitable regularity. The four-dimensional variational argument §§3.2,3.6 needs differentiation under integration, integration by parts and the fundamental lemma, with compact-support variations or explicit boundary conditions. Global gauge choices require solvability and boundary/initial conditions for Poisson/wave operators, absent from the source. Delta currents Eq.(3.33) need distributions and pullbacks/integration on embedded curves. Noether currents require hypotheses distinguishing global symmetry from gauge redundancy; merely copying §3.7 is insufficient for a rigorous gauge-Noether treatment.

The course assumes elementary electrostatics/vector calculus and relativity while developing relativistic notation; it does not construct all of these from zero. It does not prove initial-boundary-value existence/uniqueness for Maxwell, retarded Green functions, radiation conditions, electromagnetic radiation-reaction/self-force, macroscopic constitutive closure, material interfaces, static BVP methods or full radiation multipoles. Its plane-wave energy example p.9 is a special explicit solution, not a full radiation theory. Curved-spacetime Maxwell §5.2 uses additional geometry; the gravity chapters are separate models and should not become prerequisites of flat-space EM.

## Source fidelity and empirical qualifications

Eq.(1.45)'s gamma omission is the clearest directly checked source defect. Global potential/gauge existence, group-exponential surjectivity and arbitrary diagonal coordinate construction are asserted too casually for library proofs. §3's initial quantum-interference account is motivation, not a deduction of classical mechanics/EM or evidence establishing quantum theory. Classical Maxwell waves themselves interfere by linear superposition. No finite sample can establish an exact distribution without statistical qualification.

The course's experiment accounts concern gravity or cosmology, largely summary/secondary references; no new EM experiment dataset was found. Do not create quantitative EM evidence from this text. Any reported experiment needs primary source apparatus, calibration, sampling/uncertainty and qualified model comparison. Dated graviton forecasts, vacuum-energy discussion, numerical cosmology estimates and claims excluding every alternative are not transferable authoritative current facts. The completed reading identifies these limitations rather than certifying every source statement.
