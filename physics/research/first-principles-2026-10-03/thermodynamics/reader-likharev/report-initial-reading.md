## Supplier roles and macroscopic formulation

Chapter 1 adopts macroscopic assumptions; Chapter 2 introduces additional statistical and quantum assumptions. Neither measurements nor a stationary entropy maximization prove those assumptions.

A suitable explicit local formulation, more precise than the source's informal state language, starts with a convex extensive-coordinate cone/domain D in R^(r+2), coordinates x=(U,V,N_1,...,N_r), with V>0, N_i≥0 and admissible energies specified by the material model. Counts N_i are dimensionless; differentiating in N_i uses a macroscopic continuous approximation, not exact differentiation on integer counts. An equilibrium entropy fundamental relation S:D→R has SI units J/K. Adopt extensivity S(λx)=λS(x) for λ>0 when λx∈D and additivity only for negligibly coupled composites. On an open single-phase region adopt S∈C² (or C¹ when only first derivatives are used), concavity, and S_U>0 if restricting to positive absolute temperature. Boundary/coexistence/nonsmooth states require subgradients or another explicit treatment; do not silently extend C² identities there.

A constrained equilibrium of two weakly coupled systems is a maximizer of S_1(x_1)+S_2(x_2) under declared conservation constraints and allowed exchanges. Fixed U,V,N thermal exchange gives S_1,U=S_2,U; allowing volume exchange gives equality of P/T; allowing species exchange gives equality of −μ_i/T. At an interior differentiable equilibrium with common positive T, these give equal T,P,μ_i. First derivatives give stationarity; concavity/global feasible-set arguments give maximality. Strict concavity may imply uniqueness on the allowed directions but cannot be assumed at coexistence or along the homogeneity scaling ray. Existence requires hypotheses such as compact feasible set/upper semicontinuity, or an explicit coercivity argument.

The entropy maximum is an **adopted equilibrium principle**. A macroscopic relaxation inequality is a separate physical assumption for the stated isolation/coarse description and class of protocols. It is not an assertion that every microscopic realization has nondecreasing stochastic entropy. The source's “closed” means isolated (no heat, work or matter exchange), unlike widespread usage “closed”=matter impermeable. Use the explicit word *isolated* in new prose.

Source anchors: adaptation §§1.1–1.2, (1)–(13), §1.7 footnotes 14–17; original §1.2, printed Chapter1 pp5–7/PDF9–11, equations (1.4)–(1.13). Original p5 explicitly assumes internal relaxation faster than intersystem exchange; that is a local equilibrium/time-scale assumption, not a theorem from energy conservation.

The zeroth-law equivalence relation of thermal equilibrium is not explicitly axiomatized by the source: its footnote calls equilibrium existence sometimes the zeroth law. A new item should instead define thermal-contact equivalence on specified equilibrium states and adopt transitivity, distinguish it from equilibration existence, and identify what establishes a temperature label.

Likharev uses **T=k_B T_K in energy units and dimensionless S=S_SI/k_B**. Thus U=TS remains energy; C=∂U/∂T is dimensionless, and physical C_SI=k_B C. This must be declared before importing formulas. In SI k_B=1.380649×10^−23 J/K is exact; temperature being an SI base unit does not make it primitive in this chosen formulation. Source §1.2 (9), original printed p6/PDF10. Do not copy the erroneous Celsius/calorie footnotes: the inspected original prints T_C=T_K+273.15 rather than −273.15, and 4.148 J rather than the thermochemical calorie's 4.184 J.

A heat bath is a reservoir idealization with fixed T_b>0 over exchanges of interest; it requires capacity/size/time-scale assumptions. For a system equilibrated internally but exchanging heat with that reservoir, entropy balance uses δQ/T_b, not arbitrary system instantaneous T. If no non-volume work occurs and T_b,V,N are fixed, d(U−T_b S)≤0 follows from total system+bath entropy production and energy conservation. Analogously fixed T_b,P_b,N gives G decrease including environment mechanical work. Strong coupling, driven protocols, particle exchange, finite reservoirs and feedback require amended balances.

## Mathematical deductions and stability/phase scope

For U(S,V,N)∈C² on a single-phase open region, dU=T_KdS−PdV+Σμ_idN_i is a state identity. It is not a claim that δQ=T_KdS along irreversible paths. Mixed partial equality yields Maxwell relations only with appropriate regularity and natural variables. Changing variables from S to T or V to P needs a nonzero Jacobian/implicit-function theorem; at coexistence a classical invertible Legendre transformation can fail. Convex conjugates/infima with stated domains provide the global formulation.

Extensivity plus differentiability gives Euler U=T_KS−PV+Σμ_iN_i; differentiating it and the fundamental relation gives Gibbs–Duhem S dT_K−V dP+ΣN_i dμ_i=0. In a single homogeneous pure phase G=μN and Ω=−PV. These are **not universal identities for finite nonextensive systems** with surface/long-range contributions. Source §1.5 (55)–(62) derives G/N informally; explicit Euler/Gibbs–Duhem suppliers should precede consumers.

Local equilibrium stability needs constrained Hessian signs; a stationary point alone is insufficient. Positive C_V for a canonical equilibrium follows from Var(H)=k_B T_K² C_V when partition function derivatives exist and H has no T dependence. Mechanical stability in a uniform single phase has κ_T=−V^−1(∂V/∂P)_T,N>0, equivalently F_VV>0. Under differentiability and κ_T>0, C_P−C_V=T_K V α²/κ_T≥0, α=V^−1(∂V/∂T_K)_P,N. This requires fixed composition and well-defined finite derivatives; microcanonical nonadditive models can have negative heat capacity without contradicting the canonical identity. Source §§4.1,5.2,5.3; Ch1 exercise9 asks for the capacity difference.

§4.1 gives a phenomenological van der Waals equation P=NT/(V−Nb)−aN²/V² in source units, with domain V>Nb, a,b>0. It is not a controlled derivation of liquid formation from a short virial series. Spinodal slope (∂P/∂V)_T>0 diagnoses local instability; a negative slope does not distinguish global stability from metastability. Bulk two-phase equilibrium needs common T,P,μ with allowed exchanges and negligible interfaces/external-field differences. Finite interfaces can require Laplace pressure jumps; do not assert P equality across curved interfaces without ignoring surface terms.

Maxwell's equal-area construction §4.1 (9)–(11) follows from equal chemical potential and common pressure in this model. A rigorous item should prove the convex-envelope/common-tangent construction, not treat the unstable middle branch as physically attained equilibrium. Clapeyron dP_coex/dT_K=Δs/Δv requires differentiable coexistence branches and Δv≠0; latent heat per particle ℓ=T_KΔs. Gibbs phase rule f=C−R+2 is a generic dimension count requiring independent constraints, permitted exchanges, no extra fields/reactions and appropriate rank. Source (20)'s “equality at just one point”/triple-point uniqueness is not a theorem from monotonicity alone.

§§4.2–4.5 cover scalar/vector order parameters, Ising/Heisenberg models, Landau expansions, mean-field assumptions, critical exponents, transfer matrices, Metropolis and a simple RG transformation. These require separate finite lattice configuration spaces, boundary conditions, Hamiltonians and order of infinite-volume/zero-field limits. A finite Gibbs system at h=0 has zero mean Ising magnetization by spin-flip symmetry; spontaneous magnetization requires selecting a phase via limits/boundaries. Finite analyticity cannot prove a singular transition. Critical scaling relations require explicit scaling hypotheses; hyperscaling is not a universal consequence in all dimensions. Landau theory's fluctuation failure near criticality is explicitly discussed with the Levanyuk–Ginzburg criterion; it is useful model-scoped content, not a generic rigorous critical-theory supplier.

## Statistical/quantum bridge and limiting obligations

Define a classical ensemble as (Γ,Σ,ν), Γ a specified phase space, ν a probability measure. A density p is a Radon–Nikodym derivative relative to a specified measure m; differential entropy −k_B∫p logp dm needs a dimensionless density/reference scale, integrability and cannot be identified with discrete entropy without that convention. A microcanonical shell is {H∈[E,E+δE]} with finite positive measure and normalized restricted measure; the shell width, allowed constraints and whether δE scales with size must be specified. The equal-weight hypothesis is adopted. Ergodicity means invariant measurable sets have probability 0 or1 for the specified measure-preserving dynamics; it is not automatic from large N, chaos, or weak interactions.

For finite/countable discrete state set K with energies E_k, canonical p_k=e^−βE_k/Z, β=(k_BT_K)^−1, requires 0<Z=Σe^−βE_k<∞. Derivative/interchange claims require finite weighted moments or dominating bounds on a β-neighborhood. For a quantum system specify a Hilbert space H, self-adjoint Hamiltonian bounded below, trace-class e^−βH, density operator ρ=e^−βH/Z and observable A with TrρA well-defined. The trace formula/purity/quantum coherences require genuine operator definitions; nondegenerate dephasing arguments do not erase degenerate-block coherences or establish relaxation. A grand ensemble on particle-number sectors needs [H,N]=0 (or another carefully specified conserved charge), Ξ=Tr e^−β(H−μN)<∞, and species/number conventions. These are missing mathematical prerequisites, not unqualified facts established by §§2.1–2.8.

Finite-dimensional Shannon maximization under normalized probabilities gives uniform probabilities; an additional mean-energy constraint yields Gibbs weights, and strict concavity gives uniqueness when feasible interior conditions hold. Infinite countable/continuous analogues need existence/integrability arguments. Neither calculation gives dS/dt≥0 for Hamiltonian/unitary microscopic evolution: fine-grained Gibbs/von Neumann entropy is conserved under regular measure-preserving/unitary evolution. Markov coarse dynamics with detailed balance can yield monotone relative entropy to its stationary measure; Shannon entropy itself need not increase in a nonuniform Gibbs bath. A stochastic trajectory can have negative entropy-production fluctuations. A fluctuation theorem needs a specific path measure, initial ensemble, bath/protocol/time-reversal assumptions, and definition of entropy production; this textbook supplies no general fluctuation-theorem proof.

§2.4 derives a reservoir Gibbs weight by Taylor expansion of bath entropy. A controlled statement needs a remainder bound (e.g. a C² bath entropy bound across all materially occupied energies), weak interaction energy, a reservoir limit and support/tail controls. Merely E_bath≫T is not a universal error guarantee. §2.7 repeats the approximation for energy/particle exchange. Ensemble equivalence needs concavity and concentration/large-deviation or equivalent hypotheses; it can fail at nonconcavity, transitions, long-range interactions or for selected observables. §3.2's N^−1/2 replacement is heuristic, not a universal proof.

§3.1 uses classical dilute nondegenerate gas conditions, separation of translational/internal energies, large-box density of states and correct N! counting. Define λ_T=h/√(2πmk_BT_K); nλ_T³/g≪1 is the quantum dilute limit, distinct from weak interparticle interactions. Logs must have dimensionless arguments. Weyl/state-counting, Stirling remainder estimates, and uniform asymptotics should be supplied before thermodynamic limits. §§3.3–3.4 use noninteracting fermions/bosons and 3D free parabolic dispersion; BEC results are bulk/continuum-limit formulas, not exact finite-box equalities μ=0 or universal trapped-gas formulas. At finite Ξ, bosonic μ lies below the lowest energy; handle the ground state separately when taking the condensation limit. Sommerfeld expansion needs local smoothness, tail control and an explicit small-temperature remainder, not a claim for every arbitrary function.

§3.5 develops classical virial/cluster expansions heuristically. A rigorous supplier needs a measurable stable pair potential (Σpairs u≥−BN), suitable integrable Mayer function/temperedness, a specified low-activity convergence region, boundary/domain sequence, and bounds before rearranging sums and taking V,N→∞ at fixed density. The source's footnote justification by the desirable result is not such a convergence proof. The canonical log-expansion in a nominally small interaction term is not uniform in N merely because each pair interaction is short range.

## Nonequilibrium and empirical boundaries

§5.1 defines fluctuations as centered random variables; its independent finite-variance sum has VarΣX_i=NVarX_1. Covariance zero is sufficient for that variance sum but **not a definition of independence**, contrary to the source. For interacting variables retain the covariance sum. Finite-size concentration, summable correlations and nonzero mean denominator are needed for relative N^−1/2 claims; critical correlations can spoil them.

§§5.2–5.3 correctly emphasize ensemble constraints. A reservoir-set temperature in the canonical law is fixed; inferred fluctuating subsystem temperature is a separate local-equilibrium estimator/construction with internal equilibration much faster than bath exchange. It is not an additional canonical random parameter without explanation. §5.4's stationary process needs a probability space, time-shift invariant joint law and finite second moment; a mean constant alone is insufficient for stationarity. Ergodic time-average identification needs an ergodic theorem. Spectral measure existence/positivity and Fourier conventions must be stated; the source uses S(ω)=(2π)^−1∫K(t)e^(iωt)dt and one-sided per-Hz density 4πS(2πν). White noise is generalized/distributional; pointwise variance is not finite for exact white noise.

§§5.5–5.8 use equilibrium baths, weak coupling, time-scale separation, linear response, Markov/diffusion approximations, and an overdamped limit where declared. A precise Langevin equation is an SDE with chosen filtration, Wiener noise, coefficients/regularity and stochastic interpretation. A Fokker–Planck equation needs initial/boundary conditions and solution class; stationary div j=0 in more than one dimension does **not** imply constant or zero vector current. Reversible Gibbs density with j=0 is a chosen stronger condition. Kramers results are asymptotics for a specified smooth potential, nondegenerate well/barrier, bath noise and damping regime; low-damping turnover cannot be inferred by extrapolation to a dissipation-free isolated particle.

Chapter6 explicitly distinguishes stationary driven transport from equilibrium. Boltzmann's collision closure requires molecular-chaos assumptions and a specified collision kernel; an H-theorem is conditional on them, positivity, conservation and integration hypotheses. RTA is a phenomenological collision approximation; it need not preserve conserved moments unless its target distribution is chosen consistently. Energy and momentum relaxation times can differ. The source says this explicitly. Conductivity/thermoelectric models require local-equilibrium scales, weak gradients/fields, band/dispersion/relaxation assumptions and a sustained energy sink. Semiconductor device examples mix further electrostatic and quantum premises; they are downstream applications, not thermodynamic foundations.

For a Fourier-conduction topic define Ω⊂R³, time interval I, T:Ω×I→(0,∞) of declared smoothness, heat flux j_Q:Ω×I→R³ in W/m², internal energy density u(T,x) in J/m³, c=∂u/∂T in J/(m³K), conductivity tensor K(x,T) positive semidefinite in W/(m K), j_Q=−K∇T. The balance ∂_tu+divj_Q=r with specified source r, boundary and initial conditions is conditional on rigid/local-equilibrium continuum assumptions. For no volume work and compatible entropy relation, entropy flux j_Q/T and production −j_Q·∇T/T²=(∇T)·K∇T/T²≥0 follow. This is deterministic model production, not a monotone stochastic entropy on every trajectory. Source §6.5 (114)–(119) and §6.6 exercise14; T-independent capacity is an additional assumption if writing u=cT.

The source discusses experiments qualitatively (gas velocities, metal capacity, BEC figures, Johnson noise/CMB) and cites selected studies. **No primary empirical papers were retrieved/read here.** Do not mint experiment items from this review alone with invented sampling, calibration, precision or independence. Reported table uncertainties/estimates need original experimental sources and apparatus assumptions. Agreement cannot prove the entire framework. Landauer/Szilard arguments (§2.3) require explicit memory, bath, measurement/feedback/reset cycle, initial distribution and work conventions; getting information does not universally cost k_BT ln2, and resetting a biased/correlated memory has different qualified bounds.

## Concrete source problems and safeguards

These findings are local mathematical/source checks, not independent proof certification.

1. **Original and adaptation: GL energy negative gradient.** Original (4.56), PDF123/printed Ch4 p17, visually shows −ℏ²/(2m)| (∇−iqA/ℏ)ψ |² with positive m. At A=0, arbitrarily rapid smooth oscillations at fixed amplitude drive the energy down without bound. A variational minimum and the displayed positive-stiffness physics require a positive gradient coefficient. The original also has a conjugation/sign issue to review in current (4.57a); no corrected full GL theorem was proved here.
2. **Original and adaptation: crossover reciprocal.** Original (5.161)–(5.163), PDF177/Ch5 p35, visually gives thermal exponent U0/T and tunneling exponent 36U0/(5ℏω0), then T_c=(36/5)ℏω0. Matching the two leading exponents gives T_c=(5/36)ℏω0. Prefactors/turnover remain additional qualifications.
3. **Original and adaptation: independence vs uncorrelatedness.** §5.1, (10), calls zero covariance a definition of independence; this is false. E.g. symmetric X supported on {−1,0,1} and Y=X² are uncorrelated but dependent. Variance summation only needs uncorrelatedness.
4. **Adaptation-only confirmed transcription errors:** §1.2 (7) has plus instead of minus; (10) has addition instead of multiplication; (12) replaces equality by a minus; §2.4 (65) reverses the exponent relative to Z normalization. Original (2.65), PDF48/Ch2 p20, visually correctly writes exp((F−E_m)/T). §5.6 (133) wrong negative well curvature and (134) positive Gaussian exponent are corrected in original PDF172/Ch5 p30. §5.8 (185) has positive exponential and negative variance; original PDF181/Ch5 p39 visually has 1−e^(−2Γτ). These must not be attributed to latest original as defects.
5. **Additional adaptation issues:** §1.6 (68) equality malformed; §2.8 (118) exponent parentheses malformed; §3.1 (12) n! vs N!; several h/ℏ confusions; §6.4 valence dispersion/charge-density/screening equations severely malformed. Source text is not ready for literal harvesting.
6. **Conceptual gap:** §2.2 maximization proves an equilibrium variational fact, not general temporal entropy increase. §2.3's statement that gaining any information has nonzero energy cost conflates measurement with cyclic memory reset; qualify, derive, or replace.
7. **Monte Carlo gap:** §4.5 says to approximate Z by partial sum over visited states. A Gibbs-biased trajectory is not an unbiased list of states for unweighted Z summation. Need an actual justified estimator/reweighting or thermodynamic integration, irreducibility/aperiodicity and sampling error treatment. Detailed balance is sufficient for invariance, not alone convergence or an absolute partition-function estimate. Latest original PDF137 contains the same partial-sum wording (located, but that entire page was not independently audited).
8. **Third law:** §1.3 treats S→0 as merely convention and §1.6 demonstrates unattainability only for restricted heat-capacity/cycle assumptions. S→0 requires scope (e.g. equilibrium perfect crystal/unique ground state); finite degeneracy gives k_B ln g residual entropy. Parameter-independent zero-T entropy differences and unattainability are distinct statements, not automatically equivalent. Thermodynamic entropy's additive calibration does not erase physically relevant degeneracy or arbitrary state-dependent residual entropy.
