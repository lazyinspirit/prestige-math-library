## Chosen framework and exact objects

For a simple compressible equilibrium model, first specify a connected state domain D⊂R^(r+2) with coordinates (U,V,N1,…,Nr), V>0 and positive species amounts, and entropy S:D→R. Use an open homogeneous cone for differentiable single-phase calculations; coexistence and boundary states need separate charts/branches or a nonsmooth convex formulation. Particle counts are integers in finite microscopic models; continuousN is a macroscopic interpolation/limit assumption, not literal differentiation on integers. Choose S∈C² on a phase chart, S_U>0 for the positive-temperature branch, and explicitly adopt concavity, extensivity and additive composition where needed. Equilibrium under specified admissible exchanges maximises the sum of subsystem entropies at fixed total constraints. These requirements are adopted macroscopic model assumptions; Ackland motivates them, but does not prove a general entropy representation theorem.

Temperature T=(S_U)^−1:D→(0,∞), pressure P=T S_V:D→R, and chemical potentials µi=−T S_Ni:D→R are scalar state functions on that chart. SI dimensions: U inJ; V inm³; entropy inJ K^−1; T inK; P inPa=J m^−3; µi inJ per particle if Ni is a dimensionless count, orJ mol^−1 if ni is a mole amount. Never mix these conventions: N=NA n, R=NA kB. kB has unitsJ K^−1 and is exact1.380649×10^−23J K^−1 in currentSI; NA is exact6.02214076×10^23mol^−1. Some source values/metrological descriptions predate the revisedSI and must not be copied as current definitions.

Internal energyU is primitive as a calibrated scalar state potential in the macroscopic first-law formulation, up to a consistent additive reference. In a statistical model it may instead be defined as an expectation of a specified microscopic Hamiltonian; that identification is an additional bridge assumption. Pressure has an operational force-per-area meaning plus the thermodynamic derivative definition; equality requires the mechanical coupling model. Zeroth-law thermal equilibrium should be defined as a relation on prepared equilibrium systems under a specified diathermal coupling. Reflexivity/symmetry/transitivity make equivalence classes; a real-valued ordered smooth temperature representation needs further assumptions. Transitivity alone is insufficient to conjure its differentiable numerical scale.

A reversible equilibrium protocol is a piecewiseC¹ curve γ:[0,1]→D plus coupling/constraint information allowing reversal of both system and surroundings. Quasistatic progression alone does not guarantee this. In the closed simple pressure-work model define work **on** the system as W_on[γ]=−∫γP dV and heat into it as Q_rev[γ]=∫γT dS; both inJ. These are functions of protocols, generally not functions of endpoints. Their sum is ΔU by the equilibrium identity. For an actual irreversible protocol, specify external controls, boundary forces and time-resolved energy transfer: when piston balance is appropriate W_on=−∫P_ext(t)V'(t)dt, and Q=ΔU−W_on for a closed system after including every other work channel. There is no general license to put the nonequilibrium system pressure into an equilibrium line integral. Conversely, irreversible dynamics can certainly have time integrals; the source's broad contrary wording is not a theorem.

For open systems state the matter/energy-flux accounting explicitly. The equilibrium differential µi dNi is not automatically all the energy carried by an actual entering stream. Other work coordinates Xi and forcesYi require dU=T dS−P dV+Σµi dNi+ΣYi dXi, with productunitsJ, coordinate domains and force sign fixed. Magnetic/electrical conventions depend on which field/source energy lies inside the system; Ackland's non-SI weak-magnetization model must not be silently imported intoSI or fullEM.

## Postulates, conditional deductions and restricted models

Ackland topic01 adopts relaxation to equilibrium and describes uniformity; scope must exclude unresolved external fields, persistent currents and metastable preparations, and allow piecewise homogeneous phase coexistence. Topic03 adopts path-independent adiabatic work/energy conservation and defines heat by the remaining transfer. Topics04–06 adopt Kelvin–Planck/Clausius restrictions and deduce Carnot/Clausius consequences conditional on ideal scalable reversible devices and reservoirs. Topic15 gives several third-law formulations. These are physical assumptions with empirical motivation, not consequences of pure mathematics or authority.

Ideal gasPV=NkBT, constantCV, vdW parameters, Fourier heat conduction, Curie susceptibility, Schottky heat-capacity form, isotropic photon pressureP=u/3, ideal/dilute solution activities and neglected surface energies are separate restricted model premises. They must not become universal postulates. For example, an EOSP(T,V,N) alone ordinarily leaves an arbitrary caloric function ofT: the identity U_V=T P_T−P integrates only theV-dependence. A complete fundamental potential supplies the remaining information.

The reversible caloric entropy construction requires reachable endpoints and path-independent ∫δQ_rev/T on the selected connected domain. Ackland's finite Carnot composition is useful, but its passage to arbitrary continuously varying reservoirs is an informal limiting argument. Either provide convergence hypotheses/proof or adopt the entropy/fundamental-relation formulation first. Do not assert entropy existence because a sketch tiled a planar cycle, especially with more work/species coordinates.

Nernst's statement concerns vanishing **entropy differences** between appropriate equilibrium states asT↓0. Planck's zero entropy convention concerns perfect equilibrium crystals with appropriate nondegeneracy. Unattainability concerns a specified class of finite processes; it is not automatically equivalent to either statement. For a finite quantum Hamiltonian with ground multiplicityg and a canonical zero-temperature limit, S_vN→kB ln g; a unique ground state gives0. Interchange of system-size and temperature limits can matter. Glass residual entropy and frozen disorder require preparation/equilibration times; they do not automatically refute an equilibrium postulate. Vanishing differences alone do not justify interchanging limits and derivatives to conclude expansivity/heat-capacity limits; add uniformity, regularity and denominator conditions.

## Potentials, Maxwell relations and stability

On a regular entropy chart withT>0, invert S(U,V,N) locally to U(S,V,N). The implicit-function theorem needsS_U≠0. Then dU=T dS−P dV+Σµi dNi. Define H=U+PV, F=U−TS, G=U−TS+PV, and grand potentialΩ=U−TS−ΣµiNi. They have unitsJ. Their natural coordinates are respectively(S,P,N), (T,V,N), (T,P,N), (T,V,µ) only where the required partial gradient-coordinate maps are invertible. Euler degree-one homogeneity yields U=TS−PV+ΣµiNi and henceG=ΣµiNi andΩ=−PV; surface/long-range contributions and finite-size terms can invalidate exact homogeneity.

For aC² potentialΦ(x), equality of mixed second derivatives gives Maxwell relations. Examples at fixedN: T_V|S=−P_S|V; S_V|T=P_T|V; T_P|S=V_S|P; S_P|T=−V_T|P. Require the stated chart and regularity; do not differentiate through a first-order cusp as ifC². A closedC¹ one-form is locally exact, but global exactness needs a domain condition such as simple connectivity or vanishing periods. The source's unconditional closed↔exact assertion needs that correction. Coordinate reciprocal/cyclic formulas require denominators/Jacobians not zero.

For convex differentiableU, the minimum formulation F(T,V,N)=inf_S[U(S,V,N)−TS] has a stationary minimizer satisfyingU_S=T, assuming the infimum is attained. Strict curvatureU_SS>0 gives local smooth invertibility; multivariable transformations require the relevant Hessian block nonsingular. At coexistence use convex conjugates/subgradients and explicitly defined mixtures; smooth Legendre inversion need not exist. Total degree-one homogeneity itself gives a Hessian zero mode, so global positive-definite Hessians of all extensive coordinates are not appropriate. Concavity ofS is a global mixing inequality; a negative-semidefinite Hessian is itsC² local expression, and additional zero modes may exist. A stationary point need not be a stable minimum, and a positive-definite constrained Hessian is sufficient local stability, not an exhaustive characterization of all stable degenerate minima.

Combined system/bath entropy provides the potential inequalities: with constantT0,P0, only pressure work and closed material balance, Δ[U−T0S+P0V]≤0. If endpoint systemT,P equal bath values this becomesΔG≤0. Additional useful work obeys W_useful,out≤−Δ[U−T0S+P0V]. Fixed reservoir chemical potentials add−Σµ0iNi. This is a complete conditional accounting statement, not a claim that arbitrary real protocols attain the reversible bound. Entropy additivity presupposes negligible interaction/interface entropy or correct inclusion of it.

UnderT>0,V>0, differentiability and stable nondegenerate response, CV=T S_T|V, CP=T S_T|P, κT=−V^−1V_P|T, β=V^−1V_T|P yield CP−CV=TVβ²/κT and CP/CV=κT/κS. EqualityCP=CV is possible; strict inequality needsβ≠0. Negative microcanonical heat capacities in nonadditive long-range models do not contradict a theorem whose premises are additive homogeneous equilibrium and canonical stability. Ackland topic14 appropriately flags self-gravity as outside its simple stability argument.

## Phase equilibrium, reactions and finite limits

Define a phase branch by a stated homogeneous equilibrium fundamental potential and its domain. At fixedT,P with a conserved component, competing phases minimize totalG; variation of transferable amounts gives equal chemical potentials, with equalT/P where energy/volume exchange is allowed. For one component µ1=µ2; in multicomponent systems equalµi for **each transferable species**. FixedV permits phase fractions satisfying the volume constraint; the lower convex envelope of free energy gives stable mixtures and the lever rule. A local negative curvature marks a spinodal instability; metastability is a local minimum above the global mixture energy and requires a kinetic/nucleation discussion to predict lifetime.

If twoC¹ phase branches satisfyµ1(T,P)=µ2(T,P), differentiate this equality: (v2−v1)dP=(s2−s1)dT. To write dP/dT=(s2−s1)/(v2−v1), require nonzero volume jump and an appropriate implicit-function chart. Latent heatℓ=T(s2−s1) uses reversible conversion per consistently normalized amount. Constantℓ, ideal vapor and negligible liquid volume are approximations for integrating the Clausius form. Gibbs phase rule is a dimension count **only if** constraints are independent and have the requisite rank; singular/critical points require separate analysis. Ehrenfest equations require continuous first derivatives, well-defined branch second derivatives and nonzero jump denominators, and do not classify all modern critical phenomena.

For reactions use an elemental conservation matrixA and species vectorn, withAn fixed; permitted reaction extentsξ satisfydn=ν dξ andAν=0. Stationarity at fixedT,P givesν·µ=0 for every independent allowed reaction. Ackland's single scalar “atom conservation” equation forH2/O2/H2O is incorrect; two elemental constraints leave one stoichiometric reaction direction. Gibbs–Duhem constrains variations of intensive variables through homogeneity; it does not replace the reaction-affinity stationarity condition.

A finite microscopic canonical system with finitely many levels has Z(β)=Σexp(−βEi)>0 and an analytic free energy for finite realβ in its allowed range. Its exact finite partition sum does not have the ideal sharp nonanalytic phase transition of a thermodynamic limit. Define that limit by a sequenceN,V→∞ at fixed density and specified interactions/boundary conditions; existence, boundary independence and differentiability require actual theorems. Short-range stable additive interactions are relevant sufficient hypotheses in established treatments, but **neither the supplemental Ackland corpus nor the Arovas sketches prove all those theorems**. Its critical exponents are illustrative examples, not universal values. Do not infer them fromN being numerically large.

## Entropy functions and statistical scope

Entropy variants have different domains. MacroscopicS:D→R is an equilibrium state function. Given a measurable microstate spaceΓ, reference measurem, and macro-mapM:Γ→K, Boltzmann entropy is S_B(x)=kB ln[m(M^−1(M(x)))/m_ref] when a finite positive cell measure and dimensionless normalization are specified. A discrete macrocell countW giveskB lnW. The Gibbs entropy of a distributionp on a finite set is S_G(p)=−kBΣp_i ln p_i, with0ln0=0; a density version depends on a specified reference measure and integrability. Quantum vonNeumann entropy acts on a density operatorρ≥0, Trρ=1: S_vN(ρ)=−kB Tr(ρ lnρ), when finite. Shannon entropyH(p)=−Σp_i log_b p_i is information inbits/nats, not by units alone a calorimetric state function. Equality of selected values needs a model and bridge argument, not synonymy. Fine-grained Gibbs/vonNeumann entropy can remain constant under isolated reversible Hamiltonian/unitary dynamics while a coarse macro-entropy changes.

Equiprobability of an isolated energy shell, a maximum-entropy inference rule, dynamical ergodicity, mixing and equipartition are different premises/results. Define ergodicity of a measure-preserving flow by invariant measurable sets having measure0or1; reachability is insufficient. For Hamiltonian phase flow use a regular Hamiltonian and Liouville measure; equipartition requires canonical weights, integration-by-parts boundary/integrability conditions and a classical quadratic term. Neither is “every atom has the same energy.” A quantum statistical calculation needs a specified Hilbert space,Hamiltonian,spectrum, preparation and measurement probabilities. Arovas chapters1,3–5 discuss these objects, but their rigorous probability, dynamical and operator-theoretic suppliers must still be proved independently; Ackland topic15 alone does not provide them.

For a finite canonical model prepared in weak thermal contact with an ideal fixedT bath, p_i=e^−βEi/Z, β=1/(kBT), assume Hamiltonian independent ofT and permitted differentiation: Var(H)=∂β²lnZ=kBT²CV. This conditional algebra identifies an **energy** variance; it does not define a universal temperature fluctuation. Forgrandcanonical number fluctuations use explicitly exchangingN, weights exp[−β(E−µN)], convergence and differentiation, yielding Var(N)=kBT ∂⟨N⟩/∂µ at fixedT,V. Neither formula describes an arbitrary time series or nonequilibrium preparation. Canonical/microcanonical ensemble equivalence is not exact for finite systems; limiting agreement requires a specified observable class, concave differentiable limiting entropy and concentration/large-deviation arguments. Coexistence, criticality, nonconcave entropy or long-range interactions can obstruct the naïve bridge. These are open rigorous suppliers here. Arovas chapter4 provides finite-bath expansions and model examples, but not a general proof of ensemble equivalence.

N^−1/2 uncertainty requires independent or adequately controlled correlated samples and finite variance; near criticality correlation lengths/response can grow. Preparation,bath,protocol,measurement function and sampling probability model precede any statistical claim. Ackland's Wang reference alone lacks apparatus/calibration/data/statistical detail here, so no experiment item is authorized by this reading. Finite agreement does not prove a law; finite negative entropy-production trajectories in a stochastic small-system model do not automatically contradict an idealized macroscopic monotonicity postulate. A fluctuation relation would require its actual theorem hypotheses and primary experiment reading.

Natural units may write s=S/kB and θ=kBT, with dimensionlesss and energy-valuedθ; restore kB in β,T derivatives, heat capacities and covariances. Logs need dimensionless probabilities or ratios. Never read suppressedkB=1 as a loss of the kelvin dimension.

## Arovas microscopic frameworks, bridges and limitations

For a classical model first define a measurable phase space Gamma_N with coordinates(x,p), a sufficiently regular Hamiltonian H_N:Gamma_N→R measured inJ, controls lambda and a reference measure dm_N=dx dp/(N!h^(dN)) (dimensionless after the stated units). Boundary conditions and allowed energies are part of the model. For an isolated shell A_E,Delta={E≤H_N<E+Delta}, specify Delta inJ and finite positive shell measure; the normalized shell distribution is an equiprobability premise. The DOS D(E)=∫delta(E−H_N)dm_N has unitsJ^−1; logD(E) requires a fixed energy reference/width. Regular shell measures use the coarea factor1/|gradH|. Arovas4.2 derives shell entropy/DOS examples and finite size logarithmic corrections, and4.3 distinguishes a small system from a finite comparable bath (printed183–195). The usual canonical exponential follows an expansion of bath entropy under weak coupling, fixed total energy and a bath much larger than the subsystem; retaining finite-bath curvature gives corrections. These are controlled only with explicit differentiability, capacity/size scaling and error hypotheses; equal systems do not automatically have Gibbs marginal weights.

For a quantum model define a complex Hilbert space H, a self-adjoint Hamiltonian operator with specified domain, density operator rho positive trace class of trace1, and observables with finite expectations. Canonical rho_beta=exp(−beta H)/Z requires Z=Tr exp(−beta H)<infinity, beta=1/(kBT) inJ^−1 and admissible differentiation. Quantum energy is Tr(rho H). A lower spectral bound alone does not ensure finiteZ. With commuting conserved N and H the grand trace exp[−beta(H−mu N)] defines a distinct prepared ensemble; noncommuting constraints need an explicitly chosen construction. Arovas4.4–4.7 use these traces, coherent states/semiclassical approximations, canonical, grand canonical and isothermal-isobaric volume integration. Reference volume in the latter affects subextensive constants and must be stated. Finite canonical variance identity is exact under these conditions; energy-shell/statistical entropy equality and extensivity are separate limiting bridges. The source's level-population heat/work split (printed200) is a restricted diagonal slow-control model; coherent states, changing bases, interactions and actual control definitions require more care.

Microstate flow g_t preserving dm is a dynamical model, not a probability preparation. Liouville invariance follows smooth Hamiltonian flow; Poincare recurrence needs finite invariant measure and an almost-everywhere theorem, not bounded position alone. Ergodicity means invariant measurable sets have measure0or1, and mixing means correlations of specified integrable observables factor as t→infinity. Arovas3.3–3.4 carefully separates ergodicity/mixing and gives an ergodic nonmixing torus example; its recurrence sketch establishes less than its broad prose claims. Time averages require an ergodic theorem and integrability. Quantum diagonal-ensemble dephasing requires nondegenerate spectral gaps/energies as appropriate and an observable topology. Eigenstate thermalization (3.5–3.6) is explicitly a hypothesis, not a general proof of all Hamiltonians thermalizing. Fine-grained entropy conservation is compatible with reduced/coarse entropy increase; one must name the state map and entropy functional.

Phase models in chapters5–7 support sharply scoped examples. Bose/Fermi distributions depend on the Fock statistics and noninteracting Hamiltonian. Bose trace convergence imposes mu<minimum single-particle energy at finite volume; condensation requires a separate ground-state occupation and an integrable continuum excited density in a declared thermodynamic sequence. Condensate fraction, superfluid response and entropy are different objects; Arovas explicitly distinguishes condensate and superfluid fractions in real helium (printed279–282). Finite Ising Gibbs distributions at zero field are symmetry invariant; spontaneous order requires infinite-volume/boundary-selected states or N→infinity before external field→0 (6.2.1 printed319–320). Exact finite transfer sums and their asymptotic limits should be separately named. Mayer graph expansion assumes integrable tails and additionally needs stability/convergence/limit control. Lee–Yang finite polynomial positivity forbids positive-real zeros, but limiting pressure/differentiability requires the omitted theorem hypotheses. Kramers–Wannier algebra omits torus winding sectors and uniqueness before identifying self-dual criticality. Mean field and Landau truncations drop fluctuations/higher terms; they do not furnish universal critical exponents. An exact Hubbard–Stratonovich representation is not an exact truncated GL theory.

For a kinetic model define a nonnegative one-particle density f(r,p,t) relative to dr dp with units(action)^−d, specified initial data and boundary conditions, nonnegative collision rates and conservation/symmetry assumptions. Boltzmann's molecular-chaos incoming-pair factorization is an additional closure of the reversible BBGKY hierarchy; Arovas8.10 explicitly shows post-collision factorization would reverse the H theorem (printed534). H[f]=∫f log(f a^d)dr dp, with action referencea, is a one-particle functional; its negative derivative under the selected collision dynamics does not imply fine-grained entropy monotonicity. The limit from Hamiltonian hard spheres needs initial chaos, recollision control and time windows; cited Lanford/Bodineau papers were not themselves read. Relaxation time models can violate conserved moments; the supplied projected model−gamma(I−P) repairs those moments, not every model error. Linear response/Onsager reciprocity requires an equilibrium preparation and microscopic reversal convention. Heat currents, entropy currents and stochastic work must not be inferred universally from a single local equilibrium differential.

For stochastic models first specify(Omega,F,P), filtration(F_t), adapted random variables X_t, state space, transition kernels, initial distribution, external protocol and bath. White Gaussian forcing is a generalized process; an equation with it needs an Ito/Stratonovich interpretation and stochastic integration, especially with multiplicative noise. Arovas9 specifies noise mean/covariance and uses equipartition to fix damping/noise balance in the Langevin model; that is a bath assumption rather than experimental verification. First-order equations alone do not prove Markovity with colored noise, and the position-only inertial process is not Markov. Relative entropy contraction does not by itself give strict convergence to a unique equilibrium. A variance calculation gives L² convergence of one time-average, not all almost-sure ergodicity. Diffusion approximations have infinite propagation tails; compact-support nonlinear fronts need not respect a universal finite speed. Statistical laws here require system, preparation, bath, protocol, observable and probability measure; finite data never supply universal logical proof.
