# Lieb–Yngvason: rigorous equilibrium thermodynamics

## Source and completed scope

I retrieved and read the complete official [arXiv cond-mat/9708200v2 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/9708200v2), *The Physics and Mathematics of the Second Law of Thermodynamics*, Elliott H. Lieb and Jakob Yngvason, version 28 January 1999, scheduled on its title page for *Physics Reports* 310, 1–96 (1999). This is a complete foundational monographic review, not a comprehensive thermal-physics course. The retrieved document has 101 pages: main text I–VII pp1–87, symbols/index pp88–90, references pp91–93, eight figure sheets pp94–101. No appendix exists in this version. The source explicitly studies equilibrium states of macroscopic scalable systems; paths between endpoints can be violent and nonequilibrium. It neither constructs nonequilibrium entropy nor invokes statistical mechanics (abstract; I.A pp3–6; II.A pp13–15). Small systems and gravitating bodies need changed axioms. It gives no exact operational size cutoff and no measured dataset.

## Defined objects, formulation and dimensions

For smooth reversible curves γ:I→Γ on an adiabat, the source's consistent sign convention is dU=−ΣPi dVi and dS=(dU+ΣPi dVi)/T. This requires γ at least C1, entropy C1, and fixed-system equilibrium coordinates. A claim of Maxwell relations based on equality of mixed second derivatives requires additional C2 regularity on the region considered; the established C1 entropy theorem alone is insufficient.

## Adopted physical assumptions and actual deductions

A1 reflexivity; A2 transitivity; A3 consistency X≺X′ and Y≺Y′ ⇒(X,Y)≺(X′,Y′); A4 positive scaling invariance; A5 splitting X∼A(tX,(1−t)X), 0<t<1; A6 stability: if (X,εkZ0)≺(Y,εkZ1) for fixed Z0,Z1 and εk>0 tending to zero, then X≺Y (pp20–21). These are postulates when asserted of physical accessibility. A6 does not mean arbitrary norm convergence of all state coordinates; its exact quantifiers matter.

CH is an extra assumption in II. It is needed on multiple scaled products, not just Γ. Thm2.2 (p23) compares (t1Y1,…,tNYN) with (t′1Y′1,…,t′MY′M) only when all scales positive and Σti=Σt′j. The iff is ΣtiSΓ(Yi)≤Σt′jSΓ(Y′j). It does not assert free conversion of different matter amounts. Lemma2.1 (p24) bounds the supremum using stability and CH; Lemma2.3 (pp25–26) proves X∼A((1−λ)X0,λX1) at λ=SΓ(X), with supremum approximation plus A6. Lemma2.2 converts accessibility of reference mixtures to λ ordering; Thm2.3 (pp26–27) then gives uniqueness up to S↦aS+B, a>0. Monotone transformations preserve a single-system order but usually destroy additivity. The single Γ order alone does not determine additive entropy.

Thm2.1 cancellation (p21) follows from A1–A6 by running repeated scaled spectator-assisted conversion and then shrinking spectator size. It is a deduction, not an additional postulate in this formulation. A7 adds convex combination access (tX,(1−t)Y)≺tX+(1−t)Y (p31); given entropy representation it yields concavity (Thm2.8 p32). Coordinates matter: replacing (U,V) by (T,P) does not preserve this axiom automatically.

Simple-system postulates (pp39–42) are S1 every state has a strict successor, S2 unique finite-slope support plane with locally Lipschitz pressure, S3 arcwise-connected relative boundary ∂AX, AX={Y∈Γ:X≺Y}. Boundaries/interiors are relative to open Γ. Under A1–A7 and S1–S3, Thm3.7 (pp48–49) derives nested sectors and CH **inside Γ**. Theorem3.5's boundary graph uX on open connected projection ρX solves ∂Vi uX=−Pi(uX,V), Eq3.17, and uniqueness needs Lipschitz ODE reasoning. CH for products has not yet followed.

Thermal assumptions T1/T2 adopt thermal join, accessible equilibration and reversible splitting; T3 adopts transitive thermal equilibrium (zeroth law); T4 says each X has X0≺≺X≺≺X1 with X0∼T X1 (pp51–54). T4 entails S1; it is not equivalent merely to existence of an isotherm somewhere. Under A1–A7/S1–S3/T1–T4, Thm4.8 (p59) derives CH on arbitrary scaled products; Thm2.5 (pp28–29) calibrates additive/extensive entropy across systems. Its general coherent-reference construction invokes AC/Hamel bases, or explicitly replaces this with unique decomposition into elementary systems; carry whichever route is used. Additivity does not mean subsystem entropy separately increases: compound interactions can lower one while total increases.

T5 (p54) adds that every state has a thermally equivalent state in each other system at every allowed work coordinate. This universal range assumption is additional to Thm4.8 and is used in single-valued continuous temperature and C1 entropy, Thms5.1–5.3 (pp64–66). Concavity initially gives one-sided slopes and intervals [T−,T+], not automatically differentiability. Given consistent S1/S2, thermal equilibrium is precisely maximization of S1(W,V1)+S2(Utotal−W,V2) over W for which both states lie in their respective Γ (Thm4.3 pp53–54). Subsequent equal-T equilibrium results require the full regularity/thermal assumptions.

Planck's principle (Thm3.4 pp45–46) says same-work-coordinate accessibility iff final U≥initial U with selected positive orientation; physically its no-work-extraction interpretation also uses energy conservation and the work/weight sign convention. Thm5.4 (pp66–67) derives energy direction for an isolated equilibration of two simple systems at fixed work coordinates/total energy, initially T1>T2: U′1<U1 and U′2>U2. It is an endpoint result, not a time-resolved heat-current equation.

For the two-reservoir cyclic machine pp67–68, let Q1>0 be hot-reservoir delivered energy, Q0<0 cold-reservoir delivered energy (thus cold absorbs −Q0), machine returns to initial state, combined steps are adiabatic, and all endpoint states satisfy the entropy framework. **The source's claimed finite-reservoir final-temperature extension fails:** Eq5.7 uses ΔS_res≤−Q/T_final. For differentiable concave S, the tangent inequality at the initial U_i instead gives S(U_f)−S(U_i)≤(U_f−U_i)/T_i=−Q/T_i. The tangent inequality at final U_f gives the reversed inequality ΔS_res≥−Q/T_f. Consequently the printed Eq5.7 cannot establish its final-temperature Eq5.10. Using INITIAL reservoir temperatures, the valid upper bounds combine with total entropy nondecrease and cyclic machine entropy cancellation to give Q1/T1_initial+Q0/T0_initial≤0 and η=(Q1+Q0)/Q1≤1−T0_initial/T1_initial. η is dimensionless. Ideal constant-temperature reservoirs permit the ordinary Carnot bound because initial and final T agree; equality needs reversibility/saturation conditions. Do not accept the source's final-temperature finite-reservoir statement as a proved result.

An independently checked conditional arithmetic witness illustrates the failure. Adopt two simple reservoirs with equal constant heat capacity C>0, U=C T plus a constant, and S=C ln(T/T_ref) plus a constant for T>0, where T_ref>0 is fixed and units are kelvin. Start at T_h,i=400 K, T_c,i=300 K; take T_h,f=350 K, T_c,f=(2400/7) K. Their temperature product is unchanged, so total entropy change is zero. Q_h=50 C K and Q_c=−(300/7) C K yield work-out=(50/7) C K, η=1/7, while 1−T_c,f/T_h,f=1/49. Thus the claimed final-temperature bound would exclude entropy-neutral endpoint energy accounting that its entropy premises permit. This is a hypothetical model calculation, not a measured engine realization. The initial-temperature bound is 1/4 and is respected. This check was prompted by scaffold's independent review; my initial summary had incorrectly accepted the source's extension.

## Mixing, scope failures, evidence and source defects

Composition is not mixing (VI.A pp72–73). For reactions/new substances separate additive entropy constants must be fixed. Define D(Γ,Γ′)=inf{SΓ′(Y)−SΓ(X):X∈Γ,Y∈Γ′,X≺Y}, +∞ when empty; E is infimum of sums of D over finite connecting chains; F=infΓ0 E(Γ×Γ0,Γ′×Γ0) (Eqs6.6/6.9/6.10 pp74–75). These calibrated entropy differences have entropy units; the auxiliary catalyst returns as a system, possibly in a different state. Thm6.1 gives X≺Y iff SΓ(X)+F(Γ,Γ′)≤SΓ′(Y). Axiom M excludes one-way connected-system sinks (p77). With M, Thm6.2 (pp78–79) obtains additive/extensive entropy monotonically nondecreasing across mixing/reactions through a Hahn–Banach sandwich; it does not generally establish strict increase for every irreversible transition or universal iff from entropy alone. The no-gap good-case Eq6.34 (p80), or specially justified finite/countable strict-extension treatment (pp81–82), is extra work. The review's empirical assertion of the good case is not measured data supplied here. Do not turn its informal “92 constants” discussion into an exact current nuclear/species theorem.

The thermometer world (IV.C pp59–61) satisfies other favorable axioms but violates T4 and fails product CH. The candidate log U entropy inequality is necessary and insufficient for its restricted operations. It is a useful conditional counterexample, not an experiment. The spin example p44 explains failure of S1 at maximal entropy if one includes both positive and negative temperature branches; one cannot use this paper to dismiss all negative-temperature statistical models.

The review is mathematical/phenomenological. Operational observations would be specified endpoint preparations, device restoration, mechanical displacement and equilibrium criteria with calibration errors. Model predictions are exact conditional relations once the stated axioms hold. Statistical inference about how well observations satisfy those predictions requires an independent measurement/sampling model, repeated data, systematic errors and uncertainty; none is supplied here. Finite measurements do not prove universal postulates. Rare fluctuations in finite stochastic systems do not automatically contradict this macroscopic equilibrium/order idealization; a comparison requires a precise map to the macroscopic state variables and regime plus the competing stochastic law. No fluctuation theorem, finite-system entropy-production probability, calorimetry dataset or third-law theorem was retrieved/read from this source.

Rendered original has typographical errors that matter for harvesting: p41 Eq3.6 and p63 adiabat equations print +P rather than −P, whereas plane Eq3.3 and boundary Eq3.17 imply −P; p67 prints 1/T=(∂US)^−1 rather than T=(∂US)^−1 and refers to “convexity” of S where concavity is needed. More substantially, its Eq5.7 reverses the concavity bound when using the final reservoir temperature, invalidating the stated finite-reservoir final-temperature extension; see the independent inequality calculation above. These are confirmed in the PDF, not only extraction artifacts. Other internal reference slips include p69 “S4” for boundary-connectedness S3 and p86 calling stability “A3” instead of A6. On p76 remark2 prints D≤F while definitions and adjoining proofs give F≤D. Use the precise earlier assumptions and independently reconstructed argument, not transcription. A local author must also repair/prove abbreviated calculus, ODE and Hahn–Banach steps before production acceptance; full reading is no such acceptance.
