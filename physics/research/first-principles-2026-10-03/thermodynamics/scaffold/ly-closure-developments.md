## L0. Exact system operations and hypotheses

A primitive simple type i has a nonempty open convex set Γ_i⊂R^(n_i+1), n_i≥1, with numerical coordinates (u,v). The coordinates have separately fixed reference units; u is later J, ordinary volume later m³. A scaled type t i, t>0, has its distinct tagged copy tΓ_i and bijection x↦tx. A separated compound is a finite tuple of such copies, with permutation/reassociation identifications and an empty unit type whose single state has entropy zero. Types and states are distinct objects. A1–A6 mean exactly M11's reflexivity/transitivity/consistency/scaling/splitting/stability, but apply to the entire declared family, including different endpoint types when mixing is admitted. In a simple coordinate domain A7 is (tx,(1−t)y)≺tx+(1−t)y for 0<t<1. The relation is a preorder; x~y means both directions and x≺≺y means only the forward direction. Cancellation is M11's proof, which does not require CH.

For each x∈Γ let A_x={y:x≺y}. S1 says some y satisfies x≺≺y. S2 says there is exactly one supporting hyperplane to A_x at x, of the form u−u_x+P(x)·(v−v_x)=0, with finite slope P:Γ→R^n locally Lipschitz. The supporting plane is the geometric set, not a chosen normal multiple; it exists at x and therefore x is not an interior point of A_x. S3 says the relative boundary ∂_ΓA_x is arcwise connected. Relative interior/closure/boundary are in Γ.

A thermal join J_ij has coordinates (u,v_i,v_j), u=u_i+u_j, over the full set where such component energies exist, and is itself a simple system satisfying A7/S1–S3. T1 permits (x,y)≺J(x,y). T2 says every joined state z has a reversible splitting z~(x,y) at the same total energy/work coordinates; the equal-state scaled splitting stipulated in source PDF52 is included. Thermal equilibrium x~T y means (x,y)~J(x,y); T3 is transitivity, and T4 supplies x_0≺≺x≺≺x_1 with x_0~T x_1. Scaling invariance x~T y⇒tx~T sy follows from T2's equal-state split, A4/A5 and T3. T5, where used later, is the stated universal thermal-equilibrium range at each work coordinate; it is not needed for the strip comparison argument.

**Contact coherence interface to be checked.** The source's calibrator proof additionally uses the statement that a joined equilibrium state is thermally equivalent to each of its equilibrium constituents, J(x,y)~T x~T y when x~T y. This does not follow from the mere set formula u=u_x+u_y; it concerns the accessibility relation on joins-of-joins. An explicit operational contact model may adopt it as join coherence, or supply a deduction from its nested-join identifications. L7 records precisely where it is needed. No claim below silently derives it from Cartesian product definitions.

## L0a. Explicit adopted contact branch and consistency witness

For the completed contact branch adopt **C1 equilibrium-join coherence**: if x~T y, then J(x,y)~T x and J(x,y)~T y. Also use T5's universal thermal range, already separately formulated by LY, for the calibrator existence proof below. C1 is a precise physical operation postulate about repeated contact of an equilibrated compound with a constituent; it is motivated by the equilibrium interpretation of a thermal join in LY PDF51 and is used implicitly in PDF59's constituent-to-join thermal equivalence. It is not a mathematical corollary of the energy-coordinate sum and not a substitute for a missing convex/ODE theorem. The completed physical result will explicitly carry C1 and T5. The narrower bare T1–T4 source assertion remains distinguished.

An actual consistency witness is a family of ideal positive-temperature logarithmic fundamental functions. For finitely many named bodies, give each a heat parameter c_i>0 in J/K and a volume coefficient r_i>0 in J/K. At fixed material amount its state set is u_i>0,v_i>0 and σ_i(u_i,v_i)=c_i log(u_i/u_(ref,i))+r_i log(v_i/v_(ref,i))+b_i, with reference values in the corresponding units. Scaling multiplies c_i,r_i,u_i,v_i and the reference extensive values and multiplies b_i, so σ_(ti)(tu,tv)=tσ_i(u,v). The definitions on the scaled labeled family are explicit; the log arguments are dimensionless. Declare accessibility on every admitted compound/thermal-join state by nondecrease of the sum of these σ values when the underlying material labels agree, and permit the corresponding auxiliary weight-energy record. This supplies a mathematical model of the operational postulates, not a measured gas experiment.

For a thermal join, total u>0 and the retained positive volume coordinates v_i are its state coordinates. Maximize Σc_i log(u_i/u_ref,i) with Σu_i=u. Strict concavity in energies and the derivative condition c_i/u_i equal give the unique positive allocation u_i=c_i u/(Σc_i). Its global maximality follows from M2's supporting inequality, and all competing positive allocations are feasible; no unattained boundary maximum is asserted. Define σ_J as the entropy sum at that allocation. It has the form (Σc_i)log u+Σr_i log v_i+constant, and its temperature is T_J=u/(Σc_i). Any two thermal-join groupings have the same allocation and entropy: first allocating to groups by their sum c, then within a group, gives each original c_i u/(Σc_i). Hence this model has an actual coherent nested join, and an equilibrium constituent has T_i=u_i/c_i=T_J. Thermal equilibrium between any two of its bodies/joins means equal such T, as checked by the same strict-concavity maximization. This proves C1 in the witness and T3 without assuming the desired conclusion there.

Every simple or joined σ is concave on its positive coordinate orthant. A forward sector is its superlevel set, convex and relatively closed, with smooth unique support and slope P_j=r_j u/[(Σc_i)v_j], locally Lipschitz. Its boundary is the graph u=constant·∏v_j^(−r_j/(Σc_i)), defined on the connected positive work orthant, so S3 holds. Increasing u gives S1. Along fixed T (hence fixed u for this material type), increasing or decreasing one volume changes σ strictly and keeps T fixed, giving T4. For any positive T at any retained work coordinates take u=TΣc_i, giving T5. Entropy ordering supplies A1–A4 and stability by taking the finite real inequality limit; splitting a state into scaled identical copies preserves σ and supplies A5; concavity supplies A7. T1 follows from the proved entropy maximum, T2 from its attained allocation, and equal-state scaling splitting is the same calculation. Thus the adopted stronger contact structure has a fully computed nondegenerate consistency witness satisfying all declared hypotheses. This witness demonstrates mathematical compatibility, not empirical universality or a derivation of all thermodynamics from an ideal-gas example.

## L1. Finite convex geometry actually needed

A nonempty convex set C⊂R^d has nonempty relative interior in its affine hull H. Choose d_H+1 affinely independent points in C spanning H. Their simplex is contained in C; in the affine coordinate chart from their difference basis, its barycenter has all barycentric coefficients positive, and sufficiently small coefficient perturbations preserve positivity. Thus it contains a relative open neighborhood of its barycenter. If H is all of R^d this is an ordinary interior. This proves the dimension/interior dichotomy without an unproved general convex theorem.

If w is in the relative interior of convex C and y∈closure C, then (1−t)w+ty∈C for 0≤t<1. For t>0 choose y_k∈C approaching y; w_k=((1−t)w+ty−ty_k)/(1−t) approaches w and lies in C for sufficiently large k. The target equals (1−t)w_k+ty_k and is therefore in C. The same argument with nearby target points proves the open segment lies in the relative interior. Bounded subsequences used here and below are supplied by M32.

A closed convex set K has a support at each boundary point x. Choose z_k∉K approaching x and nearest points a_k∈K. Nearest points exist by M13/M32 (or minimize squared distance on a large compact intersection using the reviewed extreme-value theorem). The projection inequality (z_k−a_k)·(y−a_k)≤0 for y∈K follows by expanding the distance at a_k+t(y−a_k) and t↓0. Since |z_k−a_k|≤|z_k−x|, a_k→x. Unit normals have a subsequence approaching a unit n, and the inequalities give n·(y−x)≤0 for every y∈K. For a convex set relatively closed in an open Γ, its global closure has the same local boundary at points of Γ, so this support construction applies there as well. In a proper affine subspace use its Euclidean affine chart.

**Inward-direction lemma.** Suppose K has full-dimensional interior, x∈K and exactly one supporting hyperplane at x, with inward normal n. Let T be the closed cone generated by K−x. Every nonzero functional nonnegative on T supports K at x, and therefore is a positive multiple of n. Separation from a closed convex cone by M13 implies T={v:n·v≥0}: if an excluded vector v had n·v>0, its separating functional would be a positive multiple of n and could not be negative at v. To see every v with n·v>0 yields x+tv∈Interior K for all sufficiently small t>0, take a small full-dimensional simplex of directions enclosing v inside Interior T. Approximate its vertices by directions a_j(y_j−x), y_j∈K,a_j≥0, close enough that v is still interior to their simplex. A small neighborhood of v is made of convex combinations of these finitely many directions. For t with t max a_j<1, x+tΣθ_ja_j(y_j−x) is the convex combination (1−tΣθ_ja_j)x+Σtθ_ja_jy_j∈K. This supplies a neighborhood of x+tv in K. The simplex approximation follows from solving its affine coefficient matrix: nonsingular matrices have continuously varying inverse by the determinant/adjugate formula, and strictly positive barycentric coordinates remain positive under small perturbations. In particular a finite-slope unique support at x has an interior vertical successor on one of the two energy sides.

A finite convex function f on an open convex set is locally Lipschitz. Around x_0 choose a cube whose dilate stays in its domain. Finite convexity bounds f above on that cube by the maximum of its vertex values. Reflection about x_0 gives a lower bound from f(x)+f(2x_0−x)≥2f(x_0). Thus it is bounded above/below in a ball. For x,y in a smaller ball, extend the ray from x through y by a fixed distance r within the larger ball. Convexity on that segment gives f(y)−f(x)≤(oscillation/r)|y−x|; reverse x,y to get the absolute bound. Negation supplies the concave case. This also proves the continuity used in source §IV.

## L2. Convex, closed, full-dimensional forward sectors

A_x is convex: if x≺y,z, consistency/scaling/splitting give x~(tx,(1−t)x)≺(ty,(1−t)z)≺ty+(1−t)z. Let y∈closure A_x∩Γ. Choose w in its relative interior by L1, and choose a fixed z beyond y on the line from w to y, sufficiently close to remain in Γ. For sufficiently large n, y_n=(1+1/n)y−z/n lies on [w,y) and hence in A_x by L1. A7 at the weights n/(n+1),1/(n+1), followed by scaling, gives (y_n,z/n)≺(y,y/n). Since x≺y_n, (x,z/n)≺(y,y/n). Stability with fixed auxiliaries z,y proves x≺y. The trivial case y already in A_x needs no construction. This proves relative closedness, including lower-dimensional sectors; no coordinatewise continuity of ≺ was assumed.

For three collinear points y=t x+(1−t)z, 0<t<1, y≺z implies x≺y: A7 gives (tx,(1−t)z)≺y~(ty,(1−t)y)≺(ty,(1−t)z); cancel the common z-part and scale. Also x≺z implies x≺y directly from A5/A7. These are source Lemma3.1/Eq3.9 with complete cancellation uses.

Suppose A_x has empty ambient interior. L1 places it in an affine hyperplane H, which is a support at x; S2 makes H the unique plane. If its affine hull has codimension greater than one, multiple planes through that hull already contradict S2. Otherwise its affine hull is H. S1 chooses y∈A_x with x∉A_y. A_y⊂A_x⊂H is closed relative Γ by the previous argument. If A_y has lower affine dimension in H, it again has multiple ambient supports at y. Otherwise take a relative-interior w∈A_y and the segment to x∈Γ\A_y. It encounters a relative boundary point z∈Γ∩H. L1 supplies a supporting hyperplane to A_y within H at z. Transitivity gives A_z⊂A_y, so that within-H support and H itself yield distinct ambient supporting planes to A_z at z, contradicting S2. Hence every A_x has nonempty interior. This supplies all existence steps abbreviated in source Theorem3.2.

## L3. Vertical accessibility, orientation and Planck's principle

Let a sector A_x point upward at x, meaning its inward support normal has positive energy component. Its intersection J_x with the fixed-v energy fiber is a convex relatively closed interval containing x and a positive interior successor by L1. It lies at u≥u_x because of the support at x. If it fails to contain the entire upward part of the Γ-fiber, its upper endpoint lies inside Γ and J_x is a compact nondegenerate interval. Pick its midpoint x_1. Then A_(x_1)⊂A_x and x_1 is a boundary point of its own sector by S2; the vertical fiber interval J_(x_1) lies entirely in one of the two halves of J_x, since its support has nonzero energy slope, and is closed. Repeat midpoint selection. Nested compact intervals have lengths at most 2^−k times the initial length and a common limit x_∞ inside Γ. Relative closedness puts x_∞ in every sector A_(x_k), and transitivity gives A_(x_∞)⊂A_(x_k). Its vertical slice is therefore only {x_∞}, contradicting the interior vertical-successor lemma L1. Thus J_x is the full upward Γ-fiber. The downward case is the same with energy sign reversed.

All points on a fixed energy fiber have the same orientation. If x is upward and z above x were downward, the full-fiber property would let x≺z≺w with w below x, contradicting x's support. If y below x were downward, choose z strictly above x with x≺z; the collinear lemma gives y≺x≺z, contradicting y's downward slice. The symmetric argument covers downward fibers.

Orientation is locally constant across work coordinates. Choose x and points on its vertical line, one just below its support (outside A_x) and one just above in Interior A_x by L1. Openness of Γ and of these two regions makes the same upper/lower membership hold on every sufficiently nearby vertical line. Each line therefore meets ∂A_x at a point z. A support of A_x at z also supports A_z⊂A_x and equals the S2 plane of A_z; the upper-inside/lower-outside vertical intersection forces its orientation upward. All states on that line have z's sign by the preceding paragraph. Hence nearby states have x's sign. The open convex Γ is connected (straight segments join points), so a locally constant two-valued sign is constant: otherwise the two open sign sets disconnect Γ, which a continuous connecting segment cannot do. Select the upward convention. Then at fixed work coordinates x≺y iff u_y≥u_x, and strict inequality gives strict accessibility. This proves the full geometric Planck statement, not merely a same-coordinate postulate.

## L4. Boundary graph, Lipschitz uniqueness and global continuation

Fix x and put ρ_x=projection_v(∂A_x). If z∈∂A_x, any support of A_x at z is a support of A_z, hence S2 makes it unique and equal to Π_z. By L1 an upper vertical point near z lies in Interior A_x; a lower point lies outside its support. Neighborhoods of those two points show every nearby work fiber meets ∂A_x, so ρ_x is open. S3 and continuity of projection make it connected. Each fiber has exactly one boundary point: if z is its lower boundary, the full vertical ray A_z⊂A_x includes all higher fiber points, and their interior membership follows by taking an interior point just above z and a still higher A_z point and applying L1's segment-interior argument. Define that unique boundary energy u_x(v). It is the infimum of energies on A_x in that fiber.

On each convex ball B⊂ρ_x, convex combinations of boundary states lie in A_x and Γ, so u_x is convex on B. L1 therefore gives local continuity/Lipschitz regularity. The supporting inequalities at (u_x(v),v) and (u_x(v'),v') yield

−P(u_x(v),v)·(v'−v)≤u_x(v')−u_x(v)≤−P(u_x(v'),v')·(v'−v).

The continuity of P and u_x makes the difference from the first linear expression o(|v'−v|), hence Du_x(v)=−P(u_x(v),v). Since u_x is locally Lipschitz and P locally Lipschitz, this derivative is locally Lipschitz. Thus the boundary is a C¹ graph with locally Lipschitz gradient, exactly the needed source interface. No C² entropy/Maxwell claim is inferred.

**Graph uniqueness.** Two C¹ solutions u,w of Du=−P(u,v), Dw=−P(w,v) agreeing at one work point agree locally: on any sufficiently short segment in a convex coordinate ball, subtract the integral equations along that segment. Local Lipschitz of P gives |u−w|(t)≤L∫_0^t|u−w|(s)ds, and the explicit integral-inequality argument of M23 forces zero. Any open connected subset of R^n is polygonally connected: the set of points reachable by finitely many straight segments lying in small domain balls is both open and has open complement, so is the entire connected domain. Continue the local uniqueness along a finite polygonal path to prove global uniqueness on a common connected domain. The source's distributional formulation is not needed for these actually C¹ boundary graphs; no weak-solution uniqueness is silently used.

If y∈∂A_x then A_y⊂A_x. On the connected overlap component of ρ_x∩ρ_y containing v_y, the two boundary graphs agree by uniqueness. This equality component is open in ρ_y and relatively closed there: at a limit work point v in ρ_y, continuity of u_y keeps (u_y(v),v) in Γ; relative closedness of ∂A_x puts it on ∂A_x, so v∈ρ_x and equality persists. Thus the component is all of ρ_y. Now ρ_y is open and relatively closed in connected ρ_x: if v_k∈ρ_y approach v∈ρ_x, u_y(v_k)=u_x(v_k)→u_x(v), and closedness of ∂A_y puts the limit on ∂A_y, so v∈ρ_y. Therefore ρ_x=ρ_y and ∂A_x=∂A_y. Their full upward fibers and convexity imply A_x=A_y; more directly x lies on ∂A_y, giving y≺x, and transitivity gives both sector inclusions. This repairs the unproved domain-inclusion shortcut in source Theorem3.6.

## L5. Nesting and single-system comparison

If y∈A_x, then A_y⊂A_x. If y∈∂A_x, L4 gives equality. Otherwise y∈Interior A_x. There cannot be z∈∂A_y∩∂A_x, since L4 would equate both sectors. Nor can A_y contain a boundary point of A_x: such a point z belongs to A_y and A_z=A_x; transitivity would give A_x=A_z⊂A_y⊂A_x, contradicting the strict interior case. Thus A_y⊂Interior A_x. The case x∈A_y is symmetric.

If neither state lies in the other's sector, take any z∈Interior A_x. The segment from y to z meets ∂A_x at w, lying strictly between its endpoints. L4 gives A_w=A_x, hence w≺z. The collinear lemma L2 gives y≺w≺z, so Interior A_x⊂A_y. Reverse x,y to get Interior A_y⊂A_x. A full-dimensional closed convex sector is the closure of its interior: fix an interior point and use L1's segment argument for every sector point. Relative closedness therefore forces A_x=A_y, contradicting neither membership. These exhaustive cases prove the source's nesting theorem and CH on Γ, with equality exactly on common boundaries and strict accessibility exactly into the interior.

## L6. Thermal strips give comparison on arbitrary scaled copies

If x_0≺≺x_1 and x_0~T x_1, then for any 0<λ<1 the reference compound R_λ=((1−λ)x_0,λx_1) is equivalent to its thermal join by scaling invariance, and x~((1−λ)x,λx) is equivalent to a state in the same join space by T2. L5's CH for that simple join thus compares R_λ with x. For x in the strip x_0≺x≺x_1, the set of λ∈[0,1] with R_λ≺x is nonempty/bounded. M11's reference ordering and the stability/epsilon argument (which here needs only these reference comparisons) give a unique attained λ with x~R_λ. Endpoints λ=0,1 are included by their direct single-system comparisons.

A complete gluing lemma avoids source Lemma4.1's skipped denominator algebra. Suppose two strips with strict endpoints (a,b),(c,d) overlap in an open set. Choose two states e≺≺f in the overlap: at any overlap point choose two sufficiently close same-work energy states, using openness and L3. Their respective reference coefficients are s_e<s_f in (a,b) and t_e<t_f in (c,d), strictly by M11 reference ordering. The two equivalences imply, by substitution, positive scaling and cancellation,

b−a ~ [(t_f−t_e)/(s_f−s_e)](d−c),

in signed comparison notation. Indeed subtract the e-equivalence from the f-equivalence by adding its terms across the relation; the result is (s_f−s_e)(b−a)~(t_f−t_e)(d−c). Also a+s_e(b−a)~c+t_e(d−c), so c~a+[s_e−t_e(t_f−t_e)^−1(s_f−s_e)](b−a). Every state represented by c+t(d−c) therefore has an affine coefficient in the (a,b) references with a positive slope. This extends both strips into a common signed reference chart without ever asserting an unproved λ-denominator nonzero.

T4 covers Γ by open strips {x:x_0≺≺x≺≺x_1} with thermally equivalent strict endpoints. These are open by L5/L4 closed boundary/sector descriptions. A finite list of target states lies in a compact connected polygonal path in Γ (straight segments suffice because Γ convex). Compactness gives a finite strip subcover. The intersection graph of the strips meeting that connected path is connected: if it split into two groups, the intersections of their unions with the path would be disjoint nonempty relatively open subsets covering it, contradicting connectedness. A spanning tree of that finite graph gives an order in which each added strip overlaps an earlier one in an open Γ set; strips merely touching the path at its endpoints can be omitted or replaced by the strip containing a small path neighborhood. Repeated gluing puts all target states in one signed affine reference chart with positive orientation. Any equal-total scaled compounds of those states reduce by recombination to the two fixed references, and M11's reference-order lemma compares them. This proves product CH for every number of scaled copies of the same simple system. M11 then supplies its entropy representation, and A7 gives concavity; L1 gives continuity.

## L7. Thermal calibrators: precisely checked remaining interface

For a single simple system the entropy from L6 is concave and strictly increasing in u at fixed v by L3/M11. For a finite concave one-variable function, secant monotonicity gives finite one-sided derivatives s'_−≥s'_+>0 at every interior point: neighboring endpoint secants bound the derivative limits, and a zero right derivative would force a constant subsequent interval, contradicting strict increase. Two energy-exchanging copies have an attained interior maximum at their initial split iff their inverse-temperature intervals [s'_+,s'_−] overlap, by the one-sided maximum criterion. That criterion is complete: for concave g, all right secants are ≤g'_+(0), all left secants ≥g'_−(0); the maximum is equivalent to g'_+(0)≤0≤g'_−(0). This proves the entropy-maximum/thermal interval interface for same-system copies using T1/T2 and their already proved consistent entropy.

The source Theorem4.6 concludes either the entire system is thermally equivalent, or some adiabat contains thermally inequivalent points. Its continuation step uses the just-established one-sided slopes, T3 and T4. Suppose every adiabat is a single thermal class. Local fixed-v entropy intervals give a nondecreasing order of thermal classes along the entropy interval; overlapping such entropy intervals patch that order globally. More explicitly, on each fixed-v interval, disjoint temperature intervals are strictly ordered with u and hence entropy. If every point on a given entropy level is in the same thermal class, T3 prevents an order reversal when overlapping two such entropy intervals: two intervals in one class overlap, while intervals in distinct classes are disjoint, and a class cannot be on both sides of another. A finite chain of overlapping local entropy intervals covers any compact subinterval of the entropy range, since that range is an interval by continuity/connectedness and each level has a local fixed-v interval. Thus the class order is nondecreasing globally. T4 puts two states of the same class at entropy values on either side of any given level. Monotonicity then forces the entire between-interval, including the given level, into that class. Every class is consequently open in the entropy range; connectedness makes it the sole class. This proves the dichotomy with the intermediate patching made explicit.

For the explicitly adopted C1/T5 branch of L0a, the calibrator proof is complete as follows. Consider any adiabat of the simple join J_12 and reversible splittings (x,y) of its states. If one join state has two distinct energy splits, fixed-work L3 orders their x-components oppositely to their y-components, and reversibility gives an entropy calibrator (x_0,y_1)~(x_1,y_0) with x_0≺≺x_1,y_0≺≺y_1. Otherwise all splits are unique. Fix one split (x̄,ȳ). If another split differs in either component's accessibility class, their total compounds are equivalent (L4), and consistency/cancellation force the other component to differ in the opposite strict direction; this again is a calibrator.

If no calibrator exists, every split of this join adiabat lies on ∂A_(x̄)×∂A_(ȳ). By L4 there is exactly one state on a component adiabat at any given work coordinate. Thus varying v_2 while retaining v_1 leaves the x-state fixed and every y-state thermal with that x; T3 and join coherence put the joined states in one thermal class. The same holds retaining v_2 and varying v_1. The join graph's work projection is open connected; small coordinate boxes and polygonal paths show the whole adiabat is thermally equivalent. This applies to every join adiabat. The L7 dichotomy therefore forces the entire join to be thermally equivalent. T5 makes every state of either component thermally equivalent to some state of the join, and the entire join is now in one class; T3 therefore makes every component state belong to that class. Consequently every nearby positive energy redistribution at fixed work coordinates is a thermal equilibrium split of the same joined state, contradicting the assumed uniqueness. Openness supplies at least two such redistributions. This is the exact use of T5 in the adopted completed branch. Thus a calibrator exists.

For a calibrator, each component entropy reference construction gives x~(1−s)x_0+s x_1 and y~(1−t)y_0+t y_1. The calibrator equivalence x_0+y_1~x_1+y_0 lets the signed increment x_1−x_0 be replaced by y_1−y_0. Consequently (x,y)~(1−(s+t)/2)(x_0,y_0)+((s+t)/2)(x_1,y_1). Recombine any finite equal-material scaled products and use reference ordering to get CH. Induct on the number of different simple types, treating each already calibrated compound as a compound state set with A1–A6 and the reference equivalences. This completes the algebraic calibrator-to-product step.

## L8. Universal calibration on arbitrary elementary families

Let I be any set of primitive simple labels, not required finite/countable. A separated compound type is a finitely supported positive amount assignment together with its explicit elementary factors; scaled products do not mix elementary materials. Assume product CH from an adequate route (or adopt it physically), and A1–A6. This is exactly the source's coherent elementary-decomposition alternative in PDF29; it avoids its unspecified positive-cone Hamel decomposition assertion. Under AC choose one reference state r_i for every i∈I, and a strict meter pair z_0≺≺z_1 in one type. Define the reference of every compound by taking the corresponding scaled tuple of r_i. The same probability/negative-tail/positive-expectation argument then is uniform. This supplies exactly Nguyen–Zessin6.6's conditional log-information conclusion without importing an unchecked Shannon theorem.

The cube information decomposes into its successive cell log conditional densities. Let Z=logk∞∈L¹(P); stationarity translates this variable from C to every cell. The lattice mean converges in L¹ to its translation-invariant conditional mean for the Z^d subgroup. ## L32. Exact canonical free-wall limits on arbitrary van Hove shapes

Assume L29's superstable regular interaction, a van Hove sequenceΩ_k, β>0 and interior attainable densityρ>0, with N_k/|Ω_k|→ρ. Then |Ω_k|^−1logZ_(Ω_k,N_k,free)→F(ρ,β), the same exact-N canonical rate as cubes. Upper bound is immediate from e^(αN_k)Z_(Ω_k,N_k)≤Ξ_(Ω_k), L29 pressure and L26 duality, giving limsup≤F.

For the complete lower bound, choose smallδ>0 soρ±δ are both interior. Fix a large core cube sideℓ and gapg(ℓ) from L27; choose integers n_± with n_±/ℓ^d→ρ±δ asℓ→∞. Their positive finite core partition weights are supplied by L24, eventually. Pack every whole(ℓ+g)-cell contained inΩ_k with its centralℓ-core; at fixedℓ,g their numberM_k satisfies M_k/|Ω_k|→(ℓ+g)^−d by van Hove geometry. For sufficiently largeℓ the effective core densities n_±/(ℓ+g)^d bracketρ. Consequently N_k/(M_k−1) lies strictly between n_- andn_+ for all sufficiently largek.

Assign n_- orn_+ particles to M_k−1 cores, and an exact remainder r to one correction core. Put Δ=n_+−n_->0 and

j=floor([N_k−(M_k−1)n_-]/Δ),
r=N_k−(M_k−1)n_-−jΔ.

Then0≤j≤M_k−1 and0≤r<Δ≤n_+. Assign j regular cores n_+, the other regular cores n_-, and correction core r. The total is exactlyN_k. Every correction weight Z(ℓ,r)>0: positivity of Z(ℓ,n_+) means a positive-measure set of n_+-tuples has all pair energies finite; deleting particles preserves that property. Tonelli projection gives a positive-measure set of finite-energy r-tuples, hence its positive Boltzmann integral, including r=0. Stability gives finiteness. Sinceℓ is fixed, the minimum of the finitely many logZ(ℓ,r),0≤r≤n_+, is a finite constant. Thus the one correction core costsO_ℓ(1)/|Ω_k|→0, without any assumption on smooth boundary or low-density space for extra particles.

All cores have count≤n_+, so L27's shell cross-energy bound applies to this finite subset of the common lattice regardless of the assignment of the two preparations. Exact N! cancellation leaves the product of their canonical weights. Letk→∞ first; the n_+ fraction tends [ρ(ℓ+g)^d−n_-]/Δ. Then letℓ→∞: it tends1/2, the packing fraction tends1, the uniform cross error tends0, and the two canonical core rates tendF(ρ±δ,β). Therefore liminf≥[F(ρ−δ,β)+F(ρ+δ,β)]/2. Letδ↓0 and use finite-concave continuity from L24. This proves the lower bound and the full arbitrary-shape exact-N limit. Superstability is used in the pressure **upper** theorem L29; the lower construction itself needs only L27 stability/tail hypotheses. No stability-alone arbitrary-shape upper proof is silently supplied by this argument.
