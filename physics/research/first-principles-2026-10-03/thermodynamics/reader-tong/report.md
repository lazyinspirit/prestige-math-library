## Explicit vocabulary and mathematical setup for using these findings

The following are proposed precise formulations needed before consumers; they are not claims that Tong states all these hypotheses.

A thermodynamic equilibrium state space Γ is a specified set of equilibrium macrostates. On a single smooth simple-compressible branch one may take Γ open in R³ with coordinates (U,V,N), U energy in J, V>0 volume in m³, and N>0 a dimensionless continuum approximation to particle count; composition and external fields are fixed. Boundary states and coexistence need separate treatment. A state function is a map Γ→R, with stated units; derivatives below require C¹ or C² regularity as specified. A process is an admissible protocol, with equilibrium endpoints; a quasi-static idealization is a piecewise C¹ curve γ:[0,1]→Γ. Reversibility requires an admissible inverse protocol restoring the system and surroundings, not merely a slowly traversed curve. An adiabatic process exchanges no heat; an isolated system also has no matter or external work exchange. These distinctions prevent the source's occasional conflations.

Thermodynamic entropy S_th:Γ→R, in J K⁻¹, is an adopted or independently constructed state function. In the smooth entropy formulation let S_th∈C² and (∂S_th/∂U)_(V,N)>0; define T=1/(∂S_th/∂U), p=T(∂S_th/∂V), μ=−T(∂S_th/∂N). T is in K, p in Pa, μ in J per particle. Invertibility to U(S,V,N) is local and uses the nonzero derivative; arbitrary variable pairs are not automatically coordinates. The fundamental differential dU=T dS_th−p dV+μ dN follows from these definitions by the chain rule, conditional on this state model. Identifying p with piston force per area or T with a thermometer requires operational/coupling premises.

Heat and work are protocol functionals Q,W_on, in J, with adopted balance ΔU=Q+W_on for a closed system. For reversible quasi-static pressure work alone, the one-form α_W=−p dV and α_Q=dU+p dV=T dS_th on fixed-N Γ supply their integrals along γ. A one-form assigns a linear map on each tangent space. Exact means α=df for a state function f; these heat/work forms need not be exact. General irreversible protocols need boundary temperature and external pressure; do not substitute equilibrium p,T or T dS for all heat. Matter transfer adds energetic bookkeeping.

For a finite microscopic model take a nonempty finite set X of states, energy H:X→R in J, and possibly number map n:X→N₀. A probability state is p:X→[0,1] with Σp=1. Gibbs entropy S_G(p)=−kB Σ_x p(x)ln p(x), with 0ln0=0, is a function on this probability simplex. Boltzmann shell entropy S_B(E,δ)=kB ln|{x:E≤H(x)<E+δ}| is a different function, defined only for nonempty chosen shells δ>0. Uniform-shell p makes these equal; arbitrary probability states do not. An observable A:X→R has mean ΣpA and variance Σp(A−mean A)². These define distributions and moments, not time evolution or a guarantee of a measured value.

For countably many microscopic states, sums require absolute convergence; for a classical configuration domain Λ⊂R³ bounded and N particles, phase space is Λᴺ×R³ᴺ with measure dq dp/(N!h³ᴺ) for the stated indistinguishability convention and Hamiltonian H_N. A canonical probability density relative to this measure exists only if 0<Z_N(β)=∫exp(−βH_N)<∞. Classical Gibbs entropy is relative to a chosen reference measure; raw coordinate differential entropy is not invariant under changing the reference scale. Quantum states instead are positive trace-class operators ρ with Trρ=1 on a specified Hilbert space; von Neumann entropy is S_vN(ρ)=−kB Trρlnρ when finite. Self-adjoint Hamiltonians, operator domains, trace-class exponentials, and observable moment domains are required. This source briefly introduces density matrices but does not develop those analytic foundations.

Use β=1/(kBT) in J⁻¹ and fugacity z=exp(βμ) dimensionless. kB carries J K⁻¹ and h carries J s, with ℏ=h/(2π). Suppressing kB requires restoring dimensional entropy S=kB s and energy-temperature θ=kBT, so β=1/θ. Tong uses kB explicitly. His rounded kB value and historical carbon-12 mole definition (§2.2.2 pp37–38) must not become current SI definitions: current SI kB=1.380649×10⁻²³ J K⁻¹ and N_A=6.02214076×10²³ mol⁻¹ exactly. Those modern constants are a normalization requirement, not an empirical result retrieved from Tong.

Homogeneity means S(λU,λV,λN)=λS(U,V,N) on a scaling cone; it is a bulk thermodynamic idealization. Euler's theorem yields U=TS−pV+μN and the single-component Gibbs relation G=μN. Surface/interfacial contributions and long-range forces need not satisfy it. Smooth free-energy minima require fixed constraints and ideal reservoirs; thermodynamic potential decrease is not a universal pointwise random-trajectory assertion.

## Models, limits, stability, and transitions

Chapter 2 supplies a conditional model sequence. §2.1 pp32–34 uses a semiclassical trace approximation (operator exponential factorization); unbounded-operator commutator remainder is not controlled. §2.2 pp34–41 supplies Gaussian ideal-gas calculation, corrected Z=Vᴺ/(N!λ³ᴺ), λ=(2πℏ²/mkBT)½, leading Sackur–Tetrode entropy and Poisson number fluctuations. Require dilute classical regime nλ³≪1 and stated indistinguishability/reference measure. §2.3 pp42–44 Maxwell velocity law needs separable quadratic momenta and position-only interactions in the canonical measure. Rotational invariance alone is insufficient: the source's product of component densities also assumes independence. Velocity-dependent interactions, constraints, nonequilibrium, or quantum statistics change the conclusion.

§2.4 pp45–48 uses independent rigid-rotor and harmonic-vibration model terms. §§2.5.1–2.5.3 pp50–60 use two-body position potentials, f(r)=exp(−βU(r))−1, and B₂=−½∫R³f(r)dr with negligible boundary corrections. The long-range power tail requires exponent greater than spatial dimension for integrability; the source's integer example says n≥4 in d=3. Formal graph combinatorics does not prove infinite cluster-series convergence. Rigorous thermodynamic-limit/cluster suppliers must state stability H_N≥−BN, suitable integrable tail/tempering, volume sequence/boundary conditions, activity/temperature bounds, and a convergence theorem; no claim these sufficient packages were proved in Tong. van der Waals equation is derived under low density and βU₀≪1, pp53–55. Chapter 5 extrapolates it into liquid/critical regions and explicitly acknowledges this on p136.

Chapter 3 is additional quantum physics. A single-particle Hilbert space and self-adjoint Hamiltonian with spectrum ε_r are supplied by each model; bosonic/fermionic N-particle spaces are symmetric/antisymmetric tensor powers, and grand space is their direct sum over N. Noninteracting additive energies and occupation basis are further assumptions. §§3.5.1 pp78–79 and 3.6 pp90–91 derive mean occupations (exp[β(ε_r−μ)]∓1)⁻¹; Bose geometric sums need β>0 and μ<inf ε_r and summability of the complete product/trace. Fermion single-mode sum always converges but infinitely many modes still require trace convergence. Quantum statistics, spin-statistics input, dispersion, boundary conditions, and continuum state-count approximation are not consequences of the thermodynamic laws.

§3.1 pp62–64 supplies continuum density of states for box plane waves; replacing discrete sums by integrals needs a limit/error argument, especially at the zero mode. §3.2 pp64–70 photons have two polarizations, dispersion ε=ℏc|k| and no conserved number constraint; Planck spectrum needs quantized occupation. §3.3 pp70–75 phonons have harmonic-lattice origin, adopted linear acoustic dispersion, three polarizations and Debye cutoff chosen to match 3N modes. §3.4 pp75–77 supplies quantum gaps to freeze rotor/oscillator contributions. These are model assumptions and approximations; the source labels Debye's construction as deliberately simplified.

§3.5.3 pp82–86 correctly recovers omitted ground-state occupation and distinguishes finite-N z<1 from limiting z→1. But §3.5.1 p79's blanket dismissal of occupation fluctuations fails in the condensate grand ensemble. For geometric occupation P(n₀=j)=(1−q)qʲ, j∈N₀, q=exp[−β(ε₀−μ)]∈(0,1), mean q/(1−q), variance q/(1−q)²=mean(1+mean); relative standard deviation approaches 1 for macroscopic mean. This elementary conditional calculation identifies the issue; it does not make a claim about measured condensate noise under an unspecified laboratory protocol. Canonical fixed-N and grand ensembles need careful separate interpretation.

A thermodynamic limit here must specify a sequence Λ_L growing regularly, N_L→∞ and N_L/|Λ_L|→n, interactions and boundaries fixed, and the object converging (e.g. f=lim F_L/|Λ_L|). It is not simply saying N→∞. Ensemble equivalence has different possible meanings (limiting potentials, local expectations, entropy densities, large-deviation minimizers). Tong pp21,23–24,29 gives a saddle/concentration heuristic with extensive U,C_V. Concentration is conditional on those scalings and, for example, Chebyshev gives P(|H−U|≥aU)≤kBT²C_V/(a²U²) when U≠0; it does not prove equality of all observables/distributions or uniqueness of equilibrium. Need entropy concavity, admissible energies, ensemble transforms, and existence/regularity of limit; nonconcave branches, coexistence, long-range interactions and critical singularities need separate analysis. Chapter 5 pp146–147 explicitly reintroduces enhanced density fluctuations near divergent compressibility.

Stability requires the appropriate constrained curvature. On convex Γ, concavity means S(λx+(1−λ)y)≥λS(x)+(1−λ)S(y); for C² S it implies negative-semidefinite Hessian. One-dimensional S_UU<0 alone does not supply this. Isothermal compressibility κ_T=−V⁻¹(∂V/∂p)_(T,N) must be nonnegative for bulk mechanical stability; using coordinate invertibility, this gives (∂p/∂V)_T≤0. Local minima represent metastability; global minimizers give equilibrium selection within the specified model. No lifetime/escape probability is supplied without dynamics.

§5.1 pp135–147 derives equal T,p,μ coexistence, Maxwell equal-area condition, and Clapeyron slope dp/dT=Δs/Δv=L/(TΔv). A rigorous Maxwell supplier should use convexification/common supporting tangent of bulk F(V), not a physical process through the unstable branch (source acknowledges this p138). Define s=S/N and v=V/N; L=TΔs is energy per particle, not per kilogram. Equality of chemical potential and smooth phase-specific potentials yields the slope away from critical Δv=0. The example approximating vapor pressure additionally fixes L, neglects liquid volume and adopts ideal vapor law.

§5.2 pp147–155 gives finite Ising X={−1,+1}^Λ with nearest-neighbor energy −JΣs_is_j−bΣs_i. Here b has energy units: source absorbs magnetic moment into its symbol B. Mean field discards neighbor correlations and is a model approximation, not the exact finite measure. At zero field a symmetric finite-volume measure has mean magnetization zero; spontaneous magnetization requires explicit boundary/field selection and order of limits. §5.3.1 pp156–157 transfer-matrix eigenvalues give no positive-temperature singularity for the nearest-neighbor 1D model. The source's broader statement that all 1D systems lack transitions is unsupported; longer-range/unbounded-state models require their own theorems.

For finite X, Z is a finite positive sum of real analytic weights for T>0, so F is real analytic; a phase transition in the chosen equilibrium definition is a nonanalyticity of a limiting thermodynamic potential. This precise claim does not say every finite continuous/infinite-state system has analytic partition function at all parameter values. §5.4.3 pp177–179 also makes grand Ξ a finite positive-coefficient polynomial for finite-volume hard cores. Require measurable finite-volume potentials/integrability. Source quotes, but explicitly does not prove, a thermodynamic-limit/zero-free-domain theorem; its potential hypotheses and locally uniform analytic control are incomplete. The made-up polynomial pp179–180 is explicitly artificial, not a derived Hamiltonian or observation.

§5.4 pp170–176 Landau theory extends equilibrium free energy to a constrained order-parameter function; analytic expansion/coefficient behavior are adopted model hypotheses. For b>0 and a crossing zero linearly, quartic minimization gives square-root order parameter and mean-field exponents. These are not universal measured constants; upper critical dimension d=4 needs logarithmic corrections and full fluctuation treatment beyond source. §5.5 pp180–185 uses a continuum functional F[m]=∫(a m²+b m⁴+c|∇m|²) on specified domain with b,c>0 and fixed boundary values. To formulate rigorously choose functions in H¹∩L⁴ (Sobolev space of square-integrable functions with square-integrable weak first derivatives, intersected with integrable fourth powers). Integration by parts requires variations/boundaries controlling the boundary term. A stationary Euler–Lagrange solution is not automatically a global minimum. Source's final ∫Dm has no defined infinite-dimensional Lebesgue measure; supply a finite lattice/cutoff probability measure and justified limit before a theorem uses it.

## Specific source defects and open proof obligations

- §1.2.1 p8 says finite fluctuations essentially never violate entropy increase but gives no dynamics/protocol or tail/time bound. Equal-weight equilibrium counting cannot prove monotone microscopic trajectory entropy, mixing rates, or claimed recurrence times. Keep finite statistical distributions separate from idealized macroscopic second-law inequalities.
- §1.4.3 p29 writes μ=T S_N while p26 definition has minus sign; visually confirmed. §2.6 p61 visibly prints (∇²+λ_D⁻²)φ=4πqδ, inconsistent with the Yukawa solution φ=−qexp(−r/λ_D)/r and preceding Poisson linearization; required sign is minus. Treat these as local algebra errors.
- §3.6.6 p103 gives μ_B=|e|ℏ/(2m), while §3.6.7 p107 uses denominator 2mc after unchanged minimal coupling/cyclotron formulas; visually confirmed unit-convention drift. Also specify magnetization derivative constraints (e.g. −∂F/∂B at T,V,N), not arbitrary derivative of mean U.
- §4.1 pp109–110 thermal-equilibrium transitivity yields an equivalence relation, not automatically a smooth real scalar ordering/coordinate. Need ordering, regularity and thermometer assumptions. §4.4 p128 saying any pair of variables labels states needs nonzero Jacobians and phase-branch restrictions.
- §4.3.3 pp120–122 staircase/corner approximation needs exact hypotheses and a limiting proof. Entropy must be globally well-defined on the intended accessible component, or local integrating-factor statements must be labeled local. Statistical count entropy and thermodynamic entropy are not proved equal merely because their differentials share a form; constants, equilibrium identification, limit and scale must be matched.
- §4.3.4 p123 equates δQ=0 with dS=0 without retaining reversible qualifier, despite previous page distinguishing irreversible entropy growth. Subsequent Pfaff discussion pp124–126 needs nonvanishing C¹ one-form and local Frobenius hypotheses; accessibility argument does not by itself prove a global foliation/unique scalar entropy or its arrow. Carathéodory shorthand is not a rigorous equivalent axiom here.
- §4.5 p133 infers C_V~Tⁿ with n≥1 from ∫C_V/T finite. Counterexample: C_V(T)=C₀(T/T₀)¹ᐟ² on 0<T<T₀ gives integral 2C₀√(T/T₀), finite, with exponent 1/2. Even vanishing pointwise is not implied by integrability without more regularity. p134's generic perturbation lifting degeneracy is not a proof of universal third law; ground-state residual entropy and order of limits must be allowed explicitly.
- §5.3.2 pp160–162 droplet counting is heuristic, not a rigorous Peierls argument or exact critical temperature. Finite partition functions do not actually diverge at the guessed threshold. A proof needs contour bound, plus boundary state, probability estimate and infinite-volume construction.
- §5.3.4 pp168–170 duality claims every even-link configuration is represented by dual spins two-to-one; periodic topology has noncontractible sectors omitted by this local construction. Boundary conditions/global compatibility must be supplied. Further, p169 coefficient is 2^(N−1)(sinhβJ coshβJ)^N, but p170 prints 2^(N−1)(sinh2βJ)^N, introducing factor 2^N. Visually confirmed, not extraction corruption. Do not use that stated finite-volume formula as proved.
- §5.4.3 p178 quoted thermodynamic-limit theorem lacks a proved existence argument/potential bounds and zero-free-domain analytic-limit hypotheses. p180's illustrative pressure bounds omit α even though transition pressure is αkBTln2; dimensional/formula normalization must be checked locally.
