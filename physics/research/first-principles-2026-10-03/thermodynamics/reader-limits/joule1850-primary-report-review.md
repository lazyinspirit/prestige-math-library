## Apparatus, preparation and measured quantities

Falling paired lead weights drive a paddle through strings and pulleys. The water apparatus has eight rotating sets of brass arms and four stationary vanes in a sealed copper vessel; a boxwood interruption reduces shaft heat conduction. Mercury uses iron apparatus; a further apparatus rubs cast-iron wheels in mercury. A slotted wooden support reduces conduction and a screen blocks the experimenter's radiant heat. Trials occur in a cellar selected for temperature uniformity. Each friction trial repeats about63-inch drops20 times; the initial and final apparatus temperatures and initial/middle/final laboratory temperatures are observed. Matched resting trials before or after friction estimate heat exchange with the environment, with the same duration, fluid quantity and observation arrangement.

Thermometer tubes are calibrated and graduated by Regnault's method. The report says their readings agree to about **1/100°F**, not a modern uncertainty specification. A standard thermometer uses freezing and boiling points; the boiling calibration reports29.94inHg and50°F air, corrected to the chosen0.760m/0°C pressure convention. Comparing A/B to that standard and C to A gives13.951,9.829,11.647 divisions per°F. Joule reports reading1/20division and an appreciable1/200°F. These are historical resolution/comparison claims, not certified confidence intervals or independent calibrations performed in this audit.

Table I has40 water-friction and matched resting trials; II has20 mercury trials with heavier weights; III has20 mercury-calorimeter mixing trials; IV has20 wetting corrections; V has30 lighter-weight mercury trials; VI has10 heavier-weight iron trials; VII has12 iron calorimeter trials; VIII has10 lighter-weight iron trials; IX summarizes five series. Tables supply fall heights, air temperatures, initial/final apparatus temperatures and gains/losses, rather than an invented modern dataset. For example Table I trial1 gives1256.96in,57.698°F air,55.118→55.774°F apparatus,0.656°F gain; its resting control gives0.108°F gain with57.868°F air. That raw gain is not the friction-only heat.

## The report's correction chain, independently arithmetically checked

The water means are fall1260.248in, friction rise0.575250°F and resting rise0.012975°F. Mean apparatus–air offsets differ; the report adds0.000832°F and then0.000102°F for nonuniform heating in time, giving **0.563209°F**. This reconstructs exactly as0.575250−0.012975+0.000832+0.000102. The calorimeter water-equivalent capacity is97470.2grains (water93229.7, copper2430.2, brass/stopper1810.3). The wheel capacity is taken from metal composition/specific heats rather than its less reliable direct mixing estimate. Using7000grains/lb gives97470.2×0.563209/7000=7.84229912lb·°F, agreeing with7.842299 printed.

The two suspended weights plus strings total406152grains. Pulley/string correction2837grains leaves403315. Corrected fall1260.096in removes residual impact kinetic energy; the report adds16.928foot-pounds for string elasticity, obtaining6067.114foot-pounds. The ratio6067.114/7.842299=773.640foot-pounds/(lb-water·°F), the printed water series result in air. The air-buoyancy correction gives772.692 in vacuo. Original image p318 corrects severe OCR substitutions: mercury capacity is **22071.68**, temperature rise **2.491218**, corrected weight **403242**, mean fall **1262.731**, corrected fall **1262.579**. None of the OCR's33071.68/3.491318/1363.731 substitutions is admissible measurement evidence.

Table IX's in-vacuo values are water772.692, mercury772.814/775.352 (mean774.083), iron776.045/773.930 (mean774.987). Joule selects the water result for its40 trials and larger calorimeter capacity. He explicitly acknowledges possible remaining vibration/sound upward bias and possible iron abrasion; the iron sound correction uses a comparison with a violoncello and another observer, not a precision acoustic calorimeter. Its lighter-weight correction assumes proportionality to friction. These limitations prevent treating this as a modern complete uncertainty budget. No standard deviation, confidence coverage, unreported replication or statistical error bar is invented here.

## Interpretation and historical units

The reported unit is work from a pound weight falling a foot divided by the heat that raises a pound of water between55–60°F by1°F. It is not automatically the modern international-table Btu or a temperature-independent calorie. A transparent **conventional modern-unit comparison**, not a recalibration, uses exact modern foot0.3048m and standard gravity9.80665m/s²:772.692×0.3048×9.80665×(9/5)=4157.331J/(kg·K). The pound mass cancels between numerator and denominator. Actual historical local gravity, historical standards, water heat capacity variation and buoyancy corrections matter if interpreting the physical SI value; this comparison does not replace them or attach uncertainty. The experiment supports mechanical work/heat equivalence in the tested preparations. A universal first law for all systems remains a physical framework postulate, not a logical consequence of these five series alone.
