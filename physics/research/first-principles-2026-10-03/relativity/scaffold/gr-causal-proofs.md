## C1 — Lorentz Gauss lemma and radial maximum

At $p$ choose the small star-shaped exponential coordinate domain from G2. For a smooth family $v(r)\in T_pM$, put $F(s,r)=\exp_p(sv(r))$, $T=\partial_sF$, $J=\partial_rF$, with $0\le s\le1$ and the relevant vectors in that domain. Metric compatibility and the geodesic equation give $g(T,T)=g_p(v(r),v(r))$ and $D_sT=0$. Torsion freedom gives $D_sJ=D_rT$. Therefore $\partial_sg(T,J)=g(T,D_rT)=\frac12\partial_rg(T,T)=g_p(v,v')$, independent of $s$. Since $J(0,r)=0$, integrate to get $g(T,J)=s\,g_p(v,v')$. At $s=1$, $T=d\exp_p|_v(v)$ and $J=d\exp_p|_v(v')$, hence

$$g_{\exp_pv}(d\exp_p(v),d\exp_p(w))=g_p(v,w).$$

Here the first $d\exp_p(v)$ means $d\exp_p|_v(v)$, and the second is $d\exp_p|_v(w)$; the explicit formula removes any map/vector ambiguity. This is the nondegenerate-metric Gauss lemma, with no positive-definite premise.

Let $\alpha:[0,1]\to M$ be a piecewise $C^1$ future timelike curve from $p$ to $q$ whose image lies in this exponential chart, with finitely many pieces, nonzero one-sided tangents, and initial future timelike tangent. Write $v(t)=\exp_p^{-1}(\alpha(t))$. For small $t>0$, $v(t)=t\alpha'(0)+o(t)$ in normal coordinates, so $v(t)$ is future timelike at $p$. It stays so: if the first exit were through $g_p(v,v)=0$, $v\ne0$ and future, then the radial vector $T=d\exp_p|_v(v)$ is nonzero future null by geodesic norm and time-orientation preservation. The derivative of $Q(t)=g_p(v(t),v(t))$ is $2g(T,\alpha')<0$ at that exit, by the null/timelike rest-frame inequality. A function negative immediately before a first zero has nonnegative left derivative at that zero, a contradiction. Corners use the left tangent. It cannot switch future components while timelike, and $v=0$ cannot be a later first exit: future timelike curves in a sufficiently small chart strictly increase a local time coordinate, since at $p$ $dx^0$ is positive on the future cone and, after shrinking, remains positive on all future causal tangents by continuity and compactness of their Euclidean-normalized cone slice. Thus it cannot return to $p$. This supplies the causal containment that the length inequality needs.

For $t>0$, define $R(t)=\sqrt{-g_p(v(t),v(t))}$. The radial future unit field $N=T/R$ satisfies $g(N,N)=-1$. Gauss lemma gives $R'=-g(N,\alpha')$. S6's pointwise reverse Cauchy inequality applies at $\alpha(t)$, yielding $\sqrt{-g(\alpha',\alpha')}\le R'$. Integrate from $\delta$ to $1$ and let $\delta\downarrow0$; $R(\delta)\to0$ and the curve length over $[0,\delta]$ tends to zero by bounded piecewise-continuous derivative and metric. Therefore

$$\int_0^1\sqrt{-g(\alpha',\alpha')}dt\le\sqrt{-g_p(\exp_p^{-1}q,\exp_p^{-1}q)}.$$

Equality forces $\alpha'$ to be a positive multiple of the radial $N$ at each smooth point, because a continuous nonnegative difference with zero integral vanishes and the reverse inequality's equality case is positive proportionality. Since $d\exp_p$ is invertible this means $v'(t)$ is proportional to $v(t)$, so the direction of $v$ is constant on each piece, and by continuity globally. The maximizing image is exactly the radial timelike geodesic, with increasing parametrization. Dividing by $c$ proves proper-time maximum for this explicit contained curve class. It does not assert global maximality after leaving the normal region, or maximality before every possible conjugate/cut point. No claim about null proper-time extremality is obtained from division by a zero radius.

## C2 — conjugacy and local maximum along a longer segment

Let $\gamma:[0,b]\to M$, $b>0$, be a smooth future unit timelike geodesic with $\gamma(0)=p$, tangent $T$, and $g(T,T)=-1$. A Jacobi field is a smooth field along it with $D_s^2J=R(T,J)T$. Its initial values and initial derivative determine it uniquely by G2's linear ODE argument, and every pair of initial vectors is allowed. A conjugate instant $s>0$ means a nonzero Jacobi field vanishes at both $0$ and $s$; multiplicity is the dimension of that finite-dimensional solution subspace. Vary initial velocity $v$ through $v+rw$ and differentiate $\exp_p(t(v+rw))$ in $r$. G3 gives its Jacobi equation, initial value zero and initial derivative $w$. Uniqueness identifies it with the Jacobi field with those data. At $t=1$ this proves $d\exp_p|_v(w)=J_w(1)$. Thus a nonzero kernel of this differential is exactly conjugacy along the radial segment; rescaling $v=sT(0)$ proves the equivalent claim at every instant $s$. No Riemannian conjugacy theorem is consumed.

Assume now no conjugate instant on $(0,b]$. The derivative of $\exp_p$ is invertible at every $sT(0)$ there. Near zero its invertibility is already G2. Hence along the compact parameterized radial segment there are finitely many inverse charts of $\exp_p$, all on neighborhoods of the appropriate branch $sT(0)$, and a fixed-endpoint $C^1$ curve $\alpha$ sufficiently close to $\gamma$ has a unique continuous lift $v(s)$ in these chosen local branches, close to $sT(0)$. On adjacent overlaps lifts agree after shrinking because both lie in the same local injectivity neighborhood of their common branch vector; subdivide the parameter interval into finitely many such overlaps. This construction requires no global injectivity or absence of other distant geodesics. It gives $v(0)=0$ and $v(b)=bT(0)$, since endpoints are fixed and these local inverses are injective. Near zero use its one exponential chart; $C^1$ closeness gives $v(s)=s(T(0)+O(\delta))$ uniformly there, with a small error $\delta$ from its derivative. The open future cone then makes $v(s)$ future timelike for sufficiently close curves. Away from zero, the compact branch has a uniformly open timelike neighborhood, so the same is true for the whole lifted curve. The radial tangent $d\exp_p|_{v(s)}v(s)$ is consequently future timelike. The Gauss-lemma inequality of C1 now applies along this lift, even when the images of the finitely many inverse charts overlap elsewhere. Integrating its radial length derivative gives $L(\alpha)\le b$, with equality only for the monotone image of $\gamma$. Thus a timelike geodesic segment before its first conjugate instant is a local proper-time maximum among sufficiently $C^1$ close timelike fixed-endpoint curves, modulo increasing reparametrization. A different distant curve, cut locus or topology can still destroy global maximality. This argument supplies the required local conclusion without an unproved infinite-dimensional inference from negative second variation.

The Riemannian `thm-index-lemma` and DG-22 plan were checked as comparison sources. Their positive ambient metric hypothesis is false here, so neither is a direct supplier. The normal rest-space Jacobi operator $C(J)=R(T,J)T$ is selfadjoint by G3 symmetries, and its positive normal metric allows the finite-dimensional factorization used by that proof: in a parallel normal frame solve $A''=CA$, $A(0)=0$, $A'(0)=I$. Its Wronskian $A^TA'-A'^TA$ has zero derivative and zero initial value, so $W=A'A^{-1}$ is symmetric before conjugacy; $W'=C-W^2$. For any normal piecewise $C^1$ field $J$ with zero endpoints,

$$\int_0^b(|J'|^2+J^TCJ)ds=\int_0^b|J'-WJ|^2ds+[J^TWJ]_0^b.$$

The boundary at zero vanishes since $A(s)=sI+O(s^3)$, $W(s)=I/s+O(s)$ and $J(s)=O(s)$; the other endpoint vanishes directly. The index form is nonnegative and vanishes only for $J=A v$ with constant $v$, hence only $J=0$ because $A(b)$ is invertible. This is the explicit Lorentz timelike-normal extension of the needed index argument; positivity is used in the normal three-space, not presumed on the Lorentz tangent space. The local maximality proof above is independently complete and avoids claiming this alone proves a global cut theorem.

## C3 — causal vocabulary with objects and hypotheses

For global causal statements use continuous locally absolutely continuous curves in coordinate charts, with nonzero future causal tangent almost everywhere on each compact parameter subinterval. Absolute continuity means that for every positive error bound there is a total interval-length bound making the sum of coordinate increments over any finite disjoint collection of intervals smaller than that error bound. The reviewed `thm-fundamental-theorem-of-calculus-for-absolutely-continuous-functions` supplies almost-everywhere derivatives and integration of increments. Its countable-choice and dependent-choice assumptions are explicitly adopted in this global causal module. Smooth coordinate transitions preserve this definition: on a sufficiently small compact convex coordinate subregion their derivatives are bounded, so the mean-value integral gives a Lipschitz bound; applying that bound to each disjoint interval preserves absolute continuity. At every parameter where the original coordinate curve is differentiable, the ordinary derivative expansion gives the chain rule, hence the cone condition transforms almost everywhere. Piecewise $C^1$ regular causal curves are included. Chronological reachability may equivalently use piecewise $C^1$ future timelike curves here; the examples below use that subclass. An exact proof of equivalence of the different general causal regularity conventions is not assumed. $I^+(p)$ consists of endpoints joined from $p$ by a future timelike curve; $J^+(p)$ consists of $p$ and endpoints joined by a future causal curve. A spacetime is chronological if no future timelike curve is closed, causal if no nonconstant future causal curve is closed. Strong causality means for every point $p$ and open neighborhood $U$ there is an open $p\in V\subset U$ such that no causal curve leaves $V$ and later returns to $V$. Global hyperbolicity here means strong causality and compact diamonds $J^+(p)\cap J^-(q)$ for all points. A spacelike hypersurface is a smooth embedded codimension-one submanifold with positive-definite induced metric. A Cauchy hypersurface meets every inextendible causal curve exactly once; inextendible means the curve image cannot be extended as a causal curve at either endpoint, with reparametrizations allowed. These definitions are exact but no equivalence between the global-hyperbolicity and Cauchy definitions is asserted without a splitting/existence theorem.

An acausal set has no two distinct points joined causally; an achronal set has no two distinct points joined chronologically. A time function is a continuous real-valued function strictly increasing along every future nonconstant causal curve. A temporal function is a smooth function with past timelike gradient, so its differential is positive on future causal nonzero vectors by the pointwise inequality. Temporal implies time function by integration; any time function forbids a closed causal curve by strict increase. This elementary implication is proved and does not supply the converse hierarchy or global smooth splitting.

## C4 — Minkowski Cauchy proof and two flat countermodels

In affine Minkowski space $t=x^0/c$ is temporal. On a future causal curve, $|\dot{\mathbf x}|\le c\dot t$ almost everywhere. Integrating by the absolute-continuity FTC gives $|\mathbf x(b)-\mathbf x(a)|\le c(t(b)-t(a))$. Since $\dot t>0$ almost everywhere, $t$ is strictly increasing, and this integrated estimate proves its spatial graph in $t$ is $c$-Lipschitz. A Lipschitz graph is absolutely continuous directly from the disjoint-interval definition: the sum of increments is bounded by $c$ times the total interval lengths. Its almost-everywhere derivative then obeys the same speed bound by taking difference quotients. If a future-inextendible curve had finite upper endpoint $T$ in coordinate time, its spatial positions would be Cauchy as $t\uparrow T$ by this Lipschitz bound and have a finite Euclidean limit. Appending the limiting event and a vertical future timelike segment extends it, contradicting inextendibility. The past argument is identical. Thus every inextendible causal curve has $t$-range all $\mathbb R$ and meets each $t=t_0$ exactly once. These are Cauchy hypersurfaces. The same cone bounds make each diamond closed and bounded in $\mathbb R^4$, hence compact. Sufficiently small rectangular causal diamonds give causally convex neighborhoods: if two events are in the diamond, every event between them is still in it by transitivity of the cone order. This proves strong causality and global hyperbolicity directly, without a general splitting theorem.

For $M=(\mathbb R/L\mathbb Z)_{x^0}\times\mathbb R^3$, $L>0$, the constant metric $-(dx^0)^2+d\mathbf x^2$ is well defined by translation charts and is time oriented by the descended $\partial_0$. The curve $s\mapsto(s\bmod L,0,0,0)$ is closed future timelike. Thus smoothness, flatness and time orientation do not imply causality or a Cauchy hypersurface. It is not affine Minkowski space globally, despite identical local metric coefficients.

For the open Minkowski slab $M=(0,1)_{x^0}\times\mathbb R^3$, the restricted metric is flat and time oriented, but a vertical proper-time geodesic reaches the absent slab boundary after finite proper time, hence is incomplete. Its $x^0=1/2$ slice is nevertheless Cauchy by the Lipschitz endpoint argument within the slab: an inextendible curve's coordinate range must be $(0,1)$. Completeness and global hyperbolicity are therefore distinct. An open bounded spatial fragment of that slice is spacelike and fails to meet vertical lines outside the fragment, proving that a spacelike surface need not be Cauchy. These are proved countermodels, not merely causal diagrams.

## C5 — local focusing and its precise limit

Let $n$ be a smooth future unit timelike geodesic congruence on an open domain, $\nabla_nn=0$. Use affine length $s$ with tangent $n$; proper time is $s/c$. Put $B_{ab}=\nabla_b n_a$. Unit norm gives $n^aB_{ab}=0$; the geodesic condition gives $B_{ab}n^b=0$, so it is a rest-space tensor. Let $\vartheta=B^a{}_a$, $\sigma_{ab}=B_{(ab)}-\vartheta h_{ab}/3$, $\omega_{ab}=B_{[ab]}$. In a rest orthonormal basis $B_{ab}B^{ba}=\vartheta^2/3+\sigma_{ab}\sigma^{ab}-\omega_{ab}\omega^{ab}$; cross symmetric/antisymmetric terms and the shear trace vanish. To differentiate the expansion, commute covariant derivatives on $n$: $n^b\nabla_b\nabla_an^a=\nabla_a(n^b\nabla_bn^a)-(\nabla_an^b)(\nabla_bn^a)-\operatorname{Ric}_{ab}n^an^b$. The first term is zero for this geodesic field, and the curvature sign is fixed by G3's $R^a{}_{bcd}$. Hence

$$D\vartheta=-\vartheta^2/3-\sigma_{ab}\sigma^{ab}+\omega_{ab}\omega^{ab}-\operatorname{Ric}_{ab}n^an^b.$$

This derives timelike Raychaudhuri with the stated normalization. Suppose further $\omega=0$ and $\operatorname{Ric}(n,n)\ge0$ along one flow line, with initial $\vartheta_0<0$. Positivity of the rest metric implies $D\vartheta\le-\vartheta^2/3$. Thus $y=-\vartheta>0$ satisfies $y'\ge y^2/3$, giving $(1/y)'\le-1/3$ and $1/y(s)\le1/y_0-s/3$ as long as the smooth congruence exists. It cannot remain smooth past affine length $3/y_0$: the right side would become nonpositive while the left stays positive. This proves a local focusing/breakdown bound. It does not prove that spacetime or every individual geodesic ends there: even flat geodesics can intersect and destroy a congruence. A global singularity theorem requires its additional causal/compactness hypotheses and proof.
