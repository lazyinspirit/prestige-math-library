## H0 — convex normal neighborhoods and local causal tools

G2 proves local smooth geodesics and the exponential map. The joint endpoint map $(x,v)\mapsto(x,\exp_xv)$ has invertible derivative at $(p,0)$: its block derivative is $(\delta x,\delta v)\mapsto(\delta x,\delta x+\delta v)$ in one chart. Fix a coordinate ball where $|\Gamma(v,v)|\le C|v|^2$ and shrink so all these small-velocity segments stay in a larger ball. Thus the joining segments of any two points in a smaller ball stay in it, since a convex real function has its maximum at an endpoint.

There cannot be a second geodesic wholly contained in this small ball with a large initial velocity. ## H2 — Cauchy equivalence and global causal compactness

A Cauchy set under our convention is a subset met exactly once by every inextendible causal curve. It is acausal, since a causal segment between two distinct points can be extended and would meet it twice. It is closed: a limit point outside it lies timelike before or after its unique intersection with an inextendible timelike curve, and chronological openness then places nearby points of the set timelike before/after that intersection, contradicting acausality. The same argument gives $M=I^-(S)\sqcup S\sqcup I^+(S)$. In local coordinates whose vertical lines are timelike flow lines, each sufficiently nearby line meets $S$ between one point in $I^-(S)$ and one in $I^+(S)$; acausality and the local cone bounds give a Lipschitz graph. Thus it is an embedded topological hypersurface. This establishes the terminology, not merely the intersection count.

If such an $S$ exists, strong causality follows from H1. Otherwise arbitrarily small-endpoint causal curves escape a fixed neighborhood. A bounded length subsequence gives a closed causal loop, impossible for a Cauchy set. An unbounded subsequence gives a two-sided inextendible limit with the reverse-pair closure relation from H1. That limit crosses $S$; choose points $a\in I^+(S)$ and $b\in I^-(S)$ on its two sides. The relation $(a,b)\in\overline J$ and openness of chronology give a timelike connection between two points of $S$, a contradiction. Now suppose a causal diamond were noncompact or nonclosed. Join $p$ to its offending sequence $r_k$ to $q$. If lengths were bounded, H1 gives a joining limit containing the limit of $r_k$ and lying in a compact ball, excluding both possibilities. If lengths are unbounded, H1 again gives future/past inextendible tails with the closure relation; each tail has a point respectively in $I^+(S)$ and $I^-(S)$, producing the same achronality contradiction. Hence all diamonds are compact. This is the full Cauchy-to-global-hyperbolicity deduction used by E2 and the gluing theorem.

Conversely suppose strong causality and compact diamonds. The local causal bound and finitely many causally convex chart neighborhoods on a compact diamond bound the total auxiliary length of every contained causal curve: each chart is traversed at most once and each traversal is bounded by its local time range. H1 then makes $J\subset M\times M$ closed, since curves with converging endpoints stay in a slightly larger compact diamond and have joining limits. $$t(p)=\log\frac{\mu(I^-(p))}{\mu(I^+(p))}$$

### H4.1 — causal inclusions, uniqueness and maximal common development

An open subdevelopment $U\subset M$ sharing the same Cauchy surface $\Sigma$ is causally convex. For $x\in U\cap I^+(\Sigma)$, every past causal segment from $x$ to $\Sigma$ stays in $U$: the maximal connected portion of any such curve in $U$ is inextendible there in the past if it exits, and must meet $\Sigma$, impossible before the supposed exit. Extend an arbitrary past segment to an inextendible curve in $M$ and use the same argument to obtain past convexity within $J^+(\Sigma)$. The past side is dual. A causal segment with endpoints in $U$ cannot leave and reenter on either side of $\Sigma$, nor can it cross $\Sigma$ twice. The intersection of two such subdevelopments remains open and Cauchy: the component of a curve in the intersection must meet $\Sigma$ by these past/future convexity assertions. Every connected component meets $\Sigma$; for connected $\Sigma$ the intersection is connected.

Two isometric immersions with equal value and derivative at one point agree on a connected domain. In a normal neighborhood they commute with the exponential and hence agree; the set of equal value/derivative is open by this argument and closed by continuity. Two time-oriented immersions fixing $\Sigma$ have equal tangential derivative and send its future unit normal to the same normal, hence equal full derivative. The preceding intersection argument therefore makes identifications agree on every overlap. The same conclusion carries matter: each immersion is required to preserve the matter fields.

A time-oriented isometric immersion between Cauchy developments that is a diffeomorphism on $\Sigma$ is injective. If two points have the same image, lift the velocity of a timelike geodesic through that image at each point. Their maximal geodesics each meet $\Sigma$. Their images agree where their domains overlap, by geodesic uniqueness; the union of their images is one geodesic in the target. Its unique $\Sigma$ intersection makes the affine intersection parameters equal. The initial points and velocities on $\Sigma$ agree, so G2 identifies the two source geodesics and their original points. A local diffeomorphism that is injective is an open embedding.

For two developments $M,M'$, local geometric uniqueness supplies a common Cauchy neighborhood. Take the union $U\subset M$ of all common subdevelopments, with their unique maps to $M'$. This is a set of subsets of $M$, not a choice over a proper class. Overlap uniqueness patches the maps. A maximal connected component of an inextendible causal curve inside any one member is inextendible in that member, so meets $\Sigma$; ambient Cauchy uniqueness gives only one crossing. Thus $U$ is a development. The patched immersion is an embedding by the preceding paragraph. It is the maximal common development (MCD), and contains every common subdevelopment.

The whole segment lies in the boundary: entry into $U$ violates past convexity at its lower boundary point, while entry into the interior of $U^c$ allows a timelike perturbation preceding a point of $U$. Continue that null geodesic to the past while it lies in the boundary. It reaches a last boundary point before $\Sigma$: the maximal past geodesic meets $\Sigma$, the boundary is closed and disjoint from the open neighborhood of $\Sigma$ in $U$, and the preceding compactness/velocity argument excludes a missing affine endpoint along the compact past region.

Call that last boundary point $b$. Every point along this boundary segment remains in $C$. Openness was proved above. For closedness, the corresponding null geodesic in $M'$ stays in compact $J^-(p')\cap J^+(\Sigma')$; its affine continuation exists by the same length/velocity argument. At a limiting parameter, timelike interior curves approaching earlier corresponding points give points simultaneously approaching the two limiting endpoints, proving correspondence. Thus the relatively open/closed parameter set is the whole segment. Finally $J^-(b)\cap\partial U\cap J^+(\Sigma)=\{b\}$. Otherwise concatenate with a further boundary null segment; it cannot continue the same geodesic by the definition of the last boundary point, so has a genuine null corner and push-up contradicts achronality.

For any neighborhood $W$ of this exposed point $b$, choose $q\gg b$ arbitrarily close so that

$$J^-(q)\cap U^c\cap J^+(\Sigma)\subset W.$$

If this fails for $q_k\to b$ along a timelike curve, the offending points outside $W$ lie in one compact past region. A subsequence gives $x\le b$ in $U^c\cap J^+(\Sigma)$ outside $W$. If $x$ is on the boundary it contradicts the exposed property. If it is interior to $U^c$, a small timelike perturbation of $x$ still outside $U$ lies chronologically before $b$ and then before a nearby point inside $U$, contradicting past convexity.

### H4.4 — spacelike restart removes every corresponding pair

Choose convex $V$ around $b$ with $V\cap\partial U\subset C$, and relatively compact causally convex $W\Subset V$ around $b$; choose $q\in W\cap I^+(b)$ close enough to satisfy H4.3. Such $W$ can be a chronological diamond between two close timelike points; push-up makes it causally convex and strong causality puts its compact closure in $V$.

Time separation $\tau_q(x)=\sup L(x\to q)$ is finite and continuous. On one compact diamond, a maximizing sequence has bounded auxiliary length and an H1 limit; length upper semicontinuity gives a maximizer. H0 makes each local piece radial and the absence of a length-improving corner makes it a geodesic. Upper semicontinuity for moving endpoints follows by putting all curves in a slightly larger compact diamond and applying H1. Lower semicontinuity follows by using the inner compact part of a timelike maximizing geodesic and joining nearby endpoints to its inner endpoints by short timelike segments; when the limiting distance is zero it is immediate. For $x\in W\cap I^-(q)$, causal convexity keeps maximizers in $W\subset V$, so H0 gives the exact formula

$$\tau_q(x)=\sqrt{-g_q(\exp_q^{-1}x,\exp_q^{-1}x)}.$$

Take the maximum $\tau_0>0$ on the compact set $\overline W\cap U^c\cap J^+(\Sigma)$. Every positive-distance point of that set lies in $W$ by the uncovered-past inclusion. A maximizing point cannot be interior to $U^c$: continue its radial timelike geodesic slightly to the past, still inside that open complement, above $\Sigma$ and inside $W$, to increase its distance. Hence all maximizers lie on $\partial U$, and

$$S=\{\tau_q=\tau_0\}\cap W\cap I^+(\Sigma)$$

is a smooth spacelike hypersurface contained in $U\cup C$ and meeting $C$. Smoothness and its timelike normal follow from the displayed radial formula and Gauss lemma. Every point just to its past within $W$ is in $U$: otherwise its larger positive $\tau_q$ would exceed the maximum. This past collar is essential for continuation.

The extended map $\widehat\psi$ identifies the induced metric, second form and selected matter data on $S$, since all jets agree at its boundary points by H4.2. E2/E3 local geometric uniqueness, applied to these actual induced data on this open hypersurface, supplies a common Cauchy neighborhood $N$ of $S$ and isometry $\phi:N\to M'$ extending its identification. Choose $N$ within $W$ in $M$ with a sufficiently small local product width. On $N\cap U$, $\phi$ and $\psi$ agree: the past collar supplies agreement in each component, and isometry open/closed continuation propagates it. A future point of $N\cap U$ has its backward product segment in $U$ by past convexity; if its point on $S$ were a boundary point, that would contradict past convexity, so every such component reaches the same collar.

Thus the maps patch on $U\cup N$. This union is Cauchy with surface $\Sigma$: an inextendible curve through $N\setminus U$ meets $S$ in its maximal $N$ component and then its past collar in $U$; its maximal $U$ component meets $\Sigma$. Every other curve already has such a $U$ component. Ambient Cauchy uniqueness forbids a second crossing. The patched immersion is an embedding by H4.1. Since $N$ contains a boundary point of $U$, this is a strictly larger common development. Consequently the MCD cannot have corresponding boundary points.

### H4.5 — pair gluing, set-sized universal development and second countability

Glue $M$ and $M'$ along the MCD map. The quotient maps are open injections: saturation of an open subset of $M$ is that subset together with its open image from $U$ in $M'$. Points represented in the same member separate there. Points in opposite members can fail separation only if they are a corresponding boundary pair, excluded by H4.4. The quotient is Hausdorff and the two original countable atlases give its smooth second-countable structure. Metric, orientation and matter agree on overlaps, so the field equations hold locally.

A maximal connected portion of an inextendible causal curve in either member is inextendible there and meets $\Sigma$. Two crossings would require switching members between them: the component in their common intersection at a switch would be an inextendible curve in the MCD missing $\Sigma$, impossible. Therefore the pair quotient is a common globally hyperbolic extension. The member images are causally convex by H4.1.

Every development is representable on an open neighborhood of $\Sigma\times\{0\}$ in $\Sigma\times\mathbb R$: choose a smooth future timelike field and its maximal flow starting on $\Sigma$. Every maximal flow curve is causally inextendible unless the ODE extends it, hence meets $\Sigma$ exactly once. Its domain is open, its flow smooth, and its derivative sends the initial tangent basis and transverse time vector to a basis. Thus it is a diffeomorphism onto the development, fixing the initial surface. Take **all** tuples of open domain, metric and selected finite-rank matter fields in these fixed bundles that satisfy the development definition. Graphs are subsets of fixed sets; power set and separation make this collection a set $\mathcal X$. No representatives of proper-class isometry classes are selected and no Zorn lemma is used.

In the disjoint union of $\mathcal X$, identify points through the unique pairwise MCD maps. This is transitive: the intersection in a middle member is a Cauchy subdevelopment by H4.1, and composed identification is a common development contained in the outer MCD. Each member and each pair quotient maps openly and injectively to the total quotient (the same saturation formula uses the pairwise open maps). Pair quotients separate any two inequivalent points, so the total quotient is Hausdorff and locally smooth with consistent metric/matter/time orientation.

Every inextendible causal curve meets $\Sigma$ by a maximal member component. It cannot meet twice: a compact curve segment between crossings is covered by finitely many member images. Repeated pair gluing gives one development containing this finite family; represent it in $\mathcal X$. Uniqueness of all embeddings fixing $\Sigma$ makes its image contain that segment and its identifications, so two crossings contradict its own Cauchy property. Hence $\Sigma$ is Cauchy in the total quotient.

For completeness, second countability of this uncountable gluing requires an argument beyond the union of atlases. Start with a countable dense subset of $\Sigma$. At each recursively obtained countable point choose a tangent basis and add endpoints of all rational timelike vectors whose exponential is defined, in both directions. This yields a countable set $D$. It is dense: every point is reached from $\Sigma$ by a finite broken timelike geodesic in one member; smooth dependence allows approximating its initial point by the chosen dense initial points and each successive vector by rational timelike vectors, keeping the final endpoint inside any prescribed open neighborhood. Countable choices are the same $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ already declared.

At each point of $D$ take the images of all rational tangent balls on which the exponential is a diffeomorphism. They are a countable family of open charts and cover the quotient. To check covering at $p$, fix a small convex normal neighborhood of $p$ with a larger convex chart. The joint inverse exponential theorem gives uniform inverse domains for centers in a smaller neighborhood of $p$; choose $d\in D$ there. The inverse vector to $p$ at $d$ lies in one rational ball compactly inside that domain, so its exponential chart contains $p$. Pull back the rational Euclidean bases of these countably many charts to obtain a countable base. This establishes the required manifold axiom without invoking a cited Geroch theorem.

The total quotient is consequently a smooth globally hyperbolic development, and every member embeds into it. Every arbitrary development has a representative in $\mathcal X$, so the quotient contains every development. If two developments have this universal property, their data-fixing embeddings in opposite directions compose to self-isometries fixing $\Sigma$; H4.1 makes each composition the identity. Thus they are isometric with their matter fields and initial embeddings. This proves the full smooth maximal-development claim for the stated local PDE branches.
