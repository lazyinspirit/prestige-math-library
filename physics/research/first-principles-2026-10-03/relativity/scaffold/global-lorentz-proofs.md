## H0 — convex normal neighborhoods and local causal tools

G2 proves local smooth geodesics and the exponential map. The joint endpoint map $(x,v)\mapsto(x,\exp_xv)$ has invertible derivative at $(p,0)$: its block derivative is $(\delta x,\delta v)\mapsto(\delta x,\delta x+\delta v)$ in one chart. The reviewed inverse theorem therefore gives a smooth inverse for pairs of nearby points and small initial velocities. Fix a coordinate ball where $|\Gamma(v,v)|\le C|v|^2$ and shrink so all these small-velocity segments stay in a larger ball. Along a geodesic, $f(x)=|x|^2$ satisfies $f''=2|v|^2-2x\cdot\Gamma(v,v)\ge(2-2C|x|)|v|^2>0$ there. Thus the joining segments of any two points in a smaller ball stay in it, since a convex real function has its maximum at an endpoint.

There cannot be a second geodesic wholly contained in this small ball with a large initial velocity. If $v_0=|v(0)|$, then on $0\le t\le\delta=\min(1,(4Cv_0)^{-1})$ the ODE bound gives $|v(t)|\le4v_0/3$ and $|v(t)-v(0)|\le4v_0/9$. Its displacement projected along $v(0)$ is at least $5v_0\delta/9$. If $v_0>4r$ and $r<1/(100C)$ this exceeds the diameter $2r$ of the ball, a contradiction (the $C=0$ case is straight motion). Hence every contained joining geodesic lies in the small-velocity inverse domain and is unique. For every center in the ball the corresponding inverse exponential domain is star-shaped: each initial segment of a contained radial geodesic remains in the ball by the same convexity argument. This proves the smooth convex normal-neighborhood theorem for a nondegenerate connection, not just a point-normal chart.

Shrink a chart so $dx^0$ is future positive and its gradient past timelike; compact normalized cone sections then give $|\dot{\mathbf x}|\le K\dot x^0$ for every future causal tangent in that patch. Integrating gives the corresponding bound for locally absolutely continuous causal curves. A causal curve can therefore be parametrized locally by $x^0$ as a Lipschitz spatial graph. In a convex normal neighborhood, causal reachability is exactly the future exponential cone, and its proper-length time separation is the nonnegative square root of minus the squared inverse exponential displacement on that cone, zero otherwise. The timelike maximum is C1's Gauss lemma. To justify the causal boundary as well, the same lemma gives $dQ(\dot\alpha)=2g(T,\dot\alpha)$ for $Q=g_p(v,v)$ and radial $T=d\exp_p(v)$. Away from the vertex, in a future oriented local rest frame,

$$g(T,z)\le (|\mathbf T|-T^0)z^0=\frac{Q}{|\mathbf T|+T^0}z^0\quad(Q\ge0, z\text{ future causal}).$$

Thus the positive part $Q_+$ cannot become positive after starting at zero: $Q_+'\le C Q_+|\dot\alpha|$ on compact subregions away from the vertex, and integrating the inequality gives $Q_+=0$. For a curve starting at the vertex, first prepend a short timelike segment from a nearby past point and apply this barrier away from that new vertex; take the past point to the original vertex using the smooth joint inverse exponential map. This proves causal containment without an invalid inference from a zero boundary derivative. On the null boundary, orthogonality of a future causal tangent to its future radial null vector forces positive proportionality, by rest-frame Lorentz algebra. A causal curve with null-related endpoints is therefore that null geodesic; a broken null segment with a genuine corner can be replaced locally by a timelike curve. Finite covers by these neighborhoods give openness of $I^\pm$ and the push-up rule $p\ll q\le r\Rightarrow p\ll r$, with its dual, by extending the first timelike segment through each succeeding local causal segment. These are the exact local tools used below.

The continuous/local-order and absolutely-continuous causal conventions agree after these local time reparametrizations: one direction follows by integrating cone bounds; the other gives a Lipschitz graph with future causal derivative almost everywhere. The nonsingular derivative condition is retained up to monotone reparametrization. A merely a.e. causal derivative without absolute continuity would be insufficient, as the Cantor-function counterexample in Minguzzi's source shows.

## H1 — complete auxiliary metric, limit curves and length

For the supplied smooth future unit field $n$, $h=g+2n^\flat\otimes n^\flat$ is a positive smooth metric. The actually read published proper-exhaustion theorem supplies a smooth nonnegative proper $f$. Put $h'=(1+|df|_h^2)h$. Then $|df|_{h'}\le1$, so a curve of finite $h'$ length cannot escape every sublevel of $f$. A Cauchy sequence for the induced metric distance is eventually in one such compact sublevel; its coordinate-topology accumulation point is its metric limit since smooth positive local metrics give the same topology. Thus $h'$ is complete. The reviewed positive-metric Hopf–Rinow/proper-length-space supplier then makes its closed metric balls compact. Positivity is used only for this auxiliary Riemannian metric.

Parametrize causal curves by $h'$ length. They are 1-Lipschitz in its distance; in a compact local time chart, the preceding causal cone bound gives $l_{h'}(\alpha)\le K\Delta x^0$. This prevents a limit of length-parametrized curves from becoming constant on a nonempty parameter interval. On each fixed compact parameter interval the curves lie in one compact metric ball around their converging initial points and are equi-Lipschitz. The actual proper-target Ascoli subsequence theorem gives uniform convergence; a diagonal sequence over integer intervals gives convergence on compact intervals. The closed local causal relation in H0 makes the limit causal, and the length/time bound just given makes it nonconstant. A limit on an unbounded length interval is inextendible: if it approached a finite endpoint, its tail would lie in a local time chart with finite time-coordinate range, contradicting the inherited lower time-increment bound on consecutive unit length intervals. Conversely a finite total $h'$ length gives a metric Cauchy endpoint by completeness and hence an extendible causal image. These arguments supply the basic limit-curve lemma rather than relying on an unexamined citation.

The exact two-endpoint result needed below is Minguzzi, *Limit curve theorems in Lorentzian geometry*, arXiv:0712.3942v2, Theorem3.1(2), statements pp11–13 and full proof pp14–17, with Lemmas2.5–2.7 pp6–8. It was actually read. For causal curves from $p_k\to p$ to $q_k\to q$, either bounded length gives a joining limit curve or unbounded length gives a future-inextendible limit from $p$ and a past-inextendible limit to $q$, and every point of the first and every point of the second satisfy the relation in the closure of $J$. Its proof is now fully supplied: extend the sequence endpoints, apply the basic lemma above twice and note that for sufficiently long original parameter intervals each fixed initial-tail point precedes each fixed terminal-tail point. Passing to limits gives the closure relation. If $p=q$ the two tails concatenate; bounded nonzero lengths give a closed causal loop. Strong causality forbids total or partial compact imprisonment: a compact accumulation point has a neighborhood that no causal curve can exit and reenter, while a tail confined to that neighborhood has bounded length by its local time bound. This establishes the precise inextendibility/non-imprisonment uses.

For length upper semicontinuity, the actually read Minguzzi living review (2019), Theorems2.37/2.41 pp41–44, supplies both the integral/interpolating-geodesic length equality and its full proof. The equality uses refining dyadic partitions inside finite convex/time charts: geodesic ODE bounds make the derivative of an interpolating segment converge at every differentiability point to the curve derivative, and the local time-speed bound supplies a common integrable bound, so dominated convergence identifies lengths. Each segment's maximum property supplies the reverse bound. Given a uniformly convergent causal sequence, a fixed sufficiently fine partition has its endpoints in the same convex neighborhoods; the local time separation is continuous by the smooth inverse exponential formula. Sum their local bounds and let the partition's excess length tend to zero. Hence $\limsup L(\alpha_k)\le L(\alpha)$. No statement about Euclidean length upper semicontinuity is substituted for this Lorentzian result.

## H2 — Cauchy equivalence and global causal compactness

A Cauchy set under our convention is a subset met exactly once by every inextendible causal curve. It is acausal, since a causal segment between two distinct points can be extended and would meet it twice. It is closed: a limit point outside it lies timelike before or after its unique intersection with an inextendible timelike curve, and chronological openness then places nearby points of the set timelike before/after that intersection, contradicting acausality. The same argument gives $M=I^-(S)\sqcup S\sqcup I^+(S)$. In local coordinates whose vertical lines are timelike flow lines, each sufficiently nearby line meets $S$ between one point in $I^-(S)$ and one in $I^+(S)$; acausality and the local cone bounds give a Lipschitz graph. Thus it is an embedded topological hypersurface. This establishes the terminology, not merely the intersection count.

If such an $S$ exists, strong causality follows from H1. Otherwise arbitrarily small-endpoint causal curves escape a fixed neighborhood. A bounded length subsequence gives a closed causal loop, impossible for a Cauchy set. An unbounded subsequence gives a two-sided inextendible limit with the reverse-pair closure relation from H1. That limit crosses $S$; choose points $a\in I^+(S)$ and $b\in I^-(S)$ on its two sides. The relation $(a,b)\in\overline J$ and openness of chronology give a timelike connection between two points of $S$, a contradiction. Now suppose a causal diamond were noncompact or nonclosed. Join $p$ to its offending sequence $r_k$ to $q$. If lengths were bounded, H1 gives a joining limit containing the limit of $r_k$ and lying in a compact ball, excluding both possibilities. If lengths are unbounded, H1 again gives future/past inextendible tails with the closure relation; each tail has a point respectively in $I^+(S)$ and $I^-(S)$, producing the same achronality contradiction. Hence all diamonds are compact. This is the full Cauchy-to-global-hyperbolicity deduction used by E2 and the gluing theorem.

Conversely suppose strong causality and compact diamonds. The local causal bound and finitely many causally convex chart neighborhoods on a compact diamond bound the total auxiliary length of every contained causal curve: each chart is traversed at most once and each traversal is bounded by its local time range. H1 then makes $J\subset M\times M$ closed, since curves with converging endpoints stay in a slightly larger compact diamond and have joining limits. Compact sets $K_1,K_2$ have compact $J^+(K_1)\cap J^-(K_2)$: cover the sets by finitely many chronological neighborhoods of earlier/later points, use the resulting finite union of compact diamonds, and then the closed-relation/compact-endpoint subsequence argument to prove closedness.

Choose a finite positive smooth volume density $\mu$ using a countable locally finite partition and positive summable weights, rescaled so total mass is one; each relatively compact partition term has finite integral. Chronological boundaries are achronal and locally Lipschitz graphs by the same local cone argument, hence have zero smooth volume by Fubini. Openness of chronology and closedness of $J$ now imply the indicators of $I^\pm(p_k)$ converge almost everywhere to those of $I^\pm(p)$ for $p_k\to p$; finite-measure dominated convergence gives continuity of their volumes. For $p<q$, their inclusions are strict in positive volume: $q\notin J^-(p)$ by causality, so a small open piece of $I^-(q)$ avoids the closed $J^-(p)$; use the dual argument to the future. Consequently

$$t(p)=\log\frac{\mu(I^-(p))}{\mu(I^+(p))}$$

is a continuous strictly increasing time function. Along a future-inextendible causal curve, the decreasing future sets have empty intersection; otherwise its tail would be imprisoned in one compact diamond, contradicting strong causality. Their measures tend to zero, while past measures have a fixed positive lower bound, so $t\to+\infty$. Dually $t\to-\infty$ at the past end. Every level is therefore Cauchy and acausal. These are the complete Geroch volume/time arguments, with the exact finite-measure and boundary-null premises supplied. A compact spacetime cannot satisfy them; no unsupported compact/global conclusion is made.

For a smooth spacelike acausal initial $S$ in a local product neighborhood with temporal coordinate $s$ and $S=\{s=0\}$, its domain of dependence is open and has $S$ Cauchy. Here $D^+(S)$ means points whose every past-inextendible causal curve meets $S$, and the dual defines $D^-(S)$. If a positive-time point in $D^+$ were not interior, curves from nearby points missing $S$ would have an inextendible past limit by H1, entirely in $s\ge0$; the required intersection with spacelike $S$ forces the limit to enter $s<0$, a contradiction. The negative-time argument is dual, and compact small cones put every initial point in the interior. Past portions from a point in $D^+$ remain there until $S$ because any missing-intersection curve could be concatenated to contradict the defining property; a positive-time endpoint escape would itself give such an inextendible curve. Thus inextendible curves in this open domain meet $S$, and the temporal coordinate gives uniqueness. The Cauchy implication above makes it globally hyperbolic. This is the exact local Cauchy-neighborhood restriction needed in E2, without assuming a uniform noncompact slab.

## H3 — smooth temporal Cauchy splitting

The exact published supplier is Bernal–Sánchez, arXiv:gr-qc/0401112v3 Theorem1.1 and §§2–3 (all9 PDF pages read), with the actual predecessor construction in arXiv:gr-qc/0306108v2 Lemmas4.12–4.13/Proposition4.14 (all12 PDF pages read). Hypotheses are smooth connected time-oriented globally hyperbolic Lorentzian manifold, any dimension at least two; no compactness or completeness of spacetime is assumed. It gives a smooth Cauchy temporal function $T:M\to\mathbb R$ and diffeomorphism $M\cong\mathbb R\times\Sigma$ with $g=-\beta dT^2+h_T$, $\beta>0$ smooth and $h_T$ positive smooth on each level. Source statements were checked against the following actual argument; the original Cauchy/timelike conventions cause no mismatch because the constructed temporal levels are spacelike and acausal and the limits below hold for causal curves too.

Start with H2's continuous Cauchy time $t$. In a convex normal neighborhood, the function $\exp(-1/\tau(p',q)^2)$ on the future timelike cone and zero outside it is smooth across the null boundary: every derivative has an inverse-power factor dominated by this exponential. Its gradient is past timelike where positive, by Gauss lemma. For a point on $S_b=\{t=b\}$ choose $p'$ sufficiently close below it so $J^+(p')\cap J^-(S_b)$ is in that neighborhood and compact. If this failed, compact Cauchy/diamond bounds would give a limiting point causally after the point of $S_b$ and before a distinct point of $S_b$, contradicting acausality. Multiply by a smooth cutoff equal to one on this compact past region; cutoff derivatives occur only outside it, preserving the required gradient sign there. Normalize its value at the selected point. Cover $S_b$ by these positive cone bumps. The proper auxiliary metric/shell compact-cover argument in the predecessor Lemma4.13 selects them with locally finite supports, so their sum is smooth, zero below $S_a$ and positive above a fixed threshold on $S_b$, with past timelike gradient in the transition. Its regular levels between $S_a,S_b$ are Cauchy directly: every inextendible curve crosses both bounding Cauchy levels and the transition value exactly once by the gradient sign. No unexamined intersection-theory theorem is needed for this particular construction.

The source's $h^+,h^-$ construction then produces a step $\hat\tau=2h^+/(h^+-h^-)-1$, constantly $+1$ outside its past support neighborhood, constantly $-1$ sufficiently far in the past. The denominator is positive wherever the quotient is used; its gradient is $2(h^+\nabla h^- -h^-\nabla h^+)/(h^+-h^-)^2$, a nonnegative combination of past timelike vectors, strictly timelike on the required level neighborhood. Time-reversal gives the negative bump. In source Lemma3.8 the stated cutoff derivative positivity at a plateau endpoint is understood on the open transition interval; a smooth plateau must have zero derivative at its endpoint. This repairs the endpoint wording without changing the construction.

To cover an entire finite $t$ strip, take compact exhaustions $K_j$, obtain a finite average of steps whose gradients are timelike around $K_j$, and choose $A_j>1$ bounding that step and all coordinate derivatives through order $j$ on the first $j$ compact chart regions. The weighted series $\sum_j2^{-j}A_j^{-1}\hat\tau_j$ and every finite derivative converge uniformly on compact chart regions, so the sum is smooth. Convexity of the past causal cone, with at least one strict timelike summand, proves its gradient timelike on the strip. Rescale by a smooth increasing scalar function to normalize its plateau values to $\pm1$. This is the actual full derivative-controlled series argument in Theorem3.11, not convergence of functions alone.

Finally choose these steps around integer levels with outer strips $(j-2,j+2)$ and sum $\tau_0+\sum_{j\ge1}(\tau_{-j}+\tau_j)$. The paired plateau constants cancel on every bounded $t$ strip for all sufficiently large $j$, so the sum is locally finite and smooth. At each point at least one step has past timelike gradient; the sum has that property. Along every inextendible causal curve the original $t$ tends to both infinities, and successively more steps take their future/past plateau values, so the new $T$ also tends to $\pm\infty$. Its levels are smooth spacelike Cauchy hypersurfaces. The vector field $X=\nabla T/g(\nabla T,\nabla T)$ satisfies $X(T)=1$. Its maximal flow lines are future timelike and meet each $T$ level exactly once; finite flow endpoints extend by the local ODE theorem, and an escape would contradict the Cauchy divergence of $T$. Thus its flow is defined for every level value. The local inverse theorem and uniqueness give a global diffeomorphism with $\mathbb R\times\Sigma_0$. Orthogonality to levels gives no cross terms and $g(X,X)=-\beta$, $\beta=-1/g(\nabla T,\nabla T)>0$. This completes the smooth orthogonal splitting and defines all its objects.

## H4 — maximal globally hyperbolic development, exact checked source

Let smooth connected initial data satisfy E2's constraints and the chosen matter hypotheses in E3 (or vacuum), with a specified initial embedding orientation. A development is a smooth Lorentzian solution with an embedding of the initial manifold as a Cauchy hypersurface, inducing the specified $h,K$ and matter data. An extension is a time-orientation-preserving isometric embedding fixing the initial data and carrying the matter fields. Maximal means every globally hyperbolic development of those data embeds into it; it does not mean every arbitrary non-globally-hyperbolic extension is excluded.

The adequate published global proof supplier is Jan Sbierski, arXiv:1309.7591, retrieved July3,2015 text, Theorems2.7–2.8, full §§2–3 proof pp10–24 actually read. Its stated vacuum restriction is explicitly generalized to matter/constant $\Lambda$ when local existence and geometric uniqueness hold; E0–E3 supply those exact premises and H0–H3 supply the causal/normal/time-separation prerequisites. The proof's actual uses are:

1. Isometries agreeing with their first derivative at one point agree on a connected domain by geodesic uniqueness in normal charts and open/closed continuation. Isometries fixing a spacelike initial hypersurface have the same first derivative because they agree tangentially and carry the future unit normal to the future normal. Thus all common developments have unique identifications on overlaps.
2. The union inside one fixed development of all common globally hyperbolic developments is the maximal common development. It remains Cauchy: any causal curve's component in a member must meet the shared initial hypersurface, and causal convexity/intersection uniqueness forbids another meeting. Compatible isometries patch uniquely, and the timelike-geodesic argument in Lemma3.3 proves the patch immersion is injective. No arbitrary union of unrelated Lorentzian regions is declared hyperbolic.
3. The crucial corresponding-boundary-point proof is source §§3.2 pp15–20, not an omitted gluing assumption. Corresponding points give matching limits along timelike curves by closedness of $J$, chronological openness and strong causality. Their identification extends smoothly to the boundary through the two normal exponential charts. Following a past null generator of the achronal common-domain boundary must reach a first exposed boundary point before the Cauchy surface; otherwise an inextendible causal curve would miss that surface. Compactness and the closed relation then give a nearby small causally convex region whose uncovered past is confined there. The continuous proper-time separation from a later point attains a maximum on its compact uncovered part. Its maximum level is a smooth spacelike hyperboloid piece (Gauss lemma), meeting the boundary and otherwise in the common domain. Its induced geometric and matter data agree by boundary continuity. Local geometric uniqueness E2 extends the common isometry across it, contradicting maximal commonality. Hence the maximal common development has no corresponding boundary pair. These are exactly the hypotheses/argument of Theorem3.6; the actual length-continuity/maximizing prerequisites are H1 and compact-limit arguments below, not a diagram.
4. Gluing two developments along this maximal common development is Hausdorff: potential nonseparable points must lie on opposite common boundaries and would be a forbidden corresponding pair. The quotient maps are open injections, so the original chart atlases give a smooth Lorentz metric, time orientation and matter fields agreeing on overlaps. Each causal curve meets the initial hypersurface once; an alleged second crossing would have a component inside the common development missing its Cauchy surface. This proves a common global extension, hence global uniqueness.
5. To avoid a proper-class union, use only representatives on open subsets of $\Sigma\times\mathbb R$ with canonical initial embedding. Every development has such a representative by the smooth timelike-flow map from its Cauchy surface (G2 and the same transversal argument as H3). Metric and finite-rank matter fields are graphs in fixed tensor/field bundles over this product; their admissible tuples form a set by power set and separation. Glue that set of representatives using the unique pairwise common identifications; transitivity follows because the intersection/composed identification is itself a common Cauchy development. Pairwise Hausdorff common extensions separate any two inequivalent points, and the same finite-cover curve argument gives the Cauchy property. The resulting representative contains every development and uniqueness follows by its universal embedding property. Maximal smooth development is not automatically maximal in every lower-regularity category; no such extra assertion is imported from the source's introductory discussion.

For completeness the two additional global prerequisites used in this proof have explicit checks. First, a globally hyperbolic spacetime has finite continuous time separation and maximizing causal geodesics between causally related distinct points: on a compact diamond take a length-supremum sequence; bounded auxiliary lengths/H1 give a joining limit and Lorentz upper semicontinuity gives its maximum. A curve with a nongeodesic local piece can be lengthened by H0, so the maximizing curve is a geodesic. Continuity follows by adding short timelike endpoint segments for lower semicontinuity and applying H1 to sequences of maximizing curves in one larger compact diamond for upper semicontinuity. The same compact-Cauchy intersection argument gives compact $J^-(q)\cap J^+(\Sigma)$ in a development and a maximum from the initial hypersurface. This is the actual proof in Minguzzi living review Theorems3.45–3.48/4.123–4.124 pp99–102,148, inspected with exact hypotheses.

Second, the unrestricted set quotient needs second countability; a union of uncountably many chart atlases alone would not prove it. Here every point is joined timelike to the common second-countable initial hypersurface inside one of the original developments. Start with a countable dense subset of that hypersurface. At each recursively obtained countable point choose a tangent basis and add endpoints of all rational timelike initial vectors whose exponential is defined, in both time directions. Smooth geodesic dependence and approximation of a connecting timelike curve by finitely many short timelike geodesics show this countable union is dense in the quotient. At every such point take all rational tangent balls on which its exponential is nonsingular and injective; their images form a countable family of open charts. They cover the whole quotient: choose a small convex normal neighborhood of any point, a dense selected point inside it and its inverse exponential vector to the original point; that vector lies in one of the eligible rational balls. The inverses of Euclidean rational bases in these countably many charts give a countable base. This supplies the exact quotient second-countability step without presuming it from an arbitrary uncountable union or relying solely on the source's cited Geroch theorem.

The global theorem concerns smooth developments of the selected classical PDE models. Singular/shock/free-boundary or rough-metric extensions need their own class-specific arguments. Neither maximal development nor smooth splitting proves cosmic censorship, collapse settling, geodesic completeness, Einstein global stability for arbitrary data or a quantum reconciliation.
