## S0 — `def-sr-affine-minkowski-model`

Let $V$ be a real vector space of dimension four, $A$ a nonempty set with a free transitive additive $V$ action, and $\eta:V\times V\to\mathbb R$ a symmetric nondegenerate bilinear form with one negative and three positive directions. For $p,q\in A$, $q-p$ is the unique vector taking $p$ to $q$. Supply an orientation of $V$ and a component $C^+$ of $\{v:\eta(v,v)<0\}$. The affine atlas consists of $x(p)=E^{-1}(p-o)$ for origins $o\in A$ and linear isomorphisms $E:\mathbb R^4\to V$; transitions are affine bijections, so this gives a Hausdorff second-countable smooth manifold diffeomorphic to $\mathbb R^4$. Its tangent spaces are canonically $V$. No origin is distinguished. An oriented inertial frame is $(o,e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3)$ with $e_0\in C^+$, positive orientation and $\eta(e_a,e_b)=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)_{ab}$. An inertial chart is its global affine chart; a general chart is a smooth diffeomorphism of an open subset onto an open subset of $\mathbb R^4$.

For completeness the diagonal basis exists without a positive-definite metric hypothesis. A nonzero symmetric form has some $v$ with $\eta(v,v)\ne0$, since otherwise polarization makes the form zero. Split $V=\mathbb Rv\oplus v^\perp$ by $w=(\eta(v,w)/\eta(v,v))v+w_\perp$. The restriction to $v^\perp$ is nondegenerate: a vector orthogonal there and to $v$ is orthogonal to all of $V$ and is zero. Iterate this finite construction and rescale each vector by $\sqrt{|\eta(v,v)|}$. The inertia hypothesis supplies exactly one negative sign. In that basis timelike vectors satisfy $|v^0|>|\mathbf v|$. The sets $v^0>0$ and $v^0<0$ are the two components: each is convex by the Euclidean triangle inequality and they cannot join without crossing $v^0=0$. Choose the first as $C^+$. A sign reversal and spatial sign adjustment make a frame future and oriented. Future causal vectors are $v\ne0$ with $v^0\ge |\mathbf v|$; null means equality and timelike strict inequality. Spacelike means $\eta(v,v)>0$. The zero vector is excluded from causal tangent vectors but may be included in the closed cone for algebraic sums.

Displacement components $x^a$ are in metres, $x^0=ct$, $c>0$ in metres/second; metric components in length coordinates are dimensionless. The oriented volume form is $\varepsilon=dx^0\wedge dx^1\wedge dx^2\wedge dx^3$. Thus $\varepsilon_{0123}=+1$, but $\varepsilon^{0123}=-1$ after raising all four indices with $\eta$. The spatial alternating symbol has $\epsilon_{123}=+1$. Euclidean norms and cross products below belong to the positive spatial three-space of the named inertial frame, not an invariant four-dimensional norm.

## S2 — `thm-sr-proper-orthochronous-lorentz-algebra`

A linear Lorentz map $L$ satisfies $L^T\eta L=\eta$; hence it is invertible and $(\det L)^2=1$. Proper means $\det L=1$; orthochronous means $L(C^+)=C^+$. An affine Lorentz map is $x'=Lx+b$, $b\in\mathbb R^4$. For $\mathbf v\in\mathbb R^3$, $|\mathbf v|<c$, write $\boldsymbol\beta=\mathbf v/c$ and $\gamma=(1-|\boldsymbol\beta|^2)^{-1/2}$. The passive boost is

$$x'^0=\gamma(x^0-\boldsymbol\beta\cdot\mathbf x),\qquad \mathbf x'=\mathbf x+\frac{\gamma-1}{|\boldsymbol\beta|^2}(\boldsymbol\beta\cdot\mathbf x)\boldsymbol\beta-\gamma\boldsymbol\beta x^0.$$

At $\boldsymbol\beta=0$ the boost is the identity. Resolve spatial vectors into their component along $\boldsymbol\beta$ and its perpendicular complement. The perpendicular components are fixed; on the time/parallel plane the matrix is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\gamma&-\gamma\beta\\-\gamma\beta&\gamma\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Expansion proves metric preservation and determinant one; $x^0>|\mathbf x|$ gives $x'^0>0$. The inverse is the boost with $-\boldsymbol\beta$. This proves all asserted properties, including null-cone preservation by continuity or direct equality. Composition is group multiplication, so inverse/composition preserve all defining properties. Collinear boosts have parameter $(\beta_1+\beta_2)/(1+\beta_1\beta_2)$ by multiplying these two-by-two matrices.

A $C^2$ metric-preserving diffeomorphism $F:A\to A$ is necessarily affine. In affine charts let $A_i=\partial_iF$ and $C_{ijk}=\eta(\partial_i\partial_jF,A_k)$. Differentiating $\eta(A_j,A_k)=\eta_{jk}$ gives $C_{ijk}=-C_{ikj}$, while commuting partials gives $C_{ijk}=C_{jik}$. Iterating these two symmetries yields $C_{ijk}=C_{jik}=-C_{jki}=-C_{kji}=C_{kij}=C_{ikj}=-C_{ijk}$, hence zero. Since the $A_k$ are a basis, every second derivative of $F$ vanishes. Integrating the now constant first derivative along line segments in the global affine chart gives $F(x)=Lx+b$, and its metric-preservation equation gives $L^T\eta L=\eta$. Thus smooth global affine isometry classification is proved; preserving orientation/future further selects the named component.

Every proper orthochronous $L$ is a boost followed or preceded by an oriented spatial rotation. Indeed $u=Le_0$ is future unit timelike. A boost $B$ with $\boldsymbol\beta=\mathbf u/u^0$ sends $u$ to $e_0$ because $u^0=\sqrt{1+|\mathbf u|^2}$. Then $BL$ fixes $e_0$, preserves its orthogonal complement and acts there as $R\in SO(3)$, proving $L=B^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(1,R)$. A boost that fixes $e_0$ is the identity, so this specified boost/rotation factorization is unique. This proves a classification of linear Lorentz maps in the chosen component, not that every Lorentz map is a single exponential or boost. Noncollinear boosts may contain a spatial rotation.

For any $C^1$ path $L(s)$ of Lorentz maps with $L(0)=I$, its derivative $K$ satisfies $K^T\eta+\eta K=0$. Conversely the matrix exponential power series converges absolutely on bounded $s$ by $\|K^n\|\le\|K\|^n$, permits termwise differentiation and satisfies $(e^{sK})'=Ke^{sK}$. Differentiating $e^{sK^T}\eta e^{sK}$ gives zero, proving it is a Lorentz path in the proper future component. The generators $(M_{ab})^\mu{}_{\nu}=\delta_a^\mu\eta_{b\nu}-\delta_b^\mu\eta_{a\nu}$ obey

$$[M_{ab},M_{cd}]=\eta_{bc}M_{ad}-\eta_{ac}M_{bd}-\eta_{bd}M_{ac}+\eta_{ad}M_{bc}.$$

To verify, multiply the two rank-one differences, producing four products $\delta_a^\mu\eta_{bc}\eta_{d\nu}-\delta_a^\mu\eta_{bd}\eta_{c\nu}-\delta_b^\mu\eta_{ac}\eta_{d\nu}+\delta_b^\mu\eta_{ad}\eta_{c\nu}$, and subtract the expression with $(a,b)$ and $(c,d)$ interchanged. Pairing matching terms gives the displayed four generators. Parity/time reversal are outside the adopted component. Rapidity is $r=\frac12\log((1+\beta)/(1-\beta))$; defining $\sinh r=(e^r-e^{-r})/2$, $\cosh r=(e^r+e^{-r})/2$ gives $\beta=\tanh r$ and $\gamma=\cosh r$. ## S4 — `thm-sr-proper-time-and-observer-decomposition`

Let $x:I\to A$ be $C^1$ on an interval with nonzero future timelike derivative. For $a<b$ in $I$, define $\tau(b)-\tau(a)=c^{-1}\int_a^b\sqrt{-\eta(\dot x,\dot x)}ds$. Piecewise $C^1$ curves use a finite partition of each compact subinterval with future timelike one-sided derivatives. A continuous positive integrand makes $\tau$ strictly increasing, with derivative the integrand: the difference quotient is an average over the small interval and continuity makes its difference from the value tend to zero. Locally the derivative has a positive lower bound, so the inverse is continuous and differentiable, since its difference quotient is the reciprocal quotient of $\tau$; the derivative is $1/\tau'$. For increasing $C^1$ reparametrizations with positive derivative, ordinary substitution proves the integral unchanged: differentiate the integral antiderivative composed with the reparametrization and use the preceding derivative formula. Thus proper-time velocity $U=dx/d\tau$ satisfies $\eta(U,U)=-c^2$. If $x$ is $C^2$, differentiating gives $\eta(U,A)=0$, $A=dU/d\tau$. These are complete local parametrization arguments, not clock behavior or worldline completeness.

An observer at an event is a future unit vector $n$, $\eta(n,n)=-1$, representing $U_o=cn$. Its rest space is $n^\perp$, and $h(v)=v+\eta(n,v)n$ is the spatial projection. Algebra gives $h^2=h$, $h(n)=0$ and $\eta(n,h(v))=0$. S2 sends $n$ to $e_0$, proving $n^\perp$ positive definite. Uniquely $v=-\eta(n,v)n+h(v)$. For future unit $u=U/c$, put $\gamma_o=-\eta(n,u)\ge1$ and $\mathbf w=c\,h(u)/\gamma_o\in n^\perp$. Then $u=\gamma_o(n+\mathbf w/c)$ and $|\mathbf w|^2/c^2=1-\gamma_o^{-2}<1$. The inequality follows in the rest basis: $u^0=\sqrt{1+|\mathbf u|^2}\ge1$. This is a pointwise observer-relative velocity; it does not compare remote GR vectors.

In an inertial chart $U=\gamma(c,\mathbf w)$. Applying a boost with speed $v$ along the first axis and dividing its transformed spatial components by transformed time gives

$$w'_1=\frac{w_1-v}{1-vw_1/c^2},\qquad \mathbf w'_\perp=\frac{\mathbf w_\perp}{\gamma_v(1-vw_1/c^2)}.$$

The denominator is positive for $|\mathbf w|,|v|<c$. Differentiating $U=\gamma(c,\mathbf w)$ with $d/d\tau=\gamma d/dt$, $\mathbf a=d\mathbf w/dt$, gives $A^0=\gamma^4(\mathbf w\cdot\mathbf a)/c$ and $\mathbf A=\gamma^2\mathbf a+\gamma^4(\mathbf w\cdot\mathbf a)\mathbf w/c^2$. Therefore $\eta(A,A)=\gamma^4|\mathbf a|^2+\gamma^6(\mathbf w\cdot\mathbf a)^2/c^2\ge0$. If $A$ is parallel to $U$ orthogonality forces $A=0$.

## S5 — clocks, simultaneity, radar and Doppler

Comparing equal primed times is a different pair of events. For unprimed events with $\Delta t=0$, $\Delta t'=-\gamma_v v\Delta x^1/c^2$, showing frame-dependent simultaneity.

Radar synchronization for the inertial observer $x^i=0$ is an explicit two-null-segment protocol: emission $t_-$, ideal instantaneous reflection at $(t,\mathbf r)$, reception $t_+$. Null straight segments give $t-t_-=|\mathbf r|/c=t_+-t$, so assigned radar time $(t_-+t_+)/2=t$ and distance $c(t_+-t_-)/2=|\mathbf r|$. Availability of the ray/reflector and ideal clock is assumed. This proves equivalence of this protocol with the named inertial chart, not arbitrary accelerated global synchronization.

## S6 — `thm-sr-future-cone-and-flat-domain-of-dependence`

Finite sums of future causal vectors are causal or zero since $\sum v_i^0\ge\sum|\mathbf v_i|\ge|\sum\mathbf v_i|$. If one summand is timelike the sum is timelike. For any nonconstant piecewise $C^1$ future causal path, integrating $\dot x^0\ge|\dot{\mathbf x}|$ gives $D^0\ge|\mathbf D|$; a timelike path gives strict inequality by positivity of the continuous difference on each nonempty smooth piece. Conversely a future causal displacement is joined by the straight causal segment. Thus $J^+(p)=p+\overline C^+$ and $I^+(p)=p+C^+$ in affine Minkowski geometry, with $p$ included in $J^+(p)$ by the constant-path convention. Future nonconstant causal paths strictly increase $x^0$, so cannot form a closed loop. This is a kinematic cone/reachability result; a PDE's support inside these cones requires its separate finite-propagation proof in EM.

Flat proper-time extremality has no curved analogue without further geometry. For a timelike curve with endpoints $p,q$, the displacement is timelike by the integral inequality. A boost sends $q-p$ to $(T,0,0,0)$, $T=\sqrt{-\eta(q-p,q-p)}$. Pointwise $\sqrt{(\dot x^0)^2-|\dot{\mathbf x}|^2}\le\dot x^0$; integrating gives $c\Delta\tau\le T$. Equality forces all spatial derivatives zero on all pieces by continuity, so precisely the monotone straight image maximizes. The reverse Cauchy inequality $-\eta(v,w)\ge\sqrt{-\eta(v,v)}\sqrt{-\eta(w,w)}$ follows by sending $v$ to $(a,0)$; then $w^0\ge\sqrt{-\eta(w,w)}$, with equality exactly for a positive proportional vector. An ideal two-leg traveler with speeds $\pm v$ and total laboratory duration $2T$ has elapsed time $2T\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}$; the laboratory observer has $2T$. The ideal instantaneous corner is a piecewise model; a finite acceleration phase requires its actual integral.

For the conditional superluminal countermodel assume signals at fixed speed $u>c$ in both directions in *each emitter's* rest frame, unrestricted emission and relaying, and ordinary SR boosts of their spacelike event separations. A rests at $x=0$; B has worldline $x=L+vt$, $L>0$, $0<v<c$. Emit at A's $(t,x)=(0,0)$: outgoing ray $x=ut$ meets B at $t_1=L/(u-v)$, $x_1=ut_1$. B returns a ray with primed velocity $-u$. Transforming its displacement, $\Delta t=\gamma\Delta t'(1-vu/c^2)$ and $\Delta x=\gamma\Delta t'(v-u)$, imposes $\Delta x=-x_1$, hence $\Delta t'=x_1/[\gamma(u-v)]>0$. Arrival at A is $t_2=L(2u-v-vu^2/c^2)/(u-v)^2$. Choose $v>2u/(1+u^2/c^2)$, which is possible since $u>c$ makes this bound less than $c$. Then $t_2<0$. The conclusion is conditional on precisely these signaling assumptions, not a claim that all superluminal phenomena or phase velocities create such loops.

## S7 — `pthm-sr-momentum-and-four-force`

Differentiating the mass shell gives $\eta(P,F)=0$. Differentiating $E^2=m^2c^4+c^2|\mathbf p|^2$ gives $dE/dt=\mathbf f\cdot\mathbf w$, so $F=\gamma(\mathbf f\cdot\mathbf w/c,\mathbf f)$. ## S8 — `thm-sr-antisymmetric-observer-decomposition`

Let $F$ be any alternating covariant two-tensor on the oriented Lorentz vector space. Define its Hodge dual by $(*F)_{ab}=\frac12\varepsilon_{ab}{}^{cd}F_{cd}$; define wedge $(\alpha\wedge\beta)_{ab}=\alpha_a\beta_b-\alpha_b\beta_a$. For future unit $n$, put $e_a=F_{ab}n^b$ and $b_a=(*F)_{ab}n^b$. Both annihilate $n$ by antisymmetry. Then

$$F=n^\flat\wedge e-*(n^\flat\wedge b),\qquad *^2F=-F.$$

Proof in the oriented rest frame $n=e_0$: $e_i=F_{i0}$, $b_i=-\frac12\epsilon_{ijk}F_{jk}$. In this basis $F_{0i}=-e_i$, $F_{ij}=-\epsilon_{ijk}b_k$, proving reconstruction of all six independent components. Directly $*(dx^0\wedge dx^1)=-dx^2\wedge dx^3$ and $*(dx^2\wedge dx^3)=dx^0\wedge dx^1$, with cyclic spatial permutations, proving $*^2=-1$ on the six basis bivectors. This also verifies the displayed reconstruction signs. The tensor/volume transformation law S3 transfers the proof to any oriented rest frame. These definitions establish generic algebra; EM must choose the dimensions and identification with its measured $\mathbf E,\mathbf B$. A common SI convention is $F^{i0}=E_i/c$, $F^{ij}=-\epsilon_{ijk}B_k$, which gives $e_i=-E_i/c$, $b_i=B_i$ in this particular generic definition. Stating this sign conversion prevents silently mixing $F_{i0}$ and $F^{i0}$.

For a passive boost parallel to axis one with speed $v$, direct multiplication $F'^{ab}=L^a{}_cL^b{}_dF^{cd}$ in that SI convention gives

$$E'_1=E_1,\quad B'_1=B_1,\quad \mathbf E'_\perp=\gamma_v(\mathbf E+\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)_\perp,\quad \mathbf B'_\perp=\gamma_v(\mathbf B-\mathbf v\times\mathbf E/c^2)_\perp.$$

For example $F'^{20}=\gamma(F^{20}-\beta F^{21})=\gamma(E_2/c-vB_3/c)$ and $F'^{31}=\gamma(F^{31}-\beta F^{30})=-\gamma(B_2+vE_3/c^2)$; the other transverse components follow by exchanging 2 and 3 with the alternating sign. Parallel components give $\gamma^2(1-\beta^2)F^{10}=F^{10}$ and $F^{23}$ fixed. Thus both invariants $|\mathbf B|^2-|\mathbf E|^2/c^2=\frac12F_{ab}F^{ab}$ and $\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B$ are invariant: in this optional SI convention $F_{0i}=E_i/c$, $F_{ij}=-\epsilon_{ijk}B_k$, so direct wedge expansion gives $F\wedge F=-2(\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B)\varepsilon/c$. Both $F\wedge F$ and $\varepsilon$ transform tensorially under proper Lorentz maps, proving the second invariant with its exact coefficient. Proper orientation is necessary for the pseudoscalar's sign. These are algebraic identities, not deductions of Maxwell equations or detector models.

## S9 — stress balance and finite flux

## S10 — accelerated observers and synchronization counterexample

Define $\sinh,\cosh$ as S2. For $\alpha>0$ in m/s², the worldline $(x^0,x^1)=(c^2/\alpha)(\sinh(\alpha\tau/c),\cosh(\alpha\tau/c))$ has $U=(c\cosh,c\sinh)$ and $A=(\alpha\sinh,\alpha\cosh)$; hence the stated norm identities follow from $\cosh^2-\sinh^2=1$. The chart $x^0=\rho\sinh\theta$, $x^1=\rho\cosh\theta$, $\rho>0$, maps bijectively onto the right wedge $x^1>|x^0|$, with inverse $\rho=\sqrt{(x^1)^2-(x^0)^2}$, $\theta=\frac12\log[(x^1+x^0)/(x^1-x^0)]$. Both maps are smooth on the specified open domains. Expanding differentials gives $-\rho^2d\theta^2+d\rho^2+dy^2+dz^2$. The stationary congruence $n=\rho^{-1}\partial_\theta$ is unit future; its proper time is $d\tau=\rho d\theta/c$ and acceleration magnitude $c^2/\rho$. The hypersurfaces $\theta=\mathrm{const}$ are orthogonal to $n$. Their induced spatial metric $d\rho^2+dy^2+dz^2$ is independent of $\theta$, proving Born rigidity, defined by $h_a{}^ch_b{}^d\partial_{(c}n_{d)}=0$, with covariant derivative when curvilinear coordinates are used. This is a wedge, not all spacetime; the null boundaries are limits of the chart and imply communication restrictions by S6, not a physical singularity.

The coefficient is antisymmetric relative to $\eta$: substituting two vectors makes the sum of their scalar-product derivatives zero. Taking $X=U$ gives its required derivative $A$. Thus an initially orthonormal tetrad with time vector $U/c$ remains orthonormal and adapted. Existence/uniqueness is the separate linear ODE obligation, not proved by the preservation calculation.

For a rotating observer ring of radius $R$ in a plane of Minkowski space, fix $\Omega R<c$ and worldlines $r=R$, $\phi=\phi_0+\Omega t$. In rotating angular coordinate $\varphi=\phi-\Omega t$, the induced cylinder metric is $-(c^2-\Omega^2R^2)dt^2+2\Omega R^2dt\,d\varphi+R^2d\varphi^2$. Hence no single-valued global ring time can have these rest-simultaneity level sets. This is the explicit guided-ray Sagnac model; free null straight rays do not circle by themselves.

## S11 — smooth local Herglotz–Noether classification

Here $\Omega$ is an open subset of the affine Minkowski four-space S0, $n$ a smooth future unit vector field ($g(n,n)=-1$), $a=\nabla_n n$, and $h=g+n^\flat\otimes n^\flat$. A Born motion means $\mathcal L_nh=0$, equivalently the projected symmetric derivative of $n^\flat$ is zero; its vorticity is $\omega_{ab}=h_a{}^ch_b{}^d\nabla_{[c}n_{d]}$. All derivatives use the flat connection and length parameters; SI velocity/acceleration are $cn,c^2a$. Statements below are local on flow boxes. On a simply connected connected region where $\omega$ is nowhere zero, the local rotational conclusion gives a single Killing field with the same oriented trajectories. No global orbit quotient is assumed to be a manifold.

The proof supplies the calculational omissions in Giulini, math-ph/0602018, §5.5, pp41–49 (actual arguments and limitations read). G2 supplies a local flow box. Born rigidity makes $h$ descend to its three-dimensional local orbit slice: in coordinates $(s,y)$ with $n=\partial_s$, $h_{si}=h_{ss}=0$ and $\partial_sh_{ij}=0$. Choose an orthonormal coframe $\theta^i$ for this positive slice metric, pulled back to the box; $i_n\theta^i=0$ and $\mathcal L_n\theta^i=0$. Put $\theta^0=-n^\flat$, so $\theta^0(n)=1$ and $g=-(\theta^0)^2+\sum_i(\theta^i)^2$. The slice has its nondegenerate Levi–Civita connection $\bar\gamma^i{}_j$ by G1, pulled back and therefore basic. Write $a=a_i\theta^i$, $\omega=\tfrac12\omega_{ij}\theta^i\wedge\theta^j$. The kinematic decomposition is $\nabla_an_b=-n_a a_b+\omega_{ab}$; contraction with $n$ and the Born condition verify both terms. Thus $d\theta^0=-\theta^0\wedge a-2\omega$ and $d\theta^i=-\bar\gamma^i{}_j\wedge\theta^j$.

Torsion-free metric connection forms are now explicitly $\gamma^0{}_i=\gamma^i{}_0=a_i\theta^0-\omega_{ij}\theta^j$, $\gamma^i{}_j=\bar\gamma^i{}_j-\omega_{ij}\theta^0$. Substitution verifies $d\theta^A=-\gamma^A{}_B\wedge\theta^B$ and the Lorentz skew conditions; uniqueness G1 identifies them. Applying $d\gamma+\gamma\wedge\gamma$ and taking purely horizontal components gives

$$0=\bar R_{ijkl}+2\omega_{ij}\omega_{kl}+\omega_{ik}\omega_{jl}-\omega_{il}\omega_{jk},\qquad 0=D_k\omega_{ij}-D_j\omega_{ik}-2a_i\omega_{jk}.$$

These two calculations follow respectively from the horizontal terms $d\bar\gamma^i{}_j-\omega_{ij}d\theta^0+\bar\gamma^i{}_k\wedge\bar\gamma^k{}_j+\omega_{ik}\omega_{jl}\theta^k\wedge\theta^l$, and $-2a_i\omega-D\omega_{ij}\wedge\theta^j$. Here $D$ is the slice covariant derivative on the displayed indices, and curvature is zero because the ambient metric is Minkowski. In three dimensions $\omega_{ij}\omega_{kl}-\omega_{ik}\omega_{jl}+\omega_{il}\omega_{jk}=0$: it is the component of $\omega\wedge\omega$, a four-form on a three-space. Hence $\bar R_{ijkl}=-3\omega_{ij}\omega_{kl}$. The left side is basic. Its $n$ derivative shows $(n\omega_{ij})\omega_{kl}+\omega_{ij}(n\omega_{kl})=0$. Wherever a component $\omega_{kl}\ne0$, first choose the same pair to obtain $n\omega_{kl}=0$, then every pair gives $n\omega_{ij}=0$. Therefore $\omega$ is basic. Taking the $n$ derivative of the second displayed curvature equation (the frame and $D$ are basic) gives $\omega_{jk}n(a_i)=0$, so $n(a_i)=0$ wherever $\omega\ne0$.

Applying $d^2$ to $\theta^0$ now gives $0=2\omega\wedge a+\theta^0\wedge da-2d\omega$. Since $\omega$ is basic, the term containing $\theta^0$ says $(da)_{\perp}=0$. Also $i_n da=\mathcal L_na=0$, because $a(n)=0$ and its coefficients in the basic frame are invariant. Thus $da=0$ on the rotational region. On a star-shaped coordinate box the explicit radial primitive $F(x)=\int_0^1 a_{tx}(x)dt$ obeys $dF=a$ by differentiating and using $\partial_i a_j=\partial_j a_i$. On a simply connected region integration along paths gives the same primitive: the integral is invariant under an endpoint-fixed homotopy by integrating $da=0$ over its coordinate subdivisions, and every loop contracts. Put $f=e^F>0$. The decomposition gives $\mathcal L_{fn}g=-f(n^\flat\otimes a+a\otimes n^\flat)+df\otimes n^\flat+n^\flat\otimes df=0$. Hence $K=fn$ is Killing. Conversely every timelike Killing field, normalized to unit future direction, satisfies projected rigidity. This proves the smooth rotational theorem with exact local/global-domain hypotheses; no analyticity is used.

For the irrotational case $\omega=0$, G2 Frobenius makes the rest planes leaves. Rigidity gives $\nabla_Xn=0$ for every leaf-tangent $X$, so $n$ is constant on a connected sufficiently small leaf. Its tangent space is therefore the constant hyperplane orthogonal to that $n$, and the leaf is an open subset of its affine hyperplane (coordinate integration along leaf curves proves its displacement stays in the plane). Choosing one flow line $z(s)$ parameterized by proper length, these leaves are the planes $g(z'(s),x-z(s))=0$. On a sufficiently small tubular box the implicit function theorem gives their unique label $s=\sigma(x)$, since its label derivative on the line is $g(z',-z')=1$. More generally this derivative is $1+g(z'',x-z)$, required nonzero on the chosen box. Set $n(x)=z'(\sigma(x))$; differentiation gives $d\sigma=-n^\flat/[1+g(z'',x-z)]$, and $\nabla n=d\sigma\otimes z''$. Its horizontal projection vanishes, proving both Born rigidity and zero vorticity. Conversely the preceding leaf argument proves every smooth irrotational Born motion has this form locally. Arbitrary smooth timelike $z$ is allowed while the tubular label remains unique with positive lapse; different planes may intersect outside that domain. This also explains the acceleration/size restriction in Rindler's example rather than asserting unlimited global rigid translation.
