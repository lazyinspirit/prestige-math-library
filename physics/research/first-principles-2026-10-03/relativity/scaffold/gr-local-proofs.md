# Lorentzian geometry, variation and matter arguments

2026-10-03 research development. All geometric results are mathematical conditional statements; physical adoption appears only in G7. These arguments extend M05–M07, M09–M13, M19, M21–M22 and part of M26. They use arbitrary nondegenerate signature until a timelike/spatial assertion explicitly requires $(-+++)$.

## G0 — objects and exact conventions

Let $M$ be a smooth Hausdorff second-countable four-manifold without boundary, $g$ a smooth symmetric nondegenerate covariant two-tensor of signature $(-+++)$, and specify time orientation by a smooth timelike field $N$, normalized as $n=N/\sqrt{-g(N,N)}$. Future nonzero causal vectors at $p$ satisfy $g(v,N_p)<0$. Specify a manifold orientation only for oriented integrations; time orientation does not supply it. Smooth means $C^\infty$; the second Bianchi proof uses three metric derivatives. Geodesic existence below requires at least a smooth connection in the invoked suppliers. No low-regularity/distributional extension is asserted. Local coordinates map open domains to open Euclidean sets; $T_pM$ can be defined by derivations on smooth function germs. In a chart each derivation is $v^a\partial_a$: for a function germ use $f(x)=f(p)+(x^a-x^a(p))\int_0^1\partial_af(p+s(x-p))ds$; Leibniz at $p$ kills the constant and evaluates the second factor at $p$. This proves the coordinate representation and hence transformation by the Jacobian using the chain rule.

Tensor fields are compatible smooth component families under the tensor transition rules. A tangent bundle chart is $(p,v)\mapsto(x(p),v^a)$; transitions $(x,v)\mapsto(y(x),Dy(x)v)$ are smooth bijections with smooth inverses. Hausdorffness follows by separating base points or two vectors in one chart. Assuming countable choice as in the published tangent supplier, select a countable subatlas; Euclidean chart bases then give second countability of the total space. Tensor bundles use finite multilinear fibres and the corresponding Jacobian/inverse-Jacobian transition laws; this same construction proves local products, overlap compatibility, Hausdorffness and second countability. This makes explicit the inherited $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ assumption; it is not a physics prerequisite. A tensor section is a field whose components in these charts are smooth. The inverse metric exists by nondegeneracy and is smooth by its adjugate/determinant formula. A local orthonormal tetrad is a smooth ordered basis field $e_A$ with $g(e_A,e_B)=\eta_{AB}$, time vector future; it is not automatically a coordinate frame or global tetrad. A congruence is a smooth future unit timelike field with its local integral curves. An adapted chart holds its three spatial coordinates constant along those curves. The precise flow existence follows from G2, not from the name congruence.

Length coordinates have $x^0=ct$, all $x^a$ in metres and dimensionless $g_{ab}$. In such coordinates $\Gamma$ has inverse-length units, curvature inverse-length-squared units; angular/time coordinates change individual component units by their Jacobians. $c>0$ has m/s, $U$ m/s, $\tau$ seconds. The proper-time/observer definitions S4 apply pointwise with $g_p$ and along curves with varying $g$, using the same positive-integrand inverse argument. A massive worldline is regular piecewise $C^1$ and future timelike, with $C^2$ required for acceleration. A null worldline has nonzero future null tangent and has no proper-time velocity. A geodesic is a $C^2$ curve with $D\dot\gamma/ds=0$ in an affine parameter. Physically interpreted curves require the ideal-clock/test-body premises separately.

## G1 — nondegenerate metric connection and induced tensors

An affine connection assigns $\nabla_XY$ to smooth local vector fields and is real bilinear, function-linear in $X$ and satisfies $\nabla_X(fY)=X(f)Y+f\nabla_XY$. The bracket is the commutator on smooth functions, with coordinates $[X,Y]^a=X^b\partial_bY^a-Y^b\partial_bX^a$. These definitions directly give $[X,fY]=X(f)Y+f[X,Y]$ and its swapped version. Define $K$ exactly as M05 and use nondegeneracy to set $2g(\nabla_XY,Z)=K(X,Y,Z)$. Explicit substitution of $fZ$ makes the $X(f)g(Y,Z)$ and $Y(f)g(X,Z)$ derivative terms cancel their bracket terms, leaving $fK$; thus it is a covector in $Z$. Substitution of $fX$ cancels $Y(f)$ and $Z(f)$ terms and gives $fK$. Substitution of $fY$ leaves $2X(f)g(Y,Z)$, giving the connection rule. The identities $K(X,Y,Z)-K(Y,X,Z)=2g([X,Y],Z)$ and $K(X,Y,Z)+K(X,Z,Y)=2Xg(Y,Z)$ prove zero torsion and metric compatibility. Conversely permute the three compatibility identities and use zero torsion to recover $K$, proving uniqueness. Smooth inverse components prove smoothness. Intrinsic uniqueness on overlaps proves patching without a global frame. This is a full extension to nondegenerate metrics, independent of positive-definite Levi–Civita suppliers.

For a covector define $(\nabla_X\alpha)(Y)=X(\alpha(Y))-\alpha(\nabla_XY)$. The connection rules cancel derivatives of any factor multiplying $Y$, giving a covector; coordinate components are $\nabla_a\alpha_b=\partial_a\alpha_b-\Gamma^d{}_{ab}\alpha_d$. For a tensor, differentiate its evaluation and subtract the evaluation with the derivative of each vector/covector argument. Expanding in a finite product frame gives exactly one $+\Gamma$ term for each upper slot and one $-\Gamma$ for each lower slot, as well as the derivative of its component. This defines a smooth tensor independent of frame, since its evaluation is intrinsic. For a contraction the two derivatives in its paired upper/lower factors cancel their $\Gamma$ terms, so differentiation commutes with contraction. The same finite expansion proves Leibniz and commutation with permutations. Thus $\nabla g=0$ also implies $\nabla g^{-1}=0$ by differentiating $g^{-1}g=I$. Along a smooth curve use $D_sV^a=dV^a/ds+\Gamma^a{}_{bc}\dot\gamma^bV^c$; applying the coordinate change and its second derivatives cancels the inhomogeneous connection term and gives vector transformation. This defines differentiation of arbitrary fields along the curve, without an ambient extension assertion.

## G2 — geodesics, local tetrads and charts

In a coordinate region the geodesic system on $(x,v)$ is $x'=v$, $(v^a)'=-\Gamma^a{}_{bc}(x)v^bv^c$. Its vector field is smooth on the open subset of $\mathbb R^8$; the reviewed `thm-picard-lindelof-local-existence-and-uniqueness` gives local unique solutions. Joint smooth initial/time dependence is supplied by the reviewed repaired `thm-smooth-dependence-of-ode-solutions-on-parameters`, applied to this jointly smooth autonomous field (no parameter variables are needed). Its explicit jet-system proof uses the highest jet to upgrade regularity, with uniform common compact intervals; this avoids relying on the older smooth-dependence proof's imprecise first-component upgrade sentence. Coordinate overlap uniqueness patches solutions; union of all agreeing local extensions through the fixed initial data produces a unique maximal solution on an open interval. There is no completeness assertion. Differentiating the norm gives $d[g(\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma)]/ds=2g(D_s\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma)=0$. Hence nonzero timelike/null initial tangents retain causal type, and continuity retains their future component. For timelike geodesics with initial norm $-c^2$, affine parameter is proper time up to origin; for null ones proper time is identically zero.

Linear transport $D_sV=0$ has continuous smooth coefficients on each compact subinterval of a smooth curve. The same local ODE theorem applies; norm and inner-product preservation follow from compatibility. Solutions cannot diverge on a compact subinterval: if coefficient norm is bounded by $C$, successive interval estimates give $|V(s)|\le |V(s_0)|e^{C|s-s_0|}$ (expand the integral inequality iteratively into $\sum(C|s-s_0|)^k/k!$). Cover the compact interval by finitely many coefficient neighborhoods and extend. This closes the Fermi–Walker existence assertion in S10 too, because its coefficient is smooth and linear on any compact smooth worldline subinterval. A transported adapted tetrad remains adapted by its norm/orthogonality identities.

For a local tetrad take one Lorentz orthonormal basis at $p$ by S0, extend its components constantly in a coordinate chart, and shrink so the first field remains timelike. Normalize it; subtract its component from the other fields to project into its positive orthogonal complement. Apply ordinary three-step Gram–Schmidt there. The denominators are positive at $p$ and remain positive on a neighborhood; square root is smooth on the positive reals. This proves a smooth future tetrad near each point with no global trivialization assertion. For a nonzero field, choose a three-dimensional coordinate slice transverse to its value at $p$. The local flow map $(s,q)\mapsto\Phi_s(q)$ has derivative at $(0,p)$ equal to the nonzero field in the $s$ direction and the identity on the slice; it is invertible. The reviewed smooth Euclidean inverse theorem supplies a local chart, where the field is $\partial_s$. This is the adapted chart construction. Orthogonal spatial leaves, however, require integrability; no such conclusion follows from the flow-box theorem alone.

For observer rest leaves set $\alpha=n^\flat$, a smooth nowhere-zero one-form, and $\mathcal D=\ker\alpha$, a smooth rank-three subbundle. The reviewed `cor-codimension-one-frobenius-criterion` gives local integral hypersurfaces precisely when $\alpha\wedge d\alpha=0$. Its actual argument is suitable here: $d\alpha(X,Y)=-\alpha([X,Y])$ for $X,Y\in\mathcal D$ proves equivalence to involutivity; in a local coframe extending $\alpha$, vanishing of the wedge means all components of $d\alpha$ have an $\alpha$ factor. Frobenius' frame-reduction proof was checked: in a flow box for one nonzero frame field, subtract the time components of the remaining fields; involutivity writes their time brackets as a smooth matrix times these transverse fields. Solve $\partial_tB=-C^TB$, $B(0,u)=I$ using G2's jointly smooth parameter-dependent ODE supplier, shrinking so $\det B\ne0$. The recombined fields commute with the time field. Restrict to the initial transverse slice and induct on rank, then transport its coordinate distribution along the flow. This proves the local coordinate-leaf theorem and supplies the implicit matrix ODE omitted from the reduction lemma's dependency list. Rank is constant and regularity smooth; no variable-rank or global simultaneity theorem follows. In a torsion-free connection $d\alpha_{ab}=2\nabla_{[a}n_{b]}$, so the rest-space projected antisymmetric derivative vanishes exactly under this Frobenius condition, as verified in an adapted orthonormal coframe.

There are two normal-coordinate claims. For vanishing connection at a point, after shifting $p$ to zero set $x^a=y^a-\frac12\Gamma^a{}_{bc}(p)y^by^c$; the derivative at zero is identity and the reviewed choice-free inverse theorem gives a chart. The connection transformation law gives $\Gamma'^a{}_{bc}(p)=0$; compatibility then gives $\partial'_cg'_{ab}(p)=0$. A further constant Lorentz basis change makes $g'(p)=\eta$. This proves a point-normal chart sufficient for G3–G5. For exponential normal coordinates, G2 smooth dependence defines $\exp_p(v)=\gamma_{p,v}(1)$ for small $v$; the scaling identity $\gamma_{p,sv}(t)=\gamma_{p,v}(st)$ follows by uniqueness, so $d\exp_p(0)v=v$. The inverse theorem gives a star-shaped smaller neighborhood with radial geodesics. This argument proves exponential coordinates near one point. It does not by itself prove a convex normal neighborhood for every pair of nearby points or the full cut/conjugate-point extremality theorem; H0 supplies the convex-neighborhood step and C1–C2 the exact local extremality/conjugacy statement.

## G3 — curvature, all symmetries and signed Bianchi

Define $R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_X\nabla_YZ-\nabla_Y\nabla_XZ-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z$ and $R^a{}_{bcd}\partial_a=R(\partial_c,\partial_d)\partial_b$. Function-linearity in $X$ follows from $[fX,Y]=f[X,Y]-Y(f)X$: its two $Y(f)\nabla_XZ$ terms cancel; skewness gives linearity in $Y$. In $Z$, the leftover scalar derivative is $(X(Yf)-Y(Xf)-[X,Y]f)Z=0$, while the first derivative cross terms cancel. Therefore curvature is a tensor. The coordinate expression is M06's expression. Lower the first index, $R_{abcd}=g_{ae}R^e{}_{bcd}$, and define $\operatorname{Ric}_{bd}=g^{ac}R_{abcd}$, $S=g^{bd}\operatorname{Ric}_{bd}$, $G_{bd}=\operatorname{Ric}_{bd}-Sg_{bd}/2$.

At an arbitrary point use G2's point-normal chart. The formula is

$$R_{abcd}=\tfrac12(g_{ad,bc}+g_{bc,ad}-g_{bd,ac}-g_{ac,bd}).$$

Interchanging $a,b$ or $c,d$ negates these four terms; exchanging the pairs preserves them by symmetry of $g$ and commuting derivatives. Adding the three cyclic permutations of $(b,c,d)$ cancels every second derivative pair, proving the algebraic first Bianchi identity. All expressions are tensors, so these identities hold in every chart. Pair exchange and dummy-index interchange then give symmetric Ricci: $g^{ac}R_{abcd}=g^{ac}R_{cdab}=g^{ac}R_{adcb}$ after relabeling $a,c$.

For the differential identity at the same normal point, differentiate the *full* curvature formula $\partial\Gamma+\Gamma\Gamma$. The derivative of each product has a factor $\Gamma(p)=0$, so

$$\nabla_eR^a{}_{bcd}=\partial_e\partial_c\Gamma^a{}_{db}-\partial_e\partial_d\Gamma^a{}_{cb}\quad\text{at }p.$$

The cyclic sum over $(e,c,d)$ is zero because six second derivatives cancel in pairs. Lowering and metric compatibility gives $\nabla_eR_{abcd}+\nabla_cR_{abde}+\nabla_dR_{abec}=0$. Contract with $g^{ac}$ to obtain $\nabla_e\operatorname{Ric}_{bd}+\nabla^aR_{abde}-\nabla_d\operatorname{Ric}_{be}=0$. Contract once more with $g^{bd}$. Since $g^{bd}R_{abde}=-\operatorname{Ric}_{ae}$ by first-pair skewness and the Ricci definition, this is $\partial_eS-2\nabla^a\operatorname{Ric}_{ae}=0$. Hence $\nabla^aG_{ae}=0$. All traces use $g^{-1}$; in an orthonormal tetrad they are $\sum_a\epsilon_a(\cdots)$ with $\epsilon_0=-1$, $\epsilon_i=1$. No unsigned Riemannian trace is consumed, and the conclusion is divergence-free, not $\nabla G=0$ in all slots.

For a smooth two-parameter family $\gamma(s,r)$ of affine geodesics, let $T=\partial_s\gamma$ and $J=\partial_r\gamma$ along the family. On its pullback bundle torsion freedom gives $D_sJ=D_rT$, since mixed coordinate partials commute. The curvature commutator is $(D_sD_r-D_rD_s)V=R(T,J)V$, checked by the same component expansion as M06 and valid even where the parameterization is not an immersion. Therefore $D_s^2J=D_sD_rT=D_rD_sT+R(T,J)T=R(T,J)T$. This proves the claimed Jacobi sign and geodesic-deviation equation, without assuming a globally extending variation field.

## G4 — density, divergence, finite-region flux

For an invertible differentiable matrix $A(s)$, differentiate its permutation determinant polynomial by one entry at a time; the cofactor formula gives $(\det A)'=\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}(A)A')=\det A\operatorname{tr}(A^{-1}A')$. Thus for $w=\sqrt{|\det g|}$, $\partial_aw=(w/2)g^{bc}\partial_ag_{bc}$. Contracting the Christoffel formula cancels its second and third terms after exchanging their dummy indices, giving $\Gamma^b{}_{ba}=\frac12g^{bc}\partial_ag_{bc}=w^{-1}\partial_aw$. Hence $\nabla_aJ^a=w^{-1}\partial_a(wJ^a)$ for a $C^1$ vector field. This proof uses no real logarithm of a Lorentz matrix.

On an oriented chart the volume form is $\mathrm{vol}_g=w\,dx^0\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^3$. Its transition is consistent since $\det g'=(\det Dx/Dy)^2\det g$ and an oriented transition has positive determinant. Define current flux through an oriented parameterized three-face $\sigma$ by $\int\sigma^*(\iota_J\mathrm{vol}_g)$, where interior product inserts $J$ in the first slot. In chart components the alternating omission formula for this three-form gives $d(\iota_J\mathrm{vol}_g)=\partial_a(wJ^a)d^4x$. A finite rectangular chart region satisfies Stokes by four applications of FTC as S9. A finite smooth singular four-chain satisfies it by the reviewed singular-chain Stokes theorem with this globally defined smooth three-form. These are complete admitted integration domains, not all arbitrary rough/noncompact regions. A non-null face additionally has an induced metric volume density and a unit normal $\nu$, and its pullback flux is $g(J,\nu)$ times its induced oriented density with the appropriate outward sign: choose an orthonormal basis with normal first and evaluate the four-volume determinant to verify this formula. For null faces use the three-form itself; no unit null normal is defined.

If a finite chain has boundary $\Sigma_2-\Sigma_1+B$ and $\nabla_aJ^a=0$, its fluxes satisfy $Q(\Sigma_2)-Q(\Sigma_1)=-Q(B)$. Equal charges therefore need zero side flux, not merely divergence zero. Infinite exhaustion additionally needs convergence of both slices and limiting side flux. This handles exact finite-chain flux consumers. The stronger arbitrary smooth-region and asymptotic charge developments remain separate obligations and are not hidden in this theorem.

## G5 — compact metric and particle variation

Work in an oriented coordinate domain, with a smooth metric family $g(s)$ of fixed signature near $s=0$ and variation supported in a fixed compact subset of that domain. Such a family exists for any prescribed compactly supported smooth symmetric variation: invertibility and inertia persist for sufficiently small $|s|$ by compactness and continuity. Write $q^{ab}=\delta g^{ab}$, $h_{ab}=\delta g_{ab}=-g_{ac}g_{bd}q^{cd}$. The determinant argument G4 gives $\delta w=-wg_{ab}q^{ab}/2$. The connection difference of two connections is a tensor, since the difference cancels the scalar derivative term in the Leibniz rule. In a point-normal chart differentiation of the Christoffel formula therefore proves intrinsically

$$\delta\Gamma^a{}_{bc}=\tfrac12g^{ad}(\nabla_bh_{cd}+\nabla_ch_{bd}-\nabla_dh_{bc}).$$

Differentiate the full curvature expression. At the same point its product variation has a zero $\Gamma$ factor; tensoriality gives $\delta R^a{}_{bcd}=\nabla_c\delta\Gamma^a{}_{db}-\nabla_d\delta\Gamma^a{}_{cb}$ everywhere. Contract to obtain $\delta\operatorname{Ric}_{bd}=\nabla_a\delta\Gamma^a{}_{db}-\nabla_d\delta\Gamma^a{}_{ab}$. Consequently

$$\delta(wS)=wG_{ab}q^{ab}+w\nabla_aV^a,\qquad V^a=g^{bd}\delta\Gamma^a{}_{db}-g^{ab}\delta\Gamma^d{}_{db}.$$

The family is smooth and supported in a fixed compact set, so difference quotients converge uniformly there to the derivative (use the integral average of the parameter derivative and its uniform continuity). This justifies differentiating under the integral. The density-divergence identity and coordinate FTC annihilate the compact-support divergence integral. With $S_{EH}=c^3(16\pi G_N)^{-1}\int(S-2\Lambda)w\,d^4x$, $G_N>0$, $\Lambda$ constant, this proves

$$\delta S_{EH}=\frac{c^3}{16\pi G_N}\int(G_{ab}+\Lambda g_{ab})q^{ab}w\,d^4x.$$

Define a chosen matter action's Hilbert tensor by $\delta S_m=-(2c)^{-1}\int T_{ab}q^{ab}w\,d^4x$ on shell in matter variables, with metric variations arbitrary as above. Stationarity of the total adopted action is equivalent to $G_{ab}+\Lambda g_{ab}=8\pi G_NT_{ab}/c^4$: if a continuous coefficient has a nonzero component at a point, choose a smooth nonnegative compact bump supported where that component has fixed sign and vary that component alone, contradicting zero integral. A bump may be constructed as $\exp(-1/(1-|x|^2))$ inside a coordinate ball and zero outside; its derivatives vanish at the boundary because exponential decay dominates every inverse power. This supplies the fundamental variational lemma used here. Noncompact action values themselves must be finite or interpreted as compactly supported action differences; no infinity-minus-infinity operation is assumed. Nonzero boundary metric variations require additional boundary terms and are not covered.

For a $C^2$ future timelike curve with fixed endpoints, put $L(s)=\int\sqrt{-g(T,T)}d\lambda$ for a smooth variation with variation field $J$ zero at endpoints and remaining timelike. Compatibility/torsion freedom gives $\delta L=-\int g(D_\lambda J,T)/\sqrt{-g(T,T)}d\lambda$. Choose the original parameter proportional to proper time, with $g(T,T)=-c^2$. Integration by parts gives $\delta L=c^{-1}\int g(J,D_\lambda T)d\lambda$. Arbitrary compact curve variations are realized in a finite chain of coordinate pieces; the same bump argument and nondegeneracy imply stationarity exactly when $D_\lambda T=0$. A null curve is outside this square-root calculation. Stationarity does not imply maximum; C1–C2 supply local maximum with exact conjugacy/branch restrictions. The particle action $-mcL$ has the same stationary curves for $m>0$.

## G6 — Killing, matter Noether identity and explicit models

The Lie derivative of the metric is $(\mathcal L_\xi g)(X,Y)=\xi[g(X,Y)]-g([\xi,X],Y)-g(X,[\xi,Y])$. Compatibility and torsion freedom reduce it to $g(\nabla_X\xi,Y)+g(X,\nabla_Y\xi)$, so a Killing field is exactly a smooth $\xi$ with $\nabla_a\xi_b+\nabla_b\xi_a=0$. Along an affine geodesic, $d[g(\xi,T)]/ds=g(\nabla_T\xi,T)=0$. For symmetric divergence-free $T^{ab}$, $J^a=T^{ab}\xi_b$ has zero divergence by G1's product rule and symmetry. G4 states the actual flux conditions for a charge. A generic Lorentz metric need not have any such field; a Killing scalar on one geodesic is not a universal global gravitational energy.

If a matter action is invariant under pullback by compactly supported diffeomorphisms, its fields satisfy its Euler–Lagrange equations and its first variation exists with G5's support/regularity conditions, vary by the flow of a compactly supported smooth vector field $\xi$. The infinitesimal inverse metric variation is $q^{ab}=-(\nabla^a\xi^b+\nabla^b\xi^a)$, from the preceding Lie formula and differentiation of the inverse. Matter-field terms vanish on shell. Invariance and the Hilbert definition give $0=c^{-1}\int T_{ab}\nabla^a\xi^b\,\mathrm{vol}_g=-c^{-1}\int(\nabla^aT_{ab})\xi^b\,\mathrm{vol}_g$. Compact divergence integrates to zero by G4; arbitrary bump-supported $\xi$ then gives $\nabla^aT_{ab}=0$. This is an on-shell identity; off-shell arbitrary prescribed matter is not conserved by coordinate covariance alone.

For an explicit scalar branch choose a real smooth scalar $\phi$, dimensionless, potential $V:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ smooth in inverse-square-metres, and positive coefficient $K$ in joules/metre. Adopt $S_m=-(K/c)\int[\frac12g^{ab}\partial_a\phi\partial_b\phi+V(\phi)]\mathrm{vol}_g$. Compact scalar variations and G4 give $\Box_g\phi-V'(\phi)=0$, $\Box_g\phi=\nabla_a\nabla^a\phi$. The metric variation gives $T_{ab}=K[\partial_a\phi\partial_b\phi-g_{ab}(\frac12\partial_c\phi\partial^c\phi+V)]$, in J/m³. Differentiation gives $\nabla^aT_{ab}=K(\Box_g\phi-V'(\phi))\partial_b\phi$: the second-gradient terms cancel using scalar Hessian symmetry, itself obtained from commuting partials and symmetric $\Gamma$. Thus conservation is explicitly checked, including the stated units. Scalar well-posedness is a PDE question beyond this variational algebra.

Dust is a separate smooth model with positive rest density $\rho$ in kg/m³ and physical future $U$, $g(U,U)=-c^2$, $T^{ab}=\rho U^aU^b$. Its divergence is $U^b\nabla_a(\rho U^a)+\rho U^a\nabla_aU^b$. Contract with $U_b$: the second term vanishes by fixed norm, hence divergence zero implies $\nabla_a(\rho U^a)=0$; subtracting then gives $U^a\nabla_aU^b=0$ wherever $\rho>0$. Conversely those two equations imply conservation. This proves the precise dust equivalence; zero-density regions do not determine $U$. Dust caustics may invalidate the smooth model.

A perfect fluid specifies a smooth future unit $n$, rest energy density $\epsilon$ and pressure $p$ in J/m³, $T^{ab}=(\epsilon+p)n^an^b+pg^{ab}$ and a chosen equation of state. Put $D=n^a\nabla_a$, $\vartheta=\nabla_an^a$, $a^b=n^a\nabla_an^b$, $h^b{}_c=\delta^b{}_c+n^bn_c$. Contracting/projection of the product expansion gives respectively $D\epsilon+(\epsilon+p)\vartheta=0$ and $(\epsilon+p)a^b+h^{ba}\partial_ap=0$. The first follows by contraction with $n_b$, with sign reversed; the second removes the terms parallel to $n$. These equations are conservation consequences, not a thermodynamic derivation of EOS or general fluid existence.

## G7 — conditional Einstein consistency and energy-condition examples

Adopt Einstein's equations on the stated smooth domain, a constant $\Lambda$, and the selected matter/test-body/ideal-clock premises. G3 proves that every solution must satisfy $\nabla^aT_{ab}=0$; a hand-picked nonconserved source cannot solve them. Conversely conservation is necessary and does not suffice to solve the Einstein PDE. If $\Lambda$ is promoted to a varying function without additional fields, the divergence identity instead requires $(8\pi G_N/c^4)\nabla^aT_{ab}=\partial_b\Lambda$, so the constant-parameter assumption matters. The field equation trace in four dimensions gives $-S+4\Lambda=(8\pi G_N/c^4)T^a{}_a$; vacuum with $\Lambda=0$ gives Ricci zero, not Riemann zero. Minkowski metric then is a solution with $T=0$, since its constant metric gives $\Gamma=R=0$.

Energy conditions are additional pointwise mathematical inequalities on a chosen $T$: WEC is $T(v,v)\ge0$ for all timelike $v$; NEC for all null $v$; DEC means for all future timelike $v$, $-T^a{}_bv^b$ is future causal or zero; SEC is $(T_{ab}-T^c{}_cg_{ab}/2)v^av^b\ge0$ for all timelike $v$. For the perfect fluid rest basis $T_{AB}=\operatorname{diag}(\epsilon,p,p,p)$. Evaluating on $v=(v^0,\mathbf v)$ gives WEC equivalent to $\epsilon\ge0$, $\epsilon+p\ge0$: write the value as $\epsilon[(v^0)^2-|\mathbf v|^2]+(\epsilon+p)|\mathbf v|^2$ and take the rest and null-limit velocities for necessity. NEC is $\epsilon+p\ge0$. The DEC current has components $(\epsilon v^0,-p\mathbf v)$, so it is future causal for every timelike $v$ exactly when $\epsilon\ge|p|$; use $|\mathbf v|/v^0\to1$ for necessity. The SEC rest diagonal is $((\epsilon+3p)/2,(\epsilon-p)/2,(\epsilon-p)/2,(\epsilon-p)/2)$; the same WEC argument gives $\epsilon+3p\ge0$ and $\epsilon+p\ge0$. A positive cosmological-constant effective fluid $p=-\epsilon<0$ obeys NEC/WEC/DEC but violates SEC, an explicit counterexample to confusing them. No energy condition is universally proved from the Einstein postulate.
