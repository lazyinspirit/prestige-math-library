This is a global additional condition; a Lorentz metric alone need not be time orientable. Assume the selected model's nongravitational fields/matter and their explicit equations/couplings; diffeomorphism covariance alone does not select those equations. Adopt ideal freely falling massive test bodies as future timelike geodesics and ideal geometric-optics light rays as null geodesics in the specified test-field approximation. For a classical massive-particle model, separately adopt constant positive rest mass m and P=mU; this is not derived from geodesic motion. An ideal massless-particle model, if used, needs its own nonzero-null-momentum postulate and energy normalization. Adopt ideal clocks measuring metric proper time locally along their worldlines. These curved-spacetime assumptions are GR postulates in their own stated regime; the flat-only SR clock/light postulate is a comparison, not a supplier for them. They exclude unmodeled self-force, finite-size/tidal and dispersion corrections.

There are two distinct gravitational model formulations. In the **direct-equation formulation**, adopt

$$G_{ab}+\Lambda g_{ab}=\kappa T_{ab},\qquad \kappa:=\frac{8\pi G_N}{c^4},$$

Primitive inputs are differentiable spacetime, metric/future orientation, the selected clock/matter interpretation, and physical parameters c, G_N and Λ (unless Λ is fixed to zero for a restricted branch). Derived mathematical objects include the compatible connection, curvature, proper time, geodesics and observer decompositions. The constants c and G_N are positive, with SI dimensions m s^-1 and m^3 kg^-1 s^-2; Λ is a signed parameter of dimension m^-2.
