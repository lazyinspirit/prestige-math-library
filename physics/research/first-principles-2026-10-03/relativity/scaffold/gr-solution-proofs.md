# Explicit GR solutions and controlled local approximations

2026-10-03. Mathematical calculations use G0–G6 of `gr-local-proofs.md`; Einstein/matter, ideal clocks and test geodesics are adopted physical premises. No calculation supplies observations, full nonlinear stability or a global uniqueness theorem.

## Q1 — Schwarzschild exterior and horizon extension

For $m_*>0$ in kg put $s=2G_Nm_*/c^2$ in metres, $f(r)=1-s/r$. On $\mathbb R_{x^0}\times(s,\infty)_r\times S^2$ use $x^0=ct$, $r$ in metres, sphere angular charts $(\theta,\varphi)$ and

$$g=-f(dx^0)^2+f^{-1}dr^2+r^2(d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta d\varphi^2).$$

The sphere metric is the metric induced by embedding the unit sphere in $\mathbb R^3$, and its displayed angular chart avoids poles; other sphere charts patch it smoothly. The diagonal entries give signature $(-+++)$ for $r>s$; choose future $\partial_0$. For any positive $C^2$ function $f$, all nonzero Christoffel symbols, apart from lower-index symmetry, are

$$\Gamma^0{}_{0r}=\frac{f'}{2f},\quad\Gamma^r{}_{00}=\frac{ff'}2,\quad\Gamma^r{}_{rr}=-\frac{f'}{2f},\quad\Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta}=-rf,\quad\Gamma^r{}_{\varphi\varphi}=-rf\sin^2\theta,$$

$$\Gamma^\theta{}_{r\theta}=\Gamma^\varphi{}_{r\varphi}=1/r,\quad\Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi}=-\sin\theta\cos\theta,\quad\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi}=\cot\theta.$$

These follow individually by G1's formula and the only nonzero metric derivatives $g_{00,r}=-f'$, $g_{rr,r}=-f'/f^2$, $g_{\theta\theta,r}=2r$, $g_{\varphi\varphi,r}=2r\sin^2\theta$, $g_{\varphi\varphi,\theta}=2r^2\sin\theta\cos\theta$. Substitution in $\operatorname{Ric}_{bd}=\partial_a\Gamma^a{}_{db}-\partial_d\Gamma^a{}_{ab}+\Gamma^a{}_{ae}\Gamma^e{}_{db}-\Gamma^a{}_{de}\Gamma^e{}_{ab}$ gives diagonal components

$$\operatorname{Ric}_{00}=f(f''/2+f'/r),\quad\operatorname{Ric}_{rr}=-f^{-1}(f''/2+f'/r),\quad\operatorname{Ric}_{\theta\theta}=1-f-rf',\quad\operatorname{Ric}_{\varphi\varphi}=\sin^2\theta(1-f-rf').$$

For clarity the $00$ terms reduce to $(ff')'/2+(2/r)(ff'/2)-2(f'/2f)(ff'/2)=ff''/2+ff'/r$; the $rr$ terms reduce to $-f''/(2f)-f'/(rf)$ after cancellation of $f'^2$ and $r^{-2}$. The angular terms reduce using $\csc^2\theta-\cot^2\theta=1$ to the displayed expression; all mixed terms cancel, since radial/angle derivatives of their diagonal factors occur in equal opposite pairs. For $f=1-s/r$, $f'=s/r^2$, $f''=-2s/r^3$, all four components vanish. Thus this is a vacuum $\Lambda=0$ solution, not a conclusion that every vacuum solution has this form.

The independent rest-orthonormal curvature magnitudes are $f''/2$ in the $0r$ plane, $f'/(2r)$ in each $0\theta,0\varphi,r\theta,r\varphi$ plane and $(1-f)/r^2$ in the $\theta\varphi$ plane; obtain them by inserting the same symbol list into G3's curvature formula and multiplying each index by the inverse frame factors. Each plane entry contributes four equal squares to the full contraction, giving

$$R_{abcd}R^{abcd}=f''^2+4f'^2/r^2+4(1-f)^2/r^4=12s^2/r^6.$$

This invariant diverges as $r\downarrow0$ if such an interior extension is used, but is finite at $r=s$. Define $r_*=r+s\log|r/s-1|$ separately on $r>s$ or $0<r<s$; $dr_*/dr=1/f$. Advanced coordinate $v=x^0+r_*$ gives $g=-f dv^2+2dvdr+r^2d\Omega^2$. This new formula is smooth and nondegenerate at every $r>0$, including $r=s$, because the $(v,r)$ determinant is $-1$. Its two-by-two block has one negative and one positive eigenvalue, with angular positive directions, so signature remains Lorentzian. It agrees with the exterior by the explicit diffeomorphism off the horizon. This proves a smooth horizon-crossing extension; it does not establish the maximal Kruskal extension, global event-horizon characterization or uniqueness.

The exact isotropic exterior radial coordinate is $r=\rho(1+s/(4\rho))^2$, $\rho>s/4$. Its derivative $1-s^2/(16\rho^2)$ is positive, it maps onto $(s,\infty)$ and its inverse is smooth. Substitution gives $f=[(1-s/(4\rho))/(1+s/(4\rho))]^2$ and spatial metric $(1+s/(4\rho))^4(d\rho^2+\rho^2d\Omega^2)$. Cartesian isotropic coordinates therefore have $g_{ij}=(1+s/(4\rho))^4\delta_{ij}=\delta_{ij}(1+s/\rho)+O(s^2/\rho^2)$ uniformly where $s/\rho$ is bounded away from the horizon regime. This is the coordinate conversion required when comparing the isotropic weak-field ansatz with areal-radius Schwarzschild; equality of their spatial coefficients in one unchanged chart would be false.

In the exterior $\xi=\partial_0$ is Killing since all components are independent of $x^0$. Stationary observers have $n=f^{-1/2}\xi$. Along a future null affine ray with covector $k=g(\dot\gamma,\cdot)$, G6 gives constant $C=-k(\xi)>0$ (positivity since $\xi$ is future timelike). Their frequency is $\omega=-ck(n)=cC/\sqrt f$, giving $\omega_R/\omega_E=\sqrt{f(r_E)/f(r_R)}$ for stationary emitter/receiver connected by that ray and the adopted phase/ray measurement interpretation. Moving observers use their own $n$ and give extra Doppler factors. This is a conditional prediction, not a recorded redshift measurement.

For equatorial timelike geodesics proper-time velocity satisfies $\mathcal E=f\dot x^0$ and $\ell=r^2\dot\varphi$ constant by G6. Equatorial initial conditions $\theta=\pi/2$, $\dot\theta=0$ remain so by the geodesic ODE (the $\theta$ force vanishes there), and the norm gives $\dot r^2=\mathcal E^2-f(c^2+\ell^2/r^2)$. At a circular orbit, the radial geodesic equation requires the derivative of the effective potential to vanish, yielding $\ell^2=c^2sr^2/(2r-3s)$, requiring $r>3s/2$. Its second derivative at fixed $\ell$ is $2c^2s(r-3s)/[r^3(2r-3s)]$, so local radial potential stability occurs exactly for $r>3s$, instability for $3s/2<r<3s$. For null circular orbits the potential is $f\ell^2/r^2$; its stationary radius is $3s/2$ with negative second derivative. These are exterior ideal-geodesic calculations, not full nonlinear orbital stability or a proof of an observed perihelion value.

## Q2 — FLRW model, conservation and explicit branches

Supply a three-manifold $\Sigma$ with a dimensionless smooth positive metric $\gamma$ whose curvature satisfies $\operatorname{Ric}(\gamma)_{ij}=2k\gamma_{ij}$ for a fixed dimensionless $k\in\{-1,0,1\}$. This is a hypothesis on a chosen spatial model, not a classification theorem. Let $a:I\to(0,\infty)$ be smooth in metres on an open time interval $I$ in seconds. On $I\times\Sigma$, use $g=-c^2dt^2+a(t)^2\gamma$, future $n=c^{-1}\partial_t$. Then $g$ is smooth Lorentzian, with dimensionless spatial coordinates; the components have their corresponding dimensional factors. Adopt comoving perfect-fluid matter with $\epsilon(t),p(t)$ in J/m³ and an explicit EOS. The new connection coefficients are $\Gamma^t{}_{ij}=a\dot a\gamma_{ij}/c^2$, $\Gamma^i{}_{tj}=(\dot a/a)\delta^i_j$ and $\Gamma^i{}_{jk}=\Gamma(\gamma)^i{}_{jk}$; all others vanish.

Writing $H=\dot a/a$, substitution in G3 gives $\operatorname{Ric}_{tt}=-3\ddot a/a$, $\operatorname{Ric}_{ti}=0$, $\operatorname{Ric}_{ij}=[(a\ddot a+2\dot a^2)/c^2+2k]\gamma_{ij}$. Indeed the time trace in $\operatorname{Ric}_{tt}$ is $-\partial_t(3H)-3H^2$; the spatial expression separates $\operatorname{Ric}(\gamma)$ and time terms $\partial_t(a\dot a)/c^2+3H(a\dot a)/c^2-2H(a\dot a)/c^2=(a\ddot a+2\dot a^2)/c^2$. Contracting gives $S=6[\ddot a/(ac^2)+H^2/c^2+k/a^2]$. Einstein equations reduce to

$$H^2+kc^2/a^2=\frac{8\pi G_N}{3c^2}\epsilon+\frac{\Lambda c^2}{3},\qquad \frac{\ddot a}{a}=-\frac{4\pi G_N}{3c^2}(\epsilon+3p)+\frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}.$$

These follow by the $tt$ and spatial components, where $T_{tt}=\epsilon c^2$ and $T_{ij}=pa^2\gamma_{ij}$. G6 conservation gives $\dot\epsilon+3H(\epsilon+p)=0$. Conversely the Friedmann equation, conservation and the acceleration equation give all components; differentiating the first plus conservation gives the second when $H\ne0$, but at $H=0$ the division is invalid, so do not discard the acceleration equation there.

For $k=\Lambda=0$, EOS $p=w\epsilon$, constant $w>-1$, $\epsilon>0$, conservation integrates by differentiating $\epsilon a^{3(1+w)}$ to zero. Choose expanding branch $H>0$, set $q=3(1+w)/2>0$, $C=\epsilon a^{2q}>0$. Friedmann gives $\dot a=\sqrt{8\pi G_NC/(3c^2)}a^{1-q}$; hence $a^q=q\sqrt{8\pi G_NC/(3c^2)}(t-t_0)$ for $t>t_0$. This explicitly proves the $a\propto(t-t_0)^{2/[3(1+w)]}$ model and its interval. Dust $w=0$ gives exponent $2/3$, radiation EOS $w=1/3$ gives $1/2$; treating radiation as actual matter needs its own model/EOS justification. For vacuum $\epsilon=p=0$, $k=0$, $\Lambda>0$, $a=a_0e^{Ht}$ with $H=c\sqrt{\Lambda/3}$ solves both equations on all $\mathbb R$. These are explicit solutions and do not assert uniqueness for every EOS or universal cosmological topology/singularity behavior.

For an affine future null ray with tangent/raised phase covector $k$, its $t$ geodesic equation is $dk^t/d\lambda+(a\dot a/c^2)\gamma_{ij}k^ik^j=0$. Nullness gives $a^2\gamma_{ij}k^ik^j=c^2(k^t)^2$, so $dk^t/d\lambda+H(k^t)^2=0$. Future direction gives $k^t>0$ and using $dt/d\lambda=k^t$ yields $d\log k^t/dt=-H$, hence $a k^t$ constant. A comoving ideal receiver has $U=\partial_t$ and angular frequency $\omega=-g(k,U)=c^2k^t$, according to the adopted geometric-optics phase/clock measurement interpretation. Thus $\omega_R/\omega_E=a(t_E)/a(t_R)$ and redshift $1+z=\omega_E/\omega_R=a(t_R)/a(t_E)$. This is an observer/ray relation, not a universal remote relative velocity or an observation; transparency/luminosity/detector claims need extra models.

## Q3 — linearized Einstein and physical wave screen

Let $g_{ab}(\lambda)=\eta_{ab}+\lambda h_{ab}+O(\lambda^2)$ on an open affine region, with a fixed smooth symmetric $h$ and the remainder bounded in $C^2$ on every specified compact subset. For sufficiently small $\lambda$ the metric is nondegenerate of the adopted signature there. Indices on first-order quantities are raised with $\eta$. Differentiating G1/G3 at $\lambda=0$ gives

$$\Gamma^{(1)a}{}_{bc}=\tfrac12\eta^{ad}(\partial_bh_{cd}+\partial_ch_{bd}-\partial_dh_{bc}),$$

$$\operatorname{Ric}^{(1)}_{ab}=\tfrac12(\partial_c\partial_ah^c{}_b+\partial_c\partial_bh^c{}_a-\Box h_{ab}-\partial_a\partial_bh),\qquad S^{(1)}=\partial_a\partial_bh^{ab}-\Box h,$$

where $h=\eta^{ab}h_{ab}$ and $\Box=\eta^{ab}\partial_a\partial_b=-c^{-2}\partial_t^2+\Delta$. Derivation substitutes $\Gamma^{(1)}$ in $\partial_c\Gamma^{(1)c}{}_{ab}-\partial_b\Gamma^{(1)c}{}_{ac}$; its repeated partial terms combine to the four displayed terms. Put $\bar h_{ab}=h_{ab}-\eta_{ab}h/2$. Expanding $G^{(1)}$ gives

$$G^{(1)}_{ab}=-\tfrac12\Box\bar h_{ab}+\partial_{(a}\partial^c\bar h_{b)c}-\tfrac12\eta_{ab}\partial^c\partial^d\bar h_{cd}.$$

For the passive infinitesimal chart change $x'^a=x^a+\lambda\xi^a$, expansion of its inverse Jacobian gives $h'_{ab}=h_{ab}-\partial_a\xi_b-\partial_b\xi_a$, and $\partial^a\bar h'_{ab}=\partial^a\bar h_{ab}-\Box\xi_b$. Thus if a solution of $\Box\xi_b=\partial^a\bar h_{ab}$ with chosen initial/boundary conditions is supplied, harmonic gauge is attained and $G^{(1)}_{ab}=-\Box\bar h_{ab}/2$. This statement alone does not prove existence of that gauge PDE solution on arbitrary domains. For the all-space smooth branch this prerequisite is now supplied by the actually read and checked pure-mathematical `thm-em-wave-cauchy-kirchhoff`, W2 of `../../classical-electromagnetism/scaffold/completed-expansion-arguments.md`, with W1 spherical means. Its operator is $W=c^{-2}\partial_t^2-\Delta=-\Box$; apply it componentwise with source $-\partial^a\bar h_{ab}$ and zero initial gauge data on any finite time slab. It accepts arbitrary smooth all-space data/source (smooth extension across slab endpoints), with no spatial-infinity integrability condition; compact-support consequences require compact data/source support. Its explicit Duhamel endpoint differentiation and shrinking-ball energy uniqueness were checked, along with the bounded-ball divergence, sphere-measure and polar-coordinate suppliers. Thus flat harmonic gauge exists in this exact all-space smooth regime. An arbitrary spatial domain, variable-metric curved wave or Einstein nonlinear IVP is not covered. Residual gauge requires $\Box\xi=0$.

An explicit vacuum plane wave avoids this missing existence step: for any smooth real functions $A_+,A_\times$ of $u=x^0-x^3$, set $h_{11}=A_+(u)$, $h_{22}=-A_+(u)$, $h_{12}=h_{21}=A_\times(u)$ and all other components zero. Its trace/divergence vanish directly, and $\Box A(u)=0$ because the $00$ and $33$ second derivatives cancel. It solves the linearized vacuum equations in harmonic gauge. The transverse spatial screen is the two-dimensional span of $e_1,e_2$, where its tracefree symmetric matrix is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A_+&A_\times\\A_\times&-A_+\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. It has two real independent polarizations. A four-dimensional metric trace subtraction would not be this two-dimensional screen projection. A screen tensor for propagation unit spatial vector $s$ is $P_i{}^kP_j{}^lh_{kl}-(P_{ij}/2)P^{kl}h_{kl}$ with $P_{ij}=\delta_{ij}-s_is_j$; idempotence $P^2=P$ and $\operatorname{tr}P=2$ prove its transverse tracefree property.

Curvature has $R^{(1)}_{0i0j}=-\partial_0^2h_{ij}/2$ for these waves, by G3's four-term formula. Along an initially resting geodesic observer, the Jacobi equation to first order is $d^2J^i/d\tau^2=(1/2)\partial_t^2h^i{}_jJ^j$, since $R^i{}_{0 0j}=+\partial_0^2h^i{}_j/2$ and $T^0=c$. This is a conditional local tidal response; detector elasticity, light propagation, noise and observations require separate assumptions. The metric $\eta+\lambda h$ is generally not an exact nonlinear vacuum solution; the uncontrolled large-amplitude/nonlinear conclusion is excluded.

## Q4 — uniform weak-field calculation with exact norm assumptions

Fix a compact coordinate region inside an open stationary domain, a length scale $L>0$, and a family of smooth stationary metrics with, for $0<\epsilon\le\epsilon_0$, $g_{00}=-(1+2\epsilon\phi)+r_{00}$, $g_{ij}=(1-2\epsilon\phi)\delta_{ij}+r_{ij}$, $g_{0i}=r_{0i}$, where $\phi$ is fixed dimensionless smooth and its derivatives through order two are bounded by $C/L^{|\alpha|}$. Require $|\partial^\alpha r_{00}|+|\partial^\alpha r_{ij}|\le C\epsilon^2/L^{|\alpha|}$ and $|\partial^\alpha r_{0i}|\le C\epsilon^{3/2}/L^{|\alpha|}$ for $|\alpha|\le2$. Shrink $\epsilon_0$ uniformly so inverse matrices are bounded and signature Lorentzian. Set $\Phi=\epsilon c^2\phi$ in m²/s². Directly expanding $g^{-1}=\eta^{-1}-\eta^{-1}(g-\eta)\eta^{-1}+O(\epsilon^2)$ follows from $(I+H)^{-1}=I-H+H^2(I+H)^{-1}$; differentiating this exact identity through two orders bounds every stated remainder.

G1 now gives $\Gamma^i{}_{00}=\partial_i\Phi/c^2+O(\epsilon^2/L)$, $\Gamma^i{}_{0j}=O(\epsilon^{3/2}/L)$, $\Gamma^i{}_{jk}=O(\epsilon/L)$, $\Gamma^0{}_{00}=O(\epsilon^{5/2}/L)$, $\Gamma^0{}_{0j}=O(\epsilon/L)$, $\Gamma^0{}_{jk}=O(\epsilon^{3/2}/L)$. Every bound is uniform on the chosen compact region. For a timelike geodesic parameterized by $t=x^0/c$ with $|\mathbf w|/c\le C\sqrt\epsilon$, changing affine parameter in the geodesic equation gives

$$\frac{dw^i}{dt}=-c^2\Gamma^i{}_{00}-2c\Gamma^i{}_{0j}w^j-\Gamma^i{}_{jk}w^jw^k+\frac{w^i}{c}(c^2\Gamma^0{}_{00}+2c\Gamma^0{}_{0j}w^j+\Gamma^0{}_{jk}w^jw^k).$$

The bounds above prove $d\mathbf w/dt=-\nabla\Phi+O(c^2\epsilon^2/L)$, as long as the geodesic stays in the region with the stipulated speed bound. This is an error-controlled conditional local equation, not a full convergence theorem on unbounded times.

For the diagonal first-order perturbation $h_{00}=-2\phi$, $h_{ij}=-2\phi\delta_{ij}$, Q3 gives $\operatorname{Ric}^{(1)}_{00}=\Delta\phi$, $S^{(1)}=2\Delta\phi$ and $G^{(1)}_{00}=2\Delta\phi$. Stationarity makes the $g_{0i}=O(\epsilon^{3/2})$ terms contribute only $O(\epsilon^2/L^2)$ to $G_{00}$ (linear time derivatives vanish; remaining contributions are products). Thus $G_{00}=2\Delta\Phi/c^2+O(\epsilon^2/L^2)$. Under Einstein with $\Lambda=0$ and an explicit matter hierarchy $T_{00}=\rho_\epsilon c^2+O(\epsilon^2c^4/(G_NL^2))$, one obtains

$$\Delta\Phi=4\pi G_N\rho_\epsilon+O(\epsilon^2c^2/L^2).$$

For the same diagonal first-order perturbation Q3 gives $\operatorname{Ric}^{(1)}_{ij}=\delta_{ij}\Delta\phi$, hence $G^{(1)}_{ij}=0$ and $G^{(1)}_{0i}=0$. The off-diagonal remainder may have nonzero spatial second derivatives. Consequently $G_{ij}=O(\epsilon^2/L^2)$ and $G_{0i}=O(\epsilon^{3/2}/L^2)$. Einstein therefore imposes the full consistent component hierarchy $\kappa T_{00}=O(\epsilon/L^2)$, $\kappa T_{0i}=O(\epsilon^{3/2}/L^2)$, $\kappa T_{ij}=O(\epsilon^2/L^2)$, with $\kappa=8\pi G_N/c^4$. These are necessary bounds for an Einstein family under this ansatz; the single Poisson equation alone is not sufficient for the full equations.

There is also an actual conditional trajectory limit. Set $X=x/L$, $S=\sqrt\epsilon ct/L$, $W=w/(\sqrt\epsilon c)$ and $\psi(X)=\phi(LX)$. On a fixed finite scaled interval $0\le S\le S_*$ assume the stipulated geodesics remain in a common compact subset of the chart and the stipulated uniform speed bound holds. Then $X'=W$, $W'=-\nabla_X\psi(X)+R_\epsilon$ with $|R_\epsilon|\le C\epsilon$. Let $(X_*,W_*)$ solve the Newtonian system with limiting initial data and remain in this subset; its existence is the checked local ODE supplier, continued along the finite interval. A common compact neighborhood has $|D^2\psi|\le K$. Writing the integral equations and taking the sum of position/velocity differences gives $D(S)\le D(0)+C\epsilon S+(1+K)\int_0^S D(u)du$. Iterating this inequality (the $n$-fold integral has bound $S^n/n!$) proves $D(S)\le (D(0)+C\epsilon S_*)e^{(1+K)S_*}$. Thus convergent initial data give uniform position/velocity convergence on this finite scaled interval, and identical initial data give rate $O(\epsilon)$. This is a proof of the conditional weak/slow correspondence, including the physically relevant long coordinate times $t=LS/(c\sqrt\epsilon)$ while the assumptions hold. In the same scaling $R=G_NL^2\rho_\epsilon/(\epsilon c^2)$ obeys $\Delta_X\psi=4\pi R+O(\epsilon)$, directly from the proved Poisson estimate.

An Einstein family satisfying these assumptions is an input to this theorem. Constructing such a family from arbitrary Newtonian initial data, or proving a global singular limit for different gauges/data classes, is a distinct stronger theorem and is not asserted. The exact conditional correspondence and all its error/convergence deductions are proved here.

## Q5 — full Kruskal model and precise local Birkhoff theorem

For $s>0$ define $\mathcal K=\{(U,V):UV<1\}\times S^2$, and define its smooth positive function $r$ by $UV=-(r/s-1)e^{r/s}$. The right side maps $(0,\infty)$ strictly decreasing onto $(-\infty,1)$, with derivative $-(r/s^2)e^{r/s}\ne0$, so its inverse is globally smooth. Put

$$g=-\frac{4s^3}{r}e^{-r/s}\,dU\,dV+r^2\gamma_{S^2}.$$

The cross-product denotes the symmetric metric with $g_{UV}=g_{VU}=-2s^3e^{-r/s}/r$. Its two-dimensional determinant is negative and its sphere metric positive, giving a smooth nondegenerate Lorentz metric everywhere in the stated domain, including $U=0$, $V=0$ and their bifurcation sphere. Time orientation is chosen so $U,V$ increase along future timelike vectors. On the right exterior $U<0,V>0$, the diffeomorphism $U=-e^{-(x^0-r_*)/(2s)}$, $V=e^{(x^0+r_*)/(2s)}$ gives Q1's Schwarzschild metric: $dU\,dV=(UV/(4s^2))[(dx^0)^2-(dr_*)^2]$ and $UV=-(r/s-1)e^{r/s}$. The same substitutions with signs on the other three quadrants give Q1's $0<r<s$ interior or second exterior. Vacuum Ricci therefore vanishes on the dense complement of the two axes, hence everywhere by smoothness. The invariant $12s^2/r^6$ extends by the same continuity argument. Thus $r=0$, absent from $\mathcal K$, cannot be appended as a regular $C^2$ metric boundary point approached with $r\to0$: continuous curvature of any such extension would contradict its divergence.

A future causal tangent has $\dot U,\dot V\ge0$ (the angular positive term can only narrow the two-dimensional future cone). Define right-exterior escape precisely as a future causal path eventually in $U<0,V>0$ along which $r\to\infty$, rather than presuming an undefined conformal infinity. A point with $U\ge0$ cannot escape because $U$ cannot decrease. Every point with $U<0$ can escape by holding $U$ fixed and increasing $V$ to $+\infty$ along a radial future null curve, with $UV\to-\infty$ and $r\to\infty$. Hence the exact nonescaping set for this definition is $U\ge0$, and its boundary is $U=0$. The past dual gives $V=0$ as the white-hole boundary for the right exterior. A circular sphere in $U,V>0$ has decreasing $r$ in both future radial null directions: $\partial_Ur=-s^2Ve^{-r/s}/r<0$, $\partial_Vr=-s^2Ue^{-r/s}/r<0$. It is a trapped sphere under the explicit expansion definition $2\ell(r)/r$. These calculations describe the full four-region model, not merely a coordinate singularity diagram. They do not establish uniqueness among every possible topology/quotient or maximality in every lower-regularity extension category.

The following local Birkhoff result has a complete argument. Assume on an open region a smooth vacuum metric already has the spherically symmetric warped form $g=q_{AB}dy^A dy^B+r(y)^2\gamma_{S^2}$, where $q$ is a smooth two-dimensional Lorentz metric, $r>0$, and $z=q^{AB}\partial_Ar\partial_Br\ne0$. This hypothesis specifies the orbit geometry and excludes a constant-radius/null-gradient exceptional chart; it does not assume a global orbit quotient or asymptotic flatness. The warped Christoffel coefficients in addition to the two factor connections are $\Gamma^A{}_{ij}=-r\partial^Ar\,\gamma_{ij}$ and $\Gamma^i{}_{Aj}=r^{-1}\partial_Ar\,\delta^i_j$. Substituting them into G3 gives

$$\operatorname{Ric}_{AB}=\operatorname{Ric}(q)_{AB}-2r^{-1}\operatorname{Hess}_q(r)_{AB},\qquad \operatorname{Ric}_{ij}=(1-z-r\Delta_qr)\gamma_{ij},\qquad \operatorname{Ric}_{Ai}=0.$$

For example the cross terms in the base trace are $-2\partial_B(r_A/r)-2r_Ar_B/r^2$, giving $-2r^{-1}\operatorname{Hess}_{AB}r$; the sphere trace gives its unit Ricci $\gamma_{ij}$ minus $z\gamma_{ij}$ and $r\Delta r\gamma_{ij}$. In two dimensions curvature symmetry leaves just one independent component, so $\operatorname{Ric}(q)=(S_q/2)q$. Vacuum therefore gives $\operatorname{Hess}r=(rS_q/4)q=(\Delta r/2)q$ and $r\Delta r=1-z$. Differentiating $z$ now gives $dz=2\operatorname{Hess}r(\nabla r,\cdot)=\Delta r\,dr=(1-z)dr/r$, so $d[r(1-z)]=0$. On each connected patch this is a constant $s$, hence $z=1-s/r$.

Let $K^A=\varepsilon_q^{AB}\partial_Br$ with either chosen base orientation. Its covector derivative is the contraction of the parallel volume form with $\operatorname{Hess}r$, whose symmetric part is zero. Thus $K$ is Killing, $K(r)=0$ and $q(K,K)=-z$. Since $z\ne0$, $K$ and $\nabla r$ are independent. G2's flow chart gives coordinates $(T,r)$ with $K=\partial_T$. Its invariant coefficients have cross term removed by replacing $T$ by $T+F(r)$ (integrate its coefficient divided by $q_{TT}=-z$). In these orthogonal coordinates inverse $q^{rr}=z$ gives $q_{rr}=1/z$, hence

$$g=-(1-s/r)dT^2+(1-s/r)^{-1}dr^2+r^2d\Omega^2.$$

This proves local Schwarzschild form and a local extra Killing symmetry on the entire specified non-null-gradient patch, including $z<0$ where that symmetry is spacelike. The constant $s$ need not be positive without additional physical/regular-center/asymptotic assumptions. The stronger assertion of one global Schwarzschild exterior with specified mass and topology requires exactly those additional premises; spherical symmetry alone does not fix them.
