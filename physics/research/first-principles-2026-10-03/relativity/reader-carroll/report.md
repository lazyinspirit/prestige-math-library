## Framework objects and conventions to supply before consumers

The following is an explicit research formulation, strengthening unspecified hypotheses in the source. These definitions are not assertions that any such structure describes nature.

* **Manifold and spacetime:** a smooth four-dimensional manifold M means a Hausdorff, second-countable topological space locally homeomorphic to open subsets of R⁴, equipped with a maximal atlas of charts whose transition maps are C∞ diffeomorphisms. A classical spacetime here is (M,g), with g a smooth section of Sym²T*M, nondegenerate at every point, with inertia one negative and three positive eigenvalues. In coordinates, g=gμν dxμ⊗dxν and gμνgνρ=δμρ. The inertia convention is (−,+,+,+); Carroll's scalar “signature” is the difference between positive and negative counts (pp.49–50). Curved coordinates do not make curvature. Tangent vectors are derivations at points, cotangent vectors linear functionals on tangent spaces, and (k,l) tensors multilinear maps on k cotangent and l tangent arguments (Chs.1–2, pp.8–16, 37–45).
* **Regularity:** use smooth fields for the baseline rather than “as differentiable as necessary” (source p.38). Classical curvature uses at least C² metric regularity; the ordinary differentiated Bianchi argument uses C³. C² metric coefficients give C¹ Christoffel symbols and local uniqueness for geodesic ODEs. Minimal lower-regularity versions need separate theorems. Curves used for geodesics are C²; ordinary proper-time integrals allow piecewise C¹ timelike curves. Point sources, cone vertices, thin shells and shocks are not smooth-field cases; restrict away from their support or develop a distributional/weak formulation.
* **Orientation:** an orientation is a continuously consistent choice of orientation of tangent bases (equivalently a nowhere-zero top-degree form for orientable M). Only then is the metric volume form vol_g=√|det g| dx⁰∧dx¹∧dx²∧dx³ globally defined using oriented charts. Metric volume densities can be integrated without orientation. A **time orientation** is an independent continuous choice of one of the two timelike cones, equivalently a nowhere-zero continuous timelike vector field; future-directed vectors lie in the selected cone. A Lorentzian metric alone supplies two cones, not a global naming of future. Carroll assumes orientability at p.52 but does not separately develop time orientability. Hodge duality requires metric plus orientation (Eqs.1.87–1.88, 2.42–2.48).
* **Connection and curvature:** an affine connection ∇ on TM is R-bilinear, C∞-linear in its first vector-field argument and obeys ∇X(fY)=X(f)Y+f∇XY. Its induced tensor derivative respects contraction and the Leibniz rule. Torsion is Tor(X,Y)=∇XY−∇YX−[X,Y]. The Levi-Civita connection is the unique torsion-free connection with ∇g=0. Its coefficients are Γρμν=(1/2)gρσ(∂μgνσ+∂νgσμ−∂σgμν), Eq.3.21, p.60. Curvature convention is R(X,Y)Z=∇X∇YZ−∇Y∇XZ−∇[X,Y]Z, Eq.3.71, p.77; in components Rρσμν=∂μΓρνσ−∂νΓρμσ+ΓρμλΓλνσ−ΓρνλΓλμσ, Eq.3.67. Ricci is Rμν=Rλμλν; R=gμνRμν; Gμν=Rμν−(1/2)Rgμν (Eqs.3.90–3.96, p.81). Keep this sign convention when importing formulas from other texts.
* **Worldline and observer:** a massive test worldline is a C² map γ:I→M from an open real interval, with nonzero future-directed timelike tangent. Proper time in SI is dτ=√(−g(γ̇,γ̇))dλ/c when coordinates x⁰=ct and ds has length units. Its four-velocity U=dγ/dτ satisfies g(U,U)=−c²; in c=1 units it is −1. A point observer is such a worldline together with an ideal clock assumption and, when spatial measurements are needed, an orthonormal tetrad along it. An observer congruence on an open set is a smooth future unit timelike vector field whose local integral curves are observer worldlines. A null curve has nonzero future-directed tangent k with g(k,k)=0; it has no proper-time parametrization. A **null geodesic light ray** additionally assumes the adopted optical/matter model and geometric-optics approximation.
* **Frames:** a coordinate chart is a map assigning coordinates on an open subset; a coordinate basis is ∂μ. A tetrad on a patch is four smooth linearly independent fields e_a with g(e_a,e_b)=η_ab, with e_0 future-directed when chosen. It need not be a coordinate basis. A comoving chart has spatial coordinates constant on a chosen congruence. A local inertial chart at p has gμν(p)=ημν and ∂ρgμν(p)=0; it does not make curvature zero nearby. An observer tetrad, congruence and chart are different objects. Arbitrary global tetrads require tangent-bundle triviality; use patches (source pp.88–91).
* **Matter and stress:** adopt specified matter fields ψ with a matter action/equations, rather than treating T as a free label. T is a symmetric rank-two tensor; with lower indices it belongs to Γ(Sym²T*M). Observer energy density is T(U,U)/c² when U is SI normalized, or T(u,u) for g(u,u)=−1. Rest-frame spatial stresses are tensor projections onto the orthogonal complement of u. A perfect-fluid ansatz is Tμν=(ε+p)uμuν+pgμν in c=1, with u future unit timelike and ε,p scalar fields; isotropic rest stress and no rest energy flux are additional model assumptions. EOS and matter evolution must be supplied independently. For a conventional SI stress tensor, the metric-variation definition is Tμν=−2/√|g| δS_m/δgμν with consistent coordinates/action units; its conservation is on shell. Carroll uses a factor-one definition and doubles the gravitational action normalization together (Eqs.4.68–4.70); do not mix his displayed normalization with a conventionally normalized matter action without checking factors.
* **Units:** use x⁰=ct, all four coordinates measured as lengths, dimensionless gμν, Γ of dimension L⁻¹ and R/Gμν of L⁻². With ε and p in J m⁻³, Einstein coupling is 8πG_N/c⁴. G_N has SI dimension L³M⁻¹T⁻²; Λ has L⁻². In the notes c=1 from Eq.1.6, Newton's G is not generally set to 1, and electromagnetic examples use Gaussian 4π conventions. With time expressed in length and masses in mass units, c=1 gives [G]=L/M, [ε]=M/L³. Schwarzschild length is GM/c². Photon frequency, energy and classical wave phase require distinct definitions: measured angular frequency is −k·u in c=1, while E=ℏω introduces quantum assumptions. No universal identification with quantum operators follows from shared units.

## Deductions suitable for rigorous development

1. **Levi-Civita uniqueness/existence.** Eq.3.21 follows by permuting metric-compatibility equations and using torsion freedom (pp.59–60). The text explicitly leaves the coefficient transformation check to the reader. A complete supplier must verify compatibility/torsion and patchwise agreement, or prove the Koszul formula. Smooth nondegenerate pseudo-Riemannian metrics suffice; no field equation is needed.
2. **Parallel transport and affine geodesics.** Transport is ∇γ̇V=0; a geodesic solves ∇γ̇γ̇=0 (Eqs.3.30–3.47, pp.65–68). Metric compatibility preserves g(V,W), hence geodesic causal type. On compact parameter subintervals continuous coefficients give linear transport existence; geodesic local existence/uniqueness follows from ODE theory under local Lipschitz coefficients. Neither implies global completeness. Timelike proper-time variation with fixed endpoints gives the geodesic equation (Eqs.3.48–3.56, pp.69–70). Null curves require an affine formulation; do not vary a square-root proper-time integrand at null tangents.
3. **Curvature identities and geodesic deviation.** Algebraic symmetries and Bianchi contractions give ∇μGμν=0 (pp.79–81, Eqs.3.77–3.96). This is a geometric identity, not evidence of a physical field equation. For a smooth two-parameter family of affinely parametrized geodesics with commuting tangent T and variation S, torsion-free ∇ gives ∇T∇TS=R(T,S)T in the source's convention (pp.86–88, Eqs.3.107–3.113). Physical tidal interpretation adds test-body modeling.
4. **Matter conservation and Killing quantities.** With diffeomorphism-invariant matter action, matter equations satisfied and compact-support variations, integration by parts gives ∇μTμν=0 (pp.138–139, Eqs.5.34–5.38). On an affine geodesic, any Killing vector ξ (∇(μξν)=0) yields d[g(ξ,γ̇)]/dλ=0, explicitly proved in Eq.5.43, p.140. For symmetric divergence-free T, Jμ=Tμνξν is divergence free; equality of integrated charges additionally requires an oriented spacetime region, suitable hypersurface measures, integrability and zero appropriate side-boundary flux. A timelike ξ permits an energy interpretation; many spacetimes have none. Local ∇T=0 alone does not define universally conserved GR energy. ADM/Bondi charges need precise asymptotic/boundary definitions not developed fully here.
5. **Newtonian correspondence.** Source pp.105–106, Eqs.4.10–4.21: nearly Cartesian g=η+h, |h|≪1, slow test velocity, approximately static field; retain leading orders. Then d²x^i/dt²=(1/2)∂ih00=−∂iΦ in c=1 when h00=−2Φ. Restoring SI gives g00=−(1+2Φ/c²), [Φ]=L²/T². Source pp.111–112, Eqs.4.46–4.52: nonrelativistic rest energy dominates stresses, R00≈−(1/2)Δh00 and trace reversal reduce the adopted Einstein equations to ΔΦ=4πG_Nρ_mass. Exterior Φ=−GM/r also needs boundary conditions and source idealization. This is an asymptotic leading-order correspondence, not exact GR = Newtonian gravity.
6. **Linearized waves.** In a chosen approximately Minkowski patch/identification, trace reverse h̄μν=hμν−(1/2)ημνh and impose harmonic gauge ∂μh̄μν=0. To first order, □h̄μν=−16πGTμν; vacuum null plane waves have two polarizations after gauge reduction (pp.142–151, Eqs.6.1–6.54). This uses perturbative gauge freedom, not arbitrary global TT coordinates. Source-generated quadrupole amplitude uses isolated localized conserved source, slow motion, source size ≪ wavelength, far observer and retarded boundary conditions (pp.155–159). Power requires second-order calculation, TT/averaging/asymptotic conventions; Eq.6.105 is stated without proof (p.162). “Gravitons” require a separately adopted quantization/representation theory; classical polarization alone is not a quantum-particle proof.
7. **Schwarzschild local exterior and redshift.** The spherical vacuum ansatz integrates to ds²=−fdt²+f⁻¹dr²+r²dΩ², f=1−2GM/r, with mass fixed by asymptotic Newtonian matching (pp.168–171, Eqs.7.13–7.29). The derivation is in a region with nonconstant areal radius and suitable causal character; it is not a global uniqueness or timelike-Killing statement across horizons. For static observers at radii r1,r2>2GM and stationary propagation, measured frequency ratio is √[f(r1)/f(r2)] (pp.180–181, Eqs.7.58–7.62). Null-geodesic/geometric-optics interpretation is extra optical input. Comparing at fixed Schwarzschild spatial coordinates is not comparing freely falling observers.
8. **FLRW evolution/redshift/distance.** With chosen comoving u and T perfect-fluid, ∇T=0 gives ε̇+3H(ε+p)=0; constant w EOS p=wε gives ε∝a⁻³(1+w) (pp.221–222). Einstein equations give ä/a=−4πG(ε+3p)/3 and H²=8πGε/3−k/a² in c=1, with Λ represented consistently as fluid or geometric term (p.223). EOS does not follow from these equations. Comoving observer photon redshift gives 1+z=a0/a1 (p.229); photon conservation, transparent propagation, isotropic luminosity and specified geometry give d_L=a0r(1+z), then the small-z Taylor law (pp.230–231). Redshift does not measure an observer-independent remote relative velocity. Local constant spatial curvature does not settle global topology. Positive density/nonnegative pressure, Λ=0, initial expansion and smooth extension assumptions restrict recollapse arguments (pp.224–227).

## Empirical evidence and cross-framework bridges

The notes mention Pound–Rebka (p.103), binary pulsar (p.163), eclipse deflection/Mercury (pp.179–180), radar delay and proposed Gravity Probe B (p.181), CMB smoothness/Hubble estimates (pp.217,223,228–231). These were actually read. They are secondary historical summaries, without complete raw data, apparatus preparation, calibration, sampling model, statistical method, systematic-error budget or quantified uncertainties. No experiment report is established by this reading. Source dates matter: 1997 descriptions of cosmology or planned frame-dragging observations must not be presented as today's state. Do not invent updated measurements. Obtain and read primary experimental papers before recording numerical observations, and separate model predictions from finite observations and inference. Agreement supports/constrains specified GR plus matter/apparatus assumptions; it proves neither GR nor a geometric theorem.

EM coupling requires Maxwell action/equations, Gaussian versus SI conventions, charge-current regularity and matter-force exchange; null light rays require geometric optics and source/detector assumptions. Newtonian mechanics enters only through the declared weak/static/slow limit, matching of mass/potential and specified accuracy order. Quantum remarks about QED duality, Planck-scale corrections, gravitons, degeneracy pressure, vacuum energy and Hawking radiation require independent quantum/QFT postulates, state/renormalization/measurement definitions, and exact coupling approximations. GR geometry does not supply the Born rule, fermion statistics, quantum oscillator energies or unitary evaporation. Classical black-hole area/first-law algebra does not by itself establish thermodynamic entropy or Hawking temperature; their identification needs statistical/semiclassical input and appropriate regimes. The text's illustrative bridges are not shared laws silently available to consumers.
