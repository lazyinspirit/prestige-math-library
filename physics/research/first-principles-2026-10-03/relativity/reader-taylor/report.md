# Special relativity: complete Crowell reading and scaffold recommendations

This research report uses Benjamin Crowell, *Special Relativity*, revision December 10, 2020, as the fully read replacement for inaccessible Taylor/Wheeler. Source authority, retrieval hashes, complete 10-chapter inventory, bounded reading and visual recovery are documented in [reading-ledger.md](reading-ledger.md). This is a pedagogical undergraduate text with useful geometric and operational breadth. It is **not sufficient as the sole rigorous supplier**: several definitions omit domains/global hypotheses, some arguments are sketches or exercises, and concrete mathematical errors are present. Findings below concern this actual revision, not an unexamined later edition.

## Source-led assessment and exact locators

The source begins with vectors representing observer motion and simultaneity (§1.1 pp12–19), then constructs coordinates with repeated vectors and radar calibration (§1.2 pp20–21). It gives the Minkowski bilinear form (§1.3.2 pp23–24), gamma and the piecewise-inertial twin setup (§1.3.3 pp25–27), and an explicit 1+1 boost (§1.4 p31 Eq(1)). The reverse Cauchy-Schwarz and triangle inequalities appear in §1.5 pp36–37, but some verification is assigned to problems 17–19 pp41. These are strong teaching motifs; “scissors,” arrow slopes and lattice-dot counting do not replace definitions or a classification theorem.

Chapter 2's postulates are P1 existence of a timelike displacement in every frame (p44); P2 path-independent parallel transport (p46); P3 homogeneity/isotropy and P4 inertial frames (p46); P5 equivalent inertial frames and P6 frame-dependent simultaneity (p47). Einstein-style E1/E2 and maximal-proper-time T1/T2 appear pp48–49. Their equivalence is asserted at p49 without a full classification proof. “Timelike” and “frame” already carry structure, making this unsuitable as a minimal axiomatization without reconstruction. Flatness alone also does not fix global topology; p48 acknowledges cylinders/Möbius possibilities. Adopt a stated global Minkowski model, or separately develop the geometry/classification theorem with every needed assumption.

The four-velocity, four-acceleration and normalization constraints are §3.5 pp60–64; projection into an observer rest space is §3.7 pp66–68. Doppler/composition/rapidity are §§3.2–3.3 pp55–59 and identities §3.6 p65. Constant proper acceleration is §3.5.2 pp61–62; local failure of accelerated simultaneity is §3.9.3 pp73–74. Rindler coordinates and their nonuniform proper acceleration are §§7.1–7.3 pp147–151. Rotating charts/synchronization occupy §8.1 pp165–169. Congruences, expansion and Born rigidity are §9.5 pp205–211; the presented Herglotz-Noether proof explicitly assumes analytic motion (p209) and refers smooth/second-derivative strengthening elsewhere (p210).

Energy-momentum is §4.3.1 pp88–89; invariant collision mass and center-of-momentum frame are §4.3.2 p90; massive momentum/velocity and nonzero massless momentum are §§4.3.4–4.3.5 pp96–97. The source itself admits limitations of its early emission argument (§4.2 pp82–83) and repairs composite-body reasoning (§4.4 pp101–103) through stress-energy. Current and stress-energy are §§9.1–9.2 pp179–191. The more useful global result is §9.3.4 pp195–197: divergence-free total stress-energy, an isolated system with vacuum side boundary, and Gauss's theorem relate differently tilted simultaneity surfaces. Include the container/support/field contribution; excluding the wire (pp101–103) or capacitor supports (§10.6 pp233–234) destroys isolation.

## An explicit candidate framework, not an assertion that nature is proved

The following is a proposed precision layer for authoring; it is not copied wholesale from Crowell and is not a production item or independent audit.

**Mathematical structure.** Let M be a real four-dimensional affine space, modeled on V, equipped with its standard smooth manifold structure. Differences q−p belong to V; points do not themselves form a vector space until an origin is chosen. Adopt a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form η:V×V→R of signature (+,−,−,−), an orientation if needed, and a time orientation choosing one component C+ of {v:η(v,v)>0}. Fix a positive constant c with dimension length/time. An event is a point of M; treating physical coincidences as points is an idealization.

Nonzero v is timelike, null or spacelike according as η(v,v)>0, =0 or <0. Future timelike vectors lie in C+; future nonzero null vectors lie on its boundary. Future causal vectors are either of those. Define p≼q by q−p belonging to the closed future causal cone (including zero). The operational claim that information propagates only on future causal curves is an adopted physical restriction, distinct from merely defining this cone.

**Worldlines and observers.** A massive ideal particle/observer worldline is a C1 regular map z:I→M on a real interval I with nonzero future timelike tangent everywhere. For acceleration use C2; for idealized instantaneous turns use a piecewise C1 curve with continuous positions and finitely many timelike segments, and state that the kink idealizes a finite turn. Proper time between parameter values is τ=(1/c)∫sqrt(η(z′,z′))dλ. It is invariant under increasing C1 regular reparameterization. Parameterizing by τ gives U=dz/dτ with η(U,U)=c². For C2 motion define A=dU/dτ; differentiating normalization gives η(U,A)=0. Proper acceleration is sqrt(−η(A,A)) and has units m/s². A null curve is instead a C1 regular map with future nonzero null tangent; it has no proper-time parametrization or normalized massive four-velocity. Calling it a photon trajectory additionally assumes geometric optics/free massless propagation. In Minkowski space a freely propagated ray is an affine null geodesic, not an arbitrary bending null curve.

An observer at an event may mean the future timelike unit four-velocity U there, while an observing history means a timelike curve. These must be distinguished. A congruence on an open Ω⊂M is generated by a C1 (or stated higher regularity) future timelike field U with η(U,U)=c², whose maximal integral curves locally partition Ω; existence/uniqueness is a mathematical prerequisite. Neither an isolated observer nor a congruence automatically supplies a global coordinate chart.

**Frames.** An inertial coordinate frame is an origin O and an oriented orthonormal basis (e0,e1,e2,e3) with e0 future directed, η(e0,e0)=1, η(ei,ej)=−δij, η(e0,ei)=0. Write z−O=ct e0+xi ei; this defines a global affine chart (t,x1,x2,x3). Its observer family has constant velocity U=c e0 and worldlines at fixed xi. A tetrad field is a smooth orthonormal basis at each point of Ω; an observer-adapted tetrad has e0=U/c. A tetrad need not be a coordinate basis: commuting vector fields/integrability are separate conditions. A noninertial adapted chart is a diffeomorphism from an open region to an open subset of R4 whose time coordinate lines are the specified observer curves and timelike on the stated domain. Metric components, clock readings and tetrad-measured components are different quantities.

**Operational assumptions.** Adopt (i) inertial applicability in a stated region/regime, (ii) inertial-frame covariance of the selected physical laws, (iii) freely propagated ideal vacuum light rays along future null geodesics, and (iv) the ideal-clock hypothesis: a sufficiently ideal clock's reading difference equals proper time along its timelike history, independent of acceleration except through the history. Real clocks have finite resolution, environmental and acceleration-related apparatus limitations; time as “whatever any clock reads” is not a precise universal definition. Radar synchronization assigns reflection time (t_send+t_receive)/2 and distance c(t_receive−t_send)/2 in a specified inertial frame; it assumes the adopted two-way light propagation model and calibrated clock. One-way simultaneity convention is not an independent measured fact.

This geometric adoption avoids claiming that a few flight-clock differences prove SR, or that the “scissors” diagram establishes all smooth transformations. If using Einstein's two postulates to derive boosts instead, explicitly add affine homogeneity, isotropy, continuity/group/reciprocity assumptions, common calibration, orientation and time orientation, and prove the allowed transformations. Do not assert equivalence with Crowell P1–P6 as an already audited theorem.

## Conditional deductions and units

In the stated inertial chart, the displacement interval is η(Δz,Δz)=c²Δt²−|Δx|² (units m²). For a straight future timelike segment its clock time is sqrt(η(Δz,Δz))/c. For a curved worldline use the integral, not the endpoint formula. The p25 Pioneer calculation tacitly uses a straight-path approximation and omits gravitational effects; its numerical estimate cannot be advertised as an exact spacecraft clock prediction.

For a boost along +x with |v|<c and β=v/c, γ=(1−β²)^(−1/2), the formulas are t′=γ(t−vx/c²), x′=γ(x−vt), y′=y, z′=z. Specify passive coordinate convention; direct expansion proves interval invariance and reversing v gives the inverse. For a uniformly moving ideal clock dτ=dt/γ; a received tick frequency also includes light-travel/Doppler effects. For an inertial rod with fixed proper endpoint separation L0 and a declared simultaneous slice in the measuring frame, L=L0/γ along motion. Optical photographs sample a past light cone, not this slice (§1.4 p35; §6.5 pp139–140).

The twin result can be supplied by a complete short argument: if w=q−p is future timelike, choose its rest frame, so Δx=0. Every future timelike connecting curve has strictly increasing t and τ=∫sqrt(1−|dx/dt|²/c²)dt≤Δt. Equality requires dx/dt=0 throughout each smooth segment, hence the straight inertial curve; piecewise turns give strict inequality when the spatial motion is nonzero on a set of positive measure. This depends on fixed endpoints and allowed curve class. Acceleration enables reunion/asymmetric paths; it is not an additional aging term. A scenario with clocks meeting later supplies a conditional proof example, not new measurements.

For massive point particles adopt a positive invariant mass parameter m and the dynamical identification P=mU (units kg m/s). Relative to an observer Uo define Eo=η(P,Uo) (joules), spatial momentum po=P−[η(P,Uo)/c²]Uo, and rest-space norm |po|=sqrt(−η(po,po)). Then η(P,P)=m²c² gives Eo²−c²|po|²=m²c⁴. In an inertial chart P=(E/c,p); E=γmc², p=γmv, kinetic energy E−mc². Energy is observer dependent; m is invariant. A massless particle has nonzero future null P; identifying its propagation direction with P is an additional particle-model premise. It yields E=c|p| and no massive rest frame. P=mU is not a massless definition.

For a collision model specify finite lists of incoming/outgoing future causal nonzero four-momenta, on-shell masses, isolation, and the adopted conservation law ∑P_in=∑P_out at the interaction. The total Q has a center-of-momentum frame only when future timelike; parallel future null momenta give a null total with no such frame. For timelike Q define M=sqrt(η(Q,Q))/c and E_CM=Mc². Reverse triangle inequality gives M≥∑m_i for massive incoming particles. Kinematic allowance is not a guarantee that an interaction occurs: amplitudes, cross sections and additional conservation laws are further physical inputs. A single isolated null photon cannot produce a massive pair since its total squared momentum is zero, whereas a pair has positive invariant mass. The p94 title “pair production requires matter” must be restricted to the one-photon process; two-photon pair production in vacuum is kinematically allowed.

For extended matter specify a C1 symmetric total tensor T on Ω (or an explicit distributional formulation for sheets/point sources), conservation ∇_aT^{ab}=0, chosen spacelike integration surfaces, convergent integrals, orientation and no side flux/support conditions. Global integrated momentum is hypersurface independent in Minkowski space under these hypotheses. A mathematical Stokes theorem is needed; the source pp192–195 gives an instructive limit argument rather than every analytic hypothesis. In curved spacetime tangent vectors at different points cannot automatically be added; symmetries/asymptotics may nevertheless supply special conserved charges. “No global energy ever in GR” is too strong.

Natural units c=1 identify time/length scales and energy/momentum/mass numerically; restore dimensions systematically. Use c=299792458 m/s exactly in modern SI as a unit-definition value. h, ℏ, electric charge and Coulomb constant enter wave/QM/EM models as additional declared constants, not consequences of bare SR. Define angular frequency in radians/s and phase as a dimensionless scalar; the phase differential k=dφ is a covector, and measured frequency is k(Uo) with sign convention explicitly chosen. Mass, momentum, temperature and density must not be silently identified across frameworks merely because natural-unit numbers match.

## Diagrams translated into requirements

The “scissors” picture (pp16–17) should mean a timelike normalized observer direction and its η-orthogonal negative-definite rest subspace; allowed boosts preserve η and the chosen future cone. “Light rectangles” (pp22–23) mean causal diamonds J+(p)∩J−(q) in 1+1 Minkowski space with a stated volume form. Dot density (pp49–50) should be replaced by detΛ=1 for proper boosts after ΛᵀηΛ=η and continuity/orientation are established. Straight arrows between distinct events require affine structure; in curved spacetime tangent vectors live at points.

Rindler's map in 1+1, ct=ρ sinhθ and x=ρ coshθ, is a diffeomorphism only for ρ>0 onto the right wedge x>|ct|. Constant ρ has proper acceleration c²/ρ, and dτ=ρ dθ/c. Thus this chart's coordinate time is not every observer's proper time. Horizons refer to the indefinitely accelerated history/domain; finite-duration acceleration does not justify the same permanent-observability claim. Local simultaneity hyperplanes for a general accelerating observer can overlap: one must prove chart injectivity on a chosen neighborhood. An acceleration bound alone does not settle every global self-overlap/topology question.

Bell's two ships (pp71–72,210–211) require explicit trajectories x2(t)=x1(t)+L in one inertial chart, |dx1/dt|<c. This does not describe Born-rigid motion. In the constant-acceleration example U=(cγ,γv) and the local expansion is nonzero. Predicting an actual thread's rupture additionally requires an elastic constitutive law, initial state, finite stress propagation and breaking threshold; SR kinematics proves failure of Born rigidity, not universal breaking for every finite acceleration. A rigidly accelerating congruence is locally possible with position-dependent acceleration; it does not permit instantaneous signaling by a naturally perfectly rigid material.

Rotating-frame diagrams (pp165–168) concern nonintegrable local Einstein simultaneity/synchronization around a loop. Coordinate time exists; no globally synchronized comoving proper-time slice follows from that fact. Define the congruence and domain rω<c, compute the circulation and distinguish synchronization gap from counterpropagating beam arrival-time difference. Do not copy the source's approximate/exact factors without recalculation.
