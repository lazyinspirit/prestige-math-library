## Exact geometric objects and conventions for proposed content

These are candidate definitions and necessary strengthening of the source's exposition, not already accepted library items.

1. **Affine Minkowski geometry.** Let V be a real four-dimensional vector space with a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form η:V×V→R of signature (−+++), and E an affine space modeled on V: a free transitive V-action on E gives differences q−p∈V without a preferred event origin. Equip E with its smooth affine-manifold structure and the constant metric induced by η. Select a connected component C+ of {v∈V:η(v,v)<0}; future null vectors are nonzero vectors on the corresponding cone boundary. Orientation and time orientation are separate choices. Reall works with (R4,η) and inertial coordinates rather than proving the coordinate-free affine formulation (§2.1 p25); the affine construction and time-orientation suppliers remain required.

2. **Lorentz objects.** O(1,3)={Λ∈GL(V):η(Λv,Λw)=η(v,w) for all v,w}. Relative to an η-orthonormal basis, this is ΛᵀηΛ=η (§2.2 p27, equations 2.9–2.10). SO+(1,3) further requires detΛ=1 and Λ(C+)=C+. Basis change and active spacetime map are distinct: with new basis e'μ=(A−1)νμ eν, vector components obey X'μ=AμνXν. Affine Minkowski isometries include translations and Lorentz maps; proving their complete classification requires another theorem. Reall's relation is valid pointwise in TpM as well as globally in V. A position-dependent orthonormal tetrad change is not generally an affine coordinate transformation (§8.2 pp100–101).

3. **Curve and ideal clock.** On E, let γ:I→E be C2, with I an interval, nonzero future-directed timelike tangent. For λ1<λ2 in I, define proper time in seconds by τs=(1/c)∫[λ1,λ2]√(−η(γ̇,γ̇))dλ using length coordinates x0=ct. Parameterization independence assumes orientation-preserving C1 reparameterization with nonzero derivative. A physical ideal-clock postulate identifies a clock's reading with this integral; the geometry alone does not establish that apparatus follows it. Define dimensionless unit four-velocity u=dγ/d(cτs), η(u,u)=−1, and physical four-velocity U=dγ/dτs=cu. Define physical four-acceleration A=dU/dτs in affine Minkowski geometry; η(U,A)=0 follows by differentiation. In curved spacetime replace the derivative by the Levi-Civita derivative along γ. Reall defines τ and u in c=1 units (§2.2 p28), derives geodesic stationarity (§2.3 pp28–30), and uses ∇uu=0 for free particles (§3.3 pp37–38).

4. **Null curve.** An ideal ray is a C2 curve γ:I→E with nonzero future null tangent; free ray motion imposes ∇γ̇γ̇=0 for an affine parameter. Its proper-time integral is zero, so it cannot use timelike τ as a parameter or possess a normalized timelike four-velocity. Massive P=mu cannot be copied to m=0 to define photon momentum. A future null momentum field along a ray and its energy normalization require a separate physical construction. Reall states the free null-geodesic postulate and gives a high-frequency scalar geometric-optics deduction (§6.2 pp62–63); regular phase/amplitude and scale separation must remain explicit.

5. **Observer, congruence, chart and tetrad.** A single observer is a future timelike curve as above, together with any declared measurement model. A congruence on an open Ω⊂E is the family of local integral curves of a C1 future unit timelike vector field u:Ω→V; local ODE uniqueness partitions small neighborhoods into such curves, but does not imply complete worldlines or a global quotient space. An adapted chart χ:Ω0→J×W, Ω0⊂Ω open, is a diffeomorphism in which each integral curve has fixed spatial coordinates and its time coordinate increases; flow-box construction is local. An observer tetrad along a C2 curve is a C1 family e0=u,e1,e2,e3 with η(eμ,eν)=diag(−1,1,1,1). A tetrad field on Ω is a local section of the orthonormal-frame bundle; it need not be a coordinate basis (commutators need not vanish). Reall gives coordinate and field suppliers in Chapter 1; geodesic observer parallel tetrad in §7.3 pp79–80; local tetrad field in §8.2 pp100–101. Nongeodesic transport needs a separate supplier. For example, Fermi–Walker transport of X along the observer with physical U,A is ∇UX=[η(A,X)U−η(U,X)A]/c², a convention that yields ∇UU=A and preserves the rest-space metric. Reall does not develop this accelerated-observer construction.

6. **Measured energy and invariant mass.** Let m>0 be adopted particle invariant mass in kg and P=mU=mc u its four-momentum with units kg·m/s. For an observer with unit dimensionless uo at the same event, Eo=−cη(uo,P) in J, and the rest-space projection is p_o=P+η(uo,P)uo. Its Euclidean rest-space norm gives Eo²=c²‖p_o‖²+m²c⁴. Thus Eo=mc² only if p_o=0, not for every observer. For a system with future timelike total P, M=√(−η(P,P))/c, and E=Mc² only in its center-of-momentum frame. Reall's c=1 equations P=mu, η(P,P)=−m² and E=−g(v,P) are in §§6.3 pp63–64; its center-of-momentum equality is explicitly stated in §7.4 p86. In GR, vectors at different events cannot be directly contracted without transport/identification choices; transmitted light measurements are local receiving events, not direct remote contractions.

7. **Curved structure and flat-local bridge.** A GR candidate spacetime is a smooth Hausdorff second-countable four-manifold M, a smooth Lorentzian metric g, and a chosen time orientation (existence must be required, not assumed automatic). Its Levi-Civita connection is the unique torsion-free metric connection. A pointwise orthonormal frame identifies (TpM,gp) with (V,η); this is algebraic, not an isometry between neighborhoods. Normal coordinates have g(p)=η and ∂g(p)=0; curvature appears at second order (§3.4 p39, §4.6 p49). Riemann-flat Levi-Civita geometry is locally Minkowski (§4.2 p42), but global topology/holonomy/completeness require further hypotheses. Ricci flatness is weaker than Riemann flatness. No global SR equivalence follows from an Einstein vacuum equation alone.

Units must be attached to each formulation. Reall uses c=1 throughout and G=c=1 from p69 onward. With SI mass m, geometrized mass length is Gm/c², energy length GE/c⁴, time length cτs and dimensionless velocity v/c. A Klein–Gordon parameter of inverse-length units is mc/ℏ, not automatically a mass in kg. Source Maxwell equations use Gaussian units (pp61,64–65); importing them into SI requires an explicit electromagnetic convention/conversion supplier.

## Deductions checked locally; source proof gaps retained

**Flat proper-time maximum, independently checked elementary argument.** Assume affine Minkowski geometry, future-directed timelike endpoints p,q, and a global inertial chart in which q−p=(cT,0,0,0) with T>0. Existence of such a rest chart uses the Lorentz rest-frame supplier and is not proved by this argument. For any C1 future timelike joining curve on a compact parameter interval, t is strictly increasing; reparameterize by t∈[0,T]. Then

τs=∫₀ᵀ√(1−‖v(t)‖²/c²)dt≤T.

Equality holds iff v=0 throughout, because the integrand is continuous and is strictly less than 1 wherever v≠0. The unique maximizing image is the straight timelike segment. This is a conditional mathematical statement; turning it into a twin-clock thought experiment also requires the clock postulate, ideal reunion setup and any acceleration/clock limitations. It does not say that a general curved-spacetime geodesic globally maximizes proper time. Reall derives the Euler–Lagrange necessary condition and states maxima without proof (p30).

**Lorentz Lie algebra, independently checked finite-dimensional calculation.** For smooth Λ(s) with Λ(0)=I, differentiate ΛᵀηΛ=η to obtain Kᵀη+ηK=0. Conversely exp(sK) preserves η because differentiating exp(sK)ᵀη exp(sK) gives zero. Therefore exp(sK) belongs to the identity component and preserves orientation and the chosen time cone. A spatial reflection with determinant −1 belongs to O(1,3) but cannot equal expK since det expK=exp trK>0. Thus exponentiation cannot describe every Lorentz transformation. With source generators (Mρσ)μν=ημρδσν−ημσδρν, direct matrix multiplication gives

[Mμν,Mρσ]=ηνρMμσ−ημρMνσ−ηνσMμρ+ημσMνρ.

This is a local calculation, not an independent whole-item certification. Reall equation (8.38), printed p104 / PDF105, literally leaves the commutator as an ellipsis; the source cannot be cited as a supplied complete proof. Its spinor discussion needs explicit representation/covering-group conventions and spin structures before global use. The gamma-matrix factors of i were visually read but not independently audited; no spinor theorem acceptance is claimed.

**Observer energy identity, independently checked algebra.** With η(uo,uo)=−1 and η(P,P)=−m²c², p_o is orthogonal to uo and η(p_o,p_o)=η(P,P)+η(uo,P)². Multiplying by c² gives the stated mass-shell identity. Positive energy additionally uses future direction of P and uo. The invariant is m; measured Eo and three-momentum depend on the observer.

Source proof obligations still open include Sylvester inertia; maximal atlas/topological conditions; derivations↔tangent curves; local flow/ODE extension/completeness; Frobenius/coordinate-basis characterization; inverse function theorem for exp; convex normal neighborhoods and curved local proper-time maximality; flat local-isometry theorem; contracted Bianchi proof/exercises; partitions of unity and Stokes integrability; tensor PDE well-posedness; gauge residual/boundary control; global spin structures; Lovelock hypotheses/proof; Choquet–Bruhat and maximal Cauchy-development hypotheses/proofs. They cannot all be treated as proved by this course merely because theorem statements occur in it.

Other precision repairs: §8.5 p107's count of precisely two orientations needs connectedness; general disconnected manifolds can choose signs per component. The partition of unity in §8.6 p108 needs smoothness, local finiteness and subordinate support. Stokes/variational statements need compact support or adequate integrability/boundary conditions. §10.5 p124's uniform time slab (−T,T)×Σ for arbitrary smooth noncompact initial data requires additional qualifications; the source's concise theorem is not a complete hypothesis supplier. ## Physical assumptions, constructions and empirical scope

Physical postulates must be recorded separately: Minkowski geometry as an adopted flat-spacetime model; clock/rod measurement interpretation; no preferred inertial background beyond stated structures; particle momentum/mass interpretation and force/dynamics assumptions; in GR the Lorentzian metric model, geodesic test-body approximation, matter stress model and Einstein field equation. Choosing coordinates/tetrads, defining causal cones or forming g contractions are mathematical constructions. Lorentz transformations, variational geodesics, local normal charts and conservation consequences are deductions with stated assumptions, not new measurements.

Reall explicitly calls §6.1 motivating principles mathematically imprecise and ambiguous (pp57–60), and warns that covariantization does not uniquely determine curvature couplings (p62). These cautions should travel into any first-principles design. General covariance as tensor-writing alone does not prove Lorentz invariance: the source's preferred-frame wave equation on p59 is the useful counterexample.

This course is not a primary experimental report. Its LIGO, Hulse–Taylor and GW170817 descriptions (pp81–82,96–98) were read as reported textbook summaries; underlying datasets, detector calibration, noise likelihoods, selection effects, source assumptions and uncertainty budgets were not retrieved here. No numerical experimental confidence or new measurements are asserted by this report. In particular, finite clock or particle observations constrain specified SR/GR predictions plus apparatus assumptions; they never deductively prove the framework. Multimessenger arrival-time comparison also needs emission-delay and distance modeling. Detailed experiment items must use primary source retrieval and statistical review separately.
