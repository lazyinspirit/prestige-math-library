## Explicit mathematical objects and physical quantities

The following is a proposed precise formulation compatible with Tong's chosen signature. It makes implicit assumptions explicit; it is not a claim that Tong proves all existence results.

- **Spacetime:** a connected, Hausdorff, second-countable smooth 4-manifold M, with smooth symmetric nondegenerate tensor g ∈ Γ(Sym²T*M) of signature (−,+,+,+). For elementary calculations assume C∞; reduced-regularity statements need separate hypotheses. A chosen continuous/smooth timelike field V gives time orientation. A nonzero causal vector X is future-directed when g(X,V)<0. Time orientability and spacetime orientation are distinct; the latter is required for global volume forms/integration. Source: §§2.1 pp50–55;3.1 pp91–96; source omits second countability/time-orientation construction.
- **Chart:** (U,χ), U open in M, χ:U→χ(U)⊂R⁴ a homeomorphism with smooth compatible transition maps. Coordinates x^μ are scalar functions on U, not vectors on M. A coordinate basis ∂μ is tangent, dual dx^μ is cotangent. Tong §§2.1–2.3 pp51–73. A chart does not supply an observer and need not have a timelike coordinate vector.
- **Tangent/cotangent/tensor:** T_pM is the real vector space of derivations D of germs of smooth real functions at p satisfying D(fh)=f(p)D(h)+h(p)D(f). T*_pM=Hom_R(T_pM,R). An (r,s) tensor is a multilinear map (T*_pM)^r×(T_pM)^s→R; a field is a smooth section of the corresponding bundle. Coordinate components transform with Jacobians/inverse Jacobians, not as arbitrary matrices. Tong §§2.2–2.3 pp56–75; use germs or justify extension/locality in the source's global C∞ formulation.
- **Tetrad:** four smooth local tangent fields e_a on U forming a basis at each p, g(e_a,e_b)=η_ab. Its dual coframe θ^a satisfies θ^a(e_b)=δ^a_b. A tetrad need not commute or arise from coordinates, and an orthonormal tetrad with flat components does not mean curvature vanishes. Local Lorentz changes preserve η. A tetrad with future e_0 gives one observer plus spatial axes; it still is not a coordinate chart. Tong §3.4.2 pp132–136.
- **Observer congruence:** a smooth future timelike field U on a region, normalized g(U,U)=−c², whose local integral curves give observer worldlines parametrized by proper time. Only local flow is automatic; global coverage, completeness and adapted coordinates are extra assumptions. Spatial projector is h=g+U♭⊗U♭/c². A hypersurface rest space exists locally only with the required Frobenius/hypersurface-orthogonality condition; arbitrary rotating congruences do not define global simultaneity. Tong has integral curves §2.2.3 pp63–65 and fluid u p187 but does not develop this complete observer framework.
- **Worldline/proper time:** C² regular curve γ:I→M, I interval, with everywhere future timelike tangent for an ideal massive body. For finite parameter endpoints, Δτ=(1/c)∫√[−g(γ′,γ′)]dλ, assuming integrability. With parameter τ, U=γ̇ and g(U,U)=−c². A piecewise C¹ version can be defined separately. A light ray is a future null regular curve, γ′≠0; its metric proper time is zero, so affine λ must replace τ. Normalization alone does not distinguish past from future. Tong §§1.2.1–1.2.6 pp12–29;3.1.2 pp93–94.
- **Connection:** affine connection ∇:Γ(TM)×Γ(TM)→Γ(TM), R-bilinear, C∞-linear in first input, ∇_X(fY)=X(f)Y+f∇_XY. Adopt the unique torsion-free, metric-compatible Levi-Civita connection. Its coefficients Γ^μ_{νρ} are not a tensor. ∇ extends by Leibniz and contraction compatibility. ∇_{γ′}γ′=0 defines an affinely parametrized geodesic; general parametrization gives a tangent RHS. Parallel transport is a linear ODE along a specified path, not canonical identification between all tangent spaces. Tong §§3.2–3.3 pp101–123, especially Koszul formula (3.17) p109 and Christoffel formula (3.18) p110.
- **Curvature:** R(X,Y)Z=∇_X∇_YZ−∇_Y∇_XZ−∇_[X,Y]Z; R^σ_{ρμν} denotes the σ component of R(∂μ,∂ν)∂ρ. Ric_{μν}=R^ρ_{μρν}, R=g^{μν}Ric_{μν}, G_{μν}=Ric_{μν}−Rg_{μν}/2. This fixes the sign and argument ordering before use. Torsion T(X,Y)=∇_XY−∇_YX−[X,Y]. Bianchi gives ∇^μG_{μν}=0, not ∇_λG_{μν}=0. Tong §§3.2.2,3.4 pp105–108,130–132.
- **Matter/stress:** classical scalar φ:M→R, Maxwell curvature F∈Γ(Λ²T*M), fluid scalar proper energy density ε and pressure P with future velocity U, etc., each with its chosen action or constitutive law. Symmetric stress-energy T∈Γ(Sym²T*M) is defined by metric variation, with units restored consistently. For coordinates x⁰=ct, δS_m=−(1/2c)∫√−g T_{μν}δg^{μν}d⁴x. Diffeomorphism invariance plus matter equations and suitable boundary variations yields ∇_μT^{μν}=0. Source formula (4.46) p183 uses c=1. In an orthonormal length-coordinate basis T components have energy-density units, ML⁻¹T⁻². Fluid T^{μν}=(ε+P)U^μU^ν/c²+Pg^{μν}. ε is comoving density; another observer measures T(U_obs,U_obs)/c². Tong §§4.5.1–4.5.5 pp181–191.
- **Einstein equations:** G_{μν}+Λg_{μν}=(8πG_N/c⁴)T_{μν}, smooth classical form with chosen matter equations, initial constraints and boundary conditions. G_N is the gravitational coupling, to distinguish it from Einstein tensor G_{μν}. Λ has dimension L⁻². Vacuum with Λ=0 gives Ric=0; this does not imply Riemann=0 (Schwarzschild is an example). Point/shell sources demand a separate distributional/junction formulation. Tong §§4.1,4.5.2 pp141–147,183–184.

Tong begins with x^μ=(ct,x,y,z) (§1.2.1 p12) and η=diag(−1,1,1,1), retains c explicitly in particle actions. The conventions page (PDF7) says this is opposite to his SR/QFT notes. §2.4.2 p80 explicitly sets c=1 for EM forms; §3.4.3 p136 also uses c=1 Schwarzschild expressions before the general §4.1.1 p145 declaration. §4.1.1 adopts c=1 and retains G; it discusses but does not adopt c=G=1 as the standing convention. ℏ=1 is discussed for Planck dimensions, not an automatic classical-GR convention. Proper-time integral p94 omits 1/c consistently only with c=1. With t in seconds, metric coefficient multiplying dt² carries c²; with x⁰=ct it does not. Restore constants and choose chart dimensions before importing any formula. SI and natural-unit EM normalizations cannot be mixed using symbol matching alone.

## Conditional and global statements

**Local SR versus global geometry.** Tangent spaces have Lorentzian inner products and normal coordinates give g(p)=η, ∂g(p)=0. Curvature affects second-order neighborhood terms; finite laboratories need a scale ℓ with curvature corrections controlled, roughly |R|ℓ²≪1 in an appropriate frame and with derivative bounds. This is a local approximation, not exact Minkowski physics over an arbitrary region. A globally flat metric can have nontrivial topology/holonomy or incomplete domains. Global Minkowski identification requires suitable topology and completeness hypotheses; Ric=0 is far weaker. §§3.1.2,3.3.2 pp93–94,119–123.

**Observers and conserved energy.** For affine geodesic tangent X and Killing field K, g(K,X) is constant because ∇_(μK_{ν)}=0 and ∇_XX=0. For a future timelike observer U, −g(p,U) is a local measurement and need not be conserved along another worldline. Positive E_K requires K and p future causal with K timelike and a declared normalization. Killing currents J^μ=−T^{μν}K_ν have ∇_μJ^μ=0 when T is symmetric/on shell. Surface integrals require spacelike hypersurfaces, orientation, convergence and vanishing/recorded side flux; no global charge follows from a local identity alone. Komar, ADM, Bondi and approximate wave energy have different domains and definitions. de Sitter static energy is not globally positive; a Kerr ∂t Killing energy can be negative in the ergoregion. §§4.3.1–4.3.3 pp161–168;4.5.5 pp188–192;6.3.3 pp275–280.

**Causality.** Define J⁺(p) using future-directed causal curves, I⁺(p) using timelike curves; future orientation is independent of coordinate-time monotonicity. A Cauchy hypersurface intersects every inextendible causal curve exactly once (state which causal convention is used); existence/global hyperbolicity and evolution well-posedness need rigorous suppliers, not just a spacelike slice. AdS universal cover has a timelike conformal boundary and needs boundary data; simply removing periodic AdS time does not make it globally hyperbolic. §§4.2.2,4.4.2 pp159–160,179–181;6.2.4 pp264–266.

**Black holes.** With a specified asymptotically flat conformal completion and future null infinity component ℐ⁺, define B=M\J⁻(ℐ⁺) and H⁺=∂J⁻(ℐ⁺)∩M, with conventions checked on boundary inclusion. This is global and future-dependent. Full Kruskal has two asymptotic ends; which component is selected matters. EF extension versus eternal Kruskal versus collapse cannot be equated. A smooth nondegenerate horizon chart removes coordinate failure; bounded curvature scalars alone do not prove regularity. Singularity theorems generally establish causal geodesic incompleteness under specified hypotheses, not a universal “infinite-density point” description. §§6.1.2–6.1.5 pp237–258.

Birkhoff requires vacuum, spherical SO(3) orbits and correct treatment of exceptional coordinate regions; source proof is incomplete. It controls an exterior model, not every rotating star. Kerr uniqueness needs stationary asymptotically flat vacuum/electrovacuum black-hole hypotheses plus relevant horizon regularity, connectedness, analyticity or replacement assumptions from the exact theorem. It does not alone prove generic collapse settles to Kerr. RN/Kerr inner analytic extensions are ideal models with Cauchy-horizon instability concerns. Weak/strong cosmic censorship and chronology protection are explicitly conjectural here; “generic” requires a topology/function space, not an invented probability measure. The scalar no-hair argument applies only to a precisely restricted free scalar model/background and suitable boundary behavior. §§6.1.1,6.1.5,6.2.4,6.3 pp234–236,256–258,264–283.

**Energy/matter.** Weak: T(X,X)≥0 for every timelike X; null: T(N,N)≥0 for every null N; dominant: for future timelike X, −T^μ_νX^ν is future causal or zero; strong: (T−½tr_g(T)g)(X,X)≥0 for timelike X when Λ is absorbed appropriately. Geometric Ric(X,X)≥0 is not equivalent to an unqualified matter condition with nonzero Λ. These are pointwise hypotheses, not empirical universal truths. Their focusing consequences also need Raychaudhuri, congruence vorticity/initial expansion and global hypotheses. Tong §§4.5.7 pp195–199. Classical nonminimal/negative-potential matter and quantum stress can violate conditions; semiclassical ⟨T⟩ needs renormalization and separate coupling postulates.

**Cosmology.** FLRW adopts homogeneous/isotropic spatial geometry and comoving perfect fluids; it is not an exact inference from observed redshifts alone. Choose either dimensionless spatial coordinates with a dimensionful scale factor and normalized k=±1,0, or dimensionful spatial coordinates with compatible k units and dimensionless a. Friedmann/continuity equations follow conditional on this model, equation of state and Λ. Redshift is an observer/ray relation; dynamical scale-factor interpretation needs the FLRW construction. §§4.6 pp199–204.

| Schwarzschild precession | Test geodesic exterior, negligible spin/multipoles/other-body effects, small β=3G²M²/(l²c²), secular expansion used for leading per-orbit shift, fixed orbital conventions | §§1.3.1–1.3.4 pp30–43 |

| Near-horizon Rindler | Nonextremal Schwarzschild exterior, η=r−2GM≪2GM, suitable coordinate patch and order control; not extremal RN, whose throat tends to AdS₂×S² | §§6.1.2,6.2.5 pp238–239,267–268 |

Newtonian Poisson reduction alone does not establish Newtonian particle motion; combine it with the slow geodesic equation and explicit identification of t, Φ and density. Asymptotic limits must specify families/scaling and a topology or error estimate; “c→∞” as a dimensionful slogan is insufficient. Point masses/delta densities used for illustration require distributional suppliers or restriction to the exterior. Tong's claim p210 that isotropic spatial h_ij=−2Φδ_ij coincides directly with the areal-radius Schwarzschild spatial metric needs the isotropic coordinate transformation (compare §6.1.4 pp251–252).

Maxwell, fluid, scalar, spinor, quantum, and thermodynamic couplings are separate frameworks/model assumptions. In particular: minimal replacement η→g is not uniquely forced for scalars (ξRφ² p182); geometric optics needs a derivation; classical fluids made of fermions need an effective equation of state; semiclassical Einstein equations and black-hole entropy cannot enter a classical-GR proof from units or informal quantum remarks. Cosmological topology quotients may be locally constant-curvature without full global isotropy; no global classification without stated completeness/symmetry assumptions.
