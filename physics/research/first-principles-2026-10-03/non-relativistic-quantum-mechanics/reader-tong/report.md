## Framework and adopted assumptions

The notes restrict themselves to a single nonrelativistic particle (§1.1 p3; §1.2.1 p10). The physical content should be classified as follows.

| Adopted framework postulate | Tong locator | Library formulation needed |
|---|---|---|
| State described by complex wavefunction; constant nonzero complex multiples identify the same pure state | §1.1 pp3–4; §1.1.1 pp6–7 | Specify complex Hilbert space H; pure states are rays of nonzero vectors. Normalised representatives differ by constant phase. H includes zero, pure state space does not. This notes set does not supply density-operator/mixed-state formalism. |
| Superposition | §1.1.2 pp7–9; §3.1 p63 | A nonzero linear combination of vectors represents a state after normalisation. The ray space itself is not a vector space; exclude cancellations to zero. Do not claim this follows from empirical interference alone. |
| Born probabilities | §1.1 p4; §3.3 pp76–77, equations3.10/3.12 | For self-adjoint observable A with spectral PVM E_A, μψ(B)=⟨ψ,E_A(B)ψ⟩ for unit ψ and Borel B. The source explicitly gives discrete nondegenerate/degenerate formulas; continuous generalisation requires mathematical supplier. |
| Observable represented by Hermitian operator; measured values lie in spectrum | §§3.2–3.2.2 pp66–74 | Use densely defined self-adjoint A:D(A)⊂H→H and its spectral measure. “Hermitian” alone or a formal differential expression does not specify observable. Spectral outcomes include continuous spectrum, not merely normalisable eigenvalues. |
| Schrödinger dynamics | §1.2 p9 equation1.1; §3 introduction p63 | For isolated autonomous model choose self-adjoint H and U(t)=exp(−itH/ℏ). All initial vectors have strongly continuous unitary evolution; norm-differentiable equation requires initial vector in D(H). Time-dependent H needs additional hypotheses and propagator theory. |
| Ideal projective measurement update and repeatability | §1.2.3 pp13–14; §3.3 pp76–78 equations3.11/3.12 | Conditional state E_A(B)ψ/||E_A(B)ψ|| when outcome event has positive probability, under declared ideal Lüders instrument. Degenerate eigenspaces need projections rather than an arbitrary eigenvector. Measurement model is an adopted idealisation, not deduced from learning a value. Continuous exact-position collapse does not yield an L² state. |

Restricted **model assumptions**, not universal postulates: configuration space Q=R, circle or interval (chapter2); Euclidean R³ (chapter4); mass m>0; external real potential V; differential Hamiltonian −ℏ²Δ/(2m)+V; chosen boundary conditions; time-independent potential when separating energy eigenstates; ideal monochromatic stationary scattering beams with no incidence from the right; isotropic central potentials; fixed point proton and spinless electron in hydrogen. Singular delta potentials and infinite walls require separate operator constructions. No derivation from a classical Hamiltonian establishes that quantisation choice universally describes nature.

The photon relation E=ℏω and E=pc enters as extra input (§4.4.2 pp121–122, equation4.34), not a consequence of the single massive-particle Hamiltonian. The notes explicitly defer a quantum-field treatment. Radiation emission, photon absorption and transition rates therefore belong in a separately scoped extension. Energy differences alone do not prove that an isolated stationary atom emits light.

**Reference frame is an open supplier, not defined by Tong.** A precise nonrelativistic choice can use Galilean spacetime M=R×R³ with absolute clock dt, Euclidean metric on spatial slices and flat inertial structure. An inertial coordinate frame is a global chart (t,x) compatible with those structures, with changes t′=t+b, x′=Rx−vt+a, R∈SO(3). More general frames need their own conventions. This is proposed mathematical formulation, not a claim extracted from the notes. Wavefunction boost phases and moving-potential transformations require further derivation/source before use. If “worldline” is used for a classical comparator, define a C² curve γ:I→M with t(γ(s))=s; it is not an assumed quantum-particle trajectory. Quantum measurement records do not themselves supply an unmeasured path. No relativistic causal restrictions belong in this Galilean definition.

## Conditional results and essential proof hypotheses

1. **Local continuity / conserved norm:** equations1.5–1.7, §1.2.2 pp11–13. The algebra needs real V and ψ sufficiently differentiable; surface integration needs appropriate region and integrability; conservation needs no net boundary flux. Bare L² integrability does not imply pointwise decay or vanishing derivative flux. A robust general norm proof uses self-adjoint H and unitarity instead.
2. **Circle / box spectra:** §§2.1.1–2.1.2 pp22–26. Declare circle measure and periodic H² domain; box Hamiltonian acts on L²(0,L) with H²∩H¹_0 domain. Infinite V is shorthand for Dirichlet confinement, not ordinary multiplication by infinity. The source's two-plane-wave box interpretation does not establish a self-adjoint Dirichlet momentum observable or a two-point momentum distribution; specify measurement after release if using full-line momentum.
3. **Gaussian packet:** §§2.1.3–2.1.4 pp27–33. Gaussian coefficient parameter σ>0, m>0; Gaussian integrals require Re α>0 and justified interchange/differentiation. Arbitrary A(k) in equation2.12 does not guarantee a normalisable differentiable Schrödinger solution; invoke Plancherel and relevant weighted-domain conditions.
4. **Oscillator spectrum:** §§2.2.1–2.2.2 pp35–40. m,ω>0, standard self-adjoint confining oscillator. Series-growth arguments are sketches; use complete Hermite-basis or quadratic-form/ladder proof with domain and completeness suppliers. Nondegenerate smooth minimum with V″>0 and controlled Taylor remainder supplies a local harmonic approximation, not an exact arbitrary-potential theorem.
5. **1D matching and bound states:** §§2.3.1–2.3.3 pp42–51. Bounded jump potentials produce continuous ψ,ψ′ for domain solutions. Delta point interaction is continuous ψ with derivative jump eq2.39 and piecewise H² domain. Wronskian simplicity needs real locally regular potential, common boundary conditions, endpoint control of ψ and derivatives, and ODE uniqueness across zeros. Connected 1D setup matters; impenetrable barriers or disjoint intervals can produce degeneracy. Reality uses real coefficients and nondegeneracy. Finite bound-state count needs decay/integrability hypotheses, not just V→0; Coulomb chapter itself later exhibits infinitely many levels. Ground-state no-node argument is explicitly only a semi-proof; smoothing must control potential/norm terms. nth-state n-node assertion is explicitly “No Proof” (p52); supply Sturm oscillation theorem.
6. **Double well:** §2.3.4 pp52–54. Symmetric smooth confining potential, deep/separated wells and quantitative approximation assumptions needed. Closed time-independent dynamics conserves mean energy; a localised packet does not automatically relax to a lower-energy symmetric ground state as p54 suggests. Two-level approximation gives oscillations, not irreversible equilibration, unless environment/measurement assumptions are added.
7. **Stationary scattering:** §§2.4.1–2.4.2 pp56–62. Real time-independent potential, chosen no-incoming-right boundary condition, 0<E<U for sub-barrier result, m,a,U>0. R+T=1 uses flux conservation and absence of absorption/extra channels; “any potential” (p59) must be restricted accordingly. Exponential approximation needs ηa≫1, not merely an undefined “low energy.” Alpha decay is a qualitative analogy, not an empirical lifetime report or full 3D decay derivation.
8. **Spectral expectations / commutation:** §§3.2.2–3.4 pp71–84. Discrete expansion proofs need complete orthonormal eigenbasis and convergence. General self-adjoint operators need spectral integrals; a symmetric operator need not have discrete complete eigenfunctions. For unbounded operators, common-core algebraic [A,B]=0 is not enough for strong spectral commutation. Finite dimensional joint diagonalisation or a bounded/discrete restricted theorem can be proved separately. Stone–von Neumann mention (p83) needs strongly continuous regular irreducible Weyl representation, finite degrees of freedom and fixed nonzero ℏ, not formal CCR alone.
9. **Uncertainty:** §3.4.1 pp84–88. Use finite second moments/ψ in D(X)∩D(P), with common product domain if following Tong's algebraic commutator proof; Schwartz class is safe for displayed derivation. Extend by analysis afterward. ΔXΔP≥ℏ/2 is preparation spread, not a general statement about instrument errors or recoil. General [A,B]≠0 does not preclude a shared eigenvector or both uncertainties zero: noncommutation may hold elsewhere. l=0 orbital states are also exceptions to claims that all angular components cannot be sharp (§4.1.1 p96; §4.1.2 p101).
10. **Central potentials / radial reduction:** §§4.1–4.2 pp95–111. Rotationally invariant operator and domain, not merely V(r), establish symmetry. L²(S²,sinθ dθ dφ) harmonic completeness/regularity must be supplied. The map R↦u=rR is unitary from radial L²(r²dr) to L²(dr), with origin/domain condition from the chosen 3D operator. §4.2.3 pp110–111 tests a boundary form, which proves symmetry on a domain, not equality with the adjoint domain. Do not replace H by a smaller Hilbert space; D(H) is normally dense in the unchanged H. Point-interaction extensions are different models.
11. **Hydrogen:** §§4.3–4.3.2 pp111–118, equations4.30/4.31. Fixed proton, spinless nonrelativistic electron, attractive Coulomb interaction; n² degeneracy is orbital only. Two-body relative-coordinate version uses reduced mass, and requires new supplier. Relativity, spin, fine/hyperfine structure, Lamb shifts and radiation omitted. Radial probability includes r² measure; p118 heuristic density peak must not be confused with radial probability peak.
12. **2D renormalised contact interaction:** §§4.5.1–4.5.2 pp124–130, equations4.35–4.42. Formal δψ with ψ(0) is not a defined self-adjoint model for singular limiting states. Define a regulated separable operator/cutoff or self-adjoint extension and prove limit before theorem. Λ has inverse-length units; rescaled E has inverse-length squared units. The Taylor-flat statement for exp(−2π/g) is a right-sided smooth extension at g=0, not analyticity or valid two-sided Taylor expansion.

## Empirical qualifications and source gaps

The opening claim that QM is “undoubtably the correct description” (p2), categorical ontological statements (§1.1 pp4–5), broad measurement-disturbance claims (§3.3 p78), and interpretation judgments (§3.5 pp88–94) are exposition, not formal experiment-backed conclusions. A library should distinguish adopted state completeness from tested alternatives.

Double slit (§1.3 pp14–18) explicitly compares simple classical particles and classical waves and explains cross terms. Figure5 p17 shows Hitachi image accumulation at 8,270,2000,160000 electrons. The notes do not provide the complete apparatus, detection efficiency, binning, independence model, uncertainties, coherence controls, raw dataset or statistical test. These counts cannot support invented significance or a complete reported-experiment item. Finite coherent samples can lack recognisable fringes with nonzero probability under ordinary nondegenerate sampling, and agreement does not prove QM. This qualification is required by the repository; it is not a computed failure rate from Tong. Specify competing incoherent model and auxiliary conditions; classical waves also interfere. The source's statement that each electron necessarily passes both slits (p17) is interpretation, not an inference uniquely forced by the image. Path-marker decoherence needs composite-state mathematics, absent here.

Bell (§3.5.1 pp89–91) is described qualitatively, with no inequality proof or detailed experimental report. The text first announces hidden variables falsified, then qualifies local hidden variables. A production account must state locality/factorisation, measurement-setting assumptions, sampling and loophole treatment, and particular reports; do not infer every deterministic alternative is ruled out. This notes set cannot supply that chain.

Balmer/Rydberg (§4.4 p118) supplies historical fit and line formula, not calibration, uncertainty or measured wavelength table. Coupling to radiation (§4.4 pp119–122) remains external input. Photoelectric text (§4.4.2 p122) uses ideal one-photon threshold language; “no matter how strong” cannot be universal because multiphoton/strong-field regimes lie outside that model. Renormalisation plot (§4.5 p124) is not a complete experimental record and does not supply uncertainty/source analysis. No experiment item should be produced solely from these sketches.

Missing deep topics from this complete introductory set: spin and spin addition; density operators and open dynamics; tensor-product composition/entanglement; identical particles/statistics; perturbation theory; variational proof; adiabatic theorem; rigorous WKB/error control; full 3D scattering theory; time-dependent transitions and radiation; Bell proofs and experimental statistics. These are genuinely out of this source's scope, often explicitly deferred to Topics in Quantum Mechanics, not omissions in my reading.
