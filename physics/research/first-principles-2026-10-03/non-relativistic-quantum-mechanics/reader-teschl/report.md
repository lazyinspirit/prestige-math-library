## Explicit objects and domains required before physical terminology

Use a complex separable Hilbert space `(H,<.,.>)`, with inner product conjugate-linear in its first argument, linear in its second. An element ψ of unit norm represents a pure preparation; a physical ray identifies ψ and exp(iθ)ψ. The text uses normalized vectors (§2.1, pp55–57); its four axioms do not fully discuss the ray equivalence or general mixed states. Define these separately using the physical sources. For a spinless particle on R^d, H=L²(R^d,dx); these are equivalence classes modulo Lebesgue-a.e. equality, not arbitrary pointwise functions. A time evolution is t↦ψ(t)∈H; representative ψ(x,t) needs additional regularity when pointwise derivatives are asserted.

An operator is a specified linear map A:D(A)⊂H→H. The domain is part of the operator. Dense definition means closure(D(A))=H; it does not mean every normalized vector is in D(A). Its adjoint domain comprises η for which φ↦<η,Aφ> has a bounded representing functional; A*η is that representing vector (§2.2 (2.19), p59). Symmetric means <φ,Aψ>=<Aφ,ψ> for φ,ψ∈D(A). Self-adjoint means equality A=A* including D(A)=D(A*). Closed means closed graph; essentially self-adjoint means the closure is self-adjoint. A core is dense in D(A) for the graph norm sqrt(||ψ||²+||Aψ||²), rather than only dense in H (pp63–66).

A self-adjoint observable A has a projection-valued measure P_A:Borel(R)→bounded orthogonal projections with P_A(R)=I and countable additivity in the strong operator topology (§3.1, p88). For unit ψ, μ_ψ(B)=<ψ,P_A(B)ψ>=||P_A(B)ψ||² is a probability measure. Interpreting it as outcome probabilities is a physical Born-rule assumption, not a mathematical consequence of self-adjointness alone. Expectation through the spectral integral can exist for ψ∈D(|A|^(1/2)); the operator expression <ψ,Aψ> needs ψ∈D(A). Finite variance requires ∫λ²dμ_ψ<∞, i.e. ψ∈D(A), while writing <ψ,A²ψ> additionally requires D(A²); use the norm ||(A-mean)ψ||² to avoid adding that stronger hypothesis accidentally.

For the free model, define Fourier F:L²(R^n_x)→L²(R^n_k) as the unitary extension of (2π)^(-n/2)∫exp(-ik·x)f(x)dx on Schwartz functions. Define H^r={f∈L²: |k|^r Ff∈L²}; weak derivatives use test functions Cc∞ and integration by parts (§7.1 pp161–165). H0=-Δ is a map H²→L², with form domain H¹ (§7.2 pp167–168). Momentum component has maximal Fourier multiplier domain {ψ:k_j Fψ∈L²}; it is not necessary to impose all H¹ derivatives for one component. Position component has maximal multiplication domain {ψ:x_jψ∈L²}. On Schwartz functions the canonical commutators are calculable, but unbounded commutation relations need that common domain and do not imply a bounded-operator identity on H.

Restore units explicitly. Teschl uses ℏ=1 and m=1/2 (§7.2 p167), giving H0=-Δ and momentum -i∇. In SI, H0=-(ℏ²/2m)Δ has units of energy, p=-iℏ∇ has momentum units, iℏ dψ/dt=Hψ and U(t)=exp(-itH/ℏ). Coordinate x has length units and L=x×p has action units. For L²(R^d,dx) normalized with dimensional dx, a representative ψ has units length^(-d/2); |ψ|² is a spatial density. V:R^d→R has energy units. For H=-Δ-γ/r in the scaled source, γ and r refer to nondimensionalized variables; the dimensional Coulomb model needs stated charge/coupling, mass/reduced mass, length/energy scales and fixed-nucleus approximation. A framework's units cannot establish that its Hamiltonian accurately represents a real atom.

## Source weaknesses that must remain visible

Reading a rigorous textbook is not a theorem audit. Several concrete problems were detected during full reading. The saved rendered pages distinguish real printed problems from extraction errors.

1. Lemma12.3 p249 (PDF261) prints D(H)⊂D(H0) but applies (H-H0)exp(-itH0) to ψ∈D(H0). That expression requires exp(-itH0)ψ∈D(H), usually secured by **D(H0)⊂D(H)** plus suitable integrability/strong differentiation; in the intended H²-potential application domains are equal, so the application survives. Supply a corrected Cook lemma and proof locally.
2. Equation(6.42) p151 (PDF163) uses (1-C_q)^(-1), while the defining positive perturbation and proof calculation use (1+C_q)^(-1). Scalar A=0, q=b||ψ||² gives resolvent 1/(λ+b), confirming the plus sign. Do not import this resolvent formula without correction. Other adjacent sign/shift slips warrant a full local proof.
3. §8.3 p178 (PDF190) defines D from polynomial times exp(-x²/2) but claims exp(-ωx²/2)∈D for all ω>0. This is false for ω≠1. Use ω=1 then a unitary dilation, or the ω-dependent Gaussian polynomial core. The oscillator spectrum itself is standard; the printed core argument needs repair.
4. Thm10.8 p230 (PDF242) asserts pure ac positive spectrum/no positive eigenvalues from the preceding broad real radial V∈L∞∞+weighted L² condition, but invokes Lem9.37 requiring stronger asymptotic conditions. General decaying oscillatory radial potentials need not satisfy them. Retain the self-adjoint construction, restrict spectral conclusions to Coulomb/free or a separately proved stronger potential class. Boundary-domain transfer near singular V also needs its own proof.
5. Thm9.10 p192 wording includes simple eigenvalues of any self-adjoint extension at two limit-circle endpoints. Simplicity proof uses separated boundary conditions; regular periodic -d² has multiplicity two. Declare separated conditions explicitly, and distinguish compact resolvent from eigenvalue simplicity.
6. Physical prose p235 claims eventual energy transfer to surroundings/ground-state relaxation. An isolated time-independent unitary model conserves energy; radiation and environment require extra couplings. HVZ p242 describes ionization energy with reversed subtraction. Neither is an empirical report. The source also contains smaller coefficient/sign slips; formulas must be verified rather than transferred verbatim.

These findings concern the retrieved edition only. No claim is made here about corrections in a later edition. The authoritative source supports theorem discovery and conditional reasoning, not automatic correctness of every displayed formula.

## Physical and empirical gaps

The text introduces four axioms in §2.1 pp55–57, which are useful formulations, but does not develop a full account of mixed states/density operators, operational measurement update/instruments/POVMs, preparation procedures, incompatibility tests, decoherence or Bell experiments. Tensor products are mathematical constructions; their physical system-composition meaning and identical-particle symmetrization require separate adopted assumptions. Orbital angular momentum does not supply intrinsic spin or Stern–Gerlach apparatus. It does not establish empirical charge/mass/ℏ values, detector calibrations, photon postulates, relativity, QED, fine/hyperfine structure, radiative transitions or environmental relaxation.

There are no primary experimental reports with apparatus records, event counts, calibration, uncertainty budgets and statistical inference to harvest here. The statement that superposition follows from experiments (p56) is broad motivation with no experimental detail. Double slit/photoelectric/Compton/Stern–Gerlach/Bell evidence needs distinct full experimental sources. Do not label any mathematical wave packet, box, atom or scattering example as a reported experiment. Finite samples drawn under spectral probabilities are uncertain evidence for specified physical and apparatus models; agreement cannot prove this framework, and a single unlikely event is not a deductive contradiction. Imported theorem certainty is conditional on mathematical hypotheses and completed proofs; application to nature inherits physical model scope, approximations and empirical uncertainty.
