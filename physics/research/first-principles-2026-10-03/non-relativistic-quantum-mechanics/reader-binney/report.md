## Adopted formulation: primitives and deductions

The following proposed precise formulation is this report's library recommendation, not a quotation or claim that Binney supplies all its hypotheses.

1. **Probability language first (mathematics).** A probability space (Ω,F,P) has σ-algebra F and countably additive P:F→[0,1], P(Ω)=1. A real observable outcome is a measurable map X:Ω→R. Expectation and variance require integrability and square integrability respectively. Binney §1.1 pp3–4 motivates these quantities only in discrete language. Coherent alternatives must not be represented as simultaneously measured disjoint events with the source's p5 assertion that QM violates probability additivity.
2. **State postulate (physics).** Associate a complex separable Hilbert space H to a specified system. A pure state is a rank-one orthogonal projection |ψ><ψ| with ||ψ||=1, identifying global phases. A general state is a positive trace-class operator ρ:H→H with Trρ=1. Inner product is conjugate-linear in first argument. Binney §1.3 pp7–14 and §6.3 pp121–126 motivate these; his finite-dimensional dual construction must not replace Hilbert completeness/Riesz representation.
3. **Measurement postulate (physics).** A sharp real observable is a self-adjoint operator A:D(A)⊂H→H with projection-valued spectral measure E_A on R. Outcome probability in Borel B is Tr(ρ E_A(B)). For finite-dimensional projective measurements this reduces to normalized squared amplitudes and trace rules; Binney (1.10), p5, (2.9), p18, and (6.67), p123. Ideal selective update for a discrete outcome projection P_j with nonzero probability is ρ↦P_jρP_j/Tr(ρP_j). Degeneracy requires a projection onto an eigenspace, not choosing one eigenvector. General apparatus/POVM/instrument theory needs another source/mathematical treatment; ideal repeatability is restricted model input (§1.4 p15, §6.1.1 pp108–109), not a theorem about every measurement.
4. **Closed-system dynamics postulate (physics).** For a time-independent self-adjoint Hamiltonian H_E:D(H_E)⊂H→H, use U(t)=exp(−itH_E/ℏ). For ψ0∈D(H_E), ψ(t)=U(t)ψ0 solves the strong TDSE; U(t) still acts on all H for general initial states without claiming pointwise differentiability. Binney (2.26), p21; (4.51)–(4.52), p68. Time-dependent generators require additional regularity/common-domain or form hypotheses, not blanket exponentiation.
5. **Composition postulate (physics).** Distinct subsystem space is H_A⊗H_B; local bounded operator A acts as A⊗I. Noninteracting Hamiltonian is H_A⊗I+I⊗H_B, with mathematical domain specified. Tensor structure and Hamiltonian form are separate assumptions. Product-basis completeness follows mathematically once Hilbert tensor product is defined; it is not derived from probability multiplication alone. Binney §6.1 pp105–111 motivates composition but its p106 proof is inadequate.
6. **System-specific kinematics/dynamics (physics).** For a spinless particle on R^d adopt H=L²(R^d,dx), position multiplication, momentum −iℏ∂, and a Hamiltonian justified in a declared nonrelativistic regime. Mass m>0, ℏ>0, charge q, external V/A/Φ, and coupling law are model parameters/assumptions, not deductions from Hilbert/Born axioms. Binney explicitly calls H=p²/(2m)+V an informed guess (§2.3.1 p26), magnetic minimal coupling conjectural (§3.3 p45), and rigid-rotor H conjectural (§7.1.1 p142).
7. **Identical-particle postulate (physics).** Use symmetric/antisymmetric subspace of H_1^⊗N in three spatial dimensions for stipulated boson/fermion species. Nonrelativistic spin-statistics association is adopted, not derived from probability equality (§10.1 pp226–227). Pauli exclusion is then a deduction, pp227–228. Defer quantum circuits/algorithms despite reading §6.2.

Conditional deductions suitable after these assumptions: unitary norm conservation; finite-dimensional spectral probabilities; Schrödinger/Heisenberg equivalence; tensor local independence/no-signalling with specified operations; uncertainty via Cauchy–Schwarz on common operator/form domains; Gaussian free dispersion; oscillator spectrum with completeness proof; continuity equation; symmetry conservation with strong commutation; angular multiplets; reduced states; variational bounds; atomic energies in stipulated Coulomb model; controlled perturbative/adiabatic/WKB statements; scattering amplitudes/cross sections under existence/completeness and asymptotic hypotheses.

## Operator obligations and mathematical adequacy

For R^d, define X_j on D(X_j)={ψ∈L²:x_jψ∈L²}; define P_j via Fourier transform as self-adjoint multiplication by p_j, equivalently weak derivative with ∂_jψ∈L². Schwartz space S(R^d) is a convenient common invariant core for finite polynomial CCR calculations: [X_j,P_k]ψ=iℏδ_jkψ there. Plane waves and point-position kets are distributions, not normalizable states or a uniform probability law on R. Bounded finite matrices can be developed first with fully rigorous proofs.

For bounded real V, H_E=−ℏ²Δ/(2m)+V is self-adjoint on H²(R^d). Other potentials need exact relative-bound/quadratic-form hypotheses and extension results. On an interval an infinite well means a Dirichlet self-adjoint Laplacian with D=H²∩H¹_0, not multiplication by an actual infinite function. Delta wells need an operator specified by continuous ψ, piecewise H² regularity, and derivative jump ψ'(0+)−ψ'(0−)=−2mgψ(0)/ℏ² for attractive strength g>0. Coulomb and magnetic Hamiltonians require their own self-adjointness results. Positive semibounded forms are often the correct variational setting.

Do not promote the following source steps as library proofs:

- Box2.2 p26 proves formal momentum symmetry by integration by parts; it does not prove D(P)=D(P*). Unbounded products/commutators need domains, and commuting on a core does not alone give strongly commuting spectral measures (§2.1 pp20–21).
- Radial momentum −iℏ(∂_r+1/r), p152 (7.66), on L²((0,∞),r²dr), is formally symmetric with boundary restrictions. Under ψ↦rψ it becomes half-line −iℏ∂_r; usual minimal half-line derivative has unequal deficiency indices and no self-adjoint extension. Define radial second-order Hamiltonians separately with boundary conditions; formal squaring does not solve this.
- The tensor completeness claim p106 sums amplitudes over B; marginals require summing probabilities/partial trace. The composition rule remains physical input.
- Degenerate perturbation §9.1.2 p207 correctly suggests diagonalizing PΔP but incorrectly conjectures immediate occupation of its lowest branch. Spectral splitting is deduced; branch population requires preparation/evolution/dissipation hypotheses. No automatic relaxation under unitary dynamics.
- Bell §6.1.4 pp113–115 assumes setting-independent ρ(v), local predetermined ±1/2 responses and perfect anticorrelation. Inequality (6.46) refutes this specified local class when the relevant qualified evidence violates it; it excludes neither all hidden-variable theories nor arbitrary alternatives.
- Adiabatic §11.1 pp245–246 is oscillatory-integral reasoning without quantitative error bound/gap theorem. Berry diagonal connection can be locally gauged away, not globally ignored. WKB §11.6 pp253–254 requires slow variation away from turning points and estimates for dropped terms.
- Wave operators §12.1 p258 are strong limits with existence obligations. A strong limit of unitary operators need not be unitary; common complete scattering ranges are needed for unitary S. AppendixK pp301–302 suggests Cook's criterion but does not prove dense-set integrability: ||Vψ|| need not be finite from ||ψ||=1, and o(1/t) is not necessarily integrable. State ∫||VU0(t)φ||dt<∞ explicitly with dense domain and extension proof.
- Golden rule §9.3.1 pp214–215 and exponential radioactive decay §12.6.2 pp280–282 use continuum, weak-coupling/time-window, smooth-resonance and energy-range approximations. Rates are not exact probabilities for arbitrarily long times.
- AppendixC pp288–289 does not prove general Fourier completeness/inversion; AppendixF pp292–293 cannot identify global group purely from Lie algebra; AppendixJ pp300–301 asserts a matrix nonsingularity without complete proof. Use independent suppliers.

For geometric language specify nonrelativistic spacetime M=R×R³ with time projection and Euclidean spatial slices; an inertial coordinate chart is adopted, with Galilean coordinate changes stated. ## Experimental evidence: what this text does and does not supply

The text is primarily theory, with secondary reports and schematic apparatus. It does not provide primary raw datasets, calibrations, sampling independence proofs, confidence intervals or systematic-error budgets sufficient for fully quantitative experiment items. All cited reports below are leads **not retrieved/read primary sources in this assignment**. Numerical values in the book remain secondary reporting, and contradictory Lamb-shift direction pp192/197 must be resolved before use.

- Double slit §1.2.1 p6 is explicitly imaginary. §2.3.4 p31 supplies ideal geometrical prediction under fixed momentum, coherent paths and far-field approximations. It supplies no actual counts. Define normalized amplitudes a1,a2 on detector region D; predicted bin probabilities integrate |a1+a2|², or the declared incoherent mixture, over actual bins. Counts would be multinomial conditional on iid identically prepared events and fixed N; Poisson models require flux/time assumptions. A missing visually apparent pattern has nonzero finite-sample probability under ordinary coherent models; phase drift, path marking, resolution and background are separate alternatives. Classical waves also interfere. A comparison rejects a stated particle/mixture model, not all classical physics or proves QM.
- SG §7.4.2 pp161–164 describes silver vapor, collimation, nonuniform magnetic field and two-beam splitting; cites Gerlach/Stern, Z.Phys.9,349(1922). Sequential rotated-filter cos²(θ/2) results are ideal model predictions, not a reconstructed original 1922 multi-filter dataset. Equal populations require preparation symmetry, not universal equality. Add actual beam geometry, field gradient, selection/calibration, counts/uncertainty from primary reports.
- Aharonov–Bohm §§3.3.3 pp51–52 gives ideal-solenoid phase shift and cites Chambers, PRL5,3(1960). Field leakage, geometry/flux calibration and observed fringe shift uncertainty missing here; retain prediction as thought experiment until actual experimental report read.
- Bell §6.1.4 pp111–115 cites Freedman/Clauser PRL28,938(1972), Tittel et al.PRL81,3563(1998), but no raw coincidences, settings schedule, efficiencies, finite-trial test or loophole assumptions. Actual experimental record must specify locality/setting independence/detection/selection assumptions and sampling method. Do not turn secondary 'vindication' prose into proof or certainty.
- Fig5.19 p94 cites Bollinger et al.Phys.Rev.105,661(1957) uranium neutron cross sections. Fig12.2 p270 cites Kreisler et al.PRL16,1217(1966) neutron–proton scattering. Fig12.4 p273 cites Williams,J.Phys.B8 no13(1975) elastic electron–hydrogen at 4.9,30,680eV. The model/measurement distinction is visible; apparatus, counts and uncertainty are insufficient. Report qualitative comparison only pending primary retrieval. To estimate cross sections, specify incident fluence, target number, acceptance/efficiency/background and a count likelihood; the book's ratio definitions pp267–268 are not a completed statistical analysis.
