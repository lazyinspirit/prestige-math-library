## Adopted framework and exact formulation obligations

These courses adopt nonrelativistic Hilbert-space quantum mechanics; physical postulates are not deductions from vector-space axioms. For the foundational library, a safe dependency order begins with finite-dimensional systems before unbounded analytic models.

- **Pure state.** Fix a nonzero complex Hilbert space H with inner product conjugate-linear first, linear second. A pure state is a ray `[ψ]={zψ:z∈C\{0}}` for nonzero ψ∈H; use a representative with ‖ψ‖=1. The zero vector is not a state. MIT Ch1 pp1–4 and Ch2 pp7–8; Skinner file02 §2.4 PDF pp15–17. H=C² for isolated spin degrees of freedom; H=L²(R³,d³x) for a spinless particle; H=L²(R³) completed-tensor C² for a spin-1/2 particle. Choice of H and physical interpretation are modeling assumptions, not derived solely from mathematics.
- **Mixed state.** A density operator ρ is positive, trace-class, self-adjoint on H with trρ=1. In finite dimension the trace-class condition is automatic. Pure means rank one, equivalently ρ²=ρ; mixed means otherwise. An ensemble `ρ=Σ pα|ψα><ψα|` is a representation, generally nonunique. Skinner file10 §§10.1–10.1.1 PDF pp1–4. Counting nonzero ensemble weights does not correctly distinguish pure/mixed if several labels represent the same ray.
- **Observable and measurement.** In finite dimension, use a self-adjoint map A:H→H with spectral projections Pa and real outcomes a. Probability p(a)=⟨ψ,Paψ⟩ or trρPa. For infinite dimension use a self-adjoint densely defined A:D(A)⊂H→H and its projection-valued spectral measure EA on Borel R; probability of a Borel set B is ⟨ψ,EA(B)ψ⟩. These are adopted measurement laws. Exact continuous point outcomes are not normalizable eigenvectors. Ideal selective projective measurement with nonzero p(a) updates ψ to Paψ/√p(a) (or ρ to PaρPa/p(a)); nonselective update sums over outcomes. State this as an additional ideal-instrument assumption, not “apply A to measure A.” MIT Ch3 p20 and Ch8 pp11–12 give projections/partial measurement; Skinner file02 p17 explicitly separates Born/observable postulates from Copenhagen collapse and leaves measurement mechanism unresolved.
- **Dynamics.** For an isolated closed system, adopt unitary propagation U(t,s), U(s,s)=I and composition U(t,r)U(r,s)=U(t,s). For time-independent self-adjoint H, U(t,s)=exp(−i(t−s)H/ℏ) via spectral calculus. Strong continuous evolution exists for every ψ∈H; differentiable Schrödinger equation iℏψ̇=Hψ requires ψ∈D(H). Time-dependent H needs a separately established propagator theorem and common-domain/regularity hypotheses. MIT Ch6 §§2–3 pp10–25 derives differentiable generator/Heisenberg formulas formally; Skinner file04 §§4.4–4.5 PDF pp15–20. Unitarity alone does not supply differentiability, an energy identification, or a specific kinetic/potential Hamiltonian.
- **Composition and statistics.** Composite distinguishable subsystems use the completed Hilbert tensor product; pure entangled means not a simple product relative to the specified splitting. Partial trace is uniquely defined by tr[(A⊗I)ρAB]=tr[AρA] for bounded A. MIT Ch8 pp1–9; Skinner file02 §§2.3–2.3.1 pp11–15 and file10 §§10.3–10.5 pp6–10. Identical N-particle bosonic/fermionic models restrict H^{⊗N} to Sym^N H or exterior-power sector using the permutation representation. Adopting only ± exchange sectors is an additional symmetrization premise, not a consequence of bare indistinguishability. Spin–statistics is explicitly a QFT result outside this nonrelativistic source; adopt or record external, never pretend proved here (Skinner file07 pp1–3).

## Quantities, units, and model status

Use one explicit unit convention per item. The sources switch between SI and cgs, and some Skinner equations mix conventions, so importing their symbols unchanged is unsafe.

| Quantity | Mathematical type / role in selected formulation | Dimensions / units |
|---|---|---|
| t | real external evolution parameter, not time observable in these courses | s |
| x | coordinate in chosen Euclidean configuration space; reference frame needs an explicit Newtonian spatial chart and time coordinate | m |
| ψ(x) | L² representative; position probability density | in d dimensions ψ has units m^(−d/2), ‖ψ‖ dimensionless |
| X, P | position multiplication and translation generator; on L²(R), Xψ=xψ with D(X)={ψ:xψ∈L²}, P=−iℏ weak derivative with domain H¹(R) | m; kg m s^(−1) |
| H, V | self-adjoint energy/dynamics operator and potential multiplication/form; kinetic-plus-potential form adopted for massive model | J, or eV with conversion specified |
| ℏ | primitive scale linking generators to physical momentum/angular momentum/energy; h=2πℏ | J s |
| m, q, magnetic g/γ | model parameters, not derived by Schrödinger framework | kg; C; g dimensionless; γ s^(−1) T^(−1) |
| L, S, J | operator triplets with rotation behavior; L=X×P, S additional intrinsic representation, J=L+S | J s; quantum labels j,m,ℓ dimensionless |
| μ, electric dipole D | operator triplets or model constants defined through adopted coupling | J/T and C m in SI |
| ρ, Pa, transition probability | trace-class state / projection / scalar probability | dimensionless |
| rate Γ, frequency ω | derived from evolution/transition model, angular convention explicit | s^(−1); ω radians/s with radians dimensionless |

For [X,P]=iℏI use a common invariant dense domain such as Schwartz space; it is not an operator identity defined on every L² state, and cannot hold for finite matrices (trace contradiction). For momentum on a bounded interval the boundary condition is essential; integration by parts alone establishes symmetry, not self-adjointness. Harmonic/Coulomb/delta models each need a specified self-adjoint realization or quadratic-form construction. The source's half-line radial momentum is a formal symmetric differential expression; do not assert it is a self-adjoint observable without a domain theorem.

MIT Ch6 §5.3 pp48–50 provides an especially useful **photon distinction**: one EM cavity mode has q with dimensions time×√energy and p with √energy, [q,p]=iℏ, and H=(p²+ω²q²)/2. These are field mode amplitudes, not a massive photon's position and mechanical momentum. Photon number n has energy increment ℏω; field expectation in a number state is zero, coherent states produce classical standing-wave expectation. Field quantization is an extra coupling/model postulate. Skinner file05 p15 footnote explicitly says a massless photon has two polarization/helicity states and requires Lorentz-group treatment omitted from the course. No photon localization foundation is supplied.

## Deductions and mathematical prerequisites

Finite self-adjoint spectral decomposition and Born projection make spin directional probabilities, finite uncertainty and exact C² dynamics suitable early conditional physical theorems. MIT Ch3/5 finite-dimensional arguments can also motivate purely mathematical suppliers, with no physics dependency. For uncertainty, state ψ∈D(A)∩D(B); use the quadratic-form version from ⟨Aψ,Bψ⟩ unless ψ∈D(AB)∩D(BA) justifies literal commutator. Variance is statistical spread in preparation outcomes, not automatically apparatus measurement error or disturbance. The time scale τQ=ΔQ/|d⟨Q⟩/dt| needs nonzero denominator; no universal time operator follows.

Needed mathematics: complex linear algebra and duals; complete inner products and Cauchy–Schwarz; orthogonal projections and finite spectral theorem; tensor-product construction/completion and permutation representations; probability measures, distributions of independent trials, expectation/variance, multinomial/binomial analysis and error propagation; L²/Lebesgue equivalence classes, weak derivatives/Sobolev spaces, distributions/Schwartz space and Fourier–Plancherel; densely defined adjoints/self-adjointness, unbounded spectral calculus, Stone's theorem and unitary propagators; semibounded forms/variational principle; ODE uniqueness and Sturm oscillation/completeness under exact endpoint hypotheses; rotations/SU(2), Lie groups/algebras and representation integration; spherical harmonics/Bessel; analytic operator perturbation with spectral gaps, asymptotic expansions; trace-class operators, partial trace and finite entropy inequalities.

Unsupported first-principles steps include choice of Hilbert space, Born rule, ideal measurement instrument, unitary closed-system law, canonical representation/coupling identification, spin values and magnetic moment parameters, symmetrization and spin–statistics, minimal EM coupling and field quantization, Dirac/Thomas/Darwin corrections, and Boltzmann/Planck equilibrium laws. These need adopted postulates, accurately sourced empirical inputs or explicitly external recorded results. Classical analogy cannot serve as their deduction.

## Source defects that must not become suppliers

MIT: completeness/discrete-spectrum expansion, nodal theorem, infinite sums/delta manipulations and oscillator existence are partly assumed/deferred. Ch1 normalizable/decay equivalence and division/logarithms at nodes require repairs. Ch5 finite normal spectral theorem is not the infinite-dimensional theorem. Ch10 hyperfine and spin–orbit sections are empty; hidden SO(4) construction does not by itself establish all levels occur or multiplicity one. Sources call formal manipulations proofs without enough domain/convergence control for library acceptance.

Skinner: full linked course has draft omissions (§5.2 editorial text; §§6.2.1–6.2.2 Wigner–Eckart/dipole sections empty; §7.1.3 stellar subsection empty; oscillator nondegeneracy footnote unfinished; Thomas-precession instruction unresolved). Actual serious errors:

- File06 PDF p16/printed p93 Eq6.72 counts all ℓ from 0 to N, while oscillator level N has ℓ=N,N−2,…; the printed chain of equalities is arithmetically false. Visually verified.
- File08 PDF p16/printed p122 Eq8.63 promotes an eigenstate of PΔP to an eigenstate of Δ. A nonzero (I−P)ΔP invalidates this; the compressed matrix gives first-order splittings, not exact full spectrum. “Jump to an eigenstate” also lacks an open-system relaxation or measurement law. Visually verified.
- File10 PDF p17/printed p160 reverses variance inequality (correct ⟨C⟩²≤⟨C²⟩) and bounds individual commutator expectations where a norm bound on their product is needed. Repair via bounded self-adjoint involutions, C²=4I−[A1,A2][B1,B2], ‖[Ai,Aj]‖≤2, hence ‖C‖≤2√2. Visually verified.
- Static Stark calculation sums only discrete bound states; full second-order reduced resolvent includes continuum. Normalization alone does not set complex first-order overlap to zero without phase gauge. Entropy maximum I/d requires finite d; infinite-dimensional entropy/partition function require trace finiteness. Thermal/EM unit formulas need independent dimensional checks.

These are research findings, not edits or comprehensive independent source audits. Ledger locators identify additional omitted proofs/assumptions. Reliable sources are still fallible.

## Empirical and statistical claims

Both texts mainly provide theory and secondary historical illustrations. MIT silver SG Ch2 pp1–7, ammonia/maser/NMR/MRI Ch7, Bell Ch8 pp16–19, and Skinner SG file05 pp8–10, hydrogen/helium numerical comparisons file08, pion parity file07, photoionization file09 and Bell file10 do not supply primary datasets/calibration/sampling models. Sequential SG, ideal EPR, teleportation and toy dephasing are conditional thought experiments, not observed experiments.

Skinner file10 PDF pp17–18/printed pp160–161 cites calcium-cascade polarization experiments and quotes |⟨C⟩|=2.697±0.0515. This is a textbook-reported value only: no counts, uncertainty type, sampling model, fair-sampling/detection assumptions or original-paper reading was established here. Do not assign it an invented significance/p-value or enter it as a verified experimental item. Its general “any hidden variables” claims are overbroad: CHSH bound assumes a common setting-independent probability space and local response functions (or conditional factorization); nonlocal models are not ruled out by that deduction. Quantum marginals/no signaling require a separate conditional proof, not vague collapse ontology.

A future double-slit item needs explicit preparation, apparatus, coherence/phase, detection bins and sampling law. Under coherent amplitudes p(x)∝|a1+a2|² versus specified incoherent p(x)∝|a1|²+|a2|², finite independent detections are random; a chosen visible-fringe criterion can fail with nonzero probability. Neither source here supplies a primary double-slit experimental report. Classical waves interfere; agreement tests specified competing models plus auxiliary assumptions and does not prove QM. Missing visible fringes do not automatically falsify it; point-density zero is not finite-bin event exclusion. A p-value is not probability the theory is false. No empirical counts or precision invented in this report.
