# Reported electron double-slit experiment: source-backed research account

**Classification:** reported experiment candidate, not a theorem, simulation or proof of quantum mechanics. Primary source actually retrieved/read: Roger Bach, Damian Pope, Sy-Hwang Liou and Herman Batelaan, *Controlled double-slit electron diffraction*, arXiv:1210.6243v1, dated 22 October 2012, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.6243. This receipt describes that exact preprint, not an invented claim of reading its later journal version. Eight PDF pages read in full; retained extraction and retrieval metadata are adjacent. PDF pages 6 and 8 were visually inspected to verify captions/figure counts.

## Setup and operational quantities

The report's §2 p3 describes a 600 eV electron beam from a thermionic tungsten filament with electrostatic lenses. A collimation aperture 2 μm wide and 10 μm high is placed 16.5 cm from the source; the double-slit lies 30.5 cm beyond the collimation aperture. The two slits are 50 nm wide, with 280 nm center separation, 4 μm height and 150 nm supports midway up. Three 100 nm silicon-nitride membranes were milled with a 30 keV Ga⁺ focused-ion-beam system and coated with approximately 2 nm gold.

A movable mask, operated by a piezoelectric actuator, is placed 230 μm downstream of the double-slit. The body gives mask aperture 5 μm × 10 μm; Figure1 caption p6 gives 5 μm × 20 μm. This is a real source inconsistency, visually checked, and the mask height is therefore not settled by this receipt. Figure1 supports the downstream placement used in the body; the abstract's “in front” wording is not treated as a more precise apparatus specification.

An electrostatic quadrupole magnifies the detector pattern. Detection uses a two-dimensional microchannel plate/phosphor screen imaged by a CCD. A recorded event location is an output of the image-processing “blob” localization procedure, not an exact position eigenvalue. The paper states that blob size represents the localization error but supplies no numerical uncertainty budget for that error.

## Procedure and reported observations

For slit-control measurements the authors sum image-frame intensities and normalize the accumulated image. Sweeping the mask yields source-labeled P1, P12 and P2 patterns (Figures1–2): one slit predominantly transmitting, both transmitting, and the other predominantly transmitting. The paper reports weak double-slit contributions in a first diffraction order even in the nominal single-slit configurations, attributed to finite mask-to-slit separation. Thus these are not ideal exact projective which-path preparations.

For event buildup the mask is centered, source intensity is reduced to a reported detection rate about 1 Hz, and the lens magnifies the central five orders. Figure3 caption p8 lists accumulated localized-event counts 2, 7, 209, 1004 and 6235; these numbers were visually verified. The authors report that the accumulated final pattern took about 2 hours. The figure shows an increasingly discernible modulation in the accumulated events. It does not show a fringe distribution deposited by one detection. Approximate 1 Hz, 2 hours and 6235 accumulated events are not converted into an exact trial count, efficiency or rate calibration.

The paper estimates an average electron separation 2.3 × 10⁶ m and apparatus length 1 m. This indicates a low average occupancy under the stated beam model; an average spacing alone is not a deductive guarantee that every realization has at most one electron. No arrival-process law or bound on residual two-electron occupancy is supplied, so this receipt does not elevate the paper's categorical “ensures” wording to a theorem.

The cited computer-vision localization papers and supplementary movies were not retrieved in this audit; the present claim is limited to the report and its figures. ## Qualified interpretation and dependency disposition

The reported slit-dependent patterns and localized-event buildup support a coherent electron-wave-amplitude model under the apparatus/preparation assumptions. A controlled alternative comparison would need a specified incoherent/path law and account for mask effects, losses and background. Agreement does not deduce P1–P7, prove the entire framework or exclude every classical/hidden-variable theory. Classical waves also interfere.

Operational mathematics: a position record is a measured random element on a detector plane with finite resolution; image normalization is division by a positive total; frequencies/count likelihoods require a declared sampling law. These mathematical methods justify formulations/analysis, not the observed counts.
