**P1 — State and composition postulate.** Assign each system a nonzero separable complex Hilbert space H. Adopt all unit rays in H as pure preparations within a declared sector: `[ψ]={zψ:z∈C\{0}}`, represented by ||ψ||=1. General preparations are positive self-adjoint trace-class operators ρ:H→H with tr ρ=1. The rank-one pure operator is `Rψ u=(u,ψ)ψ`. Statistical mixing with probabilities w_j gives sum_j w_jρ_j in trace norm. Independent distinguishable subsystems have H_A⊗̂H_B, the completed Hilbert tensor product, and local quantities act as A⊗I and I⊗B. Product preparations are a special case; general composite states need not factor. Superselection restrictions, if imposed, are extra model assumptions, not consequences of vector-space linearity. For identical species choose symmetric or antisymmetric tensor sectors as P6 below. The existence of a Hilbert tensor product is mathematics; its assignment to a composite physical system is a physical assumption.

**P2 — Observable and probability postulate.** An ideal sharp real-valued observable is a self-adjoint densely defined operator A:D(A)⊂H→H, with a unit declared for its real spectral coordinate a. Its spectral PVM E_A maps Borel subsets of R to bounded orthogonal projections. The probability of outcome in B is μρ^A(B)=tr(ρE_A(B)); for a unit pure ψ it is ||E_A(B)ψ||². This is a probability measure, not necessarily a density or a discrete sum. Continuous spectrum does not furnish a normalizable eigenvector at every a. An outcome belongs to the spectral support, and “an exact continuous value” is not a finite-resolution detector bin. To describe unsharp finite outcomes additionally allow a finite family of bounded effects 0≤F_a≤I with sum F_a=I and p_a=tr ρF_a. This operational extension requires an apparatus instrument, not just an observable symbol.

**P3 — Ideal conditional measurement postulate.** For a declared ideal repeatable sharp finite-resolution or discrete projective measurement with a finite or countable family of mutually orthogonal bounded projections P_a whose strong sum is I, outcome a with p_a=tr(ρP_a)>0 updates ρ to P_aρP_a/p_a; discarding the outcome gives the trace-norm convergent sum_a P_aρP_a. This is the adopted Lüders instrument, preserving coherence inside a degenerate eigenspace. It is not derived from the Born probabilities or asserted for every laboratory detector. General instruments specify their own state update; photon absorption in particular need not leave an outgoing one-photon polarization state. Measurement of an exact continuous eigenvalue is not represented by normalizing a delta distribution. Unitary system–apparatus models explain conditional correlations but do not, alone, establish a unique recorded outcome or solve the interpretation problem.

**P4 — Dynamics postulate.** An isolated autonomous system has a specified self-adjoint energy operator H with units J. Its evolution is U(t)=exp(−itH/ℏ), t in seconds, and ρ(t)=U(t)ρ(0)U(t)*. Every Hilbert vector has continuous evolution; `iℏ dψ/dt=Hψ` is a norm-differentiable equation only for ψ(0)∈D(H). A norm-continuous finite-dimensional Hermitian H(t) uses a unitary two-time propagator U(t,s), not the exponential of an integral unless a commutation theorem applies. More general unbounded time-dependent H(t) needs a separate existence/domain theorem. Open-system maps are extensions obtained from an explicitly chosen environment/model, not a replacement secretly built into P4.

**P5 — Nonrelativistic kinematics and massive-particle model postulate.** Adopt Newtonian spacetime M=R×R³ with absolute time t and Euclidean spatial metric on each time slice. An inertial frame means a global coordinate chart obtained from a spatial origin, a time origin, and an orthonormal spatial triad; it is not an observer congruence. Two such charts are related by t'=t+t₀ and x'=Rx−vt+a, where R is a real 3×3 matrix with RᵀR=I and detR=1, t₀ is in seconds, a is in metres, and v is in metres per second; these are the adopted Galilei frame transformations, not Lorentzian geometry or a derived limit of special relativity. A classical preparation/apparatus trajectory is a C¹ curve γ:I→M, γ(t)=(t,x(t)); a quantum particle does not thereby acquire a definite worldline between measurements. For one spin-s particle choose H=L²(R³,d³x)⊗C^(2s+1), with the spin factor carrying the separately assigned representation in P6. The position operators Q_j multiply by x_j, with maximal domain {ψ:x_jψ∈L²}; momentum P_j=−iℏ∂_j has maximal weak-derivative domain {ψ:∂_jψ∈L²}. Their self-adjointness follows from multiplication and Fourier conjugation. They obey [Q_j,P_k]=iℏδ_jk on the Schwartz core, not as unrestricted products on all H. The baseline scalar Hamiltonian is H=−ℏ²Δ/(2m)+V(Q), with m>0 and real V∈L∞(R³); its exact domain is H²(R³), and M6 proves self-adjointness. Coulomb, oscillator, boundary, and other singular/unbounded models are separate realizations whose domains and self-adjointness are established by their own arguments before use. This model choice does not follow merely from P1–P4.

**P6 — Identical-particle and spin assignments.** Particle labels in an n-fold tensor product are mathematical factor labels. For a specified bosonic species choose Sym^n H; for a specified fermionic species choose Λ^n H. Permutation invariance and the resulting exclusion statement are then conditional results. Spin is a specified unitary SU(2) representation of dimension 2s+1, s∈{0,1/2,1,...}; S_i=ℏJ_i are self-adjoint finite matrices with [S_i,S_j]=iℏε_ijk S_k. The species' mass, charge, spin, statistics and magnetic coupling constants are empirical model parameters/assignments. The relativistic spin–statistics theorem is not a theorem of this nonrelativistic postulate set.

**P7 — Preparation and apparatus assumptions.** A comparison with data requires a declared preparation map, dynamical isolation approximation, detection efficiency/resolution/background model, and sampling assumptions. IID repetitions are an additional statistical model, not a quantum postulate guaranteed by the apparatus. The claim “this experiment realizes ρ,H,E” is a physically tested identification with uncertainty. It does not follow from the mathematical existence of those objects.

**R1–R3 — Radiation extensions, introduced only on the radiation pages.** A fixed propagation-mode one-photon polarization model has a two-dimensional transverse internal space; it is a restricted manifestation of the abstract Born/state framework, not the massive spatial Hamiltonian with m=0. To derive photon number/energy statistics and spontaneous emission, add quantized transverse radiation modes, bosonic Fock space, and a specified light–matter Hamiltonian and detector model. In a chosen inertial laboratory frame, adopt for free vacuum modes k∈R³\{0}, ω=c|k|, photon energy E=ℏω (J), and momentum p=ℏk (kg m s⁻¹), with k measured in m⁻¹ and ω in s⁻¹. These are separate quantum-radiation model assignments; they are neither consequences of the classical Maxwell or special-relativity framework nor a mass-zero limit, and no Lorentz transformation law for the quantum mode labels is claimed here. This nonrelativistic matter-plus-radiation extension does not claim Galilean covariance of the radiation sector. The optional finite-level dipole coupling and its long-wavelength regime are specified immediately below for the absorption/stimulated-emission branch. A classical prescribed electromagnetic drive yields stimulated transition calculations but does not by itself derive vacuum spontaneous emission. Relativistic matter, antiparticles, pair creation, covariant local field dynamics and full QED are deferred to a named field-theory destination; they are not disguised as solved core prerequisites.

These additional model postulates are adopted only on the pages that name them; they do not follow from P1–P7, classical Maxwell theory, or a relativistic Lorentz-force postulate.

**P5a — Prescribed external electromagnetic coupling.** For a spinless particle in a named Newtonian inertial frame, with prescribed static real potentials $\mathbf A_{\rm ext}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb R^3;\mathbb R^3)$ and $\Phi_{\rm ext}\in L^\infty(\mathbb R^3;\mathbb R)$, charge $q$ in coulombs, and mass $m>0$, adopt the minimal-coupling Hamiltonian
$$H_{\rm ext}=\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{j=1}^3(P_j-qA_{{\rm ext},j}(Q))^2+q\Phi_{\rm ext}(Q),\qquad D(H_{\rm ext})=H^2(\mathbb R^3).$$
Here $W^{1,\infty}$ means the components and weak first spatial derivatives are essentially bounded; $L^\infty$ functions are understood up to almost-everywhere equality. The vector potential $\mathbf A_{\rm ext}$ has units T m (equivalently V s m$^{-1}$), $\Phi_{\rm ext}$ has units V, $q\mathbf A_{\rm ext}$ has momentum units, and $q\Phi_{\rm ext}$ has energy units. Adopt the field definitions $\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A_{\rm ext}$ and, for time-dependent gauges, $\mathbf E=-\nabla\Phi_{\rm ext}-\partial_t\mathbf A_{\rm ext}$, in T and V m$^{-1}$ respectively; these definitions assert no Maxwell equation or source model. For a real gauge function $\chi$ in the regularity class of M27, adopt $\mathbf A'_{\rm ext}=\mathbf A_{\rm ext}+\nabla\chi$, $\Phi'_{\rm ext}=\Phi_{\rm ext}-\partial_t\chi$ and $\psi'=e^{iq\chi/\hbar}\psi$; $\chi$ has units J s C$^{-1}$, so the phase is dimensionless. M27 proves self-adjointness on the displayed domain and the stated gauge covariance for its bounded-potential hypotheses. A time-dependent family is used only with an explicit common-domain evolution hypothesis such as M43.

**P6a — Effective magnetic-moment coupling.** For a selected finite-spin model, separately assign a real gyromagnetic parameter $\gamma_s$ in s$^{-1}$ T$^{-1}$ and set $\boldsymbol\mu=\gamma_s\mathbf S$ (units J T$^{-1}$); adopt $H_Z=-\boldsymbol\mu\cdot\mathbf B(Q)$ only for the specified bounded real field profile. This is an empirical effective interaction, not a consequence of the spin representation or the spinless minimal-coupling postulate. M27’s bounded matrix-potential argument preserves the stated $H^2$ domain; M50 proves the more specialized smooth bounded $B_z(Q_x)S_z$ force calculation used for the Stern–Gerlach model.

**R3a — Finite-level electric-dipole coupling.** On $L^2(\mathbb R^{3N},d^{3N}x)$ define the uncompressed $D_j$ as multiplication by $\sum_{n=1}^Nq_n(x_{n,j}-x_{0,j})$ on its maximal $L^2$ domain, where each $q_n$ is in coulombs and $x_0$ is in metres; it has units C m. Choose a finite-dimensional atomic subspace contained in the common domains of the $D_j$ and compress each $D_j$ to a Hermitian matrix. In the adopted long-wavelength approximation, where a mode's phase variation across system size $L$ obeys $|k|L\ll1$, use $H_{\rm int}(t)=-\mathbf D\cdot\mathbf E_{\rm ext}(t)$ with prescribed continuous $\mathbf E_{\rm ext}$ in V m$^{-1}$. The interaction has units J and is bounded on the chosen finite-dimensional subspace, so M14 supplies its finite-time evolution and error bounds. A uniform static electric field has zero spatial phase gradient and uses the same finite-level dipole Hamiltonian in the Stark examples; no propagating $k=0$ radiation mode is asserted. The field approximation and finite-level truncation are model assumptions; neither supplies spontaneous emission without the separate quantized-mode/environment assumptions in R1–R3.

The postulate count depends on how assumptions are packaged. Tong begins with wavefunction/Born/superposition/Schrödinger assumptions; Binney begins with amplitudes; MIT 8.05 begins from previous wave mechanics and develops operator/composition machinery; Teschl formalizes states/observables/dynamics through spectral theory. Our P1–P7 package deliberately distinguishes mathematical constructions from their physical assignments and exposes the extra Lüders and particle-Hamiltonian assumptions. P5a, P6a and R3a are further model assumptions only on their named pages. Density/POVM language is an operational extension, not a claim that all four sources use the same enumeration.

## Fundamental quantities, definitions and units

Use SI throughout and keep ℏ explicit. Primitive model quantities include t∈R (s), x∈R³ (m), assigned H and measurement/preparation structures, ℏ=h/(2π)>0 (J s), species mass m>0 (kg), charge q∈R (C), spin label s (dimensionless), and a specified scalar potential V:R³×I→R (J) or explicitly defined external couplings. In the current SI, h=6.62607015×10⁻³⁴ J s, c=299792458 m s⁻¹, and the elementary charge e=1.602176634×10⁻¹⁹ C are exact defining constants; this convention does not impose charge quantization or establish the model's physical applicability. m, q and g factors are empirical model parameters, not derived by the Hilbert formalism. c enters the radiation extension and validity comparisons, not Newtonian kinematics. For the optional electromagnetic model, μ₀ is measured in current SI and ε₀=(μ₀c²)⁻¹; no exact numerical value for μ₀ is asserted.

Derived quantities and their types: ψ∈L²(R^d;C^r) is an a.e. equivalence class with position representation units m^(−d/2), ||ψ||² dimensionless; abstract normalized kets and density operators are dimensionless. Probability μ(B) is dimensionless and a position density ψ*ψ has units m^(−d). A momentum wavefunction normalized against d^dp has units (kg m s⁻¹)^(−d/2). Amplitudes for finite outcomes are complex dimensionless scalars; amplitudes depending on detector coordinate have the square-root density units of their specified measure. Energy eigenvalues E and H have J; momentum P has kg m s⁻¹; Q has m; L=Q×P and S have J s. Velocity P/m has m s⁻¹ in the free spinless model; probability current j has m^(1−d) s⁻¹, not charge current. Force −∇V has N in a differentiable external-potential model. For N charged particles, the uncompressed electric dipole component D_j is multiplication by ∑_{n=1}^N q_n(x_{n,j}−x_{0,j}) on its maximal L² domain, with unit C m; its finite-level compression is a Hermitian matrix. A magnetic moment has unit J T⁻¹, and γ_s in μ=γ_s S has unit s⁻¹ T⁻¹. Mean, variance and standard deviation have respectively unit u, u² and u of the outcome; a mean exists only with its required moment integrability. Density-matrix eigenvalues, purity tr ρ² and entropy −tr ρ log ρ are dimensionless; physical entropy includes a separately assigned k_B in J K⁻¹. Temperature T (K) and canonical equilibrium ρ∝exp(−H/k_BT) are added ensemble assumptions, not derived from unitary mechanics. Cross section has m² and dσ/dΩ m² sr⁻¹; frequency ν has Hz and angular frequency ω rad s⁻¹ (radian dimensionless), with ω=2πν. State phase is dimensionless and global phase is redundant; relative phase affects probabilities for specified measurement.

**Frame transformations in the massive-particle model.** These are passive changes between the Newtonian inertial charts just defined. First take aligned time origins. Under a spatial rotation/translation x'=Rx+a, t'=t, the scalar wavefunction representative is ψ'(x',t)=ψ(Rᵀ(x'−a),t); a spin-s wavefunction additionally transforms through the chosen SU(2) lift of R, with the convention that its spin components transform as a vector. The two lifts differ by the central phase on a fixed irreducible spin sector. For a pure boost x'=x−vt, the free or real bounded scalar-potential model uses the unitary phase map proved in M50: ψ'(x',t)=exp[−i(mv·x'+m|v|²t/2)/ℏ]ψ(x'+vt,t). At equal times, Q'=Q−vtI and P'=P−mvI; for the composed rotation/translation/boost, Q'=RQ−vtI+aI and P'=RP−mvI. For a pure boost the free Hamiltonian is H'=H−v·P+(m|v|²/2)I. A scalar external potential is pulled back as V'(x',t)=V(Rᵀ(x'−a+vt)); its transformed evolution uses the same phase map after applying the spatial unitary. A separate shift of time origin t'=t+t₀ is handled by evaluating the same evolution at t=t'−t₀. The Born density and probability law transform by the unit-Jacobian coordinate map; in the free/real-scalar-potential model the probability current transforms as j'=Rj−v|ψ|². Spin components transform by the chosen representation convention, and m, q, s and ℏ are invariant scalars in this model. M50 proves boost covariance only on its stated free/real-bounded-potential domain; other potential or boundary realizations need their own covariance proof. The external-field Hamiltonian is defined in its named inertial frame: its gauge transformations are as specified above, but no general Galilean transformation law for $\mathbf A_{\rm ext},\Phi_{\rm ext}$ is adopted or claimed.

Every planned technical concept below uses this dictionary, a local proof, or an exact currently published canonical supplier. Further objects: D(A) is a dense linear subspace, the adjoint has its actual domain, a common core is graph-norm dense, spectrum is the complement of bounded everywhere-invertible resolvent, projection is P²=P=P*, and spectral compatibility means commuting PVMs, not merely a formal zero commutator. A Hamiltonian quadratic form q_H has a declared form domain D(|H|^(1/2)); use it for variational claims rather than assume every trial vector lies in D(H). A wave packet is a normalized L² state with specified localization/moments, not a normalizable plane wave. A stationary state is an energy eigenray; a general superposition of energy eigenvectors has time-dependent relative phase. Degeneracy means dimension of an eigenspace. A bound state is a normalizable eigenstate in the selected spectral regime; an asymptotic scattering state requires defined wave operators. Operators representing a conserved quantity commute in the appropriate spectral/dynamical sense, with domain conditions. Orbital rotations act by `(U(R)ψ)(x)=ψ(R⁻¹x)`; spin rotations act by their declared SU(2) representation; translations act by ψ(x−a), generated by P. Gauge transformations of external potentials and ψ require an explicit coupling theorem. A WKB expression is an asymptotic ansatz with an error regime, not an exact state construction.

### M30 — Quadratic free-packet decay

Let $m,\hbar>0$, $\alpha=\hbar/(2m)$, and $a\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^3)$. In the $e^{ik\cdot x}$ Fourier convention, the free packet with momentum-wave-number amplitude $a$ is $\psi_t(x)=\int_{\mathbb R^3}e^{i(k\cdot x-\alpha t|k|^2)}a(k)\,dk$; a fixed unitary Fourier normalization can be absorbed into $a$, and no normalization is needed for this decay estimate. Its phase is dimensionless for $k$ in m$^{-1}$, $x$ in m, and $\alpha$ in m$^2$s$^{-1}$. At $x=tv$ set $I_t(v)=\psi_t(tv)=\int e^{it(k\cdot v-\alpha|k|^2)}a(k)\,dk$, where $v$ is a velocity. Put $\widehat a(\xi)=\int e^{-ik\cdot\xi}a(k)\,dk$. Repeated integration by parts in $k$ shows $|\widehat a(\xi)|\le C(1+|\xi|)^{-4}$, so $\widehat a\in L^1$. Fourier inversion here can be proved directly: for $\epsilon>0$, Fubini and the real Gaussian integral identify $(2\pi)^{-3}\int e^{ik\cdot\xi}e^{-\epsilon|\xi|^2}\widehat a(\xi)\,d\xi$ with convolution of $a$ and $(4\pi\epsilon)^{-3/2}e^{-|k|^2/(4\epsilon)}$. This Gaussian is a unit-mass approximate identity, so its convolution tends to $a(k)$; dominated convergence in $\xi$ gives $a(k)=(2\pi)^{-3}\int e^{ik\cdot\xi}\widehat a(\xi)\,d\xi$. Thus no unpublished Fourier-inversion result is used.

Complete the phase square as $k\cdot v-\alpha|k|^2=|v|^2/(4\alpha)-\alpha|k-v/(2\alpha)|^2$ and substitute this inversion formula in $I_t(v)$. After shifting $q=k-v/(2\alpha)$, the remaining factors $e^{it|v|^2/(4\alpha)}$ and $e^{iv\cdot\xi/(2\alpha)}$ have modulus one. Insert a factor $e^{-\epsilon|q|^2}$ before interchanging the $q$ and $\xi$ integrals. The complex Gaussian identity, already proved locally in M28 from the published real Gaussian transform and the identity theorem, gives
$$\int e^{-(\epsilon+i\alpha t)|q|^2+iq\cdot\xi}\,dq=\left(\frac{\pi}{\epsilon+i\alpha t}\right)^{3/2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{|\xi|^2}{4(\epsilon+i\alpha t)}\right).$$
Its modulus is at most $\pi^{3/2}(\alpha|t|)^{-3/2}$, uniformly in $\epsilon>0$ and $\xi$. Dominated convergence against $|\widehat a|$ as $\epsilon\downarrow0$ therefore yields
$$|I_t(v)|\le (2\pi)^{-3}\pi^{3/2}(\alpha|t|)^{-3/2}\|\widehat a\|_{L^1},$$
uniformly for all $v\in\mathbb R^3$. Thus the free packet has the uniform $|t|^{-3/2}$ decay on every compact velocity set, including where the stationary point $k=v/(2\alpha)$ lies in the interior of $\operatorname{supp}a$. Write $\phi_v(k)=k\cdot v-\alpha|k|^2$. If a compact velocity set is separated from $2\alpha\operatorname{supp}a$, then $|\nabla_k\phi_v(k)|$ has a uniform positive lower bound on the amplitude support. Repeated integration by parts with $\nabla\phi_v\cdot\nabla_k/(it|\nabla\phi_v|^2)$ gives $O(|t|^{-N})$ for every integer $N$, uniformly on that set. These are the full claims needed from the former draft-supplier application; they do not assert an Airy turning-point estimate.

### M40 — Local $H^2$ regularity used in stationary scattering

Let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^3$ be open, $u\in H^1_{\rm loc}(\Omega)$, and assume the distribution $\Delta u=g$ belongs to $L^2_{\rm loc}(\Omega)$. For any compact $K\subset\Omega$, choose $\chi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ equal to $1$ on a neighborhood of $K$. The product rule for weak derivatives gives $w=\chi u\in H^1(\mathbb R^3)$ and
$$\Delta w=\chi g+2\nabla\chi\cdot\nabla u+(\Delta\chi)u\in L^2(\mathbb R^3).$$
By the published Plancherel theorem and the distributional Fourier derivative identity, $\widehat{\Delta w}(\xi)=-4\pi^2|\xi|^2\widehat w(\xi)\in L^2$. Since also $\widehat w\in L^2$, the published Fourier characterization of integer-order Sobolev spaces gives $(1+|\xi|^2)\widehat w\in L^2$ and hence $w\in H^2(\mathbb R^3)$. As $w=u$ near $K$ and $K$ was arbitrary, $u\in H^2_{\rm loc}(\Omega)$. For the compact-source Helmholtz equation $(-\Delta+V-k^2)\Psi=0$, one has $\Delta\Psi=(V-k^2)\Psi$, which is locally square-integrable because $V\in L^\infty$ and $\Psi\in H^1_{\rm loc}$. This proves the only local regularity step formerly assigned to planned PDE-18.

### M42 — Local Carleman, unique continuation and outgoing invertibility

The scattering variables in this mathematical argument are nondimensional, as in M40. For integer $s\ge1$, put $\tau=s+1$. For $u\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^3\setminus\{0\})$, use $r=e^{-t}$ and write $u=r^{-1/2}v$, $w=e^{st}v$. Direct differentiation gives $\Delta u=r^{-5/2}(v_{tt}+\Delta_{S^2}v-v/4)$. In both squared weighted norms $\|r^{-\tau}u\|_2^2$ and $\|r^{-\tau+2}\Delta u\|_2^2$, the radial Jacobian leaves the common factor $r^{2-2\tau}dt=e^{2st}dt$. On a spherical harmonic of degree $l$, the conjugated operator is $(\partial_t-s)^2-(l+1/2)^2$. Its one-dimensional Fourier multiplier has squared modulus $[\xi^2+(s-l-1/2)^2][\xi^2+(s+l+1/2)^2]\ge s^2/4$, since $s$ is an integer and $l+1/2$ is a half-integer. Plancherel in $t$ and the published complete spherical basis therefore give
$$\|r^{-\tau}u\|_2\le\frac2s\|r^{-\tau+2}\Delta u\|_2.$$
Approximation on any fixed annulus extends this estimate to compactly supported $H^2$ functions vanishing near the center.

Now let $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^3$ be connected and open, $B\in L^\infty_{\rm loc}(\Omega)$, and $\psi\in H^2_{\rm loc}(\Omega)$ satisfy $\Delta\psi=B\psi$ distributionally. Suppose $\psi$ vanishes on a nonempty open set. Choose a ball $B_R(a)\Subset\Omega$ centered at a point having a zero neighborhood, with $\chi\in C_c^\infty(B_R(a))$ equal to $1$ on $B_{R/2}(a)$. Then $u=\chi\psi$ vanishes near $a$, and $\Delta u=Bu+e$, where $e=2\nabla\chi\cdot\nabla\psi+(\Delta\chi)\psi$ is in $L^2$ and supported where $R/2\le r\le R$. If $M=\|B\|_{L^\infty(B_R)}$, the estimate above and $r^2\le R^2$ give
$$\|r^{-\tau}u\|_2\le\frac{2MR^2}{s}\|r^{-\tau}u\|_2+\frac2s\|r^{-\tau+2}e\|_2.$$
For integer $s\ge4MR^2$, absorb the first term. For each $\rho<R/2$, the resulting bound is at most $C_Rs^{-1}(2\rho/R)^\tau\|e\|_2$, so $\|\psi\|_{L^2(B_\rho(a))}=0$ as $s\to\infty$. Thus $\psi=0$ on $B_{R/2}(a)$. Every connected open subset of $\mathbb R^3$ is polygonally path-connected: the points reachable from $a$ by polygonal paths form a nonempty open subset, and its complement in $\Omega$ is also open, so connectedness makes it all of $\Omega$. Along any such compact path, its positive distance from $\Omega^c$ permits a finite chain of fixed-radius balls, propagating the zero set to every point. This proves weak unique continuation without analyticity.

For real bounded $V$ supported in a ball and $k>0$, let $\Psi\in H^2(\mathbb R^3)$ be an eigenfunction of $-\Delta+V$ at $k^2$. Outside the support, each spherical coefficient $R_{lm}(r)$ solves the free radial equation. The explicit solutions are $j_l(z)=(-z)^l(z^{-1}\partial_z)^l(\sin z/z)$ and $y_l(z)=(-z)^l(z^{-1}\partial_z)^l(-\cos z/z)$; differentiation verifies the radial equation and the asymptotics $j_l(z)=\sin(z-l\pi/2)/z+O(z^{-2})$, $y_l(z)=-\cos(z-l\pi/2)/z+O(z^{-2})$. Since $rR_{lm}(r)\in L^2(dr)$, a nonzero combination is impossible: multiplying it by $r$ leaves a nonzero periodic leading term plus $O(r^{-1})$, not square-integrable. Every exterior coefficient therefore vanishes. The unique-continuation result with $B=V-k^2$ forces $\Psi$ to vanish everywhere, so there are no positive eigenvalues.

For the outgoing equation on $D=B_R$, define $K_ku(x)=\int_D e^{ik|x-y|}V(y)u(y)/(4\pi|x-y|)\,dy$. Its kernel is in $L^2(D\times D)$ because $|x-y|^{-2}$ is locally integrable in three dimensions; the published $L^2$-kernel and Hilbert–Schmidt compactness theorems make $K_k$ compact. If $(I+K_k)f=0$, extend $\psi=-G_k*(Vf)$ to $\mathbb R^3$, where $G_k(x)=e^{ik|x|}/(4\pi|x|)$. For $r>0$, the radial equation gives $(-\Delta-k^2)G_k=0$. For $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^3)$, integrate by parts on $\mathbb R^3\setminus\overline{B_\epsilon}$; the inner boundary has outward normal $-\partial_r$, $G_k=(4\pi r)^{-1}+O(1)$ and $-\partial_rG_k=(4\pi r^2)^{-1}+O(1)$, while the term involving $G_k\partial_r\varphi$ has integral $O(\epsilon)$. The remaining boundary term tends to $\varphi(0)$, proving $(-\Delta-k^2)G_k=\delta_0$ distributionally. Since $Vf$ is compactly supported and in $L^2\subset L^1(D)$, the kernel and its first derivatives, of orders $|x|^{-1}$ and $|x|^{-2}$ near zero, are locally in $L^1$. The convolution bound from the published Bochner integral inequality gives $\psi\in H^1_{\rm loc}$. On $D$, $\psi=f$, so its equation is $\Delta\psi=(V-k^2)\psi$; the local $H^2$ lemma above yields $H^2_{\rm loc}$.

For $r>2R$, expansion of $|r\Omega-y|=r-\Omega\cdot y+O(R^2/r)$ gives $\psi(r\Omega)=e^{ikr}A(\Omega)/r+O(r^{-2})$ and $\partial_r\psi=ik e^{ikr}A(\Omega)/r+O(r^{-2})$, uniformly in $\Omega$, with $A(\Omega)=-(4\pi)^{-1}\int_D e^{-ik\Omega\cdot y}V(y)f(y)\,dy$. Test the weak equation against a smooth radial cutoff times $\overline\psi$, approximate this compactly supported $H^1$ test by smooth tests, and take imaginary parts; all volume terms are real and the cutoff transition lies outside the potential support where $\psi$ is smooth. Letting its width tend to zero yields $\operatorname{Im}\int_{|x|=r}\overline\psi\,\partial_r\psi\,dS=0$. The far-field expansion gives $k\int_{S^2}|A|^2d\Omega=0$, so $A=0$. For each fixed $l$, the outgoing and incoming spherical waves $h_l^{(1)}=j_l+i y_l$ and $h_l^{(2)}=j_l-i y_l$ have leading terms proportional to $e^{ikr}/r$ and $e^{-ikr}/r$. On $h_l^{(2)}$, $\partial_r-ik$ has a nonzero $r^{-1}$ leading term, whereas on $h_l^{(1)}$ it is $O(r^{-2})$; the Sommerfeld relation therefore forces the incoming coefficient to vanish. The corresponding spherical-harmonic coefficient of $A$ is a nonzero constant multiple of the outgoing coefficient, so $A=0$ forces that coefficient to vanish too. Thus every exterior coefficient is zero and $\psi=0$ outside $D$. Unique continuation gives $\psi=0$ and hence $f=0$. The published Fredholm alternative for identity minus compact, applied to $I-(-K_k)$ on $L^2(D)$, now makes $I+K_k$ boundedly invertible for every $k>0$. This is exactly the real bounded compact-support scalar-potential case used by M40; no planned PDE result or unpublished helper is a premise.
