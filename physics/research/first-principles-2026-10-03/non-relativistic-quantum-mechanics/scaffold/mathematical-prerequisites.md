This is research prose, not authored library items, a proof certification, or Step-1 evidence. Proposed IDs below are reservations for design discussion only. Work in ZFC (the published unbounded spectral calculus assumes AC); restricted finite constructions require no choice. ## M1 — Hilbert tensor products (proposed def-qm-hilbert-tensor-product, lem-qm-hilbert-tensor-construction)

Let H,K be complex Hilbert spaces. Form the algebraic tensor product over C, the vector space generated by symbols u⊗v modulo bilinearity. On finite sums put `(sum_i u_i⊗v_i,sum_j a_j⊗b_j)=sum_ij (u_i,a_j)_H (v_i,b_j)_K`. This descends through the bilinear relations, since each relation pairs to zero with every elementary tensor. For any finite sum choose orthonormal bases e_a,f_b of the finite-dimensional spans of its factors. The tensor has coordinates c_ab and its squared length is sum_ab |c_ab|². Product coordinate functionals `(u,e_a)(v,f_b)` are bilinear, hence descend to linear functionals on the algebraic tensor product; consequently a nonzero coordinate gives a nonzero tensor and zero coordinates imply the tensor is zero. Thus the form is positive definite, independently of representation. Complete it using published `thm-completion-of-an-inner-product-space-is-hilbert`. Denote the result H⊗̂K.

If supplied orthonormal systems e_a,f_b have dense spans, the products are orthonormal and have dense span in the completion: approximate each u,v separately by finite basis sums and use `||u⊗v-u'⊗v'||≤||u-u'|| ||v||+||u'|| ||v-v'||`. Finite sums are dense by construction. Bounded S:H→H', T:K→K' extend to S⊗T with norm at most ||S||||T||. To check the estimate, expand the second factors in an orthonormal basis of their finite span: `z=sum_b u_b⊗f_b`, whence `||(S⊗I)z||²=sum_b ||Su_b||²≤||S||²||z||²`; applying the analogous estimate for I⊗T gives the assertion. Continuous extension to the completion is unique. Products, adjoints and the interchange `(S⊗I)(I⊗T)=S⊗T` follow on dense elementary tensors and therefore everywhere. This argument does not prove a rule for arbitrary unbounded tensor operators or their sums.

Dependencies: published algebraic tensor construction/product-basis theorem; finite orthonormal bases; Cauchy–Schwarz; completion and bounded extension. Local argument closes the Hilbert completion step. M11 supplies the Euclidean Lebesgue-product identification; no arbitrary-measure identification is claimed.

## M2 — Density operators and spectral probabilities (proposed def-qm-density-operator, lem-qm-density-spectral-probability)

On nonzero separable H, a density operator is a positive self-adjoint trace-class operator ρ with tr ρ=1. Published positive compact spectral and trace theorems give `ρ=sum_n w_n R_{u_n}`, `w_n≥0`, `sum w_n=1`, with orthonormal u_n for its positive eigenvalues and `R_u x=(x,u)u`. A rank-one R_u for unit u has trace 1. Every probability-weighted sum of such rank-one operators converges in trace norm: its tail trace norm is at most the tail weight, by the trace-class ideal and completeness. The limit is positive since quadratic forms converge, and its trace is 1 by trace continuity. Distinct decompositions may represent the same ρ.

For a PVM E on a measurable space define `μρ(B)=tr(ρE(B))`. This equals `sum_n w_n(E(B)u_n,u_n)=sum_n w_n||E(B)u_n||²`. Justification: multiply the trace-norm spectral expansion by the bounded projection and use trace continuity. Every term is nonnegative; total mass is 1; countable additivity follows by nonnegative double-series interchange and strong additivity of E. Hence μρ is a probability measure, without a physical interpretation being presumed. For pure ρ=R_u it is ||E(B)u||². For general self-adjoint A, define moments from this scalar law, not by automatically multiplying unbounded A with ρ. A finite first absolute moment is sufficient for the scalar mean; a finite second moment for the variance. For a pure u∈D(A), `(Au,u)=integral a dμu` and `||Au||²=integral a² dμu`. The first mean can also exist on the larger form domain D(|A|^(1/2)).

Dependencies: published PVM, unbounded spectral theorem, positive trace-class spectral/trace/ideal results, monotone convergence or Tonelli. Closed as a local mathematical argument, subject to later independent review.

## M3 — Finite partial trace and Schmidt decomposition (proposed def-qm-finite-partial-trace, lem-qm-finite-reduced-state, thm-qm-finite-schmidt)

For H=C^m, K=C^n with orthonormal bases e_i,f_j and a matrix R on H⊗K, define `(Tr_K R)_{ik}=sum_j R_{ij,kj}`. Then `tr((Tr_K R)A)=tr(R(A⊗I))` for every matrix A, by expanding finite sums. This identity uniquely determines Tr_K R, since taking matrix units extracts each entry, and proves basis independence. For positive R and x∈H, `(Tr_K R x,x)=sum_j (R(x⊗f_j),x⊗f_j)≥0`; its trace is tr R. Thus partial trace takes density matrices to density matrices. For R=R_Ψ, Ψ=sum_ij c_ij e_i⊗f_j, the reduced matrix is CC*.

Finite matrix singular-value decomposition (or diagonalize CC* and explicitly construct the partner vectors for positive eigenvalues) gives `Ψ=sum_r s_r u_r⊗v_r`, s_r>0, orthonormal factors, sum_r s_r²=||Ψ||². More explicitly, the coefficients of v_r are obtained by contracting Ψ with u_r and dividing by s_r; the eigenvector identities of CC* make their pairings δ_rs. This proves the Schmidt formula and rank-one criterion: a nonzero Ψ is a product iff C has rank one, equivalently iff exactly one Schmidt coefficient is nonzero. For unit Ψ, reduced-state purity tr ρ_H²=sum s_r⁴ is 1 iff Ψ is a product. This equivalence concerns pure bipartite states, not mixed-state separability.

Finite nonselective local maps of the form `R↦sum_a (I⊗M_a)R(I⊗M_a*)`, with sum_a M_a*M_a=I, leave Tr_K R unchanged: test against A and use finite trace cyclicity and the completeness identity. This is a mathematical no-signalling lemma for the specified instrument. Selective conditioning need not preserve the reduced state.

Dependencies: M1; finite spectral theorem; finite trace cyclicity. Infinite-dimensional partial trace and Schmidt convergence are supplied below by M9–M10. General continuous instruments retain the separate residual contract.

## M4 — Finite effects and instruments (proposed def-qm-finite-effect-and-instrument, lem-qm-instrument-normalization)

For H=C^d, an effect is a positive Hermitian matrix F with 0≤F≤I. A finite POVM is a list (F_a) with sum F_a=I. Then `p_a=tr(ρF_a)≥0` and sum p_a=1, by diagonalizing ρ and summing `(F_a u_n,u_n)`. An instrument specified by matrices M_{a,r} has outcome effect F_a=sum_r M_{a,r}*M_{a,r} and unnormalized update I_a(ρ)=sum_r M_{a,r}ρM_{a,r}*. If sum_ar M_{a,r}*M_{a,r}=I, positivity and finite trace cyclicity show tr I_a(ρ)=p_a and normalized update I_a(ρ)/p_a is a density matrix when p_a>0. Effects alone do not determine an update. The single-projector case I_a(ρ)=P_aρP_a is an ideal Lüders instrument, not every apparatus.

Dependencies: finite Hermitian spectral theorem, trace cyclicity, positivity; M2 terminology. Closed finite mathematics. M9/M32 supply complete positivity and explicit countable/continuous Kraus dilations. Universal classification outside the selected instrument constructions is a retained non-premise extension.

## M5 — Variance and uncertainty without illegal operator products (proposed lem-qm-weak-robertson-bound)

Let A,B be self-adjoint and u a unit vector in D(A)∩D(B). Set a=(Au,u), b=(Bu,u), x=(A-a)u, y=(B-b)u. The real means exist and ΔA=||x||, ΔB=||y||. Cauchy–Schwarz gives `ΔA ΔB≥|(x,y)|≥|Im(x,y)|`. If u additionally belongs to D(AB)∩D(BA), then `([A,B]u,u)=(Bu,Au)-(Au,Bu)=-2i Im(Au,Bu)` in the first-linear convention, so `ΔA ΔB≥|( [A,B]u,u)|/2`. Means subtract only real terms and do not change the imaginary part. Without the product-domain hypothesis state only the weak pairing inequality. In particular, a Schwartz u on R satisfies the position–momentum commutator `QP-PQ=i c I` with P=−ic∂, c>0, by the product rule, yielding ΔQΔP≥c/2. M29 supplies the separate maximal-domain cutoff argument.

Dependencies: Cauchy–Schwarz, dense adjoint/self-adjoint definitions, weak/Schwartz differentiation. Closed conditional argument. No energy–time inequality follows by declaring time an observable.

## M6 — Free and bounded-potential Hamiltonian operators (proposed lem-qm-real-multiplier-self-adjoint, thm-qm-free-plus-bounded-potential)

For a real measurable finite-valued a on R^d, multiplication M_a on `D(M_a)={f∈L²:af∈L²}` is self-adjoint. Density follows by truncating f on {|a|≤n}. It is symmetric by integral pairing. If g∈D(M_a*), a vector h represents `(M_a f,g)=(f,h)` for all f in that domain. Test on bounded-support sets where |a| and |g| and |h| are bounded (or use all L² tests supported on {|a|≤n}); these tests show h=ag a.e. on {|a|≤n}. Their union has full measure, hence ag=h∈L² and g∈D(M_a). Conversely each such g is in the adjoint domain. Thus domains and actions agree.

Use the published unitary Fourier transform `F f(ξ)=integral exp(−2πi x·ξ)f(x)dx`. For constants c>0 and b real, set `H0=F^{-1}M_{c|ξ|²+b}F`, domain `{f:|ξ|² Ff∈L²}`. Unit conjugation preserves self-adjointness by the defining adjoint identity. The published Sobolev Fourier theorem identifies this domain as W^{2,2}(R^d) (equivalent norms, not identical norms). On Schwartz functions H0=−c/(4π²)Δ+b. For real V∈L∞, M_V is bounded symmetric with relative H0-bound zero, so published Kato–Rellich gives H=H0+M_V self-adjoint on exactly W^{2,2}. Stone supplies U(t)=exp(−itH), with differentiability exactly for initial data in D(H).

Dependencies: L² definitions, Plancherel, Fourier weak-derivative characterization, dense unbounded adjoint, Kato–Rellich, Stone. Closed for the displayed domain and bounded real potential. Coulomb V∝1/|x|, oscillator V∝|x|² and delta interaction are outside the bounded-potential proof; their model-specific self-adjointness cannot be asserted from this lemma.

## M7 — Finite CHSH and coherence accounting (proposed lem-qm-chsh-classical-bound, lem-qm-finite-fringe-fluctuation)

On any probability space let A0,A1,B0,B1 take values ±1. Pointwise `A0(B0+B1)+A1(B0−B1)` is ±2, because precisely one parenthesis is zero and the other ±2. Integrating gives `|E[A0B0]+E[A0B1]+E[A1B0]−E[A1B1]|≤2`. Applying this to an experimental model requires an explicitly common setting-independent distribution and stated factorization/local-response assumptions; the mathematical lemma contains no physics.

For N independent categorical samples with bin probabilities p_j, a specified sequence j_1,...,j_N has probability product_k p_{j_k}. If all selected probabilities are positive, the sequence has positive probability. Therefore any declared fringe-detection criterion which rejects at least one such sequence has a nonzero failure probability under that model. No universal numerical failure rate follows. For a bin indicator the published binomial mean/variance theorem gives E frequency=p, Var frequency=p(1−p)/N, and Chebyshev gives its declared finite-sample bound. Multinomial count likelihood is `N!/(prod n_j!) prod p_j^{n_j}` for nonnegative integers n_j summing to N: the number of orderings is the multinomial coefficient, each ordering has the same product probability. An inference item must state the sampling and detector model; no theorem turns this likelihood into an observed measurement.

Dependencies: published probability, independence, binomial/expectation/variance, finite combinatorics. Closed algebraic/conditional arguments; advanced finite-data Bell statistics remain open.

## M8 — Finite permutation sectors (proposed def-qm-finite-symmetric-antisymmetric-sector)

On the n-fold Hilbert tensor power of H let U_π permute factors with `U_π(u_1⊗...⊗u_n)=u_{π^{-1}(1)}⊗...⊗u_{π^{-1}(n)}`. The tensor norm construction makes each U_π unitary and U_πU_σ=U_{πσ}. Define `P_+=1/n! sum_π U_π`, `P_-=1/n! sum_π sign(π)U_π`. Group reindexing gives P_±²=P_±, inverse reindexing gives P_±*=P_±; thus they are orthogonal projections with ranges satisfying U_πu=u or sign(π)u respectively. For u⊗u, antisymmetrization in two factors is zero, as swapping leaves it unchanged. Choosing these sectors for particular species is a physical postulate, not a theorem of this construction; a spin–statistics theorem needs relativistic field hypotheses not present here.

Dependencies: M1, finite permutation group/sign and finite sums. Closed construction. M17/M37 provide the separate Fock operator domains.

### M9 — Trace-class cyclicity and separable partial trace

**Objects.** H,K are nonzero separable complex Hilbert spaces. A nuclear rank-one map is T_(v,u)x=(x,u)v. Its trace norm is ||v||||u||. Every trace-class T is a trace-norm convergent sum of these maps with summable products; its trace is the sum (v,u). For bounded B, BT_(v,u)=T_(Bv,u) and T_(v,u)B=T_(v,B*u). Thus tr(BT)=sum(Bv,u)=sum(v,B*u)=tr(TB), with absolute convergence bounded by ||B||sum||v||||u||. The ideal theorem justifies passing to the nuclear limit. This is bounded–trace-class cyclicity, with no unbounded cyclic permutation.

Choose an ONB (f_j) of K. Write v=sum_j v_j⊗f_j and u=sum_j u_j⊗f_j using M1 and Parseval. Define C(T_(v,u))=sum_j T_(v_j,u_j); convergence is in trace norm because sum_j||v_j||||u_j||≤||v||||u||. Hence C extends to every nuclear series T, with ||C(T)||_1≤||T||_1 after taking the nuclear infimum. For bounded A on H its trace pairing is tr(C(T)A)=tr(T(A⊗I)), by the rank-one calculation and absolute convergence. This identity determines C uniquely: for A=T_(y,x), tr(CA)=(Cy,x), so zero pairings force C=0. It proves independence of the ONB and nuclear representation and defines Tr_K T. For T≥0, (Tr_K T x,x)=sum_j(T(x⊗f_j),x⊗f_j)≥0 (first check finite-rank compressions, then nuclear convergence). Taking A=I gives trace preservation. Therefore Tr_K maps density operators to density operators and is trace-norm continuous.

**Uses.** Unitary density evolution preserves positivity and trace. Countable bounded Kraus maps with sum M_a*M_a=I strongly define a trace-norm convergent positive series on each positive trace-class input: partial traces increase to trρ, since the scalar expansion of ρ and monotone convergence give sum tr(M_aρM_a*)=trρ. For general trace-class inputs extend by four positive parts. Amplifying M_a to I_C⊗M_a proves complete positivity for each finite-dimensional ancillary space C: each summand preserves positivity and the trace-norm limit does too. Local trace-preserving Kraus maps leave the opposite reduced state fixed, by testing against A and bounded cyclicity; for countable sums pass using trace-norm convergence. This proves the specified no-signalling statement. It does not prove a general dilation/representation theorem for arbitrary instruments.

### M10 — Infinite Schmidt decomposition

For Ψ∈H⊗K expand Ψ=sum_j h_j⊗f_j, sum||h_j||²=||Ψ||². Define the linear coefficient map C:ell²→H by Cz=sum_j z_jh_j. Cauchy–Schwarz proves boundedness; truncation has operator-norm error≤(sum_(j>N)||h_j||²)^(1/2), so C is compact. The positive compact operator CC* has orthonormal eigenvectors u_r for positive eigenvalues s_r². Define c_rj=(h_j,u_r), v_r=sum_j c_rj f_j/s_r. The identity (CC*u_t,u_r)=sum_j(h_j,u_r)overline((h_j,u_t)) gives (v_r,v_t)=δ_rt. If u∈ker CC*, then C*u=0, hence every (h_j,u)=0. Compact spectral completeness on ker(CC*)^perp therefore expands every h_j in the u_r; double Parseval gives sum_r s_r²=sum_j||h_j||². Consequently Ψ=sum_r s_ru_r⊗v_r in tensor norm. M9 gives reduced eigenvalues s_r²; for a unit Ψ, trρ_H²=sum s_r⁴. This equals one iff precisely one coefficient is nonzero, proving the pure-state product criterion. Mixed separability remains the explicitly defined closed convex-hull property, not this criterion.

### M11 — Euclidean L² tensor identification

For Lebesgue spaces R^a,R^b, a,b≥1, map f⊗g to the a.e. class f(x)g(y). Fubini on products of absolute integrable pairings proves preservation of inner products on finite sums, hence an isometric extension from M1. Its image is closed. To prove density, suppose h∈L²(R^(a+b)) is perpendicular to every product of bounded-box indicator functions. For each fixed rational box B⊂R^b, local Fubini gives the locally integrable function h_B(x)=∫_B h(x,y)dy; all rational-box integrals of h_B vanish. Uniqueness of the measure defined by a locally integrable density (on each bounded box, rational rectangles generate its Borel sigma-algebra by the monotone-class theorem) implies h_B=0 a.e. The boxes are countable, so outside one null set, every rational B has ∫_B h(x,y)dy=0. Apply the same uniqueness in y to get h(x,·)=0 a.e.; Fubini gives h=0 a.e. The orthogonal complement is zero, hence the range is all L². Vector/spin factors follow by finite component sums. All quotients and exceptional sets are Lebesgue a.e.; this argument supplies sigma-finite Euclidean configuration measures, not unrestricted product measures.

### M12 — A diagonal operator lemma and the oscillator

For a specified ONB (e_n) and real numbers λ_n define A(sum c_ne_n)=sum λ_nc_ne_n on D(A)={c:sum λ_n²|c_n|²<∞}. Finite support proves density. Testing the adjoint identity against e_n forces the adjoint coefficient λ_nc_n, so D(A*)=D(A) and A*=A. Finite truncations converge in graph norm, hence their span is a core. If dist(z,{λ_n})>0, multiplication by (λ_n−z)^(-1) is the bounded inverse; if z is in the closure of {λ_n}, basis vectors with |λ_n−z|→0 contradict any bounded inverse. Thus the spectrum is exactly that closure.

**Hermite proof rather than an unchecked basis assertion.** On L²(R,dy), put g_0=π^(-1/4)e^(-y²/2), a=(y+∂_y)/√2 and a†=(y−∂_y)/√2 on polynomial Gaussians. Integration by parts gives adjoint pairing on this space; [a,a†]=I, ag_0=0. Set g_n=(a†)^ng_0/√n!. Induction gives ag_n=√n g_(n−1), a†g_n=√(n+1)g_(n+1), and a†ag_n=ng_n. Symmetry gives orthogonality of distinct n and induction gives unit norms. Each g_n is a degree-n polynomial with nonzero leading coefficient times g_0. If f is perpendicular to all g_n, every moment of fg_0 vanishes. For every real k, the Taylor sums for e^(-iky) are dominated by e^(|k||y|), and |f|g_0e^(|k||y|) is integrable by Cauchy–Schwarz and Gaussian decay. Dominated convergence gives the L¹ Fourier transform of fg_0 identically zero. Published L¹ Fourier uniqueness gives fg_0=0 a.e.; g_0>0 gives f=0. Thus (g_n) is complete. Explicitly the polynomials H_n=(-1)^ne^(y²)∂_y^ne^(-y²) obey g_n=H_ng_0/√(2^nn!), by induction of the displayed differentiation recurrence.

The diagonal lemma defines the self-adjoint closure of −∂_y²+y² from the core span(g_n), with eigenvalues 2n+1 and exact coefficient domain sum(2n+1)²|c_n|²<∞. It agrees with the differential expression distributionally by graph limits of the core. No assertion equating its domain with two independently maximal differential/multiplication domains is needed or made here. For m,ω,ℏ>0 put ℓ=√(ℏ/(mω)), y=x/ℓ and (Df)(x)=ℓ^(-1/2)f(x/ℓ); a change of variable proves D unitary. H=(ℏω/2)DAD* therefore has spectrum ℏω(n+1/2), an ONB Dg_n and exact transported domain. It is the selected self-adjoint oscillator model and is essentially self-adjoint on its displayed core. The closed ladder domains are sum n|c_n|²<∞ for a, sum(n+1)|c_n|²<∞ for a†, obtained by coefficient adjoint testing; their commutator holds on the finite span, not on arbitrary vectors.

For z∈C, ψ_z=e^(−|z|²/2)sum z^ng_n/√n! converges, is normalized by the exponential series, belongs to all polynomial number domains by the ratio test, and aψ_z=zψ_z. Evolution gives U(t)ψ_z=e^(−iωt/2)ψ_(ze^(−iωt)). From Q=ℓ(a+a†)/√2 and P=ℏ(a−a†)/(iℓ√2), pairing gives means √2ℓ Re z and √2ℏ Im z/ℓ, variances ℓ²/2 and ℏ²/(2ℓ²). Their product is ℏ²/4. These physical interpretations additionally assume P1,P2,P4,P5; the coefficient calculations are mathematical.

For d≥1 the product functions g_(n1)(y1)...g_(nd)(yd) are an ONB by M1's product-basis proof and M11's Euclidean tensor unitary. Applying the diagonal lemma to eigenvalues sum_jℏω_j(n_j+1/2), ω_j>0, defines the anisotropic d-dimensional oscillator with exact squared coefficient domain and polynomial-Gaussian graph core; differentiation on that core identifies its usual sum kinetic/quadratic expression. In the isotropic3D case the Nth energy has multiplicity sum_(n1=0)^N(N−n1+1)=(N+1)(N+2)/2, by counting nonnegative triples of sumN. No unbounded tensor-product operator domain is inferred from a merely algebraic tensor symbol.

### M13 — Hardy estimate and Coulomb self-adjointness

For u∈C_c^∞(R³\{0}), div(x/|x|²)=1/|x|². Integration by parts gives ∫|u|²/r²=−2Re∫overline(u)(x/r²)·∇u≤2||u/r||||∇u||, so ||u/r||≤2||∇u||. Removing the excluded origin is legitimate: multiply a compact smooth u by a radial cutoff which is zero on r<ε and one on r>2ε, with gradient O(1/ε). The cutoff derivative's squared L² norm is O(ε); the other omitted terms converge by dominated convergence. Approximation by smooth compact functions extends the estimate to H¹, with u/r converging in L² and agreeing with multiplication away from zero. Fourier/Plancherel gives ||∇u||²≤||u||||−Δu|| for u∈H². Hence ||u/r||≤2√(||u||||−Δu||)≤η||−Δu||+η^(-1)||u|| for every η>0. A real Coulomb multiplier −κ/r, κ>0, is thus infinitesimally −Δ-bounded. Kato–Rellich applies to H=−ℏ²Δ/(2m)−κ/r on H²(R³), with any chosen relative bound<1, and gives self-adjointness and a finite lower bound. Units are κ in J m, m in kg, ℏ in J s. This settles the operator-domain obligation, **not** the separate hydrogen bound/continuum completeness or radial endpoint classification contracts.

### M14 — Bounded time-dependent dynamics and remainder

For a norm-continuous bounded self-adjoint H(t) on a compact interval I, let K=sup||H(t)||/ℏ<∞. Ordered integrals U_n(t,s)=(-i/ℏ)^n∫_(s<t_n<...<t_1<t)H(t_1)...H(t_n)dt_n...dt_1 satisfy ||U_n||≤K^n|t−s|^n/n!. The series U=sum_(n≥0)U_n converges uniformly in norm and differentiates to ∂_tU=−iH(t)U/ℏ, U(s,s)=I. Uniqueness follows by iterating the integral equation: a difference bounded by M is bounded by M(K|t−s|)^n/n! for all n, hence vanishes. The same argument constructs reverse evolution, and uniqueness gives composition/inverses. Differentiating U*U gives zero, so U is unitary. The order-N omitted tail is ≤e^(K|t−s|)(K|t−s|)^(N+1)/(N+1)!.

For a specified self-adjoint H0 and bounded strongly continuous V(t), define V_I(t)=e^(itH0/ℏ)V(t)e^(−itH0/ℏ). It is strongly continuous and uniformly bounded on compact intervals; the same vector-valued ordered integrals converge uniformly as operators by the same bounds and yield a unitary interaction propagator. This constructs mild evolution; no invariant D(H0) or norm differentiability of unbounded H0+V(t) is claimed without further regularity. A first-order transition amplitude in a finite H0 eigenbasis is −i/ℏ∫V_ba(t)e^(i(E_b−E_a)t/ℏ)dt, with the just-proved tail bounding the amplitude error. Thus first-order perturbative transitions have a quantitative finite-time regime.

### M15 — WKB away from turning points, with an explicit error

On [a,b], p∈C² is strictly positive, h>0, θ(x)=∫_a^xp, and w_±=p^(-1/2)e^(±iθ/h). Direct differentiation gives w_±''+(p²/h²)w_±=q w_±, where q=3(p')²/(4p²)−p''/(2p); W(w_+,w_-)=−2i/h. For an exact solution y''+(p²/h²)y=0 write y=w_+c_++w_-c_- and impose w_+c_+'+w_-c_-'=0. Solving the two linear equations gives c'=B(x)c, where B is the conjugation of the error column (0,−qy) by the fundamental matrix (w_±,w_±'). Since |w_±|=p^(-1/2) and |W|=2/h, every entry has modulus≤h|q|/(2p). In the ell¹ vector norm ||B||≤h|q|/p. Integral iteration proves ||c(x)||_1≤||c(a)||_1exp(h∫_a^x|q|/p) and ||c(x)−c(a)||_1≤||c(a)||_1(exp(h∫|q|/p)−1). Therefore |y−c_+(a)w_+−c_-(a)w_-|≤p^(-1/2)||c(a)||_1(exp(h∫|q|/p)−1). For Schrödinger p=√(2m(E−V)), h=ℏ, this is a rigorous allowed-region estimate. It fails when p vanishes; it does not discharge Airy matching or the tunnelling connection formula contract.

### M16 — Cook's criterion with its required domain direction

Let H0,H be self-adjoint, D(H0)⊂D(H), and for ψ in a dense H0-invariant linear subset D⊂D(H0) assume t↦(H−H0)e^(−itH0)ψ is norm-continuous and its norm is integrable on each required half-line.
