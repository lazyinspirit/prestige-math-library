## Statement

Assume Countable Choice, $\mu\ge0$, and the selected free-field model of
[[post-qft-free-field-model]]. Use the four-dimensional affine Minkowski chart,
signature $\eta=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$, length coordinate $x^0=ct$,
and inverse-length parameter $\mu$ of [[def-qft-framework-units]]. For an open
region $O\subseteq\mathbb R^4$, use the scalar and Dirac test functions and
bounded local observable algebras defined in
[[def-qft-free-field-observables]]: scalar tests are real members of
$C_c^\infty(O)$, Dirac tests are members of
$C_c^\infty(O;\mathbb C^4)$, and the Dirac observable algebra is generated by
even products of bounded smeared CAR fields and their adjoints.

For a real Schwartz scalar test $f$, the closure of $\Phi_\mu(f)$ is
self-adjoint on its exact Segal-field multiplier domain. The scalar number,
Hamiltonian and momentum operators and the Dirac Hamiltonian and momentum
operators have the exact maximal sector-sum domains stated in
[[lem-qft-free-domain-observables]]. In particular,
$$D(N_s)=\left\{\Psi=(\Psi_n):\sum_{n\ge1}n^2\|\Psi_n\|^2<\infty\right\}.$$
If $A$ is one of these self-adjoint operators and $\Psi$ is normalized with
$\Psi\in D(A)$, then its model mean $\langle\Psi,A\Psi\rangle$ and variance
$$\operatorname{Var}_\Psi(A)=\|(A-\langle\Psi,A\Psi\rangle)\Psi\|^2$$
are finite; this needs $\Psi\in D(A)$, not $\Psi\in D(A^2)$. The product
$AB\Psi$ is defined only when $\Psi\in D(B)$ and $B\Psi\in D(A)$.

If $O_1,O_2$ are open regions and
$\eta_{ab}(x-y)^a(x-y)^b>0$ for every $x\in O_1$ and $y\in O_2$, then the
scalar Weyl algebras of the two regions commute, and the even local Dirac
algebras of the two regions commute. These are algebraic properties of the
specified free representations.

The chosen construction supplies only test-smeared fields and does not define
$\Phi_\mu(x)$, $\Psi(x)$, or coincident diagonal products. Fix a point
$x_0\in\mathbb R^4$ and a nonnegative Schwartz function
$\rho\in\mathscr S(\mathbb R^4)$ with $\int\rho(y)\,d^4y=1$. For each
width $\epsilon>0$ measured in metres, put
$\rho_{\epsilon,x_0}(x)=\epsilon^{-4}\rho((x-x_0)/\epsilon)$. The rescaled
coordinate is dimensionless, so $\rho_{\epsilon,x_0}$ has unit
$\mathrm m^{-4}$. These tests
converge on Schwartz tests to the point-evaluation functional
$\delta_{x_0}(g):=g(x_0)$, but
$\|\Phi_\mu(\rho_{\epsilon,x_0})\Omega\|\to\infty$ as $\epsilon\downarrow0$.
For the bounded equal-time CAR field $\Psi_t(u)$ defined in
[[lem-qft-free-dirac-car-locality]], fix a spinor $z\in\mathbb C^4$ with
$z^\dagger z=1$ and a nonnegative Schwartz function
$\sigma\in\mathscr S(\mathbb R^3)$ with
$\int\sigma(y)\,d^3y=1$, and put
$u_{\epsilon,x_0}(\mathbf x)=\epsilon^{-3}
\sigma((\mathbf x-\mathbf x_0)/\epsilon)z$ with the same width $\epsilon$.
The spatial rescaled coordinate is dimensionless and $u_{\epsilon,x_0}$ has
unit $\mathrm m^{-3}$. These tests approximate a
spatial point evaluation $h\mapsto z^\dagger h(x_0)$ for each
$h\in\mathscr S(\mathbb R^3;\mathbb C^4)$. Write
$\mathcal F_D:=\mathcal F_a(\mathcal H_+\oplus\overline{\mathcal H_-})$
for the Dirac Fock space of [[lem-qft-free-dirac-car-locality]], and define
$\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}:=\sup\{\|A\xi\|: \xi\in\mathcal F_D,\ \|\xi\|=1\}$
for each bounded linear operator $A:\mathcal F_D\to\mathcal F_D$. For each
fixed coordinate time $t$ in seconds,
$\|\Psi_t(u_{\epsilon,x_0})\|_{\mathrm{op}}\to\infty$. Neither
family has a finite limit in its respective Fock or operator-norm topology.
This restriction does not rule out other distributional or renormalized
constructions. The Born interpretation of
[[post-qft-constructed-model-and-measurement]] applies only after a density
state and normalized finite bounded instrument have actually been supplied;
this item reports no apparatus or outcome.

## Proof

**Given:** [A1], [F1]–[F6], and [F8]–[F11].

1.1 The adopted physical systems are exactly the two selected Fock representations in [F2], and [F1] fixes the spacetime and unit convention. The test spaces and meanings of $\mathfrak A_s(O)$ and $\mathfrak A_D(O)$ are those defined in [F4]; thus all fields used here are smeared and all Dirac local observables are even products. [A1, F1, F2, F4, given]

1.2 Apply [F6] to the listed real scalar smearings and sector-sum generators. It supplies their exact maximal domains and, for every normalized $\Psi\in D(A)$, finite $\langle\Psi,A\Psi\rangle$ and finite variance. The same statement gives the product-domain condition, so no claim about $D(A^2)$ or an everywhere-defined product follows. [F6, algebra]

1.3 The region hypotheses in the Statement are exactly the spacelike-support hypotheses of [F8] and [F10]. The first gives commutativity of the scalar Weyl algebras; the second gives commutativity of the bounded even Dirac local algebras. If either region is empty, its test space is $\{0\}$ and its unital generated algebra is $\mathbb C I$, which commutes with the other algebra as well. [F4, F8, F10, given, algebra]

1.4 Let $(\varepsilon_n)$ be any sequence of positive widths tending to zero. For every Schwartz $g$, changing variables $y=(x-x_0)/\varepsilon_n$ gives
$$\int\rho_{\varepsilon_n,x_0}(x)g(x)\,d^4x=\int\rho(y)g(x_0+\varepsilon_n y)\,d^4y.$$ 
The integrand tends pointwise to $\rho(y)g(x_0)$ and is bounded by the integrable function $\|g\|_\infty\rho(y)$, so [F11] gives convergence to $g(x_0)$. For every spatial Schwartz spinor $h$, the analogous change of variables gives
$$\int u_{\varepsilon_n,x_0}(\mathbf x)^\dagger h(\mathbf x)\,d^3\mathbf x=\int\sigma(y)z^\dagger h(x_0+\varepsilon_n y)\,d^3y.$$ 
Its integrand is bounded by $\|h\|_\infty\sigma(y)$ and converges pointwise to $\sigma(y)z^\dagger h(x_0)$; [F11] and normalization of $\sigma$ give limit $z^\dagger h(x_0)$. Since the width sequence was arbitrary, both families converge to the stated point masses on Schwartz tests. [F11, algebra]

1.5 Let $(\varepsilon_n)$ again be any positive sequence tending to zero. Define $\widehat\rho(\xi)=\int e^{-i\xi\cdot y}\rho(y)\,d^4y$. Positivity and normalization give $|\widehat\rho(\xi)|\le1$, and [F11] gives $\widehat\rho(\xi)\to1$ as $\xi\to0$. The shell formula in [F2] yields $K_\mu\rho_{\varepsilon_n,x_0}(p)=e^{ip\circ x_0}\widehat\rho(\varepsilon_n(-p^0,\mathbf p))$. Fix $r_0=\max(1\,\mathrm m^{-1},\mu)$ and, for each finite $R\ge r_0$, let $A_R=\{p\in H_\mu^+:r_0\le|\mathbf p|\le R\}$. This annulus has finite measure, and dominated convergence [F11] gives $\int_{A_R}|K_\mu\rho_{\varepsilon_n,x_0}|^2\,d\nu_\mu\to\nu_\mu(A_R)$. In radial coordinates $d\nu_\mu=r^2(4\pi^2\sqrt{r^2+\mu^2})^{-1}dr$; for $r\ge r_0$ its density is at least $r/(4\sqrt2\pi^2)$, so $\nu_\mu(A_R)\to\infty$ as $R\to\infty$. Since the full shell norm dominates each annular integral and these limiting lower bounds are arbitrarily large, $\|K_\mu\rho_{\varepsilon_n,x_0}\|\to\infty$. The scalar field formula and vacuum in [F2] give $\|\Phi_\mu(\rho_{\varepsilon_n,x_0})\Omega\|=\|K_\mu\rho_{\varepsilon_n,x_0}\|$. Arbitrariness of $(\varepsilon_n)$ proves the claimed scalar norm divergence. [F2, F11, algebra]

1.6 Changing variables gives $\|u_{\varepsilon,x_0}\|_2=\varepsilon^{-3/2}\|\sigma\|_2\to\infty$. Let $A_\varepsilon=\Psi_t(u_{\varepsilon,x_0})$, the bounded equal-time field of [F9]. Its CAR give $A_\varepsilon A_\varepsilon^*+A_\varepsilon^*A_\varepsilon=\|u_{\varepsilon,x_0}\|_2^2I$. By the operator norm definition and adjoint identity, $\|A_\varepsilon^*\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\|A_\varepsilon\|_{\mathrm{op}}$; the operator-norm triangle inequality and submultiplicativity then imply $\|u_{\varepsilon,x_0}\|_2^2\le2\|A_\varepsilon\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2$, hence $\|A_\varepsilon\|_{\mathrm{op}}\to\infty$. Thus the Dirac family has no finite operator-norm limit. [F9, algebra]

2.1 The selected definition [F5] supplies no other point field or diagonal product. The norm obstructions in steps 1.5 and 1.6 are specific to the displayed representations and topologies. [F5, step 1.5, step 1.6, algebra]

3.1 When the state and finite instrument required by [F3] are supplied, the Born postulate assigns the stated model probabilities. No such state, instrument, apparatus, or observation is introduced by this theorem, so it establishes no empirical outcome. [F3, given] ∎
