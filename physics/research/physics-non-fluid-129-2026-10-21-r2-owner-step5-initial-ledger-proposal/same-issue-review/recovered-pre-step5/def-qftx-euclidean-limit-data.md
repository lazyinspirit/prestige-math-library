## Definition

Fix an integer $d\ge1$, write Euclidean space as $E_d=\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^{d-1}$ with coordinates $x=(t,\mathbf x)$, and put $S_d=\mathcal S(E_d;\mathbb C)$ and $S_{d,n}=\mathcal S(E_d^n;\mathbb C)$ for $n\ge1$, with $S_{d,0}=\mathbb C$. For $d=1$, $\mathbb R^0$ is the one-point vector space with unit measure. A Schwinger hierarchy is a sequence $S=(S_n)_{n\ge0}$ with $S_0=1$ and each $S_n\in S_{d,n}'$; its moments on elementary tests are $\langle S_n,f_1\otimes\cdots\otimes f_n\rangle$. The associated unital algebraic test algebra is $T_d=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}^{\rm alg}S_d^{\otimes_{\rm alg}n}$ with concatenation multiplication, unit $1\in S_{d,0}=\mathbb C$, and involution $(f_1\otimes\cdots\otimes f_n)^*=\overline f_n\otimes\cdots\otimes\overline f_1$ and $(c1)^*=\overline c1$. The moments define $\omega_S:T_d\to\mathbb C$ by linear extension. At $d=4$ this is the same four-dimensional algebra and moment convention used by the positive-tempered-moment definition.

Ordinary moment positivity means $\omega_S(1)=1$ and $\omega_S(a^*a)\ge0$ for every $a\in T_d$. It is distinct from reflection positivity. Let $\vartheta(t,\mathbf x)=(-t,\mathbf x)$ and $E_{d,+}=\{(t,\mathbf x):t>0\}$. The positive-time polynomial algebra $T_{d,+}$ is the unital star subalgebra generated by tests in $C_c^\infty(E_{d,+};\mathbb C)$. Define the antilinear reflection $\Theta$ by $\Theta f=\overline{f\circ\vartheta}$ on degree one, $\Theta(c1)=\overline c1$, and $\Theta(ab)=\Theta(b)\Theta(a)$. Reflection positivity is $\omega_S(\Theta a\,a)\in[0,\infty)$ for every $a\in T_{d,+}$. For the finite-site version, fix a finite set $J$, a permutation $\rho:J\to J$ with $\rho^2=\operatorname{id}$, and a specified subset $J_+\subseteq J$. Let $A_J=\mathbb C[z_j:j\in J]$ be the commutative polynomial algebra with $z_j^*=z_j$ and conjugation of coefficients, and let $A_{J,+}$ be its unital subalgebra generated by $z_j$ for $j\in J_+$. Define $\Theta_J$ by conjugating coefficients and replacing every $z_j$ by $z_{\rho(j)}$. For a specified complex-linear functional $\omega:A_J\to\mathbb C$ with $\omega(1)=1$, ordinary positivity means $\omega(a^*a)\in[0,\infty)$ for all $a\in A_J$, and reflection positivity means $\omega((\Theta_Ja)a)\in[0,\infty)$ for all $a\in A_{J,+}$. One may also use a quotient by an ideal invariant under both $*$ and $\Theta_J$, taking the image of $A_{J,+}$ as its positive-site algebra; these maps and the two forms then have the same definitions on the quotient. An exchanging pair of sites is $J=\{-,+\}$ with $\rho(-)=+$, $\rho(+)=-$, and $J_+=\{+\}$.

A regulated hierarchy is a family $S^\lambda=(S^\lambda_n)_{n\ge0}$ indexed by a nonempty directed set $(\Lambda,\preceq)$, where directed means every two indices have a common upper bound. A distributional continuum limit is a hierarchy $S$ such that for every fixed $n$ and every $\Phi\in S_{d,n}$, $\langle S^\lambda_n,\Phi\rangle\to\langle S_n,\Phi\rangle$ as $\lambda$ advances in $\Lambda$. A limit contract records these pairings, the regulator family, and any additional uniform estimates; pointwise-in-$n$ convergence by itself asserts no uniform bound as $n$ varies.

For explicit comparison, define the Schwartz seminorm $P_k(\Phi)=\max_{|\alpha|,|\beta|\le k}\sup_{x\in E_d^n}|x^\alpha\partial^\beta\Phi(x)|$ on $S_{d,n}$. A per-order uniform continuity record is a choice, for each fixed $n$, of $r_n\in\mathbb N_0$ and $C_n\ge0$ such that $|\langle S^\lambda_n,\Phi\rangle|\le C_nP_{r_n}(\Phi)$ for every $\lambda\in\Lambda$ and $\Phi\in S_{d,n}$. Separately, a uniform-order bound on the limit hierarchy is the explicit cross-order condition that there exist fixed $s\in\mathbb N$ and $A,B,q>0$ with $|\langle S_n,\Phi\rangle|\le A B^n(n!)^q P_{sn}(\Phi)$ for every $n\ge1$ and $\Phi\in S_{d,n}$. This bound is recorded separately and is not inferred from distributional convergence or asserted equivalent to a corrected Osterwalder–Schrader regularity hypothesis.

For $\gamma(x)=Rx+a$ with $R\in O(d)$ and $a\in E_d$, define $(U_\gamma\Phi)(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=\Phi(\gamma^{-1}x_1,\ldots,\gamma^{-1}x_n)$. Euclidean invariance means $\langle S_n,U_\gamma\Phi\rangle=\langle S_n,\Phi\rangle$ for every such $\gamma$ and $\Phi\in S_{d,n}$. Permutation symmetry means invariance of the pairing under each permutation of its $n$ argument blocks. Clustering means that for every $n,m\ge1$, $f\in S_{d,n}$ and $g\in S_{d,m}$,

$$
\langle S_{n+m},f\otimes\tau_{ae_0}g\rangle\longrightarrow \langle S_n,f\rangle\langle S_m,g\rangle\quad(a\to+\infty),
$$

where $e_0=(1,\mathbf0)$ and $(\tau_{ae_0}g)(x_1,\ldots,x_m)=g(x_1-ae_0,\ldots,x_m-ae_0)$. This contract definition asserts neither existence of a regulated limit nor any of these additional properties.

## Remarks

The reflection form, the ordinary positive-moment form, and the clustering limit are three distinct interfaces. Fixed-order distributional convergence can pass a reflection quadratic form; it does not by itself establish clustering or a uniform-order growth bound. The locally defined uniform-order bound above is not asserted equivalent to an Osterwalder–Schrader hypothesis. Any later reconstruction claim must state the exact corrected theorem version and prove its test-space and order-growth hypotheses. This definition does not invoke a reconstruction theorem or assert construction of a null-quotient Hilbert space, positive-time translation semigroup, self-adjoint generator, analytic continuation, locality, or spectrum condition.
