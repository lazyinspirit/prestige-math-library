## Actual proof and prerequisites

Edgelessness itself gives a local barrier for vertical timelike curves. In temporal coordinates, each vertical fiber consequently meets S once, and a narrower constant cone gives a Lipschitz graph. No invariance-of-domain theorem, Zorn maximal achronal set, arbitrary choice, or unproved horizon-generator theorem is needed. Future/past causal segments through S lie on the corresponding strict side of the graph. Compact uniform approximations therefore cross S as well.

With a complete auxiliary Riemannian metric, reparametrize avoiding causal halves by arclength. The candidate includes the admissibility argument: the accumulated length is locally AC and strictly increasing, maps its null exceptional set to a null set, and inverse difference quotients divide causal velocity by its positive h-speed. The new curve is locally Lipschitz and has h-speed one almost everywhere. Completeness makes every inextendible half have infinite arclength. The existing basic-limit-curve supplier is then applied to avoiding curves through points approaching D+(S) outside S, or, at S, to both avoiding halves through points outside D(S). Strict graph crossing contradicts avoidance, proving openness.

Causal convexity follows from concatenating an avoiding half at a putative exterior intermediate point with the two remaining causal segments. Cauchy intersection uses connectedness and the chronological-side property: an avoiding curve in D(S) lies in a single side; its relevant ambient end either is already inextendible, contrary to domain membership, or converges. A limit outside S is forced into the corresponding causal domain by endpoint extension and an avoiding-tail contradiction. It is then a point limit in D(S), contrary to intrinsic inextendibility. No closedness of D or parameter-preserving full extension is asserted.
