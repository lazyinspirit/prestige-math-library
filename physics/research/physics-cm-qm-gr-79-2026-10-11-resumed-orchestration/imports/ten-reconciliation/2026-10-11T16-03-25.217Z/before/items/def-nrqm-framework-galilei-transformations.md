## Definition

**Coordinate space.** Let $C:=\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^{3}$, whose elements are written $(t,x)$ with $t\in\mathbb R$ and $x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb R^{3}$. The Euclidean inner product and norm on the second factor are those of [[def-euclidean-inner-product]]; addition and scalar multiplication on $C$ are componentwise, and $I_{3}$ is the identity matrix of [[def-matrix-product-and-identity-matrix]].

**The transformations.** For a quadruple $\theta=(b,a,v,R)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^{3}\times\mathbb R^{3}\times SO(3)$ ([[def-cm-special-orthogonal-group]]) define the map $g_{\theta}:C\to C$ by

$$g_{(b,a,v,R)}(t,x):=\bigl(t+b,\ Rx+v\,t+a\bigr),$$

where $Rx$ and $vt$ are the usual matrix–vector products with $R=(R_{ij})$ and with the column vectors $x,v,a$. The four components of $\theta$ are called the **time shift** $b$, the **spatial translation** $a$, the **boost velocity** $v$ and the **rotation** $R$, and $\theta$ is called the **parameter quadruple** of $g_{\theta}$. Write

$$G:=\bigl\{g_{(b,a,v,R)}:(b,a,v,R)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^{3}\times\mathbb R^{3}\times SO(3)\bigr\}$$

for the set of all these maps and $\Theta:=\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^{3}\times\mathbb R^{3}\times SO(3)$ for the parameter set. The elements of $G$ are the **Galilean transformations of the coordinate space**, and the assignment $\theta\mapsto g_{\theta}$ is the **parameter map**.

**Elementary readings.** For $R=I_{3}$ and $v=0$ the map $g_{(b,a,0,I_{3})}$ is the pure time-and-space translation $(t,x)\mapsto(t+b,x+a)$; for $b=0$, $a=0$ and $R=I_{3}$ it is the pure boost $(t,x)\mapsto(t,x+vt)$; and for $b=0$, $a=0$, $v=0$ it is the rotation $(t,x)\mapsto(t,Rx)$ acting only on the spatial factor. The time coordinate of $g_{\theta}(t,x)$ depends only on $t$ and $b$, in accordance with the absolute-time convention of the model that consumes this definition.

**What is and is not asserted.** This definition introduces the maps and names their parameters. It asserts no property of the parameter map (such as injectivity), no closure of $G$ under composition or inversion, no continuity, smoothness or differentiability of the maps, and no physical interpretation: whether these maps describe changes between inertial frames of a physical model is a separate, explicitly adopted assumption of the physics items that consume this definition. The group structure of $G$ is proved in the group lemma of this page, and the units in which the parameters are read are fixed by the model's unit conventions, not here.

## Remarks

- The definition is purely mathematical. Its content is the set of maps displayed above, the naming of the parameters and the reading of the elementary cases; nothing is assumed about which coordinate systems a physical model uses.
- The composition and inverse computations of [[lem-nrqm-framework-galilei-transformations-form-a-group]] show that $G$ is a group of bijections of $C$ with respect to composition, with the parameters of a composite given by the displayed polynomial expressions. In the model, the composition of frame changes is used only through that lemma.
- Notation $Rx$ requires the identification of matrices over $\mathbb R$ with linear maps of $\mathbb R^{3}$; the products displayed are the usual ones of [[def-matrix-product-and-identity-matrix]] read componentwise, and the case $R\in SO(3)$ is the one of [[def-cm-special-orthogonal-group]].
