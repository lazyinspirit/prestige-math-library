## Confirmed counterexample

Use signature (-,+,+,+), Lambda=0 and fixed kappa>0. On (-epsilon,epsilon) times R^3 compare Minkowski g1=-dt^2+delta and g2=-dt^2+a(t)^2 delta, where a=1+t^4. Set T1=0 and T2=G[g2]/kappa. Both satisfy their Einstein equations identically. Contracted Bianchi gives covariant conservation on each development; no matter evolution law was imposed.

For g2, H=a'/a and the components are

$$G_{00}=3H^2=48t^6/(1+t^4)^2,\qquad G_{0i}=0,\qquad G_{ij}=-(2aa''+(a')^2)\delta_{ij}=-(24t^2+40t^6)\delta_{ij}.$$

At t=0 both metrics induce h=delta, K=aa' delta=0, every component of T is zero and every component of its ordinary normal derivative is zero. The connection of g2 also vanishes at t=0, hence covariant normal derivatives are zero as well. The full initial stress 1-jets match. But Ric[g2]00=-3a''/a=-36t^2/(1+t^4), which is nonzero for every t nonzero, whereas Ric[g1]=0 everywhere. No open neighborhood of the initial surface can be isometric to a Minkowski neighborhood. This directly refutes the current arbitrary-conserved-T statement even with the extra stress derivative data it demands.
