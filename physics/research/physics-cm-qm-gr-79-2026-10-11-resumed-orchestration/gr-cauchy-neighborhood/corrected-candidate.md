## Full corrected product-chart proof

For any compact parameter interval $[t_1,t_2]$ on a future causal curve, absolute-continuity calculus and the speed hypothesis give

$$|\sigma(\gamma(t_2))-\sigma(\gamma(t_1))|\le\int_{t_1}^{t_2}|(\sigma\circ\gamma)'|\le K\int_{t_1}^{t_2}(s\circ\gamma)'=K(s(\gamma(t_2))-s(\gamma(t_1))).$$

Strict temporal monotonicity makes the image an $s$-graph $(s,z(s))$, and the displayed inequality makes $z$ $K$-Lipschitz. This does not require that the inverse parameter map be $C^1$. The graph is also a causal AC parametrization: at a differentiability parameter $s_0$, integrate original velocities over the original-parameter interval corresponding to $(s_0,s_0+h)$ and divide by $h$. On this shrinking arc, all velocities lie in cones arbitrarily close to the cone at its limiting point. More explicitly, for each $\epsilon>0$ the constant Lorentz form $g_0-\epsilon H_0$, with $H_0$ any fixed positive-definite Euclidean form, is a wider cone containing every nearby $g$-causal vector; choose its future component consistently. Its closed future cone is convex, so the normalized integrated displacement lies in that cone. Let $h\to0$, then $\epsilon\downarrow0$. The limiting vector $(1,z'(s_0))$ is future $g_0$-causal and nonzero. Thus the graph has nonzero future causal derivative almost everywhere. The same proof extends a graph continuously to a finite endpoint whenever its limit lies in $U$: on the closed endpoint interval it is Lipschitz, and the endpoint is a measure-zero exception. This supplies a causal segment from the endpoint to any interior point without claiming preservation by an arbitrary singular reparametrization.

For (a), if $s(p)<0$, its past vertical fiber on $(\inf I,s(p)]$ avoids $S$ and has no limit in $U$ at its past end. When $\inf I$ is finite its limiting time coordinate is excluded from $I$; when it is $-\infty$ there is no finite time-coordinate limit. Thus $p\notin D^+_U(S)$. Reverse time for $D^-_U(S)\subset\{s\le0\}$.

If a past-inextendible curve through $q$, $s(q)>0$, avoids $S$, its past time infimum $a$ is in $[0,s(q))$. The Lipschitz graph has a limit $z_*$ in the **closed** Euclidean ball as $s\downarrow a$. Since $a\in I$, an interior limit $|z_*|<R$ would be a limit point in $U$, forbidden by inextendibility. Consequently $|z_*|=R$, and

$$R-|\sigma(q)|\le|z_*-\sigma(q)|\le K(s(q)-a)\le Ks(q).$$

This contradicts the corrected strict inequality for $q\in Q$. The $s=0$ case meets $S$ at $q$ itself, and the negative case reverses time. Hence $Q\subset D_U(S)$. As $Q$ is open and contains every $(0,\sigma)$ with $|\sigma|<R$, it is a neighborhood of the **entire** slice and $Q\subset W$.

Here is the past-heredity fact used to establish (c). If $r\in D^+_U(S)$ and $p\le_U r$ with $s(p)>0$, then $p\in D^+_U(S)$. Otherwise choose a past-inextendible avoiding curve through $p$ and retain its past half ending at $p$. Append a causal segment $p\to r$. All its times are $\ge s(p)>0$, so the concatenation avoids $S$ and retains the same past end, contradicting $r\in D^+_U(S)$. Concatenation is legitimate after using temporal graph parameters on the finite connecting segment; local AC holds at the junction and one exceptional parameter is null. This is a proof of heredity, not an assertion of openness of the entire one-sided domain.

Suppose a two-sided inextendible causal curve $\gamma$ in $W$ avoids $S$. Its temporal values are entirely positive or entirely negative; assume positive. By (a) every image point is in $D^+_U(S)$. If its past end has no limit in $U$, it is past-inextendible in $U$ and immediately contradicts this membership. Otherwise it converges to $q\in U$, with $s(q)=a\ge0$. If $a=0$, then $q\in S\subset W$, contradicting inextendibility in $W$.

If $a>0$, choose a small convex normal set $C\Subset U$ about $q$ and an interior point $r$ on a sufficiently short tail of $\gamma$ contained in $C$. The tail including $q$ is an $s$-Lipschitz causal segment as proved above; the same local estimate applies on a relatively compact chart if needed. Hence $v=\exp_q^{-1}(r)$ is nonzero future causal. Let $T=\partial_s|_r$ and move $r$ a small positive distance along its timelike coordinate fiber to $r_\epsilon\in C\cap W$. If $v$ is timelike, continuity already makes $\exp_q^{-1}(r_\epsilon)$ future timelike. If it is null, the Gauss differential gives

$$\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}D_q^2(r_\epsilon)\right|_{\epsilon=0}=2g_r(P(q,r),T)<0.$$

Therefore for sufficiently small $\epsilon>0$, $D_q^2(r_\epsilon)<0$, and its position vector is future timelike (future orientation persists since $v\ne0$). Radial reachability gives $q\ll_C r_\epsilon$. Smooth dependence of the two-point inverse and openness of the timelike cone now give an open neighborhood $V$ of $q$, contained in $C\cap\{s>0\}$, such that every $p\in V$ satisfies $p\ll_C r_\epsilon$. The point $r_\epsilon\in W$ has positive time, hence is in $D^+_U(S)$; heredity gives $V\subset D^+_U(S)$ and thus $q\in W$. Again this contradicts inextendibility in $W$. Reverse time for the negative case. Every inextendible $W$-curve therefore meets $S$, and strict temporal increase gives exactly one parameter visit.

This proves the strongest stated (c). Global hyperbolicity is exactly the conditional consequence of the existing `thm-gr-cauchy-surface-implies-globally-hyperbolic` interface; its proof is outside this subtask's assigned scope. No additional ambient causal-convexity or ambient diamond claim has been added. All reachability, domain, and any resulting compact diamond refer to the induced open submanifold $W$, not to $M$ or the full chart $U$.

## Generic spacelike hypersurface and noncompact variable width

Let $\Sigma$ be any smooth embedded spacelike hypersurface without boundary, in a smooth second-countable Hausdorff time-oriented Lorentzian four-manifold. It need not be compact or globally acausal in $M$. The claim is existence of an **intrinsic** Cauchy neighborhood, not that $\Sigma$ is Cauchy/acausal in $M$. Include countable/dependent choice used by the actual suppliers.

The global future timelike orientation field $N$ is transverse to $\Sigma$: a vector tangent to a spacelike hypersurface cannot be timelike. The imported published `thm-flowout-theorem` gives an open zero-section neighborhood $\mathcal O\subset\mathbb R\times\Sigma$ and an injective flow map $F(s,x)=\Phi_s^N(x)$ which is a diffeomorphism onto an open neighborhood. Its supplier `thm-fundamental-theorem-on-flows` provides smoothness on the open maximal flow domain, interval fibers containing zero, and the group law. No completeness/global flow time is asserted.

At $(0,x)$, the inverse time covector annihilates $T_x\Sigma$ and takes the value $1$ on $N_x$, so it is past timelike. Shrink $\mathcal O$ to its open subset where the pulled-back $ds$ remains past timelike. Retain a symmetric variable-width strip inside that subset as follows. Take the family of all open $V\subset\Sigma$ and positive rational $a$ with $(-a,a)\times V$ inside it. This is a cover of $\Sigma$. The imported published `thm-smooth-partitions-of-unity-exist-on-manifolds`, under $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, gives a locally finite subordinate smooth partition $(\rho_j)$ indexed by these pairs $(V_j,a_j)$. Put

$$\delta(x)=\tfrac12\sum_j a_j\rho_j(x)>0,\qquad\Omega=\{(s,x):|s|<\delta(x)\},\qquad U=F(\Omega).$$

For fixed $x$, only finitely many partition values are nonzero; choose one with maximal $a_j$. Then $x\in V_j$ and $\delta(x)\le a_j/2$, so the **whole** fiber interval $|s|<\delta(x)$ is inside the temporal flow domain. This verifies the strip inclusion; it is not an unjustified assertion of a positive uniform radius. On $U$, the global coordinate functions are time $s$ and projection $\pi:U\to\Sigma$; there is generally **no** global $\mathbb R^3$ spatial chart. The zero slice is exactly $\Sigma$. Since $\partial_s=F_*N$ is future timelike, negative-time vertical fibers prove the intrinsic domain version of (a).

For each $x\in\Sigma$, choose a local spatial chart $\sigma_i:V_i\to B_{R_i}(0)$ and $a_i>0$ whose closed product box $[-a_i,a_i]\times\overline{B_{R_i}}$ lies in a larger temporal-flow chart inside $U$. Such boxes exist pointwise by openness, after shrinking the spatial chart and the time interval. One may choose a countable subordinate family by the supplied manifold cover/partition construction. The conical speed constants are defined on those compact enlarged boxes, not globally: normalize future causal vectors by $ds(v)=1$ and maximize $|d\sigma_i(v)|$. This normalized cone section is compact, uniformly over the box. To verify boundedness, an unbounded normalized sequence, divided by its Euclidean norms, would have a limiting nonzero future causal vector annihilated by $ds$, impossible because $\ker ds$ is spacelike. Closedness follows from continuity of $g$ and the future orientation. Put $K_i=\max(1,\sup|d\sigma_i(v)|)<\infty$.

Define intrinsic funnels using the actual charts and times,

$$Q_i=F\{(s,x):x\in V_i,\ |s|<a_i,\ |\sigma_i(x)|+K_i|s|<R_i\}.$$

They are open and their zero slices cover $\Sigma$. For $q\in Q_i$, $s(q)>0$, suppose a past-inextendible **U**-curve through $q$ avoids $\Sigma$. Its times remain positive. Until it first leaves the box, its spatial displacement is at most $K_i(s(q)-s)$. It cannot reach the spatial boundary with $s\ge0$, because that would imply $R_i-|\sigma_i(q)|\le K_i s(q)$, contrary to the funnel inequality. It cannot leave through the negative or positive time boundary while proceeding to the past with $0<s\le s(q)<a_i$. If it never leaves, the Lipschitz estimate gives an endpoint with $s\ge0$ and spatial norm strictly less than $R_i$, hence a limit point in $U$, contradicting U-inextendibility. Thus every such curve meets $\Sigma$ and $q\in D^+_U(\Sigma)$. Reverse time for negative $s$; zero-time points are already in $\Sigma$. This explicitly proves $\bigcup_iQ_i\subset D_U(\Sigma)$ despite changing the ambient domain from a single local box to $U$.

Consequently $W=\operatorname{int}_U D_U(\Sigma)$ is open and contains the entire, possibly noncompact, $\Sigma$. The full proof of (c) above applies: the endpoint argument is local; a curve without a U-endpoint contradicts the domain definition directly, and a U-endpoint is excluded by the local radial timelike perturbation. No uniform $a_i$, $K_i$, $R_i$, or positive lower bound for $\delta$ is available or needed. A curve can escape spatially at infinity; if it does, it has no U-limit and is precisely one of the inextendible curves quantified over by the domain definition.

These constructions preserve tilt. For a local graph $s=f(\sigma)$ in an existing temporal product chart, set $\tau=s-f(\sigma)$. On the graph, spacelikeness says $d\tau$ is timelike, and $d\tau(\partial_s)=1$ fixes its past-gradient orientation. Shrink around the graph to retain the temporal condition and choose product boxes for $(\tau,\sigma)$. Recompute the **new** speed bound using $d\tau$, rather than recycling $K$ for $ds$. In a quantitative regime $|df|K\le\theta<1$, $d\tau(v)\ge(1-\theta)ds(v)$ and $|d\sigma(v)|\le K/(1-\theta)d\tau(v)$; otherwise compact cone normalization supplies a finite new local constant directly. No small global tilt assumption is necessary. The flow construction works equally with any future timelike transverse field, including a prescribed dust field, without setting it equal to the unit normal.
