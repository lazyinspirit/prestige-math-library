## Dyadic formulation, if the owning lead needs it

For the actual nonincreasing nonnegative tail $h(r)=\|VR_0(i)\mathbf1_{|x|\ge r}\|$ and any $c>0$,

$$\sum_{k\ge0}2^kh(c2^k)\le\frac2c\int_{c/2}^\infty h(r)\,dr<\infty.$$

Indeed, on $[c2^{k-1},c2^k]$, monotonicity gives $h(r)\ge h(c2^k)$, so its integral bounds $(c2^{k-1})h(c2^k)$ from below. The intervals have disjoint interiors; summing the resulting inequalities proves the displayed estimate. On a time interval $[2^k,2^{k+1}]$, the integral of $h(ct)$ is at most $2^kh(c2^k)$. Thus dyadic time summation gives the same operator-norm integrability as the current exact substitution $\int_1^\infty h(2at)dt=(2a)^{-1}\int_{2a}^\infty h(r)dr$. No separate output-tail hypothesis or input/output equivalence is needed.
