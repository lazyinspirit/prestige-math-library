## Statement

Assume Countable Choice ([[def-countable-choice]]). Adopt the hypotheses and notation of [[thm-nrqm-controlled-dynamics-strong-bounded-interaction-picture]]: $H$ is a complex Hilbert space with inner product linear in its first argument, $H_0$ is self-adjoint on its exact dense domain $D(H_0)$, $\hbar>0$, $I=[a,b]$ is a nonempty compact interval, $V:I\to\mathcal B(H)$ is strongly continuous with self-adjoint values, $v=\sup_{r\in I}\|V(r)\|<\infty$, $K=v/\hbar$, and $U(t,s)$ is that supplier's mild unitary propagator. Put $E(\sigma)=e^{-i\sigma H_0/\hbar}$. Suppose $(\varphi_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}$ is a complete orthonormal family of eigenvectors, $H_0\varphi_n=E_n\varphi_n$, $E_n\in\mathbb R$ ([[def-orthonormal-family-complete-orthonormal-system-and-hilbert-basis]]). Fix indices $a_0,b_0$ and $s\le t$ in $I$, and write $\tau=t-s$, $\varphi_a=\varphi_{a_0}$, $\varphi_b=\varphi_{b_0}$, $E_a=E_{a_0}$, $E_b=E_{b_0}$. Define the transition amplitude by
$$A_{ba}(t,s):=e^{iE_b\tau/\hbar}\langle U(t,s)\varphi_a,\varphi_b\rangle.$$
Then
$$A_{ba}(t,s)=\delta_{a_0b_0}-\frac{i}{\hbar}\int_s^t e^{i(E_b-E_a)(r-s)/\hbar}\langle V(r)\varphi_a,\varphi_b\rangle\,dr+R_{ba}(t,s),\qquad |R_{ba}(t,s)|\le e^{K\tau}\frac{(K\tau)^2}{2}.$$

Consequently let $\eta:I\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}$ be Lebesgue integrable and let $V_0(r)$ be self-adjoint bounded operators with $\|V(r)-V_0(r)\|\le\eta(r)$ for every $r\in I$. Assume in addition that the selected scalar coefficient $c_0(r)=\langle V_0(r)\varphi_a,\varphi_b\rangle$ is Lebesgue measurable. It is then integrable, and the first-order expression
$$A^{(1,0)}_{ba}(t,s):=\delta_{a_0b_0}-\frac{i}{\hbar}\int_s^t e^{i(E_b-E_a)(r-s)/\hbar}c_0(r)\,dr$$
satisfies
$$|A_{ba}(t,s)-A^{(1,0)}_{ba}(t,s)|\le\frac1\hbar\int_s^t\eta(r)\,dr+e^{K\tau}\frac{(K\tau)^2}{2}.$$
Direct Lebesgue integrability of $c_0$ may replace its measurability premise. This comparison requires no strong continuity of $V_0$ and asserts no exact propagator for it. No physical transition probability, measurement postulate or long-time limit is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** precisely the Statement's CC, $H,H_0,I,V,\hbar,v,K$, complete eigenfamily, fixed indices and times; the comparison additionally has the stated $\eta,V_0,c_0$ assumptions. Inner products are linear in the first argument and conjugate-linear in the second.

[F1] [[thm-nrqm-controlled-dynamics-strong-bounded-interaction-picture]] gives the autonomous unitary group $E$, its generator derivative and domain preservation on $D(H_0)$, $V_I(r)=E(-r)V(r)E(r)$, and $U(t,s)=E(t)U_I(t,s)E(-s)$. Its vector strong Dyson terms $D_n(t,s)$ have $D_0=I$, $D_1\psi=-(i/\hbar)\int_s^tV_I(r)\psi\,dr$, latest-time-left order, $\|D_n\|\le(K\tau)^n/n!$, and $U_I=\sum_{n\ge0}D_n$ in operator norm. In particular its actual tail bound gives $\|U_I-I-D_1\|\le e^{K\tau}(K\tau)^2/2$. This uses continuous $H$-valued vector integrals, with no norm-Bochner integral into $\mathcal B(H)$.

[F2] Orthonormality gives $\langle\varphi_a,\varphi_b\rangle=\delta_{a_0b_0}$ and unit norms ([[def-orthonormal-family-complete-orthonormal-system-and-hilbert-basis]]). The inner-product inequality $|\langle x,y\rangle|\le\|x\|\|y\|$ follows directly, when $y\ne0$, by expanding $\|x-\langle x,y\rangle y/\|y\|^2\|^2\ge0$; for $y=0$ it is immediate. Thus $x\mapsto\langle x,\varphi_b\rangle$ is a bounded linear functional of norm one. Operator bounds are [[def-bounded-linear-operator]] and [[def-operator-norm]].

[F3] A bounded linear functional commutes with a continuous vector integral: it commutes with each defining finite Riemann sum and with its norm limit. The integral norm inequality is [[lem-bochner-integral-norm-inequality]], also for scalar integrals. For the comparison, the explicitly assumed scalar measurability and an integrable modulus majorant imply Lebesgue integrability.

## Proof

1.1 [Forward pairing and first-order phase] For every eigenvector, [F1]'s autonomous domain derivative gives $\frac{d}{d\sigma}E(\sigma)\varphi_n=-(iE_n/\hbar)E(\sigma)\varphi_n$. The derivative of $e^{iE_n\sigma/\hbar}E(\sigma)\varphi_n$ is therefore zero, and its value at $0$ is $\varphi_n$; hence $E(\sigma)\varphi_n=e^{-iE_n\sigma/\hbar}\varphi_n$. Unitarity, this formula and the first-variable-linear convention give $\langle E(t)x,\varphi_b\rangle=e^{-iE_bt/\hbar}\langle x,\varphi_b\rangle$ and therefore $A_{ba}=e^{i(E_a-E_b)s/\hbar}\langle U_I(t,s)\varphi_a,\varphi_b\rangle$. Its order-zero term is $e^{i(E_a-E_b)s/\hbar}\delta_{a_0b_0}=\delta_{a_0b_0}$. Further, $\langle V_I(r)\varphi_a,\varphi_b\rangle=e^{i(E_b-E_a)r/\hbar}\langle V(r)\varphi_a,\varphi_b\rangle$. Apply [F3] to $D_1\varphi_a$ and combine its phase with the prefactor to obtain exactly the displayed first-order integral. Its selected coefficient is continuous by strong continuity of $V$ on the fixed vector $\varphi_a$. [F1, F2, F3, given, algebra]

2.1 [Exact strong-vector remainder] Put $T_2=U_I(t,s)-I-D_1(t,s)=\sum_{n\ge2}D_n(t,s)$ and $R_{ba}=e^{i(E_a-E_b)s/\hbar}\langle T_2\varphi_a,\varphi_b\rangle$. This is exactly the remainder in step 1.1, and [F2] gives $|R_{ba}|\le\|T_2\|$. The actual vector-tail supplier [F1] proves the retained bound $e^{K\tau}(K\tau)^2/2$. Explicitly, $\|T_2\|\le\sum_{n\ge2}(K\tau)^n/n!\le (K\tau)^2e^{K\tau}/2$, since $(j+2)!\ge2j!$. The argument includes $K=0$ and $t=s$ without division by $K$, and uses operator-norm convergence of operators built from vector integrals, without imposing norm continuity on $V_I$ or $V$. [step 1.1, F1, F2, given, algebra]

3.1 [Measurable coefficient comparison] Write $c(r)=\langle V(r)\varphi_a,\varphi_b\rangle$. The operator bound and [F2] give $|c-c_0|\le\eta$ and $|c_0|\le v+\eta$. Because $I$ is finite and $c_0$ is explicitly measurable, it is integrable; the alternative direct integrability premise suffices as well. Its product with the continuous modulus-one phase is integrable. Subtract the two first-order scalar expressions; [F3] bounds their difference by $\hbar^{-1}\int_s^t\eta(r)dr$. Adding the exact remainder from step 2.1 proves the full comparison bound. Only a selected scalar integral of $V_0$ has been used. [step 1.1, step 2.1, F2, F3, given, algebra] ∎
