def-gr-causal-conditions-and-global-hyperbolicity
Let $(M,g,N)$ be a time-oriented smooth Lorentzian four-manifold with the
causal curves and the relations $\le$, $\ll$, $I^{\pm}$, $J^{\pm}$ of
[[def-gr-causal-curves-and-reachability]]. All curves below are curves in
that sense.

**(a) Chronology and causality.** $(M,g,N)$ is **chronological** when there is
no $p\in M$ with $p\ll p$, equivalently when there is no nonconstant future
timelike curve $\gamma:[a,b]\to M$ with $\gamma(a)=\gamma(b)$. It is **causal**
when there is no nonconstant future causal curve $\gamma:[a,b]\to M$ with
$\gamma(a)=\gamma(b)$; a nonconstant future causal curve with equal endpoints
is called a **closed causal curve**, and a closed future timelike curve is
called a **closed timelike curve**.

**(b) Strong causality.** $(M,g,N)$ is **strongly causal** when for every
$p\in M$ and every open set $U\subseteq M$ with $p\in U$ there is an open set
$V$ with $p\in V\subseteq U$ such that for every future causal curve
$\gamma:[a,b]\to M$ and all $a\le s<t\le b$ with $\gamma(s)\in V$ and
$\gamma(t)\in V$ one has $\gamma([s,t])\subseteq V$. Thus no causal curve can
leave $V$ and later re-enter it; a set $V$ with this property for all causal
curves is called **causally convex for causal curves that meet it twice**.

**(c) Global hyperbolicity.** $(M,g,N)$ is **globally hyperbolic** when it is
strongly causal and for all $p,q\in M$ the **causal diamond**
$J^+(p)\cap J^-(q)$ is compact in the subspace topology
([[def-compact-space]]).

**(d) Time functions.** A **time function** on $(M,g,N)$ is a function
$u:M\to\mathbb R$ which is continuous ([[def-continuity-real]]) and satisfies
$u(\gamma(a))<u(\gamma(b))$ for every future causal curve
$\gamma:[a,b]\to M$ with $a<b$ that is not constant on $[a,b]$.

**(e) Temporal functions.** A **temporal function** on $(M,g,N)$ is a smooth
function $u:M\to\mathbb R$ ([[def-c-r-and-smooth-maps-between-smooth-manifolds]])
whose gradient is past-directed timelike: writing $du$ for its differential
([[def-differential-of-a-smooth-map]]) and $\nabla u$ for the vector field
with $g(\nabla u,X)=du(X)$ for every smooth vector field $X$, constructed from
the inverse metric of [[def-gr-inverse-metric-and-musical-isomorphisms]], one
has
$$g(\nabla u,\nabla u)<0\qquad\text{and}\qquad du(N)=g(\nabla u,N)>0 .$$
The first inequality says that the gradient is nowhere zero and timelike; the
second fixes it as past-directed for the chosen time orientation. No level set
of a temporal function is asserted here to be a hypersurface or to be
spacelike, and no relation between temporal functions and compactness is
asserted; those claims are proved separately where they are used.

**(f) Scope and conventions.** These are mathematical conditions on the
Lorentzian model. Different sources define global hyperbolicity by equivalent
but differently phrased conditions; this page fixes the formulation of (c)
and proves the implications it uses. In particular no equivalence between
global hyperbolicity and the existence of a Cauchy hypersurface is asserted
by this definition, and the physical reading of strong causality or global
hyperbolicity as predictability of a classical model is adopted only in the
framework postulates.

def-gr-curvature-tensor-and-derived-tensors
Let $(M,g,N)$ be a time-oriented smooth Lorentzian four-manifold
([[def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects]]) with metric-selected connection
$\nabla$ ([[def-gr-metric-selected-connection]]), whose existence and
uniqueness are supplied by
[[thm-gr-nondegenerate-levi-civita-and-tensor-derivative]]; that theorem also
extends $\nabla$ to a derivative on tensor fields commuting with contractions
and annihilating $g$ and $g^{-1}$. Let $U\subseteq M$ be open and let
$X,Y,Z$ be smooth vector fields on $U$; brackets are those of
[[def-lie-bracket-of-smooth-vector-fields]], and contractions those of
[[def-contraction-of-a-mixed-tensor]].

**(a) The curvature operator.** The **curvature operator** of $\nabla$ is the
assignment

$$R(X,Y)Z:=\nabla_X\nabla_YZ-\nabla_Y\nabla_XZ-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z .$$

The right-hand side is a smooth vector field on $U$: $\nabla_YZ$ is smooth
because $\nabla$ is an affine connection
([[def-affine-connection-on-a-smooth-manifold]]), $[X,Y]$ is smooth, and
$\nabla_X(\cdot)$ and $\nabla_{[X,Y]}(\cdot)$ preserve smoothness. The
assignment is defined for the given smooth fields; that its value at a point
depends only on the values of $X,Y,Z$ at that point, so that $R$ is a smooth
tensor field of type $(1,3)$, is the content of
[[thm-gr-curvature-tensoriality-and-coordinate-formula]] clause (a), and
likewise the algebraic symmetries used below are proved in
[[thm-gr-curvature-algebraic-symmetries]].

**(b) Component functions.** Let $(V,x)$ be a smooth chart of $M$ and let
$\Gamma^a{}_{bc}$ be the Christoffel symbols of $\nabla$ in this chart,
characterized by $\nabla_{\partial_b}\partial_c=\sum_a\Gamma^a{}_{bc}\partial_a$
([[def-christoffel-symbols-of-an-affine-connection]]); the coordinate fields
commute, so $\nabla_{\partial_c}\partial_d$ and $\nabla_{\partial_d}\partial_c$
are obtained from the same symbols. The **components** of the curvature in the
chart are the smooth functions $R^a{}_{bcd}$ on $V$ defined by

$$R(\partial_c,\partial_d)\partial_b=\sum_a R^a{}_{bcd}\,\partial_a .$$

The first index is the output slot, the second is the differentiated slot and
the last two are the two directions of the loop; indices are $a,b,c,d=0,1,2,3$
and the Einstein summation convention of
[[def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects]] applies.

**(c) Lowered tensor, Ricci tensor, scalar curvature, Einstein tensor.** On
the chart domain define, using the inverse metric
$g^{ab}$ of [[def-gr-inverse-metric-and-musical-isomorphisms]],

$$R_{abcd}:=\sum_e g_{ae}R^e{}_{bcd},\qquad \operatorname{Ric}_{bd}:=\sum_{a,c}g^{ac}R_{abcd},\qquad S:=\sum_{b,d}g^{bd}\operatorname{Ric}_{bd},\qquad G_{ab}:=\operatorname{Ric}_{ab}-\tfrac12 S\,g_{ab}.$$

The fields $R_{abcd}$, $\operatorname{Ric}_{ab}$, $S$ and $G_{ab}$ are called
the **Riemann tensor with lowered indices**, the **Ricci tensor**, the
**scalar curvature** and the **Einstein tensor** of the metric-selected
connection. That $R_{abcd}$ are the components of a smooth $(0,4)$-tensor
field and that $\operatorname{Ric}_{ab}$ are the components of a smooth
symmetric $(0,2)$-tensor field, so that the contractions above are
basis-independent and do not depend on the chart, is proved in
[[thm-gr-curvature-algebraic-symmetries]] clauses (a)-(d); the Ricci symmetry
is part of that statement.

def-gr-inverse-metric-and-musical-isomorphisms
Let $(M,g,N)$ be a time-oriented smooth Lorentzian four-manifold and let
$p\in M$ ([[def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects]]). The cotangent space $T_p^*M$ is
the algebraic dual of $T_pM$ ([[def-algebraic-dual-and-linear-functional]]).

**Musical isomorphisms.** Define the **flat map**

$$\flat_p:T_pM\longrightarrow T_p^*M,\qquad \flat_p(v):=g_p(v,\cdot),$$

so that $\flat_p(v)(w)=g_p(v,w)$; it is $\mathbb R$-linear by bilinearity of
$g_p$. Its kernel is $\{v:g_p(v,w)=0\text{ for all }w\}=\{0\}$ because $g_p$ is
nondegenerate, and $\dim_{\mathbb R}T_pM=\dim_{\mathbb R}T_p^*M=4$ since the
coordinate derivations of a chart form a basis of $T_pM$
([[thm-coordinate-derivations-form-a-basis-of-the-tangent-space]]); hence
$\flat_p$ is a linear isomorphism and has an inverse
$\sharp_p:T_p^*M\to T_pM$.

**Inverse metric.** The **inverse metric** at $p$ is the symmetric bilinear form

$$g_p^{-1}:T_p^*M\times T_p^*M\longrightarrow\mathbb R,\qquad g_p^{-1}(\alpha,\beta):=g_p(\sharp_p\alpha,\sharp_p\beta).$$

It is symmetric because $g_p$ is, and it is nondegenerate: if
$g_p^{-1}(\alpha,\beta)=0$ for all $\beta$, then $g_p(\sharp_p\alpha,\cdot)=0$,
so $\sharp_p\alpha=0$ and hence $\alpha=0$.

**Components in a chart.** Let $(U,x)$ be a smooth chart and
$g_{ab}(q)=g_q(\partial_a|_q,\partial_b|_q)$ the components of $g$; let
$dx^a|_q$ be the differential of the coordinate function $x^a$ at $q$
([[def-differential-of-a-smooth-map]]), so that
$dx^a|_q(\partial_b|_q)=\partial_b|_q(x^a)=\delta^a{}_b$. Define the components
of the inverse metric in this chart by

$$g^{ab}(q):=g_q^{-1}\bigl(dx^a|_q,\,dx^b|_q\bigr),\qquad q\in U,$$

so that $\sharp_q(dx^a|_q)=\sum_bg^{ab}(q)\,\partial_b|_q$; equivalently
$(g^{ab}(q))$ is the inverse matrix of $(g_{ab}(q))$ at every $q$, that is

$$g^{ac}(q)\,g_{cb}(q)=\delta^a{}_b=\delta^c{}_bg^{ab}(q),$$

because $g_q(\sharp_qdx^a|_q,\partial_c|_q)=dx^a|_q(\partial_c|_q)=\delta^a{}_c$
and also $g_q(\sharp_qdx^a|_q,\partial_c|_q)=\sum_bg^{ab}(q)g_{bc}(q)$. In
particular $g^{ab}=g^{ba}$. The components $g^{ab}$ depend smoothly on $q$;
this is proved in [[lem-gr-inverse-metric-is-smooth]].

**Raising, lowering and metric trace.** Let $A$ be a smooth symmetric covariant
$2$-tensor field on an open set $V\subseteq M$
([[def-gr-smooth-covariant-two-tensor-field]]). At each $p\in V$ define the
endomorphism

$$A^\sharp_p:T_pM\to T_pM,\qquad A^\sharp_p(v):=\sharp_p\bigl(A_p(v,\cdot)\bigr),$$

characterized by $g_p(A^\sharp_p(v),w)=A_p(v,w)$ for all $w\in T_pM$; it is
self-adjoint for $g_p$ because $A_p$ is symmetric. Its **metric trace** is

$$\operatorname{tr}_g(A)(p):=\operatorname{tr}\bigl(A^\sharp_p\bigr)$$

with the trace of an endomorphism as in [[def-trace-of-an-endomorphism]]. In a
chart the raised, mixed and doubly raised components are

$$A^{ab}:=g^{ac}g^{bd}A_{cd},\qquad A^a{}_b:=g^{ac}A_{cb},\qquad\text{and}\qquad \operatorname{tr}_g(A)=g^{ab}A_{ab},$$

the last identity obtained by evaluating in a $g_p$-orthonormal basis
$(e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3)$, where $g_p(e_a,e_b)=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)_{ab}$ by
[[lem-timelike-cone-has-two-convex-components]] so that
$g^{ab}A_{ab}=\sum_a g^{aa}A_{aa}=-A(e_0,e_0)+A(e_1,e_1)+A(e_2,e_2)+A(e_3,e_3)$,
which is the trace of the endomorphism $v\mapsto\sharp_p(A_p(v,\cdot))$ in that
basis. All these operations are pointwise and depend only on $g$, $A$ and the
duality pairing, not on a chart.

**Conventions.** Raising and lowering apply to one index at a time and preserve
index order; $A_{ab}$, $A^a{}_b$ and $A^{ab}$ denote the same tensor $A$ with
indices positioned by $g$. The musical isomorphisms and the inverse metric are
defined for every smooth Lorentzian four-manifold of this library; no choice
principle is used in this definition, every ingredient being pointwise linear
algebra over $\mathbb R$ together with the pointwise nondegeneracy of $g_p$.

def-gr-metric-selected-connection
Let $(M,g,N)$ be a time-oriented smooth Lorentzian four-manifold
([[def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects]]) and let $\nabla$ be an affine connection
on $M$ ([[def-affine-connection-on-a-smooth-manifold]]). Smooth vector fields
are smooth sections of the tangent bundle
([[def-smooth-vector-field-as-a-tangent-bundle-section]]); as there, their use
rests on the canonical smooth structure of $TM$, which assumes the axiom
$\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ of [[def-countable-choice]]. This is the only use of that
axiom in this definition.

**(a) Metric compatibility.** The connection $\nabla$ is **$g$-compatible**, or
**compatible with the metric** $g$, when for all smooth vector fields $X,Y,Z$ on
$M$ the identity of smooth functions

$$X\bigl(g(Y,Z)\bigr)=g(\nabla_XY,Z)+g(Y,\nabla_XZ)$$

holds on $M$. Here $g(Y,Z)$ is the smooth function $p\mapsto g_p(Y_p,Z_p)$ and
$X$ acts on functions as a derivation; the identity uses only the action of
$\nabla$ on vector fields and requires no extension of $\nabla$ to tensor
fields. When such an extension is available, this identity is the statement
$\nabla_Xg=0$, and the compatibility of $\nabla$ with the inverse metric
$g^{-1}$ follows; neither statement is part of this definition.

**(b) Torsion freedom.** The **torsion** of $\nabla$ is the tangent-valued
$2$-tensor $T(X,Y)=\nabla_XY-\nabla_YX-[X,Y]$ of
[[def-torsion-tensor-of-an-affine-connection]], with bracket as in
[[def-lie-bracket-of-smooth-vector-fields]]. The connection is **torsion-free**
when $T(X,Y)=0$ for all smooth $X,Y$, that is, when
$\nabla_XY-\nabla_YX=[X,Y]$ identically.

**(c) Metric-selected connections.** A **metric-selected connection** for
$(M,g)$ is an affine connection on $M$ that is both torsion-free and
$g$-compatible. Equivalently, it is an element of the class

$$\operatorname{LC}(M,g):=\{\nabla:\nabla\text{ is an affine connection on }M,\ \nabla\text{ is torsion-free and }g\text{-compatible}\}.$$

The name records that the pair of conditions selects the connection out of all
affine connections; it is the pair of conditions imposed on the connection that
conventional general relativity uses, and the connection so selected is called
the **Levi–Civita connection** of $g$.

def-gr-observer-and-rest-space
Let $(M,g,N)$ be a time-oriented smooth Lorentzian four-manifold and let
$p\in M$ ([[def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects]]).

**(a) Observers.** An **observer at $p$** is a tangent vector
$n\in T_pM$ such that $g_p(n,n)=-1$ and $n$ is future-directed in the sense of
[[def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects]], that is, $g_p(n,N_p)<0$. Because
$g_p(n,n)=-1\neq0$, an observer is timelike and in particular nonzero
([[def-causal-classification-of-lorentz-vectors]]). Observers at $p$ exist:
the vector $N_p$ is timelike, and
$n:=N_p/\sqrt{-g_p(N_p,N_p)}$ satisfies $g_p(n,n)=-1$ and
$g_p(n,N_p)=-\sqrt{-g_p(N_p,N_p)}<0$, where the square root is the
nonnegative root of [[def-rational-power]]; then $-n$ is a unit timelike
vector with $g_p(-n,N_p)>0$, so it is not an observer, and the two vectors
$n,-n$ point in opposite time directions.

**(b) Rest space.** For an observer $n$ at $p$, the **rest space** of $n$ is
the $g_p$-orthogonal complement of the line $\mathbb Rn$,

$$n^{\perp}=\{v\in T_pM: g_p(v,n)=0\},$$

in the sense of [[def-lorentz-orthogonality-and-orthogonal-complement]]. It is
a linear subspace of $T_pM$. The further facts that $\dim n^{\perp}=3$ and
that the restriction of $g_p$ to $n^{\perp}$ is positive definite are proved
in [[lem-gr-observer-projection-decomposition]]; they are consequences, not
part of the definition.

**(c) Spatial projection.** The **spatial projection** of the observer $n$ is
the map

$$h_n:T_pM\longrightarrow T_pM,\qquad h_n(v):=v+g_p(n,v)\,n .$$

The map is linear because $g_p$ is bilinear, its image is contained in
$n^{\perp}$, and it fixes $n^{\perp}$ pointwise; that it is the
$g_p$-orthogonal projection onto $n^{\perp}$ with kernel $\mathbb Rn$ is
proved in [[lem-gr-observer-projection-decomposition]].

**(d) Scope.** An observer at $p$ is a single tangent vector. It is not an
ordered basis or frame of $T_pM$, not a chart, not a field of observers such as
the congruences defined later on this page, and not a curve or a worldline: no
parametrized curve, clock reading, proper time or measurement is attached to
$n$ by this definition. The physical reading of an observer as the direction
of an ideal clock's worldline, and the measurement interpretation of the
observer-relative quantities introduced later on this page, belong to the
physical postulates and unit conventions of the framework and are not asserted
here.

def-gr-perfect-fluid-matter-model
Let $(M,g,N)$ be a time-oriented smooth Lorentzian four-manifold with
metric-selected connection $\nabla$ ([[def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects]],
[[def-gr-metric-selected-connection]],
[[thm-gr-nondegenerate-levi-civita-and-tensor-derivative]]) and let
$U\subseteq M$ be a nonempty open matter domain
([[prop-an-open-subset-of-a-smooth-manifold-has-a-canonical-restricted-smooth-structure]]).

**(a) Data.** A **perfect-fluid model** on $U$ is a triple $(\varepsilon,p,n)$
consisting of two smooth functions $\varepsilon,p:U\to\mathbb R$, the **rest
energy density** and the **pressure**, and a smooth future-directed unit
timelike vector field $n$ on $U$ with $g_p(n_p,n_p)=-1$ and
$g_p(n_p,N_p)<0$ at every $p$ ([[def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects]],
[[def-c-r-and-smooth-maps-between-smooth-manifolds]]). No positivity of
$\varepsilon$ or of $p$ is required or implied. A model may carry the
additional data of an **equation of state**: a function
$P:D_P\to\mathbb R$ on a subset $D_P\subseteq\mathbb R$ with
$\varepsilon(U)\subseteq D_P$, together with the relation $p=P(\varepsilon)$;
the relation is a further assumption and is not part of the basic triple.

**(b) Stress-energy tensor and derived fields.** The model's
**stress-energy tensor** is

$$T^{ab}:=(\varepsilon+p)n^an^b+p\,g^{ab},$$

a smooth symmetric contravariant $2$-tensor field ([[def-gr-smooth-covariant-two-tensor-field]],
[[def-gr-inverse-metric-and-musical-isomorphisms]]). The **comoving
derivative**, **expansion** and **acceleration** of $n$ are

$$D f:=n^a\partial_af\quad(f\in C^\infty(U)),\qquad \vartheta:=\nabla_an^a,\qquad a^b:=n^a\nabla_an^b ,$$

and the **rest-space projection** is the smooth tensor field

$$h^{ab}:=g^{ab}+n^an^b .$$

The physical reading of $\varepsilon$ and $p$ as a rest-frame energy density
and pressure, and their units, are fixed by the framework's matter postulate,
not by this definition.

**(c) Fluid equations.** The **perfect-fluid equations** on $U$ are

$$D\varepsilon+(\varepsilon+p)\vartheta=0,\qquad (\varepsilon+p)a^b+h^{ba}\partial_ap=0 .$$

That the stress-energy tensor is divergence-free if and only if these two
equations hold is proved in
[[prop-gr-dust-and-perfect-fluid-conservation-equations]]; the definition
asserts neither divergence-freedom nor the equations.

def-gr-smooth-covariant-two-tensor-field
Let $M$ be a smooth $n$-manifold ([[def-smooth-manifold]]), with smooth atlas
$\mathcal A$ and maximal smooth atlas $[\mathcal A]$
([[def-smooth-atlas]], [[def-smoothly-compatible-charts]]). For $p\in M$ the
tangent space $T_pM$ is the real vector space of derivations at $p$
([[def-derivation-at-a-point-and-tangent-space]]), and the cotangent space is its
algebraic dual $T_p^*M:=(T_pM)^*$
([[def-cotangent-space-and-cotangent-bundle-as-a-disjoint-union]]).

A **covariant $2$-tensor at $p$** is a bilinear map
$A_p:T_pM\times T_pM\to\mathbb R$. It is **symmetric** when
$A_p(v,w)=A_p(w,v)$ for all $v,w\in T_pM$, and **nondegenerate** when the only
$v\in T_pM$ with $A_p(v,w)=0$ for all $w\in T_pM$ is $v=0$.

A **smooth covariant $2$-tensor field on $M$** is a family $A=(A_p)_{p\in M}$
assigning to each $p$ a covariant $2$-tensor $A_p$ at $p$, such that for every
chart $(U,x)$ of the maximal smooth atlas $[\mathcal A]$ the **component
functions**

$$A_{ab}(q):=A_q\!\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^a}\Big|_q,\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x^b}\Big|_q\right),\qquad q\in U,$$

are $C^\infty$ on $U$. Here $\partial/\partial x^a|_q$ is the coordinate
derivation at $q$ ([[def-derivation-at-a-point-and-tangent-space]],
[[thm-coordinate-derivations-form-a-basis-of-the-tangent-space]]), the index
ranges over $a,b\in\{1,\dots,n\}$, and smoothness of the real-valued functions
$A_{ab}$ on the open set $U\subseteq M$ is the notion of
[[def-c-r-and-smooth-maps-between-smooth-manifolds]] applied to $M$ and
$\mathbb R$.

Because the coordinate derivations at $q$ form a basis of $T_qM$
([[thm-coordinate-derivations-form-a-basis-of-the-tangent-space]]), specifying
$A$ is the same as specifying the matrix of component functions in each chart;
[[lem-gr-covariant-two-tensor-transformation-law]] proves that the smoothness
requirement is independent of the chart used, so that the condition may be
checked on any smooth atlas of $M$, and derives the transformation law relating
components in two overlapping charts.

The field is **symmetric** when $A_p$ is symmetric for every $p\in M$, which in
a chart is the identity $A_{ab}=A_{ba}$ of component functions; it is
**nondegenerate** when $A_p$ is nondegenerate for every $p$, which in a chart is
the nonvanishing at every point of the polynomial function
$\det(A_{ab})$ of the components. The **matrix** of $A$ in a chart means the
matrix $\bigl(A_{ab}(q)\bigr)$ of its component functions at $q$.

**Convention.** In the four-dimensional case $n=4$ used in this library, indices
are $a,b,c,d=0,1,2,3$ with $0$ the distinguished time index, and we write
$A_{ab}$ for the components. A covariant $2$-tensor field is also called a
**$(0,2)$ tensor field**.

def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects
Let $M$ be a smooth four-manifold without boundary, so that $M$ is Hausdorff
and second countable and carries a maximal smooth atlas
([[def-smooth-manifold]]). Let $g$ be a smooth covariant $2$-tensor field on $M$
in the sense of [[def-gr-smooth-covariant-two-tensor-field]]. Then $g$ assigns
to every $p\in M$ a bilinear form $g_p:T_pM\times T_pM\to\mathbb R$
([[def-derivation-at-a-point-and-tangent-space]]).

**(a) Lorentzian metric.** The field $g$ is a **Lorentzian metric of signature
$(-,+,+,+)$** on $M$ when it is symmetric and nondegenerate, and at every
$p\in M$ the pair $(T_pM,g_p)$ is a Lorentz vector space of signature
$(-,+,+,+)$ in the sense of [[def-lorentz-form-and-inertia]]: that is, $g_p$ has
inertia $(3,1,0)$, so that some — equivalently every — basis of $T_pM$ in which
the matrix of $g_p$ is diagonal has exactly one negative and three positive
diagonal entries. The pair $(M,g)$ is a **smooth Lorentzian four-manifold** (or
Lorentzian manifold of signature $(-,+,+,+)$). In a smooth chart $(U,x)$ the
**components** of $g$ are the $C^\infty$ functions
$g_{ab}(q)=g_q(\partial_a|_q,\partial_b|_q)$ of
[[def-gr-smooth-covariant-two-tensor-field]], and $g_{ab}=g_{ba}$ by symmetry.
We use indices $a,b,c,d=0,1,2,3$ and the Einstein summation convention, with
components in a chart written as lower-case indexed symbols and equations
between indexed fields understood basis-independently. These conventions are
fixed once for this library and the physical reading of the symbols is recorded
with the framework postulates, separately from this mathematical definition.

**(b) Pointwise causal structure.** For every $p\in M$ the vocabulary of
[[def-causal-classification-of-lorentz-vectors]] applies to the Lorentz vector
space $(T_pM,g_p)$: a tangent vector $v\in T_pM$ is **timelike** when
$g_p(v,v)<0$, **null** when $v\neq0$ and $g_p(v,v)=0$, **spacelike** when
$g_p(v,v)>0$, and **causal** when it is timelike or null. Every nonzero
$v\in T_pM$ has exactly one causal character. The **timelike set**
$T_p:=\{v\in T_pM:g_p(v,v)<0\}$ has exactly two connected components, each
nonempty, open, convex and path-connected; each $g_p$-orthonormal basis
$e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3$ of $T_pM$ satisfies $g_p(e_a,e_b)=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)_{ab}$
for some ordering, so in particular nonzero orthonormal vectors exist at every
point ([[lem-lorentz-form-has-orthonormal-basis]],
[[lem-timelike-cone-has-two-convex-components]]).

**(c) Time orientation.** A **time orientation** of $(M,g)$, also called a
**choice of future cone**, is a smooth vector field $N$ on $M$
([[def-smooth-vector-field-as-a-tangent-bundle-section]], whose tangent-bundle
smooth structure uses the axiom $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ of
[[def-countable-choice]]; this is the only use of that axiom in this
definition) such that

$$g_p(N_p,N_p)<0\qquad\text{for every }p\in M .$$

The function $p\mapsto g_p(N_p,N_p)$ is $C^\infty$ because $g$ is a smooth
covariant $2$-tensor field and $N$ a smooth field, so the condition is a
pointwise strict inequality between a smooth function and $0$; since
$g_p(N_p,N_p)<0$ implies $N_p\neq0$, a time orientation is nowhere vanishing.
A **time-oriented smooth Lorentzian four-manifold** is a triple $(M,g,N)$
consisting of a smooth Lorentzian four-manifold and a time orientation $N$.

Given a time orientation $N$ and $p\in M$, the vector $N_p$ is timelike, and we
**define** a nonzero causal vector $v\in T_pM$ to be **future-directed** when
$g_p(v,N_p)<0$ and **past-directed** when $g_p(v,N_p)>0$. The classification of
nonzero causal vectors into exactly these two classes, and its agreement with
the two components of the timelike set (the future component is the component
$C_p^+$ containing $N_p$, and the future closed null cone is
$\operatorname{cl}(C_p^+)\setminus\{0\}$), is proved in
[[lem-gr-future-causal-classification-by-a-timelike-field]]; in particular no
nonzero causal $v$ satisfies $g_p(v,N_p)=0$, so the definition is exhaustive and
mutually exclusive. Replacing $N$ by $-N$ reverses every future/past label, and
two time orientations $N,N'$ determine the same future-directed causal vectors
at every point where $g_p(N_p,N'_p)<0$, while they determine opposite ones
where $g_p(N_p,N'_p)>0$, by
[[lem-gr-timelike-fields-define-equal-or-opposite-future-cones]]; that lemma
also shows the sign of $p\mapsto g_p(N_p,N'_p)$ is constant on every connected
component of $M$.

**(d) The inverse metric.** Because $g_p$ is nondegenerate, it induces at each
$p$ an isomorphism $\flat_p:T_pM\to T_p^*M$, $v\mapsto g_p(v,\cdot)$, and an
inverse metric $g_p^{-1}$ on the dual space; the inverse metric, the musical
isomorphisms and the components $g^{ab}$ are defined separately on this page,
where the smoothness of those components is also proved. Neither construction
uses any choice principle beyond the one already recorded in (c).

**Convention and scope.** The physical reading of a smooth time-oriented
Lorentzian four-manifold as a classical spacetime model, and the domain in which
that reading is adopted, are recorded in the framework postulate
`post-gr-spacetime-and-classical-domain`; the words spacetime and event carry no
mathematical content beyond this definition, and this item asserts no claim
about nature. The dimension $4$ and the absence of boundary are part of the
data; nothing in the definition assumes that $M$ is nonempty or connected, and
no completeness, extendibility or maximality property of $(M,g)$ is asserted.

def-gr-spacelike-hypersurfaces-and-cauchy-sets
Let $(M,g,N)$ be a time-oriented smooth Lorentzian four-manifold with the
causal curves of [[def-gr-causal-curves-and-reachability]], and let
$S\subseteq M$.

**(a) Spacelike hypersurfaces.** A subset $\Sigma\subseteq M$ is a **spacelike
embedded hypersurface** when it is an embedded three-dimensional submanifold
of $M$ ([[def-embedded-submanifold-and-slice-chart]]) such that the
restriction of $g_p$ to $T_p\Sigma\times T_p\Sigma$ is positive definite for
every $p\in\Sigma$; equivalently, every nonzero vector tangent to $\Sigma$ is
spacelike ([[def-causal-classification-of-lorentz-vectors]]). A spacelike
embedded hypersurface is automatically a nondegenerate hypersurface, so its
$g$-orthogonal complement in $T_pM$ is one-dimensional and nondegenerate, and
it carries a unique future-directed unit timelike normal; that normal is
constructed where it is used, not here.

**(b) Achronality and acausality.** $S$ is **achronal** when no two distinct
points $p,q\in S$ satisfy $p\ll q$ or $q\ll p$; $S$ is **acausal** when no two
distinct points $p,q\in S$ satisfy $p\le q$ or $q\le p$. Every acausal set is
achronal, since $p\ll q$ implies $p\le q$.

**(c) Cauchy sets.** $S$ is a **Cauchy set** when for every inextendible
causal curve $\gamma:I\to M$ the parameter set
$$\gamma^{-1}[S]:=\{t\in I:\gamma(t)\in S\}$$
has exactly one element. Because a strictly increasing continuous surjection
of intervals induces a bijection of parameter sets, this condition is
invariant under reparametrization
([[lem-gr-causal-curve-calculus]] clause (e)); it is stated in terms of
parameters rather than of the point set $\gamma(I)\cap S$, so that a closed
causal curve counts two visits to the same point as two parameter values. A
**Cauchy hypersurface** is a Cauchy set that is also a spacelike embedded
hypersurface in the sense of clause (a).

**(d) Domains of dependence.** For any $S\subseteq M$ define
$$D^+(S):=\{p\in M:\text{every past-inextendible causal curve through }p \text{ meets }S\},$$
and dually $D^-(S):=\{p\in M:\text{every future-inextendible causal curve
through }p\text{ meets }S\}$, where "meets $S$" means that some parameter
value is mapped into $S$, and $D(S):=D^+(S)\cup D^-(S)$ is the **domain of
dependence** of $S$.

**(e) Cauchy horizon.** For a closed set $S\subseteq M$ the **future Cauchy
horizon** is
$$H^+(S):=D^+(S)\setminus I^-(D^+(S)),$$
the set of points of $D^+(S)$ that are not chronologically preceded by any
point of $D^+(S)$; dually $H^-(S):=D^-(S)\setminus I^+(D^-(S))$, and the
**Cauchy horizon** is $H(S):=H^+(S)\cup H^-(S)$.

**(f) Scope.** These are mathematical definitions. Neither existence of
Cauchy sets nor any relation between the objects defined here is asserted.

def-gr-spacelike-initial-data-and-constraints
Let $(M,g,N)$ be a time-oriented smooth Lorentzian four-manifold with
metric-selected connection $\nabla$ and Einstein tensor $G$
([[def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects]], [[def-gr-metric-selected-connection]],
[[thm-gr-nondegenerate-levi-civita-and-tensor-derivative]],
[[def-gr-curvature-tensor-and-derived-tensors]]), let
$\Sigma\subseteq M$ be a spacelike embedded hypersurface
([[def-embedded-submanifold-and-slice-chart]],
[[def-gr-spacelike-hypersurfaces-and-cauchy-sets]] clause (a)), and fix real
constants $\kappa$ and $\Lambda$ (the framework coupling constants, purely
parameters for this definition).

**(a) The geometric data.**
For $p\in\Sigma$ the orthogonal complement $T_p\Sigma^{\perp}$ is
one-dimensional and nondegenerate
([[def-lorentz-orthogonality-and-orthogonal-complement]],
[[lem-lorentz-form-has-orthonormal-basis]]); there is therefore a unique
$n_p\in T_p\Sigma^{\perp}$ with $g_p(n_p,n_p)=-1$ and $g_p(n_p,N_p)<0$ and it
depends smoothly on $p$, the **future unit normal field** of $\Sigma$; this
and the properties of $h$ and $K$ below are proved, not assumed, in
[[lem-gr-spacelike-hypersurface-normal-and-second-fundamental-form]]. Define
on $\Sigma$ the **induced metric** $h$ and the **second fundamental form**
$K$ by
$$h_p(V,W):=g_p(V,W),\qquad K_p(V,W):=g_p(\nabla_Vn,W)\qquad(V,W\in T_p\Sigma),$$
where $\nabla_Vn$ is the covariant derivative at $p$ of the ambient field $n$
in the tangential direction $V$; $h$ is a smooth Riemannian metric on
$\Sigma$ and $K$ a smooth symmetric covariant $2$-tensor field on $\Sigma$
([[def-gr-smooth-covariant-two-tensor-field]]).

**(b) Matter data and constraints.** Let $T$ be a smooth symmetric covariant
$2$-tensor field on $M$ (the stress-energy field of a matter branch). Its
**matter data on $\Sigma$** are the smooth fields
$$\rho:=T(n,n),\qquad j(V):=-T(n,V)\quad(V\in T\Sigma).$$
The tuple $(\Sigma,h,K,\rho,j)$ **satisfies the constraint equations** for
$(\kappa,\Lambda)$ when
$$G(n,n)-\Lambda=\kappa\,\rho,\qquad G(n,V)=\kappa\,T(n,V)\quad\text{for every }V\in T_p\Sigma,\ p\in\Sigma .$$
The first is the normal projection of the equation $G+\Lambda g=\kappa T$
evaluated on $(n,n)$, namely $G(n,n)+\Lambda g(n,n)=\kappa T(n,n)$, with
$g(n,n)=-1$; the second is the normal-tangential projection, the term in
$\Lambda$ dropping because $g(n,V)=0$. That a solution of the field equation
satisfies these two projections is proved in
[[lem-gr-constraint-projections]]. The purely tangential projection
$G(V,W)+\Lambda h(V,W)=\kappa T(V,W)$ is not part of the constraints and is
not required here.

**(c) Developments.** A **development** of the data
$(\Sigma,h,K,\rho,j)$ is a time-oriented smooth Lorentzian four-manifold
$(M',g',N')$ together with a smooth embedding $\varphi:\Sigma\to M'$ whose
image is a spacelike embedded hypersurface with induced metric $\varphi^*g'=h$
and second fundamental form $K$ with respect to the future unit normal of
$\varphi(\Sigma)$, such that the matter fields of the model pull back to
$\rho$ and $j$. The development is a **globally hyperbolic development** when
$\varphi(\Sigma)$ is a Cauchy hypersurface of $(M',g',N')$, so that
$(M',g',N')$ is globally hyperbolic
([[def-gr-causal-conditions-and-global-hyperbolicity]],
[[def-gr-spacelike-hypersurfaces-and-cauchy-sets]] clause (c)).

**(d) Maximality.** A globally hyperbolic development $(M_*,g_*,N_*;
\varphi_*)$ is **maximal** for the data when for every globally hyperbolic
development $(M',g',N';\varphi')$ there is a smooth isometric embedding
$\psi:M'\to M_*$ with $\psi\circ\varphi'=\varphi_*$ and $\psi$ preserving the
time orientations and the matter fields. Thus maximality is uniqueness up to
data-fixing isometry among globally hyperbolic developments.

**(e) Scope.** The definition asserts neither existence nor uniqueness of
developments, and it does not assert that a development's matter fields solve
any equation: those claims require the local and global existence theory of
the Einstein-matter system and are not made on this page. The constraint
equations are displayed only as equations satisfied or not by given data.
The equivalent formulation of the constraints in terms of the induced scalar
curvature of $h$ and the divergence of $K$, and the existence theory for
solutions of the constraints, rest on the Gauss-Codazzi relations and on PDE
theory and are deferred to the later pages of this track; no such
equivalence is asserted here.

def-gr-tensor-fields-and-components
Let $(M,g,N)$ be a time-oriented smooth Lorentzian four-manifold
([[def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects]]) and let $r,s\ge0$ be integers.

**(a) Tensors at a point.** For $p\in M$, the tangent space $T_pM$ is the real
vector space of derivations at $p$
([[def-derivation-at-a-point-and-tangent-space]]) and the cotangent space is
its algebraic dual, $T_p^*M=(T_pM)^*$
([[def-cotangent-space-and-cotangent-bundle-as-a-disjoint-union]]). A **type
$(r,s)$ tensor at $p$** is a multilinear map

$$T_p:(T_p^*M)^r\times(T_pM)^s\longrightarrow\mathbb R,$$

in the sense of [[def-type-r-s-tensor-on-a-finite-dimensional-vector-space]]:
the first $r$ arguments are covectors and the last $s$ are vectors. In
particular a type $(1,0)$ tensor is a vector, a type $(0,1)$ tensor is a
covector and a type $(0,2)$ tensor is a bilinear form, so the covariant
$2$-tensors of [[def-gr-smooth-covariant-two-tensor-field]] are exactly the
type $(0,2)$ tensors of this definition.

**(b) The tensor bundle and smooth fields.** The **type $(r,s)$ tensor bundle**
$T^r_sM$ is the disjoint union of the fibres $T^r_sM|_p=\operatorname{Mult}((T_p^*M)^r\times(T_pM)^s,\mathbb R)$
over $p\in M$ ([[def-type-r-s-tensor-bundle]]); it carries the smooth
vector-bundle structure defined by the tensor-coordinate change rules
([[thm-tensor-transition-laws-define-a-smooth-vector-bundle]]). A **smooth
tensor field of type $(r,s)$** on $M$ is a family $T=(T_p)_{p\in M}$ assigning
to each $p$ a type $(r,s)$ tensor at $p$, such that for every smooth chart
$(U,x)$ of the maximal smooth atlas the **component functions**

$$T^{a_1\cdots a_r}{}_{b_1\cdots b_s}(q):=T_q\bigl(dx^{a_1}|_q,\dots,dx^{a_r}|_q,\ \partial_{b_1}|_q,\dots,\partial_{b_s}|_q\bigr),\qquad q\in U,$$

are $C^\infty$ on $U$; here the coordinate derivations
$\partial_b|_q=\partial/\partial x^b|_q$ form a basis of $T_qM$
([[thm-coordinate-derivations-form-a-basis-of-the-tangent-space]]) and the
coordinate differentials $dx^a|_q$ form the dual basis of $T_q^*M$
([[thm-coordinate-differentials-form-the-dual-cotangent-basis]]). This is
equivalent to $T$ being a smooth section of $T^r_sM$ in the sense of
[[def-smooth-tensor-field]]: the component functions are the frame
coefficients of the section in the local frame induced by the chart. The
smoothness condition is independent of the chosen smooth atlas and the
components in two overlapping charts are related by the transformation law
proved in the lemma on this page; the definition is therefore chart
independent rather than merely chart-wise.

**(c) Index conventions.** Indices are written $a,b,c,\dots$ for components in
a chart, with the Einstein summation convention: a repeated index, once upper
and once lower, is summed over $0,1,2,3$. An upper index is a contravariant
slot in which a covector is evaluated, a lower index a covariant slot in which
a vector is evaluated. Slots are moved between these positions with the
musical isomorphisms $\flat_p,\sharp_p$ of
[[def-gr-inverse-metric-and-musical-isomorphisms]]; for example, lowering the
first slot of a type $(1,1)$ field uses $(g^\flat T)^a{}_b=g_{ac}T^c{}_b$ and
raising uses $g^{ac}$. This is an identification of slots, not a change of the
field into a field of another kind, and it uses the metric nondegeneracy.

**(d) Scope.** The definition is purely local and chart-based; no global
triviality of $T^r_sM$, no global frame and no choice of metric-compatible
fields is assumed or implied. All fields are smooth ($C^\infty$); no
lower-regularity or distributional tensor field is defined here. The smooth
structure of the tangent bundle, and hence of $T^r_sM$
([[thm-tensor-transition-laws-define-a-smooth-vector-bundle]]), inherits the
axiom $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ of [[def-countable-choice]] recorded with
[[def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects]]; the component formulation of smoothness
itself makes no choice.

lem-gr-globally-hyperbolic-causal-structure
Assume the Axioms of Countable Choice and Dependent Choice
([[def-countable-choice]], [[def-dependent-choice]]). Let $(M,g,N)$ be a
time-oriented smooth Lorentzian four-manifold that is globally hyperbolic in
the sense of [[def-gr-causal-conditions-and-global-hyperbolicity]] clause
(c): strongly causal and with compact causal diamonds
$J^+(p)\cap J^-(q)$ for all $p,q\in M$. Let $h$ be the auxiliary metric of
[[thm-gr-auxiliary-complete-riemannian-metric]] and let $\le$, $J^\pm$ be the
relations of [[def-gr-causal-curves-and-reachability]]. Then:

**(a)** the causal relation is closed: if $p_k\le q_k$ for all $k$ and
$p_k\to p$, $q_k\to q$, then $p\le q$; consequently $J^+(x)$ and $J^-(x)$
are closed for every $x$, and so are $J^+(K)$ and $J^-(K)$ for every compact
$K\subseteq M$;

**(b)** for all compact $K_1,K_2\subseteq M$ the set
$J^+(K_1)\cap J^-(K_2)$ is compact.

lem-gr-spacelike-hypersurface-normal-and-second-fundamental-form
Let $(M,g,N)$ be a time-oriented smooth Lorentzian four-manifold
([[def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects]]) and let $\Sigma\subseteq M$ be a
spacelike embedded hypersurface in the sense of
[[def-gr-spacelike-hypersurfaces-and-cauchy-sets]] clause (a), so that
$\Sigma$ is an embedded three-dimensional submanifold
([[def-embedded-submanifold-and-slice-chart]]) and the restriction of $g_p$
to $T_p\Sigma$ is positive definite for every $p\in\Sigma$
([[def-causal-classification-of-lorentz-vectors]]). In the notation of
[[def-gr-spacelike-initial-data-and-constraints]] clause (a), whose claims
this lemma establishes:

**(a) The future unit normal.** For every $p\in\Sigma$ the orthogonal
complement $T_p\Sigma^{\perp}$ is one-dimensional, nondegenerate for $g_p$,
and negative definite: every nonzero $u\in T_p\Sigma^{\perp}$ is timelike,
$g_p(u,u)<0$. Hence there is exactly one $n_p\in T_p\Sigma^{\perp}$ with
$g_p(n_p,n_p)=-1$ and $g_p(n_p,N_p)<0$.

**(b) Smoothness along the hypersurface.** The assignment $p\mapsto n_p$ is
smooth along $\Sigma$: for every $p\in\Sigma$ there are an open neighborhood
$O$ of $p$ in $M$ and a smooth vector field $\tilde n$ on $O$
([[def-smooth-vector-field-as-a-tangent-bundle-section]]) with
$\tilde n|_{\Sigma\cap O}=n|_{\Sigma\cap O}$, and for any two such local
extensions the covariant derivative at a point of $\Sigma$ in a direction
tangent to $\Sigma$ agrees. Thus for $V\in T_p\Sigma$ the vector
$\nabla_Vn\in T_pM$ is well defined as $\nabla_{\tilde X}\tilde n|_p$ for any
smooth extensions $\tilde n$ of $n$ and $\tilde X$ of a tangent field with
$\tilde X_p=V$ ([[def-covariant-derivative-along-a-curve]],
[[def-gr-metric-selected-connection]]).

**(c) The induced metric and second fundamental form.** The **induced
metric** $h_p(V,W):=g_p(V,W)$ is a smooth Riemannian metric on $\Sigma$, and
$$K_p(V,W):=g_p(\nabla_Vn,W)\qquad(V,W\in T_p\Sigma)$$
(the **second fundamental form**) defines a smooth symmetric covariant
$2$-tensor field $K$ on $\Sigma$
([[def-gr-smooth-covariant-two-tensor-field]]) with
$$K_p(V,W)=-g_p(\nabla_VW,n_p),$$
where $\nabla_VW$ is the ambient covariant derivative at $p$ of any smooth
extension of a tangent field with value $W$ at $p$, in the direction of any
extension with value $V$ at $p$.

**(d) The shape operator.** The endomorphism $S_p:T_p\Sigma\to T_p\Sigma$
defined by $h_p(S_pV,W)=K_p(V,W)$ for all $W\in T_p\Sigma$ is well defined,
linear and self-adjoint for the positive definite form $h_p$, and the
Weingarten relation $\nabla_Vn=S_pV$ holds for every $V\in T_p\Sigma$.

thm-gr-cauchy-surface-implies-globally-hyperbolic
Assume the Axioms of Countable Choice and Dependent Choice
([[def-countable-choice]], [[def-dependent-choice]]). Let $(M,g,N)$ be a
time-oriented smooth Lorentzian four-manifold
([[def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects]]) and suppose there exists a Cauchy set
$S\subseteq M$ ([[def-gr-spacelike-hypersurfaces-and-cauchy-sets]] clause
(c)): every inextendible causal curve meets $S$ in exactly one parameter
value. Then:

**(a)** there is no nonconstant future causal curve with equal endpoints
(so $(M,g,N)$ is causal and chronological in the sense of
[[def-gr-causal-conditions-and-global-hyperbolicity]] clause (a));

**(b)** $(M,g,N)$ is strongly causal (clause (b) of that definition);

**(c)** for all $p,q\in M$ the causal diamond
$J^+(p)\cap J^-(q)$ is compact; consequently $(M,g,N)$ is globally
hyperbolic in the sense of clause (c) of that definition.

thm-gr-nondegenerate-levi-civita-and-tensor-derivative
Let $M$ be a smooth four-manifold without boundary and let $g$ be a smooth
symmetric covariant $2$-tensor field on $M$ such that $g_p$ is nondegenerate at
every $p\in M$ ([[def-gr-smooth-covariant-two-tensor-field]],
[[def-gr-inverse-metric-and-musical-isomorphisms]]). This holds in particular
when $(M,g,N)$ is a time-oriented smooth Lorentzian four-manifold and $g$ is
its metric ([[def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects]]). For smooth vector fields
$X,Y,Z$ put

$$K(X,Y,Z):=X\bigl(g(Y,Z)\bigr)+Y\bigl(g(Z,X)\bigr)-Z\bigl(g(X,Y)\bigr)+g([X,Y],Z)+g([Z,X],Y)-g([Y,Z],X),$$

with brackets as in [[def-lie-bracket-of-smooth-vector-fields]].

**(a) Existence and formulas.** There is exactly one affine connection
$\nabla$ on $M$ ([[def-affine-connection-on-a-smooth-manifold]]) satisfying

$$2g(\nabla_XY,Z)=K(X,Y,Z)\qquad\text{for all smooth }X,Y,Z .$$

It is torsion-free and $g$-compatible, so it is a metric-selected connection
for $(M,g)$ ([[def-gr-metric-selected-connection]]); in every smooth chart its
Christoffel symbols are

$$\Gamma^a{}_{bc}=\tfrac12\,g^{ad}\bigl(\partial_bg_{dc}+\partial_cg_{bd}-\partial_dg_{bc}\bigr),$$

with $g^{ad}$ the inverse-metric components
([[def-gr-inverse-metric-and-musical-isomorphisms]],
[[def-christoffel-symbols-of-an-affine-connection]]).

**(b) Uniqueness.** If an affine connection $\nabla'$ on $M$ is torsion-free
and $g$-compatible, then $\nabla'=\nabla$. Consequently the class
$\operatorname{LC}(M,g)$ of metric-selected connections has exactly one
element; it is the **Levi–Civita connection** of $g$.

**(c) Induced derivative on tensor fields.** Let $\nabla^*$ be the dual
connection on $T^*M$ and let the product connection of
[[def-product-connection-on-tensor-and-hom-bundles]] act on the tensor bundles
of [[def-gr-tensor-fields-and-components]]. Then, in every smooth chart, for
every smooth tensor field $T$ of type $(r,s)$,

$$T^{a_1\cdots a_r}{}_{b_1\cdots b_s;c}=\partial_cT^{a_1\cdots a_r}{}_{b_1\cdots b_s}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\Gamma^{a_i}{}_{dc}\,T^{a_1\cdots d\cdots a_r}{}_{b_1\cdots b_s}-\sum_{j=1}^{s}\Gamma^{d}{}_{b_jc}\,T^{a_1\cdots a_r}{}_{b_1\cdots d\cdots b_s},$$

where the slot marked $d$ replaces the named index; this derivative commutes
with every contraction in the sense of [[def-contraction-of-a-mixed-tensor]],
and it annihilates the metric and the inverse metric:

$$g_{ab;c}=0,\qquad g^{ab}{}_{;c}=0 .$$
