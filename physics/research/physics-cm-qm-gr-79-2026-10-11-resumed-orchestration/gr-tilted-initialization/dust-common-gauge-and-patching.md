## Initial velocity without a pre-existing spacetime

The initial velocity is a smooth section of $\mathcal V=\mathbb RN\oplus T\Sigma$ with the Lorentz bundle metric $\gamma(aN+X,bN+Y)=ab-h(X,Y)$, positive $N$ coefficient, and $\gamma(U_0,U_0)=1$. Thus $U_0=W(N+v)$ with $h(v,v)<1$. It is not a tangent timelike vector field on the Riemannian manifold $\Sigma$, and no pre-existing ambient solution is assumed. For an embedding $i:\Sigma\to(M,g)$ in a development, identify $aN+X$ with $a n+i_*X$, where $n$ is its future $g$-unit normal. Matching initial velocity means $u\circ i$ equals this image of $U_0$.

## Direct dust uniqueness in a common gauge

Take two smooth full Einstein-dust developments, including prescribed geodesic velocity even where the density is zero. On each, flow the future unit velocity $u$ from the embedded slice. By `thm-fundamental-theorem-on-flows` the flow is smooth on an open domain, and by `thm-flowout-theorem` (Countable Choice) its restriction $F_A(s,p)=\Phi^A_s(i_A(p))$ is an embedding on an open neighborhood of $\{0\}\times\Sigma$. Transversality follows since $u$ is timelike and the slice spacelike. The parameter $s$ is unit proper length: $g(u,u)=-1$ and $dF_A(\partial_s)=u$; physical proper time is $s/c$. Intersect the two domains and shrink as needed. Coordinates on each fixed initial patch are the same labels $x(p)$ transported constantly along flow; they transform on overlaps by $(s,x)\mapsto(s,y(x))$, independent of $s$.

Choose one initial tetrad in $\mathcal V$ over the comparison patch, using the explicit boost to $U_0$, and place its image in each development. Extend it by $\nabla_u e_I=0$. Linear ODE uniqueness determines this extension. Its timelike member remains $u$, since $u$ is geodesic with the same initial value. Metric compatibility preserves orthonormality. The resulting tetrad is therefore comoving and parallel, with $e_0=\partial_s$ and $\Gamma_0{}^I{}_J=0$, in both developments. Smooth dependence follows from the same linear ODE argument in local coordinates; coefficients are smooth.

Pull back both solutions to $(s,p)$. Because they solve the full equations, their actual torsion, curvature and Bianchi residuals vanish, and their reduction variables satisfy the corrected reduced equations. Their initial reduction variables agree exactly: the frame coefficients come from the same bundle tetrad and initial chart, the connection from the tangential bundle connection plus parallel gauge, the density from $\varepsilon_0=\rho_0c^2$, and the electric/magnetic variables from the same full initial curvature construction. Both initial time matrices are positive since $|v|<1$. On a sufficiently small relatively compact comparison patch, coefficients, solution ranges and derivatives have finite bounds and the time matrix has a uniform positive lower bound. `thm-gr-local-symmetric-hyperbolic-well-posedness` and `lem-gr-finite-speed-independence-of-extension` then give equality of the two reduction solutions on an open cone neighborhood of every initial comparison point; compact subpatches admit positive uniform width after finite shrinking. This is genuine PDE uniqueness, not an identity-on-the-slice assertion. The equality of frame coefficients gives equality of the reconstructed metric; scalar density and $u=\partial_s$ agree as well. Consequently $F_2\circ F_1^{-1}$ is the required data-fixing future-preserving isometry between the resulting neighborhoods, matching all matter fields.

The density equation has $\varepsilon(s,p)=\varepsilon_0(p)\exp(-\int_0^s\chi(t,p)\,dt)$, so empty regions remain empty along their prescribed velocity flow. All comparison steps work when $\varepsilon_0=0$; none divide by density. The proof requires prescribed geodesic velocity in the vacuum branch, as stated by the dust consumer, rather than deriving that velocity from vanishing stress.

## Frame changes on construction overlaps

For local existence use independently chosen initial frames on coordinate patches. On a connected overlap, choose compatible local orientation and write $\widetilde e_a(0,p)=R_a{}^b(p)e_b(0,p)$ with $R(p)\in SO(3)$ and $\widetilde e_0=e_0=U_0$. Extend $R$ by $\partial_sR=0$. Then $\nabla_U\widetilde e_a=0$ whenever $\nabla_Ue_a=0$. Thus the same time-independent rotation relates the parallel gauges throughout the overlap. Together with the time-independent base-coordinate transition it transforms frame variables, connection and curvature to the other's common gauge. The connection transforms by the ordinary inhomogeneous gauge law: writing frame columns $\widetilde e=eR$, $\widetilde\omega=R^{-1}\omega R+R^{-1}dR$; its time component remains zero because $\partial_sR=0$. Curvature and Weyl transform tensorially. The reduced equations are their fixed tensor projections and Cartan transports, so these transformations preserve them. After this transformation both reduced solutions have identical initial variables, and the previous local uniqueness applies on a genuine open overlap.

No global orientation or global tetrad is required. On an orientation-reversing overlap the spatial transition is in $O(3)$; the locally chosen volume form and magnetic representative transform with the corresponding sign. Equivalently compare after choosing a common local orientation on that overlap, and glue the full curvature tensor and metric, which are ordinary tensors. The overlap proof uses proper frames locally but does not impose orientability of $\Sigma$. Coordinate and frame transition identities satisfy their usual cocycles before solving, so equalities of reconstructed tensors satisfy the cocycle automatically on triple overlaps. A data-fixing isometry is not asserted to be the identity in arbitrary unrelated original charts.

## Noncompact patching with positive variable width

For each sufficiently small initial coordinate patch the corrected symmetric hyperbolic existence/persistence theorem provides a local smooth solution. Form the cover by all such admissible patches, avoiding point-indexed arbitrary choices. Apply the already imported published `lem-every-open-cover-of-a-manifold-has-a-countable-cover-by-relatively-compact-coordinate-balls-subordinate-to-it` and `lem-a-countable-coordinate-ball-cover-has-a-countable-locally-finite-shrinking`. Their exact conclusions give a countable family of open sets $W_i$ covering $\Sigma$ and coordinate balls $V_i$, with $\overline{W_i}\subset V_i\subset\overline{V_i}\subset O_i$, each closure compact, and $(V_i)$ locally finite, where $O_i$ is a selected admissible solution patch. Countable Choice is inherited explicitly from these suppliers and the local PDE existence theorem; no stronger choice assumption is introduced. Empty $\Sigma$ has the empty solution domain.

Let $K_i=\overline{W_i}$. The family $(K_i)$ is locally finite since $K_i\subset V_i$. A compact $K_i$ therefore meets only finitely many $K_j$: a finite neighborhood subcover reduces local finiteness to a finite union of finite lists. Shrink the $i$th solution's time domain to some positive width $t_i$ over $K_i$, which exists by compactness and its open initial neighborhood. For every intersecting pair, $K_i\cap K_j$ is compact and contained in $O_i\cap O_j$. The common-gauge uniqueness above gives open equality neighborhoods over each point of this compact set; finitely many suffice. Their widths have a positive minimum $t_{ij}$, giving equality over $K_i\cap K_j$ for $|s|<t_{ij}$. Finite choice suffices within each pair, and Countable Choice selects these data for the countable family of pairs if needed. Set
$$\delta_i=\tfrac12\min\bigl(\{t_i\}\cup\{t_{ij}:K_i\cap K_j\ne\varnothing\}\bigr)>0.$$
The minimum is finite. Restrict the $i$th solution to $(-\delta_i,\delta_i)\times W_i$. Whenever two such tubes overlap, their time coordinate obeys $|s|<\min(\delta_i,\delta_j)<t_{ij}$, and their base point belongs to $K_i\cap K_j$; therefore their geometric tensors and matter fields agree there after the fixed transition. They glue on the literal open subset
$$\mathcal W=\bigcup_i(-\delta_i,\delta_i)\times W_i\ \subset\ \mathbb R\times\Sigma.$$
This uses an ordinary open product manifold, so no quotient separation issue occurs.

For an explicit smooth positive width, use the already imported `thm-smooth-partitions-of-unity-exist-on-manifolds` to choose a locally finite subordinate partition $(\psi_i)$ for $(W_i)$ and put
$$\alpha(p)=\tfrac12\sum_i\delta_i\psi_i(p)>0.$$
The sum is smooth by neighborhood local finiteness. At a fixed point choose a nonzero term having maximal $\delta_i$ among the finitely many nonzero terms. Then $p\in W_i$ and $\alpha(p)\le\delta_i/2<\delta_i$, so $\{|s|<\alpha(p)\}$ lies inside $\mathcal W$. Restrict the glued solution to that open variable-width collar. On noncompact data $\inf\alpha$ may be zero; no uniform slab is asserted or needed. Arbitrary smooth data, tilt and empty regions retain full scope. The positivity of $ds$ as a timelike conormal persists after further pointwise shrinking by continuity; the same partition construction realizes that shrink smoothly.
