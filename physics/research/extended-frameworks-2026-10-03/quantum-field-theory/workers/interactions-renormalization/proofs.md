## IR05G — General finite-scaling-degree extension, with the omitted test-space estimates supplied

Here t∈D'(R^n\{0}) is arbitrary, with finite real scaling degree δ as defined in IR05 using tests supported away from0. The theorem is that it has an extension T∈D'(R^n) of the same scaling degree; if δ<n it is unique, and if δ≥n its ambiguity at that degree is Σ_(|α|≤floor(δ−n))c_α∂^αδ₀. This is the precise point-extension theorem of the fully read Brunetti–Fredenhagen Theorems5.2/5.3. We supply its functional-analytic and decomposition details below, so it is not merely a citation or a claim about arbitrary products.

Fix compact K away from0 and E_K={φ∈C∞(R^n):suppφ⊂K}, with p_m(φ)=max_(|α|≤m)||∂^αφ||∞. The metric Σ_(m≥0)2^−m p_m(φ−ψ)/(1+p_m(φ−ψ)) is complete: a metric-Cauchy sequence is uniformly Cauchy at every derivative order; its derivative limits are compatible by passing FTC on coordinate line segments through the uniform limits, hence define a smooth limit with supportK. The metric topology is precisely the seminorm topology (control a finite prefix then its geometric tail, and conversely each prefix is controlled by the metric). The exact published complete-metric Baire theorem assumes DC, supplied here by AC.

For every ω>δ the family L_ρ=ρ^ωt_ρ,0<ρ≤1, is pointwise bounded on E_K: it tends to0 at smallρ, while on compact positive ρ intervals test rescaling is continuous in E_K transported into a common compact subset of the punctured domain, so distribution continuity gives boundedness. The closed sets E_M={φ:sup_ρ|L_ρφ|≤M}, M∈N, cover E_K. Baire gives one with interior; some φ₀ plus a seminorm ball p_m(ψ)<ε lies there. Taking differences bounds |L_ρψ|≤2M on that ball. Scaling any ψ gives sup_ρ|L_ρψ|≤C p_m(ψ). Thus
|t_ρ(ψ)|≤C_ωρ^−ω p_m(ψ)
uniformly on a fixed annular test support. This proves the required uniform distribution order directly rather than assuming weak convergence controls moving tests.

Choose χ∈D,χ=1 near0, and f=χ−χ(2·), whose support is a fixed compact annulus. For δ<n set k=−1 and Wφ=φ. For δ≥n set k=floor(δ−n) and Wφ=φ−χΣ_(|α|≤k)x^α∂^αφ(0)/α!. Define
T(φ)=t((1−χ)Wφ)+Σ_(j≥0)t(f(2^j·)Wφ).
Every argument is compact and avoids0. The summands telescope in their cutoffs, so on tests supported away0 the sum is exactly t(φ). Put λ=2^−j. Changing the distributional scaling variable gives t(f(·/λ)Wφ)=λ^n t_λ(f Wφ(λ·)). All constants are controlled by finitely many test seminorms on a fixed compact support; outer terms obey an ordinary finite-order distribution bound. Hence T is a well-defined continuous distribution, independent of any nonunique polynomial ideal decomposition. On λ≤Cρ, scaled Taylor remainder bounds the annular seminorm by C_φρ^−n−k−1λ^(k+1) when k≥0, and by C_φρ^−n when k=−1 (derivative factors (λ/ρ)^r are uniformly bounded). The geometric tail is ≤C_φρ^−ω. On λ≥Cρ beyond the scaled test support, Wφ_ρ is only its subtracted jet; a degreej≤k term has seminorm ≤C_φρ^−n−jλ^j. Since ω>δ≥n+k, summing λ^(n+j−ω) from λ≈ρ to1 gives ≤C_φρ^−ω. The finitely many comparable annuli have the same bound. The fixed outer term is O(ρ^−n−k), also ≤Cρ^−ω. In the k=−1 case coarse annuli and the outer term vanish for smallρ, leaving only the fine tail. Therefore |T_ρφ|≤C_φρ^−ω, and ρ^ΩT_ρφ→0. Restriction to punctured tests gives sd(T)≥δ, so equality follows. This also explicitly closes the scaling-bound step in the general source proof.
