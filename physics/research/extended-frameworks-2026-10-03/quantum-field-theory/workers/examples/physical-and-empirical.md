## EXP01 — Fan et al reported electron magnetic-moment determination

### Setup

Primary report: X.Fan,T.G.Myers,B.A.D.Sukra,G.Gabrielse, *Measurement of the Electron Magnetic Moment*, arXiv:2209.13084v2 (7Dec2022), corresponding PRL130,071801(2023). The complete original six-page report was retrieved and read, including all procedure/uncertainty text and references. This item records that reported 2022 determination, not a claim that it is the latest measurement in2026. One electron is trapped in a cylindrical silver-electrode Penning trap in a very stable magnetic field, using a nominal50mK trap on a4.2K solenoid. At B≈5.3T the source reports trap-modified cyclotron≈149GHz, anomaly≈173MHz and axial≈114MHz frequencies; its magnetron motion is sideband cooled. The axial detection amplifier heats the axial motion to the source-reportedTz≈0.5K; the nominal50mK trap/cavity temperature is not the inferred axial-motion temperature. A magnetic bottle couples spin/cyclotron states to the axial detection frequency, and the trap cavity inhibits cyclotron spontaneous emission so excitations can be detected. These are reported conditions, not invented simulated apparatus.

### Procedure and analysis

The operational quantity is g_s/2=−μmag/μB,μB=eℏ/(2m), obtained from spin/cyclotron frequency ratios and anomalyνa=νs−νc. The ideal apparatus calibrationνc²=barνc²+barνz²+barνm² is justified within QX10's explicitly restricted stable linear quadrupole/uniform-field model; the actual measurement additionally uses the report's relativistic frequency-shift and cavity corrections, not merely that ideal invariant. Authors' Eq5 computes the corrected ratio from fitted barνa,barf_c,barνz and an added cavity shift.

The report alternates cyclotron and anomaly quantum-jump trials. It prepares a spin-up cyclotron ground state, applies magnetron cooling, drives cyclotron transitions for5s (less than20% excitation to avoid saturation) or anomaly transitions for30s, then detects axial-frequency shifts using self-excited-oscillator feedback. The source fits line shapes to infer frequencies and corrects magnetic-field drift using a quadratic fit. It describes an extra Gaussian convolution to accommodate unwanted cyclotron broadening; the unbroadened predicted cyclotron shape does not match observations. Anomaly broadening is less significant for the final ratio. These are source apparatus/analysis assumptions, not a reproduced likelihood calculation.

The largest correction is the cavity shift. Authors measure cavity-mode frequencies/Q values by three methods and use a renormalized cavity calculation, replacing ideal contributions for72 observed modes with contributions using measured frequencies/Q values. Imperfection/misalignment effects dominate its uncertainty. The eleven different-field determinations are cavity corrected and then averaged with weights. The ideal invariance, ratio-error and covariance-weighted-estimator mathematics are QX10/QX11; the paper does not publish the numerical covariance matrix/weights, raw trial dataset or complete fit likelihood, so our helper theorems do not independently reconstruct the quoted estimate or its statistical uncertainty.
