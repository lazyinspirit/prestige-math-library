# Exact BBGKY and a closed smooth mean-field limit

Every result here is mathematical on an explicitly specified model. Physical interpretation additionally adopts the finite-N nonrelativistic Hamiltonian and the prepared exchangeable probability law. This N^-1 interaction scaling is a mean-field model, not the hard-sphere Boltzmann–Grad scaling or a derivation for every gas.

## D6 — finite-N model, global flow and marginals

Let d≥1, q_i∈T_L^d=(R/LZ)^d, p_i∈R^d, m,L>0, and W∈C∞(T_L^d,R) be even. Position is in m, momentum kg m/s, m is kg and W is J. The primitive Hamiltonian is H_N=Σ_i |p_i|²/(2m)+(1/N)Σ_{i<j}W(q_i-q_j). Define K=-∇W (newtons), bounded and globally Lipschitz on the compact torus. Since W is even K is odd and K(0)=0. Equations are q'_i=p_i/m, p'_i=N^-1Σ_{j≠i}K(q_i-q_j). Each |p_i(t)-p_i(0)|≤∥K∥∞|t|. Thus finite-time states remain in a compact position/momentum subset; the local smooth ODE/continuation argument gives a unique complete flow for every finite N. Its Jacobian preserves canonical phase volume by D0. Permutations commute with the flow, so a symmetric initial law stays symmetric.

For a classical density use F_N(t,z_1,…,z_N)≥0 normalized with respect to product dq dp, initially smooth with all momentum derivatives rapidly decreasing uniformly in torus positions. Its transported density is a classical Liouville solution. This regular class is preserved on finite slabs: momentum changes are uniformly bounded, all spatial force derivatives are bounded, and the differentiated flow equations recursively bound each finite derivative by finite-slab linear integral bounds; initial rapid decay under bounded momentum shifts then bounds the transported derivatives. Define f_k=∫F_N dz_{k+1}…dz_N, k=1,…,N, as normalized probability marginals. Fast momentum tails and periodic positions justify every boundary integration below; compact momentum support is an even simpler allowed subclass.

## D7 — complete exact hierarchy and correlations

The N-particle transport equation is ∂tF_N+Σ_i(p_i/m)·∇_{q_i}F_N+(1/N)Σ_{i<j}K(q_i-q_j)·(∇_{p_i}-∇_{p_j})F_N=0. Integrate its last N-k variables. For each j>k its spatial derivative integrates to zero by periodicity, and its momentum derivative by decay. Pairs entirely beyond k vanish this way. Pairs within k yield their derivatives of f_k. Cross pairs retain the i≤k derivative; symmetry makes each of the N-k partners have the same (k+1)-marginal. Hence, exactly,

∂tf_k+Σ_{i≤k}(p_i/m)·∇_{q_i}f_k+(1/N)Σ_{i<j≤k}K(q_i-q_j)·(∇_{p_i}-∇_{p_j})f_k+((N-k)/N)Σ_{i≤k}∇_{p_i}·∫K(q_i-q_{k+1})f_{k+1}dz_{k+1}=0.

For k=N the last term is zero. No factorization is assumed in this BBGKY identity. It is reversible and retains all correlations; it supplies no entropy-increasing closed one-body equation by itself.

A product law is generally not preserved. For N=2 choose W nonconstant even, initial uniform q and centered identical Gaussian p distributions. Initially ∂tf_1=0: free advection of uniform q vanishes and ∫K(q-y)dy=0. But the internal pair contribution in ∂tF_2 is a nonzero multiple of K(q_1-q_2)·(p_1-p_2)F_2, since ∇p Gaussian=-a p Gaussian. It is nonzero on an open set when K is nonzero. Thus derivative of the joint law differs from derivative of f_1⊗f_1, and correlations develop. Instantaneous molecular factorization is not an exact finite-N Hamiltonian theorem.

## D8 — actual nonlinear mean-field measure existence

Choose momentum scale P0>0, time scale τ0=mL/P0, and dimensionless variables x=q/L∈T^d, v=p/P0, s=t/τ0. The dimensionless interaction is Φ(x)=mW(Lx)/P0², force k=-∇Φ, bounded by B and globally Lipschitz with constant A. The rescaled finite-N equations are x'=v,v'=N^-1Σ_j k(x_i-x_j). For a probability μ_s on Z=T^d×R^d with finite first velocity moment, define F[μ](x)=∫k(x-y)μ(dy,dw). The nonlinear characteristic equation is x'=v,v'=F[μ_s](x), with μ_s the pushforward of μ_0 by these same characteristics. Its weak equation is ∂sμ+v·∇xμ+F[μ]·∇vμ=0, meaning differentiation against smooth compact-velocity test functions gives ∫[v·∇xφ+F·∇vφ]dμ. This definition covers atomic laws without claiming a classical density.

Here is a complete construction, not a strategy citation. Lift x periodically to R^d and integrate initial μ0 over the fixed half-open fundamental cube; the chosen section is Borel. Write the position characteristic X(s,z)=x+sv+u(s,z). On 0≤s≤T use bounded continuous u(s,x,v), periodic in x, with u(0)=0, sup norm≤BT²/2. This is a complete metric space: uniform Cauchy sequences have unique uniform continuous bounded limits with the same conditions. Define

(Tu)(s,z)=∫_0^s(s-r)∫k(x+rv+u(r,z)-y-rw-u(r,z'))μ0(dz')dr.

Periodicity of k removes any lift ambiguity. The map has the stipulated bound by B, and dominated convergence gives joint continuity; in s its correction is uniformly Lipschitz by BT. The Lipschitz force gives ∥Tu-Tu'∥∞≤AT²∥u-u'∥∞. For AT²<1 the explicit iterates form a geometric Cauchy sequence, whose uniform limit is the unique fixed point. Define V=v+∂su and μ_s=(X mod Z^d,V)_#μ0. Then |V-v|≤Bs and the first moment stays finite. The integral equation is exactly the nonlinear characteristic equation; its force is continuous in time and smooth in x with bounded derivatives of every order, because each x derivative differentiates the bounded periodic k under the probability integral. State smoothness needs no unproved time-smoothness of a general measure curve: the Volterra difference-quotient argument gives the first state derivative as a linear variational equation with continuous coefficients. Inductively each higher state jet solves a linear equation with the same coefficient and finite forcing built from lower jets and bounded state derivatives of k; difference quotients and uniqueness identify it with the actual derivative. This is the explicit checked NR E2/repaired ODE jet argument applied only to state derivatives, and gives smooth-in-state characteristic maps on each common finite interval. The same time step works from any later initial law, so iteration constructs a global solution for finite times in both time directions. Tests have compact velocity support, making their differentiated characteristic integrands uniformly integrable; this proves the weak equation. For smooth initial densities with all velocity moments finite, their bounded velocity increments preserve all those moments. Differentiating force integrals in time introduces only finitely many velocity powers and bounded derivatives of k at each finite order, so dominated convergence inductively gives joint smooth time/space force. For smooth rapidly decreasing initial densities, the force field and the characteristic Jacobian have the required smooth/tail bounds, its divergence in (x,v) is zero, and the transported density gives the classical Vlasov equation. No singular Coulomb or gravitational force is covered by the bounded Lipschitz hypothesis.

## D9 — stability, finite-time limit and genuine propagation of chaos

Define distance on Z by d((x,v),(y,w))=dist_T(x,y)+|v-w|. For finite-first-moment probabilities let W1(μ,ν) be the infimum of ∫d dπ over all couplings π; the product coupling makes this finite. Transport a chosen initial coupling by both characteristic families. Denote its cost D(s). The torus distance has the integrated speed bound for paired curves (triangle inequality on short subintervals and periodic lifts), and the force difference satisfies

|F[μ](X)-F[ν](Y)|≤A dist_T(X,Y)+A∫dist_T(X',Y')dπ_s.

Consequently D(s)≤D(0)+(1+2A)∫_0^sD(r)dr. Iterating the integral inequality, whose n-fold integral is bounded by s^n/n!, gives D(s)≤e^((1+2A)s)D(0). Taking infima, or couplings within an arbitrary positive error of the infimum, proves W1(μ_s,ν_s)≤e^((1+2A)|s|)W1(μ0,ν0). W1=0 separates probabilities: bounded Lipschitz test differences are bounded by their Lipschitz constant times coupling cost. The functions max(0,1-n dist(z,F)) decrease to the indicator of any closed F, so dominated convergence gives equal masses on closed sets; the inspected π–λ uniqueness theorem then gives equality on their generated Borel sigma-algebra. This proves uniqueness of the characteristic solution and quantitative stability. The same conclusion covers narrowly continuous finite-first-moment weak solutions with the displayed test identity: for their own continuous force use the backward characteristic test φ_s=φ∘Φ_(T,s), whose velocity support stays compact by the force bound. Its time derivative plus transport generator is zero, so integrating the weak equation gives μ_T=(Φ_(T,0))#μ_0. The C1-time smooth-space test is legitimate by time mollification on compact support, with derivatives converging uniformly; an explicit smooth unit-integral bump and the FTC give that approximation. Thus these weak solutions are the characteristic solutions already compared.

The empirical μ_N(s)=N^-1Σ_iδ_(x_i(s),v_i(s)) exactly solves this mean-field characteristic equation, because k(0)=0 makes including the self index harmless. Thus any deterministic initial empirical laws with W1(μ_N(0),μ0)→0 converge uniformly on each finite slab to μ_s by the proved inequality. This is an actual particle-to-Vlasov limit, with its error constant and timescale stated.

For iid initial particles of compact phase support of diameter D, partition that support into M measurable cells of diameter at most δ. Match the common mass of μ_N and μ0 inside each cell; couple the remaining mass arbitrarily at cost≤D. This explicit coupling gives W1≤δ+(D/2)Σ_j|n_j/N-p_j|. Independence gives variance p_j(1-p_j)/N, hence E W1≤δ+(D/2)√(M/N) by Cauchy–Schwarz. Taking δ→0 after N→∞ proves convergence in mean and probability; for a bounded 2d-dimensional support M≤Cδ^-2d and δ=N^-1/(2d+2) give an explicit nonoptimal algebraic rate. These bounds propagate uniformly to each finite time slab via the stability estimate.

Almost-sure initial convergence is also proved without assuming an IID strong law. For centered cell indicators Y_i bounded by one, expansion using independence gives E(N^-1ΣY_i)^4≤4/N²: only single-index fourth moments and paired second moments survive. Markov's inequality bounds deviations by 4/(N²ε⁴). The sum is finite, so the probability of infinitely many deviations is zero by the tail union bound. Apply to finitely many cells for each of countably many partitions with δ→0 and rational ε. Then the preceding coupling bound gives almost-sure W1 convergence. The iid law itself is supplied by the inspected countable-product/Ionescu–Tulcea theorem under its explicit AC assumption.

Permutation-equivariant dynamics preserves exchangeability. For a bounded Lipschitz test ψ of k phase points, sampling k indices without replacement has law of (Z1,…,Zk); sampling with replacement has conditional law μ_N^⊗k. Their difference in expectation is bounded by 2∥ψ∥∞ times the repeated-index probability, at most k(k-1)/(2N). A k-fold product coupling bounds |∫ψdμ_N^⊗k-∫ψdμ_s^⊗k| by k Lip(ψ)W1(μ_N,μ_s). Expectations and the preceding estimate therefore prove every fixed-k joint law converges to μ_s^⊗k in this precise bounded-Lipschitz sense. This is proved finite-time propagation of chaos for this mean-field regime. It is not hard-sphere molecular chaos at collision boundaries, a hydrodynamic limit or a Lanford theorem.
