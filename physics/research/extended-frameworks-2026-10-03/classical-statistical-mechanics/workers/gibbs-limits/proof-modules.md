## G2. Countable product compactness and measure construction

Enumerate Z^d as i1,i2,… and give X metric d_X(σ,η)=Σ_n2^−n1_(σ_in≠η_in). Coordinatewise convergence is equivalent to this metric convergence: a common first K coordinates leave tail≤2^−K; conversely the n-th discrepancy forces distance≥2^−n. A sequence has a coordinatewise convergent subsequence by successively fixing a value repeated infinitely often at each coordinate and taking the diagonal subsequence. The resulting sequential compact metric space is compact: if an open cover had no finite subcover, the usual balls-of-radius1/n finite-net argument constructs a sequence with no convergent subsequence; finite nets exist because only finitely many initial-K coordinate assignments occur. This proves compactness directly, without uncountable product choice.

Cylinder sets depend on finitely many coordinates. Their finite unions form a countable algebra of clopen compact sets. A consistent family of finite-dimensional probabilities gives a well-defined finitely additive normalized weight m on this algebra, by passing any two cylinder presentations to the union of their finite coordinate sets. It is a premeasure: if a cylinder C is a disjoint countable union of cylinders C_n, the C_n are open and cover compact C, so finitely many cover it; disjointness makes every remaining C_n empty. Finite additivity therefore gives countable additivity whenever the union lies in the algebra. The fully read published `thm-caratheodory-extension-theorem` extends m to the generated σ-field, under its explicit countable-choice assumption. The fully read `thm-dynkin-pi-lambda` implies uniqueness: the sets on which two probabilities agree form a λ-system containing the cylinder π-system. The Borel σ-field equals the cylinder σ-field because cylinders form a countable topological basis. These two published mathematical prerequisites are the only general extension/uniqueness tools consumed here; their statements retain countable choice where required.

For a sequence of probabilities on X, diagonalize their probabilities on the countably many cylinders (bounded sequences in[0,1] admit convergent subsequences). The limits retain consistency/normalization, so the preceding construction supplies a unique probability μ. Thus cylinder convergence is sequentially compact. Every bounded local function is a finite linear combination of cylinder indicators. Every continuous f is uniformly approximable by local functions: otherwise pairs agreeing on growing coordinate prefixes with an f-gap≥ε would have convergent subsequences to the same point, contradicting continuity; fixing the outside prefix defines a local approximation. Consequently cylinder convergence is equivalent to convergence of integrals of all continuous functions. No continuum point-process compactness is inferred from finite alphabet compactness.

## G3. Lattice specifications, DLR identity, existence and stationary states

For finite Λ define γ_Λ(f|η)=Σ_(σΛ∈A^Λ) f(σΛηΛc)exp(−βE_Λ(σ|η))/Z_Λ^η for bounded measurable f. Positive finite normalization holds for every η. It is a probability kernel: each finite replacement map η→σΛηΛc is Borel, and each weight depends on finitely many boundary spins. For an exterior-measurable B, γ_Λ(1_B|η)=1_B(η); this is properness. For Δ⊂Λ, E_Λ−E_Δ contains only interactions not meeting Δ and is independent of σ_Δ. Conditioning the finite Λ sum on σ_(Λ\Δ) therefore gives exactly the Δ weights; summing proves γ_Λ γ_Δ=γ_Λ. Such a proper consistent family is a specification.

A DLR state is a probability μ on X satisfying μγ_Λ=μ for every finite Λ. This equals the conditional-probability formulation: for B measurable outside Λ, γ_Λ(1_B f)=1_B γ_Λ f by properness, so μ(1_Bf)=μ(1_Bγ_Λf). Since γ_Λf is exterior measurable, it is a version of Eμ(f|F_Λc). Conversely this conditional identity with B=X gives μγ_Λ=μ. Equality initially for cylinder f extends to all bounded measurable f by simple functions, monotone approximation and the π–λ theorem. It does not identify γ_Λ with an unconditional finite marginal; those marginals generally depend on the whole state.

For any Λ_n exhausting Z^d and any exterior η_n, embed γ_(Λ_n)(·|η_n) as probability on X. G2 gives a subsequential cylinder limit μ. For fixed finite Δ and cylinder f, γ_Δf depends only on the finite support of f and the R-boundary of Δ, so is local. Finite consistency μ_n γ_Δ=μ_n holds for n sufficiently large. Pass both local integrals to the limit to obtain μγ_Δ(f)=μ(f). The preceding extension gives DLR on the entire σ-field. Thus states exist. The same argument shows the DLR set is closed and convex in cylinder topology.

Translation-invariant interactions admit at least one stationary DLR state, even when the chosen boundary limit is not stationary. Translating a DLR law translates its kernels covariantly and leaves it DLR. Average its translates over cubes B_n to obtain μ_n^av; convexity preserves DLR and compactness gives a subsequential DLR limit. For a fixed translate j and bounded local f, the difference between translated and untranslated average expectations is at most2||f||∞ |(B_n+j)ΔB_n|/|B_n|→0 by direct lattice boundary counting. Thus the limiting state is translation stationary. This theorem constructs states, not a unique phase or automatic ergodicity.

## G4. High-temperature boundary influence and unique interacting Ising Gibbs state

Such a coupling is explicit: match each label a with mass min(p_a,q_a), then couple the remaining positive and negative residual masses by product divided by their common total; their supports are disjoint. ## G6. Two-dimensional interacting low-temperature coexistence, proved by contours

Consider nearest-neighbor Ising on Z², J>0 in J, h=0, β>0, boxes B_n, all-plus exterior. For a configuration let E_dis be dual edges crossing opposite-spin nearest-neighbor bonds. Each dual vertex has even degree, since multiplying the four neighboring spin products around a primal square gives1; degrees are0,2,4. E_dis is finite because the exterior is all plus. A finite even-degree graph decomposes into edge-disjoint cycles: follow unused edges until a vertex repeats, remove the simple cycle between repeats, and continue with the remaining even-degree graph. Termination follows from finite edges. Each resulting simple dual cycle γ has length ℓ≥4 and crosses only disagreeing bonds.

To avoid a hidden Jordan-curve prerequisite, define the inside of a dual cycle by mod-2 parity of intersections with the horizontal ray from a primal site to +∞. Primal sites have integer heights, dual vertices half-integer heights, so rays hit no dual vertices. For adjacent sites, their parity differs exactly when their joining primal edge crosses γ: for horizontal neighbors subtract the ray crossings; for vertical neighbors use the boundary of the strip between the rays, whose total intersections with a closed cycle are even (a cycle enters and leaves any finite rectangle equally modulo2); choose its far boundary beyond the finite cycle. This proves that the set of bonds crossing the parity-inside is exactly the dual edge set γ. Its inside is finite, since sites outside the coordinate bounding rectangle have zero ray parity. For any path from origin to an exterior plus site, the mod-2 number of disagreements is1 if s0=−1. Summing these intersections over the edge-disjoint cycle decomposition gives an odd total, so at least one cycle has origin parity-inside. This is the precise combinatorial separation fact consumed; no general Jordan theorem is claimed proved.

For a fixed simple cycle γ, let A_γ be the event all its crossing bonds disagree. Flip every primal spin in its parity-inside. The operation is an injective involution; for γ arising inside the plus box its inside is contained in the box. Every crossing bond changes from unlike to like and lowers its energy by2J; all other bonds keep their spin product. At h=0 no field term changes. Therefore H(ω)=H(flipγω)+2Jℓ on A_γ. Summing positive weights and bounding the image sum by the entire partition yields γ_Bn^+(A_γ)≤e^−2βJℓ. This direct energy injection remains valid even if a particular contour-pairing convention changes after flipping; it avoids requiring equality of contour sets.

A cycle containing the origin inside intersects the ray at a positive half-integer x=u−½, with a dual vertex(u−½,½). A length-ℓ closed grid cycle surrounding origin has horizontal span≤ℓ/2 (at least twice its span in horizontal steps) and extends left of the origin, so1≤u≤ceil(ℓ/2)+1; use this harmless generous count. For each such starting vertex, there are at most4·3^(ℓ−1) nonbacktracking length-ℓ walks, since the first edge has4 choices and each subsequent edge at most3.
