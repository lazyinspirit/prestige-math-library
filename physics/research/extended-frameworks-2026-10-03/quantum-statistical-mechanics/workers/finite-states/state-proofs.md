## Q0 — finite matrix spectral, functional and state calculus

Let H=C^D, 1≤D<∞, complex inner product linear in its first argument, operator norm induced by it, adjoint A* characterized by ⟨Ax,y⟩=⟨x,A*y⟩. A density matrix is ρ=ρ*≥0, Trρ=1. A bounded observable is Hermitian A with spectral values in its declared physical units. Expectation is ωρ(A)=TrρA; positivity and normalization are mathematical statements until a measurement postulate identifies probabilities.

For completeness a Hermitian matrix admits an orthonormal real-eigenvalue basis without presuming a probabilistic model. The continuous real quadratic form ⟨Ax,x⟩ has a maximum on the unit sphere. The actually read thermo M32 deterministic bounded-sequence proof gives the needed sequential compactness of this closed bounded subset of R^(2D). To justify the extremum directly, enumerate normalized nonzero rational complex-coordinate vectors, a dense subset of the sphere (rational approximation followed by continuous normalization proves density). For each positive integer k choose the least enumerated index whose quadratic value exceeds its sphere supremum minus 1/k; the supremum is finite by the matrix norm bound and density ensures that index exists. M32 gives a convergent subsequence with unit limit, and continuity makes its value the supremum. This is a deterministic maximizing-sequence construction, with no hidden countable selection. At a maximizer u its derivative along v⊥u is 2Re⟨Au,v⟩; replacing v by iv also annihilates the imaginary part, so Au is proportional to u. Its eigenvalue is real by Hermiticity. The orthogonal complement is invariant since ⟨Av,u⟩=⟨v,Au⟩=0. Induction on dimension diagonalizes that restriction. Repeated eigenvalues pose no difficulty; within each eigenspace a function f(A) acts by f(λ). This defines basis-independent f(A), positive square roots, e^A and, on the positive support, logA. The finite trace is the diagonal sum; expanding entries gives TrAB=TrBA and Tr(UAU*)=TrA for unitary U. Positivity gives TrρA=Σp_i⟨Au_i,u_i⟩, with p_i≥0,Σp_i=1, hence |TrρA|≤||A|| and real expectation for Hermitian A.

For arbitrary matrices X,Y define (X,Y)_ρ=Trρ X*Y. The quadratic form Trρ(X+zY)*(X+zY)≥0 for all complex z gives |TrρX*Y|²≤TrρX*X·TrρY*Y: minimize the scalar quadratic if the second factor is positive; if it is zero, varying z with arbitrarily large magnitude forces the cross term zero. Thus state Cauchy–Schwarz holds even for nonfaithful ρ. A PVM is a finite family of orthogonal projections P_j summing to I; probabilities TrρP_j≥0 sum to1. A finite effect E satisfies 0≤E≤I, yielding expectation in[0,1]. For an actual self-adjoint observable its spectral projections define the same scalar law.

The eigenvalues p_i of a density lie in[0,1]. Trρ²=Σp_i²≤1, with equality exactly when one p_i=1, i.e. ρ=|u⟩⟨u| for a unit u. Such rank-one states are extreme: if ρ=tσ+(1-t)τ,0<t<1, every v⊥u has zero nonnegative expectations in σ and τ; positivity implies σv=τv=0 because ||sqrtσ v||²=0, so both supported on the one-dimensional span and both equalρ. Conversely a state with at least two positive eigenvalues admits opposite small changes to two eigenvalues, giving distinct densities whose average isρ. A mixed state is consequently a nonextreme density, not an orthogonal classical label attached to each preparation. The same matrix can have different ensembles of vectors: I/2=(|0⟩⟨0|+|1⟩⟨1|)/2=(|+⟩⟨+|+|−⟩⟨−|)/2. Its physically testable expectation functional alone does not select a decomposition.
