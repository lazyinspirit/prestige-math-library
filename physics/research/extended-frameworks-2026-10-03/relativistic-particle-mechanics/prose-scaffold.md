# Relativistic Particle Mechanics

This category develops classical relativistic particles on a stipulated smooth
spacetime. It starts with causal curves and clocks, derives the equations selected
by massive and massless particle actions, and then develops energy, momentum,
collision kinematics and prescribed external forces. The second strand develops
the singular Legendre transform, constrained phase space, observable symmetries
and local Hamilton–Jacobi theory. The final strand treats covariant spin and
finite-size models, explicit orbits and scattering, controlled low-speed limits,
and the boundaries of radiation-reaction approximations. Geometry, mathematical
properties of functionals and physical adoption of those functionals are separate
claims. Every deduction states its assumptions; none supplies empirical truth
merely because the equations are covariant.

The canonical subject is a test-particle category. A test particle has stipulated
mechanical parameters and follows a worldline in a prescribed metric and, when
used, a prescribed electromagnetic field. Ignoring the particle's effect on these
backgrounds is a model assumption. Smooth-background existence can be supplied
by the exact Einstein–matter frameworks E/H, but their smooth matter hypotheses
do not cover a distributional point-particle source. The coupled singular Einstein
or Maxwell self-field problem therefore cannot be smuggled into a test-body
existence claim. Extended-body truncations and radiation models have their own
explicit assumptions and errors.

The machine inventory fixes supplier-first ordering and precise canonical homes.

1. **Geometry and causal worldlines.** Reuse SR S0–S6 and GR G0–G3 to
   specify affine Minkowski or smooth time-oriented Lorentz spacetime, coordinate
   charts, local tetrads, observer congruences, future causal curves, proper time
   and covariant differentiation. Give every curve a domain, regularity and
   parameter, distinguish pointwise rest space from remote simultaneity, and
   explain the absence of proper time on a null curve. Local geodesic IVPs do
   not imply complete worldlines. H0/C1–C2 govern restricted proper-time
   extremality; H2–H3 govern genuinely global causal hypotheses.
2. **Actions and parametrization.** Introduce the massive length action,
   positive-einbein quadratic action and massless constrained action as precise
   finite-interval functionals. Show invariance under increasing parameter
   diffeomorphisms and derive the Euler–Lagrange equations with fixed endpoints.
   Gauge-fixing is valid only on the named domain. A square-root action cannot
   be varied at a null tangent. Stationarity is a mathematical property;
   adopting it as physical motion is a postulate.
3. **Momentum, force and collisions.** S7 supplies the stipulated massive
   momentum premise; `dynamics-developments.md` supplies inverse velocity,
   work, variable mass, finite total mass, center-of-momentum existence,
   fixed-target and two-null thresholds, two-body rest-frame momenta and
   elastic-channel direction freedom. Conservation selects permissible
   kinematics, not interaction existence, probability or cross section.
   Thought experiments calculate fusion and decay recoil with all outgoing
   products included in the model.
4. **External charge and field coupling.** Reuse EM C1–C5/V1–V2 with their
   actual sign conventions. Set $F=dA$, $F_{0i}=-E_i/c$,
   $F_{ij}=\epsilon_{ijk}B_k$ and $K^a=qg^{ab}F_{bc}U^c$.
   The optional SR S8 electromagnetic identification uses the opposite
   two-form; here $F_{\rm SR}=-F_{\rm EM}$. Covariance does not select this
   coupling. Explain endpoint gauge changes of the action, local potentials
   and global topology, mass-shell preservation and smooth prescribed-field
   IVPs. Keep external work distinct from closed particle-plus-field balance.
5. **Canonical constraints and symmetries.** Use the canonical cotangent
   one-form $\lambda=p_a dx^a$, $\omega=-d\lambda$ and
   $\iota_{X_H}\omega=dH$. The singular homogeneous length Lagrangian has
   a shell constraint; it is outside the hyperregular Legendre theorem's
   hypotheses. Develop multiplier dynamics, local regular first/second-class
   constraints, explicit Dirac brackets and the exact flat future-shell
   quotient. Prove time-gauge reduction and the sign $p_0=-E/c$; do not
   assume a global quotient chart on an arbitrary curved background.
   Killing/Noether quantities, Poincaré charges and local Hamilton–Jacobi
   phases have their actual regularity and invariance hypotheses.
6. **Spin and finite extent.** Introduce spin as covariant tensor/vector
   data with stated constraints and units, then adopt and analyze the specified
   BMT and pole-dipole/MPD equations. The supplementary condition selects a
   representative centroid; momentum need not equal mass times centroid
   velocity in the general spinning model. Tensor identities, constraint
   preservation and stipulated linear-spin restrictions are deductions;
   multipole truncation, a gyromagnetic parameter and constitutive response
   are model inputs. Taylor estimates for a finite distribution require
   bounded moments and smooth background derivatives, not just a small label.
7. **Explicit trajectories and scattering.** In flat spacetime compute
   accelerated/Rindler and constant-field trajectories and check their
   domains directly. In Schwarzschild reuse Q1's metric/symbols and derive
   massive and null effective potentials, circular orbit restrictions,
   radial motion and stationary-observer redshift. An effective-potential
   local stability calculation differs from nonlinear full-system stability.
   Scattering angles and cross sections require specified incoming/outgoing
   asymptotic states and a preparation map; a collision threshold alone
   gives no rate. Curved scattering calculations remain in the exact
   exterior/test-body model being solved.
8. **Controlled limits and self-force boundaries.** Supply uniform
   low-speed remainder bounds and the finite-time external-field ODE
   comparison in D4, and use GR Q4 only with its precise stationary metric,
   derivative bounds, speed scaling and compact containment. Radiation
   far-field fluxes R2–R4 are evaluated away from the worldline and do not
   define a force of the particle's singular field on itself. Analyze the
   stipulated LAD/LL and small-body equations with their exact approximation
   status. Runaway and preacceleration examples explain a model's properties;
   an unproved uniform self-force convergence theorem remains unconsumed.

## Quantities, units and adoption

The base interpretation uses SI. The framework adopts $c>0$ (m/s), massive
particle $m>0$ (kg), and, in the charged model, $q$ (C) as parameters. A
given background $g$ has dimensionless components in length coordinates;
$G_N$ (m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻²) and $\Lambda$ (m⁻²) enter only the chosen background
Einstein model. Classical EM adopts its electromagnetic normalization,
$\mu_0$ (N A⁻²) and $\epsilon_0=1/(\mu_0c^2)$ (F/m). Their measured
modern SI numerical values are not all exact constants by unit definition.
The optional spin parameter and finite-size material coefficients are separate
model data. No quantum $\hbar$ or operator identification is assumed.

Proper time (s), $U$ (m/s), $P=mU$ (kg m/s), energy (J), force (N), kinetic
energy and system invariant mass are derived by the constructions supplied
before their uses. An action has J s; $A_a$ in length coordinates has T m,
$F_{ab}$ has T, so $q\int A_a dx^a$ has J s. Canonical momentum includes
$qA_a$; mechanical momentum remains the metric dual of $mU$. Spin angular
momentum has J s. Coordinate components in angular or time charts acquire
their Jacobian units and must not be compared as dimensionless tetrad entries.
Worker modules provide the remaining einbein, multiplier and multipole units.
