## H0 — phase space, signs, regularity and dimensions

For a smooth $n$-manifold $Q$, $T^*Q=\coprod_q\operatorname{Hom}(T_qQ,\mathbb R)$ is the smooth cotangent bundle, with projection $\pi(q,p)=q$, covector coordinate change by the inverse transpose Jacobian, and tautological form $\theta_{(q,p)}(v)=p(d\pi v)$. Its canonical two-form is $\omega=-d\theta$. Exact inspected published suppliers are `def-tautological-one-form-on-a-cotangent-bundle` and `thm-the-canonical-cotangent-two-form-is-symplectic`: in induced charts $\theta=p_i dq^i$, $\omega=dq^i\wedge dp_i$, $d\omega=0$, and contraction with $(a,b)$ is $a^i dp_i-b_i dq^i$, whose zero kernel proves nondegeneracy. A symplectic manifold is a smooth manifold with a smooth closed nondegenerate two-form. A smooth symplectomorphism is a diffeomorphism pulling one such form back to the other.

The exact library conventions are $\iota_{X_H}\omega=dH$ and $\{F,G\}=\omega(X_F,X_G)=X_G(F)$. Thus $X_H=(H_p,-H_q)$ and $\{F,G\}=F_qG_p-F_pG_q$. Smooth observables form a Poisson algebra: the bilinear skew Leibniz rules follow from differentiation; expansion of the cyclic sum of $J^{ij}J^{kl}F_i(G_{kj}H_l+G_kH_{lj})$ cancels all Hessian terms in pairs by Hessian symmetry and $J^T=-J$, proving Jacobi for constant canonical matrix $J$. Coordinate invariance follows from the intrinsic contraction definition. The general symplectic Jacobi/Poisson suppliers were inspected in the root corpus. Their needed argument is also explicit: Cartan gives $\mathcal L_{X_F}\omega=d^2F=0$ and $\iota_{[X_F,X_G]}\omega=\mathcal L_{X_F}dG=d(X_FG)=-d\{F,G\}$, so $[X_F,X_G]=-X_{\{F,G\}}$. Expanding $d\omega(X_F,X_G,X_H)=0$ then gives twice the cyclic Jacobi sum, up to one common sign, hence zero; this works without assuming canonical coordinates on an arbitrary symplectic leaf. For time-dependent $H$ on an open domain $D\subset\mathbb R\times T^*Q$, an evolution solves $\dot z=X_{H_t}(z)$ on its actual interval; $dF(t,z(t))/dt=F_t+\{F,H\}$. A $C^2$ Hamiltonian gives a $C^1$ locally Lipschitz field and hence local uniqueness, not completeness. Smooth joint flow dependence is supplied by the inspected ODE theorem. Differentiating $Y^TJY$ for the variational equation $Y'=J\operatorname{Hess}H\,Y$ gives zero; $Y(0)=I$ therefore proves local flow preservation of $\omega$ and canonical phase volume. Time-dependent $H$ has the same result on its actual evolution domain.

An interpreted covector $p$ pairs with displacement to give action: $[p_i]=[\text{action}]/[q^i]$, $[\theta]=[\omega]=\mathrm{J\,s}$; exterior differentiation changes form degree, not physical dimension. Hence $[\{F,G\}]=[F][G]/(\mathrm{J\,s})$. A time Hamiltonian is in joules and generates seconds; a constraint generator need not be energy and its multiplier supplies the dimensions. Coordinates of different dimensions are scaled before using Euclidean norms. Momentum coordinates on spacetime charts whose four coordinates are metres are all kg m/s; angular momentum has kg m²/s. Nothing here identifies an arbitrary canonical momentum with mechanical momentum.

## H1 — regular Legendre equivalence and its failure for homogeneous actions

Let $L(t,q,v)$ be smooth on an open velocity domain in $\mathbb R\times TQ$. Its fibre derivative is $\mathbb FL(t,q,v)=(t,q,L_v)$. The derivative at fixed $t$ has blocks $(I,0;L_{vq},L_{vv})$. An invertible velocity Hessian gives a local smooth inverse by the inspected inverse theorem. A global diffeomorphism of the declared velocity and momentum domains, smooth jointly in $t$, is the stronger hyperregular condition. On an inverse branch set $H=p_iv^i-L$; differentiating cancels the $dv$ terms and gives $dH=v^i dp_i-L_{q^i}dq^i-L_tdt$. Thus Hamilton equations and Euler–Lagrange equations are equivalent on that branch; global equivalence requires the global inverse. This is the exact published hyperregular theorem with its domains made explicit. $L(v)=e^v$ is regular but misses $p\le0$, so regularity alone is insufficient.

If $L(q,av)=aL(q,v)$ for all $a>0$ in a conic velocity domain, differentiating at $a=1$ gives Euler's identity $v^i L_{v^i}=L$. Differentiating it in $v^j$ gives $v^iL_{v^iv^j}=0$: the Hessian is singular at nonzero $v$, the canonical energy $E_L=pv-L$ vanishes, and hyperregular inversion is impossible. One cannot apply the NR regular Legendre theorem to this action and conclude that every worldline is frozen because $H=0$.

A precise almost-regular alternative is conditional on supplied regular geometry. Assume $\mathbb FL$ has constant rank, image a specified embedded submanifold $N$, is a surjective submersion onto $N$, and has connected fibres. These hypotheses are not consequences of singularity. The energy descends smoothly to $N$: a tangent to a Legendre fibre has $\delta q=0,L_{vv}\delta v=0$, and $dE_L(\delta v)=v^iL_{v^iv^j}\delta v^j=0$. Connected manifolds are locally path connected and hence path connected, so energy is constant on each fibre. Smooth local sections of the submersion make the descended function smooth and unique. These are sufficient assumptions to formulate a canonical Hamiltonian on the primary constraint surface, not a universal assertion for every singular Lagrangian.

Equality restricted to $N$ is denoted $\approx$. For chosen primary constraints and a smooth ambient extension $H_c$ of a descended canonical Hamiltonian, the total ansatz is $H_T=H_c+u^a\chi_a$, where multipliers are functions of the trajectory parameter with declared units. ## H3 — complete local Dirac bracket for second-class constraints

Suppose $2k$ smooth constraints have invertible matrix $C_{ab}=\{\chi_a,\chi_b\}$ on an open neighborhood $U$. Its inverse $C^{ab}$ is smooth by the adjugate formula. Independence of $d\chi_a$ follows: a linear combination zero, evaluated on each $X_{\chi_b}$, gives a row vector in the kernel of $C$. Every joint level in $U$ is therefore an embedded submanifold. Define

$$\{F,G\}_D=\{F,G\}-\{F,\chi_a\}C^{ab}\{\chi_b,G\}.$$

For every $F$ put $X_F^D=X_F-X_{\chi_a}C^{ab}\{\chi_b,F\}$. Direct evaluation gives $d\chi_c(X_F^D)=\{\chi_c,F\}-C_{ca}C^{ab}\{\chi_b,F\}=0$, so this is tangent to every level. If $v$ is tangent to a level, $\omega(X_F^D,v)=dF(v)$ because each $d\chi_a(v)=0$. The level restriction of $\omega$ is nondegenerate: a tangent radical vector must lie in its symplectic orthogonal, the span of $X_{\chi_a}$ (apply the isomorphism $\omega^\flat$ to the annihilator of the tangent space). A vector in that span tangent to the level has coefficients annihilated by $C$, hence is zero. Closedness is preserved by pullback. Thus each level is symplectic, $X_F^D$ is its actual Hamiltonian field, and the displayed bracket is its Poisson bracket on restricted observables. This proves independence of ambient extensions: a function zero on a level has zero tangent differential and zero Hamiltonian field there.

All $\chi_a$ are Casimirs of the Dirac bracket, meaning their bracket with every smooth observable is zero, by multiplication of $C$ and $C^{-1}$. Bilinearity, skewness and Leibniz follow directly. Jacobi holds on every joint level by the proved symplectic Poisson result, and hence at every point of $U$; its derivatives only differentiate tangent directions since every $X_F^D$ is tangent to that level. This is a full local Poisson argument, not an unproved algebraic cancellation or merely a bracket on one surface. The number of constraints must be even because an invertible alternating matrix has even dimension: an odd matrix has determinant equal to its negative under transpose. Units satisfy $[C_{ab}]=[\chi_a][\chi_b]/\text{action}$ and $[C^{ab}]=\text{action}/([\chi_a][\chi_b])$, so each correction has the original bracket dimension.
