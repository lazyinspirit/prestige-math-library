A fluid is modeled here by fields on Newtonian spacetime $I\times\Omega$, where $I\subset\mathbb R$ is a time interval and $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^3$ an open spatial region, a smooth moving region, or an explicitly identified periodic cell. A Newtonian inertial chart uses time $t$ and Euclidean coordinates $x$; a Galilean change is $t'=t+t_0$, $x'=Qx+ct+a$, with constant $Q\in SO(3)$ and constants $c,a$. An observer frame and a material description are different objects. A particle trajectory is a $C^1$ curve $X:I\to\Omega$ satisfying $\dot X=u(t,X)$ where the stated regularity ensures existence; a material map is an orientation preserving spatial diffeomorphism $\chi(t,\cdot)$ from a reference region with $\partial_t\chi=u(t,\chi)$. Weak solutions do not silently possess this smooth material map. The adopted microscopic description, when used, is a separate distribution $f(t,x,v)$, never a reinterpretation of a mass-density field as a probability without normalization.

## Physical premises and primitive quantities

Continuum resolution is an adopted approximation: mass, momentum and energy are represented by local fields at scales appropriate to the material and preparation. No theorem here derives it universally from molecules. Adopt conservation of mass on every material subregion; linear momentum balance with specified body acceleration and contact traction; angular momentum balance without body or surface couples where symmetric Cauchy stress is used; and energy balance with specified stress power, heat flux and volumetric heating. Contact locality/boundedness and smooth balance hypotheses yield Cauchy's traction representation in the foundations argument. A micropolar fluid with couples falls outside that symmetry inference.

The compressible thermodynamic branch additionally adopts local equilibrium with a differentiable caloric equation of state $e=e(\rho,s)$ and Gibbs identity $de=T\,ds+p\rho^{-2}d\rho$. Here $e$ is specific internal energy, $s$ specific entropy, $\rho>0$ mass density, and $T>0$ temperature. This relation and positivity/stability conditions are constitutive hypotheses. Pressure is $p=\rho^2\partial_\rho e$ and temperature $T=\partial_s e$ within this branch. An equivalent $e(\rho,T),p(\rho,T)$ presentation needs compatibility and invertibility before switching charts. Equilibrium thermodynamic suppliers do not themselves establish nonequilibrium local equilibrium.

An isotropic Newtonian branch adopts $\sigma=-p\,\mathrm{Id}+2\mu D_0u+\zeta(\nabla\cdot u)\mathrm{Id}$, where $D_0u=(Du+Du^T)/2-(\nabla\cdot u)\mathrm{Id}/3$, and $q=-k\nabla T$. Nonnegative $\mu,\zeta,k$ make the local dissipative production nonnegative; these coefficients and their temperature/density dependence are material inputs. Stokes' hypothesis $\zeta=0$ is an additional special choice. Inviscid Euler adopts $\mu=\zeta=k=0$; dropping a coefficient is a model definition unless a separately proved limit controls it. Constant-density incompressibility adopts $\rho=\rho_0>0$, $\nabla\cdot u=0$; pressure then enforces the constraint and is not necessarily an equilibrium equation-of-state pressure. Barotropic Euler adopts a positive density and specified $p(\rho)$; isentropic polytropic Euler uses $p=\kappa\rho^\gamma$, $\kappa>0,\gamma>1$. These branches should not be conflated.

Boundary conditions are further physical/model inputs. A stationary impermeable wall has $u\cdot n=0$. No slip has $u=0$; a moving wall has $u=U_w$ with compatible normal speed. Navier slip supplies an explicit tangential traction/friction law. Thermal walls prescribe temperature, normal heat flux, or a stated exchange law. Periodicity identifies opposite faces and carries no real-wall claim. Free surfaces need kinematic transport and dynamic stress conditions with an explicitly defined interface, surface tension coefficient and curvature convention. They are formulated here as model language; unrestricted free-boundary existence is not asserted.

Within this continuum formulation the spacetime/time scale, mass assignment, initial density and velocity, external acceleration, heating, and constitutive material parameters are primitive inputs. Stress, momentum density, energy density, vorticity, strain, circulation, fluxes, dimensionless groups and observables are derived from those fields and premises. In an incompressible initial-value problem pressure is derived by projection with a stated gauge. This primitive/derived status is framework-local; microscopic mass and Boltzmann's constant enter as extra primitives in the kinetic bridge.

## Supplier-first reading route

Begin with pure calculus, flow maps, transport and weak-test language; then adopt the balance premises and derive local mass/momentum/energy equations. Constitutive choices close those balances rather than follow from them. Study incompressible, barotropic and full thermal systems separately. Define classical, variational and distributional solution classes before proving existence or interpreting shocks. Smooth periodic incompressible Euler/Navier–Stokes and polytropic compressible Euler get explicit local constructions. Global weak Navier–Stokes is developed both on a torus and with bounded no-slip data in the closure of smooth solenoidal fields. It has a different solution class and does not provide global smoothness or general uniqueness in three dimensions. In two dimensions positive-viscosity periodic smooth Navier–Stokes has a complete all-time enstrophy/Fourier-logarithm/continuation proof. A bounded rectangular stationary Stokes theorem constructs both the H1_0 velocity and a distribution pressure through an explicit compact-test divergence inverse; it asserts no unsupported L2 pressure regularity. Scalar entropy laws illustrate shock selection but are not a theorem for arbitrary multidimensional compressible Euler.

Vorticity, circulation and Bernoulli are conditional transport consequences. Boundaries and stability require their actual hypotheses, not a stationary formula alone. Exact channel/vortex/diffusion solutions demonstrate the equations; nondimensionalization fixes which parameters change together. Linear acoustics, waves and low-parameter estimates are controlled statements in specified models, whereas turbulent closure and universal scaling are recorded modeling questions. A kinetic distribution satisfying an explicitly adopted regular Boltzmann collision model supplies exact moments and entropy production. A controlled conditional convergence result specifies convergent moments/fluxes; it is not a general hard-sphere hydrodynamic limit.
