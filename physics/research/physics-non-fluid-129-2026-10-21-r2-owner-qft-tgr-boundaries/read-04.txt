
ITEM cex-qftx-eft-outside-band

## Statement

Assume AC. Let $H=L^2(\mathbb R,dp;\mathbb C)$ with pairing $\langle u,v\rangle=\int_\mathbb R u(p)\overline{v(p)}\,dp$, let
$$A=M_{p^2},\qquad D(A)=\{u\in H:p^2u\in H\},$$
and fix $M>0$, $0\le E<M$, and $N\in\mathbb N_0$. This is the no-band case for the estimate in [[thm-qftx-heavy-derivative-bound]]: the polynomial approximation to $(M^2+p^2)^{-1}$ has multiplier remainder
$$r_N(p^2)=M^{-2}\frac{(-p^2/M^2)^{N+1}}{1+p^2/M^2}.$$
Let $r_N(A)$ denote multiplication by $r_N(p^2)$ on its maximal $L^2$ domain. There is a unit vector $u\in\bigcap_{r=0}^{N+1}D(A^r)$ for which
$$\|r_N(A)u\|>M^{-2}(E^2/M^2)^{N+1}.$$
Thus the derivative estimate's low-energy factor cannot hold uniformly on the full momentum spectrum. Here $p,M,E$ have mass units, $dp$ has mass units, $A$ has mass-squared units, and $\hbar=c=1$. This is a mathematical counterexample to removing the spectral-band hypothesis; it is not a measured physical failure.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The complex $L^2$ space, the multiplier operator $A$, $M>0$, $0\le E<M$, and $N\ge0$.

[A1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: every family of nonempty sets has a choice function.

[A2] [[lem-ac-supplies-sequential-choices-for-probability-constructions]]: AC implies Countable Choice, which is also required by the multiplication and L2 Hilbert suppliers.

[F1] [[thm-qftx-heavy-derivative-bound]]: its estimate assumes $J$ belongs to the spectral subspace $E_A([0,E^2])H$.

[F2] [[lem-nrqm-observables-l2-multiplication-spectral-measure]]: for real measurable multiplier $m$, $M_m$ on $D(M_m)=\{u:mu\in L^2\}$ is self-adjoint and its spectral projections are multiplication by indicators of $m^{-1}(B)$.

[F3] [[def-l-p-space-as-a-quotient-by-null-functions]]: $L^2$ consists of measurable complex functions modulo equality almost everywhere.

[F4] [[lem-l-two-with-the-integral-pairing-is-a-hilbert-space]]: on complex $L^2$, $\langle f,g\rangle=\int f\overline g$ is a Hilbert-space inner product with $\|f\|_2^2=\int|f|^2$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct multiplier calculation and compactly supported unit vectors.

**Given:** The hypotheses and notation in the Statement.

1.1 By [A1]–[A2], the AC and Countable Choice assumptions of [F2] and [F4] hold. Apply [F2] with $m(p)=p^2$ on the sigma-finite positive-measure space $(\mathbb R,dp)$; sigma-finiteness follows from $\mathbb R=\bigcup_{n\ge1}[-n,n]$ and $[-1,1]$ has positive finite measure. It gives the self-adjoint nonnegative multiplication operator $A$ with the displayed domain and spectral calculus. The multiplier $(M^2+p^2)^{-1}$ is bounded by $M^{-2}$ and maps $H$ into $D(A)$ because $p^2/(M^2+p^2)\le1$; pointwise multiplication then shows it is the two-sided inverse of $M^2I+A$. For $u$ with bounded support, every $A^ru$ is in $H$. Pointwise finite geometric division gives
$$
(M^2+p^2)^{-1}-M^{-2}\sum_{r=0}^{N}(-p^2/M^2)^r=r_N(p^2),
$$
so the remainder on such $u$ is multiplication by $r_N(p^2)$. [A1, A2, F2, F3, F4, given, algebra]

2.1 The target coefficient $C_N:=M^{-2}(E^2/M^2)^{N+1}$ is finite and is strictly less than $M^{-2}$. If $N=0$, $|r_0(q)|=M^{-2}(q/M^2)/(1+q/M^2)\to M^{-2}>C_0$ as $q\to\infty$. If $N\ge1$, $|r_N(q)|=M^{-2}(q/M^2)^{N+1}/(1+q/M^2)\to\infty$. In either case choose $P>0$ so that $|r_N(p^2)|>C_N$ for every $p\in[P,P+M]$, and put $u=M^{-1/2}\mathbf1_{[P,P+M]}$. The interval has Lebesgue measure $M$, so $\|u\|_2^2=M^{-1}\int_P^{P+M}1\,dp=1$; its bounded support puts it in every $D(A^r)$, while
$$\|r_N(A)u\|_2^2=M^{-1}\int_P^{P+M}|r_N(p^2)|^2\,dp>C_N^2.$$
Thus the full-space bound fails. The restriction $E<M$ is precisely what makes the proposed low-energy coefficient smaller than the $N=0$ high-momentum limit; the example does not contradict the band-limited theorem. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, algebra] ∎

ITEM def-tgrx-finite-fusion-count-model

## Definition

A **finite fusion transfer count model** consists of a finite nonempty label set $I$, a distinguished label $0\in I$, and an explicitly supplied matrix $N=(N_{ab})_{a,b\in I}$ whose entries lie in $\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$. The entry $N_{ab}$ is the multiplicity of elementary transitions from label $b$ to label $a$; formally, the transition copies from $b$ to $a$ are the triples $(b,a,j)$ with integer $1\leq j\leq N_{ab}$. A length-$n$ path is a sequence of $n+1$ labels together with a chosen transition copy between each consecutive pair. For each integer $n\geq0$, define the vacuum-return count $D_n:=(N^n)_{00}$. If $D_n>0$, set $H_n:=\mathbb C^{D_n}$ with its standard inner product and define $\rho_n:=D_n^{-1}I_{H_n}$. If $D_n=0$, no normalized operator $\rho_n$ is assigned. The matrix $N$ is model data; no fusion category, quantum group, or representation is presumed to realize it.

## Remarks

The definition records a finite matrix count only. It does not assert the existence of a fusion category or identify $D_n$ with a gravitational state count.

ITEM prop-tgrx-fusion-count-entropy

## Statement

For a finite fusion transfer count model, let $e_0\in\mathbb R^I$ be the coordinate vector equal to $1$ at label $0$ and $0$ elsewhere, and define $v_n:=N^n e_0$. Then $v_0=e_0$, $v_{n+1}=Nv_n$, and the number of length-$n$ paths from $0$ to a label $a$ is $(v_n)_a=(N^n)_{a0}$. Whenever $D_n>0$, the operator $\rho_n=D_n^{-1}I_{\mathbb C^{D_n}}$ is a density matrix and has von Neumann entropy $s(\rho_n)=\log D_n$ in nats. For the supplied two-label matrix $N=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$, with labels ordered $0,1$, one has $D_0=1$, $D_1=0$, $D_2=1$, and $D_{n+2}=D_{n+1}+D_n$ for every $n\geq0$.

## Diagram convention

The diagram displays the two-label matrix only. An arrow $b\to a$ with label $m$ represents $N_{ab}=m$ transitions; the count proof below uses matrix multiplication and does not rely on the drawing.

```tikz
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.9]
  \node[circle,draw] (v0) at (0,0) {$0$};
  \node[circle,draw] (v1) at (2,0) {$1$};
  \draw[->] (v0) .. controls (0.65,0.65) and (1.35,0.65) .. node[above] {$1$} (v1);
  \draw[->] (v1) .. controls (1.35,-0.65) and (0.65,-0.65) .. node[below] {$1$} (v0);
  \draw[->] (v1) .. controls (2.9,0.7) and (2.9,-0.7) .. node[right] {$1$} (v1);
\end{tikzpicture}
```

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite set $I$, label $0$, matrix $N$, and counts $D_n$ of the Definition, together with the displayed two-label specialization.

[F1] [[def-tgrx-finite-fusion-count-model]]: $N_{ab}$ counts transitions from $b$ to $a$, $D_n=(N^n)_{00}$, and $\rho_n=I/D_n$ is assigned only for $D_n>0$.

[F2] [[def-tgrx-sector-code]]: a density matrix on a finite-dimensional complex inner-product space is self-adjoint, has nonnegative eigenvalues, and has trace one.

[F3] [[prop-tgrx-code-area-entropy]]: for a finite density matrix $\tau$, $s(\tau)=-\sum_{\lambda_j(\tau)>0}\lambda_j(\tau)\log\lambda_j(\tau)$, with $0\log0=0$ and natural logarithms, so entropy is in nats.

[F4] [[def-trace-of-an-endomorphism]]: the trace is the basis-independent sum of diagonal entries of a finite-dimensional endomorphism.

[F5] [[thm-natural-logarithm-laws]]: for $x>0$, $\log(1/x)=-\log x$ and $\log1=0$.

[A1] The two-label matrix is $N=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$ in the ordered basis $(0,1)$.

## Proof

1.1 For each $a\in I$, matrix multiplication gives $(Nv_n)_a=\sum_{b\in I}N_{ab}(N^n)_{b0}=(N^{n+1})_{a0}$. Each summand appends one of the $N_{ab}$ transitions from $b$ to $a$ to a length-$n$ path from $0$ to $b$. Thus $v_{n+1}=Nv_n$ and $(v_n)_a=(N^n)_{a0}$, with $v_0=e_0$. [given, F1, algebra]

1.2 If $D_n>0$, then $D_n$ is a positive integer and $\rho_n=D_n^{-1}I$ has eigenvalue $1/D_n$ with multiplicity $D_n$. Its trace is $D_n(1/D_n)=1$, and it is self-adjoint with nonnegative eigenvalues; hence it is a density matrix by [F2]. Substitution into [F3] gives $s(\rho_n)=-D_n(1/D_n)\log(1/D_n)=\log D_n$ by [F5]. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, algebra]

2.1 Under [A1], direct multiplication gives $N^2=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}=N+I$. Therefore $D_0=1$, $D_1=0$, and $D_2=1$. For every $n\geq0$, $N^{n+2}=N^nN^2=N^{n+1}+N^n$; taking the $(0,0)$ entry proves $D_{n+2}=D_{n+1}+D_n$. [A1, F1, algebra] ∎

ITEM def-tgrx-tl-boundary-representation

## Definition

Fix an integer $n\geq1$ and a real loop parameter $\delta$. Define $[0]_\delta=0$, $[1]_\delta=1$, and $[r+1]_\delta=\delta[r]_\delta-[r-1]_\delta$ for $r\geq1$. Assume $[k]_\delta\neq0$ for $2\leq k\leq n$; this list is empty when $n=1$. The abstract generator algebra $P_n(\mathbb C,\delta)$ is the unital complex algebra with generators $u_1,\ldots,u_{n-1}$ and relations $u_i^2=\delta u_i$, $u_i u_{i\pm1}u_i=u_i$, and $u_i u_j=u_j u_i$ when $|i-j|>1$. The diagram algebra $\mathrm{TL}_n(\mathbb C,\delta)$ has basis the noncrossing pairings of $n$ ordered bottom and $n$ ordered top points; its product stacks the left factor above the right, deletes each closed loop, and multiplies by $\delta$ per loop. Its elementary diagram $d_i$ has a cap joining top points $i,i+1$, a cup joining bottom points $i,i+1$, and vertical strands elsewhere. The isomorphism $\Phi_n:P_n(\mathbb C,\delta)\to\mathrm{TL}_n(\mathbb C,\delta)$ sends $u_i$ to $d_i$.

For every $1\leq i\leq n-2$, the adjacent relations in the presentation mean both $u_i u_{i+1}u_i=u_i$ and $u_{i+1}u_i u_{i+1}=u_{i+1}$. For matching the imported recursion's notation, write $U_i:=d_i$.

Set $p_1=1_1$. For $1\leq r<n$, put $Q_r=p_r\otimes1_1$ by adjoining one rightmost vertical strand and define

$$p_{r+1}:=Q_r-\frac{[r]_\delta}{[r+1]_\delta}Q_r d_r Q_r.$$

The denominator hypotheses make this recursion defined. For each $1\leq r\leq n$, let $\widehat p_r:=\Phi_r^{-1}(p_r)$. The cited Jones-Wenzl result gives $p_n^2=p_n$ under these unit-denominator hypotheses. For $1\leq r\leq n$, let $\iota_{r,n}:P_r(\mathbb C,\delta)\to P_n(\mathbb C,\delta)$ send each generator $u_i$ to the same-named generator, and let $\jmath_{r,n}:\mathrm{TL}_r(\mathbb C,\delta)\to\mathrm{TL}_n(\mathbb C,\delta)$ append $n-r$ rightmost vertical strands. The maps commute with $\Phi$: on each generator, both composites send $u_i$ to $d_i$ with those added strands, and both send the unit to the identity diagram. Since generators and unit generate $P_r$, the composites agree on every element.

Let $H$ be a nonzero finite-dimensional complex inner-product space. A unital representation is a unital complex-algebra homomorphism $\pi:P_n(\mathbb C,\delta)\to\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(H)$, where $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(H)$ is the algebra of complex-linear endomorphisms of $H$. Require each $\pi(u_i)$ to be self-adjoint, meaning $\langle\pi(u_i)x,y\rangle=\langle x,\pi(u_i)y\rangle$ for all $x,y\in H$. Define $P:=\pi(\widehat p_n)$, define $\operatorname{rank}P:=\dim_{\mathbb C}\operatorname{im}P$, and restrict to data with $\operatorname{rank}P>0$. The algebraic representation as a physical boundary state space is an additional model identification and is not part of this definition.

## Tower-map convention

For $r<n$, the presentation tower map $\iota_{r,n}$ is well-defined because the defining relations are unchanged. The diagram tower map $\jmath_{r,n}$ appends $n-r$ rightmost vertical strands. The commuting identity $\Phi_n\circ\iota_{r,n}=\jmath_{r,n}\circ\Phi_r$ transfers the unit-denominator recursion on diagrams to the presentation used by $\pi$.

## Diagram convention

The box is notation for the three-strand algebra element $p_3$; strands are ordered from left to right and continue through the box. For general $n$, the same box notation has $n$ strands. The picture records no physical apparatus, and no proof below relies on it.

```tikz
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.9]
  \foreach \x in {0,1,2} {
    \draw (\x,0) -- (\x,0.72);
    \draw (\x,1.28) -- (\x,2);
  }
  \draw (-0.3,0.72) rectangle (2.3,1.28);
  \node at (1,1) {$p_3$};
\end{tikzpicture}
```

## Remarks

Only $[2]_\delta,\ldots,[n]_\delta$ are required to be nonzero; $[n+1]_\delta$ may vanish. The item asserts no root-of-unity quotient, modular representation classification, trace-preserving realization, or physical area law.

ITEM prop-tgrx-tl-projector-entropy

## Statement

Under the finite Hilbert representation data, let $r:=\operatorname{rank}P>0$ and $\rho:=P/r$. Then $P$ is an orthogonal projection, $\rho$ is a density matrix with eigenvalue $1/r$ of multiplicity $r$ and eigenvalue $0$ on $\ker P$, and its entropy is $s(\rho)=\log r$ in nats. Under the published closure convention, the unnormalized diagrammatic closure trace of the algebraic Jones-Wenzl element is $\operatorname{Tr}_n(p_n)=[n+1]_\delta$. The proposition records this separately from the ordinary Hilbert-space trace $\operatorname{tr}_H(P)=r$ and does not assert the two values agree. A sufficient additional hypothesis for equality on this element is that the representation preserve these traces, $\operatorname{tr}_H(\pi(\Phi_n^{-1}(X)))=\operatorname{Tr}_n(X)$ for every $X\in\mathrm{TL}_n(\mathbb C,\delta)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The data of the finite Hilbert representation definition, including $n\geq1$, $\delta\in\mathbb R$, nonzero denominators $[2]_\delta,\ldots,[n]_\delta$, a unital representation with self-adjoint generator images, and $r=\operatorname{rank}P>0$.

[L1] [[def-tgrx-tl-boundary-representation]]: $\widehat p_n$ is the presentation image of the recursively defined diagram projector, $P=\pi(\widehat p_n)$, every $\pi(u_i)$ is self-adjoint, and $r=\dim_{\mathbb C}\operatorname{im}P>0$.

[F1] [[def-temperley-lieb-jones-wenzl-recursion-over-unit-denominators]]: $p_1=1_1$ and $p_{k+1}=(p_k\otimes1_1)-([k]_\delta/[k+1]_\delta)(p_k\otimes1_1)d_k(p_k\otimes1_1)$.

[F2] [[lem-temperley-lieb-jones-wenzl-partial-trace-and-uniqueness]]: if $[2]_\delta,\ldots,[n]_\delta$ are units, then $p_n^2=p_n$ and its unnormalized diagram closure trace is $[n+1]_\delta$.

[F3] [[def-temperley-lieb-closure-trace-and-partial-trace]]: $\operatorname{Tr}_n$ is the linear functional obtained by closing each top point to the corresponding bottom point and assigning a factor $\delta$ to each resulting loop.

[F4] [[def-tgrx-sector-code]]: a finite density matrix is self-adjoint, has nonnegative eigenvalues, and has trace one.

[F5] [[def-trace-of-an-endomorphism]]: the trace is the basis-independent sum of diagonal entries in any basis.

[F6] [[prop-tgrx-code-area-entropy]]: for a density matrix $\tau$, $s(\tau)=-\sum_{\lambda_j(\tau)>0}\lambda_j(\tau)\log\lambda_j(\tau)$, with $0\log0=0$ and natural logarithms.

[F7] [[thm-natural-logarithm-laws]]: $\log(1/x)=-\log x$ for $x>0$.

[F8] [[def-kernel-and-image-of-a-linear-map]]: $\ker T=\{x:T(x)=0\}$ and $\operatorname{im}T=\{T(x):x\in H\}$ for a linear endomorphism $T$ of $H$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** Induct along the Jones-Wenzl recursion index to prove self-adjointness, then use the range-kernel decomposition of an idempotent.

1.1 By [F2], $p_n^2=p_n$. The map $\Phi_n$ and the representation $\pi$ are algebra homomorphisms, so $P^2=\pi(\widehat p_n^2)=\pi(\widehat p_n)=P$. [L1, F2, algebra]

1.2 The imported projector result [F2] gives the unnormalized closure value $\operatorname{Tr}_n(p_n)=[n+1]_\delta$, where [F3] defines $\operatorname{Tr}_n$ by closing the corresponding diagram endpoints and weighting each resulting loop by $\delta$. This is the diagram-algebra functional recorded separately from the Hilbert-space trace. [F2, F3]

1.3 At recursion index $1$, $Q_1:=\pi(\iota_{1,n}(\widehat p_1))=I_H$ is self-adjoint because $\widehat p_1=1$. [L1, F1, base]

1.4 Suppose $1\leq k<n$ and $Q_k:=\pi(\iota_{k,n}(\widehat p_k))$ is self-adjoint. By the commuting tower identity in [L1], the diagram recursion [F1] transfers under $\Phi_n^{-1}$ and $\pi$ to $Q_{k+1}=Q_k-c_kQ_k\pi(u_k)Q_k$, where $c_k=[k]_\delta/[k+1]_\delta\in\mathbb R$ because $\delta$ is real and the recurrence defines real quantum integers. Since $\pi(u_k)$ and $Q_k$ are self-adjoint, $(Q_k\pi(u_k)Q_k)^*=Q_k\pi(u_k)Q_k$, so $Q_{k+1}^*=Q_{k+1}$. [L1, F1, ih, algebra]

2.1 Applying the finite step 1.4 from base step 1.3 through $k=n-1$ gives $Q_n=P=P^*$; when $n=1$, step 1.3 already gives the conclusion. [step 1.3, step 1.4, induction, discharge-induction]

3.1 For every $x\in H$, $x=Px+(I_H-P)x$, with $Px\in\operatorname{im}P$ and $P(I_H-P)x=0$, so $(I_H-P)x\in\ker P$ by [F8]. If $y=Pu\in\operatorname{im}P$ and $z\in\ker P$, then $\langle y,z\rangle=\langle Pu,z\rangle=\langle u,P^*z\rangle=0$ by step 2.1. Thus the two subspaces are orthogonal and sum to $H$, so their intersection is zero and $H=\operatorname{im}P\oplus\ker P$. The idempotent $P$ acts as the identity on $\operatorname{im}P$ and as zero on $\ker P$, so it is an orthogonal projection with eigenvalues $1$ on its $r$-dimensional image and $0$ on the kernel. [F8, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 By step 3.1 and [F5], the ordinary Hilbert-space trace is $\operatorname{tr}_H(P)=r$, while step 1.2 gives the separate diagrammatic closure value. The operator $\rho=P/r$ is self-adjoint and has eigenvalues $1/r$ on $\operatorname{im}P$ and $0$ on $\ker P$. In a basis formed from bases of these subspaces its trace is $r(1/r)=1$, so it is a density matrix by [F4,F5]. Its entropy is $-r(1/r)\log(1/r)=\log r$ by [F6,F7]. This concludes the proof using the self-adjointness established by induction in step 2.1. [F4, F5, F6, F7, step 1.2, step 3.1, step 2.1, algebra, discharge-induction] ∎

ITEM ex-tgrx-count-is-not-area-law

## Example

For the two-label matrix $N=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$ in the finite fusion count model, $D_4=2$. Choose a positive scalar $a$ independently of $N$, assign this same scalar to each of the four labels, and set $A_4:=4a$. The ratio between the model entropy $\log D_4$ and this separately assigned scalar is $\log2/(4a)$, and it changes when $a$ is replaced by $2a$ although $N$ and $D_4$ are unchanged. Thus the finite count alone does not determine a proportionality coefficient between its entropy and an independently assigned area-like scalar. The scalar $A_4$ is formal data in this mathematical example; no geometrical area, physical entropy, or gravitational law is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The two-label matrix and return-count recursion, an independently assigned scalar $a>0$, and $A_4=4a$.

[F1] [[prop-tgrx-fusion-count-entropy]]: for the two-label matrix, $D_0=1$, $D_1=0$, and $D_{n+2}=D_{n+1}+D_n$; when $D_n>0$, the uniform-state entropy is $\log D_n$.

[F2] [[thm-natural-logarithm-laws]]: $\log$ is strictly increasing and $\log1=0$.

[A1] The scalar assignment $a>0$ is independent of the transition matrix $N$ and its counts.

## Verification

1.1 Applying the recurrence in [F1] gives $D_2=1$, $D_3=1$, and $D_4=2$. Since $D_4>0$, the corresponding finite-state entropy is $s_4=\log2$. [F1, given, algebra]

2.1 With the assignment $a$, the ratio is $c(a):=s_4/A_4=\log2/(4a)$. Replacing $a$ by $2a$ leaves $N$, $D_4$, and $s_4$ fixed while giving $c(2a)=\log2/(8a)=c(a)/2$. [A1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Because $2>1$, [F2] gives $\log2>\log1=0$; since $a>0$, $c(a)>0$ and therefore $c(2a)\neq c(a)$. The count model consequently does not fix this coefficient. [F2, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
