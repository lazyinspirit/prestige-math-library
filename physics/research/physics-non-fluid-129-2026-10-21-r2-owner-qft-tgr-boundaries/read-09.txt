
ITEM def-qftx-scaling-cocycle

## Definition

Assume Countable Choice.

Let $\mathbb M=\mathbb R^4$ have Minkowski metric $\eta=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$. A scalar local polynomial functional is a finite sum
$$F(\phi)=c+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb M} f_j(x)P_j(j^{s_j}\phi(x))\,d^4x,$$
where $N$ is a finite nonnegative integer, $\phi\in C^\infty(\mathbb M;\mathbb R)$, $c\in\mathbb C$, and $f_j\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb M;\mathbb C)$. For a finite integer $s_j\ge0$, the $s_j$-jet is $j^{s_j}\phi(x)=(\partial^\alpha\phi(x))_{\alpha\in\{0,1,2,\ldots\}^4,\,|\alpha|\le s_j}$; each $P_j$ is a complex polynomial in these finitely many jet coordinates. This is the local functional class used by the supplied scalar time-ordered system.

For every mass label $m\ge0$, suppose a normalized scalar time-ordered system $T_n^{(m)}$ with the same local scalar contract is supplied. Define its coefficientwise formal S-map by
$$\mathcal S_m(V)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}T_n^{(m)}(V,\ldots,V),\qquad T_0^{(m)}=1,\quad T_1^{(m)}=\operatorname{id}.$$
The series is formal in the interaction degree; no analytic convergence is asserted.

Use units $c=\hbar=1$, so the mass label $m$ has mass dimension one. The scale parameter $\rho>0$ is dimensionless. On scalar field configurations put
$$(C_\rho\phi)(x)=\rho^{-1}\phi(\rho^{-1}x),$$
the engineering scaling of a four-dimensional scalar field. On a family of mass-labelled functionals or maps $A=(A_m)_{m\ge0}$, define
$$(\sigma_\rho A)_m=C_\rho^*(A_{\rho^{-1}m}),\qquad (C_\rho^*F)(\phi)=F(C_{\rho^{-1}}\phi).$$
Extend this action coefficientwise to formal series and maps. It obeys $\sigma_1=\operatorname{id}$ and $\sigma_{\rho\tau}=\sigma_\rho\sigma_\tau$; the mass label scales as $m\mapsto\rho m$. The dilation sends compact supports to compact supports and normalizes the proper orthochronous Poincare action.

Assume for each $\rho>0$ that $\mathcal S$ and its scale conjugate $\sigma_\rho\mathcal S\sigma_\rho^{-1}$ are both normalized scalar time-ordered systems for the same mass-indexed free data after the stated mass relabelling. Define $Z_\rho$ to be the unique formal local map with identity linear term satisfying
$$\sigma_\rho\mathcal S\sigma_\rho^{-1}=\mathcal S\circ Z_\rho.$$
Here $Z_\rho(V)=V+O(V^2)$ means that all terms after the linear term have interaction degree at least two. This is a conditional definition: no existence of a time-ordered system for any particular model is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice, the mass-indexed normalized systems, and the scale-admissibility condition in the Definition.

[A1] Assume Countable Choice [[def-countable-choice]] for the supplied scalar contraction contract and the given-systems comparison result. No new arbitrary graph extension is chosen in this scale-action construction.

[F1] [[def-qftx-local-time-ordered-system]]: a scalar time-ordered system is a sequence of symmetric maps on compactly supported finite-jet polynomial functionals of a smooth real scalar field, with $T_0=1$, $T_1=\operatorname{id}$, and the stated causal, covariance, scaling, and formal unitarity conditions.

[F2] [[prop-qftx-finite-renormalization-composition]]: for two systems satisfying that contract for the same free data and first-order normalization, there is a unique formal local map $Z(V)=V+O(V^2)$ with $S'=S\circ Z$; these maps preserve support and scalar Poincare covariance.

[F3] [[def-qftx-local-time-ordered-system]]: for $\gamma=(a,\Lambda)$ in the proper orthochronous Poincare group, $(\gamma\cdot\phi)(x)=\phi(\Lambda^{-1}(x-a))$ and $(\gamma\cdot F)(\phi)=F(\gamma^{-1}\cdot\phi)$.

[F4] [[def-qftx-local-time-ordered-system]]: the time-ordered maps are equivariant under this action, as expressed by the covariance condition in its Definition.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct verification of the scaling action and conditional well-definedness.

**Given:** [A1] and [F1]–[F4].

1.1 Direct substitution gives $C_\rho C_\tau\phi(x)=(\rho\tau)^{-1}\phi((\rho\tau)^{-1}x)=C_{\rho\tau}\phi(x)$. Repeated chain rule gives $\partial^\alpha(C_\rho\phi)(x)=\rho^{-1-|\alpha|}(\partial^\alpha\phi)(\rho^{-1}x)$, so field substitution preserves finite polynomial jets; positive dilations also send compact coefficient supports to compact sets. Thus the induced functional pullback preserves the local polynomial class and has inverse $C_{\rho^{-1}}^*$. The mass relabelling composes as $\tau^{-1}\rho^{-1}m=(\rho\tau)^{-1}m$, including $m=0$, so $\sigma_{\rho\tau}=\sigma_\rho\sigma_\tau$ and $\sigma_{\rho^{-1}}$ is the inverse action. For $T_a\phi(x)=\phi(x-a)$, direct substitution gives $C_\rho T_a C_\rho^{-1}=T_{\rho a}$; scalar dilations commute with each Lorentz transformation. By [F3] this action normalizes the proper orthochronous Poincare group, and by [F4] conjugation preserves covariance of the time-ordered system. [given, F1, F3, F4, algebra]

2.1 At each mass component the unscaled and scale-conjugated systems have the same free data and satisfy the normalization hypotheses of [F2] by the scale-admissibility condition in the Definition. Under [A1], applying [F2] gives a unique formal local map $Z_\rho(V)=V+O(V^2)$ preserving support and covariance and satisfying the displayed equation. This defines $Z_\rho$ only under these hypotheses. [A1, F1, F2, step 1.1]

3.1 For $\rho=1$, step 1.1 gives $\sigma_1=\operatorname{id}$, so the defining equation is $\mathcal S=\mathcal S\circ Z_1$. The identity map also satisfies the finite-renormalization conditions, and uniqueness in [F2] gives $Z_1=\operatorname{id}$. [A1, F2, step 1.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The time-ordered systems and scale-admissibility condition are supplied inputs. Their comparison map is determined uniquely by a triangular coefficient recursion under Countable Choice from the actual contraction contract. Constructing time-ordered systems is a separate theorem with its own Choice hypotheses; no existence of a system for a particular model is asserted here.

ITEM lem-qftx-two-dimensional-covariance-log-integrability

## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice, its countable-family consequence, $m>0$, and the fixed mollifier from the earlier cutoff definition. Let $C_m$ be the translation-invariant covariance distribution whose Fourier multiplier is $(|p|^2+m^2)^{-1}$ in the convention $\widehat f(p)=\int e^{-ip\cdot x}f(x)\,d^2x$. It is represented by the even locally integrable function

$$G_m(z)=\frac1{4\pi}\int_0^\infty t^{-1}\exp\!\left(-m^2t-\frac{|z|^2}{4t}\right)dt,\qquad z\ne0.$$

There are constants $K_m,K_{m,R}<\infty$ such that $|G_m(z)|\le K_m(1+|\log|z||)$ for $0<|z|\le1$, $|G_m(z)|\le K_m e^{-m|z|}/\sqrt{m|z|}$ for $|z|\ge1$, and $G_m^q\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R^2)$ for every finite $q\ge1$. For the mollified fields of the earlier cutoff definition, $\kappa_{\epsilon,\delta}(w)=\int\rho_\epsilon(a)\rho_\delta(a-w)\,d^2a$ and $C_{\epsilon,\delta}=G_m*\kappa_{\epsilon,\delta}$ satisfy $\mathbb E[\Phi_\epsilon(x)\Phi_\delta(y)]=C_{\epsilon,\delta}(x-y)$. Moreover, $C_{\epsilon,\delta}(z)\to G_m(z)$ for every $z\ne0$ as $\epsilon,\delta\downarrow0$, and for every $R>0$ there are $K_{m,R},\epsilon_{m,R}>0$ with

$$|C_{\epsilon,\delta}(z)|\le K_{m,R}(1+|\log|z||)\quad(0<|z|\le R,\ 0<\epsilon,\delta\le\epsilon_{m,R}).$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Assume the Axiom of Choice and its countable-family consequence. Let $m>0$, use the Fourier convention and Gaussian covariance from the Statement and preceding definition, and fix the nonnegative compactly supported mollifier $\rho$ with unit integral.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is required by the Gaussian random distribution supplier. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[A2] Countable Choice is the countable-family instance inherited from [A1] and is used by the Gaussian Fourier transform supplier. [[def-countable-choice]]

[F1] For real Schwartz tests, the centered Gaussian field has covariance multiplier $(|p|^2+m^2)^{-1}$ with inverse-transform normalization $(2\pi)^{-2}$. [[thm-qftx-tempered-massive-gaussian-field]]

[F2] Under $\mathcal F_{2\pi}f(\xi)=\int e^{-2\pi i x\cdot\xi}f(x)dx$, $\mathcal F_{2\pi}(e^{-\pi s|x|^2})(\xi)=s^{-1}e^{-\pi|\xi|^2/s}$ in dimension two. [[lem-euclidean-gaussian-fourier-transform-with-two-pi-normalization]]

[F3] Absolutely integrable functions on sigma-finite product measure spaces may be integrated in either order. [[thm-fubini-theorem-for-l-one-on-sigma-finite-product-spaces]]

[F4] Almost-everywhere convergence under one integrable majorant implies convergence of the integrals. [[thm-dominated-convergence]]

[F5] $\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-u^2}du=\sqrt\pi$. [[thm-gaussian-integral]]

[F6] A monotone differentiable substitution on an improper interval preserves the integral under the supplier's compact-truncation hypotheses. [[thm-substitution-for-improper-integrals]]

[F7] The real exponential is differentiable with derivative itself and is given by its all-real power series. [[thm-derivative-of-exponential]] [[def-real-exponential-function-and-e]]

[F8] $\exp(-x)=1/\exp(x)>0$. [[cor-exponential-reciprocal-and-positivity]]

[F9] For each fixed integer power and positive scale, the exponential dominates that power at $+\infty$. [[thm-exponential-beats-every-polynomial]]

[F10] If a continuous function $G$ has a Riemann-integrable interior derivative on a compact interval, Newton-Leibniz gives the integral as the endpoint difference. [[thm-newton-leibniz-with-interior-derivative]]

[F11] A convergent nonnegative improper Riemann integral on a half-line equals the Lebesgue integral there. [[thm-nonnegative-improper-riemann-integral-agrees-with-the-lebesgue-integral-on-a-half-line]]

[F12] A continuous real function on a closed interval is Riemann integrable. [[thm-continuous-on-a-rectangle-is-riemann-integrable]]

[F13] The $2\pi$-normalized Fourier transform maps Schwartz functions to Schwartz functions; rescaling the frequency variable gives the same property for $\widehat f(p)=\int e^{-ip\cdot x}f(x)dx$. [[cor-fourier-transform-is-a-topological-automorphism-of-schwartz-space]]

[F14] The $2\pi$-normalized Fourier transform is $\mathcal Ff(\xi)=\int f(x)e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}dx$. [[def-fourier-transform-on-l-one-of-rn]]

[F15] For a $C^1$ diffeomorphism $T$ between Euclidean open sets, substitution in the Lebesgue integral multiplies by $|\det DT|$; in particular translations and the reflection $p\mapsto-p$ preserve Lebesgue measure. [[cor-c-one-change-of-variables-for-l-one-functions]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** heat-kernel representation followed by explicit one-dimensional bounds.

**Given:** [A1]–[A2], [F1]–[F6], $m>0$, and the fixed mollifier.

1.1 For $a>0$ and $T>0$, substitute $u=-at$ by [F6]. The function $u\mapsto\exp(u)$ is continuous and has derivative $\exp(u)$ by [F7], and this derivative is Riemann integrable on $[-aT,0]$ by [F12]; hence [F10] gives $\int_0^T\exp(-at)dt=a^{-1}(1-\exp(-aT))$. By [F9] with power $1$ and scale $a$, $T/\exp(aT)\to0$; for $T\ge1$, [F8] gives $0<\exp(-aT)\le T/\exp(aT)$, so $\exp(-aT)\to0$. Thus $\int_0^\infty\exp(-at)dt=1/a$ as a nonnegative improper Riemann integral, and as a Lebesgue integral by [F11]. Next, [F2] and [F14] with $s=t/\pi$ and $\xi=-z/(2\pi)$ give $(2\pi)^{-2}\int e^{ip\cdot z}e^{-t|p|^2}d^2p=(2\pi)^{-2}(\pi/t)e^{-|z|^2/(4t)}=(4\pi t)^{-1}e^{-|z|^2/(4t)}=:h_t(z)$. Its integral is one: [F5] in each coordinate and $x=2\sqrt t\,u$ by [F6] give $\int h_t(z)d^2z=1$. For every $p$, the Laplace identity gives $(|p|^2+m^2)^{-1}=\int_0^\infty e^{-t(|p|^2+m^2)}dt$. If $\psi\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R^2)$, the displayed inverse-kernel formula and Fubini give $\int h_t(z)\psi(z)d^2z=(2\pi)^{-2}\int e^{-t|p|^2}\widehat\psi(-p)d^2p$; the exchanges are absolute because $e^{-t|p|^2}\in L^1$ and $\psi\in L^1$. Therefore $\int_0^\infty e^{-m^2t}\int h_t(z)|\psi(z)|d^2zdt\le\|\psi\|_\infty/m^2$, while the Fourier-side integral is absolutely convergent since [F13] makes $\widehat\psi$ Schwartz and $m>0$. Integrating the Fourier identity over $t$ and applying Fubini [F3] in $(t,z)$ and $(t,p)$ gives the heat-kernel superposition's pairing $(2\pi)^{-2}\int\widehat\psi(-p)(|p|^2+m^2)^{-1}d^2p$; reflection $p\mapsto-p$ preserves Lebesgue measure by [F15] and yields the covariance pairing. For real Schwartz $f,g$, set $\Psi(z)=\int\overline{f(y+z)}g(y)d^2y$. Differentiation under this integral is justified locally in $z$ by bounded derivatives of $f$ and the integrability of $g$. For multiindices $\alpha,\beta$, write $x=y+z$ and expand $z^\alpha=(x-y)^\alpha$; each term of $z^\alpha\partial^\beta\Psi(z)$ is bounded uniformly in $z$ by a finite product of seminorms $\sup_x|x^\gamma\partial^\beta f(x)|$ and integrals $\int|y^{\alpha-\gamma}g(y)|d^2y$, which are finite because $f,g$ are Schwartz. Thus $\Psi\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R^2)$. Absolute Fubini and the substitution $x=y+z$ give $\widehat\Psi(-p)=\int\!\!\int e^{ip\cdot(x-y)}\overline{f(x)}g(y)d^2x\,d^2y=\overline{\widehat f(p)}\widehat g(p)$; absolute integrability follows from $\|f\|_1\|g\|_1<\infty$. Applying the just-proved kernel pairing to this Schwartz test $\Psi$ recovers exactly the covariance $C_m(f,g)$ in [F1]. Thus the heat-kernel superposition is the a.e. spatial density of the covariance distribution, and its value for each $z\ne0$ is the defining integral for $G_m(z)$. [A1, A2, F1, F2, F3, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, F10, F11, F12, F13, F14, F15, given, algebra]

2.1 For $0<r=|z|\le1$, the part of the defining $t$-integral over $t\ge1$ is bounded by $\int_1^\infty e^{-m^2t}dt<\infty$ by step 1.1. On $0<t<1$, drop $e^{-m^2t}\le1$ and substitute $u=r^2/(4t)$ by [F6] to bound the remaining integral by $\int_{r^2/4}^\infty e^{-u}du/u\le\log(4/r^2)+\int_1^\infty e^{-u}du/u$; the last tail is finite because $1/u\le1$ and step 1.1 gives $\int_1^\infty e^{-u}du<\infty$. This gives $|G_m(z)|\le K_m(1+|\log r|)$. For $r\ge1$, substitute $t=(r/(2m))e^v$ in [F6]; then $m^2t+r^2/(4t)=mr\cosh v$ and $dt/t=dv$. Define $\cosh v=(\exp(v)+\exp(-v))/2$. The exponential power series gives $\cosh v=\sum_{j\ge0}v^{2j}/(2j)!\ge1+v^2/2$. [F5] and a further scaling substitution from [F6] give $G_m(z)\le (4\pi)^{-1}e^{-mr}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-mr v^2/2}dv\le K_m e^{-mr}/\sqrt{mr}$. Together with the almost-everywhere density from step 1.1, this proves that $G_m$ is a locally integrable representative of the covariance distribution. [F5, F6, F7, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For every finite $p\ge1$ and $0<s\le1$, the same annuli scaled to $s$ show $\int_{|z|\le s}(1+|\log|z||)^p d^2z\le K_p s^2(1+|\log s|)^p$: on $s2^{-j-1}<|z|\le s2^{-j}$ the area is at most $\pi s^22^{-2j}$ and the logarithmic factor is at most $(1+|\log s|+(j+1)\log2)^p$. Put $A=1+|\log s|\ge1$; then this factor is at most $A^p(1+(j+1)\log2)^p$. The ratio of successive terms $4^{-j}(1+(j+1)\log2)^p$ tends to $1/4$, so after an index depending only on $p$ it is at most $1/2$; the remaining series is bounded by its finite initial sum plus a geometric tail. This proves the bound with $K_p$ independent of $s$, hence $G_m^p\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$. The integral formula is continuous for $z\ne0$: for any sequence $z_k\to z\ne0$, choose $c>0$ so $|z_k|^2/4\ge c$ for all sufficiently large $k$. On $0<t\le1$, substitution $u=c/t$ by [F6] gives $\int_0^1t^{-1}e^{-c/t}dt=\int_c^\infty u^{-1}e^{-u}du<\infty$; the finite interval up to $1$ is bounded and the tail is at most $\int_1^\infty e^{-u}du$, finite by step 1.1. On $t\ge1$, $t^{-1}e^{-m^2t}\le e^{-m^2t}$, integrable by step 1.1. Thus [F4] applies and gives sequential continuity. [F4, F6, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Let $R_\rho>0$ satisfy $\operatorname{supp}\rho\subseteq B(0,R_\rho)$. For $f_{\epsilon,x}(u)=\rho_\epsilon(x-u)$ and $f_{\delta,y}(v)=\rho_\delta(y-v)$, the Gaussian covariance [F1] and the kernel representation in steps 1.1–2.1 give $\mathbb E[\Phi_\epsilon(x)\Phi_\delta(y)]=\int\!\!\int f_{\epsilon,x}(u)f_{\delta,y}(v)G_m(u-v)d^2u\,d^2v$. This integral is absolute because the tests are bounded and compactly supported and $G_m\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$. Put $z=x-y$, $a=x-u$, and $b=y-v$; [F15] preserves Lebesgue measure and yields $\int\!\!\int\rho_\epsilon(a)\rho_\delta(b)G_m(z-a+b)d^2a\,d^2b$. With $w=a-b$, Fubini [F3] gives this as $\int G_m(z-w)\kappa_{\epsilon,\delta}(w)d^2w=C_{\epsilon,\delta}(z)$. The nonnegative correlation kernel $\kappa_{\epsilon,\delta}$ has integral one, support in a disk of radius $2R_\rho q$ for $q=\max(\epsilon,\delta)$, and $\|\kappa_{\epsilon,\delta}\|_\infty\le\|\rho\|_\infty q^{-2}$. Its support shrinks to zero, so continuity of $G_m$ away from zero gives $C_{\epsilon,\delta}(z)\to G_m(z)$ for each $z\ne0$. To get the uniform bound, fix bounded $|z|\le R$. If $|z|$ exceeds twice the support radius, then $|z-w|\ge|z|/2$ throughout the convolution and step 2.1 bounds the integrand by $K_{m,R}(1+|\log|z||)$. Otherwise $|z|\le4R_\rho q$ and $|z-w|\le6R_\rho q$; the scaled dyadic-annulus estimate in step 3.1 gives $\int_{|u|\le6R_\rho q}|G_m(u)|d^2u\le K_mq^2(1+|\log q|)$. Multiplying by $\|\kappa\|_\infty$ bounds the convolution by $K_m(1+|\log q|)\le K_{m,R}(1+|\log|z||)$ because $q\ge|z|/(4R_\rho)$. Choose $\epsilon_{m,R}$ small enough that $6R_\rho q\le1$. This proves the uniform estimate for all $0<\epsilon,\delta\le\epsilon_{m,R}$. [F1, F3, F4, F15, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, given, algebra] ∎

ITEM lem-qftx-anyonic-vacuum-reverse-adjacent-relation

## Statement

Let $\mathcal C$ be a unitary spherical fusion category over $\mathbb C$. Let $a,b,c$ be simple objects, with $a$ self-dual, $N_{aa}^{\mathbf1}=1$, and supply the normalized vacuum-channel self-duality datum with sign $\kappa_a\in\{+1,-1\}$ and positive spherical dimension $d_a$. For $n\ge3$, take the fixed-boundary repeated-$a$ path space $H_{a,n;b,c}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal C}(c,T_n)$ with $T_0=b$ and $T_{j+1}=T_j\otimes a$, as in [[def-anyonic-fusion-path-space]]. Let $P_i=P_{i,\mathbf1}$ be the orthogonal local vacuum-channel projectors and put $U_i=\delta_aP_i$, where $\delta_a=\kappa_ad_a$. Then, for every $1\le i\le n-2$,

$$U_{i+1}U_iU_{i+1}=U_{i+1}.$$

This supplies the reverse adjacent relation for the repeated-charge Temperley–Lieb family.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The unitary spherical fusion category, self-dual charge $a$, normalized vacuum-channel cup/cap datum, fixed-boundary path space, and indices $1\le i\le n-2$.

[F1] [[def-anyonic-fusion-path-space]]: $H_{a,n;b,c}=\operatorname{Hom}(c,b\otimes a^{\otimes n})$ has split-tree bases indexed by intermediate simple charges and every vertex multiplicity coordinate.

[F2] [[def-anyonic-local-channel-projector]]: $P_{i,s}=F_i^{-1}\Pi_sF_i$, where $F_i$ is the typed F-move coordinate matrix and $\Pi_s$ retains all multiplicity coordinates of pair output $s$.

[F3] [[def-qc-tqc-f-symbol]]: for the three-site associator, the coefficient $F_{f,e}$ is the target right-tree coordinate of the left-tree basis map after applying the inverse associator; the tree bases are orthonormal.

[F4] [[thm-anyonic-local-channel-projectors-are-orthogonal]]: in a unitary fusion category with orthonormal split-tree bases, each local channel projector is an orthogonal projection.

[F5] [[def-anyonic-frobenius-schur-sign]]: $d_a>0$, $\kappa_a\in\{+1,-1\}$, and the normalized duality maps are $\operatorname{coev}=\sqrt{d_a}\,v$ and $\operatorname{ev}=\kappa_a\sqrt{d_a}\,v^\dagger$.

[F6] [[lem-anyonic-vacuum-f-symbol-frobenius-schur-normalization]]: in the stated orthonormal vacuum gauge, $(F^{aaa}_a)_{\mathbf1,\mathbf1}=\kappa_a/d_a$ and $\delta_a=\kappa_ad_a$.

[F7] [[thm-anyonic-channel-projectors-are-reparenthesization-independent]]: canonical F-move paths between the same fixed parenthesizations give the same local channel operator.

## Proof

**Given:** The category and path data in the Statement, with the split-tree inner products and normalized vacuum gauge from [F2]–[F6].

1.1 Choose a parenthesization grouping the three sites $a_i,a_{i+1},a_{i+2}$ and decompose the split-tree basis by their simple total charge $y$ and the spectator labels. If the first pair fuses to $\mathbf1$, then its fusion with the third $a$ has total charge $y=\mathbf1\otimes a\cong a$; the same is true if the last pair fuses to $\mathbf1$. On the $y=a$ block each vacuum-channel subspace has dimension $N_{aa}^{\mathbf1}N_{\mathbf1 a}^{a}=1$, since $N_{aa}^{\mathbf1}=1$ and the tensor unit acts as the identity. On every $y\not\cong a$ block both projectors vanish. Each fixed spectator label and multiplicity coordinate gives a copy of this local block, where $P_i$ and $P_{i+1}$ act as rank-one orthogonal projections onto unit vectors $e$ and $f$, by [F4]. [F1, F2, F4, given, construct]

2.1 By the F-symbol coordinate convention in [F3], the vacuum-to-vacuum entry of the three-site F-move is the coordinate of one unit basis vector in the other tree basis. Therefore $|\langle e,f\rangle|=|(F^{aaa}_a)_{\mathbf1,\mathbf1}|=|\kappa_a/d_a|=1/d_a$ by [F5] and [F6], so $|\langle e,f\rangle|^2=d_a^{-2}$. [F3, F5, F6, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For unit vectors $e,f$, the rank-one projection formula gives $P_fP_eP_f=|\langle e,f\rangle|^2P_f$: on $x$, its value is $\langle f,x\rangle\langle e,f\rangle\langle f,e\rangle f$. Therefore $P_{i+1}P_iP_{i+1}=d_a^{-2}P_{i+1}$ on each $y=a$ spectator copy; on every other block both sides are zero. Tensoring with the spectator identities and transporting to the fixed path parenthesization preserves the relation by [F7]. [F4, F7, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Since $\delta_a=\kappa_ad_a$ and $\kappa_a^2=1$, $\delta_a^2=d_a^2$. Thus $U_{i+1}U_iU_{i+1}=\delta_a^3P_{i+1}P_iP_{i+1}=\delta_a^3d_a^{-2}P_{i+1}=\delta_aP_{i+1}=U_{i+1}$. The sign is retained in the final equality. [F5, F6, step 3.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The published anyonic projector proposition supplies the square relation, the left-oriented adjacent relation, and distant commutation, but its Statement does not list this reverse adjacent relation. The rank-one calculation above proves that missing direction from the same normalized F-coefficient, so the Hecke braid theorem can be applied to the actual anyonic family without assuming the omitted relation.
