
ITEM pthm-tgrx-hydrostatic-variation

## Statement

Fix the background data $\Sigma,h,N,\Phi,q,T_0,p$ of the adopted compact hydrostatic functional and vary only its chemical-potential parameter $\mu_0$ inside the open interval $U$ specified by that model. The local temperature profile $T=T_0/N$ has constant Killing-normalized value $NT=T_0$; this is the model's Tolman profile. Write $p_\mu$ and $p_{\mu\mu}$ for the first and second derivatives of the pressure function with respect to its chemical-potential argument, evaluated at $(T_0/N(x),(\mu_0-q\Phi(x))/N(x))$. Then

$$\partial_{\mu_0}W_{\rm hs}=\int_\Sigma\frac{p_\mu(T_0/N,(\mu_0-q\Phi)/N)}{k_BT_0}\,dV_h,\qquad \partial_{\mu_0}^2W_{\rm hs}=\int_\Sigma\frac{p_{\mu\mu}(T_0/N,(\mu_0-q\Phi)/N)}{N k_BT_0}\,dV_h.$$

The model chemical-potential susceptibility $\chi_{\rm hs}:=\partial_{\mu_0}^2W_{\rm hs}$ is nonnegative if $p_{\mu\mu}(T_0/N(x),(\mu_0-q\Phi(x))/N(x))\ge0$ for every $x\in\Sigma$. With the stated SI conventions, $W_{\rm hs}$ is dimensionless and $\chi_{\rm hs}$ has units $\mathrm{J^{-2}}$. This is a deduction inside the adopted effective model, not an empirical charge-fluctuation claim.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A compact nonempty smooth spatial manifold $\Sigma$ without boundary, smooth $h,N,\Phi$, $N>0$, fixed $q\in\mathbb R$ and $T_0>0$, $p\in C^2(D)$, and an open interval $U$ around $\mu_0$ on which all local $(T,\mu)$ profiles remain in $D$; hold these background inputs fixed while varying $\mu_0$.

[F1] [[post-tgrx-hydrostatic-effective-model]]: $W_{\rm hs}=(k_BT_0)^{-1}\int_\Sigma Np(T_0/N,(\mu_0-q\Phi)/N)\,dV_h$ on the specified parameter interval, with the background held fixed.

[F2] [[def-tgrx-static-hydrostatic-data]]: $N$ is smooth and strictly positive, $dV_h$ is the Riemannian volume density on compact $\Sigma$, and $p$ is $C^2$ on an open domain containing the local profiles.

[F3] [[def-riemannian-volume-density]]: the Riemannian density has positive coordinate coefficient $\sqrt{\det(h_{ij})}$ and is integrated independently of orientation.

[A1] The pointwise convexity condition $p_{\mu\mu}(T_0/N(x),(\mu_0-q\Phi(x))/N(x))\ge0$ holds for every $x\in\Sigma$ when asserting the nonnegative conclusion.

## Proof

1.1 Choose a closed interval $I_0\subset U$ whose interior contains $\mu_0$, and for $r\in I_0$ define $f(r,x)=N(x)p(T_0/N(x),(r-q\Phi(x))/N(x))/(k_BT_0)$. The image of $I_0\times\Sigma$ in $D$ is compact, so $p_\mu$ and $p_{\mu\mu}$ are bounded and uniformly continuous there. Since $N$ is continuous and positive on compact $\Sigma$, its image has a minimum $N_{\min}>0$; also $dV_h(\Sigma)<\infty$. The one-variable mean value theorem and uniform continuity of $\partial_r f$ make the difference quotients of $f$ converge uniformly in $x$ to $\partial_r f=p_\mu(T_0/N,(r-q\Phi)/N)/(k_BT_0)$. For these quotients $q_h$, the estimate $|\int_\Sigma q_h\,dV_h-\int_\Sigma\partial_r f\,dV_h|\le dV_h(\Sigma)\sup_x|q_h(x)-\partial_r f(r,x)|$ tends to zero. Thus differentiation passes through the finite-volume integral and yields $\partial_{\mu_0}W_{\rm hs}=\int_\Sigma p_\mu/(k_BT_0)\,dV_h$. [given, F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 Applying the same uniform difference-quotient argument to $\partial_r f$, whose derivative is $\partial_r^2 f=p_{\mu\mu}(T_0/N,(r-q\Phi)/N)/(N k_BT_0)$, gives $\partial_{\mu_0}^2W_{\rm hs}=\int_\Sigma p_{\mu\mu}/(N k_BT_0)\,dV_h$. The factors $N$ cancel once in the first derivative, while the second derivative retains the positive factor $1/N$. [step 1.1, F1, F2, algebra]

3.1 By [A1], each integrand value in the second formula is nonnegative because $N(x)>0$, $k_B>0$, and $T_0>0$. By [F3], the Riemannian volume density is positive, so its integral is nonnegative and therefore $\chi_{\rm hs}\ge0$. [step 2.1, F3, A1, algebra] ∎

ITEM ex-tgrx-entropy-does-not-close-hydrodynamics

## Example

Suppose an application supplies a shared thermodynamic state map on which the pressure in the static hydrostatic functional equals the equilibrium pressure used by the local dissipative constitutive branch. At a common admissible local input with nonzero expansion $\theta$, fix any $\zeta_*>0$ in pascal-seconds and compare two model instances that agree on all hydrostatic data and use constant bulk-viscosity coefficients $\zeta_1=\zeta_*$ and $\zeta_2=2\zeta_*$. The bulk-viscosity coefficient here is the coefficient $\zeta$ multiplying the expansion in $\Pi=-\zeta c\theta$; $\theta=\nabla_a u^a$ is the expansion of the stated observer field. Their adopted hydrostatic functionals are identical, while their bulk-pressure corrections differ by $\Pi_2-\Pi_1=-\zeta_*c\theta\ne0$. This difference has units of pressure. Thus hydrostatic data alone do not fix the bulk viscosity. This is a conditional comparison of constitutive maps; it asserts neither a solution of the balance equations nor an empirical response.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Common hydrostatic data and the same pressure function in both model instances; a common admissible local constitutive input with $\theta\ne0$; and a constant $\zeta_*>0$ in $\mathrm{Pa\,s}$.

[F1] [[post-tgrx-hydrostatic-effective-model]]: the adopted $W_{\rm hs}$ depends on the static data and pressure function $p(T,\mu)$, and contains no bulk-viscosity coefficient.

[F2] [[post-tgr-first-order-dissipation]]: the constitutive formula includes the bulk-pressure correction $\Pi=-\zeta c\theta$.

[F3] [[post-tgr-first-order-dissipation]]: $\theta=\nabla_a u^a$ is the expansion of the smooth future unit observer field.

[F4] [[post-tgr-first-order-dissipation]]: $\theta$ has inverse-length units, $c>0$ is in metres per second, $\zeta$ is in pascal-seconds, and $\Pi$ is in pascals.

[A1] The state-map identification between the pressure in the hydrostatic functional and the equilibrium pressure of the dissipative branch is an additional application-specific assumption for this comparison.

[A2] Both constitutive maps are evaluated at the same admissible local input with $\theta\ne0$; no global solution of the balance equations is assumed.

## Verification

1.1 The two instances have the same $T_0,\mu_0,h,N,\Phi,q$ and $p(T,\mu)$ by [A1]. The formula in [F1] therefore has exactly the same integrand and integration measure for both instances; changing $\zeta$ does not alter $W_{\rm hs}$. [given, F1, A1, algebra]

1.2 The constants $\zeta_1=\zeta_*$ and $\zeta_2=2\zeta_*$ are $C^1$ and nonnegative because $\zeta_*>0$. By [F2], at the common input in [A2] their corrections satisfy $\Pi_2-\Pi_1=-(2\zeta_*)c\theta-(-\zeta_*c\theta)=-\zeta_*c\theta$, which is nonzero since $\zeta_*,c>0$ and $\theta\ne0$; $\theta$ has the definition in [F3] and the units are pascals by [F4]. [given, F2, F3, F4, A2, algebra]

2.1 Thus the same hydrostatic functional is compatible with two distinct bulk-pressure responses under the declared constitutive assumptions, so the functional alone does not identify $\zeta$. This conclusion is conditional on the stated model identification and local input; it is not a claim about a measured fluid or an actual solution. [step 1.1, step 1.2, A1, A2] ∎

ITEM def-tgrx-static-hydrostatic-data

## Definition

Let $\Sigma$ be a nonempty compact smooth three-manifold without boundary, let $h$ be a smooth Riemannian metric on $\Sigma$, and give $M=\mathbb R\times\Sigma$ the product smooth structure. Write $x^0=ct_{\rm phys}$ for the standard length coordinate on $\mathbb R$ and use spatial coordinates measured in metres. For a smooth function $N:\Sigma\to(0,\infty)$, extend $N$ constantly along $\mathbb R$ and define the metric on $M$ by $g=-N^2\,dx^0\otimes dx^0+h$, where $h$ is pulled back from $\Sigma$. The metric components and lapse $N$ are dimensionless in these length coordinates. This is a static spacetime model: the coefficients are independent of $x^0$, $\partial_{x^0}$ is the selected future timelike Killing field, and $N$ is its lapse, so $g(\partial_{x^0},\partial_{x^0})=-N^2$.

The Riemannian volume density of $h$ is denoted by $dV_h$; in length coordinates it has units of cubic metres and finite total volume, and no orientation of $\Sigma$ is required. In a fixed SI gauge, let $\Phi:\Sigma\to\mathbb R$ be a smooth electrostatic scalar potential in volts, let $q\in\mathbb R$ be a fixed charge in coulombs, let $T_0>0$ be a reference temperature in kelvins, and let $\mu_0\in\mathbb R$ be a reference chemical-potential parameter in joules. The sign convention is that the local electrochemical parameter is $\mu_0-q\Phi$. For the static Killing field $\xi=\partial_{x^0}$, its lapse is $N_\xi=N$; fix the Killing-normalized temperature $T_K:=N_\xi T$ to be the constant $T_0$, so the local temperature is $T(x)=T_0/N(x)$. Define the local chemical-potential field by $\mu(x)=(\mu_0-q\Phi(x))/N(x)$ for $x\in\Sigma$; then $q\Phi$ and $\mu$ have units of joules.

Let $D\subset(0,\infty)\times\mathbb R$ be an open domain for the pair $(T,\mu)$, measured in kelvins and joules, and let $p:D\to\mathbb R$ be a supplied $C^2$ pressure function with values in pascals. Require $(T(x),\mu(x))\in D$ at every $x\in\Sigma$. The function $p$ is model input; this definition does not derive an equation of state or assert that the prescribed data have a material realization.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The data in the Definition, including a nonempty compact smooth $\Sigma$, a smooth positive-definite metric $h$, a smooth positive lapse $N$, and the stated SI fields and parameters.

[F1] [[def-smooth-manifold]]: a smooth manifold is a topological manifold equipped with a maximal smooth atlas.

[F2] [[def-cartesian-product]]: $\mathbb R\times\Sigma$ is the set of ordered pairs with first entry in $\mathbb R$ and second in $\Sigma$.

[F3] [[prop-products-of-smooth-manifolds-have-a-canonical-product-smooth-structure]]: the product of smooth manifolds has the canonical product smooth structure and dimension equal to the sum of the dimensions.

[F4] [[def-riemannian-metric-and-riemannian-manifold]]: $h$ is a smooth symmetric tensor and $h_y(v,v)>0$ for each nonzero $v\in T_y\Sigma$.

[F5] [[def-gr-smooth-lorentzian-objects]]: a smooth symmetric nondegenerate metric with inertia $(3,1,0)$ is Lorentzian of signature $(-,+,+,+)$; a smooth timelike field supplies a time orientation.

[F6] [[def-gr-killing-field]]: a smooth vector field is Killing exactly when its metric Lie derivative vanishes, $\mathcal L_\xi g=0$.

[F7] [[def-riemannian-volume-density]] and [[lem-the-riemannian-volume-density-is-coordinate-independent]]: $dV_h=\sqrt{\det(h_{ij})}\,|dx^1dx^2dx^3|$ is a positive smooth density independent of coordinates.

[F8] [[def-tgr-framework-units]]: physical unit assignments use length coordinates $x^0=ct_{\rm phys}$ and spatial coordinates in metres.

[F9] [[def-tgr-framework-units]]: in a specified charged stationary gauge, charge $q$ is in coulombs and potential $\Phi$ is in volts, so $q\Phi$ has units of joules.

[F10] [[def-tgr-framework-units]]: for a smooth Killing field $\xi$, the lapse is $N_\xi:=\sqrt{-g(\xi,\xi)}>0$.

## Proof

1.1 By [F1] and [F2], $M=\mathbb R\times\Sigma$ is the Cartesian product equipped with the smooth product structure of [F3], hence is a smooth four-manifold. The tensor $g=-N^2 dx^0\otimes dx^0+h$ is smooth and symmetric. At each $(x^0,y)$ the tangent space splits as $\mathbb R\partial_{x^0}\oplus T_y\Sigma$, and for $a\in\mathbb R$ and $v\in T_y\Sigma$ its quadratic form is $-N(y)^2a^2+h_y(v,v)$. Since $N(y)>0$ and $h_y$ is positive definite by [F4], this form has one negative direction and three positive directions, and its direct-sum matrix is nondegenerate; hence it has signature $(-,+,+,+)$ by [F5]. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, algebra]

1.2 The field $\partial_{x^0}$ is smooth and has squared norm $-N^2<0$, so it is future timelike for the selected time orientation. Its local flow translates $x^0$ and fixes $y\in\Sigma$; since $N$ and $h$ are independent of $x^0$, this flow pulls $g$ back to itself and hence $\mathcal L_{\partial_{x^0}}g=0$ by [F6]. Thus the field is Killing, and its lapse equals the prescribed $N$ by [F10]. [given, F5, F6, F10, algebra]

2.1 By [F7], $dV_h$ is a globally defined positive smooth density, so the integral measure does not depend on an orientation or chart. Each point has a coordinate ball whose closure is compact inside a chart; compactness supplies a finite subcover by such balls. On each closure the smooth density coefficient is bounded and the coordinate ball has finite Euclidean volume, so $dV_h(\Sigma)<\infty$. The profiles $T=T_0/N$ and $\mu=(\mu_0-q\Phi)/N$ are smooth because $N$ is smooth and strictly positive. Their length-coordinate conventions follow from [F8], while $q\Phi$ has energy units by [F9]. This establishes that all prescribed data and the volume density used by the hydrostatic functional are well defined. [given, F7, F8, F9, algebra] ∎

ITEM rem-tgrx-island-assumptions

## Statement

For selected semiclassical gravity-plus-bath models, an island or quantum-extremal-surface calculation uses a specified boundary state and region factorization, a regulator and renormalization prescription, and gravitational path-integral assumptions. The replica prescription for a density matrix $\rho$ computes $S=(1-n\partial_n)\log\operatorname{Tr}(\rho^n)|_{n=1}$ by analytic continuation from replica number $n$; the gravitational calculation must also specify which saddle configurations contribute and which dominates. A Page-like curve here means a model entropy profile that rises and later falls under its stipulated parameter evolution. Such a curve is a conditional prediction of that selected calculation; it does not establish microscopic unitarity of an unspecified four-dimensional evaporating black hole or prove that a candidate saddle contributes in another theory.


ITEM def-qftx-kz-connection

## Definition

Let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra with Killing form $B$, let $n\ge2$, and let $V_1,\ldots,V_n$ be finite-dimensional complex vector spaces. A representation here is a complex-linear Lie algebra homomorphism $\rho_i:\mathfrak g\to\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(V_i)$, so $[\rho_i(x),\rho_i(y)]=\rho_i([x,y])$. Let $F_n(\mathbb C)=\{(z_1,\ldots,z_n)\in\mathbb C^n:z_i\ne z_j\text{ for }i\ne j\}$ be the ordered configuration space and put $V:=V_1\otimes\cdots\otimes V_n$ in the indicated factor order. For any pair of $B$-dual bases $(x_a),(x^a)$ define $\Omega_{ij}:=\sum_a\rho_i(x_a)^{(i)}\rho_j(x^a)^{(j)}\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(V)$, where each superscript means that the operator acts on that tensor factor and as the identity on all other factors. For $i<j$ put $\eta_{ij}:=(dz_i-dz_j)/(z_i-z_j)$, a globally defined complex-valued one-form on $F_n(\mathbb C)$. For $\kappa\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$, the **Casimir Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov connection** on the trivial vector bundle $F_n(\mathbb C)\times V\to F_n(\mathbb C)$ is

$$\nabla^{\mathrm{KZ}}_\kappa:=d-\kappa^{-1}\sum_{i<j}\Omega_{ij}\eta_{ij}.$$

Here a connection on this trivial bundle is a complex-linear operator on smooth $V$-valued sections satisfying $\nabla(fs)=df\otimes s+f\nabla s$ for every smooth scalar function $f$ and section $s$. The associated quadratic Casimir is $C_B:=\sum_a x_ax^a$ in the universal enveloping algebra; with the Killing-form convention above it is central, and $\Omega_{ij}$ is its tensor-level Casimir counterpart.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra, its Killing form and dual bases, the ordered configuration space with $n\ge2$, finite-dimensional representations $\rho_i$, and $\kappa\ne0$ as in the Definition.

[F1] [[def-ordered-configuration-space]]: $F_n(X)$ is the subspace of $X^n$ consisting of ordered tuples with pairwise distinct coordinates; here $X=\mathbb C$.

[F2] [[def-killing-form-of-a-semisimple-lie-algebra]]: $B(x,y)=\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{ad}_x\operatorname{ad}_y)$ on the finite-dimensional vector space $\mathfrak g$.

[F3] [[lem-the-casimir-element-is-independent-of-dual-bases]]: $\Omega=\sum_a x_a\otimes x^a$ is independent of the selected $B$-dual bases.

[F4] [[def-quadratic-casimir-element]]: the product image $C_B=\sum_a x_ax^a$ defines the quadratic Casimir in the universal enveloping algebra.

[F5] [[prop-the-quadratic-casimir-element-is-central]]: for complex semisimple $\mathfrak g$ with its Killing-form Casimir, $C_B$ lies in the center of the universal enveloping algebra.

## Proof

**Given:** [F1]–[F5], finite-dimensional representations $\rho_i$, and $\kappa\ne0$.

1.1 For each $i<j$, the sum defining $\Omega_{ij}$ is a finite endomorphism sum. Changing the $B$-dual bases leaves $\Omega$ unchanged by [F3], and applying $\rho_i\otimes\rho_j$ and identities on the other factors therefore leaves $\Omega_{ij}$ unchanged. Thus every $\Omega_{ij}\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(V)$ is well-defined. [F2, F3, given, algebra]

1.2 On $F_n(\mathbb C)$ the scalar $z_i-z_j$ is never zero by [F1]. Hence its reciprocal is smooth, so $\eta_{ij}=(dz_i-dz_j)/(z_i-z_j)$ is a globally defined smooth one-form; the finite sum $A=\kappa^{-1}\sum_{i<j}\Omega_{ij}\eta_{ij}$ is a smooth $\operatorname{End}(V)$-valued one-form. [F1, given, algebra]

2.1 For a smooth section $s:F_n(\mathbb C)\to V$ and smooth scalar function $f$, $\nabla(fs)=d(fs)-A(fs)=df\otimes s+f(ds-As)=df\otimes s+f\nabla s$, because each endomorphism coefficient in $A$ acts $\mathbb C$-linearly on $s$. Thus $\nabla=d-A$ is a connection on the stated trivial bundle. The operator is the Casimir KZ connection because its coefficients are the pairwise tensor Casimirs from [F3] and the associated quadratic Casimir [F4] is central by [F5]. [F3, F4, F5, step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra] ∎

ITEM ex-qftx-two-insertion-monodromy

## Example

Let $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb C)$, let $V=\mathbb C^2$ be its standard representation with its standard Hermitian inner product, and use the Killing-form Casimir tensor $\Omega_{12}$ on $V\otimes V$. For two insertions set $w=z_1-z_2\ne0$. At the basepoint $w_*=1$, take $z_2=0$ and $z_1=w$; along a chosen logarithm branch, a horizontal section with value $v_*$ at $w_*$ is $s(w)=\exp((\Omega_{12}/\kappa)\log(w/w_*))v_*$. The positive loop $w(t)=w_*e^{2\pi i t}$, equivalently $z_1(t)=e^{2\pi i t}$ and $z_2(t)=0$, has horizontal transport

$$P_\gamma=\exp(2\pi i\Omega_{12}/\kappa).$$

For the library's first-then-second fundamental-group product, the associated left monodromy representation uses $P_\gamma^{-1}$; equivalently, $P_\gamma$ is the right monodromy action by horizontal transport. This transport need not be unitary: with $\kappa=i$, it has eigenvalue $e^{\pi/4}$.

## Diagram and convention

The diagram uses $w=z_1-z_2$ as the relative coordinate; the arrow indicates increasing $t$ in $w(t)=w_*e^{2\pi i t}$, and the removed origin is the collision $z_1=z_2$. It records the positive loop only; the proof below derives transport from the differential equation and does not rely on the picture.

```tikz
\begin{tikzpicture}[>=stealth,thick]
  \draw[->] (-1.35,0) -- (1.45,0) node[right] {$\operatorname{Re}w$};
  \draw[->] (0,-1.35) -- (0,1.45) node[above] {$\operatorname{Im}w$};
  \draw (0,0) circle (1);
  \fill (0,0) circle (0.045) node[below right] {$0$};
  \fill (1,0) circle (0.045) node[below right] {$w_*$};
  \draw[->] (0.707,0.707) arc[start angle=45,end angle=90,radius=1];
  \node at (-0.25,1.12) {$w(t)$};
\end{tikzpicture}
```

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Two insertions, $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb C)$, the standard two-dimensional representation and Hermitian inner product, $w_*=z_1-z_2\ne0$, a selected logarithm branch near $w_*$, and $\kappa\ne0$.

[F1] [[def-qftx-kz-connection]]: for two insertions, the connection is $\nabla=d-\kappa^{-1}\Omega_{12}\,dw/w$ on $\mathbb C^\times$.

[F2] [[thm-qftx-kz-flatness]]: this connection is flat; horizontal transport is the local-system monodromy, with the left-representation convention obtained by inverting path transport when loops multiply first-then-second.

[F3] [[def-killing-form-of-a-semisimple-lie-algebra]]: the form is $B(x,y)=\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{ad}_x\operatorname{ad}_y)$.

## Verification

**Given:** [F1]–[F3] and the specified $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ representation.

1.1 The horizontal-section equation $\nabla s=0$ is $ds/dw=(\Omega_{12}/(\kappa w))s$. On a simply connected branch domain, differentiate the matrix exponential $s(w)=\exp((\Omega_{12}/\kappa)\log(w/w_*))v_*$; because its exponent is a scalar multiple of the fixed matrix $\Omega_{12}$, differentiation gives $s'(w)=\Omega_{12}s(w)/(\kappa w)$ and $s(w_*)=v_*$. [F1, given, algebra]

1.2 In the defining two-dimensional representation take $H=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1)$, $E=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$, and $F=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$. Their brackets are $[H,E]=2E$, $[H,F]=-2F$, and $[E,F]=H$. Computing the traces in [F3] gives $B(H,H)=8$, $B(E,F)=B(F,E)=4$, and all other pairings among these basis elements zero; hence the dual basis is $H/8,F/4,E/4$. [F3, given, algebra]

2.1 The loop $w(t)=w_*e^{2\pi i t}$ changes the continued logarithm by $2\pi i$. Substitution into the solution from step 1.1 gives $P_\gamma=\exp(2\pi i\Omega_{12}/\kappa)$. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 With the first-then-second group product, uniqueness of horizontal transport gives $P_{\alpha*\beta}=P_\beta P_\alpha$. Therefore $\rho([\gamma]):=P_\gamma^{-1}$ satisfies $\rho([\alpha][\beta])=(P_\beta P_\alpha)^{-1}=P_\alpha^{-1}P_\beta^{-1}$, so the left representation convention agrees with [F2] while the right action uses $P_\gamma$. [F2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Thus $\Omega_{12}=\tfrac18H\otimes H+\tfrac14(E\otimes F+F\otimes E)$. On $e_1\otimes e_1$ it acts as $\tfrac18(e_1\otimes e_1)$, so for $\kappa=i$ the positive-loop transport in step 2.1 has eigenvalue $\exp(2\pi i(1/8)/i)=e^{\pi/4}$. A unitary operator on a finite-dimensional Hermitian space has every eigenvalue of modulus one, whereas $|e^{\pi/4}|>1$; thus this KZ monodromy is not unitary. Its inverse has eigenvalue $e^{-\pi/4}$ and is also nonunitary. [F1, step 2.1, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
