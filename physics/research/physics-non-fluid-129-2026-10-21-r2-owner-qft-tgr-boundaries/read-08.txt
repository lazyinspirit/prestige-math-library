
ITEM prop-qftx-categorified-braid-euler

## Statement

Fix $n\ge2$ and the type-A Rouquier categorical braid-line model. Let $\chi(C)=\sum_m(-1)^m[C^m]$ be the alternating class of a bounded cochain complex in the split Grothendieck ring $K_0^{\mathrm{split}}(\mathrm{SBim}_n)$, and use the shift convention $v[X]=[X(1)]$. Let $\Phi:K_0^{\mathrm{split}}(\mathrm{SBim}_n)\to H_{S_n}$ be the algebra isomorphism with $\Phi([B_i])=H_i=v(T_i+1)$ and Hecke parameter $q=v^{-2}$. For $\beta\in B_n$, let $e(\beta)$ be its exponent sum, the number of positive letters minus the number of negative letters in any signed word for $\beta$, and let $T_\beta$ be the image of $\beta$ under the group homomorphism $B_n\to H_{S_n}^{\times}$ sending $\sigma_i\mapsto T_i$. Then

$$
\Phi\!\left(\chi(F(\beta))\right)=v^{e(\beta)}T_\beta.
$$

Consequently the normalized map $\beta\mapsto v^{-e(\beta)}\Phi(\chi(F(\beta)))$ is the standard Hecke braid homomorphism, and its normalization is multiplicative under braid stacking. Homotopy-equivalent complexes have the same Euler class, but need not be isomorphic as graded complexes.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $n\ge2$, a braid $\beta\in B_n$, and the Rouquier complexes and grading convention of [[def-qftx-categorified-line-model]].

[F1] [[prop-decategorification-of-a-rouquier-complex-is-the-hecke-braid-generator]]: in the stated shift convention, $\Phi(\chi(F_i))=vT_i$ and $\Phi(\chi(F_i^{-1}))=v^{-1}T_i^{-1}$ for each simple generator.

[F2] [[lem-euler-class-of-a-rouquier-complex-is-homotopy-invariant-and-multiplicative]]: $\chi$ is invariant under homotopy equivalence and satisfies $\chi(C\otimes_R D)=\chi(C)\chi(D)$ for the signed tensor totalization of bounded complexes with terms in $\mathrm{SBim}_n$.

[F3] [[thm-rouquier-complex-is-well-defined-up-to-canonical-homotopy-equivalence]]: signed words representing the same braid determine canonically homotopy-equivalent Rouquier complexes.

[F4] [[def-braid-group-by-the-artin-presentation]]: $B_n$ is generated by $\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_{n-1}$ with adjacent braid relations and distant commutation relations; inserting or deleting an inverse pair is also a word equality.

[F5] [[def-complex-homotopy-and-contractibility-in-an-additive-category]]: a cochain complex is contractible when there are maps $h^m:C^m\to C^{m-1}$ satisfying $1_{C^m}=d^{m-1}h^m+h^{m+1}d^m$ for every $m$.

[F6] [[def-qftx-categorified-line-model]]: a signed word $\sigma=\sigma_{i_1}^{\epsilon_1}\cdots\sigma_{i_r}^{\epsilon_r}$ is assigned the signed tensor totalization $F(\sigma)=F_{i_1}^{\epsilon_1}\otimes_R\cdots\otimes_R F_{i_r}^{\epsilon_r}$, and its braid has this complex up to canonical homotopy equivalence.

[A1] Use $T_\beta$ for the standard homomorphism image specified in the Statement; no physical interpretation of $v$ is assumed.

## Proof

1.1 Choose a signed word $\sigma=\sigma_{i_1}^{\epsilon_1}\cdots\sigma_{i_r}^{\epsilon_r}$ representing $\beta$. By the word definition and multiplicativity in [F2], $\chi(F(\sigma))=\prod_{k=1}^r\chi(F_{i_k}^{\epsilon_k})$. Applying the algebra homomorphism $\Phi$ and the generator values in [F1] gives $\Phi(\chi(F(\sigma)))=v^{\sum_k\epsilon_k}T_{i_1}^{\epsilon_1}\cdots T_{i_r}^{\epsilon_r}$. [A1, F1, F2, F6, construct]

2.1 The exponent sum is unchanged by each defining relation: the two sides of an adjacent braid relation each have sum $3$, the two sides of a distant commutation relation each have sum $2$, and an inverse pair has sum $0$. Thus $e(\sigma)$ descends to $e(\beta)\in\mathbb Z$ and satisfies $e(\beta\gamma)=e(\beta)+e(\gamma)$. The product of the $T_i^{\epsilon_k}$ is $T_\beta$ by the specified group homomorphism, so step 1.1 gives the displayed formula independent of the chosen word. [F4, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The comparison in [F3] identifies $F(\beta\gamma)$ in the homotopy category with the signed tensor totalization $F(\beta)\otimes_R F(\gamma)$. Applying homotopy invariance and multiplicativity from [F2], then applying the algebra homomorphism $\Phi$, gives $\Phi(\chi(F(\beta\gamma)))=\Phi(\chi(F(\beta)))\Phi(\chi(F(\gamma)))$. Dividing by $v^{e(\beta\gamma)}=v^{e(\beta)}v^{e(\gamma)}$ proves multiplicativity of the normalized map. [F2, F3, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 For a positive generator the formula is $vT_i$ and for its inverse it is $v^{-1}T_i^{-1}$ by [F1]. Since $v$ is an indeterminate in the Laurent polynomial coefficient ring, these factors cannot be discarded for nonzero exponent sum. The normalized value is exactly $T_\beta$ by step 2.1. [F1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Let $D$ be the two-term complex with $D^0=D^1=R$, differential $d^0=1_R$, and all other terms zero. The map $h^1=1_R:D^1\to D^0$ (all other $h^m=0$) satisfies the contracting-homotopy equation in [F5], so $D$ is homotopy equivalent to the zero complex. But its graded terms are $R$ in degrees $0$ and $1$, whereas the zero complex has zero terms; since $R=\mathbb Q[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ has $1\ne0$, the complexes are not isomorphic as graded complexes. Their Euler classes are both zero by [F2] and $[R]-[R]=0$. [F2, F5, F6, step 1.1] ∎

ITEM rem-tgrx-qnec-qfc-status

## Statement

For a smooth codimension-two cut $\Sigma$ on a stationary null hypersurface,
one proved form of the quantum null energy condition (QNEC) bounds the null
stress $\langle T_{kk}\rangle:=\langle T_{ab}k^ak^b\rangle$ below by
$\hbar/(2\pi)$ times $S_{\mathrm{out}}''/\mathcal A$. Here
$S_{\mathrm{out}}$ is the von Neumann entropy of the state restricted to one
side of $\Sigma$, $S_{\mathrm{out}}''$ is its second variation under a null
deformation, and $\mathcal A$ is the infinitesimal transverse area used in the
source limit. The cited proof applies to its stated free or superrenormalizable
bosonic field theories on stationary null hypersurfaces; it is not an
unrestricted assertion for every state, geometry or dimension. The quantum
focusing conjecture (QFC) instead proposes that, for a specified generalized
entropy functional $S_{\mathrm{gen}}=S_{\mathrm{grav}}+S_{\mathrm{out}}$ on a
smooth null cut, its source-defined quantum expansion
$\Theta[V;y]=(4G/\sqrt h)\,\delta S_{\mathrm{gen}}/\delta V(y)$ cannot
increase under a further forward affine deformation $V(y)$ along the null
generators; $h$ is the determinant of the cut's induced transverse metric.
This remains a conjecture in general. Weakly curved
Einstein-Hilbert-Gauss-Bonnet models have QFC counterexamples in dimensions
$d\ge5$, and later tests prove a restricted QFC for a class of two-dimensional
Jackiw-Teitelboim toy models while also reporting QFC counterexamples in a
particular regime of that model. Those theory- and regime-specific results
supply no universal QFC theorem for arbitrary higher-curvature effective
gravity. The linearized horizon model on this page defines no outside entropy,
generalized entropy functional or entropy shape derivative, so it neither
assumes nor derives QNEC or QFC.

## Remarks

The QNEC and QFC claims above are reported source formulations, not local
proof premises. The higher-curvature result is limited to the models and
dimensions in its cited analysis; its discussion also distinguishes the
pointwise conjecture from possible effective-field-theory resolution
conditions.

ITEM lem-qftx-hermite-coefficient-decay

## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $\mathcal S(\mathbb R^2;\mathbb C)$ be the space of smooth complex-valued functions $f$ for which $\sup_{x\in\mathbb R^2}|x^\alpha\partial^\beta f(x)|<\infty$ for every pair of multi-indices $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb N_0^2$. Let $(h_\alpha)_{\alpha\in\mathbb N_0^2}$ be the product Hermite basis of $L^2(\mathbb R^2)$ obtained from the multidimensional oscillator at $\hbar=1$, $m_1=m_2=1/2$, and $\omega_1=\omega_2=2$, and write $|\alpha|=\alpha_1+\alpha_2$. For $H=-\partial_{x_1}^2-\partial_{x_2}^2+|x|^2$ on $\mathcal S(\mathbb R^2;\mathbb C)$, every integer $r\ge0$ and every $f\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R^2;\mathbb C)$ satisfy

$$\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb N_0^2}(1+|\alpha|)^{2r}|\langle f,h_\alpha\rangle_{L^2}|^2\le\|H^r f\|_{L^2}^2<\infty.$$

Consequently the map $f\mapsto(\langle f,h_\alpha\rangle)_\alpha$ is continuous from Schwartz space to each weighted square-summable sequence space with norm $\bigl(\sum_\alpha(1+|\alpha|)^{2r}|c_\alpha|^2\bigr)^{1/2}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice, the two-dimensional Schwartz space, the common product Hermite basis in the Statement, and the differential operator $H$ acting on Schwartz functions.

[A1] [[def-countable-choice]]: every countable family of nonempty sets has a choice function.

[F1] [[lem-qft-hermite-oscillator-research-supplier]]: at $N=2$ and $a_1=a_2=c=1$, the product Hermite functions form a complete orthonormal basis and have oscillator eigenvalues $2|\alpha|+2$.

[F2] [[thm-nrqm-oscillator-multidimensional-oscillator-completeness]]: at $d=2$, $\hbar=1$, $m_j=1/2$, and $\omega_j=2$, the unscaled product basis is $h_\alpha=g_{\alpha_1}(x_1)g_{\alpha_2}(x_2)$, with eigenvalue $2|\alpha|+2$.

[F3] [[thm-nrqm-oscillator-hermite-recurrence]]: $g_n=H_n g_0/\sqrt{2^n n!}$, where $H_n$ is a degree-$n$ polynomial and $g_0(y)=\pi^{-1/4}e^{-y^2/2}$.

[F4] [[thm-hilbert-space-fourier-expansion]]: a complete orthonormal family in a Hilbert space gives the norm-convergent coefficient expansion and Parseval's identity.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** [A1], [F1]–[F3], $f\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R^2;\mathbb C)$, and an integer $r\ge0$.

1.1 With these parameters, $\hbar^2/(2m_j)=1$, $m_j\omega_j^2/2=1$, and $\ell_j=\sqrt{\hbar/(m_j\omega_j)}=1$. The parameter substitution in the inspected proof of [F1] uses precisely this scaling; [F2] specifies the resulting unscaled product basis $h_\alpha=g_{\alpha_1}(x_1)g_{\alpha_2}(x_2)$ and its eigenvalue $\lambda_\alpha=\sum_{j=1}^2(2\alpha_j+1)=2|\alpha|+2$. [A1, F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 By [F3] each Hermite factor is a polynomial times $e^{-x_j^2/2}$, so $h_\alpha$ and all its derivatives decay rapidly enough for integration by parts against any Schwartz function. The operator $H^r$ is a finite sum of polynomial-coefficient derivatives and maps Schwartz space continuously to itself, hence $H^r f\in L^2$. Repeated integration by parts in $x_1$ and $x_2$ has no boundary terms; it gives $\langle H^r f,h_\alpha\rangle=\langle f,H^r h_\alpha\rangle=\lambda_\alpha^r\langle f,h_\alpha\rangle$. [F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Parseval for the complete orthonormal basis [F1]–[F2] is supplied by [F4] and gives $\|H^r f\|_2^2=\sum_\alpha|\langle H^r f,h_\alpha\rangle|^2=\sum_\alpha(2|\alpha|+2)^{2r}|\langle f,h_\alpha\rangle|^2$. Since $1+|\alpha|\le2|\alpha|+2$, the asserted weighted bound follows. [F1, F2, F4, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Each $H^r$ is a finite differential expression with polynomial coefficients, so its Schwartz seminorms bound the pointwise polynomially weighted derivatives in that expression. In two dimensions, $\|u\|_2\le C\sup_x(1+|x|)^2|u(x)|$ because $(1+|x|)^{-4}$ is integrable. Thus $f\mapsto\|H^r f\|_2$ is a continuous seminorm on Schwartz space, and the weighted coefficient map is continuous. [step 3.1, algebra] ∎


ITEM prop-qftx-anomaly-obstruction

## Statement

Assume AC and Countable Choice, fix admissible flat scalar free data and a local scalar polynomial class under a scalar formal time-ordering theorem, and work conditionally in a time-ordering scheme satisfying that theorem's statement. At order $n$, let $\mathcal K_n$ be an $s,d_H$-stable subcomplex of the compactly supported local-form spaces defined earlier, with $\mathcal K_n^{0,4}$ exactly the allowed local counterterm densities. For each ghost number $r$, put
$$\mathscr F_n^r:=\mathcal K_n^{r,4}/d_H\mathcal K_n^{r,3},$$
the local-functional quotient formed using this same subcomplex, and let $\mathscr F_n^\bullet\llbracket\hbar\rrbracket$ denote formal power series. Let $\mathcal W_\hbar=s+\sum_{j\ge1}\hbar^j\mathcal W_j$ be an odd formal operator of ghost number one on this module, with $\mathcal W_\hbar^2=0$ and leading coefficient the induced $s$. Let $X\in\mathscr F_n^0\llbracket\hbar\rrbracket$ and suppose its first possible breaking is
$$\mathcal W_\hbar X=\hbar^n\mathcal A+O(\hbar^{n+1}),\qquad n\ge1,\qquad \mathcal A=[a_4]_{\mathscr F_n},$$
where $\hbar$ is a formal indeterminate, $O(\hbar^{n+1})$ means terms of formal degree at least $n+1$, every lower coefficient vanishes, and $a_4\in\mathcal K_n^{1,4}$ is a compactly supported local four-form of ghost number one. Then $s a_4$ is $d_H$-exact within $\mathcal K_n$. The counterterm shift $X\mapsto X-\hbar^n[b_4]_{\mathscr F_n}$, with $b_4\in\mathcal K_n^{0,4}$, cancels the order-$n$ breaking in $\mathscr F_n^1$ if and only if
$$[a_4]_{s|d_H}=0\quad\text{in}\quad H^{1,4}(s|d_H;\mathcal K_n).$$
This is a conditional obstruction statement. Locality of $a_4$, nilpotence of the formal Ward differential, and admissibility of the counterterm space are assumptions; the result does not establish a quantum action principle.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The relative local-form conventions, the Abelian nilpotent differential, and the conditional scalar time-ordering result.

[F1] [[lem-qftx-brst-nilpotence-doublets]]: the Abelian local differential $s$ is nilpotent.

[F2] [[def-qftx-brst-relative-local-cohomology]]: a ghost-number-one four-form $a_4$ is a relative cocycle when $s a_4$ is $d_H$-exact; its class vanishes exactly when $a_4=s b_4+d_Hc_3$ in the chosen local subcomplex. For compact support, $\int d_Hc_3=0$.

[F3] [[thm-qftx-scalar-eg-induction]]: assuming the Axiom of Choice and Countable Choice and fixing any admissible flat scalar free datum and local scalar polynomial class, there exists a sequence of symmetric multilinear maps $T_n$ satisfying the scalar time-ordering contract at every finite order, with finite graph scaling bounds and the stated local extension ambiguities. This supplier does not establish a BRST Ward identity, locality of an anomaly, a quantum action principle, or a positive physical Hilbert quotient.

[A3] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: every family of nonempty sets has a choice function. This is a hypothesis of [F3] and is carried here only through that conditional scalar-system supplier.

[A4] [[def-countable-choice]]: every countable family of nonempty sets has a choice function. This is a hypothesis of [F3] and is carried here only through that conditional scalar-system supplier.

[A1] On $\mathscr F_n^\bullet\llbracket\hbar\rrbracket$, $\mathcal W_\hbar=s+O(\hbar)$ is an odd formal ghost-number-one operator with $\mathcal W_\hbar^2=0$, and $\mathcal W_\hbar X=\hbar^n\mathcal A+O(\hbar^{n+1})$ with all lower coefficients zero.

[A2] The order-$n$ anomaly density $a_4$ is local and compactly supported; the chosen $s,d_H$-stable subcomplex $\mathcal K_n$ contains it, and every element of $\mathcal K_n^{0,4}$ is an admissible counterterm for this formal scheme.

## Proof

**Given:** [F1], [F2], [F3], [A1], [A2], [A3] and [A4].

1.1 Under the hypotheses of [F3], including [A3] and [A4], the scalar time-ordering input exists as a formal coefficientwise scheme. Fix such a scheme as background. The additional operator $\mathcal W_\hbar$, its nilpotence, and the locality premise in [A1]–[A2] are separate hypotheses not supplied by [F3]. [given, F3, A1, A2, A3, A4]

2.1 Apply $\mathcal W_\hbar$ to the expansion in [A1]. Since $\mathcal W_\hbar^2X=0$, its order-$\hbar^n$ coefficient is the leading operator $s$ applied to $\mathcal A$; all terms from the positive powers of $\hbar$ in $\mathcal W_\hbar$ have order at least $n+1$. Hence $s\mathcal A=0$ in $\mathscr F_n^2$. [A1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Since $\mathcal A=[a_4]_{\mathscr F_n}$ and $s\mathcal A=0$ in $\mathscr F_n^2$, the quotient definition in [F2] gives $s a_4=d_Hc_3$ for some $c_3\in\mathcal K_n^{2,3}$, after absorbing a sign into $c_3$. Thus $a_4$ is a cocycle in the specified relative local cohomology. [F1, F2, A2, step 2.1]

4.1 If $[a_4]_{s|d_H}=0$, the quotient definition in [F2], restricted to $\mathcal K_n$, supplies $b_4\in\mathcal K_n^{0,4}$ and $c_3\in\mathcal K_n^{1,3}$ such that $a_4=s b_4+d_Hc_3$. This uses the cocycle conclusion of step 3.1 so the relative class is defined. [F2, A2, step 3.1]

5.1 The counterterm class $[b_4]_{\mathscr F_n}$ is admissible by [A2]. Replacing $X$ by $X-\hbar^n[b_4]_{\mathscr F_n}$ changes the order-$n$ breaking by $-s[b_4]_{\mathscr F_n}$, because the leading term of $\mathcal W_\hbar$ is $s$. Step 4.1 gives $[a_4]_{\mathscr F_n}=s[b_4]_{\mathscr F_n}$, since $d_Hc_3$ is zero in that quotient, so the breaking is canceled. [F2, A1, A2, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Conversely, if an admissible local counterterm class $[b_4]_{\mathscr F_n}$ cancels the order-$n$ breaking in $\mathscr F_n^1$, then $[a_4]_{\mathscr F_n}-s[b_4]_{\mathscr F_n}=0$. By the quotient definition in [F2], $a_4-sb_4=d_Hc_3$ for some $c_3\in\mathcal K_n^{1,3}$. This is exactly the relative coboundary condition; step 3.1 ensures that $[a_4]_{s|d_H}$ is the class under discussion. [F2, A1, A2, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
