
ITEM def-qftx-finite-flat-connection-groupoid

## Definition

Let $K$ be a finite connected CW complex and $G$ a finite group. Choose an orientation of each 1-cell and, for each 2-cell, its based attaching word in the finite edge presentation of $\pi_1(K)$; the choice of base paths is part of that presentation. A **flat edge assignment** is a label $g_e\in G$ for every oriented 1-cell with $g_{\bar e}=g_e^{-1}$, such that each attaching word has holonomy $1$. If a word traverses edges $e_1,\ldots,e_m$ in that order with exponents $\epsilon_j\in\{1,-1\}$, its holonomy is $g_{e_m}^{\epsilon_m}\cdots g_{e_1}^{\epsilon_1}$.

Write $\operatorname{Flat}(K,G)$ for the finite set of flat assignments and $K_0$ for the vertices. The finite vertex gauge group $G^{K_0}$ acts by

$$(h\cdot g)_e=h_{t(e)}g_eh_{s(e)}^{-1},$$

where $s(e)$ and $t(e)$ are the source and target vertices. The **finite flat connection groupoid** $\mathcal C_G(K)$ is the action groupoid $\operatorname{Flat}(K,G)//G^{K_0}$: its objects are flat assignments and its arrows $g\to g'$ are the $h\in G^{K_0}$ with $h\cdot g=g'$. Its untwisted finite BF partition function is the groupoid cardinality

$$Z_G(K):=|\mathcal C_G(K)|=\sum_{[g]\in\operatorname{Flat}(K,G)/G^{K_0}}\frac{1}{|\operatorname{Stab}(g)|} =\frac{|\operatorname{Flat}(K,G)|}{|G|^{|K_0|}}.$$

This is a finite combinatorial model; it has no continuous Lie-algebra-valued connection integral or additional cocycle phase.

For a finite disconnected CW complex, define $\mathcal C_G(K)$ to be the product of the groupoids of its connected components; for the empty complex use the terminal groupoid.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite connected CW complex $K$, its finite edge-and-2-cell presentation, and a finite group $G$.

[F1] [[def-qftx-finite-groupoid-integration]]: a finite action groupoid has cardinality $\sum_{[g]}1/|\operatorname{Aut}(g)|$, and its invariant-function integrals are finite.

[F2] [[thm-qftx-cw-fundamental-group-presentation]]: the oriented non-tree edges generate $\pi_1(K)$ and the 2-cell attaching words give its relators; cells of dimension at least three do not change this presentation.

## Proof

1.1 Reversing an edge replaces $g_e$ by $g_e^{-1}$, and the gauge formula sends the reverse label to $(h_{t(e)}g_eh_{s(e)}^{-1})^{-1}=h_{s(e)}g_e^{-1}h_{t(e)}^{-1}$. For a closed attaching word based at a vertex $v$, the transformed holonomy is $h_v\operatorname{Hol}(w)h_v^{-1}$. Thus the inverse-edge rule and the flatness equations are preserved by every vertex gauge transformation. [given, F2, algebra]

2.1 For $h,k\in G^{K_0}$, applying $k$ and then $h$ sends an edge label to $h_{t(e)}k_{t(e)}g_ek_{s(e)}^{-1}h_{s(e)}^{-1}=(hk)_{t(e)}g_e(hk)_{s(e)}^{-1}$. The identity vertex labeling acts as the identity, so this is a group action and its action groupoid has the composition and inverses stated in the Definition. [given, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 There are at most $|G|^{|E(K)|}$ edge assignments and exactly $|G|^{|K_0|}$ gauge transformations, so the action groupoid is finite. Products over the finitely many connected components are finite groupoids as well; the empty product is terminal. By [F1], the connected groupoid cardinality is the orbit sum of reciprocal stabilizer sizes. [given, F1, step 2.1]

4.1 For an orbit represented by $g$, the map $G^{K_0}/\operatorname{Stab}(g)\to G^{K_0}\cdot g$, $h\operatorname{Stab}(g)\mapsto h\cdot g$, is a bijection: equality of images is equivalent to $h_2^{-1}h_1\in\operatorname{Stab}(g)$. Hence $|G^{K_0}\cdot g|/|G^{K_0}|=1/|\operatorname{Stab}(g)|$. Summing over the disjoint orbits gives the final ratio in the Definition. [given, step 3.1, algebra] ∎

ITEM def-qftx-cpt-spin-statistics-interface

## Definition

A **CPT/spin-statistics proof interface** is the minimum set of typed data,
hypotheses, and proof obligations that must be fixed before a scalar or spinor
Wightman CPT or spin-statistics theorem is stated. It is a checklist, not a
sufficiency theorem, and it asserts neither conclusion.

The proposed field system must specify a nonzero complex Hilbert space
$\mathcal H$ as in [[def-hilbert-space]], with inner product linear in its
first argument as in [[def-real-and-complex-inner-product-space]], a dense
complex linear field domain
$\mathcal D_{\mathrm{field}}\subseteq\mathcal H$, and a finite-dimensional
complex component space $V$ with $1\le\dim_{\mathbb C}V<\infty$, together with a
distinguished unit vector $\Omega\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{field}}$. A field multiplet is a finite family of complex-linear maps on Schwartz test functions, with the Schwartz topology of [[def-schwartz-space-and-its-seminorms]] and the Poincaré pullback convention of [[def-qft-poincare-test-action]]:
$\Phi_\alpha:\mathcal S(\mathbb R^4;\mathbb C)\to
\operatorname{Lin}(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{field}})$, where
$\operatorname{Lin}(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{field}})$ is the vector space of
linear maps from that domain to itself. Each field map preserves
$\mathcal D_{\mathrm{field}}$ and is weakly tempered: for every
$u,v\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{field}}$,
$f\mapsto(\Phi_\alpha(f)u,v)$ is a continuous linear functional on Schwartz
space. Every finite product on $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{field}}$ must be defined.
The associated
$n$-point function is the multilinear functional
$$(f_1,\ldots,f_n)\longmapsto (\Phi_{\alpha_1}(f_1)\cdots\Phi_{\alpha_n}(f_n)\Omega,\Omega);$$
the interface must require and identify its continuous extension to
$\mathcal S(\mathbb R^{4n};\mathbb C)$ for each component sequence.

The domain must satisfy $U(g)\mathcal D_{\mathrm{field}}=\mathcal D_{\mathrm{field}}$ for every group element $g$. The symmetry
data must include a strongly continuous unitary group representation $U$ of
the spin-cover Poincaré group
$\widetilde P^\uparrow_+:=SL(2,\mathbb C)\ltimes\mathbb R^4$, with product
$(A,a)(B,b)=(AB,a+\Lambda(A)b)$. Give $SL(2,\mathbb C)$ its subspace topology
from $M_2(\mathbb C)$ and the semidirect product the product topology. Here
$SL(2,\mathbb C)=\{A\in M_2(\mathbb C):\det A=1\}$, and $GL(V)$ denotes the
invertible complex-linear maps on $V$. The specified continuous surjective
double-cover homomorphism
$\Lambda:SL(2,\mathbb C)\to SO^+(1,3)$ has kernel $\{I,-I\}$; $-I$ is the
central order-two kernel element called the $2\pi$-rotation element here.
$SO^+(1,3)$ and the affine translation conventions are those of the
Poincaré supplier. Each $U(g)$ is a surjective linear isometry of
$\mathcal H$, $U(gh)=U(g)U(h)$, and $g\mapsto U(g)\xi$ is norm-continuous
for each fixed $\xi\in\mathcal H$. The field system also specifies a
continuous finite-dimensional representation $S:SL(2,\mathbb C)\to GL(V)$,
so $S(AB)=S(A)S(B)$ and $S(I)=I$.
Its field covariance identity must be stated explicitly as an equality in
$\operatorname{Lin}(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{field}})$ and checked against the
group law. It must identify whether the component matrix is $S$, its dual, or
its complex conjugate and fix the test-function pullback or its inverse, the
adjoint convention, and all component indices. The vacuum $\Omega$ must be invariant under
translations, with translation-invariant subspace exactly $\mathbb C\Omega$,
and cyclic: the span of all vectors
$\Phi_{\alpha_1}(f_1)\cdots\Phi_{\alpha_n}(f_n)\Omega$, including $\Omega$,
is dense in $\mathcal H$.

The positive-energy premise must be the joint PVM input of
[[def-qftx-joint-spectrum-input]], including its Countable Choice assumption.
For $f,g\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R^4;\mathbb C)$ with compact spacelike-separated
supports, the field locality identity and its sign must be stated on
$\mathcal D_{\mathrm{field}}$. Here $\operatorname{supp}f$ is the closure of
$\{x:f(x)\ne0\}$. Spacelike separation here means
$(x^0-y^0)^2-|\mathbf x-\mathbf y|^2<0$ for every support pair. If a proof
uses **weak locality**, that term means the equality of the original and
reversed vacuum $n$-point distributions on the specific real set of relative
coordinates required by the selected theorem; the set, reversal, field
grading, and distributional boundary interpretation must be stated rather
than left under the name “Jost points.”

The proof interface must then identify, with the Fourier sign and relative
coordinates fixed, the multivariable tube domain to which each $n$-point
distribution is continued. A **complexified Lorentz action** here is a
specified holomorphic action on $\mathbb C^4$ of a specified complex matrix
group extending the real action through $\Lambda$ and preserving the
complex-bilinear extension of the Minkowski form. A **complex Lorentz
extension** means a single-valued holomorphic continuation on a stated domain
generated by that action; the acting group, its action, and the domain must be
given and the covariance formula checked there. Any
**edge-of-the-wedge** step must name its exact theorem, both holomorphic
domains, their common distributional boundary value, the open real set where
they agree, and the conclusion of uniqueness used.

A claimed **CPT conclusion** means the combined field transformation by
charge conjugation and spacetime inversion $x\mapsto-x$ (simultaneous parity
and time reversal); it must preserve
$\mathcal D_{\mathrm{field}}$, define a surjective conjugate-linear isometry
$\Theta:\mathcal H\to\mathcal H$ (antiunitarity), state its action on
$\Omega$, translations, every field component, and test functions, and specify
the conjugate-linear charge-conjugation map and all phases. A claimed
**spin-statistics
conclusion** must state the action $S(-I)=\epsilon_{\rm spin}I_V$ of the
central $2\pi$-rotation element $-I\in SL(2,\mathbb C)$, with
$\epsilon_{\rm spin}\in\{+1,-1\}$, and the actual spacelike exchange sign
$\epsilon_{\rm loc}\in\{+1,-1\}$ in
$\Phi_\alpha(f)\Phi_\beta(g)=\epsilon_{\rm loc}\Phi_\beta(g)\Phi_\alpha(f)$
on $\mathcal D$. For a multiplet with a single spin parity, the theorem must
derive the precise relation $\epsilon_{\mathrm{loc}}=\epsilon_{\mathrm{spin}}$. Any additional
theorem-specific assumptions remain explicit; this interface does not claim
that its checklist alone suffices.

## Remarks

The bounded-insertion theorem
[[thm-qftx-spectral-tube-holomorphy]] proves neither analyticity for
unbounded field products nor the distributional boundary and uniqueness steps
listed here. The published
[[thm-qft-tempered-field-reconstruction]] supplies only its stated
matrix-coefficient spectrum condition, not a joint PVM. The existing free
scalar and Dirac constructions are examples and do not discharge a general
CPT or spin-statistics proof obligation.

ITEM def-tgr-regulated-generalized-entropy

## Definition

Fix a smooth, oriented, time-oriented Lorentzian four-manifold $(M,g,N)$ of signature $(-,+,+,+)$, its metric-selected connection, and one smooth embedded null hypersurface $\mathcal H\subset M$. Use the null-hypersurface convention in the mathematical definition of the smooth null screen family. Supply a smooth, nonzero, future null generator field $k$ tangent to $\mathcal H$ with $\nabla_k k=0$. Choose a nonempty compact smooth embedded spacelike two-surface $\Sigma\subset\mathcal H$ transverse to $k$, meaning $T_p\Sigma\oplus\mathbb Rk_p=T_p\mathcal H$ at every $p\in\Sigma$. If $i:\Sigma\hookrightarrow M$ is inclusion, the induced metric $h=i^*g|_{T\Sigma}$ is positive definite. In any local surface chart $(y^1,y^2)$, define its area density by $\mu_h=\sqrt{\det(h_{AB})}\,|dy^1dy^2|$ and set $A_\Sigma=\int_\Sigma\mu_h$. Admissible model data require $0<A_\Sigma<\infty$.

A named finite-regulator sequence consists of labels $(\Lambda_n)_{n\ge1}$, nonzero finite-dimensional complex inner-product spaces $H_{\mathrm{in},n}$ and $H_{\mathrm{out},n}$, and a density operator $\rho_n$ on the ordered tensor product $H_{\mathrm{in},n}\otimes H_{\mathrm{out},n}$ for each $n$. The physical Boltzmann constant $k_B>0$ has units $\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$, while $c>0$ and $\hbar>0$ have units $\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and $\mathrm{J\,s}$, respectively. Define

$$S_{\mathrm{out},n}:=S_{\mathrm{out}}(\rho_n;H_{\mathrm{in},n},H_{\mathrm{out},n}),\qquad S_{\mathrm{gen},n}[\Sigma]:=S_{\mathrm{grav},n}[\Sigma]+S_{\mathrm{out},n}.$$

Here $S_{\mathrm{out},n}$ is the finite spectral entropy of the partial-trace state specified by the finite-regulator outside-entropy definition, using natural logarithms and $0\log 0:=0$. The assigned geometric value $S_{\mathrm{grav},n}[\Sigma]$ is finite and has units $\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$; its terms, including every chosen local counterterm and finite normalization condition, are fixed as part of the declared scheme. Any dimensional regulator label has its units recorded with that scheme.

On the optional area-term branch, one allowed summand of $S_{\mathrm{grav},n}[\Sigma]$ is

$$\frac{k_Bc^3A_\Sigma}{4G_{\Lambda_n}\hbar},$$

where each $G_{\Lambda_n}>0$ is specified model data with the units of Newton's constant, $\mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$. The relation of $G_{\Lambda_n}$ to the reference $G_N$ is also declared model data. The renormalized generalized entropy $S_{\mathrm{gen}}^{\mathrm{ren}}[\Sigma]$ is defined exactly when the sequence $(S_{\mathrm{gen},n}[\Sigma])_{n\ge1}$ converges to a finite real value in the declared scheme; its value is that limit. If the sequence has no finite limit, this definition assigns no renormalized value. The limit belongs only to the named sequence and scheme.

With the physical $k_B$, both terms in each sum have units $\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$, and $A_\Sigma$ is in $\mathrm{m^2}$.

## Remarks

The finite-matrix outside-entropy definition establishes that each $S_{\mathrm{out},n}$ is a finite real number; it does not establish convergence as $n\to\infty$. The finite-part ambiguity result shows why the subtraction and its finite normalization condition must be fixed: changing a subtraction by a constant changes the resulting finite limit by that constant.

The conditional area-calibration theorem gives the coefficient $k_Bc^3/(4G_N\hbar)$ only on its selected strict subextremal stationary Kerr–Newman family, with its explicit $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ premise, adopted reversible-heat identification, and reduced-temperature model. It does not calibrate a regulator-dependent $G_{\Lambda_n}$ on an arbitrary background. In this definition any such $G_{\Lambda_n}$ is specified model input; when it is identified with $G_N$, the theorem's full hypotheses still apply.

Here $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ means that every sequence $(X_n)_{n\ge1}$ of nonempty sets has a choice function $f$ with $f(n)\in X_n$, as in [[def-countable-choice]]. This axiom is part of the conditional calibration interface only; this finite-regulator definition selects no such sequence of objects.

No state evolution, Hawking emission, change of the classical metric caused by matter, or generalized-second-law inequality is part of this definition.

ITEM pthm-tgrx-conditional-hawking-occupation

## Statement

Assume the fixed-background collapse model and all its conditions stated in the preceding postulate on this page. Let $\ell\in\mathbb N_0$, let $K=[\omega_-,\omega_+]\subset(0,\infty)$ with $\omega_-<\omega_+$, and let $f\in C_c^\infty(K;\mathbb C)$ satisfy $\int_K|f(\omega)|^2d\omega=1$. Write
$$\mathcal N_{\ell,f}(s):=B_{\ell,s}(f,f),\qquad n_\theta(\omega):=\frac{1}{e^{\theta\omega}-1},\qquad \theta:=\frac{2\pi}{k}\ {\rm seconds}.$$
Then the late-time packet occupation exists and is
$$\lim_{s\to+\infty}\mathcal N_{\ell,f}(s)=\int_K|f(\omega)|^2\,\Gamma_\ell(\omega)\,n_\theta(\omega)\,d\omega.$$
Thus a finite-width packet has a spectral integral; its value is not, in general, the integrand at a chosen central frequency.

If $\omega_0$ is in the interior of $K$ and $f_\epsilon$ is a specified normalized family supported in $K$ with $|f_\epsilon(\omega)|^2d\omega\rightharpoonup\delta_{\omega_0}$, then the ordered limit, first $s\to+\infty$ and then $\epsilon\downarrow0$, is
$$\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\lim_{s\to+\infty}\mathcal N_{\ell,f_\epsilon}(s)=\frac{\Gamma_\ell(\omega_0)}{e^{2\pi\omega_0/k}-1}.$$
Here $M$ is the fixed mass in kilograms, $G$ has units $\mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$, $c$ has units $\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, $\hbar$ has units $\mathrm{J\,s}$, and $k_B$ has units $\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$. Matching the Bose exponent to $\hbar\omega/(k_BT_\infty)$ defines the asymptotic spectral temperature parameter
$$T_\infty:=\frac{\hbar k}{2\pi k_B}=\frac{\hbar c^3}{8\pi G M k_B}\ {\rm kelvins}.$$
This parameter labels the unfiltered Planck factor; a frequency-dependent $\Gamma_\ell$ need not leave the spectrum Planckian. It is not an observed temperature.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The fixed-background model, its operator-norm convergence on each compact positive-frequency band, the passive two-mode scattering map, the continuous transmission probability, and the normalized packet family from the Statement.

[A1] [[post-tgrx-free-collapse-scattering]] adopts the positive-frequency Fock convention that the spectral coefficient $S_k(\omega)$ corresponds to mode occupation $2S_k(\omega)/\omega$ for $\omega>0$; this is a model normalization, not a detector response or observed particle count.

[A2] [[post-tgrx-free-collapse-scattering]] assumes that on each fixed compact positive-frequency band the outgoing number forms converge in operator norm to the direct integral of the exponential near-horizon channel and an independent incoming vacuum channel mixed by the passive map. It does not derive the four-dimensional scattering estimate.

[F1] [[prop-tgrx-collapse-channel-spectrum]] proves that the exponential channel has frequency spectrum $S_k(\omega)=\omega/[2(e^{2\pi\omega/k}-1)]$ for $\omega>0$.

[F2] [[prop-tgrx-collapse-channel-spectrum]] proves that the passive two-mode map with an independent vacuum satisfies $\mathfrak n_{\rm out}(\omega)=\Gamma(\omega)n_\omega$ for each positive frequency.

[F3] [[prop-tgrx-collapse-channel-spectrum]] states that the Planck occupation $n_\theta(\omega)=(e^{\theta\omega}-1)^{-1}$ is positive for every $\omega>0$ when $\theta>0$.

[F4] [[post-tgrx-free-collapse-scattering]] fixes the time-frequency parameter $k=c^3/(4GM)>0$ for the fixed mass $M$ and the stated SI constants.

## Proof

1.1 Fix $\ell$ and a normalized $f$ supported in $K$. The postulate identifies the near-horizon occupation with $2S_k(\omega)/\omega$ at each $\omega\in K$. By [F1] and $\theta=2\pi/k$, this is $2S_k(\omega)/\omega=1/(e^{\theta\omega}-1)=n_\theta(\omega)$. For a mixed input state with this finite mode occupation and an independent vacuum environment, expanding $b^*b$ from the passive map gives $\varpi(b^*b)=\Gamma_\ell(\omega)\varpi(a^*a)$: the environment number term vanishes in its vacuum and the two cross terms vanish by independence and zero vacuum one-point function. Thus [F2] applies to this mixed-state channel as well. Since [A2] makes the limiting packet form the direct integral of these frequency-diagonal mode forms, its diagonal value is $\int_K|f(\omega)|^2\Gamma_\ell(\omega)n_\theta(\omega)d\omega$. [given, A1, A2, F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 Operator-norm convergence in [A2] implies $|B_{\ell,s}(f,f)-B_{\ell,\infty}(f,f)|\le\|B_{\ell,s}-B_{\ell,\infty}\|\|f\|_2^2\to0$; using $\|f\|_2=1$ and the value computed in step 1.1 proves the finite-packet limit. [A2, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Set $g(\omega)=\Gamma_\ell(\omega)n_\theta(\omega)$ and $d\mu_\epsilon=|f_\epsilon(\omega)|^2d\omega$. The common band $K$ is compact and bounded away from zero, and [F3] gives $n_\theta(\omega)>0$ there; with the postulated continuous $\Gamma_\ell$, $g$ is bounded and continuous on $K$. For any $\eta>0$, continuity gives a neighborhood $V$ of $\omega_0$ in $K$ on which $|g(\omega)-g(\omega_0)|<\eta$. Choose a continuous $q:K\to[0,1]$ with $q(\omega_0)=0$ and $q=1$ outside $V$. Weak convergence $\mu_\epsilon\rightharpoonup\delta_{\omega_0}$ gives $\int q\,d\mu_\epsilon\to0$, hence $\mu_\epsilon(K\setminus V)\to0$. Therefore $\left|\int_Kg\,d\mu_\epsilon-g(\omega_0)\right|\le\eta+2\|g\|_\infty\mu_\epsilon(K\setminus V)\to\eta$; as $\eta$ is arbitrary, the integral tends to $g(\omega_0)$. [given, F1, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Apply step 2.1 at each fixed $\epsilon$, then step 2.2 to the resulting spectral integral. This order yields $\Gamma_\ell(\omega_0)/(e^{2\pi\omega_0/k}-1)$ and does not replace a finite-width integral by its central-frequency value. [A2, step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 By the definition of $T_\infty$ in the Statement, $\theta\omega=\hbar\omega/(k_BT_\infty)$ for every $\omega>0$; solving $\theta=2\pi/k$ gives $T_\infty=\hbar k/(2\pi k_B)$. Substituting the model value $k=c^3/(4GM)$ from [F4] gives $T_\infty=\hbar c^3/(8\pi GMk_B)$. Its units are $(\mathrm{J\,s})(\mathrm{s^{-1}})/(\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}})=\mathrm K$. The conclusion is a model-derived spectral parameter and contains no observed temperature. [given, F4, algebra] ∎
