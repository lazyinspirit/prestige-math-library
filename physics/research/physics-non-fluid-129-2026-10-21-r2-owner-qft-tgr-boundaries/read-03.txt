
ITEM lem-tgrx-compensator-implies-exact

## Statement

Let $O$ be a smooth time-oriented Lorentzian domain, let $\xi$ be a smooth future-timelike Killing field on $O$, and let $A$ be a global smooth one-form with $F=dA$. Suppose a global smooth scalar $\Lambda_\xi$ satisfies $\mathcal L_\xi A=d\Lambda_\xi$. Define $\Psi_\xi:=\xi^aA_a-\Lambda_\xi$ and $\omega:=\iota_\xi F$, with $(\iota_\xi F)_a=\xi^bF_{ba}$. Then

$$\omega=-d\Psi_\xi,$$

so $\omega$ is exact. This identity uses the global potential symmetry and assumes no charged trajectory or conservation law.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A smooth time-oriented Lorentzian domain $O$, a smooth future-timelike Killing field $\xi$, a global smooth one-form $A$ with $F=dA$, and a global smooth scalar $\Lambda_\xi$ satisfying $\mathcal L_\xi A=d\Lambda_\xi$.

[F1] [[def-tgr-charged-liouville-data]]: $F=dA$, $(\iota_\xi F)_a=\xi^bF_{ba}$, $(\mathcal L_\xi A)_a=\xi^b\partial_bA_a+A_b\partial_a\xi^b$, and $\Psi_\xi=\xi^aA_a-\Lambda_\xi$ for the supplied global potential data.

[F2] [[def-closed-and-exact-differential-forms]]: a one-form is exact when it is the exterior derivative of a globally defined smooth scalar.

[A1] The global compensator equation $\mathcal L_\xi A=d\Lambda_\xi$ holds on all of $O$.

## Proof

1.1 In any smooth chart, the definitions give $(\iota_\xi F)_a=\xi^b(\partial_bA_a-\partial_aA_b)$ and $\partial_a\Psi_\xi=A_b\partial_a\xi^b+\xi^b\partial_aA_b-\partial_a\Lambda_\xi$. By [A1] and [F1], $\partial_a\Lambda_\xi=\xi^b\partial_bA_a+A_b\partial_a\xi^b$. Substitution yields $(\iota_\xi F)_a=-\partial_a\Psi_\xi$. Since this calculation holds in every chart, it proves the stated one-form identity. [given, A1, F1, algebra]

2.1 The scalar $\Psi_\xi=\xi^aA_a-\Lambda_\xi$ is global and smooth because it is the contraction of globally defined smooth fields minus a global smooth scalar. Thus step 1.1 gives $\omega=-d\Psi_\xi=d(-\Psi_\xi)$ globally, which is exact by [F2]. [given, F2, step 1.1, algebra] ∎

ITEM lem-tgrx-exact-one-form-zero-period

## Statement

On any smooth manifold $M$, if a smooth one-form $\omega$ is exact, so $\omega=d\phi$ for a globally defined smooth scalar $\phi:M\to\mathbb R$, then $\operatorname{Per}_\gamma(\omega)=0$ for every closed piecewise-$C^1$ curve $\gamma$ in $M$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A smooth exact one-form $\omega=d\phi$ on a smooth manifold $M$ and a closed piecewise-$C^1$ curve $\gamma:[0,1]\to M$ with an admissible finite subdivision.

[F1] [[def-tgrx-period-of-one-form]]: the period is the sum of the integrals of $\omega_{\gamma(t)}(\dot\gamma(t))$ over the pieces of the chosen subdivision.

[F2] [[def-closed-and-exact-differential-forms]]: exactness means $\omega=d\phi$ for a globally defined smooth scalar $\phi$.

[F3] [[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]]: in local coordinates, the total derivative of a composition is the composition of the total derivatives.

[F4] [[thm-continuous-implies-integrable]]: each continuous derivative on a closed subinterval is integrable there.

[F5] [[thm-ftc-second-part]]: if $h$ is differentiable on a closed interval and $h'$ is integrable, then the integral of $h'$ equals the endpoint increment of $h$.

## Proof

1.1 Fix the finite subdivision from [F1]. On each chartwise $C^1$ piece choose a chart containing the piece, write its coordinate curve as $x(t)$ and $\widetilde\phi=\phi\circ x^{-1}$. By [F2] the coordinate coefficients of $\omega$ are $\partial_i\widetilde\phi$; applying [F3] to $\widetilde\phi\circ x$ gives $\frac{d}{dt}(\phi\circ\gamma)(t)=\omega_{\gamma(t)}(\dot\gamma(t))$ on that piece. This derivative is continuous and therefore integrable by [F4]. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, algebra]

2.1 Apply [F5] on each subdivision interval to $h=\phi\circ\gamma$. By [F1] the period is the sum of those integrals, so it equals the sum of the increments $\phi(\gamma(t_{j+1}))-\phi(\gamma(t_j))$. The internal endpoint terms cancel, and the remaining increment is $\phi(\gamma(1))-\phi(\gamma(0))=0$ because $\gamma$ is closed. Thus the period is zero. [given, F1, F5, step 1.1, algebra] ∎

ITEM lem-qftx-finite-heavy-gaussian-square

## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $d\ge1$, let $K\in\mathbb R^{d\times d}$ be symmetric positive definite, and let $j\in\mathbb R^d$. Let $\gamma_{K^{-1}}=N_d(0,K^{-1})$, where covariance is the matrix $\mathbb E[XX^T]$. Then
$$\frac{\int_{\mathbb R^d}\exp(-\tfrac12x^TKx+j^Tx)\,dx} {\int_{\mathbb R^d}\exp(-\tfrac12x^TKx)\,dx} =\int_{\mathbb R^d}e^{j^Tx}\,d\gamma_{K^{-1}}(x) =\exp\!\left(\tfrac12j^TK^{-1}j\right).$$
Equivalently, in the finite real Gaussian regulator with normalized weight $e^{-S}$ and $\hbar=1$, completing the source shift $\chi=K^{-1}j+\eta$ gives
$$\tfrac12\chi^TK\chi-j^T\chi=\tfrac12\eta^TK\eta-\tfrac12j^TK^{-1}j.$$
Use $\hbar=1$ and coordinate/source units for which $x^TKx$ and $j^Tx$ are dimensionless. Both integrals are finite; their common Gaussian normalization cancels in the ratio. No infinite-dimensional Lebesgue measure or determinant is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; a finite dimension $d\ge1$; symmetric positive-definite $K$; $j\in\mathbb R^d$; and the covariance convention in the Statement.

[A1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: AC is the assumption used by the finite-dimensional normal-law and density suppliers.

[F1] [[def-multivariate-normal-law]]: $N_d(m,\Sigma)$ has covariance $\Sigma$ for every real symmetric positive-semidefinite $\Sigma$.

[F2] [[thm-nondegenerate-multivariate-normal-law-has-density]]: if $\Sigma$ is positive definite, $N_d(m,\Sigma)$ has density $(2\pi)^{-d/2}\det(\Sigma)^{-1/2}\exp(-\tfrac12(x-m)^T\Sigma^{-1}(x-m))$.

[F3] [[thm-lebesgue-outer-measure-and-measurability-are-translation-invariant]]: Lebesgue measure is invariant under translations.

[F4] [[def-measure-preserving-transformation-and-system]]: a measurable self-map is measure preserving when inverse images preserve measure.

[F5] [[thm-integrals-are-invariant-under-measure-preserving-maps]]: a measure-preserving map preserves integrals of nonnegative measurable functions, including extended-valued integrals.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct Gaussian density and translation.

**Given:** The objects and conventions in the Statement.

1.1 By [F1]–[F2], $\gamma_{K^{-1}}$ has Lebesgue density $c_K\exp(-\tfrac12x^TKx)$, where $c_K=(2\pi)^{-d/2}\det(K^{-1})^{-1/2}>0$. The expectation in the Statement is therefore the ratio of the displayed Lebesgue integrals, provided the numerator is finite. [A1, F1, F2, given]

2.1 Since $K$ is invertible, direct expansion gives
$$\tfrac12x^TKx-j^Tx=\tfrac12(x-K^{-1}j)^TK(x-K^{-1}j)-\tfrac12j^TK^{-1}j.$$
The translation $x\mapsto x-K^{-1}j$ preserves Lebesgue measure by [F3]–[F5], so the numerator is the denominator multiplied by $\exp(\tfrac12j^TK^{-1}j)$. The denominator is finite and positive because it is the normalizing integral of the probability density in [F2]; hence the numerator is also finite, and division proves both equalities. Expanding the same square proves the action identity. [F2, F3, F4, F5, step 1.1, algebra] ∎

ITEM def-qftx-heavy-gaussian-data

## Definition

Assume AC. Let $H$ be a nonzero complex Hilbert space ([[def-hilbert-space]]), let $A:D(A)\subseteq H\to H$ be densely defined, self-adjoint and nonnegative, and let $M>0$. Set $K=M^2I+A$ on $D(A)$ and let $E_A$ be the spectral measure supplied by [[thm-qftx-positive-unbounded-spectral-calculus]]. Define $f_M(a)=(M^2+a)^{-1}$ for $a\ge0$. The bounded spectral operator $f_M(A)$ is the inverse $K^{-1}$ and satisfies $\|K^{-1}\|\le M^{-2}$. For $J\in H$ define the matched quadratic effective-action contribution
$$\Delta S_{\mathrm{eff}}(J):=-\tfrac12\langle J,K^{-1}J\rangle.$$
The inner product is the complex Hilbert pairing of [[def-real-and-complex-inner-product-space]], linear in its first variable; the displayed quadratic form is real and nonpositive. In natural units $\hbar=c=1$, $A$ and $K$ have mass-squared units; normalize the source/pairing so $\langle J,K^{-1}J\rangle$ is dimensionless, hence $\|J\|^2$ has mass-squared units. In a finite real regulator, integration of $\exp[-\tfrac12\chi^TK\chi+j^T\chi]$ divided by the zero-source Gaussian integral yields $\exp[\tfrac12j^TK^{-1}j]$ by [[lem-qftx-finite-heavy-gaussian-square]], so the corresponding effective action changes by $-\tfrac12j^TK^{-1}j$. Taking $-\log$ of that normalized integral uses [[def-natural-logarithm]]. Determinants and Lebesgue integration are finite-regulator quantities only; this definition supplies no infinite-dimensional Gaussian measure or determinant.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; a nonzero complex Hilbert space; a densely defined nonnegative self-adjoint $A$; $M>0$; and $J\in H$.

[A1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: every family of nonempty sets has a choice function.

[A2] [[lem-ac-supplies-sequential-choices-for-probability-constructions]]: AC implies countable choice.

[F1] [[thm-qftx-positive-unbounded-spectral-calculus]]: for nonnegative self-adjoint $A$, the spectral measure is supported on $[0,\infty)$ and bounded Borel functions define bounded operators with norm at most their supremum.

[F2] [[thm-cauchy-schwarz-in-an-inner-product-space]]: $|\langle x,y\rangle|\le\|x\|\|y\|$.

[F3] [[lem-unbounded-adjoint-is-well-defined-and-closed]]: under countable choice the adjoint of a densely defined operator is closed; hence a self-adjoint $A$ is closed.

[F4] [[lem-qftx-finite-heavy-gaussian-square]]: the normalized finite Gaussian source-shift ratio is $\exp(\tfrac12j^TK^{-1}j)$ for symmetric positive-definite $K$.

[A3] [[def-hilbert-space]]: a complex Hilbert space is a complete complex inner-product space.

[A4] [[def-real-and-complex-inner-product-space]]: the complex inner product is linear in the first variable and conjugate-linear in the second.

[F5] [[def-natural-logarithm]]: $\log$ is defined on $(0,\infty)$ as the inverse of $\exp$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct spectral calculus with a finite-regulator Gaussian identity.

**Given:** The objects and hypotheses in the Definition.

1.1 The actual proof of [F1] uses Cauchy–Schwarz for its resolvent lower bound and closedness of $A$ for its resolvent graph argument. [F2] supplies the former. By [A1]–[A2] countable choice is available, so [F3] applies and self-adjointness gives $A=A^*$, hence $A$ is closed. This records the actual inputs of [F1]'s proof; the carrier's complete conditional proof was inspected. [A1, A2, F1, F2, F3]

2.1 Let $g_M(a)=(M^2+a)^{-1}$. By [F1], $g_M(A)$ is bounded with norm at most $M^{-2}$. Moreover, $\int a^2g_M(a)^2\,d\langle E_A(a)u,u\rangle\le\|u\|^2$ for every $u\in H$, so the domain formula for $A$ puts $g_M(A)u$ in $D(A)$. For any $u\in H$, truncate the spectral integrals to $[0,n]$; on each truncation the bounded Borel calculus is multiplicative, and the domain criterion just verified gives the graph limit $A g_M(A)u=\int a g_M(a)\,dE_A(a)u$. Hence $(M^2I+A)g_M(A)u=\int(M^2+a)g_M(a)\,dE_A(a)u=u$. For $v\in D(A)$, the same truncation and scalar multiplication give $g_M(A)(M^2I+A)v=\int g_M(a)(M^2+a)\,dE_A(a)v=v$. Thus $K^{-1}=g_M(A)$ and $\|K^{-1}\|\le M^{-2}$. Finally, the positive scalar multiplier gives $\langle J,K^{-1}J\rangle=\int g_M(a)\,d\langle E_A(a)J,J\rangle\ge0$, so the quadratic form is real. [A3, A4, F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 In a finite real regulator with symmetric positive-definite matrix $K$, [F4] gives the normalized source integral $\exp(\tfrac12j^TK^{-1}j)$. Taking its negative logarithm using [F5] gives the effective-action shift $-\tfrac12j^TK^{-1}j$. No determinant or Lebesgue integral beyond that finite regulator enters the definition. [F4, F5, given, algebra] ∎

ITEM thm-qftx-heavy-derivative-bound

## Statement

Assume the hypotheses and notation of [[def-qftx-heavy-gaussian-data]]. Let $E\ge0$ be an energy and let $P_E:=E_A([0,E^2])$ be the spectral projection of $A$. For $J=P_EJ\in H$ and an integer $N\ge0$, define
$$S_NJ:=M^{-2}\sum_{r=0}^{N}(-A/M^2)^rJ,$$
which is well-defined since $J$ has bounded spectral support. Put $R_NJ:=K^{-1}J-S_NJ$. Then
$$\|R_NJ\|\le M^{-2}(E^2/M^2)^{N+1}\|J\|,$$
and
$$\left|\Delta S_{\mathrm{eff}}(J)+\tfrac12\langle J,S_NJ\rangle\right| =\tfrac12|\langle J,R_NJ\rangle| \le \frac{\|J\|^2}{2M^2}(E^2/M^2)^{N+1}.$$
Use natural units $\hbar=c=1$: $A$ has units of mass squared, $E,M$ have mass units, and $E^2/M^2$ is dimensionless. Under the source normalization of [[def-qftx-heavy-gaussian-data]], $\|J\|^2/M^2$ and the action remainder are dimensionless. These are exact conditional bounds for every $E\ge0$; the truncation is small/useful when $E<M$. No claim is made for unrestricted sources or arbitrary interacting matching.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, $H,A,M,K,E_A,K^{-1}$ as in [[def-qftx-heavy-gaussian-data]], $E\ge0$, and $J=P_EJ$.

[A1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: the spectral construction assumes AC.

[A2] [[lem-ac-supplies-sequential-choices-for-probability-constructions]]: AC supplies countable choice required in the actual proof of the spectral supplier.

[F1] [[thm-qftx-positive-unbounded-spectral-calculus]]: bounded Borel functions of $A$ have norm at most their supremum and $A$ has its spectral PVM on $[0,\infty)$.

[F2] [[def-qftx-heavy-gaussian-data]]: $K^{-1}=f_M(A)$ for $f_M(a)=(M^2+a)^{-1}$, and $\Delta S_{\mathrm{eff}}(J)=-\tfrac12\langle J,K^{-1}J\rangle$.

[F3] [[thm-cauchy-schwarz-in-an-inner-product-space]]: $|\langle x,y\rangle|\le\|x\|\|y\|$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct scalar remainder and the spectral functional calculus.

**Given:** The hypotheses and notation in the Statement.

1.1 For every $a\ge0$, putting $x=a/M^2$ and multiplying by $1+x$ gives
$$\frac1{M^2+a}=M^{-2}\sum_{r=0}^{N}(-a/M^2)^r+ M^{-2}\frac{(-a/M^2)^{N+1}}{1+a/M^2}.$$
This finite geometric identity also holds at $a=0$ and for $N=0$. [given, algebra]

2.1 On $J=P_EJ$, the spectral variable satisfies $0\le a\le E^2$. Therefore every polynomial term $A^rJ$ is defined, and the remainder multiplier satisfies
$$\left|M^{-2}\frac{(-a/M^2)^{N+1}}{1+a/M^2}\right| \le M^{-2}(E^2/M^2)^{N+1},$$
because $1+a/M^2\ge1$. Extend this bounded multiplier by zero outside $[0,E^2]$ and apply [F1]. With [F2] this identifies the inverse kernel and proves the vector identity defining $R_NJ$ and its norm bound. [A1, A2, F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 By [F2], the action remainder equals $\tfrac12|\langle J,R_NJ\rangle|$. Cauchy–Schwarz [F3] and step 2.1 give the claimed bound. The factor $(E^2/M^2)^{N+1}$ decreases to zero only when $E<M$; for $E\ge M$ the same finite-order bound remains true but need not be small. [F2, F3, step 2.1, algebra] ∎

ITEM def-qftx-finite-gaussian-shell-transform

## Definition

Assume AC and let $d\ge1$. For a real symmetric positive-semidefinite covariance matrix $C$, write $\gamma_C=N_d(0,C)$, allowing singular $C$. For a nonnegative Borel function $f:\mathbb R^d\to[0,\infty]$, define the normalized shell transform
$$T_Cf(x):=\int_{\mathbb R^d}f(x+z)\,d\gamma_C(z)\in[0,\infty].$$
This is a Borel function of $x$. If $V:\mathbb R^d\to[0,\infty)$ is finite-valued and Borel, define
$$V_C(x):=-\log\bigl(T_C(e^{-V})(x)\bigr).$$
Then $V_C$ is a finite nonnegative Borel function, and $0<T_C(e^{-V})(x)\le1$ for every $x$. The covariance convention is $\mathbb E[ZZ^T]=C$; $x,z,V$ use the same finite-regulator field-coordinate convention, so $C$ has the square units of those coordinates. The measures $\gamma_C$ are normalized probabilities, with no determinant/vacuum factor in $T_C$. The transform is defined at finite regulator only.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, finite $d\ge1$, symmetric positive-semidefinite $C$, and the functions in the Definition.

[A1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: the finite-dimensional Gaussian-law definition assumes AC.

[F1] [[def-multivariate-normal-law]]: for positive-semidefinite $C$, the Borel probability $\gamma_C=N_d(0,C)$ exists, including singular $C$.

[F2] [[thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces]]: for product-measurable nonnegative functions, parameter integrals are measurable and iterated integrals agree.

[F3] [[def-natural-logarithm]]: $\log$ is defined on $(0,\infty)$ as the inverse of $\exp$.

[F4] [[thm-exponential-is-strictly-increasing]]: $\exp$ is continuous and strictly increasing on $\mathbb R$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct measurability and positivity check.

**Given:** The objects and hypotheses in the Definition.

1.1 The map $(x,z)\mapsto f(x+z)$ is Borel because addition is continuous and $f$ is Borel. Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb R^d$ is sigma-finite because $\mathbb R^d=\bigcup_{n\ge1}[-n,n]^d$ and each cube has finite measure; $\gamma_C$ is a probability by [F1]. Thus [F2] makes $x\mapsto T_Cf(x)$ Borel, including when the integral is infinite. [A1, F1, F2, given]

2.1 For finite nonnegative Borel $V$, $g_x(z):=e^{-V(x+z)}$ is Borel and satisfies $0<g_x(z)\le1$. Its integral against the probability $\gamma_C$ is at most one. It is strictly positive: if the integral were zero, each set $\{z:g_x(z)\ge1/n\}$ would have measure zero, while their countable union is all of $\mathbb R^d$, contradicting $\gamma_C(\mathbb R^d)=1$. Thus $T_C(e^{-V})(x)\in(0,1]$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 By [F3], the logarithm is defined at these values. Its continuity follows directly from [F4]: for $y_0>0$ and $\epsilon>0$, strict increase and continuity of $\exp$ give $\exp(\log y_0-\epsilon)<y_0<\exp(\log y_0+\epsilon)$; put $\delta=\min\{y_0-\exp(\log y_0-\epsilon),\exp(\log y_0+\epsilon)-y_0\}>0$. If $y>0$ and $|y-y_0|<\delta$, then $\exp(\log y_0-\epsilon)<y<\exp(\log y_0+\epsilon)$, and strict monotonicity gives $|\log y-\log y_0|<\epsilon$. Hence $V_C$ is finite, nonnegative, and Borel. If $C=0$, then for each coordinate $Z_i$, $\mathbb E Z_i^2=C_{ii}=0$. For every $n\ge1$, $0=\mathbb E Z_i^2\ge n^{-2}\mathbb P(|Z_i|\ge n^{-1})$, so $Z_i=0$ almost surely; the finite intersection gives $Z=0$ almost surely and $\gamma_0=\delta_0$; hence $T_0f=f$ and $V_0=V$. [F1, F3, F4, step 2.1, algebra] ∎

ITEM prop-qftx-finite-gaussian-shell-semigroup

## Statement

Assume AC. Let $d\ge1$, let $C_1,C_2$ be real symmetric positive-semidefinite $d\times d$ covariance matrices, and let $f:\mathbb R^d\to[0,\infty]$ be Borel. With $T_C$ as in [[def-qftx-finite-gaussian-shell-transform]],
$$T_{C_2}(T_{C_1}f)(x)=T_{C_1+C_2}f(x)\qquad(x\in\mathbb R^d),$$
as an equality in $[0,\infty]$. In particular, for every finite nonnegative Borel potential $V$,
$$\exp[-(V_{C_1})_{C_2}(x)]=\exp[-V_{C_1+C_2}(x)].$$
The Gaussian measures are normalized probabilities, so no determinant/vacuum constant appears; changing to unnormalized Gaussian weights requires carrying their normalization factors. This is an exact finite-regulator semigroup identity. Differentiating it to obtain a Polchinski-type flow is not asserted and would require a differentiable covariance path and separate differentiability/integrability hypotheses.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; finite dimension; the covariance matrices and Borel function in the Statement.

[A1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: AC supplies the finite-dimensional Gaussian laws.

[F1] [[def-qftx-finite-gaussian-shell-transform]]: $T_Cf(x)=\int f(x+z)\,d\gamma_C(z)$, and $V_C=-\log T_C(e^{-V})$ when $V$ is finite nonnegative Borel.

[F2] [[def-multivariate-normal-law]]: the law $\gamma_C$ exists for every positive-semidefinite covariance $C$, including singular $C$.

[F3] [[thm-sigma-finite-product-measure-exists-is-rectangular-and-is-unique]]: the product of sigma-finite measures is a sigma-finite measure with the rectangle product formula.

[F4] [[thm-fubini-theorem-for-l-one-on-sigma-finite-product-spaces]]: integrable complex functions on a sigma-finite product may be integrated in either order.

[F5] [[lem-characteristic-function-of-a-multivariate-normal-law]]: $\gamma_C$ has characteristic function $\exp(-u^TCu/2)$, and that transform uniquely determines its law, including singular $C$.

[F6] [[thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces]]: nonnegative product-measurable functions have measurable section integrals and equal iterated integrals.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** product Gaussian law and Tonelli.

**Given:** The hypotheses and conventions in the Statement.

1.1 On $(\mathbb R^d,\gamma_{C_1})\times(\mathbb R^d,\gamma_{C_2})$, the sum map $(y,z)\mapsto y+z$ has characteristic function
$$\int e^{iu\cdot(y+z)}\,d(\gamma_{C_1}\times\gamma_{C_2}) =\exp(-u^TC_1u/2)\exp(-u^TC_2u/2) =\exp[-u^T(C_1+C_2)u/2].$$
The product measure exists by [F3], the integrand is bounded and therefore integrable, and [F4] justifies the iterated integral/product factorization. Since $C_1+C_2$ is positive semidefinite, [F2] and [F5] identify the pushforward under addition as $\gamma_{C_1+C_2}$. This includes singular and zero covariances. [A1, F2, F3, F4, F5, given]

2.1 The function $(x,y,z)\mapsto f(x+y+z)$ is nonnegative and Borel. Apply [F6] to integrate first in $y$ and then in $z$; the resulting iterated integral equals the integral against the product law of $(y,z)$. By step 1.1 its pushforward under addition is $\gamma_{C_1+C_2}$. Therefore
$$\int\left(\int f(x+z+y)\,d\gamma_{C_1}(y)\right)d\gamma_{C_2}(z) =\int f(x+w)\,d\gamma_{C_1+C_2}(w),$$
including the value $+\infty$. By [F1] this is exactly $T_{C_2}(T_{C_1}f)(x)=T_{C_1+C_2}f(x)$. [F1, F6, step 1.1, given]

3.1 For finite nonnegative Borel $V$, [F1] gives $\exp[-V_{C_1}(x)]=T_{C_1}(e^{-V})(x)$ and the corresponding identity after applying $C_2$. Substituting $f=e^{-V}$ into step 2.1 proves the stated potential-weight identity. Since the normalized Gaussian integrals are expectations under probability laws, no determinant factor occurs. No differentiation or covariance-path limit was used. [F1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
