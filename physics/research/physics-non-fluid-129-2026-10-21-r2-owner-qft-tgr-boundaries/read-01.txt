
ITEM lem-qftx-casimir-tensor-diagonal-invariance

## Statement

Let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra, let $B$ be its Killing form, and let $(x_a)$ and $(x^a)$ be $B$-dual bases. The tensor $\Omega:=\sum_a x_a\otimes x^a\in\mathfrak g\otimes\mathfrak g$ corresponds to the identity map under $\Phi_B(u\otimes v)(y):=uB(v,y)$, and is invariant under the diagonal adjoint action:

$$([x,\cdot]\otimes 1+1\otimes[x,\cdot])\Omega=\sum_a [x,x_a]\otimes x^a+x_a\otimes[x,x^a]=0\qquad(x\in\mathfrak g).$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, its Killing form $B$, and a pair of $B$-dual bases $(x_a),(x^a)$.

[F1] [[prop-killing-form-is-invariant-and-nondegenerate-on-a-complex-semisimple-lie-algebra]]: $B$ is symmetric, nondegenerate, and invariant, so $B([u,v],w)=B(u,[v,w])$.

[F2] [[lem-the-casimir-element-is-independent-of-dual-bases]]: $\sum_a x_a\otimes x^a$ is independent of the chosen pair of $B$-dual bases.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** identify $\mathfrak g\otimes\mathfrak g$ with $\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak g)$ using the nondegenerate form, then compute the two adjoint actions.

1.1 Nondegeneracy [F1] makes $\beta_B:v\mapsto B(v,\cdot)$ an isomorphism $\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g^*$. Since $\mathfrak g$ is finite-dimensional, $u\otimes\lambda\mapsto(y\mapsto\lambda(y)u)$ is an isomorphism $\mathfrak g\otimes\mathfrak g^*\to\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak g)$: in any basis and its dual, it sends the tensor basis to the matrix-unit basis. Their composite is $\Phi_B$, hence $\Phi_B$ is an isomorphism. Duality gives $\sum_a x_a B(x^a,y)=y$ for every $y$, so $\Phi_B(\Omega)=I_{\mathfrak g}$. [F1, given, algebra]

2.1 Invariance and symmetry [F1] imply $B([x,u],v)=-B(u,[x,v])$: indeed $B(u,[x,v])=B([u,x],v)=-B([x,u],v)$. Thus $\Phi_B(( [x,\cdot]\otimes 1)\Omega)(y)=[x,y]$, while $\Phi_B((1\otimes[x,\cdot])\Omega)(y)=-\sum_a x_aB(x^a,[x,y])=-[x,y]$, using step 1.1's dual-basis identity. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The two images in step 2.1 sum to zero for every $y$. Since $\Phi_B$ is injective by step 1.1, the sum of tensors is zero. The tensor itself is independent of the dual bases by [F2], so this is the diagonal adjoint invariance of the canonical Casimir tensor. [F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎

ITEM thm-qftx-kz-flatness

## Statement

Let $n\ge2$, let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra with Killing form $B$, let $V_i$ be finite-dimensional complex $\mathfrak g$-modules, and let $\kappa\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$. Fix a basepoint $q\in F_n(\mathbb C)$. On $F_n(\mathbb C)=\{(z_1,\ldots,z_n):z_i\ne z_j\text{ for }i\ne j\}$ let $\nabla^{\mathrm{KZ}}_\kappa=d-\kappa^{-1}\sum_{i<j}\Omega_{ij}\eta_{ij}$ be the connection defined from the pairwise Casimir tensors $\Omega_{ij}=\sum_a\rho_i(x_a)^{(i)}\rho_j(x^a)^{(j)}$ and forms $\eta_{ij}=(dz_i-dz_j)/(z_i-z_j)$, using $B$-dual bases. Then $\nabla^{\mathrm{KZ}}_\kappa$ is flat. Its sheaf of local horizontal sections is a local system with right monodromy action $T$ of the fundamental group of the ordered configuration space based at $q$. With the library convention that $[\alpha][\beta]$ traverses $\alpha$ first and $\beta$ second, the associated left representation is $[\gamma]\mapsto T_{[\gamma]}^{-1}$; on piecewise smooth loops, $T_{[\gamma]}$ is horizontal parallel transport $P_\gamma$. The homeomorphism $z\mapsto z/(1+|z|)$ identifies this fundamental group with the configuration pure braid group. If all $V_i$ are the same module with the same action, simultaneous label and tensor-factor permutation makes the connection equivariant: for $\sigma\in S_n$, set $\sigma\cdot(z_1,\ldots,z_n)=(z_{\sigma^{-1}(1)},\ldots,z_{\sigma^{-1}(n)})$ and $\tau_\sigma(v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes v_n)=v_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}$, and use $\sigma\cdot(z,v)=(\sigma\cdot z,\tau_\sigma v)$. The quotient bundle is $(F_n(\mathbb C)\times V^{\otimes n})/S_n$ under this action, and the connection descends to the unordered configuration space, giving the corresponding full configuration braid-group representation based at $[q]$. No unitarity or equivalence with a quantum-group ribbon category is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $n\ge2$, finite-dimensional complex semisimple $\mathfrak g$ with its Killing form, finite-dimensional representations $\rho_i$ on $V_i$, $\kappa\ne0$, and the KZ connection as in the Statement.

[F1] [[def-qftx-kz-connection]]: on $F_n(\mathbb C)$ the trivial bundle with fiber $V_1\otimes\cdots\otimes V_n$ has connection $\nabla=d-\kappa^{-1}\sum_{i<j}\Omega_{ij}\eta_{ij}$, with $\Omega_{ij}$ the pairwise Casimir tensor actions and $\eta_{ij}=(dz_i-dz_j)/(z_i-z_j)$.

[F2] [[lem-qftx-casimir-tensor-diagonal-invariance]]: the Casimir tensor obeys $\sum_a[x,x_a]\otimes x^a+x_a\otimes[x,x^a]=0$ for every $x\in\mathfrak g$.

[F3] [[thm-a-flat-connection-admits-local-parallel-frames]]: a connection on a finite-rank smooth vector bundle is flat exactly when every point has a neighborhood with a frame of parallel sections.

[F4] [[def-parallel-transport-along-a-piecewise-smooth-curve]]: parallel transport along a piecewise smooth curve sends an initial vector to the endpoint of the unique parallel section along that curve.

[F5] [[thm-existence-and-uniqueness-of-parallel-sections]]: on a piecewise smooth curve, an initial vector determines exactly one continuous piecewise parallel section, with continuity at corners.

[F6] [[def-based-loops-and-fundamental-group]]: $[\alpha][\beta]=[\alpha*\beta]$, where the first-then-second loop product traverses $\alpha$ first and $\beta$ second.

[F7] [[def-ordered-configuration-space]]: $F_n(X)$ consists of ordered $n$-tuples in $X$ with pairwise distinct coordinates.

[F8] [[def-pure-braid-group-from-ordered-configurations]]: $PB_n=\pi_1(F_n(D^2),q)$, and the open-disc model induces the same fundamental group at the corresponding base configuration.

[F9] [[def-unordered-configuration-space]]: $C_n(X)=F_n(X)/S_n$ with the quotient topology under coordinate permutation.

[F10] [[thm-ordered-configurations-cover-unordered-configurations-regularly]]: for a connected Hausdorff manifold of dimension at least two, the ordered-to-unordered configuration projection is a regular covering with deck transformations induced by the free $S_n$ coordinate-permutation action.

[F11] [[def-braid-group-from-unordered-configurations]]: $B_n^{\mathrm{conf}}=\pi_1(C_n(D^2),[q])$, with its open-disc configuration-space model.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct curvature expansion followed by the local-system monodromy construction.

1.1 If $\{i,j\}$ and $\{k,l\}$ are disjoint pairs, then $\Omega_{ij}$ and $\Omega_{kl}$ act on disjoint tensor factors. Each factorwise operator commutes with every operator on the other factors, so $[\Omega_{ij},\Omega_{kl}]=0$. [F1, given, algebra]

1.2 For three distinct indices $i,j,k$, write $\Omega=\sum_a x_a\otimes x^a$. Apply [F2] with $x=x_b$, place its two tensor factors in slots $i,j$, apply $\rho_i\otimes\rho_j$, multiply on slot $k$ by $\rho_k(x^b)$, and sum over $b$. The first resulting sum is $- [\Omega_{ij},\Omega_{ik}]$ and the second is $-[\Omega_{ij},\Omega_{jk}]$, because $[x_b,x_a]=-[x_a,x_b]$ and $[x_b,x^a]=-[x^a,x_b]$. Hence $[\Omega_{ij},\Omega_{ik}+\Omega_{jk}]=0$. [F2, given, algebra]

1.3 Set $u=z_i-z_j$ and $v=z_j-z_k$, so $z_i-z_k=u+v$. Then $\eta_{ij}=du/u$, $\eta_{jk}=dv/v$, and $\eta_{ik}=(du+dv)/(u+v)$. Direct expansion gives $\eta_{ij}\wedge\eta_{jk}+\eta_{jk}\wedge\eta_{ik}+\eta_{ik}\wedge\eta_{ij}=\left(1/(uv)-1/(v(u+v))-1/(u(u+v))\right)du\wedge dv=0$, since the numerator after taking a common denominator is $(u+v)-u-v=0$. [F1, algebra]

2.1 Put $B_0:=\sum_{i<j}\Omega_{ij}\eta_{ij}$, so $\nabla=d-\kappa^{-1}B_0$. Each $\eta_{ij}$ is closed because $d(dw/w)=0$ on $w\ne0$, hence $dB_0=0$, and the curvature is $R^\nabla=\kappa^{-2}B_0\wedge B_0$. Expanding the wedge product groups terms by disjoint pairs and by triples. The disjoint terms vanish by step 1.1. For $i<j<k$, the triple contribution is $C_{ijk}(\eta_{ij}\wedge\eta_{ik}-\eta_{ij}\wedge\eta_{jk}+\eta_{ik}\wedge\eta_{jk})$, where $C_{ijk}:=[\Omega_{ij},\Omega_{ik}]$: step 1.2 gives the other two commutators as $-C_{ijk}$ and $C_{ijk}$. This form is the negative of the zero Arnold sum in step 1.3, so every triple contribution vanishes and $R^\nabla=0$. [F1, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, algebra]

3.1 By [F3], every point has a neighborhood with a parallel frame. A parallel section has constant coordinates in that frame. On an overlap, if one parallel frame is the other times a smooth matrix $G(x)$, the connection equation gives $dG=0$, so $G$ is constant on each connected component of the overlap. These charts therefore define a locally constant sheaf of horizontal sections; define its transport along a continuous path by subdividing the path into chart intervals and retaining the same frame coordinates. For a piecewise smooth path this agrees with differential parallel transport by [F4] and uniqueness in [F5]. [F3, F4, F5, step 2.1, construct]

3.2 Suppose $V_i=V_0$ and $\rho_i=\rho_0$ for every $i$. The coordinate-permutation action from [F7] lifts to the same permutation of tensor factors. Since $\eta_{ij}$ is symmetric in $i,j$, and the factor permutation sends $\Omega_{ij}$ to the correspondingly relabeled $\Omega$, it preserves the connection. The action is free on $F_n(\mathbb C)$ by [F7]. The usual $\mathbb C$ is a connected Hausdorff real 2-manifold, so [F10] makes the quotient map a regular covering; the equivariant bundle and connection therefore descend to the associated vector bundle over $C_n(\mathbb C)$, with descended fiber identified with $V_0^{\otimes n}$ after choosing the representative $q$. [F1, F7, F9, F10, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 For an endpoint-fixed homotopy of paths, pull back the parallel-frame neighborhoods to the compact square. A finite subcover has a positive Lebesgue number: choose finitely many smaller metric balls covering the square whose doubled balls lie in cover members, and take the minimum radius $\delta$ of those smaller balls. Choose a rectangular grid whose cell diameters are less than $\delta$; every cell then maps into one parallel-frame neighborhood. Transport around each cell is the identity because its four edges use one constant frame. Multiplying the cell identities cancels every interior edge with its reverse, so the boundary paths have identical local-system transport. By concatenating the unique parallel sections on successive path segments, [F5] gives $P_{\alpha*\beta}=P_\beta P_\alpha$; reversing a parallel section gives $P_{\bar\alpha}=P_\alpha^{-1}$. The same composition and inverse rules hold for local-system transport by its subdivision definition. [F3, F4, F5, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 Let $T_\gamma$ denote the local-system transport for a continuous path. Step 4.1 makes it depend only on the path-homotopy class. Under [F6], the first-then-second product satisfies $T_{\alpha*\beta}=T_\beta T_\alpha$, so $\rho([\gamma]):=T_\gamma^{-1}$ is a left representation because $\rho([\alpha][\beta])=(T_\beta T_\alpha)^{-1}=T_\alpha^{-1}T_\beta^{-1}$. On piecewise smooth loops $T_\gamma=P_\gamma$ by step 3.1. The map $h(z)=z/(1+|z|)$ has inverse $h^{-1}(w)=w/(1-|w|)$ from $D^\circ$ to $\mathbb C$; its coordinatewise product restricts to a homeomorphism of ordered configuration spaces. It induces an isomorphism of fundamental groups, and [F8] identifies the resulting group with $PB_n$. [F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 The descended connection is flat because its pullback is the flat connection of step 2.1. Applying the local-system monodromy construction of steps 3.1 and 5.1 on the quotient gives its left monodromy representation of $\pi_1(C_n(\mathbb C),[q])$. The coordinatewise homeomorphism $h$ is $S_n$-equivariant, so it descends to a homeomorphism of unordered configuration spaces; [F11] identifies that fundamental group with the full configuration braid group. No identification with any quantum-group ribbon representation follows from this construction. [F6, F9, F11, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 5.1, step 3.2] ∎

ITEM def-tgr-finite-cutoff-outside-entropy

## Definition

Let $H_{\rm in}$ and $H_{\rm out}$ be nonzero finite-dimensional complex inner-product spaces, and let $\rho$ be a density operator on $H_{\rm in}\otimes H_{\rm out}$. Choose orthonormal bases $(e_i)_{i=1}^{d_{\rm in}}$ and $(f_a)_{a=1}^{d_{\rm out}}$, and use the ordered product basis $(e_i\otimes f_a)$, with the $H_{\rm in}$ index first. The outside density $\rho_{\rm out}:H_{\rm out}\to H_{\rm out}$ is the linear map whose matrix entries in $(f_a)$ are

$$ (\rho_{\rm out})_{ab}:=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\rm in}}\rho_{(i,a),(i,b)}. $$

Fix $k_B>0$ as a real scale. If $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{d_{\rm out}}$ are the eigenvalues of $\rho_{\rm out}$ counted with multiplicity, define

$$ S_{\rm out}(\rho;H_{\rm in},H_{\rm out}):=-k_B\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\rm out}}\lambda_j\log\lambda_j, $$

where $\log$ is the natural logarithm and $0\log0:=0$. The finite matrix argument below proves that $\rho_{\rm out}$ is a density operator and that the displayed endomorphism is basis-independent, so $S_{\rm out}$ is a finite real number independent of the chosen bases. In this mathematical definition $k_B$ is only a positive scalar; no physical units or model are asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Nonzero finite-dimensional complex inner-product spaces $H_{\rm in},H_{\rm out}$; a density operator $\rho$ on their ordered tensor product; orthonormal bases of each factor; and a fixed real $k_B>0$.

[F1] [[def-qc-density-operator]]: on a nonzero finite-dimensional complex inner-product space, a density operator is a non-negative endomorphism of trace one.

[F2] [[def-non-negative-and-positive-operator]]: a non-negative operator is self-adjoint and has non-negative quadratic form on every vector.

[F3] [[def-qc-tensor-register-and-permutation]]: the factor order is retained and the ordered product of the chosen factor bases is a basis of $H_{\rm in}\otimes H_{\rm out}$.

[F4] [[def-linear-map]]: a linear map is a function between vector spaces over the same field that preserves linear combinations.

[F5] [[def-coordinate-column-and-matrix-of-a-linear-map]]: the column for an input basis vector is the coordinate column of its image in the output basis.

[F6] [[thm-unique-coordinates-with-respect-to-an-ordered-basis]]: coordinates in a finite ordered basis are unique.

[F7] [[def-trace-of-an-endomorphism]]: the trace is the sum of diagonal matrix entries in any ordered basis and is basis-independent.

[F8] [[def-it-von-neumann-entropy]]: for a finite density operator, entropy is the finite spectral sum $-\sum_{\lambda_j>0}\lambda_j\ln\lambda_j$, with zero-eigenvalue contribution $0$ and natural logarithms.

## Proof

1.1 For each basis vector $f_b$, prescribe $\rho_{\rm out}f_b:=\sum_{a=1}^{d_{\rm out}}(\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\rm in}}\rho_{(i,a),(i,b)})f_a$. The product basis in [F3] supplies the matrix entries of $\rho$, and [F4]–[F6] give a unique linear extension from the prescribed images; its matrix is exactly the displayed formula. [given, F3, F4, F5, F6, construct, algebra]

1.2 Since $\rho$ is self-adjoint by [F1]–[F2], its matrix satisfies $\overline{\rho_{(i,b),(i,a)}}=\rho_{(i,a),(i,b)}$, so $\rho_{\rm out}$ is self-adjoint. For every $y\in H_{\rm out}$, expansion in $(f_a)$ gives $\langle\rho_{\rm out}y,y\rangle=\sum_i\langle\rho(e_i\otimes y),e_i\otimes y\rangle\ge0$ by [F2]–[F3]. Its trace is $\sum_a(\rho_{\rm out})_{aa}=\sum_{i,a}\rho_{(i,a),(i,a)}=\operatorname{tr}\rho=1$ by [F1] and [F7]; hence $\rho_{\rm out}$ is a density operator by [F1]. [given, F1, F2, F3, F7, algebra]

1.3 For every endomorphism $B$ of $H_{\rm out}$, define $I_{\rm in}\otimes B$ on the product basis by $(I_{\rm in}\otimes B)(e_i\otimes f_b):=e_i\otimes Bf_b$; [F3]–[F6] give its unique linear extension. For arbitrary $x,y$, expansion in the chosen bases shows this map sends $x\otimes y$ to $x\otimes By$, so it is independent of those bases. Finite matrix multiplication gives $\operatorname{tr}(\rho_{\rm out}B)=\sum_{i,a,b}\rho_{(i,a),(i,b)}B_{ba}=\operatorname{tr}(\rho(I_{\rm in}\otimes B))$. The right side is therefore basis-independent. If a second choice produced $\widetilde\rho_{\rm out}$, then $\operatorname{tr}((\widetilde\rho_{\rm out}-\rho_{\rm out})B)=0$ for all $B$; taking $B$ to be the matrix unit with sole nonzero entry in row $b$, column $a$ gives $\operatorname{tr}((\widetilde\rho_{\rm out}-\rho_{\rm out})B)=(\widetilde\rho_{\rm out}-\rho_{\rm out})_{ab}$, so every entry of the difference is zero. Thus the endomorphism is basis-independent. [given, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, algebra]

2.1 By [F8] and step 1.2, the eigenvalues of $\rho_{\rm out}$ are nonnegative and sum to one, so each term of the finite spectral sum is real, including zero eigenvalues by convention. The eigenvalue multiset is basis-independent, and multiplying by the fixed real $k_B$ preserves finiteness and basis-independence. [given, F8, step 1.2, step 1.3, algebra] ∎

ITEM ex-tgr-generalized-entropy-not-monotone-from-definition

## Example

Let $s\in\mathbb R$ be a fixed additive term, let $H_{\rm in}=\mathbb C$ with its standard inner product and basis $(1)$, and let $H_{\rm out}=\mathbb C^2$ with its standard inner product and orthonormal basis $(f_0,f_1)$. In the ordered product basis $(1\otimes f_0,1\otimes f_1)$, set the joint density matrices $\rho_0=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&0\\0&1/2\end{pmatrix}$ and $\rho_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$. On the ordered two-point set $\{0,1\}$ define $G(t):=s+S_{\rm out}(\rho_t;H_{\rm in},H_{\rm out})$. Then

$$ G(1)=G(0)-k_B\log2<G(0). $$

This is a finite matrix comparison for an additive definition; the labels $0,1$ do not describe a physical evolution.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The one-dimensional input space $\mathbb C$, the two-dimensional output space $\mathbb C^2$, a fixed real $s$, and $k_B>0$.

[F1] [[def-qc-density-operator]]: on a nonzero finite-dimensional complex inner-product space, a density operator is non-negative and has trace one.

[F2] [[def-tgr-finite-cutoff-outside-entropy]]: the outside density is the input-factor partial trace, and outside entropy is $-k_B$ times the finite natural-log spectral sum, with $0\log0=0$.

[F3] [[def-qc-tensor-register-and-permutation]]: the ordered tensor product of orthonormal factor bases is an orthonormal basis in the displayed factor order.

[F4] [[def-natural-logarithm]]: $\log$ is the inverse of $\exp$ on the positive reals.

[F5] [[cor-exponential-reciprocal-and-positivity]]: for every real $x$, $\exp(x)>0$ and $\exp(-x)=1/\exp(x)$.

[F6] [[def-real-exponential-function-and-e]]: the real exponential is defined by its power series; at input $0$, every positive-degree term vanishes and the zero-degree term is $1$, so $\exp(0)=1$.

[F7] [[thm-exponential-is-strictly-increasing]]: $\exp$ is strictly increasing and therefore injective.

## Verification

1.1 Both displayed matrices are self-adjoint, non-negative, and trace one in the ordered product basis from [F3], so they are density operators by [F1]. [given, F1, F3, algebra]

2.1 Since $H_{\rm in}$ is one-dimensional, the partial trace in [F2] has one term and returns $\rho_t$. The eigenvalues of $\rho_0$ are $(1/2,1/2)$ and those of $\rho_1$ are $(1,0)$. [given, F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 By [F4]–[F7], $\log1=0$, $\log(1/2)=-\log2$, and $\log2>0$: the first follows from [F6], the second from [F5] and injectivity in [F7], and the third from $1=\exp(0)<2=\exp(\log2)$ by [F6] and [F7]. Therefore [F2] gives $S_{\rm out}(\rho_0)=k_B\log2$ and $S_{\rm out}(\rho_1)=0$. [given, F2, F4, F5, F6, F7, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The fixed term $s$ cancels, so $G(1)-G(0)=-k_B\log2<0$ by $k_B>0$ and step 3.1. Thus the additive definition alone does not force a nondecreasing value on these ordered data. [given, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
