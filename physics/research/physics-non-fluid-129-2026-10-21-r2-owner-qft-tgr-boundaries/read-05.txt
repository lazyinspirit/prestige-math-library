
ITEM ex-qftx-vortex-paraxial-label

## Example

Fix $\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}$, $\ell\in\mathbb Z$, $0<\kappa_-<\kappa_+<\infty$, and $0<\theta_1<\theta_2<\theta_{\max}<\pi/2$. Use the finite-bandwidth vortex packet $f_{\sigma,\ell}$ constructed in the preceding lemma, with $d\nu=d^3\mathbf k/(2|\mathbf k|)$, north-gauge factor $e^{i\ell\varphi}e_\sigma^N$, and normalization $\|f_{\sigma,\ell}\|_\nu=1$. In the specified free radial model with phase $V_t f(\mathbf k)=e^{-it|\mathbf k|}f(\mathbf k)$, take $c=1$ and define the one-photon energy functional by $E_0(f)=\hbar\int_M|\mathbf k||f(\mathbf k)|^2d\nu(\mathbf k)$. Then $E_0(f_{\sigma,\ell})\le\hbar\kappa_+<\infty$, $J_zf_{\sigma,\ell}=(\ell+\sigma)\hbar f_{\sigma,\ell}$, and
$$\langle f_{\sigma,\ell},S'_zf_{\sigma,\ell}\rangle_\nu=\hbar\sigma\int_M\cos\theta|f_{\sigma,\ell}|^2d\nu,$$
$$|\langle S'_z\rangle-\hbar\sigma|\le\hbar(1-\cos\theta_2)\le\frac{\hbar\theta_2^2}{2},\qquad \langle L'_z\rangle=(\ell+\sigma)\hbar-\langle S'_z\rangle.$$
The support has nonzero radial bandwidth and angular width. An ideal exact cone or exact radial shell is not a nonzero normalizable vector of $\mathcal H_T^\nu$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice and Countable Choice, $\hbar>0$, $c=1$, the transverse photon Hilbert space and its measure, the north-patch gauge, and the specified radial phase model.

[A1] Assume the Axiom of Choice and Countable Choice for the rotation and transverse-space suppliers. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[def-countable-choice]]

[F1] The preceding lemma constructs $f_{\sigma,\ell}\in\mathcal D$ with norm one, helicity $\sigma$, radial support in $(\kappa_-,\kappa_+)$, angular support in $(\theta_1,\theta_2)$, and $J_z$ eigenvalue $(\ell+\sigma)\hbar$. [[lem-qftx-paraxial-vortex-packet-existence]]

[F2] For a normalized helicity packet, $\langle S'_z\rangle_\nu=\hbar\sigma\int_M\cos\theta|f|^2d\nu$ and the paraxial-cone spin and orbital bounds hold. [[prop-qftx-paraxial-spin-error]]

[F3] The free radial phase is $V_t f(\mathbf k)=e^{-it|\mathbf k|}f(\mathbf k)$; with $|\mathbf k|$ in inverse-length units, $t$ has length units in the $c=1$ convention. [[prop-qftx-photon-fock-angular-lift]]

[F4] $d\nu=(r/2)\,dr\,d\Omega$, the measure is sigma-finite, and radial annuli have finite measure. [[def-qftx-photon-helicity-bundle]]

[F5] On the spherical chart, $d\Omega=\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\varphi$. [[lem-euclidean-chart-measure-agrees-with-polar-surface-measure]]

[F6] Fubini's theorem applies to integrable functions on sigma-finite product measures. [[thm-fubini-theorem-for-l-one-on-sigma-finite-product-spaces]]

## Verification

**Given:** [A1], [F1]–[F6], the interval bounds in the Example, and the definition of $E_0$ there.

1.1 By [F1], $f_{\sigma,\ell}$ is a unit vector and vanishes unless $|\mathbf k|<\kappa_+$. Since the integrand is nonnegative, $E_0(f_{\sigma,\ell})=\hbar\int|\mathbf k||f_{\sigma,\ell}|^2d\nu\le\hbar\kappa_+\int|f_{\sigma,\ell}|^2d\nu=\hbar\kappa_+<\infty$. The radial multiplier is the phase generator in [F3]; with $|\mathbf k|$ in inverse-length units and $c=1$, this functional has energy units. [A1, F1, F3, F4, given, algebra]

1.2 Fix $0<\theta_0<\pi$ and $m\ge2$. On the radial annulus $1/m\le r\le m$, the polar formula in [F4] and the chart formula in [F5] give
$$\nu(\{1/m\le r\le m,\ \theta=\theta_0\})=\frac12\int_{1/m}^{m}r\,dr\int_0^{2\pi}\int_{\{\theta_0\}}\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\varphi=0,$$
because the singleton $\{\theta_0\}$ has one-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero; [F6] justifies the iterated integral on this finite-measure annulus. The whole exact cone is the countable union of these null annular intersections, hence is $\nu$-null. Also, for any fixed $r_0>0$, the polar formula gives $\nu(\{|\mathbf k|=r_0\})=\frac12\int_{\{r_0\}}r\,dr\int_{S^2}d\Omega=0$, since the radial singleton has one-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero and the sphere measure is finite. Thus an exact monochromatic shell is null as well. An $L^2(M,\nu;\mathbb C^3)$ vector supported on either null set is zero almost everywhere and cannot be normalized. The construction uses genuine finite radial and angular widths rather than a distributional delta-cone mode. [A1, F4, F5, F6, given, algebra]

2.1 The same supplier gives $J_zf_{\sigma,\ell}=(\ell+\sigma)\hbar f_{\sigma,\ell}$ and helicity $\sigma$. Substitution into [F2] yields the stated exact projected-spin expectation and, by the orbital identity there, $\langle L'_z\rangle=(\ell+\sigma)\hbar-\langle S'_z\rangle$. The normalization and finite radial support are as established in step 1.1. [A1, F1, F2, step 1.1, given, algebra]

3.1 The angular support has $0\le\theta\le\theta_2$. The cosine monotonicity and quadratic bound proved in the preceding proposition give $|\langle S'_z\rangle-\hbar\sigma|\le\hbar(1-\cos\theta_2)\le\hbar\theta_2^2/2$. The packet is normalized, so this is a model expectation bound, not an empirical statement. [A1, F1, F2, step 2.1, given] ∎

## Remarks

The energy functional and operator expectations are part of the stated mathematical free-photon model. No detector, apparatus, OAM measurement, or empirical outcome is asserted; extracting an experimental spectrum would require a specified mode analyzer and calibration.

ITEM def-qftx-paraxial-cone

## Definition

Assume the Axiom of Choice and fix constants $0<\kappa_-<\kappa_+<\infty$ and $0<\theta_{\max}<\pi/2$. Let $M=\mathbb R^3\setminus\{0\}$, $r=|\mathbf k|$, $\mathbf n=\mathbf k/r$, and use the positive $z$ axis as the propagation axis. Set $\theta(\mathbf k)=\arccos(n_z)\in[0,\pi]$ and let $\varphi\in\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)$ be the azimuth on $0<\theta<\pi$. Use the transverse Hilbert space $\mathcal H_T^\nu=\{f\in L^2(M,d\nu;\mathbb C^3):\mathbf k\cdot f(\mathbf k)=0\ \nu\text{-almost everywhere}\}$ with $d\nu=d^3\mathbf k/(2|\mathbf k|)$ and the helicity projectors $P_\sigma(\mathbf n)=(P(\mathbf n)+\sigma h(\mathbf n))/2$, where $P=I-\mathbf n\mathbf n^T$, $h=i(\mathbf n\times\cdot)$, and $\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}$. A normalized helicity-$\sigma$ packet in the paraxial cone is a vector $f\in\mathcal H_T^\nu$ such that $\|f\|_\nu=1$, $P_\sigma(\mathbf n(\mathbf k))f(\mathbf k)=f(\mathbf k)$ almost everywhere, and $f(\mathbf k)=0$ almost everywhere unless $\kappa_-\le |\mathbf k|\le\kappa_+$ and $0\le\theta(\mathbf k)\le\theta_{\max}$.

On the north patch $U_N=S^2\setminus\{-e_z\}$, write $\mathbf e_\theta=(\cos\theta\cos\varphi,\cos\theta\sin\varphi,-\sin\theta)$ and $\mathbf e_\varphi=(-\sin\varphi,\cos\varphi,0)$. Define $e_\sigma=(\mathbf e_\theta+i\sigma\mathbf e_\varphi)/\sqrt2$ and $e_\sigma^N=e^{i\sigma\varphi}e_\sigma$, with its smooth extension at $+e_z$. The south gauge is $e_\sigma^S=e^{-i\sigma\varphi}e_\sigma$; the transition is $e_\sigma^N=e^{2i\sigma\varphi}e_\sigma^S$, and neither helicity line has a global smooth unit frame. For an integer $\ell$, a north-gauge vortex coefficient has azimuthal factor $e^{i\ell\varphi}$. For the class defined here, a coefficient whose support reaches the axis is required to have the sufficient regular form $b(r,\cos\theta)(\sin\theta)^{|\ell|}e^{i\ell\varphi}$ with $b$ smooth; a smooth envelope supported away from the axis also makes $e^{i\ell\varphi}$ regular for every integer $\ell$. The winding $\ell$ is a label in this chosen patch, not a global orbital label. Use the rotation convention $U(R_z(t))f(\mathbf k)=R_z(t)f(R_z(-t)\mathbf k)=e^{-itJ_z/\hbar}f(\mathbf k)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, $\hbar>0$, the Euclidean cross product with $\epsilon_{123}=1$, and the weighted transverse one-photon Hilbert space.

[A1] Assume the Axiom of Choice, the hypothesis used for the Stone rotation generator. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] The Hilbert space uses $d\nu=d^3\mathbf k/(2|\mathbf k|)$; $P_\sigma=(P+\sigma h)/2$ is the rank-one helicity projection in the transverse plane and $h$ has eigenvalue $\sigma$ on its range. [[def-qftx-photon-helicity-bundle]]

[F2] The local frame $e_\sigma=(\mathbf e_\theta+i\sigma\mathbf e_\varphi)/\sqrt2$ gives smooth north and south gauges with transition $e_\sigma^N=e^{2i\sigma\varphi}e_\sigma^S$; the helicity line has no global smooth unit section. [[prop-qftx-helicity-chern-obstruction]]

[F3] For $R_z(t)=\exp(t[e_z]_\times)$, the unitary action is $U(R_z(t))f(\mathbf k)=R_z(t)f(R_z(-t)\mathbf k)$ and its unique self-adjoint Stone generator satisfies $U(R_z(t))=e^{-itJ_z/\hbar}$. [[thm-qftx-photon-rotations-helicity]]

## Proof

**Given:** [A1], [F1]–[F3], and the spherical-coordinate definitions in the Statement.

1.1 The vectors $\mathbf e_\theta$ and $\mathbf e_\varphi$ are orthonormal, transverse to $\mathbf n$, and satisfy $\mathbf n\times\mathbf e_\theta=\mathbf e_\varphi$ and $\mathbf n\times\mathbf e_\varphi=-\mathbf e_\theta$. Thus $e_\sigma$ has unit norm and $h e_\sigma=\sigma e_\sigma$; multiplication by the phase $e^{i\sigma\varphi}$ preserves both properties. By [F2], the resulting north gauge extends smoothly through $+e_z$, while the stated transition prevents treating it as a global frame. [F1, F2, given, algebra]

1.2 For $\ell\ne0$, $(\sin\theta)^{|\ell|}e^{i\ell\varphi}=(n_x+i\operatorname{sgn}(\ell)n_y)^{|\ell|}$, a polynomial in the smooth Cartesian coordinates $n_x,n_y$; for $\ell=0$ the factor is $1$. Therefore the stated vanishing condition makes the vortex coefficient smooth at the axis. An envelope supported away from the axis has no coordinate singularity there. [given, algebra]

2.1 Since $R_z(t)\mathbf e_\theta(\theta,\varphi-t)=\mathbf e_\theta(\theta,\varphi)$ and $R_z(t)\mathbf e_\varphi(\theta,\varphi-t)=\mathbf e_\varphi(\theta,\varphi)$, the local frame obeys $R_z(t)e_\sigma^N(\theta,\varphi-t)=e^{-i\sigma t}e_\sigma^N(\theta,\varphi)$. This identity fixes the phase contribution of the chosen gauge under the rotation convention in [F3]. [A1, F2, F3, given, algebra] ∎

ITEM lem-qftx-compact-green-support-overlap

## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $(M,g,N)$ have the geometric hypotheses of the Green-data definition. If $K,L\subseteq M$ are compact and $A,B\subseteq M$ are closed with $A\subseteq J^+(K)$ and $B\subseteq J^-(L)$, then $A\cap B$ is compact. More generally every closed subset of $J^+(K)\cap J^-(L)$ is compact. This assertion requires no Green maps and does not assume that $J^\pm(K)$ themselves are closed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice, the smooth globally hyperbolic spacetime, the compact sets $K,L$, and the indicated closed sets.

[F1] [[def-gr-causal-conditions-and-global-hyperbolicity]] includes compactness of each causal diamond $J^+(p)\cap J^-(q)$. [[def-gr-causal-curves-and-reachability]] defines causal reachability by locally absolutely continuous curves with nonzero causal tangent almost everywhere; finite concatenations of two such curves again have this property after affine rescaling of their parameter intervals.

[F2] [[def-compact-space]] supplies finite subcovers; finite unions and closed subsets of compact sets are compact by applying the open-cover definition. The smooth metric and time orientation in [[def-qftx-green-hyperbolic-interface]] give local coordinates and continuity of the metric coefficients. Countable Choice is inherited from those geometric data as in [[def-countable-choice]]; the argument below uses only finite selections.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** constructive.

1.1 (Elementary timelike coordinate neighborhoods.) At $x$ choose linear coordinates about $x$ in which $\partial_0$ is future timelike at $x$. Shrinking the coordinate box, continuity of $g$ and $N$ gives $\epsilon>0$ such that every vector with positive zeroth component and spatial-to-time coordinate ratio below $\epsilon$ is future timelike throughout that box. Choose a point $p$ on the negative time axis and a smaller open box $O_x$ about $x$, with closures inside the first box, so that every straight coordinate segment from $p$ to any point of $O_x$ has such a tangent. Then $O_x\subseteq J^+(p)$ by the definition in [F1]. The reversed construction gives an open box about $x$ contained in $J^-(q)$ for a point $q$ on its positive time axis. This uses local smooth metric continuity and straight smooth segments, without a limit-curve, global chronology-openness or dependent-choice theorem. [F1, F2, construct]

2.1 (Finite compact enclosure.) Take the family of all pairs of boxes and past points furnished by step 1.1 over $K$. These boxes form an open cover of $K$; taking a finite subcover names finitely many $p_i$ with $K\subseteq\bigcup_iJ^+(p_i)$. No past-point choice for an infinite family is made. Similarly take finitely many $q_j$ with $L\subseteq\bigcup_jJ^-(q_j)$. Concatenating the finite causal segments as in [F1] proves transitivity directly, so
$$J^+(K)\cap J^-(L)\subseteq\bigcup_{i,j}\bigl(J^+(p_i)\cap J^-(q_j)\bigr).$$
The right side is a finite union of compact diamonds by [F1]. Every closed subset of the left side is a closed subset of this compact union and therefore compact by [F2]. In particular $A\cap B$ is closed and has the required containment. If either input compact is empty the conclusion is immediate from the empty cover. [step 1.1, F1, F2, construct, discharge-construct] ∎
## Remarks

Closedness is supplied by the actual smooth-function supports in the Green arguments. The result does not assert a general Countable-Choice proof of causal simplicity. It suffices for integration by parts on an advanced-retarded overlap and for compactness of a smooth function supported both to the past and future of compact tests.
