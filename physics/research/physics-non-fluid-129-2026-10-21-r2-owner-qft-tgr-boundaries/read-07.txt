
ITEM prop-qftx-local-projection-no-signal

## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $H$ be a nonzero complex Hilbert space and let $\mathcal A,\mathcal B\subseteq\mathcal B(H)$ be unital $*$-subalgebras (each contains $I_H$ and is closed under adjoints and linear combinations) such that $AC=CA$ for every $A\in\mathcal A$ and $C\in\mathcal B$. Let $M_1,\ldots,M_n\in\mathcal A$ be a finite family, $n\ge1$, satisfying
$$\sum_{r=1}^n M_r^*M_r=I_H.$$
For any state $\omega$ on $\mathcal B(H)$, meaning a complex-linear functional with $\omega(I_H)=1$ and $\omega(X^*X)\in[0,\infty)$ for every $X\in\mathcal B(H)$, define the nonselective expectation on $C\in\mathcal B$ by
$$\omega'(C):=\sum_{r=1}^n\omega(M_r^*CM_r).$$
Then $\omega'(C)=\omega(C)$ for every $C\in\mathcal B$. This is an algebraic conditional deduction; it does not assert that a physical apparatus realizes these operators.

For the finite flat scalar mass-shell representation of [[lem-qft-scalar-local-weyl-algebra]], open regions whose point differences are all spacelike have commuting local Weyl algebras, so the proposition applies to those two algebras. The proposition itself only assumes the stated commutation relation and does not require a tensor-product factorization of $H$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice, a nonzero complex Hilbert space $H$, commuting unital $*$-subalgebras $\mathcal A,\mathcal B\subseteq\mathcal B(H)$, a finite normalized family $M_1,\ldots,M_n\in\mathcal A$, a state $\omega$, and $C\in\mathcal B$.

[A1] Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ as stated in [[def-countable-choice]]; it is needed for the cited Hilbert-adjoint and instrument interfaces.

[F1] [[def-qft-finite-bounded-kraus-instrument]]: a finite bounded Kraus family is normalized when $\sum_{a,r}M_{a,r}^*M_{a,r}=I_H$; for one outcome this is $\sum_rM_r^*M_r=I_H$.

[F2] [[lem-qft-finite-instrument-normalization]]: for a normalized finite bounded instrument and density operator $\rho$, the nonselective output $\sum_a\mathcal J_a(\rho)$ is a density operator, and the outcome probabilities sum to one. Thus its one-outcome case is a trace-preserving finite operation of the stated Kraus form.

[F3] [[lem-nrqm-composites-nuclear-bounded-cyclicity]]: for bounded $B$ and trace-class $T$, $BT$ and $TB$ are trace class and $\operatorname{tr}(BT)=\operatorname{tr}(TB)$.

[F4] [[lem-qft-scalar-local-weyl-algebra]]: in the flat scalar mass-shell Fock representation on open subsets of $\mathbb R^4$, the norm-closed local Weyl algebras commute when every point difference between the two open regions is spacelike. This fact is used only for the stated flat specialization.

[F5] [[lem-qftx-free-locality]]: on a Green-data background, causally disjoint open sets have commuting algebraic local Weyl subalgebras; the lemma does not assert existence of a Hilbert-space representation.

## Proof

1.1 Fix $C\in\mathcal B$. Since $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B$ commute elementwise and $M_r^*\in\mathcal A$, both $M_rC=CM_r$ and $M_r^*C=CM_r^*$ hold. Therefore
$$M_r^*CM_r=M_r^*M_rC.$$
Summing this equality over the finite family and using [F1] gives
$$\sum_{r=1}^nM_r^*CM_r=\left(\sum_{r=1}^nM_r^*M_r\right)C=I_HC=C.$$
This is an equality in $\mathcal B(H)$. [A1, F1, given, algebra]

2.1 By linearity of $\omega$ and the finite sum in the definition, step 1.1 yields
$$\omega'(C)=\sum_{r=1}^n\omega(M_r^*CM_r)=\omega\!\left(\sum_{r=1}^nM_r^*CM_r\right)=\omega(C).$$
When $\omega(X)=\operatorname{tr}(\rho X)$ for a density operator, put $\rho':=\sum_rM_r\rho M_r^*$. The cyclic trace identity [F3] gives $\operatorname{tr}(\rho' C)=\sum_r\operatorname{tr}(\rho M_r^*CM_r)=\omega'(C)$, and [F2] identifies $\rho'$ as the normalized one-outcome output of the finite instrument. Fact [F4] gives a flat scalar local-algebra instance of the commuting hypothesis; [F5] gives the corresponding abstract Green-background locality statement. Whenever a $*$-representation of those algebras is defined, it preserves the commutation equality because it preserves products. [A1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 1.1, given, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

A selective outcome is conditioned on one Kraus branch and need not preserve the remote expectation. The equality proved here concerns the sum over all outcomes. No measurement postulate, apparatus model, or empirical signal is asserted.

ITEM lem-qftx-laurent-negative-projection-rota-baxter

## Statement

Let $K$ be a commutative unital $\mathbb Q$-algebra, let $\epsilon$ be formal, and put $A=K((\epsilon))$ with projection $R$ onto strictly negative powers. For all $x,y\in A$, $R$ satisfies
$$R(x)R(y)=R\bigl(R(x)y+xR(y)-xy\bigr).$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Laurent-series algebra and projection of the Definition.

[F1] [[def-qftx-laurent-subtraction-target]]: $A=A_-\oplus A_+$, $R$ projects onto $A_-$, $A_-$ is closed under multiplication, and $A_+$ is closed under multiplication.

## Proof

**Given:** $x,y\in A$ and [F1].

1.1 Write $x=x_-+x_+$ and $y=y_-+y_+$ using the direct sum in [F1], so $R(x)=x_-$ and $R(y)=y_-$. Then
$$R(x)y+xR(y)-xy=x_-(y_-+y_+)+(x_-+x_+)y_--(x_-+x_+)(y_-+y_+)=x_-y_- -x_+y_+.$$ [given, F1, algebra]

2.1 Since $x_-y_-\in A_-$ and $x_+y_+\in A_+$, applying $R$ to the identity in step 1.1 gives $R(R(x)y+xR(y)-xy)=x_-y_-=R(x)R(y)$. [F1, step 1.1, algebra] ∎

ITEM cex-qftx-euler-class-loses-extension-data

## Statement refuted

A bounded complex of graded type-A Soergel bimodules whose alternating class in the split Grothendieck ring is zero must be contractible in the homotopy category.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $n\ge2$, $R=\mathbb Q[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$, and the type-A Soergel category with unit bimodule $R$.

[F1] [[def-qftx-categorified-line-model]]: $R$ is the unit object of $\mathrm{SBim}_n$, and bounded cochain complexes are considered in $K^b(\mathrm{SBim}_n)$.

[F2] [[def-split-grothendieck-rings-of-type-a-soergel-categories]]: the split Grothendieck group identifies isomorphic objects and has $[X\oplus Y]=[X]+[Y]$; the unit bimodule $R$ is a nonzero object.

[F3] [[lem-euler-class-of-a-rouquier-complex-is-homotopy-invariant-and-multiplicative]]: the Euler class of a bounded complex is $\chi(C)=\sum_m(-1)^m[C^m]$.

[F4] [[def-complex-homotopy-and-contractibility-in-an-additive-category]]: a contracting homotopy satisfies $1_{C^m}=d^{m-1}h^m+h^{m+1}d^m$ in every cochain degree $m$.

[A1] Take $C^0=C^1=R$, $d^0=0$, with all other terms and differentials zero; use the unshifted grading on both copies of $R$. Cohomology means $H^m(C)=\ker(d^m)/\operatorname{im}(d^{m-1})$.

## Counterexample

1.1 The complex $C$ is bounded and its two nonzero terms are objects of $\mathrm{SBim}_n$. Its Euler class is $\chi(C)=[R]-[R]=0$ by [F2] and [F3]. [A1, F1, F2, F3, algebra]

1.2 Since every differential is zero, $H^0(C)=R$ and $H^1(C)=R$. Each is nonzero because $1\in\mathbb Q[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ is nonzero. [A1, F1, algebra]

2.1 If $C$ were contractible, the equation of [F4] in degree $0$ would give $1_R=d^{-1}h^0+h^1d^0=0$, contradicting $1_R\ne0$. Thus $C$ is not contractible although its Euler class vanishes, refuting the Statement. [A1, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2] ∎

ITEM def-qftx-categorified-line-model

## Definition

Fix an integer $n\ge2$, let $R=\mathbb Q[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ with $\deg x_j=2$, and let $S_n$ act on $R$ by permuting variables. Let $\mathrm{SBim}_n$ be the idempotent-complete additive monoidal category of graded $(R,R)$-Soergel bimodules generated by the unit bimodule $R$, the bimodules $B_i=R\otimes_{R^{s_i}}R(1)$ for the adjacent transpositions $s_i=(i\ i+1)$, internal grading shifts, finite direct sums, tensor products over $R$, and direct summands. Use the shift convention $M(1)_d=M_{d+1}$; on the split Grothendieck ring it is recorded by $v[M]=[M(1)]$. Let $K^b(\mathrm{SBim}_n)$ denote the category of bounded cochain complexes in $\mathrm{SBim}_n$ with chain maps modulo chain homotopy.

For $1\le i<n$, let $F_i=[B_i\xrightarrow{\varepsilon_i}R(1)]$ in cohomological degrees $0,1$ and $F_i^{-1}=[R(-1)\xrightarrow{\eta_i}B_i]$ in degrees $-1,0$, with the maps and grading conventions of the positive and negative Rouquier generator complexes. For a signed word $\sigma=\sigma_{i_1}^{\epsilon_1}\cdots\sigma_{i_r}^{\epsilon_r}$, $\epsilon_k\in\{+1,-1\}$, let $F(\sigma)=F_{i_1}^{\epsilon_1}\otimes_R\cdots\otimes_R F_{i_r}^{\epsilon_r}$, using signed tensor totalization and left-to-right bracketing; the empty word gives $R$ in degree zero. If $\beta\in B_n$ is the braid represented by $\sigma$, the **Rouquier categorified line operator** $F(\beta)$ is this complex considered up to the canonical homotopy equivalence between words representing $\beta$. Tensor product is the categorical stacking operation. The comparison maps give the unit and coherent multiplication maps $F(\beta)\otimes_R F(\gamma)\simeq F(\beta\gamma)$; equivalently, left tensoring defines the coherent braid-group action on $K^b(R\text{-grmod})$.

A **physical defect realization** is additional data. A monoidal category $\mathcal D$ here means a category with a tensor bifunctor, a unit object, associator and unit isomorphisms satisfying the pentagon and unit triangles; its objects are declared line-defect types, its morphisms are declared junction maps, and its tensor product is the chosen stacking operation. The realization is a strong monoidal functor $\mathcal R:K^b(\mathrm{SBim}_n)\to\mathcal D$, meaning a functor with natural product and unit isomorphisms satisfying those same coherence equations and assigning images to every morphism, including junction maps, in its domain. Calling $\mathcal D$ a category of defects in a particular quantum field theory also requires an independently specified physical model. The Soergel and Rouquier data alone supply neither $\mathcal D$ nor this functor.

ITEM def-qftx-klr-defect-action-contract

## Definition

Fix a field $\Bbbk$, a finite color set $I$, a symmetrizable Cartan matrix $A=(a_{ij})_{i,j\in I}$, and positive integers $d_i$ satisfying $d_i a_{ij}=d_j a_{ji}$. Here $a_{ii}=2$, $a_{ij}\le0$ for $i\ne j$, and $a_{ij}=0$ exactly when $a_{ji}=0$. Fix a weight lattice $X$ (a free abelian group), simple roots $\alpha_i\in X$, and integer-valued pairings $\langle\lambda,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle$ with $\langle\alpha_j,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle=a_{ij}$. Choose polynomials $Q_{ij}(u,v)\in\Bbbk[u,v]$ with $Q_{ii}=0$, $Q_{ij}(u,v)=Q_{ji}(v,u)$, and $Q_{ij}\ne0$ for $i\ne j$; each $Q_{ij}$ is homogeneous for $\deg u=2d_i$, $\deg v=2d_j$, of weighted degree $-2d_i a_{ij}$.

For $\nu=\sum_{i\in I}m_i i\in\mathbb N[I]$ of height $r=\sum_i m_i$, a **quiver Hecke algebra** $R_Q(\nu)$ is the graded $\Bbbk$-algebra generated by idempotents $e(\mathbf i)$ for sequences $\mathbf i=(i_1,\ldots,i_r)$ with content $\nu$, dots $y_1,\ldots,y_r$, and crossings $\psi_1,\ldots,\psi_{r-1}$. Put $s_k\mathbf i=(i_1,\ldots,i_{k+1},i_k,\ldots,i_r)$. The defining relations are

$$
e(\mathbf i)e(\mathbf j)=\delta_{\mathbf i,\mathbf j}e(\mathbf i),\qquad \sum_{\mathbf i}e(\mathbf i)=1,\qquad y_a e(\mathbf i)=e(\mathbf i)y_a,\qquad y_a y_b=y_b y_a,
$$

$$
\psi_k e(\mathbf i)=e(s_k\mathbf i)\psi_k,\qquad \psi_k y_a=y_a\psi_k\ \ (a\ne k,k+1),
$$

$$
(\psi_k y_{k+1}-y_k\psi_k)e(\mathbf i)=(y_{k+1}\psi_k-\psi_k y_k)e(\mathbf i)=\delta_{i_k,i_{k+1}}e(\mathbf i),\qquad \psi_k^2e(\mathbf i)=Q_{i_k,i_{k+1}}(y_k,y_{k+1})e(\mathbf i),
$$

$$
\psi_k\psi_l=\psi_l\psi_k\ \ (|k-l|>1),\qquad (\psi_{k+1}\psi_k\psi_{k+1}-\psi_k\psi_{k+1}\psi_k)e(\mathbf i)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{Q_{i_k,i_{k+1}}(y_{k+2},y_{k+1})-Q_{i_k,i_{k+1}}(y_k,y_{k+1})}{y_{k+2}-y_k}e(\mathbf i),&i_k=i_{k+2}\ne i_{k+1},\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
$$

The displayed quotient is a polynomial because its numerator vanishes when $y_{k+2}=y_k$. The degrees are $\deg e(\mathbf i)=0$, $\deg(y_k e(\mathbf i))=2d_{i_k}$, and $\deg(\psi_k e(\mathbf i))=-d_{i_k}a_{i_k i_{k+1}}$.

For this definition, a small additive $\Bbbk$-linear graded abelian category has a set of objects and morphisms, finite biproducts, kernels and cokernels with every monomorphism a kernel and every epimorphism a cokernel, $\Bbbk$-vector-space Hom groups with bilinear composition, and a decomposition of each Hom space into integer degrees with composition adding degrees. An exact functor is a $\Bbbk$-linear functor preserving short exact sequences. An adjunction $F\dashv E$ is specified by natural unit and counit maps satisfying the two triangle identities. A natural transformation is a family of morphisms commuting with every source morphism; homogeneous degree $s$ means each component has internal degree $s$. For an abelian category $\mathcal C$, its exact Grothendieck group $K_0(\mathcal C)$ is generated by isomorphism classes $[M]$ with relations $[B]=[A]+[C]$ for each short exact sequence $0\to A\to B\to C\to0$.

A **KLR categorical action contract** specifies, for each $\lambda\in X$, such a small additive $\Bbbk$-linear graded abelian category $\mathcal C_\lambda$, exact functors $F_i:\mathcal C_\lambda\to\mathcal C_{\lambda-\alpha_i}$ with specified exact right adjoints $E_i$, and homogeneous natural transformations $x_i:F_i\Rightarrow F_i$ of degree $2d_i$ and $\tau_{ij}:F_iF_j\Rightarrow F_jF_i$ of degree $-d_i a_{ij}$. For each $\nu$ and $\lambda$, the direct sum of composite functors $F_{i_1}\cdots F_{i_r}$ over sequences of content $\nu$ must carry an action of $R_Q(\nu)$: $e(\mathbf i)$ is projection to the indicated sequence summand, $y_k$ acts by the dot on the $k$th factor, and $\psi_k$ acts by the crossing on adjacent factors, all with the relations displayed above. Functor-composition order and grading shifts must be fixed in the proposed action and used consistently.

This contract records required data and checks; it does not assert that any such categories, functors, or transformations exist. A claim of an **integrable** action additionally states and proves local nilpotence: for every object $M$ and color $i$, some powers $E_i^N(M)$ and $F_i^N(M)$ vanish. A claimed highest-weight action additionally specifies a weight $\lambda$, a chosen object in $\mathcal C_\lambda$ killed by every $E_i$, and the precise subcategory generated from it by the $F_i$, shifts, finite sums, and summands. To assert that the induced operators on $K_0(\mathcal C):=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in X}K_0(\mathcal C_\lambda)$ form a representation of $U_q(\mathfrak g)$, the contract must identify the exact Grothendieck-group convention, establish every required quantum-group relation, and cite a decategorification theorem whose hypotheses match the specified categories and functors. Merely satisfying the positive KLR relations does not establish those extra conclusions.

A physical interpretation as line-changing defects requires a separately specified defect 2-category, whose objects are declared defect sectors, whose 1-morphisms are declared defects changing sectors, and whose 2-morphisms are declared junction maps. A 2-functor here assigns objects, functors, and natural transformations while preserving identities and both compositions; a weak target additionally requires its coherence isomorphisms and equations. No physical defect realization is part of this mathematical contract.

## Remarks

The Khovanov–Lauda and Rouquier presentations use convention choices for root data, grading shifts, and polynomial parameters. The data above fixes one symmetrizable Cartan convention explicitly. A proposed action in another convention must provide a checked translation to these formulas before it can be used as a supplier.
