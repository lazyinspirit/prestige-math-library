0 cex-td-clapeyron-zero-volume-jump
Fix $T_0>0$, $p_0>0$, $g_0\in\mathbb R$, $v_0,c,\kappa>0$, and distinct positive molar entropies $s_\alpha,s_\beta$. Use units $g_0\in\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}}$, $v_0\in\mathrm{m^3\,mol^{-1}}$, $c\in\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}K^{-1}}$, and $\kappa\in\mathrm{m^3\,mol^{-1}Pa^{-1}}$. On a common open chart define
$$
g_i(T,p)=g_0-s_i(T-T_0)-\frac{c}{2T_0}(T-T_0)^2+v_0(p-p_0)-\frac{\kappa}{2}(p-p_0)^2,
$$
with $i\in\{\alpha,\beta\}$. Let $G_i=n_i g_i$ and adopt $S_i=-\partial_TG_i$, $V_i=\partial_pG_i$, $\mu_i=\partial_{n_i}G_i$, and $U_i=G_i+TS_i-pV_i$. This gives same-tag scaling and smooth local Gibbs charts. At $(T_0,p_0)$ the molar Gibbs values agree and the molar-volume jump is zero; the entropies differ.

1 cex-td-integrable-capacity-not-integer-power
Integral convergence alone does not imply that a positive-temperature fixed-$V,n$ heat capacity satisfies $C_V(T)=O((T/T_*)^m)$ for some integer $m\ge1$ as $T\downarrow0$, even when the entropy has a finite limit and the local response-chart identities apply. The conclusion below is conditional on the stated local thermodynamic supplier interfaces and chart assumptions; it is not a claim that a particular substance realizes the chart.

2 cex-td-noncompact-phase-mixture-no-attained-maximum
Let the one-dimensional phase-generator domain be $K=(-1,0)\cup(0,1)$, which is noncompact, and let its dimensionless coordinate be $x$. Assign entropy per mole
$$
s(x)=s_0-a|x|,\qquad a>0,
$$
where $s_0>a>0$ have units $\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}K^{-1}}$. Consider all Borel probability mixtures on $K$ with fixed barycenter $\bar x=0$ and declare them feasible with entropy $\int_Ks\,dP$. This is a constructed mixture model, not a claim that a particular material has this phase domain.

3 cex-tdmath-bare-contact-interface-incoherent
The coordinate set formula for a thermal join, without an accessibility or thermal-equilibrium specification, forces C1 join coherence.

4 cex-tdmath-bulk-field-pressure
At fixed activity $z>0$ and inverse temperature $\beta>0$, adding any spatially constant one-particle external energy throughout a continuum ideal-gas region leaves the thermodynamic-limit log grand partition sum per volume unchanged, as would a negligible boundary perturbation.

5 def-td-admissible-van-hove-and-tempered-exterior
Use the dimensionless coordinates, simple configurations, and pair potential of [[def-td-stable-tempered-continuum-preparation]]. A sequence $(\Lambda_k)_{k\geq1}$ of bounded measurable regions is van Hove if $|\Lambda_k|\to\infty$ and, for every fixed $s>0$,
$$\frac{|\{x\in\Lambda_k:\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Lambda_k)\leq s\}|}{|\Lambda_k|}\longrightarrow0.$$
This is an explicit condition on the boundary-layer volume; increasing volume alone does not imply it. A free wall permits particles only in $\Lambda_k$ and includes no exterior particles. A periodic wall uses the specified periodic cell and its image convention.

For the deterministic exterior class, let $C=[-1/2,1/2)^d$ and $\Lambda_n=[-n-1/2,n+1/2)^d$, with $V_n=|\Lambda_n|$. For a locally finite exterior configuration $\zeta\subset\mathbb R^d$, write $N_{C+i}(\zeta)=|\zeta\cap(C+i)|$. Given $t<\infty$, define
$$\Omega(t)=\left\{\zeta:\sum_{i\in\Lambda_n\cap\mathbb Z^d}N_{C+i}(\zeta)^2\leq tV_n\ \text{for every integer }n\geq0\right\}.$$
For an interior configuration $\omega\subset\Lambda_n$, its exterior interaction is
$$W_n(\omega,\zeta)=\sum_{x\in\omega}\sum_{y\in\zeta\setminus\Lambda_n}\varphi(x-y),$$
interpreted as an extended-real sum. A positive infinite value has zero Boltzmann weight. The regular tail controls distant terms; the class $\Omega(t)$ is a fixed uniform bound on exterior cell counts, not permission for an arbitrary volume-filling external potential. The van Hove and uniformly tempered exterior conditions are separate boundary regimes.

6 def-td-controlled-fixed-n-energy-shell
Use the periodic boxes $\Lambda_n$ and positional energy of [[def-td-stable-tempered-continuum-preparation]], and the exact-$N$ measure $\mu_{\Lambda_n,N}$ of [[post-td-classical-indistinguishable-particle-measure]]. Given $N_n\in\mathbb N$, a reduced configuration-energy center $e\in\mathbb R$ joules per dimensionless reference volume, and a width $\delta_n>0$ in the same units, define
$$S_n(N_n,e,\delta_n)=\{\omega\subset\Lambda_n:|\omega|=N_n,\ |H_{N_n}^{\mathrm{per}}(\omega)/V_n-e|\leq\delta_n\}.$$
When $0<\mu_{\Lambda_n,N_n}(S_n)<\infty$, the uniform positional shell law is
$$M_n(A)=\frac{\mu_{\Lambda_n,N_n}(A\cap S_n)}{\mu_{\Lambda_n,N_n}(S_n)}.$$
A shrinking energy-density shell uses some specified sequence $\delta_n\downarrow0$. Its total energy width is $2\delta_nV_n$, which is $o(V_n)$ but need not be microscopic or bounded. Existence of a normalizing shell and the existence of a suitable shrinking sequence are separate theorem obligations. This is not a surface measure on an exact-energy hypersurface and not a phase-space shell for $K(p)+H_N^{\mathrm{per}}$.

7 def-td-detailed-balance-rate-model
A finite rate model consists of a finite nonempty state set $S$ and rates $q_{ij}\ge0$ in seconds$^{-1}$ for $i\ne j$. Set $q_{ii}=-\sum_{j\ne i}q_{ij}$. A row probability vector is $p=(p_i)_{i\in S}$ with $p_i\ge0$ and $\sum_i p_i=1$. Its time-dependent law $p(t)$ follows the continuous-time master equation $\dot p_i=\sum_{j\ne i}[p_jq_{ji}-p_iq_{ij}]$. A strictly positive normalized vector $\pi$ is a detailed-balance law when $\pi_iq_{ij}=\pi_jq_{ji}$ for every distinct pair. Its undirected rate graph has an edge $\{i,j\}$ exactly when $\pi_iq_{ij}>0$; detailed balance makes this equivalent to $\pi_jq_{ji}>0$.

For a discrete transition kernel $K$ on a finite state set, $\pi$-detailed balance means $\pi_iK(i,j)=\pi_jK(j,i)$ for every pair. If $\pi$ is strictly positive, this identity pairs the supports of forward and reversed transitions. A rate model or a reversible kernel is a mathematical/physical model class; no observed trajectory is asserted by the definition.

8 def-td-driven-finite-path-model
Take $\Omega$ to be a finite nonempty microstate set as in [[def-td-finite-microstate-model]]: distinct elements count as distinct microstates, and the physical energy values are finite and measured in joules in the declared rest frame. Fix an integer $m\ge1$ and stage energy functions $E_k:\Omega\to\mathbb R\,\mathrm J$ for $k=0,\ldots,m$, with the energy offsets fixed by the declared protocol model. Fix inverse temperature $\beta>0$ in joules$^{-1}$. The finite partition sum and canonical probability vector at stage $k$ are $Z_k=\sum_{i\in\Omega}e^{-\beta E_k(i)}$ and $\pi_k(i)=e^{-\beta E_k(i)}/Z_k$. Since $\Omega$ is nonempty and finite and each energy is finite, $0<Z_k<\infty$ and $\pi_k$ is a strictly positive probability vector.

A transition kernel at stage $k$ is a matrix $K_k(i,j)\ge0$ with $\sum_{j\in\Omega}K_k(i,j)=1$. In the forward stage order, the energy changes instantaneously from $E_{k-1}$ to $E_k$ while the state is still $i_{k-1}$; the subsequent bath transition samples $i_k$ using $K_k(i_{k-1},i_k)$ at fixed energy $E_k$. A path is $\omega=(i_0,\ldots,i_m)\in\Omega^{m+1}$. For an initial law $\mu_0$, its path probability is $P_F(\omega)=\mu_0(i_0)\prod_{k=1}^mK_k(i_{k-1},i_k)$.

Work on the system is positive when energy is supplied to it, and heat into the system is positive when a bath transition raises its energy. Define $W(\omega)=\sum_{k=1}^m[E_k(i_{k-1})-E_{k-1}(i_{k-1})]$ and $Q_{\mathrm{in}}(\omega)=\sum_{k=1}^m[E_k(i_k)-E_k(i_{k-1})]$, both in joules. Define $F_k=-\beta^{-1}\log Z_k$ and $\Delta F=F_m-F_0=-\beta^{-1}\log(Z_m/Z_0)$. These are finite-model quantities; their physical interpretation is conditional on the adopted preparation and transition assumptions.

9 def-td-finite-memory-reset-model
Let $M$ be a finite memory-state set and $R$ a finite retained-register set. A joint preparation is a probability law $p(m,r)\ge0$ with $\sum_{m,r}p(m,r)=1$. Write $a_r=\sum_m p(m,r)$ and, for $a_r>0$, $q(m|r)=p(m,r)/a_r$. Use the finite Shannon entropy in nats and the conditional entropy $H(M|R)$ exactly as defined by [[lem-tdmath-conditional-memory-entropy]]. For a fixed target memory state $m_0$, a reset is the endpoint map $p'(m,r)=\mathbf 1_{\{m=m_0\}}a_r$: it places the memory at $m_0$ while leaving the register marginal unchanged. The definition alone assigns no physical entropy, work cost, heat transfer, or implementation.

10 def-td-finite-microstate-model
A finite statistical microstate model is a pair $(\Omega,\epsilon)$ where $\Omega$ is a finite nonempty set and $\epsilon:\Omega\to\mathbb R$ assigns a finite numerical energy to each microstate. The physical energy of $x\in\Omega$ is $E(x)=\epsilon(x)\,\mathrm J$, with its zero and any retained internal offsets fixed by the declared material model. Energies are evaluated in the center-of-mass rest frame specified by [[def-td-galilean-rest-frame]], and $U$ is the corresponding macroscopic internal-energy coordinate of [[def-td-equilibrium-state-domain]]. Equal energy values at distinct elements of $\Omega$ count as distinct microstates.

When two such models are declared independent, their joint microstate set is the Cartesian product $\Omega_A\times\Omega_B$, and additivity of the microscopic energy is the explicit condition $\epsilon_{AB}(x,y)=\epsilon_A(x)+\epsilon_B(y)$. Independence and this energy equation are model data; they do not follow merely from the two systems being separated. The definition itself assigns no weights, temperature, probability law, or dynamics.

11 def-td-isolated-compound-feasible-set
Fix a finite list of named subsystems $s_1,\ldots,s_m$, where $m\in\mathbb N$ and $m\ge1$ ([[def-natural-numbers]]), each having its own equilibrium state domain $D_{c_i}$ as defined in [[def-td-equilibrium-state-domain]]. Their separated endpoint state space is the Cartesian product ([[def-cartesian-product]])

$$\mathcal D:=D_{c_1}\times\cdots\times D_{c_m}.$$

An **isolated compound** is the system consisting of those subsystems and any named auxiliaries or work devices included inside a declared outer boundary, with no matter or energy transfer across that outer boundary during the admitted process, in the sense of [[def-td-system-and-permitted-exchanges]]. Transfers between constituents are internal to the compound. Its total energy and other extensive coordinates use the composition convention of [[post-td-bulk-additivity]]. The additive entropy sum applies only when the constituent split is admitted by [[post-td-concave-fundamental-entropy]] as a collection of independent separated macroscopic systems; an interacting compound outside that scope needs its own total-entropy model.

For specified totals and retained barriers, an **equilibrium feasible set** is the model-declared subset $\mathcal F\subseteq\mathcal D$ of endpoint tuples satisfying those totals and exactly the exchange permissions in the declared protocol. Totals are imposed on the dimensioned coordinate maps $U_c,V_c,n_{c,j}$ of [[def-td-equilibrium-state-domain]], not by treating the dimensionless numerical coordinates as quantities with units. For example, if two constituents are in thermal contact through [[def-td-thermal-contact-constraints]] while their volumes and mole amounts remain fixed, $\mathcal F$ consists of the energy partitions $(x_A,x_B)$ with the prescribed total energy and those fixed volumes and amounts. Allowing volume or species exchange changes $\mathcal F$ only when the protocol explicitly permits that exchange; retained walls keep the corresponding coordinates fixed.

The set $\mathcal F$ may be empty, noncompact, or otherwise fail to contain an entropy-maximizing state. This definition imposes no existence, compactness, convexity, uniqueness, or attainment assumption.

12 def-td-legendre-potentials
Fix a material constitution $c$ with finite species list $(1,\ldots,r)$ and a local energy representation $U_c=U_c(s,V,n_1,\ldots,n_r)$ from [[pthm-td-local-energy-representation]]. Here $s$ is entropy in $\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$, $V>0$ is volume in $\mathrm{m^3}$, each $n_j>0$ is in $\mathrm{mol}$, $U_c$ is in joules, and the conjugates are $T=U_{c,s}$ in kelvins, $p=-U_{c,V}$ in pascals, and $\mu_j=U_{c,n_j}$ in $\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}}$. The derivatives hold every other listed natural coordinate fixed; the physical-coordinate and rest-frame conventions are those of [[def-td-equilibrium-state-domain]] and [[def-td-si-quantities]].

For an open chart, a **local Legendre transformation** means the following partial transformation of $U_c$: select one or more of its extensive coordinates $q$, take their conjugate coordinates $x=D_qU_c$, and use only a neighborhood on which $(q,z)\mapsto(x,z)$, with $z$ the untransformed coordinates, has a $C^1$ inverse. Such a neighborhood is a valid local transform chart in the sense of [[def-td-response-functions]]. No inverse or potential as a function of the proposed natural coordinates is asserted outside that chart.

On their respective valid charts define the enthalpy, Helmholtz potential, Gibbs potential, and grand potential by

$$H_c:=U_c+pV,\qquad F_c:=U_c-Ts,\qquad G_c:=U_c-Ts+pV,\qquad \Omega_c:=U_c-Ts-\sum_{j=1}^r\mu_jn_j.$$

Their natural-coordinate charts are respectively $(s,p,n)$, $(T,V,n)$, $(T,p,n)$, and $(T,V,\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_r)$. In the volume transform the conjugate coordinate is $-p=U_{c,V}$, which accounts for the plus sign in $H_c$ and $G_c$; in the chemical transform the conjugates are $\mu_j=U_{c,n_j}$, which accounts for the minus signs in $\Omega_c$. All four potentials have units of joules. A displayed differential or global convex-duality characterization is not part of this definition; critical, coexistence, and other non-C2 boundaries are excluded.

13 def-td-rectangular-local-equilibrium-fields
Let $I\subseteq\mathbb R$ be an open interval measured in seconds, let $L_1,L_2,L_3>0$ be lengths in metres, and set $\Omega=\prod_{i=1}^3(0,L_i)\subseteq\mathbb R^3$ with closure $\overline\Omega$. A rectangular local-equilibrium field record consists of an open temperature interval $\mathcal T\subset(0,\infty)$, a temperature field $T:\overline\Omega\times I\to\mathcal T$ whose time derivative through order one and spatial derivatives through order two extend continuously to $\overline\Omega\times I$, a function $u\in C^1(\mathcal T,\mathbb R)$ of internal-energy density in $\mathrm{J\,m^{-3}}$, and its volumetric heat capacity $c(T):=u'(T)>0$ in $\mathrm{J\,m^{-3}\,K^{-1}}$. The coordinates $x_i$ are lengths in metres, and $T$ is measured in kelvins, using [[def-td-si-quantities]].

The record may also include a heat-flux field $j_Q:\overline\Omega\times I\to\mathbb R^3$ with components in $\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$ and a conductivity field $K:\overline\Omega\times\mathcal T\to\mathbb R^{3\times3}$ with entries in $\mathrm{W\,m^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$. The flux and tensor are field data here; no relation between them is imposed by this Definition. In Euclidean coordinates, $\nabla T=(\partial_{x_1}T,\partial_{x_2}T,\partial_{x_3}T)$ and $\operatorname{div}j_Q=\sum_i\partial_{x_i}(j_Q)_i$. On a flat face, $n$ denotes its outward unit normal.

This is a specified regular field class on a fixed rigid body. It does not assert that every physical medium has local-equilibrium fields, nor does it adopt a heat equation, a boundary condition, Fourier's law, or an entropy-production law.

14 def-td-regular-elastic-collision-model
Fix a particle mass $m_p>0$ in kilograms and velocity space $V=\mathbb R^3$ with components in $\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. The elastic collision set is
$$Q_{\rm el}=\{(v,w,v',w')\in V^4:v+w=v'+w',\ |v|^2+|w|^2=|v'|^2+|w'|^2\}.$$
Thus each allowed event conserves momentum $m_p(v+w)$ and kinetic energy $m_p(|v|^2+|w|^2)/2$. Equip $Q_{\rm el}$ with the Borel sets generated by its relative topology.

Collision-rate data consist of a nonnegative, countably additive, $\sigma$-finite Borel measure $\nu$ on these Borel sets. It is invariant under exchanging $v$ with $w$, exchanging $v'$ with $w'$, and exchanging the incoming pair $(v,w)$ with the outgoing pair $(v',w')$: for each of these maps $S$ and Borel set $E$, $\nu(S^{-1}E)=\nu(E)$. This is the three-dimensional physical restriction of the collision set and exchange conventions in [[def-tdmath-elastic-collision-kernel]], with $\sigma$-finiteness stated explicitly for the product integrals used by the entropy theorem. Its physical units and rate normalization are chosen so the associated weak collision operator has units of density per second. The measure is a declared model input; no differential cross section or microscopic limit is inferred from this Definition.

15 def-td-regular-hamiltonian-shell-preparation
Fix an integer $n\ge1$, a canonical chart with physical coordinates $q_i$ in metres and $p_i$ in $\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$, and the corresponding dimensionless coordinates $\hat q_i=q_i/(1\,\mathrm m)$ and $\hat p_i=p_i/(1\,\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-1}})$. Let $O\subseteq\mathbb R^{2n}$ be the open domain of the numerical vector $\hat x=(\hat q,\hat p)$, let $\hat H:O\to\mathbb R$ be the numerical energy map with physical energy $H=(1\,\mathrm J)\hat H$, and require $\hat H$ to have continuous partial derivatives through order two. A specified open interval $J_E\subseteq\mathbb R$ is a regular compact-shell band when $\hat H^{-1}(K)$ is compact in $O$ for every compact $K\subset J_E$ and $\nabla\hat H$ is nonzero at every point of $\hat H^{-1}(J_E)$.

For $\hat E\in J_E$, the energy shell is $\Sigma_{\hat E}:=\{x\in O:\hat H(x)=\hat E\}$. The graph-chart definition of the weighted shell integral is [[def-tdmath-regular-energy-shell]]: in a local graph chart $x_i=h(z,\hat E)$, its coarea measure is $d\mu_{\hat E}=dA_{\hat E}/|\nabla\hat H|$, with graph-area factor and gradient in the dimensionless Euclidean coordinates. Set $D(\hat E):=\int_{\Sigma_{\hat E}}d\mu_{\hat E}$ and admit the preparation only when $\Sigma_{\hat E}\ne\varnothing$ and $0<D(\hat E)<\infty$. Its normalized shell law is $\nu_{\hat E}(A):=D(\hat E)^{-1}\mu_{\hat E}(A\cap\Sigma_{\hat E})$. The current draft theorem [[thm-tdmath-regular-energy-shell-measure]] proves the coarea identity, chart independence and positive finite shell mass under its stated $C^2$, compact-regular-band hypotheses; its statement and proof are present, while its acceptance remains unresolved. Multiplying the unnormalized shell measure by a fixed positive phase-volume normalization does not change $\nu_{\hat E}$.

16 def-td-response-functions
Fix a constitution $c$ and a smooth physical-coordinate chart in $D_c$ from [[def-td-equilibrium-state-domain]], with fixed amounts $n=(n_1,\ldots,n_r)$ and $T,p$ as in [[def-td-temperature-pressure-chemical-potential]]. A **valid response chart** is an open neighborhood on which the relevant independent coordinates used below have a $C^1$ inverse to the physical $(U,V)$ coordinates; a $C^1$ diffeomorphism has a $C^1$ inverse on open neighborhoods by [[def-ck-euclidean-maps-and-diffeomorphisms]]. The existence of such a chart is a hypothesis for a response derivative, not a consequence of this definition. The derivatives use the indicated natural variables and hold all other listed coordinates fixed.

The **constant-volume heat capacity** and **constant-pressure heat capacity** of the whole system are, where the derivatives exist and are finite,

$$C_V:=\left(\frac{\partial U}{\partial T}\right)_{V,n},\qquad H:=U+pV,\qquad C_p:=\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial T}\right)_{p,n}.$$

Here $H$ is the enthalpy in joules; both heat capacities are in $\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$, not per mole. The **isothermal compressibility** and **isobaric thermal expansion coefficient** are

$$\kappa_T:=-\frac{1}{V}\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial p}\right)_{T,n},\qquad \alpha_p:=\frac{1}{V}\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial T}\right)_{p,n}.$$

Since $V>0$ and the coordinates use SI units ([[def-td-si-quantities]]), $\kappa_T$ has units $\mathrm{Pa^{-1}}$ and $\alpha_p$ has units $\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. This definition asserts no sign for any response, no identity among them, and no finite value at a critical or otherwise singular chart.

17 def-td-retained-hard-core-zero-beta-preparation
For the pair potential $\varphi$ of [[def-td-stable-tempered-continuum-preparation]], let $B=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:\varphi(x)=+\infty\}$. A finite configuration is hard-admissible if every pair displacement avoids $B$. For $z>0$, define the retained-admissibility zero-inverse-temperature weight of $\omega$ to be
$$w_0(\omega)=z^{N(\omega)}\mathbf 1_{\{H_{N(\omega)}(\omega)<\infty\}}.$$
Its finite-volume partition sum, when finite, is $\Xi_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{ret},0}(z)=\sum_{N\geq0}\int w_0(\omega)\,\mu_{\Lambda,N}(d\omega)$, using [[post-td-classical-indistinguishable-particle-measure]]. This definition sets the weight of a forbidden configuration to zero; it does not assign a value to the undefined product $0\cdot(+\infty)$. When $B=\varnothing$ the measure is the unrestricted Poisson activity model; for a nonempty hard core it is generally not Poisson. The convention is explicit model data and carries no statement about an arbitrary zero-width energy shell.

18 def-td-reversible-process
Let $P=(I_P,x_P^-,x_P^+,z_P,\kappa_P,r_P)$ be an admitted closed process record as defined in [[def-td-process-and-protocol]], with equilibrium endpoints $x_P^-,x_P^+\in D_c$. The record is **reversible relative to its declared boundary and protocol** when the model also admits a reverse record $P^{\mathrm{rev}}$ for the same system and material constitution, starting at $x_P^+$ and ending at $x_P^-$, such that every auxiliary and device state specified by the protocol is restored to its initial state and every signed boundary transfer is reversed. In particular,

$$Q[P^{\mathrm{rev}}]=-Q[P],\qquad W_{\mathrm{on}}[P^{\mathrm{rev}}]=-W_{\mathrm{on}}[P].$$

If the protocol records additional transfer channels, their signed values must likewise be negatives of their forward values. A record that is slow is not reversible merely because it is slow; it must satisfy the reverse-record condition above. Reversibility is therefore relative to what the model includes inside the boundary and which reverse controls it admits.

19 def-td-stable-tempered-continuum-preparation
Fix an integer spatial dimension $d\geq 1$, a reference length $\ell_0>0$, and a bounded measurable region $\Lambda\subset\mathbb R^d$ with dimensionless volume $V=|\Lambda|$. Its physical region is $\ell_0\Lambda$. A finite positional configuration of $N$ classical point particles in $\Lambda$ is an unordered simple set $\omega=\{x_1,\ldots,x_N\}\subset\Lambda$; simple means that no point is repeated. Particle number $N$ is a dimensionless nonnegative integer and the physical number density corresponding to $N/V$ is $N/(\ell_0^dV)$.

A pair potential is an even measurable function $\varphi:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\}$, where $\varphi(x)$ is the interaction energy in joules at physical displacement $\ell_0x$ and evenness means $\varphi(x)=\varphi(-x)$. For a labelled representative of $\omega$, define the positional pair energy
$$H_N(\omega)=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\varphi(x_i-x_j),\qquad H_0=H_1=0.$$
If any pair is forbidden by the potential, its energy and hence $H_N$ are $+\infty$. This model contains no kinetic energy, internal molecular coordinates, quantum exchange terms, or external field.

The interaction is stable if there is a constant $B\geq0$, measured in joules, such that $H_N(\omega)\geq-BN$ for every finite simple configuration. It has a regular integrable tail if there are $R>0$ and a nonnegative nonincreasing measurable function $\psi:[R,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$, measured in joules, such that $|\varphi(x)|\leq\psi(|x|)$ for $|x|\geq R$ and
$$\int_R^\infty r^{d-1}\psi(r)\,dr<\infty.$$
A stable potential with this tail condition is the stable regularly tempered class used for the free-cube results on this page. Superstability and a positive divergent core are stronger, separate hypotheses and are not implied by stability.

At temperature $T>0$, write $\beta=(k_BT)^{-1}$ in joules inverse. The activity $z>0$ is dimensionless relative to the reference intensity $\ell_0^{-d}$. A free-wall finite-volume model contains only positions in $\Lambda$ and uses $H_N$ above. A periodic model uses the declared periodic cell and sums its periodic-image pair interactions with each unordered pair counted once. These are distinct boundary preparations. The limits on this page are positional configurational limits and do not by themselves include a momentum integral or a full phase-space energy shell.

20 def-td-temperature-pressure-chemical-potential
Fix a material constitution $c$ and its equilibrium state domain $D_c$ from [[def-td-equilibrium-state-domain]]. Let $\Omega\subseteq D_c$ be a specified open, single-phase chart in physical SI coordinates $(U,V,n_1,\ldots,n_r)$, with $U$ evaluated in the center-of-mass rest frame of [[def-td-galilean-rest-frame]]. On $\Omega$, use the finite $C^2$ entropy function $S_c$ and the strict positivity $S_{c,U}>0$ adopted by [[post-td-concave-fundamental-entropy]]. Here $C^2$ means that all coordinate partial derivatives through order two exist and are continuous ([[def-ck-euclidean-maps-and-diffeomorphisms]]). A coordinate partial such as $S_{c,U}$ is the ordinary derivative in that coordinate direction while every other physical coordinate is held fixed, as in [[def-directional-and-partial-derivatives]].

Define the **absolute thermodynamic temperature**, **entropic pressure**, and **molar chemical potential** of species $j$ by

$$T_c:=\frac{1}{S_{c,U}},\qquad p_c:=T_cS_{c,V},\qquad \mu_{c,j}:=-T_cS_{c,n_j}.$$

The entropy unit is $\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$, the energy $U$ is in joules, $V$ is in cubic metres, and $n_j$ is in moles ([[def-td-si-quantities]]). Thus $S_{c,U}$ has units $\mathrm{K^{-1}}$, $T_c$ is finite and positive in kelvins, $S_{c,V}$ has units $\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,m^{-3}}$ and $p_c$ has units pascals, and $S_{c,n_j}$ has units $\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}$ and $\mu_{c,j}$ has units $\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}}$. If a microscopic model instead uses a dimensionless particle count $N_j=N_{\!A}n_j$, the corresponding per-particle chemical potential is $\mu_{c,j}/N_{\!A}$ joules, with the exact SI Avogadro constant from [[def-td-si-quantities]].

These are scalar state functions of the stated rest-frame equilibrium chart. The pressure in this definition is a derivative-defined conjugate; identifying it with force per area requires a separate adopted mechanical coupling. Neither an empirical thermometer calibration nor any equation of state is supplied by this definition.

21 def-td-thermal-contact-constraints
For each material constitution $c$, let $D_c$ be the equilibrium state domain from [[def-td-equilibrium-state-domain]]. A **thermal-contact family** is a model-declared set $\mathcal T$ of tagged pairs $(c,x)$ with $x\in D_c$. A **thermal-contact protocol** for two states $x,y\in\mathcal T$ is an admitted preparation of their compound system in which the two constituents are separated by a declared diathermal contact: matter transfer and mechanical or composition work across that contact are forbidden, while the protocol permits thermal energy transfer between the constituents. The compound's external boundary and all retained constraints are specified as in [[def-td-system-and-permitted-exchanges]] and [[def-td-process-and-protocol]].

The pair is in **thermal contact equilibrium**, written $x\sim_T y$, when the admitted contact preparation leaves both constituent coordinates unchanged, is an equilibrium preparation, and permits no net heat transfer between them. The relation $\sim_T$ is defined only on the stated family $\mathcal T$ and for the declared contact protocols. This definition does not assert that $\sim_T$ is reflexive, symmetric, or transitive, and it does not assign a real-valued temperature to its equivalence classes.

22 def-tdmath-comparison-and-stability
Fix a nonempty set $X$ and the compounds of [[def-tdmath-tagged-scalable-compound]]. A **comparison relation** $\preceq$ is defined only for pairs $(A,B)$ with $m(A)=m(B)$. Write $A\sim B$ when both $A\preceq B$ and $B\preceq A$; this is equivalence, not equality of coefficient functions. The following are explicit hypotheses on $\preceq$:

1. **Preorder:** $A\preceq A$, and $A\preceq B\preceq C$ implies $A\preceq C$.
2. **Composition consistency:** $A\preceq B$ implies $A+C\preceq B+C$ for every compound $C$.
3. **Positive scaling:** $A\preceq B$ implies $tA\preceq tB$ for every real $t>0$.
4. **Splitting and recombination:** for $x\in X$, $t>0$ and $0<s<t$, the one-state compound $te_x$ is equivalent to $se_x+(t-s)e_x$.
5. **Stability:** for equal-mass $A,B$, fixed positive compounds $Z_0,Z_1$ with $m(Z_0)=m(Z_1)$, and a sequence $\epsilon_n>0$ decreasing to $0$, if $A+\epsilon_n Z_0\preceq B+\epsilon_n Z_1$ for every $n$, then $A\preceq B$.
6. **Comparison hypothesis (CH):** for every equal-mass pair $A,B$, either $A\preceq B$ or $B\preceq A$ (or both).

These are mathematical assumptions, not conclusions about a physical system. No antisymmetry, continuity, convexity, or strict comparison is included. Strict comparison means $A\preceq B$ and not $B\preceq A$.

23 def-tdmath-cost-offset-difference-space
For the type set $\mathcal G$ and cost $F$ of [[def-tdmath-decorated-chain-costs]], define the relation $\Gamma\approx_0\Gamma'$ by $F(\Gamma,\Gamma')=F(\Gamma',\Gamma)=0$. Let $\mathcal P:=\mathcal G\times\mathcal G$ be the set of raw type pairs. Define a relation on raw pairs by

$$(A,B)\equiv(A',B')\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists C\text{ such that }A\times B'\times C\approx_0 A'\times B\times C.$$

The separate well-definedness lemma proves that $\approx_0$ is an equivalence compatible with composition and positive scaling, that $\equiv$ is an equivalence on $\mathcal P$, and that the following operations are well-defined on its classes. After that proof, write $V_F:=\mathcal P/\equiv$. Define addition by $[(A,B)]+[(D,E)]:=[(A\times D,B\times E)]$, negation by $-[(A,B)]:=[(B,A)]$, and positive scaling by $t[(A,B)]:=[(tA,tB)]$. Set zero to $[(1,1)]$, where $1$ is the empty unit type. The same lemma establishes that these operations make $V_F$ a real vector space; no vector-space law is assumed by this Definition.

24 def-tdmath-decorated-chain-costs
Let $\mathcal G$ be a set of tagged system types closed under finite composition $T\times T'$ and positive scaling $tT$. Scaling is associative and acts componentwise on products: $s(tT)=(st)T$ and $t(T\times T')=(tT)\times(tT')$. Each type $T$ has a nonempty state set $X_T$ of finite tagged compounds, every state in $X_T$ is represented by the coefficient-map interpretation of [[def-tdmath-forward-sector-hypotheses]] and has total coefficient mass $m(T)=w_T\ge0$, and $m(T\times T')=m(T)+m(T')$, $m(tT)=t\,m(T)$. The entropy $\sigma_T:X_T\to\mathbb R$ is the finite additive extensive entropy from [[thm-tdmath-arbitrary-family-calibration]]; in particular $\sigma_{tT}(ta)=t\sigma_T(a)$.

Include the formal empty unit type $1$, with $X_1=\{*\}$, $m(1)=0$, $\sigma_1(*)=0$, $T\times1=T$, and $t1=1$. A transition edge from type $T$ to type $T'$ is a pair $a\in X_T$, $b\in X_{T'}$ such that the underlying equal-mass compounds satisfy $a\prec b$ under [[def-tdmath-forward-sector-hypotheses]]. Thus an edge can exist only when $m(T)=m(T')$. Its cost is the finite real number $\sigma_{T'}(b)-\sigma_T(a)$.

A decorated finite chain from $\Gamma$ to $\Gamma'$ consists of a catalyst type $C\in\mathcal G$, an integer $N\ge1$, types $T_0=\Gamma\times C,T_1,\ldots,T_N=\Gamma'\times C$, and states $a_k\in X_{T_k}$, $b_{k+1}\in X_{T_{k+1}}$ with an edge $a_k\prec b_{k+1}$ for every $0\le k<N$. Its cost is the finite real sum

$$c=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\bigl(\sigma_{T_{k+1}}(b_{k+1})-\sigma_{T_k}(a_k)\bigr).$$

Define $F(\Gamma,\Gamma')$ as the infimum of all such witness costs. Set $F=+\infty$ if there is no witness and $F=-\infty$ if the witness costs are unbounded below. The infimum is over real witness costs, so no subtraction of extended-real infima is used. Type permutations, reassociation, reversible identifications, and the identity $T\times1=T$ are part of the declared type structure. The cost identities and inequalities require a separate proof.

25 def-tdmath-forward-sector-hypotheses
Let $P$ be a set of primitive type labels. A formal type $\Gamma$ records a finitely supported amount function $\nu_\Gamma:P\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}$, not identically zero. Primitive generators have their unit amount function. Positive scaling, separated product, and thermal-join constructors retain distinct formal tags and obey

$$\nu_{s\Gamma}=s\nu_\Gamma,\qquad \nu_{\Gamma\otimes\Gamma'}=\nu_\Gamma+\nu_{\Gamma'}\qquad(s>0).$$

Separated and joined types are distinct constructors even when their amount functions agree. Permutation, reassociation, and scaled-splitting conventions preserve the amount function. Define the positive **bookkeeping weight** $w_\Gamma:=\sum_{p\in P}\nu_\Gamma(p)$, a finite sum over support. Thus $w_{s\Gamma}=s w_\Gamma$ and every product or join has weight $w_\Gamma+w_{\Gamma'}$. The optional empty unit has zero amount function and weight zero; it is represented only by the empty compound, not as a simple chart. These are formal amounts, not masses in kilograms or an empirical conservation law. The functions and pointwise operations use [[def-function-space]]; their finite-support convention and finite sums are those of [[def-tdmath-tagged-scalable-compound]] and [[lem-tdmath-compound-mass-well-defined]].

Let $I$ index the declared family of simple types $\Gamma_i$. For each simple type $\Gamma$ in that family, let $n_\Gamma\ge1$ and let $\Gamma\subset\mathbb R^{n_\Gamma+1}$ be nonempty, open and convex, with coordinates $x=(u,v)$. The notation for a type and its chart is shared, but each state has a distinct raw tag $(\Gamma,x)$. Scaled charts are distinct tagged copies $s\Gamma=\{sx:x\in\Gamma\}$ with coordinate transport $x\mapsto sx$; transported raw tags remain distinct from the original raw tags. Let $e_{(\Gamma,x)}$ be the unit point mass from [[def-tdmath-tagged-scalable-compound]]. The represented state is

$$\mathsf{J}_\Gamma(x):=w_\Gamma e_{(\Gamma,x)},\qquad m(\mathsf{J}_\Gamma(x))=w_\Gamma.$$

Write $\mathsf{J}(x):=\mathsf{J}_\Gamma(x)$ when $x$ already carries its type tag. All state notation in this framework's compound comparisons, including a bare $x$, denotes $\mathsf{J}_\Gamma(x)$; a raw unit point mass is always written $e_{(\Gamma,x)}$. A separated pair is addition of represented states, not a unit product tag. Nonnegative multiples are pointwise and zero terms are omitted. For an encoded compound $c$, its aggregate composition is the finite amount function

$$\nu(c):=\sum_{(\Gamma,x)\in\operatorname{supp}(c)}\frac{c(\Gamma,x)}{w_\Gamma}\nu_\Gamma;\qquad m(c)=\sum_{p\in P}\nu(c)(p).$$

Fix the formal interpretation $\Pi(c)$ as the finite separated preparation whose factor at each nonzero raw tag $(\Gamma,x)$ is the amount $c(\Gamma,x)/w_\Gamma$ of that typed state, using the declared coordinate scaling transport. The accessibility relation on coefficient maps is the pullback of the declared relation on these preparations. Merging or splitting repeated copies of the same preparation is reversible; permutation and reassociation preserve the preparation. Thus $\Pi(\mathsf{J}_{s\Gamma}(sx))$ and $\Pi(s\mathsf{J}_\Gamma(x))$ are the same declared scaled preparation and yield

$$\mathsf{J}_{s\Gamma}(sx)\sim s\mathsf{J}_\Gamma(x),$$

although their raw point masses have different tags and are not equal. This reversible transport commutes with finite composition, nested type constructors, and scaled splitting. It is part of the precise formal scaling interpretation, not a deduction about physical accessibility from coordinate multiplication. A physical use must adopt the stated preparation maps and accessibility assumptions; fixed-composition models compare only preparations with the same $\nu$, while a declared channel model may additionally compare different compositions of equal $m$.

A comparison $A\prec B$ is defined only when $m(A)=m(B)$, as in the coefficient-map supplier. Write $A\sim B$ when both directions hold, and $A\prec\prec B$ when only the forward direction holds. On any fixed simple type, dividing every represented state by the same positive constant $w_\Gamma$ gives its unit-point-mass comparison. Positive scaling and its inverse preserve and reflect order; whenever the required CH and reference hypotheses hold, the unit-state entropy theorem applies after that normalization. If $S$ represents unit-point-mass comparisons, the entropy of a represented state is $w_\Gamma S(x)$ and the entropy of a finite sum $\sum_j t_j\mathsf{J}_\Gamma(x_j)$ is $\sum_j t_j w_\Gamma S(x_j)$. No independent rescaling across different types is assumed without a calibrator.

The common relation satisfies A1–A6 on all equal-mass finite compounds: (A1) reflexivity; (A2) transitivity; (A3) if $A\prec B$, then $A+C\prec B+C$ for every compound $C$; (A4) if $A\prec B$, then $tA\prec tB$ for every $t>0$; (A5) $t\mathsf{J}_\Gamma(x)$ is equivalent to $s\mathsf{J}_\Gamma(x)+(t-s)\mathsf{J}_\Gamma(x)$ for $t>0$ and $0<s<t$, with the reversible scaled-copy transport above; (A6) for equal-mass $A,B$, fixed positive equal-mass spectators $Z_0,Z_1$, and every sequence $\epsilon_n>0$ decreasing to zero, $A+\epsilon_n Z_0\prec B+\epsilon_n Z_1$ for all $n$ implies $A\prec B$. These are hypotheses, not conclusions. No comparison hypothesis is included here.

For each $i$ and $x\in\Gamma_i$, define the forward sector $A_x:=\{y\in\Gamma_i:\mathsf{J}_{\Gamma_i}(x)\prec\mathsf{J}_{\Gamma_i}(y)\}$. The simple-domain assumptions are: (A7) for $x,y\in\Gamma_i$ and $0<t<1$, the encoded compound $t\mathsf{J}_{\Gamma_i}(x)+(1-t)\mathsf{J}_{\Gamma_i}(y)$ compares to $\mathsf{J}_{\Gamma_i}(tx+(1-t)y)$; both have bookkeeping mass $w_{\Gamma_i}$, and convexity puts the recombined coordinate in the chart; (S1) each $x$ has a strict successor; (S2) $A_x$ has exactly one supporting hyperplane at $x$, expressible as $u-u_x+P_i(x)\cdot(v-v_x)=0$ with finite slope $P_i(x)$, and $P_i$ is locally Lipschitz; (S3) the boundary of $A_x$ relative to $\Gamma_i$ is arcwise connected. A supporting hyperplane means that $A_x$ lies in one of its closed half-spaces and meets the plane at $x$. The affine hull is the smallest affine subspace containing a set; relative interior is interior in that affine hull. Closure and boundary are taken in the indicated affine hull or in $\Gamma_i$, as named; boundary is closure intersected with closure of the complement in that relative topology.

This definition imposes no physical interpretation, units, or empirical claim. A contact theorem that uses joins must separately assume that each join is a simple domain satisfying these same forward-sector hypotheses.

26 def-tdmath-forward-signed-comparison
Let $u,v$ be finite-support real coefficient maps on the tagged state labels of [[def-tdmath-tagged-scalable-compound]] with equal total coefficient. For a typed state $x$, compound notation uses the represented map $\mathsf{J}(x)$ from [[def-tdmath-forward-sector-hypotheses]]; its raw unit point mass is $e_{(\Gamma,x)}$. Signed maps still use the ordinary total of their raw coefficients, exactly the bookkeeping mass of the represented nonnegative maps. Put $d=v-u$, $d^+(x)=\max(d(x),0)$ and $d^-(x)=\max(-d(x),0)$. Define $u\preceq_s v$ to mean $d^-\prec d^+$ under the compound relation of [[def-tdmath-forward-sector-hypotheses]]. Write $u\sim_s v$ when both $u\preceq_s v$ and $v\preceq_s u$. These are formal signed comparison maps, not physical negative amounts. The positive and negative parts are finite-support compounds of equal mass. If $u\sim_s v$, then $cu\sim_s cv$ for every real $c$: positive $c$ uses scaling, $c=0$ is reflexivity, and negative $c$ swaps the positive and negative parts while equivalence supplies both comparison directions. This Definition introduces the signed notation; ordinary-order compatibility and the signed-order laws are proved separately.

27 def-tdmath-periodized-empirical-field
Use the cube $\Lambda_L=[0,L)^d$ and the translation $T_x$ defined in [[def-tdmath-poisson-configuration-reference]]. For a finite configuration $\omega\subset\Lambda_L$, let $\omega^{(L)}=\{q+Lk:q\in\omega,\ k\in\mathbb Z^d\}$ be its periodic continuation. Define its **periodized empirical field**
$$R_{L,\omega}:=\frac1{L^d}\int_{\Lambda_L}\delta_{T_x\omega^{(L)}}\,dx.$$
The integral is the push-forward of normalized Lebesgue measure on $\Lambda_L$ under $x\mapsto T_x\omega^{(L)}$. For a probability law $C_L$ on finite configurations in $\Lambda_L$, define its **empirical-field barycenter** $\bar C_L$ by
$$\bar C_L(f):=\int C_L(d\omega)\,R_{L,\omega}(f)=\frac1{L^d}\int_{\Lambda_L}E_{C_L}[f(T_x\omega^{(L)})]\,dx$$
for local tame $f$ from [[def-tdmath-local-tame-topology]]. This is a spatial average over the finite box, not the raw finite-volume law viewed at a fixed origin.

28 def-tdmath-signed-comparison
Let $u,v:X\to\mathbb R$ be finite-support real coefficient maps with equal total coefficient $\sum_xu(x)=\sum_xv(x)$. These signed maps are formal comparison notation, not compounds. Put $d:=v-u$ and define $d^+(x):=\max\{d(x),0\}$ and $d^-(x):=\max\{-d(x),0\}$. Then $d^+,d^-$ are nonnegative finite-support compounds of equal mass, as verified in [[lem-tdmath-spectator-cancellation]]. Define

$$u\preceq_s v\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d^-\preceq d^+,$$

using the compound comparison relation of [[def-tdmath-comparison-and-stability]]. Thus positive coefficients of $v-u$ remain on the right and negative coefficients move to the left. Signed notation is never a claim that a negative amount of a physical system exists.

29 def-tdmath-superstable-regular-interaction
Assume AC. Fix an integer dimension $d\geq1$, use the Euclidean norm $|\cdot|_2$ from [[def-euclidean-inner-product]], define $|x|_\infty=\max_j|x_j|$, and use the numerical coordinate conventions in [[def-tdmath-poisson-configuration-reference]]. A finite configuration is a finite subset $\omega\subset\mathbb R^d$ with no repeated points; its number of points is $N(\omega)$. A pair potential is an even Borel function, measurable for the Borel sigma-algebra generated by open sets ([[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]), $\phi:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\}$, where even means $\phi(-x)=\phi(x)$. Define
$$H_\phi(\omega)=\sum_{\{x,y\}\subset\omega}\phi(x-y),\qquad H_\phi(\varnothing)=0.$$
The sum is over unordered two-point subsets, so every term is evaluated at a nonzero separation; if any term is $+\infty$, the sum is $+\infty$.

The potential is stable if some $B\geq0$ satisfies $H_\phi(\omega)\geq-BN(\omega)$ for every finite configuration. It is superstable in the Georgii splitting convention if $\phi=\phi_s+\phi_r$, where $\phi_s$ is stable and $\phi_r\geq0$ everywhere and, for some $r_0,\delta>0$, $\phi_r(x)\geq\delta$ whenever $|x|_\infty\leq r_0$. It is lower regular if a nonnegative nonincreasing $\psi:[0,\infty)\to[0,+\infty]$ satisfies
$$\phi(x)\geq-\psi(|x|_\infty),\qquad \int_0^\infty\psi(r)r^{d-1}\,dr<\infty.$$
It is regular if it is lower regular and, for some $R<\infty$, $\phi(x)\leq\psi(|x|_\infty)$ whenever $|x|_\infty\geq R$. These are the conventions used for the interacting results on this page. A genuine divergent positive core is the separate additional condition that some nonincreasing $\chi:(0,r_1]\to(0,\infty)$ satisfies $\phi(x)\geq\chi(|x|_\infty)$ for $0<|x|_\infty\leq r_1$ and $\int_0^{r_1}\chi(r)r^{d-1}dr=+\infty$; it is not implied by superstability.

For $L>0$ put $\Lambda_L=[0,L)^d$, $V_L=L^d$, and let $H_{L,\mathrm{free}}$ be $H_\phi$ for configurations restricted to $\Lambda_L$. For sufficiently large periodic cubes, periodize $\omega\subset\Lambda_L$ by $\omega^{\mathrm{per}}=\{x+Lk:x\in\omega,\ k\in\mathbb Z^d\}$ and set
$$H_{L,\mathrm{per}}(\omega)=\frac12\sum_{x\in\omega}\sum_{y\in\omega^{\mathrm{per}},\ y\ne x}\phi(y-x),$$
interpreting the value as $+\infty$ if a positive infinite term occurs. The far image sum is absolutely convergent for all sufficiently large $L$ by [[lem-tdmath-integrable-tail-diagonal-gap]]; this is the image convention used below. With fixed numerical length and energy units, the canonical positional partition is
$$Z(L,N;\beta)=\frac1{N!}\int_{\Lambda_L^N}e^{-\beta H_\phi(q_1,\ldots,q_N)}dq_1\cdots dq_N,\quad Z(L,0;\beta)=1,$$
where $\beta>0$, and the grand partition is $\Xi(L;a,\beta)=\sum_{N\geq0}a^NZ(L,N;\beta)$ for numerical activity $a>0$. The normalized unit-intensity Poisson version is $Z_Q(L;z,\beta)=E_{Q_{\Lambda_L}}[z^{N_{\Lambda_L}}e^{-\beta H_{L,\mathrm{free}}}]$; since the Poisson density contributes $e^{-V_L}$, $\Xi(L;z,\beta)=e^{V_L}Z_Q(L;z,\beta)$. A physical pressure identification is not part of this mathematical definition.

For a stationary probability law $P$ on the configuration space of [[def-tdmath-poisson-configuration-reference]], write $\rho(P)=E_PN_{[0,1)^d}$. When the limit exists, write
$$\mathcal E(P)=\lim_{L\to\infty}V_L^{-1}E_PH_{L,\mathrm{free}},\qquad I(P)=\lim_{L\to\infty}V_L^{-1}D(P_{\Lambda_L}\Vert Q_{\Lambda_L}).$$
The cited results below establish existence under their stated hypotheses; this definition does not assume it for every stationary law.

30 def-tdmath-tagged-scalable-compound
Let $X$ be a set of state labels. A **tagged scalable compound over $X$** is a function $c:X\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}$ whose support $\operatorname{supp}(c):=\{x\in X:c(x)\ne0\}$ is finite. The coefficient is a dimensionless formal amount; state labels retain their tags, so equal coefficients on different labels do not identify those labels. Compounds are equal exactly when their coefficient functions are equal.

For compounds $c,d$ and $t\ge0$, define composition and nonnegative scaling pointwise by $(c+d)(x):=c(x)+d(x)$ and $(tc)(x):=t c(x)$. The zero function is the empty compound; in particular $0c=0$. Comparison axioms involving scaling still require a strictly positive scalar unless explicitly stated otherwise. The **mass** of $c$ is the finite sum $m(c):=\sum_{x\in\operatorname{supp}(c)}c(x)$. It is dimensionless bookkeeping mass, not thermodynamic mass or energy. The compound is positive when $m(c)>0$.

For every state label $x\in X$, the **unit-state compound** $e_x$ is the coefficient function with $e_x(y)=1$ if $y=x$ and $e_x(y)=0$ otherwise. Its support is $\{x\}$, so it is a tagged compound. In compound comparisons, a state label $x$ denotes $e_x$. Every compound has the canonical finite decomposition $c=\sum_{x\in\operatorname{supp}(c)}c(x)e_x$, with an empty sum when $c=0$; coefficients on this support are strictly positive. The mass-one property $m(e_x)=1$ and the equivalence $m(c)=0\Longleftrightarrow c=0$ are proved in [[lem-tdmath-compound-mass-well-defined]].

The formula for mass is independent of the chosen finite set containing the support, and composition and scaling obey $m(c+d)=m(c)+m(d)$ and $m(tc)=t m(c)$; [[lem-tdmath-compound-mass-well-defined]] proves these assertions.

31 def-tdmath-thermal-contact-hypotheses
For two simple domains $\Gamma_i\subset\mathbb R^{n_i+1}$ and $\Gamma_j\subset\mathbb R^{n_j+1}$, write their coordinates $(u_i,v_i)$ and $(u_j,v_j)$. Their thermal-join domain is

$$\Delta_{ij}:=\{(u,v_i,v_j):\exists u_i,u_j,\ (u_i,v_i)\in\Gamma_i,\ (u_j,v_j)\in\Gamma_j,\ u=u_i+u_j\}.$$

The joined simple type is a distinct formal tag with amount function $\nu_{\Delta_{ij}}=\nu_{\Gamma_i}+\nu_{\Gamma_j}$ and weight $w_{\Delta_{ij}}=w_{\Gamma_i}+w_{\Gamma_j}$, as defined in [[def-tdmath-forward-sector-hypotheses]]. Encode the separated pair by $\mathsf{J}_{\Gamma_i}(x)+\mathsf{J}_{\Gamma_j}(y)$ and a joined coordinate $Z$ by $\mathsf{J}_{\Delta_{ij}}(Z)$. Their bookkeeping masses agree, and their aggregate composition functions agree. These encoded maps and their raw tags remain distinct. Positive coordinate-scaled copies use the reversible preparation transport of that definition; nested joins preserve their finite primitive amount function under reassociation and permutation. The empty type contributes the zero map.

Write $J_{ij}(x,y)$ for the join state with the summed energy coordinate and the unchanged work coordinates. A compound pair $(x,y)$ and $J_{ij}(x,y)$ have the same total energy and work coordinates but are distinct tagged objects. Define $x\sim_T y$ exactly when $\mathsf{J}_{\Gamma_i}(x)+\mathsf{J}_{\Gamma_j}(y)$ and $\mathsf{J}_{\Delta_{ij}}(J_{ij}(x,y))$ are accessible in both directions. Nested joins use the declared reassociation and permutation identifications.

Assume every finite join is itself a simple domain satisfying [[def-tdmath-forward-sector-hypotheses]]. The contact hypotheses are mathematical assumptions on the common relation: (T1) $(x,y)\prec J_{ij}(x,y)$; (T2) every join state has at least one reversible split into component states at its stated coordinates, and the equal-state split of any state into positive scaled copies of itself is reversible; (T3) $\sim_T$ is transitive; (T4) for every simple state $x$ there are states $x_0,x_1$ in its domain with $x_0\prec\prec x\prec\prec x_1$ and $x_0\sim_T x_1$; (T5) for every ordered pair of simple systems, every $x$ in the first and every work coordinate $v$ in the second's projection $\rho(\Gamma_j):=\{v:\exists u,(u,v)\in\Gamma_j\}$, there is a state $y$ with work coordinate $v$ and $x\sim_T y$.

For the completed calibrator branch, add (C1) **join coherence**: whenever $x\sim_T y$, the join state $J_{ij}(x,y)$ is thermally equivalent to each of $x$ and $y$ under the nested-join identifications. The coordinate sum in the definition alone does not assert C1. In later theorem statements, T1–T5 and C1 are explicitly listed as hypotheses; they are not treated as established physical laws.

32 def-tdmath-type-offset-vector-space
Let $\mathcal G$ be a set of formal type labels closed under the formal operations $(\Gamma,\Gamma')\mapsto\Gamma\times\Gamma'$ and $(t,\Gamma)\mapsto t\Gamma$ for every real $t>0$. Let $F:=\mathbb R^{(\mathcal G)}$ be the vector space of finite-support real functions on $\mathcal G$, and let $e_\Gamma$ be the function equal to $1$ at $\Gamma$ and $0$ elsewhere.

Form the set of relation vectors

$$\mathcal R:=\{e_{\Gamma\times\Gamma'}-e_\Gamma-e_{\Gamma'}:\Gamma,\Gamma'\in\mathcal G\}\cup\{e_{t\Gamma}-t e_\Gamma:t>0,\Gamma\in\mathcal G\}.$$

Let $N$ be the set of all finite real linear combinations of members of $\mathcal R$, and define the **type-offset vector space**

$$V_{\mathcal G}:=F/N.$$

The class of $c\in F$ is $[c]$. The defining relations are $[e_{\Gamma\times\Gamma'}]=[e_\Gamma]+[e_{\Gamma'}]$ and $[e_{t\Gamma}]=t[e_\Gamma]$. The set existence, subspace properties, quotient operations and vector-space laws are proved in [[lem-tdmath-type-offset-vector-space-well-defined]]. These are formal algebraic type labels; no actual catalyst-chain cost or physical existence claim is built into $V_{\mathcal G}$.

33 def-tdmath-van-hove-sequence
For a bounded set $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^d$ and $R>0$, define its two-sided boundary layer
$$\partial_R\Omega=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)\leq R\},$$
where distance is Euclidean distance and $|\cdot|$ denotes $d$-dimensional Lebesgue measure. A van Hove sequence is a sequence of bounded Lebesgue measurable sets $(\Omega_k)_{k\geq1}$ with $0<|\Omega_k|<\infty$, $|\Omega_k|\to\infty$, and
$$\frac{|\partial_R\Omega_k|}{|\Omega_k|}\longrightarrow0\qquad\text{for every fixed }R>0.$$
The condition controls both inner and outer boundary neighborhoods. It does not impose connectedness, a bounded aspect ratio, smooth boundaries, or a periodic identification.

Let $P\in\mathcal P^1_{\rm stat}(\Omega)$, as defined in [[def-tdmath-local-tame-topology]], have finite specific relative entropy $I(P)=\gamma<\infty$ relative to the unit-intensity reference $Q$ of [[def-tdmath-poisson-configuration-reference]]. Let $C=[0,1)^d$ be the reference cell and order the other unit cells lexicographically; $F_+$ is the set of cells preceding $C$. For each finite $A\subset F_+$ let $k_A$ be the conditional density of the configuration in $C$ given the configuration in $A$, relative to $Q_C$, as supplied by [[thm-conditional-density-formula]]. Then
$$g(A):=E_P[\log k_A]=D(P_{A\cup C}\Vert Q_{A\cup C})-D(P_A\Vert Q_A)$$
is finite, nonnegative, and increasing with finite $A$, with $\sup\{g(A):A\subset F_+\text{ finite}\}=\gamma$. For any increasing finite exhaustion $A_m\uparrow F_+$, the densities $k_{A_m}$ converge in $L^1(P_{F_+}\otimes Q_C)$ to a density $k_\infty$ of $P_{F_+\cup C}$ relative to $P_{F_+}\otimes Q_C$, and
$$\log k_{A_m}\longrightarrow\log k_\infty\quad\text{in }L^1(P).$$
The logarithmic convergence is directed: for every $\varepsilon>0$ some finite $A_m$ satisfies $\sup_{B\supseteq A_m,\ B\subset F_+\text{ finite}}E_P|\log k_B-\log k_\infty|<\varepsilon$.

85 lem-tdmath-conditional-memory-entropy
Let $M$ and $R$ take values in finite sets under a joint probability law $p(m,r)\ge0$ with $\sum_{m,r}p(m,r)=1$. For any finite random variable $Y$ with law $p_Y$, define its Shannon entropy by $H(Y):=-\sum_{y:p_Y(y)>0}p_Y(y)\log p_Y(y)$; for a pair, $H(M,R)$ means this definition applied to its joint law. Put $a_r:=\sum_m p(m,r)$ and, when $a_r>0$, $q(m|r):=p(m,r)/a_r$. Thus $H(R)=-\sum_{r:a_r>0}a_r\log a_r$. Define $H(M|R):=\sum_{r:a_r>0}a_r[-\sum_{m:q(m|r)>0}q(m|r)\log q(m|r)]$. Then $H(M,R)=H(R)+H(M|R)$ and $H(M|R)\ge0$.

93 lem-tdmath-euler-identity-for-degree-one-homogeneous-function
Let $C\subseteq\mathbb R^d$ be open and $f:C\to\mathbb R$ be $C^1$. Here nondegenerate means that $J_x$ contains a point different from $1$; $1$ may be an endpoint. Then

97 lem-tdmath-integrable-tail-diagonal-gap
Let $d\geq1$ and let $\phi$ be a regular potential with a nonnegative nonincreasing envelope $\psi:[R_0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$, where $R_0<\infty$ and $\int_{R_0}^\infty r^{d-1}\psi(r)dr<\infty$. Thus $|\phi(x)|\leq\psi(|x|_\infty)$ for $|x|_\infty\geq R_0$. For $g>2R_0$ set
$$\eta(g)=g^d\psi(g)+\int_g^\infty r^{d-1}\psi(r)dr.$$
Then $\eta(g)\to0$ and
$$\sum_{m=1}^\infty m^{d-1}\psi(mg)\leq 2^{d-1}g^{-d}\eta(g).$$
Moreover, there is a choice $g(\ell)>0$, for all sufficiently large real $\ell$, such that $g(\ell)\to\infty$, $g(\ell)/\ell\to0$, and
$$\left(\frac{\ell}{g(\ell)}\right)^d\eta(g(\ell))\longrightarrow0.$$
For every fixed finite configuration and all sufficiently large periods $L$, the noncoincident periodic-image tail $\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^d,\ |k|_\infty\geq K_0}|\phi(x-y+Lk)|$ is absolutely convergent. The tail of the cell envelope $\psi(|j|_\infty-1)$ over all sufficiently large integer offsets is summable. This lemma is a deterministic tail estimate; it uses no superstability or thermodynamic-limit conclusion.

Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$
([[def-countable-choice]]). Let $d\ge1$, let $\phi$ be a stable pair

to be $0$ when $U_N=+\infty$. Also,
$\int_{\Lambda_L^N}f\,d\lambda_d^{\otimes N}$ abbreviates the nonnegative

which satisfies $K L^d<n_L\le K L^d+1$ by the integer-part lemma

107 lem-tdmath-relative-entropy-variational-and-chain-rules
Assume AC. Let $P,Q$ be probability measures on one measurable space and use $D(P\Vert Q)$ as defined in [[def-tdmath-finite-region-relative-entropy]]. Then
$$D(P\Vert Q)=\sup_{\phi\in B_b}\left(P(\phi)-\log Q(e^\phi)\right),$$
where $B_b$ is the set of bounded real measurable functions. In particular $D\geq0$. If $T$ is measurable, then $D(P\circ T^{-1}\Vert Q\circ T^{-1})\leq D(P\Vert Q)$.

For standard-Borel spaces $X,Y$, probability reference measures $Q_X,Q_Y$, and a joint probability $P_{XY}\ll Q_X\otimes Q_Y$ with finite $D(P_{XY}\Vert Q_X\otimes Q_Y)$, let $P_{Y|X=x}$ be the conditional law supplied by [[thm-conditional-density-formula]]. Then
$$D(P_{XY}\Vert Q_X\otimes Q_Y)=D(P_X\Vert Q_X)+\int_X D(P_{Y|X=x}\Vert Q_Y)\,P_X(dx).$$
More generally, for nested standard-Borel observations $R$ and $S$ with $R$ a measurable function of $S$, and a fixed probability reference $Q_Y$ for a further variable $Y$, the conditional increments
$$G(R):=E_P\!\left[D(P_{Y|R}\Vert Q_Y)\right],\qquad G(S):=E_P\!\left[D(P_{Y|S}\Vert Q_Y)\right]$$
satisfy $0\leq G(R)\leq G(S)$ whenever the joint relative entropy is finite.

$$
\mathcal U_i(\lambda s,\lambda V,\lambda n)=\lambda\mathcal U_i(s,V,n)
$$
for all $\lambda$ in a neighborhood of $1$ for which the scaled state remains in the chart. This is a phase-branch hypothesis: the general tagged law of [[post-td-bulk-additivity]] does not imply it. It may be stipulated independently on each local branch, or supplied by the common-law bulk subclass of [[pthm-td-euler-and-gibbs-duhem]] when each phase's local inverse states lie in its designated entropy cone with fixed species and intensive parameters and common SI units and energy/entropy reference conventions. That supplied subclass uses local inverse uniqueness and does not require a global energy inverse.

Suppose a state $(T_0,p_0)\in O$ is an attained interior coexistence equilibrium with bilateral energy, volume, and species exchange between the two branches, and both branches admit the valid local Gibbs charts of [[def-td-legendre-potentials]]. Write $g_i=G_i/n_i$, $s_i=S_i/n_i$, and $v_i=V_i/n_i$ in the units specified by [[def-td-si-quantities]]. If
$$
\Delta v(T_0,p_0):=v_\beta(T_0,p_0)-v_\alpha(T_0,p_0)\ne0,
$$
then, on some open interval about $T_0$, the equal-Gibbs-per-mole coexistence condition $g_\alpha(T,p(T))=g_\beta(T,p(T))$ defines a unique local $C^1$ curve and
$$
\frac{dp}{dT}=\frac{s_\beta-s_\alpha}{v_\beta-v_\alpha}.
$$
If, in addition, conversion from $\alpha$ to $\beta$ is admitted as a reversible process in contact with an ideal thermal reservoir at the same fixed $T$, then its molar latent heat absorbed by the system is $\ell_{\alpha\to\beta}=T(s_\beta-s_\alpha)$. These are conditional model deductions. The slope conclusion is not asserted when $\Delta v=0$ or where the branch charts are not $C^1$.

120 pthm-td-collision-h-theorem

$$S_{\rm kin}(t)=-k_B\int_{\Omega_{\rm per}}\int_{\mathbb R^3}f(x,v,t)\log\!\left(\frac{f(x,v,t)}{f_{\rm ref}}\right)\,d^3v\,d^3x.$$

$$\frac{dS_{\rm kin}}{dt}=\frac{k_B}{4}\int_{\Omega_{\rm per}}\int_{Q_{\rm el}}(A-B)\log\!\left(\frac AB\right)\,d\nu\,d^3x\ge0,$$
where $A=f(x,v,t)f(x,w,t)$ and $B=f(x,v',t)f(x,w',t)$; the supplier's $\log A-\log B$ denotes the dimensionless difference $\log(A/B)$ for these positive quantities of equal units. 122 pthm-td-compressibility-capacity-ratio

173 thm-tdmath-continuous-ideal-gas-block-limit
Fix dimension $d\ge1$, particle mass $m>0$, action reference $h>0$, inverse energy $\beta>0$, and positive cube volumes $V_N$ with $N/V_N\to\rho\in(0,\infty)$. For $N$ indistinguishable classical noninteracting particles in a cube of volume $V_N$, use the phase-space reference $dq\,dp/(N!h^{dN})$ and Hamiltonian $H_N(q,p)=\sum_{i=1}^N|p_i|^2/(2m)$. Define $\lambda_\beta=h\sqrt{\beta/(2\pi m)}$. Then the dimensionless partition integral and free-energy limits are
$$Z_N=\frac{V_N^N}{N!\lambda_\beta^{dN}},\quad\frac{-\beta^{-1}\log Z_N}{N}\longrightarrow\beta^{-1}[\log(\rho\lambda_\beta^d)-1],\quad\frac{-\beta^{-1}\log Z_N}{V_N}\longrightarrow\beta^{-1}\rho[\log(\rho\lambda_\beta^d)-1].$$
There is also the following finite-momentum-block limit. Let $M=dN$, let $G_1,\ldots,G_M$ be independent standard normal variables, set $A_N=M^{-1}\sum_{a=1}^MG_a^2$, and define canonical momenta $P_a^{\rm can}=\sqrt{m/\beta}\,G_a$ and shell-prepared momenta $P_a^{\rm sh}=\sqrt{m/\beta}\,G_a/\sqrt{A_N}$. The shell-prepared vector has exactly energy $dN/(2\beta)$, and for every fixed $r$ and bounded continuous $\psi:\mathbb R^r\to\mathbb R$,
$$\mathbb E\psi(P_1^{\rm sh},\ldots,P_r^{\rm sh})\longrightarrow\mathbb E\psi(P_1^{\rm can},\ldots,P_r^{\rm can}).$$
This is weak convergence for each fixed block in this free-particle model. The shell preparation is the stated normalized-Gaussian construction, and no finite-width shell or interacting-gas equivalence is asserted.

174 thm-tdmath-continuum-variational-pressure
Assume AC, the ultrafilter extension principle, DC, and HB. Let $\phi$ be superstable and regular as in [[def-tdmath-superstable-regular-interaction]], and fix $z,\beta>0$. For stationary finite-intensity configuration laws, let $I(P)$ be relative entropy density with respect to the independent unit-intensity Poisson law, $\rho(P)=E_PN_{[0,1)^d}$, and $\Phi(P)$ the limiting positional energy density. The existence and lower semicontinuity of $\Phi$ under these hypotheses are part of the proof below. For free and periodic cubes define
$$Z_{L,z,\beta,bc}=E_{Q_{\Lambda_L}}\!\left[z^{N_{\Lambda_L}}e^{-\beta H_{L,bc}}\right],\qquad bc\in\{\mathrm{free},\mathrm{per}\}.$$
Then the limit $p_Q(z,\beta)=\lim_{L\to\infty}L^{-d}\log Z_{L,z,\beta,bc}$ exists, is independent of $bc$, and
$$p_Q(z,\beta)=-\min_{P\in\mathcal P^1_{\rm stat}}\{I(P)+\beta\Phi(P)-\rho(P)\log z\}.$$
The minimizer set is nonempty and compact in the local-tame topology $\tau_L$. This theorem concerns positional configurations only. If $\Xi_{L,z,\beta}$ is instead normalized against Lebesgue measure with $1/N!$, then $\Xi_{L,z,\beta}=e^{L^d}Z_{L,z,\beta,\mathrm{free}}$, so its logarithmic rate is $p_Q+1$.

183 thm-tdmath-general-trace-gibbs-model
Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\mathcal H\ne\{0\}$ be a complex Hilbert space and let $A$ be self-adjoint with regular Borel projection-valued measure $E$ from [[thm-spectral-theorem-for-unbounded-self-adjoint-operators]], with $E((-\infty,a))=0$ for finite $a\in\mathbb R$. For each $\gamma>0$, define the bounded Gibbs operator $K_\gamma:=\Phi_E(f_\gamma)$ using the bounded Borel PVM integral of [[thm-bounded-borel-pvm-integral]], where $f_\gamma(\lambda)=e^{-\gamma\lambda}$ for $\lambda\ge a$ and $f_\gamma(\lambda)=e^{-\gamma a}$ for $\lambda<a$. Fix $\beta>0$ and suppose $K_\beta$ is trace class in the sense of [[def-trace-class-operator]]. Then $K_\beta$ is injective and has a finite or countable orthonormal basis $(u_j)$ of $\mathcal H$ with $Au_j=E_ju_j$, $E_j\ge a$, and $K_\beta u_j=e^{-\beta E_j}u_j$. Its partition function $Z_\beta:=\operatorname{Tr}K_\beta=\sum_j e^{-\beta E_j}$ is positive and finite, and $\rho_\beta:=K_\beta/Z_\beta$ is positive with $\operatorname{Tr}\rho_\beta=1$. For every $B\in\mathcal B(\mathcal H)$, $\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_\beta B)=\sum_j(e^{-\beta E_j}/Z_\beta)\langle Bu_j,u_j\rangle$ absolutely. Further, let $J\subset(0,\infty)$ be open and suppose that for every compact $C\subset J$ there is a summable family $a_j\ge0$ with $(1+|E_j|^2)e^{-\gamma E_j}\le a_j$ for all $j$ and $\gamma\in C$. Then for each $\gamma\in J$, $K_\gamma$ is trace class with partition function $Z_\gamma:=\operatorname{Tr}K_\gamma=\sum_j e^{-\gamma E_j}$. With $p_j(\gamma):=e^{-\gamma E_j}/Z_\gamma$, define $\bar E_\gamma:=\sum_jp_j(\gamma)E_j$, $\operatorname{Var}_\gamma(E):=\sum_jp_j(\gamma)(E_j-\bar E_\gamma)^2$, and $S_\gamma:=-\sum_jp_j(\gamma)\log p_j(\gamma)$. These quantities are finite and satisfy $-\partial_\gamma\log Z_\gamma=\bar E_\gamma$, $\partial_\gamma^2\log Z_\gamma=\operatorname{Var}_\gamma(E)$, and $S_\gamma=\log Z_\gamma+\gamma\bar E_\gamma$ as in [[thm-tdmath-summable-canonical-differentiation]]. Countable nonnegative sums use the finite-subsum supremum convention of [[def-tdmath-countable-canonical-model]].

184 thm-tdmath-grand-response-covariance
Let $(X,\Sigma,\mu)$ be a measure space, let $w:X\to[0,\infty)$ and $X_a:X\to\mathbb R$ ($1\le a\le r$) be measurable, and let $U\subseteq\mathbb R^r$ be open. For $\theta=(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_r)$, define $\theta\cdot X(x):=\sum_{a=1}^r\theta_aX_a(x)$. Set $Z(\theta):=\int w\exp(\theta\cdot X)\,d\mu$ and assume $0<Z(\theta)<\infty$ on $U$. Suppose for every compact $K\subset U$ there is an integrable $G_K\ge0$ such that $w e^{\theta\cdot X}(1+\sum_a|X_a|^2)\le G_K$ pointwise for all $\theta\in K$. For the normalized density $p_\theta=w e^{\theta\cdot X}/Z(\theta)$, define $\mathbb E_\theta Y:=\int Yp_\theta\,d\mu$ when integrable and $\operatorname{Cov}_\theta(X_a,X_b):=\mathbb E_\theta[(X_a-\mathbb E_\theta X_a)(X_b-\mathbb E_\theta X_b)]$. Then $\partial_a\log Z=\mathbb E_\theta X_a$ and $\partial_b\mathbb E_\theta X_a=\operatorname{Cov}_\theta(X_a,X_b)$. Thus the Hessian of $\log Z$ is positive semidefinite. This is a mathematical model identity; it assumes no independence or absence of correlations.

185 thm-tdmath-independent-bulk-limit
Let $\Omega_0$ be a nonempty finite set, let $E:\Omega_0\to\mathbb R$, and fix $\beta\in\mathbb R$. 186 thm-tdmath-infinite-no-sinks-sandwich
Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $V$ be a real vector space and $p:V\to\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\}$ satisfy $p(0)=0$, $p(tx)=tp(x)$ for every $t>0$, and $p(x+y)\le p(x)+p(y)$ in extended-real arithmetic. Assume **no sinks**: $p(x)<+\infty$ if and only if $p(-x)<+\infty$. Put $W:=\{x\in V:p(x)<+\infty\}$. Then $W$ is a linear subspace and there exists a real-linear $L:V\to\mathbb R$ such that

$$-p(-x)\le L(x)\le p(x)\qquad(x\in V),$$

with $-\infty\le r\le+\infty$ interpreted in the usual extended order. The result is valid in arbitrary dimension and allows $p=+\infty$ outside $W$.

For the conditional offset application, let $V=V_{\mathcal G}$ from [[def-tdmath-type-offset-vector-space]], let $F:\mathcal G\times\mathcal G\to\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\}$ satisfy $p([e_\Gamma]-[e_{\Gamma'}])=F(\Gamma,\Gamma')$, and define $B(\Gamma):=L([e_\Gamma])$. Then $B(\Gamma\times\Gamma')=B(\Gamma)+B(\Gamma')$, $B(t\Gamma)=tB(\Gamma)$ for $t>0$, and

$$-F(\Gamma',\Gamma)\le B(\Gamma)-B(\Gamma')\le F(\Gamma,\Gamma').$$

For each $\Gamma\in\mathcal G$, let $X_\Gamma$ be a state set and $\sigma_\Gamma:X_\Gamma\to\mathbb R$ a baseline state function. If every admitted transition $(\Gamma,x)\to(\Gamma',y)$, with $x\in X_\Gamma$ and $y\in X_{\Gamma'}$, satisfies $\sigma_{\Gamma'}(y)-\sigma_\Gamma(x)\ge F(\Gamma,\Gamma')$, then $\sigma_\Gamma(x)+B(\Gamma)$ is nondecreasing across those transitions. The existence or properties of such a cost $p$, including no-sinks, are hypotheses and are not proved by this theorem.

194 thm-tdmath-phase-dimension

$$x^\alpha\in\Omega_C:=\{x\in\mathbb R^{C-1}:x_i>0,\ \sum_{i=1}^{C-1}x_i<1\},$$

with the convention $\mathbb R^0=\{0\}$ and $\Omega_1=\{0\}$. The final component fraction is $1-\sum_{i<C}x_i>0$. For $P\ge2$, define the $q=C(P-1)$ coexistence equations

$$F_{i,\alpha}(T,p,x^1,\ldots,x^P)=\mu_i^\alpha(T,p,x^\alpha)-\mu_i^1(T,p,x^1)=0\quad(1\le i\le C,\ 2\le\alpha\le P).$$

At a solution $z_*\in U$, assume some $q\times q$ derivative minor of $F:U\to\mathbb R^q$ is invertible. Then the local solution set is a graph of dimension

$$n-q=2+P(C-1)-C(P-1)=C-P+2.$$

For $P=1$ there are no coexistence equations and the coordinate domain itself has dimension $n=C+1=C-P+2$. In a specified reactive variant with $R\ge1$, let $\nu^1,\ldots,\nu^R\in\mathbb R^C$ be linearly independent dimensionless stoichiometric vectors and add the $R$ affinity equations $A_r=\sum_{i=1}^C\nu_i^r\mu_i^1=0$. If the combined equation map has an invertible $(q+R)\times(q+R)$ derivative minor at a solution and $q+R\le n$, its local zero set has dimension

$$n-q-R=C-P+2-R.$$

These are conditional local dimensions under the displayed nonsingular-minor assumptions. No dimension formula follows at singular solutions, on composition boundaries, or from equation counting alone; no empirical or general physical phase rule is asserted.

195 thm-tdmath-poisson-reference-count-properties
Assume AC, fix $d\geq1$ and $\lambda>0$, and let $Q_\lambda$ be the configuration law of [[def-tdmath-poisson-configuration-reference]]. 196 thm-tdmath-reaction-offset-feasibility
Let $A$ be a finite set of reaction channels, let $r\in\mathbb N$, let $\nu_a\in\mathbb R^r$ be the output-minus-input type vector of channel $a$, and let $d_a\in\mathbb R$ be a finite lower bound on its already calibrated entropy change. There exists an offset vector $b\in\mathbb R^r$ satisfying

$$b\cdot\nu_a+d_a\ge0\qquad(a\in A)$$

if and only if every $\lambda\in\mathbb R^A$ with $\lambda_a\ge0$ and $\sum_{a\in A}\lambda_a\nu_a=0$ satisfies $\sum_{a\in A}\lambda_ad_a\ge0$. If an actual transition has entropy change $\Delta\sigma_a\ge d_a$, such a vector guarantees $\Delta\sigma_a+b\cdot\nu_a\ge0$. The criterion is exact for the finite lower-bound inequalities; it does not claim necessity for actual transitions when $d_a$ is only a loose bound.

197 thm-tdmath-reference-strip-gluing
Assume [[def-tdmath-forward-sector-hypotheses]] and T1–T4 of [[def-tdmath-thermal-contact-hypotheses]] for every finite join. For any one simple type $\Gamma_i$, every two finite compounds of its scaled copies with the same total coefficient mass are comparable. Thus CH holds on all multiple scaled products of that type. No entropy across different material types is asserted here.

198 thm-tdmath-retained-hard-zero-beta-limit
Let $\phi$ be superstable and regular as in [[def-tdmath-superstable-regular-interaction]], and let $B=\{x:\phi(x)=+\infty\}$. For a finite configuration $\omega$, call it admissible when no pair displacement belongs to $B$; for periodic boxes require this also for every periodic image pair. Define the zero-inverse-temperature-parameter continuation (the mathematical $\beta=0$ branch) to be $z^{N(\omega)}\mathbf 1_{\rm admissible}(\omega)$, explicitly retaining the hard exclusion. Then the normalized Poisson free and periodic partition rates exist and equal
$$-\min_{P\in\mathcal K_B}\{I(P)-\rho(P)\log z\},$$
where $\mathcal K_B$ is the set of stationary laws with no forbidden pair almost surely. The minimizer set is nonempty and compact in $\tau_L$. The periodically continued grand laws are relatively compact in $\tau_L$, all their accumulation points are minimizers, and if this minimizer is unique they converge to it for every measurable local observable of at-most-linear count growth. If $B=\varnothing$, this is the Poisson activity-$z$ model. For $B\ne\varnothing$, it is not an unrestricted Poisson law and does not assign a value to the undefined product $0\cdot(+\infty)$.

199 thm-tdmath-sector-nesting
Under [[def-tdmath-forward-sector-hypotheses]], the sectors are nested: if $y\in A_x$, then $A_y\subseteq A_x$; equality holds when $y\in\partial A_x$, and $A_y\subset\operatorname{int}_{\Gamma_i}A_x$ when $y\in\operatorname{int}_{\Gamma_i}A_x$. For every $x,y\in\Gamma_i$, either $x\prec y$ or $y\prec x$ (possibly both). Strict comparison is exactly interior membership: $x\prec\prec y$ if and only if $y\in\operatorname{int}_{\Gamma_i}A_x$.

200 thm-tdmath-spatial-mean-information
Assume AC. Let $d\geq1$, let $P\in\mathcal P^1_{\rm stat}(\Omega)$, with this class defined in [[def-tdmath-local-tame-topology]], be ergodic under the full translation group $\mathbb R^d$, and suppose its specific relative entropy $I(P)=\gamma$ relative to the unit-intensity Poisson law $Q$ is finite. Ergodic means that every measurable event invariant modulo $P$-null sets under each translation has probability $0$ or $1$. For an integer $N\geq1$, let $\Lambda_N=[0,N)^d$, and let $h_N=dP_{\Lambda_N}/dQ_{\Lambda_N}$, which exists because $D(P_{\Lambda_N}\Vert Q_{\Lambda_N})<\infty$. Then
$$\frac{1}{N^d}\log h_N\longrightarrow\gamma\qquad\text{in }L^1(P).$$
Without the full-translation ergodicity hypothesis the proof yields an invariant $L^1(P)$ limit of mean $\gamma$; it need not be constant.

201 thm-tdmath-specific-entropy-density
Assume AC and let $P\in\mathcal P^1_{\rm stat}(\Omega)$, with this class defined in [[def-tdmath-local-tame-topology]], be stationary for the translations of [[def-tdmath-poisson-configuration-reference]]. For a bounded Borel set $G$, write $P_G$ and $Q_G$ for the restrictions of $P$ and the unit-intensity Poisson reference $Q$ to configurations in $G$. For $L>0$, put $\Lambda_L=[0,L)^d$ and
$$D_L:=D(P_{\Lambda_L}\Vert Q_{\Lambda_L}).$$
Then the specific relative entropy density
$$I(P):=\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{D_L}{L^d}$$
exists in $[0,+\infty]$ and satisfies
$$I(P)=\sup_{m\in\mathbb N,\ m\geq1}\frac{D(P_{\Lambda_m}\Vert Q_{\Lambda_m})}{m^d}.$$
If $I(P)<\infty$, every bounded Borel restriction has finite relative entropy. This is an information functional relative to the specified unit-intensity Poisson law; changing the reference changes its value.

202 thm-tdmath-splitting-cost-quotient
Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $F$ be the decorated cost of [[def-tdmath-decorated-chain-costs]], and assume the **no-sinks condition**

$$F(\Gamma,\Gamma')<+\infty\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F(\Gamma',\Gamma)<+\infty$$

for every type pair. Let $V_F$ be the real difference space of [[def-tdmath-cost-offset-difference-space]]. Then there is an additive extensive offset $B:\mathcal G\to\mathbb R$ such that

$$-F(\Gamma',\Gamma)\le B(\Gamma)-B(\Gamma')\le F(\Gamma,\Gamma').$$

If every admitted cross-type transition $x\prec y$ from $\Gamma$ to $\Gamma'$ satisfies the calibrated cost inequality of [[thm-tdmath-unattained-cost-entropy-inference]], then $\sigma_\Gamma(x)+B(\Gamma)$ is nondecreasing across every such transition. Only this weak inequality is asserted; no-gap equalities and strict extensions are not concluded.

203 thm-tdmath-stable-tempered-free-cube-grand-pressure
Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ and let $d\ge1$. Let $\phi$ be a stable,
power-tempered pair potential as in
[[def-tdmath-stable-power-tempered-particle-model]], with constants
$B,C,R,\epsilon$ as there. Fix activity $a>0$ and inverse-energy parameter
$\beta>0$ in fixed reference units. Use the free-boundary grand partition sums
$\Xi_L(a,\beta)$ defined in
[[lem-tdmath-particle-exponential-cutoff]]. For $L\ge1$, put
$$p_L(a,\beta):=L^{-d}\log\Xi_L(a,\beta).$$
Then the limit
$$p(a,\beta):=\lim_{L\to\infty}p_L(a,\beta)$$
exists as a finite real number. The assertion is for cubes with free boundary
and the displayed power-tail hypothesis.

204 thm-tdmath-summable-canonical-differentiation
Let $I,E_i,g_i,J,Z$ be as in [[def-tdmath-countable-canonical-model]]. Suppose that for every compact interval $K\subset J$ there is a summable sequence $a_i\ge0$ such that $g_i(1+|E_i|^2)\exp(-\beta E_i)\le a_i$ for every $i\in I$ and $\beta\in K$. Then $Z$ is positive, finite, and $C^2$ on $J$, with $Z'(\beta)=-\sum_i g_iE_i\exp(-\beta E_i)$ and $Z''(\beta)=\sum_i g_iE_i^2\exp(-\beta E_i)$. The probabilities $p_i$ are normalized; their mean $\bar E$ and variance are finite and satisfy $-\partial_\beta\log Z=\bar E$ and $\partial_\beta^2\log Z=\operatorname{Var}_\beta(E)$. Moreover $H_g(p)=\log Z+\beta\bar E$, and its defining series is absolutely convergent.

205 thm-tdmath-superstable-van-hove-pressure
Assume a superstable regular pair potential as in [[def-tdmath-superstable-regular-interaction]] and the van Hove definition [[def-tdmath-van-hove-sequence]]. For every van Hove sequence $(\Omega_k)$, the free-wall physical grand pressure satisfies
$$|\Omega_k|^{-1}\log\Xi_{\Omega_k}(z,\beta)\longrightarrow p(z,\beta),$$
the free-cube limit from [[thm-tdmath-decreasing-integrable-tail-free-cube-pressure]]. The extra superstability hypothesis is essential to this proof's uniform cell-occupation bound; no arbitrary-shape theorem under stability alone is claimed.

206 thm-tdmath-unattained-cost-entropy-inference
Assume the calibrated entropy family of [[thm-tdmath-arbitrary-family-calibration]], the cross-type accessibility axioms A1–A6 of [[def-tdmath-forward-sector-hypotheses]], and the decorated cost $F$ of [[def-tdmath-decorated-chain-costs]]. For every pair of types $\Gamma,\Gamma'$ and states $x\in X_\Gamma$, $y\in X_{\Gamma'}$,

$$x\prec y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sigma_\Gamma(x)+F(\Gamma,\Gamma')\le \sigma_{\Gamma'}(y).$$

The infimum defining $F$ need not be attained. If $F=+\infty$, the right side is false; if $F=-\infty$, the right side is true.

207 thm-tdmath-uniform-tempered-exterior-pressure
Assume a stable regular pair potential with the genuine positive-core condition of [[lem-tdmath-superquadratic-positive-core]]: a nonincreasing $\chi(r)>0$ satisfies $\phi(x)\geq\chi(|x|_\infty)$ near zero and $\int_0^{r_0}\chi(r)r^{d-1}dr=\infty$. For fixed $z,\beta>0$, define the exterior class
$$\Omega(t)=\{\zeta:\sum_{i\in\Lambda_n\cap\mathbb Z^d}N_{C_i}(\zeta)^2\leq t(2n+1)^d\text{ for every }n\}.$$
Then the free-wall grand pressure in $\Lambda_n=[-n-\tfrac12,n+\tfrac12)^d$, with the exterior pair energy against any $\zeta\in\Omega(t)$, converges to the free-cube pressure uniformly in $\zeta$. This statement is conditional on the genuine positive core; it does not infer that condition from superstability alone.

208 thm-tdmath-van-hove-fixed-n-limit
Assume superstability and regularity, let $(\Omega_k)$ be van Hove, and let $N_k/|\Omega_k|\to\rho$ for an interior attainable density $\rho>0$. For the free-wall exact-$N_k$ positional partition with $1/N_k!$ normalization,
$$|\Omega_k|^{-1}\log Z(\Omega_k,N_k;\beta)\longrightarrow F_\beta(\rho),$$
the free-cube canonical rate of [[thm-tdmath-canonical-interior-density-limit]]. Superstability is used for the arbitrary-shape pressure upper bound; no stability-only upper theorem is asserted.
