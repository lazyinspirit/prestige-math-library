### def-em-monochromatic-source-free-phasors

Let $\omega>0$, let $D\subseteq\mathbb R^3$ be a nonempty open set, and let $c>0$, $\mu_0>0$, $\varepsilon_0=(\mu_0c^2)^{-1}$ be the vacuum constants of [[def-em-si-conventions]]. (a) [Phasor encoding] For every pair of $C^\infty$ maps $\widetilde E,\widetilde B:D\to\mathbb C^3$ ([[def-ck-euclidean-maps-and-diffeomorphisms]]) define the **associated real fields** $$E(x,t):=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\widetilde E(x)e^{-i\omega t}\bigr),\qquad B(x,t):=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\widetilde B(x)e^{-i\omega t}\bigr)\qquad((x,t)\in D\times\mathbb R),$$ componentwise, with the complex exponential and real part of [[def-complex-exponential]] and [[def-complex-conjugate-real-imaginary-part-and-modulus]]; this is the encoding convention of [[def-em-complex-plane-wave]] specialized from plane-wave amplitudes to a general $C^\infty$ amplitude, and the complex fields are not themselves physical fields. (b) [Source-free class] The pair $(\widetilde E,\widetilde B)$ is called a **frequency-$\omega$ source-free vacuum phasor pair on $D$** when it is $C^\infty$ and satisfies, on $D$, $$\operatorname{div}\widetilde E=0,\qquad \operatorname{div}\widetilde B=0,\qquad \operatorname{curl}\widetilde E=i\omega\widetilde B,\qquad \operatorname{curl}\widetilde B=-i\omega\varepsilon_0\mu_0\widetilde E=-i\omega c^{-2}\widetilde E,$$ with the divergence and curl of [[def-divergence-and-curl-of-a-c1-vector-field]] applied componentwise to the complex fields. The word 'source-free' records that the displayed equations carry no charge or current term; the associated real fields are read as an electric and a magnetic field of [[def-em-fields]], $\omega$ is the angular frequency and $T:=2\pi/\omega$ the period. (c) [Status and scope] This item fixes the encoding, the four phasor equations and the terminology only. It asserts no equation for the associated real fields, no outgoing or boundary condition, no existence or uniqueness statement, no energy or flux reading and no empirical content. In particular the deduction that the associated real fields satisfy the smooth-branch vacuum Maxwell equations with zero sources is not part of this definition; for the pairs used on this page it is established by the exterior-scattering theorem recorded later on this page. Only $C^\infty$ pairs on an open set are defined, so no boundary trace, weak or distributional class is introduced here.

This is a definition contract assembled from earlier items; well-definedness is discharged by the cited suppliers. (i) The encoding (a) is the real-part-of-complex-amplitude convention of [[def-em-complex-plane-wave]] with a general $C^\infty$ spatial amplitude; the componentwise real part of [[def-complex-conjugate-real-imaginary-part-and-modulus]] and the complex exponential of [[def-complex-exponential]] are total on $\mathbb C^3$, so the associated real fields are defined for every $C^\infty$ pair, with no equation assumed. (ii) The class (b) is cut out by four equations on the open set $D$: the divergence and curl of [[def-divergence-and-curl-of-a-c1-vector-field]] act componentwise on the $C^\infty$ complex fields, and the two constants satisfy $\varepsilon_0\mu_0=c^{-2}$ by [[def-em-si-conventions]]; the displayed third and fourth equations use only complex scalar multiplication, so the class is well-defined without any choice principle. (iii) The physical reading of the associated real fields as an electric and a magnetic field uses [[def-em-fields]]; the units of $\widetilde E$ are V$\,$m$^{-1}$ and those of $\widetilde B$ are T, inherited from that definition. (iv) Clause (c) records exactly what is not asserted; in particular the complex Maxwell-to-real-Maxwell deduction is deliberately left to a consumer, because a definition must not assert the field equations of its associated real fields.

### lem-em-phasor-real-part-calculus-and-equivalence

Let $D\subseteq\mathbb R^3$ be open, $c>0$ and $\omega>0$, and let $\widetilde E,\widetilde B:D\to\mathbb C^3$. Define $E,B:D\times\mathbb R\to\mathbb R^3$ by $E(x,t):=\operatorname{Re}(\widetilde E(x)e^{-i\omega t})$ and $B(x,t):=\operatorname{Re}(\widetilde B(x)e^{-i\omega t})$ componentwise ([[def-complex-exponential]], [[def-complex-conjugate-real-imaginary-part-and-modulus]]; [[def-ck-euclidean-maps-and-diffeomorphisms]] for the classes). Then: (a) [Calculus] if $\widetilde E,\widetilde B$ are $C^1$, then $E,B$ are $C^1$ and at every $(x,t)\in D\times\mathbb R$: $\partial_tE(x,t)=\operatorname{Re}(-i\omega\widetilde E(x)e^{-i\omega t})$, and likewise for $B$; for each $j\in\{1,2,3\}$, $\partial_jE(x,t)=\operatorname{Re}(\partial_j\widetilde E(x)e^{-i\omega t})$, and likewise for $B$; and with the divergence and curl of [[def-divergence-and-curl-of-a-c1-vector-field]] acting on the spatial variable, $\operatorname{div}E(x,t)=\operatorname{Re}((\operatorname{div}\widetilde E)(x)e^{-i\omega t})$, $\operatorname{curl}E(x,t)=\operatorname{Re}((\operatorname{curl}\widetilde E)(x)e^{-i\omega t})$, and likewise for $B$. (b) [Injectivity] if $w\in\mathbb C^3$ and $\operatorname{Re}(we^{-i\omega t})=0$ for all $t\in\mathbb R$, then $w=0$; more generally, if $w:D\to\mathbb C^3$ is continuous and $\operatorname{Re}(w(x)e^{-i\omega t})=0$ for all $(x,t)\in D\times\mathbb R$, then $w\equiv0$ on $D$. (c) [Equivalence] if $\widetilde E,\widetilde B$ are $C^1$, the phasor system $$\operatorname{div}\widetilde E=0,\quad\operatorname{div}\widetilde B=0,\quad\operatorname{curl}\widetilde E=i\omega\widetilde B,\quad\operatorname{curl}\widetilde B=-i\omega c^{-2}\widetilde E$$ holds on $D$ if and only if the real system $$\operatorname{div}E=0,\quad\operatorname{div}B=0,\quad\operatorname{curl}E=-\partial_tB,\quad\operatorname{curl}B=c^{-2}\partial_tE$$ holds on $D\times\mathbb R$.

Write the encoding in real components: by the Cartesian form and Euler identity of [[cor-complex-exponential-cartesian-form-modulus-and-eulers-identity]], $\operatorname{Re}(w(x)e^{-i\omega t})=(\operatorname{Re}w(x))\cos(\omega t)+(\operatorname{Im}w(x))\sin(\omega t)$ componentwise, with the real and imaginary parts of [[def-complex-conjugate-real-imaginary-part-and-modulus]] and the complex arithmetic of [[def-complex-numbers-and-arithmetic]]. (a) For fixed $x$ each component is a constant-coefficient combination of $\cos(\omega t)$ and $\sin(\omega t)$; the affine map $t\mapsto\omega t$ has derivative $\omega$ by [[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]], and [[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]] applied to the differentiable real sine and cosine maps of [[thm-sine-and-cosine-derivatives]] gives their composite derivatives. The sum, scalar-multiple and product rules of [[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]] then give $\partial_tE=\operatorname{Re}(-i\omega\widetilde Ee^{-i\omega t})$, and the spatial derivatives pass through the constant coefficients, giving the spatial formulas for each component; the divergence and curl formulas follow by applying the componentwise formulas and the linearity of [[lem-divergence-and-curl-are-linear-and-obey-the-scalar-product-rules]] (for complex fields, componentwise in real and imaginary parts). (b) Evaluating at $t=0$ gives $\operatorname{Re}w=0$; evaluating at $t=\pi/(2\omega)$ gives $\operatorname{Re}(-iw)=\operatorname{Im}w=0$ by the quarter-turn values of [[thm-quarter-turn-values-and-shift-formulas]] and the Cartesian form of [[cor-complex-exponential-cartesian-form-modulus-and-eulers-identity]], so $w=0$. The continuous case applies the pointwise result at each $x\in D$; no choice principle is used. (c) Direction (i)$\Rightarrow$(ii): substitute the phasor equations into the calculus formulas of (a); e.g. $\operatorname{curl}E=\operatorname{Re}((\operatorname{curl}\widetilde E)e^{-i\omega t})=\operatorname{Re}(i\omega\widetilde Be^{-i\omega t})=-\partial_tB$ and $\operatorname{curl}B=\operatorname{Re}(-i\omega c^{-2}\widetilde Ee^{-i\omega t})=c^{-2}\partial_tE$. Direction (ii)$\Rightarrow$(i): subtract the complex expressions and apply (b), e.g. $\operatorname{Re}((\operatorname{curl}\widetilde E-i\omega\widetilde B)e^{-i\omega t})=0$ for all $(x,t)$ forces $\operatorname{curl}\widetilde E=i\omega\widetilde B$; the other three equations are analogous. All statements are pointwise algebraic identities; no PDE existence, no boundary condition and no choice principle is used.

### post-em-scalar-aperture-model

Assume Countable Choice ($\mathrm{AC}_\omega$) as carried by [[lem-em-outgoing-source-helmholtz-and-diffraction]] and [[lem-em-helmholtz-boundary-representation]] ([[def-countable-choice]]); the smooth modal full-Choice branch of the latter is not used. Adopt as a model assumption of the framework the following scalar aperture-diffraction model. [Data] Fix an angular frequency $\omega>0$ and wavenumber $k:=\omega/c$, with $c$ the vacuum speed of [[def-em-si-conventions]]; let $S\subset\{y\in\mathbb R^3:y_3=0\}\cap\{y:|y|\le a\}$ be a nonempty compact subset of a declared aperture plane $y_3=0$ with aperture radius $a>0$ (the screening plane and spatial region are read in the laboratory chart of [[def-em-lab-geometry]]), and let $\alpha$ be a finite complex measure on $\mathbb R^3$ supported in $S$ (the **aperture distribution**), with $\alpha$ read in volts so that the model field below carries the unit $\mathrm V\,\mathrm m^{-1}$ of an electric-field component and its pairings are those of [[def-integration-against-a-signed-or-complex-measure]]. [Model field] The model assigns to every observation point $x$ with $x_3>0$ the complex scalar amplitude $$u(x):=\int G_k(x-y)\,d\alpha(y),$$ where $G_k(z)=e^{ik|z|}/(4\pi|z|)$ for $z\ne0$ is the outgoing Helmholtz kernel of [[lem-em-outgoing-source-helmholtz-and-diffraction]], together with the real scalar field $U(x,t):=\operatorname{Re}(u(x)e^{-i\omega t})$ and the model period-averaged squared field $(1/2)|u(x)|^2$. All model predictions are declared comparisons of this squared field between observation points; no absolute radiometric power or intensity calibration is adopted. [Adopted regime and status] The model is the declared polarization-insensitive scalar approximation of aperture diffraction. It ignores the vector nature and polarization of the electromagnetic field, takes the aperture distribution rather than the exact screen traces as its datum, assigns no back-reaction of the diffracted field on the illumination and no screen interaction beyond the aperture, and is not derived from either branch of [[post-em-vacuum-maxwell]] or from the material-closure branches of [[post-em-material-closure]]; it is a declared optical approximation, not an exact electromagnetic model. It does not assert that $u$ or $U$ satisfies the Maxwell equations exactly, and it does not identify $\alpha$ with the unknown exact Maxwell boundary traces: assigning both $u$ and $\partial_nu$ on the aperture independently is not a well-posed exact boundary problem, as recorded in [[lem-em-helmholtz-boundary-representation]], and the error terms of [[lem-em-outgoing-source-helmholtz-and-diffraction]] are the controlled content of the model. No measured intensity, apparatus, sampling model, uncertainty budget, empirical comparison or quantitative radiometric calibration is adopted.

This is an explicit adopted-assumption contract; it is deliberately not a derivation. (i) The mathematical object it adopts is the outgoing source field $u=G_k*\alpha$ of [[lem-em-outgoing-source-helmholtz-and-diffraction]], whose kernel and outgoing/Fraunhofer technique are fixed there. This model permits the compact closed-radius support $|y|\le a$, including aperture corners. Its integral and off-support regularity fall under that supplier by choosing any containing open ball of radius $A>a$; the sharp-radius estimates at $r\ge2a$ are proved directly in the consuming diffraction theorem, so this extension does not evaluate an open-support hypothesis at a boundary point; the integral against the finite complex measure is that of [[def-integration-against-a-signed-or-complex-measure]]. (ii) The physical reading uses the aperture plane and spatial region of [[def-em-lab-geometry]], the wavenumber-frequency relation $k=\omega/c$ and the units of [[def-em-si-conventions]]. (iii) The non-derivation clause is essential: neither the smooth nor the weak branch of [[post-em-vacuum-maxwell]] implies this scalar source model, and the finite-conductivity/material branches of [[post-em-material-closure]] contain no ideal aperture or screen model; the trace caveat is the one recorded in [[lem-em-helmholtz-boundary-representation]]. (iv) The scope sentences exclude exact Maxwell, vector, polarization, back-reaction, absolute-radiometric and empirical content. The item uses no choice principle beyond those carried by the cited mathematical suppliers (the kernel lemma carries Countable Choice, as stated there). The Countable Choice hypothesis is inherited verbatim from the cited kernel and boundary-representation suppliers; this postulate adds no choice use of its own.

### pthm-em-scalar-aperture-diffraction

Assume Countable Choice ($\mathrm{AC}_\omega$) as carried by [[lem-em-outgoing-source-helmholtz-and-diffraction]] and [[lem-em-helmholtz-boundary-representation]] ([[def-countable-choice]]), whose trace qualification is used in clause (d); the full-Choice modal branch of the latter is not used. Adopt the scalar aperture model and its notation from [[post-em-scalar-aperture-model]]: frequency $\omega>0$, $k=\omega/c$, aperture radius $a>0$, finite complex aperture distribution $\alpha$ supported in the planar aperture $S\subset\{y_3=0\}\cap\{y:|y|\le a\}$, model field $u=G_k*\alpha$, real field $U(x,t)=\operatorname{Re}(u(x)e^{-i\omega t})$ and $T=2\pi/\omega$ ([[def-em-si-conventions]], [[def-em-lab-geometry]], [[def-complex-exponential]], [[def-complex-conjugate-real-imaginary-part-and-modulus]], [[def-integration-against-a-signed-or-complex-measure]]). Set $I(x):=(1/2)|u(x)|^2$. Then: (a) [Model field and Fraunhofer estimate] $u$ is $C^\infty$ on the half-space $\{x_3>0\}$ and on $\mathbb R^3\setminus S$, satisfies $(\Delta+k^2)u=0$ there, and is outgoing in the sense of [[lem-em-outgoing-source-helmholtz-and-diffraction]]: for $x=rn$ with $r\ge2a$ and $n\in S^2$, $$u(rn)=\frac{e^{ikr}}{4\pi r}\int e^{-ikn\cdot y}\,d\alpha(y)+O\Bigl(\frac{\|\alpha\|_{\rm TV}}{r}\Bigl[\frac ar+\frac{ka^2}{r}\Bigr]\Bigr)$$ uniformly in direction, and $r(\partial_ru-iku)=O(r^{-1})$ uniformly. (b) [Fresnel estimate] write $y_t=(y_1,y_2)$ and $\xi=(\xi_1,\xi_2)$ for transverse vectors; for the planar aperture and observation $x=(\xi_1,\xi_2,z)$ with $z>0$, $b\ge0$, $|\xi|\le b$ and any declared fixed small-angle range $(a+b)/z\le\eta_0<1$, where $0<\eta_0<1$, the Fresnel approximation replaces $R=|x-y|$ by $z+|\xi-y_t|^2/(2z)$ and $1/R$ by $1/z$ in the model integral with phase error $O(k(a+b)^4/z^3)$, relative amplitude error $O((a+b)^2/z^2)$ and absolute field error $O(\|\alpha\|_{\rm TV}z^{-1}[(a+b)^2/z^2+k(a+b)^4/z^3])$. (c) [Intensity and error transfer] at every $x$ with $x_3>0$ the model period average satisfies $\frac1T\int_0^TU(x,s)^2\,ds=I(x)$ ([[lem-em-period-average]]). If $u_{\rm approx}$ is any approximation of $u$ with $|u_{\rm approx}(x)-u(x)|\le\delta(x)$, then $|\frac12|u_{\rm approx}(x)|^2-I(x)|\le\frac12\delta(x)(|u(x)|+|u_{\rm approx}(x)|)$; if moreover $u(x)\ne0$ and $|u_{\rm approx}(x)-u(x)|\le\epsilon(x)|u(x)|$, the relative intensity error is at most $2\epsilon(x)+\epsilon(x)^2$. In particular the Fraunhofer and Fresnel field errors of (a) and (b) bound the corresponding intensity errors; no new approximation is introduced by passing to $I$. (d) [Trace and scope] the aperture distribution $\alpha$ is an adopted model datum and is not identified with the exact Maxwell boundary data on the screen; assigning both $u$ and $\partial_nu$ on the aperture independently does not produce a well-posed exact boundary problem ([[lem-em-helmholtz-boundary-representation]]). The construction ignores polarization and aperture back-reaction, makes no claim that $u$ or $U$ satisfies the Maxwell equations exactly, and makes no measured-intensity, apparatus, sampling or empirical claim; only comparisons of $I$ between observation points are read as model predictions.

The theorem instantiates the mathematical field estimates of [[lem-em-outgoing-source-helmholtz-and-diffraction]] at the adopted model datum of [[post-em-scalar-aperture-model]]. (a) The model field is the supplier's convolution. Choose any open containing ball of radius $A>a$ to obtain its off-support smoothness and Helmholtz equation. For the advertised sharp-radius endpoint, argue directly for every $|y|\le a$ and $r\ge2a$. Write $R=|rn-y|$, so $R\ge r-a\ge r/2$. For $h(s)=|rn-sy|$, the exact norm derivative formula from the supplier gives $|h''(s)|\le2a^2/(r-a)\le4a^2/r$; twice FTC therefore gives $|R-r+n\cdot y|\le2a^2/r$, and $|r/R-1|\le2a/r$. The supplier's real phase bound $|e^{i\theta}-1|\le|\theta|$ now gives $$\left|G_k(rn-y)-\frac{e^{ikr}e^{-ikn\cdot y}}{4\pi r}\right|\le\frac1{2\pi r}\left(\frac ar+\frac{ka^2}r\right).$$ For a bounded continuous kernel $f$ on the compact support, $|\int f\,d\alpha|\le\sup|f|\,\|\alpha\|_{\rm TV}$ follows by [[prop-simple-integrals-are-bounded-by-total-variation]] and uniform finite-simple approximation in [[def-integration-against-a-signed-or-complex-measure]]; hence this pointwise bound proves the original field error, including $r=2a$. The exact derivative $\partial_rR=(r-n\cdot y)/R$ has modulus at most1 and $|\partial_rR-1|\le2a^2/r^2$. Differentiating the explicit kernel gives $r|\partial_rG_k-ikG_k|\le(\pi r)^{-1}(1+ka^2/r)$; the same finite-measure bound gives the uniform outgoing residual. All derivative passages under the source integral use the supplier's compact-product uniform difference-quotient argument, not an unsupported complex-measure dominated-convergence claim. (b) On the explicitly declared range put $d_0=a+b$ and $s=|\xi-y_t|^2/z^2\le\eta_0^2<1$. The actual supplier proof gives $|\sqrt{1+s}-1-s/2|\le s^2/8$ and $|(1+s)^{-1/2}-1|\le s/2$ by twice FTC. Consequently the phase error is at most $kd_0^4/(8z^3)$ and the relative amplitude error at most $d_0^2/(2z^2)$. The complex-measure kernel bound above gives $$|u-u_{\rm Fresnel}|\le\frac{\|\alpha\|_{\rm TV}}{4\pi z}\left[\frac{d_0^2}{2z^2}+\frac{kd_0^4}{8z^3}\right],$$ which preserves and quantifies all original Fresnel estimates for the closed-support datum. (c) applies [[lem-em-period-average]] with $a=d=u(x)$: $\operatorname{Re}(u(x)e^{-i\omega t})^2$ averages to $\frac12\operatorname{Re}(u(x)\overline{u(x)})=\frac12|u(x)|^2$. The first error bound is the identity $\frac12\bigl||u_{\rm approx}|^2-|u|^2\bigr|=\frac12\bigl||u_{\rm approx}|-|u|\bigr|(|u_{\rm approx}|+|u|)$ together with $\bigl||u_{\rm approx}|-|u|\bigr|\le|u_{\rm approx}-u|$, obtained by applying the triangle inequality of [[lem-complex-conjugation-and-modulus-laws]] in both directions; the relative bound is $||u_{\rm approx}|^2-|u|^2|/|u|^2\le\epsilon(2+\epsilon)$. No choice principle beyond that carried by the suppliers is used. (d) reproduces the trace distinction of [[lem-em-helmholtz-boundary-representation]] and the model-scope declarations of the postulate; the intensity reading is the declared one, so no radiometric claim is added. The Countable Choice hypothesis is inherited from the cited suppliers; the intensity computation of (c) uses only the choice-free period-average lemma.

### pthm-em-pec-monochromatic-exterior-scattering

Assume the Axiom of Choice as carried by [[lem-em-exterior-vector-helmholtz-mixed-boundary-existence]], whose variational, Sobolev-compactness and compact-spectral steps use it; the algebraic computations below use no further choice. Let $O\subset\mathbb R^3$ be a bounded open set with $C^\infty$ boundary, $D:=\mathbb R^3\setminus\overline O$ connected, $k>0$, $\omega:=ck$ with $c$ the vacuum speed of [[def-em-si-conventions]], and $R>0$ with $\overline O\subset B_R(0)$; let $V\subseteq\mathbb R^3$ be open with $\overline D\subseteq V$, and let $(\widetilde E_{\rm inc},\widetilde B_{\rm inc})$ be a $C^\infty$ frequency-$\omega$ source-free vacuum phasor pair on $V$ ([[def-em-monochromatic-source-free-phasors]], [[def-em-lab-geometry]], [[def-em-fields]], [[def-em-si-conventions]]). Plane-wave instances are provided by [[pthm-em-plane-wave-maxwell]]: if $\kappa\cdot e=0$, $b=\kappa\times e/\omega$ and $|\kappa|=\omega/c$, then $(\widetilde E_{\rm inc},\widetilde B_{\rm inc})(x)=(e\,e^{i\kappa\cdot x},b\,e^{i\kappa\cdot x})$ satisfies the four phasor equations on $\mathbb R^3$ ([[def-em-plane-wave-definition]]). Adopt the ideal perfectly conducting (PEC) boundary model: the tangential component of the total electric field vanishes on $\partial O$. This is a declared idealization of the stationary interface conditions of [[pthm-em-material-stationary-jumps]] with vanishing fields inside the conductor; it is not one of the finite-conductivity branches of [[post-em-material-closure]], and the induced surface charge and current are neither prescribed nor determined here. Let $e_T$ be the tangential projection of $-\widetilde E_{\rm inc}$ on $\partial O$, and let $\widetilde E_{\rm scat}$ be the unique $C^\infty$ field on $\overline D$ of [[lem-em-exterior-vector-helmholtz-mixed-boundary-existence]] with $(\Delta+k^2)\widetilde E_{\rm scat}=0$ and $\operatorname{div}\widetilde E_{\rm scat}=0$ on $D$ ([[def-laplacian-of-a-c2-function]], [[def-divergence-and-curl-of-a-c1-vector-field]]), tangential trace $e_T$ on $\partial O$ and that lemma's outgoing modal condition on $S_R$. Set $\widetilde B_{\rm scat}:=(i\omega)^{-1}\operatorname{curl}\widetilde E_{\rm scat}$ and, on $D$, $\widetilde E_{\rm tot}:=\widetilde E_{\rm inc}+\widetilde E_{\rm scat}$, $\widetilde B_{\rm tot}:=\widetilde B_{\rm inc}+\widetilde B_{\rm scat}$. Then: (a) [Phasor Maxwell] $(\widetilde E_{\rm scat},\widetilde B_{\rm scat})$ and $(\widetilde E_{\rm tot},\widetilde B_{\rm tot})$ are $C^\infty$ frequency-$\omega$ source-free vacuum phasor pairs on $D$; and for every $C^\infty$ frequency-$\omega$ source-free vacuum phasor pair $(\widetilde E,\widetilde B)$ on an open set $O'\subseteq\mathbb R^3$, the associated real fields of [[def-em-monochromatic-source-free-phasors]] satisfy on $O'\times\mathbb R$ the smooth-branch vacuum Maxwell equations of [[post-em-vacuum-maxwell]] with $\rho\equiv0$ and $J\equiv0$. Consequently the associated real total fields $E_{\rm tot},B_{\rm tot}$ satisfy those source-free equations on $D\times\mathbb R$. (b) [PEC boundary condition] the tangential trace of $\widetilde E_{\rm tot}$ on $\partial O$ vanishes; hence for every unit normal field $n$ of $\partial O$ the associated real total electric field $E_{\rm tot}(x,t)=\operatorname{Re}(\widetilde E_{\rm tot}(x)e^{-i\omega t})$ ([[def-complex-conjugate-real-imaginary-part-and-modulus]]) satisfies $n\times E_{\rm tot}=0$ on $\partial O\times\mathbb R$. (c) [Uniqueness] if $(\widetilde E',\widetilde B')$ is a $C^\infty$ frequency-$\omega$ source-free vacuum phasor pair on an open set $V'\supseteq\overline D$ that is outgoing on $S_R$ in the modal sense of the vector Helmholtz lemma and has tangential trace $e_T$ on $\partial O$, then $(\widetilde E',\widetilde B')=(\widetilde E_{\rm scat},\widetilde B_{\rm scat})$ on $D$; equivalently $(\widetilde E_{\rm tot},\widetilde B_{\rm tot})$ is the unique total source-free phasor pair in the corresponding $C^\infty$ closure class with that prescribed incident phasor, vanishing tangential electric trace, and outgoing scattered correction $\widetilde E_{\rm tot}-\widetilde E_{\rm inc}$ in the lemma's modal class. The total field itself is not required to be outgoing; an incident plane wave is generally not outgoing. (d) [Scope] one positive frequency $\omega=ck$, one smooth bounded stationary PEC obstacle with connected exterior, and $C^\infty$ incident and scattered classes only; no lossy, impedance, nonsmooth or dispersive obstacle, no determination of the induced surface charge or current, no radiation-zone amplitude or far-field asymptotics beyond the lemma's outgoing modal class, no point or singular source, no finite-energy or empirical claim.

Assume the Axiom of Choice exactly through [[lem-em-exterior-vector-helmholtz-mixed-boundary-existence]] (its symmetry/coercivity, compactness and compact-spectral steps); all other steps are pointwise algebra. (a) For the scattered pair: $\operatorname{div}\widetilde E_{\rm scat}=0$ is the boundary-problem condition; $\operatorname{curl}\widetilde E_{\rm scat}=i\omega\widetilde B_{\rm scat}$ holds by the definition of $\widetilde B_{\rm scat}$; $\operatorname{div}\widetilde B_{\rm scat}=(i\omega)^{-1}\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{curl}\widetilde E_{\rm scat})=0$ and $\operatorname{curl}\widetilde B_{\rm scat}=(i\omega)^{-1}\operatorname{curl}(\operatorname{curl}\widetilde E_{\rm scat})=(i\omega)^{-1}(\nabla\operatorname{div}\widetilde E_{\rm scat}-\Delta\widetilde E_{\rm scat})=(i\omega)^{-1}k^2\widetilde E_{\rm scat}=-i\omega c^{-2}\widetilde E_{\rm scat}$ by [[cor-the-curl-of-a-curl-of-a-c2-field]], [[thm-the-divergence-of-a-curl-vanishes]] and $(\Delta+k^2)\widetilde E_{\rm scat}=0$; the sum of two solutions of the linear system is a solution by [[lem-divergence-and-curl-are-linear-and-obey-the-scalar-product-rules]]. The real-field Maxwell statement is clause (c) of [[lem-em-phasor-real-part-calculus-and-equivalence]] with the constant $c$ of [[def-em-si-conventions]], combined with the identification of its real system with the zero-source smooth branch of [[post-em-vacuum-maxwell]]; the incident pair is a pair on $V$, hence on $D$. (b) The tangential traces satisfy $\widetilde E_{\rm tot,tang}=\widetilde E_{\rm inc,tang}+\widetilde E_{\rm scat,tang}=\widetilde E_{\rm inc,tang}+e_T=0$ on $\partial O$; the associated real field is the real part of the tangential trace, hence vanishes (the encoding and real-part calculus are those of [[def-em-monochromatic-source-free-phasors]] and [[lem-em-phasor-real-part-calculus-and-equivalence]]). (c) For a candidate phasor pair, $\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}\widetilde E'=i\omega\operatorname{curl}\widetilde B'=\omega^2c^{-2}\widetilde E'=k^2\widetilde E'$, and $\operatorname{div}\widetilde E'=0$. The componentwise real/imaginary curl-curl identity thus gives $(\Delta+k^2)\widetilde E'=0$. Its prescribed tangential trace and outgoing correction are the declared boundary class. Hence the candidate $\widetilde E'$ satisfies all hypotheses of the uniqueness assertion of [[lem-em-exterior-vector-helmholtz-mixed-boundary-existence]] for the same tangential data, so $\widetilde E'=\widetilde E_{\rm scat}$; then $\widetilde B'=(i\omega)^{-1}\operatorname{curl}\widetilde E'=(i\omega)^{-1}\operatorname{curl}\widetilde E_{\rm scat}=\widetilde B_{\rm scat}$ by the third phasor equation of [[def-em-monochromatic-source-free-phasors]]. For the total-field formulation subtract the fixed incident pair: linearity gives a source-free correction with tangential trace $e_T$, and the required outgoing condition is on that correction. Conversely adding the fixed incident pair recovers the total boundary condition. Thus uniqueness transfers exactly to total-minus-incident outgoing; it does not impose the outgoing class on an incident plane wave or on their total. (d) records the exact scope; in particular the ideal PEC condition is adopted, not derived, and the material-closure branches of [[post-em-material-closure]] contain no $\sigma\to\infty$ branch, while [[pthm-em-material-stationary-jumps]] supplies only the stationary-interface idealization context.

### ex-em-rectangular-aperture

Assume Countable Choice ($\mathrm{AC}_\omega$) as carried by [[pthm-em-scalar-aperture-diffraction]] and by the Riemann-Lebesgue comparison and Fubini suppliers used in the integral reduction below ([[def-countable-choice]]). Work in the scalar aperture model of [[post-em-scalar-aperture-model]] and [[pthm-em-scalar-aperture-diffraction]], with frequency $\omega>0$, $k=\omega/c$, aperture plane $y_3=0$, model field $u=G_k*\alpha$ and $I(x)=\frac12|u(x)|^2$. Fix $a>0$, $b>0$ and $A_0\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$; identify the rectangle $R:=[-a,a]\times[-b,b]\subset\mathbb R^2$ with the planar aperture $\{(y_1,y_2,0):(y_1,y_2)\in R\}$ and take the **uniform rectangular aperture distribution** let $\iota:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^3$ be $\iota(y_1,y_2)=(y_1,y_2,0)$, and define its plane-embedding pushforward $$\alpha(E):=A_0\,\lambda_2(R\cap\iota^{-1}(E))\qquad(E\subseteq\mathbb R^3\text{ Borel}).$$ This is a finite complex measure on $\mathbb R^3$, supported on the embedded compact rectangle $S=\iota(R)$ with $|y|\le a':=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$ and total variation $\|\alpha\|_{\rm TV}=4ab|A_0|$ ([[def-multidimensional-rectangle-and-volume]], [[def-measure-with-density]], [[def-integration-against-a-signed-or-complex-measure]]). Then: (a) [Fraunhofer amplitude] with $\operatorname{sinc}\xi:=\sin\xi/\xi$ for $\xi\ne0$ and $\operatorname{sinc}0:=1$, the Fraunhofer amplitude factor of [[pthm-em-scalar-aperture-diffraction]](a) is exactly $$\int e^{-ikn\cdot y}\,d\alpha(y)=4abA_0\operatorname{sinc}(ka\,n_1)\operatorname{sinc}(kb\,n_2)\qquad(n=(n_1,n_2,n_3)\in S^2),$$ an identity for every direction $n$ ([[def-euclidean-inner-product]], [[def-complex-exponential]], [[cor-complex-exponential-cartesian-form-modulus-and-eulers-identity]], [[thm-ftc-second-part]], [[thm-sine-and-cosine-derivatives]], [[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]], [[thm-riemann-fubini-on-product-rectangles]], [[thm-continuous-on-a-rectangle-is-riemann-integrable]], [[lem-riemann-lebesgue-comparison-for-distribution-test-integrands]], [[thm-linearity-of-the-lebesgue-integral-on-l-one]]). (a) The continuous embedding $\iota$ has Borel inverse images by [[thm-continuous-preimages-of-borel-sets-are-borel]]. For $f(y)=e^{-ik(n_1y_1+n_2y_2)}$ (the $y_3$-coordinate is zero on the aperture, and $n\cdot y=n_1y_1+n_2y_2$ by [[def-euclidean-inner-product]]), $f$ is continuous on the compact rectangle $R$ ([[thm-continuous-on-a-rectangle-is-riemann-integrable]]), so [[lem-riemann-lebesgue-comparison-for-distribution-test-integrands]] identifies the Lebesgue integral with the Riemann double integral; [[thm-riemann-fubini-on-product-rectangles]] factors it into the product of the two one-dimensional integrals.
