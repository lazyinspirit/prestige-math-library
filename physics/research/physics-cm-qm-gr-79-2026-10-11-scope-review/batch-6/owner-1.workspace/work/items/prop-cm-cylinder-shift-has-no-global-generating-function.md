## Statement

Let $\mathbb T:=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ be the one-dimensional torus with quotient map
$q:\mathbb R\to\mathbb T$ ([[def-the-one-dimensional-torus-and-normalized-haar-integral]]), let
$C:=\mathbb T\times\mathbb R$ be the cylinder, and identify $C$ with the quotient of $\mathbb R^2$
by the deck transformation $\tau(\theta,I)=(\theta+1,I)$, with quotient map
$\pi:\mathbb R^2\to C$ ([[def-quotient-topology]]); assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ where the cited
torus definition requires it. Since $d\theta\wedge dI$ and $I\,d\theta$ are invariant under $\tau$,
there are unique smooth forms $\omega_C,\lambda_C$ on $C$ with
$\pi^*\omega_C=d\theta\wedge dI$ and $\pi^*\lambda_C=I\,d\theta$; while $d\theta$ descends to a
smooth one-form $d\theta_C$ on $C$ with $\pi^*d\theta_C=d\theta$. Let $a\ne0$ and let
$\Phi:C\to C$ be the **symplectic shift** given in quotient coordinates by
$\Phi(\theta,I)=(\theta,I+a)$. Then:

1. $\Phi$ is a $C^\infty$ diffeomorphism, $\Phi^*\omega_C=\omega_C$, and
   $\Phi^*\lambda_C=\lambda_C+a\,d\theta_C$;
2. $a\,d\theta_C$ is closed but not exact: there is no $C^1$ function $W:C\to\mathbb R$ with
   $dW=a\,d\theta_C$;
3. for every point of $C$ there is a neighbourhood on which $\Phi$ is generated by the second-kind
   generating function $S(\theta,P)=\theta P-a\theta$, but there is no $C^1$ function
   $S:(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)\times\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ whose generated map is $\Phi$; the local
   generating functions do not glue to a global one.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The cylinder $C$ with the descended forms, the real number $a\ne0$ and the shift $\Phi$.

[F1] Pullback and descent ([[def-pullback-of-a-differential-form]], [[thm-the-exterior-derivative-commutes-with-pullback]]): for a smooth map of smooth manifolds, $\pi^*$ is a homomorphism of the graded algebra of forms, it commutes with $d$, and if $\pi$ is a local diffeomorphism then $\pi^*$ is injective, so an identity of forms on $C$ holds iff its $\pi$-pullback holds on $\mathbb R^2$.

[F2] On $\mathbb R^2$ one has $\tau^*(d\theta\wedge dI)=d\theta\wedge dI$ and $\tau^*(I\,d\theta)=(I+0)\,d\theta=I\,d\theta$, so the forms descend as asserted; moreover $\Phi\circ\pi=\pi\circ\tilde\Phi$ for the map $\tilde\Phi(\theta,I)=(\theta,I+a)$ on $\mathbb R^2$. [F1, def-the-one-dimensional-torus-and-normalized-haar-integral]

[F3] Fundamental theorem of calculus and chain rule ([[thm-newton-leibniz-with-interior-derivative]], [[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]]): if $g$ is continuous on $[a,b]$ with integrable interior derivative $g'$, then $g(b)-g(a)=\int_a^bg'$; in particular, if $g'$ is the constant $c$ on $(a,b)$, then $g(b)-g(a)=c(b-a)$.

[F4] The second-kind relations $Q=S_P$, $p=S_\theta$ ([[def-cm-generating-function-of-the-first-and-second-kind]]): for $S(\theta,P)=\theta P-a\theta$ one has $S_P=\theta$ and $S_\theta=P-a$ on $\mathbb R^2$, and $\det S_{\theta P}=1\ne0$; the generated local map sends $(\theta,I)$ with $I=P-a$ to $(\theta,I+a)$, i.e. it is the local description of $\Phi$, and it is canonical by [[prop-cm-generating-functions-yield-canonical-maps]]. [F1]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; compute the pullbacks of $\lambda_C$ and $\omega_C$ in the covering $\mathbb R^2$, and integrate the hypothetical primitive along the loop $\theta\mapsto(\theta,0)$.

1.1 Clause 1: by [F2] and [F1], $\pi^*(\Phi^*\omega_C)=(\Phi\circ\pi)^*\omega_C=(\pi\circ\tilde\Phi)^*\omega_C=\tilde\Phi^*(\pi^*\omega_C)=\tilde\Phi^*(d\theta\wedge dI)=d\theta\wedge dI=\pi^*\omega_C$, so injectivity of $\pi^*$ gives $\Phi^*\omega_C=\omega_C$; likewise $\pi^*(\Phi^*\lambda_C)=\tilde\Phi^*(I\,d\theta)=(I+a)\,d\theta=I\,d\theta+a\,d\theta=\pi^*(\lambda_C+a\,d\theta_C)$, which gives $\Phi^*\lambda_C=\lambda_C+a\,d\theta_C$. [F1, F2, algebra]

1.2 Clause 2, closedness: $d(a\,d\theta_C)=a\,d(d\theta_C)=0$ because $\pi^*(d(d\theta_C))=d(d\theta)=0$ and $\pi^*$ is injective; so $a\,d\theta_C$ is closed. [F1, F2]

2.1 Clause 2, non-exactness: suppose $W:C\to\mathbb R$ is $C^1$ with $dW=a\,d\theta_C$, and set $g(t):=W(\pi(t,0))$ for $t\in[0,1]$. By the chain rule and $d\theta_C(\partial_\theta)=1$ at the image point, $g$ is $C^1$ with $g'(t)=a$ for $0<t<1$; by [F3], $g(1)-g(0)=a$. But $\pi(1,0)=\pi(0,0)$ in $C$, so $g(1)=g(0)$; this contradicts $a\ne0$. Hence no such $W$ exists, and $\Phi^*\lambda_C-\lambda_C=a\,d\theta_C$ is a closed form representing a nonzero class. [F3, step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Clause 3, global failure: suppose a $C^1$ function $S$ exists on $(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)\times\mathbb R$ whose generated second-kind map is $\Phi$, that is, with $p=S_\theta=I=P-a$ and $Q=S_P$ equal to the circle coordinate $\theta$ at every point, the former because $\Phi$ carries $(\theta,I)$ to $(\theta,I+a)$, so the new momentum is $P=I+a$ and the old momentum is $I=P-a$. Lift $S$ to a $1$-periodic $C^1$ function $\hat S$ on $\mathbb R^2$. The relation $Q=\theta$ reads $\hat S_P(\theta,P)-\theta\in\mathbb Z$; since this difference is continuous and integer-valued on $\mathbb R^2$, it is constant: if it took two distinct values, the intermediate value theorem applied along the segment joining the corresponding points would produce a non-integer value. Write the constant as $m\in\mathbb Z$, so $\hat S_P(\theta,P)=\theta+m$; the fundamental theorem of calculus in $P$ gives $\hat S(\theta,P)=\theta P+mP+c(\theta)$ with $c(\theta):=\hat S(\theta,0)$. Differentiating in $\theta$ gives $\hat S_\theta(\theta,P)=P+c'(\theta)=P-a$, so $c'(\theta)=-a$ and, by [F3] in the variable $\theta$, $c(1)-c(0)=-a\ne0$, contradicting the $1$-periodicity $c(1)=c(0)$ of the lift. Hence no global $S$ exists, while [F4] exhibits the local generating function $S(\theta,P)=\theta P-a\theta$ on every chart where $\theta$ is a single-valued coordinate. [F3, F4, step 2.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

- The obstruction is global: locally, on any chart of the circle, $\theta P-a\theta$ is a perfectly good generating function, and $a\,d\theta=d(-a\theta+\text{const})$ on that chart. What fails globally is single-valuedness of the primitive around the circle, i.e. the class of $\Phi^*\lambda_C-\lambda_C$ in $H^1(C)$.
- The same computation shows that the shift is symplectic but not "exact"; the published [[def-canonical-transformation]] and [[def-symplectomorphism-local-symplectomorphism-and-symplectic-embedding]] distinguish canonical maps from those admitting global generating functions, and this item is the explicit example of the distinction.
