## Example

Declare the one-label model of the force branch
[[post-cm02-force-superposition]] with $N=1$, mass $m>0$, time window
$\mathbb R$, configuration domain $Q=\mathbb R^{3}$, zero potential $U\equiv0$
and no residual forces (the **free particle instance**,
[[def-cm01-configuration]], [[def-cm03-potential-and-mechanical-energy]],
[[def-cm07-action-and-mechanical-lagrangian]]). By
[[pthm-cm12-mechanical-hyperregularity]] the branch Hamiltonian is
$H(x,p)=\lvert p\rvert^{2}/(2m)$ with Hamiltonian system
$\dot x=m^{-1}p$, $\dot p=0$
([[def-cm12-hamiltonian]], [[pthm-cm13-hamiltonian-field-integrates-the-model-equations]]).
Then:

1. for every initial datum the maximal solution is

$$\Phi_{t}(x,p)=\bigl(x+(t/m)\,p,\ p\bigr)\qquad(t\in\mathbb R),$$

   the free-particle flow of the presentation;
2. for $t\neq0$ the function
   $S_{t}(q,Q):=-\frac{m}{2t}\lvert Q-q\rvert^{2}$ is of class $C^{\infty}$
   on $\mathbb R^{3}\times\mathbb R^{3}$ with
   $S_{t,qQ}=(m/t)I_{3}$ invertible everywhere, and the first-kind map it
   generates is exactly $\Phi_{t}$: indeed
   $p=S_{t,q}=m(Q-q)/t$ gives $Q=q+(t/m)p$, and
   $P=-S_{t,Q}=m(Q-q)/t=p$; consequently $\Phi_{t}$ is a canonical map of
   the presentation with $\lvert\det D\Phi_{t}\rvert=1$
   ([[pthm-cm13-generating-functions]] clauses 1,
   [[def-cm-generating-function-of-the-first-and-second-kind]],
   [[prop-cm-generating-functions-yield-canonical-maps]]);
3. projection check: the map
   $(q,p)\mapsto(q,Q)=(q,q+(t/m)p)$ has invertible derivative
   $\begin{pmatrix}I_{3}&0\\ I_{3}&(t/m)I_{3}\end{pmatrix}$ for $t\neq0$,
   while the family degenerates at $t=0$: $S_{t}$ has no value there and the
   projection becomes singular, so this family does not represent the
   identity map;
4. the canonical one-form changes by an exact form along the flow:
   $\Phi_{t}^{*}\lambda=\lambda+t\,dK$ with $K=\lvert p\rvert^{2}/(2m)$,
   because $\Phi_{t}^{*}\lambda=P\cdot dQ=p\cdot\bigl(dq+(t/m)dp\bigr)=\lambda+(t/m)\,d\bigl(\lvert p\rvert^{2}/2\bigr)$;
   in particular $\Phi_{t}^{*}d\lambda=d\lambda$, recovering canonicity
   directly ([[def-cm13-two-form]],
   [[prop-cm-canonical-map-tests-and-hamiltonian-transport]]).

All quantities are model values of the declared idealization; no measurement
is reported.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The one-label free-particle instance with its Hamiltonian, flow
and two-form, the generating datum $S_{t}$, and the canonical one-form
$\lambda=\sum_{a}p_{a}dq^{a}$ of the presentation.

[F1] Model and Hamiltonian ([[pthm-cm12-mechanical-hyperregularity]], [[def-cm12-hamiltonian]], [[def-cm03-potential-and-mechanical-energy]], [[post-cm02-force-superposition]], [[def-cm01-configuration]], [[def-cm01-units]], [[def-cm02-newtonian-flow]]): $H=\lvert p\rvert^{2}/(2m)$ on $\mathbb R^{6}$ with Hamilton system $\dot x=m^{-1}p$, $\dot p=0$, whose solutions are $x(t)=x_{0}+(t/m)p_{0}$, $p(t)=p_{0}$.

[F2] Generating functions ([[pthm-cm13-generating-functions]], [[def-cm-generating-function-of-the-first-and-second-kind]], [[prop-cm-generating-functions-yield-canonical-maps]]): for $C^{3}$ first-kind data with $\det S_{qQ}\neq0$ the map defined by $p=S_{q}(q,Q)$, $P=-S_{Q}$ is a canonical $C^{2}$ local diffeomorphism with unit determinant, and $p\cdot dq-P\cdot dQ=dS$.

[F3] Forms and calculus ([[def-cm13-two-form]], [[pthm-cm13-two-form-is-symplectic]], [[def-cm13-canonical-map]], [[prop-cm-canonical-map-tests-and-hamiltonian-transport]], [[def-pullback-of-a-differential-form]], [[def-smooth-differential-k-form]], [[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]], [[def-ck-euclidean-maps-and-diffeomorphisms]], [[def-jacobian-matrix-and-gradient]], [[def-directional-and-partial-derivatives]], [[def-total-derivative-in-euclidean-space]], [[def-matrix-product-and-identity-matrix]], [[def-transpose-of-a-matrix]], [[def-linear-map]], [[def-invertible-matrix-and-general-linear-group]], [[cor-square-matrix-invertible-iff-determinant-is-a-unit]], [[def-determinant-of-a-square-matrix]], [[def-euclidean-inner-product]]): the canonical one-form of the presentation is $\lambda=\sum_{a}p_{a}dq^{a}$ and pulls back as displayed, the canonical two-form is in canonical coordinates, and the algebraic and differential computations of the example use the ordinary product, chain and mixed-partial rules; a block matrix with invertible diagonal blocks is invertible.

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct. Verify the explicit solution, compute the mixed Hessian and the generated map of $S_{t}$, check the projection matrix, and pull back the canonical one-form.

1.1 The displayed curve has $\dot x=m^{-1}p$ and $\dot p=0$, hence solves the Hamiltonian system for all $t$, so by the solutions clause of [F1] it is the free-particle flow; this is claim 1. [F1, algebra]

1.2 Mixed Hessian and generated map: $S_{t,q}(q,Q)=-\frac{m}{2t}\cdot2(Q-q)\cdot(-1)=\frac{m}{t}(Q-q)$, so $S_{t,qQ}=\frac{m}{t}I_{3}$ is invertible for $t\neq0$; the first-kind relations of [F2] read $p=S_{t,q}=\frac{m}{t}(Q-q)$ and $P=-S_{t,Q}=\frac{m}{t}(Q-q)=p$, hence $Q=q+\frac{t}{m}p$ and $P=p$: the generated map is $\Phi_{t}$; by [F2] it is canonical with unit determinant, which is claim 2. [F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 Projection and degeneration: the derivative of $(q,p)\mapsto(q,Q)$ is the displayed block matrix with invertible diagonal blocks $I_{3}$ and $(t/m)I_{3}$ for $t\neq0$, so the projection is invertible there; at $t=0$ the function $S_{t}$ is undefined and the lower-right block vanishes, so the projection is singular, and no member of the family represents the identity map; this is claim 3. [step 1.2, F3, algebra]

2.2 Pullback of the one-form: $\Phi_{t}^{*}\lambda=P\cdot dQ$ with $Q=q+(t/m)p$ and $P=p$, so $\Phi_{t}^{*}\lambda=p\cdot(dq+(t/m)dp)=\lambda+(t/m)\,d\bigl(\lvert p\rvert^{2}/2\bigr)=\lambda+t\,dK$ by the chain rule and the symmetry of the Euclidean product [F3]; differentiating gives $\Phi_{t}^{*}d\lambda=d\lambda$, and the matrix identity of claim 2 was already computed; this is claim 4. [step 1.2, F2, F3, algebra]

3.1 Collecting steps 1.1, 1.2, 2.1 and 2.2 proves the four claims on the declared data. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 2.2] ∎
