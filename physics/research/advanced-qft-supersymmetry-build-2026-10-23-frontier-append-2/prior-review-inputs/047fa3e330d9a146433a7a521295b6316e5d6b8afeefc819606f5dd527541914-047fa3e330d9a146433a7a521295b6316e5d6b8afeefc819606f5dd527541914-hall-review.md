## Mathematical verification

1. **HN and descent.** Every nonzero representation has finitely many possible subobject dimension vectors, even over an infinite extension field. Maximize attained phase and then total dimension. The resulting subobject is semistable. A quotient subobject of greater phase contradicts the first maximum; one of equal phase contradicts the second. The strict upper-half-plane sum rule supplies both contradictions. Induction on total dimension constructs the decreasing HN filtration. For semistable A,B of phases p>p', a nonzero map would have image of phase at least p as a quotient of A and at most p' as a subobject of B, impossible. Applying this vanishing successively to quotient filtrations identifies the first terms of any two HN filtrations, then all terms by induction. This proves uniqueness without assuming generic stability.
