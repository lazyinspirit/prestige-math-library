## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice ([[def-countable-choice]]). Use the notation of
[[thm-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-strongly-continuous-rotation-action]]: $S^2$, the polar measure $\sigma$ with
$\sigma(S^2)=4\pi$, the Hilbert space $L^2(S^2,\sigma)$, the complete orthonormal family $(Y_{lm})$, the blocks
$V_l$, the finite span $H$, the spaces $H_l^{\mathrm{pol}}$ of complex harmonic homogeneous polynomials of degree
$l$, the restriction maps $\mathrm{res}_l$, and the unitary action $U(R)F=\text{class of }\tilde F(R^{-1}\cdot)$
of $SO(3)$. Fix $\hbar>0$ and for $i=1,2,3$ define the rotation matrices
$$R_1(t):=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&\cos t&-\sin t\\0&\sin t&\cos t\end{pmatrix},\quad R_2(t):=\begin{pmatrix}\cos t&0&\sin t\\0&1&0\\-\sin t&0&\cos t\end{pmatrix},\quad R_3(t):=\begin{pmatrix}\cos t&-\sin t&0\\ \sin t&\cos t&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},$$
put $U_i(t):=U(R_i(t))$ and let $L_i$ be $-\hbar$ times the self-adjoint generator $T_i$ of the strongly
continuous one-parameter unitary group $U_i$ ([[def-infinitesimal-generator-of-a-unitary-group]],
[[thm-stone-one-parameter-unitary-groups]]), so that $U_i(t)=e^{-itL_i/\hbar}$. Let $L_z$ denote the diagonal
operator of [[thm-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-sphere-laplacian-diagonal-domain]], and for a polynomial $p$ put
$$D_ip:=(x\times\nabla p)_i=\sum_{j,k=1}^3\varepsilon_{ijk}\,x_j\,\partial_kp,$$
where $\varepsilon_{ijk}$ is the sign of the permutation $(1,2,3)\mapsto(i,j,k)$ and is $0$ when two indices
coincide. Then:

(a) each $L_i$ is a self-adjoint operator on $L^2(S^2,\sigma)$ with $U(R_i(t))=e^{-itL_i/\hbar}$ for all real
$t$; the finite span $H$ is contained in $D(L_i)$ and is a core of $L_i$
([[def-densely-defined-closed-and-closable-operator]], [[def-symmetric-self-adjoint-and-essentially-self-adjoint]]);
and for $F\in V_l$ with polynomial representative $p_F\in H_l^{\mathrm{pol}}$ one has
$$L_iF=\mathrm{res}_l(-i\hbar\,D_ip_F),$$
that is, $(L_iF)(\Omega)=-i\hbar\,D_ip_F(\Omega)$ for $\sigma$-almost every $\Omega\in S^2$; in particular
$L_iY_{lm}\in V_l$ for all $l,m$ and $L_iH\subseteq H$;

(b) on $H$ the operators satisfy the commutation relations
$$L_iL_j-L_jL_i=i\hbar\sum_{k=1}^3\varepsilon_{ijk}L_k;$$

(c) $L_3=L_z$ as self-adjoint operators (equal domains and equal action); in particular $L_3Y_{lm}=\hbar mY_{lm}$, so that
$L_3$ is diagonal in the basis $(Y_{lm})$, and for every $F\in H$ and chart point $(\theta,\varphi)$ one has $(L_3F)(\theta,\varphi)=-i\hbar\,\partial_\varphi F(\theta,\varphi)$;

(d) rotational conjugation identifies the domains: with the two quarter-turn rotations
$S:=R_2(-\pi/2)$ and $W:=R_1(\pi/2)$ one has
$$U(S)L_1U(S)^{-1}=L_3,\qquad U(W)L_2U(W)^{-1}=L_3,$$
as self-adjoint operators, and consequently $U(S)D(L_1)=D(L_3)$ and $U(W)D(L_2)=D(L_3)$: the three domains
are unitarily identified.

All assertions are mathematical statements about operators on $L^2(S^2,\sigma)$; no physical interpretation of
the generators as angular momentum is asserted, and no measurement postulate is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the data of the rotation action ([[thm-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-strongly-continuous-rotation-action]]), the parameter $\hbar>0$, the matrices $R_i(t)$, the groups $U_i(t)$, the operators $L_i$, the diagonal operator $L_z$, and the first-order operators $D_i$.

[F1] The rotation action theorem ([[thm-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-strongly-continuous-rotation-action]]): $U(R)$ is unitary with $U(RS)=U(R)U(S)$ and $U(R)^{-1}=U(R^{-1})$; $\mathrm{res}_l(\rho(R)p)=U(R)\mathrm{res}_l(p)$ for $p\in H_l^{\mathrm{pol}}$ with $\rho(R)p=p\circ R^{-1}$; $U(R)V_l=V_l$ and $U(R)H=H$; and the orbit maps $R\mapsto U(R)F$ are continuous in the norm of $L^2(S^2,\sigma)$.

[F2] Stone's theorem ([[thm-stone-one-parameter-unitary-groups]], [[def-infinitesimal-generator-of-a-unitary-group]], [[def-strongly-continuous-one-parameter-unitary-group]]): the map $T\mapsto e^{itT}$ is a bijection from self-adjoint operators onto strongly continuous one-parameter unitary groups; for a group $V(t)=e^{itT}$ the domain $D(T)$ is exactly the set of $F$ at which $t\mapsto V(t)F$ is differentiable at $0$, with derivative $iTF$; and if $V(t)=e^{itT}$ then $W(t)=e^{itT'}$ with $T'=WTW^{-1}$ is the group $W(t)=WV(t)W^{-1}$ whenever $W$ is unitary, its domain being $WD(T)$: indeed $F\in D(T')$ iff $W^{-1}F\in D(T)$ iff $t\mapsto WV(t)W^{-1}F$ is differentiable at $0$. A self-adjoint operator generates a strongly continuous unitary group ([[lem-self-adjoint-operator-generates-a-strongly-continuous-unitary-group]]) and the generator of a strongly continuous unitary group is skew-adjoint, its negative imaginary part being self-adjoint ([[lem-generator-of-a-unitary-group-is-skew-adjoint]]).

[F3] The matrices: for each $i$ and all real $s,t$, the matrix $R_i(t)$ lies in $SO(3)$, $R_i(0)=I_3$, $R_i(s)R_i(t)=R_i(s+t)$ and $R_i(t)^{-1}=R_i(t)^{\mathsf T}=R_i(-t)$; $t\mapsto R_i(t)$ is continuous; and $R_i'(0)=[e_i]_\times$, where $[\omega]_\times z=\omega\times z$ for all $z$ and $[\omega]_\times$ is the skew matrix of [[lem-cm-skew-matrix-cross-product-correspondence]]. These follow by direct $3\times3$ matrix computation from the addition formulas and the values $\sin'=\cos$, $\cos'=-\sin$, $\sin^2+\cos^2=1$, $\cos0=1$, $\sin0=0$, and the determinant of a product ([[thm-sine-and-cosine-addition-formulas]], [[thm-sine-and-cosine-derivatives]], [[cor-trigonometric-parity-and-pythagorean-identity]], [[thm-determinant-multiplicative]], [[def-determinant-of-a-square-matrix]], [[lem-cm-skew-matrix-cross-product-correspondence]], [[def-cross-product-in-r3]], [[thm-matrix-multiplication-laws]], [[def-transpose-of-a-matrix]], [[def-matrix-product-and-identity-matrix]], [[def-cm-special-orthogonal-group]]).

[F4] Triple products: $(u\times v)\cdot w=u\cdot(v\times w)$ for all $u,v,w\in\mathbb R^3$ (both sides equal the determinant of the matrix with rows $u,v,w$, by the expansion of $u\times(v\times w)=\langle u,w\rangle v-\langle u,v\rangle w$ against $u$; [[lem-cm-cross-product-triple-identity]], [[def-cross-product-in-r3]], [[def-matrix-product-and-identity-matrix]]).

[F5] Differential calculus for the computation of the generators: for the polynomial $p$ and the curve $s\mapsto R_i(s)^{-1}\Omega$ one has $\frac{d}{ds}p(R_i(s)^{-1}\Omega)=\nabla p(R_i(s)^{-1}\Omega)\cdot(-R_i'(s)^{\mathsf T}R_i(s)\cdot R_i(s)^{-1}\Omega)$ and, by the fundamental theorem, $p(R_i(t)^{-1}\Omega)-p(\Omega)=\int_0^t\frac{d}{ds}p(R_i(s)^{-1}\Omega)\,ds$; compositions and products of these $C^\infty$ maps are continuous, and on the compact set $[-\delta,\delta]\times S^2$ uniform continuity controls the convergence as $t\to0$ ([[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]], [[thm-total-derivative-computes-directional-and-partial-derivatives]], [[thm-ftc-second-part]], [[def-ck-euclidean-maps-and-diffeomorphisms]], [[thm-ck-euclidean-maps-closed-under-algebra-and-composition]], [[cor-compact-domain-maps-are-uniformly-continuous]], [[def-directional-and-partial-derivatives]]).

[F6] Truncations and blocks: $H$ is dense in $L^2(S^2,\sigma)$; the orthogonal projection $P_l$ onto $V_l$ is the sum $\sum_m\langle\cdot,Y_{lm}\rangle Y_{lm}$; the truncations $P_{\le N}:=\sum_{l\le N}P_l$ converge strongly to the identity; and $V_l\perp V_k$ for $l\ne k$; a unitary operator mapping $V_l$ onto itself commutes with $P_l$ ([[thm-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-strongly-continuous-rotation-action]], [[thm-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-sphere-onb]], [[def-orthogonal-projection]], [[def-orthogonality-and-orthogonal-complement]], [[thm-parseval-equivalences-for-a-complete-orthonormal-family]], [[def-orthonormal-family-complete-orthonormal-system-and-hilbert-basis]]).

[F7] The diagonal operator $L_z$ of [[thm-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-sphere-laplacian-diagonal-domain]] (a),(c): $L_z$ is self-adjoint with domain $\{F:\sum_{l,m}m^2|\langle F,Y_{lm}\rangle|^2<\infty\}$ and $L_zF=\sum_{l,m}\hbar m\langle F,Y_{lm}\rangle Y_{lm}$; $L_zY_{lm}=\hbar mY_{lm}$; and for every $F\in H$ and every chart point $(\theta,\varphi)\in(0,\pi)\times(0,2\pi)$, $(L_zF)(\theta,\varphi)=-i\hbar\partial_\varphi F(\theta,\varphi)$, where $F(\theta,\varphi)$ denotes evaluation at $\Phi(\theta,\varphi)=(\sin\theta\cos\varphi,\sin\theta\sin\varphi,\cos\theta)$.

[F8] The diagonal lemma ([[lem-nrqm-oscillator-diagonal-adjoint-and-graph-core-lemma]] (a)-(d)): for a complete orthonormal family $(e_n)$ and real eigenvalues $(\lambda_n)$, the diagonal operator $A$ with $Ae_n=\lambda_ne_n$, $D(A)=\{x:\sum_n\lambda_n^2|\langle x,e_n\rangle|^2<\infty\}$, is self-adjoint, is the unique self-adjoint extension of its finite span $D_0$, and $\overline{D_0}=A$; the finite span is a core. Eigenspaces of a self-adjoint operator for distinct real eigenvalues are orthogonal, because $\langle TF,G\rangle=\langle F,TG\rangle$ for $F,G$ in the domain of a self-adjoint $T$ ([[def-symmetric-self-adjoint-and-essentially-self-adjoint]], [[def-self-adjoint-and-normal-endomorphism]], [[def-orthogonality-and-orthogonal-complement]]).

[F9] The harmonic polynomials: the restriction map $\mathrm{res}_l:H_l^{\mathrm{pol}}\to V_l$ is a linear isomorphism, $H_l^{\mathrm{pol}}$ is spanned by the polynomial representatives $h_{lm}$ of the $Y_{lm}$, and $\Delta\partial_i=\partial_i\Delta$ on polynomials, so $\partial_ih_{lm}\in H_{l-1}^{\mathrm{pol}}$ ([[thm-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-harmonic-polynomial-dimension]] (b),(c), [[thm-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-associated-polynomial-normalization]], [[def-laplacian-of-a-c2-function]], [[thm-ck-euclidean-maps-closed-under-algebra-and-composition]]).

[F10] On polynomials in $x_1,x_2,x_3$: $\partial_ix_j=\delta_{ij}$, the partial derivatives commute (${}\partial_a\partial_b=\partial_b\partial_a$), and the product rule $\partial_a(fg)=(\partial_af)g+f\partial_ag$ holds, so that the first-order operators $x_a\partial_b$ satisfy $[x_a\partial_b,\,x_c\partial_d]=x_a\delta_{bc}\partial_d-x_c\delta_{ad}\partial_b$ ([[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]], [[def-directional-and-partial-derivatives]], [[thm-ck-euclidean-maps-closed-under-algebra-and-composition]]).



## Proof

1.1 [(a), finite span lies in the domain] For $F\in V_l$ with representative $p=p_F\in H_l^{\mathrm{pol}}$ ([F9]) and $i$ fixed, compute the difference quotient for $t\ne0$ at $\Omega\in S^2$: by [F5], $U_i(t)F(\Omega)-F(\Omega)=p(R_i(t)^{-1}\Omega)-p(\Omega)=\int_0^t\nabla p(R_i(s)^{-1}\Omega)\cdot\bigl((R_i(s)^{-1})'\,\Omega\bigr)ds=-\int_0^t\nabla p(R_i(s)^{-1}\Omega)\cdot\bigl(J_iR_i(s)^{-1}\Omega\bigr)ds$, because $\frac{d}{ds}R_i(s)^{-1}=-R_i(s)^{-1}R_i'(s)R_i(s)^{-1}$ and $R_i(s)^{-1}R_i'(s)R_i(s)^{-1}\Omega=R_i(s)^{-1}J_iR_i(s)^{-1}\Omega$, whose inner product with $\nabla p(R_i(s)^{-1}\Omega)$ equals that of $J_iR_i(s)^{-1}\Omega$ with $\nabla p(\cdot)$ transported, giving the displayed form by [F3],[F4] with $J_i=[e_i]_\times$. Hence the quotient $(U_i(t)F-F)/t=-\frac1t\int_0^t g_i(s,\Omega)ds$ with $g_i(s,\Omega):=\nabla p(R_i(s)^{-1}\Omega)\cdot(J_iR_i(s)^{-1}\Omega)$, and $g_i$ is continuous with $g_i(0,\Omega)=\nabla p(\Omega)\cdot(J_i\Omega)=D_ip(\Omega)$ by [F4]. Continuity on the compact set $[-\delta,\delta]\times S^2$ makes $g_i$ uniformly continuous, so $(U_i(t)F-F)/t\to-D_ip$ uniformly on $S^2$ as $t\to0$; since uniform convergence implies $L^2$ convergence, the derivative at $0$ of $t\mapsto U_i(t)F$ in $L^2(S^2,\sigma)$ exists, so $F\in D(T_i)$ and $T_iF=i\,D_ip$ as an $L^2$ class; equivalently $L_iF=-\hbar T_iF=\mathrm{res}_l(-i\hbar D_ip)$, and $D_ip$ has degree $l$, so $L_iF\in V_l$ ([F9]). [F1, F3, F4, F5, F9, algebra]

2.1 [(a), self-adjointness and the group relation] By [F2], $T_i$ is self-adjoint and $U_i(t)=e^{itT_i}$; since $R_i(t)$ depends continuously on $t$ and the orbit maps are continuous ([F1]), $U_i$ is a strongly continuous one-parameter unitary group, and $-\hbar T_i=L_i$ is self-adjoint with $U_i(t)=e^{-itL_i/\hbar}$; the statements of clause (a) about the domain mean exactly $D(L_i)=D(T_i)$, the differentiability set of [F2]. [F1, F2, step 1.1]

2.2 [(b), commutators] On polynomials define the operators $D_i$ as in the statement; expanding with the product rule for partial derivatives [F10], $[D_1,D_2]p=[x_2\partial_3-x_3\partial_2,\ x_3\partial_1-x_1\partial_3]p=(x_2\partial_1-0)-(0-x_1\partial_2)p=-D_3p$ and cyclically $[D_i,D_j]=-\sum_k\varepsilon_{ijk}D_k$ (both sides alternating and bilinear in $i,j$). By step 1.1, on $H$ one has $L_i=(-i\hbar)D_i$, so $L_iL_j-L_jL_i=(-i\hbar)^2[D_i,D_j]=-\hbar^2\bigl(-\sum_k\varepsilon_{ijk}D_k\bigr)=\hbar^2\sum_k\varepsilon_{ijk}D_k=i\hbar\sum_k\varepsilon_{ijk}L_k$ on $H$. [step 1.1, F10, algebra]

2.3 [(c), $L_3=L_z$] Let $q$ be a polynomial and let $\Phi(\theta,\varphi)=(\sin\theta\cos\varphi,\sin\theta\sin\varphi,\cos\theta)$; the chain rule gives $\partial_\varphi(q\circ\Phi)(\theta,\varphi)=\nabla q(\Phi)\cdot\partial_\varphi\Phi=\nabla q(\Phi)\cdot(e_3\times\Phi)=(e_3\times\Phi)\cdot\nabla q=D_3q(\Phi)$ by [F4]. Hence for $F\in H$ with representative $p$, at every chart point $L_3F=\mathrm{res}(-i\hbar D_3p)$ has the value $-i\hbar\partial_\varphi F(\theta,\varphi)$, which by [F7] is the value of $L_zF$; both are continuous functions on $S^2$ (finite sums of the continuous $Y_{lm}$ and of $\hbar mY_{lm}$), so $L_3F=L_zF$ for all $F\in H$. Thus $L_3$ and $L_z$ are both self-adjoint extensions of the symmetric diagonal operator $S:=L_3|_{H}=L_z|_{H}$, whose orthonormal basis $(Y_{lm})$ consists of eigenvectors with real eigenvalues $\hbar m$; by [F8] applied to $(Y_{lm})$ and the eigenvalues $(\hbar m)$, the operator $S$ is essentially self-adjoint with a unique self-adjoint extension, so $L_3=L_z$, and in particular $L_3Y_{lm}=\hbar mY_{lm}$. [step 1.1, F7, F8, algebra]

3.1 [(a), $H$ is a core] For every $t$, $U_i(t)$ maps $V_l$ onto itself and is unitary, so it commutes with $P_l$ and hence with $P_{\le N}$ ([F6]). Therefore for $F\in D(T_i)$ the orbit $t\mapsto U_i(t)P_{\le N}F=P_{\le N}U_i(t)F$ is differentiable at $0$ with derivative $P_{\le N}\,iT_iF$: $P_{\le N}F\in D(T_i)$ and $T_iP_{\le N}F=P_{\le N}T_iF$. Given $F\in D(L_i)$, the truncations $F_N:=P_{\le N}F$ lie in $H$, $F_N\to F$ and $L_iF_N=P_{\le N}L_iF\to L_iF$ by strong convergence of the projections ([F6]); hence $H$ is a core of $L_i$. [step 2.1, F6, algebra]

4.1 [(d), conjugation for the quarter turns] Let $S:=R_2(-\pi/2)$ and $W:=R_1(\pi/2)$. Directly from the entries in [F3] and the values $\cos(\pm\pi/2)=0,\ \sin(\pm\pi/2)=\pm1$ ([[def-pi-via-first-positive-cosine-zero]], [[cor-pi-is-the-first-positive-sine-zero]], [[thm-sine-cosine-signs-monotonicity-and-ranges]]) one computes the $3\times3$ products $SR_1(t)S^{-1}=R_3(t)$ and $WR_2(t)W^{-1}=R_3(t)$ for all $t$. Hence $U(S)U_1(t)U(S)^{-1}=U(SR_1(t)S^{-1})=U_3(t)$ by [F1]; the left side is the unitary conjugate group, whose generator has domain $U(S)D(T_1)$ and action $U(S)T_1U(S)^{-1}$ by [F2], while the generator of $U_3$ is $T_3$; by the injectivity part of Stone's bijection the generators are equal: $U(S)T_1U(S)^{-1}=T_3$, with $U(S)D(T_1)=D(T_3)$. Multiplying by $-\hbar$ gives $U(S)L_1U(S)^{-1}=L_3$ and $U(S)D(L_1)=D(L_3)$; the same argument with $W$ gives $U(W)L_2U(W)^{-1}=L_3$ and $U(W)D(L_2)=D(L_3)$. [step 2.1, F1, F2, F3, algebra] ∎
