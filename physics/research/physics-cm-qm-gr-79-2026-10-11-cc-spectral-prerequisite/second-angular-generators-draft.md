## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice ([[def-countable-choice]]). Use the notation of
[[thm-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-strongly-continuous-rotation-action]]: $S^2$, the polar measure $\sigma$ with
$\sigma(S^2)=4\pi$, the Hilbert space $L^2(S^2,\sigma)$, the complete orthonormal family $(Y_{lm})$, the blocks
$V_l$, the finite span $H$, the spaces $H_l^{\mathrm{pol}}$ of complex harmonic homogeneous polynomials of degree
$l$, the restriction maps $\mathrm{res}_l$, and the unitary action $U(R)F=\text{class of }\tilde F(R^{-1}\cdot)$
of $SO(3)$. Fix $\hbar>0$ and for $i=1,2,3$ define the rotation matrices
$$R_1(t):=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&\cos t&-\sin t\\0&\sin t&\cos t\end{pmatrix},\quad R_2(t):=\begin{pmatrix}\cos t&0&\sin t\\0&1&0\\-\sin t&0&\cos t\end{pmatrix},\quad R_3(t):=\begin{pmatrix}\cos t&-\sin t&0\\ \sin t&\cos t&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},$$
put $U_i(t):=U(R_i(t))$. Let $L_z$ denote the diagonal operator of
[[thm-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-sphere-laplacian-diagonal-domain]], set $S:=R_2(-\pi/2)$, $W:=R_1(\pi/2)$,
and define
$$L_3:=L_z,\qquad L_1:=U(S)^{-1}L_zU(S),\qquad L_2:=U(W)^{-1}L_zU(W),$$
with domains $D(L_1)=U(S)^{-1}D(L_z)$, $D(L_2)=U(W)^{-1}D(L_z)$ and $D(L_3)=D(L_z)$.
Here $e^{-itL_z/\hbar}$ is the explicit diagonal unitary sending $Y_{lm}$ to $e^{-itm}Y_{lm}$;
$e^{-itL_1/\hbar}$ and $e^{-itL_2/\hbar}$ mean its conjugates by $U(S)$ and $U(W)$, respectively.
The proof identifies these operators with the complete strong derivative generators, so this construction
has the same generator meaning without an appeal to an unrestricted spectral or Stone theorem. For a polynomial $p$ put
$$D_ip:=(x\times\nabla p)_i=\sum_{j,k=1}^3\varepsilon_{ijk}\,x_j\,\partial_kp,$$
where $\varepsilon_{ijk}$ is the sign of the permutation $(1,2,3)\mapsto(i,j,k)$ and is $0$ when two indices
coincide. Then:

(a) each $L_i$ is a self-adjoint operator on $L^2(S^2,\sigma)$ with $U(R_i(t))=e^{-itL_i/\hbar}$ for all real
$t$; the finite span $H$ is contained in $D(L_i)$ and is a core of $L_i$
([[def-densely-defined-closed-and-closable-operator]], [[def-symmetric-self-adjoint-and-essentially-self-adjoint]]);
and for $F\in V_l$ with polynomial representative $p_F\in H_l^{\mathrm{pol}}$ one has
$$L_iF=\mathrm{res}_l(-i\hbar\,D_ip_F),$$
that is, $(L_iF)(\Omega)=-i\hbar\,D_ip_F(\Omega)$ for $\sigma$-almost every $\Omega\in S^2$; in particular
$L_iY_{lm}\in V_l$ for all $l,m$ and $L_iH\subseteq H$;

(b) on $H$ the operators satisfy the commutation relations
$$L_iL_j-L_jL_i=i\hbar\sum_{k=1}^3\varepsilon_{ijk}L_k;$$

(c) $L_3=L_z$ as self-adjoint operators (equal domains and equal action); in particular $L_3Y_{lm}=\hbar mY_{lm}$, so that
$L_3$ is diagonal in the basis $(Y_{lm})$, and for every $F\in H$ and chart point $(\theta,\varphi)$ one has $(L_3F)(\theta,\varphi)=-i\hbar\,\partial_\varphi F(\theta,\varphi)$;

(d) rotational conjugation identifies the domains: with the two quarter-turn rotations
$S:=R_2(-\pi/2)$ and $W:=R_1(\pi/2)$ one has
$$U(S)L_1U(S)^{-1}=L_3,\qquad U(W)L_2U(W)^{-1}=L_3,$$
as self-adjoint operators, and consequently $U(S)D(L_1)=D(L_3)$ and $U(W)D(L_2)=D(L_3)$: the three domains
are unitarily identified.

All assertions are mathematical statements about operators on $L^2(S^2,\sigma)$; no physical interpretation of
the generators as angular momentum is asserted, and no measurement postulate is used.

## Proof

1.1 [(a), the complete diagonal domain and adjoint] Write $c_{lm}=\langle F,Y_{lm}\rangle$. On the domain of [F2], Parseval defines $AF=\sum_{l,m}\hbar m c_{lm}Y_{lm}$ with convergence in norm, and finite sums prove symmetry. If $G\in D(A^*)$ and $K=A^*G$, testing $F=Y_{lm}$ in $\langle AF,G\rangle=\langle F,K\rangle$ gives $\langle K,Y_{lm}\rangle=\hbar m\langle G,Y_{lm}\rangle$. Parseval then implies $\sum\hbar^2m^2|\langle G,Y_{lm}\rangle|^2=\|K\|^2<\infty$: $G\in D(A)$ and $K=AG$. Conversely, for such $G$, the equality holds first on finite sums and then for all $F\in D(A)$ by their coefficient truncations. Thus $A=A^*$ on exactly the full domain of [F2], and $A=L_z$. For $F\in D(A)$, both $P_{\le N}F\to F$ and $AP_{\le N}F\to AF$ by the two convergent coefficient square sums; hence $H$ is a graph core. All sums use the fixed countable index family; no fiber selection or uncountable basis choice occurs. [F2, algebra]

2.1 [(a),(d), adjoints and cores of the conjugates] If $Q$ is either $U(S)$ or $U(W)$, define $B=Q^{-1}AQ$ on $Q^{-1}D(A)$. For $G,K$, the adjoint identity $\langle BF,G\rangle=\langle F,K\rangle$ for every $F\in Q^{-1}D(A)$ is, on putting $X=QF$, exactly $\langle AX,QG\rangle=\langle X,QK\rangle$ for every $X\in D(A)$. By step 1.1 this holds exactly when $QG\in D(A)$ and $QK=AQG$. Thus $B^*=B$ with this full domain. Since $QH=H$, unitary transport of the graph approximations in step 1.1 gives $H$ as a core of $B$. With $L_3=A$, this proves self-adjointness, cores and the two stated conjugation/domain identities. [step 1.1, F1, algebra]

2.2 [(a), explicit groups and the full derivative domains] Define $E(t)F=\sum e^{-itm}c_{lm}Y_{lm}$. Parseval proves unitarity and the group law. For each $F$, a finite coefficient truncation controls the tail by $2\|F-P_{\le N}F\|$ and its finite head is continuous, proving strong continuity. If $F\in D(A)$, $|(e^{-itm}-1)/t|\le |m|$ and convergence on finite heads with square-summable weighted tails give $(E(t)F-F)/t\to-iAF/\hbar$. Conversely, if the quotient converges in norm to $G$, each coefficient tends to $-imc_{lm}$; Parseval for $G$ gives $\sum m^2|c_{lm}|^2=\|G\|^2<\infty$ and $G=-iAF/\hbar$. Thus this is exactly the full strong derivative domain, not merely a core calculation. The same argument transported by $Q$ gives exactly $D(Q^{-1}AQ)$ for $Q^{-1}E(t)Q$, with derivative $-iQ^{-1}AQF/\hbar$. [step 1.1, F2, algebra]

3.1 [(a),(c), identification with the actual rotation groups] Pullback by $R_3(t)^{-1}$ replaces $\varphi$ by $\varphi-t$. Therefore $U_3(t)Y_{lm}=e^{-itm}Y_{lm}$ for every integer $m$ by [F2], including negative $m$ with its conjugation phase. Equality on the dense finite span, and boundedness, imply $U_3(t)=E(t)$ on all $L^2$. The two finite matrix identities in [F3], transported by the group law, imply $U_1(t)=U(S)^{-1}E(t)U(S)$ and $U_2(t)=U(W)^{-1}E(t)U(W)$. These are precisely the explicitly defined exponentials in the Statement. Step 2.2 gives the exact derivative domains for all three groups and their actions $-iL_i/\hbar$; it also proves uniqueness if an operator is defined by the strong derivative. In particular $L_3=L_z$ as full operators, without an AC Stone premise. [step 2.2, F1, F2, F3, algebra]

4.1 [(a), polynomial differentiation with the correct inverse-curve sign] For $F=\mathrm{res}_l(p)\in V_l$, set $g_i(s,\Omega)=\nabla p(R_i(-s)\Omega)\cdot J_iR_i(-s)\Omega$. The correct chain derivative is $\frac{d}{ds}p(R_i(-s)\Omega)=-g_i(s,\Omega)$, directly from $R_i(-s)'=-J_iR_i(-s)$ in [F3]. The fundamental theorem gives $(U_i(t)F-F)/t=-t^{-1}\int_0^t g_i(s,\cdot)\,ds$. Uniform continuity on $[-\delta,\delta]\times S^2$ proves convergence uniformly, and hence in $L^2$, to $-g_i(0,\cdot)=-D_ip|_{S^2}$. By step 2.2, $F\in D(L_i)$ and $-iL_iF/\hbar=-\mathrm{res}(D_ip)$, so $L_iF=\mathrm{res}(-i\hbar D_ip)$. The curve $p\circ R_i(-t)$ lies in the finite-dimensional polynomial space $H_l^{\mathrm{pol}}$ by [F1]; its coefficientwise derivative at zero also lies there, because homogeneity and the harmonic equations are finite linear coefficient constraints. This derivative is $-D_ip$, proving that $D_ip$ is harmonic homogeneous of degree $l$, including the zero polynomial. Thus $L_iV_l\subseteq V_l$ and $L_iH\subseteq H$. [step 2.2, step 3.1, F1, F3, F4, algebra]

5.1 [(a), finite blocks and their ODE identification] Put $A_i^{(l)}=L_i|_{V_l}$. This is an everywhere-defined Hermitian matrix: symmetry follows from self-adjointness on the whole space and $V_l\subseteq D(L_i)$. The quarter turns preserve $V_l$, so $A_1^{(l)}$ and $A_2^{(l)}$ are unitarily conjugate to the diagonal $A_3^{(l)}$ with entries $\hbar m$. Consequently $\|A_i^{(l)}\|\le\hbar l$. Polynomial differentiation at time $t$, or the finite group law and step 4.1, gives $(U_i(t)|_{V_l})'=(-i/\hbar)A_i^{(l)}U_i(t)|_{V_l}$ with initial value the identity. The absolutely convergent matrix exponential power series solves this equation; uniqueness follows by differentiating its inverse times any other solution. Hence the finite polynomial action equals $\exp(-itA_i^{(l)}/\hbar)$. Orthogonal block truncation then identifies its all-vector extension with the groups of step 3.1. Explicitly $D(L_i)=\{F:\sum_l\|A_i^{(l)}P_lF\|^2<\infty\}$ and $L_iF=\sum_l A_i^{(l)}P_lF$: this is the diagonal domain of step 1.1 transported by a block-preserving quarter turn. Thus no hidden narrower domain or additional time-continuity hypothesis is introduced. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, F1, F2, algebra]

5.2 [(b), commutators] The polynomial product rule gives $[x_a\partial_b,x_c\partial_d]=x_a\delta_{bc}\partial_d-x_c\delta_{ad}\partial_b$. Substituting $D_1=x_2\partial_3-x_3\partial_2$, $D_2=x_3\partial_1-x_1\partial_3$ gives $[D_1,D_2]=-D_3$, and cyclic permutation and antisymmetry give $[D_i,D_j]=-\sum_k\varepsilon_{ijk}D_k$. All products are defined on $H$ by step 4.1. Therefore $[L_i,L_j]=(-i\hbar)^2[D_i,D_j]=\hbar^2\sum_k\varepsilon_{ijk}D_k=i\hbar\sum_k\varepsilon_{ijk}L_k$. [step 4.1, F4, algebra]

6.1 [(c), the chart action] The chain rule gives $\partial_\varphi p(\sin\theta\cos\varphi,\sin\theta\sin\varphi,\cos\theta)=\nabla p(\Omega)\cdot(e_3\times\Omega)=D_3p(\Omega)$. Apply step 4.1 and linearity to finite harmonic sums. Together with the full equality $L_3=L_z$ of step 3.1 and the domain conjugations of step 2.1, this proves every clause (a)-(d). [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, F4, algebra] ∎

## Example

[[thm-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-orbital-self-adjoint-generators]]: $S^2=\{x\in\mathbb R^3:|x|=1\}$ with polar

operators $L_1,L_2,L_3$ with $L_\pm:=L_1\pm iL_2$, the finite span $H$, the blocks $V_l$, and the first-order
operators $D_i$, so that $L_i=-i\hbar D_i$ on $H$. Let $d:=\sqrt{3/(4\pi)}$ and, writing $\Omega=(\Omega_1,\Omega_2,\Omega_3)$ for the points of $S^2$ and restricting the coordinate polynomials,
$$v_1:=-\frac{d}{\sqrt2}(\Omega_1+i\Omega_2),\qquad v_0:=d\,\Omega_3,\qquad v_{-1}:=\frac{d}{\sqrt2}(\Omega_1-i\Omega_2)\in V_1 .$$
Then:

(a) $(v_1,v_0,v_{-1})$ is an orthonormal basis of $V_1$, and it is the degree-one part of the family
$(Y_{lm})$, in the sense that
$$v_m=Y_{1,m}\qquad (m\in\{1,0,-1\})$$
as elements of $L^2(S^2,\sigma)$ ([[thm-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-associated-polynomial-normalization]]);

(b) the core commutators hold on the whole three-dimensional space $V_1$:
$$L_3v_m=\hbar m\,v_m,\qquad L_+v_1=0,\qquad L_+v_0=\sqrt2\,\hbar\,v_1,\qquad L_+v_{-1}=\sqrt2\,\hbar\,v_0,$$
with $(L_-|_{V_1})=(L_+|_{V_1})^*$ (adjoint in the finite Hilbert space $V_1$) giving $L_-v_{-1}=0$, $L_-v_0=\sqrt2\hbar v_{-1}$, $L_-v_1=\sqrt2\hbar v_0$; and
$$[L_1,L_2]v=i\hbar\,L_3v,\qquad (L_1^2+L_2^2+L_3^2)v=2\hbar^2\,v\qquad (v\in V_1);$$

(c) the assignment $e_m\mapsto v_m$ ($m=-1,0,1$) is a unitary isomorphism of the spin-$1$ model
$(V_1^{\mathrm{spin}},J_x,J_y,J_z)$ of [[prop-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-finite-spin-matrices]] onto $V_1$ carrying
$J_i$ to $\hbar^{-1}L_i$ for $i=x,y,z$: the orbital degree-$1$ block is unitarily equivalent to the spin-$1$
block, and $L_1^2+L_2^2+L_3^2$ acts on $V_1$ as $\hbar^2A$, where $A$ is the diagonal sphere Laplacian of
[[thm-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-sphere-laplacian-diagonal-domain]] with $A|_{V_1}=2\,\mathrm{id}$.

All claims are finite-dimensional matrix computations inside $V_1$; no physical interpretation of the operators
as angular momentum of a particle is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the operators $L_1,L_2,L_3,L_\pm$ of the generators theorem, the functions $\Omega_i$, the number $d=\sqrt{3/(4\pi)}$, and the vectors $v_m$.

[F1] Generators theorem ([[thm-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-orbital-self-adjoint-generators]] (a),(b)): for $F\in V_l$ with representative $p_F$ one has $L_iF=\mathrm{res}_l(-i\hbar D_ip_F)$ with $D_ip=(x\times\nabla p)_i$, so $L_i$ maps $V_l$ into itself and $L_i=-i\hbar D_i$ on $H$; $L_1,L_2,L_3$ are self-adjoint and $L_iL_j-L_jL_i=i\hbar\sum_k\varepsilon_{ijk}L_k$ on $H$; and the everywhere-defined finite restrictions $A_i:=L_i|_{V_1}$ are Hermitian. Hence $A_\pm:=A_1\pm iA_2$ satisfy $A_-=(A_+)^*$ as operators on $V_1$: for $f,g\in V_1$, $\langle A_+f,g\rangle=\langle f,(A_1-iA_2)g\rangle$, since the inner product is first-variable linear. This is solely a finite-block adjoint identity, with no assertion about the full unbounded sum adjoint.

[F2] The $L^2$ inner products of the coordinate functions: integrating over $S^2$ in angle coordinates, $\int_{S^2}\Omega_1^2\,d\sigma=\int_0^\pi\int_0^{2\pi}\sin^2\theta\cos^2\varphi\,\sin\theta\,d\varphi\,d\theta=\pi\cdot\tfrac43=\tfrac{4\pi}3$ and likewise for $\Omega_2^2,\Omega_3^2$, while all mixed integrals vanish, since $\int_0^{2\pi}\cos\varphi\,d\varphi=\int_0^{2\pi}\sin\varphi\,d\varphi=\int_0^{2\pi}\sin\varphi\cos\varphi\,d\varphi=0$ and the $\theta$-integrals factor; hence $\langle\Omega_i,\Omega_j\rangle=\tfrac{4\pi}3\delta_{ij}$ ([[lem-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-sphere-integral-in-angle-coordinates]], [[thm-ftc-second-part]], [[thm-sine-and-cosine-derivatives]], [[def-sine-and-cosine-by-power-series]], [[def-complex-exponential]]).

[F3] The degree-one harmonics: $Y_{1,0}=b_{10}w_{10}(\cos\theta)/\sqrt{2\pi}$ with $P_1(t)=t$ and $w_{10}=P_1$, $b_{10}=(\int_{-1}^1t^2dt)^{-1/2}=\sqrt{3/2}$, so $Y_{1,0}=\sqrt{3/(4\pi)}\Omega_3$; and $Y_{1,1}=b_{11}w_{11}(\cos\theta)e^{i\varphi}/\sqrt{2\pi}$ with $w_{11}=-(1-t^2)^{1/2}$ and $b_{11}=(\int_{-1}^1(1-t^2)dt)^{-1/2}=\sqrt{3/4}$, so $Y_{1,1}=-\sqrt{3/(8\pi)}(\Omega_1+i\Omega_2)$; moreover $Y_{1,-1}=-Y_{1,1}$-conjugate: $Y_{1,-1}=(-1)^1\overline{Y_{1,1}}=\sqrt{3/(8\pi)}(\Omega_1-i\Omega_2)$, using $\Omega_1+i\Omega_2=\sin\theta e^{i\varphi}$ on $S^2$ ([[thm-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-associated-polynomial-normalization]] (a),(c), [[thm-ftc-second-part]], [[thm-sine-and-cosine-derivatives]], [[def-sine-and-cosine-by-power-series]], [[def-complex-exponential]]).

[F4] The space $V_1$ and the diagonal operator: $V_1$ consists of the classes of the restrictions of the linear polynomials $\Omega_i$, the restriction map on harmonic homogeneous polynomials of degree $1$ is a linear isomorphism onto $V_1$, and $AY_{1,m}=2Y_{1,m}$, so $A|_{V_1}=2\,\mathrm{id}$ ([[thm-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-harmonic-polynomial-dimension]] (b),(c), [[thm-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-sphere-laplacian-diagonal-domain]] (a), [[def-laplacian-of-a-c2-function]]).

[F5] Spin-one data ([[prop-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-finite-spin-matrices]]): in the basis $(e_m)_{m=1,0,-1}$ one has $J_ze_m=me_m$, $J_+e_1=0$, $J_+e_0=\sqrt2e_1$, $J_+e_{-1}=\sqrt2e_0$, $J_-=(J_+)^*$, and $J_x=\tfrac12(J_++J_-)$, $J_y=\tfrac1{2i}(J_+-J_-)$; the arrows $e_m\mapsto v_m$ with orthonormal domain and target bases are unitary ([F2], [[def-inner-product-space]], [[cor-inner-product-induces-a-norm]]).



## Verification

1.1 [(a) orthonormality and identification] By [F2], $\|v_0\|^2=d^2\tfrac{4\pi}3=1$, $\|v_{\pm1}\|^2=\tfrac{d^2}2(\tfrac{4\pi}3+\tfrac{4\pi}3)=1$, and $\langle v_1,v_0\rangle=\langle v_1,v_{-1}\rangle=\langle v_0,v_{-1}\rangle=0$; so the $v_m$ are orthonormal, and since $\dim V_1=3$ they form an orthonormal basis of $V_1$ ([F4]). Comparing with the displayed values of [F3]: $Y_{1,0}=d\Omega_3=v_0$, $Y_{1,1}=-\sqrt{3/(8\pi)}(\Omega_1+i\Omega_2)=-\tfrac{d}{\sqrt2}(\Omega_1+i\Omega_2)=v_1$ (because $\sqrt{3/(8\pi)}=d/\sqrt2$), and $Y_{1,-1}=+\tfrac{d}{\sqrt2}(\Omega_1-i\Omega_2)=v_{-1}$. [F2, F3, F4, algebra]

2.1 [(b) $L_3$ and ladder] Compute the polynomial actions with $D_1=x_2\partial_3-x_3\partial_2$, $D_2=x_3\partial_1-x_1\partial_3$, $D_3=x_1\partial_2-x_2\partial_1$ and [F1]: $D_3(\Omega_1\pm i\Omega_2)=\pm i(\Omega_1\pm i\Omega_2)$ (since $D_3\Omega_1=-\Omega_2$, $D_3\Omega_2=\Omega_1$), hence $L_3(\Omega_1\pm i\Omega_2)=\pm\hbar(\Omega_1\pm i\Omega_2)$ and, after the normalisation $v_{\pm1}=\mp\frac{d}{\sqrt2}(\Omega_1\pm i\Omega_2)$, $L_3v_1=\hbar v_1$, $L_3v_{-1}=-\hbar v_{-1}$, and $L_3v_0=0$; further $(D_1+iD_2)(\Omega_1+i\Omega_2)=0$, $(D_1+iD_2)\Omega_3=-i(\Omega_1+i\Omega_2)$, $(D_1+iD_2)(\Omega_1-i\Omega_2)=2i\Omega_3$, so $L_+=-i\hbar(D_1+iD_2)$ satisfies $L_+v_1=0$, $L_+v_0=-i\hbar\,d\,(-i(\Omega_1+i\Omega_2))=-\hbar d(\Omega_1+i\Omega_2)=\sqrt2\hbar v_1$, and $L_+v_{-1}=-i\hbar\,d\,\tfrac{2i}{\sqrt2}\Omega_3=\sqrt2\hbar\,d\,\Omega_3=\sqrt2\hbar v_0$; the relations for $L_-|_{V_1}=(L_+|_{V_1})^*$ follow by the finite-block adjunction of [F1]. [step 1.1, F1, algebra]

3.1 [(b) commutators on the core] With the same operators, $L_1x_1=0$, $L_1x_2=i\hbar x_3$, $L_1x_3=-i\hbar x_2$; $L_2x_1=-i\hbar x_3$, $L_2x_2=0$, $L_2x_3=i\hbar x_1$; $L_3x_1=i\hbar x_2$, $L_3x_2=-i\hbar x_1$, $L_3x_3=0$. Evaluating $L_1L_2-L_2L_1$ on $x_1,x_2,x_3$: since $L_2x_1=-i\hbar x_3$ and $L_1x_1=0$ one has $L_1L_2x_1=-i\hbar\,L_1x_3=-\hbar^2x_2$ and $L_2L_1x_1=0$, so $[L_1,L_2]x_1=-\hbar^2x_2=i\hbar(i\hbar x_2)=i\hbar L_3x_1$; $[L_1,L_2]x_2=+\hbar^2x_1=i\hbar L_3x_2$; and $[L_1,L_2]x_3=0=i\hbar L_3x_3$. Hence $[L_1,L_2]=i\hbar L_3$ on $V_1$, and cyclically for the other two pairs, consistent with [F1]. Likewise, squaring the displayed actions: $L_1^2x_1=0$, $L_2^2x_1=\hbar^2x_1$, $L_3^2x_1=\hbar^2x_1$; $L_1^2x_2=i\hbar\,L_1x_3=\hbar^2x_2$, $L_2^2x_2=0$, $L_3^2x_2=\hbar^2x_2$; $L_1^2x_3=\hbar^2x_3$, $L_2^2x_3=\hbar^2x_3$, $L_3^2x_3=0$; in each case the sum is $2\hbar^2x_i$, so $L_1^2+L_2^2+L_3^2=2\hbar^2\,\mathrm{id}$ on $V_1$, which equals $\hbar^2A|_{V_1}$ by [F4]. Alternatively the sum identity follows from the ladder relations of step 2.1 via $L_1^2+L_2^2=\tfrac12(L_+L_-+L_-L_+)$ and $L_1^2+L_2^2+L_3^2=2\hbar^2$ on each $v_m$. [step 1.1, F1, F4, algebra]

4.1 [(c) spin-one equivalence] By steps 1.1 and 2.1 the assignment $e_m\mapsto v_m$ matches the spin-one relations of [F5]: $J_ze_m=me_m$ with $L_3v_m=\hbar mv_m$, and $J_+e_1=0$, $J_+e_0=\sqrt2e_1$, $J_+e_{-1}=\sqrt2e_0$ with $L_+v_1=0$, $L_+v_0=\sqrt2\hbar v_1$, $L_+v_{-1}=\sqrt2\hbar v_0$; since both bases are orthonormal and $L_x=\tfrac12(L_++L_-)$, $L_y=\tfrac1{2i}(L_+-L_-)$ mirror $J_x,J_y$, the assignment is a unitary isomorphism carrying $J_i$ to $\hbar^{-1}L_i$ for $i=x,y,z$. The last assertion is step 3.1 with [F4]. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, F4, F5, algebra] ∎

## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice ([[def-countable-choice]]). Let $S^2:=\{x\in\mathbb R^3:|x|=1\}$, let
$\sigma$ be the polar surface measure on $S^2$ ([[def-polar-surface-measure-on-the-unit-sphere]]), a finite Borel
measure with $\sigma(S^2)=4\pi$ ([[lem-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-sphere-integral-in-angle-coordinates]]), and let
$L^2(S^2,\sigma)$ be the complex Hilbert space of equivalence classes of square-integrable functions with inner
product linear in the first argument ([[def-l-p-space-as-a-quotient-by-null-functions]],
[[def-complex-lp-and-euclidean-test-function-conventions]], [[def-hilbert-space]],
[[def-inner-product-space]]). Let $(Y_{lm})_{l\ge0,\,-l\le m\le l}$ be the complete orthonormal family of
[[thm-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-associated-polynomial-normalization]] and
[[thm-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-sphere-onb]], put $V_l:=\operatorname{span}\{Y_{l,-l},\dots,Y_{ll}\}$ and let
$H$ be the algebraic span of all $Y_{lm}$. For $l\ge0$ let
$$H_l^{\mathrm{pol}}:=\{\,p:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb C:\ p\ \text{is a homogeneous polynomial of degree } l \text{ with }\Delta p=0\,\}$$
be the complex vector space of harmonic homogeneous polynomials of degree $l$
([[def-homogeneous-polynomial-and-homogeneous-ideal]], [[def-monomials-multidegree-and-total-degree]],
[[def-laplacian-of-a-c2-function]]), let $\mathrm{res}_l:H_l^{\mathrm{pol}}\to L^2(S^2,\sigma)$ send $p$ to
the class of the restriction $p|_{S^2}$, and let $R\in SO(3)$ range over the special orthogonal group, a
subgroup of the general linear group with $R^{-1}=R^{\mathsf T}$ and unit operator norm, the set $M_3(\mathbb R)$
being identified with $\mathbb R^9$ and given the Euclidean metric ([[def-cm-special-orthogonal-group]],
[[lem-cm-so3-is-a-matrix-group]]). For $F\in L^2(S^2,\sigma)$ define $U(R)F$ to be the class of the function
$\Omega\mapsto\tilde F(R^{-1}\Omega)$, where $\tilde F$ is any representative of $F$. Then:

(a) $U(R)$ is a well-defined $\mathbb C$-linear isometry of $L^2(S^2,\sigma)$ onto itself with inverse
$U(R^{-1})$, hence a unitary operator ([[def-linear-isometry-and-orthogonal-or-unitary-operator]]);
$U(I_3)=\mathrm{id}$ and $U(RS)=U(R)U(S)$ for all $R,S\in SO(3)$, so $R\mapsto U(R)$ is a group homomorphism
from $SO(3)$ into the group of unitary operators on $L^2(S^2,\sigma)$;

(b) for every $R\in SO(3)$ and $p\in H_l^{\mathrm{pol}}$ one has $p\circ R^{-1}\in H_l^{\mathrm{pol}}$; the map
$\rho(R):p\mapsto p\circ R^{-1}$ is a $\mathbb C$-linear automorphism of $H_l^{\mathrm{pol}}$ with inverse
$\rho(R^{-1})$, and $\mathrm{res}_l(\rho(R)p)=U(R)\,\mathrm{res}_l(p)$; consequently $U(R)V_l=V_l$ for every
$l\ge0$, and $U(R)H=H$;

(c) (strong continuity) for every $F\in L^2(S^2,\sigma)$ and every $R_0\in SO(3)$, for every $\varepsilon>0$
there is $\delta>0$ such that $\|R-R_0\|_F<\delta$ for the Euclidean (Frobenius) norm on $M_3(\mathbb R)$
implies $\|U(R)F-U(R_0)F\|_2<\varepsilon$; thus every orbit map $R\mapsto U(R)F$ is continuous
([[def-metric-continuity]]). More precisely, if $P_NF:=\sum_{l\le N}\sum_{m}\langle F,Y_{lm}\rangle Y_{lm}$
denotes the orthogonal truncation of $F$ to $\bigoplus_{l\le N}V_l$ ([[def-orthonormal-family-complete-orthonormal-system-and-hilbert-basis]]),
then for all $R$
$$\|U(R)F-F\|_2\le 2\,\|F-P_NF\|_2+\|U(R)P_NF-P_NF\|_2 .$$

All assertions are mathematical statements about the action of $SO(3)$ on $L^2(S^2,\sigma)$; no physical
interpretation of the rotation action is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the sphere $S^2$, the polar measure $\sigma$, the complete orthonormal family $(Y_{lm})$, the blocks $V_l$, the finite span $H$, the spaces $H_l^{\mathrm{pol}}$, the restriction maps $\mathrm{res}_l$, and the group $SO(3)$.

[F1] The polar surface measure equals the chart surface measure on $S^2$, is finite with $\sigma(S^2)=4\pi$, and is preserved by orthogonal transformations: $\sigma(OE)=\sigma(E)$ for every orthogonal $O$ and every Borel $E\subseteq S^2$ ([[lem-euclidean-chart-measure-agrees-with-polar-surface-measure]], [[def-polar-surface-measure-on-the-unit-sphere]], [[lem-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-sphere-integral-in-angle-coordinates]]).

[F2] $(Y_{lm})$ is a complete orthonormal family of $L^2(S^2,\sigma)$; each $V_l$ has the orthonormal basis $(Y_{l,-l},\dots,Y_{ll})$; $V_l\perp V_k$ for $l\ne k$; $H$ is dense; and the orthogonal truncations $P_NF$ converge to $F$ in norm (Parseval) ([[thm-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-sphere-onb]], [[def-orthonormal-family-complete-orthonormal-system-and-hilbert-basis]], [[thm-parseval-equivalences-for-a-complete-orthonormal-family]]).

[F3] Each $Y_{lm}$ has a continuous polynomial representative on $S^2$ by [F4] and [[thm-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-associated-polynomial-normalization]]. For $0\le m\le l$, its chart formula is $Y_{lm}(\theta,\varphi)=b_{lm}w_{lm}(\cos\theta)e^{im\varphi}/\sqrt{2\pi}$; for $1\le m\le l$, $Y_{l,-m}=(-1)^m\overline{Y_{lm}}$. Thus the negative-index formula includes the factor $(-1)^m$, not the phase-free positive-index expression. Continuity at chart boundaries follows from the polynomial representative, and conjugation preserves it. Every $F\in H$ is consequently continuous on the whole sphere.

[F4] The restriction map $\mathrm{res}_l$ is a $\mathbb C$-linear isomorphism $H_l^{\mathrm{pol}}\to V_l$, so every $F\in V_l$ has a unique representative $p_F\in H_l^{\mathrm{pol}}$ with $\mathrm{res}_l(p_F)=F$ ([[thm-nrqm-orbital-and-spin-harmonic-polynomial-dimension]] (b),(c)).

[F5] Polynomial maps on $\mathbb R^3$ are $C^\infty$; for a $C^2$ function $p$ and the linear map $L(x)=R^{-1}x$ the chain rule gives $J(p\circ L)(x)=Jp(Lx)\,R^{-1}$ and therefore $\Delta(p\circ L)(x)=\operatorname{tr}\bigl((R^{-1})^{\mathsf T}D^2p(Lx)R^{-1}\bigr)=\Delta p(Lx)$, because $R^{-1}(R^{-1})^{\mathsf T}=I_3$; the top-degree part of $p\circ L$ is the composition of the top-degree part of $p$ with $L$, so invertibility of $L$ preserves the degree of a nonzero polynomial and preserves homogeneity; sums and scalar multiples of harmonic homogeneous polynomials of one degree are again harmonic homogeneous of that degree ([[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]], [[thm-total-derivative-computes-directional-and-partial-derivatives]], [[def-laplacian-of-a-c2-function]], [[def-directional-and-partial-derivatives]], [[def-ck-euclidean-maps-and-diffeomorphisms]], [[thm-ck-euclidean-maps-closed-under-algebra-and-composition]], [[def-homogeneous-polynomial-and-homogeneous-ideal]], [[def-monomials-multidegree-and-total-degree]]).

[F6] Let $\Phi:S^2\to S^2$ be a measurable bijection with $\sigma(\Phi^{-1}E)=\sigma(E)$ for all Borel $E$; then for $F,G\in L^2(S^2,\sigma)$ the functions $F\circ\Phi$, $G\circ\Phi$ lie in $L^2(S^2,\sigma)$, with $\langle F\circ\Phi,G\circ\Phi\rangle=\langle F,G\rangle$ (substitution formula for the measure-preserving bijection), and $F=G$ $\sigma$-a.e. implies $F\circ\Phi=G\circ\Phi$ $\sigma$-a.e.; these identities are those of the change-of-variables formula applied to indicators and to nonnegative Borel functions and then to the four positive and negative parts ([[lem-euclidean-chart-measure-agrees-with-polar-surface-measure]], [[def-l-p-space-as-a-quotient-by-null-functions]], [[def-complex-lp-and-euclidean-test-function-conventions]]).

[F7] A continuous function on the compact metric space $S^2$ is uniformly continuous ([[cor-compact-domain-maps-are-uniformly-continuous]]); if $g:S^2\to\mathbb C$ is continuous and bounded then $\int_{S^2}|g|^2\,d\sigma\le 4\pi\,\|g\|_\infty^2$, because the nonnegative integral is monotone and $\sigma(S^2)=4\pi$ ([[def-nonnegative-lebesgue-integral]], [F1]).


[F8] Euclidean norms on $M_3(\mathbb R)\cong\mathbb R^9$ and on $\mathbb R^3$: for the Frobenius norm $\|A\|_F=(\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}^2)^{1/2}$, $|Ax|\le\|A\|_F|x|$ for all $x\in\mathbb R^3$ by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality; for $R\in SO(3)$, $\|R\|_F=\sqrt3$ and $\|R\|=1$, and $R^{-1}-R_0^{-1}=R^{-1}(R_0-R)R_0^{-1}$ for $R,R_0\in SO(3)$ ([[thm-cauchy-schwarz-for-real-and-complex-inner-product-spaces]], [[lem-cm-so3-is-a-matrix-group]], [[def-cm-special-orthogonal-group]]).

[F9] $SO(3)$ is a subgroup of the general linear group with $R^{-1}=R^{\mathsf T}$, so $(RS)^{-1}=S^{-1}R^{-1}$ and $I_3^{-1}=I_3$ for all $R,S\in SO(3)$ ([[lem-cm-so3-is-a-matrix-group]], [[def-cm-special-orthogonal-group]]).



## Proof

1.1 [(a), substitution map] Let $R\in SO(3)$ and $\Phi:=R^{-1}$. Then $\Phi$ maps $S^2$ bijectively onto $S^2$, since $|R^{-1}y|=|y|$, and by [F1] with the orthogonal matrix $R$ one has $\sigma(\Phi^{-1}E)=\sigma(RE)=\sigma(E)$ for every Borel $E$. If $\tilde F=\tilde F'$ is any representative pair of a class $F\in L^2$, then $\{F\ne F'\}$ has $\sigma$-measure zero, hence so does $R\{F\ne F'\}$ by [F1], so all representatives produce the same class: $U(R)$ is well defined; and $U(R)F\in L^2$ with $\|U(R)F\|_2=\|F\|_2$, $\langle U(R)F,U(R)G\rangle=\langle F,G\rangle$, by [F6]. [F1, F6, algebra]

1.2 [(b), harmonicity] Let $p\in H_l^{\mathrm{pol}}$ and $R\in SO(3)$; by [F5] with $L(x)=R^{-1}x$, $p\circ R^{-1}$ is $C^\infty$ with $\Delta(p\circ R^{-1})(x)=\Delta p(R^{-1}x)=0$ for all $x$, and since $R^{-1}$ is an invertible linear substitution, $p\circ R^{-1}$ is a homogeneous polynomial of degree $l$ (for $p=0$ it is $0$); hence $p\circ R^{-1}\in H_l^{\mathrm{pol}}$. The assignment $\rho(R)$ is $\mathbb C$-linear because composition with a fixed map is linear, and $\rho(R)\rho(R^{-1})=\rho(R^{-1})\rho(R)=\mathrm{id}$ because $p\circ R^{-1}\circ R=p$ and $p\circ R\circ R^{-1}=p$; so $\rho(R)$ is a linear automorphism of $H_l^{\mathrm{pol}}$. [F5, algebra]

1.3 [(c), continuity on the finite span] Let $F\in H$ and $R_0\in SO(3)$. By [F3] $F$ is continuous on $S^2$, hence uniformly continuous by [F7]: given $\varepsilon$ there is $\eta>0$ with $|x-y|<\eta$, $x,y\in S^2$, implying $|F(x)-F(y)|<\varepsilon/(2\sqrt{4\pi})$. For $\Omega\in S^2$ and $R\in SO(3)$, $U(R)F(\Omega)-U(R_0)F(\Omega)=F(R^{-1}\Omega)-F(R_0^{-1}\Omega)$ and, by [F8], $|R^{-1}\Omega-R_0^{-1}\Omega|=|R^{-1}(R_0-R)R_0^{-1}\Omega|\le\|R^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|R_0-R\|_F\|R_0^{-1}\Omega\|=\|R-R_0\|_F$. Choosing $\delta:=\eta$ one gets $\sup_{S^2}|U(R)F-U(R_0)F|\le\varepsilon/(2\sqrt{4\pi})$ whenever $\|R-R_0\|_F<\delta$, and then [F7] gives $\|U(R)F-U(R_0)F\|_2<\varepsilon/2<\varepsilon$. [F3, F7, F8, algebra]

2.1 [(a), group law and unitarity] For $F\in L^2$ and $\Omega\in S^2$, $(U(R^{-1})U(R)F)(\Omega)=(U(R)F)(R\Omega)=\tilde F(R^{-1}R\Omega)=\tilde F(\Omega)$ and symmetrically $U(R)U(R^{-1})=\mathrm{id}$, so $U(R)$ is a bijective linear isometry, i.e. unitary, with inverse $U(R^{-1})$ ([[def-linear-isometry-and-orthogonal-or-unitary-operator]]); $U(I_3)=\mathrm{id}$ since $R^{-1}=R^{\mathsf T}=\Omega$-fixing for $R=I_3$; $(U(R)U(S)F)(\Omega)=(U(S)F)(R^{-1}\Omega)=\tilde F(S^{-1}R^{-1}\Omega)=\tilde F((RS)^{-1}\Omega)=(U(RS)F)(\Omega)$ by [F9] the inverse identity $(RS)^{-1}=S^{-1}R^{-1}$. [step 1.1, F9, algebra]

3.1 [(b), intertwining and blocks] Let $F\in V_l$ with representative $p=p_F\in H_l^{\mathrm{pol}}$ ([F4]) and let $R\in SO(3)$. For $\Omega\in S^2$, $U(R)F(\Omega)=\tilde F(R^{-1}\Omega)=p(R^{-1}\Omega)=(p\circ R^{-1})(\Omega)$: here $R^{-1}\Omega$ lies in $S^2$, so the identity of classes $\mathrm{res}_l(\rho(R)p)=U(R)\mathrm{res}_l(p)$ follows. Hence $U(R)V_l\subseteq V_l$ for every $l$ by step 1.2 and [F4], and applying $U(R^{-1})$ (step 2.1) gives equality; since $H$ is the span of the $V_l$ and $U(R)$ is linear, $U(R)H=H$. [step 2.1, step 1.2, F4, algebra]

4.1 [(c), the estimate and general $F$] For arbitrary $F\in L^2(S^2,\sigma)$ and any $R,R_0$, the unitarity of step 2.1 gives $\|U(R)F-U(R_0)F\|_2=\|U(R)(F-P_NF)+U(R)P_NF-U(R_0)P_NF+U(R_0)(P_NF-F)\|_2\le 2\|F-P_NF\|_2+\|U(R)P_NF-U(R_0)P_NF\|_2$, with $P_NF\in H$ by [F2]. Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose $N$ with $\|F-P_NF\|_2<\varepsilon/4$ by [F2], then by step 1.3 a $\delta>0$ with $\|U(R)P_NF-U(R_0)P_NF\|_2<\varepsilon/2$ when $\|R-R_0\|_F<\delta$; then $\|U(R)F-U(R_0)F\|_2<\varepsilon$. Taking $R_0=I_3$ and keeping the terms separately yields the displayed estimate $\|U(R)F-F\|_2\le2\|F-P_NF\|_2+\|U(R)P_NF-P_NF\|_2$. [step 2.1, step 1.3, F2, algebra] ∎
