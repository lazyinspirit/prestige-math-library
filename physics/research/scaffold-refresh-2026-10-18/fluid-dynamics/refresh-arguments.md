## N1 — Bounded Stokes pressure, forcing and observables

Let D be nonempty bounded connected Lipschitz in R^d, d=2 or3, V=H^1_0(D)^d with gradient norm, Q=L^2_0(D), ν>0, ε(v)=(∇v+∇vᵀ)/2, a(u,v)=2ν∫ε(u):ε(v), b(v,q)=−∫q divv. Assume Countable Choice exactly as `thm-na-weak-stokes-solvability-lipschitz-domain`. For F in V′ that supplier gives unique (u,p)∈V×Q satisfying a(u,v)+b(v,p)=F(v), b(u,q)=0, with ||u||V≤ν⁻¹||F||V′ and ||p||2≤3Cdiv(D)||F||V′. It is a genuine upgrade over the inherited rectangular distribution-pressure construction, which remains a valid separate weaker route. No assertion of H² velocity follows from Lipschitz boundary alone. Mesh-based consumers must further restrict to matching affine shape-regular simplicial meshes of polygonal/polyhedral D.

For two loads subtract the linear equations; the supplier applies to their difference and gives ||δu||V≤ν⁻¹||δF||V′ and ||δp||2≤3Cdiv||δF||V′. A bounded linear observation ℓ∈V′ therefore obeys |ℓ(δu)|≤||ℓ||V′ν⁻¹||δF||V′. A volume sensor ℓ(v)=∫ψ·v, ψ∈L²(D)^d, has norm≤CP||ψ||2 by Poincare/Cauchy–Schwarz. An H¹ velocity has no generally defined point value in d=2,3; point sensors require a separate continuous representative or smoothing kernel. A pressure volume observation ∫χp with χ∈L² has bound 3||χ||2Cdiv||δF||V′. Mean-zero gauge is required before treating absolute pressure as observable; a difference of two normalized averaging kernels is gauge invariant even before fixing a mean.

A physical wrapper adopts the separate creeping-flow force balance, Newtonian stress, stationary homogeneous no-slip wall and density ρ0>0. Divide physical momentum by ρ0 and set ν=μ/ρ0, F(v)=∫b·v, p_math=p_phys/ρ0. The preceding bounds then apply, with physical pressure reconstructed by multiplication by ρ0. They do not adopt full inertial momentum simultaneously on this same Stokes field. If μ or force is measured, their uncertainties remain input qualifications; none is inferred from the mathematical theorem.

## N3 — Compressible finite-volume admissibility and entropy

Use the existing first-order HLL update, entropy-stable flux, LLF positivity theorem and convex scaling limiter. In one spatial dimension, U=(ρ,m,E) with ρ>0 and internal energy E−m²/(2ρ)>0; γ>1, p=(γ−1)(E−m²/(2ρ)), v=m/ρ, c=√(γp/ρ). F(U)=(m,m²/ρ+p,(E+p)m/ρ). The ideal-gas positive set Gγ is convex. On a periodic uniform grid, U_i^+=U_i−λ(Fhat_{i+1/2}−Fhat_{i−1/2}), λ=Δt/Δx. At a face choose the symmetric HLL branch SL=−a, SR=a with a≥max(|vL|+cL,|vR|+cR), 2λa≤1. The exact published `thm-pe03-ideal-gas-llf-branch-preserves-positivity` gives U_i^+∈Gγ. For arbitrary asymmetric HLL speeds the general theorem needs its explicit fan-average/intermediate-state invariant-domain hypothesis; estimated endpoint speeds alone do not prove it. Do not reuse the symbol ν for λ when physical viscosity is also present.

Finite-volume conservation is cancellation of equal opposite shared-face fluxes: summing |Ki|U_i^+ over periodic cells telescopes. It does not by itself establish entropy admissibility, compactness, convergence or positivity. `def-pe03-entropy-stable-dissipative-flux` provides a separate entropy-variable dissipation condition. A semidiscrete entropy identity cannot be advertised as a fully discrete bound without a time-integrator argument. A positivity limiter preserving a cell mean cannot repair a mean already outside Gγ; its admissibility branch must therefore precede reconstruction. These are separate contracts, not interchangeable uses of “stable.” The published `thm-conservative-finite-volume-weak-limit` is a scalar one-dimensional result on R, assuming Countable Choice, continuous consistent flux, strong L1_loc reconstruction convergence, local uniform bounds and initial cell averages. It supports the scalar conservation-law lane only; it is not an Euler-system convergence supplier. No compressible-system convergence is claimed from it. General multidimensional compressible entropy-solution uniqueness remains outside these claims.

The physical compressible wrapper adopts ideal-gas local equilibrium, conservative weak balances and entropy law with their units and qualifications. Mathematical convex entropy has the opposite sign to increasing physical entropy after the specified positive normalization. A shock-capturing simulation is a computed model solution, not an observed experiment or proof of microscopic entropy production.

## S1 — Random fields, sensor data and turbulent statistics

A random velocity is a strongly measurable map U:(Ξ,F,P)→X into a specified separable Banach/Hilbert state space X. E||U||²_X<∞ permits Bochner means and covariances of bounded finite-dimensional observations. Specify whether randomness describes initial data, forcing, measurement or an adopted stochastic closure. None follows from deterministic continuum balances. On X=L²(D)^d define Y_j=∫ψ_j·U, ψ_j∈L²; Y∈R^r is measurable and square integrable since |Y_j|≤||ψ_j||2||U||2. For sensors of H¹ states, N1 gives the proper bounded interface. Singular point evaluation needs higher regularity and is not supplied by an L²/H¹ declaration.

For deterministic finite ensemble weights the inherited covariance formulas remain exact. For a probabilistic ensemble with E||U||²2<∞, E||U⊗U||L1≤E||U||²2; Tonelli yields R=E[(U−EU)⊗(U−EU)]∈L¹(D) and zᵀRz≥0 a.e. Apply this first to countably many rational vectors on one common full-measure set and then use continuity in z to obtain simultaneous positivity for every real vector. The covariance tensor is an L¹ equivalence class, not a pointwise everywhere continuous field. If testing time-dependent equations, assume measurable random paths and E∫_K(|U|²+|P|+|f|)<∞ plus the integrability of each weak linear term. Fubini then commutes expectation and the compact test integrals. Expand E[U⊗U]=EU⊗EU+R in L¹ to obtain the Reynolds weak equation; no unproved commutation with classical derivatives is needed. This is a new conditional expectation interface extending, not replacing, the finite calculation.

For time-series Y_k=ℓ(U(kΔt)), strict stationarity and mixing are additional sampling assumptions. Under AC, centered strict stationarity, finite (2+δ)-moment and Σα(k)^{δ/(2+δ)}<∞, `thm-strong-mixing-clt-with-moment-conditions` gives sqrt(n)(Ybar−EY0)⇒N(0,σLR²), σLR²=VarY0+2Σ_{k≥1}Cov(Y0,Yk), absolutely convergent. σLR² may be zero. Independence cannot be inferred from a sampling interval or a visually decorrelated plot. For Reynolds stress estimated as averages of velocity products, apply the theorem to the product observable itself: finite (2+δ)-moment of that product is an additional requirement, typically implied by corresponding (4+2δ)-velocity moments. Nonstationary transients and evolving forcing do not meet this stationary theorem. Long-run variance is not the single-time variance. No mixing rate for Navier–Stokes is asserted without a separate stochastic/dynamical theorem.

## S2 — Uncertainty propagation and an explicit finite procedure

Let Θ carry a specified probability law μ and let S:Θ→X be a measurable selected solution map on a common [0,T]. A smooth uniqueness regime supplies a single-valued solution; a nonunique weak theory needs an explicitly given measurable selection before defining its pushforward. Let J:X→R be measurable; use bounded linear sensors when only energy norms are available. Q=J(S(θ)), Q_h=J(S_h(θ)). Quantitative moments and error bounds are stated for this random variable, not “the fluid” without a probability model.

Algorithm `alg-fd-uq-observable-budget`: inputs N≥2, a sampler declared iid μ, a total measurable solver callback returning either (state,certified observable bias bound b≥0) or `solver-failed`, and observable J; a finite maximum computational budget is part of the callback. Draw θ1,…,θN independently. Call the solver once per draw. If any call fails, return the failed indices and `incomplete`; do not delete/resample those cases and claim iid sampling of μ. Otherwise form q_i=J(S_h(θ_i)), qbar, s²=Σ(q_i−qbar)²/(N−1), bbar=Σb_i/N. Return N,qbar,s²,s/√N,bbar and `complete`; if s²=0 return `zero-sample-variance` as an extra diagnostic, without declaring the population deterministic. Stop after exactly N solver calls. This composes the published Monte Carlo estimators; it defines no new MC method. Successful complete execution needs finite arithmetic values. Its s/√N is a Monte Carlo standard-error estimate, not an exact confidence guarantee.

For this guarantee assume the callback succeeds μ-almost surely, so Q_h is a total μ-a.e. observable and the returned sample is iid. If failure has positive μ-probability, conditioning on `complete` changes the input law and this guarantee does not apply to μ. Under iid Q_h with finite nonzero variance, reuse the IID MC CLT and sample-variance consistency. For deterministic |Q−Q_h|≤b(θ) with Eb<∞, |EQ−EQ_h|≤Eb by integration. More generally for J L-Lipschitz, E|J(S)−J(S_h)|≤L E||S−S_h||. A finite expected callback error does not certify each realization; define whether b is pointwise or only expected. The decomposition qbar_h−EQ=(qbar_h−EQ_h)+(EQ_h−EQ) separates random sampling error and deterministic/mean discretization bias. A Gaussian asymptotic interval expanded by a deterministic bound B≥Eb targets EQ only under the stated CLT; bbar by itself is not a rigorous upper bound on Eb. A known bounded b can instead be bounded by its deterministic envelope. Physical model discrepancy and measurement uncertainty remain additional terms, absent unless separately quantified.

For independent input components, reuse `alg-pick-freeze-monte-carlo-sobol-estimators` and `thm-pick-freeze-sobol-ratio-joint-clt-and-mcse` with output in L⁴(μ), VarQ>0, iid rows and their exact zero-empirical-variance branch. Product-law independence is needed; correlated physical inputs require a different attribution, not the same Sobol interpretation. These sensitivity indices concern the chosen random model and observable, not causal material laws.

## S3 — Dependent resampling and spatial observations

The published moving-block bootstrap CLT is a specific Hilbert-valued theorem, not a generic strong-mixing theorem. Use H=L²([0,1]) observations, centered causal Bernoulli shift, L²-m-approximability with its actual summability definition, block lengths b_n→∞ and b_n/√n→0. `thm-moving-block-bootstrap-clt-for-dependent-data` then gives conditional weak convergence of sqrt(n)(Xbar*_n−E*Xbar*_n) and Hilbert–Schmidt convergence of nCov*(Xbar*_n) to Γ. Merely satisfying the S1 mixing CLT does not imply this bootstrap theorem's hypotheses.

## A1 — Assimilation and inverse interfaces

A finite representation a_k∈R^m, linear dynamics a_k=F_ka_{k−1}+q_k, observations y_k=H_ka_k+r_k, Gaussian a0 and mutually independent centered Gaussian noises, and positive definite innovation covariance define the exact linear-Gaussian filtering model. Reuse `alg-kalman-filter` and `thm-kalman-filter-is-exact-posterior`; singular state/process covariances are allowed, but every solved innovation matrix is positive definite. Outputs are means and covariances of this declared finite statistical model. A Galerkin truncation, linearization and adopted process noise are modeling steps; nonlinear Navier–Stokes observations do not become linear-Gaussian by naming this algorithm.

For nonlinear ensemble assimilation reuse `alg-ensemble-kalman-filter`. The published large-ensemble exactness theorem applies only to linear-Gaussian dynamics/observations, iid Gaussian initialization, independent member noises and positive definite observation covariance, fixed finite horizon as N→∞, with the specified divisor-N covariance. Finite ensembles become dependent after update; no iid-member posterior claim survives automatically. Localization, inflation and non-Gaussian transport change the algorithm and require new model/procedure contracts. Compare an assimilated forecast with held-out observations, respecting reuse of data and calibration uncertainty; do not call fitting a validation proof.

For deterministic finite inverse problems y=Ga+e with covariance R>0, whiten by R^{-1/2}. Identifiability of a in a chosen linear subspace is injectivity of G there: if Ga=Gb then G(a−b)=0. Stable reconstruction needs s_min(R^{-1/2}G)>0; the least-squares pseudoinverse error is ≤||R^{-1/2}e||/s_min by singular-value coordinates. If s_min=0, pick v≠0 in kerG; a and a+v are observationally indistinguishable. This exact finite argument is a prerequisite interface to the inverse-problems scaffold, not a general PDE coefficient-identifiability theorem. Pressure gauges create an additional null direction unless fixed or removed from G.

## T1 — Filtering, LES and conditional dissipation

On T^d use a smooth nonnegative periodic convolution kernel Gℓ, integral one, and smooth divergence-free u. Define ubar=Gℓ*u and τℓ=Gℓ*(u⊗u)−ubar⊗ubar. Periodic convolution commutes with derivatives by differentiating the smooth kernel/integrand; Fubini applies on the compact cell. Expand the positive weighted covariance to show zᵀτℓz=∫Gℓ(r)[z·(u(x−r)−ubar(x))]²dr≥0. This positive-semidefiniteness needs nonnegative G; a sharp spectral cutoff does not automatically share it. Filtering the equation gives ∂t ubar+div(ubar⊗ubar)+∇pbar=νΔubar−divτℓ+fbar.

Dot with ubar and integrate: (1/2)d||ubar||²/dt+ν||∇ubar||²=∫τℓ:∇ubar+∫fbar·ubar. Define forward transfer Πℓ=−τℓ:∇ubar. Its spatial integral has no general sign, despite τℓ≥0. A gradient can have either sign against a PSD tensor; for trace-free strain the eigenvalues include both signs. The familiar eddy-viscosity choice τ_model=2kI/d−2νtD(ubar), k≥0,νt≥0, is an empirical constitutive closure, generally not the exact filtered covariance. It yields −τ_model:∇ubar=2νt|D|²≥0 when divubar=0. This dissipativity is a consequence of the adopted formula, not a universal no-backscatter theorem. The modeled tensor is not guaranteed PSD unless k is large enough relative to its deviatoric part.

For compactly supported rescaled kernels and a spatially Hölder field |u(x−r)−u(x)|≤A|r|^α, 0<α<1, one gets ||τℓ||∞≤C A²ℓ^{2α}: write τ=∫G δu⊗δu−(∫Gδu)⊗² and bound both terms. Since ∫∇Gℓ=0, ∇ubar=∫∇Gℓ(r)(u(x−r)−u(x))dr has bound C Aℓ^{α−1}. Therefore |Πℓ|≤C A³ℓ^{3α−1}. If A(t)³ is integrable, its integrated bound tends to zero for α>1/3. This is a transfer estimate for fields with an assumed Hölder bound; it neither constructs singular Euler solutions nor proves a turbulent 4/5 law, critical-endpoint Onsager theorem or positive anomalous dissipation. The inequality's proof and sharp distinction from empirical scaling are the promised content.

## G1 — Rotation, stratification and shallow-water models

Adopt a rotating Newtonian frame with constant angular velocity Ω and choose an effective gravitational potential Φ_eff absorbing the centrifugal term on the considered region; Coriolis acceleration is −2Ω×u and does zero work because u·(Ω×u)=0. The choice of frame, effective potential and domain are physical assumptions; inertial-frame balance remains distinct. For a Boussinesq branch adopt constant reference density in inertia/divergence, advected buoyancy b in force, and a specified background stratification. Small fractional density variation is an applicability condition, not a proved compressible limit.

The inviscid linear constant-stratification model on T³ is u_t+f e3×u+∇π=b e3, divu=0, b_t+N²u3=0, N>0. Dot momentum with u and the buoyancy equation with b/N², integrate and cancel ∫bu3; pressure and Coriolis terms vanish. Thus (1/2)∫(|u|²+b²/N²) is conserved for smooth periodic solutions. ν,κ≥0 diffusion terms yield dissipation ν||∇u||²+κ||∇b||²/N². The positivity N²>0 matters; negative stratification does not have this positive energy. N has units s⁻¹, b acceleration, π specific pressure. A discrete skew Coriolis operator and compatible pressure/divergence pairing inherit this algebra only if the actual discretization has those properties.

Adopt separately the one-layer inviscid shallow-water model on T², depth h>0, horizontal velocity v, fixed flat bottom and g>0: h_t+div(hv)=0, (hv)_t+div(hv⊗v)+(g/2)∇h²+fh e3×v=0. Define energy E=h|v|²/2+gh²/2. Continuity and momentum give E_t+div((E+gh²/2)v)=0: kinetic balance contributes −gv·h∇h and potential balance contributes −gh² divv/2 after writing flux, which combine into the displayed conservative form. More directly hDtv=−gh∇h−fh e3×v; combine with Dth=−hdivv and expand. This is an exact smooth adopted-model identity; it is not a hydrostatic/aspect-ratio error bound or a positivity guarantee of an arbitrary discretization. Rossby Ro=U/(|f|L) for f≠0, Froude Fr=U/√(gH), aspect ratio H/L and the boundary assumptions must be explicit in any asymptotic discussion.

## M1 — Diffuse interfaces and total-energy exchange

A concrete two-phase model on T^d uses smooth incompressible u, phase φ, chemical potential μc=ε⁻¹W′(φ)−εΔφ, ε>0, W∈C² bounded below, mobility M>0 and ν>0. Equations are φ_t+u·∇φ=MΔμc and u_t+u·∇u+∇π=νΔu−φ∇μc, divu=0. These are dimensionless mathematical equations; dimensional physical use must specify density, phase scale, surface-energy coefficient, length/time scales and physical chemical-potential units before identification. φ labels a diffuse order parameter, not necessarily a literal probability or positive mass density.

Set E=∫(|u|²/2+ε|∇φ|²/2+ε⁻¹W(φ)). Differentiating phase energy and integrating by parts gives ∫μcφ_t=−∫μcu·∇φ−M∫|∇μc|². Momentum gives (1/2)d||u||²/dt=−ν∫|∇u|²−∫φu·∇μc. Since divu=0, ∫μcu·∇φ=−∫φu·∇μc; the two coupling contributions cancel exactly. Hence E′=−ν||∇u||²−M||∇μc||². Sign conventions for capillary forcing matter. Boundaries require compatible no-slip/no-flux conditions and actual boundary work; periodic proof cannot silently acquire contact-angle conditions.

For W(φ)=(φ²−1)²/4 the energy does not force |φ|≤1 pointwise. The fourth-order Cahn–Hilliard equation has no generic maximum-principle/positivity theorem from this identity. This energy identity is conditional on smooth solutions and is not a proof of global smooth 3D coupled flow, sharp-interface convergence, contact-line selection or physical immiscibility. Those remain separate prerequisite/theory obligations. A physical postulate adopting this free energy and mobility is a model, with empirical calibration kept separate from the mathematical dissipation theorem.

## B1 — Wall defects, boundary layers and capillary surfaces

For a bounded C² D, assume smooth incompressible exact u and comparison v have identical prescribed no-slip trace g and viscosity ν>0, with a common forcing. Let the residual of v in the full momentum equation be r∈L¹_tL²_x and let e=v−u; e has zero boundary trace. The same subtraction as R1 gives (1/2)(||e||²)′+ν||∇e||²≤||∇u||∞||e||²+||r||2||e||2 because the transport boundary flux is (v·n)|e|²/2=0 and the pressure boundary term is zero. Thus R1's integrating-factor bound holds with the actual bounded-domain norms. It is conditional on a smooth reference, common wall data and a controlled continuous residual.

For approximate field w with mismatched trace, first supply a smooth divergence-free boundary lift l with trace w−g and define v=w−l. Explicitly assume such l exists, with stated regularity/norms; a zero net flux is necessary, but no arbitrary-data lifting theorem is hidden here. An explicit channel branch has l(t,x,y,z)=(a(t)(1−y/h)+b(t)y/h,0,0), periodic x,z, whose divergence is identically zero, trace (a,0,0)/(b,0,0), and every required derivative is elementary. Compute the residual of v from the definition in R1, including l_t, Δl and all convection cross terms, before applying the estimate. Then ||w−u||2≤||l||2+||v−u||2. This repairs the common error of using homogeneous-wall energy estimates on an approximation with nonzero wall defect. It is not an existence theorem for general Prandtl profiles or a viscosity-uniform no-slip inviscid limit. The inherited exact diffusion layer retains its quantified penetration thickness, corner exclusion and nonuniform wall limit.

A capillary-gravity free surface has a separately adopted linear potential model in −h<z<0: Δφ=0, φ_z(−h)=0, η_t=φ_z(0), φ_t(0)+gη−(σ/ρ)η_xx=0, where h,g,ρ>0, σ≥0. Here σ is surface tension in N/m, φ in m²/s and η in m. With k>0 put η=a cos(kx−ωt), φ=(aω/(k tanh(kh)))[cosh(k(z+h))/cosh(kh)]sin(kx−ωt). Direct derivatives give the kinematic condition and the dynamic condition exactly when ω²=(gk+σk³/ρ)tanh(kh). The σ=0 limit agrees with the inherited gravity-wave theorem. The energy per horizontal period is (ρ/2)∫_{−h}^0|∇φ|²+(ρg/2)∫η²+(σ/2)∫η_x². Green's formula gives its kinetic derivative ρ∫φ_t(0)φ_z(0); differentiate the two surface terms, integrate η_xη_xt by parts, substitute the two boundary conditions and obtain zero. This justifies the restoring sign and conserved linear energy. No nonlinear free-boundary or sharp-interface convergence theorem follows from this calculation. Moving-mesh methods must use existing ALE transport/geometric-conservation suppliers with actual face kinematics; remeshing or topology change needs additional transfer/error contracts.
