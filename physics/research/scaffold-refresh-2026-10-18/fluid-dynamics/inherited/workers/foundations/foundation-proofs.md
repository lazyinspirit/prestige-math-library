## F00 — Geometry, types, units and physical adoption

## F01 — Inverse-label calculus, Jacobian and transport (mathematics)

## F03 — Cauchy contact, local momentum and angular momentum

The mathematical local-contact lemma assumes continuous bounded t(x,n), continuous bounded volume/inertial force densities, and zero total contact-plus-volume resultant on arbitrarily small regions in the balance sense. Under F00 P-MOM these follow from smooth material acceleration. To use face polyhedra while balance is stated for C¹ boundaries, round edges at relative width δh; removed and added surface areas are O(δh²), volumes O(h³), and bounded traction bounds their force error by O(δh²). Take δ↓0 for fixed h before h↓0. This extension avoids silently invoking a C¹ surface theorem on corners.

For fixed-size planar cross-section of scale h and thickness εh, opposite faces contribute area O(h²); side areas O(εh²), volume O(εh³). Let ε↓0, then h↓0. Continuity yields t(x,n)+t(x,−n)=0. For a tetrahedron of diameter h with oblique face n in a fixed coordinate octant, its coordinate opposite faces have normals −sign(n_i)e_i and areas |n_i|A, obtained by projecting oriented face area onto the coordinate planes. Balance and oddness give A[t(x,n)−Σ_i n_i t(x,e_i)]+o(h²)+O(h³)=0. Divide by A proportional to h² and take h↓0. Define σ's i-th column as t(x,e_i); then t(x,n)=σ(x)n. Zero coordinates follow continuity; σ is unique because its action on e_i fixes all columns. This proof requires orientation-only local traction and bounded volume forces; it fails for couples or curvature-dependent singular surface forces.

With σ C¹, divergence rowwise, transport and continuity give ρD_tu=ρb+div σ, equivalently ∂t(ρu)+div(ρu⊗u−σ)=ρb. This is an algebraic integral/PDE equivalence; the physics theorem adopts mass/contact/momentum. For H_i=ε_{ijk}x_jρu_k, expand ∂_l(ε_{ijk}x_jσ_{kl})=ε_{ilk}σ_{kl}+ε_{ijk}x_j∂_lσ_{kl}. For i=1 this is σ32−σ23=0, and cyclic indices give all off-diagonal symmetries. Conversely symmetric stress makes this residual zero, proving angular integral balance from linear balance. No intrinsic spin/couple balance is claimed.

For a C¹ auxiliary motion of a control region C(t), with boundary velocity w [m/s], transport with w and the local PDE give d/dt∫_Cρ=−∮ρ(u−w)·n and d/dt∫_Cρu=∫_Cρb+∮σn−∮ρu((u−w)·n). ## F04 — Total, kinetic and internal energy

Given smooth mass/momentum and symmetric σ, dot momentum with u: ρD_tk=ρb·u+u·div σ. Product rule gives u·div σ=div(σᵀu)−σ:L, while σ:L=σ:D because symmetric and skew contractions cancel pairwise. Thus ∂t(ρk)+div(ρku−σᵀu)=ρb·u−σ:D. Localizing P-ENERGY gives ∂t(ρE)+div(ρEu−σᵀu+q)=ρb·u+ρr. Subtract the kinetic identity to obtain ρD_te=σ:D−div q+ρr. Conversely adding these identities and integrating recovers total energy. This distinguishes kinetic loss from destruction of total energy; heat generated by viscosity is stored in e.

If b=−∇V for a time-independent C¹ specific potential V [J/kg], then ρD_tV=ρu·∇V, so source ρb·u cancels on adding potential energy to E. A time-dependent V leaves source ρ∂tV [W/m³]. For fixed closed no-slip walls u=0 and insulating q·n=0, or periodic domains, with r=0, total energy including such time-independent potential is constant whenever a sufficiently smooth solution exists; surface power/flux vanish. Without an e equation the mechanical incompressible model only has a kinetic-energy decay theorem and is not a closed thermodynamic model.

## F06 — Conservative weak and interface formulations (mathematics)
