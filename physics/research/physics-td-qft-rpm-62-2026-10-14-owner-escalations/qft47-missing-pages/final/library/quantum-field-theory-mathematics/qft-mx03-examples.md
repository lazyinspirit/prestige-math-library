The first example evaluates the balanced one-charge angular coefficient at velocity $v=1/2$ and an energy cutoff ratio $e$, obtaining exact coherent-sector squared norms, finite free-energy expectation and a projective-ray obstruction. These are mathematical Hilbert-space quantities; they carry no Born interpretation or measured photon-emission claim.

The second example chooses the subtraction scale equal to the mass scale, so its selected subtracted integral tends to zero while the positive bare integral grows quadratically. Its separate factorial toy series has finite coefficients and zero convergence radius. Neither conclusion asserts a complete renormalization or continuum-existence theorem.
