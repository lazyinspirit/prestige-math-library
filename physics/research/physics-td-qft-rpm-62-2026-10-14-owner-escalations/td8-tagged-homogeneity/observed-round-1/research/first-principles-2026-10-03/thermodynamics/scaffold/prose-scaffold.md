## Adopted framework and primitive quantities come first

The principal route is **macroscopic equilibrium thermodynamics in a concave entropy representation**, for ordinary stable, additive, bulk, simple compressible systems in a positive-temperature sector. It adopts physical postulates; it is not deduced from unspecified mechanics, quantum mechanics, or data. A separate accessibility route explains what stronger operational axioms can prove. Statistical mechanics is a separate interpretation branch with additional microscopic and probability assumptions. The routes share conditional results only where an explicit identification is made.

Here “ordinary” names this model class only; it is not a universal physical classification. “Stable” means a global entropy maximum on the explicitly declared feasible set for the declared totals and permitted exchanges; a local maximum, constrained local maximum, or long-lived metastable preparation is not thereby globally stable. “Additive” means that for the specified separated bodies the model sets total energy and entropy equal to the sums of the constituent values, after neglecting the named interface and long-range interaction terms. “Bulk” means a tagged family of material systems with an explicitly given positive scaling map on extensive coordinates and an entropy homogeneity postulate; it does not assert exact finite-size scaling. “Simple compressible” means that the only mechanical generalized coordinate admitted in the smooth equilibrium chart is volume and its adopted reversible mechanical work is −p dV; composition, fields, polarization, surface, and other work coordinates require their own declared extension. The positive-temperature sector is the region where the entropy derivative S_U exists, is finite and strictly positive, so T=1/S_U is finite and positive.

A material constitution c has r named species. Its equilibrium coordinate domain D_c is a physically specified subset of the numerical SI coordinate space (U,V,n_1,…,n_r)∈R^(r+2), with internal energy U in J, volume V in m³, and mole amounts n_j in mol. U is the energy in the center-of-mass rest frame, excluding bulk translation and external-field potential energy; its numerical zero, including any subtracted or retained constituent rest-energy offsets, is fixed by the material model and held consistently across allowed composition changes. For a fixed closed body of mass M in kg and center-of-mass velocity v_cm in m/s, a Galilean boost by b changes the separately tracked kinetic term to K′_cm=½M|v_cm−b|²; it leaves this internal U, V, and the equilibrium scalar functions below unchanged. The principal process class holds the omitted bulk/field energy changes fixed at zero. Records with nonzero bulk kinetic or external-potential energy changes require an expanded state energy balance and are outside the balance postulate below. In the bulk continuous approximation n_j>0 are real mole amounts, not exact particle counts; an absent-species boundary n_j=0 belongs to a separately stated closed feasible domain and is not part of the smooth positive-mole chart. For fixed amounts n, Γ_(c,n) is a specified nonempty open convex subset of the (U,V) slice, with V>0. When amounts vary, D_c and its convexity are stated for the particular claim; no formula assumes every formal coordinate point is realizable. Convexity uses affine combinations with dimensionless coefficients in these fixed numerical units. An admitted equilibrium preparation set E_c and representation map π_c:E_c→D_c identify each preparation with its coordinate state; D_c is the image of π_c. U, V, and fixed composition n are primitive coordinates here; n does not vary inside Γ_(c,n). Extra work coordinates a_i require separately declared state spaces, units, and exchange rules.

A process record is a tuple P=(I_P,x_P^-,x_P^+,z_P,c_P,r_P): I_P=[t_0,t_1] is a closed time interval measured in seconds, x_P^-,x_P^+∈D_c are the endpoint representations, z_P:I_P→Z_c records the system together with its named auxiliaries and devices, c_P records controls and contacts, and r_P records boundary transfers. Z_c is the model-declared configuration space of the system, auxiliaries, and devices; c_P is a control/contact record; r_P is the signed transfer record, with heat and work components assigned by the protocol below and any other component separately named and unit-typed. The admitted record set is part of the model; z_P has no regularity requirement unless a process subclass states one. The trajectory may leave D_c and may include nonequilibrium states. In this core, 𝒫_cl is the admitted set of closed records whose center-of-mass kinetic and external-potential energy changes are zero. Other records require an expanded state energy balance and are outside this 𝒫_cl. Operational assignment maps Q,W_on:𝒫_cl→R have numerical values in joules under the declared transfer protocol. Q[P]>0 means heat enters the system and W_on[P]>0 means work is done on it. They depend on the process record, not only its endpoints. A process is adiabatic exactly when Q[P]=0. A closed process forbids matter exchange across the system boundary; an open process permits only named matter fluxes; an isolated compound permits no external energy or matter exchange. “System” means the chosen body or collection bounded by that declared boundary; “preparation” means the experimentally or mathematically specified rule that selects its initial state or probability law, not a claim that subsequent dynamics equilibrates it.

A process P is reversible when there is an admitted reverse record P^rev from x_P^+ to x_P^- that restores its auxiliaries and devices to their initial states and reverses the boundary energy transfers, so Q[P^rev]=−Q[P] and W_on[P^rev]=−W_on[P]. Reversibility is relative to the declared device boundary; a slow or quasistatic process need not be reversible. A quasistatic equilibrium path is a piecewise-C¹ curve γ:[a,b]→D_c generated by a declared limiting protocol. A named bath b has an open energy interval 𝒰_b⊂R measured in J and an entropy function S_b:𝒰_b→R measured in J/K, with S_b∈C¹ and S′_b(U)>0; its temperature is T_b(U)=1/S′_b(U) in K. An ideal bath holds T_b fixed, which idealizes infinite heat capacity or controlled regulation; finite baths generally have varying T_b. The relevant contact family 𝒯 is a declared subset of the disjoint union of equilibrium state domains, including named material types. For x,y∈𝒯, x~_T y means the admitted diathermal contact preparation of the pair is an equilibrium contact with no permitted net heat transfer. This relation is postulated to be an equivalence relation. That postulate alone supplies no real thermometer scale or differentiable temperature function.

Postulates for the principal route, with each intended as a source-reviewed physical item:

1. **Equilibrium description and first-law energy.** The admitted equilibrium domain is Γ_(c,n) or the designated variable-composition domain D_c. U is the adopted internal-energy coordinate. For every P∈𝒫_cl from x=x_P^- to y=x_P^+, U(y)−U(x)=Q[P]+W_on[P] in the stated process class. Thus an adiabatic process has W_on[P]=U(y)−U(x). Records with nonzero center-of-mass kinetic or external-potential energy change require a separately declared enlarged balance and are not covered by this equation. Connectivity/reachability needed to construct U operationally is not claimed; U and this balance law are adopted. For weakly interacting separated systems, total internal energy is additive after neglecting interface and long-range interaction energy.
2. **Entropy fundamental relation.** On the specified equilibrium domain adopt S:D_c→R_(J/K), finite, continuous, and concave on the stated convex regions, additive for independent separated macroscopic systems, and degree-one homogeneous on the declared bulk scaling family. On each smooth single-phase open region take S∈C² and S_U>0. Where only C¹ or concavity is justified, derivative formulas are restricted accordingly. S is a primitive fundamental relation in this route: specifying its form is constitutive information, not a consequence of the word entropy.
3. **Equilibrium and allowed constraints.** When an isolated compound is allowed to relax with given totals and retained partitions, the equilibrium macrostate maximizes the sum of the constituent entropies on the explicit feasible set. This includes an existence assumption or an independently proved attainment statement. Equilibrium is global stable equilibrium under those permissions; local maxima describe restricted/metastable states only after a separate dynamical or kinetic scope statement.
4. **Macroscopic second law.** For admitted isolated macroscopic relaxation or adiabatic accessibility, total entropy of the system and included auxiliaries is nondecreasing. A reversible admissible operation has zero total entropy change. Irreversibility permits strict increase of system entropy in an adiabatic process but does not require strict increase for every irreversible operation.
5. **Thermal contact and zeroth law.** The relation ~_T is adopted as an equivalence relation realized by the admitted contacts. Compatible temperature equality is subsequently deduced from the entropy maximum plus regularity. Transitivity alone does not produce a real numerical, let alone differentiable, temperature coordinate.
6. **Low-temperature branch.** Any Nernst or unattainability assertion is an additional scoped postulate, not a consequence of 1–5. It applies only to stated equilibrium families and permitted cooling operations. Its alternative formulations and residual-entropy exceptions are kept explicit.

The choice to postulate entropy rather than derive it is transparent. Another route may derive entropy from accessibility or a specified microscopic model. On a smooth single-phase open region Ω⊂D_c, S∈C² and S_U,S_V,S_nj are partial derivatives at fixed remaining coordinates, with units K^−1, J K^−1 m^−3, and J K^−1 mol^−1. Define positive scalar temperature T=(S_U)^−1 in K, pressure p=T S_V in Pa, and molar chemical potential μ_j=−T S_nj in J mol^−1. For dimensionless particle count N_j, set n_j=N_j/N_A and μ_j^(particle)=μ_j/N_A in J per particle. These are derived state functions only on charts where the derivatives exist. They are Galilean scalars of the rest-frame equilibrium state; this framework assumes no relativistic temperature law or observer-dependent tensor. An empirical thermometer is a declared calibration map from a specified reading domain to positive real temperatures, with reference preparations and uncertainty stated; it is not automatically the absolute T above. Pressure may instead be primitive as an operational force per area; identifying it with T S_V needs the mechanical coupling stated below.

Extensivity is a precise part of the chosen bulk family. For each λ>0 let c^(λ) denote the tagged scaled material type and δ_λ(U,V,n)=(λU,λV,λn) the declared scaling map into its state domain. The postulate is S_(c^(λ))(δ_λx)=λS_c(x) for admitted x; it does not identify the scaled material with the original one. For independent separated bodies A and B with interface and long-range interaction energy neglected, the composition map sends (x_A,x_B) to the compound state, with U_tot=U_A+U_B and S_tot=S_A+S_B. These are model postulates, not consequences of the units. A different formulation or nonadditive system must state its own scaling and composition maps.

The reversible heat identity is conditional on a bath coupling. For an admitted reversible quasistatic protocol with a C¹ equilibrium path x(t), an absolutely continuous bath energy U_b(t), no matter or work transfer to the bath, and integrable heat rate q̇(t) into the system in J/s, energy conservation gives U̇_b=−q̇ almost everywhere. Additivity and zero total entropy change give dS(x(t))/dt+S′_b(U_b(t))U̇_b(t)=0 almost everywhere. Since S′_b=1/T_b, this yields q̇=T_b dS(x(t))/dt; at thermal contact equilibrium T_b=T(x(t)), so δQ_rev=T dS along this path. For these protocols Q[P]=∫q̇(t)dt; general Q[P] remains the process functional defined above. No such identity is asserted for irreversible transfer or an unspecified instantaneous temperature. For mechanical work, separately adopt the piston coupling: with outward displacement ℓ in m, piston area A in m², and quasistatic frictionless force balance, the normal force is pA in newtons and dV=A dℓ, so the work-on rate is −p V̇ and δW_on=−p dV. This identifies derivative-defined p with wall pressure only in that model. Other generalized-force identifications need their own couplings.

Use SI throughout the core: U,Q,W,F,H,G,Ω_grand (the grand potential) in joules J=kg m² s^−2; V in m³; S and heat capacities in J K^−1; T in kelvin K; p in pascals Pa=J m^−3; n_j in mol and μ_j in J mol^−1. For particle count N_j (dimensionless), use μ_j^(particle) in J and n_j=N_j/N_A; μ_j=N_A μ_j^(particle). Boltzmann's constant k_B=1.380649×10^−23 J K^−1 and Avogadro's constant N_A=6.02214076×10^23 mol^−1 are exact modern SI defining constants; R=N_A k_B=8.31446261815324 J mol^−1 K^−1 is derived. Absolute entropy scaling in J/K must be distinguished from an accessibility construction normalized to S(x_0)=0,S(x_1)=1, which is dimensionless until calibrated. Additive constants depend on material labels and permitted transformations; chemical reactions and mixing restrict compatible choices. The core does not set k_B=1. The microscopic branch introduces h=6.62607015×10^−34 J s (exact SI) and ℏ=h/(2π) in J s, together with any mass or Hamiltonian parameters explicitly. ## Composition, constraints, and accessibility are actual objects

For two equilibrium systems, the separated compound state space is Γ_A×Γ_B, with states (x_A,x_B). Under an additive bulk model U_tot=U_A+U_B and S_tot=S_A+S_B. An isolated diathermal contact at fixed constituent volumes and amounts has feasible energy partitions

C={u:(u,V_A,n_A)∈D_A, (U_tot-u,V_B,n_B)∈D_B}.

The entropy maximum is over u∈C. C need not be compact or nonempty; these must be checked before asserting an attained maximum. An impermeable movable partition also permits V_A+V_B=V_tot; a permeable partition permits each relevant n_Aj+n_Bj=n_tot,j. Retained walls fix the corresponding variables. There is no pressure or chemical-potential equality conclusion if the corresponding exchange is forbidden. If reactions are admitted, define a stoichiometric matrix ν=(ν_jα), reaction extents ξ∈R^k, and n=n^0+νξ with n_j≥0; an interior maximum gives Σ_jν_jα μ_j=0 for each allowed independent reaction. Boundary inequalities and absent species are separately analyzed.

An isolated compound plus its declared bath and work device is the object to which reversible entropy accounting is applied. The process record may include nonequilibrium intermediate states even when both endpoints lie in Γ_(c,n) or D_c.

## Heat, work, and the first law

Our sign is W_on positive for work on the system. A reversible simple-compressible displacement has δW_on=−p dV, with p the system pressure when mechanical equilibrium with the actuator is assumed. General finite compression uses the external generalized force and protocol, not a substituted equilibrium p along nonexistent intermediate states. Heat Q[P] is energy transfer assigned to thermal exchange under the operational first-law convention; it is not contained in a state as a second kind of energy. Within the admitted closed class, ΔU=Q+W_on; a cycle in that class has ΔU=0 while Q and W can each be nonzero. The mathematical counterexample M6 makes path dependence concrete.

For a fixed-composition reversible quasistatic path in the piston model, the first law and the bath/work couplings give dU=T dS−p dV. On a smooth variable-composition fundamental-relation chart, rearranging dS=S_UdU+S_VdV+Σ_jS_njdn_j gives the state differential dU=T dS−p dV+Σ_jμ_jdn_j. The last term compares equilibrium states of different composition; it is not a universal matter-energy flux law. Open control volumes require separately defined energy/enthalpy fluxes and kinetic/potential-energy terms. The core confines matter-exchange results to equilibrium variation and reaction equilibrium.

## Entropy and energy representations, temperature, and stability

For S_U>0 the scalar inverse M3 gives a local C² energy representation U=U(S,V,n). Then U_S=T, U_V=−p, U_nj=μ_j by differentiating S(U(S,V,n),V,n)=S. Global inversion needs monotonicity and domain-image coverage, not just a local nonzero derivative. Smooth regularity is restricted to one phase region; the macroscopic fundamental function across coexistence may have affine portions and lose strictness. Negative-temperature statistical models require bounded spectra and separately stated applicability and contact rules; they are not silently inserted into the positive-temperature family.

Concavity of S and the maximum principle make equilibrium exchange conditions conditional deductions. At an interior diathermal equilibrium, S_A,U=S_B,U, so T_A=T_B. Allowing volume exchange also yields p_A/T_A=p_B/T_B, hence p_A=p_B; allowing species exchange yields μ_Aj/T_A=μ_Bj/T_B and μ_Aj=μ_Bj. These statements need a common entropy calibration and positive finite T. M2 gives global sufficiency of a stationary point under concavity and uniqueness under strict concavity on the permitted slice. Hessian negative semidefiniteness is the C² concavity criterion; it is not strict stability. Homogeneity forces a Hessian null scaling direction (M4). Boundary equilibria use inequalities or supergradients rather than the interior equalities.

For fixed V,n, C_V=(∂U/∂T)_(V,n)=T(∂S/∂T)_(V,n), where a valid temperature chart exists. C_V>0 follows from U_SS>0 and T>0 on this slice; the inverse derivative and finite response are hypotheses. Define κ_T=−(1/V)(∂V/∂p)_(T,n), α=(1/V)(∂V/∂T)_(p,n), and C_p=(∂H/∂T)_(p,n). M8 proves C_p−C_V=TVα²/κ_T on the declared C² chart with T,V>0 and p_V|_(T,n)<0, equivalently κ_T>0, by chained derivative transformations. Response inequalities across ensembles require their own constraints; they cannot be imported from grand-canonical variances without model identification.

## Legendre potentials and reversible-cycle deductions

Define H=U+pV, F=U−TS, G=U−TS+pV and Ω_grand=U−TS−Σμ_jn_j as scalar functions. Their natural-variable forms require invertible coordinate maps. In those valid charts,

dH=T dS+V dp+Σμ_jdn_j; dF=−S dT−p dV+Σμ_jdn_j; dG=−S dT+V dp+Σμ_jdn_j; dΩ_grand=−S dT−p dV−Σn_jdμ_j.

These follow by product rule and cancellation, not by a new law. Potentials give constrained equilibrium minima by including an ideal reservoir's entropy or by the smooth convex minimization argument M5. An imposed reservoir fixes T or p and changes the correct feasible set; a blanket rule that every system minimizes every potential is false. Homogeneity gives U=TS−pV+Σμ_jn_j and Gibbs–Duhem S dT−V dp+Σn_jdμ_j=0 only in the specified bulk extensive model.

C² mixed-partial equality yields Maxwell relations: (∂T/∂V)_(S,n)=−(∂p/∂S)_(V,n), (∂T/∂p)_(S,n)=(∂V/∂S)_(p,n), (∂S/∂V)_(T,n)=(∂p/∂T)_(V,n), and (∂S/∂p)_(T,n)=−(∂V/∂T)_(p,n). Further chemical relations follow pairwise from the same Hessian symmetry. Each derivative is taken in the stated valid coordinate chart at fixed displayed coordinates; these identities are local on open charts where the relevant Legendre map is invertible. For this scaffold’s Maxwell/response consumer, the supplier map selects published `thm-clairaut-schwarz-mixed-partials` (C² scalar function on an open Euclidean domain); its Peano proof uses the published rectangle second-difference lemma. The existing local M5 derivation also has a complete route through the separately published rectangular-difference Clairaut lemma, whose proof is sound and whose closure is published; M8 consumes the resulting local mixed-partial identity. These two source routes have the same C² chart scope, so no mathematical defect is alleged. Closed-form potential differentials are exact where defined; cross-partial equality alone on a multiply connected domain does not prove global exactness.

For a cyclic engine exchanging heat Q_h>0 with an ideal bath T_h and rejecting Q_c>0 to a bath T_c<T_h, energy balance gives work output W=Q_h−Q_c. Total entropy change of the baths is −Q_h/T_h+Q_c/T_c≥0 if the engine returns to its initial state and no omitted auxiliary changes occur; thus η=W/Q_h≤1−T_c/T_h. Equality requires reversible total exchange. For a refrigerator W_on>0 and Q_c>0 extracted from the cold bath, the same accounting gives COP≤T_c/(T_h−T_c). For finite concave reservoirs, M10 gives an initial-temperature upper bound using the tangent inequality; LY pp67–68 instead prints a final-temperature bound with the wrong concavity direction. That extension is rejected, with an explicit hypothetical constant-capacity witness in M10. These are conditional thought-experiment/theorem items; no measured engine performance is invented. Clausius ∮δQ/T≤0 requires defining the reservoir/boundary temperatures and admitted cycle decomposition; it is not proved by treating arbitrary δQ/T_system as an exact differential.

## Third-law scope

For an explicit Nernst heat-theorem branch specify an equilibrium-state map φ:(0,T_0)×K→D_+ with T(φ(t,a))=t, where T_0>0, K is a declared compact parameter space of fixed external constraints, and D_+ is the positive-temperature equilibrium domain. Define S_K(t,a)=S(φ(t,a)) and postulate sup_(a,b∈K)|S_K(t,a)−S_K(t,b)|→0 as t↓0; this uniform formulation and its parameter family are additional assumptions. A finite zero-temperature entropy value S_K(0,a) is defined only if the separate limit lim_(t↓0)S_K(t,a) exists and is finite. Weaker pairwise limits quantify only the stated pairs. A Planck-style convention instead assigns absolute entropy zero to a unique accessible ground state in the declared microscopic model; that is not a proof of the Nernst theorem for arbitrary equilibrium families. Unattainability is a separate operational postulate and requires an explicit zero-temperature boundary space. Declare a nonempty set D_0 of zero-temperature equilibrium preparations, the disjoint augmented state space D̄=D_+⊔D_0, and an extended temperature map T̄:D̄→[0,∞) with T̄|D_+=T and T̄(y)=0 for y∈D_0. For a declared cooling-protocol family 𝒞, let Reach_𝒞⊆D_+×D̄ be the relation of endpoint pairs connected by a finite chain of admitted operations, each with declared controls and total duration τ∈[0,∞) seconds; the operational unattainability assertion is Reach_𝒞∩(D_+×D_0)=∅. If the model has no declared D_0 or the protocols are defined to stay in D_+ by construction, this assertion is absent or vacuous, not evidence for unattainability. A restricted cycle model establishes only its own reachability result. None of these formulations is equivalent to the others without additional assumptions. Along a declared fixed-constraint family define C(t)=t∂_tS_K(t,a) where the derivative exists; C has units J K^−1. The identity S_K(T,a)−S_K(0,a)=∫_0^T C(T′)/T′ dT′ requires the derivative identity, the finite zero-limit definition above, and convergence. Integrability does not imply an integer power law or C∝T^n for n≥1; C(T)∝√T is a counterexample. A finite ground-state degeneracy g means the ground eigenspace has dimension g; k_B log g is the residual entropy only in a model whose canonical low-temperature limit and entropy convention yield it. Disorder, glass kinetics, and unresolved degeneracy require separate scope; thermodynamics alone does not prove universal S(0)=0. Boundary T=0 is excluded from charts dividing by T.

## Phases, finite size, and the thermodynamic limit

A phase in this scaffold is a declared branch of homogeneous equilibrium preparations with stated constitutive regularity. Coexistence means an admissible mixture of the named phase branches that attains the global entropy maximum for the declared totals, constraints, and exchange permissions. Only at an interior maximum with the corresponding exchanges allowed do the relevant intensive variables agree. For one-component phases at fixed composition per mole, define molar Gibbs potential g=G/n (J mol^−1), where G=U−TS+pV and n>0 is amount of substance in mol; equal g_a(T,p)=g_b(T,p) is the coexistence condition in a bulk T,p chart. If both phase branches are C¹, share an open coexistence chart, and Δv=v_b−v_a≠0 with v_i=V_i/n_i and s_i=S_i/n_i, differentiating equality gives dp/dT=Δs/Δv. Reversible conversion at fixed T,p has molar latent heat TΔs under the specified heat coupling. Critical points with Δv=0 are outside that division.

For the local regular Gibbs phase rule, take r named species, P coexisting phases, and R independent reactions.
