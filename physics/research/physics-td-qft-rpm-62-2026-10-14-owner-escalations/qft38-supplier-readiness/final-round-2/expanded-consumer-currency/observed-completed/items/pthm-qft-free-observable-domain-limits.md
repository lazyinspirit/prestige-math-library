## Statement

Assume Countable Choice, $\mu\ge0$, and the selected free-field model of
[[post-qft-free-field-model]]. Use the four-dimensional affine Minkowski chart,
signature $\eta=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$, length coordinate $x^0=ct$,
and inverse-length parameter $\mu$ of [[def-qft-framework-units]]. For an open
region $O\subseteq\mathbb R^4$, use the scalar and Dirac test functions and
bounded local observable algebras defined in
[[def-qft-free-field-observables]]: scalar tests are real members of
$C_c^\infty(O)$, Dirac tests are members of
$C_c^\infty(O;\mathbb C^4)$, and the Dirac observable algebra is generated by
even products of bounded smeared CAR fields and their adjoints.

For a real Schwartz scalar test $f$, the closure of $\Phi_\mu(f)$ is
self-adjoint on its exact Segal-field multiplier domain. The scalar number,
Hamiltonian and momentum operators and the Dirac Hamiltonian and momentum
operators have the exact maximal sector-sum domains stated in
[[lem-qft-free-domain-observables]]. In particular,
$$D(N_s)=\left\{\Psi=(\Psi_n):\sum_{n\ge1}n^2\|\Psi_n\|^2<\infty\right\}.$$
If $A$ is one of these self-adjoint operators and $\Psi$ is normalized with
$\Psi\in D(A)$, then its model mean $\langle\Psi,A\Psi\rangle$ and variance
$$\operatorname{Var}_\Psi(A)=\|(A-\langle\Psi,A\Psi\rangle)\Psi\|^2$$
are finite; this needs $\Psi\in D(A)$, not $\Psi\in D(A^2)$. The product
$AB\Psi$ is defined only when $\Psi\in D(B)$ and $B\Psi\in D(A)$.

If $O_1,O_2$ are open regions and
$\eta_{ab}(x-y)^a(x-y)^b>0$ for every $x\in O_1$ and $y\in O_2$, then the
scalar Weyl algebras of the two regions commute, and the even local Dirac
algebras of the two regions commute. These are algebraic properties of the
specified free representations.

The chosen construction supplies only test-smeared fields and does not define
$\Phi_\mu(x)$, $\Psi(x)$, or coincident diagonal products. Fix a point
$x_0\in\mathbb R^4$ and a nonnegative normalized Schwartz Gaussian
$\rho\in\mathscr S(\mathbb R^4)$, and put
$\rho_{\epsilon,x_0}(x)=\epsilon^{-4}\rho((x-x_0)/\epsilon)$. These tests
approximate $\delta_{x_0}$ on Schwartz functions, but
$\|\Phi_\mu(\rho_{\epsilon,x_0})\Omega\|\to\infty$ as $\epsilon\downarrow0$.
For an equal-time Dirac field, fix a unit spinor $z$ and a nonnegative
normalized Schwartz Gaussian $\sigma$ on $\mathbb R^3$, and put
$u_{\epsilon,x_0}(\mathbf x)=\epsilon^{-3}
\sigma((\mathbf x-\mathbf x_0)/\epsilon)z$. These tests approximate a
spatial point mass, while $\|\Psi_t(u_{\epsilon,x_0})\|\to\infty$. Neither
family has a finite limit in its respective Fock or operator-norm topology.
This restriction does not rule out other distributional or renormalized
constructions. The Born interpretation of
[[post-qft-constructed-model-and-measurement]] applies only after a density
state and normalized finite bounded instrument have actually been supplied;
this item reports no apparatus or outcome.

## Proof

**Given:** [A1] and [F1]–[F11].

1.1 The adopted physical systems are exactly the two selected Fock
representations in [F2], and [F1] fixes the spacetime and unit convention.
The test spaces and meanings of $\mathfrak A_s(O)$ and $\mathfrak A_D(O)$ are
those defined in [F4]; thus all fields used here are smeared and all Dirac
local observables are even products. [A1, F1, F2, F4, given]

1.2 Apply [F6] to the listed real scalar smearings and sector-sum generators.
It supplies their exact maximal domains and, for every normalized
$\Psi\in D(A)$, finite $\langle\Psi,A\Psi\rangle$ and finite variance.
The same statement gives the product-domain condition, so no claim about
$D(A^2)$ or an everywhere-defined product follows. [F6, algebra]

1.3 The region hypotheses in the Statement are exactly the spacelike-support
hypotheses of [F8] and [F10]. The first gives commutativity of the scalar
Weyl algebras; the second gives commutativity of the bounded even Dirac local
algebras. If either region is empty, its test space is $\{0\}$ and
its unital generated algebra is $\mathbb C I$, which commutes with the other
algebra as well. [F4, F8, F10, given, algebra]

1.4 For every Schwartz $g$, changing variables gives
$\int\rho_{\epsilon,x_0}(x)g(x)\,d^4x=
\int\rho(y)g(x_0+\epsilon y)\,d^4y\to g(x_0)$ by dominated convergence [F11].
For every spatial Schwartz spinor $h$, the corresponding pairing is
$$\int u_{\epsilon,x_0}(\mathbf x)^\dagger h(\mathbf x)\,d^3\mathbf x=\int\sigma(y)z^\dagger h(\mathbf x_0+\epsilon y)\,d^3y\longrightarrow z^\dagger h(\mathbf x_0),$$
again by dominated convergence with the integrable bound
$\|h\|_\infty\sigma(y)$ [F11]. The equal-time Dirac operator in this family
is the $\Psi_t(u)$ of [F9].
The actual current proof of [F7] gives diverging scalar vacuum-vector norms
and equal-time Dirac operator norms for these Gaussian families. A norm-convergent vector or bounded-operator family
must have bounded norms, so neither family has such a limit. [F7, F9, F11, algebra]

2.1 The selected definition [F5] supplies no other point field or diagonal
product. The norm obstruction in step 1.4 is specific to the displayed
representations and topologies. [F5, step 1.4, algebra]

3.1 When the state and finite instrument required by [F3] are supplied, the
Born postulate assigns the stated model probabilities. No such state,
instrument, apparatus, or observation is introduced by this theorem, so it
establishes no empirical outcome. [F3, given] ∎
