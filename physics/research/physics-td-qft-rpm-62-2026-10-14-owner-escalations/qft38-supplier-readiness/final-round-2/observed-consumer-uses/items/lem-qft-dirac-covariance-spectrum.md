## Statement

Assume Countable Choice [[def-countable-choice]] and the mathematical free Dirac data of [[def-qft-dirac-projections]], [[lem-qft-dirac-projection-matrices]], and [[lem-qft-free-dirac-car-locality]]. Let $G=\mathbb R^4\rtimes SO^+(1,3)$ act by $x\mapsto a+\Lambda x$, with product $(a_2,\Lambda_2)(a_1,\Lambda_1)=(a_2+\Lambda_2a_1,\Lambda_2\Lambda_1)$. Let $\widetilde G$ be the subgroup of triples $(a,\Lambda,S)$ generated by translations $(a,I,I)$ and the explicit rotation/boost triples $(0,R_{\mathbf n}(\theta),S_R(\theta))$ and $(0,B_{\mathbf n}(\chi),S_B(\chi))$ of [[lem-qft-dirac-projection-matrices]], with product $(a_2,\Lambda_2,S_2)(a_1,\Lambda_1,S_1)=(a_2+\Lambda_2a_1,\Lambda_2\Lambda_1,S_2S_1)$ and the subspace topology from $\mathbb R^4\times M_4(\mathbb R)\times GL(4,\mathbb C)$. Its projection onto $G$ is surjective by [[lem-qft-lorentz-boost-rotation-factorization]]. For $\tilde g=(a,\Lambda,S)$ define the spinor test pullback
$$\tau_{\tilde g}F(x)=(S^{-1})^\dagger F(\Lambda^{-1}(x-a)).$$
The fermionic Fock space of [[def-qft-fock-and-test-spaces]] carries a strongly continuous unitary representation $U$ of $\widetilde G$ such that
$$U(\tilde g)\Psi(F)U(\tilde g)^{-1}=\Psi(\tau_{\tilde g}F).$$
Let $\mathcal A_D^{\mathrm{gr}}$ be the algebra generated by all bounded $\Psi(F)$ and $\Psi(F)^\dagger$ for Schwartz spinor tests $F$. The vacuum $\Omega$ is invariant and cyclic for $\mathcal A_D^{\mathrm{gr}}$. A $2\pi$ spatial spin rotation acts as $(-1)^N$ on the $n$-particle sector with value $(-1)^n$; a $4\pi$ rotation acts as the identity. The translation generators and joint spectral measure are those of [[lem-qft-dirac-fermionic-translation-spectrum]], so the joint spectrum is contained in the closed future cone, $\Omega$ is the unique zero-energy vector, and for $\mu>0$ the nonvacuum energy has gap at least $\mu$. The first-quantized $h_D$ has both signs of energy; this Fock-space generator is a different operator on a different Hilbert space. No general spin-statistics theorem or local cyclicity claim is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The fixed spinor test convention $\int F^\dagger\Psi$, the shell phase $p\circ x=\omega x^0-\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x$, explicit matrix lifts, proper orthochronous Lorentz geometry, and the actual draft supplier interfaces recorded below.

[A1] Countable Choice is the choice strength assumed for the completed Fock spaces. [[def-countable-choice]]

[F1] The Clifford matrices, $h_D$, $P_\pm$, particle and conjugate negative-energy subspaces, and the second-variable-linear pairing are those of [[def-qft-dirac-projections]].

[F2] The explicit $S_R,S_B$ obey $S\Gamma(p)S^{-1}=\Gamma(\Lambda p)$ and $S\beta S^\dagger=\beta$; each proper orthochronous $\Lambda$ factors into one boost and one rotation. [[lem-qft-dirac-projection-matrices]] [[lem-qft-lorentz-boost-rotation-factorization]]

[F3] The future mass shell is $p=(\omega(\mathbf k),\mathbf k)$ with $d\nu_\mu=d^3\mathbf k/((2\pi)^3 2\omega)$, and this measure is invariant under every proper orthochronous Lorentz map. [[def-qft-scalar-mass-shell]] [[lem-qft-mass-shell-measure-invariant]]

[F4] The free Dirac field is the bounded smeared CAR field defined by $\Psi(F)=c(P_+u_F)+c^\dagger(\overline{P_-u_F})$; $F\mapsto u_F$ is continuous from Schwartz space to $\mathcal H_D$, and its separate positive and negative kernels are the Fourier distributions $C_+$ and $C_-$ displayed there, with tested pairings $\langle P_+u_F,P_+u_G\rangle$ and $\langle P_-u_F,P_-u_G\rangle$. [[lem-qft-free-dirac-car-locality]]

[F5] The antisymmetric Fock sectors, finite-particle domain, creation formulas, and CAR relations use the conventions of [[def-qft-fock-and-test-spaces]] and [[lem-qft-fock-ccr-car]].

[F6] Smooth compactly supported vector functions are dense in $L^2$ on an open Euclidean set, componentwise. [[lem-smooth-compactly-supported-functions-are-dense-in-ltwo-of-an-open-set]]

[F7] The exact fermionic translation phases, maximal generator domains, joint PVM, forward-cone support, vacuum eigenspace and mass gap are established in [[lem-qft-dirac-fermionic-translation-spectrum]].

[F8] The spacetime metric, inertial coordinates, and proper orthochronous Lorentz group are those of [[def-sr-affine-minkowski-model]], [[def-inertial-frame-and-inertial-chart]], and [[def-lorentz-map-and-lorentz-group]].

[F9] The quarter-turn values give $\cos\pi=-1$, $\sin\pi=0$, $\cos2\pi=1$, and $\sin2\pi=0$. [[thm-quarter-turn-values-and-shift-formulas]]

## Proof



1.1 For $p=(\omega,\mathbf k)$, the projection identity in [F1] gives $P_+(\mathbf k)\beta=(\Gamma(p)+\mu)/(2\omega)$. For $q=(\omega,-\mathbf k)$ it gives $P_-(\mathbf k)\beta=(\Gamma(q)-\mu)/(2\omega)$. Thus, using $d\nu_\mu=d^3\mathbf k/((2\pi)^3 2\omega)$, the separate kernels in [F4] are $$C_+(z)=\int(\Gamma(p)+\mu)\beta e^{-ip\circ z}\,d\nu_\mu(p),\qquad C_-(z)=\int(\Gamma(q)-\mu)\beta e^{+iq\circ z}\,d\nu_\mu(q),$$ where $p\circ z=p^0z^0-\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf z$ and both $p,q$ range over the future mass shell. The matrix relations in [F2] and $S^{-1}\beta=\beta S^\dagger$ give $S(\Gamma(p)\pm\mu)\beta S^\dagger=(\Gamma(\Lambda p)\pm\mu)\beta$. In each shell integral, change variables $r=\Lambda p$ (or $r=\Lambda q$); invariance of $d\nu_\mu$ [F3] and $(\Lambda p)\circ z=p\circ(\Lambda^{-1}z)$ show $C_\pm(\Lambda z)=S C_\pm(z)S^\dagger$. The coefficients grow at most linearly on the shell, so these Fourier integrals define tempered distributions and their pairings with Schwartz tests converge. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F8, algebra]

2.1 For $F,G\in\mathscr S(\mathbb R^4;\mathbb C^4)$, [F4] identifies $\langle P_+u_F,P_+u_G\rangle$ and $\langle P_-u_F,P_-u_G\rangle$ with the respective kernel pairings $\int F(x)^\dagger C_\pm(x-y)G(y)\,d^4x\,d^4y$. Set $F'=\tau_{\tilde g}F$ and $G'=\tau_{\tilde g}G$. In each pairing, the spin factors are $S^{-1}$ and $S^{-\dagger}$; changing variables $x=a+\Lambda x'$, $y=a+\Lambda y'$ has unit Jacobian, cancels the translation in $x-y$, and the kernel identity of step 1.1 cancels both spin factors. Hence both polarized one-particle pairings are preserved, and the maps $P_\pm u_F\mapsto P_\pm u_{F'}$ are well-defined isometries on their test-generated subspaces. Applying the inverse group element proves they are onto after completion. For the conjugate negative-energy sector, conjugating the same preserved pairing and reversing its arguments gives the corresponding isometry on $\overline{\mathcal H_-}$. [F3, F4, F8, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 The test-generated subspaces are dense. For $u\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^3\setminus\{0\};\mathbb C^4)$, $P_\pm u$ is smooth and compactly supported away from the massless apex; such projected vectors are dense in $\mathcal H_\pm$ by [F1] and [F6], since removing the single point does not change an $L^2$ class. Choose $g\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$ with $\int g(t)dt=1$ and set $\widehat F(t,\mathbf k)=g(t)W_D(-t,\mathbf k)u(\mathbf k)$. This is smooth and compactly supported in $(t,\mathbf k)$; its inverse spatial Fourier transform is Schwartz by repeated integration by parts. The definition in [F4] then gives $u_F=u$, and hence $P_\pm u_F=P_\pm u$. [F1, F4, F6, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 By steps 2.1 and 2.2 the particle and conjugate antiparticle test maps extend to unitaries on $\mathcal H_+$ and $\overline{\mathcal H_-}$. Their direct sum $T(\tilde g)$ is unitary. Define $U(\tilde g)=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}T(\tilde g)^{\otimes_a n}$ on the antisymmetric Fock sectors. Each tensor power is unitary by its isometric action on elementary tensors and inverse tensor power; it commutes with the signed permutation average and therefore restricts to a unitary on the antisymmetric sector. The direct sum is unitary and fixes $\Omega$. On elementary wedge tensors the insertion/contraction formulas of [F5] give $U c^\dagger(h)U^{-1}=c^\dagger(Th)$ and $U c(h)U^{-1}=c(Th)$; density extends these identities to the finite-particle domain. Thus $U(\tilde g)\Psi(F)U(\tilde g)^{-1}=\Psi(\tau_{\tilde g}F)$. Direct substitution in the affine and spinor formulas gives $\tau_{\tilde g_2}\tau_{\tilde g_1}=\tau_{\tilde g_2\tilde g_1}$: the coordinate argument is $(\Lambda_2\Lambda_1)^{-1}(x-a_2-\Lambda_2a_1)$ and the matrix factor is $(S_2S_1)^{-\dagger}=S_2^{-\dagger}S_1^{-\dagger}$. Hence $T$ and its Fock lift $U$ obey the same product law and $U$ is a representation. [A1, F1, F4, F5, step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 The map $\tilde g\mapsto\tau_{\tilde g}F$ is continuous in Schwartz seminorms for each $F$, since it is composition with affine maps and finite matrices whose entries vary continuously. By the continuous test estimate of [F4], the one-particle representation is continuous on the dense test-generated subspaces of step 2.2; its operators have norm one, so approximation extends strong continuity to all one-particle vectors. On an elementary $n$-tensor, the difference of $T(g)^{\otimes n}$ and $T(g_0)^{\otimes n}$ is a sum of $n$ terms, each bounded by the norm of one factor difference times the norms of unchanged factors. It tends to zero as $g\to g_0$. Density and the unit norm bound extend this to each completed antisymmetric sector; finite-sector truncation then extends strong continuity to their direct sum. [F4, F5, step 2.2, step 3.1, algebra]

4.2 The range of $F\mapsto P_+u_F$ is dense in $\mathcal H_+$ and the range of $F\mapsto\overline{P_-u_F}$ is dense in $\overline{\mathcal H_-}$ by step 2.2. Applying $\Psi(F)^\dagger$ to $\Omega$ gives the particle component, and applying $\Psi(F)$ gives the antiparticle component. Products of these creation operators on $\Omega$ generate the algebraic antisymmetric tensors of their one-particle ranges; their span is dense in every sector by the definition of the Hilbert tensor completion. Finite-sector vectors are dense in Fock space, so $\Omega$ is cyclic for the global graded field algebra. [F1, F4, F5, step 2.2, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 For a pure translation, $\widehat{F(\cdot-a)}(t,\mathbf k)=\chi(-\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf a)\widehat F(t-a^0,\mathbf k)$, so $u_{F(\cdot-a)}=W_D(a^0,\mathbf k)\chi(-\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf a)u_F$. On $\mathcal H_+$ this is the phase $\chi(\omega a^0-\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf a)$; after conjugation on $\overline{\mathcal H_-}$ it is $\chi(\omega a^0+\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf a)$. Thus the induced Fock translations are precisely those of [F7], whose joint spectrum is in the forward cone, whose vacuum is the unique zero-energy vector, and whose positive-$\mu$ gap is at least $\mu$. By [F9], $S_R(2\pi)=-I_4$ and $S_R(4\pi)=I_4$; hence a $2\pi$ rotation acts as $-I$ on each one-particle summand and $(-1)^n$ on sector $n$, while a $4\pi$ rotation is the identity. The first-quantized $h_D$ has eigenvalues $\pm\omega$, while the Fock translation time generator in [F7] multiplies both particle and antiparticle excitations by $+\omega$; their domains and Hilbert spaces differ. [F1, F2, F7, F9, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
