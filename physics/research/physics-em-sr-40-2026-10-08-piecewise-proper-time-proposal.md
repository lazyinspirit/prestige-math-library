Complete proof route:

1. At alpha, finite quotient derivative v means x(alpha+h)-x(alpha)=h(v+o(1)), so x(alpha+h)->x(alpha); similarly at beta. Interior C1 already implies continuity. In coordinates the future cone criterion is y0'>=|z'|, with y0'>0 for nonzero causal tangents. For interior alpha<u<v<beta, the derivative is continuous on [u,v], hence its coordinates and their absolute values, spatial norm, and f are ordinary Riemann integrable. Truncated FTC gives integral_u^v yj'=yj(v)-yj(u).
2. Fix an interior r. Define the two-ended improper integral by the sum of limits integral_u^r as u decreases to alpha and integral_r^v as v increases to beta. This is a local explicit definition of the improper convention, not a new assumption of integrability. For y0' the truncated FTC and endpoint continuity give these limits as y0(r)-y0(alpha) and y0(beta)-y0(r); both are finite and nonnegative. Thus its total integral is the endpoint increment.
3. For any continuous 0<=g<=y0' on the open piece, integral_u^r g increases as u decreases and is bounded by y0(r)-y0(alpha); its supremum S exists. Given epsilon>0, a truncation u* has integral_u*^r g>S-epsilon by the definition of supremum, and every alpha<u<u* lies between that value and S. This proves the left limit is S. The same argument proves the right limit. Apply this to |yj'| for spatial j, |z'|, and f. The time derivative is already nonnegative. All coordinate derivatives are absolutely improperly integrable. Signed convergence and endpoint increments follow directly from the truncated FTC (or by its positive/negative parts); no closed-piece derivative integrability is used.
4. Additivity and independence of r follow by ordinary truncated additivity with fixed interior cuts and taking the finite limits. Refine a partition at finitely many interior points and use this identity; any two admissible partitions share a common refinement. Sum the coordinate increments by telescoping. On each truncation, |z(v)-z(u)|<=integral_u^v |z'|<=integral_u^v y0'. Pass to the endpoints and sum using the Euclidean triangle inequality to obtain |z(b)-z(a)|<=y0(b)-y0(a).
5. Time strictly increases on every nondegenerate piece: y0'>0 on its interior, so a compact interior interval has positive time increment by FTC and positivity/continuity; monotonicity then carries it to the full piece. Thus the complete displacement is nonzero. If an interior tangent is timelike, y0'-|z'| is positive in a compact interior neighborhood, and its integral there is positive; the preceding inequalities become strict. If only a one-sided endpoint tangent v is specified timelike, the difference quotient of a sufficiently short inward segment is future timelike by openness of the cone. That segment's displacement is timelike. The remaining displacement is future causal by the non-strict argument, so their sum is timelike by the coordinate triangle inequality.
