Each example states its own model and mathematical assumptions; the A-page stable-equilibrium hypotheses are not inherited automatically. The finite two-state partition function is a finite model calculation. The reduced van der Waals equation is a stipulated phenomenological equation, and the lever rule uses declared additive molar-volume data. The reaction-boundary example is a constructed units-labelled tangent-cone optimization illustration, not a thermodynamic equilibrium claim.

The compact two-generator example illustrates the finite-support attainment bound with an affine entropy extension restricted to the two-point generator set and a prescribed barycenter. The noncompact counterexample uses a bounded positive concave extension whose admissible generator set omits its maximizing barycenter, so no feasible mixture attains the supremum. Constructed Gibbs branch models make same-tag homogeneity explicit in their potentials; they do not infer it from general tagged scaling. The linear coexistence example preserves its reservoir and attained-endpoint assumptions, while the equal-volume counterexample separates a zero volume jump from a regular Clapeyron graph.

The one-component and reacting three-component examples verify local equation-map rank under their displayed model data. A regular equation-set solution does not alone establish physical entropy-maximizing coexistence. Numerical values are specified model parameters, not measurements. These draft examples retain the conditional proof status of their exact suppliers and remain pending independent review.
