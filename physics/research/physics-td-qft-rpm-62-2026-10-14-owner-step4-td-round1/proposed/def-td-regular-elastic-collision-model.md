## Definition

Fix a particle mass $m_p>0$ in kilograms and velocity space $V=\mathbb R^3$ with components in $\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. The elastic collision set is
$$Q_{\rm el}=\{(v,w,v',w')\in V^4:v+w=v'+w',\ |v|^2+|w|^2=|v'|^2+|w'|^2\}.$$
Thus each allowed event conserves momentum $m_p(v+w)$ and kinetic energy $m_p(|v|^2+|w|^2)/2$. Equip $Q_{\rm el}$ with the Borel sets generated by its relative topology.

Collision-rate data consist of a nonnegative, countably additive, $\sigma$-finite Borel measure $\nu$ on these Borel sets. It is invariant under exchanging $v$ with $w$, exchanging $v'$ with $w'$, and exchanging the incoming pair $(v,w)$ with the outgoing pair $(v',w')$: for each of these maps $S$ and Borel set $E$, $\nu(S^{-1}E)=\nu(E)$. This is the three-dimensional physical restriction of the collision set and exchange conventions in [[def-tdmath-elastic-collision-kernel]], with $\sigma$-finiteness stated explicitly for the product integrals used by the entropy theorem. Use one-particle number density $f$ relative to $d^3x\,d^3v$: $f$ has units $\mathrm{s^3\,m^{-6}}$, so $\int f\,d^3v$ is spatial number density in $\mathrm{m^{-3}}$. A dimensionless measurable velocity test $\phi$ has no unit, and the weak collision term $\int\phi Q_\nu(f)\,d^3v$ has units $\mathrm{m^{-3}\,s^{-1}}$ when $Q_\nu(f)$ has units $f/\mathrm s$. Since the product of two densities has units $\mathrm{s^6\,m^{-12}}$, the collision measure $\nu$ is normalized with units $\mathrm{m^9\,s^{-7}}$, making $\int(f'f_*'-ff_*)\Delta\phi\,d\nu$ have those same weak-rate units. This refers to the units of the measure itself, not an unspecified density relative to a parametrization. Dimensioned tests carry their declared extra unit on both sides.

For the purely mathematical supplier, fix $v_*=1\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, $n_*=1\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}$, and $t_*=1\,\mathrm s$. Set $f_*=n_*/v_*^3$, use numerical velocity $\widehat v=v/v_*$, numerical density $\widehat f=f/f_*$, and numerical measure $\widehat\nu$ defined by pushforward of $\nu/(v_*^6/(n_*t_*))$ under the coordinate conversion. The numerical collision operator $\widehat Q$ gives $Q_\nu(f)=(f_*/t_*)\widehat Q(\widehat f)$. Both sides of its dimensionless weak identity are multiplied by $n_*/t_*$ to obtain the physical identity. The numerical rescaling preserves all three exchange symmetries, measurability and sigma-finiteness. This normalization declares units and matching mathematical data; it asserts no collision-operator existence for arbitrary measures or densities. The measure is a declared model input; no differential cross section or microscopic limit is inferred from this Definition.
