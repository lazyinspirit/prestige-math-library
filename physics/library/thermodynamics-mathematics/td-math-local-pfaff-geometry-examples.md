These examples compute a global exact multiple in two dimensions, show that $\omega=dz-y\,dx$ on $\mathbb R^3$ satisfies $\omega\wedge d\omega=dx\wedge dy\wedge dz\ne0$ and has no local exact multiple, and show that a one-form with $d\omega=0$ on a punctured domain need not equal $dS$ for a globally defined scalar $S$. The last example uses a closed-loop period and does not claim that no other global integrating factor exists.
