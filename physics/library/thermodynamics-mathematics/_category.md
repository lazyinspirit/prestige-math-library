These pages develop mathematical constructions for thermodynamic models: scalable compounds and ordered composition, local Pfaff geometry, nonsmooth equilibrium, convex contact and entropy calibration, ensembles and thermodynamic limits, mixture dynamics, continuum pressure, interacting-ensemble geometry, spatial measure, and information. Definitions and results make regularity, admissibility, integrability, boundary, and choice assumptions explicit; they concern specified state spaces, functions, and measures. Physical interpretation belongs to the framework that uses a construction and is not part of its mathematical conclusion. Examples isolate boundary and limit behavior in concrete models.

Real analysis and differential geometry are named prerequisites, with measure theory, functional analysis, probability, topology, and linear algebra used throughout. Thermodynamics and Classical Statistical Mechanics use these tools for equilibrium representation, ensembles, mixtures, and continuum models. Quantum Statistical Mechanics and Fluid Dynamics reuse selected ensemble, pressure, and mixture results; Thermodynamics and General Relativity mathematics uses local-equilibrium and flow results. Those consumers add their own physical assumptions.
