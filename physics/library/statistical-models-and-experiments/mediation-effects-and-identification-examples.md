These finite examples separate the assumptions that a mediation calculation
needs. Two treatment-outcome and mediator-outcome models can share the same
single-world observed law while assigning different natural effects when the
cross-world coupling changes. A mediator-outcome common cause and a
treatment-induced mediator-outcome confounder each give a separate exact
calculation where the observed conditional-mean functional differs from the
true nested counterfactual means. The examples do not treat a fitted
regression coefficient as an identified natural effect.
