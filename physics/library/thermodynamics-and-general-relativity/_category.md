These pages couple thermodynamic interpretations to specified relativistic geometries and matter models: fluid currents and local balances; stationary, KMS/Gibbs, photon, static-fluid, and supported-heat preparations; restricted dissipative constitutive laws; and regulated gravitational or horizon entropy, focusing, Hawking, AdS/JT, and renormalized-stress analyses. Every assignment of energy, heat, or entropy, and each observer, boundary, regulator, equation of state, smoothness condition, and constitutive choice, is tied to a stated model. Mathematical identities and adopted physical assumptions remain distinct, so conclusions are conditional and do not establish a universal law of gravitational entropy or material response.

Thermodynamics and General Relativity Mathematics supplies geometric currents, local fluid-state identities, stationary thermal geometry, and regulated quantum-entropy calculations. General Relativity and Special Relativity provide spacetime and observer structure; Quantum Statistical Mechanics and Quantum Field Theory supply selected state and field constructions; Einstein–Maxwell models and Thermodynamics Mathematics support particular horizon and equilibrium analyses. No separate physics framework currently uses these coupled conclusions as a prerequisite.
