These pages specify thermodynamic systems, exchanges, units, and entropy, then treat accessibility, equilibrium representations, potentials and responses, cycles, low-temperature limits, phase behavior, finite statistical bridges, irreversible boundaries, and stochastic protocols. Continuum heat transport and interacting equilibrium preparations add separately stated kinetic, material, and constitutive assumptions. The category distinguishes adopted operational or model assumptions from mathematical consequences, tying each conclusion to its system, process, state domain, and regularity conditions. Results do not assert that every material follows a chosen equation of state or transport law; each remains within its stated regime.

Thermodynamics Mathematics supplies the convex, ordered-composition, ensemble, mixture, and continuum constructions used here; Non-relativistic Quantum Mechanics supports the finite statistical bridge. Real analysis, differential geometry, probability, and measure theory underlie further arguments. Classical Statistical Mechanics uses the thermodynamic framework and unit conventions in its ensemble models, while Fluid Dynamics uses the continuum heat model. These connections preserve the different assumptions of each physical framework.
