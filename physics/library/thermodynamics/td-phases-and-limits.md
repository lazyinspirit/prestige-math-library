This page develops phase coexistence and reaction equilibrium as conditional conclusions of the declared thermodynamic models. The finite-state thermodynamic-limit contract fixes state spaces, energies, weights, normalizations, boundary conditions and limiting preparation; it does not assert that an arbitrary sequence has a limit. Phase branches use explicit smooth physical-coordinate charts, positive temperatures and interior compositions. Bilateral feasible exchange at an attained global entropy maximum supplies the intensive equilibrium conditions.

The fixed-temperature and fixed-pressure potential result ties heat, work, reservoir energy and pressure-store work to one admitted closed process record. Its global-minimum conclusion uses the projection of that record's exact isolated-compound feasible set. Reaction-boundary conditions require their stated smooth extension through zero amounts; the positive-mole state chart does not supply that extension or a boundary equilibrium model. The regular Gibbs phase rule counts the local dimension of the specified full-rank equation set, with all exchange and reaction constraints retained.

The local Clapeyron theorem requires same-tag energy homogeneity on each phase branch. General tagged bulk scaling does not imply this hypothesis. It can be stipulated for the branch or obtained in the designated common-law bulk subclass of [[pthm-td-euler-and-gibbs-duhem]], with its entropy-cone, local-inverse, intensive-parameter, SI-unit and reference-convention assumptions preserved. No global energy-cone or universal physical homogeneity claim is made.

The compact-mixture result proves its finite-dimensional bridge locally: every feasible Borel mixture's integrated generator and entropy vector lies in the compact convex hull of the continuous graph, and a maximizing vector at the fixed barycenter reduces to at most one more atom than the coordinate dimension. Every Borel probability mixture satisfying that constraint must be admitted by the physical model. The result derives neither the entropy map nor a general phase-transition limit. The scope remark distinguishes finite-state analyticity, singular limits, boundaries and noncompact failures of attainment.
