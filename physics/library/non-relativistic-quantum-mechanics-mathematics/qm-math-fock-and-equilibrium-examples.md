The companion page to [[qm-math-fock-and-equilibrium]] records three explicit
mathematical checks on its equilibrium and exclusion statements. All three
items are mathematics: they carry `domain: mathematics`, depend only on the
parent page, earlier mathematical suppliers and published mathematics, and make
no physical or empirical claim.

[[ex-nrqm-fock-and-equilibrium-example-infinite-identical-mode-divergence]]
takes the degenerate mode list $\varepsilon_j=\varepsilon_0>0$ on a separable
infinite-dimensional one-particle space: the weight sum $\sum_je^{-\beta
\varepsilon_j}$ diverges, the partial products
$(1-e^{-\beta\varepsilon_0})^{-N}$ grow at least like
$e^{Ne^{-\beta\varepsilon_0}}$, and the diagonal weight operator $D_\beta$ of
[[thm-nrqm-fock-and-equilibrium-countable-mode-product-criterion]] is not
trace class, while a single mode of the same frequency has the finite trace
$1/(1-e^{-\beta\varepsilon_0})$ by
[[thm-nrqm-fock-and-equilibrium-single-mode-thermal-trace]]; the divergence is
caused by the infinite multiplicity, not by the value of the frequency.

[[ex-nrqm-fock-and-equilibrium-example-no-automatic-equilibration]] exhibits
the non-stationary unit vector $\psi_0=(e_0+e_1)/\sqrt2$ of the one-mode Fock
space: under the unitary group generated by $\omega N$ the state keeps
returning to itself, the expectation of $A_+(1)+A_-(1)$ equals
$\cos(\omega t/\hbar)$ and has no limit, and the state has no norm limit,
whereas the weight operator $G_\beta$ of
[[thm-nrqm-fock-and-equilibrium-single-mode-thermal-trace]] is invariant under
the same dynamics; unitary evolution alone therefore does not equilibrate a
non-stationary state.

[[ex-nrqm-fock-and-equilibrium-example-repeated-orbital-fermion-exclusion]]
verifies concretely that $P_-^{(2)}(u\otimes u)=0$ for every $u$ because the
transposition fixes $u\otimes u$, that the Slater vector with a repeated orbital
is the zero vector (so the norm-one statement of
[[thm-nrqm-fock-and-equilibrium-slater-direct-and-exchange-calculation]]
genuinely needs orthonormal orbitals), and that exclusion survives for the
non-basis vector $u=e_1+e_2$ in $\mathbb C^2$: it is a property of the state,
not of basis labels.
