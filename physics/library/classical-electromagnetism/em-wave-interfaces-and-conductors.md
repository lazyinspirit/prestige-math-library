This page builds the interface and conducting-medium wave layer of the
classical electromagnetic framework. Two mathematical lemmas first fix the
mathematics used later: pairwise-distinct complex exponential characters are
independent on a nonempty open set, with the real-part grouping rule that
identifies which frequency groups a real-linear relation can constrain; and the
two-equation interface admittance system is solved in closed form, with the
identity $|r|^2+(\operatorname{Re}Y_2/Y_1)|t|^2=1$ and its pure-imaginary and
real-positive specialisations. On that basis the homogeneous isotropic medium
parameters are defined — phase speed, wave impedance, refractive index, the
complex propagation constant selected by $\operatorname{Re},\operatorname{Im}\ge0$
and the skin depth — and the plane-wave amplitude conditions, dispersion
relation, transverse structure, period-averaged energy density and flux, and
the evanescent decay identities are derived for the explicit ideal plane-wave
fields. Phase matching at the plane interface then gives the common tangential
wavevector and frequency, coplanarity, the reflection law and Snell's law;
normal and oblique incidence are solved as admittance systems with the flux
budgets, the Ohmic absorption reading of the entering flux, the Brewster
condition with its equal-medium qualification, and total internal reflection
with its critical-angle case. The conducting-medium theorem gives the exact
attenuation constant, the closed-form skin depth and its bounded good- and
poor-conductor regimes. Every item states its own hypotheses, regularity,
units, conventions and exclusions, and no empirical content is asserted.
