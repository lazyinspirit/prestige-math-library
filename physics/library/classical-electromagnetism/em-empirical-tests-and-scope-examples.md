The two worked cases keep the objects that empirical comparison involves
separate. The first proves an exact algebraic nonidentifiability of a declared
affine response model: the parameter pairs $(s,b)$ and $(s+\delta,b-G\delta)$
produce identical predictions $Gs+b$. An exact recorded equality fixes that
combination; a declared bound interval only constrains it. Neither separates
the auxiliary source-strength and background parameters without additional
independent information; the example asserts nothing about any apparatus,
dataset or probability.
The second distinguishes an ideal pointwise electric zero of a coherent
two-beam superposition — where the magnetic amplitude and hence the averaged
energy density remain positive — from a declared measurable detector bin with
response coefficient and nonnegative background, whose response integral is
defined in $[0,+\infty]$, is positive whenever the background integral is, and
is finite and positive on the explicit finite-volume constant-background
branch. No data, sampling law, event probability or classical/quantum
discrimination is inferred.
