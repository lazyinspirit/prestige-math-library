The worked cases fix the role of the framework's SI conventions and of two
canonical interface configurations. The first shows that matching units does
not determine the permittivity or the response law: the proposed law
$E=\varepsilon_0D$ fails dimensionally, dimensionally admissible laws leave the
constant free, and a frequency-domain symbol is not an instantaneous
time-domain coefficient. The second solves a planar dielectric interface with a
prescribed constant free surface charge, deriving the tangential-electric and
normal-displacement matching conditions and their magnetic companions from
local compact-test integration by parts rather than from global compactness. The
third exhibits the uniform-field dielectric-sphere candidate, verifies its
matching conditions and decay, proves uniqueness in the declared decay class by
an $\varepsilon$-weighted Green identity, and computes the uniform interior
field, the exterior dipole moment and the bound surface charge. Each example
states its own hypotheses, is conditional on the declared linear closure
branch, and asserts no material data, no empirical claim and no dynamic
polarisability.
