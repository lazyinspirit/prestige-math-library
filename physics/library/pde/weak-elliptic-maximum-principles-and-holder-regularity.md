This page develops the weak maximum principle for coercive divergence-form equations with bounded measurable coefficients, the De Giorgi level-set iteration that proves it, and the De Giorgi--Nash--Moser interior regularity theory built on the same Caccioppoli and level-set machinery. The principal coefficients are real, measurable, bounded and uniformly elliptic; the De Giorgi and Harnack items additionally assume symmetry with $\theta I\le A\le M_a^2I$, while the maximum principle uses the coefficientwise bounds of its operator definition; the principal operator is $L_0u=-D_i(a^{ij}D_ju)$ with its sesquilinear form $a_0$, and lower-order terms $b,c$ appear only in the maximum-principle items under explicit sign hypotheses. The homogeneous weak maximum principle assumes $c\ge0$ a.e. together with the weak sign condition $\int(c\zeta+b^iD_i\zeta)\ge0$ for nonnegative test functions $\zeta$, the weak form of $c-\operatorname{div}b\ge0$; the forcing case assumes $b\equiv0$, $c\ge0$ and an $L^q$ source with $q>n/2$, whose maximum bound adds $C\|f^+\|_{L^q}$ to the positive boundary supremum; the local forcing estimates explicitly carry the scale factor $R^{2-n/q}$. The truncated positive part is an admissible test, the truncated Caccioppoli inequality and the Sobolev level-set iteration are recorded for arbitrary levels, and the local boundedness, oscillation reduction, Holder regularity, Moser iteration, weak Harnack and Harnack theorems follow with constants depending only on $n,q,\theta,M_a$ and each estimate's stated exponent and radius ratios. The zero-set propagation and the strong maximum principle are recorded for $n\ge3$ on connected open sets and close the page, and the superscript conventions of the trace and of the boundary supremum are those of the weak-subsolution definition.
