The fluid pages fix Newtonian frames, quantities, units, and boundary or initial data, then adopt stress and heat-flux closures and specified continuum or kinetic models. Conditional results include local periodic incompressible evolution, exact viscous and inviscid flows, mechanical power balances, restricted non-Newtonian, wall/free-surface and turbulent models, geophysical and phase-field assumptions, and model-based numerical or assimilation interpretations. Turbulent averaging leaves closure questions explicit; computational results separate solver properties from continuum predictions. Finite observation and calibration assumptions belong to the stated measurement models, and filtering results are conditional on adopted observation and noise laws. The Navier–Stokes equations are distinct from global regularity claims, and conclusions retain closure, domain, regularity and approximation limits.

The models use Fluid Dynamics mathematics for material kinematics, weak formulations, periodic estimates, kinetic moments, numerical error and statistical constructions. Classical-mechanics, thermodynamics, analysis and PDE results supply additional definitions and methods. These tools support the stated physical closures and boundary assumptions; they do not validate a model empirically.
