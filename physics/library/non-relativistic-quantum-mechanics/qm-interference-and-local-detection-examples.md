The companion page to [[qm-interference-and-local-detection]] collects two
exact worked computations and one source-backed reported experiment.
[[ex-nrqm-interference-ideal-flat-two-path-screen-distribution]] computes, for
a declared pair of flat product-form path amplitudes, the exact coherent and
which-path probabilities of the overlap and outer regions of the declared
detection coordinate: at relative phase $\pi$ the coherent law gives the whole
overlap region probability zero while the balanced which-path law gives it
positive probability, a bin meeting a bright region retains positive coherent
probability, and the example states no detector response or data.
[[ex-nrqm-interference-uniform-phase-averaging]] shows that for orthogonal path
components a declared ensemble whose relative phase is uniform over the
finitely many $n$-th roots of unity has the which-path law with the norm
weights as its averaged bin law, while each single phase retains the cross
term; the continuous uniform distribution is a separate model not computed.
[[exp-nrqm-interference-bach-electron-double-slit]] then reports, from the
primary preprint of Bach, Pope, Liou and Batelaan, the controlled double-slit
electron experiment at 600 eV: slit-selective distributions from a movable
mask and an accumulated single-electron event build-up over 2, 7, 209, 1004 and
6235 localized events, with the source's omissions recorded — no calibrated
efficiency or background, no numerical localization error, no apparatus-parameter
uncertainties, no occupancy bound and no statistical comparison with a
specified competing model. [[rem-nrqm-interference-examples-scope-limits]]
records the combined scope of the page: exact conditional computations plus
one faithfully reported experiment, no photon data, no simulation presented as
measurement, and no implication that a finite sample proves or falsifies the
declared models.
