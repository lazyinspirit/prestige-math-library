This page defines monotone likelihood ratio for a scalar statistic and proves
the randomized one-sided Karlin–Rubin result. Boundary randomization gives an
exact level when the statistic has atoms. The upper-tail rule applies when
larger parameter values increase the likelihood ratio in the statistic; the
lower-tail rule applies to the reversed hypotheses. The theorem uses MLR in
the likelihood of the full data and does not require the statistic to be
sufficient. A separate normal-location result shows why the one-sided
conclusion does not extend to every two-sided alternative.
