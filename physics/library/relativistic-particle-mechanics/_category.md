Relativistic particle mechanics defines spacetime, observers, worldlines, momentum and mass shells before adopting test-clock, particle-action, null and prescribed-force models. Massive trajectories are future-timelike curves with stated parameter conditions, while null motion has its own action and causal assumptions. Conditional results cover free geodesics, Killing charges, observer work, constrained Hamiltonian data, Poincaré moment components, spin transport, accelerated clocks, and orbital stability and scattering. A flat-space radiation-reaction page adopts an effective LAD equation, proves local existence and shell preservation for the LL ODE, and gives finite-time LAD-to-LL comparisons only for families meeting stated coefficient and defect bounds. Massive actions, null actions and force laws remain distinct model inputs; the pages distinguish test fields from self-fields and retain regularity, approximation, background and finite-time scopes.

They use special relativity, general-relativistic geometry, classical electromagnetic couplings, and mathematical ODE, differential-geometric and mechanics results. Particle results in turn support selected Einstein–Maxwell and General Relativity pages; the category also uses the electromagnetic mathematics companion. Force laws are adopted model inputs, not derived from observations here.
