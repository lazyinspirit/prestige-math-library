This page develops flat-spacetime momentum identities and finite isolated-collision kinematics. The mass shell determines a massive particle's velocity from its momentum; in one inertial laboratory, the work identity follows by differentiating the mass shell. A separate variable-mass formula is only a differentiated kinematic ansatz and adopts no mass evolution law.

The local collision definition fixes finite complete incoming and outgoing lists at one event. The collision-balance postulate is a scoped reuse of the earlier special-relativity assumption. Conditional deductions give the invariant-mass threshold, fixed-target threshold, two-product center-of-momentum kinematics, the invariant energy of two null momenta, and the direction freedom of an elastic channel. They do not establish that an interaction occurs, supply a reaction mechanism or rate, or imply an angular distribution.

The finite-sum result is mathematical. Every collision conclusion is conditional on the stated isolated-balance postulate and the inspected momentum interfaces. No curved-spacetime total-energy conservation or empirical observation is claimed.
