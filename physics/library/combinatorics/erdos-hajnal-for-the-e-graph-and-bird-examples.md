The companion page proves that the $E$-graph and Bird have the
Erdős–Hajnal property, and the introduction states that both results
generalize the earlier five-vertex-path and bull theorems. These two examples
check the class containments behind that phrase by finite adjacency data rather
than by any numerical comparison of exponents.

The first example shows that $P_5$ is $E$-free but not $P_5$-free, and that
every $P_5$-free graph is $E$-free because the path vertices $p_1,\dots,p_5$ of
$E$ induce a $P_5$. The second shows that the bull is Bird-free but not
bull-free, and that every bull-free graph is Bird-free because deleting Bird's
added leaf $w$ leaves an induced bull. In both cases the larger hypothesis class
of the new theorem is witnessed by a single five-vertex graph, so the phrase
"generalizes" is a strict containment of forbidden-pattern classes. Both
examples are leaves and no later page cites them.
