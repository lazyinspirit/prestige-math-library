Quantum statistical mechanics organizes equilibrium and dynamical questions using specified quantum states, observables, Hamiltonians and preparation assumptions. This category begins with finite density matrices and normal trace states, spectral entropy, Gibbs optimization and response. It then develops qualified subsystem ensemble comparisons, quantum spin algebras, finite-range dynamics, thermodynamic limits and equilibrium conditions expressed through time evolution. Further pages address occupation descriptions for identical particles, selected interacting examples, finite quantum channels and restricted relaxation models. Computational Gibbs-state preparation is treated with its declared finite input-access and error conditions.

The distinction between finite matrices, trace-class densities and algebraic states remains explicit throughout. Infinite-system equilibrium is not silently identified with a finite normalized trace, and unitary stationarity does not itself establish thermalization. Physical interpretation depends on the stated preparation and measurement postulates; probability assignments are not asserted to equal measured frequencies. Examples and counterexamples show how support, normalization, representation, subsystem size and limiting assumptions affect conclusions. Read the mathematical state space and the physical model scope together before using any equilibrium or transport prediction.
