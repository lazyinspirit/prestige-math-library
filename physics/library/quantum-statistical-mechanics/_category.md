These pages begin with finite density states, spectral entropy, Gibbs optimization, and response, while distinguishing finite-dimensional traces from trace-class and infinite-system algebraic states. They develop quantum-spin algebras and finite-range dynamics, KMS equilibrium and modular time conversion, thermodynamic limits, Bose/Fermi occupation models, exact interacting examples, open-system channels and restricted relaxation, transport obstructions, and selected integrable and topological lattice models. Each model states its Hamiltonian, representation, state class, dynamics, and limit conditions. Unitary stationarity or a KMS condition alone does not prove thermalization or empirical applicability.

Non-relativistic Quantum Mechanics and its Mathematics supply state, composition, operator, and occupation foundations; Classical Statistical Mechanics and Thermodynamics Mathematics supply selected ensemble and response results. Functional analysis, real and measure theory, representation theory, and operator-algebra mathematics provide further prerequisites. Quantum-mechanical pages use the channel and open-dynamics results, while Thermodynamics and General Relativity uses specified KMS, Bose/Fermi, and modular-state constructions. The two physics frameworks keep their assumptions distinct.
