These pages define classical configuration, cotangent-phase, reference-measure, and ensemble data, then analyze Hamiltonian statistics, exact Gaussian and gamma tools, lattice models, continuum limits, mean-field hierarchies, local ensemble comparisons, collision and transport models, and stochastic response. Further results treat large-deviation ensembles and inequivalence, inverse-Gibbs identification, fluctuation identities, sampling and error certificates, rare-event methods, hydrodynamic limits, planar criticality, polymers, and critical Gaussian fields. Probability preparations, interactions, boundaries, dynamics, and limit assumptions remain explicit. A stationary distribution alone does not prove equilibration or ergodicity, and a model result is not a universal empirical law.

Non-relativistic Classical Mechanics and its Mathematics supply phase-space, Hamiltonian-flow, unit, and dynamics foundations. Thermodynamics and Thermodynamics Mathematics provide selected energy, entropy, ensemble, and limit constructions; measure theory, probability, and real analysis support the statistical arguments. Quantum Statistical Mechanics uses specified classical ensembles and results in its interacting-model, Bose/Fermi, and relaxation pages. These uses do not identify distinct ensembles or dynamics as physically interchangeable.
