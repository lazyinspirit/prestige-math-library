Classical statistical mechanics studies specified probability preparations on classical state spaces and the observables evaluated in those preparations. This category introduces configuration and phase spaces, reference measures, canonical and energy-band ensembles, and the assumptions connecting statistical quantities to physical energy, entropy and response. Its scope includes finite lattice and continuum models, qualified local ensemble comparisons, Hamiltonian averages, mean-field descriptions, selected collision models and finite stochastic dynamics. Exact gas, quadratic, spin and exclusion examples provide concrete settings in which normalization, fluctuations and limiting procedures can be examined.

The pages keep model adoption separate from mathematical deduction. A stationary distribution is not by itself a statement of equilibration, mixing or ergodicity, and a thermodynamic limit retains the interaction, boundary and regularity assumptions under which it is formulated. Counterexamples identify the limits of stronger interpretations, including claims about coarse entropy and ensemble equivalence. Read the reference conventions and preparation hypotheses before applying a result; examples companions illustrate those same conditions through calculations and carefully specified alternative models.
