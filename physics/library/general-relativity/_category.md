General Relativity develops classical gravitation through Lorentzian spacetime, observers, curvature, matter, and Einstein’s field equations. The pages connect geodesic motion and conservation laws to Newtonian limits, cosmological models, spherical vacuum solutions, gravitational waves, causality, and initial-value evolution. Further topics include constrained initial data, null focusing and trapped surfaces, singularity theorems, asymptotically flat energy and Bondi radiation, gravitational memory, Kerr geometry, black-hole perturbations, and stability methods. Covariant phase space, boundary charges, tetrads, spin structures, and holonomy provide additional geometric tools. Local constructions, conditional estimates, and cited frontier results retain their stated regimes; singularity theorems concern geodesic incompleteness rather than automatically establishing curvature blow-up.

Special Relativity supplies causal and observer conventions, while Differential Geometry provides manifolds, connections, curvature, and differential forms. Real Analysis, Functional Analysis, and Partial Differential Equations support the evolution and energy arguments. Einstein–Maxwell Models uses this geometry for coupled fields; Relativistic Particle Mechanics uses its backgrounds and symmetries; Thermodynamics and General Relativity builds on its spacetime and horizon constructions. Selected mathematical results also support Quantum Field Theory Mathematics.
