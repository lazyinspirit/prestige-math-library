The general-relativity category develops the classical relativistic theory of
gravity from explicit postulates and mathematical definitions: the smooth
time-oriented Lorentzian geometry of the framework, tensors and observers,
connections and free fall, curvature and tides, the Einstein field law and its
matter branches, local and global energy, the flat and Newtonian bridges, the
spherical vacuum and cosmological models, linearized waves, causal and
initial-value structure, and the experimental interface. Physical postulates
are adopted assumptions of a model; the mathematical results of the category
are complete conditional deductions from those postulates and stated
hypotheses, and no theorem establishes that the model describes nature.
Curvature, geodesic and field-equation claims are always relative to a
specified smooth model and its matter content, with the smooth-branch and
regularity restrictions stated on the page where they are used. Empirical
comparisons are recorded only with source-backed observations, apparatus and
uncertainty qualifications; agreement of predictions with observations is
evidence about the combined model and apparatus assumptions, not a proof of
the postulates.
