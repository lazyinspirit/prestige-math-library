The first worked case separates coordinate components from a geometric observable. The circle diffeomorphism $x^1\mapsto x^1+\epsilon\sin x^1$ changes the displayed flat metric component, while the pullback formula preserves the proper time of the corresponding curve exactly. The diagram records the commuting curve and isometry maps; the verification is an explicit derivative and tensor calculation.

The second case is the scalar symmetric-hyperbolic equation $z_t=2tz$. Its solution map is continuous on each chosen finite interval, with amplification $e^{T^2}$ at time $T$. This exceeds every fixed $Ce^{CT}$ bound as $T$ increases. It illustrates the scope of a finite-slab estimate for general systems and makes no claim about an Einstein solution.
