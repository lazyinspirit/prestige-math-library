This is a mathematical page about a specified smooth, static, spherically symmetric metric and its state profiles. The exact product manifold, areal-radius form, signature, lapse, diagonal isotropic source tensor, mass function and formal SI dimensions are defined in [[def-tgr-spherical-static-state-domain]]. The countable-choice convention is only the $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ inherited from the cited smooth Lorentzian and tensor-bundle suppliers; no physical postulate or empirical claim is made.

The reduction lemma [[lem-tgr-spherical-einstein-tov-reduction]] computes the signed Einstein components from the metric and proves both directions between the full smooth component equations and the mass, lapse and pressure equations on $r>0$. Its converse uses the contracted Bianchi identity only after the mass, lapse and conservation equations hold. It does not assert an equation for anisotropic, rotating, nonstatic, weak, or distributional sources.

The center result [[lem-tgr-even-radial-center-regularity]] supplies the explicit even-radial-to-Cartesian $C^2$ calculation. The local theorem [[thm-tgr-tov-regular-center-local-existence]] constructs a positive-lapse, positive-$b$ center by a Volterra integral contraction, gives uniform local dependence on central pressure, and permits continuation only while the positive-$b$ EOS domain is compactly retained. The exact constant-density result [[thm-tgr-constant-density-star-verified]] checks the pressure, lapse, mass and vacuum match for the family-specific range $0<u<8/9$; it proves neither a general Buchdahl theorem nor realistic causal transport.

Finally, [[def-tgr-spherical-entropy-constraint-functional]] defines proper spherical entropy, particle number and mass-energy on a supplied $C^2$ local state chart. The theorem [[thm-tgr-constrained-entropy-tov-criticality]] proves the first-variation equivalence with the Tolman/Klein products and derives the TOV pressure equation under its fixed-radius, positive-$b$, state-chart and boundary-normalization hypotheses. It proves criticality, not a maximum, existence theorem, or dynamical stability.
