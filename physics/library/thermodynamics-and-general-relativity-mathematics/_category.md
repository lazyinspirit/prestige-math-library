These mathematics pages define geometric and statistical constructions for relativistic thermodynamic models: spacelike slabs and flux charges, observer rest spaces and fluid currents, local Gibbs and Euler identities, stationary thermal data, finite gravitational ensembles, static spherical solutions, smooth null screens, photon-mode traces, detector smearing, and finite modular-entropy balances. Their hypotheses specify manifolds, metrics, observers, fields, state functions, boundaries, regularity, and regulators. The results establish mathematical identities or constructions; assigning physical energy, heat, entropy, or applicability requires a separately adopted model. Examples make normalization and boundary choices concrete.

Thermodynamics Mathematics supplies local-equilibrium and flow results, while General Relativity supplies Lorentzian geometry and field-equation ingredients. Quantum Statistical Mechanics, Non-relativistic Quantum Mechanics Mathematics, Quantum Field Theory Mathematics, Special Relativity, and Einstein–Maxwell Mathematics supply selected state, spectrum, and horizon calculations. Thermodynamics and General Relativity uses these constructions in its fluid, stationary, photon, and regulated-entropy models; the mathematical results do not assert a universal gravitational-entropy law.
