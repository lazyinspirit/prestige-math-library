This page develops the branching rule for the symmetric group, its bookkeeping
by the Young graph, Young's rule for permutation modules, and the Schur–Weyl
decomposition of a tensor power. It uses the tabloid and permutation-module
conventions of [[young-diagrams-tableaux-and-permutation-modules]], the complex
Specht modules and their classification from
[[specht-modules-and-the-irreducibles-of-the-symmetric-group]], the induction
adjunction of [[induced-representations-and-frobenius-reciprocity]], and the
tensor constructions of [[tensor-products-of-modules]].

The Specht module construction is developed over arbitrary commutative rings;
the restriction filtration itself is proved over every field.
[[def-polytabloid-specht-module-over-an-arbitrary-field]] defines the tabloid
modules, the antisymmetrizers and the Specht modules over a ring, and
[[lem-integral-specht-garnir-straightening-and-field-basis]] proves the integral
Garnir relation together with the standard-polytabloid basis that survives
reduction to every field, including characteristic two. The corner order on
removable rows and the associated deletion maps are recorded in
[[def-corner-order-and-specht-deletion-map]]; they build the nested
$S_{n-1}$-stable subspaces of [[lem-specht-branching-subspaces-are-invariant]],
whose successive quotients are computed in
[[lem-specht-branching-successive-quotients]]. Assembling these gives the
field-uniform filtration of [[thm-specht-restriction-branching-filtration]].
Over $\mathbb C$ Maschke's theorem splits the filtration, and
[[cor-complex-specht-restriction-branching-rule]] identifies the restriction as
the direct sum over the removable corners, each shape occurring once. Frobenius
reciprocity converts that statement into the induction rule of
[[thm-complex-specht-induction-branching-rule]].

The combinatorial shadow of the two rules is the Young graph of
[[def-young-graph]], whose vertices are partitions and whose edges add one box.
Paths from the empty partition encode standard tableaux, as proved in
[[cor-paths-in-the-young-graph-index-standard-tableaux]], so the branching
multiplicities are path counts.

Young's rule computes the multiplicity of a Specht module in a permutation
module as a Kostka number. The linear maps attached to semistandard tableaux are
constructed in [[lem-semistandard-tableau-homomorphisms-to-young-permutation-modules]];
their triangular independence is
[[lem-semistandard-homomorphisms-are-independent-and-dominance-triangular]] and
the spanning argument that avoids a separate RSK input is
[[lem-semistandard-homomorphisms-span-in-characteristic-zero]]. Together they
identify the dimension of the homomorphism space with the number of semistandard
tableaux, and [[thm-youngs-rule-for-permutation-modules]] states the resulting
decomposition.

The final part concerns the tensor power $V^{\otimes n}$ with its commuting
actions. [[def-commuting-symmetric-and-linear-actions-on-tensor-power]] fixes
the left place action of $S_n$, the diagonal action of $\operatorname{GL}(V)$
and the place-sum operators; [[lem-tensor-place-operators-span-the-symmetric-centralizer]]
shows that the unital algebra generated by those operators is the centralizer
of the symmetric-group action.
The double-centralizer theorem [[thm-schur-weyl-double-centralizer]] identifies
the two commutants, and [[lem-schur-weyl-length-cutoff-by-column-antisymmetrization]]
determines exactly which shapes occur for finite-dimensional $V$.
[[lem-schur-weyl-polytabloid-highest-weight]] computes the highest weight and
its uniqueness, and [[thm-schur-weyl-decomposition-with-length-cutoff]]
assembles the decomposition
$$V^{\otimes n}\cong\bigoplus_{\ell(\lambda)\le\dim V}S^\lambda\otimes M_\lambda,$$
the irreducibility of the multiplicity factors, and their pairwise
inequivalence for the nonzero factors; the zero factors are exactly those of
length exceeding $\dim V$.

Restriction is taken along the subgroup of permutations fixing $n$, and the
left place action is normalized with the inverse permutation on positions so
that it commutes with the diagonal action. The modular filtration is stated
without any splitting assertion; the companion page shows that the splitting
genuinely fails in positive characteristic.
