The non-relativistic classical mechanics category develops the Newtonian model
from explicit postulates and explicit definitions: the affine spacetime with
absolute time and Euclidean simultaneous slices, the inertial frames and the
Galilean transformations between them, the particle labels with their
worldlines and fixed masses, the configuration spaces of constrained systems,
the force and initial-data assumptions of the equations of motion, the
balance laws of momentum, angular momentum and energy, one-dimensional
conservative and dissipative models, two-body and central-force motion,
regular constraints and moving frames, the stationary-action and Hamiltonian
reformulations, and the stability, approximation and continuum developments
that follow. Physical postulates are adopted assumptions of a model; the
mathematical results of the category are complete conditional deductions from
those postulates and their stated hypotheses, and no theorem of the category
establishes that the model describes nature. Kinematical and dynamical claims
are always relative to a specified frame, a specified force law and the stated
regularity and idealization hypotheses, with empirical qualifications carried
on the pages where measured input is used. Package: the mathematics of the
track is developed on the companion mathematics category, and the physical
pages consume those items through the schema's dependency roles.
