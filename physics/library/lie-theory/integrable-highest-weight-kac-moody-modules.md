Verma universality and the unique simple quotient lead to the integrability criterion through explicit simple-root string calculations. The power-relation quotient is proved nonzero and integrable before it is used to establish sufficiency of dominant integrality.

The Weyl action on weights is implemented by actual invertible maps. A local coroot-sign and word-deletion argument supplies dominant representatives and the full stabilizer generated by zero-label reflections. In category O, the finite set of weights above any given weight ensures termination of the upward reflection procedure.

For symmetrizable matrices, the Casimir argument constructs its generalized eigenspaces and separates comparable primitive weights. The resulting direct-sum proof states its use of AC for choosing singular-space bases. Tensor products retain finite weight spaces. The final affine results use the intrinsic primitive central coroot, a finite trace contradiction at positive level, and exact rank-one powers at level zero; complementary Cartan scalar actions remain unrestricted.

All finite GCMs are permitted until symmetrizability or affine type is explicitly required. The weight-space isomorphisms and local string, chamber and affine arguments are choice-free. Only the arbitrary-basis interpretation of infinite multiplicities and the stated complete-reducibility decomposition invoke AC.
