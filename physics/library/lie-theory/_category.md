Lie theory connects smooth groups with the infinitesimal algebra that governs their local behavior. The foundational pages construct Lie algebras from invariant vector fields, develop the exponential map and the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula, and distinguish immersed subgroups from closed subgroups. Smooth actions, homogeneous spaces and covering groups provide the global geometric language.

The algebraic branch develops representations, universal enveloping algebras and the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem before studying solvable, nilpotent and semisimple Lie algebras. Cartan subalgebras, root decompositions, root systems and Dynkin diagrams lead to classification and finite-dimensional highest-weight representations. Compact groups bring maximal tori, Weyl integration, root data and Peter–Weyl theory; real forms add Cartan and Iwasawa decompositions.

The advanced representation-theory track continues through central characters, Verma modules, category O, BGG reciprocity and resolutions, character formulas, geometric representation theory and Kac–Moody algebras. General smooth-manifold machinery remains in Differential Geometry. Page statuses distinguish the published foundational material from draft advanced pages, and all prerequisites remain explicit across category boundaries.
