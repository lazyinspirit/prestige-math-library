Complete regularity supplies continuous $[0,1]$-valued functions that distinguish a point from a disjoint closed set. Together with the product topology, this gives the evaluation map and the cube-embedding characterization of Tychonoff spaces. The compactness route uses the ultrafilter lemma through the compact Hausdorff product theorem; density is then expressed by taking the closure of the evaluation image.

The page defines Hausdorff compactifications and the Stone–Čech extension property. It proves interval-valued extensions by coordinate projection, then uses dependent choice separately to embed an arbitrary compact Hausdorff target in a cube. Closedness of the embedded target ensures that the assembled coordinate map lands in that target, giving the universal property and its uniqueness consequences.
