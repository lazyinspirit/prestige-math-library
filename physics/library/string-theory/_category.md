String theory studies models in which extended objects and their interactions organize physical and mathematical questions. Its constructions bring together worldsheet field theory, symmetry, geometry, topology, and the analysis of spaces of solutions. Different models require different hypotheses, and statements about their mathematical structures must be distinguished from physical postulates and proposed interpretations. This collection records those distinctions explicitly rather than treating every formal construction as an established description of nature.

The development pages introduce the objects and assumptions needed for each topic, while their example companions work through applications and test the limits of the stated results. Links to conformal field theory and to the mathematical prerequisite pages supply the surrounding language. The material includes geometric and categorical settings as well as field-theoretic constructions; readers can follow the dependency links to identify which results a particular argument actually uses. Published open problems remain labeled as questions, and recorded physical assumptions retain their stated status. These conventions let the collection serve as a connected reference while keeping the scope of each claim visible.
