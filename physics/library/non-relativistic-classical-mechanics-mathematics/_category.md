These mathematics pages develop the structures used by classical mechanics: affine-time spaces and Galilean charts; rotations and their double cover; configuration and constrained systems; variational and ordinary differential equations; Hamiltonian and symplectic mechanics; balance identities, rigid motion, continua, stability and chaos, KAM, approximation, numerical integration, and evidence calculations. Definitions and results state mathematical hypotheses such as regularity, compactness, and domain conditions; their physical interpretation and applicability belong to the consuming physics models. Companion examples work through finite cases and countermodels and show where hypotheses enter.

Real analysis, linear algebra, topology, functional analysis, measure theory, differential geometry, complex analysis, probability, and PDE supply much of the prerequisite mathematics. Non-relativistic Classical Mechanics is the principal user. Specific affine-time, rotation, dynamics, balance, and continuum results also support Non-relativistic Quantum Mechanics, Relativistic Particle Mechanics, Classical Electromagnetism, General Relativity, Classical Statistical Mechanics, Quantum Statistical Mechanics, and Fluid Dynamics, as well as mathematical work in those fields. Quantum Field Theory and Einstein–Maxwell mathematics also use selected complex and dynamics results.
