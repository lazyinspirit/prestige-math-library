The companion page records explicit mathematical hypothesis boundaries of the
wave and retarded-root results used by the main page. The first clause of
[[ex-em-wave-equation-prerequisites-hypothesis-boundaries]] exhibits the
transport solution $u(x,t)=f(x_1-ct)$ of the wave operator for a nonzero
$f\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$: it is $C^\infty$ with $Wu=0$, its nonzero set is
$(ct+U)\times\mathbb R^2$, where $U=\{s:f(s)\ne0\}$ is nonempty and open,
and its spatial $L^2$ norm is infinite for every time, so
compact support of the data is a genuine hypothesis of the compact-support
clause of the wave Cauchy-Kirchhoff theorem and not a consequence of
smoothness. The second clause exhibits the curve
$z(s)=(\sqrt{c^2s^2+a^2},0,0)$, which is subluminal at every finite time but
has no uniform bound $|z'|\le v_*<c$ and for which the observation $(0,0)$ has
no retarded root, so the uniform speed bound is essential and the substitution
denominator $1-n\cdot\beta$ degenerates in the same limit. The third clause
shows that at an event on the worldline the unique root is $s=t$ and no root
below $t$ exists, so excluding the worldline is a genuine hypothesis of the
main theorem. All three checks are explicit mathematics with no measurement,
physical field identification or empirical claim.
