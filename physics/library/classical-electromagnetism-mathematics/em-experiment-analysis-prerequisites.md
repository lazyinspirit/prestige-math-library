The deterministic layer beneath the electromagnetic empirical-testing page: a
bounded-error comparison that confines the parameter of a monotone $C^1$
prediction to an explicit interval when the recorded indicator and the
calibration error are separately bounded. The companion lemma on the preceding
prerequisite page treats a real interval and a one-variable prediction; this
page transports it to an open Euclidean domain along a declared unit direction,
showing how the one-variable statement is applied to the restriction of a
$C^1$ prediction to a line, with the directional derivative bounded below by a
declared positive constant $m$.

Nothing here is a sampling law or a statistical inference: the bounds $s$,
$\delta$ and $m$ are parameters of the statement, and the conclusion
$|\theta|\le(s+\delta)/m$ is a conditional inequality whose empirical reading
belongs to the consuming physical item. The companion examples page records
the sharpness of this bound for an explicitly displayed linear prediction and
the failure of the conclusion once the positive lower bound is dropped.
