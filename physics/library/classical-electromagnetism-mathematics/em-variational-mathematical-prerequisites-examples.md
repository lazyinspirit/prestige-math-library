The companion page records, in one worked example,
[[ex-em-variational-mathematical-prerequisites-hypothesis-boundaries]], the
boundaries of the two variational lemmas of the main page. Part (i) exhibits
the saddle $S(x,y)=x^2-y^2$: its differential vanishes at the origin while
$S(\varepsilon,0)>0>S(0,\varepsilon)$ for every $\varepsilon\ne0$, so the
equivalence of [[lem-em-compact-field-variation]] between stationarity and the
coefficient equation, and the conversion of [[lem-em-fundamental-variation-lemma]]
of a vanishing first variation into pointwise equations, carry no minimality
and no existence content. Part (ii) fixes $U=(-1,1)^4$, $\kappa=1$, $J=0$ and
produces two smooth but non-compactly-supported directions: for
$A_0=(x^1)^2$ the coefficient equation fails ($\partial_1F^{10}=-2$) while the
first variation vanishes, and for $A_0=x^1$ the coefficient equations all hold
while $S[A+sh]=8(1+s)^2$ has derivative $16$ at $s=0$. The two witnesses show
that the compact-support hypothesis of the variation class cannot simply be
removed: the boundary-free coefficient conversion is exact precisely in the
declared compact class. No physical action, postulate or experimental claim is
used; the polynomial branch is choice-free and the Lebesgue-functional branch
carries Countable Choice exactly as the compact-field lemma does.
