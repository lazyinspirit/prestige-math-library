The Green correspondence compares indecomposable modules of a finite group with those of a subgroup containing the normalizer of a chosen p-subgroup. The admissible class is defined by exceptional intersections, and the restriction and induction errors use different subgroup families. Restriction errors need not have smaller-order vertices.

Begin with the family containment and fusion argument. Vertex retention and the separate inducing witness then supply the two existence statements. Mackey decomposition and finite cancellation isolate the errors before either uniqueness construction. The restriction construction precedes induction; their actual summand witnesses prove both inverse identities. The final theorem preserves a common vertex and does not assert a common source.

All modules are finite dimensional and all indecomposable inputs are nonzero. The module-theoretic arguments explicitly assume AC through the inherited finite-length decomposition chain. The elementary subgroup argument is choice-free. When the intermediate subgroup is the whole group, both exceptional families are empty and the correspondence is the identity.
