These examples keep the principal series constructions at concrete sizes. For
$\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_q)$ the finite Hecke algebra is two-dimensional
with basis $\{T_1=e_B,T_s\}$ and multiplication $T_s^2=(q-1)T_s+q\,T_1$, so it
is isomorphic to $\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C$ and to
$\mathbb C[T]/(T^2-(q-1)T-q)$; the same rank-one information determines the
spherical principal series $\mathbb C[\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_q)]=\mathbf 1\oplus
\operatorname{St}$ with the Steinberg representation of dimension $q$, the two
constituents each of multiplicity one, and the Hecke generator acting by $q$ on
the trivial constituent and by $-1$ on the Steinberg constituent.

The boundary case $q=2$ shows the collapse of the parametrising torus: over
$\mathbb F_2$ the diagonal torus is trivial, the Weyl action is trivial, the
regular characters disappear for $n\ge2$, and every principal series is
spherical, while the general theorems on constituents, endomorphism algebras
and Tits deformation remain valid. For $\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb F_q)$ the
examples separate the regular case $W_\chi=1$, where the principal series is
irreducible, the singular cases $W_\chi\cong S_2$ and $W_\chi=S_3$, where the
constituents are indexed by tuples of partitions with multiplicities
$1,1$ and $1,2,1$ respectively, and the endomorphism algebra is
$\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C$ or $\mathbb C[S_3]$.

The closing remark warns that the Tits isomorphism and the consequent partition
labelling of constituents are noncanonical; only the numerical invariants — the
number of simple constituents and their multiplicities as dimensions of simple
modules for the stabiliser — are independent of the deformation choices.
