This page develops the modular function of a locally compact Hausdorff group and the Banach $\ast$-algebra structure of its $L^{1}$ space. From a fixed left Haar measure $\mu$ it first derives that right translation scales $\mu$ by a unique positive scalar, which defines the modular function through $\int_{G}f(xg^{-1})\,d\mu(x)=\Delta_{G}(g)\int_{G}f\,d\mu(x)$; a cutoff-function argument proves that $\Delta_{G}$ is a continuous homomorphism into $\mathbb R_{>0}$, Haar change of variables under inversion follows, and compact, discrete and abelian groups are shown to be unimodular. The complex Haar spaces $L^{1}(G)$ and $L^{2}(G)$ are then defined and proved complete with $C_{c}(G)$ dense, convolution is constructed on $C_{c}(G)$ and extended to a submultiplicative product on $L^{1}(G)$, and the modularly corrected involution $f^{*}(x)=\Delta_{G}(x^{-1})\overline{f(x^{-1})}$ makes $L^{1}(G)$ a complex Banach $\ast$-algebra without a required unit.

The final items build a contractively bounded two-sided approximate identity, characterise discreteness of $G$ by the existence of a convolution unit, and prove that the left and right regular representations on $L^{2}(G)$ are strongly continuous unitary representations whose left action is faithful. Conventions fixed here: the left Haar measure is fixed once and for all and the modular convention is the one displayed above, so that a right translate by $g^{-1}$ scales the integral by $\Delta_{G}(g)$; the modular function is normalisation independent, and on a unimodular group the involution reduces to naive inversion. AC is assumed in the items whose statements say so and is declared together with its exact use: it supplies general Haar uniqueness, Urysohn-type cutoffs for compact sets inside open sets, and the density and completeness statements for the complex $L^{p}$ spaces.
