These computations specialise the main page to concrete groups. Under AC, the
positive affine group $G=(0,\infty)\rtimes\mathbb R$ carries the left Haar measure
$a^{-2}\,da\,db$ and has modular function $\Delta_{G}(a,b)=a^{-1}$, so it is
nonunimodular. On a discrete group with Haar measure
$\mu=c\cdot\text{counting}$, calculus on the absolutely convergent convolution
series gives the naive involution $f^{*}(x)=\overline{f(x^{-1})}$ and the norm-one
identity $c^{-1}\mathbf 1_{\{e\}}$. On a compact group with its normalized Haar
probability the constant function is idempotent, while the convolution of
matrix coefficients vanishes across inequivalent continuous finite-dimensional
irreducible unitary representations and equals the matrix unit
$(\langle u,z\rangle/\dim\pi)f^{\pi}_{w,v}$ for one and the same representation.

Under AC, the counterexample completes the picture negatively: on the
nonunimodular affine group, naive inversion without the modular factor fails to
be isometric on $L^{1}(G)$ and therefore is not the $L^{1}$ involution, whose
isometry is exactly what the modular correction supplies.
