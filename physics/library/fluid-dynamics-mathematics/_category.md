These pages construct mathematical models and estimates for fluid analysis: material configurations and Eulerian fields, conservative weak equations, periodic Fourier and Sobolev spaces, local Euler and Navier–Stokes evolution and energy identities, kinetic moments, filtered and non-Newtonian stresses, diffuse interfaces, rotating/stratified systems, reduced dynamics, and finite-ensemble or dependent-data statistics. Further results treat pressure recovery, numerical observables, conservation, admissibility, wall-defect and capillary-wave estimates, residual bounds and inverse identifiability. Statistical pages handle dependent profiles, uncertainty and numerical bias under stated data assumptions; numerical pages bound pressure and observable errors or enforce conservation and admissibility. Results carry their spaces, boundary and regularity conditions.

The pages use real and measure theory, functional analysis, PDE, probability and numerical-analysis suppliers, as well as selected mechanics and thermodynamics mathematics. Fluid Dynamics uses these estimates as mathematical premises while stating its own physical closures and interpretations. The conclusions here are mathematical bounds or constructions under declared data models, not empirical claims.
