These pages provide mathematical foundations for the fluid models in the companion collection. Material configurations, Eulerian fields and weak formulations specify the objects under study. Fourier and Sobolev constructions support periodic evolution and energy identities, while exact flow calculations and residual estimates give concrete analytic examples. Further pages develop filtered stresses, finite ensembles, kinetic moments, rotating and stratified systems, diffuse interfaces, reduced dynamics and inverse identifiability. Numerical constructions address pressure, observable error, conservation and admissibility; statistical constructions address dependent observations, uncertainty and bias budgets.

Every result retains its stated regularity, domain and parameter assumptions. These mathematical conclusions do not establish the physical applicability of a constitutive law or an adopted approximation. Consult the linked prerequisites for the precise spaces, operators and earlier estimates used in an argument, and use the examples companions to examine special cases and hypothesis boundaries.
