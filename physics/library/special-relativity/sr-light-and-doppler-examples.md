These computations accompany [[sr-light-and-doppler]]. The two pure
mathematical workups come first in subject matter: the geometry of the phase
level sets of a future null wave covector, where the kernel is positive
semidefinite with radical the line through $k^\sharp$ and that line is the only
null direction in the wave front ([[ex-sr-phase-level-sets-and-null-generators]]),
and a rational frame-data computation whose boost and inverse boost return the
original data ([[ex-sr-null-covector-frame-data-worked]]).

The model computations use the exact rational values $\gamma=5/4$ and
$\beta=3/5$. The longitudinal cases at $s=\pm e_1$ give the factors $1/2$ and
$2$ ([[ex-sr-doppler-longitudinal-beta-three-fifths]]); the two transverse
protocols give the factors $5/4$ and $4/5$ and show that a transverse Doppler
statement is incomplete until the frame in which transversality holds is named
([[ex-sr-transverse-doppler-protocols-worked]]); and the aberration workup at
$\cos\theta=4/5$ gives $s'=(5/13,12/13,0)$ with the frequency factor $13/20$
([[ex-sr-aberration-worked-beta-three-fifths]]). The companion
[[cex-sr-transverse-doppler-is-not-unshifted]] turns the two transverse
configurations into an exact counterexample to the universal claim that a
transverse orientation leaves the frequency unchanged.

The two mathematical examples use affine Minkowski geometry; the other four
items are conditional computations inside the adopted ideal plane-wave model.
None performs a measurement, reports data or makes an empirical claim,
and none may be used as a dependency of another page's content.
