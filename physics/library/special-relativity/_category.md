Special relativity adopts affine Minkowski spacetime with inertial covariance and ideal-clock and light assumptions, then develops frames and charts, worldlines, proper time, observer measurements, Lorentz transformations, energy and momentum, forces and continuum matter, causality and signals, Doppler effects, accelerated observers, and a Newtonian bridge. Radar synchronization, clock comparisons and signal order use explicit observer protocols and causal conditions. The affine model, causal cones and inertial charts are defined as distinct structures, with curve domains and causal conditions stated for worldlines. Mathematical results are conditional deductions; continuum conservation requires the stated model equations and, for integrated balances, an isolation premise. The pages do not claim gravity or electromagnetic coupling unless stated.

The definitions and calculations use mathematics in linear algebra, real analysis and differential geometry. Special-relativistic structures support classical electromagnetism, general relativity, relativistic particle mechanics and quantum field theory, including mathematical companion pages. Experimental comparisons remain distinct from deductions of the framework.
