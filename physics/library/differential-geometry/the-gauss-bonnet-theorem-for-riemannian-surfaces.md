The page fixes the orientation and sign conventions first, then proves the Gauss-Bonnet identity from them. An oriented Riemannian surface carries the positive quarter-turn $J$; a positively oriented orthonormal frame $(E_1,E_2)$ has connection one-form $\omega(X)=g(\nabla_XE_1,E_2)$, so that $\nabla_XE_1=\omega(X)E_2$ and $\nabla_XE_2=-\omega(X)E_1$ (the negative of the Lee-Datar convention). The signed geodesic curvature is $k_g=g(\nabla_TT,JT)$ for a positively oriented unit-speed curve, the signed exterior angle is fixed at ordinary corners, and the regular oriented surface region carries the outward-normal-first boundary orientation. From these definitions come the rotation law $\omega'=\omega+d\varphi$ under a frame rotation by angle $\varphi$, the structure equation $d\omega=-K\,dA$, the angle-derivative formula $\theta'=k_g-\omega(\dot\gamma)$ on a framed arc, the behaviour of $k_g$ under orientation and parameter reversal, and the identity expressing the total turning of a closed framed curve as the integral of geodesic curvature plus the frame holonomy. In the plane the Hopf turning tangent theorem proves $\int_\gamma k_{\mathrm{plane}}\,ds+\sum_j\alpha_j=2\pi$ for a simple closed curve bounding a positively oriented disk region, with the polygon corollary $\sum_j\alpha_j=2\pi$ for simple Euclidean polygons.

The local layer begins with Stokes for piecewise smooth surface regions and with a finite planar graph lemma for regular embedded edges with distinct incident tangent rays; under full choice it cuts such a graph in a coordinate disk into triangular disk faces with $V-E+F=1$. A regular disk region admits a finite frameable decomposition, and summing the frameable local Gauss-Bonnet formula over the face-to-face pieces, with interior-edge cancellation, yields
$$\int_DK\,dA+\int_{\partial D}k_g\,ds+\sum_j\alpha_j=2\pi$$
first for frameable disk regions and then for arbitrary compact regular oriented disk regions with finitely many ordinary corners. The geodesic-triangle theorem reads $\int_TK\,dA=\alpha+\beta+\gamma-\pi$, and the accompanying comparison records $\alpha+\beta+\gamma$ greater than, equal to, or less than $\pi$ according as the curvature integral is positive, zero, or negative.

The global layer builds the combinatorial machinery needed to sum the local formula. Under full choice a compact Riemannian surface region carries a finite curvilinear triangulation: the metric extends across a smooth boundary, generic small metric circles make a finite regular arrangement, and compatible planar face cuts yield regular triangular disks while preserving prescribed boundary arcs. A separate short-geodesic polygon cellulation follows by replacing finely subdivided interior edges with minimizing chords while retaining the boundary arcs. The stronger prescribed-boundary geodesic triangulation is also proved: a relative $C^2$ triangulation is refined conformingly, small corner stars are replaced by geodesic caps, and only interior edges are straightened by short minimizing chords while prescribed boundary arcs are retained. Curvilinear triangulations induce finite CW structures, and the Euler characteristic $\chi(M)=V-E+F$ is proved independent of the triangulation and of any metric, invariant under edge and face subdivision, additive under gluing along a compatible finite one-dimensional subcomplex, and computed face by face by the local summation lemma. The Gauss-Bonnet expression itself is independent of the metric. The global identities then follow in three forms: $\int_MK\,dA=2\pi\chi(M)$ for closed oriented surfaces, $\int_MK\,dA+\int_{\partial M}k_g\,ds+\sum_j\alpha_j=2\pi\chi(M)$ for compact oriented surfaces with boundary and corners (in two presentations, with the smooth-boundary corollary when the corner set is empty), and the orientation-free density form $\int_MK\,\mu_g=2\pi\chi(M)$ for closed nonorientable surfaces, which uses no orientation cover, classification, or characteristic-class theory. The corollaries record that a flat closed oriented surface has $\chi=0$, that a closed oriented surface with $K>0$ everywhere has $\chi>0$, and that the total Gaussian curvature of a closed oriented surface does not depend on the metric, while the polygon corollary removes the geodesic-curvature boundary term for geodesic polygons.

Six false statements delimit the theory: geodesic curvature is not the ordinary Euclidean curvature of a space curve in general; the connection one-form is not independent of the chosen orthonormal frame; not every geodesic triangle has angle sum $\pi$; the Gauss-Bonnet formula for a surface with boundary does have a boundary term; one may not define $\chi(M)$ by choosing a triangulation without proving independence; and classical Gauss-Bonnet by itself does not classify compact surfaces. A closing scope remark separates the surface theorem proved here from higher-dimensional Chern-Gauss-Bonnet statements. Full AC is declared in the finite curvilinear triangulation and its local and global Gauss–Bonnet consumers because its planar Jordan and graph suppliers use it. Countable choice suffices for the separate collar, metric-extension, Stokes, and density-integration inputs.
