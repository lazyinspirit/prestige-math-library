## Definition

Let $\mathcal T$ be the set of isomorphism classes of finite nonempty rooted trees. A rooted tree is a finite connected acyclic graph with one distinguished vertex, the root; each edge is oriented from the vertex nearer the root (the parent) to the vertex farther from the root (the child). A forest is a finite multiset of rooted trees, including the empty forest $1$. Let $H=\mathbb Q[\mathcal T]$ be the commutative polynomial algebra generated by $\mathcal T$; its monomial basis consists of forests, and the degree of a forest is its total number of vertices.

For a rooted tree $t$, an admissible cut is a subset $C$ of its edges meeting every root-to-leaf path in at most one edge. Removing the edges of $C$ leaves one component containing the root; denote it by $R_C(t)$. The components on the child side of the cut edges form a forest denoted by $P_C(t)$. For the empty edge cut, set $P_{\varnothing}(t)=1$ and $R_{\varnothing}(t)=t$. Define the coproduct on a tree generator by
$$\Delta(t)=t\otimes1+\sum_{C\text{ admissible}}P_C(t)\otimes R_C(t),$$
where the sum includes the empty edge cut and retains multiplicities of distinct edge subsets. The term $t\otimes1$ is the separate virtual full cut above the root. Extend $\Delta$ multiplicatively to forests. Define $\varepsilon(1)=1$ and $\varepsilon(F)=0$ for every nonempty forest $F$. This specifies the rooted-tree subtraction model; it does not assert a canonical rooted-tree Hopf algebra or a graph-to-forest map for every Feynman graph.
