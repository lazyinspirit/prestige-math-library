## Statement

Assume full AC and the inherited countable choice of colored ribbon evaluation. Fix a complex modular ribbon fusion category C with simple unit, and the full cyclic framed genus-zero structure A realized by its ribbon cup/cap calculus, including the two-point pairing, its coevaluation, the vacuum normalization, rotation and duality. The space of modular-functor extensions of these fixed data, with the source extension-comparison maps of the supplied equivalence definition, is contractible whenever it is nonempty. A concrete terminal comparison extension is constructed from boundary-linear handlebody skein functors below.

For the strongly rational VOA modular functor F_V and its functor-induced modular fusion category C_V, the full cyclic structure A is canonically identified with that of the RT construction of C_V. There is a specified comparison through the common skein extension, preserving the genus-zero section and every sewing map. In particular this matches the Hodge and RT anomalies, rather than assuming that their bare surface representations agree. For a direct equivalence between scalar-central models, first push the integer-weight RT extension out by its actual central scalar kappa; both scalar extensions then map equivalently to the same skein extension. No equality between an integer kernel and a scalar kernel is claimed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The modular category and the exact cyclic datum A; in the final application use the proved VOA modular functor and its induced category. Every extension comparison is relative to those cyclic data, not only to a balanced braided equivalence. Below C^n means the Deligne power $\mathcal C^{\boxtimes n}$, with C^0 equal to finite-dimensional vector spaces. First work on a connected surface, corresponding to one corolla. For a disjoint union take the Cartesian product of its connected extension groupoids, retain componentwise natural isomorphisms, and tensor the value functors; one does not replace that product groupoid by all scalar automorphisms of the total tensor functor.

## Proof

1.1 We first identify the cyclic genus-zero data, keeping variance explicit. Write k_F(X,Y) for the two-point coinvariant functor of F_V. It is the dual of the block functor Hom(X,Y'), hence covariant in both X and Y. Let epsilon(Z)=k_F(1,Z). Vacuum propagation and the simple unit give epsilon(S)=0 unless S=1, and epsilon(1)=C with its fixed vacuum vector. Therefore the map Hom(1,Z)→epsilon(Z), f↦epsilon(f)(1), is a natural isomorphism: check on each simple and extend over finite direct sums. The product induced from the three-point functor has, by its representing property and cyclicity, the canonical identification k_F(X,Y)≅epsilon(X⊗Y). Consequently $$k_F(X,Y)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(1,X\otimes Y),$$ with its vacuum normalization, which is exactly the RT two-point pairing. This identifies the pairing itself, not only its dimensions or its dual-object permutation. [F1, F3, construct]

1.2 We describe a concrete universal recipient for extensions of A. For an oriented surface Sigma let K(Sigma) be its linear skein category. Its objects are finite embedded disks carrying objects of C. Its morphisms are finite colored framed ribbon graphs with coupons in Sigma×[0,1], with the prescribed endpoint disks, modulo boundary-relative isotopy and every local ball relation from the actual C-valued evaluator of F2. Adjoin finite sums and cokernels with their universal relations. This gives a finitely cocomplete category: a finite presentation is a finite sum of object symbols modulo finitely many morphism relations; a morphism is represented on presentations, with two representatives identified exactly when their difference factors through those relations. Thus a linear functor to a finitely cocomplete target extends uniquely, up to its unique coherent isomorphism, when it respects these presentations. On a disk this category is C itself: tensor the disk labels, evaluate the ribbon graph, and replace any local ball diagram by its coupon; these are inverse constructions. Boundary collars act by inserting annular skeins. Denote that monoidal collar category by B(Sigma); it acts also on C^n by capping the n boundary circles by labeled disks. We retain this collar-module action throughout. [F2, F5, construct]

2.1 The same representing identification at three points gives the RT Hom realization for the pair of pants. Under it, the induced associator and braiding are by definition the two genus-zero sewing and exchange comparisons of F_V. The unit and the two rotations give its chosen duality and ribbon twist. Every cyclic framed genus-zero operation is obtained by placing finite coupons, crossings, cups, caps and twists in a punctured sphere: choose one output temporarily, cut the sphere along arcs joining its other boundary disks to that output, and read the resulting ribbon diagram. Moving the cutting arcs changes that diagram by the finite relations of F2. Their evaluations agree in F_V and RT because their product, associator, braiding, chosen dual maps and twist have just been identified; F2 includes full coupon rotations and mates. Thus the identifications respect cyclic output changes, the pairing, coevaluation and all genus-zero mapping classes. They constitute a fixed equivalence of cyclic framed data A. No comparison with a different VOA tensor construction is used. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 We will use the following explicit excision property of K, rather than an unproved factorization-homology theorem. Cut a ribbon graph transversely to the gluing annulus of a sewn surface. Compactness and transversality give finitely many crossings, each with its actual color, orientation and framing. The two cut diagrams are glued by the matching evaluation/coevaluation. The imposed relations identifying the two collar actions are exactly the balanced gluing relations. Cutting then gluing is the identity. Gluing then cutting can create extra consecutive intersections, but their removal is the appropriate typed snake of F2. An isotopy of the graph has, after finite generic subdivision relative its coupons, only a transverse crossing, a birth/death pair at the annulus, or a coupon passage; the local equations are respectively collar naturality, the snake, and the coupon relation of F2. Hence the construction is independent of the chosen transverse representative. This proves the universal balanced gluing property on generators, relations, and their natural transformations. For several annuli the same finite cuts in either order give the same diagram, so the excision isomorphisms are coherently associative. [F2, F3, step 1.2, algebra]

2.3 For a marked handlebody H with boundary Sigma, construct a B(Sigma)-linear functor P_H:K(Sigma)→C^n as follows. For an internal disk configuration X, take the finite RT skein space of H with those disks and n external disk labels Y. As a functor of Y it has a unique representing object for the pairing of step 1.1. Explicitly, for any finite right-exact functor L(Y), its representative is $$R(L)=\bigoplus_{i_1,\ldots,i_n}L(S_{i_1}',\ldots,S_{i_n}')\otimes(S_{i_1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes S_{i_n}),$$ since k(S_i,Y)=Hom(S_i',Y). This formula and the finite identity decompositions prove both existence and uniqueness, including natural transformations. Use it to define P_H(X). Pushing a ribbon graph from Sigma×[0,1] into H defines its morphism map by F2–F4. The local relations and finite presentation construction make it a right-exact functor. Collar linearity follows by moving the collar ribbon graph before or after its insertion; both are the same marked graph in H. Thus its module structure is specified as well. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2, construct]

3.1 We record carefully its automorphisms. On a closed capped boundary, place a zero-framed Kirby meridian along each disk of a complete meridian system, and divide by D² per meridian. F4 makes their product P the projector to the all-vacuum handle colors. On the empty boundary-label list this has rank one, with nonzero vector v_H, by the finite Hom formula of F3. For any disk-label configuration, a colored spine state of H can be pushed into a boundary collar: use a standard bouquet of longitudes, move its vertex ball to the boundary, and route each longitude and its endpoint bands at distinct collar heights. Its actual coupons and twists are retained. Therefore every state is T_a v_H for a finite linear combination of collar skeins T_a. Choose complete composition-dual coupons for the opposite state spaces. F4's full meridian/coupon identity says that P T^a T_b P is its computed cap Gram entry times P. Its Gram matrix is invertible by F4; multiplying by its inverse gives collar skeins with $$P T^aT_bP=\delta_{ab}P.$$ Consequently T_aPT^b are all matrix units on the state spaces. This is an actual operator construction, not a dimension or irreducibility assertion. A natural automorphism of P_H commutes with these operators and hence is scalar on its empty-configuration space. It is the same scalar on every disk configuration because those spaces are generated by T_av_H. With external boundary circles, pair the output object with arbitrary external labels and move their labeled disks into the capped surface. Collar-module linearity identifies these paired maps with the same disk-configuration calculation. The representing formula in step 2.3 then shows that the scalar is common on all output objects. Hence every boundary-linear automorphism of P_H is multiplication by one scalar in C*. [F2, F3, F4, step 2.3, algebra]

3.2 We prove universality using only the modular-functor axioms. Let (Q,B) be any extension of the same fixed cyclic A, and let q be an object in the fiber over Sigma. For a disk configuration phi remove those disks. Capping them with the vacuum gives an equivalence of Q fibers by F5. The groupoid of its lifts over q is contractible: for an equivalence E→D, the category of pairs (e,Ee→q) has a unique arrow between any two such pairs, by full faithfulness. Choose a lift and evaluate B on the punctured surface, putting the internal labels on those new boundaries. The functor of external labels is represented by an object in C^n using step 2.3. Disk motions, fusions and coupons give its skein morphism maps; the contractible lift choices supply their canonical identifications. The local ball relations are exactly the cyclic A operations, so F2 makes this a well-defined functor L_q:K(Sigma)→C^n. Boundary gluing gives its B(Sigma)-module structure. The construction is natural in q and compatible with every graph operation by step 2.2 and the coherence already required of B. [F2, F5, step 2.2, step 2.3, construct]

4.1 Let Omega(Sigma) be the full groupoid of B(Sigma)-linear functors K(Sigma)→C^n whose objects are isomorphic to some P_H. It is connected. Indeed the full marked RT functor identifies the skein realizations for any two reference handlebodies through their parametrized mapping cylinders; the maps are natural under insertion of collar graphs by F3. Different weights give scalar natural isomorphisms, which are retained in the full groupoid. Step 3.1 identifies every automorphism group with C*. A diffeomorphism of Sigma acts on K by transporting all graphs and sends P_H to the corresponding P_fH with its actual boundary parametrization. Gluing handlebodies along their marked disks and applying the excision of step 2.2 gives the graph operations on Omega. On the objects these are exactly F3's finite sewing/cutting resolutions; on morphisms use the universal balanced gluing property of step 2.2. Its associativity and tensor interchange prove all modular-operad coherences. The category of pairs (Sigma,P), with these transported isomorphisms as morphisms, is therefore an extension Surf_A of Surf, with nonempty connected fibers Omega(Sigma). [F2, F3, step 2.2, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 A ball gives the genus-zero section: its skein functor is exactly the cyclic datum A and all its maps are its actual coupon evaluations. Genus-zero weight anomalies vanish because its closed homology is zero, so this section is coherent under genus-zero gluing. Capping an auxiliary boundary with the vacuum is an equivalence on Omega fibers. Its functor is induced by unit insertion; it is essentially surjective because these fibers are connected, and is fully faithful because it sends the scalar automorphism group C* identically to C*. This proves the insertion-of-vacua requirement. Evaluation at the empty internal configuration, followed by pairing with external labels, defines the Surf_A-algebra F_A. The representative formula of step 2.3 and the skein excision identify its sewing with the actual finite coevaluation contractions. Its genus-zero restriction is A. Thus (Surf_A,F_A) is a specific modular-functor extension, not a formal assumption about existence. [F2, F3, F5, step 2.3, step 4.1, algebra]

5.2 Its image lies in Omega(Sigma), with a specified identification over each handlebody. To verify this without assuming a handlebody-envelope classification, cut a handlebody H into balls by a complete meridian system and form the lift q_H by the genus-zero section and those sewings. Factorization for B identifies its states with the same finite Hom resolutions as F3, since all ball functors are the fixed cyclic A. This identification is natural on every collar skein: cut the graph at the meridian disks, apply the local coupon evaluations, and glue the repeated pairs by the exact snakes. These are the same finite words in B and RT, by the fixed cyclic identification. Choose this one marked handlebody and this one cut system once for Sigma. No independence from that choice is needed to define L_q, which was already constructed intrinsically in step 3.2; the cut system only witnesses that one of its values lies in the full groupoid Omega. That groupoid is independent of the reference handlebody by the actual RT comparisons in step 4.1. Thus no theorem about the contractibility of meridian systems or a derived handlebody envelope is being assumed. Since the Q fiber is connected and L is functorial, every L_q is isomorphic to P_H. Thus L defines a map psi_B:Q→Surf_A of extensions, relative to A, and an equivalence B≃psi_B*F_A. [F2, F3, F5, step 3.1, step 3.2, algebra]

6.1 This construction is universal on comparison maps and their coherent isomorphisms. Evaluation at the empty configuration recovers B by the representing formula, so pulling back F_A along psi_B returns B with its specified equivalence. Conversely start with theta:Q→Surf_A. Apply step 3.2 to theta*F_A. Removing internal disks and representing the resulting functors reconstructs theta(q) on every disk configuration: for P_H this is its definition using H with those extra marked disks; every object of Omega is isomorphic to such a P_H, and its full boundary-linear natural transformations carry that identity to the chosen object. Step 2.2 verifies the compatibility on all morphisms. The same assertion on skein generators reconstructs every morphism and its coherence. Hence psi_(theta*F_A) is canonically isomorphic to theta. These two identifications are inverse on natural transformations because the full subgroupoid Omega contains all boundary-linear isomorphisms and a transformation is determined by all its configuration components. This proves an equivalence of comparison groupoids $$\operatorname{Map}(Q,\mathsf{Surf}_A)\simeq\operatorname{Ext}(A;Q),\qquad\theta\mapsto\theta^*F_A,$$ natural in Q. It is a proved finite skein universal property, not an invocation of the general classification theorem. [step 2.3, step 5.1, step 3.2, step 5.2, algebra]

7.1 Let Ext(A) be the bicategory of all extensions with fixed cyclic A and their comparison maps from F5. Step 6.1 identifies it with the slice bicategory of extension maps into Surf_A: an object is psi:Q→Surf_A, a morphism is a triangle commuting up to its specified isomorphism, and a 2-morphism is its compatible modification. The identity of Surf_A is terminal in this slice, in the coherent sense: its comparison from any object is that object's own psi, and its comparison groupoid has a terminal specified triangle. Thus its nerve is contractible. Explicitly append this terminal vertex to every simplex; the triangle isomorphisms give its faces and the compatibility modifications give its degeneracies, producing a contraction to that vertex. Localizing the comparison arrows, as in F5, gives the same resulting homotopy type; therefore the space of extensions and all relative coherent equivalence choices is contractible. This is the claimed uniqueness for the full cyclic datum. It would be false if one merely required direct equivalences of all original extension groupoids. [F5, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 Apply this to F_V and RT after the actual cyclic identification already constructed. Their universal comparison maps give the explicit zigzag $$(Q_V,F_V)\longrightarrow(\mathsf{Surf}_A,F_A)\longleftarrow(Q_{RT},F_{RT}).$$ The two algebra equivalences are those constructed configurationwise by the skein universal property above. They preserve pairings, duality, rotation, every mapping-class operator, capping and sewing because their maps were boundary-linear and graph-compatible at each construction. This supplies the cyclic and framing comparisons; it is not an inference from a balanced braided category alone. Step 7.1 gives the contractible-choice assertion with the required relative data. [F1, F3, F5, step 2.1, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 One may also compare faithful scalar models directly. If the RT central integer weight acts by kappa^n, replace its extension by the scalar pushout: pairs (g,z) are identified by $(g\cdot n,z)\sim(g,\kappa^nz)$, and act by z times the given weighted RT operator. This defines a modular extension because weights and their Maslov composition are additive with precisely the already proved central multiplier; its genus-zero section and vacuum maps are unchanged. Its fiber is BC* and its scalar center acts faithfully. The Hodge model likewise has scalar center acting by multiplication. Under the universal comparisons both scalar kernels map identically to the C* automorphisms of step 3.1. On each arity the base mapping-class group is the same. The exact kernel-to-total-group sequences then make both universal maps fully faithful and essentially surjective: lift a base arrow, and correct the remaining arrow by its unique scalar; the same argument proves injectivity. Hence they are equivalences of extensions relative to genus zero. A quasi-inverse and its coherence are transported through these fully faithful maps; their unit/counit choices form a contractible groupoid. This verifies an actual matched-extension realization of the RT-type equivalence as well as the localized one. Raw Z and C* kernels were never identified. All claims now follow. [F1, F3, F5, step 3.1, step 5.1, step 8.1, algebra] ∎
