## Statement

Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ ([[def-countable-choice]]). Every morphism of the
framed oriented tangle category $\mathcal T$ of
[[def-the-framed-oriented-tangle-category]] is a finite composite of tensor
products of identities, crossings, cups, caps and full twists.

A complete presentation on this elementary family is Turaev's reduced
presentation (3.1.a), (3.2.a)--(3.2.h), **together with** the expressions
(2.5.d)--(2.5.e) and (3.1.b)--(3.1.c) defining its additional orientation
variants. Thus two elementary words represent the same framed tangle exactly
when these relations and the strict monoidal axioms relate them.

Here the source's $X^\delta$ denotes our $X^\delta_{+,+}$, its
$Z^\delta$ denotes our $X^{-\delta}_{+,-}$, and its $Y^\delta$ denotes our
$X^{-\delta}_{-,+}$; its $T^\delta$ denotes our $X^\delta_{-,-}$.
The source's positive cup and cap are $\cup_+$ and $\cap_+$, and its
$\varphi,\varphi'$ are $\varphi_+^+,\varphi_+^-$.
The reduced generators are therefore $X^\delta_{+,+}$,
$X^{-\delta}_{+,-}$, $\varphi_+^{\pm1}$, $\cup_+$ and $\cap_+$.
The other cups, caps and crossings are the expressions above; a twist on a
negative strand is obtained by bending the corresponding twisted positive
strand with a cup and cap. Turaev's convention that a positive strand points
downwards is transported to ours by reversing all strand orientations; this
preserves composition, tensor product and framed isotopy. Crossing
superscripts in his mixed-orientation pictures are oriented signs, explaining
the minus signs in the dictionary.

The reduced relations are Yang--Baxter, the two zig-zags, inverse crossings
and twists, curl slide, the bent-crossing inverse relation (3.2.g), and the
twist-square relation (3.2.h). For example, the latter has the well-typed form

$$(\varphi_+^+)^2=(\cap_+\otimes1_+)(1_-\otimes X^+_{+,+})(X^-_{+,-}\otimes1_+)(\cup_+\otimes1_+).$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ and the category $\mathcal T$ with its fixed framing and crossing conventions.

[L1] The objects, elementary tangles, composition, tensor product and boundary-relative framed isotopies are those of [[def-the-framed-oriented-tangle-category]].

[L2] Parametric transversality is [[thm-parametric-transversality]]. Its relative use below is obtained by multiplying perturbations by a cutoff vanishing on the fixed collars; no perturbation is made there.

[F1] The source for the notation and the reconstruction below is Turaev, I §§2.1, 3.1–3.2 and 4.1–4.8, printed pp. 34–35, 49–51 and 57–69. The source's completeness assertion is not used as a premise.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **Algebra to be checked.** Put $A=+$, $B=-$, $b=\cup_+$, $d=\cap_+$, $p=\varphi_+^+$, $q=\varphi_+^-$, and write $x_\delta,z_\delta,y_\delta,t_\delta$ for the source's $X^\delta,Z^\delta,Y^\delta,T^\delta$ under the dictionary in the Statement. Composition is read right to left. Here are the reduced relations in full, with identities on the indicated letters understood: $$ (1_A\otimes x_+)(x_+\otimes1_A)(1_A\otimes x_+)=(x_+\otimes1_A)(1_A\otimes x_+)(x_+\otimes1_A), $$ $$ (1_A\otimes d)(b\otimes1_A)=1_A,\qquad(d\otimes1_B)(1_B\otimes b)=1_B, $$ $$ x_+x_-=x_-x_+=1_{AA},\qquad pq=qp=1_A,\qquad x_\delta(1_A\otimes p)=(p\otimes1_A)x_\delta, $$ $$ z_\delta y_{-\delta}=1_{BA},\qquad y_{-\delta}z_\delta=1_{AB},\qquad p^2=(d\otimes1_A)(1_B\otimes x_+)(z_+\otimes1_A)(b\otimes1_A). $$ The abbreviation in the bent-crossing relation is $$ y_\delta=(d\otimes1_A\otimes1_B)(1_B\otimes x_\delta\otimes1_B)(1_B\otimes1_A\otimes b). $$ The remaining defining abbreviations are $$ t_\delta=(d\otimes1_B\otimes1_B)(1_B\otimes y_\delta\otimes1_B)(1_B\otimes1_B\otimes b),\quad b'=(1_B\otimes q)z_+b,\quad d'=dz_-(p\otimes1_B). $$ Thus $b'=\cup_-$ and $d'=\cap_-$. These equations are precisely the numbered source relations in the Statement, with no additional relation silently imposed. [L1, given, construct]

2.1 **Geometric soundness and framings.** A framed core thickens to a narrow band by using the oriented tangent and its normal vector; a band gives its core and band normal. Compactness gives uniform width, and homotopies relative to the endpoint collars give isotopies of sufficiently narrow bands. A framing relative to the blackboard framing on an interval, or on a circle cut at a regular point, is a map to the circle; lift its angle to the real line on that interval. Its endpoint difference is an integral multiple of $2\pi$. Put those full rotations into disjoint small subintervals and straighten the rest by interpolating the lifted angle. Hence any framing can be encoded by finitely many full twists. The displayed relations are band isotopies: three successive crossings slide past one another, a cup–cap retracts, inverse crossings cancel, opposite full rotations cancel, and a small twist translates along a band past a crossing. Bending a crossing by a cup and cap gives $y_\delta,t_\delta$; cancellation of the resulting two crossings gives the two $z_\delta y_{-\delta}$ relations. Turning a cup or cap to the opposite side drags its adjacent band through a full rotation, giving exactly $b',d'$. In the last relation, follow the band from its input through the cup, the two crossings, and the cap: its transverse direction makes two positive full rotations and its core straightens. This proves the twist-square relation with the displayed signs, rather than identifying a single curl with the identity. All these motions have compact support away from the fixed collars. [L1, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 **Generic diagrams and words.** Project a band core to the $(x,t)$ strip, separate overpasses using its $y$ coordinate, and record the framing by the full twists of step 2.1. Perturb only the compact part away from the collars so that the projection is immersed with finitely many transverse double points, no triple points, and the height on the projected arcs is Morse, with distinct crossing and critical levels. Here and in a one-parameter family, this genericity can be obtained as follows. Embed the finite union of parameter intervals and circles times the isotopy interval in Euclidean space. Perturb the two projected coordinates by a collar cutoff times all coordinate monomials of degree at most $43$. At at most six distinct source points these perturbations prescribe independent jets through order three: multiply a prescribed degree-three Taylor polynomial at one point by the product of the squared distances to the other points to the fourth power, divide its nonzero Taylor factor there, and sum. The degree is at most $8(6-1)+3=43$. Apply [L2] to the resulting submersive jet maps and choose a small parameter; openness of slice embeddings keeps the spatial family an isotopy. For a single diagram the same construction with no family parameter gives the asserted regularity and Morse conditions. A slice has finitely many singularities: regular projected arcs are locally injective; away from their deleted diagonals the double configurations are compact and transverse, and Morse critical points form a closed discrete subset of a compact set. Cut the strip between consecutive singular heights. Every resulting factor is an identity tensored with a crossing, cup, cap or recorded twist. Replace the orientation variants by step 1.1. Conversely every formal word can be put into this sliced form: $(f\otimes g)=(f\otimes1)(1\otimes g)$, and repeat by induction on the word. Changing cuts adds identities; interchanging disjoint factors is interchange. [L1, L2, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 **The finite movie list, relative to the boundary.** For a one-parameter isotopy the same independent-jet construction gives the following events. A zero projected tangent has two equations on $(u,s)$, hence is isolated; its additional degeneracies have positive extra codimension and are absent. Its local form is $(u^2,u^3+su)$ after changes of coordinates, giving a curl birth or death. Double equality has two equations on $(u,v,s)$; tangency adds one equation, giving isolated quadratic contacts, locally two branches differing by $v^2+s$. Triple equality has four equations on $(u,v,w,s)$, giving isolated transverse triple events; quadruple equality has codimension six on five variables and is absent. Degenerate contacts and coincidences of event times add a further equation and are absent, using independent jets at at most six points. Near a regular source diagonal one coordinate is strictly monotone; near a cusp the displayed local form has just its single local double arc, with no tangency or triple event accumulating there. Thus the exceptional set is finite. Crossing heights in space stay distinct, so the quadratic contacts give R2, the triple events give precisely compatible R3, and the cusp gives R1. The same argument applied to height jets on a planar isotopy gives: exchange of two disjoint singular levels; birth/death of two extrema; and passage of a crossing through an extremum. A cap passage reduces to a cup passage by inserting a cup–cap pair outside its disk, transporting it below the crossing, and retracting it afterwards. All cutoffs vanish on the boundary collars, so there are no endpoint events. On the complementary intervals, continue crossings in disjoint disks and extend the motions over the intervening edges by normal-coordinate cutoffs, producing planar isotopies. This proves the relative finite movie assertion instead of applying a theorem about closed unframed links. [L2, step 3.1, construct]

4.2 **Second moves: eight cases.** Encode orientation by $A,B$ at the lower ends and choose which branch is over. For $AA$, the two cases cancel by $x_-x_+=1$ and $x_+x_-=1$. For $BA$, the two cancel by $z_+y_-=1$ and $z_-y_+=1$; for $AB$ they cancel by $y_-z_+=1$ and $y_+z_-=1$. For $BB$, expand both $t$'s. Interchange separates the two outer cup–cap pairs, retract the inner pair by $(1_A\otimes d)(b\otimes1_A)=1_A$, cancel the central $x_+x_-$ (or $x_-x_+$), then retract the remaining pair by $(d\otimes1_B)(1_B\otimes b)=1_B$. The result is $1_{BB}$ in both orders. This accounts for all $4\cdot2=8$ oriented second moves. [step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 **Third moves: reduction of all orientations and crossing orders.** Label the crossings in the triangular three-strand picture $C_1,C_2,C_3$, with the strands labelled by the pairs of crossings they traverse. Reflecting a crossing horizontally replaces its type/sign by $X^\delta\mapsto X^{-\delta}$, $Y^\delta\mapsto Z^{-\delta}$, $Z^\delta\mapsto Y^{-\delta}$, $T^\delta\mapsto T^{-\delta}$; call this operation a bar. Cancellation from step 4.2 shows that the truth of a third move is unchanged by the two operations $$(C_1,C_2,C_3)\mapsto(C_2,\overline C_1,\overline C_3),\qquad(C_1,C_2,C_3)\mapsto(\overline C_1,\overline C_3,C_2).$$ Indeed multiply both candidate words at top and bottom by the reflected end crossing; cancel the adjacent inverse pairs on opposite sides. Thus these are reversible deductions, including when the strands have opposite directions. If all types are $X$, these operations arrange equal signs on the first and third crossings. Compatibility (one strand lies above both others) forces the middle sign to agree; the positive case is the first equation of step 1.1 and the negative case is its inverse. To reduce orientations, first use the two operations to remove $Z$ from the first and third slots. If both other slots have type $Z$, the remaining slot is $X$, and applying the first then the second operation does this. Compatibility then excludes $Z$ from the middle slot too. The types are now $X,Y,T$. If the strand through the first two crossings points in the source's positive direction, all three strands do, since otherwise one of those crossings would be $Z$. Otherwise bend that strand with a cup and cap. The definitions of $y,t$ replace each incident type by $T^\delta\mapsto Y^\delta$ or $Y^\delta\mapsto X^\delta$, and the zig-zag retracts the auxiliary bend. The new triple is $(C_3,C_1^*,C_2^*)$, and its third strand is positive. If necessary repeat on its first strand; then all are positive. Each bending is reversible by tensoring with the opposite bend and using a zig-zag. There are at most two bends. These explicit reductions cover the eight orientation choices and the six possible spatial height orders; an incompatible cyclic over/under triangle is never an isotopy event. [step 1.1, step 4.2, algebra]

6.1 **Cup passages and opposite snakes.** Define $C_{A,A}=x_+$, $C_{A,B}=z_-$, $C_{B,A}=y_-$, $C_{B,B}=t_+$; the opposite family is $\overline C_{U,V}=C_{V,U}^{-1}$, using step 4.2. The four cup passages are the following two equations, each for $V=A,B$: $$(1_A\otimes C_{V,B})(C_{V,A}\otimes1_B)(1_V\otimes b)=b\otimes1_V,\qquad(1_A\otimes\overline C_{V,B})(\overline C_{V,A}\otimes1_B)(1_V\otimes b)=b\otimes1_V.$$ Here is an algebraic way to check them without assuming naturality. Bend the $A$ output on each side down by a $d$. This is an injective operation on words modulo the two snakes: its inverse inserts $b$ and retracts the adjacent snake. For $V=A$ the bent equation is precisely $z_-y_+=1_{BA}$, respectively $z_+y_-=1_{BA}$, after the definition of $y$ is substituted. For $V=B$, substitute the definition of $t$ first; the same bending retracts an additional $b,d$ pair and leaves those same inverse equations with one outer $B$ identity. The inverse-order cancellations supply the two reversed passages. Bending the other external endpoint gives the cap-passage equations; injectivity is proved by the other snake. Thus the checks cover both strand directions and both over/under choices. Next put $e=dz_-$ and $h=z_+b$. The same cup and cap equations, with their outer $b,d$ pairs retracted, give $(e\otimes1_A)(1_A\otimes h)=1_A$ and $(1_B\otimes e)(h\otimes1_B)=1_B$. This is the usual braided-dual cancellation, here derived from the reduced equations. Now $d'=e(p\otimes1_B)$ and $b'=(1_B\otimes q)h$. Their first snake is $q[(e\otimes1_A)(1_A\otimes h)]p=qp=1_A$; in the second snake the internal factors $p,q$ are adjacent and cancel, leaving $(1_B\otimes e)(h\otimes1_B)=1_B$. For a passage across $b'$ expand it as $(1_B\otimes q)z_+b$, perform the $b$ passages above and the third move on the extra $z_+$, then move $q$ out. This last slide follows for $x$ from the $p$ slide and $pq=1$; for $y$ it follows by substituting its definition, sliding on its middle $x$ and retracting its snake; for $z$ invert that identity. These are all crossings incident to the $A$ leg carrying $q$. Thus the four opposite-cup passages are checked as well. Cap passages across $d'$ use the same argument with $p$ in place of $q$. [step 1.1, step 4.2, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 **All height and paired-curl moves.** An exchange of disjoint levels is interchange. Creation/deletion of two extrema uses one of the four snakes proved in steps 1.1 and 6.1. Passage of a crossing past an extremum is one of the eight cup passages of step 6.1 or its cap counterpart. To check the remaining paired-curl move, expand the opposite cup/cap in the curl directed toward the other side of the strand and move $q$ to its exterior by the slides of step 6.1. Reading the remaining cup, two crossings and cap from bottom to top gives exactly $$q(d\otimes1_A)(1_B\otimes x_+)(z_+\otimes1_A)(b\otimes1_A)=qp^2=p.$$ For the other sign the central inverse crossings retract after a second move and a cup passage, leaving $q$. Thus a pair of opposite curls is $pq=1_A$; reversing the order gives $qp=1_A$. Bending these equations with $b,d$ checks the two directions on $B$, since bending is injective by the snakes. The cup passages move the pair to the other side of a strand or an extremum. Inverting every crossing gives the inverse passages, already available in step 6.1. This accounts for both curl signs, both strand directions and both lateral placements. A single curl remains $p$ or $q$ throughout. [step 1.1, step 4.2, step 5.1, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 **Carry the framing through the movie.** One may allow R1 events in the core movie of step 4.1 provided a compensating full twist is recorded on the band. To see the compensation, trivialize its normal plane on the small interval supporting the event and compare its transported framing angle with the blackboard normal: the small new curl changes the angle lift by $+2\pi$ or $-2\pi$; the framing itself extends across the event, so the remaining recorded rotation changes by the negative of that integer. Away from events these integers are constant. A twist may be transported along its component, past crossings by the slide relations and past extrema by the cup/cap computations of steps 6.1–7.1. At a cusp the new curl and compensating inverse twist therefore cancel by the paired-curl computation of step 7.1. This description also works on a circle by choosing a regular cut away from the finite event disks; moving that cut uses the same slide computations. No twist can leave through a fixed boundary collar. Consequently the framed word is unchanged, modulo the reduced relations, at every event of a boundary-relative framed isotopy. [step 2.1, step 4.1, step 4.2, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, construct]

9.1 **Completeness.** Every word has the sliced-diagram form of step 3.1. If two words represent the same framed morphism, apply the finite relative movie of step 4.1 to a representative framed isotopy; steps 4.2–8.1 replace its word at every event by an equivalent word. Between events only cuts and disjoint levels change, already handled by interchange. There are finitely many events, so this gives a finite derivation from one word to the other. Soundness was proved in step 2.1. Restoring the redundant orientation generators by the defining expressions proves the asserted enlarged presentation. Countable choice is used only for the transversality and countable coordinate-piece selection in step 3.1; all word manipulations are finite. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 4.2, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, step 8.1] ∎
