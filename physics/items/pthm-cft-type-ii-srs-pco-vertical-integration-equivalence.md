## Statement

For the perturbative closed type-II setting of Wang–Yin, assume the worldsheet SCFT amplitude form on the product of holomorphic and antiholomorphic supermoduli spaces is defined and is closed for the chosen on-shell external states. The source constructs an integration contour from horizontal local lifts over a triangulation of reduced bosonic moduli together with interpolation chains on overlaps and higher-codimension faces. Integrating the odd coordinates on these patches yields the PCO prescription, with vertical-integration chain components chosen to avoid spurious singularities; the resulting integral agrees with the SRS prescription under the source’s contour and on-shell hypotheses.

## Statement references

[Wang–Yin, *On the Equivalence between SRS and PCO Formulations*, arXiv:2205.01106v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.01106v2)

## Facts & Assumptions

## Proof

1.1 Work over a sufficiently small contractible face chart of the regulated reduced moduli. Take the union of the finitely many disjoint gluing discs used by its incident horizontal lifts, adding generic auxiliary discs if needed. With independent odd disc parameters $\eta_i$, the explicit superconformal transitions $w=z-\theta\eta_i/(z-z_i)$, $\zeta=\theta-\eta_i/(z-z_i)$ define an actual SRS family: transition functions on the disjoint discs glue to the undeformed complement, and their inverse is the finite nilpotent inverse. In a supplied supermoduli chart its parameter map has even derivative the identity in the reduced even parameters and odd derivative a matrix $C$. A nonspurious horizontal lift is already a local odd coordinate system, so the columns for its discs form an invertible minor; thus $C$ has full row rank. Writing $\eta=P\nu$, its odd derivative is exactly $CP$, by the chain rule. This proves the concrete rank interface needed in [F4], rather than assuming that an arbitrary abstract coefficient matrix is a gluing map. [given, F1, F3, algebra]

1.2 All face maps are represented by these actual gluing words, using the same reduced family $\Sigma_t$ over the common face chart and the union of its fixed discs. Adding a disc with zero odd gluing parameter is the identity operation. The equality $P'=PQ$ therefore gives exact equality of transition functions after $\nu\mapsto Q\nu$, not merely equality of tangent maps. Extend the coefficient matrices with their exact prescribed boundary values; substituting them in the explicit transitions constructs the entire SRS family to all Grassmann orders. Higher terms in a target supermoduli chart are induced by this family and are not independently prescribed or corrected. In particular no extra spectator-only or mixed correction jets are introduced. An invertible body $CP$ gives an invertible full odd block by the finite nilpotent geometric-series inverse. [given, F1, F3, F4, step 1.1, algebra]

2.1 The allowed boundary frame changes remove all-dimensional extension obstructions. Keep the spectator discs and their independent odd parameters fixed. Quotient the full odd tangent space by their independent columns. The derivative $C_{\rm active}$ of the active gluing parameters in this quotient has $q$ rows and full row rank, because any nonspurious full horizontal frame completes the spectator columns to a basis. All matrix operations below act on these active columns only. For a face with $q$ active odd coordinates, the obstruction is the boundary map into $\mathrm{GL}_q(\mathbb C)$ given by $CP$. Changing a lower face's odd coordinates changes this map by right multiplication and changes neither its image family nor its integral. For a merger cell of dimension greater than its number of active labels, choose a facet on which a merger direction remains, so all active labels are still moving; a full $\mathrm{GL}_q$ frame change on that facet can insert the inverse boundary homotopy class. For a cube, where a facet freezes one label, the restricted frame change required for this purpose is proved in2.2. It may be chosen to equal the identity near every subface, so it preserves all previously fixed corners. After such changes, extend the boundary frame $A=CP$ into the invertible matrices and independently extend $\widehat P=P(CP)^{-1}$ in the affine right-inverse space by [F4]. Set $P_{\rm ext}=\widehat P_{\rm ext}A_{\rm ext}$. This preserves the corrected boundary $P$ exactly; in particular it does not apply a full inverse frame to a face whose frozen label must remain fixed. Repeat in increasing face dimension. There is no inference that higher homotopy groups of $\mathrm{GL}_q$ vanish: the maps are permitted to change by these face reparameterizations before extension. The exact gluing-word equality in1.2 supplies all-order matching. Compact face charts permit a finite refinement; matching maps on the refinement are equivalent under these allowed changes. [given, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2, construct]

2.2 On a $q$-dimensional elementary cube with $q$ independently moving odd labels, the boundary reparameterizations can preserve the frozen label on each face. Here is the required relative assertion. For $q=1$, $\mathbb C^\times$ is path connected. For $q\ge2$, every class of maps $S^{q-1}\to\mathrm{GL}_q(\mathbb C)$ has a representative in the subgroup $\mathrm{GL}_{q-1}(\mathbb C)$ fixing a chosen first coordinate. To prove this, deform by Gram–Schmidt to a unitary matrix. Its first column maps $S^{q-1}$ into $S^{2q-1}$ and misses a point: a smooth image of a compact $(q-1)$-manifold has zero $(2q-1)$-volume, as is seen by covering it by $O(\epsilon^{-(q-1)})$ coordinate cubes whose images have diameter $O(\epsilon)$. The first-column map therefore contracts in the punctured sphere. Lift this contraction to unitary frames by solving $U'=KU$, with $K=w'w^*-ww'^*-(w^*w')ww^*$ for the unit first column $w$; this skew-adjoint matrix satisfies $Kw=w'$. The remaining frame lies in the stated subgroup. Represent its inverse class by a map on one closed face, equal to the identity near that face's boundary, and use it as the allowed odd reparameterization there. The new boundary map is null-homotopic and extends. The same construction is smooth in a contractible base chart by contracting the base to one point before the construction. Thus a boundary correction need act only on its $q-1$ moving labels, leaving its frozen insertion fixed. [given, F3, F4, step 2.1, construct]

3.1 Use the cellular chain complex of the product of simplices in the individual PCO labels. Each simplex factor has the explicit vertex-cone contraction $h$ with $\partial h+h\partial=1-\epsilon$, where $\epsilon$ projects to the chosen vertex. Tensor these contractions one factor at a time, with the ordinary chain-degree signs; the intermediate terms telescope and give $\partial H+H\partial=1-\bigotimes\epsilon$. Hence every positive-dimensional cellular boundary has a filling by a finite chain of product cells, relative to its already prescribed boundary. These chains, rather than an unjustified claim that every cone is itself a product cell, are used as the interpolation chains. Each product cell may be triangulated and its parameterized map restricted to the resulting pieces without changing the integral or its boundary. A factor of dimension at least two is a higher merger of one odd label. A product of intervals in distinct labels is a cube. For a merger product cell, let $p$ be its dimension and $q$ its number of distinct active odd labels. At least one simplex factor has dimension greater than one, so $p>q$. By the actual gluing-word coupling calculation in3.2, every interpolation differential has active Grassmann degree at least one. A product of $p$ such differentials has degree at least $p>q$ and is zero in the Grassmann algebra on the $q$ active parameters, even after arbitrary invertible mixing of those active labels or inclusion of higher-degree terms. Thus merger cells do not contribute to the odd integral. Cubes with distinct moving labels are the only contributions. There can be no nonzero cube with more distinct labels than the available odd coordinates. The holomorphic and antiholomorphic sectors are constructed independently and their ordered products carry the given Berezin signs. [given, F1, F2, step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra]

3.2 The following ordered formal-distribution calculation gives every cube contribution, including arbitrary admissible interior frames. Set $H_j=\sum_iP_{ij}\beta(z_i)$ for its $q$ active labels. In the product of its $q$ interpolation differentials, the surviving active odd coefficient is $(-2)^q\nu_1\cdots\nu_q\det(dH_j)$. Terms of higher active odd degree from the SCFT deformation, quadratic ghost terms, or the nilpotent correction to the delta factors vanish, since all $q$ active parameters have already been used. These assertions follow by summing the actual general contour couplings in the amplitude definition: on each active disc, substitute $\eta_i=\sum_jP_{ij}\nu_j$ into its transition map. Its even-parameter coupling has leading term $-2\sum_j\nu_jdH_j$; the terms involving $b$ contain at least two active parameters. The odd coupling has leading term $2H_j$ with every correction containing an active parameter. Spectator discs are disjoint and constant in the interpolation parameters, so they supply no such terms and cannot introduce a spectator-only correction into an active coupling. The constant active term in the product of the $\delta(B_{\nu_j})$ is $2^{-q}\prod_j\delta(H_j)$. Keep the source's label order for the odd integrations and ghost delta factors. After the $q$ one-face minus signs, the local coefficient product is $(-1)^q$ times the pullback of the form
$$\alpha_q=d\Theta(H_1)\cdots d\Theta(H_q).$$
The product here is in the total graded algebra: $\Theta(H_j)$ is ghost odd, $d\Theta(H_j)$ has even total parity, and the fixed Berezin ordering determines the equivalent delta/wedge formula's overall sign. This avoids treating the fermionic $\xi$ as an ordinary numerical even Heaviside function. The elementary source cube is oriented by $\kappa_q=(-1)^{q(q-1)/2}$ relative to $ds_1\cdots ds_q$, identifying ordered Berezin labels with the ordered interpolation directions. The exponent counts the $0+1+\cdots+(q-1)$ crossings when collecting the active odd parameters past the preceding interpolation one-forms; the ghost delta factors remain in their fixed label order. This is a cell-orientation/Berezin-order identification, not a new scalar factor inserted into $\Omega$. Face incidence signs are the induced signs of this orientation; relative to product-oriented faces they are multiplied by $\kappa_q/\kappa_{q-1}=(-1)^{q-1}$. Spectator odd integrations give their horizontal $X+d_Y\xi$ factors by [F2]. This is the same coefficient extraction as the one-face computation, now retaining the full determinant rather than assuming diagonal gluing coefficients. [given, F1, F2, step 3.1, algebra]

3.3 This form is invariant under a smoothly varying invertible change of the active odd frame. The ordinary odd coordinate-change law of $\Omega$ gives $\delta^{(q)}(QH)=\det(Q)^{-1}\delta^{(q)}(H)$. In $d(QH)=Q\,dH+(dQ)H$, every term containing $H_j\delta(H_j)$ vanishes. That identity itself follows by differentiating $\Theta(cH_j)=\Theta(H_j)$ with respect to $c$. Thus the remaining determinant cancels the delta determinant. Also $d\alpha_q=0$, because differentiating a delta factor produces another copy of its own differential. These are identities in the supplied formal ghost algebra, not ordinary numerical Heaviside functions or an exchange with a functional measure. [given, F1, step 3.2, algebra]

4.1 Put $\vartheta_j=\Theta(H_j)$ and use the ordered primitive
$$\lambda_q=\frac1q\sum_{j=1}^q\vartheta_j\,d\vartheta_1\cdots\widehat{d\vartheta_j}\cdots d\vartheta_q.$$
Each $d\vartheta_j$ is even in total parity, so it commutes with the other such factors. Leibniz's rule in this total graded algebra gives $d\lambda_q=\alpha_q$. The corresponding primitive for the local coefficient is $(-1)^q\lambda_q$, integrated on the source-oriented cube. On a cube face where label $j$ is frozen, $\vartheta_j$ is its fixed endpoint value and $d\vartheta_j=0$, including a nonzero scalar frame factor by [F1]. All terms in the boundary restriction of $\lambda_q$ vanish except the one omitting that differential. The frame correction in2.2 acts only on the other $q-1$ labels, and3.3 makes their form invariant. Stokes with these induced face signs and induction on $q$ therefore identifies the boundary integral with that of the diagonal independent-move cube with the same endpoints. Its sign can be computed explicitly, without hiding it in an unspecified ordering: in the standard total-graded convention $ds_i$ anticommutes with the ghost-odd $\vartheta_j'$, so
$$\int_{\rm product}\alpha_q=\kappa_q\prod_{j=1}^q(\vartheta_{j,\mathrm{out}}-\vartheta_{j,\mathrm{in}}).$$
Consequently
$$\int_{\rm source}(-1)^q\alpha_q=(-1)^q\kappa_q^2\prod_{j=1}^q\Delta\vartheta_j=\prod_{j=1}^q\Delta\xi_j,$$
with the endpoint products in fixed label order and $\xi_j=-\vartheta_j$. For $q=1$ this is $-\int d\Theta=\Delta\xi$. For $q=2$, $\int_{\rm product}d\Theta_1d\Theta_2=-\Delta\Theta_1\Delta\Theta_2$ while the source square has the additional orientation $\kappa_2=-1$, giving $+\Delta\xi_1\Delta\xi_2$ exactly as Wang–Yin(5.37). This calibrates the general-q convention against both the source edge and square rather than extrapolating from the edge alone. [given, F1, step 2.2, step 3.2, step 3.3, algebra]

4.2 Only the fixed all-face construction just described is needed. Its boundary frame changes are exact odd coordinate reparameterizations of the same gluing words, and adding a zero-parameter disc is the identity; neither operation changes an integral. The cube calculation4.1 depends only on its matched boundary data, regardless of the chosen invertible interior frame, and merger integrals are zero. Thus no homotopy between arbitrary invertible-frame fillings, and no arbitrary higher-jet correction, is being assumed. Refining an existing patch means subdividing its parameter domain and pulling back the same map, so ordinary additivity gives the same integral. If a supplied contour is compared with a relative-homologous contour, closedness and super-Stokes give equality with its prescribed physical boundary held fixed; no degeneration term is set to zero. [given, F1, F3, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1]

5.1 Orient each interpolation simplex times its base face by the ordered face convention. Its simplex-face restriction is the corresponding lower-face map with the alternating boundary sign, up to the permitted coordinate change. Consequently the horizontal boundaries cancel their one-face interpolations, the latter cancel the two-face boundaries, and the cancellation continues in every codimension because the cellular boundary has square zero. What remains is exactly the prescribed physical relative boundary. This constructs the contour as a relative cycle; it does not replace a physical boundary by zero. [given, F1, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.2 On horizontal patches the odd integrations give the PCO form by [F2]. Steps3.1–4.1 identify all higher components with the PCO vertical terms, and4.2 justifies the exact parameterizations and subdivisions used in the construction. Additivity on the finite regulated chain therefore gives equality of the SRS and PCO integrals. If a common exhaustion is required, the equal regulated expressions have equal prescribed limits whenever those limits exist; no limit is interchanged with an infinite patch sum or proved to exist here. Closedness is used only in the stated relative-chain homotopies. [given, F1, F2, step 4.1, step 4.2, step 5.1]

5.3 The calculation uses NS discs at PCO positions away from every puncture and carries the external insertions unchanged inside the given correlator. It therefore applies to every external-state sector for which the Statement's supplied form and its on-shell/source gluing hypotheses hold, without asserting construction of a new Ramond correlator package. If there are no odd coordinates, no vertical odd contribution occurs and the assertion is ordinary equality of the two names for the supplied bosonic integral. Empty chains give zero on both sides. Thus the fixed-chain conclusion retains the original conditional scope. [given, F1, F2, step 3.1, step 5.2] ∎
