## Statement

Let $n\ge1$, let $U,V\subseteq\mathbb R^{2n}$ be phase domains with standard two-forms
$\omega_U,\omega_V$, and let $C:U\to V$ be a $C^2$ diffeomorphism with $C^*\omega_V=\omega_U$
([[def-cm-phase-domain-and-standard-symplectic-two-form]],
[[prop-cm-canonical-map-tests-and-hamiltonian-transport]]). Write $C(z)=(Q(z),P(z))$ and
$z=(q(z),p(z))$ for the coordinate functions, and let $z_0\in U$. If the map
$$F:U\to\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^n,\qquad F(z):=\bigl(q(z),Q(z)\bigr)$$
has invertible derivative at $z_0$, then there are an open neighbourhood $W\subseteq U$ of $z_0$ and
an open ball $W''\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^n$ about $F(z_0)$ with $F(W)=W''$ on which
$F$ is a $C^2$ diffeomorphism, and a $C^3$ function $S:W''\to\mathbb R$ with
$\det S_{qQ}(F(z_0))\ne0$, such that the restriction of $S$ to a product neighbourhood $O_q\times O_Q\subseteq W''$ of $F(z_0)$ is a first-kind generating function and its generated local map agrees with $C$ on a neighbourhood of $z_0$.

The words canonical and the finite-regularity form operations here use [[def-cm-finite-regularity-canonical-calculus]], with the displayed $C^2$ maps; smoothness is not inferred.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The canonical $C^2$ diffeomorphism $C$, the point $z_0$, and the projection $F$.

[F1] Since $C$ is $C^2$, so is $F$, with block derivative $DF(z_0)=\begin{pmatrix}I_n&0\\ Q_q(z_0)&Q_p(z_0)\end{pmatrix}$; invertibility of $DF(z_0)$ is therefore equivalent to $\det Q_p(z_0)\ne0$, the determinant being that of [[def-determinant-of-a-square-matrix]] ([[def-total-derivative-in-euclidean-space]], [[def-jacobian-matrix-and-gradient]], [[def-invertible-euclidean-linear-map]], [[def-matrix-product-and-identity-matrix]]).

[F2] Euclidean inverse function theorem ([[thm-euclidean-inverse-function-theorem]]): a $C^2$ local diffeomorphism at $z_0$ has a $C^2$ local inverse; hence there are open neighbourhoods $W$ of $z_0$ and $W''$ of $F(z_0)$ such that $F:W\to W''$ is a $C^2$ diffeomorphism, and the inverse $G:W''\to W$, $(q,Q)\mapsto z$, is $C^2$.

[F3] Pullback and canonicity in coordinates ([[def-pullback-of-a-differential-form]], [[def-wedge-product-of-differential-forms]], [[prop-cm-canonical-map-tests-and-hamiltonian-transport]]): $C^*\omega_V=\omega_U$ reads $\sum_i dQ^i\wedge dP_i=\sum_i dq^i\wedge dp_i$ as forms on $U$.

[F4] Poincare lemma for one-forms ([[thm-poincare-lemma-for-star-shaped-domains]], [[def-star-shaped-open-subset-of-rn]]): a closed $C^1$ one-form on a star-shaped open subset of $\mathbb R^m$ is exact, with a $C^2$ potential given by the radial integral; the potential of a $C^2$ closed form is $C^3$ because its gradient is that $C^2$ form ([[def-ck-euclidean-maps-and-diffeomorphisms]], [[def-directional-and-partial-derivatives]]).

[F5] First-kind generating functions and the exterior derivative ([[def-cm-generating-function-of-the-first-and-second-kind]], [[thm-local-coordinate-formula-for-the-exterior-derivative]], [[thm-the-exterior-derivative-squares-to-zero]], [[def-closed-and-exact-differential-forms]]): $S$ is a first-kind generating function when $\det S_{qQ}\ne0$, and then $p=S_q$, $P=-S_Q$; conversely a function with those two properties and nonvanishing mixed determinant satisfies the definition.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; invert the projection to coordinates $(q,Q)$, show that the one-form $p\,dq-P\,dQ$ is closed by canonicity, and integrate it on a star-shaped neighbourhood.

1.1 By [F1] and [F2], $F:W\to W''$ is a $C^2$ diffeomorphism with $C^2$ inverse $G$; on $W''$ the functions $p_i:=p_i\circ G$ and $P_i:=P_i\circ G$ are $C^2$ in $(q,Q)$. [F1, F2]

2.1 Define the $C^2$ one-form $\alpha:=\sum_i p_i\,dq^i-\sum_i P_i\,dQ^i$ on $W''$, with the functions of step 1.1. Its exterior derivative is $d\alpha=\sum_i dp_i\wedge dq^i-\sum_i dP_i\wedge dQ^i =\sum_i dQ^i\wedge dP_i-\sum_i dq^i\wedge dp_i$ (using $dp\wedge dq=-dq\wedge dp$ and $dP\wedge dQ=-dQ\wedge dP$); by [F3] this vanishes, so $\alpha$ is closed ([[def-closed-and-exact-differential-forms]]). [step 1.1, F3, algebra]

3.1 The ball $W''$ is open and star-shaped about its centre $F(z_0)$ ([[def-star-shaped-open-subset-of-rn]], [[def-metric-topology]]), and the one-form $\alpha$ of step 2.1 is a closed $C^1$ one-form on it (indeed $C^2$); by [F4] there is a $C^3$ function $S:W''\to\mathbb R$ with $dS=\alpha$, that is, $S_{q^i}=p_i$ and $S_{Q^j}=-P_j$. [step 2.1, F4]

4.1 At $F(z_0)$ one has $\det S_{qQ}=\det(\partial p_i/\partial Q^j)$; since $DG$ is the inverse of $DF$ and $DG$ has block form $\begin{pmatrix}I_n&0\cr \partial p/\partial q&\partial p/\partial Q \end{pmatrix}$, the identity $DG\,DF=I_{2n}$ gives $(\partial p/\partial Q)(z_0)\,(Q_p(z_0))=I_n$; by [F1] the factor $Q_p(z_0)$ is invertible, hence $\det S_{qQ}(F(z_0))\ne0$. [F1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 By step 4.1 and continuity of the mixed determinant, choose a product neighbourhood $O_q\times O_Q\subseteq W''$ of $F(z_0)$ on which $\det S_{qQ}\ne0$. Its restriction is precisely first-kind data in [F5]; the relations $p=S_q$, $P=-S_Q$ of step 3.1 are exactly the defining relations of the map generated by $S$ ([[def-cm-generating-function-of-the-first-and-second-kind]]), and they hold on the image of $C$ under the identification $F$; hence the generated local map agrees with $C$ near $z_0$. [step 3.1, step 4.1, F5] ∎

## Remarks

- The hypothesis that $F(z)=(q(z),Q(z))$ have invertible derivative at $z_0$ is the classical no-caustic condition for the first-kind description; when it fails one uses a second-kind or mixed generating function, and globally one may meet genuine obstructions such as the cylinder shift of [[prop-cm-cylinder-shift-has-no-global-generating-function]].
- The published [[thm-liouville-arnold-action-angle-theorem]] uses generating functions only through its action-angle construction; no global generating function is asserted here.
