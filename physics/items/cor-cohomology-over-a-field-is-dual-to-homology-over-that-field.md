## Statement

Assume AC. For every field $k$, every space $X$, and $n\ge0$, evaluation gives a natural $k$-linear isomorphism
$$H^n(X;k)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\operatorname{Hom}_k(H_n(X;k),k).$$
There is no finite-dimensional hypothesis. The right side is the full algebraic dual.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] [[def-singular-cochain-complex-with-coefficients]] identifies $k$-valued singular cochains with $\operatorname{Hom}_k(C_\bullet(X;k),k)$; [[def-singular-cohomology-with-coefficients]] takes the cohomology quotient.

[F2] [[lem-singular-uct-extension-from-cycle-projections]] gives the natural exact evaluation sequence over every PID, with the Ext term computed by restrictions from $Z_{n-1}$ to $B_{n-1}$.

[F3] Under [[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[thm-every-independent-set-extends-to-a-basis]] supplies a basis of a vector space and extends an independent subset to a basis.

## Proof

**Given:** $k,X,n$ as stated and AC. Set $C=C_\bullet(X;k)$.

1.1 A field is a PID: any nonzero ideal contains a nonzero $a$, hence $1=a^{-1}a$, and is the whole ring; the zero ideal is generated by zero. The complex $C$ is nonnegative and free on the singular simplex sets. Thus [F2] applies, and [F1] identifies its middle group with $H^n(X;k)$. [F1, F2, given]

1.2 Put $B=B_{n-1}C\subseteq Z=Z_{n-1}C$. Apply [F3] first to the empty independent set in $B$ to obtain a basis $L$ of $B$, then to $L\subseteq Z$ to extend it to a basis $T$ of $Z$. For any linear $\psi:B\to k$, define a map on $T$ by its existing values $\psi(l)$ on $L$ and zero on $T\setminus L$. Every vector of $Z$ has a unique finite basis expansion, so summing its coefficients against these values defines a linear $g:Z\to k$ with $g|_B=\psi$. Consequently restriction $\operatorname{Hom}_k(Z,k)\to\operatorname{Hom}_k(B,k)$ is onto, and the cokernel Ext term in [F2] is zero. [F2, F3, given]

2.1 Exactness from step 1.1 and vanishing from step 1.2 make evaluation both injective and surjective. Its formula is $[\varphi]\mapsto([c]\mapsto\varphi(c))$, and its naturality in $X$ is exactly the chain-map naturality in [F2]. The auxiliary bases used to prove Ext vanishing do not occur in this map. Arbitrary functionals are admitted: step 1.2 uses finite expansions of individual vectors, with no finite support requirement on the functional or finite bound on the basis. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 For $n=0$, $B_{-1}=Z_{-1}=0$ and the same result follows with empty bases. For empty $X$, both sides are zero. On a point in degree zero, evaluation identifies $k$ with $\operatorname{Hom}_k(k,k)$ via multiplication by the value at the constant zero-simplex; the value $1$ yields the identity. If $H_n(X;k)=0$, the isomorphism forces $H^n(X;k)=0$. AC is inherited in [F2] and used explicitly in both basis applications of step 1.2. No identification of an infinite-dimensional vector space with its double dual is made. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1] ∎
