## Statement

Let $X$ be a connected second-countable Hausdorff smooth complex manifold of complex dimension $n\ge1$, with holomorphic tangent bundle $T_X^{1,0}$ and canonical line $K_X:=\Lambda^n_{\mathbb C}(T_X^{1,0})^*$. Write $TX_{\mathbb R}$ for its underlying real tangent bundle and $J:TX_{\mathbb R}\to TX_{\mathbb R}$ for its complex structure. The complex-linear extension of $J$ to $TX_{\mathbb R}\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C$ has $+i$ and $-i$ eigensubbundles $T_X^{1,0}$ and $T_X^{0,1}$, respectively; set $\Lambda^{0,*}T_X^*:=\bigoplus_{q=0}^n\Lambda^q_{\mathbb C}(T_X^{0,1})^*$. Let $h$ be a smooth positive Hermitian metric on $T_X^{1,0}$, let $\Omega$ be a nowhere-zero holomorphic section of $K_X$, and write $h_{\mathbb R}$ for the underlying real Riemannian metric. Give $TX_{\mathbb R}$ the complex orientation, in which $(v_1,Jv_1,\ldots,v_n,Jv_n)$ is positive for a complex basis $(v_1,\ldots,v_n)$ of $(T_xX,J)$.

Define $U(n)=\{A\in GL(n,\mathbb C):A^*A=I\}$, $SU(n)=\{A\in U(n):\det_{\mathbb C}A=1\}$, and $SO(2n)=\{Q\in GL(2n,\mathbb R):Q^{\mathsf T}Q=I,\det Q=1\}$. Give $\mathbb R^{2n}$ its standard Euclidean inner product. The real Clifford algebra $\mathrm{Cl}_{2n}$ is the unital real algebra generated by $\mathbb R^{2n}$ with relations $vw+wv=-2\langle v,w\rangle$. Define $\operatorname{Spin}(2n)\subset\mathrm{Cl}_{2n}$ to be the subgroup generated by products of an even number of Euclidean unit vectors, and define $\lambda(s)(v)=svs^{-1}$; this is the standard double cover $\lambda:\operatorname{Spin}(2n)\to SO(2n)$. A complex spinor bundle is the vector bundle associated to a Spin or Spin$^c$ structure by the complex Clifford spin representation; for Spin$^c$, the central $U(1)$ acts with weight one. For each base point $p\in X$, define the holonomy group to be the subgroup of orientation-preserving $h_{\mathbb R}$-orthogonal maps of $T_pX_{\mathbb R}$ given by Levi-Civita parallel transports around piecewise-smooth loops based at $p$, and use the following induced connection to define parallel spinors.

For a smooth Spin structure $\widehat\lambda:Q\to P_{SO}$, let $\omega_{LC}$ be the $\mathfrak{so}(2n)$-valued principal Levi-Civita connection form, whose horizontal frames are Levi-Civita parallel frames. The differential $d\lambda:\mathfrak{spin}(2n)\to\mathfrak{so}(2n)$ is an isomorphism. Define the lifted principal connection and its associated spinor connection by
$$\omega_Q=(d\lambda)^{-1}\widehat\lambda^*\omega_{LC},\qquad \nabla^{\mathrm{spin}}[q,f]=[q,df+\sigma_*(q^*\omega_Q)f],$$
where $q:U\to Q$ is a local spin frame, $f:U\to\Sigma$ is the local coefficient of the spinor, and $\sigma:\operatorname{Spin}(2n)\to GL(\Sigma)$ is the complex Clifford spin representation. The proof verifies that these local formulas define a connection independent of the local spin frame. A parallel spinor is a smooth section $\psi$ with $\nabla^{\mathrm{spin}}\psi=0$.

Use $\operatorname{Spin}^c(2n):=(\operatorname{Spin}(2n)\times U(1))/\langle(-1,-1)\rangle$, and define $\pi([s,z])=\lambda(s)$, $\delta([s,z])=z^2$, and $\Pi=(\pi,\delta)$. The projection $\pi$ has $U(1)$ kernel, whereas $\Pi$ is the two-fold cover of $SO(2n)\times U(1)$. For $A\in U(n)$ let $A_{\mathbb R}\in SO(2n)$ be its underlying real map in the complex orientation; let $\tau:U(n)\to\operatorname{Spin}^c(2n)$ be the unique continuous lift of $A\mapsto(A_{\mathbb R},\det_{\mathbb C}A)$ with $\tau(I)=1$. The proof establishes that this lift exists and is a homomorphism. Let $P_U$ be the principal bundle of ordered $h$-unitary complex frames of $T_X^{1,0}$ and $P_{SO}$ the principal bundle of ordered, positively oriented $h_{\mathbb R}$-orthonormal real frames of $TX_{\mathbb R}$. The induced principal bundle $P_c=P_U\times_\tau\operatorname{Spin}^c(2n)$ maps to $P_{SO}$ by $[u,s]\mapsto u\cdot\pi(s)$; this map has $U(1)$ fibers. Its determinant line $P_c\times_\delta\mathbb C$ is $K_X^{-1}$, equipped with the Hermitian metric induced by $h$. Dividing $\Omega^{-1}$ by its positive pointwise norm gives a smooth unit section of this line; under the pair cover $\Pi$, it reduces $P_c$ to a Spin structure. The associated complex spinor bundle is $\Lambda^{0,*}T_X^*$ as a Clifford module, and its $\tau(SU(n))$-fixed fiber subspace is exactly the sum of degrees $0$ and $n$ (for $n=1$, $SU(1)$ is trivial and these are the two degrees of the whole module). The characteristic classes $c_1$ and $w_2$ denote the first Chern class with values in $H^2(X;\mathbb Z)$ and the second Stiefel–Whitney class with values in $H^2(X;\mathbb Z/2)$, and $\rho_2$ is induced by coefficient reduction $\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z/2$.

If, in addition, $X$ is compact Kähler and $h$ is induced by a Ricci-flat Kähler metric $g$, then $\Omega$ and the unit determinant section are parallel. The Levi-Civita spin connection on the volume-form-induced Spin structure has holonomy contained in the standard lift $\tau(SU(n))\subseteq\operatorname{Spin}(2n)$, so the degree-zero and degree-$n$ invariant lines give global parallel spinors. The characteristic-class consistency check, already valid under the general complex-manifold hypotheses, is $w_2(TX_{\mathbb R})=\rho_2c_1(T_X^{1,0})=0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A connected second-countable smooth complex manifold $X$ of complex dimension $n\ge1$, a smooth Hermitian metric $h$ on $T_X^{1,0}$, and a nowhere-zero holomorphic section $\Omega$ of $K_X$. For the parallel-spinor conclusion, additionally assume that $X$ is compact connected Kähler and $h$ is induced by a Ricci-flat Kähler metric.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** canonical Spin-c lift, determinant reduction, and the exterior-algebra spin module.

1.1 Identify $U(1)$ with the multiplicative unit circle. The based homeomorphism $\epsilon:\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\to U(1)$ from [F13] and [F14] show $\pi_1(U(1),1)\cong\mathbb Z$ with generator $t\mapsto e^{2\pi i t}$: composing based loops with $\epsilon$ and its inverse gives inverse maps on loop classes and preserves concatenation. Every $z\in U(1)$ is joined to $1$ by a path $s\mapsto e^{2\pi i st}$ for a representative $z=e^{2\pi i t}$, so $U(1)$ is path-connected. [F13, F14, algebra]

1.2 Write $\pi([s,z])=\lambda(s)$ and $\delta([s,z])=z^2$, and put $\Pi=(\pi,\delta):\operatorname{Spin}^c(2n)\to SO(2n)\times U(1)$. The projection $\pi$ is onto with kernel $\{[1,z]:z\in U(1)\}\cong U(1)$, so it is not a double cover. The pair map $\Pi$ is onto and has kernel $\{[1,1],[1,-1]\}$; local covering charts follow from [F11] and local square-root charts for $z\mapsto z^2$, so $\Pi$ is the two-fold cover. [F1, F11, algebra]

1.3 Put $E=T_X^{1,0}$. By [F5], choose a homotopy equivalence $g:X\to Y$ to a path-connected CW complex and a homotopy inverse $f:Y\to X$. Set $E_Y=f^*E$. It is numerable; because $f\circ g$ is homotopic to the identity, [F6] gives a bundle isomorphism $g^*E_Y\cong E$. Apply [F4] to $E_Y$ over the actual CW base $Y$. Pulling that equality back along $g$, [F7] gives Chern-class naturality, [F8] gives Stiefel–Whitney naturality, and reduction modulo two commutes with pullback; the bundle isomorphism then yields $w_2(E_{\mathbb R})=\rho_2c_1(E)$ on $X$. This uses the CW theorem only on $Y$, not directly on the smooth manifold $X$. [A1, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, given, algebra]

2.1 The determinant map $d:U(n)\to U(1)$ is globally trivial as a bundle: the homeomorphism over $U(1)$ is
$$SU(n)\times U(1)\longrightarrow U(n),\qquad (B,z)\longmapsto B\operatorname{diag}(z,1,\ldots,1),$$
with inverse $A\mapsto(A\operatorname{diag}((\det A)^{-1},1,\ldots,1),\det A)$. Under this homeomorphism $d$ is the product projection and has the homotopy-lifting property for every space: for any initial map $x\mapsto(B_x,z_x)$ and homotopy $H:X\times[0,1]\to U(1)$ starting at $z_x$, the lift is $(x,t)\mapsto(B_x,H(x,t))$. Hence $d$ is a Serre fibration and the exact sequence [F10] applies. For $n\ge2$, [F9] gives $SU(n)$ path-connected with $\pi_1(SU(n))=0$, and step 1.1 gives $\pi_1(U(1))\cong\mathbb Z$. Exactness therefore makes $d_*:\pi_1(U(n),I)\to\pi_1(U(1),1)$ an isomorphism. For $n=1$, $d$ is the identity. In every dimension $U(n)$ is path-connected by this product description and the path-connectedness of $SU(n)$ and $U(1)$. The loop $A(t)=\operatorname{diag}(e^{2\pi it},1,\ldots,1)$ therefore generates $\pi_1(U(n),I)$, because its image under $d_*$ is the generator from step 1.1. [F9, F10, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 The matrix group $U(n)$ is locally path-connected. Use the operator norm induced by the Euclidean norm on $\mathbb C^n$. For $\|A-I\|<1$, the convergent principal series $L=\log A=\sum_{k\ge1}(-1)^{k+1}(A-I)^k/k$ satisfies $\exp L=A$ and $L^*=-L$: since $A$ is unitary, $\|A^{-1}-I\|=\|A-I\|$, and the absolutely convergent power-series identities $\log(e^z)=z$ and $\log(z^{-1})=-\log z$ near $1$ give these relations. For sufficiently small $\delta>0$, the open set $V_\delta=\{A\in U(n):\|A-I\|<1,\ \|\log A\|<\delta\}$ is path-connected by $s\mapsto\exp(s\log A)$: these exponentials are unitary since $L$ is skew-Hermitian; choose $\delta<\log 2$, so $\|\exp(s\log A)-I\|\le e^\delta-1<1$ and $\log(\exp(s\log A))=s\log A$. As $\log A\to0$ when $A\to I$, these path-connected open sets form a neighborhood basis at $I$; left translations give local path-connectedness everywhere. Define $\Phi(A)=(A_{\mathbb R},\det A)$. The loop $A(t)$ of step 2.1 has image $\Phi(A(t))$ with the closed lift under $\Pi$ given by $t\mapsto[\cos(\pi t)+\sin(\pi t)e_1e_2,e^{\pi it}]$: the spin factor covers the real-plane rotation through $2\pi t$, the second coordinate squares to $e^{2\pi it}$, and the endpoint $[-1,-1]$ equals $[1,1]$. Since step 2.1 shows that $A(t)$ generates $\pi_1(U(n),I)$, every loop class lies in the covering subgroup for $\Pi$. The based lifting criterion [F15] therefore gives a unique based lift $\tau:U(n)\to\operatorname{Spin}^c(2n)$ with $\Pi\circ\tau=\Phi$. It is a homomorphism because the lifts of $(A,B)\mapsto\tau(A)\tau(B)$ and $(A,B)\mapsto\tau(AB)$ differ by a discrete kernel element, agree at $(I,I)$, and $U(n)\times U(n)$ is connected by step 2.1. The local smooth spin section in [F16] and the local square-root charts make $\Pi$ a smooth covering; since $\Phi$ is smooth, its lift $\tau$ is smooth in these local charts. In particular, $\delta\circ\tau(A)=\det A$. [F1, F11, F15, F16, step 2.1, step 1.2, given, construct, algebra]

4.1 Let $P_U\to X$ be the unitary frame bundle of $(T_X^{1,0},h)$ and $P_{SO}\to X$ the oriented orthonormal frame bundle of $(TX_{\mathbb R},h_{\mathbb R})$, where the orientation is the complex orientation in the Statement. The map $A\mapsto A_{\mathbb R}$ makes $P_U$ a reduction of $P_{SO}$. Form the induced principal bundle $P_c=P_U\times_\tau\operatorname{Spin}^c(2n)$, with equivalence $(uA,s)\sim(u,\tau(A)s)$. Its map to the oriented frame bundle is $\lambda_c([u,s])=u\cdot\pi(s)$; this is well-defined and Spin$^c$-equivariant because $\pi\circ\tau(A)=A_{\mathbb R}$. Its fiber is $U(1)=\ker\pi$, so this structure map is not a double cover. Define the determinant line by $L_{\det}:=P_c\times_\delta\mathbb C$ with its Hermitian metric induced by the unitary character $\delta$, and let $P_{L_{\det}}$ be its unit-circle bundle. Since $\delta\circ\tau=\det_{\mathbb C}$, the associated-bundle construction identifies $L_{\det}\cong\det_{\mathbb C}(T_X^{1,0})=K_X^{-1}$ with the determinant metric induced by $h$. The pair map is explicitly $\widehat\lambda_c(p)=(\lambda_c(p),[p,1])\in P_{SO}\times_XP_{L_{\det}}$; on each fiber its group map is $\Pi$, hence it is a two-fold cover. This is the canonical Spin$^c$ structure under the definition in [F1]. [F1, step 1.2, step 3.1, given, construct, algebra]

5.1 The nowhere-zero section $\Omega^{-1}$ of $L_{\det}\cong K_X^{-1}$ has positive smooth norm for the determinant metric induced by $h$. Its pointwise unit normalization $s=\Omega^{-1}/\|\Omega^{-1}\|$ is a smooth unit section of the determinant line, even when $\|\Omega\|$ is not constant. Use the pair cover from step 4.1 to define
$$P_{\operatorname{Spin}}:=\widehat\lambda_c^{-1}\bigl(P_{SO}\times_X s(X)\bigr),$$
where $\widehat\lambda_c:P_c\to P_{SO}\times_XP_{L_{\det}}$ is the pair map. Since $\ker\delta\cong\operatorname{Spin}(2n)$ by $s_0\mapsto[s_0,1]$, this is a principal Spin$(2n)$ bundle. Its map to $P_{SO}$ is the two-fold cover $\operatorname{Spin}(2n)\to SO(2n)$. Thus the topological Spin structure exists for every complex manifold and Hermitian metric in the Statement. Construct the connection in the Statement as follows. The smooth local section in [F16], together with the covering charts [F11], gives smooth local inverse branches for $\lambda$; hence $d\lambda:\mathfrak{spin}(2n)\to\mathfrak{so}(2n)$ is an isomorphism. In the negative-square Clifford convention, differentiating $s(t)=\exp(te_ae_b/2)$ gives $[e_ae_b/2,e_a]=e_b$ and $[e_ae_b/2,e_b]=-e_a$, so these bivectors have the stated elementary-rotation normalization. The same local smooth Spin branch and local square-root branches make $\Pi$ a smooth covering; since $\Phi(A)=(A_{\mathbb R},\det A)$ is smooth, its lift $\tau$ from step 3.1 is smooth. For any smooth Spin structure $\widehat\lambda:Q\to P_{SO}$, its equivariance implies that $\omega_Q=(d\lambda)^{-1}\widehat\lambda^*\omega_{LC}$ reproduces every vertical generator and obeys $R_g^*\omega_Q=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\omega_Q$; thus it is a principal connection. The local spinor formula in the Statement transforms under $q\mapsto qg$ and $f\mapsto\sigma(g)^{-1}f$ by the product rule, so it defines a global covariant derivative. Its horizontal paths project to Levi-Civita horizontal paths, and conversely the unique covering lift of a Levi-Civita horizontal path with specified initial spin frame is horizontal. In the additional compact Ricci-flat Kähler case, the Levi-Civita connection preserves $J,h$ and hence $P_U$. Extend this unitary connection to $P_c$ via $\tau$: in a local unitary frame its connection form is $d\tau(\theta_U)$, where $\theta_U$ is the unitary connection form. Its pair projection is the Levi-Civita connection together with the induced determinant connection. By [F2], $\Omega$ and the unit determinant section $s$ are parallel, so this connection is tangent to $P_{\operatorname{Spin}}$. Its restriction projects to $\omega_{LC}$ and therefore equals the uniquely defined $\omega_Q$ above. More explicitly, let $P_{SU}\subset P_U$ consist of frames whose determinant is $s$. It is preserved by parallel transport, and $[u,g]\mapsto[u,g]$ identifies $P_{SU}\times_{\tau|_{SU(n)}}\operatorname{Spin}(2n)$ with this very $P_{\operatorname{Spin}}$, because any unitary frame can be adjusted to determinant $s$ and the remaining Spin-c coordinate then has determinant one. For a general Hermitian metric the induced Spin connection still exists by the pullback formula, but preservation of this unitary connection or of $s$ is not asserted. [F1, F2, F11, F15, F16, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Model the negative-square complex Clifford module on $S=\Lambda^*\mathbb C^n$ with creation $\epsilon_j$ and contraction $\iota_j$ for each unitary coordinate: put $c(e_{2j-1})=\epsilon_j-\iota_j$ and $c(e_{2j})=i(\epsilon_j+\iota_j)$. The identities $\iota_j\epsilon_j+\epsilon_j\iota_j=1$ and the cross-coordinate anticommutation give $c(e_a)c(e_b)+c(e_b)c(e_a)=-2\delta_{ab}$, so this is the Clifford spin module. For diagonal $A=\operatorname{diag}(e^{i\theta_1},\ldots,e^{i\theta_n})$, the canonical lift from step 3.1 is represented by $[\prod_j(\cos(\theta_j/2)+\sin(\theta_j/2)e_{2j-1}e_{2j}),e^{i\sum_j\theta_j/2}]$. Since $e_{2j-1}e_{2j}=i(2N_j-1)$ with $N_j=\epsilon_j\iota_j$, the Spin factor acts on a wedge basis vector indexed by $I$ by $\exp(i\sum_j\theta_j(1_{j\in I}-1/2))$; the central weight-one factor makes the total character $\prod_{j\in I}e^{i\theta_j}$. For a diagonal unitary frame change, the induced right-frame action on anti-holomorphic covectors has weights $z_j$, so the calculation is the exterior action on $\Lambda^{0,*}T_X^*$. To justify equality for every $A\in U(n)$, let $R(A)=\Lambda^*A$ on this exterior model and let $B(A)$ denote the action of $\tau(A)$. Creation by $v\in\mathbb C^n$ and its Hermitian-adjoint contraction obey $R(A)\epsilon(v)R(A)^{-1}=\epsilon(Av)$ and $R(A)\epsilon(v)^*R(A)^{-1}=\epsilon(Av)^*$, since $A$ is unitary. These identities and the displayed Clifford formulas give $R(A)c(v)R(A)^{-1}=c(A_{\mathbb R}v)$ for real $v$; Clifford conjugation gives the same identity for $B(A)$. Therefore $R(A)^{-1}B(A)$ commutes with all creation and contraction operators. Their common contraction kernel is the vacuum line $\mathbb C1$; commuting with creation then forces this operator to be a scalar $a(A)I$ on every wedge basis vector. Since $R$ and $B$ are representations, these scalars form a character: $a(AB)=a(A)a(B)$, hence $a(UAU^{-1})=a(A)$. The diagonal calculation gives $a=1$ on diagonal matrices, and unitary diagonalization now gives $a=1$ everywhere. Thus $B=R$. The unitary representation on anti-holomorphic covectors is $\bar A^{-\mathsf T}=A$, so this proves the asserted associated bundle identification with $\Lambda^{0,*}T_X^*$, intertwining Clifford multiplication. The determinant section identifies the reduced Spin spinor bundle with this same complex vector bundle; this identification is parallel when the additional compact Ricci-flat Kähler hypotheses of step 5.1 hold. For $n=1$, $SU(1)=\{1\}$ and $S=\Lambda^{0,0}\oplus\Lambda^{0,1}$, so both degrees $0$ and $n$ are exactly the invariant subspace. For $n\ge2$, restrict to the determinant-one diagonal torus $T=\{\operatorname{diag}(z_1,\ldots,z_n):\prod_jz_j=1\}$. The wedge vector indexed by $I$ has character $\chi_I(z)=\prod_{j\in I}z_j$, which is trivial on $T$ exactly when its indicator vector $(1_{j\in I})_j$ is an integer multiple of $(1,\ldots,1)$; since its entries are $0$ or $1$, this occurs exactly for $I=\varnothing$ or $I=\{1,\ldots,n\}$. Thus the $T$-fixed subspace is these two lines, and both are fixed by all of $SU(n)$ because their characters are $1$ and $\det A=1$. The exterior-module identification also intertwines connections in the Kähler case: both are induced by the same unitary connection $\theta_U$ and the same exterior representation, while step 5.1 identifies its determinant-reduced connection with $\omega_Q$. Fix $u_0\in P_{SU}$ over a base point and the compatible spin frame $[u_0,1]$. For any based piecewise-smooth loop $\gamma$, let $u(t)$ be its Levi-Civita parallel unitary frame with $u(0)=u_0$. By [F2], $u(t)$ stays in $P_{SU}$ and $u(1)=u_0A_\gamma$ for $A_\gamma\in\operatorname{Hol}_{u_0}(g)\subseteq SU(n)$. The path $[u(t),1]$ is horizontal for the lifted connection of step 5.1, and its endpoint is $[u_0A_\gamma,1]=[u_0,\tau(A_\gamma)]$. Uniqueness of the horizontal covering lift therefore proves, for all based loops and not only contractible ones, $\operatorname{Hol}_{[u_0,1]}(\omega_Q)=\tau(\operatorname{Hol}_{u_0}(g))$. In particular no additional central minus sign occurs for this chosen Spin structure. The associated spinor transport is the representation $\sigma$ of this lifted transport, so both invariant vectors are fixed by full spinor holonomy. Parallel transport of either vector from the base point is path independent: two paths differ by a based loop whose holonomy fixes it. Local parallel transport in coordinate neighborhoods makes the resulting section smooth and covariantly constant. Connectedness thus gives the two global parallel spinors, without a claim for any other Spin structure. [F1, F2, step 3.1, step 5.1, algebra]
