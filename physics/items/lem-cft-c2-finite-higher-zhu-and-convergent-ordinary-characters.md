## Statement

Let V be a C2-cofinite conformal vertex algebra of CFT type over C. Then every higher Zhu algebra A_n(V), n≥0, is finite dimensional. There are finitely many isomorphism classes of irreducible ordinary V-modules. For every such module M, its character Tr_M(exp(2πiτ(L(0)−c/24))) converges absolutely and locally uniformly on Im τ>0. Every cyclic weak V-module is C1-cofinite, where C1(W)=span{a_{(-1)}w:wt(a)>0}. No modular transformation law, rationality, existence of modules with prescribed Zhu top, or semisimplicity of A_n(V) is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** V has the hypotheses of the Statement. All state and module weights and modes use the linked conformal vertex algebra convention.

[F1] [[lem-cft-cyclic-weak-module-difference-one-spanning]] supplies a finite homogeneous positive-weight representative set A and, for any cyclic weak module generated by w, a cutoff N≥1 and spanning words a^1_{(i_1)}⋯a^l_{(i_l)}w with i_1<⋯<i_l<N. It preserves operator degree.

[F2] [[lem-cft-locality-to-jacobi-residue-calculus]] supplies translation of states, the weight formula and finite mode commutators. In particular (T^j a)_{(m)}=(-1)^j m(m−1)⋯(m−j+1)a_{(m−j)}.

[F3] [[thm-cft-higher-zhu-algebra-structure]] supplies the actual associative quotient A_n(V)=V/O_n(V), with circle residue denominator z^{2n+2}.

[F4] [[thm-cft-zhu-algebra-structure-and-top-action]] supplies the unital A_0(V)-action by zero modes on any admissible top.

[F5] [[def-ordinary-vertex-module]] requires a semisimple L(0), finite-dimensional weight spaces, and a lower real-part bound. [[def-cft-c1-cofinite-module]] uses the C1 space displayed in the Statement.

## Proof

1.1 Put C_k(V)=span{a_{(-k)}b:a,b∈V}, k≥2. Apply F1 to V generated by its vacuum. Since every nonnegative mode annihilates the vacuum, a nonzero strictly ordered spanning word has only negative indices. Write them −m_1<⋯<−m_l<0, so m_1>⋯>m_l≥1. If m_1≥k, the word lies in C_k(V): F2 gives (T^{m_1−k}a^1)_{(-k)}=(k(k+1)⋯(m_1−1))a^1_{(-m_1)}. This coefficient is nonzero, including the empty product. Otherwise the m_j are distinct members of {1,…,k−1}; there are finitely many such words with letters from finite A. Thus V/C_k(V) is finite dimensional. C_k is homogeneous by F2, so there exists B_k such that V_d⊂C_k(V) for every d>B_k. [given, F1, F2, algebra]

1.2 Let M be irreducible ordinary. The sum of weight spaces in any fixed coset of C/Z is a submodule, so simplicity puts all its weights in one coset. Lower boundedness then gives a least occupied weight h, and M=⊕_{j≥0}M_{h+j}. A nonzero top vector generates M. Degree reordering uses F2: in any finite word, move positive-degree modes left, zero-degree modes middle and negative-degree modes right. Swapping decreases the inversion count, while commutators have shorter length and the same total degree. Induction on length and then inversions terminates. Negative-degree modes kill the top, and a surviving word of total degree zero contains only zero-degree modes. It follows that for every subspace U⊂M_h stable under zero modes, the submodule generated by U has degree-zero space exactly U. Simplicity implies U=M_h when U≠0. By F4, M_h is therefore a simple A_0(V)-module. [F2, F4, F5, algebra]

1.3 Fix an irreducible ordinary M and a nonzero homogeneous cyclic vector w of weight h. Choose N as in F1. For each 0<ρ<1 define $$P(ρ)=\prod_{i=0}^{N-1}\left(1+\sum_{a∈A}ρ^{\mathrm{wt}(a)-i-1}\right)\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\sum_{a∈A}ρ^{\mathrm{wt}(a)+k-1}\right).$$ Each ordered spanning word chooses at most one letter for each index. Thus the sum of ρ^{degree} over all such words is exactly P(ρ). The first product is finite, even if some exponents are negative. Since wt(a)≥1, the infinite factors are bounded above by 1+|A|ρ^k; their product converges because log(1+t)≤t and Σρ^k<∞. Degree preservation in F1 implies Σ_{j≥0}dim(M_{h+j})ρ^j≤P(ρ)<∞: at each degree dimension is at most the number of spanning words of that degree. Words of negative degree evaluate to zero and need not be counted on the left. [F1, F5, algebra]

2.1 Fix n and k=2n+2. A homogeneous product a_{(-k)}b is congruent modulo O_n to −Σ_{j≥1} binom(wt(a)+n,j)a_{(j−k)}b, by the circle definition. This sum is finite, and every summand has smaller weight than a_{(-k)}b. For d>B_k, express any v∈V_d as a finite sum of homogeneous products a_{(-k)}b of weight d, using step 1.1 and homogeneity. The displayed congruence reduces its class to lower weights. Induction on d reduces every class to the finite-dimensional space ⊕_{d≤B_k}V_d. This proves dim A_n(V)<∞ for every n, including n=0. No assertion identifying C_k with an ideal is needed. [F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 On a compact subset K of the upper half-plane, |exp(2πiτ)|≤ρ<1 and exp(2πiτ(h−c/24)) is bounded. Step 1.3 is a uniform summable majorant for the homogeneous trace terms. Hence the character series converges absolutely and locally uniformly and defines a holomorphic function. This argument allows complex h and c; it uses no reality or rationality of conformal weights. [F5, step 1.3, algebra]

3.1 If two irreducible ordinary modules M,N have isomorphic A_0-tops, identify their tops by that isomorphism. The zero mode of ω is L(0), so their least weights coincide. Inside M⊕N let K be the submodule generated by the diagonal copy of the common top. The degree reordering just proved gives K_h equal to that diagonal. Both projections K→M,N are surjective. The kernel of K→M is a submodule of 0⊕N, hence is either 0 or 0⊕N. The latter would put 0⊕N_h inside the diagonal K_h, a contradiction. Thus K→M is an isomorphism; likewise K→N, and M≅N. Consequently ordinary simple modules inject, by their tops, into the simple A_0-modules. A finite-dimensional unital algebra has finitely many simple module classes: its finite-dimensional left regular module has a composition series, and any simple module is a quotient of the regular module by sending 1 to a nonzero generating vector, hence is among its composition factors. Step 2.1 now proves finiteness of the ordinary simple set. This uses no existence or induction theorem for Zhu tops. [F4, step 2.1, step 1.2, algebra]

4.1 Finally let W be any cyclic weak module. In the F1 spanning words, every word with at least one negative index has a negative leftmost index −m, m≥1. Its leftmost operator is a_{(-m)}=(T^{m−1}a)_{(-1)}/(m−1)! by F2, with a positive-weight state whenever it is nonzero. It therefore lies in C1(W). The remaining words have distinct indices in {0,…,N−1}; there are finitely many, with letters from A. Their classes span W/C1(W). This proves C1-cofiniteness without any ordinary, grading or semisimplicity premise on W. [F1, F2, F5, algebra] ∎
