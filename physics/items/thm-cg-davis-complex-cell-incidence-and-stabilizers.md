## Statement

Let $(S,m)$ be a Coxeter matrix with $S$ finite, $W$ its presented group with length $\ell$ ([[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]), and let $\mathbb S$, $WS$, $\Sigma=|WS|$, $K=|\mathbb S|$, $j\colon K\to\Sigma$ be as in [[def-cg-spherical-nerve-coset-poset-and-davis-realization]]. Let $C_T$, $z_{T,U}$ and $\varphi^{T,U}_w$ be as in [[lem-cg-finite-coxeter-orbit-polytopes-and-face-metrics]]. For each spherical coset $q=wW_T$, let $\dot q$ be its unique element of minimum length, which exists by [[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]] (3). Then:

**(1) The cellulation is an isometric polyhedral gluing.** Adjoining to $WS$ a formal least element $\varnothing$ corresponding to the empty face gives a poset $P$ whose principal down-sets are finite and are face posets of the compact convex polyhedral cells $C_T$. For each $q=wW_T$, take a copy $C_q$ of $C_T$ with its coordinates in the chart determined by $\dot q$. For $p=w'W_U\subseteq q=wW_T$, define the face isometry $h_{p,q}:=\varphi^{T,U}_{\dot q^{-1}\dot p}$ from $C_p$ onto the face of $C_q$ indexed by $p$. These cells and maps form an isometric polyhedral gluing $X$ of shape $P$ in the sense of [[def-cg-abstract-isometric-polyhedral-gluing-and-chain-metric]]: the cocycle condition follows from [[lem-cg-finite-coxeter-orbit-polytopes-and-face-metrics]] (4), and the intersection condition holds because the intersection of two spherical cosets is a spherical coset of type $T\cap T'$ or empty ([[lem-cg-spherical-coset-inclusion-and-intersection]] (3)), so the images of $C_p$ and $C_q$ meet exactly in the image of the face of $C_{p\wedge q}$. The standing hypotheses (H1)-(H3) hold: $X$ is connected (it contains the Cayley graph on $W$), locally finite and has finitely many cell shapes (one for each $T\in\mathbb S$, and $S$ is finite). Consequently the chain metric $d$ is a metric on $X$ with the weak topology, and $(X,d)$ is complete and proper in the sense of [[def-complete-metric-space]] and [[def-metric-compactness]], by [[thm-cg-polyhedral-chain-metric-topology-and-properness]] (1)-(3); and the canonical barycentric-subdivision map $|WS|\to X$ of [[lem-cg-polyhedral-face-coherence-and-uniform-star-radius]] (i) is a homeomorphism $\Sigma\cong X$ that carries the subposet $WS_{\leq q}$ onto the barycentric subdivision of the cell $C_q$.

**(2) Cells, incidence and stabilizers.** Under this identification the cells of $\Sigma$ are the images of the cells $C_{wW_T}$, of dimension $|T|$; there is one $W$-orbit of cells for each spherical $T$; $\Sigma$ has finitely many cell shapes and every closed cell meets only finitely many cells; the setwise stabilizer of the cell $wW_T$ is $wW_Tw^{-1}$; and every point of $\Sigma$ lies in the relative interior of exactly one cell. For $q=wW_T$, use the chart fixed by its unique minimum-length representative $\dot q$; if a point $y$ in the relative interior of that cell corresponds to $y'\in C_T$ and $y'$ lies in the relative interior of the chamber face $w_0C^T_I$ of the finite-type chamber decomposition of $V_T$ (where $w_0\in W_T$, $I\subseteq T$, $B(w_0^{-1}y',e_s)=0$ for $s\in I$ and $B(w_0^{-1}y',e_s)>0$ for $s\in T\setminus I$), then $$\operatorname{Stab}_W(y)=(\dot q w_0)W_I(\dot q w_0)^{-1},$$ a conjugate of the spherical parabolic $W_I$. In particular every point stabilizer is finite, is a spherical parabolic, and is contained in the setwise stabilizer $wW_Tw^{-1}$ of its cell. The relative interiors of the cells partition $\Sigma$.

**(3) The action is proper.** The $W$-action on $\Sigma$ is cellular and isometric for $d$, and it is proper: for every compact subset $C\subseteq\Sigma$ the set $\{w\in W:wC\cap C\ne\emptyset\}$ is finite.

**(4) Compact chamber quotient.** Give $W\backslash\Sigma$ the quotient topology of the orbit projection $\Sigma\to W\backslash\Sigma$ ([[def-quotient-topology]]). The projection $WS\to\mathbb S$ induces a $W$-invariant continuous map $p\colon\Sigma\to K$ that is the identity on the chamber and maps every translated chamber simplex back to its simplex of $K$; passing to quotients gives a continuous bijection $\bar p\colon W\backslash\Sigma\to K$, and $W\backslash\Sigma$ is compact as the image of the compact chamber $K$ under the quotient map, so $\bar p$ is a homeomorphism ([[thm-compactness-under-continuous-maps]] (1),(3)). In particular $W\backslash\Sigma$ is compact, and $K$, the chamber, is a strict fundamental domain for the action.

**(5) The model $U(W,K)$.** Give $W$ the discrete topology, $W\times K$ the product topology, and $U(W,K):=(W\times K)/\!\sim$ the quotient topology ([[def-standard-topologies]], [[def-product-topology]], [[def-quotient-topology]]), where $(w,x)\sim(w',x')$ iff $x=x'$ and $w^{-1}w'$ lies in the subgroup generated by $S(x):=\{s\in S:x\in K_s\}$ with $K_s:=|\mathbb S_{\geq\{s\}}|$ ([[def-generated-subgroup]]). Then $[w,x]\mapsto w\cdot j(x)$ is a well-defined $W$-equivariant homeomorphism $U(W,K)\to\Sigma$ with inverse given by the carrier-simplex coordinates of [[def-cg-spherical-nerve-coset-poset-and-davis-realization]]: a point of $\Sigma$ lies in the relative interior of a unique carrier simplex, a chain $w_0W_{T_0}\subset\cdots\subset w_kW_{T_k}$, and by [[lem-cg-spherical-coset-inclusion-and-intersection]] (2) the chain equals $w_0W_{T_0}\subset\cdots\subset w_0W_{T_k}$, so the barycentric coordinates define a point of the simplex of $K$ on $T_0\subset\cdots\subset T_k$; the two maps are mutually inverse by construction and continuous for the stated quotient and weak topologies.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite Coxeter matrix $(S,m)$, its presented group $W$, and the objects $\mathbb S$, $WS$, $\Sigma=|WS|$, $K=|\mathbb S|$, $j$, the cells $C_T$, the projections $z_{T,U}$ and the face isometries $\varphi^{T,U}_w$ constructed in [[def-cg-spherical-nerve-coset-poset-and-davis-realization]] and [[lem-cg-finite-coxeter-orbit-polytopes-and-face-metrics]].

[F1] The realization: $WS$ is the poset of spherical cosets with the inclusion order and $W$ acts on it by left multiplication preserving the type $\pi(wW_T)=T$; $\Sigma=|WS|$ is its order complex; $K=|\mathbb S|$ and $j\colon K\to\Sigma$ is the simplicial map $T\mapsto W_T$ ([[def-cg-spherical-nerve-coset-poset-and-davis-realization]] (1)-(4)).

[F2] Coset calculus: $wW_U\subseteq w'W_{U'}$ iff $U\subseteq U'$ and $w^{-1}w'\in W_{U'}$; $wW_U=w'W_{U'}$ iff $U=U'$ and $w^{-1}w'\in W_U$; if $wW_U\cap w'W_{U'}\ne\emptyset$ then the intersection is $uW_{U\cap U'}$ for every $u$ in it, and it is nonempty iff $w^{-1}w'\in W_UW_{U'}$; the left action is order-preserving, $\pi$-invariant and transitive on the cosets of each fixed parabolic ([[lem-cg-spherical-coset-inclusion-and-intersection]] (1)-(4)).

[F3] Cell and face-map data: for spherical $T$, $C_T$ is a compact convex polyhedral cell of dimension $|T|$. Its nonempty faces are $conv(uW_U x_T)$ for $u∈W_T$ and $U⊆T$, each occurring for exactly one coset $uW_U$, and face inclusion agrees with inclusion of the indexing cosets. The vector $z_{T,U}$ is fixed by $ρ(W_U)$, and the affine map $φ_w^{T,U}:C_U→C_T$, $v↦ρ(w)(v+z_{T,U})$, is an isometry onto the face indexed by $wW_U$ ([[lem-cg-finite-coxeter-orbit-polytopes-and-face-metrics]] (1)-(3)).

[F4] Gluing definition: an isometric polyhedral gluing has finite face down-sets, affine face isometries satisfying the cocycle, an intersection condition, the weak topology, and standing hypotheses (H1)-(H3); its chain metric is defined from lengths of finite chains ([[def-cg-abstract-isometric-polyhedral-gluing-and-chain-metric]]).

[F5] Finite-type chamber facts: if $T\in\mathbb S$, then $W_T$ is finite by [F1], clause (2) of [[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]] makes $(W_T,T)$ a Coxeter system, and [F17] identifies its reflection space with $(V_T,B_T,\rho|_{W_T})$. The finite chamber theorem and arrangement definition then imply that the relative interiors of the faces $w_0C^T_I$ ($w_0\in W_T$, $I\subseteq T$) partition $V_T$, and $\operatorname{Stab}_{W_T}(y')=w_0W_Iw_0^{-1}$ for $y'$ in the relative interior of $w_0C^T_I$ ([[thm-cg-finite-chamber-tiling-and-coset-face-identification]] (1),(3), [[def-cg-finite-reflection-arrangement-and-spherical-chambers]]).

[F6] Topological compactness: $K$ is compact and Hausdorff because it is a finite simplicial realization; continuous images of compact spaces are compact; and a continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism ([[prop-a-finite-simplicial-complex-has-compact-hausdorff-realization]], [[thm-compactness-under-continuous-maps]] (1),(3), [[def-homeomorphism-and-open-maps]]).

[F7] Every left coset $q=aW_T$ has a unique minimum-length representative $\dot q$ ([[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]] (3)); if $p\subseteq q$ then $\dot q^{-1}\dot p\in W_T$ by [F2].

[F8] A left group action satisfies $e\cdot x=x$ and $(gh)\cdot x=g\cdot(h\cdot x)$ ([[def-group-action]]).

[F9] The compatible barycentric triangulation map $|K'|\to X$ of the order complex of the nonempty faces is a homeomorphism for the weak topologies ([[lem-cg-polyhedral-face-coherence-and-uniform-star-radius]] (i)).

[F10] The chain metric is a metric inducing the gluing weak topology; every closed bounded subset is compact and the metric is complete ([[thm-cg-polyhedral-chain-metric-topology-and-properness]] (1)-(3), [[def-metric-compactness]], [[def-complete-metric-space]]).

[F11] Give $W$ the discrete topology and $W\times K$ the product topology; each slice map $x\mapsto(w,x)$ is continuous, and the quotient topology makes the orbit and model quotient projections continuous ([[def-standard-topologies]], [[def-product-topology]], [[def-quotient-topology]]).

[F12] For a left action, $\operatorname{Stab}_W(x)=\{g\in W:g\cdot x=x\}$ ([[def-orbit-and-stabilizer]]).

[F14] Every point has a neighborhood contained in a finite closed star meeting only finitely many cells ([[lem-cg-polyhedral-face-coherence-and-uniform-star-radius]] (iii)).

[F15] The group $W$ is generated by the Coxeter generators $S$ and $\ell$ is the word-length function from the Coxeter presentation ([[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]).

[F16] The realization $K=|\mathbb S|$ is compact and Hausdorff because it is a finite simplicial complex ([[prop-a-finite-simplicial-complex-has-compact-hausdorff-realization]]).

[F17] A4 defines $V_T=\operatorname{span}\{e_s:s\in T\}$ and $B_T=B|_{V_T}$. For $s\in T$, the canonical reflection homomorphism sends $s$ to $r_{e_s}$, and the Coxeter-form reflection formula restricts to $v\mapsto v-2B_T(v,e_s)e_s$ on $V_T$; thus $V_T$ is invariant and $\rho|_{W_T}$ is precisely the canonical reflection representation of $(W_T,T)$ ([[lem-cg-finite-coxeter-orbit-polytopes-and-face-metrics]], [[def-cg-canonical-reflection-homomorphism]], [[def-cg-real-coxeter-form-and-reflection]]).

[F13] For spherical $U\subseteq T\subseteq T'$, the face isometries satisfy $\varphi^{T',U}_{g_2g_1}=\varphi^{T',T}_{g_2}\circ\varphi^{T,U}_{g_1}$ for $g_1\in W_T$ and $g_2\in W_{T'}$ ([[lem-cg-finite-coxeter-orbit-polytopes-and-face-metrics]] (4)).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The poset $P:=WS\cup\{\varnothing\}$ with $\varnothing$ below every coset [F1]: for $p=wW_T$ the principal down-set $P_{\le p}$ consists of $\varnothing$ and the cosets $w'W_U\subseteq wW_T$, which by [F2] are exactly the cosets $w'W_U$ with $U\subseteq T$ and $w'\in wW_T$; it is finite because $W_T$ and the set of subsets of the finite $T$ are finite. The map $w'W_U\mapsto\operatorname{conv}((w^{-1}w')W_Ux_T)$ is a bijection from $P_{\le p}\setminus\{\varnothing\}$ onto the nonempty faces of $C_T$ by [F3], it is order-preserving and reflecting by [F2] and [F3], and extending it by $\varnothing\mapsto\emptyset$ sends the formal least element to the empty face; hence $P_{\le p}$ is isomorphic to the face poset of $C_T$. [given, F1, F2, F3, algebra]

1.2 Gluing data. For $p=\dot pW_U\le q=\dot qW_T$, [F2] gives $U\subseteq T$ and [F7] gives $g:=\dot q^{-1}\dot p\in W_T$. Put $C_p:=C_U$, $C_q:=C_T$ and $h_{p,q}:=\varphi^{T,U}_g$; by [F3] this is an isometry from $C_p$ onto the face of $C_q$ indexed by $p$. If $p\le q\le r$ with types $U\subseteq V\subseteq T$, then [F3] gives $h_{q,r}\circ h_{p,q}=\varphi^{T,V}_{\dot r^{-1}\dot q}\circ\varphi^{V,U}_{\dot q^{-1}\dot p}=\varphi^{T,U}_{(\dot r^{-1}\dot q)(\dot q^{-1}\dot p)}=\varphi^{T,U}_{\dot r^{-1}\dot p}=h_{p,r}$, so the cocycle condition of [F4] holds. The unique representatives in [F7] make every map independent of notation for coset representatives; no choice of representatives is made. [given, F2, F3, F4, F7, F13, algebra]

2.1 Intersection condition. Let $p=wW_T$, $q=w'W_{T'}$ be cosets. For $y\in C_p$, define its address $r\le p$ to be the unique spherical coset indexing the face whose relative interior contains $y$; existence and uniqueness are the face decomposition of the polytope $C_T$ in [F3]. If $p\le q$, write $p=\dot pW_U$, $q=\dot qW_V$, and $r=\dot p\,uW_J$. The map $h_{p,q}=\varphi^{V,U}_{\dot q^{-1}\dot p}$ sends the face of $C_U$ indexed by $uW_J$ to the face of $C_V$ indexed by $(\dot q^{-1}\dot p)uW_J$ [F3], which has global label $\dot q(\dot q^{-1}\dot p)uW_J=r$; hence it preserves the address. For a point $y\in C_p$ with address $r$, set $\xi(y):=h_{r,p}^{-1}(y)\in C_r$. If $p\le q$, the cocycle gives $h_{r,q}=h_{p,q}\circ h_{r,p}$, so $\xi$ is unchanged by the generating identification $y\sim h_{p,q}(y)$; it is also unchanged by the inverse identification. Therefore equivalent points have the same address and the same $\xi$. Conversely, if $y\in C_p$ and $y'\in C_q$ have the same address $r$ and the same coordinate $\xi$, each is identified with that common point of $C_r$, so $y\sim y'$. Thus equivalence is exactly equality of address and $\xi$. In particular each map $C_p\to X$ is injective, since equal classes from $C_p$ have the same address and coordinate and $h_{r,p}$ is injective. If two cell images meet, their common class has an address $r\le p,q$, hence $r\le p\wedge q$ and lies in the image of $C_{p\wedge q}$; conversely every point of $C_{p\wedge q}$ lies in both images, with the empty meet interpreted as the empty set. This is the intersection condition of [F4]. [step 1.2, F2, F3, F4, algebra]

3.1 The standing hypotheses. $X$ is connected: the $1$-cells $C_{wW_{\{s\}}}=\operatorname{conv}(\{x_{\{s\}},sx_{\{s\}}\})$ join the $0$-cells with addresses $wW_\emptyset=\{w\}$ and $wsW_\emptyset=\{ws\}$ (which are faces of those $1$-cells by [F3]), so the image of the disjoint union contains a copy of the connected Cayley graph of $(W,S)$ on the vertices $w$; and every cell $C_{wW_T}$ contains the $0$-cell of $w$ as the face indexed by $wW_\emptyset\le wW_T$ [F3], so every cell is attached to that graph and $X$ is connected. It is locally finite: by [step 2.1] the cells whose image contains the class of a point $y$ of address $r=w_rW_{T(r)}$ are exactly the cells $C_q$ with $q\ge r$, and by [F2] a coset containing $w_rW_{T(r)}$ has the form $w_rW_{T'}$ with $T'\supseteq T(r)$; these are finitely many because $S$ is finite. There are finitely many shapes because the cells are the $C_T$, $T\in\mathbb S$, and $\mathbb S$ is finite. [step 2.1, F2, F3, F4, F15, algebra]

4.1 Conclusion of (1). By [step 1.1] the shape $P$ has finite down-sets isomorphic to face posets of the cells $C_T$; by [step 1.2] the maps $h_{p,q}$ are the face isometries of a gluing satisfying the cocycle condition; by [step 2.1] the intersection condition holds; by [step 3.1] the hypotheses (H1)-(H3) hold. Hence the metric theorem [F10] applies: the chain metric $d$ is a metric on $X$ inducing the weak topology, and every closed $d$-bounded subset of $X$ is compact, so $(X,d)$ is complete and proper. The canonical map $f\colon|WS|\to X$ of [F9] is a bijection and is affine on each simplex of $|WS|$; it is continuous because $|WS|$ carries the weak topology and each restriction to a closed simplex is affine; and its inverse is described on each closed cell of $X$ by the carrier-simplex coordinates of [F9], hence is also affine on each simplex of the subdivision and continuous. So $f$ is a homeomorphism $\Sigma\cong X$, and by [F9] it carries the subposet $WS_{\leq wW_T}$ onto the barycentric subdivision of the cell $C_T$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1, F4, F9, F10]

5.1 Clause (2), incidence. Under the homeomorphism $\Sigma\cong X$ the cells of $\Sigma$ are the images of the cells $C_{wW_T}$ of dimension $|T|$ by [F3] and [step 4.1]; $W$ acts on the set of cosets of each fixed type transitively by [F2], so there is one orbit per spherical $T$; there are finitely many shapes since $\mathbb S$ is finite; and every closed cell meets only finitely many cells: the cells meeting the closed cell $wW_T$ are the $vW_V$ with $vW_V\cap wW_T\ne\emptyset$, equivalently $v\in wW_TW_V$ by [F2]; as $V$ ranges over the finitely many spherical subsets and $W_V$ and $W_T$ are finite, these are finitely many cells. The relative interiors of the cells partition $\Sigma$ because two cells meet in the image of $C_{p\wedge q}$ [step 2.1], and within one cell the relative interiors of its faces partition it [F3]. The setwise stabilizer of the cell $wW_T$ is $wW_Tw^{-1}$: $v\cdot wW_T=vwW_T$ equals $wW_T$ iff $w^{-1}vw\in W_T$ by [F2]. [step 2.1, step 4.1, F2, F3]

5.2 Clause (4). Let $\pi\colon WS\to\mathbb S$ be the type map. On each simplex of $\Sigma$ belonging to a chain $q_0\subset\cdots\subset q_k$, map the vertex $q_i$ to $\pi(q_i)$ and extend affinely; this defines a continuous map $p\colon\Sigma\to K$ because the type map preserves inclusions [F2] and $\Sigma$ has the weak topology. It satisfies $p\circ j=\mathrm{id}_K$ and is $W$-invariant because $\pi(vq)=\pi(q)$ [F2]. Every simplex is a left translate of a simplex of $K$: if $q_0=\dot q_0W_{T_0}$, then for each $i$, [F2] gives $q_i=\dot q_0W_{T_i}$, so left multiplication by $\dot q_0^{-1}$ takes the chain to $W_{T_0}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq W_{T_k}$. Thus the orbit projection $q_\Sigma\colon\Sigma\to W\backslash\Sigma$ restricts to a surjection on $K$. If $x,x'\in K$ and $v\cdot x=x'$, then $W$-invariance gives $x=p(x)=p(v\cdot x)=p(x')=x'$, so $K$ meets each orbit exactly once. The induced map $\bar p\colon W\backslash\Sigma\to K$ is continuous because $\bar p\circ q_\Sigma=p$ and the quotient topology makes $q_\Sigma$ a quotient map: for open $V\subseteq K$, $q_\Sigma^{-1}(\bar p^{-1}(V))=p^{-1}(V)$ is open. The restriction $q_\Sigma|_K$ is continuous and surjective, so $W\backslash\Sigma$ is compact as a continuous image of compact $K$ [F6]. Since $K$ is Hausdorff, [F6] makes $\bar p$ a homeomorphism. Hence $K$ is a strict fundamental domain. [step 4.1, F1, F2, F6, F7, F8, F11, F16, algebra]

6.1 Clause (3), action and properness. For a coset $q=wW_T$ and $v\in W$, put $a(v,q):=\dot{vq}^{-1}v\dot q\in W_T$ by [F2], [F7], and define $L_v\colon C_q\to C_{vq}$ in the type-$T$ charts by $L_v=\rho(a(v,q))|_{C_T}$. This is an isometry because $a(v,q)\in W_T$ permutes the orbit vertices of $C_T$. Also $a(1,q)=1$, so $L_1=\mathrm{id}$, and $a(u,vq)a(v,q)=\dot{uvq}^{-1}uv\dot q=a(uv,q)$, so $L_uL_v=L_{uv}$; these are the left-action identities [F8]. If $p\le q$ has types $U\subseteq T$, let $g:=\dot q^{-1}\dot p$, $a_q:=a(v,q)$, $a_p:=a(v,p)$, and $g':=\dot{vq}^{-1}\dot{vp}=a_qga_p^{-1}$. For $x\in C_U$, [F3] says $a_p\in W_U$ fixes $z_{T,U}$, so $L_v|_{C_q}(h_{p,q}(x))=\rho(a_qg)(x+z_{T,U})=\rho(g')\rho(a_p)(x+z_{T,U})=h_{vp,vq}(L_v|_{C_p}(x))$. Thus $L_v$ preserves the equivalence relation defining $X$ and descends to an action by cellwise isometries. It preserves the chain metric because it sends each chain to one of the same length, and its inverse is $L_{v^{-1}}$. Each $L_v|_{C_q}$ carries the vertices of $C_q$ to those of $C_{vq}$, hence sends their barycentres to each other; the affine barycentric maps on carrier simplices show that this action agrees, under [step 4.1], with the natural left action on $\Sigma$. For properness, let $C\subseteq\Sigma$ be compact. By [F14], every point has a neighborhood contained in a finite closed star, hence meeting only finitely many cells; finitely many such neighborhoods cover $C$, so $C$ meets only finitely many cells. For cells $uW_T$ and $vW_{T'}$, the set of $w$ with $w(uW_T)\cap vW_{T'}\ne\emptyset$ equals $vW_{T'}W_Tu^{-1}$: [F2] says exactly that $(wu)^{-1}v\in W_TW_{T'}$. This set is finite because $W_T$ and $W_{T'}$ are finite. There are only finitely many pairs of cells meeting $C$, so only finitely many $w$ satisfy $wC\cap C\ne\emptyset$. [step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, F2, F3, F4, F7, F8, F9, F10, F14]

6.2 Clause (5). Let $R$ be the relation on $W\times K$ in the statement and put $S(x)=\{s:x\in K_s\}$. If the carrier chain of $x\in K$ is $T_0\subset\cdots\subset T_k$, then $x\in K_s=|\mathbb S_{\geq\{s\}}|$ exactly when every vertex of that carrier chain contains $s$, which is equivalent to $s\in T_0$. Thus $S(x)=T_0$ and the subgroup it generates is $W_{T_0}$ ([[def-generated-subgroup]]). For $u\in W_{T_0}$, the chamber carrier chain $W_{T_0}\subset\cdots\subset W_{T_k}$ has each vertex fixed by $u$, since [F2] gives $uW_{T_i}=W_{T_i}$; hence $u$ fixes $j(x)$. The relation $R$ is an equivalence relation because on each fibre over $x$ it is equality of left cosets of the subgroup $W_{T_0}$, and $[w,x]\mapsto w\cdot j(x)$ is well defined by the fixed-point calculation. It is $W$-equivariant and surjective: a point with carrier chain $q_0\subset\cdots\subset q_k$ has, by [F2], the form $\dot q_0W_{T_0}\subset\cdots\subset\dot q_0W_{T_k}$; its barycentric coordinates give $x$ in the chamber simplex on $T_0\subset\cdots\subset T_k$, and its image is $\dot q_0\cdot j(x)$. It is injective: if $w\cdot j(x)=w'\cdot j(x')$, equality of carrier simplices and barycentric coordinates gives $x=x'$ and $wW_{T_0}=w'W_{T_0}$; therefore $w^{-1}w'\in W_{T_0}=W_{S(x)}$ and $[w,x]=[w',x']$. For continuity, the map $F\colon W\times K\to\Sigma$, $F(w,x)=w\cdot j(x)$, is continuous: the slices $\{w\}\times K$ are open and its restriction to each is the continuous map $x\mapsto w\cdot j(x)$. It is constant on $R$-classes, so it descends continuously through the quotient projection by [F11]. For the inverse, on a simplex with chain $q_0\subset\cdots\subset q_k$, use the unique representative $\dot q_0$ and the affine barycentric-coordinate map to its chamber simplex in $K$, then send that point $x$ to $[\dot q_0,x]$. This is continuous on the simplex by the product and quotient topologies [F11]. These formulas agree on common faces: when the minimum coset rises to $q_j$, both representatives lie in $q_j$, so their quotient classes agree because $\dot q_0^{-1}\dot q_j\in W_{T_j}=W_{S(x)}$. The weak topology of $\Sigma$ is simplexwise, so the inverse is continuous. The two maps are mutually inverse by the carrier-chain construction, proving the claimed homeomorphism.
