## Statement

Assume AC for the supplied well-definedness and isotopy invariance of geometric
intersection numbers, including the normal-form contribution table, and to
move between the boundary-fixed mapping class $f$ and its braid class
(the Artin-to-smooth dictionary of [[def-basic-arcs-admissible-curves-and-normal-form]],
including presentation completeness; the topological comparison is
[[thm-braid-group-is-the-boundary-fixed-mapping-class-group-of-the-punctured-disk]]). Let
$b_0,\dots,b_m$ be a basic set of curves in $(D,\Delta)$ and let $f\in G$ be a
boundary-fixed mapping class. If
$$I(b_j,f(b_k))=I(b_j,f^2(b_k))=I(b_j,b_k)\qquad\text{for all }0\le j,k\le m,$$
then $[f]=1$ in $G$. Both iterates are required: the hypothesis on $f$ alone
only forces $f$ to act on the basic arcs as a product of the standard twists,
and the second iterate together with the freeness of the twist subgroup is what
kills the exponents.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** The standard picture with basic arcs $b_0,\dots,b_m$, nested curves $l_0,\dots,l_{m-1}$ and twists $\tau_j$, and a boundary-fixed mapping class $f$ whose intersection table with the basic arcs is the identity table for both $f$ and $f^2$.

[L1] The action of $G$ on isotopy classes of admissible curves preserves the ordinary intersection number $I$; the hypotheses of the statement are invariant under replacing the basic set by a $G$-translate, and the conclusion $[f]=1$ is conjugation invariant, so it suffices to prove the statement in the standard picture ([[def-basic-arcs-admissible-curves-and-normal-form]], [[lem-geometric-intersection-numbers-are-isotopy-invariants]]).

[L2] If $g\in G$ satisfies $g(b_k)\simeq b_k$ for all $k$, then $[g]=1$: the source's Lemma 3.4 isotopes $g$ to a representative fixing the spine $b_0\cup\dots\cup b_m$ pointwise and applies the disk mapping-class theorem, and the endpoint bookkeeping uses that the $b_k$ meet only consecutively ([[def-basic-arcs-admissible-curves-and-normal-form]]).

[L3] If $c$ is an admissible curve with $I(b_j,c)=I(b_j,b_k)$ for all $j$, then, in the standard picture, $c\simeq b_0$ or $\tau_0^{-1}(b_0)$ when $k=0$, $c\simeq b_k$ or $\tau_k^{\pm1}(b_k)$ when $1\le k<m$, and $c\simeq b_m$ when $k=m$; the proof is the source's Lemma 3.5, a finite read-off from the intersection tables of the normal form ([[lem-normal-form-string-types-and-their-geometric-intersection-contributions]]).

[L4] The twists $\tau_j$ commute, $\tau_j$ fixes $b_k$ up to isotopy for $k\ne j$, and the subgroup generated by their classes is free abelian of rank $m$ ([[lem-standard-twists-fix-the-complementary-basic-arcs-and-commute]], [[lem-standard-disk-twists-generate-a-free-abelian-subgroup]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 *A preliminary identification.* Suppose $g\in G$ satisfies $g(b_k)\simeq b_k$ for all $k$. Then $[g]=1$ by [L2], and in particular $g(b_k)$ is isotopic to $b_k$ for every $k$ and the intersection table of $g$ with the basic set is the identity table. [L1, L2]

1.2 *The intersection table determines the action on the basic curves.* Let $c$ be admissible and suppose $I(b_j,c)=I(b_j,b_k)$ for all $j$. For $k<m$, [L3] gives $c\simeq\tau_k^{\nu}(b_k)$ for an integer $\nu\in\{-1,0,1\}$, with $\nu\le0$ when $k=0$; for $k=m$, it gives $c\simeq b_m$ directly. Consequently, applying this to $c=f(b_k)$ for each $k$ separately, there are integers $\nu_0\in\{-1,0\}$ and $\nu_1,\dots,\nu_{m-1}\in\{-1,0,1\}$ with $f(b_k)\simeq\tau_k^{\nu_k}(b_k)\quad(0\le k\le m-1),\qquad f(b_m)\simeq b_m .$ [L3]

2.1 *An explicit model for $f$.* Let $g:=\tau_0^{\nu_0}\tau_1^{\nu_1}\cdots\tau_{m-1}^{\nu_{m-1}}\in G$. Since the twists commute and $\tau_j$ fixes the complementary basic arcs by [L4], one has $g(b_k)\simeq\tau_k^{\nu_k}(b_k)$ for $k\le m-1$ and $g(b_m)\simeq b_m$; comparing with step 1.2, $f(b_k)\simeq g(b_k)$ for every $k$. Applying step 1.1 to $g^{-1}f$, which fixes each $b_k$ up to isotopy, gives $[f]=[g]$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, L4]

3.1 *The same for the square, and comparison of exponents.* Applying the same argument to $f^2$, whose intersection table with the basic set is also the identity table by hypothesis, produces integers $\mu_0\in\{-1,0\}$ and $\mu_1,\dots,\mu_{m-1}\in\{-1,0,1\}$ with $f^2\simeq\tau_0^{\mu_0}\cdots\tau_{m-1}^{\mu_{m-1}}.$ Since $[f]=[g]$, one has $[f^2]=[g]^2=\prod_j\tau_j^{2\nu_j}$, and therefore $\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\tau_j^{2\nu_j-\mu_j}\simeq 1 .$ [step 2.1, L4]

4.1 *Freeness kills the exponents.* By the freeness of the twist subgroup [L4], the relation $\prod_j\tau_j^{2\nu_j-\mu_j}\simeq1$ forces $2\nu_j=\mu_j$ for every $j$. Since each $\mu_j$ lies in $\{-1,0,1\}$ and each $2\nu_j$ is even, the only possibility is $\nu_j=\mu_j=0$ for all $j$; hence $[f]=[g]=1$, as required. [step 3.1, L4]

5.1 *Conclusion.* The identity intersection table for $f$ and $f^2$ forces $[f]=1$. AC is inherited through the braid/mapping-class dictionary and the supplied well-definedness and isotopy invariance of geometric intersection numbers used in [L1] and [L3]; the local counts and exponent bookkeeping are finite. [L1, L3, step 4.1] ∎
