## Example

On $T=\{a,b\}$ take mean zero and constant kernel $k(s,t)=1$. Observe both
indices with independent noise covariance $I_2$, and test the repeated list
$X_*=(b,b)$. At observation vector $y=(1,0)$, the selected posterior-kernel
mean is $(1/3,1/3)$ and covariance is
$$\frac13\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},$$
which is singular, although the observed covariance is positive definite.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; the two-index constant-kernel process; and noise
$\epsilon\sim N_2(0,I_2)$ independent of the process.

[A1] AC is required by the finite Gaussian-process and multivariate-normal
constructions. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] Any symmetric positive-semidefinite kernel on an arbitrary set defines a
canonical cylinder-space Gaussian process under AC.
[[thm-gaussian-process-exists-from-mean-and-positive-semidefinite-covariance-kernel]]

[F2] Under AC, every finite symmetric positive-semidefinite covariance matrix
defines a possibly singular Gaussian law. [[def-multivariate-normal-law]]

[F3] A finite product of sigma-finite measures exists and has the rectangle
product formula. [[thm-sigma-finite-product-measure-exists-is-rectangular-and-is-unique]]

[F4] Product-coordinate random elements are independent when their rectangle
probabilities factor. [[def-independent-random-elements]]
[[thm-rectangle-criterion-for-independent-random-elements]]

[F5] Coordinates on the cylinder sigma-algebra are measurable, and a finite
product sigma-algebra is generated by its coordinate sets.
[[def-coordinate-maps-and-cylinder-sigma-algebra]]
[[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]]

[F6] With positive-definite observed covariance, the finite posterior law is
Gaussian with the Schur-complement covariance, which may be singular.
[[thm-finite-gaussian-process-posterior-and-predictive-laws]]

## Verification

**Given:** The parameters, repeated test list, and observation vector in the Example.

1.1 For any finite coefficient list, the constant kernel's quadratic form is $(\sum_i a_i)^2\ge0$. Thus [F1] constructs the cylinder GP and [F2] supplies $N_2(0,I_2)$. Their product probability exists by [F3]; its process and noise random elements are measurable by [F5] and independent by [F4]. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 The observation covariance is $K_{XX}=J_2$, which has rank one, while $C=J_2+I_2=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}$ has quadratic form $u^2+v^2+(u+v)^2>0$ for every nonzero $(u,v)$. Hence it is positive definite and $C^{-1}=\frac13\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\-1&2\end{pmatrix}$. For the repeated test list, $K_{*X}=K_{**}=J_2$. Since $C$ is positive definite, the hypotheses of [F6] hold. [F6, given, algebra]

2.1 Multiplication gives $K_{*X}C^{-1}y=(1/3,1/3)^T$. Also $C^{-1}J_2=\frac13J_2$, so $K_{*X}C^{-1}K_{X*}=\frac23J_2$ and the conditional covariance is $J_2-\frac23J_2=\frac13J_2$. Its determinant is zero and its rank is one. By [F6] this is a valid singular Gaussian conditional law. [F6, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 The prior observation covariance and conditional predictive covariance are both singular, whereas the matrix inverted by the formula is the positive-definite observed covariance $C$. This verifies why singular prior and predictive blocks are permitted under the stated condition. [F6, step 1.2, step 2.1] ∎
