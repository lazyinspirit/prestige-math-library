## Statement

In the ungraded HLZ setting A=Ã={0}, let V be a conformal vertex algebra and C a full category satisfying HLZ Assumption 10.1: generalized weights are real, each object has a finite nilpotence exponent for the nilpotent part of L(0), and C is closed under images, contragredients, finite direct sums, and the required P(z)-tensor products. If every object is C1-cofinite and quasi-finite-dimensional, products and iterates of logarithmic intertwiners in C have the property of [[def-cft-hlz-convergence-extension-property]]. The convergence and extension property without logarithms also holds if C is ordinary semisimple with finitely many irreducible objects and finite intertwining spaces, and the following additional ambient-admission condition holds: every V-module generated by one generalized L(0)-eigenvector and having an algebraic generalized-weight decomposition is grading-restricted and belongs to C. No lower bound or finite-dimensionality of the weight spaces is assumed before this admission condition is applied. This is an explicit additional qualification of the no-log assertion; existence of relative P(z)-tensor products alone does not imply it.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Exactly the original Statement, including its real weights, objectwise uniform nilpotence and closure hypotheses. The additional ambient-admission condition belongs only to the corrected ordinary no-log branch; the multipoint logarithmic analytic branch retains its original full scope.

## Proof

1.1 First each module in question has finitely many weight cosets modulo Z and a lower bound. Quasi-finiteness excludes weights tending to minus infinity. Choose finitely many homogeneous representatives of its finite C1 quotient. Repeatedly replace a vector outside their span by a finite sum u_{-1}v, with positive integral wt(u). Each replacement lowers the input weight by a positive integer; the lower bound makes the process terminate. Hence every weight is a representative weight plus a nonnegative integer. In addition, for an intertwiner of objects with nilpotence exponents K1,K2,K3 the L0 bracket on fixed generalized-weight blocks identifies differentiation in log(x) with N3 minus the two input nilpotent actions. These three commuting actions have joint nilpotence exponent at most K1+K2+K3-2. Its log degree is therefore at most K1+K2+K3-3, uniformly in the intermediate weight. Every fixed finite chain consequently has finitely many exponent cosets in every sewing variable and one finite logarithmic degree bound. [given, F2, algebra]

1.2 We use a quotient that also makes coordinate inversion honest. Let J_W be the span of ALL rational global-current Ward residues on the punctured sphere: the state is any finite state combination, its scalar coefficient is rational in the sphere coordinate with poles only at the marked points, finite insertions act in their local coordinates, and the exterior residue is transposed using the opposite field. Every such relation annihilates a formal intertwiner chain: partial fractions reduce the scalar multiplier to polynomials and finite-order poles; repeated intertwiner Jacobi moves the current across each vertex, with paired internal-edge residues canceling. This is a coefficientwise formal identity with finite exterior-mode contributions, not analytic contour deformation. The specified A_i,C,D are among these relations, so J is contained in J_W and Q_W=T/J_W remains finite over R_n. No assertion that A_i,C,D alone span every global-current relation is needed. In the rest of the proof Q means this finite quotient Q_W. The quotient has an actual flat connection. On T set D_l=partial_{z_l}+U_l(L(-1)). Its summands commute for distinct l. Direct differentiation of the displayed relations in F1 gives $$D_l A_i(u,\mathbf w)=A_i(u,U_l(L(-1))\mathbf w)+\delta_{il}A_i(Tu,\mathbf w),\quad D_l C(u,\mathbf w)=C(u,U_l(L(-1))\mathbf w),\quad D_l D(u,\mathbf w)=D(u,U_l(L(-1))\mathbf w).$$ For the first two identities use [L(-1),u_k]=-k u_{k-1} and (Tu)^*_k=-k u^*_{k-1}; differentiation of each rational coefficient cancels the corresponding reindexed commutator. Here is the possibly nontrivial last identity explicitly. With g(z)=exp(z^{-1}L1)(-z^2)^{L0}, K(u_k)=g u_k g^{-1}, one has g'g^{-1}=2z^{-1}L0+z^{-2}L1 and $$g^{-1}(L(-1)+g'g^{-1})g=-z^{-2}L(-1),\qquad \partial_zK(u_k)+[L(-1),K(u_k)]=kz^{-2}K(u_{k-1}).$$ Thus the derivative plus commutator of sum_{k>=0}(-1)^k z^{k+1}K(u_k) is zero: its two terms cancel after k is replaced by k+1 in the second sum. All sums are finite on the chosen vector. For a general rational global-current residue R(u,r(t,z)), differentiation and [L(-1),Y(u,t)]=partial_tY(u,t), followed by formal integration by parts, give D_l R(u,r)-R(u,r)U_l(L(-1))=R(u,partial_{z_l}r), where the derivative of r is taken at fixed global t. Thus J_W is preserved too; the preceding explicit identities are its checks on the originally used reduction relations. Leibniz extends this to their R_n-span, so D_l descends to Q_W and the descended operators commute. Horizontal linear functionals are exactly the derivative identities of the scalar coefficient map. 1.3 We describe the boundary coordinates rather than assuming regularity survives a coordinate change. For a collection of colliding finite points, blow up its diagonal and choose two ordered labels as pivot. Write the first pivot as c and their difference as q, and write every other point in the cluster as c+q a_i, with the two pivot shapes 0 and 1. Where some shapes collide, repeat this construction for that subcluster. Distinct top-level clusters have distinct centers. Repeating for at most n+1 labels gives finite nested trees of clusters; at each stage at least one nontrivial cluster is separated into smaller ones. On a chart where the remaining shapes are distinct, every coordinate z_i and difference z_i-z_j is a monomial in the cluster scale variables times a holomorphic unit, unless its limiting value is nonzero, in which case it is a unit. Each individual scale q occurs in such a monomial with exponent zero or one. The formulas and their inverses away from the diagonals are rational; restricting to one such shape neighborhood inverts only finitely many nonzero polynomials. These charts cover the successive blowups: the ordinary blowup charts correspond exactly to choosing a nonzero pivot difference, and repeating resolves the remaining equal-shape diagonals. Thus the resulting boundary has coordinate equations q1...qr=0. This is a direct construction of the coordinate charts needed here, not an appeal to a general resolution theorem. At a generic finite boundary q=0 all z_i are holomorphic and delta z_i=q partial_q z_i is divisible by q. Other scale variables are inverted when inspecting this one boundary. [algebra, construct]

2.1 The finite R_n-module Q becomes a finite-type analytic module on the nonsingular configuration space X. It is locally free there, without assuming flatness of the module as an algebraic input. Indeed choose finitely many generators and express their connection derivatives by finite holomorphic matrices on a small polydisc. At a point, any functional on the finite quotient fiber has a unique formal horizontal extension by the commuting connection. All derivatives of a local relation remain relations, so its pairing with that extension is zero to every order. Solve the finite generator matrix equation on each coordinate line by Picard iteration; the kth iterated-integral term is bounded by M^k|z-p|^k/k!. The formal relation calculation and the identity theorem show these analytic solutions annihilate every relation. Integrability makes the line-by-line solutions independent of order: their Taylor coefficients are the same commuting covariant derivatives, hence agree on a polydisc. This gives inverse horizontal transports between quotient dual fibers and proves constant fiber dimension. Choose fiber-basis generators; Nakayama gives a local epimorphism from the free sheaf of that rank. Every local relation among these generators has coefficients vanishing at each point, since every fiber map is an isomorphism. Holomorphic coefficients on a manifold that vanish pointwise are zero. Thus the epimorphism is an isomorphism. The zero-rank case is included. We have obtained a finite-rank flat holomorphic connection on X, not merely unrelated scalar differential equations. 2.2 We give the needed parameter convergence estimate. Let delta F=A(q,b)F be an actual formal solution with finitely many exponent cosets, one finite log-degree bound K, coefficients holomorphic in b, and A holomorphic for |q|<R and b in a parameter neighborhood. On vector polynomials of degree at most K, coefficient comparison gives $$(n+\lambda+\partial_t-A_0(b))P_n=\sum_{j=1}^n A_j(b)P_{n-j},\qquad t=\log q.$$ On each compact parameter set the inverse on the left exists for every sufficiently large n and has norm at most C/n, by its finite-dimensional Neumann expansion. Cauchy's inequality on a circle of radius R1<R bounds ||A_j|| by M R1^{-j}, uniformly on that compact set. For rho<R1 enlarge H to bound the finitely many initial coefficient polynomials by H rho^{-n}. For all larger n the inequality $$||P_n||\le (CM/n)\sum_{j=1}^n R1^{-j}||P_{n-j}||$$ proves the same bound by induction, after choosing n large enough that (CM/n)sum_{j>=1}(rho/R1)^j<=1. This proves normal convergence on compact parameter sets and every |q|<R, with termwise differentiation. No nonresonance, parameter constancy of indicial roots or prior convergence of the formal solution is required. [step 1.1, algebra]

2.3 On such a chart let B_q be its polynomial coordinate ring localized at those unit polynomials and at the other scale variables, but not at q. It is Noetherian. The verified inequalities of F1 remain valid: every coordinate/difference has q-valuation zero or one, so each ordinary-mode coefficient has pole order at most k+1; opposite terms have no finite pole; and the D term has exactly the bound checked in F1. The pulled-back connection operator $$\mathcal D_q=q\partial_q+\sum_i(q\partial_q z_i)U_i(L(-1))$$ also preserves it. Its coefficient derivative preserves pole order, while every nonzero second coefficient is divisible by q and compensates the unit increase of tensor weight. Starting with a homogeneous tuple v of weight sigma, every mathcal D_q^k v consequently has a representative v_k in the cutoff with this same filtration bound sigma. If B is a lower bound on the cutoff tensor weights, fix an integer N>=sigma-B. Then q^N v_k belongs to the ONE finite B_q-module B_q tensor E for every k. 3.1 Infinity is handled by actual reflected intertwiners. For an intertwiner I of type (B;A,M) put K(x)=exp(xL1)exp(pi i L0)x^{-2L0} on A and define $$\langle I^\vee(a,x)b',m\rangle=\langle b',I(K(x)a,x^{-1})m\rangle.$$ It has type (M';A,B'). This is not just a vector-space transpose. The identity $$K'K^{-1}=-2x^{-1}L0-L1,\qquad K L(-1)K^{-1}=-x^{-2}L(-1)-2x^{-1}L0-L1$$ gives K'-x^{-2}L(-1)K=K L(-1), hence its derivative identity. Transpose intertwiner Jacobi, substitute the inverted variables and the opposite-state operator exp(xL1)(-x^{-2})^{L0}; the change of formal residue has Jacobian -x^{-2}, and the opposite-field identity in F1 transforms the three delta terms to precisely the three terms of Jacobi for I^\vee. This proves its Jacobi identity and sl2 brackets; truncation follows from the weight rule, the lower bounds and the finite L1 exterior family. Repeating this pairing identity from the left end of a chain to the right reverses the chain, replaces every intermediate object by its contragredient, and replaces zi by xi=zi^{-1}. All these objects are in C. The inverse K(xi) applied to a fixed input is a finite exterior family times real powers of xi and finite powers of log(xi), by step 1.1 and local nilpotence of L1. At a generic infinity boundary the reflected insertion coordinates are all holomorphic: those tending to infinity become a finite zero-cluster, and the other coordinates are units after the other boundary parameters are inverted. Step 2.3 applies to that reflected chain. The same inversion defines a change of the quotient connection itself: swap the first and last exterior factors and apply K(xi) inverse to each finite insertion factor. The opposite-state identity sends each global-current residue to its inverted global-current residue, so it carries the FULL global-current relation span J_W onto the reflected full global-current relation span; applying the inverse inversion proves bijectivity. Hence the argument applies to every horizontal functional, not just to the originally prescribed intertwiner coefficients. On any fixed finite generating tensor space both this change and its inverse have a finite power/log exterior expansion. Finite products with those powers and log polynomials preserve one-variable regular singularity, by tensoring their finite Euler systems. Thus every boundary component, including each infinity component, has actual one-variable regular-singular equations. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.3, algebra]

4.1 We now prove the simultaneous assertion from these equations. Pull the finite flat connection of step 2.1 back to a boundary chart P=(Delta*)^r times Delta^s. A horizontal fundamental frame exists on its universal cover by the same Picard construction. Evaluate its columns on the fixed finite quotient generators to obtain a possibly rectangular matrix F of scalar functions; no trivialization of the bundle on the punctured chart is assumed here. flatness makes its continuation depend only on path homotopy, as follows by subdividing a homotopy square into small coordinate rectangles and using the unique local Taylor solution. The boundary monodromies M1,...,Mr of this frame commute, since loops around distinct coordinate axes commute. Choose commuting logarithms Bj with exp(2pi i Bj)=Mj: on each generalized eigenspace of Mj choose a scalar logarithm and use the finite nilpotent logarithm series. Hermite polynomial interpolation expresses this Bj as a polynomial in Mj, so all chosen Bj commute. Then $$H(q,b)=F(q,b)\exp(-\sum_j B_j\log q_j)$$ is single-valued and holomorphic on P. Holding the other variables fixed, steps 2.3 and4.1 and F3 imply that every entry of F has at most a power times a finite log-polynomial growth as qj tends to zero on a sector. Multiplication by exp(-Bj log qj) preserves this kind of bound. Since H is single-valued, the same bound holds around a full punctured disk. The one-variable Laurent coefficient formula shows that H has only finitely many negative Laurent coefficients in qj. Thus H is separately meromorphic across each coordinate axis, for every fixed choice of the other nonzero coordinates. [F3, step 2.1, step 2.3, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 We justify a UNIFORM pole bound rather than presuming separate meromorphicity is enough. Fix j and expand a single entry H=sum_{k in Z}h_k(q_other,b)qj^k on a product annulus. Each h_k is holomorphic on the connected punctured parameter polydisc. For every parameter value all h_k with sufficiently negative k vanish, by step 4.1. The closed sets Z_N={all h_k=0 for k<-N} therefore cover that parameter polydisc. One Z_N has interior: otherwise a nested sequence of closed balls, each avoiding the next closed set and with radii tending to zero, has a common point outside their union, a contradiction. The balls can be chosen inside an initially compact parameter ball, so completeness supplies the common point. The identity theorem now gives h_k=0 everywhere for every k<-N. Repeat for the finitely many entries and boundary variables. Hence q1^N1...qr^Nr H has no negative Laurent exponent in ANY variable. Product Cauchy integrals give its Taylor series, convergent on every smaller full polydisc; equivalently its multiple Laurent series is normally convergent there after that finite monomial is removed. This explicitly extends H meromorphically through every boundary intersection, with holomorphic shape-parameter coefficients. The columns of H are an actual frame of the quotient dual away from the boundary. At the meromorphic generic point some full-rank minor of its evaluated matrix is nonzero. Its adjugate gives the inverse change of frame over the meromorphic field; quotient relations make the changes obtained from different minors agree. Thus H is a meromorphic frame, even if the finite set of quotient generators is redundant. The gauge H therefore transforms the pulled-back connection into the ACTUAL simultaneous system $$q_j\partial_{q_j}U=B_jU,\qquad \partial_{b_a}U=0.$$ Finally exp(sum Bj log qj) is a finite sum of products q^lambda times bounded log polynomials after simultaneous generalized eigenspace decomposition of the commuting Bj. Multiplying by the convergent Laurent series of H gives the required convergent finite-exponent, finite-log expansion at every simultaneous boundary, with the prescribed continued branches. No unspecified several-variable Fuchsian theorem has been invoked. [step 4.1, algebra]

This is a bound on the coefficient support for fixed exterior vectors; it is NOT falsely asserted to bound the whole module subsequently generated by a coefficient.
