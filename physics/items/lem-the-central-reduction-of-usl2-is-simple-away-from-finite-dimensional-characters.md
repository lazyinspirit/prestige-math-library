## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb C)$ with
standard basis $e,f,h$, Casimir element $\Omega$, and center
$Z(U(\mathfrak g))=\mathbb C[\Omega]$. Let $\chi$ be a central character,
determined by the scalar $\chi(\Omega)$. Then the central reduction $U_\chi$ is a
simple ring if and only if $\chi$ is not the central character of any nonzero
finite-dimensional $U(\mathfrak g)$-module, equivalently if and only if
$\chi(\Omega)$ is not the eigenvalue of $\Omega$ on any finite-dimensional
simple module $L(n)$, $n\ge0$ (these eigenvalues are pairwise distinct). When
$U_\chi$ is simple, every nonzero $U(\mathfrak g)$-module with central character
$\chi$ is faithful over $U_\chi$, every Verma module $M(\lambda)$ with
$\chi_\lambda=\chi$ is simple, and
$\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak g)}M(\lambda)=U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb C)$ with basis $e,f,h$ and Casimir $\Omega=ef+fe+\tfrac12h^2$ (a nonzero scalar multiple of the library Casimir), a central character $\chi$, the scalar $c:=\chi(\Omega)\in\mathbb C$, and $U_\chi=U(\mathfrak g)/U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi$.

[F1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). The PBW symbol of a central element is invariant under the adjoint action: for each $x\in\mathfrak g$, [[lem-adjoint-action-preserves-the-associated-graded-of-a-two-sided-ideal]] gives $\sigma_n([x,z])=D_x\sigma_n(z)$ for $z\in F^nU(\mathfrak g)$, so $D_x\sigma(z)=0$. Chevalley restriction identifies $S(\mathfrak g)^{\mathfrak g}$ with $S(\mathfrak h)^W$ ([[thm-symmetric-invariants-restrict-to-weyl-invariants]]); for $\mathfrak{sl}_2$, $\mathfrak h=\mathbb Ch$ is the Cartan subalgebra, the roots are $\pm\alpha$ with $\alpha(h)=2$, and the reflection sends the coordinate $h$ to $-h$, so $S(\mathfrak h)^W=\mathbb C[h^2]$ ([[def-special-linear-lie-algebra-sl-two]], [[def-cartan-subalgebra-of-a-lie-algebra]], [[def-normalizer-of-a-lie-subalgebra]], [[def-root-and-root-space-relative-to-a-cartan-subalgebra]], [[def-root-reflections-and-the-weyl-group-action]]). The library Casimir $C$ of [[def-quadratic-casimir-element]] has PBW symbol restricting to $h^2/8$ on $\mathfrak h$, by the Killing-form calculation in step 1.1 ([[def-killing-form-of-a-semisimple-lie-algebra]]); hence $\sigma(C)$ generates $S(\mathfrak g)^{\mathfrak g}$. Subtracting the matching scalar multiple of $C^k$ from any central element of degree $2k$ lowers its PBW degree, so induction and centrality of $C$ give $Z(U(\mathfrak g))=\mathbb C[C]$. Step 1.1 gives $\Omega=4C$, hence $Z(U(\mathfrak g))=\mathbb C[\Omega]$ and $\ker\chi=(\Omega-c)$ as an ideal of this polynomial algebra.

[F2] $U_\chi$ is the quotient of $U(\mathfrak g)$ by the two-sided ideal $U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi=(\Omega-c)U(\mathfrak g)$; a $U(\mathfrak g)$-module has central character $\chi$ exactly when it is a $U_\chi$-module, and $U_\chi$ is a unital associative $\mathbb C$-algebra ([[def-central-reduction-of-the-enveloping-algebra]], [[def-central-character-of-a-lie-algebra-module]]).

[F3] Ordered monomials in a basis of $\mathfrak g$ form a basis of $U(\mathfrak g)$; the PBW filtration by tensor degree has $\operatorname{gr}U(\mathfrak g)=\mathbb C[e,f,h]$, a polynomial algebra; symbols multiply and the symbol of a product of nonzero symbols is nonzero ([[thm-pbw-ordered-monomial-basis-for-the-enveloping-algebra]], [[def-pbw-filtration-by-tensor-degree-on-the-enveloping-algebra]], [[prop-associated-graded-of-the-pbw-filtration-is-commutative]]).

[F4] $[h,e]=2e$, $[h,f]=-2f$, $[e,f]=h$, so $he=e(h+2)$, $hf=f(h-2)$ ([[def-special-linear-lie-algebra-sl-two]]). The finite-dimensional simple modules $L(n)$, $n\ge0$, have $\Omega$-eigenvalue $n(n+2)/2$, these values are pairwise distinct, and every finite-dimensional simple module is some $L(n)$ ([[prop-casimir-eigenvalue-on-a-highest-weight-module]], [[thm-finite-dimensional-simple-modules-are-classified-by-dominant-highest-weights]]).

[F5] The Verma module $M(\lambda)$ is simple if and only if $\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^\vee\rangle\notin\mathbb Z_{>0}$ for all positive roots; for $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ there is one positive root and $\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^\vee\rangle=\lambda+1$ in the standard identification $\lambda=\lambda(h)$, so $M(\lambda)$ is simple exactly when $\lambda\notin\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$, and for $\lambda\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ its central character is that of the finite-dimensional module $L(\lambda)$ ([[cor-verma-irreducibility-criterion-from-shapovalov-determinants]], [[def-verma-module]], [[prop-casimir-eigenvalue-on-a-highest-weight-module]]).

[F6] $\mathbb C[h]$ has polynomial division with remainder ([[cor-polynomial-ring-over-a-field-is-euclidean]]). In a nonzero ideal, a nonzero polynomial $P$ of least degree generates the ideal: dividing any member by $P$ leaves a remainder in the ideal of smaller degree, which must be zero.

[F7] The field $\mathbb C$ is algebraically closed, so every nonconstant polynomial in $\mathbb C[h]$ has a root ([[thm-the-complex-numbers-are-algebraically-closed]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The element $\Omega=ef+fe+\tfrac12h^2$ is central. Indeed, using $[h,e]=2e$, $[h,f]=-2f$, $[e,f]=h$: $[h,\Omega]=2ef-2ef-2fe+2fe=0$; $[e,ef]=[e,e]f+e[e,f]=eh$, $[e,fe]=[e,f]e+f[e,e]=he$, and $[e,h^2]=[e,h]h+h[e,h]=-2eh-2he$, so $[e,\Omega]=eh+he-eh-he=0$; and $[f,ef]=[f,e]f+e[f,f]=-hf$, $[f,fe]=[f,f]e+f[f,e]=-fh$, $[f,h^2]=[f,h]h+h[f,h]=2fh+2hf$, so $[f,\Omega]=-hf-fh+fh+hf=0$. The Killing form in the basis $e,f,h$ has $B(h,h)=8$ and $B(e,f)=B(f,e)=4$ with all other pairings $0$ (the adjoint matrices have columns $ad_h(e)=2e$, $ad_h(f)=-2f$, $ad_h(h)=0$ and $ad_e(f)=h$, $ad_e(h)=-2e$, $ad_f(e)=-h$, $ad_f(h)=2f$, giving traces $8$ and $4$), so dual bases are $h/8$, $f/4$, $e/4$ and the library Casimir is $C=\tfrac18h^2+\tfrac14(ef+fe)=\tfrac14\Omega$. To prove that this Casimir generates the center, first $\mathbb Ch$ is a Cartan subalgebra: it is abelian, hence nilpotent, and the displayed brackets show $N_{\mathfrak g}(\mathbb Ch)=\mathbb Ch$ by [[def-cartan-subalgebra-of-a-lie-algebra]] and [[def-normalizer-of-a-lie-subalgebra]]. The root spaces are $\mathbb Ce$, $\mathbb Cf$ with roots $\alpha,-\alpha$, where $\alpha(h)=2$; therefore the unique root reflection acts by sign on $\mathfrak h^*$, and $S(\mathfrak h)^W=\mathbb C[h^2]$ ([[def-root-and-root-space-relative-to-a-cartan-subalgebra]], [[def-root-reflections-and-the-weyl-group-action]]). By Chevalley restriction, $S(\mathfrak g)^{\mathfrak g}\cong S(\mathfrak h)^W$, and the PBW symbol $\sigma_2(C)=\tfrac18h^2+\tfrac12ef$ restricts to $h^2/8$, so it generates $S(\mathfrak g)^{\mathfrak g}$ ([[thm-symmetric-invariants-restrict-to-weyl-invariants]]). If $z\in Z(U(\mathfrak g))$ has PBW degree $d>0$, the adjoint-symbol identity in [[lem-adjoint-action-preserves-the-associated-graded-of-a-two-sided-ideal]] shows $\sigma(z)$ is invariant. It is homogeneous; since the invariant ring is the polynomial ring generated by the degree-two element $\sigma(C)$, one has $d=2k$ and $\sigma(z)=a\sigma(C)^k$ for some $a\in\mathbb C^\times$. Thus $z-aC^k$ is central of strictly smaller PBW degree. Induction on degree, with degree-zero elements being scalars and $C$ central by the direct commutator calculation above, yields $Z(U(\mathfrak g))=\mathbb C[C]=\mathbb C[\Omega]$. Consequently $\ker\chi=(\Omega-c)$. Since $ef=fe+h$, one has $\Omega=2fe+h+\tfrac12h^2$ in $U(\mathfrak g)$, hence $fe=\tfrac12(\Omega-h-\tfrac12h^2)$ exactly; in $U_\chi$, where $\Omega$ acts by $c$, this reads $fe=p(h):=\tfrac12(c-h-\tfrac12h^2)$ and $ef=p(h)+h=:q(h)$. Equivalently $p(h)=-\tfrac14(h^2+2h-2c)$ and $q(h)=p(h-2)=-\tfrac14(h^2-2h-2c)$; also $he=e(h+2)$ and $hf=f(h-2)$. [F1, F2, F4, algebra]

2.1 The associated graded of $U_\chi$ for the induced PBW filtration is $\mathbb C[e,f,h]/(\sigma)$, where $\sigma$ is the symbol of $\Omega-c$, namely $2ef+\tfrac12h^2$ up to a nonzero scalar: the kernel of $U(\mathfrak g)\twoheadrightarrow U_\chi$ is generated by $\Omega-c$, whose symbol is a nonzerodivisor of the polynomial algebra $\mathbb C[e,f,h]$, so an element of that ideal has top-degree part in the ideal $(\sigma)$ and conversely every element of $(\sigma)$ occurs as the top-degree part of a product $u(\Omega-c)v$. Multiplying $\sigma$ by $2$ identifies the quotient with $\mathbb C[e,f,h]/(h^2+4ef)$; multiplication by $h^2+4ef$ from degree $d-2$ to degree $d$ is injective, so the degree-$d$ part has dimension $\binom{d+2}{2}-\binom{d}{2}=2d+1$. [F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Let $N$ be the $\mathbb C$-span of the normal forms $f^ih^a$ $(i,a\ge0)$ and $e^jh^a$ $(j\ge1,a\ge0)$, filtered by degree $i+a$, respectively $j+a$. Left multiplication by the generators preserves $N$ and raises degree by at most one: using $hf=f(h-2)$ and $he=e(h+2)$ from step 1.1 gives $h\cdot f^ih^a=f^ih^{a+1}-2i f^ih^a$ and $h\cdot e^jh^a=e^jh^{a+1}+2j e^jh^a$; $e\cdot f^ih^a=f^{i-1}q(h-2i+2)h^a$ for $i\ge1$ by step 1.1 and $e\cdot h^a=eh^a$; $e\cdot e^jh^a=e^{j+1}h^a$; $f\cdot f^ih^a=f^{i+1}h^a$ and $f\cdot h^a=fh^a$; $f\cdot e^jh^a=e^{j-1}p(h+2j-2)h^a$ for $j\ge1$. Since $F^nU_\chi$ is spanned by words of length at most $n$ in $e,f,h$, induction on $n$ gives $F^nU_\chi\subseteq N\cap F^nU_\chi$, so the normal forms span $U_\chi$. Exactly $(n+1)^2$ of them have degree at most $n$, and their symbols span $(\operatorname{gr}U_\chi)_{\le n}$, whose dimension is $\sum_{d\le n}(2d+1)=(n+1)^2$ by step 2.1; hence those symbols are a basis of $(\operatorname{gr}U_\chi)_{\le n}$ and, comparing top degrees, the normal forms are linearly independent. They therefore form a $\mathbb C$-basis of $U_\chi$, so $U_\chi$ is infinite-dimensional, and the basis exhibits the $\operatorname{ad}_h$-weight decomposition $W_{2k}=e^k\mathbb C[h]$ for $k\ge0$, $W_{-2k}=f^k\mathbb C[h]$ for $k\ge1$. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Let $J\ne0$ be a two-sided ideal of $U_\chi$. Since $[h,u]\in J$ for $u\in J$, and the weight components of $u$ are polynomial combinations of the elements $(\operatorname{ad}_h)^iu$, the ideal $J$ contains a nonzero weight vector. If it contains $u=e^kQ(h)$ with $k\ge1$ and $Q\ne0$, then $uf^k=Q(h-2k)\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}q(h-2i)\in J\cap\mathbb C[h]$ is nonzero, because $e^kf^k=\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}q(h-2i)$ and $\mathbb C[h]$ has no zero divisors and $Q(h-2k)\ne0$; if it contains $u=f^kQ(h)$ with $k\ge1$, then $e^ku=Q(h)\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}q(h-2i)$ is such a nonzero polynomial; and if it contains a nonzero element of weight $0$, that element already lies in $\mathbb C[h]$. Hence $J\cap\mathbb C[h]\ne0$. [step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 By [F6] the nonzero ideal $J\cap\mathbb C[h]$ is generated by a polynomial $P$, chosen of least degree; if $J\ne U_\chi$ then $P$ is nonconstant, since a nonzero constant in $J\cap\mathbb C[h]$ would put $1$ in $J$ and force $J=U_\chi$. [F6, step 4.1]

6.1 For $P\in J\cap\mathbb C[h]$ one has $[e,P]=eP-Pe=(P(h-2)-P(h))e\in J$ and $[f,P]=(P(h+2)-P(h))f\in J$, because $eP(h)=P(h-2)e$ and $fP(h)=P(h+2)f$. Multiplying by $f$ respectively $e$ and using $fe=p$, $ef=q=p+h$ gives the divisibilities $P\mid p\,(P(h+2)-P(h))$ and $P\mid q\,(P(h-2)-P(h))$ in $\mathbb C[h]$. [step 1.1, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 Let $r$ be a root of $P$. If $r+2$ is not a root of $P$, then $(P(h+2)-P(h))(r)=P(r+2)-P(r)=P(r+2)\ne0$, so the first divisibility of step 6.1 forces $p(r)=0$. If $r-2$ is not a root of $P$, then $(P(h-2)-P(h))(r)=P(r-2)\ne0$, so the second divisibility forces $q(r)=p(r)+r=0$. [step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 Since $P$ has finitely many roots, for every root $r$ the upward chain $r,r+2,r+4,\dots$ has a last member $s=r+2k$ with $s$ a root of $P$ and $s+2$ not a root, and the downward chain has a last member $s'=r-2l$ with $s'-2$ not a root. Step 7.1 then gives $p(s)=0$ and $q(s')=0$, so $s\in\{r_+,r_-\}$ and $s'\in\{q_+,q_-\}=\{r_++2,r_-+2\}$, where by step 1.1 $r_\pm=-1\pm t$ with $t^2=1+2c$ are the roots of $p$, and $r=-2k+s=s'+2l$ for some $k,l\ge0$. [step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 If $t\notin\mathbb Z$ then the two roots $r_\pm$ are distinct and not congruent modulo $2$ (their difference is $2t$). Writing any root $r$ as in step 8.1, the identity $s-s'=2(k+l)\ge0$ forces $s-s'\in\{-2,\ 2t-2,\ -2t-2\}$ to be nonnegative and even; the first value gives $k+l=-1$; the second gives $t=1+k+l\in\mathbb Z$; the third gives $t=-(1+k+l)\in\mathbb Z$. All are impossible, so $P$ has no roots and [F7] makes it constant, hence $J=U_\chi$ by step 5.1. Thus $U_\chi$ is simple whenever $t\notin\mathbb Z$, in particular whenever $1+2c$ is not the square of an integer. [F7, step 8.1, algebra]

9.2 If $t=0$ then $p(h)=-\tfrac14(h+1)^2$ has the single root $r_+=-1$, while $q(h)=p(h-2)=-\tfrac14(h-1)^2$ has the single root $q_+=1$; step 8.1 would give $-1-2k=r=1+2l$, i.e. $2(k+l)=-2$, which is impossible. So $P$ has no roots and [F7] makes it constant, whence $J=U_\chi$; $U_\chi$ is simple for $c=-\tfrac12$ as well. [F7, step 8.1, algebra]

10.1 Conversely let $t\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$, and put $n:=|t|-1\ge0$; then $c=(t^2-1)/2=n(n+2)/2$ is the $\Omega$-eigenvalue of the nonzero finite-dimensional simple module $L(n)$ by [F4], so $L(n)$ is a nonzero $U_\chi$-module. The action map $\varphi\colon U_\chi\to\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}L(n)$ is a nonzero algebra homomorphism (it sends $1$ to the identity), and its image is finite-dimensional while $U_\chi$ is infinite-dimensional by step 3.1, so $\ker\varphi\ne0$ and $\ker\varphi\ne U_\chi$: $U_\chi$ has a nonzero proper two-sided ideal and is not simple. Conversely, if a nonzero finite-dimensional $U(\mathfrak g)$-module has central character $\chi$, choose a nonzero submodule of least positive dimension; it is simple and $\Omega$ acts there by $c$, so [F4] makes it some $L(m)$ and $c=m(m+2)/2$. Thus $\chi$ is a finite-dimensional central character exactly when $1+2\chi(\Omega)$ is a positive square, equivalently when $\chi(\Omega)=n(n+2)/2$ for some $n\ge0$ (the integer parameter is $n=|t|-1$, since $t^2-1=(|t|-1)(|t|+1)$). Together with steps 9.1 and 9.2 this is the asserted criterion, and the eigenvalues $n(n+2)/2$ are pairwise distinct by [F4]. [step 3.1, step 9.1, step 9.2, F4, algebra]

11.1 Finally suppose $U_\chi$ is simple, let $N\ne0$ be a $U_\chi$-module, and let $\psi\colon U_\chi\to\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(N)$ be the action. Its kernel is a two-sided ideal and is proper because $N\ne0$ makes $\psi(1)=\operatorname{id}_N\ne0$; simplicity forces $\ker\psi=0$, so $N$ is faithful over $U_\chi$. If $\chi_\lambda=\chi$ for a weight $\lambda$ then $\lambda\notin\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$, because otherwise $\chi_\lambda$ would be the central character of the finite-dimensional module $L(\lambda)$ by [F5], contrary to the established criterion; hence $M(\lambda)$ is simple by [F5]. The annihilator of $M(\lambda)$ in $U(\mathfrak g)$ is the preimage of $\ker\psi$ for $N=M(\lambda)$, which is $\ker(U(\mathfrak g)\to U_\chi)=U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi$ by the definition of the annihilator as a kernel; therefore $\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak g)}M(\lambda)=U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi$, as claimed. [step 10.1, F2, F5] ∎
