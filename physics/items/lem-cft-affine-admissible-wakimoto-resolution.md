## Statement

Let $\lambda$ be a G-integrable admissible affine highest weight at positive rational $k+h^\vee$, with the vacuum integral root system. There is an actual exact two-sided Wakimoto complex
$$C^p(\lambda)=\bigoplus_{w\in W_\lambda,\ \ell^{\infty/2}(w)=p}W(w\mathbin\cdot\lambda),\qquad H^p(C)=\begin{cases}L(\lambda)&p=0,\\0&p\ne0.\end{cases}$$
Here $W(\mu)$ is the algebraically stabilizing antidominant-polarization limit defined in the proof. Its character is the Verma character. Every differential component on a semi-infinite cover is nonzero and spans the one-dimensional corresponding Wakimoto Hom space. In rank one the extra vacuum component takes its highest vector to the actual maximal-ideal singular state. Fixed full weights see finite sums and finite contractions; exactness is proved by compatible finite twisted complexes, not by an Euler-character identity.

[F5] The terminology in the Statement has the following precise meaning. Put $\widehat\Delta(\lambda)=\{\beta\in\widehat\Delta^{\mathrm{re}}:\langle\lambda+\widehat\rho,\beta^\vee\rangle\in\mathbb Z\}$. Thus on every inherited positive integral root the shifted pairing is a positive integer, and the integral system has full affine rank: these are exactly the regular-dominance and rank hypotheses of [F1]. “Vacuum integral root system” means equality with $\widehat\Delta(k\Lambda_0)$, where $\Lambda_0|_{\mathfrak h\oplus\mathbb Cd}=0$ and $\Lambda_0(K)=1$. “G-integrable” means that the finite simple raising and lowering generators act locally nilpotently.
