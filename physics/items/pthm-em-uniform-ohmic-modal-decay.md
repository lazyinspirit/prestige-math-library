## Statement

Assume the constant isotropic inertial-rest material branch of [[post-em-material-closure]] with $\varepsilon,\mu>0$, $\sigma\ge0$, $D=\varepsilon E$, $H=B/\mu$, $J_{\rm ext}=0$. The exact broadband physical reading additionally requires $\varepsilon\mu\ge c^{-2}$ as in that closure; otherwise the positive coefficients define its finite-band effective phase model without a universal front-speed claim. Put $v^2=(\varepsilon\mu)^{-1}$ and $\gamma=\sigma/\varepsilon$. The smooth equations of [[pthm-em-macroscopic-maxwell]] with initially zero volume charge give $E_t=v^2\operatorname{curl}B-\gamma E$, $B_t=-\operatorname{curl}E$ and zero divergences; in the finite-energy classes below these first-order equations with the declared PEC weak/variational boundary realization are adopted explicitly, rather than deduced from a smooth-only postulate. The quadratic stored energy is $\mathcal U(t)=\tfrac12(\varepsilon\|E(t)\|_2^2+\mu^{-1}\|B(t)\|_2^2)$, consistent with [[pthm-em-restricted-material-energy]].
(a) [Rectangular cavity] In the exact zero-normal-$B$ reflection-parity and divergence class of [[lem-em-rectangular-pec-energy-completeness]] and [[def-em-guide-cavity-geometry]](c), every initial pair has a unique $C(\mathbb R;L^2)$ distributional solution of this damped system in the same class. Finite Fourier mode sums converge in energy norm uniformly on compact time intervals and are dense at every time. The Fourier coefficient equations at $k_j=\pi n_j/L_j$ are $\widehat E_t=iv^2 k\times\widehat B-\gamma\widehat E$, $\widehat B_t=-ik\times\widehat E$, with both transverse constraints and the original parities retained.
(b) [Smooth closed PEC cavity] Assume the full Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]) inherited from the smooth spectral and elliptic suppliers. On a bounded smooth cavity $\Omega$, let $X,V,H$ and its smooth orthonormal eigenfields $u_j$, $\lambda_j\ge0$, be exactly [[lem-em-smooth-pec-cavity-spectral-closure]] and [[thm-em-waveguide-spectral-completeness]]. Define the full weak normal-zero divergence-free magnetic space $$M:=\{B\in L^2(\Omega;\mathbb R^3):\int_\Omega B\cdot\nabla\psi=0\text{ for every smooth scalar }\psi\text{ on }\overline\Omega\}.$$ This is the distributional divergence constraint together with weak zero normal flux; for smooth fields it is $\operatorname{div}B=0$, $n\cdot B=0$. For every positive eigenvalue put $w_j=\operatorname{curl}u_j/\sqrt{\lambda_j}$ and put $K_M=\{B\in M:\operatorname{curl}B=0\text{ distributionally}\}$. Then $$M=\overline{\operatorname{span}\{w_j:\lambda_j>0\}}^{L^2}\mathbin{\oplus}K_M.$$ The declared first-order PEC modal energy class is $H\times M$, with weak modal equations $a_j'=v^2\sqrt{\lambda_j}b_j-\gamma a_j$, $b_j'=-\sqrt{\lambda_j}a_j$ for positive modes, $a_j'=-\gamma a_j$ for electric zero modes, and $(B,K_M)'=0$ for every kernel test. Define $D_M$ as the graph-norm completion of finite $w_j$ sums plus $K_M$, with norm squared $\|w\|_2^2+\|\operatorname{curl}w\|_2^2$. The equivalent weak-in-time variational equations are $\langle E_t,\phi\rangle=v^2\langle B,\operatorname{curl}\phi\rangle-\gamma\langle E,\phi\rangle$ for $\phi\in V$, and $\langle B_t,\psi\rangle=-\langle E,\operatorname{curl}\psi\rangle$ for $\psi\in D_M$. They use the actual electric tangential-zero domain $V$ and magnetic normal-zero space $M$, with no claim of maximal-trace equality. For every initial pair in $H\times M$ there is a unique $C(\mathbb R;H\times M)$ solution of that declared realization, which also satisfies the displayed evolution equations distributionally in the interior. Finite electric and paired-magnetic modal sums, with the exact static magnetic kernel component retained, converge uniformly on compact time intervals in energy norm. No equality with every maximal rough tangential-trace realization is asserted.
(c) [Exact energy balance, decay and zero sectors] In both declared classes, for every $t_0<t_1$, $$\mathcal U(t_1)+\sigma\int_{t_0}^{t_1}\|E(t)\|_2^2\,dt=\mathcal U(t_0),\qquad \mathcal U'(t)=-\sigma\|E(t)\|_2^2\le0.$$ For positive smooth-cavity modes $a_j''+\gamma a_j'+v^2\lambda_ja_j=0$. If $\Delta_j=\gamma^2/4-v^2\lambda_j$ is positive, the two roots are $-\gamma/2\pm\sqrt{\Delta_j}$; if negative they are $-\gamma/2\pm i\sqrt{-\Delta_j}$; at zero include $(A+Bt)e^{-\gamma t/2}$. This finite scalar characteristic classification applies to every parameter $\lambda\ge0$ and uses no choice principle; its general zero-eigenvalue associated-wave solutions and the narrower first-order compatibility are stated separately in (d). The full Choice premise belongs to the smooth-cavity spectral realization, not to these finite root formulas. For $\sigma>0$ every positive mode decays, electric zero modes obey $a_j(t)=a_j(0)e^{-\gamma t}$, and magnetic kernel data stay constant. In the smooth class the solution therefore tends in energy norm as $t\to+\infty$ to $(0,P_{K_M}B(0))$. For $\sigma=0$ energy is constant, electric zero modes are static and the original lossless branch is recovered. The zero sectors are retained, not divided by their eigenvalues; no universal decay to zero in the presence of magnetic kernel data is claimed.
(d) [Associated second-order branch] Independently of first-order compatibility, the same spectral basis gives the damped associated electric wave for every $q_0\in V$, $q_1\in H$, with $q\in C(\mathbb R;V)$, $q'\in C(\mathbb R;H)$ and modal convergence uniformly on compact times. Its coefficients satisfy $a_j''+\gamma a_j'+v^2\lambda_ja_j=0$ with arbitrary prescribed initial coefficients. At $\lambda_j=0$ they are $\alpha_j+\beta_j(1-e^{-\gamma t})/\gamma$ when $\gamma>0$, and $\alpha_j+t\beta_j$ when $\gamma=0$. Such arbitrary second-order data need not arise from the first-order Maxwell system: its zero modes additionally satisfy $a_j'=-\gamma a_j$. This broader wave is retained alongside the physical first-order branch. All square roots and real derivatives use [[thm-of-square-roots]], [[thm-sine-and-cosine-derivatives]], [[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]], [[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]] and the real-component exponential formula [[cor-complex-exponential-cartesian-form-modulus-and-eulers-identity]].
(e) [Scope] The conductor occupies the declared cavity interior while its stationary walls obey the independent ideal PEC boundary model. No arbitrary loss tensor, memory law, changing interface, open-end, rough-wall or empirical conclusion is made. Countable Choice ([[def-countable-choice]]) suffices for the rectangular/Fourier branch; the smooth branch retains its actual full Choice premise. Weak energy balance is proved by modal limits and does not assert an unprovided nonlinear weak local Poynting theorem.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The constant isotropic inertial-rest branch of [[post-em-material-closure]] with constants $\varepsilon,\mu>0$, $\sigma\ge0$, relations $D=\varepsilon E$, $H=B/\mu$, $J_{\rm ext}=0$ (the isotropic broadband condition $\varepsilon\mu\ge c^{-2}$ is additional and is not used); the smooth equations of [[pthm-em-macroscopic-maxwell]] with initially zero volume charge; the PEC guide/cavity geometry and weak reflection class of [[def-em-guide-cavity-geometry]]; $v^2:=(\varepsilon\mu)^{-1}$ and $\gamma:=\sigma/\varepsilon$.

[F1] The first-order system and energy: with initially zero volume charge the macroscopic equations give $E_t=v^2\operatorname{curl}B-\gamma E$, $B_t=-\operatorname{curl}E$ and zero divergences, and the quadratic stored energy is $\mathcal U(t)=\tfrac12(\varepsilon\|E(t)\|_2^2+\mu^{-1}\|B(t)\|_2^2)$, consistent with [[pthm-em-restricted-material-energy]] ([[post-em-material-closure]], [[pthm-em-macroscopic-maxwell]], [[pthm-em-restricted-material-energy]]).

[F2] Rectangular energy class: [[lem-em-rectangular-pec-energy-completeness]] states that for a rectangular box $Q=\prod_j(0,L_j)$, in the reflection-parity and distributional-divergence class defined there, the finite separated Fourier modes are dense and the class is complete for the $L^2$ pairing, and every initial datum of the class has a unique $C(\mathbb R;L^2)$ weak solution of the lossless system $\partial_tE=(\varepsilon\mu)^{-1}\operatorname{curl}B$, $\partial_tB=-\operatorname{curl}E$ in the class with constant energy. Its system is written for the wavevector $k_j=\pi n_j/L_j$ and the finite-dimensional Fourier coefficients; the class and completeness are the objects used below, and Countable Choice is assumed as there ([[def-countable-choice]], [[lem-l-two-with-the-integral-pairing-is-a-hilbert-space]]).

[F3] Smooth PEC cavity classes (supplied by the completed in-run items): [[lem-em-smooth-pec-cavity-spectral-closure]] and [[thm-em-waveguide-spectral-completeness]] declare, under the full Axiom of Choice, a bounded $C^\infty$ domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^3$, the graph-norm completion $X$ in $H(\operatorname{curl})\cap H(\operatorname{div})$ of the smooth fields on the closure with $n\times u=0$, the subspace $V=\{u\in X:\operatorname{div}u=0\}$ and $H=\overline V^{\,L^2}$, and supply a complete orthonormal $L^2$ eigenbasis $\{u_j\}$ of $H$ with nonnegative finite-multiplicity eigenvalues $\lambda_j$, the distributional equation $\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}u_j=\lambda_ju_j$ with PEC trace, the full-$X$ variational identity $\int_\Omega\operatorname{curl}u_j\cdot\operatorname{curl}v=\lambda_j\int_\Omega u_j\cdot v$ for every $v\in X$, and $C^\infty$ eigenfields on the closure. Their statements are the sole premises taken from them, and the proof steps below name the supplier consumed at each use.

[F4] Elliptic solvability: [[thm-em-elliptic-boundary-existence]] (a) supplies, under the Axiom of Choice and Countable Choice, for a bounded $C^\infty$ domain and smooth data the unique smooth solution of the Dirichlet problem $-\operatorname{div}(\varepsilon\nabla u)=\rho$, $u|_{\partial\Omega}=g$.

[F5] Hilbert-space tools: orthogonal decomposition by a closed subspace and Fourier expansion ([[thm-orthogonal-decomposition-by-a-closed-subspace]], [[thm-hilbert-space-fourier-expansion]]); Cauchy–Schwarz and completeness of the complex and real $L^2$ pairings ([[cor-cauchy-schwarz-inequality-for-l-two]], [[lem-complex-lp-completeness-density-and-inner-product]]).

[F6] Distributional and one-variable tools: distributional derivative and its compatibility with $C^1$ regular distributions, with differentiation commuting with the embedding ([[def-distributional-derivative]], [[thm-distributional-differentiation-is-continuous-and-commutes]]); a distribution with zero derivative on a connected open set is constant ([[thm-a-distribution-with-zero-derivatives-on-a-connected-open-set-is-constant]]); the oriented primitive of a continuous function is $C^1$ with the given derivative and continuous functions on compact intervals are Riemann integrable ([[thm-ftc-first-part]], [[thm-ftc-second-part]], [[thm-continuous-implies-integrable]]); dominated convergence for passing integrals of uniformly convergent families ([[thm-dominated-convergence]]).

[F7] ODE tools: globally Lipschitz autonomous systems on a finite-dimensional real space have unique global solutions ([[cor-globally-lipschitz-odes-have-global-solutions]]); the scalar characteristic alternatives are the elementary algebra of $a''+\gamma a'+v^2\lambda a=0$ with the real square roots of [[thm-of-square-roots]], the real exponential limits ([[thm-exponential-limits-and-range]], [[thm-exponential-beats-every-polynomial]]) and the sine/cosine derivative rules with the real cartesian form of the complex exponential ([[thm-sine-and-cosine-derivatives]], [[cor-complex-exponential-cartesian-form-modulus-and-eulers-identity]]); for a nonnegative real $\lambda$ the positive square root $\sqrt\lambda$ is the one of [[thm-of-square-roots]].

[F8] Vector-calculus identities: $\operatorname{div}(a\times b)=b\cdot\operatorname{curl}a-a\cdot\operatorname{curl}b$, the curl of a gradient vanishes, the divergence theorem on bounded $C^1$ domains, the cross product is bilinear, alternating and orthogonal, and the Euclidean inner product is the coordinate dot product ([[lem-the-divergence-and-curl-of-a-cross-product]], [[thm-the-curl-of-a-gradient-vanishes]], [[thm-divergence-theorem-for-bounded-c-one-euclidean-domains]], [[lem-cross-product-is-bilinear-alternating-and-orthogonal]], [[def-cross-product-in-r3]], [[def-euclidean-inner-product]], [[def-divergence-and-curl-of-a-c1-vector-field]]).

Complex modulus and conjugation laws are [[lem-complex-conjugation-and-modulus-laws]].



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct: solve the finite-dimensional damped modal systems, sum the exact energy identities, pass finite sums to the energy-norm limit, classify the scalar characteristics, and identify the limiting zero sectors.

1.1 [Rectangular damped modal system] Assume Countable Choice. Fix the box $Q$ and the reflection class of [F2], and let $(E,B)$ be a $C(\mathbb R;L^2)$ distributional solution of the damped first-order system of [F1] in that class. The finite separated Fourier modes are dense in the class by [F2], and for a wavevector $k\in(\pi/L_1)\mathbb Z\times(\pi/L_2)\mathbb Z\times(\pi/L_3)\mathbb Z$ the coefficient system of the damped system reads $\widehat E_t=iv^2\,k\times\widehat B-\gamma\widehat E$, $\widehat B_t=-ik\times\widehat E$: this is [F2]'s recorded coefficient system with the additional scalar term $-\gamma\widehat E$ of [F1], and the algebraic constraint $k\cdot(k\times w)=0$ preserves $k\cdot\widehat E=k\cdot\widehat B=0$, while the finite Fourier modes carry the parities of the class because the reflection rules are diagonal in the separated basis. [F1, F2]

1.2 [Smooth PEC cavity spaces and the magnetic decomposition] Assume the full Axiom of Choice as in [F3], let $\Omega$ be a bounded $C^\infty$ domain and let $X,V,H$, $\{u_j\}$, $\lambda_j\ge0$ be the classes and eigenbasis of [F3]. Put $M:=\{B\in L^2(\Omega;\mathbb R^3):\int_\Omega B\cdot\nabla\psi=0\text{ for every smooth scalar }\psi\text{ on }\overline\Omega\}$, the weak normal-zero divergence-free space, and $K_M:=\{B\in M:\operatorname{curl}B=0\text{ distributionally}\}$. For each positive eigenvalue put $w_j:=\operatorname{curl}u_j/\sqrt{\lambda_j}$. Then the $w_j$ are orthonormal, $w_j\in M$, and $M=\overline{\operatorname{span}\{w_j:\lambda_j>0\}}^{L^2}\oplus K_M$. Orthonormality: the full-$X$ identity of [F3] with the test $v=u_l\in V\subseteq X$ gives $(\operatorname{curl}u_j,\operatorname{curl}u_l)=\lambda_j(u_j,u_l)=\lambda_j\delta_{jl}$, so $(w_j,w_l)=\delta_{jl}$. Membership and the forward inclusion: for a smooth scalar $\psi$, [F8] gives $\operatorname{div}(u_j\times\nabla\psi)=\nabla\psi\cdot\operatorname{curl}u_j-u_j\cdot\operatorname{curl}\nabla\psi=\nabla\psi\cdot\operatorname{curl}u_j$, and its integral over $\Omega$ is the boundary integral $\int_{\partial\Omega}(n\times u_j)\cdot\nabla\psi=0$ because $u_j$ is smooth on the closure with $n\times u_j=0$ [F3]; hence $w_j\in M$, and the closed span of the $w_j$ lies in $M$. For an element $u\in V$, the $V$-norm identity of [F3] with $\|u_j\|_V^2=1+\lambda_j$ gives the expansion $u=\sum_j(u,u_j)u_j$ in $L^2$, and applying the bounded operator $\operatorname{curl}$ on $V$ gives $\operatorname{curl}u=\sum_j(u,u_j)\sqrt{\lambda_j}w_j$ in $L^2$; hence $\overline{\operatorname{curl}V}=\overline{\operatorname{span}\{w_j\}}$, and both lie in the closed subspace $M$. Decomposition: the orthogonal decomposition theorem [F5] applied to the closed subspace $\overline{\operatorname{span}\{w_j\}}$ of $M$ writes $B\in M$ as the sum of its projections onto that span and onto its orthogonal complement inside $M$; it remains to identify the complement with $K_M$. If $B\in M$ is orthogonal to every $\operatorname{curl}v$ with $v\in V$ and $\varphi$ is smooth and compactly supported in $\Omega$, then the smooth Dirichlet solver [F4] with coefficient identity, zero boundary trace and source $-\operatorname{div}\varphi$ supplies a smooth $f$ on the closure with $\Delta f=\operatorname{div}\varphi$; then $\varphi-\nabla f$ is smooth on the closure with $\operatorname{div}(\varphi-\nabla f)=0$ and with vanishing tangential trace on $\partial\Omega$ (the first term is compactly supported, and $f|_{\partial\Omega}=0$ forces the tangential derivatives of $f$, hence $n\times\nabla f$, to vanish there), so $\varphi-\nabla f$ lies in $V$; since $\operatorname{curl}(\varphi-\nabla f)=\operatorname{curl}\varphi$ by [F8], we obtain $(B,\operatorname{curl}\varphi)=0$; as $\varphi$ is an arbitrary compactly supported smooth test, this is exactly the distributional statement $\operatorname{curl}B=0$, i.e. $B\in K_M$. Conversely, if $B\in K_M$ then for each eigenfield $u_j$ the collar cutoff of [F9] yields compactly supported smooth approximants $\eta_\delta u_j$ with $\eta_\delta u_j\to u_j$ and $\operatorname{curl}(\eta_\delta u_j)\to\operatorname{curl}u_j$ in $L^2$ (the boundary layer has width $2\delta$ and $n\times u_j=0$ makes the boundary term vanish in the limit), so the distributional pairing $\int B\cdot\operatorname{curl}(\eta_\delta u_j)=0$ passes to the limit and gives $(B,\operatorname{curl}u_j)=0$, hence $(B,w_j)=0$; by continuity of the pairing and closedness of the span, $B$ is orthogonal to $\overline{\operatorname{span}\{w_j\}}$, which is the claimed complement and finishes the decomposition. [F3, F4, F5, F8, F9]

1.3 [Scalar characteristic classification] For parameters $\gamma\ge0$, $v>0$, $\lambda\ge0$, the solutions of $a''+\gamma a'+v^2\lambda a=0$ are classified by $\Delta:=\gamma^2/4-v^2\lambda$: if $\Delta>0$ the characteristic roots are $-\gamma/2\pm\sqrt\Delta$ and the general solution is $Ae^{(-\gamma/2+\sqrt\Delta)t}+Be^{(-\gamma/2-\sqrt\Delta)t}$; if $\Delta<0$ the roots are $-\gamma/2\pm i\sqrt{-\Delta}$ and the general solution is $e^{-\gamma t/2}(A\cos(\sqrt{-\Delta}t)+B\sin(\sqrt{-\Delta}t))$; if $\Delta=0$ it is $(A+Bt)e^{-\gamma t/2}$; and if $\lambda=0$ the solutions are $A+Be^{-\gamma t}$ for $\gamma>0$ and $A+Bt$ for $\gamma=0$. These are finite real computations with the square roots of [F7] and the real exponential and trigonometric solutions certified by [F7]; no choice principle is used. [F7]

2.1 [Rectangular class: global solutions, energy and convergence] For every initial pair in the rectangular class of [F2] the damped system of [F1] has a unique $C(\mathbb R;L^2)$ distributional solution in the class, finite Fourier mode sums converge in energy norm uniformly on compact time intervals and are dense at every time, and the coefficient equations of step 1.1 hold for every wavevector. Indeed, for each $k$ the right-hand side of the finite-dimensional system of step 1.1 is globally state-Lipschitz (it is affine), so [F7] gives a unique global solution through the initial coefficients; differentiate the per-mode energy $\varepsilon|\widehat E|^2+\mu^{-1}|\widehat B|^2$. With $C:=\overline{\widehat E}\cdot(k\times\widehat B)$ and $\overline{\widehat B}\cdot(k\times\widehat E)=-\overline C$, the curl contribution is $(2/\mu)\operatorname{Re}(iC+i\overline C)=0$ by the scalar triple-product identity of [F8] and complex conjugation [F9]; this gives $\frac{d}{dt}\bigl(\varepsilon|\widehat E|^2+\mu^{-1}|\widehat B|^2\bigr)=-2\sigma|\widehat E|^2\le0$, so each modal energy is nonincreasing forward and bounded by $e^{2\gamma T}$ times its initial value on $[-T,T]$ (multiply by $e^{2\gamma t}$ and differentiate); Parseval for the complete class [F2] therefore controls the energy-norm of every omitted tail uniformly on compact times. Finite solutions converge in $C_tL^2$ to a limit in the class, and the linear distributional equations pass to the limit against compactly supported smooth periodic tests by Cauchy–Schwarz [F5] on a compact time support, exactly as in the lossless argument of [F2]; the constraints $k\cdot\widehat E=k\cdot\widehat B=0$ and the parities are preserved by step 1.1, so the limit lies in the same class. Uniqueness: a $C_tL^2$ weak solution has continuous coefficients whose distributional derivatives equal the displayed continuous right-hand sides; for each coefficient equation, subtract the oriented primitive of the continuous right-hand side, which is $C^1$ with that derivative by [F6], use the $C^1$ regular-distribution compatibility of [F6] to identify its distributional derivative, conclude that the difference has zero distributional derivative, hence is constant by [F6], and match the initial value; global uniqueness of the finite system identifies the coefficients, and completeness of the Fourier class identifies the fields. [step 1.1, F2, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9]

3.1 [Smooth PEC cavity: modal system, variational equations and uniqueness] Assume the full Axiom of Choice as in [F3]. Let $H\times M$ be the energy class of steps 1.2, with $M=\overline{\operatorname{span}\{w_j\}}\oplus K_M$. For coefficients $a_j=(\cdot,u_j)$, $b_j=(\cdot,w_j)$ declare the first-order modal system $a_j'=v^2\sqrt{\lambda_j}\,b_j-\gamma a_j$, $b_j'=-\sqrt{\lambda_j}\,a_j$ for $\lambda_j>0$, and $a_j'=-\gamma a_j$ for the electric zero modes, and declare the magnetic kernel data constant, $(B,K_M)'=0$ for every kernel test. Endow the paired-magnetic space with the completion $D_M$ of finite $w_j$-sums plus $K_M$ for the norm $\|w\|_{D_M}^2:=\|w\|_2^2+\|\operatorname{curl}w\|_2^2$, and consider the weak variational equations $\langle E_t,\phi\rangle=v^2\langle B,\operatorname{curl}\phi\rangle-\gamma\langle E,\phi\rangle$ for $\phi\in V$ and $\langle B_t,\psi\rangle=-\langle E,\operatorname{curl}\psi\rangle$ for $\psi\in D_M$.
