## Statement

Assume AC and let k be a characteristic-zero field. Let the small augmented connective derived k-algebras and their space-valued formal moduli problems have the specified mapping-space, basepoint and elementary-excision conventions. Let the ambient CE adjunction be the one with UNBOUNDED augmented commutative DG algebras. Writing D for its right adjoint, the functor
$$\Psi(\mathfrak g)(A)=\operatorname{Map}_{\mathrm{Lie}_k}(D(A),\mathfrak g)$$
is an equivalence from the infinity-category of cohomological DG Lie algebras in all integer degrees, localized at quasi-isomorphisms, to the infinity-category of those formal moduli problems. Its inverse is the enriched positive-free-test reconstruction F maps to g_F, and its underlying complex is canonically T_F[-1], with K[r]^j=K^{j+r}. The equivalence retains natural-transformation mapping spaces and all higher homotopies; it assumes no preservation of arbitrary infinite realizations or limits by a formal moduli problem.

## Proof

**Given:** F1–F3. All restrictions of functors and every natural transformation below use the actual derived mapping spaces, not their homotopy categories.

1.1 Let Theta have objects FreeLie(V), where V is a finite-dimensional complex in strictly positive cohomological degrees, including finite positive sphere and disk complexes. Its morphisms are the ACTUAL derived Lie mapping spaces. For such V the bounded-positive CE calculation in F1 gives a natural equivalence `CE(FreeLie(V)) ≃ k⊕V^vee[−1].` The right side is a small connective square-zero algebra. For this recognition component the precise algebra-side interface is the enriched finite-free comparison `Map_Lie(FreeLie(V),FreeLie(W)); ≃ Map_CAlg^aug(CE(FreeLie(W)),CE(FreeLie(V))).` Only this finite-free comparison is used at first; it is the CE/cobar unit calculation on bounded-positive free objects, not the full formal-moduli equivalence to be proved. The ambient CE target must remain unbounded for a general Lie algebra g. For example g=k in degree0 has a CE generator in degree+1. No assertion that CE(g) is a connective small test is used. If F is a formal moduli problem, restrict it along this enriched test functor and write `X(FreeLie(V))=F(CE(FreeLie(V))).` This is a functor Theta^op→Spaces, not an assignment on its homotopy category. The pullback axiom gives X(FreeLie(V⊕W))≃X(FreeLie(V))×X(FreeLie(W)), since the corresponding square-zero algebras form the pullback over k. The chosen equivalence is the actual pullback comparison and therefore is coherent for every finite direct sum, permutation and linear map. Put E_n=k⊕k[n], n>=0, and X_m=F(E_(m−1)) for m>=1. The actual square-zero pullback `k×^h_(k⊕M[1]) k ≃ k⊕M` has the required surjection on pi0 when M is connective. It gives X_m≃Omega X_(m+1), naturally with its higher comparisons. The scalar and sum maps of square-zero complexes supply the k-module structure on this spectrum. Thus its desuspension is the complex g=T_F[−1], and X_m=Omega-infinity(g[m]) in the stated cochain convention. [F1, F2, F3, construct]

2.1 The k-module structure is constructed from the ENTIRE derived finite square-zero complex category, not merely a scalar action on homotopy groups. Shift any fixed finite diagram sufficiently far into degrees whose square-zero ideals have zero pi0. Every relevant linear fiber square then has pi0=k on its vertices, so the actual excision axiom applies to both maps. Stabilize these finite exact diagrams using the loop identities just constructed; recover the degree-zero ideal test by those identities as well. Explicitly, for an arbitrary finite k-complex V choose r so large that V[−r] has degrees at least two, and define the stabilized contravariant value as Omega^r X(FreeLie(V[−r])). The disk loop comparisons identify all larger choices coherently. Shift any finite cofiber diagram the same way; the CE square-zero diagram has pi0=k at every vertex, so excision and the fact that loops preserve limits show that this extended functor carries finite colimits of finite k-complexes to homotopy limits. There is a direct representing-complex construction. Let G be the small category of finite k-complexes. The category of elements of this exact contravariant functor is filtered: a finite diagram of elements has its cone at the finite colimit of its underlying complexes, by the limit identity, including its specified higher simplices. Form its filtered colimit g in the derived category of unbounded k-complexes. Finite k-complexes are compact, as is checked on their finite free terms and cones, so Map(V,g) is that filtered colimit of representables and is the prescribed exact functor. This constructs the actual k-complex and its coherent scalar action, rather than only an action on its homotopy groups. Its values on the positive spheres are X_m, hence its associated tangent spectrum is g[1] and g=T_F[−1]. Thus the later multiadditive operations are maps of k-module spectra, not only multilinear maps of graded homotopy groups. These positive tests detect ALL degrees. A positive free generator of degree m has `pi_i Map_Lie(FreeLie(k[−m]),g)=H^(m−i)(g), i>=0.` Given any integer j, choose m>=max(1,j) and i=m−j. Consequently an equivalence on every positive test detects every cohomology group. This is not a claim that arbitrary g is a filtered colimit of positive Lie algebras. Nor are these tests generally sifted-projective in the unbounded category: Omega-infinity need not preserve a geometric realization whose unbounded shifts create a new visible homotopy group. The entire spectrum compatibility, including disks and derived mapping spaces, is used below. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For positive m,n the genuine Lie map `FreeLie(k[−m−n])→FreeLie(k[−m]⊕k[−n]), z↦[x,y]` induces beta_(m,n):X_m×X_n→X_(m+n). It vanishes when either input is zero. The free identities [x+x',y]=[x,y]+[x',y] and [lambda x,y]=lambda[x,y] show additivity and k-linearity in each input. These are coherent identities, not only identities on pi0: factor the word maps through the finite direct-sum test and apply the actual functor X to those diagrams. The same construction with any number of summands supplies the associativity, symmetry and zero coherences of that additivity. To prove that this operation is a pairing of the WHOLE spectra, use the positive disk D_m with generators x of degree m, dx of degree m+1, and d(x)=dx. It is contractible, so its free Lie algebra is weakly equivalent to0 and its CE test to k. There is an actual map of free DG Lie algebras `FreeLie(D_(m+n))→FreeLie(D_m⊕k[−n]),; z↦[x,y], dz↦[dx,y].` Both the differential and the boundary-sphere inclusion commute literally. Applying CE and F therefore gives a morphism of the square-zero homotopy-fiber diagrams which define X_m≃Omega X_(m+1). It identifies looping beta_(m+1,n) in its first input with beta_(m,n); the analogous disk in the second input does the same there. Using both disks gives `d[x,y]=[dx,y]+(−1)^m[x,dy],` so the two loop comparisons and their interchange have exactly the Koszul sign. The comparison is not inferred merely from the existence of delooping spaces. It comes from these actual DG maps between the tests. Products of disks supply every higher interchange: faces set one disk parameter to its boundary sphere, and degeneracies set it to the unit/zero map. The identities are the strict free DG Lie derivation identities before applying the functor. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 More generally, each multilinear Lie word has the same extension when any input sphere is replaced by a positive disk: extend it as a derivation in that variable. All its iterated disk faces agree. Hence its operations are compatible with all structure maps of the spectrum, not just with a fixed level m. Here is the actual k-module map without an additional prespectrum-linearity premise. For finite positive complexes V1,...,Vr a multilinear Lie word gives the DG map FreeLie(V1 tensor ... tensor Vr)→FreeLie(V1⊕...⊕Vr), by sending a pure tensor to that word. Applying X and its finite-product comparison gives the natural operation on their values with output the value on V1 tensor ... tensor Vr. The disk comparisons extend this operation through the stabilized finite-complex functor in step 2.1. For each tuple of elements (Vi,xi) in its filtered category of elements, the output is an actual map V1 tensor ... tensor Vr→g. These maps form a coherent cone, since every word map was natural on all the derived linear maps. Tensor of complexes preserves these filtered colimits in each variable, so the cone induces the map g tensor_k^L ... tensor_k^L g→g. Scalar balance and the Koszul differential are already identities in the displayed DG word map. Thus the operation is defined on the actual represented k-complex with all its coherences. No lower or upper bound on g was inserted in this construction. [F1, F2, F3, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 The preceding operations must retain all substitution homotopies. Here is an explicit resolution that does so. Resolve the Lie operad by its augmented simplicial free-operad bar. In simplicial degree q its elements are leveled rooted trees with q successive layers of substitutions of Lie words. An inner face composes two adjacent layers; an outer face evaluates the outer or inner substitution; a degeneracy inserts the identity substitution. The augmentation forgets the levels. In the augmented bar, inserting the free-operad unit is an extra degeneracy, so the alternating normalized differential contracts its augmentation kernel. Thus its realization O→Lie is an aritywise equivalence. The free tree filtration makes O cofibrant, using exact symmetric-group coinvariants in characteristic zero; this is the same explicit free-cell operad comparison used by F1's rectification calculation. A leveled tree is also a string of morphisms of positive free Lie tests, after assigning each leaf a sufficiently large positive degree and the output their sum. Apply the actual infinity-functor X to this string. Its composition simplices give the operation with precisely the homotopies for collapsing or inserting a layer. Stabilize the multilinear operations using the disk construction of the word-operation construction. Every face, degeneracy and symmetric permutation commutes with this stabilization, because it was the same free DG word substitution before applying X. This constructs an operad map `O → End_g` in complexes, including its differential and all higher coherences. It does not deduce an L-infinity action from a list of binary identities on homotopy groups. [F1, F2, F3, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 For clarity, linearity in an operad coefficient is also part of this construction. The operation for w1+w2 factors through the sum map from two output generators, followed by the maps sending those generators to w1,w2. Applying the product comparison for the two-generator test gives the sum of the two operations. Scalar multiplication is identical. Thus the passage from the spaces of free words to the k-linear Lie operad is justified. Repeated variables are obtained by diagonals of the finite product X(V), and multilinear words give arbitrary words by these diagonals. A fixed positive output degree sees only finitely many words in the finite positive input generators, so no formal infinite Lie-series or convergence hypothesis is introduced. The CE-context characteristic-zero free-cell operad comparison now rectifies the O-algebra to an actual cohomological DG Lie algebra g_F, with a quasi-isomorphism on the underlying complexes. Concretely extend scalars along O→Lie using its simplicial free-algebra bar and take a cofibrant replacement first. Filter the unit comparison by its rooted-tree cells. Each associated term is the aritywise O→Lie equivalence tensored with input complexes and divided by a finite symmetric group. Tensor products over the field preserve quasi-isomorphisms, and coinvariants are exact by averaging. At a limit take the exact filtered colimit of its finite words. This proves the unit comparison an equivalence, not merely the existence of a new strict bracket with the same cohomology. Consequently the full homotopy-coherent positive-test action is unchanged by this rectification. [F1, F2, F3, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 For V a finite sum of positive spheres, the product comparison identifies X(FreeLie(V)) with the product of the corresponding X_m. The same product is Map_Lie(FreeLie(V),g_F), by the free adjunction and the underlying complex comparison just constructed. Finite positive complexes are obtained by adjoining their disk differentials; the actual fiber comparisons identify the same products with their differential compatibility, so the identification extends to them. It is natural for all the DERIVED morphisms in Theta. A positive free source is cofibrant, and its mapping space into a DG Lie target is the Dold–Kan space of the nonpositive truncation of Hom_k(V,U target), by the enriched free/forgetful adjunction. Thus a simplex of Map(FreeLie(V),FreeLie(W)) is a finite combination of homogeneous Lie words with their disk/boundary data. Higher simplices are not discarded. The operations of the word-operation and operadic constructions were defined on exactly these words, disk maps and composition simplices. Any repeated input is handled by a product diagonal, and any sum by the output sum map as checked above. Strings of such derived morphisms become leveled trees, whose simplices are exactly those retained by O's bar resolution. Therefore the same formulas provide a homotopy-coherent natural equivalence `X(FreeLie(V)) ≃ Map_Lie(FreeLie(V),g_F), V in Theta.` This is stronger than agreement on each pi_i or on the unenriched category of graded free Lie words. The enriched resolution gives the natural transformation itself and its coherences. Likewise a natural transformation F→F' gives, on the same leveled trees and disks, an O-algebra map before rectification. Rectification transports it to a derived DG Lie map. Homotopies between natural transformations are retained by the same parameter simplices. Hence this is a construction of functors on the relevant infinity-categories, not only a reconstruction on objects. [F1, F2, F3, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 For the reverse check start with any, possibly unbounded, DG Lie algebra g and take X(V)=Map(FreeLie(V),g). Its word maps are evaluation of those words in g, so its O-action is the restriction of the given Lie action. The operadic unit comparison then identifies the reconstructed g_X with g, including its derived mapping spaces. The degree detection formula in the spectrum-and-module construction shows that this argument has not lost the negative-degree part of g. [F1, F2, F3, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 F1 constructs the adjunction in UNBOUNDED augmented CDGAs, its small-base counit, its enriched positive-free test comparison, D(k)=0, D's elementary-pullback-to-pushout comparisons, and finite elementary generation of every specified small base. Only these concrete interfaces are used here; no old abstract A1 recognition package is assumed. For any cohomological DG Lie algebra g in ALL integer degrees define `Psi(g)(A)=Map_Lie(D(A),g), A small.` It takes k to a contractible space. The CE-context pullback-to-pushout comparison makes it satisfy the formal-moduli excision axiom. Thus it is an actual formal moduli problem. Its restriction to Theta is the enriched restricted Yoneda functor V↦Map_Lie(FreeLie(V),g), by the finite-positive unit and the adjunction. the positive-free enriched reconstruction therefore apply to it and reconstruct g, naturally with all derived mapping spaces. For every small A let h_A(B)=Map_CAlg^aug(A,B), where B ranges over the specified small bases. This is also a formal moduli problem: mapping out of A preserves the actual homotopy limits, and its value at the terminal augmented algebra k is contractible. The enriched CE ADJUNCTION gives, without any mixed-cell density assertion, `h_A(CE(FreeLie(V))); = Map_CAlg^aug(A,CE(FreeLie(V))); = Map_Lie(FreeLie(V),D(A)).` Hence the restriction h_A|Theta is EXACTLY restricted Yoneda of D(A), on objects, derived arrows and all their higher simplices. The reverse reconstruction check of the enriched test comparison applies to arbitrary D(A) and identifies R(h_A|Theta) with D(A) naturally in A. Here R denotes the actual reconstruction infinity-functor of the positive-free enriched reconstruction. Now an element x of F(A) is, by the space-valued Yoneda equivalence, the actual natural transformation x:h_A→F. This equivalence retains paths between elements and higher simplices. Restrict that natural transformation to Theta and apply the reconstruction FUNCTOR R. This constructs `theta_F(A):F(A)→Map_Lie(D(A),g_F),; x↦R(x|Theta), g_F=R(F|Theta).` [F1, F2, F3, step 8.1, algebra]

10.1 Every operation in this formula is a functor on the actual mapping spaces. Consequently theta_F is natural in all small algebra maps, in F, and in every homotopy. In particular it already commutes with an arbitrary attaching arrow kappa:A0→E, even when that arrow corresponds to a mixed Lie word in earlier cell generators. No assertion that the arrow can be resolved by finitely many free words is required. On a square-zero test A=CE(FreeLie(V)), the formula is the evaluation comparison of the enriched test comparison: a transformation out of its restricted representable is determined by its value on the identity, and reconstructing it gives the same generator tuple in Map_Lie(FreeLie(V),g_F). Thus theta_F(A) is an equivalence on all those enriched tests. It is an equivalence at k because both values are contractible. Use F1's finite elementary filtration of any small A. At a new cell A=A0×^h_E k, theta_F is a map of the two actual homotopy-pullback squares because its attaching-arrow naturality was constructed by Yoneda. It is already an equivalence at A0 by induction, at E by the test calculation, and at k. Hence it is an equivalence at A. Only finitely many elementary pullbacks are used. F is not presumed to preserve an arbitrary geometric realization, infinite limit, or free-cell resolution. The finite induction proves theta_F an equivalence on EVERY specified small base, naturally. Finally R(Psi(g)|Theta)≃g is the reverse reconstruction comparison of the enriched test comparison, while theta gives F≃Psi(g_F). Both are natural equivalences of infinity-functors; their construction retained transformations, paths and higher simplices. Thus R and Psi give the full claimed equivalence between formal moduli problems and DG Lie algebras localized at quasi-isomorphisms, not only a bijection on equivalence classes. The spectrum construction in the spectrum-and-module construction fixes the shift g_F=T_F[−1], with the stated cochain convention. Neither Lie degrees nor the CE ambient algebra category have been truncated. [F1, F2, F3, step 9.1, algebra] ∎
