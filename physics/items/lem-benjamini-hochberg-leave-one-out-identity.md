## Statement

Let $m\ge1$, $q\in(0,1)$, and let $P_1,\ldots,P_m$ be measurable
$[0,1]$-valued functions on one measurable space. Apply the algorithm in
[[alg-benjamini-hochberg-step-up-procedure]]. For each $i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$
and vector $x_{-i}\in[0,1]^{m-1}$, order the remaining values as
$x_{-i,(1)}\le\cdots\le x_{-i,(m-1)}$ and define

$$s_i(x_{-i}):=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{k\in\{1,\ldots,m-1\}:x_{-i,(k)}\le(k+1)q/m\}\bigr).$$

When $m=1$, set $s_i=0$. Let $S_i=s_i(P_{-i})$, let $R$ be the number of
rejections, and let $I_i$ be the indicator that hypothesis $i$ is rejected.
Then $S_i$ is measurable with respect to the sigma-algebra generated by the
other coordinates and, pointwise,

$$\frac{I_i}{R\vee1}=\frac{\mathbf 1\{P_i\le(S_i+1)q/m\}}{S_i+1}.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $m\ge1$, $q\in(0,1)$, measurable functions $P_i:\Omega\to[0,1]$,
and the step-up algorithm specified in the Statement.

[F1] [[alg-benjamini-hochberg-step-up-procedure]]: order the p-values and let
$R$ be the largest rank $k$ satisfying $p_{(k)}\le kq/m$; reject ranks
$1,\ldots,R$, taking $R=0$ when no rank qualifies.

[F2] [[def-measurable-function-between-measurable-spaces]]: a function is
measurable when the preimage of every measurable codomain set is measurable.

[F3] [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]: the Borel sigma-algebra is generated by the
open sets; in particular, closed half-lines and their finite intersections
and unions are Borel.

## Proof

1.1 For every $k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$ and real $a$, $\{P_{(k)}\le a\}=\bigcup_{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\},\,|J|=k}\bigcap_{j\in J}\{P_j\le a\}$, since the $k$th smallest value is at most $a$ exactly when at least $k$ coordinates are at most $a$; the half-line is Borel by [F3], so [F2] makes this finite union of measurable intersections measurable, and the same formula works after deleting any coordinate. [given, F2, F3, construct]

2.1 Each $A_k=\{P_{(k)}\le kq/m\}$ is measurable by step 1.1; its indicator and the simple function $k\mathbf 1_{A_k}$ are measurable by [F2], and the finite maximum of these functions and $0$ is measurable because its sublevel sets are finite intersections. The same construction with $P_{-i,(k)}$ and thresholds $(k+1)q/m$ shows each level set of $S_i$ lies in the sigma-algebra generated by the remaining coordinates. [F2, step 1.1]

3.1 If $R=0$, no $P_i$ is zero because then $P_{(1)}=0\le q/m$, so no value is at most $Rq/m=0$. If $0<R<m$, maximality gives $P_{(R)}\le Rq/m$ and $P_{(R+1)}>(R+1)q/m>Rq/m$, so exactly the first $R$ ranks have values at most $Rq/m$; if $R=m$, all values are at most $P_{(m)}\le q=mq/m$. Thus the rejected indices are exactly $\{i:P_i\le Rq/m\}$. [F1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Fix $i$ and write $s=S_i$. If $s\ge1$, then $P_{-i,(s)}\le(s+1)q/m$, and if $s=0$ there are no other values to check. When $P_i\le(s+1)q/m$, at least $s+1$ full-family values meet that threshold, so $R\ge s+1$. If $R\ge s+2$, the qualifying rank $R$ has $P_{(R)}\le Rq/m$; deleting $P_i$ leaves at least $R-1$ other values at most $Rq/m$, making $R-1\ge s+1$ qualify in the definition of $S_i$, a contradiction. Hence $R=s+1$, and step 3.1 shows that $i$ is rejected. [given, F1, step 3.1, choose]

4.2 If $P_i>(s+1)q/m$ but $i$ is rejected, step 3.1 gives $P_i\le Rq/m$; write $r=R\ge1$. At least $r-1$ other values are at most $rq/m$, so $r-1$ qualifies in the definition of $S_i$ (with $0$ automatically qualifying when $r=1$). Thus $r-1\le s$, whence $P_i\le rq/m\le(s+1)q/m$, a contradiction; therefore $i$ is not rejected. [F1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Step 4.1 shows that $P_i\le(s+1)q/m$ implies $I_i=1$ and $R=s+1$, while step 4.2 shows that otherwise $I_i=0$; substituting these two cases gives the stated pointwise identity. [step 4.1, step 4.2, algebra] ∎
