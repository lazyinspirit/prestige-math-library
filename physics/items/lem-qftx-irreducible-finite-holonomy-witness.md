## Statement

Assume ZF together with the Axiom of Choice. Let $M$ be $S^3$ or $S^4$ with its compact, connected, oriented, boundaryless smooth structure and a smooth Riemannian metric. Let $N\ge2$, let $P\to M$ be a smooth principal right $SU(N)$-bundle, choose a smooth reference connection $A_0$, and fix a basepoint $m\in M$ and a point $p_m\in P_m$ to frame its fiber. Put $\mathcal A_\infty=A_0+\Omega^1(M;\operatorname{ad}P)$ with the Fréchet topology generated by suprema of all derivatives of section coefficients in a fixed finite smooth bundle atlas. A based piecewise-smooth loop is a continuous map $\ell:[0,1]\to M$ that is smooth on finitely many subintervals and satisfies $\ell(0)=\ell(1)=m$. For a smooth connection $A$ and such a loop, parallel transport from $p_m$ ends at $p_mg_\ell$ for a unique $g_\ell\in SU(N)$; let $\operatorname{Hol}_A\subseteq SU(N)$ be the subgroup generated by these elements. The standard representation is $g\cdot v=gv$ on $\mathbb C^N$. Call $A$ irreducible when there is no complex-linear subspace $V$ with $0\subsetneq V\subsetneq\mathbb C^N$ and $gV=V$ for every $g\in\operatorname{Hol}_A$, and write $\mathcal A_\infty^*$ for this locus. Measure $C^1$ closeness by coefficient and first-derivative suprema in the same fixed finite bundle atlas. For every $A\in\mathcal A_\infty^*$, a finite tuple of based piecewise-smooth loops has holonomies with no common nonzero proper invariant subspace; the same tuple continues to witness irreducibility on a $C^1$ neighborhood of $A$, so $\mathcal A_\infty^*$ is open in the $C^\infty$ topology. Irreducibility here does not assert $\operatorname{Hol}_A=SU(N)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** ZF and the Axiom of Choice; a compact oriented smooth sphere $M=S^3$ or $S^4$; $N\ge2$; a smooth principal $SU(N)$-bundle $P$; a smooth connection $A$; and the finite-atlas $C^\infty$ topology on $\mathcal A_\infty$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice means every family of nonempty sets has a choice function ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F1] The batch-56 configuration definition fixes $\mathcal A_\infty=A_0+\Omega^1(M;\operatorname{ad}P)$ with the finite-atlas $C^\infty$ section topology and defines irreducibility by the based holonomy action on $\mathbb C^N$. The complete definition was inspected and its current Step 1 record is ready; this proof uses exactly that interface ([[def-qftx-smooth-sobolev-gauge-configuration]]).

[F2] The union $\mathscr G_N$ of the projection models for all proper nonzero complex subspaces of $\mathbb C^N$ is compact ([[lem-qftx-complex-grassmannian-compactness]]).

[F3] A continuous real-valued function on a nonempty compact space attains its minimum ([[thm-compactness-under-continuous-maps]], claim 2).

[F4] For a continuous coefficient $C:[a,b]\to M_n(\mathbb R)$ on a compact interval, the real matrix IVP $Y'=C(t)Y$ with prescribed initial matrix has a unique solution on the whole interval ([[lem-linear-matrix-odes-have-unique-global-solutions-on-a-given-interval]]). For complex transport matrices we identify $M_N(\mathbb C)$ with $\mathbb R^{2N^2}$ by real and imaginary entries and represent $X\mapsto-B(t)X$ as a real-linear coefficient matrix, as made explicit in the proof.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F1], $\mathcal A_\infty^*$ is exactly the set of connections whose holonomy action has no common proper nonzero invariant subspace. [given, F1]

1.2 For each $V\in\mathscr G_N$, irreducibility supplies a based piecewise-smooth loop $\ell_V$ whose holonomy $g_V=\operatorname{Hol}_A(\ell_V)$ does not preserve $V$. If $P_V$ is the orthogonal projection onto $V$, then $(I-P_V)g_VP_V\ne0$. For each fixed loop $\ell$, the set of $V$ with $(I-P_V)\operatorname{Hol}_A(\ell)P_V\ne0$ is open, since the projector topology and matrix multiplication are continuous. These open sets cover $\mathscr G_N$. [A1, F1, F2, given, choose, construct]

2.1 Compactness in [F2] gives a finite subcover, hence finitely many loops $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_r$ for which no proper nonzero subspace is invariant under every $g_j=\operatorname{Hol}_A(\ell_j)$. The case $r=0$ cannot cover the nonempty space $\mathscr G_N$, so $r\ge1$. [F2, step 1.2, choose]

3.1 Define the continuous function
$$h_A(P)=\sum_{j=1}^r\|(I-P)g_jP\|_F$$
on $\mathscr G_N$, where $\|\cdot\|_F$ is the Frobenius norm. It is positive at every $P$ by step 2.1. By [F3] it has a minimum $\delta>0$, and $h_A(P)\ge\delta$ for every $P\in\mathscr G_N$. [F2, F3, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 On each smooth segment of a chosen loop, in a smooth trivialization along that segment, parallel transport satisfies $U'=-B(t)U$, $U(a)=I_N$, where $B:[a,b]\to\mathfrak{su}(N)$ is continuous. Identify $M_N(\mathbb C)$ with $\mathbb R^{2N^2}$ by taking real and imaginary parts of all entries. Left multiplication $X\mapsto-B(t)X$ is then a real-linear endomorphism represented by a continuous matrix $C(t)\in M_{2N^2}(\mathbb R)$. Regard the realified transport vector as the first column of a $2N^2\times2N^2$ real matrix with its other columns zero; it satisfies the first-column equation of the square matrix IVP $Y'=C(t)Y$. Thus [F4] gives its unique solution on the entire compact segment, and the same realification applies to a nearby coefficient $B+b$. Since $b(t)\in\mathfrak{su}(N)$, both $B$ and $B+b$ are in $\mathfrak{su}(N)$. For either complex coefficient $B_0\in\{B,B+b\}$ and its solution $Y'=-B_0Y$, $Y(a)=I_N$, skew-Hermiticity gives $(Y^*Y)'=Y^*B_0Y-Y^*B_0Y=0$, and Jacobi's formula gives $(\det Y)'=-\operatorname{tr}(B_0)\det Y=0$. Thus $Y(t)\in SU(N)$. If $\widetilde U$ solves the equation with coefficient $B+b$, put $W=U^{-1}\widetilde U$. Then $W'=-U^{-1}bU\,W$, $W(a)=I_N$, and $W(t)\in SU(N)$. The integral equation and unitary invariance of the Frobenius norm give $\|W(b)-I_N\|_F\le\int_a^b\|b(t)\|_F\,dt$. A $C^1$ perturbation of the connection makes each of the finitely many segment integrals arbitrarily small; finite composition therefore makes all $g_j$ vary continuously in the stated $C^1$ topology. Choose a neighborhood with $\|g_j(A')-g_j(A)\|_F<\delta/(2r)$ for every $j$. [F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra, choose]

5.1 For any orthogonal projection $P$, both $\|P\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ and $\|I-P\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ are at most $1$. Thus $$\left|\|(I-P)\operatorname{Hol}_{A'}(\ell_j)P\|_F-\|(I-P)g_jP\|_F\right|\le\|\operatorname{Hol}_{A'}(\ell_j)-g_j\|_F.$$ Summing over $j$ and using step 4.1 together with the uniform positive margin from step 3.1 gives $h_{A'}(P)>\delta/2$ uniformly in $P$. Hence the same finite tuple has no common invariant proper nonzero subspace for every such $A'$. [step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 A $C^1$ neighborhood on the compact base is specified by finitely many continuous section seminorms; each is continuous in the $C^\infty$ section topology. Therefore the neighborhood in step 4.1 is also a $C^\infty$ neighborhood, and $\mathcal A_\infty^*$ is open in the $C^\infty$ topology. Step 2.1 gives the finite holonomy witness at $A$. [F1, step 2.1, step 4.1, step 5.1] ∎
