## Definition

Let $\Sigma$ be a compact connected Riemann surface with complex structure $J$ satisfying $J^2=-\operatorname{id}$, and give it the orientation induced by $J$. Choose a smooth positive-definite metric $h$ such that $h(Ju,Jv)=h(u,v)$ for all $p\in\Sigma$ and $u,v\in T_p\Sigma$. Supply a Spin structure on the oriented Euclidean bundle $(T\Sigma,h)$: a principal $\operatorname{Spin}(2)$-bundle $P\to\Sigma$ with an equivariant two-fold map $\widetilde\lambda:P\to P_{\mathrm{SO}}(T\Sigma,h)$ over $\operatorname{id}_\Sigma$, satisfying $\widetilde\lambda(pg)=\widetilde\lambda(p)\lambda_2(g)$ for the standard double cover $\lambda_2:\operatorname{Spin}(2)\to\operatorname{SO}(2)$. Let $S_+$ and $S_-$ be the associated complex line bundles for the chiral representations $\rho_\pm(e^{\frac{t}{2}e_1e_2})=e^{\pm it/2}$, where $(e_1,e_2)$ is a positive orthonormal frame and $t$ is its rotation angle. The two representations are complex conjugates, so $S_-\cong\overline{S_+}$. Let $\mathscr S_{\mathbb R}$ be the real rank-two bundle associated to the underlying real representation of $\rho_+$ on $\mathbb C$; then $\mathscr S_{\mathbb R}\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C\cong S_+\oplus S_-$. These are supplied data; no existence theorem for a Spin structure is asserted.

A local $(1,1)$ worldsheet superspace $\mathcal W$ is the split real smooth supermanifold of dimension $2|2$ with body $\Sigma$ and structure sheaf locally $C^\infty_{\Sigma,\mathbb R}\otimes_{\mathbb R}\Lambda^\bullet\mathscr S_{\mathbb R}^*$. Thus its coefficient field is $\mathbb R$ and its odd tangent bundle has real rank two. After complexifying this sheaf to use chiral notation, a local oriented chart has coordinates $(\sigma^0,\sigma^1\mid\theta^+,\theta^-)$, where $\sigma^0,\sigma^1$ are real even coordinates and $\theta^\pm$ are the odd coordinate generators in the dual chiral lines; they anticommute. The real structure exchanges the two chiral lines and, in compatible local frames, $\overline{\theta^+}=\theta^-$. The body transitions preserve the orientation, and the odd transitions are induced by the supplied spin bundle. “$(1,1)$” means one odd tangent direction of each chirality after complexification, related by this real structure.

For component bookkeeping extend coefficients to a fixed supercommutative real algebra $\mathbb A=\mathbb A_{\bar 0}\oplus\mathbb A_{\bar 1}$, with $ab=(-1)^{|a||b|}ba$ for homogeneous elements, and complexify it for chiral components. Conjugation on $\mathbb A\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C$ is the antilinear multiplicative involution $\overline{a\otimes z}=a\otimes\overline z$ for $a\in\mathbb A$ and $z\in\mathbb C$; it preserves the written order of factors. In a separate flat Lorentzian light-cone superspace (not a chart on the Euclidean $\Sigma$), take independent real odd coordinates $\theta^\pm$ and $x^{++},x^{--}$ with $\partial_{\pm\pm}:=\partial/\partial x^{\pm\pm}$. The left superderivative generators
$$Q_\pm=\partial_{\theta^\pm}+i\theta^\pm\partial_{\pm\pm}$$
satisfy $Q_\pm^2=i\partial_{\pm\pm}$ and $\{Q_+,Q_-\}=0$. These are the plus-sign operators denoted $Q_\pm$ by Hull, Papadopoulos, and Townsend in §5, equation (5.3); no flat covariant derivative is denoted by $D_\pm$ here. An even scalar superfield $X:\mathcal W\to N$ into a smooth real manifold $N$ has local expansion
$$X^\mu=x^\mu+\theta^+\Xi_+^\mu+\theta^-\Xi_-^\mu+\theta^+\theta^-A^\mu.$$
Here $TN_{\mathbb C}:=TN\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C$, $x:\Sigma\to N$ is a smooth body map, and $\Xi_\pm$ are smooth odd sections with coefficients in $\mathbb A_{\bar 1}\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C$ of $S_\pm^*\otimes x^*TN_{\mathbb C}$. The signs on $\Xi_\pm$ label the coordinate monomials they multiply, not the associated-bundle chirality. Intrinsically the linear term is a section of $\mathscr S_{\mathbb R}^*\otimes x^*TN$. We retain this dual-bundle convention. The invariant real spin metric is $g_S(u,v)=\operatorname{Re}(u\overline v)$ in a unit real spin frame. Its complex-bilinear extension pairs $S_+$ with $S_-$ and vanishes on either summand paired with itself; consequently $g_S^\flat$ maps $S_-$ to $S_+^*$ and $S_+$ to $S_-^*$. Thus raising the spin index sends $\Xi_+$ to $S_-$, and $\Xi_-$ to $S_+$; it does not preserve the written sign. The coefficient $A$ is a local even component with coefficients in $\mathbb A_{\bar 0}\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C$. For a real even superfield the induced conjugation, with this coefficient order, is $\overline{x}=x$, $\overline{\Xi_+}=\Xi_-$, and $\overline A=-A$. Under a smooth target-coordinate change $y^\alpha=f^\alpha(x)$ between real charts, the displayed coefficient order gives $\Xi_\pm'{}^\alpha=(\partial_\mu f^\alpha)\Xi_\pm^\mu$ and $A'{}^\alpha=(\partial_\mu f^\alpha)A^\mu-(\partial_\mu\partial_\nu f^\alpha)\Xi_+^\mu\Xi_-^\nu$. Thus $A$ alone need not be a section of $x^*TN_{\mathbb C}$; when $\Xi_\pm=0$, the condition $A=0$ is invariant under target-coordinate changes. A covariant global auxiliary component away from that locus would require a supplied target connection and the associated splitting. No equations of motion or field existence result is asserted.

The curved data below are constrained geometric data; arbitrary unconstrained supervielbeins are not called an RNS supergravity geometry. Choose a holomorphic coordinate-disc cover with transitions $z_j=f_{ij}(z_i)$ and spin lifts $a_{ij}^2=f_{ij}'$ satisfying the cocycle law. Use the split supercoordinate transitions
$$z_j=f_{ij}(z_i),\qquad \theta_j^+=a_{ij}\theta_i^+,\qquad \overline z_j=\overline f_{ij}(\overline z_i),\qquad \theta_j^-=\overline a_{ij}\theta_i^-.$$
The odd coordinate generators belong to the dual of the supplied smooth real spin bundle, with the conformal/orthonormal identification described below. These are conformal spin coordinates, not orthonormal spin coordinates. The body $s=S|_{\theta=0}$ is fixed by the supplied metric $h=e^{-4s}|dz|^2$. On a chart write $z=x+iy$, $\partial_z=(\partial_x-i\partial_y)/2$, $\partial_{\overline z}=(\partial_x+i\partial_y)/2$, and choose a real even smooth superfunction $S$. Its overlap law is
$$S_j=S_i+\frac12\log|f_{ij}'|,$$
where supercoordinate pullback on the right is understood. A conformal presentation consists of these local functions with these laws. More general supplied presentations may be obtained by invertible parity-preserving supercoordinate changes and local Spin rotations of such data; the construction below is covariant under these operations. No global conformal-gauge theorem for every conceivable supergeometry is asserted.

Let $M$ be the complexified Lorentz generator acting on frame-weight $w$ components by multiplication by $w$, where $w\in\frac12\mathbb Z$. Thus the component-index generator $J_0=iM$ acts by $iw$; its real matrix on component columns is $\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$. This fixes $M=-iJ_0$. The component-index convention is contragredient to the moving-bundle-frame convention: on the bundle $S_+^*$ the moving-frame representation has infinitesimal character $-i/2$, while its local coefficient has frame weight $+1/2$. The scalar connection $\Omega$ below is the negative of the moving-vector-frame connection $\omega$, so $d-i\omega/2=d+i\Omega/2$ is exactly the induced connection on $S_+^*$, with conjugate formula on $S_-^*$. No identification of opposite chiral representations is implicit in $M=-iJ_0$. An operator with label $+$ raises frame weight by $1/2$, one with label $-$ lowers it by $1/2$, and labels $++$ and $--$ change it by $1$ and $-1$. These conventions specify the connection on indexed components, including when two derivatives are composed. Define left odd derivatives
$$D_+=\partial_{\theta^+}+\theta^+\partial_z,\qquad D_-=\partial_{\theta^-}+\theta^-\partial_{\overline z},\qquad u=D_+S,\qquad v=D_-S,\qquad f=e^S.$$
The symbols $D_\pm$ refer only to this Euclidean presentation and are distinct from the flat Lorentzian $Q_\pm$ above. Define
$$\nabla_+=f(D_++2uM),\qquad \nabla_-=f(D_--2vM),$$
$$\nabla_{++}=f^2(\partial_z+2uD_++2(\partial_z S)M),\qquad \nabla_{--}=f^2(\partial_{\overline z}+2vD_--2(\partial_{\overline z}S)M).$$
Their conventional torsion/curvature constraints, as equalities on every frame-weight component, are
$$\{\nabla_+,\nabla_+\}=2\nabla_{++},\qquad \{\nabla_-,\nabla_-\}=2\nabla_{--},\qquad \{\nabla_+,\nabla_-\}=R_M M,\qquad R_M=4f^2D_-D_+S.$$
In particular the mixed odd bracket has no derivative-valued torsion term, and $[\nabla_+,\nabla_{++}]=[\nabla_-,\nabla_{--}]=0$. These identities are proved below, rather than imposed to infer the component reduction.

Writing $\nabla_A=E_A{}^I(\partial_I+\Omega_I J_0)$ defines the inverse supervielbein and scalar connection coefficient. The ordered indices are $I=(z,\overline z,\theta^+,\theta^-)$ and $A=(++,--,+,-)$. In left-coefficient convention their full finite component expressions are
$$E_A{}^I=\begin{pmatrix}f^2(1+2u\theta^+)&0&2f^2u&0\\0&f^2(1+2v\theta^-)&0&2f^2v\\f\theta^+&0&f&0\\0&f\theta^-&0&f\end{pmatrix},$$
$$E_I{}^A=\begin{pmatrix}f^{-2}&0&-2f^{-1}u&0\\0&f^{-2}&0&-2f^{-1}v\\-f^{-2}\theta^+&0&f^{-1}(1-2u\theta^+)&0\\0&-f^{-2}\theta^-&0&f^{-1}(1-2v\theta^-)\end{pmatrix},$$
$$\Omega_z=-2i\partial_zS,\qquad \Omega_{\overline z}=2i\partial_{\overline z}S,\qquad \Omega_{\theta^+}=-2i\partial_{\theta^+}S,\qquad \Omega_{\theta^-}=2i\partial_{\theta^-}S.$$
Every entry is a smooth polynomial in the two odd coordinates after expanding $e^S$; coefficients may themselves lie in the component algebra $\mathbb A$. In particular these are explicit components, not an instruction to solve unspecified torsion equations. The scalar connection $\Omega$ is conjugation compatible. For components of a frame of weight $w$, the connection is $\partial_I+iw\Omega_I$. In the bosonic restriction its coefficient is the negative of the connection acting on the moving vector basis; this is the usual opposite sign between basis and component connections.

For an even block supermatrix $T=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{pmatrix}$ with invertible $D$, use $\operatorname{Ber}T=\det(A-BD^{-1}C)/\det D$. Set
$$\mathcal E=\operatorname{Ber}(E_A{}^I),\qquad \mu=\mathcal E^{-1}=\operatorname{Ber}(E_I{}^A)=e^{-2S}.$$
Thus the measure is the Berezinian of the coframe, not its reciprocal. Use the real oriented surface measure $d^2z=dx\,dy$; equivalently its even complex-coordinate normalization is $(i/2)dz\wedge d\overline z$. To fix the real odd orientation, write $\theta^+=(\eta^1+i\eta^2)/\sqrt2$, $\theta^-=(\eta^1-i\eta^2)/\sqrt2$, with $\eta^1,\eta^2$ real odd variables. Write each local superfunction uniquely as $F=f_0+\eta^1f_1+\eta^2f_2+\eta^1\eta^2f_{12}$, with coefficients in $\mathbb A\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C$ on the right, and define $I_E(F):=f_{12}$, coefficientwise over the even coordinates. Equivalently, $I_E(1)=I_E(\eta^1)=I_E(\eta^2)=0$ and $I_E(\eta^1\eta^2)=1$, so
$$I_E(\theta^+\theta^-)=-i,\qquad I_E(\theta^-\theta^+)=i.$$
This real Berezin integral is $i$ times the purely formal ordered chiral rule $I_0(\theta^-\theta^+)=1$. The change is essential: a real even superfunction has a purely imaginary $\theta^+\theta^-$ coefficient under the specified conjugation. Integrals are $\int_\Sigma d^2z\,I_E(\mu\mathcal L)$ in such charts, using an ordinary partition of unity if needed. Their transition law is proved below.

Let $F_J$ denote the scalar coefficient of $J_0$ in the mixed frame curvature. It is defined invariantly by $\{\nabla_+,\nabla_-\}=F_JJ_0$, so $F_J=-iR_M$. Equivalently it is the contraction of $d\Omega$ with the two odd frame vectors, with the signs of the graded differential-form evaluation included. Define the real even scalar supercurvature used by the RNS model to be
$$\mathcal R=4F_J=-16i e^{2S}D_-D_+S.$$
The factor $4$ is the normalization relative to the coefficient of the real rotation generator, with the present $D_\pm$ and real odd integration convention. Its ordinary-curvature interpretation is derived below; it is not specified by assuming the desired integral identity.

The bosonic truncation means, in a conformal presentation, $S=s(z,\overline z)$ with no independent odd or top component, and $X=x$ with $\Xi_+=\Xi_-=A=0$. In a general local expansion $S=s+\theta^+\xi_++\theta^-\xi_-+\theta^+\theta^-b$, these conditions are $\xi_+=\xi_-=b=0$; define the conformal-gauge gravitino to be the odd spinor-valued one-form with local components $\psi_z{}^+=E_z{}^+|=-2e^{-s}\xi_+$, $\psi_{\overline z}{}^-=E_{\overline z}{}^-|=-2e^{-s}\xi_-$ and other components zero, and define the real curvature auxiliary scalar to be $A_{\mathrm{geom}}:=\mathcal R|=-16i e^{2s}b$. Thus the truncation removes precisely these independent component fields. They do not set the odd-coordinate-dependent coframe coefficients to zero. For the supplied body metric write
$$h=e^{-4s}(dx^2+dy^2).$$
The odd coordinates in orthonormal spin frames are $\vartheta^\pm=e^{-s}\theta^\pm$; this identifies the conformal spin sheaf with the earlier smooth spin-bundle sheaf. If an original superfield expansion is written in the orthonormal generators as $X=x+\vartheta^+\Xi_{\mathrm{orth},+}+\vartheta^-\Xi_{\mathrm{orth},-}+\vartheta^+\vartheta^-A_{\mathrm{orth}}$, then its conformal-coordinate coefficients are $\Xi_{\mathrm{conf},\pm}=e^{-s}\Xi_{\mathrm{orth},\pm}$ and $A_{\mathrm{conf}}=e^{-2s}A_{\mathrm{orth}}$. They are the local components of the same bundle-valued data in nonunit conformal spin frames; they are not numerically identified with unrescaled orthonormal components. Both reality conditions and the zero-component truncation are preserved by these positive real rescalings. On overlaps $f_{ij}^\prime=|f_{ij}^\prime|e^{i\tau}$, $\vartheta_j^\pm=e^{\pm i\tau/2}\vartheta_i^\pm$ and $\Xi_{\mathrm{orth},j,\pm}=e^{\mp i\tau/2}\Xi_{\mathrm{orth},i,\pm}$. In conformal frames $\theta_j^+=a_{ij}\theta_i^+$, $\theta_j^-=\overline a_{ij}\theta_i^-$, $\Xi_{\mathrm{conf},j,+}=a_{ij}^{-1}\Xi_{\mathrm{conf},i,+}$ and $\Xi_{\mathrm{conf},j,-}=\overline a_{ij}^{-1}\Xi_{\mathrm{conf},i,-}$. These are dual-spin section components, consistently with the intrinsic linear term. At this truncation the full geometry is
$$E_I{}^A=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-2s}&0&-2e^{-s}\theta^+s_z&0\\0&e^{-2s}&0&-2e^{-s}\theta^-s_{\overline z}\\-e^{-2s}\theta^+&0&e^{-s}&0\\0&-e^{-2s}\theta^-&0&e^{-s}\end{pmatrix},\qquad \Omega=-2s_y\,dx+2s_x\,dy,$$
$$\mu=e^{-2s},\qquad F_J=4i e^{2s}\theta^+\theta^-s_{z\overline z},\qquad \mathcal R=16i e^{2s}\theta^+\theta^-s_{z\overline z}.$$
For every smooth body map $x:\Sigma\to N$ and every smooth real target scalar $\Phi$, the resulting weighted component identity is
$$I_E\!\left(\mu\Phi(x)\mathcal R\right)=16\Phi(x)s_{z\overline z}=\sqrt{\det h}\,\Phi(x)R^{(2)}(h).$$
Here $R^{(2)}=2K_h$ is positive on the round sphere. The identity is pointwise in every oriented chart; no integration by parts of $\Phi$ is used. Consequently it gives the corresponding global integral for arbitrary such $\Phi$, not merely for constant dilaton or modulo a total derivative. This construction asserts neither a quantum measure nor a classification of all supergeometries.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The supplied smooth spin Riemann surface and its metric, the conformal spin transitions, component supercommutative algebra and left-derivative conventions in the Definition. All assertions below are mathematical identities for that construction, not physical field equations or claims about nature.

[F1] The supplied holomorphic atlas defines a Riemann surface and the supplied Spin bundle has the stated double covering and associated weight representations ([[def-riemann-surface-and-holomorphic-atlas]], [[def-given-spin-and-spinc-structures-and-associated-spinor-bundles]]).

[A1] For a homogeneous odd coefficient $q$, supercommutativity gives $q^2=0$. Derivatives with respect to an odd coordinate act from the left. A charge-$w$ component is differentiated with $M=w$; when a derivative label is added its frame weight changes as specified in the Definition.

## Proof

**Given:** The complete local formulas in the Definition, with $S$ even and real and with its body fixed by the supplied metric.

1.1 On overlaps a holomorphic coordinate derivative $f_{ij}'$ is nowhere zero. The smooth spin lift of its phase, multiplied by $|f_{ij}'|^{1/2}$, gives $a_{ij}$ with $a_{ij}^2=f_{ij}'$ and the spin cocycle law. Differentiating $a_{ij}^2=f_{ij}'$ by $\partial_{\overline z}$ shows $2a_{ij}\partial_{\overline z}a_{ij}=0$, hence $a_{ij}$ is holomorphic. For $h=e^{-4s_i}|dz_i|^2$, the metric transition gives $s_j=s_i+\frac12\log|f_{ij}'|$. Consequently $\vartheta_j^+=e^{-s_j}\theta_j^+=(a_{ij}/|a_{ij}|)\vartheta_i^+$, with its conjugate for $-$. These are the transitions of the odd coordinate generators in the dual spin sheaf. The orthonormal vector frames obey $e_j=e_i\operatorname{Rot}(-\tau)$ for $f_{ij}^\prime=|f_{ij}^\prime|e^{i\tau}$; their $S_+$ frames therefore transform by $e^{-i\tau/2}$, and their dual generators by $e^{i\tau/2}$. Invariance of the scalar linear term gives $\Xi_{\mathrm{orth},j,+}=e^{-i\tau/2}\Xi_{\mathrm{orth},i,+}$ and $\Xi_{\mathrm{orth},j,-}=e^{i\tau/2}\Xi_{\mathrm{orth},i,-}$. In conformal coordinates the same rule is $\Xi_{\mathrm{conf},j,+}=a_{ij}^{-1}\Xi_{\mathrm{conf},i,+}$ and $\Xi_{\mathrm{conf},j,-}=\overline a_{ij}^{-1}\Xi_{\mathrm{conf},i,-}$, while $A_{\mathrm{conf},j}=|a_{ij}|^{-2}A_{\mathrm{conf},i}$ and $A_{\mathrm{orth},j}=A_{\mathrm{orth},i}$. This identifies the split smooth dual-spin sheaf with the displayed conformal spin atlas. A conformal presentation with arbitrary additional real even superfunction coefficients is therefore defined on this supplied split supermanifold; the bosonic presentation $S_i=s_i$ exists for every supplied pair $(h,P)$. This proves the needed gluing construction without assuming existence of a new Spin structure. [given, F1, algebra, construct]

1.2 Left differentiation gives $D_+^2=\partial_z$, $D_-^2=\partial_{\overline z}$ and $D_+D_-+D_-D_+=0$: in $D_+^2$ the two terms with a surviving $\theta^+\partial_{\theta^+}$ cancel and $(\theta^+)^2=0$, and the mixed terms cancel by the odd signs. Conjugation exchanges $D_+$ and $D_-$. Since $\theta^+\theta^-=-i\eta^1\eta^2$ and $I_E$ extracts the $\eta^1\eta^2$ coefficient, $I_E(\theta^+\theta^-)=-i$. For a real even superfunction, conjugation reverses these two chiral generators and makes its top chiral coefficient imaginary; this $I_E$ sends that coefficient to a real value. [given, A1, algebra]

2.1 For a component $t$ of frame weight $w$, composition of $\nabla_+$ uses $w+\frac12$ on its intermediate output. The graded product rule gives
$$\nabla_+^2t=f^2\left(D_+^2t+2uD_+t+2w(D_+^2S)t\right)=\nabla_{++}t.$$
Here the terms quadratic in $u$ vanish, and the two surviving $uD_+t$ coefficients arise from differentiating $f$ and from the changed output weight. The same calculation with intermediate weight $w-\frac12$ gives $\nabla_-^2t=f^2(D_-^2t+2vD_-t-2w(D_-^2S)t)=\nabla_{--}t$. In the mixed sum the derivative-valued terms cancel; the terms quadratic in $u,v$ also cancel because $vu=-uv$, leaving
$$\{\nabla_+,\nabla_-\}t=2wf^2(D_-u-D_+v)t=4wf^2(D_-D_+S)t.$$
This proves all three stated constraints on every weight, including weight zero. Associativity implies $[\nabla_+,\nabla_+^2]=[\nabla_-,\nabla_-^2]=0$, giving the remaining same-chirality constraints. No component reduction identity was used. [given, A1, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Reading the derivative coefficients of these four operators gives the displayed $E_A{}^I$. Their coframe inverse is the displayed $E_I{}^A$: in each chiral block the even diagonal product is $1+2u\theta^+-2u\theta^+=1$, the odd diagonal product is $1-2\theta^+u-2u\theta^+=1$, and the off-diagonal products cancel, using $u^2=(\theta^+)^2=0$; replace $u,\theta^+$ by $v,\theta^-$ for the other block. Solving $E_A{}^I\Omega_IJ_0$ for the connection terms gives the four stated $\Omega_I$, since $J_0=iM$, $u=\partial_{\theta^+}S+\theta^+S_z$ and $v=\partial_{\theta^-}S+\theta^-S_{\overline z}$. Thus the entire coframe and connection are determined explicit functions of $S$, rather than independent unspecified fields. [given, A1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Put $t_+=u\theta^+$ and $t_-=v\theta^-$; these are even and satisfy $t_+^2=t_-^2=0$. In the coframe matrix its odd-odd block is $D=f^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(1-2t_+,1-2t_-)$, so $D^{-1}=f\operatorname{diag}(1+2t_+,1+2t_-)$. The Schur complement is $A-BD^{-1}C=f^{-2}\operatorname{diag}(1-2t_+,1-2t_-)$. Its determinant divided by $\det D$ is exactly $f^{-2}$, including the possible mixed product $t_+t_-$. This proves $\operatorname{Ber}(E_I{}^A)=e^{-2S}$ and hence $\mathcal E=\operatorname{Ber}(E_A{}^I)=e^{2S}$. In particular taking the reciprocal of the coframe Berezinian would give the wrong measure. [given, A1, step 3.1, algebra]

4.2 The connection on a charge-$w$ component is $d+iw\Omega$. Its curvature is $iw\,d\Omega$, since the scalar coefficient algebra supercommutes and the rotation Lie algebra is abelian. The zero derivative-valued mixed torsion in step 2.1 therefore identifies $\{\nabla_+,\nabla_-\}=R_M M=(-iR_M)J_0$ with the mixed frame curvature, including all graded evaluation signs. Thus $F_J=-iR_M$ and $\mathcal R=4F_J$ have exactly the stated formulas. Conjugation sends $R_M$ to $-R_M$ by step 1.2 and fixes $\mathcal R$; it fixes $\Omega$ because it exchanges its two chiral differential summands and conjugates $-i$ to $i$. These are real supergeometric objects in the specified real structure. [given, A1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Write $a=a_{ij}$ and $b=\log|a|=\frac12\log|f_{ij}'|$. The supercoordinate chain rule gives $D_{i,+}=aD_{j,+}$ and $D_{i,-}=\overline aD_{j,-}$; the term differentiating $a\theta^+$ a second time contains $(\theta^+)^2$ and vanishes. Since $b$ is a sum of a holomorphic and an antiholomorphic function, $D_-D_+b=0$. Also $D_-a=D_+\overline a=0$. Hence $e^{2S_j}D_{j,-}D_{j,+}S_j=e^{2S_i}D_{i,-}D_{i,+}S_i$: $R_M$, $F_J$ and $\mathcal R$ are scalar superfunctions on overlaps. The Berezinian of the coordinate transition is $|f_{ij}'|^2/(a\overline a)=|f_{ij}'|$, whereas $\mu_j=\mu_i/|f_{ij}'|$. Their product has the exact density transition law. This also shows independence from the sign of a spin lift. [given, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 4.1, step 4.2, algebra]

5.2 At $S=s$ the odd derivatives are $u=\theta^+s_z$, $v=\theta^-s_{\overline z}$, whence $u\theta^+=v\theta^-=0$ and the displayed truncated coframe follows from step 3.1. The odd connection components vanish but the odd vector coefficients remain $E_+{}^z=e^s\theta^+$ and $E_-{}^{\overline z}=e^s\theta^-$. The even connection becomes $\Omega=-2s_y dx+2s_xdy$, with $d\Omega=2\Delta s\,dx\wedge dy=8s_{z\overline z}\,dx\wedge dy$, or $F_{z\overline z}=4i s_{z\overline z}$. Contracting with those two surviving odd vector coefficients gives $F_J=4i e^{2s}\theta^+\theta^-s_{z\overline z}$, in agreement with step 4.2 since $D_-D_+s=-\theta^+\theta^-s_{z\overline z}$. This explicitly locates the curvature-producing odd-coordinate dependence that a body-only deletion would destroy. [given, step 1.2, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 4.2, algebra]

5.3 The kinetic convention can also be checked before the physical consumer uses it. For any symmetric target tensor $G$ and antisymmetric target tensor $B$, $\nabla_+x=f\theta^+\partial_zx$, $\nabla_-x=f\theta^-\partial_{\overline z}x$, and hence
$$4i I_E\!\left(\mu(G+B)_{\mu\nu}(x)\nabla_+x^\mu\nabla_-x^\nu\right)=4(G+B)_{\mu\nu}(x)\partial_zx^\mu\partial_{\overline z}x^\nu.$$
