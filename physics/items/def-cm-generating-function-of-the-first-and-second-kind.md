## Definition

**First-kind data.** Let $n\ge1$ and let $O_q,O_Q\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ be nonempty open sets
([[def-metric-topology]]). Let $S:O_q\times O_Q\to\mathbb R$ be of class $C^3$
([[def-ck-euclidean-maps-and-diffeomorphisms]]) and assume

$$\det S_{qQ}(q,Q)\ne0\qquad\bigl((q,Q)\in O_q\times O_Q\bigr),$$

where $S_{qQ}(q,Q)$ is the $n\times n$ matrix with entries
$\partial^2 S/\partial q^i\partial Q^j(q,Q)$ ([[def-directional-and-partial-derivatives]],
[[def-jacobian-matrix-and-gradient]]). Identify $O_q\times O_Q$ with the phase-domain coordinates
$(q,Q)$ of $\mathbb R^{2n}$ ([[def-cm-phase-domain-and-standard-symplectic-two-form]]) and form the
$C^2$ map

$$\Psi:O_q\times O_Q\longrightarrow\mathbb R^{2n},\qquad \Psi(q,Q):=\bigl(q,\ S_q(q,Q)\bigr),$$

whose derivative is the block matrix
$D\Psi(q,Q)=\begin{pmatrix}I_n&0\\ S_{qq}(q,Q)&S_{qQ}(q,Q)\end{pmatrix}$. A **generating function of
the first kind** is such an $S$; the **local map generated by $S$** is, on a neighbourhood of a point
$(q_*,Q_*)$, the $C^2$ map $C:(q,p)\mapsto(Q,P)$ defined by

$$p:=S_q(q,Q),\qquad P:=-S_Q(q,Q),$$

solved as follows: since $\det S_{qQ}\ne0$, the inverse function theorem
([[thm-euclidean-inverse-function-theorem]]) makes $\Psi$ a local $C^2$ diffeomorphism, so
$p=S_q(q,Q)$ determines a $C^2$ local inverse $Q=Q(q,p)$ on a neighbourhood of
$\Psi(q_*,Q_*)$; then $C(q,p):=(Q(q,p),-S_Q(q,Q(q,p)))$.

**Second-kind data.** Let $O_q,O_P\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ be nonempty open sets and let
$S:O_q\times O_P\to\mathbb R$ be of class $C^3$ with $\det S_{qP}(q,P)\ne0$ everywhere. A
**generating function of the second kind** is such an $S$; the **local map generated by $S$** is,
near a point $(q_*,P_*)$, the $C^2$ map $C:(q,p)\mapsto(Q,P)$ defined by

$$p:=S_q(q,P),\qquad Q:=S_P(q,P),$$

where again the relation $p=S_q(q,P)$ is solved locally for $P=P(q,p)$ by the inverse function
theorem applied to $(q,P)\mapsto(q,S_q(q,P))$, whose derivative is
$\begin{pmatrix}I_n&0\\ S_{qq}&S_{qP}\end{pmatrix}$.

**Recorded identities.** Wherever $S$ is of the first kind, the identity
$p\,dq-P\,dQ=dS$ holds; wherever $S$ is of the second kind, the identity $p\,dq+Q\,dP=dS$ holds,
both as identities of $C^2$ coordinate one-forms on the $(q,Q)$- resp. $(q,P)$-space with $p,P,Q$ read as the
displayed functions ([[def-cm-finite-regularity-canonical-calculus]]). The mixed-derivative hypotheses
$\det S_{qQ}\ne0$ resp. $\det S_{qP}\ne0$ are part of the data.
