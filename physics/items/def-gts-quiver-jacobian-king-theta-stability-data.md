## Definition

Work over $\mathbb C$. A finite quiver is a tuple $Q=(Q_0,Q_1,s,t)$ of finite vertex and arrow sets with source and target maps $s,t:Q_1\to Q_0$. It is 2-acyclic when it has no loops and no oriented cycles of length two. Its path algebra $\mathbb C Q$ has basis the finite directed paths, including one length-zero idempotent at each vertex, and multiplication by concatenation when endpoints match and by zero otherwise.

A finite potential $W$ is a finite complex linear combination of directed cycles, considered modulo cyclic permutation of the factors. For an arrow $a$, the cyclic derivative of a cycle $a_1\cdots a_\ell$ is $\partial_a(a_1\cdots a_\ell)=\sum_{j:a_j=a}a_{j+1}\cdots a_\ell a_1\cdots a_{j-1}$, extended linearly. The Jacobian algebra is $J(Q,W)=\mathbb C Q/\langle\partial_a W:a\in Q_1\rangle$, where the denominator is the two-sided ideal generated by these derivatives. No finite-dimensionality of $J(Q,W)$ is assumed. Write $\mathcal A=\operatorname{mod}_{\mathrm{fd}}J(Q,W)$ for its abelian category of finite-dimensional unital left modules, equivalently finite-dimensional quiver representations satisfying the Jacobian relations.

For $E\in\mathcal A$, its dimension vector is $d(E)\in\mathbb N^{Q_0}$, with coordinate $\dim_\mathbb C(e_iE)$ at vertex $i$; extend this additively to $\mathbb Z^{Q_0}$. A weight is $\theta\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(\mathbb Z^{Q_0},\mathbb R)$, and $\theta(E)$ abbreviates $\theta(d(E))$. A nonzero representation $E$ is King $\theta$-semistable when $\theta(E)=0$ and every subobject $0\ne A\subseteq E$ satisfies $\theta(A)\le0$. It is $\theta$-stable when $\theta(E)=0$ and $\theta(A)<0$ for every proper nonzero subobject $A\subsetneq E$. This abelian-category notion is distinct from a slicing on a triangulated category.
