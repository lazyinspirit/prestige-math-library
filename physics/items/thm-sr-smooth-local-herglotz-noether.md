## Statement

Let $(A,V,\eta,C^+)$ be the oriented time-oriented affine Minkowski space ([[def-sr-affine-minkowski-model]]), let $n$ be a smooth future unit timelike field on a nonempty open subset $\Omega\subseteq A$ ([[def-sr-chart-tetrad-and-congruence-distinction]]), with acceleration $a=\nabla_nn$, rest metric $h$, expansion $\theta$ and vorticity $\omega$ ([[def-sr-observer-congruence-and-kinematics]]), and assume that $n$ is Born rigid, $\theta=0$; write $a^\flat:=\eta(a,\cdot)$.

**(i) Preliminary identity.** For every smooth vector field $X$ on $\Omega$ with $\eta(n,X)=0$ one has $\nabla_Xn=0$ if and only if $\theta(X,\cdot)=0$ and $\omega(X,\cdot)=0$. In particular, if $\theta=\omega=0$ on an open set $U\subseteq\Omega$, then $\nabla_Xn=0$ for every vector field $X$ on $U$ tangent to the rest distribution $\ker n^\flat$.

**(ii) Rotational case.** Let $U\subseteq\Omega$ be a flow box of $n$ with adapted coordinates $(s,y^1,y^2,y^3)$ in which $n=\partial_s$ ([[thm-sr-local-congruence-adapted-charts-and-frobenius]]), and suppose that $\omega$ is nowhere zero on $U$. Then the vorticity and the acceleration are **basic** on $U$: they are horizontal and their components in the adapted coframe do not depend on $s$, equivalently $\mathcal L_n\omega=0$ and $\mathcal L_na^\flat=0$. Moreover $da^\flat=0$ on $U$, and for every primitive $F$ of the closed one-form $a^\flat$ on the coordinate product box $U$ ([[thm-poincare-lemma-for-differential-forms-on-star-shaped-domains]]) the field $K:=e^Fn$ is a timelike Killing field, $\nabla_aK_b+\nabla_bK_a=0$, equivalently $\mathcal L_K\eta=0$. If $U$ is connected and simply connected and $\omega$ is nowhere zero, then a single positive smooth function $f$ on $U$ satisfies $\mathcal L_{fn}\eta=0$ ([[lem-sr-closed-one-form-primitive-on-simply-connected-open-sets]]). Conversely, every future timelike Killing field on a connected open set, normalized as $n=K/\sqrt{-\eta(K,K)}$, is Born rigid, and its vorticity is either nowhere zero or identically zero on that set; hence its flow is rotational, in the sense of case (ii), exactly when its vorticity is nowhere zero.

**(iii) Irrotational case.** Suppose $\theta=\omega=0$ on $\Omega$. Then on a sufficiently small neighbourhood $U$ of every point the connected leaves of the rest distribution are open subsets of affine hyperplanes: there are a smooth future timelike curve $z:J\to A$ with $\eta(z',z')=-1$ and a smooth function $\sigma:U\to J$ such that, with

$$N(x):=1+\eta\bigl(z''(\sigma(x)),\,x-z(\sigma(x))\bigr)>0,$$

the leaves of the rest distribution in $U$ are open subsets of the hyperplanes $H_s=z(s)+z'(s)^\perp$, the field is $n=z'\circ\sigma$ on $U$, and

$$d\sigma(v)=-\frac{\eta(z'(\sigma(x)),v)}{N(x)},\qquad a=\frac{z''\circ\sigma}{N},\qquad \nabla n=d\sigma\otimes(z''\circ\sigma);$$

the tubular label has derivative $1+\eta(z''\circ\sigma,x-z\circ\sigma)$, which is nonzero and is taken positive on $U$. Conversely, for every smooth future timelike curve $z:J\to A$ with $\eta(z',z')=-1$, the field $n:=z'\circ\sigma$ on any tubular neighbourhood on which the label $\sigma$ is unique with positive derivative is a smooth future unit irrotational Born-rigid congruence satisfying these same formulas.

**(iv) Scope.** All statements are local on $\Omega$; no global classification of rigid motions, no global orbit quotient, no completeness and no global restriction on the size of a rigid body is asserted beyond what the local formulas give. No choice principle is used. The rotational case is the local form of the Herglotz-Noether statement that a rotational Born-rigid motion is Killing; the irrotational case describes the orthogonal affine-plane families.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The oriented time-oriented affine Minkowski space $(A,V,\eta,C^+)$ with tangent spaces identified with $V$, a smooth future unit timelike field $n$ on a nonempty open subset $\Omega\subseteq A$ with $\theta=0$, the objects $h,a,\theta,\omega$ of [[def-sr-observer-congruence-and-kinematics]], the flat covariant derivative $\nabla$ of [[def-flat-covariant-derivative-on-affine-minkowski-space]], and, in clauses (ii) and (iii), the flow boxes and curves described in the Statement ([[def-sr-affine-minkowski-model]], [[def-sr-chart-tetrad-and-congruence-distinction]]).


[F1] In an inertial chart of the model, $\eta(u,v)=-u^0v^0+u^1v^1+u^2v^2+u^3v^3$, the tangent space at each event is canonically identified with $V$, and the flat covariant derivative acts on components by ordinary coordinate derivatives: $(\nabla_XY)^a=X^b\partial_bY^a$. It is torsion-free, $\nabla_XY-\nabla_YX=[X,Y]$; metric compatible, $\nabla\eta=0$; and has zero curvature, $\nabla_X\nabla_YZ-\nabla_Y\nabla_XZ-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z=0$ ([[def-flat-covariant-derivative-on-affine-minkowski-space]], [[def-sr-affine-minkowski-model]], [[def-ck-tensor-field-on-minkowski-space]]).


[F2] Kinematics of the congruence: $\nabla_an_b=-n_aa_b+\theta_{ab}+\omega_{ab}$, with $a_b=\eta_{bc}a^c$ and $a=\nabla_nn$; $\theta$ is symmetric and $\omega$ antisymmetric; both are horizontal, $\theta_{ab}n^b=\omega_{ab}n^b=n^a\theta_{ab}=n^a\omega_{ab}=0$; $h=\eta+n^\flat\otimes n^\flat$ satisfies $h(v,w)=\eta(h(v),h(w))$ with $h(v)=v+\eta(n,v)n$, its radical is spanned by $n$ and its restriction to $n^\perp$ is positive definite; Born rigidity is $\mathcal L_nh=0$, equivalently $\theta=0$; and $a^\flat(n)=0$ ([[def-sr-observer-congruence-and-kinematics]], [[thm-sr-proper-time-and-observer-decomposition]] clause (c), [[def-lorentz-orthogonality-and-orthogonal-complement]]).


[F3] Flow box and adapted charts: a smooth nowhere-zero vector field is locally $\partial/\partial u^0$ in suitable coordinates, and every observer congruence admits local adapted charts; in particular $n=\partial_s$ in coordinates $(s,y^1,y^2,y^3)$ on a flow box $U$, and the integral curves of $n$ are its $s$-lines ([[thm-flow-box-theorem]], [[thm-sr-local-congruence-adapted-charts-and-frobenius]], [[def-integral-curve-of-a-vector-field]]).


[F4] Connection one-forms and curvature two-forms: for a local frame $e_A$ with dual coframe $\theta^A$, the connection matrix $\gamma^A{}_B$ is defined by $\nabla_Xe_B=\gamma^A{}_B(X)e_A$, and the curvature two-forms satisfy $\Omega^A{}_B=d\gamma^A{}_B+\gamma^A{}_C\wedge\gamma^C{}_B$ with $R(X,Y)e_B=\Omega^A{}_B(X,Y)e_A$ ([[def-connection-one-form-in-a-local-frame]], [[thm-curvature-two-form-structure-equation]]). For the flat connection of [F1], $\Omega^A{}_B=0$ ([[def-flat-covariant-derivative-on-affine-minkowski-space]], clause (c)).


[F5] Local quotient geometry. On the adapted product box of [F3], the projection $q:(s,y)\mapsto y$ has a three-dimensional open box $B$ as target. If $h=h_{ij}(y)dy^i dy^j$, it is the pullback of the positive definite metric $\bar h=h_{ij}(y)dy^i dy^j$ on $B$. That metric has a unique Levi-Civita connection $\bar\nabla$, with the metric-compatibility and torsion-free identities ([[thm-fundamental-theorem-of-riemannian-geometry]], [[def-levi-civita-connection]]), and its curvature is defined by [[def-riemann-curvature-four-tensor]]. The identification of $\bar\nabla$ with the projected spacetime derivative on horizontal lifts is proved in step 4.2; no Riemannian connection on a nonintegrable hypersurface is assumed.


[F6] Exterior calculus: $d\alpha(X,Y)=X(\alpha(Y))-Y(\alpha(X))-\alpha([X,Y])$ for one-forms, $d(d\omega)=0$, and the Lie derivative of forms satisfies $\mathcal L_X\omega=d(\iota_X\omega)+\iota_X(d\omega)$ ([[def-exterior-derivative-by-the-invariant-vector-field-formula]], [[def-wedge-product-of-differential-forms]], [[thm-the-exterior-derivative-squares-to-zero]], [[thm-cartans-magic-formula]]).


[F7] Primitives: every closed smooth one-form on a star-shaped open domain has a smooth primitive, and every closed $C^1$ one-form on a nonempty path-connected simply connected open subset of $A$ has a $C^2$ primitive; a form is closed when $d\alpha=0$ ([[thm-poincare-lemma-for-differential-forms-on-star-shaped-domains]], [[lem-sr-closed-one-form-primitive-on-simply-connected-open-sets]], [[def-closed-and-exact-differential-forms]]).


[F8] Gram-Schmidt: a finite linearly independent list in a real inner product space has an orthonormal list with the same successive spans, obtained by the displayed recursion ([[thm-gram-schmidt-orthonormalisation]]); in particular a smooth positive definite matrix function with positive successive principal minors produces smooth orthonormal fields, because the recursion divides only by the positive pivot norms.


[F9] Choice-free inverse function theorem: in ZF, a smooth map on an open subset of $\mathbb R^n$ whose derivative at a point is invertible restricts to a diffeomorphism between open neighbourhoods, with inverse derivative $Dg(y)=Df(g(y))^{-1}$ ([[lem-choice-free-smooth-inverse-function-theorem-in-euclidean-space]]).


[F10] Lie derivative and brackets: $(\mathcal L_XT)(Y,Z)=X(T(Y,Z))-T([X,Y],Z)-T(Y,[X,Z])$ for a $(0,2)$ tensor field, $(\mathcal L_X\alpha)(Y)=X(\alpha(Y))-\alpha([X,Y])$ for a one-form, $\mathcal L_{fX}T=f\mathcal L_XT+df\otimes T(X,\cdot)+T(\cdot,X)\otimes df$, and $[X,Y]$ is the Lie bracket ([[def-lie-derivative-of-a-tensor-field]], [[def-lie-derivative-of-a-differential-form]], [[def-lie-derivative-of-a-vector-field]], [[def-lie-bracket-of-smooth-vector-fields]]).


[F11] Curves and integral curves: through every point of $\Omega$ there is a unique maximal integral curve of $n$, and a $C^1$ curve with $\gamma'=n\circ\gamma$ is smooth because $n$ is smooth and $s\mapsto n(\gamma(s))$ is then $C^1$, so $\gamma'$ is $C^1$ and $\gamma$ is $C^2$; iterating gives smoothness ([[def-integral-curve-of-a-vector-field]], [[thm-unique-maximal-integral-curve-through-each-point]], [[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]], [[def-derivative]]). Every such maximal curve with $n$ future unit is a future timelike worldline ([[def-sr-regular-causal-curve-and-worldline]], [[def-causal-classification-of-lorentz-vectors]], [[def-time-orientation-and-future-causal-sets]]).


[F12] Euclidean calculus in inertial or adapted coordinates. For maps $f:W\to Z\subseteq\mathbb R^n$, $g:Z\to\mathbb R^p$ on open Euclidean domains, total differentiability gives $D(g\circ f)(u)=Dg(f(u))\circ Df(u)$ ([[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]]); continuous first partials give total differentiability with Jacobian derivative ([[thm-continuous-partial-derivatives-imply-total-differentiability]]). Smoothness means continuous iterated coordinate partials of every order, componentwise for vector maps ([[def-ck-and-multi-index-notation-in-several-variables]]). The one-variable sum, product and quotient rules applied to each coordinate line give those partial-derivative rules ([[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]]); repeated differentiation preserves smooth sums, products and quotients on the open set where the denominator is nonzero. The total chain rule gives the first partials of a composite as finite sums of products of partials; induction using these rules gives smoothness of composites. Positive square roots are smooth locally by [F9] applied to the smooth map $u\mapsto u^2$ on $u>0$, whose derivative $2u$ is nonzero; the unique positive branches agree on overlaps. Smooth mixed partials commute by [[thm-young-mixed-partial-theorem]], applied to each two-coordinate restriction. Finally $\exp$ is smooth with $(\exp)'=\exp$ and is strictly positive ([[thm-derivative-of-exponential]], [[cor-exponential-is-a-bijection-onto-positive-reals]]), so $d(e^F)=e^F dF$ for a smooth real $F$ by the total chain rule.


[F13] Frobenius criterion for rest distributions: on an open set the rest distribution $\ker n^\flat$ is integrable if and only if $\omega=0$ there, if and only if $n^\flat\wedge d(n^\flat)=0$; when it is integrable, its integral hypersurfaces have tangent spaces equal to the pointwise rest spaces $n^\perp$ ([[thm-sr-local-congruence-adapted-charts-and-frobenius]], clause (iii)).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.


1.1 Clause (i): for a horizontal smooth $X$, [F2] gives $\eta(\nabla_Xn,\cdot)=\theta(X,\cdot)+\omega(X,\cdot)$. The standing Born-rigid hypothesis is $\theta=0$, so this equals $\omega(X,\cdot)$. Nondegeneracy of $\eta$ therefore gives $\nabla_Xn=0$ exactly when $\omega(X,\cdot)=0$, which under the same standing hypothesis is exactly the conjunction in clause (i). If also $\omega=0$ on $U$, the identity gives $\nabla_Xn=0$ for every horizontal $X$ there. No separation of a symmetric and a skew tensor from their value on only one fixed argument is used. [F2, given, algebra]


1.2 Flow box and slice metric: by [F3] choose a flow box $U$ of $n$ with adapted coordinates $(s,y^1,y^2,y^3)$ in which $n=\partial_s$; we may work on a coordinate product box; for a local primitive we shrink to a coordinate cube, whose coordinate image is star-shaped about its centre. This makes no assertion that its image under the adapted chart inverse is affine star-shaped. Since $n$ is future unit, $g_{ss}=\eta(\partial_s,\partial_s)=-1$. The tensor $h=\eta+n^\flat\otimes n^\flat$ has $h_{ss}=g_{ss}+(n^\flat_s)^2=-1+1=0$ and $h_{si}=g_{si}+n^\flat_sn^\flat_i=g_{si}-g_{si}=0$, and on the $y$-directions $h_{ij}=g_{ij}+g_{si}g_{sj}$; by [F2] the restriction of $h$ to $n^\perp$ is positive definite, so $(h_{ij})$ is a smooth positive definite matrix function of $(s,y)$. Born rigidity, $\mathcal L_nh=2\theta=0$ by [F2], reads $\partial_sh_{ij}=0$ in these coordinates, because in the frame $(\partial_s,\partial_{y^1},\partial_{y^2},\partial_{y^3})$ the Lie derivative of $h=\sum_{i,j}h_{ij}dy^idy^j$ along $\partial_s$ has components $\partial_sh_{ij}$ (the coordinate differentials $dy^i$ are $\partial_s$-invariant, [F10]); hence $h=h_{ij}(y)dy^idy^j=q^*\bar h$ is $s$-independent on $U$, where $q:(s,y)\mapsto y$ and $\bar h$ is a Riemannian metric on the open $y$-box $B$ of [F5]. [F2, F3, F5, F10, given, algebra]


1.3 Smooth orthonormal coframe of the slice metric: at every $y$ all leading principal minors of the positive definite matrix $(h_{ij}(y))$ are positive, and they are smooth hence continuous functions of $y$; applying the Gram-Schmidt recursion of [F8] to the coordinate vector fields $\partial_{y^1},\partial_{y^2},\partial_{y^3}$ with respect to $h$ therefore gives, on the whole box, smooth fields $E_i=\sum_kc_i{}^k(y)\partial_{y^k}$ whose coefficients are smooth functions of the $h_{ij}$ and positive pivot square roots, so that $h(E_i,E_j)=\delta_{ij}$. Let $b=(c^{-1})^{\mathsf T}$, a smooth invertible matrix function, and put $\theta^i:=\sum_kb^i{}_k(y)dy^k=h(E_i,\cdot)$; then $\sum_i\theta^i\otimes\theta^i=h$ and $\theta^i(E_j)=\delta^i_j$, so $(\theta^1,\theta^2,\theta^3)$ is a smooth $s$-independent $h$-orthonormal coframe of the slice metric, and $d\theta^i=-\bar\gamma^i{}_j\wedge\theta^j$ for unique smooth $s$-independent one-forms $\bar\gamma^i{}_j$ characterized by $\bar\gamma^i{}_j=-\bar\gamma^j{}_i$, namely the pullbacks by $q$ of the connection one-forms of $\bar\nabla$ on $(B,\bar h)$ in this coframe; their first structure equation follows because duality gives $\bar\nabla_X\theta^i=-\bar\gamma^i{}_j(X)\theta^j$ and torsion freeness gives $d\theta^i(X,Y)=(\bar\nabla_X\theta^i)(Y)-(\bar\nabla_Y\theta^i)(X)$. Finally set $\theta^0:=-n^\flat=ds-g_{si}dy^i$; then $\theta^0(n)=1$ and, expanding, $-(\theta^0)^2+\sum_i(\theta^i)^2=-ds^2+2g_{si}ds\,dy^i+g_{ij}dy^idy^j=\eta$ on $U$, so $(\theta^0,\theta^1,\theta^2,\theta^3)$ is a $\eta$-orthonormal coframe of $U$. [F2, F5, F8, F12, algebra]


1.4 Converse, the vorticity dichotomy: in inertial coordinates the Killing equation is $\partial_aK_b+\partial_bK_a=0$. Differentiating it with cyclically permuted indices and using commuting smooth mixed derivatives gives $2\partial_a\partial_bK_c=0$. Thus $\partial_aK_b=L_{ab}$ is constant on the connected open set $O$, $L_{ab}=-L_{ba}$, and $K$ is affine there. Indeed a function with zero first derivatives is locally constant by integration on coordinate segments, and locally constant functions are constant on a connected set; applying this first to $\partial_aK_b$ and then to $K_b-L_{ab}x^a$ proves the assertion. For $n=\varphi K$, $K^\flat=\varphi^{-1}n^\flat$; projecting $dn^\flat=d\varphi\wedge K^\flat+\varphi\,dK^\flat$ horizontally therefore gives $\omega=\varphi\,L|_{n^\perp}$. [F1, F2, F10, algebra]



2.1 Adapted frame: let $e_A$ be dual to $(\theta^0,\theta^1,\theta^2,\theta^3)$. Then $e_0=n$ and $e_i=E_i+d_i(s,y)n$ with the same $y$-coefficients as $E_i$ in step 1.3; the unique $d_i$ is fixed by $\theta^0(e_i)=0$. Thus $e_i$ is horizontal and projects by $q$ to the orthonormal field $E_i$ on $B$. Since $n=\partial_s$, $[n,e_i]=(\partial_s d_i)n$, with a positive derivative sign; in particular $\theta^j([n,e_i])=0$. From [F2] and $\theta=0$, $\nabla_{e_i}n=\omega(e_i,\cdot)^\sharp$ and $\nabla_nn=a$. [F1, F2, F10, step 1.3, given, algebra]


2.2 Suppose this vorticity vanishes at some $x_0\in O$, and set $k_0=K(x_0)$, which is timelike. The constant two-form $L$ vanishes on $k_0^\perp\times k_0^\perp$. Decomposing each vector into its $k_0$ and $k_0^\perp$ components shows that $L=k_0^\flat\wedge\beta$ for a covector $\beta$ with $\beta(k_0)=0$ (specifically $\beta(v)=L(k_0,v)/\eta(k_0,k_0)$). If $L=0$, the vorticity is identically zero. Otherwise $b=\beta^\sharp$ is nonzero spacelike and orthogonal to $k_0$, so $P=\operatorname{span}(k_0,b)$ is a nondegenerate Lorentzian two-plane. The linear part of the affine vector field $K$ has image in $P$, because its matrix is obtained by raising an index of $L$; hence $K(x)-K(x_0)\in P$ and $K(x)\in P$ for all $x\in O$. Since $K(x)$ is timelike, $P$ is the orthogonal sum of its line and a spacelike line in $K(x)^\perp$. The $P$-components of two vectors in $K(x)^\perp$ therefore lie in that same spacelike line, while $L$ vanishes on $P^\perp$; consequently $L|_{K(x)^\perp}=0$ and $\omega(x)=0$. Thus a zero at one point forces $\omega\equiv0$ on $O$; if there is no zero, it is nowhere zero. No decomposition along a possibly degenerate arbitrary two-plane is used. [F1, F2, F10, step 1.4, algebra]


2.3 Irrotational case, leaves: assume now $\theta=\omega=0$ on $\Omega$. By step 1.1, $\nabla_Xn=0$ for every vector field $X$ tangent to the rest distribution $D=\ker n^\flat$; in particular, in an inertial chart, all first partial derivatives of the components of $n$ along directions in $D$ vanish. The distribution $D$ is integrable by the Frobenius criterion [F13], because $\omega=0$. Let $S$ be a connected leaf of $D$ through a point $p$. For a $C^1$ curve $\gamma$ in $S$ the tangent $\gamma'$ takes values in $D\subseteq\ker n^\flat$ and $\frac{d}{dt}n(\gamma(t))=\nabla_{\gamma'}n=0$, so $n$ is constant on $S$ with value $n_0:=n(p)$; consequently the tangent space of $S$ at each of its points is $D=n_0^\perp$, and integrating the constant fields of $n_0^\perp$ shows that $S$ is an open subset of the affine hyperplane $p+n_0^\perp$. Choosing a maximal integral curve of $n$ through a point (its parameter has speed $1$, since $\eta(n,n)=-1$) and shrinking to a neighbourhood $U$ of one of its points over which the labels of the next step are unique, we obtain a smooth future timelike curve $z:J\to A$ with $z'=n\circ z$, $\eta(z',z')=-1$, such that the local leaf through $z(s)$ is an open subset of $z(s)+z'(s)^\perp$. [F2, F11, F13, given, algebra]

3.1 Irrotational case, the label and the formulas: on a small enough neighbourhood $U$ of a point of the curve consider $f(s,x):=\eta(z'(s),x-z(s))$, smooth with $f(s,z(s))=0$ and $\partial_sf(s,x)=1+\eta(z''(s),x-z(s))$ because $\eta(z'(s),z'(s))=-1$. At the label $s=\sigma(x)$ obtained below this derivative is $N(x):=1+\eta(z''(\sigma(x)),x-z(\sigma(x)))$; in particular $\partial_sf(s,z(s))=1>0$. In an inertial chart the map $(s,x)\mapsto(f(s,x),x)$ has at $(s,z(s))$ the block derivative $\begin{pmatrix}1&\partial_xf\\0&I\end{pmatrix}$, which is invertible; by the choice-free inverse function theorem [F9] there is a uniquely determined smooth function $\sigma$ with $f(\sigma(x),x)=0$ on a neighbourhood $U$, and shrinking $U$ keeps $N>0$ because $N(z(s))=1$ and $N$ is continuous. On $U$ the level sets of $\sigma$ are exactly the leaves: $\sigma$ is constant exactly along $D$, and each level is an open subset of a hyperplane $z(s)+z'(s)^\perp$. Differentiating $f(\sigma(x),x)=0$ in a direction $v$ gives $0=N(x)\,d\sigma(v)+\eta(z'(\sigma(x)),v)$, which is the displayed formula for $d\sigma$, and $n=z'\circ\sigma$ on $U$ because both are future unit vectors orthogonal to the same leaf. Finally $\nabla_vn=\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}z'(\sigma(x+tv))=z''(\sigma(x))\,d\sigma(v)$ gives $\nabla n=d\sigma\otimes(z''\circ\sigma)$, and $a=\nabla_nn=d\sigma(n)\,z''(\sigma)=z''(\sigma)/N$ because $d\sigma(n)=-\eta(z'(\sigma),n)/N=1/N$ as $\eta(z'(\sigma),n)=\eta(n,n)=-1$. [F2, F9, F11, F12, step 2.3, algebra]


3.2 Connection coefficients, entries with a $0$: define one-forms $\gamma^0{}_i=\gamma^i{}_0:=a_i\theta^0-\omega_{ij}\theta^j$ and $\gamma^i{}_j:=\bar\gamma^i{}_j-\omega_{ij}\theta^0$, where $a_i:=a^\flat(e_i)$ and $\omega_{ij}:=\omega(e_i,e_j)$. We claim that $\gamma^A{}_B(X)=\theta^A(\nabla_Xe_B)$ for all $A,B$; since the right-hand side is the connection matrix of the Levi-Civita connection in this frame by [F4], this identifies $\gamma$ and gives in particular the skew relations $\gamma^0{}_i=\gamma^i{}_0$ and $\gamma^i{}_j=-\gamma^j{}_i$. For the entries with a zero index: $\theta^0(\nabla_ne_i)=a_i$, because $0=n(\eta(n,e_i))=(\nabla_nn^\flat)(e_i)+n^\flat(\nabla_ne_i)$ gives $n^\flat(\nabla_ne_i)=-a_i$ and $\theta^0=-n^\flat$; $\theta^i(\nabla_ne_j)=\theta^i(\nabla_{e_j}n)+\theta^i([n,e_j])=\omega(e_j,e_i)+0=-\omega_{ij}$ by step 2.1; and $\theta^0(\nabla_{e_j}e_i)=\omega_{ji}=-\omega_{ij}$, because $0=e_j(\eta(n,e_i))=(\nabla_{e_j}n^\flat)(e_i)+n^\flat(\nabla_{e_j}e_i)=\omega_{ji}+n^\flat(\nabla_{e_j}e_i)$. Thus $\gamma^0{}_i(e_0)=\gamma^i{}_0(e_0)=a_i$ and $\gamma^0{}_i(e_j)=\gamma^i{}_0(e_j)=-\omega_{ij}$. [F2, F4, step 2.1, algebra]


4.1 Irrotational case, converse: conversely let $z:J\to A$ be smooth with $\eta(z',z')=-1$, let $\sigma$ be a smooth function on a tubular neighbourhood $U$ whose level sets are $z(s)+z'(s)^\perp$ with $N>0$, and put $n:=z'\circ\sigma$; then $n$ is smooth and future unit, its rest distribution is the tangent distribution of the $\sigma$-levels, which is integrable, and for $X$ tangent to a level one has $\nabla_Xn=\nabla_X(z'\circ\sigma)=z''(\sigma)\,d\sigma(X)=0$ because $d\sigma$ vanishes on the level directions. By the computation of step 1.1 this gives $\theta(X,Y)+\omega(X,Y)=0$ for all horizontal fields $X,Y$; the rest distribution is integrable, so $\omega=0$ by the Frobenius criterion [F13]; hence $\theta(X,Y)=0$ for all horizontal $X,Y$, that is, $\theta=0$. Thus $n$ is a smooth future unit irrotational Born-rigid congruence satisfying the same formulas $\nabla n=d\sigma\otimes(z''\circ\sigma)$, $a=z''(\sigma)/N$ and $N=1+\eta(z''(\sigma),x-z(\sigma))$ as in step 3.1, with the label derivative $1+\eta(z''\circ\sigma,x-z\circ\sigma)=N$ positive on $U$. [F2, F12, F13, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra]


4.2 Horizontal connection entries: for horizontal lifts $X,Y,Z$ of fields $\bar X,\bar Y,\bar Z$ on $B$, the functions $\eta(Y,Z)=q^*\bar h(\bar Y,\bar Z)$ are basic, $q_*[X,Y]=[\bar X,\bar Y]$, and the vertical parts of the brackets pair to zero with $Z$. Metric compatibility and torsion freeness of [F1] give, by adding their three cyclic identities, $$2\eta(\nabla_XY,Z)=X\eta(Y,Z)+Y\eta(Z,X)-Z\eta(X,Y)-\eta(X,[Y,Z])+\eta(Y,[Z,X])+\eta(Z,[X,Y]).$$ Every term is the pullback of the same expression on $(B,\bar h)$. The unique connection $\bar\nabla$ of [F5] satisfies that expression by the same metric/torsion calculation. Thus $h(\nabla_XY)$ is the horizontal lift of $\bar\nabla_{\bar X}\bar Y$, by positive definiteness on the horizontal space. This also proves that its coefficients are basic. Taking $X=e_j,Y=e_k,Z=e_i$ gives $\theta^i(\nabla_{e_j}e_k)=\bar\gamma^i{}_k(e_j)$, so together with step 3.2 the displayed $\gamma^A{}_B$ are exactly the spacetime connection forms. This comparison uses the local quotient $B$, not an integral leaf of the rotational rest distribution. [F1, F2, F5, step 1.3, step 2.1, step 3.2, algebra]


4.3 First structure equation and the differential of $\theta^0$: for the coframe of step 1.3 and the connection forms of steps 3.2 and 4.2 one has $d\theta^A=-\gamma^A{}_B\wedge\theta^B$. Indeed, metric compatibility of $\nabla$ gives $X(\theta^A(e_B))=(\nabla_X\theta^A)(e_B)+\theta^A(\nabla_Xe_B)$, so $(\nabla_X\theta^A)(e_B)=-\gamma^A{}_B(X)$ and $\nabla_X\theta^A=-\gamma^A{}_B(X)\theta^B$; substituting into the invariant formula for $d\theta^A$ of [F6] and using torsion-freeness of $\nabla$ cancels the torsion term and yields $d\theta^A(X,Y)=-(\gamma^A{}_B\wedge\theta^B)(X,Y)$. In particular, using $\gamma^0{}_i=a_i\theta^0-\omega_{ij}\theta^j$ and writing $a^\flat=a_i\theta^i$, $\omega=\tfrac12\omega_{ij}\theta^i\wedge\theta^j$, we obtain $d\theta^0=-\gamma^0{}_i\wedge\theta^i=-\theta^0\wedge a^\flat-2\omega$, because $-\gamma^0{}_i\wedge\theta^i=-a_i\theta^0\wedge\theta^i+\omega_{ij}\theta^j\wedge\theta^i$ and $\omega_{ij}\theta^j\wedge\theta^i=-2\omega$ by antisymmetry of $\omega$. [F4, F6, step 3.2, algebra]


5.1 Mixed identity: in inertial coordinates, flatness on the covector $n_c$ gives $\nabla_a\nabla_bn_c-\nabla_b\nabla_an_c=0$. Substitute $\nabla_bn_c=-n_ba_c+\omega_{bc}$ and differentiate with the product rule: $$0=-(\nabla_an_b-\nabla_bn_a)a_c-n_b\nabla_aa_c+n_a\nabla_ba_c+\nabla_a\omega_{bc}-\nabla_b\omega_{ac}.$$ Project all three free indices onto the rest space. The two terms with explicit $n_a$ or $n_b$ vanish; the first term becomes $-2\omega_{ab}a_c$. The acceleration is horizontal, so its projected covector is $a^\flat$ itself, not zero. Therefore $\hat\nabla_a\omega_{bc}-\hat\nabla_b\omega_{ac}=2\omega_{ab}a_c$. No commutation of differentiation along the variable field $n$ is required. In the adapted frame this is $D_m\omega_{ij}-D_i\omega_{mj}=2\omega_{mi}a_j$, where $D_m\omega_{ij}=e_m(\omega_{ij})-\bar\gamma^k{}_i(e_m)\omega_{kj}-\bar\gamma^k{}_j(e_m)\omega_{ik}$: the vertical parts of $\nabla_{e_m}e_i$ pair to zero with the horizontal $\omega$, and step 4.2 supplies the horizontal coefficients. [F1, F2, F5, step 3.2, step 4.2, algebra]


5.2 Horizontal flatness equation: by [F4] the curvature two-forms of $\nabla$ in this frame are $\Omega^A{}_B=d\gamma^A{}_B+\gamma^A{}_C\wedge\gamma^C{}_B$, and $\Omega^A{}_B(X,Y)=\theta^A(R(X,Y)e_B)=0$ by flatness of $\nabla$ [F1, F4]; hence $\Omega^A{}_B=0$. Expanding $\Omega^i{}_j=d\gamma^i{}_j+\gamma^i{}_0\wedge\gamma^0{}_j+\gamma^i{}_k\wedge\gamma^k{}_j$ with steps 3.2 and 4.3 gives $d\gamma^i{}_j=d\bar\gamma^i{}_j-d\omega_{ij}\wedge\theta^0+\omega_{ij}\theta^0\wedge a^\flat+2\omega_{ij}\omega$, $\gamma^i{}_0\wedge\gamma^0{}_j=(a_i\theta^0-\omega_{ik}\theta^k)\wedge(a_j\theta^0-\omega_{jl}\theta^l)=-a_i\omega_{jl}\theta^0\wedge\theta^l+\omega_{ik}a_j\theta^0\wedge\theta^k+\omega_{ik}\omega_{jl}\theta^k\wedge\theta^l$ and $\gamma^i{}_k\wedge\gamma^k{}_j=\bar\gamma^i{}_k\wedge\bar\gamma^k{}_j+\omega_{kj}\theta^0\wedge\bar\gamma^i{}_k-\omega_{ik}\theta^0\wedge\bar\gamma^k{}_j$; the components with two horizontal legs therefore satisfy $0=\bar\Omega{}^i{}_j+2\omega_{ij}\omega+\omega_{ik}\omega_{jl}\theta^k\wedge\theta^l$, where $\bar\Omega{}^i{}_j=d\bar\gamma^i{}_j+\bar\gamma^i{}_k\wedge\bar\gamma^k{}_j$ is the curvature two-form of the slice metric $h$ in the coframe $(\theta^i)$. [F1, F4, step 3.2, step 4.3, algebra]


6.1 Curvature of the slice and the three-dimensional identity: by [F5] and step 4.2 the two-form $\bar\Omega{}^i{}_j$ is the pullback of the curvature of $\bar\nabla$ on $(B,\bar h)$, so $\bar\Omega{}^i{}_j=\tfrac12\bar R^i{}_{jkl}\theta^k\wedge\theta^l$ with $\bar R^i{}_{jkl}=\theta^i(R_{\bar h}(E_k,E_l)E_j)$ in the argument order of the Riemann four-tensor of [F5], and we write $\bar R_{ijkl}$ for $\bar R^i{}_{jkl}$ (indices are moved with the Euclidean slice metric). Comparing the antisymmetrized $\theta^k\wedge\theta^l$ components of step 5.2 gives $\bar R^i{}_{jkl}+2\omega_{ij}\omega_{kl}+\omega_{ik}\omega_{jl}-\omega_{il}\omega_{jk}=0$ for all $i,j,k,l$. For the antisymmetric matrix $(\omega_{ij})$ on the three-dimensional quotient write $\omega_{ij}=\varepsilon_{ijs}w^s$ with $\varepsilon$ the alternating symbol and $w$ the associated vector, unique because the slice metric is Euclidean; expanding the product of two alternating symbols and contracting with $w^sw^t$ gives $\omega_{ik}\omega_{jl}-\omega_{il}\omega_{jk}=\omega_{ij}\omega_{kl}$ for all $i,j,k,l$, both sides being equal to $-\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}|w|^2+\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}|w|^2+\delta_{il}w_kw_j-\delta_{ik}w_lw_j+w_i\delta_{jk}w_l-w_i\delta_{jl}w_k$. Substituting this identity into the previous equation gives $\bar R^i{}_{jkl}=-3\omega_{ij}\omega_{kl}$ for all $i,j,k,l$. [F5, step 4.2, step 5.2, algebra]


7.1 The frame components of the vorticity are independent of $s$: since $h$, and hence its connection forms $\bar\gamma^i{}_j$ and curvature $\bar R^i{}_{jkl}$, are independent of $s$ by steps 1.2 and 1.3, differentiating the equation $\bar R^i{}_{jkl}=-3\omega_{ij}\omega_{kl}$ of step 6.1 along $\partial_s$ gives $0=-3(\partial_s\omega_{ij}\omega_{kl}+\omega_{ij}\partial_s\omega_{kl})$. Fix a point of $U$, in which $\omega$ is nowhere zero, so there is a pair of distinct indices $(k,l)$ with $\omega_{kl}\ne0$; taking $(i,j)=(k,l)$ in the displayed identity gives $2\omega_{kl}\partial_s\omega_{kl}=0$, hence $\partial_s\omega_{kl}=0$, and then taking $(i,j)$ arbitrary gives $\partial_s\omega_{ij}\omega_{kl}=0$, hence $\partial_s\omega_{ij}=0$ for every pair. Therefore $\mathcal L_n\omega=0$: by [F10], $(\mathcal L_n\omega)(e_i,e_j)=n(\omega_{ij})-\omega([n,e_i],e_j)-\omega(e_i,[n,e_j])=\partial_s\omega_{ij}$, since $[n,e_i]$ is a multiple of $n$ by step 2.1 and $\omega$ is horizontal by [F2]. Thus the vorticity is basic on $U$. [F2, F10, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.1, step 6.1, algebra]


8.1 The acceleration is basic: by steps 1.3, 7.1 and 5.1 the functions $D_m\omega_{ij}$ are independent of $s$ on $U$, because they are obtained by applying the $s$-independent data $\theta^i,\bar\gamma^k{}_i$ to the $s$-independent component functions $\omega_{ij}$ (steps 1.3 and 7.1) together with the fields $e_m$, whose coefficients along $\partial_{y^k}$ are $s$-independent by step 2.1. Differentiating the identity $D_m\omega_{ij}-D_i\omega_{mj}=2\omega_{mi}a_j$ of step 5.1 along $\partial_s$ therefore gives $0=2(\partial_s\omega_{mi}a_j+\omega_{mi}\partial_sa_j)=2\omega_{mi}\partial_sa_j$ by step 7.1; since $\omega$ is nowhere zero, for each $j$ we may choose a pair $(m,i)$ with $\omega_{mi}\ne0$ and conclude $\partial_sa_j=0$. Hence $\mathcal L_na^\flat=0$, by the same computation as in step 7.1 applied to the horizontal one-form $a^\flat$: $(\mathcal L_na^\flat)(e_j)=\partial_sa_j$. Thus the acceleration is basic on $U$. [F2, F10, step 1.3, step 2.1, step 7.1, step 5.1, algebra]


9.1 The acceleration form is closed: applying $d$ to the identity $d\theta^0=-\theta^0\wedge a^\flat-2\omega$ of step 4.3 and using $d^2=0$ [F6] gives $0=d(d\theta^0)=-d\theta^0\wedge a^\flat+\theta^0\wedge da^\flat-2d\omega=2\omega\wedge a^\flat+\theta^0\wedge da^\flat-2d\omega$, where the term $\theta^0\wedge a^\flat\wedge a^\flat$ vanished by alternation. Contracting with $n$ and using $\iota_n\theta^0=1$, $\iota_n\omega=\iota_na^\flat=0$ (horizontality, [F2]) and Cartan's formula [F6] gives $0=2\iota_n(\omega\wedge a^\flat)+\iota_n(\theta^0\wedge da^\flat)-2\iota_nd\omega=da^\flat-2\mathcal L_n\omega$, because $\iota_n(\omega\wedge a^\flat)=(\iota_n\omega)\wedge a^\flat+\omega\wedge(\iota_na^\flat)=0$, while $\iota_n(\theta^0\wedge da^\flat)=(\iota_n\theta^0)da^\flat-\theta^0\wedge\iota_nda^\flat=da^\flat$ and $\iota_nda^\flat=\mathcal L_na^\flat-d(\iota_na^\flat)=0$ by step 8.1. Since $\mathcal L_n\omega=0$ by step 7.1, this gives $da^\flat=0$ on $U$. [F2, F6, step 4.3, step 7.1, step 8.1, algebra]


10.1 Killing field on the rotational box: first, for any smooth future unit timelike field the identity $\mathcal L_n\eta=2\theta-(n^\flat\otimes a^\flat+a^\flat\otimes n^\flat)$ holds: by [F10] and [F1], $(\mathcal L_n\eta)(Y,Z)=\eta(\nabla_Yn,Z)+\eta(Y,\nabla_Zn)=\nabla_Yn^\flat(Z)+\nabla_Zn^\flat(Y)$, and substituting the decomposition of [F2] for horizontal and general $Y,Z$ gives $\nabla_Yn^\flat(Z)+\nabla_Zn^\flat(Y)=2\theta(Y,Z)-n^\flat(Y)a^\flat(Z)-n^\flat(Z)a^\flat(Y)$. Since here $\theta=0$, $\mathcal L_n\eta=-(n^\flat\otimes a^\flat+a^\flat\otimes n^\flat)$. By step 9.1 the smooth one-form $a^\flat$ is closed on $U$; on a coordinate cube pull the closed form back to its star-shaped coordinate image, take the smooth primitive supplied by [F7], and transport it back to obtain $F$, and if $U$ is connected and simply connected let $F$ be the smooth primitive supplied by the smooth clause of [F7]; in both cases $a^\flat=dF$. Put $f:=e^F>0$ and $K:=fn=e^Fn$. By [F10], $\mathcal L_K\eta=\mathcal L_{fn}\eta=f\mathcal L_n\eta+df\otimes n^\flat+n^\flat\otimes df$; since $df=fdF=fa^\flat$, the four terms cancel and $\mathcal L_K\eta=0$. Thus $K$ is a Killing field, and $\nabla_aK_b+\nabla_bK_a=(\mathcal L_K\eta)_{ab}=0$ by [F10] and torsion-freeness of $\nabla$ [F1]; $K$ is timelike and future because $f>0$ and $n$ is future unit. [F1, F2, F7, F10, F12, step 7.1, step 9.1, algebra]


11.1 Converse, Born rigidity: let $K$ be a future timelike Killing field on a connected open set $O$, so $\mathcal L_K\eta=0$ and $\eta(K,K)<0$; put $\varphi:=(-\eta(K,K))^{-1/2}>0$ and $n:=\varphi K$, a smooth future unit field. Put $f:=1/\varphi>0$, so $K=fn$. By [F10], $0=\mathcal L_K\eta=\mathcal L_{fn}\eta=f\mathcal L_n\eta+df\otimes n^\flat+n^\flat\otimes df$, and by step 10.1, $\mathcal L_n\eta=2\theta-(n^\flat\otimes a^\flat+a^\flat\otimes n^\flat)$ where $\theta,a$ are the expansion and acceleration of $n$. Evaluating on two smooth horizontal fields $Y,Z$ kills the terms containing $n^\flat$ and gives $0=2f\,\theta(Y,Z)$; since $f>0$, $\theta=0$ and $n$ is Born rigid. [F1, F2, F10, step 10.1, algebra]

## Remarks

- **The sign conventions carried by this item.** With $\theta^0=-n^\flat$, the connection forms are $\gamma^0{}_i=\gamma^i{}_0=a_i\theta^0-\omega_{ij}\theta^j$ and $\gamma^i{}_j=\bar\gamma^i{}_j-\omega_{ij}\theta^0$. Their horizontal flatness equation gives $\bar R^i{}_{jkl}=-3\omega_{ij}\omega_{kl}$ in three spatial dimensions. Direct horizontal projection of the flat second derivative of $n^\flat$ gives $\hat\nabla_a\omega_{bc}-\hat\nabla_b\omega_{ac}=2\omega_{ab}a_c$. The quotient is only the open $y$-box of an adapted chart; no global orbit-space regularity is assumed.


- **Local scope.** The theorem does not assert that a rotational rigid motion exists on a prescribed domain, nor that the Killing field produced on a flow box extends; no global quotient of the flow is formed, and no upper bound on the spatial size of a rigid body follows from these local formulas. This is the honest local form of the Herglotz-Noether theorem; the analyticity hypothesis used in some textbook presentations is not needed.


- **What "basic" means here.** A horizontal tensor on the flow box is called basic when its components in the adapted frame $(e_1,e_2,e_3)$ are independent of $s$; for the vorticity and acceleration this is equivalent to $\mathcal L_n\omega=0$ and $\mathcal L_na^\flat=0$ by [F10] and the fact that $[n,e_i]$ is a multiple of $n$ (step 2.1).
