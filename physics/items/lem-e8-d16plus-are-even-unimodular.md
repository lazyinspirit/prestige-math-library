## Statement

For the coordinate models in [[def-e8-and-d16plus-rank16-lattices]], $E_8$ and $D_{16}^{+}$ are full lattices in their stated positive-definite Euclidean spaces; both are integral, even, and unimodular. Their orthogonal direct sum $E_8\oplus E_8$ is also a positive-definite even unimodular lattice of rank $16$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The coordinate definitions of $D_n$, $h_n$, $E_8$, $D_{16}^{+}$ and $E_8\oplus E_8$ from [[def-e8-and-d16plus-rank16-lattices]], for $n=8$ or $16$.

[F1] A subgroup of a finite free module over a PID is free with a basis aligned to an ambient basis ([[thm-simultaneous-basis-theorem-for-pid-submodules]]).

[F2] A full-rank subgroup of $\mathbb Z^n$ generated by a square integer matrix $A$ has index $|\det A|$ ([[cor-index-of-a-full-rank-integer-sublattice-is-the-absolute-determinant]]).

[F3] For a full-rank lattice with basis matrix $A$, its covolume is $|\det A|$, its dual is $A^{-T}\mathbb Z^n$, and $\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda^*)=\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)^{-1}$ ([[def-full-rank-lattice-covolume-and-dual-lattice]]).

[L1] In $\mathbb R^n$, write $D_n=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:\sum_i z_i\in2\mathbb Z\}$ and $h_n=(1/2,\ldots,1/2)$. For even $n$, set $\Lambda_n=D_n\cup(D_n+h_n)$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 For $n=8$ or $16$, the homomorphism $\epsilon:\mathbb Z^n\to\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$, $\epsilon(z)=\sum_i z_i\pmod2$, is onto (it sends $e_1$ to $1$), and its kernel is $D_n$; hence $[\mathbb Z^n:D_n]=2$. The vectors $e_i-e_n$ for $1\le i<n$ and $e_{n-1}+e_n$ lie in $D_n$ and are linearly independent, so $D_n$ has rank $n$. By [F1], it has a $\mathbb Z$-basis, and [F2] gives its index as the absolute determinant of the basis matrix; [F3] identifies this with covolume, so $\operatorname{covol}(D_n)=2\operatorname{covol}(\mathbb Z^n)=2$. [F1, F2, F3, L1, algebra]

2.1 Since $2h_n=(1,\ldots,1)\in D_n$ for even $n$, $\Lambda_n$ is the subgroup consisting of the two distinct cosets $D_n$ and $D_n+h_n$. It lies in $(1/2)\mathbb Z^n$, and $2\Lambda_n$ is a subgroup of $\mathbb Z^n$ containing $2D_n$, so it has rank $n$. By [F1], choose a basis of $2\Lambda_n$ as a subgroup of $\mathbb Z^n$; halving that basis gives a basis of $\Lambda_n$, proving it is full. Its index over $D_n$ is $2$. Writing a basis of $D_n$ in a basis of $\Lambda_n$ and applying [F2] gives the absolute determinant of that inclusion matrix as $2$; [F3] makes this the covolume ratio, so $\operatorname{covol}(D_n)=2\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda_n)$ and $\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda_n)=1$. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, L1, algebra]

3.1 If $d\in D_n$, then $\|d\|^2=\sum_i d_i^2\equiv\sum_i d_i\equiv0\pmod2$, since $d_i^2\equiv d_i\pmod2$. For $d+h_n$, $\|d+h_n\|^2=\sum_i d_i^2+\sum_i d_i+n/4$; the first two sums have even total by the same congruence, and $n/4$ is $2$ or $4$. Thus every vector in $\Lambda_n$ has even integer norm. Polarization gives $2\langle x,y\rangle=\|x+y\|^2-\|x\|^2-\|y\|^2\in2\mathbb Z$ for $x,y\in\Lambda_n$, so $\Lambda_n$ is integral. [step 2.1, L1, algebra]

4.1 Integrality implies $\Lambda_n\subseteq\Lambda_n^*$. By [F3] and step 2.1, both full lattices have covolume $1$; applying [F2] in a basis of $\Lambda_n^*$ shows their inclusion index is $1$, hence $\Lambda_n=\Lambda_n^*$. This proves that $E_8=\Lambda_8$ and $D_{16}^{+}=\Lambda_{16}$ are positive even unimodular lattices. [F2, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, L1]

5.1 The product inner product on $\mathbb R^8\oplus\mathbb R^8$ is positive definite, and the direct sum of two full lattices is full. A vector $(x,y)\in E_8\oplus E_8$ has norm $\|x\|^2+\|y\|^2\in2\mathbb Z$, so the sum is even and integral. Its dual is $E_8^*\oplus E_8^*=E_8\oplus E_8$, since testing against $(u,0)$ and $(0,v)$ gives the two coordinate dual conditions. Thus the direct sum is positive even unimodular of rank $16$. [step 4.1, L1, algebra] ∎
