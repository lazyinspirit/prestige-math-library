**Primary source and reading limits.** Henry Cavendish's original manuscripts as
edited by J. Clerk Maxwell, *The Electrical Researches of the Honourable Henry
Cavendish* (Cambridge 1879), 'Experimental determination of the law of electric
force', arts. 217-235, printed pp. 104-113, with the detector and electrometer
descriptions arts. 244-249, printed pp. 119-121; read in the Internet Archive
OCR of `electricalresea00cavegoog`. The original apparatus, procedure, reported
observations and sensitivity check are identifiable, but the fractional
calibration ratios and the exponent computation are corrupted by the OCR and no
numerical exponent limit is transcribed or recomputed here. The account below
is the report's descriptions and observations structured by the record contract
of [[def-em-experiment-record-contract]]; it is a reported finite-resolution
null, not a proof that a true charge is exactly zero.

## Setup

A conducting inner globe 12.1 inches in diameter, supported by a glass stick
coated with sealing wax, enclosed between two tinfoil-coated pasteboard
hemispheres 13.3 inches in diameter with a small clearance, the two hemispheres
applied to each other to form a complete sphere; a removable wire connected the
inner globe and the hemispheres, and could be drawn out by a silk string;
linked strings operated the apparatus so that the charging wire, the inner
connecting wire and the hemispheres were successively separated (paragraphs
219-222). A coated glass jar and an electrometer monitored and repeated the
charging level through a separate electrometer fastened to the prime conductor
(paragraphs 224-225). The inner-globe detector was a pair of pith balls
about one-fifth of an inch in diameter on fine linen threads (paragraphs
220-222 and 244). The paper-cylinder electrometer of paragraph 248 monitored
charging; the later straw electrometer with cork balls, suspended from the
prime conductor, served that monitoring role in the subsequent charge-comparison
experiments (paragraph 249), rather than replacing the inner-globe detector in
the reported null tests. A second experiment enclosed
a wooden piece between two wooden drawers forming a hollow box (paragraph 235).

## Procedure

The hemispheres were charged from a Leyden vial through a wire, which was then
withdrawn; immediately afterwards the wire connecting the inner globe and the
hemispheres was drawn out by the silk string, the hemispheres were separated
without touching the inner globe, and the outer shells and charging wire were
discharged; a pith-ball detector was then brought to the inner globe
(paragraphs 220-222 and 226). For greater sensitivity the pith balls were
precharged positively before the charge, the comparison being made against the
negative-precharge case at monitored equal charging levels (paragraphs
228-229). For the sensitivity control the hemispheres and their frames were
removed, leaving the globe and pith balls. A smaller coated glass element was
charged to the monitored level and its charge transferred to the initially
uncharged main jar; that jar then charged the exposed globe. After withdrawal
of the charging wire the precharged pith balls were compared (paragraph 230).
The report's fractional charge comparisons are unusable in this OCR; no
calibrated charge value is assigned here.

## Observations

Several repetitions without perceived pith-ball separation or signs of residual
inner charge (paragraph 227); in the precharged-ball comparison, equal small
residual separations for positive and negative precharge rather than a
detectable charge-sign asymmetry (paragraphs 228-229); in the sensitivity
control, the small deliberately imposed charge was visibly distinguished by
the same detector (paragraph 230); and a corresponding qualitative null with
the enclosed-box geometry (paragraph 235). The reported 'must be inversely as
the square' inference (paragraph 232) is recorded as the author's conclusion
from the null under his theory, not as a modern theorem.

## Uncertainty

The read passages supply charge-level monitoring, the precharge comparison, a
deliberate sensitivity check and the pith-ball and electrometer construction,
but no calibrated charge transfer function, no trial-level sampling model, no
confidence interval and no modern SI traceability. Insulation leakage, finite
separation time, imperfect spherical geometry, shell holes, accidental contact
and outside charges are apparatus-model and systematic conditions. The OCR
cannot faithfully recover all fractional calibration ratios or the exponent
calculation, so no numerical exponent bound, precision, likelihood or certainty
is invented.

## Interpretation

An ideal inverse-square enclosed-conductor equilibrium model predicts zero
charge in the connected enclosed interior under its idealization, and the
reported observations support that model within the apparatus sensitivity and
auxiliary assumptions; they constrain specified alternative force laws only
after an explicit response and sensitivity model, which the read passages do
not supply in numerically usable form. A finite null cannot prove an exact
exponent, and the empirical support is a nonlogical relation to the static
prediction.
