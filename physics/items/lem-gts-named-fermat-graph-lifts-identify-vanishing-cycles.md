## Statement

Assume AC. For $N=4,5$, put $n=N-2$, $M=\{\prod_i u_i=1\}\subset(\mathbb C^*)^N$, $W=\sum_i u_i$ and $M^0=W^{-1}(0)$. The explicit graph-sphere lifts in the declared compact relative model are categorically isomorphic, with the actual brane grading, to the distinguished vanishing spheres of $W$. The auxiliary projection $\varpi=u_1$ has actual outward dual thimbles and a finite directed inward-thimble category; the closed finite bar augmentation generates every compact probe in the chosen slit sector. Its chosen-character clean matching cone identifies the extreme two-object subcategory, and graded Kronecker uniqueness gives the object isomorphisms. They extend to the complete relative $\mathbb C[\![q]\!]$ category and the source compact coefficient category. The result is a categorical identification, not a claim of Hamiltonian isotopy of the graph spheres to the named cycles.

1.1 **Auxiliary critical points and positive real thimbles** Eliminate $u_N$ by $∏u_i=1$. For fixed $u_1=v$, the critical point equations of varpi on $W=0$ force $u_2=⋯=u_N=a, v a^{(N−1)}=1$ and $v+(N−1)a=0$. Hence
$v^N=(−1)^{(N−1)}(N−1)^{(N−1)},    a=−v/(N−1)$.
The Hessian on the sum-zero variations among $u_2,…,u_N$ is a nonzero multiple of the standard nondegenerate quadratic form; these N auxiliary critical points are Morse.
The pair-of-pants quotient coordinates reduce the positive real chamber to $z=(z_2,…,z_{(N−1)})∈(R_{>0})^n$ and
$f(z)=(1+∑z_j)^{(N−1)}/∏z_j,    v^N=(−1)^{(N−1)}f(z)$.
Its complete topology follows from strict convexity, not merely from uniqueness of a critical point. Put $z_j=\operatorname{exp}(t_j), g=\operatorname{log} f$. With $w_j=\operatorname{exp}(t_j)/(1+∑\operatorname{exp}(t))$, the Hessian is $(N−1)(diag(w)−ww^T)$. For any nonzero real vector b its quadratic value is (N−1) times the variance of the values $0,b_2,…,b_{(N−1)}$ with strictly positive weights $w_0,w_2,…,w_{(N−1)}$; it is positive. The gradient vanishes exactly when $w_j=1/(N−1)$, hence all $z_j=1$. The minimum is $f_{\operatorname{min}}=(N−1)^{(N−1)}$.
The function is proper. For a unit direction b and $ρ→∞,
g(ρb)=ρ[(N−1)\operatorname{max}(0,b_j)−∑b_j]+O(1)$.
The coefficient is strictly positive for every nonzero b: if the maximum is zero, at least one $b_j$ is negative; if it is positive, there are only N−2 summands, each at most that maximum. Compactness of the unit sphere gives a uniform positive lower bound. Along each ray from its unique minimum, strict convexity makes g strictly increasing. Every level above the minimum is thus a smooth radial graph over $S^{(n−1)}$, while the minimum is one point. Each of the N choices of v above this chamber gives an $R^n$ thimble $U_ζ$ projecting to the ray from its auxiliary critical value ζ outward.
For the symplectic assertion use conjugation rather than a complex-linearity assertion for a real metric. Every point of $U_ζ$ has first coordinate of phase arg ζ and other coordinates of phase arg ζ+π. The involution
$u_i ↦ \operatorname{exp}(2i\operatorname{arg} ζ)\bar u_i$
preserves $∏u_i=1$ and $W=0$, because exp(2Ni arg ζ)=1, and is anti-symplectic for the source real invariant Kähler data. $U_ζ$ is a component of its fixed locus and hence is Lagrangian. $q=π∑c_j f_j,    c_j=a_j−b,    0≤a_j,b≤1$.
In particular max c−min c≤1. The argument of $U_ζ$ is the single point
$θ_ζ=(α,α+π,…,α+π)$ mod 2π,    Nα=(N−1)π mod 2π.
If a representative q of that point belongs to $Z_N$, then $q_j−q_1=π(c_j−c_1)$ is π modulo 2π. Since $|c_j−c_1|≤1$, each difference is 1 or −1. They must all have the same sign σ, since using both would give range two. Hence $c_j=c_1+σ$ for $j>1$. Substituting in $q_1$ gives
$q_1/π=(c_1−(N−2)σ)/N$.
For $σ=1, c_1∈[−1,0]$, so $q_1/π∈[−(N−1)/N,−(N−2)/N]$. For $σ=−1, c_1∈[0,1]$, giving the opposite interval. Among the N critical arguments, spaced by 2π/N, only the two extreme values α=±(N−1)π/N lie in these intervals. Every other thimble argument is a positive distance from the compact zonotope.
The first coordinate of every point of $Z_N$ lies between −(N−1)π/N and (N−1)π/N: sum the positive and negative first-coordinate contributions of its defining segments. Thus its sufficiently close graph lift also has varpi-image inside the slit plane $S=\mathbb C^*\setminus\mathbb R_{≤0}$. Here is the required uniform phase estimate for the actual graph, rather than an assumption about its argument image. In the cotangent chart of [F2, proof 1.10–1.11], choose a unit homogeneous lift of its map near the real sphere $S=\{\sum x_i=0,\sum x_i^2=1\}$. Its expansion is $z_\epsilon(x)=x+i\epsilon\nabla f(x)+O(\epsilon^2)$ uniformly in $x$, with the positive normal normalization of that chart. Compactness gives a fixed neighborhood of that locus where $\operatorname{Im}(z_\epsilon)_i>0$ for all sufficiently small positive $\epsilon$. Away from that neighborhood the real part has the sign of $x_i$. Consequently every coordinate has a continuous argument $\beta_i\in(-\delta_\epsilon,\pi+\delta_\epsilon)$ with $\delta_\epsilon\to0$: use the upper-half-plane branch near $x_i=0$, and the branches near $0$ and $\pi$ on the positive and negative regions. On the centred lift to the product-one cover the first $N-1$ arguments are $q_j=\beta_j-\bar\beta$; the other sheets add their constant deck translate. The limit $x$ has at least one positive and one negative coordinate because its sum is zero and its norm is one. At these coordinates respectively $a_i=0$ and $a_j=1$. Thus $q/\pi$ is the projection of $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_N)$ by subtracting its mean. Writing $c_j=a_j-a_N$ gives exactly $q=\pi\sum_{j<N}c_jf_j$ in the displayed zonotope. Moreover the difference of its $i$th and $j$th coordinates has absolute value $\pi$, attaining the supporting bound of that zonotope; hence $q\in\partial Z_N$. If the distances to $\partial Z_N$ did not tend uniformly to zero, such a convergent subsequence would give a limit a fixed positive distance away, a contradiction. 3.1 **Actual matching path for the distinguished W-vanishing cycle** Consider the bifibration $(W,u_1)$. At $W=t$ the auxiliary critical points have $u_2=⋯=u_N=a$ and satisfy
$(N−1)a^N−t a^{(N−1)}+1=0,    v=a^{(−(N−1))}$.
Reduce a radial W-path to t∈[0,N] using the cyclic symmetry. Two roots initially have arguments ±π/N and radius $(N−1)^{(-1/N)}$; they collide at $a=1$ when $t=N$. Their trajectory has the explicit parametrization
$a=r \operatorname{exp}(±iθ),   r^N=sin((N−1)θ)/((N−1)sinθ),
t=(N−1)r$ sin(Nθ)/sin((N−1)θ),   0≤θ≤π/N.
These equations follow by taking the imaginary and real parts of $t=(N−1)a+a^{(−(N−1))}$. They give $t=0$ at θ=π/N and $t=N$ at θ=0. There is no turning point inside: $dt/da=(N−1)(1−a^{(−N)})$ is nonzero there, and da/dθ is nonzero because its argument changes strictly. Thus the real derivative dt/dθ never vanishes and has the sign fixed by the endpoints.
Moreover r increases as θ decreases. Indeed the derivative of $\operatorname{log} r^N$ is (N−1)cot((N−1)θ)−cotθ<0: the function x cot x is strictly decreasing on (0,π), as differentiation gives $(\sin x\cos x−x)/\sin^2 x<0$. The auxiliary v-trajectories therefore lie strictly inside the critical-value circle except at their initial endpoints, with arguments ∓(N−1)θ. They join the two extreme auxiliary critical values through $v=1$ and lie entirely in $S=\mathbb C^*\setminus\mathbb R_{≤0}$. They avoid all other auxiliary critical values. Their concatenation is the matching path $μ_N$ used for the distinguished W-vanishing sphere.
The local matching identification follows from the parameter-dependent holomorphic Morse normal form at $(u_i)=(1,…,1)$. The differential of $u_1$ there is nonzero. First use $u_1$ as a coordinate; applying the holomorphic Morse lemma in its remaining fibre variables makes W−N a nondegenerate quadratic form in those variables plus a one-variable function of $u_1$ with nonzero second derivative. Straightening that one-variable function gives $W−N=z_1²+⋯+z_{(N−1)}²$ and $u_1$ a regular function of $z_1$. A local W-vanishing sphere is the real sphere in the appropriate rotated quadratic form. Its sections at fixed real $z_1$ are the auxiliary vanishing (n−1)-spheres collapsing at the two endpoints. Hence it is exactly the local matching sphere.
Extend this description along t toward zero using symplectic transport and the compact two-critical-value trajectory above. Different connections and matching interpolations form a smooth family of compact exact spheres; the resulting Lagrangian isotopy is Hamiltonian because $H¹(S^n;R)=0$. Thus the named W-vanishing cycle $V_N$ is represented by the auxiliary matching cycle for $μ_N$, up to Hamiltonian isotopy and its continuously transported grading. This identifies the source's chosen matching path geometrically, rather than assuming it from a diagram. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 **Algebraic two-exceptional-object core** Let a strictly unital enhanced triangulated category be generated by two exceptional objects E,F with Hom*(F,E)=0 and
Hom*(E,F)=C a ⊕ C b,    |a|=0, |b|=n−1,
where $n=2$ or3. Its minimal A∞ model is formal: any sequence of nonidentity arrows has length at most one, so all normalized higher operations vanish. Every perfect object has a finite two-term presentation Cone(V⊗E→W⊗F). Indeed cancel the internal complexes of E- and F-summands over C; the remaining off-diagonal differential is the single E→F block, since there is no reverse arrow. The same assertion for summands follows by the length-one projective resolution of a finite module over this directed two-vertex algebra.
The arrow is α⊗a+β⊗b, with α degree zero and β degree 1−n. For $r=n−1>0$ the graded representation is a finite-support representation of a disjoint union of infinite paths
… $W_{(j−1)}$ ← $V_j → W_j$ ← $V_{(j+1)} →$ …,
where $V_j,W_j$ denote degree k+jr and the arrows are β and α. Finite path representations decompose into interval representations. One elementary basis construction is to begin at an endpoint, record the nested image/kernel subspaces in the adjacent vector space obtained by extending along successive edges, choose a basis simultaneously adapted to that finite flag, and propagate it along each edge until its image ends. At a backwards edge choose lifts of the already chosen image basis and extend by a basis of the kernel. The flag-adapted choice ensures that each new basis vector either continues one existing string or begins a new one. Induction on the number of vertices gives bases in which every arrow sends a basis vector to one next basis vector or to zero; each connected string is an interval, with all its nonzero maps rescaled to one. This algorithm uses only Gaussian elimination and finite flags.
For completeness the endomorphism dimensions can be computed directly on an interval. If its numbers of V- and W-vertices differ by one, its endomorphism complex has one-dimensional kernel and zero cokernel: the arrow equations successively make every surviving diagonal scalar equal and eliminate off-diagonal entries. It is exceptional. If both numbers equal m, one family of alternating arrows is invertible; identify the V and W spaces through it. The other is a single nilpotent Jordan string. The two-term endomorphism differential has kernel of dimension m (the successive diagonal/Jordan-shift solutions) and cokernel of dimension m, since source and target have equal dimension. The graded signs only rescale those successive equations by ±1 and do not change either dimension. Thus its total self-Ext dimension is 2m.
If an object L has $\operatorname{Hom}*(L,L)=H*(S^n;C)$, it is indecomposable because Hom⁰=C. The interval calculation forces $m=1$. There are two possibilities. Cone(a) has endomorphism cohomology C in degrees 0 and n: the differential kills the a-arrow and leaves b in degree n. The cone on b, after making b a degree-zero map by shifting its source, instead leaves a in degree 2−n. For $n=2$ that gives two degree-zero classes, and for $n=3$ it gives a negative-degree class; neither is $H*(S^n)$. Hence
L ≅ Cone(a:E→F)[s]
for some shift s. This is the precise Kronecker uniqueness implication used in the source. The hypothesis that L actually belongs to the two-object subcategory is essential. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

Include the coordinate-maximum-principle image box for raw polygons on the compact branes, and call the resulting compact set Q. Choose $L_1$ below every compact probe height and every critical height, to locate the finite low hooks; no flow cutoff is made at $L_1$. 6.1 **Stable and unstable critical charts with all direction parameters retained** Near a critical point the logarithmic modification is absent, because its whole chart lies in Q; the original Kähler form is unchanged. The holomorphic Morse normal form for f makes its real and imaginary parts quadratic to leading order. The linear Hamiltonian system for h has paired nonzero real eigenvalues. After a unitary Takagi diagonalization and a linear symplectic change, write its stable coordinates q and unstable coordinates p as
$qdot=−Λq+F(q,p),    pdot=Λp+G(q,p)$,
where Λ has positive diagonal entries and F,G have vanishing first jets. The stable and unstable manifolds are Lagrangian: their tangent planes are Lagrangian, and invariance plus exponential decay forces $ω_*$ to vanish on their transported tangent planes. Their local existence and smoothness follow directly from the integral equations: solve the stable component forward with $e^{(−Λt)}$, and solve the unstable component by the integral from t to infinity with $e^{(Λ(t−s))}$. On a small ball the nonlinear terms have arbitrarily small derivative, so the weighted sup-norm map is a contraction; differentiating that fixed-point equation gives the smooth dependence on the stable initial coordinates. Reverse time for the unstable manifold. This is also the usual elementary proof of the local stable/unstable theorem needed here.
For the actual outward chamber $C_i$, the fixed-locus calculation shows that it is the stable manifold for this inward-height flow. Denote its unstable thimble by $D_i$. It projects inward from $ζ_i$ toward the low-height region. Its outgoing tail may be bent toward a common small positive basepoint below $L_1$ without meeting compact probes; use disjoint ordered arcs there. These compactly supported end fixtures fix the ordinary directed-thimble convention.
Make a compact input brane U transverse to every $C_i$. Intersections are finite in the range under consideration. Near $x∈U∩C_i$, use p as coordinates on U, writing $q=q_x+O(p)$. In the exactly linear model its time-T image is
$q_T=e^{(−ΛT)}q_x+O(e^{(−2λ_{\operatorname{min}} T)}|p_T|),    p_0=e^{(−ΛT)}p_T$.
