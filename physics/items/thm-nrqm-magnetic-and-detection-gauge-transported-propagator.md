## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and the Axiom of Countable Choice
([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-countable-choice]]). Fix $\hbar>0$, $m>0$,
$q\in\mathbb R$, admissible magnetic data $(A,\Phi)$ on $\mathbb R^3$ and a
gauge function $\chi:\mathbb R^3\times I\to\mathbb R$ as in
[[thm-nrqm-magnetic-and-detection-gauge-h2-domain-preservation]], with
underlying open interval $I\subseteq\mathbb R$. Put
$A'_j(\cdot,t):=A_j+D_j\chi(\cdot,t)$, $\Phi'(\cdot,t):=\Phi-\partial_t\chi(\cdot,t)$
and, for $t\in I$,
$$H'(t):=K_{A'(t),\Phi'(t)},\qquad G(t):=\text{multiplication by }e^{i\frac q\hbar\chi(\cdot,t)} ,$$
so that $(A'(t),\Phi'(t))$ is admissible for every $t$ and $H'(t)$ is
self-adjoint with domain $W^{2,2}(\mathbb R^3;\mathbb C)$ by
[[thm-nrqm-magnetic-and-detection-bounded-coefficient-magnetic-operator]].
Let $K:=K_{A,\Phi}$ and $U(\tau):=e^{-i\tau K/\hbar}$ for $\tau\in\mathbb R$,
the strongly continuous unitary group generated by $-iK/\hbar$
([[lem-self-adjoint-operator-generates-a-strongly-continuous-unitary-group]],
[[def-unbounded-integral-against-a-pvm]]).

(a) For every $t\in I$ one has the operator identity on $W^{2,2}$
$$H'(t)=G(t)\,K\,G(t)^{-1}-q\,\partial_t\chi(\cdot,t),$$
where the last term is multiplication by $-q\,\partial_t\chi(\cdot,t)$;
explicitly, $G(t)KG(t)^{-1}=K_{A'(t),\Phi}$ is the magnetic operator with the
transported vector potential and the original scalar potential $\Phi$.

(b) For every $u_0\in W^{2,2}$ and $s\in I$ the curve
$$u(t):=G(t)\,U(t-s)\,G(s)^{-1}u_0\qquad(t\in I)$$
lies in $W^{2,2}$ for every $t$, is differentiable into $L^2$ on $I$, and
satisfies $u(s)=u_0$ and $i\hbar\,u'(t)=H'(t)u(t)$ for every $t\in I$. It is
the unique such curve: if $v:I\to W^{2,2}$ is continuous into
$W^{2,2}(\mathbb R^3;\mathbb C)$, differentiable into $L^2$, with
$i\hbar\,v'(t)=H'(t)v(t)$ for all $t$ and $v(s)=u_0$, then $v=u$.

(c) The two-parameter family
$$U'(t,s):=G(t)\,U(t-s)\,G(s)^{-1}\qquad(t,s\in I)$$
consists of unitary operators mapping $W^{2,2}$ onto $W^{2,2}$, satisfies
$U'(t,t)=I$ and the composition law
$U'(t,r)U'(r,s)=U'(t,s)$ for all $t,r,s\in I$, and
$u(t)=U'(t,s)u_0$ is the solution of (b).

The theorem provides dynamics for the transformed coefficient class only; it
asserts no general well-posedness for arbitrary time-dependent unbounded
generators.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $\hbar,m>0$, $q\in\mathbb R$, admissible $(A,\Phi)$, the gauge function $\chi$, the transported data $A'(t),\Phi'(t)$, the operators $K=K_{A,\Phi}$, $H'(t)=K_{A'(t),\Phi'(t)}$ and the multipliers $G(t)$.

[F1] $K$ is self-adjoint with domain $D(K)=W^{2,2}$, and $U(\tau)=e^{-i\tau K/\hbar}$ is a strongly continuous unitary group with $U(\tau)D(K)=D(K)$, $KU(\tau)=U(\tau)K$ on $D(K)$, and $\lim_{\tau\to0}\frac1\tau(U(\tau)x-x)=-\frac i\hbar Kx$ exactly for $x\in D(K)$ ([[lem-self-adjoint-operator-generates-a-strongly-continuous-unitary-group]], [[def-unbounded-integral-against-a-pvm]]).

[F2] For each $t$, $G(t)$ is unitary, preserves $W^{1,2}$ and $W^{2,2}$, and $(P_j-qA'_j(\cdot,t))G(t)u=G(t)(P_j-qA_j)u$ for $u\in W^{1,2}$; moreover $t\mapsto G(t)h$ is continuous for $h\in L^2$ and differentiable with $\frac{d}{dt}G(t)h=\frac{iq}\hbar(\partial_t\chi(\cdot,t))G(t)h$ ([[thm-nrqm-magnetic-and-detection-gauge-h2-domain-preservation]]). The same assertions hold for $-\chi$ in place of $\chi$ ([[thm-nrqm-magnetic-and-detection-gauge-h2-domain-preservation]]).

[F3] For each $t$, $(A'(t),\Phi'(t))$ is admissible: $D_kA'_j=D_kA_j+D_kD_j\chi(\cdot,t)$ and $\Phi'=\Phi-\partial_t\chi(\cdot,t)$ are bounded with the uniform bounds of the hypotheses; and $H'(t)=K_{A'(t),\Phi'(t)}$ is self-adjoint with domain $W^{2,2}$ ([[thm-nrqm-magnetic-and-detection-bounded-coefficient-magnetic-operator]], [[def-nrqm-magnetic-and-detection-magnetic-schrodinger-operator]]).

[F4] If $x\in D(K)$ and $\tau\mapsto x(\tau)\in D(K)$ is continuous into $D(K)$, then $\tau\mapsto U(\tau)x(\tau)$ is differentiable into $L^2$ with derivative $-\frac i\hbar KU(\tau)x(\tau)+U(\tau)x'(\tau)$: the group derivative exists on $D(K)$ by [F1], $KU(\tau)=U(\tau)K$, and $\tau\mapsto KU(\tau)x(\tau)=U(\tau)Kx(\tau)$ is continuous by [F1] applied to the continuous $D(K)$-valued curve $Kx(\tau)$. (This is the product rule for the strongly $C^1$ group and the $C^1$ curve.)

## Proof

1.1 [Transported magnetic square] Let $w\in W^{2,2}$ and put $u:=G(t)w$, so that $u\in W^{2,2}$ by [F2] and $w=G(t)^{-1}u$. By
[[thm-nrqm-magnetic-and-detection-bounded-coefficient-magnetic-operator]] clause (a) and [F2], $P_{A,j}w\in W^{1,2}$ and $P_{A',j}(t)G(t)P_{A,j}w=G(t)P_{A,j}(P_{A,j}w)$ for the transported momentum $P_{A',j}(t)=P_j-qA'_j(\cdot,t)$; here the covariance identity is applied twice, the first time to $w\in W^{1,2}$ and the second to the class $P_{A,j}w\in W^{1,2}$. Summing over $j$, $\frac1{2m}\sum_jP_{A',j}(t)^2u=G(t)\frac1{2m}\sum_jP_{A,j}^2w$, and adding the scalar potential term, which is multiplication by $q\Phi$ commuting with $G(t)$, gives $K_{A'(t),\Phi}u=G(t)K_{A,\Phi}w=G(t)KG(t)^{-1}u$. Since $u\in W^{2,2}$ was arbitrary, $G(t)KG(t)^{-1}=K_{A'(t),\Phi}$ on $W^{2,2}$. [given, F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 [Conclusion of (a)] By the definition of $\Phi'(t)$ and the linearity of $K$ in the scalar potential, $H'(t)=K_{A'(t),\Phi'(t)}=K_{A'(t),\Phi}-q\,\partial_t\chi(\cdot,t)$ as operators on $W^{2,2}$; combining with step 1.1 gives (a). [given, step 1.1, F3, algebra]

3.1 [The curve solves the equation] Let $u_0\in W^{2,2}$, $s\in I$, put $w_0:=G(s)^{-1}u_0\in W^{2,2}$ and $w(t):=U(t-s)w_0$; by [F1], $w(t)\in W^{2,2}$ and $t\mapsto w(t)$ is continuous into $W^{2,2}$ and differentiable into $L^2$ with $w'(t)=-\frac i\hbar Kw(t)$. Define $u(t):=G(t)w(t)$. For each $t$, $u(t)\in W^{2,2}$ by [F2], and $u'(t)=\bigl(\frac{iq}\hbar\partial_t\chi(\cdot,t)\bigr)G(t)w(t)+G(t)w'(t)$: the product rule combines the derivative of the strongly $C^1$ multiplier family from [F2] with the $C^1$ curve $w(t)$, both convergences being in $L^2$ and the multiplier difference quotients being uniformly bounded by $2\sup|\partial_t\chi|$ so that the family of difference quotients converges strongly. Hence $i\hbar u'(t)=-q\,\partial_t\chi(\cdot,t)u(t)+G(t)i\hbar w'(t)=-q\,\partial_t\chi(\cdot,t)u(t)+G(t)K w(t)=\bigl(G(t)KG(t)^{-1}-q\partial_t\chi(\cdot,t)\bigr)u(t)=H'(t)u(t)$ by step 2.1, and $u(s)=G(s)w_0=u_0$. This is the existence half of (b). [given, step 2.1, F1, F2, algebra]

3.2 [Uniqueness] Let $v$ be as in the uniqueness clause, and put $\varphi(t):=G(t)^{-1}v(t)$. By [F2] (applied to $-\chi$), $\varphi$ is continuous into $W^{2,2}$; and $\varphi'(t)=-\frac{iq}\hbar(\partial_t\chi(\cdot,t))G(t)^{-1}v(t)+G(t)^{-1}v'(t)$ by the product rule, so $i\hbar\varphi'(t)=q(\partial_t\chi)\varphi(t)+G(t)^{-1}i\hbar v'(t)=q(\partial_t\chi)\varphi+G(t)^{-1}H'(t)v(t)=q(\partial_t\chi)\varphi+\bigl(KG(t)^{-1}-q(\partial_t\chi)G(t)^{-1}\bigr)v(t)=K\varphi(t)$, where we used step 2.1 and the fact that $G(t)^{-1}$ commutes with the scalar function $q\partial_t\chi$. Thus $\varphi$ is a continuous $D(K)$-valued, $L^2$-differentiable solution of $i\hbar\varphi'=K\varphi$ with $\varphi(s)=G(s)^{-1}u_0$, so by [F4] the curve $F(t):=U(s-t)\varphi(t)$ has $F'(t)=\frac i\hbar U(s-t)K\varphi(t)+U(s-t)\varphi'(t)=U(s-t)\bigl(\frac i\hbar K\varphi(t)-\frac i\hbar K\varphi(t)\bigr)=0$ by [F1] ($U(\tau)$ commutes with $K$ on $D(K)$) and is constant; $F(t)=F(s)=\varphi(s)$, so $\varphi(t)=U(t-s)G(s)^{-1}u_0$ and $v(t)=G(t)\varphi(t)=u(t)$. This is the uniqueness half of (b). [given, step 2.1, F1, F2, F4, algebra]

4.1 [Propagator properties] Each $U'(t,s)=G(t)U(t-s)G(s)^{-1}$ is unitary as a product of unitaries and maps $W^{2,2}$ onto $W^{2,2}$ by [F1] and [F2]. The identity $U'(t,t)=G(t)G(t)^{-1}=I$ is immediate; the composition law follows from $G(r)^{-1}G(r)=I$: $U'(t,r)U'(r,s)=G(t)U(t-r)G(r)^{-1}G(r)U(r-s)G(s)^{-1}=G(t)U(t-r+r-s)G(s)^{-1}=U'(t,s)$. By step 3.1, $u(t)=G(t)U(t-s)G(s)^{-1}u_0=U'(t,s)u_0$ solves the equation, and by step 3.2 it is the unique solution. This is (c). [step 3.1, step 3.2, F1, F2, algebra] ∎
