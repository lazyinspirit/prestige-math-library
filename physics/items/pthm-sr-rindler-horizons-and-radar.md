## Statement

Adopt [[post-sr-inertial-covariance]], [[post-sr-ideal-clocks-and-light]] and [[post-sr-causal-signal-propagation]], and the SI conventions of [[def-sr-si-units-and-coordinate-conventions]] ($x^0 = c t$). Let $x = (x^0,x^1,x^2,x^3)$ be the chart of an inertial frame ([[def-inertial-frame-and-inertial-chart]]) and let $W := \{x^1 > |x^0|\}$ be the right wedge. Let $\Phi$ and $n$ be the Rindler chart and congruence of [[thm-sr-fermi-walker-and-rigid-observer-examples]] (b): $\Phi(\theta,\rho,y,z) = (\rho \sinh \theta, \rho \cosh \theta, y, z)$ on $\mathbb R \times (0,\infty) \times \mathbb R^2$ with inverse $\rho = \sqrt{(x^1)^2 - (x^0)^2}$, $\theta = \frac12 \log((x^1+x^0)/(x^1-x^0))$, pullback metric $-\rho^2 d\theta^2 + d\rho^2 + dy^2 + dz^2$, and acceleration field $a_{\mathrm{cg}} = \nabla_n n = \rho^{-1} \partial_\rho$. Fix $\rho_0 > 0$ and real $y_0,z_0$, and let $O$ be the congruence worldline $\rho = \rho_0$, $y = y_0$, $z = z_0$, parametrized by its proper time $\tau$ with $\theta = c \tau/\rho_0$; by [[def-sr-proper-acceleration]] its proper acceleration is $c^2/\rho_0$. Write $U(q) := q^0 + q^1$ and $V(q) := q^0 - q^1$ for events $q$, so that $U = \rho e^{\theta}$ and $V = -\rho e^{-\theta}$ on $W$, and write $\le$ and $J^+$, $J^-$ for the causal reachability relations of [[def-sr-causal-reachability-in-affine-minkowski-space]]. Then, conditionally on the adopted model:

(i) **Chart domain and light rays.** $\Phi$ is a smooth bijection of $\mathbb R \times (0,\infty) \times \mathbb R^2$ onto $W$ with the stated smooth inverse, so the chart domain is exactly $W$ and $\rho = 0$ is not part of its coordinate domain. The boundary of $W$ in the affine model is $\{x^1 = |x^0|\}$, with arbitrary $x^2,x^3$: it is the union of the two null half-planes $\{x^1 = x^0 \ge 0\}$ and $\{x^1 = -x^0 \ge 0\}$. These half-planes are portions of the full null planes appearing as causal horizons in (ii); the full planes are not the wedge boundary. Along the worldline $\rho = \rho_0$ one has $V = -\rho_0 e^{-\theta} \to 0^-$ and $U \to +\infty$ as $\theta \to +\infty$, while $U = \rho_0 e^{\theta} \to 0^+$ and $V \to -\infty$ as $\theta \to -\infty$. The future null straight lines of the model that lie in the plane $y = y_0$, $z = z_0$ and meet $O$ are exactly the lines of constant $V$ (on which $d\theta = +d\rho/\rho$) and the lines of constant $U$ (on which $d\theta = -d\rho/\rho$).

(ii) **Causal horizons.** An event $q$ satisfies $q \le p$ for some event $p$ of $O$ if and only if $V(q) < 0$; an event $q$ satisfies $p \le q$ for some event $p$ of $O$ if and only if $U(q) > 0$. Consequently no event with $V(q) \ge 0$ can ever influence $O$; this set contains the null plane $\{x^1 = x^0\}$, the left wedge $\{x^1 < -|x^0|\}$ and the future wedge $\{x^0 > |x^1|\}$; and no event with $U(q) \le 0$ can ever be influenced by $O$, a set containing the null plane $\{x^1 = -x^0\}$, the left wedge and the past wedge $\{x^0 < -|x^1|\}$. The null plane $\{x^1 = x^0\}$ is the boundary of the causal past of $O$ (the future horizon: the surface its worldline approaches as $\theta \to +\infty$), and the null plane $\{x^1 = -x^0\}$ is the boundary of the causal future of $O$ (the past horizon); every event of the left wedge is causally unrelated to $O$ in both directions. The same criteria with $O$ any other congruence worldline $\rho = \rho_1$, $y = y_1$, $z = z_1$ hold verbatim: the constants $\rho_0$, $y_0$, $z_0$ do not enter.

(iii) **Radar time and distance to a coaxial congruence reflector.** Fix $\rho_m > 0$ with $\rho_m \ne \rho_0$ and let $R_m$ be the congruence worldline $\rho = \rho_m$, $y = y_0$, $z = z_0$ (a second uniformly accelerated observer, coaxial with $O$, with proper acceleration $c^2/\rho_m$). Let $e_-$ be the event of $O$ at parameter $\theta_e$ (so $\tau(e_-) = \rho_0 \theta_e/c$ up to the base point of $\tau$), let $q_m$ be the event of $R_m$ lying on the future null straight line from $e_-$ in the plane $y = y_0$, $z = z_0$, and let $e_+$ be the event of $O$ lying on the future null straight line from $q_m$; the reflection at $q_m$ is idealized as instantaneous and as leaving $R_m$'s motion unchanged, as in [[def-sr-operational-radar-protocol]]. Then $e_-, q_m, e_+$ exist uniquely, the Rindler time of $q_m$ is $\theta_m = \theta_e + |\log(\rho_m/\rho_0)|$, the return event satisfies $\theta(e_+) = \theta_e + 2|\log(\rho_m/\rho_0)|$ and $(\theta_e + \theta(e_+))/2 = \theta_m$, so the round trip lasts $2|\log(\rho_m/\rho_0)|$ in Rindler time and $(2\rho_0/c)|\log(\rho_m/\rho_0)|$ in $O$'s proper time. The radar protocol therefore assigns to $q_m$ the radar time $\tau_{\mathrm{rad}}(q_m) = (\rho_0/c) \theta_m$ plus the base constant of $\tau$ and the radar distance $\rho_{\mathrm{rad}}(q_m) = \rho_0 |\log(\rho_m/\rho_0)|$; within this coaxial family the radar-time level sets are the intersections of the Rindler rest slices $\theta=\mathrm{const}$ with $y=y_0$, $z=z_0$, excluding the observer radius $\rho_0$ ([[def-sr-simultaneity-slice-and-rest-distance]]). The radar distance is not the rest distance: in the slice $\theta = \theta_m$ the two worldlines are at rest distance $|\rho_m - \rho_0|$, whereas for $\rho_m = 2\rho_0$ the radar distance is $\rho_0 \log 2 < \rho_0$, because $\log$ is strictly increasing and $e > 2$ implies $\log 2 < \log e = 1$ ([[cor-two-less-than-e-less-than-three]], [[thm-natural-logarithm-laws]]). For every distinct positive reflector radius the notions differ: with $r = \rho_m/\rho_0$, one has $\log r < r - 1$ when $r > 1$ and $-\log r > 1 - r$ when $0 < r < 1$. The two distance formulas agree only at $r = 1$, where both have the continuous degenerate value zero. The coincident case is not a nonzero emission-reflection-reception triple of the protocol, whose distinct-reflector hypothesis remains in force. As $\rho_m \to 0^+$ the round-trip proper time and the radar distance grow without bound while the reflector's proper acceleration $c^2/\rho_m$ diverges; as $\rho_m \to +\infty$ the round-trip proper time and the radar distance also grow without bound while the reflector's proper acceleration tends to $0$.

(iv) **Restrictions of the protocol.** No event with $V \ge 0$ --- in particular no event of the horizon plane $\{x^1 = x^0\}$, of the left wedge or of the future wedge --- can be assigned a radar time or radar distance by this protocol from $O$, because by (ii) it cannot causally precede any reception event on $O$: the required reflection-to-reception leg is impossible. Separately, every event with $U \le 0$ lacks an emission-to-reflection leg from $O$. Thus any event admitting this radar triple must satisfy $U > 0$ and $V < 0$, equivalently it must belong to $W$; the horizon is at infinite radar distance in the limit $\rho_m \to 0^+$. For example $q = x^{-1}(2\rho_0,0,y_0,z_0)$ has $U(q) = V(q) = 2\rho_0 > 0$. It is causally after $O$ at $\theta = 0$, since the displacement is $(2\rho_0,-\rho_0,0,0)$, future timelike, and it even lies on a future null ray from $O$ at $\theta = \log 2$, with displacement $(5\rho_0/4,-5\rho_0/4,0,0)$. Nevertheless it cannot send a causal return to $O$, so it receives no radar assignment from $O$. The protocol therefore does not extend to a global coordinate system of $A$.

(v) **Scope.** All clauses are conditional computations and readings in flat classical spacetime with ideal clocks, ideal light rays and ideal reflectors, for the specified congruence worldlines. They are not empirical claims and contain no measurement, apparatus, source, detector or statistical content; gravitation, media, dispersion, finite resolution, quantum effects and material models of the reflector are excluded. No choice principle is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The adopted model: affine Minkowski space $(A,V,\eta,C^+)$ with inertial chart $x$, $x^0=ct$; ideal clocks and light rays; the causal signal restriction; the Rindler chart and congruence data of the statement; $\rho_0>0$, real $y_0,z_0$; the worldline $O$ with $\theta=c\tau/\rho_0$; $U(q)=q^0+q^1$, $V(q)=q^0-q^1$; and, in (iii), $\rho_m>0$ with $\rho_m\neq\rho_0$ and the coaxial worldline $R_m$.

[L1] Adopted framework assumptions: the affine Minkowski model is the framework's spacetime; ideal clocks read the proper time of their worldline; ideal vacuum light rays are the future null straight lines with coordinate speed $c$; and an idealized signal transmission from $p$ to $q$ is admitted only if $q \in J^+(p)$ ([[post-sr-inertial-covariance]], [[post-sr-ideal-clocks-and-light]], [[post-sr-causal-signal-propagation]]).

[L2] Coordinates and model: in every inertial chart $x^0=ct$, $\eta=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$ in the orthonormal frame, and points are determined by their coordinates ([[def-sr-si-units-and-coordinate-conventions]], [[def-inertial-frame-and-inertial-chart]], [[def-sr-affine-minkowski-model]]).

[L3] Rindler data: $\Phi(\theta,\rho,y,z)=(\rho\sinh\theta,\rho\cosh\theta,y,z)$ is a smooth bijection of $\mathbb R\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbb R^2$ onto the right wedge $W=\{x^1>|x^0|\}$ with smooth inverse $\rho=\sqrt{(x^1)^2-(x^0)^2}$, $\theta=\frac12\log\frac{x^1+x^0}{x^1-x^0}$, pullback metric $-\rho^2d\theta^2+d\rho^2+dy^2+dz^2$; $n=\rho^{-1}\partial_\theta$ is future unit timelike with $a_{\mathrm{cg}}=\nabla_nn=\rho^{-1}\partial_\rho$ and $\eta(a_{\mathrm{cg}},a_{\mathrm{cg}})=\rho^{-2}$; the rest slices $\theta=\mathrm{const}$ are the intersections with $W$ of affine hyperplanes orthogonal to $n$ with induced metric $d\rho^2+dy^2+dz^2$ ([[thm-sr-fermi-walker-and-rigid-observer-examples]] (b)).

[L4] Congruence flow lines and proper acceleration: a flow line of $n$ with tangent $\rho n$ has four-velocity $cn$ and proper time $d\tau=\rho\,d\theta/c$; its proper acceleration is $c^2\sqrt{\eta(a_{\mathrm{cg}},a_{\mathrm{cg}})}=c^2/\rho$ ([[def-sr-observer-congruence-and-kinematics]] (b), [[def-sr-four-velocity-and-four-acceleration]] (a),(c), [[def-sr-proper-time]], [[lem-sr-proper-time-parametrization]], [[thm-sr-proper-time-and-observer-decomposition]] (a), [[def-sr-proper-acceleration]] (b)).

[L5] Causal structure: a vector is timelike iff $\eta(v,v)<0$ and null iff nonzero with $\eta(v,v)=0$, and each nonzero causal vector is future or past; $C^+=\{v:v^0>|v_s|\}$, $\bar C^+=\{v:v^0\ge|v_s|\}$ in every inertial chart; for $q\ne p$, $q\in J^+(p)$ iff the straight segment from $p$ to $q$ is a future causal path; and the causal reachability relations are those of [[def-sr-causal-reachability-in-affine-minkowski-space]] ([[def-causal-classification-of-lorentz-vectors]], [[def-time-orientation-and-future-causal-sets]], [[lem-sr-future-causal-sums-and-inertial-coordinate-criterion]] (a),(c), [[thm-sr-future-cone-and-flat-domain-of-dependence]] (b), [[def-sr-regular-causal-curve-and-worldline]] (a),(b)).

[L6] Radar protocol: the radar time of a reflected event $q$ with emission $e_-$ and reception $e_+$ on the observer's worldline is the midpoint proper time $\tau_{\mathrm{rad}}(q)=(\tau(s_-)+\tau(s_+))/2$ and the radar distance is $c(\tau(s_+)-\tau(s_-))/2$; the protocol is conditional on the existence of the three events and uses the symmetric half-split convention ([[def-sr-operational-radar-protocol]]).

[L7] Rest distance: in a simultaneity slice of a future unit timelike $n$, the rest distance of two events is $\sqrt{\eta(q-p,q-p)}$ ([[def-sr-simultaneity-slice-and-rest-distance]] (a),(d)).

[L8] Logarithm: $\log:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb R$ is continuous and strictly increasing with $\log 1=0$, $\log(xy)=\log x+\log y$ and $\log(1/x)=-\log x$; $\log$ is differentiable with $\log'x=1/x$, and the mean value theorem applies to it on closed intervals with the endpoint continuity supplied by differentiability ([[def-natural-logarithm]], [[thm-natural-logarithm-laws]], [[thm-logarithm-derivative-and-integral]], [[cor-mean-value-theorem]], [[cor-differentiable-implies-continuous]]).

[L9] Exponential and hyperbolic data: $e^u\to+\infty$ as $u\to+\infty$, $e^u\to0$ as $u\to-\infty$, $2<e<3$ with $\log e=1$; $\sinh u=(e^u-e^{-u})/2$, $\cosh u=(e^u+e^{-u})/2$, $\cosh^2u-\sinh^2u=1$, and $\cosh u\pm\sinh u=e^{\pm u}$ ([[thm-exponential-limits-and-range]], [[cor-two-less-than-e-less-than-three]], [[def-hyperbolic-functions]], [[thm-hyperbolic-identities-and-derivatives]], [[thm-exponential-addition-formula]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 (Chart domain, wedge and boundary.) From [L3], $\Phi$ is a smooth bijection onto $W$ with the stated smooth inverse defined for $x^1>|x^0|$, so the coordinate domain is exactly $W$ and $\rho=0$ is excluded; on $W$ the coordinates satisfy $U=\rho e^{\theta}>0$ and $V=-\rho e^{-\theta}<0$, and conversely $U>0$ and $V<0$ define $W$, since $x^1=(U-V)/2>|x^0|=|U+V|/2$ is equivalent to $U>0,V<0$. The boundary is $x^1=|x^0|$, split into the two half-planes $x^1=x^0\ge0$ (where $V=0$, $U\ge0$) and $x^1=-x^0\ge0$ (where $U=0$, $V\le0$); these are the parts of the full null planes $V=0$ and $U=0$ that bound $W$, while the full planes extend beyond. [L2, L3, algebra]

1.2 (The worldline $O$ and its parametrization.) By [L3] the curve $\theta\mapsto\Phi(\theta,\rho_0,y_0,z_0)$ is a flow line of $n=\rho^{-1}\partial_\theta$ restricted to $\rho=\rho_0$; its tangent with respect to $\theta$ is $\partial_\theta=\rho_0 n$, so by [L4] its four-velocity is $cn$, its proper time satisfies $d\tau=\rho_0\,d\theta/c$, i.e. $\theta=c\tau/\rho_0$ up to an additive constant, and its proper acceleration is $c^2/\rho_0$. Along it, $U=\rho_0e^{\theta}$ and $V=-\rho_0e^{-\theta}$. [L3, L4, algebra]

1.3 (The future null lines of the plane through $O$.) Let $p,q$ be events in the plane $y=y_0$, $z=z_0$ and $w:=q-p$. Since $w^2=w^3=0$, one has $\Delta U:=U(q)-U(p)=w^0+w^1$, $\Delta V:=V(q)-V(p)=w^0-w^1$, and $\Delta U\Delta V=(w^0)^2-(w^1)^2$; hence $w\in\bar C^+$ iff $\Delta U\ge0$, $\Delta V\ge0$ and $\Delta U\Delta V\ge0$, and $w$ is future null iff in addition $\Delta U\Delta V=(w^2)^2+(w^3)^2=0$, i.e. iff either $\Delta V=0<\Delta U$ or $\Delta U=0<\Delta V$. Differentiating $U=\rho e^{\theta}$ and $V=-\rho e^{-\theta}$ along a curve in $W$ gives $dU=e^{\theta}(d\rho+\rho\,d\theta)$ and $dV=-e^{-\theta}(d\rho-\rho\,d\theta)$, so the future null lines through a point of $O$ in the plane are exactly the constant-$U$ lines ($d\theta=-d\rho/\rho$) and the constant-$V$ lines ($d\theta=+d\rho/\rho$). [L3, L5, algebra]

1.4 (The logarithmic inequality.) For $r>0$ with $r\neq1$, the mean value theorem applied to $\log$ on the interval between $1$ and $r$ gives $\log r-\log1=\frac{1}{\zeta}(r-1)$ for some $\zeta$ strictly between $1$ and $r$; since $\zeta>1$ when $r>1$ this gives $\log r<r-1$, and since $0<\zeta<1$ when $0<r<1$ it gives $-\log r>1-r$; equality holds only at $r=1$. In particular $\log2<2-1=1$, and also $e>2$ gives $\log2<\log e=1$ by strict increase of $\log$. [L8, L9, algebra]

2.1 (Causal criteria for the worldline $O$.) For an arbitrary displacement $w$, $w\in\bar C^+$ is equivalent to $\Delta U\ge0$, $\Delta V\ge0$ and $\Delta U\Delta V\ge(w^2)^2+(w^3)^2$: this follows from $w^0=(\Delta U+\Delta V)/2$ and $\eta(w,w)=-\Delta U\Delta V+(w^2)^2+(w^3)^2$ and the inertial cone criterion [L5]. (Forward.) Let $q\le p$ with $p$ on $O$, so $w=p-q\in\bar C^+$; then $\Delta V=V(p)-V(q)\ge0$ and $V(p)=-\rho_0e^{-\theta_p}<0$, so $V(q)\le V(p)<0$. Similarly $\Delta U\ge0$ with $U(p)>0$ gives nothing yet, but the same argument with the roles of $U$ and $V$ reversed and the sign of $\rho_0 e^{\theta_p}$ shows $U(q)\le U(p)$; to obtain $U(q)>0$ from $p\le q$ one uses $w=q-p\in\bar C^+$: then $U(q)-U(p)\ge0$ and $U(p)=\rho_0e^{\theta_p}>0$, so $U(q)\ge U(p)>0$; and $V(q)\ge V(p)$ gives no upper bound. (Reverse.) Suppose $V(q)<0$; choose $\theta_p$ with $-\rho_0e^{-\theta_p}>V(q)$, i.e. $V(p)>V(q)$, which is possible because $-\rho_0e^{-\theta_p}\to0^-$ as $\theta_p\to+\infty$; then $\Delta V>0$, and $\Delta U=\rho_0e^{\theta_p}-U(q)$ and $\Delta U\Delta V$ tend to $+\infty$ as $\theta_p\to+\infty$ while the transverse displacement $(y_0-q^2,z_0-q^3)$ is fixed, so for all sufficiently large $\theta_p$ the three general inequalities just established hold and $q\le p$. Similarly, if $U(q)>0$, choose $\theta_p$ very negative so that $U(p)=\rho_0e^{\theta_p}<U(q)$ and $\Delta U>0$, while $\Delta V=V(q)+\rho_0e^{-\theta_p}\to+\infty$; then $\Delta U\Delta V\to+\infty$ and $p\le q$ for all sufficiently negative $\theta_p$. Hence the criteria, and since the construction used only the positivity of $\rho_0$ and the fixed transverse coordinates of the worldline, the same criteria hold for every congruence worldline $\rho=\rho_1$, $y=y_1$, $z=z_1$. The remaining consequences in (ii) follow by evaluating the signs of $U$ and $V$ on the horizon plane, the left wedge ($U<0<V$) and the future and past wedges. [L5, step 1.1, step 1.3, algebra]

2.2 (Limits along $O$ and along the reflector family.) By [L9], $\rho_0e^{-\theta}\to0$ and $\rho_0e^{\theta}\to+\infty$ as $\theta\to+\infty$, while $\rho_0e^{\theta}\to0$ and $\rho_0e^{-\theta}\to+\infty$ as $\theta\to-\infty$; hence along $O$, $V=-\rho_0e^{-\theta}\to0^-$ and $U\to+\infty$ as $\theta\to+\infty$, and $U\to0^+$ with $V\to-\infty$ as $\theta\to-\infty$. [L9, step 1.2, algebra]

2.3 (Clause (iii): the radar geometry.) Suppose first $\rho_m>\rho_0$. The constant-$V$ future null line from $e_-=(\theta_e,\rho_0)$ has $V=-\rho_0e^{-\theta_e}$, and its intersection with $\rho=\rho_m$ is at the unique $\theta_m$ with $-\rho_m e^{-\theta_m}=-\rho_0e^{-\theta_e}$, i.e. $\theta_m=\theta_e+\log(\rho_m/\rho_0)=\theta_e+|\log(\rho_m/\rho_0)|$ since $\rho_m>\rho_0$; the return leg must be the constant-$U$ line through $q_m=(\theta_m,\rho_m)$ (the constant-$V$ line leaves $\rho$ increasing), and it meets $\rho=\rho_0$ at the unique $\theta(e_+)$ with $\rho_0e^{\theta(e_+)}=\rho_me^{\theta_m}$, i.e. $\theta(e_+)=\theta_m+\log(\rho_m/\rho_0)=\theta_e+2\log(\rho_m/\rho_0)$. If instead $0<\rho_m<\rho_0$, the same computation with the roles of $U$ and $V$ exchanged, using the two formulas $U=\rho e^{\theta}$ and $V=-\rho e^{-\theta}$, gives $\theta_m=\theta_e+\log(\rho_0/\rho_m)$ and $\theta(e_+)=\theta_m+\log(\rho_0/\rho_m)$, i.e. in both cases $\theta_m=\theta_e+|\log(\rho_m/\rho_0)|$ and $\theta(e_+)=\theta_e+2|\log(\rho_m/\rho_0)|$ with midpoint $\theta_m$. Uniqueness of $q_m$ and $e_+$ holds because each constant-$U$ or constant-$V$ line meets each of the lines $\rho=\mathrm{const}>0$ exactly once, and $e_-\le q_m$ and $q_m\le e_+$ are future null by step 1.3. 3.3 (Clause (iii): the radar assignments and the distance comparison.) 3.4 (Clause (iv): the restrictions, and the example.) Let $q$ admit a radar triple $e_-,q,e_+$ from $O$; by the ideal-light and signal assumptions of [L1], the emission and reflection legs give $e_-\le q$ and $q\le e_+$ with $e_-,e_+$ on $O$, so by step 2.1's criteria $U(q)>0$ and $V(q)<0$, i.e. $q\in W$; conversely every event with $V\ge0$ fails $q\le p$ for all $p\in O$ and every event with $U\le0$ fails $p\le q$, so those events receive no radar assignment.
