## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice as inherited from the linear algebra suppliers.
Let $K'$ be a commutative $K$-algebra which is a free $K$-module of finite
rank, with a $K$-basis $x_1,\dots,x_n$, let $V$ be a $K$-module and $A$ a
$k$-subspace with $fA<A$ for every $f\in K$, and let
$V':=K'\otimes_KV$, $A':=\sum_i x_i\otimes A$. Then $K'$ acts on $V'$ with
$f'A'<A'$ for every $f'\in K'$, the commensurability class of $A'$ is
independent of the chosen $K$-basis, and for every $f\in K'$ and $g\in K$ one
has
$$\operatorname{res}^{V'}(f\,\mathrm dg) =\operatorname{res}^{V}(\operatorname{Tr}_{K'/K}(f)\,\mathrm dg),$$
where $\operatorname{Tr}_{K'/K}$ is the trace of multiplication on the free
$K$-module $K'$. This is $(R_6)$ of Tate's paper.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** a field $k$, a commutative $k$-algebra $K$, a $K$-module $V$, a $k$-subspace $A\subseteq V$ with $fA<A$ for every $f\in K$, a commutative $K$-algebra $K'$ that is free of finite rank $n\ge0$ over $K$ with $K$-basis $x_1,\dots,x_n$, and the extensions $V'=K'\otimes_KV$ and $A'=\sum_i x_i\otimes A$; the abstract residues $\operatorname{res}^{V}$ and $\operatorname{res}^{V'}$ of [[thm-abstract-residue-exists-unique]] attached to $A$ and to $A'$ once stability is checked.

[F1] The commensurability relation $<$ and the spaces $E(C),E_1(C),E_2(C),E_0(C)$ of [[def-commensurable-subspaces-and-ideals-of-endomorphisms]] satisfy: $<$ is reflexive, monotone in the second variable, transitive, compatible with $k$-linear maps, and satisfies the finite-sums rule that $\sum_iA_i<\sum_iB_i$ whenever $A_i<B_i$ for all $i$. An element of $E_0(C)$ is finite potent, and $E_0(C)$ is a finite potent $k$-subspace of $\operatorname{End}_k(V)$: products of two elements of $E_0(C)$ have finite-dimensional image.

[F2] Tensors and matrices ([[def-tensor-product-of-modules-by-generators-and-relations]], [[def-linear-map]]): the tensor product here is the abelian group generated by elementary tensors with additivity and $K$-balancing relations. Define the $K'$-action on elementary tensors by $f'\cdot(y\otimes v):=(f'y)\otimes v$ and extend additively. It is well-defined on each balancing relation because $f'\cdot((yr)\otimes v)=(f'(yr))\otimes v=((f'y)r)\otimes v=(f'y)\otimes rv=f'\cdot(y\otimes(rv))$, and additivity in the tensor arguments is inherited from multiplication and the tensor relations. Additivity in the action variable follows from $(f'+g')y=f'y+g'y$ and scalar compatibility follows from $(r'f')y=r'(f'y)$ for $r',f'\in K'$. The unit and associativity laws follow on each elementary tensor from those of $K'$. Thus $V'=K'\otimes_KV$ is a $K'$-module.

Since $x_1,\dots,x_n$ is a $K$-basis, each $y\in K'$ has unique coordinates $y=\sum_i x_i c_i(y)$, where the coordinate maps $c_i:K'\to K$ are $K$-linear. Define $\Phi:V^n\to V'$ by $\Phi((v_i)_i)=\sum_i x_i\otimes v_i$. Define $\Psi:V'\to V^n$ on elementary tensors by $\Psi(y\otimes v)=(c_i(y)v)_i$. This assignment is additive and $K$-balanced, since $c_i(yr)v=c_i(y)rv$, so it descends through the tensor relations. The identities $c_i(x_j)=\delta_{ij}$ give $\Psi\Phi=\mathrm{id}_{V^n}$, and $\Phi\Psi(y\otimes v)=\sum_i x_i\otimes c_i(y)v=\sum_i x_ic_i(y)\otimes v=y\otimes v$ give $\Phi\Psi=\mathrm{id}_{V'}$. Hence every element of $V'$ has a unique expression $\sum_i x_i\otimes v_i$, so $V'=\bigoplus_i(x_i\otimes V)$ as $k$-vector spaces and $A'=\bigoplus_i(x_i\otimes A)$.

With these coordinates, every $k$-linear endomorphism $\gamma$ of $V'$ is given by a unique matrix $(\gamma_{ij})$ of $k$-linear endomorphisms of $V$ through $\gamma(x_j\otimes v)=\sum_i x_i\otimes\gamma_{ij}(v)$; matrix addition and multiplication compute sums and compositions. For $f'\in K'$, write $f'x_j=\sum_i x_i f_{ij}$ with $f_{ij}\in K$. The $K'$-action just constructed then gives multiplication by $f'$ the block matrix $(f_{ij})$ acting on the $V$ coordinates. [def-tensor-product-of-modules-by-generators-and-relations, def-linear-map]

[F3] Trace of multiplication on a finite free module over the commutative ring $K$: for a $K$-linear map on a finite free $K$-module, define its trace in a chosen finite free basis as the diagonal sum of its matrix; in rank zero this is the empty sum $0$. For square matrices $X=(x_{ij})$ and $Y=(y_{ij})$ over commutative $K$, the finite sums give $\operatorname{tr}(XY)=\sum_{i,j}x_{ij}y_{ji}=\sum_{j,i}y_{ji}x_{ij}=\operatorname{tr}(YX)$. Therefore a change of basis by an invertible matrix $P$ preserves trace, since $\operatorname{tr}(P^{-1}MP)=\operatorname{tr}(MP P^{-1})=\operatorname{tr}(M)$. Matrix diagonal sum is additive and homogeneous, so this trace is $K$-linear in the endomorphism. For multiplication $m_{f'}$ on $K'$, its matrix is $(f_{ij})$ from [F2], and its trace is $\operatorname{Tr}_{K'/K}(f'):=\sum_i f_{ii}$; this is $K$-linear in $f'$ because $m_{af'+bf''}=a m_{f'}+b m_{f''}$. When the base is a field, this agrees with the published finite-dimensional vector-space trace ([[def-trace-of-an-endomorphism]]); for the general commutative-ring base here, the definition and basis-independence proof are the ones just given. [F2, algebra]

[F4] Finite-potent traces ([[lem-finite-potent-trace-existence-and-uniqueness]], [[lem-finite-potent-trace-linearity-and-conjugation]]): (T1) on a finite-dimensional space $\operatorname{Tr}_V$ is the ordinary trace of [[def-trace-of-an-endomorphism]]; (T2) if $W\subseteq V$ is $\theta$-stable then $\operatorname{Tr}_V(\theta)=\operatorname{Tr}_W(\theta) +\operatorname{Tr}_{V/W}(\theta)$, so that on a finite direct sum a block-diagonal endomorphism has trace the sum of the traces of its blocks; (T3) a nilpotent endomorphism has trace $0$; (T4) if $F$ is a $k$-subspace of $\operatorname{End}_k(V)$ and some exponent $e$ has the property that $\theta_1\cdots\theta_e(V)$ is finite-dimensional for every $e$-tuple $\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_e\in F$, then $\operatorname{Tr}_V|_F$ is $k$-linear. This is the finite-potent-family condition, which does not require $F$ itself to be finite-dimensional; (T5) if $\varphi\colon V_1\to V_2$ and $\psi\colon V_2\to V_1$ have $\psi\varphi$ finite potent, then $\varphi\psi$ is finite potent and $\operatorname{Tr}_{V_2}(\varphi\psi)=\operatorname{Tr}_{V_1}(\psi\varphi)$.

[F5] The abstract residue of [[thm-abstract-residue-exists-unique]] on a stable subspace $C$ is the unique $k$-linear map $\operatorname{res}_C\colon\Omega^1_{K/k}\to k$ with $\operatorname{res}_C(f\,\mathrm dg)=\operatorname{Tr}_V([f_1,g_1])$ for all $f,g\in K$ and all $f_1,g_1\in E(C)$ with $f_1\equiv f\pmod{E_2(C)}$, $g_1\equiv g\pmod{E_2(C)}$ and $f_1\in E_1(C)$ or $g_1\in E_1(C)$; for such lifts the commutator lies in $E_0(C)$. Elements of the commutative algebra $K$ commute as endomorphisms of $V$.

[F6] The Axiom of Choice is [[def-axiom-of-choice]].

[F7] By [[thm-every-independent-set-extends-to-a-basis]], every linearly independent set extends to a basis. Apply this first to the empty set in $A$ to obtain a basis of $A$, then to that basis as an independent subset of $V$ to extend it to a basis of $V$. Defining a map to be the identity on the basis of $A$ and zero on the added basis vectors gives a $k$-linear projection $V\to A$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; write endomorphisms of $K'\otimes_KV$ as matrices over $\operatorname{End}_k(V)$ and evaluate the defining trace formula of the abstract residue block by block.

1.1 (Rank-zero case.) If $n=0$, then $K'=0$, $V'=A'=0$, and the basis is empty. The only $f\in K'$ is $0$, $\Omega^1_{K'/k}=0$, multiplication by $0$ on the rank-zero free $K$-module has trace $0$, and both sides of the residue formula are $0$; stability and basis independence are immediate. Thus assume $n>0$ for the remaining steps. [F2, F3, F5, given]

1.2 (Stability of $A'$.) For $f'\in K'$, write $f'x_j=\sum_i x_i f_{ij}$ with $f_{ij}\in K$ as in [F2]. For each of the finitely many pairs $(i,j)$ choose a finite-dimensional $W_{ij}\subseteq V$ with $f_{ij}A\subseteq A+W_{ij}$, using $f_{ij}A<A$. Then for $a_j\in A$, $$f'\Bigl(\sum_jx_j\otimes a_j\Bigr)=\sum_{i,j}x_i\otimes f_{ij}a_j\in A'+W',\qquad W':=\sum_{i,j}x_i\otimes W_{ij}.$$ The space $W'$ is finite-dimensional over $k$: it is a finite sum of images of finite-dimensional spaces $W_{ij}$ under the $k$-linear maps $v\mapsto x_i\otimes v$. Thus $f'A'<A'$, so $A'$ is stable and $\operatorname{res}^{V'}$ is defined. The witnesses are chosen separately for the finitely many matrix entries; no $K$-module structure on a witness space is needed. [F1, F2, F5, given]

2.1 (Basis independence.) If $x'_1,\dots,x'_n$ is another $K$-basis of $K'$, write $x'_j=\sum_la_{lj}x_l$ with $a_{lj}\in K$; the same computation as in step 1.2 applied to the inverse change of basis shows $A''=\sum_jx'_j\otimes A<A'$ and $A'<A''$, so $A''\sim A'$; by [F1] the spaces $E,E_1,E_2,E_0$ and hence the residue depend only on the commensurability class, so the construction of $A'$ is basis-independent up to commensurability. [F1, F2, step 1.2]

2.2 (Matrix description and finite potency.) By [F2] a $k$-endomorphism $\gamma$ of $V'$ is exactly a matrix $(\gamma_{ij})$ of $k$-linear endomorphisms of $V$ acting by $\gamma(x_j\otimes v)=\sum_ix_i\otimes\gamma_{ij}(v)$. Let $F\subseteq\operatorname{End}_k(V)$ be finite potent, and choose a positive exponent $e$ from its finite-potent-family condition [F4]; if the available exponent is $0$, then $V$ is finite-dimensional and exponent $1$ also works. Let $F'$ be the $k$-subspace of $\operatorname{End}_k(V')$ of matrices with every entry in $F$. For any fixed $e$ matrices in $F'$, each entry of their product is a finite sum, over intermediate matrix indices, of length-$e$ products of elements of $F$. Every such product has finite-dimensional image by the exponent condition on $F$, and the finite sum of those images is finite-dimensional. Since there are only finitely many input and output blocks, the whole matrix product has finite-dimensional image. Thus $F'$ is finite potent with exponent $e$, independently of the matrix size $n$; no common space $F^eV$ or uniform dimension bound is asserted. [F2, F4, step 1.2]

2.3 (A projection onto $A'$.) By [F7], choose a basis of $A$ and extend it to a basis of $V$; the map equal to the identity on the first basis and zero on the added basis vectors is a $k$-linear projection $\pi\colon V\to A$. Define $\pi'(\sum_ix_i\otimes v_i)=\sum_ix_i\otimes\pi(v_i)$; it is a $k$-linear projection of $V'$ onto $A'$. For $f'\in K'$, step 1.2 gives a fixed finite-dimensional $W'\subseteq V'$ with $f'A'\subseteq A'+W'$. If $a'\in A'$ and $f'a'=b'+w'$ with $b'\in A'$ and $w'\in W'$, then $(\pi'f'-f')(a')=\pi'(w')-w'$. Thus the image of $(\pi'f'-f')|_{A'}$ lies in the fixed finite-dimensional space $(\pi'-1)(W')$, so $\pi'f'\equiv f'\pmod{E_2(A')}$; also $\pi'f'\in E_1(A')$ because its image lies in $A'$. By [F5], $\operatorname{res}^{V'}(f\,\mathrm dg)=\operatorname{Tr}_{V'}([\pi'f',g'])$ for all $f\in K'$ and $g\in K$, where $g'$ acts on $V'$ with matrix $g\delta_{ij}$. [F1, F2, F5, F7, step 1.2]

3.1 (Trace formula.) Let $\gamma\in F'$ for a finite potent $F\subseteq\operatorname{End}_k(V)$ and write $\gamma=D+U+L$ as its diagonal, strictly upper-triangular, and strictly lower-triangular block parts. All three matrices lie in the common finite potent subspace $F'$ of step 2.2. Since $U^n=L^n=0$, (T3) gives $\operatorname{Tr}_{V'}(U)=\operatorname{Tr}_{V'}(L)=0$, and (T4) applied within $F'$ gives $\operatorname{Tr}_{V'}(\gamma)=\operatorname{Tr}_{V'}(D)+\operatorname{Tr}_{V'}(U)+\operatorname{Tr}_{V'}(L)$. Each summand $V_i=x_i\otimes V$ is $D$-stable; repeated use of (T2) on this finite direct sum gives $\operatorname{Tr}_{V'}(D)=\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}_{V_i}(D|_{V_i})$. For each $i$, the isomorphism $\iota_i\colon V\to V_i$, $v\mapsto x_i\otimes v$, identifies $D|_{V_i}$ with $\gamma_{ii}$. Apply (T5) with $\varphi=\iota_i$ and $\psi=\gamma_{ii}\iota_i^{-1}$; since $\psi\varphi=\gamma_{ii}$ is finite potent, this gives $\operatorname{Tr}_{V_i}(D|_{V_i})=\operatorname{Tr}_V(\gamma_{ii})$. Therefore $\operatorname{Tr}_{V'}(\gamma)=\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}_V(\gamma_{ii})$ for every matrix with entries in one finite potent subspace $F$. [F1, F4, step 2.2]

4.1 (Matrix of the commutator.) For $f=\sum_jc_jx_j\in K'$ the matrix $(f_{ij})$ of multiplication by $f$ on $V'$ is as in [F2], and $\pi'$ has block-diagonal matrix $\pi\delta_{ij}$; hence $\pi'f'$ has matrix $(\pi f_{ij})$ and, since $g$ is central in $K'$, the commutator $[\pi'f',g']$ has matrix $([\pi f_{ij},g])_{ij}$. Each entry $[\pi f_{ij},g]$ lies in $E_0(A)$: indeed $\pi f_{ij}\in E_1(A)$ and $\pi f_{ij}\equiv f_{ij}\pmod{E_2(A)}$ as in step 2.3, while $g\equiv g\pmod{E_2(A)}$, so the commutator lies in $E_1(A)$ and is congruent to $[f_{ij},g]=0$ modulo $E_2(A)$. [F1, F2, F5, step 3.1, step 2.3]

5.1 (Evaluation of the trace.) The matrix entries of $[\pi'f',g']$ all lie in the finite potent subspace $E_0(A)$, so the trace formula of step 3.1 applies and gives $\operatorname{Tr}_{V'}([\pi'f',g'])=\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}_V([\pi f_{ii},g])$, the sum being finite over the $n$ diagonal blocks. [F4, step 3.1, step 4.1]

5.2 (Each diagonal block is a residue.) For each $i$ the endomorphism $\pi f_{ii}$ of $V$ lies in $E_1(A)$ and satisfies $\pi f_{ii}\equiv f_{ii}\pmod{E_2(A)}$ by step 2.3 applied to the stable subspace $A$; hence [F5] identifies $\operatorname{Tr}_V([\pi f_{ii},g])=\operatorname{res}^{V}(f_{ii}\,\mathrm dg)$. [F1, F5, step 2.3, step 4.1]

6.1 (The trace of the coefficient.) By [F3] one has $\sum_if_{ii}=\operatorname{Tr}_{K'/K}(f)$ for the matrix $(f_{ij})$ of multiplication by $f$ on the free $K$-module $K'$; substituting step 5.2 into step 5.1 and using the $k$-linearity of $\operatorname{res}^{V}$ ([F5]) in the coefficient, $\operatorname{Tr}_{V'}([\pi'f',g])=\sum_i\operatorname{res}^{V}(f_{ii}\,\mathrm dg)=\operatorname{res}^{V}(\operatorname{Tr}_{K'/K}(f)\,\mathrm dg)$, which together with step 2.3 is the asserted identity $\operatorname{res}^{V'}(f\,\mathrm dg)=\operatorname{res}^{V}(\operatorname{Tr}_{K'/K}(f)\,\mathrm dg)$. The choices involved (the basis $x_i$, the projection $\pi$) are the only uses of the Axiom of Choice [F6] beyond those inherited from the linear algebra suppliers; the commensurability class of $A'$ was shown in step 2.1 to be independent of the basis. [F3, F5, F6, step 2.3, step 5.1, step 5.2] ∎
