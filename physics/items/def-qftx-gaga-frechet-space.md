## Definition

A **Fréchet space** over $\mathbb C$ is a complex vector space $E$ with a Hausdorff locally convex vector-space topology generated by a separating countable family of seminorms $(p_n)_{n\ge1}$ and complete for its seminorm uniformity. Explicitly, use the translation-invariant compatible metric
$$d(x,y)=\sum_{n\ge1}2^{-n}\min\{1,p_n(x-y)\},$$
and require that this metric is complete. A seminorm is a function $p:E\to[0,\infty)$ satisfying $p(x+y)\le p(x)+p(y)$ and $p(cx)=|c|p(x)$ for $x,y\in E$ and $c\in\mathbb C$; separating means that for $x\ne0$ some $p_n(x)>0$. Locally convex means that every point has a neighborhood basis of convex sets. One may replace the defining family by the increasing family of its finite maxima.

Completeness here always refers to this vector-space uniformity. A sequence is Cauchy for the displayed metric exactly when it is Cauchy for every defining seminorm: each individual metric summand controls that seminorm at sufficiently small distances, and conversely the geometric tail of the metric series plus finitely many seminorm estimates controls the metric. Thus a seminorm-Cauchy series used in the accompanying proofs converges in $E$. The zero vector space is included, with its unique metric. This is the usual complete metrizable locally convex Fréchet convention.
