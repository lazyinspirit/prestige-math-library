## Statement

Let $E,B$ be jointly $C^k$ fields of [[def-em-fields]] on the laboratory region $U\cong I\times\Omega$ of an inertial chart of [[def-em-lab-geometry]] in SI units ([[def-em-si-conventions]]), $k\ge1$, and let $F$ be the electromagnetic two-form of [[def-em-electromagnetic-tensor]] with its fixed components $F_{0i}=-E_i/c$, $F_{ij}=\epsilon_{ijk}B_k$. Let $n\in V$ be a future unit observer vector, $\eta(n,n)=-1$, with rest space $n^\perp$ and positive rest metric ([[thm-sr-proper-time-and-observer-decomposition]] (c)), and suppose its coordinate velocity in the laboratory frame is $w$, $|w|<c$, so that $n=\gamma_w(1,w/c)$ with $\gamma_w:=(1-|w|^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$ ([[thm-sr-proper-time-and-observer-decomposition]] (d)-(e)). Then: (a) [Observer split and measured vectors] with $e_a:=F_{ab}n^b$, $b_a:=(*F)_{ab}n^b$ as in [[thm-sr-antisymmetric-observer-decomposition]], one has $e(n)=b(n)=0$, $e^\sharp,b^\sharp\in n^\perp$, the reconstruction $F=n^\flat\wedge e-*(n^\flat\wedge b)$ (so the six rest components $(e,b)$ determine $F$ at the event), and in the laboratory frame $$e_i=\frac{\gamma_w}{c}\bigl(E_i+(w\times B)_i\bigr),\qquad b_i=-\gamma_w\Bigl(B_i-\frac{(w\times E)_i}{c^2}\Bigr),\qquad e_0=-\frac{\gamma_w}{c^2}E\cdot w,\qquad b_0=\frac{\gamma_w}{c}B\cdot w.$$ Define the measured electric and magnetic vectors $E_n:=c\,e^\sharp$ and $B_n:=-b^\sharp$, the covectors first restricted to $n^\perp$ and raised with its positive rest metric (equivalently their spacetime metric-dual vectors, since both annihilate $n$); under the model assumption (A1) that an ideal field detector comoving with the observer at the reception event reads the components of $F$ in the observer's rest frame — equivalently that $E_n$ and $B_n$ are the electric and magnetic fields read there — the observed components are, in the observer's inertial rest frame, the arbitrary-direction boosted components of [[pthm-em-lorentz-field-transform]] (a′) for the vector parameter $\beta=w/c$: the components along $w$ equal $E_\parallel$ and $B_\parallel$, and the components perpendicular to $w$ equal $\gamma_w(E+w\times B)_\perp$ and $\gamma_w(B-w\times E/c^2)_\perp$. At $w=0$ the observer is the laboratory rest observer and reads $E,B$ themselves; there is no distinguished direction of motion. Orthogonality means $e(n)=b(n)=0$, not vanishing temporal covector components in an unrelated laboratory frame. (b) [Plane wave and exact frequency reading] let $K>0$, $\omega=cK$, a unit vector $s$, and complex amplitudes $e_{\mathrm a},b_{\mathrm a}\in\mathbb C^3$ be the data of a plane wave of [[def-em-plane-wave-definition]] whose amplitude equations hold as in [[pthm-em-plane-wave-maxwell]], so that the pair is a source-free smooth Maxwell field and $s$ is the propagation direction. Then the phase covector with components $k_a=(-\omega/c,(\omega/c)s_1,(\omega/c)s_2,(\omega/c)s_3)$ in the laboratory frame is a nonzero future null covector, and under the ideal-plane-wave reading rule of [[post-sr-ideal-plane-wave-model]] (ii) the observer reads the exact angular frequency $$\omega_n:=-c\,k(n)=\gamma_w\Bigl(1-\frac{w\cdot s}{c}\Bigr)\omega>0,$$ with the direction transformation (aberration), longitudinal formulas and two transverse protocols of [[pthm-sr-null-wave-doppler-and-aberration]] (i)-(v) apply with their nonzero-motion qualifications; at $w=0$ frequency and direction are unchanged. Successive inertial changes act on the same phase covector by [[thm-sr-tensor-and-current-transformation]] (ii); each stage updates the direction as well as the frequency, and positive successive frequency ratios multiply. This composition statement is a direct covector/reading calculation, not a nonexistent composition clause of the Doppler supplier. (c) [Status and scope] the observer split, the nullity of $k$ and the frequency formula are conditional deductions from the stated items, while the identification of $(E_n,B_n)$ and of $\omega_n$ with apparatus readings is the explicitly adopted ideal-detector model assumption (A1) together with the ideal-plane-wave model [[post-sr-ideal-plane-wave-model]] (with [[post-sr-ideal-clocks-and-light]] for the clock and light readings); no calibration, apparatus, statistical or empirical claim is made, no photon model and no identification of frequency with energy by a factor $\hbar$ is used, and the statement is pointwise at a reception event: the decomposition and the reconstruction of clause (a) hold pointwise for any future unit observer $n$, while the frequency and aberration readings of clause (b) are stated for inertial observers — an accelerating observer requires its own analysis, since differentiating the decomposition adds derivatives of $n$ — and media, dispersion, modulation, boundaries and finite line widths are outside the item.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The inertial chart $x$ of the laboratory region ([[def-em-lab-geometry]]) with its spatial basis $(e_1,e_2,e_3)$ and the jointly $C^k$ fields $E,B$ of [[def-em-fields]], $k\ge1$, in the SI units of [[def-em-si-conventions]]; the electromagnetic two-form $F$ of [[def-em-electromagnetic-tensor]] with the dictionary and dual conventions of [F1]; a future unit observer vector $n$ with coordinate velocity $w$ in the laboratory frame, $|w|<c$, as in [F2]; the plane-wave data of [F4]; the adopted model postulates [F5], [F6] and [F8] and the ideal-detector model assumption [A1].

[F1] In the chart $x$, with $\eta=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$, $\varepsilon_{0123}=+1$ and $\varepsilon^{0123}=-1$, the electromagnetic two-form has $F_{00}=0$, $F_{0i}=-E_i/c$, $F_{ij}=\epsilon_{ijk}B_k$, $F^{0i}=E_i/c$, $F^{i0}=-E_i/c$ and $F^{ij}=\epsilon_{ijk}B_k$; its Hodge dual is $(*F)_{ab}=\tfrac12\varepsilon_{ab}{}^{cd}F_{cd}$ with $*(*F)=-F$; for a future unit $n$ the rest components are $e_a:=F_{ab}n^b$ and $b_a:=(*F)_{ab}n^b$, one has $e(n)=b(n)=0$ and the reconstruction $F=n^\flat\wedge e-*(n^\flat\wedge b)$ with $n^\flat=\eta(n,\cdot)$, the six components $(e,b)$ determining $F$ at the event; for the laboratory rest observer one has $e_i=E_i/c$ and $b_i=-B_i$, and the contractions are $\tfrac12F_{ab}F^{ab}=|B|^2-|E|^2/c^2$ and $F_{ab}(*F)^{ab}=4\,E\cdot B/c$. The fields carry V m$^{-1}$ and T, and the fractional structure is that of the frame's spatial Euclidean space ([[def-em-electromagnetic-tensor]] (a), (b), [[thm-sr-antisymmetric-observer-decomposition]] (a), (b), (c), [[def-em-fields]] (c), [[def-em-si-conventions]] (b)).

[F2] Let $n\in V$ be future unit timelike, $\eta(n,n)=-1$, with coordinate velocity $w$ in the laboratory chart, $|w|<c$, so that $n=\gamma_w(1,w/c)$ with $\gamma_w:=(1-|w|^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$. Then $n^\perp$ is a three-dimensional subspace on which $\eta$ restricts to a positive-definite form, the map $h(v)=v+\eta(n,v)n$ is the linear projection onto $n^\perp$ along $\operatorname{span}(n)$, each $v$ has the unique decomposition $v=-\eta(n,v)n+h(v)$, and $w$ is the velocity of the worldline with four-velocity $cn$ relative to the lab-rest observer ([[thm-sr-proper-time-and-observer-decomposition]] (c), (d), (e), [[def-lorentz-boost]]).

[F3] The passive boost $B(w/c)$ is a proper orthochronous Lorentz map; its
matrix in $(e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3)$ is $B^0{}_0=\gamma_w$,
$B^0{}_i=B^i{}_0=-\gamma_w w_i/c$ and
$B^i{}_j=\delta^i{}_j+\frac{\gamma_w-1}{|w/c|^2}\frac{w_i}{c}\frac{w_j}{c}$
([[def-lorentz-boost]], [[thm-sr-proper-orthochronous-lorentz-algebra]] (i)). In
the inertial chart $x'=B(w/c)x+b$ the dictionary components of the same $F$ are
the boosted components of [[pthm-em-lorentz-field-transform]] (a′) with vector
parameter $v=w$: $E'_\parallel=E_\parallel$, $B'_\parallel=B_\parallel$,
$E'_\perp=\gamma_w(E+w\times B)_\perp$ and
$B'_\perp=\gamma_w(B-w\times E/c^2)_\perp$ along $\widehat w$, equivalently
$$E'=\gamma_w(E+w\times B)-\frac{\gamma_w^2}{\gamma_w+1}\frac{w(w\cdot E)}{c^2},\qquad B'=\gamma_w(B-w\times E/c^2)-\frac{\gamma_w^2}{\gamma_w+1}\frac{w(w\cdot B)}{c^2},$$
with $E'=E$, $B'=B$ for $w=0$; and the affine change is admissible because
$B(w/c)$ is proper orthochronous
([[pthm-em-lorentz-field-transform]] (a′), (e),
[[post-sr-inertial-covariance]] (ii)).

[F4] Plane-wave data: $K>0$, $\omega=cK$, a unit vector $s$, and complex amplitudes $e_{\mathrm a},b_{\mathrm a}\in\mathbb C^3$; the associated plane wave of [[def-em-plane-wave-definition]] has wavevector $\mathbf k:=Ks$ (so $|\mathbf k|=K=\omega/c$ and the propagation direction $\widehat{\mathbf k}=s$), phase $\theta(x,t)=\mathbf k\cdot x-\omega t$ of class $C^\infty$ on $\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R$, and amplitude equations $\mathbf k\cdot e_{\mathrm a}=0$, $\mathbf k\cdot b_{\mathrm a}=0$, $\mathbf k\times e_{\mathrm a}=\omega b_{\mathrm a}$ and $\mathbf k\times b_{\mathrm a}=-(\omega/c^2)e_{\mathrm a}$; by [[pthm-em-plane-wave-maxwell]] (a) the pair is therefore a solution of the four source-free smooth-branch vacuum Maxwell equations on $\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R$ [[def-em-plane-wave-definition]] (a), (b), (c), [[pthm-em-plane-wave-maxwell]] (a), [[post-em-vacuum-maxwell]], [[def-em-si-conventions]].

[F5] Adopted ideal plane-wave reading rule: an ideal observer of the model with four-velocity $U_o=cn$, $n$ future unit timelike, that receives the wave reads the angular frequency $\omega_n:=-ck(n)=-k(U_o)$ at the reception event of its worldline; the ideal wave is the adopted monochromatic plane-wave model with future null wave covector, and the model carries no quantum photon and no $\hbar\omega$ identification ([[post-sr-ideal-plane-wave-model]] (i)-(iv), [[post-sr-ideal-clocks-and-light]] (ii)).

[F6] The exact classical Doppler theorem [[pthm-sr-null-wave-doppler-and-aberration]] (i)-(vi) applies to the ideal wave of [F4]: for inertial charts $x'=B(\beta)x+b$ with $|\beta|<1$ and frame-$x$ data $(\omega,s)$, it gives (i) the frame-$x'$ data $(\omega',s')$ with $\omega'=\gamma(1-\beta\cdot s)\omega>0$ and $s'=\bigl[s+\frac{\gamma-1}{|\beta|^2}(\beta\cdot s)\beta-\gamma\beta\bigr]/\bigl[\gamma(1-\beta\cdot s)\bigr]$ (and $s'=s$, $\omega'=\omega$ for $\beta=0$); (ii) the reading $\omega_o=\gamma_w(1-w\cdot s/c)\omega>0$ of an ideal observer of coordinate velocity $w$, together with $\nu'=\gamma(1-\beta\cdot s)\nu$ and $\lambda'=\lambda/[\gamma(1-\beta\cdot s)]$; (iii) the longitudinal cases $s=\pm\widehat\beta$; (iv) the two transverse protocols $\beta\cdot s=0$ and $\beta\cdot s=|\beta|^2$; (v) the aberration relation $\cos\theta_{x'}=(\cos\theta_x-|\beta|)/(1-|\beta|\cos\theta_x)$ with its monotonicity; and (vi) its scope and exclusions. Its hypotheses — the adopted model postulates and frame data with future null covector — are those of the model it states.

[F7] Covector law and composition: if $\theta(p)=k_ax^a(p)+\theta_0$ is an affine scalar function with constant covector $k$ and $x'=\Lambda x+b$ is an inertial chart, then $\theta(p)=k'_ax'^a(p)+\theta'_0$ with $k'_a=(\Lambda^{-1})^b{}_ak_b$ and $\theta'_0=\theta_0-k'_ab^a$; for successive affine transitions $x_1=\Lambda_1x_0+b_1$ and $x_2=\Lambda_2x_1+b_2$ the composite is $x_2=\Lambda_2\Lambda_1x_0+(\Lambda_2b_1+b_2)$ ([[thm-sr-tensor-and-current-transformation]] (ii), [[post-sr-inertial-covariance]] (ii)).

[F8] Adopted ideal-clock and ideal-light assumptions: an ideal clock carried along a future timelike $C^1$ curve reads $c^{-1}\int_a^b\sqrt{-\eta(x'(s),x'(s))}\,\mathrm ds$, and the ideal vacuum light rays are exactly the future null straight lines with coordinate speed $c$ in every inertial chart ([[post-sr-ideal-clocks-and-light]] (i), (ii)).

[A1] Adopted ideal-detector model assumption: an ideal field detector comoving with the observer at the reception event reads the components of $F$ in the observer's rest frame; equivalently, the measured vectors $E_n=c\,e^\sharp$ and $B_n=-b^\sharp$ are the electric and magnetic fields read there. This is an adopted model assumption of this item, not a theorem, and it carries no calibration, apparatus, statistical or empirical claim.

[F9] Every proper orthochronous matrix admits the factorization $\Lambda=B(-\alpha)\operatorname{diag}(1,R)$ with $|\alpha|<1$ and a spatial rotation $R$ ([[thm-sr-proper-orthochronous-lorentz-algebra]] (iii)); in the frame's spatial coordinates $R^{\mathsf T}R=RR^{\mathsf T}=I_3$, as in [[pthm-em-lorentz-field-transform]] (c) and its Proof step 1.2.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct evaluation of the observer decomposition, the explicit boost to the observer's rest frame, and the exact Doppler theorem for the plane wave.

1.1 (Clause (a): laboratory components of $e$ and $b$) By [F2], $n^0=\gamma_w$ and $n^i=\gamma_w w_i/c$, so with the dictionary [F1] the covariant components are $e_0=F_{00}n^0+F_{0i}n^i=-(\gamma_w/c^2)E\cdot w$ and $e_i=F_{i0}n^0+F_{ij}n^j=\frac{\gamma_w}{c}E_i+\frac{\gamma_w}{c}\epsilon_{ijk}w_jB_k=\frac{\gamma_w}{c}\bigl(E_i+(w\times B)_i\bigr)$, using $F_{i0}=-F_{0i}=E_i/c$ and $(w\times B)_i=\epsilon_{ijk}w_jB_k$ [F1]. For the dual, the definition $(*F)_{ab}=\tfrac12\varepsilon_{ab}{}^{cd}F_{cd}$ and the components of [F1] give $(*F)_{0i}=B_i$ and $(*F)_{ij}=\epsilon_{ijk}E_k/c$: indeed $\varepsilon_{0i}{}^{jk}=\varepsilon_{0ijk}=\epsilon_{ijk}$ because raising the two spatial indices is free and $(0,i,j,k)$ is an even permutation of $(0,1,2,3)$ exactly when $(i,j,k)$ is even, so $(*F)_{0i}=\tfrac12\epsilon_{ijk}F_{jk}=\tfrac12\epsilon_{ijk}\epsilon_{jk\ell}B_\ell=B_i$ with $\epsilon_{ijk}\epsilon_{jk\ell}=2\delta_{i\ell}$ (direct enumeration); and $\varepsilon_{ij}{}^{0k}=\eta^{00}\eta^{kk}\varepsilon_{ij0k}=-\varepsilon_{ij0k}=-\epsilon_{ijk}$, since $(0,i,j,k)\mapsto(i,j,0,k)$ is an even permutation, so $(*F)_{ij}=\varepsilon_{ij}{}^{0k}F_{0k}=(-\epsilon_{ijk})(-E_k/c)=\epsilon_{ijk}E_k/c$. Hence $b_0=(*F)_{00}n^0+(*F)_{0i}n^i=\frac{\gamma_w}{c}B\cdot w$ and $b_i=(*F)_{i0}n^0+(*F)_{ij}n^j=-\gamma_wB_i+\frac{\gamma_w}{c^2}\epsilon_{ijk}w_jE_k=-\gamma_w\bigl(B_i-\frac{(w\times E)_i}{c^2}\bigr)$. These are the four displayed component formulas of clause (a); in particular the laboratory temporal components $e_0,b_0$ need not vanish, so the orthogonality of the decomposition cannot be read as the vanishing of these laboratory components. [given, F1, F2, algebra]

1.2 (Clause (a): annihilation, raising, reconstruction) Since $F$ and $*F$ are alternating, $e(n)=F_{ab}n^an^b=0$ and $b(n)=(*F)_{ab}n^an^b=0$; equivalently $n^ae_a=n^ab_a=0$ [F1]. For the metric-dual vectors $e^\sharp,b^\sharp$, defined by $\eta(e^\sharp,v)=e(v)$ and $\eta(b^\sharp,v)=b(v)$ for all $v$, this gives $\eta(e^\sharp,n)=\eta(b^\sharp,n)=0$, so $e^\sharp,b^\sharp\in n^\perp$; since both covectors annihilate $n$, restricting them to $n^\perp$ and raising with the positive rest metric of [F2] gives the same vectors, and this is the raising used in the definitions $E_n=c\,e^\sharp$, $B_n=-b^\sharp$. By [F1] the reconstruction $F=n^\flat\wedge e-*(n^\flat\wedge b)$ holds, and because the dictionary formulas of [F1] recover the six components of $F$ from $(e,b)$, the six rest components determine $F$ at the event. The measured vectors lie in the three-dimensional rest space $n^\perp$; they are not the laboratory coordinate triples $(e_1,e_2,e_3)$ and $(b_1,b_2,b_3)$. [given, F1, F2]

1.3 (Clause (a): the observer's inertial rest frame and the observed components) At $w=0$, $B(0)$ is the identity and $n=e_0$, so the rest frame is the laboratory frame and the displayed identity is immediate without a division by $|w/c|^2$. For $w\ne0$, applying the matrix of [F3] to the vector $n=\gamma_w(1,w/c)$ of [F2] gives $\bigl(B(w/c)n\bigr)^0=\gamma_w\bigl(\gamma_w-\frac{w}{c}\cdot\frac{\gamma_w w}{c}\bigr)=\gamma_w^2(1-|w|^2/c^2)=1$ and $\bigl(B(w/c)n\bigr)_{\mathrm{vec}}=\frac{\gamma_w w}{c}+\frac{\gamma_w-1}{|w/c|^2}\Bigl(\frac{w}{c}\cdot\frac{\gamma_w w}{c}\Bigr)\frac{w}{c}-\gamma_w\frac{w}{c}\gamma_w=\gamma_w\frac{w}{c}\bigl[1+(\gamma_w-1)-\gamma_w\bigr]=0$; hence $n$ has coordinates $(1,0,0,0)$ in the chart $x'=B(w/c)x+b$, that is $n=e'_0$: the chart $x'$ is the observer's inertial rest frame, and it is an admissible inertial change of description because $B(w/c)$ is proper orthochronous [F3]. By clause (a′) of [F3] the components of the same $F$ read in $x'$ are the boosted components with vector parameter $v=w$: $E'_\parallel=E_\parallel$, $B'_\parallel=B_\parallel$, $E'_\perp=\gamma_w(E+w\times B)_\perp$ and $B'_\perp=\gamma_w(B-w\times E/c^2)_\perp$ along $\widehat w$, with the displayed closed formulas, and $E'=E$, $B'=B$ when $w=0$. Reading the dictionary [F1] in the rest frame with $n=e'_0$ gives $e'_0=b'_0=0$, $e'_i=F'_{i0}=-F'_{0i}=E'_i/c$ and $b'_i=(*F')_{i0}=-(*F')_{0i}=-B'_i$; hence the vectors $E_n=c\,e^\sharp$ and $B_n=-b^\sharp$ have rest-frame components $(0,E')$ and $(0,B')$. Under the adopted model assumption [A1] the observed electric and magnetic fields in the rest frame are $E'$ and $B'$, that is: the components along $w$ are $E_\parallel$ and $B_\parallel$, the components perpendicular to $w$ are $\gamma_w(E+w\times B)_\perp$ and $\gamma_w(B-w\times E/c^2)_\perp$, and at $w=0$ the observer is the laboratory rest observer reading $E,B$ themselves with no distinguished direction of motion. The identification of the mathematical vectors with apparatus readings is exactly [A1] and is assumed, not proved. [given, F1, F2, F3, A1, algebra]

1.4 (Clause (b): the phase covector, nonzero, future and null) By [F4] the wavevector is $\mathbf k=Ks$ with $|\mathbf k|=K=\omega/c$ and propagation direction $\widehat{\mathbf k}=s$, and the phase is $\theta(x,t)=\mathbf k\cdot x-\omega t$. Its coordinate derivative components are $k_0=\partial\theta/\partial x^0=c^{-1}\partial_t\theta=-\omega/c$ and $k_i=\partial\theta/\partial x^i=(\omega/c)s_i$ ([[def-directional-and-partial-derivatives]] with $x^0=ct$), so the phase covector is $k_a=(-\omega/c,(\omega/c)s_1,(\omega/c)s_2,(\omega/c)s_3)$ and $\theta(p)=k_ax^a(p)$ up to an additive constant. Raising with $\eta$ gives $k^0=-k_0=\omega/c>0$ and $k_{\mathrm{vec}}=(k_1,k_2,k_3)=(\omega/c)s$, so $\eta(k^\sharp,k^\sharp)=-(\omega/c)^2+(\omega/c)^2|s|^2=0$ and $k^\sharp\ne0$: the phase covector is nonzero, future ($k^0>0$) and null. By the amplitude equations of [F4] the fields are a source-free smooth Maxwell field on the whole-space region. [given, F1, F4, algebra]

2.1 (Clause (b): exact frequency reading) At the reception event the ideal observer of [F5] with four-velocity $U_o=cn$ reads $\omega_n=-ck(n)=-k(U_o)$. With $k(n)=k_an^a=-\frac{\omega}{c}\gamma_w+\frac{\omega}{c}\frac{w\cdot s}{c}\gamma_w=-\frac{\omega}{c}\gamma_w\bigl(1-\frac{w\cdot s}{c}\bigr)$ from [F2] and step 1.4, this gives $\omega_n=\gamma_w\bigl(1-\frac{w\cdot s}{c}\bigr)\omega>0$, where the positivity is clause (ii) of [F6], whose formula this is with the frame-$x$ data $(\omega,s)$ of [F4] and the observer velocity $w$; its hypotheses hold by step 1.4 and the adopted model postulates [F5], [F6]. At $w=0$ one has $\gamma_w=1$ and $\omega_n=\omega$, and the reader is the laboratory rest observer of clause (a) reading $E,B$. [given, step 1.4, F2, F5, F6, algebra]

2.2 (Clause (b): aberration, longitudinal and transverse protocols) The data $(\omega,s)$ of [F4] are exactly the frame-$x$ data of the Doppler theorem [F6] for its future null wave covector (step 1.4), and the ideal plane-wave, ideal-clock and covariance postulates it adopts are those of the framework [F5], [F8]. 2.3 (Clause (b): composition of successive inertial changes) Let $x_1=\Lambda_1x_0+b_1$ and $x_2=\Lambda_2x_1+b_2$ be successive inertial changes with proper orthochronous $\Lambda_1,\Lambda_2$.
