## Statement

The untwisted affine Weyl group has a normal translation subgroup indexed by the finite coroot lattice $Q^\vee$. Every element is uniquely $t_\gamma w$ with $\gamma\in Q^\vee$, $w\in W_0$, and
$$t_\gamma t_\eta=t_{\gamma+\eta},\qquad wt_\gamma w^{-1}=t_{w\gamma}.$$
With $B$ the normalized invariant form used in the loop realization and $\nu(\gamma)=B(\gamma,-)$ on the finite Cartan, the translation action on the full dual Cartan is
$$t_\gamma(\lambda)=\lambda+\lambda(c)\nu(\gamma)-\left(\lambda(\gamma)+\tfrac12B(\gamma,\gamma)\lambda(c)\right)\delta.$$
Thus $W_{\mathrm{aff}}\cong Q^\vee\rtimes W_0$. The convention sometimes written $W_0\ltimes Q^\vee$ denotes the same group, with its second factor normal.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The full untwisted affine algebra in its normalized loop convention.

[F1] Its Cartan and simple generators agree with the GCM realization by [[thm-loop-and-affine-gcm-presentations-are-isomorphic]].

[F2] The affine root and coroot are $\alpha_0=\delta-\theta$, $h_0=c-\theta^\vee$, with $\theta$ long and $(\theta,\theta)=2$, by [[lem-the-affine-simple-root-alpha-zero-is-delta-minus-the-highest-root]].

[F3] Reflections act by $s_i\lambda=\lambda-\lambda(h_i)\alpha_i$, by [[def-simple-reflections-and-the-kac-moody-weyl-group]].

[F4] The normalized invariant form $B$ is fixed by [[def-residue-two-cocycle-on-a-loop-algebra]]; finite roots, coroots, its positive definite real restriction and simple generation are [[lem-finite-semisimple-cartan-root-and-string-structure]].

[F5] The finite Weyl group is finite, preserves the root/coroot lattices, and is generated by its simple reflections by [[lem-finite-weyl-positive-roots-and-simple-reflections]].

## Proof

1.1 Extend the $\nu$ in the statement to vanish on $c,d$. The displayed operators fix $\delta$ and preserve $\lambda(c)$. In a composition the additional cross term in the $\delta$ coefficient is $B(\gamma,\eta)\lambda(c)$, so symmetry gives $t_\gamma t_\eta=t_{\gamma+\eta}$. Thus $t_0=1$ and $t_{-\gamma}$ is the inverse. If $t_\gamma=1$, applying it to every level-zero functional gives $\lambda(\gamma)=0$ for all finite $\lambda$, hence $\gamma=0$. [F1, F4, algebra]

1.2 In the Euclidean coroot system let $V$ be the real span of $W_0\theta^\vee$. It is nonzero and $W_0$-invariant. For a coroot $\beta$ not orthogonal to $V$, some $v\in V$ has $(v,\beta)\ne0$; the reflection formula makes $v-s_\beta v$ a nonzero multiple of $\beta$, so $\beta\in V$. Thus every coroot belongs to either $V$ or $V^\perp$. If both classes occurred, finite roots would likewise split into two nonempty orthogonal classes. The corresponding root spaces and coroot spans would form commuting ideals: a mixed root sum lies in neither span, so is not a root; mixed Cartan actions vanish; opposite-root brackets lie in their corresponding coroot spans. This contradicts simplicity of $\mathfrak g$. Consequently $V$ is the entire coroot space. [F4, F5, algebra]

2.1 Finite $W_0$ fixes $c,d,\delta$, preserves $B$ and satisfies $w\nu(\gamma)=\nu(w\gamma)$. Substitution proves $wt_\gamma w^{-1}=t_{w\gamma}$. Since $\nu(\theta^\vee)=\theta$ and $B(\theta^\vee,\theta^\vee)=2$, substitution also gives $$t_{\theta^\vee}s_\theta(\lambda)=\lambda-\left(\lambda(c)-\lambda(\theta^\vee)\right)(\delta-\theta)=s_0\lambda.$$ Here $s_\theta\in W_0$ by F5. Hence $t_{\theta^\vee}$ and all its finite Weyl conjugates belong to $W_{\mathrm{aff}}$. [F2, F3, F4, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Let $M$ be the integer span of $W_0\theta^\vee$. For any coroot $\beta$, step 1.2 supplies an orbit coroot $\alpha$ with $(\alpha,\beta)\ne0$. It has minimal coroot length because $\theta$ is long. If proportional, reducedness gives $\beta=\pm\alpha\in M$. Otherwise integrality and strict Cauchy–Schwarz give $$0<\left|\frac{2(\alpha,\beta)}{(\beta,\beta)}\right|<\frac{2\|\alpha\|}{\|\beta\|}\le2.$$ The integer has absolute value one. Since $M$ is Weyl invariant, $\alpha-s_\beta\alpha=\pm\beta\in M$. Every coroot belongs to $M$, so $M=Q^\vee$. Steps 1.1 and 2.1 therefore put every $t_\gamma$, $\gamma\in Q^\vee$, in $W_{\mathrm{aff}}$. [F2, F4, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 1.2, algebra]

4.1 The products $t_\gamma w$ form a group by steps 1.1 and 2.1 and lattice preservation. They contain all finite simple reflections and $s_0=t_{\theta^\vee}s_\theta$, so form all of $W_{\mathrm{aff}}$. Let $\Lambda$ vanish on $\mathfrak h,d$ and have $\Lambda(c)=1$. Finite $W_0$ fixes it, whereas $t_\gamma\Lambda=\Lambda+\nu(\gamma)-B(\gamma,\gamma)\delta/2$. Thus $t_\gamma\in W_0$ forces $\nu(\gamma)=0$, hence $\gamma=0$. Trivial intersection gives uniqueness by comparing two products. Zero translation, identity finite factor and rank one are included. Every lattice expression is a finite sum and no AC is used. [F1, F4, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
