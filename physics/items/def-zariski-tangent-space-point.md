## Definition

Let $X$ be a scheme and $x\in X$. Write $C_xX=\mathfrak m_x/\mathfrak m_x^2$
for the intrinsic cotangent space from
[[def-zariski-cotangent-space-point]]. The **intrinsic Zariski tangent space**
of $X$ at $x$ is its linear dual over the residue field:
$$T_xX:=\operatorname{Hom}_{\kappa(x)}(C_xX,\kappa(x)).$$
If $\mathfrak m_x=0$, then $C_xX=0$ and $T_xX=0$.

For a $k$-scheme $f:X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$
([[def-scheme-over-base]]), the **relative tangent space**
$T_{X/k,x}$ is separately defined as the $\kappa(x)$-dual of
$\Omega_{X/k,x}\otimes_{\mathcal O_{X,x}}\kappa(x)$
([[def-relative-cotangent-space]]). This definition makes no identification
between $T_xX$ and $T_{X/k,x}$ at a nonrational point. At a $k$-rational point
they agree by the cotangent-space isomorphism
[[thm-cotangent-space-maximal-ideal-quotient]].

If $f:X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is locally of finite type, then $T_xX$ is
finite-dimensional. Indeed, an affine neighborhood has coordinate algebra
finite type over $k$, hence Noetherian; its local ring is a localization and is
Noetherian. Its maximal ideal is therefore finitely generated, so the images
of a finite generating set span $\mathfrak m_x/\mathfrak m_x^2$. The dual of a
finite-dimensional vector space is finite-dimensional. This argument uses no
Axiom of Choice.

For example, at the closed origin of $X=\operatorname{Spec}k[t]$, the local
ring is $k[t]_{(t)}$ and its maximal ideal is generated by $t$. Hence
$C_0X=(t)/(t^2)\cong k$ with basis the class of $t$, so $T_0X\cong k$. At
the generic point $\eta=(0)$, the local ring is the field $k(t)$ and its
maximal ideal is zero, so $C_\eta X=0$ and $T_\eta X=0$. In contrast, the
relative tangent space over $k$ at $\eta$ is one-dimensional: the generic stalk
of $\Omega_{X/k}$ is $k(t)\,\mathrm dt$, so $T_{X/k,\eta}\cong k(t)$. Thus
the two notions can differ at a nonrational point.

The definition also applies without a reducedness hypothesis. For the closed
point of $D_k=\operatorname{Spec}(k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2))$
([[def-dual-numbers-scheme]]), every $a+b\epsilon$ with $a\ne0$ has inverse
$a^{-1}-a^{-2}b\epsilon$. Thus its local ring is $k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$
with maximal ideal $(\epsilon)$ and square zero. Hence $C_xD_k\cong k\epsilon$
and $T_xD_k\cong k$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A scheme $X$, a point $x\in X$, and, for the finiteness assertion,
a field $k$ and a morphism $X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ locally of finite type.

[F1] [[def-zariski-cotangent-space-point]]: $C_xX=\mathfrak m_x/\mathfrak m_x^2$ is a $\kappa(x)$-vector space. At the origin of $\operatorname{Spec}k[t]$ it is $k$ with basis the class of $t$, while at the generic point it is zero.

[F2] [[def-locally-finite-type-and-finite-type-morphism]]: locally of finite type means every point has an affine open neighborhood $U=\operatorname{Spec}B$ over an affine base $\operatorname{Spec}A$, with $A\to B$ of finite type.

[F3] [[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]: a commutative $R$-algebra of finite type has the form $R[a_1,\ldots,a_n]$ for some finite list, so the evaluation map $R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\to R[a_1,\ldots,a_n]$ is surjective.

[F4] [[lem-field-is-noetherian]]: every field is a Noetherian ring.

[F5] [[cor-finite-variable-polynomial-ring-noetherian]]: if $R$ is Noetherian, then $R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ is Noetherian for every $n\in\mathbb N$.

[F6] [[thm-stalk-structure-sheaf-prime-localization]]: for $\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}B$, $\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Spec}B,\mathfrak p}\cong B_{\mathfrak p}$.

[F7] [[def-localisation-at-a-prime-ideal]]: if $\mathfrak p$ is prime in $B$, then $B_{\mathfrak p}$ consists of fractions $b/s$ with $s\notin\mathfrak p$.

[F8] [[def-noetherian-ring-and-module]]: a commutative ring is Noetherian exactly when every ideal is finitely generated.

[F9] [[def-relative-cotangent-space]]: $T_{X/S,x}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\kappa(x)}(\Omega_{X/S,x}\otimes_{\mathcal O_{X,x}}\kappa(x),\kappa(x))$.

[F10] [[lem-sheaf-differentials-affine-compatibility]]: on an affine scheme, $\Omega_{X/S}(D(g))\cong\Omega_{B_g/A}$, compatibly with the localization maps.

[F11] [[lem-differentials-polynomial-algebra-free]]: $\Omega_{A[x]/A}$ is free on $\mathrm dx$ when there is one polynomial variable.

[F12] [[lem-differentials-localization]]: Kähler differentials commute with localization.

[F13] [[thm-cotangent-space-maximal-ideal-quotient]]: at a $k$-rational point, $\mathfrak m_x/\mathfrak m_x^2\cong\Omega_{X/k,x}\otimes_{\mathcal O_{X,x}}\kappa(x)$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Finite-dimensionality in the locally finite-type case. Choose an affine neighborhood $\operatorname{Spec}B$ of $x$ provided by [F2], with $B$ a finite-type $k$-algebra. By [F3], for some finite list $b_1,\ldots,b_n$ the evaluation map $P=k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\to B$ is surjective. By [F4] and [F5], $k$ and then $P$ are Noetherian. The preimage in $P$ of any ideal of $B$ is an ideal of $P$; it is finitely generated by [F8], and its generators map to generators of the ideal in $B$. Thus $B$ is Noetherian. If $x$ corresponds to $\mathfrak p\subset B$, [F6] identifies $\mathcal O_{X,x}$ with $B_{\mathfrak p}$, and [F7] describes this localization by fractions. For any ideal $J\subseteq B_{\mathfrak p}$, its contraction $I=\{b\in B:b/1\in J\}$ is an ideal of $B$, hence is generated by finitely many $b_1',\ldots,b_r'$ by [F8]. If $b/s\in J$, then $b/1=(s/1)(b/s)\in J$, so $b\in I$ and $b=\sum_i c_i b_i'$; consequently $b/s=\sum_i(c_i/s)(b_i'/1)$. Conversely each $b_i'/1$ lies in $J$, so they generate $J$. Thus $B_{\mathfrak p}$ is Noetherian. Its maximal ideal $\mathfrak m_x$ is finitely generated by [F8], say by $a_1,\ldots,a_r$. Modulo $\mathfrak m_x^2$, every element $\sum_i b_i a_i$ is the $\kappa(x)$-linear combination $\sum_i\overline b_i[a_i]$, so $C_xX$ is finite-dimensional by [F1]. The dual is finite-dimensional as well: a surjection $\kappa(x)^r\twoheadrightarrow C_xX$ induces an injection $\operatorname{Hom}_{\kappa(x)}(C_xX,\kappa(x))\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\kappa(x)}(\kappa(x)^r,\kappa(x))$. This uses only finite generating lists and ordinary induction on the polynomial degree; no dependent choice or Axiom of Choice is invoked. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, algebra]

1.2 Difference at the generic point of the affine line. Let $X=\operatorname{Spec}k[t]$ and $\eta=(0)$. By [F6] and [F7], its local ring is the field $k(t)$ with maximal ideal zero, so [F1] gives $C_\eta X=0$ and hence $T_\eta X=0$. On this affine chart [F11] gives $\Omega_{k[t]/k}=k[t]\,\mathrm dt$; using [F10] and [F12] to pass to the generic stalk gives $\Omega_{X/k,\eta}=k(t)\,\mathrm dt$. Its residue-field fibre is the one-dimensional $k(t)$-vector space $k(t)\,\mathrm dt$, so [F9] gives $T_{X/k,\eta}\cong k(t)$. This proves that the intrinsic and relative tangent spaces need not agree at a nonrational point. [F1, F6, F7, F9, F10, F11, F12]

2.1 Rational-point comparison. If $x$ is $k$-rational, [F13] identifies the intrinsic cotangent space with the relative cotangent space. Taking $k$-linear duals and using [F9] identifies $T_xX$ with $T_{X/k,x}$. No such identification is asserted for nonrational points. [F9, F13] ∎
