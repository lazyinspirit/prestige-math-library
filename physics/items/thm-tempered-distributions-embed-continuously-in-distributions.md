## Statement

Restriction to compactly supported tests defines an injective complex-linear
map

$$\rho:\mathcal S'(\mathbb R^n)\longrightarrow\mathcal D'(\mathbb R^n).$$

It is continuous from weak to weak topology and from strong to strong
topology.  No assertion is made that the strong topology on $\mathcal S'$ is
the subspace topology inherited from $\mathcal D'$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The continuous dense inclusion $j:\mathcal D\hookrightarrow\mathcal S$ ([[lem-test-function-inclusion-in-schwartz-space-is-continuous]]).

[F1] Weak and strong topologies on $\mathcal S'$ are generated by point tests and bounded Schwartz-test sets ([[def-weak-and-strong-topologies-on-tempered-distributions]]).

[F2] The corresponding topologies on $\mathcal D'$ use point tests and bounded LF-test sets ([[def-weak-and-strong-topologies-on-distributions]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** transpose the dense continuous inclusion.

1.1 Define $\rho(u)(\psi)=u(j\psi)$.  Since $j$ and $u$ are continuous complex-linear maps, $\rho(u)$ is a distribution, and $\rho$ is complex-linear.  If $\rho(u)=0$, then $u$ vanishes on the dense image of $j$; continuity makes $u=0$ on $\mathcal S$.  Thus $\rho$ is injective. [given]

1.2 For each $\psi\in\mathcal D$, $p_\psi(\rho u)=p_{j\psi}(u)$.  Hence every weak seminorm on the target pulls back to a weak seminorm on the source, proving weak continuity. [F1, F2]

2.1 If $B\subseteq\mathcal D$ is bounded, continuity and linearity of $j$ imply that $j(B)$ is bounded in $\mathcal S$. [F1, F2, step 1.1]

$$p_B(\rho u)=\sup_{\psi\in B}|u(j\psi)|=p_{j(B)}(u),$$

which proves strong continuity.  Density was essential only for injectivity; continuity alone would not prove that conclusion. [F1, F2, given] ∎
