## Definition

Let $E$ be a finite-dimensional real vector space with a positive definite symmetric bilinear form $(\ ,\ )$. A **finite reduced crystallographic root system** is a finite subset $\Phi\subseteq E\setminus\{0\}$ spanning $E$, such that for every $\alpha,\beta\in\Phi$ the integer $\langle\beta,\alpha^\vee\rangle=2(\beta,\alpha)/(\alpha,\alpha)$ is integral, the reflection $s_\alpha(x)=x-\langle x,\alpha^\vee\rangle\alpha$ preserves $\Phi$, and the only scalar multiples of $\alpha$ in $\Phi$ are $\alpha,-\alpha$. The **Weyl group** $W$ is the subgroup of linear isometries of $E$ generated by these reflections. A **root reflection** means one of the $s_\alpha$. The empty root system on $E=0$ is allowed, with $W=\{1\}$.

Choose $t\in E$ such that $(t,\alpha)\ne0$ for every root. Put $\Phi^+=\{\alpha:(t,\alpha)>0\}$ and $\Phi^-=-\Phi^+$. A positive root is **simple** if it is not the sum of two positive roots. The following positive-root lemma proves that the simple roots form a basis $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r$ and that the simple reflections $s_i=s_{\alpha_i}$ generate $W$; no such assertion is assumed by this definition. Once proved, let $\ell(w)$ be the least number of simple reflections in a word for $w$ and let $\operatorname{Inv}(w)=\{\alpha\in\Phi^+:w\alpha\in\Phi^-\}$. A word is reduced if its length equals $\ell(w)$.

Identify $E$ with its dual by the displayed form, so $\alpha^\vee=2\alpha/(\alpha,\alpha)$. The root and coroot groups are $Q=\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi}\mathbb Z\alpha$ and $Q^\vee=\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi}\mathbb Z\alpha^\vee$. The **weight group** is $P=\{\lambda\in E:(\lambda,\alpha^\vee)\in\mathbb Z\text{ for all }\alpha\in\Phi\}$; the positive-root lemma proves it is a lattice with basis the vectors dual to the simple coroots. Put $Q_+=\sum_i\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\alpha_i$, and write $\mu\le\lambda$ when $\lambda-\mu\in Q_+$. The open and closed positive chambers are $\mathcal C=\{x:(x,\alpha_i)>0\text{ for all }i\}$ and $\overline{\mathcal C}=\{x:(x,\alpha_i)\ge0\text{ for all }i\}$. A weight is dominant if it belongs to $P\cap\overline{\mathcal C}$. More generally chambers are the connected components of $E\setminus\bigcup_{\alpha\in\Phi}\alpha^\perp$. The closed-chamber representative and stabilizer assertions are proved later, not included as axioms.
