## Statement

Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero and $X_0$ a smooth projective strict Calabi–Yau $k$-variety of dimension $d>2$, meaning $\omega_{X_0}$ is trivial and $H^i(X_0,\mathcal O_{X_0})=0$ for $0<i<d$. With the source's marked $\mathrm{Art}_k$ deformation functor $\mathrm{Def}_{X_0}$ and Morita deformation functor $\mathrm{Def}^{\mathrm{mo}}_{\mathrm{Perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X_0)}$, there is a natural isomorphism of set-valued deformation functors. The theorem concerns these exact classical Artin deformation functors and does not itself assert homological mirror symmetry or a global equivalence of arbitrary dg categories.

## Statement references

[Morimura, Theorem 7.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.11778)

## Functoriality diagram

For a morphism $R\to S$ of local Artin $k$-algebras, the vertical arrows are the respective base-change maps. The theorem asserts that the square commutes and that each horizontal map is bijective; the diagram records the claim and is not used as a proof.

```tikzcd
\mathrm{Def}_{X_0}(R) \arrow[r,"\zeta_R"] \arrow[d] & \mathrm{Def}^{\mathrm{mo}}_{\mathrm{Perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X_0)}(R) \arrow[d] \\
\mathrm{Def}_{X_0}(S) \arrow[r,"\zeta_S"] & \mathrm{Def}^{\mathrm{mo}}_{\mathrm{Perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X_0)}(S)
```

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field $k$, a strict Calabi–Yau $X_0$ of dimension $d>2$, the marked geometric deformation functor, the specified $k$-linear dg enhancement of perfect complexes with its $k$-linear triangulated equivalence, and the Axiom of Choice. Here “equivalence” for a $k$-linear enhancement is used in its $k$-linear sense; no dg lift of that supplied triangulated equivalence is assumed.

[F1] [[def-gts-strict-calabiyau-variety-in-the-morimura-source-convention]]: $X_0$ is smooth projective of pure dimension $d$, has specified $\omega_{X_0}\cong\mathcal O_{X_0}$, and has $H^i(X_0,\mathcal O_{X_0})=0$ for $0<i<d$.

[F2] [[def-gts-small-dg-categories-perfect-modules-and-morita-deformation]]: $\mathrm{Perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X_0)$ is a specified dg enhancement, and its Morita deformation functor consists of connected components of the marked derived-reduction groupoid.

[F3] [[def-infinitesimal-deformation-functor-over-square-zero-extension]]: $\mathrm{Def}_{X_0}(R)$ consists of isomorphism classes of flat, locally finitely presented $R$-scheme deformations with a specified marked special fibre; base change is the stated functor.

[F4] [[def-differential-graded-algebra]]: mapping complexes use cohomological grading and the signed Leibniz rule.

[F5] [[def-a-infinity-algebra]]: the source's uncurved cohomological $A_\infty$ operations use degree $2-n$ and the displayed Stasheff sign convention.

[F6] [[lem-gts-morimura-nilpotent-maurer-cartan-comparison]] proves the full small-extension obstruction, relative lift torsor, stabilizer correction, and natural comparison for a morphism of cohomological dg Lie algebras inducing a surjection on $H^0$, an isomorphism on $H^1$, and an injection on $H^2$. It does not identify either deformation functor here with a Maurer–Cartan functor.

[F7] [[lem-gts-morimura-perfect-generator-persistence]] proves that an existing perfect module with generating special fiber yields a Morita presentation by its endomorphism dg algebra, proves its endomorphism base-change equivalence for every algebra map, and proves compactness descent under Artin reduction.

[F8] [[lem-gts-morimura-ample-power-intermediate-ext-vanishing]] proves that sufficiently high ample powers have no intermediate cohomology for any integer exponent; finite sums of these powers have vanishing Ext in degrees one and two when $d>2$.

[F9] [[lem-gts-morimura-ample-power-perfect-generator]] constructs a finite sum of powers of a very ample line bundle that generates the perfect complexes, using the coordinate Koszul complex and affine Čech localization, and proves the corresponding derived module realization.

[F10] [[lem-gts-morimura-perfect-object-small-extension-lifting]] proves existence of perfect lifts through Artin small extensions when special-fiber Ext² vanishes, uniqueness of their marked class when Ext¹ also vanishes, and independence of graded carriers and dg presentations. Its bounded evaluation hypothesis is explicit.

[F11] [[lem-gts-morimura-flat-projective-perfect-base-change]] proves the geometric perfect-category base-change equivalence for flat projective schemes in their canonical dg enhancements, naturally under all scalar maps, successive base changes, and geometric isomorphisms. It does not assert projectivity of an arbitrary flat locally finitely presented deformation or uniqueness of an arbitrary triangulated enhancement.

[F12] [[thm-cech-computes-qc-cohomology-separated-scheme-affine-cover]] computes quasi-coherent cohomology on finite affine covers of quasi-compact separated schemes; in particular quasi-coherent modules on an affine scheme have no positive cohomology.

[F13] [[lem-smooth-projective-analytification-has-rational-kahler-polarization]]: A smooth projective complex variety analytifies to a compact Kähler manifold, via its projective polarization.

[F14] [[thm-compact-kahler-hodge-decomposition-via-elliptic-harmonic-forms]]: On a compact Kähler manifold the Dolbeault/sheaf comparison, harmonic representatives, and Hodge conjugation symmetry hold in every dimension under AC.

[F15] [[lem-coherent-sheaf-finite-twisted-locally-free-resolution-projective-space]]: Every coherent sheaf on projective N-space has a finite resolution by finite sums of twisting sheaves, for every finite N.

[F16] [[thm-qftx-gaga-analytic-local-ring-flatness]]: The algebraic-to-analytic local ring maps for finite-type complex schemes are faithfully flat in every dimension and retain nilpotents.

[F17] [[lem-qftx-gaga-projective-cover-laurent-cohomology]]: The actual Laurent–Čech proof supplies the monomial contraction and normal-convergence argument in projective dimensions at most three. The proof below explicitly extends that calculation to arbitrary finite N; its narrower Statement is not used outside its scope.

[F18] [[lem-qftx-gaga-scalar-pseudoconvex-acyclicity]]: Scalar holomorphic sheaves on Hartogs-pseudoconvex domains in any finite-dimensional complex affine space have zero positive sheaf cohomology under AC.

[F19] [[lem-kahler-commutators-and-laplacian-type-preservation]]: The Kähler identities include [Λ,∂]=i∂bar*, equality of the three Laplacians up to the factor two, and commutation with ∂, ∂bar and Λ.

[F20] [[thm-sobolev-compactness-and-elliptic-estimates-give-fredholm-hodge-decomposition]]: For a formally symmetric scalar-positive elliptic Laplacian on a compact manifold, the smooth harmonic projection and smooth Green inverse exist, with the stated inverse identities.

[F21] [[lem-gts-finite-artin-analytic-dolbeault-comparison]]: For a compact complex manifold and every finite complex Artin algebra, actual marked flat analytic deformations are equivalent to Dolbeault Maurer–Cartan solutions modulo their gauge morphisms. This is finite-Artin comparison, not analytic Kuranishi-family effectivity.

[F22] [[thm-smooth-morphism-formally-smooth-finite-presentation]]: A smooth finite-presentation algebra is formally smooth, so its maps lift through nilpotent ideals.

[F23] [[thm-jacobian-criterion-smooth-morphism]]: Smooth morphisms admit local standard-smooth presentations with an invertible Jacobian minor.

[F24] [[lem-ag-standard-smooth-flatness]]: A standard-smooth algebra is flat over its arbitrary base ring.

[F25] [[lem-regular-local-residue-field-koszul-resolution]]: A regular local ring of dimension e has the length-e parameter Koszul resolution of its residue field.

[F26] [[lem-ag-standard-smooth-fibre-regular-parameters]]: The invertible-Jacobian field calculation gives regular local rings and the regular-parameter quotient calculation.

[A1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: the Axiom of Choice is adopted for the set-valued deformation-functor and imported Ext conventions.

## Proof

**Given:** [F1]–[F26], [A1], and the marked deformation data in the Statement. All finite generator, projectivity, and base-change arguments are on the full admitted Artin functors.

1.1 Choose an ample line bundle on $X_0$. By [F8], after replacing it by a sufficiently high power, all its integral powers have zero intermediate cohomology. Enlarge the power again to obtain an embedding $X_0\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^r_k$ with this line bundle $M$ as $\mathcal O(1)$; intermediate vanishing is retained because further powers form a subset of the same integral powers. By [F9], $E_0=\bigoplus_{j=0}^r M^{-j}$ generates the perfect complexes and satisfies $\operatorname{Ext}^1(E_0,E_0)=\operatorname{Ext}^2(E_0,E_0)=0$ by [F8]. Thus there is an actual rigid unobstructed perfect generator, not merely a proposed finite sum. [F1, F8, F9, A1, construct]

1.2 First, a finite nilpotent thickening of an affine scheme is affine, without an initial separatedness hypothesis on the thickening. It suffices to consider a square-zero ideal $J$ on a scheme $Y$ with $Y_0=\operatorname{Spec}A$ and $J^2=0$. The ideal is a quasi-coherent $\mathcal O_{Y_0}$-module on the same topological space. By [F12], $H^1(Y,J)=0$, so global sections of $0\to J\to\mathcal O_Y\to\mathcal O_{Y_0}\to0$ give $0\to\Gamma(J)\to B\to A\to0$ with $B=\Gamma(Y,\mathcal O_Y)$. The canonical morphism $Y\to\operatorname{Spec}B$ restricts to the identity on $Y_0=\operatorname{Spec}A$ and is a homeomorphism, since $\Gamma(J)^2=0$. For each corresponding point, the map of stalk exact sequences has outside arrows $\Gamma(J)_{\mathfrak p}\to J_y$ and $A_{\mathfrak p}\to\mathcal O_{Y_0,y}$, both isomorphisms by quasi-coherence on $Y_0$; hence its middle arrow $B_{\mathfrak p}\to\mathcal O_{Y,y}$ is an isomorphism too. Thus the canonical morphism is an isomorphism of locally ringed spaces. For a finite nilpotent ideal, successively apply this argument to its powers, whose adjacent quotients are square-zero. [F12, algebra, construct]

2.1 Let $\mathcal A_R$ represent a marked Morita deformation over an Artin base $R$. Factor $R\to k$ into small extensions and pull back $\mathcal A_R$ to each base. Its marking transports $E_0$ to a perfect module on the special-fiber dg category. Evaluation of this module at each representable has bounded cohomology: under the marking that evaluation is the derived Hom from a perfect complex on $X_0$ to $E_0$; on a finite affine cover it is a bounded local finite dual-tensor complex, and the finite Čech direction bounds its global cohomology. Apply [F10] successively, using the same special-fiber Ext¹ and Ext² vanishings of step 1.1, to obtain a perfect module $P_R$ with the prescribed reduction, unique up to equivalence inducing the marked identity on $E_0$. Since $E_0$ generates, [F7] identifies $\mathcal A_R$ with the Morita class of $B_R=\mathbf R\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A_R}(P_R,P_R)$. Perfect Hom base change in [F7] identifies its derived special fiber with $B_0=\mathbf R\operatorname{Hom}_{X_0}(E_0,E_0)$. A Morita equivalence of marked deformations transports a lift of $E_0$ to another such lift, which [F10] identifies with the chosen one; hence this algebra presentation does not depend on the chosen categorical representative or the lifted generator class. [F2, F7, F9, F10, step 1.1, construct]

2.2 Let $X_R$ now be any geometric deformation allowed in [F3]. Fix a small-extension filtration of $R\to k$. Over every stage $R'\twoheadrightarrow R''$ with kernel $I$ killed by the maximal ideal, flatness identifies the ideal of $X_{R''}\subset X_{R'}$ with $\mathcal O_{X_0}\otimes_k I$: tensor the exact sequence $0\to I\to R'\to R''\to0$ with the flat structure sheaf. All these schemes have the same underlying space as $X_0$. Opens lifting affine opens of $X_0$ are affine by step 1.2. A finite affine cover of $X_0$ therefore lifts to a finite affine cover at every stage, and intersections remain affine by step 1.2, since the corresponding intersections on the separated $X_0$ are affine. Every kernel sheaf used below is a quasi-coherent sheaf on the separated special fiber with this same underlying space, so its cohomology is computed on $X_0$ by [F12]. No properness or separatedness of a deformation is needed for this computation. [F1, F3, F12, step 1.2, construct]

3.1 Let $M$ be the very ample line bundle chosen in step 1.1. Lift it successively across the filtration. Given an invertible sheaf $L$ at the lower stage, refine the affine cover so it is trivial on each member. Over each lifted affine overlap the reduction map on functions is surjective, since its kernel is $\mathcal O_{X_0}\otimes_k I$ with no positive cohomology there by [F12]. Lift the transition units $g_{ij}$ to units $\widetilde g_{ij}$; an element lifting a unit modulo a nilpotent ideal is a unit by the finite geometric inverse. The triple products are $1+c_{ijk}$ with $c_{ijk}\in\mathcal O_{X_0}\otimes_k I$. Associativity on quadruple overlaps says $c$ is an additive Čech two-cocycle. Its class vanishes because $H^2(X_0,\mathcal O_{X_0})\otimes_k I=0$ by $d>2$ and [F1]. Subtracting an additive one-cochain primitive, by multiplying the lifted transitions by $1-b_{ij}$, makes all triple products one; products of two kernel elements are zero. These corrected transitions glue an invertible sheaf on the upper stage with precisely the given reduction. Induction yields an invertible $M_R$ reducing to $M$. [F1, F8, F12, step 1.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

4.1 The coordinate sections of $M$ defining $X_0\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^r_k$ lift to sections of $M_R$. Indeed at any stage the kernel of the line-bundle reduction is $M\otimes_k I$, so its first cohomology is $H^1(X_0,M)\otimes_k I=0$ by [F8]. The cohomology exact sequence therefore makes reduction on global sections surjective. Choose successive lifts $s_0,\ldots,s_r$. They generate $M_R$: their cokernel reduces to zero modulo $\mathfrak m_R$, and iteration of $C=\mathfrak m_R C$ gives $C=\mathfrak m_R^n C=0$ without a finite-generation assumption. They define $f:X_R\to\mathbb P^r_R$ reducing to the specified closed embedding. On a standard chart of the target its preimage $V_i$ has affine special fiber, so is affine by step 1.2. Write $V_i=\operatorname{Spec}B_i$. The map $R[t_1,\ldots,t_r]\to B_i$ reduces modulo $\mathfrak m_R$ to the surjective coordinate map of the special-fiber closed embedding. If $D_i$ denotes its image, then $B_i=D_i+\mathfrak m_R B_i$; hence $B_i/D_i=\mathfrak m_R(B_i/D_i)=0$ by nilpotence. Thus each chart map is a closed immersion, and these local closed immersions give a closed immersion $f$. Consequently every admitted $X_R$ is projective over $R$. [F3, F8, F12, step 1.2, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Now [F11] applies to every admitted geometric deformation, in the canonical geometric dg model, and proves the special-fiber equivalence and all base-change equivalences for every Artin map $R\to S$. These are the actual pullback maps on the bounded flat affine Čech endomorphism models. Their marking, geometric-isomorphism compatibility, and successive-base-change compatibility follow from the restriction maps and tensor associativity. The embedding chosen in steps 3.1–4.1 only verifies projectivity; it is not extra data in the resulting geometric perfect category, and [F11] already supplies independence of its computational covers. Thus the assignment is defined naturally on the entire geometric functor with values in deformations of the canonical perfect enhancement. Constructions A–C below construct the necessary compatible dg Morita equivalence to the specified enhancement; they do not infer it from the defining triangulated equivalence. [F2, F3, F9, F11, A1, step 4.1]

6.1 Construction A. An explicit dg quotient lemma over a field. Let C be a small pretriangulated k-dg category and N a full pretriangulated subcategory. The following description is a construction, not an imported dg-quotient theorem. Adjoin, for every N in N, an endomorphism ε_N of degree−1 with dε_N=1_N. The resulting category Q has the same objects and morphism complexes namely $\bigoplus_{r\ge0,N_1,\ldots,N_r}\mathcal C(N_r,Y)\otimes k\varepsilon_{N_r}\otimes\cdots\otimes k\varepsilon_{N_1}\otimes\mathcal C(X,N_1)$. [F2, F4, A1, step 5.1, algebra, construct]

7.1 with r=0 term C(X,Y). Differential and composition are the graded Leibniz differential and concatenation, with dε composing its neighboring maps. In each word there are finitely many terms; this defines a unital dg category and a functor C→Q. Every N becomes contractible. A dg functor C→D with chosen contracting homotopies of its N-images extends by sending each ε_N to that homotopy. [F2, F4, A1, step 6.1, algebra, construct]

8.1 Here are the needed homotopical properties, including the roof calculation. [F2, F4, A1, step 7.1, algebra, construct]

9.1 Fix Y. As a right C-module, Q(-,Y) is obtained from h_Y by adjoining representable N-cells: its length-r word quotient is a direct sum of h_N tensored with complexes of k-vector spaces. Split each such coefficient complex into its cohomology and two-term contractible pairs; choose homogeneous bases using AC. Order the resulting cells first by word length and then by the two positions of each contractible pair. The differential of a cell is a finite sum of earlier cells, because dε lowers word length and the internal coefficient differential has only the specified two-term arrows. This is a relative semifree presentation with initial module h_Y. Any finite collection of words, together with their differentials and any specified finite nullhomotopies, is contained in a finite such cell extension P of h_Y. Its cone is a finite extension of shifted representables h_N. [F2, F4, A1, step 8.1, algebra, construct]

10.1 A closed element z∈Q(X,Y) is a map h_X→Q(-,Y). Its value is a finite word sum, so the previous finite extension P can be chosen to contain it. Since C is pretriangulated, P is quasi-represented by an object Y' and the inclusion h_Y→P corresponds to a map Y→Y' whose cone lies in the triangulated closure of N. The finite-cell maps can be represented up to homotopy; these are exactly the morphisms used in H0. Since that cone is contractible in Q, the class of z is the right roof namely $X\longrightarrow Y'\longleftarrow Y$. [F2, F4, A1, step 9.1, algebra, construct]

11.1 Conversely, if a roof class becomes zero in H0(Q), the nullhomotopy is also a finite word sum. Enlarge P to include that nullhomotopy. The corresponding further map Y'→Y'' has cone in the same closure, and the original roof map becomes zero in H0(C). This is precisely the ordinary Verdier roof relation. Apply the same argument after shifting Y to compute every H^n. Thus H0(Q) is the Verdier quotient H0(C)/H0(N); its shifts and triangles are the quotient shifts and triangles. Representable cones in Q are represented up to quasi-isomorphism by the same finite roof construction, so passing to the pretriangulated hull adds no new isomorphism classes. [F2, F4, A1, step 10.1, algebra, construct]

12.1 Also suppose q:C→D is a dg quasi-equivalence bijective on object sets, and carries N bijectively onto a full subcategory M. Then the induced Q(C,N)→Q(D,M) is a dg quasi-equivalence. Filter each morphism complex by word length. On each associated word quotient its map is a direct sum of finite tensor products of quasi-isomorphisms of k-complexes. These are quasi-isomorphisms: an acyclic k-complex is contractible by vector-space splittings, and tensoring its contraction with any complex preserves that identity with the Koszul sign. A cycle in the cone is a finite word sum. Kill its highest word length using the associated-quotient acyclicity, subtract a boundary and repeat at the next lower length. This process terminates. Thus the cone is acyclic, with no unproved convergence of an unbounded spectral sequence. Bijectivity on objects completes the quasi-equivalence assertion. [F2, F4, A1, step 11.1, algebra, construct]

13.1 Construction B. Realization from the lifted bounded heart. Let A be a small k-linear abelian category. Let C be a pretriangulated dg category with a specified triangulated equivalence E:H0(C)≃D^b(A). Replace C by the full module model closed under actual finite shifts and cones of representables. The dg Yoneda embedding is quasi-fully faithful and pretriangulatedness represents these objects, so this replacement is a quasi-equivalence and does not change the enhancement. In this model totalization of a finite twisted complex is an actual dg functor, not merely an objectwise choice of convolution. Add the retract hull when needed for the specified perfect category. Transport the usual bounded t-structure to H0(C). [F2, F4, A1, step 12.1, algebra, construct]

14.1 For each object A of A choose a lift X_A in C of its image under E^(-1). Take the full dg subcategory on these lifts and truncate each morphism complex at degree0: namely $\mathcal B(A,B)^j=\mathcal C(X_A,X_B)^j$ for $j<0$, $\mathcal B(A,B)^0=\ker(d:\mathcal C^0\to\mathcal C^1)$, and $\mathcal B(A,B)^j=0$ for $j>0$. [F2, F4, A1, step 13.1, algebra, construct]

15.1 It is a dg category: composition of nonpositive degrees remains nonpositive, and degree-zero cycles compose. There are actual dg functors namely $\mathcal A\xleftarrow{q}\mathcal B\longrightarrow\mathcal C$. [F2, F4, A1, step 14.1, algebra, construct]

16.1 where q sends a degree-zero cycle to its given heart morphism and kills negative degrees. Since H^j C(X_A,X_B)=Hom_(D^b A)(A,B[j])=0 for j<0, q is a quasi-equivalence; its H0 map is exactly the given heart equivalence. [F2, F4, A1, step 15.1, algebra, construct]

17.1 Extend to finite twisted complexes. The functor Tw(B)→Tw(A) is quasi-fully faithful: each Hom is a finite matrix complex, filtered by source/target twisting indices; its associated quotients are shifts of the original Hom comparisons. The finite filtration gives the quasi-isomorphism. It is essentially surjective in an explicit form. Given a bounded A-complex, lift its differential entries to degree-zero cycles in B. Composition failure has zero H0 class, so choose a degree−1 homotopy for each distance-two entry. At distance r≥3, the obstruction is closed of negative degree2−r, and negative cohomology vanishes; choose a primitive.
