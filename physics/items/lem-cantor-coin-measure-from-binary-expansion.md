## Statement

In ZFC the Cantor space $\mathcal C=2^{\mathbb N}$
([[def-cantor-sequence-space-for-descriptive-set-theory]],
[[lem-cantor-and-baire-sequence-coding]]) carries a probability measure $\mu$ on
its Borel sigma-algebra $\mathcal B(\mathcal C)$
([[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]) such that

$$\mu(N_s)=2^{-\lvert s\rvert}\qquad\text{for every finite binary word } s, \qquad N_s:=\{x\in\mathcal C:x\upharpoonright\lvert s\rvert=s\},$$

the construction using the Axiom of Countable Choice
([[def-countable-choice]]). The measure is the Carathéodory extension of the
**fair-coin content** $p_0$, which assigns to the cylinder prescribing the
coordinates of a finite set $F$ the value $2^{-\lvert F\rvert}$; it satisfies
$\mu(\mathcal C)=1$, and it is multiplicative on cylinders with disjoint
coordinate sets: if $F,G\subseteq\mathbb N$ are finite and disjoint, $a\in 2^F$
and $b\in 2^G$, then
$\mu([a]_F\cap[b]_G)=2^{-\lvert F\rvert-\lvert G\rvert}=\mu([a]_F)\mu([b]_G)$.

This is the fair-coin product measure on $2^{\omega}$ used by the master-code
constructions below; the cylinders $N_s$ are the basic clopen sets of the
product topology of copies of the discrete two-point space
([[def-product-topology]]), and the identification of $2^{\mathbb N}$ with
$2^{\omega}$ is the usual one, $\mathbb N=\omega$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** ZFC, hence the Axiom of Countable Choice ([[def-countable-choice]]).

[F1] $\mathcal C=2^{\mathbb N}$ is a compact metric space with no isolated points whose topology consists of unions of the cylinders $\{x:x(i)=a(i)\ \text{for all } i\in F\}$ over finite $F\subseteq\mathbb N$ and $a\in 2^F$; these cylinders form a base of clopen sets, and the metric is $d(x,y)=2^{-m-1}$ for the first coordinate $m$ at which $x$ and $y$ differ. ([[def-cantor-sequence-space-for-descriptive-set-theory]], [[lem-cantor-and-baire-sequence-coding]])

[F2] If $A,B$ are finite and disjoint then $\lvert A\cup B\rvert=\lvert A\rvert+\lvert B\rvert$; if $A$ is finite then $\lvert 2^{A}\rvert=2^{\lvert A\rvert}$, the set of functions $A\to2$; for $a\ne0$ the integer powers satisfy $a^{m+n}=a^ma^n$ for all integers $m,n$. ([[thm-sum-rule]], [[thm-cardinality-of-a-set-of-functions]], [[def-integer-power]], [[def-nat-power]], [[lem-power-laws]], [[def-finite-cardinality]])

[F3] An algebra of subsets of a set is closed under complements and finite unions; the sigma-algebra generated by a family is the smallest sigma-algebra containing it; the Borel sigma-algebra of a topological space is generated by its open sets, and for a product of discrete two-point spaces it is generated by the cylinders; in a topological space finite unions and finite intersections of closed sets are closed, a set is closed exactly when its complement is open, and a union of open sets is open. ([[def-algebra-of-subsets]], [[def-generated-sigma-algebra]], [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]], [[def-product-topology]], [[def-topological-space]])

[F4] A premeasure on an algebra vanishes at $\varnothing$ and is countably additive on disjoint sequences whose union lies in the algebra; the outer set function induced by a premeasure is defined by covering costs; assuming the Axiom of Countable Choice the restriction of that outer set function to the generated sigma-algebra is a measure extending the premeasure. ([[def-premeasure-on-an-algebra]], [[def-outer-measure-induced-by-a-premeasure]], [[thm-caratheodory-extension-theorem]], [[def-measure]])

[F5] A closed subset of a compact metric space is a compact subset, compactness of a subset is intrinsic to its metric subspace and agrees with the ambient reading, and compactness is the finite-subcover property for open covers of the space. ([[lem-closed-subset-of-a-compact-space-is-compact]], [[lem-compactness-is-intrinsic]], [[def-metric-compactness]], [[def-compact-space]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 For finite $F\subseteq\mathbb N$ and $a\in 2^F$ put $[a]_F:=\{x\in\mathcal C:x(i)=a(i)\text{ for every }i\in F\}$; each $[a]_F$ is open because the topology of $\mathcal C$ consists of unions of cylinders, and closed because $\mathcal C\setminus[a]_F=\bigcup\{[b]_F:b\in 2^F,\ b\ne a\}$ is a finite union of cylinders and hence open; in particular $[\varnothing]_{\varnothing}=\mathcal C$. Consequently the family $\mathcal C_0$ of all finite unions of cylinders, the empty union included, contains $\varnothing$ and $\mathcal C$ and is closed under complements, and it is closed under finite intersections because $[a]_F\cap[b]_G=[a\cup b]_{F\cup G}$ when $a$ and $b$ agree on $F\cap G$ and is $\varnothing$ otherwise; by de Morgan it is therefore an algebra of subsets of $\mathcal C$. [F1, F3]

1.2 If $F\subseteq G$ are finite and $a\in 2^F$, then the prescriptions $b\in 2^G$ with $b\upharpoonright F=a$ are in bijection with the functions $G\setminus F\to2$, so there are $2^{\lvert G\setminus F\rvert}$ of them, and $[a]_F$ is their pairwise disjoint union: a point of $[a]_F$ agrees with exactly one such $b$ on $G$. [F1, F2, F3]

2.1 The fair-coin content is well defined on $\mathcal C_0$. Let $A\in\mathcal C_0$ and let $A=[a_0]_{F_0}\cup\dots\cup[a_\ell]_{F_\ell}$ be a presentation; put $G:=F_0\cup\dots\cup F_\ell$, a finite set. By step 1.2 each $[a_i]_{F_i}$ is a disjoint union of cylinders $[b]_G$, and these lie inside $A$; since the $G$-cylinders partition $\mathcal C$, $A$ is the disjoint union of the $G$-cylinders contained in it, and we let $m(A,G)$ be their number and set $p_0(A):=m(A,G)\cdot2^{-\lvert G\rvert}$. If $G\subseteq H$ are finite, step 1.2 splits each $G$-cylinder into $2^{\lvert H\setminus G\rvert}$ many $H$-cylinders, so $m(A,H)=m(A,G)\cdot2^{\lvert H\setminus G\rvert}$ and $m(A,H)2^{-\lvert H\rvert}=m(A,G)2^{-\lvert G\rvert}$ because $\lvert H\rvert=\lvert G\rvert+\lvert H\setminus G\rvert$ and $2^{-\lvert G\rvert-\lvert H\setminus G\rvert}=2^{-\lvert G\rvert}2^{-\lvert H\setminus G\rvert}$; two finite sets containing all $F_i$ are compared through their union, which contains both, so the value does not depend on the presentation or on $G$. [step 1.2, F2]

3.1 From step 2.1, $p_0(\varnothing)=0$, $p_0(\mathcal C)=1$ and $p_0([a]_F)=2^{-\lvert F\rvert}$; if $A,B\in\mathcal C_0$ are disjoint and $G$ is finite and contains the supports of presentations of both, then the $G$-cylinders inside $A\cup B$ are exactly those inside $A$ together with those inside $B$, because a $G$-cylinder meets the disjoint sets $A$ and $B$ in all of itself or in nothing, so $m(A\cup B,G)=m(A,G)+m(B,G)$ and $p_0(A\cup B)=p_0(A)+p_0(B)$; hence $p_0$ is monotone, and $0\le p_0(A)\le1$ for every $A\in\mathcal C_0$. [step 2.1, F2]

4.1 Let $(A_k)_{k\in\mathbb N}$ be pairwise disjoint members of $\mathcal C_0$ whose union $A$ lies in $\mathcal C_0$. For every $m$ the set $A_0\cup\dots\cup A_m$ is contained in $A$, so step 3.1 gives $\sum_{k\le m}p_0(A_k)=p_0(A_0\cup\dots\cup A_m)\le p_0(A)$ and hence $\sum_{k\in\mathbb N}p_0(A_k)\le p_0(A)$. Conversely $A$ is a finite union of closed cylinders, hence closed by step 1.1 and [F3], so it is a compact subset of the compact space $\mathcal C$; the $A_k$ are open and cover $A$, so the finite-subcover property read in the ambient space through [F5] gives $m$ with $A\subseteq A_0\cup\dots\cup A_m$, and then $\sum_{k\in\mathbb N}p_0(A_k)\ge\sum_{k\le m}p_0(A_k)=p_0(A_0\cup\dots\cup A_m)\ge p_0(A)$ by step 3.1. Hence $p_0$ is a premeasure on the algebra $\mathcal C_0$. [step 3.1, step 1.1, F1, F3, F4, F5]

5.1 Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice. By [F4] the outer set function $\mu^*$ induced by the premeasure $p_0$ has a restriction $\mu:=\mu^*\upharpoonright\sigma(\mathcal C_0)$ that is a measure on the sigma-algebra generated by the cylinders, and $\mu(A)=p_0(A)$ for every $A\in\mathcal C_0$; in particular $\mu(\mathcal C)=p_0(\mathcal C)=1$, so $\mu$ is a probability measure. [step 4.1, F4]

6.1 The cylinders form a base of the topology of $\mathcal C$ and each is a union of open sets, so the sigma-algebra they generate is the Borel sigma-algebra: $\sigma(\mathcal C_0)=\mathcal B(\mathcal C)$. Hence $\mu$ is a Borel probability measure and $\mu([a]_F)=p_0([a]_F)=2^{-\lvert F\rvert}$ for every finite $F$ and $a\in2^F$; for a finite binary word $s$ the cylinder $N_s$ is $[s]_F$ with $F=\{0,\dots,\lvert s\rvert-1\}$, so $\mu(N_s)=2^{-\lvert s\rvert}$. [step 5.1, step 3.1, F1, F3]

6.2 If $F\cap G=\varnothing$, $a\in2^F$ and $b\in2^G$, then $[a]_F\cap[b]_G=[a\cup b]_{F\cup G}$ by step 1.1, so $\mu([a]_F\cap[b]_G)=2^{-\lvert F\cup G\rvert}=2^{-\lvert F\rvert-\lvert G\rvert}=\mu([a]_F)\mu([b]_G)$, the middle equality using $\lvert F\cup G\rvert=\lvert F\rvert+\lvert G\rvert$ and the power laws. [step 5.1, step 3.1, F2]

7.1 Steps 6.1 and 6.2 are the two claims: $2^{\mathbb N}$ carries the Borel probability measure $\mu$ with $\mu(N_s)=2^{-\lvert s\rvert}$, obtained as the Carathéodory extension of the fair-coin content, and $\mu$ is multiplicative on cylinders with disjoint coordinate sets. ∎ [step 6.1, step 6.2]
