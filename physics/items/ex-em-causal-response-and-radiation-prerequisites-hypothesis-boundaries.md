## Example

Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice ($\mathrm{AC}_\omega$), inherited from the surface-integral conventions used by [[thm-em-radiation-asymptotic-flux]].

(i) (Causality without passivity.) In this clause also assume Dependent Choice ([[def-dependent-choice]]), inherited from the exact AC supplier. Let $K(s):=-e^{-s}$ for $s\ge0$. Then $K$ is of class $C^1$ on $[0,\infty)$ with $K,K'\in L^1(0,\infty)$ and $sK(s)\in L^1(0,\infty)$ (indeed $K'=e^{-s}=-K$, $\int_0^\infty e^{-s}ds=1$ and $\int_0^\infty se^{-s}ds=1$), so the integrable branch of [[thm-em-causal-dispersive-response]] applies: $P(t)=\int_0^\infty K(s)E(t-s)ds$ is a bounded causal linear response on bounded continuous histories, its transfer function is $\chi(z)=-1/(1-iz)$ on $\operatorname{Im}z\ge0$, and its boundary value $\chi(\omega)=-(1+i\omega)/(1+\omega^2)$ satisfies $\operatorname{Im}\chi(\omega)=-\omega/(1+\omega^2)<0$ for every $\omega>0$. Hence the Hermitian dissipative part $M(\omega)=\operatorname{Im}\chi(\omega)$ is strictly negative, not positive semidefinite, and the monochromatic period-averaged work of clause (d) of that theorem is strictly negative for $d=1$, every $e\ne0$ and every $\omega>0$ (with any response normalization $\varepsilon_0>0$), although the response is causal and its entry satisfies the dispersion relations of clause (c). Nonnegative period-averaged work (passivity) is therefore an additional condition on the model, not a consequence of temporal causality.

(ii) (The flux limit needs fixed retarded time.) In the setting of [[thm-em-radiation-asymptotic-flux]], fix $t\in\mathbb R$ and $n\in S^2$, and let $r>\max\{2a,\;a+c(t-t_0)\}$. Then for every $y\in B_a$ one has $R=|rn-y|\ge r-a>c(t-t_0)$, so $t-R/c<t_0$; by hypothesis (H1) of that theorem the functions $\rho,\rho_t,\mathbf J,\mathbf J_t$ all vanish at $(y,t-R/c)$, so every integrand in the field formulas of clause (a) of that theorem vanishes and $\mathbf E(rn,t)=\mathbf B(rn,t)=0$, hence $n\cdot\mathbf S(rn,t)=0$ for every $n\in S^2$. Therefore $\int_{S^2}r^2\,n\cdot\mathbf S(rn,t)\,d\Omega(n)=0$ for all such $r$: at fixed observation time the far-zone flux integral is identically zero beyond the light cone of the compact spacetime support, whereas clause (c) of that theorem is a limit at fixed retarded time. These limits may agree for a particular source, including the zero source. They cannot be interchanged as a general theorem: the conserved smooth sources constructed below have a strictly positive fixed-retarded-time flux limit.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Countable Choice $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ ([[def-countable-choice]]) and, in clause (i), also Dependent Choice $\mathrm{DC}$ ([[def-dependent-choice]]); the kernel $K(s)=-e^{-s}$ on $[0,\infty)$; in clause (i) the causal-response theorem [[thm-em-causal-dispersive-response]] with its clauses (a), (c) and (d); in clause (ii) the radiation theorem [[thm-em-radiation-asymptotic-flux]] with its hypotheses (H1), (H2) and its clauses (a), (b), (c), together with its constants $c>0$, $\mu_0>0$, $a>0$, $t_0<T$, sources $\rho,\mathbf J$ and fields $\mathbf E,\mathbf B$, $\mathbf S=\mu_0^{-1}\mathbf E\times\mathbf B$.

[F1] The real exponential satisfies $\exp(x)=\sum_{n\ge0}x^n/n!$ with $e=\exp(1)$, is $C^\infty$ with $\exp^{(m)}=\exp$ for every $m$, and satisfies $\exp(x)>0$ and $\exp(-x)=1/\exp(x)$ for every real $x$. ([[def-real-exponential-function-and-e]], [[thm-derivative-of-exponential]], [[cor-exponential-reciprocal-and-positivity]])

[F2] Let $a<b$. Newton–Leibniz states that if $G:[a,b]\to\mathbb R$ is continuous on $[a,b]$, differentiable on $(a,b)$, and $f:[a,b]\to\mathbb R$ is a Riemann-integrable extension of $G'$ from $(a,b)$, then $\int_a^b f=G(b)-G(a)$; endpoint derivatives of $G$ are not required. Integration by parts states that if $u,v:[a,b]\to\mathbb R$ are differentiable at every point relative to $[a,b]$ and $u',v'$ are Riemann integrable, then $uv',u'v$ are Riemann integrable and $\int_a^b uv'=u(b)v(b)-u(a)v(a)-\int_a^b u'v$. Under the assumed $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, a bounded Riemann-integrable real function on $[a,b]$ is Lebesgue integrable with the same integral. ([[thm-newton-leibniz-with-interior-derivative]], [[thm-integration-by-parts]], [[thm-bounded-riemann-integrable-functions-are-lebesgue-measurable-and-have-the-same-integral]])

[F3] Monotone convergence for a nondecreasing sequence of nonnegative measurable functions. ([[thm-monotone-convergence-for-the-integral]])

[F4] Dominated convergence; the integral over a measurable set; the $L^1$ conventions; linearity of the integral, including $\int-cf=-c\int f$; and equality of integrals of integrable functions that agree almost everywhere. ([[thm-dominated-convergence]], [[def-integral-over-a-measurable-set]], [[def-l-one-of-a-measure]], [[def-integrable-real-and-complex-functions-and-their-integrals]], [[thm-linearity-of-the-lebesgue-integral-on-l-one]], [[thm-the-lebesgue-integral-respects-almost-everywhere-equality]])

[F5] A function that is continuous on a compact interval, differentiable in the interior, and whose derivative extends continuously is Lipschitz, hence absolutely continuous; absolute continuity is the finite disjoint-interval condition; and $C^k$ is the usual several-variable class. ([[thm-c1-lipschitz-ac-bv-hierarchy]], [[def-absolutely-continuous-function]], [[def-ck-euclidean-maps-and-diffeomorphisms]])

[F6] Chain rule and algebra of derivatives for the real composites used below, including the derivative of $s\mapsto e^{-s}$. ([[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]], [[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]], [[thm-derivative-of-exponential]])

[F7] The complex exponential is entire with $\exp'=\exp$, obeys $\exp(z+w)=\exp z\exp w$ and agrees with the real exponential on real arguments ([[thm-complex-exponential-addition-and-real-extension]]); for every nonzero $\lambda\in\mathbb C$ the function $F(s)=e^{\lambda s}/\lambda$ is a primitive of $s\mapsto e^{\lambda s}$ on $\mathbb C$, so for the interval path $[0,N]$ the complex line integral equals $F(N)-F(0)$; the Riemann–Stieltjes and parametric contour integrals agree for piecewise $C^1$ contours. ([[def-complex-exponential]], [[thm-complex-exponential-is-entire-with-derivative-itself]], [[thm-chain-rule-for-complex-derivatives]], [[def-complex-primitive]], [[thm-fundamental-theorem-for-complex-line-integrals]], [[def-complex-line-integral-over-a-rectifiable-path]], [[thm-riemann-stieltjes-and-parametric-contour-integrals-agree]])

[F8] $|\exp(x+iy)|=e^x$ for real $x,y$, and $\operatorname{Re},\operatorname{Im},\overline{\phantom{z}}$ are the coordinate maps of the cited definition. ([[cor-complex-exponential-cartesian-form-modulus-and-eulers-identity]], [[def-complex-conjugate-real-imaginary-part-and-modulus]])

[F9] The causal-response theorem, consumed in clause (i): clause (a) gives the bounded causal linear response of an $L^1$ kernel; clause (c) gives the transform of an integrable kernel as a holomorphic function on the upper half-plane with its boundary values, dispersion relations and the sharper bound; clause (d) gives the monochromatic period-averaged work $(\varepsilon_0\omega/2)\operatorname{Im}(e^*\chi(\omega)e)$ and its equivalence with positive semidefiniteness of $M(\omega)=(\chi(\omega)-\chi(\omega)^*)/(2i)$. ([[thm-em-causal-dispersive-response]])

[F10] The radiation theorem, consumed in clause (ii): under (H1) and (H2), its clause (a) gives the retarded field integrals on $\{|x|>2a\}$ for all times, its clause (b) gives the uniform far-zone errors and the identity $\int_{B_a}\rho_t(y,u+n\cdot y/c)dy=\frac1cn\cdot\mathbf V(n,u)$, and its clause (c) is the flux limit at fixed retarded time. ([[thm-em-radiation-asymptotic-flux]])

[F11] The Euclidean norm obeys the triangle inequality, hence $|rn-y|\ge\bigl||rn|-|y|\bigr|=r-|y|$ for $y\in B_a$, and the dot product and cross product are those of the cited definitions. ([[thm-cauchy-schwarz-and-the-euclidean-norm]], [[def-euclidean-inner-product]])

[F12] On a compact embedded $C^1$ hypersurface the surface integral is defined by finitely many regular parametrizations, and the identically zero nonnegative function has integral $0$. ([[def-surface-integral-on-a-compact-c-one-hypersurface]])

[F13] Support means the closure of the nonzero locus. For $0<r<R$ and $m\ge1$ there exists a smooth $\zeta:\mathbb R^m\to[0,1]$ equal to $1$ on $\overline B_r(0)$ with support contained in $B_R(0)$ ([[lem-smooth-bump-between-concentric-euclidean-balls]]). Sums, products and compositions of smooth Euclidean maps are smooth ([[thm-ck-euclidean-maps-closed-under-algebra-and-composition]]).

[F14] Under $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, the chart surface measure on $S^2$ equals the polar measure and is invariant under orthogonal transformations ([[lem-euclidean-chart-measure-agrees-with-polar-surface-measure]]); its total mass is $3\lambda_3(B_1)$. For $s>0$, $\Gamma(s):=\int_0^\infty t^{s-1}e^{-t}dt$ is the convergent positive Euler integral ([[def-real-gamma-function-by-the-euler-integral]]); $\lambda_3(B_1)=\pi^{3/2}/\Gamma(5/2)$, $\Gamma(s+1)=s\Gamma(s)$ and $\Gamma(1/2)=\sqrt\pi$ ([[cor-volume-of-the-unit-n-ball]], [[thm-real-gamma-functional-equation]], [[cor-real-gamma-one-half-is-root-pi]]), so $\Gamma(5/2)=3\sqrt\pi/4$ and the sphere mass is $3\lambda_3(B_1)=4\pi$. Positive-radius Euclidean balls have positive finite Lebesgue measure ([[lem-euclidean-balls-have-positive-finite-lebesgue-measure]]). The nonnegative integral is monotone ([[prop-order-and-scalar-rules-for-the-nonnegative-integral]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct computation for clause (i); direct substitution into the cited theorem and the triangle inequality for clause (ii).

1.1 The kernel $K(s)=-e^{-s}$ is of class $C^1$ on $[0,\infty)$ with $K'=e^{-s}=-K$ by [F1] and [F6]; $K(s)<0$ for every $s$, and $|K(s)|=e^{-s}$, $|K'(s)|=e^{-s}$, $|sK(s)|=se^{-s}$. For $N>0$, the functions $G(s)=-e^{-s}$ and $u(s)=s$ are smooth on $\mathbb R$, so their restrictions are continuous on $[0,N]$, differentiable there (relative to the interval at endpoints), and have continuous, hence Riemann-integrable derivatives. Thus [F2] applies to $G$ and, for integration by parts, to $u$ and $v=G$: it gives $\int_0^Ne^{-s}ds=1-e^{-N}$ and $\int_0^Nse^{-s}ds=1-(1+N)e^{-N}$, and converts these to Lebesgue integrals; the nonnegative exponential series gives $e^N\ge N^2/2$ by [F1], so $e^{-N}\to0$ and $Ne^{-N}\le2/N\to0$; since the integrands are nonnegative, [F3] lets integer $N\to\infty$, giving $\int_0^\infty e^{-s}ds=1$ and $\int_0^\infty se^{-s}ds=1$. Hence $K,K',sK\in L^1(0,\infty)$ and, by [F5], $K$ is locally absolutely continuous on $[0,\infty)$. [F1, F2, F3, F5, F6]

1.2 A conserved smooth source with nonzero radiation. For the constants of clause (ii), put $u_*:=(t_0+T)/2$, $b:=(T-t_0)/8$ and $\epsilon:=\min(a/2,bc/2)>0$. By [F13] choose $\psi:\mathbb R^3\to[0,1]$ smooth, equal to $1$ on $\overline B_{\epsilon/2}$ and supported in $B_\epsilon$, and $\theta:\mathbb R\to[0,1]$ smooth, equal to $1$ on $[-b,b]$ and supported in $(-2b,2b)$. Define $f(v):=(v-u_*)^2\theta(v-u_*)$, $\rho(y,v):=-\partial_3\psi(y)f(v)$ and $\mathbf J(y,v):=(0,0,\psi(y)f'(v))$. These functions are smooth by [F13], their spatial support is contained in $B_\epsilon\subset B_a$, and their temporal support is contained in $(u_*-2b,u_*+2b)\subset(t_0,T)$, so (H1) holds. Direct coordinate differentiation gives $\rho_t=-\partial_3\psi\,f'$ and $\operatorname{div}_y\mathbf J=\partial_3\psi\,f'$, proving (H2). By [F14] and monotonicity, $C_\psi:=\int_{B_a}\psi(y)\,dy$ satisfies $0<\lambda(B_{\epsilon/2})\le C_\psi\le\lambda(B_\epsilon)<\infty$. [F13, F14, F6, F4, construct]

2.1 Therefore the integrable branch of clause (c) of [F9] applies to $K$: $P(t)=\int_0^\infty K(s)E(t-s)ds$ is a bounded causal linear response on bounded continuous histories by clause (a), $\chi(z)=\int_0^\infty K(s)e^{izs}ds$ is well defined and holomorphic on $\operatorname{Im}z>0$, continuous on the closed upper half-plane, and its boundary values satisfy the dispersion relations of clause (c). [step 1.1, F9, F4]

2.2 Positive fixed-retarded-time flux. If $y\in\operatorname{supp}\psi$ and $n\in S^2$, then $|n\cdot y|/c\le|y|/c<\epsilon/c\le b/2$, so $\theta=1$ near $u_*+n\cdot y/c-u_*$ and $f''(u_*+n\cdot y/c)=2$. Thus $\mathbf V(n,u_*)=2C_\psi p$ for every $n$, where $p:=(0,0,1)$. Orthogonal invariance in [F14] makes the three integrals $\int_{S^2}n_j^2\,d\Omega$ equal; their sum is $\int_{S^2}|n|^2\,d\Omega=4\pi$, so each is $4\pi/3$. The cross-product coordinates give $|n\times p|^2=n_1^2+n_2^2=1-n_3^2$, hence $\int_{S^2}|n\times p|^2\,d\Omega=8\pi/3$. Clause (c) of [F10] now gives the fixed-retarded-time limit $\frac{\mu_0}{16\pi^2c}\,4C_\psi^2\,\frac{8\pi}{3}=\frac{2\mu_0C_\psi^2}{3\pi c}>0$. [step 1.2, F10, F11, F14, algebra]

3.1 Computation of the transfer function: fix $z$ with $\operatorname{Im}z\ge0$ and put $\lambda:=iz-1$, so $\lambda\ne0$ and $|e^{\lambda N}|=e^{-N}e^{-N\operatorname{Im}z}\le e^{-N}\to0$ by [F8]. For every $N>0$ the function $s\mapsto e^{\lambda s}$ is continuous, so its Lebesgue integral over $(0,N)$ agrees with its Riemann and parametric line integral over $[0,N]$ by [F2] and [F7], and the first fundamental theorem of [F7] applied to the primitive $F(s)=e^{\lambda s}/\lambda$ gives $\int_0^Ne^{\lambda s}ds=(e^{\lambda N}-1)/\lambda$; the truncated integrands $s\mapsto-e^{\lambda s}\mathbf 1_{(0,N)}(s)$ converge pointwise to $s\mapsto-e^{\lambda s}$ and are dominated by $e^{-s}\in L^1(0,\infty)$, so [F4] gives $\chi(z)=\int_0^\infty(-e^{\lambda s})ds=-\lim_{N\to\infty}(e^{\lambda N}-1)/\lambda=1/\lambda=-1/(1-iz)$, where for $\operatorname{Im}z>0$ the first equality is the defining integral of clause (c) of [F9] and for real $z$ it is its continuous boundary value. [step 2.1, F7, F8, F4, F2]

4.1 Boundary values and the dissipative part: for real $\omega$, step 3.1 gives $\chi(\omega)=-1/(1-i\omega)=-(1+i\omega)/(1+\omega^2)$ by multiplication with the conjugate denominator, so $\operatorname{Im}\chi(\omega)=-\omega/(1+\omega^2)<0$ for every $\omega>0$; for $d=1$ and $e\ne0$, $e^*\chi(\omega)e=|e|^2\chi(\omega)$ and $e^*M(\omega)e=|e|^2\operatorname{Im}\chi(\omega)<0$, so the Hermitian dissipative part $M(\omega)=\operatorname{Im}\chi(\omega)$ is strictly negative and in particular not positive semidefinite. By clause (d) of [F9] the monochromatic period-averaged work equals $(\varepsilon_0\omega/2)|e|^2\operatorname{Im}\chi(\omega)$, which is strictly negative for every $e\ne0$ and every $\varepsilon_0>0$, while steps 2.1 and 3.1 exhibit the response as causal with dispersion relations. Passivity is therefore an additional condition and not a consequence of causality. [step 3.1, F9, F8, F4]

5.1 Clause (ii): fix $t\in\mathbb R$, $n\in S^2$ and $r>\max\{2a,a+c(t-t_0)\}$, so $r>2a$ and $r-a>c(t-t_0)$. For every $y\in B_a$ the triangle inequality of [F11] gives $R=|rn-y|\ge r-|y|\ge r-a>c(t-t_0)$, hence $t-R/c<t_0$; since (H1) makes $\rho$ and $\mathbf J$ vanish on the open set $\mathbb R^3\times((-\infty,t_0)\cup(T,\infty))$, all their partial derivatives vanish there too, so $\rho,\rho_t,\mathbf J,\mathbf J_t$ all vanish at $(y,t-R/c)$ for every $y\in B_a$, and the integrands of the field formulas of clause (a) of [F10] vanish pointwise on $\mathbb R^3$. Hence $\mathbf E(rn,t)=\mathbf B(rn,t)=0$, so $n\cdot\mathbf S(rn,t)=\mu_0^{-1}n\cdot(\mathbf E\times\mathbf B)(rn,t)=0$ for every $n\in S^2$, and [F12] gives $\int_{S^2}r^2n\cdot\mathbf S(rn,t)\,d\Omega(n)=0$ for all such $r$. [step 4.1, F10, F11, F12]

6.1 For every admissible source, step 5.1 gives an eventually zero flux integral at fixed observation time. For the admissible source of step 1.2, step 2.2 gives a strictly positive limit along $t=u_*+r/c$. Consequently a fixed-observation-time limit cannot replace the fixed-retarded-time limit as a theorem about all these sources; zero or nonradiating sources may give zero in both limits. Together with step 4.1, this proves both claimed hypothesis boundaries. [step 4.1, step 5.1, step 1.2, step 2.2] ∎
