## Definition

Let $M$ be a quasi-projective Deligne–Mumford stack over $\mathbb C$ with an algebraic torus $T$ action and a $T$-equivariant perfect obstruction theory of amplitude $[-1,0]$ as defined on the A page. Assume a global two-term vector-bundle presentation exists. Write the chosen equivariant presentation as $E^\bullet=[E^{-1}\xrightarrow{d}E^0]$ in degrees $-1,0$. Define $\mathrm{Def}=(E^0)^\vee$ and $\mathrm{Obs}=(E^{-1})^\vee$, the terms of its dual complex $[\mathrm{Def}\xrightarrow{d^\vee}\mathrm{Obs}]$ in degrees $0,1$. These are vector bundles by the presentation hypothesis; the actual deformation and obstruction sheaves are its kernel and cokernel and need not be locally free. For a rank-$v$ vector bundle $V$, write $\det V=\bigwedge^v V$, with $\det(0)=\mathcal O_M$. In the source convention, the **virtual canonical line** is
$
K_{\mathrm{vir}}=\det(\mathrm{Obs})\otimes\det(\mathrm{Def})^{-1}.
$
Thus $K_{\mathrm{vir}}=\det(E^\bullet)$. For a $T$-equivariant perfect complex $G^\bullet$, its **determinant line** is $\det(G^\bullet)=\bigotimes_i(\det G^i)^{(-1)^i}$ in any locally free presentation. Let $C\subset\mathrm{Obs}$ be the pullback of the intrinsic normal cone along $\mathrm{Obs}\to[\mathrm{Obs}/\mathrm{Def}]$. On each locus where $\operatorname{rk}\mathrm{Obs}$ is constant, the **virtual structure sheaf** is $\mathcal O_M^{\mathrm{vir}}=\sum_{i=0}^{\operatorname{rk}\mathrm{Obs}}(-1)^i[\operatorname{Tor}^{\mathcal O_{\mathrm{Obs}}}_i(\mathcal O_M,\mathcal O_C)]$ in $G_0^T(M)$, the abelian group of equivariant coherent sheaves modulo short-exact-sequence relations. Here $\mathcal O_M$ is the zero-section sheaf, and the Tor sheaves are the homology of its Koszul resolution tensored with $\mathcal O_C$: for $p:\mathrm{Obs}\to M$, that resolution has degree $-j$ term $p^*(\bigwedge^j\mathrm{Obs}^\vee)$ and differential contraction with the tautological section of $p^*\mathrm{Obs}$. Locally these are the coordinate Koszul complex of the regular zero section. Thus the Tor sheaves are coherent sheaves supported on $M$. This finite alternating sum defines the $K$-theoretic zero-section Gysin operation used here. This definition is conditional on the specified equivariant obstruction theory and presentation; it does not construct either one.

For localized Euler characteristic the fixed-stack convention is the following. The finite inertia of the separated finite-type stack $M$ has a uniform geometric order bound $D$. Choose a positive integer $N$ divisible by every integer $1,\ldots,D$, and pull the torus action back along the power isogeny $q:T_N\to T$, $t\mapsto t^N$. Write $F_N$ for the stack of objects equipped with coherent $T_N$-fixed structure. The hypothesis of a **proper generalized fixed locus** means that this fixed functor is represented by a proper DM stack. Algebraicity and properness are part of the fixed-locus hypothesis, rather than a consequence of the Koszul construction. Its evaluation $i:F_N\to M$ is a closed immersion, its geometric image is exactly the coarse torus-fixed set, and further divisible power isogenies give the same fixed functor, as proved below. Thus this hypothesis uses the saturated isogenous fixed convention. Properness of an arbitrary honest fixed stack before saturation would not suffice.

Let $S$ be generated by $1-\chi$ for all nontrivial characters of $T$. The construction below proves that $i_*:S^{-1}G_0^T(F_N)\to S^{-1}G_0^T(M)$ is an isomorphism. Define
$\chi_T(M,\gamma)=\chi_T(F_N,(i_*)^{-1}\gamma)$,
where the right-hand proper Euler characteristic is the alternating coherent cohomology representation. For the virtual structure sheaf and any compatible perfect twist, its inverse image is computed after the isogeny by
$(i_*)^{-1}\mathcal O_M^{\mathrm{vir}}=\mathcal O_{F_N}^{\mathrm{vir}}/\lambda_{-1}((N^{\mathrm{vir}})^\vee)$,
with $N^{\mathrm{vir}}=((E^\bullet)^\vee|_{F_N})_{\mathrm{mov}}$ and $\lambda_{-1}(V-W)=\lambda_{-1}(V)/\lambda_{-1}(W)$. Individual weights in this displayed calculation may be fractional relative to $T$; the total inverse class descends uniquely to the original localized $T$ group. This virtual denominator is the proved formula for this virtual class, while an arbitrary input uses the ordinary inverse concentration pushforward. Specified square-root and perfect-twist choices remain hypotheses.
For a finite-type complex $T$-scheme $X$, a $T$-equivariant universal coherent sheaf $\mathcal F$ on $M\times X$, projection maps $\pi:M\times X\to M$ and $\rho:M\times X\to X$, and equivariant line bundles $L_4,L_5$ on $X$, $L_4-L_5$ denotes their virtual difference in equivariant $K$-theory, not a quotient line bundle. Assume $D=R\pi_*(\mathcal F\otimes\rho^*L_4)-R\pi_*(\mathcal F\otimes\rho^*L_5)$ is a difference of equivariant perfect complexes on $M$ (for example this requires appropriate proper-support and perfect-pushforward hypotheses). Define $\det D$ as the tensor quotient of their determinant lines. A square root of a line $Q$ means a line bundle $S$ with a specified equivariant isomorphism $S^{\otimes2}\cong Q$; the source requires such a choice and does not assert that it exists in every moduli problem.


For a specified equivariant square root $S_K^{\otimes2}\cong K_{\mathrm{vir}}$, define $\widehat{\mathcal O}^{\mathrm{vir}}=\mathcal O_M^{\mathrm{vir}}\otimes S_K$. With the additional universal-sheaf data just specified, put $Q=K_{\mathrm{vir}}\otimes\det D$; a chosen equivariant $S_Q$ with $S_Q^{\otimes2}\cong Q$ defines the determinant-modified class $\widetilde{\mathcal O}^{\mathrm{vir}}=\mathcal O_M^{\mathrm{vir}}\otimes S_Q$. These definitions require the chosen square root; they assert neither existence nor independence of that choice. This latter convention is the sheaf factor in Nekrasov–Okounkov Definition 1, equation (24); their scalar/curve-variable prefactor in equation (25) is separate.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the separated quasi-projective complex DM stack, equivariant global two-term POT, determinant/perfect-pushforward data and chosen square roots specified in the Definition. For localized pushforward, the represented proper saturated fixed functor is the explicit input, with the uniform divisible isogeny and original torus as specified there.

[F1] [[def-gts-perfect-obstruction-theory-and-intrinsic-normal-cone]] supplies the perfect-obstruction-theory axioms, intrinsic cone charts, their cotangent maps and the obstruction bundle stack. The present proof constructs the equivariant cone descent, zero-section coherent operation and presentation comparison used here.

[F2] $G_0$ means the coherent-sheaf/exact-sequence quotient in the Definition. Derived zero-section and parameter operations below are their displayed finite Koszul homology classes. All group actions, coherent descent data and fixed structures are the specified algebraic ones. The fixed-locus hypothesis includes representability and properness, and does not assume concentration or a virtual formula.

[F3] The proof below constructs the generic flat-inertia groupoid, character-torsion support induction, moving-Euler inverse, global deformation operations and compatible-POT comparison. Source locators record the published constructions being reconstructed; no source theorem is substituted as an unproved logical premise. Finite group invariants over $\mathbb C$ are exact by averaging; torus invariants are exact by weight decomposition.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct. Construct the equivariant coherent class, compare its presentations, then construct saturated fixed data, concentration, compatible virtual pullback and original-torus Euler pushforward.

**Given:** [F1]–[F3] and all data in the Definition.

1.1 The intrinsic cone and its inverse image are equivariant without a choice of unrelated cone actions. The action isomorphism $a^*E^\bullet\simeq\operatorname{pr}_2^*E^\bullet$ over $T\times M$ is an isomorphism of obstruction theories over the pulled cotangent complex. The intrinsic-cone charts use associated graded ideals, which commute with flat base change; applying that isomorphism identifies the two pulled cone charts. The group-action cocycle identifies these maps on $T\times T\times M$. Thus the inverse-image cone ideal descends with an equivariant structure in the chosen equivariant bundle $\mathrm{Obs}$. The zero section, tautological Koszul differential and resulting homology are equivariant. Coherence and finite Koszul length consequently give the asserted class in $G_0^T(M)$ for the chosen presentation. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 The following stabilization calculation verifies one necessary presentation comparison explicitly. Adding an equivariant contractible summand $[V\xrightarrow{1}V]$ adds $V^\vee$ to both dual terms. The inverse-image cone becomes $C\times_M V^\vee$ in $\mathrm{Obs}\oplus V^\vee$: the added denominator acts transitively by translations on the added numerator. Its zero-section Koszul complex is the tensor product of the old complex with the coordinate Koszul complex on $V^\vee$. The latter resolves its zero section and stays exact after tensoring with $\mathcal O_C$ because its polynomial variables form a regular sequence over every base ring. Its derived restriction is therefore $\mathcal O_C$ in degree zero. The two alternating homology classes agree. The determinant factors also cancel, since the same $\det V$ occurs once with each exponent. This proves invariance under equivariant contractible stabilization. The following common-atlas argument proves the arbitrary-presentation comparison as well; DM virtual localization is proved in the subsequent constructions. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For arbitrary two equivariant presentations of the same obstruction theory over $L_M$, write the canonically identified linear Picard stack as $\mathcal E=[F_1/F_0]=[G_1/G_0]$. Form the fibre product $P=F_1\times_{\mathcal E}G_1$. As a sheaf this consists of pairs of numerator sections together with an isomorphism between their images. Addition and multiplication by local functions are induced by the linear Picard structure, so $P$ is a sheaf of $\mathcal O_M$-modules. The first projection has kernel $G_0$: an object of its zero fibre is a section $g$ of $G_1$ and an isomorphism from zero to $g$, exactly the section of $G_0$ giving that translation. The projection is locally surjective because $G_1\to\mathcal E$ is an atlas. Thus $0\to G_0\to P\to F_1\to0$ is an exact sequence of sheaves of modules. Locally lift a finite basis of $F_1$ through this epimorphism; extending the lifts linearly splits the sequence. Hence $P$ is a vector bundle. The other projection gives $0\to F_0\to P\to G_1\to0$. Both constructions are intrinsic and equivariant; no equivariant splitting is required. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Pull back the same intrinsic cone $\mathcal C\hookrightarrow\mathcal E$ to $P$, obtaining one closed cone $C_P$. Its inverse-image descriptions along the two projections are equal scheme-theoretically by their common fibre-product definition. If $q:P\to F_1$ is the first vector-bundle epimorphism, it is flat and $\mathcal O_{C_P}=q^*\mathcal O_{C_F}$. The zero sections satisfy $q\circ0_P=0_{F_1}$. Associativity of derived tensor therefore gives the natural global equivariant isomorphism
$L0_P^*\mathcal O_{C_P}=L0_P^*Lq^*\mathcal O_{C_F}\simeq L0_{F_1}^*\mathcal O_{C_F}$.
Every zero-section pullback here has finite Tor amplitude, computed by its finite coordinate Koszul complex. The same argument for $P\to G_1$ identifies this object with $L0_{G_1}^*\mathcal O_{C_G}$. Taking alternating homology classes proves equality in $G_0^T(M)$ for arbitrary equivariant global presentations, rather than gluing an equality of $G_0$ classes from an atlas. The compatible isomorphism of obstruction theories over $L_M$ is essential: the argument compares the same embedded intrinsic cone, not unrelated POTs. The two exact bundle sequences also give $\det F_1\otimes(\det F_0)^{-1}\cong\det G_1\otimes(\det G_0)^{-1}$; this is the same virtual determinant comparison, with duals as prescribed in the Definition. [F1, F2, F3, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 **Saturation and independence of the fixed datum.** Here is the exact finite-isogeny argument. At a geometric coarse-fixed object $x$, the group of pairs $(t,x\simeq t\cdot x)$ is an algebraic group extension of $T$ by the finite inertia group $G_x$. Its connected component $H$ maps onto $T$ with finite kernel of order at most $D$. Conjugation of connected $H$ on finite $G_x$ is constant, hence trivial. The commutator in $H$ is consequently a map into a finite group and is trivial; there is no positive-dimensional unipotent part because its image in $T$ would be trivial and the kernel is finite. Thus $H$ is a torus and $H\to T$ is an isogeny. Its cocharacter-lattice cokernel has exponent dividing its kernel order $e\le D$. Since $e\mid N$, the power map $[N]:T_N\to T$ factors through $H$, uniquely because a connected torus has no nontrivial map to a finite group. This gives a unique coherent $T_N$-fixed structure at every geometric coarse-fixed object. Conversely a fixed structure makes its coarse orbit a point. Thus the geometric image is exactly the coarse fixed set, and the complement is the geometric moving complement required below. [F1, F2, F3, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 For families, evaluation is fully faithful. The discrepancy between two fixed structures, or between an underlying isomorphism and its transported one, is a section of the finite unramified inertia over the connected torus times the base. It is the identity at the torus identity. On geometric fibres it is therefore constant and equal to the identity. Two sections of an unramified separated space with equal geometric values are equal: their equalizer is an open-and-closed immersion containing every geometric point. Consequently the discrepancy is identically trivial, including over nonreduced bases. Evaluation is a monomorphism. It is proper because $F_N$ is proper and $M$ separated, by its graph and the proper projection, so it is a closed immersion. [F1, F2, F3, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 If $N$ is replaced by $Nk$, the unique geometric lift through $H$ has cocharacter exponent $Nk/e$. Its restriction to $\mu_k=\ker(T_{Nk}\to T_N)$ is trivial, since $e\mid N$. Hence the kernel of the further isogeny acts trivially on every fixed family: it is trivial on all geometric fibres, and finite unramified inertia gives equality on the family as above. Fppf descent of the fixed coherence along that isogeny gives a unique $T_N$-fixed structure. Thus $F_{Nk}\simeq F_N$ as functors on all schemes, rather than only as sets of geometric points. A common divisible refinement compares any two allowed integers. [F1, F2, F3, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 **Finite-isogeny comparison: actual equivariant coherent categories.** Let $q:T'\to T$ be an isogeny of complex algebraic tori with finite diagonalizable kernel $K$, and pull the specified $T$ action on $M$ back along $q$. The kernel acts through the identity of $T$ with the canonical coherence supplied by this pullback. Thus a $T'$-equivariant coherent sheaf has a canonical $K$ action as an $\mathcal O_M$-module. Its character projectors split it as a finite direct sum indexed by $X^*(K)$. The projectors are exact: after adjoining the roots of unity in $\mathbb C$ they are the finite sums $|K|^{-1}\sum_{k\in K}\kappa(k)^{-1}k$. All summands are coherent and preserved by $T'$ because the tori commute. [F1, F2, F3, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 The trivial-$K$ summand category is equivalent to $\operatorname{Coh}^T(M)$. To see this directly, a linearization over $T'\times M$ with trivial $K$ action identifies its two pullbacks to $(T'\times_TT')\times M=K\times T'\times M$ by the identity on the kernel factor. Faithfully flat descent along the finite flat $T'\to T$ therefore gives a unique linearization over $T\times M$; the cocycle descends because it holds after the same faithfully flat pullback. Conversely every $T$ linearization pulls back to one with trivial $K$ action. These functors are exact inverses. [F1, F2, F3, step 8.1, algebra]

10.1 Every character $\kappa$ of $K$ extends to a character $\widetilde\kappa$ of $T'$ by the exact sequence of character lattices. Tensoring by its inverse identifies the $\kappa$-summand category with the trivial summand category. Choose one lattice representative for each character of $K$. The corresponding Laurent monomials form a basis of $R(T')$ over $R(T)$; the preceding exact category decompositions consequently prove
$R(T')\otimes_{R(T)}G_0^T(M)\xrightarrow{\sim}G_0^{T'}(M)$,
where $a\otimes[F]$ maps to the character twist of the pulled-back sheaf. In particular this is a finite free, faithfully flat scalar extension, and restriction on $G_0$ is injective. [F1, F2, F3, step 9.1, algebra]

11.1 Character localization is compatible with this comparison. If $\rho\ne1$ is a character of $T'$, a positive multiple $\rho^n$ descends to $T$. Inverting $1-\rho^n$ makes $1-\rho$ invertible, since $(1-\rho)(1+\rho+\cdots+\rho^{n-1})=1-\rho^n$. Thus localizing the two character rings at all nontrivial characters gives the same scalar extension as first extending and then localizing at the $T'$ characters. It remains faithfully flat. Any equality between specified $T$-equivariant classes can therefore be checked after the isogeny. [F1, F2, F3, step 10.1, algebra]

12.1 **Character torsion on the geometric moving complement.** Let $M$ be the specified separated finite-type complex DM stack with torus $T$ action, and let $U$ contain no point whose coarse $T$ orbit is a point. This is the geometric moving complement, independent of whether an honest fixed structure exists before an isogeny. Let $S\subset R(T)$ be generated by all $1-\chi$, $\chi\ne1$. We prove $S^{-1}G_0^T(U)=0$. [F1, F2, F3, step 11.1, algebra]

13.1 Write $\mathcal Y=[U/T]$ and take any nonempty reduced integral closed support $W\subset\mathcal Y$. Denote the successive dense restrictions below by $W^\circ$. The following construction supplies the two quotient steps at exactly this characteristic-zero DM/torus scope. A gerbe over a sheaf means that objects exist fppf locally and that any two objects with the same sheaf image are isomorphic fppf locally. Every quotient below is fppf sheafification of the orbit presheaf. We use the ordinary scheme and étale-space framework of DM stacks: an actual étale equivalence-relation presentation gives its algebraic space; algebraic-space overlaps may be replaced by scheme étale charts in the same presentation. This foundational étale construction, the Jacobian criterion and generic flatness are retained. Neither the general flat-equivalence quotient Bootstrap 04S6 nor the general flat free-action quotient Bootstrap 06PH is an assumed supplier; both needed instances are constructed here by explicit smooth transverse charts.
**(a)** The diagonal of $\mathcal Y$ is affine of finite type. Over two objects, its isomorphisms are pairs of a torus element and an isomorphism in $U$; the latter is a base change of the finite diagonal of $U$. The transporter is therefore finite over the corresponding torus, and affine. These assertions descend over local trivializations of the torus torsors defining the two objects. The same applies to $W$. Choose a finite affine smooth atlas $A_0\to W$. Then $R_0=A_0\times_W A_0$ is a scheme, since it is affine over the scheme $A_0\times A_0$. Its projections are smooth. The pullback of the reduced stack $W$ is reduced, because smooth morphisms have geometrically reduced fibres.
**(b)** Shrink $W$ to its nonempty smooth locus. Here is why that locus is well defined and dense in this setting, without assuming the integral support was initially smooth. The reduced finite-type scheme $A_0$ has a dense open smooth locus over $\mathbb C$. This locus is groupoid invariant: for an arrow with source $a$ and target $b$, smoothness of both projections identifies regularity at $a$ and $b$. Equivalently, smooth local completions are formal power-series extensions after separable residue-field extension; both dimension and embedding dimension increase by the same number, so regularity holds at one end exactly when it holds on the arrow and at the other end. In characteristic zero regular finite-type local rings are smooth. Thus the smooth locus descends to a dense open of $W$. Restrict the atlas and choose finitely many affine opens covering it. From now on $A$ and $R=A\times_WA$ are smooth finite-type schemes over $\mathbb C$.
**(c)** Let $G\to A$ be the stabilizer group scheme, the pullback of the inertia. It is affine and of finite presentation. There is a dense open of $A$ on which it is flat by generic flatness. Take the union of all such opens. It is invariant: on $R$ the source and target pullbacks of $G$ are isomorphic by conjugation. If $O\subset A$ is a flat open, then $t(s^{-1}(O))$ is open because $t$ is smooth, and the group scheme is flat there by faithfully flat descent along $s^{-1}(O)\to t(s^{-1}(O))$. This map is smooth and surjective onto that open. Hence that image is contained in the same maximal flat open. Restrict $W$ once more to the corresponding dense open, and restrict $A,R,G$.
**(d)** Every geometric fibre $J$ of $G$ is smooth; the special form $[U/T]$ gives a proof without invoking the general characteristic-zero smoothness theorem for group schemes. Over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field $k$, $J\to T_k$ is the finite transporter map described in the preceding construction, with kernel $K$ the finite étale inertia of the corresponding object of $U$. Let $H\subset T_k$ be its scheme-theoretic image. Its coordinate Hopf algebra is a quotient of the Laurent group algebra $k[\Lambda]$, $\Lambda=X^*(T)$, hence is spanned by images of characters, all group-like. Distinct group-like elements in a coalgebra are linearly independent: choose a shortest dependence, apply comultiplication, subtract the first group-like element tensored with that dependence, and use independence of the remaining terms to force every group-like element in the dependence to equal the first. The only identifications are therefore a subgroup $L\subset\Lambda$, and $\mathcal O(H)=k[\Lambda/L]$. The finitely generated abelian group $\Lambda/L$ is a product of a free group and finite cyclic groups; its group algebra is a product of Laurent polynomial algebras because characteristic zero makes the torsion polynomials have distinct roots. Thus $H$ is smooth.
**(e)** The finite map $J\to H$ is surjective by the lying-over property of integral extensions. It is flat on a nonempty open of each component of $H$ by generic flatness. Translations spread a flat open to all of $H$: every geometric $h\in H(k)$ lifts to $j\in J(k)$, and translation by $j$ over translation by $h$ is an isomorphism of this finite map. Translates of a flat open therefore cover every closed point, and hence all of the finite-type Jacobson scheme $H$. Thus $J\to H$ is finite faithfully flat. The group identity gives $J\times_HJ\cong J\times K$, $(j,k)\mapsto(j,jk)$. After that faithfully flat base change it is the projection with finite étale factor $K$, so it is finite étale; this may also be checked by flat descent and vanishing of its module of differentials. Therefore $J$ is smooth over $k$. Since $G\to A$ is flat of finite presentation with geometrically smooth fibres, it is smooth.
**(f)** We now construct the first quotient in the required general relative smooth form. Let $C$ and $X$ be finite-type $\mathbb C$-schemes, let $X\to C$ be smooth, and let a smooth group scheme $H\to C$ of finite presentation act freely on $X$ over $C$. Fix a closed point $x\in X(\mathbb C)$ and shrink about its image so the relative dimensions are constant. Freeness injects $\operatorname{Lie}(H)_x$ into $T_{X/C,x}$: a tangent vector in its kernel would give a nonidentity infinitesimal stabilizer, contrary to the action map being a monomorphism. Write $d=\dim H_c$. Choose $d$ regular functions $f_1,\ldots,f_d$ on an affine neighbourhood of $x$ whose relative differentials restrict to a basis on this orbit tangent subspace. Replace $f_i$ by $f_i-f_i(x)$, using the constants $f_i(x)\in\mathbb C$. Their common zero locus, after shrinking about $x$, is therefore an actual $\mathbb C$-scheme $V$ containing $x$, smooth over $C$ of relative codimension $d$. Its relative tangent space is complementary to the orbit tangent space at all its points.
**(g)** The action map $H\times_CV\to X$ is étale. At $(1,v)$ its relative derivative is the direct-sum map from the orbit tangent space and $T_{V/C,v}$, an isomorphism. Both source and target are smooth over $C$, so the relative Jacobian criterion makes the action map étale there; translation in $H$ and the action on $X$ make it étale at every $(h,v)$. Its image is open and $H$-saturated. Take the collection of these transverse affine slices through closed $\mathbb C$-points. Their saturated open images contain every closed point of $X$ and therefore cover all of $X$: a nonempty closed complement in a finite-type $\mathbb C$-scheme would have a closed point. Quasi-compactness gives a finite subcover. Let $V$ denote their disjoint union. The map $H\times_CV\to X$ is étale and surjective.
**(h)** On $V$ the relation “lie in the same $H$ orbit” is represented by the scheme $E=V\times_X(H\times_CV)$. One projection $E\to V$ is the base change of the étale map in the preceding construction, so it is étale; inversion gives the other projection étale as well. The map $E\to V\times V$ is a monomorphism because the action is free. The groupoid identities come from multiplication and inversion in $H$. Thus $(V,E)$ is an actual étale equivalence relation and presents an algebraic space $Z=V/E$ in the étale-space sense under this ordinary étale-presentation convention. Since every orbit of $X$ meets $V$ étale locally, its sheaf quotient is exactly $X/H=Z$, not just a bijection on geometric points. Indeed local lifts exist by the preceding construction and two lifts are equal precisely through $E$; sheafification gives inverse maps.
**(i)** Freeness identifies $H\times_CX\to X\times_ZX$ as an isomorphism of fppf sheaves: an orbit pair has a group element locally, and that element is unique and so glues. In particular $X\times_ZV\cong H\times_CV$. Thus the chart base change of $X\to Z$ is the smooth surjective projection $H\times_CV\to V$. It follows directly on the étale chart $V\to Z$ that $X\to Z$ is smooth, surjective and locally of finite presentation, and is an fppf $H$-torsor. This finishes the required free-action quotient construction; no quotient theorem for a general flat group scheme was used.
**(j)** Apply this construction to $X=R$, $C=A$ via the target $t$, and $H=G$. The action $G\times_{A,t}R\to R$, $(g,r)\mapsto gr$, is free by cancellation of arrows. It preserves both endpoints. The hypotheses hold because $R\to A$ and $G\to A$ are smooth. Hence $Q=R/G$ is the algebraic space just constructed, and $R\to Q$ is a smooth surjective torsor. The source and target descend to $s_Q,t_Q:Q\to A$. The transverse slice charts used to construct $Q$ are smooth over $A$ by the preceding construction, so $t_Q$ is smooth. Inversion descends and interchanges the two maps, proving $s_Q$ smooth as well.
**(k)** The map $j_Q:Q\to A\times A$ is a monomorphism on all test schemes. Two local lifts $r,r'$ with the same endpoints differ by the unique target stabilizer element $r'r^{-1}$, so have the same class. This equality descends from the fppf cover on which they lift. Composition of classes is well defined: replacing one arrow by a target stabilizer translate changes its composite by another target stabilizer translate, with conjugation moving a source stabilizer across an intervening arrow. This also proves that inversion and identity descend. Thus $Q\rightrightarrows A$ is a smooth equivalence relation in algebraic spaces. It remains to construct its quotient; its existence is not inferred merely from that description.
