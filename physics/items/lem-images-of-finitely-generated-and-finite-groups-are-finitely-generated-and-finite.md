## Statement

Let $\varphi:G\to H$ be a group homomorphism
([[def-group-homomorphism]]). Then:

1. if $G$ is finitely generated ([[def-finitely-generated-group]]), then its
   image $\operatorname{im}\varphi\le H$
   ([[def-kernel-and-image-of-group-homomorphism]]) is finitely generated;
2. if $G$ is finite ([[def-finite-cardinality]]), then
   $\operatorname{im}\varphi$ is finite.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A group homomorphism $\varphi:G\to H$.

[F1] A group homomorphism $f:G\to G'$ satisfies $f(xy)=f(x)f(y)$ for all $x,y\in G$ ([[def-group-homomorphism]]).

[F2] For a group homomorphism $f$ with $f:G\to G'$: $f(e)=e'$ and $f(g^{-1})=f(g)^{-1}$ for every $g\in G$ ([[lem-group-homomorphism-basic-properties]]).

[F3] The subgroup $\langle S\rangle$ generated by $S$ is the smallest subgroup of $G$ containing $S$ ([[def-generated-subgroup]]).

[F4] A subset $H\subseteq G$ is a subgroup exactly when $e\in H$, $H$ is closed under the operation, and $H$ is closed under inverses ([[def-subgroup]]).

[F5] A group is finitely generated when some finite subset generates it ([[def-finitely-generated-group]]).

[F6] The image of a homomorphism $f$ is $\operatorname{im}f=\{f(g):g\in G\}$ ([[def-kernel-and-image-of-group-homomorphism]]).

[F7] First isomorphism theorem: $G/\ker f\cong\operatorname{im}f$ for every homomorphism $f:G\to H$ ([[thm-first-isomorphism-theorem-groups]]).

[F8] The image of a group homomorphism is a subgroup of the target ([[thm-image-subgroup-and-kernel-normal]]).

[F9] For a finite group $G$ and a normal subgroup $N$, the quotient $G/N$ is finite with $|G/N|=|G|/|N|$ ([[cor-order-of-a-quotient-group]]).

[F10] If $A$ is finite and $A\to B$ is a bijection, then $B$ is finite ([[def-finite-cardinality]], consequence (c)).

[F11] The power set of a finite set is finite ([[cor-cardinality-of-the-power-set]]).

[F12] A subset of a finite set is finite ([[thm-subset-of-a-finite-set]]).

[F13] A function is bijective when it is injective and surjective, and the image of a subset $S$ of its domain is $f[S]=\{f(x):x\in S\}$ ([[def-injection-surjection-bijection]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 (Preliminary: images of finite sets.) Let $A$ be a finite set and $f:A\to Y$ any function. The map $\Phi:f[A]\to\mathcal{P}(A)$, $\Phi(y):=f^{-1}[\{y\}]$, is injective: for $y\neq y'$ no $a\in A$ has $f(a)=y$ and $f(a)=y'$ simultaneously, so the two preimages are disjoint, and each is nonempty because $y\in f[A]$ [F13]. Hence $\Phi$ is a bijection from $f[A]$ onto its image, which is a subset of the finite set $\mathcal{P}(A)$ [F11] and therefore finite [F12]; by transport along the bijection, $f[A]$ is finite [F10]. [F10, F11, F12, F13]

1.2 (Words in generators.) For $S\subseteq G$ let $W(S)$ be the set of elements of $G$ expressible as $s_1^{\varepsilon_1}\cdots s_k^{\varepsilon_k}$ with $k\ge0$, $s_i\in S$, $\varepsilon_i\in\{1,-1\}$, the empty product for $k=0$ being $e$. Then $W(S)=\langle S\rangle$. Indeed $\langle S\rangle$ is a subgroup containing $S$ [F3], so by the defining closure conditions it contains $e$, every product of elements of $S$, and every inverse, whence $W(S)\subseteq\langle S\rangle$ [F4]; conversely $W(S)$ contains $S$ and $e$, is closed under multiplication by concatenating words, and is closed under inverses by reversing the word and negating all exponents, so $W(S)$ is a subgroup containing $S$ [F4], and minimality gives $\langle S\rangle\subseteq W(S)$ [F3]. [F3, F4]

1.3 (Finite case.) If $G$ is finite, the first isomorphism theorem provides an isomorphism $G/\ker\varphi\to\operatorname{im}\varphi$, in particular a bijection [F7, F13]. Since $G$ is finite, the quotient $G/\ker\varphi$ is finite [F9]. By transport along the bijection, $\operatorname{im}\varphi$ is finite [F10]. [F7, F9, F10, F13]

2.1 (Images of generated subgroups.) For every $S\subseteq G$ one has $\varphi(\langle S\rangle)=\langle\varphi(S)\rangle$. For the inclusion $\supseteq$: $\varphi(S)\subseteq\varphi(\langle S\rangle)$, and $\varphi(\langle S\rangle)$ is the image of the subgroup $\langle S\rangle$, hence a subgroup of $H$ [F8], so minimality gives $\langle\varphi(S)\rangle\subseteq\varphi(\langle S\rangle)$ [F3]. For the inclusion $\subseteq$: the elements of $\langle S\rangle$ have the word form of step 1.2, and multiplicativity together with inversion gives $\varphi(s_1^{\varepsilon_1}\cdots s_k^{\varepsilon_k})=\varphi(s_1)^{\varepsilon_1}\cdots\varphi(s_k)^{\varepsilon_k}$ [F1, F2], an element of $\langle\varphi(S)\rangle$; hence $\varphi(\langle S\rangle)\subseteq\langle\varphi(S)\rangle$. [F1, F2, F3, F8, step 1.2]

3.1 (Finitely generated case.) If $G$ is finitely generated, fix a finite $S\subseteq G$ with $\langle S\rangle=G$ [F5]; this is one existential instantiation, no choice principle is used. Then $\operatorname{im}\varphi=\varphi(G)=\varphi(\langle S\rangle)=\langle\varphi(S)\rangle$ [F6, step 2.1], and $\varphi(S)$ is the image of the finite set $S$ under the function $\varphi$, hence finite by step 1.1. Therefore $\operatorname{im}\varphi$ is generated by the finite set $\varphi(S)$ and is finitely generated [F5]. [F5, F6, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 Clause 1 is step 3.1, clause 2 is step 1.3, and the preliminary statement about images of finite sets is step 1.1, so the lemma is proved. [step 1.1, step 1.3, step 3.1] ∎
