## Statement

Let $m_*>0$, $Q_e\in\mathbb R$, and $\Phi_B\in\mathbb R$ be the SI mass, electric-charge, and magnetic-flux inputs of [[pthm-emg-xs-rn-model]]. Put $M=G_Nm_*/c^2>0$, $q_e=\sqrt{G_N/(4\pi\varepsilon_0c^4)}Q_e$, $q_m=\sqrt{4\pi G_N/(\mu_0c^4)}\Phi_B/(4\pi)$, $Q^2=q_e^2+q_m^2$, and $f(r)=1-2M/r+Q^2/r^2$. Use the selected ingoing product $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{in}}=\mathbb R_v\times(0,\infty)_r\times S^2$ and its metric, field, and future orientation from [[def-emg-xs-ingoing-rn]].

For $Q^2>0$ and $M^2\ge Q^2$, let $r_\pm=M\pm\sqrt{M^2-Q^2}$. Each positive root is a regular null hypersurface and a Killing horizon generated by $\xi=\partial_v$, with the convention

$$\kappa_H:=\frac{f'(r_H)}2=\frac{r_H-r_{\mathrm{other}}}{2r_H^2}.$$

Thus $\kappa_+=0$ at extremality and $\kappa_\pm$ has unit m$^{-1}$; the associated SI acceleration convention is $c^2\kappa_\pm$. At a simple outer root, $\kappa_+>0$, the selected ingoing extension has a local bifurcation chart with dimensionless coordinates $U,V$ satisfying $UV=-K(r)$ and an auxiliary coordinate length $\ell_*>0$. At extremality the ingoing chart is regular but this simple-root chart is not asserted.

Use the exact path and relative-boundary convention of [[def-emg-causal-solution-and-horizon-vocabulary]]: the path is continuous, future causal, and piecewise $C^1$ with a finite subdivision on each compact parameter segment; one such path must reach arbitrarily large $r$.

Define $\mathcal E$ on this product to be the set of points from which a future piecewise $C^1$ causal path has unbounded radial coordinate. Conditional on the exact draft mathematical interface of [[thm-emg-xs-rn-escape]], for $Q^2>0$ and $M^2\ge Q^2$,

$$\mathcal E=\{(v,r,\omega):r>r_+\},\qquad \partial_{\mathcal M_{\mathrm{in}}}\mathcal E=\{r=r_+\}.$$

For $Q^2=0$, the only positive root is $2M$, it is a regular simple crossing with $\kappa=1/(4M)$, and the same explicit escape observable has boundary $r=2M$. For $Q^2>M^2$, there is no positive root and every point of $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{in}}$ belongs to $\mathcal E$. These are statements about the selected metric and escape observable, not an event-horizon assertion requiring a conformal completion.

## Proof

1.1 If $Q^2>0$ and $M^2\ge Q^2$, the quadratic numerator factors as $r^2f=(r-r_+)(r-r_-)$; both roots are positive because $M>0$ and $\sqrt{M^2-Q^2}<M$. If $Q^2=0$, then $f=1-2M/r$ and its only positive root is $2M$. If $Q^2>M^2$, then $r^2f=(r-M)^2+(Q^2-M^2)>0$ for every $r>0$, so there is no positive root. [given, F1, algebra]

2.1 The inverse of the $v,r$ block in [F1] is $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&f\end{pmatrix}$, so $g^{-1}(dr,dr)=f$. The vector $\xi=\partial_v$ is Killing because the metric coefficients do not depend on $v$, and $\xi^\flat=-f\,dv+dr$ equals $dr$ at a root. Therefore each root is null and $\xi$ is its null normal. Since $\xi^2=-f$, $d(\xi^2)=-f'(r_H)dr=-2\kappa_H\xi^\flat$ there. For two distinct roots, differentiating $f=(r-r_+)(r-r_-)/r^2$ gives $f'(r_\pm)=(r_\pm-r_\mp)/r_\pm^2$; at $Q^2=M^2$ this derivative is zero. For $Q^2=0$, $f'(2M)=1/(2M)$ and $\kappa=1/(4M)$. The product definition [F1] keeps the metric nondegenerate at each root. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 For $Q^2>0$ and $M^2\ge Q^2$, [L1] gives the claimed root crossing and, when $M^2>Q^2$, the local bifurcation chart. If $Q^2=M^2$, [L1] gives the regular ingoing crossing but explicitly does not assert that simple-root chart. For $Q^2=0$, put $r_H=2M$, $\kappa=1/(4M)$, and write $f=(r-r_H)b(r)$ with $b(r)=1/r$ and $b(r_H)=2\kappa>0$. Then $$\frac1f-\frac1{2\kappa(r-r_H)}=\frac{2\kappa-b(r)}{2\kappa b(r)(r-r_H)}=-\frac1{2\kappa b(r)}\int_0^1b'(r_H+s(r-r_H))\,ds,$$ so this difference extends smoothly across $r_H$. Integrate it to $h$ with $h(r_H)=0$, and define $K(r)=(r-r_H)\ell_*^{-1}e^{2\kappa h(r)}$. Then $K'(r_H)=\ell_*^{-1}>0$ and differentiation gives $fK'=2\kappa K$ on both sides and at the root. By [F3], shrink the radial interval so $K'>0$ and $K$ has a smooth local inverse $\rho$ about zero. On the selected ingoing model define $$V=e^{\kappa v},\qquad U=-K(r)e^{-\kappa v}.$$ Thus $UV=-K(r)$, with $U<0$ for $r>r_H$ and $U>0$ for $r<r_H$, and the Jacobian is $\kappa K'(r)>0$ across the root. The inverse is $r=\rho(-UV)$ and $v=\kappa^{-1}\log V$ on $V>0$. Differentiating gives $dv=dV/(\kappa V)$ and $dr=-(V\,dU+U\,dV)/K'(r)$. Substitution in the original ingoing block $-f\,dv^2+2\,dv\,dr$ cancels its $dV^2$ coefficient by $fK'=2\kappa K$, leaving $$-\frac1{\kappa K'(\rho(-UV))}(dU\otimes dV+dV\otimes dU).$$ This coefficient is smooth and nonzero, and the sphere term is $\rho(-UV)^2\gamma$. Choose $\varepsilon>0$ so these formulas are defined with $\rho(-UV)>0$ on $(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)^2$. They give a smooth Lorentzian metric on the full rectangle times $S^2$, agreeing with the selected ingoing metric on its entire $V>0$ half, including both $r<r_H$ and $r>r_H$. Glue that half to its old coordinate preimage. Any overlap sequence tending to a new point on $V=0$ has $v=\kappa^{-1}\log V\to-\infty$, so cannot approach a finite old point; the other rectangle edges are not new points. This gives a Hausdorff local extension. The field is zero in this uncharged case. The old Killing field becomes $\kappa(V\partial_V-U\partial_U)$; it extends smoothly, preserves the displayed metric, is null and normal on either axis away from their intersection, and vanishes on the spacelike sphere $U=V=0$. Thus this is the required two-sided local bifurcation chart. [L1, F1, F3, step 1.1, algebra, construct]

2.3 For $Q^2>0$ and $M^2\ge Q^2$, apply [L2] using the exact observable [L3]. Its current Definition and manifest placement now exist and were checked against this use; the escape conclusion remains conditional on the current draft mathematical proof, with no maximal-extension or conformal-completion inference. [L2, L3, step 1.1]

2.4 If $Q^2>M^2$, then $f>0$ by step 1.1 and the future null field $n$ of [F1] satisfies $dr/dv=f/2>0$. For any starting $r_0>0$, the function $v(r)=v_0+\int_{r_0}^r2/f(s)\,ds$ is smooth and strictly increasing on $[r_0,\infty)$; since $f(r)\to1$, $v(r)\to+\infty$ and the corresponding integral curve reaches arbitrarily large $r$. Hence every point belongs to $\mathcal E$. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

2.5 If $Q^2=0$, then $f<0$ below $2M$, $f=0$ at $2M$, and $f>0$ above. The product splitting in [F1] gives a unique decomposition of every tangent as $Z=\alpha\ell+\beta n+W$ with $W$ tangent to the sphere. Since the sphere term is orthogonal to $\ell,n$ and positive, causality gives $g(Z,Z)=-2\alpha\beta+g(W,W)\le0$ and hence $\alpha\beta\ge0$. The timelike condition is $-2\alpha\beta+g(W,W)<0$, so a timelike vector has $\alpha\beta>0$ and its coefficients have the same nonzero sign. Each of the two sign branches is connected: scale $W$ to zero within the timelike cone and then vary the positive (respectively negative) coefficients while keeping them equal. Since $N=\ell+n$ has coefficients $(1,1)$, the selected future timelike component is the positive branch. Every future causal vector lies in its closure, so $\alpha,\beta\ge0$. Therefore $dr(Z)=-\alpha+(f/2)\beta\le0$ for $r\le2M$. On a collar of $2M$, put $\tau=v-r$; for nonzero future causal $Z$, $d\tau(Z)=\alpha+(1-f/2)\beta\ge\alpha+\beta/2>0$ after shrinking the collar so $|f|<1$. Smoothness and $f(2M)=0$ give $C<\infty$ with $f(r)\le C|r-2M|$ there, hence $dr(Z)\le C|r-2M|d\tau(Z)$. On each $C^1$ path piece in the collar we may use $\tau$ as parameter. If $r<2M$, then $w=2M-r$ obeys $dw/d\tau\ge-Cw$, so $d(e^{C\tau}w)/d\tau\ge0$ and $w(\tau)\ge w(\tau_0)e^{-C(\tau-\tau_0)}>0$ for every finite $\tau$; a path starting below the root cannot reach it. If a path starts at the root, $u=(r-2M)_+$ obeys $du/d\tau\le Cu$ almost everywhere on the collar: for $r>2M$ this is the displayed cone bound, for $r<2M$ its derivative is zero, and on the root the cone bound gives $dr/d\tau\le0$. Thus $d(e^{-C\tau}u)/d\tau\le0$ and $u(\tau_0)=0$ forces $u=0$, so no outward excursion starts at the root. The same estimates apply across finitely many piece joins by continuity. Every point with $r>2M$ escapes along the future null field $n$, because $dr/dv=f/2>0$ and $f(r)\to1$. Thus the uncharged escape boundary is $r=2M$. [F1, F4, step 1.1, algebra]
