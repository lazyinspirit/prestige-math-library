## Definition

Fix quiver-with-potential data $(Q,W)$, with the finite quiver and Jacobian algebra conventions stated earlier on this page. Put $N=\mathbb Z^{Q_0}$, $N^\oplus=\mathbb N^{Q_0}$, $N^+=N^\oplus\setminus\{0\}$, $M=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(N,\mathbb Z)$, and $M_\mathbb R=M\otimes_\mathbb Z\mathbb R$. For $d\in N^\oplus$, let $\operatorname{Rep}_d(J)$ be the finite-type scheme of collections of matrices for the arrows satisfying the Jacobian relations and having dimension vector $d$; its representation stack is $\mathfrak M_d=[\operatorname{Rep}_d(J)/\prod_{i\in Q_0}\operatorname{GL}_{d_i}(\mathbb C)]$. Let $\mathfrak M_d^{ss}(\theta)$ be the substack whose geometric points are the $\theta$-semistable representations under the King convention fixed earlier on this page.

Let $e_i$ be the standard basis of $N$. The source skew-symmetric form is the bilinear form determined by $\langle e_i,e_j\rangle=\#\{a:j\to i\}-\#\{a:i\to j\}$, and the associated Poisson bracket on the monoid algebra is $\{x^{d_1},x^{d_2}\}=\langle d_1,d_2\rangle x^{d_1+d_2}$.

For a stack $M$ locally of finite type over $\mathbb C$ with affine stabilizers, let $K(\mathrm{St}/M)$ be the abelian group generated by isomorphism classes $[X\to M]$ of finite-type Artin stacks with affine stabilizers, modulo the closed-substack/open-complement relation and the relation identifying Zariski locally trivial fibrations with the same fibers. It is a module over $K(\mathrm{St}/\mathbb C)$. The source homomorphism $\Upsilon:K(\mathrm{St}/\mathbb C)\to\mathbb C(t)$ sends a smooth projective complex variety $Y$ to its Poincar\'e polynomial $\sum_i\dim_\mathbb C H^i(Y^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb C)t^i$. Set $K_\Upsilon(\mathrm{St}/M)=K(\mathrm{St}/M)\otimes_{K(\mathrm{St}/\mathbb C)}\mathbb C(t)$, with the additional relation $[Y\times X\to M]=\Upsilon([Y])[X\to M]$.

The source motivic Hall algebra $H(Q,W)$ is the graded $\mathbb C(t)$-algebra with degree-$d$ part $K_\Upsilon(\mathrm{St}/\mathfrak M_d)$ and convolution product along the stack of short exact sequences $0\to E_1\to E\to E_2\to0$. Its degree completion is the inverse limit over quotients retaining only vectors $d$ with $\delta(d):=\sum_{i\in Q_0}d_i\le r$, as $r\to\infty$. The source regular coefficient ring is
$$
\mathbb C_{\mathrm{reg}}(t)=\mathbb C[t,t^{-1}][(1+t^2+\cdots+t^{2k})^{-1}:k\ge1],
$$
and $H_{\mathrm{reg}}(Q,W)$ is the $\mathbb C_{\mathrm{reg}}(t)$-subalgebra generated by classes $[X\to\mathfrak M_d]$ with $X$ a variety. Its source Poisson bracket is $\{a,b\}_{\mathrm{Hall}}=(t^2-1)^{-1}[a,b]$, where $[a,b]=a*b-b*a$ is the Hall commutator. The positive-degree completion $\widehat{\mathfrak g}_{\mathrm{Hall}}=\widehat H(Q,W)_{>0}$ is a pronilpotent Lie algebra under the commutator; its pro-unipotent group is $\widehat G_{\mathrm{Hall}}=1+\widehat H(Q,W)_{>0}$.

A scattering diagram with values in the completed Hall Lie group consists of rational codimension-one walls in $M_\mathbb R$, each contained in $n^\perp=\{\theta:\theta(n)=0\}$ for a primitive $n\in N^+$, and carrying an element of $\exp(\prod_{k\ge1}\widehat H(Q,W)_{kn})$. The degree completion means that only finitely many walls of degree at most $r$ meet a compact set, for each finite truncation degree $r$. For a generic piecewise-smooth path transverse to walls and disjoint from joints, its path-ordered product is the ordered product of the wall labels in crossing order, using the inverse label when the path crosses a wall in the reverse orientation. The diagram is consistent when every generic closed path has identity path-ordered product modulo every finite degree truncation. Equivalence means equality of all such generic path products.

For a weight $\theta$, let $\mathbf 1_d^{ss}(\theta)=[\mathfrak M_d^{ss}(\theta)\to\mathfrak M_d]$ and $\mathbf 1^{ss}(\theta)=\sum_{d\in N^\oplus}\mathbf 1_d^{ss}(\theta)$ in the degree completion, with the zero-dimensional term equal to the Hall unit. It has the source logarithmic factorization
$$
\mathbf 1^{ss}(\theta)=\exp\left((t^2-1)^{-1}\sum_{d\in N^+}\epsilon(d,\theta)\right),
$$
where each $\epsilon(d,\theta)\in H_{\mathrm{reg}}(Q,W)_d$ is the degree-$d$ term. Terms with no $\theta$-semistable representations are zero. For a finite $2$-acyclic quiver with finite potential, the source integration homomorphism is defined with the Euler specialization $\operatorname{ev}_{-1}:\mathbb C_{\mathrm{reg}}(t)\to\mathbb C$, $t\mapsto-1$. This map is well-defined because every inverted polynomial $1+t^2+\cdots+t^{2k}$ takes the nonzero value $k+1$ at $-1$. Moreover, the stated positive-Betti Poincar\'e polynomial satisfies $\Upsilon(Y)|_{t=-1}=\sum_i(-1)^i\dim H^i(Y^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb C)=e(Y)$ for smooth projective $Y$, so this specialization respects the coefficient relation. After this specialization the integration map $I:H_{\mathrm{reg}}(Q,W)\to\mathbb C[N^\oplus]$ sends $[X\to\mathfrak M_d]$ to $e(X)x^d$, where $e(X)$ is the topological Euler characteristic; for a finite-type complex variety it agrees with compactly supported Euler characteristic. Theorem 11.1 and §11.3 of the cited source establish that, under these potential and $2$-acyclic hypotheses, this is a Poisson homomorphism for the bracket above (the source uses a coefficient parameter sent to $1$, whereas the present positive-Betti Poincar\'e parameter is sent to $-1$). Thus $J(d,\theta):=e(\epsilon(d,\theta))$ is the source Joyce invariant and the integrated wall element at $\theta$ is $\exp(\sum_{d\in N^+:\theta(d)=0}J(d,\theta)x^d)$. This normalization is not an arbitrary numerical or vanishing-cycle DT invariant. This is a definition of data and consistency; it does not assert that a consistent diagram exists.
