## Statement

Let k be a characteristic-zero field and use the connective simplicial derived-affine model and its stated choice convention. For every connective derived commutative k-algebra A let C_A be the k-linear stable infinity-category of dualizable derived A-modules and let G_A be its entire maximal infinity-groupoid, so that Perf_k(A)=G_A. There is a canonical natural map of spaces
$$\operatorname{ch}_A:G_A\longrightarrow\left|\mathrm{NC}(\mathrm{HH}^{\mathrm{der}}(A/k))\right|,$$
compatible with arbitrary derived base changes, algebra-map homotopies, equivalences of perfect modules, retracts and all higher simplices. Its natural mixed-HKR weight-two image defines a global closed degree-two two-form on Perf_k using the homotopy limit over all derived affine maps into that stack. On every derived Artin locally finite-presentation open substack, at every A-point E, its tangent complex is canonically RHom_A(E,E)[1] and the underlying form is exactly one half of the graded trace-of-composition pairing; its adjoint is an equivalence. For any perfect family P on a derived affine or a derived base S with cotangent complex, its pullback character has underlying weight-two form one half of Tr(At_(S/k)(P)^2); the absolute-to-relative cotangent projection gives the same identity with the relative Atiyah coaction after every base morphism. This family identity requires no separate representability or cotangent hypothesis on Perf_k. In particular these restrictions form a compatible two-shifted symplectic structure. The construction requires no restriction to classical bases, locally free modules or degree-zero endomorphisms.

1.1 Work in the localization of the actual simplicial commutative k-algebra model, with k a characteristic-zero field. For A in that category let C_A be the k-linear stable infinity-category of dualizable derived A-modules and let G_A=C_A^equiv be its entire maximal infinity-groupoid. Thus G_A retains the actual derived mapping spaces, not merely isomorphisms in H0. Derived extension of scalars gives an infinity-functor A↦C_A. For example, the associativity comparisons for B⊗_A^L E are those of the derived tensor functor; they are not chosen independently for successive algebra maps. This can be modeled by functorial cofibrant replacements and two-sided simplicial bars. A simplicial homotopy of algebra maps gives the corresponding homotopy of those bars. No replacement of the simplicial commutative algebra by an unproved strict commutative dg model is required. For a k-linear infinity-category C define its Hochschild object by the derived cyclic bar. An n-word has objects E0,...,En and factors `C(E0,E1) ⊗k C(E1,E2) ⊗k ... ⊗k C(En,E0).` Take the homotopy sum over the objects, with its identifications along equivalences. Faces compose adjacent factors, including the last-to-first cyclic face; degeneracies insert identities; the cyclic operator rotates the factors with the Koszul symmetry. The realization is HH(C/k), as an object with an actual S1 action. On an enriched cofibrant model these are the ordinary derived cyclic-bar faces. On changing enriched models use the functorial bar resolution before realization; its augmentation has the identity-insertion extra degeneracy, so changes of such resolutions induce equivalences. This description includes the higher composition simplices of C: it is a cyclic diagram in complexes, not a choice of trace for each object. [F1, F2, F3, construct]

2.1 There is a particularly economical map into this cyclic object. Let k[G] denote free k-linearization of an infinity-groupoid G: its mapping object is the free k-module on its mapping space and composition is induced by path composition. Concretely the free module is the normalized free simplicial k-module on a Kan mapping-space model, stabilized to allow unbounded mapping complexes. The shuffle map implements products; its associativity follows by identifying the two iterated shuffles with the same three-block shuffles. Equivalently it is the strong monoidal functor Hk⊗Sigma-infinity-plus from spaces to k-module spectra. This defines the linearization on every higher composition simplex. The inclusion G_A→C_A therefore has an evaluation extension `j_A : k[G_A] → C_A.` At a mapping space this is the free-linear extension of the actual map into the underlying space of the mapping complex. At a string of composable maps it is that same extension of their composition; the higher equalities are the original composition simplices. Thus j_A is a homotopy-coherent k-linear functor, rather than a degree-zero assignment which would require later unspecified coherence corrections. The construction is the following composite in S1-equivariant spaces: `G_A with trivial action; → L G_A; → underlying-space HH(k[G_A]/k); → underlying-space HH(C_A/k); ≃ underlying-space HH(A/k).` The first map is the literal constant-loop map. The third map is induced by j_A. The second and fourth arrows, including the precise meaning of the second arrow's linearization, are justified next. Adjunction from trivial S1 action then gives `ch_A : G_A → underlying-space(HH(A/k)^(hS1)).` The entire composite is natural in the derived algebra A. It has not replaced G_A by its set of components. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Here the second arrow means the unit map from a space into its free k-module: there is an S1-equivariant equivalence `HH(k[G]/k) ≃ C_*(L G;k).` To construct it, first use a fibrant simplicially enriched groupoid model for G; its morphisms are invertible up to coherent homotopy. The cyclic bar before linearization consists of oriented necklaces of equivalences, with their mapping-space simplices. A necklace specifies a loop in G by traversing its edges. A face composes two neighboring edges and a degeneracy subdivides by an identity. Rotation moves the marked vertex around the same loop. Consequently realization gives an S1-equivariant map from that cyclic bar to LG. It is an equivalence, including for spaces with higher homotopy. One can check this on a compact family of loops. Subdivide the source circle and the parameter simplex sufficiently finely to represent the family in a simplicial nerve of the groupoid. This gives a family of necklaces and its coherent edge homotopies. For a fixed family the category of such subdivisions and refinements is contractible: any finite collection of subdivisions has their union as a common refinement, and the same assertion applies relative to the boundary of a parameter simplex. The simplicial homotopies between choices are precisely the mapping-space simplices already kept in the necklace bar. This proves surjectivity and injectivity on every relative homotopy group, by applying the same subdivision construction to a sphere and to a filling disk. It proves a weak equivalence, not only a bijection on components. Traversing and subdividing commute with moving the marked point; thus the weak equivalence is one in S1-spaces. No assertion that a discrete nerve suffices for a derived mapping space is made. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Free k-linearization commutes with the homotopy sums and realizations in this bar and carries a finite product of mapping spaces to the derived tensor product of their free modules. The latter is implemented by the shuffle map described in the cyclic-core construction; the inverse comparison is Alexander–Whitney, whose two composites differ from identity by the simplicial prism homotopy. The same formulas apply with parameters and cyclic rotation. Therefore the linearized cyclic bar is C_*(LG;k). Inverting its equivalence in the infinity-category of S1-spaces, followed by the unit LG→underlying-space C_*(LG;k), gives the arrow used in the cyclic-core construction. The space of choices of an inverse to a specified equivalence is contractible by its defining inverse identities. This is inversion of an already constructed cyclic equivalence; it is not an appeal to contractibility of possible trace operations. [F1, F2, F3, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 The one-object k-linear category with endomorphisms A maps canonically into C_A by selecting the unit module A. Denote it by i_A. It induces an actual map of cyclic bars `HH(i_A):HH(A/k) → HH(C_A/k).` It is important to prove this particular map an equivalence, not merely to exhibit unrelated isomorphic Hochschild groups. Here is the two-sided-bar proof. Set U(E)=RHom_A(A,E)=E and V(E)=RHom_A(E,A)=E^vee. They are the Yoneda bimodules between A and C_A. Dualizability gives a natural equivalence `V(E) ⊗_A^L U(F) → RHom_A(E,F).` It sends a rank-one tensor to the corresponding endomorphism and is an equivalence because E is dualizable. It applies to every perfect E, including retracts; it chooses neither a basis nor a splitting. In the reverse direction the derived coend has the augmentation `∫^(E in C_A) RHom_A(A,E) ⊗k RHom_A(E,A) → RHom_A(A,A)=A.` Use its ordinary two-sided enriched bar. Its augmentation is a quasi-isomorphism by co-Yoneda: adjoining the representable endpoint A and its identity gives an extra degeneracy. In normalized chains the alternating identities give dh+hd=1−unit·augmentation. These are identities of the augmented bar with its module parameters, so they also prove naturality under algebra maps and under equivalences and retracts of E. They do not move a projective coevaluation between different absolute Hochschild tensor slots. Insert the first bimodule equivalence into the cyclic coend for HH(C_A). Expand each of its derived tensors by its two-sided bar. Reassociate the resulting iterated homotopy sums: the words have alternating Yoneda factors and category-composition factors. Reading the same word with its marked cut at an A factor instead of a C_A factor identifies this realization with the Hochschild coend of the reverse bimodule. This is ordinary bar Fubini; in a chain model it is the bijection of the same bisimplicial tensor words, with the same Koszul permutation. Augment the reverse bimodule using the preceding contraction. The result is a map HH(C_A)→HH(A). Composing it with HH(i_A) inserts the endpoint A in the same augmented bar; the extra-degeneracy contraction makes the composite identity. In the other order use V(E)⊗_A U(F)≃RHom(E,F), followed by the same endpoint contraction. Thus the inverse just described is inverse to HH(i_A), and HH(i_A) is an underlying equivalence.
