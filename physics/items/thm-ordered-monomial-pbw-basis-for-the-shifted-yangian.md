## Statement

For any fixed order on the family $D_i^{(r)}$ ($r\ge1$),
$E_{i,j}^{(r)}$ ($i<j$, $r>s_{i,j}$), and $F_{j,i}^{(r)}$ ($i<j$, $r>s_{j,i}$),
with each root mode included once, their ordered monomials form a $\mathbb C$-basis of $Y_n(\sigma)$. Its canonical associated graded algebra is the polynomial
algebra on the corresponding symbols, while its loop associated graded algebra
is the universal enveloping algebra of the current algebra
$\mathfrak{gl}_n[t](\sigma)$; the two graded algebras are different and
non-isomorphic in general. In the finite quotient $W(\lambda)$ the ordered
monomials in the finite Gauss range
$$s_{i,j}<r\le s_{i,j}+p_{\min(i,j)}$$
form a basis. The Kazhdan/canonical associated graded is commutative,
whereas the loop associated graded is noncommutative when $\mathfrak g^e$
is nonabelian and commutative when $\mathfrak g^e$ is abelian. These
degree assignments are $r$ and $r-1$, respectively; no unconditional
noncommutativity or non-isomorphism is asserted. Equivalently, the finite
$T$-coefficients with $s_{i,j}<r\le S_{i,j}$ form an ordered PBW basis, where
$r$ is the unshifted coefficient index of $T=FDE$, not a reindexed value.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A shift matrix $\sigma$ attached to a pyramid with row lengths $0\le p_1\le\cdots\le p_n$, the shifted Yangian $Y_n(\sigma)$ with its generators and filtrations of [[def-shifted-yangian-and-pyramid-generators-for-the-type-a-finite-w-algebra]], and the quotient $W(\lambda)=Y_n(\sigma)/(D_1^{(r)},r>p_1)$.

[F1] The RTT/Drinfeld presentation, the Gauss decomposition $T=FDE$, the two filtrations and the truncation are as in [[def-shifted-yangian-and-pyramid-generators-for-the-type-a-finite-w-algebra]]; the candidate finite index set to be established below is $s_{i,j}<r\le S_{i,j}$ with $S_{i,j}=s_{i,j}+p_{\min(i,j)}$.

[F2] Ordered monomial PBW for enveloping algebras of finite-dimensional Lie algebras: an ordered monomial basis holds for $U(\mathfrak a)$ when $\mathfrak a$ is finite-dimensional ([[thm-pbw-ordered-monomial-basis-for-the-enveloping-algebra]]).

[F3] The local Gan–Ginzburg transverse-product and Chevalley–Eilenberg lifting argument proves that the Kazhdan associated graded of the entire quantum Hamiltonian reduction is the polynomial coordinate algebra of the transverse slice. Its homogeneous coordinate degrees are $s_{i,j}+1,\ldots,s_{i,j}+\min(p_i,p_j)$ for each row pair; the doubled convention gives twice these degrees ([[lem-gan-ginzburg-skryabin-inputs-hold-for-the-type-a-pyramid-good-grading]], [[def-associated-graded-algebra-of-a-filtered-algebra]]).




## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** The shifted Yangian generators and the finite quotient $W(\lambda)$.

1.1 **RTT spanning and the actual coproduct.** In the unshifted RTT algebra the commutator of modes $r,s$ has canonical degree at most $r+s-1$, so induction on total degree and the number of unordered pairs rewrites every word into ordered monomials. Matrix-unit brackets verify RTT for $L_a(u)=I+E^{(a)}/u$ in the $a$th factor of $U(\mathfrak{gl}_n)^{\otimes l}$. Multiplication of RTT matrices with commuting coefficient algebras preserves RTT: moving the two middle commuting tensor factors past each other allows the first RTT identity, then the second. Thus the iterated evaluation map is $T(u)\mapsto L_1(u)\cdots L_l(u)$. More generally shifted evaluation points give the ordered product $\prod_{a=1}^l(I+E^{(a)}/(u-c_a))$, in increasing factor order. Its $u^{-r}$ coefficient when all $c_a=0$ is $\sum_{a_1<\cdots<a_r}E^{(a_1)}\cdots E^{(a_r)}$, homogeneous of enveloping PBW degree $r$. [F1, F2, algebra, construct]

1.2 We first prove invariance of the actual path generators for both column arrangements, independently of generation. For a box a write c(a) for its column, $\rho_c=n-\sum_{d\ge c}q_d$ and $\widetilde E_{ab}=(-1)^{c(b)-c(a)}(E_{ab}+\delta_{ab}\rho_{c(a)})$. The negative PBW projection $\operatorname{pr}:U(\mathfrak g)=U(\mathfrak p)U(\mathfrak m)\to U(\mathfrak p)$ applies χ to the right factor. Its elementary rules are $\operatorname{pr}(ZB)=\chi(Z)B+\operatorname{pr}([Z,B])$ and $\operatorname{pr}(BZ)=B\chi(Z)$ for a negative matrix unit Z and a p-factor B. The bracket is $[\widetilde E_{ab},\widetilde E_{hk}]=\delta_{bh}(\widetilde E_{ak}-\delta_{ak}\rho_{c(a)})-\delta_{ak}(\widetilde E_{hb}-\delta_{hb}\rho_{c(b)})$, and $\chi(\widetilde E_{ab})=-\delta_{\operatorname{row}(a),\operatorname{row}(b)}$ at adjacent negative columns, zero at greater distance. Define $P_{ij;x}^{(r)}$ as the sum of products $E_{a_1b_1}\cdots E_{a_sb_s}$ with total degree $\sum(c(b_t)-c(a_t)+1)=r$, p-factors, shared intervening rows, starting/ending rows i,j, strict forward joins when the intervening row exceeds x and weak reverse joins otherwise, and sign $(-1)^{r-s}$ times one minus for every intervening row at most x. Its $\eta_{\rm tail}$ image replaces each factor by $E_{ab}+\delta_{ab}\rho_{c(a)}$. Equivalently, in the $\widetilde E$ factors its only external sign is one minus for every intervening row at most x, since their product already contributes $(-1)^{r-s}$. Invariance means $\operatorname{pr}([z,\eta_{\rm tail}(P)])=0$. If an element B is invariant, then $(z-\chi(z))B=B(z-\chi(z))+[z,B]$ proves $U(\mathfrak g)I_\chi B\subseteq U(\mathfrak g)I_\chi$; hence invariant elements form a subalgebra, and products, commutators and coefficients of inverse series with identity constant are invariant. [F1, F2, algebra]

1.3 The local Gan–Ginzburg supplier is stated for the original left-justified diagram; we derive the reflected version by an actual graded Lie isomorphism. Let f reflect boxes, $\tau(E_{ab})=E'_{f(b),f(a)}$ reverse products, and let S on the reflected space be diagonal with $S_{bb}=(-1)^{\operatorname{col}'(b)}$. The linear map $\phi(x)=S(-\tau(x))S^{-1}$ is a Lie isomorphism, because $[\tau(x),\tau(y)]=-\tau([x,y])$. It preserves grading degrees, p and m, and the trace pairing. The diagonal-conjugation sign on every positive adjacent arrow is −1, so $\phi(e)=e'$ and $\chi'\phi=\chi$. It extends to an ALGEBRA isomorphism of enveloping algebras and takes $U(\mathfrak g)I_\chi$ to $U(\mathfrak g')I_{\chi'}$. Thus it takes the entire untwisted left reduction $A=\eta_{\rm tail}(W)$ to $A'$: each defining commutator condition is equivalent under φ. The chart map is the explicit algebra isomorphism $\beta=\eta_{\rm tail}'^{-1}\phi\eta_{\rm tail}:W\to W'$. Scalar translations have smaller positive Kazhdan degree, so $\operatorname{gr}\beta=\operatorname{gr}\phi$. The doubled good grading, sl2 triple, trace-orthogonal slice and transverse product transport under φ. Consequently the proved original polynomial-slice and cohomological statements imply the reflected statements in its actual grading, including the homogeneous coordinate weights. The polynomial-slice part used here is choice-free; for consumers assuming AC as in the cohomological supplier, its left Skryabin quotient, local-nilpotence category, invariant space and equivalence maps also transport first through φ and then through β. This uses the original proved local supplier without enlarging its stated domain, and does not assert that a transpose subalgebra is the whole reflected reduction or that two chart twists act equally on a fixed module. [F1, F2, F3, construct, algebra]

1.4 **Index and inverse sign in the abstract quotient.** Write $A_i(u)=D_i(u)^{-1}=\sum_{t\ge0}A_i^{(t)}u^{-t}$, so $A_i^{(0)}=1$, while the defining relation uses $\widetilde D_i(u)=-A_i(u)$ with constant $-1$. Thus $[E_i^{(a)},F_i^{(b)}]=-D_{i+1}^{(a+b-1)}-\sum_{t=1}^{a+b-1}A_i^{(t)}D_{i+1}^{(a+b-1-t)}$. Put $i=c-1$, $u=s_{c-1,c}$, $v=s_{c,c-1}$, $a=r-v$, $b=v+1$. Then $a+b-1=r$ and $D_c^{(r)}=-\sum_{t=1}^rA_{c-1}^{(t)}D_c^{(r-t)}-[E_{c-1}^{(r-v)},F_{c-1}^{(v+1)}]$. We use this only for $r>p_c=p_{c-1}+u+v$, so $a>p_{c-1}+u$ is an allowed but HIGH E mode, to be eliminated before the diagonal formula is used. Also $A_i^{(r)}=-\sum_{t=1}^rD_i^{(t)}A_i^{(r-t)}$, giving polynomial inverse coefficients with the same canonical degree bound. This derives the sign and indices, rather than treating a negative inverse as a positive one. [F1, algebra]

2.1 **Product Jacobian and independence.** Let $l$ be at least the largest coefficient index in a proposed relation. In the commutative symbol algebra of $U(\mathfrak{gl}_n)^{\otimes l}$ write $P_r(X_1,\ldots,X_l)=\sum_{a_1<\cdots<a_r}X_{a_1}\cdots X_{a_r}$. At $X_a=h_aI$, with nonzero pairwise distinct $h_a$, the differential on every matrix entry is $dP_r=\sum_a e_{r-1}(h_1,\ldots,\widehat{h_a},\ldots,h_l)dX_a$. The column coefficient polynomials $\prod_{b\ne a}(1+h_bz)$ are linearly independent, since evaluating at $z=-1/h_c$ leaves only column $c$, with a nonzero value. Hence the Jacobian is invertible. A polynomial relation of minimal total degree, differentiated and multiplied by the inverse Jacobian over the rational function field, makes each of its partial derivatives a lower-degree relation; minimality and characteristic zero force the relation to be constant and zero. Thus the coordinates $P_{r,ij}$, $1\le r\le l$, are algebraically independent. A highest weighted-degree part of an enveloping relation among their ordered lifts would contradict this polynomial independence by [F2]. This proves RTT PBW for all modes. The $h_a$ are scalar-matrix points in the symbol space, independently of spectral evaluation parameters. [F2, step 1.1, algebra, construct]

2.2 Group equal row lengths into the minimal admissible parabolic blocks ν, with final nonempty block m of size t. D/E/F below are its actual Gauss path coefficients. Deleting the last column embeds the smaller shifted matrix units into the current ones; on unshifted diagonal units this compensates the rho difference by a scalar translation, and on negative units it preserves χ. A dot denotes the smaller coefficients. With final column A, preceding column B, $L_{kj}=\widetilde E_{(A,k),(A,j)}$ and $C_{hj}=\widetilde E_{(B,h),(A,j)}$, the right end recursions, for any fixed bottom row h, are $D_{a;ij}^{(r)}=\dot D_{a;ij}^{(r)}+\delta_{a,m}(\sum_k\dot D_{a;ik}^{(r-1)}L_{kj}+[\dot D_{a;ih}^{(r-1)},C_{hj}])$, $E_{a;ij}^{(r)}=\dot E_{a;ij}^{(r)}+\delta_{a,m-1}(\sum_k\dot E_{a;ik}^{(r-1)}L_{kj}+[\dot E_{a;ih}^{(r-1)},C_{hj}])$, and $F_{a;ij}^{(r)}=\dot F_{a;ij}^{(r)}$. The E boundary formula is used for r>1. Deleting the FIRST column of the reflected arrangement has the ordinary embedding on the remaining shifted factors. With first column A, next B, $L_{ik}=\widetilde E_{(A,i),(A,k)}$ and $C_{ih}=\widetilde E_{(A,i),(B,h)}$, its LEFT recursions are $D_{a;ij}^{(r)}=\dot D_{a;ij}^{(r)}+\delta_{a,m}(\sum_kL_{ik}\dot D_{a;kj}^{(r-1)}+[C_{ih},\dot D_{a;hj}^{(r-1)}])$, $E_{a;ij}^{(r)}=\dot E_{a;ij}^{(r)}$, and $F_{a;ij}^{(r)}=\dot F_{a;ij}^{(r)}+\delta_{a,m-1}(\sum_kL_{ik}\dot F_{a;kj}^{(r-1)}+[C_{ih},\dot F_{a;hj}^{(r-1)}])$, with r>1 in the F boundary formula. To verify these identities, cut each path at its final or initial visit to the added column. Paths missing that column give the dotted coefficient; paths ending or starting in its block give the product sum. For a crossing path the two neighboring cut positions at the common bottom row h have opposite signs and give the two orders in the commutator; further visits pair by moving to the neighboring cut. The identical cut rule in a changed row interval proves the Gauss identities: paths without an intermediate visit occur once; paths with such a visit have the two neighboring cuts with opposite sign. Thus these are identities for the actual Gauss coefficients. In particular LEFT deletion corrects F and leaves E unchanged; it does not use the interchanged E/F display in the source. [F1, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

2.3 **Finite spanning: earlier blocks and boundary E/F.** In the abstract quotient by $D_1^{(r)}$, $r>p_1$, let $\Omega_c$ contain D modes $1\le a\le c$, $1\le r\le p_a$, E roots $a<b\le c$, $s_{ab}<r\le s_{ab}+p_a$, and F roots $a<b\le c$, $s_{ba}<r\le s_{ba}+p_a$; let $B_c$ be their algebra. Induct on c to show every allowed mode with endpoints at most c belongs to $B_c$ with total canonical degree at most its index. The case c=1 is the imposed truncation, including $p_1=0$. For the inductive step put $u=s_{c-1,c}$ and $v=s_{c,c-1}$. For $r>u+p_{c-1}$, the D–E relation gives $E_{c-1}^{(r)}=[D_{c-1}^{(r-u)},E_{c-1}^{(u+1)}]-\sum_{t=1}^{r-u-1}D_{c-1}^{(t)}E_{c-1}^{(r-t)}$. Replace every earlier D by its block-inductive finite expression and expand its commutator by Leibniz. The bracket of an earlier finite diagonal mode of index a with the lowest boundary E gives terms $D^{(t)}E_{c-1}^{(a+u-t)}$ with $a\le p_{c-1}$ in the only nonzero diagonal block, hence new E indices at most $u+p_{c-1}$. An earlier finite root $E_{b,c-1}^{(a)}$ gives $E_{b,c}^{(a+u)}$ and any block-refinement split terms, all with $a+u\le s_{b,c-1}+p_b+u=s_{b,c}+p_b$. Earlier negative roots and earlier positive roots without endpoint $c-1$ commute with the lowest boundary E. Thus the expanded commutator is in $B_c$. In the sum $u+1\le r-t<r$, so induction on r reduces those E modes; all products have degree at most r. The reversed D–F relation and reversed product orders give the same argument with boundary bound $v+p_{c-1}$. [F1, step 1.4, algebra]

3.1 **Loop symbols and shifted PBW.** Give an RTT mode of index $r$ loop degree $r-1$. In its commutator relation the term with a zero-index factor is the current matrix-unit bracket; every term with two positive-index factors has smaller loop degree. Thus the straightening in step 1.1 preserves loop degree, and the RTT basis of step 2.1 identifies each loop-filtered space with the span of its ordered monomials of bounded loop degree. Their symbols are therefore a basis, and their brackets are $[e_{ij}t^a,e_{hk}t^b]=\delta_{jh}e_{ik}t^{a+b}-\delta_{ki}e_{hj}t^{a+b}$. This identifies the loop graded algebra with $U(\mathfrak{gl}_n[t])$. The required current-algebra PBW uses only [F2]: any finite relation among ordered current monomials maps to a jet quotient $\mathfrak{gl}_n[t]/t^M\mathfrak{gl}_n[t]$ with $M$ larger than every exponent in it, where the relevant modes are distinct basis vectors and their ordered monomials are independent. Coefficientwise Gauss inversion changes an RTT mode of index $r$ only by products of at least two positive-index modes with total index $r$, whose loop degree is at most $r-2$. Hence the unshifted Gauss modes have these same current symbols. Sending each simple shifted Drinfeld generator to its unshifted namesake preserves the defining relations and is loop-filtered. For higher roots use their actual shifted commutator recursions: the upper recursion brackets $e_{i,j-1}t^{r-s_{j-1,j}-1}$ with $e_{j-1,j}t^{s_{j-1,j}}$, giving $e_{ij}t^{r-1}$; the reversed lower recursion gives $e_{ji}t^{r-1}$ in the same way. Thus the images of all allowed shifted root modes have the distinct current symbols of the unshifted loop algebra just established. Their ordered products are independent there by the jet-PBW argument, so a relation among their lifts, taken in its highest loop degree, is impossible. The shifted coefficient relations, adjacent-root recursions and Serre relations straighten words into these ordered root monomials, with commutator corrections of total canonical index at most $r+s-1$ for modes of indices $r,s$; induction on canonical degree and unordered pairs terminates the straightening. It preserves the canonical bound and the loop bound. Independence now proves both the embedding and the filtered ordered basis. Consequently the intrinsic canonical associated graded is the polynomial algebra on the allowed shifted root symbols. This does not identify those canonical symbols with the unshifted Gauss symbols: for $s_{12}=0,s_{23}=1$, the adjacent relation gives $E_{13}^{(2)}(\sigma)=E_{13}^{(2)}(0)-E_{12}^{(1)}E_{23}^{(1)}$, whose correction has canonical degree two but loop degree zero. The independence argument uses loop degree one in this example. No independence after truncation is inferred here. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 Induct on the number of columns, deleting the last column in the original left-justified diagram and the first in the reflected diagram. A one-column diagram has no negative algebra, so every mode is invariant. Empty rows have only formal constants and zero positive coefficients. All earlier D blocks, all nonboundary E/F blocks, and F in right deletion or E in left deletion are unchanged by step 2.2. Their path monomials have no forbidden preceding-bottom target in right deletion, or following-bottom source in left deletion; commuting a new negative into such a monomial produces a negative unit of greater column distance with zero χ, and moving it to the right keeps this absent endpoint. Thus their projected brackets vanish. Induction supplies old-negative invariance of every dotted coefficient. The old negative algebra commutes with L and C in step 2.2, so its character ideal is preserved by those factors, proving old-negative invariance of the changed coefficients. The complete negative Lie algebra is generated by the old one and the new adjacent-column matrices: brackets through a row present in intervening bottom rows produce each longer negative matrix. Jacobi and the normalizer identity in step 1.2 propagate invariance from these generators. Only the new adjacent matrices need be checked below. [step 1.2, step 2.2, algebra]

3.3 **Higher roots and the final diagonal elimination.** For $b<c-1$, the defining root recursions are $E_{b,c}^{(r)}=[E_{b,c-1}^{(r-u)},E_{c-1}^{(u+1)}]$ and $F_{c,b}^{(r)}=[F_{c-1}^{(v+1)},F_{c-1,b}^{(r-v)}]$. Reduce the earlier endpoint factors by the c−1 hypothesis; the same Leibniz bounds in step 2.3 place every new root in $B_c$, including high modes, using the finite allowed new roots in $\Omega_c$. Now use step 1.4 for $D_c^{(r)}$, $r>p_c$. Every inverse earlier coefficient is in $B_{c-1}$ and every $D_c^{(r-t)}$ has smaller index, so induction on r treats its sum. First replace the upper $E_{c-1}^{(r-v)}$ by the finite expression just constructed, then expand the bracket with the lowest boundary F. Earlier D/F factors give bounded new F roots and earlier factors; earlier E factors commute with that boundary F. A finite new E root starting at c−1 has index $a\le u+p_{c-1}$, so its E–F convolution has only $D_c$ indices at most $a+v\le p_c$. A new E root starting at b<c−1 gives earlier-endpoint roots and earlier factors, reduced by the block hypothesis, and cannot create a high $D_c$ mode. Thus this commutator uses only $D_c$ indices at most $p_c$, and the induction closes with the asserted degree control. In particular higher diagonal modes are polynomial expressions, not zero modes. [F1, step 1.4, step 2.3, algebra]

4.1 The changed D^(1) is a sum of the row-pair diagonal-column blocks; its two adjacent-column projected commutators cancel. Let the neighboring column have height u≥t, with b=u−t shorter rows and cutoff x=n−t. In RIGHT deletion put $z=\widetilde E_{(A,f),(B,g)}$, f bottom and g arbitrary. In D^(2), and in boundary E^(2) when the shift is1, the single bridge contributes scalar $-u\delta_{gi}\delta_{fj}$. The bottom plus paths $\sum_kB_{ik}A_{kj}$ contribute $+t\delta_{gi}\delta_{fj}$ and matrix terms $-\delta_{gi}A_{fj}+\delta_{fj}B_{ig}$ for g bottom. The shorter minus paths in B contribute $+b\delta_{gi}\delta_{fj}$ and $+\delta_{fj}B_{ig}$ for g shorter. Moving their negative factor through the following block yields one additional χ correction for each shorter row; this is the scalar b term. The single bridge gives the negative of all the matrix terms, and $-u+t+b=0$. In LEFT deletion put $z=\widetilde E_{(B,f),(A,g)}$, f arbitrary and g bottom. The bridge scalar is $-t\delta_{gi}\delta_{fj}$; bottom plus paths $\sum_kA_{ik}B_{kj}$ give $+t\delta_{gi}\delta_{fj}$ and $-\delta_{gi}B_{fj}+\delta_{fj}A_{ig}$ for f bottom. Shorter minus paths $-\sum_hB_{ih}B_{hj}$ give $-\delta_{gi}B_{fj}$ for f shorter and NO scalar: the extra negative χ involves a shorter row h and bottom row g. The bridge has the opposite matrix terms. Therefore $-t+t=0$ and all matrices cancel, for D^(2) and boundary F^(2). Paths outside these adjacent columns have zero projection by the support argument in step 3.2. This proves the exact jump-one base cases in both actual LEFT reductions. [step 1.2, step 2.2, step 3.2, algebra]

4.2 For a larger boundary shift, or a rectangle, the first two end columns have equal height t. A double dot deletes both and $K_{s;ij}=\ddot D_{m;ij}^{(s)}$; replacing K with the corresponding boundary $\ddot E$ in RIGHT deletion or $\ddot F$ in LEFT deletion gives the same calculation. In RIGHT deletion call the last positions C,B,A, and write $B_{ck}=\widetilde E_{(B,c),(B,k)}$, $A_{kj}=\widetilde E_{(A,k),(A,j)}$, $C_{hk}=\widetilde E_{(C,h),(B,k)}$, $J_{cj}=\widetilde E_{(B,c),(A,j)}$ and $H_{hj}=\widetilde E_{(C,h),(A,j)}$. Twice expanding step 2.2 expresses the changed r-mode as the sum of eight groups $A_0=K_{r;ij}$, $B_0=\sum_cK_{r-1;ic}B_{cj}$, $C_0=[K_{r-1;ih},C_{hj}]$, $D_0=\sum_kK_{r-1;ik}A_{kj}$, $E_0=\sum_{k,c}K_{r-2;ic}B_{ck}A_{kj}$, $F_0=\sum_k[K_{r-2;ih},C_{hk}]A_{kj}$, $G_0=\sum_cK_{r-2;ic}J_{cj}$, $H_0=[K_{r-2;ih},H_{hj}]$. For $z=\widetilde E_{(A,f),(B,g)}$ their projected brackets, in that order, are $0,-\delta_{fj}K_{r-1;ig},0,+\delta_{fj}K_{r-1;ig},R,S,-R,-S$, where $R=t\delta_{fj}K_{r-2;ig}-K_{r-2;ig}A_{fj}+\delta_{fj}\sum_cK_{r-2;ic}B_{cg}$ and $S=\delta_{fj}[K_{r-2;ih},C_{hg}]$. The bracket in step 1.2 proves this table by moving each negative factor to the RIGHT. The t summation rows give the scalar in R; the bridge J gives its opposite because $\rho_A-\rho_B=t$. K lies in the twice-deleted p and commutes with z. The eight expressions cancel for EVERY r>1. [step 1.2, step 2.2, step 3.2, algebra]

5.1 At a RIGHT jump-one boundary, for a fixed earlier-block row g and r>1 the coefficient recursions in U(p) are $D_{m;ij}^{(r+1)}=[F_{m-1;ig}^{(2)},E_{m-1;gj}^{(r)}]+\sum_{s=0}^r\widetilde D_{m-1;gg}^{(r+1-s)}D_{m;ij}^{(s)}$ and $E_{m-1;ij}^{(r+1)}=[D_{m-1;ig}^{(2)},E_{m-1;gj}^{(r)}]-\sum_fD_{m-1;if}^{(1)}E_{m-1;fj}^{(r)}$. Here $\widetilde D(u)=-D(u)^{-1}$, with order-zero coefficient −I. The corresponding LEFT identities are $D_{m;ij}^{(r+1)}=[F_{m-1;ig}^{(r)},E_{m-1;gj}^{(2)}]+\sum_{s=0}^rD_{m;ij}^{(s)}\widetilde D_{m-1;gg}^{(r+1-s)}$ and $F_{m-1;ij}^{(r+1)}=[F_{m-1;ig}^{(r)},D_{m-1;gj}^{(2)}]-\sum_fF_{m-1;if}^{(r)}D_{m-1;fj}^{(1)}$. These are algebraic coefficient identities, independent of invariance and generation. Expanding the E identity by the right end recursion gives $\dot E^{(r+1)}+\sum\dot D^{(1)}\dot E^{(r)}$, its product with L, and its commutator with C; collecting with step 2.2 gives $E^{(r+1)}+\sum D^{(1)}E^{(r)}$. The left F expansion instead gives $\dot F^{(r+1)}+\sum\dot F^{(r)}\dot D^{(1)}$, its LEFT product with L and $[C,\,\cdot\,]$, which collect to $F^{(r+1)}+\sum F^{(r)}D^{(1)}$. The unchanged earlier D^(2) has no boundary endpoint and commutes with L,C. Expanding the boundary E–F bracket similarly gives the D identity: the coefficient convolution $D\widetilde D=-I$ cancels paired split paths with opposite cut signs, leaving the changed D coefficient. Thus the recursions hold in both product orders. Every earlier D/inverse-D coefficient and the unaffected E or F coefficient is invariant by step 3.2; the changed base coefficients are invariant by step 4.1. ## Remarks

- The independence argument is a finite-dimensional PBW argument in disguise: any finite relation involves finitely many modes, and mapping the corresponding finitely many generators to a jet Lie algebra makes their PBW images independent. No PBW theorem for the infinite-dimensional current algebra is assumed.

- The two related graded algebras are a recurring source of errors: the symbolic degrees of the Kazhdan filtration are the coordinates of the Slodowy slice, while the loop degrees are current-algebra degrees; the double centralizer arguments on this page use the loop filtration.
