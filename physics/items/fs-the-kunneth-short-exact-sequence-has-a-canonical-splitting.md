## Statement

The assertion that the Kunneth short exact sequence has a canonical splitting is false.

## Refutation

**Given:** let $C_1=\mathbb Ze\oplus\mathbb Zf$, $C_0=\mathbb Zg$ with $d(e)=2g$, $d(f)=0$, and let $D_1=\mathbb Za$, $D_0=\mathbb Zb$ with $d(a)=2b$.

1.1 We have $H_1(C)=\mathbb Zf$, $H_0(C)=H_0(D)=\mathbb Z/2$, and $H_1(D)=0$.  In total degree one the Kunneth sequence is therefore $0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/2\longrightarrow H_1(C\otimes D)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/2\longrightarrow0$. Writing $v=e\otimes b-g\otimes a$, a direct kernel/modulo-boundary calculation gives $H_1(C\otimes D)=(\mathbb Z/2)v\oplus(\mathbb Z/2)(f\otimes b)$; the left Kunneth term is generated by $f\otimes b$ and the quotient by the class of $v$. [given, algebra]

2.1 The chain automorphism $u(e)=e+f$, $u(f)=f$, $u(g)=g$ of $C$ induces the identity on $H_*(C)$, hence on both outer Kunneth terms, but sends $v$ to $v+f\otimes b$ in the middle term. [step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Every section of the quotient sends its generator to $v+t(f\otimes b)$ for some $t\in\mathbb Z/2$. Naturality under $u\otimes1_D$ would require this lift to be fixed, but $u$ changes $t$ to $t+1$. Thus no splitting of all Kunneth sequences can be canonical or natural. [step 2.1, contradiction] ∎
