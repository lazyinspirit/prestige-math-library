## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and Countable Choice. Let $n\ge2$, $0<\alpha<1$, let $\Omega$ be a bounded $C^{2,\alpha}$ domain, and let $L=a^{ij}\partial_i\partial_j+b^i\partial_i+c$ be uniformly elliptic on $\bar\Omega$ with constants $\lambda,\Lambda$, $[A]_{0,\alpha;\Omega}\le K$, and $b,c\in C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$ with $\|b\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}+\|c\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\le M$. Let $u\in C^2(\Omega)\cap C^0(\bar\Omega)$, $g\in C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$ and $f\in C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$ satisfy $Lu=f$ in $\Omega$ and $u=g$ on $\partial\Omega$. Then $u\in C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$ and
$$\|u\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}\le C\bigl(\|u\|_{C^0(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}+\|g\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}\bigr),$$
where $C=C(n,\alpha,\lambda,\Lambda,K,M,\Omega)$ depends on $\Omega$ only through a finite $C^{2,\alpha}$ chart atlas and its radii. The coefficient assumptions include the H\"older norms needed for the lower-order products, and no solvability is asserted: the estimate is a regularity statement for the given classical solution.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the Axiom of Choice and $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, $n\ge2$, $0<\alpha<1$, the bounded $C^{2,\alpha}$ domain $\Omega$, the operator $L$ with the stated bounds, and $u\in C^2(\Omega)\cap C^0(\bar\Omega)$, $g\in C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$, $f\in C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$ with $Lu=f$ in $\Omega$ and $u=g$ on $\partial\Omega$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed for the quoted boundary regularity and estimate inputs; Countable Choice is inherited by the measure and local estimate interfaces. The chart cover itself involves only finitely many selections. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-countable-choice]])

[F1] The classes $C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$ and $C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$ are the boundary-extension classes of [[def-holder-spaces-c-k-alpha-and-their-scaled-norms]]: an element of $C^{2,\alpha}_b(\Omega)$ lies in $C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$ when its derivatives through order $2$ extend continuously, and then $\|u\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}=\sum_{j=0}^2\sup_\Omega\max_{|\beta|=j}|D^\beta u|+[D^2u]_{0,\alpha;\Omega}$. On a ball $B_R(x_0)$ the scaled quantity is $\|w\|^*_{2,\alpha;B_R(x_0)}=\sum_{j=0}^2R^j\max_{|\beta|=j}\sup_{B_R}|D^\beta w|+R^{2+\alpha}\max_{|\beta|=2}[D^\beta w]_{0,\alpha;B_R}$ and $\|h\|^*_{0,\alpha;B_R}=\sup_{B_R}|h|+R^\alpha[h]_{0,\alpha;B_R}$, with the analogous formulas on a half-box; the scaled quantities dominate each of their defining terms. ([[def-local-holder-and-c-two-alpha-norms-on-euclidean-balls]])

[F2] Interior Schauder estimate ([[thm-interior-schauder-estimate-for-uniformly-elliptic-equations]]): let $B_R(x_0)\subseteq\mathbb R^n$, let $L$ be uniformly elliptic on $B_R(x_0)$ with constants $\lambda,\Lambda$, $[A]_{0,\alpha;B_R(x_0)}\le K$ and $\|b\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}+\|c\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\le M$ there. If $w\in C^2(B_R(x_0))\cap L^\infty(B_R(x_0))$ satisfies $Lw=F$ pointwise with $F\in C^{0,\alpha}(B_R(x_0))$, then $w\in C^{2,\alpha}(B_{R/2}(x_0))$ and $\|w\|^*_{2,\alpha;B_{R/2}(x_0)}\le C(\|w\|_{\infty;B_R(x_0)}+R^2\|F\|_{\infty;B_R(x_0)}+R^{2+\alpha}[F]_{0,\alpha;B_R(x_0)})$ with $C$ depending only on $n,\alpha,\lambda,\Lambda$ and the dimensionless coefficient bounds $R^\alpha K+R\|b\|_\infty+R^{1+\alpha}[b]_{0,\alpha}+R^2\|c\|_\infty+R^{2+\alpha}[c]_{0,\alpha}$.

[F3] Boundary flattening ([[lem-c-two-alpha-boundary-flattening-preserves-ellipticity-and-holder-norms]]): for every boundary point there are $r>0$ and a shear $\Psi$ with $\Psi(Q^+_r)=U\cap\Omega$ and $\Psi(Q'_r\times\{0\})=U\cap\partial\Omega$, where $Q_r=Q'_r\times(-r,r)$ and $Q^+_r=Q'_r\times(0,r)$; by shrinking inside a larger graph chart, take $\Psi$ defined on a neighborhood of $\overline{Q_r}$ and $\Psi(\overline{Q^+_r})\subset\bar\Omega$. Composition with $\Psi$ gives equivalent $C^{2,\alpha}$ norms on the closures of $U\cap\Omega$ and $Q^+_r$ with constants depending only on the chart, and if $L$ has $C^{0,\alpha}$ coefficients on $U$ then its pullback $\tilde L=\tilde a^{ij}\partial_i\partial_j+\tilde b^i\partial_i+\tilde c$ under $v=u\circ\Psi$ has $C^{0,\alpha}$ coefficients with ellipticity constants $\tilde\lambda=\lambda\|D\Psi\|_\infty^{-2}$, $\tilde\Lambda=\Lambda\|D\Psi^{-1}\|_\infty^2$ and coefficient bounds controlled by the chart and by $K,M$. For coefficients given only on $\bar\Omega$, extend each field to the chart image by $q(\Psi(y',y_n)):=q(\Psi(y',|y_n|))$. Reflection and the bi-Lipschitz shear preserve its H\"older bounds, and evaluating the same original matrix preserves ellipticity. Thus the lemma applies on the ambient patch. The shear is available at each boundary point of a bounded $C^{2,\alpha}$ domain because such a domain carries graph charts of class $C^{2,\alpha}$ over which the shear of the lemma straightens the boundary. ([[def-bounded-c-k-domain-and-boundary-charts]])

[F4] **Quoted boundary inputs with their distinct hypotheses.** Gilbarg–Trudinger, *Elliptic Partial Differential Equations of Second Order* (2001), Lemma 6.18 and Theorem 6.19, printed p.111, give local $C^{2,\alpha}$ regularity up to a $C^{2,\alpha}$ boundary portion for $u\in C^2(\Omega)\cap C^0(\bar\Omega)$, with Hölder coefficients and forcing and $C^{2,\alpha}$ boundary values; no sign condition on $c$ is imposed. Apply this first at the flat face. Then Simon, Lecture 12 Theorem 2', printed pp.134–135, gives the a priori estimate on a smaller half-patch. Covering the smaller half-box by such patches and interior balls gives $\|v\|^*_{2,\alpha;Q^+_{r/2}}\le C(\|v\|_{\infty;Q^+_r}+r^2\|h\|^*_{0,\alpha;Q^+_r})$ for zero flat-face data. Rescaling includes $r^\alpha\tilde K,r\|\tilde b\|_\infty,r^{1+\alpha}[\tilde b]_\alpha,r^2\|\tilde c\|_\infty,r^{2+\alpha}[\tilde c]_\alpha$ in $C$. These are explicit literature inputs; Wang Theorem 1' alone assumes $C^2$ up to the flat face and does not supply the upgrade.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Reduction to zero boundary values. Put $w:=u-g$ on $\Omega$. Since $g\in C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)\subseteq C^2(\Omega)\cap C^0(\bar\Omega)$, the function $w$ lies in $C^2(\Omega)\cap C^0(\bar\Omega)$, vanishes on $\partial\Omega$, and satisfies $Lw=F$ pointwise in $\Omega$ with $F:=f-Lg$. The product inequality $[ab]_\alpha\le\|a\|_\infty[b]_\alpha+[a]_\alpha\|b\|_\infty$ gives $[Lg]_{0,\alpha;\Omega}\le C_n\bigl(\Lambda[D^2g]_{0,\alpha;\Omega}+K\|D^2g\|_{\infty;\Omega}+\|b\|_\infty[Dg]_{0,\alpha;\Omega}+[b]_{0,\alpha;\Omega}\|Dg\|_{\infty;\Omega}+\|c\|_\infty[g]_{0,\alpha;\Omega}+[c]_{0,\alpha;\Omega}\|g\|_{\infty;\Omega}\bigr)$ and $\sup_\Omega|Lg|\le C_n(\Lambda+M)\|g\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\Omega)}$; in each flattened half-box the lower-field seminorms are bounded by the derivative suprema using integration along segments, and a finite cover plus the separation bound controls pairs in different charts; hence $F\in C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$ with $\|F\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}\le \|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}+C(n,\alpha,\Lambda,K,M,\Omega)\|g\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}$, and $\sup_\Omega|w|\le\|u\|_{C^0(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}$. It thus suffices to show that every such zero-boundary $w$ with $Lw=F\in C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$ satisfies $w\in C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$ and $\|w\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\Omega)}\le C(\|w\|_{\infty;\Omega}+\|F\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)})$ with $C=C(n,\alpha,\lambda,\Lambda,K,M,\Omega)$; adding $g$ back gives the statement. [given, F1, algebra]

1.2 A finite relative cover with a Lebesgue number. By [F3], for every $x\in\partial\Omega$ there is a flattened chart $\Psi_x:Q_{r_x}\to U_x$ with $\Psi_x(Q^+_{r_x})=U_x\cap\Omega$ and $\Psi_x(Q'_{r_x}\times\{0\})=U_x\cap\partial\Omega$. Put $\widehat V_x:=\Psi_x(Q_{r_x/2})$, an ambient open neighborhood of $x$ that includes points on both sides of the flattened boundary. These sets cover $\partial\Omega$; compactness gives finitely many, indexed by $\ell=1,\ldots,N$, and their union $V$ is an open neighborhood of $\partial\Omega$ in $\mathbb R^n$. Thus some $\delta>0$ satisfies $\{y\in\Omega:\operatorname{dist}(y,\partial\Omega)<\delta\}\subseteq V$. The set $\Omega_\delta:=\{y\in\Omega:\operatorname{dist}(y,\partial\Omega)\ge\delta\}$ is compact in $\Omega$. With $R:=\delta/4$, its balls $B_R(z)$ have doubled balls inside $\Omega$, and compactness yields finitely many centres $z_1,\ldots,z_P$ whose inner balls cover $\Omega_\delta$. The finite family consisting of the ambient open sets $\widehat V_\ell$ and the balls $B_R(z_j)$ covers $\bar\Omega$ relative to $\bar\Omega$. Let $\delta_0>0$ be a Lebesgue number for this relative open cover, so every pair $x,y\in\bar\Omega$ with $|x-y|<\delta_0$ lies in one common member. [F3, given, construct, A1]

1.3 Estimates on the boundary members. Fix $\ell\in\{1,\dots,N\}$, write $\Psi=\Psi_\ell$, $r=r_\ell$ and let $\tilde w:=w\circ\Psi$ on $Q^+_r$, with pullback operator $\tilde L$ as in [F3]. Then $\tilde w\in C^2(Q^+_r)\cap C^0(\overline{Q^+_r})$ because $\Psi$ is a $C^{2,\alpha}$ diffeomorphism of a neighbourhood of $\overline{Q^+_r}$ onto a neighbourhood of $\overline{U_\ell\cap\Omega}$ and $w\in C^2(\Omega)\cap C^0(\bar\Omega)$; moreover $\tilde w=0$ on the flat face, since that face maps onto $U_\ell\cap\partial\Omega$ where $w=0$. The pullback satisfies $\tilde L\tilde w=\tilde h$ pointwise in $Q^+_r$ with $\tilde h:=F\circ\Psi$, which lies in $C^{0,\alpha}(Q^+_r)\cap C^0(\overline{Q^+_r})$ by composition, and $\|\tilde h\|^*_{0,\alpha;Q^+_r}\le C_\ell\|F\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}$ with $C_\ell$ depending on the chart. Applying the quoted boundary estimate [F4] to $\tilde w$ gives $$\|\tilde w\|^*_{2,\alpha;Q^+_{r/2}}\le C_\ell'\bigl(\|w\|_{\infty;\Omega}+\|F\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}\bigr),$$ with $C_\ell'$ depending on $n,\alpha$, the ellipticity constants and coefficient bounds of $\tilde L$ (hence on the chart and on $\lambda,\Lambda,K,M$). By the norm equivalence of [F3], the last display controls $\sup|D^\beta w|$ for $|\beta|\le2$ and $[D^2w]_{0,\alpha}$ over $\Psi(Q^+_{r/2})=U_\ell\cap V_\ell$ with $V_\ell:=\Psi_\ell(Q^+_{r_\ell/2})$; finitely many charts give one constant $C_2$. [F3, F4, F1, given, algebra]

2.1 Estimates on the interior members. Fix $j\in\{1,\dots,P\}$ and let $B_{2R}(z_j)\subseteq\Omega$. On $B_{2R}(z_j)$ the function $w$ is of class $C^2$ and bounded, and $Lw=F$ pointwise with $F\in C^{0,\alpha}(B_{2R}(z_j))$; the coefficient bounds $[A]_{0,\alpha}\le K$ and $\|b\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}+\|c\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\le M$ hold there. By [F2] with radius $2R$, $$\|w\|^*_{2,\alpha;B_R(z_j)}\le C_j\bigl(\|w\|_{\infty;\Omega}+\|F\|_{\infty;\Omega}+[F]_{0,\alpha;\Omega}\bigr),$$ where $C_j$ depends on $n,\alpha,\lambda,\Lambda$ and the dimensionless bounds formed with the radius $2R$; as there are finitely many $j$ and all radii are comparable to $\delta$, the numbers $C_j$ are bounded by a constant $C_1$ depending on $n,\alpha,\lambda,\Lambda,K,M$ and on the cover. In particular $\sup_{B_R(z_j)}|D^\beta w|\le R^{-|\beta|}\|w\|^*_{2,\alpha;B_R(z_j)}$ for $|\beta|\le2$ and $[D^2w]_{0,\alpha;B_R(z_j)}\le R^{-2-\alpha}\|w\|^*_{2,\alpha;B_R(z_j)}$. [F2, F1, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Summation and conclusion. The interior balls $B_R(z_j)$ together with the boundary-chart sets $\widehat V_\ell\cap\bar\Omega$ cover $\bar\Omega$; on each boundary-chart set, its portion in $\bar\Omega$ lies in $\Psi_\ell(\overline{Q^+_{r_\ell/2}})$, where step 1.3 supplies the estimate. Thus for every $|\beta|\le2$, $\sup_\Omega|D^\beta w|\le\sum_j\sup_{B_R(z_j)}|D^\beta w|+\sum_\ell\sup_{\widehat V_\ell\cap\Omega}|D^\beta w|\le C_3(\|w\|_{\infty;\Omega}+\|F\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)})$ by steps 2.1 and 1.3. For the H\"older seminorm let $x,y\in\Omega$, $x\ne y$. If $|x-y|<\delta_0$, step 1.2 gives a member of the relative cover containing both points, and steps 2.1 or 1.3 bound the corresponding difference quotient; if $|x-y|\ge\delta_0$, then $|D^2w(x)-D^2w(y)|/|x-y|^{\alpha}\le2\delta_0^{-\alpha}\sup_\Omega|D^2w|$, already controlled. Therefore $$\|w\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\Omega)}=\sum_{j=0}^2\sup_\Omega\max_{|\beta|=j}|D^\beta w|+[D^2w]_{0,\alpha;\Omega}\le C\bigl(\|w\|_{\infty;\Omega}+\|F\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}\bigr)$$ with $C=C(n,\alpha,\lambda,\Lambda,K,M,\Omega)$. Since the boundary-chart sets are ambient neighborhoods of each boundary point and the controlled coordinate functions are $C^{2,\alpha}$ up to the flat face, the derivatives $D^\beta w$ for $|\beta|\le2$ extend continuously to $\bar\Omega$. Hence $w\in C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$ with the same norm over $\Omega$, as required by [F1]. [step 1.2, step 2.1, step 1.3, F1, algebra]

4.1 The estimate for $u$. By step 1.1 and step 3.1, $$\|u\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}\le\|w\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}\le C\bigl(\|u\|_{C^0(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}+\|g\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}\bigr)$$ after enlarging $C$ to absorb the constants of step 1.1; the constant depends on $n,\alpha,\lambda,\Lambda,K,M$ and on the finite cover, i.e. on $\Omega$ only through its $C^{2,\alpha}$ charts and radii. The strict range $0<\alpha<1$ enters through the quoted boundary estimate [F4] and the interior estimate [F2]; the Dirichlet condition enters through $w=0$ on the boundary, which is exactly the zero-data hypothesis of [F4] on each flat face. No solvability, compactness of the operator or boundary regularity beyond the $C^{2,\alpha}$ chart hypothesis is used, and it is the Dirichlet condition and the $C^{2,\alpha}$ boundary that make the estimate possible at every boundary point. [step 1.1, step 3.1, F2, F4, given] ∎

## Remarks

- The regularity upgrade and the subsequent a priori estimate have different hypotheses, explicitly separated in [F4].
- The cover argument is the same one used for the interior estimate, run on the compact closure; the Lebesgue number replaces the partition of unity, which is why the covering selections are finite; the quoted analytic inputs are used under [A1].
- The estimate is stated with $\|u\|_{C^0(\Omega)}$ rather than $\|u\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}$ on the right, so it is a genuine a priori bound. The interpolation lemma of the interior argument is not needed in this form of the proof, because the quoted local a priori estimate already handles the lower-order terms; the intermediate-derivative terms are not produced by cutoffs here.
