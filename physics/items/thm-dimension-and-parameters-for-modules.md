## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $(R,\mathfrak m)$ be a Noetherian local ring and let $M\neq0$ be a finite
$R$-module. Put
$$ d:=\dim\operatorname{Supp}(M). $$
Then:

1. $d$ is the least integer $r$ for which there exist $x_1,\ldots,x_r\in\mathfrak m$
   with $M/(x_1,\ldots,x_r)M$ of finite length;
2. whenever $x_1,\ldots,x_d$ have this property, they form a system of
   parameters for $M$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, a Noetherian local ring $(R,\mathfrak m)$ and a nonzero finite $R$-module $M$.

[L1] Under the assumed Choice, in a finite-dimensional Noetherian local ring the dimension is the least number of generators of an ideal whose radical is the maximal ideal ([[thm-dimension-as-minimal-number-of-radical-generators]]).

[L2] A system of parameters is a $d$-tuple in $\mathfrak m$ whose generated ideal has radical $\mathfrak m$ ([[def-system-of-parameters-and-parameter-ideal]]).

[L3] Every ideal in a Noetherian commutative ring is finitely generated ([[thm-noetherian-ring-ideal-characterisations]]).

[L4] The height of an ideal generated by $r$ elements is at most $r$ ([[thm-krull-height-theorem]]).

[L5] A finite module has support $V(\operatorname{Ann}M)$, and under Choice a radical ideal is the intersection of all primes containing it ([[thm-support-and-annihilator-of-a-finite-module]], [[thm-radical-as-intersection-of-primes]]).

[L6] Under Choice, if a finite module over a local ring equals its maximal-ideal multiple, it is zero ([[thm-nakayama-lemma]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $A:=R/\operatorname{Ann}_R(M)$ and $\mathfrak n:=\mathfrak m/\operatorname{Ann}_R(M)$. The ring $A$ is nonzero, Noetherian and local; $M$ is a faithful finite $A$-module. By [L5], $\operatorname{Supp}_A(M)=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$, and prime correspondence identifies this space with $\operatorname{Supp}_R(M)$, so $d=\dim A=\operatorname{ht}_A(\mathfrak n)$. The ideal $\mathfrak n$ has a finite generating list by [L3]; since it is maximal, it is minimal over its own generating ideal. Under the assumed AC, [L4] bounds its height by that list length. Thus $d<\infty$ and [L1] applies to $A$. [L3, L4, L5, given, algebra]

1.2 A nonzero finite $A$-module $Q$ has finite length exactly when its support is $\{\mathfrak n\}$. In one direction, a finite composition series has simple factors $A/\mathfrak n$: for a nonzero element of a simple factor, its annihilator is maximal, hence is the unique maximal ideal $\mathfrak n$. Thus $\mathfrak n^sQ=0$ for the series length $s$, and [L5] gives support contained in $\{\mathfrak n\}$; nonzero $Q$ has $\mathfrak n$ in its support by [L6]. Conversely, if support is $\{\mathfrak n\}$, [L5] gives $\sqrt{\operatorname{Ann}_A(Q)}=\mathfrak n$. Choose finite generators $t_1,\ldots,t_u$ of $\mathfrak n$ by [L3]. Each $t_i$ has a positive power annihilating $Q$, so some power $\mathfrak n^N$ annihilates $Q$. The finite filtration $Q\supseteq\mathfrak nQ\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathfrak n^NQ=0$ has finite $A/\mathfrak n$-vector-space quotients: each $\mathfrak n^iQ$ is generated by products of finite generating lists for $\mathfrak n$ and $Q$. Refining each vector-space quotient by a basis flag gives a finite composition series. [L3, L5, L6, algebra]

2.1 Fix an ideal $J\subseteq\mathfrak m$ and put $Q=M/JM$. By [L6], $Q\ne0$. For each prime $\mathfrak q$ of $A$, if $JA\nsubseteq\mathfrak q$ then $Q_{\mathfrak q}=0$. If $JA\subseteq\mathfrak q$, then $M_{\mathfrak q}\ne0$ by step 1.1 and $J A_{\mathfrak q}$ lies in the maximal ideal of the local ring $A_{\mathfrak q}$; [L6] applied there gives $Q_{\mathfrak q}\ne0$. Hence $\operatorname{Supp}_A(Q)=V_A(JA)$. By [L5], this support is exactly $\{\mathfrak n\}$ if and only if $\sqrt{JA}=\mathfrak n$. [L5, L6, step 1.1, given]

3.1 Combining steps 2.1 and 1.2, for ideals $J\subseteq\mathfrak m$ the quotient $M/JM$ has finite length exactly when $\sqrt{JA}=\mathfrak n$. Applying [L1] in $A$, the least size of a generating tuple in $\mathfrak n$ with this property is $d$. Every such tuple in $A$ lifts to a tuple in $\mathfrak m$ because the quotient map $R\to A$ sends $\mathfrak m$ onto $\mathfrak n$; finite choices suffice. This proves part 1. [L1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 1.2]

4.1 If $x_1,\ldots,x_d\in\mathfrak m$ satisfy that $M/(x_1,\ldots,x_d)M$ has finite length, step 3.1 gives $\sqrt{(x_1,\ldots,x_d)A}=\mathfrak n$. Their images in $A$ therefore form a system of parameters by [L2]; this is the asserted module parameter condition. For $d=0$, the tuple is empty and the same argument says $M$ itself has finite length. [L2, step 3.1]

5.1 Steps 3.1 and 4.1 prove both claims. [step 3.1, step 4.1] ∎
