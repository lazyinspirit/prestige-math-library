## Statement

Assume Choice. Let g be finite-dimensional complex simple, with triangular decomposition n−+h+n+, and eta a nondegenerate character of n−. On finite g category O, the functor $Hbar_0(M)=H_0(n−,M \otimes C_{(−\eta)})$ is exact with finite-dimensional values and $Hbar_i(M)=0$ for $i>0$. Its value on $M_g(\lambda)$ and on the restricted dual $M_g(\lambda)^*$ is $C_{(\gamma_{\lambda})}$. Its value on $L_g(\lambda)$ is $C_{(\gamma_{\lambda})}$ if no positive-root shifted coroot pairing is a positive integer, and zero otherwise.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The data and character sign of the Statement; $I_{\eta}$ is the ideal of U(n−) generated by x−eta(x), so $Hbar_0(M)=M/I_{\eta}$ M.

[F1] [[lem-cft-principal-whittaker-universal-equivalence]] proves an ACTUAL exact equivalence between all jointly shifted-nilpotent principal Whittaker g-modules and Z(U(g))-modules, with Wh inverse to Y $\otimes_Z−$. Characters are transferred between n+ and n− by the Chevalley anti-involution.

[F2] [[lem-enveloping-algebra-is-left-and-right-noetherian]], [[thm-category-o-has-enough-projectives]] and [[thm-projectives-in-category-o-have-verma-flags]] provide Noetherian enveloping algebras, enough finite-O projectives, and their finite Verma flags. Hence each O object is finite over U(n−): a projective Verma flag is a finite free U(n−)-module, since successive extensions with free quotient split over U(n−).

## Proof

1.1 Put $R=U(n−)$ and I its augmentation ideal. Its root-height grading is generated in degree1 and $I^r$ is the sum of height degrees at least r: a height-j root vector is an iterated bracket of j simple generators. The Rees algebra sum $I^r t^r$ is generated by the finite Lie subspace $L=\operatorname{span}\{x_{\beta} t^s:0\le s\le \operatorname{ht}(\beta)\}$. Brackets close because heights add; distributing a t-exponent among PBW factors proves generation. It is therefore a quotient of U(L), hence Noetherian by F2. For a submodule A of a finite R-module B, the graded submodule sum(A intersect $I^rB)t^r$ of the finite Rees module sum $I^rB t^r$ has finitely many homogeneous generators of degrees at most c. Therefore `I^r A subset A intersect I^rB subset I^(r−c)A` for $r\ge c$. Translating x to x−eta(x) is an algebra automorphism, so this holds for $I_{\eta}$ as well. Each $R/I_{\eta}^r$ is finite dimensional. [F2, given, algebra]

2.1 Take full algebraic duals using Chevalley transpose. The generalized Whittaker part is $Whgen(M^{\vee})=\bigcup_r(M/I_{\eta}^rM)^*$. For an exact sequence $0->A->B->C->0$ of finite R-modules with compatible g actions, this dual functor is exact: a functional on A killing $I_{\eta}^rA$ extends to $B/I_{\eta}^sB$ once `A intersect I_eta^sB subset I_eta^rA`, supplied by step 1.1. Kernel and injection follow by factoring functionals vanishing on A. These generalized parts are g-stable: commuting a sufficiently long shifted n+ word through x in g gives finitely many adjoint words on the finite nilpotent n+ module g and shorter shifted words on the original functional, all eventually zero. Hence they belong to the exact Whittaker category of F1. [A1, F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Applying the exact Wh functor of F1 to step 2.1 gives exactness of $M->Wh(M^{\vee})$. Its value is exactly $(M/I_{\eta} M)^*$. These quotient spaces are finite dimensional by F2 and step 1.1. Dualizing again proves $Hbar_0$ exact on finite g category O. This argument does not assume ideal-adic completion is exact for arbitrary noncommutative rings; the needed cofinality was proved in step 1.1. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 A Verma-filtered projective P is free over U(n−), and character translation preserves freeness, so $Hbar_i(P)=0$ for $i>0$ by the ordinary free-module Chevalley chain contraction. For arbitrary M choose $0->K->P->M->0$ by F2. Its long homology sequence and step 3.1 give $Hbar_1(M)=0$; for $i\ge 2$ they give $Hbar_i(M)=Hbar_{(i−1)}(K)$, so induction on i proves all higher vanishing. All kernels are still category-O objects. [F2, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 For a Verma module, PBW identifies the twisted n− module with the regular module. Its character coinvariants are one-dimensional, and its center acts as $\gamma_{\lambda}$ by F4. Thus $Hbar_0(M_g(\lambda))=C_{\gamma_\lambda}$, with the higher vanishing already proved. [F2, F4, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 If the finite Verma is simple this computes the simple value. Otherwise its maximal submodule contains an embedded Verma $M_g(mu)$ by F3. Exactness makes its one-dimensional $Hbar_0$ inject into the one-dimensional $Hbar_0$ of $M_g(\lambda)$, so the quotient has zero $Hbar_0$. Its simple quotient $L_g(\lambda)$ therefore has zero $Hbar_0$. F3 identifies exactly these two cases by the no-positive-integral-pairing condition. [F3, step 3.1, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 Restricted duality fixes simples and the Verma and dual Verma have the same formal character, hence the same finite composition multiplicities: subtract their finitely many highest simple terms successively. Exact $Hbar_0$ consequently gives the dual Verma the same Grothendieck class as $C_{\gamma_\lambda}$. A finite-dimensional Z-module of that class is exactly the one-dimensional $C_{\gamma_\lambda}$. Higher homology is zero by step 4.1. This proves every asserted value and the Statement. [F3, F4, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, algebra] ∎
