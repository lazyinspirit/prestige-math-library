## Statement

Assume Countable Choice ([[def-countable-choice]]) and the constant isotropic material branch of [[post-em-material-closure]] with $\varepsilon,\mu>0$, $\sigma\ge0$, $J_{\rm ext}=0$ and initially zero volume charge. For an exact broadband physical interpretation retain the closure condition $\varepsilon\mu\ge c^{-2}$; without it this is the declared effective model with no universal front-speed claim. Put $v^2=(\varepsilon\mu)^{-1}$, $\gamma=\sigma/\varepsilon$. On the infinite straight rectangular guide $Q=(0,a)\times(0,b)\times\mathbb R$ of [[def-em-guide-cavity-geometry]], declare the finite-energy reflection class as follows. Extend in the two transverse variables to $T_{ab}^2\times\mathbb R$, $T_{ab}^2=\mathbb R/(2a\mathbb Z)\times\mathbb R/(2b\mathbb Z)$. The electric components have transverse parities even/odd, odd/even, odd/odd respectively; magnetic components have odd/even, even/odd, even/even respectively. Require the extended real fields to be $L^2$ and have both divergences zero distributionally. When the reflected extensions are continuous across the walls, their odd components give the four-wall conditions $n\times E=0$, $n\cdot B=0$; it is a precise weak PEC realization, not an assertion about every rough trace class. There is no end cap and no junction or open-end scattering datum.
(a) [Complete mixed transform] Let $\phi_{mn}(x_t)=(4ab)^{-1/2}e^{i\pi(mx_1/a+nx_2/b)}$, $(m,n)\in\mathbb Z^2$, be the orthonormal transverse characters. For each component first form $F_{mn}(z)=\int_{T_{ab}^2}F(x_t,z)\overline{\phi_{mn}(x_t)}\,dx_t$, then apply the exact negative-sign $2\pi$ Fourier transform $\mathcal F_2$ of [[thm-plancherel]] in $z$. Write $\widehat F_{mn}(\xi)=\mathcal F_2F_{mn}(\xi)$ and $\beta=2\pi\xi$. The transform is a unitary representation of $L^2(T_{ab}^2\times\mathbb R)$ by $\ell^2(\mathbb Z^2;L^2(\mathbb R))$, with inverse in $L^2$ from [[thm-l-two-fourier-inversion]], and $$\|F\|_{L^2(Q)}^2=\tfrac14\sum_{m,n}\int_{\mathbb R}|\widehat F_{mn}(\xi)|^2\,d\xi$$ for these reflection fields. The parity and reality relations are retained, including $\widehat F_{-m,-n}(-\xi)=\overline{\widehat F_{mn}(\xi)}$. With $K=(\pi m/a,\pi n/b,2\pi\xi)$, the divergence constraints become $K\cdot\widehat E=K\cdot\widehat B=0$ for almost every $\xi$. This is a complete transverse sin/cos and longitudinal $L^2$ Fourier representation, not pointwise Fourier convergence or a finite-energy monochromatic plane wave.
(b) [Unique energy evolution] Adopt in that weak reflection class the constant-Ohmic first-order material evolution $E_t=v^2\operatorname{curl}B-\gamma E$, $B_t=-\operatorname{curl}E$, distributionally, consistent with the smooth [[pthm-em-macroscopic-maxwell]] and [[pthm-em-restricted-material-energy]] branches. For every initial pair in the class there is a unique $C(\mathbb R;L^2(Q))$ solution in the same class. Its mixed coefficients solve, for every transverse index and almost every $\xi$, $$\partial_t\widehat E=iv^2K\times\widehat B-\gamma\widehat E,\qquad\partial_t\widehat B=-iK\times\widehat E.$$ Finite transverse sums and bounded longitudinal frequency truncations reconstruct the solution in energy norm uniformly on compact time intervals; such reconstructed fields are dense in the class at every time. They retain the divergence constraints and the original parities, including zero coefficient cases without dividing by $K$ or $|K|$.
(c) [Energy and dispersion] The total stored energy of this whole infinite guide is finite and equals $$\mathcal U(t)=\tfrac18\sum_{m,n}\int_{\mathbb R}(\varepsilon|\widehat E|^2+\mu^{-1}|\widehat B|^2)\,d\xi.$$ For every $t_0<t_1$, $\mathcal U(t_1)+\sigma\int_{t_0}^{t_1}\|E(t)\|_{L^2(Q)}^2dt=\mathcal U(t_0)$, and $\mathcal U'(t)=-\sigma\|E(t)\|_2^2$. At $\sigma=0$ energy is conserved and each constrained electric coefficient satisfies $\widehat E''+v^2|K|^2\widehat E=0$. A nonzero harmonic coefficient $\widehat E(t)=e\,e^{-i\omega t}$ with $e\in\mathbb C^3\setminus\{0\}$ and $\omega>0$ therefore requires $\omega^2=v^2[(\pi m/a)^2+(\pi n/b)^2+\beta^2]$; the zero coefficient imposes no frequency condition. These are the exact finite transverse-mode matrices of the approved K10 branch; individual separated TE/TM candidate fields still have their own cutoff and zero-profile restrictions, and no TE$_{00}$ mode is created by a scalar constant coefficient. For $\sigma>0$ the electric equation is $\widehat E''+\gamma\widehat E'+v^2|K|^2\widehat E=0$ with the distinct/repeated-root alternatives of the choice-free finite-parameter clause of [[pthm-em-uniform-ohmic-modal-decay]](c), here at $\lambda=|K|^2\ge0$. Its full-Choice smooth-cavity realization is not used. At $K=0$ keep the direct first-order equations $\widehat E\prime=-\gamma\widehat E$, $\widehat B\prime=0$, without dividing by $K$; the original parity and $L^2$ relations still apply. No signal-speed or empirical inference is made.
(d) [Scope] This theorem supplies the previously missing complete longitudinal $L^2$ expansion of the declared straight rectangular guide, including its lossless and constant-Ohmic energy evolutions. It makes no completeness claim for arbitrary non-rectangular, dispersive, nonlocal, nonlinear, rough-wall or open-end/junction problems. The weak energy result is proved by linear mixed-transform evolution and modal limits, not by applying a nonlinear smooth Poynting identity to rough data.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice ([[def-countable-choice]]); the constant isotropic material branch of [[post-em-material-closure]] with constants $\varepsilon,\mu>0$ and $\sigma\ge0$, $J_{\rm ext}=0$ and initially zero volume charge (with the closure condition $\varepsilon\mu\ge c^{-2}$ retained for the exact broadband interpretation); $v^2=(\varepsilon\mu)^{-1}$, $\gamma=\sigma/\varepsilon$; the infinite straight rectangular guide $Q=(0,a)\times(0,b)\times\mathbb R$ and its geometry of [[def-em-guide-cavity-geometry]]; the finite-energy reflection class of the Statement, defined by transverse extension to $T_{ab}^2\times\mathbb R$ with the stated electric and magnetic parities, $L^2$ membership and distributionally zero divergences.

[F1] The material and evolution branch: $D=\varepsilon E$, $B=\mu H$, and with initially zero volume charge the macroscopic equations of [[pthm-em-macroscopic-maxwell]] give the first-order system $E_t=v^2\operatorname{curl}B-\gamma E$, $B_t=-\operatorname{curl}E$ and zero divergences; the stored energy is $\mathcal U=\frac12\int_Q(\varepsilon|E|^2+\mu^{-1}|B|^2)$ ([[post-em-material-closure]] (b), [[pthm-em-macroscopic-maxwell]] (b), [[pthm-em-restricted-material-energy]]).

[F2] Transverse completeness: the characters $\phi_{mn}(x_t)=(4ab)^{-1/2}e^{i\pi(mx_1/a+nx_2/b)}$ are orthonormal in $L^2$ of the fundamental rectangle of $T_{ab}^2$ by elementary exponential integration, and the product families of cosines and sines are complete orthogonal systems ([[lem-em-rectangular-transverse-mode-completeness]] (a),(b), [[lem-em-rectangular-pec-energy-completeness]] (i), [[thm-hilbert-space-fourier-expansion]]); Fubini and Tonelli apply to the product spaces ([[thm-fubini-theorem-for-l-one-on-sigma-finite-product-spaces]], [[thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces]]).

[F3] Fourier analysis in $z$: the unitary Plancherel transform $\mathcal F_2$ with inverse in $L^2$, the derivative Fourier identity on Schwartz functions, and Schwartz cutoffs from the standard smooth step function ([[thm-plancherel]], [[thm-l-two-fourier-inversion]], [[thm-fourier-transform-maps-schwartz-space-continuously-to-itself]], [[lem-schwartz-cutoffs-from-the-standard-smooth-step]]).

[F4] Distributional and integration tools: distributional derivative and its regular-distribution compatibility, a distribution with zero derivative on a connected open set is constant, the oriented primitive of a continuous function is $C^1$ with that derivative, continuous functions on compact intervals are integrable, and dominated convergence ([[def-distributional-derivative]], [[thm-distributional-differentiation-is-continuous-and-commutes]], [[thm-a-distribution-with-zero-derivatives-on-a-connected-open-set-is-constant]], [[thm-ftc-first-part]], [[thm-ftc-second-part]], [[thm-continuous-implies-integrable]], [[thm-dominated-convergence]]).

[F5] ODE tools: globally Lipschitz finite-dimensional systems have unique global solutions and their solutions depend continuously on initial data and parameters ([[cor-globally-lipschitz-odes-have-global-solutions]], [[thm-continuous-dependence-of-odes-on-initial-data-and-parameters]]); the characteristic alternatives for $\widehat E''+\gamma\widehat E'+v^2\lambda\widehat E=0$ at $\lambda\ge0$ are the finite-parameter classification of [[pthm-em-uniform-ohmic-modal-decay]] (c), whose smooth-cavity, full-Choice clauses are not used.

[F6] Algebra and estimates: the componentwise cross product and its identities, complex conjugation and modulus laws, the cartesian form of the complex exponential, Cauchy–Schwarz, and completeness/density in complex $L^2$ ([[def-cross-product-in-r3]], [[lem-cross-product-is-bilinear-alternating-and-orthogonal]], [[def-divergence-and-curl-of-a-c1-vector-field]], [[def-euclidean-inner-product]], [[lem-complex-conjugation-and-modulus-laws]], [[cor-complex-exponential-cartesian-form-modulus-and-eulers-identity]], [[cor-cauchy-schwarz-inequality-for-l-two]], [[lem-complex-lp-completeness-density-and-inner-product]], [[thm-sine-and-cosine-derivatives]], [[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]], [[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]], [[thm-of-square-roots]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct: build the mixed transverse-character and longitudinal-Fourier representation, transport the divergence constraints and the evolution into coefficient form, solve the finite dimensional modal systems with uniform energy bounds, and pass cutoffs to the limit.

1.1 [Complete mixed transform] With the characters of [F2], first form for each component the transverse coefficients $F_{mn}(z)=\int_{T_{ab}^2}F(x_t,z)\overline{\phi_{mn}(x_t)}dx_t$ and then $\widehat F_{mn}(\xi)=\mathcal F_2F_{mn}(\xi)$ for the exact $2\pi$-convention transform of [F3]; write $\beta=2\pi\xi$. Then the map $F\mapsto(\widehat F_{mn})_{(m,n)\in\mathbb Z^2}$ is a unitary isomorphism of $L^2(T_{ab}^2\times\mathbb R)$ onto $\ell^2(\mathbb Z^2;L^2(\mathbb R))$ and, for the reflection fields of the Statement, $\|F\|^2_{L^2(Q)}=\frac14\sum_{m,n}\int_{\mathbb R}|\widehat F_{mn}(\xi)|^2d\xi$. Indeed the characters are orthonormal on the fundamental rectangle and, being products of the complete one-dimensional character systems, their finite span is dense in the transverse $L^2$ of each torus fibre by [F2]; Fubini–Tonelli therefore gives, for almost every $z$, the transverse expansion together with $\int_{T_{ab}^2}|F(x_t,z)|^2dx_t=\sum_{m,n}|F_{mn}(z)|^2$ and, after integration in $z$, $\|F\|^2_{L^2(T_{ab}^2\times\mathbb R)}=\sum_{m,n}\|F_{mn}\|^2_{L^2(\mathbb R)}$. Applying the unitary Plancherel transform [F3] to each of the countably many scalar functions $F_{mn}\in L^2(\mathbb R)$ gives the coefficient map and the isometry on the torus product, with inverse from the $L^2$ inversion theorem of [F3]; the reflection extension doubles each transverse period, so the torus fundamental rectangle has $4ab$-area and the physical norm is one quarter of the extended norm, which is the displayed factor $\frac14$. The parity relations follow from $\phi_{-m,-n}=\overline{\phi_{mn}}$ and the transverse parities by the coordinate changes $x_j\mapsto-x_j$ on the torus, and the reality relation $\widehat F_{-m,-n}(-\xi)=\overline{\widehat F_{mn}(\xi)}$ holds first for real integrable compactly supported longitudinal data by the defining integral and passes to $L^2$ by density and continuity of the transform [F3]. No pointwise reconstruction is asserted. [F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 [Divergence constraints in coefficient form] For $K:=(\pi m/a,\pi n/b,2\pi\xi)$ the distributionally zero divergence of the extended field is equivalent to $K\cdot\widehat E=K\cdot\widehat B=0$ for almost every $\xi$, and the finite-energy reflection class is exactly the class of coefficient families satisfying these almost-everywhere constraints together with the parities and $L^2$ summability. The derivative correspondence is proved on the dense test class of finite transverse-character sums times longitudinal Schwartz functions: for such a test $\psi$, finite transverse integration by parts and the exact identity $\mathcal F_2(\partial_z\psi)=2\pi i\xi\widehat\psi$ of [F3] turn the pairing $\int F\cdot\nabla\psi$ into the sum of the coefficient pairings against $iK\cdot\widehat F$, and the compactly supported smooth tests used in the definition are obtained by multiplying with cutoffs $\chi_R$ of [F3] and passing to the limit, since the tests and their first derivatives converge in $L^2$ and all pairings are bounded by Cauchy–Schwarz [F6]; conversely a test with $K\cdot\widehat F=0$ almost everywhere has zero weak divergence pairing, and the general compact smooth tests are reached through the transverse $L^2$ expansions of their derivatives and the Schwartz transform isomorphism [F3]. Hence the exact constrained class corresponds to the stated almost-everywhere coefficient constraints, not merely to a scalar basis without Gauss equations; for fields smooth in the interior the divergences vanish pointwise, and if their reflected extensions are continuous across the walls then the four-wall PEC conditions hold by [[def-em-guide-cavity-geometry]](c). [step 1.1, F2, F3, F6]

3.1 [Unique energy evolution in the class] Adopt the constant-Ohmic first-order system of [F1] distributionally in the reflection class of step 2.1. For every initial pair in the class there is a unique $C(\mathbb R;L^2(Q))$ solution in the same class, its mixed coefficients satisfy, for every $(m,n)$ and almost every $\xi$, $\partial_t\widehat E=iv^2K\times\widehat B-\gamma\widehat E$ and $\partial_t\widehat B=-iK\times\widehat E$, and finite transverse sums with bounded longitudinal frequency truncations reconstruct the solution in energy norm uniformly on compact time intervals and are dense in the class at every time, retaining the divergence constraints and parities without dividing by $K$ or $|K|$. Existence: for each $(m,n,\xi)$ the real and imaginary parts of the displayed system form a real finite-dimensional constant-coefficient linear system on all of $\mathbb R$, hence a globally Lipschitz system with a unique global solution through the initial coefficients by [F5]; the solution matrix depends continuously on $\xi$ uniformly on compact parameter and time sets by the continuous-dependence theorem [F5] (applied on a common bounded state cylinder, which the uniform energy bound below supplies), so multiplying the measurable initial coefficient vector by the continuous fundamental matrix gives measurable coefficient trajectories [F6]; no uncountable choice of solutions is made, and the construction uses only the countable families of coefficients. Energy: with $q:=\varepsilon|\widehat E|^2+\mu^{-1}|\widehat B|^2$, conjugation and the triple-product cancellation of the displayed system give $q'=-2\sigma|\widehat E|^2$, whence $q(t)\le e^{2\gamma T}q(0)$ for $|t|\le T$ and forward contractivity at $\sigma\ge0$; the constraints $K\cdot\widehat E=K\cdot\widehat B=0$ and the parity and reality relations of step 1.1 are preserved because the right-hand side is linear, $K\cdot(K\times\widehat B)=0$ and the parities are respected by the cross product with $K$. Cutoffs $|m|,|n|\le N$, $|\xi|\le N$ preserve every relation; their initial energy tails tend to zero by Tonelli and dominated convergence [F4], and the uniform bound makes the time-dependent tails tend to zero uniformly on compact times; each truncated solution is strongly differentiable in $L^2$ with bounded multiplier $K$ on the cutoff, and its linear equations follow from the test derivative identity of step 2.1, so the compact-time $L^2$ limits are continuous, have the prescribed data and satisfy the equations against compact smooth tests. Uniqueness: for any weak $C_tL^2$ reflection solution and bounded spectral cutoff $N$, project to the cutoff coefficients; the test identity of step 2.1 turns the evolution into $f'=A_Nf$ distributionally in time in the cutoff Hilbert space with bounded constant matrix $A_N$, and the finite transverse tests and smooth frequency tests supported in the cutoff interval, whose inverse transforms are Schwartz, are dense in the cutoff space, so the identity extends to every cutoff vector by the bounded multiplier and Cauchy–Schwarz [F6]. Subtracting the continuous Hilbert-valued primitive of $A_Nf$, constructed as the norm limit of finite Riemann sums (which is independent of the tags by the uniform oscillation bound on a compact interval, exists by completeness of $L^2$, has the correct derivative by the average-increment bound, and is the vector version of the primitive argument of [F4]), pairing with every fixed cutoff vector and applying the scalar zero-derivative distribution theorem [F4] identifies the difference with its constant initial value; the difference $d$ of two solutions with zero data satisfies $d(t)=\int_0^tA_Nd(s)ds$, so on an interval of length $h$ with $h\|A_N\|<1$ the supremum norm forces $d=0$, and finitely iterating these intervals in either direction on any compact time interval gives $d=0$; letting the cutoffs exhaust the representation gives uniqueness of the whole field. The zero coefficient $K=0$ is treated directly by the same equations with $K=0$, without division by $K$ or $|K|$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, F1, F3, F4, F5, F6]

4.1 [Energy and dispersion] The total stored energy of the whole infinite guide is finite and equals $\mathcal U(t)=\frac18\sum_{m,n}\int_{\mathbb R}(\varepsilon|\widehat E|^2+\mu^{-1}|\widehat B|^2)d\xi$, and for every $t_0<t_1$ one has $\mathcal U(t_1)+\sigma\int_{t_0}^{t_1}\|E(t)\|_{L^2(Q)}^2dt=\mathcal U(t_0)$ with $\mathcal U'(t)=-\sigma\|E(t)\|_2^2$. Indeed, summing the finite/cutoff modal identities $q'=-2\sigma|\widehat E|^2$ of step 3.1 over the bounded cutoffs, integrating in $\xi$ and summing the finite transverse indices gives the truncated balance, and dominated convergence with the compact-time tail bound of step 3.1 passes it to the limit; the physical energy carries the factor $\frac12\cdot\frac14=\frac18$ from step 1.1, and the dissipation term is continuous in time, so ordinary FTC [F4] gives the derivative form. At $\sigma=0$ the energy is constant, and on the constrained coefficient subspace differentiating the first-order equation once and using $K\times(K\times e)=K(K\cdot e)-|K|^2e$ together with $K\cdot\widehat E=0$ gives $\widehat E''+v^2|K|^2\widehat E=0$. Substitution of a harmonic coefficient $e\,e^{-i\omega t}$ gives $(v^2|K|^2-\omega^2)e=0$, so for $e\ne0$ and $\omega>0$ the stated dispersion relation follows with $\beta=2\pi\xi$; for $e=0$ it supplies no condition on $\omega$; at $K=0$ the direct equations $\widehat E'=-\gamma\widehat E$, $\widehat B'=0$ are retained without dividing by $K$. For $\sigma>0$ the electric equation is $\widehat E''+\gamma\widehat E'+v^2|K|^2\widehat E=0$ at $\lambda=|K|^2\ge0$ with the distinct/repeated-root alternatives of the finite-parameter classification [F5], including the zero-$K$ case; the zero-profile restrictions and individual cutoffs of the separated TE/TM candidates are unaffected, and no TE$_{00}$ mode is created by a scalar constant coefficient. [step 1.1, step 3.1, F4, F5, F6, algebra]

5.1 [Scope] Clause (a) is steps 1.1 and 2.1, clause (b) is step 3.1, and clause (c) is step 4.1. The theorem supplies the complete longitudinal $L^2$ expansion of the declared straight rectangular guide with its lossless and constant-Ohmic energy evolutions and makes no completeness claim for arbitrary non-rectangular, dispersive, nonlocal, nonlinear, rough-wall or open-end/junction problems; the weak energy result is proved by linear mixed-transform evolution and modal limits, not by applying a nonlinear smooth Poynting identity to rough data, and no signal-speed or empirical inference is made. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, F1] ∎
