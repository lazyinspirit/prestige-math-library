## Definition

Let $k$ be a field and let $G$ be an algebraic group over $k$, that is, a group scheme of finite type over $k$ ([[def-group-scheme-over-a-field]]), with multiplication $m$, inverse $i$ and identity section $e$.

The **commutator morphism** is
$$c:G\times_kG\to G,\qquad c(g,h)=g\,h\,g^{-1}h^{-1}.$$
It is a $k$-morphism: the map $(g,h)\mapsto(g,h,g^{-1},h^{-1})$ is built from $i$ and the universal property of the fibre product ([[def-fibre-product-schemes-universal-property]]), and $c$ is obtained from it by iterated multiplication. For every $k$-algebra $R$, the induced map $G(R)\times G(R)\to G(R)$ is the group commutator.

The **derived subgroup** $DG$ is the smallest closed subgroup scheme $H\subseteq G$ ([[def-morphism-and-closed-subgroup-scheme]]) through which $c$ factors, that is, such that $c$ equals the composite of a morphism $G\times_kG\to H$ with the inclusion $H\hookrightarrow G$. Equivalently, $DG$ is the smallest closed subgroup scheme of $G$ containing the scheme-theoretic image of $c$ ([[def-scheme-theoretic-image]], [[thm-scheme-theoretic-image-quasi-compact-morphism]]). It exists: if $G$ is affine, closed subgroup schemes correspond to Hopf ideals of $O(G)$ and $c$ corresponds to a $k$-algebra map $\Delta_c:O(G)\to O(G)\otimes_kO(G)$, and the Hopf ideals contained in $\ker\Delta_c$ are closed under sums, so their largest element cuts out $DG$; in general the closed subgroup schemes through which $c$ factors are closed under schematic intersection, their intersection is computed by the ideal sheaf generated by the defining ideal sheaves, and $G$ is Noetherian because it is of finite type over a field.

The **derived series** of $G$ is the sequence
$$D^0G=G,\qquad D^{i+1}G=D(D^iG)\quad(i\ge0),$$
so each $D^iG$ is a closed subgroup scheme of $G$ for $i\ge1$. The group $G$ is **solvable** if $D^nG=1$ for some $n\ge0$, where $1$ denotes the trivial subgroup scheme, the image of the identity section $e$.

A closed subgroup scheme $N\subseteq G$ is **normal** if it is stable under the conjugation action of $G$ on itself: the morphism $G\times_kN\to G$, $(g,n)\mapsto gng^{-1}$, factors through the inclusion $N\hookrightarrow G$. For $k$-algebras $R$ this says exactly that $N(R)$ is a normal subgroup of $G(R)$. This is the sense of normality used throughout this page.
