## Example

Let $W\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\lambda)$, $\lambda>0$, and put
$N^1_t=N^2_t=\mathbf1_{\{W\le t\}}$ with common natural filtration.
For each component use $Y^i_0:=1$ and
$Y^i_t:=\mathbf1_{\{t\le W\}}=1-(N^i)_{t-}$ for $0<t\le T$,
$A^i_t=\lambda(t\wedge W)$, and $M^i=N^i-A^i$. Both component contracts
hold, but the components jump together. For every $t>0$,
$$[M^1,M^2]_t=N_t,\qquad \langle M^1,M^2\rangle_t=A_t,$$
which are nonzero with positive probability. This is why the no-simultaneous-jump condition is required for zero cross brackets.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $\lambda>0$, a waiting time $W$ with density $f_W(w)=\lambda e^{-\lambda w}$ on $[0,\infty)$ and tail $P(W>w)=e^{-\lambda w}$, and the common filtration generated by the two identical one-event processes.

[F1] A unit-jump process with $A_t=\int_0^tY_s\lambda ds$ is in the model class when $N-A$ is a square-integrable martingale and $\mathbb E A_T<\infty$. ([[def-counting-process-intensity-and-compensator-contract]])

[F2] For that contract, $[M]_t=N_t$ and $\langle M\rangle_t=A_t$. ([[thm-compensated-counting-process-brackets]])

[F3] Optional cross brackets are jump-product sums; predictable cross brackets are their continuous predictable martingale compensators. ([[def-optional-and-predictable-brackets-for-counting-processes]])

[F4] Predictability is generated by time-zero sets and rectangles $(s,t]\times B$ with $B\in\mathcal F_s$; it is preserved under pointwise limits, products, and deterministic Borel time functions. ([[def-progressively-measurable-and-predictable-process]])

[A1] AC is inherited through conditional expectation in the model contract. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

## Verification

1.1 Each $N^i$ has one unit jump and $Y^i_0=1$. For $t>0$, $Y^i_t=1-(N^i)_{t-}$ is predictable by dyadic left-step approximation using [F4]; its time-zero value is predictable on the time-zero generator. Direct integration gives $A^i_t=\lambda(t\wedge W)$; left Riemann sums make it adapted, and continuous left-step approximations make it predictable by [F4]. The exponential density gives $\mathbb E A^i_T=\lambda(\int_0^T u\lambda e^{-\lambda u}du+Te^{-\lambda T})=1-e^{-\lambda T}<\infty$. If $W\le s$, the martingale increment is zero; if $W>s$, the residual waiting time $R=W-s$ is exponential and, for $h=t-s$, $\mathbb E[N_t-N_s\mid\mathcal F_s]=1-e^{-\lambda h}=\mathbb E[A_t-A_s\mid\mathcal F_s]$ on that event, since $\lambda(\int_0^h u\lambda e^{-\lambda u}du+he^{-\lambda h})=1-e^{-\lambda h}$. Hence each $M^i$ is an all-pairs martingale; $|M^i_t|\le1+\lambda T$ makes it square-integrable and verifies [F1]. [A1, F1, F4, given, construct]

2.1 The two component martingales are identical, so [F3] gives $[M^1,M^2]=[M,M]$. By [F2], $[M,M]=N$ and $\langle M,M\rangle=A$, hence the pair has the same cross-bracket values. For every $t>0$, $\mathbb P(N_t=1)=1-e^{-\lambda t}>0$ and $A_t=\lambda(t\wedge W)>0$ almost surely because $W>0$ almost surely; neither cross-bracket process is identically zero when simultaneous jumps are allowed. [A1, F2, F3, given, step 1.1] ∎

## Source notes

Tian states zero cross brackets under a no-simultaneous-jump hypothesis;
Andersen–Borgan–Gill–Keiding impose the same condition for multivariate
counting processes. This tied-process construction shows the boundary case
directly.
