## Statement

If $Q$ is quasisimple and $M\trianglelefteq Q$ is proper, then $M\le Z(Q)$. If $P$ is perfect, $T$ normalizes $P$, and every commutator $tpt^{-1}p^{-1}$ with $t\in T$ and $p\in P$ lies in $Z(P)$, then $T$ centralizes $P$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Groups $Q,P$, subgroups $M\trianglelefteq Q$ and $T$ as in the statement. A group is perfect when it equals its commutator subgroup.

[F1] Quasisimple means $Q=[Q,Q]$ and $Q/Z(Q)$ is simple. ([[def-quasisimple-group-component-and-layer]])

[F2] A simple group has only the trivial and whole normal subgroups. ([[def-simple-group]])

[F3] The center $Z(Q)$ commutes with every element of $Q$, and $[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$. ([[def-center-of-a-group]], [[def-commutator-and-commutator-subgroup]])

## Proof

1.1 The image $MZ(Q)/Z(Q)$ is normal in the simple group $Q/Z(Q)$, so it is trivial or the whole quotient. In the trivial case $M\le Z(Q)$. In the whole case $Q=MZ(Q)$, and the quotient $Q/M$ is generated by the image of the abelian group $Z(Q)$; hence $Q/M$ is abelian. But a quotient of the perfect group $Q=[Q,Q]$ is perfect, and an abelian perfect group is trivial. Thus $M=Q$, contrary to properness. Only the first case remains. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 Fix $t\in T$. Since $t$ normalizes $P$, the formula $c_t(p)=tpt^{-1}p^{-1}$ defines a function $P\to Z(P)$ by hypothesis. Its values are central, so $t(pq)t^{-1}=c_t(p)p\,c_t(q)q=c_t(p)c_t(q)pq$ and therefore $c_t(pq)=c_t(p)c_t(q)$. Thus $c_t$ is a homomorphism to the abelian group $Z(P)$. It kills every commutator of $P$, and $P=[P,P]$, so $c_t$ is trivial. Every $t$ consequently commutes with every $p$, as claimed. The argument also covers a trivial center or a trivial $T$. [F3, given, algebra] ∎
