## Statement

For noncritical $k$, $A(V^k(\mathfrak g))\cong U(\mathfrak g)$ via the current zero modes. Every induced restricted current representation $M_k(E)$ used in this identification is an actual admissible vertex-module, even for infinite-dimensional $E$. For the simple vacuum quotient $L(k\Lambda_0)=V^k/N_k$ one has $A(L(k\Lambda_0))\cong U(\mathfrak g)/I_k$ with the actual quotient top correspondence.

If $k+h^\vee=p/q$ satisfies the admissible numerator/gcd inequalities, the ideal $N_k$ is generated by a finite-$\mathfrak g$ highest state of type $E_{m\theta}$, $m=p-h^\vee+1$, in the coprime branch, or $E_{m\theta_s}$, $m=p-h+1$, in the coprincipal branch. Its Zhu ideal is generated by that horizontal orbit. Every dominant integral finite highest weight in the corresponding alcove gives an actual $L(k\Lambda_0)$ highest module. This descent is proved from the singular-state type and an $\operatorname{End}(E_\lambda)$ weight bound, without a KL translation theorem.

## Proof


1.1 **Restricted current representations give actual vertex modules.** Let E be any unital U(g)-module and induce $M_{k}(E)$ from g[t]+CK with positive current modes zero and $K=k$. PBW gives $U(g[t^{-1}]t^{-1})$ tensor E, graded by total negative loop degree. Its current fields $J_{x}(z)$ are lower truncated and satisfy `[J_x(z1),J_y(z2)] = J_[x,y](z2) z1^-1 delta(z2/z1) +k(x,y) partial_z2 z1^-1 delta(z2/z1).` [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, algebra]

2.1 Thus they are pairwise local. This Lie action is not simply assumed to be a VOA module. Close the fields under derivatives and the nth products `(A_n B)(z)=Res_w ((w-z)^n A(w)B(z)-(-z+w)^n B(z)A(w)),` [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 with the two specified binomial expansions; negative n give normal-ordered derivative products. These are fields by lower truncation. To check locality of such a product with C, multiply by powers clearing the three pairwise poles of A,B,C, commute C past both factors, and take the displayed residue; a power larger than the sum of the three pole orders plus |n|+1 kills the resulting commutator. This proves the needed closure, including negative products. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 For local A,B, multiply their two product expansions by a common clearing power of w-z. Taylor expansion at $w=z+x$ and coefficient extraction then gives the three-delta Jacobi identity with iterate $\sum _{n}(A_{n} B)(z)x^{-n-1}$. Every coefficient has finitely many rightmost modes on a fixed vector. This proves the action identity, rather than invoking it as a missing axiom. On the field space itself, identity field is the vacuum and the derivative is translation; the same residue identity gives its state-field structure. The affine current vacuum relations hold for the identity field, so the induced-vacuum universal property gives $V^k(g)\to$ this local-field vertex algebra. Current PBW induction shows it preserves all nth products. Evaluation of its fields on $M_{k}(E)$ is therefore an actual weak $V^{k}$-module. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Its negative-loop grading is admissible: current $x_{n}$ has degree -n, and derivatives/nth products give degree wt(a)-n-1 for every state mode. Grade zero is E, with x0 acting as the original x. This argument allows infinite-dimensional E, including the left regular U(g)-module. It supplies the actual modules needed for the Zhu injection below. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 **Sugawara and the Zhu algebra.** Noncriticality gives the already proved Sugawara modes. Their commutators with current modes make L0 the loop-degree operator on the vacuum, since it kills1 and $[L0,x_{n}]=-n$ $x_{n}$. Similarly L(-1) is the vertex translation, since both kill1 and have the same commutator on every current PBW word. Thus $V^{k}$ is the nonnegative CFT-type VOA with currents of weight1 used by the basic Zhu theorem. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 The classes x=[x(-1)1] generate $A(V^{k})$. For $n\ge 2$, the circle of $T^{n-2}x$ with v is $(n-1)!(x_{-n} v+x_{-n+1}v)$, so higher negative current modes reduce to $x_{-1}$. The star formula $[x_{-1} v]$=[x]*[v]-[x0 v] then reduces every PBW state by current length. Their star commutator is the finite Lie bracket: x*y-y*x equals [x,y]_-2 1+2[x,y]_-1 1, whose class is [x,y]_-1 1 since [Ta]=-[L0 a]. Consequently x↦[x(-1)1] gives a surjective algebra map $U(g)→A(V^{k})$. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 5.1, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 On the actual module $M_{k}(E)$ just constructed, zero modes on grade zero are the given g action. The basic Zhu action theorem therefore makes the composite $U(g)→A(V^{k})$ act on E exactly as its original action. Taking E=the left regular U(g)-module shows that the surjection is injective. Hence $A(V^{k})=U(g)$. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 5.1, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 If Nk is the supplied simple vacuum ideal, the quotient map sends $O(V^{k})$ onto $O(V^{k}/Nk)$, by the circle definition and lifting homogeneous states. Thus `A(V^k/Nk)=U(g)/Ik,` [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 5.1, step 8.1, algebra]

10.1 where Ik is the image of Nk. The complete $n=0$ induction/top correspondence applies to this quotient, not merely to $V^{k}$: an Ik-annihilated simple g-module E has a simple admissible quotient module with that actual top. Conversely every simple highest quotient module has such a top. Its current highest module is the corresponding affine L(lambda), because induced negative currents give its unique simple highest quotient. No invalid implication from vanishing on the top of a prechosen module to vanishing of all ideal modes is used. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 5.1, step 9.1, algebra]

11.1 **The singular generator and admissible finite tops.** Let lambda0=k Lambda0 and let the positive integral simple roots be the finite original simple roots alpha1,...,alphar together with beta0. In the coprime branch beta0=-theta+q delta and `m=<lambda0+rhohat,beta0-coroot>=p-hvee+1.` [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 5.1, step 10.1, algebra]

12.1 In the coprincipal branch $\beta_0=-\theta _{s}+(q/r)\delta$ and $m=p-h+1$. Both are positive integers. The arithmetic/root-arrangement identification is the one already proved in the shared arithmetic work; it is not being weakened to an unshifted integrability claim. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 5.1, step 10.1, step 11.1, algebra]

13.1 The degree-one term of the ordinary BGG resolution gives `L(lambda0)=M(lambda0)/sum_(beta integral simple) M(s_beta dot lambda0),` [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 5.1, step 10.1, step 12.1, algebra]

14.1 with actual embedded Verma submodules. On the other hand the universal affine vacuum module is `V^k(g)=M(lambda0)/sum_(i=1)^r M(s_i dot lambda0).` [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 5.1, step 10.1, step 13.1, algebra]

15.1 The latter is simply parabolic induction from the one-dimensional trivial finite-g module: the relations $f_{i}$ $v=0$ for all finite simple i make the horizontal highest module trivial; PBW then leaves exactly the negative-loop induced vacuum module. Therefore the maximal ideal Nk of $V^{k}$ is generated as an affine module by the image s of the beta0 singular vector. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 5.1, step 10.1, step 14.1, algebra]

16.1 That image is nonzero. The beta0 label and each finite simple label are distinct length-one integral Weyl elements. Strong linkage says no $M(s_{i} \mathbin{\cdot}  \lambda_0)$, i finite, contains a simple subquotient $L(s_{\beta_0} \mathbin{\cdot}  \lambda_0)$, because distinct length-one elements are incomparable in Bruhat order. If the beta0 highest vector belonged to their sum, its generated copy of $M(s_{\beta_0} \mathbin{\cdot}  \lambda_0)$ would give precisely that forbidden simple subquotient. Thus s survives. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 5.1, step 10.1, step 15.1, algebra]

17.1 Its finite-g weight is eta=m theta in the coprime branch, and eta=m $\theta _{s}$ in the coprincipal branch. The homogeneous grade containing s is finite dimensional and g-integrable: it is a sum of finite tensor powers of the adjoint representation in the current PBW model. Since eta is dominant and s is finite-g highest, its horizontal orbit is the irreducible finite module $E_{\eta }$. The full ideal Nk consists of negative-current descendants of that finite orbit; positive affine modes annihilate s and all PBW words can be reordered into negative modes and horizontal words. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 5.1, step 10.1, step 16.1, algebra]

18.1 Now use the locally proved affine Zhu identification $A(V^{k})=U(g)$. The image Ik of Nk in U(g) is the two-sided ideal generated by the image of $E_{\eta }$. Here is the needed ideal-generation calculation. For a weight-one current x, the circle of $T^{n-2}x$ with v is `(T^(n-2)x) circle v=(n-1)! (x_-n v+x_(-n+1)v),  n>=2.` [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 5.1, step 10.1, step 17.1, algebra]

19.1 Indeed derivative modes annihilate the terms $j=2,...,n-1$ in the circle sum, while $j=0,1$ have equal coefficient (n-1)!. Thus $[x_{-n} v]=-[x_{-n+1}v]$. Also the star identity gives $[x_{-1} v]$=[x]*[v]-[x0 v]. Repeatedly reduce every negative-current descendant by these two formulas. Horizontal zero modes act by adjoint commutator in U(g). This proves that the whole image Ik is generated by the horizontal orbit of [s], not just that this orbit is contained in Ik. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 5.1, step 10.1, step 18.1, algebra]

20.1 Let $E_{\lambda }$ be a finite-dimensional simple g-module of dominant integral highest weight lambdabar satisfying the admissible alcove inequality. In the coprime branch `lambdabar(theta-coroot)<=p-hvee=m-1;` [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 5.1, step 10.1, step 19.1, algebra]

21.1 in the coprincipal branch the corresponding inequality is $\bar\lambda (\theta_s^\vee)\le m-1$. These follow directly by evaluating regular dominance on beta0; all finite-root pairings are integral, so the integral root set is the vacuum one. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 5.1, step 10.1, step 20.1, algebra]

22.1 Write alpha for theta or $\theta _{s}$ according to the branch. Its coroot is dominant: alpha is the dominant root of its length orbit, so $\alpha _{i}(\alpha^\vee)\ge 0$ for every finite simple $\alpha _{i}$. Hence every weight of $E_{\lambda }$ has alpha-coroot value at most lambdabar(alpha-coroot). Reflection $s_{\alpha }$ makes the weight spectrum symmetric under sign for this coroot. Therefore every weight of $\operatorname{End}(E_{\lambda })$ has alpha-coroot value at most 2 $\bar\lambda (\alpha^\vee)\le 2m-2$. But $E_{m \alpha }$ contains a vector of alpha-coroot weight2m. There is consequently no nonzero g-map `E_(m alpha)→End(E_lambda).` [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 5.1, step 10.1, step 21.1, algebra]

23.1 The zero-mode action of the singular-state orbit is such a g-map. It must vanish. The ideal-generation calculation implies that the whole Ik annihilates $E_{\lambda }$. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 5.1, step 10.1, step 22.1, algebra]

24.1 Finally apply the actual $n=0$ Zhu induction/top correspondence to the quotient $V^{k}/Nk$: $E_{\lambda }$ is an A(L(k Lambda0))-module, so its simple admissible induced quotient exists as an actual L(k Lambda0)-module. Viewed as a $V^{k}$-module it is the simple affine highest module L(lambdabar+k Lambda0), because the current top is $E_{\lambda }$ and the induced negative-current module has that unique simple highest quotient. Thus every G-integrable admissible highest module in the vacuum alcove descends. The proof does not assume that killing the top alone kills every ideal mode on a prechosen module; it uses induction for the QUOTIENT and identifies its unique simple highest quotient. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 5.1, step 10.1, step 23.1, algebra]

25.1 The same argument also proves that Nk is the maximal vacuum ideal and supplies its single finite-g singular-generator type; both conclusions came from the proved BGG H0, not from a guessed singular-vector formula. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 5.1, step 10.1, step 24.1, algebra] ∎
