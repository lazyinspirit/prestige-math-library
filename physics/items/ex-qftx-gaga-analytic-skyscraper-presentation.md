## Example

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $N\ge1$, let $D\subset\mathbb C^N$ be a polydisc centered at the origin with coordinates $z_1,\ldots,z_N$, and let $\mathcal F$ be the cokernel sheaf of $\mu:\mathcal O_D^{\oplus N}\to\mathcal O_D$, $\mu(a_1,\ldots,a_N)=\sum_{i=1}^N a_i z_i$. The stalk of $\mathcal F$ is $\mathbb C$ at the origin and zero at every other point of $D$. The sheaf $\mathcal F$ is coherent, and the kernel of $\mu$ is generated on $D$ by the coordinate Koszul sections $z_j e_i-z_i e_j$ for $1\le i<j\le N$; for $N=1$ this generating family is empty.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; the integer $N\ge1$, the centered polydisc $D$, and its coordinate functions.

[A1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: AC is the theorem's adopted ambient assumption.

[F1] [[def-qftx-analytic-coherent-module]]: the quotient sheaf is the cokernel of $\mu$, and coherence is local finite type plus locally finite-type relation kernels.

[F2] [[thm-qftx-gaga-analytic-oka-coherence]]: the structure sheaf $\mathcal O_D$ is coherent.

[F3] [[lem-qftx-coherent-analytic-module-calculus]]: coherent modules are closed under finite extensions, kernels, and cokernels.

[F4] [[def-kernel-cokernel-image-sheaves]] and [[thm-exactness-of-sheaves-stalkwise]]: the cokernel and kernel sheaves have the stated stalks, and stalkwise equalities determine exact sheaf sequences.

[F6] [[def-stalk-of-presheaf]]: a stalk consists of germs of sections on neighborhoods of the point.

[F5] [[thm-power-series-expansion-in-several-complex-variables]] and [[cor-uniqueness-of-multivariable-power-series-coefficients]]: holomorphic germs have convergent power series with unique coefficients; divisibility by a coordinate follows when all terms with its exponent zero vanish.

## Verification

1.1 If $y\in D$ is not the origin, choose an index $k$ with $z_k(y)\ne0$. Then $z_k$ is a unit in the germ ring $\mathcal O_{D,y}$, so the ideal generated by the coordinates is the whole stalk and $\mathcal F_y=0$. [F4, F6, given, choose]

1.2 At the origin, evaluation $\operatorname{ev}_0:\mathcal O_{D,0}\to\mathbb C$ is surjective. Its kernel is generated by $z_1,\ldots,z_N$: if $f(0)=0$, its convergent power series has no constant term, and grouping each nonconstant monomial according to the first coordinate with positive exponent writes $f=\sum_i z_i g_i$ with convergent germs $g_i$; each of the finitely many grouped subseries remains convergent on a smaller polydisc. Conversely every such sum vanishes at $0$. Hence $\mathcal F_0\cong\mathbb C$. [F4, F5, F6, algebra]

1.3 At a point $y\ne0$, choose $k$ with $z_k(y)\ne0$. If $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_N)$ is a relation germ in the stalk from [F6], then $a_k=-\sum_{i\ne k}a_i z_i/z_k$, so
$$
a=\sum_{i\ne k}(a_i/z_k)(z_ke_i-z_ie_k).
$$
Each vector $z_ke_i-z_ie_k$ is one of the stated pairwise sections or its negative. Thus those sections generate the relation stalk at every nonzero point, including when $N=1$, where the sum is empty. [F1, F6, given, algebra]

1.4 Let $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_N)$ be a relation germ at the origin, so $\sum_i a_i z_i=0$. We prove by induction on $N$ that it is generated by the stated pairwise sections. For $N=1$, $a_1z_1=0$ and the germ ring is a domain by [F5], so $a_1=0$. For $N>1$, restrict to $z_N=0$; the first $N-1$ coefficients form a relation among $z_1,\ldots,z_{N-1}$. By induction subtract a combination of the pairwise sections with both indices below $N$, extending their coefficient germs independently of $z_N$. The resulting first $N-1$ coefficients vanish at $z_N=0$. [F5, F6, given, construct]

2.1 By [F5], each remaining coefficient has a convergent series divisible by $z_N$, so write $a_i=z_Nc_i$ for $i<N$. The relation becomes $z_N(\sum_{i<N}c_i z_i+a_N)=0$. Since the germ ring is a domain, $a_N=-\sum_{i<N}c_i z_i$, and the remaining vector is $\sum_{i<N}c_i(z_Ne_i-z_i e_N)$, a combination of the stated pairwise sections. [F5, F6, step 1.4, algebra]

3.1 At every point, steps 1.3–2.1 show that the pairwise sections generate the kernel stalk. Therefore [F4] identifies their generated subsheaf with $\ker\mu$. The quotient sequence is exact by the definition of $\mathcal F$ as a cokernel. [F1, F4, F6, step 1.3, step 2.1]

4.1 Under the assumed Axiom of Choice [A1], [F2] makes $\mathcal O_D$ coherent. Repeatedly applying the finite-extension case of [F3] to the split sequences $0\to\mathcal O_D^{\oplus(j-1)}\to\mathcal O_D^{\oplus j}\to\mathcal O_D\to0$ shows that $\mathcal O_D^{\oplus N}$ is coherent. The cokernel case of [F3] applied to $\mu$ then makes $\mathcal F$ coherent. [A1, F2, F3, step 3.1] ∎
