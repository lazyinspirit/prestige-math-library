## Statement

Assume Countable Choice ($\mathrm{AC}_\omega$) as carried by [[lem-em-outgoing-source-helmholtz-and-diffraction]] and [[lem-em-helmholtz-boundary-representation]] ([[def-countable-choice]]), whose trace qualification is used in clause (d); the full-Choice modal branch of the latter is not used. Adopt the scalar aperture model and its notation from [[post-em-scalar-aperture-model]]: frequency $\omega>0$, $k=\omega/c$, aperture radius $a>0$, finite complex aperture distribution $\alpha$ supported in the planar aperture $S\subset\{y_3=0\}\cap\{y:|y|\le a\}$, model field $u=G_k*\alpha$, real field $U(x,t)=\operatorname{Re}(u(x)e^{-i\omega t})$ and $T=2\pi/\omega$ ([[def-em-si-conventions]], [[def-em-lab-geometry]], [[def-complex-exponential]], [[def-complex-conjugate-real-imaginary-part-and-modulus]], [[def-integration-against-a-signed-or-complex-measure]]). Set $I(x):=(1/2)|u(x)|^2$. Then: (a) [Model field and Fraunhofer estimate] $u$ is $C^\infty$ on the half-space $\{x_3>0\}$ and on $\mathbb R^3\setminus S$, satisfies $(\Delta+k^2)u=0$ there, and is outgoing in the sense of [[lem-em-outgoing-source-helmholtz-and-diffraction]]: for $x=rn$ with $r\ge2a$ and $n\in S^2$, $$u(rn)=\frac{e^{ikr}}{4\pi r}\int e^{-ikn\cdot y}\,d\alpha(y)+O\Bigl(\frac{\|\alpha\|_{\rm TV}}{r}\Bigl[\frac ar+\frac{ka^2}{r}\Bigr]\Bigr)$$ uniformly in direction, and $r(\partial_ru-iku)=O(r^{-1})$ uniformly. (b) [Fresnel estimate] write $y_t=(y_1,y_2)$ and $\xi=(\xi_1,\xi_2)$ for transverse vectors; for the planar aperture and observation $x=(\xi_1,\xi_2,z)$ with $z>0$, $b\ge0$, $|\xi|\le b$ and any declared fixed small-angle range $(a+b)/z\le\eta_0<1$, where $0<\eta_0<1$, the Fresnel approximation replaces $R=|x-y|$ by $z+|\xi-y_t|^2/(2z)$ and $1/R$ by $1/z$ in the model integral with phase error $O(k(a+b)^4/z^3)$, relative amplitude error $O((a+b)^2/z^2)$ and absolute field error $O(\|\alpha\|_{\rm TV}z^{-1}[(a+b)^2/z^2+k(a+b)^4/z^3])$. (c) [Intensity and error transfer] at every $x$ with $x_3>0$ the model period average satisfies $\frac1T\int_0^TU(x,s)^2\,ds=I(x)$ ([[lem-em-period-average]]). If $u_{\rm approx}$ is any approximation of $u$ with $|u_{\rm approx}(x)-u(x)|\le\delta(x)$, then $|\frac12|u_{\rm approx}(x)|^2-I(x)|\le\frac12\delta(x)(|u(x)|+|u_{\rm approx}(x)|)$; if moreover $u(x)\ne0$ and $|u_{\rm approx}(x)-u(x)|\le\epsilon(x)|u(x)|$, the relative intensity error is at most $2\epsilon(x)+\epsilon(x)^2$. In particular the Fraunhofer and Fresnel field errors of (a) and (b) bound the corresponding intensity errors; no new approximation is introduced by passing to $I$. (d) [Trace and scope] the aperture distribution $\alpha$ is an adopted model datum and is not identified with the exact Maxwell boundary data on the screen; assigning both $u$ and $\partial_nu$ on the aperture independently does not produce a well-posed exact boundary problem ([[lem-em-helmholtz-boundary-representation]]). The construction ignores polarization and aperture back-reaction, makes no claim that $u$ or $U$ satisfies the Maxwell equations exactly, and makes no measured-intensity, apparatus, sampling or empirical claim; only comparisons of $I$ between observation points are read as model predictions.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The model data of the Statement: $\omega>0$, $k=\omega/c$, $a>0$, the compact planar aperture $S\subset\{y_3=0\}\cap\{|y|\le a\}$, the finite complex measure $\alpha$ supported in $S$, the model field $u=G_k*\alpha$ with $G_k(z)=e^{ik|z|}/(4\pi|z|)$ for $z\ne0$, the real field $U(x,t)=\operatorname{Re}(u(x)e^{-i\omega t})$, and $I=\frac12|u|^2$. All lengths are in the laboratory chart of [[def-em-lab-geometry]], and $c$, $\varepsilon_0$, $\mu_0$ are those of [[def-em-si-conventions]].

[F1] Model interface: the aperture distribution is a finite complex measure with total variation $\|\alpha\|_{\rm TV}$ supported in the compact $S$, read in volts; for every $x$ with $x_3>0$ the pairing $\int G_k(x-y)\,d\alpha(y)$ is defined by [[def-integration-against-a-signed-or-complex-measure]] and equals the model field $u(x)$; the same formula defines $u$ at every $x\notin S$, and $U$, $I$ are as in the Statement ([[post-em-scalar-aperture-model]], [[def-total-variation-of-a-signed-or-complex-measure]], [[def-em-lab-geometry]], [[def-em-si-conventions]]).

[F2] Outgoing-source and trace suppliers: assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$; $G_k$ is locally integrable, $(-\Delta-k^2)G_k=\delta_0$ distributionally, and for a finite complex measure supported in a closed ball the field $G_k*\alpha$ is $C^\infty$ outside its support, solves $(\Delta+k^2)w=0$ there, is outgoing with the Fraunhofer and Fresnel error terms of that item, and the aperture data are an explicitly specified scalar source, not the exact Maxwell boundary traces; assigning both aperture values independently is not a well-posed exact boundary problem ([[lem-em-outgoing-source-helmholtz-and-diffraction]], [[lem-em-helmholtz-boundary-representation]], [[def-countable-choice]]).

[F3] Finite-measure integral bound: let $h$ be Borel measurable and bounded on the support of the finite complex measure $\alpha$, with $M_h:=\sup_{\operatorname{supp}\alpha}|h|<+\infty$; then $h\in L^1(\alpha)=L^1(|\alpha|)$ and $\bigl|\int h\,d\alpha\bigr|\le\int|h|\,d|\alpha|\le M_h\|\alpha\|_{\rm TV}$: the modulus bound for a general integrand is [[thm-integrals-against-signed-or-complex-measures-are-bounded-by-total-variation]], integrability in $L^1(\alpha)=L^1(|\alpha|)$ is the definition of [[def-integration-against-a-signed-or-complex-measure]], and since $\alpha$ is supported in $S$ the variation $|\alpha|$ vanishes off $S$ by its defining supremum over partitions ([[def-total-variation-of-a-signed-or-complex-measure]]) and is finite ([[thm-total-variation-is-a-measure]], [[thm-total-variation-of-a-complex-measure-is-finite]]), so monotonicity of the integral of nonnegative measurable functions gives $\int|h|\,d|\alpha|\le\int M_h\,d|\alpha|=M_h\|\alpha\|_{\rm TV}$ ([[prop-order-and-scalar-rules-for-the-nonnegative-integral]]).

[F4] Period average: for $\omega>0$, $T=2\pi/\omega$ and $u\in\mathbb C$, $\frac1T\int_0^T\operatorname{Re}(ue^{-i\omega t})^2\,dt=\frac12|u|^2$ ([[lem-em-period-average]]).

[F5] Modulus and conjugation: $z\overline z=|z|^2$, $|z|\ge0$, $|zw|=|z||w|$, and $\bigl||z|-|w|\bigr|\le|z-w|$ for all $z,w\in\mathbb C$ ([[lem-complex-conjugation-and-modulus-laws]], [[def-complex-conjugate-real-imaginary-part-and-modulus]]).

[F6] Elementary toolbox: $|a+bi|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$ and $e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta$ for real $\theta$, with the addition law $e^{z+w}=e^ze^w$ ([[thm-complex-exponential-addition-and-real-extension]]), $\sin'=\cos$, $\cos'=-\sin$, $|\cos\theta|\le1$ and $\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta=1$ ([[def-complex-conjugate-real-imaginary-part-and-modulus]], [[cor-complex-exponential-cartesian-form-modulus-and-eulers-identity]], [[thm-sine-and-cosine-derivatives]], [[cor-trigonometric-parity-and-pythagorean-identity]]); the Taylor remainder bound $|R_{n,a}f(x)|\le Ms^{n+1}/(n+1)!$ when $|f^{(n+1)}|\le M$ on the interval, with $R_{n,a}f=f-T_{n,a}f$ ([[cor-taylor-remainder-bound]], [[def-taylor-polynomial-and-remainder]]); the real-power derivatives $(x^\alpha)'=\alpha x^{\alpha-1}$ on $(0,\infty)$ ([[thm-real-power-continuity-and-derivatives]]); the chain and algebra of derivatives ([[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]], [[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]]); the Euclidean norm and inner product with $\sqrt{\ }$ the nonnegative square root ([[def-euclidean-inner-product]], [[thm-of-square-roots]], [[def-complex-exponential]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; instantiate the supplier's off-support regularity on the declared closed ball, re-derive the sharp closed-radius Fraunhofer and outgoing estimates from the explicit kernel with elementary Taylor bounds, establish the Fresnel estimates through the exact substitution $R=z\sqrt{1+s}$, and transfer the field errors to the intensity with the modulus inequalities.

1.1 [Off-support regularity and the Helmholtz equation] Since $S$ is compact and contained in the closed ball $\overline{B_a(0)}$, $\alpha$ is a finite complex measure supported in that closed ball, exactly as required by [F2]. By [F2] the field $G_k*\alpha$ is $C^\infty$ outside the support of $\alpha$ and solves $(\Delta+k^2)w=0$ there; since the support of $\alpha$ is contained in $S$, the field $u$ of [F1] is $C^\infty$ on $\mathbb R^3\setminus S$ and satisfies $(\Delta+k^2)u=0$ there; the half-space $\{x_3>0\}$ is disjoint from the plane containing $S$, hence is contained in $\mathbb R^3\setminus S$, so $u$ is $C^\infty$ there as well. [given, F1, F2]

1.2 [Elementary bounds: phase, radial expansion, kernel residual] First, for every real $\theta$ one has $|e^{i\theta}-1|\le|\theta|$: by [F6], $2(1-\cos\theta)=|e^{i\theta}-1|^2$ and $|\cos\theta|\le1$, and the Taylor bound for $\cos$ at $0$ with $n=1$ gives $|\cos\theta-1|\le\theta^2/2$, because $|\cos''|\le1$; hence $|e^{i\theta}-1|^2=2(1-\cos\theta)\le\theta^2$. Second, fix $y\in S$, $r\ge2a$, $n\in S^2$, and put $R:=|rn-y|$ and $h(s):=|rn-sy|$ for $s\in[0,1]$; then $h$ is $C^2$ on $[0,1]$ with $h(0)=r$, $h(1)=R$, $h'(0)=-n\cdot y$, $h'(s)=-(rn-sy)\cdot y/h(s)$, $h''(s)=|y|^2/h(s)-((rn-sy)\cdot y)^2/h(s)^3$, so $|h''|\le2|y|^2/h(s)\le2a^2/(r-a)\le4a^2/r$ by $h(s)\ge r-a\ge r/2$; the Taylor bound at $0$ with $n=1$ gives $|R-r+n\cdot y|\le2a^2/r$, and then $|r/R-1|=|r-R|/R\le(a+2a^2/r)/(r/2)=2a/r+4a^2/r^2\le4a/r$ since $|r-R|\le|n\cdot y|+2a^2/r\le a+a=2a$, $R\ge r/2$, and $r\ge2a$ gives $4a^2/r^2\le2a/r$. Third, with $R$ as above, $\partial_rR=(r-n\cdot y)/R$ satisfies $|\partial_rR|\le1$ and $|\partial_rR-1|\le|R-r+n\cdot y|/R\le4a^2/r^2$, and the exact kernel derivatives give $\partial_rG_k(rn-y)=\partial_rR\,(ik-1/R)\,e^{ikR}/(4\pi R)$ and hence $|\partial_rG_k-ikG_k|\le(1/(4\pi R))\,[\,k|\partial_rR-1|+|\partial_rR|/R\,]\le2ka^2/(\pi r^3)+1/(\pi r^2)$. [given, F6, algebra]

1.3 [Fresnel substitution and estimates] Fix the planar observation $x=(\xi_1,\xi_2,z)$ with $z>0$, $|\xi|\le b$, and let $y\in S$ with transverse part $y_t$; put $d_0:=a+b$, $s:=|\xi-y_t|^2/z^2$, so that $0\le s\le\eta_0^2<1$ on the declared range. Decomposing the Euclidean norm by [F6], $R=|x-y|=z\sqrt{1+s}$, hence $1/R=(1/z)(1+s)^{-1/2}$ and the Fresnel replacements are $R_F:=z(1+s/2)$ and $1/z$. The Taylor bound of [F6] at $0$ with $n=1$ gives $|\sqrt{1+s}-1-s/2|\le s^2/8$, because $|(d^2/dt^2)\sqrt{1+t}|=|\!-\!\frac14(1+t)^{-3/2}|\le1/4$ on $[0,s]$; the Taylor bound with $n=0$ gives $|(1+s)^{-1/2}-1|\le s/2$, because $|(d/dt)(1+t)^{-1/2}|=\frac12(1+t)^{-3/2}\le1/2$. Consequently the phase error satisfies $k|R-R_F|=kz|\sqrt{1+s}-1-s/2|\le kd_0^4/(8z^3)$, and the relative amplitude error satisfies $|1-(1+s)^{-1/2}|\le s/2\le d_0^2/(2z^2)$, where $s\le d_0^2/z^2$ by $|\xi-y_t|\le d_0$. [given, F1, F6, algebra]

1.4 [Period average equals the model intensity] Fix $x$ with $x_3>0$ and write $u=u(x)\in\mathbb C$; then $U(x,s)=\operatorname{Re}(ue^{-i\omega s})$, so [F4] applied with the complex number $u$ in both slots gives $\frac1T\int_0^TU(x,s)^2\,ds=\frac12|u|^2=I(x)$. [given, F4, algebra]

2.1 [Fraunhofer estimate at the closed radius] Fix $y\in S$, $r\ge2a$ and $n\in S^2$, and write $R=|rn-y|$. Using only the bounds of step 1.2 and the addition law of the exponential for $e^{ik(r-n\cdot y)}=e^{ikr}e^{-ikn\cdot y}$ from [F6], one has $\bigl|G_k(rn-y)-e^{ikr}e^{-ikn\cdot y}/(4\pi r)\bigr|=\bigl|e^{ikR}(r/R-1)+e^{ikR}-e^{ik(r-n\cdot y)}\bigr|/(4\pi r)\le(1/(4\pi r))\,[\,4a/r+k\cdot2a^2/r\,]\le(1/(\pi r))\,[\,a/r+ka^2/r\,]$, since $4/(4\pi)=1/\pi$ and $2/(4\pi)\le1/\pi$, and because the middle term was bounded by $|e^{i\theta}-1|\le|\theta|$ with $\theta:=k(R-r+n\cdot y)$ and $|\theta|\le2ka^2/r$. Integrating against $\alpha$ with the bound of [F3], applied to the bounded continuous function $y\mapsto G_k(rn-y)-e^{ikr}e^{-ikn\cdot y}/(4\pi r)$ on the compact support, gives $|u(rn)-e^{ikr}(4\pi r)^{-1}\int e^{-ikn\cdot y}\,d\alpha(y)|\le\|\alpha\|_{\rm TV}(\pi r)^{-1}[a/r+ka^2/r]$, uniformly in $n$; this is the displayed Fraunhofer estimate with a universal constant. [step 1.2, F1, F2, F3, F6, algebra]

2.2 [Absolute field error of the Fresnel replacement] With the substitutions of step 1.3, for $y\in S$: $\bigl|G_k(x-y)-e^{ikR_F}/(4\pi z)\bigr|\le(1/(4\pi))\,|1/R-1/z|+(1/(4\pi z))\,|e^{ikR}-e^{ikR_F}|\le(1/(4\pi))\,d_0^2/(2z^3)+(1/(4\pi z))\,k\,d_0^4/(8z^3)=(1/(4\pi z))\,[\,d_0^2/(2z^2)+kd_0^4/(8z^3)\,]$, where the first summand used $|1/R-1/z|=(1/z)|(1+s)^{-1/2}-1|\le d_0^2/(2z^3)$ and the second used $|e^{ikR}-e^{ikR_F}|\le k|R-R_F|\le kd_0^4/(8z^3)$ from the phase estimate of step 1.3. Integrating against $\alpha$ with [F3] gives $|u(x)-u_{\rm Fresnel}(x)|\le\|\alpha\|_{\rm TV}(4\pi z)^{-1}[d_0^2/(2z^2)+kd_0^4/(8z^3)]$, the displayed absolute field error with universal constants; the phase and relative amplitude errors of step 1.3 are those displayed in (b). [step 1.3, F1, F2, F3, F6, algebra]

3.1 [Outgoing residual] With the third bound of step 1.2 and the same integration against the total variation [F3], $r|\partial_ru(rn)-iku(rn)|\le r\int|\partial_rG_k(rn-y)-ikG_k(rn-y)|\,d|\alpha|(y)\le\|\alpha\|_{\rm TV}\,[\,2ka^2/(\pi r^2)+1/(\pi r)\,]$, which is uniformly $O(\|\alpha\|_{\rm TV}r^{-1})$ in direction; this and step 2.1 give clause (a). [step 1.2, step 2.1, F1, F2, F3, algebra]

3.2 [Intensity error transfer] Let $u_{\rm approx}$ satisfy $|u_{\rm approx}(x)-u(x)|\le\delta(x)$ at the point $x$. By the reverse triangle inequality of [F5], $\bigl||u_{\rm approx}|-|u|\bigr|\le|u_{\rm approx}-u|\le\delta$, and by $|z|^2=z\overline z$ and multiplicativity of the modulus [F5], $\bigl||u_{\rm approx}|^2-|u|^2\bigr|=\bigl||u_{\rm approx}|-|u|\bigr|\,(|u_{\rm approx}|+|u|)\le\delta(x)(|u(x)|+|u_{\rm approx}(x)|)$; multiplying by $\frac12$ and comparing with step 1.4 gives the first bound of (c). If additionally $u(x)\ne0$ and $|u_{\rm approx}-u|\le\epsilon(x)|u(x)|$, then $|u_{\rm approx}|\le(1+\epsilon(x))|u(x)|$, so the absolute intensity error is at most $\frac12\epsilon(x)|u(x)|\,(2+\epsilon(x))|u(x)|=\bigl(\epsilon(x)+\frac12\epsilon(x)^2\bigr)|u(x)|^2=(2\epsilon(x)+\epsilon(x)^2)I(x)$, which is the stated relative bound; applying these two estimates to the Fraunhofer and Fresnel field errors of steps 2.1, 3.1 and 2.2 gives the final sentence of (c) with no new approximation. [step 1.4, step 2.1, step 2.2, F5, algebra]

4.1 [Trace caveat, scope and choice accounting] The aperture distribution $\alpha$ is the adopted datum of [F1] and is not identified with the exact Maxwell boundary data on the screen; by [F2] assigning both $u$ and $\partial_nu$ on the aperture independently is not a well-posed exact boundary problem, so no such identification or well-posedness is made. The construction never asserts that $u$ or $U$ satisfies the Maxwell equations; it ignores polarization and aperture back-reaction, and it makes no measured-intensity, apparatus, sampling or empirical claim, only model comparisons of $I$ between observation points; this is clause (d). The only choice principle used is the Countable Choice carried by the two suppliers of [F2] and used in their trace and kernel interfaces; the period average of step 1.4 uses only the choice-free period-average lemma, and all other steps are explicit estimates and finite computations, so no further choice is invoked. [step 3.1, step 2.2, step 3.2, F1, F2, F4, given] ∎

## Remarks

- The two big-O statements of (a) are established by the proof in the quantitative form $|u(rn)-e^{ikr}(4\pi r)^{-1}\int e^{-ikn\cdot y}d\alpha|\le\|\alpha\|_{\rm TV}(\pi r)^{-1}[a/r+ka^2/r]$ and $r|\partial_ru-iku|\le\|\alpha\|_{\rm TV}(2ka^2/(\pi r^2)+1/(\pi r))$, both uniform in direction; the big-O form is the same statement with a universal constant.
- The Fresnel error transfer is an error statement about the adopted model field, not about any measured optical intensity; the model comparison is between observation points of $I=\frac12|u|^2$ only.
