## Statement refuted

The implication “a generated $\mathbb Z$-module is free” is false. For every integer $n>1$, the module $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$ is generated by $1+n\mathbb Z$ but is not free over $\mathbb Z$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $n>1$ and the $\mathbb Z$-module $M=\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$.

[F1] A free module on a set $X$ consists of uniquely represented finite linear combinations of its standard basis vectors; the empty basis gives the zero module ([[def-free-module-on-a-set-and-standard-basis]]).

[F2] A module is generated by a subset when every element is a finite linear combination of that subset ([[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]]).

[F3] In $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$, the coset operations are induced from $\mathbb Z$ ([[def-quotient-module]]).

## Counterexample

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Every class $k+n\mathbb Z$ equals $k(1+n\mathbb Z)$, so $M$ is generated by one element according to [F2]. [F2, F3]

1.2 Every element of $M$ is killed by the nonzero integer $n$. [given, F3, algebra]

2.1 If $M$ were free on $X$, it would be nonzero because $n>1$, so [F1] would force $X\ne\varnothing$; choose $x\in X$. The standard vector $e_x$ is not killed by $n$, since its $x$-coordinate is the nonzero integer $n$, contradicting step 1.2. [step 1.2, F1, choose]

3.1 Thus $M$ is generated but not free. At the excluded boundary $n=1$, $\mathbb Z/\mathbb Z=0$, which is free on the empty basis by [F1]. [step 1.1, step 2.1, F1] ∎
