## Example

Let $k$ be a field.

1. If $E/k$ is a finite separable extension, then $\Omega_{E/k}=0$.
2. Suppose $\operatorname{char}k=p>0$ and $a\in k\smallsetminus k^{p}$. Put
   $L=k[t]/(t^{p}-a)$ and let $t$ also denote the class of the variable. Then
   $L$ is a field and $\Omega_{L/k}\cong L\cdot\mathrm dt\cong L$, a
   one-dimensional $L$-vector space: a field extension with nonzero module of
   differentials, necessarily not separable.

Both computations are quotient computations in one variable; no separability of
$L/k$ is available in the second case, and none is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$, for clause 1 a finite separable extension $E/k$, and for clause 2 a prime $p=\operatorname{char}k$ and an element $a\in k\smallsetminus k^{p}$.

[F1] [[thm-primitive-element-theorem-for-finite-separable-extensions]]: if $E=F(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_r)$ is finite and all but possibly one of the generators are separable over $F$, then $E/F$ is simple; in particular every finite separable extension is simple.

[F2] [[thm-evaluation-kernel-and-minimal-polynomial]]: for $\alpha$ algebraic over a field $F$ the evaluation map $F[X]\to F(\alpha)$ has kernel generated by the monic minimal polynomial $P$ of $\alpha$, so $F(\alpha)\cong F[X]/(P)$, and $f(\alpha)=0$ implies $P\mid f$.

[F3] [[lem-ag-polynomial-quotient-differentials]]: for $B=P/I$ with $P=A[x]$ and $I=(f)$ the module $\Omega_{B/A}$ is the cokernel of multiplication by $f'$ on $B$, that is $\Omega_{B/A}\cong B/(f')$ with generator $\mathrm dx$; the conormal map need not be injective.

[F4] [[def-repeated-root-and-separable-polynomial]], [[thm-polynomial-is-separable-iff-coprime-to-its-derivative]]: a nonzero polynomial $f$ over a field is separable exactly when $\gcd(f,f')=1$, equivalently when $f$ and $f'$ have no common root in any extension field; a separable polynomial of degree $d$ has $d$ distinct roots in a splitting field.

[F5] [[lem-polynomial-factorisation-into-irreducibles]], [[thm-polynomial-quotient-is-a-field-iff-irreducible]]: a nonzero nonunit polynomial over a field is a product of irreducibles, so $t^{p}-a$ has a monic irreducible factor $Q$, and $k[t]/(Q)$ is a field.

[F6] [[thm-binomial-theorem-over-a-commutative-ring]], [[lem-prime-divides-intermediate-binomial-coefficients]]: in characteristic $p$ the binomial coefficients $\binom{p}{i}$ for $0<i<p$ are divisible by $p$, so $(X+Y)^{p}=X^{p}+Y^{p}$ in every commutative ring of characteristic $p$; iterating, and deriving $t^{p}-a$ termwise, gives $(t^{p}-a)'=pt^{p-1}=0$.



## Proof

1.1 The separable case. By [F1] write $E=k(\alpha)$, and let $P\in k[X]$ be the monic minimal polynomial of $\alpha$, so that $E\cong k[X]/(P)$ by [F2]. Since $E/k$ is separable the element $\alpha$ is separable, so $P$ is separable and $\gcd(P,P')=1$ by [F4]; as $P(\alpha)=0$ and $P'(\alpha)=0$ would force $P\mid P'$ by [F2], impossible for $0\ne P'$ of degree less than $\deg P$, we get $P'(\alpha)\ne0$. By [F3] applied to the presentation $E=k[X]/(P)$ the module $\Omega_{E/k}$ is $E/(P'(\alpha))$, and $P'(\alpha)\ne0$ is a unit of the field $E$, so $\Omega_{E/k}=0$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, algebra]

1.2 The polynomial $t^{p}-a$ is irreducible. Let $Q$ be a monic irreducible factor of $t^{p}-a$ [F5] and put $F=k[t]/(Q)$, a field with class $x$ of $t$ satisfying $x^{p}=a$ [F5]. By [F6] the identity $(t-x)^{p}=t^{p}-x^{p}=t^{p}-a$ holds in $F[t]$, so every root of $Q$ in a splitting field is a root of $(t-x)^{p}$, hence equals $x$: the polynomial $Q$ has exactly one distinct root. If $\deg Q=1$ then $x\in k$ and $a=x^{p}\in k^{p}$, contrary to the hypothesis, so $\deg Q\ge2$; and $\deg Q\ge2$ with $Q'\ne0$ would make $Q$ separable with $\deg Q$ distinct roots by [F4], a contradiction. Hence $Q'=0$, which in characteristic $p$ means that $Q$ is a polynomial in $t^{p}$ and, since $0\ne Q$ has degree at most $p$, forces $Q=t^{p}-a$; so $t^{p}-a$ is irreducible, $L=k[t]/(t^{p}-a)$ is a field by [F5], and $t\in L$ satisfies $t^{p}=a\notin k^{p}$ hence $t\notin k$. [F4, F5, F6, algebra]

2.1 The differentials of the inseparable field. By [F3] applied to the single equation $t^{p}-a$ with $A=k$ and $B=L$, and by the derivative computation $P'(t)=pt^{p-1}=0$ of [F6], the module is $\Omega_{L/k}=L/(0)=L$, generated by $\mathrm dt$; explicitly $\Omega_{L/k}\cong L\cdot\mathrm dt$ is one-dimensional over the field $L$ and nonzero. Since $t^{p}=a$ with $a\in k\smallsetminus k^{p}$, the element $t$ is not separable over $k$, so this is a field extension with a nonzero differential module, in contrast with clause 1. [F3, F6, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
