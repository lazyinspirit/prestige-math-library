## Statement

Assume AC (inherited through the identification of the reduced matrices with
the topological representation). Let $\Delta=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1$ be the
half twist of $B_3$ and let
$\bar\rho_3:B_3\to\operatorname{GL}_2(\Lambda_1)$ be the reduced Burau
representation. Evaluate the matrices of
[[thm-topological-and-matrix-burau-representations-agree]] at $t=-1$ and call
the resulting homomorphism
$\bar\rho_3(-1):B_3\to\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)$; in the basis
$(g_1,g_2)$ it sends
$$\sigma_1\longmapsto U=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad \sigma_2\longmapsto V=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.$$
Then $U,V$ generate $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$; the group with presentation
$\langle U,V\mid UVU=VUV,\ (UVU)^4=1\rangle$ is isomorphic to
$\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$; the map $\bar\rho_3(-1)$ is surjective with
image $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$; and its kernel is
$$\ker\bar\rho_3(-1)=\langle\Delta^4\rangle=\{\Delta^{4k}:k\in\mathbb Z\},$$
the infinite cyclic central subgroup generated by $\Delta^4$. Equivalently, the
only braid-group relation added to the Artin presentation of $B_3$ by the
specialization is $\Delta^4=1$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; the reduced representation $\bar\rho_3$ in the basis $(g_1,g_2)$; the entrywise evaluation $\Lambda_1\to\mathbb Z$, $t\mapsto-1$; the matrices $U,V$ above; the braid group $B_3=\langle\sigma_1,\sigma_2\mid\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1=\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2\rangle$; the half twist $\Delta=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1$.

[F1] In the basis $(g_1,g_2)$, $\bar\rho_3(\sigma_1)=\begin{pmatrix}-t&t\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ and $\bar\rho_3(\sigma_2)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&-t\end{pmatrix}$; a homomorphism into $\operatorname{GL}_2(\Lambda_1)$ composes with the ring homomorphism $\Lambda_1\to\mathbb Z$, $t\mapsto-1$, to give a homomorphism into $\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ ([[thm-topological-and-matrix-burau-representations-agree]], [[def-reduced-burau-representation]], [[def-the-laurent-polynomial-ring]]).

[F2] Put $S=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ and $T=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$. By definition, the quotient $q:\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)\to\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ has kernel $\{\pm I\}=\langle S^2\rangle$.

[F3] Let $C_4=\langle a\mid a^4=1\rangle$, $C_6=\langle b\mid b^6=1\rangle$ and let $C_4\ast_{C_2}C_6$ be the amalgam identifying the order-two subgroup $\langle a^2\rangle$ with the order-two subgroup $\langle b^3\rangle$. Then $C_4\ast_{C_2}C_6$ has the presentation $\langle a,b\mid a^4=1,\ b^6=1,\ a^2=b^3\rangle$: applying [[thm-presentation-of-a-free-product-with-amalgamation]] to these two presentations with the amalgamating words $u_{a^2}=a^2$, $v_{a^2}=b^3$ for the generator $a^2$ of the common subgroup $C_2$ adds exactly the relation $a^2b^{-3}=1$, and amalgamated products are pushouts along monomorphisms ([[def-free-product-with-amalgamation]]).

[F4] $\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ is the free product $C_2\ast C_3$: with $x$ the class of $S$ and $y$ the class of $ST$, every element has a unique expression $y^{i_0}xy^{i_1}x\cdots y^{i_{n-1}}xy^{i_n}$ with $i_j$ taken modulo $3$ and all inner exponents nonzero, and the emptiness of further relations is proved by the entry-sum argument for products of $SR$ and $SR^2$ (Keith Conrad, SL_2(Z), Appendix C, Theorem C.1 and its proof; see the cited locator). In particular the assignment $x\mapsto s$, $y\mapsto t$ for arbitrary elements $s,t$ of a group with $s^2=t^3=1$ extends to a homomorphism $C_2\ast C_3\to\langle s,t\rangle$ ([[thm-presentation-of-a-free-product]]); uniqueness of the normal form is the cited external theorem.

[F5] The center of $B_n$ for $n\ge3$ is infinite cyclic and generated by the full twist $\Delta^2$; hence $\Delta^4=(\Delta^2)^2$ is central of infinite order ([[thm-the-center-of-b-n-is-generated-by-the-full-twist-for-n-greater-than-two]], [[def-garside-half-twist-and-simple-positive-braid]]).

[F6] Matrix arithmetic is entrywise. For $2\times2$ matrices $A,B$, expanding the four entries of $AB$ in $\det(AB)$ and cancelling cross terms gives $\det(AB)=\det(A)\det(B)$; the relevant matrices have determinant $1$, so their products lie in $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ ([[def-invertible-matrix-and-similarity-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

[F7] The matrices $S,T$ generate $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ (Keith Conrad, *SL2(Z)*, Theorem 1.1 and its algebraic proof in Section 2, printed p. 1). That proof applies integer division to the first column, decreasing the absolute value of its lower entry until it vanishes; the resulting upper triangular determinant-one matrix is a signed power of $T$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 *The specialization.* Evaluating the matrices of [F1] at $t=-1$ gives $\bar\rho_3(-1)(\sigma_1)=U$ and $\bar\rho_3(-1)(\sigma_2)=V$ as displayed. Computations give $UVU=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}=:S=VUV$ and $S^4=I$ (indeed $S^2=-I$), so the relations $UVU=VUV$ and $(UVU)^4=1$ hold in $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$; also $\det U=\det V=1$, so $\bar\rho_3(-1)$ takes values in $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$. [F1, F6, algebra]

1.2 *The presented group is the amalgam $C_4\ast_{C_2}C_6$.* Let $P=\langle x,y\mid xyx=yxy,\ (xyx)^4=1\rangle$ and set $a=xyx$, $b=xy$ as words in $P$. Symbolically, $b^{-1}a=(xy)^{-1}(xyx)=y^{-1}x^{-1}xyx=y^{-1}yx=x$ and $a^{-1}b^2=(xyx)^{-1}(xy)^2=x^{-1}y^{-1}x^{-1}xyxy=x^{-1}y^{-1}yxy=y$, so $P=\langle a,b\rangle$; moreover $(xy)^3=(xyx)(yxy)=(xyx)^2=a^2$ using the relation, so $b^3=a^2$, and hence $b^6=a^4=1$. These relations define a homomorphism $\theta:C_4\ast_{C_2}C_6\to P$ sending the amalgam generators to the words $a,b$. Conversely, in $C_4\ast_{C_2}C_6$ the elements $x:=b^{-1}a$ and $y:=a^{-1}b^2$ satisfy $xyx=a$ and $yxy=a$, so they satisfy the relators of $P$ and define a homomorphism $\eta:P\to C_4\ast_{C_2}C_6$. Direct substitution shows that $\eta\theta$ fixes $a,b$ and $\theta\eta$ fixes $x,y$, hence the homomorphisms are inverse and $P\cong C_4\ast_{C_2}C_6$. [F3, algebra]

1.3 *Generation.* The matrices $S,T$ generate $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ by [F7]. Direct calculation gives $U=T^{-1}$ and $UVU=S$, so $\langle U,V\rangle$ contains both $S$ and $T$. Since $U,V\in\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ by [F6], it follows that $\langle U,V\rangle=\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$. [F2, F6, F7, algebra]

2.1 *The amalgam is $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$.* By von Dyck applied to [F3] with $a\mapsto S$, $b\mapsto ST$ (which satisfy $S^4=I$, $(ST)^6=I$, $S^2=(ST)^3=-I$), there is a homomorphism $\varphi:C_4\ast_{C_2}C_6\to\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$, surjective because its image contains $S$ and $ST$, which generate $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$: indeed $T=S^{-1}(ST)$. Let $q:\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)\to\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ be the quotient of [F2]. The composite $\psi=q\circ\varphi$ sends $a\mapsto\bar S$ and $b\mapsto\overline{ST}$, so it factors through the quotient by $a^2=b^3$; by [F4] its induced map on that quotient is the isomorphism $C_2\ast C_3\cong\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ with generators $a\mapsto\bar S$, $b\mapsto\overline{ST}$. Its kernel is the normal closure of $a^2$, since quotienting [F3] by $a^2=b^3$ gives $\langle a,b\mid a^2=1,b^3=1\rangle=C_2\ast C_3$. The element $a^2=b^3$ is central in the amalgam, because it is central in both cyclic factors, and has order at most two; its image under $\varphi$ is $S^2=-I\ne I$, so the kernel of $\psi$ is exactly $\{1,a^2\}$. If $\varphi(w)=I$, then $w\in\{1,a^2\}$, and the nontriviality of $\varphi(a^2)$ forces $w=1$. Hence $\varphi$ is injective and $C_4\ast_{C_2}C_6\cong\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$. By step 1.2, $P\cong\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ via $x\mapsto U$, $y\mapsto V$; in particular $\bar\rho_3(-1)$ is surjective onto $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ by steps 1.1 and 1.3. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 1.3, step 1.2]

3.1 *The kernel.* The Artin presentation of $B_3$ has the single relation $\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1=\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2$, and step 2.1 exhibits $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ as the quotient of $B_3$ by the normal closure of $\Delta^4=(\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1)^4$ under $\sigma_1\mapsto U$, $\sigma_2\mapsto V$. Hence $\ker\bar\rho_3(-1)=\langle\langle\Delta^4\rangle\rangle$. By [F5] the element $\Delta^4$ is central of infinite order, so its normal closure is the cyclic subgroup $\langle\Delta^4\rangle=\{\Delta^{4k}:k\in\mathbb Z\}\cong\mathbb Z$. This is the assertion, including the equivalent description as the only relation added to the Artin presentation. AC is inherited through the cited representation agreement; the matrix, presentation and centre computations are choice free. [F5, step 2.1] ∎
