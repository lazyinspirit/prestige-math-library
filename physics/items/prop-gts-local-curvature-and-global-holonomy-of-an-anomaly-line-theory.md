## Statement

Let $\pi:Y\to Z$ be a smooth proper submersion with closed compact even-dimensional fibers and a Spin structure on the vertical tangent. Fix a vertical metric, horizontal distribution and the associated family of chiral complex Spin Dirac operators. Let $L_{\mathrm{inv}}$ be the inverse Quillen determinant line with the inverse Bismut–Freed connection. Its curvature is

$$\Omega(L_{\mathrm{inv}})=-2\pi i\left[\int_{Y/Z}\widehat A(\Omega(T^vY))\right]_{(2)}.$$

For a smooth loop $\gamma:S^1\to Z$, let $z_0=\gamma(0)$ and write
$$\operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb C}(D^+_{z_0})=\dim_{\mathbb C}\ker D^+_{z_0}-\dim_{\mathbb C}\operatorname{coker}D^+_{z_0}$$
for the ordinary Fredholm index of the chiral operator on the fiber over $z_0$. This integer is locally constant in the parameter and hence constant along $\gamma$. Let $Y_\gamma$ be the mapping torus, put the nonbounding Spin structure on the circle, and use the adiabatic metric $g_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{-2}g_{S^1}\oplus g^{T^vY}$. With $\xi(D)=(\eta(D,0)+\dim\ker D)/2$, the holonomy is

$$\operatorname{hol}_\gamma(L_{\mathrm{inv}})=(-1)^{\operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb C}(D^+_{z_0})}\lim_{\varepsilon\to0}\exp(2\pi i\,\xi(D_{Y_\gamma,\varepsilon})).$$

The inverse-line sign and parity factor use Dai–Freed’s Corollary 5.11 convention. This proposition concerns the analytic determinant line only; it does not identify that line with a physical anomaly theory.

1.1 If the fibers have dimension zero, properness makes each fiber a finite set. On a base neighborhood each sheet and its Spin data are locally constant, so the vertical spinor spaces form a finite graded flat Hermitian bundle $H^+\oplus H^-$ and $D=0$. Thus $L_{\mathrm{inv}}=\det H^+\otimes(\det H^-)^*$ is flat. The vertical tangent curvature vanishes, $\widehat A=1$, and its fiber integral has degree zero only, proving the curvature formula in this case. The orientation-graded reference line gives $\operatorname{str}_V I=\varepsilon\operatorname{str}_W I$ at each point, so oriented integration agrees with the actual kernel superdimension, including negative points. Over a loop the mapping-torus operator is exactly $\varepsilon\Gamma_H(-i\nabla_\theta)$ on this finite bundle. For a holonomy eigenvalue $e^{2\pi i\alpha}$, parallel coordinates give spectrum $2\pi(k-\alpha)$ for the positive circle Dirac operator. Subtracting the leading integral in the Euler summation formula gives the Hurwitz value $\zeta(0,a)=1/2-a$, hence $\eta(0)=2\alpha-1$ for $0<\alpha<1$ and exponentiated reduced eta $-e^{2\pi i\alpha}$. At $\alpha=0$ the symmetric spectrum and one kernel vector give the same phase; negative chirality conjugates it. Multiplication over these finite eigenvalues therefore gives its exponentiated reduced eta as $(-1)^{\dim H^++\dim H^-}\det\operatorname{hol}(H^+)\det\operatorname{hol}(H^-)^{-1}$. Its parity equals $(-1)^{\operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb C}(D^+_{z_0})}$, proving the stated holonomy formula. Empty fibers give the trivial determinant line and phase one. 1.2 Work locally on the base with coordinate horizontal lifts $H_{\alpha}$, and choose a smooth orthonormal vertical frame $E_i$. At the point where the following coefficients are evaluated the base frame is orthonormal and its Christoffel symbols vanish. Write
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{[H\_alpha,E\_i]=B\_(alpha i)\textasciicircum{}j E\_j,}\\\texttt{vertical[H\_alpha,H\_beta]=T\_(alpha beta)\textasciicircum{}i E\_i.}\end{gathered}$$

$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{2< nabla\_(E\_i) E\_j,f\_alpha >=u(B\_(alpha j)\textasciicircum{}i+B\_(alpha i)\textasciicircum{}j),}\\\texttt{2< nabla\_(H\_alpha) E\_i,E\_j >=B\_(alpha i)\textasciicircum{}j-B\_(alpha j)\textasciicircum{}i,}\\\texttt{2< nabla\_(E\_i) f\_alpha,f\_beta >=-u\textasciicircum{}2 T\_(alpha beta)\textasciicircum{}i,}\\\texttt{2< nabla\_(f\_alpha) E\_i,f\_beta >=-u\textasciicircum{}2 T\_(alpha beta)\textasciicircum{}i,}\\\texttt{2< nabla\_(f\_alpha) f\_beta,E\_i >=+u\textasciicircum{}2 T\_(alpha beta)\textasciicircum{}i.}\end{gathered}$$
Each identity follows by inserting the three frame vectors into
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{2< nabla\_X Y,Z > = X<Y,Z>+Y<Z,X>-Z<X,Y>}\\\texttt{-<X,[Y,Z]>+<Y,[Z,X]>+<Z,[X,Y]>.}\end{gathered}$$
All derivatives of the displayed orthonormal-frame inner products vanish; the brackets above give exactly the listed terms. The all-vertical coefficients give the vertical Levi-Civita connection. The all-horizontal coefficients give the ordinary base connection, and tensoriality reinstates it away from the chosen normal base frame.
Represent the horizontal Clifford algebra on base forms by
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{c\_u(f\_alpha)=u\textasciicircum{}(-1) epsilon\_alpha-u iota\_alpha.}\end{gathered}$$
This satisfies $c_u(f_{\alpha})c_u(f_{\beta})+c_u(f_{\beta})c_u(f_{\alpha})=-2 \delta_{(\alpha \beta)}$. The vertical Clifford operators are graded tensored with this representation, so they anticommute with the horizontal odd operators. This representation is equivalent for every $u>0$ to the usual exterior Clifford representation after rescaling forms; hence the ordinary total Dirac and Lichnerowicz identities apply before taking u to zero.
The spin connection contribution to the total Dirac operator is
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{(1/4) sum\_(a,b,c) c(e\_a)<nabla\_(e\_a)e\_b,e\_c>c(e\_b)c(e\_c).}\end{gathered}$$
The symmetric B terms sum to
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{-(u/2) sum\_alpha tr(B\_alpha)c\_u(f\_alpha).}\end{gathered}$$
Indeed the symmetric contraction $\sum_{(ij)}c(E_i)c(E_j)(B_{ij}+B_{ji})$ is -2 tr B. Adding the direct horizontal derivative term and taking u to zero gives the Hilbert-space connection
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{nabla\textasciicircum{}H\_(H\_alpha)=nabla\textasciicircum{}(vertical spinor tensor E)\_(H\_alpha)}\\\texttt{-(1/2) tr(B\_alpha).}\end{gathered}$$
This connection is unitary: Lie differentiation of the vertical volume form along $H_{\alpha}$ is $-tr(B_{\alpha})$ times that volume form. Differentiating the fiberwise L2 pairing therefore gives exactly the two scalar corrections $-tr(B_{\alpha})/2$, one from each argument.
For T the vertical-derivative contribution is
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{-(u\textasciicircum{}2/8) sum\_(i,alpha,beta) T\_(alpha beta)\textasciicircum{}i}\\\texttt{c(E\_i)c\_u(f\_alpha)c\_u(f\_beta),}\end{gathered}$$
whereas the two mixed horizontal-derivative contributions together are
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{+(u\textasciicircum{}2/4) sum\_(i,alpha,beta) T\_(alpha beta)\textasciicircum{}i}\\\texttt{c(E\_i)c\_u(f\_alpha)c\_u(f\_beta).}\end{gathered}$$
Their sum is $+(u^2/8)$ times the ordered alpha,beta sum, equivalently $+(u^2/4)$ times $\alpha<\beta$. Put $\tau=-\operatorname{vertical}[H,H]$ and define
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{c(tau)=sum\_(alpha<beta) epsilon\_alpha epsilon\_beta}\\\texttt{c\_vertical(tau\_(alpha beta)).}\end{gathered}$$
The limit of the total Dirac operator is consequently
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{A=D\_vertical+nabla\textasciicircum{}H-(1/4)c(tau).}\end{gathered}$$
Twisting adds the actual given connection to both the vertical Dirac and the horizontal connection; it does not alter the universal coefficients just calculated. 2.1 Use the total metric $g_u=g_V+u^{(-2)}g_B$ and the horizontal Clifford representation $c_u(f_{\alpha})=u^{(-1)}\epsilon_{\alpha}-u \operatorname{iota}_{\alpha}$. $$\begin{gathered}\texttt{(1/4) sum\_(j,alpha)(B\_(alpha j)\textasciicircum{}i+B\_(alpha i)\textasciicircum{}j)}\\\texttt{c(E\_j) epsilon\_alpha}\\\texttt{+ (1/4) tau\_i,}\end{gathered}$$
in direction $E_i$. The first is the two mixed spin-connection terms, using the first Koszul coefficient. Here $\nabla^{aug}$ is precisely the vertical connection calculated above, and $c_0$ replaces each horizontal covector by exterior multiplication. In particular its tau/4 connection term generates the actual vertical drift when the square is expanded; no independent horizontal heat operator has been retained.

**Radial gauge and the leading curvature**
Parallel transport the limiting connection radially in the vertical coordinates. The exact radial identity for any connection with finite exterior coefficients is
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{A\_i(x)=integral\_0\textasciicircum{}1 s x\textasciicircum{}j F\_(ji)(s x) ds.}\end{gathered}$$
Its proof differentiates $s A_i(sx)$, using $x^i A_i=0$. It works coefficientwise in the finite form algebra, with no convergence issue. The constant $\tau_i/4$ term is thereby removed by the radial transport itself. For constant tau this transport is the finite nilpotent exponential $\operatorname{exp}(-\sum_i x^i \tau_i/4)$; in particular it removes both the drift term and the apparent $\tau^2/(16t)$ divergence, rather than neglecting the latter.
Assign degree one to vertical Clifford generators and horizontal exterior generators, degree minus one to x, and degree one to vertical derivatives. The O(x)epsilon correction to $c(E_i)$ has degree zero, below its constant Clifford term. Radial gauge makes the leading connection term one fourth of the curvature matrix times x. Its Clifford-degree-two symbol is the full curvature two-form of the vertical tangent connection, including its horizontal and mixed components. This last identification can be checked before taking u to zero: the Clifford curvature is $(1/4)R_{(abij)}c(e_a)c(e_b)$, and $R_{(abij)}=R_{(ijab)}$ by the usual Riemann curvature pair symmetry. With i,j vertical, the latter components are precisely the curvature of the vertical connection in the limit. The mixed Koszul coefficients above account for the horizontal components; no separate symmetry of a degenerate metric is being assumed.
The scalar-curvature term has degree zero. The relative curvature term has degree two, with the horizontal Clifford factors replaced by exterior factors. Consequently the degree-two model of $A^2$ is
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{P\_0=-sum\_i(partial\_i+(Rcal\_ij x\textasciicircum{}j)/4)\textasciicircum{}2+F\_relative,}\end{gathered}$$
where Rcal is the curvature matrix in the same sign convention as the existing radial-gauge/Getzler supplier, now with coefficients in the full exterior algebra of vertical plus horizontal covectors. The relative curvature includes the twisting connection and the Spin-c determinant curvature $F_L/2$.
**Actual kernels and the finite-algebra extension**

The Mehler calculation in the existing provider is an identity of formal power series in even curvature forms, hence remains valid after tensoring with the finite horizontal exterior algebra. 2.2 Let B have finite dimension b, and work on a compact base coordinate subset. Let D be a smooth family of odd self-adjoint compact-fiber Dirac operators, with a smooth finite-rank graded kernel bundle P H and a common positive gap on $Q H, Q=1-P$. Assume the given Hilbert connection nabla preserves the grading and is the usual smooth connection on fiberwise smooth sections. $$\begin{gathered}\texttt{A\_t=sqrt(t)D+nabla+t\textasciicircum{}(-1/2)C,    t>=1.}\end{gathered}$$

The kernel projection and its derivatives are smoothing: around a base point take a small resolvent circle inside the common gap, differentiate the resolvent identity, and integrate. $$\begin{gathered}\texttt{Str exp(-A\_t\textasciicircum{}2) = ch(P nabla P)+O(t\textasciicircum{}(-1/2)),}\\\texttt{Str((partial\_t A\_t) exp(-A\_t\textasciicircum{}2)) = O(t\textasciicircum{}(-3/2)).}\end{gathered}$$
The second estimate proves convergence of the eta integral at infinity.
**Exact diagonalization in the nilpotent base-form ideal**
Use the P,Q block decomposition. The zero-form part of $A_t$ is $diag(0,sqrt(t)D_Q)$. All its off-diagonal terms have positive base-form degree. The degree-one off-diagonal term of nabla is a bundle homomorphism, not a base derivative: the base derivative cancels in P nabla Q and Q nabla P. Its coefficients are smoothing, since derivatives of P are smoothing.
At degree k, suppose degrees below k have been made block diagonal. An even change of frame $1+K_k$, with $K_k$ off-diagonal and of form degree k, changes the degree-k off-diagonal part by $[K_k,sqrt(t)D]$. On the P,Q and Q,P blocks this equation is solved by right or left multiplication by $D_Q^{(-1)}$, with the sign determined by the graded product. Explicitly one solves the two equations independently; multiplication by $D_Q$ is invertible on Q and the other diagonal block is zero. Thus $K_k$ is $t^{(-1/2)}$ times the degree-k off-diagonal coefficient times $D_Q^{(-1)}$, in its corresponding order. Its coefficients are smoothing and bounded in every fixed Sobolev norm.
Changing frame introduces no lower-degree term, because all other factors in $A_t$ have positive form degree. Iterate $k=1,...,b$. The positive-form ideal has nilpotence degree b+1, so every inverse $1+K_k$ has a finite geometric series, and after b steps the result is EXACTLY block diagonal. Write
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{Abar\_t=R\_t A\_t R\_t\textasciicircum{}(-1)=A\_t\textasciicircum{}P direct\_sum A\_t\textasciicircum{}Q.}\end{gathered}$$
The coefficient estimates follow directly from the recursion. At degree one the new change is $O(t^{(-1/2)})$; every subsequent nonzero correction contains either that factor or the original $t^{(-1/2)} C$. Hence
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{R\_t=1+O(t\textasciicircum{}(-1/2)),    partial\_t R\_t=O(t\textasciicircum{}(-3/2)),}\\\texttt{A\_t\textasciicircum{}P=P nabla P+O(t\textasciicircum{}(-1/2)),}\\\texttt{A\_t\textasciicircum{}Q=sqrt(t)D\_Q+Q nabla Q+O(t\textasciicircum{}(-1/2)).}\end{gathered}$$
These assertions are coefficientwise in the finite form algebra; they are not operator-norm assertions about the unbounded zero-form $sqrt(t)D_Q$. Differentiating the explicit powers of t in the finite recursion gives the stated derivative estimates. No infinite asymptotic conjugation is being invoked.
**Heat estimates on the Q block**
The zero-form square is $t D_Q^2$ with spectrum at least c t for one $c>0$. The positive-form part of $(A_t^Q)^2$ has finite differential order and coefficients polynomially bounded in sqrt(t); its form degree is positive. Its heat exponential is therefore the finite Duhamel series of length at most b. For each fixed term distribute a fraction of the common gap to obtain exp(-ct/2). The remaining heat factors absorb each vertical differential factor by the spectral estimate
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{sup\_(lambda>=c) lambda\textasciicircum{}(r/2) exp(-u t lambda)}\\\texttt{<= C\_r (u t)\textasciicircum{}(-r/2) exp(-u t c/2).}\end{gathered}$$
At simplex faces with zero u, commute an insertion to an adjacent positive heat interval, expanding its commutators; equivalently use the smooth heat parametrix and its differentiated graph-norm remainder. Since at least one interval has length at least 1/(b+1), a smoothing trace-class factor remains, and the other differential factors are bounded between sufficiently separated Sobolev spaces. Thus each fixed base derivative and trace norm is bounded by a polynomial in t times exp(-c' t). The same estimate holds with a fixed differential insertion, in particular $D_Q/(2\sqrt t)$.
The P block is finite dimensional, so its heat exponential converges in all coefficient norms to $\exp(-(P\nabla P)^2)$, with error $O(t^{(-1/2)})$. Conjugation invariance of the smoothing supertrace now proves the first claim.
**The eta insertion**
Put $V_t=(\partial_t R_t)R_t^{(-1)}$. Differentiating $Abar_t$ gives
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{R\_t(partial\_t A\_t)R\_t\textasciicircum{}(-1)=partial\_t Abar\_t-[V\_t,Abar\_t].}\end{gathered}$$
The trace of the first term times $\operatorname{exp}(-Abar_t^2)$ is $O(t^{(-3/2)})$ on P, by the derivative estimate, and exponentially small times a power of t on Q, by the Q-block heat estimate just established. For the second term use the Bianchi identity. Supertracing a commutator with a superconnection is its exterior derivative; hence this contribution is, up to the fixed graded commutator sign, the exterior derivative of
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{Str(V\_t exp(-Abar\_t\textasciicircum{}2)).}\end{gathered}$$
This form and its exterior derivative are $O(t^{(-3/2)})$: $V_t$ and every fixed base derivative have that size on P, and the Q terms satisfy the preceding exponential estimate. This proves the second claim. Integrating $t^{(-3/2)}$ proves large-time convergence, with all fixed base derivatives, on compact parameter subsets.
**Producing a kernel bundle after stabilization**
For a compact base, choose finitely many smooth sections $s_j$ of $H^-$ whose values, together with im $D^+, \operatorname{span} H^-$ at every base point. Such sections exist: choose a finite-dimensional complement at each point, extend its finitely many smooth eigenvectors locally, use continuity of surjectivity in $H^1$ to $L^2$, and then a finite partition of unity and compact subcover. The smooth finite-rank operator $J:C^N->H^-$ given by these sections makes $D^+ \mathbin{\mathrm{direct\_sum}} J$ surjective. Its kernel is a smooth finite-rank bundle, as is seen from a local right inverse and the corresponding projection. The self-adjoint odd stabilization with positive space $H^+ \mathbin{\mathrm{direct\_sum}} C^N$ and negative space $H^-$ consequently has constant kernel dimension and a uniform spectral gap on compact base subsets.
