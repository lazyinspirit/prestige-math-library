## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $A$ be a commutative ring, let $M$ be an
$A$-module and let $f_1,\dots,f_r\in A$ generate the unit ideal, so that
$1=\sum_i a_if_i$ for suitable $a_i\in A$. Then the augmented alternating
Cech complex
$$0\to M\to\bigoplus_i M_{f_i}\to\bigoplus_{i<j} M_{f_if_j}\to\cdots$$
whose terms are the principal localisations
([[def-principal-localisation]], [[def-localisation-of-a-module]]) and whose
maps are the alternating Cech differentials of ([[def-cech-cochain-complex-open-cover]]),
is exact. The same is true after localising both $A$ and $M$ at an arbitrary
element $g\in A$: the images of the $f_i$ in $A_g$ still generate the unit
ideal, and the localised complex is the analogous Cech complex for $M_g$.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, a commutative ring $A$, an $A$-module $M$ and $f_1,\dots,f_r\in A$ with $\sum_i A f_i=A$.

[F1] For a cover indexed by the totally ordered set $\{1,\dots,r\}$ and a sheaf $\mathcal F$, the $p$-th Cech cochain group is $C^p=\prod_{i_0<\cdots<i_p}\mathcal F(U_{i_0}\cap\cdots\cap U_{i_p})$ with the alternating Cech differential, $C^p=0$ for $p<0$, and the empty product is zero; the differential satisfies $\delta\circ\delta=0$. Applied to $U_i=D(f_i)\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}A$ and $\mathcal F=\widetilde M$, the intersection $D(f_{i_0})\cap\cdots\cap D(f_{i_p})=D(f_{i_0}\cdots f_{i_p})$ has section module $M_{f_{i_0}\cdots f_{i_p}}$, giving the complex displayed in the statement with $\bigoplus$ in place of the finite products. ([[def-cech-cochain-complex-open-cover]], [[lem-distinguished-subset-identities]], [[lem-associated-sheaf-sections-basic-open]])

[F2] Under AC, a sequence $M'\xrightarrow{f}M\xrightarrow{g}M''$ of $R$-modules with $g\circ f=0$ is exact at $M$ if and only if every prime localisation, equivalently every maximal localisation, is exact at $M_{\mathfrak p}$. ([[thm-local-criterion-for-exactness-of-modules]], [[def-localisation-at-a-prime-ideal]])

[F3] Localisation of $R$-modules is exact, and it commutes with finite direct sums and with quotients: $(\bigoplus_k N_k)_S\cong\bigoplus_k (N_k)_S$ and $(N/N')_S\cong N_S/N'_S$. ([[thm-localisation-of-modules-is-exact]], [[thm-localisation-of-modules-commutes-with-quotients-and-sums]])

[F4] Iterated localisation: for multiplicative sets $S,T\subseteq R$ with $U$ generated by $S\cup T$ there is a canonical isomorphism $\overline T^{-1}(S^{-1}R)\cong U^{-1}R$, and in particular $(R_f)_g\cong R_{fg}$. Consequently, if $f\notin\mathfrak p$, then $(M_f)_{\mathfrak p}\cong M_{\mathfrak p}$ and $(M_{fg})_{\mathfrak p}\cong(M_g)_{\mathfrak p}$: the factor $f$ may be dropped after localisation at $\mathfrak p$, while $g$ need not become a unit. ([[prop-iterated-localisation]], [[def-principal-localisation]])

[F5] In the ring $A_{\mathfrak p}$ every element outside $\mathfrak p$ becomes a unit; hence if $f\notin\mathfrak p$ then the localisation map $M_f\to M_{\mathfrak p}$ is an isomorphism after localising at $\mathfrak p$. ([[def-localisation-at-a-prime-ideal]])



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct: localise the augmented Cech complex at each prime, where one of the $f_i$ becomes a unit and an explicit insertion homotopy contracts the complex; exactness then descends by the local criterion, and the same argument is rerun after localising at an arbitrary element.

1.1 Write $C^{\bullet}$ for the augmented alternating Cech complex of the statement: $C^{-1}=M$, $C^p=\bigoplus_{i_0<\cdots<i_p}M_{f_{i_0}\cdots f_{i_p}}$ for $p\ge0$, with the alternating differentials $\delta^p$ and $\delta\circ\delta=0$ [F1]; the augmentation is $M\to C^0$, $m\mapsto(m/1)_i$. [F1]

1.2 Let $\mathfrak p$ be a prime ideal of $A$. Since $\sum_i a_if_i=1\notin\mathfrak p$ we have $f_i\notin\mathfrak p$ for at least one $i$; fix such an index $i$ for this $\mathfrak p$. [F2, given]

2.1 Localising the complex $C^{\bullet}$ at $\mathfrak p$ and using that localisation commutes with finite direct sums [F3] gives a complex $C^{\bullet}_{\mathfrak p}$ whose degree-$p$ term is $\bigoplus_{i_0<\cdots<i_p}M_{f_{i_0}\cdots f_{i_p},\mathfrak p}$, the localisation of each principal localisation at $\mathfrak p$; by [F4] any factor $f_{i_j}$ with $i_j=i$ may be dropped, and [F5] shows that the remaining terms are computed in the ring $A_{\mathfrak p}$ in which $f_i$ is a unit. [F3, F4, F5, step 1.2]

3.1 Extend each cochain's components from increasing tuples to arbitrary tuples by alternating signs under permutations, and set the component to zero when an index is repeated. These are the same cochains, expressed with redundant indices; all restrictions to intersection localisations respect the sign rule. For $s\in C^q_{\mathfrak p}$ with $q\ge0$, define $h(s)_{i_0\cdots i_{q-1}}=s_{i,i_0,\ldots,i_{q-1}}$, using this alternating convention, and for $q=0$ define $h(s)=s_i\in M_{\mathfrak p}=C^{-1}_{\mathfrak p}$. Since $f_i$ is a unit in $A_{\mathfrak p}$, [F4] identifies the component on the intersection with $D(f_i)$ with the required target component, making $h$ well defined even when $i$ lies among the other indices. For an increasing tuple $J=(i_0,\ldots,i_p)$ and $p\ge0$, the alternating differential gives $((\delta h+h\delta)s)_J=\sum_{j=0}^{p}(-1)^j s_{i,J\setminus i_j}+(\delta s)_{i,J}=\sum_{j=0}^{p}(-1)^j s_{i,J\setminus i_j}+s_J+\sum_{j=0}^{p}(-1)^{j+1}s_{i,J\setminus i_j}=s_J$. In degree $-1$, $h\delta(m)$ is the $i$-component of the augmentation of $m$, hence equals $m$. Thus $\delta h+h\delta=1$ in every degree, the localised augmented complex is contractible, and it is exact. [F1, F4, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Since for every prime ideal $\mathfrak p$ the localisation $C^{\bullet}_{\mathfrak p}$ is exact, the local criterion [F2] applied to each consecutive pair of differentials of the complex $C^{\bullet}$ shows that $C^{\bullet}$ is exact at every term, that is, the augmented Cech complex of the statement is exact. [F2, step 3.1]

5.1 Now fix $g\in A$ and localise at $g$. The images of $f_1,\dots,f_r$ in $A_g$ still generate the unit ideal (apply the localisation ring map to $1=\sum_i a_if_i$), and by [F3] and [F4] the localisation at $g$ of each $M_{f_{i_0}\cdots f_{i_p}}$ is canonically the module $(M_g)_{f_{i_0}\cdots f_{i_p}}$ computed in $A_g$; hence the $g$-localisation of the displayed complex is the corresponding augmented Cech complex of $M_g$ with respect to those generators, and step 4.1 applied in the ring $A_g$ proves it is exact. [F3, F4, step 4.1]

6.1 The Axiom of Choice is used exactly through the local criterion [F2]; the index $i$ is chosen for one fixed prime $\mathfrak p$ at a time in the pointwise argument of step 1.2, so no simultaneous selection over primes is made, and no other step uses a choice principle. [F2, step 1.2, given] ∎
