## Example

For $X=\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb C}$, every rational Hodge class in degree $0$ or $2$ is the class of a rational algebraic cycle: the degree-$0$ class is a rational multiple of $[X]$, and every degree-$2$ rational class is a rational multiple of the class of a point. Thus the Hodge conjecture holds for the projective line in both possible codimensions.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $X=\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb C}$ with its standard complex orientation. For this example, codimension-$p$ cycles are finite integer linear combinations of codimension-$p$ irreducible closed subvarieties; rational equivalence is generated by divisors of rational functions on codimension-$(p-1)$ subvarieties; $\mathrm{CH}^p(X)_{\mathbb Q}$ is the resulting quotient tensored with $\mathbb Q$; and the cycle-class map sends an irreducible subvariety to its Poincaré-dual cohomology class.

[F1] A Hodge class of codimension $p$ is a rational class in $H^{2p}(X;\mathbb Q)$ whose image in complex cohomology belongs to $H^{p,p}(X)$; the Hodge-type convention is the usual decomposition of complex differential forms by holomorphic and antiholomorphic degree.

## Proof

1.1 The projective line is the Riemann sphere, hence is topologically $S^2$ with one cell in dimensions $0$ and $2$ and no other cells. Therefore $H^0(X;\mathbb Q)=\mathbb Q$ and $H^2(X;\mathbb Q)=\mathbb Q$, with $H^1(X;\mathbb Q)=0$. Every smooth $2$-form on a complex curve has type $(1,1)$ because there are no nonzero $(2,0)$ or $(0,2)$ forms; the constant function represents the type-$(0,0)$ generator of $H^0$. Thus the Hodge classes in codimensions $0$ and $1$ are exactly all rational classes in those degrees. [given, F1, algebra]

1.2 Codimension-$1$ cycles are zero-cycles $D=\sum_{a\in\mathbb C}m_a[a]+m_\infty[\infty]$ with finitely many nonzero integer coefficients. The degree $\deg D=\sum_am_a+m_\infty$ is unchanged by rational equivalence because the divisor of a rational function on $\mathbb P^1$ has degree zero. Conversely, if $\deg D=0$, then $m_\infty=-\sum_am_a$ and the rational function $f(z)=\prod_a(z-a)^{m_a}$ has divisor exactly $D$ (negative exponents are allowed in a rational function); hence $D$ is rationally equivalent to zero. It follows that $\mathrm{CH}^1(X)\cong\mathbb Z$ by degree, generated by $[0]$. [given, algebra]

2.1 The only codimension-$0$ irreducible closed subvariety is $X$ itself, and there are no codimension-$(-1)$ subvarieties that could generate a rational-equivalence relation; hence $\mathrm{CH}^0(X)_{\mathbb Q}=\mathbb Q[X]$. The cycle class of $[X]$ is the generator of $H^0(X;\mathbb Q)$, so the codimension-$0$ cycle map is surjective. [given, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The cycle class of a point is the generator of $H^2(S^2;\mathbb Z)$ by the definition as Poincaré dual of the positively oriented point, so after tensoring with $\mathbb Q$ the codimension-$1$ cycle map is an isomorphism $\mathrm{CH}^1(X)_{\mathbb Q}\to H^2(X;\mathbb Q)$. Together with step 2.1, both possible Hodge-class degrees are algebraic. No claim in dimensions greater than one follows from this calculation. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 1.2, given, algebra] ∎
