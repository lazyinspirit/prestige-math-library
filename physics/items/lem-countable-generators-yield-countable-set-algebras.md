## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice.

Let $\mathcal G$ be a countable family of subsets of a set $X$. Then there is a
countable algebra of subsets of $X$ that contains $\mathcal G$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Countable Choice, a set $X$, and a countable family $\mathcal G \subseteq \mathcal P(X)$.

[L1] Generated sigma-algebras are built from families of sets, and algebras are closed under complements and finite unions ([[def-generated-sigma-algebra]], [[def-algebra-of-subsets]]).

[L2] Countable sets are closed under finite products and countable unions ([[def-countable]], [[thm-product-of-countable]], [[thm-countable-union-of-countable]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** constructive.

1.1 If $\mathcal G=\varnothing$, then $\{\varnothing,X\}$ is a finite algebra [L1, given, algebra] of subsets of $X$ containing $\mathcal G$, so the conclusion holds. Assume now that $\mathcal G\ne\varnothing$. [L1, given, algebra]

1.2 Enumerate $\mathcal G = \{G_1,G_2,\dots\}$. For each $m \ge 1$, let [L1, given, choose, construct] $\mathcal A_m$ be the family of all unions of atoms of the finite partition generated by $G_1,\dots,G_m$; equivalently, the members of $\mathcal A_m$ are all finite Boolean combinations of those $m$ sets. Each $\mathcal A_m$ is a finite algebra on $X$ containing $G_1,\dots,G_m$. [L1, given, choose, construct]

2.1 Put [step 1.2, L1, choose, algebra] $$ \mathcal A := \bigcup_{m \ge 1} \mathcal A_m. $$ Then $\mathcal A$ contains every $G_j$. If $A,B \in \mathcal A$, choose $m$ with $A,B \in \mathcal A_m$; because $\mathcal A_m$ is an algebra, also $X \setminus A$ and $A \cup B$ lie in $\mathcal A_m \subseteq \mathcal A$. Thus $\mathcal A$ is an algebra of subsets of $X$. [step 1.2, L1, choose, algebra]

3.1 Each $\mathcal A_m$ is finite, so in particular countable, and [L2] makes [L2, step 2.1] their countable union $\mathcal A$ countable. Therefore $\mathcal A$ is a countable algebra containing $\mathcal G$. [L2, step 2.1] ∎
