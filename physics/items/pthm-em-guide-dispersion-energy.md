## Statement

In the setting of [[pthm-em-rectangular-mode-candidates]] let a TE or TM candidate of part (a) or (b) with $\beta>0$ and $\omega>\omega_c$ be fixed (so $\beta=\sqrt{\omega^2\varepsilon\mu-k_c^2}$ is real and nonzero), retain its positive longitudinal amplitude $H_*$ (A m$^{-1}$) in the TE case or $E_*$ (V m$^{-1}$) in the TM case, let $T:=2\pi/\omega$, and let $u:=\frac12(E\cdot D+B\cdot H)$ and $S:=E\times H$ be the stored-energy density and energy-flux vector of [[pthm-em-restricted-material-energy]], with the Euclidean inner and cross products of [[def-euclidean-inner-product]] and [[def-cross-product-in-r3]]. Define the period-averaged stored energy per unit length and the period-averaged longitudinal flux across the cross-section $$U:=\frac1T\int_0^T\!\!\int_{R_{ab}}u\,dx_1dx_2\,dt,\qquad \Phi:=\frac1T\int_0^T\!\!\int_{R_{ab}}S\cdot e_z\,dx_1dx_2\,dt,$$ both independent of $z$ and of the starting time. Then:
(a) [Cross-section identities] $\int_{R_{ab}}|\nabla_th_{mn}|^2=k_c^2\int_{R_{ab}}h_{mn}^2$ and $\int_{R_{ab}}|\nabla_te_{mn}|^2=k_c^2\int_{R_{ab}}e_{mn}^2$, with the boundary terms of the integration by parts vanishing;
(b) [Energy and flux] for the TE candidate $$U=H_*^2\frac{\mu^2\varepsilon\omega^2}{2k_c^2}\int_{R_{ab}}h_{mn}^2,\qquad \Phi=H_*^2\frac{\omega\mu\beta}{2k_c^2}\int_{R_{ab}}h_{mn}^2,$$ and for the TM candidate $$U=E_*^2\frac{\varepsilon^2\mu\omega^2}{2k_c^2}\int_{R_{ab}}e_{mn}^2,\qquad \Phi=E_*^2\frac{\omega\varepsilon\beta}{2k_c^2}\int_{R_{ab}}e_{mn}^2,$$ with the explicit norms of [[lem-em-rectangle-mode-profile-identities]](c);
(c) [Energy transport and speed relations] $\Phi=U\,\beta/(\varepsilon\mu\omega)=U\,v_g$, where $v_g:=d\omega/d\beta=\beta/(\varepsilon\mu\omega)$ is the group speed of the branch $\omega(\beta)=\sqrt{(\beta^2+k_c^2)/(\varepsilon\mu)}$; moreover the phase speed $v_p:=\omega/\beta$ satisfies $v_pv_g=1/(\varepsilon\mu)$, $v_p>1/\sqrt{\varepsilon\mu}$ and $0<v_g<1/\sqrt{\varepsilon\mu}$ for the declared indices;
(d) [Finite propagating set] if a candidate with indices $(m,n)$ is propagating at frequency $\omega$ (that is, $\beta^2=\omega^2\varepsilon\mu-k_{mn}^2>0$), then $m<\omega a\sqrt{\varepsilon\mu}/\pi$ and $n<\omega b\sqrt{\varepsilon\mu}/\pi$, so the propagating indices lie in the explicitly bounded index box $\{m,n\ge0: m<\omega a\sqrt{\varepsilon\mu}/\pi,\, n<\omega b\sqrt{\varepsilon\mu}/\pi\}$;
(e) [Scope] This is a lossless separated-guide computation: the period average is over time at fixed position, the flux is through one cross-section, $v_p$ is not asserted to be a signal speed and no bound on information or energy speeds in general dispersive media follows; the material branch and the PEC model are those of the cited theorems.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A TE or TM candidate of [[pthm-em-rectangular-mode-candidates]] with $\beta>0$ and $\omega>\omega_c$, so that $\beta=\sqrt{\omega^2\varepsilon\mu-k_c^2}$ is real and nonzero and $k_c^2=\omega^2\varepsilon\mu-\beta^2$; $T:=2\pi/\omega$; the stored-energy density $u=\frac12(E\cdot D+B\cdot H)$ and energy-flux vector $S=E\times H$ of [[pthm-em-restricted-material-energy]], with the Euclidean inner and cross products of [[def-euclidean-inner-product]] and [[def-cross-product-in-r3]]; the guide, TEM and phasor conventions of [[def-em-guide-cavity-geometry]] and the SI conventions of [[def-em-si-conventions]].

[F1] The candidate amplitudes are those of [[pthm-em-rectangular-mode-candidates]]: for the TE family, $h=H_*h_{mn}$ with $H_*>0$ in A m$^{-1}$, $k_c^2=k_{mn}^2$, $\tilde E=-\frac{i\omega\mu}{k_c^2}e_z\times\nabla_th$, $\tilde H=\frac{i\beta}{k_c^2}\nabla_th+e_zh$; for the TM family, $e=E_*e_{mn}$ with $E_*>0$ in V m$^{-1}$, $\tilde E=\frac{i\beta}{k_c^2}\nabla_te+e_ze$, $\tilde H=\frac{i\omega\varepsilon}{k_c^2}e_z\times\nabla_te$; the real fields are $E=\operatorname{Re}(\tilde Ee^{i(\beta x_3-\omega t)})$, $H=\operatorname{Re}(\tilde He^{i(\beta x_3-\omega t)})$, $B=\mu H$, $D=\varepsilon E$, and the four source-free macroscopic equations hold by that theorem (([[pthm-em-rectangular-mode-candidates]]), [[def-em-guide-cavity-geometry]]). The stored energy and flux are $u=\frac12(\varepsilon|E|^2+\mu|H|^2)$ and $S=E\times H$ in this branch, with $\partial_tu+\operatorname{div}S=0$ and $u\ge0$ ([[pthm-em-restricted-material-energy]] clause (a), lossless case $J_f=0$).

[F2] Period averages: for $\omega>0$, $T=2\pi/\omega$ and $a,d\in\mathbb C^3$, the componentwise averages over one period are $\frac1T\int_0^T\langle\operatorname{Re}(ae^{-i\omega t}),\operatorname{Re}(de^{-i\omega t})\rangle dt=\frac12\operatorname{Re}\langle a,\overline d\rangle$ and $\frac1T\int_0^T\operatorname{Re}(ae^{-i\omega t})\times\operatorname{Re}(de^{-i\omega t})dt=\frac12\operatorname{Re}(a\times\overline d)$, with the complex-bilinear extensions of the Euclidean inner and cross products, and the scalar form for $a,d\in\mathbb C$ ([[lem-em-period-average]]). The integrands are $T$-periodic because $e^{-i\omega(t+T)}=e^{-i\omega t}e^{-2\pi i}=e^{-i\omega t}$, so the average over any interval $[t_0,t_0+T]$ agrees with the average over $[0,T]$.

[F3] Cross-section identities and norms: for the profiles of [[lem-em-rectangle-mode-profile-identities]], $\int_{R_{ab}}|\nabla_th_{mn}|^2=k_{mn}^2\int_{R_{ab}}h_{mn}^2$ and $\int_{R_{ab}}|\nabla_te_{mn}|^2=k_{mn}^2\int_{R_{ab}}e_{mn}^2$, with all boundary terms of the integration by parts vanishing ([[lem-em-rectangle-mode-profile-identities]] (d)); and the transverse identities $|e_z\times\nabla_th|=|\nabla_th|$, $\nabla_th\cdot(e_z\times\nabla_th)=0$ and $e_z\times(e_z\times\nabla_th)=-\nabla_th$ hold, together with their analogues for $e_{mn}$ ([[lem-em-transverse-vector-identities]] (a)). The additional cross identities follow from [[def-cross-product-in-r3]]: for $g=(g_1,g_2,0)$, $w=e_z\times g=(-g_2,g_1,0)$ gives $w\times g=-e_z(g_1^2+g_2^2)$, $g\times w=e_z|g|^2$ and $w\times e_z=g$.

[F4] The branch function $\omega(\beta)=\sqrt{(\beta^2+k_c^2)/(\varepsilon\mu)}$ is well defined and positive for real $\beta$ by [[thm-of-square-roots]]; limits of real functions are algebraically computed by [[thm-algebra-of-function-limits]], and the derivative is that of [[def-derivative]]; sums, scalar multiples and composites obey the one-variable rules [[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]], [[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]].

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct: insert the explicit candidate amplitudes into the real quadratic densities, average over one period with the recorded period-average identities, evaluate the cross-section integrals with the profile and transverse identities, and differentiate the real branch function.

1.1 [Period-averaged quadratic densities] Fix a point $(x_1,x_2,x_3)$ and put $a:=\tilde Ee^{i\beta x_3}$, $d:=\tilde He^{i\beta x_3}$, so that $E=\operatorname{Re}(ae^{-i\omega t})$ and $H=\operatorname{Re}(de^{-i\omega t})$ at that point. By [F2], $\frac1T\int_0^T|E|^2dt=\frac12|\tilde E|^2$, $\frac1T\int_0^T|H|^2dt=\frac12|\tilde H|^2$ and $\frac1T\int_0^T(E\times H)dt=\frac12\operatorname{Re}(\tilde E\times\overline{\tilde H})$, because the phase factors $e^{\pm i\beta x_3}$ are unimodular and cancel in the inner and cross products. Hence the period averages $\langle u\rangle:=\frac1T\int_0^Tu\,dt=\frac14\bigl(\varepsilon|\tilde E|^2+\mu|\tilde H|^2\bigr)$ and $\langle S\rangle:=\frac1T\int_0^TS\,dt=\frac12\operatorname{Re}(\tilde E\times\overline{\tilde H})$ depend only on $(x_1,x_2)$ and not on $z$ or on the starting time, and therefore $U=\int_{R_{ab}}\langle u\rangle\,dx_1dx_2$ and $\Phi=\int_{R_{ab}}\langle S\rangle\cdot e_z\,dx_1dx_2$ are the stated averages, independent of $z$ and of the starting time; consistency with the local balance of [F1] means $\partial_t\langle u\rangle=0$ and $\partial_z(\langle S\rangle\cdot e_z)=0$, which the displayed formulas show directly. [F1, F2, algebra]

1.2 [Cross-section identities] For the scaled profiles $h=H_*h_{mn}$ and $e=E_*e_{mn}$ of [F1], multiplying the two gradient-integral identities of [F3] by $H_*^2$ and $E_*^2$, respectively, gives $\int_{R_{ab}}|\nabla_th|^2=k_c^2\int_{R_{ab}}h^2$ and $\int_{R_{ab}}|\nabla_te|^2=k_c^2\int_{R_{ab}}e^2$ with vanishing boundary terms (the traces $\partial_nh=0$ and $e=0$ on $\partial R_{ab}$ of [[lem-em-rectangle-mode-profile-identities]] (a),(b)), and the pointwise identities $|e_z\times\nabla_th|^2=|\nabla_th|^2$ and $(e_z\times\nabla_th)\times\nabla_th=-e_z|\nabla_th|^2$ hold, with $\nabla_th\cdot(e_z\times\nabla_th)=0$; the same holds with $e$ in place of $h$. [F1, F3, algebra]

2.1 [TE energy and flux] Let $h:=H_*h_{mn}$ and $w:=e_z\times\nabla_th$. With the TE amplitudes of [F1], $|\tilde E|^2=\frac{\omega^2\mu^2}{k_c^4}|w|^2=\frac{\omega^2\mu^2}{k_c^4}|\nabla_th|^2$ and $|\tilde H|^2=\frac{\beta^2}{k_c^4}|\nabla_th|^2+h^2$ (the cross term $\nabla_th\cdot e_z$ vanishes), so $\langle u\rangle=\frac14\bigl(\frac{\varepsilon\omega^2\mu^2+\mu\beta^2}{k_c^4}|\nabla_th|^2+\mu h^2\bigr)$; integrating with step 1.2, $\int(\varepsilon|\tilde E|^2)=\frac{\varepsilon\omega^2\mu^2}{k_c^2}\int h^2$, $\int(\mu|\tilde H|^2)=\frac{\mu\beta^2}{k_c^2}\int h^2+\mu\int h^2$, and $\mu\beta^2+\mu k_c^2=\mu\omega^2\varepsilon\mu$, so $\int(\varepsilon|\tilde E|^2+\mu|\tilde H|^2)=\frac{2\varepsilon\mu^2\omega^2}{k_c^2}\int h^2$ and $U=H_*^2\frac{\mu^2\varepsilon\omega^2}{2k_c^2}\int_{R_{ab}}h_{mn}^2$ as claimed. For the flux, $\overline{\tilde H}=-\frac{i\beta}{k_c^2}\nabla_th+e_zh$ (the profile $h$ and the parameters $\beta,\omega,\mu$ are real), so $\tilde E\times\overline{\tilde H}=-\frac{i\omega\mu}{k_c^2}\bigl[-\frac{i\beta}{k_c^2}w\times\nabla_th+h\,w\times e_z\bigr]=\frac{\omega\mu\beta}{k_c^4}|\nabla_th|^2e_z-i\frac{\omega\mu}{k_c^2}h\nabla_th$ using $w\times\nabla_th=-e_z|\nabla_th|^2$ and $w\times e_z=\nabla_th$ from [F3]; hence $\operatorname{Re}(\tilde E\times\overline{\tilde H})=\frac{\omega\mu\beta}{k_c^4}|\nabla_th|^2e_z$, and step 1.2 gives $\Phi=\frac12\frac{\omega\mu\beta}{k_c^4}k_c^2\int h^2=H_*^2\frac{\omega\mu\beta}{2k_c^2}\int_{R_{ab}}h_{mn}^2$ and the explicit norms of [[lem-em-rectangle-mode-profile-identities]] (c). [step 1.1, step 1.2, F1, F3, algebra]

2.2 [TM energy and flux] Let $e:=E_*e_{mn}$ and $w:=e_z\times\nabla_te$. With the TM amplitudes of [F1], $|\tilde E|^2=\frac{\beta^2}{k_c^4}|\nabla_te|^2+e^2$ and $|\tilde H|^2=\frac{\omega^2\varepsilon^2}{k_c^4}|\nabla_te|^2$, so $\langle u\rangle=\frac14\bigl(\frac{\varepsilon\beta^2+\mu\omega^2\varepsilon^2}{k_c^4}|\nabla_te|^2+\varepsilon e^2\bigr)$; integrating with step 1.2, $\int(\varepsilon|\tilde E|^2+\mu|\tilde H|^2)=\frac{\varepsilon\beta^2+\mu\omega^2\varepsilon^2+\varepsilon k_c^2}{k_c^2}\int e^2=\frac{2\varepsilon^2\mu\omega^2}{k_c^2}\int e^2$ because $\varepsilon\beta^2+\varepsilon k_c^2=\varepsilon\omega^2\varepsilon\mu$, giving $U=E_*^2\frac{\varepsilon^2\mu\omega^2}{2k_c^2}\int_{R_{ab}}e_{mn}^2$. For the flux, $\overline{\tilde H}=-\frac{i\omega\varepsilon}{k_c^2}w$, so $\tilde E\times\overline{\tilde H}=\bigl(\frac{i\beta}{k_c^2}\nabla_te+e_ze\bigr)\times\bigl(-\frac{i\omega\varepsilon}{k_c^2}w\bigr)=\frac{\omega\varepsilon\beta}{k_c^4}|\nabla_te|^2e_z+i\frac{\omega\varepsilon}{k_c^2}e\nabla_te$, using $\nabla_te\times w=e_z|\nabla_te|^2$ and $e_z\times w=-\nabla_te$ from [F3]; hence $\operatorname{Re}(\tilde E\times\overline{\tilde H})=\frac{\omega\varepsilon\beta}{k_c^4}|\nabla_te|^2e_z$ and $\Phi=E_*^2\frac{\omega\varepsilon\beta}{2k_c^2}\int_{R_{ab}}e_{mn}^2$. This proves clause (b) in both families with the norms recorded in the profile lemma. [step 1.1, step 1.2, F1, F3, algebra]

2.3 [Bounded propagating index set] Let a candidate with indices $(m,n)$ be propagating at frequency $\omega$, that is $\beta^2=\omega^2\varepsilon\mu-k_{mn}^2>0$. Then $k_{mn}^2<\varepsilon\mu\omega^2$; since $k_{mn}^2=(\frac{m\pi}{a})^2+(\frac{n\pi}{b})^2\ge(\frac{m\pi}{a})^2$ and $\ge(\frac{n\pi}{b})^2$ by [F3] and the definitions of the profiles, $(\frac{m\pi}{a})^2<\varepsilon\mu\omega^2$ and $(\frac{n\pi}{b})^2<\varepsilon\mu\omega^2$; taking positive square roots [F4] gives $m<\omega a\sqrt{\varepsilon\mu}/\pi$ and $n<\omega b\sqrt{\varepsilon\mu}/\pi$. Hence the set of propagating indices is contained in the displayed bounded box. [step 1.2, F3, F4, algebra]

3.1 [Energy transport and the speed relations] From steps 2.1 and 2.2, cancelling the common positive amplitude square $H_*^2$ or $E_*^2$ gives $\Phi=\frac{\beta}{\varepsilon\mu\omega}U$ in both families, since $\frac{\mu^2\varepsilon\omega^2}{2k_c^2}\frac{\beta}{\varepsilon\mu\omega}=\frac{\omega\mu\beta}{2k_c^2}$ and $\frac{\varepsilon^2\mu\omega^2}{2k_c^2}\frac{\beta}{\varepsilon\mu\omega}=\frac{\omega\varepsilon\beta}{2k_c^2}$. The branch function $\omega(\beta)=\sqrt{(\beta^2+k_c^2)/(\varepsilon\mu)}$ of [F4] is differentiable with $v_g:=\omega'(\beta)=\frac{\beta}{\varepsilon\mu\omega(\beta)}$: indeed for real $h\ne0$ the difference quotient is $\frac{2\beta+h}{\varepsilon\mu(\omega(\beta+h)+\omega(\beta))}$ (rationalizing the difference of square roots), and its limit as $h\to0$ is $\frac{2\beta}{2\varepsilon\mu\omega(\beta)}$ by [F4]; differentiating the identity $\varepsilon\mu\omega(\beta)^2=\beta^2+k_c^2$ with the chain rule gives the same value. Hence $\Phi=Uv_g$. Moreover $v_p:=\omega/\beta$ satisfies $v_pv_g=\frac{\omega}{\beta}\frac{\beta}{\varepsilon\mu\omega}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon\mu}$; $v_p=\frac{\sqrt{\beta^2+k_c^2}}{\sqrt{\varepsilon\mu}\beta}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon\mu}}\sqrt{1+\frac{k_c^2}{\beta^2}}>\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon\mu}}$ because $k_c^2>0$ and $\beta>0$; and $0<v_g=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon\mu}}\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{\beta^2+k_c^2}}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon\mu}}$. This proves clause (c). [step 2.1, step 2.2, F4, algebra]

4.1 [Scope] Clauses (a) and (b) are steps 1.2, 2.1 and 2.2, with the cross-section integrals evaluated by the profile and transverse identities and the explicit norms; clause (c) is step 3.1, with the transport relation $\Phi=Uv_g$ and the speed inequalities; clause (d) is step 2.3. Every step used the lossless separated candidate of the declared model, the ideal PEC boundary model and the choice-free local energy reading of the material-energy theorem: the period average is over time at a fixed position, the flux is through one cross-section, no signal-speed interpretation of $v_p$ is asserted, no bound on information or energy speeds in general dispersive media is claimed, and no empirical premise is used. This is clause (e). [step 1.2, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 2.3, step 3.1, F1] ∎
