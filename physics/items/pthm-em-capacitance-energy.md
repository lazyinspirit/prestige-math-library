## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice for the cited unit-Dirichlet solvability and trace interfaces, and its Countable Choice consequence for surface and integral interfaces ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-countable-choice]]). Let $(\Omega;D_1,\dots,D_m;\Omega_v)$ be the conductor geometry of [[post-em-electrostatic-conductor-model]], additionally with $\Omega$ a bounded $C^\infty$ ambient domain and $\Omega_v$ connected and with the **outer boundary $\partial\Omega$ grounded**: the data on $\partial\Omega$ are the constant Dirichlet datum $0$; let $\rho_v\equiv0$ on $\Omega_v$ (the pure capacitor configuration: no volume source), and let the framework constants be those of [[def-em-si-conventions]]. For each $j\in\{1,\dots,m\}$ let $\varphi_j\in C^\infty(\overline{\Omega_v})$ be the unit-potential solution of [[pthm-em-electrostatic-bvp-existence]](c) with the smooth data
$$\varphi_j|_{\partial D_k}=\delta_{jk},\qquad \varphi_j|_{\partial\Omega}=0,$$
so that $-\Delta\varphi_j=0$ on $\Omega_v$ with the Laplacian of [[def-laplacian-of-a-c2-function]], and let $\nu$ be the outward normal of $\Omega_v$ (so on $\partial D_j$ the normal $\nu$ points into the conductor). Define the **capacitance matrix** by
$$C_{jk}:=\varepsilon_0\int_{\partial D_j}\frac{\partial\varphi_k}{\partial\nu}\,dS,\qquad j,k=1,\dots,m,$$
whose entries carry farads (F), with the unit potentials read as numerical volt values; these are flux integrals of continuous functions over the compact embedded hypersurfaces $\partial D_j$ (normal derivative of [[def-classical-normal-derivative]]) ([[def-surface-integral-on-a-compact-c-one-hypersurface]], [[lem-em-vacuum-region-of-conductors-is-a-bounded-smooth-domain]]). Then:
(a) [Linearity and matrix form] for every $V=(V_1,\dots,V_m)\in\mathbb R^m$ the unique equilibrium potential with conductor potentials $V$ and grounded outer boundary is $\varphi^V=\sum_{j=1}^mV_j\varphi_j$, and the conductor charges of [[pthm-em-conductor-surface-charge]](d) are
$$Q_j(V)=\varepsilon_0\int_{\partial D_j}\frac{\partial\varphi^V}{\partial\nu}\,dS=\sum_{k=1}^mC_{jk}V_k,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad Q=CV.$$
(b) [Reciprocity] $C$ is symmetric: $C_{jk}=C_{kj}$ for all $j,k$; the symmetry is Green's symmetric pairing of the harmonic potentials $\varphi_j,\varphi_k$.
(c) [Energy identity] the field energy of the configuration is
$$U(V)=\frac{\varepsilon_0}{2}\int_{\Omega_v}|\nabla\varphi^V|^2\,d\lambda_3=\frac12V^{T}CV=\frac12\sum_{j=1}^mV_jQ_j(V),$$
and $U(V)>0$ for every $V\ne0$: $C$ is symmetric positive definite. In particular $U(V)\ge0$, with equality exactly for $V=0$, so every nonzero conductor-potential data set stores positive field energy.
(d) [One driven terminal] with only conductor $1$ driven and every other conductor grounded, $V=te_1$, where $e_1:=(1,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb R^m$: $Q_1=C_{11}t$, $Q_j=C_{j1}t$ for $j\ne1$, and $U=\tfrac12C_{11}t^2=\tfrac12Q_1V_1=Q_1^2/(2C_{11})$; the **capacitance of the arrangement** at conductor $1$ with respect to the grounded reference is the positive geometric number $C_{11}>0$, with $C_{11}=Q_1/V_1$ when $t=V_1\ne0$, and the energy identities $U=\tfrac12QV=Q^2/(2C)=\tfrac12CV^2$ hold with $C=C_{11}$, $Q=Q_1$, $V=V_1$. By (c) the positivity holds for every diagonal entry, $C_{jj}>0$.
(e) [Assembly reading] use the family $V_j(s)=sV_j$, $\varphi_s=s\varphi^V$, $Q_j(s)=sQ_j(V)$ for $0\le s\le1$, with fixed geometry and grounded reference. Prescribe the quasistatic external charge-transfer work by $dW_{\rm ext}=\sum_jV_j(s)dQ_j(s)$. Then $W_{\rm ext}=\int_0^1s\,ds\sum_jV_jQ_j(V)=U$. The potentials vary during assembly; only the final potentials and geometry are fixed. This derives the sheet-charge accounting directly and does not apply the volume-source theorem [[pthm-em-electrostatic-field-energy]] to singular sheets. The family is an accounting prescription, not an asserted time-dependent Maxwell/material solution.
(f) [Scope] the theorem assumes: the compact separated conductor geometry, the additional bounded smooth ambient domain, and connected vacuum region of the model, the grounded outer boundary $\partial\Omega$, zero volume source, and the existence and uniqueness of the unit-potential Dirichlet solutions ([[pthm-em-electrostatic-bvp-existence]](c), [[pthm-em-electrostatic-bvp-uniqueness]](a)). It asserts no solvability for arbitrary boundary data (the existence theorem is used only for the constant data above), no prescribed-total-charge problem, no dielectric or material response, no finite-conductivity or transient charging dynamics, no radiation, and no measurement; the matrix $C$ is a geometric object of the ideal electrostatic model, and infinite parallel planes require their own explicitly declared idealization, outside this bounded geometry.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice and Countable Choice as declared in the statement; the conductor geometry $(\Omega;D_1,\dots,D_m;\Omega_v)$ of the model in the bounded smooth ambient branch with $\Omega$ a bounded $C^\infty$ domain, $\Omega_v$ connected, outer boundary grounded and $\rho_v\equiv0$; the unit-potential solutions $\varphi_j\in C^\infty(\overline{\Omega_v})$ with $-\Delta\varphi_j=0$, $\varphi_j|_{\partial D_k}=\delta_{jk}$, $\varphi_j|_{\partial\Omega}=0$; the outward unit normal $\nu$ of $\Omega_v$; and the capacitance matrix $C_{jk}=\varepsilon_0\int_{\partial D_j}\partial\varphi_k/\partial\nu\,dS$.

[F1] The model and its vacuum geometry: in the bounded smooth ambient branch of [[post-em-electrostatic-conductor-model]], by [[lem-em-vacuum-region-of-conductors-is-a-bounded-smooth-domain]] the vacuum region $\Omega_v$ is a bounded $C^\infty$ domain (in particular a bounded $C^1$ domain), its boundary is the disjoint union $\partial\Omega\cup\partial D_1\cup\cdots\cup\partial D_m$ of nonempty compact embedded smooth hypersurfaces, and $\partial\Omega$ is nonempty; the model requires $\Omega_v$ connected and nonempty, carries the pure electrostatic regime and the fields of the framework, and its constants are those of [[def-em-si-conventions]] ([[post-em-electrostatic-conductor-model]], [[lem-em-vacuum-region-of-conductors-is-a-bounded-smooth-domain]], [[def-em-electrostatic-regime]], [[def-em-fields]]).

[F2] Unit-potential solvability and uniqueness: the datum equal to $\delta_{jk}$ on $\partial D_k$ and $0$ on $\partial\Omega$ is constant on each compact boundary piece, hence smooth on the disjoint union $\partial\Omega_v$; with $\rho_v\equiv0$ the conductor reading of [[pthm-em-electrostatic-bvp-existence]](c) is a special case of its Dirichlet branch, so there is exactly one $\varphi_j\in C^\infty(\overline{\Omega_v})$ with $-\Delta\varphi_j=0$ on $\Omega_v$ and the prescribed boundary values; uniqueness in the class of $C^\infty(\overline{\Omega_v})$ functions with identical boundary data is the Dirichlet uniqueness clause ([[pthm-em-electrostatic-bvp-existence]](a),(c), [[pthm-em-electrostatic-bvp-uniqueness]](a), [[def-laplacian-of-a-c2-function]], [[def-ck-euclidean-maps-and-diffeomorphisms]]).

[F3] Surface charges and fluxes: for an equilibrium solution of the model with the joint one-sided regularity, $Q_j=\int_{\partial D_j}\sigma\,dS=\varepsilon_0\int_{\partial D_j}\partial\varphi/\partial\nu\,dS$, where $\nu$ is the outward normal of the vacuum; the integrals exist as integrals of the continuous flux over the compact embedded hypersurfaces $\partial D_j$ ([[pthm-em-conductor-surface-charge]](d), [[def-surface-integral-on-a-compact-c-one-hypersurface]], [[def-classical-normal-derivative]]).

[F4] Green's first identity on the bounded $C^1$ domain $\Omega_v$: for real $u\in C^2(\overline{\Omega_v})$ and $v\in C^1(\overline{\Omega_v})$, $\int_{\Omega_v}(v\Delta u+Du\cdot Dv)\,d\lambda_3=\int_{\partial\Omega_v}v\,\partial_\nu u\,dS$ with the outward normal $\nu$; the boundary integral splits over the disjoint pieces $\partial\Omega,\partial D_1,\dots,\partial D_m$ ([[cor-first-green-identity-on-a-bounded-c-one-domain]], [F1]).

[F5] Positivity and constancy tools for (c): a continuous function on the compact $\overline{\Omega_v}$ is bounded and Borel, so for $\varphi^V\in C^\infty(\overline{\Omega_v})$ the function $|\nabla\varphi^V|^2$ is nonnegative, Borel, bounded and integrable over $\Omega_v$; a nonnegative measurable $f$ with $\int f\,d\lambda_3=0$ vanishes almost everywhere, and a continuous such $f$ on the connected open $\Omega_v$ then vanishes everywhere (otherwise a positive value would stay bounded below on a small Euclidean ball of positive finite measure); a totally differentiable function on a nonempty connected open Euclidean set with vanishing differential is constant; a function constant on the connected open $\Omega_v$ and continuous on $\overline{\Omega_v}$ has that constant equal to its value $0$ on the nonempty boundary piece $\partial\Omega$ ([[thm-nonnegative-integral-zero-iff-zero-almost-everywhere]], [[lem-euclidean-balls-have-positive-finite-lebesgue-measure]], [[thm-zero-derivative-on-connected-open-euclidean-set-iff-constant]], [[cor-continuous-functions-are-borel-measurable]], [[prop-order-and-scalar-rules-for-the-nonnegative-integral]], [[def-integral-over-a-measurable-set]]).

[F6] Path-work reading and the elementary integral: for open $U\subseteq\mathbb R^n$, $\phi\in C^1(U)$ and a piecewise $C^1$ path $\gamma:[a,b]\to U$, $\int_\gamma\nabla\phi\cdot d\mathbf r=\phi(\gamma(b))-\phi(\gamma(a))$; and $\int_0^1s\,ds=\tfrac12$ by the one-dimensional fundamental theorem ([[thm-gradient-theorem-for-line-integrals]], [[thm-ftc-second-part]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Setup and well-definedness: by [F1] the vacuum region $\Omega_v$ is a bounded $C^1$ domain (indeed $C^\infty$), connected, nonempty, with boundary the disjoint union of the nonempty compact pieces $\partial\Omega,\partial D_1,\dots,\partial D_m$, and with $\partial\Omega$ nonempty. By [F2] each unit-potential solution $\varphi_j\in C^\infty(\overline{\Omega_v})$ exists and is unique with $-\Delta\varphi_j=0$ and $\varphi_j|_{\partial D_k}=\delta_{jk}$, $\varphi_j|_{\partial\Omega}=0$. The normal $\nu$ is the outward unit normal of $\Omega_v$; it points into the conductor on each $\partial D_j$ [F1]. Each flux $\int_{\partial D_j}\partial\varphi_k/\partial\nu\,dS$ exists because $\partial\varphi_k/\partial\nu$ is continuous on the compact embedded hypersurface $\partial D_j$ carrying finite surface measure [F3], so $C_{jk}$ is well defined. [F1, F2, F3, given]

2.1 Linearity and the charge-potential map (a): fix $V\in\mathbb R^m$ and put $\varphi^V:=\sum_{j=1}^mV_j\varphi_j\in C^\infty(\overline{\Omega_v})$. By linearity of the Laplacian, $-\Delta\varphi^V=\sum_jV_j(-\Delta\varphi_j)=0$ on $\Omega_v$; on $\partial D_k$ one has $\varphi^V=\sum_jV_j\delta_{jk}=V_k$, and on $\partial\Omega$, $\varphi^V=0$. By the uniqueness clause of [F2], $\varphi^V$ is the unique equilibrium potential with conductor potentials $V$ and grounded outer boundary, and the solution is linear in $V$ by construction. Its charges are given by [F3] and the definition of $C$: for each $j$, $Q_j(V)=\varepsilon_0\int_{\partial D_j}\partial\varphi^V/\partial\nu\,dS=\varepsilon_0\sum_{k=1}^mV_k\int_{\partial D_j}\partial\varphi_k/\partial\nu\,dS=\sum_{k=1}^mC_{jk}V_k$, that is $Q=CV$. [F2, F3, given, step 1.1]

2.2 Reciprocity (b): apply Green's first identity [F4] on the bounded $C^1$ domain $\Omega_v$ twice, first with $u=\varphi_j$, $v=\varphi_k$ and then with $u=\varphi_k$, $v=\varphi_j$; both functions lie in $C^\infty(\overline{\Omega_v})$ and $\Delta\varphi_j=\Delta\varphi_k=0$, so subtracting the two identities gives $0=\int_{\partial\Omega_v}(\varphi_j\,\partial_\nu\varphi_k-\varphi_k\,\partial_\nu\varphi_j)\,dS$. The boundary splits into $\partial\Omega$ and the conductor surfaces [F4]; on $\partial\Omega$ both traces of $\varphi_j,\varphi_k$ vanish, so the outer piece contributes $0$, while on $\partial D_\ell$ the traces are $\delta_{j\ell}$ and $\delta_{k\ell}$, so the total reduces to $\int_{\partial D_j}\partial_\nu\varphi_k\,dS-\int_{\partial D_k}\partial_\nu\varphi_j\,dS$. Multiplying by $\varepsilon_0$ gives $C_{jk}=C_{kj}$ for all $j,k$; the symmetry is exactly Green's antisymmetric pairing of the two harmonic potentials. [F4, given, step 1.1]

3.1 Energy identity and positivity (c): apply Green's first identity [F4] once more with $u=v=\varphi^V$: $\int_{\Omega_v}(\varphi^V\Delta\varphi^V+|\nabla\varphi^V|^2)\,d\lambda_3=\int_{\partial\Omega_v}\varphi^V\partial_\nu\varphi^V\,dS$. The Laplacian term vanishes by [step 2.1], the outer boundary contributes $0$ because $\varphi^V$ vanishes on $\partial\Omega$, and on $\partial D_j$ the trace of $\varphi^V$ is the constant $V_j$, so $\int_{\partial\Omega_v}\varphi^V\partial_\nu\varphi^V\,dS=\sum_{j=1}^mV_j\int_{\partial D_j}\partial_\nu\varphi^V\,dS$. By [F3] and [step 2.1] this equals $\sum_jV_jQ_j(V)/\varepsilon_0=\frac1{\varepsilon_0}\sum_{j,k}V_jC_{jk}V_k=\frac1{\varepsilon_0}V^{T}CV$; multiplying by $\varepsilon_0/2$ gives the identity $U(V)=\frac{\varepsilon_0}{2}\int_{\Omega_v}|\nabla\varphi^V|^2\,d\lambda_3=\frac12V^{T}CV=\frac12\sum_jV_jQ_j(V)$. The energy is nonnegative because the integrand is; if $U(V)=0$, then $\int_{\Omega_v}|\nabla\varphi^V|^2\,d\lambda_3=0$, so $|\nabla\varphi^V|^2=0$ almost everywhere by [F5], hence everywhere by continuity on the connected open $\Omega_v$ [F5]; thus $\varphi^V$ is constant on $\Omega_v$ by [F5], and the constant is $0$ because $\varphi^V=0$ on the nonempty boundary piece $\partial\Omega$ and $\varphi^V$ is continuous on the closure [F5]; consequently $V_j=\varphi^V|_{\partial D_j}=0$ for every $j$, i.e. $V=0$. Hence $U(V)\ge0$ with equality exactly at $V=0$, and since $C$ is symmetric by [step 2.2], the symmetric bilinear form $V\mapsto\frac12V^{T}CV$ is positive definite. [F3, F4, F5, given, step 2.1, step 2.2]

4.1 One driven terminal (d): take $V=te_1$ with $t\in\mathbb R$. By [step 2.1], $\varphi^V=t\varphi_1$, $Q_1=C_{11}t$ and $Q_j=C_{j1}t$ for $j\ne1$; by [step 3.1], $U=\frac12C_{11}t^2=\frac12Q_1V_1$. For $t=0$ all charges and the energy vanish while the unit flux defining $C_{11}$ is unchanged; for $t\ne0$ one may divide by $t=V_1$ to obtain $C_{11}=Q_1/V_1$ and $U=Q_1^2/(2C_{11})$, using $C_{11}>0$ from the positive definiteness of [step 3.1] (take $V=e_1$: $U(e_1)=\frac12C_{11}>0$). Hence $C_{11}$ is the positive geometric capacitance of conductor $1$ with respect to the grounded reference, and the displayed identities $U=\frac12QV=Q^2/(2C)=\frac12CV^2$ hold with $C=C_{11}$, $Q=Q_1$, $V=V_1$. [given, step 2.1, step 3.1]

4.2 Assembly reading (e): for $0\le s\le1$ put $V(s):=sV$, $\varphi_s:=\varphi^{sV}=s\varphi^V$ by [step 2.1], and $Q_j(s):=Q_j(sV)=sQ_j(V)$, so that $dQ_j(s)=Q_j(V)\,ds$. The prescribed quasistatic external charge-transfer work is $dW_{\rm ext}=\sum_jV_j(s)\,dQ_j(s)=\sum_j(sV_j)Q_j(V)\,ds=s\,ds\sum_jV_jQ_j(V)$, and summing over the conductors and integrating over $s\in[0,1]$ gives $W_{\rm ext}=\bigl(\int_0^1s\,ds\bigr)\sum_jV_jQ_j(V)=\frac12\sum_jV_jQ_j(V)=U$ by [F6] and [step 3.1]. The family is an accounting prescription on the fixed geometry with grounded reference: the potentials vary during assembly, only the final potentials and geometry are fixed, and the sheet-charge accounting is derived directly rather than from the volume-source theorem [[pthm-em-electrostatic-field-energy]]; the path-work identity of [F6] gives the endpoint potential-drop reading along any interior piecewise $C^1$ path, with boundary endpoints understood as limits of the continuous closure traces. This is (e). [F6, given, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 Scope (f): the theorem used the compact separated conductor geometry and bounded smooth ambient branch with connected vacuum region [F1], the grounded outer boundary, the zero volume source, and the existence and uniqueness of the unit-potential Dirichlet solutions [F2]; the existence theorem was applied only to the constant data $V$ and $0$, so no solvability for arbitrary boundary data or for prescribed total charges is asserted, and no dielectric coefficient, material response, finite-conductivity or transient charging dynamics, radiation, measurement or apparatus claim enters [F1, F2]. The matrix $C$ is a geometric object of the ideal electrostatic model of [[post-em-electrostatic-conductor-model]], and the infinite parallel-plane example is a separate idealization outside this bounded geometry; this completes (f) and the theorem. [F1, F2, given, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 4.2] ∎
