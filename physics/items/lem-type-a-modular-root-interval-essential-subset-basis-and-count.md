## Statement

Let $F$ have characteristic $p>0$, let $N\ge2$, and let $\mu=(\mu_1\ge\cdots\ge\mu_N\ge0)$ be a polynomial highest weight. Let $L_N(\mu)$ be the nonzero Schur Weyl-cell simple at $q=1$, regarded as a left module by star, with its canonical highest vector $v$. Put $a_i=\mu_i-\mu_{i+1}$. For every $1\le r\le s<N$, define $B(i,j)=j-i+\sum_{t=i}^j a_t$ and $C(i,j)=j-i+\sum_{t=i}^{j-1}a_t$, reduced modulo $p$ for zero tests. A matching is an injection $d$ with $x\le d(x)$; it need not preserve order between different sources. Call $J\subseteq(r,s]$ essential when no $t<u$ outside $J$ in $[r,s+1]$ admits a zero-$B$ matching $\{t\}\cup(J\cap(t,u))\to[t,u)$. Write $\varepsilon_a(r,s)$ for the number of essential sets.

The vectors $T_{r,s}(J)v$ constructed by the explicit integral coefficient recursion in the proof, indexed by essential $J$, form a basis of $L_N(\mu)_{\mu-\alpha_r-\cdots-\alpha_s}$. In particular this weight-space dimension is $\varepsilon_a(r,s)$, including dimension zero. The construction has integer-polynomial coefficients, and its positive-word pivots remain valid in every field of characteristic $p$.

Define a root $x$ to be normal in $[x,y]$ when $a_x\ne0$ and its demands $m>x$ with $B(x,m)=0$ admit an injection $z$ satisfying $x<z(m)\le m$ and $B(z(m),m)=0$. If $r$ is not normal set $\nu_a(r,s)=0$; otherwise let it be the number of $x\in[r,s]$ normal in $[x,s]$ with $C(r,x)=0$. Set $\varepsilon'_a(r,t)=\varepsilon_{a+\omega_t}(r,t)$, where only coordinate $t$ is increased by one. Then, as ordinary integers,
$$\varepsilon_a(r,s)=\nu_a(r,s)+\sum_{u=r+1}^s\varepsilon'_a(r,u-1)\nu_a(u,s).$$
No restrictedness condition is imposed on $\mu$ or on the shifted auxiliary highest weights.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $F,N,\mu,r,s$ as in the statement, with the finite integer root intervals and modulo-$p$ zero tests just defined.

[F1] The actual Schur algebra is the endomorphism algebra of the sum of permutation modules and has its dominance cellular semistandard basis and Weyl cell modules, with canonical diagonal tableau element the projection onto its permutation summand ([[def-type-a-q-schur-algebra-and-weyl-modules]]). We use this basis and its cell-product definition, not a characteristic-zero highest-weight realization.

[F2] The cell rank-one multiplication implies that a nonzero form quotient is simple, remains simple on scalar extension, and that the nonzero cell quotients cover all simple modules ([[def-cellular-algebra-basis-and-cell-module]], [[thm-nonzero-cell-heads-classify-the-simple-modules-of-a-cellular-algebra]]). The local argument below proves the needed highest-vector, polynomial-matrix and weight-spanning properties explicitly.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Extend only to $K=F(t)$. This field is infinite, and extension preserves the rank of every finite Gram matrix. A nonzero Schur cell-form quotient stays simple after this extension: in any field a vector outside its radical generates the entire cell module by the cellular rank-one action, and the same argument applies to the extended nonzero Gram form. No algebraic closure or infinite-choice step is required. [F1, F2, construct]

2.1 At $q=1$, identify $\bigoplus _\nu M^\nu$ with the tensorword basis of $(K^N)^{\otimes m}, m=|\mu |$, by sending $m_\nu T_{d}$ to the word whose $j$th letter is the row containing entry $j$ in the row-standard tableau $t^\nu d$. This is the explicit right-coset/tensorword convention; for regular type and basis $d$, that letter is $j d^{-1}$ under right permutation notation. The symmetric-group action on tensor positions is transported through this map, so inverses in labels and right-position actions are fixed consistently. The symmetric group permutes the tensor positions. An endomorphism commuting with this permutation action has matrix coefficients constant on its orbits on pairs of words. Such orbits are indexed by nonnegative integer matrices $A=(A_{ab})$ with $\sum A_{ab}=m$, recording the number of positions with input $b$ and output $a$. Let $E_{A}$ be the matrix with entry 1 on that orbit and 0 elsewhere. The $E_{A}$ have disjoint support and form a basis of the endomorphism algebra. For a matrix $g$, $g^{\otimes m}=\sum _{A} (\prod _{ab} g_{ab}^{A_{ab}})E_{A}$. The transpose involution sends $E_{A}$ to $E_{A^T}$; it is precisely the $q=1$ Schur star (the corresponding semistandard Hom maps are transposes under the orthonormal tensorword/permutation forms). [step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Invertible-matrix evaluations span this Schur algebra over $K$. Indeed a linear functional annihilating all $g^{\otimes m}$ gives a polynomial $P(g)=\sum _{A} c_{A} g^A$ vanishing whenever $\det(g)\ne 0$. Then $P\det$ vanishes at every matrix over $K$. An elementary induction on the number of variables shows that a polynomial over an infinite field vanishing everywhere is zero: regard it as a polynomial in the last variable and use infinitely many values, then apply the induction to its coefficients. Thus $P\det=0$ as a polynomial, and the polynomial domain gives $P=0$, so all $c_{A}$ vanish. Finite-dimensional duality proves the spanning claim. Consequently the star-converted Schur simple is a simple rational left $\operatorname{GL}_N$-module, with the same submodules and composition multiplicities as the right Schur module. [step 2.1, construct]

4.1 The canonical semistandard tableau has weight $\mu$ and Schur form value 1. Indeed its diagonal Schur basis element is the projection onto the permutation summand $M^\mu $: the defining Hom map is the identity there and zero on the other summands. Its square is itself, so the defining cell-product coefficient, which is the canonical tableau norm, is 1. This argument uses the canonical diagonal projection only, not an idempotence assertion for all diagonal tableau elements. Every semistandard tableau weight $\nu$ is dominated by $\mu $: for each $k$, all entries $\le k$ occur in the first $k$ rows by column strictness, so there are at most $\mu _{1}+\cdots +\mu _{k}$ of them. The unique tableau of weight $\mu$ is the canonical one. Thus $L$ has highest weight $\mu$ with one-dimensional highest space $K v$, and its positive upper unipotent group fixes $v$: any nonconstant positive-root coefficient would have weight strictly above $\mu$. Torus weights are integer characters, distinct as Laurent monomials over $K$; no Lie-eigenvalue congruence substitutes for weight equality. [step 3.1, construct]

5.1 On the open set where leading principal minors are nonzero, Gaussian elimination gives $g=u^- d u^+$. The negative-unipotent coefficient operators on $v$ span $L$. To see this, let $W$ be their span and let $\ell$ annihilate $W$. Then $\ell (gv)=0$ on that open set because $u^+v=v$ and $dv$ is a scalar multiple of $v$. A polynomial matrix coefficient vanishing there vanishes identically by the same polynomial-identity argument, so $\ell$ annihilates the coefficient orbit of $v$ under all $\operatorname{GL}_{N}$. That orbit spans $L$ by simplicity. Hence $W=L$. [step 4.1, construct]

6.1 Positive coefficient operators detect every nonzero vector $x$ of weight $\mu -\gamma$. If they all had zero highest coefficient on $x$, the Gaussian factorization would imply that the highest coefficient of $gx$ was zero on the same open set: negative operators cannot raise a weight to $\mu$, and no weight exceeds $\mu$. The resulting polynomial identity would make that coefficient zero on the whole simple-module orbit of $x$, which spans $L$ and contains $v$, a contradiction. Thus some positive coefficient operator of weight $\gamma$ raises $x$ to a nonzero multiple of $v$. [step 5.1, construct]

7.1 For $\gamma =\alpha (r,s)$, every simple-root coefficient is 1. In a product expansion of a unipotent matrix, any monomial of total root weight $\gamma$ therefore has each root-variable exponent $0$ or $1$, and its roots are pairwise disjoint intervals covering $[r,s]$. They are exactly cut partitions of that interval. Put  $f_{a,b}=E_{b+1,a}, e_{i}=E_{i,i+1}, Y_{r,s}(U)=f_{r,u_{1}-1}f_{u_{1},u_{2}-1}\cdots f_{u_{k},s}$, for $U=\{u_{1}<\cdots <u_{k}\}\subset (r,s]$, with $Y(\varnothing )=f_{r,s}$. These words span the required negative coefficient space. Similarly the positive coefficient space is spanned by words using each $e_{r},\cdots ,e_{s}$ once. Root-matrix commutators expand every longer positive root into such simple words. Permutations of these simple letters are identified using $e_{i} e_{j}=e_{j} e_{i}$ for $|i-j|>1$; their relative orders on adjacent pairs determine the canonical representative  $Q_{r,s}(I)=e_{i_{k}}\cdots e_{i_{1}} \cdot \prod _{j\in [r,s]\setminus I}^{\mathrm{increasing}}e_{j}, I=\{i_{1}<\cdots <i_{k}\}\subset (r,s]$. Every word is carried to this representative by exchanging adjacent incomparable letters in the corresponding path partial order. Hence these $Q$ words span all positive tests at the interval weight. There are no divided powers greater than 1 or factorial denominators in this grade. [step 6.1, construct]

8.1 The actions and all commutator calculations below use $[E_{ab},E_{cd}]=\delta _{bc} E_{ad} -\delta _{da} E_{cb}, e_{i} v=0$, and $(E_{ii} -E_{i+1,i+1})v=a_{i} v$. They are integral matrix identities and apply to the actual Schur module. The construction descends from $K$ to $F$ because the resulting matrices have entries in $F$ and scalar extension preserves all ranks and linear relations. [step 7.1, construct]

9.1 For $M,U\subset (r,s]$, define $\zeta _{r,s}(M,U)$ recursively. For $M=\varnothing , \zeta (\varnothing ,U)=\prod _{j\in (r,s]\setminus U} C(r,j)$. For $M\ne \varnothing$ let $m=\min M$ and $M'=M\setminus \{m\}$. If $m\notin U$, put  $\zeta (M,U)=\zeta (M',U)/C(r,m)$. If $m\in U$, put  $\zeta (M,U)=[\zeta (M',U)-\zeta _{r,m-1}(\varnothing ,U\cap (r,m)) \zeta _{m,s}(M',U\cap (m,s])]/C(r,m)$. Every division here is performed in the integer polynomial ring before specialization. It is exact. To prove this, induct on $|M|$, keeping the stronger statement that $\zeta (M,U)=\zeta _{r,m-1}(\varnothing ,U\cap (r,m))P(C(r,m))$, where $P$ is an integer polynomial in the remaining differences independent of $r$ and of $U\cap (r,m)$. At the next smallest cut, use $C(r,j)=C(r,m)+C(m,j)$. If $m\notin U$, the numerator contains the explicit factor $C(r,m)$. If $m\in U$, the two terms are respectively evaluations $P(C+D)$ and $P(D)$, multiplied by the same left factor, with $C=C(r,m)$; their difference is divisible by $C$, since  $(C+D)^h-D^h=C \sum _{a=1}^h \binom{h}{a}C^{a-1}D^{h-a}$. This gives the stronger factorization at the next step. The base case is the displayed product. Thus all coefficients are integer polynomials even when their displayed denominators vanish modulo $p$. [step 8.1, construct]

10.1 Define  $T_{r,s}(M)=\sum _{U} \zeta _{r,s}(M,U)Y_{r,s}(U)$. The coefficient recursion is equivalently the exact negative-word recursion  $C(r,m)T_{r,s}(M)=T_{r,s}(M')-T_{r,m-1}(\varnothing )T_{m,s}(M')$.          $(T)$ This follows by separating cut words according to whether the cut $m$ occurs. Products of adjacent interval words concatenate with the boundary cut present; this is exactly the cut-word product in the numerator. [step 9.1, construct]

11.1 The following splitting identity is integral:  $T_{r,i-1}(E)T_{i,s}(F) =T_{r,s}(E\cup F)-T_{r,s}((E\setminus \{e\})\cup \{i\}\cup F)-C(e,i)T_{r,s}(E\cup \{i\}\cup F), (P)$ where $E\subset (r,i), F\subset (i,s], e=\max E$. If $E=\varnothing$ omit the middle term and set $e=r$. For $E=\varnothing$ this is $(T)$ rearranged at cut $i$. For nonempty $E$, remove its smallest $m$, insert $(T)$ on the left and apply the induction to the two shorter products. Use $C(r,i)=C(r,m)+C(m,i)$. The terms containing the extra cut $m$ cancel in pairs, and the remaining numerator is  $C(r,m)[T(E\cup F)-T((E\setminus \{e\})\cup \{i\}\cup F)-C(e,i)T(E\cup \{i\}\cup F)]$. Exact divisibility already proved permits cancellation in the integer polynomial ring; reduction to $F$ is made only afterwards. This proves $(P)$ by induction, including the singleton $E$ case. [step 10.1, construct]

12.1 Order subsets by their largest differing element, with the subset containing that element larger. Complements reverse this order. The transition from $T(M)$ to $Y (U)$ is unit triangular:  $T_{r,s}(M)=Y_{r,s}((r,s]\setminus M)+\sum _{U<(r,s]\setminus M} c_{U} Y_{r,s}(U)$.         $(U)$ Here is an explicit induction. First $(T)$, descending on $j$, gives  $T_{r,s}([j,s])=\sum _{U\subset (r,j)}(\prod _{d\in (r,j)\setminus U}C(r,d))Y_{r,s}(U)$. For $j=s+1$ this is the defining empty-$M$ product; to pass to $j$ subtract $T_{r,j-1}(\varnothing )T_{j,s}([j+1,s])$ in $(T)$. The terms with cut $j$ cancel, leaving the indicated sum divided by the common factor $C(r,j)$. At $j=r+1$ this gives $T((r,s])=Y(\varnothing )$. For any other $M$ choose $i\notin M$, split $M=E\cup F$ around $i$, and rearrange $(P)$. The product of the two shorter leading $Y$ words is the cut word for the complement of $M$, with coefficient 1. The two other $T$ terms have strictly larger $M$, hence strictly smaller complementary leading cuts. Induct on interval length and then downward on $M$. This proves $(U)$. Therefore the $T$ words span precisely the same interval negative coefficient space as the $Y$ words. [step 11.1, construct]

13.1 If $J$ has consecutive components $[\beta _{1},\gamma _{1}],\cdots ,[\beta _{q},\gamma _{q}]$, matrix commutation and $(T)$ give, for $i<s, e_{i} T_{r,s}(J)v=0$ unless $i=\beta _{a}-1$ for some $a$;  $e_{\beta _{a}-1}T_{r,s}(J)v =-T_{r,\beta _{a}-2}(J\cap (r,\beta _{a}-1)) T_{\beta _{a},s}((\beta _{a},\gamma _{a}]\cup \bigcup _{b>a}[\beta _{b},\gamma _{b}])v$.                  $(X)$ Empty prefix operators are interpreted as 1. The endpoint action, with $h=\max J$, is  $e_{s} T (J)v=T_{r,s-1}(J\setminus \{h\})v+B(h,s)T_{r,s-1}(J)v$  if $h<s$;  $e_{s} T (J)v=T_{r,s-1}(J\setminus \{s\})v$                         if $h=s$;  $e_{s} T(\varnothing )v=B(r,s)T_{r,s-1}(\varnothing )v$.                    $(E)$ [step 12.1, construct]

14.1 These formulas follow directly as follows. In the empty-$J$ expansion, group $Y$ words by membership of $i$ and $i+1$ in their cuts. The coefficient of each residual word cancels through  $-C(r,i+1)+C(r,i)+(a_{i}+1)=0$ for $i<s$. At the endpoint the two contributions are $C(r,s)$ and $a_{s}$, whose sum is $B(r,s)$, giving $(E)$. For general $J$, remove $m=\min J$ in $(T)$. For $i<m-1$ the empty-prefix cancellation is unchanged. At $i=m-1$, the first term has zero action; on the product term the empty-prefix endpoint coefficient is $B(r,m-1)+1=C(r,m)$, since the later negative interval increases its adjacent Cartan value by 1. This cancels the displayed denominator and gives the first component case of $(X)$. For later component starts the positive operator commutes with the initial prefix, and applying the induction to both terms in $(T)$ is exactly the same $(T)$ recursion for the shorter first factor in $(X)$. All other internal indices give two zero terms. Applying the endpoint induction to $(T)$ gives $(E)$; in the singleton case its numerator is  $B(r,s)T_{r,s-1}(\varnothing )-B(m,s)T_{r,m-1}(\varnothing )T_{m,s-1}(\varnothing )$, and $B(r,s)-B(m,s)=C(r,m)$ yields $(E)$ by $(T)$. The general case cancels in the same way after deleting the maximal cut. All identities were derived integrally before reduction, so no denominator is cancelled in $F$. [step 13.1, construct]

15.1 Set $\Xi _{r,s}(J)$ by  $e_{r} e_{r+1}\cdots e_{s} T_{r,s}(J)v=\Xi _{r,s}(J)v$. The endpoint calculation and $(T)$ give  $\Xi (\varnothing )=\prod _{a=r}^s B (r,a), \Xi (J)=[\Xi (J\setminus \{m\})-\Xi _{r,m-1}(\varnothing )\Xi _{m,s}(J\setminus \{m\})]/C(r,m), m=\min J$.     $(C)$ Again this is an integer polynomial: it is the actual integral highest coefficient of a matrix-word action, or follows by the same difference-divisibility induction as $\zeta$. The product in the empty case is essential; the historical printed sum in 1995 Definition two point nine is not used. [step 14.1, construct]

16.1 If $J$ has a bounded zero-$B$ matching $d:J\to [r,s]$, with image $D$, then  $\Xi_{r,s}(J)\equiv\prod_{a\in[r,s]\setminus D}B(r,a)\pmod p$.                     $(M)$ There is a polynomial proof preventing invalid modular cancellation. The stronger identity, for $m=\min J$ (and $m=s+1$ when $J=\varnothing$), is  $\Xi _{r,s}(J)=\prod _{t\in [r,s]\setminus D}B(r,t) +[\prod _{t=r}^{m-1}B(r,t)] \sum _{j\in J}B(j,d(j))P_{j}(C(r,m))$, where each $P_{j}$ has integer-polynomial coefficients independent of $r$. The empty case is the defining product. Remove the smallest $m$, put $x=C(r,m), b_{t}=B(m,t), v=d(m)$, and $D'=D\setminus \{v\}$. The two induction formulas for $J\setminus \{m\}$, starting respectively at $r$ and $m$, have exactly the same $P_{j}$. Their left factors differ by replacing each $b_{t}$ with $x+b_{t}$. Substitute them into $(C)$, factoring $A=\prod _{t=r}^{m-1}B(r,t)$. The principal numerator is  $\prod _{t\in [m,s]\setminus D'}(x+b_{t})-\prod _{t\in [m,s]\setminus D'}b_{t} =x \prod _{t\in [m,s]\setminus D}(x+b_{t}) +b_{v}[\prod _{t\in [m,s]\setminus D}(x+b_{t})-\prod _{t\in [m,s]\setminus D}b_{t}]$. The bracket on the second line is divisible by $x$ in the integer polynomial ring. Every old error term is likewise a difference $R(x)-R(0)$, divided by $x$, because its prefactor and $P_{j}$ undergo the same translation. Thus after the exact division the first term is the required product with $D$ removed, the second is an integer-polynomial multiple of the new matched generator $b_{v}=B(m,d(m))$, and the remaining terms are multiples of the old matched generators. For complete control of the universal cancellation, replace the variable $a_{r}$ by the formal coordinate $x=C(r,m)$, since $m>r$ and its coefficient in $C(r,m)$ is 1. Thus the integer polynomial ring is $\mathbb{Z}[x,\text{remaining }a]$. Every matched generator $B(j,d(j))$ has $j\ge m$ and is independent of $x$. Its quotient is therefore a polynomial ring in $x$ over the remaining coefficient quotient, so multiplication by $x$ is injective even if that coefficient quotient has torsion or zero divisors. The exact paired divisions retain the matched ideal before specialization; no cancellation in a characteristic-$p$ coefficient field occurs. This proves the stronger identity, hence $(M)$, without any division modulo $p$. [step 15.1, construct]

17.1 $T_{r,s}(J)v=0$ if and only if there is an endpoint $u\in (r,s+1]\setminus J$ and a bounded zero-$B$ matching  $\{r\}\cup (J\cap (r,u))\to [r,u)$.                                      $(Z)$ Write $J$ as above and set $\beta _{q+1}=s+2$. Every possible witness can be enlarged within its gap to $u=\beta _{a}-1$ for some $a\in [1,q+1]$, without changing its matched left set. Thus $(Z)$ is equivalent to one of the following finite conditions $\pi (a)$: the earlier components $\bigcup _{b<a}[\beta _{b},\gamma _{b}]$ have a bounded zero-$B$ matching into $[r,\beta _{a}-2]$, and there is an unused zero-$B$ target for the additional left point $r$. [step 16.1, construct]

18.1 Induct on interval length. The single-root case is $f_{r} v=0$ iff $a_{r}=0$ modulo $p$, by $e_{r} f_{r} v=a_{r} v$ and positive detection, exactly $(Z)$. Suppose $\pi (a)$ holds. For each component-start action in $(X)$, if its index $b <a$, the tail has the shorter witness obtained from the existing matching by taking its start $\beta _{b}$ as the extra left point; if $b\ge a$, the prefix has the earlier witness $\pi (a)$. Thus each such product is zero by the induction. All other $e_{i}$ with $i<s$ annihilate by $(X)$. If $a\le q$, the endpoint witness is $\le \beta _{q}-1$. Both shorter terms in $(E)$ retain it after the last cut $\gamma _{q}$ is deleted, so they are zero by induction. If $a=q+1$, all $J$ has a regular matching $D$ and its unused zero-$B$ target $r$ gives $\Xi (J)=0$ by $(M)$. Since $e_{i} T (J)v=0$ for $i<s$, any positive simple-word test of the full interval is either zero immediately or is forced to act in the order $s,s-1,\cdots ,r$: a smaller out-of-order first letter commutes past all the already acted larger letters and kills $T(J)v$. Its only possible surviving value is therefore $\Xi (J)$, which is zero. Positive detection from the modular foundation already proved gives $T(J)v=0$. [step 17.1, construct]

19.1 Conversely suppose $T(J)v=0$ but no $\pi (a)$ holds. Its earlier prefix in each $(X)$ is nonzero by induction; otherwise the shorter witness would already give one of the forbidden $\pi (a)$. Separated prefix and tail operators in $(X)$ commute, and their product on $v$ is nonzero whenever both factors are nonzero: choose positive tests raising the two separate factors to nonzero multiples of $v$, commute them across the opposite negative factor, and their product raises the product to the nonzero product scalar. Thus each tail in $(X)$ must be zero. [step 18.1, construct]

20.1 Work backwards through the components. For the last component, its zero tail gives a bounded zero-$B$ matching of all $[\beta _{q},\gamma _{q}]$ into $[\beta _{q},s]$. In general the zero tail beginning $\beta _{b}$ gives a matching of components $b,\cdots ,a-1$ into $[\beta _{b},\beta _{a}-2]$ for some $a>b$. All later components $a,\ldots,q$ already have a regular matching by the backward induction, using targets $\ge \beta _{a}$. These two target ranges are disjoint, so their union matches all components $b,\cdots ,q$. Thus all $J$ is regular. But $T(J)v=0$ implies $\Xi (J)=0$, and $(M)$ then supplies an unused target $a$ with $B(r,a)=0$. Match $r$ to that target to obtain $\pi (q+1)$, a contradiction. This proves $(Z)$. [step 19.1, construct]

21.1 It follows immediately that $J$ is nonessential iff $T_{i,s}(J\cap (i,s])v=0$ for some $i\in [r,s]\setminus J$: its witness is precisely the $t=i$ witness in the essential predicate. [step 20.1, construct]

22.1 If $J$ is nonessential and $T(J)v\ne 0$, choose $i\in (r,s]\setminus J$ with $T_{i,s}(J\cap (i,s])v=0$ by $(Z)$; $i=r$ would have made $T(J)v=0$ already. Put $E=J\cap (r,i), F=J\cap (i,s], e=\max E$ if nonempty. The splitting identity $(P)$ gives  $T(J)v=T((E\setminus \{e\})\cup \{i\}\cup F)v+C(e,i)T(J\cup \{i\})v$, with the first term omitted if $E=\varnothing$ and $e=r$. Both new subsets are strictly larger than $J$. Repeating terminates because there are finitely many subsets; each nonessential vector is expressed through larger essential ones or zero. Combining with $(U)$ and the modular foundation already proved shows that essential $T$ vectors span the root weight space. If there are no essential sets, the result is the zero space; no division is involved in this straightening. [step 21.1, construct]

23.1 For $I=[b_{1},c_{1}]\cup \cdots \cup [b_{H},c_{H}]$ and $J=[\beta _{1},\gamma _{1}]\cup \cdots \cup [\beta _{q},\gamma _{q}]$, write $I\ll J$ when each block of $I$ is a prefix of a block of $J$: $b_{a}=\beta _{u_{a}}, c_{a}\le \gamma _{u_{a}}$. Put  $\Delta _{0}=\bigcup _{b<u_{1}}J_{b}, \Delta _{a}=(J_{u_{a}}\setminus I_{a})\cup \bigcup _{u_{a}<b<u_{a+1}}J_{b}, \Delta _{H}=(J_{u_{H}}\setminus I_{H})\cup \bigcup _{b>u_{H}}J_{b}$. Call $I$ linked to $J$ if every $\Delta _{a}$ admits a bounded zero-$B$ matching to targets $\le b_{a+1}-2$, with $b_{H+1}=s+2$. If $H=0$ this means all $J$ is regularly matched to $(r,s]$. Define $\widehat{J}$ as the least linked $I$ in the subset order; it exists since $I=J$ is linked. [step 22.1, construct]

24.1 Let $h(I,J)$ be the highest coefficient $Q(I)T(J)v=h(I,J)v$. The action formulas give  $h(I,J)=0$ unless $I\ll J$, and $h(I,J)=\pm \Xi _{r,b_{1}-2}(\Delta _{0}) \prod _{a=1}^{H-1}\Xi _{c_{a},b_{a+1}-2}(\Delta _{a}) \Xi _{c_{H},s}(\Delta _{H})$ if $I\ll J$.     $(H)$ For $H=0$ use $h(\varnothing ,J)=\Xi _{r,s}(J)$. The signs are the explicit signs from the occurrences of $(X)$, hence $\pm 1$ and irrelevant to zero/nonzero tests. [step 23.1, construct]

25.1 To verify $(H)$, factor the positive word exactly as  $Q(I)=Q_{r,b_{1}-2}(\varnothing ) Q_{b_{1},b_{2}-2}((b_{1},c_{1}])\cdots Q_{b_{H},s}((b_{H},c_{H}]) e_{b_{1}-1}e_{b_{2}-1}\cdots e_{b_{H} -1}$. This is just the canonical $Q$ word reordered by commutations of letters at distance >1. Apply the rightmost displayed gap letters using $(X)$. It must be the endpoint of its $I$ block: if $i+1$ were in that same block and in $\widehat{J}$, it would start a $\widehat{J}$ block at $i+1$, hence also a $J$ block by $\ll$, contradicting $i\in J$. 33.1 All entries of the $T/Q$ matrices are reduced integer polynomials in the $a_{i}$. 34.1 Fix a prime $p$, a rank $l\ge 1$, and arbitrary nonnegative fundamental-weight coordinates $\Lambda =(\lambda _{1},\cdots ,\lambda _{l})$. No $p$-restricted or $p$-small coordinate assumption is imposed. All $B,C,\lambda$ zero/nonzero tests below are in $F_{p}$; all cardinalities are ordinary nonnegative integers. For $1\le a\le b\le l$ define $B(a,b)=b-a+\sum _{k=a}^b \lambda _{k}$, $C(a,b)=b-a+\sum _{k=a}^{b-1}\lambda _{k}$. The identities used throughout are $B(a,b)=C(a,b)+\lambda _{b}$ and, for $a\le x\le b$, $B(a,b)=C(a,x)+B(x,b)$ and $C(a,b)=C(a,x)+C(x,b)$. For integers $r\le s$, $(r,s]=\{r+1,\cdots ,s\}$. An injection $f:M\to N$ is **weakly increasing** here when $f(j)\ge j$ for every $j$; it need NOT preserve the order of different source elements. Weakly decreasing means $f(j)\le j$. This agrees with Definition one point twelve and is important for the augmenting paths below. [step 33.1, construct]

35.1 A subset $J\subset (r,s]$ is **nonessential** if there are $t<u$ in $[r,s+1]\setminus J$ and an injection $d:\{t\}\cup (J\cap (t,u))\to [t,u)$ with $d(x)\ge x$ and $B(x,d(x))=0$ for every source $x$. Otherwise $J$ is essential. Write $E(r,s)$ for this family and $\varepsilon (r,s)=|E(r,s)|$. A subset $J\subset (r,s]$ is **regular** if it has an injection $f:J\to (r,s]$ with $f(j)\ge j$ and $B(j,f(j))=0$. The empty subset is regular. [step 34.1, construct]

36.1 An index $a$ is **normal in $[a,b]$** if $\lambda _{a}\ne 0$ and its demand set $M=\{m\in (a,b]:B(a,m)=0\}$ has a compensation injection $z:M\to (a,b]$ with $z(m)\le m$ and $B(z(m),m)=0$. Define $\sigma (a,b)$ as its indicator. Also set $\sigma (a,a-1)=1$. The root normality depends only on $[a,b]$; an ambient earlier endpoint does not change it. [step 35.1, construct]

37.1 If $r$ is not normal, put $N(r,s)=\varnothing$. If $r$ is normal, set $N(r,s)=\{a\in[r,s]:a\text{ is normal in }[a,s]\text{ and }C(r,a)=0\}$, $\nu (r,s)=|N(r,s)|$. The primed functions refer to the last-interval-coordinate shift: $\varepsilon '_\Lambda (r,s)=\varepsilon _{\Lambda +\omega _{s}}(r,s)$, $\nu '_\Lambda (r,s)=\nu _{\Lambda +\omega _{s}}(r,s)$. Only $\lambda _{s}$ is increased by 1. An interval ending before $s$ sees no effect of that shift. All shifted weights remain dominant. [step 36.1, construct]

38.1 The desired count recurrence, for every $1\le r\le s\le l$, is $\varepsilon _\Lambda (r,s)=\nu _\Lambda (r,s)+\sum _{u=r+1}^s \nu _\Lambda (u,s) \varepsilon '_\Lambda (r,u-1)$.  $(R)$ In particular $r=i,s=l$ gives the first-level count formula with the final fundamental coordinate shifted at $u-1$, not at $l$ or at the first coordinate. [step 37.1, construct]

39.1 Order subsets colexicographically: at the largest element of their symmetric difference, the subset containing that element is larger. The regular family is finite and nonempty, so it has a unique maximum $P$. **Claim:** an essential regular subset exists iff $r$ is normal; if it exists it is exactly $P$. [step 38.1, construct]

40.1 First let $J$ be regular and essential, with matching $f$. If $\lambda _{r}=0$, add the source $r$ with $f(r)=r$; this is disjoint from $f(J)$, whose images are $>r$. It is a forbidden witness with endpoints $r,s+1$. Hence $\lambda _{r}\ne 0$. Let $R=\{m\in (r,s]:B(r,m)=0\}$. Every $m\in R$ must belong to $f(J)$, since an unused $m$ could be assigned to the extra source $r$ and would again give a forbidden witness. For each $m\in R$ choose $j=f^{-1}(m)$. Then $j\le m$ and $C(r,j)=B(r,m)-B(j,m)=0$; hence $j$ compensates $m$. These $j$ are distinct, proving $r$ is normal. Moreover, if $I>J$ were another regular subset, let $t$ be the largest element in $I\setminus J$ at the highest difference. The subsets agree above $t$. Restrict a regular matching of $I$ to $\{t\}\cup (J\cap (t,s])$; its targets lie in $[t,s+1)$ and are distinct. Since $t\notin J$, this is a forbidden witness for $J$. Thus $J=P$. [step 39.1, construct]

41.1 Conversely assume $r$ normal and let $J=P$ with a regular matching $f$. Suppose a forbidden witness $d$ has endpoints $t<u$. If $t>r$, the subset $I=\{t\}\cup (J\cap (t,s])$ is regular: use $d$ on its sources below $u$ and use $f$ on $J\ge u$. Their targets are respectively $<u$ and $\ge u$, so are disjoint. Since $t\notin J$ and all larger elements are retained, $I>J$, a contradiction. [step 40.1, construct]

42.1 It remains to treat $t=r$. Put $J_{0}=J\cap (r,u), \delta =d|J_{0}$, and $m_{0}=d(r)$. The target $m_{0}$ is not in $\delta (J_{0})$, and $m_{0}>r$ since $\lambda _{r}\ne 0$. Let $R_{0}=\{m\in (r,u):B(r,m)=0\}$. Normality gives a compensation injection $z$ on $R_{0}$; its targets satisfy $r<z(m)\le m<u$ and $C(r,z(m))=0$. Begin at the unused target $m_{0}$. If $z(m_{0})\notin J_{0}$, add that new source and assign it $m_{0}$. Otherwise set $j_{1}=z(m_{0})$, reassign $j_{1}$ to $m_{0}$, and release $m_{1}=\delta (j_{1})$. Then $B(r,m_{1})=C(r,j_{1})+B(j_{1},m_{1})=0$, so $m_{1}\in R_{0}$. Continue: use compensation $z(m_{1})$, reassign it to $m_{1}$ if it lies in $J_{0}$, and release its old $\delta$-target. All targets and compensation sources along this path are distinct: $\delta$ and $z$ are injective, and a cycle would force the initially unused $m_{0}$ to be a $\delta$-image. Finiteness therefore ends at a source $z(m_{q})\notin J_{0}$. Every reassignment has $B=0$ and respects the lower-bound constraint $z(m)\le m$. Keep $\delta$ on untouched $J_{0}$ and $f$ on $J\ge u$; those latter targets are $\ge u$ and disjoint. This constructs a regular matching for $J$ together with one new source in $(r,u)$, contradicting maximality. Thus $J$ is essential. [step 41.1, construct]

43.1 This proves the claim, including uniqueness, without assuming a source theorem or using a representation-theoretic count. Two elementary consequences of the witness definition will be used. **Truncation.** If $M$ is essential in $(r,s]$ and $q\in (r,s]\setminus M$, then $M\cap (r,q-1]$ is essential in $(r,q-1]$. Any forbidden witness for the truncated interval has endpoints $\le q$, also outside $M$, and is a witness in the original interval. **Concatenation.** If $P$ is essential in $(r,v-2]$ and $J$ essential in $(v,s]$, then $P\cup \{v\}\cup J$ is essential in $(r,s]$. A forbidden witness wholly to the left or right gives the corresponding forbidden witness. A witness crossing $v$ contains $v$ as a source; restrict it to $\{v\}\cup (J\cap (v,u))$. Since all such sources and their targets are $\ge v$, this is a forbidden witness for $J$. The virtual case $v=r+1$ uses $P=\varnothing$ and the same restriction argument. [step 42.1, construct]

44.1 Let $M$ be essential. If it is regular it is the unique $P(r,s)$ of the regular-essential matching argument already proved, and $\sigma (r,s)=1$. Otherwise let $v$ be the largest element of $M$ satisfying $v-1\notin M$ and $M\cap (r,v-2]\text{ is regular in } (r,v-2]$. Such $v$ exists: take $\min M$, with empty prefix. The prefix is essential by truncation at $q=v-1$, hence equals the unique $P(r,v-2)$; in particular $\sigma(r,v-2)=1$ (or the virtual value 1). [step 43.1, construct]

45.1 We show $J=M\cap (v,s]$ is essential. If it had a witness $t<u$ with $t>v$, that is already a forbidden witness for $M$. If $t=v$, the witness matches $M\cap (v,u)$ into $(v,u)$; combine it with the regular prefix matching, whose images are $\le v-2$. Because $v-1\notin M$, this makes $M\cap (r,u)$ regular in $(r,u-1]$. If $u=s+1$ or there is no element of $M$ above $u$, it makes $M$ regular, contrary to assumption. Otherwise let $q=\min (M\cap (u,s])$; then $q\ge u+1, q-1\notin M$, and that same matching makes $M\cap (r,q-2]$ regular. This contradicts maximality of $v$. Thus $J$ is essential, and $M=P(r,v-2)\cup \{v\}\cup J$. [step 44.1, construct]

46.1 The decomposition is unique. Indeed, in a concatenated set with essential tail $J$, no larger $m>v$ can meet the same two prefix conditions: a regular matching for its prefix, restricted to $\{v\}\cup (J\cap (v,m-1))$, would be a forbidden witness for $J$ with endpoints $v,m-1$. The regular essential case cannot equal a concatenated case, because a regular matching of the whole set restricted to $\{v\}\cup J$ would make $J$ nonessential with endpoints $v,s+1$. [step 45.1, construct]

47.1 Iterating this unique decomposition gives a bijection between essential subsets and **$\sigma$-cut paths** $Q=\{q_{1}<\cdots <q_{f}\}\subset (r,s]$ satisfying $\sigma (r,q_{1}-2)\sigma (q_{1},q_{2}-2)\cdots \sigma (q_{f-1},q_{f} -2)\sigma (q_{f},s)=1$. For $f=0$ the condition is $\sigma (r,s)=1$. The inverse recursively inserts the unique regular essential prefix $P$ and the cut point, so this is a genuine bijection, not merely equal cardinalities. In particular $\varepsilon (r,s)$ counts these paths. [step 46.1, construct]

48.1 For a fixed $r$, $B(z,m)=B(r,m)-C(r,z)$. For a demand $m$ with $B(r,m)=0$, its eligible compensation positions are exactly $z\in (r,m]$ with $C(r,z)=0$. This is a prefix/Ferrers matching problem. A compensation injection exists iff at every cutoff $x$ the number of demands $\le x$ does not exceed the number of eligible compensation positions $\le x$: necessity follows by counting distinct targets; sufficiency follows by pairing increasing demands with increasing available eligible positions. This is a complete greedy matching proof. [step 47.1, construct]

49.1 If $\lambda _{r}\ne 0$, initialize a stack with $r$. Scan $m=r+1,\cdots ,s$. When $\lambda _{m}\ne 0$, push $m$ if $C(r,m)=0$, pop the last push if $B(r,m)=0$, and otherwise do nothing. These push/pop cases are disjoint, since $B-C=\lambda _{m}$. When $\lambda _{m}=0$ and $B=C=0$, the position supplies and consumes its own compensation slot, so it changes neither the matching inequality nor the stack; all other zero-coordinate positions are likewise neutral. Kill the root walk permanently if its initial push $r$ is popped. The prefix matching inequalities prove that $r$ is normal exactly when the walk is not killed. [step 48.1, construct]

50.1 When it is not killed, its final stack is exactly $N(r,s)$. A pushed position $a$ has $C(r,a)=0$ and $\lambda _{a}\ne 0$. For every $m\ge a$ the $B,C$ identities give $B(a,m)=B(r,m)$ and $C(a,m)=C(r,m)$. Thus $a$ is normal precisely when no suffix after it has excess pops over pushes, equivalently when its push survives the last-in-first-out matching. This proves the claimed exact stack interpretation of $\nu$ and $N$. For a killed root, $N$ is empty by definition. The same proof applies verbatim to every shifted weight. [step 49.1, construct]

51.1 We record two consequences of the stack, with proofs exposing the endpoint shift. **Bridge lemma.** Suppose $x<y, \sigma (x,y-2)=1$ and $\nu '(x,y-1)=0$. If $y$ is normal in $[y,s]$, then $N(x,s)=\{x\} \sqcup N(y,s)$, and $x$ precedes all indices of $N(y,s)$. [step 50.1, construct]

52.1 For $y=x+1$, $\nu'(x,x)=0$ means $\lambda _{x}+1=0$; hence $\lambda _{x}=-1\ne 0$ and $C(x,y)=1+\lambda _{x}=0$. This proves the claim by the stack identities. For $y\ge x+2$, the unshifted walk is alive through $y-2$. Shifting only $\lambda _{y-1}$ cannot affect its earlier steps. To make $\nu '=0$ at its final step, the shifted walk must pop its last surviving root: its prior stack therefore has height 1 and $B(x,y-1)+1=C(x,y)=0$, with $\lambda _{y-1}+1\ne 0$. In the original walk $B(x,y-1)=-1\ne 0$. Also $C(x,y-1)=-1-\lambda _{y-1}\ne 0$ by the last nonzero condition. Thus the original $y-1$ step is neutral, and the stack before $y$ consists only of $x$. Since $C(x,y)=0$ and $y$ is normal, $y$ is a nonzero-coordinate push; for every later position the walks based at $x$ and $y$ have identical events. The stack is consequently $\{x\}$ followed by $N(y,s)$, as claimed. This argument works in $p=2$ as well, since $-1$ is always nonzero. [step 51.1, construct]

53.1 **Consecutive-normal lemma.** In a nonempty $N(r,s)$ let $a<b$ be consecutive final stack entries. Then $\sigma (a,b-2)=1$ and $\nu '(a,b-1)=0$. First normality of $a$ gives prefix normality and hence $\sigma =1$. Both final entries satisfy $C(r,a)=C(r,b)=0$, so $C(a,b)=0$. Before $b$ is pushed, the stack based at $a$ has exactly its initial entry: any additional unmatched push earlier than $b$ would survive whenever $b$ survives (last-in-first-out pops cannot remove it before removing $b$), and would be an intervening final normal entry. The step $b-1$ is neutral in the original walk. Indeed $B(a,b-1)=-1$; if $C(a,b-1)=0$, then $\lambda _{b-1}=-1$, and $b-1$ would be such an intervening surviving push. Therefore $\lambda _{b-1}+1\ne 0$. Increasing $\lambda _{b-1}$ by 1 creates exactly one final pop of the height-one stack, proving $\nu '=0$. The adjacent case is $\lambda _{a}+1=C(a,b)=0$, giving $\nu '(a,a)=0$ directly. These conclusions also apply to normal sets under a shift at some final endpoint $s$: when $a<b\le s$, their bridge intervals end before $s$, so their $B,C$ constants are unchanged by the outer shift. The $\lambda _{b}$ term is absent from $C(a,b)$, which is why this remains true even when $b=s$. [step 52.1, construct]

54.1 For a $\sigma$-cut path $M=\{m_{1}<\cdots <m_{h}\}$, put $m_{0}=r$. Retain exactly the cut points $m_{w}$ for which $\nu '(m_{w-1},m_{w}-1)>0$. Call the retained set $Q=\{q_{1}<\cdots <q_{f}\}$. Partition $M$ into blocks between retained cuts. Every internal removed cut has prime-zero bridge; the next retained cut has prime-positive boundary. The final block is similarly an initial segment of $N(q_{f},s)$, excluding $q_{f}$. 55.1 More explicitly, the block containing a removed-cut chain $x=a_0<a_1<\cdots<a_t$ before a retained endpoint $y$ has $\sigma (a_{j},a_{j+1}-2)=1$ and prime-zero between consecutive removed cuts, while $\nu'(a_t,y-1)>0$. 56.1 Conversely, for any $Q=\{q_{1}<\cdots <q_{f}\}$ choose integers $1\le a_{v}\le \nu '(q_{v},q_{v+1}-1)$ for $v=0,\cdots ,f-1$, and $1\le a_{f}\le \nu (q_{f},s)$. 57.1 Hence there is an explicit bijection from $\sigma$-cut paths onto the disjoint union, over all $Q\subset (r,s]$, of sets of size $\nu '(r,q_{1}-1)\nu '(q_{1},q_{2}-1)\cdots \nu '(q_{f-1},q_{f} -1)\nu (q_{f},s)$, with size $\nu (r,s)$ when $Q=\varnothing$. [step 56.1, construct]

58.1 Fix $s$ and consider indices $1,\ldots,s$. 59.1 Since $A$ is strictly upper triangular, $I+B=I+A+\cdots +A^{s-1}=(I-A)^{-1}$. Let $E$ and $N$ be column vectors with entries $\varepsilon (r,s)$ and $\nu (r,s)$. 60.1 For $r=s$, essential subsets consist only of $\varnothing$ and it is essential exactly when $\lambda _{r}\ne 0$; the normal set is then $\{r\}$. Both sides are 0 or 1 accordingly. ## Remarks

The operators, coefficient recursions and raising pivots are integral before specialization. The basis and count recurrence retain arbitrary dominant polynomial weights, including the nonrestricted shifted weights used in first-level character inversion.

In the empty-set definition of $\Xi$, the highest coefficient is the product forced by the explicit action. The printed sum in the 1995 definition is not used; neither that misprint nor a source count theorem is a proof premise.
