## Statement

For a strongly rational VOA of central charge c, the stable-curve coinvariant sheaves constructed in the preceding lemma carry a canonical action of $(c/2)A_\Lambda(-\Delta)$ on the tangent-rigidified stable-curve stack, where $\Lambda=\det\pi_*\omega$ and Delta is the nodal boundary. This is an action of the specified Lie algebroid, not merely a family of unrelated local differential operators. It induces a projectively flat logarithmic connection and is compatible with the algebraic descent of those sheaves.

Vacuum insertion intertwines this action. Normalization/formal sewing intertwines the induced boundary-specialization actions. For the normalized sector tensor $I_i(q)=\sum_dq^d\operatorname{coev}_{(S_i)_d}$, its normal-direction formula is
$$\nabla_{q\partial_q}I_i-I_i\nabla_{q\partial_q}=-h_i I_i,$$
with incoming connection convention $\nabla=\partial-\operatorname{Res}hT$. Thus the conformal lowest weight is retained in specialization; the normalized integer degree is not silently substituted for $L(0)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The stable coinvariant sheaves, their source-compatible auxiliary-vacuum construction, and the stated conformal convention.

[F1] [[lem-cft-stable-coinvariant-descent-and-formal-sewing]] supplies the actual algebraic finite locally free sheaves, nodal state frames, positive-pole lifting, tangent-coordinate descent and normalized formal sewing. It supplies no Hodge identification.

[F2] [[lem-cft-laurent-determinant-virasoro-central-normalization]] proves the polarized Laurent determinant cocycle, with value $(m^3-m)/6$ on its monomial modes: its central weight is two in the stated Virasoro normalization.

[F3] [[lem-cft-smooth-family-coinvariant-finiteness-and-virasoro-matrices]] gives the actual local Lie derivative and Ward-ideal commutator. [[lem-cft-plumbing-positive-pole-base-change-and-logarithmic-lifts]] supplies logarithmic lifts and regular node coefficients. [[lem-cft-locality-to-jacobi-residue-calculus]] and [[lem-cft-state-field-and-residue-coordinate-covariance]] supply full Virasoro commutators and finite-jet covariance.

[F4] [[thm-serre-duality-curves-vector-bundles]] supplies normalization duality. [[lem-proper-flat-fp-cohomology-perfect-complex]] supplies finite cohomology complexes with arbitrary base change. The finite determinant constructions below are specified by exterior powers and exact-sequence cancellation.

## Proof

1.1 Work on an affine smooth stable-curve chart S with coordinates and boundary equation $\prod_eq_e=0$, and add auxiliary vacuum marks so their divisor D meets every normalization component. For a logarithmic base field xi the local generators $x\partial_x,y\partial_y$ give the exact sequence $0\to\omega^{-1}(kD)\to\mathcal L(kD)\to\pi^*T_S(-\log\Delta)(kD)\to0$. The positive-pole vanishing in F1 kills the kernel H1; the affine-base finite direct-image sequence therefore lifts xi to a global algebraic meromorphic total field regular off D; its node expression is $a_ex_e\partial_{x_e}+b_ey_e\partial_{y_e}$ plus the other base terms, with $a_e+b_e=\xi(q_e)/q_e$. At incoming coordinates write it as $\xi+h_i\partial_{t_i}$. Its action on the tensor-label sheaf is $\xi-\sum_i\operatorname{Res}h_iY_i(\omega,t_i)dt_i$. The commutator calculation in F3 proves Ward preservation away from the nodes. It also holds at a node: conjugate by the explicit node frame, so every derivative is a polynomial in $x\partial_x-y\partial_y$ and positive finite state modes with regular coefficients; finite-jet covariance makes the two branch formulas agree. Thus the same displayed Lie-derivative identity is a permitted nodal current. All coefficients at the incoming marks have finite pole cutoff, so the residue operator is an actual algebraic differential operator. [F1, F3, construct]

1.2 We identify the corresponding geometric extension with the Hodge line, including its factor. Put $W=\bigcup_k\pi_*\mathcal O_C(kD)$ inside $H=\bigoplus_i\mathcal O_S((t_i))$, and $H_+=\bigoplus_i\mathcal O_S\llbracket t_i\rrbracket$. Principal parts give the exact Fredholm sequence $$0\to\pi_*\mathcal O_C\to W\to H/H_+\to R^1\pi_*\mathcal O_C\to0.$$ Indeed at every finite pole bound this is the ordinary principal-part sequence, and its H1 vanishes for sufficiently high k by F1; take the directed union. The two nonzero cohomology bundles are finite locally free of ranks one and g. To check this at the boundary, use normalization $0\to\mathcal O_C\to\nu_*\mathcal O_{\widehat C}\to\bigoplus\mathcal O_{\rm node}\to0$: its finite difference map has rank fixed by the connected dual graph. Together with F4's finite cohomology complex, constant ranks split the differentials locally by nonzero minors and give arbitrary base change. Normalization Serre duality pairs its exact sequence with the residue description of dualizing forms; the branch difference is adjoint to the pair of opposite residues. Finite-dimensional exactness therefore gives $R^1\pi_*\mathcal O_C\cong(\pi_*\omega)^*$. The identifications commute with base change and hence are bundle identifications. Since constants identify $\pi_*\mathcal O_C$ with $\mathcal O_S$, the determinant of this Fredholm sequence is canonically $$\det H^0(C,\mathcal O_C)\otimes\det H^1(C,\mathcal O_C)^{-1}=\det\pi_*\omega=\Lambda.$$ Concretely it is computed at any sufficiently high cutoff by $\det\pi_*\mathcal O(kD)\otimes\det P_k^{-1}$; increasing the cutoff cancels the same finite principal-part space in numerator and denominator. [F1, F4, construct]

2.1 Let E be the Lie algebroid of these meromorphic lifts, enhanced by the normalized Virasoro central extension in their marked Laurent expansions. Locally its central cocycle on vertical coefficients h,k is $\sum_i\operatorname{Res}h_i'''k_i\,dt_i/12$. The mixed base terms are the Leibniz derivatives of their coefficients; expanding the Virasoro commutator proves the Lie algebroid identity. Its representation in step 1.1 sends the center K to c. The function-side operator of the same lift is $\xi+h_i\partial_{t_i}$; thus the minus sign on conformal modes is fixed by the opposite Lie bracket convention, not chosen independently. The quotient of meromorphic lifts by their vertical kernel maps onto $T_S(-\log\Delta)$ by step 1.1. This constructs the anchor surjectivity used here directly from positive-pole lifting. No unproved global uniformization theorem is needed. [F3, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Here is the determinant action used in that identification. For a polarized Laurent module and a Fredholm submodule W, its determinant is the determinant of the finite kernel/cokernel of $W\to H/H_+$. An infinitesimal change of polarization $L=(1+\epsilon A)H_+$ changes this line by the relative finite determinant of L and $H_+$. Choose N exceeding the negative Laurent cutoff of A and its inverse infinitesimal change. Then $t^NH_+$ is contained in both lattices. Both quotients are finite locally free: their bases $1,\ldots,t^{N-1}$ at epsilon zero lift to bases by elimination of the nilpotent off-diagonal terms. This proves the required existence of finite determinant quotients over the dual numbers, not just over fields. Define that determinant by choosing a common finite-codimension submodule L0, taking $\det(H_+/L0)\otimes\det(L/L0)^{-1}$, and canceling the extra finite factors for a smaller common L0. With this orientation the transitivity formula is $\operatorname{Det}(W,H_+)=\operatorname{Det}(W,L)\otimes\det(H_+/L0)\otimes\det(L/L0)^{-1}$. Local finite free presentations give the same definition when the summands are projective. The exact exterior-power isomorphism for $0\to A\to B\to C\to0$, checked on a locally split basis and independent of its triangular change, proves transitivity and associativity of these identifications. An infinitesimal Laurent change $1+\epsilon A$ with finite block $A_{-+}$ changes polarization in precisely this way, and hence acts on this determinant line after choosing its relative determinant lift. Its central bracket is the finite trace in F2. One can check its orientation on a finite window: the determinant of the occupied negative-power subspace has bracket error $\operatorname{Tr}_{H_-}(A_{-+}B_{+-}-B_{-+}A_{+-})$, equal by finite-rank cyclicity to F2's beta. Thus the line in step 1.2, rather than its inverse, has central weight two. For operators with a base derivation add that derivation on coefficients; exterior powers obey the same Leibniz rule. Since the meromorphic total lift preserves functions regular off D, this construction gives E an action on Lambda with its specified base symbol and K acting by two. Everything uses finite determinants at a chosen large cutoff; increasing it cancels identical factors, so no trace of an unrestricted infinite matrix is taken. [F2, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 We specify the canonical splitting on the vertical kernel. A meromorphic relative projective coefficient p has transition $p_t=(s')^2p_s+S_ts$. It exists with poles only at D: its Schwarzian transition cocycle takes values in the relative quadratic line; sufficiently positive D twist has zero H1 by F1, so the affine cocycle is a coboundary. At a node the reference coefficients zero on the x and y branches agree under $y=q/x$, whose Schwarzian is zero. For a different node chart $x'=xu(x,y)$ the coefficient $x^2S_xx'$ is regular, by the scaled-coordinate formula in F1; this extends the same affine torsor over the node with quadratic differences in $\omega^2$. Hence p may be chosen with regular dualizing quadratic coefficients at nodes. For a global vertical field v put $\ell_p(v)=\sum_i\operatorname{Res}p_th_tdt/12$. It is independent of p: changing p adds a global meromorphic relative quadratic differential, whose product with v is a dualizing differential with poles only on D and opposite node residues, so its total residue is zero. The local Bol expression $h'''+2p h'+p'h$ is a quadratic differential; its coordinate transformation follows by differentiating the displayed Schwarzian rule three times. Multiplying by k and summing residues gives $$\sum_i\operatorname{Res}h_i'''k_i\,dt_i=\sum_i\operatorname{Res}p_i(h_ik_i'-k_ih_i')dt_i.$$ Thus $v\mapsto v+\ell_p(v)K$ is a Lie splitting of the kernel extension, with the convention of step 2.1. The corrected global current $(h\omega-(c/12)hp\mathbf1)dt$ gives the same residue identity on coinvariants: their raw conformal residue is $c\ell_p(v)$, so the lifted vertical operator $-L_h+\ell_p(v)K$ acts as zero. This is the isotropy splitting used in the quotient, independent of p. [F1, F3, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 It remains to check that the kernel splitting of step 3.1 is the one annihilating Lambda. The determinant action gives another Lie splitting, by subtracting half of the scalar action of any vertical lift. The difference of two Lie splittings is a character of the vertical vector-field Lie algebra. On a smooth curve this character is zero. To prove this without a perfection theorem, enlarge D to include the zeros and poles of the differential of a nonconstant rational function x. On the resulting affine open U, $D_x=d/dx$ is a regular derivation and $D_xx=1$; every vector field is fD_x. Then $$[D_x,xfD_x]-[xD_x,fD_x]=2fD_x,$$ so its vector-field algebra is perfect. Both constructions are compatible with enlarging D: the exact principal-part comparison identifies the determinant constructions equivariantly (a field regular at an extra mark preserves its positive lattice, so the added finite determinant factors cancel), and the determinant is always the same cohomology determinant of step 1.2, and the kernel current splitting is unchanged by adding the expansions at the extra points, since their total global residue is zero. Thus their difference on the original kernel is zero as well. This comparison may be made separately on every smooth closed fiber; no global choice of x in a family is required. Both splittings have regular algebraic coefficients on S, including its logarithmic boundary, by steps 3.1 and 2.2. Equality on the dense smooth locus consequently implies equality over S, since its local rings are reduced and the complement is a divisor. [step 3.1, step 1.2, step 2.2, algebra]

5.1 Quotient E by this common vertical splitting. It is an extension A of $T_S(-\log\Delta)$ by $\mathcal O_S$. Its determinant action is a map $A\to A_\Lambda(-\Delta)$, inducing the identity on symbols and multiplication by two on the central copy. It is an isomorphism onto the half-scaled Atiyah extension: in local line frames subtracting the scalar half identifies both kernels and symbols, and the two exact sequences give an inverse; their transition one-forms are half the $d\log$ transitions of Lambda. Thus $A\cong\tfrac12A_\Lambda(-\Delta)$. The coinvariant action has center c by step 2.1, so its corresponding unit-central extension is $cA=(c/2)A_\Lambda(-\Delta)$. The construction was intrinsic under changes of marked coordinates and all its maps commute with their finite-jet actions. It therefore descends with F1's coinvariant sheaf and agrees on atlas overlaps. This proves the stated global Atiyah action. [F1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 A Lie-algebroid module satisfies the commutator identity, so the induced projective connection is flat. More explicitly trivialize Lambda locally; its Atiyah algebra then has the bracket-preserving splitting given by differentiation in that frame. The corresponding local connection on coinvariants is flat. Replacing the line frame changes it by the scalar $(c/2)d\log$ of the transition. Its projectivization is therefore independent of the frame and flat. Its generators are ordinary coordinate derivatives and $q_e\partial_{q_e}$, whose matrices are regular by the algebraic construction; hence it has only logarithmic boundary poles. In genus zero Lambda is canonically the determinant of the zero-dimensional Hodge space, so this splitting is canonical, giving the genus-zero trivialization. [step 5.1, algebra]

6.2 Vacuum propagation intertwines the connection exactly. Choose a meromorphic lift with poles only at the old marks, also permitted after adding a new mark Q. Its coefficient at Q is regular, so its residue on the vacuum is a sum of $L(m)\mathbf1$ with $m\ge-1$, all zero. Base differentiation of the vacuum is zero. Thus insertion commutes with the raw differential operators. The same corrected projective coefficient has no pole at Q, so it adds no Schwarzian residue. The Atiyah actions agree, and inverses of the propagation isomorphisms agree as well. This proves compatibility with the auxiliary-vacuum colimit, including n=0. [F1, F3, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 For formal sewing, use the genuine node lift $a_+z_+\partial_{z_+}+a_-z_-\partial_{z_-}$ with $a_++a_-=1$. On the degree-d coevaluation in the simple sector, its conformal action in the two label slots is $-(a_++a_-)(d+h_i)=-(d+h_i)$. Differentiating its coefficient $q^d$ contributes d. Consequently their sum is $-h_i$, proving the displayed normal-direction formula. Tangential derivatives and external modes commute with the internal identity tensors by their natural evaluation pairing; their scalar terms are those of the same Hodge determinant. Normalization gives the exact Hodge sequence of step 1.2 with the extra residue-cycle spaces of the dual graph; its determinant isomorphisms are the actual exterior-power isomorphisms, so they respect composition, including the signs from permuting determinant factors. This identifies the twists in the two boundary-specialization actions. The normal residue shift is precisely the scalar $-h_i$ just computed, not zero; removing $q^{h_i}$ from a horizontal conformal sewing introduces that shift. Hence normalized formal sewing intertwines the specialization action with these actual residues. For several nodes the degree operators occupy different slots and commute, so the same calculation holds in each normal direction and in every contraction order. With F1's formal isomorphism this proves all sewing and vacuum compatibilities in the Statement. [F1, F3, step 1.2, step 5.1, step 6.2, algebra] ∎
