## Statement

Assume full AC. Let $N$ be the fan lattice, $A_\Sigma=\{0\}\cup\{v_\rho:\rho\in\Sigma(1)\}$, $T^\vee=\operatorname{Hom}(N,\mathbb C^*)$, and $\Delta_\Sigma=\operatorname{conv}(A_\Sigma)$, with the toric conventions fixed by the toric-fan definition. Write $W_\Sigma(z)=\sum_{a\in A_\Sigma}c_a z^a$, where $c_a\in\mathbb C^*$. For each nonempty face $F\subseteq\Delta_\Sigma$, define its face polynomial by $W_F(z)=\sum_{a\in A_\Sigma\cap F}c_a z^a$. Call the coefficients **face-nondegenerate** if, for one (and hence every) lattice basis of $N$ and its torus coordinates $z_1,\ldots,z_n$, no $z\in T^\vee$ satisfies both $W_F(z)=0$ and $z_j\partial_{z_j}W_F(z)=0$ for every $j$, for any such face $F$. Require the source coefficients to be face-nondegenerate and connected to the adapted source representative used in the construction by a smooth path in this same coefficient locus. Choose the selected smoothed moment-polytope component and admissible section data at that representative, and transport them along this path; the relative category in the conclusion is the resulting transported pre-category. For its smooth zero fibre and admissible relative Fukaya pre-category, as specified by the relative-Fukaya interface, Abouzaid’s Theorem 1.2 identifies the full sub-pre-category of the source’s admissible Lagrangian sections with their canonical graph gradings as quasi-equivalent, as an $A_\infty$ pre-category, to the dg category of line bundles on $X_\Sigma$. The canonical graph grading at the adapted representative is the lift of squared phase obtained from the path of tangent graphs $\operatorname{graph}(t\,Dp(q))$, $0\le t\le1$, starting with lift zero on the horizontal plane; here $p$ is an angular lift of the section on the contractible logarithmic region and $Dp$ is symmetric by the Lagrangian condition. Equivalently this lift is $\pi^{-1}\operatorname{tr}(\arctan Dp(q))$ in the fixed complex coordinates $q+i\theta$. It is independent of an integral constant added to $p$. Transport these gradings along the stipulated coefficient path. If every integral grading shift is retained, the full section pre-category is instead quasi-equivalent to the full dg subcategory of the coherent derived enhancement whose objects are all shifts $\mathcal L[k]$ of line bundles, $k\in\mathbb Z$; its graded Hom cohomology is $\operatorname{Ext}^{r+l-k}(\mathcal L,\mathcal M)$ from $\mathcal L[k]$ to $\mathcal M[l]$. Thus all grading choices remain covered and their integer shifts are preserved. This is a line-bundle subcategory result; it does not assert that the whole Fukaya category is equivalent to $D^b\operatorname{Coh}(X_\Sigma)$.

For definiteness its facet $F_{\rho }$ has outward primitive normal $v_{\rho }$ and inequality $<x,v_{\rho }>\le \lambda _{\rho }$; changing all normal conventions changes the displayed pole function accordingly, but not the construction. Put $k=C$ (the construction also works over any field). For a torus-invariant Cartier divisor $D=\sum $ $a_{\rho }$ $D_{\rho }$ write $L_{D}=O(D)$. Its weight-m rational section is $\chi ^{m}$, with order In formulas: `ord_(D_rho)(chi^m as a section of L_D)=<m,v_rho>+a_rho.`. [F1, F5, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Thus the positive/pole function is $h_{D,m}(F_{\rho })=-a_{\rho }-<m,v_{\rho }>$. Set In formulas: `B(D,m)=union of facets F_rho on which h_(D,m)>0.`. [F1, F5, given, construct, algebra, step 1.1]

3.1 It is a closed subcomplex of the face decomposition, and B(D+E,m+n) is contained in B(D,m) union B(E,n). The strict inequality is essential: order zero is permitted, so it is not part of the pole locus. [F1, F5, given, construct, algebra, step 2.1]

4.1 Weight sections on every intersection. For a cone sigma the invariant affine open is $U_{\sigma }=Spec$ $k[\sigma ^{\vee} cap M]$. Since Sigma is smooth, choose a basis extending its primitive ray generators. Then its ring is a polynomial ring in dim sigma coordinates times Laurent polynomials in the remaining coordinates. On $U_{\sigma }$ the divisor D has a Cartier character $m_{\sigma }$ with $<m_{\sigma },v_{\rho }\ge -a_{\rho }$ for $\rho $ in sigma(1). Consequently In formulas: `Gamma(U_sigma,L_D)=chi^(m_sigma) k[sigma^vee cap M],`. [F1, F5, given, construct, algebra, step 3.1]

5.1 and its weight-m subspace is k $\chi ^{m}$ if $<m,v_{\rho }>+a_{\rho }\ge 0$ for EVERY $\rho $ in sigma(1), and zero otherwise. This formula follows by writing a monomial in those polynomial and invertible coordinates; linear independence of Laurent monomials shows that no cancellation can make a prohibited weight regular. A face inclusion of cones localizes a subset of those coordinates. The restriction on an allowed weight is the identity $k\to k$, and a zero weight module has its unique zero restriction. Products of sections send $\chi ^{m}$ tensor $\chi ^{n}$ to $\chi ^{m+n}$, with coefficient 1. [F1, F5, given, construct, algebra, step 4.1]

6.1 Intersections are $U_{\sigma }$ intersection $U_{\tau }=U_{\sigma  cap \tau }$, also for finitely many cones. In particular the weight condition on an INTERSECTION is the condition for its COMMON CONE. It is not the condition that the weight extend to each original affine open separately. This follows immediately from localization, and fixes every restriction map needed by the ordered Cech differential. [F1, F5, given, construct, algebra, step 5.1]

7.1 Every algebraic line bundle is represented this way, up to a character. To verify this without assuming an equivariant linearization theorem, restrict it to the dense torus. The Laurent polynomial ring is a factorial domain (Gauss's primitive polynomial argument followed by localization); on a regular factorial affine scheme every Cartier divisor is principal, so this restriction is trivial. Choose that trivialization as a rational section. Its divisor on X is supported on the complement of the torus, hence is an integer combination of the finitely many $D_{\rho }$. Conversely the displayed local characters make each such divisor Cartier because the rays in every smooth cone extend to a basis. Two boundary divisors differing by a character have isomorphic line bundles; conversely a rational function with divisor supported only on the boundary is an invertible function on the torus, hence c $\chi ^{m}$ by comparing the units of the Laurent polynomial ring. This proves precisely the required object classification. [F1, F5, given, construct, algebra, step 6.1]

8.1 The actual Cech dg category. Index the invariant affine cover by all nonempty faces tau of Q, including Q itself: $U_{\tau }$ means the affine open associated to the normal cone of tau. All these opens are permitted; the vertex opens already cover X. An intersection $U_{\tau 0}$ intersection ... intersection $U_{taup}$ is $U_{\tau 0 join ... join taup}$, where join is the smallest face containing the listed faces. It is connected, affine, and its weight-m sections are either the one copy of k above or zero. Define In formulas: `C^p_X(D,E)=direct sum_(tau0<...<taup); Gamma(U_tau0 intersection ... intersection U_taup,L_(E-D)).`. [F1, F5, given, construct, algebra, step 7.1]

9.1 Use alternating deletion followed by the actual localization maps for the differential. Composition is the ordered Alexander--Whitney cup product followed by monomial multiplication; an input of degree p and one of degree q evaluates on the front p+1 and back q+1 indices, sharing the middle index. Restrictions commute with multiplication. Expanding alternating deletion gives $d(ab)=(da)b+(-1)^{p}$ a(db); splitting an ordered index string at two successive positions gives associativity. Thus this is an actual dg category, not only a cohomology multiplication. It splits as a direct sum over M in each morphism complex, and products add weights. [F1, F5, given, construct, algebra, step 8.1]

10.1 This cover gives the usual line-bundle dg enhancement: finite intersections are affine and the line bundles are quasi-coherent there. The elementary affine acyclicity check used here is the localization Cech contraction. For a finite principal cover $D(f_{i})$ of an affine, clear denominators in a given cocycle and choose N with $1=\sum $ $b_{i}$ $f_{i}^{N}$; insertion of sum $b_{i}$ $f_{i}^{N}$ in the first index contracts the alternating localization complex. Such principal covers form a basis and refine finite affine intersections. The augmented local Cech resolution is stalkwise split by inserting any index containing the stalk. Its double complex therefore identifies the displayed Cech complex with derived global sections of $\operatorname{Hom}(L_{D},L_{E})$; the preceding contraction gives acyclicity on its intersections. The same restriction double complex proves refinement invariance. These are finite-diagonal cochain eliminations, so no assertion that a bare cohomology isomorphism preserves dg products is being used. The products are already the same restriction-and-multiplication products in the double complex. [F1, F5, given, construct, algebra, step 9.1]

11.1 Replace algebraic weights by a sheaf on Q. For a union B of facets let $S_{B}$ be the extension by zero of the constant sheaf k from Q minus B, equivalently the relative locally constant sheaf. On a connected open star $O_{\tau }$ of a face tau, it has sections k if tau is not contained in B and zero if tau is contained in B. Indeed $O_{\tau }$ consists of relative interiors of faces containing tau. It and its intersection with B are contractible when the latter is nonempty: choose a point in the relative interior of tau and contract each incident face linearly to it. Only bad facets containing tau occur; these are preserved by the contraction. In the empty case only the first contraction is needed. A locally constant function on the connected complement which must vanish near a nonempty incident boundary facet is zero. The same description works for intersections of stars, since their intersection is the star of the join face. [F1, F5, given, construct, algebra, step 10.1]

12.1 The relative singular cochains of $(O_{\tau },O_{\tau } \bigcap  B)$ therefore have cohomology k in degree zero if the second space is empty and zero otherwise. One can prove this directly with the integral prism homotopy of the stated contractions and then tensor with k. Their restriction diagrams identify this degree-zero sheaf with $S_{B}$. Consequently the open-star cover is acyclic for $S_{B}$. Its ordered Cech complex is EXACTLY the weight-m Cech complex of $L_{D}$: the common join face is prohibited precisely when some bad facet contains that face, which says precisely that the relevant common cone contains a ray with negative order. The nonzero modules, restrictions and products all agree. [F1, F5, given, construct, algebra, step 11.1]

13.1 The canonical sheaf multiplication is In formulas: `S_(B(D,m)) tensor S_(B(E,n)) -> S_(B(D+E,m+n)).`. [F1, F5, given, construct, algebra, step 12.1]

14.1 It sends 1 tensor 1 to 1 wherever both factors are present, and is zero where either is absent. It is well defined because B(D+E,m+n) is contained in the union of the other two pole loci. 17.1 The differential is the relative simplicial differential and the product is precisely the front/back Alexander--Whitney product.
