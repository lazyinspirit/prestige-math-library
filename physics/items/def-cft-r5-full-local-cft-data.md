## Definition

Fix a connected **worldsheet** $\Sigma$: a Hausdorff, second-countable,
oriented smooth real two-manifold without boundary, equipped with an atlas of
charts to open subsets of $\mathbb C$ whose transition maps are biholomorphic
and preserve the orientation. This is the Riemann-surface structure used here;
it specifies a conformal structure and no metric length scale. Write each local
coordinate as $z=x+iy$ with conjugate $\bar z$; coordinates are dimensionless.

A **full local two-dimensional CFT data package in the state-space/OPE
presentation** is the following mathematical data satisfying the conditions
below. It is one chosen axiom package, not a theorem asserting that such data
exist for a given model.

1. **State space and spectrum.** Choose a complete Hausdorff locally convex
   complex vector space $\mathcal H$: addition and scalar multiplication are
   continuous, its topology is generated by specified continuous seminorms,
   and it is complete for the resulting uniform structure. Choose a dense
   subspace $\mathcal D\subseteq\mathcal H$ invariant under both Virasoro
   actions and all smeared fields below, so the declared operator products are
   defined on one common domain. Present $\mathcal H$ either as a completed
   direct sum
   $\widehat{\bigoplus}_{\alpha\in I}\mathcal H_\alpha$ of declared joint
   left/right sectors (each $\mathcal H_\alpha$ is invariant under both
   actions), or as a specified direct integral
   $\int_X^\oplus\mathcal H_x\,d\mu(x)$. In the direct-sum case, give the
   index set, sector spaces, topology and completion. In the direct-integral
   case, give the measurable space $X$, the $\sigma$-finite measure $\mu$ and its normalization,
   the measurable field of fibers, the fiber category, and the direct-integral
   construction; if it is not a Hilbert direct integral, define that
   construction explicitly. The common dense domain, topology and any
   multiplicity spaces are part of the data.

2. **Left and right Virasoro actions.** Give representations of two copies of
   the Virasoro algebra on $\mathcal D$, with modes $L_n$ and $\bar L_n$ and
   scalar central charges $c_L,c_R\in\mathbb C$. Thus, for $m,n\in\mathbb Z$,
   $$[L_m,L_n]=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\frac{c_L}{12}(m^3-m)\delta_{m+n,0}I,\qquad [\bar L_m,\bar L_n]=(m-n)\bar L_{m+n}+\frac{c_R}{12}(m^3-m)\delta_{m+n,0}I,$$
   and $[L_m,\bar L_n]=0$ on $\mathcal D$. **Positive energy** means the
   declared spectral decomposition of each of $L_0$ and $\bar L_0$ has real
   weights bounded below. For a discrete spectrum, specify each joint
   generalized-weight space (vectors killed by some power of both
   $L_0-hI$ and $\bar L_0-\bar hI$) and every nilpotent Jordan part; for a
   continuous spectrum, specify the spectral resolution and the measure support.
   Bounded below does not mean that all weights are nonnegative.

3. **Vacuum and correlator functional.** Choose a nonzero vacuum
   $\Omega\in\mathcal D$ and a continuous linear functional
   $\epsilon\in\mathcal H'$ with $\epsilon(\Omega)=1$, where $\mathcal H'$ is
   the continuous linear dual. Require $L_n\Omega=\bar L_n\Omega=0$ for every
   integer $n\ge -1$, and require the common global-vacuum subspace
   $\{v\in\mathcal D:L_nv=\bar L_nv=0\text{ for all }n\ge-1\}$ to equal
   $\mathbb C\Omega$. The vacuum correlation of insertions is the matrix
   coefficient obtained by applying $\epsilon$ to their radially ordered field
   product acting on $\Omega$.

4. **Local fields and state-field correspondence.** Choose a complex vector
   space $\mathcal F$ of field labels and a bijective linear state-field map
   $\sigma:\mathcal F\to\mathcal D$ with an identity field $\mathbf1$ satisfying
   $\sigma(\mathbf1)=\Omega$. For each $a\in\mathcal F$ and coordinate chart
   $U\subseteq\Sigma$, a field $Y(a;z,\bar z)$ is a weak operator-valued
   distribution: for every test function $f\in C_c^\infty(U)$ it defines a
   linear map $Y(a;f):\mathcal D\to\mathcal D$, and for every $u\in\mathcal D$
   and $\lambda\in\mathcal H'$, the map
   $f\mapsto\lambda(Y(a;f)u)$ is a continuous distribution on
   $C_c^\infty(U)$, the complex smooth functions of compact support, with the
   topology generated on each compact support by uniform seminorms of all
   derivatives. Smearing uses test densities: in a coordinate $z$ write a
   density as $f(z)d^2z$, where $d^2z=dx\,dy$; the identity field smeared
   against it is $(\int f\,d^2z)I$.

   A centered insertion chart is a chart $i=(U_i,z_i,p)$ with $p\in U_i$
   and $z_i(p)=0$. Supply such charts at every insertion, allowing restriction
   to smaller neighborhoods of the center. Supply a bijective linear map
   $\sigma_i:\mathcal F\to\mathcal D$ and chart-vacuum data
   $\Omega_i\in\mathcal D$, $\epsilon_i\in\mathcal H'$ with
   $\sigma_i(\mathbf1)=\Omega_i\ne0$ and $\epsilon_i(\Omega_i)=1$.
   One reference centered presentation has
   $(\sigma_i,\Omega_i,\epsilon_i)=(\sigma,\Omega,\epsilon)$.
   Other presentations also carry their explicitly transported Virasoro actions
   and satisfy clause 3's vacuum condition for those actions. Correlators in a
   presentation use its vacuum and functional. Only in a centered chart
   require $Y_i(a;z,\bar z)\Omega_i$ to have a continuous vector representative
   near $0$, with radial limit $\sigma_i(a)$ in $\mathcal H$.
   There is no limit-at-zero requirement for a chart whose image misses zero.

   **Finite coordinate transport.** Supply the following finite data in
   addition to the infinitesimal identities below. Give $\mathcal F$ a
   specified Hausdorff locally convex topology. For every coordinate change
   $g_{ji}=z_j\circ z_i^{-1}$ on an overlap, supply a smooth family of
   continuous linear automorphisms $R_{ji}(z)$ of $\mathcal F$, fixing
   $\mathbf1$, and a continuous linear automorphism $W_{ji}$ of
   $\mathcal H$, preserving $\mathcal D$, with
   $W_{ji}\Omega_i=\Omega_j$ and $\epsilon_j W_{ji}=\epsilon_i$.
   Uncentered charts have the same chart-vacuum presentation data, but no
   creation limit at zero. Restricting a chart preserves its presentation.
   The reference vacuum functional need not be invariant under all finite
   coordinate changes: the bra as well as the fields is transported.
   Smoothness here means that every partial derivative in the two real
   coordinate variables of $R_{ji}(z)a$ exists to every finite order in
   $\mathcal F$, and that each resulting map of $(z,a)$ is jointly
   continuous; require the same of the inverse family. On triple overlaps require the actual finite cocycles
   $$R_{ki}(z)=R_{kj}(g_{ji}(z))R_{ji}(z),\qquad W_{ki}=W_{kj}W_{ji}.$$
   Identity transitions have $R=I,W=I$, inverse transitions have the inverse
   maps, and all these assignments commute with restrictions. These are
   normalized passive changes of presentation; no representation-integration
   theorem for an arbitrary locally convex space is asserted. Scalar
   projective lifts of $W$ may also be recorded, but their scalar cocycle must
   cancel on passage to these vacuum-normalized maps.

   Specify spaces $E_i(V)$ of compactly supported smooth
   $\mathcal F$-valued test densities on each coordinate open set $V$, with
   locally convex topologies, containing the elementary densities
   $a f(z)d^2z$. Specify linear extensions
   $\mathcal Y_i:E_i(V)\to\operatorname{End}(\mathcal D)$ of
   $a f d^2z\mapsto Y_i(a;f)$, continuous in every weak matrix coefficient.
   Require the finite linear span of elementary densities to be dense in
   each $E_i(V)$, so weak continuity determines each extension uniquely.
   In a centered neighborhood write $V_i(a,z)=Y_i(a;z,\bar z)\Omega_i$ for
   its jointly continuous vacuum representative. For every test density
   $s(z)d^2z$ supported there and every $\lambda\in\mathcal H'$, require
   $$\lambda(\mathcal Y_i(s)\Omega_i)=\int\lambda(V_i(s(z),z))\,d^2z.$$
   This scalar integral identity specifies compatibility with variable
   labels; it is not inferred from pointwise smoothness of $R$ alone.
   Their restriction rule is extension of compactly supported tests by zero.
   Require the following pushforwards to be well-defined continuous maps
   between the specified test spaces, with their inverses, commuting with
   extension by zero:
   $$(P_{ji}s)(w)=|g_{ji}'(z)|^{-2}R_{ji}(z)s(z),\qquad z=g_{ji}^{-1}(w).$$
   Here $s(z)$ denotes the coefficient of $d^2z$; the output is the
   coefficient of $d^2w$. Require the finite overlap identity
   $$W_{ji}\mathcal Y_i(s)W_{ji}^{-1}=\mathcal Y_j(P_{ji}s).$$
   This is a transformation of field-valued test densities, not scalar gluing
   of every field component. For example a primary component can have
   $R_{ji}(z)a=(g_{ji}'(z))^h(\overline{g_{ji}'(z)})^{\bar h}a$ with
   specified branches; a stress component may mix with the identity through
   a Schwarzian term. Spin branches must obey clause 6. All mixing and
   branches, including those of descendants, are actual supplied data.
   Along every admissible differentiable one-parameter coordinate flow for
   which a Virasoro generator is specified, require the derivative of this
   finite transport in weak matrix coefficients to equal the corresponding
   infinitesimal covariance identity below, with the declared coordinate and
   label variation. The existence and differentiability of these finite maps
   are required data, not inferred from a Lie algebra action.

   For two centered charts at the same insertion, require compatibility
   $$W_{ji}\sigma_i(a)=\sigma_j(R_{ji}(0)a).$$
   Require the vacuum representatives to be jointly continuous in the field
   label and coordinate near the center, so this identity is also the limit
   of the overlap identity on the vacuum. Supply the finite transport of OPE
   coefficient families and their convergence domains, so transporting either
   side of each OPE by the stated maps gives the same weak distribution;
   require the same for iterated OPEs and analytic continuation. For a
   polynomial $F$ of fields in one presentation put
   $F_j=W_{ji}F_iW_{ji}^{-1}$, using $P_{ji}$ on every smearing. Then
   $\epsilon_j(F_j\Omega_j)=\epsilon_i(F_i\Omega_i)$.
   Thus local fields and correlators descend with their actual field-label
   transition maps and transported vacuum data.
   These are conditions on a package, not deductions from formal mode
   commutators.

   In the reference centered presentation, state-field covariance is part of
   the package. With $L_{j-1}a$ denoting
   $\sigma^{-1}(L_{j-1}\sigma(a))$ (and similarly for $\bar L$), require, in
   matrix coefficients on the common domain,
   $$[L_n,Y(a;z,\bar z)]=\sum_{j\ge0}\binom{n+1}{j}z^{n+1-j}Y(L_{j-1}a;z,\bar z),\qquad [\bar L_n,Y(a;z,\bar z)]=\sum_{j\ge0}\binom{n+1}{j}\bar z^{n+1-j}Y(\bar L_{j-1}a;z,\bar z).$$
   Here $\binom{n+1}{j}$ is the generalized integer binomial coefficient.
   Each sum must be locally finite on a field label or converge in the declared
   topology. In other presentations use its finite transport above; if
   local mode operators are written there, they are transported operators
   on the transported common domain. Transport includes the coordinate and
   field-label changes; modes in a new coordinate are its Laurent coefficients,
   not an assertion that identically indexed modes are merely conjugated.
   Require every local presentation to satisfy the same Virasoro and vacuum
   conditions, with its declared mode coefficients and central charges.

5. **Radial OPE data and convergence.** A **Virasoro descendant** is a state
   obtained by a finite word in the modes $L_n,\bar L_n$ from a state-field
   label. For every ordered pair of fields
   $a,b\in\mathcal F$, specify a nonempty radial domain, for example
   $0<|z-w|<R_{a,b}$ with $R_{a,b}>0$ for
   $Y(a;z,\bar z)Y(b;w,\bar w)$. On that domain specify
   an expansion into a declared family of local fields and descendants; no
   linear independence is assumed, and any nonuniqueness of coefficients is
   part of the data,
   $$Y(a;z,\bar z)Y(b;w,\bar w)=\sum_{\alpha\in I_{a,b}}C_{a,b}^{\alpha}(z-w,\bar z-\bar w)Y(\phi_\alpha;w,\bar w),$$
   or the corresponding explicitly normalized integral in a direct-integral
   model. On each declared radial domain, the weak matrix coefficients of the
   field products and coefficient functions must have holomorphic/antiholomorphic
   representatives in the complexified insertion coordinates on the specified
   branch or cover, whose restrictions give the Euclidean matrix coefficients.
   The coefficient functions, index set or
   integration measure, summation order, branches, and topology are data. The
   equality means that for every
   $u\in\mathcal D$ and $\lambda\in\mathcal H'$, the resulting matrix
   coefficients converge absolutely and locally uniformly on compact subsets of
   the declared domain, in the specified topology; they are not merely formal
   or asymptotic expansions. If generalized weights produce logarithmic terms,
   give the log powers and branch choices. For several insertions, state the
   nested radial domains on which each iterated expansion converges.

6. **Bosonic or spin locality.** Mark the package as bosonic or spin. For
   bosonic data, local fields have integral spin $h-\bar h\in\mathbb Z$ on each
   joint weight component, correlators are single-valued functions on the
   configuration space $\{(z_1,\ldots,z_n):z_i\ne z_j\}$, and fields smeared
   with test functions of disjoint support commute. These are coordinate
   components: on changing charts they transform by the supplied field-label
   maps and transported vacuum data. Single-valuedness excludes additional
   monodromy in those components; it does not assert that nonzero-weight
   fields or stress fields are scalar functions under coordinate change.
   For spin data, specify a
   spin structure (a lift of the oriented orthonormal frame bundle, for a
   declared metric representative, to its double cover $\operatorname{Spin}(2)$)
   and a $\mathbb Z_2$ grading $p$ on fields. On each joint weight component
   require $e^{2\pi i(h-\bar h)}=(-1)^p$; disjointly supported smeared fields
   supercommute with this parity. Spin correlators are sections of the declared
   spin bundles with their spin-structure holonomy, not unqualified scalar
   functions. State the parity projection. If torus amplitudes are supplied,
   give their spin-structure vector and modular covariance separately.

7. **Crossed-channel equality.** For every finite set of insertions, the
   vacuum correlator computed from any two iterated OPE trees must agree on
   their common convergence domains and after the declared analytic
   continuation, with the field order and parity signs fixed above. In
   particular, for four fields the expansions in the $(12)(34)$,
   $(13)(24)$, and $(14)(23)$ pairings give the same four-point correlator
   wherever both channel continuations are defined. The continuation paths,
   branches, and equality of matrix coefficients are stated as part of the
   data.

8. **Classification flags.** Record separately whether the data are unitary,
   reflection-positive, rational, and discrete-spectrum or continuous-spectrum;
   do not infer one flag from another. A unitary flag supplies a positive
   definite Hilbert inner product on $\mathcal H$, its adjoint convention, the
   identities $L_n^*=L_{-n}$ and $\bar L_n^*=\bar L_{-n}$ on the declared
   domains, and field adjoints. A reflection-positive flag separately supplies
   a reflection $\theta$ and a test-field domain of field polynomials $F$
   supported on one side of a chosen half-surface. Here $\Theta$ is the
   antilinear order-reversing map induced by $\theta$ and the declared
   field adjoints, defined on the algebra generated by the two reflected
   polynomial domains, with $\Theta^2=I$ and $\Theta(1)=1$; it exchanges
   the two domains. Require the sesquilinear form
   $B(F,G)=\epsilon(\Theta(F)G\Omega)$ to be Hermitian and positive
   semidefinite: $B(F,G)=\overline{B(G,F)}$ and
   $Q(F)=B(F,F)\in[0,\infty)$ for all polynomials in this domain.
   The polynomial domain is a complex vector space containing the identity,
   each polynomial acts on $\mathcal D$, and reflection sends it to
   polynomials on the opposite side, so every displayed product on $\Omega$
   is defined. If rationality is claimed, name the chiral algebra and
   module category; here “rational” means that this declared category is
   semisimple with finitely many irreducible isomorphism classes. A discrete
   spectrum uses the direct-sum presentation; a continuous spectrum uses a
   specified direct integral. A nonrational discrete spectrum is allowed.

This package specifies genus-zero local fields and their vacuum correlators.
It does not include higher-genus sewing or factorization. A torus amplitude is
not assumed: if a trace over $\mathcal H$ is used, specify the trace-class or
regularized convergence domain. Modular invariance is a separate property and
does not follow from this definition.

## Remarks

The convergence, locality and crossing conditions above are assumptions in this
chosen data package. They are not consequences of a formal vertex algebra,
Virasoro symmetry, or a modular-invariant character sum. This definition makes
no equivalence claim with Osterwalder–Schrader, conformal-net, or Segal
formulations and does not identify a formal path integral with a measure. It is
a mathematical interface for model data, not a physical action or a claim that
any specified model is realized in nature.
