## Definition

Fix real parameters $m>0$ and $q_e,q_m$ with $q_e^2+q_m^2=m^2$, and use the selected ingoing dyonic Reissner–Nordström metric on $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{in}}=\mathbb R_v\times(0,\infty)_r\times S^2$ with signature $(-,+,+,+)$:

$$g=-D(r)\,dv^2+2\,dv\,dr+r^2\gamma_{S^2},\qquad D(r)=\left(1-\frac mr\right)^2.$$

Here $\gamma_{S^2}$ is the metric induced on the unit sphere $S^2\subset\mathbb R^3$ by the Euclidean metric, and $\omega_{S^2}$ is its outward area form. The coordinates $v,r$ and parameters $m,q_e,q_m$ have units of metres; angular coordinates are dimensionless. The displayed Maxwell two-form uses the supplied geometric normalization, with no additional SI normalization: its $vr$ coordinate component has units $\mathrm m^{-1}$ and its angular components have units $\mathrm m$. In the supplied ingoing RN background with mass parameter $M=m$, the fixed Maxwell two-form is

$$\mathcal F_{\mathrm{in}}=-q_e r^{-2}\,dv\wedge dr+q_m\omega_{S^2}.$$

Use the unique torsion-free, $g$-compatible connection; in every smooth chart its coefficients are $\Gamma^a{}_{bc}=\tfrac12g^{ad}(\partial_bg_{dc}+\partial_cg_{bd}-\partial_dg_{bc})$ and are smooth. For the horizon-data result, set $M=m$, $Q^2=q_e^2+q_m^2=M^2$ and $f(r):=1-2M/r+Q^2/r^2=D(r)$. It states that $\xi=\partial_v$ obeys $\mathcal L_\xi g=0$ and is normalized by $g(\xi,\xi)\to-1$ as $r\to\infty$; at $r=M$, $g^{-1}(dr,dr)=f(M)=0$, $\xi^\flat=dr$, and $\xi$ is tangent to the level. Thus $r=m$ is the local Killing horizon generated by $\xi$, with signed surface gravity fixed by $d(g(\xi,\xi))=-2\kappa_H\xi^\flat$ and equal to $\kappa_H=f'(M)/2=0$ (units $\mathrm m^{-1}$). No maximal-extension or global event-horizon assertion is included. The metric and displayed Maxwell field are fixed. A neutral test scalar has no electromagnetic gauge coupling or scalar coupling to that field, and does not source either background.

A reduced spherical scalar profile is a real function $\psi:\mathbb R_v\times(0,\infty)_r\to\mathbb R$ whose coordinate partial derivatives through total order three are continuous. Its full scalar on $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{in}}$ is the spherical lift $\widetilde\psi(v,r,\omega):=\psi(v,r)$; in radial formulas we use $\psi(v,r)$ for the reduced profile, and the notation $\Box_g\psi=0$ means $\Box_g\widetilde\psi=0$. For a $C^3$ scalar lift, define the extension of the smooth-scalar wave operator by the trace of its $C^1$ scalar Hessian,

$$\Box_g\widetilde\psi:=g^{ab}\bigl(\partial_a\partial_b\widetilde\psi-\Gamma^c{}_{ab}\partial_c\widetilde\psi\bigr).$$

The second-order chain rule and the connection-coefficient transformation law make these components a covariant $2$-tensor, so the trace is chart independent and $C^1$. This agrees with the supplied smooth-scalar definition when the lift is smooth. In every smooth chart, with $w=\sqrt{|\det(g_{ab})|}$, metric compatibility and the density formula give

$$\Box_g\widetilde\psi=\frac1w\partial_a\bigl(wg^{ab}\partial_b\widetilde\psi\bigr).$$

For the spherical lift in an angular chart, $w=r^2\sqrt{\det(\gamma_{AB})}$ and this becomes

$$\Box_g\widetilde\psi=\frac1{r^2}\left[\partial_v(r^2\partial_r\psi)+\partial_r\!\left(r^2(\partial_v\psi+D\partial_r\psi)\right)\right].$$

The density identity used here is stated for smooth vector fields, but its coordinate proof uses only first derivatives of their components and the product rule; it therefore holds for $C^1$ vector fields as well. Here $g^{ab}\partial_b\widetilde\psi$ is $C^2$, so the formula is defined and $\Box_g\widetilde\psi$ is $C^1$. In particular the wave equation can be differentiated once in $r$ for a $C^3$ profile. Set $\phi(v,r)=r\psi(v,r)$ and take every $\partial_r$ derivative in this regular ingoing chart. If $[\psi]$ denotes the arbitrary scalar-field unit, then $[\phi]=[\psi]\,\mathrm m$.

## Remarks

This is a neutral test-field definition on a fixed exact background. Its regularity is imposed in the ingoing chart that crosses $r=m$; a static-coordinate radial derivative is not substituted for $\partial_r$ without transforming the vector field. The canonical smooth tangent-bundle structure of the geometric suppliers assumes only $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ from [[def-countable-choice]]; no stronger choice principle is used.
