## Statement

Let $i\in U$ have $\pi_i=0$ in a finite-population design as in
[[def-finite-population-and-sampling-design]]. Regard the total as the
estimand $y\mapsto T(y)$ in the induced experiment. Then this estimand is not
identifiable. Moreover, no estimator that is a function only of the observed
record can be design-unbiased for every $y\in\mathbb R^U$. The estimator
target is $\mathbb R$ with its Borel sigma-algebra.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite nonempty $U$, a design support $\mathcal S_d$ with
$p_d(s)>0$ for every $s\in\mathcal S_d$, a fixed $i\in U$ with $\pi_i=0$,
and the induced observed-record experiment.

[F1] [[def-finite-population-and-sampling-design]]: The design support has
strictly positive weights, $\pi_i=\sum_{s:i\in s}p_d(s)$, the record is
$O_y(s)=(s,y|_s)$, the common observed-data law is $P_y^d$, and the total is
$T(y)=\sum_{j\in U}y_j$. The induced-data expectation is
$E_y^d[h]=\sum_sp_d(s)h(O_y(s))$.

[F2] [[lem-finite-population-design-law-is-a-probability-measure]]: Every
$P_y^d$ is a probability measure on the common observed-record space.

[F3] [[def-parametric-statistical-model]]: The induced model is the map
$\mathsf P_d:y\mapsto P_y^d$ on parameter set $\mathbb R^U$.

[F4] [[def-estimand-on-a-statistical-model]]: An estimand is a function from
the model's parameter set to its target set; here it is $\psi_T(y)=T(y)$.

[F5] [[def-identifiability-of-an-estimand]]: An estimand is identifiable only
if equal sampling laws imply equal target values.

[F6] [[def-statistic-and-estimator]]: An estimator is a measurable statistic
with the target space of the specified estimand.

[F7] [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]: $\mathbb R$ has its Borel sigma-algebra,
generated by the open subsets.

[F8] [[def-expectation-on-a-finite-probability-space]]: Design expectation is
the finite weighted sum over $\mathcal S_d$.

[F9] [[def-unbiased-estimator-and-umvu]]: An estimator is unbiased if it is
integrable under every model law and its expectation equals the estimand at
every parameter.

## Proof

1.1 Since $\pi_i=\sum_{s:i\in s}p_d(s)=0$ and every support weight is strictly positive by [F1], no $s\in\mathcal S_d$ contains $i$. [given, F1, algebra]

2.1 Fix any $y\in\mathbb R^U$ and any $c\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}$. Define $y'_i=y_i+c$ and $y'_j=y_j$ for $j\ne i$. By step 1.1, $O_y(s)=O_{y'}(s)$ for every $s\in\mathcal S_d$. Therefore the finite sums defining $P_y^d(B)$ and $P_{y'}^d(B)$ agree for every measurable record set $B$, so $P_y^d=P_{y'}^d$; [F2] ensures these are laws in the induced experiment. But $T(y')=T(y)+c\ne T(y)$, so the total fails the defining implication for identifiability. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Let $\delta$ be any estimator from the common record space to $(\mathbb R,\mathcal B(\mathbb R))$. Since $O_y(s)=O_{y'}(s)$ for every support sample, [F8] gives $E_y^d[\delta]=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S_d}p_d(s)\delta(O_y(s))=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S_d}p_d(s)\delta(O_{y'}(s))=E_{y'}^d[\delta]$. Both expectations are finite because the design support is finite and $\delta$ is real-valued. By [F9], an estimator unbiased for every finite population would make these equal expectations equal both $T(y)$ and $T(y')$, which are unequal. Thus no such universally unbiased estimator exists. [F1, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
