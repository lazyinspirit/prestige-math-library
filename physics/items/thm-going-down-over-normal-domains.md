## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice.

Let $A\subseteq B$ be an integral extension of domains, and assume that $A$ is integrally closed. If $\mathfrak p_0\subseteq\mathfrak p_1$ are prime ideals of $A$ and $\mathfrak q_1$ is a prime ideal of $B$ with $\mathfrak q_1\cap A=\mathfrak p_1$, then there exists a prime ideal $\mathfrak q_0\subseteq\mathfrak q_1$ of $B$ such that $\mathfrak q_0\cap A=\mathfrak p_0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integral extension of domains $A\subseteq B$, an integrally closed base ring $A$, primes $\mathfrak p_0\subseteq\mathfrak p_1$ of $A$, and a prime $\mathfrak q_1$ of $B$ over $\mathfrak p_1$.

[L1] In an integral extension, every element of the upper ring satisfies a monic equation over the lower ring ([[def-integral-ring-extension]]).

[L2] Prime ideals of a localisation correspond exactly to primes disjoint from the denominator set ([[thm-prime-spectrum-of-a-localisation-bijection]]).

[L3] Assuming the Axiom of Choice, an ideal disjoint from a multiplicative set lies inside a prime ideal disjoint from that set ([[lem-prime-containing-a-multiplicative-set-disjoint-ideal]]).

[L4] If a monic polynomial in $A[T]$ factors in $\operatorname{Frac}(A)[T]$ as $(T-a)h(T)$ with $a\in\operatorname{Frac}(A)$ and $A$ integrally closed, then $h(T)$ has coefficients in $A$ ([[lem-integrally-closed-minimal-polynomial-coefficients]]).

[L5] A subalgebra generated by finitely many integral elements is module-finite ([[lem-algebra-generated-by-finitely-many-integral-elements-is-module-finite]]).

[L6] For a square matrix over a commutative ring, $A\operatorname{adj}(A)=\det(A)I$ ([[thm-adjugate-identity-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $S:=B\setminus\mathfrak q_1$. By [L2], primes of $B_S=B_{\mathfrak q_1}$ correspond exactly to primes of $B$ contained in $\mathfrak q_1$. It is therefore enough to find a prime ideal of $B_{\mathfrak q_1}$ whose contraction to $A$ is $\mathfrak p_0$. [L2, given]

2.1 Let $b\in A\cap\mathfrak p_0B_{\mathfrak q_1}$. Write $b=y/s$ with $y\in\mathfrak p_0B$ and $s\in B\setminus\mathfrak q_1$. Choose a representation $y=\sum_{j=1}^m a_jb_j$ with $a_j\in\mathfrak p_0$ and $b_j\in B$. Because the extension is integral, [L1] says that each $b_j$ and $s$ is integral over $A$. [L1, step 1.1, given]

3.1 Let $M:=A[s,b_1,\ldots,b_m]$. By [L5], $M$ is module-finite over $A$. Since $y=\sum a_jb_j$ lies in $\mathfrak p_0B$, multiplication by $y$ sends $M$ into $\mathfrak p_0M$: for $m'\in M$, one has $ym'=\sum_j a_j(b_jm')$ and each $b_jm'$ again lies in $M$. Choose $A$-generators $m_1,\ldots,m_r$ of $M$. Then there are coefficients $c_{ij}\in\mathfrak p_0$ with $ym_j=\sum_i c_{ij}m_i$. Writing $C=(c_{ij})$, the adjugate identity [L6] applied to $yI_r-C$ yields a monic polynomial $$ P(T)=T^r+c_{r-1}T^{r-1}+\cdots+c_0 $$ with every $c_i\in\mathfrak p_0$ and $P(y)=0$. [L5, L6, step 2.1, given, algebra]

4.1 Suppose $b\notin\mathfrak p_0$. Let $K:=\operatorname{Frac}(A)$. Since $s$ is integral over $A$, [L4] gives a monic minimal polynomial $$ m_s(T)=T^d+d_{d-1}T^{d-1}+\cdots+d_0 $$ with coefficients in $A$. Because $y=bs$ and $P(y)=0$, dividing by $b^r$ in $K[T]$ gives a monic polynomial $$ Q(T)=T^r+\frac{c_{r-1}}{b}T^{r-1}+\cdots+\frac{c_0}{b^r} $$ with coefficients in the localisation $A_{\mathfrak p_0}$ and satisfying $Q(s)=0$. Since $m_s$ is monic and divides every polynomial over $K$ that vanishes at $s$, it divides $Q$ in $K[T]$, hence also in $A_{\mathfrak p_0}[T]$. [L4, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Reduce that divisibility relation modulo $\mathfrak p_0A_{\mathfrak p_0}$. The image of $Q$ is $T^r$, so the image of $m_s$ is a monic divisor of $T^r$ in $\operatorname{Frac}(A/\mathfrak p_0)[T]$. Therefore $\overline{m_s}(T)=T^d$, and every coefficient $d_i$ lies in $\mathfrak p_0$. [step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 The relation $m_s(s)=0$ now shows that $s^d\in\mathfrak p_0B\subseteq\mathfrak q_1$. Because $\mathfrak q_1$ is prime, this forces $s\in\mathfrak q_1$, contradicting the choice of $s$. Hence $b\in\mathfrak p_0$. Therefore $$ A\cap\mathfrak p_0B_{\mathfrak q_1}=\mathfrak p_0. $$ [step 5.1, given, algebra]

7.1 Let $C:=B_{\mathfrak q_1}/\mathfrak p_0B_{\mathfrak q_1}$. Step 6.1 identifies $A/\mathfrak p_0$ with a subring of $C$; because $A/\mathfrak p_0$ is a domain, its nonzero elements form a multiplicative subset $T\subseteq C$. The zero ideal of $C$ is disjoint from $T$, so [L3] gives a prime ideal $\mathfrak n$ of $C$ disjoint from $T$. Its inverse image $\mathfrak q_0'$ in $B_{\mathfrak q_1}$ contains $\mathfrak p_0B_{\mathfrak q_1}$, and the disjointness from $T$ says exactly that $\mathfrak q_0'\cap A=\mathfrak p_0$. [L3, step 6.1, given]

8.1 By [L2], the prime $\mathfrak q_0'$ of $B_{\mathfrak q_1}$ is the localisation of a unique prime ideal $\mathfrak q_0\subseteq\mathfrak q_1$ of $B$. Its contraction to $A$ is $\mathfrak p_0$ by step 7.1. Therefore $\mathfrak q_0$ is the required prime below $\mathfrak q_1$. [L2, step 7.1] ∎
