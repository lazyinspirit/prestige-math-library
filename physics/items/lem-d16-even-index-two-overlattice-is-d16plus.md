## Statement

Let $D_{16}=\{z\in\mathbb Z^{16}:\sum_i z_i\in2\mathbb Z\}$ and $h=(1/2,\ldots,1/2)$. If $L$ is an even integral full lattice in $\mathbb R^{16}$ with $D_{16}\subseteq L\subseteq D_{16}^*$ and $[L:D_{16}]=2$, then $L$ is isometric to $D_{16}^{+}=D_{16}\cup(D_{16}+h)$ by a real inner-product-preserving additive bijection.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The standard inner product on $\mathbb R^{16}$ and the displayed lattice $D_{16}$.

[F1] The candidate $D_{16}^{+}$ is $D_{16}\cup(D_{16}+h)$, and $D_{16}$ is the even-coordinate-sum subgroup ([[def-e8-and-d16plus-rank16-lattices]]).

[F2] For a full lattice $\Lambda$, its dual is $\{x:\langle x,\lambda\rangle\in\mathbb Z\ \forall\lambda\in\Lambda\}$ ([[def-full-rank-lattice-covolume-and-dual-lattice]]).

[F3] Every vector of $D_{16}$ has even norm, and $D_{16}^{+}$ is a full even integral lattice ([[lem-e8-d16plus-are-even-unimodular]]).

[F4] An even lattice is one whose squared norms lie in $2\mathbb Z$; an integral lattice has integer pairings ([[def-positive-even-unimodular-lattice]]).

[L1] For a lattice inclusion $D_{16}\subseteq L$, the quotient $L/D_{16}$ consists of its additive cosets; index $2$ means this quotient has exactly two elements.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 If $x\in D_{16}^*$, testing against the roots $e_i-e_j$ and $e_i+e_j$ of $D_{16}$ gives $x_i-x_j\in\mathbb Z$ and $x_i+x_j\in\mathbb Z$ for all $i<j$. Hence $2x_i\in\mathbb Z$ and all coordinates have the same fractional part, so either $x\in\mathbb Z^{16}$ or $x\in\mathbb Z^{16}+h$. Conversely, integer vectors pair integrally with $D_{16}$, and $(z+h)\cdot d=z\cdot d+\frac12\sum_i d_i\in\mathbb Z$ for $z\in\mathbb Z^{16}$ and $d\in D_{16}$ because $\sum_i d_i$ is even. Therefore $D_{16}^*=\mathbb Z^{16}\cup(\mathbb Z^{16}+h)$. [F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 The quotient $D_{16}^*/D_{16}$ has four distinct classes represented by $0,e_1,h,h+e_1$: the integer vectors split into even- and odd-coordinate-sum classes, and the half-integer vectors split into the two corresponding classes after subtracting $h$. The order-2 subgroup $L/D_{16}$ is generated by one of the three nonzero classes. [step 1.1, L1, algebra]

3.1 The class of $e_1$ is excluded because $\|e_1\|^2=1$, contradicting evenness of $L$. The classes of $h$ and $h+e_1$ are even: their representative norms are $16/4=4$ and $9/4+15/4=6$, and for any representative $u$ of either class and $d\in D_{16}$, $\|u+d\|^2=\|u\|^2+2\langle u,d\rangle+\|d\|^2$ is even since $u\in D_{16}^*$, $D_{16}$ is even, and [F4] applies. Thus $L$ is one of $D_{16}\cup(D_{16}+h)$ or $D_{16}\cup(D_{16}+h+e_1)$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The orthogonal sign change $S(x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_{16})=(-x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_{16})$ preserves $D_{16}$ because it changes the coordinate sum by $-2x_1$, an even integer. It sends $h$ to $h-e_1$, which is in the coset $h+e_1+D_{16}$ since $2e_1\in D_{16}$. Hence $S$ maps $D_{16}\cup(D_{16}+h)$ onto $D_{16}\cup(D_{16}+h+e_1)$ isometrically. Both allowed extensions are therefore isometric to $D_{16}^{+}$. [F1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
