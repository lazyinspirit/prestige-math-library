## Statement

Assume AC. Let $X$ be an oriented smooth $2m$-manifold and $A^m\subseteq X$ a closed oriented embedded submanifold whose normal bundle $\nu_A$ is oriented compatibly, with $m$ even. Then the self-intersection number of [[def-self-intersection-number-of-an-oriented-submanifold]] satisfies $$A\cdot A=\langle e(\nu_A),[A]\rangle,$$ and $e(\nu_A)$ is Poincare dual to the zero locus of any smooth section of $\nu_A$ transverse to the zero section ([[thm-self-intersection-is-the-euler-number-of-the-normal-bundle]], [[prop-zero-locus-of-a-transverse-oriented-bundle-section-represents-the-euler-dual]]). For odd rank the Euler class of an oriented bundle is two-torsion ([[prop-euler-class-of-an-oriented-odd-rank-bundle-is-two-torsion]]), so its integral evaluation on a closed oriented odd-dimensional source is zero, whereas a nonzero pairing $\langle e(\nu_A),[A]\rangle$ in even rank makes $e(\nu_A)$ non-torsion and prevents $\nu_A$ from admitting a nowhere-zero section ([[prop-a-nowhere-zero-section-forces-the-euler-class-to-vanish]]). Over $\mathbb F_2$, with no orientation hypotheses, the same identities hold with $w_m(\nu_A)=e_2(\nu_A)$ ([[prop-mod-two-self-intersection-needs-no-orientation]], [[thm-mod-two-euler-class-is-the-top-stiefel-whitney-class]]).

Give $\mathbb R^{2m}$ its standard orientation. Let $M^m$ be a closed oriented smooth manifold with $m$ even, and let $f:M\looparrowright\mathbb R^{2m}$ be a smooth immersion. Its normal bundle $\nu_f$, the quotient in [[def-normal-bundle-of-a-formal-immersion]], is oriented by the orientations of $M$ and $\mathbb R^{2m}$. For $m>0$, [[lem-an-immersion-into-r-n-gives-a-rank-n-minus-m-representative-of-the-stable-normal-bundle]] gives $TM\oplus\nu_f\cong\varepsilon^{2m}$; for $m=0$ both summands are zero bundles, so the identity holds directly. Hence $\langle e(\nu_f),[M]\rangle$ is the signed zero count of any transverse section of $\nu_f$; it is invariant under regular homotopy ([[def-regular-homotopy-of-immersions]]) and vanishes if $\nu_f$ admits a nowhere-zero section. If $m=2s\ge2$, $f$ is self-transverse and has no triple points, set $D(f)=\sum_{r\in\Sigma(f)}\varepsilon(r)$ with the ordering-independent branch signs. Then $\langle e(\nu_f),[M]\rangle+2D(f)=0.$ In particular the Euler number is even, $D(f)=-\langle e(\nu_f),[M]\rangle/2$, and $\#\Sigma(f)\bmod2=-\langle e(\nu_f),[M]\rangle/2\bmod2$. A nonzero $D(f)$ obstructs regular homotopy to an embedding; in particular odd double-point parity does. No sufficiency criterion or odd-dimensional Whitney identification is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An oriented smooth $2m$-manifold $X$ and a closed oriented embedded $A^m\subseteq X$ with $m$ even and compatibly oriented normal bundle (first paragraph); a closed oriented $M^m$ with $m$ even and a smooth immersion $f:M\looparrowright\mathbb R^{2m}$ (second paragraph); AC.

[F1] For a closed oriented embedded $A^m\subseteq X$ of an oriented boundaryless $X$, the self-intersection number equals the evaluation of the Euler class of the normal bundle, $A\cdot A=\langle e(\nu_A),[A]\rangle$, and the Euler class is Poincaré dual to the zero locus of a transverse section ([[def-self-intersection-number-of-an-oriented-submanifold]], [[thm-self-intersection-is-the-euler-number-of-the-normal-bundle]], [[prop-zero-locus-of-a-transverse-oriented-bundle-section-represents-the-euler-dual]]); mod two the same holds without orientations with $w_m(\nu_A)=e_2(\nu_A)$ ([[prop-mod-two-self-intersection-needs-no-orientation]], [[thm-mod-two-euler-class-is-the-top-stiefel-whitney-class]]).

[F2] The Euler class of an oriented odd-rank bundle is two-torsion, so it pairs to zero against the fundamental class of a closed oriented source of odd dimension; a nowhere-zero section forces the Euler class to vanish, with no converse ([[prop-euler-class-of-an-oriented-odd-rank-bundle-is-two-torsion]], [[prop-a-nowhere-zero-section-forces-the-euler-class-to-vanish]], [[def-euler-class-by-zero-section-pullback-of-the-thom-class]]).

[F3] For $m>0$ and an immersion $f:M^m\looparrowright\mathbb R^{2m}$ of a closed oriented $M$ with the normal bundle $\nu_f=f^*T\mathbb R^{2m}/df(TM)$ oriented by the orientations of $M$ and $\mathbb R^{2m}$, one has $TM\oplus\nu_f\cong\varepsilon^{2m}$, so $\nu_f$ is a rank-$m$ stable normal inverse of $M$ ([[def-normal-bundle-of-a-formal-immersion]], [[lem-an-immersion-into-r-n-gives-a-rank-n-minus-m-representative-of-the-stable-normal-bundle]], [[def-stable-normal-inverse-of-the-tangent-bundle]]). For $m=0$, both $TM$ and the normal quotient are zero bundles, so the same bundle identity holds directly.

[F4] A regular homotopy of immersions is a smooth family $H:M\times I\to\mathbb R^{2m}$ restricting to an immersion at each time ([[def-regular-homotopy-of-immersions]]). For a smooth map $H$ the vertical derivative $d_MH:\operatorname{pr}_M^*TM\to H^*T\mathbb R^{2m}$ is a smooth bundle map over $M\times I$ ([[def-vector-bundle-map-over-a-smooth-base-map]]); if it is fibrewise injective, its image is a subbundle and the quotient is a smooth vector bundle ([[prop-constant-rank-kernels-and-images-of-bundle-maps-over-one-base-are-subbundles]], [[def-quotient-vector-bundle-by-a-subbundle]], [[thm-a-vector-bundle-quotient-by-a-subbundle-is-a-smooth-vector-bundle]]). The local minor formulas for the image and quotient bundles also apply in boundary charts. Under AC the base $M\times I$ is paracompact Hausdorff CGWH of CW type and these bundles are numerable ([[lem-second-countable-smooth-manifolds-have-cw-homotopy-type]]). The Euler class is natural for bundle maps over a base map preserving orientation, so a bundle isomorphism over the identity gives equal Euler classes ([[thm-naturality-orientation-sign-and-whitney-product-for-euler-classes]]); homotopic maps induce the same map in singular cohomology ([[thm-homotopic-maps-induce-equal-maps-in-singular-cohomology]]).

[F5] The diagonal $\Delta_{\mathbb R^{2m}}\subseteq\mathbb R^{2m}\times\mathbb R^{2m}$ is an embedded submanifold of complementary dimension to the product $M\times M$ ([[prop-the-diagonal-is-an-embedded-submanifold]]); oriented intersection numbers of complementary-dimensional maps are homotopy invariant ([[thm-oriented-intersection-number-is-homotopy-invariant]]) and satisfy the factor-interchange formula with sign $(-1)^{m^2}=1$ for $m$ even ([[thm-intersection-number-under-factor-interchange]]).

[F6] For a self-transverse immersion $f:M^{2s}\looparrowright\mathbb R^{4s}$ with $s\ge1$ of a closed oriented manifold with no triple points, a transverse section $\sigma$ and all sufficiently small $t>0$ satisfy $I(f,f_t)=\langle e(\nu_f),[M]\rangle+2\sum_{r\in\Sigma(f)}\varepsilon(r)$ ([[lem-finite-normal-push-off-count-for-an-even-dimensional-euclidean-immersion]]); if $m\ge1$ and $f$ is an embedding into $\mathbb R^{2m}$ then $e(\nu_f)=0$ and $\bar w_m(TM)=0$ ([[cor-top-normal-stiefel-whitney-and-euler-classes-vanish-for-euclidean-embeddings]]).

[F7] AC implies the countable choice used throughout the normal-bundle and Euler-class suppliers ([[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

## Proof

1.1 First consider the embedded case. By [F1] one has $A\cdot A=\langle e(\nu_A),[A]\rangle$ for the closed oriented embedded $A^m\subseteq X$, computed with the tangent-first sign convention of the self-intersection number, and $e(\nu_A)$ is Poincaré dual to the zero locus of any section transverse to the zero section: the Koszul sign of that duality is $(-1)^{m(n-m)}=(-1)^{m^2}=+1$ because $m$ is even. [F2] gives that an odd-rank oriented bundle has two-torsion Euler class, so on a closed oriented odd-dimensional source its evaluation vanishes, whereas no such vanishing is available in even rank, and $\langle e(\nu_A),[A]\rangle\ne0$ excludes a nowhere-zero section of $\nu_A$ by [F2]. Over $\mathbb F_2$ no orientation is needed and the identity reads $A\cdot_2 A=\langle w_m(\nu_A),[A]\rangle_2$ by [F1]. [F1, F2]

1.2 Now let $f:M^m\looparrowright\mathbb R^{2m}$ with $m$ even. The normal bundle $\nu_f$ of the formal immersion $(f,df)$ is oriented by the orientation of $M$ and the standard orientation of $\mathbb R^{2m}$, and by [F3] the orthogonal decomposition gives $TM\oplus\nu_f\cong\varepsilon^{2m}$; hence $e(\nu_f)$ is defined integrally and, by the section form of [F1] applied to the immersion normal bundle, $\langle e(\nu_f),[M]\rangle$ is the signed count of the zeros of any transverse section of $\nu_f$. If $\nu_f$ admits a nowhere-zero section then $\langle e(\nu_f),[M]\rangle=0$ by [F2]. We prove the asserted regular-homotopy invariance in the next step. [F1, F2, F3, F7]

2.1 If $m=0$, every map $M\to\mathbb R^0$ is the same map, so its normal data and Euler number are unchanged by any regular homotopy. For $m>0$, let $H:M\times I\to\mathbb R^{2m}$ be a regular homotopy from $H_0$ to $H_1$, and write $\mathcal N_H:=H^*T\mathbb R^{2m}/d_MH(\operatorname{pr}_M^*TM)$ for the vertical quotient. The vertical derivative $d_MH$ is a smooth bundle map over $M\times I$ and is fibrewise injective because every $H_t$ is an immersion, so its image is a rank-$m$ subbundle and $\mathcal N_H$ is a smooth rank-$m$ vector bundle over $M\times I$ by [F4]. At each time $t$ the restriction of $\mathcal N_H$ to $M\times\{t\}$ is the normal bundle of $H_t$ under the canonical identification, and the orientations of $M$ and $\mathbb R^{2m}$ induce an orientation of $\mathcal N_H$ whose restrictions are the orientations of the endpoint normal bundles. With the endpoint inclusions $j_0,j_1:M\to M\times I$, naturality of the Euler class [F4] gives $e(\nu_{H_t})=j_t^*e(\mathcal N_H)$ for $t=0,1$, and $j_0,j_1$ are homotopic through $(x,t)\mapsto(x,t)$; by [F4] they induce the same map in cohomology, so $e(\nu_{H_0})=e(\nu_{H_1})$ and hence $\langle e(\nu_{H_0}),[M]\rangle=\langle e(\nu_{H_1}),[M]\rangle$. This is invariance under every regular homotopy, with no genericity assumption on the intermediate slices. [F3, F4, step 1.2]

2.2 Suppose now $m=2s\ge2$ and $f$ is self-transverse with no triple points, and put $D(f)=\sum_{r\in\Sigma(f)}\varepsilon(r)$ with the ordering-independent signs of the self-transverse case. By [F6] there are a transverse section $\sigma$ and small $t>0$ with $I(f,f_t)=\langle e(\nu_f),[M]\rangle+2D(f)$. We show $I(f,f_t)=0$. The pair $(f,f_t)$ is transverse to the diagonal $\Delta\subseteq\mathbb R^{2m}\times\mathbb R^{2m}$ at these intersections, and by [F5] the oriented intersection number of the pair equals $I(f\times f_t,\Delta)$. Here orient $\Delta$ by $v\mapsto(v,v)$ and identify its normal quotient by $(u,v)\mapsto v-u$. At a coincidence the normal derivative of the product is $(-df,df_t)$, so its determinant differs from that of the ordered pair $(df,df_t)$ by $(-1)^m=1$, since $m$ is even. Translate the second factor: for $s\in[0,1]$ put $f_{t,s}(y)=f_t(y)+scv$ with a fixed nonzero vector $v$ and $c$ so large that $\operatorname{im}(f_t)+cv$ is disjoint from $\operatorname{im}f$; both images are compact, so such $c$ exists. This is a smooth homotopy of the product map $(f,f_{t,s})$ through the target of [F5], and the endpoint has no intersections with the diagonal, so its intersection number is $0$; by homotopy invariance [F5] the number $I(f\times f_t,\Delta)$ is $0$ as well. Hence $\langle e(\nu_f),[M]\rangle+2D(f)=0$. [F5, F6, step 1.2]

3.1 It follows that $2D(f)=-\langle e(\nu_f),[M]\rangle$, so the Euler number $\langle e(\nu_f),[M]\rangle$ is even, $D(f)=-\langle e(\nu_f),[M]\rangle/2$, and reducing the integer equality $D(f)=-\langle e(\nu_f),[M]\rangle/2$ modulo $2$ gives $\#\Sigma(f)\equiv D(f)\equiv-\langle e(\nu_f),[M]\rangle/2\pmod 2$, since every sign is $\pm1\equiv1$ mod two. Finally, if $f$ were regularly homotopic to an embedding $g:M\hookrightarrow\mathbb R^{2m}$, then step 2.1 would give $\langle e(\nu_f),[M]\rangle=\langle e(\nu_g),[M]\rangle$, while for the embedding [F6] forces $e(\nu_g)=0$; hence $D(f)=-\langle e(\nu_f),[M]\rangle/2=0$. Contrapositively, a nonzero $D(f)$, and in particular an odd double-point parity, obstructs a regular homotopy to an embedding. The statement asserts no converse: vanishing of $D$ does not produce a regular homotopy to an embedding, and no odd-dimensional Whitney identification is claimed. AC is used through the normal-bundle and Euler-class suppliers and the oriented intersection theory; the regular-homotopy invariance of step 2.1 is valid for all $m$, while the push-off count of step 2.2 uses the even-dimensional hypotheses. [F2, F4, F5, F6, step 2.1, step 2.2] ∎
