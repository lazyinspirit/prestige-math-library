## Statement

Assume the full Axiom of Choice. Let $\pi:X\to B$ be a smooth proper submersion between finite-dimensional Hausdorff second-countable smooth manifolds without boundary, with closed compact fibers of dimension $n$. Fix a differential Spin-c orientation in the Freed–Lott geometric convention. For every $x\in\widehat K^0_{\mathrm{FL}}(X)$, the analytic family index and the differential-topological pushforward agree in $\widehat K^{-n}_{\mathrm{FL}}(B)$:

$$\operatorname{ind}^{\mathrm{an}}_\pi(x)=\pi^{\mathrm{top}}_!(x).$$

Their common curvature is

$$R(\pi_!x)=\int_{X/B}\operatorname{Todd}(\nabla^{T^vX},\nabla^L)\wedge R(x),$$

where fiber integration uses the orientation determined by the Spin-c structure and the normalized Todd form from the orientation item. For odd $n$, the range and analytic index use the source's suspension convention. The equality is a conditional mathematical families-index theorem; it is not an assertion about a physical path integral.

1.1 **Geometric operator and normalization.** In the analytic heat estimates, u>0 is a REAL heat scale, distinct from the formal Bott variable of the cycle definitions; denote that formal variable by b in these estimates. A Chern-form coefficient of ordinary degree 2j carries the normalization (2 pi i)^(-j)b^(-j); an eta-insertion coefficient of ordinary degree 2j−1 carries the same normalization. These are precisely the coefficients of the extended Chern form with its additional parameter differential. They define Str_norm throughout. Equivalently the normal-current insertion is (2 pi i)^(-1/2) times the degree-normalization phi; the half powers cancel coefficientwise, leaving only integral Bott powers. The odd-cycle construction at the end reverts to its declared formal variable u. Research construction of the geometric operator, not yet a families heat-limit proof. This fixes the mean-curvature and horizontal-curvature coefficients without importing Bismut's theorem.
Work locally on the base with coordinate horizontal lifts H_alpha, and choose a smooth orthonormal vertical frame E_i. At the point where the following coefficients are evaluated the base frame is orthonormal and its Christoffel symbols vanish. Write
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{[H\_alpha,E\_i]=B\_(alpha i)\textasciicircum{}j E\_j,}\\\texttt{vertical[H\_alpha,H\_beta]=T\_(alpha beta)\textasciicircum{}i E\_i.}\end{gathered}$$
Use the total metric g_u=g_vertical+u^(-2)g_base. Its orthonormal horizontal frame is f_alpha=u H_alpha. The relevant Koszul coefficients are
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{2< nabla\_(E\_i) E\_j,f\_alpha >=u(B\_(alpha j)\textasciicircum{}i+B\_(alpha i)\textasciicircum{}j),}\\\texttt{2< nabla\_(H\_alpha) E\_i,E\_j >=B\_(alpha i)\textasciicircum{}j-B\_(alpha j)\textasciicircum{}i,}\\\texttt{2< nabla\_(E\_i) f\_alpha,f\_beta >=-u\textasciicircum{}2 T\_(alpha beta)\textasciicircum{}i,}\\\texttt{2< nabla\_(f\_alpha) E\_i,f\_beta >=-u\textasciicircum{}2 T\_(alpha beta)\textasciicircum{}i,}\\\texttt{2< nabla\_(f\_alpha) f\_beta,E\_i >=+u\textasciicircum{}2 T\_(alpha beta)\textasciicircum{}i.}\end{gathered}$$
Each identity follows by inserting the three frame vectors into
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{2< nabla\_X Y,Z > = X<Y,Z>+Y<Z,X>-Z<X,Y>}\\\texttt{-<X,[Y,Z]>+<Y,[Z,X]>+<Z,[X,Y]>.}\end{gathered}$$
All derivatives of the displayed orthonormal-frame inner products vanish; the brackets above give exactly the listed terms. The all-vertical coefficients give the vertical Levi-Civita connection. The all-horizontal coefficients give the ordinary base connection, and tensoriality reinstates it away from the chosen normal base frame.
Represent the horizontal Clifford algebra on base forms by
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{c\_u(f\_alpha)=u\textasciicircum{}(-1) epsilon\_alpha-u iota\_alpha.}\end{gathered}$$
This satisfies c_u(f_alpha)c_u(f_beta)+c_u(f_beta)c_u(f_alpha)=-2 delta_(alpha beta). The vertical Clifford operators are graded tensored with this representation, so they anticommute with the horizontal odd operators. This representation is equivalent for every u>0 to the usual exterior Clifford representation after rescaling forms; hence the ordinary total Dirac and Lichnerowicz identities apply before taking u to zero.
The spin connection contribution to the total Dirac operator is
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{(1/4) sum\_(a,b,c) c(e\_a)<nabla\_(e\_a)e\_b,e\_c>c(e\_b)c(e\_c).}\end{gathered}$$
The symmetric B terms sum to
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{-(u/2) sum\_alpha tr(B\_alpha)c\_u(f\_alpha).}\end{gathered}$$
Indeed the symmetric contraction sum_(ij)c(E_i)c(E_j)(B_ij+B_ji) is -2 tr B. Adding the direct horizontal derivative term and taking u to zero gives the Hilbert-space connection
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{nabla\textasciicircum{}H\_(H\_alpha)=nabla\textasciicircum{}(vertical spinor tensor E)\_(H\_alpha)}\\\texttt{-(1/2) tr(B\_alpha).}\end{gathered}$$
This connection is unitary: Lie differentiation of the vertical volume form along H_alpha is -tr(B_alpha) times that volume form. Differentiating the fiberwise L2 pairing therefore gives exactly the two scalar corrections -tr(B_alpha)/2, one from each argument.
For T the vertical-derivative contribution is
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{-(u\textasciicircum{}2/8) sum\_(i,alpha,beta) T\_(alpha beta)\textasciicircum{}i}\\\texttt{c(E\_i)c\_u(f\_alpha)c\_u(f\_beta),}\end{gathered}$$
whereas the two mixed horizontal-derivative contributions together are
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{+(u\textasciicircum{}2/4) sum\_(i,alpha,beta) T\_(alpha beta)\textasciicircum{}i}\\\texttt{c(E\_i)c\_u(f\_alpha)c\_u(f\_beta).}\end{gathered}$$
Their sum is +(u^2/8) times the ordered alpha,beta sum, equivalently +(u^2/4) times alpha<beta. Put tau=-vertical[H,H] and define
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{c(tau)=sum\_(alpha<beta) epsilon\_alpha epsilon\_beta}\\\texttt{c\_vertical(tau\_(alpha beta)).}\end{gathered}$$
The limit of the total Dirac operator is consequently
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{A=D\_vertical+nabla\textasciicircum{}H-(1/4)c(tau).}\end{gathered}$$
Twisting adds the actual given connection to both the vertical Dirac and the horizontal connection; it does not alter the universal coefficients just calculated. The Spin-c determinant-line connection is included in the vertical spinor connection and has relative curvature F_L/2, so with the existing minus-curvature Chern character its factor is exp(-F_L/(4 pi i)).
Finally, rescale the vertical metric by t^(-1). Vertical orthonormal frames scale by sqrt(t), hence D scales by sqrt(t), Clifford multiplication by the fixed vertical vector tau scales by t^(-1/2), and the Hilbert connection is unchanged after the canonical volume-unitary identification. Thus the family is exactly
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{A\_t=sqrt(t)D+nabla\textasciicircum{}H-c(tau)/(4 sqrt(t)).}\end{gathered}$$
This is the form used in the large-time block-diagonalization proof. The calculation supplies the operator and its constants only. The small-time local formula still requires the rescaled vertical heat-kernel calculation with the torsion terms retained; deleting the c(tau)^2/t term or claiming it tends to zero would be invalid. 1.2 **Actual normal oscillator.** Research construction of the normal model only. It is not a proof of the global embedding eta identity.
Let W be an oriented Euclidean space of even dimension r and identify the normal spinor tensor its dual with Lambda(W_C^*). On this exterior algebra put c_i=epsilon_i-iota_i and c_hat_i=epsilon_i+iota_i. The anticommutators are {c_i,c_j}=-2delta_ij, {c_hat_i,c_hat_j}=2delta_ij, {c_i,c_hat_j}=0. On Schwartz forms on W define
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{Q=sum\_i(c\_i partial\_i+c\_hat\_i x\_i).}\end{gathered}$$
It is symmetric, and direct expansion gives
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{Q\textasciicircum{}2=-Delta+|x|\textasciicircum{}2+2N-r,}\end{gathered}$$
where N=sum_i epsilon_i iota_i is exterior degree. In one dimension set a=partial_x+x and a*= -partial_x+x. Integration by parts gives a*a=-partial_x^2+x^2-1 and [a,a*]=2. The normalized Gaussian is killed by a. Applying (a*)^k gives mutually orthogonal eigenfunctions with eigenvalues 2k of a*a. Their span is dense: if a vector is orthogonal to all polynomial times exp(-x^2/2), its Fourier-Laplace transform after multiplication by exp(-x^2/2) is entire, has all derivatives zero at zero, and hence is zero. Fourier uniqueness then gives the vector zero. Tensoring this argument proves the complete r-dimensional Hermite basis, without assuming a spectral diagram.
Consequently Q^2 has eigenvalues 2(|k|+j), k in N^r, j exterior degree. Its kernel is exactly the normalized Gaussian times the exterior unit; it has positive grading, dimension one, and every other eigenvalue is at least 2. In particular
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{||(1-P\_0) exp(-s Q\textasciicircum{}2)|| <= exp(-2s).}\end{gathered}$$
Every polynomial coordinate or derivative insertion is bounded by a fixed power of 1+Q^2, as follows from x=(a+a*)/2 and partial=(a-a*)/2. This gives the differentiated exponential bounds needed for normal heat kernels away from the ground state. It does not by itself make the bounds uniform in every mixed tangential/normal scaling of an embedded family.
For an orthogonal normal bundle with metric connection, the Gaussian exterior unit is invariant under the frame action. Its induced connection is therefore the trivial connection, after the fiber-volume unitary identification. In the Spin-c normal spinor tensor its dual, the two central U(1) weights cancel, giving exactly this same exterior representation. Twisting by E identifies the normal ground-state bundle and its connection with E and its connection. Scaling the normal coordinate by sqrt(T) gives
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{Q\_T=sum\_i(c\_i partial\_i+T c\_hat\_i x\_i),}\\\texttt{Q\_T\textasciicircum{}2 unitarily equivalent to T Q\textasciicircum{}2.}\end{gathered}$$
Thus the normal spectral gap is 2T and its ground bundle remains E. This fixes the coefficient and the grading of the embedding ground state. No division by a characteristic form or a vanishing bundle rank occurs.
For the actual embedding problem one still must prove that the global family, its eta integral, and its spectral-cut connections localize to this model with estimates uniform at the corner t->0,T->infinity. The exact oscillator does not justify replacing that family by the model before those estimates are established.
**Exact diagonal Gaussian and the two normal scales**
Write tau=tT. The scalar Mehler kernel of -Delta+T^2|x|^2 at the diagonal is
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{(T/(2 pi sinh(2tau)))\textasciicircum{}(r/2) exp(-T tanh(tau)|x|\textasciicircum{}2).}\end{gathered}$$
This follows from the same Gaussian-ansatz differential equations as the existing Mehler supplier, or by differentiating the displayed kernel and its off-diagonal polarization. The exterior factor for Q_T^2 is exp(r tau)exp(-2tau N). Its supertrace is exp(r tau)(1-exp(-2tau))^r=(2sinh(tau))^r. Multiplying and integrating the scalar Gaussian gives exactly one, for every t,T>0. Thus the normal model has no small-time/large-potential transition defect: its entire supertrace, not only its ground-state asymptotic, is one.
For a scalar polynomial p(x), the same calculation gives the exact formula
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{Str(p(x)exp(-t Q\_T\textasciicircum{}2))}\\\texttt{= integral p(x) (T tanh(tau)/pi)\textasciicircum{}(r/2)}\\\texttt{exp(-T tanh(tau)|x|\textasciicircum{}2) dx.}\end{gathered}$$
In particular its even coordinate moments have the ordinary centered Gaussian moments with coordinate variance 1/(2T tanh(tau)); odd moments vanish. Since tanh(tau)>=c min(tau,1), the characteristic normal coordinate scale is bounded by
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{C( T\textasciicircum{}(-1/2) + 1/(T sqrt(t)) ).}\end{gathered}$$
This proves the two-scale coordinate bound directly. It is a signed-supertrace identity for scalar insertions and cannot be substituted without further calculation for arbitrary Clifford/differential insertions in a global Duhamel remainder. That additional insertion calculation is precisely part of the outstanding mixed-corner comparison.

1.3 **Odd cycle transgressions and suspension.** This is the actual replacement construction, not a proof of the former two-relation presentation. **1. Actual transgressions, including the missing relation**
Write ch(A)=R_u tr exp(−u^-1 A²), and omega(nabla,U)=K_t ch(d_t+(1−f(t))nabla+f(t)U nabla U^-1), where f increases from zero to one and is constant near the endpoints. Replacing f by t does not change this integral. Its differential is zero, because the endpoint Chern forms agree. On a parameter family, connections and automorphisms in this formula mean the actual connections and automorphisms on the product manifold, including their parameter components.
If U_s is a smooth unitary path at fixed nabla, put T(U_s)=K_s omega(d_s+nabla,U_s). Then dT=omega(U_1)−omega(U_0). In addition to the short-exact-sequence relation, impose
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{[G,nabla,U\_1,phi-T(U\_s)]=[G,nabla,U\_0,phi].                 (O1)}\end{gathered}$$
The short-exact-sequence CS is likewise an actual path integral: choose the orthogonal splitting and interpolate the split connection to the middle connection, with the split automorphism transported along this splitting. Its CS is K_s omega of that connection family. The word “compatible” means a unitary connection, not that the exact-sequence maps are parallel. Two choices give the same relation: interpolate the choices, use the two-parameter identity dK_sK_r+K_sK_r d equal to the signed boundary integrals, and absorb the resulting exact form. This identity follows by writing a form as a+ds b+dr c+ds dr e and differentiating.
There is also an entirely specified product CS. On G direct-sum G let
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{W\_theta=diag(U\_1,1) R\_theta diag(U\_2,1) R\_theta\textasciicircum{}-1,}\end{gathered}$$
where R_theta is the real unitary rotation from theta=0 to pi/2. Its endpoints are diag(U_1 U_2,1) and diag(U_1,U_2). Set CS_product=−K_theta omega(d_theta+nabla direct-sum nabla,W_theta). Then d CS_product=omega(U_1 U_2)−omega(U_1)−omega(U_2), and (O1) plus direct sum gives exactly the product relation with correction −CS_product. Thus this relation retains its intended scope; a primitive is not selected just by prescribing its differential. In particular [G,1,0]=0 by the product relation.
At a point the path U_s=exp(2 pi i s) has T=u^-1: its interpolating curvature is −2 pi i dt ds and the normalized first Chern term is u^-1 dt ds. Thus (O1) identifies corrections differing by an integer multiple of u^-1. The former presentation did not: at a point all of its declared CS forms vanish and its real correction coordinate survives. This is a necessary correction of the old interface.
For an actual odd cycle x over M times I_s, the relations give
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{x\_1-x\_0=a(K\_s R(x)).                                      (O2)}\end{gathered}$$
To check this, put its connection into temporal gauge using unitary parallel transport. It then becomes a path of connections and unitaries. First vary the connection with U fixed, using the exact-sequence connection relation; then vary U using (O1). The discrepancy between this broken path and the original path is the boundary of the connection-unitary square, hence exact by the preceding two-parameter identity. The correction-form term follows from dK+Kd=end1−end0. The same proof for even cycles is the even homotopy formula. This argument constructs an actual cycle over the interval; it does not infer equality of flat classes from equality of curvature.

Since K_t d=−dK_t and K_t ch(nabla_lin)=omega(nabla^G,U),

Changes of temporal trivialization are isometric isomorphisms. **4. Uniform graph regularization, including the adjoint**
Here are the functional estimates needed to pass from (4) to a pointwise kernel. They are included to specify the uniformity in the tangential parameter u and the normal parameter T. They are not obtained by replacing H by a frozen oscillator.
For either L=H or L=H_e in the scaled representation, integration by parts in the coefficient list gives
$$\begin{gathered}\texttt{Re <Ls,s> >= c||s||\_(1,T)\textasciicircum{}2-C||s||\_0\textasciicircum{}2,}\\\texttt{|Im <Ls,s>| <= C||s||\_(1,T)\textasciicircum{}2,}\\\texttt{|<Ls,t>| <= C||s||\_(1,T)||t||\_(1,T).                         (5)}\end{gathered}$$

Horizontal first-order bounded coefficients are absorbed in the derivative square. The scalar metric is uniformly elliptic. Radial spin-connection and tangential curvature coefficients are bounded by the degree weights in Section 2; their differentiated weight errors are bounded zero-order or first-order terms. These are all the coefficient types. Taking T above one fixed threshold gives (5); the remaining bounded T interval has the identical proof with a larger C, since its normal oscillator is still confining. The coefficient of every non-scalar leading exterior-degree term is triangular in exterior degree; apply the energy estimate successively in that finite degree filtration, or equivalently decrease successive degree weights by fixed constants. This last finite change is independent of u and T. First choose the finite degree constants, then take u below one fixed u_0 so that every opposite-degree contraction term, carrying u^2, remains a small form perturbation with these constants. For u_0<=u<=1 use the physical self-adjoint representation, whose change to the displayed Clifford representation has a uniformly bounded norm and inverse. For completeness, graph regularization can be organized without commuting an ordinary normal derivative through TQ^2 (which would produce a false uniform commutator assertion). Decompose into oscillator levels and use the horizontal covariant derivatives. Horizontal derivatives commute with Q^2. A normal polynomial coefficient in Section 2 has fixed bandwidth in the level index l and norm C(l+1); its derivatives have the same property. Conjugating by (1+Q^2)^a multiplies each band by ((1+l')/(1+l))^a, uniformly bounded for fixed a and |l-l'| bounded. Commuting one horizontal derivative through the connection adds its curvature, a level-preserving normal rotation of size C(l+1); it is controlled by one additional unscaled Q^2 graph factor. Repeating this rule gives, for each finite collection of horizontal derivatives and oscillator graph powers, a finite list of exactly these terms. The only coefficient containing T is TQ^2 itself, whose commutators with the chosen horizontal derivatives and oscillator graph powers vanish.
One can now obtain arbitrary smoothing as follows. First invert lambda-L at a sufficiently negative real lambda by the coercive form (5). After adding a fixed constant a to L, its numerical range lies in a sector |arg z|<=theta_0<pi/2, by (5). Choose theta strictly between theta_0 and pi/2 and a contour consisting of the two rays -a+r exp(plus or minus i theta), joined to the left of the numerical range. Projection on the separating direction gives the resolvent bounds outside this sector; the inverse continues from the negative real axis by the resolvent identity. The uniform bound makes continuation closed along every finite contour segment. The resolvent has H^-1_T -> H^1_T norm with at most polynomial growth on this contour. A parabolic numerical-range estimate is not needed: in fact the normal quadratic curvature terms need only satisfy the sectorial bound in (5).
Here is a graph-gain version of the commutator argument. Conjugate first by (1+Q^2)^b for a fixed b. Its level-band ratios are uniformly bounded, so its change to the operator is another fixed-band normal-order-two term. For large T this is absorbed by TQ^2, exactly as in (5). Thus the same resolvent energy bound holds at every prescribed oscillator graph weight, and gains one Q graph power. Iterating resolvent factors gains any specified normal graph order, uniformly. Next take horizontal difference quotients of the resolvent equation. Commuting a derivative through a coefficient gives its bounded derivative; commuting through another covariant derivative gives a normal rotation, of normal order two and independent of T. Its extra normal order is paid by two of the oscillator graph gains already obtained. The scalar uniformly elliptic horizontal principal part gives one more horizontal derivative by the same integration-by-parts estimate. Repetition gives any prescribed finite horizontal Sobolev order. The only differentiated coefficient proportional to T would have been T nabla(Q^2), and this is zero. At each stage there are finitely many commutators, with a finite additional number of resolvent factors and a polynomial contour-parameter loss. This gives arbitrary mixed graph gains without claiming that ordinary normal derivatives have uniformly bounded commutators with TQ^2.
For a precise limiting construction, truncate the oscillator levels at M and use horizontal difference quotients on compact coordinate subballs. $$\begin{gathered}\texttt{||exp(-tL)||\_(0 -> 1,T) <= C t\textasciicircum{}(-1/2),}\\\texttt{||exp(-tL)||\_(-1,T -> 0) <= C t\textasciicircum{}(-1/2),  0<t<=1.             (6)}\end{gathered}$$
