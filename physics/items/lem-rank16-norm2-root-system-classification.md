## Statement

Let $\Phi$ be a finite reduced crystallographic root system in a finite-dimensional positive-definite real inner-product space. Suppose every $\alpha\in\Phi$ has $\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle=2$, the rank of $\Phi$ is at most $16$, and $|\Phi|=480$. Then $\Phi$ has rank $16$ and is isomorphic to exactly one of the two root systems $D_{16}$ and $E_8\oplus E_8$. The integer lattice generated by its roots is isometric to the standard $D_{16}$ lattice or the standard $E_8\oplus E_8$ lattice, respectively.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite root set $\Phi$ in a positive-definite real inner-product space, with every root of squared norm $2$ and with rank at most $16$.

[F1] A reduced crystallographic root system is a finite spanning set of nonzero vectors closed under its root reflections, with integral Cartan integers and no scalar multiples other than opposite roots ([[def-reduced-crystallographic-euclidean-root-system]]).

[F2] The connected components of the nonorthogonality graph of a reduced crystallographic root system are its unique irreducible components ([[prop-root-systems-decompose-uniquely-into-irreducible-components]]).

[F3] Every nonempty irreducible reduced crystallographic root system is isomorphic to exactly one of $A_n$ ($n\ge1$), $B_n$ ($n\ge2$), $C_n$ ($n\ge3$), $D_n$ ($n\ge4$), $E_6,E_7,E_8,F_4$, or $G_2$; the low-rank identifications include $B_1=C_1=A_1$, $B_2=C_2$, $D_3=A_3$, and $D_2=A_1\sqcup A_1$. The subscript is the rank. A base is a basis of simple roots in which every root has integral coordinates all of one sign. Its Dynkin diagram has one vertex per simple root and $a_{ij}a_{ji}$ edges between vertices $i,j$, where $a_{ij}=2\langle\alpha_j,\alpha_i\rangle/\langle\alpha_i,\alpha_i\rangle$ is the Cartan integer; a multiple-edge arrow points to the shorter root. Among the listed types, the diagrams with only single edges are $A_n$ (a path on $n$ vertices), $D_n$ for $n\ge4$ (a trivalent diagram with arms of lengths $1,1,n-3$), and $E_6,E_7,E_8$ (trivalent diagrams with arms $(1,2,2)$, $(1,2,3)$, $(1,2,4)$, respectively). Thus a rank-8 irreducible system with only single-edge diagrams has type $A_8$, $D_8$, or $E_8$ ([[thm-classification-of-irreducible-reduced-crystallographic-root-systems]]).

[F4] The rank is the dimension of the real span of the roots. A root-system isomorphism is a linear isomorphism carrying the root set onto the target root set and preserving every Cartan integer ([[def-rank-and-isomorphism-of-root-systems]]).

[F5] The standard coordinate set is $E_8=D_8\cup(D_8+h_8)$, where $D_8=\{z\in\mathbb Z^8:\sum_i z_i\in2\mathbb Z\}$ and $h_8=(1/2,\ldots,1/2)$; $E_8$ is an even, integral, full lattice ([[def-e8-and-d16plus-rank16-lattices]], [[lem-e8-d16plus-are-even-unimodular]]).

[L1] The **root lattice** of a finite root set $\Psi$ is $Q(\Psi):=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Z}\Psi$, the additive subgroup of all finite integer combinations of its roots.

[L2] In the coordinates of [F5], let $\Phi_8:=\{x\in E_8:\|x\|^2=2\}$, $W_7:=\{x\in\mathbb R^8:x_7+x_8=0\}$, $W_6:=\{x\in\mathbb R^8:x_6+x_8=0,\ x_7+x_8=0\}$, and $\Phi_j:=\Phi_8\cap W_j$ for $j=6,7$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The hypothesis gives $\langle\gamma,\gamma\rangle=2$ for every root $\gamma$, so for roots $\alpha,\beta$ the Cartan integer $2\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle/\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle$ equals $\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle\in\mathbb Z$ by [F1]. For nonparallel roots, $\alpha+\beta$ and $\alpha-\beta$ are nonzero, so positive definiteness and bilinearity give $0<\langle\alpha\pm\beta,\alpha\pm\beta\rangle=4\pm2\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle$; hence $-2<\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle<2$. This integer is therefore $-1$, $0$, or $1$. In any base, distinct simple roots are nonparallel and each has self-inner-product $2$, so their Cartan integers are both zero or both equal to the same $1$ or $-1$; their product is therefore $0$ or $1$, and the diagram has only single edges. By [F3], every irreducible component has type $A_n$, $D_n$, $E_6$, $E_7$, or $E_8$. [F1, F3, algebra]

1.2 For $n\ge1$, in $H_n:=\{x\in\mathbb R^{n+1}:\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_i=0\}$ define $\Psi_{A_n}:=\{e_i-e_j:i\ne j\}$, where $e_1,\ldots,e_{n+1}$ is the standard basis of $\mathbb R^{n+1}$. For $n\ge4$, in $\mathbb R^n$ define $\Psi_{D_n}:=\{\pm e_i\pm e_j:i<j\}$. In each set all roots have squared norm $2$, pairings are integers, and the only roots on the line through a root $\alpha$ are $\pm\alpha$. For $\Psi_{A_n}$, reflection in $e_i-e_j$ exchanges coordinates $i,j$. For $\Psi_{D_n}$, reflection in $e_i-e_j$ exchanges those coordinates and reflection in $e_i+e_j$ exchanges and negates them. These maps preserve the respective root sets, so both are reduced crystallographic root systems. The vectors $\alpha_i=e_i-e_{i+1}$ for $1\le i\le n$ form a basis of $H_n$ for $\Psi_{A_n}$: in a zero linear combination, coordinates $1,\ldots,n$ successively force each coefficient to vanish. Every root $e_i-e_j$ with $i<j$ is $\alpha_i+\cdots+\alpha_{j-1}$. For $\Psi_{D_n}$ take $\alpha_i=e_i-e_{i+1}$ for $1\le i<n$ and $\alpha_n=e_{n-1}+e_n$; coordinates $1,\ldots,n-2$ in a zero linear combination force the first $n-2$ coefficients to vanish, and coordinates $n-1,n$ then force the last two to vanish. These $n$ independent vectors form a basis of $\mathbb R^n$. For $i<j$, $e_i-e_j=\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}\alpha_k$; for $i<j<n$, $e_i+e_j=\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}\alpha_k+2\sum_{k=j}^{n-2}\alpha_k+\alpha_{n-1}+\alpha_n$; and for $i<n$, $e_i+e_n=\sum_{k=i}^{n-2}\alpha_k+\alpha_n$, with empty sums taken as zero. Thus in both models every root has one sign of integral simple-root coordinates. Distinct basis roots in $\Psi_{A_n}$ have inner product $-1$ for consecutive indices and $0$ otherwise. In $\Psi_{D_n}$ the inner product is $-1$ for consecutive indices $1\le i\le n-3$ and for the pairs $(n-2,n-1)$ and $(n-2,n)$, and is $0$ for every other distinct pair. Thus the diagrams are a path for $\Psi_{A_n}$ and the trivalent diagram with arms of lengths $1,1,n-3$ for $\Psi_{D_n}$; both diagrams are connected. Because each simple-root basis is connected and spans its space, every root is nonorthogonal to at least one basis root, so the nonorthogonality graph is connected and both systems are irreducible by [F2]. By [F3], these coordinate systems realize the types $A_n$ and $D_n$ used in its classification; their root counts are therefore the counts of those types, since a root-system isomorphism is a bijection on root sets [F4]. The counts are $|A_n|=2\binom{n+1}{2}=n(n+1)$ and $|D_n|=4\binom n2=2n(n-1)$. The $D_n$ roots generate $D_n=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:\sum_i z_i\in2\mathbb Z\}$: for $z\in D_n$, the coefficients $c_i=\sum_{j\le i}z_j$ for $i\le n-2$, $c_n=(\sum_{j\le n}z_j)/2$, and $c_{n-1}=c_n-z_n$ are integers and give $z=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}c_i(e_i-e_{i+1})+c_n(e_{n-1}+e_n)$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, L1, algebra]

2.1 For systems whose components have only types $A,D$, set $g(1)=2$, $g(2)=6$, $g(3)=12$, and $g(r)=2r(r-1)$ for $r\ge4$. Step 1.2 shows that every component of rank $r$ has at most $g(r)$ roots: for $r\le3$ the listed type is $A_r$ using the low-rank identifications in [F3], and for $r\ge4$ the $A_r$ count $r(r+1)$ is at most the $D_r$ count $2r(r-1)$. The function $g$ is increasing and strictly superadditive. For $a,b\ge4$, $g(a+b)-g(a)-g(b)=4ab>0$. By symmetry take $a\le b$; the small cases $a,b\le3$ are $g(2)>2g(1)$, $g(3)>g(1)+g(2)$, $g(4)>g(1)+g(3)$, $g(4)>2g(2)$, $g(5)>g(2)+g(3)$, and $g(6)>2g(3)$. For $a=1,2,3$ and $b\ge4$, the differences are respectively $4b-2$, $8b-2$, and $12b$, all positive. Consequently, components of total rank $s$ with types only $A,D$ have at most $g(s)$ roots, with strict inequality if there is more than one component. [step 1.2, algebra]

2.2 In $\Phi_8$ the integer-coordinate roots are precisely $\pm e_i\pm e_j$ and number $4\binom82=112$. A half-integer vector has norm squared at least $8/4=2$, with equality exactly when every coordinate is $\pm1/2$; the sum-even condition permits exactly the sign patterns with an even number of minus signs, hence $2^7=128$ roots. Thus $|\Phi_8|=240$. The roots $e_i-e_{i+1}$ for $1\le i\le7$ and $e_7+e_8$ are linearly independent: in a zero linear combination, coordinates $1$ through $6$ successively force the first six coefficients to vanish, and coordinates $7,8$ then force the last two to vanish. Thus they form a basis of $\mathbb R^8$. Because $E_8$ is integral by [F5], for norm-2 roots $\alpha,\beta$ the integer $\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle$ is integral and reflection sends $\beta$ to $\beta-\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle\alpha\in E_8$ while preserving norm; the resulting vector is again in $\Phi_8$. All roots are nonzero and have the same norm, so the set is reduced. It is finite by the coordinate count, hence is a reduced crystallographic root system. The eight displayed basis roots have a connected nonorthogonality graph: consecutive differences pair to $-1$, and $e_7+e_8$ pairs to $-1$ with $e_6-e_7$. Every root is nonorthogonal to at least one of these basis roots, since they span $\mathbb R^8$ and every root is nonzero. Therefore the full nonorthogonality graph is connected, and [F2] makes $\Phi_8$ irreducible. Its rank is $8$; the irreducible types of rank $8$ with only single-edge diagrams other than $E_8$ are $A_8$ and $D_8$, whose root counts $72$ and $112$ from step 1.2 are smaller than $240$, so [F3] identifies $\Phi_8$ as type $E_8$. Finally $D_8\subseteq Q(\Phi_8)$ and $h_8\in\Phi_8$, so $Q(\Phi_8)=D_8\cup(D_8+h_8)=E_8$. [F2, F3, F5, step 1.2, L1, L2, algebra]

3.1 The seven roots $u_1=e_1-e_2,u_2=e_2-e_3,u_3=e_3-e_4,u_4=e_4-e_5,u_5=e_5-e_6,u_6=e_7-e_8$ and $v:=(-e_1+e_2+e_3+e_4+e_5+e_6+e_7-e_8)/2$ lie in $\Phi_7$. They are independent: summing coordinates $1$ through $6$ in a zero linear relation kills all $u_i$ terms and gives $2$ times the coefficient of $v$, so that coefficient is zero; coordinates $1$ through $5$ then successively force the coefficients of $u_1,\dots,u_5$ to vanish, and coordinate $7$ forces the coefficient of $u_6$ to vanish. The six roots $w_1=e_1-e_2,w_2=e_2-e_3,w_3=e_3-e_4,w_4=e_4-e_5,w_5=e_1+e_2$ and $v$ lie in $\Phi_6$. In a zero linear relation, coordinate $6$ forces the coefficient of $v$ to vanish; then $w_1,\dots,w_4$ are independent and span the sum-zero subspace of the first five coordinates, while $w_5$ has coordinate sum $2$, so all five remaining coefficients vanish. These independent sets span $W_7$ and $W_6$, respectively. Each $\Phi_j$ is finite, consists of nonzero equal-length roots, inherits integrality and reducedness from $\Phi_8$, and spans $W_j$. If $\alpha,\beta\in\Phi_j$, reflection $s_\alpha\beta$ remains in $\Phi_8$ by step 2.2 and in $W_j$ because $\alpha$ is orthogonal to every normal vector defining $W_j$; hence each is a root system. Counting in the $112$ integer and $128$ half-integer roots gives $|\Phi_7|=62+64=126$ (integer roots are supported in the first six coordinates or on the pair $7,8$ with opposite signs; half-roots have opposite signs in positions $7,8$ and an odd number of minus signs among the first six) and $|\Phi_6|=40+32=72$ (integer roots are supported in the first five coordinates; half-roots have $x_6=x_7=-x_8$ and the first-five sign parity is then forced). [F1, L2, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 The rank-6 system $\Phi_6$ cannot have only $A$ or $D$ components, because step 2.1 bounds these by $g(6)=60$ roots; it has no component of rank greater than $6$, so its $72$ roots force one irreducible $E_6$ component occupying all six ranks. If the rank-7 system $\Phi_7$ had no $E_7$ component, it would have at most $g(7)=84$ roots from $A,D$ components or $72+g(1)=74$ roots if it contained $E_6$, both below $126$; hence it is type $E_7$. Thus the coordinate root counts of types $E_6,E_7,E_8$ are $72,126,240$. [F2, F3, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 By [F2], $\Phi$ is the orthogonal union of its irreducible components, each with a diagram having only single edges. If all components have type $A$ or $D$, step 2.1 gives at most $g(16)=480$ roots, with equality only for a single $D_{16}$ component. If exactly one exceptional component occurs, the maximum totals including all remaining $A,D$ components are $72+g(10)=252$ for $E_6$, $126+g(9)=270$ for $E_7$, and $240+g(8)=352$ for $E_8$. With two exceptional components the maximal possibilities are $E_6+E_6$ with $144+g(4)=168$, $E_6+E_7$ with $198+g(3)=210$, $E_6+E_8$ with $312+g(2)=318$, $E_7+E_7$ with $252+g(2)=258$, $E_7+E_8$ with $366+g(1)=368$, and $E_8+E_8$ with $480$ and no remaining rank; three exceptional components exceed rank $16$. Thus $480$ roots occur only for $D_{16}$ or $E_8\oplus E_8$, and both have rank $16$. [F2, F3, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 In the $D_{16}$ case, the standard roots $\pm e_i\pm e_j$ generate the even-coordinate-sum lattice $D_{16}$ by step 1.2. In the $E_8\oplus E_8$ case, the orthogonal root components each generate $E_8$ by step 2.2, so their root lattice is $E_8\oplus E_8$. A root-system isomorphism preserves integer spans and, because all roots have norm squared $2$, preserves their inner products; therefore the root lattice $Q(\Phi)$ is isometric to the corresponding standard coordinate lattice. The two root-system types are distinct because $D_{16}$ is irreducible while $E_8\oplus E_8$ has two irreducible components. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.2, step 2.2, step 5.1, L1] ∎
