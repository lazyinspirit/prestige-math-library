## Statement

Let $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)$ be the common probability space for the innovations and their independent replacement family (take a countable independent product extension if needed), and write $\mathbb E$ for expectation under $\mathbb P$.

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $H=L^2([0,1],\mathcal B,\lambda;\mathbb R)$, where $\mathcal B$ is generated by intervals with rational endpoints and $\lambda$ is Lebesgue measure restricted to $\mathcal B$. Let $(S,\mathcal S,\nu)$ be a probability space, let $(\delta_t)_{t\in\mathbb Z}$ be independent identically distributed measurable $S$-valued random elements with law $\nu$, and let $f:S^{\mathbb N_0}\to H$ be measurable from the product sigma-algebra $\mathcal S^{\otimes\mathbb N_0}$ to the Borel sigma-algebra of $H$. Set $X_t=f(\delta_t,\delta_{t-1},\ldots)$ and assume its Bochner expectation is zero and $\mathbb E\|X_0\|_H^2<\infty$. For each $t\in\mathbb Z$, $m\ge1$, and $k\le t-m$, let $\delta'_{t,m,k}$ have law $\nu$, with these copies mutually independent and independent of the original innovations, and set $X_{t,m}=f(\delta_t,\ldots,\delta_{t-m+1},\delta'_{t,m,t-m},\delta'_{t,m,t-m-1},\ldots)$. Assume $\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\delta_m<\infty$, where $\delta_m=(\mathbb E\|X_0-X_{0,m}\|_H^2)^{1/2}$. For each $n\ge1$, let the integer block length $b_n$ satisfy $b_n\to\infty$ and $b_n/\sqrt n\to0$; for all sufficiently large $n$, apply moving-block resampling with block length $b_n$ to $X_1,\ldots,X_n$, truncating the last block to exactly $n$ elements. Write $\bar X_n^*=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^nX_j^*$, and let $\mathbb E^*$ denote expectation over these draws with the observations fixed. For a finite-support $H$-valued resampling random element $Z$, define its conditional covariance operator by

$$\operatorname{Cov}^*(Z):=\mathbb E^*\bigl[(Z-\mathbb E^*Z)\otimes(Z-\mathbb E^*Z)\bigr]\in\mathcal B(H),\qquad (u\otimes v)h:=\langle h,v\rangle_Hu\quad(h,u,v\in H).$$

For a finite-support real-valued resampling random variable $V$, define $\operatorname{Var}^*(V):=\mathbb E^*[(V-\mathbb E^*V)^2]$. Each convergence-in-probability assertion below is with respect to $\mathbb P$.

Write $\mathcal L^*(Z)$ for the law of $Z$ under the same conditional resampling distribution. Let $G$ be the centered $H$-valued Gaussian limit with covariance operator $\Gamma$ supplied by the Hilbert-valued sample-mean CLT for this process. For any bounded real-linear $L:H\to\mathbb R$, set $\|L\|=\sup_{\|u\|_H\le1}|L(u)|$, let $h_L\in H$ be the unique vector satisfying $L(u)=\langle u,h_L\rangle_H$ for every $u\in H$, and define $L^*r=rh_L$. Identify the scalar operator $L\Gamma L^*:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ with its value at $1$. Then
$$\mathcal L^*\!\left(\sqrt n\,L(\bar X_n^*-\mathbb E^*\bar X_n^*)\right)\Rightarrow\mathcal L(LG)\quad\text{in probability},$$
and, with $C_n=n\operatorname{Cov}^*(\bar X_n^*)$,
$$\left|\operatorname{Var}^*\!\left(\sqrt n\,L(\bar X_n^*-\mathbb E^*\bar X_n^*)\right)-L\Gamma L^*\right|\le\|L\|^2\|C_n-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\longrightarrow0\quad\text{in probability}.$$
Here $L\Gamma L^*$ is the variance of the limiting scalar Gaussian. This conclusion concerns bounded linear profile observations under the stated Bernoulli-shift and block-length hypotheses; it makes no claim for nonlinear statistics or for finite-replicate quantile accuracy.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The statement assumptions, including the Axiom of Choice and the exact block-length regime.

[A1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: every family of nonempty sets has a choice function; it is the stated assumption of the moving-block theorem below.

[F1] [[def-fd-fluid-profile-bootstrap-interface]]: $H=L^2([0,1],\mathcal B,\lambda;\mathbb R)$ is the real Hilbert space of almost-everywhere classes; the measurable causal process has Bochner mean $\mathbb E_BX_0:=\int_\Omega X_0\,d\mathbb P=0$ and finite second moment; its replacement family lies on an independent product extension whose stated setup assumes Countable Choice and Dependent Choice, with $\delta_m=(\mathbb E\|X_0-X_{0,m}\|_H^2)^{1/2}$ and $\sum_{m\ge1}\delta_m<\infty$; MBB uses independent uniform overlapping starts, preserves within-block order, truncates to length $n$, and is centered by its actual conditional expectation.

[F2] [[thm-moving-block-bootstrap-clt-for-dependent-data]]: under AC, centered causal $L^2$-$m$-approximable $H$-valued data and $b_n\to\infty,\ b_n/\sqrt n\to0$, the conditional law of $\sqrt n(\bar X_n^*-\mathbb E^*\bar X_n^*)$ converges weakly in probability to $\mathcal L(G)$, and $\|n\operatorname{Cov}^*(\bar X_n^*)-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\to0$ in probability.

[F3] [[thm-hilbert-valued-clt-for-l2-m-approximable-bernoulli-shifts]]: the sampling-law limit $G$ is centered Gaussian in $H$, with covariance $\Gamma$; the result is a sample-sum CLT, not a time-indexed functional CLT.

[F4] [[def-conditional-weak-convergence-in-probability]]: convergence in conditional weak topology in probability means convergence in probability in a metric that metrizes weak convergence. [[def-weak-convergence-of-borel-probability-measures]] characterizes weak convergence by bounded continuous test functions.

[F5] [[thm-riesz-representation-for-hilbert-space]]: under Countable Choice, every bounded real-linear functional on a real Hilbert space has a unique representing vector $h_L$, with $\|h_L\|_H=\|L\|$; thus $L^*r=rh_L$ and $\|L^*\|=\|L\|$.

[F6] [[def-l2-m-approximable-causal-bernoulli-shift]] supplies separability of $H$ under Countable Choice, and [[thm-separable-hilbert-space-has-a-countable-orthonormal-basis]] turns a fixed countable dense sequence into a finite or countable orthonormal basis.

[F7] [[def-hilbert-schmidt-operator]] defines the Hilbert-Schmidt norm relative to a supplied Hilbert basis; [[thm-hilbert-schmidt-norm-is-basis-independent]] makes its unqualified value independent of that basis; and [[thm-hilbert-schmidt-operators-form-a-two-sided-ideal]] gives $\|ATB\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le\|A\|\,\|T\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\,\|B\|$ for bounded $A,B$. On $\mathbb R$, the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of a scalar operator is its absolute value.

[F8] [[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]]: in ZF, the Axiom of Choice implies Dependent Choice and then Countable Choice, with Dependent Choice formulated using a prescribed initial point. Together with [A1], this supplies the Countable Choice and Dependent Choice assumptions in [F1], and the Countable Choice assumptions in [F5] and [F6].

[F9] [[lem-moving-block-bootstrap-conditional-covariance]] uses the conditional covariance operator and rank-one convention stated above and proves the exact finite-sample MBB covariance identity for fixed Hilbert-valued observations.

## Proof

**Given:** The assumptions and supplier facts above.

1.1 By [A1] and [F8], AC yields Countable Choice and Dependent Choice. By [F1] and [F6], $H$ is separable complete. Put $\mu_n^*=\mathcal L^*(\sqrt n(\bar X_n^*-\mathbb E^*\bar X_n^*))$ and $\mu=\mathcal L(G)$, and let $d_H$ be the metric on Borel probability laws on $H$ used in [F2] under [F4]. Thus $d_H$ metrizes weak convergence and $d_H(\mu_n^*,\mu)\to0$ in probability. For a continuous map $T:E\to F$ between metric spaces and a Borel law $\eta$ on $E$, write $T_\#\eta(B)=\eta(T^{-1}(B))$ for Borel $B\subseteq F$. Composing bounded continuous tests with $T$ in [F4] shows that $\eta_j\Rightarrow\eta$ implies $T_\#\eta_j\Rightarrow T_\#\eta$. [A1, F1, F2, F4, F6, F8, given]

2.1 To supply the scalar-law metric required by [F4], let $e=[\xi\mapsto1]\in H$, $J:\mathbb R\to H$, $J(r)=re$, and $P:H\to\mathbb R$, $P(u)=\langle u,e\rangle_H$. Here $\|e\|_H=1$, $J$ is an isometry, and $P$ is continuous: expanding $\|u-P(u)e\|_H^2\ge0$ gives $|P(u)|\le\|u\|_H$. Also $PJ$ is the identity. For Borel probability laws $\alpha,\beta$ on $\mathbb R$, define $d_{\mathbb R}(\alpha,\beta)=d_H(J_\#\alpha,J_\#\beta)$. This is a metric: symmetry and the triangle inequality come from $d_H$, and $J_\#\alpha=J_\#\beta$ implies $\alpha=P_\#J_\#\alpha=P_\#J_\#\beta=\beta$. By step 1.1 applied to $J$ and $P$, $\alpha_j\Rightarrow\alpha$ if and only if $J_\#\alpha_j\Rightarrow J_\#\alpha$; hence $d_{\mathbb R}$ metrizes scalar weak convergence. [F1, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Step 1.1 applied to $L$ shows that $L_\#$ is sequentially continuous for $d_H$ and $d_{\mathbb R}$, hence continuous: otherwise a sequence within $1/j$ of a point would have images outside one fixed positive-radius ball. Thus for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $\delta>0$ such that $d_H(\eta,\mu)<\delta$ implies $d_{\mathbb R}(L_\#\eta,L_\#\mu)<\varepsilon$, and $\mathbb P(d_{\mathbb R}(L_\#\mu_n^*,L_\#\mu)\ge\varepsilon)\le\mathbb P(d_H(\mu_n^*,\mu)\ge\delta)\to0$. The scalar metric distance is measurable: for fixed $n$ the finitely many block-start tuples have fixed weights and atoms continuous in the data $(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\in H^n$; their law is weakly continuous by bounded continuous tests, hence continuous for $d_{\mathbb R}$. Conditional expectation is the corresponding finite weighted sum, so it commutes with $L$. Therefore $L_\#\mu_n^*$ is the displayed conditional scalar law and $L_\#\mu=\mathcal L(LG)$, proving the first conclusion in precisely the sense of [F4]. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 By [A1] and [F8], the assumed AC supplies Countable Choice and Dependent Choice. Then [F5] gives the Riesz representative, while [F1] and [F6] give separability of $H$ and a countable orthonormal basis; use $\{1\}$ as the basis of $\mathbb R$. Put $T_n=C_n-\Gamma$ and $Z_n^*=\sqrt n(\bar X_n^*-\mathbb E^*\bar X_n^*)$. By the covariance definition in the Statement, $\operatorname{Cov}^*(Z_n^*)=C_n$, since centering and scaling by $\sqrt n$ multiplies covariance by $n$. The Riesz identity and the same conditional covariance convention as [F9] give $\operatorname{Var}^*(L(Z_n^*))=\mathbb E^*\langle Z_n^*,h_L\rangle_H^2=\langle C_nh_L,h_L\rangle_H=(LC_nL^*)(1)$. Apply [F7] to $A=L$, $T=T_n$, and $B=L^*$: $|LT_nL^*(1)|=\|LT_nL^*\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le\|L\|\,\|T_n\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\,\|L^*\|=\|L\|^2\|T_n\|_{\mathrm{HS}}$. By [F2] this bound tends to zero in probability. The covariance of $LG$ is $L\Gamma L^*$ by [F3], proving the variance conclusion, including the zero-variance case. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
