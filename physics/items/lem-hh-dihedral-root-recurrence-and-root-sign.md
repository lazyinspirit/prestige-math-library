## Statement

Let $(S,m)$ be a finite Coxeter matrix, and let $K$, $E$, $(\alpha_s)_{s\in S}$, the constants $c_{st}$ and the linear maps $\sigma_s$ be as in [[def-hh-geometric-coxeter-representation-and-roots]]; let $W$ and $\ell$ be as in [[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]. The involution, representation, signed-action and word assertions below hold for every finite $S$, including $S=\varnothing$ and $\#S=1$. Only the rank-two assertions require distinct generators.

1. **Involutions.** $\sigma_s^2=\mathrm{id}_E$ for every $s\in S$; each $\sigma_s$ is invertible, and $\ell(s)=1$.
2. **The rank-two block.** For any distinct $s,t\in S$, put $m:=m(s,t)$, $c:=c_{st}$, $P:=\operatorname{span}(\alpha_s,\alpha_t)\subseteq E$, $A:=\sigma_s\sigma_t$ and $B:=A|_P$. Then $A(P)\subseteq P$, and in the basis $(\alpha_s,\alpha_t)$,
$$B(\alpha_s)=(c^2-1)\alpha_s+c\,\alpha_t,\qquad B(\alpha_t)=-c\,\alpha_s-\alpha_t;\qquad \operatorname{tr}(B)=c^2-2,\qquad \det(B)=1 .$$
Moreover $A(v)-v\in P$ for every $v\in E$. Consequently $E=P\oplus Q$ with $Q:=\operatorname{span}\{\alpha_u:u\in S,\ u\notin\{s,t\}\}$, and for every $v=p+q$ with $p\in P$, $q\in Q$ and every $k\ge1$,
$$A^k(p+q)=B^k p+\bigl(\mathrm{id}_P+B+\cdots+B^{k-1}\bigr)\bigl(A(q)-q\bigr)+q .$$
3. **Exact order of the rank-two product.** For any distinct $s,t$ and the notation of (2), when $m<\infty$ put $\zeta:=\zeta_{st}$, of order $2m$, so $c=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}$; when $m=\infty$ use $c=2$, with no $\zeta$ stipulated. Then:
   (a) if $2<m<\infty$, the characteristic polynomial of $B$ is $X^2-(c^2-2)X+1=(X-\zeta^2)(X-\zeta^{-2})$ with distinct roots; $B$ is diagonalisable, $B^m=\mathrm{id}_P$, $\mathrm{id}_P+B+\cdots+B^{m-1}=0$, and $B^k\ne\mathrm{id}_P$ for $0<k<m$;
   (b) if $m=2$, then $c=0$ and $B=-\mathrm{id}_P$;
   (c) if $m=\infty$, then $c=2$, $B\ne\mathrm{id}_P$, $(B-\mathrm{id}_P)^2=0$, and $B^k\ne\mathrm{id}_P$ for every $k\ge1$.
   Hence $\sigma_s\sigma_t=A$ has order exactly $m$ when $m<\infty$, and infinite order when $m=\infty$; in particular $A^m=\mathrm{id}_E$ and $A^k\ne\mathrm{id}_E$ for $0<k<m$ in the finite case.
4. **The representation and the exact dihedral orders.** The assignment $s\mapsto\sigma_s$ respects every defining relator of $W$ and induces a unique homomorphism $\sigma:W\to\mathrm{GL}_K(E)$ with $\sigma(s)=\sigma_s$. Consequently, for any distinct $s,t\in S$, one has $s\ne t$ in $W$ and $st$ has order exactly $m(s,t)$ in $W$ (infinite when $m(s,t)=\infty$).
5. **The signed reflection action.** Let $T:=\{wsw^{-1}:w\in W,\ s\in S\}\subseteq W$. For $s\in S$ define $U_s:\{\pm1\}\times T\to\{\pm1\}\times T$ by
$$U_s(\varepsilon,r):=\bigl(\varepsilon\cdot(-1)^{\delta(s,r)},\ srs\bigr),$$
where $\delta(s,r)=1$ if $r=s$ and $\delta(s,r)=0$ otherwise. Then $U_s^2=\mathrm{id}$ for every $s$, and the assignment $(\varepsilon,r)\cdot s:=U_s(\varepsilon,r)$ extends to a well-defined **right action** of $W$ on $\{\pm1\}\times T$ (so $(\varepsilon,r)\cdot1=(\varepsilon,r)$ and $(\varepsilon,r)\cdot(wv)=((\varepsilon,r)\cdot w)\cdot v$). Indeed the only relations to check are $s^2=1$ and, for distinct $s,t$ with $m=m(s,t)<\infty$, $(st)^m=1$: $U_s^2=\mathrm{id}$ because for $r=s$ the two signs cancel while for $r\ne s$ one has $srs\ne s$ and $U_s(\varepsilon,srs)=(\varepsilon,r)$; and the alternating word $s\,t\,s\,t\cdots$ of length $2m$ acts trivially because its $2m$ prefix reflections $r_i:=w_{i-1}s_iw_{i-1}^{-1}$ (with $w_j:=s_1\cdots s_j$) satisfy $r_{i+m}=r_i$ for $1\le i\le m$ and $r_1,\dots,r_m$ are pairwise distinct, so each reflection of the dihedral subgroup $\langle s,t\rangle$ occurs exactly twice and every accumulated sign is even while the conjugating coordinate returns to $r$ because $(st)^m=1$.
6. **Prefix reflections, deletion and expression independence.** Let $(s_1,\dots,s_k)$ be a word in $S$ with value $w=s_1\cdots s_k$ and prefix reflections $r_i=s_1\cdots s_{i-1}s_is_{i-1}\cdots s_1$, and let $n(r):=\#\{i:r_i=r\}$.
   (a) If $r_i=r_j$ for some $i<j$, then $s_1\cdots\widehat{s_i}\cdots\widehat{s_j}\cdots s_k=w$: the two letters $s_i,s_j$ can be deleted.
   (b) $(-1)^{n(r)}=:\eta(r,w)$ depends only on $w$ and $r$, and the right action satisfies $(\varepsilon,r)\cdot w=(\varepsilon\,\eta(r,w),\,w^{-1}rw)$.
   (c) If the word is reduced ($k=\ell(w)$), then $n(r)\in\{0,1\}$ for every $r$, the map $i\mapsto r_i$ is injective, and the set $\Phi(w):=\{r_1,\dots,r_k\}=\{r\in T:\eta(r,w)=-1\}$ is independent of the reduced expression chosen, with $\#\Phi(w)=\ell(w)$.
7. **Ambient reducedness of dihedral words.** Let $s\ne t$, $m:=m(s,t)$, and for $q\ge1$ let $w_q$ be the value of the alternating word of length $q$ beginning with $s$. If $m<\infty$ and $q\le m$, or if $m=\infty$ and $q\ge1$, then $\ell(w_q)=q$, every word in $S$ representing $w_q$ has length at least $q$, and $w_1,\dots,w_q$ are pairwise distinct. Thus dihedral alternating words are reduced in the ambient group $W$, not merely inside $\langle s,t\rangle$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite Coxeter matrix $(S,m)$, the construction $K,E,(\alpha_s),\sigma_s$ of [[def-hh-geometric-coxeter-representation-and-roots]], the group $W$ of [[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]], with no assumption on $\#S$; distinct $s,t\in S$ are fixed only for the rank-two assertions.

[F1] [[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]: $W=F(S)/N$ is the group presented by the Coxeter matrix, with $N$ the normal closure of $R=\{s^2:s\in S\}\cup\{(st)^{m(s,t)}:s\ne t,\ m(s,t)<\infty\}$; for every group $G$ and every map $f:S\to G$ with $f(s)^2=1$ for all $s\in S$ and $(f(s)f(t))^{m(s,t)}=1$ for all $s\ne t$ with $m(s,t)<\infty$, there is a unique homomorphism $\varphi:W\to G$ with $\varphi(s)=f(s)$ for all $s\in S$. The length $\ell(w)$ is the least $k$ with $w=s_1\cdots s_k$ for some $s_1,\dots,s_k\in S$, and $\ell(1)=0$.

[F2] [[def-hh-geometric-coxeter-representation-and-roots]]: $K$ is a field of characteristic $0$; $E$ is the $K$-vector space with basis $(\alpha_s)_{s\in S}$; the map $\sigma_s$ is the unique $K$-linear map with $\sigma_s(\alpha_s)=-\alpha_s$ and $\sigma_s(\alpha_t)=\alpha_t+c_{st}\alpha_s$ for $t\ne s$; and for distinct $s,t$ with $m(s,t)<\infty$ the constant is $c_{st}=\zeta_{st}+\zeta_{st}^{-1}$ with $\zeta_{st}$ a primitive $2m(s,t)$-th root of unity, while $c_{st}=2$ when $m(s,t)=\infty$.

[F3] [[def-linear-basis]], [[def-linear-map]]: the basis vectors are linearly independent and span $E$; linear maps with the same values on that basis agree, so the primitive formulas in [F2] may be used to check every identity below on the basis.

[F4] For the distinct pair and its subspaces $P,Q$ in (2), [[def-internal-direct-sum]]: if a vector space is the sum of subspaces meeting only in $0$ it is their internal direct sum; here $P=\operatorname{span}(\alpha_s,\alpha_t)$ and $Q=\operatorname{span}\{\alpha_u:u\notin\{s,t\}\}$ complement each other because the basis $(\alpha_u)_{u\in S}$ splits into the two parts.

[F5] [[def-characteristic-polynomial-of-an-operator]], [[def-characteristic-polynomial-of-a-matrix]], [[lem-characteristic-polynomial-is-monic-and-has-extreme-coefficients]]: for an endomorphism of a two-dimensional space, represented by its matrix, $\chi(x)$ is monic of degree $2$ with second coefficient $-\operatorname{tr}$ and constant coefficient $+\det$, i.e. $\chi(x)=x^2-\operatorname{tr}(B)x+\det(B)$.

[F6] [[def-eigenvalue-eigenvector-eigenspace-and-spectrum]], [[cor-distinct-characteristic-roots-imply-diagonalisability]]: if the characteristic polynomial splits into distinct linear factors then the endomorphism is diagonalisable, and a polynomial in $B$ vanishes on $B$ as soon as it vanishes at every eigenvalue and $B$ is diagonalisable.

[F7] [[def-order-in-a-group]]: for $g$ in a group, $\operatorname{ord}(g)$ is the least $k\ge1$ with $g^k=1$ when such $k$ exist, $g^{\operatorname{ord}(g)}=1$ then, and $g$ has infinite order when $g^k\ne1$ for all $k\ge1$.

[F8] [[def-roots-of-unity-in-a-field]]: a primitive $n$-th root of unity is an element of order exactly $n$; in particular, for a distinct pair with finite $m=m(s,t)$, the fixed element $\zeta=\zeta_{st}$ satisfies $\zeta^{2m}=1$ and $\zeta^{j}\ne1$ for $0<j<2m$.

## Proof

**Given:** The data of the statement: the finite Coxeter matrix $(S,m)$, the field $K$, the space $E$ with basis $(\alpha_s)$, the maps $\sigma_s$ and constants $c_{st}$, the group $W$, with no assumption on $\#S$; a distinct pair is fixed only in steps 1.2–2.3 and the rank-two portions of steps 3.1, 4.1 and 7.1.

**Proof technique:** direct; all identities for $A$ and $B$ are checked on the basis $(\alpha_s,\alpha_t)$ of $P$ and the complementary basis of $Q$.

1.1 **Involutions and nonidentity for every generator.** Fix any $s\in S$. The primitive formulas of [F2] give $\sigma_s^2(\alpha_s)=\sigma_s(-\alpha_s)=\alpha_s$ and, for $u\ne s$, $\sigma_s^2(\alpha_u)=\sigma_s(\alpha_u+c_{su}\alpha_s)=\alpha_u+c_{su}\alpha_s-c_{su}\alpha_s=\alpha_u$. By [F3] the square is the identity on $E$, so $\sigma_s$ is invertible with inverse itself. Also $\sigma_s(\alpha_s)=-\alpha_s\ne\alpha_s$, since the basis vector $\alpha_s$ is nonzero and $\operatorname{char}K=0$; hence $\sigma_s\ne\mathrm{id}_E$. These computations require no second generator; when $S=\varnothing$, the assertions indexed by $s$ are vacuous. [F2, F3, algebra]

1.2 **The rank-two block.** Fix any distinct $s,t\in S$ and use the notation of (2). Since $\sigma_t(\alpha_s)=\alpha_s+c\,\alpha_t$ and $\sigma_t(\alpha_t)=-\alpha_t$ with $c=c_{st}$, linearity of $\sigma_s$ gives $A(\alpha_s)=\sigma_s(\alpha_s+c\,\alpha_t)=(c^2-1)\alpha_s+c\,\alpha_t$ and $A(\alpha_t)=\sigma_s(-\alpha_t)=-c\,\alpha_s-\alpha_t$, so $A(P)\subseteq P$ and the matrix of $B=A|_P$ in the basis $(\alpha_s,\alpha_t)$ is $\begin{pmatrix}c^2-1&-c\\ c&-1\end{pmatrix}$, whose trace is $(c^2-1)+(-1)=c^2-2$ and whose determinant is $(c^2-1)(-1)-(-c)c=1$. For $u\notin\{s,t\}$ one has $A(\alpha_u)=\sigma_s(\alpha_u+c_{tu}\alpha_t)=\alpha_u+(c_{su}+c_{tu}c)\alpha_s+c_{tu}\alpha_t$, so $A(\alpha_u)-\alpha_u\in P$; by linearity $A(v)-v\in P$ for every $v\in E$. [F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 **The two-block recursion.** The subspace $Q=\operatorname{span}\{\alpha_u:u\notin\{s,t\}\}$ satisfies $P\cap Q=\{0\}$ and $P+Q=E$ because the basis of $E$ splits into the basis $(\alpha_s,\alpha_t)$ of $P$ and the basis of $Q$; hence $E=P\oplus Q$, and every $v\in E$ has unique coordinates $v=p+q$ with $p\in P$, $q\in Q$. I claim that for every $k\ge1$ and all such $p,q$ one has $A^k(p+q)=B^k p+S_k\bigl(A(q)-q\bigr)+q$, where $S_k:=\mathrm{id}_P+B+\cdots+B^{k-1}$. For $k=1$ this reads $A(p)+A(q)=Bp+(A(q)-q)+q$, which holds because $A|_P=B$. If it holds for $k$, then $B^kp+S_k(A(q)-q)\in P$ and $A(q)-q\in P$ by step 1.2, so applying $A$ and using $A|_P=B$ gives $A^{k+1}(p+q)=B\bigl(B^kp+S_k(A(q)-q)\bigr)+A(q)=B^{k+1}p+\bigl(BS_k+\mathrm{id}_P\bigr)\bigl(A(q)-q\bigr)+q$, and $BS_k+\mathrm{id}_P=B+B^2+\cdots+B^k+\mathrm{id}_P=S_{k+1}$, which completes the induction. [F3, F4, step 1.2, algebra]

2.2 **The cases $m=2$ and $m=\infty$.** If $m=2$, then $\zeta$ has order $4$, so $\zeta^2=-1$ and $\zeta^{-1}=-\zeta$, whence $c=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}=0$; substituting into the matrix of step 1.2 leaves $B=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\ 0&-1\end{pmatrix}=-\mathrm{id}_P$, so $B^2=\mathrm{id}_P$ and $\mathrm{id}_P+B=0$. If $m=\infty$, then $c=2$ by the convention in [F2], and the matrix of step 1.2 gives $B=\mathrm{id}_P+M$ with $M=B-\mathrm{id}_P=\begin{pmatrix}2&-2\\ 2&-2\end{pmatrix}\ne0$ and $M^2=0$. The binomial expansion in the commutative algebra of endomorphisms then gives $B^k=(\mathrm{id}_P+M)^k=\mathrm{id}_P+kM$ for every $k\ge1$, and $kM\ne0$ because $M\ne0$ and $k\cdot1_K\ne0$ in the characteristic-zero field $K$; hence $B^k\ne\mathrm{id}_P$ for all $k\ge1$, and in particular $B\ne\mathrm{id}_P$. [F2, step 1.2, algebra]

2.3 **The finite case $2<m<\infty$.** By step 1.2 and [F5] the characteristic polynomial of $B$ is $\chi_B(x)=x^2-(c^2-2)x+1$; since $c=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}$ satisfies $c^2-2=\zeta^2+\zeta^{-2}$ and $\zeta^2\zeta^{-2}=1$, one has $\chi_B(x)=x^2-(\zeta^2+\zeta^{-2})x+1=(x-\zeta^2)(x-\zeta^{-2})$. The two roots are distinct: $\zeta^2=\zeta^{-2}$ would give $\zeta^4=1$, impossible because $\zeta$ has order $2m>4$. Hence the characteristic polynomial splits into distinct linear factors, and [F6] makes $B$ diagonalisable, with eigenvalues $\zeta^2$ and $\zeta^{-2}$. Consequently $B^m=\mathrm{id}_P$, because on an eigenvector for $\lambda\in\{\zeta^2,\zeta^{-2}\}$ the operator $B^m$ scales it by $\lambda^m=\zeta^{\pm2m}=1$ by [F7] and [F8]; the operator $S_m=\mathrm{id}_P+B+\cdots+B^{m-1}$ is zero, because the polynomial $1+x+\cdots+x^{m-1}$ vanishes at both eigenvalues, where $\zeta^{\pm2m}=1$ and $\zeta^{\pm2}\ne1$ since $0<2<2m$; and $B^k\ne\mathrm{id}_P$ for $0<k<m$, because on the eigenvector for $\zeta^2$ it scales by $\zeta^{2k}\ne1$, again since $0<2k<2m$ and $2m$ is the least positive exponent killing $\zeta$. [F5, F6, F7, F8, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 **Order of $A$, the relators, and the exact dihedral orders.** For any distinct pair $s,t$ with finite $m=m(s,t)$, step 2.1 gives $A^m(p+q)=B^mp+S_m(A(q)-q)+q=p+q$ for all $p,q$, using $B^m=\mathrm{id}_P$ and $S_m=0$ from steps 2.2 and 2.3; hence $A^m=\mathrm{id}_E$. If $0<k<m$, choose $p\in P$ with $B^kp\ne p$, which exists because $B^k\ne\mathrm{id}_P$; then $A^k(p)=B^kp\ne p$, so $A^k\ne\mathrm{id}_E$ and $A$ has order exactly $m$. For $m=\infty$, $B^k\ne\mathrm{id}_P$ for every $k\ge1$ gives $A^k\ne\mathrm{id}_E$ for every $k\ge1$, so $A$ has infinite order. Since this verifies each arbitrary distinct finite pair and step 1.1 proves $\sigma_s^2=\mathrm{id}_E$ for every generator, the map $f:S\to\mathrm{GL}_K(E)$, $f(s)=\sigma_s$, satisfies the hypotheses of the universal property in [F1], so there is a unique homomorphism $\sigma:W\to\mathrm{GL}_K(E)$ with $\sigma(s)=\sigma_s$ for all $s$. For $\#S\le1$ there are no distinct-pair relators, so the same universal property applies using only step 1.1; for $S=\varnothing$ it gives the unique homomorphism from the trivial group. For every $s\in S$, step 1.1 now gives $\sigma(s)\ne\mathrm{id}_E$, hence $s\ne1$ and $\ell(s)\ge1$ by [F1]; the one-letter expression gives $\ell(s)\le1$, so $\ell(s)=1$. If $s\ne t$ then $\sigma_s\ne\sigma_t$, because $\sigma_s(\alpha_s)=-\alpha_s$ while $\sigma_t(\alpha_s)=\alpha_s+c_{st}\alpha_t$ and $-2\alpha_s-c_{st}\alpha_t\ne0$ by linear independence of $\alpha_s,\alpha_t$ and $2\ne0$ in $K$; hence $s\ne t$ in $W$. Finally, $st$ has order exactly $m$ when $m<\infty$: writing $n$ for the order of $st$ in $W$, one has $n\le m$ because $(st)^m=1$ in $W$ and $n$ is least, and $m\le n$ because $A^n=\sigma((st)^n)=\mathrm{id}_E$ and $m$ is the least positive exponent killing $A$; hence $n=m$. When $m=\infty$, a positive exponent $k$ with $(st)^k=1$ would give $A^k=\mathrm{id}_E$, so no such exponent exists and $st$ has infinite order. [F1, F2, F7, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 2.3]

4.1 **The signed action.** First, $U_s$ is a bijection of $\{\pm1\}\times T$ with $U_s^2=\mathrm{id}$: for $r=s$ one has $U_s(\varepsilon,s)=(-\varepsilon,s)$ and applying $U_s$ again gives $(+\varepsilon,s)$, while for $r\ne s$ one has $srs\ne s$ — if $srs=s$ then multiplying on the left by $s$ gives $rs=1$ and hence $r=s^{-1}=s$ using $s^2=1$ in $W$ — so $\delta(s,srs)=0$ and the two applications of $U_s$ return $(\varepsilon,r)$ from $U_s(\varepsilon,srs)=(\varepsilon,r)$. Next, fix a word $(s_1,\dots,s_k)$ in $S$ with $w_j:=s_1\cdots s_j$ and prefix reflections $r_i:=w_{i-1}s_iw_{i-1}^{-1}$; applying $U_{s_1},U_{s_2},\dots,U_{s_k}$ successively to $(\varepsilon,r)$ returns $\bigl(\varepsilon(-1)^{n(r)},\,w_k^{-1}rw_k\bigr)$ with $n(r):=\#\{i:r_i=r\}$, by induction on $k$: the $i$-th step multiplies the sign by $(-1)^{\delta(s_i,\,w_{i-1}^{-1}rw_{i-1})}$, whose exponent is $1$ exactly when $r=w_{i-1}s_iw_{i-1}^{-1}=r_i$, and it conjugates the coordinate to $s_iw_{i-1}^{-1}rw_{i-1}s_i=(w_{i-1}s_i)^{-1}r(w_{i-1}s_i)$. Now fix any distinct $a,b\in S$ with $m=m(a,b)<\infty$; consider the alternating word $(a,b,a,b,\dots)$ of length $2m$ and write $r_i=(ab)^{i-1}a$ for $1\le i\le 2m$ for its prefix reflections. The closed form is proved by induction on $i$: the conjugating prefix is $w_{i-1}=(ab)^{(i-1)/2}$ for odd $i$ and $w_{i-1}=(ab)^{(i-2)/2}a$ for even $i$, and the identities $(ab)^{-j}=(ba)^j$ and $a(ba)^j=(ab)^ja$ rewrite $w_{i-1}s_iw_{i-1}^{-1}$ as $(ab)^{i-1}a$ in both cases. Hence $r_{i+m}=(ab)^{i-1}(ab)^ma=(ab)^{i-1}a=r_i$ for $1\le i\le m$, while $r_1,\dots,r_m$ are pairwise distinct: an equality $r_i=r_j$ with $i<j\le m$ gives $(ab)^{j-i}=1$ after right multiplication by $a$, contradicting $0<j-i<m$ and the fact from step 3.1 that $ab$ has order exactly $m$. Therefore the sequence of $2m$ prefix reflections is $r_1,\dots,r_m,r_1,\dots,r_m$, so $n(r)\in\{0,2\}$ for every $r\in T$ and every accumulated sign $(-1)^{n(r)}$ is $1$; the conjugating coordinate returns to $r$ because $w_{2m}=(ab)^m=1$. So the permutation effected by each alternating word of length $2m$ is the identity, which verifies $(U_aU_b)^m=\mathrm{id}$ and $(U_bU_a)^m=\mathrm{id}$ for this arbitrary finite pair. There are no distinct-pair relators when $\#S\le1$, so the same verification applies in those cases using only $U_s^2=\mathrm{id}$. By [F1]'s universal property applied to $f:S\to\operatorname{Sym}(\{\pm1\}\times T)$, $f(s)=U_s$, there is then a homomorphism $\rho:W\to\operatorname{Sym}(\{\pm1\}\times T)$ with $\rho(s)=U_s$ for all $s$. Define $(\varepsilon,r)\cdot w:=\rho(w^{-1})(\varepsilon,r)$; this is a right action, because $(wv)^{-1}=v^{-1}w^{-1}$ gives $(\varepsilon,r)\cdot(wv)=\rho(v^{-1})\bigl(\rho(w^{-1})(\varepsilon,r)\bigr)=((\varepsilon,r)\cdot w)\cdot v$, and $(\varepsilon,r)\cdot s=\rho(s)^{-1}(\varepsilon,r)=U_s(\varepsilon,r)$. [F1, F7, step 3.1]

5.1 **Deletion.** Suppose $r_i=r_j$ for some $i<j$, that is $w_{i-1}s_iw_{i-1}^{-1}=w_{j-1}s_jw_{j-1}^{-1}$. Multiplying on the left by $w_{i-1}^{-1}$ and on the right by $w_{j-1}$ and using $w_{i-1}^{-1}w_{j-1}=s_i s_{i+1}\cdots s_{j-1}$ gives $s_i\,s_i s_{i+1}\cdots s_{j-1}=s_i s_{i+1}\cdots s_{j-1}s_j$, hence $s_i s_{i+1}\cdots s_j=s_{i+1}\cdots s_{j-1}$. Substituting this into the word $(s_1,\dots,s_k)$ shows that deleting the two letters $s_i$ and $s_j$ leaves the value unchanged: $s_1\cdots\widehat{s_i}\cdots\widehat{s_j}\cdots s_k=w$. [step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 **Expression independence and reduced words.** For a word of $w$ the sign accumulated in step 4.1 is $(-1)^{n(r)}$, and the right action $(\varepsilon,r)\cdot w$ is independent of the word; comparing with the displayed formula for two words of $w$ shows that $(-1)^{n(r)}=\eta(r,w)$ depends only on $w$ and $r$, and that $(\varepsilon,r)\cdot w=(\varepsilon\,\eta(r,w),\,w^{-1}rw)$ for every word of $w$. If the word $(s_1,\dots,s_k)$ is reduced, then $n(r)\le1$ for every $r$: if $n(r)\ge2$, two indices would have $r_i=r_j$ with $i<j$, and step 5.1 would produce a word of length $k-2$ for $w$, contradicting $k=\ell(w)$. Consequently the map $i\mapsto r_i$ is injective, so the set $\{r_1,\dots,r_k\}=\{r\in T:\eta(r,w)=-1\}$ has exactly $k=\ell(w)$ elements; by the independence of $\eta$ from the word, this set is independent of the reduced expression chosen. [F1, step 4.1, step 5.1]

7.1 **Ambient reducedness of alternating words.** Let $s\ne t$, $m:=m(s,t)$, and let $w_q$ be the value of the alternating word $(s,t,s,\dots)$ of length $q$ beginning with $s$, where $q\le m$ if $m<\infty$ and $q\ge1$ is arbitrary if $m=\infty$. Its prefix reflections are $r_i=(st)^{i-1}s$ by the closed form of step 4.1. They are pairwise distinct: an equality $r_i=r_j$ with $i<j\le q$ gives $(st)^{j-i}=1$ after right multiplication by $s$, which is impossible when $m<\infty$ because then $0<j-i<q\le m$ and $m$ is the least positive exponent killing $st$ by step 3.1 and [F7], and equally impossible when $m=\infty$ because by step 3.1 no positive power of $st$ is $1$. Hence exactly $q$ elements $r$ satisfy $\eta(r,w_q)=-1$, namely the $r_i$; on the other hand, for any word of length $k$ in $S$ representing $w_q$ [F1], step 6.1 gives $\#\{r:\eta(r,w_q)=-1\}=\#\{r:n'(r)\text{ odd}\}\le\sum_r n'(r)=k$, where $n'(r)$ counts the occurrences of $r$ among that word's prefix reflections. Therefore $k\ge q$ for every word for $w_q$, so $\ell(w_q)=q$; in particular the values $w_1,\dots,w_q$ are pairwise distinct, having distinct lengths. [F1, F7, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 6.1] ∎
