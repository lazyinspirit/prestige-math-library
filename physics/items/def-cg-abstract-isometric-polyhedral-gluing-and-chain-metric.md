## Definition

**Shape and face posets.** Let $(P,\le)$ be a poset ([[def-partial-order]]) with least element $\varnothing$ such that:

(a) for every $p\in P$ the principal down-set $P_{\le p}:=\{r\in P:r\le p\}$ is finite and, when $p\ne\varnothing$, is isomorphic as a poset to the face poset of a nonempty compact convex polyhedral cell ([[def-finite-convex-cell-complex-and-linear-subdivision]], [[def-face-poset-and-order-complex]]);

(b) every two elements $p,q\in P$ have a greatest lower bound $p\wedge q$ in $P$, that is, an element of $P$ that is a lower bound of both and is larger than every such lower bound.

The elements of $P$ are called **faces**, $p\le q$ is read "$p$ is a face of $q$", and $\varnothing$ is the **empty face**.

**Isometric polyhedral gluing.** An *isometric polyhedral gluing of shape $P$* consists of the following data.

**(i) Cells and face isometries.** For every $p\in P\setminus\{\varnothing\}$ a nonempty compact convex polyhedral cell $C_p$ in a finite-dimensional Euclidean affine space, and for every pair $p\le q$ of nonempty faces a nonempty face $F_{p,q}$ of $C_q$ together with an affine isometry $h_{p,q}$ from the affine span of $C_p$ onto the affine span of $F_{p,q}$ ([[def-isometry-and-metric-embedding]]) which carries $C_p$ onto $F_{p,q}$, subject to:

- $h_{p,p}=\operatorname{id}_{C_p}$ for every $p$;
- the *cocycle condition* $h_{q,r}\circ h_{p,q}=h_{p,r}$ whenever $p\le q\le r$;
- for every $q$ the assignment $p\mapsto F_{p,q}=h_{p,q}(C_p)$ is an isomorphism of posets from $P_{\le q}\setminus\{\varnothing\}$ onto the set of nonempty faces of $C_q$, ordered by inclusion.

**(ii) Quotient and intersection condition.** Let $X$ be the quotient of the disjoint union $\bigsqcup_{p\in P\setminus\{\varnothing\}}C_p$ by the equivalence relation generated by $x\sim h_{p,q}(x)$ for $x\in C_p$ and $p\le q$, and let $\iota_p\colon C_p\to X$ denote the quotient map. The *intersection condition* is:

- each $\iota_p$ is injective; and
- for all nonempty faces $p,q$ one has $\iota_p(C_p)\cap\iota_q(C_q)=\iota_{p\wedge q}(C_{p\wedge q})$, where the right-hand side denotes the empty subset of $X$ when $p\wedge q=\varnothing$.

**(iii) Weak topology.** A subset $U\subseteq X$ is declared open if and only if $U\cap\iota_p(C_p)$ is relatively open in $\iota_p(C_p)$ for every nonempty face $p$ ([[def-metric-topology]]).

**Standing hypotheses.** (H1) *Connectedness*: $X\ne\emptyset$ and $X$ is connected. (H2) *Local finiteness*: every point of $X$ lies in $\iota_p(C_p)$ for only finitely many $p$. (H3) *Finite shapes*: the cells $C_p$ fall into only finitely many isometry classes. By (H1) at least one cell exists, so by (H3) the maximum $D:=\max\{\dim C_p:p\in P\setminus\{\varnothing\}\}$ of the dimensions of the cells is a well-defined natural number.

**Chains and the chain metric candidate.** Write $d_p$ for the Euclidean metric on the affine span of $C_p$. Let $x,y\in X$. A **chain** from $x$ to $y$ is a finite sequence $x=x_0,x_1,\dots,x_m=y$ of points of $X$ such that for each $i\in\{1,\dots,m\}$ some cell contains both $x_{i-1}$ and $x_i$; for $m=0$ the chain is the one-term sequence $x$. Its **length** is
$$\ell(x_0,\dots,x_m):=\sum_{i=1}^{m}d_{p_i}(x_{i-1},x_i),$$
where for each $i$ the face $p_i$ is *any* nonempty face with $x_{i-1},x_i\in\iota_{p_i}(C_{p_i})$; this number does not depend on these choices. The **chain metric candidate** of the gluing is
$$d(x,y):=\inf\bigl\{\,\ell(x_0,\dots,x_m):x_0,\dots,x_m\text{ is a chain from }x\text{ to }y\,\bigr\}.$$
Because $X$ is connected, every two points of $X$ are joined by a chain (this and the independence of $\ell$ from the chosen faces are proved in [[thm-cg-polyhedral-chain-metric-topology-and-properness]]), so the infimum is taken over a nonempty set of real numbers, and $0\le d(x,y)<\infty$. This finiteness follows from (H1) and the gluing data; it is not an additional hypothesis.

**What is asserted, and what is not.** For all $x,y,z\in X$ one has $d(x,x)=0$ (the one-term chain), $d(x,y)=d(y,x)$ (reverse a chain) and $d(x,z)\le d(x,y)+d(y,z)$ (concatenate chains), and these three facts need no hypothesis beyond the definitions. No other metric axiom, no agreement of $d$ with the weak topology, and no completeness, properness or geodesic property is asserted here: those are the conclusions of the theorems of this page, under (H1)–(H3).

**Caveat on cell metrics.** If $x,y$ lie in a common cell then the one-step chain gives $d(x,y)\le d_p(x,y)$, but equality can fail, because a chain may leave the cell and return with smaller total length (Bridson–Haefliger I.7.6). Nothing above asserts that the chain metric restricts to the Euclidean metric of a cell, and no cell is assumed to be geodesic for $d$.
