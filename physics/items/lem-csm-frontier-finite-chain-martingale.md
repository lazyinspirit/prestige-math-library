## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $E$ be finite and nonempty, let $L$ be a rate matrix on $E$, let $X$ be its uniformized realization from the preceding existence result, and let $f:E\to\mathbb R$ be bounded. Put $g=Lf$ and
$$M_t=f(X_t)-f(X_0)-\int_0^t g(X_s)\,ds,\qquad \Gamma_f(i)=L(f^2)(i)-2f(i)Lf(i).$$
For every finite $T$, $(M_t)_{0\le t\le T}$ is a square-integrable martingale relative to the construction filtration, and
$$A_t:=\int_0^t\Gamma_f(X_s)\,ds$$
is its continuous predictable bracket: $A_0=0$, $A$ is increasing and predictable, and $M_t^2-A_t$ is a martingale. The bracket is understood in the continuous predictable increasing compensator convention stated in the proof.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite-state rate matrix, the uniformized process, a bounded real function $f$, a finite horizon $T$, and the Axiom of Choice.

[A1] Assume the Axiom of Choice. The all-pairs continuous-time martingale and conditional-expectation interfaces below carry this assumption ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F1] [[def-csm-frontier-finite-generator-chain]]: with $\lambda_i=\sum_{j\ne i}L_{ij}$ and $q=\max_i\lambda_i$, if $q=0$ then $L=0$ and $X$ is constant; if $q>0$, set $R=I+L/q$ and take an $R$-Markov sequence $Y$ and mutually independent rate-$q$ exponential waiting times independent of the entire sequence $Y$, with $N_t$ counting their arrivals and $X_t=Y_{N_t}$. [[lem-csm-frontier-finite-generator-chain-existence]]: $X$ is adapted and has all-pairs conditional transition matrices $e^{hL}$; when $q>0$, its proof also shows that $N$ has independent rate-$q$ Poisson increments and is independent of $Y$.

[F2] [[def-continuous-time-adapted-process-and-martingale]]: a martingale is adapted, meaning $M_t$ is $\mathcal F_t$-measurable at every time, is integrable, and satisfies $\mathbb E[M_t\mid\mathcal F_s]=M_s$ almost surely for every $s\le t$; square-integrability requires $\mathbb E M_t^2<\infty$ at every time.

[F3] [[thm-basic-algebra-and-order-properties-of-conditional-expectation]]: conditional expectation is linear, preserves order and constants, and satisfies $|\mathbb E[Z\mid\mathcal G]|\le\mathbb E[|Z|\mid\mathcal G]$ for integrable $Z$.

[F4] [[def-progressively-measurable-and-predictable-process]]: predictability is measurability for the sigma-algebra generated by $(u,v]\times A$ with $A\in\mathcal F_u$ and by time-zero sets.

[F5] [[thm-fubini-theorem-for-l-one-on-sigma-finite-product-spaces]]: for bounded jointly measurable functions on $[0,T]\times\Omega$, the time and probability integrals may be interchanged.

[F6] [[lem-continuous-finite-variation-martingale-is-zero]]: a continuous martingale starting at zero with integrable finite total variation on $[0,T]$ is indistinguishably zero.

[F7] [[thm-taking-out-what-is-known]]: if $Z$ is integrable and $H$ is bounded and $\mathcal G$-measurable, then $\mathbb E[HZ\mid\mathcal G]=H\mathbb E[Z\mid\mathcal G]$.

[F8] [[thm-tower-property-of-conditional-expectation]]: for $\mathcal H\subseteq\mathcal G$ and integrable $Z$, $\mathbb E[\mathbb E[Z\mid\mathcal G]\mid\mathcal H]=\mathbb E[Z\mid\mathcal H]$. All these conditional-expectation interfaces assume AC.

## Proof

If $q=\max_i\lambda_i=0$, then $L=0$ and the uniformized process is constant by [F1]. Thus $g=0$, $\Gamma_f=0$, and $M=A=0$. If $B$ is any other bracket in the stated class, then $B-A$ is a continuous martingale starting at zero with total variation bounded by the integrable random variable $B_T+A_T$; [F6] gives $B=A$ indistinguishably. Hence the conclusion holds when $q=0$. For the remainder of the proof assume $q>0$.

1.1 Fix $t>0$ and a bounded function $a:E\to\mathbb R$. On $[0,t]$, approximate $a(X_u)$ by the process with value $a(X_{kt/2^n})$ on $((k-1)t/2^n,kt/2^n]$, $1\le k\le2^n$, and value $a(X_0)$ at zero. Each approximation is $\mathcal B([0,t])\otimes\mathcal F_t$-measurable by adaptedness of $X$ in [F1], and converges pointwise by right continuity. The construction uses constant paths on its exceptional explosion event, so this argument applies there too. Its time integral is the $\mathcal F_t$-measurable sum $(t/2^n)\sum_{k=1}^{2^n}a(X_{kt/2^n})$. These sums converge to $\int_0^t a(X_u)\,du$: on a path with $J$ jumps, the error is at most $2\|a\|_\infty Jt/2^n$, since only intervals containing a jump contribute. Thus the time integral is $\mathcal F_t$-measurable, without completing the filtration. Taking $a=g$ proves that $M$ is adapted, also at $t=0$. By [F1], $\mathbb E[g(X_{s+r})\mid\mathcal F_s]=(e^{rL}g)(X_s)$. The joint measurability just proved and boundedness allow [F5] on finite time intervals. Testing against each event in $\mathcal F_s$ gives $$ \mathbb E\!\left[\int_s^{s+h}g(X_u)\,du\middle|\mathcal F_s\right] =\int_0^h(e^{rL}Lf)(X_s)\,dr=(e^{hL}f-f)(X_s), $$ where the last equality follows by termwise differentiation of the absolutely convergent finite-matrix exponential series. Also $\mathbb E[f(X_{s+h})\mid\mathcal F_s]=(e^{hL}f)(X_s)$. Subtracting and using adaptedness proves $\mathbb E[M_{s+h}\mid\mathcal F_s]=M_s$ for every $s,h$. On $[0,T]$, $|M_t|\le2\|f\|_\infty+T\|Lf\|_\infty$, so every $M_t$ is square-integrable and [F2] gives the all-pairs martingale assertion. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F5, construct, algebra]

2.1 Since $\sum_jL_{ij}=0$, $$ \Gamma_f(i)=\sum_jL_{ij}(f(j)-f(i))^2=\sum_{j\ne i}L_{ij}(f(j)-f(i))^2\ge0. $$ Thus $A$ is increasing and bounded on $[0,T]$. The integral argument of step 1.1 with $a=\Gamma_f$ proves that $A$ is adapted. Also $|A_t-A_s|\le\|\Gamma_f\|_\infty|t-s|$, so $A$ is continuous. For $T>0$ and each $n$, the step process equal to $A_{kT/2^n}$ on $(kT/2^n,(k+1)T/2^n]$ and zero at time zero is predictable by [F4]; these processes converge pointwise to $A$ by continuity. Therefore $A$ is predictable; on the horizon $T=0$ the assertion is immediate from $A_0=0$. [F1, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Fix $u$ and condition on $\mathcal F_u$, writing $X_u=i$. For $0<h\le1$, the number of uniformization events during $(u,u+h]$ is Poisson with rate $q=\max_i\lambda_i$ by the exponential-clock construction in [F1]. If there is no event, $\Delta M:=M_{u+h}-M_u=-h g(i)$, whose square is $O(h^2)$. If there is exactly one event and the new state is $j\ne i$, its probability is $e^{-qh}qhR_{ij}=e^{-qh}hL_{ij}=hL_{ij}+O(h^2)$, and $\Delta M=f(j)-f(i)+O(h)$ uniformly because the drift integral has magnitude at most $h\|g\|_\infty$. Its contribution to the conditional second moment is therefore $hL_{ij}(f(j)-f(i))^2+O(h^2)$. A one-event self-transition contributes $O(h^3)$. The probability of at least two events is $O(h^2)$ and $\Delta M$ is uniformly bounded for $h\le1$, so its contribution is $O(h^2)$. Since $E$ is finite, all error bounds are uniform in $i$, and $$ \mathbb E[(\Delta M)^2\mid\mathcal F_u]=h\Gamma_f(i)+O(h^2). $$ Also $\mathbb P(X_{u+r}\ne i\mid\mathcal F_u)\le qr$, so boundedness of $\Gamma_f$ gives $$ \mathbb E\!\left[\int_u^{u+h}\Gamma_f(X_v)\,dv\middle|\mathcal F_u\right]=h\Gamma_f(i)+O(h^2). $$ Consequently $\left|\mathbb E[(\Delta M)^2-\int_u^{u+h}\Gamma_f(X_v)\,dv\mid\mathcal F_u]\right|\le C h^2$ for a finite constant $C$ depending only on $L,f,T$. [A1, F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Let $D_t=M_t^2-A_t$. On any interval $[u,u+h]$, $D_{u+h}-D_u=2M_u\Delta M+(\Delta M)^2-\int_u^{u+h}\Gamma_f(X_v)\,dv$. On $[0,T]$, $M_u$ is bounded and $\mathcal F_u$-measurable by step 1.1, so [F7] and the mean-zero increment give $\mathbb E[2M_u\Delta M\mid\mathcal F_u]=2M_u\mathbb E[\Delta M\mid\mathcal F_u]=0$, while step 3.1 bounds the conditional expectation of the remaining terms by $Ch^2$. For fixed $s<t$, divide $[s,t]$ into $n$ equal intervals of length $h=(t-s)/n\le1$. For the subdivision times $u$, $\mathcal F_s\subseteq\mathcal F_u$ and every increment of $D$ is integrable. Thus [F8], linearity and order in [F3], and the preceding bound imply $|\mathbb E[D_t-D_s\mid\mathcal F_s]|\le nCh^2=C(t-s)^2/n$. Letting $n\to\infty$ proves $\mathbb E[D_t\mid\mathcal F_s]=D_s$. Thus $M^2-A$ is a martingale. [A1, F2, F3, F7, F8, step 1.1, step 3.1]

5.1 In this item, a continuous predictable bracket means a continuous predictable increasing process $B$ with $B_0=0$, $\mathbb E B_T<\infty$, and $M^2-B$ a martingale. Steps 2.1 and 4.1 show that $A$ has these properties. If $B$ is another such process, then $B-A$ is a continuous martingale starting at zero, and its total variation on $[0,T]$ is at most $B_T+A_T$, which is integrable. By [F6], $B-A$ is indistinguishably zero. Therefore $A$ is the unique bracket in this stated continuous predictable compensator class, and it has the displayed formula. [A1, F4, F6, step 2.1, step 4.1] ∎
