## Definition

Fix an integer dimension $d\ge1$. A point multiset is a finite ordered list $P=(x_0,\ldots,x_{N-1})$ with $N\ge1$ and $x_i=(x_{i1},\ldots,x_{id})\in[0,1)^d$; repeated points are counted with their multiplicities. For $u=(u_1,\ldots,u_d)\in[0,1]^d$, its anchored half-open box and its volume are
$$B(u):=\prod_{j=1}^d[0,u_j),\qquad \operatorname{vol}(B(u)):=\prod_{j=1}^d u_j.$$
The **star discrepancy** of $P$ is
$$D_N^*(P):=\sup_{u\in[0,1]^d}\left|\frac1N\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbf 1_{B(u)}(x_i)-\prod_{j=1}^d u_j\right|.$$
An infinite sequence $(x_n)_{n\ge0}$ in $[0,1)^d$ is **uniformly distributed** if $D_N^*(x_0,\ldots,x_{N-1})\to0$ as $N\to\infty$.
For a bounded Riemann-integrable $f:[0,1]^d\to\mathbb R$, the **cube integral** and the point-set **cube quadrature** are
$$I_d(f):=\int_{[0,1]^d}f(x)\,dx,\qquad Q_P(f):=\frac1N\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}f(x_i),$$
where $I_d$ is the Riemann integral of [[def-multidimensional-darboux-integral]]. No randomness is part of $Q_P$.

Let $b\ge2$ be an integer. A **base-$b$ elementary interval** in dimension $d$ is
$$E=\prod_{j=1}^d[a_jb^{-k_j},(a_j+1)b^{-k_j}),$$
where each $k_j\ge0$ and each $a_j$ is an integer with $0\le a_j<b^{k_j}$. For integers $m\ge0$ and $0\le t\le m$, a point multiset $P$ is a **$(t,m,d)$-net in base $b$** if it has $b^m$ entries and every base-$b$ elementary interval with $\sum_{j=1}^d k_j=m-t$ contains exactly $b^t$ entries, counted with multiplicity. A sequence $(x_n)_{n\ge0}$ in $[0,1)^d$ is a **$(t,d)$-sequence in base $b$** if for every $m\ge t$ and $k\ge0$, the aligned block $(x_{kb^m},\ldots,x_{(k+1)b^m-1})$ is a $(t,m,d)$-net.

For a prime integer $p$, we have $p\ge2$ ([[def-prime]]). Write $\mathbb F_p:=\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$, which is a field by [[thm-z-mod-p-is-a-field]]. By [[thm-division-algorithm-in-z]], each residue class has exactly one representative in $\{0,\ldots,p-1\}$, and every $n\in\{0,\ldots,p^m-1\}$ has a unique base-$p$ expansion $n=\sum_{r=1}^m n_{r-1}p^{r-1}$ with digits $n_{r-1}\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}$. The integer powers use [[def-integer-power]]. A **digital point set** in base $p$ is generated from matrices $C_1,\ldots,C_d\in\mathbb F_p^{m\times m}$ as follows. For each such $n$, let $v(n)=(n_0,\ldots,n_{m-1})^T\in\mathbb F_p^m$, interpreting each digit as its residue class, and compute $y^{(j)}=C_jv(n)$ in $\mathbb F_p^m$. Taking the unique representatives $y^{(j)}_r\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}$, set
$$x_{n,j}:=\sum_{r=1}^m y^{(j)}_r p^{-r}.$$
Since $0\le y^{(j)}_r\le p-1$, the finite geometric sum gives $0\le x_{n,j}\le\sum_{r=1}^m(p-1)p^{-r}=1-p^{-m}<1$; the identity follows by telescoping with the integer-power laws [[lem-power-laws]], and $p^{-m}>0$ by [[lem-power-monotone]] and [[lem-of-inverse-positive]]. It is a **digital $(t,m,d)$-net** only if this generated point multiset also satisfies the stated $(t,m,d)$-net counting condition; arbitrary generator matrices need not do so.

A **digital sequence** is generated by infinite matrices $C_j=(c^{(j)}_{r,k})_{r,k\ge1}$ over $\mathbb F_p$. For each $n\ge0$, pad its finite base-$p$ expansion with zero digits and, for every $r,j$, compute the finite field sum $y_{r,j}(n)=\sum_{k\ge1}c^{(j)}_{r,k}n_{k-1}$; only finitely many summands are nonzero. Let $\widetilde y_{r,j}(n)\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}$ be its unique integer representative. The output coordinate is the real series
$$x_{n,j}:=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\widetilde y_{r,j}(n)p^{-r}.$$
This series is defined by [[def-series]] and converges: its terms satisfy $0\le\widetilde y_{r,j}(n)p^{-r}\le(p-1)p^{-r}$; since $p\ge2$, [[lem-of-inverse-positive]] gives $p^{-1}\in(0,1)$, and the bounding geometric series $\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}(p-1)p^{-r}$ converges to $1$ by [[thm-geometric-series]]. The comparison theorem [[thm-direct-comparison-test]] therefore gives convergence of the output series. Its sum lies in $[0,1]$; the resulting points are required to lie in $[0,1)^d$. It is a digital $(t,d)$-sequence when it also satisfies the aligned-block net condition above. No discrepancy rate follows from these definitions alone.
