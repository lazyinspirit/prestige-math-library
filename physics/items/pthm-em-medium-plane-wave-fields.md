## Statement

Assume the elementary inertial-rest branch of [[post-em-material-closure]] in its isotropic scalar case at rest in the laboratory frame, with constants $\varepsilon,\mu>0$ and $\sigma\ge0$, the constitutive relations $D=\varepsilon E$, $B=\mu H$ of [[post-em-material-closure]](b), with $D,H$ defined by [[def-em-polarization-magnetization]], the macroscopic equations of [[pthm-em-macroscopic-maxwell]], the fields of [[def-em-fields]] on the laboratory region of [[def-em-lab-geometry]] with the units of [[def-em-si-conventions]], and the parameters and phase convention of [[def-em-medium-wave-parameters]]. Consider a declared region with vanishing free charge density and no external free current, so that $\rho_f=0$ and the free current is the conduction current $J_f=\sigma E$. Let $k\in\mathbb C^{3}$, $\omega>0$ and $e,h\in\mathbb C^{3}$, and for $(x,t)\in\mathbb R^{3}\times\mathbb R$ define $E(x,t):=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(e\,e^{i(k\cdot x-\omega t)}\bigr)$ and $H(x,t):=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(h\,e^{i(k\cdot x-\omega t)}\bigr)$ componentwise, i.e. with the phase convention $e^{i(k\cdot x-\omega t)}$ and the real-part encoding of [[def-em-complex-plane-wave]]; put $D:=\varepsilon E$, $B:=\mu H$ and $J_f:=\sigma E$. Extend the Euclidean inner and cross products to $\mathbb C^{3}$ by the same componentwise polynomial formulas ([[def-euclidean-inner-product]], [[def-cross-product-in-r3]], [[lem-cross-product-is-bilinear-alternating-and-orthogonal]]). For a complex vector $z\in\mathbb C^3$, use the Hermitian norm $|z|:=\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^3|z_j|^2}$, so $|z|^2=z\cdot\overline z=|\operatorname{Re}z|^2+|\operatorname{Im}z|^2$; the dot product itself remains complex bilinear. Then:

**(a) [Amplitude vanishing]** if $\operatorname{Re}\bigl(w\,e^{i(k\cdot x-\omega t)}\bigr)=0$ for some $w\in\mathbb C^{3}$ and all $(x,t)$ in a nonempty open subset of $\mathbb R^{3}\times\mathbb R$, then $w=0$.

**(b) [Amplitude conditions]** the quadruple $(E,H,D,B)$ satisfies the four source-free macroscopic equations $\operatorname{div}D=0$, $\operatorname{div}B=0$, $\operatorname{curl}E=-\partial_tB$, $\operatorname{curl}H=J_f+\partial_tD$ on $\mathbb R^{3}\times\mathbb R$ if and only if
$$k\cdot e=0,\qquad k\cdot h=0,\qquad k\times e=\omega\mu\,h,\qquad k\times h=-(\omega\varepsilon+i\sigma)\,e .$$

**(c) [Structure, dispersion and solution family]** if $e\ne0$ and (b) holds, then $h=k\times e/(\omega\mu)$, $k\cdot h=0$, $e\cdot h=0$ and $k\cdot k=\varepsilon\mu\omega^{2}+i\mu\sigma\omega$; conversely, if $k\in\mathbb C^{3}\setminus\{0\}$ and $\omega>0$ satisfy $k\cdot k=\varepsilon\mu\omega^{2}+i\mu\sigma\omega$, then for every $e\in\mathbb C^{3}$ with $k\cdot e=0$ the pair $(e,\,k\times e/(\omega\mu))$ satisfies (b), with $(e,h)=(0,0)$ if and only if $e=0$; on the dispersion relation the solution set of (b) for fixed $(k,\omega)$ is exactly $\{(e,\,k\times e/(\omega\mu)):k\cdot e=0\}$, a complex two-dimensional space when $k\ne0$; off the dispersion relation the only solution is $e=h=0$.

**(d) [Lossless propagating waves: instantaneous identities and period averages]** if (b) holds and $\sigma=0$ and $k\in\mathbb R^{3}$ with $e\ne0$, then $|k|^{2}=\varepsilon\mu\omega^{2}$, $|k|=k(\omega)$ in the notation of [[def-em-medium-wave-parameters]], and for every $(x,t)$ $k\cdot E=k\cdot B=0$, $E\cdot B=0$, $B=\hat k\times E/v$ and $|B|=|E|/v$ with $\hat k=k/|k|$; the period-$T$ averages with $T=2\pi/\omega$ ([[lem-em-period-average]]) satisfy, for $u=\tfrac12(E\cdot D+B\cdot H)$ and $S=E\times H$ of [[pthm-em-restricted-material-energy]],
$$\langle u\rangle=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|e|^{2},\qquad \langle S\rangle=\frac{|e|^{2}}{2\mu\omega}\,k=\frac{|e|^{2}}{2Z}\,\hat k=v\,\langle u\rangle\hat k .$$

**(e) [Evanescent waves]** if $\sigma=0$, $n'\in\mathbb R^{3}$ is a unit vector, $k=\kappa_0+i\beta n'$ with $\kappa_0\in\mathbb R^{3}$, $\kappa_0\cdot n'=0$, $\beta>0$ and $k\cdot k=\varepsilon\mu\omega^{2}$ (so $|\kappa_0|^{2}=\varepsilon\mu\omega^{2}+\beta^{2}$ and $\kappa_0\ne0$; define $\hat\kappa_0:=\kappa_0/|\kappa_0|$), and if $e\ne0$ satisfies $k\cdot e=0$ with $h:=k\times e/(\omega\mu)$, then the field factorizes exactly as $E(x,t)=e^{-\beta\,n'\cdot x}\,\operatorname{Re}\bigl(e\,e^{i(\kappa_0\cdot x-\omega t)}\bigr)$, and the time-averaged flux satisfies $\langle S\rangle=e^{-2\beta n'\cdot x}\,\tfrac12\operatorname{Re}(e\times\overline h)$ with normal component $\langle S\cdot n'\rangle=-\dfrac{e^{-2\beta n'\cdot x}}{2\mu\omega}\operatorname{Re}\bigl[(e\cdot n')(\kappa_0\cdot\overline e)\bigr]$; this normal component is in fact zero for every such transverse amplitude. For the transverse-electric polarisation $e=E_0(n'\times\hat\kappa_0)$, $E_0\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$, and for the transverse-magnetic data $h=H_0\hat y'$ with $\hat y':=n'\times\hat\kappa_0$, $H_0\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$ and electric amplitude $e=-(k\times\hat y')H_0/(\omega\varepsilon)$, the normal component vanishes identically, $\langle S\cdot n'\rangle\equiv0$, while the tangential averaged flux is positive: $\langle S\cdot\hat\kappa_0\rangle=\dfrac{|\kappa_0||e|^{2}}{2\mu\omega}e^{-2\beta n'\cdot x}>0$ in the transverse-electric case and $\langle S\cdot\hat\kappa_0\rangle=\dfrac{|\kappa_0||h|^{2}}{2\omega\varepsilon}e^{-2\beta n'\cdot x}>0$ in the transverse-magnetic case.

**(f) [Scope]** the four equations are verified only for the explicit ideal whole-space plane-wave fields; for $k$ with $\operatorname{Im}k\ne0$ the factor $e^{-\operatorname{Im}k\cdot x}$ grows in some directions, so clauses (b)-(e) are read on regions where the model declares the field bounded (in (e), on the half-space $n'\cdot x>0$). No completeness, energy-finiteness, boundary, interface, material-validity or empirical claim is made, and the finite-band caveat of [[post-em-material-closure]](b) applies whenever $\varepsilon\mu<c^{-2}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The declared isotropic scalar Ohmic branch at rest with constants $\varepsilon,\mu>0$ and $\sigma\ge0$, the laboratory region of [[def-em-lab-geometry]] with the units of [[def-em-si-conventions]], the fields of [[def-em-fields]], and the plane-wave data $k\in\mathbb C^3$, $\omega>0$, $e,h\in\mathbb C^3$ with $E=\operatorname{Re}(e\,e^{i\theta})$, $H=\operatorname{Re}(h\,e^{i\theta})$, $\theta(x,t):=k\cdot x-\omega t$, $D=\varepsilon E$, $B=\mu H$, $J_f=\sigma E$; the inner and cross products are extended componentwise to $\mathbb C^3$.

[F1] Real-part encoding: $E_j=\operatorname{Re}(e_j\exp(i\theta))$ componentwise; $\operatorname{Re}$ is additive and commutes with real scalar multiples, with partial differentiation and with integration, and the real-part encoding of a product of two encoded real fields must not be read off from a product of complex expressions without conjugation ([[def-em-complex-plane-wave]]).

[F2] The complex exponential and its calculus: $\exp w=\sum_{n\ge0}w^n/n!$ converges for every $w\in\mathbb C$ with $\exp 0=1$ and $\exp'=\exp$ ([[def-complex-exponential]], [[thm-complex-exponential-is-entire-with-derivative-itself]]); the sum, real-scalar-multiple, product and quotient rules ([[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]]) and the chain rule for total derivatives ([[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]]) apply; $\exp(z+w)=\exp z\exp w$ for all complex $z,w$, and $\exp(x)=e^x$ for real $x$ ([[thm-complex-exponential-addition-and-real-extension]]); for real $x,y$, $\exp(x+iy)=e^x(\cos y+i\sin y)$ and $|\exp(x+iy)|=e^x$ ([[cor-complex-exponential-cartesian-form-modulus-and-eulers-identity]]).

[F3] Complex field arithmetic, conjugation and modulus: $\mathbb C$ is a field, a product vanishes only if a factor does ([[thm-complex-numbers-form-a-field]], [[def-complex-numbers-and-arithmetic]]); conjugation is an involutive real-field automorphism fixing the reals, $z\overline z=|z|^2$, $|zw|=|z||w|$, $|z|\ge0$ with $|z|=0$ exactly for $z=0$, and $\operatorname{Re}w=(w+\overline w)/2$ by the coordinate formulas ([[def-complex-conjugate-real-imaginary-part-and-modulus]], [[lem-complex-conjugation-and-modulus-laws]]).

[F4] The divergence and curl of a $C^1$ field as coordinate expressions in the first partial derivatives, and partial derivatives as derivatives of line maps ([[def-divergence-and-curl-of-a-c1-vector-field]], [[def-directional-and-partial-derivatives]]).

[F5] The Euclidean inner product $\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_kx_ky_k$ and the cross product $u\times v=(u_yv_z-u_zv_y,u_zv_x-u_xv_z,u_xv_y-u_yv_x)$ on $\mathbb R^3$, both extended componentwise to complex vectors; the cross product is bilinear and alternating, $\langle u\times v,w\rangle=\det[u\ v\ w]$, and $u\times v$ is orthogonal to $u$ and $v$ ([[def-euclidean-inner-product]], [[def-cross-product-in-r3]], [[lem-cross-product-is-bilinear-alternating-and-orthogonal]]).

[F6] The material model and field equations: the elementary inertial-rest branch gives $D=\varepsilon E$, $B=\mu H$ and $J_f=\sigma E+J_{\mathrm{ext}}$ with $J_{\mathrm{ext}}=0$ here ([[post-em-material-closure]]); with $\rho_f=0$ the macroscopic equations read $\operatorname{div}D=0$, $\operatorname{div}B=0$, $\operatorname{curl}E=-\partial_tB$, $\operatorname{curl}H=J_f+\partial_tD$ ([[pthm-em-macroscopic-maxwell]]).

[F7] Medium parameters: $v=(\varepsilon\mu)^{-1/2}$, $Z=(\mu/\varepsilon)^{1/2}$, $k(\omega)=\omega/v$, and $\kappa(\omega)=p+iq$ with $p,q\ge0$ and $\kappa(\omega)^2=\varepsilon\mu\omega^2+i\mu\sigma\omega$ ([[def-em-medium-wave-parameters]]).

[F8] Stored energy and flux: for the constant model, $u=\tfrac12(E\cdot D+B\cdot H)$ and $S=E\times H$ ([[pthm-em-restricted-material-energy]]).

[F9] Period averages: for $\omega>0$, $T=2\pi/\omega$ and complex amplitudes $a,d$, the average over a period of $\operatorname{Re}(ae^{-i\omega t})\operatorname{Re}(de^{-i\omega t})$ is $\tfrac12\operatorname{Re}(a\overline d)$, with the analogous componentwise inner-product and cross-product forms; the conjugation is essential ([[lem-em-period-average]]).

[F10] Square roots: every nonnegative real has a unique nonnegative square root ([[thm-of-square-roots]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; exhibit the partial derivatives of the encoded fields, reduce each equation to a complex coefficient identity by the amplitude-vanishing fact, analyse the resulting algebraic system, and compute the lossless and evanescent structures with the period-average supplier.

1.1 [Derivatives of the encoded fields] For $w\in\mathbb C^3$ and $\theta(x,t)=k\cdot x-\omega t$, each component $x\mapsto\operatorname{Re}(w_j\exp(i\theta(x,t)))$ has partial derivatives $\partial_{x_l}\operatorname{Re}(w_j\exp(i\theta))=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(i\,k_l\,w_j\exp(i\theta)\bigr)$ and $\partial_t\operatorname{Re}(w_j\exp(i\theta))=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(-i\omega\,w_j\exp(i\theta)\bigr)$: indeed $\theta$ is the real-linear map $(x,t)\mapsto\sum_lk_lx_l-\omega t$ with partial derivatives $k_l$ in $x_l$ and $-\omega$ in $t$ as derivatives of line maps by [F4], and the chain rule [F2] applied to the entire exponential gives $\partial_{x_l}\exp(i\theta)=ik_l\exp(i\theta)$ and $\partial_t\exp(i\theta)=-i\omega\exp(i\theta)$, after which the real-linear coordinate projection $\operatorname{Re}$ commutes with taking the derivative by [F1]. The complex multipliers stay inside $\operatorname{Re}$. Consequently $\operatorname{div}D=\varepsilon\operatorname{Re}(i\,k\cdot e\,e^{i\theta})$, $\operatorname{div}B=\mu\operatorname{Re}(i\,k\cdot h\,e^{i\theta})$, $\operatorname{curl}E=\operatorname{Re}(i\,k\times e\,e^{i\theta})$, $\partial_tB=\operatorname{Re}(-i\omega\mu\,h\,e^{i\theta})$, $\operatorname{curl}H=\operatorname{Re}(i\,k\times h\,e^{i\theta})$ and $J_f+\partial_tD=\operatorname{Re}\bigl((\sigma-i\omega\varepsilon)e\,e^{i\theta}\bigr)$ with the componentwise complex inner and cross products of [F5] and the uniform notation $e^{i\theta}:=\exp(i\theta)$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, algebra]

1.2 [Nonvanishing of the exponential] For every $w\in\mathbb C$, $\exp w\ne0$: by [F2], $\exp w\exp(-w)=\exp 0=1$, and a product equal to $1\ne0$ has no zero factor in the field $\mathbb C$ by [F3]. In particular $e^{i\theta}\ne0$ for all $(x,t)$, and $e^{ik\cdot x_0}\ne0$ for every fixed $x_0$. [F2, F3]

2.1 [Amplitude vanishing, part (a)] Let $Z\subseteq\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R$ be nonempty and open with $\operatorname{Re}(w\,e^{i\theta})=0$ on $Z$ for a fixed $w\in\mathbb C^3$. Fix $(x_0,t_0)\in Z$; by openness of $Z$ there is $\delta>0$ with $\{x_0\}\times(t_0-\delta,t_0+\delta)\subseteq Z$. The components of the map $t\mapsto\operatorname{Re}(W\exp(-i\omega t))$ with $W:=w\,e^{ik\cdot x_0}$ vanish on that interval, and by step 1.1 its $t$-derivative is $\operatorname{Re}(-i\omega W\exp(-i\omega t))$, so at $t_0$ both $\operatorname{Re}(W\exp(-i\omega t_0))=0$ and $\operatorname{Re}(-i\omega W\exp(-i\omega t_0))=0$. Writing $W\exp(-i\omega t_0)=u+iv$ with real coordinate vectors $u,v$ by [F3], the first identity is $u=0$ and the second is $\operatorname{Re}(-i\omega(u+iv))=\omega v=0$; since $\omega>0$, $v=0$; hence $W\exp(-i\omega t_0)=0$, and since $\exp(-i\omega t_0)\ne0$ by step 1.2 the field property in [F3] gives $W=0$, i.e. $w\,e^{ik\cdot x_0}=0$, and $\exp(ik\cdot x_0)\ne0$ by step 1.2 gives $w=0$. This proves (a). [given, step 1.1, step 1.2, F2, F3]

3.1 [Equations as coefficient identities] By step 1.1 the four equations of (b) are, on $\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R$, the identities $\operatorname{Re}(i\,k\cdot e\,e^{i\theta})=0$, $\operatorname{Re}(i\,k\cdot h\,e^{i\theta})=0$, $\operatorname{Re}(i\,k\times e\,e^{i\theta})+\operatorname{Re}(-i\omega\mu\,h\,e^{i\theta})=0$ and $\operatorname{Re}(i\,k\times h\,e^{i\theta})-\operatorname{Re}\bigl((\sigma-i\omega\varepsilon)e\,e^{i\theta}\bigr)=0$; all fields are smooth on the open set $\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R$, so the operators of [F4] apply. By the additivity of $\operatorname{Re}$ in [F1] and [F3], each of these is an identity $\operatorname{Re}(W_j\,e^{i\theta})=0$ for a constant scalar or vector $W_j$ (embedding a scalar as $(W_j,0,0)$ to apply (a)); by (a) of step 2.1, with the open set $\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R$, they hold if and only if the constants vanish. [step 1.1, step 2.1, F1, F3, F4]

4.1 [Proof of (b)] By step 3.1, the first and second equations hold if and only if $k\cdot e=0$ and $k\cdot h=0$, since multiplication by the nonzero field element $i$ is injective by [F3]. The third holds if and only if $i\,k\times e-i\omega\mu\,h=0$, that is $k\times e=\omega\mu\,h$ after dividing by the nonzero factor $i$ and using $\omega\mu\ne0$. The fourth holds if and only if $i\,k\times h-(\sigma-i\omega\varepsilon)e=0$, because $\operatorname{Re}(-i\omega\mu h e^{i\theta})=-\operatorname{Re}(i\omega\mu h e^{i\theta})$ by [F3]; dividing by $i$ and using $(\sigma-i\omega\varepsilon)/i=-(\omega\varepsilon+i\sigma)$ — indeed $(\sigma-i\omega\varepsilon)=i\cdot\bigl(-(\omega\varepsilon+i\sigma)\bigr)$ by the field arithmetic of [F3] — gives $k\times h=-(\omega\varepsilon+i\sigma)e$. Hence the quadruple satisfies the four equations if and only if the four displayed amplitude conditions hold, which is (b). [step 3.1, F3, algebra]

5.1 [Structure and dispersion, forward in (c)] Assume $e\ne0$ and the four conditions of (b). Since $\omega\mu\ne0$, the third condition gives $h=k\times e/(\omega\mu)$; then $k\cdot h=k\cdot(k\times e)/(\omega\mu)=0$ and $e\cdot h=e\cdot(k\times e)/(\omega\mu)=0$ by the orthogonality in [F5]. Crossing the third condition with $k$ and substituting the fourth, $k\times(k\times e)=\omega\mu\,k\times h=-\omega\mu(\omega\varepsilon+i\sigma)e$; the vector triple-product identity $k\times(k\times e)=k(k\cdot e)-(k\cdot k)e$, a componentwise polynomial identity for the bilinear extension by [F5], together with $k\cdot e=0$, gives $-(k\cdot k)e=-\omega\mu(\omega\varepsilon+i\sigma)e=-(\varepsilon\mu\omega^2+i\mu\sigma\omega)e$, and choosing a nonzero coordinate of $e$ and cancelling that complex scalar gives $k\cdot k=\varepsilon\mu\omega^2+i\mu\sigma\omega$. [step 4.1, F3, F5, algebra]

6.1 [Converse and solution family in (c)] Let $k\ne0$, $\omega>0$ satisfy $k\cdot k=\varepsilon\mu\omega^2+i\mu\sigma\omega$, and let $e\in\mathbb C^3$ with $k\cdot e=0$; put $h:=k\times e/(\omega\mu)$. Then $k\cdot e=0$ and $k\cdot h=0$ by orthogonality in [F5], $k\times e=\omega\mu h$ by definition, and $k\times h=k\times(k\times e)/(\omega\mu)=\bigl[k(k\cdot e)-(k\cdot k)e\bigr]/(\omega\mu)=-\bigl(\varepsilon\mu\omega^2+i\mu\sigma\omega\bigr)e/(\omega\mu)=-(\omega\varepsilon+i\sigma)e$, so the pair $(e,h)$ satisfies (b) by step 4.1. If $e=0$ then $h=0$; conversely if $h=0$ then $k\times e=0$, so the two-vector family $(k,e)$ is linearly dependent over the field $\mathbb C$: writing the vanishing minors of $(k\ e)$ — if the coordinate $k_l\ne0$ then $e_j=(e_l/k_l)k_j$ for the two other coordinates $j$ and also for $l$ — gives $e=\alpha k$ with $\alpha=e_l/k_l\in\mathbb C$ by [F3]; then $0=k\cdot e=\alpha(k\cdot k)$ and $k\cdot k=\varepsilon\mu\omega^2+i\mu\sigma\omega\ne0$ (its real part is $\varepsilon\mu\omega^2>0$ by [F3]) force $\alpha=0$, so $e=0$. Thus $(e,h)=(0,0)$ if and only if $e=0$. For the solution set with $(k,\omega)$ on the dispersion relation: every solution $(e,h)$ of (b) has $k\cdot e=0$ and $h=k\times e/(\omega\mu)$ by step 5.1, so it lies in the displayed set; conversely every element of that set is a solution by the first part of this step, and the correspondence $e\mapsto(e,k\times e/(\omega\mu))$ is injective, so the solution set is exactly $\{(e,k\times e/(\omega\mu)):k\cdot e=0\}$. If $k_l\ne0$ the map $e\mapsto(e_j)_{j\ne l}\in\mathbb C^2$ is a bijection from $\{e:k\cdot e=0\}$ onto $\mathbb C^2$ with inverse $(e_j)_{j\ne l}\mapsto e$ where $e_l=-\bigl(\sum_{j\ne l}k_je_j\bigr)/k_l$, both maps being linear over $\mathbb C$; hence that solution space is a complex two-dimensional space. Off the dispersion relation, if (b) holds then either $e=0$, which gives $h=k\times e/(\omega\mu)=0$, or $e\ne0$, which by step 5.1 forces the dispersion relation; so the only solution is $e=h=0$. [step 4.1, step 5.1, F3, F5, algebra]

6.2 [Lossless pointwise identities in (d)] Assume $\sigma=0$ and $k\in\mathbb R^3$ with $e\ne0$, so the four conditions of (b) hold and (c) applies by steps 4.1 and 5.1. From $k\cdot k=|k|^2=\varepsilon\mu\omega^2$ and $|k|\ge0$ we get $|k|=\omega(\varepsilon\mu)^{1/2}$ by [F10], which is $k(\omega)=\omega/v$ by [F7]. For every $(x,t)$: $k\cdot E=k\cdot\operatorname{Re}(e\,e^{i\theta})=\operatorname{Re}\bigl((k\cdot e)e^{i\theta}\bigr)=0$ and $k\cdot B=\mu\operatorname{Re}\bigl((k\cdot h)e^{i\theta}\bigr)=0$ by [F1] and the reality of $k$; further $B=\mu H=\mu\operatorname{Re}\bigl((k\times e)/(\omega\mu)e^{i\theta}\bigr)=\operatorname{Re}\bigl((k\times e)e^{i\theta}\bigr)/\omega=k\times\operatorname{Re}(e\,e^{i\theta})/\omega=k\times E/\omega=(\hat k\times E)/v$ because $k$ is real and $|k|/\omega=1/v$ by [F7]; and $E\cdot B=E\cdot(\hat k\times E)/v=\langle E,\hat k\times E\rangle/v=\det[E,\hat k,E]/v=0$ by [F5] because a determinant with two equal columns vanishes. 7.1 [Evanescent factorisation in (e)] Assume the hypotheses of (e). Since $k=\kappa_0+i\beta n'$ with $\kappa_0,n'\in\mathbb R^3$ and $\kappa_0\cdot n'=0$, $|n'|=1$, the bilinear extension of [F5] gives $k\cdot x=\kappa_0\cdot x+i\beta\,n'\cdot x$ for real $x$ and $k\cdot k=|\kappa_0|^2-\beta^2$ (the cross terms $2i\beta\,\kappa_0\cdot n'$ vanish), so the dispersion relation gives $|\kappa_0|^2=\varepsilon\mu\omega^2+\beta^2>0$ and hence $\kappa_0\ne0$. Writing $\varphi:=\kappa_0\cdot x-\omega t\in\mathbb R$ and $b:=\beta\,n'\cdot x\in\mathbb R$, the identity $i\theta=i(k\cdot x-\omega t)=-b+i\varphi$ combines with the addition law and real extension of [F2] to give $e^{i\theta}=\exp(-b+i\varphi)=e^{-b}\exp(i\varphi)$; the Cartesian form of [F2] gives $\exp(i\varphi)=\cos\varphi+i\sin\varphi$, and the coordinate formula for the real part of a product in [F1] and [F3] then gives, componentwise, $\operatorname{Re}\bigl(e\,e^{i\theta}\bigr)=e^{-\beta n'\cdot x}\bigl[(\operatorname{Re}e)\cos\varphi-(\operatorname{Im}e)\sin\varphi\bigr]=e^{-\beta n'\cdot x}\operatorname{Re}\bigl(e\,e^{i\varphi}\bigr)$, the last equality being the same coordinate formula read backwards. This is the stated factorisation, and $h=k\times e/(\omega\mu)$ by the hypothesis of (e); step 6.1 verifies the amplitude conditions. [given, step 6.1, F2, F3, F5, algebra]

7.2 [The cross-product norm identity] For real vectors $u,v\in\mathbb R^3$ the component expansion gives $|u\times v|^2=|u|^2|v|^2-\langle u,v\rangle^2$: both sides equal $\sum_{j\ne l}u_j^2v_l^2-2\sum_{j<l}u_ju_lv_jv_l$ by the coordinate formulas of [F5]. Applying this with $u=\hat k$ and $v=E$, where $|\hat k|=1$ since $|k|=\omega(\varepsilon\mu)^{1/2}\ne0$ and $\langle\hat k,E\rangle=(k\cdot E)/|k|=0$ by step 6.2, gives $|\hat k\times E|^2=|E|^2$, hence $|B|=|\hat k\times E|/v=|E|/v$ by step 6.2 and the positivity of $v$ in [F7]. Write $e=a+ib$ with real $a,b$. Since $k$ is real and $k\cdot e=0$, $k\cdot a=k\cdot b=0$. Applying the real norm identity separately to $(k,a)$ and $(k,b)$ gives $|k\times e|^2=|k\times a|^2+|k\times b|^2=|k|^2(|a|^2+|b|^2)=|k|^2|e|^2$. Thus $|h|=|k\times e|/(\omega\mu)=|k||e|/(\omega\mu)$, so $|h|=\omega(\varepsilon\mu)^{1/2}|e|/(\omega\mu)=|e|(\varepsilon/\mu)^{1/2}=|e|/Z$ by [F7]. [step 6.2, F5, F7, algebra]

8.1 [Evanescent averaged flux in (e)] By step 7.1 the fields are $E=e^{-b}\operatorname{Re}(e\,e^{i\varphi})$ and $H=e^{-b}\operatorname{Re}(h\,e^{i\varphi})$ with $\varphi=\kappa_0\cdot x-\omega t$, so $E\times H=e^{-2b}\operatorname{Re}(e\,e^{-i\omega t'})\times\operatorname{Re}(h\,e^{-i\omega t'})$ evaluated at $t'=t-\kappa_0\cdot x/\omega$, and the period average in $t$ equals that in $t'$; the cross-product form of [F9] applied with $a=e$, $d=h$ gives $\langle S\rangle=e^{-2\beta n'\cdot x}\tfrac12\operatorname{Re}(e\times\overline h)$. Since $\overline h=\overline{k\times e}/(\omega\mu)$ and $\overline{k\times e}=\overline{k}\times\overline e=(\kappa_0-i\beta n')\times\overline e$ by [F3], the triple-product expansion of [F5] gives $e\times\overline h=\bigl[\overline k(e\cdot\overline e)-\overline e\,(e\cdot\overline k)\bigr]/(\omega\mu)$, whose inner product with $n'$ is $\bigl[(\overline k\cdot n')|e|^2-(\overline e\cdot n')(e\cdot\overline k)\bigr]/(\omega\mu)$; here $\overline k\cdot n'=-i\beta$ and $e\cdot\overline k=\kappa_0\cdot e-i\beta\,(e\cdot n')$ by the bilinear expansion of [F5]. Hence $\operatorname{Re}\bigl[-\overline{(e\cdot n')}(e\cdot\overline k)\bigr]=-\operatorname{Re}\bigl[\overline{(e\cdot n')}(\kappa_0\cdot e)\bigr]-\operatorname{Re}\bigl[i\beta|e\cdot n'|^{2}\bigr]=-\operatorname{Re}\bigl[\overline{(e\cdot n')}\cdot\overline{(\kappa_0\cdot\overline e)}\bigr]=-\operatorname{Re}\bigl[(e\cdot n')(\kappa_0\cdot\overline e)\bigr]$, where the middle term vanishes as a purely imaginary number and the last identity uses $\overline{zw}=\overline z\,\overline w$ and $\operatorname{Re}\overline\zeta=\operatorname{Re}\zeta$ from [F3]; substituting into the previous display gives the stated normal component. [step 7.1, F3, F5, F9, algebra]

8.2 [Lossless averages in (d)] With $T=2\pi/\omega$, step 6.2 gives $E\cdot E=\operatorname{Re}(e_je^{i\theta})\operatorname{Re}(e_je^{i\theta})$ summed over $j$, and its period average is $\tfrac12\operatorname{Re}(e\cdot\overline e)=\tfrac12|e|^2$ by the vector form of [F9] applied with $a=d=e$ (the phase $e^{ik\cdot x}$ is constant in $t$ and cancels in the product $e^{ik\cdot x}e^{-ik\cdot x}=1$ by [F2]); similarly $\langle H\cdot H\rangle=\tfrac12|h|^2=\tfrac12|e|^2/Z^2$ by step 7.2. 9.1 [Vanishing on transverse amplitudes] Under the hypotheses of (e) with $k\cdot e=0$, the bilinear expansion of [F5] gives $0=k\cdot e=\kappa_0\cdot e+i\beta\,(n'\cdot e)$, so $\kappa_0\cdot\overline e=\overline{\kappa_0\cdot e}=\overline{-i\beta\,(n'\cdot e)}=i\beta\,\overline{(n'\cdot e)}=i\beta\,(n'\cdot\overline e)$ by [F3] and the reality of $\kappa_0$, and hence $(e\cdot n')(\kappa_0\cdot\overline e)=i\beta\,(e\cdot n')\overline{(e\cdot n')}=i\beta\,|e\cdot n'|^2$, a purely imaginary number; therefore its real part vanishes and step 8.1 gives $\langle S\cdot n'\rangle\equiv0$ for every transverse amplitude. 10.1 [The polarised evanescent cases] In the transverse-electric case $e=E_0(n'\times\hat\kappa_0)$ with $\hat\kappa_0=\kappa_0/|\kappa_0|$ and $E_0\ne0$: the triple-product expansion of [F5] gives $k\times(n'\times\hat\kappa_0)=(k\cdot\hat\kappa_0)n'-(k\cdot n')\hat\kappa_0=|\kappa_0|n'-i\beta\hat\kappa_0$ because $k\cdot\hat\kappa_0=\kappa_0\cdot\hat\kappa_0=|\kappa_0|$ and $k\cdot n'=i\beta$, and $k\cdot(n'\times\hat\kappa_0)=\langle n'\times\hat\kappa_0,k\rangle=\det[n',\hat\kappa_0,k]=\det[n',\hat\kappa_0,\kappa_0]+i\beta\det[n',\hat\kappa_0,n']=|\kappa_0|\det[n',\hat\kappa_0,\hat\kappa_0]+i\beta\cdot0=0$ because a determinant with two equal columns vanishes by [F5]; so this is a transverse amplitude with $h=E_0(|\kappa_0|n'-i\beta\hat\kappa_0)/(\omega\mu)$ and $\overline h=\overline E_0(|\kappa_0|n'+i\beta\hat\kappa_0)/(\omega\mu)$. With the positively oriented orthonormal triad $(\hat\kappa_0,\hat y',n')$ where $\hat y'=n'\times\hat\kappa_0$, the cross-product relations $\hat y'\times n'=\hat\kappa_0$ and $\hat y'\times\hat\kappa_0=-n'$ hold by [F5], so $e\times\overline h=|E_0|^2\bigl[|\kappa_0|\hat\kappa_0-i\beta n'\bigr]/(\omega\mu)$ and $\langle S\cdot\hat\kappa_0\rangle=e^{-2\beta n'\cdot x}|E_0|^2|\kappa_0|/(2\mu\omega)=e^{-2\beta n'\cdot x}|e|^2|\kappa_0|/(2\mu\omega)>0$ because $|e|^2=|E_0|^2\ne0$, $|\kappa_0|>0$ and $\mu,\omega,v>0$. In the transverse-magnetic case $h=H_0\hat y'$ with $H_0\ne0$ and $e=-(k\times\hat y')H_0/(\omega\varepsilon)$: the same expansion gives $k\times\hat y'=|\kappa_0|n'-i\beta\hat\kappa_0$, so $e=H_0(i\beta\hat\kappa_0-|\kappa_0|n')/(\omega\varepsilon)$; then $k\cdot e=H_0(i\beta\,k\cdot\hat\kappa_0-|\kappa_0|\,k\cdot n')/(\omega\varepsilon)=H_0(i\beta|\kappa_0|-|\kappa_0|i\beta)/(\omega\varepsilon)=0$, and $k\times e=H_0(i\beta\,k\times\hat\kappa_0-|\kappa_0|\,k\times n')/(\omega\varepsilon)$. Here $k\times n'=(\kappa_0+i\beta n')\times n'=\kappa_0\times n'=-|\kappa_0|\hat y'$ and $k\times\hat\kappa_0=(\kappa_0+i\beta n')\times\hat\kappa_0=i\beta(n'\times\hat\kappa_0)=i\beta\hat y'$ by [F5] and $\kappa_0\times\hat\kappa_0=0$, whence $k\times e=H_0\bigl((i\beta)^2+|\kappa_0|^2\bigr)\hat y'/(\omega\varepsilon)=H_0\bigl(|\kappa_0|^2-\beta^2\bigr)\hat y'/(\omega\varepsilon)=\omega\mu H_0\hat y'=\omega\mu h$, because $|\kappa_0|^2-\beta^2=k\cdot k=\varepsilon\mu\omega^2$ by step 7.1 and [F7]; so this data satisfies the amplitude conditions by step 4.1, and $\langle S\cdot\hat\kappa_0\rangle=e^{-2\beta n'\cdot x}|H_0|^2|\kappa_0|/(2\omega\varepsilon)=e^{-2\beta n'\cdot x}|h|^2|\kappa_0|/(2\omega\varepsilon)>0$ with $|h|^2=|H_0|^2\ne0$.
