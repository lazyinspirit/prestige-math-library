## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice, the Axiom of Countable Choice and the Axiom of
Dependent Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-countable-choice]],
[[def-dependent-choice]]), the choice hypotheses of the cited spectral and
Bochner suppliers. Fix $\hbar>0$, let $H\ne\{0\}$ be a complex Hilbert space
([[def-hilbert-space]]), let $K$ and $A$ be self-adjoint operators on $H$ with
spectral projection-valued measures $E_K$ and $E_A$
([[def-symmetric-self-adjoint-and-essentially-self-adjoint]],
[[thm-spectral-theorem-for-unbounded-self-adjoint-operators]],
[[def-projection-valued-measure]]), let $W\in\mathcal B(H)$ be unitary
([[def-bounded-linear-operator]],
[[def-self-adjoint-positive-unitary-and-normal-operator]]), and put
$$U_K(t):=e^{-itK/\hbar},\qquad U_A(s):=e^{-isA/\hbar}\qquad(s,t\in\mathbb R)$$
in the spectral calculus of [[def-unbounded-integral-against-a-pvm]], which are
strongly continuous one-parameter unitary groups
([[lem-self-adjoint-operator-generates-a-strongly-continuous-unitary-group]],
[[def-strongly-continuous-one-parameter-unitary-group]]). Write
$\Phi_K(g)=\int g\,dE_K$ for bounded Borel $g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb C$
([[thm-bounded-borel-pvm-integral]]), and let
$K_W:=WKW^{*}$ with $D(K_W):=WD(K)$. Then:

(a) $K_W$ is self-adjoint with spectral projection-valued measure
$$E_{K_W}(B)=WE_K(B)W^{*}\qquad(B\subseteq\mathbb R\ \text{Borel}),$$
its flow satisfies $e^{-itK_W/\hbar}=WU_K(t)W^{*}$ for every $t$, and the
conjugated family $s\mapsto WU_A(s)W^{*}$ is a strongly continuous
one-parameter unitary group whose infinitesimal generator is
$-\frac{i}{\hbar}WAW^{*}$ with domain $WD(A)$
([[def-infinitesimal-generator-of-a-unitary-group]]);

(b) the following are equivalent:

  (i) $U_A(s)U_K(t)=U_K(t)U_A(s)$ for all $s,t\in\mathbb R$;

  (ii) $E_A(B)E_K(C)=E_K(C)E_A(B)$ for all Borel sets $B,C\subseteq\mathbb R$;

  (iii) $U_K(t)D(A)=D(A)$ and $U_K(t)AU_K(t)^{*}=A$ for every $t\in\mathbb R$;

(c) if a bounded $B\in\mathcal B(H)$ satisfies $BU_K(t)=U_K(t)B$ for every
$t\in\mathbb R$, then $B\Phi_K(g)=\Phi_K(g)B$ for every bounded Borel
$g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb C$; in particular $BE_K(C)=E_K(C)B$ for every Borel
$C\subseteq\mathbb R$;

(d) if (i)-(iii) of (b) hold, then $E_A(B)U_K(t)=U_K(t)E_A(B)$ for every Borel
$B\subseteq\mathbb R$ and every $t\in\mathbb R$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, the Axiom of Countable Choice and the Axiom of
Dependent Choice; $\hbar>0$; a nonzero complex Hilbert space $H$; self-adjoint
$K,A$ with spectral PVMs $E_K,E_A$; a unitary $W$; the flows $U_K,U_A$;
bounded Borel functions $g,h$; vectors $x,y\in H$; real $s,t$; Borel sets
$B,C\subseteq\mathbb R$.

[F1] **Flow calculus.** For a self-adjoint operator $L$ with spectral PVM $E_L$
and flow $U_L(\tau)=e^{-i\tau L/\hbar}$: $U_L$ is a strongly continuous
one-parameter unitary group with $U_L(\tau)^{*}=U_L(-\tau)$, $U_L(\tau)D(L)=D(L)$
and $LU_L(\tau)=U_L(\tau)L$ on $D(L)$; the difference quotient
$\frac1\tau\bigl(U_L(\tau)x-x\bigr)$ converges as $\tau\to0$ exactly for
$x\in D(L)$, with limit $-\frac{i}{\hbar}Lx$; and for bounded Borel $f$ one has
$f(L)U_L(\tau)=U_L(\tau)f(L)$ with $f(L)=\Phi_{E_L}(f)$
([[lem-self-adjoint-operator-generates-a-strongly-continuous-unitary-group]],
[[thm-unbounded-borel-functional-calculus]],
[[thm-pvm-integral-is-a-star-homomorphism]]).

[F2] **Spectral calculus.** $\Phi_{E_L}$ is a unital $\ast$-homomorphism from
the bounded Borel functions to $\mathcal B(H)$, $\|\Phi_{E_L}(f)\|\le\sup|f|$,
$E_L(B)=\Phi_{E_L}(\mathbf 1_B)$, and the map $B\mapsto E_L(B)$ is a regular
projection-valued measure whose scalar measures $\langle E_L(\cdot)x,x\rangle$
are finite regular Borel measures
([[thm-bounded-borel-pvm-integral]],
[[thm-pvm-integral-is-a-star-homomorphism]],
[[def-projection-valued-measure]],
[[lem-scalar-and-complex-measures-from-a-pvm]],
[[def-regular-borel-measure-on-an-lch-space]]).

[F3] **Uniqueness of the spectral measure.** If a regular projection-valued
measure $F$ on the Borel sets of $\mathbb R$ satisfies $\int\lambda\,dF(\lambda)x=Lx$
for all $x\in D(L)$ and $D(L)=\{x:\int\lambda^2\,dF_x(\lambda)<\infty\}$, then
$F=E_L$ ([[thm-spectral-theorem-for-unbounded-self-adjoint-operators]]).

[F4] **Stone's theorem.** $T\mapsto e^{itT}$ is a bijection from the self-adjoint
operators onto the strongly continuous one-parameter unitary groups; two such
groups that agree at every real time have the same values, hence the same
generator, and a group is determined by its generator
([[thm-stone-one-parameter-unitary-groups]],
[[def-infinitesimal-generator-of-a-unitary-group]]).

[F5] **Laplace resolvents.** For a strongly continuous one-parameter unitary
group $U$ with infinitesimal generator $G$ and $\lambda>0$, the vectorwise
Bochner integrals
$$Q_\pm(\lambda)x:=\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}U(\pm t)x\,dt\qquad(x\in H)$$
define bounded operators with $\|Q_\pm(\lambda)\|\le1/\lambda$ and
$(\lambda\mp G)Q_\pm(\lambda)=I$, $Q_\pm(\lambda)(\lambda\mp G)=I$ on $D(G)$.
Bounded linear maps on $H$ commute with these $H$-valued integrals
([[lem-laplace-resolvents-of-a-unitary-group]],
[[thm-bounded-linear-maps-commute-with-bochner-integration]]).

[F6] **Density of the resolvent algebra.** For a closed nonempty $K\subseteq\mathbb R$
the $\ast$-subalgebra generated by the single function $\lambda\mapsto(\lambda-z)^{-1}$,
$z\in\mathbb C\setminus K$, is dense in the algebra $C_\infty(K)$ of continuous
functions vanishing at infinity; and for a finite Radon measure $\mu$ on
$\mathbb R$ the space $C_c(\mathbb R)\subseteq C_\infty(\mathbb R)$ is dense in
$L^p(\mu)$ for $1\le p<\infty$
([[thm-complex-stone-weierstrass-self-adjoint]],
[[thm-c-c-is-dense-in-l-p-for-radon-measures]]).


## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; transport the spectral data through the unitary
conjugation, prove the equivalence (i) - (iii) by comparing generators of
conjugated groups, and obtain the commutation with spectral projections through
the Laplace resolvent and the density of the resolvent algebra.

**Given:** The data of the statement and [F1]-[F6].

1.1 [Well-definedness, density and symmetry of $K_W$] The domain $D(K_W)=WD(K)$ is a linear subspace, dense in $H$ because $D(K)$ is dense and $W$ is surjective, and $K_Wx=WKW^{*}x$ is defined and linear on it. For $x=Wu$, $y=Wv$ with $u,v\in D(K)$ one has $\langle K_Wx,y\rangle=\langle WKu,Wv\rangle=\langle Ku,v\rangle=\langle u,Kv\rangle=\langle x,K_Wy\rangle$ because $W$ is unitary and $K$ is symmetric; hence $K_W$ is symmetric. [F1, given, algebra]

1.2 [(ii) implies (i)] Assume (ii). For simple Borel functions $g=\sum_ic_i\mathbf 1_{B_i}$ and $h=\sum_jd_j\mathbf 1_{C_j}$ the identities $E_A(B_i)E_K(C_j)=E_K(C_j)E_A(B_i)$ give $\Phi_A(g)\Phi_K(h)=\sum_{i,j}c_id_jE_A(B_i)E_K(C_j)=\sum_{i,j}c_id_jE_K(C_j)E_A(B_i)=\Phi_K(h)\Phi_A(g)$. Given bounded Borel $g,h$, choose simple $g_n\to g$, $h_n\to h$ uniformly; then $\Phi_A(g_n)\to\Phi_A(g)$ and $\Phi_K(h_n)\to\Phi_K(h)$ in operator norm by [F2], so the identities pass to the limits, giving $\Phi_A(g)\Phi_K(h)=\Phi_K(h)\Phi_A(g)$. Taking $g(\lambda)=e^{-is\lambda/\hbar}$ and $h(\mu)=e^{-it\mu/\hbar}$ and applying [F2] gives $U_A(s)U_K(t)=U_K(t)U_A(s)$ for all $s,t$, which is (i). [F2, given, algebra]

1.3 [Commutation with the flow implies commutation with the calculus] Let $B\in\mathcal B(H)$ satisfy $BU_K(t)=U_K(t)B$ for all $t$. Apply [F5] to $U_K$ and $G_K=-\frac{i}{\hbar}K$ with domain $D(K)$. For every $x\in H$ and either sign, $$BQ_\pm(\lambda)x=B\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}U_K(\pm t)x\,dt=\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}BU_K(\pm t)x\,dt=\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}U_K(\pm t)Bx\,dt=Q_\pm(\lambda)Bx.$$ Thus $B$ commutes with both resolvents, using only the vectorwise integrals supplied by [F5]. Now $Q_+(\lambda)=(\lambda+\frac{i}{\hbar}K)^{-1}=\frac{\hbar}{i}(K-i\hbar\lambda)^{-1}$ and $Q_-(\lambda)=(\lambda-\frac{i}{\hbar}K)^{-1}=i\hbar(K+i\hbar\lambda)^{-1}$ are nonzero scalar multiples of the resolvents of $K$ at $i\hbar\lambda$ and $-i\hbar\lambda$. Choosing $\lambda=1$ shows that $B$ commutes with $g_\pm(K)$ for $g_\pm(\mu)=(\mu\pm i\hbar)^{-1}$ and hence with every polynomial in them and their conjugates, that is with every element of the $\ast$-subalgebra generated by $g_+$. [F5, given, algebra]

2.1 [Adjoint and self-adjointness] Let $y\in D(K_W^{*})$ and put $z=K_W^{*}y$. For every $u\in D(K)$ the vector $Wu$ lies in $D(K_W)$ with $K_W(Wu)=WKu$, so $\langle Ku,W^{*}y\rangle=\langle WKu,y\rangle=\langle K_W(Wu),y\rangle=\langle Wu,z\rangle=\langle u,W^{*}z\rangle$; the functional $u\mapsto\langle Ku,W^{*}y\rangle$ is therefore bounded on $D(K)$, whence $W^{*}y\in D(K^{*})=D(K)$ and $K^{*}W^{*}y=KW^{*}y=W^{*}z$. Applying the unitary $W$ gives $y\in WD(K)=D(K_W)$ and $K_Wy=z$. Hence $D(K_W^{*})\subseteq D(K_W)$ with agreement there, and with step 1.1 the operator $K_W$ is self-adjoint. [step 1.1, F1, given, algebra]

2.2 [Continuous vanishing functions] Let $f\in C_\infty(\sigma(K))$, a closed subset of $\mathbb R$. By [F6] there are elements $p_n$ of the $\ast$-subalgebra generated by $\mu\mapsto(\mu-i\hbar)^{-1}$ with $\sup_{\sigma(K)}|p_n-f|\to0$. Each $p_n(K)=\Phi_K(p_n)$ commutes with $B$ by step 1.3, and $\|\Phi_K(p_n)-\Phi_K(f)\|\le\sup_{\sigma(K)}|p_n-f|\to0$ by the norm bound of [F2], so $\Phi_K(f)$ commutes with $B$. [step 1.3, F2, F6, algebra]

3.1 [Spectral measure transport] Put $F(B):=WE_K(B)W^{*}$ for Borel $B\subseteq\mathbb R$. Each $F(B)$ is an orthogonal projection as the conjugate of one; $F(\varnothing)=0$ and $F(\mathbb R)=I$; finite additivity and strong countable additivity follow from those of $E_K$ and boundedness of $W$; and $F$ is regular because its scalar measure in $x$ is $\langle F(B)x,x\rangle=\langle E_K(B)W^{*}x,W^{*}x\rangle$, a finite regular Borel measure by [F2]. For any $x\in H$, its scalar measure for $F$ is the scalar measure of $E_K$ at $W^{*}x$, so $\int\lambda^{2}\,dF_x(\lambda)<\infty$ exactly when $W^{*}x\in D(K)$ by the spectral theorem; this is exactly $x\in WD(K)=D(K_W)$. If $x=Wu$ with $u\in D(K)$, bounded spectral calculus first gives $\int_{[-n,n]}\lambda\,dF(\lambda)x=W\int_{[-n,n]}\lambda\,dE_K(\lambda)u$ for every $n$, and the unbounded-integral definition lets $n\to\infty$ in $H$ on both sides, giving $\int\lambda\,dF(\lambda)x=WKu=K_Wx$. Thus $F$ satisfies both hypotheses of [F3] for $K_W$, so $F=E_{K_W}$. [step 2.1, F2, F3, given, algebra]

3.2 [Bounded Borel functions] Let $g$ be bounded Borel and fix $x\in H$. The finite regular measure $\mu:=E_{K,x}+E_{K,Bx}$ is a sum of the scalar measures at $x$ and $Bx$, and is regular by [F2]. By [F6] choose $f_n\in C_c(\mathbb R)\subseteq C_\infty(\mathbb R)$ with $\int|g-f_n|^2\,d\mu\to0$. The PVM norm identity in [F2] then gives both $(\Phi_K(g)-\Phi_K(f_n))x\to0$ and $(\Phi_K(g)-\Phi_K(f_n))Bx\to0$ in $H$. Since $\Phi_K(f_n)$ commutes with $B$ by step 2.2, $$\|\,[B,\Phi_K(g)]x\,\|\le\|B\|\,\|(\Phi_K(g)-\Phi_K(f_n))x\|+\|(\Phi_K(g)-\Phi_K(f_n))Bx\|\longrightarrow0,$$ hence $B\Phi_K(g)=\Phi_K(g)B$. Taking $g=\mathbf 1_C$ and using $E_K(C)=\Phi_K(\mathbf 1_C)$ from [F2] gives $BE_K(C)=E_K(C)B$. This proves (c). [step 2.2, F2, F6, given, algebra]

4.1 [Flow and generator transport] By [F2] applied to the bounded Borel functions $f_t(\lambda)=e^{-it\lambda/\hbar}$, $e^{-itK_W/\hbar}=\Phi_{E_{K_W}}(f_t)=W\Phi_{E_K}(f_t)W^{*}=WU_K(t)W^{*}$, which is the displayed flow identity. For arbitrary $x\in H$, the difference quotients of $s\mapsto WU_A(s)W^{*}x$ at $0$ equal $W\frac1s\bigl(U_A(s)W^{*}x-W^{*}x\bigr)$. Since $W$ is an isometry, this limit exists exactly when $W^{*}x\in D(A)$ by [F1], and then it equals $-\frac{i}{\hbar}WAW^{*}x$. Therefore the conjugated group has infinitesimal generator $-\frac{i}{\hbar}WAW^{*}$ on the exact domain $WD(A)$; it is strongly continuous because $W$ is bounded and $U_A$ is. This proves (a). [step 3.1, F1, F2, given, algebra]

4.2 [(iii) implies (ii)] Assume (iii) and fix $t$. By (iii), $U_K(t)AU_K(t)^{*}=A$; by uniqueness of the spectral measure [F3] and step 3.1 applied with $W=U_K(t)$, $$E_A(B)=E_{U_K(t)AU_K(t)^{*}}(B)=U_K(t)E_A(B)U_K(t)^{*}$$ for every Borel $B$, so $E_A(B)$ commutes with $U_K(t)$ and with $U_K(t)^{*}=U_K(-t)$. Since $E_A(B)$ is bounded and commutes with the flow $U_K$, step (c) with $B:=E_A(B)$ gives $E_A(B)\Phi_K(g)=\Phi_K(g)E_A(B)$ for every bounded Borel $g$; taking $g=\mathbf 1_C$ gives $E_A(B)E_K(C)=E_K(C)E_A(B)$, which is (ii). [step 3.1, step 1.3, step 3.2, F2, F3, given, algebra]

5.1 [(i) implies (iii)] Assume (i), fix $t\in\mathbb R$, and put $V(s):=U_K(t)^{*}U_A(s)U_K(t)$; by (i) $V(s)=U_A(s)$ for every $s$. The family $V$ is a strongly continuous unitary group, and by step 4.1 with $W=U_K(t)^{*}$ its infinitesimal generator is $-\frac{i}{\hbar}U_K(t)^{*}AU_K(t)$ with domain $U_K(t)^{*}D(A)$; the right-hand side $U_A$ has generator $-\frac{i}{\hbar}A$ with domain $D(A)$ by [F1]. Since the two groups coincide, their generators coincide by [F4], so $U_K(t)^{*}D(A)=D(A)$ and $U_K(t)^{*}AU_K(t)=A$. Applying the unitary $U_K(t)$ on both sides gives $U_K(t)D(A)=D(A)$ and $U_K(t)AU_K(t)^{*}=A$, which is (iii). [step 4.1, F1, F4, given, algebra]

5.2 [(iii) implies (i)] Assume (iii) and fix $t$. Put $V(s):=U_K(t)^{*}U_A(s)U_K(t)$. By step 4.1 with $W=U_K(t)^{*}$, the generator of $V$ is $-\frac{i}{\hbar}U_K(t)^{*}AU_K(t)=-\frac{i}{\hbar}A$ with domain $U_K(t)^{*}D(A)=D(A)$, which is the generator of $U_A$ by [F1]; hence $V=U_A$ by [F4] and $U_A(s)U_K(t)=U_K(t)U_A(s)$ for all $s$. [step 4.1, F1, F4, given, algebra]

6.1 [Assembly] Steps 1.2, 5.1, 5.2 and 4.2 give the implications (ii)$\Rightarrow$(i)$\Rightarrow$(iii)$\Rightarrow$(ii), so the three conditions are equivalent, which is (b). Under these conditions, step 4.2 in its first display already gives $E_A(B)U_K(t)=U_K(t)E_A(B)$ for all Borel $B$ and all $t$, which is (d): the displayed identity $E_A(B)=U_K(t)E_A(B)U_K(t)^{*}$ multiplied on the right by $U_K(t)$ yields $E_A(B)U_K(t)=U_K(t)E_A(B)$. [step 1.2, step 5.1, step 5.2, step 4.2, algebra] ∎




## Remarks

- **Why the hypotheses are formulated with groups.** The equivalence of (i),
  (ii) and (iii) is the precise sense in which "a self-adjoint quantity
  commutes with the energy" may be used: strong commutation of the spectral
  measures, commutation of the flows, and invariance of the domain of the
  quantity under the flow. A formal commutator identity on a small domain is
  not among the equivalent conditions and is not used.
- **Choice.** The Axiom of Choice is the hypothesis of the spectral theorem and
  of Stone's theorem; Countable and Dependent Choice are the hypotheses of the
  Bochner and density suppliers used in clause (c).
