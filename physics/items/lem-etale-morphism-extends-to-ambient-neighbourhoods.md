## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $K$ be a field of characteristic zero and let $\iota_U\colon U\hookrightarrow\mathbb A^m_K$ and $\iota_V\colon V\hookrightarrow\mathbb A^n_K$ be closed immersions of affine $K$-varieties of finite type ([[def-closed-immersion-schemes]], [[def-integral-scheme]]). Let $\varphi\colon U\to V$ be étale at a $K$-rational point $u_0$, and put $v_0=\varphi(u_0)$. Choose affine coordinates on the two ambient spaces carrying $u_0$ and $v_0$ to their origins.

Then there is a closed subscheme $X\subseteq\mathbb A^n_K\times_K\mathbb A^m_K$ containing the graph copy of $U$, smooth at $(v_0,u_0)$, such that the projection
$$\Phi:=\operatorname{pr}_1|_X\colon X\to\mathbb A^n_K$$
is étale at $(v_0,u_0)$ and restricts on $U$ to $\iota_V\circ\varphi$. Thus $\varphi$ extends to a morphism of smooth ambient varieties étale at the chosen point.

After extending the ground field so the point is rational, the usual local factorization of a smooth morphism as an étale morphism followed by a projection gives the corresponding smooth ambient extension locally. This is the reduction used in the source's proof of functoriality for smooth morphisms.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice; a characteristic-zero field $K$; closed immersions $U\hookrightarrow\mathbb A^m_K$ and $V\hookrightarrow\mathbb A^n_K$ of affine varieties of finite type; and a morphism $\varphi\colon U\to V$ étale at the $K$-rational point $u_0$, with image $v_0$.

[F1] [[lem-etale-formal-local-isomorphism]]: since $u_0$ and $v_0$ are $K$-rational, their residue fields are both $K$, and the étale local map induces an isomorphism of completed local rings.

[F2] [[def-closed-immersion-schemes]]: the graph map $U\to\mathbb A^n_K\times_K\mathbb A^m_K$, $u\mapsto(\iota_V(\varphi(u)),\iota_U(u))$, is a closed immersion; its ideal is finitely generated and contains the equations of the target $V$.

[F3] [[def-ag-standard-smooth-algebra]], [[thm-ag-standard-smooth-geometric-regularity]], [[def-etale-morphism-schemes]]: if $m$ equations in the $y$-coordinates have invertible $m\times m$ Jacobian at the point, their zero scheme is standard smooth of relative dimension $0$ over $\mathbb A^n_K$, hence its projection to $\mathbb A^n_K$ is étale there.

[F4] [[lem-ag-geometrically-regular-fibres-local-presentation]], [[def-relative-dimension-smooth-morphism]]: a smooth germ of relative dimension $r$ has a standard smooth local presentation with $r$ free parameters; separating these parameters from the equations factors it locally as an étale germ to the product with $\mathbb A^r$ followed by projection.

[F5] [[thm-faithful-flatness-of-jacobson-adic-completion]]: the maximal-ideal completion of a Noetherian local ring is faithfully flat, so equality of finite defining ideals can be checked after completion.

## Proof

1.1 Embed the source by its graph. Use coordinates $x_1,\dots,x_n$ on $\mathbb A^n_K$ and $y_1,\dots,y_m$ on $\mathbb A^m_K$, centered at $v_0$ and $u_0$. Write the equations of $V$ as $f_1(x),\dots,f_l(x)$ and choose further equations $h_1(x,y),\dots,h_s(x,y)$ for the graph copy of $U$. Put $S=\widehat{\mathcal O}_{V,v_0}$. By [F1] the induced map $S\to\widehat{\mathcal O}_{U,u_0}$ is an isomorphism. In the completed local ring of $V\times_K\mathbb A^m_K$, identified with $S\llbracket y_1,\dots,y_m\rrbracket$, the graph map therefore sends each $y_j$ to some $g_j\in S$ with zero residue, and its ideal is generated by $y_1-g_1,\dots,y_m-g_m$. The images of the $h_j$ generate this graph ideal, so their classes span its conormal space modulo the maximal-ideal multiple. The classes of $y_j-g_j$ are a basis of that $m$-dimensional $K$-vector space. Hence some $m$ of the $h_j$ have coefficient matrix on these generators invertible at the point; since each $g_j$ has zero residue, this matrix is their $y$-Jacobian there. [F1, F2]

2.1 Choose equations for the ambient extension. Select $m$ of the equations $h_j$, say $h_{i_1},\dots,h_{i_m}$, whose $y$-linear parts form a basis of that vector space, and define $$X:=V(h_{i_1},\dots,h_{i_m})\subseteq\mathbb A^n_K\times_K\mathbb A^m_K.$$ Every point of the graph copy of $U$ satisfies these equations, so $U$ is a closed subscheme of $X$. The determinant of the matrix $\bigl(\partial h_{i_a}/\partial y_b\bigr)_{a,b=1}^m$ is nonzero at $(v_0,u_0)$. [F2, step 1.1]

3.1 The projection is étale. The presentation of $X$ from step 2.1 has $m$ variables $y_1,\dots,y_m$ and $m$ equations with invertible Jacobian minor, so it is standard smooth of relative dimension $0$ over $\mathbb A^n_K$ at $(v_0,u_0)$. By [F3], $\Phi\colon X\to\mathbb A^n_K$ is étale there. Consequently $X$ is smooth at $(v_0,u_0)$, and the projection restricts on $U$ to $\iota_V\circ\varphi$ by construction. Moreover the germ of $U$ equals that of $X\times_{\mathbb A^n_K}V$: in $S\llbracket y_1,\dots,y_m\rrbracket$ the selected equations generate the graph ideal, because their coefficient matrix on $y_j-g_j$ is invertible in the complete local ring. Thus the two defining ideals agree after completion, and [F5] makes them agree in the local ring itself. Their quotient is a finite module, so it vanishes on a neighbourhood of the point. Shrinking $X$ there therefore makes this square Cartesian. [F3, F5, step 2.1]

4.1 Smooth morphisms. Let $W\to S$ be smooth at a geometric point. By [F4], after a local standard smooth chart it factors as an étale germ $W\to S\times\mathbb A^r$ followed by projection. After extending the ground field so the point is rational, embed the two affine germs in affine spaces and apply steps 1.1–3.1 to the étale germ; composing the resulting ambient étale map with the projection gives a smooth ambient extension of the original germ. [F4, step 3.1] ∎

## Remarks

- The source proof uses coordinates on $\mathbb A^n\times\mathbb A^m$, hence the ambient dimension is $n+m$. Its printed line saying $X\subset\mathbb A^m$ is inconsistent with those coordinates and with its projection $\mathbb A^{m+n}\to\mathbb A^n$; the statement above follows the coordinate proof.
- The ambient extension is proved for the source's affine-embedding and rational-point setting. The smooth-morphism consequence is used after passage to a geometric point; no claim is made here that an arbitrary non-rational point over the original field is rational.
