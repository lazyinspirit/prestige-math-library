## Example

For $n\ge1$ the two extreme partitions of $n$ give the two extreme Young
permutation modules:

- for $\lambda=(n)$ there is exactly one tabloid, so $M^{(n)}$ is
  one-dimensional, and every $\sigma\in S_n$ fixes that tabloid; hence $M^{(n)}$
  is the trivial representation $\mathbb C$ of $S_n$;
- for $\lambda=(1^n)$ the row-equivalence classes are singletons and the
  tabloids are the $n!$ tableaux of shape $(1^n)$, so $M^{(1^n)}$ has a basis
  indexed by $S_n$ on which $S_n$ acts by left multiplication; hence
  $M^{(1^n)}$ is the regular representation $\mathbb C[S_n]$.

The row module is generated by its single tabloid and the column module has
dimension $n!=\dim\mathbb C[S_n]$. For $n=1$ the two partitions coincide,
$(1)=(1^1)$, and both descriptions give the one-dimensional module; for $n=0$
the only partition is $\varnothing$, there is one empty tabloid, and
$M^\varnothing=\mathbb C$ is again the one-dimensional trivial module, which is
also the regular module of the trivial group $S_0=\{1\}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** For $n\ge1$, the partitions $(n)$ and $(1^n)$ of $n$ and their standard row-filled tableaux $t_0$; separately, for $n=0$, the empty partition and empty tableau.

[F1] For $\lambda\vdash n$ the standard Young subgroup $S_\lambda$ is the subgroup preserving each consecutive block of sizes $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_k$; the Young permutation module $M^\lambda$ is the complex vector space with the tabloids $\{t\}$ of shape $\lambda$ as basis, $S_n$ acting by $\sigma\cdot\{t\}=\{\sigma\cdot t\}$; the stabilizer of $\{t\}$ is $R_t$ and the stabilizer of the tabloid $\{t_0\}$ of the standard row-filled tableau is $S_\lambda$, so $\Omega_\lambda=\{\{\sigma\cdot t_0\}:\sigma\in S_n\}$ ([[def-young-subgroup-tabloid-and-permutation-module]]).

[F2] For every $\lambda\vdash n$ there is an isomorphism of complex $S_n$-modules $M^\lambda\cong\mathbb C[S_n/S_\lambda]$, the permutation representation of $S_n$ on the left coset set $S_n/S_\lambda$, and hence $M^\lambda\cong\operatorname{Ind}_{S_\lambda}^{S_n}\mathbf 1$; this holds also for $n=0$ ([[lem-young-permutation-module-is-induced-from-the-trivial-character]]).

[F3] The trivial representation of a finite group $G$ over $\mathbb C$ is the one-dimensional representation on $\mathbb C$ in which every $g$ acts as the identity, and the regular representation is the representation on $\mathbb C[G]$ given by left multiplication by the basis units, $g\cdot[h]=[gh]$ ([[def-trivial-regular-and-permutation-representations]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 For $\lambda=(n)$ the blocks of [F1] are the single block $\{1,\dots,n\}$, so $S_{(n)}=S_n$, and every $\lambda$-tableau has the single row set $\{1,\dots,n\}$; hence any two $\lambda$-tableaux are row equivalent, there is exactly one tabloid, and $M^{(n)}$ is one-dimensional with that tabloid as basis. [given, F1]

1.2 For $\lambda=(1^n)$ the blocks are the $n$ singletons $\{1\},\dots,\{n\}$, so $S_{(1^n)}=\{1\}$, and the row set of row $i$ of a tableau is the singleton carrying its single entry, so two tableaux are row equivalent exactly when they agree entrywise and each tabloid is a singleton class; a $\lambda$-tableau is a bijection from the $n$ boxes onto $\{1,\dots,n\}$, so there are $n!$ of them, and the map $\sigma\mapsto\sigma\cdot t_0$ from $S_n$ to the tableaux is a bijection, being injective because $t_0$ is surjective and surjective because $\sigma(t_0(i,j)):=t(i,j)$ defines a permutation, whence $\sigma\mapsto\{\sigma\cdot t_0\}$ is a bijection onto the tabloids with $\tau\cdot\{\sigma\cdot t_0\}=\{\tau\sigma\cdot t_0\}$ for all $\tau\in S_n$. [given, F1]

2.1 For $\lambda=(n)$ and any $\sigma\in S_n$ the tableau $\sigma\cdot t_0$ again has the single row set $\{1,\dots,n\}$, so $\sigma\cdot\{t_0\}=\{t_0\}$: every $\sigma$ acts as the identity on the one-dimensional $M^{(n)}$, which by [F3] is the trivial representation, in agreement with [F2] because $S_n/S_n$ is a single coset. [step 1.1, F2, F3]

2.2 Transporting the basis along the bijection of step 1.2 turns the action of [F1] into left multiplication on $\mathbb C[S_n]$, which by [F3] is the regular representation, so $M^{(1^n)}\cong\mathbb C[S_n]$; the coset description of [F2] reduces to the same statement because $S_{(1^n)}=\{1\}$ and the cosets $\sigma\{1\}$ are the singletons of $S_n$. [step 1.2, F1, F2, F3]

3.1 For $n=1$ the two partitions coincide, $(1)=(1^1)$, and both conclusions above give the same one-dimensional module; for $n=0$ the only partition is $\varnothing$, whose tabloid set consists of the single empty tabloid, so $M^\varnothing=\mathbb C$ is one-dimensional with trivial action and coincides with the regular module $\mathbb C[S_0]$ of the trivial group $S_0=\{1\}$. Thus $M^{(n)}$ is the trivial module and $M^{(1^n)}$ the regular module for every $n\ge1$, and for $n=0$ both descriptions give the same one-dimensional module. ∎ [step 2.1, step 2.2, F1, F3]
