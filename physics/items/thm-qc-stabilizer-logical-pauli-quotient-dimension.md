## Statement

Let $n\ge1$ and let $S$ be an $n$-qubit stabilizer group generated by $r$ independent
commuting Hermitian Pauli checks with $-I\notin S$. Then the logical Pauli
quotient $N(S)/(Z_nS)$ is a binary vector space of dimension $2(n-r)$.
Consequently, it is nontrivial exactly when the stabilizer code encodes at
least one qubit.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $n\ge1$, a stabilizer subgroup $S$, its binary symplectic label space,
and the normalizer/logical quotient convention.

[F1] [[def-qc-binary-pauli-label-and-quotient]]: quotienting phase-aware
Paulis by the scalar center identifies their binary labels with
$V_n=\mathbb F_2^{2n}$.

[F2] [[lem-qc-stabilizer-subgroup-label-space-and-order]]: the label image
$W$ of $S$ is an isotropic subspace of dimension $r$.

[F3] [[thm-qc-pauli-commutator-is-symplectic-pairing]]: a Pauli commutes
with every element of $S$ exactly when its label belongs to $W^\perp$.

[F4] [[def-qc-stabilizer-syndrome-and-logical-operators]]: the logical
quotient is $N(S)/(Z_nS)$.

[F5] [[thm-qc-stabilizer-projector-and-dimension]]: the code dimension is
$2^{n-r}$, so the encoded-qubit count is $k=n-r$.

[F6] [[def-qc-binary-symplectic-form]]: $\omega$ is nondegenerate on
$V_n$ and $\dim V_n=2n$.

[F7] [[thm-dimension-of-a-linear-subspace]]: every linearly independent
subset of a finite-dimensional subspace extends to a basis, with no choice
principle.

[F8] [[thm-rank-nullity]]: the dimension of a finite-dimensional domain is
the sum of the kernel dimension and image dimension.

[F9] [[thm-dual-family-is-a-basis-in-finite-dimension]]: the algebraic dual
of a finite-dimensional space has the same dimension.

## Proof

1.1 By [F1], phase classes of Paulis are the vectors of $V_n$. By [F2] the stabilizer labels form an $r$-dimensional subspace $W$. The commutator criterion [F3] identifies the normalizer labels exactly with $W^\perp$. The scalar phases label zero, so the subgroup $Z_nS$ has image $W$. Therefore $N(S)/(Z_nS)$ is identified with $W^\perp/W$. [F1, F2, F3, F4]

1.2 The nondegenerate pairing [F6] gives an injective map $J:V_n\to V_n^*$, $J(v)=\omega(v,\cdot)$. Restricting functionals from $V_n$ to $W$ is onto: extend a basis of $W$ to one of $V_n$ by [F7], then extend each functional by assigning zero to the added basis vectors. Since $\dim V_n^*=\dim V_n=2n$ by [F9], injectivity and rank-nullity [F8] make $J$ onto. Thus the composite $V_n\to W^*$ is onto; its codomain has dimension $r$ by [F9], and its kernel is $W^\perp$. By [F8], $\dim W^\perp=2n-r$. [F6, F7, F8, F9, construct]

2.1 Since $W\subseteq W^\perp$, the quotient dimension is $\dim(W^\perp/W)=(2n-r)-r=2(n-r)$. By [F5], $k=n-r$ is the number of encoded qubits; hence this dimension is $2k$, which is zero exactly when $k=0$. [F2, F5, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
