## Statement

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over a field $k$, let $W\subseteq V$
be a subspace of dimension $d$, and put $D=\Lambda^dW\subseteq\Lambda^dV$
([[def-kth-exterior-power-by-quotient]], [[def-kth-exterior-power-of-a-linear-map]],
[[cor-dimension-of-the-kth-exterior-power]]). Then for every $k$-algebra $R$ and
every $\alpha\in\operatorname{GL}(V_R)$ one has $\alpha W_R=W_R$ if and only if
$(\Lambda^d\alpha)(D_R)=D_R$. In particular, if $(V,r)$ is a rational
representation of an affine group scheme $G$ over $k$ and $G$ acts on
$\Lambda^dV$ by the exterior power
([[cor-the-top-exterior-power-acts-by-the-determinant]]), then the
scheme-theoretic stabilizer of $W$ in $G$ equals the scheme-theoretic stabilizer
of the line $D$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$, a finite-dimensional $k$-vector space $V$, a subspace $W\subseteq V$ of dimension $d\ge1$, a $k$-basis $e_1,\dots,e_d$ of $W$ extended to a $k$-basis $e_1,\dots,e_n$ of $V$, and the element $w=e_1\wedge\cdots\wedge e_d\in\Lambda^dV$. For a $k$-algebra $R$ we write $V_R=V\otimes_kR$ with its $R$-basis $e_1,\dots,e_n$, $W_R=R e_1+\cdots+R e_d$, and $D_R=R w\subseteq\Lambda^d_R(V_R)$, where $\Lambda_R(V_R)$ is the exterior algebra of the finite free $R$-module $V_R$ ([[def-exterior-algebra-of-a-finite-free-module]]).

[F1] *Wedge basis over a field.* For an ordered basis $(e_1,\dots,e_n)$ of a finite-dimensional vector space and $0\le k\le n$, the increasing wedges $e_I=e_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_k}$ indexed by the $k$-element subsets $I\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}$ form a basis of $\Lambda^kV$ ([[thm-increasing-basis-wedges-form-a-basis]]). In particular $\Lambda^dV$ has the basis vector $w$ indexed by $\{1,\dots,d\}$, and $\dim\Lambda^kV=\binom nk$ ([[cor-dimension-of-the-kth-exterior-power]]).

[F2] *Exterior algebra of a finite free module.* For a commutative unital ring $R$ and a finite free $R$-module $F$ with ordered basis $f_1,\dots,f_n$, the exterior algebra is the graded quotient $\bigwedge_RF=T_R(F)/(v\otimes v:v\in F)$ of the tensor algebra by the two-sided ideal generated by the elements $v\otimes v$, and $v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge v_m$ denotes the image of $v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes v_m$; the pure tensors of basis elements form an $R$-basis of $T_R(F)$ ([[def-exterior-algebra-of-a-finite-free-module]]).

[F3] *Leibniz determinant over a commutative ring.* For every commutative ring $R$ and $m\ge1$ the Leibniz determinant $\det:M_m(R)\to R$ of [[def-determinant-of-a-square-matrix]] is multilinear in the columns, alternating, and normalized, $\det I_m=1$ ([[thm-leibniz-determinant-is-alternating-multilinear-and-normalized]]); in particular $\det(c_1,\dots,c_{m})=0$ as soon as one column is the zero column, because column multilinearity factors out the scalar $0$.

[F4] *Scheme-theoretic stabilizers.* The scheme-theoretic stabilizer $G_x$ of a $k$-point $x$ of a finite-type $k$-scheme $X$ with $G$-action is the closed subgroup scheme of $G$ whose $R$-points are $G_x(R)=\{g\in G(R):g\cdot x_R=x_R\}$ for every $k$-algebra $R$ ([[def-algebraic-group-action-and-scheme-theoretic-stabilizer]]); a closed subscheme of an affine scheme is determined by its functor of points.

[F5] *Functoriality of exterior powers over $k$.* A linear map of $k$-vector spaces induces $\Lambda^kT$ with $\Lambda^kT(v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge v_k)=Tv_1\wedge\cdots\wedge Tv_k$, and $\Lambda^k(\operatorname{id})=\operatorname{id}$ with $\Lambda^k(S\circ T)=\Lambda^kS\circ\Lambda^kT$ ([[def-kth-exterior-power-of-a-linear-map]], [[thm-universal-property-and-uniqueness-of-exterior-powers]]); for an endomorphism $T$ of an $n$-dimensional space, $\Lambda^nT=\det(T)\operatorname{id}$ ([[cor-the-top-exterior-power-acts-by-the-determinant]]).

[F6] *Exterior powers of rational representations.* For a finite-dimensional rational representation $(V,r)$, the exterior power $\Lambda^dV$ with $g\cdot(v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge v_d)=g\cdot v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge g\cdot v_d$ is a rational representation, and for every $k$-algebra $R$ the induced action on $\Lambda^d_R(V_R)$ is $\Lambda^d_R(r_R(g))$ ([[lem-tensor-and-hom-representations-are-rational]]).

## Proof
**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $R$ be a $k$-algebra. The basis $e_1,\dots,e_n$ of $V$ is an $R$-basis of $V_R$, and $W_R$ is the free direct summand $Re_1\oplus\cdots\oplus Re_d$ because $W$ is spanned by $e_1,\dots,e_d$ and is complemented by the span of $e_{d+1},\dots,e_n$. We work in the graded exterior algebra $\bigwedge_R(V_R)$ of [F2] and write $\Lambda^k_R(V_R)$ for its degree-$k$ part; for $k\le n$ this is a free $R$-module, and the field case $R=k$ agrees with the exterior power of the statement by [F1], both having the wedges $e_I$ as basis. [F1, F2, given]

1.2 *Functoriality on $\bigwedge_R(V_R)$.* An $R$-linear map $\alpha:V_R\to V_R$ induces the algebra map $T(\alpha)$ of the tensor algebra with $\alpha(v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes v_m)=\alpha v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\alpha v_m$; it maps the defining ideal into itself because $v\otimes v\mapsto\alpha v\otimes\alpha v$ and the ideal is generated by these elements, so it descends to a graded $R$-linear map $\Lambda(\alpha)$ with $\Lambda(\alpha)(v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge v_m)=\alpha v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha v_m$, equal to $\Lambda^m_R(\alpha)$ in degree $m$. If $\alpha$ is invertible with inverse $\beta$, then $\Lambda(\alpha)\Lambda(\beta)=\Lambda(\beta)\Lambda(\alpha)=\operatorname{id}$ on wedges and hence on the whole algebra by linearity, so each $\Lambda^m_R(\alpha)$ is an $R$-linear automorphism; and for an $R$-submodule $U\subseteq V_R$ one has $\Lambda^m_R(\alpha)(\Lambda^m_RU)=\Lambda^m_R(\alpha U)$, where $\Lambda^m_RU$ is the span of $m$-fold wedges of elements of $U$. For $R=k$ this is [F5]. [F2, F5]

2.1 *The increasing wedges span $\Lambda^k_R(V_R)$.* For $k\ge1$ every element of $\bigwedge_R(V_R)$ is an $R$-linear combination of wedges of basis vectors, because $T_R(V_R)$ is spanned by pure tensors of basis vectors ([F2]) and the wedge is the quotient map. In the quotient, $u\wedge u=0$ for every $u\in V_R$, since $u\otimes u$ lies in the ideal of [F2]; applying this to $u+w$ and expanding bilinearly gives $u\wedge w=-w\wedge u$ for all $u,w$, so adjacent transpositions change a wedge by a sign. Consequently any wedge of basis vectors with two equal entries is zero: move the equal entries next to each other by adjacent transpositions, the wedge picks up a sign, and the adjacent product $u\wedge u$ vanishes. Every wedge of basis vectors with pairwise distinct indices therefore equals $\pm e_I$ for the corresponding increasing subset $I$, and the $e_I$ span $\Lambda^k_R(V_R)$; for $k=0$ the element $1$ spans $\Lambda^0_R(V_R)=R$. [F2, step 1.1]

2.2 *The increasing wedges are $R$-independent.* Fix a $k$-element subset $J=\{j_1<\cdots<j_k\}\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}$ and define, for $v_1,\dots,v_k\in V_R$, the element $\varphi_J(v_1,\dots,v_k)\in R$ as the Leibniz determinant of the $k\times k$ matrix whose entry in row $m$ and column $\ell$ is the coefficient of $e_{j_m}$ in $v_\ell$; its columns depend $R$-linearly on $v_1,\dots,v_k$ and equal columns occur when two of the vectors are equal, so by [F3] it is $R$-multilinear and alternating in $v_1,\dots,v_k$. Extend $\varphi_J$ $R$-linearly to a map $\widetilde\psi_J:T_R(V_R)\to R$ on the basis of pure tensors of basis vectors of [F2], declaring it zero in tensor degree different from $k$. Then $\widetilde\psi_J$ vanishes on every element $t\,(v\otimes v)\,t'$ with $t,t'$ pure tensors of basis vectors: expanding $v=\sum_ra_re_r$, such an element is a combination of pure tensors with two adjacent entries $e_r,e_s$ in the middle, and in degree $k$ it evaluates to $\sum_{r,s}a_ra_s\,\varphi_J(\dots,e_r,e_s,\dots)$, which is zero because a bilinear form alternating in two adjacent arguments satisfies $\sum_{r,s}a_ra_s\beta(e_r,e_s)=0$ over any commutative ring (the sum has $\sum_{r<s}a_ra_s(\beta(e_r,e_s)+\beta(e_s,e_r))+\sum_ra_r^2\beta(e_r,e_r)=0$). As these elements $R$-span the defining ideal of [F2], $\widetilde\psi_J$ descends to an $R$-linear map $\psi_J:\bigwedge_R(V_R)\to R$. The matrix whose columns are $e_{i_1},\dots,e_{i_k}$ in the rows $J$ is the identity matrix indexed by $J$ when $I=\{i_1<\cdots<i_k\}=J$, so $\psi_J(e_J)=1$ by [F3]; and when $I\ne J$ some $\ell$ has $i_\ell\notin J$ (as $|I|=|J|$), so column $\ell$ is a zero column and $\psi_J(e_I)=0$ by [F3]. Therefore $\sum_Ic_Ie_I=0$ forces $c_J=\psi_J(0)=0$ for every $J$, and the $e_I$ are $R$-independent. [F2, F3, step 1.1]

3.1 *Annihilator description of $W_R$.* An element of $V_R$ is uniquely $v=\sum_{i=1}^na_ie_i$ with $a_i\in R$, and $w\wedge v=\sum_{i=1}^na_i\,e_1\wedge\cdots\wedge e_d\wedge e_i=\sum_{i=d+1}^na_i\,e_{\{1,\dots,d,i\}}$, since wedges with a repeated index vanish by step 2.1. By step 2.2 the family of wedges $e_{\{1,\dots,d,i\}}$, $d+1\le i\le n$, is part of the $R$-basis of $\Lambda^{d+1}_R(V_R)$ and so is $R$-independent; hence $w\wedge v=0$ if and only if $a_i=0$ for all $i>d$, that is, if and only if $v\in W_R$. [step 2.1, step 2.2]

3.2 *If $\alpha W_R=W_R$ then $(\Lambda^d\alpha)(D_R)=D_R$.* The submodule $\Lambda^d_R(W_R)$ of $\Lambda^d_R(V_R)$ is generated by $w$: any $d$-fold wedge of elements of $W_R$ expands in the $R$-basis $e_1,\dots,e_d$ of $W_R$ into a combination of wedges with repeated indices and of the single wedge $w$. Hence $D_R=\Lambda^d_R(W_R)$, and step 1.2 gives $(\Lambda^d_R\alpha)(D_R)=\Lambda^d_R(\alpha W_R)=\Lambda^d_R(W_R)=D_R$. [step 2.1, step 1.2]

4.1 *If $(\Lambda^d\alpha)(D_R)=D_R$ then $\alpha W_R=W_R$.* Since $D_R=Rw$ and $\Lambda^d_R\alpha$ is an automorphism of $\Lambda^d_R(V_R)$ preserving $D_R$ (step 1.2), its restriction $D_R\to D_R$ is bijective and therefore $\Lambda^d_R\alpha(w)=cw$ for a unique unit $c\in R^\times$. Let $v\in W_R$; by step 3.1 $w\wedge v=0$, so $0=\Lambda^{d+1}_R\alpha(w\wedge v)=(\Lambda^d_R\alpha)(w)\wedge\Lambda_R\alpha(v)=c\,(w\wedge\alpha v)$, and since $c$ is a unit, $w\wedge\alpha v=0$, so $\alpha v\in W_R$ by step 3.1. Thus $\alpha W_R\subseteq W_R$. The same argument applied to $\alpha^{-1}$, whose induced map $(\Lambda^d_R\alpha)^{-1}$ also preserves $D_R$, gives $\alpha^{-1}W_R\subseteq W_R$, hence $W_R\subseteq\alpha W_R$, and therefore $\alpha W_R=W_R$. [step 1.2, step 3.1]

5.1 *The stabilizers agree.* Let $(V,r)$ be a rational representation of an affine group scheme $G$ over $k$ and let $g\in G(R)$ for a $k$-algebra $R$; the $R$-point of the representation is the $R$-linear automorphism $r_R(g)$ of $V_R$, and by [F6] the induced action on $\Lambda^dV$ is the rational action $\Lambda^d_R(r_R(g))$. Steps 3.2 and 4.1 give $r_R(g)W_R=W_R$ if and only if $(\Lambda^d_R(r_R(g)))(D_R)=D_R$, that is, the $R$-points of the stabilizer of the subspace $W$ and of the stabilizer of the line $D$ coincide for every $k$-algebra $R$. [F6, step 3.2, step 4.1]

6.1 By [F4] the scheme-theoretic stabilizers of $W$ and of the line $D$ are the closed subgroup schemes of $G$ defined by exactly these two functors of points; a closed subscheme of an affine scheme is determined by its functor of points, so the two closed subgroup schemes are equal. This completes both the base-change equivalence for every $k$-algebra $R$ and the scheme-theoretic stabilizer statement. [F4, step 5.1] ∎

## Remarks

- The hypothesis $d\ge1$ is the only nondegenerate case: for $d=0$ the space $W=0$ has $D=\Lambda^0W=k$ and the statement is also true, as both stabilizers are all of $G$; the proof above covers $d\ge1$ and the degenerate case is immediate.
- The equivalence is proved over every $k$-algebra $R$, not only over fields, because that is exactly what the scheme-theoretic stabilizer statement requires: it is the $R$-points for all $R$ that determine the closed subgroup scheme.
