## Statement

Let V be an admissibly rational C2-cofinite conformal vertex algebra of CFT type. For every simple ordinary module M, its formal geometrically modified trace F_M(v;τ)=Tr_M(o_M(Uv)exp(2πiτ(L(0)−c/24))) converges near the cusp for each v, extends to a single-valued holomorphic map V→Hol(H), and satisfies the three one-point Ward identities of the linked Ward-identity lemma. Every analytic one-point Ward block labeled by V is a finite constant linear combination of these maps. Here U=(2πi)^{L(0)}exp(−Σ A_jL(j)), where exp(Σ A_jy^{j+1}∂_y)y=log(1+2πiy)/(2πi). No rigidity or general logarithmic pseudo-trace completeness is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F2] [[lem-cft-genus-one-ward-identities-modular-covariant]] defines the three exact Ward identities, their lattice coefficients and their modular covariance.

[F3] [[lem-cft-one-point-ward-blocks-cusp-fuchsian-equation]] proves finite-dimensionality of the Ward space and a common finite-class finite-log cusp expansion. ## Proof

1.1 Put a=2πi. Let A_j be determined by exp(Σ_{j≥1}A_j y^{j+1}∂_y)y=log(1+ay)/a, and put U=a^{L(0)}exp(−Σ A_jL(j)) on V and on every lower-bounded module. These are actual vectorwise finite sums after the diagonal scaling. Define U(x)=x^{L(0)}U. The exact coordinate identity is U Y(v,z)U^{-1}=Y(U(e^{az})v,e^{az}−1).  (1) Here and below this identity holds on a module as well as on V, with the corresponding U on that module. For completeness, this is a formal change-of-coordinate calculation, not an assumed analytic sewing result. The coefficient commutator of module Jacobi is [L(j),Y(v,z)]=Σ_{k=−1}^j binom(j+1,k+1)z^{j−k}Y(L(k)v,z), j≥0. For a finite positive vector field b(y)=Σ_{j≥1}b_j y^{j+1}∂_y, differentiate conjugation by exp(tΣb_jL(j)). The k=−1 term is b(z)∂_zY; the other terms are exactly the Taylor coefficients of the local jet b(z+y)−b(z). Thus, coefficient by coefficient, conjugation implements the flow exp(tb(y))y and its translated local jet on the inserted state. The same differential equation and initial value determine both sides, recursively in t and then in the total y,z degree; uniqueness proves the change-of-coordinate rule. At each prescribed coefficient one can truncate the positive vector field, so it also holds for an infinite one. Diagonal conjugation a^{L(0)}Y(v,z)a^{−L(0)}=Y(a^{L(0)}v,az) follows directly from the weight commutator. Apply these two rules to the inverse flow of log(1+ay)/a and then the scaling a. Its coordinate is e^{az}−1, and its local jet at z is e^{az}(e^{ay}−1); its state operator is therefore U(e^{az}). This proves (1), including all nonprimary state corrections. The first coefficients of the defining flow are A_1=−a/2 and A_2=a²/12: the y³ coefficient of the flow is A_2+A_1²=a²/3. Since L(1)ω=0, L(2)ω=c1/2 and higher positive modes annihilate ω, Uω=a²(ω−c1/24), U1=1.  (2) This is the central constant needed below. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, algebra]

1.2 Let M be a simple ordinary module. For v∈V define the cylinder Fourier modes by Y_M(U(e^{az})v,e^{az})=Σ_{m∈Z} v̂_m e^{−amz}. If Uv=Σ_p (Uv)_p is its finite state-weight decomposition, then v̂_m=Σ_p ((Uv)_p)_{(p−1+m)}. Thus v̂_m has module degree −m and v̂_0=o_M(Uv). Let F_M(v;q)=Tr_M(v̂_0 q^{L(0)−c/24}). This is first a formal series q^{h_M−c/24}C[[q]] with finite-dimensional coefficient traces. No analytic convergence is presumed in the following calculation. The cylindrical OPE supplied by (1) and module associativity gives the Fourier commutator [û_m,v̂_n]=Σ_{j≥0} a^{j+1}m^j/j! (u_{(j)}v)^hat_{m+n}.  (3) Its coefficient verification is particularly simple: the OPE singular term is Y_M(U(e^{az_2})u_{(j)}v,e^{az_2})(z_1−z_2)^{−j−1}; its m-th Fourier commutator residue is a Res_x e^{amx}x^{−j−1}=a^{j+1}m^j/j!. State truncation makes the j sum finite. This is equally a formal residue calculation, and requires no intertwiner convergence theorem. For each m write Z_m=Tr_M(û_m v̂_{−m}q^{L(0)−c/24}). Graded cyclicity, applied to the two finite-dimensional source/target weight spaces, gives Tr(v̂_{−m}û_m q^L)=q^m Z_m. Hence for m≠0, Z_m=(1−q^m)^{−1}Σ_{j≥0} a^{j+1}m^j/j! F_M(u_{(j)}v;q).  (4) For m=0 the trace of (3) is zero and gives F_M(u_{(0)}v;q)=0. This is the first Ward identity. The trace of the two-field product is Σ_m Z_m e^{−amx}; module associativity says that its Laurent expansion at x=0 is Σ_n F_M(u_{(n)}v;q)x^{−n−1}. All assertions can be read coefficientwise in q: negative m contribute only starting at q^{|m|}, and positive m sum geometrically to rational functions of e^{ax}; for a fixed q coefficient these rational functions have only the declared diagonal pole. Thus this comparison is well-defined before any analytic assertion about the trace. Put K_0(x;q)=aΣ_{m≠0} e^{−amx}/(1−q^m), K_j=(−∂_x)^jK_0/j!. Expanding the two geometric sums gives K_0(x;q)=ζ_τ(x)−G_2(τ)x−πi, with exactly the lattice conventions of the Ward supplier. In particular, up to the constant −πi which multiplies the already zero F_M(u_0v), its Laurent series is x^{−1}−Σ_{k≥0}G_{2k+2}(q)x^{2k+1}. Formula (4) therefore gives Σ_n F_M(u_{(n)}v)x^{−n−1}=Z_0+Σ_{j≥0}K_j(x;q)F_M(u_{(j)}v).  (5) Taking the x¹ coefficient gives F_M(u_{(−2)}v)+Σ_{k≥1}(2k+1)G_{2k+2}F_M(u_{(2k)}v)=0; the k=0 term is G_2 F_M(u_0v)=0. This is the Q Ward identity. Taking the constant coefficient gives Z_0=F_M(u_{(−1)}v)−Σ_{k≥0}G_{2k+2}F_M(u_{(2k+1)}v). Set u=ω. By (2), ω̂_0=a²(L(0)−c/24), so its left side is a²δF_M(v), with δ=q∂_q. Its right side is exactly F_M(D_qv). This is the third Ward identity, including the −c/24 shift. We have proved all identities as formal series without an analytic genus-one associativity premise. The monic cusp equation proved in the Ward-ODE supplier applies algebraically to these formal Ward series: its construction only uses the identities just verified. A formal solution of a finite analytic Fuchsian system with fixed exponent and finite logarithm degree converges by the explicit inverse-O(1/n) majorant proof in `lem-cft-analytic-fuchsian-system-finite-log-fundamental-solutions` (applied directly to its formal coefficient recurrence). Thus F_M(v;q) converges near the cusp for each v. Analytic continuation is also controlled by the Ward system away from the cusp; for the modular-completeness conclusion below it is sufficient first to work on a cusp germ and then use the already analytic transformed Ward block. No unrestricted global convergence of arbitrary inserted traces is inferred from a character bound alone. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, algebra]

1.3 Let F be any analytic Ward block, and fix one nonzero exponent class in its common convergent finite-log expansion. Write its first occupied term as q^r Σ_{k=0}^K C_k(w)(log q)^k, with some C_K nonzero. The coefficient maps C_k:V→C are linear. Contributions from positive powers of the coefficient functions vanish at this lowest exponent. Consequently each C_k kills u_0w and Q^0_u w, where the superscript means q=0. Set f_k(v)=C_k(U^{-1}v). The change t=e^{ax}−1 in (1) gives U(u_0w)=a^{-1}Res_t (1+t)^{L(0)−1}Y(Uu,t)Uw. The residue on the right represents [Uu]*[Uw]−[Uw]*[Uu] in A(V), by the explicit Zhu commutator identity. Also ζ^0−G_2^0x=πcot(πx) and wp_2^0(x)=π²csc²(πx)−π²/3. Since dx=dt/(a(1+t)), direct substitution yields UQ^0_u w=a(u'∘w')−(π²/3)U(u_0w), u'=Uu,w'=Uw.  (6) Indeed π²csc²(πx)dx=a t^{−2}dt. Each C_k therefore kills the Zhu circle relations, and each f_k is a well-defined symmetric functional on A(V). This proves the quotient and cyclicity conditions, rather than inferring them from trace identities satisfied by only some functionals. For the differential relation, substitute πcot(πx)dx=(2+t)/(2t(1+t))dt and (2). The vacuum contribution has residue −a²c/24. The ω contribution has kernel (2+t)(1+t)/(2t)=1/t+3/2+t/2. The Zhu ω-star kernel is (1+t)²/t=1/t+2+t. Their difference is −(1+t)/2; its residue acting on a state is −(T+L(0))/2, which belongs to O(V). Thus the exact quotient identity is [UD_0w]=a²[(ω−c/24)*Uw].  (7) Comparing the coefficient of q^r(log q)^k in a²δF=F(D_q w) and using (7) gives f_k((ω−c/24)*v)=r f_k(v)+(k+1)f_{k+1}(v), f_{K+1}=0.  (8) Equations (6)–(8) establish precisely the central identity with the required shift. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, algebra]

1.4 By the n=0/forward supplier, A(V) is a finite product of full matrix algebras and its simple modules are the tops T_i of the simple ordinary modules M_i. A symmetric functional on a matrix algebra is a multiple of its ordinary trace: it kills every off-diagonal matrix unit as a commutator and takes equal values on diagonal matrix units because E_ii−E_jj=[E_ij,E_ji]. Hence f_k=Σ_i b_{i,k}tr_{T_i}. The central class ω acts on T_i as h_i. Formula (8) gives (h_i−c/24−r)b_{i,k}=(k+1)b_{i,k+1}. If K>0, the k=K equation forces b_{i,K}=0 unless h_i−c/24=r, and then the k=K−1 equation forces that remaining b_{i,K}=0 as well. This contradicts C_K≠0. Thus K=0, r=h_i−c/24 for at least one i, and the leading functional is a combination of the corresponding top traces. The leading functional of F_{M_i} is precisely C(v)=tr_{T_i}(o(Uv)), so subtract these ordinary traces with their constant coefficients to remove that leading term. Coefficientwise Ward identities preserve each exponent class, since all their coefficients are holomorphic in q and differentiation preserves that class. In a fixed class the next nonzero exponent increases by a positive integer. At every stage its value must again be one of the finite set {h_i−c/24}. Hence after finitely many subtractions the class is zero; after the finitely many classes from the Ward-ODE supplier, the whole residual is zero. A hypothetical nonzero residual after the largest possible exponent would have a lowest exponent not in that finite set, contradicting (8). This proves ordinary trace completeness as a cusp-germ identity with constant coefficients. For globally analytic Ward blocks the identity continues wherever the trace representatives are continued, by uniqueness. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, algebra]

2.1 The finite set and upper-half-plane convergence of the scalar ordinary characters are independently proved by the difference-one infinite-product majorant. Their T law is termwise multiplication by exp(2πi(h_i−c/24)). For S, one needs the ordinary trace Ward maps analytically continued on H, so that their modular transforms are actual Ward blocks. The preceding formal proof gives cusp convergence; the finite Ward quotient over the ring of holomorphic functions on a neighborhood of any point of H yields monic ordinary differential equations there by the same Noetherian germ argument, because the local holomorphic-germ ring is again a one-variable discrete valuation ring. Thus inserted traces admit analytic continuation along paths in H. The exact Ward identities continue because they are analytic identities. Monic equations have no interior singularities; local finite generating sets of the quotient identify the same continued functionals on overlaps. On simply connected H the continuation is single-valued by subdivision of a homotopy into disks of regular equations and uniqueness of the initial-value problem. This constructs the ordinary trace Ward maps globally without a modular-functor supplier. Apply Ward covariance to these global trace maps. Ordinary trace completeness gives constant coefficients S_ij for their transformed maps; evaluate at v=1 to obtain χ_i(−1/τ)=Σ_jS_ijχ_j(τ). Coincident scalar characters cause no ambiguity in this argument: the full trace maps on V, rather than their vacuum values alone, have independent leading symmetric Zhu functionals after the finite-subtraction process. Inverses are supplied by the inverse modular substitution with its fixed logarithm branch, so the block action is invertible. This proves the stated finite character-span invariance. It does not prove symmetry of S, S² duality, nonzero vacuum row, fusion diagonalization, rigidity or Hopf-link equality. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, algebra] ∎
