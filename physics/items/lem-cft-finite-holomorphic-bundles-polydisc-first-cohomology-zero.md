## Statement

Assume full AC. For an arbitrary finite-dimensional open coordinate polydisc $P$ and every finite-rank holomorphic vector bundle $E$ on $P$, $H^1(P,\mathcal O(E))=0$. In particular every short exact sequence of finite holomorphic bundles on $P$ is surjective on global sections at its last term. The polydisc need not lie inside a single trivialization of $E$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The polydisc and finite holomorphic bundle in the Statement.

[F1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]] supplies full AC, hence countable choice for selecting a distance-minimizing sequence in the Hilbert projection argument below. The projections and Hilbert-space representation are proved explicitly below; AC is not used as a substitute for those arguments.

[F2] [[thm-smooth-partitions-of-unity-exist-on-manifolds]] supplies a smooth Hermitian bundle metric. [[thm-dolbeault-lemma-polydisc]] is applied in local holomorphic frames.

[F3] [[thm-smooth-function-module-sheaves-are-acyclic]] and [[thm-acyclic-resolution-theorem-for-right-derived-functors]] identify bundle sheaf cohomology with the global smooth Dolbeault complex once its local exactness is established.

## Proof

1.1 Rescale the polydisc to $|z_j|<1$, and suppose its dimension $n$ is positive; dimension zero is immediate. Put $t=\sum_j-\log(1-|z_j|^2)$ and $g=\sum_j(1-|z_j|^2)^{-2}|dz_j|^2$. Direct differentiation gives $i\partial\bar\partial t$ equal to the Kähler form of g, up to the fixed convention factor, which we absorb into g. The g-norm of dt is bounded. The metric is complete: a path leaving every compact set has unbounded t, and its length bounds the variation of t divided by that gradient bound. The functions $\eta(t/A)$, where $\eta=1$ on $[0,1]$ and $\eta=0$ on $[2,\infty)$, are compactly supported cutoffs with gradient at most $C/A$. [given, algebra]

2.1 Tensor $E$ with $K_P^{-1}$, calling the resulting bundle B. Identify an E-valued $(0,q)$ form $\alpha$ with $(-1)^{nq}(dz_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_n\wedge\alpha)\otimes(dz_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_n)^{-1}$. This frame-independent identification intertwines dbar exactly and is a B-valued $(n,q)$ form; give B the metric making this identification isometric for g. Choose a smooth convex increasing function $\chi$ of t so that the Nakano curvature of $B$ with metric multiplied by $e^{-\chi(t)}$ dominates the identity on $(n,1)$ coefficients. Here this inequality is constructed, not assumed: on each compact shell $k\le t\le k+1$ the negative part of the original curvature matrix has a finite bound $C_k$; choose $\chi'\ge 1+\max_{j\le k+1}C_j$ there and $\chi''\ge0$. The added curvature is $\chi'i\partial\bar\partial t+\chi''i\partial t\wedge\bar\partial t$. Enlarging these increasing bounds if necessary also makes any specified smooth E-valued form f square integrable: bound its unweighted integral on each compact shell by $a_k<\infty$ and arrange $\chi(k)\ge \log(1+a_k)+2k$. Smooth convex interpolation of these increasing bounds gives one such function on $[0,\infty)$. [F2, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Write $A=D'_\chi$, $B_0=\bar\partial$, $\Theta_\chi=AB_0+B_0A$, and let $\Lambda$ be adjoint to wedge multiplication by the Kähler form. In holomorphic normal Kähler coordinates and a holomorphic frame Chern-normal for the weighted metric at the point, the wedge/contraction relations $\{\epsilon_j,\iota_k\}=\delta_{jk}$ give $[\Lambda,B_0]=-iA^*$ and $[\Lambda,A]=iB_0^*$. First derivatives of g and the connection coefficients vanish there; these are equalities of first-order operators, hence hold globally. Expanding ordinary products gives $$\Delta''-\Delta'=-i\{B_0,[\Lambda,A]\}-i\{A,[\Lambda,B_0]\}=[i\Theta_\chi,\Lambda].$$ For compactly supported smooth $(n,1)$ forms, integration by parts therefore gives $$\|\bar\partial u\|^2+\|\bar\partial^*u\|^2=\|D'_\chi u\|^2+\|D'^*_\chi u\|^2+\langle[i\Theta_\chi,\Lambda]u,u\rangle\ge\|u\|^2.$$ Indeed $D'_\chi u=0$ by holomorphic degree. In a holomorphic frame $A_j=\partial_j+h^{-1}\partial_jh-\chi_j$, so the curvature coefficient in $i\Theta_\chi=i\sum R_{j\bar k}dz_j\wedge d\bar z_k$ is $R_{j\bar k}=-\partial_{\bar k}(h^{-1}\partial_jh)+\chi_{j\bar k}I$. The commutator quadratic form on $(n,1)$ coefficients is $\sum_{j,k}\langle R_{j\bar k}u_j,u_k\rangle$ in the signed top-form convention. Its lower bound is step 2.1; in particular the added weight Hessian has positive sign. No boundary term occurs. This explicitly derives the identity, rather than taking a named estimate as a premise. [step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Fix the weight chosen in step 2.1. Let $H_q=L^2_{(0,q)}(P,E;g,h e^{-\chi(t)})$, with the bundle metric understood through the isometric identification in step 2.1, and put $H_q=\{0\}$ for $q>n$. For $q=0,1$ define the maximal distributional operator by
$$\operatorname{Dom}D_q=\{u\in H_q:\bar\partial u\text{ in distributions is represented by an element of }H_{q+1}\},\qquad D_qu=\bar\partial u.$$
If $q=n$ this is the zero operator on all of $H_n$. The space $C_c^\infty(P,E\otimes\Lambda^{0,q})$ is contained in this domain and is dense in $H_q$: first truncate an arbitrary $L^2$ form by the compact cutoffs of step 1.1, then use finitely many bundle charts, a smooth partition and coefficient convolution on that compact support. Smooth positive metrics and weights are bounded above and below there, so ordinary local $L^2$ approximation also converges in the weighted norm. These same local bounds show closedness: if $u_j\to u$ in $H_q$ and $D_qu_j\to v$ in $H_{q+1}$, both convergences hold in distributions on every compact chart; differentiation is continuous on distributions, so $\bar\partial u=v$ and $(u,v)$ belongs to the graph. Set $T=D_0$ and $S=D_1$. Define $\operatorname{Dom}T^*$ to consist of $a\in H_1$ for which there is $b\in H_0$ satisfying $\langle Tv,a\rangle=\langle v,b\rangle$ for every $v\in\operatorname{Dom}T$, and put $T^*a=b$; $b$ is unique because $\operatorname{Dom}T$ is dense. For every compactly supported smooth $a$, the smooth weighted formal adjoint $T^\dagger a$ has compact support and belongs to $H_0$. The distributional definition of $T$ gives $\langle Tv,a\rangle=\langle v,T^\dagger a\rangle$ for all $v\in\operatorname{Dom}T$. Hence $C_c^\infty(P,E\otimes\Lambda^{0,1})\subseteq\operatorname{Dom}T^*$, proving that this adjoint domain is dense in $H_1$. Passing to limits in this pairing identity also proves that $T^*$ is closed. For $a\in\operatorname{Dom}T^*$, testing against compactly supported smooth $v$ identifies $T^*a$ with $T^\dagger a$ distributionally; no converse equality of maximal formal-adjoint and Hilbert-adjoint domains is assumed. In the degree-one estimate write $\bar\partial=S$ and $\bar\partial^*=T^*$. The same estimate holds on $\operatorname{Dom}T^*\cap\operatorname{Dom}S$. Here is the simultaneous graph-domain justification. For a cutoff $\zeta$ and $a\in\operatorname{Dom}T^*$, the identity $T(\zeta v)=\zeta Tv+(\bar\partial\zeta)\wedge v$ for $v\in\operatorname{Dom}T$ shows directly from the adjoint pairing that $\zeta a\in\operatorname{Dom}T^*$ with the formal-adjoint Leibniz rule. Multiplication by the cutoffs of step 1.1 therefore tends to the identity in both graph norms: the commutator with either first-order operator is contraction or wedge multiplication by its gradient, bounded by $C/A$, and dominated convergence handles the other terms. On the resulting compact support, take finitely many coordinate and bundle frames, a subordinate partition, and convolve their coefficients. For a smooth first-order coefficient a the commutator of $a\partial$ with convolution tends to zero in L2: its integral is a difference quotient of a times the approximate identity, and Taylor's formula proves the assertion for smooth inputs, while its uniformly bounded operator norm and density prove it for L2 inputs. Zero-order terms converge directly. This supplies compactly supported smooth approximants in the simultaneous graph norm. Apply step 3.1 and pass to the limit. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Take all Hilbert inner products to be linear in their first variable. Use the maximal operators $T:H_0\to H_1$ and $S:H_1\to H_2$ defined and proved closed and densely defined in step 4.1. For every $v\in\operatorname{Dom}T$, the distributional identity $\bar\partial(Tv)=\bar\partial^2v=0$ gives $Tv\in\operatorname{Dom}S$ and $STv=0$; thus this composition is defined on all of $\operatorname{Dom}T$. In particular, $\ker S$ is closed: if $u_n\in\operatorname{Dom}S$, $u_n\to u$, and $Su_n=0$, closedness gives $u\in\operatorname{Dom}S$ and $Su=0$. We give the Hilbert projection argument used here and again below. For a Hilbert space $H$ and closed linear subspace $N\subseteq H$, fix $x\in H$ and put $d=\inf_{n\in N}\|x-n\|$. By full AC choose $n_j\in N$ with $\|x-n_j\|\le d+1/j$. The parallelogram identity and $(n_j+n_k)/2\in N$ give $\|n_j-n_k\|^2\le2(d+1/j)^2+2(d+1/k)^2-4d^2$, so $(n_j)$ is Cauchy. Completeness and closedness give a limit $n_x\in N$ attaining distance $d$. For each $h\in N$, minimality of $n_x$ applied to $n_x+\lambda h$, first for real and then for purely imaginary $\lambda$, gives $\langle x-n_x,h\rangle=0$. This minimizer is unique, since the difference of two such minimizers lies in $N\cap N^\perp=\{0\}$. Thus every $x$ has a unique decomposition $x=n_x+(x-n_x)$ with $n_x\in N$ and $x-n_x\in N^\perp$. Uniqueness makes $P_Nx=n_x$ linear, and Pythagoras gives $H=N\oplus N^\perp$ and $\|P_N\|\le1$. Apply this construction to the closed subspace $N=\ker S$ in the degree-one Hilbert space and call the resulting projection $P$. If $u\in\operatorname{Dom}T^*$, then $Tv\in\ker S$ for every $v\in\operatorname{Dom}T$, so $\langle Tv,Pu\rangle=\langle Tv,u\rangle=\langle v,T^*u\rangle$. Hence $Pu\in\operatorname{Dom}T^*$ and $T^*Pu=T^*u$. Also $Pu\in\operatorname{Dom}S$ and $SPu=0$. Step 4.1 therefore gives $\|Pu\|\le\|T^*u\|$. [F1, step 4.1, choose, construct, algebra]

6.1 Now take any smooth closed E-valued $(0,1)$ form f and choose the weight of step 2.1 also for f. Since $f\in\ker S$, the functional $\ell(T^*u)=\langle u,f\rangle=\langle Pu,f\rangle$ is well defined and bounded by $\|f\|\|T^*u\|$. Extend it continuously to $N=\overline{\operatorname{ran}T^*}$ in the degree-zero Hilbert space and denote this extension by $\ell$. The projection proved in step 5.1 gives the orthogonal decomposition into $N$ and $N^\perp$, so $\widetilde\ell(x)=\ell(P_Nx)$ is a bounded linear functional on the whole degree-zero Hilbert space. We prove its representing vector directly. If $\widetilde\ell=0$, take $v=0$. Otherwise $K=\ker\widetilde\ell$ is a closed hyperplane; the projection construction of step 5.1 supplies its orthogonal projection $P_K$. Choose $x$ with $\widetilde\ell(x)\ne0$ and set $e=x-P_Kx$. Then $e\ne0$, $e\perp K$, and $\widetilde\ell(e)=\widetilde\ell(x)$. For every y, $y-(\widetilde\ell(y)/\widetilde\ell(e))e\in K$, so $v=\overline{\widetilde\ell(e)}\,e/\|e\|^2$ satisfies $\widetilde\ell(y)=\langle y,v\rangle$. Consequently $\langle T^*u,v\rangle=\langle u,f\rangle$, or equivalently $\langle v,T^*u\rangle=\langle f,u\rangle$, for every $u\in\operatorname{Dom}T^*$. Since step 4.1 proves that $\operatorname{Dom}T^*$ is dense in $H_1$, its adjoint $T^{**}$ is defined; the displayed pairing identity says exactly that $v\in\operatorname{Dom}T^{**}$ and $T^{**}v=f$. For completeness, if T is densely defined and closed, its graph satisfies $\operatorname{graph}(T)^\perp=\{(-T^*y,y):y\in\operatorname{Dom}T^*\}$. A pair $(x,z)$ is orthogonal to every $(-T^*y,y)$ exactly when $\langle T^*y,x\rangle=\langle y,z\rangle$ for every $y\in\operatorname{Dom}T^*$, which is the defining condition $(x,z)\in\operatorname{graph}(T^{**})$. Since the graph of T is closed, $\operatorname{graph}(T)^{\perp\perp}=\operatorname{graph}(T)$, so $T^{**}=T$. Hence $Tv=f$. This constructs the required global distributional solution without invoking an unnamed Hilbert-space representation theorem. [F1, step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra, choose, construct]

7.1 This v is smooth. In each relatively compact local holomorphic frame its coefficients satisfy the scalar equations $\partial_{\bar z_j}v=f_j$. Cut off and mollify. The Euclidean constant-coefficient identity $\|\nabla a\|_2^2=4\sum_j\|\partial_{\bar z_j}a\|_2^2$ bounds the first weak derivatives locally; cutoff errors are L2. Differentiating and repeating gives every Sobolev order because the right sides are smooth. Fourier Cauchy–Schwarz, or the repeated one-variable fundamental theorem of calculus on a cube, then gives smooth representatives. Hence every smooth closed degree-one form is globally exact. Local exactness in F2 and the smooth fine resolution in F3 give $H^1(P,\mathcal O(E))=0$. For a bundle short exact sequence, lift a last-term section smoothly; its dbar is a closed kernel-bundle form. Subtract the global solution just constructed. The corrected lift is holomorphic, proving the last assertion as well. [F2, F3, step 6.1, algebra] ∎
