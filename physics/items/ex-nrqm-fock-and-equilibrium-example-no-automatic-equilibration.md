## Example

Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice and the Axiom of Choice. Let $\omega>0$,
$\hbar>0$, let $F(\mathbb C)$ be the one-mode Fock space with its complete
orthonormal family $(e_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}$, number operator $N$ and operators
$A_\pm(1)$ as in [[thm-nrqm-fock-and-equilibrium-fixed-mode-number-law]] and
[[thm-nrqm-fock-and-equilibrium-number-operator-domain]], and let
$$A:=\omega N,\qquad U(t):=e^{-itA/\hbar}=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-it\lambda/\hbar}\,dE(\lambda),$$
the unitary group generated by the self-adjoint $A$ with spectral projection
valued measure $E$ ([[thm-spectral-theorem-for-unbounded-self-adjoint-operators]],
[[lem-self-adjoint-operator-generates-a-strongly-continuous-unitary-group]]).
Put
$$\psi_0:=\frac{e_0+e_1}{\sqrt2},\qquad \psi(t):=U(t)\psi_0,\qquad Y:=A_+(1)+A_-(1),\qquad D(Y):=D(A_+(1))\cap D(A_-(1)).$$
Then $\|\psi_0\|=1$, $\psi(t)\in D(Y)$ for every $t$, and with
$\theta:=\omega t/\hbar$:

(a) $\psi(t)=\dfrac{e_0+e^{-i\theta}e_1}{\sqrt2}$;

(b) the expectation $t\mapsto\langle\psi(t),Y\psi(t)\rangle$ equals
$\cos(\omega t/\hbar)$; consequently it tends to no limit as $t\to+\infty$,
since it equals $1$ at $t=2\pi\hbar n/\omega$ and $-1$ at
$t=(2n+1)\pi\hbar/\omega$;

(c) the state itself has no norm limit: $\|\psi(t)-\psi(s)\|^2
=1-\cos\bigl(\omega(t-s)/\hbar\bigr)$, so $\|\psi(t_n)-\psi(s_n)\|=\sqrt2$ for
the two sequences of (b);

(d) in contrast, the weight operator $G_\beta$ and the density operator
$\rho_\beta=G_\beta/Z(\beta)$ of
[[thm-nrqm-fock-and-equilibrium-single-mode-thermal-trace]] are invariant under
the same dynamics:
$$U(t)G_\beta U(t)^*=G_\beta,\qquad U(t)\rho_\beta U(t)^*=\rho_\beta\qquad(t\in\mathbb R,\ \beta>0).$$

Thus a stationary equilibrium weight is available on this space, but the
unitary dynamics of the non-stationary unit vector $\psi_0$ produces no
convergence of the state or of the displayed expectation: equilibration is not
a consequence of unitary evolution alone. No physical interpretation of
$\psi_0$, $Y$, $\beta$ or $\rho_\beta$ is asserted; in particular no thermal
state is derived, and the example does not assert that any physical
preparation oscillates.

## Verification

**Given:** the one-mode Fock space $F(\mathbb C)$ with complete orthonormal family $(e_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}$, the self-adjoint $\omega N$, its unitary group $U(t)$, the vector $\psi_0$, the symmetric operator $Y=A_+(1)+A_-(1)$ and the objects $G_\beta,\rho_\beta,Z(\beta)$ of [[thm-nrqm-fock-and-equilibrium-single-mode-thermal-trace]].

[F1] $A:=\omega N$ is self-adjoint on $D(N)$, $N\ge0$, $Ne_n=ne_n$, and the eigenvalues of $N$ on $F(\mathbb C)$ are exactly $\mathbb N$, each with eigenvector $e_n$; $D(N)\subseteq D_1\subseteq D(A_\pm(1))$, so $F_0\subseteq D(A_\pm(1))$ and $F_0$ is dense ([[thm-nrqm-fock-and-equilibrium-number-operator-domain]] (a), (b), (d), [[thm-nrqm-fock-and-equilibrium-hilbert-direct-sum-construction]] (c)).

[F2] Oscillator coefficients: $A_+(1)e_n=\sqrt{n+1}\,e_{n+1}$ and $A_-(1)e_n=\sqrt n\,e_{n-1}$ for $n\ge1$, $A_-(1)e_0=0$ ([[thm-nrqm-fock-and-equilibrium-fixed-mode-number-law]] (a)); $(e_n)$ is a complete orthonormal family of $F(\mathbb C)$ ([[thm-nrqm-fock-and-equilibrium-fixed-mode-number-law]], [[def-orthonormal-family-complete-orthonormal-system-and-hilbert-basis]]).

[F3] Spectral calculus: for a self-adjoint operator $T$ with PVM $E_T$ and $x\in D(T)$, $\|(T-a)x\|^2=\int(\lambda-a)^2\,dE_T^x(\lambda)$; for bounded Borel $h$ the operator $h(T)$ is bounded with $\|h(T)x\|^2=\int|h|^2\,dE_T^x$; if additionally $\int|h-h(\lambda_0)|^2dE_T^x=0$ then $h(T)x=h(\lambda_0)x$; and $U_T(t)=e^{-itT/\hbar}$ is a strongly continuous unitary group expressed by the functional calculus ([[thm-spectral-theorem-for-unbounded-self-adjoint-operators]], [[thm-unbounded-borel-functional-calculus]], [[def-projection-valued-measure]], [[def-unbounded-integral-against-a-pvm]], [[lem-self-adjoint-operator-generates-a-strongly-continuous-unitary-group]]).

[F4] A nonnegative measurable function has zero integral exactly when it vanishes almost everywhere w.r.t. the measure ([[thm-nonnegative-integral-zero-iff-zero-almost-everywhere]]).

[F5] $|e^{i\alpha}-e^{i\beta}|^2=2-2\cos(\alpha-\beta)$ and $|e^{i\alpha}|=1$ ([[cor-complex-exponential-cartesian-form-modulus-and-eulers-identity]], [[thm-sine-and-cosine-derivatives]]).

[F6] $G_\beta$ is bounded self-adjoint, $G_\beta=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb N}e^{-\beta\omega n}\mathrm{id}$ on the Fock sectors, $Z(\beta)=1/(1-e^{-\beta\omega})$, and $\rho_\beta=G_\beta/Z(\beta)$ ([[thm-nrqm-fock-and-equilibrium-single-mode-thermal-trace]] (a), (b), (d)).

[F7] Limits at $+\infty$: if a function has a limit then every sequence tending to $+\infty$ has that limit ([[def-limits-at-infinity]]).



1.1 [$U(t)$ on eigenstates] For $n\in\mathbb N$ the vector $e_n$ satisfies $Ne_n=ne_n$ by [F1], hence $Ae_n=\omega ne_n$; by [F3] with $T=A$, $a=\omega n$ and $x=e_n$, $\int(\lambda-\omega n)^2dE^{e_n}=0$ with a nonnegative integrand, so by [F4] the measure $E^{e_n}$ gives full mass to $\{\omega n\}$. Applying [F3] to the bounded Borel function $h_n(\lambda):=e^{-it\lambda/\hbar}$ and $\lambda_0=\omega n$ gives $\|h_n(A)e_n-e^{-it\omega n/\hbar}e_n\|^2=\int|h_n-h_n(\omega n)|^2dE^{e_n}=0$; hence $U(t)e_n=h_n(A)e_n=e^{-it\omega n/\hbar}e_n$. For $n\in\{0,1\}$ this gives $U(t)e_0=e_0$ and $U(t)e_1=e^{-i\theta}e_1$, so by linearity $\psi(t)=(e_0+e^{-i\theta}e_1)/\sqrt2$, which is (a); also $\|\psi_0\|^2=(\|e_0\|^2+\|e_1\|^2)/2=1$. [F1, F3, F4, algebra]

2.1 [The expectation] By [F2], $Ye_0=A_+(1)e_0=e_1$ and $Ye_1=A_-(1)e_1+A_+(1)e_1=e_0+\sqrt2\,e_2$, so $Y$ maps $\operatorname{span}\{e_0,e_1\}$ into $\operatorname{span}\{e_0,e_1,e_2\}$; since $e_0,e_1\in F_0\subseteq D(A_\pm(1))$ by [F1], both $\psi(t)$ and $Y\psi(t)$ lie in the finite-particle space, and $\langle\psi(t),Y\psi(t)\rangle$ is well-defined. With $Y\psi(t)=\frac1{\sqrt2}(e_1+e^{-i\theta}e_0+\sqrt2e^{-i\theta}e_2)$, orthonormality of $(e_0,e_1,e_2)$ and linearity in the first argument give $\langle\psi(t),Y\psi(t)\rangle=\frac12\bigl(\langle e_0,e_1\rangle+e^{i\theta}\langle e_0,e_0\rangle+e^{-i\theta}\langle e_1,e_1\rangle\bigr)=\frac12(e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta})=\cos\theta$. [F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 [The stationary thermal weight] By [F6], $G_\beta$ is the direct sum of the scalars $e^{-\beta\omega n}$ on the one-dimensional sectors of $F(\mathbb C)$; since the sector $n$ is spanned by $e_n$, this says $G_\beta e_n=e^{-\beta\omega n}e_n$, so $G_\beta$ is diagonal in the complete orthonormal family $(e_n)$. By step 1.1, $U(t)G_\beta U(t)^*e_n=U(t)G_\beta e^{i\theta n}e_n=e^{i\theta n}e^{-\beta\omega n}e^{-i\theta n}e_n=e^{-\beta\omega n}e_n=G_\beta e_n$ for every $n$, $\theta n=\omega nt/\hbar$. Both $U(t)G_\beta U(t)^*$ and $G_\beta$ are bounded and agree on the complete orthonormal family, hence on its dense span, hence everywhere; dividing by the finite positive scalar $Z(\beta)$ gives $U(t)\rho_\beta U(t)^*=\rho_\beta$. This is (d). [F2, F6, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 [Failure of convergence of the expectation and of the state] By step 2.1, $\langle\psi(t),Y\psi(t)\rangle=\cos(\omega t/\hbar)$ equals $1$ at $t_n=2\pi\hbar n/\omega$ and $-1$ at $s_n=(2n+1)\pi\hbar/\omega$ for every $n$; if a limit existed along $t\to+\infty$, [F7] would give $1=-1$, a contradiction. This is (b). For two times $t,s$, [F5] gives $\|\psi(t)-\psi(s)\|^2=\frac12|e^{-i\omega t/\hbar}-e^{-i\omega s/\hbar}|^2=1-\cos(\omega(t-s)/\hbar)$, which equals $2$ for $t=t_n$, $s=s_n$; hence the family $(\psi(t))$ is not Cauchy at $+\infty$ and has no limit in norm. This is (c). [F5, F7, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 [Conclusion and scope] Steps 1.1 and 2.1 establish the setting and the expectation $\cos(\omega t/\hbar)$, step 3.1 proves (b) and (c), and step 2.2 proves (d). The scope paragraph of the example records that no physical interpretation of the state, the coupling or the weights is asserted and that relaxation to equilibrium is not derived. This completes the verification. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 2.2] ∎
