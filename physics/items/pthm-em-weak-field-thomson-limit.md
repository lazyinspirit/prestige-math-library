## Statement

Assume Countable Choice ($\mathrm{AC}_\omega$) as carried by [[pthm-em-dipole-scattering-cross-section]]; the linearization below and [[lem-em-harmonic-forcing-periodic-response]] use no choice. Assume the setting and postulates of [[pthm-em-relativistic-lorentz-force]] for a test particle with charge parameter $q\in\mathbb R$ ([[def-em-sources]](a)) and externally supplied rest mass $m>0$, in the incident plane-wave field of [[def-em-plane-wave-definition]] with wavevector $\kappa$, complex amplitude $e\ne0$, $\kappa\cdot e=0$, $|\kappa|=\omega/c$ and $b=\kappa\times e/\omega$ as in [[pthm-em-dipole-scattering-cross-section]]; write $E_{\rm inc}(x,t)=\operatorname{Re}(e\,e^{i(\kappa\cdot x-\omega t)})$ and $B_{\rm inc}=\kappa\times E_{\rm inc}/\omega$ ([[def-complex-conjugate-real-imaginary-part-and-modulus]], [[def-em-lab-geometry]], [[def-em-si-conventions]]). Assume that for some $\eta_0>0$ and a common open interval $I\ni0$, for every $\eta\in(-\eta_0,\eta_0)$ there is a $C^2$ future timelike massive solution $r_\eta:I\to\mathbb R^3$ of the adopted four-force law in the external field $\eta(E_{\rm inc},B_{\rm inc})$ (no self-field, recoil or material force), and that the family admits a uniform first-order expansion: there are $r_1\in C^2(I;\mathbb R^3)$ and, for every compact $K\subset I$, a constant $C_K$ with

$$|r_\eta(t)-x_0-\eta r_1(t)|+|\dot r_\eta(t)-\eta\dot r_1(t)|\le C_K\eta^2\qquad(t\in K,\ |\eta|<\eta_0)$$

about a fixed position $x_0\in\mathbb R^3$. The family time interval $I$ is not required to contain a full period, and no solution $r_\eta$ outside $I$ is assumed. For the periodic-response conclusions (b), (c), impose the following explicit preparation (P): the unique global harmonic-ODE extension of $r_1$ supplied after (a) by [[lem-em-harmonic-forcing-periodic-response]](d), with its actual value and derivative at $0$, is $T$-periodic, $T=2\pi/\omega$. Equivalently its affine drift is zero, or $r_1'(0)=\operatorname{Re}(iw/\omega)$ with $w=(q/m)e\,e^{i\kappa\cdot x_0}$. The linearized equation and affine local/global classification in (a) hold for every stated family even without (P); overlap-only periodicity on a short $I$ is not substituted for (P). Then: (a) [Linearized equation] $\ddot r_1(t)=\frac qmE_{\rm inc}(x_0,t)=\frac qm\operatorname{Re}(e\,e^{i(\kappa\cdot x_0-\omega t)})$ on $I$. (b) [Induced dipole under preparation (P)] $x_0$ is the zeroth-order rest/reference position. Let $a$ be the mean of the global periodic first-order response, so on $I$ one has $r_1=z_p+a$ with $z_p(t)=\operatorname{Re}(-w\omega^{-2}e^{-i\omega t})$. The arbitrary constant $a$ is retained: no exact mean or periodicity of the full nonlinear family is asserted. The oscillating part of the first-order dipole coefficient $q(r_1-a)$ is $\operatorname{Re}(p_0e^{-i\omega t})$ with complex amplitude

$$p_0=-\frac{q^2}{m\omega^2}\,e\,e^{i\kappa\cdot x_0};$$

equivalently $p_0=\alpha e'$ with $\alpha=-\frac{q^2}{m\omega^2}$ (units $\mathrm F\,\mathrm m^2$) and the unimodular factor $e'=e\,e^{i\kappa\cdot x_0}$, $|e'|=|e|$. (c) [Leading Thomson-model cross section] substituting this $p_0$ into [[pthm-em-dipole-scattering-cross-section]](b) gives

$$\sigma_{\rm T}=\frac{q^4}{6\pi\varepsilon_0^2m^2c^4}=\frac{8\pi}{3}\Bigl(\frac{q^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0mc^2}\Bigr)^2,$$

independent of $\omega$ and of the phase factor. (d) [Scope] this is a controlled conditional first variation of the adopted relativistic external-force law under the stated solution-family hypotheses. It is not the exact Newtonian force postulate, it does not assert existence, persistence, stability or self-consistency of the family, it excludes self-force, radiation reaction and material media, and it makes no empirical, measurement, atomic-physics or frequency-response claim. In (a), every local $r_1$ has the unique global harmonic extension $z_p+a+bt$, with $b=r_1'(0)-z_p'(0)$, $a=r_1(0)-z_p(0)$. Thus the general affine-drift response is also retained; preparation (P) sets $b=0$ for (b), (c) and leaves $a$ arbitrary. For a nonzero $\eta$, the associated leading ideal harmonic model has incident amplitude $\eta e$ and induced dipole amplitude $\eta p_0$. Their squared flux factors $\eta^2$ cancel in its cross-section ratio; $\sigma_{\rm T}$ is this leading-model coefficient, not an assertion of the exact nonlinear particle's emitted field or cross section. At $q=0$ the induced amplitude and $\sigma_{\rm T}$ are zero without any division by $q$. All $\eta$ estimates apply only to the open parameter domain on which the family was declared.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The setting, family and preparation (P) of the Statement: charge $q\in\mathbb R$ and mass $m>0$ ([[def-em-sources]], [[def-em-si-conventions]]); the plane-wave data $\kappa,e,\omega$ with $|\kappa|=\omega/c$, $\kappa\cdot e=0$, $b=\kappa\times e/\omega$; the family $r_\eta$ on the common open interval $I\ni0$ with the uniform first-order expansion about $x_0$; the first-order function $r_1\in C^2(I;\mathbb R^3)$; the harmonic data $w=(q/m)e\,e^{i\kappa\cdot x_0}$ and $z_p(t)=\operatorname{Re}(-w\omega^{-2}e^{-i\omega t})$.

[F1] Adopted force law (external supplier, whose carrier is the referenced in-run item): under its postulates, with the coordinate reading $r$, velocity $v=r'$ and $\gamma=(1-|v|^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$ of a future timelike $C^2$ massive worldline in a prescribed exterior field $(E,B)$, the coordinate momentum $p=m\gamma v$ satisfies $\frac{dp}{dt}=q(E+v\times B)$ and $\frac{d(\gamma mc^2)}{dt}=qE\cdot v$; the norm $\eta(U,U)$ is constant and a solution with future timelike data remains future timelike; and for every $\delta\in(0,1)$, wherever $|v|/c\le\delta$, $|\gamma-1|\le\frac{|v|^2/c^2}{2(1-\delta^2)^{3/2}}$ and $|p-mv|\le m|v|\frac{|v|^2}{2c^2(1-\delta^2)^{3/2}}$, with constants depending only on $\delta$; the statement is conditional on the adopted postulates and excludes self-force, radiation reaction and material media ([[pthm-em-relativistic-lorentz-force]], [[def-em-sources]], [[def-em-si-conventions]], [[def-em-lab-geometry]]).

[F2] Plane-wave fields: $E_{\rm inc}(x,t)=\operatorname{Re}(e\,e^{i(\kappa\cdot x-\omega t)})$, $B_{\rm inc}=\kappa\times E_{\rm inc}/\omega$ are jointly $C^\infty$ on $\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R$ ([[def-em-plane-wave-definition]], [[def-complex-conjugate-real-imaginary-part-and-modulus]], [[cor-complex-exponential-cartesian-form-modulus-and-eulers-identity]]); in particular, on every compact subset of $\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R$ they and their first derivatives are bounded, and on a compact interval $L\subset I$ the maps $t\mapsto E_{\rm inc}(x_0,t)$, $t\mapsto B_{\rm inc}(x_0,t)$ are continuous ([[thm-extreme-value-metric]], [[thm-continuous-implies-integrable]]). On a compact spatial ball times $L$, integrating each bounded spatial derivative along the segment from $x_0$ to $x$ with [[thm-ftc-second-part]] and [[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]] gives $|E_{\rm inc}(x,t)-E_{\rm inc}(x_0,t)|\le C|x-x_0|$, uniformly for $t\in L$; the componentwise integral bounds are those of [[lem-integral-elementary-bounds]].

[F3] Harmonic response lemma: for $\omega>0$, $T=2\pi/\omega$, $w\in\mathbb C^3$, the particular solution $z_p(t)=\operatorname{Re}(-w\omega^{-2}e^{-i\omega t})$ is $C^\infty$ with $z_p''=\operatorname{Re}(we^{-i\omega t})$ and is $T$-periodic; every $C^2$ solution of that equation on $\mathbb R$ is $z_p+a+bt$ with unique $a,b$; it is $T$-periodic if and only if $b=0$; on any nonempty open interval the local solutions have a unique global extension $Z$; with $b=z'(t_*)-z_p'(t_*)$, and for $b=0$ the mean over every complete period is $a$ ([[lem-em-harmonic-forcing-periodic-response]]).

[F4] Cross-section theorem: for the ideal harmonic model with incident amplitude $e$ and dipole amplitude $p_0$, the total model cross section is $\sigma=\frac{\omega^4|p_0|^2}{6\pi\varepsilon_0^2c^4|e|^2}=\frac{\mu_0^2\omega^4|p_0|^2}{6\pi|e|^2}$, and the scalar branch with $p_0=\alpha e$ is $\frac{\omega^4|\alpha|^2}{6\pi\varepsilon_0^2c^4}$; the result assumes Countable Choice as carried by its suppliers ([[pthm-em-dipole-scattering-cross-section]], [[def-countable-choice]]).

[F5] Elementary calculus and algebra: vector-valued differentiation and integration are componentwise ([[def-vector-valued-derivative-and-integral]]); the second fundamental theorem evaluates $\int_a^bG'=G(b)-G(a)$ for integrable $G'$ ([[thm-ftc-second-part]]), a continuous real function on a closed interval is Riemann integrable ([[thm-continuous-implies-integrable]]), the first fundamental theorem differentiates the integral function ([[thm-ftc-first-part]]), and $m\le f\le M$ on $[a,b]$ gives $m(b-a)\le\int_a^bf\le M(b-a)$ with the corresponding error bound ([[lem-integral-elementary-bounds]]); sums, products and compositions of differentiable maps obey the algebra and chain rules ([[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]], [[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]]); the cross product is bilinear and alternating with $|u\times v|\le|u||v|$ ([[lem-cross-product-is-bilinear-alternating-and-orthogonal]]); and $|e^{i\theta}|=1$ for real $\theta$ ([[cor-complex-exponential-cartesian-form-modulus-and-eulers-identity]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; take the exact integrated coordinate momentum equation of the adopted force law for the family, pass the uniform first-order expansion through the finite-interval integral, differentiate the resulting identity, and then classify the first-order harmonic response with the affine/periodic lemma and substitute into the cross-section theorem.

1.1 [Uniform smallness of the velocity and of $\gamma-1$] Let $K\subset I$ be a nonempty compact interval containing $0$ in its interior (any compact subinterval of $I$ containing $0$ will do); the expansion hypothesis gives $|v_\eta(t)|\le|\eta||\dot r_1(t)|+C_K\eta^2\le|\eta|(\|\dot r_1\|_K+C_K)$ for $|\eta|<\min\{\eta_0,1\}$ and $t\in K$, where $\|\dot r_1\|_K$ is finite by continuity on the compact $K$. Choose $\eta_1\in(0,\min\{\eta_0,1\})$ with $\eta_1(\|\dot r_1\|_K+C_K)\le c/2$; then for $0<|\eta|<\eta_1$ every $t\in K$ has $|v_\eta(t)|/c\le1/2$, so the low-speed clause of [F1] with $\delta=1/2$ gives $|\gamma_\eta(t)-1|\le\frac{|v_\eta(t)|^2}{2c^2(1-1/4)^{3/2}}\le C_1\eta^2$ for a constant $C_1$ depending only on $K,\eta_1$ and the data, uniformly in $t\in K$. [given, F1, algebra]

2.1 [Uniform convergence of the scaled momentum and of its derivative] With $\gamma_\eta=(1-|v_\eta|^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$ and $P_\eta:=m\gamma_\eta v_\eta$, write $\frac1\eta P_\eta=m\gamma_\eta\frac{v_\eta}\eta=m\frac{v_\eta}\eta+m(\gamma_\eta-1)\frac{v_\eta}\eta$; by the expansion hypothesis $\frac{v_\eta}\eta\to\dot r_1$ uniformly on $K$ with error $C_K|\eta|$, and by step 1.1 $|\gamma_\eta-1|\le C_1\eta^2$ while $\bigl|\frac{v_\eta}\eta\bigr|\le\|\dot r_1\|_K+C_K|\eta|$ is uniformly bounded, so $\frac{P_\eta}\eta\to m\dot r_1$ uniformly on $K$ as $\eta\to0$ with $\eta\ne0$. Moreover, dividing the exact coordinate force law of [F1] by $\eta$ gives, for $t\in K$ and $\eta\ne0$, $\frac1\eta P_\eta'(t)=q\bigl[E_{\rm inc}(r_\eta(t),t)+v_\eta(t)\times B_{\rm inc}(r_\eta(t),t)\bigr]$, and this right-hand side converges uniformly on $K$ to $qE_{\rm inc}(x_0,t)$: the spatial Lipschitz bound of [F2] on a compact tube around $\{x_0\}\times K$ gives $|E_{\rm inc}(r_\eta(t),t)-E_{\rm inc}(x_0,t)|\le C|r_\eta(t)-x_0|\to0$ uniformly by the expansion, while $|v_\eta(t)\times B_{\rm inc}(r_\eta(t),t)|\le|v_\eta(t)|\sup|B_{\rm inc}|\le C_2|\eta|$ by [F2] and the bilinearity and norm bound of [F5]. [step 1.1, given, F1, F2, F5, algebra]

3.1 [First-order equation] Fix $\eta\ne0$ with $|\eta|<\eta_1$ and fix a component index $i$. The scalar map $t\mapsto(P_\eta)_i(t)$ is $C^1$ on $K$ with continuous derivative $(P_\eta')_i=q\eta[E_{\rm inc}+v_\eta\times B_{\rm inc}]_i$, so $(P_\eta')_i$ is Riemann integrable on every closed subinterval of $K$ by [F5]; splitting at $0$ and applying the second fundamental theorem [F5] to the intervals $[0,t]$ for $t\ge0$ and $[t,0]$ for $t\le0$ (with the orientation convention of the displayed one-sided differences) gives, for every $t\in K$, $\frac{(P_\eta)_i(t)-(P_\eta)_i(0)}\eta=\int_0^t\frac{(P_\eta')_i(s)}\eta\,ds=q\int_0^t[E_{\rm inc}(r_\eta(s),s)+v_\eta(s)\times B_{\rm inc}(r_\eta(s),s)]_i\,ds$. By the uniform convergence of step 2.1 applied componentwise, the integrands converge uniformly on $K$ to $qE_{\rm inc}(x_0,\cdot)_i$, so the integral bound of [F5] lets $\eta\to0$: the left side tends to $m[(\dot r_1)_i(t)-(\dot r_1)_i(0)]$ and the right side to $q\int_0^tE_{\rm inc}(x_0,s)_i\,ds$. The fundamental theorem [F5] differentiates the right side in $t$ to $qE_{\rm inc}(x_0,t)_i$, and the left side is differentiable with derivative $m(\ddot r_1)_i(t)$; hence $m\ddot r_1(t)=qE_{\rm inc}(x_0,t)$ componentwise on $K$. Every point $t\in I$ lies in such a compact $K\subset I$ containing $0$, so the identity holds on all of $I$ and is clause (a). [step 2.1, F2, F5, algebra]

4.1 [Harmonic classification and the induced dipole] Equation (a) reads $\ddot r_1(t)=\operatorname{Re}(w e^{-i\omega t})$ on $I$ with $w=(q/m)e\,e^{i\kappa\cdot x_0}$ by [F2]. By the local-clause of the harmonic lemma [F3], on $I$ one has $r_1=z_p+a+bt$ for a unique pair $(a,b)$, the same formula defines the unique global extension $Z$ of $r_1$, and $b=0$ exactly when $Z$ is $T$-periodic, equivalently when $r_1'(0)=z_p'(0)=\operatorname{Re}(iw/\omega)$ (differentiating $z_p$ at $0$ with the rules of [F5]). Preparation (P) therefore says exactly $b=0$, so on $I$ we have $r_1=z_p+a$ with $a$ the mean of the periodic extension over every complete period by [F3]. Then $q(r_1-a)=qz_p(t)=\operatorname{Re}(-qw\omega^{-2}e^{-i\omega t})=\operatorname{Re}(p_0e^{-i\omega t})$ with $p_0=-qw/\omega^2=-\frac{q^2}{m\omega^2}e\,e^{i\kappa\cdot x_0}$, because $w=(q/m)e\,e^{i\kappa\cdot x_0}$; writing $e'=e\,e^{i\kappa\cdot x_0}$ one has $|e'|=|e|$ by [F5], so $p_0=\alpha e'$ with $\alpha=-\frac{q^2}{m\omega^2}$. The arbitrary mean $a$ is retained and no mean or periodicity of the nonlinear family is asserted; this is clause (b). [step 3.1, F2, F3, F5, algebra]

5.1 [Thomson cross section] Apply the cross-section formula of [F4] with the incident amplitude $e$ and dipole amplitude $p_0$ of step 4.1: $\sigma=\frac{\omega^4|p_0|^2}{6\pi\varepsilon_0^2c^4|e|^2}$, and $|p_0|^2=\frac{q^4}{m^2\omega^4}|e|^2$ because $|e^{i\kappa\cdot x_0}|=1$; substituting cancels $\omega^4$ and $|e|^2$ and gives $\sigma_{\rm T}=\frac{q^4}{6\pi\varepsilon_0^2m^2c^4}$. Multiplying out, $\frac{8\pi}{3}\bigl(\frac{q^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0mc^2}\bigr)^2=\frac{8\pi}{3}\cdot\frac{q^4}{16\pi^2\varepsilon_0^2m^2c^4}=\frac{q^4}{6\pi\varepsilon_0^2m^2c^4}$, the same value, and it depends neither on $\omega$ nor on the phase factor beyond the unimodular $e'$; this is clause (c). [step 4.1, F4, F5, algebra]

6.1 [Scope and choice accounting] Clause (d) records the exact content: the result is a conditional first variation of the adopted external-force law under the stated family and preparation hypotheses, with the affine drift retained in general and set to zero only by (P) for (b) and (c); no existence, persistence, stability or self-consistency of the family, no self-force, radiation reaction or material content, and no empirical, atomic or frequency-response claim is made. The $\eta^2$ scaling statement is a remark on the leading model: for nonzero $\eta$ the incident amplitude $\eta e$ and the induced amplitude $\eta p_0$ scale together, so the ratio defining $\sigma_{\rm T}$ is unchanged; at $q=0$ the amplitude $p_0=-\frac{q^2}{m\omega^2}e\,e^{i\kappa\cdot x_0}$ and $\sigma_{\rm T}$ vanish by direct substitution without any division by $q$. The only choice principle used is the Countable Choice carried by the cross-section theorem [F4]; the uniform-limit, integral and harmonic-classification arguments use only the explicit suppliers [F1], [F3], [F5] and finite compact intervals. [step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, F1, F3, F4, given] ∎
