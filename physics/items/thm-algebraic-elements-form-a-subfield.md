## Statement

For a field extension $K/F$, the set

$$A=\{a\in K:a\text{ is algebraic over }F\}$$

is a subfield of $K$ containing $F$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field extension $K/F$ and algebraic elements $a,b\in K$.

[L1] A field generated by finitely many algebraic elements is finite over the base ([[thm-finitely-generated-algebraic-extensions-are-finite]]).

[L2] Every element of a finite extension is algebraic over the base ([[thm-finite-field-extensions-are-algebraic]]).

[L3] A subset containing $1$ and closed under subtraction, multiplication, and inverses of nonzero elements is a subfield ([[def-subfield]]).

[L4] Algebraic means annihilated by a nonzero polynomial over the base field ([[def-algebraic-and-transcendental-elements]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Every $c\in F$ is algebraic over $F$, since it is a root of $t-c$, so $F\subseteq A$ and in particular $0,1\in A$. [L4]

1.2 By [L1], $F(a,b)/F$ is finite. Thus [L2] makes all of its elements algebraic over $F$, including $a-b$ and $ab$. [given, L1, L2]

1.3 If $a\ne0$, then $a^{-1}\in F(a)\subseteq F(a,b)$ and is algebraic by the same argument. [given, L1, L2]

2.1 Therefore $A$ satisfies the subfield criterion [L3] and is a subfield containing $F$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, L3] ∎
