## Statement

Use the actual square-zero triplet module for $p\ge2$. Put $N=p-1$, $h=(2-p)/4$, $\beta=(p-2)/(2p)$, $\delta=(p-1)/p$, $\nu=p/(p-1)$, $t=e^{-\alpha/2}$, $w=e^{\beta\alpha}$, $b=e^{(\beta+1)\alpha}$, and $z=Qt$. Set
$$A_p=\binom{3p-2}{p-1},\qquad C_0=(-1)^{p-1}\binom{2p-2}{p-1},\qquad G=Q+\nu(e^{\delta\alpha})_{(-1)}.$$
Define $S_j(a x)$ by $\sum_{j\ge0}S_j(a x)u^j=\exp(a\sum_{k\ge1}x_ku^k/k)$. The actual vector
$$w'=2\nu\bigl(C_0 S_N(-x)-A_p S_N(\delta x)\bigr)w$$
lies in the Virasoro cyclic space generated by $w$, and hence in $N_2=W(p)w\subset M_1=W(p)t$. Moreover $z\in M_1$. The two classes $\bar t,\bar z$ in $M_1/N_2$ are linearly independent of common degree $N$. Their span is stable under the zero modes $o(X)=\widetilde X_{(2p-2)}$ for $X=F,H,E$ and $o(\omega)=\widetilde L(0)$, with matrices in that ordered basis
$$o(F)=\begin{pmatrix}0&A_p\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad o(H)=\begin{pmatrix}-A_p&0\\0&A_p\end{pmatrix},\qquad o(E)=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\-2A_p&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad o(\omega)=(h+N)I.$$
Thus the subspace generated from $\bar t$ by these zero modes has dimension exactly two. This assertion does not presume that it is the entire lowest space; that conclusion additionally requires the lower-bound and strong-generation arguments.

## Proof

1.1 Put $d=(e^{\delta\alpha})_{(-1)}t$ and $f=F_{(2p-2)}b$. The exponential formula gives $d=S_N(\delta x)w$ and $f=S_N(-x)w$: their initial powers are $u^{-N}$ and $u^{-3p+2}$, respectively, so the indicated coefficients select oscillator degree $N$ in each case. At $x_k=-2p$ the generating series for $S_j(a x)$ becomes $(1-u)^{2pa}$. Hence $S_N(\delta x)$ evaluates to $(-1)^N\binom{2p-2}{N}=C_0$, and $S_N(-x)$ evaluates to $\binom{2p+N-1}{N}=A_p$. Thus $Qd=C_0b$ and $Qf=A_pb$. It follows directly that $Qw'=2\nu(C_0A_p-A_pC_0)b=0$. Applying [F4] proves $w'\in U(\mathrm{Vir}_{-})w\subset N_2$. [given, F1, F4, algebra]

2.1 Directly $z=Qt=S_N(x)e^{\alpha/2}\ne0$. The ordinary $F_{(2p-2)}$ has degree shift zero. Its action on $z$ lands at charge $-\alpha/2$ and degree $N$, hence is a scalar multiple of $t$. To compute it, project its exponential field on $z$ to oscillator degree zero: the annihilation exponential replaces $x_k$ in $S_N(x)$ by $2pu^{-k}$ and the initial power is $u^{-p}$. The coefficient of $u^{-2p+1}$ is $S_N(2p,2p,\ldots)=A_p$. Therefore $F_{(2p-2)}z=A_pt$. Also $F_{(2p-2)}t=0$ and $F_{(2p-2)}d=0$, since their possible output charges $-3\alpha/2$ and $(\beta-1)\alpha$ have minimum degrees $4p-2$ and $p+1$, strictly greater than $N$. Finally $Qb=0$ and $Qz=0$ by charge and degree: their possible output charges have vacuum degrees $4p-2$ and $p+1$. Using [F3] gives $Gt=z+\nu d$, $Gz=\nu C_0b$, and $Gw'=Qw'=0$, since $w'$ is in the second sector. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Write $F_0=F_{(2p-2)}$, $H_0=\widetilde H_{(2p-2)}$, and $E_0=\widetilde E_{(2p-2)}$ in this step. By [F3] and step 2.1,
$$H_0t=[G,F_0]t=-A_pt,\qquad H_0z=G(A_pt)-F_0(\nu C_0b)=A_pz+\nu A_pd-\nu C_0f=A_pz-\tfrac12w'.$$
Taking the second commutator on $t$ gives
$$E_0t=G^2F_0t-2GF_0Gt+F_0G^2t=-2A_p(z+\nu d)+2\nu C_0f=-2A_pz+w'.$$
Since $w'\in N_2\subset M_1$ and $A_p\ne0$, this proves $z\in M_1$. On the second sector $H_0$ is ordinary, and its zero-mode derivation expression gives $H_0b=[Q,F_0]b=Qf=A_pb$. Thus the other second commutator yields $E_0z=G(A_pz-w'/2)-H_0(\nu C_0b)=0$. The deformation of $F$ is zero by [F3]. This proves every asserted generator action before taking classes. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The vectors $t$ and $z$ are in the first summand, with different charges $-\alpha/2$ and $\alpha/2$; $z\ne0$ by step 2.1. Since $N_2$ is in the second summand, their classes are linearly independent. They both have degree $N$, and [F2] makes $\widetilde L(0)$ the scalar $h+N$ on them. Reducing the identities in step 3.1 modulo $N_2$ proves the three matrices. Their span is stable, contains $\bar t$, and also contains $\bar z=-o(E)\bar t/(2A_p)$. Hence it is exactly the two-dimensional subspace generated under the four indicated zero modes. [F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
