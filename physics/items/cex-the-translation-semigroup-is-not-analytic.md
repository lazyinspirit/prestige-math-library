## Statement refuted

Assume Dependent Choice ([[def-dependent-choice]]) for the cited integral and semigroup suppliers.

On $X=L^p(\mathbb R)$ with $1\le p<\infty$, let $T(t)f:=f(\cdot+t)$ be the
right-translation semigroup
([[lem-compactly-supported-continuous-functions-are-translation-continuous-in-l-p]],
[[thm-c-c-infinity-rn-is-dense-in-l-p-of-rn]]). Then $(T(t))_{t\ge0}$ is a
strongly continuous semigroup of isometries, its generator is
$$Af=f'\quad\text{with}\quad D(A)=W^{1,p}(\mathbb R)=\{f\in L^p(\mathbb R):f'\in L^p(\mathbb R)\},$$
the derivative being the weak derivative
([[def-weak-derivative-of-a-locally-integrable-function]],
[[def-sobolev-space-wkp-and-its-norm]]), and $(T(t))_{t\ge0}$ is not an
analytic semigroup in the sense of
[[def-complex-sector-and-bounded-analytic-semigroup]]. Two independent
obstructions are recorded: (1) (range) for $f\in L^p\setminus W^{1,p}$ and
every $t>0$ one has $T(t)f\notin D(A)$ because a weak derivative of a
translate would translate back to a weak derivative of $f$; but analytic
semigroups satisfy $T(t)X\subseteq D(A)$ for $t>0$
([[lem-cauchy-estimates-for-an-analytic-semigroup-give-generator-power-bounds]]);
(2) (spectrum) $\sigma(A)={\rm i}\mathbb R$, which meets every sector
$\Sigma_{\pi/2+\delta}$, so $A$ fails the sectorial resolvent condition
([[def-sectorial-operator-with-the-semigroup-sign-convention]]); the bounded
analytic semigroup characterization
[[thm-sectorial-resolvent-characterisation-of-bounded-analytic-semigroups]]
therefore rules out bounded analytic generation. The range obstruction in (1)
rules out even an analytic semigroup extension. Countable Choice is inherited
from the $L^p$ translation-continuity, density and Sobolev vocabulary
([[lem-compactly-supported-continuous-functions-are-translation-continuous-in-l-p]],
[[thm-c-c-infinity-rn-is-dense-in-l-p-of-rn]],
[[def-sobolev-space-wkp-and-its-norm]]); no further choice principle beyond Dependent Choice is used.

**Refuted claim.** A strongly continuous semigroup of isometries on $L^p(\mathbb R)$ generated by a first-order differential operator is analytic, at least after shrinking the sector. The right-translation semigroup is a semigroup of isometries whose generator is differentiation, yet no positive time maps all of $L^p$ into the domain $W^{1,p}$, which analytic semigroups are required to do; independently, the imaginary-axis spectrum blocks every sectorial resolvent estimate.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $1\le p<\infty$, $X=L^p(\mathbb R)$ with the quotient $L^p$ norm, the right-translation family $T(t)f=f(\cdot+t)$ in the convention of [[def-translation-of-a-function-on-rn]], and the generator $(A,D(A))$ of [[def-infinitesimal-generator-of-a-c-zero-semigroup]].

[L1] For $f\in C_c(\mathbb R)$ one has $\|\tau_hf-f\|_p\to0$ as $h\to0$, and $C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$ is dense in $L^p(\mathbb R)$ (both assume Countable Choice) ([[lem-compactly-supported-continuous-functions-are-translation-continuous-in-l-p]], [[thm-c-c-infinity-rn-is-dense-in-l-p-of-rn]]).

[L2] $C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$ is dense in $W^{1,p}(\mathbb R)$ in the Sobolev norm (assuming Countable Choice) ([[cor-compactly-supported-smooth-functions-are-dense-in-wkp-of-rn]], [[def-sobolev-space-wkp-and-its-norm]]).

[L3] $v=D^1u$ weakly means $\int_{\mathbb R}u\varphi'=-\int_{\mathbb R}v\varphi$ for every $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$; $W^{1,p}(\mathbb R)$ consists of the $L^p$ classes with weak derivative in $L^p$ ([[def-weak-derivative-of-a-locally-integrable-function]], [[def-sobolev-space-wkp-and-its-norm]]).

[L4] The $L^p$ norm is translation invariant, Lebesgue measure and measurability are translation invariant, and Fubini applies to absolutely integrable integrands on products of $\sigma$-finite spaces ([[def-l-p-space-as-a-quotient-by-null-functions]], [[thm-lebesgue-outer-measure-and-measurability-are-translation-invariant]], [[thm-fubini-theorem-for-l-one-on-sigma-finite-product-spaces]]).

[L5] A strongly continuous semigroup is a family with $T(0)=I$, $T(t+s)=T(t)T(s)$ and continuous orbits; its generator has domain consisting of the vectors with convergent right difference quotients ([[def-strongly-continuous-semigroup]], [[def-infinitesimal-generator-of-a-c-zero-semigroup]]).

[L6] A bounded analytic semigroup of angle $\delta>0$ with generator $A$ satisfies $T(t)X\subseteq D(A^m)$ and $T^{(m)}(t)=A^mT(t)$ for every $t>0$ and $m\ge1$ ([[lem-cauchy-estimates-for-an-analytic-semigroup-give-generator-power-bounds]], [[def-complex-sector-and-bounded-analytic-semigroup]]).

[L7] $A$ is sectorial of angle $\delta>0$ at vertex $0$ only if $\Sigma_{\pi/2+\delta}\subseteq\rho(A)$; the conditions (a)-(e) of the characterisation theorem are equivalent, so a sectorial operator generates a bounded analytic semigroup on each smaller sector ([[def-sectorial-operator-with-the-semigroup-sign-convention]], [[thm-sectorial-resolvent-characterisation-of-bounded-analytic-semigroups]]).

[L8] A strongly measurable curve is Bochner integrable exactly when the integral of its norm is finite, and the Bochner integral obeys the norm inequality ([[thm-bochner-integrability-criterion]], [[lem-bochner-integral-norm-inequality]], [[def-bochner-integrable-function]]).

## Counterexample

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Strong continuity and isometries. Each $T(t)$ is linear and, by translation invariance of the $L^p$ norm [L4], an isometry with $\|T(t)f\|_p=\|f\|_p$; the functional equation $T(t+s)=T(t)T(s)$ and $T(0)=I$ are immediate, and the orbit of $f\in C_c(\mathbb R)$ is continuous at $0$ by [L1]; for general $f\in L^p$ and $\varepsilon>0$ choose $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$ with $\|f-\varphi\|_p<\varepsilon$ by [L1] and estimate $\|T(t)f-f\|_p\le\|T(t)(f-\varphi)\|_p+\|T(t)\varphi-\varphi\|_p+\|\varphi-f\|_p\le2\varepsilon+\|T(t)\varphi-\varphi\|_p$, so strong continuity extends to all of $X$; hence $(T(t))_{t\ge0}$ is a strongly continuous semigroup of isometries. [L1, L4, L5, given, algebra]

1.2 The generator is differentiation on $W^{1,p}$. If the difference quotient of $f$ converges in $L^p$ to $g$, then for every test function $\varphi$ the substitution $y=x+h$ and translation invariance give $\int g\varphi=\lim_h\int f(y)\frac{\varphi(y-h)-\varphi(y)}h\,dy=-\int f\varphi'$ (uniform convergence of the difference quotients of $\varphi$ with compactly supported domination), so $g=D^1f$ weakly and $f\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb R)$ by [L3]; conversely for $f\in W^{1,p}$ and $\varphi\in C_c^\infty$ one has $\|\frac{T(h)\varphi-\varphi}{h}-\varphi'\|_p\to0$ while the shift inequality $\|(T(h)-I)u\|_p\le|h|\,\|u'\|_p$ for $u\in W^{1,p}$ follows from $u(x+h)-u(x)=\int_0^hu'(x+s)ds$ for smooth $u$ and extends by the density [L2]; given $\delta>0$ choose $\varphi\in C_c^\infty$ with $\|f-\varphi\|_{W^{1,p}}<\delta$, write $u=f-\varphi$, and estimate $\|\frac{T(h)f-f}{h}-f'\|_p\le\|\frac{T(h)\varphi-\varphi}{h}-\varphi'\|_p+2\|u'\|_p\le\|\frac{T(h)\varphi-\varphi}{h}-\varphi'\|_p+2\delta$, so the limsup is $0$ and $W^{1,p}(\mathbb R)\subseteq D(A)$ with $Af=f'$; hence $D(A)=W^{1,p}(\mathbb R)$. [L2, L3, L4, L5, given, algebra]

1.3 The range obstruction. Translation commutes with weak differentiation: if $v=D^1u$ and $h\in\mathbb R$, then for every test function $\varphi$ the identities $\int u(x+h)\varphi'(x)dx=\int u(y)\varphi'(y-h)dy=-\int v(y)\varphi(y-h)dy=-\int v(x+h)\varphi(x)dx$ show $v(\cdot+h)=D^1(u(\cdot+h))$; hence if $T(t)f=f(\cdot+t)\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb R)$ for some $t>0$, then $f=T(-t)(T(t)f)$ is a translate of a $W^{1,p}$ function and lies in $W^{1,p}(\mathbb R)$, a contradiction whenever $f\in L^p\setminus W^{1,p}$; such $f$ exist, for instance $f=\mathbf 1_{[0,1]}$, whose would-be weak derivative must vanish a.e. off the two jump points by testing away from them, hence a.e. everywhere, which is incompatible with $\int_{\mathbb R}f\varphi'=\varphi(1)-\varphi(0)$ for a test function with $\varphi(1)\neq\varphi(0)$; therefore $T(t)f\notin D(A)$ for every $t>0$. [L3, L4, given, algebra]

2.1 The translation semigroup is not analytic. If $T$ had any analytic extension, norm differentiability at $t>0$ would give $h^{-1}(T(h)-I)T(t)y=h^{-1}(T(t+h)-T(t))y\to T'(t)y$ for every $y$. The generator definition [L5] would therefore put $T(t)y$ in $D(A)$, with no global sector bound required; [step 1.3] exhibits a vector $f\in X$ with $T(t)f\notin D(A)$ for every $t>0$, while [step 1.2] identifies $D(A)=W^{1,p}$ as the domain of the generator, so $(T(t))_{t\ge0}$ admits no analytic extension with generator $A$, bounded or not. [step 1.2, step 1.3, L6, given, algebra]

2.2 The half-planes lie in the resolvent set. For $\operatorname{Re}\lambda>0$ and $f\in X$ put $R_\lambda f:=\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}T(t)f\,dt$ and for $\operatorname{Re}\lambda<0$ put $R_\lambda f:=-\int_0^\infty e^{\lambda t}T(-t)f\,dt$; both are absolutely convergent Bochner integrals by [L8] because the isometries of [step 1.1] give $\|e^{-\lambda t}T(t)f\|\le e^{-\operatorname{Re}\lambda t}\|f\|$, and the substitution $y=x+t$ (respectively $y=x-t$) with [L4] gives for every test function $\varphi$ the identity $\int(R_\lambda f)\varphi'=\int f\varphi-\lambda\int(R_\lambda f)\varphi$ in both cases, so $R_\lambda f\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb R)$ with weak derivative $\lambda R_\lambda f-f$, that is $(\lambda I-A)R_\lambda f=f$ with $A$ as in [step 1.2]; conversely, for $g\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb R)$ the same Fubini computation with the weak-derivative identity of [L3] gives $\int(R_\lambda(\lambda g-g'))\varphi=\int g\varphi$ for every test function $\varphi$, so $R_\lambda(\lambda I-A)g=g$; hence $\lambda\in\rho(A)$ with $R(\lambda,A)=R_\lambda$ for every $\lambda\notin{\rm i}\mathbb R$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, L3, L4, L8, given, algebra]

2.3 The imaginary axis lies in the spectrum. Fix $\xi\in\mathbb R$, choose $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$ with $\varphi\not\equiv0$ and set $u_N(x):=\varphi(x/N)e^{{\rm i}\xi x}\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb R)$ for $N\ge1$; then $(A-{\rm i}\xi)u_N=u_N'-{\rm i}\xi u_N=N^{-1}\varphi'(x/N)e^{{\rm i}\xi x}$ by [step 1.2], and by the substitution $x=Ny$ in the Lebesgue integrals $\|(A-{\rm i}\xi)u_N\|_p=N^{1/p-1}\|\varphi'\|_p$ while $\|u_N\|_p=N^{1/p}\|\varphi\|_p$, so the ratios $\|(A-{\rm i}\xi)u_N\|_p/\|u_N\|_p=N^{-1}\|\varphi'\|_p/\|\varphi\|_p$ tend to $0$; if ${\rm i}\xi$ lay in $\rho(A)$ the bounded inverse would give the positive lower bound $\|(A-{\rm i}\xi)u\|_p\ge\|u\|_p/\|R({\rm i}\xi,A)\|$ for all $u\in D(A)$, a contradiction for large $N$; hence ${\rm i}\xi\in\sigma(A)$ for every $\xi$, that is ${\rm i}\mathbb R\subseteq\sigma(A)$. [step 1.2, L3, L4, given, algebra]

3.1 The spectral obstruction and the conclusion. By [step 2.2] and [step 2.3] one has $\sigma(A)={\rm i}\mathbb R$; since the point ${\rm i}$ has argument $\pi/2$, it lies in $\Sigma_{\pi/2+\delta}$ for every $\delta>0$, so no sector $\Sigma_{\pi/2+\delta}$ is contained in $\rho(A)$ and [L7] rules out sectoriality of every positive exponent; the characterisation theorem [L7] therefore rules out generation of a bounded analytic semigroup, independently of the range obstruction of [step 2.1], which already excludes every analytic extension; the restriction $p<\infty$ is essential, since right translation is not strongly continuous on $L^\infty$, and the argument uses no choice principle beyond Dependent Choice, which implies the Countable Choice required by the $L^p$ translation-continuity, density and Sobolev vocabulary. [step 2.1, step 2.2, step 2.3, L7, given, algebra] ∎
