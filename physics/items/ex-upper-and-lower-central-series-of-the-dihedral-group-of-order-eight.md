## Example

For
$$D_8=\langle r,s\mid r^4=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle,$$
the lower central series is $D_8>\langle r^2\rangle>1$ and the upper central series is $1<\langle r^2\rangle<D_8$. Hence $D_8$ is nilpotent of class two.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The displayed presentation of $D_8$.

[F1] $\gamma_{r+1}(G)=[G,\gamma_r(G)]$ ([[def-subgroup-commutator-and-lower-central-series]]).

[F2] $Z_1(G)=Z(G)$ and $Z_{r+1}(G)/Z_r(G)=Z(G/Z_r(G))$ ([[def-upper-central-series]]).

[L1] For $c=2$, the conditions $Z_2(G)=G$ and $\gamma_3(G)=1$ are equivalent to the existence of a central series of length two, and the least terminating index is the nilpotency class ([[thm-upper-and-lower-central-characterizations-of-nilpotence]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The relation $srs=r^{-1}$ gives $[s,r]=srs^{-1}r^{-1}=r^{-2}=r^2$. Every commutator is generated by this one because $D_8$ is generated by $r,s$, so $\gamma_2(D_8)=\langle r^2\rangle$. [given, F1, algebra]

1.2 No element outside $\langle r^2\rangle$ is central: $r,r^3$ do not commute with $s$, and $sr^k$ does not commute with $r$. Hence $Z(D_8)=\langle r^2\rangle$. [given, algebra]

2.1 The element $r^2$ commutes with $r$ and $s$, so $\langle r^2\rangle\le Z(D_8)$ and $\gamma_3(D_8)=[D_8,\langle r^2\rangle]=1$. [step 1.1, F1, algebra]

2.2 The quotient $D_8/\langle r^2\rangle$ is abelian, so its center is the whole quotient and [F2] gives $Z_2(D_8)=D_8$. [step 1.2, F2, algebra]

3.1 Steps 1.1 to 2.2 give both asserted central series, and [L1] gives nilpotency class exactly two. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 1.2, step 2.2, L1] ∎
