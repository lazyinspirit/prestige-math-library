## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice and the Axiom of Choice ([[def-countable-choice]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Fix $m>0$, $\hbar>0$, $\sigma>0$ and real $x_0,p_0$, and let

$$\psi_0(x):=(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/4}\exp\Bigl(-\frac{(x-x_0)^2}{4\sigma^2}\Bigr)\exp\Bigl(i\frac{p_0(x-x_0)}{\hbar}\Bigr),\qquad x\in\mathbb R .$$

Let $H_0$ be the free operator of clause (b) of [[thm-nrqm-composites-real-multipliers-and-free-bounded-potential-operators]] with $c:=2\pi^2\hbar^2/m$ and $b:=0$, i.e.

$$H_0:=\mathcal F_2^*\,M_{2\pi^2\hbar^2\xi^2/m}\,\mathcal F_2,\qquad D(H_0)=\{f\in L^2(\mathbb R):\xi^2\mathcal F_2f\in L^2(\mathbb R)\},$$

which is self-adjoint with $D(H_0)=W^{2,2}(\mathbb R;\mathbb C)$ and $H_0f=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\Delta f$ for $f\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$. Let $U$ be the momentum transform of [[lem-nrqm-composites-momentum-fourier-jacobian]], the surjective isometry $L^2(\mathbb R,dx)\to L^2(\mathbb R,dp)$ with $U=D_\hbar\circ\mathcal F_2$ on $\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$. For $t\in\mathbb R$ put $\tau:=\frac{\hbar t}{2m\sigma^2}$ and

$$\psi_t(x):=(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/4}(1+i\tau)^{-1/2}\exp\Bigl(-\frac{(x-x_0-p_0t/m)^2}{4\sigma^2(1+i\tau)}+i\frac{p_0(x-x_0)}{\hbar}-i\frac{p_0^2t}{2m\hbar}\Bigr),$$

where $z^{-1/2}:=\exp\bigl(-\tfrac12\operatorname{Log}z\bigr)$ is the principal branch of [[def-complex-logarithms-principal-logarithm-and-complex-powers]], legitimate at $z=1+i\tau$ because $\operatorname{Re}(1+i\tau)=1>0$. Let $Qf(x)=xf(x)$ and $Pf(x)=-i\hbar f'(x)$ act on $\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$. Then:

(a) $\psi_0\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$, $\|\psi_0\|_2=1$, and for every $t$ one has $\psi_t\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R)\subseteq D(H_0)$; the map $t\mapsto\psi_t$ is continuously differentiable $\mathbb R\to L^2(\mathbb R)$ with $\psi_t\in D(H_0)$ and $\frac{d}{dt}\psi_t=-\frac{i}{\hbar}H_0\psi_t$, and consequently $\psi_t=e^{-itH_0/\hbar}\psi_0$, where $e^{-itH_0/\hbar}:=e^{itT}$ with $T:=-H_0/\hbar$ denotes the unitary group generated by the self-adjoint operator $T$ ([[lem-self-adjoint-operator-generates-a-strongly-continuous-unitary-group]]).

(b) _Position density._ $|\psi_t(x)|^2=\bigl(2\pi\sigma^2(1+\tau^2)\bigr)^{-1/2}\exp\bigl(-(x-x_0-p_0t/m)^2/(2\sigma^2(1+\tau^2))\bigr)$; it is a probability density of mean $x_0+p_0t/m$ and variance $\sigma^2(1+\tau^2)$.

(c) _Momentum density._ $|U\psi_t(p)|^2=\sqrt{2/\pi}\,(\sigma/\hbar)\exp\bigl(-2\sigma^2(p-p_0)^2/\hbar^2\bigr)=|U\psi_0(p)|^2$ for almost every $p$; it is a probability density of mean $p_0$ and variance $\hbar^2/(4\sigma^2)$, independent of $t$.

(d) _Standard deviations._ With $\Delta Q:=\|(Q-\langle Q\psi_t,\psi_t\rangle)\psi_t\|_2$ and $\Delta P:=\|(P-\langle P\psi_t,\psi_t\rangle)\psi_t\|_2$ one has $\Delta Q=\sigma\sqrt{1+\tau^2}$, $\Delta P=\hbar/(2\sigma)$ and

$$\Delta Q\,\Delta P=\frac{\hbar}{2}\sqrt{1+\tau^2}\ \ge\ \frac{\hbar}{2},$$

with equality exactly at $t=0$; the lower bound $\hbar/2$ is the one supplied by clause (d) of [[lem-nrqm-composites-weak-form-robertson-uncertainty]] with $c=\hbar$.

(e) _Scope._ These are exact conditional calculations for the free model defined by $H_0$; no measurement, instrument or laboratory preparation is described, and nothing here asserts that a physical wave packet is Gaussian.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $m,\hbar,\sigma>0$, $x_0,p_0\in\mathbb R$, the vector $\psi_0$ above, the operator $H_0$ and the transform $U$.

[F1] $\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$ consists of the $C^\infty$ functions all of whose polynomial-weighted derivatives are bounded; every Schwartz function lies in $L^1\cap L^2$ ([[def-schwartz-space-and-its-seminorms]]).

[F2] $U:L^2(\mathbb R,dx)\to L^2(\mathbb R,dp)$ is a surjective complex-linear isometry with $U=D_\hbar\mathcal F_2$ on $\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$, where $D_\hbar g(p)=(2\pi\hbar)^{-1/2}g\bigl(p/(2\pi\hbar)\bigr)$; being a surjective isometry, $U$ preserves inner products and $U^*=U^{-1}$ ([[lem-nrqm-composites-momentum-fourier-jacobian]]).

[F3] If $\operatorname{Re}z>0$ then $\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-zx^2}dx=\sqrt\pi\,z^{-1/2}$ for the branch $z^{-1/2}=\exp(-\tfrac12\operatorname{Log}z)$, and for $G_1(z):=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-zx^2}dx$ one has $G_1'(z)=-\int_{\mathbb R}x^2e^{-zx^2}dx$ ([[lem-nrqm-composites-complex-gaussian-continuation]](a),(b)).

[F4] $H_0$ is self-adjoint, $D(H_0)=W^{2,2}(\mathbb R;\mathbb C)=\{f\in L^2:\xi^2\mathcal F_2f\in L^2\}$ with equivalent norms on this set, and $H_0f=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\Delta f$ for $f\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$ ([[thm-nrqm-composites-real-multipliers-and-free-bounded-potential-operators]](b)). Here $\mathcal F_2$ is the Plancherel transform extending the $L^1$ transform $\widehat f(\xi)=\int f(x)e^{-2\pi ix\xi}dx$; it is a surjective isometry preserving inner products ([[thm-plancherel]], [[def-fourier-transform-on-l-one-of-rn]]).

[F5] For a self-adjoint operator $T$ the operators $V(t):=e^{itT}$ form a strongly continuous one-parameter unitary group with $V(t)D(T)=D(T)$, $TV(t)=V(t)T$ on $D(T)$, and $\lim_{s\to0}\frac1s\bigl(V(s)x-x\bigr)=iTx$ exactly for $x\in D(T)$ ([[lem-self-adjoint-operator-generates-a-strongly-continuous-unitary-group]]).

[F6] Dominated convergence: if $f_n\to f$ pointwise and $|f_n|\le g$ for a fixed integrable $g$, then $\int f_n\to\int f$ ([[thm-dominated-convergence]]). A continuous real function on an interval has a primitive and $\int_a^bG'=G(b)-G(a)$ for it ([[cor-primitives-of-a-continuous-function]]); the integral over $\mathbb R$ is the limit of the compact-interval integrals when it exists ([[def-improper-integral-at-infinity]]); $(fg)'=f'g+fg'$ ([[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]]); for differentiable $u,v$ with integrable derivatives on $[a,b]$, $\int_a^buv'=u(b)v(b)-u(a)v(a)-\int_a^bu'v$ ([[thm-integration-by-parts]]). Substitutions: an invertible linear $T:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ scales Lebesgue measure by $|\det T|$ ([[thm-linear-change-of-variables-for-lebesgue-measure]]), and $\lambda_1(E+h)=\lambda_1(E)$ for every Lebesgue measurable $E$ and $h\in\mathbb R$ ([[thm-lebesgue-outer-measure-and-measurability-are-translation-invariant]]); hence for every nonnegative measurable $H$ and every real $x_0$, $\int_{\mathbb R}H(x-x_0)dx=\int_{\mathbb R}H(y)dy$, by approximation with simple functions and monotone convergence ([[thm-monotone-convergence-for-the-integral]]).

[F7] For every unit $f\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$ and every $c>0$, with $Qf(x)=xf(x)$ and $P_cf(x)=-icf'(x)$, one has $\Delta Q\,\Delta P_c\ge c/2$, the differences being centered norms ([[lem-nrqm-composites-weak-form-robertson-uncertainty]](d)).

## Proof

1.1 [The initial packet is a unit Schwartz vector] The derivative $\partial_x^k\psi_0$ equals $P_k(x)\psi_0(x)$ for a polynomial $P_k$: this follows by induction with the product rule [F6] applied to the three factors $(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/4}$, $e^{-(x-x_0)^2/(4\sigma^2)}$ and the bounded factor $e^{ip_0(x-x_0)/\hbar}$, the last being differentiated once and then constant. Since $x\mapsto x^jP_k(x)e^{-(x-x_0)^2/(4\sigma^2)}$ is bounded for all $j,k$ (the exponential decays faster than any polynomial), $\psi_0\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$ [F1]. By [F6] $\int_{\mathbb R}|\psi_0(x)|^2dx=(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/2}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-y^2/(2\sigma^2)}dy=(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/2}\sqrt\pi\,(2\sigma^2)^{1/2}=1$, using [F3] with $z=1/(2\sigma^2)$ and the substitution $x=x_0+y$. [F1, F3, F6, algebra]

1.2 [The complex shift identity] For $\operatorname{Re}a>0$ and $b\in\mathbb R$ one has $\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-ay^2}e^{iby}dy=\sqrt{\pi/a}\,e^{-b^2/(4a)}$ with $\sqrt{\pi/a}:=\sqrt\pi\,a^{-1/2}$: setting $F(b):=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-ay^2+iby}dy$ (absolutely convergent since $|e^{-ay^2+iby}|=e^{-\operatorname{Re}(a)y^2}$ is integrable) and $G_R(b):=\int_{-R}^Re^{-ay^2+iby}dy$, the fundamental theorem and [F6] give $\frac{d}{db}G_R(b)=i\int_{-R}^Rye^{-ay^2+iby}dy$, and since $|y|e^{-\operatorname{Re}(a)y^2}$ is integrable, $G_R\to F$ pointwise with the difference quotients converging, so $F$ is differentiable with $F'(b)=i\int_{\mathbb R}ye^{-ay^2+iby}dy$; integrating $\partial_y(e^{-ay^2+iby})=(-2ay+ib)e^{-ay^2+iby}$ over $[-R,R]$ gives $\int_{-R}^R(-2ay+ib)e^{-ay^2+iby}dy=e^{-aR^2+ibR}-e^{-aR^2-ibR}$, of modulus $2e^{-\operatorname{Re}(a)R^2}\to0$, so [F6] as $R\to\infty$ gives $-2aF_y(b)+ibF(b)=0$ with $F_y(b):=\int_{\mathbb R}ye^{-ay^2+iby}dy$, hence $F_y=\frac{ib}{2a}F$ and $F'=-\frac{b}{2a}F$; the product rule [F6] gives $\frac{d}{db}[F(b)e^{b^2/(4a)}]=0$, so the product is constant (fundamental theorem applied to real and imaginary parts [F6]) and its value at $b=0$ is $F(0)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-ay^2}dy=\sqrt\pi\,a^{-1/2}$ by [F3]. [F3, F6, algebra]

1.3 [The free operator in momentum coordinates] Let $B$ be the multiplication operator $(B\varphi)(p)=\frac{p^2}{2m}\varphi(p)$ on $D(B):=\{\varphi\in L^2(dp):p^2\varphi\in L^2\}$; then $U^*BU=H_0$ with $D(H_0)=U^*D(B)$, and $H_0U^*=U^*B$, because $D_\hbar^*=D_\hbar^{-1}$ and $(D_\hbar^{-1}M_gD_\hbar h)(\xi)=g(2\pi\hbar\xi)h(\xi)$ for every Borel $g$ and every $h\in L^2$, so with $g(p)=p^2/(2m)$ one has $U^*BU=\mathcal F_2^*D_\hbar^*M_{p^2/(2m)}D_\hbar\mathcal F_2=\mathcal F_2^*M_{2\pi^2\hbar^2\xi^2/m}\mathcal F_2=H_0$ as operators [F2, F4]; for the domains, $f\in D(H_0)$ means $\xi^2\mathcal F_2f\in L^2$, which by $Uf(p)=(2\pi\hbar)^{-1/2}\mathcal F_2f(p/(2\pi\hbar))$ is equivalent to $p^2Uf\in L^2$ (the substitution $\xi=p/(2\pi\hbar)$ and the constants $(2\pi\hbar)^{\pm2}$ preserve $L^2$-membership), i.e. to $Uf\in D(B)$, so $D(H_0)=U^*D(B)$, the identity $U^*BU=H_0$ holds on it, and multiplying on the right by $U^*$ gives $H_0U^*=U^*BUU^*=U^*B$. [F2, F4, algebra]

2.1 [Fourier transform of the initial packet] Since $\psi_0\in L^1\cap L^2$ [step 1.1], $\mathcal F_2\psi_0$ is computed by the $L^1$ integral [F4]; substituting $x=x_0+y$ [F6] and using $\psi_0(x_0+y)=(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/4}e^{-y^2/(4\sigma^2)}e^{ip_0y/\hbar}$ gives $\mathcal F_2\psi_0(\xi)=(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/4}e^{-2\pi ix_0\xi}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-y^2/(4\sigma^2)}e^{i(p_0/\hbar-2\pi\xi)y}dy=(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/4}e^{-2\pi ix_0\xi}2\sigma\sqrt\pi\,e^{-\sigma^2(p_0/\hbar-2\pi\xi)^2}$ by step 1.2 with $a=1/(4\sigma^2)$ and $b=p_0/\hbar-2\pi\xi$; since $\psi_0\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$, $U\psi_0=D_\hbar\mathcal F_2\psi_0$ [F2], so $\varphi_0:=U\psi_0$ satisfies $\varphi_0(p)=(2\pi\hbar)^{-1/2}\mathcal F_2\psi_0(p/(2\pi\hbar))=Ke^{-ix_0p/\hbar}e^{-\sigma^2(p-p_0)^2/\hbar^2}$ with $K:=(2\pi\hbar)^{-1/2}(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/4}2\sigma\sqrt\pi$ and $K^2=\sqrt{2/\pi}\,\sigma/\hbar$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, F2, F4, F6, algebra]

2.2 [The packet is Schwartz and its modulus is the spreading Gaussian] Putting $z_t:=1/(1+i\tau)$ (so $\operatorname{Re}z_t=1/(1+\tau^2)>0$) and $c_t:=x_0+p_0t/m$, one has $|\psi_t(x)|=(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/4}|1+i\tau|^{-1/2}e^{-\operatorname{Re}(z_t)(x-c_t)^2/(4\sigma^2)}=(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/4}(1+\tau^2)^{-1/4}e^{-(x-c_t)^2/(4\sigma^2(1+\tau^2))}$; the remaining factor $e^{-iz_t(x-c_t)^2/(4\sigma^2)}$ times a unimodular phase has $x$-derivatives that are polynomials times these bounded factors, so by the argument of step 1.1 every polynomial-weighted derivative of $\psi_t$ is bounded and $\psi_t\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$; in particular $\psi_t\in L^1\cap L^2$ and $\psi_t\in D(H_0)$ by [F4]. [step 1.1, F1, F4, algebra]

3.1 [Momentum coordinates of the packet] With $A_t:=(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/4}(1+i\tau)^{-1/2}$, $a_t:=1/(4\sigma^2(1+i\tau))$ and $b_\xi:=p_0/\hbar-2\pi\xi$, substituting $x=c_t+y$ [F6] in the $L^1$ integral [F4] of the packet $\psi_t=A_te^{-ip_0^2t/(2m\hbar)}e^{-a_t(x-c_t)^2}e^{i(p_0/\hbar)(x-x_0)}$ [step 2.2] gives $\mathcal F_2\psi_t(\xi)=A_t\sqrt{\pi/a_t}\,e^{ip_0^2t/(2m\hbar)}e^{-2\pi i\xi c_t}e^{-b_\xi^2/(4a_t)}$ by step 1.2 with $a=a_t$ (legal since $\operatorname{Re}a_t>0$); since $\psi_t\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$, $U\psi_t=D_\hbar\mathcal F_2\psi_t$ [F2], and with $p=2\pi\hbar\xi$, $\sqrt{\pi/a_t}=2\sigma\sqrt\pi(1+i\tau)^{1/2}$, $A_t(1+i\tau)^{1/2}=(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/4}$ and $1/(4a_t)=\sigma^2(1+i\tau)$ one gets $U\psi_t(p)=Ke^{ip_0^2t/(2m\hbar)}e^{-ipc_t/\hbar}e^{-\sigma^2(p-p_0)^2/\hbar^2}e^{-it(p-p_0)^2/(2m\hbar)}$ with $K$ as in step 2.1. [step 1.2, step 2.1, step 2.2, F2, F4, F6, algebra]

3.2 [Position density, mean and variance] By step 2.2, $|\psi_t(x)|^2=(2\pi\sigma^2(1+\tau^2))^{-1/2}e^{-(x-c_t)^2/(2d_t^2)}$ with $c_t=x_0+p_0t/m$ and $d_t:=\sigma\sqrt{1+\tau^2}$; substituting $x=c_t+d_tu$ and using $\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-u^2/2}du=\sqrt{2\pi}$ (that is [F3] with $z=1/2$) gives $\int_{\mathbb R}|\psi_t|^2=1$, while the centering $\int_{\mathbb R}(x-c_t)|\psi_t(x)|^2dx=d_t\int_{\mathbb R}u\frac{e^{-u^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}du=0$ and $\int_{\mathbb R}(x-c_t)^2|\psi_t(x)|^2dx=d_t^2\int_{\mathbb R}u^2\frac{e^{-u^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}du=d_t^2$, since $\int_{\mathbb R}u^2e^{-u^2/2}du=-G_1'(1/2)=\sqrt{2\pi}$ by [F3]; hence the density has mean $c_t=x_0+p_0t/m$ and variance $\sigma^2(1+\tau^2)$, which is clause (b). [step 2.2, F3, F6, algebra]

4.1 [The packet is the free evolution of the momentum Gaussian] With $\theta_t(p):=tp^2/(2m\hbar)$ and $\varphi_t:=e^{-i\theta_t}\varphi_0$, steps 2.1 and 3.1 give $U\psi_t(p)=e^{-itp^2/(2m\hbar)}\varphi_0(p)=\varphi_t(p)$ for every $p$, because the two exponents differ by $i\bigl(p_0^2t/(2m\hbar)-p(x_0+p_0t/m)/\hbar-t(p-p_0)^2/(2m\hbar)+px_0/\hbar+tp^2/(2m\hbar)\bigr)=it\bigl(p_0^2-2pp_0-(p-p_0)^2+p^2\bigr)/(2m\hbar)=0$; hence $U\psi_t=\varphi_t$ and, $U$ being a surjective isometry [F2], $\psi_t=U^*\varphi_t$. [step 2.1, step 3.1, F2, algebra]

4.2 [Momentum density, mean and variance] By steps 2.1 and 3.1, $|U\psi_t(p)|^2=|\varphi_0(p)|^2=K^2e^{-2\sigma^2(p-p_0)^2/\hbar^2}=\sqrt{2/\pi}\frac{\sigma}{\hbar}e^{-(p-p_0)^2/(2w^2)}$ with $w:=\hbar/(2\sigma)$ and $K^2=\sqrt{2/\pi}\,\sigma/\hbar$; substituting $p=p_0+wu$ as in step 3.2 gives total mass $1$, mean $p_0$ and variance $w^2=\hbar^2/(4\sigma^2)$, and the density does not depend on $t$; this is clause (c). [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, F3, F6, algebra]

5.1 [The momentum path solves the momentum equation] Put $q(p):=p^2/(2m\hbar)$ and $\varphi_t(p):=e^{-itq(p)}\varphi_0(p)$. The Gaussian formula of step 2.1 gives $q\varphi_0\in L^2(dp)$ and $p^2\varphi_0\in L^2(dp)$, so $\varphi_t\in D(B)$ for every $t$. For fixed $t$ and $h\ne0$, $\frac{\varphi_{t+h}(p)-\varphi_t(p)}h=\varphi_t(p)\frac{e^{-ihq(p)}-1}h$ tends pointwise to $-iq(p)\varphi_t(p)=-(i/\hbar)\frac{p^2}{2m}\varphi_t(p)$. Since $|e^{-is}-1|\le|s|$, the difference between the quotient and its limit has squared modulus at most $4|q(p)\varphi_0(p)|^2$, an integrable function; dominated convergence [F6] therefore gives convergence in $L^2(dp)$. Also $\varphi_{t+h}\to\varphi_t$ in $L^2(dp)$ by pointwise convergence and the integrable square majorant $4|\varphi_0|^2$, so $t\mapsto\varphi_t$ is continuous and $C^1$ with $\varphi_t'=-(i/\hbar)B\varphi_t$. Consequently $\psi_t=U^*\varphi_t$ [step 4.1] is $C^1$ in $L^2$, lies in $D(H_0)=U^*D(B)$, and $\frac{d}{dt}\psi_t=U^*\varphi_t'= -\frac{i}{\hbar}U^*B\varphi_t=-\frac{i}{\hbar}H_0U^*\varphi_t=-\frac{i}{\hbar}H_0\psi_t$, using that $U^*$ is an isometry [F2] and $H_0U^*=U^*B$ from step 1.3; at $t=0$ the formula of the Statement reduces to $\psi_0$. [step 1.3, step 2.1, step 4.1, F2, F6, algebra]

6.1 [Uniqueness of the Cauchy problem] Let $V(t):=e^{itT}$ with $T:=-H_0/\hbar$ [F5]; since $\psi_0\in D(H_0)=D(T)$ [step 1.1, F4], one has $V(t)\psi_0\in D(T)$ for all $t$ and $t\mapsto V(t)\psi_0$ is $C^1$ with $\frac{d}{dt}V(t)\psi_0=iTV(t)\psi_0=-\frac{i}{\hbar}H_0V(t)\psi_0$, because [F5] gives $\frac1h(V(t+h)-V(t))\psi_0=V(t)\frac1h(V(h)\psi_0-\psi_0)\to V(t)(iT\psi_0)$ as $\psi_0\in D(T)$ and $V(t)$ is bounded, and $TV(t)=V(t)T$ on $D(T)$ by [F5]; by step 5.1, $w_t:=\psi_t-V(t)\psi_0$ is $C^1$ with $w_t\in D(H_0)$ and $w_t'=-\frac{i}{\hbar}H_0w_t$, and $w_0=0$ since $V(0)=I$ and the Statement's formula at $t=0$ is the initial packet; for the real function $t\mapsto\|w_t\|^2$ one has $\frac{\|w_{t+h}\|^2-\|w_t\|^2}{h}=2\operatorname{Re}\langle w_t,\frac{w_{t+h}-w_t}{h}\rangle+\frac{\|w_{t+h}-w_t\|^2}{h}\to2\operatorname{Re}\langle w_t,w_t'\rangle$, the last term tending to $0$; now $\langle w_t,w_t'\rangle=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle w_t,H_0w_t\rangle$ and $\langle w_t,H_0w_t\rangle=\langle H_0w_t,w_t\rangle$ is real because $H_0$ is self-adjoint, hence symmetric, on $D(H_0)$ [F4], so $\operatorname{Re}\langle w_t,w_t'\rangle=0$ and $\|w_t\|^2$ has vanishing derivative, hence is constant by the fundamental theorem [F6] and equals $\|w_0\|^2=0$; thus $\psi_t=V(t)\psi_0=e^{-itH_0/\hbar}\psi_0$ for every $t$, which is clause (a). [step 1.1, step 5.1, F4, F5, F6, algebra]

7.1 [Operator standard deviations] For $\psi\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$ one has $U(P\psi)=M_pU\psi$ with $(M_pg)(p)=pg(p)$, because $\mathcal F_2(P\psi)(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R}(-i\hbar\psi'(x))e^{-2\pi ix\xi}dx=2\pi\hbar\xi\,\mathcal F_2\psi(\xi)$ by integration by parts, the boundary terms $[\psi(x)e^{-2\pi ix\xi}]$ vanishing at $\pm\infty$ as $\psi\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R)$ [F6], and applying $D_\hbar$ with $p=2\pi\hbar\xi$ gives the claim [F2]; the unit vector $\psi_t$ of step 6.1 is the packet of steps 3.2 and 4.2, and for it this gives $b_t:=\langle P\psi_t,\psi_t\rangle=\langle UP\psi_t,U\psi_t\rangle=\int_{\mathbb R}p|U\psi_t(p)|^2dp=p_0$, a real number, and $\Delta P:=\|(P-b_t)\psi_t\|_2=\|U(P-b_t)\psi_t\|_2=\bigl(\int_{\mathbb R}(p-p_0)^2|U\psi_t(p)|^2dp\bigr)^{1/2}=\hbar/(2\sigma)$ by step 4.2, while $a_t:=\langle Q\psi_t,\psi_t\rangle=\int_{\mathbb R}x|\psi_t(x)|^2dx=c_t$ and $\Delta Q:=\|(Q-a_t)\psi_t\|_2=\bigl(\int_{\mathbb R}(x-c_t)^2|\psi_t(x)|^2dx\bigr)^{1/2}=\sigma\sqrt{1+\tau^2}$ by step 3.2; hence $\Delta Q\Delta P=\frac{\hbar}{2}\sqrt{1+\tau^2}$. [step 3.2, step 4.2, step 6.1, F2, F6, algebra]

8.1 [Robertson comparison and scope] By [F7] with $c=\hbar$, applied to the unit Schwartz vector $\psi_t$ of step 7.1, one has $\Delta Q\Delta P\ge\hbar/2$; the exact value of step 7.1 is $\frac{\hbar}{2}\sqrt{1+\tau^2}$, which equals $\hbar/2$ exactly when $\tau=0$, i.e. exactly at $t=0$ since $\tau=\hbar t/(2m\sigma^2)$, and is strictly larger otherwise; thus clause (d) holds, and clause (e) records that all of the above are computations inside the free model defined by $H_0$, with no measurement interpretation.
