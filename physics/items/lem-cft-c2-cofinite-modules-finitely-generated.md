## Statement

Let $V$ be a C2-cofinite conformal vertex algebra of CFT type over $\mathbb C$. There is a finite set $E\subset\mathbb C$, depending only on $V$, with the following property for every positive integer $k$. Let $W$ carry $k$ commuting left $V$-module actions, with commuting operators $L_i(0)$ from their conformal vectors. Assume
$$
W=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb C^k}W_{[\lambda]},\qquad W_{[\lambda]}=\{w:\text{for every }i,\ (L_i(0)-\lambda_i)^{n_i}w=0\text{ for some }n_i\geq1\},
$$
where every joint generalized-weight space is finite dimensional and there are real numbers $b_i$ such that $W_{[\lambda]}\ne0$ implies $\operatorname{Re}\lambda_i\geq b_i$. Then the finite-dimensional subspace
$$
G(W)=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in E^k}W_{[\lambda]}
$$
generates $W$ under the component modes. In particular, $W$ is finitely generated. Every weight lies in $E^k+\mathbb Z^k$, and every subspace obtained by bounding above the real part of each coordinate, or by bounding above $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda_1+\cdots+\lambda_k)$, is finite dimensional. Moreover, $W$ has a grading $W=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb N^k}W(n)$ with finite-dimensional pieces, for which a component mode $a^{(i)}_{(m)}$ of a state of weight $r$ maps $W(n)$ into $W(n+(r-m-1)e_i)$, with a piece having a negative coordinate understood as zero. No diagonalizability of the $L_i(0)$, common weight-lattice hypothesis, rationality, semisimplicity, self-duality, or finite generation hypothesis is imposed.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] [[def-conformal-vertex-algebra]] gives $V=\bigoplus_{r\geq0}V_r$, finite-dimensional $V_r$, $V_0=\mathbb C\mathbf1$, conformal vector $\omega\in V_2$, and translation $T=L(-1)$. We use $Y(a,z)=\sum_n a_{(n)}z^{-n-1}$ and $o(a)=a_{(r-1)}$ for homogeneous $a\in V_r$, extending $o$ linearly to arbitrary states.

[F2] [[def-c2-cofinite-vertex-algebra]] states that $C_2(V)=\operatorname{span}\{a_{(-2)}b\}$ has finite codimension.

[F3] Each component action satisfies the vacuum, truncation, and module Jacobi identities of [[def-vertex-algebra-module]]. The joint decomposition, finite dimensions, and coordinatewise lower bounds are hypotheses. For $k=1$ these are precisely the restrictions in [[def-grading-restricted-generalized-vertex-module]]. A submodule generated by a subspace means the span of its images under all finite strings of component modes, including the empty string.

[F4] [[lem-cft-locality-to-jacobi-residue-calculus]] reconstructs adjoint Jacobi from the actual locality, translation and creation axioms for $V$; therefore $V$ itself is a left module. It proves $Y(Ta,z)=\partial_zY(a,z)$, the iterate and commutator coefficient identities on $V$ and on modules, and $a_{(p)}b\in V_{r+s-p-1}$ for homogeneous $a\in V_r,b\in V_s$. Adjoint Jacobi and this weight formula are results of that supplier, not axioms of the imported state-field definition.

## Proof

**Given:** $V$, $k$, and $W$ satisfying the statement.

1.1 The coefficient form of the module Jacobi identity is the iterate identity $(a_{(m)}b)_{(n)}=\sum_{t\geq0}(-1)^t\binom mt\big(a_{(m-t)}b_{(n+t)}-(-1)^m b_{(n+m-t)}a_{(t)}\big)$. Here $m,n\in\mathbb Z$, $\binom mt=m(m-1)\cdots(m-t+1)/t!$, and the sum is finite on each vector by truncation. It is obtained by multiplying the Jacobi identity by $z_0^m z_2^n$, taking the three formal residues, and using $(z_1-z_2)^m=\sum_{t\geq0}(-1)^t\binom mt z_1^{m-t}z_2^t$ in the first expansion and its reversed expansion in the second. This formula holds on $V$ itself by [F4]. On any module, substitute $b=\mathbf1,m=-2$ and use $a_{(-2)}\mathbf1=Ta$ from creation and $\mathbf1_{(j)}=\delta_{j,-1}I$: the surviving terms give $(Ta)_{(n)}=-n a_{(n-1)}$, including $n=0$. Thus $Y_M(Ta,z)=\partial_zY_M(a,z)$. The commutator coefficient of Jacobi then gives $[L_M(0),a^M_{(n)}]=(r-n-1)a^M_{(n)}$ for $a\in V_r$: explicitly it is $[\omega^M_{(1)},Y_M(a,z)]=zY_M(\omega_{(0)}a,z)+Y_M(\omega_{(1)}a,z)=z\partial_zY_M(a,z)+rY_M(a,z)$. The $i$th component mode therefore shifts joint generalized weights by $(r-n-1)e_i$. Finally, [F4] gives the state-product weight $r+s-n-1$; decomposing $a,b$ into their finitely many homogeneous components shows $C_2(V)$ is a homogeneous subspace. [F1, F2, F3, F4, algebra]

2.1 Define $\Omega(M)=\{v:a_{(n)}v=0\text{ for all homogeneous }a\in V_r\text{ and }n\geq r\}$ for any module $M$. Define $a\circ b=\sum_{j=0}^r\binom rj a_{(j-2)}b$, $a*b=\sum_{j=0}^r\binom rj a_{(j-1)}b$, and $O(V)=\operatorname{span}\{a\circ b:a\text{ homogeneous},\ b\in V\}$, extending the products linearly. We claim $o(O(V))\Omega(M)=0$ and $o(a*b)v=o(a)o(b)v$ on $\Omega(M)$. For homogeneous $a\in V_r,b\in V_s$, the state-product weight formula in [F4] identifies the zero-mode indices, so apply step 1.1 to each summand of $o(a\circ b)$ with $m=j-2,n=r+s-j$. Every first-product term kills $v$ because its rightmost $b$-index is $r+s-j+t\geq s$. The second-product terms have the form $b_{(r+s-2-t)}a_{(t)}v$; those with $t\geq r$ vanish, and for $0\leq t<r$ their coefficient is $-(-1)^t\sum_{j=0}^r(-1)^j\binom rj\binom{j-2}t=0$. The last equality is the vanishing of the $r$th finite difference of a polynomial in $j$ of degree $t<r$ (it follows by applying $r$ successive differences, each of which lowers degree). For $o(a*b)$ use $m=j-1,n=r+s-j-1$. The first-product terms vanish except $j=r,t=0$, which is $a_{(r-1)}b_{(s-1)}v$; the second-product coefficients vanish by the identical finite-difference argument with $\binom{j-1}t$. The case $r=0$ has no $0\leq t<r$ terms, so it is included. Linearity proves both claims for arbitrary states. [F4, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Any component-stable subspace $U\subseteq W$ decomposes into its intersections with the joint generalized-weight spaces. Indeed, a vector has only finitely many joint components. For each coordinate, polynomials in $L_i(0)$ project onto each of its finitely many eigenvalues on the finite-dimensional cyclic space of that vector: the polynomials exist by the finite Chinese remainder identity for the pairwise coprime powers $(x-\lambda_i)^{n_i}$. Products of those coordinate projections isolate every joint component, and preserve $U$. Therefore $W/U$ inherits the direct-sum joint generalized-weight decomposition, coordinatewise lower bounds, finite-dimensional joint spaces, and weight-shift rule of step 1.1. [F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 By homogeneity from step 1.1, choose a finite homogeneous spanning set for $V/C_2(V)$, and let $B\geq0$ bound its weights. If $n>B$ then any state in $V_n$ is a finite sum of $a_{(-2)}b$ with homogeneous $a,b$ and $\mathrm{wt}(a)+\mathrm{wt}(b)+1=n$. Modulo $O(V)$, each such product equals $-\sum_{j=1}^r\binom rj a_{(j-2)}b$, whose summands have weights $n-j<n$ by [F4]. Induction on $n$ shows that $V/O(V)$ is spanned by the image of $V_{\leq B}$, a finite-dimensional space. Neither the ideal property of $O(V)$ nor associativity of the Zhu product is needed for this conclusion. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 Every nonzero quotient $Q=W/U$ has a nonzero joint generalized-weight vector in its simultaneous top $\Omega_k(Q)=\bigcap_{i=1}^k\Omega_i(Q)$. To see this, select any occurring weight $\lambda^0$ and consider the occurring weights in its single coset $\lambda^0+\mathbb Z^k$. Write these as $\lambda^0+n$ with $n\in\mathbb Z^k$. Each $n_i$ is bounded below by the corresponding coordinatewise real-part bound, so the integer sum $\sum_i n_i$ has a minimum. Choose an occurring joint weight $\lambda$ attaining this minimum and any nonzero vector $q\in Q_{[\lambda]}$. A component mode $a^{(i)}_{(n)}$ with $n\geq r$ would lower that sum by the positive integer $n+1-r$, so its target weight space is absent. Thus it kills $q$, which proves $q\in\Omega_k(Q)$. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.2, choose]

4.1 Put $p_0=\mathbf1$ and recursively $p_{n+1}=\omega*p_n$, using the linear extension of $*$ if $p_n$ is inhomogeneous. Finite dimensionality in step 3.1 supplies a relation $\sum_{n=0}^d c_np_n\in O(V)$ with not all $c_n$ zero. Set $P(x)=\sum_{n=0}^d c_nx^n$, a nonzero polynomial, and let $E$ be its finite set of complex roots. Step 2.1 and induction give $o(p_n)v=L(0)^n v$ for $v\in\Omega(M)$: at each induction stage the star identity is applied to the original vector $v\in\Omega(M)$, so no invariance of $\Omega(M)$ is being assumed. Consequently $P(L(0))v=0$ on $\Omega(M)$ for every module $M$. In particular, [F4] makes $V$ a valid regular module, and creation puts its vacuum in $\Omega(V)$; applying the polynomial identity to that vacuum gives $P(0)=0$, so $E$ is nonempty. The relation and $P$ depend only on $V$. [F1, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Apply step 4.1 to the $i$th module action on the vector $q$ from step 3.2. Then $P(L_i(0))q=0$. Since $q$ lies in the joint generalized-weight space of $\lambda$, this forces $P(\lambda_i)=0$: if $P(\lambda_i)\ne0$, the operator $P(L_i(0))$ is invertible on the finite-dimensional cyclic space of $q$, because it is a nonzero scalar plus a nilpotent operator. Hence every nonzero quotient of $W$ has a nonzero joint generalized-weight space at some $\lambda\in E^k$. This conclusion uses generalized eigenvectors and does not assume their nilpotent parts vanish. [step 4.1, step 3.2, algebra]

6.1 The space $G(W)$ is finite dimensional because $E^k$ is finite and each of its summands is finite dimensional. Let $U$ be the submodule generated by it. The quotient $Q=W/U$ has zero joint generalized-weight spaces at every $\lambda\in E^k$, by step 2.2. If $Q$ were nonzero, step 5.1 would contradict that fact. Thus $U=W$, and a finite basis of $G(W)$ is a finite generating set. [F3, step 2.2, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 By the mode-shift rule, generation in step 6.1 puts every weight of $W$ in $E^k+\mathbb Z^k$. In each of the finitely many such cosets, coordinatewise lower bounds and coordinatewise upper bounds leave only finitely many lattice points. A bound on the sum of real parts also leaves finitely many points: the coordinatewise lower bounds convert it to an upper bound on each coordinate separately. The corresponding spaces are therefore finite sums of finite-dimensional joint generalized-weight spaces. [F3, step 1.1, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 For each of the finitely many occurring cosets in step 7.1, choose a representative $\alpha$. For each coordinate let $d_i$ be the minimum integer $n_i$ among its occurring weights $\alpha+n$, and set $\beta=\alpha+(d_1,\ldots,d_k)$. These minima exist by the coordinatewise lower bounds; $\beta$ itself need not occur. The occurring weights of this coset lie in $\beta+\mathbb N^k$. Sum $W_{[\beta+n]}$ over the distinct cosets to define $W(n)$. These are finite sums of finite-dimensional spaces, and the weight-shift rule gives exactly the stated mode-degree rule. This is an admissible multigrading; its semisimple degree operators are auxiliary and do not replace the possibly nonsemisimple conformal operators $L_i(0)$. This proves the remaining assertion. [F3, step 1.1, step 7.1, construct] ∎

## Remarks

For zero component actions, the stated joint-weight convention has one weight indexed by the empty tuple, whose weight space is the entire vector space and is finite dimensional by hypothesis; generation under scalars is then immediate. This handles the zero-outgoing-point convention without a positive-integer argument.

This lemma proves the grading-restricted-to-finitely-generated direction needed for incoming modules and test modules in the dual-fusion universal property. It does not prove that an arbitrary finitely generated weak or admissible module is grading restricted. The latter implication is proved separately by the weak-joint-module grading-restriction theorem, and fixed-surface representability is proved separately by the dual-fusion existence theorem. Relative coinvariant base change and geometric local freeness for smooth proper families are now supplied by the smooth-family local-freeness theorem and its two geometric lemmas. Nodal extension and analytic sewing remain separate obligations.
