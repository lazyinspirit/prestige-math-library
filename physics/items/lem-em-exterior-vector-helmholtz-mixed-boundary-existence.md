## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice, as used by the variational, Sobolev and compact-spectral suppliers and by the Fredholm alternative ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $O\subset\mathbb R^3$ be a bounded open set with $C^\infty$ boundary ([[def-bounded-c-k-domain-and-boundary-charts]]) whose exterior $D=\mathbb R^3\setminus\overline O$ is connected, let $k>0$, and let $R>0$ with $\overline O\subset B_R(0)$. Write $A:=B_R(0)\setminus\overline O$ and let $n$ be the outward unit normal field of $A$, so that on $\partial O$ the field $n$ points into $O$; extend $n$ to a smooth normal collar by $n(p+sn(p)):=n(p)$, so $\partial_n n=0$, using [[thm-euclidean-tubular-neighbourhood-theorem]]; put $\kappa:=\operatorname{div}n|_{\partial O}$ and, for a field $E$ on $\partial O$, $E_n:=E\cdot n|_{\partial O}$.

For $e_T:\partial O\to\mathbb C^3$ smooth in boundary charts and satisfying $e_T\cdot n=0$, put
$$\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}e_T:=\operatorname{div}\widetilde e_T-\partial_n(\widetilde e_T\cdot n)\quad\text{on }\partial O,$$
where $\widetilde e_T$ is any smooth extension of $e_T$ to a neighbourhood of $\partial O$; the expression is independent of the extension and equals the surface divergence of $e_T$ with respect to the Euclidean surface measure of $\partial O$. Write $\Delta$ for the Euclidean Laplacian ([[def-laplacian-of-a-c2-function]]), acting componentwise, and for a vector field $f=(f_1,f_2,f_3)$ on $S_R$ write $T_Rf:=(T_Rf_1,T_Rf_2,T_Rf_3)$ with $T_R$ the scalar Dirichlet-to-Neumann map of [[lem-em-sphere-outgoing-dirichlet-to-neumann-map]].

Then there is exactly one field $E\in C^\infty(\overline D;\mathbb C^3)$ with
$$(\Delta+k^2)E=0\ \text{on }D,\qquad \operatorname{div}E=0\ \text{on }D,\qquad E_{\text{tangential}}=e_T\ \text{on }\partial O,$$
which is outgoing on the sphere $S_R$ in the modal sense of that lemma: its trace $f:=E|_{S_R}$ lies in $H^{1/2}(S_R;\mathbb C^3)$ and the normal derivative of $E$ on $S_R$ equals $T_Rf$ componentwise in the weak trace sense of the sphere DtN lemma. With these data the normal boundary values of $E$ on $\partial O$ satisfy the closure relation
$$\partial_nE_n+(\operatorname{div}n)E_n=-\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}e_T$$
as an identity of $C^\infty$ functions on $\partial O$. No scalar Dirichlet condition is imposed on the three Cartesian components of $E$, and no decay rate, asymptotic expansion or pointwise Sommerfeld formula beyond the stated modal outgoing class is assumed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]); a bounded $C^\infty$ obstacle $O$ with connected exterior $D$; $k>0$; $R>0$ with $\overline O\subset B_R(0)$; the truncated annulus $A=B_R(0)\setminus\overline O$ with outward unit normal $n$ and $\kappa=\operatorname{div}n|_{\partial O}$; a smooth $\mathbb C$-valued tangential field $e_T$ on $\partial O$; the conventions of the fields $E_n$, $\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}e_T$, $T_Rf$ explained in the Statement, and the notation $H^m$, $H^m_{\mathrm{loc}}$ of [[def-sobolev-space-wkp-and-its-norm]].

[L1] Sphere Dirichlet-to-Neumann lemma: for $k,R>0$ the map $T_Rf=\sum_{l,m}t_lf_{lm}Y^R_{lm}$ is bounded $H^{1/2}(S_R)\to H^{-1/2}(S_R)$ with $\operatorname{Re}\langle T_Rf,f\rangle\le0$ and $\operatorname{Im}\langle T_Rf,f\rangle=\sum_{l,m}(\operatorname{Im}t_l)|f_{lm}|^2>0$ for $f\ne0$; for every $f\in H^{1/2}(S_R)$ the outgoing series $u(rn)=\sum_{l,m}f_{lm}h_l(kr)/h_l(kR)\,R^{-1}Y_{lm}(n)$, $r\ge R$, is the unique outgoing finite-local-energy exterior solution with trace $f$ on $S_R$ and normal derivative $T_Rf$ in the weak trace sense; it is smooth away from the sphere, and for smooth $f$ it satisfies the ordinary Sommerfeld condition ([[lem-em-sphere-outgoing-dirichlet-to-neumann-map]]).

[L2] Exterior scalar uniqueness: if $O$ has bounded $C^\infty$ boundary, $D$ is connected, $k>0$, $u\in C^\infty(\overline D)$ solves $(\Delta+k^2)u=0$ on $D$, vanishes on $\partial O$ and is outgoing in the class of [L1], then $u=0$ on $D$ ([[lem-em-exterior-outgoing-scalar-uniqueness]]).

[L3] Weak prescribed-trace Dirichlet existence: under the adopted Axiom of Choice, for a bounded $C^1$ domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^3$, a measurable symmetric real matrix field $\varepsilon:\Omega\to\mathbb R^{3\times3}$ satisfying $\alpha|\xi|^2\le\xi\cdot\varepsilon(x)\xi\le\beta|\xi|^2$ for a.e. $x$ and every $\xi\in\mathbb R^3$, with $0<\alpha\le\beta<\infty$, $\rho\in L^2(\Omega)$ and $g\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)$, there is exactly one $u\in H^1(\Omega)$ with trace $Tu=g$ solving $\int_\Omega\nabla v\cdot\varepsilon\nabla u=\int_\Omega\rho v$ for all $v\in H_0^1(\Omega)$, with $\|u\|_{H^1}\le C(\|\rho\|_{L^2}+\|g\|_{H^{1/2}})$ ([[lem-em-prescribed-fractional-dirichlet-existence]]).

[L4] Smooth-domain elliptic regularity: under the adopted Axiom of Choice, on a bounded $C^\infty$ domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^3$ with a smooth real symmetric matrix field $\varepsilon\in C^\infty(\overline\Omega;\operatorname{Sym}^3)$ satisfying the uniform bounds of [L3] and smooth $\rho$ on the closure, the weak solution with smooth Dirichlet data $g\in C^\infty(\partial\Omega)$ is $C^\infty$ on the closure and satisfies the classical equation and boundary values ([[lem-em-smooth-domain-elliptic-regularity]]).

[L5] Bounded-potential unique continuation: for nonempty open connected $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^3$, $V\in L^\infty_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ and $\psi\in H^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ with $\Delta\psi=V\psi$ distributionally, vanishing of $\psi$ on a nonempty open subset of $\Omega$ forces $\psi=0$ on $\Omega$ ([[thm-bounded-potential-weak-unique-continuation-in-three-dimensions]]).

[L6] Interior $H^2$ regularity: if $u\in H^1(\Omega)$ is a local weak solution of $-\Delta u=f$ with $f\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$, then $u\in H^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ ([[thm-interior-h-two-regularity-for-divergence-form-equations]]).

[L7] Interior $H^{k+2}$ regularity: if $u\in H^1(\Omega)$ is a local weak solution of $-\Delta u=f$ with $f\in H^k_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$, $k\ge0$, then $u\in H^{k+2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ ([[thm-interior-h-k-plus-two-elliptic-regularity]]).

[L8] Riesz representation: every bounded linear functional on a real or complex Hilbert space is represented by a unique vector ([[thm-riesz-representation-for-hilbert-space]]).

[L9] Fredholm alternative: for a compact operator $K$ on a Banach space, $I-K$ is injective if and only if it is surjective, and in that case it is boundedly invertible ([[thm-fredholm-alternative-for-identity-minus-compact]]).

[L10] Trace operator: on a bounded $C^1$ domain the trace $T:W^{1,p}(\Omega)\to L^p(\partial\Omega)$ is bounded and equals classical restriction on continuous Sobolev functions ([[thm-lp-trace-operator-on-a-bounded-c-one-domain]]).

[L11] Sobolev-Gauss-Green formula: for a bounded $C^1$ domain $\Omega$, $u,v\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)$ and every $i$, $\int_\Omega u\,\partial_iv=-\int_\Omega(\partial_iu)v+\int_{\partial\Omega}(Tu)(Tv)\nu_i\,dS$ ([[thm-sobolev-gauss-green-formula-on-c-one-domains]]).

[L12] Half-space strip trace estimate: for $u$ smooth with compact support in the closed half-space and $g=u(\cdot,0)$ one has $h|g(x')|^2\le 2(\int_0^h|u|^2dt+h^2\int_0^h|\partial_nu|^2dt)$ for a.e. $x'$ and every $h>0$ ([[thm-trace-estimate-on-the-half-space]]).

[L13] Extension theorem for bounded smooth domains: every bounded $C^\infty$ domain is a $W^{k,2}$-extension domain ([[thm-extension-theorem-for-bounded-smooth-domains]]).

[L14] Subcritical Sobolev compactness: for a bounded extension domain $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ and $p=2<n$, every sequence bounded in $W^{1,2}(\Omega)$ has a subsequence converging in $L^2(\Omega)$ ([[thm-rellich-kondrachov-for-p-less-than-n]]).

[L15] Sequential compact-embedding criterion: under Countable and Dependent Choice, $X\Subset Y$ holds if and only if every bounded sequence in $X$ has a subsequence converging in $Y$ ([[def-compactly-embedded-normed-spaces]]).

[L16] In ZF the Axiom of Choice implies the Axiom of Dependent Choice and the Axiom of Countable Choice ([[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]]).

[L17] Difference quotients are $\delta_h^iu(x)=(u(x+he_i)-u(x))/h$ ([[def-first-difference-quotient]]). Discrete integration by parts and shifted product rules hold on common localized domains, with no boundary remainder for tangential quotients on a half-space ([[lem-difference-quotient-integration-by-parts]]). The increment estimate $\|\delta_h^iu\|_{L^2(U)}\le\|\partial_iu\|_{L^2}$ holds when the used segments stay in the enlarged domain, including tangential half-space directions ([[thm-difference-quotient-characterisation-of-w-one-p-for-p-greater-than-one]]). Smooth cutoff commutators obey [[lem-cutoff-difference-quotient-commutator-estimate]].

[L18] Higher-order Sobolev embedding: on a bounded extension domain, $W^{m,2}$ membership with $m>j+\tfrac32$ yields a $C^j$ representative on the closure, compatibly for different $j$ ([[thm-higher-order-sobolev-embedding]]).

[L19] $L^2$ Cauchy-Schwarz: $\int|fg|\,d\mu\le\|f\|_2\|g\|_2$, and $2ab\le\delta a^2+\delta^{-1}b^2$ for $a,b\ge0$, $\delta>0$ ([[cor-cauchy-schwarz-inequality-for-l-two]]).

[L20] Clairaut-Schwarz: mixed partial derivatives of a $C^2$ function commute ([[thm-clairaut-schwarz-mixed-partials]]).

[L21] Fractional trace and Sobolev completeness: on a bounded $C^1$ domain, $T:H^1\to H^{1/2}$ is bounded ([[thm-sharp-trace-theorem-for-w-one-p]]); the Sobolev space $H^1=W^{1,2}$ is complete ([[thm-sobolev-spaces-are-banach-spaces]]), and with its integral inner product is a Hilbert space. Both assumptions are covered by the adopted Axiom of Choice.

[L22] Smooth ambient extension: if every derivative of a function has a continuous extension to the closure of a bounded smooth domain, the explicit extension operator, applied to a time-independent input, supplies one smooth ambient extension with all those boundary derivatives ([[lem-em-parameter-preserving-smooth-extension-on-a-smooth-domain]]).

[L23] The explicit outgoing $h_l$ solve the radial Helmholtz equation and do not vanish on positive real arguments ([[lem-em-outgoing-spherical-hankel-dtn-bounds]]).

[L24] Homogeneous harmonic polynomials of degree $l$ restrict to the finite degree-$l$ spherical harmonic space with the complete orthonormal bases of [[lem-em-spherical-harmonic-basis-and-addition]].

[L25] Under the stated Choice, the compact smooth boundary has a normal tubular neighbourhood whose normal-addition map is a diffeomorphism ([[thm-euclidean-tubular-neighbourhood-theorem]]); the normal extended constantly along its fibres has $\partial_n n=0$.

**Choice accounting.** The Axiom of Choice is assumed in the Statement. It supplies the Fredholm alternative [L9], the tensor/frame and Sobolev suppliers' own choice assumptions, and through [L16] the Countable and Dependent Choice used by the sequential compactness criterion [L15] and by the trace and difference-quotient interfaces of [L10], [L12] and [L17]. No other choice principle is used, and no choice is made in the explicit finite constructions below.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** The data and conventions of the Given paragraph above; in particular the truncated annulus $A=B_R(0)\setminus\overline O$, its outward unit normal $n$, the coefficient $\kappa=\operatorname{div}n$ on $\partial O$, and the smooth tangential field $e_T$ on $\partial O$.

1.1 **The test space and the variational form.** Define $X:=\{v\in H^1(A;\mathbb C^3):v_{\text{tangential}}=0\text{ on }\partial O\}$, the tangential part being taken with respect to the trace of $v$ on the smooth boundary component $\partial O$; by the boundedness of the trace operator [L10] applied componentwise, $X$ is a closed subspace of the complex Hilbert space $H^1(A;\mathbb C^3)$ of [L21], hence complete. Define the sesquilinear form $a(E,v):=\int_A(\nabla E:\overline{\nabla v}-k^2E\cdot\overline v)\,dx+\int_{\partial O}\kappa\,E_n\overline{v_n}\,dS-\langle T_RE,v\rangle_{S_R}$, where $\nabla E:\overline{\nabla v}:=\sum_{i,m}\partial_iE_m\overline{\partial_iv_m}$ and $\langle T_RE,v\rangle_{S_R}:=\sum_{m=1}^3\langle T_RE_m,v_m\rangle$ uses the scalar DtN pairing of [L1] on the components. Then $|a(E,v)|\le C\|E\|_{H^1(A)}\|v\|_{H^1(A)}$: the first two terms are bounded by Cauchy-Schwarz [L19]; the boundary term is bounded by $\|\kappa\|_\infty\|E\|_{L^2(\partial O)}\|v\|_{L^2(\partial O)}\le C'\|E\|_{H^1}\|v\|_{H^1}$ by [L10]; and the last by the boundedness of $T_R:H^{1/2}(S_R)\to H^{-1/2}(S_R)$ [L1] combined with the exact fractional trace bound [L21] for $H^1(A)$ functions on the smooth sphere $S_R$. [given, L1, L10, L19, L21]

1.2 **Collar trace inequality on the inner boundary.** For every $\delta>0$ there is $C_\delta$ such that every $u\in H^1(A;\mathbb C^3)$ satisfies $\|u\|_{L^2(\partial O)}^2\le\delta\|\nabla u\|_{L^2(A)}^2+C_\delta\|u\|_{L^2(A)}^2$. Indeed, cover $\partial O$ by finitely many flattening charts of the smooth boundary ([[def-bounded-c-k-domain-and-boundary-charts]]) and let $\chi$ be a smooth cutoff, equal to $1$ near $\partial O$ and supported in the resulting collar. On the collar, in normal coordinates $x=\Phi(y,t)$ with $t$ proportional to the distance to $\partial O$, the strip estimate [L12] with $p=2$, divided by $h$ and integrated over $y$ with the bounded chart Jacobians, gives $\|u\chi\|_{L^2(\partial O)}^2\le C(h^{-1}\|u\chi\|_{L^2(A)}^2+h\|\nabla(u\chi)\|_{L^2(A)}^2)$ for every smooth $u$; choosing $h$ so small that $2Ch\le\delta$, expanding $\nabla(u\chi)=\chi\nabla u+u\nabla\chi$ and absorbing the cutoff terms into $C_\delta\|u\|_{L^2(A)}^2$ yields the claim for smooth $u$, and both sides are continuous in the $H^1(A)$ norm (boundary term by [L10]), while products of global $C_c^\infty$ approximations with a fixed cutoff are dense in the $H^1$ classes supported in the collar by [[cor-compactly-supported-smooth-functions-are-dense-in-wkp-of-rn]] and [L13]; the inequality therefore extends to all $u\in H^1(A;\mathbb C^3)$. [L10, L12, L13, L25]

1.3 **Smooth componentwise lift of the tangential datum.** For $j=1,2,3$ let $(e_T)_j\in C^\infty(\partial O)$ be the Cartesian components of $e_T$ and extend them by zero to $\partial A=\partial O\cup S_R$, obtaining smooth functions $g_j\in C^\infty(\partial A)$. The domain $A\subset\mathbb R^3$ is bounded and smooth, and $\varepsilon=I_3$ is real symmetric with $\alpha=\beta=1$. Thus [L3] with $\rho=0$, applied separately to the real and imaginary parts, there is a unique $G_j\in H^1(A)$ with trace $g_j$ and $\int_A\nabla\overline v\cdot\nabla G_j=0$ for all $v\in H_0^1(A)$; by [L4] with $\rho=0$ and the smooth Dirichlet data $g_j$, each $G_j$ is $C^\infty$ on $\overline A$, is harmonic on $A$, and $G_j|_{\partial A}=g_j$. Hence $G:=(G_1,G_2,G_3)$ is $C^\infty$ on $\overline A$, satisfies $\Delta G=0$ componentwise, $G|_{\partial O}=e_T$ and $G|_{S_R}=0$, and is tangential on $\partial O$. [L3, L4]

2.1 **Coercivity of the shifted form.** With $\lambda:=\|\kappa\|_\infty(1+C_1)+k^2+1$, where $C_1$ is the constant of step 1.2 for $\delta:=\tfrac12(\|\kappa\|_\infty+1)^{-1}$, one has $\operatorname{Re}(a(E,E)+\lambda\|E\|_{L^2(A)}^2)\ge c\|E\|_{H^1(A)}^2$ for every $E\in X$, with $c=\tfrac12$. Indeed $\operatorname{Re}a(E,E)=\|\nabla E\|_2^2-k^2\|E\|_2^2+\int_{\partial O}\kappa|E_n|^2-\operatorname{Re}\langle T_RE,E\rangle$; the last term is nonnegative by $\operatorname{Re}\langle T_Rf,f\rangle\le0$ of [L1] applied componentwise to $f=E|_{S_R}$; and the boundary term obeys $|\int_{\partial O}\kappa|E_n|^2|\le\|\kappa\|_\infty\|E\|_{L^2(\partial O)}^2\le\tfrac12\|\nabla E\|_2^2+\|\kappa\|_\infty C_1\|E\|_2^2$ by step 1.2 and [L19]. [L1, step 1.2, L19, algebra]

3.1 **The shifted form is boundedly invertible.** By the Riesz representation theorem [L8], applied to the bounded conjugate-linear functional $v\mapsto a(E,v)+\lambda(E,v)_{L^2}$ on the complete space $X$, the shifted form is represented by a bounded linear operator $S:X\to X$ with $\operatorname{Re}\langle Sx,x\rangle\ge c\|x\|^2$ and $\|S\|\le M$ for constants $c,M>0$ (step 2.1 and step 1.1). Choose $0<t<2c/M^2$ and put $q:=\sqrt{1-2tc+t^2M^2}<1$. For $x,y\in X$, $\|(I-tS)(x-y)\|^2\le q^2\|x-y\|^2$ by expanding the square and using [L19]; hence for every $f\in X$ the iteration $x_{n+1}:=(I-tS)x_n+tf$ is a strict contraction of the complete space $X$, so $\|x_{n+1}-x_n\|\le q^n\|x_1-x_0\|$. The geometric tail estimate makes $(x_n)$ Cauchy; completeness supplies its limit $x$, continuity gives $x=(I-tS)x+tf$ and $Sx=f$, and the same contraction inequality makes the fixed point unique. If $Sx=0$ then $c\|x\|^2\le\operatorname{Re}\langle Sx,x\rangle=0$, so $x=0$; thus $S$ is boundedly invertible with $\|S^{-1}\|\le c^{-1}$. [L8, L19, step 1.1, step 2.1, choose, algebra]

3.2 **The kernel of the form is trivial.** Let $E\in X$ satisfy $a(E,v)=0$ for every $v\in X$. Taking $v=E$ and imaginary parts in step 2.1 gives $0=\operatorname{Im}a(E,E)=-\operatorname{Im}\langle T_RE,E\rangle$, where $f=E|_{S_R}$; by [L1] the imaginary pairing equals $\sum_m\sum_{l,m'}(\operatorname{Im}t_l)|(f_m)_{lm'}|^2$ with $\operatorname{Im}t_l>0$, so every coefficient vanishes, $f=0$ on $S_R$, and consequently $T_Rf=0$. For every $v\in X$ supported away from $\partial O$ the identity $a(E,v)=0$ has no inner-boundary contribution and reads $\int_A(\nabla E:\overline{\nabla v}-k^2E\cdot\overline v)\,dx=\langle T_RE,v\rangle_{S_R}=\langle T_Rf,v|_{S_R}\rangle=0$; in particular every $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(D)$, whose restriction to $A$ is an admissible test vanishing near $\partial O$, satisfies $\int_A\nabla E:\overline{\nabla\varphi}\,dx=k^2\int_AE\cdot\overline\varphi\,dx$, and taking $v$ compactly supported in the interior of $A$ gives $-\Delta E=k^2E$ weakly on $A$. Define $\widetilde E:=E$ on $A$ and $\widetilde E:=0$ on $\{|x|>R\}$; assign any value on the null sphere $S_R$, so $\widetilde E$ defines an $L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(D)$ class, and its only interface is the sphere $S_R$, across which the full trace of the field is $f=0$. For $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(D)$ the Sobolev-Gauss-Green formula [L11] on the bounded $C^1$ domain $A$, applied componentwise with $u=E_m\in W^{1,2}(A)$ and $v=\varphi|_A$, gives $\int_AE_m\overline{\partial_i\varphi}\,dx=-\int_A(\partial_iE_m)\overline\varphi\,dx+\int_{\partial A}(TE_m)(T\varphi)\nu_i\,dS$; the boundary term over $\partial O$ vanishes because $\varphi$ is compactly supported in $D$, and the term over $S_R$ vanishes because $TE_m=f_m=0$ there; hence $\int_D\widetilde E_m\overline{\partial_i\varphi}\,dx=-\int_A(\partial_iE_m)\overline\varphi\,dx$, so the distributional gradient of $\widetilde E$ is the field $(\nabla E)\mathbf 1_A\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(D)$, $\widetilde E\in H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(D)$, and the weak identity above gives $\int_D\nabla\widetilde E:\overline{\nabla\varphi}\,dx=k^2\int_D\widetilde E\cdot\overline\varphi\,dx$. By [L6] each real and imaginary part of each component of $\widetilde E$ lies in $H^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(D)$ and satisfies $\Delta w=-k^2w$ distributionally; $D$ is nonempty, open and connected and $\widetilde E=0$ on the nonempty open set $D\cap\{|x|>R\}$, so [L5] gives $\widetilde E=0$ on $D$, in particular $E=0$ on $A$. [L1, L5, L6, L11, step 2.1]

4.1 **Compactness of the $L^2$ pairing and the Fredholm reduction.** Use the Riesz identification fixed in step 3.1 and let $J:X\to X$ be the represented $L^2$ pairing, $\langle Jx,v\rangle_X=(x,v)_{L^2(A)}$. A conjugate-linear functional in $v$ is represented here by applying [L8] to its complex conjugate and then conjugating the pairing, so this identification is linear in $x$. The inclusion $X\hookrightarrow H^1(A;\mathbb C^3)$ is continuous and $A$ is a bounded $W^{1,2}$-extension domain [L13], so by [L14] with $p=2<n=3$ every bounded sequence in $H^1(A)$ has an $L^2$-convergent subsequence; by [L16] the Countable and Dependent Choice assumptions of the sequential criterion [L15] are available, so the inclusion $H^1(A)\hookrightarrow L^2(A)$ is compact, and so is its restriction $X\hookrightarrow L^2(A)$, hence also the composition $J$ of that restriction with the bounded represented pairing $L^2(A)\to X$. Therefore $K:=S^{-1}J:X\to X$ is compact, and the operator $A_{\mathrm{op}}:=S-\lambda J$ represented by $a$ satisfies $S^{-1}A_{\mathrm{op}}=I-\lambda K$; by the Fredholm alternative [L9] the operator $S^{-1}A_{\mathrm{op}}$ is boundedly invertible as soon as its kernel is trivial, and its kernel consists exactly of the $E\in X$ with $a(E,v)=0$ for all $v\in X$. [L9, L13, L14, L15, L16, step 3.1]

5.1 **Bounded invertibility of $a$.** By step 3.2 the kernel of $S^{-1}A_{\mathrm{op}}=I-\lambda K$ is trivial, so the Fredholm alternative [L9] makes it boundedly invertible; hence $A_{\mathrm{op}}=S(I-\lambda K)$ and the form $a$ are boundedly invertible: for every bounded conjugate-linear functional $F$ on $X$ there is exactly one $E\in X$ with $a(E,v)=F(v)$ for all $v\in X$, and $\|E\|_{H^1(A)}\le C\|F\|_{X^*}$. [L9, step 4.1, step 3.2]

6.1 **The weak solution with prescribed tangential trace.** Define on $X$ the functional $F(v):=-a(G,v)-\int_{\partial O}(\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}e_T)\overline{v_n}\,dS$. Both summands are bounded in $v$: the first by step 1.1, the second because $\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}e_T\in C^\infty(\partial O)$ and $v\mapsto v_n|_{\partial O}$ is trace-continuous into $L^2(\partial O)$ by [L10], applied in the boundary charts. By step 5.1 there is exactly one $u\in X$ with $a(u,v)=F(v)$ for all $v\in X$; set $E:=G+u\in H^1(A;\mathbb C^3)$. Then $E_{\text{tangential}}=e_T$ on $\partial O$ because $u\in X$ and $G$ is tangential there, and testing the defining identity with $v$ compactly supported in the interior of $A$ gives $-\Delta E=k^2E$ weakly on $A$. Moreover, for every $v\in X$ supported away from $\partial O$ one has $a(E,v)=a(G,v)+a(u,v)=-\int_{\partial O}(\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}e_T)\overline{v_n}\,dS=0$, so the same integration-by-parts computation as in step 3.2 shows that the normal trace of $E$ on $S_R$ equals $T_Rf$ in the weak trace sense, where $f=E|_{S_R}=u|_{S_R}\in H^{1/2}(S_R;\mathbb C^3)$. [L10, step 5.1, step 1.3, algebra]

7.1 **Gluing across the artificial sphere and smoothness on the open set $D$.** Let $E_{\mathrm{out}}$ be the outgoing series of [L1] built from the trace $f=E|_{S_R}$, defined on $\{|x|>R\}$; by [L1] it is a finite-local-energy solution there with normal derivative $T_Rf$, and it is smooth away from the sphere. Define $\widetilde E:=E$ on $A$ and $\widetilde E:=E_{\mathrm{out}}$ on $\{|x|>R\}$. The two pieces have the same trace $f$ on $S_R$; applying the Sobolev-Gauss-Green formula [L11] on $A$ and on the exterior annulus $B_M\setminus\overline B_R$ (with the outward normals $+n$ and $-n$ on $S_R$) and adding, the two interface pairings cancel, so $\widetilde E\in H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(D)$ with weak gradient the two-sided gradient. For every test $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(D)$ the weak equations on both sides then give $\int_D\nabla\widetilde E:\overline{\nabla\varphi}=k^2\int_D\widetilde E\cdot\overline\varphi$: on $A$ this is the weak identity of step 6.1, on the exterior it is the weak Helmholtz equation satisfied by the outgoing series, and the interface terms have cancelled. Since $k^2\widetilde E\in H^k_{\mathrm{loc}}(D)$ for every $k$ as soon as $\widetilde E\in H^k_{\mathrm{loc}}(D)$, repeated application of the interior gain-two estimate [L7] starting from $H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ gives $\widetilde E\in H^m_{\mathrm{loc}}(D)$ for every $m$, hence a $C^\infty(D)$ representative satisfying the classical Helmholtz equation on $D$. [L1, L7, L11, step 6.1]

7.2 **Homogeneous tangential traces before boundary localization.** Put $u:=E-G$, with the smooth harmonic lift $G$ of step 1.3; by step 6.1, $u\in X$, so its tangential trace on $\partial O$ is zero. Fix a smooth normal collar chart with coordinates $(y,s)$, $s<0$ in $A$, and a smooth orthonormal frame $Q=(\tau_1,\tau_2,n)$ extended constantly along its normal lines. Write $u=QU$; thus $U_1=U_2=0$ on the flat boundary. The weak identity for $u$ is $a(u,v)=-a(G,v)-\int_{\partial O}(\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}e_T)\overline{v_n}$. Since $\Delta G=0$, integrating the smooth lift term by parts in a chart away from $S_R$ gives the smooth volume datum $k^2G$ and normal boundary datum $-\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}e_T-\partial_nG_n$ (the trace $G_n$ is zero and $\partial_n n=0$). Transporting and localizing this identity by a cutoff $\eta$ gives, for every compactly localized $H^1$ test $V$ with $V_1=V_2=0$ on the flat boundary, $$\sum_m\int a^{ij}\partial_jU_m\overline{\partial_iV_m}+\int_B\beta U_3\overline{V_3}=\int f\cdot\overline V+\sum_i\int G_i\cdot\overline{\partial_iV}+\int_Bh\overline{V_3}.$$ The principal matrix $a^{ij}$ is smooth, uniformly positive and scalar in $m$, since $Q^{\mathsf T}Q=I$; $\beta=\kappa J_B$ is smooth and possibly signed; $f=k^2J(U+Q^{\mathsf T}G)+(\text{smooth})U+(\text{smooth})\partial U\in L^2$; $G_i=(\text{smooth})U$ plus smooth lift/cutoff terms belongs to $H^1$; and $h=-(\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}e_T+\partial_nG_n)\circ\psi\,J_B$ times the localized cutoff is smooth. Tests are admissible: their first two components have zero trace, so odd reflection of those components and even reflection of the third, followed by parity-preserving mollification and cutoff, approximate them in $H^1$; pulling back the smooth frame and chart and extending by zero produces fields in $X$. The difference-quotient tests below therefore act on $U$, whose first two traces actually vanish, rather than on $Q^{\mathsf T}E$ with its prescribed nonzero traces. [L10, L11, L25, step 1.3, step 6.1]

8.1 **Tangential second derivatives and recovery of the normal derivatives.** Let $k\in\{1,2\}$ be a tangential direction, put $W:=\delta_h^kU=(U(y+he_k,s)-U(y,s))/h$ and test the system of step 7.2 with $V:=-\delta_{-h}^k(\eta^2W)$ on a nested pair of half-boxes; this test has $V_1=V_2=0$ on $B$ and compact support, so it is admissible. The discrete integration by parts of [L17] and the shifted product rule give a principal term bounded below by $\alpha\|\eta\nabla W\|_{L^2}^2$ with $\alpha>0$ the ellipticity constant of $a^{ij}$, while all remaining terms involve $\delta_h a$, $\nabla\eta$, $W$, $\nabla U$, $f$ or the transferred quotients of $G$, and are bounded by $\epsilon\|\eta\nabla W\|_2^2+C_\epsilon(\|U\|_{H^1}^2+\|f\|_{L^2}^2+\|G\|_{H^1}^2)$ using [L17], the increment estimate $\|\delta_h^kU\|_{L^2}\le\|\partial_kU\|_{L^2}$, and [L19]. On $B$ the Robin term gives $\int_B\beta(y+he_k)\eta^2|W_3|^2+\int_B(\delta_h^k\beta)U_3\eta^2\overline{W_3}$; the trace estimate $\|Tz\|_{L^2(B)}^2\le\epsilon\|\nabla z\|_{L^2}^2+C_\epsilon\|z\|_{L^2}^2$ of step 1.2 (with $B$ the flattened $\partial O$) absorbs the first term for either sign of $\beta$, and bounded $\delta_h\beta$, the trace bound for $U_3$ and [L19] bound the second; the smooth datum $h$ contributes $\int_B(\delta_h^kh)\overline{W_3}$, bounded by $\|h\|_{H^1(B)}\|T(\eta^2W_3)\|_{L^2(B)}$ and absorbed in the same way. Choosing the finitely many $\epsilon$ small gives $\|\eta\nabla\delta_h^kU\|_{L^2}\le C(\|U\|_{H^1}+\|f\|_{L^2}+\|G\|_{H^1}+\|h\|_{H^1(B)})$ uniformly in $h$, hence every tangential second derivative of $U$ lies in $L^2$ after the limit argument in which the bounded functional $\psi\mapsto\lim_h\int(\partial_iU_m)\delta_{-h}^k(\eta^2\psi)$ is represented in $L^2$ by [L8]. From the interior equation $-\partial_i(a^{ij}\partial_jU_m)=f_m-\partial_iG_{i,m}$ one then recovers $a^{33}\partial_{ss}U_m=\partial_iG_{i,m}-f_m-\sum_{(i,j)\ne(3,3)}a^{ij}\partial_{ij}U_m-\sum_{i,j}(\partial_ia^{ij})\partial_jU_m$, whose right side is now $L^2$ and whose coefficient satisfies $a^{33}\ge\alpha>0$; so all second derivatives of $U$ lie in $L^2$ on the smaller half-box, and a finite cover of the compact boundary gives $u\in H^2$ up to $\partial O$ and hence $E=u+G\in H^2$, since $G$ is smooth. [L8, L19, step 1.2, step 7.2, algebra]

9.1 **Higher-order bootstrap and the classical boundary conclusion.** Suppose $U\in H^{r+1}$ on the larger half-box for some $r\ge1$; the data of the local system then satisfy $f\in H^r$, each divergence datum $G_i\in H^{r+1}$ and the lift-adjusted boundary datum $h$ is smooth because all coefficients are smooth. For a tangential multi-index $\alpha$ of order $r$ put $Z:=D_y^\alpha U$; differentiating the system of step 7.2 by $D_y^\alpha$ keeps the principal matrix $a^{ij}$ and the Robin coefficient $\beta$, and replaces the data by $f_\alpha\in L^2$, $G_\alpha\in H^1$ and $h_\alpha\in H^1(B)$ built from $D_y^\alpha f$, $D_y^\alpha G$ and the finitely many commutator terms $D_y^\gamma(\text{coefficient})\,D_y^{\alpha-\gamma}U$ with $0<\gamma\le\alpha$, all known by the induction hypothesis; the tangential traces of $Z$ are again zero in the first two components and free in the third, and localized tests for $Z$ are admissible by the test approximation of step 7.2; the differentiated Robin commutators contain traces of tangential derivatives of $U$ of order at most $r-1$, their tangential derivatives of one further order have $L^2$ traces because the corresponding interior derivatives of order at most $r$ belong to $H^1$; trace continuity and smooth approximation therefore place those commutators in $H^1(B)$ by [L10]. Applying step 8.1 to $Z$ gains two further tangential or mixed derivatives, and differentiating the normal-recovery equation of step 8.1 and inducting on the number of normal derivatives recovers the remaining ones; nested half-boxes and a finite boundary cover give $U\in H^m$ on each smaller patch for every $m$, with constants depending on the data, $\kappa$, the chart and the fixed nested covers. By the Sobolev embedding [L18] on a rounded bounded smooth subdomain of the chart that agrees with $s=0$ near the smaller patch and lies inside the larger half-box (hence an extension domain by [L13]) each $H^m$ class with $m>j+\tfrac32$ has a $C^j$ representative on the closed patch, compatible for different $j$; hence $u$, and therefore $E=u+G$, has all derivatives continuous up to $\partial O$; combining with the smooth gluing at $S_R$, [L22] on the bounded annulus $A$ supplies one smooth ambient representative near its closure, and the classical integration by parts in the local frame (equivalently [L11] with classical traces) turns $a(E,v)=-\int_{\partial O}(\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}e_T)\overline{v_n}$ for admissible inner-collar tests into the boundary identities $\partial_nE_n+\kappa E_n=-\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}e_T$ and $E_{\text{tangential}}=e_T$ on $\partial O$. [L10, L11, L13, L18, L21, L22, step 7.2, step 8.1, algebra]

10.1 **The divergence constraint.** Let $q:=\operatorname{div}E$ on $D$. By step 9.1 and step 7.1, $E\in C^\infty(\overline D;\mathbb C^3)$ once the two-sided solution is matched at $S_R$; the mixed partials commute [L20], so $\Delta q=\operatorname{div}\Delta E=-k^2\operatorname{div}E=-k^2q$ on $D$. On $\partial O$ the exact boundary identity $\operatorname{div}E=\partial_nE_n+(\operatorname{div}n)E_n+\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}E_{\text{tangential}}$ holds in the classical sense with the chosen normal extension, and its first two terms sum to $-\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}e_T$ by step 9.1 while the third equals $\operatorname{div}_{\partial O}e_T$; hence $q=0$ on $\partial O$. To check outgoingness without assuming it for derivatives, let $H_l(x)=|x|^lY_{lm}(x/|x|)$ be the homogeneous harmonic polynomial of a single angular mode and set $J_i(x)=x_iH_l(x)-|x|^2\partial_iH_l(x)/(2l+1)$. Product differentiation gives $\Delta(x_iH_l)=2\partial_iH_l$ and $\Delta(|x|^2\partial_iH_l)=(4l+2)\partial_iH_l$, so $J_i$ is harmonic of degree $l+1$, while $\partial_iH_l$ is harmonic of degree $l-1$ (zero when $l=0$), as in [L24]. Direct differentiation of the finite polynomial formula [L23] gives $h_l'=l h_l/z-h_{l+1}$ for $l\ge0$ and $h_l'=h_{l-1}-(l+1)h_l/z$ for $l\ge1$. The Cartesian derivative therefore is $\partial_i[h_l(kr)Y_{lm}(n)]=-k h_{l+1}(kr)J_i(n)+k h_{l-1}(kr)\partial_iH_l(n)/(2l+1)$ for $l\ge1$; for $l=0$ only the first term occurs, since $h_0'=-h_1$ and $\partial_iH_0=0$. Expand these two harmonic polynomials in the finite bases of their degrees [L24]. The all-derivative convergence of [L1] justifies differentiation and angular projection on every annulus strictly outside $S_R$; a fixed output degree receives terms only from the two adjacent input degrees, so each radial coefficient of $q$ is a multiple of its outgoing $h_j(kr)$, with no incoming partner. Since $q$ is smooth across $S_R$, its coefficients and radial derivatives extend continuously to $R$; nonvanishing of $h_j(kR)$ [L23] identifies those coefficients with the outgoing series for $q|_{S_R}$ and gives $\partial_rq=T_R(q|_{S_R})$. Thus $q$ belongs to the exact outgoing class of [L1]. The exterior scalar uniqueness lemma [L2] applies to $q$ on the connected $D$ and gives $q=0$ on $D$, that is, the divergence constraint. [L1, L2, L20, L23, L24, step 7.1, step 9.1, algebra]

11.1 **Uniqueness of the classical problem.** Let $E_1,E_2\in C^\infty(\overline D;\mathbb C^3)$ both satisfy the conditions of the Statement. Their difference $W:=E_1-E_2$ is $C^\infty$ on $\overline D$, solves $(\Delta+k^2)W=0$ componentwise on $D$, has $\operatorname{div}W=0$ and $W_{\text{tangential}}=0$ on $\partial O$, and is outgoing on $S_R$. Then $W\in X$ with $W|_{S_R}\in H^{1/2}$, and the integration by parts of step 3.2 and step 6.1 shows that $a(W,v)=0$ for every $v\in X$: the volume terms reproduce the Helmholtz equation, the inner boundary term cancels against $\operatorname{div}W=0$ on $\partial O$ through the exact identity of step 10.1, and the outer term cancels because $\partial_rW=T_R(W|_{S_R})$ by outgoingness. By step 3.2, $W=0$ on $A$; its trace on $S_R$ is zero, so [L1] gives $W=0$ outside that sphere as well, hence on $D$. [L1, step 3.2, step 6.1, step 10.1, algebra]

12.1 **Assembly and choice accounting.** Existence is step 6.1 together with the regularity and gluing of steps 7.1, 8.1 and 9.1: the constructed $E=G+u$ has the prescribed tangential trace, satisfies $(\Delta+k^2)E=0$ on $D$, is $C^\infty$ on $\overline D$ and outgoing on $S_R$ in the stated weak trace sense, and satisfies the closure relation of step 9.1; its divergence vanishes by step 10.1. Uniqueness is step 11.1. The only choice principle used is the Axiom of Choice assumed in the Statement: it supplies the Fredholm alternative, and through [L16] the Countable and Dependent Choice invoked by the compactness, trace and difference-quotient interfaces [L15], [L10], [L12], [L17]; the lift, the coercive inversion and the boundary bootstrap make only finitely many explicit selections. This proves all assertions. [given, L16, step 6.1, step 9.1, step 10.1, step 11.1, algebra] ∎
