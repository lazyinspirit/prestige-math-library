## Statement

Let V be a positive-graded CFT-type conformal vertex algebra. Consider modules with algebraic generalized-weight direct sum, finite-dimensional weight spaces and support in finitely many sets Delta+N, where Delta may be complex. Suppose each input and intermediate module is C1-cofinite and the restricted dual of each possible output module is finitely generated. A finite rooted binary tree of ordinary intertwining operators then has absolutely locally uniformly convergent scalar projection sums on its admissible cut tree domain, defined using the differences of the rightmost leaves and their child/parent ratios. Its coefficients continue along every path of the distinct-point configuration space. Grafting finite such trees commutes with this continuation: the continuation of a grafted coefficient along a path of disjoint inserted configurations equals the convergent contraction of the continued coefficients, with every branch transported along that path. This includes the independent product of two chiral blocks, a single chiral insertion, and the two simultaneous insertions associated to a conjugate pair of leaves. No tensor-product category, ambient admission, semisimplicity, real-weight assumption, or logarithm-free continuation is asserted or required.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The modules, ordinary intertwining operators, restricted-dual pairing and finite trees in the Statement. A contraction uses the sum of the canonical finite-dimensional weight-block evaluation tensors; it does not choose an infinite-dimensional trace.

[F1] [[def-cft-c1-cofinite-module]] defines the quotient by positive-state (-1)-modes. [[def-cft-intertwining-operator-space]] and [[lem-cft-locality-to-jacobi-residue-calculus]] supply the intertwiner Jacobi and derivative identities and the integer weight shift of a homogeneous state mode.

[F2] [[thm-cft-contragredient-module-well-defined]] and [[lem-cft-opposite-vertex-operator-identities]] supply the actual restricted-dual module and the opposite-field transpose. On a weight-d state its opposite modes are finite sums of transposed ordinary modes, with the exact inversion operator exp(xL(1))(-x^(-2))^L(0).

[F3] [[lem-cft-holomorphic-euler-systems-converge-with-parameters]] supplies its first, purely analytic assertion: a given finite-exponent, bounded-log formal solution of a finite holomorphic Euler matrix system converges normally with parameters. Its separate C2-cofinite module assertion is not used.

[F4] [[thm-nakayama-lemma]] supplies the local generator argument. [[thm-hilbert-basis-theorem]], [[thm-noetherian-ring-quotients-and-localisations]] and [[lem-submodules-of-finite-modules-over-noetherian-rings-are-finite-direct]] give Noetherianity of polynomial localizations and finite generation of their finite-module submodules. [[lem-cft-binary-tree-coordinates-are-biholomorphic]] gives the explicit polynomial inverse of tree coordinates.

## Proof

1.1 Split each module into its finitely many distinct integer weight cosets, choosing the lowest occurring integer shift in each coset as origin. Modes preserve a coset. Its degree pieces are finite dimensional and indexed by N; different complex origins do not enter any comparison of these integer degrees. Choose homogeneous representatives of the finite C1 quotient. Replacing a nonrepresentative by positive-state (-1)-modes lowers the argument's integer degree by a positive integer, so terminates. Thus each C1-cofinite module is generated by finitely many generalized-weight vectors. The nilpotent part N of L(0), defined separately on each generalized-weight block, commutes with every mode between its source and shifted target. A power annihilating the finitely many generators therefore annihilates the whole module. The same argument applies to every finitely generated outer dual. For each fixed finite tree this gives a uniform finite nilpotence exponent on each of its modules, finitely many exponent cosets and a finite bound on the logarithmic degrees of its coefficient series. Ordinary intertwiners have zero initial logarithmic degree; finite logarithmic degrees after continuation are permitted. [given, F1, F2, algebra]

1.2 Work first in one coset of each leaf module W_i and outer dual W_0'. Write S for the nonnegative degree in W_0' and T for the sum of the leaf degrees. The finite-coset case is their finite direct sum. Let R be the polynomial ring in insertion coordinates with all pairwise differences inverted. In R tensor W_0' tensor W_1 tensor ... tensor W_n impose every rational-current Ward relation: expand a rational multiplier at each finite insertion, act by its local modes, and subtract the transposed residue at infinity. The infinity term uses F2, not an extra sign assigned to the dual module. Partial fractions and repeated Jacobi move the current through each vertex; the two residues on every internal edge cancel. Thus every formal tree coefficient annihilates these relations. Translation gives commuting operators D_i=partial_(z_i)+L(-1)_i on the quotient: differentiating a current relation at fixed global current coordinate gives another such relation, since the commutator with L(-1) is the derivative of the field and formal integration by parts has zero residue. Hence the Ward quotient has a flat connection before any convergence assertion. [F1, F2, F4, construct]

2.1 Choose N_i bounding the degrees of C1 representatives in W_i, and N=sum N_i. In a leaf factor a_(-1)v, for a of weight d>0, the multiplier (t-z_i)^(-1) rewrites it as infinity terms z_i^k a_(-1-k)^* on the outer dual and terms (z_i-z_j)^(-1-k) a_(k) on other leaves, with the binomial signs fixed by that multiplier's Taylor series. Relative to the original leaf term the first terms decrease S by d+k and decrease T by d; the second terms leave S unchanged and decrease T by k+1. All terms are finite on a given tensor: ordinary truncation handles finite insertions, and transposed negative modes lower the outer-dual degree. For S bounded, induction on T therefore reduces every tensor to degrees S'<=S and T'<=N. This is not an assumption that the outer dual is C1-cofinite. [F1, F2, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Choose N_0 so the outer dual is generated by its degrees at most N_0. Raw transposed modes a_m^* generate the same mode algebra as the contragredient modes by the mutually inverse finite opposite-field formulas in F2. A word of these modes can be reordered with its positive-degree modes leftmost, then its zero-degree and negative-degree modes: commuting an inverted adjacent pair adds commutator words of smaller length, so finite induction on length and inversions proves this assertion. The nonpositive-degree suffix applied to the generators lies in the finite sum of degrees at most N_0. Consequently every vector of degree S>N_0 is a sum of a_(d-1+k)^*v with k>0 and v of degree S-k. The polynomial-current Ward identity transposes a_(m)^* at infinity to sum_i sum_(l=0)^m binom(m,l) z_i^(m-l)a_(l) at the leaves, where m=d-1+k>=0. This reduces S strictly, with no restriction on the resulting T. Induction on S, using step2.1 for the initial S<=N_0 strip, now reduces the whole quotient to the finite-dimensional tensor space S<=N_0,T<=N. Thus the Ward quotient is a finite R-module. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 This finite module with connection is locally free on the configuration space. Here is the elementary analytic argument needed for this use. Express connection derivatives of finite generators by holomorphic matrices on a small polydisc. On each coordinate line Picard's iterated integrals converge with bound M^k length^k/k!. A functional on a quotient fiber has a unique formal horizontal extension: successive covariant derivatives determine its Taylor coefficients, and commuting derivatives make them symmetric. Differentiated relations are again relations, so their pairings with that extension vanish coefficientwise. The analytic Picard solutions consequently annihilate all relations by Taylor uniqueness; coordinate-line solutions in different orders have the same Taylor coefficients and agree. They give inverse transports between quotient dual fibers, so fiber rank is constant. Fiber-basis generators generate locally by Nakayama's elementary local generator argument; a relation among them has coefficients zero in every fiber, hence holomorphic coefficients identically zero. The quotient is therefore a finite-rank flat holomorphic bundle. Its local horizontal solutions have unique continuation along paths, and homotopic paths give the same continuation by subdividing a homotopy into small coordinate rectangles. No tensor-product representability is involved. [F4, step 1.2, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Fix an internal tree ratio q, invert the other ratios, and keep the remaining shape variables near a distinct-point value. The polynomial inverse tree formulas express every coordinate difference as q to power zero or one times a holomorphic unit. Use coordinates relative to the cluster's rightmost leaf. Let B be the polynomial localization in these shapes, the other nonzero ratios and the unit differences, without inverting q. It is Noetherian. The reduction in step2.1 has the following stronger bound: a term of leaf degree T' and q-pole p satisfies p+T'<=T for the input tensor of leaf degree T, and its outer degree never exceeds S. Indeed an ordinary-current coefficient has pole at most k+1 and decreases T by k+1; an infinity coefficient is a polynomial of nonnegative q-valuation and lowers both S and T. Iterating preserves the bound, including terms whose coefficients have q-zeros. The Euler connection qD_q=q partial_q+sum_i(q partial_q z_i)L(-1)_i preserves that same bound: each nonzero second coefficient is divisible by q and its mode raises T by one. Thus all powers of qD_q applied to one fixed tensor have representatives in the one finite B-module sum_(S'<=S,T'<=N) q^(-T+T')B tensor(W_0' tensor...tensor W_n)_(S',T'). Noetherianity makes their span generated by a finite initial segment. Applying a horizontal coefficient gives a monic equation in q partial_q with coefficients holomorphic at q=0. Its companion vector is an actual finite holomorphic Euler system, not merely a scalar equation with unspecified singularity. [F1, F4, step 2.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Iterating the scale coordinates resolves a simultaneous nested collision explicitly: set each cluster point equal to its center plus its scale times its shape, choose a pair of distinct shapes as 0,1, and repeat inside every equal-shape subcluster. Each difference is a monomial in the scales times a holomorphic unit. Step5.1 applies to each boundary variable with the others inverted. The finite flat bundle on the product of punctured scale discs has commuting boundary monodromies M_j. Choose commuting logarithms B_j: choose scalar logarithms on the finitely many eigenvalues and use the finite nilpotent logarithm series, expressing each B_j as a polynomial in M_j by finite primary interpolation. If F is a horizontal fundamental matrix, H=F exp(-sum B_j log q_j) is single-valued. The one-variable Euler systems imply power times finite-log growth in each q_j with other parameters fixed, so H is separately meromorphic at each axis by the Laurent coefficient formula. This gives a uniform pole bound as follows. Its Laurent coefficients h_k in q_j are holomorphic in the other punctured variables. The closed sets on which every h_k with k<-N vanishes cover that parameter domain. One has interior: otherwise nested closed balls avoiding successive sets and with radii tending to zero give a point outside the cover. The identity theorem then makes all those h_k vanish throughout. Repeating for finitely many matrix entries and variables gives a common finite monomial clearing all poles. Product Cauchy integrals now give a convergent multivariable Laurent series for H through every intersection. Thus horizontal solutions have convergent finite-exponent, finite-log expansions at simultaneous tree boundaries. This argument proves the several-variable assertion rather than assuming separate regularity is sufficient. [F3, step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 A tree coefficient is a formal horizontal solution by step1.2. At a deepest sewing edge its coefficient at a fixed intermediate weight is a finite-dimensional projection sum of a shorter tree. The shorter-tree coefficients are holomorphic by induction, starting from a single intertwiner paired with homogeneous inputs, which is a finite power/log expression. The intermediate lower bound and finite cosets give a lower-bounded integer support after finitely many exponent shifts, and step1.1 bounds logarithmic degrees. The companion system of step5.1 and the parameter Euler estimate F3 therefore make this precise formal sewing series normally convergent for sufficiently small q with the other variables in a compact separated region. Repeating over the finitely many edges constructs a germ of the original full tree series. Its horizontal continuation is the one in step3.1. In any admissible product of ratio discs there is no configuration singularity. Its fundamental group consists of the commuting scale loops; extracting the finitely many exponent/log components by their finite monodromy primary projections makes each component single-valued there. Laurent uniqueness near the initial small torus identifies its coefficients with the original formal coefficients. Product Cauchy integrals on every larger admissible torus then bound the absolute coefficient sum on each smaller torus by a product of geometric series. For a fixed channel and exponent coset, the sewing exponents determine its intermediate weights triangularly from the leaves to the root; the resulting Laurent coefficient is exactly its scalar finite-block contraction. Thus this estimate controls the full projection sum, rather than only an iterated sum after cancellation between different weight tuples. Taking all admissible radii proves absolute local uniform convergence on the entire stated tree domain, not only near one boundary corner. Translation of the root is a finite polynomial after pairing with the fixed restricted-dual vector; root dilation is a finite power/log expression by generalized-weight homogeneity. Neither variable creates a missing radius condition. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 We check the continuation of insertion, including its infinite sum. At an inserted leaf use its rightmost anchor c and a small insertion scale q, and the contraction sum_h sum_b A(...,e_(h,b),...) B(e^( -c L(-1)^*)e^(h,b),...). Here the two e's are the canonical dual bases of one finite weight block, and the translation only changes the inner block's anchor; the formula is basis independent. At each formal q-degree the sum is finite by the weight shift, finite block dimensions and the lower support bound. Moving a current through its two factors gives opposite internal-edge residues, which cancel; the leaf residues left over are exactly the Ward relation of the grafted tree. The same calculation with translation gives its derivative identity. Thus the contraction is a formal horizontal coefficient of the grafted quotient. For any continuation of the exterior configurations along a compact collision-free path, choose q uniformly small enough to keep all inserted configurations disjoint. The coefficient functions of the formal contraction continue holomorphically in these parameters. Its initial finite companion-vector coefficients are therefore bounded on compact parameter neighborhoods, and the Euler recurrence from step5.1 and F3 gives local uniform absolute convergence of the contraction there. Uniqueness of horizontal continuation identifies its sum with the continuation of the initially grafted coefficient on overlapping neighborhoods. A finite subdivision of the path proves the insertion-continuation identity on the whole path. This supplies an estimate for the contraction after continuation; it does not exchange an infinite sum with continuation without justification. [F1, F2, F3, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 For two disjoint insertions choose two small scales. Coefficientwise finiteness and absolute convergence from steps7.1–8.1 allow their sums to be interchanged, so the two insertions commute. A product of left/right chiral coefficients is treated separately in its two copies. For a conjugate pair insert one tree at the holomorphic leaf and its copy at the barred leaf; along an actual real configuration path transport the two branches by the path and its conjugate. Affine insertion z=c+q w commutes literally with conjugation, and insertion in an open leaf commutes literally with the reflection double. The finite-component pairing and the opposite field already fixed in F2 introduce no additional boundary sign. These are exactly the three compositions claimed in the Statement. All arguments used only finitely many fixed modules and their given intertwiners, not a closed tensor category or an ordinary no-log factorization theorem. [F1, F2, step 7.1, step 8.1, algebra] ∎
