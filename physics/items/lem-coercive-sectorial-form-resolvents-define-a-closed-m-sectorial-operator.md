## Statement

Assume Dependent Choice ([[def-dependent-choice]]) for the cited integral and semigroup suppliers.

Let $a$ be a closed sectorial form on $V\subseteq H$ with constants $M,\theta$
and associated operator $A$
([[def-closed-sectorial-form-and-its-associated-operator]]). Choose any
$\kappa>0$ satisfying the continuous embedding bound
$\|u\|_H\le\kappa\|u\|_V$ for every $u\in V$; such a positive bound exists,
including when $H=\{0\}$. By the comparison clause of [[def-closed-sectorial-form-and-its-associated-operator]] under Dependent Choice, the closed form norm
$\|u\|_{a,M}^2:=\operatorname{Re}a(u,u)+(M+1)\|u\|_H^2$ is equivalent to
$\|u\|_V^2$; fix $c_a>0$ such that
$\|u\|_{a,M}^2\ge c_a\|u\|_V^2$. Then:

(1) $A$ is closed and $D(A)$ is dense in $H$;

(2) for every $\lambda$ with $\operatorname{Re}\lambda>M$, the form
$a_\lambda(u,v):=a(u,v)+\lambda(u,v)$ is coercive on $V$ with constant
$\beta_\lambda:=c_a\min\{1,\operatorname{Re}\lambda-M\}>0$. The Lax-Milgram
solution of $a(u,v)+\lambda(u,v)=(f,v)$ is the unique $u\in D(A)$ with
$(\lambda I-A)u=f$, and satisfies
$$\|u\|_V\le\kappa\beta_\lambda^{-1}\|f\|_H,\qquad \|u\|_H\le\kappa^2\beta_\lambda^{-1}\|f\|_H;$$

(3) $A$ is sectorial with vertex $M$ and every exponent
$\delta<\pi/2-\theta$ in the sense of
[[def-sectorial-operator-with-the-semigroup-sign-convention]]; in particular,
$\|R(\lambda,A)\|\le K_\varepsilon/|\lambda-M|$ on
$M+\Sigma_{\pi/2+\delta-\varepsilon}$;

(4) $B:=A-MI$ generates a bounded analytic semigroup $S$ on every sector
$\Sigma_\delta$ with $\delta<\pi/2-\theta$, and $T(z):=e^{Mz}S(z)$ is the
analytic semigroup generated by $A$, with
$\|T(z)\|\le C_{\delta'}e^{M\operatorname{Re}z}$ on each smaller sector
$\Sigma_{\delta'}$ with $\delta'<\delta$. If $a$ is coercive with
$\operatorname{Re}a(u,u)\ge\alpha\|u\|_V^2$, then
$\|T(t)\|\le e^{-\alpha t/\kappa^2}$ for $t\ge0$. Countable Choice is
inherited from [[thm-lax-milgram]]; no additional choice principle is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A closed sectorial form $a$ on $V\subseteq H$ with constants $M\ge0$, $\theta\in[0,\pi/2)$, associated operator $A$, and a positive embedding bound $\kappa>0$ with $\|u\|_H\le\kappa\|u\|_V$ for all $u\in V$; a form bound $C$ on $V$; and a constant $c_a>0$ with $\|u\|_{a,M}^2:=\operatorname{Re}a(u,u)+(M+1)\|u\|_H^2\ge c_a\|u\|_V^2$.

[L1] The associated operator is $D(A)=\{u\in V:\exists f\in H\text{ with }a(u,v)=-(f,v)\ \forall v\in V\}$ and $Au=f$ ([[def-closed-sectorial-form-and-its-associated-operator]]).

[L2] $V\subseteq H$ is a dense linear subspace carrying a Hilbert norm $\|\cdot\|_V$ whose inclusion into $H$ is continuous, and the chosen positive constant $\kappa$ satisfies $\|u\|_H\le\kappa\|u\|_V$; the shifted form norm satisfies $\|u\|_{a,M}^2=\operatorname{Re}a(u,u)+M\|u\|_H^2+\|u\|_H^2$ ([[def-closed-sectorial-form-and-its-associated-operator]]).

[L3] A form is coercive with constant $\alpha>0$ when $\operatorname{Re}a(u,u)\ge\alpha\|u\|^2$ for all $u$ ([[def-bounded-coercive-and-symmetric-sesquilinear-forms]]).

[L4] Lax-Milgram assumes Countable Choice, and for a bounded coercive form on a Hilbert space with coercivity constant $\alpha$ and a bounded conjugate-linear functional $F$ it produces a unique $u$ with $a(u,v)=F(v)$ for all $v$ and the estimate $\|u\|\le\|F\|/\alpha$ ([[thm-lax-milgram]], [[def-countable-choice]]).

[L5] For $u\in D(A)$ one has $\langle Au,u\rangle=-a(u,u)$ ([[lem-sectorial-form-angle-controls-the-numerical-range-of-its-operator]]).

[L6] For $u\in D(A)$ one has $\langle(A-M)u,u\rangle\in-\overline{S_\theta}$, where $\overline{S_\theta}$ is the closed sector of half-angle $\theta$ around the positive real axis ([[lem-sectorial-form-angle-controls-the-numerical-range-of-its-operator]]).

[L7] Cauchy-Schwarz: $|\langle x,y\rangle|\le\|x\|\,\|y\|$ ([[thm-cauchy-schwarz-in-an-inner-product-space]]).

[L8] The resolvent set is open: if $\lambda_0\in\rho(A)$ and $|\lambda-\lambda_0|\|R(\lambda_0,A)\|<1$ then $\lambda\in\rho(A)$ ([[lem-resolvent-identity-and-holomorphy-for-closed-operators]], [[def-resolvent-of-a-closed-operator]]).

[L9] The conditions (a)-(e) of the sectorial resolvent characterisation are equivalent; in particular condition (e) holds exactly when $A$ has a bounded analytic semigroup extension and generates a bounded strongly continuous semigroup ([[thm-sectorial-resolvent-characterisation-of-bounded-analytic-semigroups]]).

[L12] If the equivalent conditions hold, the generated semigroup is the contour semigroup ([[thm-sectorial-resolvent-characterisation-of-bounded-analytic-semigroups]]).

[L10] The contour semigroup generated by $A$ is the unique strongly continuous semigroup generated by $A$ within the class of exponentially bounded semigroups ([[lem-generator-of-the-contour-semigroup-is-the-sectorial-operator]]).

[L11] On a Hilbert space an operator is dissipative if and only if $\operatorname{Re}\langle Ax,x\rangle\le0$ for every $x\in D(A)$; and Lumer-Phillips: a densely defined dissipative operator generates a strongly continuous contraction semigroup if and only if $\operatorname{Ran}(\lambda_0I-A)=X$ for some $\lambda_0>0$ ([[def-dissipative-operator]], [[thm-lumer-phillips-generation-theorem]]).

[L13] Under Countable Choice, $N^{\perp\perp}=\overline N$ for every linear subspace $N$ of a Hilbert space ([[thm-double-orthogonal-complement-is-closure]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Coercivity and the resolvent solution. Put $q(u):=\operatorname{Re}a(u,u)+M\|u\|_H^2\ge0$, so that $\|u\|_{a,M}^2=q(u)+\|u\|_H^2$ and, for $\operatorname{Re}\lambda>M$, $\operatorname{Re}a_\lambda(u,u)=q(u)+(\operatorname{Re}\lambda-M)\|u\|_H^2\ge\min\{1,\operatorname{Re}\lambda-M\}\|u\|_{a,M}^2\ge\beta_\lambda\|u\|_V^2$, while $|a_\lambda(u,v)|\le(C+|\lambda|\kappa^2)\|u\|_V\|v\|_V$ and the functional $F(v):=(f,v)_H$ is conjugate-linear with $\|F\|\le\kappa\|f\|_H$; Lax-Milgram on the Hilbert space $V$ therefore gives, for each $f\in H$, a unique $u\in V$ with $a_\lambda(u,v)=(f,v)_H$ for all $v\in V$ and the bounds $\|u\|_V\le\kappa\beta_\lambda^{-1}\|f\|_H$ and $\|u\|_H\le\kappa^2\beta_\lambda^{-1}\|f\|_H$, and rewriting the weak equation as $a(u,v)=-(\lambda u-f,v)$ for all $v$ gives $u\in D(A)$ with $Au=\lambda u-f$, that is $(\lambda I-A)u=f$; conversely every $u\in D(A)$ with $(\lambda I-A)u=f$ satisfies the weak equation and is therefore this unique solution. [L1, L2, L3, L4, given, algebra]

2.1 Closedness of $A$. Fix a real $\lambda>M$; by [step 1.1] the map $\lambda I-A:D(A)\to H$ is bijective with $\|u\|_H\le\kappa^2\beta_\lambda^{-1}\|(\lambda I-A)u\|_H$, so if $u_n\in D(A)$ with $u_n\to u$ and $Au_n\to g$ then $\lambda u_n-Au_n\to\lambda u-g$ and $u_n=R(\lambda,A)(\lambda u_n-Au_n)\to R(\lambda,A)(\lambda u-g)$; comparing limits gives $u=R(\lambda,A)(\lambda u-g)\in D(A)$ and $(\lambda I-A)u=\lambda u-g$, that is $Au=g$, so the graph of $A$ is closed. [step 1.1, L1, given, algebra]

2.2 Density of $D(A)$. Let $f\in H$ satisfy $(f,w)=0$ for every $w\in D(A)$; by [step 1.1] with a real $\lambda>M$ there is $u\in D(A)$ with $(\lambda I-A)u=f$, so $(f,u)=0$ while, by [L5], $\operatorname{Re}(f,u)=\lambda\|u\|_H^2+\operatorname{Re}a(u,u)=q(u)+(\lambda-M)\|u\|_H^2\ge(\lambda-M)\|u\|_H^2\ge0$; hence $u=0$ and then $(f,v)=a_\lambda(u,v)=0$ for every $v\in V$; since $V$ is dense in $H$ by [L2], continuity of the inner product gives $f=0$, so $D(A)^\perp=\{0\}$ and [L13] gives $\overline{D(A)}=H$. [step 1.1, L1, L2, L5, L13, given, algebra]

3.1 Sectoriality with vertex $M$. For $u\in D(A)\setminus\{0\}$ the normalised value $z_u:=\langle Au,u\rangle/\|u\|_H^2$ lies in the closed sector $M-\overline{S_\theta}$ by [L6], so for $\lambda$ outside $M-\overline{S_\theta}$ one has $\operatorname{dist}(\lambda,M-\overline{S_\theta})>0$ and, by [L7], $\|(\lambda I-A)u\|\,\|u\|_H\ge|\langle(\lambda I-A)u,u\rangle|=|\lambda-z_u|\,\|u\|_H^2\ge\operatorname{dist}(\lambda,M-\overline{S_\theta})\|u\|_H^2$; thus $\lambda I-A$ is injective and $\|R(\lambda,A)g\|_H\le\|g\|_H/\operatorname{dist}(\lambda,M-\overline{S_\theta})$ on every surjectivity point $\lambda$. Let $\Omega:=\mathbb C\setminus(M-\overline{S_\theta})$ and $S:=\{\lambda\in\Omega:\lambda I-A\text{ is surjective}\}$: $S$ is nonempty because $(M,\infty)\subseteq S$ by [step 1.1]; $S$ is open in $\Omega$ because at $\lambda_0\in S$ injectivity and surjectivity give $\|R(\lambda_0,A)\|\le1/\operatorname{dist}(\lambda_0,M-\overline{S_\theta})$ and [L8] applies; $S$ is closed in $\Omega$ because for $\lambda_n\in S$ with $\lambda_n\to\lambda\in\Omega$ the resolvent identity gives $\|R(\lambda_n,A)f-R(\lambda_m,A)f\|\le|\lambda_n-\lambda_m|(2/\operatorname{dist}(\lambda,M-\overline{S_\theta}))^2\|f\|$, so $u_n:=R(\lambda_n,A)f$ converges to some $u$, and $\lambda_nu_n-Au_n=f$ with closedness of $A$ from [step 2.1] gives $u\in D(A)$ and $(\lambda I-A)u=f$; since $\Omega$ is connected (the complement of a closed sector of opening angle $2\theta<\pi$), $S=\Omega$; finally, for $\delta<\pi/2-\theta$ and $\lambda=M+re^{i\alpha}$ with $|\alpha|\le\pi/2+\delta-\varepsilon$ the angular distance from $\lambda$ to $M-\overline{S_\theta}$ is at least $\pi/2-\theta-\delta+\varepsilon$, so $\operatorname{dist}(\lambda,M-\overline{S_\theta})\ge|\lambda-M|\cos(\theta+\delta-\varepsilon)$ and hence $\|R(\lambda,A)\|\le K_\varepsilon/|\lambda-M|$ with $K_\varepsilon:=1/\cos(\theta+\delta-\varepsilon)$ on $M+\Sigma_{\pi/2+\delta-\varepsilon}$, which is sectoriality of vertex $M$ and every exponent $\delta<\pi/2-\theta$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, L6, L7, L8, given, algebra]

4.1 The shifted operator and the generated semigroup. Since $D(B)=D(A)$ is dense and $B$ is closed by [step 2.1] and [step 2.2], and since $\lambda I-B=(\lambda+M)I-A$ shows that $B$ is sectorial with vertex $0$ and every exponent $\delta<\pi/2-\theta$ together with the same $K_\varepsilon/|\lambda|$ bound, the characterisation theorem [L9, L12] provides a bounded analytic semigroup $S$ of angle $\delta$ generated by $B=A-MI$ on every such sector; the family $T(z):=e^{Mz}S(z)$ satisfies $T(0)=I$, $T(z+w)=T(z)T(w)$, is norm-holomorphic and strongly continuous, is bounded by $C_{\delta'}e^{M\operatorname{Re}z}$ on each $\Sigma_{\delta'}$, and has generator $A$ because for $x\in D(A)$ the difference quotient tends to $Bx+Mx=Ax$; conversely $S(t)=e^{-Mt}T(t)$ shows that a convergent $T$ difference quotient implies a convergent $S$ difference quotient, so the generator domain is exactly $D(A)$; it is therefore the analytic semigroup generated by $A$, unique among exponentially bounded semigroups by [L10]. [step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, L9, L12, L10, given, algebra]

5.1 The coercive case. If $\operatorname{Re}a(u,u)\ge\alpha\|u\|_V^2$, then for $u\in D(A)$ one has $\operatorname{Re}\langle Au,u\rangle=-\operatorname{Re}a(u,u)\le-\alpha\|u\|_V^2\le-\alpha\kappa^{-2}\|u\|_H^2$ by [L5] and $\|u\|_H\le\kappa\|u\|_V$, so $A_0:=A+\alpha\kappa^{-2}I$ is densely defined by [step 2.2] and dissipative by [L11], and $\operatorname{Ran}(\lambda_0I-A_0)=\operatorname{Ran}((\lambda_0-\alpha\kappa^{-2})I-A)=H$ for $\lambda_0:=M+\alpha\kappa^{-2}+1$ because $\lambda_0-\alpha\kappa^{-2}=M+1>M$ lies in $\rho(A)$ by [step 1.1]; hence $A_0$ generates a contraction semigroup $S'$ by [L11], and the family $t\mapsto e^{-\alpha\kappa^{-2}t}S'(t)$ is an exponentially bounded strongly continuous semigroup with generator $A$, so it equals $T$ by [L10] and $\|T(t)\|\le e^{-\alpha t/\kappa^2}$ for $t\ge0$; the argument assumes Dependent Choice and inherits Countable Choice from the Lax-Milgram step [step 1.1] and uses no further choice principle beyond Dependent Choice. [step 1.1, step 2.2, step 4.1, L5, L10, L11, given, algebra] ∎
