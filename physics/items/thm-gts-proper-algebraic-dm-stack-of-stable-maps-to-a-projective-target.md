## Statement

Let $V$ be smooth and projective over a field $k$, fix a very ample line bundle $A$ on $V$, integers $g,n\ge0$, and an effective curve class $\beta$. Use the algebraic degree convention $\beta(L)=\deg f^*L$ for $L\in\operatorname{Pic}(V)$; over $\mathbb C$ a specified homology class may be used instead. Put $d=\beta(A)$. A prestable $n$-pointed curve is a proper flat finitely presented family of geometrically connected nodal curves of arithmetic genus $g$ with $n$ ordered, pairwise disjoint sections in the relative smooth locus. A stable map is such a family with a morphism to $V$ of class $\beta$ for which every contracted geometric component $E$ satisfies $2g(\widetilde E)-2+s(E)>0$, where $\widetilde E$ is its normalization and $s(E)$ counts markings and node branches. This condition is equivalent to finiteness of the automorphism group scheme; it permits nonreduced stabilizers.

The stable-map stack $\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,n}(V,\beta)$ is a proper algebraic Artin stack of finite type over $k$ with finite stabilizers. Its Deligne–Mumford locus consists exactly of the geometric maps with no nonzero infinitesimal automorphism preserving the map and markings. In particular the whole stack is Deligne–Mumford in characteristic zero or in characteristic $p>d$. If $d=0$ and $2g-2+n>0$, it is canonically $V\times\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,n}$ and is Deligne–Mumford in every characteristic. If $d=0$ and $2g-2+n\le0$, it is empty. In arbitrary positive characteristic the full stable-map stack need not be Deligne–Mumford.
