## Statement

Let $X$ be a locally Noetherian $k$-scheme and let $x\in X(k)$ be a
$k$-rational point. Put $C_xX=\mathfrak m_x/\mathfrak m_x^2$ and
$T_xX=\operatorname{Hom}_k(C_xX,k)$. The module $C_xX$ is finite-dimensional;
write $\mathbb A(T_xX)=\operatorname{Spec}S_k(C_xX)$ for the affine space
associated to $T_xX$, using the canonical evaluation isomorphism
$C_xX\cong(T_xX)^\vee$. Multiplication in the local ring induces a graded
surjection
$$
S_k(C_xX)\longrightarrow\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m_x}\mathcal O_{X,x}
$$
whose degree-one map is the identity on $C_xX$. It therefore defines a closed
immersion of the scheme-theoretic tangent cone $\operatorname{Cone}_x(X)$ into
$\mathbb A(T_xX)$, and no proper linear closed subscheme of this affine space
contains $\operatorname{Cone}_x(X)$ scheme-theoretically. Here a linear closed
subscheme means one defined by an ideal generated by a vector subspace of
degree-one forms; the full scheme structure of the cone is retained.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A locally Noetherian $k$-scheme $X$ and a $k$-rational point $x$.

[F1] The scheme-theoretic tangent cone at $x$ is
$\operatorname{Spec}(\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m_x}\mathcal O_{X,x})$
([[def-tangent-cone-point]]).

[F2] The intrinsic tangent space is
$T_xX=\operatorname{Hom}_{\kappa(x)}(C_xX,\kappa(x))$
([[def-zariski-tangent-space-point]]); at a rational point $\kappa(x)=k$.

[F3] A locally Noetherian scheme has an affine open cover by spectra of
Noetherian rings ([[def-locally-noetherian-and-noetherian-scheme]]).

[F4] A point of an affine scheme corresponds to a prime ideal
([[def-prime-and-maximal-ideals]]).

[F5] The left regular module of a left Noetherian ring is Noetherian
([[def-noetherian-ring]]).

[F6] Every submodule of a Noetherian module is finitely generated
([[def-noetherian-module]]).

[F7] In a commutative ring the left, right and two-sided ideal notions agree
([[def-left-right-and-two-sided-ideal]]).

[F8] A submodule is an additive subgroup closed under scalar multiplication
([[def-submodule]]).

[F9] The stalk of the affine structure sheaf at a prime $\mathfrak p$ is
$R_{\mathfrak p}$ ([[thm-stalk-structure-sheaf-prime-localization]]).

[F10] The localization is $R_{\mathfrak p}=(R\setminus\mathfrak p)^{-1}R$
([[def-localisation-at-a-prime-ideal]]).

[F11] The unique maximal ideal of $R_{\mathfrak p}$ is
$\mathfrak pR_{\mathfrak p}$ ([[thm-localisation-at-a-prime-is-local]]).

[F12] The dual-numbers scheme is $D_k=\operatorname{Spec}(k[\epsilon]/
(\epsilon^2))$ and its class $\epsilon$ is nilpotent
([[def-dual-numbers-scheme]]).

[F13] The symmetric algebra $S_k(V)$ is a commutative graded algebra generated
by the degree-one image of $V$ ([[def-symmetric-algebra-of-a-vector-space]]).

[F14] A linear map from $V$ to a commutative $k$-algebra extends uniquely to
an algebra map from $S_k(V)$ ([[thm-universal-property-of-the-symmetric-algebra]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Choose an affine open $U=\operatorname{Spec}R$ containing $x$, available by [F3], and write $x=\mathfrak p$. Then $R$ is Noetherian, and $\mathfrak p$ is a submodule of the left regular module $R$ by [F4, F7, F8]. By [F5, F6], it has a finite generating list. The stalk and its maximal ideal are $\mathcal O_{X,x}=R_{\mathfrak p}$ and $\mathfrak m_x=\mathfrak pR_{\mathfrak p}$ by [F9, F10, F11], so the images of that list generate $\mathfrak m_x$. Hence $C_xX=\mathfrak m_x/\mathfrak m_x^2$ is finite-dimensional over $k$. By [F2], $T_xX$ is its dual, and the finite-dimensional evaluation map $C_xX\to (T_xX)^\vee$ is an isomorphism. Thus the coordinate algebra of $\mathbb A(T_xX)$ is $S_k(C_xX)$. [F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, F10, F11, given, algebra]

2.1 By step 1.1, $S_k(C_xX)$ is the coordinate algebra of the affine tangent space. The degree-one quotient $C_xX=\mathfrak m_x/\mathfrak m_x^2$ maps to the degree-one part of $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m_x}\mathcal O_{X,x}$ by the identity. Its multiplication extends to a graded algebra map $\phi:S_k(C_xX)\to\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m_x}\mathcal O_{X,x}$ by [F13, F14]. In degree $d$, every element of $\mathfrak m_x^d$ is a finite sum of products of $d$ elements of $\mathfrak m_x$; replacing each factor by its class modulo $\mathfrak m_x^2$ changes each product only by an element of $\mathfrak m_x^{d+1}$. Thus $\phi$ is surjective in every degree. In degree one it is the identity, so if $J=\ker\phi$, then $J_1=0$. [F13, F14, step 1.1, given, algebra]

3.1 By [F1], the spectrum of the target of $\phi$ is $\operatorname{Cone}_x(X)$. The surjection $\phi$ gives a closed immersion $\operatorname{Cone}_x(X)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}S_k(C_xX)=\mathbb A(T_xX)$, with scheme ideal $J$. If a linear closed subscheme $L$ contains the cone scheme-theoretically, its ideal is generated by a subspace $W\subseteq C_xX$ of degree-one forms and must satisfy $W\subseteq J$. Taking degree-one parts gives $W\subseteq J_1=0$, hence $W=0$ and $L=\mathbb A(T_xX)$. This proves both the embedding and the claimed scheme-theoretic linear span without replacing the cone by its reduction. [F1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 If $C_xX=0$, then the target affine space is $\operatorname{Spec}k$ and the surjection in step 2.1 forces every positive graded piece of the local associated graded ring to vanish; the cone is the whole point. For the one-dimensional nonreduced example from [F12] at $x=(\epsilon)$, the local ring is $k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$ and its maximal ideal is $(\epsilon)$, so its associated graded ring is $k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$. The cone is a doubled origin in $\mathbb A^1$, while its reduction is only the origin; no nonzero linear equation vanishes on the scheme-theoretic cone. The point hypothesis rules out an empty $X$ at the point under discussion. The argument uses one affine neighborhood and a finite generating list for its one prime ideal, and makes no simultaneous choices, basis selection, or Axiom of Choice. The statement is not an iff. [F1, F12, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
