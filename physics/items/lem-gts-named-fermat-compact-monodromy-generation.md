## Statement

Assume AC. Let $N=4,5$, $n=N-2$, and consider the actual Fermat degree-$N$ pencil $\sum_i x_i^N-z\prod_i x_i=0$ in $\mathbb P^{N-1}$ with the source scaled Fubini–Study class. Its graded large-circle monodromy is symplectically isotopic, with the actual grading lift, to one admitting arbitrarily small invariant neighborhoods of the multiple-coordinate-zero exceptional set and negative displacement on their complements. The exceptional real strata have dimension at most $2n-4<n$, so Hamiltonian general position avoids them. The full compact source rational-brane Fukaya category is split-generated by the actual finite collection of lifted vanishing spheres: $64$ for $N=4$ and $625$ for $N=5$. This carries to every smooth degree-$N$ member with the original source symplectic scaling and coefficient conventions; it does not assert a divisor-preserving equivalence of relative categories.

At a point of $C_0$ the Hermitian gradient of F in homogeneous coordinates is $N(\bar x_1^{N-1},\ldots ,\bar x_N^{N-1})$. It is horizontal because its inner product with x is $N\overline{F(x)}=0$. The Fermat divisor and all coordinate divisors therefore have mutually orthogonal complex normal lines along every intersection. 6.1 **Finite relative collar induction** Let $D=⋃_{i=0}^N$ $C_i$ be the named simple normal-crossing divisor, with the complex structures and meromorphic pencil map $p=P/F$ fixed. Write $C_I^circ$ for an open stratum, and $e_0=-1$, $e_i=1$ for $i>0$. Near $C_I^circ$ the divisor of p has exactly the multiplicities $e_i$, i∈I. We construct coordinates as smooth families indexed by the stratum point; for each fixed stratum point the fibre coordinates and their local defining germs are holomorphic. The family need not be a holomorphic tubular projection.
The required relative extension is the following elementary coordinate-germ construction. Suppose a family of such fibre charts has already been prescribed over a closed collar A in the base stratum, and the prescribed germs agree on overlapping inherited collars. Choose a complex splitting of the normal exact sequence along the stratum, extending the prescribed splitting on A. Splittings form an affine bundle, so a partition of unity extends them without changing A. Cover the rest of the base by complex normal-crossing charts. At each basepoint x normalize each chart germ by a complex affine change of its tangential coordinates and a nonzero complex scalar on each normal coordinate, so that its value and first derivative are the fixed splitting identification
$T_xX$ ≅ $T_xC_I$ ⊕ $\oplus _{i\in I}$ $N_{i,x}$.
Tangential coordinates may have linear normal terms added to achieve this normalization. Normal coordinates are not mixed, so each continues to vanish on its particular divisor. Regard the normalized germs as maps into the displayed complex vector space, not into independently chosen scalar frames. Their linear transition on that target is then just change of vector-bundle frame.
For fixed x, take their weighted sum with coefficients from a partition of unity on the BASE. Include the prescribed chart as one summand, with coefficient one on a smaller closed collar and zero outside its given collar. The sum is holomorphic in the ambient variable for fixed x, varies smoothly with x, and has the same invertible first derivative as every summand. Each normal component vanishes on its prescribed divisor, and its first derivative there is nonzero. In a local scalar frame it is the original local defining function times a holomorphic unit near x; shrinking excludes additional zeros. The parameter-dependent inverse-function theorem therefore gives a family of local coordinate germs. The inverse map at tangential coordinate zero gives the fibre over x. Its differential on the zero section is the prescribed normal splitting, so (x,normal coordinates) maps diffeomorphically onto a neighborhood of the zero section after shrinking. On noncompact open strata the radius is a positive smooth function. Relative equality holds on the smaller collar because there the prescribed summand has coefficient one.
Prescribed fibres obtained by restricting a deeper fibre chart meet the hypotheses: they are holomorphic submanifolds of its holomorphic fibre. Their local defining functions extend holomorphically in an ambient complex chart by setting the additional tangential coordinates free. This extension can be made smoothly in the base parameter by the same inverse-function construction. Thus taking an inherited subfibre does not require the deeper tubular projection itself to be holomorphic.
Now perform descending-depth induction on the finite index sets I. At maximal depth the strata are disjoint compact components, and ordinary normal-crossing coordinate germs give the initial families. At a later depth, the deeper data prescribe the new charts near $⋃_{J⊋I}C_J$: inside a J-chart the I-fibre is obtained by fixing the coordinates labelled by $J\setminus I$ and retaining the coordinates labelled by I. Its base consists of the old residual base together with those fixed omitted coordinates. Charts already prescribed by two deeper sets agree on their common deeper collar by the inductive hypothesis.
Reserve finitely many concentric collar layers at each stage, more layers than the total number of remaining depths. Extend on the outer layer and impose exact equality on the next inner layer. After that stage use only the inner layer for the deeper tube. This makes the preceding relative construction applicable at every later stage, with no infinite limiting process.
Here is the separation argument for incomparable tubes. Two closed strata $C_I,C_J$ intersect in $C_{I∪J}$. Remove an open neighborhood of that union stratum whose closure lies inside its already fixed collar. The remaining closed cores of $C_I$ and $C_J$ are disjoint compact sets, so have positive distance in a fixed auxiliary metric. Their new tubular radii may therefore be chosen to have disjoint closures outside the prescribed collar. There are finitely many pairs. Taking the minimum of the finitely many permitted radii ensures that every incomparable overlap is inside an inherited union-stratum collar. The same argument ensures that a nested smaller tube meets the deeper tube only where its inherited chart is prescribed. Subsequent finite shrinking preserves these conditions. Thus all overlap compatibility is equality of prescribed coordinates on an OPEN collar, not merely agreement of their first derivatives along the stratum. 7.1 **Normalize the pencil unit without breaking inherited collars** On a fibre chart over $x\in C_I^circ$, let $z_i$ denote its normal coordinates. Meromorphic normal-crossing factorization gives
$p(z)=u_x(z)$ $\prod _{i\in I}$ $z_i^{e_i}$,
where $u_x$ is holomorphic and nowhere zero, and varies smoothly with x. The factorization follows by successively dividing the numerator and denominator by the simple local defining functions; after those divisions neither zeros nor poles remain in a small fibre neighborhood. Fix one pivot a∈I and replace
$z_a$ by $z_a$ $[u_x(z)/u_x(0)]^{1/e_a}$,
leaving the other normal coordinates unchanged. Since $e_a$ is 1 or −1, this uses multiplication or inversion, with no choice of a root. Its derivative at the zero section is the identity, and it is a local holomorphic coordinate change. In the new coordinates p is the product monomial times the base factor $u_x(0)$. A change of frame in a normal line changes $u_x(z)$ and $u_x(0)$ by the same base scalar; their ratio is frame-independent. Hence this normalization glues over the base.
On an inherited collar, p is already a product monomial in the deeper coordinates. Restricting to an I-subfibre makes the omitted coordinates part of the base, so its unit is constant along that subfibre. The ratio $u_x(z)/u_x(0)$ is therefore one there. The normalization does not change the prescribed collar. It can consequently be applied at each relative induction stage of §1.

$p(ρ_{I,t}y)=\exp (i\sum _{i\in I}e_i t_i)p(y)$.

8.1 **Averaging with a logarithmic cutoff** The quadratic primitive estimate in the preceding note assumes that the initial form and its average agree as full tensors along the fixed stratum. The following version removes that extra requirement and is used in the relative induction.
Let ω tame the original J, and average it over one of the compact tori to obtain $\barω$. At each point of the fixed stratum S, the action derivative is complex-linear by §2, so every pullback of ω tames J there; their average does too. Uniform positivity on a compact stratum core and continuity give a tube on which both ω and $\barω$ tame J, with a common positive lower bound. The difference $Δ=\barω-ω$ is closed and restricts to zero on TS, since the torus fixes S pointwise. Fibre radial homotopy therefore gives a primitive β with $dβ=Δ$ and |β|≤Cr. Here β need only have order one.
Choose an invariant radial cutoff χ satisfying $χ=1$ at $r\le r_in$, $χ=0$ at $r\ge r_out$ and |rχ'(r)|≤ε. It exists with $r_in$ much smaller than $r_out$: take χ to be a fixed smooth cutoff of $(\log  r-\log  r_in)/(\log  r_out-\log  r_in)$, making the logarithmic ratio large. Then
$ω+d(χβ)=(1-χ)ω+χ\barω+dχ\wedge β$.
The first two terms have the common taming lower bound, while |dχ∧β|≤Cε. Choose ε below half that bound. The displayed form, and the entire same linear surgery path, tame J. It equals the averaged form on the inner tube, equals ω outside the outer tube, and differs from ω by a globally exact compactly supported form.
