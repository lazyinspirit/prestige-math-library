## Example

Take $V=W=\mathbb C$ with their standard Hermitian products and let $\zeta>0$. For scalar maps $B_1,B_2\in\mathbb C$ and $I,J\in\mathbb C$, the complex and real moment maps are $\mu_{\mathbb C}=[B_1,B_2]+IJ=IJ$ and $\mu_{\mathbb R}=|I|^2-|J|^2$; positive-FI solutions satisfy $\mu_{\mathbb C}=0$ and $\mu_{\mathbb R}=\zeta$. Stability means $I(W)$ spans $V$, and costability means no nonzero common invariant subspace is contained in $\ker J$. The datum $B_1=B_2=0$, $I=\sqrt\zeta$, $J=0$ is stable and positive-FI, but not costable, so it is outside the zero-FI regular locus. Under the complex $GL(1,\mathbb C)$ action its stable orbit has representative $I=1$, whose framed $\mathbb P^2$ monad on homogeneous coordinates $[z_0:z_1:z_2]$ is

$$\mathcal O(-1)\xrightarrow{(-z_1,-z_2,0)^T}\mathcal O^{\oplus3}\xrightarrow{(z_2,-z_1,z_0)}\mathcal O(1),$$

whose middle cohomology is the ideal sheaf of $p=[1:0:0]$; it is torsion-free and not locally free at $p$. Thus this positive-FI point lies on the non-locally-free boundary excluded from the ordinary zero-FI framed ASD correspondence.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $N=k=1$, a positive real level $\zeta>0$, and the ADHM, quotient and framed monad conventions in the cited items.

[F1] The complex moment is $[B_1,B_2]+IJ$, the real moment is $[B_1,B_1^\dagger]+[B_2,B_2^\dagger]+II^\dagger-J^\dagger J$, stability means the $B_i$-words applied to $I(W)$ span $V$, and costability forbids a nonzero common invariant subspace in $\ker J$. [[def-qftx-adhm-linear-data-stability-and-regularity]]

[F2] The positive-FI real quotient represents the stable complex orbit, while at zero FI the real quotient represents exactly the stable-and-costable locus. [[thm-qftx-adhm-real-complex-quotient-correspondence]]

[F3] For a stable complex datum the framed $\mathbb P^2$ monad has maps $\alpha=(z_0B_1-z_1,z_0B_2-z_2,z_0J)^T$ and $\beta=(-z_0B_2+z_2,z_0B_1-z_1,z_0I)$, and its middle cohomology is a framed torsion-free sheaf; local freeness is equivalent to costability. [[thm-qftx-framed-p2-monad-adhm-correspondence]]

## Proof

**Given:** The datum and hypotheses in the Example.

1.1 For $V=W=\mathbb C$, both commutators vanish. Thus $\mu_{\mathbb C}=IJ=0$ and $\mu_{\mathbb R}=|I|^2-|J|^2=\zeta$. Since $I\ne0$, the words of length zero applied to $I(W)$ span $V$, so the datum is stable. Since $J=0$, the nonzero subspace $V$ is invariant and contained in $\ker J$, so the datum is not costable; in particular it is not a zero-FI regular datum. By [F2], its positive-FI complex orbit has a representative with $I=1$ after the $GL(1,\mathbb C)$ change of basis $g=\zeta^{-1/2}$. [F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 Substituting that representative in [F3] gives $\alpha=(-z_1,-z_2,0)^T$ and $\beta=(z_2,-z_1,z_0)$. Their product is $-z_2z_1+z_1z_2=0$. The row $\beta$ is surjective at every projective point because its three entries have no common zero, and $\alpha$ is injective as a sheaf map because it is nonzero away from $p$. Let $K=\ker\beta$. Projection to the third coordinate restricts to a morphism $\gamma:K\to\mathcal O$. On the affine chart $z_0=1$, put $x=z_1$ and $y=z_2$; a vector in $K$ has the form $(u,v,-yu+xv)$, so $\gamma(K)=(x,y)$ and its kernel is generated by $(-x,-y,0)$, the image of $\alpha$. On any chart away from $p$ where $z_1$ or $z_2$ is a unit, the same equations show that $\gamma$ is onto and its kernel is generated by $\alpha$. Hence $K/\operatorname{im}\alpha\cong I_p$ globally, where $I_p$ is the ideal sheaf of $p$. [F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 At $p$, the local ideal $(x,y)$ is not principal: if it were generated by one element in the regular local ring $\mathbb C[x,y]_{(x,y)}$, its quotient by the maximal ideal would have dimension at most one, whereas $(x,y)/(x,y)^2$ has dimension two. Therefore $I_p$ is not locally free at $p$. Its framing on $L_\infty$ is the canonical trivialization because $p\notin L_\infty$. This exhibits a positive-FI stable point outside the costable, locally-free zero-FI locus; the correspondence with ordinary ASD moduli is restricted to that zero-FI locus and does not absorb this boundary point. [F2, F3, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
