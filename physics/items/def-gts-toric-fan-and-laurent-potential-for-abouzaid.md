## Definition

A **lattice** here is a finite-rank free abelian group in the sense of “Free abelian group on a set”. For such a group $N$, put $N_{\mathbb R}=N\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb R$, $M=\operatorname{Hom}(N,\mathbb Z)$ and $M_{\mathbb R}=M\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb R$, with their evaluation pairing $\langle m,v\rangle$. A **strongly convex rational polyhedral cone** is a set $\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{a}\mathbb R_{\ge0}w_i$ generated by finitely many $w_i\in N$ and satisfying $\sigma\cap(-\sigma)=\{0\}$. Write $n=\operatorname{rank}N$ and $\dim\sigma=\dim_{\mathbb R}\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\sigma$. Its dual is $\sigma^\vee=\{m\in M_{\mathbb R}:\langle m,v\rangle\ge0\text{ for all }v\in\sigma\}$. A **face** is $\sigma\cap\ell^\perp$ for a linear functional $\ell\in\sigma^\vee$, including $\sigma$ itself by taking $\ell=0$. For the smooth cones below, the zero face is obtained by a functional positive on every basis ray. A ray is a one-dimensional cone; $\Sigma(1)$ denotes the set of rays in a fan $\Sigma$. Its **primitive generator** $v_\rho$ is the unique nonzero vector with $\rho\cap N=\mathbb Z_{\ge0}v_\rho$.

A **fan** $\Sigma$ is a nonempty finite collection of these cones that contains every face of each member and for which $\sigma\cap\tau$ is a face of both $\sigma$ and $\tau$ for all members. It is **complete** when $\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\sigma=N_{\mathbb R}$ and **smooth** when every cone has a presentation $\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb R_{\ge0}v_i$ for vectors $v_1,\ldots,v_d$ that form part of an integral basis of $N$. In these basis coordinates its rays are exactly the positive coordinate axes, so these vectors are precisely its primitive ray generators. Its coordinate description also shows it is closed: the first $d$ coordinates are nonnegative and all remaining coordinates vanish. In a complete smooth fan every cone maximal under inclusion has dimension $n$: otherwise, for such a cone $\sigma$, let $x$ be the sum of its primitive rays and take a sequence tending to $x$ off its linear span. Completeness and finiteness give another cone $\tau$ containing a subsequence, and closedness gives $x\in\tau$. A nonnegative supporting functional defining the face $\sigma\cap\tau$ vanishes on $x$, hence on each ray of $\sigma$, so $\sigma\subseteq\tau$. The off-span points make this inclusion strict, contradicting maximality. Thus the rays of a maximal cone form a full integral basis. The rank-zero case is $\Sigma=\{\{0\}\}$.

For a smooth fan $\Sigma$, the **associated toric scheme** $X_\Sigma$ uses the following explicit affine charts and gluing. For an additive submonoid $S\subset M$, $\mathbb C[S]$ is the algebra with basis symbols $\chi^m$ for $m\in S$, finite linear combinations as elements, and multiplication $\chi^m\chi^{m'}=\chi^{m+m'}$. Set $S_\sigma=\sigma^\vee\cap M$ and $U_\sigma=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C[S_\sigma]$, with affine scheme meaning “Affine schemes and their coordinate rings”. For a smooth cone choose an integral basis $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ whose first $d$ vectors are its primitive rays, and let $m_1,\ldots,m_n$ be the dual basis. Then $S_\sigma=\sum_{i\le d}\mathbb Z_{\ge0}m_i+\sum_{i>d}\mathbb Zm_i$, so its chart algebra is exactly $\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_d,x_{d+1}^{\pm1},\ldots,x_n^{\pm1}]$, where $x_i=\chi^{m_i}$. This coordinate calculation also gives finite generation directly in the smooth case; no separate general semigroup theorem is assumed.

In these smooth coordinates a face is generated by a subset of the rays: its nonnegative supporting functional vanishes on precisely that subset, and the nonnegative sum can vanish only on those generators. If a face $\tau\preceq\sigma$ is generated by the subset indexed by $I\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}$, its ring is $\mathbb C[S_\tau]=\mathbb C[S_\sigma][(\prod_{j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\setminus I}x_j)^{-1}]$. Indeed the inequalities removed from the dual cone allow precisely those formerly nonnegative coordinates to have arbitrary integer exponent. The empty product is $1$. By “A principal localization identifies its spectrum with a distinguished open” this identifies $U_\tau$ with the indicated distinguished open in $U_\sigma$, including its structure sheaf. These maps are intrinsic inclusions of the monomial algebras in $\mathbb C[M]$; a different integral basis gives the same map, not additional gluing data.

For $\sigma,\tau\in\Sigma$ glue their common open $U_{\sigma\cap\tau}$ by these identity-on-character identifications. Within any one smooth chart, intersecting two face opens inverts the union of their removed coordinates and gives the open for the intersection of the faces. Hence on a triple overlap both composites are the same inclusion into $\mathbb C[S_{\sigma\cap\tau\cap\eta}]$. The identity and cocycle conditions hold, and “Gluing affine schemes along compatible open isomorphisms” constructs $X_\Sigma$ with these open charts. No additional pairwise identifications arise from chains: two consecutive identifications meet in precisely that triple overlap and compose to the stated direct one. Consequently, inside $X_\Sigma$, the intersection $U_\sigma\cap U_\tau$ is exactly $U_{\sigma\cap\tau}$, and the same common-cone rule holds for every finite intersection. The chart for $\{0\}$ is the common dense torus $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C[M]$, whose complex points are $\operatorname{Hom}(M,\mathbb C^*)$. In each chart this torus is the dense distinguished open obtained by inverting $x_1\cdots x_d$; the chart ring is a domain, so its zero prime belongs to this open and its closure is the whole chart. Its algebraic action is specified by the coaction $\chi^m\mapsto\chi^m\otimes\chi^m$ into $\mathbb C[M]\otimes_{\mathbb C}\mathbb C[S_\sigma]$. These coactions respect the displayed localizations and glue to a torus action on $X_\Sigma$. This is the original toric torus, with character lattice $M$; the mirror torus below has character lattice $N$.

In this definition **projective** retains the additional condition that $X_\Sigma\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C$ admits a closed immersion into some finite-dimensional projective space, as in “Projective morphisms before Proj”. It is a restriction on the selected fan data, not an assertion that every smooth complete fan is projective.

Let $N\cong\mathbb Z^n$ be a lattice, $M=\operatorname{Hom}(N,\mathbb Z)$ its dual, and $\Sigma$ a smooth complete fan in $N_{\mathbb R}$ whose associated toric variety $X_\Sigma$ is projective. For each one-dimensional cone $\rho\in\Sigma(1)$, let $v_\rho\in N$ be its primitive lattice generator. Set $A_\Sigma=\{0\}\cup\{v_\rho:\rho\in\Sigma(1)\}$. For coefficients $c_a\in\mathbb C^*$, the associated Laurent polynomial on the mirror algebraic torus $T^\vee=\operatorname{Hom}(N,\mathbb C^*)$, whose character lattice is $N$, is

$$W_\Sigma(z)=\sum_{a\in A_\Sigma}c_a z^a,$$

where $z^a(t)=t(a)$ is the character determined by $a\in N$ and $t\in T^\vee$. Thus the fan lattice of $X_\Sigma$ is the character lattice of its mirror torus; no identification of $N$ with its dual $M$ is assumed. An **admissible toric Landau–Ginzburg datum** additionally names the coefficient choices, the zero fibre $M_0=W_\Sigma^{-1}(0)$, and any regularity, symplectic, or Lefschetz conditions actually required for the selected Fukaya construction. This definition does not assert that generic coefficients satisfy those conditions, that $M_0$ is smooth, or that a homological mirror equivalence holds.
