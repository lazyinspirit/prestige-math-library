## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. In the Clifford construction of the preceding definition, $\Delta$ is a $16$-dimensional irreducible complex module for $\mathrm{Cl}(V,Q)\otimes_\mathbb R\mathbb C$. Its volume eigenspaces $\Delta^+$ and $\Delta^-$ each have complex dimension $8$, and the conjugate-linear involution $J$ preserves both, commutes with Spin(8), and has fixed real spaces whose complexifications are $\Delta^+$ and $\Delta^-$. The resulting real modules $8_v,8_s,8_c$ are irreducible, and the inner products specified in the definition are positive-definite and Spin(8)-invariant. The Clifford action satisfies $g c(v)g^{-1}=c(\rho_gv)$ and maps each volume eigenspace to the other. Spin(8) is generated by the lifts of coordinate-plane rotations together with its central kernel $\{1,-1\}$, and their infinitesimal actions on $\Delta$ are $C_iC_j/2$.

For the Lie-algebra weights, define $R_j\in\mathfrak{so}(V)$ by $R_je_{2j-1}=-e_{2j}$, $R_je_{2j}=e_{2j-1}$, and $R_je_k=0$ for $k\notin\{2j-1,2j\}$. Put $H_j=-iR_j\in\mathfrak{so}(8,\mathbb C)$, $\mathfrak h=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{H_1,H_2,H_3,H_4\}$, and define $\xi_j\in\mathfrak h^*$ by $\xi_j(H_k)=\delta_{jk}$. On $E=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_4\}$ use the Euclidean inner product $(\xi_i,\xi_j)=\delta_{ij}$. Then $\mathfrak h$ is a Cartan subalgebra; the complexified rotation Lie algebra $\mathfrak{so}(8,\mathbb C)$ is simple with root system $\{\pm\xi_i\pm\xi_j:i<j\}$. Choose the positive roots $\xi_i-\xi_j$ and $\xi_i+\xi_j$ for $i<j$. The simple roots are $\alpha_1=\xi_1-\xi_2$, $\alpha_2=\xi_2-\xi_3$, $\alpha_3=\xi_3-\xi_4$, and $\alpha_4=\xi_3+\xi_4$, with fundamental weights $\lambda_1=\xi_1$, $\lambda_2=\xi_1+\xi_2$, $\lambda_3=(\xi_1+\xi_2+\xi_3-\xi_4)/2$, and $\lambda_4=(\xi_1+\xi_2+\xi_3+\xi_4)/2$. The complexifications of $8_v,8_c,8_s$ have highest weights $\lambda_1,\lambda_3,\lambda_4$, respectively; reversing chirality exchanges $\lambda_3$ and $\lambda_4$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The standard oriented Euclidean space $V=\mathbb R^8$, the plus-$Q$ Clifford action on $\Delta=\Lambda^\bullet\mathbb C^4$ and the real structure $J$ defined in [[def-spin8-vector-and-half-spin-modules]], and the full-even Spin group acting on $V$ and $\Delta$.

[F1] The module construction fixes $C_{2j-1}=\epsilon_j+\iota_j$, $C_{2j}=i(\epsilon_j-\iota_j)$, $\omega=C_1\cdots C_8$, $\Delta^\pm=\ker(\omega\mp I)$, and $J=C_2C_4C_6C_8K$ ([[def-spin8-vector-and-half-spin-modules]]).

[F2] The full-even Spin group maps onto $SO(V,Q)$; its kernel is $\{1,-1\}$, and it is compact for definite $Q$ ([[thm-real-pin-spin-image-kernel-and-compactness]]).

[F3] For an eight-dimensional vector space, the Clifford algebra has dimension $2^8=256$ over its base field ([[thm-clifford-pbw-basis-and-associated-graded]]).

[F4] A linear map from $V$ to a unital associative algebra that satisfies $f(v)^2=Q(v)1$ extends uniquely to a unital Clifford algebra homomorphism ([[thm-universal-property-of-clifford-algebra]]).

[F5] For a finite-dimensional complex Lie algebra, a Cartan subalgebra is a nilpotent self-normalizing Lie subalgebra; relative to it, roots and root spaces are defined by the simultaneous eigenspaces of $\operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak h)$, and weights and weight spaces of a representation are defined by the analogous simultaneous eigenspaces of $\mathfrak h$ ([[def-finite-dimensional-lie-algebra]], [[def-cartan-subalgebra-of-a-lie-algebra]], [[def-root-and-root-space-relative-to-a-cartan-subalgebra]], [[def-representation-of-a-lie-algebra]], [[def-weight-and-weight-space-of-a-lie-algebra-representation]]).

[F6] A positive system is fixed by a regular vector in the Euclidean root span; its positive-root subalgebra is the sum of the positive root spaces. The root order is $\mu\leq\lambda$ when $\lambda-\mu$ is a nonnegative integral combination of the chosen simple roots. A highest-weight vector is a nonzero weight vector annihilated by that positive-root subalgebra; fundamental weights are dual to the simple coroots ([[def-reduced-crystallographic-euclidean-root-system]], [[def-positive-system-and-base-of-simple-roots]], [[def-positive-and-negative-nilpotent-subalgebras-and-borel-subalgebra]], [[def-partial-order-on-weights]], [[def-highest-weight-vector-and-highest-weight-module]], [[def-fundamental-weights]]).

[F7] A finite-dimensional nonabelian complex Lie algebra with no nonzero proper ideals is simple; a simple Lie algebra is semisimple under the convention that semisimple means having no nonzero solvable ideal ([[def-lie-subalgebra-ideal-and-center]], [[def-derived-series-and-solvable-lie-algebra]], [[def-finite-semisimple-lie-algebra-and-symmetric-adjoint-action]]).

[F8] The Lie-algebra coroot of a root is $h_\alpha=2H_\alpha/\alpha(H_\alpha)$, where the Killing-dual vector satisfies $B(H_\alpha,H)=\alpha(H)$ for every $H\in\mathfrak h$ ([[def-coroot-of-a-lie-algebra-root]], [[def-killing-dual-vector-of-a-root]]).

[F9] The Killing form on a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra is $B(X,Y)=\operatorname{tr}_{\mathfrak g}(\operatorname{ad}_X\operatorname{ad}_Y)$ ([[def-finite-semisimple-lie-algebra-and-symmetric-adjoint-action]]).

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is retained as a hypothesis of the imported positive-root subalgebra, root-order, and highest-weight definitions in [F6] and the Killing-dual and coroot definitions in [F8] ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Step 1.3 uses the [F8] definitions to identify the coroots, and step 5.1 uses the [F6] definitions to identify the highest weights. The explicit finite-dimensional calculations do not assert that AC is necessary for these particular constructions.

## Proof

1.1 On the ordered wedge basis $u_I=u_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{i_r}$, exterior multiplication and deletion satisfy $\epsilon_j^2=\iota_j^2=0$, $\epsilon_j\epsilon_k+\epsilon_k\epsilon_j=0$, $\iota_j\iota_k+\iota_k\iota_j=0$, and $\epsilon_j\iota_k+\iota_k\epsilon_j=\delta_{jk}I$: if a deletion index is absent its term is zero, and when both orders are nonzero they differ by one transposition. Expanding $C_{2j-1}$ and $C_{2j}$ therefore gives $C_aC_b+C_bC_a=2\delta_{ab}I$ for all $a,b$, so [F4] defines a representation of the complexified Clifford algebra on $\Delta$. [given, F1, F4, algebra]

1.2 Put $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{so}(8,\mathbb C)=\{X\in M_8(\mathbb C):X^T=-X\}$ with its commutator bracket. For $1\leq a<b\leq8$ let $E_{ab}$ be the standard matrix unit and put $X_{ab}=E_{ab}-E_{ba}$; these form a complex basis of $\mathfrak g$, and write $X_{ba}=-X_{ab}$ when indices are reversed. Let $\mathcal D$ be the finite group of diagonal matrices $D=\operatorname{diag}(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_8)$ with $\varepsilon_i\in\{\pm1\}$ and $\prod_i\varepsilon_i=1$. Each $D$ is a product of coordinate-plane rotations by $\pi$, so its adjoint action preserves every ideal $I\trianglelefteq\mathfrak g$: each rotation is an exponential, and $\exp(t\operatorname{ad}X)$ preserves any subspace stable under $\operatorname{ad}X$. Since $\operatorname{Ad}_D(X_{ab})=\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_bX_{ab}$, the characters $D\mapsto\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_b$ are distinct for distinct unordered pairs (their symmetric difference has size $2$ or $4$, so it is neither empty nor the full coordinate set). For $X=\sum_{a<b}c_{ab}X_{ab}\in I$, the average $|\mathcal D|^{-1}\sum_{D\in\mathcal D}(\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_b)\operatorname{Ad}_D(X)$ equals $c_{ab}X_{ab}$: the coefficient of $X_{cd}$ is multiplied by the average of the character $D\mapsto(\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_b)(\varepsilon_c\varepsilon_d)$, which is $1$ when the pairs agree and $0$ otherwise. For a nontrivial character the latter average is zero by pairing each $D$ with $D_0D$, where its value at $D_0$ is $-1$. If $I\ne0$, one such coefficient is nonzero, hence $X_{ab}\in I$. For $k\notin\{a,b\}$, brackets with $X_{bk}$ put each $X_{ak}$ in $I$, and brackets among these incident basis elements give every $X_{kl}$ with $k,l\ne a$. Thus $I=\mathfrak g$. Since $[X_{12},X_{23}]=X_{13}\ne0$, $\mathfrak g$ is nonabelian and simple. A solvable ideal is therefore $0$ or $\mathfrak g$; the latter is impossible because $[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g]$ is a nonzero ideal and hence equals $\mathfrak g$, so every term of the derived series is $\mathfrak g$. Thus $\mathfrak g$ is semisimple by [F7]. [F7, algebra]

1.3 Define $w_j^+=e_{2j-1}+ie_{2j}$ and $w_j^-=e_{2j-1}-ie_{2j}$. The commuting elements $H_j=-iR_j$ act on $w_j^\pm$ with eigenvalues $\pm1$. Extend the Euclidean inner product on $V$ complex-bilinearly to $V_\mathbb C$. The map $u\wedge v\mapsto(x\mapsto\langle u,x\rangle v-\langle v,x\rangle u)$ is an isomorphism $\Lambda^2V_\mathbb C\cong\mathfrak{so}(8,\mathbb C)$: the basis wedges map to a basis of skew matrices. The identity $[H,X_{u,v}]=X_{Hu,v}+X_{u,Hv}$ shows its nonzero simultaneous $\mathfrak h$-weights are $\pm\xi_i\pm\xi_j$ for $i<j$, with one-dimensional eigenspaces; its zero-weight space is $\mathfrak h$. If $Y=Y_0+\sum_\alpha Y_\alpha$ normalizes $\mathfrak h$, then $[H,Y]\in\mathfrak h$ for every $H\in\mathfrak h$, so $\alpha(H)Y_\alpha=0$ for every $H$ and each nonzero root $\alpha$; hence all $Y_\alpha=0$ and $Y\in\mathfrak h$. Thus $\mathfrak h$ is self-normalizing; it is abelian and therefore nilpotent, so it is Cartan by [F5]. The displayed roots form a reduced crystallographic root system: they span $\mathbb R^4$; all have squared length $2$, so each root line contains only that root and its negative; reflections in $\xi_i-\xi_j$ swap coordinates $i,j$, while reflections in $\xi_i+\xi_j$ send $(x_i,x_j)$ to $(-x_j,-x_i)$, so both preserve the root set; and $2(\beta,\alpha)/(\alpha,\alpha)=(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathbb Z$ for all roots $\alpha,\beta$. For the regular vector $(4,3,2,1)$, the positive roots are exactly $\xi_i-\xi_j$ and $\xi_i+\xi_j$ with $i<j$. The four $\alpha_i$ are linearly independent, and every positive difference is a nonnegative sum of adjacent differences; the positive sums decompose as $\xi_3+\xi_4=\alpha_4$, $\xi_2+\xi_4=\alpha_2+\alpha_4$, $\xi_2+\xi_3=\alpha_2+\alpha_3+\alpha_4$, $\xi_1+\xi_4=\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_4$, $\xi_1+\xi_3=\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3+\alpha_4$, and $\xi_1+\xi_2=\alpha_1+2\alpha_2+\alpha_3+\alpha_4$. Thus these four simple roots are a basis of $E=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\Phi$. For $H_p,H_q$, the Killing form is $B(H_p,H_q)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi}\alpha(H_p)\alpha(H_q)=12\delta_{pq}$: each root space is one-dimensional with weight $\alpha$, the zero-weight space $\mathfrak h$ contributes $0$, and summing over the roots gives $12$ when $p=q$ and $0$ otherwise. For $\alpha=\xi_i\pm\xi_j$, its Killing-dual vector is therefore $H_\alpha=(H_i\pm H_j)/12$, so $\alpha(H_\alpha)=1/6$ and its Lie-algebra coroot from [F8] is $h_\alpha=H_i\pm H_j$. In particular the simple coroots are $H_1-H_2,H_2-H_3,H_3-H_4,H_3+H_4$, and direct evaluation gives $(\lambda_a(h_{\alpha_b}))_{a,b=1}^4=I_4$, so the displayed $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_4$ are the fundamental weights. [A1, F8, F9, algebra]

2.1 The formulas $\epsilon_j=(C_{2j-1}-iC_{2j})/2$ and $\iota_j=(C_{2j-1}+iC_{2j})/2$ put all wedge and deletion operators in the image of this representation. The projections $P_j=\epsilon_j\iota_j$ select precisely the basis wedges containing $u_j$, so $\prod_{j\in I}P_j\prod_{j\notin I}(I-P_j)$ projects onto $\mathbb C u_I$. Composing this projection with deletions for the indices in $I$ and exterior multiplications for the indices in any $J$ gives, up to sign, the matrix unit sending $u_I$ to $u_J$ and all other basis vectors to zero. Thus the representation image is all of $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(\Delta)$; both algebras have dimension $256$ by [F3], so the representation is an isomorphism. The natural module for $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(\Delta)$ is irreducible: if $U\subseteq\Delta$ is nonzero, then for any $u\ne0$ in $U$ and any $z\in\Delta$ there is an endomorphism sending $u$ to $z$, so invariance forces $z\in U$. [F1, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Since $C_{2j-1}C_{2j}=i(I-2P_j)$ and these four even products commute, $\omega=i^4\prod_{j=1}^4(I-2P_j)$ acts on $u_I$ by $(-1)^{|I|}$. There are eight even and eight odd subsets of $\{1,2,3,4\}$, hence $\dim_\mathbb C\Delta^+=\dim_\mathbb C\Delta^-=8$. Each $C_a$ anticommutes with the product of the other seven Clifford generators in $\omega$, so $c(v)\omega=-\omega c(v)$ for every $v\in V$; thus $c(v)$ interchanges $\Delta^+$ and $\Delta^-$. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

2.3 Each $C_{2j-1}$ has real coefficients and each $C_{2j}$ has purely imaginary coefficients. The four factors of $A=C_2C_4C_6C_8$ anticommute and square to $I$, so $A^2=(-1)^6I=I$ and coefficient conjugation gives $\overline A=A$. Conjugation by $A$ fixes the four odd-indexed $C_a$ and negates the four even-indexed ones; coefficient conjugation $K$ has the same respective signs on the $C_a$. Therefore $J=AK$ is conjugate-linear, $J^2=A\overline A=I$, and $JC_a=C_aJ$ for each $a$. The volume operator is real and commutes with $A$, so $J$ preserves $\Delta^+$ and $\Delta^-$. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

2.4 For each $i<j$, $\exp(t e_ie_j/2)=\cos(t/2)+\sin(t/2)e_ie_j=e_i(\cos(t/2)e_i+\sin(t/2)e_j)$ is a product of two unit vectors in $G$. Since $[e_ie_j,e_i]=-2e_j$ and $[e_ie_j,e_j]=2e_i$, its vector action is the rotation in the $ij$-plane; its action on $\Delta$ is $\exp(tC_iC_j/2)$. Thus the infinitesimal rotation generators act on $\Delta$ by $C_iC_j/2$; their commutators, computed from step 1.1, are the standard $\mathfrak{so}(8)$ brackets. Every element of $SO(8)$ is a product of coordinate-plane rotations: successively rotating its first column to $e_1$ and repeating on the orthogonal complement reduces it to a one-dimensional determinant-$+1$ block, which is $1$. By [F2], any element of Spin(8) differs from a product of the corresponding lifted plane-rotation curves by an element of $\{1,-1\}$. Hence invariance under the plane-rotation Lie algebra and this scalar kernel is equivalent to invariance under Spin(8). [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The inner product making the ordered wedge basis orthonormal has $\epsilon_j^*=\iota_j$, so every $C_a$ is self-adjoint and satisfies $C_a^2=I$. More generally, for a unit vector $u=\sum_a u_ae_a$ with real coefficients, $c(u)=\sum_a u_aC_a$ is self-adjoint and $c(u)^2=Q(u)I=I$ by step 1.1, so every unit-vector generator is unitary and Spin(8) acts unitarily on $\Delta$. The volume eigenspaces are preserved by Spin(8), and $J$ commutes with every $c(u)$ because its coefficients are real and $J$ commutes with each $C_a$; hence $J$ commutes with the group action. For any $z\in\Delta^\pm$, the vectors $(z+Jz)/2$ and $(z-Jz)/(2i)$ are $J$-fixed and $z=(z+Jz)/2+i(z-Jz)/(2i)$; hence the fixed real subspace complexifies to $\Delta^\pm$ and has real dimension $8$. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.3, step 2.2, algebra]

3.2 Differentiating the spin action of the plane-rotation lifts in step 2.4, the complexified Cartan generator $H_j=-iR_j$ acts on $\Delta$ by $-iC_{2j-1}C_{2j}/2$. On $u_I$, this operator has eigenvalue $+1/2$ when $j\notin I$ and $-1/2$ when $j\in I$, so the corresponding weight has coordinate $+1/2$ or $-1/2$, respectively. Thus $\Delta^+$ has the eight half-integral weights with an even number of minus signs, $\Delta^-$ has the eight with an odd number of minus signs, and $V_\mathbb C$ has weights $\{\pm\xi_j\}$. Permuting the four coordinate two-planes and reversing an even number of their orientations gives elements of $SO(V)$ inducing the even signed permutations of the $\xi_j$; these act transitively on each half-spin parity set and on the vector weights. [F1, step 1.3, step 2.2, step 2.4]

4.1 A Spin-invariant complex subspace of any of $V_\mathbb C,\Delta^+,\Delta^-$ is invariant under the Lie algebra action by differentiation. The commuting Cartan generators act diagonally in the explicit weight bases of step 3.2; their spectral projections are polynomials in these generators, so the subspace is the sum of its simultaneous weight spaces and a nonzero subspace contains a weight line. The matrices realizing the even signed permutations from step 3.2 lie in $SO(V)$ and by [F2] have Spin(8) lifts. Conjugation by these matrices permutes and changes signs of the $R_j$, so the lifted group elements carry each Cartan weight space to the space with the corresponding signed-permuted weight. These lifts act transitively on the weight sets in step 3.2. Spin invariance therefore forces the subspace to contain every weight line, so it is the whole module; hence all three complex modules are irreducible. If a real invariant subspace $W$ of $8_s$ or $8_c$ is nonzero, then its complexification is all of $\Delta^+$ or $\Delta^-$. Since $\dim_{\mathbb C}W_{\mathbb C}=\dim_{\mathbb R}W$ and $\dim_{\mathbb C}\Delta^\pm=8$, this forces $\dim_{\mathbb R}W=8$; the full fixed real space has dimension $8$ by step 3.1, so $W$ is that full space. For a nonzero real invariant subspace $W\subseteq V$, the same argument gives $W_{\mathbb C}=V_{\mathbb C}$ and $\dim_{\mathbb R}W=\dim_{\mathbb C}V_{\mathbb C}=8=\dim_{\mathbb R}V$, hence $W=V$. [F2, step 3.1, step 3.2, algebra]

5.1 Relative to $\lambda_4$, each weight of $\Delta^+$ with two minus signs differs by the positive root $\xi_i+\xi_j$, and the all-minus weight differs by $(\xi_1+\xi_2)+(\xi_3+\xi_4)$; hence every weight is below $\lambda_4$ in the root order. In $\Delta^-$ the reference sign pattern $+++-$ has weight $\lambda_3$. Every other odd-minus pattern differs from it in an even number of signs: two flips among the first three give $\xi_i+\xi_j$, a flip at $i<4$ and at $4$ gives $\xi_i-\xi_4$, and four flips give $(\xi_1+\xi_2)+(\xi_3-\xi_4)$, all positive roots or sums of positive roots. Thus every weight is below $\lambda_3$. The vector weights are $\{\pm\xi_j\}$; each lies below $\lambda_1=\xi_1$, since the differences are positive roots $\xi_1-\xi_j$ or $\xi_1+\xi_j$ for $j>1$, or $2\xi_1=(\xi_1-\xi_2)+(\xi_1+\xi_2)$ for $-\xi_1$. For $M=V_\mathbb C,\Delta^+,\Delta^-$, take $\mu=\lambda_1,\lambda_4,\lambda_3$, respectively, and $0\ne v\in M_\mu$. For $x\in\mathfrak g_\alpha$, the relation $[H,x]=\alpha(H)x$ gives $xv\in M_{\mu+\alpha}$; if $xv$ were nonzero for a positive root $\alpha$, then $\mu+\alpha$ would be a weight strictly above the displayed maximal weight $\mu$, by the root-order definition in [F6]; hence every positive-root space annihilates its top weight line. By step 4.1 each module is irreducible, so those vectors generate the corresponding modules and their highest weights are $\lambda_1,\lambda_4,\lambda_3$ for $V_\mathbb C,\Delta^+,\Delta^-$, respectively. [A1, F5, F6, step 1.3, step 3.2, step 4.1]

6.1 The volume operator is the product of the oriented orthonormal Clifford generators; replacing the orientation reverses its sign and exchanges its $+1$ and $-1$ eigenspaces. Therefore the labels $8_s,8_c$ are exchanged when the chirality convention is reversed, while each fixed real space has dimension eight and is irreducible by steps 3.1 and 4.1. [step 2.2, step 3.1, step 4.1] ∎
