## Statement

Assume AC and the principal integral-grading hypotheses of the principal gauge-torsor construction. Put $R_{m}=O(g \otimes  C[t]/(t^{m+1}))$ and $R=$ increasing union $R_{m}$. Let U be an R-module which is an increasing union of finite $R_m$-modules $U_m$. Suppose each $U_{m}$ has an action $D_{x}$ of $g_{m}$ satisfying $D_{x}(a u)=(x a)u+a D_{x}(u)$ and $[D_{x},D_{y}]=D_{[x,y]}$, and the $n_m$-action integrates to a rational action of the nilpotent BCH group $N_{m}$ compatible with its action on $R_{m}$. Assume the finite-stage actions and module inclusions have the coefficient-mode transitions specified by the gauge-torsor construction. This class includes finite-jet coherent equivariant modules and their filtered unions; no freeness over R or over the constraint algebra is assumed.

Let I be the ideal generated by all Taylor coefficients of the moment constraints. The restricted polynomial classical BRST complex of U has cohomology only in cohomological degree zero, where it is $(U/IU)^{n[t]}$. The functor U to $(U/IU)^{n[t]}$ is exact on this module class. These statements also apply to submodules, quotients, and double associated graded modules which satisfy the stated finite-stage conditions.

## Proof

**Given:** F1, A1 and the Statement. Finite-stage modules are coherent modules, not assumed coordinate rings.

1.1 Fix a closed point $y$ of the constraint fibre at stage $m$. Put $A=\widehat{(R_m)_y}$, let $a_1,\ldots,a_s$ be the independent moment constraints, put $B=A/(a_1,\ldots,a_s)$, and let $M=\widehat{(U_m)_y}$. F1's formal transverse chart is an isomorphism $\varphi:A\to C:=B[\![u_1,\ldots,u_s]\!]$. Choose the transverse basis $q_i$ used in that chart and write $\delta_i$ for its infinitesimal action on functions. With F1's pullback convention, $\varphi(a)$ is the image in $B[\![u]\!]$ of $\exp(-\sum_i u_i\delta_i)a$; the minus sign compensates for the action on functions being pullback by the inverse adjoint action. Every $\delta_i$ extends to $A$, lowering maximal-ideal order by at most one. The Leibniz identity likewise extends $D_{q_i}$ to $M$: it sends the $k$th maximal-ideal filtration into the $(k-1)$st. Thus the following exponentials are legitimate $u$-adic formal series with continuous coefficients. [given, F1, A1, construct, algebra]

2.1 Choose a finite presentation $M=F/K$ with $F=A^r$ and $K$ finitely generated. For each $i$, lift $D_{q_i}$ on the images of the standard basis to a matrix $\Gamma_i\in\operatorname{Mat}_r(A)$ and define $\nabla_i(v)=\delta_i(v)+\Gamma_i v$ on $F$. This operator descends to $D_{q_i}$, so $\nabla_i(K)\subseteq K$. Put $E_A=\exp(-\sum_i u_i\delta_i)$ and $E_F=\exp(-\sum_i u_i\nabla_i)$ on $A[\![u]\!]$ and $F[\![u]\!]$. The iterated Leibniz rule gives $E_F(av)=E_A(a)E_F(v)$. Both exponentials have inverses obtained by changing the sign, and both preserve their relevant relation modules; in particular $E_F(K[\![u]\!])=K[\![u]\!]$. No commutativity of the chosen transverse operators is needed: each exponential uses the single operator $-\sum_i u_i\nabla_i$. [given, F1, step 1.1, choose, construct, algebra]

3.1 Let $P$ be the matrix whose columns are $E_F(e_j)$, so $E_F(v)=P E_A(v)$ coefficientwise and $P\equiv I\pmod{(u)}$. Hence $P$ is invertible over $A[\![u]\!]$. Apply the quotient $A[\![u]\!]\to B[\![u]\!]=C$ to the equality in step 2.1. If $K_\varphi\subseteq C^r$ is generated by the images under $\varphi$ of a finite set of generators of $K$, and $K_0\subseteq B^r$ is generated by their images modulo the constraints, the resulting equality is $P_B K_\varphi=K_0 C$, where $P_B$ is the image of $P$. Indeed $E_A$ transforms every coefficient of a relation by the scalar automorphism $E_A$, and the inverse transform gives the reverse inclusion; specialization then preserves this equality of generated submodules. Therefore multiplication by $P_B$ gives an isomorphism
$$M\otimes_{A,\varphi} C=C^r/K_\varphi\ \longrightarrow\ C^r/(K_0 C)=(M/(a_1,\ldots,a_s)M)[\![u_1,\ldots,u_s]\!].$$
Its inverse is multiplication by $P_B^{-1}$. For the last identity, lift each series coefficient in $M/(a)M$ to $B^r$. A series maps to zero exactly when each coefficient is in $K_0$; express those coefficients in its fixed finite generating set and collect the resulting coefficient series. A1 permits these choices. Thus the kernel is exactly $K_0 C$, without assuming flatness of $M$. This supplies the missing actual module transport, rather than inferring flatness from finite presentation. [F1, A1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 F1's same formal inverse replaces $u$ by the original constraints $a$, with the transverse coordinates fixed: their derivative with respect to $u$ is the invertible triangular matrix in that supplier. Applying this substitution coefficientwise to step 3.1 identifies $M$ with $(M/(a)M)[\![a_1,\ldots,a_s]\!]$, with the original constraint operators acting by the displayed variables. Successive multiplication by $a_i$ is therefore injective after quotienting by its predecessors, by coefficient comparison, even when $M/(a)M$ has torsion or nilpotent scalar action. The finite Koszul mapping-cone argument and faithful completed-local detection in F1 now apply to the finite module $U_m$, yielding zero higher Koszul homology and degree-zero quotient $U_m/I_mU_m$. Outside the constraint fibre a unit constraint contracts the complex. Thus no freeness of $U_m$ has been assumed. [F1, A1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 On the torsor $N_{m} \times  S_{m}$, the constrained module $L=U_{m}/I_{m} U_{m}$ is an equivariant module over H tensor B, where $H=O(N_{m}), B=O(S_{m})$. Here is the explicit descent, including its inverse. Write its rational coaction as $\lambda (l)=\sum  l_{-1} \otimes  l_{0}$, with compatibility $\lambda(al)=\Delta(a)\lambda(l)$ for a in H and with B coinvariant. If $S_{H}$ is the Hopf antipode, put $\pi (l)=\sum  S_{H}(l_{-1}) l_{0}$. The Hopf identities and coassociativity imply $\lambda (\pi (l))=1 \otimes  \pi (l)$. The multiplication map $H \otimes  L^{N}$ to L has inverse l maps to sum $l_{-1} \otimes  \pi (l_{0})$; composing in either order uses sum $a_{1}S_{H}(a_{2})=\epsilon (a)1$ and gives the identity. All sums are finite because the coaction is rational. This proves actual equivariant-module descent, without a statement about closed points or a choice of local trivializations. [F1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 The finite classical BRST complex is the same constraint-Koszul/Chevalley bicomplex as in F1, now with coefficients $U_{m}$. Its finite filtration first computes the Koszul cohomology in step 4.1. Its remaining Chevalley complex, by step 5.1, is $L^N$ tensor the algebraic de Rham complex of the affine space $N_{m}$. F1's Euler homotopy tensor the identity of $L^{N}$ contracts it to $L^{N}$. Thus its only total cohomology is $(U_{m}/I_{m}U_{m})^{n_{m}}$. This is valid without any dimension or smoothness hypothesis on the coefficient module. [F1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 For a short exact sequence of finite-stage equivariant modules, the Koszul complexes form a short exact sequence and their higher homology is zero. The long exact sequence therefore makes constrained modules exact, including injectivity at the left. Their constrained modules have the Hopf descent of step 5.1, and the Chevalley higher cohomology is zero by step 6.1. The cohomology long exact sequence now makes degree-zero invariants exact, including surjectivity at the right. For possibly different finite stages enlarge the common stage; every finite collection of generators, relations and witnesses is contained in one such stage. [step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 Pass to the stated restricted unions. A polynomial cochain and the finite equality saying it is a cycle occur at one finite stage. The finite-stage primitives in nonzero degree remain polynomial primitives in the union. Degree-zero cohomology is the filtered colimit; its transition maps need not be injective. Exactness of that colimit follows directly on representatives: a kernel element or a relation between two representatives is witnessed at some common finite stage, and the exact finite-stage construction then supplies the required preimage or primitive. Invariants here are the degree-zero cohomology of the restricted complex, equivalently the vectors killed by all n[t] modes. This proves concentration and exactness on the entire stated module class. [step 7.1, construct, algebra] ∎

## Scope of the finite-stage condition

For an old-loop-graded affine module with finite-dimensional graded pieces,
bounded below, every finite set of homogeneous vectors has a finite-dimensional
g[t]-stable span: positive modes lower loop grade and vanish beyond a finite
order, and g preserves each finite graded piece. In a compatible associated
graded module the same assertion holds. If E is such a finite stable span,
$R_{m} E$ is a finite module, is stable under $g_{m}$ for sufficiently large m, and
these modules exhaust the associated graded module. The positive-root modes
are nilpotent on each finite vector orbit, so their BCH exponential is a
finite rational $N_{m}$ action. This verifies the finite-stage condition for the
compatible single and double associated graded modules used in the adjoining
quantum construction. It does not claim that arbitrary unrestricted formal
cochains have the same cohomology.
