## Statement

Let $U$ be a positive-energy unitary Möbius representation and write
$$H_{\mathrm{fin}}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathrm{alg}}\{\ker(L_0-\lambda I):\lambda\in[0,\infty)\}$$
for the algebraic span of its rotation eigenspaces. On this domain, suppose the generators satisfy
$$[L_0,L_{\pm1}]=\mp L_{\pm1},\qquad [L_1,L_{-1}]=2L_0.$$
The adjoint convention on $H_{\mathrm{fin}}$ is the form identity $\langle L_n\xi,\eta\rangle=\langle\xi,L_{-n}\eta\rangle$ for $n=-1,0,1$ and $\xi,\eta\in H_{\mathrm{fin}}$.
Let $a\in H_{\mathrm{fin}}$ be a quasiprimary vector of integral weight $d\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$, meaning $L_0a=da$ and $L_1a=0$. The cyclic lowest-weight subspace generated by $a$ is $H_a=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{L_{-1}^na:n\ge0\}$; all vectors and operators in the remainder of the Statement are restricted to this subspace. Let $K$ be the self-adjoint generator normalized by
$$K=i\pi(L_1-L_{-1})$$
on the finite-energy domain. Thus the integrated real group $e^{itK}$ is already part of the supplied unitary Möbius representation; no assertion about the modular operator of any algebra is made here.

Use the Fourier convention
$$\widehat f_m=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}f(e^{it})e^{-imt}\,dt,\qquad a(f)=\sum_{n\ge0}\widehat f_{-n-d}\frac{L_{-1}^na}{n!}.$$
For arbitrary complex-valued $f\in C_c^\infty(\{e^{it}:0<t<\pi\})$, extended by zero to the circle, the conclusion is
$$a(f)\in\operatorname{Dom}(e^{K/2}),\qquad e^{K/2}a(f)=(-1)^d a(f\circ j),\qquad j(z)=z^{-1}.$$
Here $f\circ j$ is supported in the lower semicircle. In particular this is a linear identity, not an antilinear reflection identity; no complex conjugation of $f$ is inserted.

## Proof

**Given:** [A1] and exactly the data and hypotheses in the Statement. All Hilbert inner products are linear in the first argument.

1.1 The data are: Exactly the unitary lowest-weight representation, self-adjoint generator, Fourier convention and compact upper-semicircle support just specified. The quasi-primary supplier [F1], proof steps 1.1, 2.1, and 3.1, proves that every finite-energy vector of this genuine positive-energy Möbius representation is Möbius smooth, without any finite-multiplicity hypothesis. In particular $a$ and all its $L_{-1}$ descendants are in the common smooth domain, so the ladder and adjoint calculations beginning in 2.1 have their required domains. [given, F1, algebra]

2.1 The zero-vector case is immediate. If $d=0$, then $\|L_{-1}a\|^2=\langle a,L_1L_{-1}a\rangle=2d\|a\|^2=0$. Hence $L_{-1}a=L_1a=0$, $Ka=0$ and $a(f)=\widehat f_0a$. Since $\widehat{f\circ j}_0=\widehat f_0$, the asserted identity follows. Suppose henceforth $d>0$ and put $m=2d$. Repeated use of the Lie relations gives the mutually orthogonal vectors $v_n=L_{-1}^na/n!$ with $\|v_n\|^2=\|a\|^2\frac{(m)_n}{n!},\qquad (m)_n=m(m+1)\cdots(m+n-1).$ Indeed $L_1L_{-1}^na=n(2d+n-1)L_{-1}^{n-1}a$, and induction gives this norm formula. Fourier coefficients of a smooth circle function decay faster than any power, whereas $(m)_n/n!$ grows polynomially of degree $m-1$, so both $a(f)$ and $a(f\circ j)$ converge in Hilbert norm. [given, F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For $|z|<1$ define the coherent vector $v(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}z^nv_n$. Its series converges normally in Hilbert norm on compact subdisks and the binomial series gives $\langle v(z),v(w)\rangle=\|a\|^2(1-z\bar w)^{-m}.$ The ladder relations also give, as Hilbert-vector identities, $L_{-1}v(z)=\partial_zv(z),\qquad L_1v(z)=(z^2\partial_z+2dz)v(z).$ These are genuine domain identities: truncating the power series, the images under each ladder operator converge in Hilbert norm, since the additional ladder coefficients are polynomial in $n$ and $|z|^n$ decays exponentially. Closedness of the representation generators therefore puts $v(z)$ in their domains; the same estimates apply uniformly on every compact subdisk. In particular $v(z)\in\operatorname{Dom}K$ with the displayed generator action. [given, F1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 For $0<s<1/2$, set $c_s=\cos(\pi s),\quad h_s=\sin(\pi s),\quad A_s(z)=c_s-ih_sz,\quad N_s(z)=c_sz-ih_s,\quad g_s(z)=\frac{N_s(z)}{A_s(z)}.$ For $z=e^{it}$ in the upper semicircle, $|A_s(z)|^2=1+\sin(2\pi s)\sin t,\qquad |N_s(z)|^2=1-\sin(2\pi s)\sin t.$ Hence $|g_s(z)|<1$ and $A_s(z)\ne0$. Define a Hilbert-valued Bochner integral $v_s=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^\pi f(e^{it})e^{idt}A_s(e^{it})^{-m}v(g_s(e^{it}))\,dt.$ For $s$ in a compact subinterval of $(0,1/2)$ and $t$ in the compact support of $f$, the transformed points belong to a fixed compact subdisk. Step 3.1 therefore justifies the integral, its norm derivatives in $s$, and integration in the graph norm of $K$. [given, F1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Differentiate the explicit functions: $\partial_sg_s=i\pi(g_s^2-1),\qquad \partial_s(A_s^{-m})=2di\pi g_s A_s^{-m}.$ Using step 3.1 in the integrand yields the exact local differential equation $\frac{d}{ds}v_s=Kv_s\qquad(0<s<1/2).$ This equation has not yet been identified with an unbounded exponential at the endpoints; that identification will follow from a uniform norm bound and spectral cutoffs below. [given, F1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 We now prove the required uniform Hardy bound directly. Put $q=\sin(2\pi s)>0$ and $r=\cos(2\pi s)$, so $q^2+r^2=1$. The coherent kernel in step 3.1 gives $\|v_s\|^2=\frac{\|a\|^2}{(2\pi)^2}\iint f(e^{it})\overline{f(e^{iu})}\frac{e^{id(t-u)}}{\bigl(A_s(e^{it})\overline{A_s(e^{iu})}-N_s(e^{it})\overline{N_s(e^{iu})}\bigr)^m}\,dt\,du.$ Direct multiplication, with $z=e^{it},w=e^{iu}$, gives $A_s(z)\overline{A_s(w)}-N_s(z)\overline{N_s(w)}=2e^{i(t-u)/2}\left(q\sin\frac{t+u}{2}-ir\sin\frac{t-u}{2}\right).$ Since $m=2d$, its phase cancels the numerator phase exactly. In the real coordinates $x=\tan(t/2)>0$, $y=\tan(u/2)>0$, put $F(x)=f(e^{2i\arctan x})(1+x^2)^{d-1}.$ Compact upper-semicircle support means $F\in C_c^\infty(0,\infty)$. The identities for the sine of the sum/difference of arctangents and $dt=2\,dx/(1+x^2)$ then give $\|v_s\|^2=\frac{\|a\|^2}{2^m\pi^2}\iint\frac{F(x)\overline{F(y)}}{\bigl(q(x+y)-ir(x-y)\bigr)^m}\,dx\,dy.$ All integrals here are ordinary convergent integrals for fixed $0<s<1/2$. [given, F1, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 The elementary Laplace identity $z^{-m}=\Gamma(m)^{-1}\int_0^\infty u^{m-1}e^{-uz}\,du$, valid for $\operatorname{Re}z>0$, gives $\|v_s\|^2=\frac{\|a\|^2}{2^m\pi^2\Gamma(m)}\int_0^\infty u^{m-1}\left|\int_0^\infty F(x)e^{-u(q-ir)x}\,dx\right|^2du.$ For fixed $q>0$, Fubini is justified absolutely by the positive lower bound on $x+y$ on the compact support; the Laplace identity itself follows by $m-1$ integrations by parts from the exponential integral and holomorphy in the right half-plane. Crucially $|q-ir|=1$. Integrating by parts $N$ times in the inner integral, with no boundary terms because $F$ is compactly supported inside $(0,\infty)$, proves $\left|\int F(x)e^{-u(q-ir)x}\,dx\right|\le\|F\|_1\quad(0<u\le1),\qquad\left|\int F(x)e^{-u(q-ir)x}\,dx\right|\le u^{-N}\|F^{(N)}\|_1\quad(u\ge1).$ The estimate $|e^{-uqx}|\le1$ uses $x>0$. Choosing $N>d$ proves the explicit bound $\sup_{0<s<1/2}\|v_s\|^2\le\frac{\|a\|^2}{2^m\pi^2\Gamma(m)}\left(\frac{\|F\|_1^2}{m}+\frac{\|F^{(N)}\|_1^2}{2N-m}\right)<\infty.$ This is uniform at BOTH endpoints and does not appeal to a boundary distribution kernel theorem. [given, F1, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 For each fixed $n$, the coefficient of $v_n$ in $v_s$ is $b_n(s)=\frac1{2\pi}\int f(e^{it})e^{idt}A_s(e^{it})^{-m}g_s(e^{it})^n\,dt.$ Since $|A_s(e^{it})|\ge1$ on the support, these finite coefficients have the endpoint limits obtained by direct bounded convergence. At $s=0$, $A_0=1$ and $g_0(z)=z$, so $b_n(0)=\widehat f_{-n-d}$. At $s=1/2$, $A_{1/2}(z)=-iz$ and $g_{1/2}(z)=z^{-1}$, so $b_n(1/2)=(-i)^{-2d}\frac1{2\pi}\int f(e^{it})e^{-i(n+d)t}\,dt=(-1)^d\widehat{f\circ j}_{-n-d}.$ The spans of the $v_n$ are dense in the cyclic subrepresentation. Together with the uniform norm bound and the Hilbert convergence established in step 2.1, these coefficient limits imply $v_s\rightharpoonup a(f)\quad(s\downarrow0),\qquad v_s\rightharpoonup(-1)^da(f\circ j)\quad(s\uparrow1/2).$ This also fixes the phase without an independent choice of square-root or a suppressed antilinear reflection: the multiplier is precisely $(-iz)^{-2d}$ and the external density factor is $z^d$. [given, F1, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 Let $E_R=1_{[-R,R]}(K)$. By step 5.1, $E_Rv_s$ solves the bounded-generator equation $(E_Rv_s)'=KE_Rv_s$. Therefore $e^{-sK}E_Rv_s$ is constant on $(0,1/2)$, where the exponent is restricted to $E_RH$ and is bounded. Taking its weak initial limit using step 8.1 gives $E_Rv_s=e^{sK}E_Ra(f).$ Letting $R\to\infty$, the uniform finite bound on $\|v_s\|$ proves $a(f)\in\operatorname{Dom}(e^{sK})$ and $v_s=e^{sK}a(f)$ for every $0<s<1/2$, by the spectral theorem. Finally, if $\mu$ is the $K$-spectral measure of $a(f)$, Fatou's lemma gives $\int_{\mathbb R}e^\lambda\,d\mu(\lambda)\le\liminf_{s\uparrow1/2}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{2s\lambda}\,d\mu(\lambda)\le\sup_{0<s<1/2}\|v_s\|^2<\infty.$ Hence $a(f)\in\operatorname{Dom}(e^{K/2})$. Spectral dominated convergence also gives $e^{sK}a(f)\to e^{K/2}a(f)$ strongly: on positive spectrum use the integrable majorant $e^\lambda$, and on negative spectrum use one. Comparing with the weak final limit in step 8.1 proves $e^{K/2}a(f)=(-1)^da(f\circ j).$ This is the full asserted domain and vector identity for every allowed complex test function. [given, F1, step 8.1, algebra] ∎
