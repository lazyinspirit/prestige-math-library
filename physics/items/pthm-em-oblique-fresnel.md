## Statement

Assume the following plane-interface setting with the same stationary interface identities as [[pthm-em-phase-matching]]: two homogeneous isotropic lossless scalar media at rest (medium 1 in $\{x_3<0\}$, constants $\varepsilon_1,\mu_1>0$; medium 2 in $\{x_3>0\}$, constants $\varepsilon_2,\mu_2>0$; both $\sigma=0$), the plane interface $S=\{x_3=0\}$ with normal $n=e_3$, and the plane-wave structure of [[pthm-em-medium-plane-wave-fields]]. Let $\omega>0$, let $\kappa\in\mathbb R^{2}$ (identified with the tangential plane $\{x_3=0\}$) satisfy $0<|\kappa|<K_1:=\omega(\varepsilon_1\mu_1)^{1/2}$, put $k_{13}:=\sqrt{K_1^{2}-|\kappa|^{2}}>0$ and define wavevectors $$k_i:=\kappa+k_{13}\,n,\qquad k_r:=\kappa-k_{13}\,n,\qquad k_t:=\kappa+k_{t3}\,n,$$ where $k_{t3}\in\mathbb C$ satisfies $k_{t3}^{2}=K_2^{2}-|\kappa|^{2}$ with $K_2:=\omega(\varepsilon_2\mu_2)^{1/2}$ and is selected by non-growth in the $x_3>0$ half-space: $$k_{t3}=\sqrt{K_2^{2}-|\kappa|^{2}}>0\ \ (|\kappa|<K_2),\qquad k_{t3}=0\ \ (|\kappa|=K_2),\qquad k_{t3}=i\beta,\ \beta:=\sqrt{|\kappa|^{2}-K_2^{2}}>0\ \ (|\kappa|>K_2).$$ [This transmitted phase data is the interface-consistent, bounded choice: the propagating and evanescent branches of [[pthm-em-medium-plane-wave-fields]](d),(e); the common $(\kappa,\omega)$ is declared here for each actual wave, also when a reflected amplitude is zero or the critical transmitted root is zero; the nonzero phase-matching theorem explains this matching where its hypotheses apply, and an absent reflected field has no determined phase labels.] Fix the orthonormal tangential frame $\hat\kappa:=\kappa/|\kappa|$ and $\hat y:=n\times\hat\kappa$, and consider the two polarisations: (s) [transverse electric] $e_i=E_i\hat y$, $e_r=E_r\hat y$, $e_t=E_t\hat y$ with $E_i,E_r,E_t\in\mathbb C$, $E_i\ne0$, and $h_j:=k_j\times e_j/(\mu_j\omega)$; (p) [transverse magnetic] $h_i=H_i\hat y$, $h_r=H_r\hat y$, $h_t=H_t\hat y$ with $H_i,H_r,H_t\in\mathbb C$, $H_i\ne0$, and electric amplitudes $e_j:=-(k_j\times\hat y)H_j/(\omega\varepsilon_j)$ (the same computation shows that in both cases the pairs satisfy the amplitude conditions of [[pthm-em-medium-plane-wave-fields]](b) in their media). For each polarisation define the amplitudes along the fixed tangential directions $E^{(s)}_j:=e_j\cdot\hat y$ and, in the case (p), $E^{(p)}_j:=e_j\cdot\hat x$ with $\hat x:=\hat\kappa$; assume the four stationary interface identities of [[pthm-em-phase-matching]] hold on $\mathbb R^{2}\times I$ for a nonempty open interval $I$. Then:

**(a) [Amplitude system]** for $\chi=s$ in every branch, and for $\chi=p$ when $k_{t3}\ne0$, the tangential interface identities reduce to $$E^{\chi}_i+E^{\chi}_r=E^{\chi}_t,\qquad Y^{\chi}_1\bigl(E^{\chi}_i-E^{\chi}_r\bigr)=Y^{\chi}_2E^{\chi}_t,$$ with the admittances $$Y^s_1=\frac{k_{13}}{\mu_1\omega},\qquad Y^p_1=\frac{\varepsilon_1\omega}{k_{13}},\qquad Y^s_2=\frac{k_{t3}}{\mu_2\omega},\qquad Y^p_2=\frac{\varepsilon_2\omega}{k_{t3}}\quad(k_{t3}\ne0).$$ Write $k_{j3}:=k_j\cdot n$ for the signed components, so $k_{i3}=k_{13}$ and $k_{r3}=-k_{13}$. Here $Y_1^\chi>0$ uses the positive incident normal magnitude $k_{13}$, whereas the signed reflected component is $k_r\cdot n=-k_{13}$; $Y_1^\chi$ is not defined from that negative component. No $Y_2^p$ is defined at $k_{t3}=0$. Hence, whenever $Y^{\chi}_1+Y^{\chi}_2\ne0$, $$r_\chi:=\frac{E^{\chi}_r}{E^{\chi}_i}=\frac{Y^{\chi}_1-Y^{\chi}_2}{Y^{\chi}_1+Y^{\chi}_2},\qquad t_\chi:=\frac{E^{\chi}_t}{E^{\chi}_i}=\frac{2Y^{\chi}_1}{Y^{\chi}_1+Y^{\chi}_2}$$ ([[lem-em-admittance-interface-solve]](a)); the reflected and transmitted amplitude symbols are defined by these fixed-direction conventions, and sign conventions for full vector amplitudes may reverse a reflected amplitude relative to this convention.

**(b) [Propagating branch and Snell's law]** if $|\kappa|<K_2$ then all three wavevectors are real, $Y_1^s,Y_1^p,Y_2^s,Y_2^p>0$ are real, and with $\theta_j\in(0,\pi/2)$ defined by $\sin\theta_j=|\kappa|/|k_j|$ one has $\theta_r=\theta_i$ and $n_1\sin\theta_i=n_2\sin\theta_t$, $n_j=c(\varepsilon_j\mu_j)^{1/2}$ ([[pthm-em-phase-matching]](c), [[def-em-medium-wave-parameters]]); the period-averaged normal fluxes are $Y^{\chi}_1|E^{\chi}_i|^{2}/2$, $-Y^{\chi}_1|E^{\chi}_r|^{2}/2$ and $Y^{\chi}_2|E^{\chi}_t|^{2}/2$, so $$R_\chi:=|r_\chi|^{2},\qquad T_\chi:=\frac{Y^{\chi}_2}{Y^{\chi}_1}|t_\chi|^{2},\qquad R_\chi+T_\chi=1 .$$

**(c) [Brewster condition]** In the finite-admittance branches, $r_\chi=0$ if and only if $Y^{\chi}_1=Y^{\chi}_2$; in particular, if $\mu_1=\mu_2$ and $|\kappa|<K_2$, for unequal indices $n_1\ne n_2$, $r_p=0$ holds at exactly one admissible incidence angle $\theta_B\in(0,\pi/2)$, characterised by $\tan\theta_B=n_2/n_1$, and $r_s\ne0$ at that angle. For equal indices $n_1=n_2$ (and equal permeabilities), the media are identical, $Y_1^\chi=Y_2^\chi$ and $r_s=r_p=0$, $t_s=t_p=1$ for every allowed incidence angle; there is no unique distinguished Brewster angle.

**(d) [Total internal reflection]** if $|\kappa|>K_2$ (which requires $n_1>n_2$, $\theta_i:=\arcsin(|\kappa|/K_1)\in(0,\pi/2)$, $|\kappa|=K_1\sin\theta_i$ and $\theta_i>\theta_c:=\arcsin(n_2/n_1)$), then $Y^{s}_2=i\beta/(\mu_2\omega)$ and $Y^{p}_2=-i\varepsilon_2\omega/\beta$ are purely imaginary, $$|r_s|=|r_p|=1,\qquad t_s,t_p\ne0,\qquad |t_\chi|^{2}=\frac{4(Y^{\chi}_1)^{2}}{(Y^{\chi}_1)^{2}+|Y^{\chi}_2|^{2}}>0,$$ the transmitted field decays as $e^{-\beta x_3}$ and its period-averaged normal flux vanishes identically while its tangential averaged flux does not vanish ([[pthm-em-medium-plane-wave-fields]](e)); the reflected wave carries the whole incident normal averaged flux.

**(e) [Critical angle]** if $|\kappa|=K_2$ then $k_{t3}=0$: the transmitted wave travels along the interface, its tangential electric amplitude vanishes in the (p) case and $Y^{s}_2=0$ in the (s) case, and $r_s=1$, $t_s=2$, $r_p=-1$, $t_p=0$ with vanishing transmitted normal averaged flux. The critical TM branch is solved from the nonsingular equations $E_i^p+E_r^p=k_{t3}H_t/(\omega\varepsilon_2)=0$ and $H_i+H_r=H_t$, not by dividing by $k_{t3}$; they give $H_r=H_i$ and $H_t=2H_i$. The transmitted TM electric vector can have a nonzero normal component even though its tangential amplitude is zero.

**(f) [Scope]** the statements are conditional on the interface identities and on the given wave data; no existence or uniqueness of interface-consistent data is claimed. Excluded: anisotropic media, complex $\varepsilon$ or $\mu$ and absorption in either medium (only the lossless dielectric case with the evanescent transmitted branch is covered), dispersive media, moving media, the ideal conductor, nonlinear response, and all claims about signal speeds, group velocities, information transfer or empirical data. The statements for $Y^{\chi}_1+Y^{\chi}_2=0$ (which cannot occur in the stated branches, where $\operatorname{Re}Y^{\chi}_1>0$ and $\operatorname{Re}Y^{\chi}_2\ge0$) are excluded; the notation 'angle of refraction' is used only where $k_t$ is real.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The two-media lossless setting of the Statement, its common $(\kappa,\omega)$ and wavevectors $k_i,k_r,k_t$, the two polarisation data families, and the four stationary interface identities [A1] on $\mathbb R^2\times I$.

[A1] The four stationary interface identities of [[pthm-em-phase-matching]] hold for all $(y,t)\in\mathbb R^2\times I$.

[F1] Plane-wave structure ([[pthm-em-medium-plane-wave-fields]]): the amplitude conditions $k_j\cdot e_j=0$, $k_j\times e_j=\omega\mu_jh_j$, $k_j\cdot h_j=0$, $k_j\cdot k_j=\varepsilon_j\mu_j\omega^2$ and the solution form $h_j=k_j\times e_j/(\mu_j\omega)$; the lossless pointwise identities $B=\hat k\times E/v$ and period-averaged flux $\langle S\rangle=|e|^2k/(2\mu\omega)$ for real $k$; and, in the evanescent branch, the factorization with decay $e^{-\beta n'\cdot x}$, the vanishing normal averaged flux and the positive tangential averaged fluxes for the two transverse polarisations.

[F2] The angle laws ([[pthm-em-phase-matching]](c)): for real wavevectors at a lossless interface, $\theta_r=\theta_i$ and $n_1\sin\theta_i=n_2\sin\theta_t$ with the same angle conventions, together with the parameters $v_j$ and $n_j=c/v_j$ of [[def-em-medium-wave-parameters]].

[F3] Admittance algebra ([[lem-em-admittance-interface-solve]]): (a) the solution formulas for the two-equation system; (b) for real $Y_1>0$ the identity $|r|^2+(\operatorname{Re}Y_2/Y_1)|t|^2=1$ with $|t|^2=4Y_1^2/|Y_1+Y_2|^2>0$; (c) for real $Y_1>0$ and purely imaginary $Y_2$ the identities $|r|=1$ and $|t|^2=4Y_1^2/(Y_1^2+|Y_2|^2)>0$; (d) the real-positive specialisation agreeing with [[lem-em-interface-wave-linear-system]].

[F4] Period averages ([[lem-em-period-average]]) and the stored-energy/flux definitions $u=\tfrac12(E\cdot D+B\cdot H)$, $S=E\times H$ of [[pthm-em-restricted-material-energy]].

[F5] Geometry of the fixed frame: $\hat x=\hat\kappa=\kappa/|\kappa|$, $\hat y=n\times\hat\kappa$, with the right-handed orthonormal triad relations $\hat x\times\hat y=n$, $\hat y\times n=\hat x$, $n\times\hat x=\hat y$ and the reversed signs; the cross product is bilinear with $\langle u\times v,w\rangle=\det[u\ v\ w]$, and $u\times v$ is orthogonal to $u$ and $v$ ([[def-cross-product-in-r3]], [[def-euclidean-inner-product]], [[lem-cross-product-is-bilinear-alternating-and-orthogonal]]).

[F6] Medium parameters ([[def-em-medium-wave-parameters]]): $K_j=\omega(\varepsilon_j\mu_j)^{1/2}$, $v_j=(\varepsilon_j\mu_j)^{-1/2}$, $n_j=c(\varepsilon_j\mu_j)^{1/2}$. The wavevector definitions and selected roots in this item's Statement give $k_{13}^2=K_1^2-|\kappa|^2$ and $k_{t3}^2=K_2^2-|\kappa|^2$ with its declared branch selection.

[F7] Complex arithmetic, conjugation, modulus ([[def-complex-numbers-and-arithmetic]], [[thm-complex-numbers-form-a-field]], [[def-complex-conjugate-real-imaginary-part-and-modulus]], [[lem-complex-conjugation-and-modulus-laws]]) with nonnegative square roots ([[thm-of-square-roots]]) and the existence of complex square roots ([[thm-every-complex-number-has-a-square-root]]).

[F8] Trigonometry: $\sin(\arcsin y)=y$ with sine strictly increasing on $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ ([[def-principal-inverse-sine-and-cosine]]); $\tan$ restricts to a strictly increasing bijection $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)\to\mathbb R$ with the principal inverse ([[def-principal-inverse-tangent]]); $\tan x=\sin x/\cos x$ ([[def-tangent-cotangent-secant-cosecant]]); $\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1$ with $1+\tan^2x=\sec^2x$ ([[thm-pythagorean-and-parity-identities-for-all-six-trigonometric-functions]]); sine is strictly increasing on $[0,\pi/2]$ and cosine strictly decreasing on $[0,\pi]$ with $\sin 0=0$, $\cos 0=1$ and $\cos(\pi/2)=0$ ([[thm-sine-cosine-signs-monotonicity-and-ranges]], [[thm-sine-cosine-zero-sets-and-fundamental-period]]).

[F9] The laboratory setting, fields and units ([[def-em-lab-geometry]], [[def-em-fields]], [[def-em-si-conventions]]) and the declared lossless branches ([[post-em-material-closure]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; verify the amplitude conditions for the two polarisation families, reduce the tangential interface identities to scalar admittance systems in the fixed frame, and read off the propagating, Brewster, evanescent and critical cases from the admittance algebra and the medium plane-wave structure.

1.1 [Both polarisation families satisfy the amplitude conditions] For (s), $k_j\cdot e_j=E_j\,k_j\cdot\hat y=0$ because $k_j=\kappa+k_{j3}n$ and $\hat y\perp\kappa,n$; and $k_j\times e_j=E_jk_j\times\hat y=E_j(\kappa\times\hat y+k_{j3}n\times\hat y)=E_j(|\kappa|n-k_{j3}\hat x)$ by [F5], which equals $\omega\mu_jh_j$ with $h_j=k_j\times e_j/(\mu_j\omega)$ by definition. For (p), $e_j=-(k_j\times\hat y)H_j/(\omega\varepsilon_j)$ and $k_j\cdot e_j=0$ because $k_j\cdot(k_j\times\hat y)=0$ by the orthogonality in [F5]; further $k_j\times e_j=-(k_j\times(k_j\times\hat y))H_j/(\omega\varepsilon_j)=\bigl[(k_j\cdot k_j)\hat y-k_j(k_j\cdot\hat y)\bigr]H_j/(\omega\varepsilon_j)=(\varepsilon_j\mu_j\omega^2)\hat yH_j/(\omega\varepsilon_j)=\omega\mu_jH_j\hat y=\omega\mu_jh_j$, using $k_j\cdot\hat y=0$ and $k_j\cdot k_j=\varepsilon_j\mu_j\omega^2$ of [F1]. In (s), $k_j\cdot h_j=0$ by cross-product orthogonality, and $k_j\times h_j=-\omega\varepsilon_j e_j$ follows by expanding $k_j\times(k_j\times e_j)=k_j(k_j\cdot e_j)-(k_j\cdot k_j)e_j$ and using dispersion. In (p), $k_j\cdot h_j=0$ because $\hat y$ is perpendicular to $k_j$, and $k_j\times h_j=-\omega\varepsilon_j e_j$ is the definition of $e_j$. Thus all four amplitude conditions hold in both families, including zero reflected or transmitted amplitudes. [given, F1, F5, F7, algebra]

2.1 [The tangential interface identities in the fixed frame] By the orthogonal decomposition of [F5], the tangential parts of the electric amplitudes are: for (s), $e_j^\tau=e_j=E_j\hat y$; for (p), $e_j^\tau=(k_{j3}H_j/(\omega\varepsilon_j))\hat x$ because $e_j=(k_{j3}\hat x-|\kappa|n)H_j/(\omega\varepsilon_j)$ from $k_j\times\hat y=|\kappa|n-k_{j3}\hat x$ by [F5]. The magnetic tangential traces are obtained as follows: $h_j=H_j\hat y$ in (p), and in (s) $h_j=E_j(\kappa\times\hat y+k_{j3}n\times\hat y)/(\mu_j\omega)=E_j(|\kappa|n-k_{j3}\hat x)/(\mu_j\omega)$ with tangential part $-k_{j3}E_j\hat x/(\mu_j\omega)$ and, for the reflected wave with $k_{r3}=-k_{13}$, tangential part $+k_{13}E_r\hat x/(\mu_1\omega)$. Since the interface identities hold for all $(y,t)$ with the common $(\kappa,\omega)$, each has the form $\operatorname{Re}\bigl(W e^{i(\kappa\cdot y-\omega t)}\bigr)=0$ for a constant tangential vector $W$. Since $n=e_3$, $n\times(w_1,w_2,0)=(-w_2,w_1,0)$, so the cross-product trace identities are equivalent to these real-part identities. Fixing any $y$ and allowing $t$ to vary in $I$, the time-line proof of the amplitude-vanishing clause (a) of [F1] gives $W=0$. Concretely: the tangential electric identity gives $E_i+E_r=E_t$ in (s) and $E^{(p)}_i+E^{(p)}_r=E^{(p)}_t$ in (p); the tangential magnetic identity gives $k_{t3}E_t/(\mu_2\omega)=k_{13}(E_i-E_r)/(\mu_1\omega)$ in (s) and $H_i+H_r=H_t$ in (p), where $E^{(p)}_j=k_{j3}H_j/(\omega\varepsilon_j)$ by definition, and when $k_{t3}\ne0$, converting $H_j=Y^p_jE^{(p)}_j$ for $j=i,t$ and $H_r=-Y^p_1E^{(p)}_r$ (the reflected sign entering through $k_{r3}=-k_{13}$) turns the last identity into $Y^p_1(E^{(p)}_i-E^{(p)}_r)=Y^p_2E^{(p)}_t$. Writing $Y^s_1=k_{13}/(\mu_1\omega)$, $Y^s_2=k_{t3}/(\mu_2\omega)$, $Y^p_1=\varepsilon_1\omega/k_{13}$ and $Y^p_2=\varepsilon_2\omega/k_{t3}$ when $k_{t3}\ne0$ gives the two systems of (a). [given, A1, step 1.1, F5, F7, algebra]

3.1 [Solution of the systems, part (a)] In the (s) branch, $Y^s_1>0$ is real and positive, and $Y^s_2$ is real positive for $|\kappa|<K_2$, zero at the critical point, and $i\beta/(\mu_2\omega)$ for $|\kappa|>K_2$; in every case $\operatorname{Re}(Y^s_1+Y^s_2)=Y^s_1+\operatorname{Re}Y^s_2\ge Y^s_1>0$, so $Y^s_1+Y^s_2\ne0$. In the (p) branch with $k_{t3}\ne0$, $\operatorname{Re}Y^p_1=Y^p_1>0$ and $\operatorname{Re}Y^p_2=0$ or $>0$, so again $Y^p_1+Y^p_2\ne0$. The admittance lemma [F3](a) therefore applies to each scalar system of step 2.1 and gives $r_\chi=(Y^\chi_1-Y^\chi_2)/(Y^\chi_1+Y^\chi_2)$ and $t_\chi=2Y^\chi_1/(Y^\chi_1+Y^\chi_2)$; in particular $t_\chi\ne0$ because its numerator $2Y^\chi_1\ne0$. [step 2.1, F3, F7, algebra]

3.2 [Critical angle, part (e)] Let $|\kappa|=K_2$; then $k_{t3}=0$ by [F6], so $Y^s_2=0$ and no $Y^p_2$ is defined (a division by $k_{t3}$ is not performed). For (s), the system of step 2.1 reads $E_i+E_r=E_t$ and $Y^s_1(E_i-E_r)=0$ with $Y^s_1>0$, so $E_r=E_i$ and $E_t=2E_i$, i.e. $r_s=1$ and $t_s=2$. For (p), $E^{(p)}_t=k_{t3}H_t/(\omega\varepsilon_2)=0$ and $E^{(p)}_i=k_{13}H_i/(\omega\varepsilon_1)$, $E^{(p)}_r=-k_{13}H_r/(\omega\varepsilon_1)$ by the fixed-direction definitions of step 2.1; the tangential electric identity $E^{(p)}_i+E^{(p)}_r=0$ gives $H_r=H_i$, and the tangential magnetic identity $H_i+H_r=H_t$ gives $H_t=2H_i$; hence $r_p=E^{(p)}_r/E^{(p)}_i=-H_r/H_i=-1$ and $t_p=E^{(p)}_t/E^{(p)}_i=0$. The transmitted normal averaged flux vanishes: $\langle S_t\cdot n\rangle=\tfrac12\operatorname{Re}\bigl((e_t\times\overline h_t)\cdot n\bigr)$ by [F4], and with $h_t=H_t\hat y$ and $e_t=-(k_t\times\hat y)H_t/(\omega\varepsilon_2)=-|\kappa|H_tn/(\omega\varepsilon_2)$ (because $k_t=\kappa$ and $\kappa\times\hat y=|\kappa|n$ by [F5]), the vector $e_t\times\overline h_t$ is a multiple of $n\times\hat y$, which is orthogonal to $n$: $(e_t\times\overline h_t)\cdot n=0$. In the TE case $e_t=E_t\hat y$ and $h_t=|\kappa|E_tn/(\mu_2\omega)$, so $e_t\times\overline h_t$ is tangential and its normal component is also zero. The transmitted TM electric vector $-|\kappa|H_tn/(\omega\varepsilon_2)$ has a nontrivial normal component whenever $H_t\ne0$. [step 2.1, F4, F5, F6, F7, algebra]

4.1 [Propagating branch: angles and fluxes, part (b)] Let $|\kappa|<K_2$. Then $k_{t3}=\sqrt{K_2^2-|\kappa|^2}>0$ and all three wavevectors are real by [F6]; the four admittances of step 2.1 are real and positive. With $|k_i|=|k_r|=K_1$ and $|k_t|=K_2$ from $|k_j|^2=|\kappa|^2+k_{j3}^2$, the angles $\theta_j:=\arcsin(|\kappa|/|k_j|)\in(0,\pi/2)$ are well defined by [F8] since $0<|\kappa|<\min\{K_1,K_2\}$; then $\sin\theta_r=|\kappa|/K_1=\sin\theta_i$, so $\theta_r=\theta_i$ by the strict increase of sine and of $\arcsin$ in [F8], and $K_1\sin\theta_i=|\kappa|=K_2\sin\theta_t$, which is $n_1\sin\theta_i=n_2\sin\theta_t$ after multiplying by $c/\omega$ and using $n_j=cK_j/\omega=c(\varepsilon_j\mu_j)^{1/2}$ by [F6]; this is the same angle law as [F2]. The period-averaged normal fluxes: for (s), the flux of [F1] is $\langle S_j\rangle=|e_j|^2k_j/(2\mu_j\omega)$ with $|e_j|^2=|E^{(s)}_j|^2$ and $k_j\cdot n=k_{j3}$, so the normal fluxes are $k_{j3}|E^{(s)}_j|^2/(2\mu_j\omega)$, i.e. $Y^s_1|E^{(s)}_i|^2/2$, $-Y^s_1|E^{(s)}_r|^2/2$, $Y^s_2|E^{(s)}_t|^2/2$; for (p) the same $\langle S_j\cdot n\rangle=|e_j|^2k_{j3}/(2\mu_j\omega)$ equals $Y^p_j\,|E^{(p)}_j|^2/2$ up to the reflected sign, because $|e_j|^2=\mu_j|H_j|^2/\varepsilon_j$ and $|E^{(p)}_j|=|k_{j3}||H_j|/(\varepsilon_j\omega)$ give $|e_j|^2k_{j3}/(2\mu_j\omega)=k_{j3}|H_j|^2/(2\varepsilon_j\omega)$ for the incident and transmitted signs, while the reflected signed component $k_{r3}=-k_{13}$ produces the minus sign, as in the Statement. With $R_\chi=|r_\chi|^2=|E^\chi_r|^2/|E^\chi_i|^2$ and $T_\chi=(Y^\chi_2/Y^\chi_1)|t_\chi|^2=Y^\chi_2|E^\chi_t|^2/(Y^\chi_1|E^\chi_i|^2)$, the budget $R_\chi+T_\chi=1$ is [F3](b) applied with the real positive $Y^\chi_1,Y^\chi_2$. [step 3.1, F1, F2, F3, F6, F8, algebra]

4.2 [Total internal reflection, part (d)] Let $|\kappa|>K_2$; then $K_2<K_1$, so $n_2<n_1$, and $k_{t3}=i\beta$ with $\beta=\sqrt{|\kappa|^2-K_2^2}>0$ by [F6]. Hence $Y^s_2=k_{t3}/(\mu_2\omega)=i\beta/(\mu_2\omega)$ and $Y^p_2=\varepsilon_2\omega/(i\beta)=-i\varepsilon_2\omega/\beta$ are purely imaginary by [F7], while $Y^\chi_1>0$ is real; the admittance lemma [F3](c) gives $|r_s|=|r_p|=1$ and $|t_\chi|^2=4(Y^\chi_1)^2/((Y^\chi_1)^2+|Y^\chi_2|^2)>0$, so $t_\chi\ne0$. The transmitted wave is the evanescent branch of [F1] with $n'=n$, $\kappa_0=\kappa$ and $k_t\cdot k_t=K_2^2=\varepsilon_2\mu_2\omega^2$: the field factorizes with the decay factor $e^{-\beta x_3}$, its period-averaged normal flux vanishes identically, and its tangential averaged flux is strictly positive; since $|r_\chi|=1$, the reflected normal averaged flux equals minus the incident normal averaged flux, i.e. the reflected wave carries the whole incident normal averaged flux. [step 3.1, F1, F3, F6, F7, algebra]

5.1 [Brewster condition, part (c)] In each finite-admittance branch the formula of step 3.1 gives $r_\chi=0$ exactly when the numerator $Y^\chi_1-Y^\chi_2$ vanishes, i.e. $Y^\chi_1=Y^\chi_2$. Assume now $\mu_1=\mu_2=:\mu$ and $|\kappa|<K_2$, so that the (p) admittances are real positive. The condition $Y^p_1=Y^p_2$ reads $\varepsilon_1/k_{13}=\varepsilon_2/k_{t3}$; substituting $\varepsilon_j=n_j^2/(c^2\mu)$ and $k_{j3}=(\omega n_j/c)\cos\theta_j$ (from $k_{13}=K_1\cos\theta_i=(\omega n_1/c)\cos\theta_i$ and $k_{t3}=K_2\cos\theta_t=(\omega n_2/c)\cos\theta_t$) gives $n_1\cos\theta_t=n_2\cos\theta_i$. Together with Snell's law $n_1\sin\theta_i=n_2\sin\theta_t$ of step 4.1, squaring both and eliminating $\sin^2\theta_t$ gives $(n_1^2-n_2^2)\bigl[n_2^2-(n_1^2+n_2^2)\sin^2\theta_i\bigr]=0$: indeed $n_1^2(1-\sin^2\theta_t)=n_2^2\cos^2\theta_i$ and $\sin\theta_t=(n_1/n_2)\sin\theta_i$ yield $n_1^2n_2^2-n_1^4\sin^2\theta_i=n_2^4-n_2^4\sin^2\theta_i$, i.e. $n_2^2(n_1^2-n_2^2)=(n_1^2-n_2^2)(n_1^2+n_2^2)\sin^2\theta_i$. If $n_1\ne n_2$, division by the nonzero first factor gives $\sin^2\theta_i=n_2^2/(n_1^2+n_2^2)$; since $0<\theta_i<\pi/2$ and sine is strictly increasing on $[0,\pi/2]$ with $\sin 0=0$ by [F8], this determines the unique admissible angle $\theta_B:=\arcsin\bigl(n_2/\sqrt{n_1^2+n_2^2}\bigr)\in(0,\pi/2)$, This angle is admissible: with $D:=\sqrt{n_1^2+n_2^2}$, its tangential wavenumber is $K_1n_2/D<K_2$ because $n_1/D<1$. Snell's law therefore gives $\sin\theta_t=n_1/D$ and, by positivity of both cosines and the Pythagorean identity, $\cos\theta_i=n_1/D$, $\cos\theta_t=n_2/D$. Thus $n_1\cos\theta_t=n_2\cos\theta_i$, establishing $Y_1^p=Y_2^p$ and $r_p=0$; this proves sufficiency as well as uniqueness. At this angle $\cos\theta_B>0$: cosine is strictly decreasing on $[0,\pi]$ with $\cos(\pi/2)=0$ by [F8], so $0<\theta_B<\pi/2$ gives $\cos\theta_B>\cos(\pi/2)=0$. The Pythagorean identity of [F8] then gives $\cos\theta_B=\sqrt{1-\sin^2\theta_B}=n_1/\sqrt{n_1^2+n_2^2}$, so $\tan\theta_B=\sin\theta_B/\cos\theta_B=n_2/n_1$ by the definition of tangent in [F8]; no other admissible angle has this tangent, because tangent is strictly increasing on $(0,\pi/2)$ by [F8]. Also $r_s\ne0$ at $\theta_B$, because $Y^s_1=Y^s_2$ would give $k_{13}=k_{t3}$ (the $\mu$ factors being equal), hence $K_1^2=|\kappa|^2+k_{13}^2=|\kappa|^2+k_{t3}^2=K_2^2$ and $n_1=n_2$, contrary to hypothesis. If instead $n_1=n_2$ and $\mu_1=\mu_2$ then $\varepsilon_1=\varepsilon_2$ and the two media are identical: $k_{13}=k_{t3}$ and $\varepsilon_1=\varepsilon_2$ give $Y^\chi_1=Y^\chi_2$, so $r_s=r_p=0$ and $t_s=t_p=1$ at every admissible angle (where the (p) formula applies), with no distinguished Brewster angle. [step 3.1, step 4.1, F3, F6, F8, algebra]
