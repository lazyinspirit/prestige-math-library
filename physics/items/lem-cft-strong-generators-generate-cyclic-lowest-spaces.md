## Statement

Let $V=\bigoplus_{d\ge0}V_d$ be a conformal vertex algebra with $V_0=\mathbb C1$, strongly generated by homogeneous positive-weight states $S$, meaning
$$V=\operatorname{span}\{s^1_{(-k_1)}\cdots s^\ell_{(-k_\ell)}\mathbf1:\ell\ge0,\ s^i\in S,\ k_i\ge1\},$$
where the $\ell=0$ word is $\mathbf1$. Let $M=\bigoplus_{r\ge0}M(r)$ be an admissible $V$-module, so a homogeneous $a\in V_d$ satisfies $a_{(m)}M(r)\subset M(r+d-m-1)$. If $M$ is cyclic on $u\in M(0)$, meaning
$$M=\operatorname{span}\{a^1_{(m_1)}\cdots a^k_{(m_k)}u:k\ge0,\ a^i\in V,\ m_i\in\mathbb Z\},$$
where the $k=0$ word is $u$, then
$$M(0)=\operatorname{span}\{o(s_1)\cdots o(s_k)u:k\ge0,\ s_i\in S\},\qquad o(a)=a_{(\operatorname{wt}a-1)}.$$
Moreover, for each homogeneous state $a$, a noncommutative polynomial in the generator zero modes, depending only on $a$ and a fixed strong spanning expression for it, equals $o(a)$ on the lowest space of every admissible module. Thus the same generator zero-mode matrices determine the same full lowest-space zero-mode action. The statement does not require semisimple $L(0)$, irreducibility, $C_2$-cofiniteness, the existence of a universal induced module, or the general Zhu correspondence.

## Proof

1.1 Give a mode $a_{(m)}$ of homogeneous $a$ degree $\operatorname{wt}(a)-m-1$. The commutator identity in [A2] and its state-product weight rule show every term in $[a_{(m)},b_{(n)}]$ has degree $\operatorname{wt}(a)-m-1+\operatorname{wt}(b)-n-1$. The sum is finite by state truncation. Thus mode commutators preserve total degree. [given, A2, F1, algebra]

1.2 Let $a,b$ be homogeneous of weights $d,e$, with $d\ge1$, and define the inhomogeneous states $C_k(a,b)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom di a_{(i-k)}b$ for $k\ge1$. Extend $o$ linearly over homogeneous components. For the summand indexed by $i$, put $n=i-k$ and $m=d+e-n-2$, the zero-mode index of $a_{(n)}b$. The iterate identity in [A2] gives $$(a_{(n)}b)_{(m)}=\sum_{j\ge0}(-1)^j\binom nj\left(a_{(n-j)}b_{(m+j)}-(-1)^n b_{(m+n-j)}a_{(j)}\right).$$ The first term has rightmost mode $b_{(m+j)}$ of degree $n-d+1-j=i-k-d+1-j$. [A2, algebra]

2.1 Reorder a finite word of modes so positive-degree modes lie left, zero-degree modes middle, and negative-degree modes right. Swap any adjacent factors in the wrong order using $AB=BA+[A,B]$: the swapped word has one fewer inversion, and every commutator term has shorter length. Induction on length and then inversion count terminates. On applying the reordered word to $u\in M(0)$, a rightmost negative-degree mode kills $u$ by admissibility. A word containing only nonnegative modes and at least one positive-degree mode has positive total degree. Since $M$ is cyclic on $u$, its degree-zero space is therefore spanned by words in $o(a)=a_{(\operatorname{wt}(a)-1)}$, $a\in V$, applied to $u$. [given, A1, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Because $i\le d$, this first term kills $M(0)$ for every $k\ge2$. For $k=1$ it can survive only at $i=d,j=0$, when it is exactly $o(a)o(b)$. In the second term $a_{(j)}$ kills $M(0)$ for $j\ge d$. For each fixed $0\le j<d$, the mode $b_{(m+n-j)}=b_{(d+e-2-j)}$ is independent of $i$. Its coefficient after summing over $i$ is, up to the fixed sign $(-1)^{j-k+1}$, $$\sum_{i=0}^{d}(-1)^i\binom di\binom{i-k}{j}=0.$$ Indeed $i\mapsto\binom{i-k}{j}$ is a polynomial of degree $j<d$, so its $d$th finite difference vanishes; this remains true for negative upper entries by the polynomial definition of the binomial coefficient. Module truncation makes the original iterate sums finite on each vector. Therefore $o(C_1(a,b))=o(a)o(b)$ and $o(C_k(a,b))=0$ for $k\ge2$ on $M(0)$. [given, F2, F3, A2, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Induct on state weight to express every $o(v)$ on $M(0)$ as a noncommutative polynomial in $o(S)$. Weight zero gives the identity. A strong spanning monomial of positive weight is $s_{(-k)}b$ with $s\in S$, $k\ge1$, and $b$ a shorter strong monomial. For $k=1$, expand $C_1(s,b)$: its first term is $s_{(-1)}b$, while every other term $s_{(i-1)}b$ has weight $\operatorname{wt}(s)+\operatorname{wt}(b)-i$, strictly below the target weight; also $b$ has lower weight because $\operatorname{wt}(s)\ge1$. The identity in step 2.2 expresses its zero mode using $o(s)o(b)$ and those lower-weight zero modes. For $k\ge2$, expand $C_k(s,b)$; its first term is $s_{(-k)}b$, and each remaining term $s_{(i-k)}b$ has weight $\operatorname{wt}(s)+\operatorname{wt}(b)+k-1-i$, strictly lower than the target. The identity $o(C_k)=0$ gives the reduction. Negative-weight terms vanish because $V_d=0$ for $d<0$. Fix a strong spanning expression for each state: the induction decreases weight and each $C_k$ is finite, so it terminates and yields a polynomial independent of $M$. Combining with step 2.1 proves both assertions. [given, A1, F1, F3, A2, step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra] ∎
