## Statement

Assume Countable Choice ([[def-countable-choice]]), as used by the declared polar/surface and change-of-variables suppliers. Let $c>0$, $\mu_0>0$, $a>0$ and $t_0<T$ be constants, let $\rho\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R)$ and $\mathbf J\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R;\mathbb R^3)$ satisfy (H1) $\rho(y,v)=\mathbf J(y,v)=0$ whenever $|y|>a$ or $v\notin[t_0,T]$, and (H2) $\partial_v\rho+\operatorname{div}_y\mathbf J=0$ on $\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R$. Here and below $B_a:=\{y\in\mathbb R^3:|y|<a\}$ and $\overline B_a:=\{y\in\mathbb R^3:|y|\le a\}$ are the open and closed Euclidean balls of radius $a$ about the origin, and $S^2=\{n\in\mathbb R^3:|n|=1\}$ is the unit sphere. On the open exterior $U:=\{x\in\mathbb R^3:|x|>a\}$ define the retarded scalar and vector potentials $$\varphi(x,t):=\frac{\mu_0c^2}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb R^3}\frac{\rho(y,t-|x-y|/c)}{|x-y|}\,dy,\qquad \mathbf A(x,t):=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb R^3}\frac{\mathbf J(y,t-|x-y|/c)}{|x-y|}\,dy,$$ and the fields $\mathbf E:=-\nabla\varphi-\partial_t\mathbf A$, $\mathbf B:=\nabla\times\mathbf A$, where $\nabla f=(\partial_1f,\partial_2f,\partial_3f)$, $\operatorname{div}F=\sum_{j=1}^3\partial_jF_j$ and $\nabla\times F=(\partial_2F_3-\partial_3F_2,\partial_3F_1-\partial_1F_3,\partial_1F_2-\partial_2F_1)$ are the spatial operators of [[def-jacobian-matrix-and-gradient]] and [[def-divergence-and-curl-of-a-c1-vector-field]]. The fields are defined on $U\times\mathbb R$ using these ordinary derivatives; the conclusions below use $r:=|x|\ge2a$. Every displayed spatial integrand is evaluated on $\overline B_a$ and extended by zero outside it, including at $y=x$, so no quotient $0/0$ is used. All vector-valued integrals are componentwise scalar Lebesgue integrals.

(a) (Fields as retarded integrals.) On the open set $\{|x|>2a\}$ the potentials $\varphi,\mathbf A$ are of class $C^\infty$ in $(x,t)$, and there $$\mathbf E(x,t)=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb R^3}\Bigl[\frac{\mathbf N}{R^2}c^2\rho\bigl(y,t-\tfrac Rc\bigr)+\frac{c\,\mathbf N}{R}\rho_t\bigl(y,t-\tfrac Rc\bigr)-\frac{\mathbf J_t\bigl(y,t-\tfrac Rc\bigr)}{R}\Bigr]dy,\qquad \mathbf B(x,t)=-\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb R^3}\mathbf N\times\Bigl[\frac{\mathbf J(y,t-\frac Rc)}{R^2}+\frac{\mathbf J_t(y,t-\frac Rc)}{cR}\Bigr]dy,$$ where $R:=|x-y|$, $\mathbf N:=(x-y)/R$, and $\rho_t,\mathbf J_t$ are the partial derivatives in the second variable. Moreover, on $\{|x|\ge2a\}\times[t_0,T]$, $\varphi$ and each component of $\mathbf A$ coincide with the corresponding solution constructed in the wave Cauchy problem of [[thm-em-wave-cauchy-kirchhoff]] for zero Cauchy data at $t_0$ and scalar source $h=\mu_0c^2\rho$, respectively $h=\mu_0 J_k$.

(b) (Uniform far-zone expansion.) For $n\in S^2$ and $u\in[t_0,T]$ put $\mathbf I(n,u):=\int_{B_a}\mathbf J(y,u+n\cdot y/c)\,dy$ and $\mathbf V(n,u):=\partial_u\mathbf I(n,u)=\int_{B_a}\mathbf J_t(y,u+n\cdot y/c)\,dy$. Then $\int_{B_a}\rho_t(y,u+n\cdot y/c)\,dy=\frac1c\,n\cdot\mathbf V(n,u)$, and there are constants $\Gamma_E,\Gamma_B$, depending only on $a,c,\mu_0$ and on the suprema of $|\rho|,|\rho_t|,|\rho_{tt}|,|\mathbf J|,|\mathbf J_t|,|\mathbf J_{tt}|$ on $\overline B_a\times[t_0,T]$, such that for every $n\in S^2$, $u\in[t_0,T]$ and $r\ge2a$, with $\mathbf E_{\mathrm{lead}}(n,u,r):=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi r}n\times(n\times\mathbf V(n,u))$, $$\Bigl|\mathbf E(rn,u+\tfrac rc)-\mathbf E_{\mathrm{lead}}(n,u,r)\Bigr|\le\frac{\Gamma_E}{r^2},\qquad \Bigl|\mathbf B(rn,u+\tfrac rc)-\frac{n\times\mathbf E_{\mathrm{lead}}(n,u,r)}{c}\Bigr|\le\frac{\Gamma_B}{r^2}.$$

(c) (Retarded-time flux limit.) Let $\mathbf S:=\mu_0^{-1}\mathbf E\times\mathbf B$ and let $d\Omega$ be the surface measure on $S^2$ ([[def-surface-integral-on-a-compact-c-one-hypersurface]]). Then for every $u\in[t_0,T]$, $$\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{S^2}r^2\,n\cdot\mathbf S\bigl(rn,u+\tfrac rc\bigr)\,d\Omega(n)=\frac{\mu_0}{16\pi^2c}\int_{S^2}\bigl|n\times\mathbf V(n,u)\bigr|^2\,d\Omega(n).$$ This is a limit at fixed retarded time $u$, not at fixed observation time $t$; no decay or boundary condition at spatial infinity is assumed beyond the compact spatial support in (H1).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ ([[def-countable-choice]]); constants $c>0$, $\mu_0>0$, $a>0$, $t_0<T$; sources $\rho\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R)$, $\mathbf J\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R;\mathbb R^3)$ satisfying (H1) and (H2) of the Statement; the retarded potentials and fields of the Statement, with all vector integrals read componentwise. For $n\in S^2$, $u\in[t_0,T]$, $r\ge2a$ we write $x:=rn$, $t:=u+r/c$, $R:=|x-y|=\bigl((r-n\cdot y)^2+|y|^2-(n\cdot y)^2\bigr)^{1/2}$, $\mathbf N:=(x-y)/R$, $\sigma:=n\cdot y$, $v_y:=u+\sigma/c$ and $\delta_y:=(r-\sigma-R)/c$, so that $t-R/c=v_y+\delta_y$; $\Gamma_\rho,\Gamma_{\rho t},\Gamma_{\rho tt},\Gamma_J,\Gamma_{Jt},\Gamma_{Jtt}$ denote the suprema of $|\rho|,|\rho_t|,|\rho_{tt}|,|\mathbf J|,|\mathbf J_t|,|\mathbf J_{tt}|$ on $\overline B_a\times[t_0,T]$, and $\lambda(B_a)$ the Lebesgue measure of the ball.

[F1] A measurable $h$ is integrable exactly when $\int|h|<+\infty$, and then the integral is computed from the positive and negative parts or, for complex $h=u+iv$, as $\int u+i\int v$; $L^1$ denotes the integrable class and $\int_E f=\int f\chi_E$ is the integral over a measurable set. ([[def-integrable-real-and-complex-functions-and-their-integrals]], [[def-l-one-of-a-measure]], [[def-integral-over-a-measurable-set]])

[F2] The integral is linear, $|\int f|\le\int|f|$, $0\le f\le g$ measurable implies $\int f\le\int g$, $\int cf=c\int f$ for $c\ge0$, and integrable functions that agree almost everywhere have the same integral. ([[thm-linearity-of-the-lebesgue-integral-on-l-one]], [[thm-integral-triangle-inequality]], [[prop-order-and-scalar-rules-for-the-nonnegative-integral]], [[thm-the-lebesgue-integral-respects-almost-everywhere-equality]])

[F3] Under $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ the ball $B_a$ is Lebesgue measurable with $0<\lambda(B_a)<+\infty$; every continuous map $\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^m$ is Borel measurable; and sums, scalar multiples and pointwise products of measurable functions are measurable. ([[lem-euclidean-balls-have-positive-finite-lebesgue-measure]], [[cor-continuous-functions-are-borel-measurable]], [[thm-arithmetic-and-lattice-operations-preserve-measurability]])

[F4] Mean value theorem: if $\phi$ is continuous on $[p,q]$ and differentiable on $(p,q)$ then $\phi(q)-\phi(p)=\phi'(\xi)(q-p)$ for some $\xi\in(p,q)$. ([[cor-mean-value-theorem]])

[F5] Parameter integrals over a fixed compact measurable set of finite measure: if $K\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ is compact, measurable and of finite measure, if $a<b$, and if the real-valued $f$ and $\partial_tf$ are continuous on a neighbourhood of $K\times[a,b]$, then $t\mapsto\int_Kf(x,t)\,dx$ is differentiable at every $t\in(a,b)$ with derivative $\int_K\partial_tf(x,t)\,dx$ and has the corresponding one-sided relative derivatives at the endpoints of $[a,b]$; the lemma is stated pointwise in its one real parameter and explicitly asserts nothing for moving parameter sets or for singularities crossing $K$. Applying it once per parameter direction with its parameter renamed to $x_1,x_2,x_3$ or $t$ (all other variables held fixed), and iterating on the resulting integrals, yields the corresponding partial derivatives of a parameter integral whose integrand is smooth in all variables on a neighbourhood of the fixed $K$. ([[lem-em-parameter-integrals]])

[F6] Chain rule and algebra of derivatives for the composite maps appearing below, such as $y\mapsto(y,u+n\cdot y/c)$ and $(x,t)\mapsto(y,t-|x-y|/c)$. ([[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]], [[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]]) The spatial gradient, divergence and curl are the coordinate operators specified in the Statement ([[def-jacobian-matrix-and-gradient]], [[def-divergence-and-curl-of-a-c1-vector-field]]).

[F7] A $C^k$ map has continuous word partial derivatives through order $k$, and $C^\infty$ means this for every $k$ ([[def-ck-euclidean-maps-and-diffeomorphisms]]). Finite sums, products and composites preserve these classes ([[thm-ck-euclidean-maps-closed-under-algebra-and-composition]]). On $(0,\infty)$, $(q^\alpha)'=\alpha q^{\alpha-1}$ and real powers agree with rational powers ([[thm-real-power-continuity-and-derivatives]], [[thm-real-power-agrees-with-rational-exponent]]); iterating this rule makes $\sqrt q=q^{1/2}$ and $1/q$ smooth there. Closed bounded Euclidean sets are compact, continuous functions on compact sets are uniformly continuous and attain finite extrema ([[thm-heine-borel-rn]], [[thm-heine-cantor-metric]], [[thm-extreme-value-metric]]).

[F8] Zero-data wave Cauchy problem of speed $c$ from time $t_0$ with source $h\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^3\times[t_0,T])$ in the extension sense: its solution is $u(x,t)=c^2\int_{t_0}^t(t-v)M_{c(t-v)}h(\cdot,v)(x)\,dv$ on $\mathbb R^3\times[t_0,T]$, it is $C^\infty$ there, and it satisfies $c^{-2}\partial_t^2u-\Delta u=h$; here $M_rh(x,v)=\frac1{4\pi}\int_{S^2}h(x+rn,v)\,d\Omega(n)$. ([[thm-em-wave-cauchy-kirchhoff]])

[F9] Polar-coordinates formula: for every nonnegative Borel $f:\mathbb R^3\to[0,\infty]$, $\int_{\mathbb R^3}f(y)\,dy=\int_0^\infty\int_{S^2}f(r\omega)r^2\,d\sigma(\omega)\,dr$; here $\sigma$ is the polar surface measure of the cited formula, which equals the chart surface measure $d\Omega$ of [F13] ([[lem-euclidean-chart-measure-agrees-with-polar-surface-measure]]), so the two symbols are used interchangeably; a signed or complex $f$ is handled by applying the formula to positive and negative parts and subtracting. ([[thm-polar-coordinates-formula-for-lebesgue-measure]], [[lem-euclidean-chart-measure-agrees-with-polar-surface-measure]])

[F10] One-dimensional $L^1$ change of variables: for a $C^1$ diffeomorphism $T:U\to V$ of open subsets of $\mathbb R$ and $f\in L^1(V)$, $\int_Vf=\int_Uf\circ T\,|T'|$. ([[cor-c-one-change-of-variables-for-l-one-functions]])

[F11] Divergence theorem for a bounded $C^1$ Euclidean domain $\Omega$ and $F\in C^1(\overline\Omega;\mathbb R^n)$: $\int_\Omega\operatorname{div}F\,dx=\int_{\partial\Omega}F\cdot\nu\,dS$, with the outward normal. ([[thm-divergence-theorem-for-bounded-c-one-euclidean-domains]])

[F12] The Euclidean norm satisfies the triangle and Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities ([[thm-cauchy-schwarz-and-the-euclidean-norm]]). Cross product and inner product on $\mathbb R^3$: $u\times v$ is bilinear and antisymmetric, $n\cdot(u\times v)=\det(n,u,v)$, and for $|n|=1$ the identities $n\cdot(n\times w)=0$ and $n\times(n\times w)=n(n\cdot w)-w$ hold, so $|n\times(n\times w)|=|n\times w|$. ([[def-cross-product-in-r3]], [[def-euclidean-inner-product]])

[F13] Surface integral on the compact embedded $C^1$ hypersurface $S^2$: for a nonnegative Borel $f$ the integral $\int_{S^2}f\,d\Omega$ is defined by finitely many regular parametrizations and an ambient partition of unity, the empty surface integrates to $0$, the definition is independent of the chosen presentation, the total surface measure $\int_{S^2}1\,d\Omega$ is finite, and the integral is monotone in $f$; the definition carries the $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ convention already declared. ([[def-surface-integral-on-a-compact-c-one-hypersurface]])

[F14] $C^1$ change of variables for nonnegative Borel functions: under $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, for a $C^1$ diffeomorphism $T:U\to V$ of open subsets of $\mathbb R^m$ and every nonnegative Borel $h:V\to[0,\infty]$, $\int_Vh(y)\,dy=\int_Uh(T(z))|\det DT(z)|\,dz$ with equality in $[0,\infty]$; in particular the translation $T(z)=x+z$ of $\mathbb R^3$ is a $C^1$ diffeomorphism with $\det DT=1$, and a signed integrand is handled through its positive and negative parts by linearity. ([[lem-c-one-change-of-variables-for-nonnegative-borel-functions-via-radon-uniqueness]], [[thm-linearity-of-the-lebesgue-integral-on-l-one]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; part (a) differentiates the retarded integrals and identifies them with the wave Cauchy solution, part (b) expands the field integrals uniformly in the far zone and derives the continuity identity, part (c) passes to the surface integral.

1.1 Smoothness of the potentials: for $x\in U$ and $y\in\overline B_a$ one has $R=|x-y|\ge|x|-a>0$, so the maps $(x,t,y)\mapsto\rho(y,t-R/c)/R$ and $(x,t,y)\mapsto\mathbf J(y,t-R/c)/R$ are $C^\infty$ on a neighbourhood of $U\times\mathbb R\times\overline B_a$ by [F6] and [F7]. Apply [F5] to the fixed compact set $K=\overline B_a$ once for each parameter direction with its one real parameter renamed to $x_1$, $x_2$, $x_3$ and $t$ (the remaining variables held fixed) and iterate on the resulting integrals: every differentiated integrand is again continuous on a neighbourhood of $K$, so each application is covered by the lemma and $\varphi$ and each component of $\mathbf A$ have continuous partial derivatives of every order on $U\times\mathbb R$, these differentiated parameter integrals are jointly continuous: on each compact parameter neighbourhood the continuous differentiated integrands are uniformly continuous on its product with $K$, so the integral of their difference is bounded by $\lambda(K)$ times their uniformly vanishing difference [F2]. Hence the potentials are $C^\infty$ on $U\times\mathbb R$ by [F7], so the derivatives defining the fields are ordinary derivatives even at $|x|=2a$. [F5, F6, F7, F3]

1.2 Elementary estimates in the far zone: for $y\in B_a$, $r\ge2a$ and $n\in S^2$, expanding $R^2=(r-\sigma)^2+|y|^2-\sigma^2$ with $\sigma=n\cdot y$ gives $|R-(r-\sigma)|=\bigl||y|^2-\sigma^2\bigr|/(R+r-\sigma)\le a^2/r$ because $R+r-\sigma\ge r-\sigma+|r-\sigma|\ge r$; consequently $|r-R|\le a+a^2/r$, $|\mathbf N-n|=|(r-R)n-y|/R\le(a+a^2/r+a)/(r-a)\le5a/r$, $|1/R-1/r|\le(a+a^2/r)/(r(r-a))\le3a/r^2$, and $|\delta_y|\le a^2/(rc)$, all uniformly in $y,n$. [F12, F1]

2.1 Field formulas: by [F6], $\nabla_xR=\mathbf N$, $\nabla_x(t-R/c)=-\mathbf N/c$ and $\nabla_x\times[\mathbf J(y,t-R/c)]=-\mathbf N\times\mathbf J_t(y,t-R/c)/c$, while $\nabla_x(1/R)=-\mathbf N/R^2$; hence $\nabla_x[\rho(y,t-R/c)/R]=-\mathbf N[\rho/R^2+\rho_t/(cR)]$ and $\nabla_x\times[\mathbf J(y,t-R/c)/R]=-\mathbf N\times[\mathbf J/R^2+\mathbf J_t/(cR)]$. Each of these first derivatives of the integrands is continuous on a neighbourhood of $\overline B_a$, so by [F5] the derivatives may be taken under the integral: $-\nabla\varphi-\partial_t\mathbf A$ and $\nabla\times\mathbf A$ are given on $U$, in particular for $|x|\ge2a$, by the two displayed zero-extended integrals of the statement, since $\mathbf E=-\nabla\varphi-\partial_t\mathbf A$ and $\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A$. [step 1.1, F5, F6, F12]

2.2 Polar-coordinates identity: fix $x\in U$ and $t\in\mathbb R$, and let $h$ be either $\rho$ or one component $J_k$; then $y\mapsto h(y,t-|x-y|/c)/|x-y|$ vanishes off $\overline B_a$ and $|x-y|\ge|x|-a>0$ there, so the quotient on $\overline B_a$, extended by zero elsewhere, defines a bounded compactly supported Borel function $f$ on all of $\mathbb R^3$. The translation $T(z):=x+z$ is a $C^1$ diffeomorphism of $\mathbb R^3$ with $\det DT=1$, so [F14] applied to the positive and negative parts of $f$ gives $\int_{\mathbb R^3}f(y)\,dy=\int_{\mathbb R^3}f(x+z)\,dz$; applying [F9] to the positive and negative parts of $z\mapsto f(x+z)$, which at $z=\rho\omega$ equals $h(x+\rho\omega,t-\rho/c)/\rho$ and therefore has inner angular integral $\frac1\rho\int_{S^2}h(x+\rho n,t-\rho/c)\,d\Omega(n)=\frac{4\pi}{\rho}M_\rho h(x,t-\rho/c)$, gives $\frac1{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb R^3}\frac{h(y,t-|x-y|/c)}{|x-y|}dy=\int_0^\infty r\,M_rh(x,t-r/c)\,dr$ with $M_rh(x,v)=\frac1{4\pi}\int_{S^2}h(x+rn,v)\,d\Omega(n)$, because the inner angular integral is $4\pi M_rh$ and the radial factor is $r^2/r=r$. [step 1.1, F9, F14, F1, F3]

2.3 Continuity identity: fix $n,u$ and let $\rho_0>a$; the map $F(y):=\mathbf J(y,u+n\cdot y/c)$ is $C^1$ on $\overline B_{\rho_0}$, and [F6] gives $\operatorname{div}F(y)=\operatorname{div}_y\mathbf J(y,v_y)+\frac nc\cdot\mathbf J_t(y,v_y)$ with $v_y=u+n\cdot y/c$. By [F11] on $B_{\rho_0}$, whose boundary flux vanishes because $\mathbf J=0$ for $|y|>a$, $\int_{B_{\rho_0}}\operatorname{div}F\,dy=0$; substituting the chain-rule expression and using (H2), $0=\int_{B_{\rho_0}}\bigl[-\rho_t(y,v_y)+\frac1cn\cdot\mathbf J_t(y,v_y)\bigr]dy$. The integrands vanish for $|y|>a$ by (H1), so [F2] lets the integrals be taken over $B_a$; the second is $\frac1cn\cdot\mathbf V(n,u)$ by the definition of $\mathbf V$, whence $\int_{B_a}\rho_t(y,v_y)\,dy=\frac1cn\cdot\mathbf V(n,u)$. [step 1.2, F11, F6, F2]

3.1 Identification with the Cauchy solution: fix $x\in U$. For $t>t_0$ the radial integrand of step 2.2 vanishes when $r>c(t-t_0)$, because then its source time is less than $t_0$. Apply [F10] to the affine diffeomorphism $v\mapsto c(t-v)$ from $(t_0,t)$ onto $(0,c(t-t_0))$, with absolute derivative $c$; the integrand is bounded on this finite interval, hence integrable. Endpoint values do not affect the Lebesgue integrals, and this gives $\int_0^\infty rM_rh(x,t-r/c)\,dr=c^2\int_{t_0}^t(t-v)M_{c(t-v)}h(x,v)\,dv$. At $t=t_0$ both sides are zero by (H1), so the identity holds throughout $[t_0,T]$. Multiplying it by $\mu_0c^2$ for $h=\rho$, and by $\mu_0$ for $h=J_k$, identifies $\varphi$ and $A_k$ respectively with the zero-data Cauchy solutions of [F8] with sources $\mu_0c^2\rho$ and $\mu_0J_k$. This holds on $U\times[t_0,T]$ and in particular at $|x|=2a$. [step 2.2, F10, F8, F1]

3.2 Uniform error estimates: fix $n,u,r$, let $\mathrm{ret}$ denote the argument $v_y+\delta_y$ and $0$ the argument $v_y$, and write $F_{\mathrm{tot}}:=c^2\mathbf N\rho_{\mathrm{ret}}/R^2+c\mathbf N\rho_{t,\mathrm{ret}}/R-\mathbf J_{t,\mathrm{ret}}/R$ and $F_{\mathrm{lead}}:=\frac1r\bigl(c\,n\,\rho_{t,0}-\mathbf J_{t,0}\bigr)$, so that the field formula of step 2.1 and the definition of $\mathbf V$ give $\mathbf E(rn,u+r/c)-\mathbf E_{\mathrm{lead}}(n,u,r)=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int_{B_a}(F_{\mathrm{tot}}-F_{\mathrm{lead}})\,dy$ by the linearity of the integral. Every source factor is bounded by its supremum on $\overline B_a\times[t_0,T]$, and (H1) makes $\rho$ and $\mathbf J$ vanish on the open set $\mathbb R^3\times((-\infty,t_0)\cup(T,\infty))$, hence makes all their partial derivatives vanish there too, so these suprema also dominate the retarded arguments $v_y+\delta_y$; the mean value theorem [F4] gives $|\rho_{t,\mathrm{ret}}-\rho_{t,0}|\le\Gamma_{\rho tt}|\delta_y|$ and $|\mathbf J_{t,\mathrm{ret}}-\mathbf J_{t,0}|\le\Gamma_{Jtt}|\delta_y|$: when the difference is nonzero, apply [F4] to its scalar projection onto the unit direction of that difference, and use Cauchy-Schwarz from [F12], and using $R^{-1}\le2/r$, $R^{-2}\le4/r^2$ and $|\mathbf N/R-n/r|\le|\mathbf N-n|/R+|1/R-1/r|\le13a/r^2$ yields $|F_{\mathrm{tot}}-F_{\mathrm{lead}}|\le\Gamma_E'/r^2$, with $\Gamma_E':=4c^2\Gamma_\rho+13ca\Gamma_{\rho t}+a^2\Gamma_{\rho tt}+3a\Gamma_{Jt}+a^2\Gamma_{Jtt}/c$ for all $r\ge2a$; hence, by the triangle inequality for the integral and the finiteness of $\lambda(B_a)$, $\bigl|\mathbf E(rn,u+r/c)-\mathbf E_{\mathrm{lead}}(n,u,r)\bigr|\le\Gamma_E/r^2$ with $\Gamma_E:=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\Gamma_E'\lambda(B_a)$. The same computation applied to $\mathbf B(rn,u+r/c)=-\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int_{B_a}\mathbf N\times[\mathbf J_{\mathrm{ret}}/R^2+\mathbf J_{t,\mathrm{ret}}/(cR)]dy$ and to $n\times\mathbf E_{\mathrm{lead}}/c=-\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int_{B_a}\frac1{cr}n\times\mathbf J_{t,0}\,dy$ gives $\bigl|\mathbf B(rn,u+r/c)-n\times\mathbf E_{\mathrm{lead}}(n,u,r)/c\bigr|\le\Gamma_B/r^2$, for $\Gamma_B:=\frac{\mu_0\lambda(B_a)}{4\pi}(4\Gamma_J+13a\Gamma_{Jt}/c+a^2\Gamma_{Jtt}/c^2)$. [step 1.2, step 2.3, F2, F3, F4, F12]

4.1 Smoothness of the far-zone moments: the map $(n,u)\mapsto\mathbf I(n,u)=\int_{B_a}\mathbf J(y,u+n\cdot y/c)\,dy$ and its partial derivative $\partial_u\mathbf I=\int_{B_a}\mathbf J_t(y,u+n\cdot y/c)\,dy=\mathbf V(n,u)$ are $C^\infty$ in $(n,u)\in\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R$: apply [F5] repeatedly in the directions $n_1,n_2,n_3,u$ with the fixed compact set $\overline B_a$, each differentiated integrand again being continuous on a neighbourhood of $\overline B_a$; the integrals over $B_a$ and $\overline B_a$ agree because the smooth sources and all their derivatives vanish on $|y|=a$ by continuity from $|y|>a$. In particular $n\mapsto|n\times\mathbf V(n,u)|^2$ is continuous for each fixed $u$. [step 3.2, F5, F6, F7, F12]

4.2 Pointwise flux asymptotics: by step 3.2 write $\mathbf E=\mathbf E_{\mathrm{lead}}+\boldsymbol\rho_E$ and $\mathbf B=n\times\mathbf E_{\mathrm{lead}}/c+\boldsymbol\rho_B$ at $(rn,u+r/c)$ with $|\boldsymbol\rho_E|\le\Gamma_E/r^2$, $|\boldsymbol\rho_B|\le\Gamma_B/r^2$; then $n\cdot\mathbf E_{\mathrm{lead}}=0$ and $\mathbf E_{\mathrm{lead}}\times(n\times\mathbf E_{\mathrm{lead}})=|\mathbf E_{\mathrm{lead}}|^2n$ by [F12], while $|\mathbf E_{\mathrm{lead}}|=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi r}|n\times\mathbf V(n,u)|$ because $|n\times(n\times\mathbf V)|=|n\times\mathbf V|$. Expanding the cross product bilinearly, the error terms are $O(r^{-3})$, so $r^2n\cdot\mathbf S(rn,u+r/c)=\frac{\mu_0}{16\pi^2c}|n\times\mathbf V(n,u)|^2+R(n,u,r)$ with $|R(n,u,r)|\le C/r$ for a constant $C$ depending only on $a,c,\mu_0$ and the source suprema, uniformly in $n\in S^2$ and $u\in[t_0,T]$. [step 3.2, F12, F2]

5.1 Flux limit: by [F13] the surface integral over the compact sphere of a bounded Borel function is finite and monotone, so for every $u\in[t_0,T]$ and $r\ge2a$ the difference $\int_{S^2}r^2n\cdot\mathbf S(rn,u+r/c)\,d\Omega(n)-\frac{\mu_0}{16\pi^2c}\int_{S^2}|n\times\mathbf V(n,u)|^2\,d\Omega(n)=\int_{S^2}R(n,u,r)\,d\Omega(n)$ has absolute value at most $\frac{C}{r}\int_{S^2}1\,d\Omega$, which tends to $0$; the function $n\mapsto|n\times\mathbf V(n,u)|^2$ is continuous by step 4.1, so its surface integral is the displayed finite constant. Hence the stated limit at fixed retarded time $u$ follows; no step compares different values of $u$, so the limit is asserted only at fixed retarded time. [step 4.2, step 4.1, F13, F2] ∎
