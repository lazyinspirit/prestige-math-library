## Definition

Let $A\xrightarrow{\varphi}B$ be a ring homomorphism of commutative rings
([[def-commutative-ring]]), so that $B$ is an $A$-algebra. Let $F$ be the free
$B$-module on the set underlying $B$ ([[def-left-and-right-modules]]), with
basis symbol $[b]$ for $b\in B$, and let $R\subseteq F$ be the $B$-submodule
generated by all elements

$$[b+b']-[b]-[b'],\qquad [bb']-b\,[b']-b'\,[b],\qquad [\varphi(a)]$$

with $b,b'\in B$ and $a\in A$. The **module of algebraic differentials** of $B$
over $A$, also called the module of **Kähler differentials**, is the quotient
$B$-module

$$\Omega_{B/A}:=F/R ,$$

and the map $\mathrm{d}=\mathrm{d}_{B/A}\colon B\to\Omega_{B/A}$,
$\mathrm{d}b:=[b]+R$, is the **universal $A$-derivation** of $B$ over $A$. By
construction $\mathrm{d}$ is additive, satisfies the Leibniz rule

$$\mathrm{d}(bb')=b\,\mathrm{d}b'+b'\,\mathrm{d}b\qquad(b,b'\in B),$$

and is **$A$-constant**, meaning $\mathrm{d}(\varphi(a))=0$ for every $a\in A$;
these are exactly the three families of relators above.

Now let $M$ be a $B$-module. An **$A$-derivation of $B$ into $M$** is a map
$D\colon B\to M$ that is additive, is $A$-constant in the sense that
$D(\varphi(a))=0$ for all $a\in A$, and satisfies the Leibniz rule
$D(bb')=b\,D(b')+b'\,D(b)$. The set of such maps is written
$\operatorname{Der}_A(B,M)$; it is a $B$-module under the pointwise operations,
since $M$ is a $B$-module.

Three conventions are part of the definition. First, $\Omega_{B/A}$ is
presented by the whole set $B$ of generators, so no finiteness of $B$ over $A$
and no finite generation, finite presentation or Noetherian hypothesis is
assumed anywhere in this definition. Second, $\mathrm{d}1=0$, because
$1_B=\varphi(1_A)$ makes $[1_B]$ one of the relators; consequently
$\mathrm{d}(-b)=-\mathrm{d}b$, $\mathrm{d}(b^{n})=n\,b^{n-1}\mathrm{d}b$ for
$n\ge1$ by induction on the Leibniz rule, and $\mathrm{d}(ab)=a\,\mathrm{d}b$
for $a\in A$, $b\in B$. Third, the extreme case $B=A$ (with $\varphi$ the
identity) gives $\Omega_{A/A}=0$: every $[b]$ is the relator $[\varphi(b)]$, so
the quotient is the zero module and the only $A$-derivation of $A$ into any
$A$-module is the zero map.
