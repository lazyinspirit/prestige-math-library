## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and Countable Choice as declared by the cited mathematical suppliers ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-countable-choice]]), the lossless isotropic scalar constant branch of [[post-em-material-closure]] with $\varepsilon,\mu>0$ and $\sigma=0$, the source-free smooth macroscopic equations of [[pthm-em-macroscopic-maxwell]] in the smooth branches, with $\rho_f=0$, $J_f=0$, and the explicitly declared weak first-order material equations in the weak cavity branches below, and the guide, cavity, PEC and reflection-class conventions of [[def-em-guide-cavity-geometry]].
(a) [Transverse profile completeness] For the cross-section $(0,a)\times(0,b)$, the families $\{h_{mn}\}_{m,n\ge0}$ and $\{e_{mn}\}_{m,n\ge1}$ of [[lem-em-rectangle-mode-profile-identities]] are orthogonal complete systems in $L^2$ of the rectangle ([[lem-em-rectangular-transverse-mode-completeness]]); The full scalar Neumann and Dirichlet families have these $L^2$ expansions. The physical TE candidate family excludes $h_{00}=1$ by the full Maxwell equations: the remaining $h_{mn}$ are complete in the zero-mean subspace $\{f\in L^2:\int_Rf=0\}$, not in all scalar $L^2$. The Dirichlet TM profiles retain their full scalar completeness. The constant Neumann scalar profile is retained in the mathematical basis but is not asserted to be a nonzero positive-frequency TE mode.
(b) [Rectangular cavity reflection class] For the box $Q=(0,a)\times(0,b)\times(0,d)$ with the reflection class and distributional constraints $\operatorname{div}E=\operatorname{div}B=0$ of [[def-em-guide-cavity-geometry]](c): the finite separated Fourier modes are dense in the class at every time; every initial datum in the class has a unique $C(\mathbb R;L^2)$ weak solution of the lossless first-order system $\partial_tE=(\varepsilon\mu)^{-1}\operatorname{curl}B$, $\partial_tB=-\operatorname{curl}E$ in the same class, with derivatives as in [[def-distributional-derivative]]; this lossless first-order distributional system is the explicitly adopted weak material evolution of the reflection model, not inferred from the smooth-only hypotheses of [[pthm-em-macroscopic-maxwell]]; along the solution the total stored energy $\int_Q u\,dx$ equals $\frac12\int_Q(\varepsilon|E|^2+|B|^2/\mu)\,dx$ and is constant, where the weak stored-energy density is defined almost everywhere by this nonnegative quadratic expression, consistent with the smooth local definition of [[pthm-em-restricted-material-energy]]. Its integrability and constancy are furnished by the mathematical reflection-class energy result; no nonlinear weak Poynting identity is asserted. For $C^1$-in-time solutions the four macroscopic equations with $D=\varepsilon E$, $B=\mu H$ hold distributionally.
(c) [Smooth PEC cavity] For a bounded $C^\infty$ cavity domain $\Omega$ and the PEC class $X,V,H$ of [[thm-em-waveguide-spectral-completeness]](a): the curl form on $V$ has a complete orthonormal $L^2$ eigenbasis $\{u_j\}$ with nonnegative eigenvalues $\lambda_j$ of finite multiplicity, $\lambda_j\to\infty$ whenever $H$ is infinite dimensional, and a finite-dimensional zero eigenspace that is retained; the eigen-equation $\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}u_j=\lambda_ju_j$ holds distributionally with the PEC trace, and each $u_j$ has a $C^\infty$ representative on the closure. Put $v_{\mathrm{wave}}=(\varepsilon\mu)^{-1/2}>0$. The associated second-order electric wave has the full supplied interface: for arbitrary initial displacement in $V$ and velocity in $H$, its displacement is in $C(\mathbb R;V)$, its velocity in $C(\mathbb R;H)$, and finite modal sums converge in these respective norms uniformly on compact time intervals. Its zero eigenmodes may be affine in time. Separately, let $J$ be an open time interval and let a strong first-order solution obey $E\in C^1(J;H)\cap C(J;V)$ and $B\in C^1(J;L^2(\Omega;\mathbb R^3))\cap C(J;H(\operatorname{curl}))$, with the first-order equations as $L^2$ identities and both distributional divergence constraints. Electric tangential-zero boundary data are encoded by $E(t)\in V$. The magnetic normal-zero condition is declared weakly by $\int_\Omega B(t)\cdot\nabla\psi=0$ for every smooth scalar $\psi$ on the closure; when $B\in C^1(\overline\Omega)$ this is $n\cdot B=0$, using $\operatorname{div}B=0$. No magnetic membership in the electric tangential-zero class $X$ is assumed. Then $a_j(t):=\langle E(t),u_j\rangle$ is $C^2$ and satisfies $a_j''+v_{\mathrm{wave}}^2\lambda_ja_j=0$. For $\lambda_j=0$, the admissible first-order coefficients satisfy $a_j'=0$ and are constant, with the zero eigenfields retained. This extra compatibility distinguishes first-order Maxwell data from arbitrary second-order $V\times H$ data; it does not remove the broader supplied second-order wave or its affine zero modes.
(d) [Scope] The theorem asserts completeness only for the transverse $L^2$ profiles and for the two declared cavity classes with their exact hypotheses; this lossless theorem does not itself supply the longitudinal expansion or dissipative evolution. Those approved additional classes are supplied separately by [[pthm-em-open-rectangular-guide-completeness]] and [[pthm-em-uniform-ohmic-modal-decay]], respectively. None of these statements supplies arbitrary lossy, dispersive, open-end scattering, non-smooth or exterior problems, or arbitrary non-rectangular guide problems beyond the explicitly declared smooth-cavity spectral class, no trace regularity for arbitrary $H(\operatorname{curl})$ fields, and no identification of the weak classes with every maximal tangential-trace realization. The zero eigenvalues and zero modes are retained throughout.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice and Countable Choice as declared by the cited suppliers ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-countable-choice]]); the lossless isotropic scalar constant branch of [[post-em-material-closure]] with $\varepsilon,\mu>0$, $\sigma=0$; the source-free smooth macroscopic equations of [[pthm-em-macroscopic-maxwell]] with $\rho_f=0$, $J_f=0$; the explicitly declared weak first-order material equations in the weak cavity branches; the guide, cavity, PEC and reflection-class conventions of [[def-em-guide-cavity-geometry]]; the fields of [[def-em-fields]].

[F1] Transverse completeness: for the cross-section $(0,a)\times(0,b)$ the families $\{h_{mn}\}_{m,n\ge0}$ and $\{e_{mn}\}_{m,n\ge1}$ of [[lem-em-rectangle-mode-profile-identities]] are orthogonal complete systems in $L^2$ of the rectangle, with the full-family expansions of [[lem-em-rectangular-transverse-mode-completeness]] (b) and [[thm-hilbert-space-fourier-expansion]]; the profile identities include $\int_Rh_{mn}h_{m'n'}=0$ unless $(m,n)=(m',n')$ and $\int_Rh_{00}^2=ab>0$, so $\int_Rh_{mn}=0$ for $(m,n)\ne(0,0)$ by pairing with $h_{00}=1$ ([[lem-em-rectangle-mode-profile-identities]] (c)); $L^2$ completeness supplies the Hilbert-space structure ([[lem-l-two-with-the-integral-pairing-is-a-hilbert-space]]).

[F2] Rectangular reflection class: [[lem-em-rectangular-pec-energy-completeness]] states that the finite separated Fourier modes are dense in the reflection class, that every initial datum of the class has a unique $C(\mathbb R;L^2)$ weak solution of $\partial_tE=(\varepsilon\mu)^{-1}\operatorname{curl}B$, $\partial_tB=-\operatorname{curl}E$ in the class, and that the energy $\frac12\int_Q(\varepsilon|E|^2+|B|^2/\mu)$ is constant along the solution; distributions and their derivatives are those of [[def-distributional-derivative]], and the class is the one of [[def-em-guide-cavity-geometry]] (c); the packaged rectangular branch of [[thm-em-waveguide-spectral-completeness]] (b) repeats these assertions.

[F3] Smooth PEC cavity (supplied by the completed in-run items): [[thm-em-waveguide-spectral-completeness]] (a) and [[lem-em-smooth-pec-cavity-spectral-closure]] state, under the full Axiom of Choice, that for a bounded $C^\infty$ domain $\Omega$ the graph-norm class $X$ of smooth fields on the closure with $n\times u=0$, its divergence-free subspace $V$ and $H=\overline V^{\,L^2}$ carry a complete orthonormal $L^2$ eigenbasis $\{u_j\}$ of the curl form with nonnegative finite-multiplicity eigenvalues $\lambda_j$, the distributional eigen-equation $\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}u_j=\lambda_ju_j$ with the PEC trace, $C^\infty$ eigenfields on the closure, the full-$X$ variational identity $\int_\Omega\operatorname{curl}u_j\cdot\operatorname{curl}v=\lambda_j\int_\Omega u_j\cdot v$ for every $v\in X$, and the associated second-order wave at $v_{\mathrm{wave}}=(\varepsilon\mu)^{-1/2}>0$ for arbitrary $V\times H$ data with $C_tV$ displacement and $C_tH$ velocity. Their statements are the premises used here, and the proof steps below name the supplier consumed at each use.

[F4] Paring tools: for $F\in H(\operatorname{curl})$, a compactly supported smooth test $\varphi$ satisfies the distributional identity $\langle\operatorname{curl}F,\varphi\rangle=\langle F,\operatorname{curl}\varphi\rangle$ ([[def-distributional-derivative]]); Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the pairings; a bounded smooth boundary has a collar with the inward normal coordinate, and the standard smooth step builds the cutoff $\eta_\delta$ used below ([[thm-euclidean-tubular-neighbourhood-theorem]], [[def-the-standard-smooth-step-function]], [[cor-cauchy-schwarz-inequality-for-l-two]]).

[F5] The smooth stored-energy density $u=\frac12(E\cdot D+B\cdot H)$ of [[pthm-em-restricted-material-energy]] reduces to $\frac12(\varepsilon|E|^2+\mu^{-1}|B|^2)$ in the lossless scalar branch.



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct: instantiate the transverse completeness and its zero-mean restriction, read the rectangular weak branch from its supplier and verify the macroscopic rewriting, then derive the first-order modal equation for the smooth cavity by a collar pairing and differentiate it once.

1.1 [Clause (a)] The families $\{h_{mn}\}$ and $\{e_{mn}\}$ are orthogonal complete systems in $L^2(R_{ab})$ by [F1], which gives the expansion statement. Each member with $(m,n)\ne(0,0)$ has zero integral over $R_{ab}$ by the orthogonality of [F1] against $h_{00}=1$, so the closed linear span $S$ of $\{h_{mn}:(m,n)\ne(0,0)\}$ is contained in the closed zero-mean subspace $Z=\{f\in L^2(R_{ab}):\int_{R_{ab}}f=0\}$; conversely, if $f\in Z$, the expansion theorem [F1] applied to the complete orthonormal family obtained by normalizing all the $h_{mn}$ writes $f$ as the norm limit of its finite partial sums, and the coefficient of $h_{00}$ is $\langle f,h_{00}\rangle/\|h_{00}\|^2=\int_{R_{ab}}f/ab=0$, so those partial sums use only indices $(m,n)\ne(0,0)$ and $f\in S$. Hence $S=Z$: the remaining Neumann profiles are complete in the zero-mean subspace and not in all of $L^2(R_{ab})$ because the constant function is orthogonal to them and nonzero. The constant $h_{00}=1$ stays in the full mathematical basis, while the physical TE candidate family excludes it by the Maxwell index restriction of [[pthm-em-rectangular-mode-candidates]] (c), so no nonzero positive-frequency TE reconstruction is asserted from the constant scalar profile; the Dirichlet profiles keep their full scalar completeness. [F1, algebra]

1.2 [Clause (b)] By [F2] the finite separated Fourier modes are dense in the rectangular reflection class at every time, every initial datum in the class has a unique $C(\mathbb R;L^2)$ weak solution of the lossless first-order system in the class, and the energy $\frac12\int_Q(\varepsilon|E|^2+|B|^2/\mu)$ is constant along the solution; this distributional system is the explicitly adopted weak material evolution of the reflection model. The energy equals $\int_Qu$ for the weak stored-energy density $u=\frac12(\varepsilon|E|^2+\mu^{-1}|B|^2)$ of [F5], which is defined almost everywhere and nonnegative; its integrability and constancy are furnished by the supplier, and no nonlinear weak Poynting identity is asserted. Finally, the four macroscopic equations with $D=\varepsilon E$ and $H=B/\mu$ follow distributionally from the two evolution equations and the divergence constraints of the class: $\operatorname{div}D=\varepsilon\operatorname{div}E=0$ and $\operatorname{div}B=0$ are the class constraints, $\operatorname{curl}E=-\partial_tB$ is the second evolution equation, and $\operatorname{curl}H=\mu^{-1}\operatorname{curl}B=\mu^{-1}(\varepsilon\mu)\partial_tE=\partial_tD$ uses the first evolution equation $\partial_tE=(\varepsilon\mu)^{-1}\operatorname{curl}B$. [F2, F5, algebra]

1.3 [Clause (c), spectral content] Assume the full Axiom of Choice as in [F3], let $\Omega$ be a bounded $C^\infty$ cavity and let $X,V,H$, $\{u_j\}$, $\lambda_j\ge0$ be the classes and eigenbasis of [F3]. Then the curl form on $V$ has the complete orthonormal $L^2$ eigenbasis with nonnegative finite-multiplicity eigenvalues tending to infinity when $H$ is infinite dimensional and a finite-dimensional zero eigenspace retained; the eigen-equation holds distributionally with the PEC trace, each $u_j$ has a $C^\infty$ representative on the closure, and the full-$X$ variational identity holds for every $v\in X$. With $v_{\mathrm{wave}}=(\varepsilon\mu)^{-1/2}>0$, the associated second-order electric wave has displacement in $C(\mathbb R;V)$ and velocity in $C(\mathbb R;H)$ for arbitrary initial data in $V\times H$, finite modal sums converge in these norms uniformly on compact times, and its zero eigenmodes may be affine in time. These are the spectral and wave assertions of [F3], taken with the exact hypotheses of the suppliers. [F3]

2.1 [Clause (c), first-order modal equation] Let $J$ be an open time interval and let a strong first-order solution obey the hypotheses of the Statement: $E\in C^1(J;H)\cap C(J;V)$, $B\in C^1(J;L^2)\cap C(J;H(\operatorname{curl}))$, with the first-order equations holding as $L^2$ identities and both distributional divergence constraints, the magnetic normal-zero condition $\int_\Omega B(t)\cdot\nabla\psi=0$ for every smooth scalar $\psi$ on the closure, and $E(t)\in V$ encoding the electric tangential-zero data; no magnetic membership in $X$ is assumed. Put $a_j(t):=\langle E(t),u_j\rangle$. To pair $\operatorname{curl}B$ against the smooth eigenfield $u_j$, use the collar cutoff of [F4]: write $s$ for the inward normal coordinate in a tubular neighbourhood of the compact boundary, so that $|n\times u_j|\le Cs$ in the collar because $u_j$ is smooth on the closure with $n\times u_j=0$, and let $\eta_\delta$ be the standard smooth step with $\eta_\delta=0$ for $s\le\delta$ and $1$ for $s\ge2\delta$. Then $\eta_\delta u_j\to u_j$ and $\operatorname{curl}(\eta_\delta u_j)\to\operatorname{curl}u_j$ in $L^2$ as $\delta\to0$, since the derivative-cutoff term is supported on a layer of volume $O(\delta)$ and is bounded by $C/\delta$ times $O(s)$; the distributional identity of [F4] on the compactly supported smooth test $\eta_\delta u_j$ gives $\langle\operatorname{curl}B,\eta_\delta u_j\rangle=\langle B,\operatorname{curl}(\eta_\delta u_j)\rangle$, and Cauchy–Schwarz with the two limits gives $\langle\operatorname{curl}B,u_j\rangle=\langle B,\operatorname{curl}u_j\rangle$ for the actual graph-regular field $B$. Hence $a_j'=\langle E',u_j\rangle=v_{\mathrm{wave}}^2\langle\operatorname{curl}B,u_j\rangle=v_{\mathrm{wave}}^2\langle B,\operatorname{curl}u_j\rangle$ on $J$. The assumed $B\in C^1(J;L^2)$ permits one further derivative: $a_j''=v_{\mathrm{wave}}^2\langle B',\operatorname{curl}u_j\rangle=-v_{\mathrm{wave}}^2\langle\operatorname{curl}E,\operatorname{curl}u_j\rangle=-v_{\mathrm{wave}}^2\lambda_j\langle E,u_j\rangle$, where the last equality is the full-$X$ eigenvariational identity of [F3] tested at $v=E(t)\in V\subset X$; thus $a_j''+v_{\mathrm{wave}}^2\lambda_ja_j=0$, and $a_j$ is $C^2$ because $E$ and $B$ are $C^1$ in their norms. If $\lambda_j=0$, the same identity at $v=u_j$ gives $\|\operatorname{curl}u_j\|_2^2=0$, so $\operatorname{curl}u_j=0$ and $a_j'=v_{\mathrm{wave}}^2\langle B,\operatorname{curl}u_j\rangle=0$: the electric zero modes are constant under first-order Maxwell evolution, while the unrestricted second-order wave of step 1.3 retains its affine zero modes. This distinguishes first-order Maxwell data from arbitrary second-order $V\times H$ data; the pairing imposed no magnetic electric-type trace, and the divergence constraints and the declared weak magnetic normal condition remain part of the model. [step 1.3, F3, F4]

3.1 [Clause (d)] Clause (a) is step 1.1, clause (b) is step 1.2, and clause (c) is steps 1.3 and 2.1. Completeness is asserted only for the transverse $L^2$ profiles and for the two declared cavity classes with their exact hypotheses; this lossless theorem does not itself supply the longitudinal expansion or the dissipative evolution, which are supplied separately by [[pthm-em-open-rectangular-guide-completeness]] and [[pthm-em-uniform-ohmic-modal-decay]]. None of the statements supplies arbitrary lossy, dispersive, open-end scattering, non-smooth or exterior problems, or arbitrary non-rectangular guide problems beyond the declared smooth-cavity spectral class; no trace regularity for arbitrary $H(\operatorname{curl})$ fields and no identification of the weak classes with every maximal tangential-trace realization is asserted, and the zero eigenvalues and zero modes are retained throughout. The inherited Axiom of Choice and Countable Choice cover the spectral, Fourier and collar suppliers, and no lossy or open-domain step was made. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.1, F3] ∎
