## Statement

Use the rank-one lattice-screening data for $p\ge2$, and write $W(p)$ for the vertex subalgebra generated by $\omega,F,H,E$ in $V_L$. Its conformal vector is $\omega$ with central charge $1-6(p-1)^2/p$. The deformed fields on $\mathcal M$ are an actual ordinary $W(p)$-module action satisfying the full module Jacobi identity. Put $h=(2-p)/4$. The cyclic space $P_+^{p-1}=W(p)e^{\alpha/2}$ is an admissible grading-restricted generalized logarithmic module. Its lowest generalized conformal-weight space has basis $u=e^{\alpha/2}$ and $w=e^{\alpha/2-\alpha/p}$, with
$$\widetilde L(0)u=hu+w,\qquad \widetilde L(0)w=hw.$$
Moreover $M_1=W(p)e^{-\alpha/2}$ is an admissible submodule of $P_+^{p-1}$, and $\widetilde L(0)$ is semisimple on $M_1$. This statement does not identify its submodules or quotient endpoints and does not assert the nonsplit extension.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The explicit lattice-screening data and integer $p\ge2$. For oscillator degree $d$ and charge $t$ write $\operatorname{wt}(f e^{t\alpha})=d+pt^2-(p-1)t$. The notation $W(p)$ here uses the four-generator definition; equality with the full screening kernel is not required.

[F1] [[def-cft-triplet-lattice-screening-data]] gives the explicit fields and sector-zero/sector-one spaces.

[F2] [[lem-cft-triplet-square-zero-lattice-jacobi]] proves that $\mathcal A$ is a vertex algebra, $I$ is square-zero, and $\mathcal M$ is an actual $\mathcal A$-module with the full Jacobi identity.

[F3] [[lem-cft-triplet-square-zero-screening-deformation]] proves the full module Jacobi identity for the deformed action on every vertex subalgebra of $\ker v_{(0)}$.

## Proof

1.1 Write $q=(p-1)/(2p)$. The field of $\omega$ is $\mathcal T(z)={:}\alpha(z)^2{:}/(4p)+q\partial_z\alpha(z)$. The contraction $\alpha(z)\alpha(w)\sim2p/(z-w)^2$, obtained directly from the oscillator commutator, gives the singular product $\mathcal T(z)\mathcal T(w)\sim(1/2-12pq^2)/(z-w)^4+2\mathcal T(w)/(z-w)^2+\partial_w\mathcal T(w)/(z-w)$. Here the double contraction of the quadratic terms is $1/2$ in fourth order; the two improvement terms give $q^2\partial_z\partial_w(2p/(z-w)^2)=-12pq^2/(z-w)^4$; the mixed cubic poles cancel, leaving $2q\partial\alpha(w)/(z-w)^2+q\partial^2\alpha(w)/(z-w)$. The remaining quadratic single contractions give $2{:}\alpha(w)^2{:}/(4p)$ and its derivative. Taking mode residues proves the Virasoro relations with central charge $1-24pq^2=1-6(p-1)^2/p$. In particular
$$L(n)=\frac1{4p}\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}{:}\alpha_j\alpha_{n-j}{:}-q(n+1)\alpha_n.$$
The formula for $n=-1$ acts on $f e^{t\alpha}$ as $\sum_{k\ge1}k x_{k+1}\partial_{x_k}f+t x_1f$, the translation in [F2]. The $n=0$ formula is $L(0)=\sum_{k\ge1}k x_k\partial_{x_k}+pt^2-(p-1)t$. Thus it is diagonal with the displayed weights on both spaces. In $V_L$ these weights are nonnegative integers: $pn^2-(p-1)n\ge0$ for every integer $n$, with equality only at $n=0$. Its weight spaces are finite, since the quadratic bounds the possible charges and the polynomial space in each oscillator degree is finite. [given, F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 The same contraction calculation gives $\mathcal T(z)\mathcal E_t(w)\sim (pt^2-(p-1)t)\mathcal E_t(w)/(z-w)^2+\partial_w\mathcal E_t(w)/(z-w)$. Indeed the quadratic double contraction contributes $pt^2$, the improvement contributes $-(p-1)t$, and the quadratic single contraction contributes $t{:}\alpha\mathcal E_t{:}=\partial\mathcal E_t$, as follows by differentiating its explicit exponential formula. In particular $v=e^{-\alpha/p}$ is primary of weight one. To check the screening kernel on the generators directly, apply its exponential to $\omega$: the annihilation exponential substitutes $x_1+2z^{-1}$ and $x_2+2z^{-2}$ into $x_1^2/(4p)+qx_2$. The result before its creation exponential is $\omega+(x_1/p)z^{-1}+z^{-2}$; the creation exponential begins $1-(x_1/p)z+\cdots$. Hence $v_{(1)}\omega=v$, $v_{(0)}\omega=0$ and $v_{(n)}\omega=0$ for $n\ge2$. Also $Y(v,z)F$ starts with $z^2$, so $v_{(0)}F=0$. Finally $Qv=x_1e^{(1-1/p)\alpha}=\frac p{p-1}T e^{(1-1/p)\alpha}$. The zero-mode commutator formula from the adjoint Jacobi identity gives $[Q,v_{(0)}]=(Qv)_{(0)}=0$, since the zero mode of a derivative is zero. Therefore $v_{(0)}H=v_{(0)}E=0$. The same commutator formula makes $v_{(0)}$ a derivation of every state product, so its kernel is a vertex subalgebra containing $\omega,F,H,E$; it contains $W(p)$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Apply [F3] to $\mathcal A,I,v$ and $S=W(p)$. It supplies the full module Jacobi identity on $\mathcal M$, not merely the mode commutation relations. Step 2.1 gives $\Delta(z)\omega=\omega+z^{-1}v$, whence $\widetilde L(n)=L(n)+v_{(n)}$ and $\widetilde L(0)=L(0)+v_{(0)}$. The screening $v_{(0)}$ has weight zero and square zero on $\mathcal M$, since it sends sector zero to sector one and annihilates sector one. For the two module charge strings, subtracting $h$ from the weight of the charge vacuum gives respectively $pn^2+n$ and $pn^2-n$, for $n\in\mathbb Z$. These are nonnegative integers, vanishing only at $n=0$. Thus $\mathcal M=\bigoplus_{d\ge0}\mathcal M(d)$, with each degree finite and $L(0)=(h+d)I$ on it. Since $v_{(0)}$ preserves degree, these are exactly the generalized $\widetilde L(0)$-weight spaces. Counting weights in the explicit fields gives $a_{(k)}\mathcal M(d)\subset\mathcal M(d+\operatorname{wt}a-k-1)$ for homogeneous $a\in V_L$: the charge shift, oscillator multiplications and derivatives have exactly these weight changes. In a correction $v_{(n)}a$ has weight $\operatorname{wt}a-n$ and the extra factor $z^{-n}$ increases the mode index by $n$, leaving the same shift. This is the admissible grading for the deformed fields. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Put $u=e^{\alpha/2}$ and $w=e^{\alpha/2-\alpha/p}$. The screening exponential on $u$ starts with $z^{-1}w$, so $v_{(0)}u=w$; it annihilates $w$ by the sector rule. Both have original weight $h$. Therefore $\widetilde L(0)u=hu+w$ and $\widetilde L(0)w=hw$. Since the cyclic submodule containing $u$ is stable under $\widetilde L(0)$, it also contains $w$. The degree-zero space of $\mathcal M$ is precisely $\mathbb Cu\oplus\mathbb Cw$ by step 3.1, so this is exactly the degree-zero space of $P_+^{p-1}$. Every invariant subspace of the direct sum of finite generalized eigenspaces of $\widetilde L(0)$ decomposes as its intersections with them: for each vector, projection onto any one of its finitely many generalized eigencomponents is a polynomial in $\widetilde L(0)$, obtained by the coprime factors $(X-(h+d))^2$. Thus the cyclic submodule inherits the admissible grading, finite generalized weight spaces and lower bound. The nonzero nilpotent action on its degree-zero space proves it logarithmic in [F4]. [F1, F4, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 The correction to $F$ vanishes because $v_{(n)}F=0$ for all $n\ge0$. The leading term of $Y_{\mathcal M}(F,z)u$ is $z^{-p}e^{-\alpha/2}$, so $\widetilde F_{(p-1)}u=e^{-\alpha/2}$ and $M_1\subset P_+^{p-1}$. It inherits the same grading by the polynomial-projection argument in step 4.1. To check semisimplicity on $M_1$, the screening exponential on its cyclic vector has leading power $z^1$, giving $v_{(0)}e^{-\alpha/2}=0$. For $a\in W(p)$ the ordinary module zero-mode commutator gives $[v_{(0)},Y_{\mathcal M}(a,z)]=Y_{\mathcal M}(v_{(0)}a,z)=0$. Every deformation correction has state in $I$, which is also annihilated by the adjoint screening zero mode; hence this commutation holds for the deformed fields too. Therefore $v_{(0)}$ vanishes on the entire cyclic space $M_1$. On it $\widetilde L(0)=L(0)$, and the explicit oscillator-weight operator is diagonal. This proves the claimed semisimplicity and locates the Jordan block in $P_+^{p-1}$ rather than $M_1$. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
