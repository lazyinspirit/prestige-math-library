## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice ($\mathrm{AC}_\omega$), used through the cited published suppliers ([[def-countable-choice]]). Let $c>0$, $t_0\in\mathbb R$, $T>t_0$, let $a,b\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^3)$ and let $h\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^3\times[t_0,T])$ in the extension sense that $h$ is the restriction of a $C^\infty$ function on $\mathbb R^3\times J$ for some open interval $J\supset[t_0,T]$ ([[def-ck-euclidean-maps-and-diffeomorphisms]]). Write $W:=c^{-2}\partial_t^2-\Delta$, so that $c^2W=\Box_c$ is the wave operator of speed $c$ ([[def-laplacian-of-a-c2-function]], [[def-wave-equation-cauchy-data-and-wave-speed]]). For $\varphi\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^3)$ and $s\ge0$ let $H_\varphi(x,s):=s\,M_{cs}\varphi(x)$, where $M_r\varphi(x)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{S^2}\varphi(x+rn)\,d\Omega(n)$ is the spherical mean of [[lem-em-spherical-mean-wave-identity]]. Define, with $s=t-t_0$, $$u(x,t):=\partial_sH_a(x,s)+H_b(x,s)+c^2\int_{t_0}^{t}H_{h(\cdot,v)}(x,t-v)\,dv,\qquad (x,t)\in\mathbb R^3\times[t_0,T],$$ where $h(\cdot,v)$ denotes the map $x\mapsto h(x,v)$ and $\partial_sH_a$ is the partial derivative of $H_a$ in its second argument, evaluated at $s=t-t_0$. Then:

(a) $u$ is of class $C^\infty$ on $\mathbb R^3\times[t_0,T]$ in the extension sense, $Wu=h$ on $\mathbb R^3\times(t_0,T)$ (indeed on the open neighbourhood of the closed time interval on which the extension of $h$ is defined), $u(\cdot,t_0)=a$ and $\partial_tu(\cdot,t_0)=b$.

(b) Uniqueness: every $v\in C^2(\mathbb R^3\times[t_0,T])$ in the extension sense with $Wv=h$ on $\mathbb R^3\times(t_0,T)$, $v(\cdot,t_0)=a$ and $\partial_tv(\cdot,t_0)=b$ equals $u$.

(c) Domain of dependence: let $\tilde a,\tilde b\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^3)$, $\tilde h\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^3\times[t_0,T])$ and let $\tilde u$ be the corresponding solution. If $\tilde a=a$ and $\tilde b=b$ on the closed ball $\overline B_{c(t_*-t_0)}(x_*)$ and $\tilde h=h$ on the closed backward cone $K(x_*,t_*):=\{(y,v)\in\mathbb R^3\times[t_0,t_*]:|y-x_*|\le c(t_*-v)\}$ ([[def-forward-and-backward-wave-cones-domain-of-dependence-and-influence]]), then $\tilde u(x_*,t_*)=u(x_*,t_*)$ for every $x_*\in\mathbb R^3$ and $t_*\in[t_0,T]$.

(d) Compact support: if $a$ vanishes off a compact $K_a\subset\mathbb R^3$, $b$ vanishes off a compact $K_b$, and $h(\cdot,v)$ vanishes off a fixed compact $K_h$ for every $v\in[t_0,T]$, then for every $t\in[t_0,T]$ the function $u(\cdot,t)$ has compact spatial support: if $K_a\cup K_b\cup K_h\subseteq\overline B_\rho(0)$ for some $\rho>0$ (which exists, [[thm-heine-borel-rn]]), then $u(x,t)=0$ whenever $|x|>\rho+c(t-t_0)$.

No boundary condition on a finite spatial domain, no finite-total-energy hypothesis, and no decay of $a,b,h$ at infinity is assumed or asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Countable Choice $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$; a speed $c>0$; times $t_0<T$; data $a,b\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^3)$; a function $h$ of class $C^\infty$ on $\mathbb R^3\times[t_0,T]$ in the extension sense, that is, the restriction of a $C^\infty$ function on $\mathbb R^3\times J$ for some open interval $J\supset[t_0,T]$; the operators $W=c^{-2}\partial_t^2-\Delta$ and $\Box_c=\partial_t^2-c^2\Delta$; and the surface measure $d\Omega$ on the unit sphere $S^2\subset\mathbb R^3$ of total mass $4\pi$.

[F1] A map is of class $C^k$ when every word iterated coordinate partial derivative of order at most $k$ exists and is continuous, and of class $C^\infty$ when it is $C^k$ for every $k$; the Laplacian is $\Delta f=\operatorname{div}\nabla f=\sum_{i<3}\partial_i\partial_if$ ([[def-ck-euclidean-maps-and-diffeomorphisms]], [[def-laplacian-of-a-c2-function]]).

[F2] Finite sums, scalar multiples and products of $C^k$ maps are $C^k$ and composites of composable $C^k$ maps are $C^k$ ([[thm-ck-euclidean-maps-closed-under-algebra-and-composition]]); the chain rule holds for total derivatives and continuous first partial derivatives imply total differentiability ([[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]], [[thm-continuous-partial-derivatives-imply-total-differentiability]]); mixed second partials of a $C^2$ function commute ([[thm-clairaut-schwarz-mixed-partials]]); and sums, scalar multiples and products of differentiable real functions differentiate by the usual rules ([[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]]).

[F3] Spherical-mean family of a smooth spacetime function: with $h$ as given and $d\Omega$ of total mass $4\pi$, the family $m(x,s,v):=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{S^2}h(x+sn,v)\,d\Omega(n)$ is of class $C^\infty$ on $\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R\times[t_0,T]$ in the extension sense, and every word iterated partial derivative is obtained by differentiating the composed integrand under the sphere integral; the statement is local, assumes no decay, compact support or boundedness of $h$, and uses uniform bounds only on compact parameter sets ([[lem-em-spherical-mean-family-smoothness]]).

[F4] Spherical means of a fixed smooth datum: for $f\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^3)$ and $M_rf(x)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{S^2}f(x+rn)\,d\Omega(n)$, $r\ge0$, the map $r\mapsto M_rf(x)$ is smooth for $r\ge0$ and satisfies the Euler-Poisson-Darboux identity $\partial_r^2M_rf+\frac{2}{r}\partial_rM_rf=\Delta M_rf$ for $r>0$, and $H_f(x,t):=t\,M_{ct}f(x)$, $t\ge0$, satisfies $\partial_t^2H_f=c^2\Delta H_f$, $H_f(\cdot,0)=0$ and $\partial_tH_f(\cdot,0)=f$; all identities are local and assume no decay of $f$ ([[lem-em-spherical-mean-wave-identity]]).

[F5] Parameter integrals: if $K\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ is compact, measurable and of finite Lebesgue measure, $a<b$, and $f,\partial_tf$ are continuous on a neighbourhood of $K\times[a,b]$, then $t\mapsto\int_Kf(x,t)\,dx$ is differentiable on $[a,b]$ with derivative $\int_K\partial_tf(x,t)\,dx$; the same holds, under the $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ convention of [[def-surface-integral-on-a-compact-c-one-hypersurface]], with surface measure on a fixed compact $C^1$ hypersurface in place of $K$; moving parameter sets and singularities crossing the integration set are excluded ([[lem-em-parameter-integrals]]).

[F6] Differentiating an integral with moving endpoints: if $I$ is an open interval, $\alpha,\beta\in C^1(I)$ with $\alpha<\beta$, $J$ is an open interval containing the closures of the intervals $[\alpha(t),\beta(t)]$ and $F:I\times J\to\mathbb R$ is continuous with continuous $\partial_tF$, then $G(t)=\int_{\alpha(t)}^{\beta(t)}F(t,y)\,dy$ is $C^1$ on $I$ with $G'(t)=F(t,\beta(t))\beta'(t)-F(t,\alpha(t))\alpha'(t)+\int_{\alpha(t)}^{\beta(t)}\partial_tF(t,y)\,dy$ ([[lem-derivative-of-an-integral-with-moving-endpoints]]). Continuous integrands on nondegenerate compact intervals are Riemann integrable and, under $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, their Riemann and Lebesgue integrals agree ([[thm-continuous-on-a-rectangle-is-riemann-integrable]], [[lem-bounded-borel-riemann-integrands-on-boxes-have-equal-lebesgue-integrals]]); thus this rule applies to the continuous Lebesgue integrands used here.

[F7] A continuous function on a compact metric space is uniformly continuous ([[thm-heine-cantor-metric]]). For integrable functions, the Lebesgue integral is linear and $|\int f|\le\int|f|$ ([[thm-linearity-of-the-lebesgue-integral-on-l-one]], [[thm-integral-triangle-inequality]]); the nonnegative integral is monotone ([[prop-order-and-scalar-rules-for-the-nonnegative-integral]]) and $\lambda_1([0,1])=1$ under $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ ([[thm-lebesgue-measure-of-a-box-of-every-kind]]). Consequently $|\int_0^1(f-g)\,dz|\le\sup_{z\in[0,1]}|f(z)-g(z)|$ for continuous $f,g$.

[F8] Closed boxes in $\mathbb R^m$ are compact in the Euclidean metric ([[thm-heine-borel-rn]]), and a continuous real-valued function on a nonempty compact metric space attains a maximum and a minimum ([[thm-extreme-value-metric]]).

[F9] The reflection $n\mapsto-n$ of $S^2$ preserves the surface measure $d\Omega$ and $\int_{S^2}n\,d\Omega(n)=0$; orthogonal transformations of $\mathbb R^3$ preserve the surface measure ([[lem-first-moment-of-the-unit-sphere-vanishes]], [[lem-euclidean-chart-measure-agrees-with-polar-surface-measure]]).

[F10] For the Euclidean norm on $\mathbb R^m$, $\lVert u+v\rVert_2\le\lVert u\rVert_2+\lVert v\rVert_2$ and $\lVert\lambda u\rVert_2=|\lambda|\,\lVert u\rVert_2$, so also $\lVert u\rVert_2\ge\lVert u+v\rVert_2-\lVert v\rVert_2$; and $B_r(x_0)=\{x:|x-x_0|<r\}$, $\overline B_r(x_0)=\{x:|x-x_0|\le r\}$ are the open and closed Euclidean balls ([[thm-cauchy-schwarz-and-the-euclidean-norm]], [[def-euclidean-inner-product]]).

[F11] Wave operator and Cauchy data: $\Box_c=\partial_t^2-c^2\Delta$ and $W=c^{-2}\partial_t^2-\Delta$, so $\Box_c=c^2W$; a classical solution on a time slab is $C^2$ on the open slab and satisfies the equation there, and Cauchy data are the limits as $t\downarrow t_0$; the closed backward cone with vertex $(x_*,t_*)$ and speed $c$ is $K(x_*,t_*)=\{(y,v):t_0\le v\le t_*,\ |y-x_*|\le c(t_*-v)\}$ with base ball $\overline B_{c(t_*-t_0)}(x_*)$ ([[def-wave-equation-cauchy-data-and-wave-speed]], [[def-forward-and-backward-wave-cones-domain-of-dependence-and-influence]]).

[F12] Local uniqueness and domain of dependence: under $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, if $u,v\in C^2$ solve $\Box_cu=f$ and $\Box_cv=g$ on a neighbourhood of the closed backward cone $K^-(x_0,t_0)$ with $t_0>0$, if $f=g$ on the cone and $u(\cdot,0)=v(\cdot,0)$, $u_t(\cdot,0)=v_t(\cdot,0)$ on the base ball $B_{ct_0}(x_0)$, then $u=v$ on $K^-(x_0,t_0)$ ([[thm-domain-of-dependence-and-local-uniqueness]]).

[A1] The Axiom of Countable Choice $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$: every at most countable family of nonempty sets has a choice function ([[def-countable-choice]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The setting. Let $h$ also denote its $C^\infty$ extension to $\mathbb R^3\times J$, put $\Omega:=\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R\times J$, and let $S^2=\{n\in\mathbb R^3:\langle n,n\rangle=1\}$ carry the surface measure $d\Omega$ of total mass $4\pi$ with normalised probability $\nu:=d\Omega/4\pi$ of [F9]. For a fixed datum $\varphi\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^3)$ put $m_\varphi(x,s,v):=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{S^2}\varphi(x+sn)\,d\Omega(n)$ (independent of $v$), so that $H_\varphi(x,t):=t\,m_\varphi(x,ct,v_0)$ for any fixed $v_0\in J$ is the $H_\varphi$ of the statement; for the datum $h$ put $m(x,s,v):=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{S^2}h(x+sn,v)\,d\Omega(n)$ on $\Omega$, so that $m_\varphi=m_{h_\varphi}$ for the spacetime datum $h_\varphi(x,v):=\varphi(x)$. Define, for $(x,t)\in\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R$ and $s:=t-t_0$, $$u_h(x,t):=\partial_sH_a(x,s)+H_b(x,s),\qquad H(x,\sigma;v):=\sigma\,m(x,c\sigma,v),$$ and, for $t\in[t_0,T]$, $u_s(x,t):=c^2\int_{t_0}^{t}H(x,t-v;v)\,dv$ and $u:=u_h+u_s$; here $\partial_sH_a(x,s)$ is the derivative of $H_a$ in its second argument at $(x,s)$. All surface integrals are read under the $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ convention carried by [F3] and [F5]. [given, A1, F3, F4, F5, F9, F11]

1.2 Properties of the family. By [F3] applied to $h$ and to each $h_\varphi$, the families $m$ and $m_\varphi$ are of class $C^\infty$ on their domains in the extension sense, and every word iterated partial derivative equals the sphere integral of the corresponding derivative of the composed integrand. In particular $m(x,0,v)=h(x,v)$ and $m_\varphi(x,0,v)=\varphi(x)$, because the sphere integral of the constant $\varphi(x)$ is $\varphi(x)\cdot4\pi/4\pi$; consequently $H_\varphi(\cdot,0)=0$ and $\partial_tH_\varphi(\cdot,0)=\varphi$ by the product rule [F2]. The reflection substitution $n\mapsto-n$ with [F9] gives $m(x,-s,v)=m(x,s,v)$ and $m_\varphi(x,-s,v)=m_\varphi(x,s,v)$: both families are even in $s$. Hence the Euler-Poisson-Darboux identity $$\partial_s^2m+\frac{2}{s}\partial_sm=\Delta m\qquad(s\ne0)$$ holds on $\Omega\setminus\{s=0\}$ and likewise with $m_\varphi$ in place of $m$: for $s>0$ it is [F4] applied to the fixed smooth datum $x\mapsto h(x,v)$ (respectively $x\mapsto\varphi(x)$), and for $s<0$ it follows from the evenness just proved, since for an even $C^\infty$ function of $s$ the derivative $\partial_s$ is odd and $\partial_s^2$ is even while $\Delta$ differentiates only the $x$-variable. [given, F2, F3, F4, F9]

2.1 The homogeneous blocks solve the wave equation with the prescribed data. Fix $\varphi\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^3)$. By [F2] the function $H_\varphi(x,t)=t\,m_\varphi(x,ct,v_0)$ is $C^\infty$ on $\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R$. Differentiating in $t$ and using the identity of step 1.2 at $s=ct$, one has $\partial_t^2H_\varphi=2c\,\partial_sm_\varphi+c^2t\,\partial_s^2m_\varphi$ and $c^2\Delta H_\varphi=c^2t\,\Delta m_\varphi=c^2t\,\partial_s^2m_\varphi+2c\,\partial_sm_\varphi$ for $t\ne0$; at $t=0$ both sides vanish, since $H_\varphi(\cdot,0)=0$ gives $\Delta H_\varphi(\cdot,0)=0$ and $\partial_sm_\varphi(\cdot,0)=0$ by evenness. Hence $\partial_t^2H_\varphi=c^2\Delta H_\varphi$ on $\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R$. Differentiating this equation in $t$ and commuting $\partial_t$ with $\Delta$ through [F2] gives the homogeneous equation also for $K_\varphi:=\partial_tH_\varphi$; its data are $K_\varphi(\cdot,0)=\varphi$ by step 1.2 and $\partial_tK_\varphi(\cdot,0)=c^2\Delta H_\varphi(\cdot,0)=0$. [step 1.2, F1, F2, F4]

2.2 Smoothness of the source term and its extension to a neighbourhood. Put $\theta:=t-t_0$ and, for $\theta$ in an open interval $(\theta_1,\theta_2)$ containing $[0,T-t_0]$ and small enough that $t_0+\theta(1-z)\in J$ for all $z\in[0,1]$, define $$\tilde u_s(x,t):=c^2\theta^2\int_0^1z\,m(x,c\theta z,t_0+\theta(1-z))\,dz.$$ By step 1.2 and [F2] the integrand $A(x,\theta,z):=c^2z\,m(x,c\theta z,t_0+\theta(1-z))$ extends to a $C^\infty$ function on $\mathbb R^3\times(\theta_1,\theta_2)\times[0,1]$ whose word derivatives are continuous there. Iterating [F5] in the parameter directions $x_j$ and $\theta$ over the fixed compact set $[0,1]$ shows that every word derivative in $(x,\theta)$ of $\int_0^1A(x,\theta,z)\,dz$ exists and equals $\int_0^1D^wA(x,\theta,z)\,dz$; each of these integrals is jointly continuous in $p=(x,\theta)$: on a closed spatial-time box $Q\times K$ inside the parameter domain, $D^wA$ is continuous on the compact product $Q\times K\times[0,1]$, so [F7] gives uniform continuity there and $$\left|\int_0^1D^wA(p,z)\,dz-\int_0^1D^wA(p_0,z)\,dz\right|\le\sup_{z\in[0,1]}|D^wA(p,z)-D^wA(p_0,z)|\longrightarrow0\quad(p\to p_0).$$ Every parameter point has such a box with that point in its interior by openness, and compactness is [F8]. Hence $\int_0^1A\,dz$ is $C^\infty$ on $\mathbb R^3\times(\theta_1,\theta_2)$, and so is $\tilde u_s=\theta^2\int_0^1A\,dz$ by [F2]. For $\theta>0$ the substitution $v=t_0+\theta(1-z)$, $dv=-\theta\,dz$, in the defining integral of $u_s$ gives $\tilde u_s=u_s$, while $\tilde u_s(x,t_0)=0=u_s(x,t_0)$; hence $\tilde u:=u_h+\tilde u_s$ is a $C^\infty$ extension of $u$ to the open neighbourhood $\mathbb R^3\times(t_0-\delta,T+\delta)$ of the closed slab, and $u$ is of class $C^\infty$ on $\mathbb R^3\times[t_0,T]$ in the extension sense. [step 1.2, F2, F5, F7, F8, algebra, choose]

3.1 The Duhamel identity for a smooth datum. Let $g$ be smooth on $\mathbb R^3\times J_g$ with $J_g$ an open interval containing $t_0$, and let $m_g$ be its signed-radius spherical-mean family. Set $H_g(x,\sigma;v):=\sigma m_g(x,c\sigma,v)$ and, for $\tau>0$ with $t_0+\tau\in J_g$, $$J_g^*(x,\tau):=\int_0^\tau H_g(x,\tau-r;t_0+r)\,dr=\int_0^\tau\sigma m_g(x,c\sigma,t_0+\tau-\sigma)\,d\sigma.$$ The equality follows by $\sigma=\tau-r$. For each fixed $v$, step 2.1 applied to $g(\cdot,v)$ gives $\partial_\sigma^2H_g=c^2\Delta_xH_g$, $H_g(x,0;v)=0$ and $\partial_\sigma H_g(x,0;v)=g(x,v)$. At fixed $x$, the integrand $F(\tau,r)=H_g(x,\tau-r;t_0+r)$ is smooth on $\mathbb R\times(J_g-t_0)$ by [F3] and [F2], including negative $\tau-r$. Locally at each admissible $\tau_*>0$, choose an open interval $I$ about $\tau_*$ with $\inf I>0$ and $[0,\sup I]\subset J_g-t_0$; thus [F6] applies on a genuine rectangular neighbourhood with endpoints $0$ and $\tau$. Two applications give $\partial_\tau J_g^*=\int_0^\tau\partial_\sigma H_g(x,\tau-r;t_0+r)\,dr$ and $\partial_\tau^2J_g^*=g(x,t_0+\tau)+\int_0^\tau\partial_\sigma^2H_g(x,\tau-r;t_0+r)\,dr$. At each fixed $\tau$, [F5] permits two spatial derivatives through the fixed $r$-integral, hence $\partial_\tau^2J_g^*-c^2\Delta_xJ_g^*=g(x,t_0+\tau)=m_g(x,0,t_0+\tau)$. The positive-radius interval was arbitrary; no time derivative of the source is needed for these two endpoint differentiations. [step 1.2, step 2.1, F2, F3, F5, F6, algebra, choose]

3.2 The initial data. At $t=t_0$ one has $\tilde u(x,t_0)=\partial_sH_a(x,0)+H_b(x,0)+0=a(x)+0+0$, since $\partial_sH_a(x,0)=m_a(x,0,v_0)=a(x)$ and $H_b(x,0)=0$ by step 2.1, while $\tilde u_s(x,t_0)=0$ by step 2.2. Likewise $\partial_t\tilde u(x,t_0)=\partial_s^2H_a(x,0)+\partial_sH_b(x,0)+\partial_t\tilde u_s(x,t_0)=0+b(x)+0$: here $\partial_s^2H_a(x,0)=c^2\Delta H_a(x,0)=0$ and $\partial_sH_b(x,0)=b(x)$ by step 2.1, and $\partial_t\tilde u_s(x,t_0)=0$ by the product rule [F2] applied to $\theta^2\int_0^1A(x,\theta,z)\,dz$ at $\theta=0$ in step 2.2. This proves the initial-data clause of (a). [step 2.1, step 2.2, F2, algebra]

4.1 The wave equation on the whole neighbourhood. Let $\tilde H$ denote the $C^\infty$ extension of $h$ to $\mathbb R^3\times J$ and let $\tilde u=u_h+\tilde u_s$ be the extension of step 2.2, and put $R(x,t):=\partial_t^2\tilde u-c^2\Delta\tilde u-c^2\tilde H(x,t)$ on $\mathbb R^3\times(t_0-\delta,T+\delta)$; $R$ is continuous there by step 2.2 and [F2]. For $\theta>0$, [F5] gives $\Delta u_s=c^2\int_{t_0}^t\Delta_xH(x,t-v;v)\,dv$ and step 3.1 with $g=h$ and $J_g^*=u_s/c^2$ gives $\partial_t^2u_s=c^2h+c^2\Delta u_s$ on $\mathbb R^3\times(t_0,T+\delta)$; the homogeneous part satisfies $\partial_t^2u_h=c^2\Delta u_h$ on all of $\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R$ by step 2.1 and [F2], so $R=0$ on $\mathbb R^3\times(t_0,T+\delta)$. For $\theta<0$ put $\mu:=-\theta>0$ and $\hat h(x,v):=h(x,2t_0-v)$, an extension-sense smooth datum on an interval around $t_0$, with family $\hat m(x,s,v):=m(x,s,2t_0-v)$; the substitution $\sigma=\mu z$, $z\in[0,1]$, together with the evenness of step 1.2 gives $\tilde u_s(x,t_0-\mu)=c^2\int_0^\mu\sigma\,\hat m(x,c\sigma,t_0+\mu-\sigma)\,d\sigma$, so step 3.1 applied to $\hat h$ gives $\partial_t^2\tilde u_s-c^2\Delta\tilde u_s=c^2\hat m(x,0,t_0+\mu)=c^2h(x,t_0-\mu)$ on $\mathbb R^3\times(t_0-\delta,t_0)$, while the homogeneous part satisfies the homogeneous equation there by step 2.1; hence $R=0$ on $\mathbb R^3\times(t_0-\delta,t_0)$. The two open pieces cover every time except $t_0$; continuity gives $R(x,t_0)=0$ as well, so $\partial_t^2\tilde u=c^2\Delta\tilde u+c^2\tilde H$, that is $W\tilde u=\tilde H$, on the open neighbourhood $\mathbb R^3\times(t_0-\delta,T+\delta)$; restricting gives $Wu=h$ on $\mathbb R^3\times(t_0,T)$ and indeed on the open neighbourhood of the closed time interval on which the extension of $h$ is defined. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 2.2, F2, F5, F11, algebra]

5.1 Uniqueness. Let $v$ be of class $C^2$ on $\mathbb R^3\times[t_0,T]$ in the extension sense with $Wv=h$ on $\mathbb R^3\times(t_0,T)$, $v(\cdot,t_0)=a$ and $\partial_tv(\cdot,t_0)=b$, and form the function $\tilde v$ equal to $v$ for $t\ge t_0$ and to $u$ for $t\le t_0$: the pieces agree in value and in all derivatives through order two at $t=t_0$: their spatial derivatives are those of $a$, their time derivatives equal $b$, and their mixed derivatives equal $\partial_i b$; the second time derivative there being $\partial_t^2v(\cdot,t_0)=\partial_t^2u(\cdot,t_0)=c^2\Delta a+c^2h(\cdot,t_0)$ by the equation of step 4.1 and the data, and each second partial derivative is continuous on its side and has the stated common value, so $\tilde v$ is $C^2$ on a neighbourhood of the closed slab; then $w:=u-\tilde v$ is $C^2$ on that neighbourhood, satisfies $\Box_cw=0$ on each of the open half-slabs $t>t_0$ (where both $u$ and $\tilde v$ solve $Wu=h$) and $t<t_0$ (where the two pieces coincide), and since $\Box_cw$ is continuous it vanishes at $t=t_0$ as well, so $\Box_cw=0$ on $\mathbb R^3\times(t_0-\delta,T)$ for some $\delta>0$; no equation for an arbitrary extension of $v$ beyond $T$ is used; also $w(\cdot,t_0)=0$ and $\partial_tw(\cdot,t_0)=0$ on all of $\mathbb R^3$. Fix $(x_*,t_*)$ with $t_0<t_*<T$ and apply [F12] in the translated time coordinates $\tau:=t-t_0$ to the pair $(w,0)$ on the closed backward cone with vertex $(x_*,t_*)$: the vertex time is $t_*-t_0>0$, both functions are $C^2$ on a neighbourhood of the closed cone (contained in the neighbourhood of the closed slab on which $w$ is defined), the sources agree on the cone, and the Cauchy data agree on the base ball $\overline B_{c(t_*-t_0)}(x_*)$; hence $w=0$ on the cone, so $u(x_*,t_*)=\tilde v(x_*,t_*)=v(x_*,t_*)$. Since $(x_*,t_*)$ is arbitrary in $\mathbb R^3\times(t_0,T)$, this gives $u=v$ on the open slab. The initial data already give equality at $t_0$, and continuity at $T$ gives equality there by taking $t\uparrow T$ at each fixed $x$. Thus $u=v$ on the whole closed slab, which is clause (b). [step 4.1, step 3.2, F2, F11, F12]

5.2 Domain of dependence. Let $\tilde a,\tilde b\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^3)$, let $\tilde h$ be of class $C^\infty$ on $\mathbb R^3\times[t_0,T]$ in the extension sense, and let $\tilde u$ be the solution produced by the construction of steps 2.2 and 4.1 applied to $(\tilde a,\tilde b,\tilde h)$; by those steps $\tilde u$ is $C^\infty$ on $\mathbb R^3\times[t_0,T]$ in the extension sense, satisfies $W\tilde u=\tilde h$ on an open neighbourhood of the closed slab and has data $\tilde a,\tilde b$ at $t_0$. Suppose $\tilde a=a$ and $\tilde b=b$ on the closed ball $\overline B_{c(t_*-t_0)}(x_*)$ and $\tilde h=h$ on the closed cone $K(x_*,t_*)$ of [F11]. If $t_*=t_0$, the base ball is $\{x_*\}$ and the initial values give $u(x_*,t_0)=a(x_*)=\tilde a(x_*)=\tilde u(x_*,t_0)$. Now assume $t_*>t_0$. Then $w:=u-\tilde u$ is $C^2$ on a neighbourhood of the closed slab with $\Box_cw=c^2(h-\tilde h)$ on each open half-slab, hence on the whole neighbourhood because both sides are continuous, it vanishes together with its time derivative at $t_0$ on the base ball, and $h-\tilde h=0$ on the cone; applying [F12] in translated time coordinates to the pair $(w,0)$ therefore gives $w=0$ on the closed backward cone, and in particular $u(x_*,t_*)=\tilde u(x_*,t_*)$. This is clause (c). [step 4.1, step 3.2, F11, F12]

6.1 Compact support. Assume that $a$ vanishes off a compact $K_a\subseteq\mathbb R^3$, that $b$ vanishes off a compact $K_b$, that $h(\cdot,v)$ vanishes off a fixed compact $K_h$ for every $v\in[t_0,T]$, and that $K_a\cup K_b\cup K_h\subseteq\overline B_\rho(0)$ for some $\rho>0$, which exists because every compact subset of $\mathbb R^3$ is bounded by [F8]. Fix $t\in[t_0,T]$ and $x$ with $|x|>\rho+c(t-t_0)$ and compare $u$ with the solution $\tilde u$ of the construction for the data $\tilde a=\tilde b=0$, $\tilde h=0$, namely $\tilde u=0$. Every $y\in\overline B_{c(t-t_0)}(x)$ satisfies $|y|\ge|x|-|y-x|\ge|x|-c(t-t_0)>\rho$ by the reverse triangle inequality [F10], so $y\notin K_a\cup K_b$ and $a(y)=b(y)=0=\tilde a(y)=\tilde b(y)$; similarly every $(y,v)\in K(x,t)$ satisfies $|y|\ge|x|-|y-x|\ge|x|-c(t-v)\ge|x|-c(t-t_0)>\rho$, so $y\notin K_h$ and $h(y,v)=0=\tilde h(y,v)$. The hypotheses of clause (c), proved in step 5.2, are therefore met at the vertex $(x,t)$ and give $u(x,t)=\tilde u(x,t)=0$. This is clause (d). [step 5.2, F8, F10, F11, algebra]

7.1 Conclusion. Steps 2.2 and 4.1 show that $u$ is of class $C^\infty$ on $\mathbb R^3\times[t_0,T]$ in the extension sense and that $Wu=h$ on $\mathbb R^3\times(t_0,T)$, indeed on the open neighbourhood of the closed time interval on which the extension of $h$ is defined; step 3.2 gives $u(\cdot,t_0)=a$ and $\partial_tu(\cdot,t_0)=b$, completing clause (a); clause (b) is step 5.1, clause (c) is step 5.2 and clause (d) is step 6.1. The argument assumed no boundary condition on a finite spatial domain, no finite-total-energy hypothesis and no spatial decay or compact support of the data; only local bounds on compact parameter sets were used. [step 2.2, step 4.1, step 3.2, step 5.1, step 5.2, step 6.1, F1] ∎
