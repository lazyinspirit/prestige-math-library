## Example

Let $k$ be a field and let
$$A=k\llbracket x,y\rrbracket/(x^2,xy),\qquad \mathfrak m=(x,y)A.$$
Then $A$ is a one-dimensional Noetherian local ring of depth $0$, so it is
not Cohen--Macaulay.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The field and quotient ring in the example.

[F1] Depth is the supremum of regular-sequence lengths in the maximal ideal when its product with the module is proper ([[def-depth-with-respect-to-an-ideal]], [[def-regular-sequence-on-a-module]]).

[F2] A nonzero finite local module is Cohen--Macaulay when its depth equals its support dimension ([[def-cohen-macaulay-local-module-and-ring]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Every class in $A$ has a unique expression $f(y)+ax$, where $f\in k\llbracket y\rrbracket$ and $a\in k$: all monomials involving $x$ except $x$ itself belong to $(x^2,xy)$. Thus $x\ne0$, $x^2=yx=0$, and the projection $A\to k\llbracket y\rrbracket$ has kernel $kx$. An element is a unit precisely when $f(0)\ne0$: the series $f$ is then invertible by recursive coefficient multiplication, and the square-zero correction is inverted by $1-z$. If $f(0)=0$, its image in the quotient $A/(x,y)=k$ is zero, so it is not a unit. Hence $A$ is local with maximal ideal $(x,y)$. [given, algebra]

2.1 Every nonzero series in $k\llbracket y\rrbracket$ is $y^m$ times a unit, by its least nonzero coefficient. Consequently every nonzero ideal in that ring is $(y^m)$, taking the least order among its nonzero elements. For an ideal $J$ of $A$, its projection is therefore principal. If nonzero, lift one generator to $z\in J$; subtracting suitable $k\llbracket y\rrbracket$-multiples of $z$ from elements of $J$ leaves elements of $J\cap kx$. This intersection is either zero or $kx$, since it is a subspace of a one-dimensional $k$-space. Thus $J$ is generated by $z$ and, if needed, $x$; when its projection is zero it is generated by $x$ or is zero. Every ideal is finitely generated, so $A$ is Noetherian by [[def-noetherian-ring-and-module]]. [step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Every prime of $A$ contains the nilpotent $x$, so prime ideals correspond to those of $A/(x)=k\llbracket y\rrbracket$. The latter has exactly the primes $(0)$ and $(y)$ by the ideal description in step 2.1, and they are distinct. Hence $\dim A=1$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Every element of $\mathfrak m$ annihilates the nonzero $x$. None is therefore an $A$-regular element. Since $A/\mathfrak m=k\ne0$, the empty sequence is regular and no positive-length regular sequence in $\mathfrak m$ exists. Thus $\operatorname{depth}A=0$ by [F1]. The support of the regular module is the whole spectrum, of dimension one, so [F2] shows that $A$ is not Cohen--Macaulay. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
