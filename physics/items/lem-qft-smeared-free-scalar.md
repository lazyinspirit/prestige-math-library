## Statement

Assume Countable Choice [[def-countable-choice]], let $\mu\ge0$, let
$H_\mu$ and $K_\mu$ be as in [[def-qft-scalar-mass-shell]], and form
$\mathcal F_s(H_\mu)$ with vacuum $\Omega$ and finite-particle domain
$\mathcal D_{\rm fin}$. For $f\in\mathscr S(\mathbb R^4)$ define on
$\mathcal D_{\rm fin}$
$$\Phi_\mu(f)=a(K_\mu\overline f)+a^\dagger(K_\mu f).$$
This is a complex-linear map of test functions into operators
$\mathcal D_{\rm fin}\to\mathcal D_{\rm fin}$. For real $f$ it is symmetric
and equals $\sqrt2\,\Phi_{\rm Segal}(K_\mu f)|_{\mathcal D_{\rm fin}}$, where the Segal field of
[[lem-qft-segal-weyl-domain]] is
$\Phi_{\rm Segal}(h)=\overline{(a^\dagger(h)+a(h))/\sqrt2}$.
Thus its Weyl unitary is
$W_\mu(f):=W_{\rm Segal}(\sqrt2 K_\mu f)$ and, for real $f,g$,
$$W_\mu(f)W_\mu(g)= e^{-i\,\operatorname{Im}\langle K_\mu f,K_\mu g\rangle_2}W_\mu(f+g).$$
For every real $f$ the vacuum Weyl expectation is
$$\langle\Omega,W_\mu(f)\Omega\rangle_2 =e^{-\|K_\mu f\|_2^2/2}.$$
Here the inner product is second-variable-linear as fixed in
[[def-qft-fock-and-test-spaces]]. The vacuum has zero one-point function and
two-point function
$$\langle\Omega,\Phi_\mu(f)\Phi_\mu(g)\Omega\rangle_2 =\langle K_\mu\overline f,K_\mu g\rangle_2,$$
which obeys $\langle\Omega,\Phi_\mu(\overline f)\Phi_\mu(f)\Omega\rangle_2=\|K_\mu f\|_2^2\ge0$ for every complex test $f$ (ordinary diagonal positivity holds for real tests); higher vacuum moments of ordered real smeared
fields obey the CCR pairing recursion and hence the free-field Wick pairing
rule. The field is a weak operator-valued tempered distribution on $\mathcal D_{\rm fin}$: every matrix coefficient $f\mapsto\langle\Psi,\Phi_\mu(f)\Theta\rangle_2$ for $\Psi,\Theta\in\mathcal D_{\rm fin}$ is a continuous complex-linear functional on Schwartz space. Its adjoint satisfies $\Phi_\mu(f)^*|_{\mathcal D_{\rm fin}}=\Phi_\mu(\overline f)$, and its normalized vacuum defines a positive state on the finite polynomial $*$-algebra generated by these fields.

The relative-coordinate two-point tempered distribution is
$$W_\mu(z)=\int_{H_\mu^+}e^{-ip\circ z}\,d\nu_\mu(p),$$
understood by pairing with Schwartz tests. Its translation-invariant bidistribution is the shell integral of $e^{-ip\circ x}e^{ip\circ y}$; on product tests it is the displayed vacuum covariance.

For $g=(a,\Lambda)$, let $f_g$ and $U_\mu(g)$ be the affine test pullback
and one-particle action of [[lem-qft-scalar-poincare-action]]. Their Fock
lifts implement the same covariance for $\Phi_\mu$ on $\mathcal D_{\rm fin}$
and for the bounded Weyl operators.

For translations, the joint spectral measure on Fock space is the
second-quantized multiplier measure with one-particle vector
$(\omega_\mu(\mathbf k),-\mathbf k)$. Its support is contained in the closed
future cone $\{(E,\mathbf P):E\ge|\mathbf P|\}$; the vacuum is an eigenvector
at $(0,\mathbf0)$. These are conditional mathematical model deductions.
No point field, empirical observation or measurement claim is made.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice, the scalar shell conventions, second-variable-linear
Fock conventions, the QFT-MF01 draft suppliers at their inspected statements,
and the mathematical Poincare action.

[A1] Countable Choice is the choice strength assumed by the Fock construction
and the second-quantized spectrum result. [[def-countable-choice]]

[F1] The one-particle pairing is
$\langle u,v\rangle_2=\int\overline u\,v\,d\nu_\mu$; Fock creation is linear
and annihilation is conjugate-linear in their one-particle argument, with the
usual vacuum and finite-particle sectors.
[[def-qft-fock-and-test-spaces]]

[F2] On $\mathcal D_{\rm fin}$, creation and annihilation satisfy the bosonic
CCR, are mutual adjoints between adjacent sectors, and creation/annihilation
maps preserve finite-particle vectors.
[[lem-qft-fock-ccr-car]]

[F3] The inspected Segal convention is
$\Phi_{\rm Segal}(h)=\overline{(a^\dagger(h)+a(h))/\sqrt2}$ and
$W(h)W(k)=e^{-i\operatorname{Im}\langle h,k\rangle_2/2}W(h+k)$; its vacuum
expectation is $e^{-\|h\|^2/4}$ and its one-parameter groups generate these
closed fields.
[[lem-qft-segal-weyl-domain]]

[F4] The shell map is $K_\mu f(p)=\int f(x)e^{ip\circ x}d^4x$ on the future
shell with density $d^3\mathbf k/[(2\pi)^3 2\omega_\mu]$.
[[def-qft-scalar-mass-shell]]

[F5] The shell measure is sigma-finite and invariant under every proper
orthochronous Lorentz transformation.
[[lem-qft-mass-shell-measure-invariant]]

[F6] $H_\mu$ is separable and the range of $K_\mu$ on Schwartz tests is dense.
[[lem-qft-mass-shell-test-range-dense]]

[F7] A one-particle unitary has a unitary Fock lift; the lift intertwines
creation and annihilation, and strong continuity lifts to Fock space.
[[lem-qft-fock-unitary-lift]]

[F8] For a sigma-finite one-particle measure and a finite measurable real
vector multiplier, the second-quantized operators have the sector-sum PVM,
exact multiplier domains and the vacuum eigenvector at zero.
[[lem-qft-second-quantized-spectrum]]

[F9] The shell restriction $K_\mu$ is a continuous map from Schwartz tests
into $H_\mu$, and annihilates the Klein–Gordon test operator.
[[lem-qft-mass-shell-test-restriction-continuity]]

[F10] The one-particle Poincare maps are strongly continuous unitaries and
$U_\mu(g)K_\mu f=K_\mu f_g$.
[[lem-qft-scalar-poincare-action]]

[F11] [[def-qft-fock-and-test-spaces]] defines the sector insertion and contraction formulas with factors $\sqrt{n+1}$ and $\sqrt n$. [[lem-nrqm-composites-bounded-tensor-maps]] bounds tensor extensions by the product of factor norms, and [[lem-nrqm-composites-permutation-sector-projections]] makes the symmetrizers orthogonal projections with norm at most one.

[F12] [[cor-fourier-transform-is-a-topological-automorphism-of-schwartz-space]] and [[def-schwartz-space-and-its-seminorms]]: the full Fourier map is continuous on Schwartz space; the fixed reflection/rescaling in [F4] preserves this. Complex conjugation preserves the defining seminorms. Restriction along a fixed injective linear map preserves Schwartz decay and continuity by its coordinate bounds.

## Proof

1.1 Since $K_\mu f,K_\mu\overline f\in H_\mu$ by [F9], the field formula is
defined on $\mathcal D_{\rm fin}$. The first term $a(K_\mu\overline f)$ is
complex-linear in $f$ because complex conjugation is followed by the
conjugate-linear dependence of $a$; the second is linear because both
$a^\dagger$ and $K_\mu$ are linear. Both terms preserve $\mathcal D_{\rm fin}$
by [F1,F2]. For real $f$, the shell vectors coincide, so
$\Phi_\mu(f)=a(K_\mu f)+a^\dagger(K_\mu f)
=\sqrt2\,\Phi_{\rm Segal}(K_\mu f)|_{\mathcal D_{\rm fin}}$. It is symmetric on the common
finite-particle domain by [F2]. Substituting $h=\sqrt2K_\mu f$ and $k=\sqrt2K_\mu g$ into [F3]'s
Weyl relation gives the stated scalar phase, and its vacuum Gaussian gives
$\langle\Omega,W_\mu(f)\Omega\rangle_2=e^{-\|K_\mu f\|_2^2/2}$.
[A1,F1,F2,F3,F4,F6,F9,given,algebra]

2.1 For $g=(a,\Lambda)$, [F10] gives the one-particle representation and
$U_\mu(g)K_\mu f=K_\mu f_g$. Since the affine action is real,
$U_\mu(g)K_\mu\overline f=K_\mu\overline{f_g}$. The Fock lift [F7]
intertwines creation and annihilation, so the field formula in step 1.1 gives
$$\Gamma_s(U_\mu(g))\Phi_\mu(f)\Gamma_s(U_\mu(g))^{-1} =\Phi_\mu(f_g)$$
on $\mathcal D_{\rm fin}$. The explicit Segal Weyl formula [F3] and the same
lift give bounded Weyl covariance. Strong continuity of the Fock lifts
follows from [F7] and [F10]. [A1,F3,F7,F10,step 1.1,given,algebra]

2.2 Since $a(K_\mu\overline f)\Omega=0$ and
$\langle\Omega,a^\dagger(h)\Psi\rangle_2=0$, the vacuum one-point function
vanishes. Expanding the two fields, only
$a(K_\mu\overline f)a^\dagger(K_\mu g)\Omega$ contributes to the vacuum
pairing; CCR [F2] makes that value
$\langle K_\mu\overline f,K_\mu g\rangle_2$. Taking the first test to be $\overline f$ and the second $f$ gives $\|K_\mu f\|^2\ge0$; for real $f$ this is the ordinary diagonal. For an ordered product of $n$ real smeared fields,
write the first field as creation plus annihilation. The creation part
vanishes against the left vacuum; commute the annihilation part successively
past each later field using [F2]. Each commutator is the corresponding
two-point pairing, and the remaining annihilator kills the right vacuum.
This yields the recursive sum over its possible partner and proves the
Wick pairing rule by induction on $n$. [A1,F1,F2,F4,step 1.1,given,algebra]

3.1 Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\|C_h\|\le\|h\|$ for $C_h(v)=\langle h,v\rangle_2$. The tensor-extension bound and orthogonal symmetrizer in [F11] therefore give $\|a_n(h)\|\le\sqrt n\|h\|$ and $\|a_n^\dagger(h)\|\le\sqrt{n+1}\|h\|$, the latter also using $\|h\otimes v\|=\|h\|\|v\|$ from the product tensor pairing. Let $\Theta$ have sectors only through $N$. These bounds and orthogonality of distinct output sectors imply $\|\Phi_\mu(f)\Theta\|\le(\sqrt N\|K_\mu\overline f\|+\sqrt{N+1}\|K_\mu f\|)\|\Theta\|$. Continuity of $K_\mu$ [F9] and conjugation [F12], followed by Hilbert Cauchy–Schwarz, makes every stated matrix coefficient continuous; complex linearity is step 1.1. Mutual adjoints [F2] give $\langle\Phi_\mu(f)\Psi,\Theta\rangle_2=\langle\Psi,\Phi_\mu(\overline f)\Theta\rangle_2$ for finite-particle vectors. Thus every such $\Theta$ lies in the adjoint domain with the asserted restricted adjoint action. Finite-particle invariance permits reversing every finite polynomial product by this identity, so for $\omega(B)=\langle\Omega,B\Omega\rangle_2$ one has $\omega(B^*B)=\|B\Omega\|^2\ge0$ and $\omega(I)=1$. [A1,F1,F2,F9,F11,F12,step 1.1,step 2.2,algebra]

3.2 For a Schwartz test $\varphi(z)$, define $G_\varphi(p)=\int\varphi(z)e^{-ip\circ z}\,d^4z$; for $F(x,y)\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R^8)$ define $G_F(p)=\int F(x,y)e^{-ip\circ x}e^{ip\circ y}\,d^4x\,d^4y$. By [F12], these are continuous Schwartz transforms, the second followed by the injective linear frequency restriction $L$ determined by the displayed phases. Injectivity in finite dimensions gives $|Lp|\ge c|p|$ for some $c>0$; each derivative of a restricted transform is a fixed finite linear combination of its ambient derivatives at $Lp$. These bounds control every weighted restricted derivative by finitely many ambient Schwartz seminorms, proving the claimed restriction continuity and decay. In either case $|G(p)|\le q_4(G)(1+|p|)^{-4}$, with $q_4$ a continuous Schwartz seminorm. The shell integral of $(1+|p|)^{-4}$ is finite by [F4]: its radial behavior is $O(r)$ at the massless apex, $O(r^2)$ near zero when $\mu>0$, and $O(r^{-3})$ at infinity. Therefore the integrals of $G_\varphi$ and $G_F$ against $d\nu_\mu$ define continuous complex-linear tempered distributions. On product tests $F(x,y)=f(x)g(y)$ the inner integral is $\overline{K_\mu\overline f(p)}K_\mu g(p)$; its shell integral is exactly the covariance from step 2.2. This proves the relative and bidistribution interfaces without assigning point operators. [A1,F4,F12,step 2.2,algebra]

4.1 The one-particle translation phase is
$e^{i(\omega_\mu(\mathbf k)a^0-\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf a)}$, so its real
generator vector is $\varpi(\mathbf k)=(\omega_\mu(\mathbf k),-\mathbf k)$.
It is finite and measurable. The measure is sigma-finite by [F5], so [F8]
applies and gives the joint PVM and vacuum at zero. On an $n$-particle
sector the joint multiplier is $\sum_{r=1}^n
(\omega_\mu(\mathbf k_r),-\mathbf k_r)$. Since
$|\sum_r\mathbf k_r|\le\sum_r|\mathbf k_r|\le\sum_r\omega_\mu(\mathbf k_r)$,
every such value lies in the closed future cone; the vacuum value does too.
Thus the joint spectral measure is supported there, as claimed.
[A1,F4,F5,F8,given,algebra] ∎
