## Example

Assume AC and let $n\ge2$. For each fixed variance $\lambda=\sigma^2>0$, let $\widetilde P_{\mu,\lambda}=N(\mu,\lambda)^{\otimes n}$ be the full product law on $\mathbb R^n$, with mean parameter $\mu\in\mathbb R$ and independent coordinates. Use the common sample space
$D_n:=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:s(x)>0\}$, where
$\bar x=n^{-1}\sum_i x_i$ and
$s(x):=\sqrt{(n-1)^{-1}\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)^2}$ is the nonnegative square root, with the trace Borel sigma-algebra; write $P_{\mu,\lambda}$ for the restriction of $\widetilde P_{\mu,\lambda}$ to this sample space. Let $\nu=n-1$, and write $t_{\nu,u}$ for the unique $u$-quantile of the $t_\nu$ law. For $0<\alpha<1$, the interval
$$I(X)=\left[\bar X-t_{\nu,1-\alpha/2}\frac{S}{\sqrt n},\;\bar X+t_{\nu,1-\alpha/2}\frac{S}{\sqrt n}\right]$$
has exact coverage $P_{\mu,\lambda}\{\mu\in I(X)\}=1-\alpha$, for every $\mu\in\mathbb R$ and every fixed $\lambda>0$. The same formula works for all unknown variances.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, $n\ge2$, $0<\alpha<1$, and for each $\lambda>0$ the iid normal experiment indexed by $\mu\in\mathbb R$.

[A1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: AC is the axiom assumed by the normal-law construction and the two Gaussian suppliers used here.

[F1] [[thm-normal-mean-residual-independence-and-exact-t-pivot]]: for an iid $N(\mu,\sigma^2)$ sample with $n\ge2$, $S>0$ almost surely and $(\bar X-\mu)/(S/\sqrt n)$ has the $t_{n-1}$ law.

[F2] [[lem-student-t-and-f-cdfs-are-continuous-and-strictly-increasing]]: for $\nu\ge1$, the $t_\nu$ CDF is continuous, strictly increasing, symmetric about zero, and has limits zero and one, so each $u\in(0,1)$ has a unique quantile.

[F3] [[def-confidence-set-and-coverage]]: a confidence-set procedure has measurable membership sections, and its coverage at a parameter is the probability of the corresponding section.

[F4] [[def-pivot]]: a family of measurable maps, one for each parameter, is a pivot when every map has the same pushforward probability law.

[F5] [[thm-pivot-inversion-exact-coverage]]: inversion of a pivot acceptance set of probability $1-\alpha$ gives exact coverage $1-\alpha$ at every parameter.

[F6] [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]: the Borel sigma-algebra is generated by the open sets; on $D_n$ we use the trace of the Borel sigma-algebra of $\mathbb R^n$.

[F7] [[def-statistical-experiment]]: an experiment consists of a measurable sample space, a parameter set, and one probability law on that space for each parameter.

## Verification

1.1 For all $x,y\in\mathbb R^n$, $|\bar x-\bar y|\le\max_i|x_i-y_i|\le\|x-y\|_2$. The residual map $r(x)=x-\bar x\mathbf1$ is linear and orthogonal to $\mathbf1$, so Pythagoras gives $\|r(x-y)\|_2\le\|x-y\|_2$; the reverse triangle inequality then gives $|s(x)-s(y)|\le\|x-y\|_2/\sqrt{n-1}$. Hence $s$ is continuous, $D_n=\{x:s(x)>0\}$ is open, and its trace Borel sets form a sigma-algebra by [F6]. By [F1], every full law $\widetilde P_{\mu,\lambda}$ gives $D_n$ probability one, so its restriction $P_{\mu,\lambda}$ remains a probability measure; [F7] therefore gives the stated experiment for each fixed $\lambda>0$. [given, A1, F1, F6, F7]

1.2 Put $c=t_{\nu,1-\alpha/2}$, which exists uniquely by [F2]. Symmetry and continuity give $F_{t_\nu}(0)=1/2$; strict increase and $1-\alpha/2>1/2$ imply $c>0$. Since $F_{t_\nu}(c)=1-\alpha/2$, symmetry gives $F_{t_\nu}(-c)=\alpha/2$; continuity means neither endpoint is an atom, so $P(-c\le T_\nu\le c)=1-\alpha$. [given, F2, algebra]

1.3 For each candidate mean $\eta\in\mathbb R$, define $q_\eta(x)=(\bar x-\eta)/(s(x)/\sqrt n)$ on $D_n$. Its denominator is positive there, so it is continuous and hence measurable on the subspace $D_n$ by the open-set definition of its trace Borel sigma-algebra [F6]. For every Borel $B\subseteq\mathbb R$, the restricted probability satisfies $P_{\eta,\lambda}\{q_\eta\in B\}=\widetilde P_{\eta,\lambda}\{x\in D_n:q_\eta(x)\in B\}$, and [F1] gives this value as the common $t_\nu$ probability because the full law assigns probability one to $D_n$. Thus $(q_\eta)_{\eta\in\mathbb R}$ is a pivot by [F4]. [given, A1, F1, F4, F6]

2.1 Inverting $B=[-c,c]$ gives $\{\eta:q_\eta(x)\in B\}=[\bar x-cs(x)/\sqrt n,\bar x+cs(x)/\sqrt n]=I(x)$ by solving the two inequalities, and each membership section is measurable because $q_\eta$ is measurable. Step 1.2 gives the pivot mass $\nu(B)=1-\alpha$; the pivot-inversion theorem [F5] and confidence-set definition [F3] therefore give exact coverage $1-\alpha$ for every mean and every fixed variance. [given, F3, F4, F5, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
