## Example

Let $(F(X),i)$ be a free group on a set $X$, and let
$U:\mathbf{Grp}\to\mathbf{Set}$ be the underlying-set functor. Then $F(X)$
represents the functor

$$G\longmapsto\mathbf{Set}(X,U(G)).$$

The representing natural isomorphism is

$$\mathbf{Grp}(F(X),G)\longrightarrow\mathbf{Set}(X,U(G)),\qquad \phi\longmapsto U(\phi)\circ i.$$

For a singleton $X=\{x\}$, the reduced-word model $F(X)$ is infinite cyclic,
generated by the one-letter word $x$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A set $X$, a free group $(F(X),i)$, and a group $G$.

[F1] The free-group universal property gives, for every function $f:X\to U(G)$, a unique group homomorphism $\widehat f:F(X)\to G$ with $\widehat f\circ i=f$ ([[def-free-group]]).

[L1] Reduced words form a free group, with generators the one-letter positive words; reduced words are unique normal forms ([[thm-reduced-words-form-the-free-group]]).

[L2] Any two free groups on $X$ have a unique isomorphism carrying one generator map to the other ([[thm-free-groups-unique-up-to-unique-isomorphism]]).

[F2] Groups and group homomorphisms form the large locally small category $\mathbf{Grp}$ ([[prop-groups-and-homomorphisms-form-category-grp]]).

[F3] A group is cyclic when it is generated by one element, meaning every element lies in the subgroup generated by that element ([[def-generated-subgroup]]).

[F4] A covariant set-valued functor represented by $R$ is naturally isomorphic to the hom-functor $\mathcal C(R,-)$ ([[def-presheaf-representable-functor-and-representation]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F1], restriction along $i$ is a bijection $\mathbf{Grp}(F(X),G)\to\mathbf{Set}(X,U(G))$, with inverse $f\mapsto\widehat f$. [F1]

1.2 Now let $X=\{x\}$. By [L1], every reduced word is either empty, a string of $n\ge1$ copies of $x$, or a string of $n\ge1$ copies of $x^{-1}$: a reduced word containing both signs would have an adjacent sign change and hence a cancellable pair. Thus every element is an integer power of $x$, so [F3] makes $F(X)$ cyclic. [L1, F3]

2.1 If $r:G\to H$ is a group homomorphism, then $r\circ\widehat f$ and $\widehat{U(r)\circ f}$ are homomorphisms $F(X)\to H$ with the same restriction to $X$; uniqueness in [F1] makes them equal. Thus the bijections of step 1.1 are natural in $G$. [step 1.1, F1, F2]

3.1 Steps 1.1 and 2.1, together with [F4], show that $F(X)$ represents the stated functor. By [L2], changing the chosen free-group model changes this representation by the unique generator-compatible isomorphism. [step 1.1, step 2.1, L2, F4]

4.1 The positive words $x,x x,x x x,\ldots$ have different finite lengths and are distinct reduced normal forms by [L1], so $F(X)$ has infinitely many elements. Hence the singleton free group is infinite cyclic. [step 1.2, L1] ∎
