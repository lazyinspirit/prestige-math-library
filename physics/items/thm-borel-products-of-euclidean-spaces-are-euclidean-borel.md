## Statement

Let $m,n \ge 1$. Under the identification
$$\mathbb R^{m+n}=\mathbb R^m \times \mathbb R^n,$$
one has
$$\mathcal B(\mathbb R^m)\otimes\mathcal B(\mathbb R^n) = \mathcal B(\mathbb R^{m+n}).$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Positive integers $m,n$ and the identification $\mathbb R^{m+n}=\mathbb R^m \times \mathbb R^n$.

[L1] The Borel sigma-algebra on a topological space is the sigma-algebra generated by its open sets. ([[def-borel-sigma-algebra]])

[L2] Continuous maps pull Borel sets back to Borel sets. ([[thm-continuous-preimages-of-borel-sets-are-borel]])

[A1] The coordinate projections $\pi_1 : \mathbb R^{m+n} \to \mathbb R^m$ and $\pi_2 : \mathbb R^{m+n} \to \mathbb R^n$ are continuous.

[L3] Rational open boxes form a countable basis of the Euclidean product topology: every point of an open set lies in a rational box contained in that set ([[thm-rational-points-and-boxes-in-rn]], [[thm-rational-box-generators-of-the-borel-sigma-algebra-on-rn]]). These results use rational density and only finitely many coordinate choices, not an axiom of countable or arbitrary choice.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 If $A \subseteq \mathbb R^m$ and $B \subseteq \mathbb R^n$ are Borel, then $$A \times B = \pi_1^{-1}(A) \cap \pi_2^{-1}(B),$$ so [L2] makes $A \times B$ Borel in $\mathbb R^{m+n}$. Therefore every measurable rectangle for $\mathcal B(\mathbb R^m)\otimes\mathcal B(\mathbb R^n)$ belongs to $\mathcal B(\mathbb R^{m+n})$, and hence $$\mathcal B(\mathbb R^m)\otimes\mathcal B(\mathbb R^n) \subseteq \mathcal B(\mathbb R^{m+n}).$$ [A1, L2]

2.1 Let $O$ be open in $\mathbb R^{m+n}$ and let $\mathcal R_O$ be the family of all rational open boxes contained in $O$. By [L3], every $x\in O$ lies in a member of $\mathcal R_O$, so $O=\bigcup\mathcal R_O$; the reverse containment follows from its definition, including when $O$ is empty. This is a subfamily of one fixed countable basis, hence is at most countable; no choice of a box for each point is made. Each box $\prod_{j=1}^{m+n}(a_j,b_j)$ splits into the open rectangle $U\times V$, with $U=\prod_{j=1}^m(a_j,b_j)$ and $V=\prod_{j=m+1}^{m+n}(a_j,b_j)$. Both factors are Borel, so each box belongs to the product sigma-algebra. Closure under countable unions puts $O$ there as well. Since [L1] says the open sets generate the Euclidean Borel sigma-algebra, this proves the reverse inclusion. Combining with step 1.1 proves the equality, without any choice assumption. [L1, L3, step 1.1] ∎
