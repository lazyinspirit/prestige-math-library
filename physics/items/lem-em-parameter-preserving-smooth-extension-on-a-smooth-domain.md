## Statement

Let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^3$ be a **bounded smooth graph domain**: a nonempty bounded open set such that for every $x\in\partial\Omega$ there are an open neighbourhood $W\subseteq\mathbb R^3$ of $x$, a rigid motion $R(p)=Qp+b$, an open ball $B\subseteq\mathbb R^2$ and a function $h\in C^\infty(B;\mathbb R)$ such that, after shrinking $W$ so that $R(W)\subseteq B\times\mathbb R$,

$$R(\Omega\cap W)=R(W)\cap\{(y,s)\in B\times\mathbb R:s<h(y)\},$$

which is the $C^\infty$ case of the bounded $C^k$ graph domain of [[def-bounded-c-k-domain-and-boundary-charts]]. Let $V\subseteq\mathbb R^3$ be an open set with $\overline\Omega\subseteq V$. Then there exists a real-linear operator $E_\Omega$, determined only by $\Omega$, a finite smooth atlas and fixed cutoffs, such that for every open interval $J\subseteq\mathbb R$ ([[def-interval]]) and every function $u:\Omega\times J\to\mathbb R$ that is of class $C^\infty$ on the open set $\Omega\times J$ and whose every mixed derivative $D^\alpha\partial_\tau^j u:\Omega\times J\to\mathbb R$ has a continuous extension to $\overline\Omega\times J$ ([[def-ck-and-multi-index-notation-in-several-variables]], [[def-directional-and-partial-derivatives]]), the function $E_\Omega u$ is of class $C^\infty$ on $\mathbb R^3\times J$, satisfies $E_\Omega u(x,\tau)=u(x,\tau)$ for all $(x,\tau)\in\overline\Omega\times J$ and

$$D^\alpha\partial_\tau^j(E_\Omega u)\big|_{\overline\Omega\times J}=\text{the continuous extension of }D^\alpha\partial_\tau^j u$$

for all multi-indices $\alpha$ and all $j\ge0$, and is spatially supported in one fixed compact subset of $V$, in the sense that there is a compact $K_\Omega\subseteq V$ with $E_\Omega u(x,\tau)=0$ for all $x\notin K_\Omega$, $\tau\in J$. In every seminorm formula below, a supremum over an empty indexing set is defined to be $0$; for a nonempty bounded family of nonnegative reals it denotes the usual least real upper bound. This convention applies also to the local seminorm formulas in the proof. For every compact $K\subseteq J$, every integer $m\ge0$ and every $j\ge0$,

$$\sup_{\tau\in K}\|\partial_\tau^j E_\Omega u(\cdot,\tau)\|_{C^m(\mathbb R^3)}\le C_m\sup_{\tau\in K}\|\partial_\tau^j u(\cdot,\tau)\|_{C^m(\overline\Omega)},$$

where $\|w\|_{C^m(\overline\Omega)}:=\sum_{|\alpha|\le m}\sup_{x\in\overline\Omega}|D^\alpha w(x)|$ and $\|W\|_{C^m(\mathbb R^3)}:=\sum_{|\alpha|\le m}\sup_{x\in\mathbb R^3}|D^\alpha W(x)|$, and the constant $C_m$ depends only on $m$, $\Omega$, the atlas and cutoffs. The extension commutes with every time derivative: $\partial_\tau^j(E_\Omega u)=E_\Omega(\partial_\tau^j u)$ for all $j\ge0$. No bound uniform over an unbounded $J$ is asserted. Vector-valued inputs of finite dimension are extended componentwise. The construction adds no choice principle: the finite atlas is selected from the cover of all admissible smooth chart patches, finite cutoffs come from the published choice-free compact cutoff lemma, and the infinite coefficient sequence is defined by the explicit formula below. The consuming elliptic or Sobolev argument retains whatever choice principles its own suppliers declare.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A bounded smooth graph domain $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^3$, an open set $V$ with $\overline\Omega\subseteq V$, an open interval $J\subseteq\mathbb R$, and a function $u:\Omega\times J\to\mathbb R$ of class $C^\infty$ whose mixed derivatives all extend continuously to $\overline\Omega\times J$.

[L1] **Smooth graph charts and flattening** ([[def-bounded-c-k-domain-and-boundary-charts]], [[def-ck-euclidean-maps-and-diffeomorphisms]]). A bounded $C^k$ domain $\Omega$ has, at each boundary point, graph data $W,R,B,h$ with $R(\Omega\cap W)=R(W)\cap\{s<h(y)\}$; its flattening chart is $\Phi(p):=(y,s-h(y))$ on $W$, a $C^k$ diffeomorphism onto $\Phi(W)$ with $C^k$ inverse and $\Phi(\Omega\cap W)=\Phi(W)\cap\{t<0\}$; on compactly contained patches the derivatives of $\Phi$ and $\Phi^{-1}$ through order $k$ are bounded. Here $h\in C^\infty(B)$, so these charts and inverses are of class $C^\infty$.

[L2] **Compactness** ([[lem-compactness-of-a-subspace-is-ambient]]: every ambient open cover of a compact subspace has a finite subcover) ([[def-compact-space]], [[thm-heine-borel-rn]], [[def-metric-interior-closure-boundary]], [[def-metric-bounded-diameter]]). A space is compact when every open cover has a finite subcover. A subset of $\mathbb R^n$ ($n\ge1$) is compact if and only if it is closed and bounded; closed boxes are compact. $\partial\Omega$ and $\overline\Omega$ are the set-theoretic boundary and closure, $\overline\Omega$ is closed and, being the closure of a bounded set, is bounded.

[L3] **Choice-free Euclidean cutoffs** ([[lem-test-function-cutoffs-and-euclidean-localization]]). In ZF, for compact $K\subseteq O\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ with $O$ open there is $\chi\in C_c^\infty(O)$ with $0\le\chi\le1$ and $\chi=1$ on a neighbourhood of $K$.

[L4] **$C^k$ calculus** ([[def-ck-and-multi-index-notation-in-several-variables]], [[def-directional-and-partial-derivatives]], [[def-ck-euclidean-maps-and-diffeomorphisms]], [[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]], [[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]], [[thm-algebra-of-continuous-functions]], [[thm-continuous-partial-derivatives-imply-total-differentiability]]). A map $f$ is $C^k$ on an open set when every word of at most $k$ coordinate partial derivatives exists and is continuous; $C^\infty$ means $C^k$ for all $k$. Sums, scalar multiples and products of $C^k$ functions are $C^k$; a composition of $C^k$ maps is $C^k$; a map with continuous first partial derivatives everywhere on an open set is totally differentiable there, with derivative the Jacobian. Continuous functions on compact intervals are Riemann integrable.

[L5] **Fundamental theorem of calculus** ([[thm-ftc-second-part]], [[thm-ftc-first-part]], [[thm-continuous-implies-integrable]], [[def-derivative]]). If $G:[a,b]\to\mathbb R$ is differentiable at every point of $[a,b]$ (one-sided at the endpoints) and $G'=f$ is integrable on $[a,b]$, then $\int_a^bf=G(b)-G(a)$; if $f$ is continuous at $c\in[a,b]$ and $F$ is its integral function, then $F'(c)=f(c)$.

[L6] **Powers, series and monotone limits** ([[def-integer-power]], [[lem-power-laws]], [[def-series]], [[thm-geometric-series]], [[thm-monotone-convergence]]). $2^k$ is the integer power with $2^{k+1}=2\cdot2^k$, and $2^m2^n=2^{m+n}$; the series $\sum_{k=0}^\infty2^{-k}$ converges to $2$, and its tails are $\sum_{k=M}^\infty2^{-k}=2^{1-M}$; a nondecreasing sequence of reals bounded above converges to its supremum.

[L7] **Real polynomials and their roots** ([[def-real-polynomial-degree-leading-coefficient-and-monic]], [[thm-nonzero-real-polynomial-has-at-most-degree-many-distinct-roots]], [[def-finite-sum]], [[lem-finite-sum-laws]]). A formal real polynomial is $0$ or a finite coefficient list $(a_0,\dots,a_n)$ with $a_n\ne0$, evaluated by $p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^na_kx^k$, with $\deg p=n$; a nonzero polynomial of degree $n$ has at most $n$ distinct real roots. Finite sums and products of reals are defined by recursion and obey additivity, splitting, scaling, and distributivity laws.

## Proof

The construction is standard Seeley reflection; the proof defines the reflection coefficients from scratch (so that no external coefficient-existence assertion is assumed), proves the exact moment identities, performs the reflected extension on one flattened half-space with parameters, glues the two sides by the fundamental theorem of calculus, and assembles finitely many chart pieces with a partition of unity.

1.1 Since $\overline\Omega$ is bounded and closed [L2], it is compact by Heine-Borel [L2]. The family of all admissible smooth chart neighbourhoods of boundary points is an open cover of $\partial\Omega\subseteq\overline\Omega$, and $\Omega$ itself is open; hence the family formed by $\Omega$ and all admissible smooth chart neighbourhoods covers the compact set $\overline\Omega$, so it contains a finite subcover, say $W_1,\dots,W_p$ together with $\Omega$, where each $W_i$ carries chart data $(R_i,B_i,h_i,\Phi_i)$ with $h_i\in C^\infty$ and rigid motion $R_i$ [L1, L2]. Extracting the finitely many chart data is a finite choice, available in ZF; choose these chart neighbourhoods already inside $V$ and with flattened images equal to symmetric product boxes $B_i\times(-\delta_i,\delta_i)$: every boundary point has such a smaller neighbourhood, so use the cover by these smaller charts before taking the finite subcover. Fix these finitely many maps and data for the rest of the proof. [L1, L2, choose]

1.2 Put $b_k:=-2^k$ for $k\ge0$, the integer powers of [L6]. For $N\ge k\ge0$ define $a_k^{(N)}:=\prod_{\substack{0\le j\le N\\j\ne k}}\frac{1-b_j}{b_k-b_j}.$ Here $1-b_j=1+2^j>0$; for $j<k$ one has $b_k-b_j=-(2^k-2^j)<0$, and for $j>k$ one has $b_k-b_j=2^j-2^k>0$. Hence $a_k^{(N)}\ne0$ and its sign is $(-1)^k$ for every $N\ge k$. [L6, algebra]

1.3 For reals $x_0,\dots,x_M\in[0,1)$ with $\sum_{j=0}^Mx_j<1$ one has, by induction on $M$ using the field axioms, $\prod_{j=0}^M(1+x_j)\le\bigl(1-\sum_{j=0}^Mx_j\bigr)^{-1}$ and $\prod_{j=0}^M(1-x_j)\ge1-\sum_{j=0}^Mx_j$. Applying this with $x_j=2^{-j}$ and the geometric sums $\sum_{j\ge2}2^{-j}=\tfrac12$ and $\sum_{j\ge1}2^{-j}=1$ [L6] gives for every finite set $F\subseteq\mathbb N$ and every $M\ge0$ the two bounds $\prod_{j\in F}(1+2^{-j})\le6$ and $\prod_{l=1}^{M}(1-2^{-l})\ge\tfrac14$: the first because the product over $\{0,1\}$ equals $2\cdot\tfrac32=3$ and the product over $j\ge2$ is at most $\bigl(1-\tfrac12\bigr)^{-1}=2$, the second because the factor $l=1$ is $\tfrac12$ and the remaining product is at least $1-\sum_{l\ge2}2^{-l}=\tfrac12$. [L6, induction, algebra]

1.4 Let $f$ be continuous on the strip $B\times(-\delta,\delta)\times J$ (with $B\subseteq\mathbb R^2$ open, $\delta>0$) and suppose that every first partial derivative of $f$ exists and is continuous on each open half-strip $\{s>0\}$ and $\{s<0\}$ and extends continuously to the corresponding closed half-strip, the two extensions agreeing on $\{s=0\}$; let $E_i$ denote the resulting common continuous function on the whole strip, the index $i$ running over the four coordinates. Fix a point $p$ in the strip. If a segment $\beta\mapsto p+\beta e_i$, $\beta\in[0,h]$, stays inside one open half-strip, then $\beta\mapsto f(p+\beta e_i)$ is differentiable at every point of $[0,h]$ with derivative $E_i(p+\beta e_i)$ on $(0,h)$ and one-sided derivatives at the endpoints, and $E_i$ is continuous, hence integrable, on $[0,h]$ [L4, L5]; the second fundamental theorem gives $f(p+he_i)-f(p)=\int_0^hE_i(p+\beta e_i)\,d\beta$. If the segment meets $\{s=0\}$ at a parameter $\beta_0$, apply the identity on closed subintervals staying strictly in either open half-strip, then let their endpoints approach $\beta_0$. Continuity of $f$ gives convergence of the endpoint values, while the continuous extension of $E_i$ is bounded near $\beta_0$, so the omitted integrals tend to $0$. Additivity gives the same identity on the whole segment, including when $\beta_0$ is an endpoint; no derivative at the interface is assumed. For $h<0$, integrals are interpreted with reversed limits. If the segment lies entirely in the interface $s=0$ (a tangential or time direction), translate it by a positive normal distance $\epsilon$ and use the identity just proved on the positive half-strip. Continuity of $f$ and of $E_i$ on the compact translated-segment strip gives uniform convergence as $\epsilon\downarrow0$, hence the same integral identity on $s=0$. Dividing by $h$ and letting $h\to0$, continuity of $E_i$ at $p$ gives $(f(p+he_i)-f(p))/h=\tfrac1h\int_0^hE_i(p+\beta e_i)d\beta\to E_i(p)$; thus every first partial derivative of $f$ exists on the whole strip and equals the continuous function $E_i$. By [L4] $f$ is totally differentiable at every point with that Jacobian, and in particular all first partial derivatives are continuous, so $f$ is $C^1$ on the strip. [L4, L5, cases, algebra]

2.1 For $N\ge k$, rewriting each factor defined in step 1.2 as $1-b_j=2^j(1+2^{-j})$ and $b_k-b_j=\pm2^{\max(j,k)}(1-2^{-|j-k|})$ gives $\bigl|a_k^{(N)}\bigr|=2^{-k(k+1)/2}\,\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(1+2^{-j})\prod_{j=k+1}^{N}(1+2^{-j})}{\prod_{l=1}^{k}(1-2^{-l})\prod_{m=1}^{N-k}(1-2^{-m})},$ where empty products are $1$; indeed the powers of $2$ contribute $\sum_{j<k}(j-k)=-k(k+1)/2$, and the two denominator products collect the factors $1-2^{j-k}$ for $j<k$ and $1-2^{k-j}$ for $j>k$. By step 1.3 the numerator is at most $36$ and each denominator product is at least $\tfrac14$, so $\bigl|a_k^{(N)}\bigr|\le576\cdot2^{-k(k+1)/2}$ for all $N\ge k$. For fixed $k$ the ratio $\bigl|a_k^{(N+1)}\bigr|/\bigl|a_k^{(N)}\bigr|=(1+2^{-(N+1)})/(1-2^{-(N+1-k)})$ exceeds $1$ whenever $N+1>k$, because its numerator exceeds $1$ and its denominator lies in $(0,1)$; hence $N\mapsto\bigl|a_k^{(N)}\bigr|$ is nondecreasing and bounded above by $576\cdot2^{-k(k+1)/2}$, so the monotone convergence theorem [L6] gives a limit $a_k:=\lim_{N\to\infty}a_k^{(N)}$ with sign $(-1)^k$ and $\bigl|a_k\bigr|\le576\cdot2^{-k(k+1)/2}$. Consequently, for every integer $r\ge0$ and every $k\ge2r+1$ one has $-\tfrac{k(k+1)}2+kr\le-k$, so $\bigl|a_k\bigr|\,\bigl|b_k\bigr|^r\le576\cdot2^{-k}$ for such $k$ and the series $\sum_{k=0}^\infty\bigl|a_k\bigr|\,\bigl|b_k\bigr|^r$ converges, with tails bounded by $\sum_{k>M}\bigl|a_k\bigr|\,\bigl|b_k\bigr|^r\le576\cdot2^{-M}$ whenever $M\ge2r+1$. [step 1.2, step 1.3, L6, algebra]

2.2 For every $N\ge0$ and every integer $r$ with $0\le r\le N$ one has $\sum_{k=0}^{N}a_k^{(N)}b_k^r=1.$ Indeed, expanding the $N$ linear factors of each $\prod_{j\ne k}(z-b_j)$ by distributivity gives each function $z\mapsto\prod_{j\ne k}(z-b_j)/(b_k-b_j)$ the structure of a real polynomial of degree at most $N$ with explicit finite coefficient sums [L7], so $q(z):=z^r-\sum_{k=0}^{N}b_k^r\prod_{j\ne k}(z-b_j)/(b_k-b_j)$ is a real polynomial of degree at most $N$. At a node $z=b_i$ every product $\prod_{j\ne k}(b_i-b_j)$ vanishes for $k\ne i$ and equals $\prod_{j\ne i}(b_i-b_j)$ for $k=i$, so $q(b_i)=b_i^r-b_i^r=0$ for all $i=0,\dots,N$. Thus $q$ has $N+1$ distinct roots; were $q$ nonzero, its degree would be defined and at most $N$, contradicting the root bound [L7]. Hence $q$ is the zero polynomial, and evaluating at $z=1$, where $\prod_{j\ne k}(1-b_j)/(b_k-b_j)=a_k^{(N)}$, gives $0=q(1)=1-\sum_{k=0}^Na_k^{(N)}b_k^r$. [step 1.2, L7, algebra]

2.3 Let $f$ be continuous on the strip and suppose that for every word $(i_1,\dots,i_m)$ of coordinate indices with $m\ge1$ the iterated derivative $\partial_{i_m}\cdots\partial_{i_1}f$ exists and is continuous on each open half-strip and extends continuously to the closed half-strips, the two extensions agreeing on $\{s=0\}$; let $F_{i_1\dots i_m}$ be the common extension. Then $f$ is of class $C^\infty$ on the strip, and every iterated derivative of $f$ on the strip equals the corresponding common extension. Indeed, induct on $m$: for $m=0$ this is the assumed continuity, and for $m\ge1$ the iterated derivative $g:=\partial_{i_{m-1}}\cdots\partial_{i_1}f$ has, by the induction hypothesis, the continuous extension $F_{i_1\dots i_{m-1}}$ to the strip, whose first partial derivatives on each open half-strip exist and are continuous and extend continuously to the closed half-strips with the extensions $F_{i_1\dots i_{m-1}i}$ agreeing on $\{s=0\}$; step 1.4 applied to $g$ shows that $g$ is $C^1$ on the strip with $\partial_ig=F_{i_1\dots i_{m-1}i}$. Hence every iterated derivative of $f$ of every order exists and is continuous on the strip, that is, $f$ is $C^\infty$ there, and each iterated derivative equals the corresponding common extension. [step 1.4, induction]

2.4 Let $\mathcal U$ be the finite family consisting of the members $W_1,\dots,W_p$ of step 1.1 and the member $\Omega$. For each $x\in\overline\Omega$ choose a member of $\mathcal U$ containing $x$ and then an open Euclidean ball with rational centre and rational radius containing $x$ and whose closure is contained in that member; the countable, explicitly defined family $\mathcal B$ of all open balls with rational centre and rational radius whose closure is contained in at least one member of $\mathcal U$ covers the compact set $\overline\Omega$, so compactness [L2] yields finitely many balls $B_1,\dots,B_q\in\mathcal B$ covering $\overline\Omega$. For each $l\le q$ choose a member $U_{\sigma(l)}$ of $\mathcal U$ containing $\overline{B_l}$; this is a finite choice in ZF. By [L3] applied to the compact sets $\overline{B_l}\subseteq U_{\sigma(l)}$ there are $\psi_l\in C_c^\infty(U_{\sigma(l)})$ with $0\le\psi_l\le1$ and $\psi_l=1$ on a neighbourhood of $\overline{B_l}$. Then $S:=\sum_{l=1}^q\psi_l$ is smooth and positive on a neighbourhood of $\overline\Omega$ because each $\overline{B_l}$ carries such a neighbourhood; apply [L3] again, with compact $K=\overline\Omega$ and open $O=\{S>0\}$, to obtain $\chi\in C_c^\infty(\{S>0\})$ with $0\le\chi\le1$ and $\chi=1$ on a neighbourhood of $\overline\Omega$. Since the support of $\chi$ is compact and contained in $\{S>0\}$, the function $\chi/S$, defined to be $\chi\cdot(1/S)$ on $\{S>0\}$ and $0$ elsewhere, is smooth with compact support in $\{S>0\}$. For $j=1,\dots,p$ put $\chi_j:=\frac{\chi}{S}\sum_{\substack{1\le l\le q\\\sigma(l)=j}}\psi_l$ and put $\chi_0:=\frac{\chi}{S}\sum_{\substack{1\le l\le q\\\sigma(l)=0}}\psi_l$, assigning index $0$ to the member $\Omega$. Each $\chi_j$ is smooth, compactly supported and nonnegative, with $\operatorname{supp}\chi_j\subseteq W_j$ for $j\ge1$ and $\operatorname{supp}\chi_0\subseteq\Omega$, and $\sum_{j=0}^p\chi_j=\chi=1$ on a neighbourhood of $\overline\Omega$, in particular on $\Omega$. [step 1.1, L2, L3, choose, algebra]

3.1 For every integer $r\ge0$ the series $\sum_{k=0}^\infty a_kb_k^r$ converges absolutely with sum $1$. Indeed, fix $M\ge2r+1$ and $N\ge\max(r,M)$; by step 2.2, $1=\sum_{k=0}^{N}a_k^{(N)}b_k^r$, so $1-\sum_{k=0}^{M}a_kb_k^r=\sum_{k=0}^{M}(a_k^{(N)}-a_k)b_k^r+\sum_{k=M+1}^{N}a_k^{(N)}b_k^r$; the first sum tends to $0$ as $N\to\infty$ because there are finitely many coefficients and each converges to $a_k$ by step 2.1, and the second sum is bounded in absolute value by $576\cdot2^{-M}$ uniformly in $N$ by step 2.1. Letting first $N\to\infty$ and then $M\to\infty$ gives $\sum_{k=0}^\infty a_kb_k^r=1$, the absolute convergence being part of step 2.1. [step 2.1, step 2.2, L6, algebra]

4.1 Fix $B\subseteq\mathbb R^2$ open, $\delta>0$ and an interval $J$, and let $w:(B\times(-\delta,0])\times J\to\mathbb R$ be continuous, of class $C^\infty$ on $B\times(-\delta,0)\times J$, with every mixed derivative $D_y^\alpha\partial_s^r\partial_\tau^jw$ extending continuously to $B\times(-\delta,0]\times J$, and suppose $w$ vanishes outside a compact subset of $B\times(-\delta,0]$. Choosing a cutoff $\rho\in C_c^\infty((-\delta/2,\delta/2))$ with $0\le\rho\le1$ and $\rho=1$ near $0$ [L3], define $(\mathcal Ew)(y,s,\tau):=w(y,s,\tau)$ for $s\le0$ and $(\mathcal Ew)(y,s,\tau):=\sum_{k=0}^\infty a_k\rho(b_ks)w(y,b_ks,\tau)$ for $s>0$, with the limiting coefficients $a_k$ of step 2.1. In this formula $\rho(z)w(y,z,\tau)$ and each of its derivatives are extended by zero for $z\le-\delta$; this extension is smooth there because $\rho$ vanishes on a neighbourhood of $-\delta$. For fixed $s>0$ the series is finite: $|b_ks|=2^ks\ge\delta/2$ for all large $k$, where $\rho$ vanishes, so only finitely many terms are nonzero; hence $\mathcal Ew$ is well defined on $B\times(-\delta,\delta)\times J$. As $s\downarrow0$ each term tends to $a_kw(y,0,\tau)$ by continuity of $w$ and $\rho(0)=1$, and the tail is bounded by $(\sup|w|)\sum_{k>M}|a_k|$, which tends to $0$ uniformly on compact sets by step 2.1; by step 3.1 with $r=0$ the lower and upper limits both equal $w(y,0,\tau)$, so $\mathcal Ew$ is continuous on the strip. Moreover $\mathcal Ew$ vanishes on the complement of the fixed compact spatial support of $w$ on $s\le0$ together with the projection of that support in the $y$-variable times $[0,\delta/2]$, with arbitrary $\tau\in J$, so it is spatially supported in a fixed compact subset of $B\times(-\delta,\delta)$. [step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1, L3, L6, algebra]

5.1 $\mathcal Ew$ is of class $C^\infty$ on each of the two open half-strips, with the following derivative formula on $s>0$, valid for every multi-index $\alpha$ and all $r,j\ge0$: $D_y^\alpha\partial_s^r\partial_\tau^j(\mathcal Ew)(y,s,\tau)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_kb_k^r\,\bigl[D_z^rD_y^\alpha\partial_\tau^j\bigl(\rho(z)w(y,z,\tau)\bigr)\bigr]_{z=b_ks},$ a finite sum for each fixed $(y,s,\tau)$ because only the finitely many $k$ with $|b_ks|<\delta/2$ contribute. 9.2 **Seminorm bound.** Let $m\ge0$, $j\ge0$ and compact $K\subseteq J$ be given. If $K=\varnothing$, both time seminorms are $0$ by the stated convention, so the estimate is $0\le C_m0$. Suppose now that $K\ne\varnothing$. For $|\alpha|\le m$ and $\tau\in K$, step 5.1 bounds each $|D^\alpha\partial_\tau^jg_i(x,\tau)|$ by a finite constant times $\sum_{|\beta|\le m}\sup_{\overline\Omega}|D^\beta\partial_\tau^ju(\cdot,\tau)|$: for $i=0$ this is immediate from the Leibniz rule and compact support, and for $i\ge1$ the pullback introduces the bounded derivatives of $\Phi_i^{-1}$ on the compact piece, the local cutoff contributes finitely many bounded terms, and step 5.1 supplies the coefficient sum $\sum_k|a_k||b_k|^m<\infty$; the spatial derivatives of order at most $m$ and the fixed time order $j$ enter only through $D^\beta\partial_\tau^ju$ with $|\beta|\le m$, because the normal argument is sampled at $b_kt$ and differentiated at most $r\le m$ times. Summing over the finitely many pieces and over $|\alpha|\le m$ gives a constant $C_m$, independent of $u$, $j$, $K$ and $\tau$, with $\|\partial_\tau^j(E_\Omega u)(\cdot,\tau)\|_{C^m(\mathbb R^3)}\le C_m\|\partial_\tau^ju(\cdot,\tau)\|_{C^m(\overline\Omega)}$; the right-hand side is bounded on $K$ because the finitely many mixed derivatives are continuous on the compact set $\overline\Omega\times K$, and taking the supremum over $\tau\in K$ gives the displayed estimate. The commutation identity below holds for every $\tau\in J$, independently of $K$. Since each piece commutes with $\partial_\tau$, $\partial_\tau^j(E_\Omega u)=E_\Omega(\partial_\tau^ju)$. [step 5.1, step 8.1, algebra]
