## Example

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $k$ be a field,
$A=k\llbracket x,y\rrbracket/(xy)$ and $M=A/(x)\cong k\llbracket y\rrbracket$. Then $M$ is a nonfree
maximal Cohen--Macaulay $A$-module.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice and the displayed ring and module. Finite polynomial rings over a field are Noetherian and have dimension equal to their number of variables; localizations of Noetherian rings are Noetherian ([[cor-finite-variable-polynomial-ring-noetherian]], [[cor-dimension-of-a-finite-polynomial-ring-over-a-field]], [[thm-noetherian-ring-quotients-and-localisations]]). Completion of a Noetherian local ring is Noetherian local and preserves its dimension under the assumed choice premise ([[thm-completion-of-a-noetherian-local-ring]], [[thm-completion-preserves-dimension-and-hilbert-samuel-data]]). A one-variable series is a unit exactly when its constant coefficient is nonzero ([[thm-formal-power-series-unit-criterion]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 For $r=1,2$, the coordinate localization $P_r=k[x_1,\ldots,x_r]_{(x_1,\ldots,x_r)}$ is Noetherian local. Its dimension is $r$: the coordinate-prime chain has length $r$, while localization cannot increase the dimension $r$ of the polynomial ring. The quotients $P_r/(x_1,\ldots,x_r)^q$ are the degree-$<q$ truncated polynomial rings. Their inverse limit is the formal power series ring $k\llbracket x_1,\ldots,x_r\rrbracket$, because compatible truncations determine exactly one coefficient for every monomial and multiplication agrees coefficientwise. The completion results in the Given paragraph make this series ring Noetherian local of dimension $r$, with maximal ideal generated by its variables and residue field $k$. [given, algebra]

2.1 Put $Q=k\llbracket x,y\rrbracket$. Its quotient $A=Q/(xy)$ is Noetherian local. Every prime of $Q$ containing $xy$ contains $x$ or $y$. Each quotient $Q/(x)\cong k\llbracket y\rrbracket$ and $Q/(y)\cong k\llbracket x\rrbracket$ has only the primes $(0)$ and its maximal ideal: every nonzero one-variable series is a unit times a power of its variable. Hence the primes of $A$ are $(x)A$, $(y)A$ and $(x,y)A$, and $\dim A=1$. [step 1.1, given, algebra]

3.1 The cyclic $A$-module $M=A/(x)\cong k\llbracket y\rrbracket$ is nonzero and finite. Multiplication by $y$ is injective on it, and $M/yM\cong k$ is nonzero, so $\operatorname{depth}_A(M)\ge1$. If $a\in(x,y)A$ is any $M$-regular element, its image in $k\llbracket y\rrbracket$ is $y^tu$ for some $t\ge1$ and unit $u$. The nonzero class of $y^{t-1}$ in $M/aM\cong k\llbracket y\rrbracket/(y^t)$ is annihilated by both $x$ and $y$, hence by the maximal ideal. Thus no second element of that ideal is regular on $M/aM$, and $\operatorname{depth}_A(M)=1$. [step 2.1, given, algebra]

4.1 By step 2.1 and the definition of maximal Cohen--Macaulay, $\operatorname{depth}_A(M)=\dim A=1$. The class of $x$ in $A$ is nonzero (reduce modulo $y$) but annihilates all of $M$; a nonzero free $A$-module has zero annihilator. Therefore $M$ is nonfree. [step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
