## Statement

Let $G$ be any finite-dimensional Lie group and let $A$ be a graded $\mathbb Z$-algebra of finite type, with $A_0=\mathbb Z$ and every $A_j$ torsion-free. Write $A_{\mathbb C}=A\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb C$. Put $\mathfrak g=T_eG$ as a real Lie algebra and $\mathfrak g^\vee=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb R}(\mathfrak g,\mathbb C)$ as its complex dual; thus $\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb C}^*(\mathfrak g^\vee)^G$ is a complex algebra. Throughout the proof, duals of real coefficient vector spaces in scalar natural-form calculations mean this complex dual. Use the source's levelwise pro-completed grading
$$P_A^*=I_A^*(\mathfrak g^\vee)=\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathbb C}^*(\mathfrak g^\vee)^G\widehat\otimes_{\mathbb C}\overline{A_{\mathbb C}},\qquad \overline{A}_{-j}=A_j.$$
Invariant polynomials have twice polynomial degree. Inclusion of closed basic forms into the derived full de Rham complex defines a natural comparison
$$q_A:P_A^*\longrightarrow H^*(BG;A_{\mathbb C}).$$
Let $c_A:H^*(BG;A)\to H^*(BG;A_{\mathbb C})$ be coefficient extension, and define the full-scope $H$-integral subring by
$$P_{A,H}^*=q_A^{-1}(\operatorname{im}c_A).$$

For the degree-shifted Hopkins–Singer model on $\mathbf B_{\nabla}G$, curvature is a surjective graded-ring homomorphism $R:\widehat H^*(\mathbf B_{\nabla}G;A)\twoheadrightarrow P_{A,H}^*$. For every integer $q$ there is a natural exact sequence
$$0\longrightarrow H^{q-1}(BG;A_{\mathbb C}/A)\xrightarrow{j}\widehat H^q(\mathbf B_{\nabla}G;A)\xrightarrow{R}P_{A,H}^q\longrightarrow0,\qquad Ij=\beta,$$
where $I$ is forgetful and $\beta$ is the coefficient Bockstein. In particular the precise forgetful description of the curvature kernel is
$$0\longrightarrow\operatorname{coker}(c_A^{q-1})\longrightarrow\ker R\xrightarrow{I}\ker(c_A^q)\longrightarrow0.$$
For compact connected $G$, $q_A$ is the Chern–Weil isomorphism with the stipulated completion. Whenever $H^{q-1}(BG;A_{\mathbb C})=0$, $I$ therefore identifies $\ker R$ with $\ker(c_A^q)$. In particular, for $A=\mathbb Z$ concentrated in degree zero and even $q\ge0$, this gives the short exact sequence with $\operatorname{Tor}_{\mathbb Z}H^q(BG;\mathbb Z)$ as kernel. No identification of the whole graded curvature kernel with torsion is asserted.

For connective $K$-theory let $P_k^*=I^*_{\mathbb C[u]}(\mathfrak g^\vee)$ with its source grading and completion, let $q_k:P_k^*\to H^*(BG;\mathbb C[u])$ be the natural form comparison, and let $c_k:k^*(BG)\to H^*(BG;\mathbb C[u])$ be the complexified Chern character. Define $P_{k,k}^*=q_k^{-1}(\operatorname{im}c_k)$. The exponential sequence of abelian groups is

$$0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{\iota}\mathbb C\xrightarrow{z\mapsto e^{2\pi iz}}\mathbb C^\times\longrightarrow1,$$

where $\mathbb C$ is additive and $\mathbb C^\times=\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$ is multiplicative; the exponential identifies it with $\mathbb C/\mathbb Z$ as an abelian group. Define $k_{\mathbb C^\times}:=\operatorname{cofib}(k\to k\wedge H\mathbb C)$, where the map is induced by the spectrum map $\mathbb S\to H\mathbb C$ corresponding to $1\mapsto1$. This is the coefficient cofiber determined by the exponential sequence. The source's degree-shifted connective model satisfies
$$0\longrightarrow k^{q-1}_{\mathbb C^\times}(BG)\xrightarrow{j}\widehat k^q(\mathbf B_{\nabla}G)\xrightarrow{R}P_{k,k}^q\longrightarrow0,\qquad Ij=\beta.$$
Thus $I(\ker R)=\operatorname{im}\beta=\ker(c_k^q)$, and its possible kernel is exactly $\operatorname{coker}(c_k^{q-1})$. If $G$ is compact connected, curvature is an isomorphism in every even degree $q\le0$.

All groups and maps use these Hopkins–Singer graded spectrum models. No equivalence with Freed–Lott cycles or physical anomaly theory is included. The inherited classical K-theory and compact Lie-group constructions use full Axiom of Choice.

## Proof

**Given:** The graded models, coefficient conditions, arbitrary Lie group and stated compact specializations.

**Internal claim (coherent family Fredholm indices and universal flag splittings; proved here).**

Assume full AC. Let $p:X\to B$ be a locally trivial bundle of closed compact even-dimensional manifolds over a finite CW complex. A vertical smooth structure, Hermitian Clifford bundles and coefficients, and a vertical Dirac operator are required to vary continuously in the smooth vertical topology. Give the chiral operators their $H^1\to L^2$ realizations. Then finite-dimensional cokernel augmentation defines a pullback-natural, additive family index, including relative compact parameter spaces. It induces a morphism
$$\tau_p:F(X_+,KU)\longrightarrow F(B_+,KU)$$
of $KU$-module spectra. The construction commutes coherently with pullback of the whole geometric family, restrictions to closed parameter subcomplexes, suspension and exterior Bott multiplication. Here $F(-,KU)$ denotes the function spectrum, with $KU$ the Bott spectrum obtained from stable complex bundles. No equality between this index and a Thom pushforward is asserted or used.

Suppose moreover that the untwisted vertical chiral operator is surjective and its kernel is canonically the constant line. Then
$$\tau_p p^*\simeq\operatorname{id}_{F(B_+,KU)}$$
by a homotopy coherent with all these restrictions. In particular this applies to the vertical Dolbeault Dirac operator of an associated flag bundle $P/T\to B$, where $G$ is compact connected, $T$ is maximal, the invariant complex flag metric is fixed, and the only harmonic $(0,*)$ forms on $G/T$ are the constant functions.

For a countable compatible tower of such compact bases and pulled-back families, the index and this splitting induce maps and a splitting on the homotopy inverse limits of the function spectra. Consequently both inverse-limit and Milnor $\lim^1$ terms are respected. For the flag towers coming from a faithful unitary representation of $G$, their homotopy colimits are $BG$ and $BT$. Hence $KU^*(BG)\to KU^*(BT)$ is split injective in every integer degree. For the ordinary-cohomology assertion additionally fix a $G$-equivariant smooth embedding of the flag as a closed orbit in $\mathbb P(V)$ for a finite-dimensional unitary $G$-representation $V$. The same flag towers then also give a split restriction in ordinary cohomology over $\mathbb C$, using the oriented Thom collapse and the normalized vertical Euler class as constructed in the last two proof paragraphs. This extra embedding is an explicit datum for this auxiliary assertion, not an additional assumption for the KU splitting.


**Proof of the internal claim.**


**Given:** The stated family, the finite-CW compact parameter pair $(A,A_0)$ when present, and [F12]–[F14].

1.1 Local trivializations of the geometric bundle identify the vertical Sobolev spaces with fixed Hilbert spaces. A first-order operator whose smooth vertical coefficients vary continuously varies in the bounded $H^1\to L^2$ norm. The same assertion holds with extra compact parameters and after twisting by a vertically smooth Hermitian coefficient bundle. Uniform elliptic constants on each compact parameter set follow from finitely many local elliptic estimates, absorbing sufficiently small coefficient perturbations in the left side. Thus these are continuous families of bounded Fredholm maps, not operators whose domains are left unspecified. Transition maps between Sobolev trivializations and their inverses are bounded and continuous; hence the kernels constructed below glue as vector bundles. [F12, given]

1.2 Let V be finite dimensional. A natural q-form operation assigns to each smooth manifold M and each V-valued one-form alpha on M a smooth q-form $\omega _{M}(\alpha )$, commuting with all smooth pullbacks. For $q=0$, restriction to each point shows this operation is constant, independent of alpha. For $q>0$ the value at p depends only on the q-jet of alpha at p. Locality follows by restriction to open neighborhoods. If a change in alpha is $f_{0}...f_{q}$ beta with every $f_{i}(p)=0$, introduce q+1 auxiliary coordinates $t_{0},...,t_{q}$ and the universal one-form $\alpha +t_{0}...t_{q}$ beta on M times $R^{q+1}$. At (p,0), any q-tuple of coordinate tangent vectors misses at least one of the q+1 auxiliary directions. Restricting to that coordinate hyperplane makes the added term identically zero, so the two output q-forms agree on the tuple. Multilinearity gives equality of the two q-forms at (p,0). Pull back along the graph of $(f_{0},...,f_{q})$. A general one-form difference with vanishing q-jet is a FINITE sum of terms of this kind, by the integral Taylor remainder in local coordinates (monomials of degree q+1 times smooth coefficient functions). Apply the argument successively to each summand. Thus the q-jet assertion holds without an assumed continuity or differential-operator theorem. The resulting map on the finite-dimensional jet space is smooth. To prove this, put the Taylor-polynomial one-form with its coefficients as parameters on $J^{q}$ times $R^{n}$, with only coordinate dx-components. The assigned output is an ordinary smooth q-form on this finite-dimensional manifold. Evaluation on the coordinate tangent vectors at $x=0$ gives the jet-value map as a smooth function of the jet parameters. Naturality under each parameter inclusion identifies this smooth map with the one just constructed. The jet component with j derivatives has scaling weight j+1 under coordinate dilations, whereas the output has weight q. A smooth function F satisfying `F(lambda J_0,lambda^2 J_1,...,lambda^(q+1) J_q)=lambda^q F(J_0,...,J_q)` is a weighted-homogeneous polynomial of weighted degree q. Expand F at the origin through ordinary Taylor degree q. The remainder on the scaled arguments is $O(\lambda ^{q+1})$; divide by $\lambda ^{q}$ and let lambda tend to zero. Coefficients of lower weighted powers must vanish, and only weight-q Taylor monomials survive. This proves polynomial dependence from smoothness and scaling, not from an unproved theorem about arbitrary set transformations. Naturality under linear coordinate maps now forces these polynomials to be formed only from alpha and d alpha. Here is the needed invariant tensor argument. An equivariant map from N covector slots to an alternating q-form vanishes unless $N=q$, by scalar dilations. For $N=q$, use independent diagonal changes of basis: a tensor basis vector with a repeated index cannot map to an alternating basis vector, since their diagonal weights differ. For distinct indices, permutation matrices force all coefficients to differ by precisely the alternating sign. Thus the map is a scalar multiple of complete antisymmetrization. Applying this separately to the V-label coefficients proves that every $j\ge 2$ jet term vanishes, since two of its derivative covector slots are symmetric. For $j=1$ it factors through the antisymmetric part d alpha. Zeroth jets give alpha. Conversely all polynomial combinations of alpha and d alpha are natural. Their coefficients are exterior-alternating in degree1 factors and symmetric in degree2 factors. Injectivity is seen by taking sufficiently many coordinate directions, setting independent components of alpha and d alpha, and evaluating on the corresponding distinct coordinate vectors. Consequently the algebra of natural forms is exactly `Lambda(V*) tensor Sym(V*),    deg alpha=1, deg d alpha=2,` `d(alpha)=d alpha, d(d alpha)=0.` This is the Koszul algebra, with its stated differential, proved here at the level of actual natural operations. [given, F1, algebra]

2.1 Write $D_b:H_b^+\to H_b^-$ for such a Fredholm family, temporarily suppressing the additional parameters. At a point choose finitely many vectors projecting onto the cokernel, extend them to local sections, and multiply by a partition of unity. On a compact base finitely many resulting global sections $j_i$ suffice. Surjectivity is open: if $S_0$ is a surjection and $Q_0$ a bounded right inverse, then $S Q_0=1+(S-S_0)Q_0$ is invertible when the last term has norm less than one; its Neumann series gives a right inverse. Consequently $S_I=D+j_I:H^+\oplus\mathbb C^I\longrightarrow H^-$ is surjective everywhere for a suitable finite list. Importantly, $\mathbb C^I$ is the free vector space on a finite set of **formal labels**, one for each chosen section. It is not the linear span of their values or the span of the sections. Labels are retained when sections vanish, become dependent, or are pulled back. This distinction prevents dimension loss under pullback. Its kernel $K_I$ is finite-dimensional. Locally the continuous projections $1-Q_I S_I$ identify nearby kernels, so $K_I$ is a finite-rank vector bundle. Its index representative is $K_I-\underline{\mathbb C}^{I}$. [F12, A1, step 1.1, construct]

2.2 Set $V=g$. In the one-form algebra replace d alpha by the curvature variable $F=d$ alpha+(1/2)[alpha,alpha]. This is an invertible triangular change of polynomial generators. For the gauge action alpha mapsto $\operatorname{Ad}(g^{-1})\alpha +g^{-1}dg$, direct differentiation gives F mapsto $\operatorname{Ad}(g^{-1})F$. Pull back along G times M and contract with a tangent vector xi in the G direction at the identity. The resulting contractions satisfy $i_{\xi }$ alpha=xi and $i_{\xi }$ $F=0$. Therefore horizontality for all xi kills precisely the exterior alpha generators: in a basis, successive contractions detect every nonzero positive exterior-degree coefficient. A horizontal element is a polynomial in F alone. Invariance under constant g in ALL components of G says exactly that its coefficient polynomial lies in Sym(g*)^G. Conversely invariant polynomials in F satisfy the full gauge equalizer. The Bianchi identity dF=[F,alpha] makes every such invariant polynomial closed, by infinitesimal invariance. Hence the basic natural-form complex of $B_{\nabla }$ G is `I*(g*)=Sym(g*)^G,   with twice polynomial grading and zero differential,` for every finite-dimensional Lie group G, without a compactness assumption. The passage to derived maps into the sheaf of closed forms introduces no hidden higher homotopy. For a closed-form sheaf P, regarded as a discrete simplicial sheaf, maps QX times $\Delta ^{n}$ to P factor through $\pi _{0}(QX)$, because $\Delta ^{n}$ is connected. Thus the derived mapping space is discrete, with set of components equal to sheaf maps from the sheafification of $\pi _{0}$ X to P. For an action-groupoid presentation these are exactly the gauge equalizer just computed. Sheafification does not change these maps because P is a sheaf. This proves the actual closed-basic target on the stack, rather than presupposing descent of an underived Hom. For a finite-type graded coefficient algebra $A_{C}$, apply the calculation degree by degree. On each finite-dimensional test manifold there are only finitely many contributing form degrees, so ordinary and completed coefficient tensor products coincide there. On the universal stack the compatible values form the levelwise inverse limit, giving `P_A^q := I_A^q(g*) = product_(k>=0) I^(2k)(g*) tensor (A_C)_(2k-q).` The projection towers are surjective degreewise.
