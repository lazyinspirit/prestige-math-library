## Example

Let $T=\{a,b,c\}$, let $m(a)=1$, $m(b)=-1$, $m(c)=2$, and let $k(s,t)=1$
for all $s,t\in T$. Observe at $X=(a,b)$ with noise covariance
$$R=\begin{pmatrix}1&1/2\\1/2&1\end{pmatrix}$$
and evaluate the selected Gaussian conditional-kernel version at $y=(0,1)$
for the test index $c$. The posterior law is $N(16/7,3/7)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; the finite index set and mean above; the constant covariance
kernel; and observation noise with law $N_2(0,R)$.

[A1] AC is required by the finite Gaussian-process and multivariate-normal
constructions. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] Any symmetric positive-semidefinite kernel on an arbitrary set defines a
canonical cylinder-space Gaussian process under AC.
[[thm-gaussian-process-exists-from-mean-and-positive-semidefinite-covariance-kernel]]

[F2] Under AC, every finite symmetric positive-semidefinite covariance matrix
defines a possibly singular Gaussian law. [[def-multivariate-normal-law]]

[F3] A finite product of sigma-finite measures exists and has the rectangle
product formula. [[thm-sigma-finite-product-measure-exists-is-rectangular-and-is-unique]]

[F4] Product-coordinate random elements are independent when their rectangle
probabilities factor. [[def-independent-random-elements]]
[[thm-rectangle-criterion-for-independent-random-elements]]

[F5] Coordinates on the cylinder sigma-algebra are measurable, and a finite
product sigma-algebra is generated by its coordinate sets.
[[def-coordinate-maps-and-cylinder-sigma-algebra]]
[[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]]

[F6] With positive-definite observed covariance $C$, the Gaussian conditional
kernel has mean $m_*+K_{*X}C^{-1}(y-m_X)$ and covariance
$K_{**}-K_{*X}C^{-1}K_{X*}$. [[thm-finite-gaussian-process-posterior-and-predictive-laws]]

## Verification

**Given:** The parameters and observed value in the Example.

1.1 For every finite coefficient list on $T$, the constant kernel has quadratic form $(\sum_i a_i)^2\ge0$. The noise matrix is positive definite because $u^2+uv+v^2=(u+v/2)^2+3v^2/4>0$ for $(u,v)\ne(0,0)$. By [F1] there is a cylinder-space GP law $\mu$ and [F2] gives the noise law. Their product probability exists by [F3]; its process and noise coordinates are measurable by [F5] and independent by [F4]. Thus this is a model satisfying the theorem's noise assumption. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 The observed and test covariance blocks are $K_{XX}=J_2$, $K_{cX}=(1,1)$, and $K_{cc}=1$. Thus $C=J_2+R=\begin{pmatrix}2&3/2\\3/2&2\end{pmatrix}$, which is positive definite since its quadratic form is $2(u+3v/4)^2+7v^2/8$. Direct multiplication gives $C^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}8/7&-6/7\\-6/7&8/7\end{pmatrix}$. [given, algebra]

2.1 Since $m_X=(1,-1)$, $m_c=2$, and $y-m_X=(-1,2)$, $C^{-1}(y-m_X)=(-20/7,22/7)$ and $K_{cX}C^{-1}(y-m_X)=2/7$. The posterior mean in [F6] is therefore $2+2/7=16/7$. [F6, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Also $C^{-1}K_{Xc}=(2/7,2/7)^T$, so $K_{cX}C^{-1}K_{Xc}=4/7$. The posterior variance is $1-4/7=3/7$, proving the displayed conditional-kernel value. This is the value of the all-$y$ kernel version specified in [F6] at $y=(0,1)$; the theorem does not make conditional-law values pointwise unique. [F6, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
