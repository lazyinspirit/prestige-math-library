## Example

Assume the Axiom of Choice, and let $k$ be a field. In
$A=k\llbracket x,y,z\rrbracket/(xy)$, the pair $x+y,z$ is a system of
parameters and a regular sequence.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice and the displayed ring. Finite polynomial rings over a field are Noetherian and have dimension equal to their number of variables; localizations of Noetherian rings are Noetherian ([[cor-finite-variable-polynomial-ring-noetherian]], [[cor-dimension-of-a-finite-polynomial-ring-over-a-field]], [[thm-noetherian-ring-quotients-and-localisations]]). Completion of a Noetherian local ring is Noetherian local and preserves its dimension under the assumed choice premise ([[thm-completion-of-a-noetherian-local-ring]], [[thm-completion-preserves-dimension-and-hilbert-samuel-data]]). Systems of parameters have length equal to ring dimension and generate an ideal with maximal radical ([[def-system-of-parameters-and-parameter-ideal]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 For $r=1,2,3$, the coordinate localization $P_r=k[x_1,\ldots,x_r]_{(x_1,\ldots,x_r)}$ is Noetherian local. Its dimension is $r$: the coordinate-prime chain has length $r$, while localization cannot increase the dimension $r$ of the polynomial ring. The quotients $P_r/(x_1,\ldots,x_r)^q$ are the degree-$<q$ truncated polynomial rings. Their inverse limit is $k\llbracket x_1,\ldots,x_r\rrbracket$, since compatible truncations give exactly one coefficient per monomial and multiplication agrees coefficientwise. By the completion results in the Given paragraph, this series ring is Noetherian local of dimension $r$, with maximal ideal generated by the variables and residue field $k$. It is a domain: the lowest nonzero homogeneous parts of two nonzero series multiply to a nonzero polynomial over the field $k$. [given, algebra]

2.1 Put $Q=k\llbracket x,y,z\rrbracket$. Its quotient $A=Q/(xy)$ is Noetherian local. Every prime of $Q$ containing $xy$ contains $x$ or $y$; any chain of such primes lies above one of these two ideals and so has length at most $2$, because $Q/(x)\cong k\llbracket y,z\rrbracket$ and $Q/(y)\cong k\llbracket x,z\rrbracket$ both have dimension $2$. Either branch also supplies a chain of length $2$, hence $\dim A=2$. [step 1.1, given, algebra]

2.2 Suppose $(x+y)g\in(xy)$ in $Q$. Reducing modulo $(x)$ gives $yg\equiv0$ in the domain $k\llbracket y,z\rrbracket$, so $g\in(x)$; reducing modulo $(y)$ similarly gives $g\in(y)$. Coefficientwise, $(x)\cap(y)=(xy)$ in $Q$, so $g\in(xy)$. Hence $x+y$ is a nonzerodivisor on $A$. [step 1.1, given, algebra]

3.1 The quotient $A/(x+y,z)\cong k\llbracket x\rrbracket/(x^2)$ is nonzero and has only one prime, $(x)$, since $x$ is nilpotent and the quotient by $(x)$ is $k$. Thus $(x+y,z)A$ has radical the maximal ideal $(x,y,z)A$. Together with step 2.1, this makes $x+y,z$ a system of parameters. [step 2.1, given, algebra]

4.1 After quotienting by $x+y$ one has $A/(x+y)\cong k\llbracket x,z\rrbracket/(x^2) \cong k\llbracket z\rrbracket\oplus xk\llbracket z\rrbracket$ as a $k\llbracket z\rrbracket$-module. Multiplication by $z$ is injective on both summands, and the final quotient is nonzero by step 3.1. Therefore $x+y,z$ is a regular sequence on $A$, as claimed. [step 3.1, step 2.2, algebra] ∎
