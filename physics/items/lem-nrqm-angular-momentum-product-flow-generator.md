## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and the Axiom of Countable Choice
([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-countable-choice]]). Let $H$ be a nonzero
complex Hilbert space ([[def-hilbert-space]]), let $A$ be a self-adjoint
operator on $H$ with domain $D(A)$
([[def-adjoint-of-a-densely-defined-unbounded-operator]],
[[def-symmetric-self-adjoint-and-essentially-self-adjoint]]), and let $B$ be a
bounded self-adjoint operator on $H$
([[def-bounded-linear-operator]],
[[def-self-adjoint-positive-unitary-and-normal-operator]]) with
$$B\,e^{isA}=e^{isA}B\qquad(s\in\mathbb R),$$
where $e^{isA}=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{is\lambda}\,dE_A(\lambda)$ is the unitary
group of $A$ in the spectral calculus
([[lem-self-adjoint-operator-generates-a-strongly-continuous-unitary-group]],
[[thm-bounded-borel-pvm-integral]],
[[def-projection-valued-measure]]). Put $A+B$ on its natural domain
$D(A+B):=D(A)\cap D(B)=D(A)$. Then:

(a) $A+B$ is self-adjoint;

(b) $e^{isB}e^{itA}=e^{itA}e^{isB}$ for all $s,t\in\mathbb R$; the flows of
$A$ and of the bounded $B$ commute;

(c) the family $s\mapsto P(s):=e^{isA}e^{isB}$ is a strongly continuous
one-parameter unitary group
([[def-strongly-continuous-one-parameter-unitary-group]]), and its
infinitesimal generator $G$
([[def-infinitesimal-generator-of-a-unitary-group]]) is $G=i(A+B)$: the
quotient $\frac1s\bigl(P(s)x-x\bigr)$ converges as $s\to0$ exactly for
$x\in D(A)$, with limit $i(A+B)x$. Consequently $P(s)=e^{is(A+B)}$ for every
$s\in\mathbb R$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the Axiom of Choice and the Axiom of Countable Choice; a nonzero complex Hilbert space $H$; a self-adjoint operator $A$; a bounded self-adjoint operator $B$ commuting with every $e^{isA}$; real $s,t,\lambda$; a unit vector or vector $x$.

[F1] **Flow calculus.** For a self-adjoint $T$ with spectral projection-valued measure $E_T$ and $U_T(s):=e^{isT}$: $U_T$ is a strongly continuous one-parameter unitary group with $U_T(s)^{*}=U_T(-s)$, $U_T(s)D(T)=D(T)$ and $TU_T(s)=U_T(s)T$ on $D(T)$; the quotient $\frac1s(U_T(s)x-x)$ converges as $s\to0$ exactly for $x\in D(T)$, with limit $iTx$; and for every bounded Borel $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb C$ one has $\|f(T)\|\le\sup|f|$ and $f(T)U_T(s)=U_T(s)f(T)$, where $f(T)=\int f\,dE_T$. The norm bound follows from the bounded PVM integral; the commutation follows because both factors are integrals against $E_T$ and the integral map is multiplicative, so $\Phi_{E_T}(f)\Phi_{E_T}(\lambda\mapsto e^{is\lambda}) =\Phi_{E_T}(f(\lambda)e^{is\lambda}) =\Phi_{E_T}(\lambda\mapsto e^{is\lambda})\Phi_{E_T}(f)$ ([[thm-bounded-borel-pvm-integral]], [[thm-pvm-integral-is-a-star-homomorphism]]). ([[lem-self-adjoint-operator-generates-a-strongly-continuous-unitary-group]], [[thm-bounded-borel-pvm-integral]]).

[F2] **Resolvents.** For $\lambda\ne0$ put $r_{\lambda}(\mu):=(\mu+i\lambda)^{-1}$ and $R_{\lambda}:=\Phi_{E_A}(r_{\lambda})$. The bounded PVM integral gives $\|R_{\lambda}\|\le1/|\lambda|$. The spectral domain formula gives $\int\mu^{2}|r_{\lambda}(\mu)|^{2}\,dE_{A,x}(\mu)\le\|x\|^{2}$, so $R_{\lambda}x\in D(A)$ for every $x\in H$; the unbounded Borel calculus product rule then gives $(A+i\lambda)R_{\lambda}=\Phi_{E_A}(\mu r_{\lambda}+i\lambda r_{\lambda})=I$ on $H$. For $y\in D(A)$ the same product rule gives $R_{\lambda}(A+i\lambda)y=\Phi_{E_A}(r_{\lambda}(\mu+i\lambda))y=y$. Thus $R_{\lambda}=(A+i\lambda)^{-1}$ and $A+i\lambda:D(A)\to H$ is bijective. These claims use the spectral domain formula and bounded PVM norm bound ([[thm-spectral-theorem-for-unbounded-self-adjoint-operators]], [[thm-bounded-borel-pvm-integral]]) and the unbounded Borel calculus ([[thm-unbounded-borel-functional-calculus]] clause 2).

[F3] **Neumann series.** If $R\in\mathcal B(H)$ with $\|R\|<1$, then $I-R$ is invertible and $(I-R)^{-1}=\sum_{n\ge0}R^n$ in operator norm ([[lem-neumann-series-and-small-perturbations-of-bounded-inverses]] clause 1, [[def-operator-norm]]).

[F4] **Stone uniqueness.** Two strongly continuous one-parameter unitary groups with the same infinitesimal generator agree at every time; equivalently $s\mapsto e^{isT}$ is determined by the self-adjoint $T$ ([[thm-stone-one-parameter-unitary-groups]], [[def-infinitesimal-generator-of-a-unitary-group]]).

[F5] For bounded operators $S,T$ between compatible normed spaces, their composition satisfies $\|ST\|\le\|S\|\,\|T\|$ ([[lem-composition-operator-norm-inequality]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; prove self-adjointness of $A+B$ by the Neumann series for the resolvent of $A$, differentiate the conjugation curve to obtain the commutation of the two flows, and identify the generator of the product group by comparing difference quotients, concluding with Stone's theorem.

**Given:** The data of the statement and [F1]-[F5].

1.1 [Symmetry and domain of $A+B$] The domain $D(A+B)=D(A)$ is a dense linear subspace of $H$ because $D(A)$ is dense and $B$ is everywhere defined and linear; for $x,y\in D(A)$, using that $A$ is symmetric and $B$ is self-adjoint and bounded, $\langle(A+B)x,y\rangle=\langle Ax,y\rangle+\langle Bx,y\rangle=\langle x,Ay\rangle+\langle x,By\rangle=\langle x,(A+B)y\rangle$, so $A+B$ is densely defined and symmetric. [given, algebra]

1.2 [Surjectivity of $(A+B)\pm i\lambda$] Fix $\lambda>\|B\|$ (if $B=0$ take $\lambda=1$); by [F2] the map $(A+i\lambda):D(A)\to H$ is a bijection with inverse of norm at most $1/\lambda$, so [F5] gives $\|(A+i\lambda)^{-1}B\|\le\|B\|/\lambda<1$; [F3] applied to $-R$ makes $I+R$ invertible on $H$, where $R:=(A+i\lambda)^{-1}B$. Since $R(H)\subseteq D(A)$, for any $y\in D(A)$ the vector $x:=(I+R)^{-1}y$ satisfies $x=y-Rx\in D(A)$, so $I+R$ maps $D(A)$ onto $D(A)$. For $x\in D(A)$, $Rx\in D(A)$ and $(A+B+i\lambda)x=(A+i\lambda)(I+R)x$; therefore $(A+B)+i\lambda$ maps $D(A)$ onto $H$, and the same argument with $-\lambda$ shows that $(A+B)-i\lambda$ maps $D(A)$ onto $H$. [F2, F3, F5, algebra]

1.3 [Adjoint inclusion] Let $y\in D((A+B)^{*})$ and put $z:=(A+B)^{*}y$; for every $x\in D(A)$ one has $\langle Ax,y\rangle+\langle Bx,y\rangle=\langle x,z\rangle$, that is $\langle Ax,y\rangle=\langle x,z-By\rangle$ because $B^{*}=B$ and $By\in H$, and since $A$ is self-adjoint the existence of the vector $z-By$ representing $x\mapsto\langle Ax,y\rangle$ forces $y\in D(A)$ with $Ay=z-By$, so $D((A+B)^{*})\subseteq D(A)=D(A+B)$ with $(A+B)^{*}y=(A+B)y$ there. [F1, given, algebra]

1.4 [Commutation of the flows] Fix $t$ and $x\in H$ and define $g(s):=e^{isB}e^{itA}e^{-isB}x$; by [F1] applied to the self-adjoint operator $B$ (whose domain is all of $H$) the difference quotients $\frac1h(e^{i(s+h)B}y-e^{isB}y)\to iBe^{isB}y$ and $\frac1h(e^{-i(s+h)B}y-e^{-isB}y)\to-ie^{-isB}By$ for every $y$, so the product rule gives $g'(s)=e^{isB}\bigl(iBe^{itA}-ie^{itA}B\bigr)e^{-isB}x$, and the hypothesis $Be^{itA}=e^{itA}B$ makes this derivative vanish for every $s$; hence $g$ is constant with value $g(0)=e^{itA}x$, that is $e^{isB}e^{itA}=e^{itA}e^{isB}$ for all $s,t$, which is the first identity of (b). [F1, given, algebra]

1.5 [Quotient of the product group on $D(A)$] Put $P(s):=e^{isA}e^{isB}$ and let $x\in D(A)$; the algebraic identity $\frac1s(P(s)x-x)=e^{isA}\frac1s(e^{isB}x-x)+\frac1s(e^{isA}x-x)$ holds for every $s\ne0$, and as $s\to0$ the first term converges to $iBx$ because $\frac1s(e^{isB}x-x)\to iBx$ in norm by [F1] and $e^{isA}\to I$ strongly, while the second converges to $iAx$ by [F1]; hence the quotient of $P$ at $x$ converges, with limit $i(A+B)x$. [F1, algebra]

2.1 [Self-adjointness] Step 1.1 gives $A+B\subseteq(A+B)^{*}$ and step 1.3 gives $(A+B)^{*}\subseteq A+B$ including domains, so $A+B=(A+B)^{*}$, which is (a). [step 1.1, step 1.3]

2.2 [Group property and strong continuity] By step 1.4, $P(s)P(t)=e^{isA}e^{isB}e^{itA}e^{itB}=e^{isA}e^{itA}e^{isB}e^{itB}=P(s+t)$ and $P(s)^{*}=e^{-isB}e^{-isA}=e^{-isA}e^{-isB}=P(-s)$; each factor is strongly continuous, and the estimate $\|P(s)x-P(s_0)x\|\le\|e^{isA}(e^{isB}x-e^{is_0B}x)\|+\|(e^{isA}-e^{is_0A})e^{is_0B}x\|$ shows that $P$ is strongly continuous, hence a strongly continuous one-parameter unitary group. [step 1.4, F1, algebra]

2.3 [No larger domain] Let $x\in H$ and suppose that $\frac1s(P(s)x-x)$ converges, to $y\in H$; by step 1.4, $P(s)=e^{isB}e^{isA}$, so $e^{isA}x=e^{-isB}P(s)x$ and the identity $\frac1s(e^{isA}x-x)=e^{-isB}\frac1s(P(s)x-x)+\frac1s(e^{-isB}x-x)$ holds; the first term converges to $y$ because $e^{-isB}\to I$ strongly and $\frac1s(P(s)x-x)\to y$, and the second converges to $-iBx$ by [F1] applied to the self-adjoint operator $-B$, so $\frac1s(e^{isA}x-x)$ converges and [F1] gives $x\in D(A)$ with $y=i(A+B)x$. [step 1.4, F1, algebra]

3.1 [Generator identification] By step 2.2 the family $P$ is a strongly continuous one-parameter unitary group with infinitesimal generator $G$; steps 1.5 and 2.3 show that the quotient of $P$ at $x$ converges exactly for $x\in D(A)$, with limit $i(A+B)x$; hence $D(G)=D(A)$ and $G=i(A+B)$, which is the generator statement of (c). [step 1.5, step 2.2, step 2.3]

4.1 [Identification of the groups] By [F1] the family $s\mapsto e^{is(A+B)}$ is a strongly continuous one-parameter unitary group whose quotient at $x$ converges exactly for $x\in D(A+B)=D(A)$, to $i(A+B)x$; by step 3.1 it has the same infinitesimal generator $i(A+B)$ as $P$, so Stone's theorem [F4] yields $P(s)=e^{is(A+B)}$ for every $s$, completing (c); (b) is step 1.4. [step 1.4, step 3.1, F1, F4] ∎

## Remarks

- **Why the hypothesis is a commutation with the flow.** The hypothesis $Be^{isA}=e^{isA}B$ for all $s$ is a commutation of the bounded operator with every element of the unitary group of $A$; it implies, but is not assumed as, a formal commutator identity on a small domain, and it is the exact form in which the model's internal matrices commute with the free flow.
- **Bounded perturbation.** Clause (a) is the standard self-adjointness of a self-adjoint operator perturbed by a bounded self-adjoint operator; the flow hypothesis is not needed for it, only for the product formula of (b) and the generator statement of (c).
