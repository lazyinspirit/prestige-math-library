## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ ([[def-countable-choice]]). Let $f:M\to N$ be smooth and let $Z\subseteq N$ be a closed embedded
submanifold. Every neighbourhood of $f$ in the strong smooth topology contains
a smooth map $g$ that is transverse to $Z$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, a smooth map $f:M\to N$ between smooth manifolds without boundary, a closed embedded submanifold $Z\subseteq N$, and a strong smooth neighbourhood $\mathcal U$ of $f$.

[A1] Countable Choice is [[def-countable-choice]]. The manifold convention is Hausdorff and second countable ([[def-smooth-manifold]]). The embedding, tubular neighbourhood, chart-cover, partition and parametric-transversality suppliers below carry this same premise; no dependent choice or uncountable family choice is used.

[F1] The strong $C^r$ topology, for a finite integer $r\ge0$, has basic neighbourhoods specified by a locally finite source chart family $(U_i,\varphi_i)$, compact sets $K_i\subseteq U_i$, target charts $(V_i,\psi_i)$ with $f(K_i)\subseteq V_i$, and tolerances $b_i>0$. They require $h(K_i)\subseteq V_i$ and $$\max_{|\alpha|\le r}\sup_{x\in\varphi_i(K_i)}\left|D^\alpha(\psi_i\circ h\circ\varphi_i^{-1})(x)-D^\alpha(\psi_i\circ f\circ\varphi_i^{-1})(x)\right|<b_i$$ for every $i$. The strong smooth topology on $C^\infty(M,N)$ is generated by the restrictions of these topologies for finite $r$. Thus any neighbourhood contains a finite intersection of such basic sets. Combining their finitely many locally finite chart families gives locally finite compact constraints with a finite derivative order $r_i$ for each constraint. This is the coordinate definition of the topology, not a density assumption.

[L1] Under [A1], there is a smooth Euclidean embedding $e:N\hookrightarrow\mathbb R^d$ ([[thm-every-smooth-manifold-embeds-in-some-finite-dimensional-euclidean-space]]). The Euclidean tubular theorem and its normal-addition construction supply an open set $T\subseteq\mathbb R^d$ containing $e(N)$ and a smooth retraction $R:T\to N$ with $R\circ e=\operatorname{id}_N$; $R$ is a submersion because in normal-bundle coordinates it is the bundle projection ([[thm-euclidean-tubular-neighbourhood-theorem]], [[def-normal-addition-map-for-a-euclidean-submanifold]]).

[L2] Under [A1], an open cover has a countable subordinate relatively compact coordinate-ball cover and a countable locally finite shrinking; a smooth subordinate partition of unity exists ([[lem-every-open-cover-of-a-manifold-has-a-countable-cover-by-relatively-compact-coordinate-balls-subordinate-to-it]], [[lem-a-countable-coordinate-ball-cover-has-a-countable-locally-finite-shrinking]], [[thm-smooth-partitions-of-unity-exist-on-manifolds]]). In particular one obtains a locally finite compact coordinate cover and a countable partition $(\eta_j)_{j\ge1}$ with compact locally finite supports; a finite partition is padded by zero functions.

[L3] Locally finite smooth sums are smooth ([[thm-a-locally-finite-sum-of-smooth-functions-is-smooth]]). The chain and product rules, iterated in coordinates, control derivatives of smooth composites ([[thm-chain-rule-for-differentials-of-smooth-maps]], [[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]], [[def-ck-and-multi-index-notation-in-several-variables]]). Continuous functions on compact metric sets attain bounds and are uniformly continuous ([[thm-extreme-value-metric]], [[thm-heine-cantor-metric]]).

[L4] Under [A1], if a smooth evaluation map $\mathcal F:M\times B\to N$ is transverse to $Z$, its nontransverse slice parameters form a null subset of $B$ ([[thm-parametric-transversality]]). Nullity in a Euclidean chart is the cube-cover nullity of [[def-null-subset-of-a-smooth-manifold]] and [[def-null-and-content-zero-in-rn]]. A compact null subset of positive-dimensional Euclidean space has content zero ([[lem-compact-null-iff-content-zero-in-rn]]); cube volumes and finite grid additivity are [[def-multidimensional-rectangle-and-volume]].

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct, by a smooth perturbation amplitude with prescribed local derivative bounds and a finite-dimensional Sard parameter.

1.1 If $M=\varnothing$, its unique map is already transverse and lies in $\mathcal U$. Otherwise $N\ne\varnothing$. Fix $e,T,R$ as in [L1]. The ambient dimension supplied by the embedding theorem is positive, so take $d\ge1$. [given, L1]

2.1 Choose a finite intersection of the basic sets of [F1] contained in $\mathcal U$. List its compact chart constraints $(K_i,\varphi_i,\psi_i,r_i,b_i)$. Their nonempty source chart domains form an at-most-countable family: in a countable basis assign to each domain the least nonempty basis member contained in it. Each basis member is assigned only finitely many domains, since a point of that member belongs to all of them and the family is locally finite. Countable Choice enumerates these finite fibres. Add a locally finite compact coordinate cover subordinate to the open sets $f^{-1}(V)$ for target chart domains $V$, using [L2], with derivative order zero and tolerance one. The combined constraints are still countable and locally finite; their compact sets now cover $M$. They only impose extra restrictions, so satisfying all of them implies membership in $\mathcal U$. [A1, F1, L2, step 1.1]

3.1 For one nonempty $K_i$ consider, in source coordinates, $$E_i(x,v):=\psi_i\bigl(R(e(f(\varphi_i^{-1}(x)))+v)\bigr).$$ This is smooth on an open neighbourhood of $\varphi_i(K_i)\times\{0\}$. Compactness gives a positive $t_i$ and a compact neighbourhood of $\varphi_i(K_i)$ within its coordinate domain such that $E_i$ is defined on that neighbourhood times $\{|v|\le t_i\}$, with the retraction argument in $T$ and the result in $V_i$. Every finite collection of derivatives of $E_i$ is bounded and uniformly continuous on this compact product by [L3]. [L1, L3, step 2.1]

4.1 There is $a_i>0$ such that any smooth $u:M\to\mathbb R^d$ with $$\max_{|\alpha|\le r_i}\sup_{x\in\varphi_i(K_i)}|D^\alpha(u\circ\varphi_i^{-1})(x)|<a_i$$ makes $R(e\circ f+u)$ satisfy constraint $i$. Here the assertion only concerns points of $K_i$ where the expression is defined; the zeroth-order bound ensures that it is defined there. To verify the derivative estimate, repeated chain and product rules express each $D^\alpha(E_i(x,u(x)))$ as a finite sum of derivatives $D_x^\beta D_v^\gamma E_i(x,u(x))$ multiplied by products of derivatives $D^\nu u(x)$. At $u=0$ and all its derivatives zero the sum equals $D^\alpha E_i(x,0)$. As the finitely many derivative variables tend to zero, terms having a positive-order derivative of $u$ tend uniformly to zero by the compact derivative bounds of step 3.1; terms without such a factor converge uniformly by uniform continuity there. Choose $a_i<t_i$ small enough to make all these finitely many differences less than $b_i$. Take the first number $2^{-q}$, $q\ge1$, for which these conclusions hold, so choosing all $a_i$ makes no new family choice. Empty $K_i$ may be omitted. [L3, step 3.1]

5.1 Choose the partition $(\eta_j)$ from [L2]. For each $i,j$ put $$A_{ij}:=\max_{|\alpha|\le r_i}\sup_{x\in\varphi_i(K_i)}|D^\alpha(\eta_j\circ\varphi_i^{-1})(x)|.$$ These numbers are finite by [L3]. For each $j$, the set $I_j:=\{i:K_i\cap\operatorname{supp}\eta_j\ne\varnothing\}$ is finite: every point of the compact support has a neighbourhood meeting only finitely many $K_i$, and a finite subcover gives finitely many indices in total. Define $$c_j:=\min\left(\{1\}\cup\left\{\frac{a_i2^{-j-1}}{1+A_{ij}}:i\in I_j\right\}\right)>0,\qquad \varepsilon:=\sum_{j\ge1}c_j\eta_j.$$ The sum is locally finite and smooth, and $\varepsilon>0$ because the nonnegative partition sums to one and every $c_j>0$. All coefficients are explicit finite minima. [L2, L3, step 4.1]

6.1 For every $i$, the compact $K_i$ meets only finitely many partition supports, by the same compactness argument. Outside a support a smooth function and all its derivatives vanish. Therefore differentiation of the locally finite sum and the triangle inequality give $$\max_{|\alpha|\le r_i}\sup_{\varphi_i(K_i)}|D^\alpha(\varepsilon\circ\varphi_i^{-1})|\le\sum_{j: i\in I_j}c_jA_{ij}\le a_i\sum_{j\ge1}2^{-j-1}=\frac{a_i}{2}<a_i.$$ This controls the entire prescribed finite jet on every compact, with tolerances allowed to shrink independently towards infinity. [L3, step 5.1]

7.1 Let $B=\{a\in\mathbb R^d:|a|<1\}$ and define $$\mathcal F(x,a):=R(e(f(x))+\varepsilon(x)a).$$ For every $a\in B$, each derivative of $u_a(x):=\varepsilon(x)a$ in constraint $i$ is bounded by $|a|$ times the corresponding bound of step 6.1. Since the $K_i$ cover $M$, step 4.1 makes the retraction argument lie in $T$ at every $x$, so $\mathcal F$ is a globally defined smooth map. It also makes every slice $\mathcal F_a$ satisfy every original neighbourhood constraint, hence $\mathcal F_a\in\mathcal U$. Finally $\mathcal F_0=R\circ e\circ f=f$. [L1, L3, step 2.1, step 4.1, step 6.1]

8.1 The parameter differential at $(x,a)$ is $$D_a\mathcal F(x,a)=\varepsilon(x)\,dR_{e(f(x))+\varepsilon(x)a}:\mathbb R^d\longrightarrow T_{\mathcal F(x,a)}N.$$ It is surjective because $\varepsilon(x)>0$ and $R$ is a submersion. Thus the total evaluation map is a submersion and therefore transverse to $Z$: its differential already spans the whole target tangent space. [L1, L3, step 5.1, step 7.1]

9.1 By [L4], the bad slice parameters are null in $B$. They cannot exhaust $B$. Indeed $B$ contains a closed positive-side cube $Q$. If $Q$ were null, compact-null/content-zero equivalence in [L4] would give finite cube covers of arbitrarily small total volume. Every finite cube cover of $Q$ has total volume at least $\operatorname{vol}(Q)>0$: subdivide a containing rectangle at all cube endpoints, and assign each positive-volume grid cell of $Q$ to the least covering cube that contains its midpoint. Such a cell lies wholly in that cube; finite grid additivity gives the stated volume bound. This contradiction proves that some $a\in B$ is good. No countable sequence of good parameters is selected. [L4, step 8.1]

10.1 Choose this one good $a$ and set $g=\mathcal F_a$. It is smooth and transverse to $Z$ by step 9.1, and lies in $\mathcal U$ by step 7.1. This proves the full strong smooth density, including noncompact $M$. Empty source was treated in step 1.1; zero-dimensional source or target is allowed throughout, and no positive target dimension was needed for surjectivity onto its tangent spaces. Exactly [A1] is inherited through the stated suppliers; the jet radii and amplitude coefficients were defined canonically, and the final parameter selection is a single existential choice. [A1, step 1.1, step 7.1, step 9.1] ∎

## Source locator

Hirsch, *Differential Topology*, Ch. 2 §1, printed pp. 34–36, defines the strong finite-order chart neighbourhoods and the strong smooth topology generated by their restrictions. Ch. 3 §2, Theorem 2.1, printed pp. 74–77, proves transversality density through Lemma 2.3's Sard translations and Theorem 2.2's locally finite globalization/Baire argument. The proof here uses the already proved Euclidean embedding, smooth tubular retraction, partition and parametric Sard suppliers to replace that globalization: steps 3.1–7.1 prove the required noncompact derivative control directly. The external density theorem is not used as a proof premise.
