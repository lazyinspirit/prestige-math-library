## Statement

Assume AC. Let k be any characteristic-zero field, Q a sharp toric monoid, and X0 -> S0=Spec(Q -> k) a proper log-smooth saturated morphism of fine saturated log schemes of pure relative log dimension d. Then every $H^{q}(X0,\Omega ^{p}_{\log })$ is finite dimensional and the central Hodge-to-de Rham spectral sequence degenerates at E1.

In addition, take the fixed local-model, absolute-calculus and bounded finite-cover curved Thom–Whitney construction specified in the Facts, before any global deformation is assumed. Write Lambda=k[[Q]] and $C_{A}$ for its corrected total de Rham complex over an Artin local Lambda-algebra A with residue field k. Then $H^{n}(C_{A})$ -> $H^{n}(C_{k})$ is surjective for every n and every such A. Each $H^{n}(C_{A})$ is finite free of rank $\operatorname{dim}_{k}$ $H^{n}(C_{k})$, and the canonical map $H^{n}(C_{A})$ $\otimes _{A}$ A' -> $H^{n}(C_{A}')$ is an isomorphism for every map of these Artin bases, for the same fixed construction.

No projectivity assumption on X0, global deformation over A, ordinary smoothness of the underlying central scheme, or embedding of the entire field k into C is required.

## Proof

1.1 **Exact construction interface.** Fix a finite affine cover of the separated proper X0, its finite nerve, and compatible local log-smooth lift models over each monomial Artin quotient of Lambda=k[[Q]]. The local models and relative log forms commute with the actual fixed base maps. Use the same cover, gluing operators and Thom–Whitney carrier after every base change; do not select a new gluing independently. The corrected differential is d=partial+bar-partial+ell contraction on the relative de Rham carrier. Its square is zero by the curved-calculus identities. Locally there is an absolute extension DA, a closed base one-form rho when the base monoid is N, and a nilpotent finite differential-order gauge conjugating the corrected absolute total differential to the ordinary local absolute de Rham/TW differential. These maps preserve the quotient to relative forms and reduce to the identity centrally. The curved Thom–Whitney control premise below supplies precisely these objects, including boundedness, termwise Artin flatness and literal fixed-data base change. Here is the algebra behind the last requirements: degree q TW polynomial forms on a nerve simplex vanish for q greater than its dimension, and the nerve is finite. Boundary restriction of graded polynomial forms is surjective: the face ideals $(t_{i},dt_{i})$ have their expected intersection/product identities, so faces can be lifted recursively. Splitting these graded boundary maps over k expresses each term by sums/extensions of the fixed affine-intersection modules tensored with vector spaces of polynomial simplex forms. These terms are flat and base change along any Artin algebra map, even a nonflat map. The finite-dimensional central cohomology is that of the usual finite Cech/de Rham complex, via polynomial-simplex integration and its elementary integration homotopy. This is the required construction interface, not a conclusion deduced from a hypothetical global family. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 **A. Central E1, with properness and all characteristic-zero fields retained.** Finiteness first requires no analytic comparison. $\Omega ^{p}_{\log }$ is finite locally free because f0 is log smooth. The actual published root supplier `items/thm-proper-pushforward-coherent.md` gives finite-dimensional $H^{q}$ for proper X0 over k. All $p>d$ vanish and a finite affine cover bounds q, so all spectral sequences here are finite. Spread the finitely presented proper scheme, its finite chart/overlap data, and its fs log charts over a finitely generated integral Z-algebra B in k. The same finite monoids retain saturatedness; the log-smooth chart conditions are open finite-presentation conditions, so denominators may be inverted to retain them. Properness can be spread without assuming projectivity: choose a Chow cover X' -> X with X' projective, as supplied by `items/lem-chow-lemma-proper-noetherian.md`, and spread its projective equations and map. The spread target is separated after localization; the graph makes the map from the projective source proper. Surjectivity holds after localization since the constructible complement has empty generic fibre. Universally surjective proper descent of universal closedness then makes the target proper. Thus this step does not replace X by a projective variety. One may further arrange B smooth over a localization of Z. Indeed the finitely generated characteristic-zero fraction field has a separating transcendence basis, and is a finite separable extension of the rational-function field. A primitive-element equation, with its derivative and all denominators inverted, gives a finite etale model over a localization of a polynomial Z-algebra. All finite coefficients in the preceding construction can be included by further localization. Closed residue fields are finite and perfect, and one may choose residue characteristic $p>d$ outside any further finite list. For clarity, the base-change argument includes de Rham hypercohomology even though d is not $O_{X}$-linear. A finite affine cover computes it by a bounded complex of flat B-modules: its columns are Cech modules of finite locally free relative log forms on the flat scheme $X_{B}$. The differential is B-linear. Proper coherent cohomology makes each column cohomology finite, hence makes total cohomology finite by a finite spectral sequence. Here is the required universal finite replacement, without adding an unimported supplier. The smooth finite-type ring B is regular Noetherian of finite dimension, so every finite B-module has a finite resolution by finite projectives: successive finite free syzygies terminate in a projective by the finite global-dimension bound [F3]. Build a finite-projective model of a bounded complex K with finite cohomology by its finite Postnikov truncations. At the first nonzero cohomology degree, use such a resolution of that cohomology module, shifted to that degree. At the next degree j, the truncation triangle is determined by a map $H^{j}(K)[-j]$ -> $\tau_{<j}$ K[1]. Replace $H^{j}$ by its finite projective resolution $P_{j}$ and the preceding truncation by its already built finite-projective model E. The connecting map is represented by an actual chain map $P_{j}[-j]$ -> E[1]: a bounded complex of projectives lifts maps across a quasi-isomorphism by lifting successively against its acyclic mapping cone; the obstruction at each step is a cycle and exactness makes it a boundary. The shifted cone of this chain map is the next finite-projective model. There are finitely many cohomology degrees, so this constructs a bounded finite-projective E and a quasi-isomorphism E -> K. Its bounded mapping cone consists of flat modules and is acyclic. Its cycles are flat by descending induction from the top: in 0 -> $Z^{i}$ -> $Cone^{i}$ -> $Z^{i+1}$ -> 0, flatness of the quotient preserves tensor exactness, and the injection criterion then makes $Z^{i}$ flat. Every one of these rows remains exact after tensoring with ANY B-module. Thus the cone stays acyclic and E tensor M -> K tensor M is a quasi-isomorphism for every M. Apply this construction to the finitely many columns and to the total complex. After localization their finite projective terms are finite free; perform Gaussian elimination over Frac(B), then invert its finitely many pivot denominators. The remaining zero differential blocks show that Hodge dimensions and total de Rham dimensions are constant and agree with every remaining closed fibre. This treats d as a base-linear map, not an $O_{X}$-linear map. Such a closed fibre lifts globally to $W_{2}(\kappa )$: lift the transcendence coordinates arbitrarily to Witt vectors and solve the primitive-element equation by its invertible derivative modulo $p^{2}$. Pull back the spread log-smooth scheme. Local relative Frobenius lifts exist on its log-etale toric charts: send monoids by multiplication by p, coefficients by Witt Frobenius, and solve the etale equations by the same invertible-Jacobian lifting. No global Frobenius lift is asserted. Here are the local Cartier and descent calculations used for this $W_{2}$ lift. On a saturated log-smooth chart Q -> P, the special log fibre has its essential-monomial decomposition. In a relative logarithmic weight e, the de Rham differential is wedge multiplication by the class of e in $(P^{gp}/Q^{gp})$ tensor kappa. If that class is nonzero modulo p, choose a linear functional taking it to 1; its interior contraction contracts that Koszul summand. The zero-character summands are the p-th-power summands, by saturatedness: on an essential face the induced lattice in the quotient is primitive, and a character divisible by p lifts to a p-divisible essential exponent. Equivalently, the saturated base-change chart P $\mathop{\mathrm{plus}}_{Q,p}$ Q identifies these summands exactly with the relative Frobenius twist. This is a chartwise lattice assertion, not an assertion that the absolute underlying ring is smooth. Ordinary smooth factors contribute the familiar surviving monomials $x^{p-1}$ dx. Consequently the intrinsic inverse Cartier formulas a |-> $a^{p}$, da |-> $a^{p-1}$ da, dlog(m) |-> dlog(m) and their exterior products are isomorphisms onto de Rham cohomology. The formulas commute with etale maps and chart changes. For a local Frobenius lift $F_{i}$, define $\phi _{i}^{1}(da)=(dF_{i}(\widetilde  a))$/p modulo p, and $\phi _{i}^{1}(dlog m)$=$(dlog F_{i}(m))/p$. Division is in the $W_{2}$ lift before reduction. On an overlap put $h_{ij}(da)=(F_{j}(\widetilde  a)-F_{i}(\widetilde  a))$/p; on a log generator use the first-order ratio of the two lifts. These are derivations relative to Frobenius, satisfy $\phi _{j}-\phi _{i}=d$ $h_{ij}$ and $h_{ij}+h_{jk}=h_{ik}$, and hence give a degree-one map into the total Cech de Rham complex. Antisymmetrized tensor powers divided by j! give degree-j maps for $j<p$. Their cohomology is the exterior-power inverse Cartier map, so their direct sum is a quasi-isomorphism $\bigoplus_{0\le j<p}$ $\Omega ^{j}_{X'/S}[-j]$ -> $\tau_{<p}$ $F_*$ $\Omega ^{\bullet }_{X/S}$. Since $p>d$ this is the full complex. This is the local computation and Cech gluing of FFR §§10–11 specialized to the everywhere log-smooth case; no codimension-two extension or reflexive $j_*$ argument is needed. Finite relative Frobenius and proper coherent cohomology now give, for every n, dim $H^{n}_{dR}(X_{\kappa }/S_{\kappa })$ = $\sum _{p+q=n}$ dim $H^{q}(X_{\kappa },\Omega ^{p}_{\log })$. The Frobenius twist has the same Hodge dimensions over the perfect residue field. The finite universal complexes above transfer the equality back to k. A spectral sequence of finite-dimensional vector spaces can only decrease total dimensions on each diagonal by the ranks of its incoming and outgoing differentials; equality between E1 and the abutment on every diagonal forces every differential to vanish. This proves central E1 for arbitrary characteristic-zero k, proper X0, and arbitrary sharp Q. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, given, construct, algebra]

1.3 **Compact closed-set patching.** Here are the topological sheaf arguments required for F7. For a sheaf $\mathcal E$ and a subspace $K$, write $\mathcal E(K)=\Gamma(K,\mathcal E|_K)$. On a compact Hausdorff space $T$, every finite open cover has a finite closed shrinking: choose, around each point, an open neighborhood whose closure lies in one cover member by regularity, take a finite subcover, and combine the closures assigned to each member. Sections of any abelian sheaf restricted to a finite closed cover glue if they agree on overlaps. Indeed, at a point discard the closed members not containing it; represent the remaining section germs on open neighborhoods and shrink until the finitely many germs agree. These local representatives give the desired section, uniquely because its germ at every point is prescribed. Also, a section on a compact closed subset $K$ of a locally compact Hausdorff space extends to some open neighborhood of $K$. Choose finitely many local representatives on opens $U_i$ and smaller opens $W_i$ whose compact closures lie in $U_i$ and cover $K$. The disagreement set of each pair, intersected with $\overline W_i\cap\overline W_j$, is compact and disjoint from $K$. Remove their finite union from $\bigcup_i W_i$; the representatives now agree and glue. Equality of two such neighborhood extensions is likewise witnessed on a neighborhood of $K$, by their equal germs. [F7, construct]

1.4 **C. Exact analytic log-u kernel contraction.** Temporarily take the standard log base N -> $A_{m}=C[t]/(t^{m+1})$. In the absolute complex form DA[u], where u is a polynomial variable of degree zero, with $e(\alpha  u^{s})$ = $d_{abs}(\alpha )$ $u^{s}$ + s rho wedge alpha $u^{s-1}$, rho=dlog t. The composite evaluation $u=0$ followed by quotient modulo rho is a chain map to $DR_{A}$, hence to $DR_{k}$. Evaluation to DA alone is NOT a chain map. Let R be the kernel of DA[u] -> $DR_{k}$. These maps are termwise surjective and give a short exact sequence of complexes. It suffices to prove hypercohomology(R)=0. Work first in a toric local model, at its distinguished point; ordinary smooth coordinates may be contracted by the radial holomorphic Poincare homotopy with parameters. Write P for its sharp monoid, rho for the image of 1 in $P^{gp}$, $E_{m}=P$ minus (P+(m+1)rho), $E=P$ minus (P+rho), and $L=P^{gp}$ tensor C. A stalk section of absolute degree k is a finite polynomial in u whose coefficients are convergent monomial sums $z^{e}$ $\ell _{e,s}$, $\ell _{e,s}$ in $\Lambda ^{k}$ L, e in $E_{m}$. In the analytic monoid algebra convergence is characterized by an exponential bound $||\ell _{e,s}||$ $\le$ C exp(c h(e)) for a positive integral height h. A finite set of smooth factors obeys the corresponding usual convergent-power-series bound. The relative central quotient retains e in E and maps L to L/C rho. Thus the kernel condition is $\ell _{e,0}$ belongs to rho wedge $\Lambda ^{k-1}L$, for every e in E. The actual stalk differential is (e ell)_(e,s) = e wedge $\ell _{e,s}$ + (s+1) rho wedge $\ell _{e,s+1}$. To justify this analytically, first use the formula on finite monomial sums; finite truncations converge on a smaller analytic polydisc. Multiplication of coefficients by the linear weight e preserves convergence there. First-order logarithmic derivations and their finite-order composites are continuous under precisely these smaller-radius estimates. Hence the formula extends uniquely to all stalk germs. Nilpotent exponential gauges have finite order and extend by the same argument; one does not tensor an O-linear differential which does not exist. Let ell be a closed kernel section of degree k+1, with u-degree N. For e nonzero choose a linear functional $\lambda _{e}$ with $\lambda _{e}(e)=1$ and norm $\le 1/||e||$; interior contraction $i_{\lambda_e}$ satisfies $(e \wedge )i_{\lambda_e}$ + $i_{\lambda_e}(e \wedge )$ = 1. Set $\tau _{e,N+1}=0$ and descend in s from N to zero by $r_{e,s}=\ell _{e,s}-(s+1)\rho$ wedge $\tau _{e,s+1}$, $\tau _{e,s}=i_{\lambda_e}$ $r_{e,s}$. Closedness and the already constructed next coefficient give e wedge $r_{e,s}=0$, so this has e wedge $\tau _{e,s}=r_{e,s}$. For $s=0$ and e in E choose $\lambda _{e}$ also to annihilate rho; then $i_{\lambda }$ preserves the rho-wedge ideal and $\tau _{e,0}$ satisfies the required kernel condition. Such functionals exist uniformly, with saturatedness used essentially. Write the saturated sharp monoid P as the intersection of the nonnegative half-spaces of its primitive integral facet forms $v_{F}$. For a saturated map N -> P, every $v_{F}(\rho )$ is 0 or 1. Here is the direct reason: localize at F, so that modulo its unit lattice $P_{F}$ becomes N, and the base generator has height $a=v_{F}(\rho )$. If $a>1$, base change N -> N, 1 -> a, gives modulo the same units a monoid generated by v,t with relation a $v=a$ t. In its groupification the nonzero torsion element t-v has a multiple equal to a unit but is not itself in the monoid: its total generator degree is zero, whereas every nonunit represented by nonnegative generators has positive degree. Thus this base-change monoid is not saturated, contrary to saturatedness of the original map. Now e is essential exactly when e-rho is not in P. The facet inequalities imply that some F has $v_{F}(e)=0$ and $v_{F}(\rho )=1$. Consequently e lies in the corresponding facet, whose real span excludes rho. Choose that facet for e. There are finitely many facets; their minimum angle with R rho is positive. Consequently one can take $||\lambda _{e}||\le 1/(\gamma ||e||)$ with fixed $\gamma >0$. For e outside E at $s=0$ no kernel constraint is needed and the unrestricted functional suffices. For $e=0$ set $\tau _{0,0}=0$ and $\tau _{0,s+1}$ = (1/(s+1)) $i_{\lambda_\rho}$ $\ell _{0,s}$, $\lambda _{\rho }(\rho )=1$. Closedness makes $\ell _{0,s}$ rho-divisible for $s>0$, and the kernel condition does so for $s=0$. Thus (s+1)rho wedge $\tau _{0,s+1}=\ell _{0,s}$. The factor 1/(s+1) is required. The resulting primitive is polynomial in u of degree at most N+1. The recursion gives a uniform bound $||\tau _{e,s}||$ $\le$ $C_{N}$ $\operatorname{max}_{s\le j\le N}||\ell _{e,j}||$ because nonzero lattice vectors have a positive minimum norm, gamma is uniform, and there are at most N+1 recursive steps. This retains the exponential monomial bound and proves convergence of the primitive. Degree-zero closed kernel sections are zero by the same descending recursion, followed by the zero-weight kernel condition. The complex R is therefore acyclic on analytic stalks. Strict etale charts analytify to local analytic isomorphisms, so this calculation proves local acyclicity on every local log-smooth model; the log chart is centered at the chosen point. It does not require a globally toric or globally smooth underlying central fibre. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 **Global sections and filtered colimits in degree zero.** Let $\mathcal E=\operatorname{colim}_i\mathcal E_i$ be a small filtered colimit of abelian sheaves on compact Hausdorff $T$. The sheaf colimit is the sheafification of the presheaf colimit; its stalks are the filtered colimits of the stalks. If $s\in\mathcal E_i(T)$ maps to zero, it becomes zero at later stages on an open neighborhood of each point. A finite subcover and filteredness give one arrow out of $i$ on which all these restrictions vanish, hence $s$ vanishes globally. This proves injectivity, on every compact Hausdorff space. For surjectivity choose finitely many opens $U_a$ on which a given section of $\mathcal E$ has representatives in one common stage. Choose a closed shrinking $K_a\subset U_a$ covering $T$. Restriction to a subspace commutes with sheaf colimits: its stalks are unchanged, so the natural comparison is an isomorphism on stalks. Apply the injectivity just proved on each compact $K_a\cap K_b$ to make the representatives agree there at one common later stage, then glue by step 1.3. Thus $\operatorname{colim}_i\Gamma(T,\mathcal E_i)=\Gamma(T,\mathcal E)$ naturally. The same holds for sections of restrictions to any closed $K\subset T$. [F7, step 1.3, construct, algebra]

2.2 **Softness and its local lifting property.** A sheaf $\mathcal S$ on compact Hausdorff $T$ is soft if $\mathcal S(T)\to\Gamma(K,\mathcal S|_K)$ is onto for every closed $K\subset T$. A flasque sheaf is soft: extend a section near $K$ by step 1.3 and then use its surjective open-set restriction map. If $0\to\mathcal S\to\mathcal G\to\mathcal H\to0$ is exact with $\mathcal S$ soft, then $\mathcal G(K)\to\mathcal H(K)$ is onto for every closed $K$. To see this, take local lifts of a section of $\mathcal H|_K$ and a finite closed shrinking $K_a$ of their domains covering $K$. Inductively suppose a lift has been glued on $L=K_1\cup\cdots\cup K_{a-1}$. The next lift minus the existing lift on $L\cap K_a$ is a section of $\mathcal S$ there, since restriction is exact on stalks. Extend this difference to $T$ by softness, subtract it from that next lift, and glue on the two closed sets. This proves the induction, including $K=T$. If $\mathcal G$ is soft as well, the quotient $\mathcal H$ is soft: first lift over $K$, then extend its $\mathcal G$-lift to $T$. [F7, step 1.3, construct, algebra]

3.1 **Soft acyclicity, with a functorial resolution.** Under AC every sheaf embeds in an injective sheaf, and injectives are flasque. Embed a soft $\mathcal S$ into such an $\mathcal I$. Step 2.2 makes $\mathcal I/\mathcal S$ soft and makes $\Gamma(T,\mathcal I)\to\Gamma(T,\mathcal I/\mathcal S)$ onto. The long exact cohomology sequence gives $H^1(T,\mathcal S)=0$ and $H^{q+1}(T,\mathcal S)=H^q(T,\mathcal I/\mathcal S)$ for $q\ge1$, since positive cohomology of an injective vanishes by its one-term injective resolution. Induction on $q$, applied to all soft sheaves, proves their acyclicity. Use the functorial Godement resolution $C^\bullet(\mathcal E)$: $C^0(\mathcal E)(U)=\prod_{x\in U}\mathcal E_x$, and iterate this construction on cokernels of the injective germ map. Its restrictions are surjective by extending a family by zero, so every term is flasque and hence soft. The germ map is injective, making the resolution exact. An exact resolution by acyclic sheaves computes derived global sections: split it into its kernel short exact sequences and use the same long exact sequence and degree-zero kernel identity, inductively in each cohomology degree.
