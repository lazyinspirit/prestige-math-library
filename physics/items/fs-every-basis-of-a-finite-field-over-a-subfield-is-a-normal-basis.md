## Statement

**False claim.** For every extension $E/\mathbb F_q$ of finite fields, every
$\mathbb F_q$-basis of $E$ is a normal basis ([[def-normal-basis]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The ring $L:=\mathbb F_2[t]/(t^{2}+t+1)$ with $\alpha$ the class of $t$, so that $\alpha^{2}=\alpha+1$ because $\alpha^{2}+\alpha+1=0$ and $-1=1$ in characteristic two.

[L1] A polynomial of degree $2$ or $3$ over a field is irreducible if and only if it has no root in that field ([[thm-quadratic-and-cubic-irreducibility-test]]); and $F[x]/(p)$ is a field exactly when $p$ is irreducible ([[thm-polynomial-quotient-is-a-field-iff-irreducible]]).

[L2] A monic irreducible vanishing at $a$ is the minimal polynomial of $a$ ([[thm-evaluation-kernel-and-minimal-polynomial]]), and then $F(a)$ has power basis $1,a,\dots,a^{n-1}$ with $[F(a):F]=n$ ([[thm-simple-algebraic-extension-quotient-power-basis-and-degree]], [[def-extension-degree-and-finite-extension]], [[def-linear-basis]]).

[L3] An extension of finite fields of degree $n$ over $\mathbb F_q$ is Galois with cyclic Galois group generated by $\sigma_q(x)=x^{q}$, of order $n$ ([[thm-extensions-of-finite-fields-are-galois-with-cyclic-frobenius-group]], [[def-relative-frobenius-of-a-finite-field-extension]], [[lem-order-of-the-relative-frobenius]]).

[L4] A normal basis of $K/F$ is an ordered $F$-basis of the form $(\sigma_1\gamma,\dots,\sigma_n\gamma)$ for a single $\gamma\in K$, indexed by $\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)$ ([[def-normal-basis]]).

[L5] Every finite Galois extension has a normal basis ([[thm-normal-basis-theorem]]).

## Refutation

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 $t^{2}+t+1$ has no root in $\mathbb F_2$, its values at $0$ and $1$ both being $1$, so it is irreducible and $L$ is a field by [L1]; it is the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$, so $[L:\mathbb F_2]=2$ with ordered basis $(1,\alpha)$ by [L2], and $L$ has four elements $0,1,\alpha,\alpha+1$. [L1, L2, given]

2.1 By [L3] the extension $L/\mathbb F_2$ is Galois with $\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb F_2)=\{\mathrm{id},\sigma\}$, where $\sigma(x)=x^{2}$. [step 1.1, L3]

3.1 The conjugate lists of the four elements are $(0,0)$, $(1,1)$, $(\alpha,\alpha+1)$ and $(\alpha+1,\alpha)$, using $\alpha^{2}=\alpha+1$ and $(\alpha+1)^{2}=\alpha^{2}+1=\alpha$. Their underlying sets are $\{0\}$, $\{1\}$ and $\{\alpha,\alpha+1\}$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, given]

4.1 The list $(1,\alpha)$ is an $\mathbb F_2$-basis of $L$ by step 1.1, but its underlying set $\{1,\alpha\}$ is none of the three sets in step 3.1, so it is not the conjugate list of any element and hence is not a normal basis by [L4]. The false claim therefore fails already for $L/\mathbb F_2$. [step 1.1, step 3.1, L4]

5.1 What is true is the existential statement: some element of $L$ generates a normal basis, and $\alpha$ does, since $(\alpha,\alpha+1)$ is a list of two distinct elements whose only vanishing $\mathbb F_2$-combinations are trivial, as $\alpha\ne0$, $\alpha+1\ne0$ and $\alpha+(\alpha+1)=1\ne0$. That is the content of [L5], which asserts existence and never universality. [step 3.1, step 4.1, L4, L5] ∎

## Remarks

- **Where the false claim comes from.** The normal basis theorem is an existence statement, and its proofs single out an element by a nonvanishing condition — a determinant in the infinite case, a cyclic vector in the finite case. Both conditions genuinely exclude some elements, as [[ex-a-normal-basis-of-f-eight-over-f-two]] shows over $\mathbb F_8$.
