## Statement

Let $x\ne y\in S_n$ and let $r:=n-\dim V^{x^{-1}y}$ be the codimension in
either graph of the intersection $Gr(x)\cap Gr(y)$; here $x^{-1}y$ acts on
$V=k^n$ by place permutation, so $r=1$ exactly when $x^{-1}y$ is a reflection.

1. If $r\ge2$, then every graded short exact sequence of graded $R$-bimodules
   $0\to R_y(b)\to E\to R_x(a)\to0$ splits.
2. If $y=xt$ for a reflection $t$, then $r=1$ and every such extension splits
   after inverting the equation of the hyperplane fixed by the reflection
   $xtx^{-1}=yx^{-1}$, i.e. after inverting $x(\alpha_t)$ (a unit multiple of
   $\alpha_{yx^{-1}}$); restricted to the case $x=e$ this is localizing in the
   hyperplane equation $\alpha_t$ itself.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Distinct $x,y\in S_n$, the graph bimodules $R_x,R_y$, the graded bimodules $R_x(a)=R_x$ shifted by $a$ and $R_y(b)$, and, for the reduction, the generators $\ell_i:=x_i\otimes1-1\otimes x_{x^{-1}i}$ of the graph ideal $I(Gr(x))$.

[F1] $R_w$ is $R$ as a graded $k$-space with left action $f\cdot g=fg$ and right action $g\cdot h=w(h)g$, and its support is $Gr(w)=\{(w\lambda,\lambda)\}$; also $\operatorname{Hom}_{R\text{-}R}(R_v(a),R_w(b))\cong R(b-a)$ for $v=w$ and $0$ for $v\ne w$ ([[def-type-a-standard-graph-bimodules-support-filtrations-and-character]]).

[F2] For $s=s_i$ one has $R=R^{s_i}\oplus\alpha_iR^{s_i}$, the Demazure operator $\partial_i(f)=(f-s_i(f))/\alpha_i$ satisfies $\partial_i(\alpha_i h)=2h$ for $h\in R^{s_i}$, and the realization is faithful with all $2m_{st}$ invertible in $k=\mathbb Q$ ([[def-type-a-reflection-realization-and-polynomial-ring]]).

## Proof

1.1 Set $I:=I(Gr(x))=\operatorname{Ann}(R_x)$, generated by $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_n$, and fix a homogeneous lift $e\in E$ of the generator $1_{R_x(a)}$, so that the sub identifies with $R_y(b)$ through a chosen generator $\iota$ of degree $-b$. Since every $h\in I$ kills the image of $e$ in $R_x(a)$, one has $h\cdot e\in R_y(b)$, so $h\cdot e=c(h)\iota$ for a unique homogeneous element $c(h)\in R$, and $E$ is generated over $R$ by $e$ and $\iota$ with the single family of relations $h\cdot e=c(h)\iota$. [F1]

2.1 The assignment $h\mapsto c(h)$ is additive and satisfies $c(hh')=(\text{action of }h\text{ on }R_y)\cdot c(h')$ because $(hh')\cdot e=h\cdot(c(h')\iota)=h\cdot(c(h')\iota)$ in the sub-bimodule $R_y(b)$; writing $u_i$ for the image of $\ell_i$ under $R\otimes R\to R_y$, $h\mapsto h\cdot1_{R_y}$, one has $u_i\,c(\ell_j)=u_j\,c(\ell_i)$ for all $i,j$, since $\ell_i\ell_j=\ell_j\ell_i$ and $c(\ell_i\ell_j)=u_i\,c(\ell_j)$. Writing $m_i:=c(\ell_i)$, the extension is thus encoded by elements $m_1,\ldots,m_n\in R$ with $u_im_j=u_jm_i$ for all $i,j$, and nothing else. [step 1.1, F1]

2.2 The forms $\ell_i$ lie in the linear span of the variables of $R\otimes R$, and their images are $u_i=x_i-x_{yx^{-1}i}$: acting on $1_{R_y}$ one has $x_i\cdot1=x_i$ and $(1\otimes x_{x^{-1}i})\cdot1=x_{yx^{-1}i}$. Hence the ideal generated by the $u_i$ is the ideal of the subspace $V^{yx^{-1}}$ of $V$, the rank of the family $(u_i)$ is $r=n-\dim V^{yx^{-1}}=n-\dim V^{x^{-1}y}$, and $Gr(x)\cap Gr(y)=\{(x\lambda,\lambda):x^{-1}y\lambda=\lambda\}$, of dimension $\dim V^{x^{-1}y}$ in either graph. [step 1.1, F1, F2]

3.1 Splitting: the extension splits iff there is $m\in R$ with $m_i=u_im$ for all $i$. Indeed a splitting of the sequence is exactly a homogeneous $R$-bimodule section of $E\to R_x(a)$, which has the form $1_{R_x(a)}\mapsto e'$ with $e'\in E$ lifting the generator; writing $e'=e-m\iota$ for some $m\in R$, the section is well defined iff $I\cdot e'=0$, i.e. iff $\ell_i\cdot(e-m\iota)=0$ for all $i$, that is $m_i=u_im$ for all $i$. Conversely such an $m$ makes $h\mapsto h\cdot e'$ a well-defined splitting. [step 2.1, F1]

3.2 Reduction of the $r\ge2$ case to two coprime forms: view $\sigma:=yx^{-1}$ as a permutation of the coordinate set, so $u_i=x_i-x_{\sigma i}$. If some cycle of $\sigma$ has length $\ge3$, two consecutive forms of that cycle, $x_i-x_{\sigma i}$ and $x_{\sigma i}-x_{\sigma^2i}$, are distinct irreducible elements of the polynomial ring and hence coprime in the UFD $R$; if all cycles have length $\le2$ and $r\ge2$, there are at least two transposition cycles and one form from each is a pair with disjoint supports, again coprime. In both cases pick such indices $p,q$. [step 2.2]

4.1 Conclusion in the case $r\ge2$: from $u_pm_q=u_qm_p$ and $\gcd(u_p,u_q)=1$ in the UFD $R$ we get $u_p\mid m_p$ and $u_q\mid m_q$; write $m_p=u_pm$ and $m_q=u_qm'$; substituting gives $u_pu_qm'=u_qu_pm$, hence $m'=m$ as $R$ is a domain. For every index $i$, $u_pm_i=u_im_p=u_iu_pm$ forces $m_i=u_im$, since $u_p\ne0$ and $R$ is a domain. By step 3.1 the extension splits. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2]

4.2 The case $r=1$: then $\sigma=yx^{-1}$ is a reflection, so it exchanges two coordinates $p\ne q$ and fixes the rest; hence $u_p=x_p-x_q$, $u_q=-u_p$ and $u_i=0$ for all other $i$. The relations $u_im_j=u_jm_i$ give $m_i=0$ for $i\notin\{p,q\}$ and $m_q=-m_p$, and by step 3.1 the extension splits iff $u_p\mid m_p$. In the localization at $u_p$ the obstruction dies because $u_p$ becomes a unit; moreover $u_p$ is a unit multiple of $\alpha_\sigma$, the equation of the hyperplane of fixed points of $\sigma=yx^{-1}$, and for $y=xt$ that reflection is $xtx^{-1}$ with equation $x(\alpha_t)$. [step 3.1, step 2.2, F2]

5.1 Both assertions follow: for $r\ge2$ every extension splits by step 4.1, and for $r=1$ with $y=xt$ every extension splits in the stated localization by step 4.2. All constructions were made on homogeneous lifts, so the argument applies degree by degree in the graded category. ∎ [step 4.1, step 4.2]
