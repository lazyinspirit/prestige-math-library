## Postulate

Adopt as physical assumptions of the non-relativistic classical mechanics framework the following statements about spacetime.

**(i) Spacetime data.** Spacetime is the mathematical structure
$(V,\tau,g,A,\text{fixed orientation of }K)$ of [[def-cm-affine-time-space]]: a
four-dimensional real vector space $V$; a surjective linear map
$\tau:V\to\mathbb R$, the **absolute-time covector**; the kernel
$K=\ker\tau$, a three-dimensional real vector space, the **spatial displacement
space**; a **spatial metric** $g$, a symmetric positive-definite bilinear form
$g:K\times K\to\mathbb R$; a fixed **orientation** of $K$, that is, a chosen one
of the two classes of ordered bases of $K$ under the equivalence generated by
positive-determinant changes of basis; and an affine space $A$ modelled on $V$
(a nonempty set with a free and transitive $V$-action, with displacements as in
[[def-cm-affine-time-space]]). The elements of $A$ are the **events** of the
model. The structure is the arena of the model; no equation of motion, force
law or matter content is asserted by this clause.

**(ii) Interpretation of the data.** For a displacement $w\in V$, the number
$\tau(w)$ is the **elapsed time** carried by $w$, read in seconds; for a
displacement $k\in K$ inside a simultaneous slice, the number $g(k,k)$ is the
**squared spatial length** of $k$, read in square metres; the square root
$\sqrt{g(k,k)}$ is the length of $k$. The dimensions and units are those fixed
by [[def-cm01-units]], so that $[\tau(w)]=\tau$ and $[g(k,k)]=\ell^{2}$. The
orientation of $K$ is the convention relative to which spatial bases are called
positively oriented and against which orientation-preserving rotations are
defined.

**(iii) Absolute time, simultaneity and distance.** Two events $p,q\in A$ are
**simultaneous** when their displacement lies in $K$, that is, when
$\tau(q-p)=0$. Simultaneity is an equivalence relation on $A$: it is reflexive
because $\tau(0)=0$, symmetric because $\tau(p-q)=-\tau(q-p)$ and transitive
because $\tau$ is additive. Its classes are the **simultaneous slices**
$A_{t}=\{p\in A:\tau(p-o)=t\}$ of any adapted chart; the class does not depend
on the choice of adapted chart, and the elapsed time between two events is the
chart-independent number $\tau(q-p)$. Two events of a common slice carry the
**slice distance** $d_{g}(p,q):=\sqrt{g(p-q,p-q)}$, which is defined because
$p-q\in K$; distances are not defined across distinct slices by this structure.

**(iv) Smooth structure.** $A$ is read with the affine smooth structure
determined by its affine coordinates: an adapted chart of
[[def-cm-affine-time-space]] is a bijection $A\to\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^{3}$
whose inverse is the map $(t,x)\mapsto o+tu+\sum_{i}x_{i}e_{i}$, and curves are
declared $C^{k}$ when their coordinate functions in one (equivalently, every)
adapted chart are $C^{k}$. The equivalence of the two readings is supplied by
the affine transition law between adapted charts proved in
[[lem-cm-adapted-charts-and-galilean-transformations]]; this postulate adopts
the resulting smooth structure as the one in which regularity of worldlines and
of force maps is stated.

**(v) What the structure excludes.** The data contain no inner product on $V$,
no Lorentzian metric, no light cone, no universal speed bound, no null cones
and no causal classification of displacements other than the split into
$\tau$-values; they contain no distinguished origin, no distinguished vector
$u$ with $\tau(u)=1$ and no distinguished basis of $K$. Consequently the
structure by itself distinguishes no inertial frame: the class of frames and
the transformations between them are fixed separately, by the frame postulate
[[post-cm01-frames]]. No statement about the speed of any signal, about
electromagnetic propagation or about gravitation is made here.

**(vi) Status.** These clauses are adopted assumptions of the model, not
theorems and not empirical findings. They are not derived from the uniqueness
theory of differential equations, from any symmetry principle or from
experiment; the Newtonian tradition and Galilean relativity motivate them. The
model makes no claim that the physical world is exactly a four-dimensional
affine space with these data, and no numerical measurement is recorded.

## Status and scope

- **Adopted, not deduced.** Clauses (i)-(iv) fix the arena and the reading of
  its data; clause (v) records what is deliberately absent. Nothing here
  selects a force law, an equation of motion, a matter model or a solution of
  any equation; those enter through the postulates and definitions of the
  pages that follow.

- **Domain of intended use.** The model is intended for the non-relativistic
  regime, in which speeds are small compared with the speed of light and a
  classical continuum description of matter and apparatus is adequate. The
  framework sets no numerical cutoff for that regime; an application must
  state the regime in which the model, its force law and its apparatus
  description are adequate. Relativistic, quantum and quantum-gravitational
  regimes are outside the scope of this model.

- **Empirical content is downstream.** Empirical significance lies in the
  comparison of predictions computed from a fully specified model with
  reported observations; those comparisons carry apparatus, calibration,
  sampling and uncertainty qualifications and are recorded where the framework
  treats them. This postulate itself makes no quantitative empirical claim,
  and agreement of a model with data would be evidence about the model, not a
  proof of this postulate.

- **Absolute time, not absolute space.** Absolute simultaneity and Euclidean
  slice geometry are part of the adopted model. The model does not contain a
  distinguished rest frame: two inertial frames are related by the
  transformations of [[post-cm01-frames]], and the structure alone cannot
  select one of them. This is a property of the adopted data, not a
  conclusion of relativistic kinematics, and no Lorentzian or
  light-cone structure is imported.
