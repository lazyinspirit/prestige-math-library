## Definition

The finite-field generator and wall-crossing equality defined earlier on
this A page do not themselves define a Calabi–Yau-threefold
Donaldson–Thomas invariant, a cohomological Hall invariant, or a physical
BPS index. Define a **CY3/CoHA protected-state bridge package** to be the
complete data below for one specified model:

1. A specified 3-Calabi–Yau category $\mathcal C$ over a specified field $k$:
   a $k$-linear triangulated
   or dg category with finite-dimensional Hom spaces in the stated subcategory
   and functorial perfect dualities
   $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal C}(E,F[i])\cong
   \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal C}(F,E[3-i])^*$ for all stated objects and
   degrees. Alternatively, for the quiver-with-potential branch using the
   potential and rational CoHA suppliers below, specify a finite quiver
   $Q=(Q_0,Q_1,s,t)$, with finite vertex and arrow sets and source and target
   maps, and its path algebra over $\mathbb C$. Give a potential $W$, a finite $\mathbb C$-linear
   combination of directed cycles modulo cyclic rotation. Give the exact
   Jacobian, derived, or critical-locus category and coefficient data
   constructed from $(Q,W)$. A different base field or coefficient theory
   requires suppliers and integration results with that same specified
   coefficient data.

2. A stability package on a specified abelian category or heart $\mathcal A$
   of the category in (1); all subobjects, exact sequences, and filtrations
   below are taken in $\mathcal A$. Here $K_0(\mathcal A)$ is generated by
   symbols $[E]$ for isomorphism classes of objects, with relation
   $[E]=[F]+[G]$ for each short exact sequence
   $0\to F\to E\to G\to0$. Its numerical charge lattice means a specified
   finite-rank free abelian group $\Gamma$ and a class homomorphism
   $\operatorname{cl}:K_0(\mathcal A)\to\Gamma$. If $\Gamma$ is asserted to
   be a canonical numerical quotient, specify the pairing and the quotient
   relation that define it. Give a homomorphism $Z:\Gamma\to\mathbb C$. For
   the phase convention used here, require
   $Z(\operatorname{cl}(E))\in\mathbb H:=\{z\in\mathbb C:\operatorname{Im}z>0\}$
   for every nonzero $E\in\mathcal A$, and set
   $\phi_Z(E)=\arg Z(\operatorname{cl}(E))/\pi\in(0,1)$ using the principal
   argument. Call $E\ne0$ semistable if every nonzero subobject
   $F\subseteq E$ has $\phi_Z(F)\le\phi_Z(E)$. The required
   Harder–Narasimhan property is that every nonzero $E$ has a finite
   filtration by subobjects
   $$0=E_0\subsetneq E_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq E_n=E$$
   whose nonzero factors $E_j/E_{j-1}$ are semistable and satisfy
   $\phi_Z(E_1/E_0)>\cdots>\phi_Z(E_n/E_{n-1})$; this filtration is unique
   as a filtration by subobjects of $E$. State any different phase or
   semistability convention exactly and verify that it supplies the precise
   filtration property used. The support property used here is: for a
   specified norm on $\Gamma_{\mathbb R}:=\Gamma\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb R$,
   some constant $C>0$ satisfies
   $|Z_{\mathbb R}(\jmath(\gamma))|\ge C\|\jmath(\gamma)\|$ for every nonzero
   class $\gamma=\operatorname{cl}(E)$ represented by a semistable object,
   where $\jmath:\Gamma\to\Gamma_{\mathbb R}$ is $\gamma\mapsto\gamma\otimes1$
   and $Z_{\mathbb R}:\Gamma_{\mathbb R}\to\mathbb C$ is the unique real-linear
   map satisfying $Z_{\mathbb R}(\jmath(\gamma))=Z(\gamma)$.
   Any alternative support-property convention must be stated exactly.

3. The orientation data required by the chosen motivic or cohomological
   integration theory, when that theory requires it. This means a specified
   square root of its determinant line, together with the compatibility maps
   under the exact-sequence operations and the conventions required by that
   theory; the name alone is not the data.

4. A specified completed motivic or cohomological Hall algebra, its
   coefficient theory and completion, and a proved continuous integration
   homomorphism to a named completed quantum torus. The motivic stack-function
   construction uses convolution along the exact-sequence correspondence:
   the first factor is the quotient and the second is the subrepresentation.
   On the quiver-with-potential branch of (1), an untwisted rational
   equivariant Borel–Moore CoHA choice uses the finite quiver over $\mathbb C$
   specified there. Its graded groups are
   $\mathcal H_{\mathbf d}=
   H_{*+2\dim G_{\mathbf d}}^{\mathrm{BM},G_{\mathbf d}}
   (\operatorname{Rep}_Q(\mathbf d);\mathbb Q)$ and the product
   $\alpha*\beta=p_*q^!(\alpha\boxtimes\beta)$, where the first factor is the
   subrepresentation, the second is the quotient, and the homological shift
   is $-2\chi_Q(\mathbf b,\mathbf a)$ when the subrepresentation has
   dimension $\mathbf a$ and the quotient has dimension $\mathbf b$.
   State the exact coefficient map, Euler or skew form, sign, square-root and
   orientation conventions, and every hypothesis of the integration theorem.
   This displayed formula is the untwisted theory and has no $W$-dependent
   vanishing-cycle coefficients. If the quiver-with-potential branch uses
   $W$-dependent coefficients, select the critical CoHA whose groups and
   product use the specified vanishing-cycle coefficient datum and coherent
   exact-sequence operations. Its standard rational datum agrees with the
   untwisted theory when $W=0$; no identification for nonzero $W$, or
   integration map from the critical CoHA to a quantum torus, is implicit.
   That map must be supplied and proved separately as required above. These
   constructions remain distinct from finite-field groupoid integration.

5. For a physical interpretation, a named four-dimensional supersymmetric
   model and its protected sector: specify the state space or supertrace being
   counted, the charges and preserved supersymmetry, and a proved comparison
   identifying that protected index with the selected algebraic invariant.
   This comparison is an additional dictionary; it is not supplied by the
   finite-field factorization.

This definition is a list of required inputs, not an assertion that any bridge
package exists or that arbitrary Hall modules are BPS Hilbert spaces. The
displayed support property is the convention used here; any cited source using
different hypotheses must be translated and checked before use.
The rational equivariant Borel–Moore construction and standard quiver-potential
coefficient data referenced below assume the Axiom of Choice; this contract
records that supplier assumption and does not use it to establish a
finite-field Hall result.
