## Statement

Adopt the chiral Ising Virasoro data $c=\tfrac12$ and a spin highest-weight module with $h=\tfrac1{16}$, with four identical primary insertions and an actual level-two null vector. In the cross-ratio
$
x=\frac{z_{12}z_{34}}{z_{13}z_{24}},\qquad z_{ij}=z_i-z_j,
$
the global-conformal prefactor can be removed so that the reduced block $f(x)$ satisfies
$
x(1-x)f''(x)+\left(\tfrac12-x\right)f'(x)+\tfrac1{16}f(x)=0.
$
Two normalized chiral solutions are
$
\mathcal F_\pm(x)=\frac{[x(1-x)]^{-1/8}}{\sqrt2}
\left(1\pm\sqrt{1-x}\right)^{1/2}.
$
The diagonal pairing $|\mathcal F_+(x)|^2+|\mathcal F_-(x)|^2$ is single-valued under analytic continuation around $0$ and $1$. This is a mathematical pairing of chiral solutions; it does not construct a full local CFT.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Virasoro commutator convention of [[def-virasoro-algebra]], highest-weight-vector convention of [[def-virasoro-highest-weight-module]], simple-quotient theorem [[lem-cft-virasoro-verma-module-has-unique-simple-quotient]], and Ising values $c=\tfrac12,h=\tfrac1{16}$.

[A1] Adopt the chiral Ising module as $L(\tfrac12,\tfrac1{16})=M(\tfrac12,\tfrac1{16})/J$ from [[lem-cft-virasoro-verma-module-has-unique-simple-quotient]]. The vector $v$ used in steps 1.1–1.2 is the Verma highest vector before quotienting.

[F1] [[def-virasoro-algebra]]: $[L_m,L_n]=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\frac{c}{12}(m^3-m)\delta_{m+n,0}I$.

[F2] [[def-virasoro-highest-weight-module]]: a highest-weight vector satisfies $L_n v=0$ for $n>0$, $L_0v=hv$, and $Cv=cv$; its generated module is the span of finite words in Virasoro generators. Irreducibility and quotienting are additional choices.

[F3] [[lem-cft-virasoro-verma-module-has-unique-simple-quotient]]: PBW orders modes as negative modes, then $L_0,C$, then positive modes; the induced Verma module has this negative-mode basis, top $M_h=\mathbb Cv$, and unique simple quotient $M(c,h)/J$, where $J$ is the sum of all proper submodules.

[F4] [[thm-cft-bpz-equation-from-level-two-null-vector]]: an actual relation $(L_{-2}-\beta L_{-1}^2)v=0$ implies the corresponding sphere BPZ equation.

[F5] [[def-cft-fixed-coordinate-sphere-virasoro-block-family]] supplies translation covariance, primary stress-tensor singular parts, and global sphere Ward identities for the chiral blocks considered here.

## Proof

1.1 Let $v$ be the highest vector in the Verma module $M(\tfrac12,\tfrac1{16})$ specified in [A1], and set $N=(L_{-2}-\tfrac43L_{-1}^2)v$. From [F1],
$
L_1L_{-2}v=3L_{-1}v,\qquad
L_1L_{-1}^2v=2(2h+1)L_{-1}v=\tfrac94L_{-1}v,
$
so $L_1N=0$. Also
$
L_2L_{-2}v=(4h+c/2)v=\tfrac12v,\qquad
L_2L_{-1}^2v=6hv=\tfrac38v,
$
so $L_2N=0$. [A1, F1, F2, algebra]

1.2 For $m\ge2$, $[L_1,L_m]=(1-m)L_{m+1}$, so induction from $L_2N=0$ in step 1.1 gives $L_mN=0$ for every $m>0$. Inside the Verma module $M(\tfrac12,\tfrac1{16})$, the submodule generated by $N$ has weights at least $h+2$: by [F3], order modes with positive ones to the right, where they kill $N$, while $L_0N=(h+2)N$ and negative modes raise the level. This submodule is proper because it has no weight-$h$ component, whereas $M_h=\mathbb Cv$. It is therefore included in $J$, the sum of proper submodules from [F3]. The quotient in [A1] kills $J$, so $N=0$ there. This is the actual null relation with $\beta=4/3$. [A1, F1, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

1.3 Apply [F4] to the four insertions. Each has $h=1/16$, so the equation at insertion $i$ is
$
\frac43\partial_{z_i}^2G
-\sum_{j\ne i}\left(\frac{G}{16(z_i-z_j)^2}
+\frac{\partial_{z_j}G}{z_i-z_j}\right)=0.
$
The three global Ward identities in [F5] imply that
$
G(\mathbf z)=
\left(\frac{z_{13}z_{24}}{z_{12}z_{23}z_{34}z_{41}}\right)^{1/8}\phi(x),
\qquad x=\frac{z_{12}z_{34}}{z_{13}z_{24}},
$
on a simply connected branch domain: the displayed prefactor has weight $-2h=-1/8$ in each coordinate, and after dividing by it the three Ward operators annihilate the quotient, which is therefore a function of the single cross-ratio. In the Möbius frame $(z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4)=(0,x,1,R)$, $R^{2h}G$ has a finite limit proportional to $[x(1-x)]^{-1/8}\phi(x)$. Write $d_j=\partial_{z_j}G$ before taking the limit. Solving the translation, dilation, and special-conformal identities gives
$
d_4=\frac{(x-x^2)d_2-2h(x+R-1)G}{R(R-1)},\quad
d_3=-x d_2-Rd_4-4hG,\quad
d_1=(x-1)d_2+(R-1)d_4+4hG.
$
With $h=1/16$, the normalized limit $g(x)=\lim_{R\to\infty}R^{2h}G(0,x,1,R)$ therefore obeys
$
\frac43g''-\frac{2x-1}{x(1-x)}g'
-\frac1{16}\left(\frac1{x^2}+\frac1{(1-x)^2}
+\frac2x+\frac2{1-x}\right)g=0.
$
The derivatives in this limit are at fixed $z_1,z_3,z_4$; the displayed formulas eliminate the other three derivatives in the BPZ equation. [F4, F5, step 1.2, algebra]

2.1 Put $\chi=[x(1-x)]^{-1/8}$, $p=(2x-1)/(x(1-x))$, and $g=\chi f$. Then $\chi'/\chi=p/8$, and the bracket multiplying $g$ in step 1.3 simplifies to $1/[x^2(1-x)^2]$. Substitution gives
$
f''-\frac p2f'+\frac{1}{16x(1-x)}f=0,
$
which is the stated reduced ODE after multiplication by $x(1-x)$. The prefactor $\chi$ restores the primary four-point covariance, so the two chiral solutions have the displayed form after the stated normalization by $1/\sqrt2$. [F5, step 1.3, algebra]

2.2 For $s\in\{+1,-1\}$, let $u=\sqrt{1-x}$ and $f_s=(1+su)^{1/2}$ on a simply connected branch domain. Direct differentiation gives
$
f_s'=-\frac{s}{4uf_s},\qquad
f_s''=-\frac{s}{8u^3f_s}-\frac{1}{16u^2f_s^3}.
$
Substituting these expressions into the reduced ODE from step 2.1 and using $u^2=1-x$, $f_s^2=1+su$, and $1-u^2=(1-su)(1+su)$ gives zero. Near $x=0$, $f_+\sim\sqrt2$ and $f_-\sim\sqrt{x/2}$, so the normalized full blocks have exponents $-1/8$ and $3/8$, respectively, matching the identity and energy channels. [step 2.1, algebra]

2.3 A positive loop about $x=0$ leaves $u$ and $f_+$ unchanged and changes $f_-$ to $-f_-$; the common factor $\chi$ acquires $e^{-\pi i/4}$. A positive loop about $x=1$ changes $u$ to $-u$, so it exchanges $f_+$ and $f_-$, while $\chi$ again acquires only the common phase $e^{-\pi i/4}$. Both continuation matrices are therefore a unit-modulus scalar times a diagonal sign or a permutation. Hence $|\mathcal F_+|^2+|\mathcal F_-|^2$ is invariant under these generators and is single-valued on $\mathbb C\setminus\{0,1\}$. The chiral blocks themselves remain multivalued. [step 2.2, algebra] ∎
