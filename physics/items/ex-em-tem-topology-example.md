## Example

Assume the lossless isotropic scalar constant branch of [[post-em-material-closure]], the macroscopic equations of [[pthm-em-macroscopic-maxwell]] with $\rho_f=0$, $J_f=0$, and the straight-guide, TEM and separated-phasor conventions of [[def-em-guide-cavity-geometry]]; put $Z:=\sqrt{\mu/\varepsilon}$ and $v:=(\varepsilon\mu)^{-1/2}$.
(a) [Potential form] Let $A\subseteq\mathbb R^2$ be a bounded connected $C^1$ domain and let $\varphi\in C^2(\overline A;\mathbb C)$ (with real-valued potentials included) satisfy $\Delta\varphi=0$ on $A$ (the plane Laplacian of [[def-laplacian-of-a-c2-function]]; a $C^2$ function with $\Delta\varphi=0$ is called harmonic). For either sign $\zeta\in\{+1,-1\}$ define $\tilde E:=-\nabla\varphi$, $\beta:=\zeta\omega/v$ and $\tilde H:=\zeta e_z\times\tilde E/Z$; then the separated real fields $E=\operatorname{Re}(\tilde Ee^{i(\beta x_3-\omega t)})$, $B=\mu\operatorname{Re}(\tilde He^{i(\beta x_3-\omega t)})$ satisfy the source-free lossless macroscopic equations with $E_z=B_z=0$ (TEM), and the PEC condition on a smooth wall with unit normal $n$ is equivalent to $\partial_\tau\varphi=0$ along that boundary curve, so that $\varphi$ is constant on each connected component of $\partial A$. Moreover, if $A$ is star-shaped ([[def-star-shaped-open-subset-of-rn]]), every separated TEM phasor with $C^1$ transverse amplitudes has on the interior a complex harmonic potential $\varphi\in C^2(A;\mathbb C)$, obtained by applying [[cor-closed-exact-and-conservative-equivalence-on-star-shaped-domains]] to the real and imaginary electric amplitudes separately. If the field is nonzero, the equations force $\beta^2=\varepsilon\mu\omega^2$, hence $\beta=\pm\omega/v$, and $\tilde H=\beta e_z\times\tilde E/(\omega\mu)$, giving the two displayed signs. If the field is zero, $\tilde E=\tilde H=0$ solves the equations for every longitudinal label $\beta$, with no dispersion conclusion. The boundary potential description requires the stated continuous differentiable boundary trace (in particular the initial $C^2(\overline A)$ potential has it); the interior exactness supplier alone is not asserted to produce a real potential or automatic boundary extension.
(b) [Single-conductor obstruction] If in the potential-form setting $\partial A$ is connected, then $\varphi$ is constant on $\partial A$, hence $\varphi$ is constant on $\overline A$ by the two-dimensional classical Dirichlet uniqueness [[cor-uniqueness-for-the-classical-dirichlet-problem]], applied to its real and imaginary parts with their respective constants as second solutions, and therefore $\tilde E=\tilde H=0$: a single-conductor cross-section with connected boundary admits no nonzero TEM field in the potential form. The same potential-form obstruction applies to a rectangular cross-section with a potential in $C^2(A)\cap C(\overline A)$ having constant boundary trace, because this uniqueness supplier does not require a $C^1$ boundary. For rectangular separated TEM amplitudes that are $C^1$ on the interior and continuous on the closure with the classical PEC traces on open edges, the star-centre radial primitive extends continuously to the rectangle closure. The edgewise tangential derivative is the electric trace, so PEC makes the potential constant on each open edge and continuity joins those constants at the corners. Thus this stated rectangular class has no nonzero TEM field.
(c) [Coaxial example] For the following Lebesgue polar-coordinate energy calculation assume Countable Choice $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ ([[def-countable-choice]]). Let $0<a<b$, $A:=\{x\in\mathbb R^2:a<|x|<b\}$ (two boundary components), fix $V\in\mathbb R$, choose the forward label $k:=\omega/v$, and define $\varphi(x):=V\ln(b/|x|)/\ln(b/a)$, so that $\tilde E=V e_r/(|x|\ln(b/a))$ with $e_r:=x/|x|$ and $\tilde H=(V/(Z|x|\ln(b/a)))e_\varphi$ with $e_\varphi:=e_z\times e_r$. Then $\Delta\varphi=0$ on $A$: indeed $\partial_i\ln|x|=x_i/|x|^2$ by [[thm-logarithm-derivative-and-integral]] and the chain rule ([[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]]), and the second partial derivatives sum to zero; hence $\varphi$ is constant on each boundary circle (values $V$ at $|x|=a$ and $0$ at $|x|=b$), the fields satisfy the source-free lossless macroscopic equations and the PEC conditions, and the relation $k=\omega/v$ is nondispersive with no cutoff. The amplitude integrals computed in polar coordinates with [[thm-polar-coordinates-formula-for-lebesgue-measure]] (the Jacobian factor $r$ is read from that formula) are $$W_e:=\tfrac12\varepsilon\int_A|\tilde E|^2\,dA=\tfrac12CV^2,\qquad W_m:=\tfrac12\mu\int_A|\tilde H|^2\,dA=\tfrac12CV^2,\qquad C:=\frac{2\pi\varepsilon}{\ln(b/a)};$$ the instantaneous per-length energies are $\tfrac12CV^2\cos^2\theta$ and $\tfrac12LI^2\cos^2\theta$ with $\theta=kx_3-\omega t$; equivalently, with the per-length inductance $L:=\mu\ln(b/a)/(2\pi)$ in $\mathrm H\,\mathrm m^{-1}$, per-length capacitance $C$ in $\mathrm F\,\mathrm m^{-1}$, and wall-circulation amplitude $I:=2\pi V/(Z\ln(b/a))$ in $\mathrm A$ (identified with the inner-conductor sheet-current amplitude when its conductor-side magnetic intensity trace is zero) one has $W_m=\tfrac12LI^2$ and $LC=\varepsilon\mu$. For $V\ne0$ the two boundary components carry distinct constants, allowing a nonzero field; $V=0$ gives equal constants and the zero field.
(d) [Scope] The obstruction clause (b) is proved for the continuous-boundary harmonic-potential form. The star-shaped interior classification in (a) covers both signs and the zero field, with boundary conclusions under the stated potential-trace hypotheses; the general reduction of an arbitrary TEM field to the potential form on a merely simply connected non-star-shaped cross-section is not asserted here. The coaxial clause is an explicit conditional computation in the declared model with no empirical content; no lossy, radiation or signal-speed claim is made.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The lossless isotropic scalar constant branch of [[post-em-material-closure]] with constants $\varepsilon,\mu>0$ and relations $D=\varepsilon E$, $B=\mu H$; the macroscopic equations of [[pthm-em-macroscopic-maxwell]] with $\rho_f=0$, $J_f=0$; the straight-guide, TEM and separated-phasor conventions of [[def-em-guide-cavity-geometry]]; $Z:=\sqrt{\mu/\varepsilon}$ and $v:=(\varepsilon\mu)^{-1/2}$; a bounded connected $C^1$ domain $A\subseteq\mathbb R^2$ and, in the star-shaped clause, a star-shaped such $A$; a potential $\varphi\in C^2(\overline A;\mathbb C)$ with $\Delta\varphi=0$ on $A$ (the plane Laplacian of [[def-laplacian-of-a-c2-function]], a $C^2$ function with $\Delta\varphi=0$ being harmonic); for the coaxial clause $0<a<b$, $A=\{x\in\mathbb R^2:a<|x|<b\}$, real $V$, and Countable Choice $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ ([[def-countable-choice]]).

[F1] The declared real fields satisfy the source-free equations of [F1] of the theorem page exactly when their complex amplitudes satisfy $\widehat{\operatorname{div}}(\tilde E)=0$, $\widehat{\operatorname{div}}(\tilde H)=0$, $\widehat{\operatorname{curl}}(\tilde E)=i\omega\mu\tilde H$, $\widehat{\operatorname{curl}}(\tilde H)=-i\omega\varepsilon\tilde E$, where $\widehat{\operatorname{div}}(w)=\partial_1w_1+\partial_2w_2+i\beta w_3$ and $\widehat{\operatorname{curl}}(w)=(\partial_2w_3-i\beta w_2,\ i\beta w_1-\partial_1w_3,\ \partial_1w_2-\partial_2w_1)$ ([[lem-em-separated-phasor-calculus]], [[pthm-em-macroscopic-maxwell]] clause (b), [[post-em-material-closure]] clause (b)); a nonzero separated real field has nonzero amplitude, and a separated real field vanishes identically exactly when its amplitude does ([[lem-em-separated-phasor-calculus]] clause (d)).

[F2] Transverse vector identities: for a transverse vector $v$, $e_z\times(e_z\times v)=-v$, $e_z\times v$ is transverse and $|e_z\times v|=|v|$; and $\operatorname{div}(e_z\times\nabla_th)=0$ for $C^2$ $h$ ([[lem-em-transverse-vector-identities]] (a), (c)). Cross-product and scalar-triple-product manipulations are those of [[def-cross-product-in-r3]] and [[lem-cross-product-is-bilinear-alternating-and-orthogonal]]: $a\cdot(b\times c)=\det[a\ b\ c]$ and $a\times b\perp a,b$.

[F3] Plane harmonic components are smooth, indeed real analytic, in the interior ([[thm-plane-harmonic-functions-are-smooth-and-real-analytic]]); a $C^2$ function on an open subset of $\mathbb R^2$ has equal mixed partials ([[thm-clairaut-schwarz-mixed-partials]]).

[F4] Classical Dirichlet uniqueness: if $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$, $n\ge2$, is bounded, nonempty and open and $u,v\in C^2(\Omega)\cap C(\overline\Omega)$ satisfy $\Delta u=\Delta v$ in $\Omega$ and $u=v$ on $\partial\Omega$, then $u=v$ on $\overline\Omega$; the statement requires no regularity of the boundary ([[cor-uniqueness-for-the-classical-dirichlet-problem]]).

[F5] On an open star-shaped $U\subseteq\mathbb R^n$, a $C^1$ field is closed exactly when it is exact, and exactness uses a $C^2$ potential ([[cor-closed-exact-and-conservative-equivalence-on-star-shaped-domains]], [[def-star-shaped-open-subset-of-rn]]).

[F6] One-variable and measure tools: for a differentiable function on an interval a vanishing derivative makes it constant, and $\int_a^bG'=G(b)-G(a)$ for $G$ with integrable derivative ([[thm-ftc-second-part]]); $\log'(x)=1/x$ and $\log x=\int_1^xdt/t$ for $x>0$ ([[thm-logarithm-derivative-and-integral]]); composites obey the chain rule ([[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]]); the polar-coordinate formula integrates continuous functions on the annulus with Jacobian factor $r$ ([[thm-polar-coordinates-formula-for-lebesgue-measure]], [[cor-c-one-change-of-variables-for-l-one-functions]]), the omitted radial segment being Lebesgue null ([[prop-degenerate-boxes-and-coordinate-hyperplanes-are-lebesgue-null]]); positive square roots exist and are unique ([[thm-of-square-roots]]).

[F7] In the lossless branch the stored-energy density and flux are those of [[pthm-em-restricted-material-energy]]; only its local positive reading is used.

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct: verify the potential-form amplitudes against the four amplitude equations and the PEC traces, classify an arbitrary $C^1$ TEM amplitude by the transverse curl components, apply two-dimensional Dirichlet uniqueness to the constant-boundary potentials, and compute the coaxial integrals in polar coordinates.

1.1 [Potential form and the PEC boundary equivalence] Let $\varphi\in C^2(\overline A;\mathbb C)$ with $\Delta\varphi=0$, fix $\zeta\in\{+1,-1\}$, and put $\tilde E:=-\nabla\varphi$, $\beta:=\zeta\omega/v$, $\tilde H:=\zeta e_z\times\tilde E/Z$, with real fields $E=\operatorname{Re}(\tilde Ee^{i(\beta x_3-\omega t)})$, $B=\mu\operatorname{Re}(\tilde He^{i(\beta x_3-\omega t)})$. Then $\tilde E_3=0$ and $\tilde H_3=0$ because $e_z\times\tilde E$ and $\nabla\varphi$ are transverse [F2], so $E_z=B_z=0$ (TEM); and the four amplitude equations of [F1] hold: $\widehat{\operatorname{div}}(\tilde E)=-\Delta\varphi=0$; $\widehat{\operatorname{div}}(\tilde H)=\frac{\zeta}{Z}\bigl(\partial_1(-\tilde E_2)+\partial_2(\tilde E_1)\bigr)=-\frac{\zeta}{Z}\operatorname{curl}_2\tilde E=0$ because $\tilde E=-\nabla\varphi$ has equal mixed partials [F3]; $\widehat{\operatorname{curl}}(\tilde E)=(i\beta\partial_2\varphi,\ -i\beta\partial_1\varphi,\ \operatorname{curl}_2\tilde E)=i\beta e_z\times\tilde E=i\omega\mu\tilde H$, using $\tilde H=\frac{\beta}{\omega\mu}e_z\times\tilde E$, which holds because $\frac{\beta}{\omega\mu}=\frac{\zeta\omega/v}{\omega\mu}=\frac{\zeta}{v\mu}=\frac{\zeta}{Z}$ since $v\mu=\sqrt{\mu/\varepsilon}=Z$; and $\operatorname{curl}_2\tilde H=(\zeta/Z)\operatorname{div}_t\tilde E=0$, hence $\widehat{\operatorname{curl}}(\tilde H)=i\beta e_z\times\tilde H=i\beta\,\frac{\zeta}{Z}e_z\times(e_z\times\tilde E)=-\frac{i\zeta\beta}{Z}\tilde E=-i\omega\varepsilon\tilde E$ by [F2] and $\frac{\zeta\beta}{Z}=\frac{\omega/v}{Z}=\frac{\omega}{vZ}=\omega\varepsilon$ because $vZ=1/\varepsilon$. Hence the real fields satisfy the source-free lossless macroscopic equations by [F1]. If the potential has a differentiable boundary trace on a smooth wall of $A$ with outward unit normal $n$ (an in-plane unit vector) and tangent $\tau:=e_z\times n$, then $n\times\tilde E=(0,0,n_1\tilde E_2-n_2\tilde E_1)$ and, since $\tilde E=-\nabla\varphi$, $n_1\tilde E_2-n_2\tilde E_1=n_2\partial_1\varphi-n_1\partial_2\varphi=-\nabla\varphi\cdot\tau=-\partial_\tau\varphi$, so $n\times\tilde E=-e_z\partial_\tau\varphi$ (only the vanishing of this normal-cross field is used below); likewise $n\cdot\tilde H=\frac{\zeta}{Z}n\cdot(e_z\times\tilde E)=\frac{\zeta}{Z}\tilde E\cdot(n\times e_z)=-\frac{\zeta}{Z}\tilde E\cdot\tau=\frac{\zeta}{Z}\partial_\tau\varphi$ by the scalar triple product and $n\times e_z=-\tau$ [F2]. Since a separated real field vanishes identically exactly when its amplitude does [F1], the PEC conditions $n\times E=0$ and $n\cdot B=0$ hold exactly when $\partial_\tau\varphi=0$ along the wall. In a $C^1$ boundary parametrization the derivative of the potential trace is the parametrization speed times $\partial_\tau\varphi$ (up to orientation); the chain rule and FTC [F6] therefore make it locally constant when PEC is imposed. A locally constant function on a connected boundary component is constant there, since any one of its value fibres and its complement are both relatively open. [F1, F2, F3, F6, algebra]

1.2 [Converse classification and the star-shaped potential] Let $(\tilde E,\tilde H)$ be $C^1$ transverse amplitudes of a separated TEM field satisfying the four amplitude equations of [F1]. The third components of the two curl equations read $\operatorname{curl}_2\tilde E=i\omega\mu\tilde H_3=0$ and $\operatorname{curl}_2\tilde H=-i\omega\varepsilon\tilde E_3=0$, while the two divergence equations read $\operatorname{div}_t\tilde E=0$ and $\operatorname{div}_t\tilde H=0$ (the $i\beta$-terms vanish with the third components). The transverse part of Faraday reads $(0,0,0)$ in its third component and $(-i\beta\tilde E_2,\ i\beta\tilde E_1,0)=i\beta\,e_z\times\tilde E$ in its transverse components, so $i\beta\,e_z\times\tilde E=i\omega\mu\tilde H$ and, since $\omega\mu>0$, $\tilde H=\frac{\beta}{\omega\mu}e_z\times\tilde E$ with no division by $\beta$. Substituting into the transverse part of Ampere and using $e_z\times(e_z\times\tilde E)=-\tilde E$ [F2] gives $-i\frac{\beta^2}{\omega\mu}\tilde E=-i\omega\varepsilon\tilde E$, that is $(\beta^2-\varepsilon\mu\omega^2)\tilde E=0$. If $\tilde E\ne0$ then $\beta^2=\varepsilon\mu\omega^2$, so $\beta=\pm\omega/v$ by [F6]; if $\tilde E=0$ then Faraday gives $\tilde H=0$, and the zero field solves the equations for every longitudinal label $\beta$ with no dispersion conclusion. Finally, if $A$ is star-shaped and $\tilde E$ is a $C^1$ transverse closed field ($\operatorname{curl}_2\tilde E=0$), then its real and imaginary parts are closed $C^1$ fields on the star-shaped domain, so [F5] supplies $C^2$ potentials for each, hence a complex $\varphi\in C^2(A;\mathbb C)$ with $\tilde E=-\nabla\varphi$ after a sign change, and $\operatorname{div}_t\tilde E=0$ makes $\Delta\varphi=0$; no real-only or boundary-extension conclusion is inferred from interior exactness. [F1, F2, F5, F6, algebra]

2.1 [Single-conductor obstruction] Suppose $\varphi\in C^2(\overline A;\mathbb C)$ is harmonic on the bounded connected domain $A$ and has constant boundary trace on each connected component of $\partial A$, with the PEC potential description of step 1.1 available. If $\partial A$ is connected, the trace is a single constant $c$, and applying [F4] to the real and imaginary parts of $\varphi$ and of the constant function $c$ gives $\varphi=c$ on $\overline A$; then $\tilde E=-\nabla\varphi=0$ and $\tilde H\propto e_z\times\tilde E=0$. For a rectangular cross-section with a potential in $C^2(A)\cap C(\overline A)$ having constant boundary trace the same argument applies, because [F4] requires only a bounded nonempty open set and no boundary regularity. For rectangular separated TEM amplitudes that are $C^1$ on the interior and continuous on the closure with the classical PEC traces on the open edges, fix a star centre $x_*$ and use the radial primitive $\psi(x)=\int_0^1\tilde E(x_*+t(x-x_*))\cdot(x-x_*)\,dt$ furnished by [F5]. It is $C^2$ on the interior with $\nabla\psi=\tilde E$; the displayed formula extends continuously to the closure by uniform continuity of the electric amplitude there. For two points on one open edge, apply the interior FTC line-integral identity on parallel interior segments and pass to the edge using this continuity. Thus the boundary potential increment is the integral of the continuous tangential electric trace, whose difference quotients give the edge derivative; PEC makes that derivative zero; by the one-variable constancy of [F6] the potential is constant on each open edge, and continuity at the corners joins those constants, so the trace is constant on $\partial A$ and [F4] gives $\psi$ constant, hence $\tilde E=0$ and $\tilde H=0$. Thus a single-conductor cross-section with connected boundary admits no nonzero TEM field in the stated potential form, and the same holds for the stated rectangular class. [step 1.1, F1, F2, F4, F5, F6]

2.2 [Coaxial annulus: potential and fields] In the annulus $A=\{a<|x|<b\}$ put $\varphi(x):=V\ln(b/|x|)/\ln(b/a)$ and choose the forward label $k:=\omega/v$ (so $\zeta=+1$). Since $\partial_i\ln|x|=x_i/|x|^2$ by [F6] and the chain rule, $\partial_i\partial_i\ln|x|=1/|x|^2-2x_i^2/|x|^4$ and $\Delta\ln|x|=2/|x|^2-2/|x|^2=0$, so $\Delta\varphi=0$ on $A$; also $\varphi=V$ on $|x|=a$ and $\varphi=0$ on $|x|=b$, so the trace is constant on each boundary circle. The constructed fields of step 1.1 are then $\tilde E=-\nabla\varphi=V e_r/(|x|\ln(b/a))$ with $e_r=x/|x|$, and $\tilde H=e_z\times\tilde E/Z=\bigl(V/(Z|x|\ln(b/a))\bigr)e_\varphi$ with $e_\varphi:=e_z\times e_r$; by step 1.1 they satisfy the source-free lossless macroscopic equations and the PEC conditions, and the longitudinal label satisfies $k=\omega/v$, nondispersive with no cutoff, so $k$ is real and nonzero for every $\omega>0$. [step 1.1, F1, F2, F6, algebra]

3.1 [Coaxial energies in polar coordinates] Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$. With $W_e:=\frac12\varepsilon\int_A|\tilde E|^2dA$ and $W_m:=\frac12\mu\int_A|\tilde H|^2dA$, the polar formula of [F6] (Jacobian $r$; the omitted radial segment is null) and $|\tilde E|^2=Z^2|\tilde H|^2=V^2/(|x|^2\ln^2(b/a))$ give $\int_A|\tilde E|^2dA=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_a^b\frac{V^2}{r^2\ln^2(b/a)}r\,dr\,d\theta=\frac{2\pi V^2}{\ln(b/a)}$, using $\int_a^b dr/r=\ln(b/a)$ by [F6]. Hence $W_e=\frac12\varepsilon V^2\frac{2\pi}{\ln(b/a)}=\frac12CV^2$ with $C:=\frac{2\pi\varepsilon}{\ln(b/a)}$, and $W_m=\frac12\mu\frac{V^2}{Z^2}\frac{2\pi}{\ln(b/a)}=\frac12CV^2$ because $\mu/Z^2=\varepsilon$. For the sheet-current reading additionally assume zero conductor-side magnetic intensity trace. On the inner wall the filling normal is $n=-e_r$, so [[def-em-guide-cavity-geometry]](b) gives $\tilde K_f=n\times(0-\tilde H)=\tilde H_\varphi(a)e_z$. Without that additional trace assumption the following $I$ is only the filling-side wall circulation. Integrating that axial circulation around the circle gives $I=2\pi a\tilde H_\varphi(a)=2\pi V/(Z\ln(b/a))$. With $L:=\frac{\mu\ln(b/a)}{2\pi}$ one gets $\frac12LI^2=\frac12\frac{\mu\ln(b/a)}{2\pi}\frac{4\pi^2V^2}{Z^2\ln^2(b/a)}=\frac12\frac{2\pi\mu V^2}{Z^2\ln(b/a)}=\frac12CV^2=W_m$, and $LC=\frac{\mu\ln(b/a)}{2\pi}\frac{2\pi\varepsilon}{\ln(b/a)}=\varepsilon\mu$. The instantaneous per-length energies are $\frac12CV^2\cos^2\theta$ and $\frac12LI^2\cos^2\theta$ with $\theta=kx_3-\omega t$, by the definitions of $E$ and $B$ and the identity $\operatorname{Re}(we^{i\theta})^2+|{\operatorname{Im}}(we^{i\theta})|^2=|w|^2$; for $V=0$ all fields and energies vanish. For $V\ne0$ the two boundary components carry distinct constants $V$ and $0$, whereas $V=0$ gives the zero field with equal boundary constants; this respects the obstruction of step 2.1. [step 2.1, step 2.2, F1, F6, F7, algebra]

4.1 [Scope] Clause (a) is steps 1.1 and 1.2: the potential form gives TEM fields satisfying the equations and turns PEC on a smooth wall into the vanishing tangential potential derivative, the converse classification gives the two nonzero signs $\beta=\pm\omega/v$ and the unrestricted label for the zero field, and the star-shaped interior exactness is used only with the stated potential-trace hypotheses for boundary conclusions. Clause (b) is step 2.1, proved for the continuous-boundary harmonic-potential form and the stated rectangular class; the general reduction of an arbitrary TEM field to the potential form on a merely simply connected non-star-shaped cross-section is not asserted. Clause (c) is steps 2.2 and 3.1: the coaxial computation is conditional, in the declared lossless model, with the polar-measure branch the only place Countable Choice is used, and no lossy, radiation, empirical or signal-speed claim is made. [step 1.2, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1] ∎
