## Statement

Assume Countable Choice, $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ [[def-countable-choice]], as
required by the cited phase-space map and the Borel restriction of Lebesgue
measure.

Let $s>0$, dimensionless $\lambda\in\mathbb R$, parent energy
$\mathsf M_0>0$, and
$G_E\in\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$ in joules. For scattering, take the incoming $p_1,p_2$ and outgoing $k_1,k_2$
on the positive massless scalar shell, each $(E,\mathbf q)$ having
$E=|\mathbf q|>0$, with common total
$p_1+p_2=k_1+k_2=P$, $h(P,P)=s>0$, and evaluate the coefficient in a
proper orthochronous center-of-momentum chart
$\Lambda P=(\sqrt{s},\mathbf0)$ as in the phase-space supplier.
For decay, take massless positive-shell daughters summing to the rest parent
$P_0=(\mathsf M_0,\mathbf0)$. With these data the identical
final-state quartic total coefficient is
$$ \sigma_{\rm coeff}^{\rm identical} =\frac{\lambda^2(\hbar c)^2}{32\pi s}, $$
and the distinguishable-final coefficient is twice this value. The
identical-daughter decay-rate coefficient is
$$ \Gamma_{\rm coeff}=\frac{|G_E|^2}{32\pi\hbar\mathsf M_0} \quad[\mathrm{s}^{-1}]. $$
Set $\Gamma:=\Gamma_{\rm coeff}>0$. For the lifetime calculation only, let
$(\Xi,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)$ be a probability space in the sense of
[[def-probability-measure]]: $\mathcal F$ is a sigma-algebra on $\Xi$, and
$\mathbb P$ is a countably additive measure with $\mathbb P(\Xi)=1$. Let
$\mathcal B([0,\infty))$ be the sigma-algebra generated by sets
$U\cap[0,\infty)$ for open $U\subseteq\mathbb R$, as in
[[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]. Let
$T:\Xi\to[0,\infty)$ be a parent lifetime measured in seconds and measurable
in the explicit sense that $T^{-1}(B)\in\mathcal F$ for every
$B\in\mathcal B([0,\infty))$. Define its law by
$\mu_T(B):=\mathbb P(T^{-1}(B))$ on those Borel sets. Let $\ell$ denote
one-dimensional Lebesgue measure from
[[def-lebesgue-measure-and-the-lebesgue-sigma-algebra]], restricted to
$\mathcal B([0,\infty))$; this restriction is a measure by
[[thm-borel-sets-are-lebesgue-measurable]] under the stated Countable Choice
assumption. Separately assume the exponential lifetime model, defined by
$$\mu_T(B)=\int_B\Gamma e^{-\Gamma t}\,d\ell(t)\quad\text{for every }B\in\mathcal B([0,\infty)).$$
It gives $P_{\rm surv}(t):=\mathbb P(T^{-1}((t,\infty)))=e^{-\Gamma t}$ for
$t\geq0$. Define the
mean lifetime as $\mathbb E[T]:=\int_{[0,\infty)}t\,d\mu_T(t)$, and define
the half-life $t_{1/2}$ by $P_{\rm surv}(t_{1/2})=1/2$. Then the mean
lifetime is $1/\Gamma$ and the half-life is $\ln 2/\Gamma$. The exponential
law is not a consequence of the formal coefficient calculation.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice [[def-countable-choice]]; the positive-shell scattering and decay data in the Statement, and the scalar amplitude conventions and coefficient maps of [[cor-qft-example-scalar-tree-coefficients]], [[lem-qft-example-two-body-phase-space]], and [[post-qft-example-formal-observable-map]].

[F1] [[cor-qft-example-scalar-tree-coefficients]]: for identical equal-mass scalars the full-sphere quartic coefficient is $\lambda^2(\hbar c)^2/(32\pi s)$; distinguishable final particles give twice this value. The identical-daughter rate coefficient is $|G_E|^2\sqrt{1-4m_s^2/\mathsf M_0^2}/(32\pi\hbar\mathsf M_0)$.

[F2] [[def-qft-example-data]]: $[\hbar]=\mathrm{J\,s}$ and $[\hbar c]=\mathrm{J\,m}$; $s$ is in $\mathrm{J}^2$.

[F3] [[def-probability-measure]]: a probability space is a triple $(\Xi,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)$ where $\mathcal F$ is a sigma-algebra on $\Xi$ and $\mathbb P$ is a countably additive measure with $\mathbb P(\Xi)=1$.

[F4] [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]: the Borel sigma-algebra of a topological space is generated by its open sets.

[F5] [[def-lebesgue-measure-and-the-lebesgue-sigma-algebra]]: one-dimensional Lebesgue measure is the restriction of Lebesgue outer measure to the Lebesgue sigma-algebra.

[F6] [[thm-borel-sets-are-lebesgue-measurable]]: under Countable Choice, every Borel subset of $\mathbb R$ is Lebesgue measurable, so $\ell$ denotes its restriction to the stated Borel sigma-algebra.

[A1] [[post-qft-example-formal-observable-map]]: formal coefficients are adopted leading readings only; no survival law follows from the rate map.

[A2] For the lifetime calculation only, assume the exponential probability law for $T$ defined in the Statement, with $\Gamma=\Gamma_{\rm coeff}$. This assumption is not part of [A1] or the formal coefficient maps.

## Example

The massless special case exposes both the full-sphere identical-particle factor and the distinction between a rate coefficient and a decay law.

## Verification

1.1 Set the daughter rest energy to zero in [F1]. The square-root threshold factor becomes $1$, so the identical total coefficient is $\lambda^2(\hbar c)^2/(32\pi s)$ and the distinguishable total is twice that. The cross-section unit is $(\mathrm{J\,m})^2/\mathrm{J}^2=\mathrm{m}^2$. For the same massless daughter specialization, [F1] gives $$ \Gamma_{\rm coeff}=\frac{|G_E|^2}{32\pi\hbar\mathsf M_0}. $$ Its unit is $\mathrm J^2/(\mathrm{J\,s\,J})=\mathrm{s}^{-1}$, so it is a rate coefficient. Since $G_E\ne0$, $\hbar>0$, and $\mathsf M_0>0$, this coefficient is strictly positive. [given, F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 By [A2], $\mu_T(B)=\int_B\Gamma e^{-\Gamma t}\,d\ell(t)$ for Borel $B\subseteq[0,\infty)$. Taking $B=[0,\infty)$ gives total mass $1$, and the mean is $$\mathbb E[T]=\int_0^\infty t\Gamma e^{-\Gamma t}\,dt=\left[-t e^{-\Gamma t}\right]_0^\infty+\int_0^\infty e^{-\Gamma t}\,dt=\frac1\Gamma,$$ by integration by parts; $t e^{-\Gamma t}\to0$ since $\Gamma>0$. The survival function is continuous and strictly decreasing from $1$ to $0$, so the defining equation for $t_{1/2}$ has a unique solution; solving $e^{-\Gamma t_{1/2}}=1/2$ gives $t_{1/2}=\ln2/\Gamma$. These formulas use [A2], not the formal rate coefficient alone; no experiment or detector record is specified. [step 1.1, F3, F4, F5, F6, A1, A2, algebra] ∎
