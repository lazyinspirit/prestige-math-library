## Statement

Assume the elementary inertial-rest branch of [[post-em-material-closure]] in the lossless isotropic scalar case: two homogeneous media at rest in the laboratory chart of [[def-em-lab-geometry]] (frame basis $(e_1,e_2,e_3)$, units of [[def-em-si-conventions]]), medium 1 in $\{x_3<0\}$ with constants $\varepsilon_1,\mu_1>0$ and medium 2 in $\{x_3>0\}$ with constants $\varepsilon_2,\mu_2>0$, both with $\sigma=0$, sharing the plane interface $S:=\{x_3=0\}$ with unit normal $n:=e_3$, and the plane-wave amplitude conditions and solution structure of [[pthm-em-medium-plane-wave-fields]] in each medium. Let $\omega_i,\omega_r,\omega_t>0$, $k_i,k_r,k_t\in\mathbb C^{3}$, $e_i,e_r,e_t\in\mathbb C^{3}\setminus\{0\}$, and put $h_j:=k_j\times e_j/(\mu_j\omega_j)$ for $j\in\{i,r,t\}$ where $\mu_i=\mu_r=\mu_1$ and $\mu_t=\mu_2$, so that each triple satisfies the conditions of [[pthm-em-medium-plane-wave-fields]](b)-(c) in its own medium: $k_j\cdot e_j=0$, $k_j\times e_j=\omega_j\mu h_j$, $k_j\times h_j=-\omega_j\varepsilon e_j$ and $k_j\cdot k_j=\varepsilon\mu\omega_j^{2}$ with $(\varepsilon,\mu)=(\varepsilon_1,\mu_1)$ for $j=i,r$ and $(\varepsilon_2,\mu_2)$ for $j=t$. Assume the declared normal orientation conditions $k_i\cdot n\in\mathbb R_{>0}$ and $k_r\cdot n\in\mathbb R_{<0}$; no order is placed on a complex number. Tangential components may be complex. Algebraic phase matching permits every complex transmitted wavevector satisfying its amplitude conditions and dispersion relation, including a zero normal root at critical incidence. No outgoing or non-growth condition is required here. A consuming Fresnel construction may optionally select a root $k_t\cdot n$ of $(k_t\cdot n)^2=\varepsilon_2\mu_2\omega_t^2-\kappa_t\cdot\kappa_t$, where $\kappa_t:=k_t-(k_t\cdot n)n$, with $\operatorname{Im}(k_t\cdot n)\ge0$ and $\operatorname{Re}(k_t\cdot n)\ge0$ when its imaginary part is zero. Such a selection is a declared additional branch assumption of that construction, not a restriction on this theorem or an inferred material realization. Complex tangential phases may grow along the unbounded interface; no global tangential boundedness is asserted. Define the wave fields $E_j(x,t):=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(e_j e^{i(k_j\cdot x-\omega_jt)}\bigr)$, $H_j(x,t):=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(h_j e^{i(k_j\cdot x-\omega_jt)}\bigr)$, which are fields of [[def-em-fields]], and let $E:=E_i+E_r$, $H:=H_i+H_r$ on the medium-1 side and $E:=E_t$, $H:=H_t$ on the medium-2 side. [Interface hypothesis] assume that for all $(y,t)\in\mathbb R^{2}\times I$, with $I\subseteq\mathbb R$ a nonempty open interval, the one-sided traces at $(y,0,t)$ satisfy the four stationary interface identities with no free surface charge or current,
$$n\cdot\bigl(\varepsilon_2E_t-\varepsilon_1(E_i+E_r)\bigr)=0,\qquad n\cdot\bigl(\mu_2H_t-\mu_1(H_i+H_r)\bigr)=0,$$
$$n\times\bigl(E_t-(E_i+E_r)\bigr)=0,\qquad n\times\bigl(H_t-(H_i+H_r)\bigr)=0 .$$
Then:

**(a) [Phase matching]** with $\kappa_j:=k_j-(k_j\cdot n)n$ the tangential wavevector, $\kappa_i=\kappa_r=\kappa_t$ and $\omega_i=\omega_r=\omega_t$.

**(b) [Geometry]** if $\kappa_i\ne0$ then $k_i,k_r,k_t$ all lie in the complex two-dimensional subspace $\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{\kappa_i,n\}$ (the real wavevectors lie in the corresponding real plane); if $\kappa_i=0$ then all three wavevectors are parallel to $n$.

**(c) [Propagating waves: reflection law and Snell's law]** if in addition $k_i,k_r,k_t\in\mathbb R^{3}$, then with $\theta_j\in[0,\pi/2]$ defined by $\sin\theta_j=|\kappa_j|/|k_j|$ (well defined because the real dispersion relation gives $|k_j|>0$; the incident and reflected angles are below $\pi/2$, and the transmitted angle may equal $\pi/2$) one has $\theta_r=\theta_i$ and, writing $v_j=(\varepsilon_j\mu_j)^{-1/2}$ and $n_j=c/v_j$ for the parameters of [[def-em-medium-wave-parameters]],
$$n_1\sin\theta_i=n_2\sin\theta_t.$$
If $\theta_i>0$, this is equivalently $\sin\theta_t/\sin\theta_i=v_2/v_1=n_1/n_2$. At normal incidence $\theta_i=0$ it gives $\theta_t=0$ without forming $0/0$.

**(d) [Hypotheses actually used]** within the stated positive-frequency amplitude-condition setting, the only interface identities used are the two tangential identities $n\times[E]=0$ and $n\times[H]=0$ ; nonzero-wave hypotheses $e_j\ne0$ and $k_j\cdot k_j\ne0$ are retained. The two normal identities are recorded because they belong to the interface model, but they are not used. If the reflected wave is absent ($e_r=h_r=0$), the same argument matches the incident and transmitted actual waves; it does not determine the absent reflected wave's frequency or wavevector, which a Fresnel construction may conventionally assign the common phase.

**(e) [Scope]** the wave data are given: no existence of interface-consistent data, no reflection or transmission amplitudes, no energy fluxes and no well-posedness are claimed. Complex transmitted wavevectors are covered by (a) and (b) (the evanescent case of [[pthm-em-medium-plane-wave-fields]](e) satisfies $k_t\cdot n\ne0$); the physical propagating or evanescent character of the transmitted wave, the angle selection in the real case and the amplitudes are treated by the interface items that consume this theorem. The four interface identities are the stationary plane-interface conditions of the model; for compact interfaces they follow from the distributional jump theorem [[pthm-em-material-stationary-jumps]], and on the noncompact plane they are the stated interface hypothesis. No material dispersion, absorption, anisotropy, moving medium or nonlinearity is covered, and no empirical claim is made.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The two-media lossless setting of the Statement, the six wave data $(k_j,\omega_j,e_j)$, $j\in\{i,r,t\}$, with $h_j=k_j\times e_j/(\mu_j\omega_j)$, the normal $n=e_3$, the interface interval $I$, and the interface hypothesis [A1].

[A1] The four stationary interface identities of the Statement hold for all $(y,t)\in\mathbb R^2\times I$; only the two tangential identities $n\times(E_t-(E_i+E_r))=0$ and $n\times(H_t-(H_i+H_r))=0$ are used in the proof.

[F1] Plane-wave structure in each medium: $k_j\cdot e_j=0$, $k_j\times e_j=\omega_j\mu h_j$, $k_j\times h_j=-\omega_j\varepsilon e_j$ and $k_j\cdot k_j=\varepsilon\mu\omega_j^2$ with the constants of the medium of wave $j$, and $e_j\ne0$ ([[pthm-em-medium-plane-wave-fields]](b)-(c)); the parameters $v_j=(\varepsilon_j\mu_j)^{-1/2}$ and $n_j=c/v_j$ are those of [[def-em-medium-wave-parameters]].

[F2] The character-grouping lemma: if $F(z)=\sum_{k}c_k e^{i\lambda_k\cdot z}$ with pairwise distinct $\lambda_k\in\mathbb C^m$ and $\operatorname{Re}F=0$ on a nonempty open $U$, then for every $\nu\in\mathbb C^m$, $\sum_{\lambda_k=\nu}c_k+\sum_{\lambda_k=-\overline\nu}\overline{c_k}=0$ ([[lem-em-exponential-character-open-set-independence]](b)).

[F3] Inner and cross products: $\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_lx_ly_l$, $u\times v=(u_yv_z-u_zv_y,u_zv_x-u_xv_z,u_xv_y-u_yv_x)$, bilinear and alternating, with $\langle u\times v,w\rangle=\det[u\ v\ w]$ and $u\times v$ orthogonal to $u$ and $v$; orthogonality of the decomposition $x=x^\tau+(x\cdot n)n$ with $x^\tau:=x-(x\cdot n)n$ ([[def-euclidean-inner-product]], [[def-cross-product-in-r3]], [[lem-cross-product-is-bilinear-alternating-and-orthogonal]]).

[F4] Complex arithmetic and conjugation: $\mathbb C$ is a field in which a product vanishes only if a factor does ([[def-complex-numbers-and-arithmetic]], [[thm-complex-numbers-form-a-field]]); conjugation is an involutive automorphism with $\operatorname{Re}w=(w+\overline w)/2$ and $z\overline z=|z|^2$ ([[def-complex-conjugate-real-imaginary-part-and-modulus]], [[lem-complex-conjugation-and-modulus-laws]]).

[F5] The principal inverse sine: $\arcsin:[-1,1]\to[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ satisfies $\sin(\arcsin y)=y$, and sine is strictly increasing on $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ ([[def-principal-inverse-sine-and-cosine]]).

[F6] Nonnegative square roots: every nonnegative real has a unique nonnegative square root, and $\sqrt{ab}=\sqrt a\sqrt b$ for $a,b\ge0$ by squaring and uniqueness ([[thm-of-square-roots]]).

[F7] The laboratory setting, fields and units are those of [[def-em-lab-geometry]], [[def-em-fields]] and [[def-em-si-conventions]]; the material branch is the lossless isotropic scalar case of [[post-em-material-closure]].

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; evaluate the tangential traces on the interface as real parts of six-component characters, apply the character-grouping lemma to force equal frequencies, and read off the geometry and the real-angle laws.

1.1 [Nonzero tangential traces] For each $j$, the six-component tangential trace datum $A_j:=(e_j^\tau,h_j^\tau)\in\mathbb C^6$ is nonzero. Suppose $A_j=0$; then $e_j^\tau=0$ and $h_j^\tau=0$, so $e_j=a\,n$ with $a=e_j\cdot n$ and $h_j=b\,n$ with $b=h_j\cdot n$ by the orthogonal decomposition of [F3]. Taking the inner product of the amplitude condition $k_j\times e_j=\omega_j\mu h_j$ of [F1] with $n$ gives $0=\omega_j\mu\,b$: indeed $\langle k_j\times e_j,n\rangle=\det[k_j,e_j,n]=a\det[k_j,n,n]=0$ because a determinant with two equal columns vanishes and $\langle h_j,n\rangle=b$; hence $b=0$ and $h_j=0$. Then the amplitude condition $k_j\times h_j=-\omega_j\varepsilon e_j$ gives $0=-\omega_j\varepsilon\,a\,n$, so $a=0$, and then $e_j=a\,n=0$, contradicting $e_j\ne0$ in [F1]. [given, F1, F3, F4, algebra]

2.1 [Interface traces as characters] For a wave $j$ and $x=(y,0)$ on the interface, $k_j\cdot x=\kappa_j\cdot y$ because $k_j\cdot n$ multiplies the vanishing third coordinate; hence at the interface the tangential trace is $E_j^\tau(y,0,t)=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(e_j^\tau e^{i(\kappa_j\cdot y-\omega_jt)}\bigr)$ and $H_j(y,0,t)=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(h_j e^{i(\kappa_j\cdot y-\omega_jt)}\bigr)$, whose normal parts are annihilated by $n\times\cdot$. The two tangential interface identities of [A1] are identities of tangential vector fields, and for a tangential $w$ the vanishing $n\times w=0$ forces $w=0$ (in the frame $n=e_3$, $w=(w_1,w_2,0)$ gives $n\times w=(-w_2,w_1,0)$ by [F3]); hence they say, for all $z=(y,t)\in\mathbb R^2\times I$, that $\operatorname{Re}\bigl(A_t e^{i\lambda_t\cdot z}\bigr)=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(A_i e^{i\lambda_i\cdot z}\bigr)+\operatorname{Re}\bigl(A_r e^{i\lambda_r\cdot z}\bigr)$ componentwise, with $\lambda_j:=(\kappa_j,-\omega_j)\in\mathbb C^3$ and $A_j=(e_j^\tau,h_j^\tau)$; equivalently $\operatorname{Re}\bigl(A_i e^{i\lambda_i\cdot z}+A_r e^{i\lambda_r\cdot z}-A_t e^{i\lambda_t\cdot z}\bigr)=0$ on the nonempty open set $\mathbb R^2\times I$, by the additivity of the real part in [F4] and the componentwise linearity of the tangential projection in [F3]. [given, A1, step 1.1, F3, F4, algebra]

3.1 [Frequency grouping] Group the three frequencies of step 2.1 by value: let $\nu_1,\dots,\nu_M$ be the distinct values among $\lambda_i,\lambda_r,\lambda_t$ and put $c_{\nu_0}:=\sum_{j:\lambda_j=\nu_0}\epsilon_jA_j$ where $\epsilon_i=\epsilon_r:=1$ and $\epsilon_t:=-1$. Then $\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{M}c_{\nu_k}e^{i\nu_k\cdot z}\bigr)=0$ on $\mathbb R^2\times I$, the $\nu_k$ being pairwise distinct, so [F2] applied to the distinct frequencies $\nu_1,\dots,\nu_M$ with coefficients $c_{\nu_1},\dots,c_{\nu_M}$ gives $c_{\nu}+\sum_{k:\nu_k=-\overline\nu}\overline{c_{\nu_k}}=0$ for every $\nu\in\mathbb C^3$. Fix $k$ and take $\nu=\nu_k$: the left sum is $c_{\nu_k}$ alone, while $-\overline{\nu_k}=(-\overline{\kappa_k},+\omega_k)$ has last coordinate $\omega_k>0$, whereas every $\nu_l=\lambda_j$ for some $j$ has last coordinate $-\omega_j<0$; hence $\nu_l=-\overline{\nu_k}$ fails for every $l$, the second sum is empty, and $c_{\nu_k}=0$. [given, step 2.1, F2, F4, algebra]

4.1 [All frequencies agree] By step 3.1, for each distinct actual frequency $\nu$ the signed sum of the tangential data of the waves with that frequency vanishes. If the three actual frequencies were not all equal, then some actual frequency would occur exactly once among $\lambda_i,\lambda_r,\lambda_t$ (a partition of three terms into at least two nonempty groups has a group of size one), and for that $\nu$ the identity $c_\nu=0$ of step 3.1 would read $\pm A_j=0$ for the corresponding single wave, contradicting the nonzero trace datum of step 1.1. Hence $\lambda_i=\lambda_r=\lambda_t$, which is the assertion (a): reading the three coordinates, $\kappa_i=\kappa_r=\kappa_t$ and $\omega_i=\omega_r=\omega_t$. [step 3.1, step 1.1, algebra]

5.1 [Geometry, part (b)] By step 4.1, $\kappa_j=\kappa:=\kappa_i$ for every $j$, and $k_j=\kappa_j+(k_j\cdot n)n=\kappa+(k_j\cdot n)n$ by the orthogonal decomposition of [F3]; hence each $k_j$ lies in the complex span of $\kappa$ and $n$, which is two-dimensional over $\mathbb C$ when $\kappa\ne0$ because $\kappa$ and $n$ are independent: if $\kappa=zn$ for some $z\in\mathbb C$ then $0=\kappa\cdot n=z\,(n\cdot n)=z$, so $\kappa=0$, contrary to hypothesis. When the wavevectors are real, $\kappa$ and $n$ are real and each $k_j$ lies in their real span, the corresponding real plane. If instead $\kappa=0$, then every $k_j=(k_j\cdot n)n$ is parallel to $n$. [step 4.1, F3, algebra]

5.2 [Hypotheses used and the absent-wave case, part (d)] The argument of steps 1.1-4.1 used exactly: the two tangential identities of [A1], the nonzero amplitudes $e_j\ne0$ and the amplitude conditions with the dispersion relation $\varepsilon\mu\omega_j^2=k_j\cdot k_j\ne0$ of [F1] (the dispersion enters through the evaluation of $k_j\times h_j$ from $h_j=k_j\times e_j/(\mu_j\omega_j)$, so the role listed as $k_j\cdot k_j\ne0$ is the nondegeneracy actually used); the normal orientation conditions $k_i\cdot n>0$, $k_r\cdot n<0$, the two normal interface identities, and the optional normal-root selection rule were not used, and no outgoing or tangential-growth condition was used. If a consuming construction declares the reflected wave absent, $e_r=h_r=0$ (so that $A_r=0$ and only the incident and transmitted traces are matched), the same grouping argument applies to the two nonzero trace data $A_i$ and $-A_t$: a singleton group would give $\pm A_i=0$ or $\mp A_t=0$, impossible by step 1.1, so $\lambda_i=\lambda_t$, i.e. $\kappa_i=\kappa_t$ and $\omega_i=\omega_t$; no constraint on the labels $k_r,\omega_r$ of the absent field is produced, and a Fresnel construction may assign it the common phase as a convention. [step 1.1, step 4.1, A1, F1, algebra]

6.1 [Angles and laws, part (c)] Assume now that $k_i,k_r,k_t$ are real. For each $j$, [F1] gives $k_j\cdot k_j=|k_j|^2=\varepsilon_j\mu_j\omega^2$ with the common $\omega=\omega_j$ of step 4.1, so $|k_j|=\omega(\varepsilon_j\mu_j)^{1/2}=\omega/v_j>0$ by [F6] and [F1], and $|k_i|=|k_r|$. By [F3], $|k_j|^2=|\kappa_j|^2+(k_j\cdot n)^2$, so $|\kappa_j|\le|k_j|$ and the ratio $\sin\theta_j:=|\kappa_j|/|k_j|$ lies in $[0,1]$; define $\theta_j:=\arcsin(|\kappa_j|/|k_j|)\in[0,\pi/2]$ by [F5], so that $\sin\theta_j=|\kappa_j|/|k_j|$ as in the Statement. Since $\kappa_r=\kappa_i=\kappa_t=\kappa$ by step 4.1, $\sin\theta_r=|\kappa|/|k_r|=|\kappa|/|k_i|=\sin\theta_i$; the principal inverse sine is strictly increasing on its interval because sine is strictly increasing on $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ by [F5], hence injective, so $\theta_r=\theta_i$. Similarly $|\kappa|=\omega(\varepsilon_1\mu_1)^{1/2}\sin\theta_i=\omega(\varepsilon_2\mu_2)^{1/2}\sin\theta_t$, that is $n_1\sin\theta_i=n_2\sin\theta_t$ after multiplying by $c/\omega$ where $n_j=c(\varepsilon_j\mu_j)^{1/2}=c/v_j$ by [F1] and [F6]. If $\theta_i>0$ then $\sin\theta_i>0$ and division is legitimate, giving $\sin\theta_t/\sin\theta_i=(\varepsilon_1\mu_1)^{1/2}/(\varepsilon_2\mu_2)^{1/2}=v_2/v_1=n_1/n_2$; if $\theta_i=0$ then $|\kappa|=0$, so $|\kappa_t|=0$ and $\theta_t=\arcsin 0=0$, since $\sin 0=0$ and the principal inverse sine returns the unique number of $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ with sine $0$, without forming $0/0$. [step 4.1, step 5.1, F1, F3, F5, F6, algebra]

7.1 [Scope, part (e)] The four statements (a)-(d) are conditional on the given wave data and the interface hypothesis [A1]; nothing about the existence of interface-consistent data, reflection or transmission amplitudes, energy fluxes or well-posedness is proved. All frequencies are positive, all tangential quantities may be complex, and clauses (a) and (b) cover complex transmitted wavevectors; the propagating angle selection of (c) and the amplitudes are the business of the consuming interface theorems. The interface identities on the noncompact plane are the stated hypothesis; for compact interfaces the distributional jump theorem [[pthm-em-material-stationary-jumps]] supplies them, and no distributional or choice-theoretic clause of that theorem is used here (only complex arithmetic, the character lemma, and the real geometry of the propagating case), so no choice principle is inherited. [given, step 5.2, F2, F7] ∎
