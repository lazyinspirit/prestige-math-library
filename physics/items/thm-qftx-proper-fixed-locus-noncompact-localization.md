## Statement

Let $T=(\mathbb C^\times)^r$, $r\geq0$, act algebraically on a smooth separated finite-type complex variety $X$ of pure complex dimension $d$. Assume $X$ has a finite $T$-invariant affine open cover and that its fixed-point subscheme $F=X^T$ is proper. Use Borel equivariant cohomology $H_T^q(X;\mathbb Q)=H^q(E_K\times_KX;\mathbb Q)$ and the normalized Borel–Moore groups whose smooth $d$-dimensional fundamental class has degree $2d$. Let $\chi_i$ be the coordinate characters, $u_i=c_1(L_{\chi_i|_K})$, and $u_\chi=\sum_i n_i u_i$ for $\chi(t_1,\ldots,t_r)=\prod_i t_i^{n_i}$. The multiplicative set $S_T$ is generated by $u_\chi$ for all nontrivial characters. Write $F=\coprod_a F_a$ for its finitely many connected components, $i_a:F_a\hookrightarrow X$ for their inclusions, $d_a=\dim_{\mathbb C}F_a$, $c_a=d-d_a$, and $\nu_a=TX|_{F_a}/TF_a$. Each $p_a:F_a\to\mathrm{pt}$ is proper. The localized pushforward is $p_*^{\mathrm{loc}}=p_{F*}\circ(i_*)^{-1}$, where $i_*=\sum_a i_{a*}$ is the localized fixed-inclusion isomorphism and $p_F:F\to\mathrm{pt}$ is proper. For every homogeneous $\alpha\in H_T^q(X;\mathbb Q)$, it satisfies
$$ p_*^{\mathrm{loc}}(\alpha\mathbin{\cap}[X]_T)=\sum_a p_{a*}\left(\frac{i_a^*\alpha}{e_T(\nu_a)}\mathbin{\cap}[F_a]_T\right)\quad\text{in }S_T^{-1}H^{\mathrm{BM},T}_{2d-q}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb Q), $$
where $p_{a}:F_a\to\mathrm{pt}$ is proper, $e_T(\nu_a)$ is the zero-section Euler class with complex orientation, $[X]_T$ and $[F_a]_T$ are the complex-oriented fundamental classes, and division means multiplication by the localized inverse of $e_T(\nu_a)$. If $X\to\mathrm{pt}$ is proper, $p_*^{\mathrm{loc}}$ equals the genuine proper pushforward. The construction is independent of the chosen finite invariant affine cover and is compatible with proper equivariant maps between such smooth varieties with proper fixed loci. For finite $F$, the formula is the finite reciprocal-Euler sum; if $F=\varnothing$, both sides are zero. A component with $c_a=0$ has denominator $1$. Each term has degree $2d-q$, since $e_T(\nu_a)$ has degree $2c_a$ and $[F_a]_T$ has degree $2d_a$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The torus action, smooth separated variety $X$, its finite invariant affine cover, proper fixed locus $F$, normalized Borel–Moore groups, and character-localized coefficient ring.

[A1] AC is inherited through the equivariant Borel–Moore and Thom suppliers. [[def-qftx-proper-fixed-locus-localized-pushforward]] [[lem-qftx-fixed-embedding-bm-euler-self-intersection]]

[F1] The proper fixed-locus construction is $p_{F*}\circ(i_*)^{-1}$, with $i_*$ the character-localized Borel–Moore concentration isomorphism. [[def-qftx-proper-fixed-locus-localized-pushforward]]

[F2] The finite invariant affine-cover hypothesis makes $i_*:S_T^{-1}H^{\mathrm{BM},T}_*(F)\to S_T^{-1}H^{\mathrm{BM},T}_*(X)$ an isomorphism. [[thm-qftx-finite-affine-atlas-bm-concentration]]

[F3] The fixed locus is smooth, its normal weights are nontrivial, the complex-oriented Gysin map satisfies $i_a^!i_{a*}\beta=e_T(\nu_a)\cap\beta$, and $e_T(\nu_a)$ is a unit after localization when $F_a$ is proper. [[lem-qftx-fixed-embedding-bm-euler-self-intersection]]

[F4] Proper pushforward is functorial, closed inclusions are proper, and cap product with an equivariant cohomology class sends $[X]_T$ to Borel–Moore degree $2d-q$. [[def-qftx-localized-equivariant-borel-moore-data]]

## Proof

**Given:** The data in the Statement.

1.1 The map $i:F\hookrightarrow X$, its components $i_a$, and the localized groups are intrinsic; the finite affine cover is only a hypothesis used to prove [F2]. Since $i_*$ is the same canonical proper pushforward for every such cover, its inverse is unique. Therefore the definition $p_*^{\mathrm{loc}}=p_{F*}(i_*)^{-1}$ is independent of the cover. [A1, F1, F2, given]

2.1 Let $f:X\to Y$ be a proper $T$-equivariant map between smooth separated finite-type varieties with finite invariant affine covers and proper fixed loci $F_X,F_Y$. Its restriction $f^T:F_X\to F_Y$ is proper, and the square formed by $i_X,i_Y,f^T,f$ commutes because $f$ sends fixed points to fixed points. Functoriality of proper Borel–Moore pushforward gives $f_*i_{X*}=i_{Y*}f^T_*$. After localization both $i_{X*}$ and $i_{Y*}$ are isomorphisms by [F2], so applying their inverses and then the proper traces proves $p_{Y*}^{\mathrm{loc}}f_*=p_{X*}^{\mathrm{loc}}$. If $X\to\mathrm{pt}$ itself is proper, then $p_{F*}=p_{X*}i_*$; composing with $i_*^{-1}$ proves $p_*^{\mathrm{loc}}=p_{X*}$. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1]

2.2 Let $\beta=(i_*)^{-1}(\alpha\cap[X]_T)$. In a tubular neighborhood, the refined Gysin map is cap with the supported complex Thom class followed by restriction to $F_a$; associativity and naturality of cap therefore give $i_a^!(\alpha\cap[X]_T)=i_a^*\alpha\cap i_a^![X]_T=i_a^*\alpha\cap[F_a]_T$, using the normalization in [F3]. Since $\alpha\cap[X]_T=i_*\beta$, the self-intersection formula [F3] gives $e_T(\nu_a)\cap\beta_a=i_a^*\alpha\cap[F_a]_T$. Properness of $F_a$ makes $e_T(\nu_a)$ invertible after localization by [F3], hence $\beta_a=(i_a^*\alpha/e_T(\nu_a))\cap[F_a]_T$. Applying $p_{a*}$ and summing the finitely many components yields the displayed formula. The degree is $2d-q$: division by the degree-$2c_a$ Euler class lowers the localized cohomological degree of the insertion from $q$ to $q-2c_a$, and capping with the degree-$2d_a$ fundamental class gives $2d_a-(q-2c_a)=2d-q$. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 If $F=\varnothing$, [F2] makes the localized Borel–Moore groups of $X$ zero and the component sum is empty. If $F$ is finite, every $F_a$ is a point and $e_T(\nu_a)$ is the product of its nonzero tangent-character classes, so each summand is the stated reciprocal-Euler term. If $c_a=0$, the normal bundle is rank zero and its complex-oriented Euler class is $1$. These cases agree with the formula and the degree computation above. [F2, F3, step 2.2] ∎

## Remarks

For a proper equivariant map $f:X\to Y$ as in step 2.1, $f^T:F_X\to F_Y$ is its restriction to the fixed loci, and the fixed-locus square is

~~~tikzcd
F_X \arrow[r, "i_X"] \arrow[d, "f^T"'] & X \arrow[d, "f"] \\
F_Y \arrow[r, "i_Y"'] & Y
~~~

The horizontal maps are closed inclusions and the vertical maps are the restrictions of $f$; step 2.1 proves the induced pushforward identity independently of the diagram.

The theorem applies to a stable ADHM quotient only after its separate mathematical construction proves smoothness, separatedness, a finite invariant word-slice affine cover, and properness of the partition fixed locus. Those ADHM-specific hypotheses are not assumed or proved here; the present result supplies the localization calculation once they are established.
