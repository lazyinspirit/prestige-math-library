## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice, let $B$ be a standard Brownian motion
[[def-brownian-motion]]. All path operations below use a fixed
everywhere-continuous, zero-start representative: choose a measurable
probability-one event on which the paths are continuous and $B_0=0$, and
replace $B$ by the zero path off this event, as in
[[def-wiener-measure-on-continuous-path-space]]. Retain the notation $B$ for
this representative and use its own raw natural filtration and usual
augmentation. This preserves every finite-dimensional law; all maxima and
hitting times below refer to this representative, without transferring
stopping times between the raw filtrations of different versions.
Let $a>0$ and $T>0$, and put
$$M_T:=\sup_{0\le s\le T}B_s,\qquad \tau_a:=\inf\{s\ge0:B_s=a\} .$$
Then:

1. The path reflected at $\tau_a$ has Wiener law: for each $T>0$ the process
   $(B_u)_{0\le u\le T}$ and its reflection $\widetilde B_u:=B_u$ for
   $u\le\tau_a\wedge T$, $\widetilde B_u:=2a-B_u$ for
   $\tau_a\wedge T<u\le T$, have the same law on the cylinder sigma-algebra
   $\sigma(\pi_u:u\in[0,T])$ of $\mathbb R^{[0,T]}$.
2. For every $b\le a$,
   $$P(M_T\ge a,\ B_T\le b)=P(B_T\ge2a-b),$$
   and in particular $P(M_T\ge a)=2P(B_T\ge a)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a standard Brownian motion $B$ with every path continuous and $B_0=0$ everywhere, real $a>0$, $T>0$ and $b\le a$.

[F1] $\tau_a$ is a stopping time for the raw natural filtration and hence for the usual augmentation, which is right-continuous; and $M_T\ge a$ if and only if $\tau_a\le T$. The combination $S:=\tau_a\wedge T$ is a stopping time bounded by $T$. [[lem-brownian-first-hitting-times-of-closed-sets-are-stopping-times]] [[def-continuous-time-stopping-time]] [[def-natural-and-usual-augmented-brownian-filtrations]]

[F2] Strong Markov: for an a.s. finite stopping time $\tau$ of the usual augmentation, the shifted increment process $(B_{\tau+t}-B_\tau)_{t\ge0}$ is independent of $\mathcal F_\tau$ with the finite-dimensional marginals of Wiener measure; moreover $E[\Phi((B_{\tau+t}))|\mathcal F_\tau]=\Psi_\Phi(B_\tau)$ for bounded Borel path functionals $\Phi$. [[thm-strong-markov-property-of-brownian-motion]]

[F3] Wiener measure is the unique Borel probability on continuous path space whose coordinates are centered Gaussian with covariance $\min(s,t)$; hence the map $w\mapsto-w$ preserves Wiener measure, because it preserves every finite-dimensional centered Gaussian law with that covariance. [[thm-uniqueness-of-wiener-measure]] [[def-wiener-measure-on-continuous-path-space]]

[F4] For each $t>0$ the law of $B_t$ has the strictly positive density $p_t(0,\cdot)$, so $P(B_t=c)=0$ for every $c$. [[lem-brownian-transition-semigroup-property]]

[F5] By definition, the cylinder sigma-algebra $\sigma(\pi_u:u\in[0,T])$ is generated by finite-coordinate cylinder sets, which form a pi-system; a lambda-system containing that pi-system contains the generated sigma-algebra, and conditional laws agree almost surely when their defining conditional expectations agree. [[thm-dynkin-pi-lambda]] [[def-conditional-expectation-as-an-ae-class]]

[F7] If $R$ is $\mathcal F_S$-measurable and $W$ is an independent random element with law $\mu$, then for bounded product-measurable $h$, $E[h(R,W)\mid\mathcal F_S]=\int h(R,w)\mu(dw)$. The Borel sigma-algebra of continuous path space is generated by coordinates; thus coordinate measurability and cylinder independence imply path-space measurability and independence for an everywhere-continuous random path. [[lem-conditioning-a-known-state-and-independent-noise]] [[lem-borel-sigma-algebra-of-continuous-path-space-is-generated-by-coordinates]]

[F6] AC supplies the conditional-expectation and strong-Markov interfaces. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[thm-strong-markov-property-of-brownian-motion]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F1] the time $\tau_a$ is a stopping time of the usual augmentation and $S=\tau_a\wedge T$ is a stopping time bounded by the deterministic time $T$, hence a.s. finite; on the event $\{\tau_a\le T\}$ one has $S=\tau_a$ and $B_S=a$ by continuity, while on $\{\tau_a>T\}$ one has $S=T$. Also $M_T\ge a\iff\tau_a\le T$ by continuity of the path. [F1, given]

1.2 The law of any finite-dimensional marginal of $B$ is unchanged by the substitution $B\mapsto-B$, and the cylinder pi-system determines a law on $\mathbb R^{[0,T]}$; hence the law of $(B_u)_{u\le T}$ is invariant under negation, and the same holds for every process whose finite-dimensional marginals are those of Brownian motion, by [F5] and [F3]. [F3, F5]

2.1 Define $\widetilde B_u:=B_u$ for $u\le S$ and $\widetilde B_u:=2a-B_u$ for $S<u\le T$. Every coordinate is measurable by the stopping-time tests. The path is continuous: if $S<T$, then $B_S=a$, and if $S=T$ it is unchanged. For finitely many $u_i\in[0,T]$, put $R=(S,B_{u_1\wedge S},\ldots,B_{u_k\wedge S})$. This is $\mathcal F_S$-measurable: $S$ is measurable there by its test events, and [F2]'s stopped-value measurability at $u_i\wedge S$, followed by the stopped-sigma-algebra inclusion for $u_i\wedge S\le S$, gives the other coordinates. Since paths are everywhere continuous and these times finite, the version convention in [F2] gives the literal values. The path $W(r)=B_{S+r}-B_S$ is everywhere continuous, has Wiener law and is independent of $\mathcal F_S$ by [F2], [F7] and coordinate generation. Random evaluation $(s,w)\mapsto w((u_i-s)^+)$ is measurable: replace its time by the ceiling on the dyadic mesh, use the countable coordinate formula, then pass to the pointwise limit by continuity. Hence the vectors $B_{u_i}=B_{u_i\wedge S}+W((u_i-S)^+)$ and $\widetilde B_{u_i}=B_{u_i\wedge S}-W((u_i-S)^+)$ are product-measurable functions of $(R,W)$. These formulas also hold when $S=T$ because $W(0)=0$. For every bounded Borel test of this vector, [F7] and invariance of Wiener measure under $w\mapsto-w$ from step 1.2 give equal conditional expectations. Thus the finite-dimensional laws agree, and [F5] gives equality on the entire cylinder sigma-algebra. [F1, F2, F5, F7, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 On the event $\{\tau_a\le T\}$, the reflected path coincides with $B$ on $[0,\tau_a]$, so $\tau_a(\widetilde B)=\tau_a$; moreover $\widetilde B_T=2a-B_T$ there. Hence $\{\tau_a\le T,\ B_T\le b\}=\{\tau_a(\widetilde B)\le T,\ \widetilde B_T\ge2a-b\}$ as events. These are cylinder-measurable events on the continuous-path representatives: use the rational-distance formula of [F1] for the hit event and the terminal coordinate for the inequality. Thus by step 2.1 the probability equals $P(\tau_a\le T,\ B_T\ge2a-b)$. Since $b\le a$ implies $2a-b\ge a$, the event $\{B_T\ge2a-b\}$ is contained in $\{B_T\ge a\}\subseteq\{\tau_a\le T\}$, so the last probability is $P(B_T\ge2a-b)$. Together with $M_T\ge a\iff\tau_a\le T$ from step 1.1, this is the first identity of assertion 2. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 Taking $b=a$ in step 3.1 gives $P(M_T\ge a,\ B_T\le a)=P(B_T\ge a)$. Since $\{B_T>a\}\subseteq\{M_T\ge a\}$ and $\{B_T>a\}$ differs from $\{B_T\ge a\}$ by the null event $\{B_T=a\}$ by [F4], adding $P(B_T>a)=P(B_T\ge a)$ to both sides gives $P(M_T\ge a)=2P(B_T\ge a)$, the second identity of assertion 2. Assertion 1 is step 2.1. [F4, step 3.1]

5.1 The endpoint and degenerate cases are covered: the statement requires $a>0$ and $T>0$; the value $b=a$ is used in step 4.1 and is the boundary case of the constraint $b\le a$; the case $b<a$ is step 3.1 unchanged; the case $\tau_a>T$ contributes to neither event in the first probability identity, by the containment in step 3.1; the case $\tau_a\le T$ with equality $B_T=a$ is the null event excluded by [F4]. AC is used through [F6] for the strong Markov and conditional-expectation interfaces. [F1, F4, F6, given, step 3.1] ∎

## Source notes

Lawler, Proposition 2.7.2, derives the maximum formula from post-hit symmetry and absence of a terminal atom. Here the full reflected-path law is proved by conditioning on the bounded time tau_a wedge T, so the proof needs no prior almost-sure finiteness of tau_a. The measurable continuous-path representative is fixed before forming its filtration and hitting times.
