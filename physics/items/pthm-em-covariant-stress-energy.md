## Statement

Assume the smooth branch of [[post-em-vacuum-maxwell]] on the laboratory region $U\cong I\times\Omega$ of [[def-em-lab-geometry]]: $E,B$ jointly $C^1$ fields of [[def-em-fields]] with the additional derivatives required by each Maxwell equation existing and continuous, continuous sources $(\rho,J)$ of [[def-em-sources]] and the constants of [[def-em-si-conventions]]; let $F$ be the electromagnetic two-form of [[def-em-electromagnetic-tensor]] with its fixed components, $j=(c\rho,J)$ the four-current, $u$ and $S$ the energy density and Poynting vector of [[def-em-vacuum-energy-flux]], and $g$, $\sigma$, $\Pi=-\sigma$ the momentum density, Maxwell stress and momentum flux of [[def-em-vacuum-stress-momentum]]. Define the stress-energy tensor of the field by $$T^{\mu\nu}:=\frac{1}{\mu_0}\Bigl(F^{\mu\alpha}F^\nu{}_\alpha-\frac14\eta^{\mu\nu}F^{\alpha\beta}F_{\alpha\beta}\Bigr).$$ Then: (a) [Object] $T$ is a symmetric type-$(2,0)$ tensor field of [[def-ck-tensor-field-on-minkowski-space]] with $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu}$ continuous on $U$, it is traceless, $\eta_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}=0$, and under a proper orthochronous change of inertial chart with transition $x'=\Lambda x+b$ its components transform as $T'^{\mu\nu}=\Lambda^\mu{}_\alpha\Lambda^\nu{}_\beta T^{\alpha\beta}$ ([[pthm-em-lorentz-field-transform]], [[thm-sr-tensor-and-current-transformation]] (iii)). (b) [Components] in the laboratory frame $$T^{00}=u=\frac{\varepsilon_0|E|^2}{2}+\frac{|B|^2}{2\mu_0},\qquad T^{0i}=T^{i0}=\frac{S_i}{c}=c\,g_i=\frac{(E\times B)_i}{\mu_0c},\qquad T^{ij}=-\sigma_{ij},$$ where $\sigma_{ij}=\varepsilon_0\bigl(E_iE_j-\tfrac12\delta_{ij}|E|^2\bigr)+\mu_0^{-1}\bigl(B_iB_j-\tfrac12\delta_{ij}|B|^2\bigr)$; in particular the spatial block is minus the Maxwell stress of [[def-em-vacuum-stress-momentum]]. (c) [Balance] under the smooth branch, $$\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=-f^\nu,\qquad f^\nu:=F^{\nu\lambda}j_\lambda,$$ where algebraically $f=(J\cdot E/c,\rho E+J\times B)$. Its interpretation as the electromagnetic four-force density delivered to matter uses the separately adopted continuum coupling [[post-em-lorentz-coupling]] (ii) for matter models and sources satisfying that postulate's hypotheses. The component identities are $\partial_tu+\operatorname{div}S=-J\cdot E$ and $\partial_tg-\operatorname{div}\sigma=-(\rho E+J\times B)$ of [[pthm-em-poynting-balance]] (a) and [[pthm-em-momentum-balance]] (a). (d) [Matter coupling and finite-flux conservation] let $T_{\mathrm m}$ be a $C^1$ symmetric type-$(2,0)$ field on $U$ satisfying $\partial_\mu T_{\mathrm m}{}^{\mu\nu}=f^\nu$ (an adopted matter model, not derived here); then $\partial_\mu(T+T_{\mathrm m})^{\mu\nu}=0$, on every finite axis-parallel box with an open neighbourhood contained in $U$, the box and slice balance identities of [[thm-sr-stress-balance-and-finite-flux]] hold for the total tensor with zero divergence, retaining all side-flux terms. For each component $b$ and $c_0\le s_1<s_2\le d_0$, set $\mathcal F^b(s_1,s_2):=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(\int_{[s_1,s_2]\times K_i^+}T_{\mathrm{total}}{}^{ib}\,d^3x-\int_{[s_1,s_2]\times K_i^-}T_{\mathrm{total}}{}^{ib}\,d^3x\right)$, with $K:=\prod_{i=1}^{3}[c_i,d_i]$, its faces $K_i^\pm$, and $Q^b(s):=\int_K T_{\mathrm{total}}{}^{0b}(s,x)\,d^3x$ as in that theorem. Then $Q^b(s_2)-Q^b(s_1)=-\mathcal F^b(s_1,s_2)$, so $Q^b(s_2)=Q^b(s_1)$ if and only if $\mathcal F^b(s_1,s_2)=0$. In particular, $Q^b$ is constant throughout $[c_0,d_0]$ if and only if this net signed side flux vanishes on every subinterval $[s_1,s_2]$. The theorem's facewise-zero side-flux hypothesis is sufficient, but is not necessary, for constancy; zero divergence alone does not guarantee it. The angular current $M^{\lambda\mu\nu}:=x^\mu T_{\mathrm{total}}{}^{\lambda\nu}-x^\nu T_{\mathrm{total}}{}^{\lambda\mu}$ satisfies $\partial_\lambda M^{\lambda\mu\nu}=0$ by symmetry. (e) [Scope] the divergence is a product identity of $C^1$ objects and is not asserted for the weak branch, at singular source support or for any product of distributions; symmetry, zero trace and the component display are algebraic; no matter tensor is derived from vacuum Maxwell, no radiation reaction or self-force is included, and no empirical claim is made.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The smooth branch of [[post-em-vacuum-maxwell]] on the laboratory region $U\cong I\times\Omega$ of [[def-em-lab-geometry]]: the jointly $C^1$ fields $E,B$ of [[def-em-fields]] with the additional derivatives required by each Maxwell equation existing and continuous, the continuous sources $(\rho,J)$ of [[def-em-sources]], and the constants of [[def-em-si-conventions]]; the electromagnetic two-form $F$ with the components and conventions of [F2]; the four-current $j=(c\rho,J)$; the objects $u,S$ of [[def-em-vacuum-energy-flux]] and $g,\sigma,\Pi=-\sigma$ of [[def-em-vacuum-stress-momentum]]; the tensor $T$ of the statement's definition; and, in clause (d), the adopted matter tensor $T_{\mathrm m}$ of [F7].

[F1] The four smooth-branch equations hold pointwise on $\Omega\times I$; by [[pthm-em-tensor-maxwell-equivalence]] (a) the two tensor systems $(\mathrm{TM1})$ $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu}=-\mu_0j^\nu$ and $(\mathrm{TM2})$ $\partial_\lambda F_{\mu\nu}+\partial_\mu F_{\nu\lambda}+\partial_\nu F_{\lambda\mu}=0$ hold there, and every first partial derivative of a component of $F$ appearing in $(\mathrm{TM1})$ or $(\mathrm{TM2})$ exists and is continuous ([[post-em-vacuum-maxwell]] (a), [[def-em-fields]], [[def-em-si-conventions]], [[def-em-sources]] (c); $\mu_0>0$, $\varepsilon_0=(\mu_0c^2)^{-1}$).

[F2] In the laboratory chart with $\eta=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$ the components satisfy $F^{00}=0$, $F^{0i}=E_i/c$, $F^{i0}=-E_i/c$, $F^{ij}=\epsilon_{ijk}B_k$, $F_{0i}=-E_i/c$, $F_{ij}=\epsilon_{ijk}B_k$, so $F^i{}_0=F^{i0}\eta_{00}=E_i/c$ and $F^i{}_j=\epsilon_{ijk}B_k$; the contraction is $F^{\alpha\beta}F_{\alpha\beta}=2\bigl(|B|^2-|E|^2/c^2\bigr)$, and the elementary identities $\epsilon_{ijk}\epsilon_{ijk'}=2\delta_{kk'}$ and $\epsilon_{ikm}\epsilon_{jkn}=\delta_{ij}\delta_{mn}-\delta_{in}\delta_{mj}$ hold, with $|\cdot|$ and $\cdot$ the Euclidean structure ([[def-em-electromagnetic-tensor]] (a), (b), [[def-cross-product-in-r3]], [[def-euclidean-inner-product]]).

[F3] The definitions: $u=\tfrac{\varepsilon_0}{2}|E|^2+\tfrac{1}{2\mu_0}|B|^2$, $S=\mu_0^{-1}E\times B$; $g=S/c^2=\varepsilon_0E\times B$; $\sigma_{ij}=\varepsilon_0(E_iE_j-\tfrac12\delta_{ij}|E|^2)+\mu_0^{-1}(B_iB_j-\tfrac12\delta_{ij}|B|^2)$; $\Pi=-\sigma$ ([[def-em-vacuum-energy-flux]] (a), [[def-em-vacuum-stress-momentum]] (a), (b), (c)).

[F4] For a proper orthochronous change of chart $x'=\Lambda x+b$ the components of the same $F$ satisfy $F'^{ab}=\Lambda^a{}_c\Lambda^b{}_dF^{cd}$ and $F'_{ab}=(\Lambda^{-1})^c{}_a(\Lambda^{-1})^d{}_bF_{cd}$, every contraction of one contravariant with one covariant index is invariant, and $\eta'_{ab}=\eta_{ab}$ ([[thm-sr-tensor-and-current-transformation]] (iii), [[pthm-em-lorentz-field-transform]] (c)).

[F5] Regularity criterion: a map assigning to each point a type-$(r,s)$ tensor is a $\mathcal C^k$ tensor field exactly when all its components in one, or equivalently every, inertial chart are $\mathcal C^k$ functions of the chart coordinates, and regularity of components is preserved under the linear component changes of a chart transition ([[def-ck-tensor-field-on-minkowski-space]]).

[F6] The coordinate partial derivatives are those of [[def-directional-and-partial-derivatives]], and sums and products of differentiable functions obey the product rule; a finite sum of products of continuous functions is continuous ([[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]]).

[F7] Balance and interpretation suppliers: [[pthm-em-poynting-balance]] (a) gives $\partial_tu+\operatorname{div}S=-J\cdot E$ and [[pthm-em-momentum-balance]] (a) gives $\partial_tg-\operatorname{div}\sigma=-(\rho E+J\times B)$ componentwise, with $\operatorname{div}$ at fixed time and $f:=\rho E+J\times B$ the force density; clause (ii) of [[post-em-lorentz-coupling]] identifies $J\cdot E$ and $\rho E+J\times B$ as the electromagnetic power and force densities delivered to a matter model satisfying that clause, and its clause (iii) records the scope. In clause (d), $T_{\mathrm m}$ is a $C^1$ symmetric type-$(2,0)$ field on $U$ with $\partial_\mu T_{\mathrm m}{}^{\mu\nu}=f^\nu$.

[F8] Finite-flux theorem [[thm-sr-stress-balance-and-finite-flux]]: for a $C^1$ symmetric type-$(2,0)$ field on an open neighbourhood of a closed axis-parallel box $B=\prod_{a=0}^3[c_a,d_a]$, with $f^b=\partial_aT^{ab}$, the box identity and the slice-balance identity hold, the latter stating that $Q^b(s_2)-Q^b(s_1)$ equals the integral of $f^b$ over the slab minus the three side-flux differences; and if $f=0$ on the slab and the side components $T^{ib}$ vanish on the side faces, then $Q^b(s_1)=Q^b(s_2)$. All face integrals are Riemann integrals over the remaining coordinates.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct algebra from the component dictionary for the tensor, symmetry and trace; the product rule and the cyclic identity for the divergence; the finite-box theorem for the conservation identities.

1.1 (Clause (a): symmetry, trace and regularity) Since $F^b{}_a=\eta_{ac}F^{bc}$, the first term satisfies $F^{\mu\alpha}F^\nu{}_\alpha=F^{\mu\alpha}\eta_{\alpha\beta}F^{\nu\beta}$, and swapping the two factors and relabelling the dummy indices while using symmetry of $\eta$ gives $F^{\mu\alpha}\eta_{\alpha\beta}F^{\nu\beta}=F^{\nu\beta}\eta_{\beta\alpha}F^{\mu\alpha}$; the second term $\eta^{\mu\nu}$ is symmetric, so $T^{\mu\nu}=T^{\nu\mu}$. For the trace, $\eta_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}=\mu_0^{-1}\bigl(F^{\mu\alpha}F_{\mu\alpha}-\tfrac14\eta_{\mu\nu}\eta^{\mu\nu}F^{\alpha\beta}F_{\alpha\beta}\bigr)$ and $F^{\mu\alpha}F_{\mu\alpha}=F^{\alpha\beta}F_{\alpha\beta}=F^{2}$, $\eta_{\mu\nu}\eta^{\mu\nu}=4$; hence $\eta_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}=\mu_0^{-1}(F^2-F^2)=0$. Each component $T^{\mu\nu}$ is a finite sum of products of components of $F$ (a $C^1$ tensor field), so $T$ is a $C^1$ symmetric type-$(2,0)$ tensor field by [F5]; by the product rule [F6], $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=\mu_0^{-1}\bigl[(\partial_\mu F^{\mu\alpha})F^\nu{}_\alpha+F^{\mu\alpha}\partial_\mu F^\nu{}_\alpha-\tfrac12F^{\alpha\beta}\partial^\nu F_{\alpha\beta}\bigr]$, in which the first factor is continuous by $(\mathrm{TM1})$ and the remaining derivative factors exist and are continuous by the smooth-branch hypotheses of [F1]; hence $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu}$ is continuous on $U$. [given, F1, F2, F5, F6, algebra]

1.2 (Clause (a): the transformation law) Let $x'=\Lambda x+b$ be a proper orthochronous change of inertial chart. By [F4], $F'^{\mu\alpha}F'^\nu{}_\alpha=\Lambda^\mu{}_e\Lambda^\alpha{}_fF^{ef}\,\eta_{\alpha\beta}\Lambda^\nu{}_c\Lambda^\beta{}_dF^{cd}=\Lambda^\mu{}_e\Lambda^\nu{}_cF^{ef}F^c{}_f$, because $\eta_{\alpha\beta}\Lambda^\alpha{}_f\Lambda^\beta{}_d=\eta_{fd}$ and $F^{ef}\eta_{fd}=F^e{}_d$; similarly $F'{}^{2}=F'_{ab}F'^{ab}=F_{ab}F^{ab}=F^{2}$ by the two component laws and the inverse-matrix identity, and $\eta'=\eta$. Substituting in the definition gives $T'^{\mu\nu}=\mu_0^{-1}\bigl(\Lambda^\mu{}_e\Lambda^\nu{}_cF^{ef}F^c{}_f-\tfrac14\eta^{\mu\nu}F^{2}\bigr)=\Lambda^\mu{}_e\Lambda^\nu{}_cT^{ec}$, the stated type-$(2,0)$ transformation law. [given, F4, algebra]

1.3 (Clause (b): the components) Substituting [F2] into the definition: $T^{00}=\mu_0^{-1}\bigl(F^{0i}F^0{}_i-\tfrac14\eta^{00}F^{2}\bigr)=\mu_0^{-1}\bigl(|E|^2/c^2+\tfrac14\cdot2(|B|^2-|E|^2/c^2)\bigr)=\mu_0^{-1}\bigl(\tfrac12|E|^2/c^2+\tfrac12|B|^2\bigr)=\varepsilon_0|E|^2/2+|B|^2/(2\mu_0)=u$, using $\eta^{00}=-1$, $\varepsilon_0=(\mu_0c^2)^{-1}$ and [F3]. Next $T^{0i}=\mu_0^{-1}F^{0j}F^i{}_j=\mu_0^{-1}(E_j/c)\epsilon_{ijk}B_k=(E\times B)_i/(\mu_0c)=S_i/c=c\,g_i$, because $\eta^{0i}=0$ and [F3]. Finally $T^{ij}=\mu_0^{-1}\bigl(F^{i0}F^j{}_0+F^{ik}F^j{}_k-\tfrac14\delta_{ij}F^{2}\bigr)$; here $F^{i0}F^j{}_0=(-E_i/c)(E_j/c)=-E_iE_j/c^2$ and $F^{ik}F^j{}_k=\epsilon_{ikm}\epsilon_{jkn}B_mB_n=\delta_{ij}|B|^2-B_iB_j$ by [F2], so with $F^{2}=2(|B|^2-|E|^2/c^2)$, $T^{ij}=\mu_0^{-1}\bigl(-E_iE_j/c^2+\delta_{ij}|B|^2-B_iB_j-\tfrac12\delta_{ij}(|B|^2-|E|^2/c^2)\bigr)=-\varepsilon_0\bigl(E_iE_j-\tfrac12\delta_{ij}|E|^2\bigr)-\mu_0^{-1}\bigl(B_iB_j-\tfrac12\delta_{ij}|B|^2\bigr)=-\sigma_{ij}$, again by $\varepsilon_0=(\mu_0c^2)^{-1}$. This is the displayed component identification, and the spatial block is minus the Maxwell stress of [F3]. [given, F2, F3, algebra]

1.4 (Clause (c): the divergence identity) By the product rule [F6], $\mu_0\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=(\partial_\mu F^{\mu\alpha})F^\nu{}_\alpha+F^{\mu\alpha}\partial_\mu F^\nu{}_\alpha-\tfrac12F^{\alpha\beta}\partial^\nu F_{\alpha\beta}$, the last term from $\partial_\mu(\eta^{\mu\nu}F^{2})=\partial^\nu(F^{2})=2F^{\alpha\beta}\partial^\nu F_{\alpha\beta}$ and the factor $-\tfrac14$. The first term is $-\mu_0j^\alpha F^\nu{}_\alpha=-\mu_0F^{\nu\lambda}j_\lambda$ by $(\mathrm{TM1})$ of [F1] and $j_\lambda=\eta_{\lambda\alpha}j^\alpha$. For the cancellation, contract $(\mathrm{TM2})$ in the form $\partial_\mu F_{\nu\alpha}+\partial_\nu F_{\alpha\mu}+\partial_\alpha F_{\mu\nu}=0$ with $F^{\mu\alpha}$ and fix the free index $\nu$: the first term is $C_\nu:=F^{\mu\alpha}\partial_\mu F_{\nu\alpha}$, the third is $F^{\mu\alpha}\partial_\alpha F_{\mu\nu}=F^{\alpha\mu}\partial_\mu F_{\alpha\nu}$ after relabelling $\alpha\leftrightarrow\mu$, which equals $C_\nu$ because $F^{\alpha\mu}=-F^{\mu\alpha}$ and $F_{\alpha\nu}=-F_{\nu\alpha}$, and the middle term is $-\tfrac12\partial_\nu(F^{2})$, since $F^{\mu\alpha}\partial_\nu F_{\alpha\mu}=\tfrac12\partial_\nu(F^{\mu\alpha}F_{\alpha\mu})=-\tfrac12\partial_\nu(F^{2})$ (the two terms of the derivative of the product are equal after relabelling). Hence $2C_\nu-\tfrac12\partial_\nu(F^{2})=0$, that is $C_\nu=\tfrac14\partial_\nu(F^{2})$. Raising the free index, $F^{\mu\alpha}\partial_\mu F^\nu{}_\alpha=\eta^{\nu\rho}C_\rho=\tfrac14\partial^\nu(F^{2})=\tfrac12F^{\alpha\beta}\partial^\nu F_{\alpha\beta}$, so the second and third terms of the product-rule expression cancel. Therefore $\mu_0\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=-\mu_0F^{\nu\lambda}j_\lambda$, and dividing by $\mu_0$ gives $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=-f^\nu$ with $f^\nu=F^{\nu\lambda}j_\lambda$. Algebraically $f^0=F^{0i}j_i=(E_i/c)J_i=J\cdot E/c$ and $f^i=F^{i0}j_0+F^{ij}j_j=(-E_i/c)(-c\rho)+\epsilon_{ijk}B_kJ_j=\rho E_i+(J\times B)_i$, so $f=(J\cdot E/c,\rho E+J\times B)$; its reading as the electromagnetic four-force density delivered to matter uses the separately adopted clause (ii) of [[post-em-lorentz-coupling]] [F7]. [given, F1, F2, F6, F7, algebra]

2.1 (Clause (c): component identities and comparison) Reading the balance of step 1.4 with $\nu=0$ and using $T^{00}=u$, $T^{i0}=S_i/c$ from step 1.3 and $\partial_0=c^{-1}\partial_t$ gives $\partial_\mu T^{\mu0}=c^{-1}\partial_tu+c^{-1}\operatorname{div}S=c^{-1}(\partial_tu+\operatorname{div}S)$, while $-f^0=-J\cdot E/c$; multiplying by $c$ gives $\partial_tu+\operatorname{div}S=-J\cdot E$, which is clause (a) of [[pthm-em-poynting-balance]] [F7]. Reading it with $\nu=i$ and using $T^{0i}=c\,g_i$, $T^{ji}=-\sigma_{ij}$ gives $\partial_\mu T^{\mu i}=\partial_tg_i-(\operatorname{div}\sigma)_i$ and $-f^i=-(\rho E_i+(J\times B)_i)$, that is $\partial_tg-\operatorname{div}\sigma=-(\rho E+J\times B)$, which is clause (a) of [[pthm-em-momentum-balance]] [F7]; by clause (ii) of [[post-em-lorentz-coupling]] those source terms are the power and force densities delivered to the matter model, so the balance reads as the field-matter balance in that interpretation. [given, step 1.3, step 1.4, F7, algebra]

2.2 (Clause (d): matter coupling, total conservation, finite flux and angular current) Let $T_{\mathrm m}$ be the adopted $C^1$ symmetric type-$(2,0)$ field of [F7] with $\partial_\mu T_{\mathrm m}{}^{\mu\nu}=f^\nu$. Adding it to step 1.4 gives $\partial_\mu(T+T_{\mathrm m})^{\mu\nu}=0$ pointwise on $U$, and the total tensor is $C^1$ and symmetric. On every closed axis-parallel box $B=\prod_{a=0}^{3}[c_a,d_a]$ with an open neighbourhood contained in $U$, the box and slice identities of [F8] apply to the total tensor because its divergence vanishes on the box: the box identity has zero left-hand side, and the slice balance expresses $Q^b(s_2)-Q^b(s_1)$ as minus the three side-flux differences, all side terms retained; write their sum as $\mathcal F^b(s_1,s_2)$, with the same signed faces as in the Statement. Thus $Q^b(s_2)-Q^b(s_1)=-\mathcal F^b(s_1,s_2)$ by clause (b) of [F8]. Equality of the two slice totals is therefore equivalent to $\mathcal F^b(s_1,s_2)=0$, and constancy throughout $[c_0,d_0]$ is equivalent to this equality for every $c_0\le s_1<s_2\le d_0$. The facewise-zero hypothesis in clause (c) of [F8] forces every side integral to vanish and hence is sufficient. It is not necessary: take the zero Maxwell fields and sources, and the constant adopted matter tensor $T_{\mathrm m}=\operatorname{diag}(0,P,0,0)$ with $P\ne0$. It is $C^1$, symmetric and divergence-free; all $Q^b$ are zero, while for $b=1$ the integrals on the two $x^1$ side faces are both the nonzero number $P(s_2-s_1)(d_2-c_2)(d_3-c_3)$ and cancel in their signed difference. Zero divergence alone supplies the balance identity, not a guarantee that the net side flux vanishes. For the angular current $M^{\lambda\mu\nu}=x^\mu T_{\mathrm{total}}{}^{\lambda\nu}-x^\nu T_{\mathrm{total}}{}^{\lambda\mu}$, the product rule and the coordinate identities $\partial_\lambda x^\mu=\delta^\mu_\lambda$ and $\partial_\lambda x^\nu=\delta^\nu_\lambda$ [F6] give $\partial_\lambda M^{\lambda\mu\nu}=\delta^\mu_\lambda T_{\mathrm{total}}{}^{\lambda\nu}+x^\mu\partial_\lambda T_{\mathrm{total}}{}^{\lambda\nu}-\delta^\nu_\lambda T_{\mathrm{total}}{}^{\lambda\mu}-x^\nu\partial_\lambda T_{\mathrm{total}}{}^{\lambda\mu}=T_{\mathrm{total}}{}^{\mu\nu}-T_{\mathrm{total}}{}^{\nu\mu}=0$, the last equality by the symmetry of the total tensor established in step 1.1 and the vanishing divergence. [given, step 1.1, step 1.4, F5, F6, F7, F8, algebra]
