## Statement

Let $k$ be a field and let $A$ be a nonzero finite-type $k$-algebra. Then
there exist algebraically independent elements $z_1,\ldots,z_d\in A$ such that
$A$ is a module-finite algebra over the polynomial ring
$k[z_1,\ldots,z_d]$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$ and a nonzero finite-type $k$-algebra $A$.

[L1] Noether normalisation provides algebraically independent $z_1,\ldots,z_d\in A$ such that $A$ is integral over $k[z_1,\ldots,z_d]$ ([[lem-noether-normalisation-inductive-integrality]]).

[L2] A subalgebra generated by finitely many integral elements over a base ring is module-finite over that base ring ([[lem-algebra-generated-by-finitely-many-integral-elements-is-module-finite]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [L1], choose algebraically independent elements $z_1,\ldots,z_d\in A$ such that $A$ is integral over $R:=k[z_1,\ldots,z_d]$. [L1, choose]

2.1 Because $A$ is of finite type over $k$, choose generators $a_1,\ldots,a_m\in A$ with $A=k[a_1,\ldots,a_m]$. Since $R$ contains the image of $k$ and lies in $A$, we also have $A=R[a_1,\ldots,a_m]$. Each $a_i$ is integral over $R$ by step 1.1, so [L2] implies that $A$ is module-finite over $R$. [L2, step 1.1, given]

3.1 Hence $A$ is module-finite over the polynomial ring $k[z_1,\ldots,z_d]$. [step 2.1] ∎
