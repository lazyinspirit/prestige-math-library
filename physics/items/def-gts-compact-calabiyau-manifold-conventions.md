## Definition

For a smooth Riemannian metric $g$, the **Levi-Civita connection** is its unique smooth affine connection that is torsion-free and metric-compatible. For a piecewise smooth loop $\gamma:[0,1]\to X$ based at $x$, parallel transport is the linear map $P_\gamma:T_xX\to T_xX$ obtained from the unique continuous piecewise parallel section with prescribed initial vector. The **holonomy group at $x$** is the subgroup of $\operatorname{GL}(T_xX)$ generated by all such $P_\gamma$ for the Levi-Civita connection. A unitary frame is a $g$-orthonormal complex basis of $T_xX$. In such a frame, $U(n)=\{U\in\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb C):U^*U=I\}$ and $SU(n)=\{U\in U(n):\det U=1\}$, viewed as real-linear subgroups of the isometries of $(T_xX,g)$. Exact $SU(n)$ holonomy means equality of the holonomy group with this real-linear matrix group.

Let $n\ge1$. In this library, a **Calabi–Yau manifold in the broad convention** is a connected compact Kähler complex $n$-fold $X$ together with a specified holomorphic line-bundle isomorphism $K_X\cong\mathcal O_X$, where $\mathcal O_X$ is the trivial holomorphic line bundle. When the isomorphism is used as data, choose and state the corresponding nowhere-zero holomorphic $n$-form $\Omega\in H^0(X,K_X)$; the choice of $\Omega$ is not part of the abstract manifold unless specified.

The definition asks for holomorphic triviality, not merely a vanishing cohomology class. For such $X$, the integral class $c_1(K_X)$ vanishes and the real class $c_1(T_X^{1,0})_{\mathbb R}$ vanishes; these are consequences in different coefficient groups, not replacements for the holomorphic bundle isomorphism, and no converse is asserted. No assumption that every loop is homotopic to a point, that intermediate spaces of holomorphic forms vanish, that the holonomy group has no proper nonzero invariant complex subspace, or that it equals $SU(n)$ is included. The broad convention does not require exact $SU(n)$ holonomy; the flat torus calculation below in the paired example verifies a proper-subgroup case for $n\ge2$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Full AC; a connected compact smooth complex manifold $X$ of complex dimension $n\ge1$, a Kähler form on $X$, and a specified holomorphic isomorphism $K_X\cong\mathcal O_X$.

[A1] Full AC is the choice-function principle of [[def-axiom-of-choice]]; it is assumed by the imported characteristic-class theorem used below.

[F1] The earlier definition sets $K_X=\Lambda^n\Omega_X^1$, fixes the real fundamental-form convention, and gives $[\rho(\omega)]/(2\pi)=c_1(T_X^{1,0})_{\mathbb R}$ ([[def-gts-kahler-class-canonical-bundle-and-ricci-form]]).

[F2] On a path-connected CW base, a trivial line has total Chern class $1$, and Chern classes are natural under pullback from that base to a CW-type source ([[thm-naturality-normalization-and-whitney-sum-for-chern-classes]]).

[F3] A smooth second-countable manifold has CW homotopy type and its smooth bundles are numerable ([[lem-second-countable-smooth-manifolds-have-cw-homotopy-type]]).

[F6] On the allowed CW-type bases, the projective-bundle definition of topological Chern classes applies and the classes depend only on the bundle's isomorphism class ([[def-chern-classes-from-the-projective-bundle-relation]]).

[F4] The Levi-Civita connection is the unique smooth affine connection that is torsion-free and metric-compatible; every supplied smooth Riemannian metric has exactly one ([[def-levi-civita-connection]], [[thm-fundamental-theorem-of-riemannian-geometry]]).

[F5] Parallel transport along a piecewise smooth curve is the endpoint value of the unique continuous piecewise parallel section with supplied initial vector; the initial parameter may be either endpoint. In a local real frame the coefficient vector satisfies $v'=-A(t)v$, where $A(t)$ is the real connection matrix evaluated on $\dot\gamma(t)$ (the local equation used in Proof step 1.1 of [[thm-existence-and-uniqueness-of-parallel-sections]]; endpoint convention in [[def-parallel-transport-along-a-piecewise-smooth-curve]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The inverse of a holomorphic isomorphism $\tau:K_X\to\mathcal O_X$ sends the constant section $1$ to a nowhere-zero holomorphic section $\Omega=\tau^{-1}(1)$. Conversely, for a nowhere-zero holomorphic section $\Omega$ of $K_X$, the map $f\mapsto f\Omega$ is a fiberwise holomorphic line-bundle isomorphism $\mathcal O_X\to K_X$; its local inverse is holomorphic because the coefficient of $\Omega$ in every local frame is holomorphic and nowhere zero. [given, F1, algebra]

1.2 Since $X$ is a connected manifold, it is locally path-connected and hence path-connected. By [F3], choose a path-connected CW complex $Y$ and a homotopy equivalence $f:X\to Y$. The trivial line $\varepsilon_Y^1$ is numerable by its single global trivialization, and [F2] gives $c_1(\varepsilon_Y^1)=0$. Its pullback is the trivial line $\varepsilon_X^1$, so naturality in [F2], whose target is the CW complex $Y$ and whose source is the CW-type space $X$, gives $c_1(\varepsilon_X^1)=f^*c_1(\varepsilon_Y^1)=0$. The specified holomorphic isomorphism identifies $K_X$ with $\varepsilon_X^1$ as a complex bundle; by [F6], Chern classes on this CW-type base depend only on the bundle isomorphism class. Thus the integral class $c_1(K_X)$ vanishes. [A1, F2, F3, F6, given, choose, algebra]

1.3 For $n\ge2$, use the full lattice $\Lambda=\sum_{a=1}^{2n}\mathbb Z\lambda_a$ generated by a real basis $(\lambda_a)$ of $\mathbb C^n\cong\mathbb R^{2n}$. The real-linear map sending the standard basis to $(\lambda_a)$ sends $\mathbb Z^{2n}$ onto $\Lambda$, so $\Lambda$ is discrete and bounded sets meet it finitely. If a compact set $K$ meets a translate $K+\lambda$, then $\lambda\in K-K$, a bounded set with only finitely many lattice points; this gives proper discontinuity, while freeness follows from the translation action. Distinct orbits have positive distance because only finitely many lattice translates of a fixed representative lie within any fixed bounded distance of another; small disjoint balls therefore give a Hausdorff quotient. A ball smaller than half the shortest nonzero lattice length has disjoint nonidentity translates and gives a quotient chart; the transition maps are translations, hence holomorphic. The closed fundamental parallelepiped is compact and surjects onto $X_0=\mathbb C^n/\Lambda$. The standard Euclidean Hermitian metric and $\omega_0=\sum_j dx_j\wedge dy_j$ descend; $\omega_0$ is positive and closed. The translation-invariant holomorphic form $dz^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dz^n$ descends nowhere zero, so $X_0$ satisfies the broad convention. [given, F1, construct, algebra]

1.4 For any piecewise smooth curve $\gamma:[0,1]\to X$, let $V_v$ be its parallel section with initial vector $v$. For real scalars $a,b$, the section $aV_v+bV_w$ is continuous across corners and solves the homogeneous linear equation in [F5] on each smooth piece, with initial value $av+bw$. Uniqueness gives $V_{av+bw}=aV_v+bV_w$, hence $P_\gamma$ is real-linear. Define $Q:T_{\gamma(1)}X\to T_{\gamma(0)}X$ by solving the same equation with prescribed value at $1$ and evaluating at $0$, as permitted by [F5]. Uniqueness at either endpoint gives $QP_\gamma=I$ and $P_\gamma Q=I$. Thus every loop transport belongs to $\operatorname{GL}(T_xX)$, so the subgroup generated by these maps is well-defined. [F5, algebra]

2.1 Let $H=(h_{j\bar k})$ be the metric matrix from [F1], and write the chosen nowhere-zero form in a holomorphic chart as $\Omega=f(z)\,dz^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dz^n$. The induced determinant metric on $K_X$ gives $\|\Omega\|^2=|f|^2/\det H$. The globally defined real function $u=\log\|\Omega\|^2$ satisfies $\partial\bar\partial\log|f|^2=0$ locally, so [F1] gives $\rho(\omega)=i\partial\bar\partial u=d(i\bar\partial u)$. Because $\rho$ is real, $\operatorname{Re}(i\bar\partial u)$ is a real primitive. Thus $[\rho]=0$ in real de Rham cohomology, and the Chern–Weil identity in [F1] yields $c_1(T_X^{1,0})_{\mathbb R}=0$. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 On $\mathbb C^n$, define $D_X(\sum_af_aE_a)=\sum_aX(f_a)E_a$ for the constant real frame $(E_a)=(\partial_{x_1},\partial_{y_1},\ldots,\partial_{x_n},\partial_{y_n})$. Translation chart changes have identity differential, so $D$ descends to $X_0$. The fields $E_a$ commute, giving zero torsion on the frame; since torsion is tensorial it vanishes. The metric coefficients $g_0(E_a,E_b)=\delta_{ab}$ are constant and $D E_a=0$, so the connection is metric-compatible. By uniqueness in [F4], $D$ is the Levi-Civita connection. A parallel section has constant coefficients in the global parallel frame, so [F5] makes each loop transport the identity. For $n\ge2$, $SU(n)$ contains the nonidentity matrix $\operatorname{diag}(e^{it},e^{-it},1,\ldots,1)$ for $t\notin2\pi\mathbb Z$, so the holonomy is a proper subgroup of $SU(n)$. When $n=1$, $SU(1)$ is trivial and this example does not separate the two groups. [F4, F5, step 1.3, algebra] ∎
