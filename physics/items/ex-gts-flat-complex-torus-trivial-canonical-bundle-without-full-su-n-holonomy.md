## Example

Assume full AC. Let $n\ge1$, let $\Lambda=\sum_{a=1}^{2n}\mathbb Z\lambda_a\subset\mathbb C^n$ be a full lattice generated by a real basis of $\mathbb C^n\cong\mathbb R^{2n}$, and put $X=\mathbb C^n/\Lambda$. The standard Euclidean Hermitian metric $g_0=\sum_j(dx_j^2+dy_j^2)$ descends, with real fundamental form $\omega_0=\sum_jdx_j\wedge dy_j$. Then $X$ is a compact connected Kähler manifold and its canonical bundle is trivialized by the descended nowhere-zero holomorphic form $\Omega_0=dz^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dz^n$. Let $D$ be the Levi-Civita connection of the descended metric and define its curvature by $R^D(X,Y)Z:=D_XD_YZ-D_YD_XZ-D_{[X,Y]}Z$. The metric is flat when $R^D=0$; in this example it is flat and has trivial holonomy. For $n\ge2$, trivial holonomy is a proper subgroup of $SU(n)$, so the broad Calabi–Yau convention does not force exact $SU(n)$ holonomy. For $n=1$, $SU(1)$ is itself trivial and this example does not separate the groups.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Full AC; a real basis $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{2n}$ of $\mathbb C^n$; the standard complex structure and Euclidean metric; and the broad convention from the preceding item.

[F1] The broad convention requires a connected compact Kähler $n$-fold with a specified holomorphic trivialization of its canonical line bundle; it adds no holonomy equality assumption ([[def-gts-compact-calabiyau-manifold-conventions]]).

[F2] For a supplied smooth Riemannian metric the Levi-Civita connection is the unique torsion-free, metric-compatible connection; parallel transport is defined by parallel sections, and holonomy is generated by transports around based loops ([[def-gts-compact-calabiyau-manifold-conventions]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The real-linear isomorphism $L:\mathbb R^{2n}\to\mathbb C^n$ sending the standard basis to $(\lambda_a)$ maps $\mathbb Z^{2n}$ onto $\Lambda$, so $\Lambda$ is discrete; bounded sets meet $\Lambda$ finitely because their inverse images under $L$ are bounded and bounded sets meet $\mathbb Z^{2n}$ finitely. For every compact $K\subset\mathbb C^n$, if $(K+\lambda)\cap K\ne\varnothing$ then $\lambda\in K-K$, a bounded set meeting the lattice finitely; this proves proper discontinuity, and freeness follows because a translation fixing a point has vector $0$. The shortest nonzero lattice length is positive. Distinct orbits have positive distance: for fixed representatives $z,w$, only finitely many $\lambda$ can satisfy $|z-(w+\lambda)|\le R$ for any fixed $R$, so if no $w+\lambda=z$ the minimum distance is positive. Small disjoint balls therefore separate distinct orbits and give Hausdorff quotient charts; choosing their radii below half the shortest lattice length makes their nonidentity translates disjoint. The chart transitions are translations and therefore holomorphic. The closed fundamental parallelepiped $\{\sum_at_a\lambda_a:0\le t_a\le1\}$ is compact and surjects onto $X$, proving compactness; the quotient map is continuous and surjective, proving connectedness. [A1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Translations preserve $g_0$, $J$, and $\omega_0$, so these descend. The descended $\omega_0$ is real, positive, and closed, hence Kähler. Translations also preserve $\Omega_0$; it descends to a nowhere-zero holomorphic section of $K_X$, which trivializes $K_X$ by the preceding convention [F1]. [F1, given, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 On $\mathbb C^n$, define $D_X(\sum_af_aE_a)=\sum_aX(f_a)E_a$ for the constant real coordinate frame $(E_a)=(\partial_{x_1},\partial_{y_1},\ldots,\partial_{x_n},\partial_{y_n})$. Deck translations have identity differential, so this connection descends and the frame remains global and parallel. The fields $E_a$ commute, giving $T^D(E_a,E_b)=0$; torsion is tensorial, so $T^D=0$. Since $g_0(E_a,E_b)=\delta_{ab}$ is constant and $D E_a=0$, the connection is metric-compatible, hence $D$ is Levi-Civita by [F2]. Its curvature annihilates each parallel $E_a$, and since these form a frame, $R^D=0$. Along any piecewise smooth loop, a parallel field has constant coefficients in this global frame, so its endpoint equals its initial value; the holonomy group is trivial. [F2, given, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For $n\ge2$, choose $t\notin2\pi\mathbb Z$. The matrix $\operatorname{diag}(e^{it},e^{-it},1,\ldots,1)$ is unitary, has determinant $1$, and is not the identity; hence $SU(n)$ is nontrivial and cannot equal the trivial holonomy group. For $n=1$, $SU(1)=\{1\}$, as stated. [step 2.2, algebra] ∎
