## Definition

Fix a finite nonempty set of states $E$, equipped with the discrete sigma-algebra $\mathcal P(E)$ consisting of all subsets of $E$, and a finite time horizon $T\ge0$. A finite-state continuous-time Markov jump process on $[0,T]$ is a family of measurable maps $X_t:(\Omega,\mathcal F)\to(E,\mathcal P(E))$ on a probability space $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)$. Thus each $X_t$ is a random element of this measurable state space. Its paths are constant between successive jump times and take the new state at a jump time (right-continuity); path events use the sigma-algebra generated by the coordinate maps $X_t$. The Markov property here means that, conditional on the history through time $s$, the law of the future path depends on that history only through $(s,X_s)$. For each pair of distinct states $x,y\in E$, its transition rate $\kappa_{xy}:[0,T]\to[0,\infty)$ is a bounded continuous function. Write

$$\lambda_x(t):=\sum_{y\in E,\ y\ne x}\kappa_{xy}(t).$$

All time integrals without a displayed measure below are oriented Riemann integrals. When a continuous integrand is used as a Lebesgue density, its Lebesgue and Riemann integrals agree by the bounded Riemann-integral comparison theorem. The first-jump rate convention is the following. Conditional on the process history through time $s$ and state $X_s=x$, the survival probability of no jump before time $t$, $s\le t\le T$, is

$$H_x(s,t):=\exp\left(-\int_s^t\lambda_x(u)\,du\right).$$

For each $y\ne x$, the conditional density that the next jump occurs at time $t$ and goes to $y$ is $H_x(s,t)\kappa_{xy}(t)$ with respect to one-dimensional Lebesgue measure; the remaining probability $H_x(s,T)$ is that no jump occurs by time $T$. These are normalized conditional probabilities: for $s<T$, by the chain rule and the first fundamental theorem of calculus, $H_x(s,s)=1$ and $H'_x(s,t)=-\lambda_x(t)H_x(s,t)$; the density integrands are continuous, so the second fundamental theorem of calculus gives

$$H_x(s,T)+\sum_{y\ne x}\int_s^T H_x(s,t)\kappa_{xy}(t)\,dt =H_x(s,T)+\int_s^T\lambda_x(t)H_x(s,t)\,dt=1.$$

After each jump the same conditional rule restarts from the new state and jump time. For $s=T$, the next-jump law has unit mass on no further jump. The row generator is the matrix $Q(t)$ with $Q_{xy}(t)=\kappa_{xy}(t)$ for $x\ne y$ and $Q_{xx}(t)=-\lambda_x(t)$. A time-homogeneous process is the special case in which every $\kappa_{xy}(t)$ is constant. When rates carry physical time units, $\kappa_{xy}$ has units of inverse time; this definition is mathematical and asserts no physical realization. The process is called nonexplosive on $[0,T]$ when its path has finitely many jumps there almost surely.
