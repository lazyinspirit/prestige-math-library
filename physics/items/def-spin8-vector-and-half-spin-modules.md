## Definition

Let $V=\mathbb R^8$ with its standard oriented orthonormal basis $e_1,\ldots,e_8$, Euclidean inner product $\langle\ ,\ \rangle$, and quadratic form $Q(v)=\langle v,v\rangle$. Use the plus-$Q$ Clifford algebra $\mathrm{Cl}(V,Q)$, generated by $V$ with relations $v^2=Q(v)1$. Write $G=\operatorname{Spin}_{\mathrm{full}}(V,Q)$ for the even subgroup generated by unit vectors in this algebra. Its vector action is $\rho_x(v)=\alpha(x)vx^{-1}$, where $\alpha$ is the algebra grading involution that sends each vector to its negative. Write $\operatorname{Spin}(8)=G$.

Let $W=\mathbb C^4$ with ordered basis $u_1,\ldots,u_4$ and let $\Delta=\Lambda^\bullet W$. Give its ordered wedge basis the Hermitian inner product making it orthonormal. For $1\le j\le4$, let $\epsilon_j$ be exterior multiplication by $u_j$ and let $\iota_j$ delete $u_j$ from an ordered basis wedge, with the sign determined by its position, and be zero when $u_j$ is absent. Define $C_{2j-1}=\epsilon_j+\iota_j$ and $C_{2j}=i(\epsilon_j-\iota_j)$. These operators satisfy the plus-$Q$ Clifford relations for the complexification of $V$; the resulting $\mathrm{Cl}(V,Q)\otimes_\mathbb R\mathbb C$ action on $\Delta$ is the complex spin module. Write $c(v)$ for the Clifford action of $v\in V$ on $\Delta$, obtained by extending $e_i\mapsto C_i$ linearly. Its oriented volume operator is $\omega=C_1C_2\cdots C_8$, and $\Delta^+$ and $\Delta^-$ are its $+1$ and $-1$ eigenspaces.

A real structure on a complex representation is a conjugate-linear involution commuting with the group action; its fixed subspace is a real representation whose complexification is the original complex representation. Let $K$ denote coefficient conjugation on $\Delta$, put $A=C_2C_4C_6C_8$, and define the real structure $J=AK$. Give $V$ its standard real inner product and give each fixed real space $(\Delta^\pm)^J$ the restriction of the real part of the Hermitian inner product. Set $8_v=V$ with the vector action $\rho$, $8_s=(\Delta^+)^J$, and $8_c=(\Delta^-)^J$, each with the Spin(8) action induced by Clifford multiplication.

For this choice of orientation and chirality, $8_s$ and $8_c$ are the positive and negative real half-spin representations. Reversing the chirality convention exchanges their labels. The fixed dimensions, invariance, and irreducibility asserted by this construction are established in the following well-definedness lemma.

## Remarks

These are representations of the compact Euclidean group Spin(8). Their construction does not establish the existence of Lorentzian ten-dimensional Spin(1,9) spinor bundles or identify those bundles with Euclidean half-spin modules.

For comparison of names only, adopt the light-cone convention reported in Weigand, §§6.5–6.7. There $\mathbf{16}$ and $\mathbf{16}'$ denote the positive- and negative-chirality ten-dimensional Majorana–Weyl representations; $\downarrow$ denotes restriction to $\mathrm{Spin}(1,1)\times\mathrm{Spin}(8)$, with the first entry the $\mathrm{Spin}(1,1)$ weight and the second a transverse $\mathrm{Spin}(8)$ module:
$$\mathbf{16}\downarrow=(+\tfrac12,8_s)\oplus(-\tfrac12,8_c),\qquad \mathbf{16}'\downarrow=(+\tfrac12,8_c)\oplus(-\tfrac12,8_s).$$
The source's open-string $R^+$ and $R^-$ denote the $+1$ and $-1$ eigenspaces of its R-sector G-parity operator $G=\Gamma(-1)^F$, where $\Gamma$ is ten-dimensional chirality and $F$ is the source's R-sector fermion-number operator; they transform as $8_s$ and $8_c$, respectively. Its Type IIB closed-string GSO choice pairs equal transverse chiralities and Type IIA pairs opposite chiralities. These are reported light-cone naming conventions, not a derivation of the GSO projection or a Lorentzian spinor-bundle construction. Matching the source's positive transverse label $8_s$ to $(\Delta^+)^J$ is the convention adopted here.
