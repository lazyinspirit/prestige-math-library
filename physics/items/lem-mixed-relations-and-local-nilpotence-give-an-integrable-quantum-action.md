## Statement

Let $V=\bigoplus_{\mu\in P}V_\mu$ be a label-weight-graded $\mathbb Q(q)$-vector
space with toral operators $K_h$ ($h\in P^\vee$) acting on $V_\mu$ by
$q^{\langle\mu,h\rangle}$, and operators $E_i,F_i$ of weights
$\alpha_i,-\alpha_i$ mapping $V_\mu$ to $V_{\mu+\alpha_i}$ and
$V_{\mu-\alpha_i}$. Assume the toral conjugation and mixed relations, and local
nilpotence of each $E_i$ and $F_i$; vectors have finite label support. Then
these operators uniquely define an integrable type-one
$U_q(\mathfrak g)$-module structure on $V$ for EVERY real symmetrizable Cartan
datum. Every vector has a finite rank-one $U_i$ orbit and the admissible
Kashiwara $i$-string decomposition applies. Finite weight spaces do not imply
finite-dimensional full-$\mathfrak g$ orbits; no such conclusion is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A symmetrizable Cartan datum $(I,A,D,P,P^\vee,q)$ with $a_{ii}=2$, over $\mathbb Q(q)$, and a label-graded space $V=\bigoplus_{\mu\in P}V_\mu$ with operators $K_h$ ($h\in P^\vee$), $E_i$, $F_i$ satisfying:

[A1] (toral and toral conjugation) $K_0=1$, $K_hK_{h'}=K_{h+h'}$, $K_h$ acts on $V_\mu$ by $q^{\langle\mu,h\rangle}$, $K_hE_iK_h^{-1}=q^{\langle\alpha_i,h\rangle}E_i$ and $K_hF_iK_h^{-1}=q^{-\langle\alpha_i,h\rangle}F_i$; write $K_i:=K_{d_ih_i}$, so $K_i$ acts on $V_\mu$ by $q_i^{\langle\mu,h_i\rangle}$ with $q_i=q^{d_i}$.

[A2] (mixed relations) $E_iF_j-F_jE_i=\delta_{ij}(K_i-K_i^{-1})/(q_i-q_i^{-1})$ for all $i,j$; in particular $E_iF_j=F_jE_i$ for $i\ne j$.

[A3] (local nilpotence) for every $v\in V$ and every $i$ there are $N_e(v,i),N_f(v,i)$ with $E_i^{N_e(v,i)}v=F_i^{N_f(v,i)}v=0$; every vector is a finite sum of label components.

[F1] In $U_q(\mathfrak g)$ the displayed toral, toral-action, mixed and Serre relations hold, and a linear map on the generators satisfying all of them extends uniquely to a $U_q(\mathfrak g)$-module structure ([[def-drinfeld-jimbo-quantized-enveloping-algebra]], [[def-symmetrizable-cartan-datum-for-a-quantum-group]], [[thm-quotient-ring-universal-property]]).

[F2] $[m]_i=(q_i^m-q_i^{-m})/(q_i-q_i^{-1})$ and $[m]_i!\ne0$ for $m\ge0$, and $[m]_i=0$ exactly when $m=0$, because $q_i$ is transcendental over $\mathbb Q$ ([[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]).

[F3] In the rank-one algebra generated by $E_i,F_i,K_i^{\pm1}$, with
$F_i^{(b)}=F_i^b/[b]_i!$, one has
$$E_iF_i^{(b)}=F_i^{(b)}E_i+F_i^{(b-1)}\Theta_{i,b},\qquad \Theta_{i,b}=\frac{1}{q_i^b-q_i^{-b}}\sum_{s=0}^{b-1}\bigl(q_i^{-2s}K_i-q_i^{2s}K_i^{-1}\bigr)$$
for $b\ge1$, and $K_iE_iK_i^{-1}=q_i^2E_i$, $K_iF_iK_i^{-1}=q_i^{-2}F_i$
([[thm-quantized-sl-two-string-formulas]], parts (i) and (ii)).

[F4] The Gaussian quotients satisfy the Pascal recurrence $C_{m,r}=q_i^rC_{m-1,r}+q_i^{r-m}C_{m-1,r-1}$ with $C_{m,r}=0$ outside $0\le r\le m$ ([[lem-quantum-pascal-recurrence-and-gaussian-integrality]]).

[F5] Once $V$ is a chosen $P$-graded type-one quantum weight module on which $E_i$ and $F_i$ are locally nilpotent, the $i$-string decomposition theorem applies to $V$ ([[thm-unique-kashiwara-i-string-decomposition]], [[def-quantum-weight-module-and-integrability]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** finite rank-one reductions: enclose each vector in a finite-dimensional rank-one module, use the rank-one adjoint action on a Hom space to turn $F_j$ into a highest vector, bound its string by the divided-power commutation formula, and read the Serre sum off the surviving coefficient.

1.1 Finite rank-one orbits. Fix $i$ and $v\in V$. Then $W:=\operatorname{span}\{F_i^aE_i^bK_i^cv:a,b\ge0,\ c\in\mathbb Z\}$ is a finite-dimensional $A_i$-stable subspace, where $A_i$ is the algebra generated by $E_i,F_i,K_i^{\pm1}$: the toral operator $K_i$ acts diagonally on the finitely many nonzero label components of $v$, so the vectors $K_i^cv$ span a finite-dimensional subspace; by [A3] there is $B$ with $E_i^bv=0$ for $b\ge B$; the mixed relation of [A2] rewrites any word in $E_i,F_i$ as a linear combination of words $F_i^aE_i^b$ with coefficients in the algebra generated by $K_i$; and one may choose finitely many $K_i^cv$ spanning the toral orbit and use local nilpotence of $F_i$ on their finitely many $E_i^b$-images to bound $a$. The span of the resulting finite set is stable under $E_i,F_i,K_i^{\pm1}$. [A1, A2, A3]

2.1 The second finite module. Fix $i\ne j$ and set $W':=\operatorname{span}\{F_i^aE_i^bK_i^cF_jw:w\in W,\ a,b\ge0,\ c\in\mathbb Z\}$. Since $W$ is finite-dimensional, step 1.1 applied to each of the finitely many vectors $F_jw$ exhibits $W'$ as a finite union of finite-dimensional $A_i$-stable subspaces; hence $W'$ is a finite-dimensional $A_i$-stable subspace of $V$ containing $F_jW$. [step 1.1, A1, A2, A3]

3.1 The rank-one adjoint module. On the finite-dimensional space $\operatorname{Hom}(W,W')$ define operators by $\mathrm{Ad}(K_i)(T)=K_iTK_i^{-1}$, $\mathrm{Ad}(E_i)(T)=(E_iT-TE_i)K_i$ and $\mathrm{Ad}(F_i)(T)=F_iT-K_iTK_i^{-1}F_i$. Substituting these formulas, using $K_iE_i=q_i^2E_iK_i$, $K_iF_i=q_i^{-2}F_iK_i$ and $E_iF_i-F_iE_i=(K_i-K_i^{-1})/(q_i-q_i^{-1})$ on $W,W^{\prime}$, gives $\mathrm{Ad}(K_i)\mathrm{Ad}(E_i)\mathrm{Ad}(K_i)^{-1}=q_i^2\mathrm{Ad}(E_i)$, $\mathrm{Ad}(K_i)\mathrm{Ad}(F_i)\mathrm{Ad}(K_i)^{-1}=q_i^{-2}\mathrm{Ad}(F_i)$ and $[\mathrm{Ad}(E_i),\mathrm{Ad}(F_i)]=(\mathrm{Ad}(K_i)-\mathrm{Ad}(K_i)^{-1})/(q_i-q_i^{-1})$. Thus these formulas give a rank-one module directly. Moreover $\mathrm{Ad}(K_i)$ is diagonalizable because $K_i$ acts diagonally on $W$ and $W'$, and $\mathrm{Ad}(E_i),\mathrm{Ad}(F_i)$ are locally nilpotent: $\mathrm{Ad}(E_i)$ raises and $\mathrm{Ad}(F_i)$ lowers the $\mathrm{Ad}(K_i)$-weight by $2$, the weights of $\operatorname{Hom}(W,W')$ form a finite set, and decomposing any $T$ into weight components each orbit is supported on distinct weights and hence terminates. [A1, A2, step 2.1]

4.1 $F_j$ is a highest vector of weight $m$. Since $F_jW\subseteq W'$, the restriction $T:=F_j|_W$ lies in $\operatorname{Hom}(W,W')$. By [A2], $E_iF_j=F_jE_i$ for $i\ne j$, so $\mathrm{Ad}(E_i)(T)=(E_iT-TE_i)K_i=0$; and by [A1], $\mathrm{Ad}(K_i)(T)=K_iF_jK_i^{-1}=q_i^{-a_{ij}}T$, because $\langle\alpha_j,d_ih_i\rangle=d_ia_{ij}$. Put $m:=-a_{ij}\ge0$, the off-diagonal Cartan entries being nonpositive. Thus $T$ is annihilated by $\mathrm{Ad}(E_i)$ and has $\mathrm{Ad}(K_i)$-weight $q_i^m$ with $m\ge0$. [A1, A2, step 3.1]

5.1 The rank-one string bound. Assume $T\ne0$. In the rank-one module $\operatorname{Hom}(W,W')$ of steps 3.1-4.1 let $t\ge1$ be minimal with $\mathrm{Ad}(F_i)^tT=0$; it exists by the local nilpotence of step 3.1. For $b\ge1$ the divided-power formula of [F3] gives $\mathrm{Ad}(E_i)\mathrm{Ad}(F_i)^{(b)}(T)=\mathrm{Ad}(F_i)^{(b-1)}\Theta_{i,b}(T)$, and $\Theta_{i,b}$ acts on $T$ by the scalar $\frac{1}{q_i^b-q_i^{-b}}\sum_{s=0}^{b-1}(q_i^{m-2s}-q_i^{-m+2s})=\frac{q_i^{m-b+1}-q_i^{-m+b-1}}{q_i-q_i^{-1}}=[m-b+1]_i$, by summing the two geometric progressions. Taking $b=t$ and using $\mathrm{Ad}(F_i)^tT=0$ together with $\mathrm{Ad}(F_i)^{(t-1)}T=[t-1]_i!^{-1}\mathrm{Ad}(F_i)^{t-1}T\ne0$ gives $[m-t+1]_i=0$, hence $m-t+1=0$ by [F2], that is $t=m+1$; therefore $\mathrm{Ad}(F_i)^{m+1}T=0$. [F2, F3, step 4.1]

6.1 The Serre sum. By induction on $n\ge0$, using $\mathrm{Ad}(K_i)(T)=q_i^mT$ and the Pascal recurrence of [F4], the powers of the adjoint action expand as $\mathrm{Ad}(F_i)^n(T)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}(-1)^rq_i^{r(m-n+1)}\binom{n}{r}_iF_i^{n-r}TF_i^r$: the case $n=0$ is clear, and applying $\mathrm{Ad}(F_i)$ to the formula for $n$, conjugating $K_iF_i^{n-r}TF_i^rK_i^{-1}=q_i^{m-2n}F_i^{n-r}TF_i^r$ and comparing the coefficient of $F_i^{n+1-r}TF_i^r$ uses exactly $C_{n+1,r}=q_i^rC_{n,r}+q_i^{r-n-1}C_{n,r-1}$, the second form of [F4]. At $n=m+1=1-a_{ij}$ every Laurent exponent $r(m-n+1)$ is zero, so step 5.1 gives $\sum_{r=0}^{1-a_{ij}}(-1)^r\binom{1-a_{ij}}{r}_iF_i^{1-a_{ij}-r}F_jF_i^r=0$ on $W$. Since every $v\in V$ lies in such a $W$ by step 1.1, the negative Serre relation holds as an operator on all of $V$. [F4, step 1.1, step 5.1]

7.1 The positive Serre relation. Put $E_i':=F_i$, $F_i':=E_i$ and $K_h':=K_{-h}$ for all $i$ and $h$. The primed data again satisfies the toral, toral-action, mixed and local-nilpotence hypotheses [A1]-[A3]: for instance $K_h'E_i'K_h'^{-1}=K_{-h}F_iK_h=q^{\langle\alpha_i,h\rangle}F_i=q^{\langle\alpha_i,h\rangle}E_i'$, while $E_i'F_i'-F_i'E_i'=F_iE_i-E_iF_i=-(K_i-K_i^{-1})/(q_i-q_i^{-1})=(K_i'-K_i'^{-1})/(q_i-q_i^{-1})$. Applying steps 1.1-6.1 to the primed data gives $\sum_{r}(-1)^r\binom{1-a_{ij}}{r}_iE_i^{1-a_{ij}-r}E_jE_i^r=0$ on $V$, which is the positive Serre relation for the original data. [A1, A2, A3, step 6.1]

8.1 Conclusion. By [A1]-[A3] the operators satisfy all the defining relations of $U_q(\mathfrak g)$: the toral and toral-action relations, the mixed commutators, and both Serre families by steps 6.1 and 7.1. Hence the universal property [F1] equips $V$ with a $U_q(\mathfrak g)$-module structure; it is unique because $U_q(\mathfrak g)$ is generated by these operators, and it is a chosen $P$-graded type-one weight module by construction. Local nilpotence is exactly [A3], so the module is integrable, and by [F5] the admissible $i$-string decomposition applies to every vector; step 1.1 supplies the finite rank-one $U_i$ orbit. No finite-dimensionality of the full weight spaces is asserted or used. [A3, F1, F5, step 1.1, step 6.1, step 7.1] ∎
