## Statement

Let $(A,V,\eta,C^+)$ be an oriented time-oriented affine Minkowski space ([[def-sr-affine-minkowski-model]]), let $U\subseteq A$ be a nonempty open subset which, with the topology of [[lem-affine-minkowski-charts-form-a-smooth-atlas]], is path-connected and simply connected ([[def-simply-connected]]), and let $\alpha$ be a $C^1$ one-form on $U$ with $d\alpha=0$ ([[def-closed-and-exact-differential-forms]]); here closedness of a $C^1$ one-form means that in an inertial chart ([[def-inertial-frame-and-inertial-chart]]) one has $\alpha=\sum_{i=0}^3 a_i\,dx^i$ with $\partial_i a_j=\partial_j a_i$, the exterior derivative being understood by this coefficient formula ([[def-exterior-derivative-by-the-invariant-vector-field-formula]], [[thm-local-coordinate-formula-for-the-exterior-derivative]]). Then:

(a) there is a $C^2$ function $F:U\to\mathbb R$ with $dF=\alpha$;

(b) for any two piecewise-$C^1$ paths $\gamma_0,\gamma_1$ in $U$ ([[def-piecewise-c1-path-connected-conservative-and-path-independent]]) with the same endpoints, the integrals of $\alpha$ along them are equal, and consequently the integral of $\alpha$ along every piecewise-$C^1$ loop in $U$ vanishes.

If $\alpha$ is smooth, $F$ may be taken smooth.

**Conventions (what the objects mean here).** Fix one inertial chart $x:A\to\mathbb R^4$ and put $\Omega:=x(U)\subseteq\mathbb R^4$; by [[lem-affine-minkowski-charts-form-a-smooth-atlas]] the chart $x$ is a diffeomorphism of $A$ onto $\mathbb R^4$ carrying the model's topology, and every other inertial chart $x'$ satisfies $x'=M^{-1}(x-b)$ for an invertible matrix $M$ and a vector $b$. The $C^1$ one-form $\alpha$ is represented in the chart as $\alpha=\sum_{i=0}^3 a_i\,dx^i$ with coefficient functions $a_i:\Omega\to\mathbb R$ of class $C^1$; write $A:=(a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3):\Omega\to\mathbb R^4$ for the coefficient field, so that $A$ is $C^1$ and closedness reads $\partial_i a_j=\partial_j a_i$ on $\Omega$. For a piecewise-$C^1$ path $\gamma$ in $U$ the integral is $\int_\gamma\alpha:=\int_{x\circ\gamma}A\cdot d\mathbf r$, the vector-field line integral of [[def-scalar-and-vector-line-integrals-along-piecewise-c1-paths]]. For a $C^1$ function $F:U\to\mathbb R$ with $\tilde F:=F\circ x^{-1}$, the differential $dF$ is the one-form whose chart coefficients are the partial derivatives $\partial_i\tilde F$, and $dF=\alpha$ means $\partial_i\tilde F=a_i$ on $\Omega$ for all $i$. Under the transition $x'=M^{-1}(x-b)$ the chain rule gives $a'_j=\sum_i M^i_j a_i$, $\partial_j\tilde F'=\sum_i M^i_j\,\partial_i\tilde F$ and $(x'\circ\gamma)'=M^{-1}(x\circ\gamma)'$, so the integral $\int_\gamma\alpha$ and the identity $dF=\alpha$ do not depend on which inertial chart is used; likewise, $F$ being $C^2$, respectively smooth, is chart-independent. It therefore suffices to prove the following statement about the open set $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^4$ and the $C^1$ closed field $A$: every closed piecewise-$C^1$ path in $\Omega$ has zero $A$-integral, the $A$-integral is path-independent on $\Omega$, and there is a $C^2$ function $\tilde F$ on $\Omega$ with $\nabla\tilde F=A$ which can be taken smooth when $A$ is smooth.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An oriented time-oriented affine Minkowski space $(A,V,\eta,C^+)$ ([[def-sr-affine-minkowski-model]]), a nonempty open subset $U\subseteq A$ that is path-connected and simply connected ([[def-simply-connected]]) in the topology of [[lem-affine-minkowski-charts-form-a-smooth-atlas]], and a $C^1$ one-form $\alpha$ on $U$ with $d\alpha=0$ in the coefficient sense of the Statement ([[def-closed-and-exact-differential-forms]], [[def-exterior-derivative-by-the-invariant-vector-field-formula]]).

[F1] An inertial chart $x:A\to\mathbb R^4$ is a diffeomorphism of $A$ onto $\mathbb R^4$ for the model's topology (which is independent of the chart), the transitions between inertial charts are the affine bijections $x'=M^{-1}(x-b)$, and every inertial chart belongs to the same smooth structure ([[def-inertial-frame-and-inertial-chart]], [[lem-affine-minkowski-charts-form-a-smooth-atlas]], [[def-sr-affine-minkowski-model]]).

[F2] $U$ is nonempty and path-connected, and for every $u\in U$ the fundamental group $\pi_1(U,u)$ has exactly one element; equivalently, every based loop at $u$ is path-homotopic relative to the endpoints to the constant loop at $u$ ([[def-simply-connected]], [[def-based-loops-and-fundamental-group]], [[def-homotopy-relative-and-path-homotopy]], [[def-nullhomotopic-map-and-contractible-space]]).

[F3] A path homotopy relative to the endpoints from a loop $\gamma$ at $p$ to the constant loop is a continuous $H:I\times I\to U$ with $H(s,0)=\gamma(s)$, $H(s,1)=p$ and $H(0,t)=H(1,t)=p$, where $I=[0,1]$ ([[def-homotopy-relative-and-path-homotopy]]).

[F4] For a piecewise-$C^1$ path $\gamma:[a,b]\to\mathbb R^n$ and a continuous field $F$ on a set containing its trace, $\int_\gamma F\cdot d\mathbf r$ is the sum of the integrals of $\langle F(\gamma(t)),v_i(t)\rangle$ over an admissible partition with continuous derivative extensions $v_i$, the value being independent of the admissible partition ([[def-scalar-and-vector-line-integrals-along-piecewise-c1-paths]], [[lem-line-integrals-are-independent-of-the-piecewise-c1-partition]], [[def-piecewise-c1-path-operations-and-oriented-reparametrizations]]).

[F5] Reversal, concatenation and reparametrization: $\int_{\gamma^-}F\cdot d\mathbf r=-\int_\gamma F\cdot d\mathbf r$; if $\alpha,\beta:[0,1]\to\mathbb R^n$ are piecewise-$C^1$ with $\alpha(1)=\beta(0)$ then $\int_{\alpha*\beta}F\cdot d\mathbf r=\int_\alpha F\cdot d\mathbf r+\int_\beta F\cdot d\mathbf r$; and an oriented piecewise-$C^1$ reparametrization with increasing derivative leaves the integral unchanged ([[thm-line-integrals-under-reversal-and-concatenation]], [[thm-line-integrals-under-oriented-reparametrization]], [[def-piecewise-c1-path-operations-and-oriented-reparametrizations]]).

[F6] On an open star-shaped $W\subseteq\mathbb R^n$, a $C^1$ field $F:W\to\mathbb R^n$ is closed if and only if it is exact, if and only if it is conservative, if and only if it is path-independent, if and only if the integral along every closed piecewise-$C^1$ path in $W$ vanishes; a conservative field is path-independent and has vanishing closed-path integrals; and for $C^1$ $\varphi:W\to\mathbb R$ and piecewise-$C^1$ $\gamma$ in $W$ one has $\int_\gamma\nabla\varphi\cdot d\mathbf r=\varphi(\gamma(\text{end}))-\varphi(\gamma(\text{start}))$ ([[cor-closed-exact-and-conservative-equivalence-on-star-shaped-domains]], [[cor-conservative-fields-are-path-independent-and-have-zero-circulation]], [[thm-gradient-theorem-for-line-integrals]]).

[F7] Euclidean structure and compactness: $|z|_2$ and the distance $d_2$ on $\mathbb R^n$ satisfy the triangle inequality and $|tz|_2=|t|\,|z|_2$, so every ball $B(z,r)=\{y:|y-z|_2<r\}$ is convex; a continuous image of a compact space is compact ([[thm-continuous-image-of-a-compact-space-is-compact]]); a closed box $\{y:a_k\le y_k\le b_k\}$ in $\mathbb R^n$ is compact, and a subset of $\mathbb R^n$ is compact if and only if it is closed and bounded; a continuous map on a compact metric space is uniformly continuous ([[def-p-norms-on-rn]], [[lem-p-norms-are-norms-and-induce-the-published-metrics]], [[def-euclidean-inner-product]], [[def-metric-compactness]], [[lem-compactness-is-intrinsic]], [[thm-heine-borel-rn]], [[thm-heine-cantor-metric]]).

[F8] For every real $\varepsilon>0$ there is a natural number $m\ge1$ with $1/m<\varepsilon$ ([[cor-archimedean-reciprocal]]).

[F9] Calculus: for totally differentiable maps $f:W\to Z\subseteq\mathbb R^n$, $g:Z\to\mathbb R^p$ on open Euclidean domains, $D(g\circ f)(u)=Dg(f(u))\circ Df(u)$; a function whose first partial derivatives exist and are continuous is totally differentiable, so a function with continuous partial derivatives of order up to $k$ is $C^k$ in the sense of [[def-ck-and-multi-index-notation-in-several-variables]]; and the first fundamental theorem of calculus for continuous integrands ([[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]], [[thm-continuous-partial-derivatives-imply-total-differentiability]], [[def-ck-and-multi-index-notation-in-several-variables]], [[thm-ftc-first-part]], [[def-derivative]], [[def-continuity-real]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Fix an inertial chart $x$ and put $\Omega:=x(U)$; by [F1] the chart is a diffeomorphism onto $\mathbb R^4$, so $\Omega$ is a nonempty open subset of $\mathbb R^4$, and because $x|_U:U\to\Omega$ is a homeomorphism it induces a bijection between the continuous loops at $p$ in $U$ and those at $x(p)$ in $\Omega$ and a bijection between homotopies relative to the endpoints of such loops (via composition with $x$ and $x^{-1}$); hence $\Omega$ is path-connected and $\pi_1(\Omega,u)$ has exactly one element for every $u\in\Omega$ by [F2]. Write $\alpha=\sum_{i=0}^3 a_i\,dx^i$ in the chart with $C^1$ coefficients $a_i$ and $\partial_i a_j=\partial_j a_i$, set $A:=(a_0,\dots,a_3)$, and recall from the conventions of the Statement that $\int_\gamma\alpha=\int_{x\circ\gamma}A\cdot d\mathbf r$ for piecewise-$C^1$ $\gamma$, that $dF=\alpha$ means $\partial_i\tilde F=a_i$ for $\tilde F=F\circ x^{-1}$, and that these interpretations are invariant under a change of inertial chart, by the transition formula $x'=M^{-1}(x-b)$ of [F1] together with the chain rule of [F9] applied to $a'_j=\sum_i M^i_j a_i$, $\partial_j\tilde F'=\sum_i M^i_j\partial_i\tilde F$ and $(x'\circ\gamma)'=M^{-1}(x\circ\gamma)'$; consequently the Statement follows once it is proved for the open set $\Omega$ and the $C^1$ closed field $A$ on it that (i) every closed piecewise-$C^1$ path in $\Omega$ has zero $A$-integral, (ii) the $A$-integral is path-independent on $\Omega$, and (iii) there is a $C^2$ function on $\Omega$ with gradient $A$, smooth when $A$ is smooth. [F1, F2, F4, F9, given]

2.1 Local primitives on balls: let $B=B(z,\varrho)\subseteq\Omega$ be an open Euclidean ball; it is convex by [F7], hence star-shaped, and $A|_B$ is a $C^1$ closed field on $B$, so by [F6] there is a $C^1$ function $\varphi:B\to\mathbb R$ with $\nabla\varphi=A|_B$, and because $\partial_j\varphi=a_j$ is $C^1$ for every $j$ the function $\varphi$ is $C^2$ by [F9]. By the gradient theorem of [F6], every piecewise-$C^1$ path $\eta$ in $B$ satisfies $\int_\eta A\cdot d\mathbf r=\varphi(\eta(\text{end}))-\varphi(\eta(\text{start}))$; in particular the integral of $A$ along every closed piecewise-$C^1$ path in $B$ vanishes, and the integral along a piecewise-$C^1$ arc in $B$ depends only on the endpoints of the arc. [F6, F7, F9, step 1.1]

2.2 Polygonal paths in $\Omega$: let $p,q\in\Omega$; since $\Omega$ is path-connected there is a continuous $c:[0,1]\to\Omega$ with $c(0)=p$, $c(1)=q$, and $K':=c([0,1])$ is compact by [F7]. Cover $K'$ by the family of all balls $B(z,r)$ with $B(z,2r)\subseteq\Omega$, which covers $K'$ because $\Omega$ is open; compactness of $K'$ yields finitely many balls $B(z_l,r_l)$, $l<m$, with $K'\subseteq\bigcup_l B(z_l,r_l)$ and $B(z_l,2r_l)\subseteq\Omega$, and $\varrho:=\min_l r_l>0$ because $K'$ is nonempty. Uniform continuity of $c$ on the compact interval $[0,1]$ by [F7] at $\varepsilon=\varrho/2$ gives $\delta>0$ such that $|s-s'|<\delta$ implies $|c(s)-c(s')|_2<\varrho/2$. Choose $m\ge1$ with $1/m<\delta$ by [F8] and set $s_j:=j/m$. For each $j<m$ the point $c(s_j)$ lies in some $B(z_l,r_l)$, and $|c(s_{j+1})-z_l|_2\le|c(s_{j+1})-c(s_j)|_2+|c(s_j)-z_l|_2<\varrho/2+r_l\le\tfrac32r_l<2r_l$, so both $c(s_j)$ and $c(s_{j+1})$ lie in the convex ball $B(z_l,2r_l)\subseteq\Omega$ and the segment joining them is contained in $\Omega$; concatenating these finitely many segments gives a piecewise-$C^1$ path in $\Omega$ from $p$ to $q$. [F7, F8, step 1.1]

3.1 Mesh for a closed loop: let $\gamma:[0,1]\to\Omega$ be a closed piecewise-$C^1$ path with $p:=\gamma(0)=\gamma(1)$. Regarded as a based loop at $p$, it is path-homotopic relative to the endpoints to the constant loop at $p$ ([[def-simply-connected]] and [F2]), so there is a continuous $H:[0,1]^2\to\Omega$ with $H(s,0)=\gamma(s)$, $H(s,1)=p$ and $H(0,t)=H(1,t)=p$ by [F3]. The image $K:=H([0,1]^2)$ is compact by [F7], and as in step 2.2 compactness of $K$ together with openness of $\Omega$ yields finitely many balls $B(z_l,r_l)$ with $K\subseteq\bigcup_lB(z_l,r_l)$, $B(z_l,2r_l)\subseteq\Omega$ and $\varrho:=\min_lr_l>0$. Uniform continuity of $H$ on the compact square at $\varepsilon=\varrho/2$ gives $\delta>0$ such that $|u-u'|_2<\delta$ implies $|H(u)-H(u')|_2<\varrho/2$; choose $m\ge1$ with $2/m<\delta$, possible by [F8], and consider the grid cells $C_{j,k}:=[j/m,(j+1)/m]\times[k/m,(k+1)/m]$, $j,k<m$. Any two points $u,u'$ of a cell differ in each coordinate by at most $1/m$, so $|u-u'|_2\le2/m<\delta$ and therefore $|H(u)-H(u')|_2<\varrho/2$; writing $u_{j,k}:=(j/m,k/m)$ for the lower-left corner of $C_{j,k}$ we have $H(u_{j,k})\in K\subseteq\bigcup_lB(z_l,r_l)$, so $H(u_{j,k})\in B(z_{l},r_l)$ for some $l$. [F3, F7, F8, step 1.1, step 2.2]

4.1 Cell containment, cell loops and cancellation: for a cell $C_{j,k}$ with $H(u_{j,k})\in B(z_l,r_l)$ as in step 3.1, every $z\in C_{j,k}$ satisfies $|H(z)-z_l|_2\le|H(z)-H(u_{j,k})|_2+|H(u_{j,k})-z_l|_2<\varrho/2+r_l\le\tfrac32r_l<2r_l$, so $H(C_{j,k})\subseteq B(z_l,2r_l)$; the four corner images of $C_{j,k}$ therefore lie in this ball, and the piecewise-linear closed loop $\lambda_{j,k}$ running through them in the order lower-left, lower-right, upper-right, upper-left has its trace in the convex ball $B(z_l,2r_l)$, so $\int_{\lambda_{j,k}}A\cdot d\mathbf r=0$ by step 2.1. Summing over all $m^2$ cells via the additivity and reversal formulas of [F5]: every interior edge of the grid is traversed by the two cells sharing it in opposite directions, so those contributions cancel, while the right, top and left boundary edges are constant segments at the point $p$, because $H(0,t)=H(1,t)=H(s,1)=p$ by step 3.1, and constant paths have zero integral; hence $0=\sum_{j,k<m}\int_{\lambda_{j,k}}A\cdot d\mathbf r=\sum_{j<m}\int_{\sigma_j}A\cdot d\mathbf r$, where $\sigma_j$ is the straight segment from $H(u_{j,0})=\gamma(j/m)$ to $H(u_{j+1,0})=\gamma((j+1)/m)$. [F5, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 Comparison of the loop with its chords: the restricted arcs $\gamma_j:=\gamma|_{[j/m,(j+1)/m]}$ are piecewise-$C^1$ and satisfy $\int_\gamma A\cdot d\mathbf r=\sum_{j<m}\int_{\gamma_j}A\cdot d\mathbf r$ by [F4]; the trace of $\gamma_j$ is the bottom edge of the cell $C_{j,0}$ under $H$, hence is contained in $B(z_{l(j)},2r_{l(j)})$ for the ball assigned to that cell in step 4.1, and the chord $\sigma_j$ joins the same two endpoints $\gamma(j/m)$ and $\gamma((j+1)/m)$ inside the same convex ball. By step 2.1 the integral of $A$ over a piecewise-$C^1$ arc in that ball depends only on its endpoints, so $\int_{\gamma_j}A\cdot d\mathbf r=\int_{\sigma_j}A\cdot d\mathbf r$ for every $j$, and step 4.1 gives $\int_\gamma A\cdot d\mathbf r=\sum_{j<m}\int_{\gamma_j}A\cdot d\mathbf r=\sum_{j<m}\int_{\sigma_j}A\cdot d\mathbf r=0$. [F4, step 2.1, step 4.1]

6.1 Loop vanishing on $\Omega$: a path on a singleton interval has integral zero by definition; otherwise let $\gamma:[a,b]\to\Omega$ be any closed piecewise-$C^1$ path. The affine reparametrization $h(t):=a+(b-a)t$ of $[0,1]$ onto $[a,b]$ is an oriented piecewise-$C^1$ reparametrization with increasing derivative, so by [F5] it suffices to prove $\int_{\gamma\circ h}A\cdot d\mathbf r=0$, and $\gamma\circ h$ is a closed piecewise-$C^1$ path on $[0,1]$; step 5.1 (applied to $\gamma\circ h$, which by [F2] is path-homotopic relative to the endpoints to the constant loop at its basepoint, the existence of the homotopy $H$ being the sole hypothesis used there beyond steps 3.1 and 4.1) therefore gives $\int_\gamma A\cdot d\mathbf r=0$. Hence the integral of $A$ vanishes along every closed piecewise-$C^1$ path in $\Omega$. [F2, F5, step 5.1]

7.1 Path independence on $\Omega$: let $\gamma_0,\gamma_1:[0,1]\to\Omega$ be piecewise-$C^1$ with the same initial point and the same terminal point. Then the concatenation $\gamma_0*\gamma_1^-$ is a closed piecewise-$C^1$ path in $\Omega$, and by [F5] $\int_{\gamma_0*\gamma_1^-}A\cdot d\mathbf r=\int_{\gamma_0}A\cdot d\mathbf r-\int_{\gamma_1}A\cdot d\mathbf r$; step 6.1 makes the left-hand side zero, so $\int_{\gamma_0}A\cdot d\mathbf r=\int_{\gamma_1}A\cdot d\mathbf r$. For paths on general intervals $[a,b]$ and $[c,d]$ the same conclusion follows after composing with the increasing affine reparametrizations onto $[0,1]$, which leave the integrals unchanged by [F5]; hence the $A$-integral is path-independent on $\Omega$. [F5, step 6.1]

8.1 Definition of the primitive: fix a basepoint $p_0\in\Omega$, which exists because $\Omega$ is nonempty, and let $y\in\Omega$. By step 2.2 there exists at least one piecewise-$C^1$ path in $\Omega$ from $p_0$ to $y$, and by step 7.1 the value of $\int A\cdot d\mathbf r$ is the same for all such paths; so the relation consisting of the pairs $\bigl(y,\int_\eta A\cdot d\mathbf r\bigr)$ for piecewise-$C^1$ paths $\eta$ from $p_0$ to $y$ is a single-valued relation on $\Omega$, and it defines a function $\tilde F:\Omega\to\mathbb R$ by $\tilde F(y):=\int_{p_0}^yA\cdot d\mathbf r$, the common value of that integral. No selection of paths is needed for this definition: at each $y$ there is a path and the value is unique, so $\tilde F$ is a well-defined function without any choice principle. [step 2.2, step 7.1]

9.1 The primitive is $C^2$ with $\nabla\tilde F=A$: let $z\in\Omega$ and let $B=B(z,\varrho)\subseteq\Omega$ be an open ball, which exists because $\Omega$ is open; for $y\in B$ the straight segment $\eta$ from $z$ to $y$ lies in the convex ball $B$. Fix a piecewise-$C^1$ path $\gamma_z$ in $\Omega$ from $p_0$ to $z$; then $\gamma_z*\eta$ is a piecewise-$C^1$ path from $p_0$ to $y$, so by the definition of $\tilde F$ in step 8.1 and the additivity formula of [F5] we get $\tilde F(y)=\int_{\gamma_z*\eta}A\cdot d\mathbf r=\tilde F(z)+\int_\eta A\cdot d\mathbf r$, and step 2.1 on the ball $B$ gives $\int_\eta A\cdot d\mathbf r=\varphi_B(y)-\varphi_B(z)$ for the $C^2$ local primitive $\varphi_B$ of step 2.1. Hence $\tilde F(y)=\varphi_B(y)+c_B$ on $B$ with the constant $c_B:=\tilde F(z)-\varphi_B(z)$; consequently $\tilde F$ is $C^2$ on $B$ and $\partial_i\tilde F=\partial_i\varphi_B=a_i$ on $B$ for every $i$. Since $z\in\Omega$ was arbitrary, $\tilde F$ has continuous first and second partial derivatives on all of $\Omega$ and $\nabla\tilde F=A$ there. [F5, step 2.1, step 8.1]

10.1 Assembling the conclusions: if the coefficients $a_i$, hence the field $A$, are smooth, then $\partial_i\tilde F=a_i$ is smooth for every $i$ by step 9.1, so all higher partial derivatives of $\tilde F$ exist and are continuous and $\tilde F$ is smooth ([[def-ck-and-multi-index-notation-in-several-variables]]). Now transport back to $U$: define $F:=\tilde F\circ x$; since $x$ is a diffeomorphism by [F1] and $\tilde F$ is $C^2$ (smooth when $A$ is smooth), also $F$ is $C^2$ (respectively smooth), and in the chart $x$ the coefficients of $dF$ are the partial derivatives $\partial_i\tilde F=a_i$, so that $dF=\alpha$ on $U$ by the conventions of the Statement. Moreover, if $\gamma_0,\gamma_1$ are piecewise-$C^1$ paths in $U$ with the same endpoints, then $x\circ\gamma_0$ and $x\circ\gamma_1$ are piecewise-$C^1$ paths in $\Omega$ with the same endpoints and, by the definition of the integral in the conventions, $\int_{\gamma_0}\alpha=\int_{x\circ\gamma_0}A\cdot d\mathbf r=\int_{x\circ\gamma_1}A\cdot d\mathbf r=\int_{\gamma_1}\alpha$ by step 7.1; and if $\gamma$ is a piecewise-$C^1$ loop in $U$ then $x\circ\gamma$ is a closed piecewise-$C^1$ path in $\Omega$ and $\int_\gamma\alpha=\int_{x\circ\gamma}A\cdot d\mathbf r=0$ by step 6.1. This proves (a) and (b), and the extra smoothness assertion when $\alpha$ is smooth. [step 1.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, step 9.1] ∎

## Remarks

- **What is used and what is not.** The classical proof of the converse direction — a closed 1-form on a simply connected open set is exact — is reduced here to three published inputs on the coefficient side: the radial (star-shaped) local primitive, the homotopy-invariant mesh argument of steps 3.1–5.1, and the telescoping property on balls of step 2.1. The mesh argument uses only Heine–Borel, Heine–Cantor uniform continuity and the Archimedean reciprocal; in particular it does not invoke de Rham theory, smooth approximation, or the Poincaré lemma for more general domains, and it uses no choice principle: the finitely many ball and grid choices are finite, the homotopy is a single object, and the primitive is defined by uniqueness of the path integral rather than by a selection of paths.

- **The global C1-to-C2 statement.** The retrieved sources on the line-integral side supply the smooth global result and the local star-shaped C1-to-C2 result; the global C1 conclusion above is obtained by the finite grid argument of steps 3.1–5.1, which is written out here and is not claimed to be a verbatim quotation of either source. This is the exact source adaptation recorded for the scaffolding of this item, and it is the honest division of labour between the cited texts and the local argument.
