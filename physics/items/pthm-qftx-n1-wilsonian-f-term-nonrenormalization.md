## Statement

Let a local rigid four-dimensional N=1 superspace action datum use the canonical matter kinetic term $\sum_i\Phi_i^\dagger e^{-V_i}\Phi_i$, an additional real D-term integrand $G$ whose integrated action is gauge invariant under the stated boundary conditions, a holomorphic gauge-invariant derivative-free F-term integrand $F(\Phi,W)$, and the physical superspace and action-bracket conventions of Local N=1 Wilsonian Superspace Action Data. Its global gauge group is a compact closed matrix subgroup $G\subset U(N)$ with inherited embedded Lie-group structure; $\mathfrak g=T_I G$ has the matrix commutator, and each finite-dimensional unitary matter representation $\rho_i$ is smooth with differential $\rho_{i*}=d(\rho_i)_I$. Assume its formal Wilsonian cutoff prescription has scale $0<\lambda<\Lambda_0$, a local nondynamical-spurion extension, and the coefficientwise formal local background-jet convention stated in that action-data definition: independent real coefficient spurions are unconstrained, their first anti-chiral jets are independent, and chirality holds before evaluating constant backgrounds. No convergence or analyticity in those real coefficients is assumed. Also assume the prescription supplies a cutoff vertex functional whose contributing graphs are one-particle-irreducible (cutting one internal propagator does not disconnect a graph) and a local derivative expansion with finitely many derivatives at each formal order for external momenta $|p|\ll\lambda$, around the topologically trivial background. Let $\langle\cdot\rangle_\lambda$ be the normalized coefficientwise expectation of the selected regulated formal expansion. Assume its rigid N=1 and BRST changes of variables obey $\langle\delta_{\epsilon,\bar\epsilon}\mathcal O\rangle_\lambda=\langle\mathcal O\,\delta_{\epsilon,\bar\epsilon}S_\lambda\rangle_\lambda$ and $\langle s\mathcal O\rangle_\lambda=0$ for every local insertion $\mathcal O$, with counterterms satisfying the same identities and no local gauge anomaly. In the chiral density, collect every quadratic gauge-invariant polynomial in $W$ whose coefficients are independent of the dynamical matter superfields $\Phi_i$—including terms initially written in $F$, such as off-diagonal Abelian kinetic mixing—into $P_{\mathrm{kin}}(W)=\sum_{a=1}^{s}\tau_a Q_a(W^\alpha,W_\alpha)/(8\pi i)$, where $s<\infty$ and each $Q_a:\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}\times\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}\to\mathbb C$ is symmetric complex-bilinear with $Q_a(\operatorname{Ad}_gX,\operatorname{Ad}_gY)=Q_a(X,Y)$ for all $g\in G$ and $X,Y\in\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$; $Q_1,\ldots,Q_s$ form a basis of this invariant-form space, and $\operatorname{Ad}_gX=gXg^{-1}$ in the chosen matrix representation. Each $\tau_a$ is dimensionless. Let $F_{\mathrm{rem}}(\Phi,W)$ be the remaining holomorphic integrand. For a simple factor this includes the $\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm h}$ term in the action-data definition. Then $F_{\mathrm{rem},\lambda}(\Phi,W)=F_{\mathrm{rem}}(\Phi,W)$ coefficientwise, while the gauge-kinetic coefficient vector $\boldsymbol\tau_\lambda$ may receive one-loop corrections but no higher-loop corrections. If an unbroken U(1) factor is present, a Fayet–Iliopoulos parameter can receive a correction only at one loop, and that correction is proportional to the sum of the U(1) charges of all left-chiral multiplets, including multiplicities; it vanishes when this charge sum is zero. The theorem imposes no nonrenormalization condition on the additional real D-term integrand $G_\lambda$, the Kähler data, or coefficients expressed after a scale-dependent chiral-field rescaling that normalizes a positive real quadratic kinetic form. It is only a formal perturbative Wilsonian statement at nonzero cutoff: it does not assert nonperturbative exactness, a result for an arbitrary one-particle-irreducible functional, or existence of a continuum interacting quantum field theory.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A local rigid N=1 action datum, a formal high-mode split at $0<\lambda<\Lambda_0$, and the perturbative, locality, symmetry-preservation, and anomaly-free hypotheses in the statement.

[F1] [[def-qftx-wilsonian-n1-action-data]]: the bare density contains the canonical matter kinetic term $\sum_i\Phi_i^\dagger e^{-V_i}\Phi_i$, an additional real $G$ whose integrated D-term is gauge invariant under the stated boundary conditions, and the chiral term $\tau\operatorname{Tr}(W^\alpha W_\alpha)/(8\pi i)+F(\Phi,W)$. The selected spurion prescription uses the formal local background-jet algebra with independent first anti-chiral jets of unconstrained real coefficient spurions, and chirality before constant-background evaluation; it separately assumes the local derivative expansion and one-particle-irreducible vertex property.

[F2] [[def-qftx-nonabelian-brst-complex]]: the component gauge/ghost convention is $sA_\mu=\partial_\mu c+[A_\mu,c]$, $sc=-\tfrac12[c,c]$, and $s\Phi_i=-\rho_{i*}(c)\Phi_i$, extended as an odd derivation to finite jets.

[F3] [[lem-qftx-nonabelian-brst-nilpotence]]: for the stated finite-dimensional Lie algebra and representation data, the declared BRST differential satisfies $s^2=0$ on the finite-jet algebra.

[F4] [[def-qftx-n1-chiral-superfield]]: the supersymmetry generators are $Q_\alpha=\partial^L_{\theta^\alpha}-i\sum_{\mu,\dot\alpha}\sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot\alpha}\bar\theta^{\dot\alpha}\partial_\mu$ and $\bar Q_{\dot\alpha}=-\partial^L_{\bar\theta^{\dot\alpha}}+i\sum_{\mu,\alpha}\theta^\alpha\sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot\alpha}\partial_\mu$; for external odd parameters, $\delta_{\epsilon,\bar\epsilon}\Phi=\epsilon^\alpha Q_\alpha\Phi+\bar\epsilon^{\dot\alpha}\bar Q_{\dot\alpha}\Phi$.

[F5] [[def-qftx-wilsonian-n1-action-data]]: for scalar values $\phi$ in an open $\mathcal M\subseteq\mathbb C^n$ and smooth real $K$, the complex Hessian and its closed real $(1,1)$-form are real D-term data. Where the Hessian is positive definite they define a Kähler metric; if nondegenerate but indefinite they define pseudo-Kähler data. Neither belongs to the holomorphic F-term.

[A1] The perturbative expansion is formal and coefficientwise about the topologically trivial sector; no instanton or other nonperturbative contribution is included. In a local trivialization, write the connection one-form as $A=A_\mu dx^\mu$ and define its curvature by $F_A=dA+\tfrac12[A\wedge A]$, where, for tangent vectors $X,Y$, $[A\wedge A](X,Y)=[A(X),A(Y)]-[A(Y),A(X)]$. For every selected invariant form $Q_a$, the topological-angle density $Q_a(F_A\wedge F_A)$ is treated as a total derivative under the stated boundary convention.

[A2] At every perturbative order the regulated local counterterms lie in the declared superspace action class and preserve the rigid supersymmetry and BRST/gauge Ward identities. Explicitly, for the normalized coefficientwise expectation, $\langle\delta_{\epsilon,\bar\epsilon}\mathcal O\rangle_\lambda=\langle\mathcal O\,\delta_{\epsilon,\bar\epsilon}S_\lambda\rangle_\lambda$ and $\langle s\mathcal O\rangle_\lambda=0$ for every local insertion $\mathcal O$. The selected quantum measure has no local gauge anomaly.

[A3] Each independent coefficient of $F$ and $\tau$ is extended to a nondynamical chiral superfield, and each real coefficient of $G$ to an unconstrained nondynamical real superfield. The local counterterms and their Ward identities hold in the coefficientwise formal local background-jet algebra before evaluation at constant scalar components. For the finite set $P_1,\ldots,P_k$ entering a fixed coefficient, the derivative-free sector is a formal power series in $u_j=P_j-p_j^{(0)}$ over the remaining chiral coefficient algebra, and the $\bar D_{\dot\alpha}P_j$ are independent odd jets over that sector. Formal derivatives and the graded chain rule act coefficientwise. Contributions containing background superderivatives are assigned to the D-sector after chiral projection as in [F1]. No integrated field is rescaled and no quantum Jacobian is inferred.

[A4] For each U(1) qualification, the perturbative background leaves that factor unbroken. The FI parameter means the field-independent coefficient of $[V_1]_D$. A real linear superfield is a real $L$ satisfying $D^\alpha D_\alpha L=0=\bar D_{\dot\alpha}\bar D^{\dot\alpha}L$; a field-dependent coupling $[LV_1]_D$ is separate D-term data and is not this FI coefficient. The charge sum includes every left-chiral multiplet with its representation multiplicity.

## Proof

1.1 By [F1], [F4], and [A2], the local Wilsonian density at every perturbative order has the same superspace and gauge-invariant decomposition into the fixed canonical matter kinetic term, an additional real D-term, a chiral F-term, and matter-independent quadratic gauge-kinetic terms. The collection of the latter terms into $P_{\mathrm{kin}}$ is the algebraic separation specified in the Statement and changes no field variable or action. The BRST differential used to state gauge invariance is well-defined and nilpotent by [F2] and [F3]; this is a premise about the local gauge algebra, not a proof of quantum anomaly cancellation. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, A2]

2.1 Work in the formal background-jet algebra of [F1] and [A3], before setting backgrounds constant. For a derivative-free chiral coefficient write $f=\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb N^k}a_\nu u^\nu$, where each $a_\nu$ belongs to the remaining chiral coefficient algebra, so $\bar D_{\dot\alpha}a_\nu=0$. The graded chain rule gives $0=\bar D_{\dot\alpha}f=\sum_{j=1}^k(\partial f/\partial u_j)\bar D_{\dot\alpha}P_j$. Independence of these first odd background jets forces $\partial f/\partial u_j=0$ for every $j$, coefficientwise. Indeed the coefficient of $u^{\nu-e_j}$ in this derivative is $\nu_j a_\nu$ whenever $\nu_j>0$. The coefficient algebra is over $\mathbb C$, hence multiplication by the nonzero integer $\nu_j$ is invertible, and $a_\nu=0$ for every nonzero multi-index $\nu$. Thus $f=a_0$ and the real D coefficients do not enter the derivative-free chiral integrand at any formal order. Terms containing background superderivatives belong to the D-sector by [F1]; the matter-independent quadratic gauge chiral terms retain their topological-angle total-derivative qualification in [A1]. This argument requires neither a positive-body factorization nor an analytic extension of formal series, and makes no change of integrated field variables. [F1, A1, A2, A3, algebra, step 1.1]

3.1 Assign the bookkeeping R charges $R(\theta^\alpha)=1$, $R(\bar\theta^{\dot\alpha})=-1$, $R(\Phi)=R(V)=R(\tau_a)=0$, and $R(f_r)=2-r$ to a coefficient $f_r$ multiplying a term with $r$ field-strength superfields; then $R(W_\alpha)=1$ and the chiral density has total R charge two. To apply the topological-angle shifts, first use a real basis $R_b$ of the real-valued invariant symmetric bilinear forms on $\mathfrak g$. Such a basis complexifies to a basis of the space in the Statement: the real and imaginary parts of any invariant complex form restricted to $\mathfrak g$ are real invariant forms, because $\operatorname{Ad}_g$ preserves $\mathfrak g$. Finite-dimensional basis extraction adds no choice principle. Write $Q_a=\sum_b M_{ba}R_b$ with an invertible complex matrix $M$, and put $\widetilde\tau_b=\sum_a M_{ba}\tau_a$; this changes only coefficient coordinates. Real shifts of $\widetilde\tau_b$ change only topological-angle terms, which integrate to zero under [A1]. Consequently the change of a quadratic chiral coefficient in this real basis under such a shift is real. Its holomorphic derivative with respect to each $\widetilde\tau_b$ is real-valued, hence constant: writing it as $u+iv$, the Cauchy–Riemann equations and $v=0$ make both real derivatives of $u$ vanish. Applying the same argument to every other complex scalar coupling makes these derivative coefficients constant in those couplings as well; the argument applies coefficientwise in the formal coupling expansion. A coefficient of any nonkinetic chiral operator is unchanged by every real shift, and holomorphy therefore makes it independent of $\widetilde{\boldsymbol\tau}$. Transforming back gives $\Delta_\lambda=\sum_{a,b}c_{ab,\lambda}\tau_b Q_a(W^\alpha,W_\alpha)+H_\lambda(\Phi,W,f)$, where $H_\lambda$ is independent of every $\tau_b$. The uniform scaling used below commutes with this fixed linear basis change. [A1, A3, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Consider a connected graph contributing to this chiral density, with $E_V$ external left-handed gaugino lines and $I_V$ internal vector lines. Uniformly scale the complete gauge-kinetic coefficient vector by a formal parameter $t$, $\boldsymbol\tau\mapsto t\boldsymbol\tau$, holding its coefficient ratios fixed. Each internal vector propagator then contributes $t^{-1}$. Let $A_m$ count pure-gauge vertices from the quadratic gauge-kinetic polynomial with $m\geq3$ vector lines; let $B_{mr}$ count vertices from a remaining F-term containing $r$ field strengths and $m\geq r$ vector lines; and let $C_m$ count vertices from the fixed canonical matter kinetic term with two chiral lines and $m\geq1$ vector lines. Each $A_m$ vertex contributes one factor of $t$, and the vector-line ends satisfy $2I_V+E_V=\sum_{m\geq3}mA_m+\sum_r\sum_{m\geq r}mB_{mr}+\sum_{m\geq1}mC_m$. The R-charge condition gives $\sum_r\sum_{m\geq r}(2-r)B_{mr}=2-E_V$. Thus $N_t=\sum_{m\geq3}A_m-I_V$; eliminating $E_V$ and $I_V$ yields $N_t=1-\tfrac12[\sum_{m\geq3}(m-2)A_m+\sum_r\sum_{m\geq r}(2-r+m)B_{mr}+\sum_{m\geq1}mC_m]$. [F1, A2, A3, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Every coefficient inside the square brackets is a positive integer. A graph contributing to the $\boldsymbol\tau$-independent part $H_\lambda$ must have $N_t=0$, so the bracket equals two. The positive-integer solutions are two cubic or one quartic pure-gauge vertices, one $B_{mr}$ vertex with $m=r$, two $C_1$ vertices, one $C_2$ vertex, or the mixed case with one cubic gauge vertex and one $C_1$ vertex. For the $B_{mr}$ case, [step 4.1]'s R-charge equation gives $E_V=r=m$; the vector-end equation then gives $I_V=0$. It is an original single tree insertion in $F_{\mathrm{rem}}$. In the $A_3=2$ pure-gauge case the R-charge equation gives $E_V=2$ and the vector-end equation is $2I_V+2=6$, so $I_V=2$ and $L=2-2+1=1$. In the $A_4=1$ case it is $2I_V+2=4$, so $I_V=1$ and $L=1-1+1=1$. Both are one-particle-irreducible one-loop corrections to the matter-independent quadratic gauge-kinetic polynomial. For either matter-only case, the R-charge equation gives $E_V=2$ and the vector-end equation gives $2I_V+2=2$, so $I_V=0$. Each $C_m$ vertex contains one chiral and one antichiral matter line. A contribution to a holomorphic F-term cannot have an external antichiral line; with no other vertex type present, the two $C_1$ vertices therefore contract their two antichiral ends with their two chiral ends, giving $I_\Phi=2$, while the one $C_2$ vertex contracts its own chiral/antichiral pair, giving $I_\Phi=1$. Thus in each case the total internal-line count equals the vertex count and $L=1$; no matter line is external, so these graphs correct only the matter-independent quadratic gauge-kinetic polynomial, including any allowed Abelian mixing term. In the mixed case the end equation is $2I_V+2=3+1=4$, so $I_V=1$; the single $C_1$ vertex contracts its chiral/antichiral pair, giving $I_\Phi=1$. Hence $I=2$, $N_{\rm vert}=2$, and $L=1$. Its sole vector line is a bridge separating the gauge vertex from the matter vertex, so it is one-particle-reducible and excluded by the vertex convention in [F1]. Therefore every non-tree contribution to $H_\lambda$ is a one-loop shift of the matter-independent quadratic gauge-kinetic coefficient vector; no nonkinetic term in $H_\lambda$ receives a radiative correction. A contribution linear in $\boldsymbol\tau$ would require $N_t=1$, hence a zero bracket and no interaction vertices; it is absent. Thus $F_{\mathrm{rem},\lambda}=F_{\mathrm{rem}}$, and there are no higher-loop gauge-kinetic corrections. [F1, A2, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 For an unbroken U(1) factor, the prepotential shifts by $V_1\mapsto V_1-\Lambda_1-\bar\Lambda_1$. A constant coefficient $\xi$ multiplying $\int d^4x\,[V_1]_D$ is gauge invariant because the Berezin integral of the chiral and anti-chiral shifts vanishes under the boundary condition in [F1]. For a field-independent coefficient promoted to a real spurion $S$, the physical variation contains $-[P_{\mathrm{phys}}(S)\Lambda_1]_F$ and its Hermitian conjugate, where $P_{\mathrm{phys}}=-\tfrac12\partial^L_{\bar\theta^{\dot2}}\partial^L_{\bar\theta^{\dot1}}|_{\bar\theta=0}=+\tfrac14\bar D^{\dot\alpha}\bar D_{\dot\alpha}$ at fixed chiral coordinates is the normalized D-to-F projector in [F1]. Since compactly supported chiral $\Lambda_1$ is arbitrary, its chiral pairing and the conjugate pairing vanish for all tests only if $\bar D^2S=0=D^2S$. Write the derivative-free real coefficient as a formal function of the real coefficient spurions $P_j$ and the chiral/anti-chiral coefficient spurions $f_r,\bar f_r,\tau_a,\bar\tau_a$. In $\bar D^2S$, the coefficients of the independent second anti-chiral jets $\bar D^2P_j$, $\bar D^2\bar f_r$, and $\bar D^2\bar\tau_a$ are the corresponding formal partial derivatives; their independence forces $S$ to be independent of all $P_j,\bar f_r,\bar\tau_a$. Reality then makes it independent of $f_r,\tau_a$ as well, so $S$ is constant. A separate field-dependent coupling $[L V_1]_D$ with $L$ as in [A4] remains distinct D-term data, not the field-independent FI coefficient. Therefore a correction to the constant FI coefficient is independent of the coupling spurions in [A3]. The only such candidate is the matter tadpole from the canonical kinetic vertex. Since all masses and interactions are spurions, this coupling-independent regulated integral is the same for each chiral component; the representation enters only through its charge. Writing its common value as $C_\lambda$, the correction is $\delta\xi^{(1)}=C_\lambda\sum_i q_i$. This is a one-loop graph, so the correction vanishes when the charge sum is zero and there are no higher-loop FI graphs. [F1, A2, A3, A4, step 5.1]

7.1 The argument constrains the remaining holomorphic integrand, matter-independent quadratic gauge-kinetic coefficients, and FI tadpole only; it places no equality requirement on the arbitrary real function $G_\lambda$ in [F1] or on the real Kähler data in [F5]. Since the high-mode split retains momenta below $\lambda$ and [A1] excludes nonperturbative sectors, no conclusion follows for an arbitrary one-particle-irreducible functional, instanton corrections, coefficients after field rescaling, or a continuum limit. These are exactly the qualifications in the statement. [F1, F5, A1, step 1.1, step 5.1, step 6.1] ∎
