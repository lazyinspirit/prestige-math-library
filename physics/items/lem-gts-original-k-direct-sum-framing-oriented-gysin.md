## Statement

Work on an original $k_1/k_2$ concentration overlap of the constructed local-spectral or specified actual CY4 dg-quiver realizations. Let $V_i$ be the original framing bundles, let $P_i=\mathbb P(V_i)$ be the two axes in $P=\mathbb P(V_1\oplus V_2)$, and let $J_i\subset P_i$ be the stipulated original stable-pair opens with their actual virtual classes and compatible original orientations. Write $P^{\mathrm{free}}$ for the locus with trivial normalized framing-preserving inertia. Then

$$U=P^{\mathrm{free}}\setminus((P_1\setminus J_1)\cup(P_2\setminus J_2))$$

is a common algebraic-space open containing both $J_i$. For its tautological line $L$, put $B_i=L^\vee\otimes V_i$. The zero scheme of the other component section is exactly $J_i$, and $i_i:J_i\hookrightarrow U$ is a regular closed embedding with normal bundle $B_j|_{J_i}$ for $j\ne i$. The actual constructed obstruction theory on $U$ has the induced hyperbolic orientation, whose restriction is the stipulated original orientation, and satisfies

$$i_i^![U]^{\mathrm{vir}}=[J_i]^{\mathrm{vir}},\qquad (i_i)_*[J_i]^{\mathrm{vir}}=c_{\operatorname{rk}B_j}(B_j)\cap[U]^{\mathrm{vir}}.$$

These are supported identities in the source equivariant rational cycle-class convention. No mixed fixed-locus properness, divisor regularity or integral over the possibly nonproper $U$ is used. The further double Euler cap is zero because the retained component gives a nowhere-zero section on each $J_i$; it gives no injectivity or index independence. An arbitrary bare source enhancement with the same framing bundle is not identified with this constructed obstruction theory.

## Proof

1.1 **The supported Thom operation.** Let $(E,q,o)$ be an oriented complex orthogonal bundle of complex rank $2n$ over a finite-type complex space $Y$. Choose a positive real form $E_R$, so $E=E_R\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C$ and $q$ is positive definite on $E_R$. Such forms exist and their choices are contractible. One direct construction starts with a Hermitian metric: the symmetric nondegenerate $q$ determines an invertible antilinear self-adjoint operator $A$, whose square is positive Hermitian. Then $J=A(A^2)^{-1/2}$ is an antilinear involution, and its fixed real bundle is positive for $q$. Positive square roots are unique, so this construction varies continuously with the metric; the space of Hermitian metrics is convex. Averaging over a compact torus gives equivariant choices when needed. The orientation $o$ selects the real orientation. For an isotropic vector $v=x+iy$ with $x,y\in E_R$, the equality $q(v,v)=0$ implies $q(x,x)=q(y,y)$ and $q(x,y)=0$. In particular $x=0$ implies $v=0$. Thus the real projection $p_R:E\to E_R$ has no nonzero zero on any isotropic cone $C\subset E$. Pulling back the oriented Thom class of $E_R$ along $p_R\tau_C$ gives a class $$ u_C\in H^{2n}(C,C\setminus Y;\mathbb Q), $$ where $\tau_C$ is the tautological vector and the zero section is identified with its support in $C$. Capping Borel–Moore homology with this supported class defines $$ G_{E,C}:H^{BM}_k(C;\mathbb Q)\longrightarrow H^{BM}_{k-2n}(Y;\mathbb Q). \tag{9} $$ The Thom class used here can itself be characterized without any transversality of $C$: on each real oriented trivialization it is the generator of $H^{2n}(\mathbb R^{2n},\mathbb R^{2n}\setminus0;\mathbb Q)$. These generators glue, since oriented transition maps act by degree one. Pullback along $p_R\tau_C$ specifies a natural supported class on $C$; its definition does not require the other relative cohomology groups of an arbitrary cone fiber to vanish or the class to be unique in its degree. Naturality under pullback follows from naturality of the ambient Thom class. A homotopy of positive real forms gives a supported homotopy through maps whose zero locus remains the zero section, by the same positivity argument. Hence (9) is independent of the chosen form. Projection formula for cap products proves commutation with proper pushforward. For direct sums, the oriented fiber generator is the exterior product of the generators; therefore the Thom classes multiply and the corresponding operations compose. These assertions apply equivariantly by finite-dimensional Borel approximations in every fixed degree. If $C$ lies in a maximal isotropic bundle $\Lambda$, the map $p_R:\Lambda_{\mathbb R}\to E_R$ is an isomorphism: it is injective by the isotropy argument and the real ranks agree. Its sign is precisely the specified orientation comparison. Thus $G_{E,C}$ is the ordinary complex zero-section Gysin of $\Lambda$, with that sign. In particular, on the zero section $G_{E,Y}$ is cap product with $e(E_R)$, and, when an isotropic subbundle $K$ is present, $$ e(E_R)=c_{\operatorname{rk}K}(K)\, e\bigl((K^\perp/K)_R\bigr). \tag{10} $$ Indeed an orthogonal positive metric splits the positive real bundle into the real projection of $K$ and its real orthogonal complement, and the latter is the positive real form of the quadratic reduction. Multiplicativity of the ordinary oriented real Euler class proves (10). There is a useful concrete formula on fundamental cycles. For an integral isotropic cone $C$ not contained in the zero section, blow up its zero ideal. Since $C$ is a degree-one generated cone in a vector bundle, the blowup is the total space of the tautological line on $\mathbb P(C)$, with exceptional zero divisor $D=\mathbb P(C)$. Pullback of $E$ has an isotropic line $L$, and its tautological section cuts out $D$. Splitting off the positive real projection of $L$, (9) and ordinary Thom multiplicativity give $$ G_{E,C}[C] =(\rho_D)_*\left(e\bigl((L^\perp/L)_R\bigr)\cap[D]\right). \tag{11} $$ The same assertion for a cycle is obtained by blowing up its intersection with the zero section; its section generates a line after that blowup. Cycles already in the zero section instead contribute $e(E_R)$ capped with that cycle. Proper birational pushforward of the strict transform is the original fundamental cycle, so this computation does not discard any multiplicity. Formula (11) is a direct computation of the homological operation, not a theorem identifying two independently defined operations. [given, algebra]

2.1 **Cosection-localized zero Gysin on cycles and rational equivalences.** Let $F\to X$ have rank $r$ and let $\sigma:F\to\mathcal O_X$ be a cosection with zero scheme $S$. Put $F(\sigma)=F|_S\cup\ker(\sigma|_{X\setminus S})$, with its reduced cycle support. For an integral cycle $B\subset F(\sigma)$ contained over $S$, set $G_\sigma[B]=0^!_{F|_S}[B]$. Otherwise let $X_B=\overline{\pi(B)}$ and blow up the image ideal of $\sigma|_{X_B}$, taking the component dominating $X_B$. Denote this proper birational map by $\rho:\widetilde X_B\to X_B$. There is a surjective map $$ \rho^*F\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{\widetilde X_B}(-D),\qquad F'=\ker(\rho^*F\longrightarrow\mathcal O(-D)). $$ The strict transform $\widetilde B$ lies in $F'$: it does so on the dense open where $\sigma$ is nonzero, and $F'$ is closed. Define $$ G_\sigma[B]=-(\rho_D)_* \bigl(D\cdot0^!_{F'}[\widetilde B]\bigr). \tag{12} $$ The sign is essential. The quotient line in this convention is $\mathcal O(-D)$; Kiem–Li write a divisor whose line bundle is the quotient, so their divisor is $-D$. More generally a proper generically finite regularization and a cycle lift of degree $k$ give the same expression divided by $k$. To verify this, take a common integral component of the fiber product dominating both cycle lifts and regularize once more. Its two pulled-back quotient lines agree as sublines of the rational trivial line, since both are the image ideal of the same cosection. Their Cartier divisors therefore agree, including multiplicities. Their kernels agree as the saturated kernel of the same generic functional in a locally free sheaf. Ordinary Gysin commutes with proper pushforward, and the projection formula for the common divisor shows that both expressions equal the expression on the common refinement divided by its degree over $B$. This proves independence of the regularization actually used in (12). A cycle lift whose proper image has smaller dimension contributes zero. Indeed compare with a regularization generically finite over the image's base, as above. On the common refinement its image in that regularization has smaller dimension, so its proper cycle pushforward is zero. Commutation of ordinary zero Gysin with that pushforward and the projection formula make (12) zero as well. This is stronger than merely knowing that its final pushforward into $X$ vanishes. For clarity, the exact ordinary excess identity used below is $$ 0^!_F\,j_*\alpha=c_{\operatorname{rk}Q}(Q)\,0^!_{F_1}\alpha \quad\text{for }0\to F_1\xrightarrow{j}F\to Q\to0. \tag{13} $$ Pull back a base cycle along the vector-bundle projection, which is an isomorphism on Chow groups. The identity becomes the top-degree part of $c(F)c(F_1)^{-1}=c(Q)$, proving (13). This also proves it for every cycle, by the same bundle homotopy isomorphism. To verify rational equivalence for (12), let $W\subset F(\sigma)$ be integral and $f$ a rational function. Regularize once over the base of $W$, lift $W$, and pull back $f$. Proper pushforward of its zero and pole divisors is the original zero and pole divisor times the generic degree. Split the lifted divisors into three groups: components whose image dimension drops, components over $D$, and the remaining components. The first group contributes zero by the preceding paragraph. For the second group, (13) with quotient $\mathcal O(-D)$ identifies the divisor term in (12) with the ordinary $0^!_{F|_S}$ term. The remaining group is exactly the strict-transform contribution of (12). Their total difference is zero, since ordinary zero Gysin and intersection with $D$ send the principal divisor of the lifted $f$ to zero. Hence $G_\sigma(\operatorname{div}f)=0$ as a class *on $S$*. This proves that (12) defines a map on Chow groups, not only on cycles. The same common-refinement and degree argument proves commutation with proper pushforward. It uses actual zero-Gysin and divisor projection formulas, and never assumes injectivity of $A_*(S)\to A_*(X)$. One special evaluation fixes the later signs. Suppose $s$ is a section of a line subbundle $L\subset\Lambda$, with zero divisor $D$. The associated cosection of $\Lambda^\vee$ has quotient $L^\vee =\mathcal O(-D)$ and kernel $(\Lambda/L)^\vee$. Formula (12), applied to the zero-section cycle, gives $$ G_\sigma[0_{\Lambda^\vee}] =(-1)^n\,c_{n-1}(\Lambda/L)\cap[D], \qquad n=\operatorname{rk}\Lambda. \tag{14} $$ Multiplying by the $(-1)^n$ in the positive-maximal-isotropic square-root convention leaves $c_{n-1}(\Lambda/L)\cap[D]$. This is exactly the isotropic-line blowup expression (11) once an adapted maximal isotropic flag has been justified. The remaining step is that adapted-flag comparison for the source's prescribed flag construction; (14) alone does not yet assert that comparison. [F3, given, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Define the ACTUAL common open $$U=P^{\rm free}\setminus\big((P_1\setminus J_1)\cup(P_2\setminus J_2)\big).$$ The removed subsets are closed in $P$, because the axes are closed and $J_i$ is open in its axis. 3.1 **Comparison on the actual supported cycle classes.** First the global Euler class in the source flag construction has cycle-class image $e(E_R)$. To verify every normalization, form the bundle of isotropic flags of lengths $1,\ldots,n-1$. Successively its fibers are smooth quadrics of complex dimensions $2n-2,2n-4,\ldots,2$. At stage $j$ let $h_j$ be the hyperplane class of the tautological isotropic line in the quadratic reduction. Put $$ h=\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}h_j^{\,2(n-j)}. $$ Each quadric's hyperplane degree is two, as follows by intersecting its single degree-two equation with complementary generic hyperplanes in projective space. Iterated projective pushforward therefore gives $p_*h=2^{n-1}$. The final oriented rank-two quadratic quotient chooses one of its two isotropic lines, producing the positive maximal isotropic bundle $\Lambda$ on the flag space. Projection to the positive real form identifies $\Lambda_{\mathbb R}$ with $p^*E_R$ with its prescribed positive orientation. Hence $c_n(\Lambda)=p^*e(E_R)$ in cohomology. It follows directly that $$ \operatorname{cl}\left(2^{1-n}p_* (h\,c_n(\Lambda)\cap\alpha)\right) =e(E_R)\cap\operatorname{cl}(\alpha). \tag{15} $$ This proves the required cycle-class statement; it does not need an unproved equality of all characteristic classes in integral Chow. For an isotropic section $s$ and a positive maximal isotropic $\Lambda$, put $s^*=\pi_{\Lambda^\vee}s$ and $Z^*=Z(s^*)$. On $Z^*$, $s$ lies in $\Lambda$ and defines a cosection of $\Lambda^\vee|_{Z^*}$. This cosection annihilates the normal cone $C_{Z^*/Y}$. Indeed locally split $E=\Lambda\oplus\Lambda^\vee$, write $s=s_\Lambda+s^*$, and use isotropy $\langle s^*,s_\Lambda\rangle=0$. The linear function defined by $s_\Lambda$ vanishes on every graph of $t s^*$. Passing to its scheme-theoretic Rees limit gives its vanishing on the normal cone, including nilpotents. This is a polynomial identity on the Rees algebra, not just a set-theoretic limit. The source localized square-root operator is, on this flag cover, $(-1)^n$ times the cosection-localized ordinary zero Gysin applied to the normal-cone specialization. Pushing from $Z(s)$ into $Y$ and using (12)–(13) gives ordinary zero Gysin of the normal cone: the quotient-line top Chern factor is exactly the divisor in (12). Ordinary normal-cone specialization and zero Gysin compute $c_n(\Lambda^\vee)\cap\alpha$. Multiplying by $(-1)^n$ changes this to $c_n(\Lambda)\cap\alpha$. Applying the flag pushforward and (15) proves $$ i_*\operatorname{cl}\bigl(\sqrt e_{\rm alg}(E,s)(\alpha)\bigr) =e(E_R)\cap\operatorname{cl}(\alpha). \tag{16} $$ Both the construction and this identity commute with restriction to open subsets, by the blowup formula (12) and the Rees specialization construction. That open compatibility is the only localization property needed in the next step. Let now $C\subset E|_Y$ be an isotropic cone. Its canonical projective completion is $\overline C=\mathbb P(C\oplus\mathcal O)$, with projection $\overline\pi:\overline C\to Y$. In $\overline\pi^*(E\oplus\mathcal O^2)$ give the last two summands the hyperbolic quadratic form. The tautological line $L=\mathcal O_{\overline C}(-1)$ is isotropic, since its vectors have the form $(v,t,0)$ and $q(v,v)=0$ on $C$. The oriented quadratic reduction $$ \overline E=L^\perp/L $$ has rank $2n$. The constant isotropic vector $(0,1,0)$ lies in $L^\perp$ and induces a section $\overline s$ of $\overline E$. Its zero scheme is precisely the zero section $Y\subset C$: being zero means that $(0,1,0)$ lies in $L$, forcing $v=0$ and $t\ne0$. On the open chart $t\ne0$, the pair $(\overline E,\overline s)$ identifies with $(\pi^*E,-\tau_C)$. This follows by subtracting multiples of $(v,1,0)$ to set the second coordinate to zero in the orthogonal quotient. Replacing a complex section by its negative preserves the oriented localized class. For an algebraic cycle $\alpha$ on $C$, take its closure $\overline\alpha$ in $\overline C$. Apply (16) on $\overline C$ and then push by $\overline\pi$. Since $\overline\pi$ composed with the zero section is the identity, and the zero locus lies wholly in the open chart $C$, open compatibility gives $$ \operatorname{cl}\bigl(\sqrt{0_E^!}\,\alpha\bigr) =\overline\pi_* \bigl(e(\overline E_R)\cap \operatorname{cl}(\overline\alpha)\bigr). \tag{17} $$ No injectivity of the zero-section pushforward has been invoked: its proper left inverse $\overline\pi_*$ is what recovers the supported class here. Apply the same reasoning to the Thom operation (9). Its supported class extends to the isotropic section $\overline s$ on the proper completion, and its pushforward into $\overline C$ is the ordinary Euler class of $\overline E_R$. Open compatibility of the Thom class and the same proper left inverse show $$ G_{E,C}\operatorname{cl}(\alpha) =\overline\pi_* \bigl(e(\overline E_R)\cap \operatorname{cl}(\overline\alpha)\bigr). \tag{18} $$ Equations (17) and (18) prove the exact supported comparison on the cycle classes used in the virtual construction. The calculation is equivariant: make it over a finite-dimensional Borel approximation before taking each fixed degree. It is independent of which cycle closure is used. Indeed a difference is supported on the boundary $\mathbb P(C)$. There the section $\overline s$ is nowhere zero and isotropic; its positive real projection is nowhere zero, so $e(\overline E_R)$ restricts to zero. The projection formula annihilates every boundary contribution. The same formula proves commutation with a refined regular pullback: projective completion and its tautological orthogonal reduction commute with the Cartesian base change, refined pullback commutes with proper pushforward, and cap product with the pulled-back Euler class commutes with that pullback. A refined pullback of a chosen cycle closure restricts to the refined pullback of the cycle on the open chart, so (17) applies without any flatness assertion about the cone.
