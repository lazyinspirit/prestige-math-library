## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ in branch (a), and the Axiom of Choice together with $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ as declared by its weak-solvability lemmas in branch (b) ([[def-countable-choice]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $c>0$ and let $(\varphi,A)$ be a potential pair of the framework ([[def-em-potential-gauge-and-field-conventions]], [[def-em-fields]]) with field pair $(E,B)$ and Lorenz divergence $G=\operatorname{div}A+c^{-2}\partial_t\varphi$.

(a) [All-space slab; Lorenz gauge] Let $t_0<T$ and let $(\varphi,A)$ be of class $C^\infty$ in the extension sense on $\mathbb R^3\times[t_0,T]$ (the restriction of a $C^\infty$ pair on $\mathbb R^3\times J$ for some open interval $J\supset[t_0,T]$; [[def-ck-euclidean-maps-and-diffeomorphisms]]). Then there is a gauge function $\chi$ of class $C^\infty$ in the extension sense on $\mathbb R^3\times[t_0,T]$ with $\chi(\cdot,t_0)=0$, $\partial_t\chi(\cdot,t_0)=0$ and $W\chi=G$ on $\mathbb R^3\times(t_0,T)$; the transformed pair $T_\chi(\varphi,A)$ is in Lorenz gauge on $\mathbb R^3\times(t_0,T)$, has the same field pair $(E,B)$, and any two such gauge functions differ by a solution of $W\chi=0$. This is the exact content of [[lem-em-gauge-wave-reachability]] read for potential pairs of the framework; its physical content is that every smooth potential representation of a field pair on a slab admits a Lorenz-gauge representative with zero gauge Cauchy data at $t_0$, without changing the fields.

(b) [Bounded smooth domain; Coulomb gauge with admissible boundary families] Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^3$ be a bounded $C^\infty$ graph domain ([[def-bounded-c-k-domain-and-boundary-charts]]), let $I\subseteq\mathbb R$ be an arbitrary interval, possibly unbounded, and let $(\varphi,A)$ be $C^\infty$ on an actual neighbourhood of $\overline\Omega\times\overline I$. Prescribe either (i) a Dirichlet family $g_D(p,t)$ smooth in boundary charts on a neighbourhood of $\partial\Omega\times\overline I$, or (ii) an outward conormal family $\kappa(p,t)$ with the same smoothness and the compatibility $\int_{\Omega_j}\varepsilon_0\operatorname{div}A(\cdot,t)\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega_j}\kappa(\cdot,t)\,dS=0$ for every connected component $\Omega_j$ of $\Omega$ and every $t\in I$. Then there is a gauge generator $\psi$, the restriction of one $C^\infty$ function on an actual open neighbourhood of $\overline\Omega\times\overline I$, such that $\operatorname{div}(A+\nabla\psi)=0$ on $\Omega\times I$. In case (i), $\psi(p,t)=g_D(p,t)$ on $\partial\Omega\times I$, and its restriction to $\Omega\times I$ is unique with those Dirichlet values. In case (ii), $\varepsilon_0\partial_\nu\psi(p,t)=\kappa(p,t)$ there, and imposing $\int_{\Omega_j}\psi(\cdot,t)\,dx=0$ for every component and every $t$ gives a unique generator on $\Omega\times I$; without that normalization it is unique modulo smooth time-dependent spatial constants on each component. The transformed pair $T_\psi(\varphi,A)$ is in Coulomb gauge and has the same physical field pair ([[pthm-em-gauge-invariance]]). Any two Coulomb-gauge generators differ by a function spatially harmonic at every $t$; equal Dirichlet data eliminate that residual freedom, whereas equal Neumann data leave only the componentwise constants just described. Zero Dirichlet data are the corresponding special case. These uniqueness statements concern generator values on $\Omega\times I$. For $I$ with at least two elements, they also determine the transformed potential pair in the Dirichlet or normalized Neumann case, since spatial and relative time derivatives of equal smooth restrictions agree. For singleton $I$, the ambient time derivative of the generator is not determined by its restricted values, so uniqueness of the transformed pair is not asserted. Auxiliary ambient continuations away from the physical region need not be unique.

(c) [Residual freedom on star-shaped domains] Let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^3$ be open and star-shaped ([[def-star-shaped-open-subset-of-rn]]) and let $(\varphi,A)$, $(\tilde\varphi,\tilde A)$ be two potential pairs of class $C^2$ jointly on $\Omega\times I$ ([[def-interval]]) with the same field pair, both in Lorenz gauge. If $I$ is non-open, these $C^2$ classes and the resulting generator use the open-neighbourhood restriction convention of [[lem-em-joint-smooth-potentials]]; for the physical open laboratory interval they have their ordinary $C^2$ meaning. If a degenerate $I$ is used in this branch, choose the ambient $C^2$ extensions as part of the data: the same-field and Lorenz conditions refer to these extensions on an ambient time neighbourhood before restriction, and their time jets are not determined uniquely by the restricted values alone. Then there is a gauge function $\chi$ of class $C^2$ jointly with $(\tilde\varphi,\tilde A)=T_\chi(\varphi,A)$ and $W\chi=0$ on $\Omega\times I$.

(d) [Scope] Nothing is asserted about gauge functions meeting arbitrary prescribed boundary or initial restrictions: in (a) the gauge function has zero Cauchy data at $t_0$ and no spatial boundary condition or decay, and $\Omega$ is all of $\mathbb R^3$; in (b) the datum is either a smooth time-dependent Dirichlet family or a smooth outward conormal family satisfying the stated componentwise compatibility at every physical time, with zero-mean normalization for Neumann uniqueness; the older promise of a global Lorenz gauge on a bounded domain with prescribed boundary data is explicitly outside this result. The Coulomb-gauge statement concerns the spatial divergence in the chosen inertial chart, and the instantaneous appearance of the Coulomb potential is a property of the representation, not a signal: only the field pair $(E,B)$ is physical content ([[def-em-fields]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A constant $c>0$; a potential pair $(\varphi,A)$ of the framework with field pair $(E,B)$ and Lorenz divergence $G$; in branch (a) times $t_0<T$ and the joint class hypotheses of the Statement; in branch (b) a bounded $C^\infty$ graph domain $\Omega$, an interval $I$, the potentials on a neighbourhood of $\overline\Omega\times\overline I$, and one of the two boundary families with its compatibility; in branch (c) an open star-shaped $\Omega$, an interval $I$, and two $C^2$ pairs $(\varphi,A)$, $(\tilde\varphi,\tilde A)$ with the same field pair and both in Lorenz gauge. The choice assumptions of the two branches are as stated.

[F1] Potential-pair vocabulary: $E(\varphi,A)=-\nabla\varphi-\partial_tA$, $B(\varphi,A)=\operatorname{curl}A$, $G(\varphi,A)=\operatorname{div}A+c^{-2}\partial_t\varphi$, $Wu=c^{-2}\partial_t^2u-\Delta u$, the gauge transform $T_\chi(\varphi,A)=(\varphi-\partial_t\chi,\ A+\nabla\chi)$ of a class $C^m$ pair by a class $C^m$ generator with $m\ge2$ is a class $C^{m-1}$ pair, a pair is in Lorenz gauge when $G=0$ and in Coulomb gauge when $\operatorname{div}A=0$, and the definition asserts no solvability ([[def-em-potential-gauge-and-field-conventions]], [[def-em-gauge-transformation]], [[def-laplacian-of-a-c2-function]]).

[F2] Framework vocabulary: the fields $E,B$ are the fixed-time spatial fields of the framework with the stated regularity, and the physical content of a representation is its field pair ([[def-em-fields]]).

[F3] Gauge invariance: for a potential pair and a gauge function of class $C^k$ jointly with $k\ge2$, the transform has the same field pair as the original pair ([[pthm-em-gauge-invariance]]).

[F4] Gauge shift of the Lorenz divergence: for a class $C^2$ pair and a class $C^2$ generator, $G(T_\chi(\varphi,A))=G(\varphi,A)-W\chi$ on the region of definition ([[pthm-em-lorenz-gauge-wave-form]]).

[F5] Slab reachability ([[lem-em-gauge-wave-reachability]]): under $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, for a potential pair of class $C^\infty$ in the extension sense on the slab $\mathbb R^3\times[t_0,T]$ there is a gauge function $\chi$ of class $C^\infty$ in the extension sense with $\chi(\cdot,t_0)=0$, $\partial_t\chi(\cdot,t_0)=0$ and $W\chi=G$ on $\mathbb R^3\times(t_0,T)$ (a); the transformed pair is of class $C^\infty$ in the extension sense with $E,B$ unchanged (b); it is in Lorenz gauge (c); and any two functions of class $C^2$ in the extension sense with $W\chi_1=W\chi_2=G$ satisfy $W(\chi_1-\chi_2)=0$ (d). No spatial boundary condition, decay or cavity statement is included.

[F6] Parameter-dependent smooth solvers (the authored pure-mathematical supplier [[lem-em-parameter-dependent-smooth-poisson-boundary-solvers]] on the wave-equation prerequisites page, read here at its exact Statement interface): under AC and $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ carried by its variational and Sobolev suppliers, for a bounded smooth graph domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^3$, a constant $\epsilon>0$ and an open interval $J$, and for a bulk datum $f$ jointly smooth up to $\overline\Omega\times J$ and boundary families $g_D$, $\kappa$ smooth in a fixed finite boundary atlas times $J$: the Dirichlet problem $-\epsilon\Delta u=f$, $u|_{\partial\Omega}=g_D$ has a unique solution on $\Omega\times J$, the restriction of a single smooth ambient function on a neighbourhood of $\overline\Omega\times J$; the Neumann problem $-\epsilon\Delta u=f$, $\epsilon\partial_\nu u=\kappa$ has such a solution precisely when $\int_{\Omega_j}f(\cdot,t)+\int_{\partial\Omega_j}\kappa(\cdot,t)=0$ for each component and every $t\in J$, is unique modulo a separately chosen spatial constant on each component at each time, and its zero-mean normalization $\int_{\Omega_j}u(\cdot,t)=0$ gives a unique smooth family; both operations are linear in their data, time differentiation commutes with them, smoothness and estimates are local in compact time subintervals, and the ambient continuations are auxiliary and not unique off the closed spatial domain. This interface is established from [[thm-em-elliptic-boundary-existence]], [[lem-em-zero-boundary-coercivity]], [[thm-higher-order-boundary-regularity-for-dirichlet-problems]] and [[thm-higher-order-sobolev-embedding]], whose choice assumptions it carries.

[F7] First Green identity ([[cor-first-green-identity-on-a-bounded-c-one-domain]]): under $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, for a bounded $C^1$ domain $\Omega$ and real $u\in C^2(\overline\Omega)$, $v\in C^1(\overline\Omega)$, $\int_\Omega(v\Delta u+\nabla u\cdot\nabla v)\,dx=\int_{\partial\Omega}v\,\partial_\nu u\,dS$.

[F8] Radial witnesses ([[lem-em-joint-smooth-potentials]]): on an open star-shaped $\Omega$ with centre $a$ and an interval $I$, for a jointly $C^2$ field $C$ with $\operatorname{curl}C(\cdot,t)=0$ for every $t$, the negative radial integral $\phi_C(x,t)=-\int_0^1\langle C(a+s(x-a),t),x-a\rangle\,ds$ is jointly $C^2$ with $\nabla\phi_C(\cdot,t)=-C(\cdot,t)$ for every $t$; the proof constructs both witnesses as restrictions of $C^2$ functions on an open radial-admissibility neighbourhood of $\Omega\times I$; the same open-neighbourhood restriction convention governs non-open $I$.

[F9] Calculus and topology toolkit: mixed partial derivatives of $C^2$ functions commute ([[thm-clairaut-schwarz-mixed-partials]]); the divergence and curl are linear and computed from first partial derivatives ([[lem-divergence-and-curl-are-linear-and-obey-the-scalar-product-rules]], [[def-directional-and-partial-derivatives]]); the chain rule, the sum and product rules for partial derivatives and the componentwise $C^k$ class hold ([[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]], [[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]], [[def-ck-euclidean-maps-and-diffeomorphisms]]); a continuous function on an interval with vanishing derivative at every interior point is constant, with the second form for functions with a common derivative ([[cor-zero-derivative-implies-constant]]); the oriented integral $x\mapsto\int_{t_*}^x h$ of a continuous $h$ on an interval with at least two elements is a primitive of $h$ ([[cor-primitives-of-a-continuous-function]], [[def-oriented-integral]]); star-shapedness is the radial-segment condition ([[def-star-shaped-open-subset-of-rn]]); and the intervals and the bounded graph-domain charts are those of [[def-interval]] and [[def-bounded-c-k-domain-and-boundary-charts]].

[F10] Choice assumptions: the formulations of the Axiom of Choice and of Countable Choice used in the branches ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-countable-choice]]), and the Sobolev spaces $H^1$, $H_0^1$ with the $W^{k,p}$ norm convention ([[def-sobolev-space-wkp-and-its-norm]]).

[F11] Differentiation of fixed parameter integrals: a continuous integrand with continuous time partial derivative on a neighbourhood of a compact finite-measure set times a closed time interval may be differentiated under the integral sign; the same holds on a compact embedded $C^1$ hypersurface under $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ ([[lem-em-parameter-integrals]]). Applied repeatedly to smooth data on compact time subintervals, the formulas give every time derivative. Under the same choice assumption, the Lebesgue volume of a box is the product of its side lengths and bounded measurable sets have finite volume ([[thm-lebesgue-measure-of-a-box-of-every-kind]], [[prop-lebesgue-measure-is-sigma-finite-and-finite-on-bounded-sets]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; branch (a) is the slab lemma read for framework pairs; branch (b) applies the parameter-dependent Poisson solver to the corrected datum and identifies two generators through the Green identity; branch (c) constructs the gauge generator from the radial scalar witness and a time primitive.

1.1 Branch (a). The pair $(\varphi,A)$ satisfies the hypotheses of [F5] verbatim: it is of class $C^\infty$ in the extension sense on the slab and $G$ is its Lorenz divergence, with $E,B$ its field pair in the vocabulary of [F1] and [F2]. By [F5](a) there is a gauge function $\chi$ of class $C^\infty$ in the extension sense with $\chi(\cdot,t_0)=0$, $\partial_t\chi(\cdot,t_0)=0$ and $W\chi=G$ on $\mathbb R^3\times(t_0,T)$; by [F5](b) the transformed pair has the same $E$ and $B$, and by [F5](c) it is in Lorenz gauge because $G(T_\chi(\varphi,A))=G-W\chi=0$ there; by [F5](d) two such gauge functions differ by a solution of $W\chi=0$. The countable-choice assumption is carried by [F5]. This is exactly assertion (a). [given, F1, F2, F5, F10]

1.2 Branch (b): data class and an open time interval. Let $N$ be the actual neighbourhood of $\overline\Omega\times\overline I$ on which $(\varphi,A)$ is $C^\infty$, and let $N_\partial$ be a neighbourhood of $\partial\Omega\times\overline I$ on which the chosen boundary family is smooth in boundary charts. Since $\overline\Omega$ is compact ([[def-bounded-c-k-domain-and-boundary-charts]]), the set of times $t$ for which a product neighbourhood of $\overline\Omega\times\{t\}$ is contained in $N$ is open in $\mathbb R$, and it contains $\overline I$; let $J$ be its connected component containing $I$, and likewise choose $J_\partial$ for $N_\partial$ and replace $J$ by $J\cap J_\partial$. Then $J\supseteq\overline I$ is an open interval, and the potentials, the bulk datum $F:=\varepsilon_0\operatorname{div}A$ and the chosen boundary family are smooth on $\overline\Omega\times J$ and in its boundary atlas times $J$, in the sense required by [F6]; the derivative bounds below use only $\overline\Omega\times K$ with compact $K\subset J$, never compactness of all of $\overline I$. If $I$ is empty then $\Omega\times I$ is empty and the conclusions of (b) are vacuous; the construction below therefore assumes $I$ nonempty. [given, F9, F10]

1.3 Branch (c): the difference of two representations. Assume first that $I$ has at least two elements, and put $\delta\varphi:=\tilde\varphi-\varphi$ and $\delta A:=\tilde A-A$, which are jointly $C^2$ on $\Omega\times I$ in the convention of [F8]. Equality of the field pairs gives, by the componentwise linearity of $\nabla$, $\partial_t$ and $\operatorname{curl}$ [F9], $\operatorname{curl}\delta A=B(\tilde\varphi,\tilde A)-B(\varphi,A)=0$ and $\nabla\delta\varphi+\partial_t\delta A=-E(\tilde\varphi,\tilde A)+E(\varphi,A)=0$ on $\Omega\times I$. Both pairs being in Lorenz gauge means $G(\varphi,A)=G(\tilde\varphi,\tilde A)=0$ on $\Omega\times I$. [given, F1, F9]

2.1 Branch (b), Dirichlet case. Apply [F6] with $\epsilon=\varepsilon_0>0$ and bulk datum $F=\varepsilon_0\operatorname{div}A$ on the open interval $J$, and with the Dirichlet family $g_D$; the data are smooth in the required sense by step 1.2. This gives a unique solution $\psi$ on $\Omega\times J$, the restriction of one $C^\infty$ ambient function on a neighbourhood of $\overline\Omega\times J$, with $-\varepsilon_0\Delta\psi=F$ on $\Omega\times J$ and $\psi=g_D$ on $\partial\Omega\times J$. On $\Omega\times I\subseteq\Omega\times J$ we get $\Delta\psi=-\operatorname{div}A$ (since $\varepsilon_0>0$), hence $\operatorname{div}(A+\nabla\psi)=\operatorname{div}A+\operatorname{div}\nabla\psi=\operatorname{div}A+\Delta\psi=0$ by the definitions [F1]; on $\partial\Omega\times I$, $\psi=g_D$. Therefore $T_\psi(\varphi,A)$ is in Coulomb gauge on $\Omega\times I$ and has the same field pair as $(\varphi,A)$: apply [F3] to the smooth ambient pairs on the open product $\Omega\times J$ and restrict to $\Omega\times I$, including a singleton $I$. [step 1.2, F1, F3, F6, F9]

2.2 Branch (b), Neumann case. Let $\Omega_j$ be a connected component of $\Omega$ and define, for $t\in J$, $c_j(t):=\int_{\Omega_j}F(\cdot,t)\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega_j}\kappa(\cdot,t)\,dS$. The bulk integral is a parameter integral of the smooth family $F$ over the fixed compact set $\overline\Omega_j$, and the boundary integral is a finite sum of chart parameter integrals of the smooth family $\kappa$ over the fixed boundary atlas, so $c_j$ is $C^\infty$ on $J$ by repeated applications of [F11] on compact time subintervals. Here $|\Omega_j|$ below is its finite positive Lebesgue volume: boundedness gives finiteness, and the nonempty open component contains a nondegenerate cube of positive volume by [F11]; there are finitely many components by [F6], and distinct component closures cannot meet: a sufficiently small connected one-sided graph subpatch near a common boundary point would intersect both and put them in the same component. Thus componentwise constants define smooth functions up to the closed spatial domain; the compatibility hypothesis gives $c_j(t)=0$ for $t\in I$. Put $\widehat F:=F-c_j(t)/|\Omega_j|$ on each $\Omega_j$. Then $\widehat F$ is $C^\infty$ on $\overline\Omega\times J$, $\widehat F=F$ on $\Omega\times I$, and for every $t\in J$ and component $\Omega_j$, $\int_{\Omega_j}\widehat F(\cdot,t)+\int_{\partial\Omega_j}\kappa(\cdot,t)=\int_{\Omega_j}F(\cdot,t)-c_j(t)+\int_{\partial\Omega_j}\kappa(\cdot,t)=0$, so the compatibility required by [F6] holds throughout $J$. Apply [F6] with the zero-mean normalization on each component and the datum $(\widehat F,\kappa)$: this gives a unique normalized solution $\psi$ on $\Omega\times J$, the restriction of one smooth ambient function, with $-\varepsilon_0\Delta\psi=\widehat F$ on $\Omega\times J$ and $\varepsilon_0\partial_\nu\psi=\kappa$ on $\partial\Omega\times J$. On $\Omega\times I$ we have $\Delta\psi=-\operatorname{div}A$ as before, hence $\operatorname{div}(A+\nabla\psi)=0$; on $\partial\Omega\times I$, $\varepsilon_0\partial_\nu\psi=\kappa$. Hence $T_\psi(\varphi,A)$ is in Coulomb gauge on $\Omega\times I$ with the same field pair, applying [F3] on $\Omega\times J$ before restriction, also for singleton $I$. [step 1.2, F1, F3, F6, F9, F11]

2.3 Branch (c): the radial scalar witness. By step 1.3 the field $\delta A$ is jointly $C^2$ on the open star-shaped $\Omega$ times the interval $I$ and satisfies $\operatorname{curl}\delta A(\cdot,t)=0$ for every $t$; the scalar clause of [F8] applied with $C=\delta A$ gives the jointly $C^2$ function $\chi_0:=-\phi_{\delta A}$, that is $\chi_0(x,t)=\int_0^1\langle\delta A(a+s(x-a),t),\,x-a\rangle\,ds$, with $\nabla\chi_0(\cdot,t)=\delta A(\cdot,t)$ for every $t\in I$, and $\chi_0(a,t)=0$ for every $t\in I$. The construction is the radial one of that lemma, so $\chi_0$ is the restriction of a $C^2$ function on an open radial-admissibility neighbourhood of $\Omega\times I$. [step 1.3, F8, F9]

3.1 Branch (b): uniqueness and residual freedom. Let $\psi'$ be any other generator of the statement's class on $\Omega\times I$: it is $C^\infty$ near $\overline\Omega\times\overline I$, and on $\Omega\times I$ it satisfies $\Delta\psi'=-\operatorname{div}A$ as well as the same boundary data in the relevant case. Then $\chi:=\psi'-\psi$ is $C^\infty$ near $\overline\Omega\times\overline I$ and $\Delta\chi(\cdot,t)=0$ on $\Omega$ for every $t\in I$, i.e. $\chi$ is spatially harmonic at every $t$; this proves the general residual sentence. Fix $t\in I$ and apply Green's identity [F7] on $\Omega$ with $u=v=\chi(\cdot,t)$: $\int_\Omega(\chi\Delta\chi+|\nabla\chi|^2)\,dx=\int_{\partial\Omega}\chi\,\partial_\nu\chi\,dS$, whose right-hand side vanishes in the Dirichlet case because $\chi=0$ on $\partial\Omega$ (both generators have the value $g_D$) and in the Neumann case because $\partial_\nu\chi=0$ (both have conormal datum $\kappa/\varepsilon_0$); the first term vanishes because $\Delta\chi=0$, so $\int_\Omega|\nabla\chi(\cdot,t)|^2=0$ and hence $\nabla\chi(\cdot,t)=0$ on $\Omega$ (the integrand is continuous and nonnegative). Therefore $\chi(\cdot,t)$ is constant on each connected component of $\Omega$; in the Dirichlet case the constant is the boundary value of $\chi$, which is zero, so $\chi(\cdot,t)=0$ and $\psi'=\psi$ on $\Omega\times I$; in the Neumann case the constants are the only freedom, and imposing $\int_{\Omega_j}\psi'(\cdot,t)=\int_{\Omega_j}\psi(\cdot,t)=0$ makes each of them zero. This proves uniqueness of generator values with the stated data and normalization, and the componentwise constant freedom without normalization. If $I$ has at least two elements, equality of the generator restrictions implies equality of their spatial and relative time derivatives, so the transformed pairs agree in the Dirichlet or normalized Neumann case. If $I=\{t_0\}$, equality of generator values need not give equality of ambient time derivatives: with zero original potentials and zero Dirichlet data, the ambient generators $0$ and $t-t_0$ both solve the required spatial problem at $t_0$, but their transforms there are $(0,0)$ and $(-1,0)$, respectively. Thus no singleton transformed-pair uniqueness follows. The Green identity carries its own $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ hypothesis, already assumed in branch (b). [step 2.1, step 2.2, F7, F9]

3.2 Branch (c): the auxiliary scalar has zero gradient. By [F9] the mixed partials of the jointly $C^2$ function $\chi_0$ commute, so $\partial_t\nabla\chi_0=\nabla\partial_t\chi_0$ componentwise; by step 2.3 and the identity $\nabla\delta\varphi+\partial_t\delta A=0$ of step 1.3, $\nabla(\delta\varphi+\partial_t\chi_0)=\nabla\delta\varphi+\partial_t\nabla\chi_0=\nabla\delta\varphi+\partial_t\delta A=0$ on $\Omega\times I$. [step 1.3, step 2.3, F9]

4.1 Branch (c): the auxiliary scalar is position independent. Fix $t\in I$. The function $x\mapsto g(x,t):=\delta\varphi(x,t)+\partial_t\chi_0(x,t)$ is $C^1$ on $\Omega$ with $\nabla g(\cdot,t)=0$ by step 3.2, and $\Omega$ is star-shaped with centre $a$, so for $x\in\Omega$ the radial path $s\mapsto g(a+s(x-a),t)$ is defined on $[0,1]$ and, by the chain rule [F9], has derivative $\langle\nabla g(a+s(x-a),t),x-a\rangle=0$ at every interior point; the zero-derivative corollary [F9] applied on $[0,1]$ makes it constant, so $g(x,t)=g(a,t)$ for every $x\in\Omega$. In particular the common value depends on $t$ only. [step 3.2, F9]

5.1 Branch (c): construction of the gauge generator. Assume first that $I$ is open, and put $h(t):=g(a,t)=\delta\varphi(a,t)+\partial_t\chi_0(a,t)$ for $t\in I$; since $\chi_0(a,t)=0$ for every $t$, step 2.3 gives $h(t)=\delta\varphi(a,t)$, a $C^1$ function of $t$ on the open interval $I$. By [F9] the function $\eta(t):=-\int_{t_*}^t h(u)\,du$, with a fixed $t_*\in I$, is a primitive of $-h$ on $I$, hence $C^2$ because $h$ is $C^1$, and $\eta$ is independent of $x$. Put $\chi:=\chi_0+\eta$ on $\Omega\times I$, which is jointly $C^2$ there. Then $\nabla\chi=\nabla\chi_0=\delta A$ by step 2.3, and $\partial_t\chi=\partial_t\chi_0-h$; since $h(t)=g(x,t)=\delta\varphi(x,t)+\partial_t\chi_0(x,t)$ for every $x$ by step 4.1, this reads $\partial_t\chi=-\delta\varphi$. Hence $T_\chi(\varphi,A)=(\varphi-\partial_t\chi,\ A+\nabla\chi)=(\varphi+\delta\varphi,\ A+\delta A)=(\tilde\varphi,\tilde A)$ on $\Omega\times I$. For a non-open interval $I$ the same construction is performed with the ambient $C^2$ extensions supplied by the restriction convention of [F8]: $\chi_0$ is already a restriction from an open radial-admissibility set, the common value admits the ambient $C^1$ representative $t\mapsto\tilde\varphi(a,t)-\varphi(a,t)+\partial_t\chi_0(a,t)$ on an open time interval around $I$, its primitive is added to $\chi_0$ inside that neighbourhood, and the resulting function is a $C^2$ restriction whose values on $\Omega\times I$ satisfy the two displayed identities by the same pointwise computation. [step 2.3, step 4.1, F8, F9]

6.1 Branch (c): the generator has vanishing wave expression. The pair $(\varphi,A)$ is jointly $C^2$ and the generator $\chi$ of step 5.1 is jointly $C^2$, so the gauge-shift identity [F4] applies and gives $G(T_\chi(\varphi,A))=G(\varphi,A)-W\chi$ on $\Omega\times I$. By step 5.1 $T_\chi(\varphi,A)=(\tilde\varphi,\tilde A)$, and both pairs are in Lorenz gauge by step 1.3, so $0=0-W\chi$, that is $W\chi=0$ on $\Omega\times I$. [step 1.3, step 5.1, F1, F4]

7.1 Branch (c): degenerate and empty intervals. If $I=\varnothing$, then $\Omega\times I=\varnothing$ and the assertion is vacuous: there are no points at which an identity is required. If $I=\{t_0\}$ is a singleton, then by the convention stated in (c) the ambient $C^2$ extensions and the same-field and Lorenz conditions on an ambient open time neighbourhood $J\ni t_0$ are part of the data; apply the construction of step 1.3 and steps 5.1 and 6.1 with $I$ replaced by $J$ (a nondegenerate open interval) and restrict the resulting generator, its transform identity and $W\chi=0$ to $\Omega\times\{t_0\}$. This covers every degenerate case and completes (c). [step 1.3, step 5.1, step 6.1, F9]

8.1 Scope and conclusion. Step 2.1 and step 2.2 construct the required Coulomb generators in the two declared boundary families, and step 3.1 proves the uniqueness clauses and the residual classification, so (b) is complete up to the auxiliary non-uniqueness of ambient continuations recorded in [F6]. Step 1.1 is (a). Step 1.3 and steps 4.1 through 7.1 construct, for two Lorenz $C^2$ representations of one field pair on a star-shaped domain, a $C^2$ gauge generator with $W\chi=0$, completing (c). Nothing in the branches asserts a gauge function meeting arbitrary prescribed boundary or initial restrictions: in (a) the gauge Cauchy data are zero and the spatial domain is all of $\mathbb R^3$; in (b) the boundary family and its compatibility are those stated; and the instantaneous character of the Coulomb potential is a property of the chosen representation, since by [F2] only the field pair $(E,B)$ is physical content. The Axiom of Countable Choice is used in (a) through [F5] and in (b) through [F6] and [F7]; the full Axiom of Choice is used in (b) through [F6] and its Sobolev suppliers. No empirical claim is made anywhere. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 7.1, F2, F5, F6, F7] ∎

## Remarks

- **What "reachability" does and does not mean here.** The two branches solve the specific PDE problems of their declared data; they do not assert that every pair can be brought to a named gauge with arbitrary data. In particular the all-space branch fixes zero gauge Cauchy data at $t_0$, and the bounded branch controls the boundary family but not initial gauge data; the older global promise for a bounded domain with prescribed data is outside the statement.
- **Choice accounting.** Branch (a) carries only $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, through the free-space wave solution. Branch (b) carries AC and $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, declared by the variational, chart and Sobolev suppliers of [F6]; the Green identity [F7] also assumes $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$. Branch (c) uses no choice principle: its construction is the explicit radial witness and a one-variable primitive.
- **Exact supplier use.** Branch (b) consumes the authored parameter-dependent solver [[lem-em-parameter-dependent-smooth-poisson-boundary-solvers]] at the interface quoted in [F6]. The earlier fixed-time elliptic theorem alone does not supply smooth dependence on time or one smooth ambient continuation; those conclusions belong to this dedicated supplier.
