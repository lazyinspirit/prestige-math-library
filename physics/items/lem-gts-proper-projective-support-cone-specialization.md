#### 2. A flat countable module criterion, with proof

For a module M over a Noetherian ring define its tensor-product condition to be injectivity of

    M tensor_R product_j Q_j -> product_j (M tensor_R Q_j)    (ML)

for every family of R-modules. We prove the only consequences needed below.

If M is flat, condition (ML) is equivalent to the following minimum-submodule property: for every finite free F and x in `F tensor M`, there is a smallest submodule L of F with `x in L tensor M`. In one direction take the intersection of all such L, and map F to the product of the quotients F/L. Flatness identifies the kernel after tensoring with the tensor of that intersection; (ML) forces x into it. Conversely, given an element of the left-hand side of (ML) mapping to zero on the right, write it using finitely many m_i, regard these as x in `R^n tensor M`, and use the least L. Each coordinate homomorphism `R^n->Q_j` kills L, since its kernel is one of the submodules containing x after tensoring. Therefore their joint map to the product kills L, proving injectivity. All submodules of finite free F are finitely presented by Noetherianity.

A countably generated flat M is a sequential direct limit of finite free modules F_n. Here is a sufficient explicit construction. Map an initial finite free module to finitely many selected generators. Its kernel is finitely generated because R is Noetherian. The finite-equations criterion of flatness factors this finite map through another finite free module in such a way that the finitely many kernel relations become zero; include the next selected generator in the same factorization. Iterate. Every selected generator enters the system, and every kernel relation at a finite stage dies at the next, so the resulting colimit is exactly M.

To verify the finite-equations factorization explicitly, present the finitely generated image N as `R^k --A^t--> R^n ->N->0`, and let m in M^n be the images of the n generators. They satisfy `A m=0`. Flatness gives `ker(A tensor M)=ker(A) tensor M`; thus `m=sum_l v_l tensor m'_l` for finitely many v_l in ker(A). The matrix with columns v_l gives a map `N->R^l` because its transpose kills every relation A^t, and `R^l->M`, sending its generators to m'_l, recovers the original map. This proves the factorization and kills the entire prescribed kernel. It does not assert that N itself is flat.

Assume now (ML). For a fixed n, let x be the tensor in `F_n^* tensor M` corresponding to the structural map F_n->M. Let L be its least submodule. It is finitely generated. Lift a finite expression of x in `L tensor M` to a sufficiently late F_m, and enlarge m so that its equality with the actual transition-map tensor already holds at that stage. Then the image of `F_m^*->F_n^*` is contained in L. Conversely every such image contains L by minimality. Hence these images equal L for all sufficiently large m. After tensoring with any module N, the images of

    Hom(F_m,N) -> Hom(F_n,N)

are therefore stationary as well: both are the image of `L tensor N` in `F_n^* tensor N`. Thus the countable inverse system `Hom(F_n,N)` is Mittag-Leffler for every N.

For any exact sequence `0->N_1->N_2->N_3->0`, the Hom sequences for F_n are exact because F_n is finite free. The actual imported countable Mittag-Leffler inverse-limit theorem makes their inverse-limit sequence exact. Since `Hom(M,N)=lim Hom(F_n,N)`, the functor Hom(M,-) is exact. Hence M is projective.

We will also use faithful-flat descent in this countable setting. If R->R' is faithfully flat, M is countably generated flat, and `M tensor R'` is projective, apply the preceding finite-free system. Over R', projectivity implies (ML), since it is a direct summand of a free module, and free modules satisfy (ML) by their finite-support coordinates. Thus its dual-map images stabilize. Formation of those images commutes with flat base change, and faithful flatness reflects equality of their submodules. The original images stabilize as well. The preceding Hom/inverse-limit proof then makes M projective. This proves the needed descent; it is not an invocation of descent for arbitrary infinite modules without hypotheses.

#### 3. Products and completed free sums

For any set J the product `R^J` is flat when R is Noetherian: for a finitely generated ideal I, finite presentation gives `I tensor R^J=I^J`, and the map to R^J is coordinatewise injective. The ideal criterion for flatness proves the assertion. It also satisfies (ML). Indeed, for finite free F, `F tensor R^J=F^J`; the smallest submodule containing a tuple after tensoring is precisely the submodule of F generated by its coordinate vectors. Every submodule of F is finitely presented, so its tensor with R^J is its coordinate product, proving the minimum-submodule property of Section 2.

Now let R be complete along an ideal I and let `F=direct-sum_J R`. Its completion Fhat is the submodule of R^J consisting of families tending I-adically to zero: for every n only finitely many coordinates are nonzero modulo I^n. The natural map `Fhat->R^J` is universally injective. To prove this, test on a finite module Q, with a finite presentation `R^k ->R^m ->Q->0`. A vector in `Fhat^m` that vanishes in `Q tensor R^J` has each coordinate vector in the image of this relation matrix. Artin–Rees gives one constant c such that a relation vector lying in `(I^n)^m` has a preimage in `(I^(n-c))^k`. Choose these preimages coordinatewise. They still tend I-adically to zero, so lie in `Fhat^k` and show that the original element was already zero in `Q tensor Fhat`. Finite Q suffice because every module over Noetherian R is a filtered union of finite modules and tensor commutes with those unions.

A universally injective submodule of a flat module is flat, by the ideal tensor criterion. A universally injective submodule of an (ML) module also satisfies (ML), by the commutative diagram with the two tensor/product maps and its injective top and bottom horizontal arrows. Consequently Fhat is flat and (ML), being a pure submodule of the flat (ML) module R^J.

#### 4. Completion when the modulo-I module is projective

Let M be flat over an I-adically complete Noetherian R, with M/IM projective over R/I. Then Mhat is a direct summand of a completed free module.

First, for a nilpotent ideal J, a flat module L with L/JL projective is projective. Lift a free-module idempotent presenting L/JL to a column-finite matrix over R. The error `e^2-e` lies in the nilpotent matrix ideal. The polynomial correction `e -> e+(1-2e)(e^2-e)` squares the order of this error; finitely many iterations give an actual idempotent. Its image P is a projective lift. A homomorphism P->L lifting the modulo-J isomorphism exists by projectivity of P. Its cokernel C has C=JC and hence C=0 by nilpotence. Its kernel K has K/JK=0 by flatness of L, and hence K=0 by nilpotence. Thus P=L. Column-finite matrices remain column-finite under these finitely many polynomial operations, so no finite-rank hypothesis is being inserted.

Choose a free surjection F->M with kernel K. Flatness gives exact sequences modulo I^n. The preceding nilpotent lifting makes `M/I^n M` projective over `R/I^n` for every n. Choose compatible splittings of these sequences. To extend a splitting from n to n+1, start with any splitting at n+1. Its discrepancy modulo n is a map to `K/I^n K`; lift that map to `K/I^(n+1)K` using projectivity of the domain over R/I^(n+1), and correct the splitting. The inverse limits then split

    Fhat = Khat direct-sum Mhat.

Section 3 makes Fhat flat and (ML), and these properties pass to its summand Mhat. This proves the required completion assertion.

#### 5. Pure completion for the algebra B in the complete-base case

Suppose R is Noetherian and I-adically complete, B is finite type flat with nonempty geometrically integral fibers, and B/IB is projective over R/I. We prove B is projective.

For a prime p of R, the ring B/pB is a domain. It is flat over the domain R/p, so injects into its generic fiber, which is integral by the fiber hypothesis. For every finite R-module Q, choose a prime filtration. Tensoring with B remains exact, and gives a filtration of `Q tensor B` with factors `B/pB`. Consequently every associated prime of this finite B-module is some pB. Since I lies in the Jacobson radical of the complete ring R, `p+I` is proper. A maximal ideal of R containing it lifts to B because every fiber is nonempty. Hence `pB+IB` is proper as well.

Put N=`Q tensor B` and let T be the kernel of `N->Nhat`. It is `intersection_n I^n N`. At each associated prime q=pB of N choose a prime q' containing q+IB. The local Krull-intersection theorem at q' gives `T_q'=0`, and then `T_q=0`. A finite module injects into the product of its localizations at its associated primes; this follows by taking an associated prime of a hypothetical nonzero kernel. Thus T=0. The map

    Q tensor B -> Q tensor Bhat -> (Q tensor B)hat

has injective composite, so its first arrow is injective. This holds for every finite Q, hence B->Bhat is universally injective.

By Section 4, Bhat is (ML), since B/IB is projective. Therefore its pure submodule B is (ML). B is countably generated over R by the finitely many algebra generators' monomials. Section 2 now proves that B is projective. This is the complete-base purity argument specialized to the algebra actually needed; it avoids importing a general associated-prime theorem for arbitrary flat modules.

#### 6. Noetherian induction gives the general theorem

Suppose the theorem fails. Among ideals J of R for which B/JB is not projective over R/J, choose a maximal one; ascending chains terminate. Replace R,B by these quotients. Now B/JB is projective for every nonzero ideal J of the new R, but B is assumed not projective.

R must be reduced. Otherwise its nonzero nilradical is nilpotent, its corresponding quotient satisfies projectivity by maximality, and the nilpotent lifting argument in Section 4 makes B projective, a contradiction.

Choose a minimal prime of the reduced R and an element g vanishing on all other irreducible components but not on this one. Then R_g is a domain. #### 1. The isolated-point descent datum is effective

#### 2. Exact source-cover reduction, including nilpotents

For any family of maps `f:C->Y` from proper nodal curves, put

    D = C times_Y p,   C_V = C times_Y V.

The etale C_V->C is a scheme morphism and is an isomorphism after restriction to D. #### 3. A coherent-quotient reduction and its precise flatness limitation

#### 5. A genuine sufficient criterion for reducing a genus-zero mapping stack to a scheme target

Here is the family proof. A finite etale cover of a geometric nodal genus-zero tree is a disjoint union of copies of that tree. Normalize its components: a connected finite etale cover of P1 in characteristic zero has degree one by Riemann–Hurwitz. The componentwise trivial sheets are glued at nodes; because the dual graph is a tree, sheet permutations can be removed successively from a root, leaving trivial global sheets.

#### 2. Normal modifications and patching

First blow up a coherent ideal defining the complement of U, then normalize. Its exceptional ideal is invertible Cartier; work on finitely many affine charts where it has a nonzero equation a. #### 3. Universal injectivity and the exact strict-transform sequence

Let A be a Noetherian domain, B a flat finite-presentation A-algebra with nonempty geometrically integral fibers, and N a finite B-module. Suppose an actual map

    alpha:B^r -> N

is an isomorphism at the generic point pB of every fiber over Spec A. Then alpha is universally injective over A. For a finite A-module Q, a prime filtration and flatness of B give a filtration of `Q tensor B^r` with factors that are sums of `B/pB`. Each B/pB is a domain: it is flat over the domain A/p and injects into its integral generic fiber. Thus the associated primes of `Q tensor B^r` are among pB. The kernel of alpha tensor Q localizes to zero at all of them, since alpha itself is an isomorphism there. A nonzero finite submodule would have an associated prime of the containing finite module, a contradiction. Hence that kernel is zero. Finite Q suffice for all Q by filtered unions. This proves universal injectivity directly.

Put H=coker(alpha). We have a universally exact sequence

    0 -> B^r -> N -> H -> 0.                               (1)

Since B is A-flat, N is A-flat if and only if H is A-flat: if H is flat use extension of flat modules; in the other direction universal injectivity and the Tor sequence give flatness of H.

After any admissible base modification with exceptional equation c, let B',N_b,H_b be the ordinary pullbacks. Universal injectivity makes (1) exact after pullback and after quotienting by every base ideal `(c^k)`. Consequently, if `c^k n=alpha(v)`, then `v=c^k v'`: reduce the equality modulo c^k and use that injectivity. Thus the preimage in N_b of c-power torsion in H_b is exactly `alpha(B'^r)` plus c-power torsion in N_b. Since B'^r has no c-torsion, taking strict transforms gives the **exact** sequence

    0 -> B'^r -> N' -> H' -> 0.                            (2)

This proves the strict-transform sequence rather than assuming that arbitrary torsion quotients preserve exactness. The same argument works after normalizing the modification and on overlaps of exceptional frames.

#### 4. The local dimension-lowering construction

On a finite-projection chart write the smooth target as Spec B over Spec A, of relative dimension d, with nonempty geometrically integral fibers, and let N be the finite module obtained by finite pushforward of F. Its restriction over U is A-flat.

At the generic point pB of any fiber over U, N is free over B_pB. To check this, choose a residue-field basis and a surjection `B_pB^r->N_pB` by Nakayama. Its kernel stays exact modulo p by A-flatness of N, and is zero modulo the maximal ideal pB_pB; Nakayama kills that finite kernel. The free rank is locally constant as p varies over U: the freeness open on the smooth target maps openly to the base and meets the generic point of every integral fiber over that image. Apply the Fitting-coefficient construction to N. Its hypothesis that B be projective as an A-module is now supplied by the separate local proof. We obtain a blowup supported outside U, followed if necessary by its finite normalization, such that N is locally free of rank r on an open V of the smooth target meeting every fiber. Near any point s of the modified base choose r actual elements of N forming a basis at the generic point of its fiber. They define alpha:B^r->N. Every E_k,l is unit over U and contains a power of B_k; therefore H_k is coherent and unit over U, and (R) is an admissible normal projective modification. The pullback of H_k is an invertible Cartier ideal. Each factor E_k,l becomes an invertible Cartier ideal: locally, if a finite product of ideals is generated by a nonzerodivisor c, write c as a sum of products of generators and choose a summand whose coefficient relative to c is a unit. Cancelling the complementary factors proves that each factor is principal with a regular generator. Repeating this two-factor argument proves the finite-product assertion. Its regularity is preserved after etale pullback.

On each standard-etale chart over P_(k+1), identity (N) now makes Jbar_i,k an invertible Cartier ideal by the same factor argument. These charts cover T_i,k+1; invertibility is local, so the ideal is invertible on all of that scheme. The blowup universal property consequently gives an actual map

    T_i,k+1 -> Bl_(J_i,k) S_i^(k).

It remains to lift to its normalization, rather than assuming that dominating a blowup dominates its normalization. Each integral component of T_i,k+1 is normal and is an isomorphism over the dense U_i with the corresponding component of S_i. Its map to the displayed blowup is dominant and has the same function field: all maps restrict to that identity on U_i. If A_0 is an affine ring of the blowup and b is in its normalization, the pulled-back b lies in that common function field and is integral over every normal local source ring containing A_0. It therefore belongs to each such source ring. These extensions agree in the common field and glue uniquely, proving the factorization

    phi_i,k+1:T_i,k+1 -> S_i^(k+1).

The new maps are compatible with the preceding ones because they are maps over S_i^(k) and agree with the specified identity on dense U_i. This completes the explicit recursive step. After at most m stages following P_0, P_m dominates all given local normalized sequences. Its finite composite map to S is projective and remains an isomorphism on U.

#### 6. Preserve completed flatness and identify the composite strict transform

Let a coherent strict-transform module be flat over a completed local normal stage. Pulling it to any further base is flat. On the further normal integral base the pullback boundary has a nonzerodivisor equation c. Multiplication by c is injective on a flat base-module, since it is obtained by tensoring the injection O --c--> O. Thus it has no c-power torsion and its further strict transform is its ordinary pullback. Later global stages cannot destroy the flatness achieved on an earlier branch.

Successive strict transforms equal the strict transform for the composite modification. Locally write the boundary ideal as (a). The kernel removed in an earlier strict transform is a-power torsion, and its pullback is still killed locally by a power of the pulled-back a (finite generation supplies such a power). Quotienting it and then removing all remaining a-power torsion therefore gives precisely the quotient of the total ordinary pullback by all sections killed after inverting a. This is the composite strict transform. The same description holds on overlaps of boundary trivializations and after each finite normalization. Hence the local flat modules used at the end are the actual restrictions of one global strict-transform module, not unrelated replacements with the same generic restriction.

Flatness is local on source and base for the faithful etale covers in question. The final covering refinements therefore descend their flatness to the global strict transform. This proves the requested finite-sequence global join with the original U, its actual nilpotent module structures and the allowed finite normalizations retained.

#### 2. Product-ideal domination, including its nonzerodivisor condition

Let A be a local ring and let J,K be finitely generated ideals such that `JK=(c)` with c a nonzerodivisor. Write `c=sum_l j_l k_l`. Since each product belongs to `(c)`, write `j_l k_l=c u_l`, where `sum_l u_l=1`. Some u_l is a unit. For this pair j,k we have `jk=c u`, so both j and k are nonzerodivisors. For any j' in J, `j'k=cv` for some v. Cancel k from `j'k=jk(u^-1 v)` to get `j'=j(u^-1v)`. Hence J=(j), and similarly K=(k). Both are invertible Cartier ideals.

It follows that blowing up a product of finitely many ideals makes each factor invertible, provided the total exceptional ideal is Cartier, as it is on the actual blowup charts. The universal property gives factorization through each individual blowup. This is not a claim that a product principal with a zero-divisor generator has the same consequence.

For a U-admissible blowup with exceptional local equation a, strict transform of a module is `pullback/module a-power torsion`. If that module is flat over the new base, a is a nonzerodivisor on the module whenever it is one on the base; hence every further strict transform is its ordinary pullback. Flatness is then retained by every further admissible blowup. Transitivity of strict transform follows by taking the quotient by sections killed on the common dense U: the two successive torsion quotients and the total torsion quotient have precisely that same universal property.

#### 3. Etale norm ideals: a complete descent calculation

Let A be Noetherian, a a nonzerodivisor, B an etale finite-presentation A-algebra, and J a finitely generated ideal of B with `V(J) subset V(aB)`. Near any chosen prime of B, a standard etale presentation writes `B=C_h`, with C finite free of rank d over A, of the form `A[T]/(F)` for F monic, and with etaleness at the selected h-localized branch. Lift finitely many generators of J to C, clearing h denominators. Since a lies in the radical of J, adjoin `a^m` to these generators for a suitable m. Let K be their generated ideal in C, so `K B=J` and `a^m in K`.

Put `I=Fitt_0^A(C/K)`. It is finite type. Multiplication by a^m kills C/K, whose underlying A-module is a quotient of the free rank-d module C. The presentation using the d relations `a^m e_i` shows `a^(md) in I`. Thus `V(I) subset V(a)`.

Base-change the finite algebra C to B. The multiplication/selected-branch map `C tensor_A B -> B` is the chosen etale section at the selected branch. Since the whole algebra is finite over B, that etale section is both open and closed. After the stated localization it splits the algebra as

    C tensor_A B = B times C'.

Consequently `(C/K) tensor_A B = (B/J) direct-sum N` for a finite B-module N. Fitting ideals commute with arbitrary base change and multiply on direct sums; applying their matrix minors gives

    I B = J times Fitt_0^B(N).

Thus, locally at each selected etale point, the prescribed ideal J is a factor of an ideal pulled back from A, with all ideals supported away from U=D(a). This proves the required ideal descent up to a complementary factor. It does not assume that J itself descends.

#### 4. The Fitting-coefficient calculation under its exact hypothesis

For this reconstructed algebra calculation let A be a Noetherian **domain**, a nonzero, and A->B a flat finite-presentation algebra with nonempty geometrically integral fibers. Thus B is a domain: it injects into its integral generic fiber. In this section **assume in addition** that B is a projective A-module. Let N be a finite-presentation B-module whose localization at each generic fiber point above D(a) is free of a fixed rank r. The domain restriction suffices for this calculation; no general nonreduced-base version of the Fitting argument is claimed here.

Embed B as a direct summand of a free A-module F. Every element of B has finitely many coordinates in this embedding. Let J be `Fitt_r^B(N)`, and let I_0 be the ideal of A generated by the coordinates of finitely many generators of J. This ideal contains all coordinates of all elements of J: multiplication by any b in B is A-linear on the summand B, extends by the summand projection to an A-linear map of F, and therefore carries an I_0-valued coordinate vector to an I_0-valued coordinate vector. Thus `J subset I_0 B`, and I_0 is the actual coefficient ideal, not an arbitrary chosen approximation.

Set `I=a I_0`. At a prime p with a not in p, the generic fiber point pB lies outside V(J), because N has generic fiber rank r. Hence some element of J has nonzero image in `B tensor_A kappa(p)`. Its coordinate vector has a coordinate outside p, so `I_0` is not contained in p. Therefore `V(I)=V(a)`.

On a blowup chart `A'=A[I/c]`, with c one of the finite generators of I, write c=a c_0. The ideal `I_0 A'=(c_0)`, with c_0 a nonzerodivisor. Every element of `J B'`, `B'=B tensor_A A'`, is divisible by c_0, using its coordinate vector. For a selected generic fiber prime `p' B'`, at least one normalized generator `j/c_0` is nonzero modulo p'B': some original generator's coordinate equals the chosen c_0, so its normalized coordinate vector has a unit coordinate. That normalized element is a unit **in the localization at p'B'**. It need not be a unit everywhere on B'. This qualification is essential.

Thus `J B'_(p'B')=(c_0)` as an invertible nonzerodivisor ideal. Here is the required Fitting matrix calculation. Over the local domain at p'B', write a presentation with n generators and relation matrix Q. The generic rank of Q is n-r. Choose an `(n-r)`-square pivot block D whose determinant generates the ideal of all such minors; one exists because that ideal is principal in this local ring. Every minor obtained by replacing a pivot row with a complementary row is divisible by det(D). Cramer's rule therefore expresses the corresponding Schur-complement map from the n generators to the r complementary generators with coefficients **in the local ring**, not merely its fraction field. This map is the identity on the complementary r generators. Every relation column maps to zero: its Schur-complement coordinates vanish over the fraction field by generic rank, hence vanish in the domain. It gives a surjection `N -> B^r` with kernel precisely the torsion module, since it is a generic isomorphism. Inverting det(D) kills that kernel, so each of its finitely many generators is killed by a power of det(D). Here det(D) is c_0 times a unit. Since c_0 divides c, the torsion is also c-power torsion. Conversely the free quotient has no c-torsion. Thus the strict transform N' is free of rank r at p'B', and hence in an open neighborhood of that point. This proves the local matrix assertion without assuming that a pointwise rank condition alone gives freeness before taking strict transform.
