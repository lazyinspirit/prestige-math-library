## Statement

Assume full AC. Let F be a fixed compact pointed curve with two outgoing coordinate disks of radii r and $\rho $, an admitted joint module W at its incoming marks, and an incoming mark on every component. Write $D=D_F(W)\subset W^*$ for the actual partial-block dual fusion product and $\gamma(w,d)=d(w)$. Let N be the two-point sphere with coordinates $1/z,z$ at infinity and zero, and place the two-component restricted contragredient D' on these points. Sewing F to N with parameters $p_1,p_2$, $0<|p_1|<r$, $0<|p_2|<\rho$, gives a curve canonically equal to the self-plumbing of F with $q=p_1p_2$. Fix logarithm lifts $\ell_1,\ell_2\in\mathbb C$ with $e^{\ell_i}=p_i$, and use $\ell=\ell_1+\ell_2$ as the logarithm lift of $q$. All complex powers and sewing evaluations below use these lifts on the universal covers of the punctured parameter disks. Then the map
$$\Gamma:\mathrm{CB}_N(D')\longrightarrow\mathrm{CB}_{F_q}(W),\qquad\chi\longmapsto\mathrm{Sew}_{p_1,p_2}(\gamma\otimes\chi)$$
is an isomorphism. Its inverse is the coefficient functional constructed in the Proof. If Q is the sphere with the same two outgoing points and one incoming point 1 carrying V, and $D_Q(V)$ its dual fusion product, the equivalent cylinder insertion map
$$\mathrm{CB}_F(W\otimes D_Q(V)')\longrightarrow\mathrm{CB}_{F_q}(W)$$
obtained by sewing to $\omega=\gamma_Q(\mathbf1,-)$ is also an isomorphism. Modules need not split into tensor factors.

## Facts & Assumptions

## Proof

1.1 Let $\widetilde y$ be F2's logarithmic lift of $q\partial_q$, and put $A(q)=\sum_i\operatorname{Res}h_i(q,t_i)Y_i(\omega,t_i)dt_i$ on W. Let $\kappa(q)$ be the holomorphic Schwarzian scalar constructed in F3 for this lift and a meromorphic projective coefficient on the normalization. The operator $q\partial_q-A(q)+\kappa(q)$ preserves the Ward ideal on the smooth locus by F2. It is a connection there after division by q. In one complex dimension it has no curvature two-form. For completeness, every cylinder sewing is parallel for its dual connection: the sphere Ward identity makes the completed vector corresponding to chi supported on diagonal weights, with equal nilpotent component actions. After multiplication by $p_1^{L_+(0)}p_2^{L_-(0)}$ it depends only on $q=p_1p_2$ and satisfies the annular residue balance, by moving a mode across the sphere pairing and conjugating by these powers. The two paired raw conformal residues of the lift therefore add with coefficients $a+b=1$ to its q derivative. The normalization conformal Ward identity of F3 then gives $q\partial_qS(w)=S(-A(q)w)+\kappa(q)S(w)$, precisely dual parallelism. This is the same finite-coefficient calculation as F3, with the sphere pairing in place of its identity coevaluation. There is no assumption that an arbitrary sewing is parallel for the uncorrected Virasoro operator. [F2, F3, algebra]


1.2 We first establish the finite-order extension actually needed below; F2 is not assumed to supply an outgoing-jet matching inverse. Fix a finite state cutoff and an incoming pole bound $k\ge k_0$, and set $E=V_{\le d,C}\otimes\omega_{C/\Delta}(kD_{\mathrm{in}})$ on a small disk about $q=0$. By F2 there are actual total holomorphic sections $s_1,\ldots,s_a$ whose central restrictions form a basis of $H^0(C_0,E_0)$. Put $C_h=(C_0,(\mathcal O_C/(q^{h+1}))|_{C_0})$ and $E_h=E/q^{h+1}E$; these symbols denote a finite ringed-space thickening only for this local argument, not an assumed base-change theorem for nonreduced bases. Multiplication by $q$ is injective on $E$: in a node chart $q=xy$ is a nonzero divisor in $\mathbb C\{x,y\}$, and off the node it is a coordinate. Consequently the kernel of $E_h\to E_0$ is canonically $qE_{h-1}$. For any global section $t$ of $E_h$, express $t|_{C_0}=\sum_i a_{i,0}s_i|_{C_0}$ and subtract this actual section. The difference divides uniquely by $q$ as a global section of $E_{h-1}$. Repeat finitely many times. This writes $t=\sum_{j=0}^h q^j\sum_i a_{i,j}s_i$ modulo $q^{h+1}$, so the polynomial combination on the right is an actual holomorphic extension. Uniqueness of division makes the local quotients glue. The argument uses actual central section surjectivity and a finite induction; no convergence of a formal correction or Banach matching inverse is required. [F2, construct, algebra]

1.3 The normalization interpolation used below also has a direct finite-dimensional proof. At either outgoing point allow a specified finite pole order and prescribe finitely many Laurent coefficients in a finite state-weight truncation. Let $Z$ be the sum of the two outgoing points. The kernel of this finite jet map is a finite-rank bundle whose filtration quotients are fixed line bundles further twisted by a fixed multiple of $-Z$ and by $kD_{\mathrm{in}}$. Since $D_{\mathrm{in}}$ meets every normalization component, increasing $k$ makes every quotient degree exceed the corresponding canonical degree. F5 gives zero $H^1$ for the kernel, first for its line quotients and then by the finite filtration. The jet exact sequence is therefore surjective on global sections. A linear right inverse exists because its target is finite dimensional. This proves simultaneous independent interpolation at both branches with finite incoming poles; it does not assume any coefficient belongs to D. [F1, F5, construct, algebra]

2.1 Given a block $\psi_{q_0}$ on any smooth fiber, extend it uniquely to a parallel block $\psi$ on the universal cover of the punctured q-disk, with its initial value specified at the chosen lift $\ell=\ell_1+\ell_2$ of $q_0$. Locally this is the finite matrix equation in a block-bundle frame: Picard iterated integrals converge on each relatively compact disk by their factorial bound. Finite chains of such disks give continuation along any compact path; uniqueness on overlap and subdivision of homotopies give the solution on the simply connected cover. F2's fixed finite generators give its evaluation column a finite Euler system $q\partial_q f=B(q)f$ with B holomorphic on the whole disk: reduce the finitely many images under the operator in step 1.1 modulo the global Ward span. This construction is valid even if this generating column has redundant coordinates. [F2, F3, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 We prove the regular-singular expansion needed here. Take a basis of the rank-s block space at $q_0$ and let Y(q) be its d-by-s evaluation matrix on the finite generators. Parallel continuation around zero gives an invertible s-by-s monodromy matrix T, with $Y(t+2\pi i)=Y(t)T$ for $q=e^t$. Choose a matrix C with $e^{2\pi iC}=T$: on each Jordan block write $T=\lambda(I+N)$ and use a logarithm of the nonzero scalar $\lambda $ and the finite series for $\log(I+N)$. Then $H(q)=Y(q)e^{-tC}$ is single valued and holomorphic on the punctured disk. It has at most a pole at zero. Indeed boundedness of B gives along every radial segment $\|Y(q)\|\le C_1|q|^{-C_2}$ by the integral inequality and its elementary exponential iteration. A bounded interval of arguments adds a uniform finite factor. The same polynomial bound, times a finite logarithmic power, holds for $e^{-tC}$ by its Jordan form. Thus $\|H(q)\|\le C_3|q|^{-C_4}$; the Cauchy integral for its Laurent coefficients makes all sufficiently negative coefficients zero. Hence $Y=H(q)e^{tC}$ has a convergent finite-exponent, uniformly finite-log expansion. It converges on the full punctured disk, since H is holomorphic there and meromorphic at zero. [step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Every W vector is a holomorphic combination of the same finite generators modulo global Ward relations, by F2. Multiplying their expansions by those holomorphic coefficients therefore gives $$\psi(w)=\sum_{n\in E+\mathbb N}\sum_{l=0}^L\psi_{n,l}(w)q^n(\log q)^l,$$ with one finite exponent-coset set and one L for all w. Combine equal exponents and choose distinct coset representatives. Coefficients are linear functionals on W and all indicated series converge absolutely locally uniformly. Such coefficients are unique: the deck shift on the finite log space has distinct eigenvalues $e^{2\pi i e}$ for distinct cosets, so finite polynomial spectral projectors separate the cosets. In one coset repeated finite differences in $\log q\mapsto\log q+2\pi i$ separate its logarithmic powers, and Taylor uniqueness separates the remaining integral powers. The same argument proves that the Ward identities for any total holomorphic nodal current $v(q)=\sum_{k\ge0}q^kv_k$ give $$\sum_{k\ge0}\psi_{m-k,l}(v_k\cdot w)=0.$$ For fixed w the residue action of v(q) has values in a fixed finite W-span, by its finite state and pole cutoffs. Therefore its convergent Taylor series may be paired termwise and regrouped; the displayed coefficient sum is finite by the common lower exponent bound. [F2, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Each coefficient $\psi_{n,l}$ belongs to the actual D of F1. If $\psi=0$, this is immediate; otherwise the finite-exponent expansion from step 3.1 has a nonempty representative set. Let $\mathcal E$ be the finite set of exponent representatives furnished by steps 3.1–4.1, put $\beta=\min_{\alpha\in\mathcal E}\operatorname{Re}\alpha$, and let $b$ be a common real lower bound for the generalized weights of D, supplied by F1. Set $\underline b=\min\{\beta,b\}$. For the fixed complex exponent n choose an integer $h\ge0$ with $h+1>\operatorname{Re}n-\underline b$. This real-part inequality ensures that an $O(q^{h+1})$ error cannot contribute to the coefficient at exponent n: by step 4.1 every term it multiplies has exponent $\alpha+m$ with $\alpha\in\mathcal E$ and $m\in\mathbb N$, so every resulting exponent has real part at least $\beta+h+1\ge\underline b+h+1>\operatorname{Re}n$. The bound also incorporates the module lower bound b; it does not require identifying the target coefficient as a module weight. Let sigma be any normalization current in the outgoing lattice at levels $(h,h)$, with arbitrary finite incoming poles. In the node current frame its two branch coefficients vanish to order at least h+1: a state of weight d contributes $x^{d+h}dx=x^{h+1}(x^ddx/x)$, and similarly on the other branch. Define the node section by the sum of these two one-variable series. On the exterior take sigma constant in q. Its matching error is $O(q^{h+1})$, since every crossed series monomial is $q^mx^{-m}$ or $q^my^{-m}$ with $m\ge h+1$. These exterior and node sections therefore glue modulo $q^{h+1}$ to a section of $E_h$, after choosing a finite state cutoff and an incoming pole bound containing $\sigma$ and at least $k_0$. Step 1.2 extends it to an actual total current $v(q)=\sigma+O(q^{h+1})$ on the exterior, with a finite incoming pole bound. Step 4.1 then says $\psi_{n,l}(\sigma\cdot w)=0$. Since h is fixed for the coefficient, this holds for every sigma in that lattice. Thus $\psi_{n,l}\in(W/J_{h,h})^*\subset D$. This proves membership, not merely formal outgoing truncation in W*. [F1, F2, step 4.1, step 1.2, construct]

5.2 If $\Gamma(\chi)=0$ at the chosen nonzero parameter, its cylinder sewing is a parallel section for step 1.1 and hence zero everywhere by finite ODE uniqueness. Sphere Ward for $L(0)$ makes chi supported on diagonal joint weights, with matching nilpotent actions. Its constant-log coefficient at $q^n$ is the vector $\chi_{[n,n]}\in D_{[n,n]}$ evaluated on W. Expansion uniqueness from step 4.1 and the injection $D\subset W^*$ make every such vector zero, hence chi zero. This proves injectivity independently of a dimension comparison. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 We next prove the annular balance relation for these D-valued coefficients. For every state u and every power series f(x,y), $$\operatorname{Res}_x f(x,q/x)Y_+(x^{L(0)-1}u,x)\sum_n\psi_{n,l}q^n\,dx=\operatorname{Res}_y f(q/y,y)Y_-(y^{L(0)-1}Ju,y)\sum_n\psi_{n,l}q^n\,dy,$$ where $J=e^{L(1)}(-1)^{L(0)}$. Here is the finite-jet justification. Work modulo each exponent coset times $q^{h+1}$; only finitely many coefficient vectors occur, all in D by step 5.1. Choose a branch jet order Q so large that every error at order Q annihilates those finitely many vectors under both outgoing residue actions, using their uniform state-weight pole cutoff in F1. On the normalization, lift the desired branch jets of the node current $f(x,y)x^{L(0)}u\,dx/x$ coefficientwise through q order h. Step 1.3 gives these lifts with finite incoming poles: for the finitely many q-coefficients under consideration prescribe the finite Laurent jets of the desired branch expressions through order Q in the normalized node frame. Their negative powers are bounded by the q-truncation, so this is a finite interpolation problem. Their branch errors have order Q. Add these two regular one-branch error series to the local node normal form, so the same-branch errors match exactly. Choose $Q>h$ as well. The remaining crossed errors are then $O(q^{h+1})$, so the tuple is an actual section modulo $q^{h+1}$. Step 1.2 extends it to a total nodal current without changing its first h exterior coefficients, after enlarging its finite incoming pole bound if necessary. Step 4.1 annihilates its incoming residue convolution. Apply the universal evaluation block of F1 to the same normalization coefficients: its incoming residues are minus the two outgoing actions. The chosen Q kills the branch errors on our finite coefficient vectors, leaving exactly the displayed difference, with the sign $dx/x=-dy/y$. This proves the identity modulo every h, hence coefficientwise, for arbitrary f; only finitely many coefficients of f enter each truncation. [F1, F2, F4, step 4.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 Define degree-indexed modes $u[k]=\operatorname{Res}z^{k+\operatorname{wt}u-1}Y(u,z)dz$, and define $u^o[k]$ with the opposite field. In step 6.1 use $f=x^k$ if $k\ge0$ and $f=y^{-k}$ if $k<0$, and make the substitution $y=z^{-1}$ on its right side. The opposite-field formula and residue inversion give $$u_+[k]\psi_{n,l}=u_-^o[k]\psi_{n-k,l}.$$ This explicitly keeps the shifted mode convention: $u[k]$ lowers weight by k, while $u^o[k]$ raises it by k. For the conformal vector this includes $L_+(0)\psi_{n,l}=L_-(0)\psi_{n,l}$. [F4, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 Write the lift near its fixture as $a(x,y)x\partial_x+b(x,y)y\partial_y$, with $a+b=1$. The Schwarzian Ward identity from F3 applied on the normalization to each coefficient field of the lift, and the parallel equation in step 1.1, give $$q\partial_q\psi=\sum_{m\ge0}q^m\big(\nu(v_m)_++\nu(v_m)_-\big)\psi.$$ Indeed the incoming term from the parallel equation is $-\psi(A(q)w)+\kappa\psi(w)$; the raw conformal Ward scalar says this is the paired outgoing action. Step 6.1 with $u=\omega$ moves the minus residue to the plus branch, where its coefficient adds to a. Their sum is one, so the right side is $L_+(0)\psi$, and also $L_-(0)\psi$. Comparing coefficients gives $$(L_\pm(0)-n)\psi_{n,l}=(l+1)\psi_{n,l+1}.$$ The finite chain in l proves $\psi_{n,l}\in D_{[n,n]}$, and its nilpotent conformal parts satisfy $N_\pm\psi_{n,l}=(l+1)\psi_{n,l+1}$. This derives both weights and logarithmic coefficients, rather than presuming that an expansion exponent is a module weight. [F3, step 1.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 Define $\chi\in(D')^*$ by $\chi(d')=\sum_n\langle\psi_{n,0},d'\rangle$. This is a finite sum for every d', since step 8.1 puts its summands in distinct diagonal joint weight blocks and d' has finite support. Step 7.1, summed over n and transposed in the restricted pairing, says $\chi(u_+^o[k]d')=\chi(u_-[k]d')$. These are precisely the two-point sphere Ward identities in coordinates $1/z,z$: Laurent monomials and the opposite transformation exhaust the global meromorphic currents there. Thus $\chi\in\mathrm{CB}_N(D')$. Moreover $$p_1^{L_+(0)}p_2^{L_-(0)}\psi_{n,0}=q^n\sum_{l=0}^L(\log q)^l\psi_{n,l},\qquad q=p_1p_2,$$ by the nilpotent recurrence in step 8.1. Finite weight-basis contraction in the sewing tensor therefore gives $\Gamma(\chi)=\psi|_{q_0}$ exactly. Ordinary convergence in F3 justifies its analytic evaluation. This is the promised explicit right inverse. [F3, F4, step 7.1, step 8.1, construct]

10.1 Finally the representing property for D identifies $\mathrm{CB}_F(W\otimes D_Q(V)')$ with $\operatorname{Hom}(D_Q(V)',D)$. Restricted transposition identifies this with $\operatorname{Hom}(D',D_Q(V))$. The representing property for $D_Q(V)$ followed by vacuum evaluation identifies the latter space with $\mathrm{CB}_N(D')$, using F4's actual propagation isomorphism. On arguments these maps are simply composition with $\omega=\gamma_Q(\mathbf1,-)$, so finite coefficient contraction shows that their composite with Gamma is exactly the asserted cylinder insertion sewing. It is therefore an isomorphism as well. No rationality, simple-object expansion or finite tensor-category theorem is needed. [F1, F3, F4, step 9.1, step 5.2, algebra] ∎
