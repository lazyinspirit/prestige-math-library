## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice (AC). Let $A$ be a commutative ring and let
$E\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}A$ be a constructible subset
([[def-constructible-subset-scheme]]). Call $E$ **stable under specialisation**
if every specialisation of a point of $E$ lies in $E$, and **stable under
generalisation** if every generalisation of a point of $E$ lies in $E$
([[def-specialisation-and-generic-point]]).

1. If $E$ is stable under specialisation, then $E$ is closed in
   $\operatorname{Spec}A$.
2. If $E$ is stable under generalisation, then $E$ is open in
   $\operatorname{Spec}A$.

Both assertions are formulated for subsets of an affine spectrum; no other
hypothesis is imposed on $A$, and $A$ may be the zero ring, whose spectrum is
empty.

## Facts & Assumptions


**Given:** The data and hypotheses displayed in the Statement, with the conventions fixed there.

[F1] For a topological space $X$ an open $U\subseteq X$ is retrocompact when
$U\cap V$ is quasi-compact for every quasi-compact open $V$, and a subset is
constructible when it is a finite union of sets $U\cap(X\setminus V)$ with
$U,V$ retrocompact open; the empty union is allowed. On
$\operatorname{Spec}A$ the retrocompact opens are exactly the quasi-compact
opens, so a subset there is constructible exactly when it is a finite union of
sets $D(f)\cap V(\text{finitely many elements})$
([[def-constructible-subset-scheme]]).

[F2] For a ring $A$ the points of $\operatorname{Spec}A$ are the prime
ideals; the basic opens $D(f)=\{\mathfrak p:f\notin\mathfrak p\}$ form a basis
of the topology; for $A=0$ the spectrum is empty
([[def-affine-scheme-spectrum]]).

[F3] For $T\subseteq A$ the vanishing set is
$V(T)=\{\mathfrak p:T\subseteq\mathfrak p\}$, and $V(T)=V((T))$ for the ideal
$(T)$ generated by $T$ ([[def-prime-spectrum-and-vanishing-sets]]).

[F4] For $f\in A$ the distinguished open $D(f)=\{\mathfrak p:f\notin\mathfrak p\}$
is the complement of $V((f))$ ([[def-principal-distinguished-subset-of-spectrum]]).

[F5] A ring homomorphism $\varphi:A\to B$ induces the continuous contraction
map $\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A$,
$\mathfrak q\mapsto\varphi^{-1}\mathfrak q$
([[def-morphism-affine-schemes-from-ring-map]]).

[F6] For an ideal $I\trianglelefteq R$ the quotient map $R\to R/I$ induces a
bijection $\operatorname{Spec}(R/I)\to V(I)$ by contraction, with inverse
sending a prime $\mathfrak p\supseteq I$ to $\mathfrak p/I$
([[thm-prime-spectrum-of-a-quotient-bijection]]).

[F7] For $f\in R$ the localisation map $R\to R_f$ induces a homeomorphism
from $\operatorname{Spec}(R_f)$ onto the distinguished open $D(f)$
([[cor-principal-localisation-spectrum-is-distinguished-open]]).

[F8] For $r\ge1$ and rings $R_1,\dots,R_r$ with $R=\prod_iR_i$ the
distinguished opens $D(e_i)$ are pairwise disjoint and clopen,
$\operatorname{Spec}R=D(e_1)\sqcup\dots\sqcup D(e_r)$, and the morphism induced
by the projection $\pi_i:R\to R_i$ is an isomorphism of locally ringed spaces
from $\operatorname{Spec}R_i$ onto the open subscheme $D(e_i)$
([[lem-spectrum-of-a-finite-product-ring-is-a-disjoint-union]]).

[F9] A scheme is quasi-compact when its underlying topological space is
quasi-compact, that is, every open cover has a finite subcover; every affine
scheme is quasi-compact; a morphism $g:Z\to Y$ of schemes is quasi-compact
when $g^{-1}(V)$ is quasi-compact for every quasi-compact open $V\subseteq Y$
([[def-quasi-compact-and-quasi-separated-scheme]],
[[cor-affine-scheme-quasi-compact]],
[[def-quasi-compact-and-quasi-separated-morphism]]).

[F10] Assume AC. For every commutative ring $A$ and every $f\in A$ the
distinguished open $D(f)\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}A$ is quasi-compact
([[lem-basic-opens-quasi-compact]]).

[F11] A point $y$ is a specialisation of $x$ when $y\in\overline{\{x\}}$, and
then $x$ is a generalisation of $y$
([[def-specialisation-and-generic-point]]).

[F12] Assume AC. For a quasi-compact morphism $g:Z\to Y$ of schemes the
image $g(Z)$ is closed if and only if it is stable under specialisation in $Y$
([[lem-quasi-compact-scheme-image-specialization-closed]]).

[F13] The Axiom of Choice states that every family of nonempty sets has a
choice function ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F1] write $E=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\bigl(D(f_i)\cap V(J_i)\bigr)$, where $J_i\subseteq A$ is the ideal generated by the finitely many elements listed in the $i$-th vanishing set of that description; note $V(J_i)$ is the set of primes containing $J_i$ by [F3], and if the union is empty ($n=0$) then $E=\varnothing$ is closed and open and both assertions hold, so assume $n\ge1$. [F1, F3]

1.2 For each $i$ put $B_i=(A/J_i)_{f_i}$ and let $\varphi_i:A\to B_i$ be the composite homomorphism. The image of the induced map $\operatorname{Spec}B_i\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ of [F5] is exactly $D(f_i)\cap V(J_i)$: by [F6] the contraction $\operatorname{Spec}(A/J_i)\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ is a bijection onto $V(J_i)$, and by [F7] applied over the ring $A/J_i$ the localisation map $\operatorname{Spec}B_i\to\operatorname{Spec}(A/J_i)$ is a homeomorphism onto the primes of $A/J_i$ not containing the image of $f_i$, which under the bijection of [F6] correspond to the primes of $A$ containing $J_i$ and avoiding $f_i$. [F4, F5, F6, F7]

1.3 The morphism $\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ is quasi-compact: if $U\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}A$ is a quasi-compact open, then by [F2] the sets $D(a)$ contained in $U$ cover $U$, and quasi-compactness [F9] gives $U=D(a_1)\cup\dots\cup D(a_r)$ for finitely many $a_j$; writing $\varphi:A\to B$ for the structure homomorphism, the preimage of $U$ is $D(\varphi(a_1))\cup\dots\cup D(\varphi(a_r))$, each $D(\varphi(a_j))$ is quasi-compact by [F10], and a finite union of quasi-compact spaces is quasi-compact because an open cover of the union restricts to an open cover of each piece, for which a finite subcover exists. [F2, F9, F10]

1.4 For assertion 2 assume that $E$ is stable under generalisation. Then $X\setminus E$ is stable under specialisation: if $x\in X\setminus E$ and $x'\in\overline{\{x\}}$, then $x'$ is a specialisation of $x$ by [F11], and $x'\in E$ would make the generalisation $x$ of $x'$ a point of $E$ by stability under generalisation, a contradiction; hence $x'\in X\setminus E$. [F11, given]

1.5 The complement $X\setminus E$ is constructible. Indeed, by [F1] retrocompact opens are closed under finite intersections, since $U\cap V\cap W=U\cap(V\cap W)$ with $V\cap W$ quasi-compact for retrocompact $U,V$ and quasi-compact open $W$, and under finite unions, since $(U\cup V)\cap W=(U\cap W)\cup(V\cap W)$ is a union of two quasi-compact spaces; also $X$ itself is retrocompact, as $X\cap W=W$. Hence for a piece $P=U\cap(X\setminus V)$ the complement is $X\setminus P=(X\setminus U)\cup V=\bigl(V\cap(X\setminus U)\bigr)\cup\bigl(X\cap(X\setminus(U\cup V))\bigr)\cup\bigl((U\cap V)\cap(X\setminus\varnothing)\bigr)$, a union of three pieces of the same form: the first covers $V\setminus U$, the second $X\setminus(U\cup V)$, and the third $U\cap V$. The intersection of two pieces is $(U\cap(X\setminus V))\cap(U'\cap(X\setminus V'))=(U\cap U')\cap(X\setminus(V\cup V'))$, again a piece. Therefore finite unions of pieces are closed under finite unions, finite intersections and complements, by distributivity and De Morgan, so the complement of the constructible set $E$ is a finite union of pieces, hence constructible. [F1]

2.1 Put $B=\prod_{i=1}^{n}B_i$. By [F8] the spectrum $\operatorname{Spec}B$ is the disjoint union of the clopen pieces $D(e_i)\cong\operatorname{Spec}B_i$, and under these identifications the map $\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ induced by the homomorphism $A\to B$, $a\mapsto(\varphi_i(a))_i$, restricts on the $i$-th piece to the map $\operatorname{Spec}B_i\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ of step 1.2; since the image of a disjoint union is the union of the images of its pieces, step 1.2 gives $\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A)=E$. [F5, F8, step 1.2]

3.1 Assume now that $E$ is stable under specialisation in the sense of the Statement. By steps 2.1 and 1.3 the set $E$ is the image of the quasi-compact morphism $\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A$, so assertion (1) of [F12] gives that $E$ is closed; this proves assertion 1. [F12, step 2.1, step 1.3]

4.1 Apply assertion (1), proved in step 3.1, to the constructible subset $X\setminus E$ of $\operatorname{Spec}A$: it is stable under specialisation by step 1.4, hence closed, so $E$ is open, proving assertion 2. The case $n=0$ of step 1.1 covers $E=\varnothing$, and $E=X$ is handled by the same argument with the empty constructible complement; the Axiom of Choice is assumed in the Statement and used exactly through [F10] and [F12], both of which declare it, while all remaining steps are choice-free. [F13, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 1.4, step 1.5] $\square$
