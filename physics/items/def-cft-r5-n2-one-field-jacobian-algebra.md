## Definition

Let $\mathbb C$ be the complex field and let $\mathbb C[X]$ be the polynomial ring of finitely supported complex coefficient sequences, with coefficientwise addition and convolution multiplication. The field structure makes $\mathbb C$ a commutative ring, and these operations make $\mathbb C[X]$ a commutative ring; this ring structure is the one used in the quotient below. Let $W\in\mathbb C[X]$. Define the formal derivative by linearity and $(X^n)'=nX^{n-1}$ for $n\ge1$, with $1'=0$. The notation $(W'(X))$ means the ideal generated by the single polynomial $W'(X)$; in this commutative polynomial ring it is exactly $\{Q(X)W'(X):Q\in\mathbb C[X]\}$. The **one-field Jacobian algebra** is the quotient ring
$$
\operatorname{Jac}(W):=\mathbb C[X]/(W'(X)).
$$
This is a purely algebraic definition. Its use as the physical protected chiral ring of a Landau–Ginzburg theory requires the separate chiral-ring prescription stated in the model item; the quotient definition alone makes no such identification.
