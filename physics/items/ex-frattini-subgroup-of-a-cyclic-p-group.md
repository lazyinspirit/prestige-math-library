## Example

If $P=\langle g\rangle$ has order $p^n$ with $n\ge1$, then $\Phi(P)=\langle g^p\rangle$ and $d(P)=1$. For $n=1$, this says $\Phi(P)=1$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A cyclic group $P=\langle g\rangle$ of order $p^n$ with $n\ge1$.

[L1] For every finite $p$-group $P$, $\Phi(P)=P'P^p$ ([[thm-frattini-subgroup-formula-for-a-finite-p-group]]).

[L2] If $G=\langle g\rangle$ has finite order $m$, then $\operatorname{ord}(g^a)=m/\gcd(a,m)$ ([[lem-order-of-a-power-in-a-finite-cyclic-group]]).

[F1] The subgroup generated by a subset is the smallest subgroup containing it, and a group is cyclic when it is generated by one element ([[def-generated-subgroup]]).

[L3] Every subgroup of a cyclic group is cyclic ([[thm-subgroups-of-cyclic-groups-are-cyclic]]).

[F2] The generator rank $d(P)$ is the common size of a basis of $P/\Phi(P)$ ([[def-generator-rank-of-a-finite-p-group]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Every $p$th power in $P$ is a power of $g^p$, and conversely $g^p$ is a $p$th power, so [F1] gives $P^p=\langle g^p\rangle$. The group is abelian, so $P'=1$. By [L2], $\langle g^p\rangle$ has order $p^{n-1}$, including order one at $n=1$, and [L3] is consistent with this cyclic subgroup description. [given, L2, F1, L3, algebra]

2.1 Formula [L1] gives $\Phi(P)=\langle g^p\rangle$. The quotient has order $p$, so its nonidentity coset $g\Phi(P)$ is a one-vector basis; hence [F2] gives $d(P)=1$. [step 1.1, L1, F2, algebra] ∎
