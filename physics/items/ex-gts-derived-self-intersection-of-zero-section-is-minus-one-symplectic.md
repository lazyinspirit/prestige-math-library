## Example

Let $k$ be a characteristic-zero field and assume AC for the cotangent
constructions inherited from [[def-derived-scheme-and-cotangent-complex]]. Let
$F=T^*\mathbb A^1_k=\operatorname{Spec}k[x,p]$ with symplectic form
$\omega=d_{\mathrm{dR}}x\wedge d_{\mathrm{dR}}p$, and let
$i:X=\mathbb A^1_k\to F$ be the zero section $p=0$. Using $i$ for both
maps, the derived self-intersection
$$Z=X\times_F^hX$$
is represented by the commutative dg algebra
$$k[x,\eta],\qquad |x|=0,\quad |\eta|=-1,\quad \eta^2=0,\quad d\eta=0.$$
It carries the canonical closed $(-1)$-shifted symplectic form whose
underlying pairing is represented in these coordinates by
$d_{\mathrm{dR}}x\wedge d_{\mathrm{dR}}\eta$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the field $k$, the cotangent plane $F$, its standard symplectic form, and the zero section $i$.

[F1] [[def-derived-scheme-and-cotangent-complex]] supplies the derived-scheme and cotangent conventions. In the opposite derived-affine site of [[def-gts-derived-artin-stack-and-cotangent-complex]], an affine homotopy pullback is the opposite of an algebra homotopy pushout. HAG II Remark 1.1.0.9 identifies this pushout with a derived tensor product, computed by a cofibrant algebra resolution; §2.3.5.1 supplies the characteristic-zero normalization comparison for the nonpositive cdga calculation below. The local derived-scheme Definition alone does not state the tensor-product formula.

[F2] [[def-gts-closed-n-shifted-symplectic-form]]: a closed degree-$m$ 2-form is $m$-shifted symplectic when its adjoint $T\to L[m]$ is a quasi-isomorphism.

[F3] [[def-gts-derived-artin-stack-and-cotangent-complex]]: derived affine schemes are derived Artin stacks and their cotangent complexes represent derived derivations.

[F4] [[thm-gts-derived-intersection-of-lagrangians-is-shifted-symplectic]]: for two Lagrangian maps to an $n$-shifted symplectic derived Artin stack, the derived homotopy fiber product has the canonical closed $(n-1)$-shifted residue form. Its proof is conditional on its recorded local prerequisites; this example checks the exact instance used here.

[A1] Both maps in the self-intersection are the zero section, with the constant isotropic path from zero to $i^*\omega=0$.

[A2] AC is the foundational premise in the cotangent-comparison construction inherited from [[def-derived-scheme-and-cotangent-complex]].

## Verification

1.1 The cotangent plane has free cotangent complex generated by $dx$ and $dp$ in degree zero. Its form $dx\wedge dp$ has invertible pairing matrix $$\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},$$ so it is a closed $0$-shifted symplectic form. The zero section pulls it back to zero. The relative cotangent complex is the cofiber of $i^*L_F\to L_X$, the map $k[x]dx\oplus k[x]dp\to k[x]dx$ sending $dx\mapsto dx$ and $dp\mapsto0$; hence $L_{X/F}\simeq k[x]dp[1]$. The isotropic structure induces $T_X\to L_{X/F}[-1]$, sending $\partial_x$ to $dp$ up to the sign fixed by $\omega$. It is an isomorphism of free rank-one $k[x]$-modules, so each zero-section map is Lagrangian. [F2, F3, A1, A2, given, algebra]

2.1 To compute the derived self-intersection, resolve the second copy of $k[x]=k[x,p]/(p)$ over $k[x,p]$ by the Koszul dg algebra $K=k[x,p,\eta]$ with $|\eta|=-1$, $\eta^2=0$, and $d\eta=p$. The two-term complex $k[x,p]\eta\xrightarrow{p}k[x,p]$ is exact away from its cokernel $k[x,p]/(p)$ because multiplication by $p$ is injective. Thus $K$ is a cofibrant resolution. Tensoring it with the first $k[x]$ over $k[x,p]$ sets $p=0$, so $K\otimes_{k[x,p]}k[x]=k[x,\eta]$ with zero differential. This proves the stated model of $Z$. [F1, A2, step 1.1, given, algebra]

3.1 In the tangent fiber triangle for $Z\to X\times X\to F$, the degree-zero kernel is generated by the diagonal vector $(\partial_x,\partial_x)$, while the degree-one cokernel class comes from the normal vector $\partial_p$ and is identified with $\partial_\eta$ by the Koszul model in step 2.1. In the cotangent comparison of [F4], each zero-section Lagrangian map sends $\partial_x$ to its relative conormal class $dp$, and the ambient symplectic adjoint sends $\partial_p$ to $-dx$. The comparison therefore pairs $\partial_x$ with $d_{\mathrm{dR}}\eta[-1]$ and $\partial_\eta$ with $d_{\mathrm{dR}}x[-1]$, up to the Koszul sign convention; its underlying 2-form is $d_{\mathrm{dR}}x\wedge d_{\mathrm{dR}}\eta$. The construction in [F4] makes this a point of the closed-form space, not merely an ordinary pairing. [F2, F4, A2, step 2.1]

4.1 In the displayed cdga, $L_Z$ is free on $dx$ in degree $0$ and $d_{\mathrm{dR}}\eta$ in degree $-1$. Its tangent complex is dual, with $\partial_x$ in degree $0$ and $\partial_\eta$ in degree $1$. The residue pairing maps $\partial_x$ to $d_{\mathrm{dR}}\eta[-1]$ and $\partial_\eta$ to $dx[-1]$, up to the standard Koszul sign. These are degree-preserving isomorphisms between the two free rank-one summands of $T_Z$ and $L_Z[-1]$. Hence the adjoint $T_Z\to L_Z[-1]$ is a quasi-isomorphism, proving nondegeneracy. [F2, F3, A2, step 3.1]

5.1 Step 3.1 gives the closed residue form, and step 4.1 proves its nondegeneracy. Therefore $Z$ has the claimed canonical $(-1)$-shifted symplectic structure. The calculation is a mathematical derived intersection; it asserts no corresponding physical phase space or path-integral construction. [F2, F4, A2, step 3.1, step 4.1] ∎
## Remarks

The classical intersection is the entire zero section, and the degree-$-1$ generator is retained by the derived fiber product. Replacing the homotopy fiber product by the ordinary intersection would discard precisely the conormal direction paired by the shifted form.
