## Statement

Let V be a conformal vertex algebra of CFT type over C, with its actual Zhu algebra A(V). For every unital left A(V)-module U there is an admissible module M(U), generated by a copy of U in grade zero. Every A(V)-map from U into the singular space Ω(W) of a weak module W extends uniquely to a weak-module map M(U)→W. Here Ω(W) consists of vectors annihilated by every $a_{m}$ with a homogeneous and $m\ge \operatorname{wt}(a)$. The module M(U) has a largest graded submodule J(U) disjoint from U. For simple U the quotient $L(U)=M(U)/J(U)$ is simple as a weak module, and U↦L(U) is inverse to the normalized lowest-space correspondence on simple admissible modules. In addition, if V is rational in the specified admissible-module convention, A(V) is finite dimensional and semisimple, and every irreducible admissible V-module is ordinary. No reverse rationality criterion or general higher-level induction is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

AC is used in Step 2.1 to choose bases of the countable family of finite-dimensional state grades and hence a countable ordered basis for the current Lie algebra. The later PBW sorting uses that supplied basis. The largest submodule J(U) is defined as the sum of all graded submodules disjoint from U; no maximal-element principle is needed for that definition.

## Proof

1.1 Write [a] for a state class in A(V), and $d=\operatorname{wt}(a)$. Translation gives $[Tc]=-\operatorname{wt}(c)[c]$, whence $[T^{j} c]=(-1)^{j}(\operatorname{wt}(c))⋯(\operatorname{wt}(c)+j-1)[c]$. Skew-symmetry on V gives $[b*a]=\sum _{l\ge 0}$ $\operatorname{binom}(d-1,l)[a_{(l-1)}b]$. Here is its finite coefficient verification. The coefficient of $[a_{(l-1)}b]$, after expanding $e^{zT}Y(a,-z)b$ and reducing T, is $\sum _{i=0}^{min(\operatorname{wt}(b),l)}$ $(-1)^{i}$ binom(wt(b),i) $\operatorname{binom}(d+\operatorname{wt}(b)-i-1,l-i)=\operatorname{binom}(d-1,l)$. This is the coefficient of $x^{l}$ in $(1+x)^{d+\operatorname{wt}(b)-1}(1-x/(1+x))^{\operatorname{wt}(b)}=(1+x)^{d-1}$. Each state calculation is finite by truncation and the nonnegative state weights. Subtracting from [a*b] gives $[a][b]-[b][a]=\sum _{j\ge 0}$ $\operatorname{binom}(d-1,j)[a_{(j)}b]$. [F1, F2, F4, given, algebra]

1.2 For $k\ge 1$ put $C_{k}(a,b)=\sum _{i=0}^{d}$ $\operatorname{binom}(d,i)a_{(i-k)}b$. The actual higher-circle relation gives $[C_{k}]=0$ for $k\ge 2$, while $[C_{1}]=[a][b]$. These identities hold in the algebra itself, hence on every U, not only on already-existing module tops. [F1, given, algebra]

1.3 Let $D(S)=\oplus _{n\ge 0}S(n)^*$ be the direct sum of the full algebraic duals, with its natural pairing against S; no S(n) is assumed finite dimensional. For homogeneous $a\\in  V_{d}$ define $A(x)=e^{xL(1)}(-x^{-2})^{L(0)}$ and the opposite field $Y_{S}^{o}(a,x)=Y_{S}(A(x)a,x^{-1})$. Since L(1) lowers the nonnegative state weight, for $a\\in  V_{d}$
$$Y_S^o(a,x)=(-1)^d\sum_{j=0}^{d}\frac{x^{-2d+j}}{j!}Y_S(L(1)^j a,x^{-1}),\qquad a^o_{(m)}=(-1)^d\sum_{j=0}^{d}\frac{(L(1)^j a)^S_{(2d-j-m-2)}}{j!}.$$
Define the candidate dual modes by $a^{D}_{(m)}=(a^{o}_{(m)})^{t}$. An original mode of $L(1)^{j}$ a shifts S-degree by $(d-j)-(2d-j-m-2)-1=m+1-d$. Its transpose therefore sends $S(q)^*$ to $S(q+d-m-1)^*$. For fixed s∈S, $a^{o}_{(m)}s=0$ for all sufficiently negative m, since every original mode index 2d-j-m-2 then exceeds its truncation bound; thus $Y_{S}^{o}(a,x)s$ is upper-truncated. Every f∈D(S) has finite grade support, so the transpose of $a^{o}_{(m)}$ kills f for all sufficiently large m. The transpose modes thus define a lower-truncated candidate action on D(S), and the vacuum acts as the identity. This proves the required truncation directly, with no finite-dimensionality assumption on a grade. [F2, F4, F5, algebra]

1.4 We now derive the opposite-field Jacobi identity on S from the actual module Jacobi identity; this derivation is needed because the restricted-dual theorem below assumes finite-dimensional grades. Residues of the state Jacobi identity with ω give, for $j=0,1$,
$$[L(j),Y(a,z)]=\sum_{i=0}^{j+1}\binom{j+1}{i}z^{j+1-i}Y(L(i-1)a,z).$$
Thus $[L(0),Y(a,z)]=Y(L(0)a,z)+z∂_{zY}(a,z)$ and $[L(1),Y(a,z)]=Y(L(1)a,z)+2zY(L(0)a,z)+z^{2}∂_{zY}(a,z)$. Integrating these coefficientwise formal differential identities gives $b^{L(0)}Y(a,z)b^{-L(0)}=Y(b^{L(0)}a,bz)$ and
$$e^{uL(1)}Y(a,z)e^{-uL(1)}=Y\!\left(e^{u(1-uz)L(1)}(1-uz)^{-2L(0)}a,\frac{z}{1-uz}\right).$$
The second identity follows by differentiating both sides in u, using the displayed L(1) bracket, and matching their values at $u=0$; all state exponentials are finite because L(1) lowers nonnegative state weight. Combining the two identities yields the exact state-coordinate formula
$$A(s)Y(a,z_0)b=Y\!\left(A(s+z_0)a,-\frac{z_0}{s(s+z_0)}\right)A(s)b,$$
with $s+z_{0}$ expanded at $z_{0}=0$. Now fix homogeneous a,b, $s_{0}\\in  S(k)$, and $f\\in  S(ℓ)^*$. By the module Jacobi identity, the two matrix coefficients of the ordered products of $Y_{S}(a,t_{1}),Y_{S}(b,t_{2})$ and their iterate are the prescribed binomial expansions of one rational function. Here is the finiteness in this assertion: choose K with $a_{(j)}b=0$ for $j\ge K$, so the module commutator makes $(t_{1}-t_{2})^{K}$ times the two product orders equal. In the first order, truncation on the rightmost b-field bounds $t_{2}$ exponents below; in the reversed order, truncation on the rightmost a-field bounds $t_{1}$ exponents below. The fixed input and output grades force the sum of the two exponents to be fixed, so the common numerator is a Laurent polynomial. The iterate is its expansion after $t_{1}=t_{2}+t_{0}$: taking the two mode residues of the original Jacobi identity gives the coefficient formula in [F2] for every integer pair of mode indices, and its rightmost-mode sums are finite. Thus the third expansion is exactly the matrix coefficient of $Y_{S}(Y(a,t_{0})b,t_{2})$. Dividing by $(t_{1}-t_{2})^{K}$ gives the claimed rational function, with poles only at $t_{1}=0$, $t_{2}=0$, and $t_{1}=t_{2}$. This uses only mode truncation and the grade labels; it uses no sum over a basis of S(k). Replace $t_{i}$ by $x_{i}^{-1}$ and use the finite state $A(x_{i})$ on the inserted states. Inversion reverses the two product expansions: the $\iota_{x_1,x_2}$ expansion is $Y_S^o(b,x_2)Y_S^o(a,x_1)$, and the $\iota_{x_2,x_1}$ expansion is $Y_S^o(a,x_1)Y_S^o(b,x_2)$. On the iterate chart $x_{1}=x_{2}+x_{0}$ one has $t_{1}-t_{2}=-x_{0}/(x_{1}x_{2})$; the state-coordinate formula above identifies the transformed iterate with $Y_{S}^{o}(Y(a,x_{0})b,x_{2})$. For every Laurent monomial $Q=x_{1}^{q}$ $x_{2}^{r}(x_{1}-x_{2})^{s}$, the coefficientwise delta identity
$$x_0^{-1}\delta\!\left(\frac{x_1-x_2}{x_0}\right)\iota_{x_1,x_2}Q-x_0^{-1}\delta\!\left(\frac{x_2-x_1}{-x_0}\right)\iota_{x_2,x_1}Q=x_2^{-1}\delta\!\left(\frac{x_1-x_0}{x_2}\right)\iota_{x_2,x_0}Q(x_0+x_2,x_2)$$
follows by expanding each integer power with the designated binomial conventions and $δ(x)=\sum _{n\\in  Z}x^{n}$. The transformed rational function is a finite sum of such monomials with finite pole order, so linearity proves the same identity for it and every fixed coefficient is a finite sum. Functionals in $S(ℓ)^*$ separate S(ℓ), and hence this proves the full opposite-field Jacobi identity on S. The same $sl_{2}$ brackets give $A(x^{-1})A(x)=1$: conjugating $e^{xL(1)}$ by $(-x^{2})^{L(0)}$ gives $e^{-x^{-1}L(1)}$, which cancels $e^{x^{-1}L(1)}$. [F2, F4, F5, algebra]

For u∈U, projection of two fields is the expansion in $|z_{1}|>|z_{2}|$ of the rational expression $pY_{E}(a,z_{1})Y_{E}(b,z_{2})u$ $=$ $z_{1}^{-d}z_{2}^{-e}(A_{a} A_{b}+\sum _{j\ge 0}A_{a_{(j)}b}\sum _{r\ge 1}\operatorname{binom}(d-1+r,j)(z_{2}/z_{1})^{r})u$. For $r>0$, the a-mode lowers degree and annihilates U, so commuting it through the b-mode gives exactly the displayed coefficient. Put $w=(z_{1}-z_{2})/z_{2}$. The rational identity $\sum _{r\ge 1}\operatorname{binom}(d-1+r,j)(1+w)^{-r}=\sum _{i=0}^{j}$ $\operatorname{binom}(d,i)w^{i-j-1}$ is proved by summing over j with a new variable t: the left generating function is $\sum _{r\ge 1}(1+t)^{d-1+r}(1+w)^{-r}=(1+t)^{d}/(w-t)$. On the iterate side the state weight $\mathrm{wt}(a_{(n)}b)=d+e-n-1$ gives $pY_E(Y(a,z_0)b,z_2)|_U=z_2^{-d-e}H(w)$ for $z_0=z_2w$. Retaining all scalar factors, $$z_1^d pY_E(a,z_1)Y_E(b,z_2)|_U=z_2^{-e}F(w)=z_2^{-e}(1+w)^dH(w)=z_1^d pY_E(Y(a,z_0)b,z_2)|_U.$$ This is projected weak associativity on U with precisely the power defining the grade-zero defect. 3.2 Here are the powers that make the propagation work without cancellation of a binomial factor in a distribution space. Put $z_{1}=z_{0}+z_{2}$. All coefficient statements below use these conventions and only nonnegative extra powers of $z_{1}$. [F2, F5, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 For an unweighted defect D, the commutator of c(m) with its two field operators is $[c(m),D(a,b)]=\sum _{i\ge 0}\operatorname{binom}(m,i)z_{1}^{m-i}D(c_{(i)}a,b)+\sum _{i\ge 0}\operatorname{binom}(m,i)z_{2}^{m-i}D(a,c_{(i)}b)$. To check the iterate term, apply state Jacobi to $c(j)(Y(a,z_{0})b)$. Its two contributions are $Y(a,z_{0})c(j)b$ and $\sum _{i\ge 0}\operatorname{binom}(j,i)z_{0}^{j-i}Y(c(i)a,z_{0})b$. Summation over j gives the asserted $z_{1}$ factor because $\sum _{j\ge i}\operatorname{binom}(m,j)\operatorname{binom}(j,i)z_{2}^{m-j}z_{0}^{j-i}=\operatorname{binom}(m,i)(z_{2}+z_{0})^{m-i}$. This is binomial expansion, valid also for negative m. Only finitely many i remain because c(i)a and c(i)b vanish for sufficiently large i. For a fixed source degree, multiplication by the powers below makes each indicated coefficient finite, so this formal computation can be performed coefficientwise. [F2, step 3.2, algebra]

5.1 First fix $v=u\\in  U$. We prove p x $D_{d}(a,b;u)=0$ for every current-mode word x by induction on its length. The case $x=1$ is the rational identity proved above. 6.1 Now prove p x $D_{d+p}(a,b;v)=0$ for every homogeneous v∈E(U)(p), simultaneously for all preceding words x, by induction on the length of a positive-degree PBW word generating v from U. 7.1 Let I be the current-submodule generated by all coefficients of these $D_{d+p}$. Each coefficient relation is homogeneous for the mode-degree grading, so I is graded. The current bracket gives weak commutativity: if $a_{(j)}b=0$ for all $j\ge K$, then $(z_{1}-z_{2})^{K}$ $F(a,z_{1})F(b,z_{2})v=(z_{1}-z_{2})^{K}$ $F(b,z_{2})F(a,z_{1})v$. Indeed, the coefficient commutator formula expresses the field commutator as the finite sum of the fields $F(a_{(j)}b,z_{2})$ against the j-th derivatives of the formal delta; multiplication by $(z_{1}-z_{2})^{K}$ kills every derivative with $j<K$. The relations defining I give weak associativity on each homogeneous v∈M(U)(p):
$$(z_0+z_2)^{d+p}F(a,z_0+z_2)F(b,z_2)v=(z_0+z_2)^{d+p}F(Y(a,z_0)b,z_2)v.$$
Here $d=\operatorname{wt}(a)$, and the first product is expanded after $z_{1}=z_{0}+z_{2}$. Mode-degree shifts force the sum of the $z_{1},z_{2}$ exponents in $\langle λ,F(a,z_{1})F(b,z_{2})v\rangle $ to be fixed. The weak-commutativity equality therefore gives a common numerator $P(z_{1},z_{2})$ whose exponents are bounded below in both variables. Since their sum is fixed, P is a Laurent polynomial. Thus the two products are the expansions $ι_{z_{1},z_{2}}R$ and $ι_{z_{2},z_{1}}R$ of $R=P/(z_{1}-z_{2})^{K}$. $$z_0^{-1}\delta\!\left(\frac{z_1-z_2}{z_0}\right)\iota_{z_1,z_2}Q-z_0^{-1}\delta\!\left(\frac{z_2-z_1}{-z_0}\right)\iota_{z_2,z_1}Q=z_2^{-1}\delta\!\left(\frac{z_1-z_0}{z_2}\right)\iota_{z_2,z_0}Q(z_0+z_2,z_2)$$
holds by expanding the integer powers with their designated generalized-binomial expansions and using $δ(x)=\sum _{n\\in  Z}x^{n}$.
