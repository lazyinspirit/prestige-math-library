## Statement

Let $d\ge1$, let $S$ be a finite nonempty set with a fixed total order, and fix a bijective enumeration $x_1,x_2,\ldots$ of $\mathbb Z^d$. Put $\Omega=S^{\mathbb Z^d}$, give it the product topology, and let its cylinder sigma-algebra be generated by finite-coordinate assignments. Then the product topology is induced by
$$\rho(\sigma,\eta)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-k}\mathbf1_{\{\sigma_{x_k}\ne\eta_{x_k}\}},$$
$\Omega$ is compact, and its Borel sigma-algebra equals the cylinder sigma-algebra. For probability measures $\mu_n,\mu$ on this sigma-algebra, convergence of every finite-coordinate marginal is equivalent to weak convergence, where weak convergence means convergence of integrals of every bounded continuous real function on $\Omega$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite alphabet, site enumeration, product space, cylinder sigma-algebra and local weak convergence convention in the Statement.

[F1] [[def-csm-frontier-local-topologies]]: local weak convergence means convergence of every finite-coordinate marginal, equivalently every cylinder probability.

[F2] In the product topology a basic neighborhood fixes the spins on finitely many sites; every cylinder determined by a single finite assignment is both open and closed.

## Proof

1.1 If $\sigma\ne\eta$, some coordinate differs, so $\rho(\sigma,\eta)>0$; symmetry is immediate, and the coordinatewise triangle inequality gives the triangle inequality after summing. If two configurations agree on $x_1,\ldots,x_N$, then $\rho\le\sum_{k>N}2^{-k}=2^{-N}$. Conversely, if $\rho(\sigma,\eta)<2^{-N}$, no coordinate among $x_1,\ldots,x_N$ differs, because a mismatch at $x_k$ contributes at least $2^{-N}$. These bounds show that metric balls and finite-coordinate basic neighborhoods generate the same topology. [given, algebra]

1.2 If $\mu_n\to_{\mathrm{loc}}\mu$, every cylinder indicator is bounded and continuous by [F2], so its integrals converge. Every bounded function depending on finitely many coordinates is a finite linear combination of cylinder indicators, and its integrals therefore converge as well. [given, F1, F2]

2.1 Suppose an open cover of $\Omega$ has no finite subcover. Let $C_0=\Omega$. Recursively, if $C_{N-1}$ is the cylinder fixing the first $N-1$ spins and has no finite subcover, its finitely many children obtained by assigning $\sigma_{x_N}=s$ cannot all have finite subcovers; otherwise their finite union would cover $C_{N-1}$. Choose the least $s$ in the fixed order whose child still has no finite subcover and call that child $C_N$. These nested cylinders determine a configuration $\sigma$. A cover member containing $\sigma$ contains a basic neighborhood fixing finitely many sites; for sufficiently large $N$, $C_N$ lies inside that neighborhood and hence has a one-member subcover, a contradiction. Thus every open cover has a finite subcover and $\Omega$ is compact. [given, F2, step 1.1, construct]

2.2 Each cylinder is clopen by [F2], and cylinders form a countable basis: $\mathbb Z^d$ has countably many finite subsets, and each finite coordinate set has finitely many spin assignments. Therefore every open set is a countable union of cylinders and belongs to the cylinder sigma-algebra, while every cylinder is Borel. Hence the two sigma-algebras are equal. [given, F2, step 1.1]

3.1 Fix a bounded continuous $g:\Omega\to\mathbb R$ and $\varepsilon>0$. For each $\sigma$, continuity and the cylinder basis give a cylinder neighborhood $C_\sigma$ of $\sigma$ on which $|g(\eta)-g(\sigma)|<\varepsilon/3$. Compactness from step 2.1 gives a finite subcover $C_1,\ldots,C_r$. Let $F$ be the union of the finitely many coordinate sets fixed by these cylinders. If $\eta,\zeta$ agree on $F$, choose a $C_i$ containing $\eta$; because $C_i$ depends only on a subset of $F$, it also contains $\zeta$, so $|g(\eta)-g(\zeta)|<2\varepsilon/3$. Choose $N$ with $F\subseteq\{x_1,\ldots,x_N\}$ and fix one default spin $s_0\in S$. Replace all coordinates after $x_N$ by $s_0$ to define a canonical extension $\widehat\sigma$ of each first-$N$ prefix, and set $g_N(\sigma)=g(\widehat\sigma)$. Then $g_N$ depends only on the first $N$ coordinates and $\sup_\sigma|g(\sigma)-g_N(\sigma)|<\varepsilon$. [given, F2, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 For every $n$, $|\int g\,d\mu_n-\int g_N\,d\mu_n|\le\varepsilon$ and the same bound holds for $\mu$. Step 1.2 gives $\int g_N\,d\mu_n\to\int g_N\,d\mu$, so the limit superior of $|\int g\,d\mu_n-\int g\,d\mu|$ is at most $2\varepsilon$. Since $\varepsilon>0$ is arbitrary, weak convergence follows. Conversely, if $\mu_n$ converges weakly, indicators of all cylinders are bounded continuous by [F2], so their probabilities converge; finite sums give every finite marginal, hence local weak convergence by [F1]. [given, F1, F2, step 1.2, step 3.1] ∎
