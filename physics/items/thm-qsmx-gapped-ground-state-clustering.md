## Statement

Assume Countable Choice and the finite-matrix $C^*$-identity used by the quasi-local completion. Let $\mathcal A$ be the quasi-local algebra of uniformly finite-dimensional spins on $\mathbb Z^d$, $d\ge1$, with max-coordinate distance $d_\infty$. Let $\alpha_t$ be a strongly continuous automorphism group satisfying the exponential Lieb–Robinson estimate

$$\|[A,\alpha_t(B)]\|\le\frac{2\|A\|\|B\|}{C}(e^{v|t|}-1)\sum_{x\in X,\,y\in Y}F_a(d_\infty(x,y)),\qquad F_a(r)=e^{-ar}(1+r)^{-d-1},$$

for disjoint finite nonempty $X,Y$, $A\in\mathcal A_X$, $B\in\mathcal A_Y$, all real $t$, and fixed $a,C>0$, $v\ge0$. In particular this holds for the thermodynamic dynamics of a bounded finite-range interaction $\Phi$, with $C=C_{F_a}$ and $v=2C\|\Phi\|_{F_a}/\hbar$ from the weighted commutator theorem and any $\hbar>0$.

Here a **positive-energy ground state** means an algebraic state $\eta$ admitting a contractive unital $*$-representation $\pi$ on a complex Hilbert space, a unit vector $\Omega$, and a strongly continuous unitary group $U_t$ fixing $\Omega$, such that $\eta(A)=\langle\pi(A)\Omega,\Omega\rangle$, $\pi(\alpha_t(A))=U_t\pi(A)U_t^*$, and the generator is $iK$ for a positive self-adjoint densely defined $K$. Inner products are linear in their first argument. Such a representation can be restricted to the cyclic subspace generated by $\Omega$; no spectral resolution is included in the definition.

Suppose $\omega$ is the unique positive-energy ground state and its cyclic ground representation has an infinite-volume energy gap $\gamma>0$: writing $H=\hbar K$, require

$$\sigma(K)\subseteq\{0\}\cup[g,\infty),\qquad g=\gamma/\hbar>0.$$

Then for every pair of disjoint finite nonempty supports, with $r=\operatorname{dist}_\infty(X,Y)\ge1$, one has

$$|\omega(AB)-\omega(A)\omega(B)|\le\|A\|\|B\|\left(\frac{2|X||Y|}{\pi C}+1+\frac{2v+g}{\pi a g r}\right)e^{-\mu r},\qquad \mu=\frac{ag}{2v+g}>0.$$

This is exponential connected clustering for the infinite-volume state. The hypotheses do not require uniformly gapped finite-volume approximations, translation invariance, or a particular choice of finite-volume boundary ground states. The same estimate holds for any positive-energy state representation with $\ker K=\mathbb C\Omega$ and the stated gap, even without uniqueness among all ground states. Uniqueness is a sufficient hypothesis for that kernel condition, proved below. For other weighted interactions the assertion applies whenever their strongly continuous dynamics and the displayed Lieb–Robinson estimate are supplied; no missing thermodynamic-existence theorem for a general interaction is assumed to have been proved here.

For physical bookkeeping $t$ is in seconds, $H,\gamma$ are in joules, $\hbar$ is in joule-seconds and $K,g,v$ have inverse-second units. Lattice distances, $a,C,\mu$ and the exponential argument are dimensionless. The theorem does not prove a gap or uniqueness for an unspecified physical model.

## Proof

**Given:** [A1], [F1]–[F6] and the Statement hypotheses.

1.1 A cyclic representation of the spin algebra is separable. Indeed, finite tuples of the countable lattice enumerate its finite subsets, and rational real/imaginary entries form a countable dense family in each finite local matrix algebra. Countable Choice permits their countable union. Density follows, for a $D\times D$ matrix, from $\|T\|\le D\max_{i,j}|T_{ij}|$, obtained by finite Cauchy–Schwarz. The resulting countable family is dense in the completion, and its vectors $\pi(A)\Omega$ are dense in the cyclic Hilbert space by contractivity. The cyclic representation supplied by [F3] has the same state pairings as any given cyclic representation: the map $[A]\mapsto\pi(A)\Omega$ preserves $\langle[A],[B]\rangle=\omega(B^*A)$ and extends by density to a unitary. Thus the chosen cyclic ground representation is a separable realization of the actual GNS state, with no extra representation hypothesis. [A1, F1, F3, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 We verify the advertised finite-range instance of the commutator premise. If $\Phi$ has range $R$ and per-site norm bound $\kappa$, a pair separated by more than $R$ contributes no interaction term, while for a pair of distance at most $R$ the numerator of the weighted norm is at most $\kappa$. Hence $\|\Phi\|_{F_a}\le\kappa/F_a(R)<\infty$. The uniform finite-volume bound [F2] passes to the thermodynamic limit for each local $A,B$ and fixed $t$, by norm continuity of commutators. The group is isometric, so $\|[A,\alpha_t(B)]\|=\|[\alpha_{-t}(A),B]\|$, matching exactly the supplier's time/support convention. This establishes the stated constants without replacing a general infinite-volume gap by a finite-volume gap. [A1, F1, F2, given, algebra]

1.3 The cyclic restriction in the ground-state definition preserves positive energy. Let $\mathcal L=\overline{\pi(\mathcal A)\Omega}$ in a ground representation. Covariance and $U_t\Omega=\Omega$ imply that $U_t$ and $U_{-t}$ preserve $\mathcal L$; its orthogonal projection commutes with $U_t$. For $x\in D(K)$, difference quotients show that this projection preserves $D(K)$ and commutes with $K$. The generator domain inside $\mathcal L$ is dense: the vectors $h^{-1}\int_0^hU_tx\,dt$ lie in it and tend to $x$ for $x\in\mathcal L$, as their difference quotients give $iK$ equal to $(U_hx-x)/h$. The restriction is self-adjoint because $K\pm i$ are onto, their bounded inverses preserve $\mathcal L$ by the commuting projection, and the restricted symmetric operator has both ranges onto; directly, if $y$ is in its adjoint domain, solve $(K+i)x=(K_{\mathcal L}^*+i)y$ in $\mathcal L$, and the zero kernel of $K_{\mathcal L}^*+i$ forces $x=y$. It is positive by restriction. More generally, for every $z$ in the resolvent of $K$, commutation of the projection with $K$ on its domain implies commutation with $(K-z)^{-1}$: apply $(K-z)$ and its injectivity to the two candidate images. This inverse therefore restricts to the inverse on $\mathcal L$, so $\sigma(K_{\mathcal L})\subseteq\sigma(K)$ and the spectral gap is inherited, as is a simple ground kernel. The resolvent surjectivity follows from the self-adjoint closed-range estimate exactly as in the spectral-measure helper. Thus the definition using a possibly noncyclic representation agrees with its cyclic restriction, without a Stone or spectral-existence theorem. [F4, F5, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Let $P_0$ be the ground-kernel projection in the separable representation. A unit vector $\psi\in\ker K$ satisfies $U_t\psi=\psi$, since its orbit derivative is $iU_tK\psi=0$. Its vector state $\eta_\psi(A)=\langle\pi(A)\psi,\psi\rangle$ is a positive-energy ground state using the same representation; step 1.3 also supplies its cyclic version if desired. Uniqueness gives $\eta_\psi=\omega$. Scaling and polarization inside $\ker K$ therefore yield $P_0\pi(A)P_0=\omega(A)P_0$ for every $A$. If $\psi\in\ker K$ is orthogonal to $\Omega$, this identity implies $\langle\pi(A)\Omega,\psi\rangle=0$ for every $A$. Cyclicity forces $\psi=0$. Since the fixed vector $\Omega$ has zero orbit derivative, $\Omega\in D(K)$ and $K\Omega=0$, so we have $\ker K=\mathbb C\Omega$. The alternative hypothesis stated in the theorem supplies this directly and needs no uniqueness argument. [F3, F4, step 1.1, step 1.3, given, algebra]

2.2 Put $N=\|A\|\|B\|$ and $f(t)=\omega([A,\alpha_t(B)])$. The displayed Lieb–Robinson premise, $F_a(s)\le e^{-as}$ and $d_\infty(x,y)\ge r$ give $$|f(t)|\le\frac{2N}{C}|X||Y|e^{-ar}(e^{v|t|}-1),\qquad |f(t)|\le2N.$$ The first bound is $O(t)$ at zero, including $v=0$. The local observables commute by [F1], so all time-integral hypotheses in [F4] hold. The same estimates apply to the finite-range instance justified in step 1.2. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.2, given, algebra]

3.1 Apply [F4] using step 2.1. For any $q,\tau>0$, split its absolutely convergent integral at $|t|=\tau$. Step 2.2 gives the short-time bound $$\frac{2N|X||Y|}{\pi C}e^{-ar}\int_0^\tau\frac{e^{vt}-1}{t}dt\le\frac{2N|X||Y|}{\pi C}e^{-ar+v\tau},$$ by [F6], because $(e^{vt}-1)/t\le ve^{vt}$ and $e^{v\tau}-1\le e^{v\tau}$, with zero left side when $v=0$. On the long-time part the trivial bound gives $$\frac{2N}{\pi}\int_\tau^\infty\frac{e^{-qt^2}}t dt=\frac N\pi\int_{q\tau^2}^\infty\frac{e^{-u}}u du\le\frac{N}{\pi q\tau^2}e^{-q\tau^2}.$$ Adding the filter error $Ne^{-g^2/(4q)}$ bounds the connected correlation. These estimates use only the supplied dynamics and its infinite-volume ground representation. [F4, F6, step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 Choose $\tau=ar/(v+g/2)$ and $q=g/(2\tau)$. They are positive for $r\ge1$ and $g,a>0$, even when $v=0$. Then $$ar-v\tau=q\tau^2=\frac{g^2}{4q}=\frac{ag}{2v+g}r=\mu r,\qquad \frac1{q\tau^2}=\frac{2v+g}{agr}.$$ Substitution into step 3.1 proves the displayed bound. Zero observables give $N=0$ and require no division by $N$. The independent finite-volume band multiplicity and transport statements on this page are unchanged; this proof adds no claim that a degenerate gapped state has ordinary connected clustering. The clock conversion $g=\gamma/\hbar$ gives exactly the units in the Statement. [step 3.1, algebra] ∎
## Remarks

The source distinguishes the direct infinite-volume spectral-gap route from limits of finite-volume uniform gaps. This theorem proves the former under its precise ground-state and locality hypotheses. Its estimate uses the actual weighted commutator constant of this page, not the source's different interaction norm or a silently copied sharp prefactor. The full-Choice canonical unbounded spectral theorem is not a premise.
