## Example

Let $X=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2:x^2+y^2=1\}$ with the smooth structure given by the two stereographic charts that remove $N=(0,1)$ and $S=(0,-1)$, respectively, with coordinates $x/(1-y)$ and $x/(1+y)$. Orient $X$ so the smooth tangent vector field $V(x,y)=(-y,x)$ is positive; it is tangent since $V\cdot(x,y)=0$ and nowhere zero since $x^2+y^2=1$. For each $z\in X$, give the singleton $\{z\}$ its positive zero-dimensional orientation, so its integration current is the positively oriented point current $\delta_z(f)=f(z)$. Choose $y_1,y_2\in X$ and real coefficients $a,b$. The finite point-source current $\rho=a\delta_{y_1}+b\delta_{y_2}$ acts on every smooth function $f$ by $\rho(f)=af(y_1)+bf(y_2)$ and has total coefficient $\langle\rho,1\rangle=a+b$. If $y_1=y_2$, then $\rho=(a+b)\delta_{y_1}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The point-source current definition and $a,b\in\mathbb R$.

[F1] [[def-r2-compact-de-rham-current-of-a-source-cycle]] defines $\delta_y(f)=f(y)$ for every smooth function $f$ and defines finite real linear combinations of point currents.

[F2] [[lem-r2-compact-source-cycle-defines-closed-current]] proves that integration over a compact oriented embedded submanifold, including a positively oriented point, defines a current; the positive zero-dimensional orientation gives $\int_{\{y\}}f=f(y)=\delta_y(f)$.

[F3] [[def-smooth-atlas]] defines a smooth atlas as a covering family of smoothly compatible charts. [[def-smoothly-compatible-charts]] requires both transition directions on their coordinate overlap domains to be smooth. [[def-smooth-manifold]] identifies a smooth manifold structure with the maximal atlas generated by a smooth atlas on the underlying topological manifold.

[F4] [[def-oriented-smooth-manifold-and-oriented-chart]] defines an orientation as a smooth choice of a ray in the determinant line of each tangent space; in dimension one, declaring the nowhere-zero tangent field $(-y,x)$ positive specifies that ray.

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** $f\in\Omega^0(S^1)$, $y_1,y_2\in S^1$, and $a,b\in\mathbb R$.

1.1 Put $U_N=X\setminus\{N\}$ and $U_S=X\setminus\{S\}$. These are open in the subspace topology and cover $X$. For the displayed coordinates $u=\sigma_N(x,y)$ and $v=\sigma_S(x,y)$, direct substitution gives
$$\sigma_N^{-1}(u)=\left(\frac{2u}{1+u^2},\frac{u^2-1}{1+u^2}\right),\qquad \sigma_S^{-1}(v)=\left(\frac{2v}{1+v^2},\frac{1-v^2}{1+v^2}\right).$$
Their denominators are positive; the first image misses $N$, the second misses $S$, and both pairs of compositions are identities. The maps and inverses are continuous, so they are charts onto $\mathbb R$. Since $X\subset\mathbb R^2$ is Hausdorff and second-countable, these charts exhibit a topological $1$-manifold. On $U_N\cap U_S$, each coordinate image is $\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}$, and both transitions are $w\mapsto1/w$, smooth there with smooth inverse. Thus [F3] gives exactly the smooth structure specified in the Example. The field $V$ has chart components $(1+u^2)/2$ and $-(1+v^2)/2$, respectively, so is smooth and nowhere zero and gives the orientation of [F4]. In a chart centered at a point, its singleton is the coordinate zero set, hence a compact embedded zero-dimensional submanifold with the stated positive orientation. [given, F3, F4, algebra]

2.1 By [F1] and linearity, $\rho(f)=a\delta_{y_1}(f)+b\delta_{y_2}(f)=af(y_1)+bf(y_2)$; each evaluation is well-defined by [F2]. [F1, F2, given, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Taking $f=1$ gives $\rho(1)=a+b$ because $1(y_1)=1(y_2)=1$. If $y_1=y_2$, the same formula combines the two coefficients, so the displayed current equality follows. [step 2.1, algebra] ∎
