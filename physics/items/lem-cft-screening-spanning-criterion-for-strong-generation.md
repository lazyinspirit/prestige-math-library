## Statement

Let $V=\bigoplus_{m\ge0}V_m$ be a conformal vertex algebra of CFT type, with vacuum $1$, conformal vector $\omega$, and translation $T=L(-1)$. Thus $L(0)|_{V_m}=mI$ and $V_0=\mathbb C1$. A nonzero vector $u$ is a Virasoro highest-weight vector of weight $\lambda$ if $L(0)u=\lambda u$ and $L(r)u=0$ for every $r>0$. A Virasoro descendant of $u$ means $L(-r_1)\cdots L(-r_s)u$ for some $s\ge0$ and integers $r_i\ge1$.

Let $Q:V\to V$ be complex-linear and weight-preserving, meaning $Q(V_m)\subseteq V_m$ for every $m\ge0$. Say that $Q$ is a derivation of the vertex products if, for every $a,b\in V$ and $r\in\mathbb Z$,
$$Q(a_{(r)}b)=(Qa)_{(r)}b+a_{(r)}(Qb).$$
Assume this derivation identity and $QL(n)=L(n)Q$ for every $n\in\mathbb Z$. Suppose $Q\omega=0$, $QF=H$, $QH=E$, and $QE=0$, where $F,H,E$ are homogeneous positive-weight states.

Here a vertex subalgebra is a linear subspace containing $1$, stable under $T$, and closed under every mode product $a_{(r)}b$ of its elements. Let $K\subseteq V$ be a vertex subalgebra containing $\omega,F,H,E$. Assume every nonzero $Q^j f_n$ is a highest-weight vector and that $K$ is the linear span of all $L(-r_1)\cdots L(-r_s)Q^jf_n$ with $s,n,j\ge0$ and $r_i\ge1$, where $f_0=1$, $f_1=F$, and for each $n\ge1$ an integer $k_n\ge1$ satisfies $f_{n+1}=F_{(-k_n)}f_n$. Let $W$ be the smallest vertex subalgebra of $V$ containing $\omega,F,H,E$. Then $W=K$ and is strongly generated by $\omega,F,H,E$, meaning it is spanned by finite words of strictly negative modes of these four states applied to $1$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The objects, hypotheses and conventions in the Statement.

[A1] The grading, weight preservation, mode-derivation identity, commutation with all $L(n)$, four-state $Q$ table, highest-weight conditions on nonzero $Q^j f_n$, subalgebra containment, recursive sequence $(f_n)$, and descendant-spanning property in the Statement are assumptions.

[F1] [[def-conformal-vertex-algebra]] and [[def-vertex-algebra-state-field]] fix $T=L(-1)$, the vacuum creation identity, and $[T,a_{(m)}]=-m a_{(m-1)}$.

## Proof

1.1 Let $U$ be the span of finite words of strictly negative modes of $\omega,F,H,E$ on $\mathbf1$. The derivation identity gives $[Q,a_{(m)}]=(Qa)_{(m)}$. Since $1_{(-1)}1=1$, applying it to this product gives $Q1=(Q1)_{(-1)}1+1_{(-1)}Q1=2Q1$, so $Q1=0$. Applying the mode commutator successively to a word and using $Q\omega=0$, $QF=H$, $QH=E$, and $QE=0$ shows that every term of its image is again such a word; hence $QU\subset U$. Creation gives $F=F_{(-1)}1\in U$. Induction on $n$ using $f_{n+1}=F_{(-k_n)}f_n$ with $k_n\ge1$ gives $f_n\in U$, and then $Q^jf_n\in U$ for all $j$. [given, A1, F1, algebra]

2.1 For $r\ge2$, $L(-r)=\omega_{(-r+1)}$ is a strictly negative mode of a displayed generator. Also $T\mathbf1=0$, and $[T,a_{(-k)}]=k a_{(-k-1)}$ for $k\ge1$; commuting $T$ through a finite word therefore expresses its translate as a sum of words in $U$. Thus every Virasoro descendant of every $Q^jf_n$ lies in $U$, and the assumed spanning property gives $K\subset U$. [given, A1, F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 By definition $U\subset W$. Since $K$ is a vertex subalgebra containing the four generators, $W\subset K$. Hence $W=K=U$, which is precisely strong generation by the four states. [given, A1, step 2.1] ∎
