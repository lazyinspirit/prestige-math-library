## Statement

Let $d\ge1$. The **coset ring** of the additive topological group $\mathbb R^d$ is the smallest family of subsets containing every additive coset and closed under finite unions, finite intersections, and complements relative to $\mathbb R^d$. Call a subset **discrete** when it is locally finite: every compact subset of $\mathbb R^d$ meets it in finitely many points. Then every discrete element of the coset ring belongs to the ring generated by the discrete cosets, where a ring of sets is closed under finite unions, intersections, and relative differences.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $d\ge1$, the coset ring $\mathcal C$ of $\mathbb R^d$, and $E\in\mathcal C$ locally finite.

[A1] $\mathbb R^d$ has its usual Euclidean topology and additive group law.

[A2] $E$ is generated from finitely many additive cosets by finite unions, intersections, and complements.

[A3] Every compact set meets $E$ in finitely many points.

## Proof

1.1 Put a finite Boolean expression for $E$ in disjunctive normal form. Each nonempty cell has the form $A\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^m B_j$, where $A$ and the $B_j$ are cosets: intersecting positive cosets gives a coset or the empty set, and a cell with no positive literal uses $A=\mathbb R^d$. Thus $E$ is a finite union of such cells. [given, A1, A2, algebra]

1.2 We first prove the cover claim: if a non-discrete coset $A=x+H$ is covered by a locally finite set $F\subseteq A$ and finitely many cosets $B_j$ of subgroups of $H$, each disjoint from $F$, then $F=\varnothing$. Induct on the number $k$ of distinct subgroups defining the $B_j$. If a subgroup $K$ is locally finite in $\mathbb R^d$, a compact neighborhood of $0$ meets $K$ in finitely many points, so shrinking it to exclude the nonzero points gives a neighborhood $U$ with $U\cap K=\{0\}$. Choose a symmetric neighborhood $V$ with $V-V\subseteq U$. Every compact set has a finite cover by translates of $V$, and each translate meets any coset of $K$ in at most one point. Hence every discrete subgroup coset is locally finite. If every subgroup among the $B_j$ were discrete, then their finite union with $F$ would be locally finite, forcing $A$ to be discrete. Therefore at least one defining subgroup, say $K_1$, is non-discrete. If $k=1$ and $y\in F$, then $y+K_1\subseteq A$ cannot meet any $B_j$: a $K_1$-coset meeting $y+K_1$ equals it and would contain $y$, contrary to $B_j\cap F=\varnothing$. Thus $y+K_1\subseteq F$, contradicting local finiteness of $F$ because $K_1$ is non-discrete. For $k>1$, the same argument shows that $y+K_1$ is covered only by $F$ and the cosets whose defining subgroups differ from $K_1$. Translate by $-y$ and intersect with $K_1$. Each nonempty intersection with such a coset is a coset in $K_1$ of the proper subgroup $K_1\cap K_j$; it is proper because otherwise that coset would contain $y$. The set $F'=(F-y)\cap K_1$ is locally finite and contains $0$, and is disjoint from all these intersection cosets. The induction hypothesis inside the non-discrete group $K_1$, with fewer than $k$ distinct subgroups, says $F'=\varnothing$, a contradiction. This proves the cover claim for every $k$. [given, A1, A3, algebra]

2.1 Let $C=A\setminus\bigcup_j B_j$ be a cell in step 1.1 and suppose $C$ is nonempty and discrete. If $A$ were non-discrete, then $A=C\cup\bigcup_j(A\cap B_j)$ would be a cover of the type in step 1.2: $C$ is locally finite and each $A\cap B_j$ is a coset disjoint from $C$. Step 1.2 would force $C=\varnothing$, contrary to the supposition. Thus $A$ is a discrete coset. Every $A\cap B_j$ is then also a discrete coset, so $C$ belongs to the ring generated by discrete cosets. Each cell in the finite union for $E$ is a subset of the locally finite set $E$, hence is locally finite; applying this argument to every nonempty cell and taking their finite union proves the statement. [given, A1, A2, A3, step 1.1, step 1.2] ∎

## Remarks

The local-finiteness convention is explicit here so that sums of point masses on the discrete sets used in crystalline-measure arguments are locally finite. The statement is the finite-dimensional real-vector-space case needed in the $c=1$ source's reduction from coset-ring expressions to lattice cosets.
