## Definition

Let $X$ be a scheme in a fixed Grothendieck universe containing every scheme considered here. The **small étale category** of $X$ has as objects étale morphisms $U\to X$ and as arrows morphisms of $X$-schemes. A **declared covering family** of an object $U\to X$ is a set-indexed family of étale arrows $U_i\to U$ whose image union is $U$; the empty family covers the empty scheme. A family of this form is a **Grothendieck pretopology** when identity families cover, pullbacks of covers along arrows cover, and composites of covers cover. A **sieve** on $U$ is a collection of arrows with target $U$ closed under precomposition; the sieve generated by a family consists of arrows factoring through one of its members. The **Grothendieck topology generated by the declared families** is the smallest assignment of covering sieves containing every maximal sieve and every sieve generated by a declared family, and closed under pullback and local transitivity. The separate site-axiom lemma proves the declared families form a pretopology; the resulting site is denoted $X_{\acute e t}$.

## Remarks

The pretopology certification invoked in the Definition assumes full AC, as recorded in the site-axiom lemma and in [[def-axiom-of-choice]]; the category and the declared families can be specified without it.

For size bookkeeping, write $\mathcal U$ for the fixed universe in the Definition: schemes and covering index sets are $\mathcal U$-small. Work with these collections in a larger ambient Grothendieck universe $\mathcal V$ with $\mathcal U\in\mathcal V$, and take presheaf values to be $\mathcal V$-small sets. The collection of $\mathcal U$-small schemes, their arrows, and their $\mathcal U$-indexed covering families is a set in $\mathcal V$; it need not be a set belonging to $\mathcal U$. This is the ambient set-sized interpretation used in forming matching-family quotients for sheafification. The definition does not assert that an arbitrary sheaf of sets on this site is representable by a scheme, or that a torsor sheaf is representable.
