## Example

Assume Countable Choice $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ ([[def-countable-choice]]). Let
$f:S^1\to S^1$ be a diffeomorphism and let
$M_f=(\mathbb R\times S^1)/\mathbb Z$ with $k\cdot(t,y)=(t+k,f^k(y))$; let
$F_f$ be the suspension foliation of the representation $\rho(k)=f^k$ over
$B=S^1$. Then:

1. the leaf $L_y$ through $[0,y]$ is diffeomorphic to $S^1$ when the orbit of
   $y$ under $f$ is periodic, and to $\mathbb R$ otherwise;
2. if $y$ has minimal period $k\ge1$, the leaf loop
   $a(t)=[(kt,y)]$, $t\in[0,1]$, generates $\pi_1(L_y)\cong\mathbb Z$ and has
   holonomy germ at $[0,y]$ equal to the germ of $f^{-k}$ at $y$, so the
   holonomy group of $L_y$ is the cyclic group generated by that germ;
3. in particular for $f$ the identity the foliation is the product foliation of
   $\mathbb T^2$ by circles and all leaf-loop holonomy germs are trivial, while for a
   rotation by $2\pi p/q$ ($p\in\mathbb Z$, $q\ge1$) every leaf is a circle and every leaf-loop holonomy germ is
   trivial.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A diffeomorphism $f:S^1\to S^1$ ([[def-diffeomorphism-and-local-diffeomorphism-of-manifolds]]), the quotient $M_f=(\mathbb R\times S^1)/\mathbb Z$ for $k\cdot(t,y)=(t+k,f^k(y))$ ([[def-circle-as-real-line-mod-integers]]), and the suspension foliation $F_f$ of $\rho(k)=f^k$ ([[def-suspension-foliation-of-a-group-action]]).

[F1] In the suspension the leaf $L_y$ through $[0,y]$ is $\mathbb R/K_y$ with $K_y=\{k:f^k(y)=y\}$, $\pi_1(L_y)\cong K_y$, and the holonomy representation is $k\mapsto\operatorname{germ}_y(f^k)$, while the forward-path holonomy is $\operatorname{germ}_y(f^{-k})$ ([[prop-suspension-holonomy-is-the-germ-of-the-monodromy-action]]).

[F2] The holonomy group of a leaf is the image of its holonomy representation, i.e. the isotropy group of the holonomy groupoid at a point of the leaf ([[prop-isotropy-of-the-holonomy-groupoid-is-the-leaf-holonomy-group]], [[def-holonomy-groupoid-of-a-foliation]], [[def-monodromy-groupoid-of-a-foliation]]).

[F3] A subgroup $K_y\le\mathbb Z$ is either $\{0\}$ or $k\mathbb Z$ for the minimal positive element $k$; $\mathbb R/\{0\}=\mathbb R$ and $\mathbb R/k\mathbb Z\cong S^1$ ([[def-circle-as-real-line-mod-integers]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **Leaf type.** The period set $K_y=\{k\in\mathbb Z:f^k(y)=y\}$ is a subgroup of $\mathbb Z$. If the orbit of $y$ is periodic of minimal period $k$, then $K_y=k\mathbb Z$ with $k\ge1$ minimal, and $L_y\cong\mathbb R/k\mathbb Z\cong S^1$ by [F1] and [F3]; if the orbit is not periodic, $K_y=\{0\}$ and $L_y\cong\mathbb R$. This proves claim 1. [F1, F3]

1.2 **The generating loop and its germ.** If $y$ has minimal period $k$, the path $a(t)=[(kt,y)]$ is closed because $(k,y)\sim(0,y)$. Under $\pi_1(L_y)\cong K_y=k\mathbb Z$ it corresponds to the positive generator $k$. Its forward holonomy is the germ of $f^{-k}$ by [F1]. Its representation value is the inverse germ, that of $f^k$; these two germs generate the same cyclic group, proving claim 2. [F1, F2]


1.3 **The identity and finite-order cases.** If $f=\mathrm{id}$, the quotient is the torus with its product foliation, every $K_y=\mathbb Z$, and its leaf-loop holonomy germs are identities. For a rotation by $2\pi p/q$, with $p\in\mathbb Z$ and $q\ge1$, put $d=q/\gcd(p,q)$. The rotation has exact order $d$, and $f^k(y)=y$ exactly when $d$ divides $k$, so $K_y=d\mathbb Z$ for every $y$. Thus every leaf is a circle and every leaf-loop holonomy germ is the identity, since $f^k=\mathrm{id}$ for $k\in d\mathbb Z$. A path over one base circuit need not be closed and may have the nonidentity germ of $f^{-1}$; claim 3 concerns loops in the leaves. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]


2.1 **Conclusion.** Claims 1, 2 and 3 are established in steps 1.1, 1.2 and 1.3: the leaves of $F_f$ are circles exactly for periodic orbits, the holonomy of a periodic leaf is generated by $f^{-k}$, and for the identity or a finite-order rotation all holonomy is trivial even though the leaves are circles. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3] ∎
