## Statement

Assume the elementary inertial-rest branch of [[post-em-material-closure]] in its lossless isotropic scalar case at rest in the laboratory frame: constants $\varepsilon,\mu>0$, $\sigma=0$, $J_{\mathrm{ext}}=0$, the constitutive relations $D=\varepsilon E$, $B=\mu H$ of [[post-em-material-closure]] clause (b), read with the objects $D,H$ of [[def-em-polarization-magnetization]], and the macroscopic equations (b) of [[pthm-em-macroscopic-maxwell]] with $\rho_f=0$, $J_f=0$ on the guide region; suppose also $\varepsilon\mu\ge c^{-2}$ whenever the broadband interpretation of that branch is used. Let the rectangular guide, its PEC boundary model, the axis $e_z$, the TE/TM/TEM terminology and the separated phasor convention be those of [[def-em-guide-cavity-geometry]] with cross-section $(0,a)\times(0,b)$, and let $\omega>0$, $\beta\in\mathbb C$, $k_c^2:=\omega^2\varepsilon\mu-\beta^2\in\mathbb R_{>0}$, $H:=B/\mu$ and $\theta:=\beta x_3-\omega t$. Write $\tilde E,\tilde H$ for the complex amplitudes of the separated real fields $E=\operatorname{Re}(\tilde Ee^{i\theta})$, $H=\operatorname{Re}(\tilde He^{i\theta})$, so that $B=\mu H$.
All fields use SI units: $a,b,x_j$ carry $\mathrm m$, $t$ carries $\mathrm s$, $\omega$ carries $\mathrm s^{-1}$, $\beta,k_c,k_{mn}$ carry $\mathrm m^{-1}$, $\varepsilon$ carries $\mathrm F\,\mathrm m^{-1}$, $\mu$ carries $\mathrm H\,\mathrm m^{-1}$, $\tilde E$ carries $\mathrm V\,\mathrm m^{-1}$ and $\tilde H$ carries $\mathrm A\,\mathrm m^{-1}$. The scalar profiles $h_{mn},e_{mn}$ below are dimensionless. Fix positive real constant longitudinal amplitude quantities $H_*$ in $\mathrm A\,\mathrm m^{-1}$ and $E_*$ in $\mathrm V\,\mathrm m^{-1}$; the unit-amplitude choices are $H_*=1\,\mathrm A\,\mathrm m^{-1}$ for TE and $E_*=1\,\mathrm V\,\mathrm m^{-1}$ for TM.
(a) [TE family] For integers $m,n\ge0$ with $(m,n)\ne(0,0)$ put $h_{mn}:=\cos\frac{m\pi x_1}{a}\cos\frac{n\pi x_2}{b}$, $k_{mn}^2:=(\frac{m\pi}{a})^2+(\frac{n\pi}{b})^2$, and require $\beta^2=\omega^2\varepsilon\mu-k_{mn}^2$; define the complex amplitudes $$\tilde E:=-H_*\frac{i\omega\mu}{k_{mn}^2}\,e_z\times\nabla_th_{mn},\qquad \tilde H:=H_*\left(\frac{i\beta}{k_{mn}^2}\,\nabla_th_{mn}+e_zh_{mn}\right).$$ Then the real fields $E=\operatorname{Re}(\tilde Ee^{i\theta})$, $B=\mu\operatorname{Re}(\tilde He^{i\theta})$ are $C^\infty$ on the guide region, have $E_z=0$ (TE), satisfy the four source-free macroscopic equations $\operatorname{div}D=0$, $\operatorname{div}B=0$, $\operatorname{curl}E=-\partial_tB$, $\operatorname{curl}H=\partial_tD$, and satisfy $n\times E=0$ and $n\cdot B=0$ on every open wall of the guide.
(b) [TM family] For integers $m,n\ge1$ put $e_{mn}:=\sin\frac{m\pi x_1}{a}\sin\frac{n\pi x_2}{b}$, $k_{mn}^2:=(\frac{m\pi}{a})^2+(\frac{n\pi}{b})^2$, require $\beta^2=\omega^2\varepsilon\mu-k_{mn}^2$, and define $$\tilde E:=E_*\left(\frac{i\beta}{k_{mn}^2}\nabla_te_{mn}+e_ze_{mn}\right),\qquad \tilde H:=E_*\frac{i\omega\varepsilon}{k_{mn}^2}\,e_z\times\nabla_te_{mn}.$$ Then the corresponding real fields are $C^\infty$, have $B_z=0$ (TM), satisfy the same four equations and the same boundary conditions on the open walls.
(c) [Index restrictions] For $m=n=0$ one has $\nabla_th_{00}=0$ and $k_{00}^2=0$ makes the displayed divided reconstruction formulas undefined. A purely longitudinal constant-profile magnetic amplitude with zero electric amplitude cannot be a nonzero monochromatic Maxwell candidate: Faraday gives $0=i\omega\mu\tilde H$, hence $\tilde H=0$ because $\omega>0$. At $\beta=0$ a constant $H_z$ is divergence-free, so divergence alone does not exclude it. Thus $(0,0)$ is excluded by the full field equations and the reconstruction denominator, not by a false divergence claim; for $m=0$ or $n=0$ the TM profile $e_{mn}$ vanishes identically, so TM indices are restricted to $m,n\ge1$; conversely for $(m,n)\ne(0,0)$ (resp. $m,n\ge1$) the displayed profiles and fields are nonzero.
(d) [Dispersion and cutoff] On the declared family, $k_c^2=\omega^2\varepsilon\mu-\beta^2$ defines the cutoff $\omega_c:=k_c/\sqrt{\varepsilon\mu}>0$; real longitudinal wave numbers with $\beta^2=\omega^2\varepsilon\mu-k_c^2$ exist exactly for $\omega\ge\omega_c$, with $\beta\ne0$ exactly for $\omega>\omega_c$ and $\beta=0$ at $\omega=\omega_c$; for $\omega<\omega_c$ the two algebraic roots are $\beta=\pm i\sqrt{k_c^2-\varepsilon\mu\omega^2}$, and the same verified separated fields have the factor $e^{-\operatorname{Im}\beta x_3}$; a declared half-guide may select the decaying sign, and no propagating-power claim is made there.
(e) [Scope] These are separated candidate modes of the declared lossless model; the theorem asserts no completeness, no uniqueness among fields with the same data, and no lossy, dispersive or open-end behaviour, and it assumes the PEC model of the definition, not the physical infinite-conductivity limit.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The elementary inertial-rest branch of [[post-em-material-closure]] in its isotropic lossless scalar case at rest in the laboratory frame: constants $\varepsilon,\mu>0$, $\sigma=0$, $J_{\mathrm{ext}}=0$, the relations $D=\varepsilon E$, $B=\mu H$ of [[post-em-material-closure]] clause (b), read with the objects $D,H$ of [[def-em-polarization-magnetization]], and the macroscopic equations (b) of [[pthm-em-macroscopic-maxwell]] with $\rho_f=0$, $J_f=0$ on the guide region; the rectangular guide, its PEC model, its axis $e_z$, the TE/TM/TEM terminology and the separated phasor convention of [[def-em-guide-cavity-geometry]] with cross-section $(0,a)\times(0,b)$; $\omega>0$, $\beta\in\mathbb C$, $k_c^2=\omega^2\varepsilon\mu-\beta^2>0$, $\theta(x_3,t)=\beta x_3-\omega t$, $H=B/\mu$, the constant longitudinal amplitudes $H_*,E_*>0$ with the SI units of the Statement; and separated real fields $E=\operatorname{Re}(\tilde Ee^{i\theta})$, $H=\operatorname{Re}(\tilde He^{i\theta})$; for clause (a), integers $m,n\ge0$ with $(m,n)\ne(0,0)$ and $\beta^2=\omega^2\varepsilon\mu-k_{mn}^2$; for clause (b), integers $m,n\ge1$ and the same relation; the real and imaginary parts of complex numbers are those of [[def-complex-conjugate-real-imaginary-part-and-modulus]] and complex arithmetic is that of [[def-complex-numbers-and-arithmetic]].

[F1] In the stated branch, $D=\varepsilon E$ and $B=\mu H$ with constant $\varepsilon,\mu>0$, and with $\rho_f=0$, $J_f=0$ the four equations $\operatorname{div}D=0$, $\operatorname{div}B=0$, $\operatorname{curl}E=-\partial_tB$, $\operatorname{curl}H=\partial_tD$ hold on the guide region under the stated regularity ([[post-em-material-closure]] clause (b), [[pthm-em-macroscopic-maxwell]] clause (b), [[def-em-polarization-magnetization]]); the isotropic broadband condition $\varepsilon\mu\ge c^{-2}$ is required only when the broadband interpretation of the branch is used.

[F2] The guide region is $\Omega_A=(0,a)\times(0,b)\times\mathbb R$ with axis $e_z=e_3$; its open walls are $\{x_1=0\},\{x_1=a\}$ with outward normals $-e_1,+e_1$ and $\{x_2=0\},\{x_2=b\}$ with outward normals $-e_2,+e_2$, and the ideal PEC model declares $n\times E=0$ and $n\cdot B=0$ on every open wall; a $\beta$-$\omega$ separated field is $F=\operatorname{Re}(\tilde Fe^{i(\beta x_3-\omega t)})$ with complex amplitude $\tilde F$, TE means $E_z\equiv0$, TM means $B_z\equiv0$ ([[def-em-guide-cavity-geometry]] clauses (a),(b),(d),(e)).

[F3] Under the separated convention, [[lem-em-separated-phasor-calculus]] gives, componentwise and with $\operatorname{Re}$ the real part: (a) $\partial_{x_j}F=\operatorname{Re}(\partial_j\tilde Fe^{i\theta})$, $\partial_{x_3}F=\operatorname{Re}(i\beta\tilde Fe^{i\theta})$, $\partial_tF=\operatorname{Re}(-i\omega\tilde Fe^{i\theta})$; (b) $\operatorname{div}F=\operatorname{Re}\bigl((\partial_1\tilde F_1+\partial_2\tilde F_2+i\beta\tilde F_3)e^{i\theta}\bigr)$; (c) $\operatorname{curl}F=\operatorname{Re}\bigl((\partial_2\tilde F_3-i\beta\tilde F_2,\ i\beta\tilde F_1-\partial_1\tilde F_3,\ \partial_1\tilde F_2-\partial_2\tilde F_1)e^{i\theta}\bigr)$; (d) if $\operatorname{Re}(Ce^{i\varphi_1})=\operatorname{Re}(Ce^{i\varphi_2})=0$ for a complex vector $C$ and real phases with $\varphi_1-\varphi_2\notin\pi\mathbb Z$, then $C=0$, and consequently two separated fields that agree on a nonempty open subset have equal amplitudes on its projection to $\Omega_t$. In the sequel write $\widehat{\operatorname{div}}(v):=\partial_1v_1+\partial_2v_2+i\beta v_3$ and $\widehat{\operatorname{curl}}(v):=(\partial_2v_3-i\beta v_2,\ i\beta v_1-\partial_1v_3,\ \partial_1v_2-\partial_2v_1)$ for a $C^1$ complex amplitude $v$.

[F4] [[lem-em-transverse-vector-identities]] gives for a $C^2$ transverse profile $h$ and a $C^1$ transverse map $u_t$, regarded as $z$-independent fields on $\Omega_t\times\mathbb R$: $\operatorname{div}(e_z\times\nabla_th)=0$, $\operatorname{curl}(e_z\times\nabla_th)=e_z\Delta_th$, $e_z\times(e_z\times\nabla_th)=-\nabla_th$, $v\cdot(e_z\times v)=0$, $e_z\cdot(e_z\times v)=0$, $|e_z\times v|=|v|$ for $v=\nabla_th$, $\operatorname{curl}(e_zh)=-e_z\times\nabla_th$, $\operatorname{div}(e_zh)=\partial_3h$, $\operatorname{curl}(u_t)=e_z\operatorname{curl}_2u_t$, $\operatorname{div}(u_t)=\operatorname{div}_tu_t$, and $e_z\times u_t$ is transverse.

[F5] [[lem-em-rectangle-mode-profile-identities]] gives for $h_{mn}=\cos\frac{m\pi x_1}{a}\cos\frac{n\pi x_2}{b}$ and $e_{mn}=\sin\frac{m\pi x_1}{a}\sin\frac{n\pi x_2}{b}$, with $k_{mn}^2=(\frac{m\pi}{a})^2+(\frac{n\pi}{b})^2$: $\Delta_th_{mn}=-k_{mn}^2h_{mn}$, $\partial_1h_{mn}=0$ on $x_1\in\{0,a\}$, $\partial_2h_{mn}=0$ on $x_2\in\{0,b\}$; and for $m,n\ge1$, $\Delta_te_{mn}=-k_{mn}^2e_{mn}$ and $e_{mn}=0$ on $\partial R$; in particular at the corners the vanishing statements hold and $k_{mn}^2>0$ whenever $(m,n)\ne(0,0)$. By linearity, the eigen-identities and zero traces hold equally for the dimensionful profiles $H_*h_{mn}$ and $E_*e_{mn}$.

[F6] Smoothness rules: $(\sin x)'=\cos x$, $(\cos x)'=-\sin x$, the real exponential is smooth with $(\exp)'=\exp$, sums, scalar multiples and products obey the sum, scalar and product rules, composites obey the chain rule, and partial derivatives are one-variable derivatives along coordinate lines ([[thm-sine-and-cosine-derivatives]], [[thm-derivative-of-exponential]], [[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]], [[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]], [[def-directional-and-partial-derivatives]]); for real $x,y$ one has $\exp(x+iy)=e^x(\cos y+i\sin y)$, so with $\beta=p+iq$ and $\varphi=px_3-\omega t$ one has $e^{i\theta}=e^{-qx_3}(\cos\varphi+i\sin\varphi)$ ([[cor-complex-exponential-cartesian-form-modulus-and-eulers-identity]]).

[F7] For every real $X\ge0$ there is a unique $Y\ge0$ with $Y^2=X$ ([[thm-of-square-roots]]); and $i\gamma$ with real $\gamma$ satisfies $(i\gamma)^2=-\gamma^2$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct: translate the real source-free equations into complex amplitude equations with the separated phasor calculus, verify the amplitude equations and wall traces by the transverse and profile identities, then read off the index restrictions, the dispersion relation and the scope.

1.1 [Equivalence of the real and the amplitude equations] Let $E,H$ be separated fields with amplitudes $\tilde E,\tilde H$ on the guide region, and use the combinations of [F3]. Then the real equations $\operatorname{div}D=0$, $\operatorname{div}B=0$, $\operatorname{curl}E=-\partial_tB$, $\operatorname{curl}H=\partial_tD$ of [F1] hold if and only if the four amplitude equations $\widehat{\operatorname{div}}(\tilde E)=0$, $\widehat{\operatorname{div}}(\tilde H)=0$, $\widehat{\operatorname{curl}}(\tilde E)=i\omega\mu\tilde H$, $\widehat{\operatorname{curl}}(\tilde H)=-i\omega\varepsilon\tilde E$ hold on $(0,a)\times(0,b)$: indeed, since $\varepsilon,\mu$ are constants, [F3](b),(c) give $\operatorname{div}D=\varepsilon\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\widehat{\operatorname{div}}(\tilde E)e^{i\theta}\bigr)$, $\operatorname{div}B=\mu\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\widehat{\operatorname{div}}(\tilde H)e^{i\theta}\bigr)$, $\operatorname{curl}E=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\widehat{\operatorname{curl}}(\tilde E)e^{i\theta}\bigr)$, $\operatorname{curl}H=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\widehat{\operatorname{curl}}(\tilde H)e^{i\theta}\bigr)$ and [F3](a) gives $\partial_tB=\mu\operatorname{Re}(-i\omega\tilde He^{i\theta})$, $\partial_tD=\varepsilon\operatorname{Re}(-i\omega\tilde Ee^{i\theta})$, so substituting the amplitude equations and taking real parts reproduces the real equations; conversely, the four actual real residuals are $\operatorname{Re}(\varepsilon\widehat{\operatorname{div}}(\tilde E)e^{i\theta})$, $\operatorname{Re}(\mu\widehat{\operatorname{div}}(\tilde H)e^{i\theta})$, $\operatorname{Re}((\widehat{\operatorname{curl}}(\tilde E)-i\omega\mu\tilde H)e^{i\theta})$ and $\operatorname{Re}((\widehat{\operatorname{curl}}(\tilde H)+i\omega\varepsilon\tilde E)e^{i\theta})$. If the real equations hold these residuals vanish for all times in a nonempty open interval. At each fixed spatial point, varying time supplies two real phases whose difference is not in $\pi\mathbb Z$ because $\omega>0$; the nonzero spatial factor $e^{-\operatorname{Im}\beta x_3}$ may be cancelled. By [F3](d) each amplitude residual is therefore zero, proving the converse. All steps are complex-linear algebra in the amplitudes. [F1, F3]

1.2 [Smoothness of the constructed fields] In either family the amplitudes $\tilde E,\tilde H$ are finite sums of constant multiples of the components of $\nabla_th$ or $\nabla_te$ and of $h$ or $e$; the profiles $h,e$ are finite products of functions $\cos\frac{m\pi x_1}{a}$, $\cos\frac{n\pi x_2}{b}$, $\sin\frac{m\pi x_1}{a}$, $\sin\frac{n\pi x_2}{b}$ composed with affine functions, hence are $C^\infty$ by [F6] (chain rule for the affine substitutions composed with the sine and cosine derivatives, and the product rule); and $e^{i\theta}$ is $C^\infty$ by [F6] (the cartesian form exhibits it as $e^{-qx_3}(\cos\varphi+i\sin\varphi)$ with affine $\varphi$, a finite combination of products of smooth functions). Therefore $E=\operatorname{Re}(\tilde Ee^{i\theta})$ and $B=\mu\operatorname{Re}(\tilde He^{i\theta})$, as finite sums of products of $C^\infty$ complex functions and of their real parts, have all iterated partial derivatives existing and continuous: differentiating a product once in a coordinate lowers no regularity by [F6], so induction on the order gives $C^\infty$, and in particular every derivative used below exists and is continuous. The factor $(\beta/k^2)\nabla_t$ is dimensionless, $(\omega\mu/k^2)\nabla_t$ carries impedance $\mathrm V\,\mathrm A^{-1}$, and $(\omega\varepsilon/k^2)\nabla_t$ carries admittance $\mathrm A\,\mathrm V^{-1}$; multiplication by $H_*$ or $E_*$ therefore gives the stated electric and magnetic amplitude units. [given, F3, F5, F6, algebra]

1.3 [Dispersion relation and cutoff] Let $k_c^2=\omega^2\varepsilon\mu-\beta^2>0$ and put $\omega_c:=k_c/\sqrt{\varepsilon\mu}>0$; the number $\omega_c$ is well defined because $\varepsilon\mu>0$ and $k_c^2>0$ [F7]. Real $\beta$ with $\beta^2=\omega^2\varepsilon\mu-k_c^2$ exist exactly when $\omega^2\varepsilon\mu-k_c^2\ge0$, i.e. $\omega^2\ge k_c^2/(\varepsilon\mu)=\omega_c^2$, i.e. $\omega\ge\omega_c$ (both $\omega$ and $\omega_c$ are positive); then the real solutions are $\beta=\pm\sqrt{\omega^2\varepsilon\mu-k_c^2}$ [F7], and $\beta\ne0$ exactly when $\omega>\omega_c$, while $\beta=0$ occurs exactly at $\omega=\omega_c$. If $\omega<\omega_c$ the right side is negative and the two solutions of $\beta^2=-(\!k_c^2-\omega^2\varepsilon\mu)$ are $\beta=\pm i\sqrt{k_c^2-\omega^2\varepsilon\mu}$ because $(i\gamma)^2=-\gamma^2$ for real $\gamma$ [F7]. For any such root the phase factor obeys $e^{i\theta}=e^{i\beta x_3}e^{-i\omega t}$, and $e^{i\beta x_3}=e^{-\operatorname{Im}\beta\,x_3}(\cos(\operatorname{Re}\beta\,x_3)+i\sin(\operatorname{Re}\beta\,x_3))$ by [F6], so the field carries the real modulation factor $e^{-\operatorname{Im}\beta\,x_3}$ in the declared phase convention; on a declared half-guide $x_3\ge0$ with $\operatorname{Im}\beta>0$ this factor decays, and no propagating-power claim is made. The verification of clauses (a) and (b) below uses only $\beta^2=\omega^2\varepsilon\mu-k^2$ (equivalently $k^2+\beta^2=\omega^2\varepsilon\mu$), so it covers imaginary and complex $\beta$ as well. [F3, F6, F7]

2.1 [The TE family] Fix $m,n\ge0$ with $(m,n)\ne(0,0)$, put $h:=H_*h_{mn}$, $k^2:=k_{mn}^2$ (so $k^2>0$ by [F5] and $k^2=\omega^2\varepsilon\mu-\beta^2=k_c^2$ by the required dispersion relation), and let $\tilde E:=-\frac{i\omega\mu}{k^2}\,e_z\times\nabla_th$, $\tilde H:=\frac{i\beta}{k^2}\nabla_th+e_zh$, $E=\operatorname{Re}(\tilde Ee^{i\theta})$, $B=\mu\operatorname{Re}(\tilde He^{i\theta})$. First, $e_z\times\nabla_th$ is transverse by [F4], so $\tilde E_3=0$ and $E_z=\operatorname{Re}(0)=0$: the field is TE. Second, the amplitude equations of step 1.1 hold: $\widehat{\operatorname{div}}(\tilde E)=-\frac{i\omega\mu}{k^2}\bigl(\partial_1(-\partial_2h)+\partial_2(\partial_1h)+i\beta\cdot0\bigr)=-\frac{i\omega\mu}{k^2}\operatorname{div}(e_z\times\nabla_th)=0$ by [F4]; $\widehat{\operatorname{div}}(\tilde H)=\frac{i\beta}{k^2}(\partial_1^2h+\partial_2^2h)+i\beta h=\frac{i\beta}{k^2}\Delta_th+i\beta h=\frac{i\beta}{k^2}(-k^2h)+i\beta h=0$ by [F5]; $\widehat{\operatorname{curl}}(\tilde E)=\bigl(-i\beta\tilde E_2,\ i\beta\tilde E_1,\ \partial_1\tilde E_2-\partial_2\tilde E_1\bigr)=\bigl(-\frac{\beta\omega\mu}{k^2}\partial_1h,\ -\frac{\beta\omega\mu}{k^2}\partial_2h,\ -\frac{i\omega\mu}{k^2}(\partial_1^2h+\partial_2^2h)\bigr)=\bigl(\frac{i\beta}{k^2}i\omega\mu\,\partial_1h,\ \frac{i\beta}{k^2}i\omega\mu\,\partial_2h,\ i\omega\mu h\bigr)=i\omega\mu\tilde H$, using $i\beta\tilde E_1=i\beta\frac{i\omega\mu}{k^2}\partial_2h=-\frac{\beta\omega\mu}{k^2}\partial_2h$ and $-\frac{i\omega\mu}{k^2}(-k^2h)=i\omega\mu h$; and $\widehat{\operatorname{curl}}(\tilde H)=\bigl(\partial_2h-i\beta\frac{i\beta}{k^2}\partial_2h,\ i\beta\frac{i\beta}{k^2}\partial_1h-\partial_1h,\ \frac{i\beta}{k^2}(\partial_1\partial_2h-\partial_2\partial_1h)\bigr)=\bigl(1+\frac{\beta^2}{k^2}\bigr)\bigl(\partial_2h,-\partial_1h,0\bigr)=\frac{k^2+\beta^2}{k^2}\bigl(\partial_2h,-\partial_1h,0\bigr)=\frac{\omega^2\varepsilon\mu}{k^2}\bigl(\partial_2h,-\partial_1h,0\bigr)=-i\omega\varepsilon\tilde E$, where the last mixed-partial difference vanishes by the identity $\operatorname{div}(e_z\times\nabla_th)=0$ of [F4] and the final equality uses $\tilde E=-\frac{i\omega\mu}{k^2}(-\partial_2h,\partial_1h,0)$. Third, the open-wall conditions: on $\{x_1=0\}$ and $\{x_1=a\}$ (normals $\mp e_1$) the tangential components of $E$ are $E_2=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(-\frac{i\omega\mu}{k^2}\partial_1h\,e^{i\theta}\bigr)=-\frac{\omega\mu}{k^2}\operatorname{Re}(i\partial_1h\,e^{i\theta})=0$ and $E_3=0$, because $\partial_1h=0$ there by [F5], and $n\cdot B=\mp B_1=\mp\mu\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\frac{i\beta}{k^2}\partial_1h\,e^{i\theta}\bigr)=0$; on $\{x_2=0\}$ and $\{x_2=b\}$ (normals $\mp e_2$) the tangential components are $E_1=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\frac{i\omega\mu}{k^2}\partial_2h\,e^{i\theta}\bigr)=0$, since $\partial_2h=0$ there by [F5], and $E_3=0$, while $n\cdot B=\mp B_2=\mp\mu\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\frac{i\beta}{k^2}\partial_2h\,e^{i\theta}\bigr)=0$. Hence $n\times E=0$ and $n\cdot B=0$ on every open wall, and clause (a) holds. [step 1.1, step 1.2, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, algebra]

2.2 [The TM family] Fix $m,n\ge1$, put $e:=E_*e_{mn}$ and $k^2:=k_{mn}^2=k_c^2>0$, and let $\tilde E:=\frac{i\beta}{k^2}\nabla_te+e_ze$, $\tilde H:=\frac{i\omega\varepsilon}{k^2}e_z\times\nabla_te$, $E=\operatorname{Re}(\tilde Ee^{i\theta})$, $B=\mu\operatorname{Re}(\tilde He^{i\theta})$. Here $e_z\times\nabla_te$ is transverse by [F4], so $\tilde H_3=0$ and $B_z=\mu\operatorname{Re}(0)=0$: the field is TM. The amplitude equations hold: $\widehat{\operatorname{div}}(\tilde E)=\frac{i\beta}{k^2}(\partial_1^2e+\partial_2^2e)+i\beta e=\frac{i\beta}{k^2}(-k^2e)+i\beta e=0$ by [F5]; $\widehat{\operatorname{div}}(\tilde H)=\frac{i\omega\varepsilon}{k^2}\bigl(\partial_1(-\partial_2e)+\partial_2(\partial_1e)\bigr)+i\beta\cdot0=0$ by the identity $\operatorname{div}(e_z\times\nabla_te)=0$ of [F4]; $\widehat{\operatorname{curl}}(\tilde E)=\bigl(\partial_2e-i\beta\frac{i\beta}{k^2}\partial_2e,\ i\beta\frac{i\beta}{k^2}\partial_1e-\partial_1e,\ \frac{i\beta}{k^2}(\partial_1\partial_2e-\partial_2\partial_1e)\bigr)=\frac{k^2+\beta^2}{k^2}\bigl(\partial_2e,-\partial_1e,0\bigr)=\frac{\omega^2\varepsilon\mu}{k^2}\bigl(\partial_2e,-\partial_1e,0\bigr)=i\omega\mu\tilde H$ (the mixed difference vanishes by [F4] and the final equality uses $\tilde H=\frac{i\omega\varepsilon}{k^2}(-\partial_2e,\partial_1e,0)$); and $\widehat{\operatorname{curl}}(\tilde H)=\bigl(-i\beta\tilde H_2,\ i\beta\tilde H_1,\ \partial_1\tilde H_2-\partial_2\tilde H_1\bigr)=\bigl(\frac{\beta\omega\varepsilon}{k^2}\partial_1e,\ \frac{\beta\omega\varepsilon}{k^2}\partial_2e,\ \frac{i\omega\varepsilon}{k^2}(\partial_1^2e+\partial_2^2e)\bigr)=\frac{\omega\varepsilon\beta}{k^2}\nabla_te-i\omega\varepsilon e_ze=-i\omega\varepsilon\tilde E$, using $\frac{i\omega\varepsilon}{k^2}(-k^2e)=-i\omega\varepsilon e$, in agreement with $\tilde E=\frac{i\beta}{k^2}\nabla_te+e_ze$. The open-wall conditions: $e=0$ on $\partial R$ by [F5], so $E_3=\operatorname{Re}(ee^{i\theta})=0$ on the walls; on $\{x_1=0\}$ and $\{x_1=a\}$ (normals $\mp e_1$) the tangential components are $E_2=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\frac{i\beta}{k^2}\partial_2e\,e^{i\theta}\bigr)=0$, since $\partial_2e$ carries the factor $\sin\frac{m\pi x_1}{a}$ vanishing there, and $E_3=0$, while $n\cdot B=\mp B_1=\mp\mu\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\frac{i\omega\varepsilon}{k^2}(-\partial_2e)e^{i\theta}\bigr)=0$; on $\{x_2=0\}$ and $\{x_2=b\}$ (normals $\mp e_2$) the tangential components are $E_1=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\frac{i\beta}{k^2}\partial_1e\,e^{i\theta}\bigr)=0$, since $\partial_1e$ carries the factor $\sin\frac{n\pi x_2}{b}$ vanishing there, and $E_3=0$, while $n\cdot B=\mp B_2=\mp\mu\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\frac{i\omega\varepsilon}{k^2}\partial_1e\,e^{i\theta}\bigr)=0$. Hence the wall conditions hold and clause (b) follows. [step 1.1, step 1.2, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, algebra]

2.3 [Zero indices and nonvanishing] For $m=n=0$ one has $h_{00}\equiv1$, so $\nabla_th_{00}=0$ and $k_{00}^2=0$: the divided reconstruction formulas of the TE family are undefined, and this exclusion is by the reconstruction denominator. A purely longitudinal constant candidate has $\tilde E=0$ and $\tilde H=ce_z$; the Faraday amplitude equation of step 1.1 then reads $\widehat{\operatorname{curl}}(0)=(0,0,0)=i\omega\mu ce_z$, so $c=0$ because $\omega\mu>0$: it is excluded by the full field equations, not by divergence. Consistently $\widehat{\operatorname{div}}(ce_z)=i\beta c$, which vanishes at $\beta=0$, so the divergence equation alone would not exclude a constant longitudinal amplitude at $\beta=0$; the exclusion uses Faraday as displayed. For $m=0$ or $n=0$ the TM profile vanishes identically, $e_{mn}\equiv0$, so $\tilde E=e_ze=0$ and $\tilde H=0$; hence the TM indices are restricted to $m,n\ge1$. Conversely, for $(m,n)\ne(0,0)$ one has $\nabla_th_{mn}\ne0$: if $m\ne0$ then $\partial_1h_{mn}=-\frac{m\pi}{a}\sin\frac{m\pi x_1}{a}\cos\frac{n\pi x_2}{b}$ is nonzero at $x_1=\frac a{2m}$ and an interior $x_2\in(0,b)$ with $\cos(n\pi x_2/b)\ne0$ (take $x_2=b/(4n)$ when $n>0$, and $x_2=b/2$ when $n=0$), and if $m=0$ then $n\ne0$ and the analogous $\partial_2h_{mn}$ is nonzero; and for $m,n\ge1$ one has $e_{mn}\ne0$, for instance at $x_1=\frac a{2m},x_2=\frac b{2n}$. Since $H_*,E_*>0$, the TE electric amplitude is a nonzero multiple of $e_z\times\nabla_th_{mn}$ and its longitudinal magnetic amplitude is $H_*h_{mn}\ne0$; the TM longitudinal electric amplitude is $E_*e_{mn}\ne0$ and its magnetic amplitude is a nonzero multiple of $e_z\times\nabla_te_{mn}$. Thus both displayed fields are nonzero for all admissible indices, even at $\beta=0$ where the other transverse amplitude vanishes, and by the consequence clause of [F3](d) the real fields $E=\operatorname{Re}(\tilde Ee^{i\theta})$, $B=\mu\operatorname{Re}(\tilde He^{i\theta})$ are not identically zero. [step 1.1, F3, F5, F6, algebra]
