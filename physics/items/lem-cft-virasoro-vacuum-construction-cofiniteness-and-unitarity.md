## Statement

For every $c\in\mathbb C$, the induced vacuum module $V^c=U(\mathrm{Vir})\otimes_{U(\operatorname{span}\{C,L_n:n\ge-1\})}\mathbb C_c$ has its universal conformal vertex-algebra structure and a unique simple CFT-type quotient $L(c,0)$. Here C acts by c and every $L_n$, $n\ge-1$, kills the inducing vacuum. For coprime $p,q\ge2$, set $c_{p,q}=1-6(p-q)^2/(pq)$ and $D=(p-1)(q-1)/2$. Then
$$\dim L(c_{p,q},0)/C_2(L(c_{p,q},0))\le D.$$
For real $c<1$, a positive-definite invariant Hermitian form on the simple vacuum module exists exactly when $c=1-6/(m(m+1))$ for an integer $m\ge2$. The form is conjugate-linear in the first variable and satisfies $L_n^\dagger=L_{-n}$ and $C^\dagger=C$. Consequently among the coprime minimal parameters the vacuum form exists exactly for $|p-q|=1$. No complete simple-module classification, Zhu-ideal factorization or rationality conclusion is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

## Proof

1.1 For c∈C put $b_{\mathrm{vac}}=\operatorname{span}\{C,L_n:n≥−1\}$; it is a Lie subalgebra. Let $C_c$ be its one-dimensional representation with $C=c$ and every $L_n=0$. It is consistent: the central bracket coefficient vanishes for $n=−1,0,1$, so no bracket within $b_{\mathrm{vac}}$ gives a nonzero scalar. Set $V^{c}=U(\operatorname{Vir})⊗_{U(b_{\mathrm{vac}})}C_c$. PBW with the negative block $\{L_{−n}:n≥2\}$ proves that the ordered monomials $L_{−n_1}⋯L_{−n_r}1$, $n_1≥⋯≥n_r≥2$, are a basis. In particular 1≠0, $V^{c}_0=C1$, every homogeneous degree is finite dimensional, and $L_0$ acts by its nonnegative integer degree. [F1, given, algebra]

2.1 Let $T=L_{−1}$ and $Ω(z)=\sum _{n∈Z}L_n z^{−n−2}$. It is a field on $V^{c}$: a positive $L_n$ lowers the degree by n, so kills a fixed homogeneous vector when n exceeds its degree. We have T1=0 and $[T,L_n]=−(n+1)L_{n−1}$, proving [T,Ω(z)]=∂_zΩ(z). For $ω=L_{−2}1$, repeated brackets give $T^{j}ω=j!L_{−j−2}1$, so $Ω(z)1=e^{zT}ω$. [F1, given, algebra, step 1.1]

3.1 The Virasoro commutator gives the exact distribution identity $[Ω(z),Ω(w)]=∂_wΩ(w)δ(z,w)+2Ω(w)∂_wδ(z,w)+(c/12)∂_w^{3}δ(z,w)$. For example the coefficients of the central term are $(c/12)(m+1)m(m−1)δ_{m+n,0}$ in the $L_m,L_n$ convention. The identities $(z−w)δ=0$ and $(z−w)∂_w^{j}δ=j∂_w^{j−1}δ$ imply $(z−w)^{4}[Ω(z),Ω(w)]=0$. Thus Ω is local with itself. Its negative field modes are $Ω_{(m)}=L_{m−1}$, $m<0$, hence are precisely the $L_{−n}$, n≥2, which span $V^{c}$ on the vacuum by PBW. All four hypotheses of the actual reconstruction lemma are now verified. It supplies the unique vertex-algebra state-field map with Y(ω,z)=Ω(z). The Virasoro relations, $T=L_{−1}$, and the proved nonnegative finite grading verify the local conformal-vertex-algebra definition for $V^{c}$. [F1, F3, given, algebra, step 2.1]

4.1 A Virasoro-stable subspace $U⊂V^{c}$ is stable under every reconstructed state mode. Indeed every state is a linear combination of the negative Ω-mode words above. The field of Ω_{(−k)}b is the corresponding negative field product, obtained inductively by the actual reconstruction proof. Its coefficient on a fixed vector is a finite sum of compositions of Ω-modes and modes of the shorter state's field, by the explicit iterate formula in that supplier. Induction on the state word therefore proves preservation of U. Conversely a vertex ideal is stable under Ω-modes. Thus the two notions coincide. Translation is already an Ω-mode. Every Virasoro-stable subspace is graded, since $L_0$ has distinct integer eigenvalues and polynomial interpolation projects the finite support of any vector to its components. A proper one has no grade-zero vector, since 1 generates $V^{c}$. The sum J of all proper Virasoro submodules still has zero grade-zero space, so is proper; it is the unique maximal proper submodule. By the preceding paragraph it is a vertex ideal, and $L(c,0)=V^{c}/J$ is a simple vertex algebra with the descended conformal vector. Its vacuum remains nonzero, its grade-zero space is C1, and its grades are finite dimensional and nonnegative. Thus it is a simple conformal vertex algebra of CFT type exactly as used by the minimal-model Statement. This includes $c=0$: the simple quotient may be C with zero conformal vector, which the local definition allows. No nonzero-conformal-vector or nontriviality assumption has been silently added. [F1, F3, F4, given, algebra, step 3.1]

5.1 Let $B_N(c)$ be the real PBW Gram matrix on the universal vacuum quotient $V^{c}=M(c,0)/U(\operatorname{Vir})L_{−1}1$, using partitions of N with all parts≥2. It is polynomial in real c. If $G_N(c,h)$ is the Verma Gram matrix, then generically in c  $\det B_N(c)=2^{-P(N-1)}\lim_{h\to0}\det G_N(c,h)/(h^{P(N-1)}\det G_{N-1}(c,1))$. To verify this, choose at grade N the PBW block consisting of all descendants of $L_{−1}v_h$ and the complementary PBW block with no rightmost $L_{−1}$. All entries pairing a descendant of $L_{−1}v_h$ with any vector vanish at $h=0$, because $L_{−1}v_0$ is singular and lies in the radical. The first block, divided by 2h, tends to the normalized Verma form of weight1: moving positive modes across the pairing proves contravariance in the limit, the top pairing is 2h, and the order-h errors in the positive-mode highest-vector equations vanish after pairing against the other singular-descendant block. More explicitly $L_1L_{−1}v_h=2hv_h$ and $L_nL_{−1}v_h=0$ for $n>1$; pairing the exceptional $v_h$ term against a descendant of $L_{−1}v_h$ introduces one more factor h by the preceding radical vanishing. Thus the limit is the unique normalized contravariant form on M(c,1). The off-diagonal blocks are O(h). When det $G_{N−1}(c,1)$ is nonzero, the Schur complement of the first block therefore tends to $B_N(c)$, giving the displayed formula. It extends as an identity of rational functions in c and then by polynomial continuation wherever cancellation is needed. [F1, F2, given, algebra, step 4.1]

6.1 For sufficiently large positive c, $B_N(c)$ is positive definite. Rescale a PBW vector of length l by $c^{−l/2}$. At c→+∞ only complete central contractions survive. Two distinct partitions have no complete matched central contraction, and the limiting matrix is diagonal with entries $∏_{n≥2}m_n!((n³−n)/12)^{m_n}>0$. Every noncentral commutator lowers the power of c after this scaling. Hence the determinant is positive at sufficiently large c. [F1, given, algebra, step 5.1]

7.1 The Kac factors locate every possible zero of $B_N$. Put $c=1-6(b-b^{-1})^2$ and $h_{r,s}=((rb-sb^{-1})^2-(b-b^{-1})^2)/4$. Apart from the identically zero $h_{1,1}$, a zero at $h=0$ solves $(r-1)b^2=s-1$ or $(r+1)b^2=s+1$. For a reduced positive ratio $b^2=q/p$, $p,q\ge2$ coprime, the first possible additional zero occurs at $(r,s)=(p-1,q-1)$, grade $N_0=(p-1)(q-1)$; the other family begins at $(p+1,q+1)$, and multiples have larger grade. Ratios with $p=1$ or $q=1$ give $c\le-2$ and do not occur at positive $c$. At $c=1$ the numerator zeros with $r=s\ge2$ cancel the denominator zeros with $|r-s|=2$: the numerator factor $h_{r,r}=(r^2-1)(1-c)/24$ is simple in $c$, while the pair of denominator factors with indices $(s+2,s),(s,s+2)$ has a simple product zero in $c$ (their individual leading terms are opposite nonzero multiples of $\sqrt{1-c}$). Their multiplicities match because $N-1-s(s+2)=N-(s+1)^2$. Thus $c=1$ is not an extra zero of $B_N$. For $c>1$, choose $b$ with the stated central charge and put $u=b^2$. A numerator zero at $h=0$ forces $u>0$ by the two equations above. A denominator zero requires $h_{r,s}=1$, equivalently $(r^2-1)u^2-2(rs+1)u+(s^2-1)=0$. For $r>1$ its roots are $(s-1)/(r+1)$ and $(s+1)/(r-1)$; after discarding a zero root, every remaining root is positive. For $r=1$ its only possible nonzero root is $(s-1)/2$, which is positive. Thus every such root is positive, which gives $c=1-6(u-1)^2/u\le1$, a contradiction. So neither numerator nor denominator has a nontrivial Kac zero for $c>1$, and $B_N$ has no zero there. [F1, F2, given, algebra, step 6.1]

8.1 For coprime p,q≥2 put $c=1−6(p−q)²/(pq)$, $N=(p−1)(q−1)$, $D=N/2$. This proof uses the actual all-parameter Kac determinant and the universal vacuum construction. It proves the original minimal-model C2 clause, including a nonzero pure $L_{−2}$ coefficient; it does not assume a Feigin–Fuchs singular-vector formula or classify modules. Use the vacuum determinant identity proved above:  $\det B_n(c)=2^{-P(n-1)}\lim_{h\to0}\det G_n(c,h)/(h^{P(n-1)}\det G_{n-1}(c,1))$. Here $B_n$ is the Gram matrix on the universal vacuum module in its parts≥2 PBW basis. At b²=q/p, the positive-index solutions of $h_{r,s}(c)=0$, other than (1,1), have (r,s)=(kp−1,kq−1) or (kp+1,kq+1), with k≥1. Their first grade is N, attained only by (p−1,q−1). The positive-index solutions of $h_{r,s}(c)=1$ have (r,s)=(kp+1,kq−1) or (kp−1,kq+1), k≥1; their first grades are (p+1)(q−1) and (p−1)(q+1), both strictly greater than N. These statements follow by solving qr−ps=±(p−q) or ±(p+q), respectively, using gcd(p,q)=1. Thus the determinant formula makes every $B_n$ with $n<N$ nondegenerate and $B_N$ degenerate. The exceptional $h_{1,1}$ factor is exactly the factor $h^{P(n−1)}$ removed before the limit. The derivative of the additional $h=0$ Kac factor at this parameter is nonzero ($b>0$ and b≠1), so the formula has a simple zero at $n=N$, though only existence of a null vector is needed. Choose v≠0 in the radical of $B_N$. Contravariance makes $L_m$ v orthogonal to every vector of grade N−m, for every $m>0$. When N−m≥0, that grade is nondegenerate by the preceding paragraph, so $L_m v=0$; when $N−m<0$ it vanishes by the vacuum grading. Hence v is an actual singular vector, not just a vector with determinant zero. The integer N is even: if p and q are both odd, both p−1,q−1 are even; otherwise one of them is even because p,q cannot both be even. [F1, F2, given, algebra, step 7.1]

9.1 Filter the vacuum PBW space by word length in the modes $L_{−n}$, n≥2. Its associated graded is the polynomial ring $C[x_2,x_3,…]$, where $x_n$ is the symbol of $L_{−n}$; at a fixed conformal degree only finitely many variables and words occur. On the top-length symbol, every positive $L_m$ acts as the derivation  $D_m=\sum _{n≥m+2}(m+n)x_{n−m}∂/∂x_n$. Indeed $[L_m,L_{−n}]=(m+n)L_{m−n}$ plus a central term. When n≥m+2 the new negative mode still has index at most−2 and preserves word length. When $n=m+1$ it is $L_{−1}$; moving it to the right until it kills the vacuum replaces two negative factors by one in every surviving commutator. When $n=m$ it is $L_0$ or the scalar central term and lowers length by at least one; when $n<m$ the positive mode must commute through another factor before surviving, again lowering length. Thus there are no other top-length contributions. Lower-length components of v cannot contribute at its top length under any positive mode. [F1, F4, given, algebra, step 8.1]

10.1 Let f be the nonzero highest-length symbol of v. Singularity gives $D_m f=0$ for every m≥1. If $x_R$, $R>2$, is the largest variable occurring in f, then $D_{R−2}f=(2R−2)x_2∂f/∂x_R$, because no variable of index greater than R occurs. The polynomial ring is an integral domain in characteristic zero, so f is independent of $x_R$, a contradiction. Repeating removes every variable except $x_2$. Homogeneity of v forces $f=a x_2^{D}$ with a≠0. Therefore v has a nonzero coefficient of $L_{−2}^{D1}$, and every other PBW monomial in it has at least one part≥3. This proves the needed coefficient, rather than asserting it from determinant vanishing. [F1, given, algebra, step 9.1]

11.1 Let L(c,0) be the simple quotient of the constructed universal vacuum module. The vector v is in its proper radical, so its image is zero. Any PBW monomial with some part n≥3 is in C2 of this quotient: order that part leftmost, write $L_{−n}=ω_{(−n+1)}$, and use the state derivative formula $(T^{n−3}ω)_{(−2)}=(n−2)!ω_{(−n+1)}$. Thus $v=0$ gives a nonzero multiple of $L_{−2}^{D1}$ equal to a linear combination of C2 vectors. Moreover the span of PBW monomials with a part≥3 is stable under $L_{−2}$: reorder using $[L_{−2},L_{−n}]=(n−2)L_{−n−2}$, whose new index remains at least3. Applying $L_{−2}$ repeatedly to the relation therefore places every pure power $L_{−2}^{j1}$, j≥D, in C2 as well. All remaining PBW monomials already lie in C2 by the same leftmost-part argument. Consequently L(c,0)/C2 is spanned by 1, $L_{−2}1,…,L_{−2}^{D−1}1$ and has dimension at most D. The case $p,q=2,3$ gives $N=2,D=1$ and the one-dimensional trivial quotient, as expected. [F1, F4, given, algebra, step 10.1]

12.1 Set $c_m=1−6/[m(m+1)]$, m≥2. Fix $c_m<c<c_{m+1}$ and $N=m(m+1)=A$. There is precisely one possible determinant zero above $c_m$, namely $c_{m+1}$, and it is simple. Here is the elementary bound excluding every other rational root. If $c_{p,q}>c_m$, with $d=|p−q|$, then $pq>d²A$. The increasing function x−sqrt(d²+4x)+1 gives  (p−1)(q−1)>d²A−d(2m+1)+1. For d≥2 this is greater than A: $(d²−1)m(m+1)−d(2m+1)+1>0$ for m≥2, starting already at $d=2$ and increasing thereafter. If $d=1$, write $q=p+1$. Then $c_{p,p+1}>c_m$ implies p≥m+1, while (p−1)p≤A implies p≤m+1. Thus only $p=m+1,q=m+2$ occurs, with $N_0=A$. At that root the additional Kac factor has multiplicity P(0)=1. The denominator det $G_{N−1}(c,1)$ is nonzero there: the equation $h_{r,s}=1$ gives qr−ps=±(p+q), whose first positive solutions are (p+1,q−1) and (p−1,q+1), both of product greater than N−1. There are no other $h=0$ numerator factors at grade≤N there. Hence det $B_N$ has one simple zero at $c_{m+1}$ and no other zero in $(c_m,+∞)$. It is positive for large c, so it is negative on $(c_m,c_{m+1})$. [F1, F2, given, algebra, step 11.1]

13.1 A positive invariant Hermitian form on the simple vacuum quotient pulls back, after normalizing the vacuum norm to1, to the canonical vacuum Gram form, because adjointness determines each PBW pairing. Every $B_N$ would therefore be positive semidefinite. The preceding negative determinant excludes every open interval $(c_m,c_{m+1})$. Negative c is already excluded by $||L_{−2}1||²=c/2$. Since $c_m$ increases from0 to1, the only allowed c in [0,1) are the $c_m$. This proves precisely the necessary vacuum restriction used below, without an unproved FQS curve argument. [F1, given, algebra, step 12.1]

14.1 Construct the algebraic NS Fock space on two complex fermion species $p^{a}_r,q^{a}_r$, $a=1,2$, r∈Z+1/2, with $\{p^{a}_r,q^{b}_s\}=δ_abδ_{r+s,0}$, the other anticommutators zero, and all positive modes killing a vacuum of norm1. Fix the order of negative generators by increasing positive frequency, then by species and by p before q. Ordered negative-mode exterior monomials are an orthonormal basis, with $(p^{a}_r)^\dagger =q^{a}_{−r}$, $(q^{a}_r)^\dagger =p^{a}_{−r}$. This defines a positive Hermitian space directly. Give each negative generator $p^{a}_{−r},q^{a}_{−r}$, $r>0$, degree r, the vacuum degree0, and each exterior monomial the sum of its generator degrees. This is a grading by nonnegative half-integers. Each degree E has finitely many monomials, because only the four generators at each positive half-integer frequency r≤E can occur and each occurs at most once. CAR makes every mode $p^{a}_r,q^{a}_r$ homogeneous of degree−r: negative modes create, and positive modes contract the paired negative generator. Consequently each normally ordered summand of $J^{ab}_n$ below has degree−n; so does its locally finite sum. In particular $J^{ab}_n$ kills degree E whenever $n>E$. Define $J^{ab}_n=\sum _r:p^{a}_r q^{b}_{n−r}$:, with creators left and the fermionic sign on interchange. Each sum is locally finite on a finite exterior monomial. Direct CAR commutation gives $[J^{ab}_n,p^{c}_s]=δ_bc p^{a}_{n+s}$ and $[J^{ab}_n,q^{c}_s]=−δ_ac q^{b}_{n+s}$. Therefore the current-current commutator differs from δ_bc $J^{ad}_{m+n}−δ_ad J^{cb}_{m+n}$ by an operator commuting with all fermions, hence a scalar. The joint kernel of the positive modes is exactly the vacuum line: the annihilator paired with a negative generator contracts it from each ordered exterior word containing it, with sign ±1; distinct words containing that generator give distinct shortened words. Thus annihilation forces every coefficient of a word containing that generator to vanish, and applying this to every generator leaves only the empty word. A commuting operator sends the vacuum into this joint kernel, so its value there is scalar; commuting with all creators fixes that same scalar on the entire exterior basis. Energy forces that scalar to vanish unless $m+n=0$. For $m>0$ its vacuum matrix coefficient counts the m half-integers between−m and0 in the two creating modes, giving mδ_adδ_bc. Antisymmetry gives all other m. Hence  $[J^{ab}_m,J^{cd}_n]=δ_bc J^{ad}_{m+n}−δ_ad J^{cb}_{m+n}+mδ_adδ_bcδ_{m+n,0}$,   $(J^{ab}_n)^\dagger =J^{ba}_{−n}$. [given, given, algebra, step 13.1]

15.1 Take $E=J^{12}$, $F=J^{21}$, $H=J^{11}−J^{22}$. These are restricted sl2 currents at level1, with normalized form B(E,F)=1,B(H,H)=2 and adjoints $E_n^\dagger =F_{−n}$, $H_n^\dagger =H_{−n}$. Every nonnegative current mode kills the vacuum. Tensor l+1 copies of this explicitly positive Fock representation. The sum of currents in the first l factors has level l; the last factor has level1; their diagonal has level l+1. The affine brackets follow by adding independent-factor brackets. All current actions are restricted and homogeneous for the finite-piece fermion energy grading. [F5, given, algebra, step 14.1]

16.1 Apply the published Sugawara current-action and Virasoro theorems to these three actions. For sl2 the adjoint Casimir for B is4, so $h^{\vee}=2$; this follows immediately from the basis E,F,H and their three brackets. Their stress tensors have central charges 3l/(l+2), 1 and 3(l+1)/(l+3). The Sugawara modes are Hermitian in the stated adjoint convention: the quadratic Casimir sum is H H/2+EF+FE, which is fixed by adjoint and index reversal; reindexing the finite-on-vectors normal-order sums gives $L_n^\dagger =L_{−n}$. Put $C_n=L_n^{(l)}+L_n^{(1)}−L_n^{\mathrm{diag}(l+1)}$. The published current-action formula gives $[C_m,J^{\mathrm{diag}}_n]=0$. Since the denominator Sugawara modes are locally finite quadratic expressions in those currents, $C_m$ commutes with every denominator Sugawara mode as well. Subtracting the two Virasoro brackets therefore proves that $C_n$ is Virasoro with charge  3l/(l+2)+1−3(l+1)/(l+3)=1−6/[(l+2)(l+3)]. [F5, given, algebra, step 15.1]

17.1 No branching or integrable-module classification is used. These $C_n$ inherit $C_n^\dagger =C_{−n}$ on the same positive Fock space. The vacuum is killed by $C_n$ for n≥−1: each Sugawara term has this property because every nonnegative current kills the vacuum and two strictly negative integer indices cannot sum to−1 or a larger integer. Let S be the cyclic Virasoro module on that vacuum under $C_n$. It has finite-dimensional nonnegative integer graded pieces: every $C_n$ has fermion energy degree−n, and $C_0$ acts by grade on its cyclic span, since $C_0$ kills the vacuum and $[C_0,C_{−n}]=nC_{−n}$. Thus a word of total index N has both fermion degree N and $C_0$ eigenvalue N, including the empty word at $N=0$. Thus its inherited positive form is an actual vacuum invariant Hermitian form. It is simple. Indeed any proper submodule is graded by interpolation in $C_0$, has zero vacuum grade, and has a grade-by-grade orthogonal complement stable under every $C_n$ by the adjoint relation. That complement contains the vacuum and hence all its cyclic span S, so the submodule is zero. The universal vacuum construction therefore identifies S with L(c,0) at $c=1−6/[(l+2)(l+3)]$. Taking $l=m−2$ gives every $c_m$ for m≥3. For $m=2$ use the one-dimensional trivial Virasoro representation at $c=0$, whose vacuum norm is positive. This proves sufficiency for every $c_m$, including the degenerate minimal model. [F1, F3, F5, given, algebra, step 16.1]
