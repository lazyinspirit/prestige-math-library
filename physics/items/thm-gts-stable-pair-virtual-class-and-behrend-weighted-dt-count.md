## Statement

Let $X$ be a nonsingular projective threefold over $\mathbb C$, with effective curve class $\beta$ and integer $n$. For the stable-pair moduli $P_n(X,\beta)$ of [F1], the Pandharipande–Thomas Theorem 2.14 states that there is an algebraic virtual class
$
[P_n(X,\beta)]^{\rm vir}\in A_{c_\beta}(P_n(X,\beta),\mathbb Z),\qquad c_\beta=\int_\beta c_1(X)=-\chi(R\operatorname{Hom}(I^\bullet,I^\bullet)_0),
$
induced by the fixed-determinant obstruction theory of the stable-pair complex $I^\bullet=[\mathcal O_X\to F]$. The source constructs the trace-free complex and Atiyah-class map, but explicitly invokes results [24, 41, 42] to prove that this map is a perfect obstruction theory. The local Behrend–Fantechi construction [F2] gives the zero-section mechanism only after a POT is established and by itself proves neither this pair-specific POT nor the source's integral Chow refinement.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; construct the moduli from a bounded square-zero Hilbert quotient, prove the pair-specific Atiyah obstruction and duality on all tests, and transport integral cone classes through explicit scheme replacements.

1.1 We construct the named moduli object before using it. Choose a very ample A and put d=β(A). If the pure-dimension-one convention makes the moduli empty, the zero virtual class proves the assertion. For the relative calculations below write Y→B for a smooth projective threefold family, $Y_{S}=Y×_{B}$ S, and $π:Y_{S}→S$; the absolute claim is $Y=X$, B=Spec C. Work on affine Noetherian test bases S. The relative construction is included to supply the next family's compatibility, not as an extra hypothesis on the absolute claim. Choose a very ample A on X and put d=β(A). For a stable pair the image of $O_{X}→F$ is $O_{C}$ for a Cohen–Macaulay curve C, and $0→O_{C}→F→Q→0$ [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, algebra]

2.1 has Q of finite length. Here C has degree d: away from the finite support of Q, $F=O_{C}$, with their full scheme multiplicities. To check the support assertion, if a function annihilates the section but not F, its image aF is a nonzero finite-support subsheaf, contradicting purity. The image $O_{C}$ is pure as a subsheaf of F, so C has no embedded zero-dimensional associated point and is Cohen–Macaulay of dimension one. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 There is a coarse uniform lower bound $χ(O_{C})≥d(2−d)$. Pick a pencil of hyperplanes with centre disjoint from C. It gives q:C→P¹ with q*O(1)=A|_C. No component is contracted, since A has positive degree on every component; thus q is proper quasi-finite and finite. The pullback of a parameter on P¹ is regular on $O_{C}$, since it contains no component. Therefore $E=q_*O_{C}$ is torsion-free on P¹ and locally free. Its rank is d: the leading coefficient of $χ(C,A^{t})=χ(P¹,E(t))$ is rank E. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Coordinate sections $x_{j}$ give algebra generators of E after dividing by either nonvanishing pencil coordinate. In any d-dimensional fibre algebra, the subspace generated by monomials of total degree at most m is all the algebra by $m=d−1$: it starts with dimension one, and if two successive subspaces agree, multiplication by every generator preserves it and it is already the whole generated algebra. Otherwise its dimension increases at each step. Consequently the global monomial maps $⊕_{m=0}^{d−1}$ $O_{P¹}(−m)^{N_{m}}$ → E [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 are fibrewise surjective, hence surjective by Nakayama. Thus E(d−1) is globally generated, so its determinant has a nonzero section and nonnegative degree. It follows that deg E≥−d(d−1). Riemann–Roch for E on P¹ (also directly GRR in degree one) gives $χ(O_{C})=χ(E)=d+\operatorname{deg}$ E≥d(2−d). [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Since $χ(O_{C})≤χ(F)=n$, only finitely many support Hilbert polynomials dt+c occur, with d(2−d)≤c≤n. If this interval is empty there are no pairs. Thus every stable-pair fibre has the same uniform generation and H¹ vanishing. This bound uses no semistable-sheaf or Quot boundedness theorem. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 For families these conditions give π_*F(mA) locally free of rank r with every base change by the actual nonnegative cohomology complex and finite-free base-change criterion. 8.1 The actual published finite-over-projective lemma makes Y projective. A quotient V→F with kernel K defines the ideal 0⊕K in $O_{Y}$ and a closed subscheme Z⊂Y containing the canonical X⊂Y. 9.1 Use the actual Hilbert scheme of Y for polynomial $P_{O_{X}}(t)+(dt+n)$, with the finite-pullback ample polarization. 10.1 Add a section $O_{X}→F$ as follows. On affine charts the nonnegative finite-free complex K of the universal F represents its global sections by Spec Sym(coker((K¹)∨→(K⁰)∨)). 11.1 Purity is an actual open on this scheme. For any flat family F on the fixed smooth threefold, the fibre modules have projective dimension at most three. 12.1 The cokernel of the universal section is coherent. 20.1 For reference the complex can be encoded by a Čech twisting cochain a: its Čech-degree-zero component is the local differential, its degree-one component is the change of basis, and a component of Čech degree p has internal degree 1−p. The equation is δa+a²=0, with the Alexander–Whitney multiplication sign (−1)^{internal degree of the first factor times Čech degree of the second}. In this equation δ deletes internal vertices; consequently its degree-two equation for degree-zero transition matrices is $g_{ij}$ $g_{jk}−g_{ik}=0$. For the initial strict presentation this is the ordinary Čech complex of the sheaf complex Hom(E,E), so its cohomology is Ext(E,E), by affine Čech comparison. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, step 19.1, algebra]

21.1 We spell out why higher twisting data are effective, rather than assuming a moduli theorem. From local complexes with a twisting cochain form the total complex of the finite sums of $j_{i_{0}…i_{p},*}E_{i_{p}}$, using δ and the maps $a_{p}$ for its differential. The twisting equation is exactly the assertion that its square is zero. After restriction to $U_{i}$, the augmented Čech complex has the extra-vertex contraction inserting i. For lifted data use the same contraction and the finite perturbation formula: successive corrections either raise Čech degree or acquire a factor from the square-zero ideal. Hence the geometric series for the corrected contraction terminates. It identifies the reconstructed complex locally with the lifted finite-free $E_{i}$. It is therefore perfect, and the maps and isomorphisms reconstructed by the same totalization are the actual morphisms and isomorphisms. Conversely, a perfect deformation, after adding split contractible pairs, is locally a lift of these graded free modules; its local identifications and their homotopies give precisely such twisting data.
