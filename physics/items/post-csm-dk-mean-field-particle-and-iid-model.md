## Postulate

Adopt [[def-csm-dk-periodic-mean-field-model]] as the selected finite-particle model: for each $N\ge1$, the phase points $(q_i,p_i)$ evolve by its displayed Hamilton equations with smooth even periodic potential $W$ and interaction coefficient $1/N$. This is a model assumption with $q_i$ in metres, $p_i$ in kilogram-metres per second, $m$ in kilograms, $W$ in joules, and force $K=-\nabla W$ in newtons.

For the propagation-of-chaos preparation branch, give $Z=\mathbb T^d\times\mathbb R^d$ its product topology and the Borel sigma algebra generated by its open sets. Choose a Borel probability $\mu_0$ and a compact set $K\subset Z$ with $\mu_0(K)=1$. Put $\Omega=Z^{\mathbb N}$ with product sigma algebra $\mathcal B(Z)^{\otimes\mathbb N}$. Under the full Axiom of Choice, [[thm-ionescu-tulcea-construction-of-a-markov-chain]] applied to the constant kernels $K_n(z_0,\ldots,z_n,A)=\mu_0(A)$ supplies a probability $P$ on $\Omega$. Let $Z_i(0):\Omega\to Z$ be the coordinate projections, and use the first $N$ coordinates for the finite-$N$ preparation. For every finite list of indices $i_1<\cdots<i_r$ and Borel sets $A_1,\ldots,A_r\subseteq Z$, this construction gives

$$P\bigl(Z_{i_1}(0)\in A_1,\ldots,Z_{i_r}(0)\in A_r\bigr)=\prod_{j=1}^r\mu_0(A_j).$$

Thus each coordinate has law $\mu_0$ and the coordinates are mutually independent. Here “iid” means exactly this common-marginal and finite-rectangle factorization property. The cited construction uses Choice to choose a compatible infinite coordinate sequence and again to choose successive coordinates in its continuity-at-the-empty-set argument; this postulate does not weaken that assumption.

These assumptions define the selected physical model and preparation. They do not assert that a real gas, hard-sphere system, or experiment realizes them, and they do not deduce the assumptions from mechanics or observation.
