## Statement

Let $n\ge1$ and let $S$ be a generating function of the first kind or of the second kind, with data
$O_q,O_Q$ resp. $O_q,O_P$ and the local map $C$ it generates
([[def-cm-generating-function-of-the-first-and-second-kind]]). Then $C$ is a $C^2$ local
diffeomorphism and it is canonical: on its domain,

$$C^*\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^n dQ^i\wedge dP_i\Bigr)=\sum_{i=1}^n dq^i\wedge dp_i .$$

Moreover $|\det DC|=1$ wherever $C$ is defined
([[prop-cm-canonical-map-tests-and-hamiltonian-transport]]).

The words canonical and the finite-regularity form operations here use [[def-cm-finite-regularity-canonical-calculus]], with the displayed $C^2$ maps; smoothness is not inferred.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The first- or second-kind generating data $S$, the maps $\Psi$, $\Theta$ and $C$ introduced in [[def-cm-generating-function-of-the-first-and-second-kind]], and a point $(q_*,Q_*)$ resp. $(q_*,P_*)$ as in that definition.

[F1] Local diffeomorphism data ([[def-cm-generating-function-of-the-first-and-second-kind]], [[thm-euclidean-inverse-function-theorem]]): $\Psi(q,Q)=(q,S_q(q,Q))$ has invertible derivative because $\det S_{qQ}\ne0$ and, in the second-kind case, $(q,P)\mapsto(q,S_q(q,P))$ has invertible derivative because $\det S_{qP}\ne0$; the generated map $C$ is obtained by composing the $C^2$ local inverse of that map with the $C^2$ map $(q,Q)\mapsto(Q,-S_Q(q,Q))$ resp. $(q,P)\mapsto(S_P(q,P),P)$, so $C$ is a $C^2$ map. Its derivative is shown invertible in the proof before the canonical-diffeomorphism test is applied.

[F2] The $\Psi$-$\Theta$ conjugation: in the first-kind case, with $W$ a suitable neighbourhood of $(q_*,Q_*)$ and $\Psi(q,Q)=(q,p)$, the map $\Theta(q,Q):=(Q,-S_Q(q,Q))$ satisfies $C\circ\Psi=\Theta$ on $W$, because the inverse of $\Psi$ maps $(q,p)$ back to $(q,Q)$ and $C(q,p)=(Q,-S_Q(q,Q))$; in particular $C^*\tau=\Psi^{-\star}\Theta^*\tau$ for every two-form $\tau$ on the target. In the second-kind case the same holds with $\Psi(q,P)=(q,p)$ and $\Theta(q,P)=(S_P(q,P),P)$.

[F3] Derivative computation ([[def-directional-and-partial-derivatives]], [[def-jacobian-matrix-and-gradient]], [[cor-hessian-is-symmetric]]): in the first-kind case, $\Psi^*(dq\wedge dp)=\sum_{i,j}\partial^2S/\partial q^i\partial Q^j\ (q,Q)\,dq^i\wedge dQ^j$ because $dp=S_{qq}dq+S_{qQ}dQ$ on $W$; and $\Theta^*(dQ\wedge dP)=\sum_{i,j}\partial^2S/\partial q^i\partial Q^j\,dq^i\wedge dQ^j$ because $P=-S_Q$ gives $dP=-S_{QQ}dQ-S_{Qq}dq$ on $W$ and the mixed partials $\partial^2S/\partial Q^j\partial q^i=\partial^2S/\partial q^i\partial Q^j$ agree. In the second-kind case, $dp=S_{qq}dq+S_{qP}dP$ gives $\Psi^*(dq\wedge dp)=\sum_{i,j}\partial^2S/\partial q^i\partial P^j\,dq^i\wedge dP^j$, and $dQ=d(S_P)=S_{Pq}dq+S_{PP}dP$ gives $\Theta^*(dQ\wedge dP)=\sum_{i,j}\partial^2S/\partial P^j\partial q^i\,dq^i\wedge dP^j$, the same formula.

[F4] Finite-regularity coordinate calculus ([[def-cm-finite-regularity-canonical-calculus]], [[cor-hessian-is-symmetric]]): for the $C^3$ function $S$, $d(dS)=0$ because the coefficients of this two-form are differences of equal mixed second partials. Pointwise pullbacks commute with sums and wedge products by their alternating multilinear definition, and with composition by the chain rule.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; conjugate the generated map to the coordinate map $\Theta$ along the local diffeomorphism $\Psi$, and compare the pulled-back two-forms using $d^2S=0$ and the symmetry of mixed partials.

1.1 First-kind canonicity: by the recorded identity $p\,dq-P\,dQ=dS$ of [[def-cm-generating-function-of-the-first-and-second-kind]] and [F4], applying $d$ gives $dp\wedge dq-dP\wedge dQ=0$ on the $(q,Q)$-space $W$, i.e. $\Psi^*(dq\wedge dp)=\Theta^*(dQ\wedge dP)$; by [F2] and [F4] this is $\Psi^*(dq\wedge dp)=\Psi^*(C^*(dQ\wedge dP))$. Since $D\Psi$ is invertible at every point of $W$ by [F1] and hence $\Psi^*$ is injective on two-forms, $C^*(dQ\wedge dP)=dq\wedge dp$ near $(q_*,p_*)$. [F1, F2, F4, algebra]

1.2 Second-kind canonicity: the recorded identity $p\,dq+Q\,dP=dS$ gives, after applying $d$, $dp\wedge dq+dQ\wedge dP=0$ on the $(q,P)$-space, i.e. $\Psi^*(dq\wedge dp)=\Theta^*(dQ\wedge dP)$, and the same injectivity argument yields $C^*(dQ\wedge dP)=dq\wedge dp$. [F1, F2, F4, algebra]

2.1 Explicit form of the identities: [F3] exhibits both sides of the two equalities of steps 1.1 and 1.2 as the same coordinate combination $\sum_{i,j}S_{q^i\alpha^j}dq^i\wedge d\alpha^j$ (with $\alpha=Q$ resp. $P$), which confirms that $dS$ has the displayed derivative and that the two pullbacks agree. [F3, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 By steps 1.1, 1.2 and 2.1 the generated local map satisfies $C^*(dQ\wedge dP)=dq\wedge dp$, hence $DC^{\mathsf T}JDC=J$ by the pointwise matrix test. If $DCv=0$, then for every $w$ one has $v^{\mathsf T}Jw=(DCv)^{\mathsf T}JDCw=0$; nondegeneracy of $J$ gives $v=0$. Thus $DC$ is invertible at each point. By the inverse function theorem shrink about the reference point so that $C$ is a $C^2$ diffeomorphism onto its open image; by [[prop-cm-canonical-map-tests-and-hamiltonian-transport]] clause 2 it is a local canonical transformation with $|\det DC|=1$, and by [F1] it is a $C^2$ local diffeomorphism. [F1, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1] ∎

## Remarks

- The first-kind relations put the old and new positions in the generating function; the second-kind relations put the new momenta there. Both reduce the canonicity question to $d^2S=0$, and the inverse function theorem supplies the local diffeomorphism; no global statement about $S$ or about $C$ is made.
