## Definition

Keep the ambient ring $R'=\mathbb Q[x_1,\dots,x_m]$, the reduced ring
$R\subset R'$ generated by the differences $y_j=x_j-x_{j+1}$, the invariant
rings $R^{s_i}$, the invariant coordinates $t_i=x_i+x_{i+1}$ and the reduced
bimodules $B_i=R\otimes_{R^{s_i}}R$ of
[[def-reduced-type-a-polynomial-ring-for-hhh]], with the internal shift
convention $(M\{r\})_d=M_{d-r}$ of
[[def-graded-ring-module-bimodule-and-internal-shift]].

**The unreduced simple-reflection bimodule.** For a simple reflection $s_i$ put
$$R'_i:=\mathbb Q[x_1,\dots,x_{i-1},\,x_i+x_{i+1},\,x_ix_{i+1},\,x_{i+2},\dots,x_m]=(R')^{s_i},$$
the $s_i$-invariant subring of $R'$ (symmetric polynomials in $x_i,x_{i+1}$),
and
$$B'_i:=R'\otimes_{R'_i}R'$$
with **no internal shift**. Since $R'=R[t_i]$ and
$(R')^{s_i}=R^{s_i}[t_i]$ by
[[def-reduced-type-a-polynomial-ring-for-hhh]], and
$R'=R^{s_i}[t_i]\oplus y_iR^{s_i}[t_i]$, the bimodule $B'_i$ is free of rank
two as a left and as a right $R'$-module, with left basis
$1\otimes1,1\otimes y_i$ and right basis $1\otimes1,y_i\otimes1$,
of degrees $0,2$ on either side.

**The trivial polynomial factor.** Write $t=t_i$ and let $R_t\subseteq R'$ denote
the polynomial ring $\mathbb Q[t]$ regarded as a subring of $R'$; the coordinate
$t$ is $s_i$-invariant, hence central. The assignment on $R'_i$-balanced tensors
$$\Phi\colon B'_i\longrightarrow B_i\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb Q[t],\qquad \Phi\bigl(r\,t^{a}\otimes r'\,t^{b}\bigr)=(r\otimes r')\otimes t^{a+b} \qquad(r,r'\in R,\ a,b\ge0)$$
is an isomorphism of graded $(R',R')$-bimodules, where $R'=R[t]$ acts on the
target by $R$ on the $B_i$-factor and by multiplication on $\mathbb Q[t]$. It is
well defined: if $h=\sum_ah_at^a\in R^{s_i}[t]=R'_i$ with $h_a\in R^{s_i}$, then
$\Phi(ht^c\otimes1)=\sum_a(h_a\otimes1)\otimes t^{a+c}$ and
$\Phi(1\otimes ht^c)=\sum_a(1\otimes h_a)\otimes t^{a+c}$ agree because
$h_a\otimes1=1\otimes h_a$ in $B_i$. It is bijective because both sides are free
of rank two over $R'$ on the respective generators $1\otimes1,1\otimes y_i$ and
$(1\otimes1)\otimes1,(1\otimes y_i)\otimes1$, which $\Phi$ matches. The factor
$\mathbb Q[t]$ is the **trivial polynomial factor**: the coordinate $t$ acts by
the same multiplication on both sides of the target, which is exactly the
statement that it lies in the invariant ring $(R')^{s_i}$ and hence balances
in $B'_i$.

**The reduced maps and their trivial extensions.** Define degree-zero maps of
graded $(R,R)$-bimodules
$$br_i\colon B_i\to R,\qquad br_i(a\otimes b)=ab,$$
$$rb_i\colon R\{2\}\to B_i,\qquad rb_i(1)=y_i\otimes1+1\otimes y_i .$$
Both are well defined: $br_i$ kills the balancing relation
$h\otimes1-1\otimes h$ for $h\in R^{s_i}$, and $rb_i$ is left $R$-linear by
construction while the identity $r\bigl(y_i\otimes1+1\otimes y_i\bigr)
=\bigl(y_i\otimes1+1\otimes y_i\bigr)r$ holds for every invariant generator $z_j$ because it balances, and for
$r=y_i$ because $y_i^2\in R^{s_i}$. These elements generate $R$, as proved in
[[def-reduced-type-a-polynomial-ring-for-hhh]]. Both maps have degree
zero in the internal grading, since $\deg y_i=2$ and $\deg 1\in R\{2\}=2$.
Likewise
$$br'_i\colon B'_i\to R',\qquad br'_i(a\otimes b)=ab,$$
$$rb'_i\colon R'\{2\}\to B'_i,\qquad rb'_i(1)=(x_i-x_{i+1})\otimes1+1\otimes(x_i-x_{i+1}),$$
are well-defined degree-zero $(R',R')$-bimodule maps by the same argument with
$R'$ in place of $R$ and $(R')^{s_i}$ in place of $R^{s_i}$. Under $\Phi$ the
elements $1\otimes1\mapsto(1\otimes1)\otimes1$ and
$y_i\otimes1+1\otimes y_i\mapsto rb_i(1)\otimes1$ correspond, so $br'_i$ and
$rb'_i$ restrict to the trivial-factor extensions of $br_i$ and $rb_i$.

**Products over the layers.** Let $D$ be a marked MOY resolution with $r$ wide
edges, at positions $s_1,\dots,s_r$ in the layer order, and put
$$B'(D):=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{r}B'_{s_j},\qquad B(D):=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{r}B_{s_j},$$
the balanced tensor products over $R'$ respectively over $R$ in the layer
order. Choose the common invariant coordinate
$t=(x_1+\cdots+x_m)/m$; then $R'=R[t]$ and $t$ is fixed by every simple
reflection. Applying the preceding single-factor construction with this $t$
gives an isomorphism of graded $(R',R')$-bimodules
$$B'(D)\longrightarrow B(D)\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb Q[t].$$
Indeed, each factor is $B_{s_j}\otimes\mathbb Q[t]$, and balancing over
$R[t]$ combines the polynomial factors by multiplication. The inverse sends
$(b_1\otimes_R\cdots\otimes_Rb_r)\otimes t^a$ to the tensor with $t^a$
in its first factor. For $r=0$ this is the ring identity $R'=R[t]$. The two
maps preserve the left and right actions and grading. An arbitrary coordinate
such as $x_1$ need not act equally on both sides; its left and right actions
are recovered from $x_1=t+(x_1-t)$, with $x_1-t\in R$.

**Normalization dictionary with the published bimodule.** The published
[[def-type-a-soergel-bimodule-for-a-simple-reflection]] has
$$B_i^{\mathrm{lib}}=R'\otimes_{(R')^{s_i}}R'(1)=B'_i\{-1\}$$
because the Elias-Williamson shift $(1)$ is the internal shift $\{-1\}$ in the
library convention, that is $M(r)=M\{-r\}$; equivalently
$$B'_i=B_i^{\mathrm{lib}}(-1)=B_i^{\mathrm{lib}}\{1\}.$$
The dictionary $M(r)=M\{-r\}$ is used throughout this page, and the generator
$1\otimes1$ of $B^{\mathrm{lib}}_i$ has degree $-1$ while the generator
$1\otimes1$ of $B'_i$ has degree $0$.

**Source convention.** Khovanov writes the polynomial factor as
$\mathbb Q[x_1]$. The ring splitting $R'=R[x_1]$ is valid, but a literal
trivial factor for the bimodule actions uses a common invariant coordinate
$t$ as above. The change of coordinates $x_1=t+(x_1-t)$ translates the source's
ring notation into this bimodule description.

No choice principle is used in this definition.
