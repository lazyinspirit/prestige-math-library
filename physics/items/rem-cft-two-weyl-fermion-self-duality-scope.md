## Remark

Arias-Tamargo et al. study the theory $\mathcal T$ of **two free right-moving complex Weyl fermions** in two dimensions. In their convention the single-component quantum fields $\psi_1,\psi_2$ on $(t,x)\in\mathbb R^{1,1}$ have Lagrangian density $\mathcal L=i\sum_{j=1}^2\psi_j^\dagger(\partial_t+\partial_x)\psi_j$. Right-moving means $(\partial_t+\partial_x)\psi_j=0$ as an operator-valued distribution; the fields are not ordinary pointwise functions. Use natural units $\hbar=c=1$, so each field has length dimension $-1/2$. Their coprime pair $(p,q)=(1,2)$ gives the cyclic group $G=G(1,2)\cong\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z$, generated by
$$\psi_1\mapsto e^{4\pi i/5}\psi_1,\qquad \psi_2\mapsto e^{-2\pi i/5}\psi_2.$$
The source reports that this action is anomaly-free, meaning that it admits the consistent finite gauging $\mathcal T/G$: sum over flat $G$ gauge backgrounds and project onto invariant states. Its twisted sectors on a spatial circle with dimensionless coordinate $\sigma\bmod 2\pi$ have boundary conditions $\psi_1(\sigma+2\pi)=(-1)^{1-u}e^{-4\pi i\alpha/5}\psi_1(\sigma)$ and $\psi_2(\sigma+2\pi)=(-1)^{1-u}e^{2\pi i\alpha/5}\psi_2(\sigma)$, with $\alpha\in\{0,\ldots,4\}$ and $u=0$ (Neveu–Schwarz) or $u=1$ (Ramond). On their charge eigenspaces labelled by $n_1,n_2\in\mathbb Z-u/2$, the commuting charges $Q_1,Q_2$ have eigenvalues $n_1+2n_2$ and $2n_1-n_2+\alpha$. The possible extensions of the generator to these sectors are $\exp(2\pi i[Q_2+t(2Q_1-Q_2)]/5)$, with $t\in\{1,3,5,7,9\}$. The source's modular-consistency condition (2.50) is $2pt\equiv1\pmod5$, hence selects $t_*=3$. It requires the torus amplitudes, including spin structures and gauge holonomies, to transform with the same phases as those of $\mathcal T$, not bosonic modular invariance.

A self-duality isomorphism here is a bijection between the local and extended operators of $\mathcal T/G$ and $\mathcal T$ preserving conformal weights and operator-product coefficients. Choose the source's reference $D$ in (2.67), with the phases fixed in (3.15), which exchanges the two $G$-neutral charge generators without extra phases. Gauging only on one side of a line, with gauge trivialization fixed on the interface (the source's Dirichlet boundary condition), and then applying $D$ gives the defect $N$. Write $L_g$ for the symmetry line implementing $g\in G$, $\mathbf1=L_0$, $\bar N$ for orientation reversal, and $\otimes$ for spatially ordered fusion; $\cong$ means an invertible junction identification and $\oplus$ a direct sum of line sectors. The reported fusion rules are
$$L_g\otimes L_h\cong L_{g+h},\qquad L_g\otimes N\cong N\cong N\otimes L_g,\qquad N\otimes\bar N\cong\bigoplus_{g\in G}L_g.$$
For this phase-fixed reference choice the source also reports $N\cong\bar N$, hence $N\otimes N\cong\bigoplus_{g\in G}L_g$. These are the Tambara–Yamagami fusion rules for $G$; specifying a category additionally requires coherent associativity data, which these equations alone do not determine.

Changing $D$ by composition with a symmetry of $\mathcal T$ changes $N$ by fusion with its invertible symmetry line. Define $\widehat G=\operatorname{Hom}(G,U(1))$, the character group whose Wilson lines in the gauged theory evaluate a character on gauge holonomy, and $G'=D(\widehat G)$, its image among symmetry lines of $\mathcal T$. The reference choice gives $G'=G$; other choices in the source give $G'\ne G$ and fail the same Tambara–Yamagami rules. These are source-reported results for this chiral fermion theory and these choices, not locally proved fusion rules or empirical observations, and imply no universal rule for compact-boson defects or arbitrary noninvertible lines.
