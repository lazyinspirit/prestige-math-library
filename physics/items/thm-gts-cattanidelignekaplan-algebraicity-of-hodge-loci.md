## Statement

Let $\pi:\mathcal X\to S$ be a smooth projective morphism of smooth quasi-projective complex varieties. For a fixed even weight $2p$ and an integral class $h\in H^{2p}(X_{s_0};\mathbb Z)$ of type $(p,p)$, transport $h$ in a local trivialization of the integral local system near $s_0$. The germ at $s_0$ of the locus where this flat translate remains of type $(p,p)$ is algebraic. Globally, the Hodge locus of integral classes is a countable union of closed algebraic subvarieties. This result concerns algebraicity of loci in a family and does not assert that every Hodge class on an individual fibre is algebraic.

2.1 Near a chosen parameter b₀, take homogeneous linear forms $l₀,…,l_M$ and a small circle |t|=ρ so that l₀ is nonzero on the circle image at b₀. 3.1 The loci where all (d+1)-row Hankel minors vanish are closed, and their countable union covers B. Baire gives one such locus with nonempty interior. 96.1 (Projective analytic sets are algebraic: the finite-projection proof used here.)
