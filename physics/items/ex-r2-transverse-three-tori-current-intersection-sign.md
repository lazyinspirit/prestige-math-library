## Example

Let $S^1=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2:x^2+y^2=1\}$ with the smooth structure induced by its embedding in $\mathbb R^2$, oriented so that $V(x,y)=(-y,x)$ is positive. Give $X=(S^1)^6$ the product smooth structure and write $V_i$ for this tangent field in its $i$th factor. Give every fixed singleton factor its positive zero-dimensional orientation. Orient each displayed product by the determinant-line ray determined by the ordered product of its factor orientations: on $X$ the ray is generated by $V_1\wedge\cdots\wedge V_6$, on $C=S^1\times S^1\times S^1\times\{y_4\}\times\{y_5\}\times\{y_6\}$ by $V_1\wedge V_2\wedge V_3$, and on $D=\{y_1\}\times\{y_2\}\times\{y_3\}\times S^1\times S^1\times S^1$ by $V_4\wedge V_5\wedge V_6$. For fixed $y_i\in S^1$, the resulting compact embedded three-manifolds $C,D$ have no boundary, meet transversely at one point, and the C-first geometric sign is $+1$. The regularized current pairing of $\delta_C$ with $D$ is $-1$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The current-compatible intersection convention and the transverse-cycle pairing proved on the A page.

[F2] The choice-free local calculation in Proof 1.2–3.1 of [[lem-r2-source-current-pairs-with-transverse-dual-cycle]] proves that the regularized pairing $\langle\delta_C,D\rangle_{\mathrm{reg}}$ equals $C\cdot_{\mathrm{cur}}D$. Its separate AC-qualified construction of a topological dual class is not used here.

[F3] [[prop-products-of-smooth-manifolds-have-a-canonical-product-smooth-structure]] states that the product charts of smooth manifolds form the canonical product smooth structure.

[F4] [[def-smooth-manifold]] defines a smooth manifold by a maximal smooth atlas.

[F5] [[def-embedded-smooth-submanifold-with-boundary]] defines an embedded smooth submanifold by a manifold-with-boundary structure for which its inclusion is a smooth embedding.

[F6] [[def-oriented-smooth-manifold-and-oriented-chart]] defines an orientation by a smooth choice of tangent-orientation ray and a positive chart frame by membership in that ray.

[F7] [[thm-heine-borel-rn]] states that a subset of Euclidean $\mathbb R^n$ is compact exactly when it is closed and bounded.

[F8] [[thm-finite-products-of-compact-spaces]] states that every finite product of compact spaces with the product topology is compact.

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** $n=6$, $p=3$, and the coordinate orientations in the Example.

1.1 Put $N=(0,1)$, $S=(0,-1)$, $U_N=S^1\setminus\{N\}$ and $U_S=S^1\setminus\{S\}$. The maps $\sigma_N(x,y)=x/(1-y)$ and $\sigma_S(x,y)=x/(1+y)$ have inverses $$\sigma_N^{-1}(u)=\left(\frac{2u}{1+u^2},\frac{u^2-1}{1+u^2}\right),\qquad \sigma_S^{-1}(u)=\left(\frac{2u}{1+u^2},\frac{1-u^2}{1+u^2}\right).$$ Substitution verifies both inverse identities; their rational components are smooth. The subspace $S^1\subset\mathbb R^2$ is Hausdorff, and intersections with Euclidean balls having rational centers and positive rational radii give a countable base. The sets $U_N,U_S$ are open and cover $S^1$; the displayed maps and inverses are continuous, so they are homeomorphisms onto $\mathbb R$ and exhibit a topological $1$-manifold. On their overlap both transition maps are $u\mapsto1/u$, smooth on $\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}$. Thus they give a smooth atlas, whose maximal smooth structure makes $S^1$ a smooth manifold by [F4]. In each chart the inverse parametrization has derivative norm $2/(1+u^2)>0$, so it is an immersion; as the chart inverse onto the corresponding open arc it is also a topological embedding. Hence the inclusion $S^1\hookrightarrow\mathbb R^2$ is a smooth embedding under [F5]. The vector field $(-y,x)$ is tangent because its dot product with $(x,y)$ is zero, and nonzero because $x^2+y^2=1$; its smooth components define the stated orientation under [F6]. [F4, F5, F6, given, construct]

2.1 The unit circle is closed and bounded in $\mathbb R^2$, so it is compact by [F7]. A singleton is compact, and [F8] makes $X,C,D$ compact. Product charts from [F3] give $X$ its product smooth structure and make $C,D$ local coordinate slices; their inclusions are smooth embeddings by [F5]. By the explicit factor-order orientation in the Example, the first three positive factor tangents orient $C$, the last three orient $D$, and their concatenation is positive for $X$. The defining coordinate conditions show $C\cap D=\{(y_1,\ldots,y_6)\}$. Their tangent spaces are the spans of the first and last three factor tangents, so they sum directly to $T_xX$ and meet transversely. The C-first local intersection sign is therefore $+1$. [F3, F5, F6, F7, F8, given, step 1.1]

3.1 Here $p(n-p)=3\cdot3=9$, so [F1] gives $C\cdot_{\mathrm{cur}}D=(-1)^9(+1)=-1$. [F2] identifies that number with $\langle\delta_C,D\rangle_{\mathrm{reg}}$. [F1, F2, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
