## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $F$ be a $C^1$
transversely oriented codimension-one foliation of a smooth manifold $M$, and
let $L$ be a compact leaf with $H^1(L;\mathbb R)=0$
([[def-singular-cohomology-with-coefficients]]). Then $L$ has a saturated open
neighbourhood whose leaves are all $C^1$-diffeomorphic to $L$. In fact the holonomy
of $L$ is trivial and the neighbourhood can be chosen to be a product foliated
neighbourhood $L\times D$ for an open interval $D$. This is the local
cohomological refinement; no global fibration conclusion is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A $C^1$ transversely oriented codimension-one foliation $F$ of a smooth manifold $M$, a compact leaf $L$ with $H^1(L;\mathbb R)=0$, and a base point $x\in L$.

[F1] A compact leaf of a $C^1$ codimension-one foliation is an embedded hypersurface, and plaque transport along leafwise loops defines the holonomy homomorphism $\rho_x:\pi_1(L,x)\to\operatorname{Diff}^{1,+}_x(T)$ ([[lem-c1-holonomy-is-a-well-defined-representation-into-transverse-germs]], [[def-c1-regular-codimension-one-foliation-and-transverse-orientation]]).

[F2] Under the Axiom of Choice, $\pi_1(L,x)$ is finitely generated for a compact leaf $L$ ([[lem-compact-c1-leaf-has-finitely-generated-fundamental-group]]).

[F3] The image of a finitely generated group under a homomorphism is finitely generated ([[lem-images-of-finitely-generated-and-finite-groups-are-finitely-generated-and-finite]]).

[F4] $\operatorname{Diff}^{1,+}_0(\mathbb R)$ is locally indicable: every nontrivial finitely generated subgroup admits a surjection onto $\mathbb Z$ ([[thm-thurston-stability-for-c1-interval-germ-groups-are-locally-indicable]]).

[F5] For a path-connected space $L$, $H_0(L;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$ is free, hence projective, and the universal coefficient theorem in degree one gives $H^1(L;\mathbb R)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(H_1(L;\mathbb Z),\mathbb R)$; the degree-one Hurewicz map identifies $H_1(L;\mathbb Z)$ with the abelianization of $\pi_1(L,x)$, so $H^1(L;\mathbb R)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_1(L,x),\mathbb R)$ ([[prop-zero-th-singular-homology-is-free-on-path-components]], [[thm-universal-coefficient-theorem-for-cohomology-over-a-pid]], [[prop-the-first-hurewicz-map-in-degree-one-is-abelianization]], [[thm-free-modules-are-projective-with-choice-boundary]], [[def-singular-chain-complex-and-singular-homology]], [[def-singular-cochain-complex-with-coefficients]]).

[F6] A compact leaf with trivial $C^1$ holonomy has a saturated product neighbourhood $(U,F|_U)\cong(L\times D,\{L\times\{t\}\})$, and $U$ is a union of leaves ([[lem-trivial-c1-holonomy-gives-a-saturated-product-neighbourhood]], [[def-saturated-neighbourhood-of-a-leaf]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 ($\pi_1$ and the real cohomology of $L$.) The leaf $L$ is compact and connected, so $\pi_1(L,x)$ is finitely generated by [F2], and $L$ is path-connected; [F5] gives $H^1(L;\mathbb R)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_1(L,x),\mathbb R)$. Thus the hypothesis $H^1(L;\mathbb R)=0$ says exactly that every homomorphism $\pi_1(L,x)\to\mathbb R$ is zero. [F2, F5]

2.1 (Holonomy is trivial.) The holonomy of $L$ is the homomorphism $\rho_x:\pi_1(L,x)\to\operatorname{Diff}^{1,+}_x(T)$ of [F1]. Suppose its image $H$ were nontrivial. Then $H$ is a finitely generated subgroup of the group of $C^1$ germs, by [F3] applied to $\rho_x$ and [F2]; by local indicability [F4] there is a surjective homomorphism $H\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb Z$. Composing with the inclusion $\mathbb Z\hookrightarrow\mathbb R$ and with $\rho_x$ yields a nonzero homomorphism $\pi_1(L,x)\to\mathbb R$, that is, by step 1.1 a nonzero element of $H^1(L;\mathbb R)$, contradicting the hypothesis. Hence $H$ is trivial and the holonomy of $L$ vanishes. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 (Product neighbourhood and diffeomorphic leaves.) Since $L$ is compact and has trivial $C^1$ holonomy, [F6] provides a saturated open neighbourhood $U$ of $L$ and a $C^1$ foliated diffeomorphism $U\cong L\times D$ carrying $F|_U$ to the product foliation by the slices; in particular every leaf of $F$ meeting $U$ is $C^1$-diffeomorphic to $L$, and $U$ is a union of leaves. This proves the local cohomological stability statement. [F6, step 2.1] ∎
