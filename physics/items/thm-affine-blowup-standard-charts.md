## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice as inherited from the Proj construction. Let $A$ be
a ring, $I=(f_0,\dots,f_r)\subseteq A$, $S=R(I)=\bigoplus I^nt^n$ and
$B_i=A[I/f_i]=\bigl(S[(f_it)^{-1}]\bigr)_0$. The standard opens
$U_i=D_+(f_it)=\operatorname{Spec}B_i$ cover
$\operatorname{Bl}_I\operatorname{Spec}A$. Put
$u_{ij}=(f_jt)/(f_it)$ in $B_i$. Then
$U_i\cap U_j=D(u_{ij})$ in $U_i$, with canonical $A$-algebra identifications
$(B_i)_{u_{ij}}=S[(f_it)^{-1},(f_jt)^{-1}]_0=(B_j)_{u_{ji}}$. They send
$f_l/f_i$ to $(f_l/f_j)/(f_i/f_j)$, and $u_{ij}$ to $u_{ji}^{-1}$. These
identifications satisfy the identity and cocycle conditions and preserve the
structural maps to $\operatorname{Spec}A$. Different finite generating
families give compatible chart covers of the same canonical blowup; no
bijection between the chart families is asserted. The formulas include zero
divisors and empty charts; nilpotent $f_i$ gives $B_i=0$. Localization at the
base element $f_j$ is generally smaller than this overlap and is not its
formula.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A ring $A$, an ideal $I=(f_0,\dots,f_r)\subseteq A$, the Rees algebra $S=R(I)=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}I^nt^n$ ([[def-rees-algebra-ideal-sheaf]]), the blowup $\operatorname{Bl}_I\operatorname{Spec}A$ ([[def-blowup-scheme-along-ideal]]), and the Axiom of Choice as inherited from the Proj construction ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F1] [[def-blowup-scheme-along-ideal]]: For $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$ and $\mathcal I=\widetilde I$, the blowup is the absolute Proj of the Rees algebra $R(I)=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}I^nt^n$, with structural morphism to $\operatorname{Spec}A$.

[F2] [[lem-affine-blowup-algebra-properties]]: For a ring $A$, an ideal $I\subseteq A$ and $a\in I$, the affine blowup algebra is $A[I/a]=(R(I))_{(a)}$, the degree-zero part of the localisation of $R(I)$ at the multiplicative set generated by $at$.

[F3] [[thm-proj-structure-sheaf-scheme]]: For a commutative nonnegatively graded ring $S$, $\operatorname{Proj}S$ carries open subscheme identifications $\varphi_f\colon D_+(f)\to\operatorname{Spec}S_{(f)}$ for homogeneous $f\in S_+$, the $D_+(f)$ form an affine open cover, and for homogeneous $f,g$ of degrees $d,e$ the set $D_+(f)\cap D_+(g)=D_+(fg)$ is carried by $\varphi_f$ onto $D(g^d/f^e)\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}S_{(f)}$, with transition induced by $S_{(f)}[(g^d/f^e)^{-1}]\cong S_{(fg)}\cong S_{(g)}[(f^e/g^d)^{-1}]$; the underlying space is $\operatorname{Proj}S$ with the standard-open basis, the scheme is unique for these identifications, and if $f$ is nilpotent then $D_+(f)=\varnothing$ and $S_{(f)}=0$.

[F4] [[def-standard-open-proj]]: For homogeneous $f\in S_+$, $D_+(f)=\{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Proj}S:f\notin\mathfrak p\}$ is a standard open.

[F5] [[lem-standard-opens-proj-affine]]: The canonical chart map $\varphi_f\colon D_+(f)\to\operatorname{Spec}S_{(f)}$ is an isomorphism, including the empty case: nilpotent $f$ gives $D_+(f)=\varnothing$ and $S_{(f)}=0$.

## Proof

1.1 Put $g_i=f_it\in S$, a homogeneous element of degree one, so that $S=R(I)=A[It]$ is generated as an $A$-algebra by $I$, and $S_+$ is generated as an ideal by $g_0,\dots,g_r$; hence no homogeneous prime $\mathfrak p$ of $S$ contains all $g_i$ without containing $S_+$, and the standard opens $D_+(g_i)$ cover $\operatorname{Proj}S=\operatorname{Bl}_I\operatorname{Spec}A$ by [F1], [F3], [F4]. Moreover $B_i=A[I/f_i]=S_{(g_i)}$ by [F2], so $U_i=D_+(g_i)=\operatorname{Spec}B_i$ by [F5]. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5]

2.1 For each pair $i,j$, [F3] applied to the degree-one elements $g_i,g_j$ identifies $D_+(g_i)\cap D_+(g_j)=D_+(g_ig_j)$ with $D(u_{ij})\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}B_i$, where $u_{ij}=g_j/g_i$, and identifies the two charts through the canonical isomorphisms $B_i[u_{ij}^{-1}]=S_{(g_i)}[(g_j/g_i)^{-1}]\cong S_{(g_ig_j)}\cong S_{(g_j)}[(g_i/g_j)^{-1}]=(B_j)_{u_{ji}}$. [F3, F5, step 1.1]

3.1 The identification of step 2.1 can be checked directly and torsion-safely: the canonical map $B_i[u_{ij}^{-1}]\to\bigl(S[g_i^{-1},g_j^{-1}]\bigr)_0$ is surjective, because a degree-zero fraction $c/(g_i^mg_j^n)$ with $c$ homogeneous of degree $m+n$ equals $\bigl(c/g_i^{m+n}\bigr)u_{ij}^{-n}$ with $c/g_i^{m+n}\in B_i$; and it is injective, because vanishing of the image means $g_i^pg_j^qc=0$ in $S$ for some $p,q\ge0$, whence $u_{ij}^q\bigl(c/g_i^m\bigr)=g_i^pcg_j^q/g_i^{m+p+q}=0$ in $B_i[u_{ij}^{-1}]$. No cancellation of $g_i$ in $S$ is used. The same identification sends $f_l/f_i=(f_lt)/(f_it)$ to $(f_lt)/(f_it)$ computed in $S_{(g_ig_j)}$, which equals $(f_l/f_j)/(f_i/f_j)$, and sends $u_{ij}=g_j/g_i$ to $u_{ji}^{-1}$. [F3, step 2.1]

4.1 The identifications satisfy the identity condition (for $i=j$, $u_{ii}=1$ and the transition is the identity) and the cocycle condition: on a triple overlap every transition is induced by the localisation map $S\to S[g_i^{-1},g_j^{-1},g_k^{-1}]$ and taking degree zero, so the three compositions around the cycle coincide with the identity on $S_{(g_ig_jg_k)}$. They preserve the structural maps to $\operatorname{Spec}A$, because they are isomorphisms of $A$-algebras for the structure maps $A=S_0\to S_{(g)}$ of the charts. [F3, step 2.1, step 3.1]

4.2 For $I=(x,y)\subseteq A=k[x,y]$ with $f_0=x$, $f_1=y$, one has $B_0=A[I/x]=k[x,y/x]=k[x,s]$ with $s=y/x$ and $y=xs$, and $u_{01}=s$, so the overlap $D(u_{01})=D(s)$ in $\operatorname{Spec}B_0$ retains the points with $x=0$ and $s\ne0$. Localising instead at the base element $f_1=y$ gives $D(y)=D(xs)=D(x)\cap D(s)$, which is strictly smaller than $D(s)$ and omits those points; hence localisation at the base element is not the overlap formula. [F2, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 A second finite generating family $I=(h_0,\dots,h_{r'})$ gives the charts $D_+(h_kt)=\operatorname{Spec}A[I/h_k]$ of the same scheme $\operatorname{Proj}S$, with their own overlap identifications supplied by the same formulas of [F3] applied to the degree-one elements of $S$; on the intersection of a chart of the first family and a chart of the second, $D_+(g_i)\cap D_+(h_kt)=D_+(g_ih_kt)$, the transition is again induced by the canonical localisation of $S$, so the two cover structures are compatible. No bijection between the two chart families is asserted: the charts are indexed by different generating sets and need not correspond individually. [F3, step 4.1]

6.1 The formulas allow zero divisors and empty charts: step 3.1 never cancels $g_i$ in $S$, and if $f_i$ is nilpotent then $g_i=f_it$ is nilpotent, so $D_+(g_i)=\varnothing$ and $B_i=S_{(g_i)}=0$ by [F3] and [F5]; the same holds for the overlap formula in the degenerate cases. This completes the proof. [F3, F5, step 5.1] ∎
