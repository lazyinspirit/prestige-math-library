## Statement

For the actual rank-one square-zero lattice fields at every integer $p\ge2$, put $F=e^{-\alpha}$, $H=QF$, $E=Q^2F$ and $Q=(e^\alpha)_{(0)}$. Each of $X=F,H,E$ is primary of weight $2p-1$ and satisfies the state identity
$$\left(L(-1)^3-4pL(-2)L(-1)+2p(2p-1)L(-3)\right)X=0.$$
Consequently, on any actual lattice module $M$, for a Virasoro-invariant subspace $B\subseteq M$ and a Virasoro-module quotient map $\pi:M\to C$, the assignment $w\mapsto\pi Y(w,z)|_B$ on the Virasoro submodule of the state space generated by any one of $F,H,E$ satisfies the derivative, primary commutator, and descendant-residue identities of a formal Virasoro intertwining field. These assertions concern the actual vertex algebra and module fields, not merely the oscillator Lie algebra representation.

## Proof

1.1 Put $q=(p-1)/(2p)$. The translation formula in [A2] gives $L(-1)(f e^{t\alpha})=(\sum_{k\ge1}k x_{k+1}\partial_{x_k}f+t x_1f)e^{t\alpha}$. The conformal modes in [A3] give $L(-2)e^{t\alpha}=((t+q)x_2+x_1^2/(4p))e^{t\alpha}$, $[L(-2),x_1]=x_3$, and $L(-3)e^{t\alpha}=((t+2q)x_3+x_1x_2/(2p))e^{t\alpha}$. Starting with $F=e^{-\alpha}$ and applying these operators successively yields $$L(-1)^3F=(-2x_3+3x_1x_2-x_1^3)e^{-\alpha},$$ $$L(-2)L(-1)F=\left(-x_3+(1-q)x_1x_2-\frac{x_1^3}{4p}\right)e^{-\alpha},\qquad L(-3)F=\left(-\frac{x_3}{p}+\frac{x_1x_2}{2p}\right)e^{-\alpha}.$$ The first equality follows by applying the displayed $L(-1)$ formula three times; the second uses $L(-1)F=-x_1e^{-\alpha}$ and the commutator with $x_1$; the third substitutes $t=-1$. [given, A1, A2, A3, algebra]

2.1 In the asserted linear combination of these three states, the coefficients of $x_1^3$, $x_3$, and $x_1x_2$ are respectively $-1+1=0$, $-2+4p-2(2p-1)=0$, and $3-4p(1-q)+(2p-1)=0$ because $q=(p-1)/(2p)$. These monomials exhaust the three displayed expressions, so the null relation holds for $F$. [given, A3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The differential operator $P(L)=L(-1)^3-4pL(-2)L(-1)+2p(2p-1)L(-3)$ commutes with $Q$ by [A3]. Thus $P(L)H=QP(L)F=0$ and $P(L)E=Q^2P(L)F=0$. The same supplier gives their weight and positive-mode equations; [A4] ensures that $F,H,E$ are all nonzero, so these are primaries in its stated convention. [given, A3, A4, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Write $Y_M(w,z)$ for the actual module field and $T_M(y)=Y_M(\omega,y)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}L_M(n)y^{-n-2}$. For every state $w$, module translation gives $Y_M(L(-1)w,z)=\partial_zY_M(w,z)$. Taking first the residue in the iterate variable and then $\operatorname{Res}_y y^{n+1}$ in module Jacobi gives, for every integer $n$, $$[L_M(n),Y_M(w,z)]=\sum_{j\ge0}\binom{n+1}{j}z^{n+1-j}Y_M(L(j-1)w,z).$$ Indeed the delta substitution replaces $y^{n+1}$ by $(z+x)^{n+1}=\sum_{j\ge0}\binom{n+1}{j}z^{n+1-j}x^j$, where $x$ is the iterate variable, and $\operatorname{Res}_x x^jY(\omega,x)w=L(j-1)w$. Truncation makes the sum finite on a fixed $w$. For $w=X$, primarity from step 3.1 leaves exactly $j=0,1$, giving $[L_M(n),Y_M(X,z)]=z^n(z\partial_z+(n+1)(2p-1))Y_M(X,z)$. [given, A2, A3, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 For $r\ge2$, take $\operatorname{Res}_x x^{1-r}\operatorname{Res}_y$ of the same module Jacobi identity. Since $\omega_{(1-r)}=L(-r)$, this yields $$Y_M(L(-r)w,z)=\operatorname{Res}_y\left(\iota_{y,z}(y-z)^{1-r}T_M(y)Y_M(w,z)-\iota_{z,y}(y-z)^{1-r}Y_M(w,z)T_M(y)\right).$$ Here $\operatorname{Res}_y$ means the coefficient of $y^{-1}$, and the two prescribed expansions are $$\iota_{y,z}(y-z)^{1-r}=\sum_{j\ge0}\binom{r+j-2}{j}z^jy^{1-r-j},\qquad \iota_{z,y}(y-z)^{1-r}=(-1)^{r-1}\sum_{j\ge0}\binom{r+j-2}{j}y^jz^{1-r-j}.$$ The first residue contains $L_M(-r-j)Y_M(w,z)$ multiplied by $z^j$; each coefficient on a fixed input is a finite sum by the lower bound on powers in $Y_M(w,z)$. The second contains $Y_M(w,z)L_M(j-1)$, and truncation of $T_M(y)$ makes all sufficiently large $j$ vanish on that input. Thus both residues are well-defined coefficientwise, with no analytic convergence assumption. [given, A2, A3, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Let $A_X$ be the linear span of finite Virasoro mode words on $X$, let $\iota:B\hookrightarrow M$ be the given inclusion, and define the complex-linear assignment $I(w,z)=\pi Y_M(w,z)\iota$ for $w\in A_X$; its coefficients are maps $B\to C$. Put $T_B(y)=\sum_nL_B(n)y^{-n-2}$ and $T_C(y)=\sum_nL_C(n)y^{-n-2}$. Virasoro invariance and equivariance give $L_M(n)\iota=\iota L_B(n)$ and $\pi L_M(n)=L_C(n)\pi$. Composing step 4.1 with $\pi$ and $\iota$ therefore gives $$I(L(-1)w,z)=\partial_z I(w,z),\qquad L_C(n)I(w,z)-I(w,z)L_B(n)=\sum_{j\ge0}\binom{n+1}{j}z^{n+1-j}I(L(j-1)w,z).$$ Composing step 5.1 gives precisely $$I(L(-r)w,z)=\operatorname{Res}_y\left(\iota_{y,z}(y-z)^{1-r}T_C(y)I(w,z)-\iota_{z,y}(y-z)^{1-r}I(w,z)T_B(y)\right),\quad r\ge2.$$ These are the derivative, commutator (primary at $w=X$), and descendant-residue identities asserted in the Statement. They hold even when $I=0$; no simplicity or highest-weight grading of arbitrary $B,C$ is asserted. Finally the null state from steps 2.1–3.1 maps to zero by linearity of $I$. [given, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra] ∎
