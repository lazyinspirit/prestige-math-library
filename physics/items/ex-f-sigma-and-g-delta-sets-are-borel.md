## Example

Every $F_\sigma$ subset and every $G_\delta$ subset of $\mathbb R$ is Borel.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A subset $A\subseteq\mathbb R$.

[L1] An $F_\sigma$ set is a countable union of closed subsets of $\mathbb R$, and a $G_\delta$ set is a countable intersection of open subsets ([[def-f-sigma-g-delta]]).

[L2] The Borel sigma-algebra is generated by the open sets ([[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]).

[L3] Sigma-algebras are closed under countable intersections ([[thm-sigma-algebra-closure-laws]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 If $A$ is $F_\sigma$, [L1] writes it as a countable union of closed sets. Closed sets are complements of the open generators in [L2], so they and their countable union are Borel. [L1, L2]

2.1 If $A$ is $G_\delta$, [L1] writes it as a countable intersection of open, hence Borel, sets; [L3] makes the intersection Borel. [L1, L2, L3] ∎
