## Statement

Let $V$ be a C2-cofinite conformal vertex algebra of CFT type over $\mathbb C$. Choose a finite set $A$ of homogeneous positive-weight states such that $\{\mathbf1\}\cup A$ represents a basis of $V/C_2(V)$. Let $W$ be a left $V$-module generated by a vector $w$, with no assumed grading or local finiteness of its conformal operator. Choose an integer $N\geq1$ such that $a_{(n)}w=0$ for every $a\in A$ and $n\geq N$. For each integer $d$ let $H_d(w)$ be the span of all finite strings of homogeneous state modes applied to $w$ whose total operator degree is $d$, where $\deg(a_{(n)})=\mathrm{wt}(a)-n-1$, and the empty string has degree zero. Then $H_d(w)$ is spanned by strings
$$
a^1_{(i_1)}\cdots a^\ell_{(i_\ell)}w,\qquad a^j\in A,\quad i_1<\cdots<i_\ell<N,\quad \sum_{j=1}^{\ell}(\mathrm{wt}(a^j)-i_j-1)=d.
$$
Every such string satisfies $d\geq \ell(\ell-1)/2-\ell(N-1)$. Consequently each $H_d(w)$ is finite dimensional, $H_d(w)=0$ for all sufficiently negative $d$, and $W=\sum_{d\in\mathbb Z}H_d(w)$. This sum is not asserted to be direct. In particular the definition of $H_d(w)$ does not assume that $w$ is a conformal eigenvector.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] [[def-conformal-vertex-algebra]] gives the nonnegative integral state grading, finite-dimensional state-weight spaces, and $V_0=\mathbb C\mathbf1$. [[def-c2-cofinite-vertex-algebra]] gives finite codimension of $C_2(V)$.

[F2] [[lem-cft-locality-to-jacobi-residue-calculus]] proves adjoint Jacobi, state-product weights, and the module iterate and commutator identities from the actual locality-based state-field convention. Thus $\mathrm{wt}(a_{(n)}b)=\mathrm{wt}(a)+\mathrm{wt}(b)-n-1$ for nonzero homogeneous products, and $C_2(V)$ is homogeneous.

[F3] [[def-vertex-algebra-module]] supplies vectorwise mode truncation and the vacuum action. For the finite set $A$, truncation supplies the common cutoff $N$ in the Statement.

## Proof

**Given:** The algebra, representatives, cyclic vector, cutoff, and degree spans in the Statement.

1.1 Fix $d$. Filter its mode words by total state weight $p\geq0$, then length $\ell\geq0$, in lexicographic order. All reductions below preserve $d$. A homogeneous state $v$ is a linear combination of same-weight representatives and homogeneous $a_{(-2)}b$. Replacing $v_{(n)}$ by the iterate expansion of $(a_{(-2)}b)_{(n)}$ replaces its weight $\mathrm{wt}(a)+\mathrm{wt}(b)+1$ by the smaller total weight $\mathrm{wt}(a)+\mathrm{wt}(b)$. The expansion is finite on the fixed tail vector, by the rightmost-mode truncations in the iterate identity. Thus these replacements lower $p$, irrespective of length. A weight-zero representative is a scalar vacuum; its mode is either zero or the identity and so shortens the word without changing $p,d$. Induction on $p$, and within a fixed $p$ on length, therefore reduces the problem to words in $A$. When $p=0$ only vacuum words occur, reducing to the empty word or zero. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 Interchanging adjacent modes uses $[a_{(m)},b_{(n)}]=\sum_{t\geq0}\binom mt(a_{(t)}b)_{(m+n-t)}$. Every nonzero commutator state has weight $\mathrm{wt}(a)+\mathrm{wt}(b)-t-1$, strictly smaller than the replaced total weight; these errors are already treated by induction on $p$. The commutator sum is finite by state truncation. Bubble sorting the unchanged-length terms therefore reduces any $A$-word modulo lower $p$ to nondecreasing mode indices. A sorted word with last index at least $N$ kills $w$. This cutoff is obtained before any claim of strict indices, and uses only truncation on $w$, not an assumed lower conformal-weight bound. [F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 At fixed $p,\ell,d$, there are only finitely many nondecreasing index tuples with last index less than $N$ that can occur in $A$-words of total state weight $p$. Indeed their sum is $p-\ell-d$, so each index is at most $N-1$ and at least $p-\ell-d-(\ell-1)(N-1)$. This supplies a finite set of tuples with its usual lexicographic order. We may therefore induct upwards in that finite order to remove equal indices. Fix an adjacent repetition $a_{(i)}b_{(i)}$ and write its tail as $u$. The normal-product iterate formula is $(a_{(-1)}b)_{(2i+1)}u=\sum_{t\geq0}\big(a_{(-1-t)}b_{(2i+1+t)}+b_{(2i-t)}a_{(t)}\big)u$. If $i<0$, the first sum has exactly one term with both indices $i$, at $t=-i-1$; if $i\geq0$, the second has exactly one, at $t=i$, with reversed order $b_{(i)}a_{(i)}$. In the latter case interchange that pair using step 2.1; its error has lower $p$. Every remaining two-mode term has unequal indices summing to $2i$, hence one less than $i$. Sorting the resulting full word by step 2.1 gives a lexicographically smaller tuple, or zero by its last-index cutoff. The collapsed mode of $a_{(-1)}b$ has the same $p$ and degree as the pair but shorter length, so is already treated by the length induction; replacing its state by representatives if needed is covered by step 1.1. Thus the finite tuple induction removes this repetition. All iterate sums are finite on $u$, and no uniform tail truncation bound is presumed. At fixed filtration the reductions shorten length or lower the finite tuple order; terms at lower filtration were already fully reduced by the outer induction and may have different lengths. All final strings retain degree $d$. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The previous inductions apply to every finite $p,\ell$, so they span the actual $H_d(w)$, which allows unbounded filtration and length. We now bound the resulting spanning family independently of that filtration. Every representative has weight at least one. A strict length-$\ell$ tuple with indices less than $N$ satisfies $i_j\leq N-1-(\ell-j)$, so $d=\sum_j(\mathrm{wt}(a^j)-i_j-1)\geq-\sum_j i_j\geq\ell(\ell-1)/2-\ell(N-1)$. This quadratic has a finite lower bound and tends to positive infinity with $\ell$. At fixed $d$ only finitely many lengths are possible. For each such length there are finitely many choices of the states in $A$; their total weight fixes the index sum, and the argument of step 3.1 gives finitely many index tuples. Therefore the full spanning family for $H_d(w)$ is finite; below the minimum of that quadratic it is empty. Finally the cyclic generation definition gives $W=\sum_dH_d(w)$, without requiring directness. [F1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
