## Statement

Work in $\mathrm{ZF}+\mathrm{AC}$. Let a compact torus $T$ act smoothly on a compact smooth manifold $M$ without boundary, and let $F=M^T$. Then $F$ is a compact smooth embedded submanifold, its connected components $F_j$ are finite in number, and $T_pF=(T_pM)^T$ for every $p\in F$. If $F=\varnothing$, the component assertions are vacuous. The normal bundle $\nu_j$ of each component has no trivial $T$-representation in any fiber. Give $\nu_j$ a $T$-equivariant orientation, write $c_j=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb R}\nu_j$, and choose an invariant complex structure on it; let $\epsilon_j\in\{+1,-1\}$ compare that complex orientation with the chosen orientation. If the nonzero characters of the normal representation on a fiber, counted with multiplicity, are $\chi_{j,1},\ldots,\chi_{j,c_j/2}$, then the component of $e_T(\nu_j)$ in $H^0(F_j;\mathbb R)\otimes\mathcal R$ is

$$\epsilon_j\prod_{a=1}^{c_j/2}u_{\chi_{j,a}}.$$

Consequently $e_T(\nu_j)$ is invertible in $S^{-1}H_T^*(F_j;\mathbb R)$. When $c_j=0$, the product is empty, equals $1$, and the Euler class is already a unit.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a compact torus $T$ acting smoothly on compact $M$ and the equivariant normal-bundle orientations and Thom/Euler conventions of the Statement.

[A1] Assume AC, as required by the real coefficient-ring supplier used in the preceding Borel conventions. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[A2] $F=M^T$, and $\nu_j$ denotes the normal bundle of a component once its smooth compact-submanifold property has been established. No orientation of $M$ is assumed.

[F1] [[def-abbv-equivariant-integration-conventions]]: $\mathcal R=H^*(BT;\mathbb R)$, $S$ is generated by the nonzero character classes $u_\chi$, and $e_T(E)$ is the zero-section pullback of a fiber-normalized Borel Thom class.

[F2] [[lem-qftx-borel-base-is-paracompact-of-cw-type]] gives paracompact Hausdorff CW type for $BT\times F_j$ when $F_j$ is compact smooth. The product Milnor principal bundle is numerable, and pulling it back preserves numerability. Thus its associated normal vector bundle is an oriented numerable bundle over that base and the general Thom theorem applies without an ambient orientation. [[thm-milnor-join-model-is-a-contractible-free-g-space]] [[thm-thom-isomorphism-for-oriented-vector-bundles]]

[F3] $H_q(BT;\mathbb R)$ is finite dimensional in every degree, so the field Künneth cross product is a graded-ring isomorphism $\mathcal R\otimes H^*(F_j;\mathbb R)\cong H^*(BT\times F_j;\mathbb R)$. [[lem-qftx-rational-torus-classifying-space-cohomology]] [[thm-cohomological-kunneth-cross-product-is-a-ring-isomorphism]]

[F4] For a compact smooth Riemannian manifold $F_j$, some $N\geq1$ satisfies $H^q(F_j;\mathbb R)=0$ for $q\geq N$, and its positive-degree ideal has $N$th power zero. [[lem-qftx-compact-manifold-cohomology-positive-ideal-nilpotent]]

[F5] Euler classes are natural and multiply for ordered direct sums. Orientation reversal multiplies the real Thom and Euler classes by $-1$, directly by their fiber normalization and uniqueness. [[thm-naturality-orientation-sign-and-whitney-product-for-euler-classes]]

## Proof

1.1 Average a Riemannian metric over normalized Haar measure on $T$. At $p\in F$, the exponential map of this invariant metric is $T$-equivariant and is injective on a sufficiently small ball in $T_pM$. Thus, for $v$ in that ball, $\exp_p(v)$ is fixed by every $t\in T$ exactly when $v$ is fixed by every linear map $d_pt$; the fixed set near $p$ is the exponential image of $(T_pM)^T$. Therefore $F$ is locally a smooth submanifold and $T_pF=(T_pM)^T$. The fixed locus is closed in compact $M$, hence compact; its connected components are open in this locally connected manifold and compactness gives only finitely many. [given, A1, A2, construct]

2.1 The orthogonal complement of $(T_pM)^T$ in $T_pM$ is the normal fiber, so it contains no $T$-fixed vector. The complexification of an orthogonal representation of the abelian group $T$ is a commuting family of unitary maps and decomposes into simultaneous character eigenspaces; complex conjugation pairs the $\chi$ and $\bar\chi$ spaces. A real one-dimensional character would have image in $\{+1,-1\}$ and is trivial because $T$ is connected. Thus every nontrivial real summand is two-dimensional. Averaging the fiber action against a character gives a smooth idempotent projection on the complexified normal bundle. Its rank is locally constant and hence constant on connected $F_j$. At one point only finitely many characters occur, since the fiber has finite dimension; constant ranks therefore imply exactly those finitely many characters occur everywhere on $F_j$. The real conjugate-pair subbundles are consequently smooth. [given, A1, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 If $\nu_j$ has nonzero weights, choose $X\in\mathfrak t$ outside the finite union of their kernels. For a character $\chi$, define the real linear weight $\omega_\chi(X)$ by $\chi(\exp(sX))=\exp(2\pi i s\omega_\chi(X))$. On its real weight subbundle the infinitesimal action $A_X$ satisfies $A_X^2=-(2\pi\omega_\chi(X))^2I$, so $J_\chi=A_X/(2\pi\omega_\chi(X))$ is invariant and satisfies $J_\chi^2=-I$. Their direct sum gives an invariant complex structure on $\nu_j$. Its complex orientation may agree or disagree with the chosen normal orientation, with comparison sign $\epsilon_j$. The weight projections vary continuously, so their ranks, and therefore the character multiplicities, are constant on connected $F_j$. If there are no weights, then $\nu_j=0$ and the complex structure is vacuous. [given, A1, A2, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 By step 1.1, $F_j$ is compact smooth, so [F2] applies. In local normal charts and Milnor principal charts the associated Borel normal bundle is an oriented rank-$c_j$ vector bundle over $BT\times F_j$; the pulled-back principal numeration, combined with a finite normal-bundle trivialization over compact $F_j$, supplies a numeration. Thus the general Thom theorem supplies its normalized real Thom class independently of any orientation of $M$. The torus acts trivially on $F_j$, hence $ET\times_TF_j\cong BT\times F_j$ and [F3] gives $H_T^*(F_j;\mathbb R)=H^*(F_j;\mathbb R)\otimes\mathcal R$. Restrict $e_T(\nu_j)$ to any point of $F_j$. The normal representation is the direct sum of the complex character lines in step 3.1; the Euler class of a direct sum is the product because the product of the normalized Thom classes restricts to the positive generator on each direct-sum fiber and hence is its normalized Thom class. By [F1] each line has Euler class $u_{\chi_{j,a}}$, and the orientation comparison gives $f_j=\epsilon_j\prod_a u_{\chi_{j,a}}$ as the degree-zero-in-$H^*(F_j)$ component. Every factor is nonzero and belongs to $S$, so $f_j$ is a unit in $S^{-1}\mathcal R$. [given, A1, F1, F2, F3, F5, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Write $e_T(\nu_j)=f_j+n_j$, where $n_j$ has positive degree in $H^*(F_j;\mathbb R)$. By [F4], the ideal $\mathcal R\otimes H^{>0}(F_j;\mathbb R)$ has $N$th power zero for some $N\geq1$, hence $n_j^N=0$; this remains true after localization. In the localized ring the inverse is $f_j^{-1}\sum_{q=0}^{N-1}(-f_j^{-1}n_j)^q$, since multiplying by $f_j+n_j=f_j(1+f_j^{-1}n_j)$ telescopes to $1-(-f_j^{-1}n_j)^N=1$. If $c_j=0$, $n_j=0$ and $f_j=1$, giving the empty-product case as well. [given, A1, F3, F4, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
