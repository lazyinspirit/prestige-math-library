## Statement

**(a) Preservation.** Let $x:I\to A$ be a $C^2$ future timelike worldline and let $X,Y:I\to V$ be $C^1$ Fermi–Walker transported fields along $x$ ([[def-sr-fermi-walker-transport]]). Then $\tau\mapsto\eta(X(\tau),Y(\tau))$ is constant on $I$; in particular Fermi–Walker transport preserves norms and orthogonality, and the unique Fermi–Walker transported family with initial values a tetrad $(E_0(\tau_0),\dots,E_3(\tau_0))$ with $E_0(\tau_0)=U(\tau_0)/c$ is an orthonormal tetrad $(E_0(\tau),\dots,E_3(\tau))$ at every $\tau$ with $E_0(\tau)=U(\tau)/c$ (observer-adapted tetrad), by [[lem-sr-fermi-walker-transport-existence-and-uniqueness]].

**(b) Rindler wedge congruence.** In an inertial chart $(x^0,x,y,z)$ consider $\Phi(\theta,\rho,y,z):=(\rho\sinh\theta,\rho\cosh\theta,y,z)$ on $\mathbb R\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbb R^2$, with $\theta\in\mathbb R$ and $\rho>0$. Then $\Phi$ is a smooth bijection onto the right wedge $W:=\{(x^0,x):x>|x^0|\}$, with smooth inverse $\rho=\sqrt{x^2-(x^0)^2}$, $\theta=\tfrac12\log\bigl((x+x^0)/(x-x^0)\bigr)$ ([[thm-hyperbolic-identities-and-derivatives]], [[def-hyperbolic-functions]]); the pullback of $\eta$ is $g=-\rho^2d\theta^2+d\rho^2+dy^2+dz^2$; the field $n:=\rho^{-1}\partial/\partial\theta$ is a future unit timelike field on $W$ whose congruence is Born rigid and irrotational, with acceleration $a=\nabla_nn=\rho^{-1}\partial/\partial\rho$, hence $\eta(a,a)=\rho^{-2}$; its rest slices $\theta=const$ are open subsets of hyperplanes orthogonal to $n$ with induced metric $d\rho^2+dy^2+dz^2$ independent of $\theta$; and the frame $(n,\partial/\partial\rho,\partial/\partial y,\partial/\partial z)$ is a tetrad field which is not a coordinate frame, since $[n,\partial/\partial\rho]=+(1/\rho)n$ is nonzero ([[def-lie-bracket-of-smooth-vector-fields]]).

**(c) Rigid rotation, ambient nonintegrability and ring holonomy.** Fix real $\Omega$. In the inertial chart let $U_\Omega$ be the open timelike region $r^2=(x^1)^2+(x^2)^2<c^2/\Omega^2$ when $\Omega\ne0$, and the whole chart when $\Omega=0$. On this four-dimensional open set put $K=\partial_0+(\Omega/c)(-x^2\partial_1+x^1\partial_2)$, $\gamma(r)=(1-\Omega^2r^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$, and $n=\gamma(r)K$. This is a smooth future unit timelike Born-rigid congruence. If $\Omega\ne0$, its three-dimensional rest distribution in the ambient open set is nowhere integrable: its vorticity is nowhere zero and no ambient rest-simultaneity hypersurface exists locally. If $\Omega=0$, $n=\partial_0$ is inertial and irrotational, and the rest hypersurfaces are $x^0=const$. Now fix $R>0$ with $|\Omega|R<c$ and restrict to the timelike ring cylinder $r=R$, $x^3=const$, whose rotating angle is $\varphi=\phi-\Omega t$ modulo $2\pi$, with $x^0=ct$. The ring observer $\phi=\phi_0+\Omega t$ has $n=\gamma(R)\bigl(1,-(\Omega R/c)\sin\phi,(\Omega R/c)\cos\phi,0\bigr)$. The induced metric is $-(c^2-\Omega^2R^2)dt^2+2\Omega R^2\,dt\,d\varphi+R^2d\varphi^2$. The ambient rest covector restricted to the cylinder is proportional to $-(c^2-\Omega^2R^2)dt+\Omega R^2d\varphi$, so the one-dimensional rest distribution on that cylinder is locally integrable and satisfies $dt/d\varphi=\Omega R^2/(c^2-\Omega^2R^2)$. After one positive circuit in the rotating label, its lift starting at $t=0$ has signed time offset $\Delta t=2\pi\Omega R^2/(c^2-\Omega^2R^2)$. For $\Omega\ne0$ it returns to the same rotating label at a different time and does not close as a ring rest-simultaneity curve; there is no globally single-valued real rest-synchronization time on the entire cylinder with $C^1$ regularity, $df(\partial_t)>0$ and differential annihilating these rest lines. This global ring holonomy is distinct from the ambient local Frobenius obstruction. For $\Omega=0$ the offset is zero and ring rest lines are $t=const$.

**(d) Bell first-order field.** Let $q:J\to\mathbb R^3$ be $C^2$ with $q(t)=(q_1(t),0,0)$ and $|q_1'(t)|<c$, and consider the $C^1$ future unit field of the translated worldlines $x_\xi(t)=(ct,q(t)+\xi)$ parametrized by $\xi$ in an open subset of $\mathbb R^3$, with $n=\gamma\bigl(1,q_1'(t)/c,0,0\bigr)$, $\gamma=(1-q_1'(t)^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$, and unit longitudinal rest vector $s=\gamma\bigl(q_1'(t)/c,1,0,0\bigr)$. For this $C^1$ field define the first-order rest projection $h^a{}_b=\delta^a{}_b+n^an_b$ and projected deformation $\theta_{ab}=h^c{}_ah^d{}_b\partial_{(c}n_{d)}$, using the explicit first-order version of [[def-flat-covariant-derivative-on-affine-minkowski-space]]. These coordinate formulas need only $C^1$ $n$, give a continuous tensor, and agree with the smooth congruence definitions when $q$ is smooth; in this first-order sense Born rigidity means $\theta=0$. Then

$$\theta(s,s)=s^as^b\partial_an_b=\gamma^3q_1'(t)q_1''(t)/c^3,$$

which is nonzero exactly when $q_1'(t)q_1''(t)$ is nonzero; hence the congruence is not Born rigid on any neighbourhood of a parameter value with $q_1'(t)q_1''(t)\ne0$, although for every fixed chart time $t$ the separation between the members labelled $\xi_1$ and $\xi_2$ is the constant $|\xi_1-\xi_2|$. A breaking or fracture conclusion about a material rod would additionally require an elasticity model and a failure criterion and is not a kinematic statement.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The affine Minkowski model with its inertial chart calculus, the Fermi–Walker transport rule of [[def-sr-fermi-walker-transport]], the congruence kinematics and Frobenius criterion of [[def-sr-observer-congruence-and-kinematics]] and [[thm-sr-local-congruence-adapted-charts-and-frobenius]], and the four explicit data sets (a)–(d) of the Statement ([[def-sr-affine-minkowski-model]], [[def-inertial-frame-and-inertial-chart]]).

[F1] Fermi–Walker derivative: $D_{\mathrm{FW}}X/d\tau=X'/\tau'-c^{-2}\bigl(\eta(A,X)U-\eta(U,X)A\bigr)$; the four-velocity satisfies $D_{\mathrm{FW}}U/d\tau=0$; where primes refer to the generic curve parameter $s$ and $\tau'>0$; existence and uniqueness of transported fields with prescribed initial values hold, and the solution map is linear ([[def-sr-fermi-walker-transport]] clauses (a),(b), [[lem-sr-fermi-walker-transport-existence-and-uniqueness]]).

[F2] A smooth vector field $K$ is Killing when $\mathcal L_K\eta=0$, equivalently $\nabla_aK_b+\nabla_bK_a=0$; this follows from the Lie-derivative evaluation formula and the torsion-free metric-compatible derivative. In an inertial chart, $\eta(u,v)=-u^0v^0+u^1v^1+u^2v^2+u^3v^3$, the flat covariant derivative acts on components by ordinary coordinate derivatives $\partial_a$, is torsion-free and metric compatible, and its first-order $C^1$ version is available: for $C^1$ fields $(\nabla_XY)^a=X^b\partial_bY^a$ and $[X,Y]^a=X^b\partial_bY^a-Y^b\partial_bX^a$ ([[def-flat-covariant-derivative-on-affine-minkowski-space]], [[def-ck-tensor-field-on-minkowski-space]]).

[F3] Kinematics: $\nabla_an_b=-n_aa_b+\theta_{ab}+\omega_{ab}$, $\theta$ symmetric, $\omega$ antisymmetric, both horizontal, $(\mathcal L_nh)_{ab}=2\theta_{ab}$, so Born rigidity is $\theta=0$; for the metric dual $\alpha=n^\flat$, $\alpha\wedge d\alpha=0$ on an open set if and only if $\omega=0$ there, if and only if the rest distribution $\ker\alpha$ is integrable there ([[def-sr-observer-congruence-and-kinematics]], [[thm-sr-local-congruence-adapted-charts-and-frobenius]]).

[F4] Hyperbolic functions: $\cosh^2\theta-\sinh^2\theta=1$, $\cosh>0$, $\cosh\theta>|\sinh\theta|$, $\tanh=\sinh/\cosh$, and the derivatives $\sinh'=\cosh$, $\cosh'=\sinh$ ([[thm-hyperbolic-identities-and-derivatives]], [[def-hyperbolic-functions]]).

[F5] Exterior calculus: $d(\alpha\wedge\beta)=d\alpha\wedge\beta+(-1)^k\alpha\wedge d\beta$ for a $k$-form $\alpha$, $d^2=0$, $df=\sum_a\partial_af\,dx^a$ for functions in a chart, and $d\alpha_{ab}=\partial_a\alpha_b-\partial_b\alpha_a$ for one-forms ([[def-wedge-product-of-differential-forms]], [[def-exterior-derivative-by-the-invariant-vector-field-formula]], [[def-closed-and-exact-differential-forms]]).

[F6] Lie bracket: $[X,Y]^a=X^b\partial_bY^a-Y^b\partial_bX^a$ and $[fX,Y]=f[X,Y]-Y(f)X$ for a smooth function $f$ ([[def-lie-bracket-of-smooth-vector-fields]], [[def-flat-covariant-derivative-on-affine-minkowski-space]]).

[F7] A tetrad at an event is an ordered basis $(e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3)$ with $\eta(e_a,e_b)=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)_{ab}$ and $e_0$ future; a congruence is a smooth field $n$ with $\eta(n,n)=-1$, $n$ future; a vector with components $(u^0,u^1,u^2,u^3)$ in an inertial frame is future timelike if and only if $-u^0{}^2+|\vec u|^2<0$ and $u^0>0$ ([[def-sr-chart-tetrad-and-congruence-distinction]], [[def-causal-classification-of-lorentz-vectors]], [[lem-timelike-cone-has-two-convex-components]], [[def-time-orientation-and-future-causal-sets]]).

[F8] Chain rule and smoothness of composites of $C^1$ or smooth maps; the component function $\gamma(r)=(1-\Omega^2r^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$ is smooth where $r<c/|\Omega|$ ([[thm-chain-rule]], [[def-derivative]]).

[F10] The functions $\sin,\cos:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ are the everywhere-convergent real power series of [[def-sine-and-cosine-by-power-series]]. Their derivatives are $\cos$ and $-\sin$ ([[thm-sine-and-cosine-derivatives]]), so repeated differentiation makes them smooth; $\sin^2\phi+\cos^2\phi=1$ ([[cor-trigonometric-parity-and-pythagorean-identity]]). Both have period $2\pi$ ([[thm-sine-cosine-zero-sets-and-fundamental-period]]), and $(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)$ identifies the angle modulo $2\pi$ with the unit circle ([[thm-sine-and-cosine-parametrize-the-unit-circle]]). The logarithm is the inverse of $\exp$ on $(0,\infty)$ ([[def-natural-logarithm]]). A smooth Euclidean map with invertible derivative has a smooth local inverse ([[lem-choice-free-smooth-inverse-function-theorem-in-euclidean-space]]).

[F9] Flow box: a smooth nowhere-zero vector field on a smooth manifold is locally $\partial/\partial u^0$ in suitable coordinates ([[thm-flow-box-theorem]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Preservation (a): write $s$ for the given parameter and $\tau'(s)>0$ for its proper-time derivative. By [F1], $X'=\tau'c^{-2}(\eta(A,X)U-\eta(U,X)A)$ and likewise for $Y$. Bilinearity gives $\frac{d}{ds}\eta(X,Y)=\eta(X',Y)+\eta(X,Y')=\tau'c^{-2}[\eta(A,X)\eta(U,Y)-\eta(U,X)\eta(A,Y)+\eta(A,Y)\eta(U,X)-\eta(U,Y)\eta(A,X)]=0$. The two pairs cancel, so the scalar pairing is constant on the interval by [[cor-zero-derivative-implies-constant]]; the fields are $C^1$, including continuity at any included endpoints. Norms and orthogonality are therefore preserved. For the unique transported fields with prescribed tetrad initial values, all pairings remain $\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)_{ab}$, and the time leg is $U/c$ because [F1] makes $U/c$ transported with the same initial value. Hence the family remains an observer-adapted tetrad. [F1, F7, given]

1.2 Rindler coordinates (b), part 1: the map $\Phi(\theta,\rho,y,z)=(\rho\sinh\theta,\rho\cosh\theta,y,z)$ is smooth on $\mathbb R\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbb R^2$ by [F4] and [F8], and its image is contained in $W$ because $\cosh\theta>|\sinh\theta|$ for every $\theta$; conversely, for $(x^0,x)$ with $x>|x^0|$ the numbers $\rho:=\sqrt{x^2-(x^0)^2}>0$ and $\theta:=\tfrac12\log\bigl((x+x^0)/(x-x^0)\bigr)\in\mathbb R$ are well defined, and the identities $\rho\sinh\theta=x^0$, $\rho\cosh\theta=x$ follow from $e^{\pm\theta}=\sqrt{(x\pm x^0)/(x\mp x^0)}$ and $\cosh^2-\sinh^2=1$; hence $\Phi$ is a bijection of its domain onto $W$. Its $(\theta,\rho)$ derivative block has determinant $\rho(\cosh^2\theta-\sinh^2\theta)=\rho>0$ and the transverse block is the identity; the inverse-function theorem in [F10] therefore makes its inverse smooth locally at every point of $W$, hence smooth on $W$. Differentiating the four component functions gives $dx^0=\sinh\theta\,d\rho+\rho\cosh\theta\,d\theta$ and $dx=\cosh\theta\,d\rho+\rho\sinh\theta\,d\theta$, so the pullback metric is $-(\sinh\theta\,d\rho+\rho\cosh\theta\,d\theta)^2+(\cosh\theta\,d\rho+\rho\sinh\theta\,d\theta)^2+dy^2+dz^2=d\rho^2-\rho^2d\theta^2+dy^2+dz^2$, the cross terms cancelling and the $\sinh^2-\cosh^2=-1$ identity producing the term $-\rho^2d\theta^2$. [F4, F8, F10]

1.3 Rigid rotation (c), part 1: $K=\partial_0+(\Omega/c)(-x^2\partial_1+x^1\partial_2)$ is a smooth vector field on the whole chart, and $\eta(K,K)=-1+\Omega^2r^2/c^2<0$ exactly on the stated region $U_\Omega$ (and everywhere when $\Omega=0$) by [F2]; its components in the inertial chart are $K=(1,-\tfrac{\Omega}{c}x^2,\tfrac{\Omega}{c}x^1,0)$, so $K$ is future where it is timelike, and $\gamma(r)=(1-\Omega^2r^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$ is smooth and positive on $U_\Omega$ by [F8], so $n=\gamma K$ satisfies $\eta(n,n)=\gamma^2(-1+\Omega^2r^2/c^2)=-1$ and is future unit, that is, a congruence on $U_\Omega$ with the stated observer field. Computing the symmetric derivative of the components $\eta_{ab}K^b=(-1,-\tfrac{\Omega}{c}x^2,\tfrac{\Omega}{c}x^1,0)$ gives $\partial_a(\eta K)_b+\partial_b(\eta K)_a=0$: the only nonzero first partials are $\partial_1(\eta K)_2=\Omega/c$ and $\partial_2(\eta K)_1=-\Omega/c$, which cancel in the symmetrization, so $K$ is a Killing field. [F2, F8]

1.4 Bell setup (d): for $q$ of class $C^2$ with $|q_1'|<c$, the functions $\gamma=(1-q_1'^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$ and $\beta=q_1'/c$ are $C^1$ in $t$ by [F8], so the field $n(x)=\gamma\bigl(1,\beta,0,0\bigr)$ evaluated at $t=x^0/c$ is a $C^1$ future unit field on any open subset $\Omega$ of the chart with $\Omega\subseteq\{|q_1'(x^0/c)|<c\}$; it is tangent to the translated worldlines $x_\xi(t)=(ct,q(t)+\xi)$, since their coordinate derivative $(c,q_1',0,0)=c(1,\beta,0,0)$ is a positive multiple of $n$. The longitudinal unit vector $s=\gamma(\beta,1,0,0)$ satisfies $\eta(s,s)=\gamma^2(-\beta^2+1)=1$ and $\eta(n,s)=\gamma^2(-\beta+\beta)=0$, so $s$ is a unit rest vector of $n$. [F2, F7, F8]

2.1 Rindler congruence (b), part 2: in the coordinates $(\theta,\rho,y,z)$ the field is $n=\rho^{-1}\partial_\theta$; its acceleration in these coordinates is computed from the inertial components of $n$, namely $n=\cosh\theta\,e_0+\sinh\theta\,e_1$ (because $\rho^{-1}\partial_\theta=\cosh\theta\,\partial_0+\sinh\theta\,\partial_1$ in the inertial chart, as $\partial_\theta=\rho(\cosh\theta\,\partial_0+\sinh\theta\,\partial_1)$): the coordinate partial derivatives of the components are $\partial_{x^0}\theta=x/\rho^2$ and $\partial_{x}\theta=-x^0/\rho^2$, whence $\nabla_{\partial_0}n=(x/\rho^2)\partial_\rho$ and $\nabla_{\partial_1}n=-(x^0/\rho^2)\partial_\rho$; therefore $a=\nabla_nn=\cosh\theta\cdot(x/\rho^2)\partial_\rho-\sinh\theta\cdot(x^0/\rho^2)\partial_\rho=(x\cosh\theta-x^0\sinh\theta)\rho^{-2}\partial_\rho=\rho^{-1}\partial_\rho$. Its norm is $\eta(a,a)=\rho^{-2}\eta(\partial_\rho,\partial_\rho)=\rho^{-2}$ by the pullback metric of step 1.2. The metric has no $d\theta d\rho$, $d\theta dy$ or $d\theta dz$ cross terms, so $n$ is orthogonal to $\partial_\rho,\partial_y,\partial_z$; the slices $\theta=const$ have tangent space $\ker(n^\flat)$ (they are the level sets of the coordinate $\theta$), hence the rest distribution is integrable, and by [F3] its vorticity vanishes; since the induced metric $d\rho^2+dy^2+dz^2$ on the slices is independent of $\theta$ and $h(n,\cdot)=0$, while $[n,\partial_\rho]=(1/\rho)n$ and $[n,\partial_y]=[n,\partial_z]=0$, the Lie-derivative formula $\mathcal L_nh(X,Y)=n(h(X,Y))-h([n,X],Y)-h(X,[n,Y])$ gives zero on this rest basis, $(\mathcal L_nh)(\chi,\chi')=0$ for $\chi,\chi'\in\{\partial_\rho,\partial_y,\partial_z\}$ and the congruence is Born rigid by [F3]. Finally $[n,\partial_\rho]=[\rho^{-1}\partial_\theta,\partial_\rho]=-\partial_\rho(\rho^{-1})\partial_\theta=\rho^{-2}\partial_\theta=+(1/\rho)n\ne0$ by [F6], so the frame $(n,\partial_\rho,\partial_y,\partial_z)$, whose members are orthonormal by the metric displayed in step 1.2, is not the coordinate frame of any chart (for coordinate frames all brackets vanish). [F2, F3, F6, step 1.2]

2.2 Ambient rigid rotation (c), part 2: with $n=\gamma K$ and $n_b=\gamma K_b$, the symmetric derivative is $\nabla_{(a}n_{b)}=K_{(a}\partial_{b)}\gamma+\gamma\nabla_{(a}K_{b)}$ by [F2], and the second term vanishes because $K$ is Killing (step 1.3); projecting with $h$ kills the first term, because $h^c{}_aK_c=K_a+n_a\langle n,K\rangle=K_a-n_a/\gamma=0$ (since $\eta(n,K)=\gamma\eta(K,K)=-1/\gamma$ and $n_a/\gamma=K_a$); hence $\theta=\gamma\,h^ch^d\nabla_{(c}K_{d)}+0=0$ and the congruence is Born rigid by [F3]. For the vorticity put $\kappa:=\eta(K,\cdot)=(-1,-\tfrac{\Omega}{c}x^2,\tfrac{\Omega}{c}x^1,0)$ in components; then $d\kappa=2(\Omega/c)\,dx^1\wedge dx^2$ and $\kappa\wedge d\kappa=-2(\Omega/c)\,dx^0\wedge dx^1\wedge dx^2$ by [F5], the terms containing $(x^1dx^2-x^2dx^1)\wedge dx^1\wedge dx^2$ vanishing by alternation. Since $n^\flat=\gamma\kappa$ and $d\gamma\wedge\kappa\wedge\kappa=0$ by alternation, $n^\flat\wedge d(n^\flat)=\gamma^2\kappa\wedge d\kappa=-2\gamma^2(\Omega/c)dx^0\wedge dx^1\wedge dx^2$, which is nonzero at every point of $U_\Omega$ when $\Omega\ne0$; by [F3] the vorticity $\omega$ is nowhere zero there and the ambient rest distribution is nowhere integrable, so no rest-simultaneity hypersurface exists locally. For $\Omega=0$ the field is $n=\partial_0$, which is inertial and irrotational with rest hypersurfaces $x^0=const$. [F2, F3, F5, step 1.3]

2.3 Bell deformation (d): with $\beta=q_1'/c$ and $\beta'=q_1''/c$, the components of $n$ are $n=(n^0,n^1,n^2,n^3)=(\gamma,\gamma\beta,0,0)$, hence $n_b=\eta_{bc}n^c=(-\gamma,\gamma\beta,0,0)$, and $n$ depends on the coordinates only through $t=x^0/c$, so $\partial_1n=\partial_2n=\partial_3n=0$ and $\partial_0=\tfrac1c\tfrac{d}{dt}$ by [F2]. Using $\tfrac{d\gamma}{dt}=\gamma^3\beta\beta'$ (from $\gamma=(1-\beta^2)^{-1/2}$ and the chain rule) and $1+\gamma^2\beta^2=\gamma^2$ one gets $\partial_0n_0=-\gamma^3\beta\beta'/c$ and $\partial_0n_1=\tfrac1c\tfrac{d}{dt}(\gamma\beta)=\tfrac{\gamma^3\beta'/c}{1}$; contracting with $s=\gamma(\beta,1,0,0)$ gives $s^as^b\partial_an_b=(\gamma\beta)^2\bigl(-\gamma^3\beta\beta'/c\bigr)+(\gamma\beta)(\gamma)\bigl(\gamma^3\beta'/c\bigr)=\tfrac{\gamma^5\beta\beta'}{c}(1-\beta^2)=\tfrac{\gamma^3\beta\beta'}{c}=\gamma^3q_1'q_1''/c^3$, where $1-\beta^2=\gamma^{-2}$. Since $s$ is a rest vector ($\eta(n,s)=0$ by step 1.4), the projections act as the identity on $s$ and $\theta(s,s)=s^as^b\partial_an_b$ in the first-order sense; all transverse components of $\partial_an_b$ vanish because $n$ depends only on $t$, so $\theta(s,s)$ is nonzero exactly when $q_1'(t)q_1''(t)\ne0$, and then the first-order rigidity condition $\theta=0$ fails on a neighbourhood of $t$; the translated worldlines have, at every fixed chart time, the constant separation $|\xi_1-\xi_2|$, so this failure of Born rigidity is not in conflict with the constant labelling. No crack, fracture or elasticity conclusion is drawn. [F1, F2, F8, step 1.4]

3.1 Ring cylinder geometry (c), part 3: parameterize the cylinder $r=R$, $x^3=const$ by $(t,\varphi)$ with $x^0=ct$ and $\varphi=\phi-\Omega t$, so that the inertial angle is $\phi=\varphi+\Omega t$ and the point is $R(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)$. Substituting $d\phi=d\varphi+\Omega\,dt$ into $ds^2=-c^2dt^2+R^2d\phi^2$ gives the induced metric $-(c^2-\Omega^2R^2)dt^2+2\Omega R^2dtd\varphi+R^2d\varphi^2$, a nondegenerate Lorentzian metric on the cylinder since $|\Omega|R<c$. The observer with label $\phi_0$ has inertial angle $\phi(t)=\phi_0+\Omega t$, hence tangent $c\partial_0+\Omega R(-\sin\phi\,\partial_1+\cos\phi\,\partial_2)=cK$ evaluated at the ring, so its dimensionless four-velocity is $n=\gamma(R)K$ and its four-velocity is $cn$, whose components at the ring are $c\gamma(R)(1,-(\Omega R/c)\sin\phi,(\Omega R/c)\cos\phi,0)$ as stated. The covector $\kappa$ of step 2.2 restricts to the cylinder as $\kappa=-c\,dt+(\Omega/c)R^2d\phi=-c\,dt+(\Omega R^2/c)(d\varphi+\Omega\,dt)=-\tfrac{c^2-\Omega^2R^2}{c}dt+\tfrac{\Omega R^2}{c}d\varphi$, so the ambient rest covector $n^\flat=\gamma(R)\kappa$ is proportional to $-(c^2-\Omega^2R^2)dt+\Omega R^2d\varphi$; its kernel on the cylinder is the line field generated by $\partial_\varphi+\tfrac{\Omega R^2}{c^2-\Omega^2R^2}\partial_t$, since the kernel condition is $-(c^2-\Omega^2R^2)dt(v)+\Omega R^2d\varphi(v)=0$ for $v=a\partial_t+b\partial_\varphi$, that is $-(c^2-\Omega^2R^2)a+\Omega R^2b=0$, so $v$ is a multiple of $\partial_\varphi+\tfrac{\Omega R^2}{c^2-\Omega^2R^2}\partial_t$; equivalently $dt/d\varphi=\Omega R^2/(c^2-\Omega^2R^2)$ along rest lines. [F2, step 1.3, step 2.2, F10]

4.1 Ring holonomy (c), part 4: the vector field $Z:=\partial_\varphi+\tfrac{\Omega R^2}{c^2-\Omega^2R^2}\partial_t$ on the cylinder is smooth and nowhere zero (the coefficient of $\partial_\varphi$ is $1$), so by the flow box theorem [F9] applied on the two-dimensional cylinder there are local coordinates in which $Z=\partial/\partial u^0$; in particular the rest line field is locally integrable, its integral curves satisfy $dt/d\varphi=\tfrac{\Omega R^2}{c^2-\Omega^2R^2}$ by the kernel computation of step 3.1, and integrating from $\varphi=0$ to $\varphi=2\pi$ at the initial label gives the signed offset $\Delta t=\tfrac{2\pi\Omega R^2}{c^2-\Omega^2R^2}$, which returns to the same rotating label $\varphi$ at a different chart time when $\Omega\ne0$. If $f$ were a globally single-valued real function on the whole cylinder with $df=\mu\,(dt-\tfrac{\Omega R^2}{c^2-\Omega^2R^2}d\varphi)$ for a continuous multiplier $\mu>0$ (the time-orientation convention $df(\partial_t)>0$) (so that the kernel of $df$ is exactly the rest-line distribution, so the level sets of $f$ are tangent to those lines), then the angular lift $F(\varphi):=f(0,\varphi)$ would have $F(0)=F(2\pi)$ by single-valuedness on the circle. Its derivative is $F'(\varphi)=-\mu(0,\varphi)\Omega R^2/(c^2-\Omega^2R^2)$, with a fixed nonzero sign because the multiplier is time-oriented and $\Omega\ne0$, $R>0$. The mean value theorem [[cor-mean-value-theorem]] on $[0,2\pi]$ therefore gives $F(2\pi)-F(0)=2\pi F'(\xi)\ne0$ for some $\xi\in(0,2\pi)$; this contradiction shows that no such global synchronization function exists. The obstruction is the holonomy of the global ring and is distinct from the ambient pointwise nonintegrability of step 2.2, which is a local statement in the four-dimensional open set and does not apply to the one-dimensional rest line field on the cylinder. For $\Omega=0$ the kernel condition reads $dt=0$ and both the offset and the obstruction vanish. [F5, F9, step 3.1, F10]

5.1 Conclusion: step 1.1 proves (a), including the preservation of norms and orthogonality and the observer-adapted tetrad; steps 1.2 and 2.1 prove (b), including the Rindler metric, the unit future field, the acceleration $\rho^{-1}\partial_\rho$, Born rigidity, vanishing vorticity, the rest slices and the non-coordinate-frame bracket; steps 1.3, 2.2, 3.1 and 4.1 prove (c), including the Born-rigid rotating congruence, its nowhere-zero vorticity and ambient nonintegrability for $\Omega\ne0$, the inertial case $\Omega=0$, the ring metric, the rest-line equation, the time offset $2\pi\Omega R^2/(c^2-\Omega^2R^2)$ and the impossibility of a globally single-valued ring synchronization time; and steps 1.4 and 2.3 prove (d), with the first-order deformation $\theta(s,s)=\gamma^3q_1'q_1''/c^3$ and the exclusion of material fracture claims. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 1.4, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 2.3] ∎

## Remarks

- **First-order versus smooth calculus.** Part (d) is deliberately stated for a $C^1$ field $n$ using the first-order clause of [[def-flat-covariant-derivative-on-affine-minkowski-space]]: only first derivatives of $n$ occur in the projected deformation, the result is a continuous tensor, and no smooth extension of the Bell congruence or its flow is asserted. When $q$ is smooth the formulas agree with the smooth congruence definitions of [[def-sr-observer-congruence-and-kinematics]].

- **Two different obstructions in part (c).** The ambient rest distribution over the four-dimensional rotating open set is nowhere integrable for $\Omega\ne0$ (nowhere-zero vorticity), while the one-dimensional rest line field on the ring cylinder is locally integrable with a nonzero global holonomy; the second is not an instance of the first, and no locally defined synchronization function on the cylinder can be single-valued around the ring.
