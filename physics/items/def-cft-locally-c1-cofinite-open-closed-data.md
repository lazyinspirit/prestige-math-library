## Definition

Fix a positive-graded chiral vertex operator algebra $V=\bigoplus_{n\geq0}V_n$ with $V_0=\mathbb C\mathbf1$ and put $V_{>0}=\bigoplus_{n>0}V_n$. Here positive-graded has the exact conformal-vector, Virasoro, finite-dimensional weight-space, and CFT-type conventions of [[def-conformal-vertex-algebra]]. Use the ordinary non-super mode convention. The source category $V\text{-mod}_{\rm f.g.}$ consists of $V$-modules $W$ with locally finite $L_W(0)$, finite-dimensional generalized eigenspaces $W_{[h]}$, and a finite set $\Delta_1,\ldots,\Delta_m\in\mathbb C$ such that $W=\bigoplus_{j=1}^m\bigoplus_{n\geq0}W_{[\Delta_j+n]}$. Also require $C_1(W)=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{u_{-1}w:u\in V_{>0},\ w\in W\}$ to have finite codimension and the source restricted-dual module $W^\vee=\bigoplus_hW_{[h]}^*$ to be finitely generated by a finite subset under the $V$-modes. The restricted-dual space and opposite-field action are those in [[def-cft-contragredient-module]]. That item defines the candidate action but explicitly leaves its module axioms to a separate theorem; [[thm-cft-contragredient-module-well-defined]] supplies the actual module used here. Finite-coset support, finite-dimensional generalized weight spaces, $C_1$-cofiniteness, and dual finite generation are separate clauses; the R2 $C_1$-quotient condition alone is not this source category.

For any such generalized modules $W_1,W_2,W_3$, write $\mathcal I_{\log}\binom{W_3}{W_1\ W_2}$ for the logarithmic intertwining-operator space of [[def-cft-logarithmic-intertwining-operator]]. Define
$$\mathcal I^0\binom{W_3}{W_1\ W_2}:=\left\{\mathcal Y\in\mathcal I_{\log}\binom{W_3}{W_1\ W_2}: (w_1)_{n;k}w_2=0\text{ for every }w_1\in W_1,w_2\in W_2,n\in\mathbb C,k\geq1\right\},$$
where $\mathcal Y(w_1,x)w_2=\sum_{n\in\mathbb C}\sum_{k\geq0}(w_1)_{n;k}w_2\,x^{-n-1}(\log x)^k$ is the source expansion.
Thus $\mathcal I^0$ is the no-log subspace on generalized modules. It is not an assertion that generalized modules are ordinary; when all three modules are ordinary it agrees with the ordinary intertwining-operator space defined in [[def-cft-intertwining-operator-space]]. All OPE coefficients below are required to lie in these no-log spaces, while analytic continuation of their blocks is allowed to acquire logarithmic terms.

The **locally $C_1$-cofinite full bulk data over $V$** consist of a countable index set $I_c$ and pairs $(M_i,\bar M_i)$ of source-category modules, with $F_c=\bigoplus_{h,\bar h\in\mathbb R}F^c_{h,\bar h}$ satisfying integral-spin support ($F^c_{h,\bar h}=0$ unless $h-\bar h\in\mathbb Z$), common lower bounds on $h,\bar h$, and finite-dimensional cumulative spaces $\bigoplus_{h+\bar h\leq H}F^c_{h,\bar h}$. Require $F_c\cong\bigoplus_{i\in I_c}M_i\otimes\bar M_i$ as a $V\otimes V$-module, the two chiral copies of $V$ to embed in the left and right kernels of the full translation modes, and for each $i,j\in I_c$ a finite set $I_c(i,j)\subset I_c$ containing every intermediate label in the full OPE of $M_i\otimes\bar M_i$ with $M_j\otimes\bar M_j$. For each pair $(i,j)$ the module-valued OPE coefficient lies in
$$\bigoplus_{k\in I_c(i,j)}\mathcal I^0\binom{M_k}{M_i\ M_j}\otimes\mathcal I^0\binom{\bar M_k}{\bar M_i\ \bar M_j}.$$
The two factors are respectively for the left and right copies of $V$. Define the source graded dual by $F_c^\vee=\bigoplus_{h,\bar h}(F^c_{h,\bar h})^*$.

The **locally $C_1$-cofinite boundary data over $V$** consist of a countable index set $I_o$ and a decomposition $F_o=\bigoplus_{h\in\mathbb R}F^o_h\cong\bigoplus_{k\in I_o}N_k$ into source-category modules, with finite-dimensional cumulative spaces $\bigoplus_{h\leq H}F^o_h$, and a finite set $I_o(k,\ell)\subset I_o$ supporting each boundary OPE of $N_k$ with $N_\ell$. Require $L^o(0)|_{F^o_h}=hI$ for every $h\in\mathbb R$, where $L^o(0)$ is the conformal zero mode of the prescribed boundary $V$-action specified below. Thus the displayed boundary decomposition is its conformal eigenspace grading, not an independent regrading of the underlying module. For each pair $(k,\ell)$ the boundary OPE coefficient lies in $\bigoplus_{m\in I_o(k,\ell)}\mathcal I^0\binom{N_m}{N_k\ N_\ell}$. Set $F_o^\vee=\bigoplus_h(F^o_h)^*$. For each $i\in I_c$ the bulk-to-boundary operator restricted to $M_i\otimes\bar M_i$ lies in the finite direct sum $\bigoplus_{j\in B(i)}\mathcal I^0\binom{N_j}{M_i\ \bar M_i}$ for a finite set $B(i)\subset I_o$, where this notation denotes the no-log generalized-module intertwiner space just defined.

The full and boundary algebra axioms are additional premises distinct from local $C_1$-cofinite decompositions. The precise full-vertex-algebra clauses are Moriwaki Definition 3.1 and its energy-momentum-tensor conditions preceding Proposition 3.5; the boundary clauses are equation (3.10) and Definition 3.15. Definitions 3.6 and 3.19 add the finite-support/local-$C_1$ clauses to these algebras; they do not replace the algebra axioms. Adopt the following explicit content.

For the bulk, give a nonzero vacuum $\mathbf1_c\in F^c_{0,0}$ and a linear formal field map
$$Y_c(a;z,\bar z)=\sum_{r,s\in\mathbb R}a_{(r,s)}z^{-r-1}\bar z^{-s-1}.$$
For each fixed pair of states, its exponent support is bounded below in each variable and finite below every bound on the sum of the two exponents; only integer differences of the two exponents occur. For homogeneous $a\in F^c_{h,\bar h}$ its modes obey the grading rule $a_{(r,s)}F^c_{k,\bar k}\subset F^c_{h+k-r-1,\bar h+\bar k-s-1}$. Require $Y_c(\mathbf1_c;z,\bar z)=I$, $Y_c(a;z,\bar z)\mathbf1_c\in F_c[\![z,\bar z]\!]$, and constant term $a$. The coefficient fields use the actual full mode products, not a product inferred from the unrelated R5 field-label space.

For every three homogeneous bulk states $a,b,c$ and every homogeneous restricted-dual functional $u$, require absolute convergence of the product $\langle u,Y_c(a;z_1,\bar z_1)Y_c(b;z_2,\bar z_2)c\rangle$ when $|z_1|>|z_2|>0$, of the iterate $\langle u,Y_c(Y_c(a;z_0,\bar z_0)b;z_2,\bar z_2)c\rangle$ when $|z_2|>|z_0|>0$, and of the reversed product when $|z_2|>|z_1|>0$. They must be the respective channel expansions of one single-valued real-analytic function $\mu_{u;a,b,c}$ on $\{(z_1,z_2)\in\mathbb C^2:z_1,z_2\ne0,\ z_1\ne z_2\}$, with $z_0=z_1-z_2$ in the iterate. This is the three-channel full-algebra axiom, independent of the later bulk-to-boundary bootstrap.

Define $D_l a$ and $D_r a$ as the coefficients of $z$ and $\bar z$ in $Y_c(a;z,\bar z)\mathbf1_c$. Give conformal vectors $\omega_l\in F^c_{2,0}$ and $\omega_r\in F^c_{0,2}$ with $D_r\omega_l=D_l\omega_r=0$. Require $Y_c(\omega_l;z,\bar z)=\sum_nL^l(n)z^{-n-2}$ and $Y_c(\omega_r;z,\bar z)=\sum_nL^r(n)\bar z^{-n-2}$ to give commuting Virasoro actions with the declared central charges, $L^l(-1)=D_l$, $L^r(-1)=D_r$, $L^l(0)|_{F^c_{h,\bar h}}=hI$, and $L^r(0)|_{F^c_{h,\bar h}}=\bar hI$. The lower bounds and finite cumulative weight spaces are the ones required above. These are the energy-momentum data of the full VOA; they are not inferred merely from the decomposition as a $V\otimes V$-module.

For the boundary, give a nonzero $\mathbf1_o\in F^o_0$ and a linear map $Y_o(a,z)=\sum_{r\in\mathbb R}a^o_{(r)}z^{-r-1}$ with $Y_o(a,z)b\in F_o((z^{\mathbb R}))$: for each real bound $H$, only finitely many exponents at most $H$ occur. For the prescribed $V$-module action $a^o_{(n)}$ and $v\in F_o$, require
$$[L^o(-1),Y_o(v,z)]=\partial_zY_o(v,z),\qquad [a^o_{(n)},Y_o(v,z)]=\sum_{k\ge0}\binom nkY_o(a^o_{(k)}v,z)z^{n-k},$$
$$Y_o(a^o_{(n)}v,z)=\sum_{k\ge0}\binom nk\left(a^o_{(n-k)}Y_o(v,z)(-z)^k-Y_o(v,z)a^o_{(k)}(-z)^{n-k}\right).$$
Here $a\in V$, $n\in\mathbb Z$, and coefficientwise lower truncation makes each action meaningful. Require $Y_o(\mathbf1_o,z)=I$, $Y_o(v,z)\mathbf1_o\in F_o[\![z]\!]$ with constant term $v$, and the following ordered associativity axiom. For $a,b,c\in F_o$ and $u\in F_o^\vee$, the product and iterate series converge absolutely on their product and iterate nested domains, respectively, and restrict to one real-analytic function on $\{(x_1,x_2)\in\mathbb R^2:x_1>x_2>0\}$. Its product expression is $\langle u,Y_o(a,x_1)Y_o(b,x_2)c\rangle$, and its iterate expression is $\langle u,Y_o(Y_o(a,x_1-x_2)b,x_2)c\rangle$ on $x_2>x_1-x_2>0$. Complex powers use the declared branches. Boundary commutativity is not an extra axiom.

To type the chiral embeddings, specify injective linear maps $\iota_l,\iota_r:V\to F_c$ and $\iota_o:V\to F_o$ carrying the vacuum to $\mathbf1_c,\mathbf1_c,\mathbf1_o$ and the conformal vector to $\omega_l,\omega_r,\omega_o$, respectively. The left image lies in $\ker D_r$ and the right image in $\ker D_l$. Require the full-field identities $Y_c(\iota_l(a);z,\bar z)=Y^l_{F_c}(a,z)$ and $Y_c(\iota_r(a);z,\bar z)=Y^r_{F_c}(a,\bar z)$, where the right sides are the specified $V$-module actions. Their mode products on the respective images satisfy $\iota_l(a_{(n)}b)=(\iota_l a)_{(n,-1)}\iota_l b$ and $\iota_r(a_{(n)}b)=(\iota_r a)_{(-1,n)}\iota_r b$. On the boundary require $Y_o(\iota_o(a),z)=Y_{F_o}(a,z)$, the specified module field. Thus the mode actions and product-preservation interfaces used below are actually supplied. The two chiral actions commute, and $L^o(n)$ are the conformal-vector modes of the boundary $V$-action.

Write $Y_c,Y_o,Y_b$ for the full, boundary, and bulk-to-boundary OPE maps, respectively; $Y_b$ is specified explicitly below. Thus $a^l_{(n)},a^r_{(n)}$ denote the two chiral mode actions on $F_c$, $a^o_{(n)}$ the boundary mode action on $F_o$, and $L^l(-1),L^r(-1),L^o(-1)$ their translation modes.

More explicitly, the bulk-to-boundary field is a linear map $Y_b(\cdot,z):F_c\to F_o[\![z^{\mathbb R}]\!]$, $v\mapsto\sum_{r\in\mathbb R}B_r(v)z^{-r-1}$, with the source's mode actions $a^l_{(n)},a^r_{(n)}$ on $F_c$ and $a^o_{(n)}$ on $F_o$. It satisfies, for $a\in V$, $v\in F_c$, and $n\in\mathbb Z$,
$$Y_b(L^l(-1)v,z)=\partial_zY_b(v,z),\qquad a^o_{(n)}Y_b(v,z)=Y_b(a^r_{(n)}v,z)+\sum_{k\geq0}\binom nkY_b(a^l_{(k)}v,z)z^{n-k},$$
and
$$Y_b(a^l_{(n)}v,z)=\sum_{k\geq0}\binom nk\left(a^o_{(n-k)}Y_b(v,z)(-z)^k-Y_b(a^r_{(k)}v,z)(-z)^{n-k}\right).$$
The sums are interpreted coefficientwise with the module truncation convention in the source. These are the bulk-boundary covariance identities of Moriwaki, Definition 3.22 and equation (3.15); its Definition 3.28 imposes the finite support $B(i)$. These finite-support requirements are separate from $C_1$-cofiniteness of an individual module.

We fix the source's explicit tree domains. For a binary tree $A$ with $n\geq2$ leaves labelled by distinct coordinates $z_1,\ldots,z_n$, let $V(A)$ and $E(A)$ be its internal vertices and internal edges, and let $t_A$ be the root. For each internal vertex $v$, let $R(v)$ be the label of the rightmost leaf below $v$, and let $L(v)$ be the label of the rightmost leaf in the left child subtree of $v$. Put $z_v=z_{L(v)}-z_{R(v)}$. If $e$ joins a child vertex $d(e)$ to its parent $u(e)$, put $\zeta_e=z_{d(e)}/z_{u(e)}$. Let $r_A$ be the rightmost leaf label, set $z_A=z_{r_A}$ and $x_A=z_{t_A}$, and use the tree-coordinate map $\Psi_A=(z_A,x_A,(\zeta_e)_{e\in E(A)})$. A positive radius tuple $p=(p_e)_{e\in E(A)}$ is $A$-admissible when the full product region in these coordinates, with $z_A\in\mathbb C$, $x_A\in\mathbb C^\times$, and $0<|\zeta_e|<p_e$, maps into the distinct-point configuration space. Let $C_{\rm cut}=\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb R_{\leq0}$ and $D^{\rm cut}_{p_e}=\{\zeta\in C_{\rm cut}:0<|\zeta|<p_e\}$. Define
$$U_A=\bigcup_{p\ {\rm A\text{-}admissible}}\Psi_A^{-1}\!\left(\mathbb C\times C_{\rm cut}\times\prod_{e\in E(A)}D^{\rm cut}_{p_e}\right)\subset X_n(\mathbb C).$$
For the zero-input vacuum tree $\varnothing$, set $X_0(\mathbb C)=\{()\}$ and $U_{\varnothing}=X_0(\mathbb C)$. For the one-input identity tree $\bullet$, set $X_1(\mathbb C)=\mathbb C$ and $U_{\bullet}=\mathbb C$. These cases have no root-scale or internal-edge coordinates. For a two-coloured tree $E$ with $r$ bulk and $s$ boundary leaves, define its doubled chiral tree $\widetilde E$ recursively using its typed vertices. An open leaf is retained. Open multiplication becomes ordinary binary multiplication of the doubled open subtrees. A closed subtree $A$ entering the bulk-to-boundary vertex $\tau$ becomes the binary tree $(A,\bar A)$, where $\bar A$ is a copy of the entire closed subtree with barred leaf labels. Inserting a closed subtree at a closed leaf therefore inserts $A$ and $\bar A$ at the corresponding paired chiral leaves. The coordinate tuple below interleaves the labels $(i,\bar i)$; their order in a doubled tree need not be adjacent. In particular $\tau(\mu_c(1,2))$ doubles to $((1,2),(\bar1,\bar2))$, whereas $\mu_o(\tau(1),\tau(2))$ doubles to $((1,\bar1),(2,\bar2))$; these are distinct channel trees. Set
$$X_n(\mathbb C)=\{(z_1,\ldots,z_n)\in\mathbb C^n:z_i\ne z_j\text{ for }i\ne j\},\qquad X_{r,s}(\mathbb H)=\{(z_1,\ldots,z_r,x_1,\ldots,x_s):\operatorname{Im}z_i>0,\ x_j\in\mathbb R,\ z_i\ne z_k,\ x_j\ne x_\ell,\ z_i\ne x_j\},$$
$$\Phi(z_1,\ldots,z_r,x_1,\ldots,x_s)=(z_1,\bar z_1,\ldots,z_r,\bar z_r,x_1,\ldots,x_s),\qquad \widetilde U_E=U_{\widetilde E},\qquad U_E=\Phi^{-1}(\widetilde U_E)\cap X_{r,s}(\mathbb H).$$
For a bulk tree $A_c$ with $r$ bulk leaves, use its ordinary nested domain $U_{A_c}\subset X_r(\mathbb C)$ in each chiral coordinate copy and define
$$\Phi_c(z_1,\ldots,z_r)=(z_1,\ldots,z_r;\bar z_1,\ldots,\bar z_r),\qquad U^c_{A_c}=\Phi_c^{-1}(U_{A_c}\times U_{A_c})\subset X_r(\mathbb C).$$
Thus the holomorphic and antiholomorphic bulk OPE series live on $U_{A_c}\times U_{A_c}$, and their restriction is real analytic on $U^c_{A_c}$. For zero or one bulk leaf the corresponding closed OPE is the declared vacuum or state-field map on $U^c_{\varnothing}=\{()\}$ or $U^c_{\bullet}=\mathbb C$, respectively, with no internal projection sum. The same pullback convention gives $U_E=\{()\}$ for the zero-input open vacuum tree and $U_E=\mathbb R$ for the single open leaf; a single bulk leaf entering a bulk-to-boundary vertex still doubles to two chiral leaves and uses the two-leaf domain. Branches of all real powers use the principal logarithm on $C_{\rm cut}$.

The genus-zero bulk-boundary bootstrap data are these five channel conditions:

1. The bulk vacuum maps to the boundary vacuum.
2. For $a_1,a_2\in F_c$, a restricted-dual functional $u\in F_o^\vee$, and $z_1,z_2\in\mathbb H$ with $\operatorname{Re}z_1>\operatorname{Re}z_2$, the two series
$$\left\langle u,e^{\bar z_2L^o(-1)}Y_o\!\left(Y_b(a_1,z_1-\bar z_1),\bar z_1-\bar z_2\right)Y_b(a_2,z_2-\bar z_2)\right\rangle$$
and
$$\left\langle u,e^{\bar z_2L^o(-1)}Y_b\!\left(Y_c(a_1,z_1-z_2,\bar z_1-\bar z_2)a_2,z_2-\bar z_2\right)\right\rangle$$
are absolutely convergent respectively when
$$\frac{|z_1-\bar z_1|+|z_2-\bar z_2|}{|\bar z_1-\bar z_2|}<1,\qquad \frac{|z_1-z_2|+|\bar z_1-\bar z_2|}{|z_2-\bar z_2|}<1,$$
and are restrictions of one single-valued real-analytic function on $X_{2,0}(\mathbb H)$.
3. For $a\in F_c$, $b\in F_o$, $u\in F_o^\vee$, $z\in\mathbb H$, and $x\in\mathbb R$, the series
$$\left\langle u,e^{xL^o(-1)}Y_o\!\left(Y_b(a,z-\bar z),\bar z-x\right)b\right\rangle,\qquad \left\langle u,e^{\bar zL^o(-1)}Y_o\!\left(b,x-\bar z\right)Y_b(a,z-\bar z)\right\rangle$$
are absolutely convergent respectively on $\operatorname{Re}z>x$, $|z-\bar z|<|\bar z-x|$ and on $x>\operatorname{Re}z$, $|z-\bar z|<|x-\bar z|$, and are restrictions of one single-valued real-analytic function on $X_{1,1}(\mathbb H)$.

The configuration space is $X_{r,s}(\mathbb H)=\{(z_1,\ldots,z_r,x_1,\ldots,x_s):\operatorname{Im}z_i>0,\ x_j\in\mathbb R,\text{ and all displayed points are distinct}\}$; when open leaves are ordered, use the chamber $x_1>\cdots>x_s$. The five equations counted by the source are the vacuum identity and the two ordered expansions in each of conditions 2 and 3. This definition records the exact analytic and finite-support package; it does not assert existence of a physical CFT or a path-integral measure.

## Remarks

The quoted C1 quotient, dual finite generation, finite intermediate-label support, boundary finite support, and finite bulk-to-boundary support are distinct clauses. The R2 quotient definition supplies only the first. The no-log requirement is imposed inside the logarithmic intertwiner spaces for generalized modules; this keeps the tree-block and theorem types aligned without treating generalized modules as ordinary. In particular, the higher-genus sewing and the annulus Cardy condition are not included.

Moriwaki's Theorem 3.30 uses the analytic convergence theorem for iterated intertwining-operator products and the two-colored parenthesized permutation-braid action. Those results are not included in this definition; their local proof status is recorded in the consuming theorem. This item specifies the data and bootstrap interfaces, not the convergence theorem.
