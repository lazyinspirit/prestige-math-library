## Statement

Fix an integer $p\ge2$. Let $L=\mathbb Z\alpha$ with $\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle=2p$, and use the explicit oscillator, trivial-cocycle and square-zero lattice conventions below. Let $W(p)\subset V_L$ be the vertex subalgebra generated by $\omega=(\alpha_{-1}^2/(4p)+(p-1)\alpha_{-2}/(2p))\mathbf1$, $F=e^{-\alpha}$, $H=QF$, and $E=Q^2F$, where $Q=(e^\alpha)_{(0)}$. Set $v=e^{-\alpha/p}$ and
$$\mathcal M=V_{L+\alpha/2}\oplus V_{L+\alpha/2-\alpha/p},\qquad \widetilde Y(a,z)=Y_{\mathcal M}(a,z)+\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}n z^{-n}Y_{\mathcal M}(v_{(n)}a,z).$$
Then these fields give an actual admissible $W(p)$-module action on $\mathcal M$. Its cyclic submodule $P_+^{p-1}=W(p)e^{\alpha/2}$ is grading-restricted generalized and logarithmic. Writing $h=(2-p)/4$, its lowest generalized conformal-weight space has basis $u=e^{\alpha/2}$, $w=e^{\alpha/2-\alpha/p}$ with $\widetilde L(0)u=hu+w$ and $\widetilde L(0)w=hw$. The cyclic submodule $M_1=W(p)e^{-\alpha/2}$ lies in $P_+^{p-1}$ and fits into a nonsplit short exact sequence of admissible $W(p)$-modules
$$0\longrightarrow\Lambda(p-1)\longrightarrow M_1\longrightarrow\Pi(1)\longrightarrow0.$$
Here the endpoint modules use the undeformed lattice actions: $\Lambda(p-1)=W(p)e^{\alpha/2-\alpha/p}$ in $V_{L+\alpha/2-\alpha/p}$ and $\Pi(1)=W(p)e^{-\alpha/2}$ in $V_{L+\alpha/2}$. The injection identifies $\Lambda(p-1)$ with the corresponding cyclic submodule in the second summand of the deformed module. The quotient map is the restriction to $M_1$ of projection onto the first summand, with image $\Pi(1)$. All three terms use the common admissible grading $d=\operatorname{wt}-h$ inherited from the two lattice summands; in particular $\Pi(1)$ first occurs in degree $p-1$, without resetting its bottom degree to zero. The operator $\widetilde L(0)$ is semisimple on $M_1$; the Jordan block asserted above is in $P_+^{p-1}$.

## Proof

1.1 Between the two data models in [F1], send each polynomial charge vector $f e^{t\alpha}$ in a specified sector to the vector with the same polynomial, charge and sector. This is a linear isomorphism on the algebra and module spaces. The oscillator multiplications and derivatives, charge shifts and their zero products, zero-mode powers, scalar cocycle $1$, and every summand of the finite normal-order rule agree. Thus it intertwines every coefficient of the state and module fields. It fixes the displayed conformal state and $v$, intertwines $Q$, and carries $F,H,E$ and their generated vertex subalgebra to the corresponding original ones. Therefore [F5]–[F6] apply to exactly the original algebra and ordinary lattice endpoints in the Statement. Apply [F2] to the actual data [F1]. It supplies the complete Jacobi action, admissible grading and finite generalized conformal-weight spaces. Its degree-zero space in $P_+$ is exactly $\mathbb C e^{\alpha/2}\oplus\mathbb Cw$, with conformal zero-mode action $(\widetilde L(0)-h)e^{\alpha/2}=w$ and $(\widetilde L(0)-h)w=0$. This is a nonzero Jordan block, so $P_+$ is logarithmic in [F8]. The same supplier proves that the actual $M_1$ is a submodule of $P_+$ and its conformal zero mode is semisimple. [given, F1, F2, F5, F6, F8]

1.2 By [F3], $N_2\subset M_1$ and projection onto the first summand gives a surjective graded homomorphism $\pi:M_1\to\Pi(1)$ with kernel $K=M_1\cap B_0$. Put $R=M_1/N_2$ and $J=K/N_2$. The induced sequence $0\to J\to R\to\Pi(1)\to0$ is exact in every inherited degree, and $J$ embeds as a submodule of the ordinary $B_0/N_2$. Both $B_0/N_2$ and $\Pi(1)$ have no degrees below $N$ by [F5], so exactness gives $R(d)=0$ for $d<N$. [F3, F5, F8, algebra]

2.1 The actual quotient $R$ is cyclic on $\bar t$ in degree $N$. Reindex $R(N+r)$ by $r\ge0$ and apply [F7], using [F6] for actual strong generation. Thus all of $R(N)$ is generated by the four indicated zero modes on $\bar t$. By [F4] that generated subspace is exactly the two-dimensional span of $\bar t,\bar z$: writing $A=\binom{3p-2}{p-1}\ne0$, its actions are $o(F)\bar t=0$, $o(F)\bar z=A\bar t$, $o(H)\bar t=-A\bar t$, $o(H)\bar z=A\bar z$, $o(E)\bar t=-2A\bar z$, $o(E)\bar z=0$, and $o(\omega)=(h+N)I$. Therefore $\dim R(N)=2$. [F4, F6, F7, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Simplicity of $B_0/N_2$ in [F5] says that its submodule $J$ is zero or the whole module. In the latter case $\dim J(N)=2$. The degree-$N$ part of the exact sequence in step 1.2 is a short exact sequence of finite-dimensional vector spaces, and [F5] gives $\dim\Pi(1)(N)=2$. It would force $\dim R(N)=2+2=4$, contradicting step 2.1. Hence $J=0$ and $K=N_2$. This proves exactly the kernel equality, rather than only the inclusion of the cyclic endpoint in that kernel. [F5, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 It follows that the original sequence $0\to\Lambda(p-1)=N_2\to M_1\xrightarrow{\pi}\Pi(1)\to0$ is short exact, with the actual simple endpoint identifications from [F5]. This is precisely the projection studied in [F3], which has no admissible section; consequently the sequence is nonsplit. The proof there uses the explicit nonzero value $\widetilde E_{(3p-3)}t=C_pw$ and the vanishing of that mode on every possible second-sector correction to a degree-$N$ lift, and requires no Jordan block on $M_1$. All maps retain the common grading, so the quotient's bottom stays in degree $N$. Together with step 1.1 this proves every assertion of the Statement with the stated module conventions. [F2, F3, F5, F8, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
