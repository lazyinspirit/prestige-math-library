## Statement

Assume AC. For an independent sequence of random elements and its tail $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal T$, every $A\in\mathcal T$ has probability zero or one.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The hypotheses, objects, and conventions in the Statement.

[F1] [[def-tail-sigma-algebra-of-a-sequence]] places every tail event in the full sequence $\sigma$-algebra.

[F2] [[lem-tail-events-are-independent-of-every-finite-initial-sigma-algebra]] makes a tail event independent of each finite initial $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal H_n$.

[F3] [[lem-conditioning-a-known-variable-and-an-independent-variable]] identifies the corresponding conditional expectation with a constant, and with the variable itself when it is measurable.

[F4] [[thm-levy-upward-convergence-of-conditional-expectations]] gives the limiting conditional expectation along $\mathcal H_n$.

[F5] [[def-axiom-of-choice]] is inherited from conditional-expectation existence and the convergence theorem.

## Proof

1.1 Fix $A\in\mathcal T$ and let $\mathcal H_n$ be generated by the first $n$ random elements. F2, F3 give $$\mathbb E[1_A\mid\mathcal H_n]=\mathbb P(A)\quad\text{a.s. for every }n.$$ [F2, F3]

2.1 The increasing union of the $\mathcal H_n$ generates the $\sigma$-algebra of the entire sequence. By F1, $A$ belongs to that $\sigma$-algebra. F4 therefore says the constants in step 1.1 converge almost surely to $$\mathbb E[1_A\mid\sigma(\bigcup_n\mathcal H_n)]=1_A,$$ where the last identity is F3's known-variable clause. Hence $1_A=\mathbb P(A)$ almost surely. [F1, F3, F4]

3.1 An indicator that is almost surely the constant $p=\mathbb P(A)$ can take only $0$ or $1$ on a nonnull set, so $p\in\{0,1\}$. This proof does not consume the already-published zero-one theorem that it recovers. AC has only the dependence in F5. [F5, step 2.1] ∎
