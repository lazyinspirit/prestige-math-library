## Definition

Let $D$ be a finite marked oriented tangle diagram, with at least one mark
on every internal edge and every circle, and any finite number of marks
(possibly none) on boundary edges. Label all marks and boundary points by
variables $x_1,\dots,x_m$. For the braid-homology construction specialize to
a braid diagram, a generic projection of the closure of a clockwise-oriented
braid ([[def-braid-group-by-the-artin-presentation]],
[[def-closure-of-a-geometric-braid]]); crossings are resolved as in
[[def-positive-and-negative-khovanov-rozansky-crossing-complexes]].

Define
$$C(D):=\bigotimes_{p\text{ crossing}}C_p\otimes\bigotimes_{c\text{ arc}}C_c,$$
the tensor product over the polynomial ring generated by $a$ and all labels,
then restricted to the ring generated by $a$ and the boundary labels as in
[[def-factorization-of-a-marked-moy-graph]], viewed as a complex of objects of $\mathrm{hmf}_w$ with
$w=a\sum_p\epsilon_px_p$ over the boundary points; for a closed braid diagram
$w=0$. For a nonempty closed braid diagram $D$, let $CH^j(D)$ be the direct sum
of the two inner parity cohomologies of $C^j(D)$, with their parity labels
forgotten. It is a bigraded $\mathbb Q$-vector space on which $a$ acts trivially; the differential $\partial$ of $C(D)$ induces a differential on
$CH(D)$, and the cohomology $H(D)=\bigoplus_{j,k,l}H^j_{k,l}(D)$ of the
resulting complex is a triply graded $\mathbb Q$-vector space (cohomological
degree $j$, first bigrading $k$ and second bigrading $l$). Its Euler
characteristic is
$$\langle D\rangle:=\sum_{j,k,l}(-1)^jt^kq^l\dim_{\mathbb Q}H^j_{k,l}(D).$$

For the zero-strand empty diagram all tensor products are empty: $C(\varnothing)=\mathbb Q[a]$ in outer degree $0$ and even parity, with zero differential. Its cohomology is $\mathbb Q[a]$, on which $a$ acts by multiplication, not trivially. Since $\deg a=(2,0)$, its raw integer-graded Euler series is $\langle\varnothing\rangle=(1-t^2)^{-1}$. This tensor-unit boundary case is distinct from the nonempty-link normalization proved later.

Caveats: the link-invariance results below use braid diagrams only (the oriented IIb
move is not used); the marking data are auxiliary, but independence of the
marking is proved later on this page; no independence of the diagram is
asserted here; the trigrading is kept separate throughout, and $t$ records the
first bigrading while $q$ records the second, in the source's convention.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** a marked braid diagram $D$ with its crossings, arcs and labels, the local factorizations $C_c$ and the crossing complexes $C_p$, and the tensor product $C(D)$ over the shared polynomial ring.

[F1] Each crossing complex is a two-term complex of matrix factorizations with potential $w=a(x_1+x_2-x_3-x_4)$, the differential $\chi_0$ or $\chi_1$ having bidegree $(0,0)$ on the shifted terms ([[def-positive-and-negative-khovanov-rozansky-crossing-complexes]]).

[F2] Each local factorization is an object of $\mathrm{hmf}_w$ whose differential squares to its own potential: $a(x_1-x_2)$ for an arc with endpoint labels $x_1,x_2$, $a(x_1+x_2-x_3-x_4)$ for a wide edge, and the potential of a marked graph is $a\sum_p\epsilon_px_p$ over its boundary points, vanishing for closed graphs; the empty graph tensor unit is $\mathbb Q[a]$ with zero differential ([[def-factorization-of-a-marked-moy-graph]]).

[F3] For a nonempty closed graph, row reduction extracts a row $(a,0)$. After restricting scalars to $\mathbb Q$, its polynomial splitting reduces cohomology to the remaining Koszul complex at $a=0$, retaining its odd parity and internal shift; $a$ acts trivially on that cohomology ([[lem-koszul-row-operations-and-variable-exclusion-preserve-factorization-homotopy-type]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct assembly of the tensor product and termwise reduction to the Koszul standard form.

1.1 *The tensor product is a complex with the stated potential.* The differential of $C(D)$ is the sum, with the Koszul signs of the totalization, of the differentials of the factors $C_p$ and $C_c$. Each $C_p$ is a two-term complex by [F1] and each $C_c$ is a single factorization by [F2], so the totalized differential satisfies $\partial^2=0$ strictly, and $C(D)$ is an object of $K(\mathrm{hmf}_w)$. The square of the internal differential of a tensor product accumulates the individual potentials, so that of $C(D)$ is $a\sum_p\epsilon_px_p$ over the boundary points: every internal label occurs in exactly two local factors with opposite signs and cancels, exactly as for a marked graph in [F2]. For a closed braid diagram there are no boundary points and the potential is $w=0$, so $C(D)$ is a genuine complex of bigraded $\mathbb Q[a]$-modules. [F1, F2, algebra]

1.2 *Termwise cohomology and trivial $a$-action.* Every resolution of the nonempty closed braid is a nonempty closed marked graph with at least one linear row: a crossingless circle has a mark and hence an arc factor, while each resolved crossing contributes arc or wide-edge factors. The row reduction of [F3] turns its first linear row into $(a,0)$ and leaves all other linear and quadratic rows with first entry zero. The explicit polynomial splitting of that first row over $\mathbb Q$ reduces its cohomology to the specialization $a=0$ of the remaining Koszul complex, with the first row's parity and internal shift retained. Thus $a$ acts trivially on every $CH^j(D)$ and these are bigraded $\mathbb Q$-vector spaces. This reduction does not imply finite rank over $\mathbb Q[a]$; the one-mark circle already leaves the polynomial variable $x$. Each resolution nevertheless has finite-dimensional pieces in each bigrading, since it uses finitely many polynomial variables of positive degrees and finitely many shifted Koszul terms. [F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 *The empty boundary case.* For the zero-strand diagram there are no crossings or arc factors. The empty tensor is the graph tensor unit $\mathbb Q[a]$ of [F2], in degree $0$ with zero differential. Thus $CH^0=H^0=\mathbb Q[a]$ and all other outer degrees vanish. Its homogeneous monomials $a^d$ have bigrading $(2d,0)$, so each fixed bidegree is finite and the raw Euler series is $\sum_{d\ge0}t^{2d}=(1-t^2)^{-1}$. Multiplication by $a$ is nonzero, as asserted separately. [F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 *The induced differential and the trigraded cohomology.* The differential $\partial$ of $C(D)$ is a sum of morphisms $\chi_0,\chi_1$ of bidegree $(0,0)$ between factorizations, so it commutes with the internal differentials of the terms, hence maps cycles to cycles and boundaries to boundaries in each term and induces a map $\partial\colon CH^j(D)\to CH^{j+1}(D)$. Since $\partial^2=0$ on $C(D)$ by step 1.1, the induced maps satisfy $\partial^2=0$ on $CH(D)$, and since they preserve the bigrading, the cohomology $H(D)=\bigoplus_{j,k,l}H^j_{k,l}(D)$ is triply graded with $j$ the cohomological degree and $k,l$ the two bigrading degrees; the Euler characteristic $\langle D\rangle=\sum_{j,k,l}(-1)^jt^kq^l\dim_{\mathbb Q}H^j_{k,l}(D)$ is therefore defined. This is the integer-graded construction of Khovanov-Rozansky II, section 1; invariance is established by later items. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1] ∎
