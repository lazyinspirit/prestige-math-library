## Definition

Let $W$ be a five-dimensional complex vector space with ordered basis $e_1,\ldots,e_5$ and dual coframe $e^1,\ldots,e^5$. On $V=W\oplus W^*$ set

$$q(x+\xi)=\xi(x),\qquad B(x+\xi,y+\eta)=\frac12(\eta(x)+\xi(y)),$$

so $q(v)=B(v,v)$ and $q$ is nondegenerate and split. The complex Clifford algebra $\mathrm{Cl}(V,q)$ is generated by $V$ with relation $c(v)c(v')+c(v')c(v)=2B(v,v')\,1$; in particular $c(v)^2=q(v)\,1$. On $S=\Lambda^\bullet W^*$ use

$$c(x+\xi)\omega=\iota_x\omega+\xi\wedge\omega.$$

Write $S^+=\Lambda^{\mathrm{even}}W^*$ and $S^-=\Lambda^{\mathrm{odd}}W^*$. For a homogeneous $k$-form put $\tau(\omega)=(-1)^{k(k-1)/2}\omega$, extend $\tau$ linearly, and let $[\omega]_{\mathrm{vol}}$ mean the coefficient of $\mathrm{vol}=e^1\wedge\cdots\wedge e^5$. The invariant pairing between $S^-$ and $S^+$ and the chiral gamma bilinear are fixed by

$$\langle s_-,t_+\rangle=[\tau(s_-)\wedge t_+]_{\mathrm{vol}},\qquad \gamma(s_+,t_+)(v)=\langle c(v)s_+,t_+\rangle\quad(v\in V).$$

The nondegenerate form $B$ identifies $V$ with $V^*$, so the components of $\gamma$ in a $B$-orthonormal basis are the symmetric ten-dimensional matrices $\gamma^m_{\alpha\beta}$ in the convention used here; the displayed pairing fixes their overall normalization. Define $\operatorname{Spin}(V,q)$ as the subgroup of $\mathrm{Cl}^0(V,q)^\times$ generated by even products of vectors $v$ with $q(v)=1$. It acts on $S^+$ by left Clifford multiplication and on $V$ by $g\cdot v=gvg^{-1}$. This is the complex group $\operatorname{Spin}(10,\mathbb C)$; it preserves $q$, the spinor pairing, and $\gamma$. A nonzero $\lambda\in S^+$ is pure when

$$\operatorname{Ann}(\lambda)=\{v\in V:c(v)\lambda=0\}$$

has dimension five, the maximal possible dimension of an isotropic subspace. Equivalently, $\gamma(\lambda,\lambda)(v)=0$ for every $v\in V$, or $\lambda\gamma^m\lambda=0$ in the stated component convention.

The equivalence and the complete scalar-component chart can be checked directly. Write $\lambda=\rho+\eta+\chi$ with $\eta\in\Lambda^2W^*$, $\chi\in\Lambda^4W^*$ and $\rho\in\mathbb C$. If $\rho\ne0$, the $W^*$ components of $\gamma(\lambda,\lambda)=0$ are $2\rho\,\xi\wedge\chi-\xi\wedge\eta\wedge\eta=0$ for every $\xi\in W^*$, hence $\chi=\eta\wedge\eta/(2\rho)$. The $W$ components then vanish because $\eta^3=0$ in dimension five. This is exactly $\lambda=\rho\exp(\eta/\rho)$; with $\eta/\rho=\frac12u_{ab}e^a\wedge e^b$ and summation over $a,b$, the chart is

$$\lambda=\rho\exp\!\left(\frac12u_{ab}e^a\wedge e^b\right),\qquad \rho\in\mathbb C^*,\qquad u_{ab}=-u_{ba}.$$

There are ten independent coordinates $u_{ab}$ with $a<b$. With $B_2=\frac12u_{ab}e^a\wedge e^b$, the annihilator is exactly $\{x-\iota_x B_2:x\in W\}$, spanned by $e_a-u_{ab}e^b$ acting as $\iota_{e_a}-u_{ab}e^b\wedge$. Conversely, if $\rho\ne0$ and $\dim\operatorname{Ann}(\lambda)=5$, projection of the annihilator to $W$ is injective (a pure covector would act on the scalar term as $\rho\xi$), hence is an isomorphism. Its isotropic graph has the form $x-\iota_x B_2$ for a unique two-form $B_2$; the degree-one and degree-three components of $c(x-\iota_xB_2)\lambda=0$ give $B_2=\eta/\rho$ and $\chi=\eta^2/(2\rho)$. Thus the displayed coordinates exhaust the whole domain $\rho\ne0$, rather than merely providing pure points.

For each even subset $I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,5\}$ let $\lambda_I$ be the coefficient of $e^I$ in $S^+$ and put $U_I=\{\lambda\in C:\lambda_I\ne0\}$, where $C$ denotes the punctured pure-spinor cone. Every $\lambda\in C$ lies in some $U_I$ because a nonzero vector in $S^+$ has a nonzero component. These are Spin-translated domains: for $a=1,\ldots,5$, $v_a=e_a+e^a$ has $q(v_a)=1$, and $c(v_a)=\iota_{e_a}+e^a\wedge$ toggles whether $a$ occurs in a basis wedge $e^J$. For even $I=\{i_1,\ldots,i_{2k}\}$ the even Clifford product $g_I=v_{i_1}\cdots v_{i_{2k}}$ belongs to $\operatorname{Spin}(V,q)$, sends each basis line $\mathbb C e^J$ to $\mathbb C e^{J\mathbin\triangle I}$, and sends $1$ to $\pm e^I$. Consequently the $I$-component of $g_I\lambda$ is $\pm\lambda_\varnothing$, so $U_I=g_IU_\varnothing$. On $U_I\cap U_J$ the chart change is the spin action of $g_J^{-1}g_I$, followed by division by its nonzero scalar component; the four-form coordinates are fixed by the same quadratic relation above. This proves the sixteen-domain cover and the claimed translated chart domains. The same scalar-chart calculation now proves the equivalence globally: for any nonzero spinor, a nonzero component is moved to the scalar component by a suitable $g_I^{-1}$; the Spin action preserves both $\gamma(\lambda,\lambda)=0$ and $\dim\operatorname{Ann}(\lambda)$. The coordinates agree with Nekrasov's sixteen nonzero-component charts (hep-th/0511008, §§3.3.1–3.3.2, equations (3.34)–(3.44)); his Euclidean $SO(10)$ module is complexified, which is valid here because all nondegenerate ten-dimensional complex quadratic spaces are isometric.

On the scalar chart fix the ordering $a<b$ of the ten $du_{ab}$ and set

$$\Omega_\varnothing=\rho^7\,d\rho\wedge\bigwedge_{a<b}du_{ab}.$$

On $U_I$ let $(\rho_I,u^I_{ab})$ be the scalar and two-form coordinates of $g_I^{-1}\lambda$, and use the spin-translated expression $\Omega_I=\rho_I^7d\rho_I\wedge\bigwedge_{a<b}du^I_{ab}$ with the same ordering. These are the local volume expressions whose transition identity is proved by [[lem-st-pure-spinor-cone-volume-current-cocycle]]; Nekrasov's nowhere-zero invariant form is $e^{8\varphi}d\varphi\wedge\bigwedge_{a<b}du_{ab}$ with $\rho=e^\varphi$, equal to the displayed expression. This is a holomorphic top form on the punctured cone only; it makes no assertion at the apex or about an integration boundary.

The ambient conjugate $w\in(S^+)^*$ restricts to a cotangent covector on $C$. If $F_v(\lambda)=\frac12\gamma(\lambda,\lambda)(v)$, then $dF_v|_\lambda$ is conormal to $C$. On $U_\varnothing$ the five independent components $2\rho\chi-\eta^2=0$ have derivative $2\rho\,\mathrm{Id}$ in the $\chi$ directions, so the covectors $dF_v|_\lambda$ span the full five-dimensional conormal space; Spin translates give the same fact on every $U_I$. Thus for a fixed basis $v_1,\ldots,v_{10}$ of $V$, $w$ and $w+\sum_{m=1}^{10}\Lambda^m dF_{v_m}|_\lambda$ represent the same cotangent vector; in components this is $w_\alpha\sim w_\alpha+(\gamma_m\lambda)_\alpha\Lambda^m$. Locally write the cone coordinates as $q^i=(\rho,u_{ab})$, $i=1,\ldots,11$, with cotangent coordinates $p_i$ and canonical one-form $p_i\,dq^i$; in the source convention $p_i=w_i$. For the local holomorphic $\beta\gamma$ OPE used in this pair, set $\gamma^i=q^i$, $\beta_i=p_i=w_i$, take $\alpha'\in\mathbb C^\times$ as the formal OPE normalization, and use $\beta_i(z)\gamma^j(w)\sim-\alpha'\delta_i{}^j/(z-w)$ and the current $j_V=-:\!\beta_iV^i\!:$; thus a source insertion $+w_iV^i$ is $-j_V$. The dimensional interpretation of $\alpha'$ is specified by any consuming physical model. No sixteen-component unconstrained-pair measure is part of this definition.

For a finite graded coordinate space with coordinates $x^I$ of parity $\epsilon_I$ and ghost number $g_I$, introduce antifields $x_I^*$ of parity $\epsilon_I+1$ and ghost number $-g_I-1$. Use the canonical bracket of [[def-qftx-bv-antibracket-master-action]]. A compatible odd Laplacian is an odd operator $\Delta$ with $\Delta1=0$, $\Delta^2=0$, and

$$\Delta(FG)=\Delta F\,G+(-1)^{|F|}F\Delta G+(-1)^{|F|}(F,G).$$

Let $\rho_0$ be the coordinate Berezinian. In these Darboux coordinates, $\Delta_0$ denotes the canonical coordinate BV Laplacian associated with the bracket and $\rho_0$; explicitly,

$$\Delta_0F=\sum_I(-1)^{\epsilon_I}\frac{\partial_l}{\partial x^I}\left(\frac{\partial_lF}{\partial x_I^*}\right).$$

Thus $\Delta_0f=0$ for every function $f$ independent of all antifields. Let $\mu(x)$ be a nowhere-zero even density factor, independent of antifields and admitting a chosen logarithm on the chart. The BV Berezinian is $\rho_{\mathrm{BV}}=\mu^2\rho_0$, its semidensity is locally a function times $\sqrt{\rho_0}$ with the square-root Berezinian transition law, and its compatible operator is $\Delta_\rho=\Delta_0+(\log\mu,-)$. These are finite chart data, not an infinite-dimensional path-integral measure.

A finite odd-symplectic split into low and high dual pairs uses the product reference semidensity. A high integration cycle is a specified maximal isotropic subspace or smooth deformation on which the formal or convergent integral is defined, together with its contour/continuation and determinant branch. BV pushforward is integration of the semidensity over that cycle. Its boundary remainder is the actual integral of $\Delta_H$ of the integrand and is not set to zero by definition. For metric-dependent spectral subspaces, the chosen odd-symplectic transport pulls them back to fixed Darboux coordinates before differentiating. This definition makes no global cone-cycle or no-boundary assertion.
