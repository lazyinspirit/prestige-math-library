## Statement

Let $n\ge1$, let $J\subseteq\mathbb R$ be an open interval, let $O_q,O_Q\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ be
nonempty open sets ([[def-metric-topology]]), and let
$S:J\times O_q\times O_Q\to\mathbb R$ be of class $C^3$ with
$\det S_{qQ}(t,q,Q)\ne0$ for every $(t,q,Q)$ ([[def-ck-euclidean-maps-and-diffeomorphisms]]). For
each $t\in J$ let $S_t:=S(t,\cdot,\cdot)$ and let $C_t$ be the first-kind map generated by $S_t$
([[def-cm-generating-function-of-the-first-and-second-kind]]). Let
$H:J\times V\to\mathbb R$ be $C^2$ on a phase domain $V\subseteq\mathbb R^{2n}$ such that $(q,S_q(t,q,Q))\in V$ for every $(t,q,Q)\in J\times O_q\times O_Q$; thus every Hamiltonian argument in the identity below is in its declared domain. Then:

1. for every $t\in J$ the map $C_t$ is a canonical $C^2$ local diffeomorphism
   ([[prop-cm-generating-functions-yield-canonical-maps]]);
2. on $J\times O_q\times O_Q$, with $p:=S_q(t,q,Q)$, $P:=-S_Q(t,q,Q)$ and
   $K(t,q,Q):=H\bigl(t,q,S_q(t,q,Q)\bigr)+S_t(t,q,Q)$, the one-form identity
   $$p\,dq-H(t,q,p)\,dt=P\,dQ-K\,dt+dS$$
   holds identically: on the region where both descriptions are defined, the Poincare-Cartan
   one-forms of $H$ and of $K=H+S_t$ differ by the exact form $dS$, with all quantities read as
   functions of $(t,q,Q)$.

The words canonical and the finite-regularity form operations here use [[def-cm-finite-regularity-canonical-calculus]], with the displayed $C^2$ maps; smoothness is not inferred.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The time-dependent first-kind data $S$, the Hamiltonians $H$ and the functions $t\mapsto C_t$ they generate.

[F1] Fixed-time canonicity ([[prop-cm-generating-functions-yield-canonical-maps]]): for each $t$, $C_t$ is a $C^2$ local diffeomorphism satisfying $C_t^*(dQ\wedge dP)=dq\wedge dp$.

[F2] Differential of $S$: since $S$ is $C^3$, the coordinate formula for the exterior derivative gives $dS=S_t\,dt+\sum_iS_{q^i}dq^i+\sum_iS_{Q^i}dQ^i$ on $J\times O_q\times O_Q$ ([[def-cm-finite-regularity-canonical-calculus]], [[def-directional-and-partial-derivatives]]).

[F3] The defining relations of the first-kind generating function ([[def-cm-generating-function-of-the-first-and-second-kind]]): $p=S_q(t,q,Q)$ and $P=-S_Q(t,q,Q)$, with $(q,p)$ the old and $(Q,P)$ the new canonical coordinates.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; read the fixed-time canonicity from the preceding proposition and substitute the defining relations into $dS$.

1.1 Clause 1 is exactly [F1], applied at each fixed $t$. [F1]

2.1 By [F2] and [F3], $dS=S_t\,dt+p\,dq-P\,dQ$ on $J\times O_q\times O_Q$; rearranging gives $p\,dq-H\,dt=P\,dQ-(H+S_t)\,dt+dS$ with $H=H(t,q,p)$ evaluated at $p=S_q$, which is the identity of clause 2 with $K=H+S_t$. [F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The identity of step 2.1 exhibits the asserted Hamiltonian shift: on the overlap of the two descriptions the Poincare-Cartan one-forms $p\,dq-H\,dt$ and $P\,dQ-K\,dt$ of $H$ and of $K=H+S_t$ differ by the exact form $dS$, with all quantities read as functions of $(t,q,Q)$; the fixed-time maps $C_t$ are canonical by step 1.1. [step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

- The sign in $K=H+S_t$ is fixed by the convention $P=-S_Q$ of the first-kind generating function and by the identity $p\,dq-H\,dt=P\,dQ-K\,dt+dS$; the opposite sign convention for the generating function flips the sign of $S_t$ together with that of $S$.
- The statement is local in $(q,Q)$ and in $t$; it does not assert propagation of the generating function along the flow, which would be the Hamilton-Jacobi equation, treated in [[def-cm-hamilton-jacobi-equation-and-complete-integral]].
