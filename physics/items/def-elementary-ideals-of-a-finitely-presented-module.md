## Definition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring ([[def-commutative-ring]]) and let $M$ be a
finitely presented $R$-module ([[def-finitely-presented-module-and-algebra]]),
so that there are $m,n\in\mathbb N$ and a presentation

$$R^m\xrightarrow{\ A\ }R^n\xrightarrow{\ \beta\ }M\longrightarrow0 .$$

Fix the standard bases $e_1,\dots,e_m$ of $R^m$ and $f_1,\dots,f_n$ of $R^n$
([[def-finitely-presented-module-and-algebra]]) and let $A=(a_{ij})$ be the
$n\times m$ matrix of the $R$-linear map $\alpha$ with respect to them: the
$j$-th column of $A$ is the coordinate vector of $\alpha(e_j)$, so
$\alpha(e_j)=\sum_{i=1}^n a_{ij}f_i$. Such a matrix is called a **presentation
matrix** of $M$ (for the chosen presentation). A **minor** of $A$ of size
$r\times r$ is the determinant $\det(a_{i_sj_t})_{s,t=1}^r$
([[def-determinant-of-a-square-matrix]]) of the square submatrix obtained by
choosing rows $i_1<\dots<i_r$ and columns $j_1<\dots<j_r$; determinants of
$1\times1$ and $0\times0$ submatrices are allowed, with the empty determinant
equal to $1$.

For $k\ge0$ the **$k$-th elementary ideal** $E_k(M)$ of the presented module $M$
is the ideal of $R$ generated by the determinants of **all**
$(n-k)\times(n-k)$ minors of $A$ ([[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]],
[[def-left-right-and-two-sided-ideal]]): if $n-k>n$ or $n-k>m$ there is no such
minor, and the ideal generated by the empty set is $0$. In particular no minor
is nonzero-by-convention: the conventions are
$$E_k(M)=R\quad\text{for }k\ge n,\qquad E_k(M)=0\quad\text{for }k<n-m .$$
For $0\le k\le\min(n,m)$ the ideal $E_k(M)$ is the ideal generated by the
$(n-k)\times(n-k)$ minors; e.g. $E_1(M)$ is generated by the
$(n-1)\times(n-1)$ minors of $A$, and $E_n(M)=R$ because the $0\times0$ minor
is $1$. This is the $k$-th **Fitting ideal** $\operatorname{Fit}_k(M)$ of the
Stacks Project (Tag 07Z6, Lemma 15.8.2 and Definition 15.8.3), which is
indexed by the size $n-k$ of the complementary minors; the two conventions
$0=E_{-1}\subset E_0\subset\cdots$ agree on the range $0\le k\le\min(n,m)$.

**Caveats.** (i) The notation $E_k(M)$ suppresses the chosen presentation; the
ideal defined here is a priori attached to the presentation and it is the
content of [[lem-elementary-ideals-are-independent-of-the-presentation]] that
it is in fact an invariant of $M$. (ii) The conventions in the degenerate
ranges are conventions, not theorems; they are the ones compatible with the
Stacks numbering and with independence of the presentation.
