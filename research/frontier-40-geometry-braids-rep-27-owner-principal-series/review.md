## Exact parameter computation

Dudas–Michel's complete notes, §11.3, Theorem 11.11 (printed p. 50), supply a Coxeter system, powers `q_s` and a basis rescaling `T_w -> lambda_w B_w`. The preceding paragraph says the quadratic relations depend on the dimensions of the two rank-one summands; it **does not print a degree-quotient formula**. Taylor Exercise 5.11 belongs to the spherical section. Neither statement is promoted into an unread arbitrary-character parameter formula.

The repair instead computes everything locally. Sort the character tuple into contiguous equal-coordinate blocks `eta=(a_1^{n_1},...,a_k^{n_k})`. In the block Levi `L=prod GL_{n_r}`, set `rho(l)=prod a_r(det l_r)`. The principal series of `L` is the tensor product of its spherical modules twisted by `rho`. The group-algebra automorphism `g -> rho(g)g` sends `e_eta` to the trivial Borel idempotent and sends the raw corner basis to `rho(dot w)` times the spherical basis. Since `rho(dot w)=prod a_r(-1)^{ell(w_r)}`, the exact normalization is

`lambda_w=prod a_r(-1)^{-ell(w_r)}`.

Use `B_w=R_{Theta_{w^{-1}}}`: right multiplication reverses composition, so inverse indexing is essential. For simple `s`, the raw eigenvalues are `a(-1)q,-a(-1)` and the normalized eigenvalues are `q,-1`. The full braid, commuting and length-additive relations are transferred from the spherical algebra by the twist, rather than inferred from permutation identities alone.

Oi Proposition 2.8(2), printed p. 12, states and proves `a x a = (1 x 1) tensor (a o det)`; Proposition 2.8(1) gives constituent degrees `1,q`. Dudas–Michel §11.1, printed pp. 45–46, proves the spherical normalized relations. These are the exact source-backed inputs to the local calculation. For `q=3` and the nontrivial character of `F_3^x`, `a(-1)=-1`; the raw generator acts by `-3` on the determinant character and therefore cannot satisfy the polynomial with roots `3,-1`. This negative example is an explicit local deduction, not a quotation invented from a source.

Harish–Chandra induction embeds `End_L I_L(eta)` into `End_G I_G(eta)`: a nonzero map remains nonzero on an induced function supported on `P`, evaluated at `1`. The tensor product of block Hecke algebras has dimension `prod n_r!`; the Mackey supplier gives exactly the same dimension for the target endomorphism algebra. The injective map is therefore an isomorphism. In the tensor/idempotent model, `e_eta^G=e_{U_P}e_eta^L` shows that the induced standard operators are the displayed corner operators. This closes the split general-linear statement without a black-box appeal to the deep general cuspidal theorem.

For unsorted characters the equal-coordinate sets need not be intervals: `(a,b,a)` has stabilizer generated by `(1,3)`, not by adjacent equal ambient coordinates. Define the intrinsic Coxeter system by sorting and conjugation. The Weyl-orbit module isomorphism is proved by Mackey pairings: both self pairings and the cross pairing equal `N=|W_chi|`, so the difference of the two characters has norm zero. Maschke and the finite-dimensional complex Schur argument then give module isomorphism. Conjugation of functions by a permutation does **not** preserve the fixed Borel or commute with its fixed `G` action, and that false proof was removed. Transport the sorted Hecke basis through a module isomorphism for the arbitrary character. No equality between this basis and the unsorted raw ambient-length Bruhat basis is asserted.

Representative independence is preserved in its correct form: `n_w=t dot w` requires the factor `chi(t)^{-1}`. More general `b_1 dot w b_2` representatives require division by the two inflated character values. The Borel-intersection covariance makes this compensated quantity independent of its factorization. Uncompensated corner elements are not independent of representatives.

## Exact Tits supplier applicability

Losev Theorem 2.6, complete PDF p. 5, contains the six-step argument. Step 5 suppresses the proof that lifted direct summands are free and the residue-isomorphism criterion. Step 6 says formal triviality makes the locally closed orbit preimage open, without printing that implication's algebraic argument. The published `thm-complete-nakayama-lemma` begins “Assume the Axiom of Dependent Choice” and proves generation of a separated module, not directly the free-module isomorphism claim. These are interface mismatches in the original uses, not defects in the published suppliers.

The added local lemma `lem-formal-triviality-of-one-parameter-semisimple-algebras` proves the needed formal statement. Lift a diagonal primitive idempotent in each matrix factor using the existing idempotent lemma. Right multiplication by each lifted idempotent is a split projection on a finite free module. Lifting finite bases of its residue image and kernel gives a square matrix invertible modulo `t`; its determinant is a unit, so its adjugate proves it invertible. Thus each lifted natural module is free of the correct rank. The action map to the product of their endomorphism rings is likewise a square map invertible modulo `t`, so determinant inversion proves the algebra is trivial over `C[[t]]`. No Complete Nakayama or dependent choice is used.

The idempotent lemma was repaired too: compressed representatives in `(1-e)A(1-e)` are approximate idempotents, and the lifting series must be applied again in that complete direct summand. Only a **finite** family is asserted. Binomial coefficients define a deterministic convergent series, with no repeated choices.

For a principal open `D(f)` of the line, completion at `x` is explicitly the compatible system of truncated polynomials in `z-x`; the denominators with nonzero value at `x` are units in each quotient. This gives `C[[t]]` with `z=x+t` directly. No stronger general completion theorem is presumed.

After shrinking to the semisimple locus `Y=D(fd)` (the trace determinant is `d/f^m`), the incidence set of fiber isomorphisms with a fixed fiber is affine: add inverse variables for `det P` and `fd`, and clear the finite multiplication denominators. Its projection is a classical morphism under `def-classical-affine-variety-morphism`. The exact published `thm-chevalley-constructible-image-varieties` supplies constructibility and explicitly assumes AC. Its statement and proof were read, along with Milne Theorem 9.7 and its full proof, printed pp. 199–201.

If the isomorphism locus were finite, its vanishing polynomial would vanish at all complex points of the incidence set. The exact published `thm-affine-nullstellensatz-correspondence` supplies `I(V(J))=sqrt(J)` under AC. Thus a power of that polynomial belongs to the equations ideal, and substitution of the formal isomorphism into the ideal forces a nonzero polynomial in `x+t` to vanish in the power-series domain, contradiction. Milne Theorem 2.16 and its full proof, printed pp. 42–43, were read as the authoritative source. An infinite constructible subset of the line is cofinite, by the elementary finite-or-cofinite classification of closed sets and the definition of constructibility. The loci attached to any two semisimple fibers therefore intersect, proving their isomorphism. Formal triviality alone is not asserted to imply Zariski openness.

AC is carried explicitly through the Tits theorem, its noncanonical isomorphism corollary, spherical constituents, the general endomorphism theorem's group-algebra conclusion, general constituents, the three affected examples and the noncanonicity remark. The first general-character Hecke identification is proved using finite algebra and does not need AC. The trace-form lemma now declares the actual published finite-dimensional Jacobson-radical nilpotence and semisimple-quotient supplier.
