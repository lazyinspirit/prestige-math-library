4.4. The slice genus. We can now prove the ﬁrst two theorems from the introduction.
Proof. (of Theorem 1.) Suppose K ⊂S3 bounds an oriented surface of genus g in B4. Then
there is an orientable connected cobordism of Euler characteristic −2g between K and the
unknot U in R3 × [0, 1]. Let x ∈Kh′(K) −{0} be a class for which s(x) is maximal. Then
φS(x) is a nonzero element of Kh′(U). Now φS is a ﬁltered map with ﬁltered degree −2g,
so
s(φS(x)) ≥s(x) −2g.
On the other hand, smax(U) = 1, so
s(φS(x)) ≤1.
It follows that s(x) ≤2g+1, so smax(K) ≤2g+1 and s(K) ≤2g. To show that s(K) ≥−2g,
we apply the same argument to K (which bounds a surface S of genus g) and use the fact
that s(K) = −s(K).

5. Computations and Relations with other Invariants
Although the invariant s(K) is algorithmically computable from a diagram of K, it is
impossible to compute by hand for all but the smallest knots. In this section, we describe
some techniques which enable us to eﬃciently compute s.
