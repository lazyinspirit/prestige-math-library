## Changes and mathematical audit

The scaffold covers strict and mod-null invariance, their sigma-algebra properties and the strict-representative bridge; ergodicity and measurable invariant functions; strong/weak mixing and the implication chain; finite pi-system and precisely hypothesized sigma-finite preservation criteria; Koopman isometries; infinite-return Poincare recurrence and its countable-base topological version; measurable inducing, preservation, ergodicity and noninvertible Kac formulas; circle rotations, doubling and a locally constructed fair-coin shift; and compact-metric Krylov–Bogolyubov through a diagonal argument and RMK.

Corrections to the design and source conventions:

- Doubling with the point mass at zero IS ergodic. The nonergodic invariant measure used here is half the point mass at zero plus half the uniform measure on the period-two orbit {1/3,2/3}. The corresponding strict invariant eventual-zero set has measure 1/2.
- Strict and mod-null invariant families may differ; they need not differ (the identity is an immediate equality case). For doubling, {0} is mod-null but not strictly invariant.
- Cylinders form a pi-system; their finite unions form the algebra used for extension. Compactness is proved by deterministic binary-tree descent, without Tychonoff. The published Caratheodory theorem nonetheless assumes countable choice, so the resulting measure construction inherits it.
- The published density theorem and compact-metric countable-dense-subset lemma explicitly assume countable choice. Rotation's prescribed density argument, doubling's density argument and the compact-metric probability subsequence argument retain that strength. No full choice, BPI, Banach–Alaoglu or deferred catalogue supplier is asserted.
- Krylov–Bogolyubov requires a NONEMPTY compact metric space. Probability on the empty space is impossible. The proof explicitly produces a positive normalized functional and invokes RMK uniqueness for regular Borel measures.
- Inducing is defined on the infinitely returning core, conull in E for finite total measure, so the induced map is a self-map. The mere finite-first-return domain in Sarig's presentation need not be pointwise closed under the return map.
- Sarig Theorem 1.7 supplies the noninvertible inducing/Kac route. An invertible tower proof cannot establish the stated noninvertible theorem. The Kac scalar proof here telescopes the measures of finite avoidance sets; the excursion formula handles nonnegative functions first and then integrable signed/complex functions.
- Rotation ergodicity uses density plus dense irrational orbits, not Fourier completeness. A single exponential test function is used only for the separate non-weak-mixing example.
- The Gauss example proves only invariance of the logarithmic density; its continued-fraction ergodicity proof is deferred to an owner-decision destination with the missing distortion machinery identified.
