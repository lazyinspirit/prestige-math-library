## Sources and independent mathematical checks

I read Jacob Lurie's complete three-page note, [A Proof of the
Borel-Weil-Bott Theorem](https://www.math.harvard.edu/~lurie/papers/bwb.pdf).
Theorem 2 proof, printed p. 2, supplies the big-cell/rank-one extension route;
Theorem 3 and Lemma 4, printed pp. 2–3, supply the relative shift; Theorem 5,
printed p. 3, supplies the regular/singular alternatives and Serre-duality
finish. The note has inconsistent character/action conventions. I used the
library's explicit bundle relation and left translation, and checked the
matrix factorization rather than copying those inconsistent signs.
Other bibliography URLs were not independently fetched in this review.

The relative shift has the correct direction and fibre degree:
$s_\alpha\cdot\lambda=\lambda-(n+1)\alpha$, with relative canonical bundle
$\mathcal L_{-\alpha}$ and $n=\langle\lambda,\alpha^\vee\rangle$.
For singular shifted weights, the increasing-degree chain covers degrees
at least $m=n_-(\lambda+\rho)$; the Serre-dual chain covers the rest because
$m+n_-(-\lambda-\rho)\le N-1$. For regular weights the complementary Weyl
length is $N-\ell(w)$, giving low-degree vanishing by the same duality.
These arguments close; I found no further missing cohomological prerequisite
in the assigned proofs after the local repairs.

The examples were checked directly: the rank-one dot translate is
$-(m+2)\omega_1$, with the only dot-singular parameter $m=-1$; the $A_2$
degree-one weight is $-3\omega_1+3\omega_2$; and the top-degree weight is
$-3\rho$. The Weyl dimension product at $\rho$ is $2^3=8$, and
$w_0\rho=-\rho$ proves the self-duality used in the degree-one example.
The Euler character retains the dual module, with highest weight
$-w_0(w\cdot\lambda)$; Weyl invariance does not remove that dual.

## Repairs and evidence

1. **`lem-sections-of-an-associated-line-bundle-as-equivariant-functions`,
   [F2] (line 57) and Remarks (line 77).** The original fact described the
   dual map as inversion of scalars, which is not linear; it now uses duality
   of one-dimensional character modules. The remark falsely said that
   changing the character sign dualizes the function space. It now states
   only the valid line-bundle duality supplied by
   `def-borel-character-equivariant-line-bundle`. The counterexample and the
   projective-line cohomology supplier show why section-space duality fails.

2. **`lem-a-nonzero-dominant-section-is-determined-on-the-big-cell`,
   step 1.1 (line 59), new step 5.1 (line 67), and Remarks.** The original
   inference “derivative at the origin is zero, hence the polynomial is
   constant” was false (for example $t^2$). The corrected proof uses
   $p_g'(t_0)=(x\cdot f)(u_{-\alpha}(-t_0)g)=0$ for every $t_0$.
   Its original statement also promised the entire weight-$(-\lambda)$
   space, while the proof established only the invariant line. The new step
   proves that a section of that weight restricts to a torus-conjugation
   invariant polynomial on $U^-$, whose negative-root coordinates have no
   nonconstant weight-zero monomial. Thus it is constant and belongs to the
   invariant line. The full original claim is preserved. The Lurie locator
   was corrected from p. 1 to pp. 1–2.

3. **`lem-the-borel-weil-section-extends-from-the-big-cell-to-the-flag-variety`,
   [F3], [F5], new [F8] (line 76), and proof steps 1.2–4.1 (lines 82–90).**
   Applying Bruhat decomposition to the opposite positive system alone gives
   two opposite Borels, not the claimed mixed cells; left multiplication by
   $n_{w_0}$ now derives the mixed decomposition explicitly. The original
   fibre-count paragraph assigned negative-root parameters to an intersection
   lying in $B$; the new dimension calculation uses the Bruhat cell
   isomorphism and $\ell(w_0w)=N-\ell(w)$ instead.
   The original rank-one step inferred ambient multiplicity one from the
   étaleness of $SL_2\to G_\alpha$, without proving transversality inside
   $G$. The replacement uses the published DVR theorem: at a generic
   boundary point write $v_0=z^m h$, shrink until $h$ is a unit, and translate
   that neighborhood to $n_{s_\alpha}$. The explicit curve
   $\varphi_\alpha\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}t&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$
   has positive contact order $e$ with the boundary and pulls $v_0$ back to
   $t^k$. Thus $me=k\ge0$ implies $m\ge0$, without assuming $e=1$.
   The normality criterion closes the extension. Unsupported subgroup
   intersection assertions in [F5] were removed because this route does not
   need them. The character domain $\lambda\in X^*(T)$ is now explicit;
   the normalized-function/nonzero-section equivalence is proved in both
   directions. Added item dependency:
   `thm-height-one-localisation-of-normal-noetherian-domain-is-dvr`.
