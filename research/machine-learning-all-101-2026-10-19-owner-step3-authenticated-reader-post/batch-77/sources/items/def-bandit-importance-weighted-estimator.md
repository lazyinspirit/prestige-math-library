## Definition

Let $k,n\ge1$ be integers, let $[k]=\{1,\ldots,k\}$, and let
$$
\Delta_k=\left\{p\in[0,1]^k:\sum_{i=1}^k p_i=1\right\}.
$$
On a probability space $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)$, let $(\mathcal F_t)_{t=0}^n$ be the discrete filtration generated by the complete histories through round $t$, with $\mathcal F_0$ trivial; a complete pre-action history records past reward vectors, actions, and observed rewards. A reward vector is either fixed in advance or a deterministic function of the complete pre-action history. Thus, at a finite horizon with finitely many arms, there are finitely many possible histories. Before action $A_t$ is drawn, the current reward vector $x_t=(x_{t,1},\ldots,x_{t,k})\in[0,1]^k$ and sampling distribution $P_t=(P_{t,1},\ldots,P_{t,k})\in\Delta_k$ are $\mathcal F_{t-1}$-measurable, and $P_{t,i}>0$ for every $i$. The finite-valued measurable action $A_t\in[k]$ has conditional law $\mathbb P(A_t=i\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})=P_{t,i}$. The learner observes only $X_t=x_{t,A_t}$ and sets
$$
Y_t=1-X_t,\qquad y_{t,i}=1-x_{t,i},\qquad \widehat Y_{t,i}=\frac{\mathbf 1\{A_t=i\}Y_t}{P_{t,i}},\qquad \widehat X_{t,i}=1-\widehat Y_{t,i}.
$$
The quantity $\widehat Y_{t,i}$ is the importance-weighted loss estimate for arm $i$, and $\widehat X_{t,i}$ is its corresponding reward estimate. A fixed reward sequence is the oblivious case; allowing $x_t$ to depend on the history available before $A_t$ is drawn gives the non-anticipating past-adaptive case. In either case the current reward vector is chosen before the current action. For finite horizons and finite action histories generated by such a fixed or deterministic history-dependent sequence, all these estimates are finite and integrable.

[[def-adversary-and-feedback-model]]: bandit feedback reveals only the played reward, and non-anticipating rewards are fixed before the current action.
