## Statement

Assume AC. Let $d<\infty$, let the product-law output satisfy $f\in L^4(\mu)$,
and suppose $V=\operatorname{Var}(f(X))>0$. For the iid row vector $Z$ and
estimates of [[alg-pick-freeze-monte-carlo-sobol-estimators]], write
$$\eta=\mathbb EZ=(V_{\{1\}},\ldots,V_{\{d\}},\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_d,V),$$
where $\tau_i=\sum_{u\ni i}V_u$, and let $\Sigma=\operatorname{Cov}(Z)$. The
probability that the algorithm returns `zero-empirical-variance` tends to
zero. For asymptotic statements, let $\widetilde S_n$ be the returned ratio
vector when $\bar D>0$ and the zero vector otherwise. Define the Borel map
$g:\mathbb R^{2d+1}\to\mathbb R^{2d}$ by
$$g(n_1,\ldots,n_d,t_1,\ldots,t_d,s)=\begin{cases}(n_1/s,\ldots,n_d/s,t_1/s,\ldots,t_d/s),&s>0,\\0,&s\le0.\end{cases}$$
As $n\to\infty$, $\widetilde S_n$ is consistent in probability and
$$\sqrt n\bigl(\widetilde S_n-g(\eta)\bigr)\Rightarrow N(0,J\Sigma J^{\mathsf T}),$$
where $J=Dg(\eta)$; since $V>0$, $g$ is smooth on a neighborhood of $\eta$.
Every fixed extension on the zero-status event has the same limit. Write
$M_n=\widehat J\widehat\Sigma\widehat J^{\mathsf T}/n$ for the returned MCSE
covariance matrix when $\bar D>0$. Then
$nM_n\to J\Sigma J^{\mathsf T}$ in probability; any fixed extension of $M_n$
on the zero-status event has the same convergence. For each finite $n\ge2$ and
$d$, the procedure terminates after $n$ rows with at most $n(2d+5)$ evaluations
of $f$. Forming the sample covariance and plug-in MCSE takes
$O(nd^2+d^3)$ scalar arithmetic operations. The finite-sample ratios need not
be unbiased.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, $d<\infty$, $f\in L^4(\mu)$, $V>0$, and iid rows generated by the algorithm.

[A1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: AC is assumed for the multivariate CLT supplier.

[F1] [[alg-pick-freeze-monte-carlo-sobol-estimators]]: each row draws six mutually independent vectors $X,X',U,V,W,W'$ with law $\mu$, evaluates $f(X)$ once, the $d$ first-order and $d$ total-effect hybrids, and $f(U),f(V),f(W),f(W')$, and forms the row vector $Z=(N_1,\ldots,N_d,T_1,\ldots,T_d,D)$; the output ratios use the final coordinate as denominator.

[F2] [[thm-pick-freeze-sobol-covariance-identities]]: the first and total pick-freeze numerators have means $V_{\{i\}}$ and $\tau_i=\sum_{u\ni i}V_u$, respectively.

[F3] [[cor-iid-finite-variance-strong-law]]: square-integrable iid variables have sample means converging almost surely to their means.

[F4] [[lem-measurable-functions-preserve-independence]]: measurable coordinatewise functions of an independent family remain independent.

[F5] [[thm-khinchin-weak-law-for-iid-integrable-variables]]: iid integrable variables have sample means converging in probability.

[F6] [[thm-multivariate-iid-central-limit-theorem]]: under AC, iid finite-dimensional random vectors with finite second moment satisfy a multivariate CLT, including singular covariance.

[F7] [[thm-first-and-second-order-delta-method-for-random-vectors]]: a differentiable map transfers a vector CLT to the derivative image.

[F8] [[def-moments-variance-and-covariance]]: $\Sigma$ is the covariance matrix of the row vector.

[F9] [[cor-cauchy-schwarz-inequality-for-l-two]]: square-integrable row coordinates have integrable pairwise products.

[F10] [[def-independent-random-elements]] and [[thm-independent-random-elements-have-product-joint-law]]: independent vectors have product joint law, so integrable functions of separate vectors factor under iterated integration.

[F11] [[cor-expectation-linearity-monotonicity-and-modulus-bound]]: expectation is linear on integrable variables.

## Proof

**Given:** The hypotheses in the Statement and facts [A1], [F1]–[F11].

1.1 For each row, Cauchy–Schwarz [F9] and $f\in L^4$ make $f(X)f(X^{[i]})$ and $f(U)f(V)$ square-integrable; $(a-b)^4\le8(a^4+b^4)$ makes $T_i$ and $D$ square-integrable as well. Thus every coordinate of $Z$ has finite second moment. By [F10]–[F11], $\mathbb E[f(U)f(V)]=\mu^2$ and $\mathbb ED=\frac12(2\mathbb E[f(X)^2]-2\mu^2)=V$ by [F8]. The covariance identity [F2] gives $\mathbb E[f(X)f(X^{[i]})]-\mu^2=V_{\{i\}}$ and $\mathbb ET_i=\tau_i$, hence $\mathbb EN_i=V_{\{i\}}$ and $\mathbb EZ=\eta$. Independent rows make these row vectors iid. [F1, F2, F8, F9, F10, F11, given, algebra]

2.1 Apply [F3] to each of the finitely many coordinates of $Z$. Then $\bar Z\to\eta$ almost surely. Since $V>0$, almost surely its last coordinate is eventually positive, so the continuous ratio formula gives $\widetilde S_n=g(\bar Z)\to g(\eta)$ almost surely and $\mathbb P(\bar D=0)\to0$. Every fixed extension on the zero-status event differs only on an event whose probability tends to zero; for any bounded continuous test function the expectation difference is at most twice its supremum norm times that probability. Hence it has the same distributional limit, and convergence in probability is also unchanged. [F1, F3, step 1.1, given]

3.1 The finite second moment from step 1.1 and [A1] satisfy [F6]. Hence $\sqrt n(\bar Z-\eta)\Rightarrow N(0,\Sigma)$. The map $g$ in the Statement is Borel, being the continuous ratio map on the open half-space $s>0$ and zero on its complement. Since $V>0$, it is smooth near $\eta$ with derivative $J$. Applying [F7] gives the joint delta-method limit, and step 2.1 transfers it to every stated fixed extension. [A1, F6, F7, step 1.1, step 2.1, given]

3.2 For every two coordinates $Z_a,Z_b$, Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\mathbb E|Z_aZ_b|<\infty$. Each product is the same measurable function of every iid row; [F4] gives independence and the common row law gives identical distributions. Thus [F5] gives convergence in probability of the sample means of all finitely many products $Z_aZ_b$. Step 2.1 gives convergence of the row means. Expanding the sample covariance around those means by [F11] proves $\widehat\Sigma\to\Sigma$ in probability. Continuity of the derivative of $g$ near $\eta$ then gives $\widehat J\widehat\Sigma\widehat J^{\mathsf T}\to J\Sigma J^{\mathsf T}$ in probability. Since the algorithm returns $M_n=\widehat J\widehat\Sigma\widehat J^{\mathsf T}/n$, this proves the stated scaled covariance convergence. An arbitrary fixed finite extension on the zero-status event preserves convergence in probability because that event has probability tending to zero by step 2.1. The argument does not claim an unbiased ratio. [F1, F4, F5, F7, F8, F11, step 1.1, step 2.1, given, algebra]

4.1 The loops over $n$ rows and $d$ coordinates are finite, so the procedure terminates after the $n$ rows. In each row [F1] evaluates $f(X)$ once and reuses it in every hybrid expression, evaluates the $d$ first-order and $d$ total-effect hybrids, and evaluates the four additional values $f(U),f(V),f(W),f(W')$. This requires $1+d+d+4=2d+5$ evaluations per row, hence at most $n(2d+5)$ overall. Forming the covariance of the $(2d+1)$-dimensional row vectors costs $O(nd^2)$ scalar operations, and the plug-in matrix products cost $O(d^3)$. [F1, given, algebra] ∎
