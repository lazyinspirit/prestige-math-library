## Example

For $n\ge1$, a coordinate reflection $r:S^n\to S^n$ has degree $-1$. A reflection-invariant triangulation exhibits this sign by sending its oriented fundamental cycle to its negative.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The objects and hypotheses in the statement above.

[F2] For an abstract simplicial complex $K$ and an integer $n \geq 0$, the **simplicial chain group** $C_n(K)$ is the free abelian group generated by the oriented $n$-simplices of $K$, subject to the relation $$[v_{\pi(0)},\dots,v_{\pi(n)}]=\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)[v_0,\dots,v_n]$$ for every permutation $\pi$ of the vertices of a simplex. For $n<0$, set $C_n(K)=0$. The **boundary operator** $$\partial_n:C_n(K)\to C_{n-1}(K)$$ is $\partial_0=0$ in degree $0$. For $n\geq1$, it is defined on an oriented simplex by $$\partial_n[v_0,\dots,v_n]=\sum_{i=0}^n (-1)^i [v_0,\dots,\widehat{v_i},\dots,v_n].$$ The well-definedness of this formula with respect to the chosen oriented representative is recorded in lem-simplicial-boundary-is-independent-of-oriented-representative through `justified_by`. ([[def-simplicial-chain-groups-and-boundary]])

[F3] For every simplicial complex $K$, the natural simplicial-to-singular chain map induces $H_n^{\mathrm{simp}}(K;G)\cong H_n(|K|;G)$ for all $n$. ([[thm-simplicial-and-singular-homology-agree-for-simplicial-complexes]])

## Verification

1.1 Take a regular $(n+1)$-simplex in $\mathbb R^{n+1}$ centered at the origin, with vertices $v_0,\ldots,v_{n+1}$. Radial projection of its boundary to $S^n$ is a homeomorphism: each ray meets its convex boundary exactly once, and the continuous bijection has compact source and Hausdorff target. The orthogonal symmetry interchanging $v_0,v_1$ and fixing the other vertices is reflection across the perpendicular bisector hyperplane. Rotate this configuration so that this hyperplane is the coordinate hyperplane of $r$; radial projection then commutes with the reflection. [construct]

1.2 With the orientation convention of [F2], put $c=\partial[v_0,\ldots,v_{n+1}]=\sum_{j=0}^{n+1}(-1)^j[v_0,\ldots,\widehat v_j,\ldots,v_{n+1}]$. Each codimension-two face occurs twice with opposite signs, so $\partial c=0$. Conversely, cancellation along every common facet forces the coefficients of any top cycle to be a common integer multiple of those of $c$. There are no $(n+1)$-simplices in the boundary, so $c$ generates its top simplicial homology and hence its oriented fundamental class under the natural simplicial-to-singular isomorphism [F3]. [F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 The vertex transposition $\tau$ commutes with the boundary formula. In the filled simplex its action on $[v_0,\ldots,v_{n+1}]$ is multiplication by $-1$. Thus $\tau c=\partial\tau[v_0,\ldots,v_{n+1}]=-c$. Radial projection carries this equality to $r_*[S^n]=-[S^n]$, independently of which sign was chosen for the generator. [F2, step 1.1, step 1.2] ∎
