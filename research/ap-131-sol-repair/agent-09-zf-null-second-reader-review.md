The compact-null union lemma is valid in ZF. A finite closed real-interval cover at budget \(b_n/2\) can be enlarged to a finite rational *open* interval cover using only finitely many rational-density witnesses, spending less than the remaining \(b_n/2\). Each original endpoint lies strictly inside an enlarged interval; closing the rational intervals therefore still covers the endpoints without changing length. This works for degenerate intervals and the empty compact set. The supplier's pairing theorem exhibits the raw bijection \(J_0(m,j)=2^m(2j+1)\) onto positive integers and its normalized bijection \(J=J_0-1\) onto \(\mathbb N\). The latter satisfies \(J(m,j)\ge j\). Thus the recursive code \(C((a_0,\ldots))=1+J(a_0,C(\text{tail}))\) is injective and decodes by strictly decreasing tail codes. Integer and positive-denominator fraction codes enumerate all rationals, hence all finite rational-interval lists. For each \(n\), the set of codes for covers of \(K_n\) within budget \(b_n\) is nonempty and has a least natural member; Replacement forms the sequence of those least codes without countable choice. Flattening their finite lists through the same normalized pairing, and padding with zero-length intervals, gives one sequence of closed intervals. Every finite initial segment lies in a finite rectangle of row indices, so its length is at most a finite geometric-budget sum, hence at most \(\varepsilon\).

The forward Lebesgue criterion uses the lemma with its exact hypotheses. For each \(\sigma>0\), the oscillation superlevel set \(E_\sigma\) is closed in \([a,b]\) by the published supplier and is therefore compact. The integrability/partition argument covers points in interiors of marked cells by those closed cells and covers *every partition endpoint* by an added degenerate interval; the total length is the sum of marked cell lengths, made arbitrarily small by the Darboux gap. Hence each \(E_\sigma\) has content zero. The pointwise identity \(D=\bigcup_{k\ge0}E_{1/(k+1)}\) follows from continuity iff zero oscillation and the Archimedean property, including points with oscillation at least one. The compact-null union lemma then makes \(D\) null in ZF. The converse in this item uses a single finite cover of the compact null \(E_{\sigma'}\), not a countable choice step.
