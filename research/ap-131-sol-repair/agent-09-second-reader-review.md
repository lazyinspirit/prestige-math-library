## Agent 03's finite prime-chain lemma

I read `lem-module-finite-domain-extension-lifts-finite-prime-chains`, its direct supplier `lem-determinant-trick-for-nakayama`, the localization and quotient prime-correspondence suppliers, and its direct normalization-dimension consumer `lem-affine-domain-normalisation-dimension-lower-bound`. I made no edit to agent 03's files.

The lying-over argument is choice-free as stated. Localization preserves the supplied finite module generators. Because both original rings are domains and the extension is injective, localizing at \(B\setminus\mathfrak p\) leaves a nonzero algebra \(M\). If \(\mathfrak pB_{\mathfrak p}M=M\), the determinant trick produces \(1-a\) annihilating \(M\), with \(a\in\mathfrak pB_{\mathfrak p}\); \(1-a\) is a unit in the local ring, contradiction. Hence the fiber \(C=M/\mathfrak pM\) is a nonzero finite-dimensional algebra over \(\kappa(\mathfrak p)\). Its zero ideal is proper. The attained dimensions of proper ideals lie in the finite set \(\{0,\ldots,\dim C-1\}\), so a proper ideal of maximal attained dimension exists without Zorn's lemma or a choice function. A larger proper ideal would have larger dimension; the selected ideal is maximal and therefore prime. The field embeds into nonzero \(C\), so its contraction is zero. Localization then yields a prime contracting to \(\mathfrak p\).

At each supplied chain link, \(B/\mathfrak p_i\hookrightarrow A/\mathfrak q_i\) is again an injective finite extension of domains. Lying over \(\mathfrak p_{i+1}/\mathfrak p_i\) gives \(\mathfrak q_{i+1}\supsetneq\mathfrak q_i\), strictness following from the distinct contractions. Finite induction uses only finitely many existential witnesses. The normalization-dimension consumer applies this to the explicit coordinate-prime chain of length \(d\) in the polynomial subring; the resulting chain in \(A\) proves \(\dim A\ge d\), including \(d=0\). I found no logical gap in these two items. Focused precheck and rendercheck passed on both.

## KU skeletal differential

The prior `lem-ku-representability-and-skeletal-postnikov-d-three-comparison` had a real justification gap: its [A3] supplied only the *first* stable \(ku\) invariant, while step 2.1 asserted without a map that the link between the higher coefficient groups \(\pi_{-q}\) and \(\pi_{2-q}\) was a Bott translate of it. I repaired the owned item. Adams, *Stable Homotopy and Generalised Homology*, Proposition 16.6, printed p. 391, explicitly gives the Bott cofiber sequence \(\Sigma^2bu\to bu\to H\mathbb Z\) and identifies the first invariant as the nonzero integral \(\delta_2Sq^2\). The cofiber long exact sequence makes the Bott map an isomorphism on \(\pi_j\) for \(j\ge1\). If \(-q=2k\), its \(k\)-fold iterate \(\Sigma^{2k}ku\to ku\), evaluated at total-degree space index \(t=p-2k\), is \(ku_p\to ku_t\): an isomorphism on \(\pi_i\) for \(i\ge p\), with no lower positive homotopy in the source. It therefore transports the relevant first Postnikov fibration of \(ku_p\) to the \(p\)-connective part of \(ku_t\). The already-published `lem-first-connective-complex-k-theory-postnikov-invariant-is-integral-sq-three` derives the universal integral operation from Adams's same computation. For \(p=1\), the universal \(H^4(K(\mathbb Z,1);\mathbb Z)\) group vanishes.

I also expanded the exact-couple comparison: an \(E_3\) representative extends over the \((p+2)\)-skeleton after the allowed adjustment; its values on the attaching spheres of \((p+3)\)-cells are both the cellular lifting obstruction and the generalized-cohomology connecting map of the corresponding skeletal cofiber sequence. This is the \(j(x)\) representative in the local exact-couple formula, so its \(E_3\) class is \(d_3\). Adams Chapter 7, printed pp. 257–260, actually constructs that AHSS from skeletal pair/triple sequences; the published exact-couple and obstruction suppliers give the local identifications. This route does not assume a separate AHSS/Postnikov comparison theorem or infer the invariant from homotopy groups alone. Focused precheck, rendercheck and `git diff --check` passed for the repaired KU item. No independent judge was run.
