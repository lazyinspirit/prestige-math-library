## Channel, code, and error contracts

PW Definition 19.1 (printed pp. 371–372 / PDF pp. 396–397) defines a channel as a sequence of Markov kernels `P_{Y^n|X^n}: A^n → B^n`, one for each blocklength. This general sequence need not be causal, stationary, or memoryless. “Memoryless” means each length-n kernel factors as a product of possibly position-dependent kernels; “stationary memoryless” means `P_{Y^n|X^n}=W^{⊗n}` for one fixed single-letter kernel `W`. “Discrete” means both alphabets are finite; a DMC has all three properties. Non-anticipation is an additional prefix-consistency/causality condition, not part of the bare channel definition. A new library statement should not silently promote a result about `W^{⊗n}` to channels with memory.

For an `(n,M,ε)` code, a uniform message is encoded to `A^n` and decoded from `B^n` (an erasure output is allowed in PW’s convention). Average error averages over messages. The maximal-error version bounds each message’s conditional error. The corresponding limits `C_ε=liminf_n n^{-1}log M*(n,ε)` and `C=lim_{ε↓0}C_ε` are operational definitions; log base sets the units. PW Definition 19.3 and Theorem 19.4 (printed pp. 373–375 / PDF pp. 398–400) give the exact expurgation comparison, for `τ∈(0,1)`,

`τ M*(n, ε(1−τ)) ≤ M*max(n,ε) ≤ M*(n,ε)`.

Thus asymptotic average/maximal capacities agree at left-continuity points of `C_ε`, in particular at Shannon capacity. This does not identify the finite-blocklength optima or erase the criterion from a theorem statement.

## Shannon capacity, converse, and memory limits

For any channel sequence, PW Theorem 19.7 (printed p. 375 / PDF p. 400) gives the weak converse `C_ε ≤ C(I)/(1−ε)`, where `C(I)=liminf_n n^{-1} sup_{P_{X^n}} I(X^n;Y^n)`. The proof is Fano plus data processing. For a stationary-memoryless channel, Theorem 19.8 uses an arbitrary single-letter input law, the Shannon/Feinstein achievability bound, additivity of the information density under iid sampling, and the weak law of large numbers; optimizing the law yields `C_ε ≥ sup_{P_X} I(X;Y)` for fixed `ε>0`. Theorem 19.9 and Proposition 19.10 (printed pp. 376–377 / PDF pp. 401–402) conclude

`C = C(I) = sup_{P_X} I(X;Y)`.

This proof needs no assertion that a maximizing `P_X` exists: approximate a supremum. For finite alphabets, continuity on the compact probability simplex gives attainment. For general alphabets it can fail. PW Example 5.1 (printed p. 98 / PDF p. 123) uses real AWGN `Y=X+N(0,1)` under the exact equalities `E X=0`, `E X²=P`, and `E X⁴=s>3P²`. The capacity is still `½ log(1+P)`, but no feasible input attains it; Gaussian-plus-vanishing-spike laws approach the value. The output law is nevertheless uniquely optimal by Theorem 5.9 (Kemperman; printed pp. 97–98 / PDF pp. 122–123). Do not infer input-law attainment from output-law attainment or from a finite-dimensional analogy.

The strong converse has a narrower scope. PW Theorem 22.1 (printed pp. 422–427 / PDF pp. 447–452) proves `C_ε=C` for every fixed `ε∈(0,1)` if a stationary-memoryless channel has finite input alphabet **or** finite output alphabet. Its proof is hypothesis-testing based and handles those two cases separately. PW §22.2 (printed pp. 427–428 / PDF pp. 452–453) gives a stationary-memoryless counterexample with countable input and output `[0,1]`: its Shannon capacity is `C`, yet `C_ε≥2C` whenever `ε>e^{-1/2}`. The strong converse is therefore not available merely from stationarity and memorylessness for arbitrary alphabets.

## Cost constraints and Gaussian channels

PW Definitions 20.1–20.3 (printed pp. 395–396 / PDF pp. 420–421) distinguish two constraints. Operationally, every transmitted codeword must satisfy `n^{-1}Σ_i c(x_i)≤P`. In information capacity, the optimization instead ranges over block input distributions with expected total cost `E[Σ_i c(X_i)]≤nP`. These are not the same finite-block condition. An admissible `P` has at least one input point (equivalently an input law) of cost at most `P`; write `P_0=inf_x c(x)`. PW allows extended-real costs, but a library contract should rule out undefined sums or expectations such as simultaneous `+∞` and `−∞` contributions, for example by assuming nonnegative or bounded-below measurable cost and stating integrability where used. Corollary 20.5 (printed p. 397 / PDF p. 422) single-letterizes the information capacity for stationary-memoryless channels with a separable per-letter cost:

`C(I)(P)=sup_{P_X:E c(X)≤P} I(X;Y)`.

Theorem 20.9 (printed pp. 398–399 / PDF pp. 423–424) equates operational and information capacity for an information-stable channel at admissible `P`. Its definition requires input laws whose normalized information density converges in probability to `C(I)(P)` and whose probability of exceeding the cost threshold `P+δ` vanishes for every `δ>0`. The proof for `P>P_0` uses strict cost slack, the extended Feinstein lemma, and continuity of the concave capacity-cost function on the interior; the boundary `P=P_0` is handled separately (Exercise IV.23), because continuity there need not hold. For a stationary-memoryless finite-cost source, the usual iid/WLLN construction supplies the relevant information stability under the stated integrability conditions. The cost proof cannot simply replace every codeword constraint by an expected-cost constraint.

The real AWGN channel of Definition 20.10 is `Y=X+Z`, `Z∼N(0,σ²)` independent of `X`, input/output `R`, and cost `c(x)=x²`; its n-letter channel is the product extension. The complex version uses circular Gaussian noise and cost `|x|²`. Theorem 20.11 (printed pp. 399–400 / PDF pp. 424–425) gives the operational and information capacities under average per-codeword power `P`: real `½ log(1+P/σ²)` and complex `log(1+P/σ²)`. Fix logarithm base in any library statement; the usual bit formulas use base 2. The information-capacity maximizer is the corresponding Gaussian input. The proof is not by assuming a Gaussian codeword: operational achievability must still satisfy the codeword constraint.

For the finite `L`-branch parallel real AWGN channel with independent noises `N(0,σ_j²)` and total expected power `Σ_j E X_j²≤P`, PW Theorem 20.14 (printed pp. 402–403 / PDF pp. 427–428) gives channel water-filling: `C=½Σ_j log⁺(T/σ_j²)` where `P=Σ_j (T−σ_j²)⁺`. The proof reduces the product channel to a finite concave power-allocation problem. This is channel water-filling; it is not a proof of reverse water-filling for stationary Gaussian source coding. PW §20.5* sketches continuous-time bandlimited AWGN and explicitly flags unresolved functional-analytic rigor; do not present that sketch as a proved continuous-time theorem.

## Feedback and channels with memory

PW Definition 23.1 (printed pp. 442–443 / PDF pp. 467–468) specifies full noiseless output feedback by causal maps `X_t=f_t(W,Y^{t−1})`. Theorem 23.3 (printed p. 443 / PDF p. 468) says feedback does not increase Shannon capacity for a stationary-memoryless channel: `C_fb=C=sup_{P_X}I(X;Y)`. Its result concerns fixed-length Shannon capacity with this feedback model. PW treats zero-error, variable-length feedback, and variable transmission power separately; gains in those regimes do not contradict Theorem 23.3. General channels with memory require their own model and capacity theorem.

## Finite-blocklength bounds, dispersion, and the correction

PPV’s complete 2010 manuscript treats general one-shot measurable channels and separates average and maximal error. It proves one-shot achievability bounds (including dependence-testing and random-coding/union bounds), converse/meta-converse bounds based on binary testing, and specialized DMC/AWGN normal approximations. Its constraint-specific AWGN results distinguish equal energy for each codeword, a maximum-energy constraint, and an average-over-codebook constraint. These distinctions should be retained in any use of its bounds. PW §22.3 (printed pp. 428–430 / PDF pp. 453–455) gives the meta-converse: a code of size `M` and average error at most `ε` obeys `1/M ≥ β_{1−ε}(P_{XY},P_XQ_Y)` for each auxiliary output law `Q_Y`, with the minimax form optimizing over input and auxiliary output distributions. The test convention and admissible measures must be defined in a self-contained library item; this is a bridge to the r1 testing lane, not a substitute for that lane’s measure conventions.

PW Theorem 22.2 (printed pp. 435–437 / PDF pp. 460–462) states, for DMC, DMC with cost, AWGN, and parallel AWGN, at each fixed `0<ε<1/2`,

`log M*(n,ε)=nC−√(nV) Q^{-1}(ε)+O(log n)`.

The source’s dispersion is the conditional variance averaged over the chosen capacity-achieving input: `V=E[Var(i(X*;Y*)|X*)]`; if multiple capacity-achieving laws exist, choose one minimizing this value. It is not an unqualified unconditional variance `Var(i(X*;Y*))`, which can contain the extra variability of the conditional mean. The theorem’s proof is expressly a sketch: it invokes Berry–Esseen for the achievability remainder and specialized converses, and its footnote acknowledges a subtle nonunique-CAID gap in earlier proofs.

PPV’s default `M*` convention is maximal (per-message) error; the average-error optimum is marked `M*_{avg}`. Theorem 48’s fixed-type converse is explicitly for maximal-error subcodes. Its proof splits into `ε≤1/2,V_min>0`, `ε≤1/2,V_min=0`, `ε>1/2,V_max>0`, and `ε>1/2,V_max=0`. For `ε≤1/2` its stated upper bound is `nC−√(nV_min)Q^{-1}(ε)+½log n+O(1)`; for `ε>1/2` and a non-exotic DMC it uses `V_max` in that expression, while the exotic case has only `nC+O(n^{1/3})`. PPV calls a DMC exotic when `V_max=0` and there is an input letter outside the support of every capacity-achieving law whose output divergence from the unique capacity-achieving output is `C` but whose conditional information variance is positive. The Appendix I Case 1 proof has the gap identified by its authors and author-page erratum: a direction in the Hessian kernel may point outside the probability simplex, so a derivative inequality along that direction is not justified. This also invalidates using that proof as if it established the later nonunique-CAID normal approximation. PPV Theorem 49 gives the DMC maximal-error expansion at `ε≤1/2` with the minimum conditional dispersion over capacity-achieving input laws; Corollary 51 separately establishes the same `O(log n)` expansion for average error in that range. The stronger `O(1)` refinement has an additional support-variance condition. `ε>1/2` and zero-dispersion cases require their own hypotheses and formulas. Theorem 54 gives the AWGN expansion for `0<ε<1` with equal-power, maximal-power, and average-power constraints treated separately (the average-power log term differs); error criterion follows PPV’s stated `M*`/average-specific convention and must be named when used. The BSC/BEC refinements (Theorems 52–53) explicitly hold for maximal or average error. Do not extend the DMC `ε<1/2` expression to the other regimes by symmetry or by replacing a minimum with a maximum without checking the theorem.

CT Theorem 1 (complete six-page v2 preprint, pp. 1–4) supplies an alternative proof of local quadratic decay for a finite DMC: near the set `Π` of capacity-achieving input distributions, `I_W(p)≤C(W)−α dist₂(p,Π)²` for some `α,μ>0`. CT Theorem 3 (pp. 5–6) extends the assertion to finite linear input constraints. The constants are existential, not explicit. The proof’s substantive route is: (i) express the local Taylor expansion at a nearest capacity-achiever using a channel-dependent Hessian and a uniform third-order remainder; (ii) show every valid unit direction leaving the maximizing set has a strictly negative linear or quadratic term; (iii) prove the set of valid directions is closed using a polyhedral normal-cone characterization, so its intersection with the unit sphere is compact; (iv) take a positive minimum over directions and choose the neighborhood small enough to dominate the uniform remainder. The remainder is bounded by a convergent geometric series for `t<q_min/√|X|`, where `q_min` is the minimum mass of the optimal output law on the relevant output support.

CT must itself be presented with the following edge repairs, independently identified by the auditor: if `Π=P(X)`, the set `J` of valid directions outside `Π` is empty, so handle the inequality as trivial instead of writing `min_{d∈J}`. In the constrained proof, the affine description is `(p*+V)∩P_A`, not `V∩P_A` (vectors in `V` have coordinate sum zero). The feasible polytope must be nonempty. In the constrained case restrict the output alphabet to outputs reachable from some feasible input; merely removing outputs unreachable from every input in the original unconstrained alphabet may leave the constrained optimal output law without full support. Then redo `q_min` and the uniform Taylor estimate on that restricted support. The local quadratic-decay result repairs one proof ingredient, but it does not by itself validate every later PPV/PW normal-approximation step; those consumers still need a complete proof and all conditions checked.

## Error exponents and computability

PW §22.4* (printed pp. 430–434 / PDF pp. 455–459) explicitly says the general DMC reliability function is not known. For `R<C`, it gives random-coding and expurgated achievability bounds, sphere-packing and minimum-distance converse bounds, exact coincidence over the high-rate interval where the bounds match, and a remaining low-rate gap/open problem. Its displayed definition only defines `E(R)` when the limit exists. For `R>C`, the source states the exact exponent `sup_{−1<ρ<0}(E_0(ρ)−ρR)` with Dueck achievability and Arimoto converse. Bounds must not be called an exact reliability function when they do not coincide.

PW §5.6 (printed pp. 102–105 / PDF pp. 127–130) gives finite-alphabet alternating updates for fixed `W`: `Q_{X|Y}(x|y) ∝ P_X(x)W(y|x)` and `P_X(x) ∝ exp(Σ_y W(y|x)log Q_{X|Y}(x|y))`. It provides the computable sandwich `I(P_n,W)≤C≤sup_x D(W(·|x)∥P_nW)` and says both bounds converge monotonically to `C`, citing [113] for convergence details. The book does not prove that convergence, provide a convergence rate, specify finite-precision arithmetic or stopping tolerance, or settle zero-support conventions. A finite implementation contract should specify a full-support initialization (otherwise input letters assigned zero mass cannot revive and the upper divergence may be infinite), omit globally unreachable outputs or define the posterior arbitrarily there, define `0 log 0`, and use explicit precision/tolerance and resource limits. Those are contract requirements to develop locally, not claims proved by the source. Convergence of the gap implies eventual tolerance termination if the exact finite-iterate bounds can be evaluated, but the source supplies neither a finite-n gap bound nor a floating-point/resource guarantee.

Published probability/measure/statistics/convexity/topology/functional-analysis pages can support prerequisites; the exact page and item interfaces, plus absent suppliers, are enumerated in `supplier-map.json`. The new category still needs local proofs for channel coding statements; external citations identify literature and never replace those arguments.

Coverage limits of this lane: PW is an extensive book draft, not asserted to be the final CUP edition. Its continuous-time Gaussian discussion is a sketch. Its normal-approximation proof is a sketch and points to a corrected local lemma but not a full corrected derivation of every listed case. Reverse water-filling for stationary Gaussian sources belongs to the r4 source lane, not to the channel-water-filling result in PW Theorem 20.14.
