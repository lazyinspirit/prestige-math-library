# Independent proof constructions for scaffold repair

These are independently checked local developments, not published carriers or acceptance stamps. Natural logarithms are used unless stated otherwise.

## Reference-kernel boundary witness

Let Y={0,1}, X={0,1}, M_Y=delta_0 and P_Y=delta_1. Let M_{XY}=delta_{(0,0)} and P_{XY}=delta_{(0,1)} (coordinate order X,Y). Two versions of M(X|Y) can agree at Y=0 but be delta_0 or delta_1 at Y=1. Recombining the first with P_Y gives P_{XY}, hence conditional KL zero; recombining the second gives delta_{(1,1)}, hence conditional KL infinity. Both joint KL and marginal KL against M are infinity. Thus the extended chain-rule identity does not determine conditional KL, and reference-kernel choice is indispensable without P_Y << M_Y. This is an explicit finite counterexample, needing no RN existence.

Consumers: arbitrary-reference conditional KL; disintegration chain rule; variational conditional identities. Same-law CMI does not inherit this defect.

## Finite-sum / measure KL compatibility

For p,q on finite nonempty A with power-set sigma-algebra, if some p(a)>0=q(a), the atom {a} witnesses P not << Q and both conventions give infinity. Otherwise put r(a)=p(a)/q(a) for q(a)>0 and r(a)=0 at q(a)=0. For every subset E, sum_{a in E}r(a)q(a)=sum_{a in E}p(a), so r is an explicit RN representative. Its measure integral is exactly the finite sum over p(a)>0. No choice is used to construct this finite representative; if citing the existing general KL supplier verbatim, retain its stated AC contract. Base-b values are natural-log values divided by ln b, b>1; probabilities and code lengths must not be silently equated in different units.

## Sharp Pinsker from binary reduction

Define TV(P,Q)=sup_E |P(E)-Q(E)|, half the L1 norm when densities exist. Assume the local finite-partition KL lower bound has been established. For p,q in (0,1), let f(p)=d_Ber(p||q)-2(p-q)^2. Then f(q)=f'(q)=0 and f''(p)=1/[p(1-p)]-4>=0, hence f>=0. Extend to p=0,1 by continuity, and q=0,1 by the explicit infinity conventions. For each event E, the partition inequality gives D(P||Q)>=d_Ber(P(E)||Q(E))>=2|P(E)-Q(E)|^2. Taking the supremum proves TV<=sqrt(D/2). This proves the exact constant without assuming a maximizing event or standard-Borel sample space. If KL is in bits, TV<=sqrt((ln2)D_bits/2).

Finite-partition lower bound: if P not << Q there is nothing to prove when total divergence is infinite. Otherwise with r=dP/dQ, on every cell E with Q(E)>0 Jensen gives integral_E r ln r dQ >= P(E)ln(P(E)/Q(E)). For infinite positive integral use truncation/supporting-line Jensen, or state/prove the extended convex-integral form. Q-null cells have P mass zero. Sum finitely; the negative part of r ln r is bounded by 1/e. Do not invoke an L1-only Jensen theorem on an infinite-divergence integrand. Alternatively the scalar log tangent proof on the normalized cell density handles the integrable/extended split directly.

## Finite CMI without subtraction ambiguities

For finite X,Y,Z define P^*(x,y,z)=P_Z(z)P_{X|Z}(x|z)P_{Y|Z}(y|z). At zero P_Z choose any fixed probability vectors, which disappear after multiplication. Its finite sum is one. Every positive P(x,y,z) has positive P_Z and both conditional marginals, so P << P^*. Then I(X;Y|Z)=D(P||P^*) is finite and nonnegative. Expanding only positive joint cells yields sum_z P_Z(z)D(P_{XY|z}||P_{X|z}P_{Y|z}); zero-weight z contributes zero. KL equality shows I=0 iff each positive-weight conditional joint is a product, precisely conditional independence. All finite entropy chain rules follow by factoring positive joint probabilities and summing; zero cells contribute zero. This supplies the key equality condition needed for finite Markov DPI, rather than merely citing nonnegativity.

## One-shot DT bound and decoder ties

Let finite input/output alphabets be nonempty, W(y|x) a row-stochastic channel, P_X an input distribution, and P_Y=P_XW. Define information density i(x;y)=ln[W(y|x)/P_Y(y)] where W>0 and P_Y>0, with -infinity where W=0<P_Y; pairs outside support can be assigned consistently because neither the joint nor independent law charges a zero P_Y output. For M>=2 let a=(M-1)/2>0. Draw codewords independently from P_X. Decode to the smallest m with i(c_m;y)>ln a; output an error symbol if none. Conditional message j fails only if its own score is <=ln a, or an earlier k<j has score >ln a. Averaging the union bound over codebooks and uniform j gives

P[i(X;Y)<=ln a]+a(P_XP_Y)[i(X;Y)>ln a].

On the threshold event the independent measure equals e^{-i} times the joint measure (finite explicit sums, including zero probabilities). Thus the bound equals E_joint[min(1,a e^{-i})]. A codebook with error at most its expectation exists because finitely many possible codebooks have a finite weighted average. For M=1 simply send the sole message and decode it regardless of output; error zero. This is average error, with no maximal-error assertion. Converting to bits requires 2^{-i_bits}, not e^{-i_bits}.

## Meta-converse through the full experiment

Define beta_alpha(P,Q)=inf{E_Q T: measurable 0<=T<=1, E_P T>=alpha}; T=1 means accepting P. For a finite uniform message U, stochastic encoder E(x|u), channel W(y|x), and stochastic decoder D(uhat|y) with independent private randomization, form full laws P(u,x,y,uhat)=M^{-1}E(x|u)W(y|x)D(uhat|y) and Q with W replaced by output law Q_Y. The success indicator 1{u=uhat} has P expectation at least 1-epsilon and Q expectation exactly 1/M, because under Q the output/decoded message is independent of U. Thus beta_{1-epsilon}(P_full,Q_full)<=1/M. To reduce to (X,Y), use T(x,y)=sum_u P(U=u|X=x)D(u|y), assigning any probability vector at P_X(x)=0. This kernel is identical under P and Q; explicit finite factorization gives E_{P_XW}T>=1-epsilon and E_{P_XQ_Y}T=1/M. Therefore beta_{1-epsilon}(P_XW,P_XQ_Y)<=1/M. This proof avoids incorrectly regarding a randomized encoder as an invertible deterministic map from codeword to message.

## Finite rate-distortion Blahut: a complete convergence/gap route

Assume finite nonempty source/reproduction sets, p_i>0 after removing zero source symbols, finite real distortion d_ij>=0, finite s>=0, and K_ij=exp(-s d_ij)>0. For q in reproduction simplex set Z_i(q)=sum_j q_j K_ij>0, F(q)=-sum_i p_i ln Z_i(q), c_j(q)=sum_i p_i K_ij/Z_i(q). Minimize L(W)=I_p(W)+s sum_ij p_iW_ij d_ij over stochastic W. For any q, Gibbs' scalar KL inequality gives

G(W,q)=sum_ij p_i W_ij ln[W_ij/(q_jK_ij)] >= F(q),

with equality at W_ij=q_jK_ij/Z_i(q); zero q_j requires W_ij=0. Also G(W,q)=L(W)+D(q_W||q) whenever finite, q_W(j)=sum_i p_iW_ij. Therefore min_W L(W)=min_q F(q), attained by compactness/continuity of F and finite entropy continuity of L.

Blahut update: start q^0 strictly positive; put W^t_ij=q^t_j K_ij/Z_i(q^t) and q^{t+1}_j=q^t_j c_j(q^t)>0. Normalization follows sum_j q_j c_j=1. Alternating minimization gives F(q^{t+1})<=G(W^t,q^{t+1})=G(W^t,q^t)-D(q^{t+1}||q^t)<=F(q^t). This proves monotonicity but more is needed.

Take a minimizer q*. Differentiability gives c_j(q*)<=1, with equality for q*_j>0: the directional derivative toward vertex j is 1-c_j; on support equality follows sum_j q*_j c_j=1. For each support j, weights a_ij=p_iK_ij/Z_i(q*) sum to one. Then c_j(q^t)=sum_i a_ij Z_i(q*)/Z_i(q^t), so concavity of log yields

sum_j q*_j ln c_j(q^t) >= sum_i p_i ln[Z_i(q*)/Z_i(q^t)]=F(q^t)-F(q*).

The left side is D(q*||q^t)-D(q*||q^{t+1}). Telescoping and nonnegativity show sum_{t<T}(F(q^t)-F*)<=D(q*||q^0), hence by monotonicity F(q^{T-1})-F*<=D(q*||q^0)/T. Thus objective convergence is global, including boundary minimizers.

For a computable certificate, convexity gives F(q*)>=F(q)+gradient F(q)·(q*-q)>=F(q)+1-max_j c_j(q). Thus [F(q)+1-max c,F(q)] brackets the optimum, and the actual channel W(q) has L(W(q))=F(q)-D(q_W||q) between the optimum and F(q). Gap max c-1<=epsilon certifies epsilon optimality. Because K has a positive finite minimum, F and c are continuous on the entire compact simplex. Every cluster point of q^t is a minimizer by F convergence, hence has max c=1 by KKT. If max c failed to approach 1, compactness would give a nonoptimal-score cluster point, contradiction. Therefore for every epsilon>0 the gap criterion terminates after finitely many exact-real iterations. This gives no explicit iteration bound for the gap criterion. Uniform initialization gives objective suboptimality <=ln|Y|/T but does not by itself bound the displayed certificate iteration count.

Algorithm contract must specify exact-real oracle operations or interval-enclosed exponentials/logs with rigorous accumulated error. The proof treats fixed Lagrange parameter s, not automatic inversion to an exact requested distortion D, and not general alphabets or infinite distortion entries. s=0 is legal and yields K=1, c=1 immediately. A finite-iteration output can always be defined without an unproved stopping claim.

## k-source Slepian–Wolf with every collision class explicit

Let k>=2 be fixed, A_i nonempty finite, and X_t=(X_{1,t},...,X_{k,t}) iid with law p on product A_i. Define code rates by limsup_n n^{-1}ln M_{i,n}<=R_i, encoders f_{i,n}:A_i^n->[M_{i,n}], joint deterministic decoder, and block error tending to zero. Region is the closed nonnegative set sum_{i in S}R_i>=H(X_S|X_{S^c}) for every nonempty S subset [k]. No auxiliary alphabet bound or time-sharing theorem is needed for this proof.

For delta>0 define T_n by requiring for every nonempty S that all joint probabilities are positive and

-n^{-1}ln p(X_S^n|X_{S^c}^n) <=H(X_S|X_{S^c})+delta.

Conditional probability is the product of finite one-letter conditionals at positive-probability complement words. The finitely many single-letter conditional surprisals are bounded on p's positive support; iid weak LLN and a union bound give P(T_n)->1. For fixed complement word x_{S^c}^n of positive probability, at most exp[n(H(X_S|X_{S^c})+delta)] words x_S^n yield a tuple in T_n, since each has conditional probability at least the reciprocal of this number and their total probability is <=1.

Independently assign each sequence in every A_i^n uniformly to one of M_{i,n} bins, with all assignments mutually independent. Decode the unique tuple in T_n whose bin indices match; otherwise output a fixed tuple and count failure. For transmitted tuple x in T_n and competitor x', classify S={i:x'_i!=x_i}, a nonempty subset. This competitor has collision probability product_{i in S}M_{i,n}^{-1}; unchanged users contribute no factor. The preceding conditional count bounds the number of competitors in its class by exp[n(H(X_S|X_{S^c})+delta)]. Thus expected error is at most P(T_n^c)+sum_{S!=empty} exp[n(H(X_S|X_{S^c})+delta)]/product_{i in S}M_{i,n}. Strict inequalities with 2delta slack and M_{i,n}=ceil(exp(nR_i)) make every term vanish. Finite averaging selects deterministic assignments. Every boundary point follows by taking closure (or diagonal rate slack tending to zero after fixing sufficiently large thresholds). Integer rounding contributes at most ln2/n when R_i>=0.

Converse: Fix S. Giving X_{S^c}^n to the decoder determines all outside encoder outputs, so there is an estimator of X_S^n from (M_S,X_{S^c}^n) with error <=P_e. Conditional Fano gives H(X_S^n|M_S,X_{S^c}^n)<=ln2+P_e n ln|A_S| (valid also |A_S|=1 via the weaker ln2 bound). Therefore

n H(X_S|X_{S^c})=H(X_S^n|X_{S^c}^n)<=sum_{i in S}ln M_{i,n}+ln2+P_e n ln|A_S|.

Divide by n and take limsup. This uses finite entropy chain/conditioning inequalities plus iid conditional factorization; no unconditional-vs-conditional typicality substitution or unproved random-binning lemma is hidden. The finite conditional Fano proof must precede it, and the algorithm's random codebook is a design distribution whose existence argument does not claim efficient construction or polynomial decoding.

## Quantum relative-entropy DPI: finite-dimensional resolvent ladder

This supplies an alternative to adopting Wilde's much larger recoverability theorem as an unproved supplier. All spaces below are finite-dimensional complex inner-product spaces; states require nonzero spaces. Use ordinary positive operators A,B first, and natural logarithms. Define D(A||B)=tr[A(log A-log B)] on supp A subset supp B, with log evaluated spectrally on positive support and 0 log0=0; otherwise infinity. When A=0 set D=0. The expression tr(A log B) means compression to supp B, not multiplication by an undefined log0.

1. **Inverse compression.** If T>0 on K and V:H->K is an isometry, then V*(T+sI)^{-1}V >=(V*TV+sI)^{-1} for s>=0. Proof: for any x, put y=Vx and use the variational identity <y,C^{-1}y>=max_z[2 Re<y,z>-<z,Cz>] with C=T+sI. Restrict z to Vw; maximizing the resulting quadratic gives <x,(V*CV)^{-1}x>. The identity follows by completing the square with C^{1/2}; adapt inner-product argument order to the root first-linear convention. This is an operator-order assertion because it holds for every x.

2. **Operator Jensen for -log.** For t>0, -ln t=integral_0^infinity[(t+s)^{-1}-(1+s)^{-1}] ds, verified by evaluating integrals at finite R and taking R->infinity. Finite spectral calculus gives the same matrix identity in norm. Apply inverse compression to T+sI and subtract the scalar resolvent term (V*V=I); integrate finite cutoffs then pass to norm limits. Obtain -log(V*TV)<=V*(-log T)V. This locally proves the special operator-convex Jensen theorem actually used, without assuming a general operator Jensen supplier.

3. **Relative modular identity.** On the finite Hilbert space of endomorphisms with inner product <X,Y>=tr(XY*) (linear in X), define L_B(X)=BX and R_{A^{-1}}(X)=XA^{-1} for A,B>0. These commuting positive definite self-adjoint maps give Delta_{B,A}=L_B R_{A^{-1}}>0. Eigenoperator |b_j><a_i| has eigenvalue b_j/a_i. Hence log Delta(X)=(log B)X-X(log A), and <(-log Delta)A^{1/2},A^{1/2}>=tr[A(log A-log B)]=D(A||B). This eigenoperator proof supplies all logarithm/product commutation facts needed here.

4. **Joint convexity, positive definite case.** For finitely many A_i,B_i>0 and lambda_i>0 summing to one, put A=sum lambda_i A_i and B=sum lambda_i B_i. Define U_i(X)=sqrt(lambda_i) X A^{-1/2} A_i^{1/2}. With the stated Hilbert–Schmidt inner product its adjoint is U_i*(Y)=sqrt(lambda_i)Y A_i^{1/2}A^{-1/2}. Thus sum U_i*U_i=I, so U:X->directsum_i U_i(X) is an isometry. Direct multiplication gives U*(directsum Delta_{B_i,A_i})U=Delta_{B,A}. Also U_i(A^{1/2})=sqrt(lambda_i)A_i^{1/2}. Apply step 2 and evaluate on A^{1/2}; step 3 yields D(A||B)<=sum lambda_i D(A_i||B_i). Zero weights can be omitted explicitly.

5. **Support/regularization lemma.** For A,B>=0, D(A+epsilon I||B+epsilon I)->D(A||B) as epsilon downarrow0, allowing infinity. Diagonalize B: tr[A log(B+epsilon I)]=sum_j <b_j,A b_j>log(b_j+epsilon). If supp A subset supp B, every coefficient on ker B is zero, and remaining terms converge finitely; epsilon tr log(B+epsilon I)->0 because epsilon|log epsilon|->0 and there are finitely many eigenvalues. Meanwhile tr[(A+epsilon I)log(A+epsilon I)] converges by continuity of t log t at zero. If supp A not subset supp B, some diagonal coefficient of A on ker B is positive: zero quadratic form of a positive operator implies A^{1/2}v=0, so all zero coefficients on ker B would force ker B subset ker A. Thus the cross logarithmic trace diverges to minus infinity, while the entropy term stays finite; D tends to infinity. This also proves lower semicontinuity along this particular regularization; no global continuity at singular pairs is asserted. Regularizing every A_i,B_i by the same epsilon I and using the limit extends joint convexity to PSD pairs.

6. **Unitary invariance and partial-trace DPI.** Spectral conjugation and trace cyclicity give D(UAU*||UBU*)=D(A||B) for unitary U. If dim H_B=d>=1, define shift/phase unitaries X,Z on an ordered ONB by X e_j=e_{j+1 mod d}, Z e_j=exp(2pi i j/d)e_j. Direct matrix-unit summation shows d^{-2}sum_{a,b}(I tensor X^aZ^b)R(I tensor X^aZ^b)*=Tr_B(R) tensor I_B/d. Joint convexity plus unitary invariance bounds D of the averaged pair by D(A||B). Spectral eigenvalues directly give D(C tensor I/d||D tensor I/d)=D(C||D), including the support-inclusion/infinite cases. Thus D(Tr_B A||Tr_B B)<=D(A||B). d=1 is the identity; d=0 cannot carry a state and is excluded from this channel-use argument.

7. **CPTP DPI.** Use the actual finite Kraus/Stinespring supplier after checking its statement: a trace-preserving Kraus family K_j has sum K_j*K_j=I, so Vx=sum_j K_j x tensor e_j is an isometry and Phi(R)=Tr_environment(VRV*). The support/eigenvalue argument proves D(VAV*||VBV*)=D(A||B), with zero complement eigenvalues contributing zero. Partial-trace DPI now gives CPTP DPI. A channel with nonzero input and output has a nonempty Kraus family; pad with zero operators if necessary rather than introducing a zero-dimensional environment.

8. **Downstream consumer gates.** Subadditivity uses D(rho_AB||rho_A tensor rho_B)=S(A)+S(B)-S(AB) with support inclusion proved first: if v in ker rho_A then sum_j <v tensor e_j,rho_AB(v tensor e_j)>=0, all nonnegative summands vanish, forcing rho_AB to annihilate v tensor H_B; similarly for B. SSA applies partial-trace DPI to (rho_ABC,rho_A tensor rho_BC), obtaining I(A;BC)>=I(A;B), hence I(A;C|B)>=0 by finite spectral entropy algebra. Holevo's measurement bound applies CPTP DPI to each pair (rho_x,rho_bar), weights positive x only, and sums; prove block-diagonal cq entropy identity by listing eigenvalues p_x lambda_{x,j}. No quantum-capacity single-letter formula follows from any of these inequalities. Exact equality/recovery criteria require their own separate proof and are not implied by the ladder above.

This route is an independent derivation. It still requires actual local carriers for finite spectral calculus, Hilbert–Schmidt maps, trace cyclicity, isometry/Kraus dilation and finite matrix-unit twirling; the named steps are substantive intermediate obligations, not citations replacing proofs.

## Classical leftover hashing with side information: full finite proof

Let X,Z be finite-valued with joint law p, let H be a finite seed independent of (X,Z), and let h_H:X->[M] be two-universal: for each distinct x,x', P_H[h_H(x)=h_H(x')]<=1/M. Define K=h_H(X). Define average conditional guessing probability g(X|Z)=sum_z max_x p(x,z), including zero-mass z with contribution zero; H_min(X|Z)=-log_2 g. This is a precise classical definition, distinct from worst-case min_z entropy and from quantum conditional min-entropy. The seed is public, so the target comparison is P_{KHZ} versus U_M tensor P_{HZ}.

For p_Z(z)>0 let a_x=p(x|z), b_{h,z}(k)=sum_{x:h_h(x)=k}a_x. Finite expansion and universality give

E_H sum_k b_{H,z}(k)^2 =sum_x a_x^2 +sum_{x!=x'}a_xa_{x'}P_H[h_H(x)=h_H(x')] <=1/M+(1-1/M)sum_xa_x^2.

Hence E_H sum_k (b_{H,z}(k)-1/M)^2 <=sum_xa_x^2. Cauchy–Schwarz first over k and then over the probability weights (H,Z) gives

TV(P_{KHZ},U_M P_{HZ})=(1/2)sum_{h,z}p_H(h)p_Z(z)sum_k|b_{h,z}(k)-1/M| <=(1/2)sqrt[M sum_z p_Z(z)E_H sum_k(b_{H,z}(k)-1/M)^2] <=(1/2)sqrt[M sum_z p_Z(z)sum_xp(x|z)^2] <=(1/2)sqrt[M g(X|Z)].

The last step uses sum_x a_x^2<=max_x a_x. Therefore M=2^ell gives TV <=(1/2)2^{(ell-H_min(X|Z))/2}; output ell<=H_min-2log_2(1/(2epsilon)) guarantees TV<=epsilon when epsilon>0 and ell a nonnegative integer. A common stronger sufficient choice ell<=H_min-2log_2(1/epsilon) gives TV<=epsilon/2. State constants rather than silently swapping conventions.

All entropy and collision inequalities above are finite exact computations. This proves extraction/privacy amplification in TV, not strong secrecy in unnormalized mutual information without an additional finite-alphabet continuity/rate estimate. For growing output alphabet, TV->0 alone does not ensure I(K;HZ)->0; entropy continuity gives a bound of order delta log M+h_2(delta), so delta log M->0 suffices. Seed independence and public inclusion are load-bearing: deterministic hashing chosen after seeing X is outside this theorem. Quantum side information requires a separate cq/operator proof and cannot reuse this scalar Cauchy–Schwarz argument unchanged.

## Finite-alphabet SMB: domination and triangular ergodic lemma

For a bilateral stationary source (X_i)_{i in Z} on a finite alphabet A, set F_n=sigma(X_{-1},...,X_{-n}), p_n(a)=P(X_0=a|F_n), p_infinity(a)=P(X_0=a|F_infinity), and g_n=-ln p_n(X_0). Include n=0 with the trivial past. Finite joint atoms give explicit finite-past versions; Levy upward/martingale convergence supplies simultaneous a.s. limits p_n(a)->p_infinity(a). For every a, P(X_0=a,p_infinity(a)=0)=E[1_{p_infinity(a)=0}p_infinity(a)]=0, so g_n->g_infinity finite a.s. outside one finite union of null sets.

The load-bearing domination proof: for delta=e^{-t}, let tau_a be the first n>=0 with p_n(a)<delta. The disjoint event {tau_a=n} belongs to F_n. Hence P(X_0=a,tau_a<infinity)=sum_n E[1_{tau_a=n}p_n(a)]<=delta sum_nP(tau_a=n)<=delta. Summing a gives P(sup_n g_n>t)<=|A| e^{-t}. Therefore G=sup_n g_n is integrable, with E G<=ln|A|+1 (the singleton case also works). Dominated convergence gives L1 convergence g_n->g_infinity. This step is stronger than merely bounded martingale convergence for probabilities; -log is unbounded and must be controlled explicitly.

Triangular averaging: h_N=sup_{k>=N}|g_k-g_infinity| decreases to zero and is bounded by 2G, hence integrable. For n>N,

n^{-1}|sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(g_i-g_infinity)(T^i omega)| <=n^{-1}sum_{i<N}|g_i-g_infinity|(T^i omega)+n^{-1}sum_{i=N}^{n-1}h_N(T^i omega).

The first term tends to zero for fixed N; Birkhoff bounds the limsup by E[h_N|I]. Conditional monotone/dominated convergence makes these conditional expectations decrease to zero a.s.; taking N->infinity proves the triangular difference vanishes a.s. The same L1 conclusion follows by averaging E|g_i-g_infinity|. Chain factorization gives -ln p(X_0^{n-1})=sum_{i=0}^{n-1}g_i(T^i omega) a.s. Stationarity identifies the finite-past conditional versions at each shift; the countably many exceptional sets remain null. Thus n^{-1}[-ln p(X_0^{n-1})]->E[g_infinity|I] a.s. and in L1. Under ergodicity the limit is the constant E g_infinity. Finite entropy chain rule and Cesaro L1 convergence give n^{-1}H(X_0^{n-1})->E g_infinity.

For a one-sided stationary process, construct its bilateral stationary extension through consistent finite-dimensional laws and a countable-product probability extension theorem before applying this proof, or give an independently complete one-sided reversed-index proof. Merely writing 'by Birkhoff' skips the triangular/conditioning argument. This proof treats finite alphabet and stationarity, with ergodicity needed only to make the invariant limit constant; it does not establish countable-alphabet or AMS variants.

SMB exact existing suppliers inspected: `thm-levy-upward-convergence-of-conditional-expectations` is published, assumes AC, and gives both a.s./L1 convergence for an increasing filtration and L1 variable. `thm-kolmogorov-extension-for-standard-borel-coordinate-spaces` is published, assumes AC, and supplies unique cylinder-sigma probability for consistent laws. For a one-sided finite-alphabet stationary law, define each finite bilateral-coordinate law by shifting all indices into nonnegative integers. Stationarity makes the choice of shift irrelevant; marginal consistency follows from the original process. Extension yields a bilateral stationary law by cylinder uniqueness. Its nonnegative marginal is the original law. If the original is ergodic, so is the extension: approximate the indicator of any bilateral invariant event in L1 by finite-coordinate cylinder-simple functions; translate each approximant far enough forward to involve only nonnegative coordinates. Invariance and stationarity preserve its L1 error. The invariant indicator is therefore measurable modulo null sets in the nonnegative-coordinate sigma-algebra (choose an a.s. convergent subsequence of the L1 approximants). Its factor on the one-sided process is shift-invariant modulo null sets, so original ergodicity makes it constant a.s. This proves the needed ergodicity inheritance rather than asserting it.

## Bounded variational formula: singular and zero-density cases

For probability measures P,Q on one measurable space with the existing AC/RN KL convention,

D(P||Q)=sup_{f bounded measurable real}[E_P f-ln E_Q exp f].

Upper bound: Q_f=exp(f)Q/Z with Z=E_Qexp f in (0,infinity), because f is bounded. Q_f and Q are equivalent. If D(P||Q)=infinity the upper bound is immediate. Otherwise P<<Q, log r is P-integrable, and the bounded correction gives D(P||Q_f)=D(P||Q)-E_P f+ln Z>=0; this proves the upper bound with no infinity subtraction.

Lower bound for P not<<Q: choose measurable A with Q(A)=0<P(A) and f_t=t1_A. Then E_Qexp f_t=1 and the objective is tP(A)->infinity. For P<<Q with finite-valued nonnegative density r, let f_n=clip(ln r,-n,n), interpreting f_n=-n on {r=0}. These functions are bounded measurable. exp f_n converges Q-a.e. to r and is bounded by r+1, so dominated convergence gives E_Qexp f_n->1. Under P, r>0 a.e.; the positive parts of f_n increase to (ln r)^+, and the negative parts converge dominated by (ln r)^-, whose P-integral is finite by the existing KL well-definedness proof. Consequently E_Pf_n->D(P||Q), possibly infinity, and the objectives converge to D. This proves the full extended-real statement.

The supremum need not be attained: on a two-point alphabet, P=(1,0), Q=(1/2,1/2), D=ln2. Every finite real f gives objective f(1)-ln[(exp f(1)+exp f(2))/2]<ln2; the limit f(2)->-infinity approaches it. More generally a bounded maximizer forces P=Q_f, hence an essentially bounded likelihood ratio bounded away zero, and this condition is also sufficient by taking f=ln r. ## Finite point-to-point lossy source–channel separation

Let U_i iid on finite A, reconstruction alphabet B finite nonempty, d:A×B->[0,d_max] finite, and W a finite DMC of capacity C (nats/use). An (n,m) joint code has source encoder f:A^n->X^m and reconstruction g:Y^m->B^n; the channel has no feedback and uses W tensor m. Distortion is D_n=n^{-1}sum_i E d(U_i,hat U_i). Define bandwidth by m/n. The closed achievable (kappa,D) region is characterized by R(D)<=kappa C, for kappa>=0 and feasible D, with strict inequality giving concatenated achievability. Boundary inclusion is by operational closure or an explicit slack/diagonal convention; do not claim a fixed finite pair attains equality merely from the asymptotic formula.

Converse local chain: put D_i=E d(U_i,hat U_i). Finite entropy chain and conditioning reduction yield

I(U^n;hat U^n)>=sum_i I(U_i;hat U_i)>=sum_i R(D_i)>=n R(D_n).

The first inequality follows because H(U^n)=sum_i H(U_i), while H(U^n|hat U^n)=sum_i H(U_i|U^{i-1},hat U^n)<=sum_i H(U_i|hat U_i). The middle inequality is the definition of R for each one-letter induced reconstruction channel; the last is convexity. The no-feedback Markov chain and memorylessness yield I(U^n;hat U^n)<=I(X^m;Y^m)<=sum_j I(X_j;Y_j)<=mC. The middle inequality follows H(Y^m)<=sum_j H(Y_j) and H(Y^m|X^m)=sum_j H(Y_j|X_j), even when encoder inputs are correlated. Thus R(D_n)<=mC/n. Compact finite reproduction-channel sets prove lower semicontinuity of R by selecting convergent optimizing channels along a sequence of distortions; together with monotonicity this justifies passage to limits, including D_min endpoints, without assuming differentiability.

Achievability: choose D'<D and source rate r with R(D')<r<kappa C (or an equivalent positive-slack pair). Finite RD theorem supplies a source encoder with at most exp(nr+o(n)) indices and expected distortion <=D'+o(1). Channel coding with m≈kappa n supplies a code for at least this many indices with maximal error epsilon_m->0 (use the actual average-to-maximal expurgation lemma, not just average-error coding for nonuniform source indices). On channel success the distortion is the source code distortion; on failure the extra distortion is bounded by d_max. Hence total expected distortion <=D'+o(1)+d_max epsilon_m. Integer rounding is o(n). This explicitly explains why bounded distortion and maximal channel error suffice for the interface. For source RD endpoint or kappa=0 cases use closure and trivial optimal constant reconstruction rather than a nonexistent D'<D. This does not prove separation for unbounded distortion, correlated network sources, feedback/cost variants or distributed encoders.

## Finite entropy continuity suppliers without hidden asymptotics

The same Jordan-mixture argument gives conditional-entropy continuity with no dependence on dim B:

|S(A|B)_rho-S(A|B)_sigma|<=2delta ln d_A+(1+delta)h_2(delta/(1+delta)).

## Jointly measurable kernel densities and conditional KL integration

Let nu be a probability on a measurable base S and let K,L be probability kernels into a standard-Borel T. Put R_s=(K_s+L_s)/2, mu=nu R, P=nu K, Q=nu L. Both P,Q are dominated by 2mu. Choose joint measurable RN densities f,g and clip them to [0,2] (the clipping changes only mu-null sets). Their section integrals are jointly measurable by the kernel integration lemma. For each A in one countable determining algebra of T and each measurable base event B, the RN identities imply integral_B[integral_A f(s,t)R_s(dt)]nu(ds)=integral_B K_s(A)nu(ds). Equality of the bounded measurable integrands follows by testing their positive/negative difference events. A countable union of exceptional base null sets makes this identity hold simultaneously for all A, including T, for f and g. Measure uniqueness on the determining algebra then gives f(s,.)R_s=K_s and g(s,.)R_s=L_s off a SINGLE nu-null set. This constructs jointly measurable conditional densities; merely choosing an RN derivative separately in every section is insufficient.

Define psi(u,v)=u ln(u/v) when u,v>0, psi(0,v)=0 (including v=0), and psi(u,0)=infinity for u>0. It is Borel extended-real and psi(u,v)^-<=v/e. Thus C(s)=integral psi(f,g)dR_s is a measurable extended scalar with finite negative part (at most1/e) outside the exceptional set, and equals D(K_s||L_s)>=0 there. Set C=0 on the exceptional base set. The set of sections failing K_s<<L_s is measurable because it is {integral 1_{g=0}f dR_s>0}. If that set has positive nu mass, P not<<Q and both D(P||Q) and integral C dnu are infinite. Otherwise f/g, zero on g=0, is the joint RN density dP/dQ; Tonelli on positive/negative parts (global negative bound1/e) yields

D(nu K||nu L)=integral D(K_s||L_s)nu(ds).

For different base marginals nu<<beta, put alpha=dnu/dbeta and S0=nu L. Then dS0/d(beta L)=alpha(s); P=nu K is dominated by beta L iff it is dominated by S0, since P has no mass where alpha=0 and alpha is finite/positive nu-a.e. In the dominated case the density factors as alpha(s) dP/dS0. Integrate the log factorization: the alpha logarithm has negative integral<=1/e under nu, and the conditional logarithm has negative integral<=1/e by the shared-base lemma, so no infinity subtraction occurs. Obtain D(nu K||beta L)=D(nu||beta)+integral D(K_s||L_s)nu(ds). In the non-dominated case both appropriate sides are infinity. If nu not<<beta the joint and marginal divergences are already infinity; the extended equality holds for any explicitly selected L, but the conditional scalar need not be version-invariant. ## Finite stationary ergodic decomposition from actual local suppliers

Let A be finite nonempty, Omega=A^N with its product Borel sigma-algebra, T the left shift, and m stationary. Enumerate the countable algebra of finite cylinders C_j.
