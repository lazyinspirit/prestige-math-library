# Information Theory — prose scaffold

Status: independent audit and expansion in progress, 2026-10-17. This proposes twenty-four A/B pairs and the local proof contracts; it is not a production item frontier or an acceptance decision.

The course starts by fixing logarithms, finite probability vectors, zero conventions and units. It distinguishes coding entropy from general law entropy, which is defined through KL and finite quantizers, and it keeps conditional mutual information tied to the same conditioning law or to an explicitly supplied reference kernel. Subsequent code theorems name the source/channel model, error criterion, cost, distortion, and asymptotic limit at each use. Classical and quantum KL are directional and support-sensitive. Every network formula is labelled exact, inner, outer, or a scoped approximation. Quantum claims remain finite-dimensional unless an energy-constrained theorem is separately proved.

## Proposed A/B pages in dependency order

**IT01 — Finite-alphabet entropy and divergence.** The A page fixes nats/bits and finite pmfs, proves finite log-sum with equality including zero-mass coordinates, connects finite KL choice-free to measurable KL, and establishes entropy chain rule, finite conditional entropy, general-prior Fano, and finite Markov data processing. The B page calculates binary entropy and product/deterministic information, then shows KL direction and absolute-continuity failures with explicit distributions.

**IT02 — Measure-theoretic information and conditioning.** The A page defines general MI by D(P_XY||P_X tensor P_Y), general law entropy H(X)=I(X;X), finite-X conditional entropy for arbitrary measurable observations, standard-Borel CMI through the same-marginal conditional-product law, and reference-kernel conditional KL. It proves measurable kernel integration and joint conditional RN densities before chain rules, KL partition supremum/variational formula, Markov-kernel DPI and same-condition CMI DPI. The B page separates atomic, continuous and mixed laws, gives a version-dependence witness for a separately supplied kernel, and disproves DPI after coarsening the conditioning variable. Differential entropy appears only in later density/moment classes.

**IT15 — Method of types and finite large deviations.** The A page defines empirical types and proves type-class cardinality, exact probability, and multinomial-mode bounds before stating finite Sanov on the support face of the source law. Its liminf uses relative-interior points and its limsup uses relative closure; equality requires a continuity set. The B page works a binary type count and a Bernoulli tail, and shows the polynomial type factor prevents an exact finite-n exponential formula.

**IT16 — F-divergences, Rényi information, and hypothesis testing.** The A page supplies singular-aware f-divergence perspectives, finite Rényi endpoint/support conventions, its KL limit, a beta testing function with acceptance orientation, the finite Stein exponent and likelihood-ratio strong testing converse. It reuses exact general KL/Pinsker and Neyman–Pearson suppliers without broadening their AC hypotheses. The B page computes binary Rényi and testing curves and gives a support counterexample to careless order monotonicity.

**IT03 — Prefix codes and finite lossless coding.** The A page defines a finite source code as a concatenation map, separates unique decodability from prefix-freeness, proves McMillan by concatenated-word counting, then proves Shannon length bounds and Huffman optimality with fixed tie rules and |A|-1 merges. It keeps ideal-real arithmetic intervals separate from an integer finite-register coder with a known input-length counter bound, widened range arithmetic, EOS and decoder synchronization. The B page traces exact Kraft-tight/Huffman examples and gives a UD nonprefix code and a code whose Kraft sum does not prove that the given words are UD.

**IT04 — IID lossless source coding and the AEP.** The A page defines a finite IID source and information-typical set, proves the finite AEP by the law of large numbers, and establishes fixed-length achievability and converse with a separate rate slack so correct typical words vanish below H. It distinguishes fixed-length vanishing block error from variable length and zero error. The B page calculates Bernoulli typical counts and gives a block process with no assumed normalized information limit outside IID/stationary hypotheses.

**IT05 — Stationary ergodic sources and universal coding.** The A page begins with stationary/ergodic/AMS laws and entropy rate, proves Fekete’s stationary entropy limit, the finite-alphabet SMB theorem, a local finite stationary ergodic decomposition and entropy-rate affinity, then the componentwise nonergodic SMB and corrected AMS transfer using asymptotic/tail domination rather than false full-law AC. It proves fixed-length stationary-ergodic source coding and separates three Lempel–Ziv conclusions: expected LZ78 length for stationary finite alphabets, Ziv–Lempel individual-sequence finite-state optimality, and Kontoyiannis’ overlapping-window LZ77 match-length theorem for stationary ergodic sources. A separate finite-input/window LZ77 encoder and overlapping-copy decoder have explicit EOS framing and a complete correctness/termination proof; the asymptotic match statistic alone does not establish their rate. The B page compares IID/Markov rates, exhibits a nonergodic component mixture, and traces an EOS LZ78 parse.

**IT06 — Rate distortion and lossy source coding.** The A page defines single-letter and block distortion, operational expected-distortion rate and information RDF; proves finite-alphabet compactness/attainment, convexity, converse and random-code achievability; and gives finite Hamming and Gaussian squared-error formulas. It adds the bounded-distortion stationary-ergodic theorem only with the source’s process-limit and block-approximation obligations. Finite Blahut–Arimoto specifies full support, positive row normalizers, the alternating minimization objective, KL telescope, log-gamma certificate and finite positive-tolerance stopping. The B page computes a binary RDF point, a finite Gaussian waterline, and a discrete reconstruction counterexample to unrestricted output-density claims.

**IT07 — Discrete memoryless channel capacity.** The A page defines arbitrary sequence kernels before the finite DMC, then block codes, average/maximal error, capacity, converse and random-coding achievability. It proves the finite-DMC maximum mutual information formula and confines the strong converse to finite-input or finite-output stationary-memoryless channels. The B page calculates BSC and BEC capacities and keeps finite-block average/maximal criteria distinct.

**IT08 — Cost-constrained, Gaussian, and feedback channels.** The A page distinguishes codeword cost from expected block-law cost, defines real/complex AWGN and differential entropy only on a justified density class, proves scalar/vector Gaussian formulas, and proves no capacity gain only for full noiseless feedback on a stationary-memoryless point-to-point channel. The B page computes a real AWGN value and active waterfilling set, and shows a finite capacity supremum may lack an attaining input.

**IT09 — Finite-blocklength bounds and reliability.** The A page defines beta with P-acceptance orientation, proves DT achievability and the average-error meta-converse, defines CAID conditional dispersion, imports the independent-array Berry–Esseen proof, and proves the finite unconstrained DMC normal approximation for fixed 0<epsilon<1/2 and V_min>0 using the KKT/output support identity, a global finite-polytope KL/Pinsker gap, proved with a direct simplex-slice residual lemma, type-uniform converse, and quantile-rate bounds. The independent audit checked the matching r3 proof note and closed this precise normal-approximation branch. Reliability expressions are called exact only where matching bounds establish exactness. The B page evaluates a finite one-shot bound and binary meta-converse and shows expected ensemble error does not imply maximal error without expurgation.

**IT17 — Point-to-point source–channel separation.** The A page defines joint source-channel code and proves asymptotic finite-memoryless lossless and bounded-distortion lossy separation. Lossy composition handles the nonuniform compressor index with a maximal-error channel code and charges at most d_max times channel error; the converse single-letterizes through finite RDF and channel capacity. The B page designs a strict-slack Bernoulli/BSC code and separates the asymptotic theorem from zero-delay and finite-block design.

**IT10 — Distributed source coding.** The A page states the finite-IID k-terminal Slepian–Wolf lower-region with every nonempty subset, proves the genie/Fano converse and independent random-binning achievability, and distinguishes Gács–Körner deterministic common part from Wyner common information. The B page works two-source corners and a correlated binary pair and demonstrates why independent binning is load-bearing.

**IT18 — Lossy source coding with side information.** The A page proves the finite-alphabet decoder-only Wyner–Ziv formula with its distortion independence and auxiliary bound; states the sufficient no-rate-loss condition explicitly: a both-terminal RDF optimizer extends to U-X-Y with Xhat=g(Y,U) and I(X;U|Xhat,Y)=0; and presents Berger–Tung as a finite multiterminal inner bound. It also states the exact WTV two-scalar normalized jointly Gaussian squared-error region without generalizing it to arbitrary sources. The B page compares binary WZ candidates, exhibits side-information rate loss, computes a Berger–Tung inner point and evaluates a WTV boundary.

**IT11 — Multiuser channel networks.** The A page proves the product-input independent-private-message DM-MAC region and develops the AWGN-MAC theorem through a separate continuous Gaussian random-coding route and physically degraded BC superposition region; it separately gives point-to-point feedback no-gain, exact Ozarow feedback AWGN MAC, Cover–Leung feedback inner bound and correlated-source CES sufficient conditions. Auxiliary cardinality reductions require the actual connected-set/support argument. The B page traces time-sharing, superposition and feedback boundaries and shows correlated-source separation failure and invalid correlated MAC inputs.

**IT19 — Relay and interference channel bounds.** The A page defines causal relay/feedback kernels and directed information, proves decode-forward and compress-forward/NNC inner bounds and the relay cut-set outer bound, and gives exact feedback/common-output two-way statements only under their named models. It preserves the sharp complete-article Gaussian multicast gap and ETW within-one-bit regime. Strong-interference formulas retain their all-product-input hypothesis; general relay capacity is a dated scope question, not a registered open problem. The B page computes an inner/outer relay gap and Gaussian cut gap and checks exact strong-interference conditions.

**IT12 — Secrecy and information privacy.** The A page separates weak, strong, equivocation, leakage, total-variation privacy and composable notions. It proves finite-DMC auxiliary wiretap capacity with resolvability, degraded simplification and classical leftover hashing under independent public seed and average conditional guessing entropy; TV extraction implies unnormalized MI only with an output-size continuity/rate condition. A finite privacy-utility model and perfect-privacy kernel criterion are separate from channel secrecy. The B page calculates a degraded wiretap value, shows weak secrecy need not be strong without normalization, and solves a small finite information-bottleneck objective.

**IT13 — Quantum information measures.** The A page reuses the published finite-dimensional QC state/channel/partial-trace interfaces under the root’s linear-first inner-product convention. It defines von Neumann entropy, support-sensitive QRE, cq states and Holevo information, proves support and block-spectrum identities, and keeps conditional entropy’s possible negativity explicit. The B page computes qubit entropy and an orthogonal ensemble, and gives a non-full-support QRE infinity example.

**IT20 — Quantum relative entropy, data processing, and strong subadditivity.** The A page derives finite QRE joint convexity/DPI from inverse compression, the resolvent integral for -log, relative modular operators, support regularization, finite twirling, and Stinespring dilation. Only then does it prove SSA, nonnegative quantum CMI, Holevo measurement bound and nonsharp entropy-continuity tools for coding converses. The B page calculates dephasing DPI and pure-state SSA and blocks the false trace-distance-to-QRE-DPI shortcut.

**IT14 — Quantum source compression and cq capacity.** The A page proves finite-dimensional iid Schumacher compression via spectral typical subspaces and proves HSW capacity for finite-input memoryless cq channels using typical projectors, packing and Holevo converse. The B page computes a qubit source rate and binary pure-state Holevo information and shows a fixed POVM need not attain chi.

**IT21 — Quantum channel capacities and entanglement assistance.** The A page states and proves regularized finite-dimensional quantum coherent-information and private-information capacity formulas, the additive single-letter entanglement-assisted capacity, and degradable/erasure cases. General single-letter quantum/private formulas are not asserted. The B page evaluates qubit erasure regimes and a degradable example and proves a fixed-input coherent-information limitation; broader general nonadditivity remains nonlogical source context.

**IT22 — Quantum key distribution and composable secrecy.** The A page defines the authenticated classical channel, insecure quantum channel, Eve transcript, abort-capable ideal resource, correctness error and abort-weighted trace-distance secrecy; it proves the simulator and sum-of-errors composition statement. It is not a finite-key rate or implementation proof. The B page computes abort weighting and demonstrates secrecy without correctness does not ensure agreement.

**IT23 — Finite quantization, Lloyd iteration, and compact companders.** The A page defines Borel nearest-center quantizers and proves the weighted centroid identity; gives exact finite-data Lloyd iteration, strict descent and finite termination, and a separate exhaustive global optimizer; then proves the restricted positive C1 compact one-dimensional compander distortion limit and Holder-optimal point density. The B page computes the uniform exact constant and a five-point suboptimal Lloyd fixed point. Broader high-rate, nonsmooth, unbounded and vector claims remain separate.

**IT24 — Finite trellises, Viterbi, and sequence decoding.** The A page defines a finite layered path problem, gives Viterbi with deterministic ties and unreachable-state handling, and proves finite termination, optimality and exact arithmetic resource bounds. A finite HMM reduction states positive evidence before posterior MAP. The B page computes an unreachable terminal and a concrete impossible concatenation of coordinate marginal MAP states.
