### ML-29 — Federated averaging, local SGD, and SCAFFOLD

**Pages:** ml02-federated-optimization / ml02-federated-optimization-examples. **Scope:** Define client objectives $F_i$ and the weighted deployment objective $f_{\rm wt}(w)=\sum_i(n_i/n)F_i(w)$; distinguish it from the equal-client objective $f(w)=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N f_i(w)$ used by the convergence theorems below. Define FedAvg as uniform client sampling, multiple local updates, and server averaging; McMahan et al.’s article is algorithm/setup plus experiments, not a general convergence proof or privacy guarantee. State local SGD separately for a finite sum $f(x)=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^n f_j(x)$: $K$ workers independently sample $i_t^k\sim{\rm Unif}\{1,\ldots,n\}$, take stochastic gradient steps, and synchronize at a declared index set $I_T$, with maximum gap $H=\operatorname{gap}(I_T)$. Under $L$-smooth, $\mu$-strongly convex $f$, uniform-sampling gradient variance $\mathbb E_i\|\nabla f_i(x)-\nabla f(x)\|^2\le\sigma^2$, second moment $\mathbb E_i\|\nabla f_i(x)\|^2\le G^2$, steps $\eta_t=4/(\mu(a+t))$, and $a>\max\{16L/\mu,H\}$, let $S_T=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}(a+t)^2$ and $\hat x_T=(KS_T)^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^K\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}(a+t)^2x_t^k$. Then
$$
\mathbb E[f(\hat x_T)-f^\star]\le\frac{\mu a^3\|x_0-x^\star\|^2}{2S_T}+\frac{4T(T+2a)\sigma^2}{\mu K S_T}+\frac{256TG^2H^2L}{\mu^2S_T}.
$$
For mini-batch size $b$, replace $\sigma^2$ by $\sigma^2/b$; the source’s variance-dominated linear-computation-speedup discussion gives $H=O(\sqrt{T/(Kb)})$ after suppressing constants depending on $L,\mu,\sigma^2,G^2$. This guarantee is for the displayed weighted average, not the last iterate or one-shot averaging. For the equal-client heterogeneous setting, define $(G,B)$-bounded gradient dissimilarity by $N^{-1}\sum_i\|\nabla f_i(x)\|^2\le G^2+B^2\|\nabla f(x)\|^2$, $B\ge1$; when each $f_i$ is convex, the source also permits $N^{-1}\sum_i\|\nabla f_i(x)\|^2\le G^2+2\beta B^2(f(x)-f^\star)$. Define $f_i$ bounded below collectively, $\beta$-smooth, and stochastic gradients $g_i(x)=\nabla f_i(x;\zeta_i)$ unbiased with within-client variance $\mathbb E\|g_i(x)-\nabla f_i(x)\|^2\le\sigma^2$; do not replace this with a variance bound across clients. In each FedAvg round sample uniformly a fixed-size subset of $S$ among $N$ clients, perform $K$ local steps of size $\eta_l$, and average updates using global step $\eta_g$. Under the source’s $(G,B)$-dissimilarity, $\beta$-smoothness, stochastic-gradient assumptions and $ \eta_g\ge\sqrt S,\ \eta_l\le[(1+B^2)32\beta K\eta_g]^{-1}$, state its strongly-convex communication rate
$$
\widetilde O\!\left(\frac{\sigma^2}{\mu KS\epsilon}+\frac{(1-S/N)G^2}{\mu S\epsilon}+\frac{\sqrt\beta G}{\mu\sqrt\epsilon}+\frac{B^2\beta}{\mu}\right),
$$
with the local functions $\mu$-strongly convex. If general-convex or nonconvex FedAvg rates are included, use their separate source A3 conditions and outputs rather than extending this line. Define SCAFFOLD with stateful client controls $c_i$, server control $c$, corrected local update $y_i\leftarrow y_i-\eta_l(g_i(y_i)-c_i+c)$, aggregate updates $\Delta x=S^{-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal S}(y_i-x)$ and $\Delta c=S^{-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal S}(c_i^+-c_i)$, then server updates $x\leftarrow x+\eta_g\Delta x$, $c\leftarrow c+(S/N)\Delta c$; its control update has the source’s Option I $c_i^+=g_i(x)$ or Option II $c_i^+=c_i-c+(K\eta_l)^{-1}(x-y_i)$. For Theorem III use Option II with gradient-consistent controls $c_i^0=\nabla f_i(x_0)$ and $c^0=N^{-1}\sum_i c_i^0$, and use the source’s equal-client objective, uniform fixed-size client sampling, $f$ bounded below, $\beta$-smooth client functions and within-client unbiased variance bound $\sigma^2$, with no gradient-dissimilarity assumption; convex regimes additionally require source A3 ($\mu$-convex $f_i$, $\mu>0$ for strong convexity and $\mu=0$ for general convexity). With $\eta_g=\sqrt S$, the locally proved sufficient local-step limits and round rates are: strongly convex,
$$ 0<\eta_l\le\min\!\left\{\frac1{81\beta K\eta_g},\frac{S}{15\mu NK\eta_g}\right\},\quad R=\widetilde O\!\left(\frac{\sigma^2}{\mu KS\epsilon}+\frac{\beta}{\mu}+\frac NS\right); $$
general convex, $\eta_l\le(81\beta K\eta_g)^{-1}$ and
$$
R=O\!\left(1+\frac{\sigma^2D^2}{KS\epsilon^2}+\frac{\beta D^2}{\epsilon}+\frac{D\sqrt{\beta NF/S}}{\epsilon}\right);
$$
nonconvex, $\eta_l\le(100K\eta_g\beta)^{-1}(S/N)^{2/3}$ and
$$
R=O\!\left(\frac{\beta\sigma^2F}{KS\epsilon^2}+\left(\frac NS\right)^{2/3}\frac{\beta F}{\epsilon}\right).
$$
For general convexity use uniform server-iterate output and the exact finite-round bound $(D^2+28\beta N\widetilde\eta^2F/S)/(\widetilde\eta R)+19\widetilde\eta\sigma^2/(KS)$; choose $\widetilde\eta=\min\{1/(81\beta),D/\sqrt{28\beta NF/S},\epsilon KS/(57\sigma^2)\}$ with zero-denominator restrictions omitted. The original source/plan headline $NF/S$ is false for the literal constant-step uniform-server output: preserve it as `fs-scaffold-general-convex-uniform-output-round-headline`, with the complete identical-quadratic counterexample, rather than silently deleting it. The source appendix permits other output weights; this refutation does not purport to rule out all alternate outputs. The nonconvex proof uses sufficient constant $100$ in place of the source’s $24$, retaining the full communication order after treating adaptive oracle noise and selection-correlated stored anchors explicitly. FedAvg uses sufficient constant $32$ in place of the source’s $6$, with the same rate order. Here $D=\|x_0-x^\star\|_2$, $F=f(x_0)-f^\star$; convex branches target expected objective suboptimality, and the nonconvex branch targets expected squared-gradient stationarity of the source output, not objective error. The local stepsize/global-scale and output convention must be kept with each branch. Output indices are independent of the optimization randomness: geometric weights in the strongly convex branch and uniform weights in the other two branches. SCAFFOLD Theorem IV’s extra benefit from bounded Hessian dissimilarity is only proved for quadratic client functions; do not generalize it to arbitrary smooth losses. Client data staying local is not a DP theorem, and weighted-count objectives do not inherit these equal-client rates without a separate weighted proof.
