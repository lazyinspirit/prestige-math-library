Conflicts and sharpenings, all resolved in favour of the current plan:

1. **Prerequisite range.** The design says "Requires: DT-2--DT-5", but the plan's exact `requires` array (and the dispatch) omits DT-4, `stable-unstable-manifolds-and-morse-smale-transversality`. The plan controls. The scaffold is therefore built so as to need no Morse--Smale transversality at all: rearrangement follows the Milnor/Pajitnov route, which is available for arbitrary gradient-like fields and perturbs the field only where an inverted pair has to be separated. This also avoids consuming DT-4's published items, which reach DT-3's `thm-global-stable-and-unstable-manifolds-are-immersed-euclidean-spaces`, a published item with the asserted-not-proved defect recorded in the design §12.7(3).
2. **Gradient convention.** Design item 2 says the adapted function's *gradient* points inward at $M_0$ and outward at $M_1$. That is the classical upward convention ($\xi(f)>0$, Milnor). The library's published DT-3/DT-5 items use the descending convention ($df(X)<0$, $X=(2u,-2v)$). The item contract states the adapted *field* as the one belonging to the descending convention (outward at $M_0$, inward at $M_1$) and records the translation "$-X$ is the classical upward field". All other items are stated internally consistently.
3. **Source locator.** The design's per-pair source matrix gives Pajitnov only as "Ch. 5, pp. 163--194". The rearrangement material used by design items 9--11 is Pajitnov **Ch. 4 §3, pp. 132--162** (separated functions, links, almost transverse gradients, Rearrangement Lemma 3.38). The coverage file records both ranges rather than the design's narrower locator.
4. **Counterexample sharpening.** The design's B item 4 ("critical levels cannot always be interchanged across a connecting trajectory") is false if read as a claim about the full rearrangement theorem, because item 22 can first destroy the connection when the dimension count allows and item 23 can then swap. The counterexample is therefore stated exactly: on the circle, with the *same* gradient-like field kept, the values of the two critical points cannot be reversed. This is exactly the role of the disjointness hypothesis in item 21 (design item 9).
5. **Gradient perturbation.** Design item 10 says "after a gradient-like perturbation". This is kept: the separation lemma 22 alters the field near the intermediate level and the conclusion of the rearrangement theorem names the altered field.

Checks made item by item: hypothesis match (compactness, regularity of levels, `0<a<b<1` making interior slabs disjoint from the boundary), direction of the flow against the library's descending convention, index conventions ($k$ = number of negative squares), endpoint cases ($k=0$, $k=n$, empty incoming/outgoing face), and the exact hypothesis under which the interchange can be applied (two clusters that are the only critical levels of the sub-triad, with disjoint compact trajectory sets). The interval $[c,c']$ of the interchange lemma is required to contain no third critical value, and the separation lemma requires the lower index to be at least the upper one, which is exactly the inversion case of the rearrangement.

Potential gaps found and closed locally, not waved at: (i) DT-5's attachment theorem is stated for a boundaryless ambient manifold, while the correspondence theorem works in a cobordism; `lem-interior-slab-handle-attachment` isolates the verbatim interior-slab argument and records that only compactness of the band is used. (ii) DT-2's genericity is stated for compact boundaryless manifolds and does not fix a boundary collar; the existence theorem is routed through the published *relative* jet-transversality theorem on the interior with a closed collar set, plus the local value-separation lemma, and the extension back to the faces is checked. (iii) Milnor's interchange theorem needs compact, disjoint trajectory sets; the lemma is stated for the sub-triad whose critical levels are exactly the two clusters and whose other ends are regular, which is the setting in which compactness holds. (iv) The 0-handle elimination needs the boundary-sum absorption $N\natural D^n\cong N$; this is not on the published pages, so it is supplied by two local lemmas (boundary-sum as a 1-handle, and disk absorption via a collar).
