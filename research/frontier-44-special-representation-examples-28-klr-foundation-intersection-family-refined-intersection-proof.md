# Refined fibre intersection and the costalk-to-stalk map

This is a proof-only component for the SAME simultaneous projective induction, authored by `/root/klr_classical_hodge_theory`. It changes no existing item interface or metadata. It proves the refined-intersection stage from the exact already-ordered induction inputs below; it does not assert that those earlier stages have received native acceptance. The semisimplicity owner retains the core theorem and its consumers.

## Statement

Let $f:X\to Y$ be a projective morphism of complex projective varieties, with $X$ smooth projective of pure complex dimension $n$, $K=Rf_*\mathbb Q_X[n]$, $P_b={}^pH^bK$, and $P=P_0$. Choose a very ample source class $\eta$, a very ample target line bundle $A$, and set $L=f^*c_1(A)$. Let $y$ be a zero-dimensional stratum, $i:\{y\}\hookrightarrow Y$, and $Z=f^{-1}(y)$. Set $A_y=H^0(i^!P)$ and $B_y=H^0(i^*P)$. Under the ordered inputs below, the canonical map $I_y:A_y\to B_y$ is the degree-zero graded refined intersection map $H^{\mathrm{BM}}_{n,0}(Z)\to H^n_0(Z)$ and is invertible. Its factorization through $H^n_0(X)$ is the class map followed by restriction. The class image is a pure Hodge substructure stable under the canonical perverse $\eta$ primitive projectors. The raw graded cup form restricts nondegenerately to it, although that raw form need not be a polarization of the entire middle graded group.

## Proof

1.1 Define induced filtrations without choosing a lift. For an additive triangulated functor $R$ on constructible complexes, let $F_bH^j(RK)$ be the image of $H^j(R,{}^p\tau_{\le b}K)\to H^j(RK)$. This definition is intrinsic. Any normalized finite splitting in [F2] identifies it with $\bigoplus_{a\le b}H^{j-a}(RP_a)$, since truncating that splitting gives the direct sum with exactly those indices and $R$ preserves finite direct sums. For a natural transformation $R\to R'$ of such functors, naturality identifies its matrix under these splittings with the direct sum of the individual maps on each $P_a[-a]$. It is therefore strict: its image intersected with $F_b$ is the sum of its images in indices $a\le b$. Apply this to open restriction, fibre restriction, supported class adjunction and their composites. No arbitrary choice of splitting changes the induced filtration or its graded maps. [F1, F2, algebra]

1.2 Let $\Sigma=\operatorname{supp}\mathcal H^0(P)$, a finite set by the perverse support inequality. If $y\notin\Sigma$, then $B_y=0$ and $A_y=0$ by the self-duality in [F1], so the claimed map is already the isomorphism of zero spaces. Assume henceforth $y\in\Sigma$. Choose a target hyperplane avoiding this set; its complement $U$ is affine and contains $\Sigma$. For every ordinary constructible sheaf $E$ supported in dimension at most $d$, $E[d]\in{}^pD^{\le0}$. Finite perverse truncations and Artin's vanishing on heart objects therefore give $H^k(U,E)=0$ for $k>d$. Apply this with $E=\mathcal H^q(P|_U)$, whose support dimension is at most $-q$. In the ordinary hypercohomology spectral sequence $E_2^{p,q}=H^p(U,\mathcal H^qP)$, all terms of total degree greater than zero vanish. The term $E_2^{0,0}=H^0(U,\mathcal H^0P)$ receives no differential because negative $p$ terms vanish and has no outgoing differential because its targets have total degree one. Hence the edge $H^0(U,P)\twoheadrightarrow H^0(U,\mathcal H^0P)=\bigoplus_{z\in\Sigma}B_z$ is surjective. The last equality uses finite-point support and exact stalk restriction; it is not an unproved affine restriction theorem. [F1, F3, algebra]

2.1 Proper base change identifies $i^!K=R\Gamma(Z,\omega_Z[-n])$ and $i^*K=R\Gamma(Z,\mathbb Q_Z[n])$. For proper $Z$, Verdier trace identifies $R\Gamma(Z,\omega_Z)$ with the finite dual of $R\Gamma(Z,\mathbb Q)$; consequently its degree-$-n$ group is the usual $H_n(Z)$ by the finite field-dual comparison. Trace is dual to ordinary pullback, so its proper class adjunction agrees with the homological class map in [F3]. Thus $H^0(i^!K)=H^{\mathrm{BM}}_n(Z)$ and $H^0(i^*K)=H^n(Z)$. Under the finite splitting, their summands are respectively $H^{-b}(i^!P_b)$ and $H^{-b}(i^*P_b)$. Costalk nonnegativity kills the first for $b>0$, whereas stalk nonpositivity kills the second for $b<0$. Consequently the source filtration has $F_0$ equal to the entire group, and the target has $F_{-1}=0$. Their grade-zero groups are exactly $A_y$ and $B_y$. The natural supported adjunction followed by ordinary adjunction is diagonal on $P_b[-b]$ by step 1.1; hence its degree-zero action is zero outside the matching grade $b=0$, and on that grade it is precisely $I_y$. At the source, the same morphism is $\omega_Z[-n]\to\omega_X[-n]=\mathbb Q_X[n]\to\mathbb Q_Z[n]$ after proper pushforward, which defines the geometric refined intersection map. This proves the claimed identification with both shifts and orientation fixed. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Put $U'=f^{-1}(U)$, which is smooth, with $Z\subset U'\subset X$ and $Z$ proper. The weight-image theorem gives equality of the actual full images $\operatorname{im}(H^n(X)\to H^n(Z))=\operatorname{im}(H^n(U')\to H^n(Z))$. The restriction maps are strict for the induced filtrations by step 1.1. Step 1.2 gives surjectivity at grade zero from $U'$; because $F_{-1}H^n(Z)=0$, this grade-zero target is the actual subspace $F_0H^n(Z)$. An element of that subspace in the full global image consequently has a preimage in $F_0H^n(X)$ by strictness. Passing to grade zero proves $r_y:H^n_0(X)=H^0(Y,P)\twoheadrightarrow B_y$. The same proof works simultaneously for the finite set. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 1.2, algebra]

4.1 The supported adjunction induces $c_y:A_y\to H^n_0(X)$. Proper duality and trace compatibility identify it with the dual of $r_y$ under the perfect local pairing $A_y\times B_y\to\mathbb Q$ and the perfect global graded Poincaré pairing. Explicitly, tracing the composite from a supported class and a global class gives the same scalar whether restriction is first applied to the global class or the supported class is first pushed into $X$; this is the adjunction/projection-formula identity. With $q_0=\epsilon(n)\int_X(-)\wedge(-)$ on the middle grade, scale the local pairing by the same nonzero $\epsilon(n)$ so that $\langle a,r_yv\rangle=q_0(c_ya,v)$. Thus $c_y$ is injective by step 3.1 and $I_y=r_yc_y$ has matrix $c_y^{\vee}q_0c_y$. This formula uses the raw graded cup form. It does not replace the geometric intersection matrix by the sign-adjusted full-Hodge polarization. [F1, F2, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 The untwisted full class image $J=\operatorname{im}(H_n^{\mathrm{BM}}(Z)(-n)\to H^n(X))$ is pure Hodge by the properly twisted class-map supplier [F3]. Its underlying vector space lies in $F_0H^n(X)$ by step 2.1 and filtered class adjunction. Projecting to $F_0/F_{-1}=H^n_0(X)$ is a Hodge morphism by the already proved Hodge filtration clause [F2], so $W_y=\operatorname{im}c_y$ is a pure Hodge substructure. This proves Hodge purity without assuming a pre-existing Hodge structure on the induced fibre perverse filtration. Also $L\,c_y=0$: the target line bundle restricts trivially to the fibre over one point; the cup/proper-pushforward projection formula gives $L\,\operatorname{cl}(a)=\operatorname{cl}(c_1(f^*A|_Z)\cap a)=0$. Equivalently use a representative target hyperplane avoiding $y$. Thus $W_y\subset\ker L\subset H^n_0(X)$. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 The canonical heart-level relative $\eta$ decomposition yields morphism projectors $e_r:P\to P$ onto the summands $\eta^r\mathcal P_{-2r}$, where $\mathcal P_{-2r}=\ker(\eta^{2r+1}:P_{-2r}\to P_{2r+2})$. Supported adjunction is natural for these genuine morphisms of $P$, so $e_rc_y=c_yH^0(i^!e_r)$ and $W_y=\bigoplus_re_rW_y$. No projector on an arbitrarily chosen global splitting of $K$ is used. The induced cohomology projectors are Hodge: their summands are kernels of Hodge $\eta$ maps on canonical graded groups, transported by injective Hodge maps $\eta^r$ and their inverses on their images. Hence each $e_rW_y$ is a sub-Hodge structure. Since the target cup operator $L$ commutes with every heart projector, the base $L$-Lefschetz centre on $\mathcal P_{-2r}$ in ordinary degree $n-2r$ identifies its first-power kernel with the double primitive $P^0_{-2r}$. Therefore $\ker L\subset H^n_0(X)$ decomposes as $\bigoplus_rE_r$, $E_r=\eta^rP^0_{-2r}$, and $e_rW_y\subset E_r$. [F2, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 The earlier double primitive HR clause makes the $E_r$ mutually $q_0$-orthogonal. For $a,b\in P^0_{-2r}$ of ordinary degree $t=n-2r$, one has $q_0(\eta^ra,\eta^rb)=\epsilon(n)\int_X\eta^{2r}a\wedge b=(-1)^rQ_t(a,b)$, because $\epsilon(n)/\epsilon(n-2r)=(-1)^r$. The Weil operator is unchanged by the type shift $(r,r)$. Thus $(-1)^rq_0$ polarizes $E_r$, including $r=0$ by the already completed middle positivity stage. Its restriction to the sub-Hodge structure $e_rW_y$ is nondegenerate by [F4]. A finite orthogonal sum of these restrictions is nondegenerate on $W_y$. Since $c_y$ is injective, the matrix identity in step 4.1 now proves $I_y$ invertible. The existing closed-support costalk/stalk criterion supplies the supported perverse summand; that criterion is applied only after invertibility has been proved. [F2, F4, step 4.1, step 6.1, algebra] ∎
