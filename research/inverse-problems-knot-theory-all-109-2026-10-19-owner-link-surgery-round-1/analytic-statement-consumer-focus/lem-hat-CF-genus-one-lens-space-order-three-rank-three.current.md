## Statement

Assume the ACω hat Floer disk-count theory for closed three-manifolds. For either orientation of $L(3,1)$, the standard genus-one pointed Heegaard diagram has three intersection generators and its $\widehat{CF}$ differential over $\mathbb F_2$ is zero. Consequently $\widehat{HF}(L(3,1);\mathbb F_2)$ has total rank three.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The standard genus-one pointed diagram for $L(3,1)$, with meridian curves intersecting transversely in three points.

[F1] The hat chain module is generated by transverse intersection points and its differential counts Maslov-index-one disks avoiding the basepoint ([[def-hat-heegaard-floer-complex-of-a-closed-three-manifold]]).

[F2] Holomorphic disk counts are finite and are determined by the admissible index-one domains ([[lem-os-disk-and-polygon-analytic-foundations]]).

[F3] The two orientations of the lens space use the corresponding reversed Heegaard diagram and the same finite rank statement ([[def-lens-space-surgery-orientation-convention]]).

## Proof

1.1 The genus-one diagram has exactly three intersections, so [F1] gives a three-dimensional chain module. The standard parallel-slope diagram divides the torus into quadrilateral regions; a Maslov-index-one Whitney disk would require a positive bigon domain, and the finite domain list must therefore be checked against the basepoint and index conventions. [F1, given, construct]

1.2 The required exclusion or mod-two cancellation of all basepoint-free index-one disks is an analytic domain-count calculation under [F2]. That calculation, including the opposite orientation case [F3], is not locally supplied here. If the differential is zero as asserted, homology has the same rank three as the chain module; the missing local Floer count keeps this claim escalated. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1]
