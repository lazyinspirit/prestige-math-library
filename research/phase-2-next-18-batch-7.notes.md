## Mathematical and dependency audit

The load-bearing proofs were checked for hypotheses, direction, well-definedness, conventions, and axiom strength.

- In the BPI proof, a parameter formula receives finitely many disjoint basic clopen supports. The OD(A,f) well-order and transfinite recursion produce an ideal maximal only in the supported definability class. If it were not prime, minimal finite support, two maximality witnesses, a finite clopen refinement, and the expansion of `1` over all Boolean truth assignments force `1` into the ideal. The finite selections do not invoke AC. The semantic model result is separated from the finite-fragment proof of `Con(ZF) -> Con(ZF+BPI+not AC)`.
- The Feferman-Levy forcing is not silently identified with Cohen forcing. A condition is a finite union of partial functions from `omega` into the successive ground ordinals. The group permutes only the `i` coordinates within each layer, and `H_m` fixes all layers below `m`. Jech's Lemma 10.7 shows that `H_m`-fixed Boolean values come from those initial layers.
- Every real name has some bounded layer support. Under ground GCH the corresponding real layer has ground size at most `aleph_{m+1}`, and the next canonical collapse map makes that ordinal countable inside the symmetric model. These are individually specified maps; no simultaneous family of arbitrary enumerations is chosen. Hence every layer is countable and their canonical countable union is all reals, while Cantor's ZF theorem keeps the union uncountable.
- Every ordinal below ground `aleph_omega` falls below a finite ground aleph and is countable. Conversely a supported name for a surjection onto ground `aleph_omega` would require too many incompatible initial-layer decisions. Thus the new `omega_1` is the old `aleph_omega`; the explicit ground-aleph sequence gives cofinality `omega`, refuting regularity and Countable Choice.
- Feferman's source construction is a transfinite ramified type and type-free hierarchy whose individual formulas use finitely many predicate symbols. The batch does not identify that hierarchy with a literal union of hereditary finite-parameter stages. Its local construction is instead the distinct tail-flip HS interpretation, where each HS name has some support `H_m`; the general symmetric-model theorem supplies ZF. For a purported nonprincipal prime ideal on `P(omega)`, the tail-complement argument isolates the relevant support and flips the whole unused tail of a fresh coordinate real. This sends the set essentially to its complement, and finite-modification invariance then contradicts propriety. A prime ideal extending `Fin` would be nonprincipal, giving the concrete failure of BPI and the Ultrafilter Lemma.
- Tachtsis's Blass construction is adequate for the local definition and Russell-set lemma. It is not adequate for `thm-blass-model-has-only-principal-ultrafilters`: the missing issue is the uniform treatment of a supported free ultrafilter on an arbitrary set, not just on `omega` or on the displayed Russell set. Consequently its finite-formalization lemma and strongest consistency corollary are also escalated.

No local dependency is circular or forward. Page membership and publication status were not treated as proof adequacy. The three escalated Blass items do not block the independent Feferman supplier showing that UFL/BPI are not ZF theorems.

All four target models are ZF inner/symmetric models; none inherits AC merely because the forcing metatheory starts in ZFC. The basic Cohen BPI proof and both tail-flip contradictions are choice-free once their forcing systems are fixed. Finite witness selection is explicitly finite.

Ground-model AC+GCH is declared on the Feferman-Levy forcing definition and the cardinal count of real-layer names. It is also declared on the finite-fragment construction lemmas and is used through reflection, forcing construction, well-ordering/cardinal arithmetic, and the source-model transfer `Con(ZF) -> Con(ZFC+GCH)`. The resulting models respectively refute AC, Countable Choice, or BPI. Every nonprovability conclusion is conditional on `Con(ZF)`; no countable-transitive-model hypothesis replaces formal consistency transfer.

The Feferman bit-flip model preserves a choice-free branch distinct from the basic Cohen model, where BPI holds. No incompatible choice and anti-choice branches are joined.
