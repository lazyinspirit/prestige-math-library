5.3.2 BQP ⊆PSPACE

A randomized classical computer can simulate any quantum circuit if we
grant the classical computer enough time and storage space. But how
much memory does the classical computer require?
Naively, since the
simulation of an n-qubit circuit involves manipulating matrices of size 2n,
it may seem that an amount of memory space exponential in n is needed.
But we will now show that the classical simulation of a quantum computer
can be done to acceptable accuracy (albeit very slowly!) in polynomial
space. This means that the quantum complexity class BQP is contained
in the class PSPACE of problems that can be solved with polynomial
space on a classical computer.
The object of the randomized classical simulation is to sample from
a probability distribution that closely approximates the distribution of
measurement outcomes for the specified quantum circuit. We will actually
exhibit a classical simulation that performs a potentially harder task —
estimating the probability p(a) for each possible outcome a of the final
measurement, which can be expressed as
