What is a ray?
It is an equivalence class of vectors that differ by
multiplication by a nonzero complex scalar. For any nonzero ray, we can
by convention choose a representative of the class, denoted |ψ⟩, that has
unit norm:
⟨ψ|ψ⟩= 1.
(2.1)

Thus states correspond to normalized vectors, and the overall phase of
the vector has no physical significance: |ψ⟩and eiα|ψ⟩describe the same
state, where |eiα| = 1.
Since every ray corresponds to a possible state, given two states |ϕ⟩, |ψ⟩,
another state can be constructed as the linear superposition of the two,
a|ϕ⟩+ b|ψ⟩. The relative phase in this superposition is physically sig-
nificant; we identify a|ϕ⟩+ b|ϕ⟩with eiα(a|ϕ⟩+ b|ψ⟩) but not with
a|ϕ⟩+ eiαb|ψ⟩.
We use the notation ⟨ψ| (Dirac’s bra notation) for a linear function (a
dual vector) that takes vectors to complex numbers, defined by |ϕ⟩→
⟨ψ|ϕ⟩.

an observable is a self-adjoint operator.
