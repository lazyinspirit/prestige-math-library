2.1
Quantum circuits with mixed states

A quantum unitary gate, g, of order k is a complex unitary matrix of size 2k ×2k. A density matrix
ρ will transform by the gate to g ◦ρ = ˜Uρ ˜U †, where ˜U is the extension of U. A Quantum circuit is a
directed acyclic graph with n inputs and n outputs. Each node v in the graph is labeled by a quantum
gate gv. The in-degree and out-degree of v are equal to the order of gv. Some of the outputs are
labeled “result” to indicate that these are the qubits that will give the output of the circuit. The wires
in the circuit correspond to qubits. An initial density matrix ρ transforms by a circuit Q to a final
density matrix Q◦ρ = gt ◦...◦g2 ◦g1 ◦ρ, where the gates gt...g1 are applied in a topological order. For
an input string i, the initial density matrix is ρ|i⟩. The output of the circuit is the outcome of applying
basic measurements of the result qubits, on the final density matrix Q ◦ρ|i⟩. Since the outcomes of
measurements are random, the function that the circuit computes is a probabilistic function, i.e. for
input i it outputs strings according to a distribution which depends on i.

2.2
Noisy Quantum Circuits

As any physical system, a quantum system is subjected to noise. The process of noise is dynamic
and depends on time. We therefore divide the quantum circuit to levels, or time steps. We permit
that qubits are input and output at different times, and we say a qubit is alive from t1 to t2 if it is
input to the circuit at t1 and output at t2. The “space time” of the noisy quantum circuit is a two
