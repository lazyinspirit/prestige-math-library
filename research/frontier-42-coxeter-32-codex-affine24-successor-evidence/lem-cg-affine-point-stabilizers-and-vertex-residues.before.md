## Statement

Let $v\in E$ and let $\mathcal A(v):=\{H\in\mathcal A_\Phi:v\in H\}$ be the finite set of affine walls through $v$. Let $J$ be the affine facet-type set from [[lem-cg-affine-alcove-separation-and-facet-types]], including one label $0_i$ for each nonempty irreducible component.

For $a\in J$, write $s_a$ for the fundamental facet reflection of type $a$ from [[lem-cg-affine-alcove-separation-and-facet-types]].

**(1) Point stabilizers and local sectors.** The subgroup $K_v:=\operatorname{Stab}_{W_a}(v)=\{g\in W_a:g(v)=v\}$ is finite and is generated by the reflections $r_H$ for $H\in\mathcal A(v)$. It acts simply transitively on the sectors at $v$, meaning the connected components of $E\setminus\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal A(v)}H$. After translating $v$ to the origin, these sectors are the chambers of the finite local reflection group on the span of the normals to the walls through $v$, times the common fixed subspace; in particular, $K_v$ has finite orbits on the sectors.

**(2) The link and vertex type.** If exactly two distinct walls pass through $v$, they are orthogonal. For each rank-two local root subsystem, the mirrors form a finite dihedral arrangement whose adjacent lines meet at angle $\pi/m$ for $m\in\{2,3,4,6\}$. Define $t(v)\subseteq J$ as the complement of the set of types of panels through $v$. The panel types through $v$ are independent of the incident alcove: they are exactly the types in $J\setminus t(v)$, and every incident alcove has exactly one panel of each such type through $v$. In particular, if $v$ is a vertex of $\overline{g(A)}$, then $J\setminus t(v)$ is the set of types of the facets of $g(A)$ containing $v$.

**(3) Rank-two boundary words.** Let $p\ne q$ be types in $J\setminus t(v)$ whose panels meet in a codimension-two face through $v$, and put $m:=\operatorname{ord}(s_ps_q)$, the finite order of the two corresponding facet reflections. The alcoves incident to that face form a cycle of length $2m$, with panel types alternating $p,q$. Writing $\sigma_p,\sigma_q$ for the canonical generators of the abstract rank-two Coxeter group $W_{pq}$ with label $m$, the word read from this cycle is $(\sigma_p\sigma_q)^m$ or $(\sigma_q\sigma_p)^m$. It is trivial in $W_{pq}$ and maps to the identity in every quotient of a Coxeter group whose matrix restricts to this rank-two submatrix. No axiom of choice is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The reduced crystallographic root system $\Phi\subset E$, the affine walls and group $W_a$, the fundamental alcove $A$, and the affine panel types $J$ from the cited items.

[F1] The roots are finite and span $E$; root reflections preserve $\Phi$, Cartan integers $B(\beta,\alpha^\vee)$ are integral, and each root line meets $\Phi$ in $\{\pm\alpha\}$ ([[def-reduced-crystallographic-euclidean-root-system]], [[def-coroot-and-dual-root-system]]).

[F2] $W_a$ is generated by all affine wall reflections and permutes the walls and alcoves ([[def-cg-affine-root-hyperplane-reflection-and-alcove]], [[lem-cg-affine-reflection-identities-and-local-finiteness]]).

[F3] $W_a$ acts by Euclidean isometries, so its elements preserve metric balls ([[def-cg-affine-root-hyperplane-reflection-and-alcove]], [[def-isometry-and-metric-embedding]]).

[F4] A facet reflection takes an alcove to the adjacent alcove across that facet ([[lem-cg-affine-alcove-separation-and-facet-types]]).

[F5] Panel types on a shared facet agree from both adjacent alcoves; every alcove in $W_a\cdot A$ has one facet of each type ([[lem-cg-affine-alcove-separation-and-facet-types]]).

[F6] Every reduced crystallographic root system has a finite Weyl group acting faithfully on its roots ([[prop-the-weyl-group-is-finite-and-acts-faithfully-on-the-root-system]]).

[F7] The Weyl group of a reduced crystallographic root system acts simply transitively on its open Weyl chambers ([[thm-the-weyl-group-acts-simply-transitively-on-weyl-chambers]], [[def-open-and-closed-weyl-chambers]]).

[F8] A positive-definite crystallographic Coxeter scaling has allowed rank-two labels $m\in\{2,3,4,6\}$ ([[lem-cg-integer-pairings-and-allowed-dihedral-labels]]).

[F9] A finite Coxeter matrix defines the presented group with relators $s^2$ and $(st)^{m(s,t)}$ for finite labels; its rank-two groups are obtained by restricting the matrix ([[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]).

[F10] A group homomorphism preserves products and the identity, so it sends every defining relator to the identity ([[def-group-homomorphism]]).

[F11] Sectors and connected components are maximal connected subsets of the relevant complements ([[def-connected-component-and-quasicomponent]]).

[F12] A finite-dimensional real vector space is not a finite union of proper linear subspaces ([[lem-finite-dimensional-space-over-an-infinite-field-is-not-a-finite-union-of-proper-subspaces]]).

[F13] The closure of the fundamental alcove is a finite product of geometric simplices ([[lem-cg-highest-root-and-fundamental-alcove]]).

[F14] The induced norm is a norm and satisfies $|B(u,w)|\le\|u\|\|w\|$ ([[def-inner-product-space]], [[def-inner-product-norm]], [[cor-inner-product-induces-a-norm]], [[thm-cauchy-schwarz-in-an-inner-product-space]]).

[F15] Open balls $B_E(x,r)$ for the induced metric are convex and form neighborhoods in the induced topology ([[def-inner-product-norm]], [[cor-inner-product-induces-a-norm]], [[def-metric-topology]], [[def-metric-ball]]).

[F16] Every real $c$ has a unique integer $n$ with $n\le c<n+1$ ([[lem-integer-part]]).

[F17] Every alcove is open and convex ([[lem-cg-affine-reflection-identities-and-local-finiteness]]).

[F18] The fundamental alcove stabilizer in $W_a$ is trivial ([[lem-cg-affine-alcove-separation-and-facet-types]]).

[F19] The reflection in the wall of a type-$a$ facet of $g(A)$ is $g s_a g^{-1}$ ([[lem-cg-affine-alcove-separation-and-facet-types]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** identify the walls through $v$ with a finite root subsystem, apply finite Weyl-chamber simple transitivity, and then read the codimension-two link in its normal plane.

1.1 Put $\Phi_v:=\{\alpha\in\Phi:B(v,\alpha)\in\mathbb Z\}$ and $V_v:=\operatorname{span}\Phi_v$. This is finite and spans $V_v$. If $\alpha,\beta\in\Phi_v$, then $s_\alpha\beta=\beta-B(\beta,\alpha^\vee)\alpha\in\Phi$ and $B(v,s_\alpha\beta)=B(v,\beta)-B(\beta,\alpha^\vee)B(v,\alpha)\in\mathbb Z$; hence $s_\alpha\beta\in\Phi_v$. The reducedness and crystallographic conditions restrict from $\Phi$, so $\Phi_v$ is a reduced crystallographic root system in $V_v$. Its root hyperplanes translated through $v$ are exactly the walls in $\mathcal A(v)$. [F1, algebra]

2.1 Suppose exactly two distinct walls through $v$ have normals $\alpha,\beta$. Their normals are not parallel, since distinct parallel hyperplanes through one point coincide. If $B(\alpha,\beta)\ne0$, then $\gamma:=s_\alpha\beta$ is a root distinct from $\pm\alpha,\pm\beta$, and $B(v,\gamma)=B(v,\beta)-B(\beta,\alpha^\vee)B(v,\alpha)\in\mathbb Z$. The wall normal to $\gamma$ through $v$ is therefore a third distinct wall, a contradiction. Hence $B(\alpha,\beta)=0$ and the two walls are orthogonal. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Let $P$ be the span of two nonparallel normals in $\Phi_v$ and set $\Psi:=\Phi_v\cap P$. This is a finite reduced crystallographic root system spanning $P$: reflections in its roots preserve $P$, and the root-system conditions restrict from step 1.1. Its Weyl group $W(\Psi)$ is finite by [F6] and acts simply transitively on its chambers by [F7]. The mirror arrangement in $P$ consists of finitely many lines through the origin, so its sectors occur cyclically and the sector-adjacency graph is connected. Let $r$ and $s$ be the reflections in the two boundary lines of one sector $D$. Each sends $D$ to its neighbor across that line. Inductively, if $h(D)$ is reached for some $h\in\langle r,s\rangle$, the reflections across its two boundary lines are $hrh^{-1}$ and $hsh^{-1}$, so both neighboring sectors are also in the orbit. Thus $\langle r,s\rangle$ is transitive on sectors; simple transitivity of $W(\Psi)$ then gives $\langle r,s\rangle=W(\Psi)$. If $m$ is the order of $rs$, the two line reflections generate a dihedral group of order $2m$. Simple transitivity therefore gives exactly $2m$ sectors, and transitivity by orthogonal maps makes their angles equal, hence each angle is $\pi/m$. Choose inward-pointing root normals $\alpha,\beta$ to the boundary lines of $D$ and put $e_1:=\alpha/\|\alpha\|$, $e_2:=\beta/\|\beta\|$, $c_1:=\|\alpha\|$, and $c_2:=\|\beta\|$. Their Gram matrix has diagonal entries $1$ and off-diagonal entry $-\cos(\pi/m)$, so it is the rank-two Coxeter form with label $m$; the scaled roots $c_1e_1=\alpha$ and $c_2e_2=\beta$ have integral Cartan numbers by [F1]. Thus these data form a positive-definite crystallographic scaling, and [F8] gives $m\in\{2,3,4,6\}$. Thus every rank-two local mirror arrangement is the stated dihedral arrangement. [F1, F6, F7, F8, step 1.1, algebra]

2.3 For $H=H_{\alpha,k}\in\mathcal A(v)$, one has $k=B(v,\alpha)$ and $r_{\alpha,k}(v+u)=v+s_\alpha(u)$. Thus, after translating $v$ to $0$, the group $R_v:=\langle r_H:H\in\mathcal A(v)\rangle$ is the Weyl group of $\Phi_v$ on $V_v$ and acts trivially on $V_v^\perp$. By [F6] it is finite, and by [F7] it acts simply transitively on the chambers of the local root arrangement; these chambers times $V_v^\perp$ are exactly the sectors at $v$. If $\Phi_v=\varnothing$, then $V_v=\{0\}$, $R_v=\{1\}$, and there is one sector, namely $E$. [F6, F7, F11, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 If $E=\{0\}$, then $\Phi=\varnothing$ by [F1], there are no walls, and $W_a$ is generated by the empty family by [F2]; hence there is one alcove and $K_v=R_v=\{1\}$. Otherwise, for every $z\in E$ there is $r_z>0$ such that the metric ball $B_E(z,r_z)$ meets only walls through $z$: for each of the finitely many roots $\alpha$, put $c:=B(z,\alpha)$ and let $\delta_\alpha:=1$ if $c$ is an integer, and $\delta_\alpha:=\min(c-\lfloor c\rfloor,\lfloor c\rfloor+1-c)>0$ otherwise. If $\|u-z\|<\delta_\alpha/(2\|\alpha\|)$, then [F14] gives $|B(u,\alpha)-c|<\delta_\alpha/2$, so $u$ lies on no integer level except the level through $z$ when $c$ is an integer. The minimum of these positive radii over the finite nonempty set $\Phi$ gives the required $r_z$. For the fixed point $v$, every local sector $S$ is an intersection of open half-spaces, so $S\cap B_E(v,r_v)$ is convex and lies in one global alcove $C_S$; since $v\in\overline S$, also $v\in\overline{C_S}$. Conversely, if $v\in\overline C$ for an alcove $C$, then $C\cap B_E(v,r_v)$ is nonempty and connected by [F17] and [F15], so it lies in one local sector $S$. The connected set $S\cap B_E(v,r_v)$ lies in $C_S$ and intersects $C\cap B_E(v,r_v)$, hence $C=C_S$. Thus local sectors at $v$ correspond bijectively to alcoves whose closures contain $v$. Now fix any alcove $C$, choose $x\in A$ and $y_0\in C$, and choose $\delta>0$ with the open ball $O:=B_E(y_0,\delta)\subset C$ by [F17]. For each root $\alpha$, [F14] gives $|B(y,\alpha)|\le|B(y_0,\alpha)|+\delta\|\alpha\|$ for all $y\in O$, so the values of $B((1-t)x+ty,\alpha)$ for $y\in O$ and $0\le t\le1$ lie in a bounded interval. Since $\Phi$ is finite and each bounded interval contains finitely many integers, only finitely many walls can meet any such segment. For each pair of distinct intersecting walls in this finite list, let $P$ be their codimension-two intersection; $x\notin P$ since $x\in A$, so $\operatorname{aff}(\{x\}\cup P)$ is a proper affine subspace. If there are any such bad subspaces, apply [F12] to their proper direction subspaces, choose a nonzero direction outside their union, and vary from a point of $O$ along a short interval in that direction, excluding the finitely many parameters that meet the bad subspaces; if there are none, take any $y\in O$. The resulting segment $[x,y]$ meets only finitely many walls, never two at the same point, and crosses each at most once. At a crossing, the local-ball construction gives a ball meeting only the crossed wall; its two sides determine alcoves whose closures share a relatively open patch of that wall, so it is a facet of each, and [F4] says its reflection carries the first alcove to the second. The resulting finite gallery expresses $C$ as an element of $W_a\cdot A$. Now take $g\in K_v$ and a sector $S$ at $v$. By step 2.3, choose $h\in R_v$ with $h(S)=g(S)$. The ball $B_E(v,r_v)$ is invariant under the isometries $g$ and $h$, so $h^{-1}g$ preserves $S$ and therefore stabilizes its unique corresponding global alcove. Every alcove is in $W_a\cdot A$, and its stabilizer is trivial by [F18] and conjugation to $A$; hence $h^{-1}g=1$. Since each generator $r_H$ fixes $v$, $R_v\le K_v$; the preceding argument gives the reverse inclusion. Thus $K_v=R_v$, proving finiteness, generation by the reflections through $v$, and simple transitivity on sectors. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F12, F14, F15, F16, F17, F18, step 2.3, choose, algebra]

4.1 If $E=\{0\}$, then $\Phi=\varnothing$ by [F1] and $J=\varnothing$ by its definition; there is one sector and one incident alcove with no facets, so the panel-type assertion is immediate. Otherwise, for an incident alcove $C$, let $I_C(v)\subseteq J$ be the types of its facets containing $v$. The sectors of the finite central arrangement of walls through $v$ are connected by galleries: choose a regular point $x$ in one sector and $y_0$ in the other, both inside the ball from step 3.1. For each pair of distinct local walls, their intersection $P$ has codimension at least two and $x\notin P$, so $\operatorname{aff}(\{x\}\cup P)$ is a proper affine subspace. If there are any such subspaces, apply [F12] to their proper direction subspaces to choose a direction outside their union, then vary from $y_0$ along a short interval in that direction inside the other open sector and ball, excluding the finitely many parameters that meet the bad subspaces; if there are none, take $y=y_0$. The ball is convex by [F15], so the segment stays in it; it crosses each local wall at most once and never crosses two at the same point. By step 3.1, these local galleries give galleries of incident global alcoves crossing only walls through $v$. At each crossing [F4] gives the adjacent alcove and [F5] preserves the type of the shared panel. Hence $I_C(v)$ is independent of $C$; call it $I(v)$ and set $t(v):=J\setminus I(v)$. This proves the panel-type clause, including the case $I(v)=\varnothing$. If $v$ is a vertex of $\overline{g(A)}$, the facets of that alcove containing $v$ are exactly its panels through $v$, so their types are $I(v)=J\setminus t(v)$. [F1, F4, F5, F12, F15, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Let $p\ne q$ lie in $I(v)$ and choose any incident alcove $C=g(A)$. By the definition of $I(v)$, its facets of types $p,q$ both contain $v$; [F13] says they meet in a codimension-two face $F$ with $v\in\overline F$. Their wall reflections are $g s_p g^{-1}$ and $g s_q g^{-1}$ by [F19], so their product has the same finite order $m$ as $s_ps_q$. At a point $u$ in the relative interior of $F$, every wall through $u$ contains $F$, so its normal lies in the two-dimensional normal plane; the p- and q-facets give two independent normals. Thus the local root subsystem has rank two. The sector-to-alcove bijection from step 3.1 identifies its $2m$ sectors with exactly the $2m$ alcoves incident to $F$. Since the closure of $C$ is a product of geometric simplices, precisely its p- and q-facets contain $u$; step 4.1 therefore shows that the panel labels around this local cycle alternate p,q. Writing $\sigma_p,\sigma_q$ for the abstract generators of $W_{pq}$, the boundary word is $(\sigma_p\sigma_q)^m$ or $(\sigma_q\sigma_p)^m$. By [F9] the first is a defining relator, and the reverse is its conjugate by $\sigma_p$; [F10] then shows every homomorphism from a Coxeter group with this rank-two restriction sends the boundary word to $1$. No axiom of choice is used: all root, chamber, wall, and word lists here are finite. [F9, F10, F13, F19, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 2.2, algebra] ∎
