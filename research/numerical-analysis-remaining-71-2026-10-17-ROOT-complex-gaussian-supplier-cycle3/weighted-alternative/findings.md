## Why the phase-modulus algebra does not close the gap

For |c|,|d|<=1, the useful identity is

|1+c conjugate(d)|^2 - |c+d|^2 = (1-|c|^2)(1-|d|^2) >=0.

It compares the magnitudes of two augmented complex covariances. It does not establish the real covariance/increment ordering needed by ordinary Slepian or Sudakov–Fernique. A comparison theorem based only on entrywise covariance magnitudes cannot be imported without additional hypotheses.

A concrete warning against a blanket distributional modulus-Slepian assertion: let Sigma+ have diagonal1 and all off-diagonal entries+1/2; let Sigma- have diagonal1 and all off-diagonal entries-1/2. Both are positive semidefinite Hermitian covariance matrices of proper circular complex Gaussian vectors. Their entrywise covariance magnitudes agree. But Sigma+ has rank3, whereas Sigma- has rank2 (its eigenvalues are3/2,3/2,0). The probabilities that all three coordinate moduli are at most t have different small-t orders: Theta(t^6) for Sigma+, Theta(t^4) for Sigma-. Thus entrywise covariance magnitudes do not determine the law of the maximum modulus. Any theorem claiming stochastic comparison solely from their ordering would give equality by applying itself in both directions and is false.

This does not disprove a specialized comparison valid for the tensor covariance kernels in the weighted target, nor does it alone rule out a comparison of expectations under stronger hypotheses. No such valid theorem/proof was located in this round.
