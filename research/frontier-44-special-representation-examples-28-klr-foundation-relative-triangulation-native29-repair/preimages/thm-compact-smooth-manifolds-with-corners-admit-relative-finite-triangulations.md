## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice as an ambient hypothesis; the finite construction and the ODE interfaces below use no additional choice, so this hypothesis is dispensable. A smooth manifold with corners here is Hausdorff and second countable, with charts in relatively open subsets of $[0,\infty)^c\times\mathbb R^{n-c}$ and transition maps smoothly extendible with invertible derivative at corner points; transitions preserve the local depth and its face germs. Let $M$ be compact. Then $M$ has a finite corner-adapted whole-star smooth triangulation. Every local corner face, and any specified finite union of such closed faces, is a subpolyhedron.

If the total boundary already has a finite piecewise smooth triangulation $f:L\to\partial M$ compatible on all local face intersections, with its differential fan giving a homeomorphism onto the union of boundary faces of the tangent orthant at each point, the triangulation extends to $M$, pointwise preserving $f$ with compatible subdivisions allowed. In the resulting finite union construction all intersections may be made full subcomplexes. The same relative conclusion applies to a fixed triangulated closed neighbourhood piece whose smaller protected subcomplex lies in its relative interior: fitting is performed away from that protected subcomplex. No extension of arbitrary incompatible boundary data is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** compact $M$ with the corner atlas in the Statement, and in the relative case the admissible boundary triangulation.

[F1] Strong approximation preserves whole-star embeddings; secants, controlled relative extension and finite fitting have full local proofs in the preceding items ([[lem-piecewise-smooth-embeddings-are-stable-under-fine-c-one-approximation]], [[lem-relative-shape-controlled-secants-approximate-smooth-simplex-maps]], [[lem-relative-smooth-triangulations-can-be-fitted-while-preserving-a-bundle-projection]]).

[F2] Under the adapted face/tangent hypotheses, small plane-preserving strong approximations preserve orthant sides and zero sets ([[lem-corner-adapted-strong-approximations-remain-in-the-same-orthant]]). Finite convex product cells have compatible triangulations ([[lem-finite-convex-cell-complexes-admit-compatible-triangulations]]).

[F3] Continuous ball maps have fixed points; smooth vector fields in coordinate neighbourhoods have unique local solutions with smooth dependence on initial values and time ([[thm-brouwer-fixed-point-theorem]], [[thm-picard-lindelof-local-existence-and-uniqueness]], [[thm-smooth-dependence-of-ode-solutions-on-parameters]]).

## Proof

1.1 If $M$ is empty take the empty complex; if it has dimension zero, compactness makes it a finite discrete set and its points are the required vertices. Otherwise choose finitely many corner charts and closed coordinate boxes $Q_i=[0,R_i]^{c_i}\times[-R_i,R_i]^{n-c_i}$ contained in them, so their smaller relative interiors cover $M$. At a point of smaller depth, shrink the chart so all the remaining corner coordinates are strictly positive and are treated as ordinary coordinates. Each box has a finite cell triangulation by [F2]; its inverse chart map $F_i:K_i\to M$ is a whole-star embedding, since it is the restriction of an invertible smooth coordinate map to an actual geometric complex. Its preimages of local corner planes are subcomplexes. In any other corner chart on an overlap, the derivative maps the corresponding tangent orthant and its faces isomorphically to the target orthant and faces: the chart transition and its inverse preserve their local sectors, and their derivatives preserve these tangent cones by taking directional limits. Thus the adapted face/tangent condition of [F2] holds for these box pieces in every chart used to fit them. [F1, F2, given, algebra]

1.2 If the boundary is empty, the nonrelative construction also gives the relative result. For a nonempty prescribed boundary, construct a piecewise smooth collar directly, including corner points. Around each boundary point choose a small corner chart and a smooth vector field whose active corner-coordinate components are strictly positive. Restrict ordinary Euclidean smooth bump functions to these orthants, extend by zero and normalize their finite sum to obtain a smooth partition of unity near the compact boundary. The weighted sum $V$ of the local fields is strictly inward along every active local face: the inward tangent cone is preserved by chart transitions and its strict interior is convex. Extend $V$ over $M$ by the same finite bump construction. In each corner chart its coefficients extend smoothly to a neighbourhood in $\mathbb R^n$, so [F3] solves the local ODE there; uniqueness makes these solutions agree while in $M$. Compactness gives a common positive time for starts on $\partial M$. Write $\Phi(y,t)$ for the forward flow. It lies in the interior for $t>0$ sufficiently small, because every active boundary coordinate has positive derivative; the finite chart cover gives one time bound. [F3, algebra]

2.1 The finite fitting proof of [F1] works in these corner charts with one additional verification. In a local orthant chart, carry every simplex lying in a specified corner plane into that same plane during secant interpolation and correction extension. Those operations preserve the plane because it is affine. By [F2], sufficiently small strong errors keep each whole box piece in the same orthant, with the same face zero sets; the derivative supporting inequalities remain strict off those zero cones by the same positive-bound estimates. The changed maps can therefore be carried back through the original corner chart, without evaluating its inverse outside the chart domain. All other steps of the finite fitting induction are unchanged. On overlaps, local face germs are permuted by the chart transition, so preserving them in the active chart preserves the same geometric faces in every chart. The support margins keep the images inside the relatively open chart. Fitting the finitely many box pieces successively gives one finite complex and an embedding whose image is their union; its corner-face inverse images are subcomplexes, and intersections are full after the prescribed barycentric refinement. [F1, F2, step 1.1]

2.2 The map $\partial M\times[0,\epsilon]\to M$, $(y,t)\mapsto\Phi(y,t)$, is injective for sufficiently small $\epsilon$: if two values agree, reverse the common interior orbit by uniqueness. If the times differ, the earlier boundary start would occur at a positive time on an orbit starting at another boundary point, impossible because the forward orbit has already entered the interior and cannot hit a boundary while its inward derivatives remain positive there. Equal times give equal starting points. On the compact product it is a homeomorphism onto its image. That image contains a neighbourhood of $\partial M$: in a sufficiently small boundary chart, following $-V$ decreases every sufficiently small active face coordinate at a uniformly positive rate until the first one reaches zero, within a time bounded by a constant times the distance to the boundary. The inverse path gives its representation as $\Phi(y,t)$. Finitely many boundary charts give a uniform neighbourhood and time. The flow is smooth on each boundary face by [F3], so its composite with $f$ is smooth on each cell of $L\times[0,\epsilon]$. At a corner its differential is $(w,t)\mapsto d f(w)+tV$, where the boundary differential fan maps onto the union of orthant faces. Each orthant vector $v$ has the unique expression $v=w+tV$ with $w$ in that boundary union and $t\ge0$: take $t=\min_j v_j/V_j$ over its active corner coordinates. Hence this product differential is injective on the full tangent star and fills the orthant. The supplied admissible boundary data therefore give a whole-star corner collar. [F2, F3, step 1.2]

3.1 Verify that the union covers $M$, rather than merely lying close to it. Each initial box has a compact smaller core and these cores cover $M$. In its coordinates a sufficiently small perturbation of its map is $x\mapsto x+e(x)$ on the original box, with $\|e\|_\infty$ less than the margin of that core from the artificial box faces, and $e_j(x)=0$ on $x_j=0$ for a true corner face. For a target point $y$ in the smaller core, consider the self-map of the box $x\mapsto\operatorname{clip}_{Q_i}(y-e(x))$, clipping each coordinate to its box interval. A box is homeomorphic to a ball by radial parametrization from its centre (the ray endpoint is continuous by its finitely many facet inequalities), so [F3] supplies a fixed point $x$. Clipping at an artificial face cannot occur at a fixed point, because the error is smaller than the core margin. At a true corner face $x_j=0$, fixed-point clipping would imply $y_j-e_j(x)\le0$, hence $y_j=0$ because $e_j(x)=0$ and $y_j\ge0$; the equation $x_j+e_j(x)=y_j$ still holds. For unclipped coordinates it holds by definition. Thus $F_i'(x)=y$ and the perturbed box still contains its entire core. Choose the total fitting error below all finitely many such margins. Its union image contains the cores and equals $M$. The local differential fans are consequently homeomorphisms onto the tangent orthant. For surjectivity, take a target ray $y+t v$ in that orthant and its preimages $x_t$ under the finite union homeomorphism. They converge to the preimage $b$ of $y$; on a subsequence they lie in one closed simplex containing $b$. Its full-rank derivative gives $|x_t-b|\le C t$ by the first-order expansion and its positive least singular value. Passing to a subsequence of $(x_t-b)/t$ gives a tangent-cone vector $w$ with $dF_b(w)=v$. Injectivity is [F1]; the finite piecewise linear inverse on the resulting finite fan is continuous. Thus the fan fills the orthant, with its faces retained by step 2.1. This proves the nonrelative triangulation assertion. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1]

4.1 Triangulate the finite product cells $\sigma\times[0,2\epsilon/3]$, $\sigma\in L$, by [F2], keeping the given bottom-face subdivisions; coning untriangulated higher cells extends those subdivisions without changing their bottom parametrization. The composite $C$ with the collar flow is a whole-star embedding by step 2.2 and agrees with $f$ on its bottom. Protect the smaller collar through time $\epsilon/4$. The compact complement of the open collar through time $\epsilon/3$ lies in the smooth interior of $M$ and is covered by finitely many ordinary closed chart boxes with smaller cores; choose them and all fitting stars disjoint from the protected collar. Fit these boxes and the outer collar pieces by [F1]'s supported construction. All modifications occur away from the protected region, in a boundaryless interior target, so the original $f$ remains pointwise fixed. The coverage argument of step 3.1 applies to the interior cores and to the compact intermediate collar annulus, while the protected collar is unchanged. The resulting finite union covers $M$ and is a whole-star smooth triangulation, with full intersection subcomplexes. Any fixed triangulated neighbourhood piece with a smaller protected subcomplex is handled by the same supported fitting and core-coverage argument. [F1, F2, step 3.1, step 2.2]

5.1 The constructions use only finitely many chart pieces and finite subdivisions. Their face zero sets are retained by step 2.1, and the relative construction keeps the prescribed boundary map and its face intersections by step 4.1. Thus every specified finite compatible collection of corner faces is a subpolyhedron, and all claimed relative and finiteness assertions hold. The total-boundary collar was explicitly built in steps 1.2 and 2.2; it was not inferred from the smooth-boundary collar theorem. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 2.2, step 4.1] ∎
