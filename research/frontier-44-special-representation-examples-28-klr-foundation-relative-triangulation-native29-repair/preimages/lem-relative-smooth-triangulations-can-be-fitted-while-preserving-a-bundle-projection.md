## Statement

Let $F:K\to M$ and $G:L\to M$ be whole-star smooth embeddings of finite complexes into a smooth manifold without boundary. Given any positive strong $C^1$ tolerance, there are approximations on finite subdivisions fitting in a full subcomplex whose union is a whole-star smooth embedding.

The same conclusion holds for bundle diagrams $\pi F=hr_K$, $\pi G=hr_L$ over a fixed whole-star smooth base embedding $h:D\to B$, when all relevant images are in a smooth bundle with product trivializations. The final approximations satisfy these **same** diagrams with $h$ and $r_K,r_L$ unchanged as point maps, so the bundle projection is preserved exactly. The union map has the induced affine map to $D$.

The construction may be supported in a prescribed open neighbourhood $V$ of the new piece being fitted, including only the old pieces whose images meet that neighbourhood. In particular it fixes a closed protected region $R$ pointwise whenever all newly processed pieces and their required closed stars have images in $V$, with $\overline V\cap R=\varnothing$, and the maps already fit on the pieces outside this support. This is the fixed-collar relative interface: it does not assert arbitrary pointwise fixed-side fitting without the neighbourhood/separation hypotheses. Additional local affine plane constraints are preserved if the chosen charts make them coordinate planes and the already supplied diagrams respect them. For corner targets the same construction applies when all pieces satisfy the adapted face/tangent hypotheses of [[lem-corner-adapted-strong-approximations-remain-in-the-same-orthant]] in these charts; that lemma proves every altered piece remains in its original orthant.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** finite whole-star embeddings and, in the bundle case, the supplied genuine local product diagrams in the Statement.

[F1] Whole-star embeddings are stable under strong piecewise $C^1$ approximation ([[lem-piecewise-smooth-embeddings-are-stable-under-fine-c-one-approximation]]).

[F2] There are sufficiently fine, face-consistent secant approximations, and small boundary corrections extend with controlled derivatives by $H(x)=F(x)+a(t)(H(y)-F(y))$. Zero affine components and affine planes are preserved ([[lem-relative-shape-controlled-secants-approximate-smooth-simplex-maps]]).

[F3] Finite convex cell complexes have compatible simplicial triangulations; simplexwise chain differentiation controls changes between fixed compact charts ([[lem-finite-convex-cell-complexes-admit-compatible-triangulations]], [[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]]).

[F4] All fitting, affine-base and protected-region terms have their stated meaning in [[def-relative-smooth-triangulation-and-projection-preserving-fitting]].

## Proof

1.1 First supply the Euclidean geometric fitting step explicitly. Two finite linear embedded complexes in $\mathbb R^q$ can be placed in one finite convex cell complex, even if their underlying sets differ: choose a bounding box and cut it by all affine hyperplanes defining the affine hulls and barycentric facet inequalities of their finitely many simplices. Each resulting cell is determined by choosing, for each hyperplane, one of its two weak sides or the equality; intersection activates additional equalities and is a common face. There are finitely many cells and each original simplex is a union of cells, since its equalities and weak inequalities are among the cuts. Triangulate this complex by [F3] and restrict to the two original polyhedra. Their intersection is now a subcomplex. One barycentric subdivision makes it full: any face chain whose barycentre vertices lie in the intersection has its largest face there, hence its entire simplex there. Pulling these subdivisions back through the original linear embeddings gives fitting in a full subcomplex. This proves the needed arbitrary-overlap refinement directly; a same-polyhedron common-refinement theorem is not used outside its scope. [F3, F4, algebra]

2.1 Work in $\mathbb R^q$ with a finite complex $P=Q\cup A$ and a piecewise smooth map $T$, such that $T|_Q$ and $T|_A$ are embeddings; $A$ is the new piece. Let $P_0$ contain the closed stars of every simplex meeting $T^{-1}(T(A))$, and let $P_1$ be its closed star. The inverse image of $T(A)$ is in the relative interior of $|P_0|$. The compact image of the complement of this interior is therefore a positive distance from $T(A)$; if that complement is empty, omit this distance constraint. Choose the approximation tolerance less than one quarter of that distance, and small enough for [F1] on the two separate embeddings. Apply [F2] to make $T$ secant on $P_1$ and extend this change to its star, retaining $T$ outside that star. Call the result $T'$. Every new intersection of $T'(Q)$ with $T'(A)$ lies in $P_0$, by the distance bound and triangle inequality; both maps remain embeddings. On $P_1$ the two maps are linear on a finite subdivision, so step 1.1 fits their images there. Pull back the subdivisions and extend them to the whole complexes by coning subdivided faces from original simplex barycentres, without changing $T'$. All intersections are in the linearized region, so this fits the entire images. [F1, F2, step 1.1]

3.1 The union is a whole-star embedding, not merely a homeomorphism. At a point in the intersection, a neighbourhood in the union comes from $P_1$ and its image is an actual geometric linear complex. Its differential is the same affine linear realization translated to that point, hence is injective on the whole tangent star. At other points one is locally in a single old complex, which is an embedding by [F1]. The map from the glued finite union realization is continuous and bijective onto the union image; compactness makes it a homeomorphism. The closed-star supports in step 2.1 can be confined to any prescribed open neighbourhood of the intersection by preliminary sufficiently fine subdivision: continuity on compact pieces supplies a positive chart/support margin, and finitely many successive stars have arbitrarily small image diameter. Thus the alterations fix all points outside that neighbourhood. [F1, F2, F4, step 2.1]

3.2 Now use a local product $M=B\times N$ with $B\subset\mathbb R^b$, $N\subset\mathbb R^n$, and projection $p_1$. Write $T(x)=(h(r(x)),v(x))$. First suppose $h$ is affine on every base simplex reached by the affected domain simplices. A secant of $T$ then still has first coordinate $h r$, since both $r$ and $h$ are affine there and the secant has the same vertex values. Every boundary correction has zero first-coordinate component; [F2]'s explicit extension multiplies that correction by a scalar and therefore retains zero first-coordinate component. Hence every perturbation of step 2.1 preserves $p_1T=h r$ exactly. At points identified by the fit, equality of $T'$ and injectivity of $h$ give equality of $r$, so $r$ descends to the glued union and is affine on its simplices. [F2, F4, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Remove the affine hypothesis on $h$ by the actual shift construction. On a finite base subcomplex covering the affected projections and their stars, choose a secant approximation $h'$ to $h$, sufficiently close for [F1]; extend it to the required base neighbourhood by [F2]. Replace $T$ temporarily by $T_1=(h'r,v)$. This is an embedding on each original embedded piece: equality of two values gives equal $r$ by injectivity of $h'$, then equal $v$, hence equal original values. At the differential level, the first component is $d h'_{r(x)}d r_x$. Its equality on two star vectors forces equality of their $d r_x$ values by whole-star injectivity of $h'$; equality of the fibre component then gives equality under $dT_x$ and thus of the original vectors. Compactness gives the homeomorphism property. Apply step 3.2 to fit $T_1$, obtaining $T_2=(h'r,v')$. Shift back by defining $T_3=(hr,v')$. The same injectivity and differential argument proves that shifting back preserves each embedding and the embedding of their fitted union. Two points intersect under $T_3$ exactly when they intersect under $T_2$, because $h$ and $h'$ are both injective and the fibre coordinate is unchanged; the full-subcomplex identifications therefore remain identical. The first-coordinate changes cancel completely, leaving the original diagram. Choosing the temporary secant and fitting errors successively small gives the prescribed final strong error by the chain rule on finitely many compact charts. [F1, F2, F3, step 3.2, algebra]

5.1 In a general smooth target, subdivide the new complex so each closed simplex and the old pieces in its intersection neighbourhood lie in one coordinate chart. In the bundle case choose bundle product charts and base charts with the same property. Order its finitely many simplices with faces first. Begin with the old embedding, and suppose the already processed new simplices fit it in a full subcomplex and their union is an embedding. Attach the next simplex along its already processed boundary, giving $P=Q\cup A$; apply the local step 2.1, or the projection-preserving steps 3.2–4.1. The changed map on the processed new subcomplex extends to the unprocessed simplices by [F2], with arbitrarily small prescribed error; in product charts its correction has zero base component and so preserves the diagram. Choose the error at each of the finitely many stages small enough to retain previous embeddings and fit margins, with total error less than the requested tolerance. This inductively completes the fit. Unlike an infinite locally finite argument, there is no unevidenced limiting map or infinite subdivision here. [F1, F2, F3, step 3.1, step 4.1]

6.1 Every local change and its extension is supported on the closed stars used in that local construction. If those supports have images in $V$ disjoint from the protected region, the original parametrizations and diagrams on that region are untouched. Pieces already fitted outside the supports retain their identifications; the induction in step 5.1 adds only the new fitted regions. For coordinate affine-plane constraints, secants remain in the same plane, and the extension formula remains there because the correction lies in its translation space. In the bundle shift, constraints on fibre coordinate planes persist because that coordinate is unchanged by either shift. For a corner target, the stated adapted face bounds and small strong error invoke [[lem-corner-adapted-strong-approximations-remain-in-the-same-orthant]] after each plane-preserving local change; the image remains in the chart orthant before the chart inverse is applied. These facts prove precisely the relative and projection-preserving assertions under the stated separation and product-chart hypotheses. [F2, F4, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1] ∎
