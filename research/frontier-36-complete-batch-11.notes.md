## Mathematical and dependency audit

The local sign convention is $-\Delta\Phi=\delta_0$, $G=\Phi-H$, $P=-\partial_{\nu_y}G(x,y)$, with outward normals on the outer boundary and reversed normals on every excised inner sphere. The $n=2$ logarithmic limit and $n\ge3$ power limit are handled separately. The one-dimensional interval Green kernel is a B-page analogue only; the A-page Green theory assumes $n\ge2$.

- `thm-minus-laplacian-of-the-fundamental-solution-is-dirac` uses the published local-$L^1$ distribution embedding, distributional derivative/Dirac definitions, polar integration, Euclidean chart/sphere measure agreement and second Green identity. The inner-boundary flux and small-sphere remainder are explicit. For complex tests the real Green identity applies separately to real and imaginary parts.
- `thm-newtonian-potential-solves-poisson-distributionally` uses Tonelli for local $L^1$ and almost-everywhere finiteness, Fubini for the distribution pairing, the local-$L^1$ embedding, Hölder on finite-measure support for $L^p\subset L^1$, and dominated convergence for smoothness away from support. It does not claim everywhere convergence for general compact $L^1$ data. `thm-newtonian-potential-for-holder-data-is-classical` subtracts $f(x)$ before integrating $D^2\Phi$, obtains the centered-ball correction $-\delta_{ij}f(x)/n$, and uses the published classical/distributional compatibility plus injectivity of regular distributions to obtain the pointwise equation. The B Hessian counterexample records why the unsubtracted absolute integral is invalid.
- `lem-dirichlet-green-function-is-unique-and-positive` uses the published weak maximum/minimum and strong principles, with the puncture connectedness argument in $n\ge2$. `thm-green-function-symmetry` excises both poles and uses the second Green identity only after assuming the harmonic correctors have the $C^2$ closure regularity it requires. `thm-green-representation-formula` treats the inner sphere, outer Poisson term and their signs separately; positivity supplies $P\ge0$, and the constant test gives mass one. Classical Dirichlet/Neumann uniqueness and the Neumann compatibility sign use the published first Green and divergence identities.
- The punctured-ball B counterexample uses the published smooth sphere-data harmonic replacement, positivity of the ball Green function, removable singularity for a bounded harmonic difference and the weak maximum principle. It asserts failure only for the naive continuous pointwise-zero boundary definition, not for weaker potential-theoretic Green kernels. Newton's shell result uses the published spherical mean property and a local radial-harmonic argument.
