## Actual local constructions

The definition retains derivative injectivity on the entire tangent star; simplexwise immersion alone would be insufficient, exactly as Munkres8.1–8.4 explains. The stability proof constructs positive finite-star separation angles and derives both local injectivity and global separation under strong approximation.

The secant proof constructs uniformly thick subdivisions by periodic convex lattice cells and their translation-compatible triangulation, proves the derivative bound from a simplex barycentre ball, and retains a fixed subcomplex through an explicit cutoff. The boundary-correction extension proves uniform derivative estimates on coned faces without pretending those coned simplices have uniform thickness.

Fitting first proves arbitrary-overlap rectilinear refinement by cutting a bounding box by all simplex support hyperplanes. It then makes both images linear in a controlled neighbourhood of their intersection, fits and fully subdivides that intersection, and extends the correction over finitely many remaining simplex faces. In a bundle chart it shifts to a secant base map, carries the exact base coordinate through every change, and shifts back. Embedding and whole-star injectivity under both shifts are proved, rather than assumed.

The extra orthant lemma addresses the actual Munkres9.8(e) warning: affine-plane preservation alone does not imply half-plane preservation. Adapted face/tangent hypotheses give a positive normal coordinate lower bound proportional to distance from the zero subcomplex; the approximation error obeys the same distance bound. Hence small changes preserve the side and zero set.

The compact-corners theorem constructs finite chart-box triangulations, uses orthant-preserving fitting, and proves the union still covers each core by a clipped-Brouwer argument. It constructs the total-boundary collar from an inward smooth vector field; the earliest-hit coordinate formula proves the full tangent-star contract at corners. Thus the corners case is not silently borrowed from Munkres's smooth-boundary theorem.

The compact-bundle theorem starts with genuine finite product trivializations and product diagrams, fits without changing the original base point map, and proves coverage fibre by fibre. Its relative claim requires an ALREADY supplied projection-preserving diagram on a closed collar/neighbourhood of the protected piece. It does not assert arbitrary boundary-to-bundle-collar extension or use mere proper smoothness as a substitute for actual bundle charts.
