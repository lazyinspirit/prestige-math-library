## Statement

Assume AC ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]) and the uniform all-fibre-open dimension bound $d$ of [[lem-finite-fibre-dimension-gives-bounded-fibrewise-soft-resolutions]]. For a continuous map $f:X\to Y$ of locally compact Hausdorff spaces, the proper-support functor $f_!$ of [[def-derived-operations-on-analytic-sheaves-over-locally-compact-spaces]] is left exact, satisfies $(Rf_!A)_y\simeq R\Gamma_c(X_y,A|_{X_y})$ on every fibre, and maps bounded derived categories into bounded derived categories. Its exceptional inverse-image candidate $f^!$ represents the natural Hom transposition $$\operatorname{Hom}(Rf_!A,B)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(A,f^!B)$$ for bounded source objects $A$ and bounded-below target objects $B$, and has lower cohomological bound $\inf(B)-d$. When the later geometric theorem proves that $f^!$ preserves the bounded constructible subcategory, this becomes the right adjunction $Rf_!\dashv f^!$ there; on that bounded constructible specialization the adjunction unit, counit and resolution comparisons satisfy the triangle, identity and cocycle laws. The proper-support evaluation constructs the counit directly. The proof constructs this adjoint from a finite fibrewise-soft resolution and generator complexes, independently of Verdier biduality; it does not infer an unrestricted $D^+$-source adjunction from the bounded-source generator argument.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; a continuous map $f:X\to Y$ of locally compact Hausdorff spaces; a field $k$; the uniform all-fibre-open cohomological dimension bound $d$; and the operations, resolution datum $k_X\to K$ and exceptional candidate of [[def-derived-operations-on-analytic-sheaves-over-locally-compact-spaces]].

[F1] The operations are those of [[def-derived-operations-on-analytic-sheaves-over-locally-compact-spaces]]: $f_!$ consists of sections whose closed support maps properly to the base, $K_U=j_{U!}j_U^*K$ for the chosen finite resolution $k_X\to K$ in degrees $0,\dots,d$, $H_K(I)(U)=\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet_Y(f_!K_U,I)$ for a bounded-below injective complex $I$ on $Y$, and $Rf_*$, $Rf_!$ are computed on bounded-below $k$-linear injective replacements ([[lem-bounded-below-complexes-admit-injective-replacements]]).

[F2] The ordinary-operations lemma gives exactness of $f^*$, the tensor–Hom adjunction, flasque acyclicity for sections over every open set, and the fact that bounded-below flasque complexes compute their derived sections: for a bounded-below complex of flasque sheaves the natural map from its ordinary section complex to the derived sections over any open is a quasi-isomorphism ([[lem-bounded-analytic-field-sheaves-have-derived-tensor-hom-and-pullback-pushforward-adjunctions]]).

[F3] Sections near a compact set are sections on it: for compact $K$ in a locally compact Hausdorff space the canonical map $\varinjlim_{U\supseteq K}\mathcal F(U)\to\Gamma(K,i^{-1}\mathcal F)$ is an isomorphism, and pairwise disjoint compact pieces of sections can be glued over a neighbourhood of their union with the zero section prescribed on a disjoint compact set ([[lem-sections-near-a-compact-set-are-sections-on-that-set]]).

[F4] A $k$-sheaf is $c$-soft when its sections over compact subsets extend to global sections; flasque implies $c$-soft, and $c$-softness is stable under restriction to open and closed subsets, extension by zero and direct sums ([[def-c-soft-sheaf]]).

[F5] Compact-support acyclicity: injective $k$-sheaves are $c$-soft; $c$-soft sheaves have $H^q_c=0$ for $q>0$; sections over compact sets extend to compactly supported global sections with support in a prescribed neighbourhood; and in a short exact sequence with $c$-soft kernel and middle term the compactly supported sections are short exact ([[lem-c-soft-field-sheaves-are-compact-support-acyclic]], [[def-compactly-supported-global-sections]]).

[F6] $\Gamma_c$ is left exact and its right derived functors form a cohomological delta functor with long exact sequences; when the space is compact $\Gamma_c=\Gamma$ and $H^q_c=H^q$ ([[def-compactly-supported-global-sections]]).

[F7] The uniform all-fibre-open bound $d$ gives every $k$-sheaf on $X$ a bounded resolution $0\to\mathcal A\to\mathcal K^0\to\dots\to\mathcal K^d\to0$ whose terms are $c$-soft on every fibre, and every bounded complex has a bounded resolution by complexes with fibrewise $c$-soft terms; the constant sheaf in particular has such a resolution ([[lem-finite-fibre-dimension-gives-bounded-fibrewise-soft-resolutions]]).

[F8] Proper maps are closed inclusions, composites and base changes of proper maps, and have compact fibres and compact-support-normal form: if $f$ is proper every section's support maps properly to the base, while properness of the support map into a locally compact Hausdorff space forces that support to be closed in the base ([[def-proper-continuous-map]], [[lem-proper-continuous-map-stability]]).

[F9] Extension by zero $j_!$ along an open inclusion is exact and satisfies $\operatorname{Hom}_X(j_!H,G)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_U(H,j^{-1}G)$; supports of sections are closed in their domain of definition and restrict compatibly ([[thm-extension-by-zero-adjunction-exactness]], [[def-extension-by-zero-abelian-sheaf]], [[def-support-of-a-sheaf-section]]).

[F10] Bounded-above complexes of abelian sheaves admit canonical bounded-above resolutions by flat sheaves with stalk-surjective quasi-isomorphism, and bounded-above flat complexes are K-flat; the construction uses the generators $j_{U!}\mathbb Z_U$ ([[lem-abelian-sheaves-admit-bounded-above-flat-resolutions]]).

[F11] The derived internal Hom of [[def-derived-hom-in-the-bounded-setting]] is computed by $\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(A,I_B)$ for bounded $A$ and bounded-below injective models $I_B$, with the cochain cone convention of [[def-derived-category-of-an-abelian-category]]; flasque $k$-sheaves are acyclic also in the abelian-group sense ([[thm-flasque-sheaves-acyclic]], [[def-flasque-sheaf]]).

[F12] In a locally compact Hausdorff space every point has an open neighbourhood with compact closure, and compact subsets of a Hausdorff space are closed; continuous images of compact sets are compact ([[thm-locally-compact-hausdorff-basics]], [[thm-compact-subset-of-a-hausdorff-space-is-closed]], [[thm-compactness-under-continuous-maps]], [[thm-closed-subspace-of-a-compact-space-is-compact]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The proper-support functor is a sheaf, additive and left exact; for proper $f$ it equals $f_*$, for an open inclusion it is extension by zero, and for a closed inclusion it is direct image. For a sheaf $A$ and open $V\subseteq Y$ let $f_!A(V)$ be the set of $s\in A(f^{-1}V)$ whose support maps properly to $V$, with restrictions inherited from $A$; these are well defined because for open $W\subseteq V$ the support of $s|$ is $\operatorname{Supp}(s)\cap f^{-1}W$ by [F9], and the preimage of a compact subset of $W$ under $\operatorname{Supp}(s)\cap f^{-1}W\to W$ is the corresponding preimage under the proper map $\operatorname{Supp}(s)\to V$. Additivity is evident from linearity of restriction and gluing. For the sheaf property let $V=\bigcup V_\alpha$ and let $s_\alpha$ be compatible sections; they glue to $s\in A(f^{-1}V)$ with $\operatorname{Supp}(s)\subseteq\bigcup_\alpha\operatorname{Supp}(s_\alpha)$ and $s|=s_\alpha$ over $f^{-1}V_\alpha$. If $L\subseteq V$ is compact, cover $L$ by finitely many open $W_1,\dots,W_n$ with compact closures $\overline{W_j}$ contained in some $V_{\alpha(j)}$, possible by [F12]; then $\operatorname{Supp}(s)\cap f^{-1}(L)\subseteq\bigcup_j\bigl(\operatorname{Supp}(s_{\alpha(j)})\cap f^{-1}(\overline{W_j})\bigr)$, a finite union of compact sets because $\overline{W_j}\subseteq V_{\alpha(j)}$ is compact and $\operatorname{Supp}(s_{\alpha(j)})\to V_{\alpha(j)}$ is proper; and $\operatorname{Supp}(s)\cap f^{-1}(L)$ is closed in $f^{-1}(L)$ by [F9], so it is a finite union of closed subsets of these compacta, hence compact by [F12]. Thus $f_!A$ is a sheaf. Kernels are computed sectionwise, so $(f_!\ker\varphi)(V)=\ker(f_!\varphi)(V)$ and $f_!$ is left exact. If $f$ is proper, every support map is proper by [F8], so $f_!=f_*$. If $f=j:U\hookrightarrow Y$ is an open inclusion, properness of $\operatorname{Supp}(s)\to V$ is equivalent to closedness of $\operatorname{Supp}(s)$ in $V$ by [F8], which is exactly the extension-by-zero condition of [F9]; if $f=i:Z\hookrightarrow Y$ is a closed inclusion then $\operatorname{Supp}(s)$ is closed in $V\cap Z$, hence in $V$, and $i_!=i_*$. [given, F8, F9, F12]

1.2 The comparison map to compactly supported fibre sections is injective. For $y\in Y$ and a sheaf $A$ on $X$, restriction of representatives defines $(f_!A)_y\to\Gamma_c(X_y,A|_{X_y})$: a germ represented by $s\in f_!A(V)$ restricts to the fibre $X_y$, its support there is $\operatorname{Supp}(s)\cap X_y$, compact because $f|_{\operatorname{Supp}(s)}$ is proper and $\{y\}$ is compact, and the class depends only on the germ. If it is zero, then $s$ vanishes near every point of $\operatorname{Supp}(s)\cap X_y$; since $\operatorname{Supp}(s)$ is closed in $f^{-1}V$ and its fibre part is compact, [F3] and [F12] let us shrink $V$ around $y$ so that the support of $s$ over the shrunk base meets no point of $X_y$, and the remaining part of the support over the shrunk base is closed and differs from $\operatorname{Supp}(s)$ by a closed set on which $s$ vanishes; hence the germ of $s$ at $y$ is zero. [given, F3, F8, F9, F12]

1.3 The comparison map is surjective, hence an isomorphism. Let $t\in\Gamma_c(X_y,A|_{X_y})$ be represented by a section near its compact support $Q=\operatorname{Supp}(t)\subseteq X_y$; by [F3] applied to the sheaf $A$ on $X$ there is a section $s_0$ of $A$ over an open neighbourhood $W$ of $Q$ restricting to $t$ near $Q$. Choose a relatively compact open neighbourhood $W_0$ of $Q$ with $\overline{W_0}$ compact inside $W$, possible by [F12], and let $C=\overline{W_0}\setminus W_0$ be the compact collar. On $C$ the fibre germs of $s_0$ are zero by construction; [F3] again, applied to the zero section on the compact set $C\cap X_y$ and the section $s_0$ on the compact set $Q$, produces a section over a neighbourhood of $\overline{W_0}$ that agrees with $s_0$ near $Q$ and vanishes near $C\cap X_y$. Every point of $C$ not on the fibre has a base-image neighbourhood missing $y$, and since $C$ is compactly covered by finitely many such, there is a base neighbourhood $Y_0$ of $y$ with $f^{-1}(Y_0)\cap C\subseteq$ the vanishing neighbourhood of the constructed section. Restricting to $W_0\cap f^{-1}(Y_0)$ and gluing with the zero section outside gives a section $s$ of $A$ over $f^{-1}(Y_0)$ whose support is contained in the fixed compact set $\overline{W_0}$; the support therefore maps to any subset properly, so $s\in f_!A(Y_0)$ represents $t$. Naturality is restriction of representatives. [given, F3, F8, F12]

2.1 Fibrewise acyclicity and the fibre formula. If $A$ is injective then $A$ is $c$-soft by [F5], and restriction of a $c$-soft sheaf to a closed subspace is $c$-soft by [F4]; a fibre $X_y=f^{-1}(\{y\})$ is closed because $\{y\}$ is closed in $Y$, so $A|_{X_y}$ is $c$-soft and hence $\Gamma_c$-acyclic in positive degrees by [F5]. An injective resolution of $A$ on $X$ restricts to a $\Gamma_c$-acyclic resolution of $A|_{X_y}$, so $(Rf_!A)_y\simeq R\Gamma_c(X_y,A|_{X_y})$ by the fibre formula of step 1.3 and exactness of stalks, which evaluates the derived functor on the fibre. The uniform bound $d$ of [F7] makes every fibre $X_y$ have $H^q_c(X_y,-)=0$ for $q>d$, so $Rf_!$ has cohomological dimension at most $d$ fibrewise and maps $D^b(X)$ to $D^b(Y)$: a bounded complex has bounded fibrewise cohomology, and the fibre formula exhibits $Rf_!$ of it as fibrewise compact-support cohomology concentrated in bounded degrees. A fibrewise $c$-soft sheaf is $f_!$-acyclic by the same stalk computation. [given, F5, F7, step 1.3, F6]

3.1 The complex $H_K(I)$ is a termwise flasque sheaf complex with lower bound $\inf(I)-d$. Let $I$ be a bounded-below injective complex on $Y$ and $K$ the finite fibrewise-soft resolution of [F7] in degrees $0,\dots,d$, with $K_U=j_{U!}j_U^*K$ for open $U\subseteq X$. For an open cover $U=\bigcup U_i$ of an open $U$, the augmented extension-by-zero Čech complex of $K_U$ is exact on stalks: at a point $x\in U$, the simplex of indices $i$ with $x\in U_i$ has the elementary contracting homotopy that adds a fixed such index, and on $U$ the complex resolves $k_U$ columnwise. Each term is a direct sum of sheaves $j_{W!}(K|_W)$, which are fibrewise $c$-soft by [F4] because $K$ is, and hence $f_!$-acyclic by step 2.1. The cycles of the augmented complex are again $f_!$-acyclic: dimension shifting along its short exact cycle sequences identifies $R^qf_!$ of a cycle with $R^{q+r}f_!$ of an earlier cycle for every $r$, and choosing $r>d$ makes the latter vanish by step 2.1; consequently $f_!$ carries the resolution to an exact complex. Since each $I^q$ is injective, $\operatorname{Hom}_Y(-,I^q)$ preserves exactness, and the first three terms give the sheaf equalizer for the presheaf $U\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet_Y(f_!K_U,I)$, so $H_K(I)$ is a sheaf complex. For $V\subseteq U$ the map $K_V\to K_U$ is monic and $f_!$ is left exact by step 1.1, so $f_!K_V\to f_!K_U$ is monic; injectivity of $I^q$ then makes the restriction maps of $H_K(I)$ surjective in every total degree, because a monomorphism is split by injectivity and finite products over the finite $K$-degree preserve surjectivity. Thus $H_K(I)$ is termwise flasque. Its degree-$n$ term is a finite product of terms $\operatorname{Hom}_Y(f_!K_U,I^{p+n})$ with $p\in[0,d]$, so it vanishes for $n<\inf(I)-d$. [given, F1, F4, F5, F7, F9, step 2.1]

4.1 The generators $E_U=j_{U!}k_U$ are resolved by $K_U$ against $f_!$, and derived sections of $H_K(I)$ are computed by the ordinary section complex. By [F9] and [F1], $\operatorname{Hom}_X(E_U,H_K(I))=\operatorname{H}^0$ of the complex $\Gamma(U,H_K(I))=\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet_Y(f_!K_U,I)$. The complex $K_U$ resolves $E_U$ by $f_!$-acyclic terms by step 3.1, so $f_!K_U$ represents $Rf_!E_U$ and the identity $\Gamma(U,H_K(I))=\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet_Y(f_!K_U,I)$ represents the derived Hom $\operatorname{RHom}_Y(Rf_!E_U,I)$. Since $H_K(I)$ is a bounded-below flasque complex by step 3.1, its ordinary section complex computes its derived sections by [F2], so $\operatorname{RHom}_X(E_U,H_K(I))\simeq\Gamma(U,H_K(I))$ and the comparison is evaluation on generators, natural in $U$ and in morphisms of generators. [given, F1, F2, F9, step 3.1]

5.1 The generator resolution of a bounded complex and the derived comparison. Let $A$ be a bounded complex of $k$-sheaves. By [F10] the descending kernel recursion produces a bounded-above complex $M$ of direct sums of generators $E_U=j_{U!}k_U$, together with a stalk-surjective quasi-isomorphism $M\to A$; the recursion is $k$-linear, and each $M^n=\bigoplus E_U$ is a direct sum of generators. The bounded complex $K$ of [F7] is K-flat over $k$ by [F2], so $K\otimes_kM\to A$ is a resolution of $A$ whose terms are direct sums of the $K_U$ and hence $f_!$-acyclic by step 3.1; applying $f_!$ computes $Rf_!A$, using that an acyclic complex with $f_!$-acyclic terms has acyclic $f_!$-image by dimension shifting past the bound $d$, truncating below any prescribed degree before a comparison. Termwise evaluation on the generator summands and additivity of $f_!$ give the chain identity $\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet_X(M,H_K(I))=\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet_Y(f_!(K\otimes_kM),I)$: on a single generator $E_U$ this is the defining identity of [F1], and direct sums on the left correspond to direct sums of $f_!K_U$ on the right while $\operatorname{Hom}$ turns them into products, which are exact over a field. Let $J$ be a bounded-below injective replacement of $H_K(I)$. Since $M\to A$ is a quasi-isomorphism and $J$ is K-injective, $\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet_X(A,J)\to\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet_X(M,J)$ is a quasi-isomorphism; by [F2] each generator comparison $\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet_X(E_U,H_K(I))\to\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet_X(E_U,J)$ is a quasi-isomorphism, and products and direct sums over $k$-vector spaces preserve these; the Hom bicomplex has bounded diagonals because $M$ is bounded above and $H_K(I),J$ are bounded below, so row comparison proves the total comparison $\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet_X(M,H_K(I))\to\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet_X(M,J)$ is a quasi-isomorphism. Combining, $\operatorname{RHom}_X(A,H_K(I))\simeq\operatorname{RHom}_Y(Rf_!A,I)$ for bounded $A$ and bounded-below injective $I$. [given, F1, F2, F7, F10, F11, step 3.1, step 4.1]

6.1 The Hom transposition, the adjoint candidate and its lower bound. Taking $I=I_B$ a bounded-below injective replacement of a bounded-below complex $B$ with $I_B^n=0$ for $n<\inf(B)$ by [F1], step 5.1 gives $\operatorname{RHom}_X(A,H_K(I_B))\simeq\operatorname{RHom}_Y(Rf_!A,I_B)$, and $H^0$ yields the natural exceptional Hom transposition $\operatorname{Hom}(Rf_!A,B)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(A,f^!B)$ for bounded $A$, with $f^!B$ represented by $H_K(I_B)$. By step 3.1 the representative has lower bound $\inf(I_B)-d=\inf(B)-d$, so $f^!$ has lower cohomological bound $\inf(B)-d$. The construction uses the chosen finite resolution and the chosen injective model; any two choices differ by the unique identity-transposition comparisons induced by the resolution comparisons of [F2], so the transposition is well defined. [given, F1, F2, step 3.1, step 5.1]

7.1 The counit and the coherence laws on the bounded constructible specialization. For each closed $Q\subseteq f^{-1}V$ with $Q\to V$ proper, the complex of $Q$-supported sections of $H_K(I)$ is $\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet_Y(f_!(K|_Q),I)$ by the exact open/closed support sequence and fibrewise acyclicity; since $Q\to V$ is proper, $f_!(K|_Q)=f_*(K|_Q)$ by [F8], and precomposing with $k_V\to f_*k_Q\to f_*(K|_Q)$, induced by the constant-section unit and the coaugmentation $k_X\to K$, gives a map to $I(V)$. These maps commute with enlargement of supports and with restriction, so they assemble to a morphism $f_!H_K(I)\to I$; the flasque terms of $H_K(I)$ are $f_!$-acyclic by step 3.1, so this is the derived counit and on every generator $E_U$ its induced Hom transposition is exactly the defining evaluation of step 4.1. When the later geometric theorem proves that $f^!$ preserves the bounded constructible subcategory, the Hom transposition becomes a genuine adjunction $Rf_!\dashv f^!$ on that specialization, and its two triangle identities follow by inverse transposition; in that category the unique identity-transposition comparisons between choices of $K$ and $I$ satisfy the identity and cocycle laws. The lemma does not infer an unrestricted $D^+$-source adjunction from this bounded-source generator argument. [given, F1, F2, F8, step 4.1, step 6.1] ∎
