## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $f:X\to Y$ be a continuous map of topological spaces and let $k$ be a field. Inverse image of sheaves of $k$-vector spaces ([[def-sheaf-on-topological-space]], [[def-inverse-image-presheaf-and-sheaf]]) is exact and $f^*\dashv Rf_*$, where $Rf_*$ is computed on bounded-below $k$-linear injective replacements ([[lem-bounded-below-complexes-admit-injective-replacements]], [[thm-injective-complexes-model-the-bounded-below-derived-category]]). For bounded complexes $A,B$ ([[def-bounded-bounded-below-and-bounded-above-complex]]) the derived tensor over $k$ is their signed total tensor ([[def-sheaf-tensor-product]]), and for bounded $A$ and bounded-below $B$ the derived internal Hom $R\mathcal Hom(A,B)$ is computed by $\mathcal Hom^\bullet(A,I_B)$ in a $k$-linear bounded-below injective model $B\to I_B$ ([[def-sheaf-hom]], [[def-derived-hom-in-the-bounded-setting]]). Flasque $k$-sheaves ([[def-flasque-sheaf]]) are acyclic for sections on every open set ([[def-sheaf-cohomology-derived-global-sections]]). The tensor-Hom adjunction and the dg associativity, evaluation and symmetry identities are natural. No bounded-constructibility claim for the internal Hom is made here; that requires the local geometric supplier of the later constructible items.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice; a continuous map $f:X\to Y$ of topological spaces; a field $k$; the category $\operatorname{Sh}_k$ of sheaves of $k$-vector spaces on a topological space, viewed as modules over the constant structure sheaf $k_X$; and the bounded-complex, derived-category and derived-Hom conventions of [[def-bounded-bounded-below-and-bounded-above-complex]], [[def-derived-category-of-an-abelian-category]] and [[def-derived-hom-in-the-bounded-setting]].

[F1] AC implies DC, so the choice-dependent replacement and resolution statements below are available ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]]).

[F2] Under AC every sheaf of $k$-vector spaces on a space embeds functorially into an injective $k$-sheaf, and iterating cokernels supplies one specific injective resolution of every such sheaf; this is supplied injective resolution data on all of $\operatorname{Sh}_k(X)$ ([[lem-ringed-space-module-sheaves-enough-injectives]], [[def-sheaf-on-topological-space]]).

[F3] Under DC a bounded-below complex in a category with enough injectives admits a termwise injective bounded-below replacement once a functorial embedding datum is supplied, and the functor $K^+(\operatorname{Inj})\to D^+$ is an equivalence: bounded-below complexes of injectives are K-injective, morphisms into them are computed without roofs, and any two bounded-below injective replacements of one complex are homotopy equivalent ([[lem-bounded-below-complexes-admit-injective-replacements]], [[thm-injective-complexes-model-the-bounded-below-derived-category]]).

[F4] For a continuous $f$ one has $(f^{-1}G)_x\cong G_{f(x)}$, naturally in $G$, and the induced maps on stalks are the corresponding maps of $G$; a sequence of sheaves is exact exactly when all its stalk sequences are. Inverse image $f^{-1}$ is left adjoint to direct image $f_*$ with a natural bijection $\operatorname{Hom}_X(f^{-1}G,F)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_Y(G,f_*F)$ ([[lem-stalk-inverse-image-sheaf]], [[thm-exactness-of-sheaves-stalkwise]], [[thm-inverse-direct-image-adjunction]], [[def-inverse-image-presheaf-and-sheaf]], [[def-direct-image-sheaf]]).

[F5] The tensor product of modules over a ringed space is the sheafification of the sectionwise tensor presheaf, sheafification preserves stalks, and the internal Hom has as its sections over an open $U$ the module morphisms over $U$ between the restricted sheaves ([[def-sheaf-tensor-product]], [[def-sheaf-hom]], [[thm-sheafification-preserves-stalks]]).

[F6] Extension by zero along an open inclusion is exact and satisfies $\operatorname{Hom}_X(j_!H,G)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_U(H,j^{-1}G)$ naturally; for the constant sheaf, $(j_{U!}k_U)(W)$ is $k$ for $W\subseteq U$ and $0$ otherwise ([[thm-extension-by-zero-adjunction-exactness]], [[def-extension-by-zero-abelian-sheaf]]).

[F7] Under DC, for an additive left exact functor carrying supplied injective resolution data the right derived functors form a cohomological delta functor with $R^0$ naturally isomorphic to the functor, and an injective object is acyclic: $R^nF(J)=0$ for $n>0$. For the sections functor at an open $U$ these derived functors are the groups compared with the published sheaf cohomology ([[thm-right-derived-functors-form-a-cohomological-delta-functor]], [[prop-positive-right-derived-functors-vanish-on-injective-objects]], [[def-sheaf-cohomology-derived-global-sections]]).

[F8] Assume AC, and let $0\to F\to G\to H\to0$ be a short exact sequence of sheaves of abelian groups with $F$ flasque. Then $G(U)\to H(U)$ is surjective for every open $U$, and $H$ is again flasque when $G$ is; flasque means that all restriction maps of sections are surjective ([[lem-flasque-kernel-lifts-quotient-sections]], [[def-flasque-sheaf]]).

[F9] A chain map is a quasi-isomorphism exactly when its cone is acyclic ([[cor-the-cone-criterion-from-the-general-long-exact-sequence]], [[def-derived-category-of-an-abelian-category]]). A short exact sequence of cochain complexes has a natural long exact cohomology sequence ([[thm-long-exact-sequence-in-cohomology]]). For a first-quadrant double cochain complex with finite diagonal sums, if a complex maps to the bottom edge compatibly and every augmented column is exact, then the map to the total complex is a quasi-isomorphism; in particular, if every column is acyclic then the total complex is acyclic ([[lem-acyclic-assembly-by-exact-columns]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The inverse image $f^*=f^{-1}$ is exact and $f^*\dashv f_*$, and $f_*$ preserves injective objects. By [F4] there is a natural isomorphism $(f^{-1}G)_x\cong G_{f(x)}$ under which the stalk of $f^{-1}\varphi$ is $\varphi_{f(x)}$; since exactness is tested stalkwise, $f^{-1}$ preserves kernels, images and cokernels and is exact. The adjunction bijection of [F4] is $k$-linear, its two directions being given on sections by the canonical $k$-linear maps $G\to f_*f^{-1}G$ and $f^{-1}f_*F\to F$; hence $f^*$ is left adjoint to $f_*$ in $\operatorname{Sh}_k$. If $I$ is injective, then $\operatorname{Hom}_Y(-,f_*I)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_X(f^*(-),I)$ is a composite of the exact functor $f^*$ and the exact representable functor $\operatorname{Hom}_X(-,I)$, so $f_*I$ is injective. [given, F4, algebra]

1.2 The tensor product has the expected stalks, and tensoring is exact in each variable. Define $A\otimes_kB$ as the sheafification of the sectionwise tensor presheaf of [F5]. By [F5] sheafification preserves stalks, so $(A\otimes_kB)_x$ is the filtered colimit $\varinjlim_U A(U)\otimes_kB(U)$ over the open neighbourhoods of $x$; since tensor products of vector spaces commute with filtered colimits, this is $A_x\otimes_kB_x$, and the stalk of $A\otimes\varphi$ is $\operatorname{id}\otimes\varphi_x$. For fixed $B$, the functor $A\mapsto A\otimes_kB$ is therefore stalkwise $A_x\mapsto A_x\otimes_kB_x$, which is exact because every $k$-vector space is flat, and exactness is stalkwise by [F4]; the same holds in the other variable. The restriction to opens is the evident one, so all these maps are compatible with restriction, and no choice is used in this step. [given, F4, F5, algebra]

1.3 Injective $k$-sheaves are flasque. Let $I$ be injective and let $U\subseteq V$ be open. The canonical map $j_{U!}k_U\to j_{V!}k_V$ of [F6] is a monomorphism of $k$-sheaves: over an open $W$ it is the identity $k\to k$ when $W\subseteq U$, and the zero map out of $0$ otherwise. By [F6], $\operatorname{Hom}_X(j_{U!}k_U,I)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_U(k_U,I|_U)\cong I(U)$ and similarly with $V$. Injectivity of $I$ extends every morphism $j_{U!}k_U\to I$, that is, every section over $U$, along this monomorphism to a morphism $j_{V!}k_V\to I$, that is, a section over $V$ restricting to it. Hence the restriction $I(V)\to I(U)$ is surjective, and $I$ is flasque by [F8]. [given, F6, F8, algebra]

2.1 The adjunction $f^*\dashv Rf_*$ holds on bounded-below derived categories. Let $F$ be a bounded-below complex on $X$ and let $j:F\to I_F$ be a bounded-below termwise injective replacement, supplied under DC by [F3]; declare $Rf_*F:=f_*I_F$, well defined up to canonical isomorphism because any two such replacements are homotopy equivalent by [F3] and $f_*$ preserves homotopy equivalences. Since $f_*$ preserves zero objects in each degree, $f_*I_F$ is bounded below, and it is termwise injective by step 1.1; hence it is K-injective by [F3], and for every bounded-below complex $G$ on $Y$ the localization identifies $\operatorname{Hom}_{D(Y)}(G,f_*I_F)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{K(Y)}(G,f_*I_F)$. The levelwise adjunction of [F4] identifies this with $\operatorname{Hom}_{K(X)}(f^*G,I_F)$, and because $I_F$ is K-injective and quasi-isomorphic to $F$, this is $\operatorname{Hom}_{D(X)}(f^*G,F)$. All identifications are natural and $k$-linear, and the triangle identities are the triangle identities of the levelwise adjunction, so $f^*\dashv Rf_*$ on $D^+$. [given, F1, F3, F4, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Flasque $k$-sheaves are acyclic for sections on every open set. Let $F$ be flasque and embed it into an injective $I$ using [F2], with quotient $Q$. Viewed as a short exact sequence of underlying abelian sheaves, the sequence $0\to F\to I\to Q\to0$ has flasque kernel $F$ and flasque middle term $I$ by step 1.3, so [F8] gives that $I(U)\to Q(U)$ is surjective for every open $U$ and that $Q$ is flasque. Write $H^q(U,-)$ for the right derived functors of the $k$-linear sections functor at $U$ formed with the functorial datum of [F2]; these are the groups compared with the published sheaf cohomology by [F7]. By [F7] the family $(H^q(U,-))_{q\ge0}$ is a cohomological delta functor, so the displayed short exact sequence yields a long exact sequence; by [F7] again $H^q(U,I)=0$ for $q>0$ because $I$ is injective. Hence $H^1(U,F)=0$ and, for every $n\ge1$, $H^{n+1}(U,F)\cong H^n(U,Q)$. Since $Q$ is again flasque, the same argument applies to $Q$, and induction on $n$ gives $H^n(U,F)=0$ for all $n\ge1$ and every open $U$, uniformly over all flasque sheaves. [given, F2, F7, F8, step 1.3, algebra]

2.3 Bounded complexes are K-flat, and the signed total tensor computes the derived tensor. Let $A$ be a bounded complex, say $A^p=0$ for $p\notin[a,b]$, and let $C$ be an acyclic bounded-below complex. Form the double cochain complex $K^{p,q}=A^p\otimes_kC^q$ with horizontal differential induced by $d_A$ and vertical differential $(-1)^p\operatorname{id}\otimes d_C$. After translating bidegrees it is first quadrant with finite diagonal sums, and for fixed $p$ its column is $A^p\otimes_kC^\bullet$, which is acyclic because tensoring with the sheaf $A^p$ is exact by step 1.2. By [F9] the total complex $\operatorname{Tot}(A\otimes_kC)$ is acyclic; this is the statement that bounded $A$ is K-flat. Now let $f:B\to B'$ be a quasi-isomorphism of bounded complexes with cone $\operatorname{Cone}(f)$ in the cochain convention of [def-derived-category-of-an-abelian-category] ([[def-derived-category-of-an-abelian-category]]), so that $0\to B'\to\operatorname{Cone}(f)\to B[1]\to0$ is a short exact sequence of complexes. Tensoring with $A$ is termwise exact by step 1.2, so it carries this sequence to a short exact sequence of complexes of sheaves of $k$-vector spaces, and $\operatorname{Tot}(A\otimes_k\operatorname{Cone}(f))$ is acyclic by the K-flatness just proved because $\operatorname{Cone}(f)$ is acyclic by the cone criterion [F9] and bounded. The long exact cohomology sequence [F9] then shows that $\operatorname{Tot}(A\otimes_kB)\to\operatorname{Tot}(A\otimes_kB')$ is a quasi-isomorphism; by symmetry the same holds in the other variable. Therefore the signed total tensor of bounded representatives descends to a bifunctor $D^b\times D^b\to D$, independent of the chosen bounded representatives, and it represents the derived tensor $A\otimes_k^LB$. [given, F9, step 1.2, algebra]

2.4 The sheaf tensor–Hom adjunction holds on sections over each open. For $k_X$-modules $H,A,G$ there is a natural bijection $\operatorname{Hom}_X(H\otimes_kA,G)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_X(H,\mathcal Hom(A,G))$: on sections over an open $U$ it is the vector-space tensor–Hom transposition $g\mapsto(h\mapsto g(h\otimes-))$, with inverse $s\mapsto(h\otimes a\mapsto s(h)(a))$, and these sectionwise bijections are compatible with restriction to smaller opens and with the $k_X$-module structures because both constructions are built from restriction maps and scalar multiplication. Hence for fixed $A$ the functor $\mathcal Hom(A,-)$ is right adjoint to the exact functor $-\otimes_kA$ of step 1.2; consequently $\mathcal Hom(A,J)$ is injective whenever $J$ is injective, its representable functor being the composite of $-\otimes_kA$ and $\operatorname{Hom}_X(-,J)$. The bijection is natural in all three variables. [given, F5, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 The internal derived Hom is computed by the injective model. Let $A$ be a bounded complex, let $B$ be a bounded-below complex, and let $j_B:B\to I_B$ be a bounded-below termwise injective replacement supplied by [F3]; the complex $\mathcal Hom^\bullet(A,I_B)$ has degree-$n$ term the finite product over $p$ of the internal Hom sheaves $\mathcal Hom(A^p,I_B^{p+n})$ and differential $d\varphi=d_{I_B}\varphi-(-1)^n\varphi d_A$ in the convention of [[def-derived-hom-in-the-bounded-setting]]. Each term is injective by step 2.4, because $A^p$ is a sheaf and $-\otimes_kA^p$ is exact; the complex is bounded below because $A$ is bounded and $I_B$ is bounded below, and only finitely many $p$ contribute; hence it is K-injective by [F3]. Therefore $\mathcal Hom^\bullet(A,I_B)$ computes $\operatorname{Hom}_D(-,B)$ without roofs, and we declare $R\mathcal Hom(A,B):=\mathcal Hom^\bullet(A,I_B)$; it is the actual internal derived Hom: its terms are internal Hom sheaves on $X$, not sectionwise Hom spaces of stalks, and its restriction to an open $U$ is the same construction over $U$. [given, F3, F5, step 1.2, step 2.4, algebra]

3.2 Bounded-below flasque complexes are acyclic for derived sections. Let $C^\bullet$ be a bounded-below complex of flasque $k$-sheaves, with $C^n=0$ for $n<b$, let $\varphi:C^\bullet\to J^\bullet$ be a bounded-below termwise injective replacement supplied by [F3], and fix an open $U$. I first record a lemma: if $K^\bullet$ is an acyclic bounded-below complex whose every term is acyclic for the sections functor at $U$, then $\Gamma(U,K^\bullet)$ is acyclic. Indeed, put $Z^n=\ker(K^n\to K^{n+1})$; the complex is acyclic, so $0\to Z^n\to K^n\to Z^{n+1}\to0$ is a short exact sequence for every $n$, and $Z^n=0$ for $n<b$. If $Z^n$ is acyclic for sections at $U$, then the long exact sequence of [F7] for this sequence, together with the acyclicity of $K^n$, shows that $Z^{n+1}$ is again acyclic for sections at $U$; inducting on $n$ upward from $n<b$, every $Z^n$ is acyclic. With all $Z^n$ acyclic, the same sequences give short exact sequences $0\to\Gamma(U,Z^n)\to\Gamma(U,K^n)\to\Gamma(U,Z^{n+1})\to0$ on sections, so the kernel of $\Gamma(U,K^n)\to\Gamma(U,K^{n+1})$ equals the image of $\Gamma(U,K^{n-1})\to\Gamma(U,K^n)$: the former is the kernel of $\Gamma(U,K^n)\to\Gamma(U,Z^{n+1})$, which is the image of $\Gamma(U,Z^n)$ because $\Gamma$ of a short exact sequence is left exact and the sequence is right exact at the quotient, and $\Gamma(U,K^{n-1})\to\Gamma(U,Z^n)$ is surjective, so the two images agree. Hence $H^n(\Gamma(U,K^\bullet))=0$ for every $n$. Now apply this lemma to the cone $K^\bullet=\operatorname{Cone}(\varphi)$ of the cochain map $\varphi$ in the convention of [[def-derived-category-of-an-abelian-category]]. Its terms $J^n\oplus C^{n+1}$ are direct sums of an injective term, acyclic for sections at $U$ by [F7], and a flasque term, acyclic by step 2.2; a direct sum of two acyclic objects is acyclic by the long exact sequence [F7] applied to $0\to J^n\to J^n\oplus C^{n+1}\to C^{n+1}\to0$. The cone is acyclic and bounded below, so the lemma gives that $\Gamma(U,\operatorname{Cone}(\varphi))$ is acyclic. The canonical identification of complexes $\Gamma(U,\operatorname{Cone}(\varphi))\cong\operatorname{Cone}(\Gamma(U,\varphi))$ holds because sections over $U$ preserve finite direct sums and the differentials are those of $\varphi$; by the cone criterion [F9] the map $\Gamma(U,C^\bullet)\to\Gamma(U,J^\bullet)$ is therefore a quasi-isomorphism for every open $U$. Since any two bounded-below injective replacements of $C^\bullet$ are homotopy equivalent by [F3], the ordinary section complex $\Gamma(U,C^\bullet)$ represents the derived sections $R\Gamma(U,C^\bullet)$ of the bounded-below flasque complex. [given, F3, F7, F9, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 The internal derived Hom is well defined on the bounded derived categories. Let $A\to A'$ be a quasi-isomorphism of bounded complexes and let $I$ be a bounded-below complex of injectives. The canonical map $\mathcal Hom^\bullet(A',I)\to\mathcal Hom^\bullet(A,I)$ has cone canonically identified with $\mathcal Hom^\bullet(\operatorname{Cone}(A\to A'),I)$, with the Koszul signs of the Hom complex; since $I$ is K-injective by [F3] and the cone of $A\to A'$ is acyclic and bounded below, $\mathcal Hom^\bullet$ of an acyclic bounded-below complex into $I$ is acyclic; hence the map is a quasi-isomorphism by the cone criterion. If $I\to I'$ is a homotopy equivalence of bounded-below complexes of injectives, then $\mathcal Hom^\bullet(A,-)$ carries it to a homotopy equivalence, hence a quasi-isomorphism; and any two bounded-below injective replacements of $B$ are homotopy equivalent by [F3]. Combining the two comparisons, $\mathcal Hom^\bullet(A,I_B)$ depends only on the isomorphism classes of $A$ and $B$ in the derived categories, up to canonical quasi-isomorphism, and is natural in both variables. [given, F3, F9, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 The tensor–Hom adjunction and its coherence identities hold on the derived category. For a bounded complex $C$ and a bounded-below complex $I$ of injectives, the currying map $\mathcal Hom^\bullet(A\otimes_kC,I)\to\mathcal Hom^\bullet(C,\mathcal Hom^\bullet(A,I))$, which sends a morphism $\varphi$ to $c\mapsto(a\mapsto(-1)^{|c||a|}\varphi(a\otimes c))$ on pure tensor sections and extends by linearity and gluing, is an isomorphism of complexes: over each open it is the vector-space tensor–Hom transposition of step 2.4, bijective on sections, and the Koszul sign makes both sides carry the single differential rule $d\psi=d_I\psi-(-1)^n\psi d_{\text{source}}$ of [[def-derived-hom-in-the-bounded-setting]], so it commutes with differentials and with restriction. Taking $I$ to be an injective replacement of the bounded-below complex $B$ and using that $C$ is K-flat and $I$, $\mathcal Hom^\bullet(A,I)$ are K-injective, the $H^0$ transposition identifies $\operatorname{Hom}_D(C\otimes_k^LA,B)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_D(C,R\mathcal Hom(A,B))$ naturally in the bounded $C,A$ and bounded-below $B$; this is the tensor–Hom adjunction, with unit and counit the canonical currying maps. The associativity isomorphism $(A\otimes_kB)\otimes_kC\cong A\otimes_k(B\otimes_kC)$, the symmetry isomorphism $A\otimes_kB\cong B\otimes_kA$ with its Koszul sign, the evaluation morphism $R\mathcal Hom(A,B)\otimes_k^LA\to B$, and the currying bijection all hold on pure tensor sections, where they are the usual vector-space identities, and extend by linearity, gluing and the comparison maps of step 4.1; being defined on sections they are natural and compatible with restriction to opens, hence internal. The triangle identities are those of step 2.4 at chain level. [given, F9, step 2.3, step 2.4, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 The claims are now assembled. Inverse image is exact and $f^*\dashv Rf_*$ on $D^+$ is step 2.1; the derived tensor of bounded complexes is the signed total tensor by step 2.3; the internal derived Hom is the injective model of step 3.1, well defined by step 4.1; flasque sheaves are acyclic for sections on every open set by step 2.2, and bounded-below flasque complexes compute their derived sections by step 3.2; the tensor–Hom adjunction and the dg associativity, symmetry, evaluation and currying identities are step 5.1. AC is assumed and consumed exactly as follows: AC yields DC by [F1], DC is used by the injective replacement suppliers of [F3] and the delta-functor and injective-acyclicity statements of [F7], the functorial injective resolutions of [F2] use the Grothendieck embedding theorem under AC, and the flasque section lifting of [F8] uses Zorn's lemma under AC; the stalk, tensor, Hom and adjunction verifications are choice-free. No bounded-constructibility claim for the internal Hom is made: that is supplied later on this page by the geometric finite conical and incidence models, which are not prerequisites of this lemma. [given, F1, F2, F3, F7, F8, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 2.3, step 3.1, step 3.2, step 4.1, step 5.1] ∎
