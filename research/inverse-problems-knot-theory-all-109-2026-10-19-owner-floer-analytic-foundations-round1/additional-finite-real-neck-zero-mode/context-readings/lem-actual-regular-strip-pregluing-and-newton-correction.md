## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Use the compact transverse strip data and exact all-$t$ product generator charts of the actual higher Sobolev configuration-space lemma. Let $u^-:x_0\to y$ and $u^+:y\to x_2$ be actual smooth nonconstant finite-energy $J_t$ strips. Assume their actual geometric linearizations are surjective $H^1\to L^2$. No abstract moduli-space regularity is being substituted for this operator hypothesis.

For all sufficiently large $R$ there is an actual smooth finite-energy regular strip $g_R:x_0\to x_2$ obtained by correcting the explicit pregluing $u_R$ which translates $u^-$ to $u^-(s+R,t)$, translates $u^+$ to $u^+(s-R,t)$, and joins their small tails by a fixed linear cutoff in the generator chart about $y$. In unweighted geometric $H^3\to H^2$ local-addition coordinates there are constants $C,M,L,r>0$, independent of $R$, and right inverses $Q_R$ for $D_R=D\mathcal F_R(0)$ such that
$$\|Q_R\|\le M,\qquad \|\mathcal F_R(0)\|_{H^2}\le Ce^{-\pi R/2},\qquad \|d\mathcal F_R(\xi)-d\mathcal F_R(\eta)\|\le L\|\xi-\eta\|_{H^3}$$
on the radius-$r$ ball. The right inverse and corrected strip may be chosen smoothly in $R$. The correction $g_R=\mathcal E(u_R,\xi_R)$ satisfies
$$\|\xi_R\|_{H^3}\le Ce^{-\pi R/2},\qquad \|\xi_R+Q_R\mathcal F_R(0)\|_{H^3}\le Ce^{-\pi R}.$$
It is the unique small correction in $\operatorname{im}Q_R$, and the corrected linearization is surjective with a uniform bounded right inverse. The translated maps $g_R(s-R,t)$ and $g_R(s+R,t)$ converge smoothly locally to $u^-(s,t)$ and $u^+(s,t)$ respectively.

This proves actual regular-strip gluing existence and a specified-complement uniqueness. It does not assert uniqueness modulo the broken-component moduli, a collar parametrization, or exhaustion of every nearby solution.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual broken pair, fixed compact geometry, exact product-integrable generator charts, and actual surjective component linearizations in the statement.

[F1] [[lem-higher-sobolev-strip-map-charts-and-cauchy-riemann-section]] supplies genuine local-addition $H^3\cap\mathcal X^1\to H^2$ charts, the actual nonlinear section and its bounded quadratic derivative estimates. Its construction uses a boundary-preserving connection and local addition flat in the generator charts. The actual uniform local higher-derivative bounds are supplied by [[lem-local-sobolev-bootstrap-for-almost-complex-strips]] once uniform $C^1$ bounds and small target charts are obtained. Only unweighted spaces are used in this gluing statement.

[F2] [[lem-product-integrable-transverse-strip-end-exponential-asymptotics]] supplies all-order tail estimates with rate $\pi/2$ for the exact $\mathbb R^m,i\mathbb R^m$ end model. The negative-end assertion follows by the same Fourier proof, or in that local model by $(s,t,v)\mapsto(-s,1-t,-iv)$, which preserves $D_0$ and exchanges the two boundary lines.

[F3] The Hilbert parametrix proof of [[lem-variable-zero-order-strip-cauchy-riemann-fredholm]] gives an orthogonal kernel complement with a positive lower operator bound under Countable Choice. The actual frame and [[lem-strip-fredholm-index-agrees-in-higher-sobolev-spaces]] transfer that bound to the geometric $H^1$ operator and give $H^3$ regularity with $\|\xi\|_{H^3}\le C(\|P\xi\|_{H^2}+\|\xi\|_{H^1})$.

[F4] [[lem-countable-choice-quantitative-newton-correction-and-smooth-zero-chart]] proves actual correction and smooth parameter dependence for a specified right inverse, with a quadratic correction error and persistence of surjectivity, under precisely Countable Choice.



## Proof

1.1 Choose a fixed $S$ so that both tails adjacent to $y$ lie in a common smaller product chart $q$ for $s\ge S$ on $u^-$ and $s\le-S$ on $u^+$. Write their coordinates $v^-,v^+$, centered at zero. By [F2], every fixed derivative order satisfies $|D^kv^-(s,t)|\le C_ke^{-\pi s/2}$ on the positive tail and $|D^kv^+(s,t)|\le C_ke^{\pi s/2}$ on the negative tail. Let a real smooth cutoff $\chi(s)$ equal one for $s\le-1$ and zero for $s\ge1$. For large $R$ define $u_R$ to equal the two stated translates outside $[-1,1]$, and on that joining region put $$q(u_R)=\chi(s)v^-(s+R,t)+(1-\chi(s))v^+(s-R,t).$$ The smallness of the tails keeps this in the common chart. It is smooth, has the exact two boundary conditions, and is unchanged from its component maps where the cutoff is constant. The entire interval $[-R+S,R-S]$ lies in that same chart after increasing $S$ if necessary. [F2, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 Construct fixed component right inverses $Q^\pm:H^2\to H^3\cap\mathcal X^1$. The actual component operators are Fredholm by [F3] and surjective by hypothesis. In an $H^1$ orthogonal complement of their kernels the positive lower bound in [F3] gives a unique bounded inverse to each full $L^2$ range, hence a bounded right inverse $L^2\to H^1$. For $H^2$ inputs the higher regularity estimate in [F3] puts its image in $H^3$ with a bounded norm. Restricting produces the claimed $Q^\pm$. Their boundary condition is exactly the $H^1$ domain intersected with $H^3$, not any unproved stronger closure. They are fixed operators, independent of $R$. [F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 In this chart $J_t=i$ and the local addition is coordinate addition. The two tail maps solve $D_0v=0$, so the pregluing error has the exact coordinate expression $$\chi'(s)(v^-(s+R,t)-v^+(s-R,t)).$$ It is supported in the fixed-width join and its derivatives through order two are $O(e^{-\pi R/2})$ by step 1.1. This proves the claimed $H^2$ error bound. The maps $u_R$ have uniform derivatives of every fixed order. They have finite energy, uniform derivative Sobolev bounds at every fixed order, and exponentially decaying ends, because they are two translates of fixed smooth component maps plus this small fixed-width join. Truncating their outer ends gives actual constant-end references and puts them in the configuration space of [F1]. The same local-addition chart construction can be centered at $u_R$: its coefficients have the stated uniform bounds and its end offsets are exponentially Sobolev integrable. Thus [F1] applies with a uniform radius and uniform first and second section derivative bounds. The nonlinear derivative Lipschitz bound follows, with one constant $L$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 For clarity fix norms suitable for the matching. Use the boundary-preserving connection of [F1], flat on smaller generator neighborhoods, and a smooth target metric equal to the coordinate Euclidean metric there. Such a metric is obtained by positive finite cutoff interpolation with any original metric; it is uniformly equivalent to $g$. Covariant derivative Sobolev norms of orders through three with this connection and metric are equivalent to the norms of [F1]. On a common generator chart they are exactly the ordinary coordinate norms. Hence identifying a section over one nearby base map with a section over another by equal $q$ coordinates is an isometry of these local derivative norms. Where base maps coincide the identification is the identity. This fixes all domain and target norms and makes their matching constants independent of neck length. [F1, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 The linearization $D_R$ matches these two operators exactly on the appropriate halves. On $s\le-1$, the base map is exactly the left translate and the pregluing section vanishes, so its linearization is the corresponding translated component operator; likewise on $s\ge1$ for the right. On the whole common-generator interval from step 1.1 the nonlinear expression is $D_0(q(u_R)+\eta)$, since both the coefficient and local addition are exactly linear there. Thus its derivative is exactly $D_0$, even though the preglued map itself has a small residual in its join. The same is true for the shifted component operators on their ends. Identify bundles on these overlaps by $q$ coordinates and elsewhere by equality of the base maps. These identifications preserve boundary tangent spaces and the exact operator expressions. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

3.2 We arrange smooth dependence without claiming operator-norm smoothness of translation on a fixed Sobolev space. The actual map $R\mapsto u_R$ is smooth in the configuration topology: its derivatives differentiate the two fixed translates and the fixed cutoff, and every resulting component derivative has finite Sobolev norm by [F2]. Strong continuity and differentiation of these translations follow by smooth approximation and the fundamental theorem in the longitudinal variable. These derivatives have uniform bounds at each fixed order. The local-addition identifications between $u_R^*TX$ and $u_{R_j}^*TX$ for $R$ close to $R_j$ preserve boundary planes and give smooth norm-bounded maps on $H^3$ and $H^2$. In those fixed spaces the explicit local section formulas in [F1] show that $D_R$ depends smoothly in operator norm, with a uniform local Lipschitz bound. Only pointwise smooth coefficient multiplication and bounded first differentiation occur; no conjugation of an arbitrary bounded operator by norm-differentiable translations is being presumed. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

4.1 Choose real cutoffs $\beta_-^R,\beta_+^R$ with $(\beta_-^R)^2+(\beta_+^R)^2=1$, $\beta_-^R=1$ for $s\le-R/4$ and zero for $s\ge R/4$, and $|D_s^k\beta_\pm^R|\le C_kR^{-k}$ for $k\ge1$. For example take cosine and sine of a fixed smooth step from zero to $\pi/2$ evaluated at $s/R$. For large $R$ their transition lies in the exact common-generator interval. Define $P_R$ as follows: multiply a target section by $\beta_-^R$, identify and translate it to the left component, extend by zero where its cutoff vanishes, apply $Q^-$, translate and identify the result back where defined, and multiply by $\beta_-^R$ again; add the identical right construction with $\beta_+^R,Q^+$. Smooth flatness of the cutoffs where they vanish makes the zero extensions legitimate in $H^2$ and $H^3$. The bundle identifications and translations have the norm control of step 2.2, and multiplication by these cutoffs is uniformly bounded through order three. Thus $\|P_R\|_{H^2\to H^3}\le C$. All boundary domains are preserved by the pointwise tangent identifications and real cutoffs. [step 2.2, step 1.2, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Applying $D_R$ gives $$D_RP_R=I+K_R.$$ Indeed the two main terms are $(\beta_-^R)^2f+(\beta_+^R)^2f=f$ by the exact matching in step 3.1 and $D^\pm Q^\pm=I$. The only errors are the outer cutoff commutators $\partial_s\beta_\pm^R$ times the transported component solutions. Their $H^2$ norms are at most $C R^{-1}$ times the $H^3$ norm of those solutions: the product formula differentiates the cutoff at most three times, all bounded by $C/R$ for $R\ge1$. Therefore $\|K_R\|_{H^2\to H^2}\le C/R$. Choose $R$ large enough that this is at most $1/2$ and set $$Q_R^{\rm raw}=P_R(I+K_R)^{-1},\qquad (I+K_R)^{-1}=\sum_{n\ge0}(-K_R)^n.$$ It is an actual right inverse for $D_R$ with a uniform norm $M_0$. This proves surjectivity by construction, without assuming a glued Fredholm estimate. [step 1.2, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Choose a uniform small parameter radius $a>0$ so that the operator difference in those identifications satisfies $\|D_R-D_{R_j}\|M_0\le1/2$ for $|R-R_j|<a$. Use a fixed arithmetic grid of $R_j$ covering a large half-line. At each grid point take the explicitly constructed $Q_{R_j}^{\rm raw}$ from step 5.1. In its fixed bundle coordinates define $$Q_j(R)=Q_{R_j}^{\rm raw}\bigl[I+(D_R-D_{R_j})Q_{R_j}^{\rm raw}\bigr]^{-1}.$$ This is a smooth local right inverse, by the actual Neumann inverse and its differentiability from [F4], with uniform norm after transferring back to the bundle over $u_R$. Choose a smooth nonnegative parameter partition supported in these radius-$a$ intervals by translating one fixed bump and normalizing its locally finite sum. Their weighted sum $Q_R$ is an actual right inverse, since $D_RQ_j(R)=I$ and the weights sum to one. It is smooth in $R$ and has a uniform norm $M$. The construction uses countably many explicit grid points and locally finite sums only. [F4, step 5.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra]

7.1 Now apply the actual Newton theorem [F4] in each local bundle chart to $\mathcal F_R$, its derivative $D_R$, and this specified $Q_R$. Choose a fixed smaller $r$ inside the uniform chart radius with $MLr\le1/2$. The residual $\varepsilon_R\le Ce^{-\pi R/2}$ from step 2.1 eventually satisfies $2M\varepsilon_R<r$. It gives the unique zero correction $\xi_R\in\operatorname{im}Q_R\cap\overline B_r$ and the bounds $$\|\xi_R\|_{H^3}\le2M\varepsilon_R,\qquad \|\xi_R+Q_R\mathcal F_R(0)\|_{H^3}\le2M^3L\varepsilon_R^2.$$ Its smooth-family clause and step 6.1 make the correction smooth in $R$. The corrected derivative remains surjective with a uniform right inverse of norm at most $2M$. These are precisely the claimed exponential estimates and specified-complement uniqueness. [F4, step 2.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 Let $g_R=\mathcal E(u_R,\xi_R)$. It is a zero of the actual section, so [F1] makes it smooth with finite energy and the prescribed end generators. Its actual geometric $H^3\to H^2$ linearization is surjective by step 7.1. The higher Sobolev range agreement in [F3] implies surjectivity of its $H^1\to L^2$ realization as well: its closed $L^2$ range contains the dense $H^2$ target, so is all of $L^2$. Thus it is regular in the original actual operator sense. On each shifted compact core, step 7.1 and the $H^3\subset C^1$ estimate give convergence to the corresponding component in $C^1$. The actual local smooth bootstrap in [F1] gives uniform bounds of every higher order there; any subsequence therefore has a smoothly convergent subsubsequence with that same $C^1$ limit. This proves smooth local convergence of the entire family to $u^-$ and $u^+$ under the two stated opposite translations. Nonconstancy follows because these nonconstant limits are retained. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 7.1, construct, algebra]

9.1 Every infinite operation used was an explicit geometric series, a countable parameter grid, or a Sobolev limit already justified under Countable Choice. The actual geometric pregluing, uniform matching estimate, right inverse and nonlinear correction have now all been supplied. The uniqueness in step 7.1 concerns only the specified complement to the kernel. Identifying kernel coordinates with the broken moduli, proving injective collar parametrizations, and proving that all nearby solutions arise this way still require their own arguments. [step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, step 8.1, construct] ∎
