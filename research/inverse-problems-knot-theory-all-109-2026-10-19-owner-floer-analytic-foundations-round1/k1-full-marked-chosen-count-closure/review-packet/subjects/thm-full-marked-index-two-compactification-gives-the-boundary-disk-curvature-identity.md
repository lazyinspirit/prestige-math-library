## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Use the actual balanced $S^3$ knot data with $k\ge1$ and THE chosen surface complex structure and genuine constant-collar path constructed in the corresponding multipointed or singly pointed full-marked low-index count theorem, whose construction excludes every nonconstant strip of index at most zero as well as giving full-marked index-one and index-two regularity and finite counts. For $k=1$ this is the sphere-avoiding path from the singly pointed theorem. Label the individual cut components so $A_i$ contains $w_i,z_i$ and $B_i$ contains $w_i,z_{i+1}$, with cyclic indices.

For fixed generators $x,y$ and nonnegative integer vectors $W,Z$, let $\widehat{\mathcal M}_2(x,y;W,Z)$ consist of ALL actual index-two strips with these ends and marked-degree vectors, modulo longitudinal translation, across all realized Whitney classes. This is a Hausdorff second-countable topological one-manifold. It has a compactification as a compact one-manifold with finite boundary, whose boundary points are exactly:

1. Ordered pairs of actual rigid index-one strip orbits with matched intermediate generator and marked vectors summing to $W,Z$.
2. When $x=y$, the actual unit $A_i$ or $B_i$ boundary-disk orbits with boundary node value $x$ and the prescribed marked vector, attached to the constant generator strip.

Each point has the actual exhaustive one-sided gluing collar of its respective type. No extra boundary points occur, and no differentiability at a prescribed compactifying parameter is asserted.

Let $a_i(x),b_i(x)\in\mathbb F_2$ be the actual finite node-fixed affine-orbit counts of the $A_i,B_i$ disks at $x$. On the free module generated by the generators over $R=\mathbb F_2[U_1,\ldots,U_k,V_1,\ldots,V_k]$, define $D$ by the actual finite rigid-strip counts with weight $U^{n_w}V^{n_z}$. Then
$$D^2x=\left(\sum_{i=1}^k a_i(x)U_iV_i+\sum_{i=1}^k b_i(x)U_iV_{i+1}\right)x.$$
Setting every $V_i=1$ gives the actual full filtered minus count operator $d$ and
$$d^2x=\left(\sum_{i=1}^k(a_i(x)+b_i(x))U_i\right)x.$$
For $k=1$ the actual singly pointed unit-disk theorem supplies $a_1(x)=b_1(x)=0$, and hence $D^2=d^2=0$. For $k\ge2$ the counts $a_i,b_i$ are not evaluated here, so square-zero in that case and unit disk count one are not concluded. Invariance and absolute normalization are not asserted. The full marked compactification and curvature identity retain the actual remaining multipointed boundary counts explicitly.

## Proof

1.1 Every strip in the specified space is regular of actual index two by [F1]. Its actual fixed-parameter zero chart is two-dimensional, with the $H^3$ topology and smooth higher solution regularity constructed in the suppliers of [F1]. Endpoints and marked degrees are locally constant: the local-addition straight section homotopy preserves the physical boundary and fixed ends, and the actual relative divisor line degree is homotopy invariant by [F2]. Thus these are also charts of the specified marked-vector space. Translation is free by finite energy: a nonzero period of a nonconstant strip repeats a positive energy rectangle infinitely often. It is jointly continuous in the strong $H^3$ chart topology by scalar Sobolev shift continuity and the smooth coefficient-composition estimates of those actual charts. It is sequentially proper: if $u_n\to u$ and $\tau_{c_n}u_n\to v$ strongly in $H^3$ and $|c_n|\to\infty$, uniform $C^0$ convergence and the uniform ends of the fixed map $u$ force $v$ to be constant on every compact rectangle, impossible for an index-two strip. Bounded $c_n$ instead have a convergent subsequence and joint continuity identifies its limit as a translate. A local quotient slice is obtained by an actual compactly supported source test $b$: embed the compact target in a finite Euclidean space as in [F4], and choose $b$ with $\ell'(u)[u_s]\ne0$ for $\ell(u)=\int b\cdot F(u)$. The scalar phase $\ell(\tau_c w)$ is jointly smooth because changing source variable puts every $c$ derivative on $b$. Its one-variable implicit theorem on the two-dimensional zero chart gives a one-dimensional phase slice. Sequential properness and freeness exclude large-phase returns near that slice. They also separate distinct orbits by saturated neighborhoods. The quotient is consequently Hausdorff and is locally an interval; it is second-countable because translation is an open quotient of the actual second-countable configuration zero space. No differentiability of joint translation on all rough $H^3$ maps is used. [F1, F2, F4, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 By [F1] every sequence of these strips has uniformly bounded energy, so [F2]'s actual bubble compactness supplies a limit before any regularity assertion. Every nonconstant strip level belongs to the SAME fixed path and hence has positive integer index by the nonpositive-index exclusion in [F1]. Every nonconstant boundary disk has index at least two and every sphere at least $2k$, by the actual domain/index inputs of [F2]'s balanced classification proof; these positivity conclusions do not use its limiting-strip regularity premise. Apply the actual index-additivity theorem first. The total index is two, so every nonconstant strip level has index one or two and is therefore regular by [F1]. The regularity premise of the low-index classification is now satisfied. It yields exactly a one-level unbubbled strip, two rigid strip levels, or a constant generator with one unit boundary disk, with no sphere when $k\ge2$. When $k=1$, the retained alternative is one unit-incidence sphere joined through constant components to a constant generator strip. Its matched node value is that generator, so [F1]'s explicit sphere avoidance for every path value excludes it. Thus no sphere stratum remains in either case. An entirely constant no-bubble limit is also excluded by that same index sum, which would give zero. In the one-level unbubbled case, the exact generator-tail cutoff inverse gives global $H^3$ convergence after translation, exactly as proved in the chosen-data count theorem [F1]: $D_0(\chi w_n)=\chi'w_n$ and $\|\chi w_n\|_{H^3}\le C\|\chi'w_n\|_{H^2}\to0$ on each tail, while the core converges smoothly. Thus it converges in an interior quotient interval. The other cases in [F2] are the stated broken pairs and unit disk limits. Their marked vectors add to $W,Z$ by actual degree additivity, including the constant base in the disk case. There are finitely many candidate boundary points: rigid orbits are globally finite by [F1], hence so are their pairs, and unit disks are finite at each of finitely many generators and components by [F1]. No bound on the number of arbitrary formal Whitney classes is substituted for this actual finiteness. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Every candidate is realized by an actual collar. For a broken pair use [F3]'s regular-pair gluing and exhaustion; its glued solutions are actual regular index-two strips. Actual degree additivity on that convergent family makes their marked vectors eventually the sum of the two vectors, so a sufficiently long collar lies in the specified space. For a unit disk use [F3]'s boundary-attachment theorem with its actual regular node-fixed endpoint operator in [F1]. Its actual corrected solutions have index two, the same generator at both ends, and no energy loss. Their marked vectors are eventually those of the disk, again by actual degree additivity. Each affine disk orbit gives one half-interval end after longitudinal quotient: the attachment theorem explicitly normalizes the disk's marked node and exhausts every actual one-disk nodal sequence, and its positive scale is the one remaining parameter. Its free affine action produces no stabilizer factor. Thus EVERY listed disk and broken point is present, not just a candidate extracted from some sequence. All collars belong to the full index-two family with its uniform bound in [F1]; there is no artificial energy-cut boundary. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 We verify separation and uniqueness at the boundary. Two proposed different broken-pair limits of one sequence must agree: a fixed positive-energy core of each component of one pair cannot recede from both cores of the other, because all intervening neck and tail energy tends to zero. The two component shifts cannot stay close to the same core, since their difference diverges. Their longitudinal order therefore matches the original two cores. Bounded shift subsequences identify the corresponding components smoothly up to translation, so the orbit pairs are equal, as in [F4]. For two disk limits use the ACTUAL collar from the first limit, available eventually by [F3]. A second nonconstant profile cannot attach to the opposite physical boundary edge, since that whole edge lies outside the first shrinking boundary disk and its maps converge uniformly to the generator. Its physical attachment point must likewise coincide with the first after translation normalization. Thus perform both extractions in ONE fixed holomorphic physical boundary chart about that point, as permitted by the actual rescaling construction in [F2]; both extractions have real centers and positive scales in that same chart. In its normalized coordinates the first disk profile converges smoothly on every finite closed halfplane core and has the outside-bubble estimate in [F3]. Express the second extracted boundary profile by a real affine change $\zeta\mapsto\beta_n+\lambda_n\zeta$ relative to the first, with $\lambda_n>0$. If $\lambda_n\to\infty$, every compact interior set maps outside every first bubble radius, forcing the second profile to be the generator there, a contradiction. If $\lambda_n\to0$ and $\beta_n$ stays bounded, smooth convergence near its finite boundary limit makes the second profile constant; if $|\beta_n|\to\infty$ and $\lambda_n$ is bounded, the outside-bubble estimate again makes it the generator. These cases exhaust escape after subsequences, including $\lambda_n\to0,|\beta_n|\to\infty$. Hence the relative affines have a subsequence with finite real translation and positive finite scale. The two disk limits differ by that node-fixing positive affine map and define the same orbit. A disk limit cannot simultaneously be a broken-pair limit: away from its shrinking physical boundary disk, its actual collar converges uniformly to the constant generator. Any bounded-shift fixed-scale strip limit is therefore constant off at most that one boundary point, hence constant by smoothness; an unbounded shift sees the same constant outer tail. This excludes either nonconstant component of a broken pair. Finally strong global $H^3$ convergence to an interior strip excludes both two receding positive-energy cores and a positive bubble: its tails have uniformly small energy and its derivatives are uniformly bounded on compact sets, so no such concentration or separated level remains. These arguments use actual collar estimates and component convergence, not uniqueness of an assumed abstract stable-map compactification. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Adjoin these finitely many points with their actual exhaustive collars. Distinct collar tails can be made disjoint: common points with both parameters tending to their nodal endpoints would give two distinct limits, excluded by step4.1. If one parameter instead stays in a bounded closed portion of its collar while the other tends to its endpoint, continuity of the first gluing chart gives an interior strong limit, also excluded by step4.1. These two cases exclude every failure after taking a subsequence. Each added point can also be separated from any fixed interior point; a failure of separation along shrinking interval neighborhoods would give the excluded simultaneous strong interior and boundary convergence. The interior topology and these half-interval charts thus define a Hausdorff second-countable topological one-manifold with precisely the listed boundary. The intersections of the charts with the interior are open there by the actual local-zero-chart parts of [F3], and their countable nodal tests and exhaustion give exactly the half-interval topology at the endpoint. Any interior sequence has, by step2.1, a subsequence converging either in an interior interval or in one of these exhaustive collars. A sequence with infinitely many boundary points has a constant subsequence. Thus the space is sequentially compact. Under Countable Choice its second-countability implies compactness: choose a countable subcover from any open cover using basis elements, and if no finite part covers, choose a point outside the first $n$ members for each $n$; a convergent subsequence contradicts the member containing its limit. This proves the asserted actual compactification, including the possible empty moduli space and the case where a collar creates a nonempty interior. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Its number of boundary points is even by the elementary argument in [F4], which applies to this actual compact topological one-manifold. Explicitly cover it by finitely many relatively small interval or boundary half-interval charts whose closures lie in larger charts. Cut at their finitely many frontier points and at the actual boundary. Each component of the complement lies in one of the small charts, since membership in each such chart is locally constant after deleting its frontier; it is an ordinary interval with endpoints in the finite cut set. A component without endpoints would be a compact open interval contained in one chart, which is impossible. There are finitely many interval pieces since each cut point has exactly two incident germs in the interior and one at an actual boundary point. Counting germs gives $2e=2i+b$, so the actual boundary count $b$ is even. Apply this separately for every fixed $x,y,W,Z$; the boundary sets are exactly those in steps2.1–3.1. [F4, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 The rigid count operator $D$ is well defined over $R$ because [F1] gives finitely many ALL rigid orbits, each with nonnegative integer marked degrees. The coefficient of $U^WV^Zy$ in $D^2x$ is the parity of the finite ordered broken-pair set of step2.1, by multiplication of its actual counts and addition of exponents. The other boundary points exist only for $y=x$. A unit $A_i$ disk has marks exactly $n_{w_i}=n_{z_i}=1$ and all other marks zero; a unit $B_i$ disk has exactly $n_{w_i}=n_{z_{i+1}}=1$. Their actual orbit parities are by definition $a_i(x),b_i(x)$. The even boundary count in step6.1 therefore gives the displayed polynomial curvature identity coefficient by coefficient. Specializing the ring homomorphism $V_i\mapsto1$ gives the stated formula for the actual full filtered minus count operator; finite sums make the specialization legitimate. For $k=1$, [F1] supplies the actual zero counts on both tori, hence the displayed curvature is zero and both operators square to zero. For $k\ge2$ no step computed the remaining disk parities. Their evaluation or comparison is needed to deduce square-zero, and is expressly not replaced by the parity of this larger boundary set. This proves the compactification and exact curvature identity with all positive marked multiplicities retained. [F1, F2, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 6.1, algebra] ∎
