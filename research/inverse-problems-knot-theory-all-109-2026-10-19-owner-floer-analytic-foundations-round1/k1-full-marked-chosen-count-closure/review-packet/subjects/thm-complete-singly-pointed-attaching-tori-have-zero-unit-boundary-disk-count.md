## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let a complete system of $h\ge1$ attaching circles on a genus-$h$ surface bound compression disks cutting a handlebody to one ball. Use the actual broad smooth disk witnesses, finite planar cut data and node-chart hypotheses of the single-system real-model construction. Put $L=q(\gamma_1\times\cdots\times\gamma_h)\subset\operatorname{Sym}^h\Sigma$. Fix finitely many nodes $x\in L$ and a surface complex structure $j_0$ for which the attaching circles have actual real-axis holomorphic charts at their node coordinates. Use autonomous nearly symmetric perturbations fixing the full boundary complex endomorphism on $L$ and exact node charts.

There are arbitrarily small such perturbations $J$ of $\operatorname{Sym}^h(j_0)$ for which no incidence-degree-one sphere passes through any chosen $x$, every actual unit-domain boundary disk is node-fixed regular of index two, and there are finitely many positive-affine disk orbits at each node. Their count modulo two is ZERO. Here the unit domain is the whole surface $\Sigma$, since this attaching complement is connected. More generally every sufficiently small perturbation with that pointed-sphere avoidance and actual node-fixed disk regularity has the same finite zero count.

The comparison is proved by an actual compact one-parameter disk orbit space, with pointed degree-one sphere bubbling excluded by a simultaneously chosen family. It makes no assertion that the standard disk moduli for an arbitrary starting complex structure is empty. It neither counts higher domains nor supplies strip regularity, boundary attachment or Floer invariance.

## Facts & Assumptions

Its standard torus has no nonconstant holomorphic disk and meets no nonconstant standard sphere. At every fixed finite energy bound this absence of disks persists near that model when the full structure on $L$ is fixed. ## Proof

1.1 Use [F1] and [F2] to choose the actual smooth compact integrable background path $K_s$, $0\le s\le1$, on one target, constant near its two parameter endpoints. It fixes the full structure on $L$, the exact node neighborhoods, the diagonal and all chosen incidence divisors. The endpoint is biholomorphic to the real model by a map preserving each attaching circle setwise and all the nodes. Hence its empty disk and sphere hypotheses are exactly those in [F1]. In the finite relative coefficient atlas the energy proof in [F3] has uniform constants on this compact path. Every nearby unit-surface disk or unit-incidence sphere has energy bounded by one finite $E_0$. Shrink the endpoint perturbation neighborhood so [F1]'s bounded-energy emptiness theorem excludes every unit disk there. All endpoint structures in this neighborhood still have the fixed full restriction on $L$. [F1, F2, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 We prove the free-site assertion for ALL sufficiently near-family unit-surface disks BEFORE imposing sphere avoidance. For any countersequence tending smoothly to one standard parameter, [F3] gives a finite tree whose nonnegative component incidence degrees sum to one. A one-torus disk degree is constant on the connected complement, so each nonconstant disk contributes a positive integer multiple of $\Sigma$; spheres likewise have a nonnegative integer common degree, and degree zero components are constant. Thus precisely one component is nonconstant, either a unit disk or a unit sphere; all others are constant. Every constant component connected to the base by a path avoiding that unique positive component has the base value $x$, since the marked point of the base disk has value $x$ and nodes agree. In the positive sphere case all preceding components are constant, so that sphere passes through $x$. No further positive component exists. The actual tree convergence and no-neck image-diameter collapse imply that outside successively large cores of the unique positive component the image is uniformly close to its limiting nodal value $x$; every other component is constant with that value. Constant disk ghosts between the base and a positive sphere do not change this conclusion. [F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 The positive standard component has a free unique immersion site away from $x$. If it is a sphere use [F4]'s actual unit-sphere site proof. If it is a disk, [F4]'s zero-one disk incidence proof gives dense unique immersion points even for the unit-surface domain. Its image cannot be contained in the protected union $D\cup L\cup\bigcup A_x$: $D$ is separated from the other sets, and the boundary lies in $L$, so connectedness excludes $D$. An open source region in $L$ has zero complex differential and forces a constant holomorphic map; if the whole disk instead lies in one separated exact node chart, each coordinate has harmonic imaginary part zero on the real boundary, hence is constant by the elementary polar Green identity. Thus there is an open free-image preimage, which meets the dense immersion-uniqueness set. Choose a small target neighborhood $W$ about that value, disjoint from $x$ and all protected sets, whose entire preimage on the positive component lies in one immersed source patch. The smooth core convergence gives a uniformly injective two-coordinate projection on that patch. All the rest of the original disk avoids $W$ for sufficiently late maps: its compact positive-component remainder avoids a smaller $W$ by uniqueness, and its remaining tree cores and necks converge uniformly to $x$ by step2.1. Therefore the original nearby disk has a globally unique free immersion site. This contradicts the alleged countersequence. A compact parameter interval and finitely many nodes give one uniform near-family neighborhood. In particular no compactness of the standard unit-disk orbit set was assumed here; a limiting pointed sphere was treated explicitly. [F3, F4, step 2.1, construct, contradiction]

4.1 Construct one protected rapidly convergent spatial Hilbert bump family near $K_s$, as in [F5], with optional parameter cutoffs. Choose its ball within the neighborhood of step3.1 and the endpoint empty-model neighborhood in step1.1. At every actual unit disk its universal derivative is onto by the actual localized adjoint argument in [F5]: the weighted obstruction is smooth and has unique continuation, the free site localizes its whole pullback, and an anti-$J$ field supported there pairs positively. Dense actual Hilbert tangents contradict annihilation. The compact/weighted node-evaluation comparison in [F5] is valid for this oriented product torus independently of the domain, so yields the actual compact $H^3\to H^2$ universal surjectivity. Its fixed node index is two by [F4]. Simultaneously the sphere universal derivative is onto, by [F4]'s pointed-sphere theorem proof, on the same enlarged bump family: both arguments use all compactly supported fields in the same free region. The point-fixed sphere index is two and its affine symmetry kernel has dimension four. Apply actual Sard--Smale to the interior parameter projections of both zero loci, of index three, and their endpoint slices of index two, for every finite node. The intersection is dense residual. At such a parameter the sphere zero spaces are empty by the four-versus-three (or two) kernel contradiction, and all disk equations, including parameter endpoints, are regular. The actual Hilbert zero chart uses a bounded right inverse formed by finite range-quotient lifts and the kernel-complement inverse, exactly as in [F5]; no transversality axiom is substituted. [F4, F5, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Disk orbit compactness now holds along this fixed perturbed family. A convergent-parameter sequence of unit disks has the tree in step2.1. Its positive sphere alternative would pass through $x$ and is excluded by the simultaneous sphere avoidance in step4.1. Thus it has exactly one positive disk and no positive spheres. Stable constant bubble vertices with two descendant branches would require two positive leaves, so the disk tree reduces to that disk and possibly a constant base. Use the explicit mark-preserving normalization from [F3]'s proof: set the physical mark at $z=\infty$; a bubble away from it is rescaled by a real affine map. If it bubbles at the mark, put $w=-1/z$ and write $w=c_n+r_n\zeta$ with $c_n\in\mathbb R$, $r_n>0$, and $\xi_n=-c_n/r_n$. If $\xi_n$ has finite limit precompose with $T_n(\zeta)=\xi_n-1/\zeta$; if it tends to real infinity use $T_n(\zeta)=\zeta/(1+\zeta/\xi_n)$. Then $g_n(\zeta)=-1/(c_n+r_nT_n(\zeta))$ fixes the original infinity and is a positive real affine map. The new profile has all the limiting energy. Small-energy estimates about its only possible remaining point, including boundary estimates using the fixed FULL structure on $L$, give uniform derivatives there and global smooth convergence. Thus convergence retains the fixed node value, exactly as proved in [F3]'s normalization proof, without its proper-domain exclusion argument. [F3, F4, step 2.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 At a fixed regular parameter the disk zero spaces have dimension two and their free affine orbits have dimension two. Higher zero charts in [F5] make the affine action smooth on solutions; its two tangent directions are independent because the map is nonconstant. The actual properness argument applies: global uniform convergence and an escaping positive affine source map force a constant limit off at most one source point. Consequently the quotient is Hausdorff, second-countable and locally discrete. Step5.1 makes it sequentially compact, so finite. For the parametrized regular disk zero spaces, the same higher-chart argument gives a smooth three-manifold with boundary and a free proper two-dimensional affine action. Its quotient is a Hausdorff second-countable one-manifold with boundary the two finite endpoint orbit sets. Step5.1 again gives sequential compactness and hence compactness under Countable Choice by the countable-base subcover argument proved in [F5]. There are no sphere boundary strata, by step4.1, and no extra disk strata, by step5.1. The compact-one-manifold finite interval graph in [F5] shows its boundary cardinality is even. Its endpoint-$1$ orbit set is empty by step1.1, so the endpoint-$0$ unit disk count is zero modulo two. [F1, F4, F5, step 1.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 This applies to arbitrarily small generic endpoint-$0$ perturbations. To include any other sufficiently small regular endpoint perturbation with the stipulated pointed-sphere avoidance, prescribe it on a small parameter collar of zero and prescribe an empty-model endpoint near one. Extend their small Cayley coefficients smoothly along the standard family, stationary on those collars, and add Hilbert variations multiplied by a smooth scalar positive on the remaining open interval and zero on the closed collars. Disk universal surjectivity follows there from step3.1 and step4.1. Sphere universal surjectivity does likewise; on the two collars no pointed spheres exist by their prescribed absence. Apply the same interior Sard--Smale argument; at the collars disk regularity is already given. This yields a connecting disk-regular sphere-avoiding family, whose compactness and boundary parity are exactly steps5.1--6.1. The starting count is therefore finite and zero too. No assertion of standard-disk emptiness for $j_0$ or any higher-degree sphere or disk claim was used. This proves the actual singly pointed unit-boundary count, leaving strip and gluing obligations separate. [F1, F2, F4, F5, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra] ∎
