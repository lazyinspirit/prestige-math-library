## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $E\to S^2=\mathbb{CP}^1$ be a smooth complex bundle of rank $m\ge1$. Let $P:\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(\Lambda^{0,1}T^*S^2\otimes E)$ be a smooth real-linear Cauchy--Riemann type operator: its principal symbol is that of $\bar\partial$, with no assumption that its zeroth-order part is complex linear. Let $d$ be the determinant clutching degree, with convention that the line $O(d)$ has transition $u_N(z)=z^d u_S(1/z)$ across the equator. Equivalently for a unitary connection with curvature $F$ this convention is $d=-(2\pi i)^{-1}\int_{S^2}\operatorname{tr}F$.

For every integer $q\ge0$, the actual bounded operator
$$P_q:H^{q+1}(E)\longrightarrow H^q(\Lambda^{0,1}T^*S^2\otimes E)$$
is Fredholm with real index $2m+2d$. Its kernel consists of the same smooth sections for every $q$, its range is the intersection of the $L^2$ range with the higher target, and inclusion identifies its finite-dimensional cokernel with the $L^2$ cokernel. It has a bounded inverse on its $H^1$ kernel complement, and
$$\|u\|_{H^{q+1}}\le C_q(\|Pu\|_{H^q}+\|u\|_{H^1}).$$
For a prescribed point $p\in S^2$ and $q\ge2$, restriction to the actual closed space $\{u\in H^{q+1}(E):u(p)=0\}$ is Fredholm of real index $2d$. No point trace on ordinary $H^1$ is asserted. In particular if the pullback tangent bundle of a pointed sphere has $d=1$, its actual node-fixed $H^3\to H^2$ linearization has index two. Surjectivity, generic avoidance of spheres, and any compactification or gluing conclusion remain separate.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual smooth bundle over the standard complex sphere, its exact Cauchy--Riemann principal symbol, and smooth positive metrics for the finite-chart weak Sobolev norms. Scalars are complex for bundle frames and real for the Fredholm dimensions of a possibly real-linear operator.

[F1] The scalar disk operator with boundary trace $u(e^{it})=e^{ikt}\overline{u(e^{it})}$ is an actual real Fredholm operator of index $k+1$, with a bounded range inverse, finite kernel, explicit finite cokernel and the exact $H^1$ boundary completion, for EVERY integer $k$. ([[lem-scalar-disk-riemann-hilbert-fredholm-index]])

[F2] A smooth invertible matrix loop extends over a disk exactly when its determinant winding is zero, including prescribed collar data. The geometric disk proof constructs global complex frames by a finite atlas embedding, orthogonal projection and an explicit radial matrix ODE. Its weak interior difference-quotient proof supplies the local higher-order estimate used below. ([[lem-complex-frame-loop-extension-determinant-winding]], [[lem-geometric-oriented-disk-cauchy-riemann-fredholm-and-higher-regularity]])

[F3] Two bounded parametrices with norm-limit-finite-rank errors give actual Hilbert Fredholmness and a bounded range inverse. Norm-limit-finite-rank perturbations preserve index under Countable Choice. ([[lem-countable-choice-hilbert-parametrices-and-index-stability]])

[F4] The planar $W^{1,p}$ Morrey estimate for $p>2$, together with the finite compact-chart Sobolev estimates in the geometric disk proof, gives bounded point evaluation for $H^3$ sections. ([[lem-countable-choice-planar-morrey-estimate]], [[lem-geometric-oriented-disk-cauchy-riemann-fredholm-and-higher-regularity]])

## Proof

1.1 Trivialize the bundle on slightly enlarged north and south closed hemispheres by the actual radial projection-ODE construction of [F2]. Let $C(z)$ be its smooth transition on an equatorial annulus, so $u_N(z)=C(z)u_S(1/z)$, and put $d=\operatorname{wind}\det C$ along $|z|=1$ with increasing argument. The matrix $C(z)\operatorname{diag}(z^{-d},1,\ldots,1)$ has determinant winding zero. By the collar version of [F2] it extends invertibly across the north disk, agreeing with that matrix on a smaller equatorial annulus. Use this extension as the north-frame change. The new transition is exactly $\operatorname{diag}(z^d,1,\ldots,1)$ there. Thus the actual smooth bundle is identified, by bounded smooth frame maps in every integer Sobolev norm, with $O(d)\oplus O(0)^{m-1}$. Determinant winding is independent of the disk frames by [F2]. To verify the curvature convention, write the determinant connection forms in those frames as $A_N,A_S$. Their equatorial relation is $A_S=A_N+d\log\det C$. Integrating their derivatives on the two disks, whose equatorial orientations are opposite, gives $\int\operatorname{tr}F=\int_{|z|=1}(A_N-A_S)=-2\pi i d$. This is an elementary two-disk Stokes calculation, obtained in polar coordinates; no general Chern index theorem is assumed. [F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Consider the standard complex-linear operator $\bar\partial_d$ on the line $O(d)$ in these holomorphic transition frames. There is an anti-complex-linear involution $\mathcal C$ on sections covering reflection across the equator: on the two disk coordinates it sends $u_N(z)$ to $\overline{u_S(\bar z)}$ in the north coordinate and $u_S(w)$ to $\overline{u_N(\bar w)}$ in the south coordinate. These formulas are compatible with the transition $z^d$ and square to the identity. They induce the corresponding anti-linear involution on line-valued $(0,1)$ forms by the same coefficient conjugation in the reflected disk coordinates; $\bar\partial_d\mathcal C=\mathcal C\bar\partial_d$. Its fixed $H^1$ sections restrict exactly to $X_d$ in [F1] on the north disk. Indeed on its equator the relation becomes $u_N(z)=z^d\overline{u_N(z)}$; conversely a north section with that trace reflects to the south, and the two weak $H^1$ pieces glue precisely when their traces match under that relation. To see the weak assertion, integrate by parts on the two annuli up to the seam: their boundary terms cancel by the trace identity, leaving exactly their piecewise square-integrable derivatives. For arbitrary $X_d$ sections the same conclusion follows by [F1]'s smooth boundary approximation; reflected norms on the two disks are equivalent to their original disk norms. Thus restriction and reflection are bounded inverse real Hilbert maps between the fixed sphere domain and $X_d$. [F1, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 There is no boundary condition on an $L^2$ target form, since the seam has measure zero. The fixed target subspace restricts, by the same reflection formulas, boundedly and bijectively onto arbitrary north-disk $L^2$ form coefficients. In the south coordinate $w=\bar z$ under reflection the coefficient is the complex conjugate of the north coefficient, exactly as follows by differentiating $\overline{u_N(\bar w)}$ with $\partial_{\bar w}$. Therefore restriction identifies the fixed real operator with [F1]'s $\bar\partial:X_d\to L^2(D)$, of real index $d+1$. Every section has the unique decomposition $u=a+ib$ with $a=(u+\mathcal Cu)/2$ and $b=(u-\mathcal Cu)/(2i)$ fixed by $\mathcal C$; the same holds in the target. Complex linearity identifies the full operator, as a real Hilbert operator, with two copies of that fixed real operator. It is consequently Fredholm of real index $2(d+1)$, with a bounded range inverse and finite-error parametrices obtained from those in [F1]. Taking the direct sum with $m-1$ copies for $O(0)$ yields a reference operator $P^{\rm ref}$ on the actual bundle of index $2m+2d$, with actual finite-error parametrices on both sides. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 The original operator and $P^{\rm ref}$ have identical principal symbols, so their difference is a smooth real-linear multiplication operator $B$. It is a norm limit of finite-rank operators from $H^1$ to $L^2$. Here is the exact compactness argument: choose finitely many relatively compact coordinate disks with smooth bundle frames and cutoffs summing to one, and slightly larger disks for each support. A cutoff section extends by zero in that frame to a fixed plane square as a weak $H^1$ function. Replace it on each square cell of side $h$ by its average, and multiply back by a second cutoff equal to one on the first support before returning to the bundle chart. Coordinate-segment integration and the squared double-integral variance identity give $\|v-\operatorname{av}_h v\|_2\le Ch\|\nabla v\|_2$ on that plane square. Summing over the finite charts therefore gives finite-rank $J_h:H^1(E)\to L^2(E)$ with $\|u-J_hu\|_2\le Ch\|u\|_{H^1}$. This is first proved for smooth sections; local plane convolution and translation continuity prove smooth density in the actual weak domain and pass the estimate to it. Multiplication by bounded $B$ composed with $J_h$ approximates $B$ in operator norm. Applying [F3] to the reference finite-error parametrices proves actual Fredholmness, index $2m+2d$, closed range and a bounded kernel-complement range inverse for $P_0$. [F2, F3, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Prove actual higher weak regularity without a boundary trace issue. In any holomorphic source coordinate and complex bundle frame, divide the nonzero scalar symbol factor to write $2P=D_0+C$, where $D_0=\partial_x+i\partial_y$ and $C$ is smooth real linear. For a compactly supported smooth local section, integration by parts gives $\|D_0v\|_2^2=\|v_x\|_2^2+\|v_y\|_2^2$; the mixed terms cancel and no boundary occurs. Plane convolution passes this to weak $H^1$ sections. For $(D_0+C)v=f\in H^1$, apply a difference quotient in either real direction and nested cutoffs. Its equation has forcing $\delta_hf-C(\cdot+h)\delta_hv-(\delta_hC)v$ plus controlled cutoff terms. The $L^2$ norms of $\delta_hv$ and $\delta_hf$ are bounded by their already known first weak derivatives. The preceding identity gives a uniform $H^1$ bound for $\delta_hv$ on a smaller patch. The Countable-Choice weak-derivative argument in [F2] supplies the next weak derivative there. Repeating the differentiated equations and using bounded smooth coefficient derivatives gives, at every integer order, $\|v\|_{H^{q+1}(D')}\le C_q(\|f\|_{H^q(D)}+\|v\|_{H^1(D)})$ on nested disks. A finite patch cover gives the stated global estimate. All source and target frame changes have bounded derivatives, so this is the actual geometric estimate. [F2, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Hence every higher kernel is the same smooth kernel and $\operatorname{Ran}P_q=H^q\cap\operatorname{Ran}P_0$. This intersection is closed in $H^q$. Smooth target sections are dense in $L^2$ by finite-chart cutoff and convolution. Their image in the finite-dimensional quotient $L^2/\operatorname{Ran}P_0$ is dense, and as a linear subspace of a finite-dimensional space it is all that quotient. Thus inclusion identifies the higher cokernel with it and preserves the actual index. Restrict the bounded $H^1$ range inverse from step4.1; the estimate in step5.1 makes it a bounded higher range inverse. The domain and target weak norms are Hilbert norms since their finite weak-derivative graphs are closed in a finite sum of complete $L^2$ spaces, as in [F2]. This verifies the actual realizations, not just their formal smooth kernels. [F2, F3, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 For $q\ge2$ evaluation at $p$ is bounded by the compact-chart estimate in [F4], and is onto the fiber because a fixed smooth cutoff times any constant local frame vector has exactly that value. Let $K:E_p\to H^{q+1}(E)$ be this finite-dimensional lift. Its image complements the closed point-zero domain $Z=\ker\operatorname{ev}_p$. The operator $P_q-P_qK\operatorname{ev}_p$ differs from $P_q$ by finite rank, so is Fredholm of index $2m+2d$ by [F3]. In the splitting $H^{q+1}=Z\oplus K E_p$ it is exactly $P_q|_Z\oplus0$, with zero second summand of real dimension $2m$. Consequently $P_q|_Z$ has the same closed range, finite kernel and cokernel, and index $2m+2d-2m=2d$. All imposed point values occurred only in the actual $H^{q+1}$ space, never in ordinary $H^1$. For a pullback tangent bundle with $d=1$ this gives the asserted actual node-fixed index two. Nothing here establishes universal surjectivity or sphere avoidance. [F3, F4, step 4.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra] ∎
