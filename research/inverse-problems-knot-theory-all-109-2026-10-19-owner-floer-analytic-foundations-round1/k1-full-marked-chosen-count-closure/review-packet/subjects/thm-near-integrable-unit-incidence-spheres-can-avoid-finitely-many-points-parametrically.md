## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $m=h\ge1$ and let $j_s$, $s\in[0,1]$, be a smooth interval of complex structures on a closed genus-$h$ surface, with the finite smooth surface-polygon data required for the symmetric-product sphere index formula. Use an adapted relative symmetric-product trivialization fixing finitely many off-diagonal node values $x_1,\ldots,x_N$ and exact holomorphic neighborhoods of them. Fix compact protected diagonal and incidence-divisor neighborhoods disjoint from those node neighborhoods. Optionally fix finitely many compact submanifolds which are totally real for every standard background and whose full complex restrictions are fixed along the family.

There are arbitrarily small smooth autonomous nearly symmetric perturbations $J_s$ of the standard relative structures, fixed on all those protected neighborhoods and along those real submanifolds, for which NO $J_s$-holomorphic sphere of incidence degree one passes through any $x_i$, for ANY $s\in[0,1]$. They may be chosen from a genuine smooth separable Hilbert family; the parameters with this simultaneous avoidance contain a dense residual subset of a sufficiently small parameter ball.

For one fixed background this includes arbitrary sufficiently small generic protected perturbations in that family. This is an actual sphere-avoidance theorem for $m=h$, whose sphere determinant degree is one. It asserts neither avoidance of higher-incidence spheres nor a disk count, strip compactification or gluing result.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual compact relative target family, common exact node neighborhoods, fixed protected values, and the finite surface data in the sphere index theorem. Every perturbed structure is autonomous on the source sphere.

[F1] Unit-incidence spheres through the given nodes are compact modulo node-fixing holomorphic automorphisms, and EVERY such sphere for a sufficiently small perturbation of this compact standard family has an open set of globally unique immersion points outside all protected sets. The actual node-fixed $H^3\to H^2$ sphere operator is Fredholm of index $2d$, where $d$ is its determinant degree. ([[lem-unit-incidence-spheres-are-pointedly-compact-and-have-nearby-free-sites]], [[lem-countable-choice-geometric-sphere-cauchy-riemann-fredholm-and-point-index]])

[F2] The actual arbitrary-smooth-class sphere formula gives $d=(m+1-h)n_p$. Thus for $m=h$ and incidence degree one it gives $d=1$. This remains the complex determinant degree for every sufficiently close nearly symmetric structure: its pointwise intertwiner with the standard structure is invertible, giving an actual complex bundle isomorphism. ([[lem-multipointed-symmetric-product-spheres-have-index-shift-two-k]])

[F3] Every nonzero functional on the $H^2$ target annihilating the point-zero domain has a smooth nonzero adjoint representative on every nonempty source open set away from the node. The actual protected rapidly convergent Hilbert bump construction and Cayley density are proved in the near-integrable disk genericity theorem; their parameter construction is spatial and not particular to disks. ([[lem-point-fixed-sphere-cokernel-functionals-are-smooth-off-the-node]], [[thm-countable-choice-near-integrable-proper-unit-domain-disks-are-generically-regular]])

[F4] The actual local-addition, Sobolev algebra and composition proofs give smooth charts and a smooth section by their local finite-chart estimates, repeated below for a compact closed sphere. Actual split Hilbert zero charts and separable Hilbert Sard--Smale hold under Countable Choice. ([[lem-boundary-preserving-local-addition-for-transverse-product-tori]], [[lem-countable-choice-weighted-strip-sobolev-algebra-and-composition]], [[lem-higher-sobolev-strip-map-charts-and-cauchy-riemann-section]], [[lem-countable-choice-quantitative-newton-correction-and-smooth-zero-chart]], [[thm-countable-choice-sard-smale-for-separable-hilbert-manifolds]])

## Proof

1.1 Pull the compact relative target family to one smooth target by the stipulated adapted trivialization, and denote its standard structures by $K_s$. Construct the actual Hilbert variation family by [F3]'s finite-coordinate projected bumps, allowing smooth parameter cutoffs as well: project an endomorphism field to its anti-$K_s$ part $(A+K_sAK_s)/2$, support every bump in the common free open region, and multiply the countable list by explicit positive coefficients small enough that Cauchy--Schwarz bounds every spatial and parameter derivative of its sum. Rational-coordinate polynomial bumps are dense among supported smooth fields; finite parameter patches and cutoffs retain this density at every fixed $s$. The Cayley formula $J_{s,a}=K_s(I+B_{s,a})(I-B_{s,a})^{-1}$ gives a smooth family on one small $\ell^2$ ball, with all protected values fixed. Its derivative transfers that bump density to anti-$J_{s,a}$ variations. Shrink the ball so [F1]'s open free-site assertion holds simultaneously for every node and $s$, and so the prescribed tameness conditions hold on the compact unprotected region. Density remains valid at the physical parameter endpoints using one-sided smooth coefficient families. [F1, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Here are the actual configuration charts needed on the compact closed source. Around a smooth pointed sphere $u$ use [F4]'s smooth local addition and write $v(z)=\operatorname{add}(u(z),\xi(z))$ for small $H^3$ sections with $\xi(p)=0$. The finite compact-chart Sobolev estimates in [F4] give $H^2\subset C^0$, the $H^2$ product bound, and smooth coefficient composition; their proofs apply on sphere disks using plane cutoffs, with no strip weight or physical boundary. Thus this is a genuine open chart for maps with $v(p)=x$, and its overlap maps are smooth by the same finite chain and product formulas. Evaluation is bounded and has the finite point lift in [F1], so its tangent model is exactly the closed point-zero subspace. The equation $\bar\partial_{J_{s,a}}v$ is a smooth $H^2$ section on these $H^3$ charts, with fixed $(s,a)$ derivative the actual geometric sphere operator. It has index two by [F1] and [F2]. These charts are second-countable: the finite chart-cutoff embedding of the compact target into a finite Euclidean space used in [F4] embeds this map topology into $H^3(S^2,\mathbb R^M)$; that Hilbert space is separable by plane rational smooth approximation in finitely many source charts. Its subspace has a countable metric base. Every zero is smooth by [F1]'s actual weak higher regularity and the local nonlinear bootstrap in [F4]. This constructs the sphere setup rather than importing a strip configuration Statement with different source. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 The universal derivative in $u,a$ is onto at EVERY pointed unit-incidence zero. Its range is closed: the fixed sphere operator $P$ is Fredholm, and adjoining parameter forcings changes its range only by a linear subspace of its finite-dimensional range quotient. If the universal range were proper, orthogonal projection in the Hilbert $H^2$ target would give a nonzero continuous real functional $\ell$ annihilating it. In particular it annihilates $P$ on the point-zero domain. [F3] gives a smooth adjoint representative nonzero on every source open set away from $p$. By [F1], the sphere has a free unique immersion site; choose such a site where that representative is nonzero. A two-coordinate projection is an embedding on a smaller source patch, and global source compactness plus uniqueness gives a target neighborhood whose ENTIRE preimage lies in that patch. Prescribe an anti-$J$ field $Y$ there making its value on a nonzero image tangent pair positively with the adjoint representative. This is possible by defining $Y$ on the invariant real plane spanned by that tangent vector and its $J$ image, extending anti-complex-linearly on a complementary invariant plane, and using a smooth supported cutoff. Shrink so the integrated local pairing is positive. This forcing is zero near the fixed node and is supported entirely where the distributional representative is smooth, so its pairing with $\ell$ is exactly that positive ordinary integral. The dense finite actual parameter tangents in step1.1 approximate this supported field in a coefficient norm controlling the smooth pullback forcing in $H^2$. Their $\ell$ pairings converge to that positive number, contradicting annihilation. No assumption that an $H^2$-dual functional initially lies in $L^2$ entered the argument. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, contradiction]

4.1 Apply the actual split zero theorem to the open universal regular zero locus. A bounded right inverse follows explicitly by choosing finitely many parameter lifts of a basis of the fixed operator's finite range quotient and correcting its remaining range component by the bounded kernel-complement inverse of $P$; this is [F3]'s finite-lift construction. With $s$ fixed, projection of the universal zero manifold to $a$ has kernel and cokernel those of $P$, hence index two. With $s$ an interior variable its fixed derivative is $P\xi+c\tau$ and its index is three, because adjoining one real source variable raises the finite Schur-block index by one. The tangent equation gives that same index for projection to $a$. On the second-countable interior zero space apply [F4]'s actual Countable-Choice Sard--Smale theorem. Apply it also to each endpoint slice, whose universal derivative is onto by step3.1 and whose projection has index two. Intersect these residual sets for the finitely many nodes. Their intersection is dense residual in the parameter ball by the proved countable-chart Hilbert Baire theorem. At a selected parameter every interior zero would therefore have a surjective parameter-augmented operator of index three, and every endpoint zero a surjective fixed operator of index two. [F1, F3, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Neither kind of zero can exist. Put the marked source point at infinity in a global holomorphic sphere coordinate $z$. The holomorphic affine group $z\mapsto az+b$, $a\in\mathbb C^*$, $b\in\mathbb C$, fixes infinity and has real dimension four. It preserves the autonomous equation and the point value. Its four infinitesimal directions at a smooth sphere belong to the kernel of the actual node-fixed linearization, and to the augmented kernel with $s$ held fixed. They are independent: a real infinitesimal combination has source vector field $V(z)=\alpha z+\beta$, and $du(V)=0$ would force $V=0$ on the nonempty immersion patch of [F1], hence $\alpha=\beta=0$. Each such tangent really is an $H^3$ tangent, since the fixed solution is smooth and its finite smooth source automorphism curves differentiate in every finite Sobolev norm. Thus every alleged zero has kernel dimension at least four. A surjective Fredholm operator has kernel dimension its index, which is only three in the interior and two at the endpoints by step4.1. This contradiction excludes all unit-incidence spheres through the nodes for every $s$. Since the chosen parameters form a dense residual subset of an arbitrarily small ball, the asserted arbitrarily small simultaneous perturbations exist. Only incidence degree one and $m=h$ were used in the index contradiction; no higher-degree avoidance or disk-count conclusion is inferred. [F1, F2, step 2.1, step 4.1, construct, contradiction] ∎
