## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $A$ be the open interior of a bounded round disk with finitely many pairwise disjoint round CLOSED disks removed, all strictly inside the outer disk. Let its $e\ge1$ boundary circles be $\Gamma_j$, and choose $x_j\in\Gamma_j$. For each $j$ choose a real-circle Möbius coordinate $w_j$ taking $x_j$ to zero and the side of $A$ locally to the upper half-plane.

There is a unique real harmonic function $P_j$ on $A$ which is smooth up to every boundary point except $x_j$, vanishes there, and has
$$P_j=\operatorname{Im}(-1/w_j)+v_j$$
near $x_j$, with $v_j$ harmonic and smooth across that boundary arc and zero on the arc. It is strictly positive in $A$. Define $C_{ij}$ for $i\ne j$ as the outward flux $\int_{\Gamma_i}\partial_\nu P_j\,ds$. Define $C_{jj}$ as the flux across any nearby interior parallel of $\Gamma_j$, with normal pointing toward $\Gamma_j$. Then
$$C_{ij}<0\quad(i\ne j),\qquad \sum_iC_{ij}=0.$$
The latter diagonal flux is finite and independent of the parallel. The real matrix $C$ has rank $e-1$. Its kernel is generated by a vector with every entry strictly positive, uniquely normalized by $\sum_jc_j=1$.

Consequently $H=\sum_jc_jP_j$ is a positive harmonic function with one prescribed positive simple boundary pole at each $x_j$ and zero flux around every boundary component. Every harmonic function vanishing on the boundary apart from simple poles at these points, with the same local type and zero boundary periods, is a real multiple of $H$. The last uniqueness permits arbitrary real residues; positivity makes that multiple positive for a nonzero nonnegative function.

This supplies actual harmonic kernels and their finite period equation. It asserts no holomorphic proper-cover or symmetric-product disk construction by itself.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite disjoint round boundary circles, one point and the chosen Möbius coordinate at each, and the outward normal convention above. Periods of a harmonic function mean the integrals of its conjugate closed differential, equivalently these normal fluxes with a consistent orientation.

[F1] [[thm-perron-solves-dirichlet-on-regular-plane-domains]] supplies a continuous harmonic extension of continuous data on a regular bounded plane domain. [[lem-planar-barrier-controls-perron-solutions]] proves regularity from a negative subharmonic barrier. [[cor-uniqueness-for-the-bounded-plane-dirichlet-problem]] proves uniqueness.

[F2] [[thm-poisson-integral-solves-the-disc-dirichlet-problem]] gives the actual harmonic extension of continuous circle data. [[thm-local-holomorphic-potential-for-harmonic-functions]] implies interior harmonic functions are smooth. The weak and strong maximum principles are [[thm-weak-maximum-principle-for-the-laplacian]] and [[thm-strong-maximum-principle-for-harmonic-functions]].

[F3] [[thm-hopf-boundary-point-lemma-for-the-laplacian]] gives strictly negative outward derivative at a smooth boundary zero of a strictly positive harmonic function with an interior tangent ball. All the round boundaries have such balls locally on the side of $A$.

## Proof

1.1 Every boundary point $x$ is regular for [F1]. Indeed choose a sufficiently small closed round disk in the complement tangent to its boundary circle only at $x$, with center $a$ and radius $R$. It is contained in the deleted hole for an inner boundary, or outside the outer disk for an outer boundary. The function $b(z)=-\log(|z-a|/R)$ is harmonic and negative on $A$, tends to zero at $x$, and is uniformly negative on the compact boundary away from any neighborhood of $x$. It is therefore the required barrier. For the chosen $j$, the rational harmonic function $K_j=\operatorname{Im}(-1/w_j)$ has its only possible singularity at $x_j$. A pole of $w_j$ causes none, since its reciprocal vanishes smoothly there. Its boundary values on $\Gamma_j\setminus\{x_j\}$ are zero, and on the other circles they are smooth. Give [F1] the continuous boundary datum equal to $K_j$ on those other circles and zero everywhere on $\Gamma_j$, including $x_j$. Let its extension be $V_j$, and set $P_j=K_j-V_j$. This constructs a harmonic function with the required boundary values away from $x_j$. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 We justify smooth boundary extension rather than assume a Schauder theorem. A harmonic function continuous on a closed upper half-disk and zero on its diameter extends oddly to a smooth harmonic function on a smaller full disk. To see this, prescribe on the boundary circle the given upper semicircle values and their negative reflected lower values. They match at the endpoints since the diameter values are zero. The Poisson extension in [F2] is odd by its uniqueness and reflection invariance, so it is zero on the diameter. Its upper restriction equals the original harmonic function by uniqueness on the bounded half-disk. Thus it is the desired extension and is smooth by [F2]. Apply this after a local Möbius straightening of each round edge: to $P_j$ away from $x_j$, and to $V_j$ near $x_j$, where its boundary data are zero. Consequently $P_j=\operatorname{Im}(-1/w_j)$ plus a smooth harmonic zero-edge remainder at its pole. In the straightened coordinate the remainder is bounded by a constant times $\operatorname{Im}w_j$, while the leading term is $\operatorname{Im}w_j/|w_j|^2$. Hence $P_j>0$ sufficiently near that punctured boundary point. Excise a sufficiently small half-disk there and use the weak minimum principle on its compact remainder, whose other boundary values are zero. This gives $P_j\ge0$ on all of $A$. It is not zero, by its pole, so the strong principle makes it strictly positive. The difference of two claimed kernels has canceled pole, is continuous with zero boundary data, and is harmonic; [F1] makes it zero. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 For $i\ne j$, $P_j$ is smooth up to $\Gamma_i$, positive inside and zero on it. [F3] gives $\partial_\nu P_j<0$ at every point of that circle, and integration gives $C_{ij}<0$. For two sufficiently close interior parallels of $\Gamma_j$, apply the harmonic divergence identity to the annulus between them; their fluxes toward $\Gamma_j$ agree. They are finite because those curves are compact in $A$. On the region with all boundary circles pushed slightly inward, the sum of all outward fluxes is zero. Let the parallels to $\Gamma_i$, $i\ne j$, tend to their boundaries; their derivatives converge smoothly by step2.1, while the remaining flux is constant. Thus $C_{jj}=-\sum_{i\ne j}C_{ij}$, proving the column sum. The divergence identity used here is the elementary plane Green identity: for a smooth field, subdivide the compact region into finitely many coordinate rectangles and boundary graph patches, integrate its two coordinate derivatives by the fundamental theorem of calculus and cancel their common edges. It applies away from the pole, so no divergent integral at $x_j$ is taken. [F2, F3, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 If $e=1$, step3.1 gives $C=(0)$, and the claimed kernel is generated by $(1)$. Suppose $e>1$. To compute its rank, let $C^Tz=0$ and choose a largest entry $z_j$. Using the column sum gives $$0=(C^Tz)_j=\sum_{i\ne j}(-C_{ij})(z_j-z_i).$$ Every coefficient is strictly positive and every difference nonnegative, so every entry equals $z_j$. Conversely constants lie in this kernel. Thus the rank is $e-1$. For a positive vector in $\ker C$, choose $\alpha>\max_jC_{jj}$ and put $T=I-C/\alpha$. All entries of $T$ are strictly positive and its columns sum to one. Starting with any probability vector $v_0$, the averages $v_N=N^{-1}\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}T^rv_0$ lie in the compact finite simplex. Extract a convergent subsequence. Since $(I-T)v_N=(v_0-T^Nv_0)/N\to0$, its limit $c$ satisfies $Tc=c$, $\sum c_j=1$ and $c_j\ge0$. Strict positivity of $T$ now makes every $c_j>0$. The rank computation makes this normalized vector unique. This is a finite-dimensional compactness argument, not a Perron--Frobenius theorem requiring an unreviewed supplier. [step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 The function $H=\sum_jc_jP_j$ has the stated positivity and poles, and its boundary period vector is $Cc=0$. For any other function of the stated local type, let $d_j\in\mathbb R$ be its leading coefficient relative to $\operatorname{Im}(-1/w_j)$. Subtract $\sum_jd_jP_j$. The local expansions in step2.1 cancel every pole, so the difference is continuous on the closed region, harmonic inside and zero on all boundary circles. Uniqueness in [F1] makes that difference zero. Its period vector is therefore $Cd$; zero periods and step4.1 make $d$ a real multiple of $c$. If the function is nonnegative and nonzero, its nonzero leading coefficients cannot be negative, and that multiple must be positive. This proves the complete kernel and uniqueness assertions, without asserting any proper holomorphic map or disk not yet constructed. Only finite choices and finite-dimensional subsequence extraction were added to the actual harmonic suppliers. [F1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
