## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Put $X=\operatorname{Sym}^m\Sigma$ for a closed connected Riemann surface, and fix finitely many product tori $L_j$ from systems of pairwise disjoint attaching curves. Let $a\in\Sigma$ lie off all these curves. The actual point-divisor bundle of $E_a=\{a\}+\operatorname{Sym}^{m-1}\Sigma$, with its canonical section on the boundary, defines integer degrees $n_a$ for disks and spheres, including contained spheres. It also defines an integer for every smooth strip with its two boundary edges on the specified tori and uniform ends at their intersections: truncate and cap its ends inside sufficiently small generator charts avoiding $E_a$. The integer is independent of the sufficiently remote cuts and these local caps, and agrees with proper holomorphic intersection multiplicity whenever the latter is defined.

In the actual bounded-energy compactness conclusions of the bordered-source bubble-tree theorem, when the fixed source is a disk, or of the bubbled transverse-strip breaking theorem, these degrees satisfy, for all sufficiently large sequence indices,
$$n_a(u_n)=\sum_{\text{base or strip levels }v}n_a(v)+\sum_{\text{bubble components }w}n_a(w).$$
The hypotheses of those compactness theorems, including their common exact transverse generator charts for strips, are retained. No avoidance of $E_a$ by interior nodes or sphere bubbles is required. The formula holds simultaneously for any fixed finite set of such points.

In particular suppose the strip sequence satisfies that transverse-strip compactness theorem and the compact-bubble domain-energy lemma, with common diagonal and region-divisor neighborhoods and uniformly bounded geometric constants. Let $z_1,\ldots,z_k$ be marked points off all attaching curves, with $J_{n,t}$ and its limit equal to the standard symmetric-product structure near their divisors. Require that each connected component of the complement of each individual boundary curve system contains at least one of these marked points. If $n_{z_i}(u_n)=0$ for every $i,n$, its limit has no nonconstant sphere or disk bubbles. In fact no bubble vertices remain after discarding redundant constant vertices, and every retained strip level is nonconstant and has $n_{z_i}=0$ for every $i$. Thus that actual compactness theorem reduces to finite breaking of unbubbled zero-marked strips, with its smooth local convergence and full energy and neck-collapse conclusions. This does not assert regularity of same-generator index-two strips, a Maslov/domain-index identity, low-index stratum dimensions, or gluing.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual compact symmetric product, boundary tori and avoided marked divisors in the statement, and an actual subsequence and limit furnished by one of the specified compactness theorems. In the last assertion all its additional nearly symmetric and complete marked-complement hypotheses are imposed.

[F1] [[lem-symmetric-product-divisor-intersections-and-point-movement]] constructs the actual smooth point-divisor line bundle and canonical section, its disk relative degree and sphere clutch degree by winding, their homotopy invariance, and their agreement with positive proper holomorphic intersections.

[F2] [[thm-countable-choice-totally-real-boundary-gromov-bubble-tree-compactness]] constructs the actual finite disk/sphere tree on a fixed bordered source, with smooth convergence on all component cores, equal node values and complete energy identity. [[thm-countable-choice-bubbled-transverse-strip-breaking-compactness]] gives those actual component limits on finitely many translated strip levels, their common end generators, complete energy identity and vanishing energy and image diameter on all intervening necks and outer tails.

[F3] [[lem-nearly-symmetric-compact-bubble-domain-positivity-and-energy]] proves nonnegative compact-bubble region degrees and domain-controlled energy, even for spheres wholly in the diagonal or in a point divisor. A sphere with one zero point degree is constant. A one-torus disk with zero degree at a point in each component of its individual curve-system complement is constant. [[lem-pointed-heegaard-integrable-divisor-positivity]] proves proper divisor positivity for actual strips with ends outside the divisor.

## Proof

1.1 Write $\mathcal H$ for the actual point-divisor line bundle and $\sigma$ for its canonical section from [F1]. The compact set $L=\bigcup_jL_j$ is disjoint from $E_a$, so choose open sets $L\subset U_1\Subset U_0\subset X\setminus E_a$. On $U_0$ use the frame $\sigma$ and its flat connection. Cover the rest of the compact target by finitely many smooth complex frames of $\mathcal H$, each with its flat local connection. Choose a smooth partition subordinate to these sets with the coefficient of the first one identically one on $U_1$: take a cutoff $\chi$ supported in $U_0$, equal to one near $\overline U_1$, and multiply a subordinate partition on the remaining cover by $1-\chi$. Such finite partitions are obtained from smooth coordinate bumps and division by their positive finite sum. The weighted sum of the local connections defines a smooth connection $\nabla$ on $\mathcal H$. Indeed each local summand obeys $\nabla^i(fs)=df\,s+f\nabla^is$, and their weights sum to one, giving that identity globally; frame-change identities therefore also agree. On $U_1$ it is exactly the flat connection with $\nabla\sigma=0$. In a local frame $e$ write $\nabla e=Ae$ and define $F=dA$. If $e'=g e$, then $A'=A+g^{-1}dg$ and $d(g^{-1}dg)=0$, so $F$ is a global smooth complex-valued two-form. It vanishes on $U_1$ and has bounded pointwise norm on compact $X$. This is a constructed connection and curvature, not an imported characteristic-class formula. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 For every smooth disk map with boundary in $L$, pull the bundle back and choose its disk frame $e$ as constructed in [F1]. On the boundary write $u^*\sigma=f e$. Since $\nabla\sigma=0$ near $L$, its connection form there is $A=-f^{-1}df$. The two-variable fundamental theorem of calculus, with a finite coordinate partition, gives Stokes and hence $$\int_Du^*F=\int_{\partial D}A=-2\pi i\,\operatorname{wind}f=-2\pi i\,n_a(u).$$ For a sphere use frames $e_N,e_S$ on hemispheres and $e_S=g e_N$ on their equator, positively oriented as the northern boundary. The transition identity of step 1.1 and opposite southern boundary orientation give $$\int_{S^2}u^*F=\int_{\mathrm{eq}}(A_N-A_S)=-2\pi i\,\operatorname{wind}g=-2\pi i\,n_a(u).$$ Thus in both cases $n_a(u)=-(2\pi i)^{-1}\int u^*F$, with exactly the winding conventions in [F1]. No statement about the zeros of the pulled-back canonical section was used, so contained spheres and nodes on the divisor cause no exception. [F1, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 The same formula defines and computes the strip integer. Its two uniform ends lie in arbitrarily small generator charts inside $U_1$, chosen so the two tori become transverse product lines. On each sufficiently remote end multiply its coordinate map by a smooth real cutoff which equals one at the inner end and zero beyond a further finite coordinate. The chart can be a star-shaped ball. Real scalar multiplication preserves both sets of boundary lines, and the modified map becomes identically the generator outside that compact interval. Close the resulting rectangle by constant caps, rounding its corners inside these constant regions. It is a smooth disk map with boundary in the union of the tori. All modified and capped portions lie in $U_1$, where $F=0$. Consequently its relative degree is exactly $$n_a(u)=-\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb R\times[0,1]}u^*F.$$ This integral is absolutely convergent for a finite-energy strip; more generally it has compact source support once the ends lie in $U_1$. The formula proves independence of all such cutoffs and local caps and agrees with [F1] for the capped map. If the strip has proper holomorphic intersections with $E_a$, they all lie on its unchanged compact part, since the tails and caps avoid that divisor. Its degree is their positive local sum by [F1]. This is in particular the degree in [F3], with the same sign. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 We give the analytic bound which makes this degree stable through all the actual necks. For any smooth map from an oriented conformal surface, bounded curvature gives pointwise $$|u^*F(\partial_x,\partial_y)|\le C|u_x|_g|u_y|_g\le \tfrac C2(|u_x|_g^2+|u_y|_g^2).$$ Thus $|\int_Au^*F|\le C E_g(u;A)$ on every measurable source subset, for one fixed constant. Both the two-form integral and this two-dimensional Dirichlet energy are invariant under orientation-preserving conformal reparametrization, by ordinary change of variables and cancellation of the conformal scale. Smooth convergence on a compact component core gives convergence of its curvature integral and energy, since all first derivatives converge uniformly there. These assertions hold as well for the affine rescalings, half-plane rescalings and longitudinal translations in the actual [F2] construction. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Apply these bounds to the finite limit supplied by [F2]. Choose pairwise disjoint compact cores in every component, with boundary avoiding its finite nodes, and with the sum of omitted component energies less than $\epsilon$. Such cores exhaust each punctured component, and there are finitely many components. Their images under the actual source translations and rescalings are disjoint for all large $n$: child scales tend to zero inside the deleted parent-node neighborhoods, distinct siblings have distinct nodes, and distinct strip levels separate longitudinally. Smooth core convergence gives the sum of core energies and curvature integrals. By the full energy identity in [F2], the energy of $u_n$ outside all these cores has limit equal to the omitted component energy, hence is less than $2\epsilon$ for large $n$. Step 4.1 bounds the absolute curvature integral of that remaining source by $2C\epsilon$ and bounds the omitted limit-component integrals by $C\epsilon$. First let $n$ tend to infinity and then let $\epsilon$ tend to zero. We obtain $$\lim_n\int u_n^*F=\sum_v\int v^*F+\sum_w\int w^*F.$$ Steps 2.1 and 3.1 convert this to convergence of the integer $n_a(u_n)$ to the displayed finite sum of component integers. An integer converging to an integer is eventually equal to it, proving the precise asserted additivity. In particular no neck winding or hidden divisor charge remains, even when an interior node lies in $E_a$. For finitely many marked points repeat the constructed connection and take the maximum of their finitely many eventual index thresholds. [F2, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Now impose the additional zero-marked nearly symmetric hypotheses. For every strip level, its ends are generator tuples avoiding $E_{z_i}$, and the limiting almost complex structures are standard near that divisor. The local holomorphic defining function and zero-order argument in [F3] show that its intersections are isolated; they form a compact finite set because of end avoidance, and the map cannot be contained in the divisor because its ends avoid it. Hence $n_{z_i}(v)\ge0$, also for a constant generator level where the degree is zero. Every sphere and one-torus disk bubble satisfies the compact-map hypotheses of [F3] for its actual frozen almost complex structure. Its marked degrees are nonnegative: for a sphere this holds at every point by point movement, and for a disk at every point off its individual boundary curves by the constant-on-complement domain identity there. Additivity in step 5.1 has zero left side for every mark. Each summand is a nonnegative integer, so every strip and bubble summand is zero for every mark. A sphere is therefore constant by [F3]. Each disk has a mark in each actual component of its boundary-system complement by hypothesis, so [F3] likewise makes it constant. This exclusion does not use weak admissibility for two-torus periodic domains, or an identification of an analytic index with a Maslov formula. [F3, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 No nonempty finite bubble tree in the actual [F2] construction can consist only of constant bubbles. Its leaves would be constant, whereas a constant sphere there must have at least three nodes and a constant disk must have boundary-node count plus twice interior-node count at least three. A leaf has only its parent node, giving respectively one node or weighted count at most two; it violates those inequalities. Removing redundant constant vertices therefore leaves no bubble vertices at all. Every retained strip level in the strip theorem has strictly positive total energy including its bubbles. Once the bubbles are absent, its strip is nonconstant. Step 6.1 gives its zero marked degrees, and all its remaining smooth local convergence, matched ends, energy identity and collapsing-neck assertions are exactly the actual conclusions of [F2]. No regularity, index or gluing assertion has been added. The construction used finite bundle charts, explicit connections, the actual previously proved countable compactness extraction, and finite sums; it preserves Countable Choice. [F2, step 6.1, construct, algebra] ∎
