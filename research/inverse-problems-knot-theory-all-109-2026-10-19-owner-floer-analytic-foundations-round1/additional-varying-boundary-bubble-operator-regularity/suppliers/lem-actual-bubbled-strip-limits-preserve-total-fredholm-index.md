## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Retain all the compact-target, coefficient convergence, fixed boundary structures, bounded energy and common exact product generator-chart hypotheses of the bubbled transverse-strip breaking theorem. Write $m$ for the complex dimension, and require that each compact boundary manifold $L_0,L_1$ is oriented. Thus the common generator charts identify their tangent planes with $\mathbb R^m,i\mathbb R^m$, and every $J_{n,t}$ is exactly $i$ there for every $t$.

For any actual subsequence and finite bubbled strip limit supplied by that theorem, let $v_j$ denote its strip levels, $d_b$ its disk bubbles, and $s_c$ its sphere bubbles, including constant components if retained. Give each disk its actual frozen boundary structure $J_{\infty,0}$ or $J_{\infty,1}$ and each sphere its actual frozen structure $J_{\infty,t_c}$. Let $\mu(d_b)$ be its squared real-determinant boundary winding, and let $c_1(s_c)$ be the determinant clutch degree of its pulled-back complex tangent bundle. For all sufficiently large $n$,
$$\operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb R}D_{u_n}=\sum_j\operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb R}D_{v_j}+\sum_b\mu(d_b)+2\sum_c c_1(s_c).$$
Strip indices are the actual $H^1\to L^2$ Fredholm indices before translation quotient, including index zero for constant generator strips. Disk Maslov indices are equivalently compact disk operator indices minus $m$. No regularity, sign of an index, sphere or disk count, domain-index formula, or gluing is assumed or concluded. In particular the formula includes boundary bubbling on constant strip levels and does not require a bubble node to be a generator or to avoid a divisor.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual sequence, its actual compactness subsequence and finite component tree, and the oriented boundary manifolds in the Statement.

[F1] The geometric strip frame theorem constructs a global complex frame, constant in both end charts, with lower real boundary and upper boundary $e^{i\vartheta(s)}\mathbb R\oplus i\mathbb R^{m-1}$, where $\vartheta_-=\pi/2$, $\vartheta_+=\pi/2+\ell\pi$. Its actual Fredholm index is $-\ell$. ([[lem-geometric-strip-linearization-frame-and-fredholm-index]])

[F2] The actual oriented compact disk operator is Fredholm of index $m+\mu$, where $\mu$ is its squared real-determinant boundary winding. Its proof constructs global complex disk frames rather than assuming a bundle trivialization. ([[lem-geometric-oriented-disk-cauchy-riemann-fredholm-and-higher-regularity]])

[F3] The actual strip compactness theorem gives finitely many separated translated levels, finite rescaled disk/sphere trees, smooth convergence on disjoint component cores, full energy identity, and image collapse to a generator on the long interlevel necks and outer tails. Its proof gives fixed $A$ and a bound $C e^{-c(R-A)/2}$ for the distance to a generator outside the translated radius-$R$ cores, uniformly for sufficiently large $n$. ([[thm-countable-choice-bubbled-transverse-strip-breaking-compactness]])

[F4] Finite bundle connections, their frame-change identity, Stokes on smooth disk and sphere pieces, and the conversion of boundary or clutch winding to $-(2\pi i)^{-1}$ times curvature integral are explicitly proved in the divisor additivity supplier. We use that local connection argument for a different actual line bundle below, not its divisor claim as a Maslov premise. ([[lem-divisor-degree-additivity-and-zero-marked-strip-bubble-exclusion]])

## Proof

1.1 Put $Y=[0,1]\times X$ and let $\mathcal E_{(t,x)}=(T_xX,J_{\infty,t}(x))$ be its actual smooth complex rank-$m$ bundle. Set $\mathcal L=(\det_{\mathbb C}\mathcal E)^2$. Choose a smooth Hermitian metric by a finite complex chart partition, equal to the standard one on smaller disjoint generator neighborhoods for all $t$. Each oriented totally real boundary manifold gives a canonical unit section $\sigma_j$ of $\mathcal L$ on $N_j=\{j\}\times L_j$: square the wedge of any real tangent basis and divide by its norm. Replacing that basis multiplies its squared wedge by a positive real number, so the unit section is independent of it. In a standard generator frame its values are $1$ on $N_0$ and $i^{2m}=(-1)^m$ on $N_1$. Construct a unitary connection $\nabla^0$ by a finite partition sum of unit-frame flat connections, with connection form zero in these generator neighborhoods, as in [F4]. On $N_j$ write $\nabla^0\sigma_j=i\eta_j\sigma_j$, where $\eta_j$ is a real smooth tangential one-form. It vanishes on the generator neighborhoods. Extend $\eta_j$ to $Y$: in finitely many coordinates straightening $N_j$, extend its coefficient functions constantly in the normal directions and use normal cutoffs and a finite partition whose restrictions sum to one on $N_j$. Supports can avoid smaller generator neighborhoods because $\eta_j$ is zero on larger ones. The two constructions have disjoint supports near $t=0,1$. Subtract $i$ times these extended one-forms from $\nabla^0$. The resulting unitary connection $\nabla$ satisfies $\nabla\sigma_j=0$ tangentially on $N_j$ and remains flat with zero connection form on common smaller generator charts for every $t$. Its curvature $F$ is a bounded imaginary two-form on compact $Y$. This constructs the actual boundary-flat connection; no extension of the two boundary phases across a generator intersection is needed, since $N_0,N_1$ are disjoint in $Y$. [F4, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The same connection computes the indices for all sufficiently large $n$, despite their different almost complex structures. Smooth convergence makes $$A_n(t,x)=\tfrac12(I-J_{n,t}(x)J_{\infty,t}(x))$$ uniformly close to the identity and therefore invertible by its convergent finite-dimensional Neumann series. Direct multiplication gives $A_nJ_\infty=J_nA_n$. On $N_0,N_1$ the boundary structures are fixed, so $A_n=I$ there; it is also $I$ on every exact generator chart for all $t$. Its squared determinant is thus a complex line isomorphism from $\mathcal L$ to the corresponding line for $J_n$, equal to the identity on the physical boundary and generator charts. Transport $\nabla$ through this isomorphism. Its curvature is the SAME scalar two-form $F$ on $Y$, its boundary phases stay parallel, and its generator connection forms stay zero. A transported Hermitian metric makes it unitary. This is an actual isomorphism of the varying complex bundles and preserves the boundary winding; it is not an identification of real bundles alone. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 For a compact disk $d$ frozen at $t=j$, use [F2]'s complex disk frame and square its determinant to frame $d^*\mathcal L$. On its boundary write $\sigma_j=f e$ with $|f|=1$ after normalizing that frame. Parallelism gives its connection form $a=-f^{-1}df$ on the boundary. Stokes, with [F4]'s convention, gives $$\mu(d)=-\frac1{2\pi i}\int_D(j,d)^*F.$$ For a sphere $s$ frozen at $t_c$, use two hemisphere determinant frames. Their equatorial transition $g$ has winding $c_1(s)$, and their squared transition has winding $2c_1(s)$. Applying the same frame-change and opposite-boundary-orientation computation of [F4] gives $$2c_1(s)=-\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{S^2}(t_c,s)^*F.$$ These computations use actual line frames and clutch windings, including spheres meeting arbitrary loci, and constant components have zero integral and zero contribution. [F2, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.3 We control the time-dependent curvature term explicitly. Decompose $F=F_X+dt\wedge\beta$, where $F_X$ is its restriction to tangent vectors in $X$ and $\beta$ is a bounded family of one-forms in those directions. Writing $\widehat u_n(s,t)=(t,u_n(s,t))$, for the physical strip map, $$|\widehat u_n^*F(\partial_s,\partial_t)|\le C|u_{n,s}||u_{n,t}|+C|u_{n,s}|.$$ On a measurable set of physical area at most $B$ this implies $$\left|\int\widehat u_n^*F\right|\le C E_g(u_n)+C\sqrt{2B E_g(u_n)}.$$ There is one finite $B$ sufficient for ALL nonzero curvature integrands in the sequence. Indeed choose a fixed $R_0>A$ in [F3] large enough that its uniform generator-distance estimate lies inside the smaller flat charts of step1.1. Outside the union of its $M$ translated rectangles $[q_{j,n}-R_0,q_{j,n}+R_0]\times[0,1]$, the images lie in those flat charts for large $n$. The curvature integrand is zero there. Their total physical area is at most $B=2MR_0$, independent of the separating translations and neck lengths. The same support property holds for the limiting strip levels. Thus a remainder with energy tending to zero has curvature integral tending to zero, including its $dt$ term. No estimate of a mixed $dt$ term by energy alone, and no bounded length for a breaking neck, has been presumed. [F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 For any one of the actual strips $u$ let $\widehat u(s,t)=(t,u(s,t))$. Its far ends lie in the flat generator charts. Apply [F1]'s frame, using the transported connection of step2.1 for $u_n$. In its unit-normalized squared determinant frame the lower boundary phase is constant $1$, and the upper phase is $\exp(2i\vartheta(s)+(m-1)i\pi)$. On the two remote vertical edges the connection form is zero because that frame is exactly the fixed end frame up to a constant real sign matrix, whose determinant square is one. On the bottom the connection form is zero by boundary parallelism, and on the top it is $-2i\vartheta'(s)ds$. The top edge has orientation from positive to negative $s$. Consequently Stokes on a sufficiently long rectangle gives $$-\frac1{2\pi i}\int\widehat u^*F=-\frac{\vartheta_+-\vartheta_-}{\pi}=-\ell=\operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb R}D_u.$$ The integral has compact source support, since $F$ is zero on its two far generator tails. The constant phases $1$ and $(-1)^m$ at each vertical edge need not coincide; no parallel boundary section around the whole rectangle was assumed. The displayed connection-form calculation accounts for them without an extra corner term. For a constant generator strip [F1] has $\ell=0$ and the same integral is zero. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Choose disjoint compact component cores as in [F3], excluding small disks about their nodes, with total omitted component energy less than $\epsilon$. Strip cores use longitudinal translations; their time coordinate remains $t$, so smooth convergence gives the integrals of $(t,v_j)^*F$ on them. On a bubble core, its actual source rescaling has physical time coordinate converging smoothly to its frozen value $t_c$, with derivative tending to zero. This follows directly from its vanishing affine rescaling factor; successive child rescalings have the same property. Thus the $F_X$ part converges to the integral on the actual frozen bubble, while the pulled-back $dt\wedge\beta$ part tends to zero on that compact core. The actual conformal change of variables preserves its two-form integral and Dirichlet energy. These core images are disjoint for large $n$, by the separated levels and nested vanishing child scales in [F3]. Its full energy identity bounds the energy of the remaining physical source by $2\epsilon$ for large $n$. Step2.3, restricted to the fixed-area support, bounds its curvature integral by $C\epsilon+C\sqrt{B\epsilon}$. The omitted sphere and disk core integrals are bounded by $C\epsilon$, since their time coordinate is constant. The omitted limiting strip integrals obey the same fixed-area estimate. Taking the sequence limit and then $\epsilon\downarrow0$ therefore proves $$\lim_n\int\widehat u_n^*F=\sum_j\int(t,v_j)^*F+\sum_b\int(j_b,d_b)^*F+\sum_c\int(t_c,s_c)^*F.$$ This uses the actual component and neck conclusions of compactness, rather than an assumed topological bubble-class splitting. [F3, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 2.3, construct, algebra]

5.1 By steps2.2 and3.1 the preceding equality is convergence of the integer $\operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb R}D_{u_n}$ to the finite integer $\sum_j\operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb R}D_{v_j}+\sum_b\mu(d_b)+2\sum_c c_1(s_c)$. Every integer within distance less than $1/2$ of that limiting integer equals it; hence equality holds for all sufficiently large $n$. Constant ghost disks and spheres have trivial pullback bundle and constant boundary phase, so contribute zero and may be discarded. [F2] identifies each disk contribution with its actual compact operator index minus $m$, as claimed. All added constructions used finite charts, explicitly invertible matrices, scalar winding, and ordinary integral estimates; the only infinite extraction is the already proved Countable-Choice compactness in [F3]. No index sign, regularity, domain formula, node-evaluation surjectivity, or gluing theorem was inserted. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
