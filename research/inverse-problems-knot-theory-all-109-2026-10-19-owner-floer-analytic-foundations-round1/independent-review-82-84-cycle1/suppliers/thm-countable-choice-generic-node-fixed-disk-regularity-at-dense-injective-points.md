## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Use the compact target and one oriented product torus $L$ of the actual node-fixed disk-chart lemma. Fix a smooth autonomous background $J^b$, totally real on $L$, and finitely many disjoint compact generator neighborhoods $A_x$ inside larger exact background charts taking $J^b$ to $i$ and $L$ to $\mathbb R^m$. Choose smaller working generator charts with closures inside $\operatorname{Int}(A_x)$. Let $D$ be compact and disjoint from $L\cup\bigcup_xA_x$, containing any protected diagonal or divisor neighborhoods. Put $P_X=D\cup L\cup\bigcup_xA_x$. For optional tameness assume the closure of $X\setminus P_X$ is a compact subset of a smooth two-form's open taming region and that $J^b$ tames it there.

There is an actual separable Hilbert ball of smooth autonomous structures $J(a)$ preserving all values on $P_X$, the smaller working charts and optional tameness. A dense residual subset of its parameters has the following property, simultaneously for all the finitely many node values $x$: every nonconstant $J(a)$ disk with boundary on $L$, marked boundary value $x$, and dense set
$$I(u)=\{z\in\mathbb D^\circ:du(z)\ne0,\ u^{-1}(u(z))=\{z\}\}$$
of interior injective points has surjective actual compact node-fixed $H^3\to H^2$ linearization. It is also surjective in every weighted node-fixed realization at weights $0<\delta<\pi$ and every integer order. The actual universal derivative is onto at every such disk, not merely postulated to be onto.

The density hypothesis on $I(u)$ is retained. No equivalence with a weaker somewhere-injective condition is asserted. In particular the index-two cover obstruction does not alone prove this hypothesis for every disk. The result supplies neither generic regularity of all boundary degenerations, their global count, nor attachment gluing or cancellation.

## Proof

1.1 In $O=X\setminus P_X$ enumerate smooth compactly supported projected rational-coordinate matrix bumps $B_n$ anti-commuting with $J^b$, by the actual construction in [F2]. Finite partitions, convolution and Riemann sums prove density of their finite span in every fixed $C^k$ norm on compactly supported anti-$J^b$ fields. Their evaluations span each free anti-complex-linear fiber. Define $c_n=2^{-n}/(1+\max_{j\le n}\|B_n\|_{C^j})$ and $B(a)=\sum_na_nc_nB_n$, $a\in\ell^2$. Cauchy–Schwarz gives uniform convergence of every derivative and a bounded map into every $C^k$. In particular all fields and their derivatives vanish on $P_X$; its overlap $L\cap A_x$ causes no exception. On a small Hilbert ball put $J(a)=J^b(I+B)(I-B)^{-1}$. The exact Cayley multiplication in [F2] gives $J(a)^2=-I$, compatible smooth maps into every $C^k$ and the required protected values. A uniform positive taming minimum on the stipulated compact free closure preserves tameness on a smaller ball. The tangent isomorphism $dJ[C]=2J^b(I-B)^{-1}C(I-B)^{-1}$ and its explicit inverse transfer density to compactly supported anti-$J(a)$ fields. In particular $Yv$ can be prescribed arbitrarily for any nonzero $v$: specify it on the real plane spanned by $v,J(a)v$ and set it zero on an invariant orthogonal complement, as in [F2]. These are spatial autonomous fields, not source-dependent perturbations. [F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Any nonconstant disk has a nonempty open free-image preimage. Suppose instead its full image were contained in $P_X$. It contains its node $x$ in $L\cup\bigcup A_x$, and $D$ is separated from that compact union, so connectedness excludes $D$. If an interior point mapped into $L\setminus\bigcup A_x$, a small target neighborhood would miss all the $A_x$ and $D$; its nonempty interior source preimage would lie wholly in $L$. There $du$ takes values in $TL$, and the equation makes both $du$ and $Jdu$ tangent, forcing $du=0$ by total reality. The section $u_s$ satisfies the actual linearized homogeneous Cauchy–Riemann equation in each interior chart. Unique continuation [F3] propagates this open vanishing through the connected interior, and the equation then forces $u$ constant. Thus for a nonconstant disk every interior value must lie in the disjoint union of the $A_x$. Connectedness puts them all in one $A_x$, and continuity does the same on the boundary. On that protected background chart $J=i$ and the boundary coordinate values are real. Each imaginary coordinate is harmonic with zero boundary values; integration by parts gives its gradient square integral zero. The Cauchy–Riemann equation then makes the real coordinates constant too, again a contradiction. Hence some interior point has value in $O$, and openness gives the required nonempty source open set. This proof explicitly handles $L$ meeting the generator neighborhoods; it does not treat them as separated protected components. [F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 We justify the actual obstruction test despite the compact node-value constraint. The compact universal section on the node-fixed charts of [F1] is jointly smooth by the finite Sobolev product and coefficient estimates there and step 1.1. At a smooth zero its derivative is $P\xi+Kb$, with $Kb=(d_aJ[b])(u)u_t$ in source coordinates. Every $Kb$ is smooth and vanishes on a common node neighborhood, since $u$ takes that neighborhood into the protected $A_x$. Thus it belongs boundedly to every compact or weighted target. Consider first the full, node-free compact and weighted operators. Their finite range quotients identify by [F1], and the images of $Kb$ in those quotients are the same; hence surjectivity of their full augmented operators is equivalent. Their augmented kernels have the same actual pairs $(b,\xi)$: a kernel equation has smooth forcing $-Kb$ zero near the node, so compact elliptic regularity gives a smooth solution there; its homogeneous exact real-boundary node expansion is a real constant plus positive integer modes, placing it in every weight below $\pi$. Conversely a weighted solution is globally $H^1$ and has the same smooth regularity, as proved in [F1]. Thus evaluations of these full augmented kernels have the same image at the node. For a full augmented operator, imposing zero node value is onto exactly when it is onto and node evaluation of its kernel is onto: solve a forcing, then correct its node value by a kernel element; conversely subtract a zero-node solution from a cutoff node lift. This finite-dimensional criterion is valid even though the parameter contributes an infinite-dimensional kernel. Full augmented ranges are closed because their quotients by the full compact disk range are finite-dimensional; finite basis lifts and the actual range inverse give bounded right inverses when onto. It follows that the compact node-fixed universal derivative is onto if and only if its weighted $H^1_\delta\to L^2_\delta$ node-fixed realization is onto. The same comparison works at all weighted higher orders. No bounded point evaluation on ordinary $H^1$ has been assumed. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Suppose the weighted node-fixed universal derivative were not onto. Its range is closed, since its fixed-parameter operator is Fredholm by [F1], so a nonzero weighted $L^2$ section $\eta$ annihilates that range. On every compact interior patch away from the node, weights and bundle metrics are smooth bounded positive factors. The interior adjoint reduction and weak energy proof in [F3] therefore make its metric-weighted representative smooth and a solution of the actual smooth adjoint equation. Unique continuation there implies its zeros are discrete, since it is not identically zero. For a disk with dense $I(u)$, the free source open set from step 2.1 thus contains a point $z_0\in I(u)$ with $\eta(z_0)\ne0$. The equation gives both nonzero coordinate derivatives there. The injective immersion is a local embedding by choosing two independent target coordinates. A sufficiently small target neighborhood $W\Subset O$ of $u(z_0)$ has its whole preimage in that embedding patch: otherwise compactness of the closed source disk supplies a limiting second preimage, contrary to $u^{-1}(u(z_0))=\{z_0\}$. The point is off the boundary image because $W$ avoids $L$. [F1, F3, step 2.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

4.1 Choose a smooth anti-$J(a)$ field $Y$ supported in $W$ with $Y u_t(z_0)$ the positive dual direction of $\eta(z_0)$ in its local section metric. This is possible by the explicit invariant-plane prescription in step 1.1; project a local extension to anti-$J(a)$ fields and multiply by a nonnegative bump. Shrink the embedding patch first so the pointwise pairing is positive, and then $W$ so its full preimage stays there. The weighted pairing $\langle\eta,Y(u)u_t\rangle$ is now nonnegative everywhere and positive on a source neighborhood, with finite integral. Approximate $Y$ uniformly by finite parameter tangent combinations using step 1.1. Their pairing error tends to zero: all fields vanish on $A_x$, so their restrictions along $u$ vanish on the same node neighborhood, where the singular weights could matter. On the remaining compact source the weighted metric is bounded, and Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the error by a fixed multiple of $\|Y-d_aJ[b_n]\|_{C^0}\|\eta\|_{L^2_\delta}\|du\|_{L^2}$. Some actual parameter pairing is therefore nonzero, contradicting annihilation. The weighted universal derivative is onto, and step 2.2 proves actual compact universal submersion and all higher equivalents at every disk stipulated in the Statement. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 To apply Sard–Smale we use the actual open universal regular locus, not an unproved openness of the dense-injective-point condition. At any zero where the compact universal derivative is onto, its fixed node operator $P:E\to Y$ is Fredholm. Let $q:Y\to C=Y/\operatorname{Ran}P$. Choose finitely many parameter vectors lifting a basis under the onto $qK$ and denote the resulting right inverse by $j$. With the actual kernel-complement range inverse $Q$ of $P$, the map $f\mapsto(jqf,Q(f-Kjqf))$ is a specified bounded right inverse of the universal derivative. The actual theorem [F4] gives a Hilbert zero chart there. Surjectivity remains open by the Neumann series for nearby derivatives, so these charts form the open universal regular zero locus in the configuration-parameter product. It is second-countable by [F1]. Its parameter projection has kernel $\ker P$ and cokernel naturally the finite $C$, since its tangent equation is $P\xi+Kb=0$ and $qK$ is onto. Its actual Fredholm index is therefore $\operatorname{ind}P$. The index is locally constant: eliminate the invertible infinite range block of a nearby Fredholm operator by its Neumann inverse, leaving a finite block whose index is the fixed domain-minus-target dimension. Apply [F4] to each open fixed-index locus, for each integer index and each of the finitely many chosen node values. Their countable Baire intersection is dense residual in the one Hilbert parameter ball. At those parameters every zero belonging to the universal regular locus has onto fixed node operator. Step 4.1 places every stipulated dense-injective disk in that locus, proving the conclusion and its weighted equivalents from [F1]. Nothing in this argument proved the dense-injective-point hypothesis for arbitrary index-two or nonsimple disks. Boundary classification, attachment and counts remain separate. [F1, F4, step 2.2, step 4.1, construct, algebra] ∎
