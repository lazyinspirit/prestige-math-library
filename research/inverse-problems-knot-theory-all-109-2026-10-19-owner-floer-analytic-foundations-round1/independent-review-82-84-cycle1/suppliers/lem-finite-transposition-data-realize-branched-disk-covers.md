## Statement

Assume countable choice. Fix integers $m\ge2$, $b\ge1$, $p\ge0$, and positive integers $d_1,\ldots,d_b$ with $\sum_i d_i=m$. There is a compact connected smooth oriented surface $S$ with exactly $b$ boundary components and a positive branched covering
$$\pi:S\longrightarrow\overline{\mathbb D}$$
of degree $m$, unramified near its boundary, such that its boundary components have covering degrees $d_1,\ldots,d_b$. It has exactly
$$N=m+b-2+2p$$
distinct simple interior branch values, each with one ramified preimage of local form $w\mapsto w^2$. Its Euler characteristic is
$$\chi(S)=m-N=2-2p-b.$$
The construction specifies actual charts and a complex structure making the interior map holomorphic. It is a finite slit-gluing construction from a factorization into transpositions; no existence theorem for Riemann surfaces or generic-map selection is assumed.

For degree one a connected positive branched disk cover from a compact surface has no ramification and is a disk mapped diffeomorphically onto the target. Thus positive degree-one covers cannot realize the values $2-2p-b$ above with $b=1,p>0$. This result constructs a surface and covering; it does not identify an arbitrary supplied surface with that surface, realize a given Whitney class, or supply an incidence map into a symmetric product.

## Proof

**Given:** The finite integers and positive boundary degrees in the Statement. A positive branched cover means a map locally of the form $w\mapsto w^r$ in oriented interior charts, $r\ge1$, and an ordinary covering in boundary charts. Its degree is the number of preimages of a regular interior point. Euler characteristic of the finite cell surface below is the alternating number of its cells.



1.1 Partition the labels $1,\ldots,m$ into ordered blocks of sizes $d_i$, and let $\sigma$ be the permutation whose cycles are these blocks. A cycle $(a_1\ a_2\ \cdots\ a_d)$ is the product $(a_1\ a_d)\cdots(a_1\ a_2)$, with the rightmost permutation applied first. Thus $\sigma$ is a product of $m-b$ transpositions. For each block after the first append two identical transpositions interchanging the first label of that block with label $1$. These pairs have identity product. The group generated by the resulting transpositions is transitive: the original factors connect all labels within each block, and the appended factors join every block to the first. Finally append $p$ pairs $(1\ 2),(1\ 2)$. The product is still $\sigma$, its factors still act transitively, and their total number is $(m-b)+2(b-1)+2p=N$. Only finite explicitly labeled operations occur. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Choose $N$ distinct radial segments in the unit disk, each from a point $q_j$ of radius $1/2$ to its point on the unit circle, with distinct angles in their cyclic order. They are pairwise disjoint. Cut the disk open along all these segments, retaining two banks of each slit, its interior tip, and its boundary ends. The cut disk is a compact topological disk: opening one segment attached to the boundary merely replaces its boundary endpoint by two sides of a notch; straightening that notch gives a disk, and the disjoint segments can be opened successively. This also follows directly by drawing a smaller parallel two-bank boundary around each segment and shrinking its rounded end to the tip. Take $m$ labeled copies. At slit $j$ glue the positive bank in sheet $a$ to the negative bank in sheet $\tau_j(a)$, using the identical target coordinate, where $\tau_j$ is the corresponding transposition from step 1.1. Do this for every sheet and slit. The projection to the original disk is well defined on the quotient. Enumerate the slits in the order in which a positively traversed boundary collar encounters their banks, with the right-action convention corresponding to the product in step 1.1. Its fiber permutation is then that product; reversing the product convention would only replace it by its inverse, with the same cycle lengths. [step 1.1, construct]

3.1 The quotient is a surface, including at the tips. Away from a slit use the target coordinate in each sheet. At an interior point of a bank the identified two half-neighborhoods give an ordinary disk and the map is their common target coordinate. At a slit tip fixed by $\tau_j$, its two banks rejoin in the same sheet and give an ordinary disk. At the two labels interchanged by $\tau_j$, two slit disks join crosswise. In polar coordinates their combined angle runs from $0$ to $4\pi$; the coordinate $w=\sqrt{|z-q_j|}\,e^{i\arg(z-q_j)/2}$ is single valued on this glued neighborhood and the projection is exactly $z=q_j+w^2$. At a boundary end of a slit the crosswise bank gluing pairs half-disk pieces into ordinary boundary half-disks; projection is a local boundary diffeomorphism. This can also be checked by taking the target collar, cutting it along the segment, and following its two sides with the sheet labels. These charts orient the quotient consistently, since the same target orientation is used on every unramified part. Their interior transition functions are the identity target coordinate or one of the two local holomorphic square roots, so they give the asserted complex structure. Boundary charts use smooth coordinates flattening the target circle and give a smooth boundary with no corner left at a bank endpoint. [step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 The surface is compact and Hausdorff. Compactness follows from the finite compact cut disks. For Hausdorffness, two points with different target values have disjoint inverse images of small disjoint target neighborhoods. If their target values coincide, use a sufficiently small target neighborhood containing no other branch value or slit endpoint. The explicit gluings in step 3.1 separate its preimage into finitely many disjoint disk or boundary-half-disk neighborhoods, one about each preimage point: off a slit these are the original sheets; along a bank they are the paired half-sheets; at a branch tip they are its one two-sheet disk and the remaining one-sheet disks; at a boundary endpoint they are the paired boundary half-disks. These are open in the quotient because their preimages in the cut copies are open relative neighborhoods. Distinct points of the fiber therefore have disjoint neighborhoods as well. This proves Hausdorffness directly for this finite construction, without an arbitrary-net selection theorem. The unramified fiber over a point off the slits consists of its $m$ sheet labels. Loops about the branch values act by the transpositions $\tau_j$. Their transitivity in step 1.1 joins these labels by paths. Every point of the surface joins this fiber: project a path to the base point avoiding the finitely many branch values, lift it sheet by sheet across the slit banks, and use a local tip chart first if starting at a ramification point. Thus the surface is connected and the map has degree $m$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct]

4.2 The restriction to a sufficiently thin target boundary collar is unramified by step 3.1. Following a full circle carries its labels by $\sigma$. Therefore each cycle of length $d_i$ gives one boundary component mapping with degree $d_i$, and there are no others. This is an actual circle covering: start with its $d_i$ copies of a cut boundary interval and join each interval's terminal endpoint to the next one's initial endpoint in the cycle. The resulting circle maps with that degree, and its inward collars are exactly the same glued interval products. In particular there are precisely $b$ boundary components. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Compute Euler characteristic by finite cells, without assuming a surface-classification theorem. Choose a finite cyclic list of angles including the branch-ray angles, adding auxiliary angles so every successive gap is less than $\pi$ and the list has $l\ge4$ elements. Put a vertex at the center and, on each listed ray, vertices at radii $1/2$ and $1$. The edges are the inner polygon sides, the outer circular arcs and the two radial intervals on every ray. They bound $l$ inner triangles and $l$ outer curvilinear quadrilaterals, each parametrized by a disk. In particular every branch value is a vertex and every original slit is an edge. There are $V=1+2l$, $E=4l$ and $F=2l$, so $V-E+F=1$. Above each open edge or face are exactly $m$ copies, since open faces miss the slits, while an open slit edge has its $m$ bank pairings and an ordinary edge has its $m$ sheet copies. Above a nonbranch vertex are $m$ vertices, and above $q_j$ there are $m-1$: two labels meet in its one ramified point and the other $m-2$ labels remain distinct. These lifted cells and the charts of step 3.1 give a finite cell structure on $S$. Hence $$\chi(S)=(mV-N)-mE+mF=m-N=2-2p-b.$$ There is exactly one local degree-two point over each $q_j$ and no other branching by those same charts. [step 3.1, step 4.1, step 4.2, construct, algebra]

6.1 Finally let a positive connected cover have degree one. Any local exponent $r\ge2$ would give at least $r$ distinct preimages of a sufficiently close regular value by the local power formula, contradicting degree one. Thus it is unramified, including at every interior point. Every fiber then has one point: path lifting in finitely many local covering charts along a compact target path makes the fiber cardinality locally constant, and the connected disk has the same cardinality as a regular fiber. Alternatively this is part of the covering property after branching is excluded. The map is consequently a bijective local diffeomorphism of compact surfaces with boundary, and its local smooth inverses agree to give a global diffeomorphism. A disk with the identity map realizes that case. Its one boundary component and Euler characteristic one exclude $b=1,p>0$. All constructions and sheet choices above were finite, so the stated countable-choice assumption is sufficient and no stronger choice or unproved arbitrary-surface representativity has entered. [given, step 3.1, construct, algebra] ∎
