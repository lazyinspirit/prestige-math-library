## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $X$ be the compact target in the actual boundary-preserving local-addition theorem, let $L$ be one of its oriented product tori, and let $x$ be a generator. Let $J$ be a smooth autonomous almost complex structure for which $L$ is totally real. Near $x$ require one fixed chart taking $J$ to $i$, $L$ to $\mathbb R^m$ and $x$ to zero. Fix a boundary point $p$ of the disk.

There is an actual second-countable Hilbert manifold of $H^3$ disk maps with boundary in $L$ and value $x$ at $p$. Near a smooth reference map its model is the space of $H^3$ sections with the zeroth $TL$ boundary trace and $\xi(p)=0$, intersected with the actual $H^1$ completed boundary domain, not an asserted higher-norm smooth closure. The Cauchy–Riemann expression is an actual smooth $H^2$ section; its zeros are smooth maps. If the boundary determinant winding of the full disk problem is $k$, its actual full linearization has real index $m+2k$, and its node-fixed realization has index $2k$. Write $\mu=2k$ for this actual node-fixed operator index.

At a smooth zero, compact node-fixed surjectivity is equivalent to surjectivity of the actual weighted node-fixed punctured-disk realization, for every integer order and each weight $0<\delta<\pi$. No surjectivity is assumed without this explicit operator condition. At a nonconstant zero satisfying it, the actual node-fixed zero space is locally a smooth $\mu$-dimensional manifold. The affine holomorphic automorphisms of the half-plane fixing its boundary point at infinity act freely and properly, and smoothly on this regular zero manifold. Its quotient has local smooth slices of dimension $\mu-2$. In particular when $\mu=2$ every regular such disk is locally isolated modulo these automorphisms. No global count or compactness is concluded.

Finally if $u=v\circ\psi$ for a smooth degree-$d$ disk map $\psi$ taking boundary to boundary and the marked point to the marked point, then $\mu(u)=d\mu(v)$ for the corresponding actual Cauchy–Riemann-symbol indices. In particular a disk of actual node-fixed index two cannot be a nontrivial proper holomorphic disk cover of degree $d>1$: the oriented torus makes $\mu(v)$ even. This does not assert that every nonsimple disk admits such a covering factorization, or prove generic regularity, boundary-degeneration classification, gluing or cancellation.

## Proof

1.1 Construct compact-disk map charts using the actual local addition in [F1]. In finitely many disk and straightened-boundary source patches the elementary $H^3$ extensions and product estimates of [F1] apply. A conformal local source chart flattens the disk boundary, and the actual complex frame and real boundary gauge of [F2] identify the reference section's boundary planes with a fixed real plane locally. The finite-jet reflection extension for arbitrary vector components matches derivatives through order two and therefore gives $H^3$ bounds without extra normal traces. Its plane embeddings give $H^3\subset C^1$ and $H^2$ multiplication; third derivatives of products have one $L^2$ factor and bounded remaining first derivatives. The actual weak chain and Taylor arguments of [F1] give smooth compact-disk compositions, including changes of reference. A continuous $H^3$ section belongs to the $H^1$ completed boundary domain exactly when its pointwise boundary lies in the reference $TL$ subbundle: one implication is the weak trace theorem; for the other cut into local edge pieces, rotate to the real plane, reflect the real and imaginary parts in the $H^1$ boundary-compatible way, convolve, and sum a finite partition, exactly as in [F1]. This proves only the required $H^1$ approximation. Impose $\xi(p)=0$, a closed linear condition since $H^3\subset C^1$. The local addition and its inverse identify it exactly with maps taking $p$ to $x$, and preserve boundary membership in both directions. The charts are smooth by the same composition bounds. The model is separable Hilbert: its weak derivative graph is a closed subspace of finitely many separable $L^2$ patch spaces, with the closed boundary and node conditions; the CC orthogonal projection of a countable ambient dense set supplies separability. For second-countability embed $X$ by a finite atlas partition into Euclidean space and send a map to its $H^3$ coordinate map. The same inverse-composition bounds make this an embedding with each chart image relatively open, as in [F1]. The ambient compact-source Sobolev space is second-countable. This constructs the claimed actual manifold, without a higher-boundary smooth-density assertion. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 In a local-addition chart the actual section is $\mathcal T_\xi(\partial_s\mathcal E(u_0,\xi)+J(\mathcal E(u_0,\xi))\partial_t\mathcal E(u_0,\xi))$, with the target interpreted intrinsically as vector-valued $(0,1)$-forms. The finite compact source atlas and $H^2$ algebra in step 1.1 give a genuine smooth $H^3\to H^2$ expression and smooth target trivializations. At a zero its derivative is the actual Cauchy–Riemann-symbol geometric linearization; derivatives of the target trivialization multiply the zero section and vanish. Every zero is smooth by the local weak-to-smooth result in [F1], including at the node. For the full boundary domain this operator $T$ is Fredholm of index $m+2k$ by [F2]. Node evaluation $e$ is onto its real $m$-dimensional tangent plane: a cutoff section in the exact node chart equals any specified real constant near $p$ and vanishes away from a small half-disk. To compute the restriction to $\ker e$, write $K=\ker T$, let $r=\dim e(K)$, and use the bounded full range inverse $Q$ in [F2]. Its restricted kernel has dimension $\dim K-r$. On $\operatorname{Ran}T$ the bounded map $f\mapsto e(Qf)$ modulo $e(K)$ is onto $\mathbb R^m/e(K)$, because $e$ on the full domain is onto. Its kernel is exactly $T(\ker e)$. Thus this restricted range is closed of codimension $m-r$ inside $\operatorname{Ran}T$, and the restricted cokernel dimension is $\dim\operatorname{coker}T+m-r$. The restricted index is $m+2k-m=2k$. This proves Fredholmness and the index without assuming evaluation is onto the full kernel. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 We prove the regularity equivalence, which is needed before using weighted node models. For any of these full-domain realizations, the node-fixed operator is onto if and only if the full operator is onto and node evaluation on its kernel is onto. If the fixed operator is onto, the full one is onto; subtract a fixed-node solution of $T\ell(c)$ from the cutoff lift $\ell(c)$ to obtain a kernel section evaluating to any prescribed $c$. Conversely solve a forcing with the full right inverse and correct its node value with such a kernel section. The full compact disk operator is onto at level $H^3\to H^2$ exactly when it is onto at $H^1\to L^2$, by the actual cokernel/range agreement in [F2]. The full weighted operator has this same finite cokernel and the same smooth kernel by [F2], at each integer order; its evaluation on this kernel is the same actual node value. The just-proved criterion therefore identifies compact node-fixed surjectivity with weighted node-fixed surjectivity, and with compact node-fixed surjectivity at every higher integer order where point evaluation is defined. Bounded right inverses follow from their actual closed-range Hilbert kernel complements and estimates in [F2]. This argument compares the realizations via their common full cokernel and kernel; it does not identify a higher weighted forcing space with the ordinary compact higher forcing space. [F2, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 At a zero satisfying this actual surjectivity condition, [F3] gives a genuine smooth finite-dimensional zero chart of dimension $\mu$ in the node-fixed $H^3$ topology. It has the same smooth families at all higher orders. Indeed finite-jet extensions on the disk exist at every integer order by solving the finite Vandermonde matching equations. The $H^2$ algebra and the usual higher Leibniz splitting, putting the highest differentiated factor in $L^2$ and the lower factors in $L^\infty$ or the two middle factors in $L^4$, give smooth higher section formulas. Their actual linearizations are onto by step 3.1 and have the same finite smooth kernel. Apply the actual split zero theorem at orders three and any higher order. Inclusion of the higher zero chart into the $H^3$ one has derivative the identity on that kernel, so the finite-dimensional inverse theorem makes it a local smooth diffeomorphism. Thus a local smooth family of regular solutions is smooth in every fixed higher Sobolev topology. Precomposition by a smooth finite-dimensional family of disk diffeomorphisms is then smooth into $H^3$: each derivative in the diffeomorphism parameter differentiates the map at most once, and any fixed number of these derivatives is controlled by a sufficiently high Sobolev topology and the weak chain rule. Difference quotients converge by integrating the corresponding derivatives on the compact source. This proves smoothness on the actual regular zero manifold, without differentiating the precomposition action on all rough $H^3$ maps. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Identify the disk punctured at $p$ with the half-plane, with $p=\infty$. Its holomorphic automorphisms fixing that point are $g_{a,b}(z)=az+b$, $a>0$, $b\in\mathbb R$; in the node coordinate $w=-1/z$ this is $w\mapsto w/(a-bw)$, so every such map is smooth at the fixed node. The action preserves the autonomous equation and boundary value. Its two infinitesimal directions are the actual independent kernel sections in [F3], both vanishing at the node. It is free on nonconstant disks: if $u\circ g_{a,b}=u$ for a nonidentity element, iterating it in one of the two integer directions sends every interior point to infinity. For $a=1$ use the nonzero translation; for $a\ne1$ choose the direction in which $a^n$ grows. Continuity at the fixed node then makes $u$ constant, a contradiction. The action is proper in the actual Sobolev topology. If $u_n\to u$ and $u_n\circ g_{a_n,b_n}\to v$ strongly in $H^3$ with both limits nonconstant, the compact-source supremum estimate makes these convergences uniform. If the affine parameters have no compact subsequence in $(0,\infty)\times\mathbb R$, pass to a subsequence with $a_n\to\infty$, or $a_n$ bounded above and away from zero with $|b_n|\to\infty$, or $a_n\to0$. The first two cases send every interior point to infinity. In the last case extract $b_n\to b$ finite or $|b_n|\to\infty$; the maps send every interior point to $b$ or infinity. Thus $u\circ g_{a_n,b_n}$ has a constant interior pointwise limit in every case, forcing $v$ constant, a contradiction. Hence the parameters stay in a compact set along a subsequence. Step 4.1 gives the smooth action on the regular zero manifold. Choose a linear complement to its two independent orbit directions in an actual finite-dimensional zero chart. The map from a small affine-group neighborhood times this complement has invertible derivative onto that chart, so the finite-dimensional inverse theorem gives an actual local slice. Freeness and the just-proved properness exclude additional nonlocal returns after shrinking it. The quotient is Hausdorff and has these local smooth slices of dimension $\mu-2$. When $\mu=2$, the slice is a point, proving local rigidity only; a global count still needs actual compactness and exclusion of further strata. [F1, F2, F3, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Finally take the stated degree-$d$ map $\psi$ and $u=v\circ\psi$. A global complex disk frame for $v^*TX$ from [F2] pulls back to a global frame for $u^*TX$, because $J$ is autonomous. An oriented real boundary frame for $v^*TL$ has complex determinant loop of winding $k(v)$ in that frame. Along $u$ its loop is precomposed with the degree-$d$ boundary map. Lifting arguments on finitely many circle arcs shows that winding multiplies by that integer degree: its total argument increment is traversed $d$ times, or equivalently subtract its winding monomial to obtain a nonzero loop with a periodic argument, whose pullback adds zero net increment. Thus $k(u)=d k(v)$, and step 2.1 gives $\mu(u)=2k(u)=d\mu(v)$. This index calculation needs neither holomorphicity of the representative maps nor their regularity; it uses their actual complex bundles and oriented boundary frames. Since $\mu(v)$ is even, the integer equation $2=d\mu(v)$ is impossible for $d>1$. It excludes a nontrivial proper holomorphic cover of an index-two disk when such a factorization is supplied. No step proved that every nonsimple disk has that form, or that an index-two disk is regular. Those and the actual boundary-degeneration count remain separate obligations. All charts, inverse constructions and finite-dimensional slices used Countable Choice only through the stated local suppliers. [F2, step 2.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
