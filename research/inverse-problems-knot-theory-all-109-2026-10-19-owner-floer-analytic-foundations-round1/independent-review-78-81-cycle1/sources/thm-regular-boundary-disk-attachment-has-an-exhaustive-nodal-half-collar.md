## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Use the actual regular boundary-disk attachment data and the family $g_\epsilon$ with its fixed phases in the matching theorem. Consider the specified nodal configuration consisting of the constant strip $x$ and this one nonconstant disk $v$ at its lower boundary, with node value $x$ and no other bubble or strip component.

After applying the longitudinal normalization used in the convergence, every bounded-energy sequence of actual $J_t$ strips $u_n:x\to x$ converging to this configuration in the actual smooth component-core, complete energy and collapsed neck/tail sense of bubbled-strip compactness is eventually
$$u_n(s,t)=g_{\epsilon_n}(s-b_n,t),\qquad \epsilon_n\downarrow0$$
after a subsequence, with $b_n$ tending to its attaching position in the chosen longitudinal normalization. If only convergence, rather than monotone scales, is desired, the conclusion is $\epsilon_n\to0$ for the whole sufficiently late sequence with its chosen component normalization.

After decreasing $\epsilon_0>0$, two members with $0<\epsilon,\epsilon'<\epsilon_0$ are longitudinal translates only if $\epsilon=\epsilon'$ and that translate is the same center. The actual translation quotient therefore has a local nodal half-collar parametrized by $0\le\epsilon<\epsilon_0$, with zero the specified nodal disk orbit. Here the local topology is generated by the countable actual component-core, energy and neck/tail image tests described in the proof, and the ordinary actual solution topology at positive parameters. Thus this is a collar of actual solutions, with exactly one interval germ at this regular disk stratum.

The theorem presupposes an actual regular node-fixed index-two disk, not that all degenerations have that form. It supplies no low-index classification of other sphere/disk strata, no count for internal-pair components, and no target connected-sum or polygon gluing theorem.

## Proof

1.1 Translate the attaching position to zero. The actual rescalings in [F3] give $b_n\to0$, $\epsilon_n\to0$ and maps $V_n(\zeta)=u_n(b_n+\epsilon_n\zeta)$ converging smoothly to a representative of $v$ on every compact subset of the closed upper half-plane. There are no child bubbles by the specified limit. One fixed marked affine change makes that limiting representative precisely the chosen $v$. Adjust each rescaling by a further positive affine $a_n\zeta+d_n$ near the identity so that the two fixed disk-core phases vanish. This is a genuine finite implicit equation: its derivative at $v$ is the invertible phase Gram matrix on the two independent affine directions. Smooth convergence on a compact neighborhood of the phase supports makes these finite derivatives converge uniformly. The finite inverse theorem [F4] therefore gives unique $a_n\to1,d_n\to0$ with zero phases. Equivalently change the centers to $b_n+\epsilon_nd_n$ and scales to $\epsilon_na_n$. These still tend to zero. The phase equation can be differentiated by changing integration variables in its compactly supported integrals, as in [F4], so no rough-map composition derivative is assumed. Keep these normalized names $b_n,\epsilon_n$. [F3, F4, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Fix the cap cut far enough that its background disk tail lies in the smaller exact generator chart. Smooth bubble-core convergence makes the normalized disk cap and a fixed collar of its cut $H^3$-close to $v$, with phases zero. The physical strip outside a fixed small half-disk about $b_n$ converges smoothly locally to $x$. Its ENTIRE outer cap image, including the two infinite tails, is eventually in the generator chart: choose first far tail cutoffs where [F3]'s image collapse is small, and then use constant-base core convergence on the remaining compact part. On that outer cap the equation is exactly $D_0$ and the boundary planes are the exact transverse planes. Its incoming trace on the fixed cut tends to zero in $G_-^{5/2}$ by smooth convergence on a collar there and the real holomorphic Fourier trace estimate. The actual cap map lies in the weak $H^1$ cap domain: finite energy controls derivatives and slice Poincare on its far transverse ends controls the function norm, while the intervening compact part is bounded. Thus [F2]'s constant-cap uniqueness identifies it with its bounded incoming-data solution and upgrades this convergence to the required GLOBAL cap $H^3$ smallness. This step derives the outer Sobolev neighborhood, rather than presuming compact convergence controls infinite ends. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 The entire connecting annulus lies in the exact generator chart for this same sufficiently far fixed cut. Indeed choose the outer half-disk radius small and the bubble-core radius large so that the two limiting node neighborhoods are inside a smaller chart; [F3]'s intervening image collapse and matched node value $x$ put the remaining annulus there as well. The small affine adjustments in step1.1 preserve buffered versions of these neighborhoods. In the exact source plumbing it has length $T_n=\pi^{-1}\log(r^2/\epsilon_n)\to\infty$. Both cap maps now meet the actual hypotheses of [F1]–[F2], and the equation on that full annulus is the homogeneous linear real-boundary equation. Its means and all Fourier modes therefore satisfy the exact matching system in [F1]. That theorem's actual uniqueness gives $u_n(s+b_n,t)=g_{\epsilon_n}(s,t)$ for all sufficiently large $n$. This proves sequential exhaustion using actual core convergence and image collapse. A subsequence can make the positive scales strictly decrease to zero if desired. The same argument applies whenever the full normalized sequence has these convergence properties. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 We prove an estimate needed to rule out nonlocal parameter returns. In the normalized source $\zeta=z/\epsilon$, the family satisfies uniform node-image convergence outside its disk core: $$\lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{\epsilon\to0}\ \sup_{\substack{\zeta\text{ in the scaled strip}\\|\zeta|\ge R}}d(g_\epsilon(\epsilon\zeta),x)=0.$$ This follows directly from [F1]'s actual neck construction. Its background disk positive modes decay as $C/|\zeta|$ on the disk-end annulus. The additional disk response, outer cap response and common constant are $O(e^{-\pi T})$. The positive modes from the outer cap only decrease into the neck. On the outer cap the entire map is $O(e^{-\pi T})$ by its bounded linear solution map. Consequently for $R$ beyond the fixed cut the displayed supremum is bounded by $C/R+Ce^{-\pi T}$, as long as the annulus reaches that radius, and the outer-cap bound covers larger radii. These estimates concern actual coordinate maps, and target coordinate and metric distances are uniformly comparable there. [F1, F2, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Suppose tail injectivity failed. Choosing counterexamples with both scales below $1/n$ gives two equal translated strips whose normalized material maps obey $G_n=G'_n\circ h_n$, where $h_n$ is a positive affine map and both $G_n,G'_n$ converge smoothly on compact closed half-planes to $v$. If $h_n$ has no compact subsequence in the positive affine group, pass to a subsequence of its two real parameters. A diverging dilation or translation sends every fixed interior point to infinity; step4.1 then makes the right side tend to the constant $x$. A dilation tending to zero with bounded translation sends every fixed interior point to one real boundary point; compact closed-source convergence makes the right side tend to its single value under $v$. A translation tending to infinity in that case is covered by step4.1. All possibilities contradict the nonconstant limit $v$ on the left. Thus the affine parameters are precompact. Every limiting affine map $h$ satisfies $v=v\circ h$ and is the identity by [F4]. Hence $h_n\to\operatorname{id}$. Both $G'_n$ and $G'_n\circ h_n$ have zero fixed phases. The finite phase inverse in step1.1, now uniform for $G'_n\to v$, has exactly one zero near the identity; since the identity is already such a zero, $h_n$ is the identity for large $n$. The two centers and scales are therefore exactly equal, contradicting their selection. This proves injectivity for some sufficiently small common scale tail, including uniqueness of center. [F1, F4, step 1.1, step 4.1, construct, contradiction]

6.1 Specify the local convergence topology without assuming a compactification axiom. Work modulo longitudinal translations. Choose increasing finite disk-core radii $R_j$ so the background disk tail beyond $R_j$ is within $1/(4j)$ of $x$, decreasing positive outer half-disk radii $r_j\to0$, and an increasing strip exhaustion. For each integer $N$ use the finite collection of tests $j\le N$: existence of a center and scale $0<\epsilon<1/N$ such that the rescaled map is $C^j$-within $1/j$ of $v$ on its radius-$R_j$ closed core, the original strip on the chosen compact core outside radius $r_j$ is $C^j$-within $1/j$ of $x$, the intervening annulus and outer tails have image distance less than $1/j$ from $x$, and the energy differs from $E_g(v)$ by less than $1/j$. Take buffered radii when writing annulus tests, and include the previous tests to make these sets nested. Distances and $C^j$ differences are defined by the fixed finite target charts on the cores. These give a countable local basis at the specified nodal orbit; at positive solutions use the actual solution topology. A sequence passing all these tests is exactly the local component/energy/collapsed-neck convergence used in steps1.1–3.1; the radii and tolerances may be refined without changing that local convergence. The actual family passes every fixed finite test as $\epsilon\to0$ by [F1], step4.1 and its energy limit. If no tested neighborhood were contained in this family's small-scale image, Countable Choice could select one outsider in the $N$th tested neighborhood for every $N$, contradicting sequential exhaustion. Thus its image together with the nodal orbit contains a genuine tested neighborhood. [F1, F3, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, contradiction]

7.1 At positive scales the parameter map is a local diffeomorphism onto the actual translation quotient by [F1], and is injective by step5.1. It is therefore a homeomorphism onto its positive image. It is continuous at zero by the tests in step6.1. Its inverse is also continuous there. If a sequence of its scales stayed bounded below by a positive number, the positive-parameter family on that compact scale interval would have uniformly bounded physical derivatives, including after any longitudinal translation. It could not have a nonconstant disk limit under a source rescaling tending to zero, since the rescaled first derivative would tend uniformly to zero on every fixed core. This contradicts the bubble-core test for $v$. The family is smooth on a slightly larger positive interval than the chosen tail, so the same argument includes sequences approaching its upper cutoff. Hence any tested nodal convergence forces scale tending to zero. Countable basing turns these sequential assertions into continuity; failure of continuity would select a contradicting point from each nested basis neighborhood. After restricting the tail once more, step6.1 and this inverse continuity make its image an actual neighborhood of the nodal orbit. This gives the claimed half-collar, with a single interval germ and no unproved classification of other strata. [F1, F4, step 3.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra] ∎
