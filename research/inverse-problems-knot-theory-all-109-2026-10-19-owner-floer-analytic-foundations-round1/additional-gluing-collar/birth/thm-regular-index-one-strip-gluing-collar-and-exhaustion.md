## Statement

Assume Countable Choice and the compact transverse strip geometry with fixed exact all-$t$ product-integrable generator charts used for actual strip pregluing. Let $u^-:x_0\to y$ and $u^+:y\to x_2$ be nonconstant smooth finite-energy $J_t$ strips whose actual geometric $H^1\to L^2$ linearizations are surjective and have real index one, before translation quotient. Use the explicit pregluing $u_R$, local addition, and corrected solutions $g_R$ constructed with a smooth uniformly bounded right inverse. Write $\tau_a u(s,t)=u(s+a,t)$.

The right inverses in that construction can be chosen so that, for all sufficiently large $R_0$, the map
$$\mathbb R\times(R_0,\infty)\longrightarrow\{\text{actual regular strips }x_0\to x_2\},\qquad (a,R)\longmapsto\tau_a g_R$$
is smooth, injective, and a local diffeomorphism onto the actual two-dimensional zero manifold. Thus $R\mapsto[g_R]$ is an injective smooth local coordinate in the translation quotient. The endpoint $R=\infty$ is the broken pair $([u^-],[u^+])$.

It has the following precise exhaustion property. Suppose actual strips $w_n$ admit shifts $c_n^-<c_n^+$ with $c_n^+-c_n^-\to\infty$ and
$$w_n(s+c_n^-,t)\longrightarrow u^-(s,t),\qquad w_n(s+c_n^+,t)\longrightarrow u^+(s,t)$$
smoothly on compact sets. Assume their breaking has exactly these two nonconstant levels and no bubbles or additional energy loss, in the energy-identity and neck-collapse sense of actual bubbled strip compactness. Equivalently for the neck requirement used here, outside large fixed translated component cores, the left tail, middle neck, and right tail lie uniformly close to $x_0,y,x_2$, respectively, with the closeness tending to zero as the core radius tends to infinity. Then, for all sufficiently large $n$, there are unique $a_n\in\mathbb R$ and $R_n>R_0$ with $w_n=\tau_{a_n}g_{R_n}$, and
$$R_n-\tfrac12(c_n^+-c_n^-)\longrightarrow0,\qquad a_n+\tfrac12(c_n^-+c_n^+)\longrightarrow0.$$
Conversely $[g_R]$ converges to this broken pair as $R\to\infty$, with no bubbles or energy loss. Consequently this family, together with its broken endpoint, is a topological one-sided collar for this fixed two-level limit, with a smooth interior. No differentiability at a chosen compactifying parameter is asserted. The statement makes no claim that index hypotheses exclude other bubble or boundary-degeneration strata.

## Proof

1.1 Put $\epsilon_R=e^{-\pi R/2}$. We first choose the right inverses with uniform bounds on their first two parameter derivatives in the moving bundle coordinates of [F1]. In its construction, the coefficients of $D_R$, the local-addition identifications and their first two $R$ derivatives have uniform bounds: they differentiate translates of fixed smooth component maps, with uniformly bounded derivatives and integrable tails, and a fixed joining cutoff. Fix a parameter radius $a>0$ for the local Neumann formula, uniformly small so its denominator has inverse norm at most two. At arithmetic grid points $R_j$ use the already constructed right inverses of uniform norm. On $|R-R_j|<a$ the formula is $Q_j(R)=Q_j(R_j)[I+(D_R-D_{R_j})Q_j(R_j)]^{-1}$ in the fixed local spaces. Differentiating the Neumann inverse twice gives uniform bounds in terms of the first two coefficient derivatives and the fixed inverse bound. Transfer back using the same bounded smooth bundle identifications. Average these exact right inverses with translates of one fixed parameter bump divided by their locally finite sum. The overlap number and derivatives of this partition are uniformly bounded. Thus $Q_R,Q_R',Q_R''$ are bounded independently of $R$. Replacing the original smooth choice by this specified choice preserves all conclusions of [F1] and [F2]. [F1, F2, F4, construct, algebra]

1.2 The corrections also have the differentiated estimates $\|\xi_R'\|_{H^3}+\|\xi_R''\|_{H^3}\le C\epsilon_R$ in these local coordinates. Indeed write $e_R=\mathcal F_R(0)$, $N_R(\xi)=\mathcal F_R(\xi)-e_R-D_R\xi$, and $\xi_R=Q_R y_R$. The fixed-point equation of [F4] is $y_R=-e_R-N_R(Q_Ry_R)$. The exact error in [F1] and its first two $R$ derivatives are $O(\epsilon_R)$ in $H^2$. The explicit local section formulas give, uniformly on a fixed small ball, $\|N_R(\xi)\|+\|\partial_R N_R(\xi)\|+\|\partial_R^2N_R(\xi)\|\le C\|\xi\|^2$, $\|d_\xi N_R(\xi)\|+\|\partial_Rd_\xi N_R(\xi)\|\le C\|\xi\|$, and bounded second $\xi$ derivative. To justify the parameter estimates, subtract the value and the linear term at zero before differentiating the coefficient formulas; each remaining Taylor term has at least two variation factors. Their $H^2$ products are bounded by the actual Sobolev algebra in [F3]. Now $\|y_R\|_{H^2}=O(\epsilon_R)$ follows from its equation and [F1]. Differentiating once gives $(I+d_\xi N_R Q_R)y_R'=-e_R'-\partial_RN_R-d_\xi N_R Q_R'y_R$. Its inverse has norm at most two for large $R$, and the right side is $O(\epsilon_R)$. Differentiate again; its highest unknown is the same invertible factor times $y_R''$, all other terms being $e_R''$, quadratic terms in $y_R,y_R'$ and bounded $Q_R',Q_R''$. They are $O(\epsilon_R)$. The product rule for $Q_Ry_R$ proves the claim. All differentiations are legitimate by the actual smooth parameter conclusion of [F4]. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.1 We record carefully the smoothness of translation on the regular zero manifold. For each integer $k\ge3$, the construction of [F3] extends to $H^k\cap\mathcal X^1\to H^{k-1}$. The normal extension is the same finite reflected linear combination with coefficients satisfying $\sum_j a_j(-j)^\ell=1$ for $0\le\ell<k$, a nonsingular Vandermonde system. It matches precisely the weak derivatives through order $k-1$ and adds no boundary condition. The $H^2$ algebra and $H^k\subset C^{k-2}$ give the higher product bounds: in a derivative of a product of total order $k\ge3$, at most one factor has order greater than $k-2$, so that factor is estimated in $L^2$ and the others in $L^\infty$. The low order $H^2$ case is already [F3]. Taylor's integral formula gives smooth composition and the actual higher chart and section formulas, with the same zeroth boundary trace argument. At any fixed smooth regular zero the higher operator is surjective by [F3], has the same finite-dimensional kernel, and has a bounded right inverse obtained by restricting its $H^1$ inverse and using the higher regularity estimate. Apply [F4] at orders $3$ and $k$. Inclusion of the higher zero chart into the $H^3$ zero chart has derivative the identity on this common kernel. The finite-dimensional inverse theorem, or the contraction proof of [F4] on that finite-dimensional kernel, makes this inclusion a local smooth diffeomorphism. Thus every local smooth family in the $H^3$ regular zero manifold is locally smooth in every $H^k$ topology. Translation of such a family is then smooth into $H^3$: its $a$ derivatives are longitudinal derivatives, and any prescribed finite order is controlled by taking a sufficiently high $k$. The translation difference quotients converge by the fundamental theorem and smooth approximation, as in [F1]. This is a statement on the actual regular zero manifold; it does not assert norm differentiability of translation on all $H^3$ maps. [F3, F4, construct, algebra]

2.2 The family $g_R$ has a uniform bound for its longitudinal derivative in geometric $H^3$. Its uniform $H^3$ correction and [F3] give a global uniform $C^1$ bound. The local bootstrap in [F3], using finitely many target charts and fixed-radius source patches, then gives uniform bounds of every fixed derivative order. On the two fixed-width translated component cores these imply uniform Sobolev bounds. Outside those cores choose the cores large enough that $u_R$ lies in the corresponding exact generator charts; the small $C^1$ correction puts $g_R$ there as well. There its coordinate map $v$ satisfies $D_0v=0$. Cut $v$ off to zero in fixed-width collars inside those cores, separately for the two tails and middle neck. Each resulting section belongs to the whole-strip $H^1$ domain: this follows from exponential end decay on infinite tails and finiteness of each middle interval. Its $D_0$ image is supported in the finitely many collars, and has uniformly bounded $H^3$ norm by the local bootstrap. The actual inverse of $D_0$, together with the higher estimate of [F3], bounds its $H^4$ norm uniformly, independently of neck length. The omitted collars are already uniformly bounded. Thus $\|(g_R)_s\|_{H^3}\le C$. Also $\|\partial_Rg_R\|_{H^3}\le C$ by step 1.2 and the fixed-component pregluing formulas. It follows by the Banach-valued fundamental theorem that, in the uniformly valid local-addition identifications, $\|\tau_a g_{R+\rho}-g_R\|_{H^3}\le C(|a|+|\rho|)$ for uniformly small $a,\rho$. [F1, F3, step 1.2, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 The actual component kernel is spanned by $u_s^\pm$. Translation invariance of the equation gives these kernel sections, their nonzero norm follows from nonconstancy, and their exponential tails put them in every indicated Sobolev realization. Surjectivity and index one make each kernel one-dimensional. Choose smooth compactly supported real test covectors $\psi^\pm$ on the component cores with $\ell^\pm(u_s^\pm)=1$, where $\ell^\pm(k)=\int\psi^\pm(k)$. Explicitly use a nonnegative compact cutoff times the metric dual of $u_s^\pm$, divided by its positive squared integral. Translate these tests to the two cores of $g_R$, using the fixed local-addition identifications, and call their pair $\ell_R$. Its operator norm on geometric $H^3$ is uniform. By [F2] it maps the actual two-dimensional kernel at $g_R$ isomorphically to $\mathbb R^2$, with a uniform inverse bound: on the glued copies of $(u_s^-,0),(0,u_s^+)$ its matrix is $I+O(e^{-cR})$. All harmless tangent identifications between the preglued and corrected maps differ by $O(\epsilon_R)$ on these compact tests. [F1, F2, construct, algebra]

3.2 The actual zero set has uniform phase charts near $g_R$. Its derivative has a uniformly bounded right inverse by [F1], and its nonlinear derivative is uniformly Lipschitz. In centered local-addition coordinates let $P_R=I-\widetilde Q_R\widetilde D_R$. Its norm is bounded uniformly, since those of $\widetilde Q_R,\widetilde D_R$ are. The proof of [F4] gives a zero chart $k\mapsto k+h_R(k)$ over a fixed-radius ball of the kernel, with $h_R(0)=dh_R(0)=0$ and uniform first two derivative bounds after making that radius uniformly smaller. Indeed its defining contraction has ratio at most $1/2$, and its first two derivative formulas involve only the uniform right-inverse and coefficient bounds. The phase map $k\mapsto\ell_R(k+h_R(k))$ has uniformly invertible derivative at zero by step 3.1 and uniformly bounded second derivative. The explicit finite-dimensional Newton proof, shrinking the radius until the derivative differs by at most half the lower bound, gives an injective phase chart whose image contains a fixed Euclidean ball. Every zero sufficiently close in $H^3$ belongs to this chart, because its $P_R$ and $I-P_R$ components lie in the fixed product neighborhood of the contraction. Both this closeness radius and the phase ball radius can be chosen independent of $R$. [F1, F3, F4, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 For $H_R(a,\rho)=\tau_a g_{R+\rho}$, evaluate the difference from $g_R$ with these same two phase tests, using its centered local-addition inverse. At $(a,\rho)=(0,0)$ the phase derivative matrix is $$\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}+O(\epsilon_R).$$ On the left core the pregluing is $u^-(s+R,t)$, so both a longitudinal translation and increasing $R$ differentiate to $u_s^-$; on the right core increasing $R$ differentiates to $-u_s^+$ while translation differentiates to $u_s^+$. Step 1.2 bounds the correction's $R$ derivative in $H^3$, and its longitudinal derivative in $H^2$ is $O(\epsilon_R)$; hence the phase errors have the stated bound. The second derivatives of these scalar phase expressions are uniformly bounded for uniformly small $a,\rho$: the terms use two longitudinal derivatives of $g_R$, one longitudinal derivative of $\partial_Rg_R$, or $\partial_R^2g_R$, all bounded in $L^2$ on the fixed tests by [F1] and step 1.2. The local-addition inverse coefficients and derivatives are uniformly bounded there. By step 2.1 the expressions are smooth. Thus the finite-dimensional contraction argument gives a fixed parameter neighborhood on which this phase map is injective and covers a fixed ball. Step 2.2 keeps the corresponding maps within the uniform zero chart from step 3.2, after shrinking this neighborhood once. Comparing their phases in that zero chart shows that every actual zero sufficiently close to $g_R$ equals a unique $\tau_a g_{R+\rho}$ with small $a,\rho$. This proves a uniform local diffeomorphism and uniform local exhaustion. [F1, F4, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra]

5.1 We next turn the prescribed no-bubble breaking into global $H^3$ closeness, rather than assuming it. Put $d_n=(c_n^-+c_n^+)/2$, $T_n=(c_n^+-c_n^-)/2$, and $v_n(s,t)=w_n(s+d_n,t)$. Its cores are at $-T_n,+T_n$. Fix a sufficiently large core radius $S$. The assumed smooth convergence makes $v_n$ converge to the corresponding translates of the two components on these fixed-width cores and collars. The neck-collapse part of the hypothesis puts the remaining three regions, for large $S,n$, in the exact charts about $x_0,y,x_2$. On the cores the maps are uniformly close to $u_{T_n}$, while on those three regions they are close because both approach the same generator. Consequently the inverse local addition between them is defined everywhere and preserves the boundary tangent domain. On each of these three chart regions write the coordinate difference $z_n=q(v_n)-q(u_{T_n})$. Choose a real cutoff equal to one outside fixed-width collars of its endpoints, vanishing smoothly before it reaches the component cores, and extend $\zeta_n=\chi_n z_n$ by zero to the whole strip. The derivatives of these cutoffs through every fixed order are bounded independently of $n$. Each $\zeta_n$ belongs to $H^3\cap\mathcal X^1$: for finite necks this follows from smoothness and finite length, and on outer tails from the actual exponential asymptotics of both finite-energy maps. There is the exact equation $$D_0\zeta_n=\chi_n' z_n-\chi_n D_0q(u_{T_n}).$$ The first term tends to zero in $H^2$, since it is supported in finitely many fixed-width translated collars of smooth component convergence. The second is zero except at the fixed-width pregluing join, where it is $O(\epsilon_{T_n})$. Apply the actual whole-strip inverse and higher regularity in [F3] to conclude $\|\zeta_n\|_{H^3}\to0$, independently of the three region lengths. On their omitted collars and the component cores convergence already holds smoothly. The inverse local-addition formula is coordinate difference on exact generator charts and a bounded smooth composition on the cores. Patching these finitely many regions therefore proves $$\|\mathcal E_{u_{T_n}}^{-1}(v_n)\|_{H^3}\longrightarrow0.$$ This argument also proves global energy convergence from these hypotheses. [F1, F3, F5, given, construct, algebra]

5.2 Since $g_{T_n}$ differs from $u_{T_n}$ by $O(\epsilon_{T_n})$, the chart transition estimates in [F3] turn step 5.1 into $H^3$ closeness to $g_{T_n}$. Apply the uniform phase exhaustion of step 4.1 to get $v_n=\tau_{\alpha_n}g_{T_n+\rho_n}$ with $\alpha_n,\rho_n\to0$. The convergence of these parameters follows quantitatively from the uniform inverse phase derivative and the phases tending to zero. Undoing $v_n=\tau_{d_n}w_n$ gives $w_n=\tau_{\alpha_n-d_n}g_{T_n+\rho_n}$. Thus the asserted parameter asymptotics and eventual representation are proved. At this point uniqueness holds within each local parameter neighborhood; global uniqueness is proved next. [F1, F3, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Fix a smooth target metric and use the translation-invariant energy density $e(u)=\tfrac12(|u_s|^2+|u_t|^2)$. Define $A^\pm(b)=\int_{[b-2,b+2]\times[0,1]}e(u^\pm)$. These smooth nonnegative functions decay with all derivatives at both infinities and have positive maxima. By [F5] choose $0<\delta<\min(\max A^-,\max A^+)$ which is a regular value of both functions. Each level set $(A^\pm)^{-1}(\delta)$ is finite: it is compact by the end decay, and its points are isolated by nonzero derivative, so an infinite set would have an accumulation point contradicting that same derivative condition. Choose a fixed $S$ containing all these crossings and sufficiently large that $A^\pm<\delta/4$ outside $[-S,S]$. Let $b_-$ be the first crossing of $A^-$ and $b_+$ the last crossing of $A^+$. These choices only use a common regular value, finite minima and maxima. [F1, F5, construct, algebra]

6.2 For every sufficiently large $R$, the energy-window profile $A_R(b)=\int_{[b-2,b+2]\times[0,1]}e(g_R)$ has its first $\delta$ crossing near $-R+b_-$ and its last near $R+b_+$. To verify this globally, on each shifted compact core the profile and its first $b$ derivative converge to the respective $A^\pm$, while outside those cores it is below $\delta/2$. The latter follows from component tail smallness, the fixed join estimate and the global $H^3$ correction: $H^3\subset C^1$ bounds the change in each fixed-length window energy uniformly by $C\|\xi_R\|_{H^3}$. The derivative of a window integral is the difference of its two endpoint slice integrals, giving the same uniform bound for its $b$ derivative. Compactness of the component core complements of small crossing neighborhoods excludes any extra crossings. Nonzero derivative at each component crossing gives exactly one nearby crossing by the one-dimensional inverse theorem. Call the first and last crossings $B_L(R),B_R(R)$. They are smooth by step 2.1 and the implicit-function proof of [F4]. On the fixed translated cores step 1.2 also bounds the correction's $R$ derivative in $C^1$. The derivative identities for the pregluing there are exactly $\partial_R A_R=\partial_b A_R$ on the left and $\partial_R A_R=-\partial_b A_R$ on the right, up to $O(\epsilon_R)$ correction terms. Implicit differentiation at the crossings therefore gives $$B_L'(R)=-1+O(\epsilon_R),\qquad B_R'(R)=1+O(\epsilon_R).$$ In particular their separation $T(R)=B_R(R)-B_L(R)$ has $T'(R)>1$ after increasing $R_0$. [F1, F4, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Separation of the first and last energy-window crossings is invariant under translation. Hence $\tau_a g_R=\tau_{a'}g_{R'}$ implies $T(R)=T(R')$, so $R=R'$ by step 6.2. Then $g_R$ would have longitudinal period $a-a'$ if these translations differed. A nonconstant smooth periodic strip has positive energy on one period (zero energy would make both derivatives identically zero), and summing its disjoint period translates contradicts finite total energy. Thus $a=a'$. This proves the global injectivity and uniqueness asserted in the statement. The local diffeomorphism of step 4.1 is in the actual two-dimensional regular zero manifold because [F2] gives kernel dimension two and [F4] gives its actual local zero charts. Translation is free by the same finite-energy argument, and the local phase charts give actual local quotient coordinates. Consequently the injective map $R\mapsto[g_R]$ is a smooth local coordinate there. [F1, F2, F4, step 4.1, step 6.2, construct, algebra]

8.1 Finally the translated compact-core convergence of [F1] and the global $H^3$ correction imply no energy loss for $g_R$: the pregluing has energy tending to $E(u^-)+E(u^+)$, and the change under its correction is at most $C\|\xi_R\|_{H^3}+C\|\xi_R\|_{H^3}^2$ by the $L^2$ derivative bound and the uniformly bounded coefficient formulas. Away from the two translated cores both pregluing and correction have uniformly small image distance to the corresponding generators. Thus $[g_R]$ has precisely the stated two-level no-bubble limit. Conversely step 5.2 and global uniqueness say that every sequence with that limit eventually lies in this family and has $R\to\infty$. The convergence conditions use compact smooth seminorms on integer-radius cores, tail image bounds and energy bounds, all countably many tests; adjoining this endpoint to the local quotient therefore has a countable neighborhood base. The two implications just proved identify its induced topology on this end with $(R_0,\infty]$, or with $[0,1/R_0)$ by $R\mapsto1/R$, after restricting to this end. Indeed if no neighborhood of the endpoint were contained in any given tail of the family, a sequence chosen from a decreasing countable base would contradict sequential exhaustion or $R\to\infty$; the reverse continuity follows from the convergence of $g_R$. This is the claimed topological collar with smooth interior, without a claim of smoothness in $1/R$ at zero. Every choice was finite or countable, or a prescribed contraction limit. The construction concerns actual regular strips and actual no-bubble breaking and leaves classification and gluing of other strata to separate proofs. [F1, F3, F4, F5, step 5.2, step 6.2, step 7.1, construct, algebra] ∎
