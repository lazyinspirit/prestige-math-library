## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $U$ be a connected oriented smooth handlebody with genus-$h$ boundary $\Sigma$, and let $m=h+k-1>0$ disjoint proper compression disks cut it into $k\ge1$ balls. Broad smooth boundary tangencies of these auxiliary disks are permitted. Write $\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_m$ for their boundary circles and $A_1,\ldots,A_k$ for the components of $\Sigma\setminus\bigcup\gamma_j$. Fix ANY orientation-compatible Riemann surface structure $j$ on $\Sigma$ and put $X=\operatorname{Sym}^m(\Sigma)$ and $L=q(\gamma_1\times\cdots\times\gamma_m)$.

For every actual smooth based disk $u:(\overline{\mathbb D},\partial\mathbb D,p)\to(X,L,x)$, let its domain be the componentwise relative point-divisor degrees
$$D(u)=\sum_{i=1}^k b_i A_i,\qquad b_i\in\mathbb Z.$$
Its boundary Maslov index satisfies
$$\mu_L(u)=2\sum_{i=1}^k b_i.$$
This includes all signed multiplicities and all realized smooth relative classes. If $k\ge2$, each unit component domain $A_i$ is itself realized by an explicitly constructed smooth based disk at ANY $x\in L$, of boundary Maslov index two; this is not an assertion of a holomorphic representative.

Every actual compact geometric Cauchy--Riemann-symbol operator along $u$ has real index $m+2\sum b_i$. In the bounded point-trace realizations $H^{q+1}\to H^q$, $q\ge2$, imposing its fixed boundary-node value subtracts $m$ and gives index $2\sum b_i$. The same indices hold for any autonomous nearly symmetric tame structure equal to $\operatorname{Sym}^m(j)$ near the diagonal, with its actual boundary torus totally real; the comparison homotopy is proved below. No ordinary $H^1$ point trace is used.

In particular an actual nonconstant holomorphic boundary disk of Maslov index two, in the protected nearly symmetric class for which compact-domain positivity holds, has domain EXACTLY one $A_i$. This proves the domain/index classification only: it computes neither its count nor its regularity or attachment gluing. For $k=1$ it asserts the index of every realized class without asserting a nonzero-degree sphere or disk exists. For the omitted point target $h=0,k=1,m=0$, every actual map is constant, its divisor domain and index are zero.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual balanced handlebody cut, its attaching circles, the fixed background $j$, the actual smooth disk and its based node when used.

[F1] A balanced attaching cut admits actual smooth round planar component parametrizations and compatible signed gluing collars, after auxiliary same-boundary neat preparation if needed. Its construction works for an abstract handlebody by first transporting it to a standard embedded handlebody. We use the supplied SMOOTH parametrizations, not its auxiliary complex structure in place of the fixed $j$. The broad same-boundary preparation supplies actual neat disks and smooth standard cut balls, and those data supply an actual finite oriented surface polygon. ([[lem-balanced-attaching-cuts-admit-round-component-complex-models]], [[lem-proper-compression-disks-admit-neat-witnesses-with-standard-cut-balls]], [[lem-neat-balanced-handlebody-cuts-supply-finite-surface-polygons]])

[F2] Finite transposition data realize a positive degree-$r$ disk cover with $r$ boundary components of degree one and genus $\ell$ for $r\ge2$; degree one is realized when $\ell=0$. These cover surfaces and finite planar collar gluings have actual finite smooth triangle data. Equal Euler characteristic and boundary count give an actual oriented smooth matching with prescribed boundary collars and finitely many positive interior germs. ([[lem-finite-transposition-data-realize-branched-disk-covers]], [[lem-finite-planar-gluings-and-branched-covers-have-smooth-triangle-data]], [[lem-finite-smooth-surface-presentations-have-prescribed-collar-matches]])

[F3] The actual quotient determinant has the Vandermonde factor. A smooth field tangent and nonzero on a finite disjoint circle family can be extended and perturbed, relative those collars and a finite set, to have finitely many isolated transverse zeros. Its zero indices in a finite smooth cut component sum to its Euler characteristic. ([[lem-symmetric-product-quotient-determinant-has-vandermonde-ramification]], [[thm-parametric-transversality]], [[lem-manifold-bump-for-a-compact-set-inside-an-open-set]], [[lem-finite-triangulated-surface-tangent-field-index-sum]])

[F4] Point-divisor degrees are homotopy invariant and additive. Actual equal-domain based disks on a single product torus have equal Maslov index. Their full-circle boundary winding differences equal the corresponding coefficient jumps. A smooth sphere has determinant degree $(m+1-h)n_z$. ([[lem-symmetric-product-divisor-intersections-and-point-movement]], [[lem-equal-whitney-domains-have-equal-boundary-windings-and-fredholm-index]], [[lem-multipointed-symmetric-product-spheres-have-index-shift-two-k]])

[F5] An actual disk operator with oriented totally real boundary has index $m$ plus its squared-determinant boundary winding, with all higher integer realizations and bounded range inverses. ([[lem-geometric-oriented-disk-cauchy-riemann-fredholm-and-higher-regularity]])

[F6] The relative tame-extension supplier proves the explicit Cayley contraction toward a compatible background structure: its anti-complex contraction coefficient has norm strictly below one and can be multiplied by any scalar in $[0,1]$. ([[lem-product-integrable-generator-neighborhoods-and-relative-tame-extension]])

[F7] Actual protected nearly symmetric compact disks have nonnegative component degrees, and a zero-domain disk is constant. This does not require an index formula or a compactification theorem. ([[lem-nearly-symmetric-compact-bubble-domain-positivity-and-energy]])

## Proof

1.1 First obtain the geometric polygon data required by [F4]. Transport an abstract handlebody to a standard ball with finitely many one-handles embedded in $S^3$, by the finite handlebody parametrization construction in [F1]. Apply its broad same-boundary neat preparation there, retaining every attaching circle, and select the finitely many actual smooth cut-ball parametrizations it supplies. The neat-cut polygon construction of [F1] then gives an actual oriented finite polygon, which transports back to the original surface. Its chosen target complex structure $j$ is never replaced. These same cut-ball data give the smooth planar component parametrizations used below. Suppose now $k\ge2$ and fix $A=A_i$. The ball-cut parametrizations in [F1] make its compact cut closure a planar surface. Let $e$ be the number of attaching circles with just one boundary copy on this component and $\ell$ the number with both copies on it. Its boundary count is $e+2\ell$ and $$\chi(A)=2-e-2\ell.$$ Here $e\ge1$: the finite graph with the cut components as vertices and each attaching circle as an edge is connected, since regluing it recovers the connected $\Sigma$. A vertex with no edge to another vertex would be isolated, impossible when there are at least two vertices. Glue the two boundary copies of each internal circle using the actual signed collars. The result $S_0$ is the actual embedded subsurface obtained by filling those internal circles in $\Sigma$, with $e$ external boundary circles, Euler characteristic still $\chi(A)$ and an orientation-preserving inclusion into $\Sigma$. These assertions follow by the collar gluing: a circle has Euler characteristic zero, so identifying a pair changes no finite cell count. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Put $a=0$ if $e\ge2$ or $\ell=0$, and put $a=1$ otherwise. If $a=0$ take $S=S_0$ and let $f$ be its inclusion. If $a=1$, choose a tiny proper closed arc $I$ of one internal circle, with $x_j$ in its interior. A positive smooth surface chart $H$ straightens this arc to $[-2c,2c]\subset\mathbb R$, with $x_j=H(0)$. It need not be holomorphic for the given $j$. Replace the interior of a small ellipse around this interval by the parametrized annulus $1\le|\zeta|\le R$, $R>1$, with map $$f(\zeta)=H(c(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})).$$ On its outer circle this is a diffeomorphism onto that ellipse boundary; use its univalent outer collar as the gluing collar to the unchanged remainder of $S_0$. Thus $S$ is a smooth compact surface, $f$ is smooth, and its interior maps diffeomorphically onto $\operatorname{Int}S_0\setminus I$. The inner circle maps to the two banks of $I$, with two quadratic tips, smooth even at those tips. Its label is that internal $\gamma_j$; choose either of the two inverse points of $x_j$ on it as its marked boundary point. Each external boundary circle has its unique marked point over its generator coordinate. The resulting surface has $$r=e+a\quad\hbox{boundary circles},\qquad \chi(S)=2-e-2\ell-a.$$ It has finite smooth triangle data by [F2]: separate the internal seam collar as an annular piece before deleting the ellipse, then use its planar gluing and annulus-insertion clauses. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 If $r=1$, the choice in step 2.1 forces $e=1,\ell=0$, so take the identity disk cover. Otherwise [F2] supplies an actual positive branched cover $p:F\to\overline{\mathbb D}$ of degree $r$, with exactly $r$ boundary components of degree one and $$N=2r-2+2\ell\quad\hbox{simple ramification points},\qquad\chi(F)=2-2\ell-r=\chi(S).$$ Its branch values are distinct. Choose for each ramification point a distinct target point of $\operatorname{Int}S$, avoiding the finitely many inverse images under $f$ of all inactive generator coordinates. Since $f$ is an orientation-preserving local diffeomorphism on the interior, prescribe at this point an orientation-preserving germ $B$ such that $f\circ B$ is complex linear in the ramification coordinate of $F$ and a $j$-holomorphic target coordinate. This is the explicit smooth germ $f^{-1}$ composed with that linear coordinate map. By [F2] extend these finitely many germs to an actual oriented diffeomorphism $B:F\to S$, with every boundary component paired to its label and its unique point over $p=1$ sent to the selected point over $x_j$. This supplies the required smooth matching; a topological surface homeomorphism alone would not suffice. [F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 For $z\in\overline{\mathbb D}$ take the unordered fiber values of $f\circ B$ over $p^{-1}(z)$ and adjoin the remaining $m-r$ generator coordinates as constants. This defines a smooth map $u_A$ to $X$. Away from ramification the root functions are smooth sheet inverses. Near a ramification point their two active values are holomorphic functions of the two square roots of $z-q$, by the exact prescribed germs in step 3.1; their elementary symmetric functions are therefore smooth there. All other roots have regular sheet coordinates there, and no branch value has two ramification points. This proves smoothness at every repeated-root tuple, even though the whole map is not asserted holomorphic. On the physical boundary each active labelled circle contributes exactly one root, because its $p$ boundary degree is one. The other labels have their distinct fixed generator roots. Thus $u_A(\partial\mathbb D)\subset L$ and $u_A(1)=x$. The interior root map $f\circ B$ is an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism onto $\operatorname{Int}S_0$ with the possible slit removed. Every generic point of $A$ has exactly one inverse root, and every point off the attaching system outside $A$ has none. Its relative divisor degrees are consequently exactly the unit chain $A$. [F2, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Choose a smooth field $V$ on $\Sigma$, tangent and nonzero on every $\gamma_j$, and nonzero at all the finitely many ramification image values. Extend its prescribed circle collars by finite bumps and apply [F3] on their compact complement, also prescribing nonzero values at those finitely many additional points. Apply the same finite parameter transversality on the finitely many supplied triangle edges and vertices: their dimensions are less than the field rank two, so a transverse zero set there is empty. Thus it has finitely many isolated zeros away from the circle system, ramification images and triangle one-skeleton. Their indices inside $A$ sum to $\chi(A)$ by [F3], with every hypothesis of its facewise index calculation met. To meet the positive-boundary convention of that supplier, on the CUT planar component multiply $V$ by $e^{i\theta}$, with $\theta=0$ or $\pi$ on each boundary collar according to its orientation. Extend this scalar by finite collar bumps. This introduces no zero or index change, so the sum for the original global field is still $\chi(A)$, even at the two opposite copies of an internal circle. Pulling its zeros back by $f\circ B$ preserves their indices and gives exactly one inverse point for each. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Off root collisions take the square of the wedge of $dq(V)$ in the $m$ roots, a section $\sigma$ of $(u_A^*\det TX)^2$. Its squared Vandermonde expression in [F3] extends continuously by zero at collisions. Every pulled-back field zero contributes twice its field index. Every simple ramification contributes one positive zero: the two exchanged roots are local holomorphic coordinates there by step 3.1. The active map meets exactly $\ell-a$ constant roots, namely those on the filled internal circles; each is met once in the interior and at a point where $p$ is unramified, by the exclusions in step 3.1. Its squared root difference contributes index two, since $f\circ B$ preserves orientation. Constant roots on all other labels lie outside this image. Active roots cannot coincide elsewhere, since that interior map is injective. Thus the complete zero-index sum of $\sigma$ is $$2\chi(A)+N+2(\ell-a)=2(2-e-2\ell)+(2(e+a)-2+2\ell)+2(\ell-a)=2.$$ Coincident zero values at different regular roots add by the product winding rule. The section on the boundary is its actual squared real tangent determinant, up to a positive real factor, since $V$ is nonzero and tangent to every labelled circle. Finite line-section excision therefore identifies its zero-index sum with the boundary Maslov phase. This proves $\mu_L(u_A)=2$, and realizes every proper unit component domain at any prescribed $x$. No holomorphic disk existence has been asserted. [F3, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 For $k\ge2$, take any smooth based disk $u$ and concatenate the unit models $u_{A_i}$ with integer exponents $b_i$, reversing source orientation for negative exponents. Flatten their node neighborhoods by real scalar cutoffs in a smooth adapted chart of $L$ and glue their constant boundary collars; finite rounding gives actual smooth based disks. Their divisor degrees and physical boundary phases add by winding, so the result has domain $D(u)$ and Maslov index $2\sum b_i$. The equal-domain one-torus assertion of [F4] gives that same index for $u$. For $k=1$, its domain is $b\Sigma$. Compare its boundary loop with the constant disk. Every coefficient jump across every $\gamma_j$ is zero, so the full-circle winding comparison in [F4] makes each labelled loop winding zero. Their real argument lifts contract the boundary loop inside $L$, fixing its node. Capping by this contraction gives a sphere $A$ with $n_z(A)=b$. Since $m=h$ in this case, [F4] gives $\deg\det(A^*TX)=b$. Comparing determinant frames across that cap makes the disk's squared boundary phase twice this sphere clutch degree, namely $2b$. This proves the formula for all realized classes, without presuming a nonzero sphere exists when the topology forbids it. [F4, F5, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 Formula [F5] gives compact operator index $m+\mu_L$. For a nearly symmetric tame $J$, contract it to the fixed $J_0=\operatorname{Sym}^m(j)$ by [F6]'s actual Cayley formula off the protected diagonal neighborhood and extend by the identity inside it. The product area form is nondegenerate off that neighborhood and $J_0$ compatible there; the Cayley coefficient is zero on the fixed neighborhood, so this is smooth across it. Its scalar contraction remains tame. The product torus is Lagrangian for that form, so remains totally real: $w,Jw\in TL$ would give $\omega(w,Jw)=0$, contradicting tameness unless $w=0$. Joint complex frames over the disk times this compact homotopy parameter are constructed by finite-chart projections and the matrix-commutator ODE in [F5]. The physical real boundary planes then give a continuous squared-phase loop homotopy. Their winding, and hence the actual index, is unchanged. At order $H^{q+1}$, $q\ge2$, node evaluation is bounded and onto its real $m$-plane, using a cutoff local real frame. Its kernel restriction has index smaller by $m$. Explicitly, for a bounded range inverse $Q$ of $P$, its restricted range consists of $y\in\operatorname{Ran}P$ with $\operatorname{ev}(Qy)\in\operatorname{ev}(\ker P)$, a closed finite-dimensional constraint. The finite exact kernel/range sequence for evaluation therefore subtracts $m$ from the index. This proves the claimed fixed-node formula without an $H^1$ point trace or surjectivity assertion. [F5, F6, step 7.1, construct, algebra]

9.1 Finally [F7] gives $b_i\ge0$ for every actual protected holomorphic compact disk. If its Maslov index is two, step 7.1 gives $\sum b_i=1$. These are integers, so exactly one coefficient is one and all others are zero. A zero-domain disk would be constant by [F7]; no nonconstant zero-degree component has been introduced. This is only the asserted index/domain restriction. Counts, generic regularity, the value of a unit count, boundary-attachment exhaustion and cancellation do not follow from this integer argument. If $m=0$ the target is a point, so its only actual maps and linearizations have domain and index zero directly. All constructions above used finite covers, fields, germs and collars under the stated Countable Choice suppliers. [F7, step 7.1, step 8.1, algebra] ∎
