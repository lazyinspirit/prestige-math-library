## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $\Sigma$ be a connected closed Riemann surface, $m\ge1$, and $D=p_1+\cdots+p_m$ a divisor with pairwise distinct entries. If there is a nonconstant holomorphic map $u:\mathbb {CP}^1\to\operatorname{Sym}^m(\Sigma)$ for the standard complex symmetric-product structure whose image contains $D$, then there is a nonconstant meromorphic function on $\Sigma$ all of whose poles are simple and belong to $\{p_1,\ldots,p_m\}$. Some of these entries may not be poles.

Suppose in addition that $\Sigma$ has an antiholomorphic involution $\rho$ with at least $m+1$ pairwise disjoint embedded circles in its fixed set, and that $p_1,\ldots,p_m$ lie on different such circles. Then no meromorphic function as above exists, and no nonconstant standard holomorphic sphere passes through $D$. This is a statement about the supplied real Riemann surface and divisor: it does not construct such a complex structure for a given attaching system, assert avoidance for nearby almost-complex structures, or compute any sphere index or degeneration count.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual closed complex surface of complex dimension one, the distinct divisor, and, in the second assertion, the supplied antiholomorphic involution and disjoint fixed circles. A pole bound by $D$ means order at most one at its entries and no other poles.

[F1] [[lem-holomorphic-symmetric-product-curves-admit-finite-branched-ordered-lifts]] proves the local root and monodromy statements for a holomorphic symmetric-product curve, including finite exceptional support changes and bounded holomorphic extension after finite power substitutions.

[F2] [[thm-zero-order-factorization-holomorphic-function]], [[thm-holomorphic-implicit-function-theorem]] and [[thm-removable-singularity-characterizations]] supply local finite zero order, holomorphic coordinates at a nonzero derivative, holomorphic roots of a nonvanishing germ after its value is fixed, and bounded puncture removal. [[thm-identity-theorem-holomorphic-functions]] supplies uniqueness across an accumulation set. These chart statements extend over a connected surface by overlap and connectedness.

[F3] [[lem-finite-chart-localization-defines-choice-free-integration-and-compact-stokes]] proves finite-chart integration on a smooth oriented manifold without boundary and $\int_Md\eta=0$ for a compactly supported smooth form. We apply it on the closed oriented surface, to an explicitly cut-off complex one-form, componentwise over real and imaginary parts. No triangulation or general-choice partition is needed.

## Proof

1.1 Write $D_t=u(t)$ and choose $t_0$ with $D_{t_0}=D$. First construct the degree-$m$ incidence cover appropriate for distinct generic entries. By [F1] there is a finite exceptional set $B$ such that the distinct support roots are locally holomorphic with constant multiplicities off $B$. The complement of a finite subset of a connected surface is connected: a path made from finitely many coordinate segments can be detoured in small coordinate disks around the excluded points. Near $t_0$ there are $m$ distinct roots, by disjoint coordinate neighborhoods of the entries of $D$ and continuity. The locally constant support count is consequently $m$ throughout the complement of $B$; we may omit $t_0$ from $B$. The set of pairs $(t,a)$ with $a$ an entry of $D_t$, for $t\notin B$, is thus an actual unbranched $m$-sheeted complex covering, with the root itself giving a holomorphic map to $\Sigma$. Fill it at each member of $B$ by splitting its finite sheet permutation into cycles: a cycle of length $e$ is filled with the coordinate $t-t_b=\zeta^e$, and the root extends by the bounded removal in [F1]. This produces a compact possibly disconnected Riemann surface $C$, a degree-$m$ branched holomorphic covering $p:C\to\mathbb {CP}^1$, and a holomorphic map $f:C\to\Sigma$. Compactness follows from finitely many filled covering disks and a finite evenly covered chart cover of the remaining compact base. In particular for $t\notin B$ the pushforward under $f$ of the fiber $p^{-1}(t)$ is exactly the divisor $D_t$, each entry counted once. This construction uses the root monodromy proved in [F1], rather than identifying the degree with the degree of its ordered-tuple cover. [F1, given, construct]

2.1 On any connected component $C_j$ where $f$ is nonconstant, it is a finite branched covering onto $\Sigma$. Here are the needed details. At any point a target coordinate minus its value has a zero of finite order $e\ge1$, since otherwise [F2] and connectedness make $f$ constant. Factor it as $z^e h(z)$, $h(0)\ne0$. The implicit function theorem in [F2] gives a holomorphic $e$th root of $h$ on a smaller disk, and $\zeta=z h(z)^{1/e}$ is a coordinate, so the map is exactly $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^e$. It is therefore open. Its compact image is also closed, and connectedness of $\Sigma$ makes it surjective. Each fiber is discrete and closed and hence finite by compactness. Derivative zeros are isolated unless the map is constant, again by [F2], so there are finitely many branch points. Over the complement of their values, local inverses and compact separation give a finite covering: otherwise points outside finitely many neighborhoods of a fiber would have an accumulation point in that same fiber. Thus local products over the finite fibers below are well defined without an infinite selection. [F2, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Choose $t_1\notin B$ with $D_{t_1}\ne D_{t_0}$. Such a point exists because $u$ is nonconstant and the complement of the finite set is dense. On $\mathbb {CP}^1$ choose a rational function $h$ having one simple zero at $t_1$, one simple pole at $t_0$, and no other zeros or poles, by a fractional linear coordinate. On the union of the components in step2.1 put $g=h\circ p$. For a regular value $a$ of their map $f$, away from the finitely many images of zeros and poles of $g$, define $$N(a)=\prod_{q\in f^{-1}(a),\ f|_{C_j}\ \mathrm{nonconstant}}g(q).$$ This is single-valued and holomorphic there: local inverse branches are holomorphic and changing their labels permutes the factors. It extends meromorphically across every omitted point. Indeed around a point of the fiber use step2.1 to write $f(\zeta)=w=\zeta^e$ and $g(\zeta)=\zeta^r b(\zeta)$ with $r\in\mathbb Z$ and $b$ holomorphic nonvanishing. The product over the $e$ roots of $w$ is $((-1)^{e-1}w)^r$ times the product of the $b$ values. That latter product and its reciprocal are bounded holomorphic single-valued functions on the punctured disk. Both extend by [F2], and their product stays one; hence it is a holomorphic unit. Multiplying over the finitely many points of the fiber proves both meromorphic extension and $$\operatorname{ord}_a N=\sum_{q\in f^{-1}(a)}\operatorname{ord}_q g.$$ The empty union would give $N=1$; the divisor computation in the next step rules it out in the case at hand. [F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Since $t_0,t_1$ are unbranched values of $p$, the divisor of $g$ is the difference of their simple fibers, restricted to the nonconstant components. A component on which $f$ has one constant value contributes the same multiple of that point to both pushforward fibers: its covering degree under $p$ is independent of the regular value. These constant-component contributions cancel. The order formula in step3.1 and the fiber identity in step1.1 therefore give $$\operatorname{div}(N)=D_{t_1}-D_{t_0}.$$ This divisor is nonzero, so $N$ is nonconstant. Its negative part has coefficient at most one at each entry of $D_{t_0}=D$ and vanishes elsewhere. Thus $N$ is the meromorphic function required in the first assertion. In particular the norm has not multiplied this pole divisor by the degree of $f$: the order formula sums the orders at the individual points of the simple $p$ fiber. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 We record explicitly the zero-and-pole count used for the real assertion. For every nonzero nonconstant meromorphic function $H$ on a closed connected Riemann surface, its zeros and poles are finite, and their orders sum to zero when poles are given negative orders. Local factorization and [F2] make them isolated and closed, hence finite by compactness. Off that finite set, $\eta=dH/H$ is a smooth closed complex one-form. Choose pairwise disjoint coordinate disks about the exceptional points and smooth radial cutoffs $\chi$ equal to zero on smaller disks and one outside the chosen disks. Such finitely many cutoffs can be made from one real smooth step function, and their product is a global cutoff. Then $\chi\eta$ extends by zero smoothly across those points, and [F3] gives $0=\int_\Sigma d(\chi\eta)=\int_\Sigma d\chi\wedge\eta$. On a chosen disk write $H=z^r b(z)$, $r\in\mathbb Z$, $b(0)\ne0$. Here $\eta=r\,dz/z+(b'/b)dz$. The holomorphic function $b'/b$ has a primitive on a smaller coordinate disk by termwise integration of its convergent Taylor series, so its integral on each centered circle is zero. Integrating the radial derivative of $\chi$ over its annulus gives a contribution $2\pi i r$, because $\chi$ increases from zero to one. Thus $\sum r=0$. Applying this to $H-a$, which has the same poles as $H$ for every finite $a$, shows that its zeros counted with positive multiplicity equal the total pole order. The same argument applies if there are no poles; in that case a nonconstant $H$ cannot take any finite value, a contradiction. [F2, F3, construct, algebra]

6.1 Now assume the real hypotheses and suppose a nonconstant meromorphic function $F$ has poles bounded by $D$. The function $F^\rho=\overline{F\circ\rho}$ is meromorphic with the same pole bound, since $\rho$ fixes every entry of $D$. Put $$H_1=(F+F^\rho)/2,\qquad H_2=(F-F^\rho)/(2i).$$ Both satisfy $\overline{H_j\circ\rho}=H_j$, and $F=H_1+iH_2$, so at least one $H=H_j$ is nonconstant. Its poles are simple and lie at a subset of the entries of $D$. By step5.1 it has a positive number $r$ of poles. On each fixed circle it is real wherever finite. Near a fixed point there is a holomorphic coordinate $w$ with $w\circ\rho=\overline w$: starting with $z$ vanishing there, one of $(z+\overline{z\circ\rho})/2$ and $(z-\overline{z\circ\rho})/(2i)$ has nonzero derivative, so [F2] makes it a coordinate with that property. Its fixed set is locally the real axis. The Laurent coefficient of the simple pole of $H$ in that coordinate is a nonzero real number, since the Laurent expansion is preserved by conjugation. Each circle containing a pole contains exactly one of them, by the distinct-oval assumption. On that circle with the pole removed the two approaches to the pole therefore have values tending to opposite real infinities. The intermediate value theorem makes $H$ take every finite real value on that punctured circle. There is also a fixed circle containing none of the entries of $D$, since at least $m+1$ circles were supplied. Choose a point $q$ on it and put $a=H(q)\in\mathbb R$. The function $H-a$ has a zero at $q$ and at least one zero on each of the $r$ different pole circles. These are $r+1$ distinct zeros, whereas step5.1 counts exactly $r$ zeros with multiplicity. This contradiction excludes $F$, and step4.1 excludes the sphere. The proof used only finite covers, finite products, local holomorphic arguments and finite-chart Stokes. It asserts no real-model construction, deformation openness or numerical index formula beyond its stated scope. [F2, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
