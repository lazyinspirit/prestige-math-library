## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Fix the compact nearly symmetric Heegaard geometry with complete marked cut systems, protected diagonal and region-divisor neighborhoods, and common exact product generator charts used in zero-marked bubble exclusion. Every complement component of each individual boundary curve system must contain a marked point. Whitney classes mean continuous two-marked disk relative-homotopy classes with the two boundary arcs on the specified product tori. Their integer $\mu$ is the actual fixed-end Fredholm index constructed by continuous relative smoothing, not a domain Euler formula.

Fix a class $\phi:x\to z$ with $\mu(\phi)=2$ and with all marked divisor degrees zero. Its region divisor degrees $n_R(\phi)$ are well-defined integers. For a common domain-energy constant $C$ put
$$B_\phi=C\sum_R|n_R(\phi)|\int_R\eta.$$
Choose a smooth path with genuine constant endpoint collars, the stipulated protected data, and actual regularity for every nonconstant zero-marked strip of energy at most a number $E_0>B_\phi$. Such paths exist by the auxiliary flat-jet regularity and actual bounded-energy collar-approximation theorems, with $C$ taken uniform in the approximating neighborhood as proved below.

For this path, the moduli space $\widehat{\mathcal M}(\phi)$ of actual strips in $\phi$ modulo longitudinal translation is a Hausdorff second-countable topological one-manifold. It has a compactification by finitely many boundary points. These points are exactly the ordered pairs of actual index-one strip orbits
$$([u],[v]),\qquad u:x\to y,\quad v:y\to z,\quad [u]*[v]=\phi.$$
Each such component has zero marked degrees and energy at most $B_\phi$. Each boundary point has the actual one-sided gluing collar with smooth interior from the regular-pair collar theorem. No differentiability at a prescribed compactifying parameter is asserted.

The number of these boundary points is even. Equivalently the sum modulo two of the products of rigid counts over the factorizations $\phi=\phi_1*\phi_2$ with $\mu(\phi_1)=\mu(\phi_2)=1$ is zero; only finitely many factors with nonzero counts occur. This is the cancellation identity for this fixed zero-marked class. It does not establish finiteness over all classes of prescribed index, a domain-index formula, positive-marked boundary-degeneration cancellation, or polygon/target-degeneration gluing.

## Proof

1.1 First justify the class degrees and every required energy bound. By [F1] choose a smooth disk model constant near its two marked ends. Its relative divisor degree is invariant under changing the model: the actual smooth common-tail homotopy in [F1] gives a smooth family of pullback line bundles with canonical boundary sections nonzero, and the explicit degree invariance there applies. A capped actual strip has the same degree as its continuous class model by that same homotopy argument. Concatenation adds the degrees: choose eventually constant models, join their constant ends in a generator ball avoiding each divisor, and use disk frames or the connection constructed in the degree-additivity proof [F3]. Its curvature vanishes in that ball, so the two curvature integrals add exactly. Therefore $n_R(\psi_1*\psi_2)=n_R(\psi_1)+n_R(\psi_2)$, and likewise for marks. Actual strip degrees are nonnegative by protected-divisor positivity, as used in [F1] and [F3]. Thus every actual strip in $\phi$ has energy at most $B_\phi$. If two actual strips concatenate in class $\phi$, their nonnegative region degrees sum to $n_R(\phi)$; each energy is therefore at most $B_\phi$, and zero total marked degrees make both zero-marked. These assertions are vacuous when a region coefficient of $\phi$ is negative and no such strips exist. The energy constant can be chosen uniformly on a small coefficient neighborhood: on the compact complement of a smaller diagonal neighborhood the background taming function on unit vectors has a positive minimum; small $C^0$ perturbations preserve half that minimum and an upper bound for $|J|$. Inside the fixed integrable neighborhood the product metric and quotient derivative bound are unchanged. These are precisely the two constants in the actual proof of the domain-energy bound [F1]. Choose this common $C$, an auxiliary generic path in that small family from [F2], and $E_0>B_\phi$. Its sufficiently close genuine constant-collar approximants are regular up to $E_0$ by [F2] and have the same $C$. This proves the stated existence as well as the energy and class-degree assertions. [F1, F2, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 We construct the genuine translation quotient. Every solution in $\phi$ has actual index two by [F1] and energy less than $E_0$ by step 1.1, so is regular. The actual split zero chart [F4] is consequently two-dimensional. Translation acts freely: a nonzero period of a nonconstant finite-energy strip repeats one positive energy rectangle infinitely often, contradicting finite energy. It acts continuously in $H^3$: for a fixed reference and its finitely many compact coefficient charts, strong continuity of scalar Sobolev shifts follows first for smooth compactly supported functions by the fundamental theorem, and then by smooth approximation and the locally bounded shift norms; composition and the constant-tail reference differences are controlled by [F4]. The action is proper in the concrete sequential sense needed here. If $u_n\to u$ and $\tau_{a_n}u_n\to v$ strongly in $H^3$ and $|a_n|\to\infty$, uniform $C^0$ convergence from the map charts and the uniform ends of the fixed map $u$ force $v$ to be one constant generator on every compact source rectangle. This is impossible for a solution of class index two. Thus the $a_n$ are bounded; a subsequence converges and joint continuity identifies $v$ as a translate of $u$. To produce a local slice, embed the compact target smoothly in a finite Euclidean space by the finite-atlas partition construction in [F4]. A nonzero derivative $u_s$ gives a compactly supported smooth source test $b$ for which $\ell(u)=\int b\cdot F(u)$ has $\ell'(u)[u_s]\ne0$. The scalar function $\ell(\tau_a w)$ is jointly smooth in $a$ and the local $H^3$ coordinates of $w$: change variables to put all $a$ derivatives on the compactly supported test, and use the smooth local composition estimates for $F(w)$. The actual finite-dimensional zero chart and the one-variable implicit theorem give a unique small phase making this functional constant. Its level slice is one-dimensional. Properness and freeness exclude additional large-phase returns after shrinking that neighborhood: otherwise nearby points give such a return sequence, whose bounded-phase limit stabilizes $u$, forcing zero phase. The continuous action and this phase identify the local quotient with that interval slice. The same sequential properness separates any two distinct orbits by saturated neighborhoods. The quotient is Hausdorff and second-countable, since the quotient map is open and images of a countable configuration base form a base. It is therefore an actual topological one-manifold, obtained without differentiating translation on the entire rough $H^3$ space. [F1, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Apply the actual compactness in [F3] to any sequence in this quotient, choosing representatives by Countable Choice when necessary. Its limit has no bubbles, all its nonconstant strip levels have energy at most $B_\phi<E_0$ and are regular, and full class splitting holds. The finite-level kernel proof of [F3] needs only those finitely many regular components: patch their right inverses with squared cutoffs and exact product necks, giving an error tending to zero; correct by the Neumann series, and use the cut-back kernel projections to obtain inverse kernel maps. Thus its actual index sum is two, without assuming that the chosen path is regular at larger energies. Each nonconstant level has a nonzero translation kernel and hence positive actual index. There are therefore either one index-two level or two index-one levels. In the one-level case the exact-end cutoff inverse argument in [F4] upgrades convergence after translation to global $H^3$, so the quotient sequence converges to the actual unbroken limit in its local quotient interval. In the two-level case the ordered pair has concatenated class $\phi$ by [F3]. Conversely any pair of actual index-one strips whose classes concatenate to $\phi$ has, by step 1.1, zero marks and component energy at most $B_\phi$, so is regular and is among finitely many pairs by the actual index-one finiteness theorem [F4] and the finite generator set. This proves finiteness of the candidate broken boundary set and identifies every possible sequential degeneration. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Each of these finitely many pairs is an actual boundary limit, with its actual collar. Apply [F4] to its two regular index-one components. The produced $g_R$ has the concatenated class $\phi$ by [F3]; therefore it is zero-marked and has energy at most $B_\phi$ by step 1.1. Its actual index is two by [F1], and its regularity and injective local quotient coordinate are the actual gluing conclusions in [F4]. That theorem supplies both convergence to this broken pair and exhaustion of every sequence with precisely this two-level, no-loss limit. Thus adjoining one point at $R=\infty$ gives a genuine topological half-interval chart, with smooth interior. No differentiability at a chosen value of $1/R$ at zero is required. There is no artificial energy-cut boundary, because the bound of step 1.1 applies to the whole fixed class, including every strip in this collar. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 We verify uniqueness and separation at the adjoined points rather than presuming a Hausdorff compactification. A sequence with a given two-level limit cannot have a different two-level limit. Choose in each component of a proposed second limit a fixed compact source window with positive energy. Its translations cannot recede from both original level cores: the original neck and outer-tail energy outside large cores tends uniformly to zero by [F3]. Hence each such translation stays within a bounded distance of one original core along a subsequence. Both cannot remain near the same core because their separation tends to infinity. Their longitudinal order therefore matches the two original cores. Pass to limits of these bounded offsets. Smooth local convergence then identifies each proposed component with a translate of the corresponding original component. The broken orbit pair is identical. The same argument rules out simultaneous one-level strong $H^3$ convergence and two-level degeneration: the second positive-energy core would have to recede to infinity, whereas strong global $H^3$ convergence and the tail cutoff inverse give zero energy there. If tails of collars for distinct pairs met at arbitrarily large parameters, their common points would yield just such a sequence with two different limits, a contradiction. Shrink their finitely many tails to make them disjoint. They also separate each adjoined point from any fixed unbroken point; otherwise a countable shrinking neighborhood base at that unbroken point would give the excluded simultaneous one- and two-level convergence. Declare the original quotient opens and these collar half-interval charts to be a base. The injective local-coordinate and exhaustion conclusions of [F4] show that their intersections with the interior have its original topology, and the preceding separation proves Hausdorffness. There are finitely many added charts, so this is second-countable and is a topological one-manifold with exactly the stated boundary points. [F3, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 This manifold is compact. Any sequence of interior points has, by step 3.1, a subsequence converging either in an unbroken quotient interval or, by the actual exhaustion of [F4], in one of the adjoined collar charts. A sequence containing infinitely many boundary points has a constant subsequence because there are finitely many. Thus the space is sequentially compact. For clarity its second-countability suffices to turn this into compactness under Countable Choice. An open cover has a countable subcover by choosing one cover member for each basis set contained in some member; the basis-cover property guarantees that these chosen members cover. If no finite part of this countable cover covered the space, Countable Choice would choose a point outside its first $n$ members for each positive integer $n$. A convergent subsequence and a cover member containing its limit contradict that construction. Hence every open cover has a finite subcover. This proves actual compactness, not just a formal compactified-moduli assertion. [F3, F4, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Here is an elementary parity proof that applies to this topological compactification. Cover any compact Hausdorff topological one-manifold by finitely many relatively small coordinate intervals or boundary half-intervals whose closures lie in larger charts. Let $K$ be the finite set consisting of their frontiers and all actual boundary points. The latter is finite: it is a closed discrete subset, hence compact discrete, and its singletons form an open cover in its subspace topology. Outside $K$, membership in each chosen small interval is locally constant; each connected component of the complement therefore lies in one of them, since they cover. In that chart it is an open interval. Its closure has two endpoint germs in $K$, since the containing chart closure is compact; a finite deleted set produces ordinary intervals, and no such interval can close into a circle inside one chart. There are only finitely many such components, because each endpoint germ is incident to one of the finitely many points of $K$, with exactly two germs at each interior cut point and one at each actual boundary point. Counting endpoint germs gives $2e=2i+b$, where $e$ is the number of interval pieces, $i$ the number of interior points of $K$ and $b$ the number of actual boundary points. If a component had no cut point, it would be a compact open interval in one chart, which is impossible; equivalently refine that component by one additional small interval before making $K$. Thus every piece is included in this finite count. It follows that $b$ is even. Apply this to the actual compactification established in steps 5.1–6.1. Each boundary point is one ordered pair of rigid orbits by steps 3.1–4.1. Partitioning those finitely many pairs by their intermediate generator and their two Whitney classes gives exactly the stated sum of products of rigid counts. Its parity is zero. All these counts are finite by [F4] and step 1.1. This proves the fixed-class cancellation identity, while leaving unrestricted class finiteness, positive-marked degenerations and polygon or target gluing outside the conclusion. [F1, F4, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra] ∎
