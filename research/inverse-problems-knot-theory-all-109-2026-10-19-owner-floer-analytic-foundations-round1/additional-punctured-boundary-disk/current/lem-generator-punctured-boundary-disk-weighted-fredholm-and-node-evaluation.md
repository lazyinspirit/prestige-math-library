## Statement

Assume Countable Choice and the actual oriented totally real compact-disk operator $P$ of the geometric disk Fredholm theorem. Let its rank be $m$ and its boundary winding integer be $k$, so its actual compact $H^1\to L^2$ index is $m+2k$. Choose a marked boundary point $p$. Suppose a holomorphic boundary coordinate $w$ centered at $p$, and a smooth complex bundle frame near it, identify the boundary with $\mathbb R^m$ and the actual local operator with $D_0=\partial_{\operatorname{Re}w}+i\partial_{\operatorname{Im}w}$. Operator and forcing components in a changed source coordinate use the Cauchy–Riemann symbol transformation. This exact local hypothesis holds for a boundary disk attached in a fixed product-integrable generator chart.

Write $w=-e^{-\pi\zeta}$, $\zeta=s+it$, $t\in[0,1]$, for its positive cylindrical end. If $f_w$ is the local disk forcing, its cylindrical component is
$$f_{\rm cyl}(s,t)=\overline{\frac{dw}{d\zeta}}\,f_w(w).$$
Fix $0<\delta<\pi$. For an integer $q\ge0$ let $Y_{q,\delta}$ consist of forcings which are weak $H^q$ on a fixed compact source core and have $e^{\delta s}f_{\rm cyl}\in H^q$ on the end. Let $X_{q,\delta}$ consist of sections in the actual compact-disk $H^1$ boundary domain which are weak $H^{q+1}$ on that core and, on the end, have the unique form
$$\xi_{\rm cyl}=c+r,\qquad c\in\mathbb R^m,\qquad e^{\delta s}r\in H^{q+1}.$$
Use the sum-of-squares core, weighted-end and $|c|$ norms. These spaces are Hilbert; changing the fixed core cutoff gives equivalent norms. Only the original zeroth real boundary condition is imposed.

The actual weighted realization $P_{q,\delta}:X_{q,\delta}\to Y_{q,\delta}$ is Fredholm of real index $m+2k$. Its kernel equals the actual compact-disk $H^1$ kernel, its range is $Y_{q,\delta}\cap\operatorname{Ran}P_{\rm compact}$, and inclusion in compact-disk $L^2$ identifies its cokernel with the compact-disk cokernel. Its range has a specified bounded right inverse obtained by restricting the compact operator's range inverse. More precisely any compact $H^1$ solution of $P\xi=f\in Y_{q,\delta}$ lies in $X_{q,\delta}$ and satisfies
$$\|\xi\|_{X_{q,\delta}}\le C_{q,\delta}\bigl(\|f\|_{Y_{q,\delta}}+\|\xi\|_{H^1(\mathbb D)}\bigr).$$
The asymptotic constant map $\operatorname{ev}_p:\xi\mapsto c$ is bounded and onto $\mathbb R^m$. On its kernel the weighted operator is Fredholm of index $2k$, with a bounded right inverse on its actual range. For $q\ge1$ the constant is the actual continuous node value; on homogeneous solutions it is the smooth compact-disk value for every $q$. At level $q=0$, its definition is the weighted asymptotic decomposition and does not assume that an arbitrary $H^1$ section is pointwise continuous. These punctured higher spaces are not being identified with higher unweighted compact-disk Sobolev spaces. The theorem supplies neither surjectivity of the node-fixed operator nor nonlinear boundary-bubble gluing.

## Proof

1.1 The spaces and their comparisons with compact norms are actual Hilbert spaces. On the end a different constant in the decomposition would make a nonzero constant exponentially weighted $L^2$, which is impossible, so $c$ is unique. On the finite core and fixed overlap, all coordinate and frame changes and their inverse derivatives are bounded. Derivative-graph closedness proves completeness of the weak Sobolev pieces; equality on overlaps and the original closed $H^1$ boundary condition are closed constraints. A Cauchy sequence of constants and pieces therefore has a unique compatible limit, proving Hilbert completeness. The stated norms include $|c|$ and are equivalent under moving the finite cutoff, since changes affect only a fixed compact overlap. Moreover $X_{0,\delta}$ continuously embeds in the compact $H^1$ domain: Dirichlet derivative energy in two real dimensions is conformally invariant on the end, and the exponentially weighted remainder controls its unweighted cylindrical derivatives. Its disk $L^2$ norm is controlled as well by $dA_w=\pi^2|w|^2ds\,dt$; the constant part is disk integrable. The forcing transformation gives $\int|f_w|^2dA_w=\int|f_{\rm cyl}|^2ds\,dt$ in the fixed local coefficient norm, so $Y_{q,\delta}$ continuously embeds in compact $L^2$. Other smooth metrics only change equivalence constants. These facts also justify the closed constraints used in the completeness argument. [given, construct, algebra]

1.2 There is an actual inverse for the end equation on the whole cylinder with the one-sided weight $e^{\delta s}$. For real-boundary $v$ on both edges put $a=e^{\delta s+i\delta t}v$. Its upper boundary is $e^{i\delta}\mathbb R^m$ and its lower boundary is $\mathbb R^m$, and direct differentiation gives $$D_0a=e^{\delta s+i\delta t}D_0v.$$ Thus the whole-strip inverse [F2] with angle $\theta=\delta$ gives a specified inverse $G_\delta$ to $D_0$ in this weighted real/real problem. The same identity and the actual higher theorem in [F2] give $\|e^{\delta s}G_\delta g\|_{H^{q+1}}\le C_{q,\delta}\|e^{\delta s}g\|_{H^q}$ for every integer $q\ge0$. Multiplication by $e^{i\delta t}$ and its inverse has bounded derivatives of all orders on $[0,1]$, so these are exactly the asserted norms. The weight here is one-sided on the whole line; no two-sided zero-mode inverse has been presumed. [F2, construct, algebra]

2.1 Take any actual compact $H^1$ solution $\xi$ with forcing in $Y_{q,\delta}$. On the cylindrical end cut its forcing off to zero below a fixed large $S$, with a smooth cutoff equal to one above $S+1$, and extend that cutoff forcing by zero to the whole strip. This is a legitimate weighted $H^q$ forcing because its zero extension is flat at the cutoff endpoint; its norm is bounded by the prescribed end norm. Apply $G_\delta$ to obtain a real-boundary particular solution $v$ with the estimate of step 1.2. On $s>S+1$ the difference $h=\xi_{\rm cyl}-v$ solves $D_0h=0$. It has the actual real boundary condition, and in the disk coordinate it belongs to $H^1$ on the punctured halfdisk, including finite derivative energy and finite disk $L^2$ norm, by step 1.1. The particular solution's disk-coordinate $L^2$ and derivative integrals on that punctured halfdisk are bounded by its weighted end norm; extension across the point is not being assumed at this stage. Thus the $H^1$ norm of $h$ on this halfdisk is bounded by the original $\|\xi\|_{H^1}$ and $\|f\|_{Y_{q,\delta}}$. [F1, step 1.1, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 We prove exactly what a homogeneous remainder can do at the puncture. Reflect $h$ across its real boundary by conjugation. Its real trace makes this a weak $H^1$ map on the full punctured disk: the matching values cancel seam terms in integration by parts. It solves the ordinary holomorphic equation distributionally there. Its distributional Laplacian is zero there because $D_0h=0$, so the actual Weyl lemma [F4] makes it smooth on the full punctured disk. For each circle Fourier coefficient the polar Cauchy–Riemann equation gives $a_n'(r)=na_n(r)/r$, hence $a_n(r)=b_nr^n$. Every negative $n$ is zero because its nonzero coefficient would give infinite $L^2$ norm near zero by radial Parseval. Fix one smaller circle of radius $R$. Its $L^2$ trace is bounded by the $H^1$ norm on a fixed annulus, using the radial fundamental theorem and Cauchy–Schwarz. Thus $|b_n|R^n\le C\|h\|_{H^1}$ for $n\ge0$. Fourier completeness [F3] identifies the resulting convergent power series on a still smaller disk. Reflection makes all $b_n$ real. In particular $h$ fills smoothly with real constant $c=b_0$, and $$|c|+\|e^{\delta s}(h-c)\|_{H^j(s>S')}\le C_{j,\delta}\|h\|_{H^1}$$ for every fixed $j$ and $0<\delta<\pi$. To check the last bound, substitute $w=-e^{-\pi(s+it)}$ in the positive-power series. Its differentiated terms have factors at most $C_jn^j e^{-\pi ns}R^{-n}$; for $S'$ fixed sufficiently large their sums and weighted squared integrals converge geometrically, beginning with $n=1$. This proves the needed actual filling and end estimate without a compact-target removability assumption for this linear section. [F1, F3, F4, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Combining the particular solution and homogeneous remainder proves the weighted regularity estimate in the statement on a sufficiently far end. On the remaining fixed compact source part the forcing is $H^q$; the actual local weak interior and boundary estimates of [F1] give $H^{q+1}$ with a bound by that forcing and the original $H^1$ norm. Moving the fixed cutoff between these regions changes only equivalent norms by step 1.1. Thus every compact solution with forcing in $Y_{q,\delta}$ lies in exactly $X_{q,\delta}$, with the asserted bound. The constant is the unique one from step 3.1. For $q\ge1$, $H^{q+1}$ on unit cylinder windows has the local supremum bound of [F4], and its weighted remainder tends uniformly to zero on the end because its tail Sobolev norms tend to zero. This gives the actual continuous node value $c$. Homogeneous compact solutions are smooth by the local estimates of [F1] and step 3.1, so that assertion holds at every level for them. No pointwise continuity was needed at level zero for general inhomogeneous sections. [F1, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 The actual punctured operator is bounded: on the finite core it is first order with smooth coefficients, and on the end it is exactly $D_0$, which kills the constant part and maps weighted $H^{q+1}$ to weighted $H^q$ with the product-rule bound. Every punctured homogeneous solution is a compact $H^1$ solution by step 1.1. Conversely every compact homogeneous solution lies in the punctured domain by step 4.1, so the kernels agree. The same two implications for arbitrary forcing prove $$\operatorname{Ran}P_{q,\delta}=Y_{q,\delta}\cap\operatorname{Ran}P_{\rm compact}.$$ This is closed in $Y_{q,\delta}$ because its inclusion in compact $L^2$ is continuous and that compact range is closed by [F1]. The induced map $Y_{q,\delta}\to L^2/\operatorname{Ran}P_{\rm compact}$ is onto: it contains smooth forcing sections compactly supported away from the marked point and inside the disk, dense in $L^2$ by cutoff and convolution; its image is a linear subspace of a finite-dimensional space and hence closed. Its kernel is precisely the displayed punctured range. Therefore the actual cokernels agree, and the actual free-constant Fredholm index is $m+2k$ at every indicated level. [F1, step 1.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Let $Q$ be the actual bounded compact $H^1$ range inverse in [F1]. For $f\in Y_{q,\delta}\cap\operatorname{Ran}P_{\rm compact}$, its compact solution $Qf$ has $H^1$ norm bounded by $C\|f\|_{L^2}\le C'\|f\|_{Y_{q,\delta}}$. Step 4.1 gives a bounded map $Q:Y_{q,\delta}\cap\operatorname{Ran}P_{\rm compact}\to X_{q,\delta}$ with $PQf=f$. Thus the range inverse is an actual restriction of one specified operator, not an appeal to a different choice of complement at each weight. The asymptotic constant evaluation is bounded by the domain norm. It is onto: a fixed local smooth real cutoff section equal to an arbitrary real vector in the exact node frame near $p$, and zero outside that boundary chart, lies in every indicated domain, respects $F$ on the boundary, and has precisely that asymptotic constant. This also gives a bounded finite-dimensional right inverse to evaluation. [F1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 We verify the index after fixing the node, including closed range. Write $T=P_{q,\delta}$, $N=\ker T$, $e=\operatorname{ev}_p$, and let $r$ be the rank of $e|_N$. Use the actual range inverse $Q$ of step 6.1. Inside the already closed range of $T$, the range of $T|_{\ker e}$ is exactly the solutions of the continuous finite-dimensional condition $eQf\in eN$. It is therefore closed. The map $f\mapsto eQf\bmod eN$ from $\operatorname{Ran}T$ onto $\mathbb R^m/eN$ is surjective: for any $a$ choose the fixed evaluation lift $x$ from step 6.1; then $QTx-x\in N$ and $eQTx\equiv a\pmod{eN}$. Thus this restriction adds $m-r$ to the cokernel dimension, while its kernel is $N\cap\ker e$ of dimension $\dim N-r$. Its real index is consequently $(\dim N-r)-(\dim\operatorname{coker}T+m-r)=\operatorname{ind}T-m=2k$. Choose a finite-dimensional lift $L:eN\to N$ of $e|_N$, by taking a basis and one preimage for each basis vector. On this closed restricted range the formula $f\mapsto Qf-L(eQf)$ is a bounded actual right inverse with evaluation zero. All claims concern the exact completed/weak domains and actual coefficient equation; the proof did not assume node-fixed surjectivity or infer a nonlinear gluing result. Every choice was finite, countable, or an already specified inverse and Fourier sum under Countable Choice. [step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra] ∎
