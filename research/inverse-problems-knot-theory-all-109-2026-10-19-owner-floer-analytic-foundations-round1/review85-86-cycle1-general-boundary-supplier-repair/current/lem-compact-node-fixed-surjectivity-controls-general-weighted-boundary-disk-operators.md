## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $E$ be a smooth complex rank-$m$ bundle on the closed disk and let $F$ be a smooth totally real rank-$m$ boundary subbundle, NOT required to be oriented. Let $P$ be a smooth real-linear operator with exact Cauchy--Riemann symbol. Fix one boundary point $p$. In a holomorphic source half-disk coordinate $w$ centered there and a smooth complex bundle frame, require that $F=\mathbb R^m$ on the straight edge and $P=\partial_x+i\partial_y$ throughout that node neighborhood.

Let $X_0$ be the $H^1$ completion of smooth sections satisfying $F$, and suppose the ACTUAL bounded compact node-fixed operator
$$P_2:\{\xi\in H^3(E)\cap X_0:\xi(p)=0\}\longrightarrow H^2(E)$$
is Fredholm and onto, of real index $r$. The point trace here is the bounded $H^3$ trace. Necessarily $r\ge0$.

Remove $p$ and use $w=e^{-\pi(\sigma+i\tau)}$, up to a fixed harmless holomorphic normalization, as its half-cylinder coordinate, with $\sigma\to\infty$ at the node and $0\le\tau\le1$. Transform the operator and forcing by their ACTUAL nonzero Cauchy--Riemann symbol factor so the end operator is $D_0=\partial_\sigma+i\partial_\tau$. For $0<\delta<\pi$, use ordinary $H^1$ on a compact core and $e^{\delta\sigma}H^1$ on this end, with its actual real boundary completion and no free constant. Use the corresponding weighted $L^2$ target.

This actual weighted node-fixed operator is onto and Fredholm of index $r$, with a bounded right inverse. Its kernel is exactly the compact node-fixed $H^3$ kernel. For every integer $q\ge0$ it remains onto in the actual $H^{q+1}_\delta\cap X_\delta$ realization with target $H^q_\delta$, with the same kernel and index. No additional forcing trace or higher normal boundary condition is imposed. No global oriented disk-index theorem or product-torus hypothesis is needed.

If $P$ is an actual geometric linearization at a nonconstant autonomous disk, $r=2$, and the node chart is as above, its two node-fixing affine kernel sections span this common kernel. Two bounded compact-core phases can therefore be imposed to make the weighted node-fixed operator an isomorphism, and free real end constants admit bounded zero-phase homogeneous lifts.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual smooth bundle/operator, arbitrary totally real boundary bundle, exact node chart, and the compact node-fixed Fredholm surjectivity assumption.

[F1] The transverse whole-strip $D_0$ inverse with real lower and imaginary upper boundary is an actual bounded $L^2\to H^1$ inverse; its integer higher estimates have no extra forcing trace. The real-boundary strip supplier proves the integer-mode basis $e^{-i\pi n\tau}$ for two real edges, including its zero mode. [F2] The weak local real-boundary estimates are obtained by the actual $D_0$ energy identity, tangential difference quotients and recovery of the normal derivative by the equation. We repeat the needed localization below rather than invoking any global oriented disk theorem. The higher-strip supplier proves these local arguments under Countable Choice. [F3] Half-chart extension and the integer Sobolev estimates give bounded $H^3\to C^1$ on compact disk pieces; closed subspaces of these separable Hilbert Sobolev spaces are separable Hilbert under Countable Choice. ## Proof

1.1 The assumed compact node-fixed map has a bounded linear right inverse $Q$. Here is the Countable-Choice functional argument, so an unrestricted-choice inverse theorem is not imported. Its domain is a closed Hilbert subspace of $H^3$: its $H^1$ boundary condition is closed and its point trace is bounded by [F3]. Its finite kernel has a finite orthonormal basis, so orthogonal subtraction gives a closed separable complement $Z$, on which $P_2$ is a bounded linear bijection onto $H^2$. Baire from [F3], applied to $\bigcup_n\overline{P_2(B_Z(0,n))}=H^2$, shows that one of these closures has interior. Taking differences and rescaling shows $B(0,a)\subset\overline{P_2(B_Z(0,1))}$ for some $a>0$. Fix one countable dense set of $Z$. For any nonzero residual $y$, take the FIRST dense point $z$ with $\|z\|<2\|y\|/a$ and $\|P_2z-y\|<\|y\|/2$. Such a point exists by that closure inclusion and density. Repeat this deterministic rule on the residuals. The successive source norms sum to at most $4\|y\|/a$ and the target residual tends to zero. Completeness gives a preimage of this bound. On $Z$ it is unique; hence the resulting inverse is linear and bounded. This constructs $Q$ without dependent arbitrary choices. [F3, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 First remove a rough weighted forcing near the puncture. In the exact end expand it in the real basis $\phi_n(\tau)=e^{-i\pi n\tau}$. Cut its forcing to zero near a fixed initial section $\sigma=S$, equal to the original forcing for $\sigma\ge S+2$. Solve each coefficient $a_n'+\pi n a_n=f_n$ on $[S,\infty)$ by $$a_n(\sigma)=\int_S^\sigma e^{-\pi n(\sigma-t)}f_n(t)\,dt\quad(n>0),\qquad a_n(\sigma)=-\int_\sigma^\infty e^{\pi n(t-\sigma)}f_n(t)\,dt\quad(n<0),$$ and $a_0(\sigma)=-\int_\sigma^\infty f_0(t)dt$. Multiplication by $e^{\delta\sigma}$ turns their kernels into one-sided exponential kernels of masses $(\pi n-\delta)^{-1}$, $(|\pi n|+\delta)^{-1}$, and $\delta^{-1}$. Cauchy--Schwarz followed by integration in the remaining variable gives their $L^2$ bounds; the coefficient equations and multiplication by $|\pi n|$ give the $\sigma$ and $\tau$ derivative bounds. Parseval therefore gives a bounded weighted $H^1$ solution with zero node constant, with real physical boundary completion, for arbitrary weighted $L^2$ data and no forcing-edge constraint. Multiply it by a smooth cutoff zero before $S$ and one on the support of the cut forcing. Its residual forcing is supported on one fixed compact core, because the derivative of that cutoff has fixed support and the original forcing has been solved on the far end. Its compact-core $L^2$ norm and the weighted norm of this particular section are bounded by the original forcing norm. All source/target symbol factors on that fixed core are bounded invertible smooth factors. It remains to solve an arbitrary $L^2$ forcing supported on a fixed compact set away from $p$. [F1, construct, algebra]

1.3 Construct local smoothing parametrices for that remaining compact-core problem. On a boundary interval choose a real frame of $F$; its columns are complex independent by total reality. In an arbitrary local complex frame extend their coefficient matrix into a collar, keeping it invertible after shrinking. This is a smooth complex gauge making the boundary condition real. A holomorphic disk-source coordinate straightens its circular boundary, and division by its nonzero symbol factor gives $D_0+B$ with smooth real-linear zeroth-order $B$. Interior patches need no boundary gauge. The given compact support is covered by finitely many such patches away from $p$, with finite partitions $\varphi_a$ and cutoffs $\chi_a=1$ near their supports. Identify each coordinate patch with a patch inside the constant transverse whole strip of [F1], placing a physical boundary patch at its real lower edge and all artificial edges outside the cutoff support. Extend $\varphi_a f$ by zero as an $L^2$ forcing, apply that actual inverse, and multiply by $\chi_a$. Transfer the resulting sections back with the gauges and sum. This defines $R_0:L^2\to H^1$, with compact support away from $p$ and the correct physical boundary completion, and $$PR_0f=f+K_0f.$$ Every error is a smooth coefficient or cutoff derivative times the local inverse output. Thus $K_0:H^q\to H^{q+1}$ for $q=0,1,2$, with fixed compact support away from $p$, by [F1]'s higher estimates. No global boundary frame, orientation or disk-index formula was used. [F1, F2, construct, algebra]

2.1 To avoid a support assumption hidden in iterating this construction, use finitely many NESTED compact cores. Cover the support of $K_0f$ by a slightly larger compact core still disjoint from $p$, and perform the same construction there to obtain $PR_1g=g+K_1g$. Repeat once on the resulting support to get $PR_2h=h+K_2h$. All three covers and supports are fixed independently of $f$, and only three enlargements are needed. Then $$P(R_0f-R_1K_0f+R_2K_1K_0f)=f+K_2K_1K_0f.$$ The last error belongs to $H^3$, hence to $H^2$, with norm bounded by $C\|f\|_2$, and is supported away from $p$. Correct it by subtracting $Q(K_2K_1K_0f)$ from step1.1. This gives an actual solution of the compact-core forcing. The three parametrix terms are supported away from the node. The correction is in compact $H^3$, has node value zero, and hence belongs to weighted end $H^1_\delta$ for $\delta<\pi$: in the exact node coordinate [F3] gives $|h(w)|\le C\|h\|_{H^3}|w|$ and $|\nabla_wh|\le C\|h\|_{H^3}$; cylindrical function and first derivatives are bounded by $Ce^{-\pi\sigma}\|h\|_{H^3}$. Its weighted norm is bounded accordingly. Combining with the end particular solution of step1.2 constructs a bounded actual weighted right inverse on ALL forcings. The node constraint was used only in the bounded $H^3$ correction, never as an ordinary $H^1$ point trace. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, construct, algebra]

3.1 The kernels agree. A weighted homogeneous end section has coefficient equations $a_n'+\pi n a_n=0$. Weighted square integrability excludes every $n\le0$. A trace on a finite end section is in the half-order space by the elementary one-dimensional endpoint estimate and Parseval in [F1]. The remaining positive series is $\sum_{n>0}c_nw^n$. Moving one unit toward the node gives exponential coefficient decay, so its series and every derivative converge smoothly down to $w=0$, with value zero. On the remaining compact core a weak homogeneous $H^1$ section is smooth up to the physical boundary by the following local orientation-free bootstrap. In the gauge of step1.3 the compactly supported real-boundary identity $\|D_0h\|_2^2=\|h_x\|_2^2+\|h_y\|_2^2$ follows by integration by parts; its boundary mixed term vanishes because $h,h_x$ are real there. It extends to the actual $H^1$ boundary completion. Tangential difference quotients preserve that real condition; their equation for $D_0+B$ and this estimate bound them in $H^1$ on smaller patches. The bounded-test Hilbert argument in [F2] gives a weak tangential derivative in $H^1$, and $h_y=-i(-Bh-h_x)$ recovers the normal one. Repeating this argument with smooth coefficient derivatives gives every integer derivative on nested patches. Thus the homogeneous section is in compact $H^3\cap X_0$ with node zero. Conversely any compact node-fixed $H^3$ kernel section lies in the weighted domain by the estimate in step2.1 and solves its transformed equation. Hence this is exactly the common kernel of dimension $r$. The weighted operator is onto by step2.1, so it is Fredholm of index $r$, without invoking an unoriented global compact Fredholm theorem. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.3, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 For a higher weighted forcing, bootstrap the actual global weak solution from step2.1. On finitely many compact patches use the same tangential argument, now differentiating the forcing through its prescribed order, to get the estimate by its $H^q$ norm and the original $H^1$ norm. On the exact end conjugation by $e^{\delta\sigma}$ replaces $D_0$ by $D_0-\delta$. Its local real-boundary estimate and derivative recovery are the same, with this bounded zeroth-order term. Apply them on unit windows with slightly larger windows of uniformly bounded overlap, and square and sum. This gives $$\|h\|_{H^{q+1}_\delta}\le C_q(\|P_\delta h\|_{H^q_\delta}+\|h\|_{H^1_\delta}).$$ The kernel remains that in step3.1 and the right inverse restricts boundedly to these higher targets, giving onto Fredholm operators of index $r$. At no point is a normal derivative trace or a boundary trace of the forcing imposed; only tangential derivatives preserve the original real boundary condition. [F2, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Finally consider an actual autonomous nonconstant disk with $r=2$. Its two infinitesimal node-fixing affine reparametrizations are smooth compact node-fixed kernel sections, hence weighted sections by step3.1. They are independent: at any interior point where $dv\ne0$, holomorphicity makes its real derivative injective on the two-dimensional source tangent plane. A relation $a v_x+b(\xi v_x+\eta v_y)=0$ there forces $b\eta=0$, hence $b=0$ and $a=0$, since $\eta>0$. They therefore span the common two-dimensional kernel. Choose two compact-core test phases with invertible integrated Gram matrix on these fields. Correct the bounded inverse of step2.1 by these kernel sections to impose zero phases; the resulting zero-phase operator is an isomorphism. To free the real end constant choose cutoff lifts $k_c$ equal to $c$ on the end, first correct their phases by compact-core sections, and put $L c=k_c-Q^0P_\delta k_c$, with $Q^0$ the zero-phase node-fixed inverse. Their forcings are compactly supported and bounded, so these are bounded zero-phase homogeneous lifts of every end constant. This proves the last assertion with no product-torus or orientation premise and closes the actual compact-to-weighted comparison from its specified high-order surjectivity input. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra] ∎
