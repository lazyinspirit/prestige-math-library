## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $B=[0,1]^d$, where $d$ is finite, and let $j_{b,s}$ be a smooth family of orientation-compatible complex structures on a compact oriented surface $\Sigma$, for $(b,s)\in B\times[0,1]$. Smoothness at the boundary means local smooth extendibility. For every $m\ge1$, the relative symmetric products $X_{b,s}=\operatorname{Sym}^m(\Sigma,j_{b,s})$ admit a smooth coefficient atlas over $B\times[0,1]$, with smooth vertical complex structure. There are diffeomorphisms
$$\Phi_{b,s}:X_{b,0}\longrightarrow X_{b,s},\qquad \Phi_{b,0}=I,$$
smooth jointly in all variables in that relative atlas, and genuine smooth permutation-equivariant ordered lifts on $\Sigma^m$. Both the maps and their inverses have bounded derivatives of each fixed order on the compact parameter-product spaces. They preserve every multiplicity stratum and every chosen finite point-incidence divisor, and their ordered lifts preserve each pairwise diagonal and each coordinate incidence set.

As in the one-parameter construction, the maps may be the raw identity near any fixed compact off-diagonal subset avoiding the prescribed point divisors, for example the union of two product tori. If the structures are independent of $s$ on a fixed spatial region, given smooth $b$-dependent holomorphic charts there may be retained unchanged in $s$, and the transport may be the raw identity on a smaller such region. Prescribed constant charts may likewise be retained unchanged wherever the whole family is already fixed. All simultaneous prescriptions must agree on their overlaps. If the entire surface family is independent of $s$ on a parameter-end collar, the horizontal field can be the raw constant-point field on that collar, so the transport is constant in $s$ there. This does not assert that its value on the terminal collar is the identity.

These are actual smooth finite-parameter transports, including parameter squares used for homotopies. No extension of an arbitrarily prescribed trivialization on the entire boundary of a parameter cube is asserted.

## Proof

1.1 First reconstruct the needed several-parameter holomorphic coordinates, without substituting separate one-parameter choices. Write $a=(b,s)$ and work near one $(a_0,x_0)$. In a fixed positive real surface chart use the smooth basis $(e,j_a(x_0)e)$ to normalize $j_a(x_0)$ to $i$. The coefficient $\mu_a$ of its Beltrami equation then has $\mu_a(0)=0$. Rescale by a radius $r$ and multiply by the same spatial cutoff as [F1], obtaining $\nu_a$ supported in a fixed disk. On a small closed parameter box every fixed mixed parameter/spatial derivative of $\nu_a$ is $O(r)$: Taylor's formula in the spatial variable applies also to each parameter derivative, whose value at the center vanishes. In particular ONE radius makes $\sup_a\|\nu_a\|_\infty\|S\|_{4\to4}<1/2$ and the spatial $W^{1,4}$ bound small. Define the specified norm-convergent solution $h_a=(I-M_{\nu_a}S)^{-1}\nu_a$ and $f_a(z)=z+Th_a(z)$. The actual operator identities and spatial weak-derivative estimates in [F1] apply uniformly on this box. For any parameter multiindex $\gamma$, differentiation of the equation gives
$$ (I-M_{\nu_a}S)\partial_a^\gamma h_a=\partial_a^\gamma\nu_a+\sum_{0<\beta\le\gamma}{\gamma\choose\beta}(\partial_a^\beta\nu_a)S\partial_a^{\gamma-\beta}h_a.$$
Prove this in increasing $|\gamma|$ by difference quotients and the resolvent identity. All right-hand terms contain strictly lower parameter derivatives of $h_a$; Taylor remainders in the smooth coefficient tend to zero in operator norm, and the inverse norm is at most two. Thus each derivative exists and is continuous in $L^4$. Applying the spatial differentiated equations to these same identities gives all mixed spatial/parameter derivatives in $W^{k,4}$. The Morrey argument of [F1] makes $f_a$ jointly smooth and $\|df_a-I\|_\infty<1/2$. Segment integration and the actual Euclidean inverse theorem give injective holomorphic coordinate charts with smooth joint inverses. This proves the several-parameter local result with no branch selection. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Retain a finite number of these charts on the compact parameter-surface space. On an already fixed spatial region retain the given $b$-dependent, $s$-independent charts there instead: their fiber complex-linear derivatives show that their overlaps with every newly constructed chart are holomorphic, and both charts are jointly smooth. When a prescribed point is present subtract its smooth coordinate value, so its coordinate is zero. To form relative symmetric-product charts, take disjoint support disks and the elementary coefficients of their root polynomials. The proof of [F2] that this is a homeomorphism uses only the finite root bound and convergent subsequences in a compact finite product; it is independent of the number of base parameters. Its transition formula is
$$p_l(a,c)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\partial D}g_a(z)^l\frac{P_c'(z)}{P_c(z)}\,dz.$$
The denominator is uniformly separated from zero on each small coefficient neighborhood and contour. Every mixed $a,c$ derivative may therefore be taken under this fixed finite contour integral. Newton identities recover the new coefficients, and the same contour extraction handles changes of support-cluster decomposition. Thus the transitions and inverses are smooth in all base variables and holomorphic vertically, including at every higher collision. The projection is locally $(a,c)\mapsto a$ and is a proper submersion, since its underlying space is the compact finite quotient. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Construct transport in the specified $s$ direction, keeping $b$ fixed. In each relative coefficient chart use the vector with $ds=1$, $db=0$ and all coefficients constant. It is tangent to every multiplicity stratum. Centering a support coordinate at each protected point makes the corresponding incidence condition the zero constant coefficient, so the field is tangent to every protected divisor. Near the fixed off-diagonal compact subset, instead prescribe the raw constant-point field. It is well-defined smoothly there because the roots have distinct labels in disjoint small disks. On a fixed spatial region with $s$-independent charts the coefficient-constant field is also the raw field, even when roots repeat. On a parameter-end collar where the surface structure is entirely independent of $s$, use the raw field everywhere on that collar in its $s$-independent relative atlas. Choose nested smaller prescription neighborhoods, explicit chart bumps and a finite subordinate partition on the compact total space, as in [F2]. Combine the local fields with weights summing to one, taking weight one for prescribed fields on the smaller neighborhoods. Their differences are vertical, and every nonzero term is tangent to the specified stratum/divisor there, so the resulting smooth field $V$ has all the asserted tangencies and prescriptions. No arbitrary-index partition is used. [F2, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Lift this actual field before integrating it. On an ordered support chart, the coefficient-constant lift holds each individual $z_{a,\nu}(x_i)$ constant while $b$ is fixed and $s$ increases. Its root velocity is $-(d_xz_{a,\nu})^{-1}\partial_sz_{a,\nu}$, smooth jointly in $(a,x_i)$. Equal roots have equal velocities. The ordered quotient map is smooth, because its coefficient components are elementary symmetric polynomials in the smooth root coordinates. Pull back the same finite partition weights by that map and sum the root fields. This is a genuinely smooth equivariant field, related to $V$ by the quotient differential even at collisions. A root at a protected point has velocity zero, and equal roots have equal velocities; hence every coordinate incidence set and pairwise diagonal is invariant. The raw prescriptions lift to the raw field. These assertions use no inverse derivative of the ramified quotient. [F2, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Integrate both fields with $b$ constant, using [F3]. The base $s$ coordinate increases at speed one. Compactness gives continuation to every $s\in[0,1]$ and the actual differentiated ODE gives smooth joint dependence on $(b,s)$ and all initial data. Reverse-time flow gives the inverse with the same parameter smoothness. Uniqueness identifies the quotient of the ordered flow with the coefficient-space flow, since their fields are related. The invariant hypersurfaces are preserved by restricted-flow uniqueness. For multiplicity types use their actual locally closed smooth root-position parametrizations from [F2]; the set of times in each type is open by two-sided uniqueness. The finitely many disjoint types partition a connected time interval, so only one occurs. Thus the flows have all asserted invariances. Their fixed-order derivatives, and those of the inverse flows, are bounded on the compact parameter-product spaces by continuity. This also proves smoothness up to all faces of the cube, using the local smooth extensions when solving the ODE. [F2, F3, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 A prescribed raw field fixes the underlying tuple there, giving the claimed identity maps on each fixed spatial prescription. On a parameter-end collar where the entire horizontal field is raw, continuing the flow leaves its tuple unchanged as $s$ varies within that collar; the tuple arriving at the terminal collar can already have moved earlier, explaining precisely the constant-in-$s$ conclusion. The construction is jointly smooth in all remaining parameters and does not choose transports independently on different faces. It therefore supplies actual square/cube families of transports, but makes no assertion about filling an unrelated boundary trivialization. All limits and inverses were the specified Neumann/flow constructions in the Countable Choice suppliers. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
