## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. For $a=0,1$ let
$$P_a=D_{a,0}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^r\operatorname{int}D_{a,i}\subset\mathbb R^2,$$
where every $D_{a,i}$ is the closed bounded region of a smooth embedded circle, the inner disks are pairwise disjoint, and each lies in the interior of the outer disk $D_{a,0}$. The number $r$ is finite and may be zero. Give $P_a$ the plane orientation. After any chosen labeling of the inner boundary components, let $b_i:\partial D_{0,i}\to\partial D_{1,i}$ be smooth diffeomorphisms all preserving their induced boundary orientations, or all reversing them, $0\le i\le r$.

There is a smooth diffeomorphism $B:P_0\to P_1$ extending all $b_i$, preserving orientation in the first case and reversing it in the second. Moreover, for any specified inward smooth collars $c_{a,i}$ of these boundary components into $P_a$, it may satisfy
$$B(c_{0,i}(p,t))=c_{1,i}(b_i(p),t)$$
for every $i$ and all sufficiently small $t\ge0$, with one common smaller collar width. No parametrization of either hole domain or general classification of smooth surfaces is an additional hypothesis or an imported conclusion.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual finite planar disk regions, labeling, uniformly oriented boundary maps, and any prescribed inward collars. A collar is an embedding on a common positive interval after shrinking, available here as part of the prescribed data.

[F1] [[lem-smooth-jordan-disk-boundary-maps-extend-to-diffeomorphisms]] proves under Countable Choice that each individual smooth Jordan region is a smooth standard disk and that its orientation-preserving boundary map extends, with any prescribed collar germs.

[F2] [[lem-isotopy-extension-for-a-compact-source-with-boundary]] extends a smooth isotopy of full-dimensional compact disk or annulus embeddings into the boundaryless plane, constant near its time endpoints, to an ambient isotopy supported in any specified neighborhood of its compact track. In particular the support may miss any finite collection of other compact sets disjoint from that track.

[F3] [[thm-euclidean-inverse-function-theorem]] gives a local smooth inverse at an invertible derivative. Compactness upgrades local inverses along one compact annulus or disk to a common sufficiently small neighborhood when used below; a collision sequence at widths tending to zero would contradict the inverse at its limiting point.

## Proof

1.1 Treat the orientation-preserving case first. Extend the outer boundary map to an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism $B_0:D_{0,0}\to D_{1,0}$ using [F1], initially with its prescribed outer collar if supplied. The images $E_i=B_0(D_{0,i})$ are disjoint smooth disks strictly inside $D_{1,0}$. We will construct an ambient plane diffeomorphism supported in $\operatorname{int}D_{1,0}$ carrying all $E_i$ to their labeled target disks. We first justify the local disk moves used for this. A smooth disk parametrization $\phi:\overline{\mathbb D}\to E$, supplied by [F1], can be chosen orientation-preserving; write $p=\phi(0)$. The embeddings $z\mapsto\phi(az)$, with $a$ decreasing from one to a positive $\epsilon$, shrink it within itself. For sufficiently small $\epsilon$, the further embeddings $$z\longmapsto p+\frac{\phi(t\epsilon z)-p}{t}\quad(0<t\le1)$$ extend smoothly at $t=0$ to $p+\epsilon D\phi(0)z$, by the integral formula for the first difference of a smooth map. Their images stay in a small neighborhood of $p$, inside $E$, after decreasing $\epsilon$. They are embeddings for $t>0$ and at zero by the invertible derivative; the uniform small-neighborhood assertion follows from [F3]. Connect the positive matrix $D\phi(0)$ to a positive scalar multiple of the identity by polar decomposition: interpolate its positive definite factor linearly and its rotation angle smoothly. After multiplication by a sufficiently small $\epsilon$ these ellipse embeddings also stay inside $E$. Reparametrize the finitely many time intervals smoothly to be constant at their endpoints. [F2] extends these disk embedding isotopies with support in any small neighborhood of $E$ and then of $p$. Thus any one disk can be made a small round disk about a specified interior point while all disjoint compact obstacles and the outer boundary remain fixed. Reversing this procedure expands a small round disk at an interior point to any prescribed smooth disk, using support near that disk. [F1, F2, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 For completeness the disk transports can be performed simultaneously without a hidden planar classification. Choose $r$ distinct parking points in an outer collar inside $D_{1,0}$ disjoint from all the target disks and from the $E_i$. Such a collar band exists by their compact separation from the outer boundary and its disk parametrization in [F1]; choose the band away from the still smaller fixed outer collar. Make all $E_i$ round and small by step1.1 with disjoint supports. Move them one at a time to small round disks at the parking points, keeping the others fixed. The needed paths exist in the outer disk minus finitely many disjoint closed disk obstacles: an initial path exists by a disk parametrization; approximate it by finitely many coordinate segments inside the open outer disk. Each obstacle has a disjoint exterior collar. If a path meets that obstacle, replace the segment between its first and last encounter with a slightly displaced path in its exterior collar, with short collar connectors. Taking the encounters on a sufficiently small exterior collar rather than on the obstacle itself keeps this new path strictly outside the obstacle. These collars can miss every other obstacle, so finitely many replacements remove all obstacles without creating any earlier one. The resulting path has a finite piecewise smooth representative, obtained in its open complement by a finite coordinate approximation; round its corners in disjoint small disks. Its compact image has positive separation from every obstacle and the outer boundary. Shrink the moving round disk further if necessary and translate it along this path, with a smooth time parameter constant near the ends. The embeddings of that small closed disk extend by [F2], fixing all obstacles. At each stage the unused parking points can be avoided too, by finitely many small path detours; keep the moving radius smaller than their separations. After this parking phase, move the first parked disk to a chosen interior point of $D_{1,1}$ along such a path avoiding the other parked disks and already assigned target disks, then expand it to $D_{1,1}$ by the reverse procedure of step1.1. That expansion misses all other current disks, since all remaining parking points lie outside every target disk and their radii may be made smaller in advance. Repeat through the finite target list. Supports stay away from a common smaller outer collar. This constructs the desired ambient diffeomorphism $G$ with $G(E_i)=D_{1,i}$ for every $i$, with all labels retained. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 The preliminary map $GB_0$ therefore sends each boundary circle to its desired target but might have the wrong parametrization. Its error on the $i$th target circle is an orientation-preserving self-diffeomorphism $d_i=b_i\circ(GB_0|_{\partial D_{0,i}})^{-1}$. Choose a smooth angular parametrization $\theta$ of that circle and a two-sided annular coordinate $(\theta,t)$ about it, with distinct annuli disjoint and away from the outer boundary. Such annuli follow by taking a unit normal in the plane and applying [F3] on the compact circle. The map $d_i$ has an increasing smooth lift $g_i$ with $g_i(\theta+2\pi)=g_i(\theta)+2\pi$: lift along successive short arcs using its local inverse, with its positive derivative and degree one guaranteeing the displayed period. On this annulus set $$\Theta_i(\theta,t)=\bigl(\theta+\chi(t)(g_i(\theta)-\theta),t\bigr),$$ where $0\le\chi\le1$, $\chi=1$ near zero and $\chi=0$ near both ends. The angular derivative is $1+\chi(t)(g_i'(\theta)-1)>0$, so it is a diffeomorphism of the annulus, identity near its ends, and extends by the identity to the plane. It preserves both sides of the circle, matches $d_i$ there, and fixes all other boundary circles. Compose these finitely many corrections with $GB_0$. The result is an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism $B$ extending all boundary maps. If collars were not prescribed, restriction to $P_0$ already proves the first assertion. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 We impose the prescribed collar germs without assuming they agree with the auxiliary annuli. On the $i$th target boundary compare the actual collar $B c_{0,i}$ with the desired collar $(p,t)\mapsto c_{1,i}(b_i(p),t)$. Their relative map fixes that boundary pointwise. In a two-sided normal coordinate it has form $(\theta,t)\mapsto(S(\theta,t),T(\theta,t))$, with $S(\theta,0)=\theta$, $T(\theta,0)=0$, and $T_t(\theta,0)>0$ on the side pointing into $P_1$. The difference $S-\theta$ has a smooth periodic lift after shrinking. Extend these smooth expressions slightly across $t=0$: boundary smoothness supplies local extensions, and finitely many smooth angular cutoffs combine them while retaining their given values for $t\ge0$. Interpolate to the identity by $$e_u(\theta,t)=\bigl(\theta+u(S(\theta,t)-\theta),(1-u)t+uT(\theta,t)\bigr),\qquad0\le u\le1.$$ At $t=0$ its derivative is triangular with positive diagonal $1,(1-u)+uT_t(\theta,0)$. On one common sufficiently thin closed annulus every $e_u$ is an embedding: local invertibility follows uniformly by compactness and [F3]; if injectivity failed for widths tending to zero, the two limiting angular parameters would coincide since the map is the identity on the central circle, contradicting that local inverse. Smooth the time parameter to be constant near the ends. Apply [F2] to this annulus isotopy, with support in an arbitrarily small target boundary neighborhood. The ambient isotopy fixes the central circle, since its prescribed embeddings do, and hence preserves its disk and exterior sides. Its terminal map agrees with the desired relative map on a smaller inward collar. Use disjoint supports for the finitely many boundary circles, including the outer circle, and compose these corrections with $B$. They retain every boundary value and impose exactly all the prescribed collar formulas. [F2, F3, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 If the boundary maps all reverse induced orientations, first reflect the source plane in one line. This gives an orientation-reversing diffeomorphism from $P_0$ to its reflected planar hole domain and transports every prescribed source collar. Composing each $b_i$ with the inverse reflection now preserves boundary orientations. Steps1.1–4.1 give the required orientation-preserving extension from that reflected domain to $P_1$; composing with the source reflection gives the required orientation-reversing $B$. The case $r=0$ is already the disk case [F1], or the same proof with no hole moves. All moving disks, obstacles, annuli, coordinate patches and compositions are finite; the only stated choice assumption is that of [F1]–[F2]. This proves the collar-prescribed extension without importing general surface classification or the arbitrary-AC Jordan–Schoenflies theorem. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct] ∎
