## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $m=h+k-1>0$ disjoint attaching circles on a closed genus-$h$ surface bound a complete balanced compression system cutting a handlebody into $k\ge2$ balls. Use the actual broad proper disk witnesses, with their same-boundary neat replacements and finite planar cut data as supplied by the balanced complex-model construction. Fix a product node with one point on each circle, or finitely many such nodes. Let $A_i$ be any cut component and let its unit domain be one on that component and zero on the others. Fix a positive surface complex structure $j$ with actual real-axis holomorphic charts for the attaching circles at these node coordinates, and finite protected point divisors off the circles.

For every sufficiently small autonomous nearly symmetric perturbation of $\operatorname{Sym}^m(j)$ which fixes its full boundary value and exact node charts, whenever its actual unit-domain node-fixed operators are regular, there are finitely many positive-affine unit-disk orbits and their count modulo two is ONE, separately for every $A_i$ and every chosen node. A common small neighborhood works for the finite list. This includes components with arbitrarily many internal paired circles.

For each one of those component/node problems there is moreover an auxiliary integrable structure on the ACTUAL surface, with the same labelled attaching circles and node tuple, for which the COMPLETE standard unit-disk moduli consists of exactly one affine orbit, with all internal circles active, regular marked banks, and onto node-fixed index-two operator. Its two-dimensional scalar kernel is the actual affine kernel. This model is constructed by a finite sequence of coupled target plumbings, with exhaustive uniqueness at each step, not by an assumed destabilization invariant.

The comparison to $j$ is only the actual proper-unit-disk parameter cobordism supplied below. No Heegaard Floer invariance, handleslide map, absolute grading or general polygon count is an input or conclusion.

## Proof

1.1 Work first with one component $A$ and one node tuple. Let $e$ count its external boundary copies and $\ell$ its internal pairs. The dual graph of the complete disk cut has the cut balls as vertices and disk labels as edges. It is connected: gluing the cut pieces recovers the connected handlebody. The component $A$ corresponds to one vertex. A loop edge there is exactly an internal pair; a nonloop incident edge is exactly an external copy. Since there is more than one vertex, connectedness gives $e\ge1$. Remove its $\ell$ loop edges from this finite graph, leaving every other labelled edge and its actual ball-face gluing. The graph stays connected, so gluing those standard balls and their remaining one-handles gives an old balanced handlebody with genus $h-\ell$, $m-\ell$ disks and the SAME $k$ cut balls. The distinguished component now has $e$ external faces and no internal pair; all its other labelled circles lie outside its closure. Use [F1]'s actual round construction there and put each active node at its chosen point on the corresponding external circle. Select the inactive nodes on their other circles. [F2] then gives the complete one-orbit standard unit-disk moduli. The two affine sections exhaust its actual kernel, and [F2]'s kernel correspondence gives the VERIFIED scalar pole space $\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{1,p\}$. External marked banks are regular by the proper cover's nonzero boundary derivative. This establishes the induction base from actual harmonic and period proofs. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Reinsert the removed loop edges one at a time, by the target torus plumbing in [F3]. At each step the old COMPLETE moduli is a finite single orbit with all its internal labels active, marked banks regular and verified two-dimensional scalar kernel, by the preceding step. Choose the new root point $q$ in its open cut region so its physical projection value is not any branch value. The prohibited set is finite: a proper finite-degree map on the compact bordered surface has finitely many branch points and finitely many preimages of their values. Its complement in that open region is nonempty. Normalize the old projection at $q$ to $i$, choose a flat torus with its new labelled straight circle and the prescribed new node, and choose a phase avoiding the two excluded real phase directions for its nonzero derivative. The local theorem gives the actual coupled solution. The exhaustion theorem gives the COMPLETE bijection, so the new standard moduli again has exactly one orbit; its actual node-fixed operator is onto with kernel precisely the affine directions, every internal label is active and all marks are regular. The moving-slit kernel identification then exports the required two-dimensional scalar kernel to the NEXT step. Choose the new neck sufficiently small after the preceding finite constants have been fixed. The induction is finite, so no uniform inverse bound as all neck parameters simultaneously vanish is assumed. This proves the complete one-orbit model after all $\ell$ insertions, without assuming a component response or new-target exhaustion. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Identify this model with the ACTUAL labelled surface and its circles; do not appeal to an unspecified surface classification. Cut every attaching circle of the constructed model. The torus insertion at the distinguished component adds exactly two new boundary faces there: cutting the straight torus circle gives an annulus, removing the old and torus plumbing disks and joining their rims keeps that cut component planar and adds those two faces. No other cut component changes. Thus the final finite cut pieces have exactly the original labelled planar boundary lists and paired seams from [F1]. Choose parametrizations of each matched rim carrying its node to the required original node. For each pair choose the first rim map arbitrarily orientation-preserving and define its partner by composing the two actual orientation-reversing seam identifications. In signed collars prescribe these maps with the same normal parameter on the plus and minus sides. Their jets match across the paired seam. [F1]'s prescribed-collar extension gives a diffeomorphism on each finite planar cut piece; the compatible collar formulas make them descend to ONE orientation-positive smooth diffeomorphism of the glued surfaces, with smooth inverse, preserving every labelled attaching circle, component and node. This is the same finite collar argument as [F1], now for the explicitly constructed torus pieces. The finite cut triangulations can be carried through these maps; alternatively refine the existing finite cells by the finite annular plumbing rectangles. Pull back the model complex structure and disk. Biholomorphic coefficient charts induce an actual holomorphic symmetric-product map, so all kernel, domain, node and count assertions transfer exactly. The resulting structure is on the original surface, not on a replacement diagram. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 All unused parts and node charts have legitimate complex structures. On the model they retain their round charts, and every inserted torus has the flat structure, with holomorphic plumbing transitions. All attaching circles are real analytic: old collars were untouched, and the new circle is straight on its flat torus. The smooth identification in step3.1 pulls this full atlas back, preserving the real analytic circle property and the node charts. If only a prescribed distinguished-component model and its external collars were initially retained, the complement can equivalently be filled by any positive structure, keeping those collars fixed and giving the remaining disjoint circle collars the explicit cylindrical structure $s+it$ modulo their period. Finite cutoffs in [F5]'s compatible Cayley coordinates interpolate on the complement. Positivity is preserved, and the local coordinate proof supplies its actual integrability. This alternative is unnecessary for the full round/torus atlas just built, but makes explicit that no arbitrary symmetric power of a nonholomorphic map has been taken. The transported model map is holomorphic for the transported complex structure, so its symmetric power IS holomorphic in its written coefficient coordinates. [F1, F5, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 The model count persists for every sufficiently small protected perturbation. Its actual fixed-node operator is onto with two affine kernel sections. Add two smooth compact-source phase functionals with invertible two-by-two derivative on those sections, as in [F4]'s actual disk slice proof; such phases exist by their independence on an immersed root patch. The augmented map derivative is invertible, so quantitative Newton gives a unique nearby phase-zero disk for every sufficiently small coefficient perturbation. The source group acts smoothly on the higher solution charts; to define the phase at rough $H^3$ maps, change variables in its compact-source integral so derivatives fall on the smooth weight rather than on the map. Thus the same local phase uniqueness applies in the genuine $H^3$ topology. Onto-ness and index persist by Neumann and finite Fredholm blocks. If there were another orbit for a sequence of perturbations tending to zero, proper-unit marked compactness in [F4] would give global smooth convergence after positive-affine reparametrization to a standard model disk. The COMPLETE one-orbit property from step2.1 puts that limit in the phase neighborhood just constructed, contradicting local uniqueness. Hence a smaller ball has exactly one orbit at every parameter. This step uses compactness on the now FIXED smooth target only AFTER the genuine degenerating-target exhaustion has already proved the standard one-orbit model. [F2, F3, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, contradiction]

6.1 Compare this model background with the desired $j$ by the actual proper-unit-disk cobordism in [F4]. 7.1 Repeat the proof for the finite list of component/node problems. Separate auxiliary models may be used: the count comparison ends on the SAME desired $j$ and the SAME original node and component labels. Every such count is one. The conclusion retains arbitrary numbers of internal pairs and all original balanced values $m=h+k-1$, $k\ge2$; it has neither invoked a maximal-real-oval model in that range nor substituted a no-internal-pair assumption.
