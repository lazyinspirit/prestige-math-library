## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $u:\overline{\mathbb H}\to\operatorname{Sym}^m\Sigma$ be a smooth nonconstant integrable unit-domain disk for a planar cut component $A$ of a real analytic attaching-circle system, with its marked boundary point at $\infty$ and $u(\infty)=x$. Use the actual incidence cover of the zero-one-domain construction. Write $p:S\to\overline{\mathbb H}$ for its unique active component and $f:S\to\Sigma$ for its root map. Assume EVERY internal paired circle of $A$ is active, with its proper slit, and every inactive constant root is disjoint from $f(S)$. These are actual geometric premises, not assertions of existence of such a disk. The cover is unbranched at the physical boundary. Let $Q=p^{-1}(\infty)$ and let $T$ be the finite set of its quadratic slit tips.

Let $\mathcal M$ be the real vector space of functions on $S$ holomorphic in the interior, extending meromorphically across its real analytic boundary by reflection, real on that boundary away from their poles, and with at most a SIMPLE pole at each point of $Q\cup T$ and no other pole. Then the actual real $H^3\to H^2$ node-fixed disk linearization has kernel naturally isomorphic to $\mathcal M$. For an individual regular active root the correspondence is
$$\delta p(w)=-p_w(w)\,\xi(p(w)),$$
where $w=f$ is the local target coordinate and $\xi$ its root-coordinate variation. At a slit tip with $w=a+\zeta^2$ this reads $\delta p=-p_\zeta\delta w/(2\zeta)$: the real simple-pole coefficient is exactly the actual real tip displacement, multiplied by $-p_\zeta(0)/2$. The correspondence extends through every interior cover branch in the symmetric coefficients; no derivative of a root branch is assumed bounded there.

In particular, if the ACTUAL space $\mathcal M$ equals $\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{1,p\}$, the node-fixed operator is onto, since its actual index is two. Under that verified condition it supplies genuine localized scalar response operators as follows. Choose an interior point $q\in S$ such that $p(q)$ is not any branch value, and a small disk $U$ about $q$ on which $p$ and $f$ are injective, disjoint from $Q\cup T$. For each fixed compact $K\Subset U\setminus\{q\}$ and integer $k\ge2$, there is a bounded real-linear solution operator for
$$\bar\partial F=g,\qquad F(q)=0,\qquad \operatorname{supp}g\subset K,\quad g\in H^k(S),$$
where $F$ has real boundary values and at most the same prescribed real simple poles at $Q\cup T$. Its pole coefficients and its renormalized $H^{k+1}$ norm are bounded by $C_k\|g\|_{H^k}$. The solution is unique in this class. Its traces and derivatives on any fixed interior circle separated from $K\cup Q\cup T$ are bounded to every order by the corresponding forcing norm. This response is derived from the actual geometric operator, not postulated.

This is an exact linear kernel and localized response theorem. It does NOT assert that nonlinear nearby disks have been parametrized by independent slit endpoints, that the condition on $\mathcal M$ always holds, or that target-neck gluing/exhaustion or a count has been proved.

## Proof

1.1 Since every internal circle is active, the active image meets no inactive constant value; the latter are separated from its compact image by the stated premise. Their root components therefore split off smoothly and holomorphically in fixed disjoint support neighborhoods throughout the disk. On such a component, a kernel section is a holomorphic complex scalar with its boundary in one fixed real tangent line and value zero at infinity. In a coordinate at that constant value the line is real. Its imaginary part is a zero-boundary harmonic function, hence zero by integrating its gradient square; the real holomorphic function is constant and the node condition makes it zero. Thus the kernel is determined entirely by its active coefficient variations. By [F2] these variations are smooth and holomorphic in every actual complex coefficient chart. In a neighborhood of an active incidence point in the interior, use the target coordinate $w=f$, possible by [F1]. The reduced incidence curve is the graph $z=p(w)$, including a ramification point of $p$. Its local coefficient polynomial is a nonvanishing holomorphic unit times $z-p(w)$: the root map has multiplicity one, and different active incidence points cannot have the same target value, by [F1]. Therefore its $z$ derivative does not vanish on the graph. Differentiating its coefficient polynomial in the direction of the section defines a unique holomorphic graph variation $F=\delta p$, by division by that nonzero derivative. On a regular cover sheet this is exactly $F=-p_w\xi(p(w))$. Unit changes cancel in that quotient, so the construction agrees on overlaps and extends holomorphically through every interior cover branch. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 At a physical boundary point all tuple roots are distinct, so individual root sections are smooth up to that boundary. Real analytic target-circle charts and Schwarz reflection make them holomorphic across the edge: their values lie in the real tangent line, and reflection matches the tangential and normal derivatives. Away from the tips the root map is a local biholomorphism, and differentiating the real boundary relation $p(w)\in\mathbb R$ gives real $F$ there. At a tip choose the actual real-preserving coordinates from [F1] with $w=a+\zeta^2$. The physical coordinate $z=p(\zeta)$ has $p_\zeta(0)\ne0$, since the cover is unbranched on the boundary. Differentiating $w$ at fixed physical $z$ gives
$$F(\zeta)=-\frac{p_\zeta(\zeta)}{2\zeta}\,\delta w(p(\zeta)).$$
The section on the right is holomorphic across the edge and real there. Thus $F$ has at most a simple pole, with real coefficient $-p_\zeta(0)\delta w(p(0))/2$. This also proves the asserted exact moving-tip formula. At a marked boundary root use a real target coordinate $w$ with $w(x_j)=0$. The nonzero derivative of $p$ at the compactified physical infinity gives $p(w)=c/w+O(1)$ with real $c\ne0$. The node condition says $\xi(z)=O(1/z)$ in that root coordinate. Consequently $-p_w\xi(p(w))=O(1/w)$, again with real leading coefficient. At all other boundary points it is regular. This constructs the map from the actual fixed-node kernel to $\mathcal M$, without imposing any point trace on $H^1$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Conversely take $F\in\mathcal M$. On unbranched active roots define $\xi=-F/p_w$. At an interior cover branch this expression for individual roots may be singular; construct the symmetric variation instead. On a small target contour enclosing one root cluster, replace $p(w)$ formally by $p(w)+tF(w)$. Here both functions are holomorphic in $w$ and there are no boundary poles. Its roots over $z$ have power sums given by the fixed contour integral of $w^l(p_w+tF_w)/(p+tF-z)$. Differentiating that integral at zero gives a holomorphic function of $z$ on a smaller base disk: the denominator stays separated from zero on the contour. Newton's finite identities give the actual coefficient variations. They agree with $-F/p_w$ where the roots are simple, so the local constructions agree on overlaps by holomorphic identity and define a genuine bundle section. At a slit tip the formula is instead $\delta w=-2\zeta F/p_\zeta$. It is holomorphic in $\zeta$ through the tip and hence in $z$, since $p_\zeta\ne0$; its boundary value is real. At a marked root it is $O(w)$ by the pole orders in step2.1, so extends with value zero at infinity. All other boundary points are regular. Set the inactive variations to zero. The resulting global smooth section has the actual real boundary trace and fixed node, and its holomorphic coefficient components satisfy the actual linearization. The two constructions are inverse on the unbranched roots and hence everywhere. This proves the exact real-linear kernel identification. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 These spaces are finite dimensional with the indicated local norm control, not merely formal Laurent data. A function of $\mathcal M$ is determined by its finitely many real principal coefficients and one real additive constant: if all those coefficients vanish, it extends holomorphically and smoothly across the whole compact boundary with real values, and its imaginary part is zero by the gradient-square argument, so it is a real constant. Thus the principal-coefficient and one-point-value map embeds $\mathcal M$ in a finite-dimensional real space. The formulas in steps1.1--3.1 are bounded on the actual finite-dimensional kernel norms. More explicitly [F2]'s homogeneous estimates bound every smooth local section norm by its $H^3$ norm; on regular patches the formulas are smooth, at tips and nodes Cauchy bounds control the just computed finite principal coefficients and regular parts, and at interior cover branches the fixed-contour formula controls coefficients uniformly. Conversely the same finite patch formulas bound the section's $H^3$ norm by any fixed high enough smooth norm of the pole-subtracted function and its coefficients. If $\mathcal M=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{1,p\}$ its dimension is two. The actual node-fixed Fredholm index is two by [F1, F2], so its cokernel is zero and its range is all $H^2$. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Derive the localized response rather than assuming it. Shrink $U$ so $p$ embeds it in a disk disjoint from every branch value and every physical boundary point; all tuple roots there are distinct. Given $g\in H^k$ supported in $K$, define a target bundle forcing on this physical disk in its simple-root coordinates by taking the selected root component to be $-2g(w)/|p_w(w)|^2$, with $z=p(w)$, and every other component zero. Extend it by zero; its $H^k$ norm is bounded by $C_k\|g\|_{H^k}$, since its support stays in the fixed interior compact set and $p_w$ is bounded away from zero there. By step4.1 and [F3] solve the actual node-fixed $H^3\to H^2$ operator with its bounded kernel-complement inverse. The inhomogeneous section is holomorphic away from this support. Apply the graph formula from step1.1 to it on all regular root patches, and extend through the other interior branches and boundary tips exactly as in steps2.1--3.1, where the forcing is zero. This gives a function $F$ with the prescribed real simple boundary poles. On the forcing patch the chain rule gives
$$\bar\partial_w(-p_w\xi(p(w)))=-|p_w|^2(\bar\partial_z\xi)(p(w))=g(w),$$
because the geometric coordinate operator is $2\bar\partial_z$ and the forcing was chosen with the factor $-2$. Everywhere else $\bar\partial F=0$. In particular $F$ is holomorphic near $q$ and its evaluation there is bounded by the Cauchy estimate. Subtract the unique real combination of $1,p$ that makes $F(q)=0$: explicitly subtract $tp+s$, where $t=\operatorname{Im}F(q)/\operatorname{Im}p(q)$ and $s=\operatorname{Re}F(q)-t\operatorname{Re}p(q)$. The denominator is positive, since $p(q)\in\mathbb H$. This does not change its equation or permitted poles. A difference of two normalized solutions belongs to $\mathcal M$ and vanishes at $q$, so is zero by this same two-dimensional evaluation isomorphism. This is a genuine bounded real-linear construction. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 The norm estimates have the stated orders. The actual inhomogeneous higher estimate in [F2] bounds the solved section in $H^{k+1}$ by its already bounded $H^3$ norm plus the $H^k$ forcing norm, with only the original boundary and node constraints. On the forcing patch the root-coordinate graph formula is smooth with nonzero denominators and therefore bounds $F$ in $H^{k+1}$. Every other singular-looking patch is separated from the forcing. There the section is holomorphic, with the real edge reflection from step2.1, so the Cauchy estimate bounds all its fixed derivatives on smaller patches by the controlled section norm. The contour construction and the explicit tip/node formulas then bound the real pole coefficients and every fixed derivative of their subtracted regular parts. Use fixed disjoint real cutoffs to subtract those simple principal parts, and a finite patch cover; this gives the renormalized $H^{k+1}$ bound. The same interior estimates give the asserted trace and derivative bounds away from the forcing and pole set. All Hilbert inverses and higher estimates are the actual Countable Choice suppliers. The proof identifies the linear moving-tip problem and derives its localized response under a verified kernel condition; it introduces no nonlinear slit chart, target-neck exhaustion or count claim. [F2, F3, step 2.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
