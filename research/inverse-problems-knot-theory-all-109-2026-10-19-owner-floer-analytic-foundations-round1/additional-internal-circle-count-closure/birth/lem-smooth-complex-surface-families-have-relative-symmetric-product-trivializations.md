## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $j_s$, $s\in[0,1]$, be a smooth family of orientation-compatible almost complex structures on a compact oriented surface $\Sigma$. Fix an integer $m\ge1$. The topological space
$$\mathcal X=([0,1]\times\Sigma^m)/\mathfrak S_m,$$
where permutations act only on the surface factors, has a smooth manifold structure with boundary for which the projection to $[0,1]$ is a proper smooth submersion. Its fiber at $s$ is the complex manifold $\operatorname{Sym}^m(\Sigma,j_s)$, with its usual coefficient coordinates. The fiberwise complex structures are smooth on the vertical tangent bundle. There is a smooth trivialization $\Phi_s:X_0\to X_s$, $\Phi_0=I$.

The trivialization can simultaneously preserve every multiplicity stratum and each of finitely many incidence divisors $E_p=\{p\}+\operatorname{Sym}^{m-1}\Sigma$ for prescribed distinct points $p$. It may moreover be chosen to be the identity on a fixed neighborhood, in the underlying unordered-tuple space away from repeated points, of any prescribed compact subset disjoint from those divisors and the repeated-point locus. In particular this applies to a finite union of product tori formed from disjoint attaching circles avoiding those points. On such a torus, if the full endomorphism $j_s$ along every attaching circle is independent of $s$, the pulled-back full vertical complex structure on its ambient tangent bundle is independent of $s$ too. If the family is fixed on a neighborhood of finitely many surface points, the trivialization may also be the identity on coefficient-chart neighborhoods of tuples supported there, whenever these requirements are compatible with the prescribed compact subset.

This constructs smooth maps between the symmetric products using the relative complex family. It makes no assertion that the symmetric power of an arbitrary nonholomorphic surface diffeomorphism is smooth.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual compact surface family, integer $m$, finite incidence points, and optional compact off-diagonal subset specified in the Statement. Neighborhoods to be fixed are chosen disjoint from the incidence divisors when using the arbitrary off-diagonal prescription; fixed complex-chart neighborhoods may meet them.

[F1] Smooth families of positive surface structures have actual jointly smooth local holomorphic coordinate families, including unchanged charts on already fixed regions. ([[lem-countable-choice-parametric-holomorphic-coordinates-for-oriented-surfaces]])

[F2] A complex polynomial factors into linear factors, by repeated application of the proved fundamental theorem of algebra. The circle Cauchy formula computes a holomorphic function at an interior point. ([[thm-fundamental-theorem-of-algebra-minimum-modulus-proof]], [[thm-cauchy-integral-formula-circle]])

[F3] Smooth finite-dimensional vector fields have unique local flows with smooth parameter dependence, by the explicit local Picard and derivative argument used to construct the boundary-preserving local addition. On a compact target these flows extend for every time for which the prescribed base coordinate stays in its compact interval. ([[lem-boundary-preserving-local-addition-for-transverse-product-tori]])

## Proof

1.1 Construct the relative charts directly. Near a tuple with distinct support points $a_1,\ldots,a_r$ and multiplicities $d_1+\cdots+d_r=m$, choose disjoint small disks and [F1] holomorphic coordinates $z_{s,\nu}$ on them on a common parameter interval. A nearby unordered tuple is recorded by $s$ and the coefficients of the monic polynomial whose $d_\nu$ roots are its coordinates in the $\nu$th disk. These coefficients are continuous symmetric functions. They give a homeomorphism onto an open subset of $\mathbb R\times\mathbb C^m$. Here are the needed inverse and openness details at repeated roots: by [F2] a monic coefficient tuple has its full multiset of roots, uniquely by division and induction. Every root satisfies $|z|\le1+\max_k|c_k|$, since outside this bound the leading term dominates the geometric sum of lower terms. For a convergent sequence of coefficient tuples, choose arbitrary orderings of their finitely many roots; every subsequence has a further subsequence with all ordered roots converging in a fixed compact disk. The limit roots factor the limit polynomial, hence have its prescribed unordered multiset. This proves unordered-root continuity, since otherwise a subsequence staying outside a fixed neighborhood would contradict that conclusion. It also shows that requiring all roots to remain in the specified coordinate disks is an open coefficient condition. The tuple quotient is metrizable, for example by minimizing the maximum coordinate distance over finitely many permutations in any fixed compact surface metric, so this sequential continuity is ordinary continuity. [F1, F2, construct, algebra]

2.1 All chart transitions are smooth even at collisions. It suffices first to change one coordinate $z$ to $g_s(z)$ on a disk containing a cluster of roots and with a fixed contour circle inside their common coordinate domain. For $P(z)=\prod_{i=1}^d(z-z_i)$, its logarithmic derivative on that contour is $P'/P=\sum_i1/(z-z_i)$, an identity valid with repeated roots as well. [F2] gives each power sum of the transformed roots by the actual formula
$$\sum_{i=1}^d g_s(z_i)^l=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\partial D}g_s(z)^l\frac{P'(z)}{P(z)}\,dz\quad(1\le l\le d).$$
On the contour the denominator stays uniformly away from zero on a small coefficient neighborhood. Thus differentiating the displayed finite-interval integral proves smooth dependence on $s$ and all real coefficient variables; its dependence on the coefficients is holomorphic because the integrand is. Newton's finite identities $k e_k=\sum_{l=1}^k(-1)^{l-1}e_{k-l}p_l$, proved by differentiating $\prod_i(1+z_i t)$ and comparing coefficients, recover all elementary coefficients from these power sums. For transitions involving two cluster decompositions, refine them near the tuple to disjoint smaller support disks. The same contour formula with $g_s(z)=z$ extracts each smaller cluster's power sums from a larger polynomial; multiplying the resulting finite monic factors reverses this refinement. Applying these operations and the coordinate-change formula proves smoothness of every transition and its inverse. Consequently these charts give a smooth manifold with the asserted fiberwise holomorphic atlas; multiplication by $i$ in their coefficient tangent coordinates gives a smooth vertical complex structure. Projection is the first coordinate, hence a submersion. The quotient of the compact product is compact, so this projection is proper. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 We now construct horizontal fields adapted to all the stated subsets. In a relative coefficient chart the vector field $\partial_s$ with coefficients held fixed projects to one. It is tangent to every multiplicity stratum: fixing a polynomial's coefficients keeps exactly its root multiplicities. For a prescribed incidence point in a support disk use $z_s(p)=0$, achieved by subtracting the smooth value of the coordinate there. Then $E_p$ is the coefficient hyperplane where the polynomial's constant term is zero, so this field is tangent to $E_p$. Choose disks small enough that each contains at most one of the finitely many prescribed points; disks about any support not containing such a point avoid all of them. Thus these local horizontal fields are simultaneously tangent to every incidence divisor and every multiplicity stratum. On the off-diagonal locus the original smooth unordered-tuple charts also exist: one labels the disjoint support disks, so their transitions are only permutations. On a neighborhood of the prescribed compact off-diagonal subset disjoint from the divisors use instead the raw constant-tuple horizontal field; it too is tangent to every stratum there, since only the open distinct-root stratum occurs. On a fixed complex-chart neighborhood use the relative coefficient field from those unchanged charts, which is precisely the requested fixed-coordinate lift and is tangent to any incidence divisors there. These prescriptions coincide wherever both fixed-coordinate requirements apply: away from repeated points both hold the underlying points fixed. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Patch these fields by a finite smooth partition constructed in their actual charts. Shrink a finite cover to a finite cover by smaller relatively compact chart sets, place Euclidean bump functions equal to one on the smaller sets and supported in the larger ones, and divide each by their positive finite sum. If a horizontal field is prescribed on a neighborhood of a compact subset, first take two nested smaller neighborhoods there and a cutoff equal to one on the smaller and zero outside the larger; assign this cutoff to the prescribed field and multiply the remaining partition by its complement. For the optional fixed-chart neighborhoods use the same finite operation, shrinking them so prescriptions are compatible on intersections as in step3.1. The resulting smooth horizontal field $V$ projects to one, since its scalar weights sum to one. At any point of an incidence divisor or of a multiplicity stratum, each field with nonzero weight is tangent there; affine combinations whose weights sum to one are tangent there too. Thus $V$ has all these tangencies and the required local fixed values. This construction uses only finite covers, explicit bumps and positive finite division, not an arbitrary-index partition theorem. [step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Integrate $V$ from base time zero to time $s$ by [F3]. The base coordinate increases at unit speed. Compactness of the total space gives extension to every $s\in[0,1]$: finitely many local flow charts give a common positive continuation time on each compact subinterval, and a putative finite terminal time has a convergent subsequence of points and a local continuation there. Uniqueness and reverse-time integration show that each map $\Phi_s:X_0\to X_s$ is a smooth diffeomorphism, with smoothly varying inverse. It preserves the incidence divisors and every multiplicity stratum. To justify this also for a nonclosed stratum, in a coefficient chart a fixed multiplicity type is a smooth locally closed submanifold: parameterize its distinct root positions, whose derivatives are independent because a vanishing polynomial derivative at each distinct root forces all the position derivatives to be zero, or divide the identity $\dot P/P=-\sum d_i\dot z_i/(z-z_i)$ and examine its distinct poles. The inverse to these local position coordinates follows from step1.1 and the ordinary inverse theorem on their independent derivatives. A field tangent to this submanifold has a local restricted flow; ambient uniqueness identifies it with the ambient flow for a short two-sided time interval. Hence the set of times at which a given integral curve belongs to each stratum is open relative to the whole time interval. The finitely many disjoint multiplicity types partition that connected interval, so only one type occurs. The same argument for the closed smooth hyperplanes $E_p$ gives preservation of each $E_p$; their intersections cause no difficulty because each individual invariant subset is preserved. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Wherever the prescribed horizontal field is the raw constant-tuple lift the flow is the identity on the underlying points, and its vertical derivative there is the identity in the smooth off-diagonal tuple charts. On a product torus, the standard complex structure in such charts is the direct sum of the surface endomorphisms $j_s$ at its labeled coordinates, modulo the fixed finite permutation transition. If those full endomorphisms along each attaching circle are fixed, their full ambient direct sum is fixed, proving the stated full boundary conclusion after pullback. Likewise the unchanged complex-chart horizontal prescription gives the identity on the chosen coefficient neighborhoods, including collisions there. All constructions used the actual relative coefficient atlas, whose smoothness across collisions was proved in step2.1; no regularity of a nonholomorphic symmetric-power map was assumed. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
