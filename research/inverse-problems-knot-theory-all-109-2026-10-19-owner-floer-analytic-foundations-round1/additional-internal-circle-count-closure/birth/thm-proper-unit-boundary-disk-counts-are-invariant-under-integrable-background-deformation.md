## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let one attaching system on a compact oriented surface be balanced: its compression disks cut the handlebody into $k\ge2$ balls, with $m=h+k-1$ attaching circles on a genus-$h$ surface. Supply finite smooth planar triangulations of their cut components. Choose a product node $x$ with one coordinate on each attaching circle, finitely many allowed such nodes, and finite incidence points off the circles which meet every complementary region. Let $j_0,j_1$ be orientation-compatible surface structures for which the attaching circles have actual real-axis holomorphic charts at those node coordinates. Take proper cut components $A_i$ and their unit domains.

For each endpoint background, all sufficiently small autonomous nearly symmetric perturbations which fix its full value along the product torus and its exact node charts have a well-defined finite mod-two unit-domain count whenever their actual node-fixed disk operators are regular. The counts for the two endpoint backgrounds agree, separately for every chosen node and every $A_i$, after the endpoint diffeomorphism preserving the attaching circles and nodes constructed below. More precisely one can choose a smooth family of relative symmetric products and arbitrarily small regular perturbations of its endpoint standard structures, together with a connecting perturbed family, whose node-fixed unit-disk orbit spaces are compact smooth one-manifolds with exactly those two finite endpoint orbit sets as boundary.

The family is allowed to change the surface complex structure. It fixes the full complex endomorphism along the boundary torus, all node charts, the incidence divisors and the repeated-point locus after the constructed trivialization. Endpoint counts for other sufficiently small regular perturbations agree by local deformation invariance. No unit-count value, disk existence, target-neck gluing, $k=1$ case, second attaching-system comparison, or Floer differential identity is asserted.

## Proof

1.1 Apply [F1] to the single attaching system and all marked node coordinates. The resulting endpoint surface map is a composition of circle-preserving supported isotopies, hence preserves each complementary cut component and fixes every chosen incidence point and node coordinate. Reparametrize the resulting structure path smoothly so it is stationary on small parameter collars of zero and one. Pull its relative symmetric products back to $X_0$ by the adapted trivialization. Denote the resulting smooth standard vertical structures by $K_s$ on $X_0$. The full restriction of $K_s$ along $L$ and the exact generator charts are fixed; every chosen incidence divisor and the repeated-point locus are fixed smooth subsets. The endpoint biholomorphism induces a genuinely holomorphic symmetric-product diffeomorphism in coefficient coordinates. Thus endpoint domain labels and nodes agree under that map. All uniform analytic statements below are made on this one compact smooth target with its actual family $K_s$, rather than on symmetric powers of nonholomorphic surface maps. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 We establish the required compactness uniformly along this path, including for small perturbations. Suppose $s_n\to s$ and $J_n-K_{s_n}\to0$ smoothly, with disks of a fixed proper unit domain and a fixed allowed node. In the relative coefficient charts, all local derivatives of the target structures and changes of charts have uniform bounds on a finite cover of the compact parameter-target space. Positive surface area forms and the corresponding local target metrics can be chosen smoothly, with uniform comparison constants on that cover. The proof of [F3]'s energy estimate is the actual degree-area identity on the ordered incidence cover; it bounds the energy by one uniform constant times the finite area of the fixed unit domain. The local small-energy estimates, rescaling, removability and neck estimates in [F2]'s compactness proof therefore have common constants on the finite cover: their constants depend only on finitely many derivatives, metric comparisons and the fixed real node charts. Applying that proof yields a bubble tree with degree additivity for the fixed incidence divisors. A sphere has the same nonnegative incidence degree in every surface region, so its presence would contribute positive degree outside the proper component; it cannot occur. Nonnegative disk domains add to the unit component vector; a disk whose boundary is on this one attaching torus has constant degree on each cut component, so precisely one nonconstant unit disk remains, all other components being constant. The marked-node normalization in [F2] applies unchanged in the common fixed node charts, and gives a sequence of node-fixing positive affine reparametrizations converging globally smoothly, including at the node, to that unit disk. This also proves uniform near-family compactness for any smoothly convergent sequence of sufficiently small perturbations of $K_s$: otherwise a sequence approaching the family contradicts the conclusion just proved. No invariance of the descended area form under the target trivialization is assumed; only its smooth uniform bounds in the actual relative charts were used. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 There is a single smooth neighborhood of the compact family in which EVERY actual proper unit disk has a free globally unique interior immersion site outside the protected diagonal/divisor neighborhoods, $L$ and the fixed node neighborhoods. Indeed at each limiting standard disk the argument of [F2]'s free-site proof applies to the actual integrable structure $K_s$: its incidence degree is zero or one, so its integrable injective-site theorem applies to arbitrary smooth attaching circles. Its free-image argument excludes containment in the integrable protected divisor/diagonal neighborhoods by the absence of boundary there, excludes an open immersed image in $L$ by total reality, and excludes containment in a single protected node chart by the harmonic real-boundary argument. It then supplies an open unique immersion site. Choose the protected neighborhoods sufficiently small; a failure of any uniform choice would, by step2.1 after affine normalization, converge to one standard disk lacking such a site. A chosen globally unique immersion site persists for global $C^1$ convergence: on a smaller source patch a two-coordinate projection is an embedding, and compactness of the closed source excludes another preimage in its complement. Thus the alleged countersequence contradicts its limiting site. If the standard moduli set is empty the same compactness argument excludes all nearby disks instead. Finitely many nodes and components give one uniform neighborhood. The full node-fixed index there is two by [F2]'s actual all-background domain formula; alternatively local-addition identifies the smoothly converging compact operators and their finite Schur block proves eventual index equality. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, contradiction]

4.1 Construct protected perturbations of this family directly. On the fixed smooth target choose countably many rational-coordinate smooth spatial bumps supported in the free open region, and project their matrix values to anti-$K_s$ endomorphisms by $(A+K_sAK_s)/2$. Charts and supports can depend smoothly on $s$ through finitely many parameter patches. Include rational parameter cutoffs as well, extend each supported bump by zero, and choose explicit positive coefficients decreasing fast enough that their sum and every parameter-spatial derivative converge by Cauchy--Schwarz on one $\ell^2$ ball. As in [F4], approximating compactly supported smooth fields by finite rational-coordinate bumps gives density in every fixed $C^r$ norm at each interior parameter value; a parameter cutoff equal to one there retains that density. The actual family is $J_{s,a}=K_s(I+B_{s,a})(I-B_{s,a})^{-1}$. It fixes all protected values, is smooth jointly into every coefficient norm, and is tame on a sufficiently small ball by the same pointwise Cayley computation as [F4]. The shrinking in step3.1 ensures an open free site for every actual unit disk at every parameter. At fixed $s$, the weighted universal derivative is onto: an orthogonal $L^2$ obstruction is smooth and has unique continuation by [F5]; choose a nonzero value on that free open site and an anti-$J$ bump pairing positively there. Global uniqueness localizes its entire pullback to the source embedding patch. Dense finite parameter tangents contradict obstruction annihilation. The compact/weighted node-evaluation comparison in [F4] transfers this to the compact $H^3\to H^2$ universal derivative. All these are oriented product-torus disk realizations, exactly the boundary scope of that comparison. [F1, F2, F4, F5, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Choose arbitrarily small regular parameters separately for the two stationary endpoint families by [F4]'s actual Sard--Smale construction, and hold their structures fixed in the parameter collars. Extend their small Cayley coefficients smoothly into the family using cutoffs supported in those collars, which is possible since $K_s$ is constant there. Add the family bumps of step4.1 multiplied by a smooth scalar positive on $(0,1)$ and zero on the endpoint collars' outer endpoints; alternatively retain small fixed collars and apply the argument on their middle closed interval. More explicitly make the extension constant on $[0,\epsilon]$ and $[1-\epsilon,1]$, and multiply the perturbation space by a smooth factor positive on $(\epsilon,1-\epsilon)$ and zero on the two closed collars. Interior universal surjectivity follows from step4.1 because the scalar factor is nonzero; on the closed collars it follows from the fixed endpoint disk regularity. Use the open universal regular zero locus and the split right inverse obtained by a finite lift of the fixed operator's finite range quotient and its kernel-complement inverse, as in [F4]. Its projection to the perturbation Hilbert ball has Fredholm index three: the fixed disk operator has index two, and adjoining the one real path variable adds one by finite block elimination. Countable second-countable map charts and [F5] give a residual set of parameters where the fixed family is regular simultaneously for every chosen node and component. At the two physical parameter endpoints the fixed operator is already onto, so the resulting finite-dimensional three-dimensional zero spaces have actual boundary exactly those endpoint zero spaces. [F4, F5, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Divide these regular solution families by the node-fixing positive affine group. The action is free by the existence of a globally unique immersion site. It is proper by the exact argument in [F4]: global uniform convergence on the compactified disk and diverging affine parameters force the pulled-back limit to be constant off at most one point, contradicting the nonconstant unit domain. The extra path parameter has a convergent subsequence and does not affect this argument. The action is smooth on the finite-dimensional solution families without an unsupported differentiability assertion on rough $H^3$ maps: higher-order zero charts for the parameter-augmented operator have the same smooth kernel, by actual weak boundary regularity, and are local diffeomorphisms to the $H^3$ zero chart by the finite-dimensional inverse theorem. Composition can be differentiated after passing to sufficiently high order. The two independent affine tangent directions admit finite-dimensional local complements, giving smooth one-dimensional quotient charts. Properness makes the quotient Hausdorff and excludes distant returns into such a slice. The quotient map is open, so the second-countable source supplies a second-countable quotient. Its actual boundary is exactly the two endpoint orbit sets. [F4, F5, step 2.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 The quotient is compact. For any sequence of quotient points pass to a convergent path parameter subsequence. Step2.1 gives global smooth convergence after precisely the allowed node-fixing affine reparametrizations, so the quotient points converge in a local slice. Thus it is sequentially compact. A countable base gives, under Countable Choice, a countable subcover of every open cover by choosing one cover member for each subordinate basic set. If no finite subcover exists, choose a point outside its first $n$ members for each integer $n$; a convergent subsequence contradicts eventual containment in a cover member of its limit. Consequently the quotient is compact. A finite interval-chart decomposition gives boundary parity exactly as proved in [F4]'s local count-invariance theorem: cut the finitely many charts at their finite frontiers, forming a finite graph with valence two at interior vertices and one at boundary vertices; its degree sum is twice its edge number. Its finite number of boundary points is even. These are precisely the endpoint orbit sets, so their mod-two counts agree. Any other sufficiently small regular protected endpoint perturbation has the same count by [F4]'s actual local deformation-invariance theorem at that fixed endpoint background. This proves the asserted comparison while leaving the internal-circle count value and every target-neck assertion separate. [F4, step 2.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, contradiction, algebra] ∎
