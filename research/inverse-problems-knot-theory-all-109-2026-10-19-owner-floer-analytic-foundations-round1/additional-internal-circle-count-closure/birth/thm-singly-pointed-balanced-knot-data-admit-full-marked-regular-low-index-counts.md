## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. For the actual singly pointed balanced $S^3$ knot diagram with $m=h\ge1$ and full filtered marked-count conventions, there is a choice of surface complex structure and ONE allowed smooth almost-complex path, constant on genuine endpoint collars, such that EVERY nonconstant strip of index at most zero is absent, every actual index-one and index-two strip is regular, and there are finitely many index-one translation orbits across ALL actual Whitney classes and ALL nonnegative marked degrees.

The endpoint structures are autonomous. All actual unit-surface boundary disks through generators are node-fixed regular of index two and have finite affine-orbit counts zero modulo two, on both attaching tori. No unit-incidence sphere for any structure along the chosen path passes through a generator. These are actual chosen-data conclusions, not hypotheses on a formal moduli system. When $h=0$ the symmetric product and tori are single points and the corresponding nonconstant moduli are empty directly.

This supplies full-marked low-index existence and finite counts in the singly pointed case. Compactification identities, invariance and grading normalization are not asserted here.

## Proof

1.1 Choose the actual alpha real-oval surface structure from [F1]. Every generator lies in the alpha torus, so no nonconstant standard sphere passes through any generator. The unit-incidence sphere energy bound is uniform for all nearby nearly symmetric structures by the compact bubble degree-energy proof used in [F1]; call it $E_s$. Apply [F1]'s bounded-energy sphere-avoidance openness at the finite generators for this bound. The resulting neighborhood excludes every unit-incidence sphere through a generator for ANY structure in it. The unit-surface disk energy bound gives another finite bound $E_d$. Shrink so alpha unit disks are absent by [F1]'s full-boundary disk-emptiness openness. Choose protected autonomous endpoint perturbations in this neighborhood, fixing the full structures on BOTH tori and the exact generator charts, with all beta unit disks regular. The actual beta free-site proof in the new singly pointed unit theorem still permits this additional restriction: on its open immersed free preimage, the other totally real alpha torus has closed preimage with empty interior. Its complement therefore supplies a smaller free open set, and the same supported anti-$J$ pairing and Hilbert Sard argument apply. Pointed spheres remain absent by the chosen neighborhood. Hence alpha unit counts are empty and beta unit counts are finite and zero by that theorem. Both endpoint operators have exactly the regularity needed in [F3]. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Construct the actual auxiliary flat-endpoint-jet Hilbert path family of [F2] with these autonomous endpoints, and restrict its ball so EVERY value $J_t$ stays in the sphere-avoiding neighborhood of step1.1. Its endpoint-preserving variations and genuine-collar approximations can be kept in that same open neighborhood by their smooth coefficient convergence. Choose an auxiliary path $J_*$ regular for ALL nonconstant strips by [F2]. Obtain a finite global index lower bound without assuming a domain-index Euler formula. For each nonempty set of realized classes with endpoints $(x,y)$ choose ONE class $\psi_{xy}$; there are finitely many endpoint pairs, so only finite choice is used. [F2]'s full signed mark-index relation gives, for every actual holomorphic strip with those ends and every path under consideration, $$\mu(u)=c_{xy}+2n_w(u),\qquad c_{xy}=\mu(\psi_{xy})-2n_w(\psi_{xy}).$$ Positivity gives $\mu(u)\ge c_{xy}$. Put $L=\min(0,\{c_{xy}\})$, omitting empty pairs. The finite band $I=\{L,L+1,\ldots,2\}$ therefore contains EVERY nonpositive strip and all strips of indices one and two. [F2]'s full bounded-index theorem supplies one common energy bound for this entire band and all sufficiently close collar approximations, retaining all positive marked multiplicities. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Approximate $J_*$ by genuine constant-collar paths $J_n$ as in [F2]. Suppose arbitrarily late approximations possess a nonconstant strip of index at most zero or a nonregular strip of index one or two. Countable Choice selects such a sequence; pass to one index in the finite band and one endpoint pair. [F3] gives actual bounded-energy breaking and bubbles for the limit path $J_*$. Every nonconstant strip level is regular by the choice of $J_*$ and has positive integer index, since its nonzero longitudinal translation tangent lies in the kernel of an onto operator. Every nonconstant boundary disk has index at least two; every sphere has index $2d$ for positive incidence degree $d$. Apply the actual index sum FIRST. A nonpositive total index permits no nonconstant component. If the limit were entirely constant with no bubbles, the energy identity would give energies tending to zero. The actual uniform small-window estimate of [F4] then puts the whole strip into one exact transverse generator chart: every fixed-size window has small energy, connectedness chooses one component of the finite generator neighborhood, and its total image stays there. In that chart it solves exactly $D_0w=0$ with the two fixed transverse real boundary planes; the transverse whole-strip inverse in [F4]'s proof forces $w=0$. This contradicts nonconstancy and explicitly eliminates the entirely constant alternative. Hence all nonpositive counterexamples are excluded. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, contradiction]

4.1 For a total index one or two, the positive index budget in step3.1 places every nonconstant limiting strip level in indices one or two, and it is regular. Thus [F3]'s actual limiting-level premise is now satisfied. Its singly pointed sphere alternative would be a constant generator strip with one unit-incidence sphere, possibly with constant ghosts. All those constant components have the generator value by matched nodes, so the sphere passes through a generator for one $J_*(t)$. Step1.1 excludes it. Every remaining limit is one regular strip, two regular rigid strips, or a constant generator with one regular unit-surface boundary disk. In the first two cases the actual squared-cutoff parametrix proof in [F4] gives right inverses for the nearby actual operators; this local proof uses the exact generator tails and regular limiting operators, and not its zero-marked bubble-exclusion Statement. In the boundary-disk case [F3]'s actual varying-boundary-bubble inverse applies to the converging endpoint structures and that regular node-fixed unit disk, with no collar-width/neck-rate assumption. Thus no sequence of nonregular index-one or index-two counterexamples exists. Choose a sufficiently late $J_n$: it has no nonconstant index at most zero, all index-one and index-two strips regular, the fixed endpoint unit-disk data of step1.1, and the sphere avoidance for every $t$. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, contradiction]

5.1 All actual index-one strips for this path have the common energy bound of step2.1. For any sequence apply actual compactness and the index sum once more. Every nonconstant strip level has positive index by step4.1's exclusion; every bubble has index at least two. Therefore the total index one forces exactly one nonconstant index-one level and no bubbles, including no sphere. Its exact generator-tail cutoff inverse upgrades translated core convergence to global $H^3$: if $w_n$ is the coordinate difference on a tail, $D_0(\chi w_n)=\chi' w_n$ and $\|\chi w_n\|_{H^3}\le C\|\chi' w_n\|_{H^2}\to0$, while the finite core converges smoothly. The actual regular zero chart is one-dimensional and its nonzero translation tangent makes it locally a single orbit. These charts make the orbit space discrete. If there were infinitely many distinct orbits, Countable Choice selects an ordered $n$-tuple of distinct orbits for each $n$; flatten and retain first occurrences to obtain a sequence of distinct orbits, then choose representatives by Countable Choice. The just proved compactness yields a convergent translated subsequence into one of the single-orbit neighborhoods, a contradiction. Hence there are finitely many ALL rigid orbits, not just finitely many for a chosen class or mark vector. The endpoint data and zero disk counts were already established in step1.1. For $h=0$ the target is a point and all nonconstant assertions are empty directly. This completes the singly pointed full-marked chosen-data statement. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 4.1, construct, contradiction] ∎
