## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $X$ be an oriented topological $4$-manifold with boundary, and let $V\subset X$ be a connected relatively open neighborhood endowed with a specified smooth structure compatible with its smooth boundary collar and the given framed disk data, whose specified boundary collars and normal framings are smooth in this structure. Assume $\pi_1(V)$ is good for disk embedding. Let $f_i:(D^2,S^1)\looparrowright(V,V\cap\partial X)$ be a finite proper smooth generic disk datum with pairwise disjoint embedded framed boundaries, and framed smooth immersed spheres $g_i$ satisfying $\lambda(f_i,g_j)=\delta_{ij}$, $\lambda(g_i,g_j)=0$, $\mu(g_i)=0$, $\lambda(f_i,f_j)=0$ for $i\ne j$, and $\mu(f_i)=0$, computed in $\mathbb Z[\pi_1(V)]$.

There is a finite family of proper framed one-story capped-grope towers in $V$, of first-story height at least four, whose attaching regions are the Whitney circles for a regular homotopy of the $f_i$. Their bodies extend to embedded convergent skyscraper pinched neighborhoods, each with a relative standard-two-handle parameterization preserving its attaching solid torus and framing. The corresponding embedded framed Whitney disks give pairwise disjoint proper locally flat disks $\bar f_i$ homotopic to $f_i$ rel boundary and framing. They carry product normal neighborhoods supplied by this construction and framed immersed geometric duals $\bar g_i$ homotopic in $V$ to $g_i$.

In particular one may take $V=X$ when $X$ and the given maps, boundary collars and framings are smooth. No existence of a smoothing of a general topological neighborhood is asserted. The towers are capped-grope skyscrapers; an unrestricted Casson-handle construction is not assumed.

## Proof

1.1 **Section1: Finite contraction and cyclic-label cancellation.** The following finite construction and its limiting steps use the supplied framed data. [given, construct]

1.2 A finite framed four-dimensional capped-grope neighborhood is supplied, with its usual product collars and self-plumbing charts. Its body is embedded; only cap interiors have generic double points. An integer labeling means a homomorphism from the fundamental group of this actual neighborhood to Z. Paths to individual cap sheets are fixed once. Conjugating a based loop does not change its integer label. No arbitrary locally flat surface is given a tubular neighborhood by this hypothesis: the actual grope's framed block neighborhood is input. [construct]

1.3 Contract one top surface stage. Designate a finite subset of cap double points to be pushed off at that contraction, and choose a first and second sheet at each designated point. Then the resulting shorter capped grope has the same lower body, attaching circle and framing. Every old intersection not designated has parallel descendants with its old label. No designated old intersection remains. Every new cap intersection is a descendant of two designated intersections removed across dual caps at this same contraction, and its label is a difference of their labels, up to overall sign. Consequently, if all designated labels lie in an interval [a,b], every new label has absolute value at most b−a. [construct]

1.4 Cut the top surface at each pair of symplectic attaching curves. Replace the cut part by two small parallel copies of each of its two caps and the small central square at their unique attaching-curve intersection. Cutting a genus-g one-boundary surface along its complete symplectic system leaves the disk region with paired edges; the four parallel cap sectors and this square fill each pair of edges. Thus this replaces the surface with its contracted disk. All replacements take place in the supplied framed block. Parallel copies are made in its chosen normal coordinate; the central square uses the untwisted product collar, so the external attaching framing is unchanged. The result may be immersed, which is allowed. [construct]

1.5 For a designated point, take an embedded path on its second cap from that point to its attaching boundary, with interior avoiding all other double-point preimages. Finitely many paths on one cap can be chosen with disjoint interiors by an embedded finite tree and small parallel offsets. The two parallel copies of that path, closed across the central square, bound a ribbon Whitney disk pairing the two intersections of the first sheet with the contracted cap. In a product rectangle around the path, this is just the rectangle between the two parallel copies, closed by the boundary square. Its interior is disjoint from the old body and all unmodified sheets. A generic sufficiently small choice of the finitely many product widths is possible because their unwanted incidences are finitely many closed disjoint compact sets. No appeal to a normal bundle over an arbitrary TOP surface occurs. [construct]

1.6 Push the first sheet across this ribbon. This removes precisely the two paired intersections. The only ribbons that can meet are ones running across dual cap sectors: their boundary paths cross in the central square. Resolving those crossings by the prescribed order of the pushes creates intersections between the pushed first sheets. Ribbons on the same cap have been chosen parallel and disjoint, and ribbons belonging to different handles have disjoint block neighborhoods. Therefore every new intersection has exactly the stated two designated ancestors. Repeating for all parallel descendants removes the designated old point altogether. This is a finite sequence of ordinary finger/Whitney operations in explicit smooth block charts; a small relative perturbation makes the resulting intersections transverse. [construct]

1.7 Here is the label calculation, including the path issue. Let the two designated points have based first-to-second loops g,h. A path to the new intersection follows the first pushed sheet along the ribbon to its second-sheet cap, crosses the common central square, and returns along the other ribbon in reverse. Cancel the common paths in the old body. The resulting word is g h^{-1}; changing starting path conjugates it and exchanging the two resulting sheets inverts it. This is a calculation in the original supplied neighborhood, since every ribbon and push lies there. Its Z label is consequently ±(phi(g)−phi(h)). An old point copied to parallel sheets has its old loop with a small contractible product strip added, so its label is unchanged. The construction, classification of new intersections and claimed bound follow. [construct]

1.8 **Section2: The finite asymmetric bootstrap.** The following finite construction and its limiting steps use the supplied framed data. [given, construct]

1.9 Start with one symplectic pair above the bottom surface: on the positive side a framed capped surface R of positive genus, on the negative side a framed immersed cap W. The same construction is repeated for the finitely many pairs; their body blocks are disjoint, and interactions between cap systems are allowed. All operations fix a neighborhood of the original bottom surface and its attaching region. [construct]

1.10 Take two small framed parallel copies of R, at negative and positive normal coordinate, and join their boundary circles by the normal annulus crossing the attaching curve of W once. The result is an embedded closed surface of genus twice that of R. Its caps are the corresponding parallel copies of the caps of R. This is a sphere-like capped surface F: compressing its symplectic system gives a sphere. Its body meets W in one point and is disjoint from R; its cap intersections with all the old caps are unrestricted. The annulus and the single crossing are read in the supplied symplectic handle block, not in a hypothetical neighborhood of an arbitrary locally flat surface. [construct]

1.11 Construct a small parallel copy of F and contract its top stage to an immersed framed sphere T. During contraction, push every other cap sheet off T by the ribbons of Section1, excluding the one prescribed body intersection T with W. This includes the original caps of F, the caps of R and the remaining parts of W. All intersections newly produced by those pushes are between those cap sheets. The sphere T is therefore disjoint from both R and the body/caps of F, and from the caps of R, and meets W at its one prescribed point only. Self-intersections of T are allowed. The ribbons have support near cap arcs and their central squares, away from the lower body, so they do not alter any of the displayed body intersections. [construct]

1.12 Use T as a Norman sphere to remove every intersection of W with a cap of R or F: for each such point, choose a path in W from that point to the unique point W meets T. Remove a small disk from the intersecting cap and connect it by an untwisted tube, along a parallel copy of that path, to T with a small disk removed. The second endpoint consumes the one intersection of that parallel T with W. The two disks removed and the tube are the boundary of a product neighborhood of the path, so the resulting cap misses W at the chosen point and has the same boundary and framing. Choose the finitely many paths as branches of a tree in the abstract disk W, with separate parallel approach paths at their common endpoint; their interiors avoid all other double-point preimages. Normal widths are chosen successively small. The added copies of T are framed immersed spheres; their self- and mutual intersections belong entirely to the modified CAPS. Thus they may create cap intersections but no body intersections. Their original intersections with R or the body of F are empty, and sufficiently small parallel copies retain that property. After this finite operation W is disjoint from every other part except its own self-intersections and the single point on the body of F. In particular F remains a proper capped surface transverse to W. [construct]

1.13 For each self-intersection of W, tube one of its sheets to a separate small parallel copy of the BODY of F, along the other sheet and then along a chosen arc in W to the transverse point. This is the same local Norman cancellation, now using a capped surface instead of the contracted sphere. The new body consists of W with that sheet disk deleted, the punctured closed body of F, and their connecting annulus. It is a one-boundary surface with additional genus, with the old W boundary/framing. The caps of the added F provide the symplectic caps for its new handles. Its body is embedded: before the tubing, distinct body copies were disjoint and met W only at their distinct transverse endpoints; the tube arcs avoid every other double-point preimage. Choose disjoint parallel branches of a finite tree in W and nested normal widths. The tube neighborhoods consequently miss all other body sheets. Every old self-intersection of W is removed once, and none is introduced into the new body. Its new caps may intersect each other or the old caps, but they miss every body: the prior operation made the old caps disjoint from W, and properness of F makes its caps disjoint from all the parallel body sheets. At the attaching collars this assertion is the defining product chart. Consequently W has become an actual proper framed capped surface, not merely a surface with unaccounted body intersections. [construct]

1.14 This changes the side heights (1,0) to (1,1), preserving the original bottom stage. All choices and moves were finite. The scorecard is: R/body(F) embedded and mutually disjoint throughout; T immersed auxiliary, intersecting W once; old caps arbitrary among themselves; after the Norman cancellations all old caps miss W; after the final tubing every new intersection lies among caps. The auxiliary T is discarded after its copies have been inserted into caps. No disjointness of its parallel copies is needed, because their intersections are allowed cap intersections. [construct]

1.15 **Section3: Finite bootstrap to height three.** The following finite construction and its limiting steps use the supplied framed data. [given, construct]

1.16 Here the two side heights count stages ABOVE the fixed bottom surface. Section2 gives (1,0) to (1,1). The following side extension, used only finitely often in this bootstrap, supplies (1,1) to (2,1) to (2,3). Let P be the side to be raised and Q its other side, of heights p,q at least one. Construct F transverse to the bottom surface of P by taking two normal parallels of Q and closing their rims with the normal annulus. It is sphere-like of height q. Its body is embedded, meets P only at the specified first-stage point, and misses all other body stages; intersections of its caps with the old caps are allowed. [construct]

1.17 First make a contracted parallel sphere T transverse to the bottom of P, using Section1 and pushing off all original cap sheets and the caps of F. Its only body intersection is that prescribed transverse point. To make the caps of F disjoint from the old P caps, push an F-cap sheet at each intersection down the old P cap to its attaching stage and then down successive P stages to its bottom. A finger across one attaching collar replaces one intersection with two on the immediately lower stage; this is two parallel paths around the two ends of that collar. After p stages there are finitely many points on the bottom of P. Tube them off over parallel copies of T. These changes affect only caps of F. They cannot reintroduce intersections with old P caps because T misses those caps and the finger paths on the old caps avoid the other finitely many double-point preimages. The new intersections are among caps of F and possibly the old Q caps, which are still caps. All old body stages are fixed. [construct]

1.18 Now for an intersection of an old P cap with a Q cap, push the Q-cap sheet down the P stages and tube it off over T. The Q cap is still an immersed disk; its boundary and framing are fixed. It may acquire self/intersections with other caps, which are allowed. It remains disjoint from the old P caps and all body stages: the punctured T used in the tubing has had its unique bottom-P intersection removed. Crucially T is also disjoint from the body of F, so the Q caps miss every body piece to be added in the next step. [construct]

1.19 Finally treat every self/mutual intersection among the old P caps by pushing one of its P sheets down the P stages and tubing it off over copies of the full F, rather than T. This replaces each old P cap by a capped grope: its first added stage is the old disk with punctured bodies of F attached, followed by the higher stages of those copies of F. Orient each added copy so its transverse intersection cancels the chosen point, and use the untwisted framed tubes. All old P-cap intersections have been removed; the added bodies are embedded and disjoint from each other because distinct body parallels of F are disjoint, and the tube paths avoid all other old preimages. The new caps are copies of F's caps. Those were made disjoint from ALL old P caps before this step, and properness makes them disjoint from F's parallel bodies. Consequently every new intersection is among caps, not on a new body. A cap with no intersections may be regarded as a genus-zero stage with no attached higher surface, as in the disk-like model. We have obtained side heights at least (p+q,q), with the previous body and its attaching framing preserved. The first and second steps are needed: merely asserting that tubing adds height would leave the old cap intersections on the new body. [construct]

1.20 All choices involve finite paths, parallels and normal widths in supplied framed blocks. Use the move first with p=q=1, then exchange the two sides and use p=1,q=2. Both sides then have height at least two, so the entire grope has height at least three. The induced integer labels on its finite plumbing generators are obtained by following their actual paths back to the original neighborhood; let B be the maximum of their absolute values. B exists without any growth estimate for this finite bootstrap. [construct]

1.21 **Section4: Efficient symmetric raising and the word bound.** The following finite construction and its limiting steps use the supplied framed data. [given, construct]

1.22 Once both sides have height at least two, there is an efficient variant which contracts only the TOP stage of a transverse grope. For the side P to be extended, form the transverse grope from the opposite side Q. Its height is that of Q. Contract its top stage and push the old caps off that contraction by Section1. The transverse grope now has height one less and its caps are disjoint from all old caps. Push each intersection on a P cap down the P stages, and at the bottom tube off over this transverse grope. The same body/cap scorecard as Section3 applies: new bodies consist of separate parallel copies of the old embedded opposite-side bodies, and all cap intersections have been removed from the new body before it is declared a body. This replaces each old P cap by a capped grope with the height of the contracted transverse grope. In terms of full path heights a,b INCLUDING the shared bottom, the updated path height is a+b−2. Alternate the sides. Starting with a_{−1}=a_0=3, the successive updated path heights obey a_n=a_{n−1}+a_{n−2}−2. The uniform height is the smaller of the two current path heights. Writing a_n−2 as a Fibonacci recursion gives, after 2k steps, uniform height at least 2^k: consecutive pairs increase the smaller Fibonacci term by at least a factor two. Additional initial height only improves the bound. [construct]

1.23 For clarity, the required estimate concerns WORDS, not numbers of double points. Base every old cap-point loop by a path in the old grope. Body-stage paths contribute no new plumbing generator. Contraction copies an old point without changing its generator. A push off a contraction creates the difference loop of Section1, a word of length at most two in the OLD cap-point generators. A new intersection among caps of the parallel transverse grope has length one, since its sheets are old parallel sheets. The only remaining type is a loop using a newly pushed-down finger: follow its first sheet across at most two old cap-point loops to the parallel transverse copy, cross its new cap intersection (one old generator), and return across at most two old loops on the other finger. The two bottom-stage paths cancel, as do the parallel collar paths. This is a concatenation of at most 2+1+2 old generators or inverses. These exhaust the new intersections: they occur on unchanged parallels, on a contraction push, or between two inserted fingers. Thus each step sends every NEW generator to a word of length at most five in the PRECEDING generators. Induction gives length at most 5^(2k) after 2k steps, and absolute Z label at most 5^(2k)B. No count of the possibly much larger number of points is substituted for this word bound. [construct]

1.24 **Section5: The special relative disk-to-disk operation in standardness.** The following finite construction and its limiting steps use the supplied framed data. [given, construct]

1.25 This is the operation of FQ4.6, with the actual relative data made explicit. Let f:B^4→B^4 be continuous and onto, with a homeomorphic boundary restriction. Its point-inverse collection is null (only finitely many fibres have diameter at least any positive epsilon), its singular IMAGE is nowhere dense, and E is a closed subset of the target containing the whole boundary. Suppose f:f^−1(E)→E is a homeomorphism. Then there is a homeomorphism h:B^4→B^4 equal to f on f^−1(E). Here E CONTAINS the boundary; it is not merely a subset of that boundary. The latter misreading would make the relative assertion too weak for the collared-tower application. [construct]

1.26 Use the following intermediate configuration. W is compact metrizable; e,f:B^4→W and g:W→B^4 are onto. The composites ge,gf are identity on the boundary. The fibres of g,ge,gf are null; their relevant singular images Sigma_g, g(Sigma_e),g(Sigma_f) are nowhere dense and pairwise well separated: neither set meets the closure of another. A protected closed set E_0 containing the boundary is disjoint from all three, and the relation e^−1 f is fixed there. Nullity makes each singular image countable (group the nontrivial fibres by diameter at least1/m). We will decrease both e- and f-fibre diameters while controlling the relations e^−1 f and f^−1 e between the TWO fixed domain balls. [construct]

1.27 Here is the local replacement. Choose a closed round ball B in the TARGET of g, disjoint from E_0,Sigma_g,g(Sigma_f), and a smaller concentric ball B_0 whose boundary misses g(Sigma_e). The restrictions over B for g and over the sphere boundary of B_0 for ge are homeomorphisms: they are continuous bijections of compact spaces to Hausdorff spaces. We require no open neighborhood of that sphere free of e singular values, which may accumulate on it. Replace g^−1(int B_0) in W by (ge)^−1(B_0), gluing their boundaries by e. The result is W'. On the inserted piece define e' to be the identity and g' to be ge; outside use the old e,g. These maps match at the gluing and satisfy g'e'=ge. The map e' is a homeomorphism over the inserted piece, so every e-fibre with g-image in B_0 has been eliminated. This is a finite compact adjunction, not a claim that W' is already a manifold. [construct]

1.28 To transfer f, take a homeomorphism w:B→B^4 fixed on B_0. Such a map exists by radial extension from the center of these convex balls. On g^−1 B define
H=g^−1 w^−1 g e w g, [construct]

1.29 where g^−1 is single-valued on B because B misses Sigma_g. Outside use the identity. On the boundary of B the definitions agree because ge is identity on the outer sphere. In the part whose image lies in g^−1 B_0, the first g^−1 w^−1 cancels the g before e; the remaining map e w g lifts to the inserted piece as w g. Thus H has a continuous lift H':W→W', and put f'=H'f. This gives f'=f outside the region with gf-image in B. On the inserted piece its formula is wgf. On the remaining annular region its fibres are the transported fibres of ge. Since gf is a homeomorphism over B, these new fibres are coordinate copies of a null family, so they remain null. The other fibres are unchanged. For g' the outside fibres are subsets of old g fibres, and the inside fibres are old ge fibres in the inserted compact piece. Both coordinate inclusions are uniformly continuous on their compact domains; a finite union of the corresponding null collections is null. For g'f', outside B nothing changes; in the inner piece it equals ge composed with the homeomorphism wgf, and in the annular piece it equals w^−1 ge wgf. Thus its nontrivial fibres are homeomorphic coordinate images of ge fibres. This is the actual nullity argument; composition of arbitrary null maps is not being asserted to preserve nullity. [construct]

1.30 The only possible failure of the separation condition is between the transported countable singular image and the retained image of ge. Choose w so these are well separated off B_0. This choice needs no unproved Baire assertion under the original countable-choice branch. Fix one radial reference map w_0:B→B^4. Enumerate the two countable singular sets. For each point condition require that its image avoid the CLOSED closure of the other singular set, and for each inverse-point condition require the analogous inverse avoidance. Each requirement is open and dense among homeomorphisms with the fixed inner/outer boundary data: a small coordinate ball around the free point lies off those fixed boundaries, and a compactly supported translation moves that point off the prescribed nowhere-dense closed set. Explicitly use x→x+rho(x)v with |v|Lip(rho)<1; the contraction inverse proves it is a homeomorphism. The required target complement is open dense, so a least satisfactory rational v in a fixed enumeration exists. At step m choose the translation and its inverse uniformly smaller than2^−m times every currently protected avoidance margin. Later changes then preserve all earlier requirements. Choose supports and rational radii from a fixed coordinate enumeration. The forward and inverse maps converge uniformly, and their limits are inverse by uniform continuity. The limit homeomorphism meets every avoidance requirement. This is a definable countable recursion with explicit finite rational searches, not dependent choice for arbitrary nonempty sets of homeomorphisms. It gives the required local configuration e',f',g'. [construct]

1.31 For the global iteration, first normalize the boundary: let e_0 be the radial cone on the given boundary homeomorphism, let W_0=B^4, g_0=e_0^−1 and f_0=f. Protect E_0=g_0(E). All conditions hold; the other singular sets are empty. Rescale the two fixed source metrics initially to diameter<1. Suppose all e_n- and f_n-fibres have diameter less than2^−n. There are only finitely many fibres of either map with diameter at least2^−n−1. Their g-images avoid Sigma_g; the separation condition makes the OTHER composite a local homeomorphism there. Around each large e-fibre choose a ball B, and then B_0, so that (ge)^−1 B has diameter less than2^−n while (gf)^−1 B has diameter less than2^−n−1. These choices follow from compactness: if inverse images of shrinking neighborhoods did not enter a prescribed neighborhood of a fibre, a convergent countersequence would give a point outside that fibre mapping to its center. Choose these finite balls disjoint and away from the protected set and the irrelevant singular images. Their boundary radii can avoid the countably many singular points. [construct]

1.32 Apply the local replacement. It kills the chosen large e-fibres, and all new f-fibres lie in the small (gf)^−1 B regions, hence have diameter less than2^−n−1. Reverse e and f and do the same to the remaining large f-fibres. The newly introduced e-fibres are now small by the analogous local-homeomorphism condition. The finite two-pass step therefore makes ALL fibres smaller than2^−n−1. It changes e^−1 f only within domains and ranges of diameter less than2^−n, and gives the same bound for the inverse relation. Make these bounds strictly summable, for instance use2^−n−3 after reindexing. On the protected set the relation stays e_0^−1 f_0 exactly. No source or target manifold classification is used in these replacements. [construct]

1.33 The relations R_n=e_n^−1 f_n and their inverses are thus uniformly Cauchy in the fibrewise Hausdorff distance. Their values have diameter tending to zero, so their limits are single-valued continuous maps R,S of the fixed balls. For completeness, continuity is not inferred from continuity of the set-valued R_n. Given x_j→x, fix n and select y_(n,j) in R_n(x_j) within the uniform tail bound of R(x_j). A subsequence of these selections converges to y_n in R_n(x), by compactness and the closed graph of R_n. The distance from y_n to R(x) is at most the tail bound plus diam R_n(x). Thus every subsequential limit of R(x_j) is within twice the tail bound plus diam R_n(x) of R(x). Letting n→infinity proves continuity. The identical argument applies to the inverse relations. Passing to the inverse graphs shows SR=RS=identity. Hence R is a homeomorphism. The fixed relation on the protected set yields R=e_0^−1 f on f^−1(E). Then h=e_0 R is the asserted homeomorphism agreeing with f there. [construct]

1.34 This proves the needed relative disk-to-disk replacement operation. It does NOT by itself prove that a tower is a disk: that application still needs the actual dual-handle/design/common-quotient construction, including the other approximation whose fibres are explicit disk-with-boundary-neighborhood pieces. No general4D cell-like approximation theorem is imported. [construct]

1.35 **Section6: From a null grope to the first actual tower story.** The following finite construction and its limiting steps use the supplied framed data. [given, construct]

1.36 This step has an exact input which the cyclic construction must supply: an actual properly immersed framed disk-like capped grope A of height at least three, whose entire image has trivial fundamental-group image in the smooth ambient four-manifold, together with framed geometric transverse spheres, each missing A except for its one prescribed bottom-stage point. It does not start with vanishing algebraic intersections alone. All modifications below take place in supplied plumbing charts and finitely many immersed disk charts; the ambient manifold is smooth at this stage. [construct]

1.37 First raise the height as far as needed by Section4, reserving one more full stage than the desired number of stages in the story. Because the raising maps factor through the previous grope neighborhood, its image remains group-null. Order the finitely many components of the extra top stage. Contract the first one, and push the cap sheets of every other component off that contraction by the ribbon Whitney disks of Section1. Contract the second and push all still uncontracted cap sheets off it; continue. The resulting grope caps are pairwise disjoint. They need not be embedded, but every self-intersection is part of the finger-pair model, with a specified Whitney disk. Here is the local pairing check: pushing a sheet off a contraction replaces its old cap intersection by the two parallel intersections with the opposite cap through the central square. Their signs are opposite, and the ribbon between the two parallel cap copies, continued along the chosen arc on the dual cap, is the Whitney disk. At an old cap self-intersection the four parallel-sheet intersections split into two such opposite-sign pairs. Order the pushes so each newly contracted component is protected from all later cap sheets. Distinct ribbon arcs are disjoint except at their prescribed central-square crossings; any interior crossings are among the disks which will be declared top caps, rather than intersections among the remaining grope caps. This is the actual disk-with-finger-pairs model, not the assertion that zero algebraic self-intersection supplies embedded Whitney disks. [construct]

1.38 For each finger pair choose an accessory circle: start at its chosen double point, follow one sheet to the Whitney arc and return on the other sheet. It is a loop in the image of the contracted grope. It is therefore nullhomotopic in the ambient manifold by the input nullness; no conclusion about the fundamental group of the complement is needed. A nullhomotopy relative to this fixed embedded boundary arc can be put in general position as an immersed disk with its prescribed boundary collar. The boundary collar is the accessory collar in the standard finger model.
