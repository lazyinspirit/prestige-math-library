categories 1–3. Every dependency below points to a previous ML pair or an
earlier category/item. There is no reverse dependency from categories 1–3 to
Category 4. In particular, ML owns its learning-specific optimization,
training, and online-decision claims; Bayesian computation’s generic
optimization and factorization interfaces are not made prerequisites here.
Examples companions contain worked examples and counterexamples only and are
never suppliers. An operational procedure is proposed as an `algorithm` item
with an `alg-` ID and a `## Definition`; a guarantee about that procedure is a
separate result item. Approximation and convergence theorems are mathematical
claims with their full topology, architecture, data, distribution, scaling,
and numerical assumptions. Experimental performance, including foundation
model scaling, remains empirical evidence and never supplies a theorem.

### ML-02 — Finite-class PAC learning and ERM guarantees

**Pages:** finite-class-pac-learning-and-erm / finite-class-pac-learning-and-erm-examples. **Scope:** Define realizable and agnostic PAC for binary classifiers and bounded loss. For finite H and iid observations in [0,1], state the uniform deviation R(h)≤R̂(h)+√((log|H|+log(2/δ))/(2m)) simultaneously for all h with probability 1−δ, and the exact-ERM excess bound twice the uniform deviation; an η-approximate ERM adds η. In the realizable binary case, when a target lies in H and the learner returns a consistent member, error is at most (log|H|+log(1/δ))/m with probability at least 1−δ.
