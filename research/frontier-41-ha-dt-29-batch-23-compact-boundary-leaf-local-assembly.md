Novikov Theorem 7.1, printed p.19, says a leaf A with nontrivial Π^j₁ on a compact manifold with a smooth orientable foliation “is compact and lies on the boundary of a connected component of the foliation.” The component is **not** an ordinary component of `M\A`: §1, Definition 1.4 and following paragraph, printed p.2, declare A≥B when a positive transverse segment runs from A to B, and identify A and B when A≥B and B≥A. Theorem 1.1(c), pp.3–4, makes the closure of a nontrivial such component a compact manifold with finitely many compact boundary leaves. Foliation orientation here means transverse orientation; the standing category is smooth.

The current complement conclusion is much weaker. The current strategy's requirement to “rule out additional boundary leaves” is unnecessary for this **particular** complement conclusion: the boundary of any component of `M\L` is a subset of L.

A richer source-aligned eventual formulation would assert that L is a boundary leaf of the compact closure of a nontrivial open saturated **transverse-accessibility component**, defining that notion explicitly. Its closure's manifold/finitely-many-boundary-leaves clause consumes §1 Theorem1.1, which still needs a C²/ACω local carrier. ## Lemma A: uniform short-time separation of compact leafwise sets

**Contract.** Let F be a C¹ codimension-one foliation of a Hausdorff smooth manifold, let `K_i⊂L_i`, i=1,…,m, be intrinsically compact subsets of finitely many leaves, and let X be a smooth transverse field near their ambient union K, with local flow φ. Then some η>0 satisfies
`φ_s(K)∩K=∅` for every `0<|s|<η`, with all indicated trajectories defined. The empty-set case is immediate. Duplicate leaves may first be grouped by finite union.

**Proof.** Each compact intrinsic K_i maps homeomorphically onto its ambient image: the inclusion is continuous and injective and the ambient manifold is Hausdorff. Finite local ODE neighborhoods give one common short interval for K.

For each a∈K_i choose an intrinsic relatively compact neighborhood W_a whose inclusion lies in a single plaque of a foliation box, shrinking its closure inside that box. Shrink the flow interval so φ_s(p) remains in the box for p∈closure(W_a), and so its transverse-coordinate derivative `dz_a(X)` keeps one strict sign. Therefore if p,q∈W_a, `z_a(φ_s(p))≠z_a(q)` for every sufficiently small nonzero s. In particular φ_s(p)≠q. Choose finitely many such W_a covering each K_i, retaining the minimum of their flow intervals.

In the finite disjoint union K* of the intrinsic K_i, remove from K*×K* the open union of the sets `(W_a∩K_i)×(W_a∩K_i)`. The remaining set Q is compact and contains no pair with equal ambient image: every diagonal pair lies in a removed set, and distinct leaves have disjoint images. On Q the continuous maps `(p,q,s)↦φ_s(p)` and `(p,q,s)↦q` are unequal at s=0. Their inequality is open because the ambient diagonal is closed. For each pair in Q choose a product neighborhood and a time interval of inequality; compactness gives finitely many, hence a single common interval. On the removed sets the transverse-coordinate argument already gives inequality. Taking the smaller of the two finite bounds proves the claim for K. ∎

This supplies Novikov Lemma7.1, printed p.20, including the finite-union clause, without a sequential compactness argument. It does **not** make an immersed loop into an embedding or supply leaf-synchronized fence levels.

## Lemma B: a noncompact leaf meets a closed transversal

**Contract.** Assume ACω. Let F be a C² cooriented codimension-one foliation of a compact boundaryless smooth manifold M, and L an intrinsically noncompact leaf. Then there is a positively transverse C² immersed circle meeting L. No embeddedness or arbitrary-neighborhood clause is asserted.

**Proof, finite plaque detection.** Choose finitely many C² foliation boxes U_i with coordinates `(-2,2)^(n−1)×(-2,2)`, whose smaller open cores V_i, coordinates `(-1,1)^(n−1)×(-1,1)`, cover M. If L met each V_i in only finitely many U_i-plaques, then the finitely many corresponding compact plaque squares `[-1,1]^(n−1)×{r}` would be intrinsically compact subsets of L whose union contains L. Their union would equal L and make it compact. Hence one V_i meets infinitely many plaques of L. In particular, on its central vertical interval choose two distinct plaque points x=(0,r_x) and y=(0,r_y) of L with r_x<r_y.

**Proof, positive perturbation of the leaf path.** Connectedness of L and its local Euclidean charts give a finite piecewise C² leafwise path from y to x. Smooth its finitely many corners intrinsically in convex leaf charts, giving a C² path γ:[0,1]→L with γ(0)=y and γ(1)=x. It may have stationary points; regularity of γ is unnecessary. Such corner smoothing uses finite local interpolation, not manifold-valued Whitney approximation.

Construct ω and X near the compact path as described above, with smooth local flow φ. For small a≥0 put `v(s,a)=ω_(φ_a(γ(s)))(Dφ_a(γ′(s)))`. Since γ is leafwise, v(s,0)=0. The C¹ form, smooth flow and compact parameter domain imply uniform bounds `|v(s,a)|≤A a` and `ω(X)≥b>0` there. Indeed ∂_a v is continuous on a fixed compact neighborhood, so the ordinary mean-value estimate gives A.

Choose B>A/b and δ>0, and set `a(s)=δ(e^(Bs)−1)/B`. Then a(0)=0, a′=Ba+δ. For δ small, the path `γ_δ(s)=φ_(a(s))(γ(s))` is defined and C², and
`ω(γ_δ′) = v(s,a(s))+a′(s)ω(X) ≥ −Aa+b(Ba+δ)>0`.
Thus it is positively transverse, starting at y and ending at x_δ near x. In the original box, the transverse coordinate of x_δ is strictly above r_x, because X is positive there; make δ so small that it remains below r_y and the tangential coordinates stay in the core. The straight box-coordinate segment from x_δ to y lies in that core and has strictly increasing transverse coordinate. Its image under the C² chart inverse is positively transverse. Together these paths form a closed piecewise C² positive transverse curve.

**Proof, seam smoothing and intersection.** At each of its two joins, take a small foliation box and a parameter interval on which both one-sided transverse-coordinate derivatives have a common positive lower bound c. In chart coordinates mollify the continuous piecewise C² curve with a nonnegative smooth kernel. The mollified transverse derivative is an average of derivatives and remains ≥c. Blend the original curve and its mollification using a smooth cutoff equal to one near the join and zero outside the chosen interval. The added transverse derivative is the cutoff derivative times the difference of the transverse coordinates, which tends uniformly to zero with mollifier radius. Choose the radius so its absolute value is <c/2. The resulting chart curve is C², still positively transverse, and equals the old curve on open end collars; finite gluing gives a C² immersed circle.

At the join y, before smoothing the incoming transverse coordinate lies below r_y and the outgoing one lies above r_y. Keep those interval endpoints fixed. After smoothing the coordinate remains strictly increasing and crosses r_y by the intermediate value theorem. Its crossing point lies in the local plaque through y, hence in L. Therefore the smoothed closed transversal still meets L. ∎

This supplies a precise C²/ACω version of Novikov §1 Lemma1.2, printed p.3, without his “obvious approximation.” Batch22 already has `lem-a-non-closed-leaf-of-a-codimension-one-foliation-meets-a-closed-transversal`, but that statement concerns a leaf **not closed as an ambient subset**, outputs an embedded circle, and is in the smooth setting. It cannot silently be substituted for this intrinsic-noncompact/C² contract. The draft deliberately proves only the immersed closed-transversal conclusion needed here.

## Lemma C: compact cooriented leaf has a one-sided complement collar

**Contract.** Let F be a C² cooriented codimension-one foliation of a Hausdorff smooth manifold and let L be an intrinsically compact leaf. There is a C² ambient neighborhood diffeomorphism `Ψ:L×(-ε,ε)→U`, with Ψ(p,0)=p and positively transverse parameter tracks. Each half collar belongs to a connected component C_± of `M\L`, and `∂C_±=L` as ambient sets. C_+ and C_- may be the same component. The collar is not asserted to be a **foliated product**.

**Proof.** The compact injective leaf inclusion is an embedding. Choose a smooth positive transverse field X near L and its local flow φ on a uniform time interval, and define Ψ(p,s)=φ_s(p). At s=0 its differential is invertible because `T_pL⊕R X(p)=T_pM`. For small uniform s it stays invertible by compactness; alternatively flow invariance of X transports the initial linear independence. The inverse function theorem and inverse differentiation give a C² local diffeomorphism.

Apply Lemma A with K=L, and shrink ε so the flow is defined for all compositions with |s−t|<2ε and that interval lies in the separation range. If Ψ(p,s)=Ψ(q,t), flow uniqueness gives φ_(s−t)(p)=q. Lemma A forces s=t; injectivity of φ_s then gives p=q. Thus Ψ is an injective local diffeomorphism, hence a C² diffeomorphism onto an open neighborhood U. Its only points in L have s=0, again by Lemma A.

L is connected, so each half collar is connected and lies in a single complement component C_±. Every point of L is a limit of points in either half collar; since L is disjoint from those components, `L⊂∂C_±`. Conversely, `M\L` is a manifold and locally connected, so its connected components are open in `M\L` and closed there. Since L is closed in M, a component has no boundary point outside L. Therefore `∂C_±⊂L`, proving equality. ∎

The components are saturated because any connected leaf disjoint from L lies entirely in a component of its complement. This establishes the target's exact complement conclusion once compactness is known. It does not give the transverse-accessibility component promised by Novikov or a sole-boundary Reeb region. General smooth collar theorems and full-AC surface classification are unnecessary.

## A further finite observation: saturation of a transversal is open

Given any C² transverse curve c and any point p on a leaf meeting c, join a crossing point to p by a finite leafwise path. A finite box chain along that path transports a small open interval of c to a local transverse interval through p. The union of the associated plaques in the terminal box is an open neighborhood of p, all of whose leaves meet c. Taking all such p proves that the leaf saturation Sat(c) is open. Only finite chart transport is used for each point; taking the union over all eligible paths is a set construction, not a choice of one path for each point.

Consequently, if leaves L'_ν with no closed transversal meet every sufficiently small neighborhood of a compact representative loop f in L, then no closed transversal can meet L: otherwise its open saturation contains L and hence the compact image of f; choose one such L'_ν meeting that saturation, and it necessarily meets the same transversal, contradiction.

This closes the original-leaf **compactness transfer** without assuming nearby compact leaves form a trivial product. Novikov's final p.25 product/diffeomorphism contradiction is needed for his richer identification of the accessibility-boundary component, not for the current compactness-plus-complement claim.
