## Statement

Let $E$ be the real span of a finite reduced crystallographic root system
$\Phi$, with chosen positive roots $\Phi^+$, simple roots
$\Delta=\{\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r\}$, Weyl group $W$ and root lattice
$Q=\sum_i\mathbb Z\alpha_i$. Extend the pairing complex linearly to
$E_\mathbb C$. For $\kappa\in E_\mathbb C$, call $\kappa$ antidominant if
$\langle\kappa,\alpha^\vee\rangle\notin\mathbb Z_{>0}$ for every
$\alpha\in\Phi^+$, and regular if
$\langle\kappa,\alpha^\vee\rangle\ne0$ for every root. Then
$$\kappa-w\kappa\notin Q_+\setminus\{0\}\qquad(w\in W)$$
for antidominant $\kappa$, and
$$\kappa-w\kappa\notin Q_+\qquad(w\ne1)$$
when $\kappa$ is also regular. Here
$Q_+=\sum_i\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\alpha_i$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite reduced crystallographic root system, a positive system,
its Weyl group and root lattice, and a complex weight $\kappa=a+ib$ with
$a,b\in E$.

[F1] The simple roots form a basis of $E$ and of $Q$ over $\mathbb Z$;
positive roots have nonnegative simple-root coordinates, and the positive and
negative root cones meet only at zero
([[def-finite-weyl-root-system-lattice-and-chamber-conventions]],
[[lem-finite-weyl-positive-roots-and-simple-reflections]]).

[F2] For any $b\in E$, its stabilizer $W_b$ is generated by the reflections
in roots perpendicular to $b$; these roots form a finite root subsystem
$\Phi_b$, and $Q\cap\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\Phi_b$ is its root
lattice $Q_b$ ([[lem-finite-weyl-closed-chambers-and-stabilizers]]).

[F3] For the dual root system, the affine Weyl group is
$Q\rtimes W$ acting by translations and finite Weyl transformations. Its
alcoves are simply transitive under this action, and the affine walls have
equations $\langle x,\alpha^\vee\rangle=m$ with $m\in\mathbb Z$
([[def-coroot-and-dual-root-system]],
[[def-root-lattice-coroot-lattice-weight-lattice-and-coweight-lattice]],
[[thm-cg-affine-alcove-transitivity-presentation-and-length]]).

[F4] In a finite crystallographic root system, a reduced word
$w=s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_m}$ has positive prefix roots
$s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_{j-1}}\alpha_{i_j}$ for every $j$
([[lem-finite-weyl-strong-exchange-and-deletion]],
[[lem-finite-weyl-positive-roots-and-simple-reflections]]).

## Proof

1.1 Suppose $q=\kappa-w\kappa\in Q$. Since $q$ is real, $b=wb$. Thus $w\in W_b$. Each reflection generating $W_b$ fixes the orthogonal complement of its root, so $a-wa\in\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\Phi_b$; also $q=a-wa$, hence $q\in Q_b$ by [F1]–[F2]. The affine transformation $t_qw$ of the dual affine Weyl group $Q_b\rtimes W_b$ fixes the orthogonal projection $a_b$ of $a$ to $E_b=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\Phi_b$. [F1, F2, F3, given]

2.1 In any locally finite affine root-wall arrangement, the stabilizer of a point $a_b$ is generated by the reflections in walls through that point. To see this, choose a ball about $a_b$ meeting no wall not containing $a_b$. Inside it, the finitely many hyperplanes through $a_b$ cut local chambers; generic line segments join any two local chambers by crossing one wall at a time and avoiding their codimension-two intersections. The corresponding wall reflections therefore act transitively on the local chambers. If an affine Weyl element fixes $a_b$, choose a local chamber $C$ and a product $h$ of these reflections carrying $C$ to its image. Then $h^{-1}g$ preserves the global alcove containing $C$, so it is the identity by the simple transitivity in [F3]. Hence $g=h$. Apply this to $g=t_qw$ from [step 1.1]. Every generating wall has a root $\alpha\in\Phi_b$ and $\langle a,\alpha^\vee\rangle\in\mathbb Z$; since $b$ is perpendicular to $\alpha$, also $\langle\kappa,\alpha^\vee\rangle\in\mathbb Z$. Taking finite parts shows that $w$ belongs to the subgroup $W_{\mathrm{int}}$ generated by the reflections $s_\alpha$ for roots with integral $\kappa$-pairing. [F2, F3, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 The set $\Phi_{\mathrm{int}}=\{\alpha\in\Phi: \langle\kappa,\alpha^\vee\rangle\in\mathbb Z\}$ is stable under its root reflections: if $m=\langle\kappa,\alpha^\vee\rangle\in\mathbb Z$ and $\beta\in\Phi_{\mathrm{int}}$, then $$\langle\kappa,(s_\alpha\beta)^\vee\rangle =\langle s_\alpha\kappa,\beta^\vee\rangle =\langle\kappa,\beta^\vee\rangle-m\langle\alpha,\beta^\vee\rangle\in\mathbb Z,$$ using crystallographic integrality. It is therefore a finite root system in its span, with positive system $\Phi_{\mathrm{int}}\cap\Phi^+$. For every $\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathrm{int}}\cap\Phi^+$, antidominance makes the integer $\langle\kappa,\alpha^\vee\rangle$ nonpositive. If $w=s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_m}$ is reduced in $W_{\mathrm{int}}$, telescoping gives $$\kappa-w\kappa=\sum_{j=1}^m\langle\kappa,\alpha_{i_j}^\vee\rangle s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_{j-1}}\alpha_{i_j}.$$ Every prefix root is positive in $\Phi_{\mathrm{int}}$ by [F4], so every term lies in the negative cone generated by positive roots of $\Phi_{\mathrm{int}}$, which is contained in $-Q_+$. Thus $q\in-Q_+$. [F1, F4, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Combining [step 1.1] and [step 3.1], an assumed $q\in Q_+\setminus\{0\}$ also lies in $-Q_+$, contradicting [F1]. This proves the antidominant assertion. If $\kappa$ is regular, every integral root pairing in [step 3.1] is strictly negative. For any nonidentity $w\in W_{\mathrm{int}}$, its reduced word has at least one term, and each term is a strictly negative multiple of a positive root; the sum is nonzero because $Q_+$ is pointed. Hence $q=0$ forces $w=1$, proving the regular assertion. [F1, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
