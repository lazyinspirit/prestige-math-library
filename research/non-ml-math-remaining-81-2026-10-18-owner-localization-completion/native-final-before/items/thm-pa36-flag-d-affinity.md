## Statement

Assume the flag-variety setup of
[[def-pa36-flag-tdo-and-module]]. If $\kappa$ is antidominant, then every
quasi-coherent left $\mathcal D_\kappa$-module $M$ satisfies
$$H^i(X,M)=0\qquad(i>0).$$
If $\kappa$ is also regular, then $\Gamma(X,M)=0$ implies $M=0$. The
antidominance and regularity conditions are independent hypotheses; regularity
alone does not imply the vanishing assertion.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, $X=G/B$, an ample line bundle
$\mathcal O_X(-\eta)$ for sufficiently dominant integral $\eta$, an
antidominant (and, for conservativity, regular) $\kappa$, and a quasi-coherent
left $\mathcal D_\kappa$-module $M$.

[F1] Every quasi-coherent $\mathcal O_X$-module is a filtered union of
coherent submodules, and cohomology commutes with those filtered colimits
([[lem-pa36-qcoh-filtered-union-and-cech-colimits]]).

[F2] For coherent $\mathcal F$ and ample $L$, there is an integer $n_0$
such that $H^i(X,\mathcal F\otimes L^{\otimes n})=0$ for every $i>0$ and
$n\ge n_0$ ([[thm-serre-vanishing]]).

[F3] For coherent $\mathcal F$ and ample $L$, the twist
$\mathcal F\otimes L^{\otimes n}$ is globally generated for all sufficiently
large $n$ ([[lem-eventual-global-generation-coherent-twists]],
[[def-globally-generated-sheaf]]).

[F4] The natural injection
$M\to M\otimes L\otimes V$ splits for antidominant $\kappa$, and the natural
surjection $M\otimes V^*\to M\otimes L$ splits for regular antidominant
$\kappa$, with $L=\mathcal O_X(-\eta)$ and
$V=\Gamma(X,L)^*$ ([[lem-pa36-ample-translation-splittings]]).

## Proof

1.1 Let $i>0$ and write $M=\varinjlim_j\mathcal F_j$ as the filtered union
of coherent $\mathcal O_X$-submodules from [F1]. Fix one $\mathcal F_j$.
Choose a sufficiently dominant integral $\eta$ so that
$H^i(X,\mathcal F_j\otimes L)=0$ by [F2], with $L=\mathcal O_X(-\eta)$.
The composite
$$H^i(X,\mathcal F_j)\to H^i(X,M)\to
H^i(X,M\otimes L\otimes V)$$
factors through $H^i(X,\mathcal F_j\otimes L\otimes V)$ by naturality of
the coevaluation map. This last group is
$H^i(X,\mathcal F_j\otimes L)\otimes V=0$ because $V$ is finite-dimensional.
By [F4], the map $H^i(X,M)\to H^i(X,M\otimes L\otimes V)$ is injective.
Thus $H^i(X,\mathcal F_j)\to H^i(X,M)$ is zero for every $j$. Taking the
filtered colimit and using [F1] gives $H^i(X,M)=0$. [F1, F2, F4]

2.1 Now assume $\kappa$ is regular antidominant and $\Gamma(X,M)=0$.
For any coherent $\mathcal F_j\subseteq M$, choose a sufficiently dominant
integral $\eta$ so that $\mathcal F_j\otimes L$ is globally generated by
[F3]. By the split surjection in [F4],
$\Gamma(X,M\otimes L)$ is a direct summand of
$\Gamma(X,M\otimes V^*)=\Gamma(X,M)\otimes V^*=0$; hence
$\Gamma(X,M\otimes L)=0$. The inclusion
$\mathcal F_j\otimes L\hookrightarrow M\otimes L$ remains injective on
global sections because $\Gamma$ is left exact, so
$\Gamma(X,\mathcal F_j\otimes L)=0$. A globally generated sheaf with no
global sections is zero, hence $\mathcal F_j=0$. All coherent subsheaves in
[F1] vanish, so $M=0$. This applies the splitting to $M$, not to the
arbitrary coherent $\mathcal O_X$-submodule. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1,
algebra] ∎
