## Statement

Let $\kappa$ be antidominant, not necessarily regular, and let
$$\Gamma=\Gamma_\kappa:\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal D_\kappa)
\longrightarrow A_\kappa\text{-}\mathbf{Mod},\qquad
K=\ker\Gamma.$$
Then $K$ is a Serre subcategory and the induced functors give an equivalence
of abelian categories
$$\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal D_\kappa)/K
\simeq A_\kappa\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}.$$
The quotient has representatives precisely among the quasicoherent
$\mathcal D_\kappa$-modules that are generated by global sections and have no
nonzero quasicoherent $\mathcal D_\kappa$-submodule with zero global sections.
No equivalence with the whole $\mathcal D_\kappa$-module category is asserted
without regularity.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Antidominant $\kappa$, the exact global-sections functor
$\Gamma_\kappa$, the localization functor and their adjunction.

[F1] $\Gamma_\kappa$ is exact for antidominant $\kappa$
([[thm-pa36-flag-d-affinity]]).

[F2] The adjunction unit $V\to\Gamma_\kappa\operatorname{Loc}_\kappa V$
is an isomorphism for every $A_\kappa$-module
([[lem-pa36-global-section-unit]]).

[F3] $\operatorname{Loc}_\kappa$ is left adjoint to $\Gamma_\kappa$
([[lem-pa36-localization-adjunction]]).

[F4] Global sections on $X$ commutes with filtered colimits of underlying
quasicoherent sheaves, and therefore with arbitrary direct sums
([[lem-pa36-qcoh-filtered-union-and-cech-colimits]]).

## Proof

1.1 Since $\Gamma_\kappa$ is exact, its kernel $K$ is closed under
subobjects, quotients and extensions; hence $K$ is a Serre subcategory.
The functor $\Gamma_\kappa$ therefore descends to the Serre quotient.
[F1, given]

2.1 Localization is right exact. For a short exact sequence
$0\to V'\to V\to V''\to0$ of $A_\kappa$-modules, applying localization
gives a right-exact sequence
$$\operatorname{Loc}V'\longrightarrow\operatorname{Loc}V
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Loc}V''\longrightarrow0.$$
Apply the exact functor $\Gamma_\kappa$ and use [F2]. The resulting sequence
is $0\to V'\to V\to V''\to0$. Therefore the kernel of
$\operatorname{Loc}V'\to\operatorname{Loc}V$ has zero global sections and
lies in $K$. Thus localization becomes exact after passage to the Serre
quotient. [F1, F2, step 1.1]

3.1 The unit is an isomorphism by [F2], so it remains an isomorphism in the
quotient. For any $M$, the triangular identity in [F3] shows that applying
$\Gamma_\kappa$ to the counit
$\operatorname{Loc}_\kappa\Gamma_\kappa M\to M$ gives an isomorphism. By
exactness [F1], the kernel and cokernel of the counit lie in $K$; hence the
counit is an isomorphism in the quotient. The descended unit and counit
prove the claimed equivalence. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1]

4.1 For an arbitrary $M$, let $E$ be the image of the counit
$\operatorname{Loc}_\kappa\Gamma M\to M$. By step 3.1 its cokernel has zero
global sections, so $E$ and $M$ define isomorphic objects of the Serre
quotient. The source is generated by its global sections, hence so is $E$.
Among the submodules of $E$ with zero global sections, take their sum $T$.
This is a set-indexed sum: a submodule sheaf is specified by a family of
subsets of the sets $E(U)$, one for each open $U\subseteq X$. The direct sum
of any such family is again a quasicoherent $\mathcal D_\kappa$-module, and
$\Gamma$ commutes with it by [F4]. Exactness of $\Gamma$ [F1] then gives
$\Gamma T=0$, so $T$ is the largest submodule of $E$ in $K$. The quotient
$E/T$ remains generated by global sections. If $S\subseteq E/T$ has
$\Gamma S=0$, its inverse image $P\subseteq E$ fits into
$0\to T\to P\to S\to0$; exactness [F1] gives $\Gamma P=0$, so $P\subseteq T$
by maximality and $S=0$. Thus $E/T$ is a representative of the same
quotient object, is generated by global sections, and has no nonzero
submodule in $K$. Conversely, every module with those two properties is by
definition such a representative. [F1, F4, step 3.1, construct] ∎
