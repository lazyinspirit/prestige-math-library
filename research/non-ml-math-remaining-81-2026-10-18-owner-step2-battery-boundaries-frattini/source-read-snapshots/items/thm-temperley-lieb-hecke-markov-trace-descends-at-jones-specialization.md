## Statement

Let $\ell\ge3$, $q=e^{\pi i/\ell}$, $v=q^2$, $\delta=q+q^{-1}$ and $z_0=-1/(v+1)$. Specialize the type-A Hecke tower at $v,z=z_0$. For each $n$, let $J_n$ be the two-sided ideal generated by the six-term parabolic sums

$$E_i=1+T_i+T_{i+1}+T_iT_{i+1}+T_{i+1}T_i+T_iT_{i+1}T_i\qquad(1\le i\le n-2).$$

The assignment $T_i\mapsto qU_i-1$ defines a surjective tower map $\phi_n:H(n)\to\mathrm{TL}_n(\mathbb C,\delta)$ that kills $J_n$. The specialized Ocneanu Markov trace therefore vanishes on $J_n$ and descends to $H(n)/J_n$. Under the induced isomorphism with the diagram algebra presentation, the descended trace is the normalized closure trace $\tau_n=\delta^{-n}\operatorname{Tr}_n$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $\ell\ge3$, $q=e^{\pi i/\ell}$, the Hecke parameter $v=q^2$, the tower trace parameter $z_0=-1/(q^2+1)$, and the planar convention $U_i^2=\delta U_i$, $U_iU_{i+1}U_i=U_i$.

[F1] The generic type-A Hecke generators obey $T_i^2=(v-1)T_i+v$, the braid relation, and distant commutation ([[def-generic-type-a-hecke-algebra]]).

[F2] $\operatorname{tr}$ is cyclic, restricts along the tower, and satisfies $\operatorname{tr}_{n+1}(xT_n)=z\operatorname{tr}_n(x)$; its recursive proof on the free tower basis works after any scalar specialization ([[def-markov-trace-on-the-type-a-hecke-tower]], [[thm-the-ocneanu-markov-trace-exists-and-is-unique]]).

[F3] For $n\ge0$, the diagram closure has $\tau_n(1_n)=1$ and $\tau_{n+1}(x\otimes1)=\tau_n(x)$. For $n\ge1$, it also has $\tau_{n+1}((x\otimes1)U_n)=\delta^{-1}\tau_n(x)$ because $\delta\ne0$ ([[def-temperley-lieb-closure-trace-and-partial-trace]], [[lem-temperley-lieb-closure-trace-component-identities]]).

[F4] The diagram algebra has the presentation on generators $U_i$ with relations $U_i^2=\delta U_i$, $U_iU_{i\pm1}U_i=U_i$, and distant commutation; its generator presentation is isomorphic to the planar diagram algebra over every commutative ring ([[def-temperley-lieb-generator-presentation]], [[thm-temperley-lieb-diagram-generators-span]], [[thm-temperley-lieb-presentation-isomorphism]]).

[F5] The Hecke tower is free over its preceding rank, with $H(n+1)=H(n)\oplus H(n)T_nH(n)$ as an $H(n)$-bimodule, and the standard Hecke basis persists after scalar specialization ([[lem-the-hecke-tower-is-free-over-the-previous-level]], [[thm-standard-basis-of-the-generic-type-a-hecke-algebra]]).

## Proof

1.1 Since $v+1=q^2+1=q\delta\ne0$, the assignment $T_i\mapsto qU_i-1$ is defined. Its quadratic relation follows from $(qU_i-1-v)(qU_i)=q^2(U_i-\delta)U_i=0$, using $v=q^2$ and $U_i^2=\delta U_i$. For adjacent generators, expanding both braid words gives difference $q^3(U_iU_{i+1}U_i-U_{i+1}U_iU_{i+1})-q^2\delta(U_i-U_{i+1})+q(U_i-U_{i+1})=0$, since $q^2\delta=q^3+q$. Distant commutation is inherited, so the assignment defines a tower homomorphism; it is surjective by [F4]. [F1, F4, given, algebra]

2.1 Put $E_i=\sum_{w\in\langle s_i,s_{i+1}\rangle}T_w$, the six-term expression in the Statement. Expanding with the Hecke quadratic and braid relations gives $(T_i+1)(T_{i+1}+1)(T_i+1)=E_i+v(T_i+1)$. Under $T_j+1=qU_j$, the left side maps to $q^3U_iU_{i+1}U_i=q^3U_i$ and the second term maps to $q^3U_i$, so $\phi_n(E_i)=0$. Since $J_n$ is the two-sided ideal generated by the $E_i$, $\phi_n(J_n)=0$ and $\phi_n$ factors through $H(n)/J_n$. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 The pullback $\widetilde\tau_n:=\tau_n\circ\phi_n$ is cyclic and has $\widetilde\tau_n(1)=1$ by [F3]. For $n\ge1$ and $x\in H(n)$, its image in $\mathrm{TL}_{n+1}$ is $\phi_n(x)\otimes1$, and $T_n$ maps to $qU_n-1$, so $\widetilde\tau_{n+1}(xT_n)=(q/\delta-1)\tau_n(\phi_n(x))=-\widetilde\tau_n(x)/(q\delta)=-\widetilde\tau_n(x)/(q^2+1)=z_0\widetilde\tau_n(x)$. The tower restriction rule follows from [F3]; hence $\widetilde\tau$ satisfies the specialized Markov rules. [F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The presentation [F4] gives $\psi_n:\mathrm{TL}_n\to H(n)/J_n$ by $U_i\mapsto q^{-1}(T_i+1)$. The Hecke quadratic relation gives $U_i^2=\delta U_i$ for these images, while step 2.1 in the quotient gives $(T_i+1)(T_{i+1}+1)(T_i+1)=q^2(T_i+1)$ and hence $U_iU_{i+1}U_i=U_i$; the other adjacent relation follows by interchanging indices and distant commutation is inherited. Thus $\psi_n$ is well defined. Its composites with the map induced by $\phi_n$ fix each generator: $U_i\mapsto q^{-1}(qU_i-1+1)=U_i$ and $T_i\mapsto q(q^{-1}(T_i+1))-1=T_i$. Therefore $H(n)/J_n\cong\mathrm{TL}_n$, proving the quotient isomorphism. [F1, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The decomposition in [F5] writes every element of $H(n+1)$ as a sum of an element of $H(n)$ and terms $xT_ny$ with $x,y\in H(n)$. For any Markov trace, restriction determines the first term; cyclicity and (M4) force $\operatorname{tr}_{n+1}(xT_ny)=z\operatorname{tr}_n(xy)$ on each remaining term. Induction from $H(1)=\mathbb C$ therefore shows at most one specialized trace can satisfy (M1)--(M4), even when $v,z$ have been specialized: this uses only the free decomposition [F5], not division by a parameter. By step 2.2 the pullback $\widetilde\tau$ is such a trace, so it is the specialized Ocneanu trace. Since $\phi_n(J_n)=0$, the trace vanishes on all of the two-sided ideal $J_n$. The quotient isomorphism of step 3.1 identifies the descended functional with the normalized closure trace $\tau_n$. [F2, F5, step 2.2, step 3.1, induction, algebra] ∎
