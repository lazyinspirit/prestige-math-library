This page develops two-sample order-$(r,r)$ U-statistics, their first projections, and second-moment control of the higher-order remainder. A finite permutation variance estimate strengthens the exact triangular-mixture coupling and proves the conditional permutation CLT under the weak parameter null, block antisymmetry, mixed second moments and positive pooled projection variance. No assignment-wise centering or sample-fraction convergence rate is required. The all-assignment centered theorems remain useful special cases.

The studentized pivot divides by the estimated standard error $r\{\widehat\sigma_m^2/m+\widehat\sigma_n^2/n\}^{1/2}$; that ratio itself has the standard-normal limit. General joint variance estimates require direct permutation consistency. For estimates depending on their own group alone, IID triangular-mixture consistency is sufficient by the composition-count transfer lemma. The finite-support rank example verifies permutation consistency of its joint estimates directly.

When $P=Q$, finite-group invariance gives finite-sample exactness for every measurable studentized statistic, with the stated tie conventions. That exactness does not establish validity of unstudentized Wilcoxon statistics under a weak mean or median null.

The explicit finite-enumeration test recomputes both variance estimates on every permuted split and supplies inclusive or tie-randomized p-values. The heterogeneous-law Wilcoxon specialization uses tie-corrected pairwise comparisons, verifies its empirical projection variance estimates under both the true samples and permutation, and tests the probabilistic-index null. It does not replace that null with equality of means or medians.

## Objective

This page separates the blank decision threshold $L_C$, the concentration

for the known-blank, equal-variance model, with the normal quantile and

## Scope and limitations

The formulas depend on the response law and calibration convention. An
estimated blank changes the variance used for the calculation; concentration-
dependent variance can change or eliminate a finite detection or
quantification limit. The page makes no universal claim for estimated-blank,
heteroscedastic, discrete, or empirically unvalidated procedures. A report
that a value lies below a limit is a censored observation and requires its
measurement likelihood.

The Benjamini–Yekutieli (BY) step-up rule uses critical values $kq/(mH_m)$, where $H_m=\sum_{j=1}^m1/j$. For any finite family of valid p-values, its FDR is at most $(m_0/m)q$ under arbitrary dependence. The proof sums each true-null rejection contribution over the possible rejection counts and applies only marginal superuniformity.

Ordinary Benjamini–Hochberg (BH) uses critical values $kq/m$; these equal the BY cutoffs when $m=1$ and are larger when $m>1$. Its separate guarantee here assumes that the p-value vector is positive regression dependent on the true-null coordinates (PRDS): conditional probabilities of all increasing Borel sets must have nondecreasing versions as each true-null p-value increases. Under that exact condition, BH also has FDR at most $(m_0/m)q$. PRDS is a distributional condition; pairwise positive covariance alone does not verify it, and two-sided Gaussian tests have no blanket PRDS guarantee. See the companion examples for a discrete independent threshold comparison and a counterexample to the pairwise-correlation shortcut.


The two arrows label the Bob and Eve marginals of the joint law $W(y,z\mid x)$. They do not assert that $Y$ and $Z$ are conditionally independent given $X$. The block law is the coordinate product of this joint kernel. A code uses a uniform message, private encoder randomness and no feedback or preshared secret key; its public description is available to Eve.

Information leakage and equivocation are defined in [[def-it-information-leakage-and-equivocation]]. Weak secrecy requires $I(M;Z^n)/n\to0$, while strong secrecy requires $I(M;Z^n)\to0$, as in [[def-it-weak-and-strong-secrecy]]. Strong secrecy implies weak secrecy, but the converse fails; the companion page gives a noiseless example. Capacity here is defined operationally for both average and maximal error, and for both secrecy criteria. The local finite information-density lemma supports fixed-auxiliary threshold decoding, while the resolvability lemma makes Eve's induced output law approach a product target in expected KL and total variation. The capacity theorem proves the same auxiliary formula for all four capacities:

$$\max_{P_{U,X}:\,U-X-(Y,Z)}\bigl[I(U;Y)-I(U;Z)\bigr],\qquad |\mathcal U|\le|\mathcal X|.$$

For a degraded channel in the sense of [[def-it-degraded-wiretap-channel]], Eve's output is generated from Bob's by a finite stochastic kernel, the auxiliary reduces to $U=X$, and the formula becomes $\max_{P_X}[I(X;Y)-I(X;Z)]$.

For finite source privacy in [[def-it-finite-source-privacy-utility-region]], a release kernel gives the factorization $P_{SXZ}=P_{SX}K_{Z\mid X}$:

```tikzcd
\begin{tikzcd}
S \arrow[r] & X \arrow[r, "K"] & Z
\end{tikzcd}
```

The privacy–utility coordinates are $(I(S;Z),I(X;Z))$: the first measures leakage about sensitive data and the second measures retained information about useful data. Perfect privacy is exactly equality of the conditional output laws across positive-support sensitive values, by [[thm-it-perfect-privacy-kernel-criterion]]. This criterion is a kernel condition and does not impose a distortion or rate constraint.

Universal hashing is treated separately by [[thm-it-universal-hashing-leftover-bound]] as a total-variation guarantee with the public seed included alongside Eve's side information. For conditional guessing probability $g(X\mid Z)$ and an $M$-valued two-universal hash, the bound is $\operatorname{TV}\le\tfrac12\sqrt{M g(X\mid Z)}$; in particular, $M g(X\mid Z)\le4\varepsilon^2$ guarantees distance at most $\varepsilon$. This bound does not by itself state an unnormalized mutual-information guarantee.

Step 2.1 gives $P_0(\{c_-\})=0$, and its density then gives equality $P_0$-almost surely, proving uniqueness up to $P_0$-almost-sure equality of rejection functions against $\theta=-1$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, F4, F5, F7, F10, F11, F12, algebra]

100: 4.1 If a UMP test $\psi^*$ existed, its optimality at $\theta=1$ and step 3.1 would force $\psi^*=\phi_+$ $P_0$-almost surely, while its optimality at $\theta=-1$ and step 3.2 would force $\psi^*=\phi_-$ $P_0$-almost surely. Choose $x_0>\max(c_+,c_-)$; positivity and continuity of $p_0$ give a nondegenerate interval above both cutoffs on which $p_0$ is bounded below by a positive constant. By [F6] and the countable choice in step 2.1, that interval has positive Lebesgue and hence positive $P_0$ measure by [F4] and [F7], but $\phi_+=1$ and $\phi_-=0$ there, contradicting the two almost-sure equalities.
