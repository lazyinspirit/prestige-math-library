**Objective.** This companion page records the two boundary phenomena of the
incidence construction of [[quiver-full-flag-projective-semisimplicity]]. The
first example takes the loopless quiver with one vertex and no arrows and
$\dim V=n\ge2$: the representation space is a point, the incidence is the full
flag variety of dimension $n(n-1)/2$, and the defect
$2\dim(\text{fiber})+\dim(\text{image})-\dim X_\nu$ of the proper incidence map
equals $n(n-1)/2$, so the map is not semismall; for $n=0,1$ the flag space is a
point and the defect vanishes, exhibiting the exact boundary. The second
example fixes two vertices with $h\ge0$ parallel arrows between them and
one-dimensional vertex spaces: the stable incidence is the point $x=0$ for the
word $(i,j)$ and all of $\mathbb C^h$ for the word $(j,i)$, and the two
line-projective compactifications are the point $[0:1]$ inside $\mathbb P^h$
and the identity of $\mathbb P^h$, so the whole multiplicity range including
$h=0$ is visible in one computation.
