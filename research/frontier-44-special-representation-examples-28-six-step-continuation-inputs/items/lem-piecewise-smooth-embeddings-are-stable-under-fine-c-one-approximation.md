## Statement

Let $K$ be finite and $f:K\to\mathbb R^q$ a whole-star smooth immersion in the
sense of [[def-relative-smooth-triangulation-and-projection-preserving-fitting]].
There is $\delta>0$ such that every strong piecewise $C^1$
$\delta$-approximation $g:K'\to\mathbb R^q$, on any finite subdivision $K'$,
is a whole-star immersion. If $f$ is also an embedding, $\delta$ may be chosen
so that $g$ is an embedding. The subdivision need not have a mesh or shape
bound.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite geometric simplicial complex $K$ with Euclidean realization in some $\mathbb R^N$, a whole-star smooth immersion $f:K\to\mathbb R^q$, a finite subdivision $K'$ of $K$ and a strong piecewise $C^1$ $\delta$-approximation $g:K'\to\mathbb R^q$ ([[def-relative-smooth-triangulation-and-projection-preserving-fitting]]).

[F1] Whole-star condition and approximation contract: $f$ is piecewise smooth with $df_b$ injective on the tangent star at every $b$, the restriction of $f$ to each closed simplex is smooth, and $g$ is piecewise smooth on $K'$ with $\sup_b|g(b)-f(b)|<\delta$ and $|dg_b(v)-df_b(v)|<\delta|v|$ for every $b$ and every tangent-star vector $v$ ([[def-relative-smooth-triangulation-and-projection-preserving-fitting]]). In particular $g$ is continuous, and the differentials on the tangent cones agree on common faces and vary continuously with the base point.

[F2] Compactness and minima ([[thm-heine-borel-rn]], [[thm-extreme-value-metric]], [[lem-closed-subset-of-a-compact-space-is-compact]]): a subset of $\mathbb R^m$, $m\ge1$, is compact exactly when it is closed and bounded; closed simplices, unit spheres in linear subspaces, and products of two such closed bounded sets are closed and bounded in the relevant $\mathbb R^m$, hence compact, so every continuous real function on them attains a minimum; the realization $|K|$, its square $|K|^2$ (a closed bounded subset of $\mathbb R^{2N}$) and closed subsets of these are compact; and a continuous bijection from a compact metric space onto a metric space is a homeomorphism onto its image ([[thm-continuous-bijection-from-a-compact-space-has-continuous-inverse]]).

[F3] Vector fundamental theorem of calculus ([[cor-vector-valued-ftc-and-lipschitz-bound]]): if $\varphi:[0,1]\to\mathbb R^q$ is $C^1$, then $\varphi(1)-\varphi(0)=\int_0^1\varphi'$. It applies to $s\mapsto G(c+s h)$ along a segment $[c,c+h]$ lying in a single simplex on which $G$ is $C^1$.

[F4] Angle arithmetic: for nonzero vectors $V,W,W'$ one has $\operatorname{angle}(V,W')\le\operatorname{angle}(V,W)+\operatorname{angle}(W,W')$; and if $|V|\ge\mu|v|$ while $|W-V|<\delta|v|$ with $\delta<\mu/2$, then $\operatorname{angle}(V,W)\le4\delta/\mu$. Both are elementary estimates for the Euclidean angle of nonzero vectors.

[F5] Disjoint faces have disjoint affine hulls: if $\eta,\tau$ are disjoint faces of a simplex with affinely independent vertices, then $\operatorname{aff}(\eta)\cap\operatorname{aff}(\tau)=\varnothing$; indeed an equality $\sum_i\lambda_i v_i=\sum_j\mu_j w_j$ of affine combinations with $\sum_i\lambda_i=\sum_j\mu_j=1$ is a vanishing affine relation among the affinely independent vertices, forcing all coefficients to vanish and contradicting both sums being $1$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Full rank and a uniform lower bound. If $K$ has no positive-dimensional simplex, then its tangent stars are $\{0\}$, every $g$ is trivially a whole-star immersion, and the embedding assertion follows from the finite separation of the values of $f$ at the finitely many vertices; assume therefore that some simplex has positive dimension. For a simplex $\sigma$ and $b\in\sigma$, the tangent cone $\sigma-b$ spans the direction space $D_\sigma:=\operatorname{span}\{x-y:x,y\in\sigma\}$, so injectivity of $df_b$ on that cone gives injectivity on $D_\sigma$: if $df_b(w)=0$ for $w\in D_\sigma$, write $w=w_1-w_2$ with $w_i\in\sigma-b$ and conclude $df_b(w_1)=df_b(w_2)$, whence $w_1=w_2$ and $w=0$. The map $(b,v)\mapsto|D(f|\sigma)(b)v|$ is continuous and positive on the compact set of pairs with $b\in\sigma$ and $v$ a unit vector of $D_\sigma$ ([F2] applied to the closed bounded set $\sigma\times\{v:|v|=1\}$), so it has a positive minimum $\mu_\sigma$; set $\mu:=\min_\sigma\mu_\sigma>0$ over the finitely many positive-dimensional simplices. Then $|df_b(v)|\ge\mu|v|$ for every tangent-star vector $v$, and if $\delta<\mu/2$ the approximation estimate of [F1] gives $|dg_b(v)|\ge(\mu-\delta)|v|>0$ together with $\operatorname{angle}(df_b(v),dg_b(v))\le4\delta/\mu$ for every $v\ne0$ ([F1], [F4]); in particular every $g$ with $\delta<\mu/2$ is non-degenerate on every simplex cone. [F1, F2, F4, algebra]

1.2 Separation of two simplices meeting in a common face. Let $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$ be simplices with $\tau=\sigma_1\cap\sigma_2$ a common face that is proper in both, let $\eta$ be the face of $\sigma_1$ opposite $\tau$ and let $c$ be the barycenter of $\tau$. Every $p\in\sigma_1\setminus\tau$ has a unique expression $p=(1-t)x+ty$ with $x\in\tau$, $y\in\eta$ and $t\in(0,1]$; the point $X(p):=(1-t)x+tc$ lies in $\tau$, satisfies $p-X(p)=t(y-c)$ and is a continuous function of $p$ fixing $\tau$ (at $t=1$ the formula gives $X(p)=c$, the limit of $(1-t)x+tc$). Define $\theta(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)$ as the minimum of $\operatorname{angle}(y-c,z-c)$ over $y\in\eta$ and $z\in\sigma_2\setminus\{c\}$. This minimum is positive: the unit vectors $(y-c)/|y-c|$ and $(z-c)/|z-c|$ range over compact subsets of the unit sphere (the second is the set of unit directions in the polyhedral cone $\sigma_2-c$), the angle is continuous there, and an angle $0$ would give $y-c=\lambda(z-c)$ with $\lambda>0$; if $\lambda\le1$, then $y=(1-\lambda)c+\lambda z\in\sigma_2$, so $y\in\sigma_1\cap\sigma_2=\tau$, contradicting $y\in\eta$ with $\eta\cap\tau=\varnothing$, while if $\lambda>1$, then $z=(1-\lambda^{-1})c+\lambda^{-1}y\in\sigma_1$, so $z\in\tau$ and $y\in\operatorname{aff}(\tau)\cap\eta$, contradicting [F5]. Moreover, for every $p\in\sigma_1\setminus\tau$ and every $q\in\sigma_2\setminus\{X(p)\}$ the angle between $p-X(p)$ and $q-X(p)$ is at least $\theta(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)$: indeed $p-X(p)$ is a positive multiple of $y-c$ for the corresponding $y\in\eta$, while $q-X(p)=(q-c)+(c-X(p))$ lies in the cone $\sigma_2-c$, because $q$ and $X(p)$ both lie in $\sigma_2$ and every nonzero vector of that cone is a positive multiple of some $z-c$ with $z\in\sigma_2\setminus\{c\}$. [F2, F5, algebra]

1.3 Two-segment lemma. Let $L=e_1\cup e_2$ be two closed segments with a common vertex $v$, let $F$ be linear and injective on $L$, let the images $F(e_1)$ and $F(e_2)$ make an angle $\beta\in(0,\pi]$, and let $G$ be $C^1$ on each segment with $\operatorname{angle}(DG(c)\tau_i,F(\tau_i))<\beta/2$ at every $c\in e_i\setminus\{v\}$, where $\tau_i$ is the unit tangent of $e_i$ pointing away from $v$. Then $G$ is injective on $L$. Proof: let $\ell$ be the line of the plane of $F(L)$ perpendicular to the internal bisector of $\beta$ when $\beta<\pi$, and the line containing $F(L)$ when $\beta=\pi$; let $\pi_\ell$ be the orthogonal projection onto $\ell$. With $\tau_i$ as above, $\pi_\ell(F(\tau_1))>0>\pi_\ell(F(\tau_2))$: for $\beta<\pi$ these numbers are $\pm|F(\tau_i)|\sin(\beta/2)$ in the unit normal direction of the bisector, and for $\beta=\pi$ the two image directions are opposite, so the projections are nonzero with opposite signs. For $c=v+s\tau_1\in e_1$ with $s>0$, the directional derivative of $G$ along $\tau_1$ at $c$ is $DG(c)\tau_1$, and $\operatorname{angle}(DG(c)\tau_1,\ell)\le\operatorname{angle}(F(\tau_1),\ell)+\operatorname{angle}(DG(c)\tau_1,F(\tau_1))<(\pi/2-\beta/2)+\beta/2=\pi/2$ when $\beta<\pi$, while for $\beta=\pi$ it is at most $0+\pi/2$, again strictly below $\pi/2$; hence $\pi_\ell(DG(c)\tau_1)>0$ and, by [F3], $\pi_\ell(G(c))>\pi_\ell(G(v))$ for all $c\in e_1\setminus\{v\}$. Symmetrically $\pi_\ell(G(c))<\pi_\ell(G(v))$ for all $c\in e_2\setminus\{v\}$, so $G$ carries the two open segments into opposite open half-lines of $\ell$; on each single segment $\pi_\ell\circ G$ is strictly monotone, and therefore $G$ is injective on $L$. [F3, F4, algebra]

2.1 Naturality of the projection under linear injections. Every linear injection $A$ defined on $\operatorname{aff}(\sigma_1\cup\sigma_2)$ maps $\sigma_i$ onto a simplex $A(\sigma_i)$ with intersection $A(\tau)$ and carries the projection of $\sigma_1$ onto $\tau$ to the projection of $A(\sigma_1)$ onto $A(\tau)$: the defining condition $p-X(p)\parallel y-c$ becomes $A(p)-A(X(p))\parallel A(y)-A(c)$, and $A(c)$ is the barycenter of $A(\tau)$. Consequently, for the pair $(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)$, every $b\in\tau$ and all $p\in\sigma_1\setminus\tau$, $q\in\sigma_2\setminus\{X(p)\}$ satisfy $\operatorname{angle}(A(p-X(p)),A(q-X(p)))\ge\theta(A(\sigma_1),A(\sigma_2))$, by step 1.2 applied to the image simplices. [step 1.2, algebra]

2.2 Choice of $\delta$ and local angle consequences. Take $\delta>0$ so small that $\delta<\mu/2$ and $4\delta/\mu<\alpha/8$, with $\mu$ from step 1.1, and in addition so small that for every $b\in|K|$ there is a neighbourhood $U_b$ on which the derivative fields vary by less than $\alpha/8$: by continuity of the finitely many maps $(c,h)\mapsto D(f|\sigma)(c)h$ and $(c,h)\mapsto D(g|\sigma')(c)h$ on compacta, one may require $\operatorname{angle}(Df(c)h,Df(b)h)<\alpha/8$ and $\operatorname{angle}(Dg(c)h,Dg(b)h)<\alpha/8$ whenever $c\in U_b$ and $h$ is a nonzero vector of a tangent cone at $c$ in a simplex containing both $b$ and $c$ ([F1], [F2], step 1.1). Then for such $c,b,h$ the triangle inequality gives $\operatorname{angle}(Dg(c)h,Df(b)h)\le\operatorname{angle}(Dg(c)h,Df(c)h)+\operatorname{angle}(Df(c)h,Df(b)h)<\alpha/8+\alpha/8=\alpha/4$, and likewise $\operatorname{angle}(Dg(b)h,Df(b)h)<\alpha/8+\alpha/8+\alpha/8=3\alpha/8$; both are strictly below $\alpha/2$. [F4, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Uniform finite-star separation angle. For each pair $(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)$ with $\tau=\sigma_1\cap\sigma_2$ a proper face of both and for each $b\in\tau$, the differential $df_b$ is injective on each of the cones $\sigma_1-b$, $\sigma_2-b$, hence injective on $\sigma_1\cup\sigma_2$, so step 2.1 applies with $A=df_b$ and $b\in\tau$. Define $\Theta_{\sigma_1\sigma_2}(b):=\theta(df_b(\sigma_1),df_b(\sigma_2))$. It is positive for every $b\in\tau$: expanding the definition, it is the minimum of $\operatorname{angle}(df_b(u_1),df_b(u_2))$ over $u_1$ in the compact set of unit directions of the cone $\sigma_1-b$ parallel to $\eta$ and $u_2$ in the compact set of unit directions of the cone $\sigma_2-c$, a pointwise positive continuous function on a compact product, whose minimum is therefore positive ([F2]); positivity rather than vanishing comes from injectivity of $df_b$ on each cone together with the fact that the two unit directions cannot agree: a common direction would lie in both cones, hence in the cone of $\tau-b$, forcing the corresponding $y\in\eta$ into $\operatorname{aff}(\tau)$ and contradicting [F5] as in step 1.2. Taking the minimum of $\Theta_{\sigma_1\sigma_2}(b)$ over the finitely many pairs and all $b\in\tau$ gives a positive number $\alpha$, and replacing it by $\min(\alpha,\pi/2)$ we may assume $0<\alpha\le\pi/2$; by steps 1.2 and 2.1, $\operatorname{angle}(df_b(p-X(p)),df_b(q-X(p)))\ge\alpha$ for all such $b,p,q$. [F2, F5, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Stability of immersion. Let $b\in|K|$ and suppose $dg_b$ is not injective on the tangent star at $b$: there are nonzero tangent-star vectors $u\ne v$ with $dg_b(u)=dg_b(v)$. Multiplying both by any $\lambda\in(0,1]$ keeps the equality, so we may assume $p:=b+u$ and $q:=b+v$ are distinct points of the star of $b$ lying in a prescribed small neighbourhood of $b$. If the carriers of $p$ and $q$ are comparable, both lie in one simplex $\sigma$; let $x:=(p+q)/2\in\sigma$ be their midpoint and apply step 1.3 to $L=[x,p]\cup[x,q]$, $F=df_b$, $G=dg_b$ and $\beta=\pi$: $F$ is injective on $L$, and the required angle bound $\operatorname{angle}(dg_b(\tau_i),df_b(\tau_i))<3\alpha/8<\pi/2$ holds by step 2.2, because the two segment directions are tangent-cone vectors at the points of their segments. If the carriers $\sigma_1\ne\sigma_2$ are incomparable, let $\tau=\sigma_1\cap\sigma_2$ and let $x$ be the projection of $p$ onto $\tau$ as in step 1.2; choosing $p,q$ closer to $b$ if necessary, the segments $[p,x]$ and $[x,q]$ lie in $U_b$ because the projection is continuous and fixes $b$. Then step 2.2 gives $\operatorname{angle}(dg_b(h),df_b(h))<3\alpha/8$ for their directions $h$, and steps 1.2 and 2.1 show that the images $df_b([p,x])$ and $df_b([x,q])$ make an angle $\beta\ge\alpha$; since $3\alpha/8<\alpha/2\le\beta/2$, step 1.3 with $G=dg_b$ gives $dg_b(p)\ne dg_b(q)$. This contradiction in both cases shows that $dg_b$ is injective on the tangent star at every $b$; hence every strong $\delta$-approximation with this $\delta$ is a whole-star immersion. [step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1]

4.2 Local injectivity of $g$. Fix $b\in|K|$ and retain the neighbourhood $U_b$ and the estimates of step 2.2. We claim that $g$ is injective on a suitable neighbourhood $V_b$ of $b$. Let $p\ne q$ be points of $U_b$. If $p$ and $q$ lie in a common original simplex $\sigma$, put $x:=(p+q)/2$ and apply step 1.3 to $L=[x,p]\cup[x,q]$, $F=df_b$, $G=g$ and $\beta=\pi$: the two segments lie in the single simplex $\sigma$, $F$ is injective on $L$, and the required bound $\operatorname{angle}(Dg(c)h,df_b(h))<\alpha/4<\pi/2$ for the segment directions $h$ at points $c$ of the segments holds by step 2.2; hence $g(p)\ne g(q)$. If instead the carriers $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$ meeting $U_b$ are incomparable, let $\tau=\sigma_1\cap\sigma_2$ and let $x$ be the projection of $p$ onto $\tau$ as in step 1.2; shrinking $U_b$ using continuity of the finitely many projections and choosing $p,q$ in a small ball inside $U_b$, we may assume that $x$ and the segments $[p,x]$ and $[x,q]$ all lie in $U_b$. Step 2.2 gives $\operatorname{angle}(Dg(c)h,df_b(h))<\alpha/4$ for their directions $h$, while steps 1.2 and 2.1 show that $df_b([p,x])$ and $df_b([x,q])$ make an angle $\beta\ge\alpha$; since $\alpha/4<\alpha/2\le\beta/2$, step 1.3 with $G=g$ yields $g(p)\ne g(q)$. Shrinking further by the finitely many continuity conditions (each projection fixes $b$) gives a neighbourhood $V_b$ of $b$ with $g(p)\ne g(q)$ for all distinct $p,q\in V_b$, and this neighbourhood is independent of the choice of the strong $\delta$-approximation $g$. [step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1]

5.1 Global injectivity when $f$ is an embedding. Assume $f$ is an embedding and let $\{V_{b_1},\dots,V_{b_m}\}$ be a finite subcover of $|K|$ by the neighbourhoods of step 4.2, which exists because $|K|$ is compact ([F2]). Let $D:=|K|^2\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^m(V_{b_i}\times V_{b_i})$. This is a closed subset of the compact metric space $|K|^2$, hence compact ([F2]), and it is disjoint from the diagonal, because every point of $|K|$ lies in some $V_{b_i}$. The continuous function $(p,q)\mapsto|f(p)-f(q)|$ attains a minimum $\eta$ on $D$; since $f$ is injective and $p\ne q$ on $D$, one has $\eta>0$ (if $D=\varnothing$ take any $\eta>0$). Require in addition $\delta<\eta/3$. For $(p,q)\in D$ one gets $|g(p)-g(q)|\ge|f(p)-f(q)|-|f(p)-g(p)|-|f(q)-g(q)|>\eta-2\delta>\eta/3>0$, while for $(p,q)\notin D$ both points lie in one $V_{b_i}$ and step 4.2 gives $g(p)\ne g(q)$. Hence $g$ is injective. [F1, F2, step 4.2, choose]

6.1 Conclusion. Choose $\delta$ as the minimum of the bounds of steps 2.2, 4.1 and 5.1. Every strong $\delta$-approximation $g$ is a whole-star immersion by step 4.1 and, when $f$ is an embedding, also injective by step 5.1. Then $g$ is continuous and injective on the compact space $|K|$, so $g$ is a homeomorphism onto its image and hence an embedding ([F2]); no mesh or shape bound on the subdivision was used anywhere. [F2, step 4.1, step 5.1] ∎
