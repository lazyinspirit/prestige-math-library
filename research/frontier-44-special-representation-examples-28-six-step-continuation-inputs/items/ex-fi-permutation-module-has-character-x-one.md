## Example

For every finite set $S$ let $V_S$ be the free $k$-module on $S$, and for an
injection $f\colon S\to S'$ let $V(f)$ be the linear extension of $f$. Then
$V$ is an FI-module ([[def-fi-category-and-fi-module]]), isomorphic to $M(1)$
in the notation of [[def-free-fi-module-generated-by-an-sm-module]]; it
satisfies $V_n\cong k^n$ with the permutation action of $S_n$
([[def-finite-symmetric-group-and-permutation-notation]]) and is generated by
$V_{\{0\}}$ in degree $\le1$
([[def-finite-generation-and-generation-degree-of-an-fi-module]]). Its
character is the character polynomial $X_1$ of weighted degree $1$
([[def-character-polynomial-in-cycle-counts]]): for every $n\ge0$ and every
$\sigma\in S_n$ the trace $\operatorname{tr}(\sigma;V_n)$
([[def-trace-of-a-square-matrix]]) equals $X_1(\sigma)$, the number of fixed
points of $\sigma$. No choice principle is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$ and, for every finite set $S$, the free $k$-module $V_S$ on $S$ with its basis $\{e_s:s\in S\}$, and for an injection $f\colon S\to S'$ the $k$-linear extension $V(f)(e_s):=e_{f(s)}$.

[F1] An FI-module over $k$ is a covariant functor from finite sets and injections to $k$-modules; functoriality means $V(\mathrm{id}_S)=\mathrm{id}_{V_S}$ and $V(g\circ f)=V(g)\circ V(f)$ ([[def-fi-category-and-fi-module]]).

[F2] For $m\ge0$, $M(m)_S$ has as $k$-basis the injections $[m]\hookrightarrow S$, the transition map along $g\colon S\to S'$ sends the basis injection $u$ to $g\circ u$, and $M(1)$ represents the functor $V\mapsto V_{[1]}$ ([[def-free-fi-module-generated-by-an-sm-module]]).

[F3] For a set $\Sigma$ of elements, $\operatorname{span}_V(\Sigma)_T$ is the $k$-span of the elements $f_*(s)$ with $s\in\Sigma\cap V_S$ and $f\colon S\to T$ an injection; $V$ is generated in degree $\le d$ when $\operatorname{span}_V(V_{\le d})=V$ ([[def-finite-generation-and-generation-degree-of-an-fi-module]]).

[F4] $X_1$ is the class function with $X_1(\sigma)=c_1(\sigma)$, the number of fixed points of $\sigma$; it has weighted degree $1$ ([[def-character-polynomial-in-cycle-counts]]).

[F5] The trace of a square matrix is the sum of its diagonal entries; for $n=0$ the empty matrix has trace $0$ ([[def-trace-of-a-square-matrix]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 $V$ is a functor: $V(\mathrm{id}_S)$ sends each basis element $e_s$ to $e_s$, hence is the identity, and for composable injections $f\colon S\to T$, $g\colon T\to U$ one has $V(g\circ f)(e_s)=e_{g(f(s))}=V(g)(e_{f(s)})=V(g)V(f)(e_s)$ on every basis element, so $V(g\circ f)=V(g)V(f)$; hence $V$ is an FI-module with basis $S$ of $V_S$. [F1, given, algebra]

2.1 Identify $V_S$ with $M(1)_S$ by sending the basis element $e_s$ to the injection $u_s\colon[1]\to S$ with $u_s(0)=s$; this is a $k$-linear isomorphism because the $u_s$ are exactly the basis injections of $M(1)_S$ [F2]. It is compatible with transition maps: for $f\colon S\to S'$, $M(1)(f)(u_s)=f\circ u_s=u_{f(s)}$ corresponds to $e_{f(s)}=V(f)(e_s)$. Hence $V\cong M(1)$; in particular $V_n\cong k^n$ with basis $[n]$, and $\sigma\in S_n$ acts by $\sigma\cdot e_s=e_{\sigma(s)}$, the permutation representation. [F2, step 1.1]

2.2 The element $e_0\in V_{[1]}$ generates: for any finite set $S$ and $s\in S$, the injection $u_s\colon[1]\to S$ satisfies $(u_s)_*(e_0)=e_{u_s(0)}=e_s$, so $\operatorname{span}_V(e_0)$ contains every basis element and equals $V$; thus $V$ is generated in degree $\le1$ by $V_{\{0\}}$. [F3, step 1.1]

3.1 For $\sigma\in S_n$ and basis elements $s,t\in[n]$, the coefficient of $e_t$ in $\sigma\cdot e_s=e_{\sigma(s)}$ is $\delta_{t,\sigma(s)}$; the diagonal entry at $s$ is therefore $\delta_{s,\sigma(s)}$, equal to $1$ if $\sigma$ fixes $s$ and to $0$ otherwise. By [F5], $\operatorname{tr}(\sigma;V_n)=\sum_{s\in[n]}\delta_{s,\sigma(s)}$, the number of fixed points of $\sigma$, which is $X_1(\sigma)$; for $n=0$ both sides are $0$. This holds for every $n$, so the trace function of $V_n$ is the character polynomial $X_1$ on the whole range. [F4, F5, step 2.1]

4.1 Steps 1.1, 2.1 and 2.2 exhibit $V\cong M(1)$ as the permutation FI-module generated in degree $\le1$, and step 3.1 identifies its character with $X_1$, a character polynomial of weighted degree $1$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1] ∎
