## Statement

Assume AC. Let $K$ be a finite simplicial complex, $D$ a subcomplex and $X=|K|\setminus|D|$. Let $P$ be the face poset of the open simplices of $K$ not in $D$, ordered by inclusion of faces. The exact realization of finite-dimensional covariant $k$-diagrams on $P$ induces a fully faithful functor into $D^b(\operatorname{Sh}_k(X))$, with essential image the complexes having bounded finite-rank locally constant cohomology on each open simplex. Ordinary and compact-support cohomology, ordinary Hom and internal derived Hom have bounded finite cochain models and commute with every coefficient field extension. For a local product projection $\operatorname{pr}:V\times F\to V$ with supplied finite deleted-boundary models, there is a natural local comparison $R\operatorname{pr}_*\operatorname{pr}^*B\simeq B\otimes_k C^*(F;k)$, taking the unit to $B\otimes(k\to C^*(F;k))$. This does not assert that an arbitrary algebraic map is simplicial.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite abstract simplicial complex $K$ with geometric realization $|K|$, a subcomplex $D\subseteq K$, the open subspace $X=|K|\setminus|D|$ with the subspace topology, the finite face poset $P=\{\sigma\in K:\sigma\notin D\}$ ordered by inclusion of faces, its Alexandrov topology (open sets are the upward-closed subsets, with basis the principal upsets $P_{\ge\sigma}$), a field $k$, the category $\operatorname{Sh}_k(X)$ of sheaves of $k$-vector spaces with the conventions of [[def-sheaf-on-topological-space]] and [[def-presheaf-of-groups-rings-modules]], the constant sheaf $k_X$, and the Axiom of Choice [[def-axiom-of-choice]]. Write $\sigma^\circ$ for the relative interior of a simplex $\sigma$, $\operatorname{St}(\sigma)$ for its open star, $q:X\to P$ for the carrier map $q(x)=\sigma$ if $x\in\sigma^\circ$, and $b_\sigma$ for the barycentre of $\sigma$. The bounded derived category $D^b(\operatorname{Sh}_k(X))$ and derived Hom are understood with [[def-derived-category-of-an-abelian-category]] and [[def-derived-hom-in-the-bounded-setting]]; cohomology is that of [[def-sheaf-cohomology-derived-global-sections]].

[F1] Realization, stars and links ([[def-geometric-realization-of-an-abstract-simplicial-complex]], [[def-simplicial-subcomplex-star-closure-and-link]], [[prop-a-finite-simplicial-complex-has-compact-hausdorff-realization]]): a subcomplex is closed under faces; $|\sigma|$ is the closed simplex and $\sigma^\circ$ its relative interior; $|K|$ carries the weak topology in which a set is open exactly when its trace on every $|\sigma|$ is relatively open, and is compact Hausdorff for finite $K$; the open star $\operatorname{St}(\sigma)$ is the union of the open simplices $\tau^\circ$ with $\sigma\subseteq\tau$, and the closed star $\bigcup_{\sigma\subseteq\tau}\tau$ is the realization of the subcomplex $\{\eta:\eta\subseteq\tau\text{ for some }\tau\supseteq\sigma\}$.

[F2] The sheaf category and stalk exactness ([[def-sheaf-on-topological-space]], [[def-presheaf-on-topological-space]], [[def-presheaf-of-groups-rings-modules]], [[def-module-on-ringed-space]], [[thm-abelian-sheaves-form-abelian-category]], [[def-stalk-of-presheaf]], [[thm-exactness-of-sheaves-stalkwise]], [[thm-sheaf-morphism-isomorphism-stalkwise]]): $\operatorname{Sh}_k(X)$ is the category of modules over the ringed space $(X,k_X)$ with constant structure sheaf $k_X$, hence an abelian category; a sequence of its objects is exact exactly when every stalk sequence is exact, and a morphism is an isomorphism exactly when every stalk map is bijective; the stalk is the filtered colimit of the section groups over the open neighbourhoods.

[F3] Inverse image ([[def-inverse-image-presheaf-and-sheaf]], [[thm-inverse-direct-image-adjunction]], [[lem-stalk-inverse-image-sheaf]]): inverse image $f^{-1}$ is left adjoint to direct image $f_*$ with natural bijection $\operatorname{Hom}_X(f^{-1}\mathcal G,\mathcal F)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_Y(\mathcal G,f_*\mathcal F)$, and $(f^{-1}\mathcal G)_x\cong\mathcal G_{f(x)}$; consequently $f^{-1}$ is exact, because exactness is tested on stalks.

[F4] Direct image and restriction ([[def-direct-image-sheaf]], [[lem-direct-image-is-sheaf]], [[def-restriction-sheaf-open-subspace]]): $(f_*\mathcal F)(V)=\mathcal F(f^{-1}V)$ on open $V$, and $f_*\mathcal F$ is a sheaf; restriction to an open subspace is inverse image along the inclusion, carried out by the same neighbourhood colimit, so that for open $W\subseteq U\subseteq X$ one may identify $(\mathcal F|_U)(W)=\mathcal F(W)$.

[F5] Extension by zero ([[def-extension-by-zero-abelian-sheaf]], [[thm-extension-by-zero-adjunction-exactness]]): for an open inclusion $j:U\hookrightarrow X$, $j_!\mathcal F$ has the sections $s$ of $\mathcal F$ over $V\cap U$ whose support is closed in $V$, the functor $j_!$ is exact, and $\operatorname{Hom}_X(j_!\mathcal F,\mathcal G)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_U(\mathcal F,j^{-1}\mathcal G)$ naturally.

[F6] Local constancy ([[def-locally-constant-sheaf-of-finite-rank]]): a sheaf is locally constant of finite rank when every point has an open neighbourhood on which it is isomorphic to a finite power of the constant sheaf; on a connected, locally path-connected and simply connected space such a sheaf is constant, since local trivializations transport stalks canonically along paths and simple connectivity makes the transport independent of the path.

[F7] The constant-polyhedral comparison ([[lem-locally-contractible-metric-spaces-have-singular-constant-sheaf-cohomology]]): for a metric space with a neighbourhood basis of contractible open sets, $H^q(X,k_X)\cong H^q_{\operatorname{sing}}(X;k)$ on every open subset, so a nonempty contractible such space has constant-sheaf cohomology $k$ in degree $0$ and zero otherwise; the statement applies to every finite-polyhedral open star and to its nonempty intersection with a closed simplex.

[F8] Closed pushforward ([[lem-sheaf-supported-on-a-closed-subset-is-a-pushforward]], [[lem-closed-immersion-preserves-sheaf-cohomology]]): a sheaf whose stalks vanish off a closed subset $Z$ is the pushforward of its restriction to $Z$; and for the closed inclusion $i:Z\hookrightarrow X$ there are isomorphisms $H^q(Z,\mathcal F)\cong H^q(X,i_*\mathcal F)$ for every degree.

[F9] Cohomology and Hom conventions ([[def-sheaf-cohomology-derived-global-sections]], [[lem-sheaf-cohomology-classes-as-derived-morphisms]]): under AC the functorial injective resolution datum computes $H^q(X,\mathcal F)$ as $R^q\Gamma(X,\mathcal F)$, independently of the datum up to canonical natural isomorphism, and classes in $H^p(X,\mathcal F)$ are the derived morphisms $\operatorname{Hom}_{D}(\mathbb Z_X[-p],\mathcal F)$.

[F10] Finite bar models for diagrams ([[lem-finite-poset-diagrams-have-bounded-finite-bar-models]]): on a finite nonempty poset $P$ with maximal strict-chain length $h$, a bounded complex $M$ of finite-dimensional diagrams concentrated in internal degrees $[a,b]$ has $\mathbf R\lim M=\mathbf R\operatorname{Hom}(\underline k,M)$ represented by a finite-dimensional cochain model concentrated in $[a,b+h]$, and two such complexes $A,B$ in ranges $[a,b]$ and $[c,d]$ have $\mathbf R\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)$ represented by a finite-dimensional cochain model concentrated in $[c-b,d-a+h]$; dualizing a finite projective bar resolution over the opposite poset gives the corresponding bounded injective model, with terms that are finite sums of the standard injectives of step 1.4; these models commute with every field extension, and for the empty poset the sections and Hom model are zero by convention.

[F11] Injective and K-injective models over a field ([[lem-field-module-sheaves-have-injective-derived-models]], [[thm-injective-complexes-model-the-bounded-below-derived-category]], [[thm-a-bounded-below-complex-of-injectives-is-homotopically-injective]], [[def-homotopically-injective-bounded-below-complex]], [[prop-morphisms-into-a-homotopically-injective-complex-need-no-roof]], [[thm-a-bounded-below-complex-of-injectives-is-homotopically-injective]]): under AC, $\operatorname{Sh}_k(X)$ has functorial injective embeddings and bounded-below injective replacements, $K^+(\operatorname{Inj}\operatorname{Sh}_k(X))\to D^+(\operatorname{Sh}_k(X))$ is an equivalence, and morphisms into a bounded-below injective complex are computed by chain maps without roofs.

[F12] Projective and K-projective models ([[def-homotopically-projective-bounded-above-complex]], [[thm-a-bounded-above-complex-of-projectives-is-homotopically-projective]], [[prop-morphisms-from-a-homotopically-projective-complex-need-no-roof]]): a bounded-above complex of projective objects is K-projective, and for a K-projective complex $P$ the localization map $\operatorname{Hom}_K(P,X)\to\operatorname{Hom}_D(P,X)$ is bijective for every complex $X$.

[F13] The acyclic-resolution theorem ([[thm-acyclic-resolution-theorem-for-right-derived-functors]], [[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]]): AC supplies dependent choice; if $0\to A\to J^0\to J^1\to\cdots$ is a resolution whose terms are acyclic for a left exact functor $F$, and each syzygy lies in the domain class of the supplied injective resolution datum, then $R^nF(A)\cong H^n(F(J^\bullet_{\mathrm{del}}))$.

[F14] Derived conventions and truncation ([[def-canonical-truncation-of-a-complex]], [[thm-canonical-truncations-fit-a-distinguished-triangle]], [[thm-the-derived-category-inherits-a-triangulated-structure]], [[prop-bounded-derived-localizations-embed-fully-faithfully]]): canonical truncations produce the distinguished triangles $\tau^{\le n}X\to X\to\tau^{\ge n+1}X\to(\tau^{\le n}X)[1]$ and $H^n(X)[-n]\to\tau^{\ge n}X\to\tau^{\ge n+1}X\to H^n(X)[-n+1]$; the derived category is triangulated with exact localization functor and long exact cohomology sequences; and the canonical functors $D^-,D^+,D^b\to D$ are fully faithful with essential images the correspondingly bounded complexes.

[F15] Compact-support calculus ([[def-compactly-supported-global-sections]], [[lem-c-soft-field-sheaves-are-compact-support-acyclic]], [[lem-compact-support-calculus-of-extension-by-zero-and-closed-pushforward]]): AC is assumed; $\Gamma_c$ is left exact with right derived functors $H^q_c$, computed from the supplied injective datum; for an open subspace $j:U\hookrightarrow X$ of a locally compact Hausdorff space there are natural isomorphisms $R^q\Gamma_c(X,j_!\mathcal A)\cong R^q\Gamma_c(U,\mathcal A)$ for all $q$, and when $X$ is compact $\Gamma_c(X,-)=\Gamma(X,-)$.

[F16] Internal Hom ([[def-sheaf-hom]]): for sheaves of modules $\mathcal F,\mathcal G$ on a ringed space, the internal Hom is the sheaf $U\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O|_U}(\mathcal F|_U,\mathcal G|_U)$; for $\operatorname{Sh}_k(X)$ this is the sheaf of $k$-linear morphisms, and on a fixed open $U$ the complex of sections computes internal Hom after restriction.

[F17] Künneth over a field ([[cor-kunneth-over-a-field]]): for complexes of $k$-vector spaces with finite diagonals the cross product is a natural isomorphism $\bigoplus_{p+q=n}H_pC\otimes_kH_qD\cong H_n(C\otimes_kD)$.

## Proof

1.1 **The carrier map.** The principal upsets $P_{\ge\sigma}=\{\tau\in P:\tau\supseteq\sigma\}$ form a basis of the Alexandrov topology: a subset is open exactly when it is upward closed, and an upward-closed set is the union of the principal upsets of its elements. Since $D$ is a subcomplex, every face of a simplex in $D$ lies in $D$, so contraposition makes the set $P$ of simplices outside $D$ upward closed under taking cofaces; hence $q^{-1}(P_{\ge\sigma})=\{x\in X:\sigma\subseteq q(x)\}=\operatorname{St}(\sigma)$, because a point of $|K|$ whose carrier contains $\sigma$ cannot lie in $|D|$ and every coface of a simplex of $P$ again lies in $P$. The star $\operatorname{St}(\sigma)=\bigcup_{\tau\supseteq\sigma}\tau^\circ$ is open in $|K|$, since its trace on each closed simplex $|\eta|$ is $\{x\in|\eta|:\sigma\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(x)\}$, which is relatively open when $\sigma\subseteq\eta$ and empty otherwise; being contained in $X$ it is also open in $X$. As the principal upsets form a basis, $q$ is continuous. Each $\sigma^\circ$ is convex in the affine simplex spanned by $\sigma$, hence connected, locally path-connected and simply connected, and each $\operatorname{St}(\sigma)$ is open and nonempty. [given, F1]

1.2 **Realization of diagrams is exact and constant on each open simplex.** A covariant diagram $V:P\to k\text{-}\mathbf{Vect}$ determines a presheaf on $P$ by $U\mapsto\varprojlim_{\sigma\in U}V_\sigma$ on open (upward-closed) $U$, with restriction maps induced by the universal property of limits; this presheaf is a sheaf, because a compatible family of compatible families glues by choosing for each $\sigma\in U$ a covering member containing it, and the value at $\sigma$ is independent of the choice by the compatibility on overlaps. Its stalk at $\sigma$ is $V_\sigma$, because $P_{\ge\sigma}$ is the least neighbourhood of $\sigma$ and the limit over $P_{\ge\sigma}$ projects isomorphically to $V_\sigma$. Realizing by $q^{-1}$ gives a sheaf $\widehat V:=q^{-1}V$ on $X$ with $(\widehat V)_x\cong V_{q(x)}$ and, on $\sigma^\circ$, $(\widehat V)|_{\sigma^\circ}$ constant with value $V_\sigma$: both sheaves have the same stalks on the connected set $\sigma^\circ$ and the identity on stalks is an isomorphism. Moreover $V\mapsto\widehat V$ is exact: a sequence of diagrams is exact exactly when it is exact at every $\sigma$, the stalk of $\widehat{(-)}$ at $x$ is $V_{q(x)}$, and sheaf exactness is stalkwise; a map of diagrams induces a map of the inverse-limit presheaves, so the construction is functorial. [given, F2, F3]

1.3 **Reconstruction: generization maps.** Let $F$ be a sheaf on $X$ whose restriction to every open simplex in $P$ is locally constant of finite rank. Fix barycentres $b_\sigma\in\sigma^\circ$ and put $V_\sigma:=F_{b_\sigma}$. For $\sigma\subseteq\tau$ in $P$ define $V_\sigma\to V_\tau$ as follows. Every $a\in F_{b_\sigma}$ is represented by a section $s\in F(U)$ on some open neighbourhood $U$ of $b_\sigma$; shrinking $U$ inside $\operatorname{St}(\sigma)$, and then to a member of the cofinal family of open metric balls around $b_\sigma$ small enough that $U\cap\eta^\circ$ is convex for every $\eta\supseteq\sigma$, we may assume $U\cap\tau^\circ$ is a nonempty convex and hence connected open subset of $\tau^\circ$. By [F6] the sheaf $F|_{\tau^\circ}$ is trivializable because $\tau^\circ$ is connected, locally path-connected and simply connected, so $s|_{U\cap\tau^\circ}$ is the restriction of a unique locally constant section $\tilde s$ of $F|_{\tau^\circ}$ over all of $\tau^\circ$; set the image of $a$ to be the germ of $\tilde s$ at $b_\tau\in\tau^\circ$. This is well-defined: if $s,s'$ represent the same germ, they agree on a smaller neighbourhood, which we may shrink to a ball meeting $\tau^\circ$ convexly, and there the two constructions have the same germ; and the construction is independent of the chosen ball by the same argument applied to two balls and a common smaller ball. The maps are $k$-linear because addition of germs and scalar multiplication are represented on a common neighbourhood. They are functorial: the identity of $\sigma$ gives the identity of $V_\sigma$, and for $\sigma\subseteq\eta\subseteq\tau$ the composites $V_\sigma\to V_\eta\to V_\tau$ and $V_\sigma\to V_\tau$ agree, because both are obtained by restricting representatives to a common small incident neighbourhood and extending inside $\tau^\circ$ by the uniqueness part of [F6]. [given, F1, F6]

1.4 **The standard injectives.** For $\tau\in P$ and a finite-dimensional $k$-vector space $W$ let $I_\tau(W)$ be the diagram with value $W$ at every $\sigma\subseteq\tau$ and $0$ elsewhere, the structure maps being identities where both values are $W$. A diagram map $V\to I_\tau(W)$ is exactly a $k$-linear map $V_\tau\to W$, since its $\tau$-component determines all components and conversely any such linear map extends by the zero maps; therefore $I_\tau(W)$ is injective because $\operatorname{Hom}(-,W)$ is exact. Every diagram $V$ embeds into the finite direct sum $\bigoplus_{\tau\in P}I_\tau(V_\tau)$ by the identity component at $\tau$, a nonzero vector of $V_\tau$ being detected by that component. Hence the finite diagram category has enough injectives and explicit finite injective embeddings, and by [F10] every bounded complex of finite-dimensional diagrams has a bounded injective model whose terms are finite sums of these standard injectives with the stated finite-dimensional ranges. [given, F10]

1.5 **Realized representables and projective resolutions.** For $\sigma\in P$ let $P_\sigma$ be the representable diagram with value $k$ at every $\tau\supseteq\sigma$ and $0$ elsewhere. Since $\operatorname{Hom}(P_\sigma,V)=V_\sigma$, evaluation at $\sigma$ is exact and $P_\sigma$ is projective, so the representables form a projective generating family and the finite diagram category has enough projectives. Its realization is the extension by zero of the constant sheaf: $q^{-1}P_\sigma\cong j_\sigma{}_{!}k_{\operatorname{St}(\sigma)}$ for $j_\sigma:\operatorname{St}(\sigma)\hookrightarrow X$, since both sheaves have stalk $k$ at points whose carrier contains $\sigma$ and zero elsewhere, and both are constant on each open simplex. By [F10] a bounded complex $U$ of finite-dimensional diagrams admits a bounded-above resolution $P^\bullet\to U$ whose terms are finite direct sums of the $P_\sigma$, with finite-dimensional Hom complexes; concretely this is the dualized finite bar construction of [F10]. [given, F5, F10]

1.6 **Internal derived Hom and its restriction compatibility.** For bounded complexes $A,B$ on $X$ with finite-rank locally constant cohomology on every open simplex, define the internal derived Hom by the complex $\mathcal{H}om^\bullet(A,J^\bullet(B))$ of [F16], where $J^\bullet(B)$ is a bounded-below injective replacement of $B$ in $\operatorname{Sh}_k(X)$ by [F11]; it initially represents an object of $D^+(\operatorname{Sh}_k(X))$, with boundedness established below, whose derived sections over an open $U$ compute $R\operatorname{Hom}(A|_U,B|_U)$, and its stalk cohomology is $\varinjlim_{U\ni x}\operatorname{Ext}^q_{D(U)}(A|_U,B|_U)$. Restriction to an open subspace preserves injectives, because its exact left adjoint is extension by zero by [F5], so internal Hom commutes with restriction and $\bigl(R^q\mathcal{H}om(A,B)\bigr)_x=\varinjlim_{U\ni x}\operatorname{Ext}^q_{D(U)}(A|_U,B|_U)$ for every point $x$. [given, F5, F11, F16]

2.1 **Sections on a star.** The generization maps of step 1.3 are locally constant along each face: trivialize the finite-rank restriction near a point of that face, lift its finitely many basis germs to ambient sections, and shrink one neighbourhood where their restrictions to each of the finitely many incident cofaces are constant. Their coface matrices are then constant on that face neighbourhood. Since each face interior is connected, these local matrices define the same transported map throughout it; restrictions through nested faces compose as in step 1.3. For $\sigma\in P$, a section on $\operatorname{St}(\sigma)$ with zero germ at $b_\sigma$ vanishes on a nonempty subset of every incident open simplex, hence on that whole simplex by [F6], so it is zero. Conversely, start with a germ $a\in F_{b_\sigma}$ and define its value along every $\tau^\circ$ with $\tau\supseteq\sigma$ by the generization map followed by the unique constant transport on $\tau^\circ$. At a point of $\eta^\circ$, lift that assigned germ to an ambient local section. Its values on every incident $\tau^\circ$ are the prescribed ones, by the local constant matrices and their composition rule; shrinking finitely many times ensures agreement for all cofaces. Thus the assigned family of germs is locally represented by ambient sections. These sections agree stalkwise on their overlaps and glue by the sheaf axiom to a section on the whole star. This proves $F(\operatorname{St}(\sigma))\cong F_{b_\sigma}$. The simplex interiors were not treated as open subsets of the star. [given, F2, F6, step 1.3]

2.2 **Compact supports: reduction to the compact model.** Let $V$ be a bounded complex of finite-dimensional diagrams and regard $\operatorname{Sh}_k(X)$ inside $\operatorname{Sh}_k(|K|)$ along the open inclusion. Since $P$ is upward closed, extending the diagram by zero on $D$ gives consistent maps (there is no arrow from a retained simplex to a deleted coface), and extension by zero of $q^{-1}V$ to $|K|$ is the realization of the diagram $\widetilde V$ on the full face poset $P_K$ of $K$ obtained by extending $V$ by zero over the simplices in $D$: both sheaves have stalk $V_\sigma$ at points of $X$ with carrier $\sigma$ and stalk $0$ over $|D|$, and both are constant on each open simplex of $K$, so they agree by [F2]. Therefore [F15] gives $R\Gamma_c\bigl(X,q^{-1}V\bigr)\cong R\Gamma_c\bigl(|K|,q_K^{-1}\widetilde V\bigr)$, and since $|K|$ is compact by [F1] this is $R\Gamma\bigl(|K|,q_K^{-1}\widetilde V\bigr)$. [given, F1, F2, F15, step 1.2]

2.3 **The product pair.** Let $V=|K_V|\setminus|D_V|$ and $F=|K_F|\setminus|D_F|$ be realizations of finite pairs with face posets $P_V,P_F$ as in the statement, let $\operatorname{pr}:V\times F\to V$ be the projection, and let $B$ be a bounded complex of finite-dimensional diagrams on $P_V$, realized as $A:=q_V^{-1}B$, with the product realized along $q_{V\times F}:V\times F\to P_V\times P_F$. The product $|K_V|\times|K_F|$ has the finite regular product-cell decomposition whose closed cells are the convex polytopes $|\sigma|\times|\eta|$; the star, diagram and acyclicity arguments apply verbatim to these regular cells, using their convex faces, so no product of simplices is asserted to be a simplex, and $V\times F$ is the complement of the subcomplex $D$ consisting of the cells lying in $|D_V|\times|K_F|$ or in $|K_V|\times|D_F|$; a product cell $\sigma\times\eta$ lies outside $D$ exactly when $\sigma\notin D_V$ and $\eta\notin D_F$, so its poset is exactly $P_V\times P_F$, and $\operatorname{pr}^*A=q_{V\times F}^{-1}(B\boxtimes k)$, where $B\boxtimes k$ denotes the product diagram with value $B_\sigma$ at $(\sigma,\eta)$ and the identity in the $\eta$-variable. [given, F1, F2, step 1.2]

3.1 **Realized standard injectives and their acyclicity.** For $\tau\in P$ put $Z_\tau=|\tau|\cap X$, a relatively closed subset of $X$, with inclusion $i_\tau:Z_\tau\hookrightarrow X$. The diagram $I_\tau(W)$ is realized as $i_{\tau*}W_{Z_\tau}$: on every retained face the value is $W$ and the face maps within $\tau$ are identities, and it is zero elsewhere; step 2.1 supplies the corresponding natural isomorphism on star sections. Hence [F8] identifies its cohomology on open $V\subseteq X$ with the constant cohomology of $V\cap Z_\tau$. If $\sigma\not\subseteq\tau$ the star intersection is empty. Otherwise $\operatorname{St}(\sigma)\cap Z_\tau=\operatorname{St}(\sigma)\cap|\tau|$, since any point in that star has a coface of the retained $\sigma$ and cannot lie in $D$. The intersection is convex and contractible in barycentric coordinates, with constant-sheaf cohomology $W$ in degree zero and zero above by [F7]. Globally $Z_\tau$ also contracts to the barycentre of $\tau$: a straight segment starting at a retained face enters $\tau^\circ$ at every positive time, and so never meets the deleted subcomplex. It is a finite-polyhedral open subset of $|\tau|$ and has a contractible-open neighbourhood basis; [F7] again gives cohomology $W$ in degree zero and zero above. These computations show the realized standard injectives, and their finite sums, are acyclic for ordinary global sections and for each retained star. No deleted face is included in $X$. [given, F2, F7, F8, step 1.4, step 2.1]

3.2 **The ordinary equivalence.** The constructions of steps 1.2 and 1.3 are inverse equivalences between finite-dimensional covariant $k$-diagrams on $P$ and sheaves on $X$ whose restriction to every open simplex is locally constant of finite rank. On the diagram side, the reconstruction of $\widehat V$ has $V_\sigma(\widehat V)=(\widehat V)_{b_\sigma}\cong V_\sigma$ by step 1.2, and the generization maps of step 1.3 reproduce the maps of $V$, since the canonical sections over the stars have the prescribed values. On the sheaf side, the canonical morphism $\widehat{V(F)}\to F$ is adjunct to the family $V_\sigma\cong F(\operatorname{St}(\sigma))$ of inverse isomorphisms from step 2.1 and is an isomorphism on stalks by steps 1.2, 1.3 and 2.1, hence an isomorphism by [F2]. Both functors are exact: realization is exact by step 1.2, and reconstruction is exact because a sequence of such sheaves is exact exactly when it is exact at the stalks, which by step 1.3 are the values of the reconstructed diagrams and the generization maps are functorial, so a diagram sequence is exact exactly when the corresponding sheaf sequence is. [given, F2, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.1]

4.1 **Star acyclicity and the ordinary cohomology model.** Let $V$ be a bounded complex of finite-dimensional diagrams. Choose a bounded injective resolution $V\to I^\bullet$ with terms finite sums of standard injectives, supplied by steps 1.4 and [F10], and realize it: since $q^{-1}$ is exact by [F3], the complex $q^{-1}V\to q^{-1}I^\bullet$ is a resolution whose terms are acyclic for $\Gamma(\operatorname{St}(\sigma),-)$ and for $\Gamma(X,-)$ by step 3.1. The syzygies of this resolution lie in the whole category $\operatorname{Sh}_k(X)$, for which the functorial injective resolution datum exists by [F11] under AC, and AC supplies dependent choice by [F13]; applying the acyclic-resolution theorem [F13] twice, we obtain for every $\sigma$ that $H^q(\operatorname{St}(\sigma),q^{-1}V)\cong H^q\bigl(\Gamma(\operatorname{St}(\sigma),q^{-1}I^\bullet)\bigr)=H^q(I^\bullet_\sigma)$, which is $H^q(V_\sigma)$ because evaluation at $\sigma$ is exact and $V\to I^\bullet$ is a quasi-isomorphism; for a sheaf diagram in degree zero this is $V_\sigma$ in degree zero and zero above; and likewise $H^q(X,q^{-1}V)\cong H^q\bigl(\Gamma(X,q^{-1}I^\bullet)\bigr)=H^q\bigl(\varprojlim_{\sigma\in P}I^\bullet_\sigma\bigr)$. The complex $\varprojlim_\sigma I^\bullet_\sigma$ is a finite-dimensional model for $R\Gamma(X,q^{-1}V)$: it is concentrated in the bounded range supplied by [F10] and its terms are finite-dimensional, because $P$ is finite and each $I^j_\sigma$ is finite-dimensional. Every map in the construction is $k$-linear between finite-dimensional spaces, so extending scalars along a field extension $k\to K$ produces the corresponding model for the extended diagrams; hence the ordinary cohomology $R\Gamma(X,q^{-1}V)$ has a bounded finite cochain model commuting with every coefficient field extension, and the same holds for the star cohomologies. [given, F3, F9, F10, F11, F13, step 3.1]

4.2 **The model on base stars.** Choose a bounded injective resolution $B\to I^\bullet$ on $P_V$ and the bounded injective resolution $k\to J^\bullet$ of the constant diagram on $P_F$, both with terms finite sums of standard injectives [F10]; their product double complex $I^\bullet\boxtimes J^\bullet$ realizes a resolution of $\operatorname{pr}^*A$ by sheaves acyclic for $\Gamma(\operatorname{pr}^{-1}\operatorname{St}_V(\sigma),-)$. For $\sigma\in P_V$, $\tau\supseteq\sigma$ and $\eta\in P_F$ the set $\operatorname{pr}^{-1}\operatorname{St}_V(\sigma)\cap(\tau\times\eta)^\circ$ is the product of the convex set $\operatorname{St}_V(\sigma)\cap\tau^\circ$ with the convex set $\eta^\circ$, hence convex and contractible, and the relatively closed support pieces are products $Z_\tau\times Z_\nu$ of the retained closed-cell intersections, contractible as proved in step 3.1; the argument of step 3.1 therefore shows $H^q\bigl(\operatorname{pr}^{-1}\operatorname{St}_V(\sigma),q^{-1}(I_\tau(W)\boxtimes J_\nu(U))\bigr)\cong H^q\bigl((\operatorname{St}_V(\sigma)\cap Z_\tau)\times Z_\nu,\,W\otimes U\bigr)=0$ for $q>0$ with value $W\otimes U$ in degree $0$. Applying the acyclic-resolution theorem [F13] and the Künneth isomorphism of finite-dimensional vector-space complexes [F17] gives, for every $\sigma$, $H^q\bigl(\operatorname{pr}^{-1}\operatorname{St}_V(\sigma),\operatorname{pr}^*A\bigr)\cong H^q\bigl(I^\bullet_\sigma\otimes_k C^\bullet(F;k)\bigr)\cong\bigoplus_{p+r=q}H^p(I^\bullet_\sigma)\otimes_kH^r(F;k)\cong B_\sigma\otimes_kH^q(F;k)$, where $C^\bullet(F;k)$ denotes the finite model $\varprojlim_{\eta\in P_F}J^\bullet_\eta$ for $R\Gamma(F,k)$ supplied by [F10] and the last isomorphism uses that $I^\bullet_\sigma$ is a resolution of the vector space $B_\sigma$ concentrated in degree $0$. [given, F10, F13, F17, step 3.1]

5.1 **The direct image of a realized diagram.** For a diagram $V$, the sheaf $q_*q^{-1}V$ on $P$ has $q_*q^{-1}V(P_{\ge\sigma})=q^{-1}V(\operatorname{St}(\sigma))$, and I claim this equals $V_\sigma$; more precisely $q_*q^{-1}V\cong V$, where $V$ is regarded as the inverse-limit sheaf of step 1.2. Indeed, the comparison map $V\to q_*q^{-1}V$ has $\sigma$-component the canonical map $\varprojlim_{\tau\ge\sigma}V_\tau=V_\sigma\to q^{-1}V(\operatorname{St}(\sigma))$, namely the restriction of the canonical sections of $q^{-1}V$ to the star; for each $\sigma$ the composite $\varprojlim_{\tau\ge\sigma}V_\tau\to q^{-1}V(\operatorname{St}(\sigma))\to(q^{-1}V)_{b_\sigma}=V_\sigma$ is the projection of the limit, which is an isomorphism; step 4.1 independently identifies the star section group with $V_\sigma$, so the comparison itself is an isomorphism at every $\sigma$, and two sheaves on the Alexandrov space that agree on all principal opens are equal. Similarly, for each $j>0$ and each $\sigma$ one has $H^j(\operatorname{St}(\sigma),q^{-1}V)=0$ by step 4.1, and the assignment $U\mapsto H^j(q^{-1}U,q^{-1}V)$ is local on the base: on an open $U\subseteq P$ the restriction of $q_*$ to the opens below $U$ is direct image along the restricted map $q^{-1}U\to U$, so the sections of its derived functors over $U$ are computed by the same construction over $q^{-1}U$; since a sheaf on the finite Alexandrov space whose sections vanish over every principal open is zero, the higher derived sheaves $R^jq_*\bigl(q^{-1}V\bigr)$ vanish for $j>0$. [given, F2, F4, step 1.2, step 4.1]

5.2 **The compact-support cochain model.** Apply the argument of steps 3.1 and 4.1 to the pair $(K,\varnothing)$, whose face poset is $P_K$ and whose realization is the compact space $|K|$: for a bounded complex $\widetilde V$ of finite-dimensional diagrams on $P_K$, choose a bounded injective resolution $\widetilde V\to J^\bullet$ with terms finite sums of standard injectives [F10], realize it, use the acyclicity of realized standard injectives for $\Gamma(|K|,-)$ from step 3.1, and apply the acyclic-resolution theorem [F13]; the outcome is that $R\Gamma\bigl(|K|,q_K^{-1}\widetilde V\bigr)$ is computed by the finite-dimensional bounded complex $\varprojlim_{\eta\in P_K}J^\bullet_\eta$, with the range bound of [F10] for the poset $P_K$ and its coefficient range. By step 2.2 this complex is a bounded finite cochain model for the compact-support cohomology $R\Gamma_c(X,q^{-1}V)$, and as in step 4.1 every map in the construction is $k$-linear between finite-dimensional spaces, so the model and hence the cohomology commute with every coefficient field extension by [F10]. [given, F10, F13, step 3.1, step 2.2, step 4.1]

5.3 **The comparison and its unit.** The realizability of the product diagrams together with the natural map $k\to J^\bullet$ of the fiber resolution and the identity of $B$ induce, on the models of step 4.2, a morphism $B\otimes_kC^\bullet(F;k)\to R\operatorname{pr}_*\operatorname{pr}^*A$ in $D^b(\operatorname{Sh}_k(V))$, natural in $B$; here $C^\bullet(F;k)$ is the finite complex of $k$-vector spaces of step 4.2 and $B\otimes_kC^\bullet(F;k)$ denotes the tensor product of the realized diagram complex on $V$ with this finite complex of vector spaces, with the product differential. The morphism is obtained from the canonical maps $\Gamma(\operatorname{St}_V(\sigma),B)\otimes\Gamma(\eta,J^\bullet)\to\Gamma\bigl(\operatorname{St}_V(\sigma)\times\eta,q^{-1}(I^\bullet\boxtimes J^\bullet)\bigr)$ of the limit description of step 1.2, so it is compatible with restrictions to smaller stars. On each base star the source has $\Gamma\bigl(\operatorname{St}_V(\sigma),B\otimes_kC^\bullet(F;k)\bigr)=B_\sigma\otimes_kC^\bullet(F;k)$ up to canonical isomorphism by steps 1.2 and 4.1, while the target has $\Gamma\bigl(\operatorname{St}_V(\sigma),R\operatorname{pr}_*\operatorname{pr}^*A\bigr)=R\Gamma\bigl(\operatorname{pr}^{-1}\operatorname{St}_V(\sigma),\operatorname{pr}^*A\bigr)$, and on cohomology the comparison is the isomorphism computed in step 4.2; since the base stars form a basis and a morphism whose cohomology sheaves are isomorphisms on every member of a basis is an isomorphism, the comparison is an isomorphism. The unit of the adjunction between $\operatorname{pr}^*$ and $\operatorname{pr}_*$ sends $B$ to the image of $B\otimes(k\to C^\bullet(F;k))$ under this comparison, because the identity component of the tensor model is the identity of $B$ and the fiber unit is the inclusion of $k$ in degree $0$ given by the constant cocycle $1$. [given, F10, F16, step 1.2, step 4.1, step 4.2]

6.1 **Full faithfulness.** Let $U,W$ be bounded complexes of finite-dimensional diagrams. Choose a bounded-above projective resolution $P^\bullet\to U$ as in step 1.5 and a bounded-below injective replacement $J^\bullet$ of $q^{-1}W$ in $\operatorname{Sh}_k(X)$, which is K-injective by [F11]; also fix a bounded injective model $I^\bullet$ of $W$ on the diagram side, which is K-injective as a bounded-below complex of injectives. Since $q^{-1}$ is exact, $q^{-1}P^\bullet\to q^{-1}U$ is a quasi-isomorphism, and since $U\to I^\bullet$ and $q^{-1}W\to J^\bullet$ are quasi-isomorphisms, the localization functors identify $\operatorname{Hom}_{D}\bigl(q^{-1}U,q^{-1}W\bigr)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{D}\bigl(q^{-1}P^\bullet,q^{-1}W\bigr)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_K\bigl(q^{-1}P^\bullet,J^\bullet\bigr)$, the last by [F11]. The adjunction of step 1.5's [F3] identifies the Hom complex of $q^{-1}P^\bullet$ and $J^\bullet$ with the Hom complex of $P^\bullet$ and $q_*J^\bullet$, levelwise and compatibly with the differentials, so $\operatorname{Hom}_K(q^{-1}P^\bullet,J^\bullet)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_K(P^\bullet,q_*J^\bullet)$. By step 5.1 the derived unit $W\to q_*J^\bullet$ is a quasi-isomorphism: apply the star-acyclic termwise model of step 4.1 to a bounded diagram representative of $W$, or build the comparison through its finitely many truncation triangles, so its cohomology is $H^j(W)$ in every degree. The complex $P^\bullet$ is a bounded-above complex of projectives, so it is K-projective by [F12], and therefore $\operatorname{Hom}_K(P^\bullet,q_*J^\bullet)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_D(P^\bullet,q_*J^\bullet)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_D(U,W)$, the first isomorphism by [F12] and the second because quasi-isomorphisms are inverted in both variables. Combining the chain of isomorphisms proves $\operatorname{Hom}_{D}(q^{-1}U,q^{-1}W)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{D}(U,W)$; by [F14] the same holds for the bounded derived categories, so the realization functor is fully faithful. [given, F3, F10, F11, F12, F14, step 1.5, step 5.1]

6.2 **The cone bound.** If $H^r(F;k)=0$ for $1\le r\le R$ and $B$ lies in $D^{\ge b}$, then the cone of the unit $B\otimes(k\to C^\bullet(F;k))$ has cohomology concentrated in degrees $\ge b+R+1$: by [F17] the cohomology of $B\otimes_kC^\bullet(F;k)$ is $\bigoplus_{p+r=n}H^p(B)\otimes_kH^r(F;k)$, which for $n<b+R+1$ reduces to $H^n(B)\otimes_kH^0(F;k)$ and is identified with $H^n(B)$ by the unit, so the cone is acyclic below $b+R+1$. The comparison is local and uses only the supplied finite deleted-boundary models of the base and fiber; no global statement about bundles and no assertion that an arbitrary algebraic map is simplicial is made. [given, F17, step 5.3]

7.1 **Field extension of the Hom model.** In the computation of step 6.1 every object is a finite-dimensional $k$-vector space and every map is $k$-linear: the resolutions supplied by [F10] have finite-dimensional terms, the Hom complexes have finitely many finite-dimensional terms, and their cohomology computes the Hom groups in each degree by [F10]'s finite model. Extending scalars along an exact field extension $k\to K$ therefore replaces the diagrams by their termwise base changes and carries the finite matrices of the model to their base changes, so the cohomology of the model is the base change of the cohomology, and [F10] records this commutation for its models. This proves that ordinary Hom of bounded objects has bounded finite cochain models commuting with every coefficient field extension. [given, F10, step 6.1]

7.2 **The essential image.** First, if $V$ is a bounded complex of diagrams, then each cohomology sheaf of $q^{-1}V$ is $q^{-1}(H^jV)$ by exactness of $q^{-1}$, and its restriction to an open simplex in $P$ is constant of finite rank, so such complexes lie in the stated image and have bounded finite-rank locally constant cohomology on each open simplex. Conversely, let $A$ be a bounded complex on $X$ whose cohomology sheaves are finite-rank locally constant on every open simplex. By step 3.2 each $H^j(A)$ is isomorphic to $q^{-1}V_j$ for a finite-dimensional diagram $V_j$, and $V_j=0$ outside a finite range of degrees. We build $A$ inductively from its cohomology. If $A$ has at most one nonzero cohomology sheaf, then $A\cong H^n(A)[-n]\cong q^{-1}\bigl(V_n[-n]\bigr)$, using that $q^{-1}$ commutes with shifts up to canonical isomorphism because it is exact. Otherwise let $m$ be the least degree with $H^m(A)\neq0$; the canonical truncation triangle of [F14] reads $H^m(A)[-m]\to A\to\tau^{\ge m+1}A\to H^m(A)[-m+1]$. By induction on the number of nonzero cohomology degrees, $\tau^{\ge m+1}A\cong q^{-1}W$ for a bounded diagram complex $W$, and $H^m(A)[-m]\cong q^{-1}\bigl(V_m[-m]\bigr)$; by step 6.1 the connecting morphism $\delta:q^{-1}W\to q^{-1}(V_m[-m+1])$ is $q^{-1}(\delta_0)$ for a unique morphism $\delta_0$ of the diagram derived category. Completing $\delta_0$ to a distinguished triangle $V_m[-m]\to Y\to W\to V_m[-m+1]$ of [F14] and applying the exact functor $q^{-1}$ yields a triangle $q^{-1}(V_m[-m])\to q^{-1}Y\to q^{-1}W\to q^{-1}(V_m[-m+1])$ whose connecting morphism is $\delta$; two distinguished triangles with isomorphic first two objects and the same connecting morphism are isomorphic, so $A\cong q^{-1}Y$ and $A$ is realized. Hence the essential image is exactly the full subcategory of complexes with bounded finite-rank locally constant cohomology on each open simplex. [given, F2, F14, step 3.2, step 6.1]

7.3 **Local models on an open simplex.** Let $x\in\sigma^\circ$, and let $\mathcal U$ be the cofinal family of open intrinsic balls $U\subseteq\operatorname{St}(\sigma)$ around $x$ with $U\cap\tau^\circ$ convex for every $\tau\supseteq\sigma$; every member of $\mathcal U$ meets exactly the open simplices $\tau^\circ$ with $\tau\supseteq\sigma$, so all members carry the restrictions of the same two diagrams, based on the principal filter $Q_\sigma=\{\tau\in P:\tau\supseteq\sigma\}$, and for $U'\subseteq U$ in $\mathcal U$ the diagram of $A|_{U'}$ is literally the diagram of $A|_U$ and the restriction is the identity on this data. The full-faithfulness computation of step 6.1 applies verbatim to each such $(U,Q_\sigma,q|_U)$, because the realizability and acyclicity inputs are the restrictions of the ones used there and every $U\cap\operatorname{St}(\tau)$ and $U\cap|\tau|$ for $\tau\in Q_\sigma$ is convex, nonempty for small $U$, and hence contractible with the metric hypothesis of [F7]; hence $\operatorname{Ext}^q_{D(U)}(A|_U,B|_U)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^q_{\operatorname{Diag}(Q_\sigma)}(V|_{Q_\sigma},W|_{Q_\sigma})$ for every $U\in\mathcal U$ where $A\cong q^{-1}V$, $B\cong q^{-1}W$, and these isomorphisms are compatible with the restriction maps, which become the identity. Consequently the restriction maps $\operatorname{Ext}^q_{D(U)}(A|_U,B|_U)\to\operatorname{Ext}^q_{D(U')}(A|_{U'},B|_{U'})$ are isomorphisms for nested members of $\mathcal U$, so the stalk colimit of step 1.6 stabilizes: the cohomology sheaves $R^q\mathcal{H}om(A,B)$ are locally constant of finite rank on each open simplex, with stalk $\operatorname{Ext}^q_{\operatorname{Diag}(Q_\sigma)}(V|_{Q_\sigma},W|_{Q_\sigma)}$ at every point of $\sigma^\circ$. [given, F7, step 1.6, step 6.1]

8.1 **Boundedness and field extension of internal Hom.** The models $\operatorname{Ext}^q_{\operatorname{Diag}(Q_\sigma)}(V|_{Q_\sigma},W|_{Q_\sigma})$ are finite-dimensional and vanish outside a range depending only on the internal amplitude of $V,W$ and the chain length of $Q_\sigma$, whose strict-chain length is at most that of $P$; since $P$ is finite and $Q_\sigma$ depends only on the simplex $\sigma$, the ranges are uniformly bounded over $\sigma$ and over points of $\sigma^\circ$, so $R\mathcal{H}om(A,B)$ is bounded with finite-rank locally constant cohomology. This proves the internal-Hom clause. [given, F10, step 7.3]
