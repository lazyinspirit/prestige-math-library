## Statement

Let $(I,A,D,P,P^{\vee},q)$ be a symmetrizable Cartan datum ([[def-symmetrizable-cartan-datum-for-a-quantum-group]], [[def-generalized-cartan-matrix]]), let $M$ be a chosen $P$-graded type-1 quantum weight module with $E_i$ and $F_i$ locally nilpotent ([[def-quantum-weight-module-and-integrability]]), and fix $i\in I$. For $\mu\in P$ put
$$H_\mu:=\ker(E_i)\cap M_\mu,\qquad a_\mu:=\langle\mu,h_i\rangle .$$
Then:

(i) $H_\mu=0$ whenever $a_\mu<0$;

(ii) if $a_\mu\ge0$, then $F_i^{(n)}:H_\mu\to M_{\mu-n\alpha_i}$ is injective for $0\le n\le a_\mu$ and is the zero map for $n>a_\mu$;

(iii) every $u\in M_\lambda$ has a unique finite **admissible decomposition** $u=\sum_{n\ge0}F_i^{(n)}u_n$ with $u_n\in H_{\lambda+n\alpha_i}$ and $u_n=0$ whenever $n>\langle\lambda+n\alpha_i,h_i\rangle$;

(iv) consequently $M_\lambda$ is the internal direct sum of the nonzero admissible string spaces $F_i^{(n)}H_{\lambda+n\alpha_i}$.

Coefficients beyond a string's bottom are specified to be zero; arbitrary killed coefficients at the top are not allowed. No finite-dimensionality of $M$ or of its weight spaces, no bounded support, no uniform nilpotence and no choice principle is assumed; $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is the integer pairing of the datum ([[def-kac-moody-integral-and-dominant-integral-weights]]) and $F_i^{(n)}$ is the divided power of [[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]].

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A symmetrizable Cartan datum with $a_{ii}=2$, a chosen $P$-graded type-1 quantum weight module $M$ on which $E_i$ and $F_i$ are locally nilpotent, and $H_\mu=\ker(E_i)\cap M_\mu$, $a_\mu=\langle\mu,h_i\rangle$.

[F1] $M=\bigoplus_{\mu\in P}M_\mu$ with $K_h$ acting on $M_\mu$ by $q^{\langle\mu,h\rangle}$, $E_iM_\mu\subseteq M_{\mu+\alpha_i}$ and $F_iM_\mu\subseteq M_{\mu-\alpha_i}$ for every $\mu$ and $h\in P^{\vee}$; $E_i$ and $F_i$ are locally nilpotent ([[def-quantum-weight-module-and-integrability]]).

[F2] $a_{ii}=2$, so $\langle\alpha_i,h_i\rangle=2$ in the row convention; with $q_i=q^{d_i}$, $d_i\in\mathbb Z_{>0}$, the element $K_i=K_{d_i h_i}$ acts on $M_\mu$ by the scalar $q_i^{a_\mu}$, and $E_i$ raises and $F_i$ lowers $a_\mu$ by $2$ ([[def-generalized-cartan-matrix]], [[def-symmetrizable-cartan-datum-for-a-quantum-group]], [F1]).

[F3] $[m]_i=(q_i^m-q_i^{-m})/(q_i-q_i^{-1})$ for integer $m$, with $[0]_i=0$, $[1]_i=1$, $x^{(m)}=x^m/[m]_i!$ for $m\ge0$, and $[m]_i=0$ exactly when $m=0$, because $q_i$ is transcendental over $\mathbb Q$ ([[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]).

[F4] In the rank-one subalgebra generated by $E_i,F_i,K_i^{\pm1}$ one has $K_iE_iK_i^{-1}=q_i^2E_i$, $K_iF_iK_i^{-1}=q_i^{-2}F_i$, $E_iF_i-F_iE_i=(K_i-K_i^{-1})/(q_i-q_i^{-1})$, the identity $F_iF_i^{(b)}=[b+1]_iF_i^{(b+1)}$ for $b\ge0$, and the divided-power commutation formula: for $b\ge1$, with $\Theta_{i,b}:=(q_i^{b}-q_i^{-b})^{-1}\sum_{s=0}^{b-1}(q_i^{-2s}K_i-q_i^{2s}K_i^{-1})$, one has $E_iF_i^{(b)}=F_i^{(b)}E_i+F_i^{(b-1)}\Theta_{i,b}$; iterating it, $E_i^{(a)}F_i^{(b)}$ is a finite sum of monomials $F_i^{(b-t)}K_i^{m}E_i^{(a-t)}$ with $0\le t\le\min(a,b)$ and coefficients in $\mathbb Q(q)$ ([[thm-quantized-sl-two-string-formulas]], parts (i)-(ii)).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct. Reduce each vector to a finite-dimensional rank-one module, classify its simple factors by the Casimir, split the module by the generalized eigenspaces of the Casimir, and read off injectivity, existence and uniqueness of the string expansion; all arguments are finite and choice-free.

1.1 Finite-dimensional reduction. Let $u\in M_\lambda$. Choose $N\ge0$ with $E_i^Nu=0$ and set $W:=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q(q)}\{F_i^{(a)}E_i^bu:0\le b<N,\ a\ge0\}$. Then $W$ is finite-dimensional: for each of the finitely many $b<N$ the local nilpotence of $F_i$ gives an integer $A_b$ with $F_i^{A_b}E_i^bu=0$, so the span of the vectors $F_i^{(a)}E_i^bu$, $a\ge0$, is already spanned by the finitely many vectors with $a<A_b$. [given, F1]

1.2 The commutation scalar. Let $v$ be a weight vector with $E_iv=0$ and $K_iv=q_i^av$, where $a=\langle\mathrm{wt}(v),h_i\rangle$. For $b\ge1$ the formula of [F4] gives $E_iF_i^{(b)}v=F_i^{(b-1)}\Theta_{i,b}v$, and $\Theta_{i,b}$ acts on $v$ by the scalar $(q_i^{b}-q_i^{-b})^{-1}\sum_{s=0}^{b-1}(q_i^{-2s}q_i^{a}-q_i^{2s}q_i^{-a})=[a-b+1]_i$ by the finite geometric-sum identities; hence $E_iF_i^{(b)}v=[a-b+1]_iF_i^{(b-1)}v$. [F3, F4, algebra]

1.3 The Casimir. Put $\varphi(K_i):=(q_iK_i+q_i^{-1}K_i^{-1})/(q_i-q_i^{-1})^2$ and $C:=F_iE_i+\varphi(K_i)\in A_i$. Then $C$ is central in $A_i$: from $K_iE_iK_i^{-1}=q_i^2E_i$ and $K_iF_iK_i^{-1}=q_i^{-2}F_i$ one computes $\varphi(q_i^{2}K_i)-\varphi(K_i)=(q_i^{2}K_i-q_i^{-2}K_i^{-1})/(q_i-q_i^{-1})$ and hence $[\varphi(K_i),E_i]=E_i(\varphi(q_i^2K_i)-\varphi(K_i))=(K_i-K_i^{-1})E_i/(q_i-q_i^{-1})=-[F_iE_i,E_i]$, while $\varphi(q_i^{-2}K_i)-\varphi(K_i)=-(K_i-K_i^{-1})/(q_i-q_i^{-1})$ gives $[\varphi(K_i),F_i]=-F_i(K_i-K_i^{-1})/(q_i-q_i^{-1})=-[F_iE_i,F_i]$; so $[C,E_i]=[C,F_i]=0$, and $C$ commutes with $K_i^{\pm1}$ visibly. Moreover $C$ acts on every weight vector $v$ with $E_iv=0$ and $K_iv=q_i^av$ by the scalar $c_a:=\varphi(q_i^a)=(q_i^{a+1}+q_i^{-a-1})/(q_i-q_i^{-1})^2$. [F4, algebra]

2.1 The scalars $c_a$ for $a\ge0$ are pairwise distinct: if $c_a=c_b$ with $a,b\ge0$, then $q_i^{a+1}+q_i^{-a-1}=q_i^{b+1}+q_i^{-b-1}$, that is $(q_i^{a+1}-q_i^{b+1})(1-q_i^{-(a+b+2)})=0$; as $q_i$ is transcendental over $\mathbb Q$, $q_i^m=1$ forces $m=0$, so the second factor is nonzero and $q_i^{a+1}=q_i^{b+1}$ gives $a=b$. [F3, step 1.3, algebra]

2.2 $W$ is a finite-dimensional $A_i$-submodule on which $E_i$ and $F_i$ are nilpotent and $K_i$ is diagonalisable with eigenvalues in $q_i^{\mathbb Z}$. Indeed $F_iF_i^{(a)}=[a+1]_iF_i^{(a+1)}$ and $K_iF_i^{(a)}K_i^{-1}=q_i^{-2a}F_i^{(a)}$ show that $F_i$ and $K_i$ preserve $W$, and for $a\ge1$ the commutation formula of [F4] gives $E_iF_i^{(a)}E_i^bu=F_i^{(a)}E_i^{b+1}u+F_i^{(a-1)}\Theta_{i,a}E_i^bu$, where $\Theta_{i,a}$ acts on the weight vector $E_i^bu$ by a scalar; for $b+1<N$ both terms lie in $W$, and for $b+1=N$ the first term is $F_i^{(a)}E_i^Nu=0$, so $E_i$ preserves $W$. Nilpotence is inherited from local nilpotence on the finite-dimensional $W$, and diagonalisability with the eigenvalue form from [F1] and [F2]. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 Claims (i) and (ii). Let $0\ne v\in H_\mu$. By steps 1.1 and 2.2 applied with $N=1$, the span $W_v$ of $F_i^{(a)}v$, $a\ge0$, is a finite-dimensional module with $F_i$ nilpotent, so there is a minimal $t\ge1$ with $F_i^{(t)}v=0$, and $F_i^{(t-1)}v\ne0$. Step 1.2 gives $0=E_iF_i^{(t)}v=[a_\mu-t+1]_iF_i^{(t-1)}v$, hence $[a_\mu-t+1]_i=0$, which by [F3] means $a_\mu-t+1=0$, that is $t=a_\mu+1\ge1$. Thus $a_\mu\ge0$, and $F_i^{(n)}v\ne0$ for $0\le n\le a_\mu$ while $F_i^{(n)}v=F_i^{(n-a_\mu-1)}F_i^{(a_\mu+1)}v=0$ for $n>a_\mu$. As $v$ was an arbitrary nonzero element of $H_\mu$, this proves $H_\mu=0$ for $a_\mu<0$ and the injectivity and vanishing clauses for $F_i^{(n)}$ on $H_\mu$. [F3, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.2]

4.1 Simple factors. Let $S$ be a simple subquotient of a finite-dimensional type-1 $A_i$-module on which $E_i$ is nilpotent. Then $S$ contains a nonzero weight vector $v$ with $E_iv=0$: choose $0\ne w\in S$, let $m\ge1$ be minimal with $E_i^mw=0$ and put $v_0:=E_i^{m-1}w\ne0$; decomposing $v_0$ into weight components and using that $E_i$ preserves weights, some nonzero weight component $v$ satisfies $E_iv=0$. With $a:=\langle\mathrm{wt}(v),h_i\rangle$, step 3.1 gives $a\ge0$, $F_i^{(a)}v\ne0$ and $F_i^{(a+1)}v=0$. The span of $v,F_iv,\dots,F_i^{(a)}v$ is a nonzero $A_i$-submodule by the formulas of steps 1.2 and 2.2 together with $[0]_i=0$, so it equals the simple module $S$; these $a+1$ vectors have distinct weights, whence $\dim S=a+1$ and $C$ acts on $S$ by the scalar $c_a$ of step 1.3. [F3, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.2, step 3.1]

5.1 Generalized-eigenspace splitting. Let $W$ be as in steps 1.1-2.2. Since $C$ is central in $A_i$, it preserves $A_i$-submodules and acts on each simple factor of $W$ by the scalar $c_a$ with $a\ge0$ from step 4.1. The characteristic polynomial of $C$ on $W$ therefore splits with roots among the pairwise distinct scalars $c_a$ of step 2.1, and the generalized eigenspaces $W[a]:=\ker(C-c_a)^{m}\cap W$ are $A_i$-submodules with $W=\bigoplus_aW[a]$. Every composition factor of $W[a]$ has highest label $a$: a factor of label $b\ne a$, $b\ge0$, would make $C-c_b$ non-invertible on $W[a]$, whereas $C-c_b$ is invertible on the generalized eigenspace of the distinct scalar $c_a$. [step 1.3, step 2.1, step 4.1]

6.1 Blocks. Fix $a\ge0$ with $W[a]\ne0$ and let $r:=\dim W[a]/(a+1)$, the number of composition factors of $W[a]$ counted with multiplicity. Each factor has by step 4.1 a weight-space decomposition into one-dimensional spaces of $K_i$-eigenvalues $q_i^{a},q_i^{a-2},\dots,q_i^{-a}$; hence the weight space of eigenvalue $q_i^{a-2n}$ has dimension $r$ for $0\le n\le a$, and $W[a]$ has no weight of eigenvalue $q_i^{m}$ with $m>a$. In particular $k:=\dim W[a]_{a}=r$, $\dim W[a]=k(a+1)$, and $E_i$ kills the top weight space $W[a]_{a}$ because it would raise the eigenvalue to $q_i^{a+2}$. [step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 String splitting of a block. For $w\in W[a]_{a}$ and $0\le n\le a$ one has $F_i^{(n)}w\ne0$: if $F_i^{(n)}w=0$ for some minimal $n\ge1$, then $F_i^{(n-1)}w\ne0$ and step 1.2 gives $0=E_iF_i^{(n)}w=[a-n+1]_iF_i^{(n-1)}w$, forcing $n=a+1>a$ by [F3]. Hence each $N_n:=F_i^{(n)}W[a]_{a}$, $0\le n\le a$, has dimension $k$, and the $N_n$ lie in the distinct weight spaces of eigenvalues $q_i^{a-2n}$, so they are independent; their sum is $A_i$-stable because $E_iF_i^{(n)}w=[a-n+1]_iF_i^{(n-1)}w$ and $F_iF_i^{(n)}w=[n+1]_iF_i^{(n+1)}w$ for $w\in W[a]_a$; being a submodule of dimension $k(a+1)=\dim W[a]$, the sum equals $W[a]$. [F3, step 1.2, step 6.1]

8.1 Existence. Let $u\in M_\lambda$ be homogeneous and let $W$ be the finite-dimensional reduction of steps 1.1-2.2, which contains $u$ and splits as $W=\bigoplus_aW[a]=\bigoplus_a\bigoplus_{n=0}^{a}F_i^{(n)}W[a]_{a}$ by steps 5.1-7.1. A summand $F_i^{(n)}W[a]_{a}$ lies in weight $\lambda$ exactly when $a-2n=\langle\lambda,h_i\rangle$, and then $W[a]_{a}\subseteq M_{\lambda+n\alpha_i}$ with $E_i$ killing it, so it is contained in $H_{\lambda+n\alpha_i}$. Collecting, for each $n\ge0$, the top vectors of all blocks with $a=\langle\lambda,h_i\rangle+2n$ into $u_n\in H_{\lambda+n\alpha_i}$ gives $u=\sum_{n\ge0}F_i^{(n)}u_n$; here $u_n\ne0$ only when $0\le n\le a=\langle\lambda,h_i\rangle+2n$, which is exactly the admissibility condition $n\le\langle\lambda+n\alpha_i,h_i\rangle$. For a general $u\in M_\lambda$, apply this to the finitely many nonzero homogeneous components of $u$ and add. [step 5.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 Uniqueness. Suppose $\sum_{n=0}^{N}F_i^{(n)}u_n=0$ with $u_n\in H_{\lambda+n\alpha_i}$ admissible and $u_N\ne0$. By [F4], $E_i^{N}F_i^{(n)}$ is a finite sum of terms $F_i^{(n-t)}K_i^{m}E_i^{N-t}$ with $0\le t\le n$; for $n<N$ every $t\le n$ satisfies $N-t\ge N-n\ge1$, so each term kills $u_n$ (as $E_iu_n=0$), while for $n=N$ only $t=N$ survives and, by iterating step 1.2, $E_i^{N}F_i^{(N)}u_N=\bigl(\prod_{t=1}^{N}[a_N-t+1]_i\bigr)u_N$ with $a_N=\langle\lambda+N\alpha_i,h_i\rangle$. Admissibility gives $a_N\ge N$, so $a_N-t+1\ge1$ for $1\le t\le N$ and the product is nonzero by [F3]. Hence $0=E_i^{N}0=\bigl(\prod_{t=1}^{N}[a_N-t+1]_i\bigr)u_N$ forces $u_N=0$, a contradiction. [F3, F4, step 1.2, step 8.1]

10.1 Direct sum. By step 3.1(ii) applied to $H_{\lambda+n\alpha_i}$, the map $F_i^{(n)}$ on $H_{\lambda+n\alpha_i}$ is injective for $n\le\langle\lambda+n\alpha_i,h_i\rangle$ and zero otherwise. Steps 8.1 and 9.1 say that every $u\in M_\lambda$ is a unique sum of vectors from the nonzero spaces $F_i^{(n)}H_{\lambda+n\alpha_i}$, which is exactly the statement that $M_\lambda$ is their internal direct sum. All selections in the argument were among finitely many vectors or finitely many scalars, so no choice principle is used. [step 3.1, step 8.1, step 9.1] ∎
