## Statement

Let $Q$ be a KLR datum as in [[def-quiver-hecke-klr-algebra-for-a-symmetrizable-cartan-datum]]. Choose a factorization $Q_{ij}(u,v)=P_{ij}(u,v)P_{ji}(v,u)$ with $P_{ij}\in k[u,v]$ nonzero for all $i\ne j$; with (Q1) one may order the pairs and take $P_{ij}=Q_{ij}$, $P_{ji}=1$. On the graded space $\mathrm{Pol}_n:=\bigoplus_{\nu\in I^n}k[x_1,\dots,x_n]e(\nu)$ define operators: $e(\nu)$ projects to the $\nu$-summand; $x_a$ multiplies by $x_a$; for $\nu_a\ne\nu_{a+1}$ set $\tau_a(f e(\nu)):=P_{\nu_a\nu_{a+1}}(x_{a+1},x_a)\,s_a(f)\,e(s_a\nu)$ where $s_a$ permutes the variables; for $\nu_a=\nu_{a+1}$ set $\tau_a(f e(\nu)):=\dfrac{f-s_a(f)}{x_a-x_{a+1}}e(\nu)$, the divided difference operator of the sign convention fixed in relation (6) of [[def-quiver-hecke-klr-algebra-for-a-symmetrizable-cartan-datum]] (the quotient lies in $k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ because $f-s_af$ vanishes at $x_a=x_{a+1}$; the variable permutation of [[def-multivariate-polynomial-ring-by-iteration]] makes this precise). Give $\mathrm{Pol}_n$ the grading of [[def-klr-grading-and-diagrammatic-composition]] with $\deg x_ae(\nu):=2d_{\nu_a}$, together with the summand shifts that make each $\tau_a$ homogeneous of degree $-b_{\nu_a\nu_{a+1}}$ (they exist by the antisymmetry of the increments $-b_{ij}-\deg P_{ij}$ and are unique up to one constant on each fixed-weight sequence orbit). Then every generator acts homogeneously of its degree, the operators satisfy relations (1)-(7), and they define a homomorphism of graded algebras $\rho:R_n(Q)\to\mathrm{End}_k^{\mathrm{gr}}(\mathrm{Pol}_n)$, the graded algebra of homogeneous endomorphisms, with $\rho(R_n(Q)_m)\,\mathrm{Pol}_{n,d}\subseteq\mathrm{Pol}_{n,d+m}$. **For nonzero $P_{ij}$ this homomorphism is injective.** After localizing at the nonzero polynomials $P_{ij}(x_a,x_b)$ ($\nu_a\ne\nu_b$) and the differences $x_a-x_b$ ($\nu_a=\nu_b$), it becomes an isomorphism onto the localized algebra $A_n(I)=k(I)[x]\rtimes S_n$; consequently $R_n(Q)$ has finite-dimensional homogeneous components and $\rho$ is a faithful representation.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A KLR datum $Q$ of [[def-quiver-hecke-klr-algebra-for-a-symmetrizable-cartan-datum]] with $b_{ij}=d_ia_{ij}=b_{ji}$ and $b_{ii}=2d_i>0$, a factorization $Q_{ij}(u,v)=P_{ij}(u,v)P_{ji}(v,u)$ with all $P_{ij}\ne0$, and the space $\mathrm{Pol}_n=\bigoplus_{\nu\in I^n}k[x_1,\dots,x_n]e(\nu)$ with the degree assignment $\deg x_a=2d_{\nu_a}$ and idempotents of degree~$0$.

[F1] (Q3) of [[def-quiver-hecke-klr-algebra-for-a-symmetrizable-cartan-datum]]: $Q_{ij}$ is homogeneous of total degree $-2b_{ij}$ for $\deg u=2d_i$, $\deg v=2d_j$.

[F2] (Q2) of [[def-quiver-hecke-klr-algebra-for-a-symmetrizable-cartan-datum]]: $Q_{ij}(u,v)=Q_{ji}(v,u)$, so $Q_{ij}$ is also homogeneous of total degree $-2b_{ij}$ for the swapped weighting $\deg u=2d_j$, $\deg v=2d_i$; hence in the factorization both $P_{ij}$ and $P_{ji}$ are homogeneous for both weightings of their pair, with the pair-degree identity $\deg_{[j,i]}P_{ij}+\deg_{[i,j]}P_{ji}=-2b_{ij}$.

[F3] Relation list (1)-(7) of [[def-quiver-hecke-klr-algebra-for-a-symmetrizable-cartan-datum]], and the identity for divided differences: with $s_a$ acting on polynomials by $x_a\leftrightarrow x_{a+1}$ and $\partial_a=(1-s_a)/(x_a-x_{a+1})$, one has $\partial_a x_a-x_{a+1}\partial_a=1$, $\partial_a x_{a+1}-x_a\partial_a=-1$, $\partial_a^2=0$ and $\partial_a(fg)=\partial_a(f)\,g+s_a(f)\,\partial_a(g)$ for all polynomials $f,g$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; construct the shifts, verify the relations case by case, then prove injectivity and the localized isomorphism.

1.1 (Shifts.) For a sequence $\nu$ and an adjacent pair with colours $(p,q)=(\nu_a,\nu_{a+1})$, $p\ne q$, put $\Delta(\nu,a):=-b_{pq}-\deg_{[q,p]}P_{pq}$, where $\deg_{[q,p]}$ is the total degree for the weighting in which the first variable has degree $2d_q$ and the second $2d_p$; by [F2] both $P_{pq}$ and $P_{qp}$ are homogeneous for that pair of weightings, and $\Delta(\nu,a)+\Delta(s_a\nu,a)=-2b_{pq}-\deg_{[q,p]}P_{pq}-\deg_{[p,q]}P_{qp}=0$, since $\deg_{[q,p]}(Q_{pq})=-2b_{pq}$ by [F2] and it equals the two-factor sum. Hence $\Delta$ is an antisymmetric integer edge labelling of the graph whose vertices are the sequences $\nu\in I^n$ (all $n$ and all sequences) and whose edges are the adjacent unequal-colour swaps: every $4$-cycle contributes $\Delta+\Delta'-\Delta-\Delta'=0$, every hexagonal cycle on three distinct colours contributes the three increments with both orientations, also $0$. Since this graph is connected on each fixed multiset of colours, integrating $\Delta$ along paths from a chosen base sequence defines an integer-valued function $w$ on each orbit with $w(s_a\nu)-w(\nu)=\Delta(\nu,a)$; any other such function differs by a constant on the orbit. Let $\mathrm{Pol}_n\{w\}$ denote the same space with the summand $k[x]e(\nu)$ shifted by $w(\nu)$. [given, F2, algebra]

2.1 (Definition and homogeneity.) Define $e(\nu)$ and $x_a$ as stated and $\tau_a$ by the two displayed formulas. Each formula is a finite sum of polynomials times a permutation of the variables, hence a well-defined $k$-linear endomorphism; the equal-colour quotient lies in $k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ because $f-s_af$ vanishes on $x_a=x_{a+1}$ ([[def-polynomial-ring-over-a-commutative-ring]], [[def-multivariate-polynomial-ring-by-iteration]]). With the shifts of step 1.1, $x_a$ is homogeneous of degree $2d_{\nu_a}$ (the shifts are constant on a summand and do not change dot degrees), and $\tau_a$ is homogeneous of degree $-b_{\nu_a\nu_{a+1}}$: on unequal colours the factor $P_{pq}(x_{a+1},x_a)$ has degree $\deg_{[q,p]}P_{pq}$, so multiplying by it and passing to the summand $s_a\nu$ with shift $w(s_a\nu)=w(\nu)+\Delta(\nu,a)$ changes the total degree by $\deg_{[q,p]}P_{pq}+\Delta(\nu,a)=-b_{pq}$; on equal colours the divided difference has degree $-2d_p=-b_{pp}$ because $f-s_af$ has degree $\deg f-2d_p$. [given, F1, F2, algebra, construct]

3.1 (Relations (1), (2), (3), (5), (6).) Relations (1)-(3) are immediate from the definitions: the $e(\nu)$ are orthogonal idempotents summing to $1$, $x$'s commute and commute with the $e(\nu)$, and both displayed $\tau_a$'s map the $\nu$-summand to the $s_a\nu$-summand. Relation (5) holds because $\tau_a$ and $\tau_b$ with $|a-b|>1$ act on disjoint sets of variables and their polynomial factors commute. For (6): if $b\notin\{a,a+1\}$ the multiplication by $x_b$ commutes with $s_a$ and with the factor $P_{\nu_a\nu_{a+1}}(x_{a+1},x_a)$, giving $\tau_ax_b=x_b\tau_a=x_{s_a(b)}\tau_a$; if $b=a$ and $\nu_a\ne\nu_{a+1}$ then $\tau_ax_af=P(x_{a+1},x_a)x_{a+1}s_af=x_{a+1}\tau_af$, and $b=a+1$ is the same with $x_a$; if $\nu_a=\nu_{a+1}$ the two identities $\partial_ax_a-x_{a+1}\partial_a=1$ and $\partial_ax_{a+1}-x_a\partial_a=-1$ of [F3] are exactly the two displayed dot moves. [step 2.1, F3, algebra]

3.2 (Relation (4).) On equal colours $\tau_a^2=\partial_a^2=0=Q_{pp}(x_a,x_{a+1})e(\nu)$ by [F3] and $Q_{pp}=0$. On unequal colours $(p,q)$ a double application gives $\tau_a^2(fe(\nu))=P_{qp}(x_{a+1},x_a)\,s_a\bigl(P_{pq}(x_{a+1},x_a)s_af\bigr)=P_{pq}(x_a,x_{a+1})P_{qp}(x_{a+1},x_a)f=Q_{pq}(x_a,x_{a+1})f$, which is relation (4) because $Q_{pq}(u,v)=P_{pq}(u,v)P_{qp}(v,u)$ evaluated at $(u,v)=(x_a,x_{a+1})$ equals $P_{pq}(x_a,x_{a+1})P_{qp}(x_{a+1},x_a)$. [step 2.1, F1, F3, algebra]

4.1 (Relation (7), equal outer colours $(i,j,i)$.) Work on $e(i,j,i)$ in variables $x_1,x_2,x_3=x_a,x_{a+1},x_{a+2}$, write $P=P_{ij}$, $P'=P_{ji}$, $Q=PP'(v,u)$, let $s=(12)$, $t=(23)$ and $r=tst=sts=(13)$; write $A=\tau_{a+1}\tau_a\tau_{a+1}$ and $B=\tau_a\tau_{a+1}\tau_a$. Expanding the definitions on $f e(i,j,i)$ and collecting the outer factor, $Af=P(x_3,x_2)\cdot t\bigl[\partial_a\bigl(P'(x_3,x_2)\,tf\bigr)\bigr]$ and $Bf=P'(x_2,x_1)\cdot s\bigl[\partial_{a+1}\bigl(P(x_2,x_1)\,sf\bigr)\bigr]$. Using $t\partial_at=(1-r)/(x_1-x_3)$ and $\partial_{a+1}(hg)=\partial_{a+1}(h)g+t(h)\partial_{a+1}(g)$ with $h=P'(x_3,x_2)$ first, then the mirrored expansion of $B$ with $h=P(x_2,x_1)$, gives $Af=P(x_3,x_2)\bigl[P'(x_2,x_3)f-P'(x_2,x_1)rf\bigr]/(x_1-x_3)$ and $Bf=P'(x_2,x_1)\bigl[P(x_1,x_2)f-P(x_3,x_2)rf\bigr]/(x_1-x_3)$. Subtracting, the two $rf$-terms cancel and the remaining coefficient is $P(x_3,x_2)P'(x_2,x_3)-P(x_1,x_2)P'(x_2,x_1)=Q(x_3,x_2)-Q(x_1,x_2)$, so $(A-B)f=\dfrac{Q(x_1,x_2)-Q(x_3,x_2)}{x_3-x_1}f$, which is relation (7) on $e(i,j,i)$. [step 2.1, step 3.2, F3, algebra]

5.1 (Relation (7), remaining colours.) If $\nu_a=\nu_{a+1}=\nu_{a+2}=i$, then all three operators are divided differences and both braid words equal the same antisymmetrizer expression $\sum_{w\in S_3}\operatorname{sgn}(w)w/\prod_{i<j}(x_i-x_j)$, so $A-B=0$, consistent with $Q_{ii}=0$; if exactly the outer two colours agree, step 4.1 applies with the two adjacent colours distinct; if only the first two colours agree, say $(i,i,j)$, then with $h=P_{ij}(x_2,x_1)P_{ij}(x_3,x_1)$, which is symmetric in $x_2,x_3$, the two expansions give $Af=\partial_{a+1}(h\cdot stf)$ and $Bf=h\cdot st\,\partial_a(f)$, and the product rule plus the Demazure identity $\partial_{a+1}st=st\,\partial_a$ valid for the $3$-cycle $w=st$ yield $A-B=0$; the case $(i,j,j)$ is the mirror image, obtained by reading the same identities from the other end. If the three colours are distinct, both $A$ and $B$ act as the same product $P_{ij}P_{ik}P_{jk}$ (with arguments placed by the two expansions, all three factors read off the three crossed pairs) times the longest permutation $r$, and $A=B$; the expansion is by the same substitution $t\partial_at=(1-r)/(x_1-x_3)$ as in step 4.1 applied to three distinct colours, where the two middle expressions coincide because the two factorizations of the long element have the same length. Hence relation (7) holds in every case. [step 3.1, step 4.1, F1, F3, algebra]

6.1 (The homomorphism.) Steps 3.1, 3.2, 4.1 and 5.1 verify relations (1)-(7) for the operators, and step 2.1 shows each generator acts homogeneously of its assigned degree, so the universal property of the presented algebra gives a homomorphism $\rho:R_n(Q)\to\mathrm{End}^{\mathrm{gr}}_k(\mathrm{Pol}_n\{w\})$ of graded algebras with $\rho(R_n(Q)_m)\mathrm{Pol}_{n,d}\subseteq\mathrm{Pol}_{n,d+m}$. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct]

6.2 (Spanning.) Every element of $R_n(Q)$ is a $k$-linear combination of monomials $\tau_wx_1^{a_1}\cdots x_n^{a_n}e(\nu)$, $w\in S_n$, $a_j\ge0$: move dots to the right across crossings by relation (6), whose corrections replace a crossing by an idempotent term or commute a dot, and reduce the number of crossings of an arbitrary $\tau$-word by far commutation (5), the quadratic relation (4) (which replaces a repeated adjacent crossing by a dot polynomial in $x$'s) and the braid relation (7) (which exchanges two reduced words for the same permutation); induction on the pair (crossing number, inversion number of the underlying permutation) terminates because each correction replaces crossings by dots or decreases the number of crossings. [step 3.1, step 3.2, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 (Faithfulness.) Localize for this step only at the nonzero elements $\{P_{ij}(x_a,x_b):\nu_a\ne\nu_b\}$ and $\{x_a-x_b:\nu_a=\nu_b\}$; the localized operators of a reduced word $w=s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_r}$ on a fixed source sequence $\nu$ have the form $c_w\cdot w+\sum_{u<w}c_u u$ where $c_w\ne0$ is the product of the nonzero $P$-factors (unequal crossings) and the leading coefficient $1$ (equal crossings, where $\partial_a$ has leading term $(1-s_a)/(x_a-x_{a+1})$), and the terms $u<w$ are strictly shorter permutations. Suppose $\sum_\nu\sum_{w,a}c_{w,a}\tau_wx^ae(\nu)$ acted as zero; for the maximal $w$ occurring in a fixed source/destination block the leading terms give a nontrivial linear relation $\sum_w d_w w=0$ among distinct automorphisms $w$ of the field $k(x_1,\dots,x_n)$. Choosing a highest $w$ and $g$ with $w(g)\ne w'(g)$ for all other occurring $w'$, the relation evaluated on $fg$ minus $w(g)$ times the relation evaluated on $f$ is a shorter such relation with nonzero coefficients, a contradiction; hence no nonzero combination of the spanning monomials of step 6.2 acts as zero and $\rho$ is injective. [step 6.1, step 6.2, choose, algebra]

8.1 (Localized isomorphism and finiteness.) Inverting the factors and differences makes every unequal-colour crossing $\tau_a$ equal to $s_a$ up to an invertible scalar, and every equal-colour crossing satisfy $s_a=1-(x_a-x_{a+1})\partial_a$, so the localized image contains all variable permutations $s_a$ and all multiplications $x_a$, i.e. the skew group algebra $A_n(I)=k(I)[x]\rtimes S_n$; with injectivity from step 7.1 and the evident containment of the image in the localized algebra, $\rho$ becomes an isomorphism after localization. For finiteness of homogeneous components: by step 6.2 the monomials with $w\in S_n$ and dot exponents $a_j\ge0$ span $R_n(Q)$; for fixed weight $\beta$ and degree $m$ the finitely many $w$ and $\nu$ occur, each crossing degree is one of finitely many integers bounded below, and each dot has positive degree $2d_{\nu_a}$, so the total dot degree of a spanning monomial of degree $m$ is bounded, leaving only finitely many exponent tuples; hence $\dim_kR(\beta)_m<\infty$. [step 6.2, step 7.1, algebra] ∎
