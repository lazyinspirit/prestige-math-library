## Statement

For a bicosimplicial complex $K^{p,r,q}$ of modules over a commutative ring, with $p,r\geq0$ and one fixed lower bound $q\geq a$, the alternating-face total complex and the diagonal alternating-face total complex are naturally chain-homotopy equivalent:
$$\operatorname{Tot}_{p,r,q}K^{p,r,q}\simeq\operatorname{Tot}_{n,q}K^{n,n,q}.$$
On the left total degree is $p+r+q$ and differential is $\delta_h+(-1)^p\delta_v+(-1)^{p+r}d$; on the right total degree is $n+q$ and differential is $\delta_{\mathrm{diag}}+(-1)^nd$. These comparisons preserve augmentation and apply to coherent derived diagrams after a supplied functorial strict resolution. They require no dimensional finiteness of the coefficient modules and no choice principle. Normalization can be applied on either side without changing the conclusion.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the bicosimplicial complex $K^{p,r,q}$ and the fixed lower bound $a$ of the Statement, read as follows. $K^{p,r,q}=0$ unless $p,r\geq0$ and $q\geq a$; horizontal operators $d^h_i:K^{p,r,q}\to K^{p-1,r,q}$ and $s^h_i:K^{p,r,q}\to K^{p+1,r,q}$, and vertical operators $d^v_j,s^v_j$, satisfy the simplicial identities in each index and commute between the two indices; the internal differential $d:K^{p,r,q}\to K^{p,r,q+1}$ squares to zero and commutes with every operator; $\delta_h,\delta_v,\delta_{\mathrm{diag}}$ are the alternating sums of the horizontal, vertical and diagonal face operators, and the displayed two differentials square to zero. Coefficients are modules over a commutative ring $R$. The cosimplicial variance, with all operators reversed, is covered by step 5.1.

[F1] [[lem-simplicial-normalization-prism-and-trivial-fibration-criterion]] proves the natural chain homotopy equivalence of normalized and unnormalized Moore complexes and constructs the prism chain homotopy induced by a simplicial-set homotopy.

[F2] [[prop-sum-and-product-totalisations-agree-on-finite-diagonal-double-complexes]] identifies the direct-sum and product totalisations of a double complex with finitely many nonzero objects on each diagonal.

[F3] [[def-direct-sum-total-complex-of-a-double-complex]] supplies the direct-sum total complex with its injection-indexed differential.

[F4] [[def-chain-complex-in-an-abelian-category]] fixes the chain-complex and differential conventions used below.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Universal models and their specified contractions. Let $Q_{p,r}$ be the free bisimplicial $R$-module whose value at $(p',r')$ is the free module $R[\Delta([p'],[p])\times\Delta([r'],[r])]$ on pairs of monotone maps; the Yoneda bijection $\operatorname{Hom}(Q_{p,r},M)\cong M_{p,r}$ sends a bisimplicial map to the image of the generator $(\mathrm{id}_{[p]},\mathrm{id}_{[r]})$. Under this bijection the diagonal alternating-face complex of $Q_{p,r}$ is the free simplicial $R$-module on the product of standard simplices $\Delta[p]\times\Delta[r]$, a chain complex in the sense of [F4], and the double total complex of $Q_{p,r}$ is the tensor product $C_*(\Delta[p];R)\otimes_RC_*(\Delta[r];R)$ of the two Moore complexes with the Koszul differential, which is the direct-sum total complex of [F3]. Each of the two complexes carries a specified contraction $H$ with $\partial H+H\partial=\mathrm{id}-\iota\varepsilon$ onto the vertex $\omega$: on the diagonal model, $(\alpha,\beta,t)\mapsto(\max(\alpha,tp),\max(\beta,tr))$ is a simplicial homotopy of $\Delta[p]\times\Delta[r]\times\Delta[1]$ from the identity to the constant map at $\omega$, and the prism formula of [F1] turns it into a chain homotopy from the identity to the constant chain map $\iota\varepsilon$; on the tensor product, $H(x\otimes y)=h_p(x)\otimes y+e(x)\otimes h_r(y)$, where $h_p,h_r$ are the two prism homotopies and $e=\iota\varepsilon$ is the vertex projector supported in degree zero, satisfies $\partial H+H\partial=\mathrm{id}-e\otimes e$ by direct expansion, because $e\partial=0$ kills the two cross terms and the remaining Koszul signs cancel. In both models every cycle of positive degree, and every degree-zero cycle annihilated by $\varepsilon$, is the boundary of its specified image under $H$. [F1, F3, F4, construct]

2.1 A natural map from the diagonal total to the double total. For each $m\geq0$ the degree-$m$ parts are $\operatorname{Diag}_m(M)=M_{m,m}$ and $\operatorname{Tot}_m(M)=\bigoplus_{p+r=m}M_{p,r}$, direct sums of representable functors; by Yoneda, natural maps $\varphi_m:\operatorname{Diag}_m\to\operatorname{Tot}_m$ are exactly choices of elements $c_m\in\operatorname{Tot}_m(Q_{m,m})$, namely their values on the universal elements $\mathrm{id}\in M_{m,m}$, a general element $x\in M_{m,m}$ being sent to the image of $c_m$ under its representing map $\tilde x:Q_{m,m}\to M$. Set $c_0$ equal to the generator of $(Q_{0,0})_{0,0}$; since $\operatorname{Diag}_0(M)=M_{0,0}=\operatorname{Tot}_0(M)$, this forces $\varphi_0=\mathrm{id}$. For $m\geq1$, having constructed $\varphi_{m-1}$ with $\partial\varphi_{m-1}=\varphi_{m-2}\partial$, put $b_m:=\varphi_{m-1}(\delta_{\mathrm{diag}}\,\mathrm{id})\in\operatorname{Tot}_{m-1}(Q_{m,m})$ and $c_m:=H_m(b_m)$, the contraction of step 1.1 applied to the positive-degree cycle $b_m$; for $m=1$ the element $b_1\in(Q_{1,1})_{0,0}$ is $(\omega_1,\omega_1)-(\omega_0,\omega_0)$, whose two vertex coefficients cancel under $\varepsilon$, so step 1.1 applies there as well. Then $\partial c_m=b_m$ and the induced natural maps satisfy $\partial\varphi_m=\varphi_{m-1}\partial$: on the universal element this is $\partial c_m=b_m$, and for general $x$ naturality of $\varphi_{m-1}$ and functoriality of the representing maps give $\partial\varphi_m(x)=\tilde x_*(\partial c_m)=\tilde x_*(b_m)=\varphi_{m-1}(\tilde x_*(\partial\,\mathrm{id}))=\varphi_{m-1}(\partial x)$, with $\varphi_m$ additive and natural because $x\mapsto\tilde x$ is $R$-linear and natural. [step 1.1, construct]

3.1 A natural map in the reverse direction. On the $(p,r)$-summand of $\operatorname{Tot}_m$ with $p+r=m$, natural maps $M_{p,r}\to M_{m,m}$ correspond by Yoneda to elements $c'_{p,r}\in\operatorname{Diag}_m(Q_{p,r})=(Q_{p,r})_{m,m}$, and a general element of $M_{p,r}$ is sent to the image of $c'_{p,r}$ under its representing map. Set $c'_{0,0}$ equal to the generator; for $m\geq1$ and each such $(p,r)$, having constructed $\psi_{m-1}$ with $\partial\psi_{m-1}=\psi_{m-2}\partial$, put $b'_{p,r}:=\psi_{m-1}(\partial\,\mathrm{id})\in\operatorname{Diag}_{m-1}(Q_{p,r})$, where $\partial\,\mathrm{id}$ is the boundary of the universal $(p,r)$-element in the total complex of $Q_{p,r}$, and $c'_{p,r}:=H'_m(b'_{p,r})$ for the diagonal contraction of step 1.1 on $Q_{p,r}$. As in step 2.1, $\partial b'_{p,r}=0$ by the induction hypothesis, and for $m=1$ the two summands $(p,r)=(1,0),(0,1)$ have $b'_{p,r}$ of degree zero with vertex coefficients cancelling, so in all cases $\partial c'_{p,r}=b'_{p,r}$; the induced maps satisfy $\partial\psi_m=\psi_{m-1}\partial$ by the same representing-map computation, and are additive and natural. [step 1.1, construct]

4.1 Homotopies between natural maps agreeing in degree zero, and the inverse identities. Let $F,F':C\to C'$ be natural chain maps between complexes whose degree parts are finite direct sums of representable functors $M\mapsto M_{p,r}$ or $M\mapsto M_{m,m}$, and suppose $F_0=F'_0$. Define natural maps $h_m:C_m\to C'_{m+1}$ by recursion: on a universal element $z$ of a representing summand of $C_m$, the element $\beta_m:=(F_m-F'_m)(z)-\partial h_{m-1}(\partial z)\in C'_m(Q)$ has $\partial\beta_m=(F-F')_{m-1}\partial z-\partial h_{m-1}\partial z=h_{m-2}\partial\partial z=0$, using $\partial h_{m-1}+h_{m-2}\partial=F-F'$ in degree $m-1$, so it is a cycle in a model complex $C'(Q)$ that is either a total or a diagonal model of step 1.1; set $h_m(z):=H(\beta_m)$ for $m\geq1$ and $h_0:=0$, fillings that are specified and linear, and extend by the representing maps. Then $\partial h_m+h_{m-1}\partial=F-F'$ holds in every degree by construction and by naturality. Taking $C=C'=\operatorname{Diag}$ and $F=\psi\varphi$, $F'=\mathrm{id}$, whose degree-zero parts are both the identity, gives $\psi\varphi\simeq\mathrm{id}$; taking $C=C'=\operatorname{Tot}$ and $F=\varphi\psi$, $F'=\mathrm{id}$ gives $\varphi\psi\simeq\mathrm{id}$. Hence $\varphi$ and $\psi$ are natural chain-homotopy equivalences inverse to each other. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 Cosimplicial variance and an internal differential. Two formal extensions. (a) Every formula and identity of steps 1.1-3.1 is a finite $R$-linear combination of composites of structure operators, together with the explicit model fillings; equivalently, each identity is an identity between such combinations in the free additive category generated by the simplex category. Reversing every arrow turns the simplicial identities into the cosimplicial identities and preserves each identity term by term, so the same construction compares the total complex of horizontal and vertical coface operators with its diagonal total. (b) Suppose in addition that an endomorphism $d$ squares to zero and commutes with every structure operator, as for the internal degree $q\geq a$ of the Statement; perform the construction family-wise in $q$. The formulas involve no $d$, so each constructed map commutes with $d$, and the displayed sign twists are exactly the ones tracked by the bidegree: $\varphi,\psi$ preserve $p+r$, so on their images $(-1)^{p+r}=(-1)^n$; the homotopies raise $p+r$ by one, so the internal contributions to $\partial H+H\partial$ are $(-1)^{p+r}Hd+(-1)^{p+r+1}dH=(-1)^{p+r}(Hd-dH)=0$. Thus the identities of steps 2.1-3.1 lift verbatim to the differentials $\delta_h+(-1)^p\delta_v+(-1)^{p+r}d$ and $\delta_{\mathrm{diag}}+(-1)^nd$. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Finiteness, normalization, augmentation and derived presentations. Since $K^{p,r,q}=0$ unless $p,r\geq0$ and $q\geq a$, in each total degree $m$ only finitely many summands occur, on both sides; every constructed map and homotopy is a finite sum over such summands, so [F2] permits using either total notation and no dimension hypothesis on the coefficient modules is needed. All fillers are specified values of explicit contractions built from face and degeneracy operators, so no choice principle is used. Since $\varphi_0=\psi_0=\mathrm{id}$ and $h_0=0$, the comparisons preserve the augmentation. By [F1] the normalization inclusion is a natural chain homotopy equivalence; applying it on the diagonal side, or in either simplicial direction of the total, and composing with the comparison of steps 2.1-3.1 gives natural chain homotopy equivalences between any mixture of normalized and unnormalized totalizations, so normalization may be applied on either side. A supplied functorial strict resolution presents a coherent derived diagram by strict bicosimplicial complexes; the comparison is natural in the coefficient diagram, hence compatible with the resolution maps, independent of the chosen presentation, and descends to a natural isomorphism of the derived objects. [F1, F2, step 2.1, step 4.1, step 5.1] ∎
