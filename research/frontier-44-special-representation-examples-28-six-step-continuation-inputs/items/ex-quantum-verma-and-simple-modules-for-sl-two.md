## Example

Let $I=\{1\}$, $A=(2)$, so that $U_q(\mathfrak g)=U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ with
generators $E,F,K^{\pm1}$ and $q_1=q$, and let $m\in\mathbb Z$; write
$\lambda$ for the weight with $\lambda(h_1)=m$ and
$[t]:=[t]_{1}=(q^{t}-q^{-t})/(q-q^{-1})$
([[def-kac-moody-integral-and-dominant-integral-weights]],
[[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]).

The quantum Verma module $M(m)=M(\lambda)$ has $\mathbb Q(q)$-basis
$\{v_t:=F^tv_0\}_{t\ge0}$ with $v_0=v_\lambda$ and weights $m-2t$, and the
actions are
$$Kv_t=q^{m-2t}v_t,\qquad Fv_t=v_{t+1},\qquad Ev_t=[m-t+1]_qv_{t-1}\ \ (t\ge1),\qquad Ev_0=0$$
([[def-quantum-verma-module-and-simple-highest-weight-quotient]],
[[thm-quantized-sl-two-string-formulas]]), and:

(a) if $m\ge0$, the submodule generated by $F^{m+1}v_0$ is
$\bigoplus_{t\ge m+1}\mathbb Q(q)v_t$, and the quotient
$\tilde L(m)=M(m)/U_q(\mathfrak g)F^{m+1}v_0$ is the $(m+1)$-dimensional simple
module with basis $v_0,\dots,v_m$, $Kv_t=q^{m-2t}v_t$,
$Fv_t=[t+1]v_{t+1}$, $Ev_t=[m-t+1]v_{t-1}$; for $m=0$ this is the trivial
one-dimensional module;

(b) if $m=-1$, then $[m-t+1]=[-t]=-[t]\ne0$ for every $t\ge1$, so $M(-1)$ is
irreducible, infinite-dimensional and not integrable: the simple module
$L(-1)$ has a non-dominant, non-integral highest weight;

(c) for every $m<0$, $[m-t+1]$ vanishes only at the nonpositive integer
$t=m+1\le0$, so as in (b) $M(m)$ is irreducible, infinite-dimensional and not
integrable; the dominance hypothesis $m\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ in
[[thm-integrable-quantum-highest-weight-module-exists-and-is-unique]] cannot be
relaxed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The rank-one Cartan datum, the Verma module $M(m)=U_q^-v_0$ with
$U_q^-=\mathbb Q(q)[F]$, and the generators $E,F,K^{\pm1}$.

[F1] $M(\lambda)$ is free of rank one over $U_q^-$ with basis
$v_t=F^tv_0$, has weights $\lambda-Q_+=\{\lambda-t\alpha_1\}=\{m-2t:t\ge0\}$,
and satisfies $Kv_0=q^mv_0$, $Ev_0=0$, $KF=q^{-2}FK$
([[def-quantum-verma-module-and-simple-highest-weight-quotient]],
[[def-quantum-weight-module-and-integrability]]).

[F2] $EF^t=F^tE+F^{t-1}\frac{1}{q-q^{-1}}\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}(q^{-2s}K-q^{2s}K^{-1})$
for $t\ge1$, and $[m-t+1]=\frac{q^{m-t+1}-q^{-m+t-1}}{q-q^{-1}}$
([[thm-quantized-sl-two-string-formulas]] (ii),
[[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]).

[F3] $[j]=0$ for an integer $j$ if and only if $j=0$, and $[j]\ne0$ otherwise;
more precisely $[-t]=-[t]$ for $t\ge1$
([[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]).

[F4] For $\lambda\in P^+$ the Serre quotient
$M(\lambda)/\sum_iU_q(\mathfrak g)F_i^{\lambda(h_i)+1}v_\lambda$ is the simple
module $L(\lambda)$ and is universal among integrable cyclic highest weight
modules of weight $\lambda$
([[thm-integrable-quantum-highest-weight-module-exists-and-is-unique]]).



## Verification

**Proof technique:** read the rank-one Verma string from the commutation formula, split off the finite string when the highest weight is nonnegative, and prove irreducibility in the negative cases by the minimal-index raising argument.

1.1 Basis and action. By [F1], $M(m)$ is free of rank one over $\mathbb Q(q)[F]$ with basis $v_t=F^tv_0$, weights $m-2t$, $Kv_t=q^{m-2t}v_t$, $Fv_t=v_{t+1}$ and $Ev_0=0$. For $t\ge1$, [F2] evaluated on $v_0$ (using $Kv_0=q^mv_0$ and $Ev_0=0$) gives $Ev_t=F^{t-1}\frac{1}{q-q^{-1}}\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}(q^{m-2s}-q^{-m+2s})v_0=\frac{q^{m-t+1}-q^{-m+t-1}}{q-q^{-1}}v_{t-1}=[m-t+1]v_{t-1}$, the geometric sum being $\frac{q^{m-t+1}-q^{-m+t-1}}{q-q^{-1}}$. In particular $Ev_t=0$ if and only if $m-t+1=0$, i.e. $t=m+1$, by [F3]. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 Case $m\ge0$. For $t=m+1\ge1$ the vector $F^{m+1}v_0$ is annihilated by $E$ by step 1.1 and is a $K$-eigenvector, so it is a highest vector; the submodule it generates is $U_q^-F^{m+1}v_0=\operatorname{span}\{v_t:t\ge m+1\}=\bigoplus_{t\ge m+1}\mathbb Q(q)v_t$, and the quotient $\tilde L(m)$ has basis the images of $v_0,\dots,v_m$ with the displayed action (the relation $F^{m+1}v_0=0$ truncates the string). It is simple: if $0\ne x=\sum_{t=T}^{T'}c_tv_t$ with $c_T\ne0$ lies in a submodule, then $E^Tx$ is a nonzero multiple of $v_0$ by step 1.1 (each application lowers the index by one with the nonzero coefficient $[m-t+1]$ for $t\le T\le m$), so the submodule contains $v_0$ and then all $v_t$ with $t\le m$; for $m=0$, $Ev_0=0=Fv_0$ gives the trivial module. This proves (a). [step 1.1, F1, F4, algebra]

2.2 Case $m=-1$. By step 1.1 and [F3], $Ev_t=[-t]v_{t-1}=-[t]v_{t-1}\ne0$ for all $t\ge1$, so no $v_t$ with $t\ge1$ is annihilated by $E$. If $0\ne x=\sum_{t=T}^{T'}c_tv_t$ with $c_T\ne0$ generates a nonzero submodule, then $E^Tx$ is a nonzero multiple of $v_0$ (the coefficient is $\prod_{j=0}^{T-1}[m-j+1]=\prod(-[j+1])\ne0$), so $v_0$ lies in the submodule, which is then all of $M(-1)$; hence $M(-1)$ is irreducible. It is infinite-dimensional by [F1] and not integrable because $F^tv_0=v_t\ne0$ for all $t$, so $F$ is not locally nilpotent on $v_0$. The highest weight $m=-1$ is not dominant integral. This proves (b). [step 1.1, F1, F2, F3, algebra]

3.1 General $m<0$. If $m\le-1$, then $m+1\le0$, so the only zero $t=m+1$ of $[m-t+1]$ is not a positive integer and step 1.1 gives $Ev_t\ne0$ for all $t\ge1$; the minimal-index argument of step 2.2 applies verbatim with the coefficients $\prod_{j=0}^{T-1}[m-j+1]\ne0$, so $M(m)$ is irreducible, infinite-dimensional and not integrable, and its simple quotient is $L(m)$ with non-dominant highest weight. Together with (a) this is the complete rank-one classification of Verma modules by the sign of $m$ and shows that the dominance hypothesis of [F4] is essential. All computations are finite evaluations of polynomials in $q$; no choice is used. [step 1.1, step 2.2, F1, F3, F4, algebra] ∎
