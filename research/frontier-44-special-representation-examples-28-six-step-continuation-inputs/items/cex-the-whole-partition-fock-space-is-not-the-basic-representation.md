## Statement refuted

The assertion that the entire partition Fock module $F$ is the basic
representation, i.e. that $F=M=U_q(\mathfrak g)|\varnothing\rangle\cong
V(\Lambda_0)$ for every finite $e\ge2$, is false. For every finite $e\ge2$ the
Fock module is strictly larger than $M$: the raw basis vector $|1^e\rangle$ is
not in $M$, and its ambient lower lift $G(1^e)$ is also not in $M$. The full
field decomposition is
$F=\bigoplus_{k\ge0}V(\Lambda_0-k\delta)^{\oplus p(k)}$, the $\Lambda_0$
multiplicity is $1$, and the higher constituents are nonzero; consequently the
full crystal has all partitions as vertices while its empty component is exactly
the $e$-regular partitions.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The partition Fock space with its crystal and global basis, the module $M=V(\Lambda_0)$, and the decomposition theorem.

[F1] The empty component of the full crystal is exactly the $e$-regular partitions and equals the crystal of $M$; the full crystal is a graph on all partitions; the decomposition is $F=\bigoplus_{k}V(\Lambda_0-k\delta)^{\oplus p(k)}$ with choice used only there ([[thm-the-empty-component-is-the-highest-weight-crystal-b-lambda-zero]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F2] $M$ is the simple integrable module generated by the vacuum and receives the lower global basis of the empty component ([[prop-the-empty-partition-generates-an-integrable-highest-weight-submodule]], [[thm-the-basic-representation-global-basis-is-unitriangular-in-the-partition-basis]]).

[F3] The top weight $\Lambda_0$ is maximal, so the top isotypic splitting of the crystal lattice gives $F_0\cap M$ with residue basis exactly the empty component; the shape $1^e$ has all parts equal and multiplicity $e$, so it is not $e$-regular ([[thm-crystal-bases-of-integrable-modules-decompose-into-highest-weight-crystals]], [[def-level-one-partition-fock-space-and-node-residues]]).



## Verification

1.1 Strictness. By [F1] the full field decomposition contains the summands $V(\Lambda_0-k\delta)$ for every $k\ge0$ with multiplicity $p(k)\ge1$, and the $\Lambda_0$ summand occurs with multiplicity $p(0)=1$; since $p(k)\ge1$ for $k\ge1$ and those constituents are nonzero, $F$ is strictly larger than its $\Lambda_0$ constituent $M$. [F1, given, algebra]

1.2 Raw-vector exclusion. The shape $1^e$ is not $e$-regular: all its parts equal $1$ and occur $e$ times, so it lies outside the empty component by [F1]; since the residue basis of $F_0\cap M$ is exactly the empty component by [F3], every vector in $F_0\cap M$ has residue a combination of regular labels. The raw vector $|1^e\rangle$ has nonzero residue in $F_0/qF_0$ equal to the nonregular shape $1^e$, so $|1^e\rangle\notin M$; the same argument applies to $G(1^e)$, which is congruent to the same shape modulo $qF_0$. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 Conclusion. The whole partition Fock space is not the basic representation: it has strictly more constituents than the single $V(\Lambda_0)$, and even the explicit raw and lower-lift vectors $|1^e\rangle$ and $G(1^e)$ lie outside $M$; the nonregular ambient lower vectors are not a basis of a single highest module. [step 1.1, step 1.2] ∎
