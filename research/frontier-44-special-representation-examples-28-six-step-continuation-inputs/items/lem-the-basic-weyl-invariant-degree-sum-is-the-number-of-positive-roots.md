## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice, inherited from invariant theory. Let $\Phi$ be a
finite reduced crystallographic root system of rank $r$ in the real Cartan
space $E$ with Weyl group $W$
([[def-finite-weyl-root-system-lattice-and-chamber-conventions]],
[[def-root-reflections-and-the-weyl-group-action]]), and let
$S=\mathbb C[E]$ carry the induced graded $W$-action with Cartan generators in
ordinary degree $1$. Let $d_1,\dots,d_r$ be the ordinary degrees of basic
invariants, so that $S^W=\mathbb C[w_1,\dots,w_r]$ with $w_i$ homogeneous of
degree $d_i$ ([[thm-chevalley-shephard-todd-for-finite-weyl-groups]]). Then
$$\sum_{i=1}^r(d_i-1)=|\Phi^+|.$$
With the page degree normalization $\deg E=2$ the coinvariant algebra
$S/(S^W_+S)$ has top degree $2\sum_i(d_i-1)=2|\Phi^+|=|\Phi|$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite reduced crystallographic root system $\Phi$ of rank $r$ in the real Cartan space $E$ with its positive definite form and Weyl group $W$, the symmetric algebra $S=\mathbb C[E]$ with the induced graded action, and homogeneous basic invariants $w_1,\dots,w_r$ of degrees $d_1,\dots,d_r$.

[F1] Under AC, $w_1,\dots,w_r$ are algebraically independent and generate $S^W$, and $\prod_i d_i=|W|$ ([[thm-chevalley-shephard-todd-for-finite-weyl-groups]], [[lem-weyl-coinvariant-hilbert-series-has-order-w-dimension]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F2] The open Weyl chambers are the connected components of $E$ minus the union of the root hyperplanes $\alpha^\perp$, $\alpha\in\Phi$, and $W$ acts simply transitively on them; in particular the stabilizer of every chamber is trivial ([[def-finite-weyl-root-system-lattice-and-chamber-conventions]], [[thm-the-weyl-group-acts-simply-transitively-on-weyl-chambers]]).

[F3] The Weyl group is generated by the root reflections $s_\alpha$, $s_\alpha=s_{-\alpha}$, and $\Phi^+$ is a set of representatives for $\{\pm\alpha\}$; every root hyperplane is $\alpha^\perp$ for exactly one positive root $\alpha$ ([[def-root-reflections-and-the-weyl-group-action]], [[def-finite-weyl-root-system-lattice-and-chamber-conventions]]).

[F4] The finite Weyl group acts by isometries of a positive definite form, so all its eigenvalues lie on the unit circle.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Molien trace identity: for $|t|<1$, $$\operatorname{Hilb}(S^W,t)=\sum_{d\ge0}\dim_{\mathbb C}(S^W)_dt^d=\frac1{|W|}\sum_{w\in W}\det(1-tw|_E)^{-1}.$$ Indeed the $W$-averaging operator $\pi=(1/|W|)\sum_ww$ is a projection onto $S^W$ in each finite-dimensional degree $S_d$, so $\dim(S^W)_d=\operatorname{tr}\pi|_{S_d}=(1/|W|)\sum_w\operatorname{tr}(w|_{S_d})$; on the other hand, for an operator with eigenvalues $\mu_1,\dots,\mu_r$ on $E$, and hence $\mu_1^{-1},\dots,\mu_r^{-1}$ on $E^*$, the degree-$d$ trace $\operatorname{tr}(w|_{S_d})$ is the complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial $h_d(\mu_1^{-1},\dots,\mu_r^{-1})$, whose generating function is $\prod_i(1-\mu_i^{-1}t)^{-1}=\det(1-tw^{-1}|_E)^{-1}$; averaging over $w$ and reindexing by $w\mapsto w^{-1}$ gives the displayed formula. [F1, algebra]

1.2 For $w\in W$ let $f$ be the dimension of its fixed space in $E$. Then $\det(1-tw|_E)=(1-t)^fg_w(t)$ with $g_w(t)=\prod_{\lambda\neq1}(1-t\lambda)$ over the remaining eigenvalues $\lambda$ of $w$ and $g_w(1)=\prod_{\lambda\neq1}(1-\lambda)\neq0$; all eigenvalues lie on the unit circle. In particular if $w\neq1$ fixes a hyperplane pointwise, then $f=r-1$, the remaining eigenvalue is $-1$, and $\det(1-tw|_E)=(1-t)^{r-1}(1+t)$. [F4, algebra]

1.3 If $w\in W$, $w\neq1$, fixes a hyperplane $H$ pointwise, then $H=\alpha^\perp$ for some root $\alpha$. A hyperplane is not the union of the finitely many proper closed subsets $H\cap\beta^\perp$, so choose $x\in H$ with $(x,\beta)\neq0$ for every root $\beta$; then $x$ lies in an open chamber $C$, and $w(x)=x$ gives $x\in C\cap w(C)$. As $w$ permutes the chambers, $C\cap w(C)\neq\varnothing$ forces $w(C)=C$, so $w$ lies in the stabilizer of $C$, which is trivial by simple transitivity. Hence $H$ is contained in a root hyperplane, and since both are hyperplanes, $H=\alpha^\perp$ for a root $\alpha$. [F2, F3, choose]

1.4 Put $u=1-t$. Since $1-t^{d_i}=1-(1-u)^{d_i}=d_iu-\frac{d_i(d_i-1)}2u^2+O(u^3)$, one has $(1-t^{d_i})^{-1}=d_i^{-1}u^{-1}(1+\frac{d_i-1}2u+O(u^2))$, and therefore $$\prod_{i=1}^r(1-t^{d_i})^{-1}=\Bigl(\prod_id_i\Bigr)^{-1}u^{-r}\Bigl(1+\frac{\sum_i(d_i-1)}2u+O(u^2)\Bigr)=\frac1{|W|}u^{-r}\Bigl(1+\frac{\sum_i(d_i-1)}2u+O(u^2)\Bigr),$$ the last equality by [F1]; this product equals $\operatorname{Hilb}(S^W,t)$ for $|t|<1$ by [F1]. [F1, algebra]

2.1 The map $\alpha\mapsto s_\alpha$ induces a bijection from the set $\Phi^+$ of positive roots onto the set of reflections in $W$; consequently $W$ has exactly $|\Phi^+|$ reflections. Injectivity: $s_\alpha=s_\beta$ forces the two reflections to have the same fixed hyperplane, so $\beta=\pm\alpha$, and among positive roots $\beta=\alpha$. Surjectivity: every reflection of $W$ has a fixed hyperplane, which is $\alpha^\perp$ for some root $\alpha$ by step 1.3, and the reflection of $E$ with fixed hyperplane $\alpha^\perp$ is unique, namely $s_\alpha$. Since $s_\alpha=s_{-\alpha}$ and every root hyperplane is $\alpha^\perp$ for exactly one positive root by [F3], distinct positive roots give distinct reflections. [F2, F3, step 1.3, algebra]

3.1 Expanding the right-hand side of step 1.1 at $t=1$ in the variable $u=1-t$ gives $$\frac1{|W|}\sum_{w\in W}\det(1-tw|_E)^{-1}=\frac1{|W|}\Bigl(u^{-r}+\frac{|\Phi^+|}2u^{-(r-1)}+O(u^{-(r-2)})\Bigr).$$ The identity $w=1$ contributes $u^{-r}$. A nonidentity $w$ with fixed space of dimension $r-1$ is a reflection, and there are exactly $|\Phi^+|$ of them by step 2.1; each contributes $\det(1-tw)^{-1}=(1-t)^{-(r-1)}(1+t)^{-1}=u^{-(r-1)}(\frac12+O(u))$ by step 1.2, because $(1+t)^{-1}=(2-u)^{-1}=\frac12+O(u)$. Every other nonidentity $w$ has $f\le r-2$, hence contributes $u^{-f}\cdot O(1)=O(u^{-(r-2)})$ by step 1.2. [F3, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Steps 1.4 and 3.1 compute the same meromorphic function of $u$ near $u=0$; comparing the coefficients of $u^{-(r-1)}$, which are finite, gives $\frac1{|W|}\cdot\frac{\sum_i(d_i-1)}2=\frac1{|W|}\cdot\frac{|\Phi^+|}2$, hence $\sum_i(d_i-1)=|\Phi^+|$. [step 1.4, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 With the page degree normalization $\deg E=2$ the basic invariant $w_i$ has page degree $2d_i$, so the coinvariant algebra has Hilbert series $\prod_i(1-t^{2d_i})/(1-t^2)^r$ and its top degree is $\sum_i2d_i-2r=2\sum_i(d_i-1)=2|\Phi^+|=|\Phi|$, by step 4.1 and the published degree computation for the coinvariant algebra. [F1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
