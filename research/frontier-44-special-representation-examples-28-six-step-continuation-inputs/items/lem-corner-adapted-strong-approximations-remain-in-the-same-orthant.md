## Statement

Let $F:K\to[0,\infty)^c\times\mathbb R^{n-c}$ be a finite whole-star smooth
embedding, and write $\rho_iF$ for its first $c$ coordinate functions. Assume
for each original simplex $\sigma$ and each $i$ that
$Z_{i,\sigma}=\{x\in\sigma:\rho_iF(x)=0\}$ is a union of faces and that, at
every $b\in Z_{i,\sigma}$, the linear form $d(\rho_iF)_b$ on the tangent cone of
$\sigma$ is nonnegative with zero set precisely the tangent cone of
$Z_{i,\sigma}$. These are the adapted face/tangent conditions, not merely
simplex rank conditions.

There is $\delta>0$ such that every strong $\delta$-approximation $G$ preserving
each of these coordinate planes on the specified zero subcomplexes maps all of
$K$ into the same closed orthant and has exactly the same coordinate zero sets.
It is an embedding for this choice of $\delta$. Thus plane-preserving fitting
and extension can be used in corner charts when the initial pieces satisfy these
adapted face conditions.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the finite whole-star smooth embedding $F:K\to[0,\infty)^c\times\mathbb R^{n-c}$, its coordinate functions $\rho_1F,\dots,\rho_cF$, the zero sets $Z_{i,\sigma}=\{x\in\sigma:\rho_iF(x)=0\}$ (unions of faces), the adapted face/tangent hypotheses, and a strong $\delta$-approximation $G$ that preserves each coordinate plane on the specified zero subcomplexes ([[def-relative-smooth-triangulation-and-projection-preserving-fitting]]).

[F1] Whole-star smooth embeddings are stable under sufficiently small strong approximation: there is $\delta_0>0$ such that every strong $\delta_0$-approximation of $F$ is again a whole-star embedding ([[lem-piecewise-smooth-embeddings-are-stable-under-fine-c-one-approximation]]).

[F2] Segment integration bounds the change of a smooth function by the supremum of its directional derivative times the segment length, and every continuous positive function on a nonempty compact set has a positive minimum ([[cor-vector-valued-ftc-and-lipschitz-bound]], [[thm-extreme-value-metric]]).

[F3] Orthant preservation means $\rho_iG\ge0$ for every $i$ and equality of the zero sets; the coordinate planes are preserved in the sense that $\rho_iG=\rho_iF=0$ on the given zero subcomplexes. All these notions are those of [[def-relative-smooth-triangulation-and-projection-preserving-fitting]].

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Local shape of the zero sets. Fix $i$ and an original simplex $\sigma$ with $Z:=Z_{i,\sigma}\ne\varnothing$. For $b\in Z$ the form $\ell_b:=d(\rho_iF)_b$ is nonnegative on the convex cone $C_b:=\sigma-b$ and vanishes exactly on the tangent cone of $Z$ at $b$. As $Z$ is a union of faces of $\sigma$, that tangent cone is the union of the cones $F-b$ over the faces $F$ of $\sigma$ with $b\in F\subseteq Z$; being also the zero set of the supporting form $\ell_b$, it is a face of $C_b$, and a face of $C_b$ that is a union of faces of $C_b$ is equal to its maximal-dimensional member. Hence there is a largest face $F_b$ of $\sigma$ with $b\in F_b\subseteq Z$, and the zero set of $\ell_b$ in $C_b$ is exactly the cone $F_b-b$; in other words $\ell_b$ is strictly positive on every vector of $C_b$ pointing out of $F_b$, and only the directions staying in $F_b$ are annihilated. On the finitely many rays of $C_b$ generated by the vertices of $\sigma$ outside $F_b$ the values of $\ell_b$ are strictly positive, so a positive minimum $\alpha_b$ is attained; and because the finitely many derivatives $d(\rho_iF)_b$ depend continuously on $b$, the same lower bound persists on a small neighbourhood of $b$ in $\sigma$: for every $c$ in that neighbourhood and every unit vector $h$ of $C_c$ whose direction is not tangent to $Z$ at $c$, the derivative $d(\rho_iF)_c(h)$ is bounded below by $\alpha_b/2$. The finitely many points $b$ and then the finitely many zero faces and simplices give a single positive lower bound $\alpha>0$ valid near all of $Z$. [F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 A distance-uniform lower bound. There is $a>0$ with $\rho_iF(x)\ge a\operatorname{dist}(x,Z_{i,\sigma})$ for every $x\in\sigma$ whenever $Z_{i,\sigma}\ne\varnothing$. Near a zero face one argues as follows. Project barycentric coordinates of $x\in\sigma$ onto that face by deleting the coordinates outside it and renormalizing the remaining ones; where the sum of the retained coordinates is at least $1/2$ the barycentric projection $\pi(x)$ is defined, continuous, lies in the face and hence in $Z$, and the segment from $x$ to $\pi(x)$ is a multiple, of uniformly bounded factor, of the distance from $x$ to the face. Writing $x=\pi(x)+w$ with $w$ pointing out of the face, the fundamental theorem of calculus gives $\rho_iF(x)-\rho_iF(\pi(x))=\int_0^1 d(\rho_iF)_{c(t)}(w)\,dt$ with $c(t)=\pi(x)+tw$; for $x$ outside $Z$ the direction of $w$ is not tangent to $Z$ along the segment, and after shrinking to a neighbourhood where the derivative varies by less than half of the bound of step 1.1 each integrand is at least $\alpha|w|/2$, whence $\rho_iF(x)\ge(\alpha/2)|w|\ge a\operatorname{dist}(x,Z)$. The points at distance bounded below from $Z$ form a compact set on which the continuous positive ratio $\rho_iF(y)/\operatorname{dist}(y,Z)$ has a positive minimum by [F2]; the finitely many zero faces and simplices give one $a>0$, and the argument is vacuous when $Z_{i,\sigma}=\varnothing$. [F2, step 1.1]

3.1 Perturbing the coordinate functions. For $x\in\sigma$ choose a closest point $z\in Z_{i,\sigma}$ when the zero set is nonempty; it exists by compactness of $Z_{i,\sigma}$ and the segment $[z,x]$ lies in the convex simplex. Since $\rho_iG(z)=\rho_iF(z)=0$ on the zero subcomplex, segment integration of the piecewise smooth difference along $[z,x]$, split into the finitely many refined pieces and added, gives $|\rho_iG(x)-\rho_iF(x)|\le\delta|x-z|=\delta\operatorname{dist}(x,Z_{i,\sigma})$, using the derivative bound of the strong approximation. Choose $\delta<a/2$ with $a$ from step 2.1. Then $\rho_iG(x)\ge(a/2)\operatorname{dist}(x,Z_{i,\sigma})$, so $\rho_iG\ge0$ on $\sigma$ with equality exactly on $Z_{i,\sigma}$. If $Z_{i,\sigma}$ is empty, then $\rho_iF$ has a positive minimum $m_\sigma$ on the compact simplex by [F2], and the value error $|G-F|<\delta$ with $\delta<m_\sigma/2$ keeps $\rho_iG$ positive on all of $\sigma$. [F2, F3, step 2.1]

4.1 Conclusion. Take the minimum of the finitely many positive bounds $a$, $m_\sigma$ and $\delta_0$ of steps 2.1, 3.1 and [F1] over all coordinates $i$ and simplices $\sigma$; call it $\delta$. Every strong $\delta$-approximation $G$ preserving the zero subcomplexes then has $\rho_iG\ge0$ with zero set exactly $Z_{i,\sigma}$ on every simplex, for every $i$: all coordinate functions are nonnegative and the coordinate zero sets are exactly the prescribed ones, so $G$ maps $K$ into the same closed orthant with the same boundary structure. By [F1] $G$ is again a whole-star embedding. This supplies the side control that affine-plane preservation alone does not give, as the half-plane warning of [F3] records. [F1, step 3.1] ∎
