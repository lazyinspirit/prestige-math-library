## Statement

Let $\lambda\in P$ be a dominant integral label weight and let
$\tilde L(\lambda)$ be the nonzero integrable quotient of the Verma module
$M(\lambda)$ of [[lem-quantum-highest-weight-serre-quotient-is-integrable]]
([[def-quantum-verma-module-and-simple-highest-weight-quotient]]), with
highest vector $v_\lambda$ and label grading
([[def-quantum-weight-module-and-integrability]]). Put
$R:=\mathbb Q[q]_{(q-1)}\subset\mathbb Q(q)$.

(a) **Character.** Every nonzero graded quotient $V$ of $\tilde L(\lambda)$
whose highest line is nonzero is generated by its highest vector and has in
every label the same dimension as the classical simple integrable highest
weight module of the regularized minimal realization of the same Cartan datum
([[lem-finite-support-regularization-of-quantum-label-weight-modules]],
[[thm-kac-moody-verma-module-has-a-unique-simple-quotient]]); in particular the
character is independent of $V$. No global $\mathbb Z[q,q^{-1}]$-freeness and
no crystal or global basis is used.

(b) **Uniqueness.** Consequently $\tilde L(\lambda)$ is simple, hence is the
canonical simple highest weight module $L(\lambda)$, and every integrable cyclic
highest weight module of highest weight $\lambda$ is a quotient of
$L(\lambda)$.

(c) **The $R$-lattice.** For a nonzero highest vector $v$ of $V$, the lattice
$V_R:=\sum_wR\,F_wv$, summed over all ordinary words $w$ in the negative
generators, is stable under every generator of the integral form $U_A$ of
[[def-lusztig-divided-power-integral-form]], is finite free over $R$ on each
label weight, and is spanned by $v$; its $q=1$ fibre
$V_R/(q-1)V_R$ is the classical simple integrable highest weight module of the
regularized minimal realization. Here the Gaussian binomials
$\binom{m}{r}_i$ of [[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]
are $R$-valued at integer labels, and the divided-power denominators
$[r]_i!$ and $q_i^{\pm1}$ are $R$-units.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The dominant integral label $\lambda$, the module $V$ with highest
vector $v\ne0$ and its $R$-lattice $V_R$, and the local ring $R$.

[F1] $R=\mathbb Q[q]_{(q-1)}$ is a DVR with uniformizer $q-1$
([[def-discrete-valuation-ring]]). Indeed, $\mathbb Q[q]$ is Euclidean, hence
a PID with ideals $(f)$; the ideals of the localization $R$ are the ideals of
$\mathbb Q[q]$ contained in the maximal ideal $(q-1)$, namely $0$ and
$((q-1)^m)$, because $(f)\subseteq(q-1)$ exactly when $(q-1)\mid f$; so the
maximal ideal of $R$ is $(q-1)R$, its residue field is
$\mathbb Q[q]/(q-1)\cong\mathbb Q$, and every nonzero element of the fraction
field $\mathbb Q(q)$ is $(q-1)^m$ times a unit of $R$ for a unique $m\in
\mathbb Z$ (multiplicities of the root $1$ in a numerator and denominator),
which is a discrete valuation with valuation ring $R$. Every element of $R$
has a well-defined value at $q=1$ (evaluate a representing fraction $f/g$ with
$g(1)\ne0$), and it is a unit precisely when that value is nonzero: if
$a(1)\ne0$ then $1/a$ is represented by a fraction with nonzero denominator
value, while $a(1)=0$ means $a\in(q-1)R$. Consequently $q$, $q_i=q^{d_i}$ and
$q-1+x$ for $x\in(q-1)R$ are units precisely when their values at $q=1$ are
nonzero. Thus $R$ is a local PID with maximal ideal $(q-1)R$.

[F2] $q_i^{\pm1}\in R^{\times}$, and for $r\ge1$,
$[r]_i!\,|_{q=1}=r!\ne0$; hence $[r]_i!\in R^{\times}$. For a label
$\mu\in P$ and $m:=\langle\mu,h_i\rangle\in\mathbb Z$,
$[m]_i=(q_i^{m}-q_i^{-m})/(q_i-q_i^{-1})$ lies in
$\mathbb Z[q_i^{\pm1}]\subseteq R$
([[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]).

[F3] $V$ is generated by $v$ as a $U_q(\mathfrak g)$-module, $V=U_q^-v$, and
its label spaces $V_\mu$ are finite-dimensional with
$\mathrm{wt}(V)\subseteq\lambda-Q_+$; the $K_h$ act on $V_\mu$ by
$q^{\langle\mu,h\rangle}$
([[def-quantum-verma-module-and-simple-highest-weight-quotient]],
[[lem-quantum-highest-weight-serre-quotient-is-integrable]],
[[def-quantum-weight-module-and-integrability]]).

[F4] The toral binomials satisfy $T_{i,c,0}=1$ and
$T_{i,c,t}=T_{i,c,t-1}(K_iq_i^{c-t+1}-K_i^{-1}q_i^{-c+t-1})/(q_i^{t}-q_i^{-t})$;
they commute with all $K_h$ and all $F_j$; and
$E_iF_j-F_jE_i=\delta_{ij}T_{i,0,1}$, $E_jT_{i,c,t}=T_{i,c-a_{ij},t}E_j$,
$F_jT_{i,c,t}=T_{i,c+a_{ij},t}F_j$
([[lem-divided-power-and-toral-quantum-binomial-reordering-identities]],
[[def-lusztig-divided-power-integral-form]],
[[def-positive-negative-and-toral-quantum-subalgebras]]).

[F5] On the $A$-module $U_A^0$, the products
$\prod_iK_i^{\delta_i}T_{i,0,t_i}$ are an $A$-basis of the simple toral parts;
in particular $T_{i,0,1}$ is a nonzero element and every element of $U_A^0$
acts on a weight vector of label $\mu$ by the scalar obtained by evaluating the
K-polynomial at $q^{\langle\mu,h\rangle}$
([[lem-unmodified-integral-torus-has-a-quantum-binomial-basis]],
[[def-lusztig-divided-power-integral-form]]).

[F6] The regularization supplier: for the finite set of labels of $V$ there is
an auxiliary Cartan datum with the same $A,D$ and fundamental weights, a
perfect toral pairing, and a finite-support category comparison carrying $V$ to
a graded module with the same labels and actions, and reconstructing the old
$K_h$ from the labels
([[lem-finite-support-regularization-of-quantum-label-weight-modules]]). In the
auxiliary datum every label is a $\mathbb C$-linear functional on the Cartan
and the classical Kac-Moody theory of the following facts applies.

[F7] For a finite symmetrizable GCM over $\mathbb C$: the Casimir $\Omega$ is
central and acts on a highest weight module with highest weight $\lambda$ by
$c(\lambda)=(\lambda+2\rho,\lambda)$; in an integrable module the primitive
weights inside one Casimir generalized eigenspace are dominant integral and
form an antichain, with
$c(\lambda)-c(\mu)=\sum_in_id_i(\lambda(h_i)+\mu(h_i)+2)>0$ whenever
$\lambda>\mu$ are dominant and $\lambda-\mu=\sum_in_i\alpha_i\ne0$; a nonzero
integrable highest weight module has dominant integral highest weight; and the
simple quotient of the Verma module of highest weight $\lambda$ is unique
([[thm-generalized-kac-moody-casimir-is-central-and-scalar-on-highest-weight-modules]],
[[lem-casimir-separates-comparable-dominant-primitive-weights]],
[[lem-dominance-is-necessary-for-an-integrable-highest-weight-module]],
[[thm-kac-moody-verma-module-has-a-unique-simple-quotient]],
[[def-integrable-kac-moody-module]],
[[def-kac-moody-integral-and-dominant-integral-weights]]).

[F8] Over the DVR $R$, finitely generated torsion-free modules are free, and a
submodule of a free module of finite rank is finitely generated and free
([[cor-finitely-generated-torsion-free-modules-over-a-pid-are-free]]).



## Verification

**Proof technique:** build the $R$-lattice spanned by negative words, show it is stable under all integral generators, reduce modulo $q-1$ to a classical integrable highest weight module, prove that fibre simple by the Casimir antichain inside one generalized eigenspace, and compare dimensions weight by weight.

1.1 The lattice and its freeness. Let $V_R:=\sum_wR\,F_wv$ over all ordinary words $w$, including the empty word, so $v\in V_R$ and $V_R$ spans $V$ over $\mathbb Q(q)$ because $V=U_q^-v$ by [F3] and the words span $U_q^-$. For a label $\mu$, the $R$-module $V_R\cap V_\mu$ is spanned by the finitely many vectors $F_wv$ of label $\mu$ (bounded word length by [F3]), so it is finitely generated; it is torsion-free as a submodule of the $\mathbb Q(q)$-space $V_\mu$; by [F8] it is free of finite rank, equal to $\dim_{\mathbb Q(q)}V_\mu$, and $V_R=\bigoplus_\mu(V_R\cap V_\mu)$. [F3, F8, algebra]

1.2 Stability under the generators of $U_A$. (i) $K_h(F_wv)=q^{\langle\mathrm{wt}(F_wv),h\rangle}F_wv$ is an $R$-unit multiple by [F1], [F2] and [F3]. (ii) $F_jF_wv=F_{jw}v\in V_R$ trivially. (iii) For $E_i$, use $E_iF_{j_1}\cdots F_{j_m}=F_{j_1}\cdots F_{j_m}E_i+\sum_{t}F_{j_1}\cdots F_{j_{t-1}}[E_i,F_{j_t}]F_{j_{t+1}}\cdots F_{j_m}$ with $[E_i,F_{j_t}]=\delta_{ij_t}T_{i,0,1}$ by [F4]; applying to $v$, the first term vanishes, and each surviving term equals $F_{j_1}\cdots\widehat{F_{j_t}}\cdots F_{j_m}\cdot[m_t]_iv$ where $m_t=\langle\mathrm{wt}(F_{j_{t+1}}\cdots F_{j_m}v),h_i\rangle\in\mathbb Z$ is an integer (the weight of the tail is a label by [F3]) and the scalar is $T_{i,0,1}$ on that tail; by [F2], $[m_t]_i\in R$. Hence $E_iV_R\subseteq V_R$, and by iteration $E_i^rV_R\subseteq V_R$. (iv) $T_{i,c,t}$ acts diagonally with scalar $\prod_{s=1}^{t}[m+c-s+1]_i/[s]_i=\binom{m+c}{t}_i$, an element of $R$ by [F2] and [F5]. (v) The divided powers are $E_i^{(r)}=E_i^r/[r]_i!$ and $F_i^{(r)}=F_i^r/[r]_i!$ with $[r]_i!\in R^{\times}$ by [F2], so they also preserve $V_R$. Thus $V_R$ is stable under every generator of $U_A$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, algebra]

2.1 The $q=1$ fibre. Let $\bar V:=V_R/(q-1)V_R$, a vector space over $R/(q-1)=\mathbb Q$, with $\bar V_\mu=(V_R\cap V_\mu)/(q-1)(V_R\cap V_\mu)$ of dimension equal to $\operatorname{rank}_R(V_R\cap V_\mu)=\dim_{\mathbb Q(q)}V_\mu$ by step 1.1; in particular the image $\bar v$ of $v$ is nonzero because $R v\subseteq V_R$ is a direct summand on the top label. By step 1.2 every generator of $U_A$ induces a $\mathbb Q$-linear operator on $\bar V$, namely $\bar E_i,\bar F_i,\bar H_i:=\bar T_{i,0,1}$ and $\bar K_h=\mathrm{id}$; the values of the toral scalars at $q=1$ are the ordinary integer binomials $\binom{m+c}{t}\in\mathbb Z$ and $m\in\mathbb Z$ by [F2] (no division by a vanishing denominator is performed: only the integral element $T_{i,c,t}$ is reduced). The defining relations of $U_q$ hold in $U_A$ as integral identities and therefore hold on $\bar V$: $\bar E_i\bar F_j-\bar F_j\bar E_i=\delta_{ij}\bar H_i$; the Serre relations with Gaussian binomials reducing to the ordinary binomials; and, from $\bar T_{i,c+1,t}=\bar T_{i,c,t}+\bar T_{i,c,t-1}$ and $\bar T_{i,c,1}=\bar H_i+c$, the identity $\bar T_{i,c,t}=\binom{\bar H_i+c}{t}$; moreover $\bar H_i\bar E_j=\bar E_j(\bar H_i+a_{ij})$ and $\bar H_i\bar F_j=\bar F_j(\bar H_i-a_{ij})$ hold by reducing $T_{i,0,1}E_j=E_jT_{i,a_{ij},1}$ and its $F$-analogue from [F4] and evaluating $\bar T_{i,a_{ij},1}=\bar H_i+a_{ij}$ on weight vectors, where the scalars agree at every label. Hence $\bar V$ is a module for the classical algebra generated by the $\bar E_i,\bar F_i,\bar H_i$ with the classical Serre and Cartan relations. [step 1.1, step 1.2, F2, F4, F5, algebra]

3.1 The fibre is an integrable highest weight module. $\bar V$ is generated by $\bar v\ne0$, and $\bar E_i\bar v=0$ because $E_iv=0$; it has finite-dimensional label pieces by step 2.1 and support in $\lambda-Q_+$ by [F3]; and it is integrable: for any $\bar x\in\bar V$, choosing a lift $x\in V_R$ and using local nilpotence of $E_i,F_i$ on $x$ in the integrable module $V$ gives some $N$ with $E_i^Nx=F_i^Nx=0$, and reducing (legitimate since $E_i^N,F_i^N$ preserve $V_R$ by step 1.2) gives $\bar E_i^N\bar x=\bar F_i^N\bar x=0$. Top and support claims are unchanged modulo $q-1$ at every label. [step 1.2, step 2.1, F3, algebra]

4.1 Simplicity of the fibre. Work in the regularized auxiliary datum of [F6], where each label is a functional on the Cartan and the classical theory of [F7] applies to $\bar V$ (the regularization preserves the grading and the module; its perfect pairing makes distinct labels distinct Cartan functionals, and the old torus is reconstructed from the labels, so dimensions per label are unaffected). The generator $\bar v$ is a highest vector of weight $\lambda$, so $\Omega$ acts on all of $\bar V$ by $c(\lambda)$ by [F7]. Let $W\subseteq\bar V$ be a proper submodule and suppose $W\ne0$; since the weights above any fixed weight inside $\lambda-Q_+$ form a finite set (the simple roots are independent, so the coefficients in $\lambda-\nu$ bound $\nu$), $W$ contains a vector $w$ of a weight $\mu$ that is maximal in the support of $W$; then $\bar E_iw=0$ for all $i$ because $\bar E_iw$ has weight $\mu+\alpha_i$, which is not in the support of $W$. Its weight $\mu$ is dominant integral by [F7] applied to the integrable highest weight module generated by $w$. Moreover $w$ lies in the generalized eigenspace $\bar V^{(c(\lambda))}$ of the central Casimir and is a primitive vector there, and $\lambda$ is a dominant integral primitive weight of the same generalized eigenspace; by the antichain statement of [F7], $\lambda$ and $\mu$ are incomparable unless $\mu=\lambda$. Since $\mu\in\lambda-Q_+$ and $\mu\ne\lambda$ would give $\lambda>\mu$ and hence $c(\lambda)\ne c(\mu)$, a contradiction, we get $\mu=\lambda$. Then $w$ lies in the one-dimensional top label space and generates $\bar V$, contradicting that $W$ is proper. Hence $\bar V$ has no proper nonzero submodule: it is simple. [step 3.1, F3, F6, F7, algebra]

5.1 Character equality. By steps 2.1 and 4.1, $\bar V$ is a simple integrable highest weight module of highest weight $\lambda$, hence a simple quotient of the classical Verma module of highest weight $\lambda$; by the uniqueness of that simple quotient [F7], $\bar V$ is the classical simple integrable highest weight module of weight $\lambda$ in the regularized realization. Comparing dimensions label by label, $\dim V_\mu=\operatorname{rank}_R(V_R\cap V_\mu)=\dim\bar V_\mu$ by step 2.1, so the character of $V$ equals the classical simple character, independent of the chosen quotient $V$; this is (a). [step 2.1, step 4.1, F6, F7, algebra]

6.1 Uniqueness and universality. Apply step 5.1 to $V=\tilde L(\lambda)$ and to its simple graded quotient $L(\lambda)$ of [[def-quantum-verma-module-and-simple-highest-weight-quotient]]: both are nonzero graded quotients with nonzero highest line, so both have the classical character; the quotient map $\tilde L(\lambda)\to L(\lambda)$ is thus a surjection whose restriction to every finite-dimensional label space is a surjection of vector spaces of the same dimension, hence an isomorphism on each label; therefore $\tilde L(\lambda)\cong L(\lambda)$, and $\tilde L(\lambda)$ is simple. The universality statement is [[lem-quantum-highest-weight-serre-quotient-is-integrable]](iii) combined with this identification. This is (b) and completes the proof of (c); no $\mathbb Z[q,q^{-1}]$-freeness, no global basis and no quantum semisimplicity assumption is used, and all scalars produced are $R$-integral. [step 5.1, F6, F7, algebra] ∎
