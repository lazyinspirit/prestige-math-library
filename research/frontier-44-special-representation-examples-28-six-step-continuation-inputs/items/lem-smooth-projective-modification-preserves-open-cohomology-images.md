## Statement

Assume AC. Let $p:X'\to X$ be a proper morphism between smooth projective complex varieties of equal pure dimension, restricting to the identity on a common open variety $U$. Then $H^n(X,\mathbb Q)$ and $H^n(X',\mathbb Q)$ have the same image in $H^n(U,\mathbb Q)$ for every $n$. Consequently the compactification-image formula of logarithmic weights extends from an SNC compactification to every smooth projective compactification.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice; a smooth quasiprojective complex variety $U$; smooth projective complex varieties $X,X'$ of the same pure dimension $d$; open immersions $j:U\hookrightarrow X$ and $j':U\hookrightarrow X'$ with dense images, exhibiting $X$ and $X'$ as compactifications of $U$ ([[def-open-immersion-schemes]]); a morphism $p:X'\to X$ with $p\circ j'=j$. Below $H^*(-;\mathbb Q)$ denotes singular cohomology of the analytic spaces of [F1, F2], and all maps between them are the induced continuous maps of [F1, F3].

[F1] Analytification and orientation ([[lem-smooth-projective-analytification-has-rational-kahler-polarization]], [[def-compact-kahler-manifold-and-hodge-polarization-conventions]], [[def-analytic-topology-and-finite-smooth-algebraic-stratifications]]). A smooth projective complex variety of pure dimension $d$ has an analytification that is a compact Hausdorff second-countable complex manifold of complex dimension $d$, a finite discrete set when $d=0$; every standard smooth algebraic chart becomes a holomorphic chart and every morphism of smooth complex varieties induces a holomorphic, hence continuous, map. A complex manifold carries the canonical orientation for which $(v_1,Jv_1,\dots,v_d,Jv_d)$ is positive on a complex basis, and it is boundaryless; an ample class is represented by a positive closed real $(1,1)$-form, so $X$ and $X'$ are compact Kaehler, hence compact oriented boundaryless $2d$-manifolds, and every open subvariety is an oriented complex submanifold. The analytic topology of a complex algebraic variety is the one of the cited definition, and regular maps are continuous for it.

[F2] Poincare duality ([[thm-poincare-duality-for-oriented-topological-manifolds]], [[def-fundamental-class-of-a-compact-oriented-manifold]]). For the oriented boundaryless manifolds $X,X'$ of real dimension $2d$ and $U$, cap with the compatible compact orientation classes gives isomorphisms $D_M:H_c^p(M;\mathbb Q)\xrightarrow{\sim}H_{2d-p}(M;\mathbb Q)$ for every integer $p$, including disconnected, noncompact and empty $M$. For compact $M$, $H_c^p(M;\mathbb Q)=H^p(M;\mathbb Q)$ canonically and $D_M(a)=a\cap[M]$, where $[M]$ is the fundamental class of the cited definition. For the open inclusions $j:U\hookrightarrow X$ and $j':U\hookrightarrow X'$ with restricted orientation, $D_X(e_Xc)=j_*D_Uc$ and $D_{X'}(e_{X'}c)=j'_*D_Uc$ for every $c\in H_c^p(U;\mathbb Q)$, where $e_X,e_{X'}$ are extension of compact supports.

[F3] Kronecker pairing ([[def-kronecker-evaluation-pairing]], [[lem-the-kronecker-pairing-is-independent-of-cocycle-and-cycle-representatives]], [[cor-cohomology-over-a-field-is-dual-to-homology-over-that-field]], [[prop-singular-cohomology-is-contravariantly-functorial]], [[prop-singular-chains-and-homology-are-covariantly-functorial]]). For every space $M$ and integer $n$, evaluation $\Phi:H^n(M;\mathbb Q)\times H_n(M;\mathbb Q)\to\mathbb Q$, $\Phi(\alpha,z)=\langle\alpha,z\rangle$, is $\mathbb Q$-bilinear, independent of cocycle and cycle representatives, natural, $\Phi(f^*\alpha,z)=\Phi(\alpha,f_*z)$ for continuous $f$, and over the field $\mathbb Q$ the map $\alpha\mapsto\Phi(\alpha,-)$ is an isomorphism onto the full linear dual of $H_n(M;\mathbb Q)$; pullback and pushforward are functorial, $(g\circ f)^*=f^*\circ g^*$ and $(g\circ f)_*=g_*\circ f_*$.

[F4] Cap product and cup product ([[def-cap-product-with-cohomology-first]], [[thm-cap-product-boundary-identity]], [[prop-cap-product-naturality-and-projection-formula]]). Cap evaluates the front face and retains the back face with no additional sign, is well defined on (co)homology classes, and in degree $n$ on an $n$-cycle it returns $\varphi(\sigma)$ times the last vertex. For continuous $f$ and classes for which the expressions are defined, $f_*(f^*\alpha\cap x)=\alpha\cap f_*x$ and $(\alpha\smile\beta)\cap x=\beta\cap(\alpha\cap x)$.

[F5] Augmentation ([[def-zero-simplex-augmentation-and-reduced-singular-homology]], [[lem-singular-augmentation-commutes-with-boundary]]). The augmentation $\varepsilon_M:C_0(M;\mathbb Q)\to\mathbb Q$ sends every singular $0$-simplex generator to $1$; it kills $1$-boundaries, so it descends to $\varepsilon_*:H_0(M;\mathbb Q)\to\mathbb Q$.

[F6] Graded commutativity ([[thm-singular-cohomology-is-graded-commutative]]). For $a\in H^p(M;\mathbb Q)$ and $b\in H^q(M;\mathbb Q)$, $a\smile b=(-1)^{pq}b\smile a$.

[F7] Compact supports and excision ([[def-compactly-supported-singular-cohomology-of-a-locally-compact-space]], [[thm-excision-for-singular-cohomology]]). $H_c^p(X;\mathbb Q)$ is the colimit of the groups $H^p(X,X\setminus K;\mathbb Q)$ over compact $K\subseteq X$, with transition maps induced by inclusion of relative cochain complexes; $X$ and $X'$ are compact Hausdorff, so $X\setminus K$ is open for compact $K$. If $K\subseteq U$ is compact, then $\overline{X\setminus U}=X\setminus U\subseteq X\setminus K=\operatorname{int}_X(X\setminus K)$ and similarly in $X'$, so excision identifies $j^*:H^p(X,X\setminus K;\mathbb Q)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^p(U,U\setminus K;\mathbb Q)$ and $(j')^*:H^p(X',X'\setminus K;\mathbb Q)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^p(U,U\setminus K;\mathbb Q)$. The extension maps $e_X:H_c^p(U;\mathbb Q)\to H_c^p(X;\mathbb Q)$ and $e_{X'}:H_c^p(U;\mathbb Q)\to H_c^p(X';\mathbb Q)$ are the colimit maps induced by $(j^*)^{-1}$ and $((j')^*)^{-1}$ at each support $K\subseteq U$; these systems are compatible with the transition maps because the same restriction maps compute both sides.

[F8] Compactification and domination ([[lem-smooth-quasiprojective-varieties-admit-snc-projective-compactifications]]). $U$ admits a smooth projective compactification with reduced SNC boundary; and a morphism of smooth quasiprojective varieties extends to a morphism of smooth projective compactifications after modifying the source compactification outside its open variety, the modified source and the target being arbitrary smooth projective compactifications as recorded in the closing remark of that lemma.

[A1] AC is assumed in the statement. It is consumed through [F1] (analytification, Chern-form and comparison suppliers), [F2] (orientation classes and exhaustions), [F3] (the full dual and bases), [F7] (the compact-support colimit and excision) and [F8] (resolution, principalization and Serre generation), and once in step 1.2 for a countable selection of points, which AC supplies through [[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]]. The cap, cup, sequence and pairing computations below are otherwise choice-free. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct: realize both compactifications as compact oriented manifolds, compare the two restriction maps through Poincare duality and the cap-cup compatibility, and finish with the domination clause of [F8].

1.1 Structure of the spaces. By [F1], $X$ and $X'$ are compact oriented boundaryless $2d$-manifolds, $U$ is an oriented boundaryless $2d$-manifold, and $j,j'$ are orientation-preserving open embeddings with dense images; $p$ is continuous with $p\circ j'=j$. The analytifications of $j,j',p$ are the maps appearing in [F1], [F2] and [F3], and $H^n(X;\mathbb Q)$, $H^n(X';\mathbb Q)$, $H^n(U;\mathbb Q)$ are the singular cohomology groups of these spaces. A nonempty Zariski-open subset of the pure $d$-dimensional variety $X'$ is analytically dense: in each of the finitely many holomorphic charts provided by [F1], the complement lies in the zero locus of nonzero polynomial functions, which has empty Euclidean interior, and the charts cover $X'$. When $d=0$ the spaces are finite discrete, the dense open embeddings are bijective, and the assertions below hold by inspection. [F1, given, algebra]

1.2 The morphism is the identity over $U$. We prove $p^{-1}(j'(U))=j'(U)$ as subsets of $X'$, so that $p^{-1}(j'(K))=j'(K)$ for every compact $K\subseteq U$. Let $x\in X'$ with $p(x)\in j'(U)$, say $p(x)=j(u_0)=p(j'(u_0))$ with $u_0\in U$. The set $j'(U)$ is a nonempty analytically dense subset of $X'$ and $X'$ is second countable, so countably many neighborhoods of $x$ meeting $j'(U)$ yield a sequence $u_m\in U$ with $j'(u_m)\to x$; this countable selection is supplied by AC, through the countable-choice theorem of [A1]. Continuity gives $j(u_m)=p(j'(u_m))\to p(x)=j(u_0)$; since $j$ is a homeomorphism onto the open subset $j(U)$, this means $u_m\to u_0$ in $U$; then $j'(u_m)\to j'(u_0)$ by continuity of $j'$, and uniqueness of limits in the Hausdorff space $X'$ gives $x=j'(u_0)\in j'(U)$. The reverse inclusion is immediate from $p\circ j'=j$. For compact $K\subseteq U$, every $x$ with $p(x)\in j'(K)$ lies in $j'(U)$, and on $j'(U)$ the map $p$ is $j\circ(j')^{-1}$, so $p(j'(u))=j(u)\in j'(K)$ exactly when $u\in K$. [A1, F1, given, construct]

1.3 The augmentation evaluates the Kronecker pairing through cap. For every space $M$, $\alpha\in H^n(M;\mathbb Q)$ and $z\in H_n(M;\mathbb Q)$ one has $\Phi(\alpha,z)=\varepsilon_*([\alpha\cap z])$. Choose a cocycle $\varphi$ and a cycle $z=\sum_\sigma c_\sigma\sigma$ representing the two classes; the defining cap formula in degree $n$ gives $\varphi\cap z=\sum_\sigma c_\sigma\varphi(\sigma)[\sigma(n)]$, a finite sum of last-vertex generators, and $\varepsilon_*$ of its class is $\sum_\sigma c_\sigma\varphi(\sigma)=\Phi(\alpha,z)$. Both sides are well defined on classes: cap descends by [F4] and $\varepsilon$ descends by [F5]. [F3, F4, F5, algebra]

1.4 The pairings. For $M=X$ or $X'$ define $\Lambda_M(\alpha,\beta):=\Phi(\alpha,D_M\beta)$ for $\alpha\in H^n(M;\mathbb Q)$ and $\beta\in H^{2d-n}(M;\mathbb Q)$, and define $\Lambda_U(\alpha,c):=\Phi(\alpha,D_Uc)$ for $\alpha\in H^n(U;\mathbb Q)$ and $c\in H_c^{2d-n}(U;\mathbb Q)$. Each $\Lambda$ is $\mathbb Q$-bilinear and perfect: $D_M$ and $D_U$ are isomorphisms by [F2], so for $\alpha\ne0$ there is $z$ with $\Phi(\alpha,z)\ne0$ and $z=D\beta$ for a unique $\beta$, whence $\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)\ne0$; conversely, if $\beta\ne0$ then $D\beta\ne0$ and evaluation at the nonzero class $D\beta$ is a nonzero functional on the full dual of homology, realized by some $\alpha$ because $\Phi$ identifies cohomology with that full dual by [F3]. [F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 Cap, cup and the fundamental class. For a closed oriented $2d$-manifold $M$, $\alpha\in H^n(M;\mathbb Q)$ and $\beta\in H^{2d-n}(M;\mathbb Q)$, one has $\Phi(\beta,D_M\alpha)=\langle\alpha\smile\beta,[M]\rangle$, where the right side abbreviates $\Phi(\alpha\smile\beta,[M])$ for the top-degree Kronecker pairing. Indeed $D_M\alpha=\alpha\cap[M]$ by [F2], and the second cap identity of [F4] gives $(\alpha\smile\beta)\cap[M]=\beta\cap(\alpha\cap[M])=\beta\cap D_M\alpha$; applying $\varepsilon_*$ and step 1.3 in degrees $2d-n$ and $2d$ gives the stated equality. [F2, F4, step 1.3, algebra]

2.2 Compatibility of the pairings with the open inclusion. For $\theta\in H^n(X;\mathbb Q)$ or $\theta\in H^n(X';\mathbb Q)$ and $c\in H_c^{2d-n}(U;\mathbb Q)$, one has $\Lambda_U(j^*\theta,c)=\Lambda_X(\theta,e_Xc)$ and $\Lambda_U((j')^*\theta,c)=\Lambda_{X'}(\theta,e_{X'}c)$. Indeed $\Lambda_U(j^*\theta,c)=\Phi(j^*\theta,D_Uc)=\Phi(\theta,j_*D_Uc)=\Phi(\theta,D_X(e_Xc))=\Lambda_X(\theta,e_Xc)$ by the naturality in [F3] and the duality naturality in [F2], and the primed identity is identical. [F2, F3, step 1.4]

2.3 The identity over compact supports. For every $n$ and every $c\in H_c^{2d-n}(U;\mathbb Q)$ one has $p^*(e_Xc)=e_{X'}c$ in $H_c^{2d-n}(X';\mathbb Q)$, where $p^*$ acts on the relative groups, possible by step 1.2. Choose by [F7] a compact $K\subseteq U$ and $c_K\in H^{2d-n}(U,U\setminus K;\mathbb Q)$ representing $c$; the extension maps send $c$ to the colimit classes represented by $(j^*)^{-1}c_K$ and $((j')^*)^{-1}c_K$. By step 1.2, $p^{-1}(K)=K$, so $p$ induces a map of pairs $(X',X'\setminus K)\to(X,X\setminus K)$ and $p^*$ is defined on these relative groups. Functoriality of pullback gives $(j')^*\circ p^*=(p\circ j')^*=j^*$, so $(j')^*(p^*((j^*)^{-1}c_K))=j^*(j^*)^{-1}c_K=c_K=(j')^*((j')^*)^{-1}c_K$, and injectivity of the excision isomorphism $(j')^*$ gives $p^*((j^*)^{-1}c_K)=((j')^*)^{-1}c_K$; the two colimit classes therefore coincide. [F3, F7, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 The transfer and its adjointness. For each $n$ let $T_n:=D_X^{-1}\circ p_*\circ D_{X'}:H^n(X';\mathbb Q)\to H^n(X;\mathbb Q)$, a composite of the duality isomorphisms and the pushforward of [F2] and [F3]. Then, for all $a\in H^n(X';\mathbb Q)$ and $b\in H^{2d-n}(X;\mathbb Q)$, one has $\Lambda_X(T_na,b)=\Lambda_{X'}(a,p^*b)$. Write $\sigma=n(2d-n)$. Using step 2.1 and the definition of $T_n$, $\Lambda_X(T_na,b)=\Phi(T_na,D_Xb)=\langle b\smile T_na,[X]\rangle=(-1)^\sigma\langle T_na\smile b,[X]\rangle=(-1)^\sigma\Phi(b,D_XT_na)=(-1)^\sigma\Phi(b,p_*D_{X'}a)$, where the third equality is [F6] and the last is the defining property $D_X\circ T_n=p_*\circ D_{X'}$; naturality from [F3] turns the last term into $(-1)^\sigma\Phi(p^*b,D_{X'}a)$. On the other side, $\Lambda_{X'}(a,p^*b)=\Phi(a,D_{X'}p^*b)=\langle p^*b\smile a,[X']\rangle=(-1)^\sigma\langle a\smile p^*b,[X']\rangle=(-1)^\sigma\Phi(p^*b,D_{X'}a)$ by step 2.1 and [F6]. The two displayed values agree. [F2, F3, F6, step 1.4, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The identity on $U$. For every $a\in H^n(X';\mathbb Q)$ one has $j^*(T_na)=(j')^*(a)$ in $H^n(U;\mathbb Q)$. By perfectness of $\Lambda_U$ in its first variable (step 1.4), it suffices to test against every $c\in H_c^{2d-n}(U;\mathbb Q)$: $\Lambda_U(j^*(T_na),c)=\Lambda_X(T_na,e_Xc)=\Lambda_{X'}(a,p^*(e_Xc))=\Lambda_{X'}(a,e_{X'}c)=\Lambda_U((j')^*a,c)$, where the first and last equalities are step 2.2, the second is step 3.1 and the third is step 2.3. [step 1.4, step 2.2, step 2.3, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Equality of the images. Let $b\in H^n(X;\mathbb Q)$. Then $(j')^*(p^*b)=(p\circ j')^*b=j^*b$, by functoriality of pullback [F1, F3], so the image of $H^n(X;\mathbb Q)$ in $H^n(U;\mathbb Q)$ is contained in the image of $H^n(X';\mathbb Q)$. Conversely, if $a\in H^n(X';\mathbb Q)$, step 4.1 exhibits $(j')^*a=j^*(T_na)$ with $T_na\in H^n(X;\mathbb Q)$, so the reverse inclusion holds and the two images agree for every $n$ with $0\le n\le 2d$. For $n<0$ or $n>2d$ both cohomology groups vanish by duality [F2] and the vanishing conventions of [F3], so both images are $\{0\}$; if $U=\varnothing$ then $X=X'=\varnothing$ because $U$ has dense image, and all groups vanish. This proves the first sentence of the statement. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.2, step 4.1, given]

6.1 Independence of arbitrary smooth projective compactifications. Let $X$ be an arbitrary smooth projective compactification of $U$, with no SNC hypothesis on its boundary, and let $Z$ be one with reduced SNC boundary, which exists by the first clause of [F8]. Apply the extension clause of [F8] to the identity morphism $U\to U$, viewed with source compactification $Z$ and target compactification $X$: by the closing remark of [F8] the clause is available for arbitrary smooth projective compactifications of the open variety, and it yields a smooth projective compactification $Y$ of $U$ with reduced SNC boundary together with morphisms $Y\to Z$ and $Y\to X$ restricting to the identity on $U$. Since $Y$ is projective and $X$ and $Z$ are separated, the morphisms $Y\to X$ and $Y\to Z$ are proper by [[lem-proper-source-to-separated-target-proper]], so step 5.1 applies to each and gives, for every $n$, $\operatorname{im}(H^n(X;\mathbb Q)\to H^n(U;\mathbb Q))=\operatorname{im}(H^n(Y;\mathbb Q)\to H^n(U;\mathbb Q))=\operatorname{im}(H^n(Z;\mathbb Q)\to H^n(U;\mathbb Q))$. Hence the image subgroup attached to a smooth projective compactification of $U$ is independent of the compactification and may always be computed from one with SNC boundary: any identity expressing that subgroup for an SNC compactification, in particular the compactification-image formula of logarithmic weights, holds for every smooth projective compactification. This proves the second sentence of the statement. [F8, step 5.1, given]

7.1 Choice accounting and boundary cases. AC is assumed in the statement and is consumed exactly through the suppliers of [F1]--[F8]: the analytification with its Chern-form, Jacobian-criterion, ample-power and comparison inputs, Poincare duality with its orientation classes and exhaustions, the full-dual universal coefficient statement, the compact-support colimit and excision, the resolution, principalization and Serre-generation inputs of [F8], and the single countable selection of step 1.2; all cap, cup, pairing and sequence computations are choice-free. Boundary cases: for $d=0$ all spaces are finite discrete, the groups vanish above degree $0$, $H^0$ has dimension the number of points, $p$ is bijective over $U$ and both images agree; for $U=\varnothing$ all groups vanish; for $n$ outside $0,\ldots,2d$ both groups vanish; for an isomorphism $p$ the transfer $T_n$ is the identity; disconnected $X,X',U$ are covered because [F2] and [F3] decompose along components and all formulas are componentwise; the compact support $K$ of step 2.3 may be enlarged and the class $c=0$ or empty $K$ give zero in the same way. Properness of $p$ is stated but not used beyond continuity and $p\circ j'=j$, both of which hold for any morphism of compactifications; no Hodge theory, strictness or mixed structure is used, and no identification of infinite-dimensional vector spaces with double duals is made. [A1, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.3, step 5.1, step 6.1, given] ∎

## Remarks

- The lemma is the independence half of the theory of logarithmic weights: it says that $\operatorname{im}(H^n(X;\mathbb Q)\to H^n(U;\mathbb Q))$ is an invariant of the pair $(U,n)$ over smooth projective compactifications, so that the identification of this image with $W_nH^n(U;\mathbb Q)$ for one SNC compactification (the companion theorem) automatically covers every compactification. It does not itself use or construct the weight filtration.

- All comparisons are made on the analytic spaces and their singular cohomology; the algebraic side enters only through the analytification of [F1] and the compactification and domination clauses of [F8].
