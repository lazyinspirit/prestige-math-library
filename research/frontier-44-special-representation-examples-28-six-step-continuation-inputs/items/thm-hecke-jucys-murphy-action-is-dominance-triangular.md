## Statement

Let $R$ be a commutative ring, $q\in R^{\times}$, $H=H_{R,q}(S_n)$, and let
$L_1,\dots,L_n$ be the Hecke Jucys-Murphy elements
([[def-hecke-jucys-murphy-elements-and-q-contents]]). For every $\lambda\vdash n$,
every standard $\lambda$-tableau $t$ and every $1\le k\le n$ there exist
$a_v\in R$ with
$$m_tL_k=[\operatorname{content}_t(k)]_q\,m_t+\sum_{v\rhd t}a_v\,m_v,$$
where $(m_t)_{t\in\mathrm{Std}(\lambda)}$ is the Murphy basis of the Murphy cell
module $S^{\lambda}_{\mathrm M}$
([[def-type-a-hecke-specht-module-and-cell-form]]) and the sum is over standard
$\lambda$-tableaux $v$ strictly dominating $t$ in the dominance order on
tableaux ([[def-dominance-order-on-partitions]],
[[def-young-tableau-standard-tableau-and-shape]]). In particular $L_k$ acts on
the Murphy cell module $S^{\lambda}_{\mathrm M}$ with Murphy basis $(m_t)$ by a
triangular matrix whose diagonal entries are the q-content values.
Consequently the $L_k$ commute pairwise and every symmetric polynomial in
$L_1,\dots,L_n$ is central in $H$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A commutative ring $R$, a unit $q$, the type-A Hecke algebra $H$, its additive Jucys-Murphy elements $L_k$ and its Murphy cell module $S^{\lambda}_{\mathrm M}$ with basis $(m_t)$.

[F1] The standard basis multiplication, the quadratic and braid relations, and the integral row action are those of [[lem-dj-bridge-row-permutation-module-standard-action]]. In particular $T_i^2=(q-1)T_i+q$, and $x_\rho T_i=q x_\rho$ if $i,i+1$ lie in one row of $t^\rho$.

[F2] The integral Murphy basis, dominance ideals, cell multiplication and finite Garnir straightening are proved in [[lem-dj-bridge-murphy-cellular-basis-and-cell-form]]. We write $I^{\rhd\lambda}=H^{\rhd\lambda}$ and $x_\lambda=m_\lambda$. The proof there supplies the dominance support rule: if $s'=ss_i$ is standard with $s\rhd s'$ and $\ell(d(s'))=\ell(d(s))+1$, multiplying any same-shape term strictly above $s$ by $T_i$ and then straightening produces only standard terms strictly above $s'$. Straightening a nonstandard row-standard tableau only raises its tableau order in the same shape. All these identities hold over $A=\mathbb Z[v^{\pm1}]$ and specialize along $v\mapsto q$.

[F3] A finite-dimensional algebra has nilpotent Jacobson radical and semisimple quotient ([[thm-jacobson-radical-is-nilpotent-and-the-quotient-is-semisimple]]). A semisimple algebra is a finite product of matrix algebras over division rings ([[thm-wedderburn-artin-theorem]]); its two-sided ideals and their quotients consequently have no nonzero nilpotent two-sided ideals.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **Braid identities.** Put $T_i=T_{(i,i+1)}$. The reduced word for $(j,k+1)$ gives $L_{k+1}=q^{-1}(T_kL_kT_k+T_k)$. We prove $T_iL_k=L_kT_i$ whenever $i\ne k-1,k$. For $i\ge k+1$, each generator in the displayed words defining $L_k$ commutes with $T_i$. For $i\le k-3$ the recurrence and induction apply. For the remaining case $k=i+2$, put $a=T_i$, $b=T_{i+1}$ and $C=L_i$; then $bC=Cb$ by the already established disjoint case and $L_{i+2}=q^{-1}b+q^{-2}bab+q^{-2}baCab$. The braid and quadratic relations give $[a,bab]=-q[a,b]$ and $a(baCab)=(baCab)a$ (replace $aba$ by $bab$ and move $C$ past $b$), so its commutator with $a$ vanishes. Thus $L_k$ centralizes $H(S_{k-1})$; since $L_j\in H(S_{k-1})$ for $j<k$, all $L_j$ commute. Multiplying the recurrence by $T_i$ and using $T_i^2=(q-1)T_i+q$ gives $T_iL_{i+1}=L_iT_i+1+(q-1)L_{i+1}$; starring gives $T_iL_i=L_{i+1}T_i-1-(q-1)L_{i+1}$. Hence $T_i$ commutes with $L_i+L_{i+1}$ and $L_iL_{i+1}$: for the product substitute the first identity into $(L_{i+1}T_i-1-(q-1)L_{i+1})L_{i+1}$. Every symmetric polynomial is a polynomial in the elementary symmetric polynomials, and each elementary symmetric polynomial splits, at the adjacent pair, as $e_d(\widehat L_i,\widehat L_{i+1})+(L_i+L_{i+1})e_{d-1}(\widehat L_i,\widehat L_{i+1})+L_iL_{i+1}e_{d-2}(\widehat L_i,\widehat L_{i+1})$. It therefore commutes with every $T_i$ and is central. [given, F1, algebra]

1.2 **Lifting smaller-rank congruences.** Here is the precise ideal check used in the induction below. Let $\tau$ be either a whole-row prefix $(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_r)$ of size $m<n$, or the prefix $(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_r,1)$ ending at the first box of the last row, of size $m<n$. In both cases $x_\lambda=h x_\tau=x_\tau h$, where $h$ is the row symmetrizer of the remaining blocks, embedded in $H_n$; in the second case its last block starts at the singleton label $m$, and no generator of $x_\tau$ meets that block. If $x_\tau L_k-cx_\tau\in I^{\rhd\tau}$ in $H_m$, expand this difference in its smaller Murphy basis. Every shape $\eta\rhd\tau$ lies in $H_mx_\eta H_m$. Embedded in $H_n$, $x_\eta$ is $x_{\kappa_\eta}$ for the partition $\kappa_\eta=(\eta,1^{n-m})$, so [F2] puts its two-sided ideal in $I^{\unrhd\kappa_\eta}$. Some prefix $j$ before the final row of $\tau$ has $\sum_{a\le j}\eta_a>\sum_{a\le j}\tau_a=\sum_{a\le j}\lambda_a$: strict dominance cannot first differ at or after the final row, where $\tau$ has total $m$. In the whole-row case the same argument permits $j<r$. Hence $\lambda$ does not dominate $\kappa_\eta$, and none of these ideals has a shape-$\lambda$ component. Multiplying the smaller congruence on the appropriate side by $h$ gives $x_\lambda L_k-cx_\lambda\in\sum_\eta I^{\unrhd\kappa_\eta}$. This difference also belongs to $I^{\unrhd\lambda}$ because both terms are in $x_\lambda H_n$. Their intersection, read in the Murphy basis, has only shapes strictly dominating $\lambda$, and the lifted congruence therefore holds modulo $I^{\rhd\lambda}$. [F2, algebra]

2.1 **An integral scalar for the central sum.** For this step work over $A=\mathbb Z[v^{\pm1}]$, with fraction field $K=\mathbb Q(v)$. The matrix of the regular trace pairing $B(a,b)=\operatorname{Tr}_K(\ell_{ab})$ in the standard basis has entries in $A$. At $v=1$ it is the matrix $n!\,\delta_{u,w^{-1}}$, since a nonidentity permutation has no fixed point in the regular permutation action. Its determinant is nonzero, so $B$ is nondegenerate over $K$. If $a$ belongs to the Jacobson radical $J$ then $ab\in J$ is nilpotent for every $b$, by [F3]; thus $\ell_{ab}$ is nilpotent and has trace zero. Nondegeneracy forces $J=0$, so $H_K$ is semisimple. In its cell layer $I^{\unrhd\lambda}_K/I^{\rhd\lambda}_K$, [F2] identifies multiplication with $X\cdot Y=XG_\lambda Y$ on $d\times d$ matrices, $d=|\operatorname{Std}(\lambda)|$. If $G_\lambda$ were singular, the nonzero subspace $N=\{X:G_\lambda X=0\}$ would be a two-sided ideal of this layer with $N^2=0$, contradicting [F3]. Hence $G_\lambda$ is invertible and $X\mapsto G_\lambda X$ identifies the layer with $\operatorname{Mat}_d(K)$. A central element of $H_K$ acts on this layer by a bimodule endomorphism of that matrix algebra, necessarily a scalar: its value at the identity commutes with every matrix and therefore is a scalar matrix. Applied to $Z_n=\sum_kL_k$, central by step 1.1, this shows that its matrix on the cell module is $\alpha_\lambda I_d$ over $K$. The cell action matrix already has entries in $A$, so $\alpha_\lambda\in A$, and freeness over $A$ makes the identity hold integrally and after every base change to $R$. Thus $m_tZ_n=\alpha_\lambda m_t$ for every $t$, without any assumption on the specialized cell form or its head. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 **Transport along standard adjacent swaps.** Suppose $s'=ss_i$ is standard with $s\rhd s'$ and length increasing by one, and suppose $m_sL_k=r_s(k)m_s+\sum_{u\rhd s}a_um_u$ for all $k$. By [F1], $m_{s'}=m_sT_i$. For $k\ne i,i+1$ step 1.1 gives $m_{s'}L_k=m_sL_kT_i$, and [F2] puts every error strictly above $s'$. For $k=i+1$, the same step gives $m_{s'}L_{i+1}=m_s(L_iT_i+1+(q-1)L_{i+1})$; its coefficient at $m_{s'}$ is $r_s(i)$, since all remaining terms are strictly above $s'$. The second adjacent identity similarly gives coefficient $r_s(i+1)$ at $m_{s'}$ for $L_i$. Thus the diagonal data are transposed in positions $i,i+1$ and unchanged elsewhere. Every standard tableau is reachable from $t^\lambda$ by such standard length-increasing swaps: reverse a right-descent reduction of $d(t)$; the two consecutive entries at a descent lie in incomparable cells, so their interchange remains standard. Consequently diagonal data at the row-filled tableau determine the asserted diagonals at every standard tableau. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 **Row-filled induction away from the final singleton.** Induct on $n$, the cases $n=0,1$ being immediate, and on $k$ within the row-filled tableau. If $k$ is in a row which is not the last row, choose the whole-row prefix $\tau$ ending with that row. It has size $m<n$, its row-filled tableau has no more dominant standard tableaux, and the induction on rank gives $x_\tau L_k=[\operatorname{content}_{t^\tau}(k)]_q x_\tau$ modulo $I^{\rhd\tau}$. Lift by step 1.2. If $k$ is in the last row but not its first column, $T_{k-1}$ belongs to its row subgroup, and the recurrence gives $m_{t^\lambda}L_k=(1+q[\operatorname{content}_{t^\lambda}(k-1)]_q)m_{t^\lambda}=[\operatorname{content}_{t^\lambda}(k)]_q m_{t^\lambda}$, by induction on $k$ and $[c+1]_q=1+q[c]_q$. If $k<n$ is in the first column of the last row, use the singleton-ended prefix $\tau$ of step 1.2 and induction on its smaller rank. These arguments leave only $k=n$ for a shape $\lambda=(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_r,1)$. All $L_k$ with $k<n$ have already been shown to act on $m_{t^\lambda}$ by their claimed scalars. Step 2.1 then supplies $m_{t^\lambda}L_n=r_nm_{t^\lambda}$ for some $r_n\in R$. Step 2.2 consequently applies with these diagonal data to every standard tableau; at this point all $L_k$ are triangular, though $r_n$ remains to be identified. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 1.2, induction, algebra]

4.1 **The final singleton is fixed by one Garnir calculation.** In the remaining shape let $i$ be the first entry in row $r$ of $t^\lambda$ and put $t=t^\lambda s_{n-1}\cdots s_{i+1}$. Then $t$ is standard, its last row contains $i+1$, and its penultimate row contains $i,i+2,\dots,n$. Step 2.2 gives $m_tL_i=[1-r]_q m_t+\sum_{v\rhd t}a_vm_v$ and $m_tL_{i+1}=r_nm_t+\sum_{v\rhd t}b_vm_v$. The tableau $g:=ts_i$ is exactly the Garnir tableau for the first-column belt $i,\dots,n$ between the last two rows. For $j=i,\dots,n$, let $u_j$ have last-row entry $j$ and the other belt labels increasingly in row $r$. The finite belt identity of [F2] gives $\sum_{j=i}^n x_\lambda T_{d(u_j)}\equiv0\pmod{I^{\rhd\lambda}}$: its other side is a sum of multiples of the symmetrizer obtained by merging these two rows, whose sorted shape strictly dominates $\lambda$. Here $u_i=g$, $u_{i+1}=t$, and all $u_j$ with $j>i+1$ are standard and strictly dominate $t$. The length-increasing row rule gives $m_tT_i=x_\lambda T_{d(g)}+I^{\rhd\lambda}$, so the belt identity explicitly yields $m_tT_i=-m_t-\sum_{j>i+1}m_{u_j}$. Put $U=\operatorname{span}\{m_v:v\rhd t\}$. Step 3.1 and transport show $UL_i\subseteq U$. Also $UT_i\subseteq U$: dominance forces each $v\unrhd t$ to agree with $t$ on the filled first $r-1$ rows and to place $i$ in node $(r,1)$. If $i+1$ occupies the singleton last row, all remaining labels must occupy row $r$ increasingly, forcing $v=t$. Thus every $v\rhd t$ places $i+1$ in $(r,2)$, and the row rule gives $m_vT_i=q m_v$. (If $\lambda_r=1$, the higher span is zero.) Working modulo $U$, the recurrence now gives $m_tL_{i+1}=q^{-1}m_t(T_iL_iT_i+T_i)=q^{-1}([1-r]_q-1)m_t=[-r]_q m_t$. Comparing its coefficient at $m_t$ proves $r_n=[-r]_q$, exactly the content of the final singleton. This completes the rank induction and identifies all row-filled diagonals. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 **The required flag and the initial left generator.** Step 2.2 now gives $m_tL_k=[\operatorname{content}_t(k)]_q m_t+\sum_{v\rhd t}a_vm_v$ for every standard $t$, over the given ring. Order the standard tableaux by any linear extension with the most dominant first. The spans of initial segments are stable under all $L_k$, and their successive rank-one quotients have exactly the q-content diagonals; this proves the claimed common triangular flag. Commutativity and symmetric-polynomial centrality were established independently in step 1.1. In particular the maximal tableau $t^\lambda$ has the exact eigenrelation $m_{t^\lambda}L_k=[\operatorname{content}_{t^\lambda}(k)]_q m_{t^\lambda}$. Since $L_k^*=L_k$, starring its defining cell congruence gives the corresponding left-cell generator relation $L_k z_\lambda=[\operatorname{content}_{t^\lambda}(k)]_q z_\lambda$. For the multiplicative normalization $X_k=1+(q-1)L_k$ this reads $X_k z_\lambda=q^{\operatorname{content}_{t^\lambda}(k)}z_\lambda$, using $1+(q-1)[c]_q=q^c$ for all integral $c$. These identities concern the cell generator, hence hold after taking the indicated cell quotient; they do not assert the same equality for the raw symmetrizer in $H$. All formulas specialize from $A$ to $R$, and no quantum integer or factorial is inverted in the specialized algebra. [step 1.1, step 2.2, step 4.1, F2, algebra] ∎
