## Example

Let $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ be the rank-one Cartan datum with $\lambda(h_1)=m\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$,
and let $L(m)$ be the explicit simple module with basis $v_0,\dots,v_m$,
$Kv_t=q^{m-2t}v_t$, $Fv_t=[t+1]_qv_{t+1}$,
$Ev_t=[m-t+1]_qv_{t-1}$
([[ex-quantum-verma-and-simple-modules-for-sl-two]],
[[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]). Let
$A=\mathbb Z[q,q^{-1}]$ and let
$V_A=\sum_{t=0}^mAF^{(t)}v_0\subseteq L(m)$ be the lattice generated by the
highest weight vector under the divided powers of the integral form $U_A$
([[def-lusztig-divided-power-integral-form]]). Let $k$ be a field of
characteristic $0$, made an $A$-algebra by $q\mapsto1$, and put
$\bar V=V_A\otimes_Ak$.

Then $V_A$ is free over $A$ with basis $F^{(t)}v_0$ ($0\le t\le m$), and
$\bar V$ is a $k$-vector space of dimension $m+1$ with basis the images
$\bar v_t$ on which
$$\bar K=1,\qquad \bar F\bar v_t=(t+1)\bar v_{t+1},\qquad \bar E\bar v_t=(m-t+1)\bar v_{t-1},\qquad \bar T_{0,1}\bar v_t=(m-2t)\bar v_t,$$
with $\bar v_{-1}=\bar v_{m+1}=0$. This is exactly the classical
$(m+1)$-dimensional simple representation of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ on the coroot
basis: $\bar T_{0,1}$ is the Cartan generator $h$ acting by the weight $m-2t$,
and $\bar E,\bar F$ are the classical raising and lowering matrices, so the
weight multiplicities are $\dim_k\bar V_\mu=1$ for $\mu=m,m-2,\dots,-m$ and $0$
otherwise. For $m=2$ the specialized matrices are
$\bar E\bar v_0=0$, $\bar E\bar v_1=2\bar v_0$, $\bar E\bar v_2=\bar v_1$ and
$\bar F\bar v_0=\bar v_1$, $\bar F\bar v_1=2\bar v_2$, $\bar F\bar v_2=0$, with
$\bar T_{0,1}=\operatorname{diag}(2,0,-2)$, the standard adjoint-type triple.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The rank-one datum, the simple module $L(m)$ with its string basis,
the lattice $V_A$, and the specialization $A\to k$, $q\mapsto1$.

[F1] $F^{(t)}v_0=\frac{1}{[t]!}F^tv_0$, the vectors $F^tv_0$ ($0\le t\le m$) are
$\mathbb Q(q)$-independent, and the action on the string is
$Kv_t=\ldots$, $Fv_t=[t+1]_qv_{t+1}$,
$Ev_t=[m-t+1]_qv_{t-1}$
([[ex-quantum-verma-and-simple-modules-for-sl-two]],
[[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]).

[F2] $[t]!\in A$ and $[t]!\ne0$; the divided powers $E^{(r)},F^{(r)}$, the
$K^{\pm1}$ and the $T_{c,t}$ generate $U_A$; on a weight vector of weight $\mu$
the toral binomial $T_{c,t}$ acts by the Gaussian value
$\binom{\mu(h)+c}{t}_q$, and $E^{(1)},F^{(1)},K^{\pm1},T_{0,1}$ already
preserve $V_A$
([[def-lusztig-divided-power-integral-form]],
[[lem-q-one-toral-specialization-identifies-the-classical-cartan-generators]] (b)).

[F3] On a $U_A$-stable label-graded lattice the specialization has
$\bar K=\mathrm{id}$, $\bar T_{i,c,t}$ acting by the integer binomials and the
classical Cartan commutators; the specialization is defined by base change of
the integral form, never by substituting into rational expressions
([[lem-q-one-toral-specialization-identifies-the-classical-cartan-generators]] (b),
[[prop-specialization-at-q-one-recovers-the-classical-enveloping-relations]] (c),(d)).

[F4] The local q=1 deformation identifies the module with the classical simple
module of the same highest weight in the regularized realization; in rank one
this is the $(m+1)$-dimensional simple $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-module
([[lem-local-q-one-deformation-proves-integrable-highest-weight-universality]]
(a), [[ex-quantum-verma-and-simple-modules-for-sl-two]] (a)).



## Verification

**Proof technique:** check the lattice is preserved by the integral generators with A-valued matrices, tensor with k, and compare the resulting matrices with the classical rank-one simple representation.

1.1 The lattice. The vectors $F^{(t)}v_0$, $0\le t\le m$, are $\mathbb Q(q)$-independent because $F^{(t)}v_0=[t]!^{-1}F^tv_0$ and the $F^tv_0$ are a basis of the string; hence their $A$-span is free of rank $m+1$. It is stable under the integral generators: $K$ acts diagonally by $q^{m-2t}\in A$; $F F^{(t)}v_0=[t+1]F^{(t+1)}v_0$ with $[t+1]\in A$; $E F^{(t)}v_0=[m-t+1]F^{(t-1)}v_0$ with $[m-t+1]\in A$ (the rank-one divided-power string formula); and $T_{c,t}$ acts diagonally by $\binom{m-2t+c}{t}_q\in A$. Since these generate $U_A$, the lattice $V_A$ is the $U_A$-stable $A$-lattice spanned by the divided-power string, and it is free by construction. [F1, F2, algebra]

1.2 Specialization. Base change to $k$ is exact on the free lattice, so $\bar V=V_A\otimes_Ak$ has the $k$-basis $\bar v_0,\dots,\bar v_m$ and dimension $m+1$. Substituting $q=1$ is legitimate here because the action matrices in the basis $F^{(t)}v_0$ are known explicitly with entries in $A$: $\bar K=\mathrm{id}$, $\bar F\bar v_t=(t+1)\bar v_{t+1}$, $\bar E\bar v_t=(m-t+1)\bar v_{t-1}$ and $\bar T_{c,s}\bar v_t=\binom{m-2t+c}{s}\bar v_t$, in particular $\bar T_{0,1}\bar v_t=(m-2t)\bar v_t$; this is the operator form of [F3] on the graded lattice. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 Comparison with the classical module. The displayed operators satisfy the classical commutation relations $\bar E\bar F-\bar F\bar E=\bar T_{0,1}$, $[\bar T_{0,1},\bar E]=2\bar E$, $[\bar T_{0,1},\bar F]=-2\bar F$ and the string ends $\bar v_{-1}=\bar v_{m+1}=0$, so $\bar V$ is the standard $(m+1)$-dimensional simple representation of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ with weights $m-2t$; this agrees with the general statement [F4]. For $m=2$ the matrices listed in the Example are read off immediately. The freeness proved in step 1.1 is specific to this rank-one lattice: it is established by the explicit string basis, not by the held general global-freeness theorem, and only the local field-level character comparison of [F4] is used for general conclusions. All computations are finite matrices over $A$ and $k$; no choice is used. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
