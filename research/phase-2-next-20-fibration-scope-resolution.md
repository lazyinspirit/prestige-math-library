## A Serre fibration whose endpoint fibers are not homotopy equivalent

Let C be the usual Cantor space (equivalently the binary sequence space). On the set E=C×I put the topology generated by the ordinary product open sets together with every vertical set

    V_c = {c} × [0,1),   c in C.

Let p:E→I and r:E→C be the coordinate projections. Both are continuous since the ordinary product topology is contained in the new topology. Each vertical map s_c:I→E, s_c(t)=(c,t), is continuous: inverse images of product opens are open, and the inverse image of V_d is [0,1) if d=c and empty otherwise.

**Serre property.** Given a continuous f:D^n→E and H:D^n×I→I with H(z,0)=p(f(z)), the map r f is constant, say c. Indeed D^n is connected, whereas distinct binary sequences are separated by complementary clopen coordinate cylinders; a connected subset of C is a singleton. Thus f(z)=(c,pf(z)). The formula

    L(z,t) = s_c(H(z,t))

is a continuous lift with L(z,0)=f(z). This includes n=0. Therefore p satisfies every disk HLP problem and is a Serre fibration. No family of lifts or AC is needed for this argument.

**Fibers.** The topology on p^{-1}(0) is discrete, because each V_c isolates its point. The topology on p^{-1}(1) is precisely the ordinary Cantor topology: none of the additional generators meets this fiber. Both fibers have only constant paths. Hence any homotopy into either fiber is pointwise constant. If maps between them were homotopy inverses, their composites would equal the identity as functions, so the maps would be inverse homeomorphisms. This is impossible: the first fiber is discrete, the second has no isolated points. Consequently the fibers over the endpoints of one base path need not have the same homotopy type for a Serre fibration.

**The example also lies in CGWH.** E is Hausdorff because its topology refines a Hausdorff product. At (c,t) with t<1, vertical intervals form a countable local basis. At (c,1), ordinary product neighborhoods form a countable local basis: a finite intersection of subbasic sets containing that point cannot use a V_d. Thus E is first countable. A first-countable Hausdorff space is compactly generated: if a set A is not closed, take x in its closure outside A and select a sequence a_n in A from a nested countable neighborhood basis at x. Then {x}∪{a_n} is compact Hausdorff and its intersection with A is not closed. This sequential selection uses countable choice, available under the owner's AC authorization; canonical use of this optional CGWH verification should cite `def-axiom-of-choice`. Hausdorff implies weak Hausdorff. Both endpoint fibers are already compactly generated Hausdorff, so kification does not erase the difference. Disk cylinders are ordinary compact Hausdorff spaces, and the same lifts work in CGWH.

**Direct failure of Hurewicz HLP.** Start with f:C→E, f(c)=(c,1), and base homotopy H(c,t)=1−t. Any lift has constant Cantor label along each time path, hence must equal (c,1−t). For fixed positive t the inverse image of V_d under this slice is the nonopen singleton {d}. Thus no continuous lift exists. This pinpoints the precise invalid substitution in the original argument: the parameter C is allowed for Hurewicz HLP but not licensed by Serre disk tests.

## Exact replacement available without adding any item ID

Keep the existing full Hurewicz statement and its proof. Add an explicit local counterexample/remark disposing of the original unrestricted Serre claim. A useful valid Serre conclusion, with a short complete proof, is the following:

For a Serre fibration and a path γ:b→c, let q:P=γ*E→I be its pullback. The inclusions F_b→P←F_c are weak homotopy equivalences. This is a zigzag of maps, not a claim of continuous transport equivalences between arbitrary fibers.

Here is a proof requiring only pullback stability and finite relative disk/CW lifting. Fix i=0 or 1. For any sphere map a:S^n→P based at a point in F_i, deform its projected map by

    K(x,s) = (1−s)q(a(x)) + s i.

Lift K with initial map a, prescribing the basepoint track to be constant. This is the finite CW relative HLP for (S^n,{*}). The endpoint is a based map into F_i; thus inclusion is surjective on every positive homotopy group. If a based homotopy in P connects two maps into F_i, apply the same height deformation to its domain S^n×I, prescribing the lift to remain the given map on both end spheres and the basepoint track. These form a finite CW subcomplex. At deformation time 1 the resulting homotopy lies entirely in F_i, proving injectivity. For π0, lift a height path to move any point of P into F_i; deform any path between fiber points into F_i by the same relative argument with its two endpoints fixed. Thus π0 is bijective as well. Empty fibers give an empty pullback by path lifting and require no based-group assertion. All lifting problems here are finite, so no AC is used.

The weak conclusion is sharp for the example: all its positive homotopy groups vanish and the component sets agree, although its endpoint fibers are not homotopy equivalent. CW-type hypotheses can yield stronger results, but introducing those hypotheses or a Whitehead-based proof is unnecessary for this local resolution and is not part of the proposed claim.

## Fixed-basepoint homotopy monodromy needs a correction

The fully typed canonical statement already written by the author is correct:

    (T_γ)_*: π_n(F_b,e) → π_n(F_c,T_γ(e)).

A path a:e'→T_γ(e) supplies β_a(T_γ)_* with target π_n(F_c,e'). Such a path need not exist. For example, a generator loop in the base of the double covering S^1→S^1 interchanges the two fiber points. This shows the endpoint-identification failure; since the higher groups of that discrete fiber are trivial, it is not offered as a nonexistence proof for every possible abstract group action.

Even with connected fiber, an arbitrary endpoint correction need not be independent of its choice. Take the trivial Hurewicz fibration RP²×S^1→S^1 with the product lifting function. For any base loop, transport on RP² is the identity. The constant endpoint path gives the identity on π2(RP²,e), while the nontrivial fiber loop a gives β_a, which is not the identity.

For completeness, this last distinction can be checked without assuming a classification of π2. Let q:S²→RP² be the antipodal covering, with chosen x0 over e. Let a be the projection of a path from x0 to −x0. In the radial-shell formula for β_a[q], its based lift to S² has an outer shell tracing that path and inner core equal to the antipodal map A:S²→S²: the seam point must be −x0, and qA=q. Removing the shell by the moving-basepoint shell homotopy identifies its unbased homotopy class with A. Its degree is therefore −1. In contrast, the based lift of [q] is the identity, with degree 1. If β_a[q]=[q] by a based homotopy downstairs, covering HLP would lift it to a homotopy of these two sphere maps, impossible by homotopy invariance of degree. The finite-strip covering proof and the full degree proof were read; their exact local interfaces suffice. Thus a mere choice of an endpoint loop changes the resulting automorphism even for a product bundle. This proves lack of a canonical unqualified construction, not that this product bundle has no action after choosing additional structure.

A fully valid fixed-group clause can be added locally as follows. Fix n≥1, assume F_b is path connected, and assume every loop a at e has β_a=id on π_n(F_b,e). (For n=1 this says the fundamental group is abelian; a simply connected fiber satisfies the condition in all degrees.) For a based base loop γ, define

    A_γ = β_a (T_γ)_*,  where a:e→T_γ(e) is any fiber path.

Such paths exist by path connectedness. If a,a' are two choices, a*reverse(a') is a loop at e, so the composition law for β and the hypothesis give β_a=β_a'. For a homotopy of transport maps, their basepoint track changes a to a followed by that track; the moving-basepoint identity makes A_γ unchanged. Hence the transport comparison theorem gives independence of lifting function and endpoint-fixed base homotopy. For two loops traversed γ then η, a correction for T_ηT_γ is a_η followed by T_η(a_γ). Naturality of the radial-shell formula under postcomposition, followed by β's concatenation law, gives A_{γ*η}=A_η A_γ. The constant-path comparison gives A_constant=id. Thus these automorphisms define a right π1(B,b) action on the fixed group. Each map is uniquely specified independently of an auxiliary path, so no global indexed choice of paths is required. This gives a complete optional simple-fiber remedy without asserting an unproved section/cofibration theorem.
