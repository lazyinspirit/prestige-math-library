## Statement

Let $n\ge2$ and let
$Z(\mathbb C[S_n],\mathbb C[S_{n-1}])=\{z\in\mathbb C[S_n]:zh=hz\text{ for every }h\in S_{n-1}\}$
be the centralizer of $\mathbb C[S_{n-1}]$ in $\mathbb C[S_n]$. Then
$$Z(\mathbb C[S_n],\mathbb C[S_{n-1}])=\langle Z(\mathbb C[S_{n-1}]),X_n\rangle,$$
the subalgebra generated by the centre of $\mathbb C[S_{n-1}]$ and the element
$X_n$. Consequently
$Z(\mathbb C[S_n])\subseteq\langle Z(\mathbb C[S_{n-1}]),X_n\rangle$, because
the centre of $\mathbb C[S_n]$ is contained in the centralizer.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The chain $S_1\subset\cdots\subset S_n$, the subalgebra
$\mathbb C[S_{n-1}]\subseteq\mathbb C[S_n]$, the centre
$Z(\mathbb C[S_{n-1}])$ ([[def-center-of-the-group-algebra]]) and
$X_n=\sum_{j<n}(j\ n)$
([[def-jucys-murphy-elements-of-the-symmetric-group-algebra]]).

[F1] For every finite-dimensional representation $V$ of a finite group $G$
over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero the centre
$Z(\mathbb C[G])$ acts by a scalar on each irreducible constituent, the scalar
being constant on isomorphic constituents; the algebra itself decomposes as
$\prod_{\lambda\vdash m}\operatorname{End}(V^\lambda)$ for
$G=S_m$, and endomorphisms of an irreducible are scalars
([[thm-group-algebra-decomposes-as-a-product-of-matrix-algebras-over-an-algebraically-closed-field]],
[[cor-endomorphisms-of-an-irreducible-over-an-algebraically-closed-field-are-scalars]]).

[F2] The complex Specht modules $V^\mu=S^\mu_{\mathbb C}$, $\mu\vdash m$,
are a complete list of pairwise inequivalent irreducible complex
$S_m$-modules, and for $\lambda\vdash n$ the restriction
$\operatorname{Res}^{S_n}_{S_{n-1}}V^\lambda\cong
\bigoplus_{x\in\operatorname{Rem}(\lambda)}V^{\lambda-x}$ is multiplicity-free
([[thm-complex-irreducibles-of-symmetric-groups-are-specht-modules]],
[[cor-complex-specht-restriction-branching-rule]]).

[F3] $s_iX_n=X_ns_i$ for $1\le i\le n-2$, hence $X_n$ commutes with every
element of $\mathbb C[S_{n-1}]$
([[lem-jucys-murphy-local-relations]]).

[F4] For a partition $\mu$, distinct addable nodes have distinct contents,
so the map sending $\lambda=\mu+x$ to the content $c(x)$ is injective on the
set of $\lambda\vdash n$ containing $\mu$
([[lem-addable-nodes-of-a-partition-have-distinct-contents]]).

[F5] $X_nv_T=c_T(n)v_T$ for every standard tableau $T$ of size $n$, the
eigenvalue being the content of the node carrying $n$; the eigenvalue of
$X_n$ on the $S_{n-1}$-irreducible copy $V^\mu\subseteq V^{\mu+x}$ is
therefore the content $c(x)$, independent of $T$
([[thm-jucys-murphy-joint-spectrum-is-the-set-of-tableau-content-vectors]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F3] the element $X_n$ commutes with $\mathbb C[S_{n-1}]$; every $z\in Z(\mathbb C[S_{n-1}])$ commutes with every element of $\mathbb C[S_{n-1}]$ by definition of the centre. Hence $X_n$ and all of $Z(\mathbb C[S_{n-1}])$ lie in $Z(\mathbb C[S_n],\mathbb C[S_{n-1}])$, and since that set is a subalgebra, $\langle Z(\mathbb C[S_{n-1}]),X_n\rangle\subseteq Z(\mathbb C[S_n],\mathbb C[S_{n-1}])$. [F3, given, algebra]

1.2 Write $E$ for the set of pairs $(\mu,\lambda)$ with $\mu\vdash n-1$, $\lambda\vdash n$ and $\mu\subseteq\lambda$; equivalently $E=\{(\mu,\mu+x):x\in\operatorname{Add}(\mu)\}$. For $\mu\vdash n-1$ let $W_\mu\subseteq\mathbb C[S_n]$ denote the $\mu$-isotypic component of $\mathbb C[S_n]$ for the left action of $S_{n-1}$; by [F2] it is the direct sum of the copies $V^\mu\subseteq V^\lambda$ over the pairs $(\mu,\lambda)\in E$. [F2, given]

2.1 Structure of the centralizer. By [F1] applied to $S_n$, an element $z\in\mathbb C[S_n]$ corresponds to a tuple $(z_\lambda)_{\lambda\vdash n}$ with $z_\lambda\in\operatorname{End}(V^\lambda)$, and $z$ lies in $Z(\mathbb C[S_n],\mathbb C[S_{n-1}])$ exactly when each $z_\lambda$ commutes with the $S_{n-1}$-action on $V^\lambda$. By [F2] and [F1] the commutant of $S_{n-1}$ in $\operatorname{End}(V^\lambda)$ is $\prod_{x\in\operatorname{Rem}(\lambda)}\mathbb C\,\mathrm{id}_{V^{\lambda-x}}$: restriction is multiplicity-free, and by Schur's lemma an $S_{n-1}$-endomorphism of each irreducible summand $V^{\lambda-x}$ is a scalar. Hence evaluation on the copies induces an algebra isomorphism $Z(\mathbb C[S_n],\mathbb C[S_{n-1}])\to\mathbb C^E$, $z\mapsto(z(\mu,\lambda))_{(\mu,\lambda)\in E}$, where $z(\mu,\lambda)$ is the scalar by which $z$ acts on the copy of $V^\mu$ inside $V^\lambda$; it is injective because $z$ acting as $0$ on all of $\bigoplus_{(\mu,\lambda)\in E}V^\mu=\mathbb C[S_n]$ forces $z=0$, and surjective by [F1] because the scalars on the finitely many copies may be prescribed independently. [F1, F2, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 The image $A$ of $\langle Z(\mathbb C[S_{n-1}]),X_n\rangle$ under the isomorphism of step 2.1 is the unital subalgebra of $\mathbb C^E$ generated by the evaluations of the two generating pieces: an element $z\in Z(\mathbb C[S_{n-1}])$ acts on every copy $V^\mu$ by the central character value $\omega_\mu(z)$ of the irreducible $V^\mu$; and $X_n$ acts on the copy $(\mu,\lambda)=(\mu,\mu+x)$ by the scalar $c(x)$ by [F5]. [F5, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Separation of edges with distinct predecessor. Let $(\mu,\lambda),(\mu',\lambda')\in E$ with $\mu\ne\mu'$. The central idempotent $e_\mu\in Z(\mathbb C[S_{n-1}])$ of the Wedderburn factor $\operatorname{End}(V^\mu)$ acts as $1$ on $V^\mu$ and as $0$ on $V^{\mu'}$, since $\mu\ne\mu'$ and the irreducible modules are inequivalent; by step 3.1 the element $e_\mu$ therefore separates the two edges. [F1, F2, step 3.1, algebra]

4.2 Separation of edges with the same predecessor. Let $(\mu,\lambda),(\mu,\lambda')\in E$ with $\lambda\ne\lambda'$; write $\lambda=\mu+x$, $\lambda'=\mu+x'$ with distinct addable nodes $x\ne x'$ of $\mu$. By [F4] their contents differ, $c(x)\ne c(x')$, so by step 3.1 the element $X_n$ assumes the distinct values $c(x),c(x')$ on the two edges and separates them. [F4, F5, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 A unital subalgebra of $\mathbb C^E$ that separates the points of the finite set $E$ is all of $\mathbb C^E$: indeed, for each $e\in E$ and each $e'\ne e$ separation gives $a_{e'}\in A$ with $a_{e'}(e)\ne a_{e'}(e')$, and then $b_e:=\prod_{e'\ne e}(a_{e'}-a_{e'}(e')\cdot1)$ lies in $A$, satisfies $b_e(e)\ne0$ and $b_e(e'')=0$ for $e''\ne e$, and is therefore a nonzero multiple of the standard basis idempotent $\delta_e$; hence every $\delta_e\in A$ and $A=\mathbb C^E$. [step 4.1, step 4.2, algebra]

6.1 By steps 4.1 and 4.2 the subalgebra $A$ of step 3.1 separates every pair of distinct edges, so step 5.1 gives $A=\mathbb C^E$. Since the evaluation map of step 2.1 is an isomorphism, the preimage of $\mathbb C^E$ is the whole centralizer; hence $\langle Z(\mathbb C[S_{n-1}]),X_n\rangle=Z(\mathbb C[S_n],\mathbb C[S_{n-1}])$. [step 5.1, step 2.1, algebra]

7.1 Every central element of $\mathbb C[S_n]$ commutes with $\mathbb C[S_{n-1}]$, so $Z(\mathbb C[S_n])\subseteq Z(\mathbb C[S_n],\mathbb C[S_{n-1}])=\langle Z(\mathbb C[S_{n-1}]),X_n\rangle$ by step 6.1. [step 6.1, given, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

- **The dimension count.** The isomorphism of step 2.1 gives $\dim Z(\mathbb C[S_n],\mathbb C[S_{n-1}])=\#E=\sum_{\mu\vdash n-1}\#\operatorname{Add}(\mu)=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\#\operatorname{Rem}(\lambda)$, the number of edges of the Young graph between levels $n-1$ and $n$. At $n=3$ this is $4$, matching the direct commutant computation in $\mathbb C[S_3]$.

- **Commutativity.** The centralizer is the algebra of functions on the finite set $E$, hence commutative; this is the content of
  [[lem-relative-centralizer-for-sn-minus-one-in-sn-is-commutative]], and the
  isomorphism of step 2.1 makes it transparent.

- **The one-cycle generation.** Over a field of characteristic zero, the centre $Z(\mathbb C[S_m])$ is generated by the class sums of the chain, and the theorem identifies the relative centralizer with the algebra generated by the previous centre together with the single element $X_n$; no symmetric-function input is used.
