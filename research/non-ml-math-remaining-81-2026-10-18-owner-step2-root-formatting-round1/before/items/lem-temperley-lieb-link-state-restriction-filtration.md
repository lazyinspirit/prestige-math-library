## Statement

Let $R$ be a commutative ring with unit, $\delta\in R$, $n\ge1$, and let $t$ be allowed for $W_{n,t}(R,\delta)$. Include $\mathrm{TL}_{n-1}(R,\delta)$ into $\mathrm{TL}_n(R,\delta)$ by adding a rightmost vertical strand, and restrict the right action along this inclusion. Set $W_{m,s}=0$ when $s<0$, $s>m$, or $m-s$ is odd. Then there is a short exact sequence of right $\mathrm{TL}_{n-1}(R,\delta)$-modules

$$0\longrightarrow W_{n-1,t-1}\longrightarrow \operatorname{Res}^{TL_n}_{TL_{n-1}}W_{n,t}\longrightarrow W_{n-1,t+1}\longrightarrow0.$$

The sequence gives a two-step filtration; it is not asserted to split.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $R$, $\delta$, $n\ge1$, $t$, and the link-state quotient $W_{n,t}(R,\delta)$.

[F1] Link states are basis half-diagrams; the right action stacks the state above the algebra diagram and kills lower-propagating terms ([[def-temperley-lieb-link-state-standard-modules]], [[lem-temperley-lieb-link-state-module-action-is-well-defined]]).

[F2] The inclusion $\mathrm{TL}_{n-1}\to\mathrm{TL}_n$ is horizontal juxtaposition with the identity strand, and composition is associative ([[def-temperley-lieb-diagram-category]], [[thm-temperley-lieb-diagram-category-laws]]).

[F3] Every basis diagram is a product of the elementary generators $d_i$; hence $\mathrm{TL}_{n-1}$ is generated by $d_1,\ldots,d_{n-2}$ ([[thm-temperley-lieb-diagram-generators-span]]).

[F4] For $m\ge1$, in $\mathrm{TL}_{m+1}$ the elementary diagrams satisfy $d_i^2=\delta d_i$ for $1\le i\le m$, both adjacent triple relations for $1\le i<m$, and $d_id_j=d_jd_i$ for $1\le i,j\le m$ with $|i-j|>1$ ([[lem-temperley-lieb-generators-satisfy-local-relations]]).

[A1] For the additional complex-case corollary below, take $R=\mathbb C$, $q\in\mathbb C^\times$, $\delta=q+q^{-1}$, and assume $q^{2(t+1)}\ne1$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $U$ be the span in $W_{n,t}$ of link states in which the rightmost bottom site $n$ is a defect. Removing that defect and its top endpoint gives a link state in $W_{n-1,t-1}$; conversely, adding a new rightmost bottom site joined to a new rightmost top defect gives the inverse map. These basis maps define an $R$-module isomorphism $W_{n-1,t-1}\to U$. [F1, given, construct]

2.1 Under the inclusion in [F2], the rightmost strand of every diagram from $\mathrm{TL}_{n-1}$ is vertical and does not meet the other $n-1$ sites. Stacking such a diagram below a link state in $U$ leaves the external site $n$ connected to its same defect. Thus $U$ is a $\mathrm{TL}_{n-1}$-submodule, and the isomorphism in step 1.1 intertwines the restricted action. [F1, F2, step 1.1]

2.2 The quotient by $U$ has basis the link states in which $n$ is paired to a site $m<n$. Cut the arc $(m,n)$ and turn $m$ into a defect. Noncrossing forces every defect to lie outside this arc, so this new defect is the rightmost one; the result is a link state with $n-1$ sites and $t+1$ defects. The inverse attaches a new site $n$ to the rightmost defect. These operations are inverse bijections of bases, so they give an $R$-module isomorphism of the quotient's underlying module with $W_{n-1,t+1}$. [F1, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Check the quotient map on a basis state whose rightmost site $n$ is paired to $m<n$. A generator $d_i$ with $i\notin\{m-1,m\}$ does not meet the endpoint $m$; stacking and cutting therefore give the same exterior matching and the same loop count. If $i=m-1$, the site $m-1$ is either a defect or is paired to a site $k<m-1$ (it cannot be paired to a site inside $(m,n)$, since that would cross the arc $(m,n)$). In the paired case, stacking joins the distinct arcs $(k,m-1)$ and $(m,n)$ and replaces them by the bottom arc $(m-1,m)$ and the arc $(k,n)$; cutting either result at the arc ending at $n$ gives the same link state, with no loop removed. In the defect case, stacking makes $n$ a defect, so the result lies in $U$ and is zero in the quotient. Cutting first makes $m-1$ and $m$ two defects; stacking $d_{m-1}$ joins their two top endpoints, leaving fewer than $t+1$ propagating strands, so its class in $W_{n-1,t+1}$ is also zero. If $i=m$, then $m+1$ lies inside the arc $(m,n)$ and must be paired to some $k$ with $m+1<k<n$. Stacking joins the distinct arcs $(m,n)$ and $(m+1,k)$, replacing them by $(m,m+1)$ and $(k,n)$; cutting at the arc ending at $n$ before or after this operation gives the same state, again with no loop removed. These cases exhaust generators touching $m$; hence the quotient map intertwines every $d_i$. By [F3] it intertwines the whole $\mathrm{TL}_{n-1}$-action. The basis descriptions in steps 1.1 and 2.2 give exactness at every term. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2]

### Complex noncritical splitting

For the additional corollary, assume $R=\mathbb C$, $q\in\mathbb C^\times$, $\delta=q+q^{-1}$, and $q^{2(t+1)}\ne1$. Under these hypotheses the short exact sequence above splits as a sequence of right $\mathrm{TL}_{n-1}(\mathbb C,\delta)$-modules.

4.1 If $t=0$, then $W_{n-1,t-1}=0$; if $t=n$, then $W_{n-1,t+1}=0$. In either case exactness from step 3.1 makes the nonzero map in the sequence an isomorphism, so the sequence splits. Now suppose $0<t<n$, set $m=n-1$, and consider a nonzero summand $W_{m,s}$ with $s=t-1$ or $s=t+1$. [A1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Choose $v\in\mathbb C$ with $v^2=q$ and, in $\mathrm{TL}_{m+1}(\mathbb C,\delta)$, put $g_i=v1-v^{-1}d_i$ for $1\le i\le m$. By [F4], multiplication gives $(v1-v^{-1}d_i)(v^{-1}1-vd_i)=1+(\delta-q-q^{-1})d_i=1$, and the reversed product is the same; hence $g_i^{-1}=v^{-1}1-vd_i$. For $1\le i<m$, expanding the adjacent products with both triple relations gives $g_ig_{i+1}g_i-g_{i+1}g_ig_{i+1}=-v^{-1}(q-\delta+q^{-1})(d_i-d_{i+1})=0$. For valid distant indices, [F4] likewise gives $g_ig_j=g_jg_i$. Define $M_m=g_m\cdots g_2g_1^2g_2\cdots g_m$. Let $T_m:\mathrm{TL}_{m+1}\to\mathrm{TL}_m$ close the last top and bottom points by an exterior arc and remove each resulting loop with factor $\delta$, and put $F_m=T_m(M_m)$. The product has $2m$ crossing factors, so $F_m$ is independent of the choice of square root $v$. [A1, F1, F2, F4, construct, algebra]

5.2 For $M_0=1$ and $M_j=g_jM_{j-1}g_j$, the braid and distant-commutation relations imply $g_iM_m=M_mg_i$ for $i<m$. Indeed, put $P=g_m\cdots g_{i+2}$ and $B_i=g_{i+1}M_i g_{i+1}=g_{i+1}g_iM_{i-1}g_i g_{i+1}$, so $M_m=PB_iP^{\mathrm{rev}}$, where $P^{\mathrm{rev}}=g_{i+2}\cdots g_m$. The element $g_i$ commutes with $P$ and $P^{\mathrm{rev}}$, while $g_{i+1}$ commutes with $M_{i-1}$. Using $g_ig_{i+1}g_i=g_{i+1}g_ig_{i+1}$ on the left and right gives $g_iB_i=g_{i+1}g_iM_{i-1}g_{i+1}g_ig_{i+1}=B_ig_i$. Closing the last strand is a $\mathrm{TL}_m$-bimodule map, since stacking on the first $m$ strands is disjoint from that closure. Thus $g_iF_m=F_mg_i$ for every $i<m$. Since $d_i=q1-vg_i$ and the $d_i$ generate $\mathrm{TL}_m$ by [F3], $F_m$ is central. [F1, F2, F3, step 5.1, algebra]

6.1 For link states $x,y$ with $s$ defects, define $\psi(x,y)$ as the coefficient of $1_s$ in $y\circ x^*$, and set it to zero when this composite has fewer than $s$ propagating components. For link states $z,w$, associativity and the quotient action give $z\cdot(x^*\circ w)=\psi(x,z)w$: when $z\circ x^*$ has fewer than $s$ propagating components, the resulting term is zero in $W_{m,s}$. This pairing is nonzero if $s>0$: for no arcs, pair the all-defect state with itself; with $p=(m-s)/2>0$, pair the states with arcs $(1,2),(3,4),\ldots,(2p-1,2p)$ and defects to their right, and with arcs $(2,3),(4,5),\ldots,(2p,2p+1)$ and a defect at site $1$, respectively. Their overlay has no loop and gives value $1$. If $s=0$, the case needed below has $t=1$ and $q^4\ne1$, hence $\delta\ne0$; pairing any perfect matching with itself gives $\delta^{m/2}\ne0$. Choose $x,y$ with $\psi(x,y)=1$. For every $w$, the element $x^*\circ w$ then satisfies $y\cdot(x^*\circ w)=w$. If $\Theta$ is a module endomorphism, $\Theta(w)=\Theta(y)\cdot(x^*\circ w)=\psi(x,\Theta(y))w$. Thus every module endomorphism of these summands is scalar. [A1, F1, F2, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 The central operator given by right multiplication by $F_m$ is therefore scalar on each such $W_{m,s}$. To compute its scalar, use the state $z_{m,s}$ with $p=(m-s)/2$ adjacent arcs followed by $s$ defects. Expand the defining crossings in $F_m$ and retain the coefficient of $z_{m,s}$ in the link-state quotient. The local identities $d_i g_{i+1}g_i=-d_id_{i+1}$ and $g_i g_{i+1}d_i=-d_{i+1}d_i$, obtained from [F4] by $d_ig_{i+1}g_i=qd_i-d_id_{i+1}-\delta d_i+q^{-1}d_id_{i+1}d_i=-d_id_{i+1}$ and $g_ig_{i+1}d_i=qd_i-d_i^2-d_{i+1}d_i+q^{-1}d_id_{i+1}d_i=-d_{i+1}d_i$, for $1\le i<m$, remove the $p$ adjacent arcs. Along one orientation of the closed strand, each of the $s$ defect crossings has one surviving through-resolution; at the terminal crossing the two surviving choices contribute $q^s\delta$ and $-q^{s-1}$. Every other choice creates more than $p$ arcs and is zero in the quotient. The coefficient from this orientation is $q^s\delta-q^{s-1}=q^{s+1}$. Reflection gives the other orientation with $q$ replaced by $q^{-1}$, contributing $q^{-s}\delta-q^{-s+1}=q^{-s-1}$. Hence $F_m$ acts on $W_{m,s}$ as $q^{s+1}+q^{-s-1}$. [A1, F1, F2, F4, step 5.1, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 For the submodule $W_{n-1,t-1}$, step 7.1 gives eigenvalue $\lambda_-=q^t+q^{-t}$; for the quotient $W_{n-1,t+1}$ it gives $\lambda_+=q^{t+2}+q^{-t-2}$. Both pairings used in step 6.1 are nonzero: the $t+1$ label has positive defects, and the $t-1$ label has positive defects unless $t=1$, in which case $\delta\ne0$ follows from $q^4\ne1$. Their difference is $\lambda_+-\lambda_-=(q-q^{-1})(q^{t+1}-q^{-t-1})$, which is nonzero by [A1]. [A1, step 6.1, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 Let $S$ be right multiplication by $F_{n-1}$ on the restricted middle module. It is a module endomorphism because $F_{n-1}$ is central. The polynomial operator $P=(S-\lambda_+I)/(\lambda_--\lambda_+)$ is the identity on the submodule and induces zero on the quotient. Therefore its image lies in the submodule and its restriction there is the identity, so $P$ is a retraction. The middle module is the direct sum of the submodule and $\ker P$, and projection to the quotient restricts to an isomorphism on $\ker P$. This proves the asserted splitting. [step 3.1, step 5.2, step 8.1, algebra] ∎
