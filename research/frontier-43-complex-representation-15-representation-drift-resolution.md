## RG-26: commission the missing local proof interfaces

The following proposed IDs are local additions, to be inserted before their first use after collision checking. They are not existing suppliers or completed proofs.

1. `lem-separable-group-c-star-representations-disintegrate-over-a-commuting-diagonal-algebra`. Start with an abelian algebra A in the commutant, diagonalize A using the published FA theorem, and disintegrate a countable norm-dense rational-complex star subalgebra of C*(G). Prove separability of C*(G) from L1 separability in the second-countable setting. On one common conull set enforce linearity, multiplication, adjoints, norm bounds and nondegeneracy of the fibre representations; nondegeneracy requires a countable approximate identity and fibrewise convergence on a countable fundamental family, not merely global strong convergence. Extend by norm continuity to C*(G), then use `thm-nondegenerate-representations-of-c-star-g-are-unitary-representations-of-g` for strongly continuous fibre group representations. Prove measurability for each g from the integrated approximate identity. Do not disintegrate operators independently for uncountably many g and assert that all relations hold on one null complement; nor extend a dense-subgroup action without establishing continuity.

2. `lem-measurable-von-neumann-algebra-fields-have-measurable-commutants-and-centers`. Prove the measurable-field assertion referred out in Bekka Proposition 1.I.3. After measurable Gram–Schmidt and dimension strata, code operator unit balls on fixed separable spaces, impose the countable commutation equations, and construct measurable countable dense sections. A measurable selection assertion used here must itself receive a local proof under the actual Borel/conull hypotheses. Countability of generators alone does not justify choosing a measurable commutant generator family. Include the necessary concrete bicommutant/bounded-density interface if not already supplied, with a complete proof. The FA definition of von Neumann algebra expressly does not assume a bicommutant theorem.

3. `lem-central-diagonal-disintegration-has-factor-fibers`. With item 1 and item 2 proved, prove the fibre commutant and center identities as in Bekka Theorems 1.I.6–1.I.7/Corollary 1.I.8: the crucial inclusion D ⊆ M ⊆ D′ is needed to identify M with the integral of its fibre algebras. An arbitrary direct integral need not satisfy M = integral M_x (the constant trivial representation is an immediate obstruction). Apply the identity to M = pi(G)″ after diagonalizing its center. The center field must be scalar almost everywhere: if non-scalar center fibres persisted on positive measure, use proved measurable sections of the center unit balls to construct a global non-diagonal central operator. This supplies `thm-central-decomposition-into-factor-representations`. If claiming pairwise disjoint fibres on a common conull set, additionally prove bounded fibrewise approximation of the countable separating central projections as in the proof of Bekka 6.C.7; it is stronger than factoriality alone.

4. `lem-central-spectral-models-transport-and-intertwiners-disintegrate`. Prove that two central models of the same representation identify their bases modulo null sets by the normal isomorphism of their diagonal algebras; this requires a standard-Borel measure-algebra spatialization argument, not just the published fixed-generator uniqueness. For equivalent measures use the square-root Radon–Nikodym unitary, and for the resulting common base apply the published diagonal-commutant theorem to a block-matrix intertwiner. Countably many integrated generators give fibrewise intertwining and unitary equivalence almost everywhere. This supplies essential uniqueness in an honest base-identification formulation. To express it literally on the quasi-dual, also prove the Borel quasi-dual map and its standard measured image as in Bekka 6.C.8; do not silently identify an arbitrary quotient with a standard Borel space.

5. `lem-type-i-factor-fields-admit-measurable-irreducible-multiplicity-splittings`. Then construct the measurable splitting of a field of such factors, including a locally proved conull Borel selection result for nonzero abelian/minimal projections and measurable matrix units. Bekka 6.D.4 explicitly uses the von Neumann selection theorem for this step; citing it without proving it locally is inadequate. Combine with items 3–4 to establish the irreducible disintegration and its multiplicity uniqueness. State all assertions modulo null sets and avoid a global selector of representatives for the entire dual.

## RG-29: constructive higher-rank proof route

Commission the following local lemmas on RG-29 before the approved higher-rank theorem. This fills one existing theorem's proof, not an additional A/B pair or a new relative-(T) topic page.

- `lem-sl2-r-has-no-invariant-probability-on-the-projective-line`. Invariance under an upper unipotent forces a probability to be supported at its unique fixed point: on the complement its action is translation on R, whose disjoint translates of a fundamental interval forbid finite invariant mass. The lower unipotent has a different fixed point, giving a contradiction. It suffices to use two fixed nontrivial upper/lower unipotents.
- `lem-sl2-r-semidirect-r2-has-relative-property-t`. Apply the published abelian spectral-measure/covariance lemma to R². Identify its Pontryagin dual explicitly with R². E({0}) is the projection onto R²-invariants, proved by the PVM character formula and a countable separating character family. If there are no such invariants, the probability measures of almost invariant unit vectors have zero mass at zero. Push them to P(R²). The PVM estimate gives total-variation displacement bounded by 2||pi(g)v-v|| (with the chosen total-variation convention checked), so for the two unipotents these projective measures become asymptotically invariant. Compactness of probability laws on this compact metric space yields a weak limit invariant under both, contradicting the preceding lemma. Use the A supplier `lem-probability-laws-on-a-compact-metric-space-have-weakly-convergent-subsequences`, not its B-page example. This proves relative (T); it does not require the general higher-rank Lie theorem.
- `lem-normal-relative-property-t-controls-distance-to-invariant-vectors`. Since R² is normal in the semidirect product, its invariant subspace and orthogonal complement are invariant under that group. The direct-sum argument supplies a compact relative Kazhdan pair (otherwise direct-sum counterexamples yield almost invariant vectors with no normal-subgroup invariants). On the complement its displacement bound gives dist(v,H^N) ≤ epsilon/delta for (Q,epsilon)-invariant v. Consequently sup_n ||pi(n)v-v|| ≤ 2 epsilon/delta. Prove this quantitative step; existence of some subgroup invariant vector is insufficient for bounded generation.
- `lem-sl-n-r-is-boundedly-generated-by-elementary-root-subgroups`. Gaussian elimination over R gives a number M(n) of elementary transvections independent of the matrix. Handle zero pivots with determinant-one row interchanges and express the diagonal determinant-one remainder using uniformly bounded products of elementary 2x2 matrices; explicitly give these formulas and a bound. This is real-field elimination, not the deep integer bounded-generation theorem.

For each root subgroup U_ij choose a third index k (n ≥ 3), and embed SL2(R)⋉R² so that its normal vector group contains U_ij (for example the two entries in column j in rows i,k). There are finitely many choices. Take the finite union of their compact relative Kazhdan sets and a tolerance small enough that the preceding quantitative bounds imply displacement less than 1/2 on every group element, using M(n) and the telescoping unitary product estimate. The closed convex hull of the orbit then lies in the radius-1/2 ball about the starting unit vector. Its unique least-norm point is nonzero and invariant by unitarity and uniqueness, using the published `thm-projection-onto-a-nonempty-closed-convex-set`, whose statement assumes Countable Choice; AC supplies this assumption. Thus every representation with almost invariant vectors has an invariant vector. This proves the preserved SLn(R) theorem. For the usual arbitrary-Hilbert-space definition first reduce to a separable invariant subspace generated by a sequence of almost invariant vectors and a countable dense subset of the second-countable group; this permits use of the published separable PVM supplier.
