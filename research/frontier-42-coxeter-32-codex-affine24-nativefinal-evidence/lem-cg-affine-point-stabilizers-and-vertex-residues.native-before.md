## Statement

Let $v\in E$ and let $\mathcal A(v):=\{H\in\mathcal A_\Phi:v\in H\}$ be the finite set of affine walls through $v$. Let $J$ be the affine facet-type set from [[lem-cg-affine-alcove-separation-and-facet-types]], including one label $0_i$ for each nonempty irreducible component.

For $a\in J$, write $s_a$ for the fundamental facet reflection of type $a$ from [[lem-cg-affine-alcove-separation-and-facet-types]].

**(1) Point stabilizers and local sectors.** The subgroup $K_v:=\operatorname{Stab}_{W_a}(v)=\{g\in W_a:g(v)=v\}$ is finite and is generated by the reflections $r_H$ for $H\in\mathcal A(v)$. It acts simply transitively on the sectors at $v$, meaning the connected components of $E\setminus\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal A(v)}H$. After translating $v$ to the origin, these sectors are the chambers of the finite local reflection group on the span of the normals to the walls through $v$, times the common fixed subspace; in particular, $K_v$ has finite orbits on the sectors.

**(2) The link and vertex type.** If exactly two distinct walls pass through $v$, they are orthogonal. For each rank-two local root subsystem, the mirrors form a finite dihedral arrangement whose adjacent lines meet at angle $\pi/m$ for $m\in\{2,3,4,6\}$. Define $t(v)\subseteq J$ as the complement of the set of types of panels through $v$. The panel types through $v$ are independent of the incident alcove: they are exactly the types in $J\setminus t(v)$, and every incident alcove has exactly one panel of each such type through $v$. In particular, if $v$ is a vertex of $\overline{g(A)}$, then $J\setminus t(v)$ is the set of types of the facets of $g(A)$ containing $v$.

**(3) Rank-two boundary words.** Let $p\ne q$ be types in $J\setminus t(v)$ whose panels meet in a codimension-two face through $v$, and put $m:=\operatorname{ord}(s_ps_q)$, the finite order of the two corresponding facet reflections. The alcoves incident to that face form a cycle of length $2m$, with panel types alternating $p,q$. Writing $\sigma_p,\sigma_q$ for the canonical generators of the abstract rank-two Coxeter group $W_{pq}$ with label $m$, the word read from this cycle is $(\sigma_p\sigma_q)^m$ or $(\sigma_q\sigma_p)^m$. It is trivial in $W_{pq}$ and maps to the identity in every quotient of a Coxeter group whose matrix restricts to this rank-two submatrix. No axiom of choice is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The reduced crystallographic root system $\Phi\subset E$, the affine walls and group $W_a$, the fundamental alcove $A$, and the affine panel types $J$ from the cited items.

[F1] The roots are finite and span $E$; root reflections preserve $\Phi$, Cartan integers $B(\beta,\alpha^\vee)$ are integral, and each root line meets $\Phi$ in $\{\pm\alpha\}$ ([[def-reduced-crystallographic-euclidean-root-system]], [[def-coroot-and-dual-root-system]]).

[F2] $W_a$ is generated by all affine wall reflections, its generators fix their walls pointwise, and $W_a$ permutes the walls and alcoves ([[def-cg-affine-root-hyperplane-reflection-and-alcove]], [[lem-cg-affine-reflection-identities-and-local-finiteness]]).

[F3] $W_a$ acts by Euclidean isometries, so its elements preserve metric balls ([[def-cg-affine-root-hyperplane-reflection-and-alcove]], [[def-isometry-and-metric-embedding]]).

[F4] A facet reflection takes an alcove to the adjacent alcove across that facet ([[lem-cg-affine-alcove-separation-and-facet-types]]).

[F5] Panel types on a shared facet agree from both adjacent alcoves; every alcove in $W_a\cdot A$ has one facet of each type. Types are $W_a$-equivariant: a facet $g(F_a)$ of $g(A)$ is carried by $h$ to the type-$a$ facet $hg(F_a)$ of $hg(A)$, by the unique labelling in that supplier's proof step 7.1 ([[lem-cg-affine-alcove-separation-and-facet-types]]).

[F6] Every reduced crystallographic root system has a finite Weyl group acting faithfully on its roots ([[prop-the-weyl-group-is-finite-and-acts-faithfully-on-the-root-system]]).

[F7] The Weyl group of a reduced crystallographic root system acts simply transitively on its open Weyl chambers ([[thm-the-weyl-group-acts-simply-transitively-on-weyl-chambers]], [[def-open-and-closed-weyl-chambers]]).

[F8] A positive-definite crystallographic Coxeter scaling has allowed rank-two labels $m\in\{2,3,4,6\}$ ([[lem-cg-integer-pairings-and-allowed-dihedral-labels]]).

[F9] A finite Coxeter matrix defines the presented group with relators $s^2$ and $(st)^{m(s,t)}$ for finite labels; its rank-two groups are obtained by restricting the matrix ([[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]).

[F10] A group homomorphism preserves products and the identity, so it sends every defining relator to the identity ([[def-group-homomorphism]]).

[F11] Sectors and connected components are maximal connected subsets of the relevant complements ([[def-connected-component-and-quasicomponent]]).

[F12] A finite-dimensional real vector space is not a finite union of proper linear subspaces ([[lem-finite-dimensional-space-over-an-infinite-field-is-not-a-finite-union-of-proper-subspaces]]).

[F13] The closure of the fundamental alcove is a finite product of geometric simplices ([[lem-cg-highest-root-and-fundamental-alcove]]).

[F14] The induced norm is a norm and satisfies $|B(u,w)|\le\|u\|\|w\|$ ([[def-inner-product-space]], [[def-inner-product-norm]], [[cor-inner-product-induces-a-norm]], [[thm-cauchy-schwarz-in-an-inner-product-space]]).

[F15] Open balls $B_E(x,r)$ for the induced metric are convex and form neighborhoods in the induced topology ([[def-inner-product-norm]], [[cor-inner-product-induces-a-norm]], [[def-metric-topology]], [[def-metric-ball]]).

[F16] Every real $c$ has a unique integer $n$ with $n\le c<n+1$ ([[lem-integer-part]]).

[F17] Every alcove is open and convex ([[lem-cg-affine-reflection-identities-and-local-finiteness]]).

[F18] The fundamental alcove stabilizer in $W_a$ is trivial ([[lem-cg-affine-alcove-separation-and-facet-types]]).

[F19] The reflection in the wall of a type-$a$ facet of $g(A)$ is $g s_a g^{-1}$ ([[lem-cg-affine-alcove-separation-and-facet-types]]).

[F20] A finite-dimensional real normed space is locally compact in its norm metric; here $E$ is finite-dimensional and its inner-product norm is a norm ([[def-reduced-crystallographic-euclidean-root-system]], [[def-dimension]], [[cor-inner-product-induces-a-norm]], [[thm-locally-compact-normed-space-iff-finite-dimensional]], [[def-norm-and-normed-space]]).

[F21] In a locally compact metric space, each point has arbitrarily small compact closed balls; this applies to the metric space $(E,d_B)$ ([[def-locally-compact-metric-space]], [[lem-compact-closed-balls-in-a-locally-compact-metric-space]]).

[F22] A normed space with its norm topology is a real topological vector space: addition and scalar multiplication are continuous, and the topological-vector-space definition is the one used by the convex-hull supplier ([[lem-vector-operations-are-continuous-in-a-normed-space]], [[def-topological-vector-space-for-local-convexity]]).

[F23] The convex hull of a finite family of nonempty compact convex sets in a real topological vector space is compact ([[lem-locally-convex-closures-and-finite-compact-convex-hulls]]).

[F24] The convex hull consists of all finite convex combinations; it is convex and contains its generating set ([[def-locally-convex-topological-vector-space]]).

[F25] Every compact subset of $E$ meets only finitely many affine walls ([[lem-cg-affine-reflection-identities-and-local-finiteness]]).

[F26] Every singleton is compact by the open-cover definition, and it is convex ([[def-metric-compactness]], [[def-locally-convex-topological-vector-space]]).

[F27] A geometric simplex is the convex hull of its finite affinely independent vertex list, and its points have barycentric coordinates ([[def-geometric-simplex-spanned-by-affinely-independent-vertices]]).

[F28] For a metric space with its metric topology, metric-compact subsets are exactly topologically compact subsets ([[thm-compactness-agrees-with-metric-compactness]]).

[F29] Every path-connected subset of a topological space is connected ([[thm-path-connected-implies-connected]]).

[F30] An affine subspace of a vector space is a translate $x+U$ of a linear subspace $U$ ([[def-affine-subspace-of-a-vector-space]]).

[F31] Each wall $H_{\alpha,k}$ is an affine hyperplane defined by the nonzero functional $B(-,\alpha)$ ([[def-cg-affine-root-hyperplane-reflection-and-alcove]]).

[F32] A path is a continuous map from $[0,1]$ with the prescribed endpoints, and a subset is path-connected when each pair of its points is joined by such a path in the subset ([[def-path-connected]]).


## Proof


**Proof technique:** identify the walls through $v$ with a finite root subsystem, apply finite Weyl-chamber simple transitivity, and then read the codimension-two link in its normal plane.

1.1 Put $\Phi_v:=\{\alpha\in\Phi:B(v,\alpha)\in\mathbb Z\}$ and $V_v:=\operatorname{span}\Phi_v$. This is finite and spans $V_v$. If $\alpha,\beta\in\Phi_v$, then $s_\alpha\beta=\beta-B(\beta,\alpha^\vee)\alpha\in\Phi$ and $B(v,s_\alpha\beta)=B(v,\beta)-B(\beta,\alpha^\vee)B(v,\alpha)\in\mathbb Z$; hence $s_\alpha\beta\in\Phi_v$. The reducedness and crystallographic conditions restrict from $\Phi$, so $\Phi_v$ is a reduced crystallographic root system in $V_v$. Its root hyperplanes translated through $v$ are exactly the walls in $\mathcal A(v)$. [F1, algebra]

1.2 If $\Phi=\varnothing$, put $V=\{0\}$ and $A=\overline A=\{0\}$. Otherwise, for each irreducible component $i$ let $V_i$ be the vertex list of the simplex $\overline{A_i}$ from [F13] and [F27], put $V:=\prod_i V_i$, and regard $V$ as a finite subset of $E=\bigoplus_iE_i$. By [F27], each component point has barycentric coordinates in its listed vertices. For $x_i=\sum_j t_{ij}v_{ij}\in\overline{A_i}$, the product weights $\prod_i t_{i,j_i}$ on tuples $(v_{i,j_i})_i\in V$ are nonnegative, sum to $1$, and their convex combination is $(x_i)_i$; hence $\overline A\subseteq\operatorname{co}(V)$. Conversely, $\overline A$ is a convex product of simplices and contains $V$, so [F24] gives $\operatorname{co}(V)\subseteq\overline A$. Thus $\overline A=\operatorname{co}(V)$. For the empty system the same equality holds with $V=\{0\}$. For any alcove $C$, choose $y_0\in C$. By [F17] it is open, so choose $R>0$ with $B_E(y_0,R)\subseteq C$. By [F20] and [F21], choose $0<\rho<R$ such that $K_0:=\overline B_E(y_0,\rho)$ is metric-compact, hence compact in the norm topology by [F28]. Since $\rho<R$, $K_0$ is contained in $B_E(y_0,R)$ and hence in $C$; put $O_C:=B_E(y_0,\rho)\subseteq K_0$. The set $K_0$ is convex by the triangle inequality in [F14]. Each singleton $\{v\}$ for $v\in V$ is metric-compact by [F26], hence compact in the norm topology by [F28], and is convex by [F24]. The finitely many singletons together with $K_0$ are therefore nonempty compact convex sets. Since $E$ with its norm topology is a real topological vector space by [F22], [F23] makes
$$K_C:=\operatorname{co}(V\cup K_0)$$
compact and convex. It contains $\overline A=\operatorname{co}(V)$ and $O_C$; hence it contains every segment joining a point of $A$ to a point of $O_C$. By [F25], only finitely many walls meet $K_C$. [F13, F14, F15, F17, F20, F21, F22, F23, F24, F25, F26, F27, F28, choose, algebra]

1.3 Let $O\subseteq E$ be nonempty and open, and let $L_1,\ldots,L_N$ be a finite family of proper affine subspaces. If $N=0$, or if $E=\{0\}$ (when every proper affine subspace is empty), any $p\in O$ avoids their union. Otherwise, write $L_j=a_j+D_j$ with $D_j$ a proper linear subspace by [F30]. By [F12], choose a direction $d\in E$ outside $\bigcup_jD_j$; then $d\ne0$. Choose $p\in O$ and, by [F15], $r>0$ with $B_E(p,r)\subseteq O$. For $|t|<r/\|d\|_B$, homogeneity in [F14] gives $\|td\|_B<r$, so $p+td\in O$. Each line $p+\mathbb Rd$ meets each $L_j$ in at most one point because $d\notin D_j$. Removing these finitely many parameters from this nonempty interval leaves an allowed $t$, so $p+td\in O\setminus\bigcup_jL_j$. This finite avoidance makes no use of the axiom of choice. [F12, F14, F15, F30, choose, algebra]

1.4 If $\Phi=\varnothing$, then $E=\{0\}$ by [F1], there are no walls, and we may take $r_0=1$. Otherwise fix $z\in E$. For each of the finitely many roots $\alpha$, put $c_\alpha:=B(z,\alpha)$ and let $\delta_\alpha:=1$ if $c_\alpha\in\mathbb Z$, while otherwise let $\delta_\alpha:=\min\{c_\alpha-\lfloor c_\alpha\rfloor,\ \lfloor c_\alpha\rfloor+1-c_\alpha\}>0$. If $\|u-z\|_B<\delta_\alpha/(2\|\alpha\|_B)$, Cauchy--Schwarz [F14] gives $|B(u,\alpha)-c_\alpha|<\delta_\alpha/2$; therefore $B(u,\alpha)$ is not an integer unless $c_\alpha$ is an integer and $B(u,\alpha)=c_\alpha$. Taking the minimum of these positive radii over $\Phi$ gives $r_z>0$ such that $B_E(z,r_z)$ meets only walls through $z$. [F1, F14, F16, algebra]

2.1 If $E=\{0\}$, then $\Phi=\varnothing$ by [F1], there are no walls, and $W_a$ is generated by the empty family by [F2]; hence there is one alcove, one sector, and $K_v=R_v=\{1\}$. Assume now $E\ne\{0\}$. For $H=H_{\alpha,k}\in\mathcal A(v)$ one has $k=B(v,\alpha)$ and $r_{\alpha,k}(v+u)=v+s_\alpha(u)$. Thus, after translating $v$ to $0$, $R_v:=\langle r_H:H\in\mathcal A(v)\rangle$ is the Weyl group of $\Phi_v$ on $V_v$ and acts trivially on $V_v^\perp$. By [F6] it is finite, and by [F7] it acts simply transitively on the chambers of the local root arrangement; these chambers times $V_v^\perp$ are exactly the sectors at $v$. If $\Phi_v=\varnothing$, then $V_v=\{0\}$, $R_v=\{1\}$, and there is one sector, namely $E$, which is connected because any two points are joined by a straight path continuous by [F22, F32] and hence connected by [F29]. [F1, F2, F6, F7, F22, F29, F32, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Fix any alcove $C$, choose $x\in A$, and use $O_C,K_C$ from step 1.2. Let $\mathcal H_C$ be the finite set of distinct walls meeting $K_C$. For every intersecting pair of distinct walls $H,H'\in\mathcal H_C$, their intersection $P=H\cap H'$ has codimension two: by [F31], distinct affine hyperplanes that intersect have independent normals, since proportional normals would make them equal. Also $x\notin P$ because $x\in A$ by [F13]. Writing $D_P$ for the direction space of $P$, choose $p\in P$ and put $w_P:=x-p\notin D_P$. The affine subspace $L_P:=p+(D_P+\mathbb Rw_P)$ is proper: $D_P$ has codimension two and adjoining the independent vector $w_P$ raises its dimension by one. Apply the finite-avoidance argument of step 1.3 to this finite family in the open set $O_C$ to choose $y$ outside every $L_P$; if the family is empty, choose any $y\in O_C$. If $[x,y]$ met an intersection $P$, then $y\in L_P$, contrary to this choice; thus the segment meets no two distinct walls at the same point. It lies in $K_C$, so it meets only walls from $\mathcal H_C$ and crosses each at most once because $x$ lies on no wall. At a crossing point $q$ on a wall $H$, choose a compact closed ball about $q$ using [F20] and [F21]. By [F25] only finitely many walls meet that ball, and none of the other walls in that list contains $q$. For each such wall $H_{\beta,l}$, the positive radius $|B(q,\beta)-l|/(2\|\beta\|_B)$ gives a ball about $q$ missing it, by Cauchy--Schwarz [F14]. Taking the minimum of these finitely many radii and the original ball radius (or the original radius if there are no other walls) yields a neighborhood meeting the arrangement only in $H$. Each half-ball is convex, hence path-connected by [F32] with continuity from [F22], and connected by [F29]; it lies in an alcove, and the two alcove closures share an open patch of $H$, so they are adjacent. Therefore the segment yields a finite gallery from $A$ to $C$; by [F4], each successive alcove is obtained from the preceding one by a wall reflection in $W_a$. In particular every alcove lies in $W_a\cdot A$. [F1, F4, F13, F14, F15, F20, F21, F25, F29, F30, F31, F32, F22, step 1.2, step 1.3, choose, algebra]

2.3 Suppose exactly two distinct walls through $v$ have normals $\alpha,\beta$. Their normals are not parallel, since distinct parallel hyperplanes through one point coincide. If $B(\alpha,\beta)\ne0$, then $\gamma:=s_\alpha\beta$ is a root distinct from $\pm\alpha,\pm\beta$, and $B(v,\gamma)=B(v,\beta)-B(\beta,\alpha^\vee)B(v,\alpha)\in\mathbb Z$. The wall normal to $\gamma$ through $v$ is therefore a third distinct wall, a contradiction. Hence $B(\alpha,\beta)=0$ and the two walls are orthogonal. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

2.4 Let $P$ be the span of two nonparallel normals in $\Phi_v$ and set $\Psi:=\Phi_v\cap P$. This is a finite reduced crystallographic root system spanning $P$: reflections in its roots preserve $P$, and the root-system conditions restrict from step 1.1. Its Weyl group $W(\Psi)$ is finite by [F6] and acts simply transitively on its chambers by [F7]. By [F31], the mirror arrangement in $P$ consists of finitely many lines through the origin, so its sectors occur cyclically and the sector-adjacency graph is connected. Let $r$ and $s$ be the reflections in the two boundary lines of one sector $D$. Each sends $D$ to its neighbor across that line. Inductively, if $h(D)$ is reached for some $h\in\langle r,s\rangle$, the reflections across its two boundary lines are $hrh^{-1}$ and $hsh^{-1}$, so both neighboring sectors are also in the orbit. Thus $\langle r,s\rangle$ is transitive on sectors; simple transitivity of $W(\Psi)$ then gives $\langle r,s\rangle=W(\Psi)$. If $m$ is the order of $rs$, the two line reflections generate a dihedral group of order $2m$. Simple transitivity therefore gives exactly $2m$ sectors, and transitivity by orthogonal maps makes their angles equal, hence each angle is $\pi/m$. Choose inward-pointing root normals $\alpha,\beta$ to the boundary lines of $D$ and put $e_1:=\alpha/\|\alpha\|$, $e_2:=\beta/\|\beta\|$, $c_1:=\|\alpha\|$, and $c_2:=\|\beta\|$. Their Gram matrix has diagonal entries $1$ and off-diagonal entry $-\cos(\pi/m)$, so it is the rank-two Coxeter form with label $m$; the scaled roots $c_1e_1=\alpha$ and $c_2e_2=\beta$ have integral Cartan numbers by [F1]. Thus these data form a positive-definite crystallographic scaling, and [F8] gives $m\in\{2,3,4,6\}$. Thus every rank-two local mirror arrangement is the stated dihedral arrangement. [F1, F6, F7, F8, F31, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 If $\Phi=\varnothing$, then $E=\{0\}$ and the unique sector and alcove are both $\{0\}$. Otherwise fix $v$ and let $r_v$ be from step 1.4. Each local sector $S$ is a sign-pattern intersection of open half-spaces by [F31], hence convex; it is a cone with apex $v$, so $v\in\overline S$ and $S\cap B_E(v,r_v)$ is nonempty and convex. It is path-connected by straight segments under [F32], which are continuous by [F22], and hence connected by [F29]. Since the ball meets no walls except those through $v$, this set lies in one global alcove, call it $C_S$, and $v\in\overline{C_S}$. Conversely, if $v\in\overline C$ for a global alcove $C$, then $C\cap B_E(v,r_v)$ is nonempty by closure and convex by [F17] and [F15], so it is path-connected by [F32] and connected by [F22, F29]. It avoids every wall through $v$, so it lies in one local sector $S$. The two connected sets overlap, hence $C=C_S$. If $C_S=C_T$, the connected set $C\cap B_E(v,r_v)$ meets both sectors, which forces $S=T$ because distinct sectors are distinct connected components of the local complement. Thus local sectors at $v$ correspond bijectively to alcoves whose closures contain $v$. [F1, F11, F15, F17, F22, F29, F31, F32, step 2.1, step 1.4, algebra]

4.1 Take $g\in K_v$ and a local sector $S$. Because $g$ fixes $v$ and permutes the wall arrangement by [F2], $g(S)$ is a local sector. By simple transitivity of $R_v$ on sectors, choose $h\in R_v$ with $h(S)=g(S)$, using [F7] and step 2.1. Both maps fix $v$ and are isometries by [F3], so $h^{-1}g$ preserves $B_E(v,r_v)$ and the local sector $S$; it therefore stabilizes the unique incident alcove $C_S$ from step 3.1. Every alcove is a $W_a$-translate of $A$ by step 2.2, and its stabilizer is trivial by [F18] and conjugation to $A$. Thus $h^{-1}g=1$, so $g=h\in R_v$. Conversely every generator $r_H$ of $R_v$ fixes $v$ pointwise by [F2], hence $R_v\le K_v$. We conclude $K_v=R_v$, proving finiteness, generation by the reflections through $v$, and simple transitivity on sectors. [F2, F3, F6, F7, F18, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 2.2, algebra]

4.2 If $E=\{0\}$, then $\Phi=\varnothing$ by [F1] and $J=\varnothing$ by its definition; there is one sector and one incident alcove with no facets, so the panel-type assertion is immediate. Otherwise, for an incident alcove $C$, let $I_C(v)\subseteq J$ be the types of its facets containing $v$. Any two distinct sectors of the finite central arrangement of walls through $v$ are connected by a gallery: choose regular points $x$ and $y_0$ in the two sectors and in the ball from step 1.4. The set $O$ given by the second sector intersected with this open ball is nonempty and open. For each pair of distinct local walls, their intersection $P$ has codimension two by the same affine-hyperplane argument in step 2.2, and $x\notin P$. By the affine-span argument in step 2.2, the affine hull $L_P$ of $x$ and $P$ is a proper affine subspace. Apply the finite-avoidance argument of step 1.3 to these finitely many subspaces in $O$ to choose $y$ outside them (if there are no pairs, take $y=y_0$). The ball is convex by [F15], so $[x,y]$ stays in it; it crosses each local wall at most once and never crosses two at the same point. By the sector-to-alcove correspondence in step 3.1, these local galleries give galleries of incident global alcoves crossing only walls through $v$. At each crossing [F4] gives the adjacent alcove by reflection in a wall through $v$. This reflection fixes $v$ and carries every facet of the first alcove containing $v$ bijectively to a facet of the second containing $v$. By the full type equivariance in [F5], it preserves all these facet types, not just the shared-panel type. Hence $I_C(v)$ is independent of $C$; call it $I(v)$ and set $t(v):=J\setminus I(v)$. This proves the panel-type clause, including the case $I(v)=\varnothing$. If $v$ is a vertex of $\overline{g(A)}$, the facets of that alcove containing $v$ are exactly its panels through $v$, so their types are $I(v)=J\setminus t(v)$. [F1, F4, F5, F15, F31, step 2.1, step 1.3, step 1.4, step 3.1, step 2.2, algebra]

5.1 Let $p\ne q$ lie in $I(v)$ and choose any incident alcove $C=g(A)$. By the definition of $I(v)$, its facets of types $p,q$ both contain $v$; [F13] says they meet in a codimension-two face $F$ with $v\in\overline F$. Their wall reflections are $g s_p g^{-1}$ and $g s_q g^{-1}$ by [F19], so their product has the same finite order $m$ as $s_ps_q$. Let $u$ lie in the relative interior of $F$. Because $\overline C$ is a product of geometric simplices, the tangent cone of $\overline C$ at $u$ has lineality space $\operatorname{span}(F-F)$. The interior of $C$ lies on one side of every wall; therefore the defining linear form of any wall through $u$ has one sign on this tangent cone and must vanish on its lineality space. Hence every wall through $u$ contains $F$, and its normal lies in the two-dimensional normal plane. The p- and q-facets give two independent normals, so the local root subsystem has rank two. By the sector-to-alcove correspondence in step 3.1 its $2m$ local sectors correspond exactly to the $2m$ alcoves incident to $F$. Since the closure of $C$ is a product of geometric simplices, precisely its p- and q-facets contain $u$; step 4.2 therefore shows that the panel labels around this local cycle alternate p,q. Writing $\sigma_p,\sigma_q$ for the abstract generators of $W_{pq}$, the boundary word is $(\sigma_p\sigma_q)^m$ or $(\sigma_q\sigma_p)^m$. By [F9] the first is a defining relator, and the reverse is its conjugate by $\sigma_p$; [F10] then shows every homomorphism from a Coxeter group with this rank-two restriction sends the boundary word to $1$. No axiom of choice is used: all root, chamber, wall, and word lists here are finite. [F9, F10, F13, F19, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.2, step 2.4, algebra] ∎
