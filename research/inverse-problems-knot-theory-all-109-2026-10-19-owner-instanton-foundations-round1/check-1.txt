Full AC in [F1] supplies countable choice by restriction to any countable family.\n\n\n\n1.1 Under the full-AC hypothesis [F1], the supplier in [F2] applies. Since $c$ is essential on the torus, it is nonseparating: a separating simple curve on a genus-one surface cuts off a disk on one side and is null-homotopic. Cutting the torus along $c$ therefore gives a connected orientable surface with two boundary circles and Euler characteristic zero, hence an annulus. An embedded arc joining its two boundary circles closes after regluing to a loop meeting $c$ transversely once. The algebraic intersection is therefore $\\pm1$, so $[c]$ is primitive in $H_1(T_K;\\mathbb Z)$. Since both $[c]$ and $[\\lambda]$ are primitive, $q=\\pm1$. Orient $c$ to make $[c]=[\\lambda]$. By [F3], $c$ is isotopic on $T_K$ to a chosen simple representative of the preferred longitude. [F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]\n\n3.1 In product coordinates $N(K)\\cong S^1\\times D^2$, the curve $c$ has core degree one by [F2], so its torus class is $(1,k)$ for an integer $k$. The graph $\\gamma_k(t)=(e^{it},e^{ikt})$ has that class. By [F3], choose a smooth isotopy $c_s(t)=(u_s(t),z_s(t))$ on the boundary torus from $c$ to $\\gamma_k$, stationary near its final time after smooth reparametrization. This isotopy need not trace an embedded annulus on the torus itself. Instead choose a smooth strictly decreasing radius $r(s)$ from $1$ to $1/2$ with nonzero derivative, and use $(s,t)\\mapsto(u_s(t),r(s)z_s(t))$ in the tube. Distinct $s$ have distinct radii, while each fixed-$s$ curve is embedded; moreover the time derivative has nonzero radial component and is independent of its circle tangent. This gives a smooth embedded annulus with outer boundary exactly $c$ and inner boundary the radius-$1/2$ copy of $\\gamma_k$. Continue it inward by $(r,t)\\mapsto(e^{it},r e^{ikt})$ for $0\\le r\\le1/2$. The stationary final curve makes the collars agree; the inward annulus is smooth and regular even at $r=0$ and has core boundary $K$. The resulting annulus lies otherwise in the interior of $N(K)$. [F2, F3, step 2.1, construct]\n\n4.1 The proper disk $D$ on the exterior side and this annulus on the tube side meet only along $c$. Their union is an embedded disk with boundary $K$; smoothing the corner along $c$ in a product collar leaves that boundary fixed. Thus $K$ bounds a smooth disk, which is a genus-zero Seifert surface. Full AC supplies the countable-choice hypothesis of [F4], so [F4] makes $K$ ambient isotopic to the unknot, contrary to the hypothesis. Therefore no such disk $D$ exists.
