# Constructing the strong-linkage supplier

This proposal gives the representation-theoretic construction that completes the geometric supplier. The explicit root-PBW input below is supplied by the owner-authored adjacent ../root-pbw/construction.md, substantively read in full for this consumer integration. It is not installed or certified. In particular the root-PBW input is obtained from that actual root-basis/braid/parabolic construction, not inferred from global-basis freeness alone.

Work with the actual Lusztig algebra at zeta, ord(zeta^2)=ell odd>h, for a finite simply-laced full weight datum. Both ord(zeta)=ell and ord(zeta)=2ell are retained. No primality of ell is used: every scalar-zero test below is exactly [a]_zeta=0 if and only if ell divides a, and all denominators have indices strictly between zero and ell. All modules below have a formal P-grading; the K eigenvalues alone do not serve as labels. Write <=_s for the transitive closure of strict downward positive-root affine reflections in shifted coordinates, allowing equality.

## Precise algebra input

The required root-PBW supplier has the following three consequences.

(P1) For a reduced word for w0 with convex root order beta_1,...,beta_N, the small negative half R has the residue root-PBW basis b_a (0<=a_j<ell), graded by their actual positive root heights. The same basis statement holds in every braid-transformed Borel. In particular its unique top-height vector is the nonzero monomial with all exponents ell-1. No leading-term/Levendorskii-Soibelman straightening or big-over-small freeness hypothesis is required.

(P2) The algebra u generated by E_i,F_i,K_i and their inverses on the type-1 category is finite dimensional (take its type-1 quotient K_i^ell=1 when ord(zeta)=ell; K_i^(2ell)=1 suffices in general). Its negative half has basis the same root monomials with 0<=a_j<ell. Its triangular decomposition and each braid-transformed triangular decomposition hold. Braid automorphisms restrict to u. For a simple root i, u is free over its minimal parabolic generated by u^0,u^+,F_i; the complementary negative-root PBW monomials give a basis.

(P3) The root-PBW constructions and statements hold in the formal P-graded modules, including twisted Borels; each highest character has the specified K eigenvalues. No divided toral element is silently dropped from the full Lusztig category: u is used solely as a restriction algebra with retained formal grading.

## 1. The small negative algebra has a one-dimensional socle

Use the actual proved local theorem [[thm-hh-finite-hopf-algebras-are-frobenius]]. Set R=u^- and let T be its finite toral group in the type-1 quotient, so the negative small Borel is H=R semidirect C[T]. The ordinary coproduct and antipode restrict to H: this follows directly on F_i and K_i from Delta(F_i)=F_i tensor K_i^(-1)+1 tensor F_i, Delta(K_i)=K_i tensor K_i, and their antipode formulas. The odd-order toral type-1 quotient is a Hopf quotient because K_i^ell-1 generates a Hopf ideal. Thus H is an actual nonzero finite-dimensional Hopf algebra, by (P1)-(P2), not a braided Hopf algebra being passed to an ordinary theorem.

The positive-height ideal R_+ of R is nilpotent. The torus preserves the root-height grading, so J=R_+H=HR_+ is a nilpotent two-sided ideal of H, and H/J=C[T]. This quotient is semisimple over C: its finite commuting toral operators diagonalize simultaneously, since each has finite order and its minimal polynomial divides a separable polynomial X^d-1. A nilpotent ideal annihilates every simple module (otherwise JM=M would imply J^nM=M), and a semisimple quotient has zero radical. Therefore J is exactly rad H. Similarly R_+=rad R, since R/R_+=C.

The local finite-Hopf Frobenius theorem supplies a functional f making (a,b) -> f(ab) nondegenerate. Consequently Theta:H -> H*, Theta(b)(a)=f(ab), is an isomorphism of left H-modules for the left dual-regular action (h phi)(a)=phi(ah). Its socle consists of those functionals annihilating J: indeed J phi=0 means phi(aj)=0 for all a in H,j in J, and HJ=J. Hence dim soc_H H=dim(H/J)=|T|.

On the other hand soc_H H={h:Jh=0}={h:R_+h=0}=soc_R H, using J=HR_+. By the PBW decomposition H=direct-sum_(t in T) R K_t as left R-modules, dim soc_R H=|T| dim soc_R R. The preceding equality proves dim soc_R R=1. Its line is the top PBW monomial: it is nonzero by (P1), and multiplication by every positive-height element vanishes since its height is maximal. This result transports to each braid-transformed half by its algebra isomorphism and to u^+ by the half-exchange isomorphism; those transformed Borels need not themselves be Hopf subalgebras for the standard coproduct.

Let Z(lambda)=u tensor_(u^0 u^+) C_lambda be the graded baby Verma. As a u^- module it is regular, so every nonzero u-submodule contains its one-dimensional lowest weight line. Its u-span is consequently the simple socle of Z(lambda). The ordinary highest character defines a unique simple head L_u(lambda): the sum of submodules missing the one-dimensional highest line is the unique maximal submodule. Every finite graded simple u-module has such a highest label, since its finite support admits a maximal weight killed by u^+. The simple formal characters are linearly independent: in a finite putative relation, a maximal highest label has coefficient one in its own character and occurs in no character with a lower or incomparable highest label. All highest labels, subobjects, heads and socles in subsequent character comparisons are taken in this formal graded category.

## 2. Rank-one intertwiners and their exact kernels

First work in rank one with highest weight coordinate m. In its ordinary baby Verma use v_j=F^(j)v_0 (0<=j<ell). In the opposite baby Verma of lowest coordinate m'=m-2(ell-1), use v'_j=E^(j)v'_0. Their rank-one action is

    F v_j=[j+1] v_(j+1),       E v_j=[m-j+1]v_(j-1),
    E v'_j=[j+1]v'_(j+1),     F v'_j=-[m'+j-1]v'_(j-1).

Out-of-range vectors are zero. The map sending v_0 to v'_(ell-1) is an intertwiner. Explicitly it sends v_j to c_j v'_(ell-1-j), where c_0=1 and

    c_(j+1)=-[m-ell-j] c_j/[j+1].

These formulas verify both E and F compatibility directly; K compatibility follows from weights. For E compatibility use [ell-j]=-s[j] and [a-ell]=s[a], with s=zeta^ell in {1,-1}. They also verify the endpoint relations. Let n be the unique integer 1<=n<=ell congruent to m+1 modulo ell. The coefficients c_j are nonzero for j<n and zero for j>=n. Thus the map is invertible if n=ell; otherwise its kernel is exactly span(v_n,...,v_(ell-1)). It is generated by the singular vector F^(n)v_0, and is an image of the baby Verma at label lambda-n alpha. This last surjection follows also directly because [m-n+1]=0 and all other simple positive generators commute with F_i.

The same calculation in the minimal parabolic and its free induction in (P2) proves the corresponding statement in arbitrary rank: for adjacent Borels w and ws_i, put beta=w alpha_i and let kappa be the w-highest label. There is a map

    Z^w(kappa) -> Z^(ws_i)(kappa-(ell-1)beta).

For n in {1,...,ell} congruent to (kappa+w rho,beta), its kernel is zero if n=ell, and otherwise is the image of Z^w(kappa-n beta). Exactness here is actual freeness over the minimal parabolic, not an inference from an equality of characters.

## 3. Baby-Verma strong linkage

Choose a reduced word w0=s_1...s_N, put w_j=s_1...s_(j-1), and beta_j=w_j alpha_(i_j)>0. Set lambda(w)=lambda+(ell-1)(w rho-rho). Compose the maps of section 2 along

    Z(lambda) -> Z^(w_2)(lambda(w_2)) -> ... -> Z^(w0)(lambda(w0)).

Every module in this chain has the same formal character as Z(lambda). This is the direct root-product identity: replacing positive roots by w-positive roots changes the PBW product character by e^((ell-1)(rho-w rho)), canceled by the highest-label shift. Each map is nonzero on the lambda weight line: beta_j is a not-yet-inverted positive root, so that line has exponent zero in its rank-one source factor, and c_0=1. Hence the composite is nonzero on that line.

The target is induced from the opposite Borel, with lowest label lambda-2(ell-1)rho. Its lambda weight line is the top of its regular u^+ representation. Section 1 shows that its u-span is the simple socle. Therefore the composite has simple image L_u(lambda): its image is generated by the lambda line, and a nonzero quotient of the cyclic ordinary baby Verma with simple image is its unique simple head.

Consequently every composition factor L_u(nu) of Z(lambda) other than its head occurs in the kernel of this composite. The filtration of a kernel of a finite composite, using its successive images, makes it a composition factor of one of the individual kernels. By section 2 this individual kernel is an image of

    Z^(w_j)(lambda(w_j)-n_j beta_j),

where 1<=n_j<ell and n_j congruent to (lambda+rho,beta_j) modulo ell. The congruence follows because lambda(w_j)+w_j rho and lambda+rho differ by ell(w_j rho-rho). The displayed module has exactly the same formal character as the ordinary Z(lambda-n_j beta_j). Linear independence of graded simple characters therefore places L_u(nu) among the composition factors of that ordinary baby Verma.

Repeat. At every repetition the highest label decreases by n beta with beta positive, and nu remains a weight; the nonnegative integer root height of highest-label minus nu strictly decreases. Termination proves nu<=_s lambda, since lambda-n beta is exactly the downward reflection of lambda+rho in the hyperplane (z,beta)=k ell, with k ell=(lambda+rho,beta)-n. This proves baby-Verma strong linkage by an actual rank-one kernel argument.

## 4. Character transfer from big Weyl modules to baby Vermas

This shorter transfer was supplied by the sibling affine-Langlands constructor and independently checked here. It removes the need for any assertion about a big-Verma restriction filtration or big-over-small freeness.

Let p(beta) be the ordinary Kostant partition number, defined by the formal product sum_(beta in Q+) p(beta)e^(-beta)=product_(alpha>0)(1-e^(-alpha))^(-1). The baby PBW character is

    ch Z(eta)=e^eta product_(alpha>0)(1-e^(-ell alpha))/(1-e^(-alpha)).

The actual Weyl character formula therefore gives the identity

    ch Delta(lambda)=sum_(w in W,beta in Q+) det(w)p(beta)ch Z(w dot lambda-ell beta).

This equality takes place in the completion of the group algebra on the cone lambda-Q+. For dominant lambda, w dot lambda<=lambda for all w. Every summand has support in lambda-Q+, and for a fixed weight nu only finitely many beta can contribute, since w dot lambda-ell beta>=nu. Thus all coefficient extractions and cancellations are finite. The identity is obtained by multiplying the finite Weyl numerator by product_(alpha>0)(1-e^(-ell alpha))^(-1), and does not posit a module filtration.

The characters of the finite graded simple u-modules form a unitriangular basis in this completion. Here is an explicit justification beyond finite linear independence. Each simple L_u(eta) has highest coefficient one at eta and all other weights strictly below it. Recursive subtraction by increasing root height constructs a unique coefficient expansion for any formal character supported in lambda-Q+; every coefficient at bounded height is determined after finitely many steps. This recursion is locally finite because there are finitely many root-lattice points in a bounded positive-root height interval. The completed baby character identity can therefore be expanded and compared in that simple-character basis.

If L_zeta(nu) is a composition factor of Delta(lambda), its restriction to u contains L_u(nu) as a composition factor: take the u-submodule generated by its big highest vector, whose baby-Verma quotient has simple head L_u(nu), and use exact restriction of the finite big composition series. The coefficient of ch L_u(nu) in the left side is consequently positive. In the right side that coefficient is a finite signed integer sum of baby composition multiplicities. A positive value forces at least one baby Z(eta), eta=w dot lambda-ell beta, to contain L_u(nu). Section 3 then gives nu<=_s eta. No inference from virtual characters to an actual filtration has occurred.

## 5. Negative lattice translations are downward chains

For every x and every positive root alpha, x-ell alpha is obtainable from x by at most two strict downward affine reflections. Write (x,alpha)=k ell+r, with 0<=r<ell. If r>0, reflect first in level k ell, subtracting r alpha, then in level (k-1)ell, subtracting (ell-r)alpha. If r=0, reflect directly in level (k-1)ell, subtracting ell alpha. Both steps are strict. Apply this successively to the positive-root summands of beta. For dominant lambda, each w dot lambda is also downward linked to lambda using level-zero reflections: construct a reduced expression by adjoining simple reflections on the left; at a length-increasing step s_i v, the root v^(-1)alpha_i is positive, hence (v(lambda+rho),alpha_i)>0 and this step is strictly downward. Thus eta=w dot lambda-ell beta<=_s lambda. Section 4 consequently proves nu<=_s lambda for every composition factor of Delta(lambda).

The involution lambda*= -w0 lambda carries positive finite roots to positive finite roots and preserves these chains. Exact ordinary duality therefore proves the same composition-factor assertion for the dual Weyl module nabla(lambda).

## 6. Upgrade from Weyl composition factors to tilting Weyl sections

Use the locally constructed finite highest weight truncation on the finite dominance ideal Gamma={eta dominant:eta<=lambda}. It has actual projective covers P_mu and reciprocity

    (P_mu:Delta(nu))=[nabla(nu):L(mu)].

Sections 4-5 imply that every Weyl section of the projective kernel K_mu=ker(P_mu -> Delta(mu)) has label nu with mu<_s nu.

Let S={eta in Gamma:eta<=_s lambda}. Initially X=Delta(lambda) has all its composition labels in S. In the finite universal-extension construction of T(lambda), suppose this is still true, and let mu not belong to S. The projective sequence yields a surjection Hom(K_mu,X) onto Ext^1(Delta(mu),X). Every section Delta(nu) of K_mu satisfies mu<_s nu. Such nu cannot be a composition label of X, since nu<=_s lambda would imply mu<=_s lambda. Thus Hom(Delta(nu),X)=0: a nonzero image of Delta(nu) is a nonzero highest quotient and has L(nu) as its simple head, contradicting the absence of that composition label in X. Induction along the flag gives Hom(K_mu,X)=0. Therefore Ext^1(Delta(mu),X)=0.

Accordingly the existing finite universal-extension algorithm never adds a Weyl section with label outside S. When it adds Delta(mu) with mu in S, that section has all composition labels in S by sections 4-5, preserving the induction. The resulting tilting X and its unique indecomposable highest-label-lambda summand T(lambda) have Weyl labels in S. Summand closure and uniqueness are the existing proved local tilting results. This proves precisely the required strong-linkage supplier for Weyl sections of indecomposable tiltings.
