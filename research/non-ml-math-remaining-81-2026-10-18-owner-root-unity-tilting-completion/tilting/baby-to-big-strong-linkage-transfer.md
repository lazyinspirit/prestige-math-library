# An elementary baby-to-big strong-linkage transfer

This is a proposed proof mechanism, with the small-quantum prerequisites below explicitly required. It does not assert those prerequisites are already installed.

Let ≤_s denote the transitive relation generated by downward dot affine reflections: s_{α,mℓ}·η is a permitted next label when η+ρ has pairing with α greater than mℓ, for α positive. Labels in chains need not be dominant.

## Required small-quantum inputs

There must be a finite small quantum algebra with its P-graded module category, finite graded simple modules l(η) and baby Vermas Z(η), satisfying:

1. Each finite graded module has finite graded composition length. Its graded simples have unique one-dimensional top η and all other weights below η. The restriction of a big highest simple L(µ) contains l(µ) as a graded composition factor (the small cyclic module generated by its highest vector surjects onto l(µ)).
2. The PBW character is ch Z(η)=e^η ∏_{α>0}(1−e^{−ℓα})/(1−e^{−α}).
3. Baby strong linkage: a factor l(µ) of Z(η) implies µ≤_sη. This is the exact AJS sections 5.10 and 6.1–6.2 Borel-transition argument; the requisite rank-one kernel and twisted-Borel construction still need actual local proofs.

No Steinberg tensor product theorem or cohomological induction is needed for the transfer below.

## Every negative ℓ-root translation is strongly downward

For x=η+ρ and a simple root α put a=(x,α). Choose integer m with mℓ≤a<(m+1)ℓ and let r=a−mℓ. If 0<r<ℓ, first reflect across (z,α)=mℓ, subtracting rα. The resulting pairing is mℓ−r. Reflect next across (z,α)=(m−1)ℓ, subtracting (ℓ−r)α. Both coefficients are positive. Their composition sends x to x−ℓα. If r=0, reflect once across (z,α)=(m−1)ℓ, subtracting ℓα. Thus η−ℓα≤_sη for every integral η, with no assumption about its chamber. Iterating shows η−ℓβ≤_sη for every β∈Q+.

If λ is dominant, λ+ρ is strictly dominant. For every w∈W a reduced finite-reflection path from λ+ρ to w(λ+ρ) is downward: equivalently, construct w using simple reflections in a reduced word in reverse order; at each step the conjugate of that simple positive root under the preceding inverse element is positive, so its pairing with λ+ρ is positive. Hence w·λ≤_sλ. Combining gives w·λ−ℓβ≤_sλ.

## Completed character identity

Let P(β) be the Kostant partition count, defined by ∏_{α>0}(1−e^{−α})^{-1}=Σ_{β∈Q+}P(β)e^{−β}. The ordinary Weyl character formula and the baby PBW character imply

    ch Δ(λ)
      = Σ_{w∈W, β∈Q+} det(w) P(β) ch Z(w·λ−ℓβ).

Indeed summing the right side first over β multiplies the baby denominator factor ∏(1−e^{−ℓα}) by its inverse. The remaining numerator is Σ_w det(w)e^{w·λ}, and the remaining denominator is ∏(1−e^{−α}). Each weight coefficient in this calculation is a finite sum: all tops lie in λ−Q+, and a top can contribute at a fixed weight only if its root height below λ is bounded by that weight's height. For each bounded height there are finitely many β and w.

The character identity also holds after expansion into graded simple characters. Every Z top η contributes only simples whose highest labels are ≤η. Therefore at every bounded root height all sums are finite. Characters of graded simples are triangular with diagonal coefficient 1, so equality of weight coefficients determines equality of simple-character coefficients recursively by root height. This avoids applying an infinite exact-functor identity without proof.

## Transfer

If L(µ) is a composition factor of the big Δ(λ), restriction is exact and the highest small cyclic module inside L(µ) has l(µ) as a quotient. Thus l(µ) has positive multiplicity in the restriction of Δ(λ). Its coefficient in the displayed signed expansion is nonzero; consequently at least one baby Verma Z(η), η=w·λ−ℓβ, has l(µ) as a factor. Baby strong linkage gives µ≤_sη. The downward-translation argument gives η≤_sλ. Concatenation proves µ≤_sλ.

This supplies the big Weyl strong-linkage transfer once the three precise small-quantum inputs are locally established. The input remains substantial but is smaller than a quantum finite-Weyl determinant or a cohomological induction construction.
