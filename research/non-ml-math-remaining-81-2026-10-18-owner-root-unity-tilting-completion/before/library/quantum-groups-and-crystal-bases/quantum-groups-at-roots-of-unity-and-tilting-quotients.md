## Root-form conventions and proved rank-one scope

For a finite-type symmetrizable Cartan datum with symmetrizers $d_i$, put
$v_i=v^{d_i}$. The De Concini–Kac form is the subalgebra generated by the
ordinary root generators and torus over
$\mathbb C[v,v^{-1},(v_i-v_i^{-1})^{-1}]$. The Lusztig form uses divided root
powers and the toral quantum binomials over $\mathbb C[v,v^{-1}]$. A root
specialization $v\mapsto\zeta$ is made only where every inverted
$\zeta_i-\zeta_i^{-1}$ is nonzero. The small root-power quotient also depends
on the chosen toral lattice; the definition here uses the torus generated by
$K_i=K_{d_i h_i}$ and imposes $E_i^{\ell_i}=F_i^{\ell_i}=0$ and
$K_i^{\ell_i}=1$, where $\ell_i=\operatorname{ord}(\zeta_i^2)$. These are
definitions; they do not assert that the quotient ideal is a Hopf ideal or
that the quotient embeds in the Lusztig specialization.

The proved rank-one branch is narrower and explicit. For $\mathfrak{sl}_2$,
take a primitive odd root $\zeta$ of order $\ell>2$ and the coproduct
convention
$$\Delta(E)=E\otimes K^{-1}+1\otimes E,\qquad \Delta(F)=F\otimes1+K\otimes F.$$
Then $F^aK^bE^c$ ($a,c\ge0$, $b\in\mathbb Z$) is a basis of the specialized
De Concini–Kac algebra, and quotienting by $(E^\ell,F^\ell,K^\ell-1)$ gives
a Hopf algebra of dimension $\ell^3$. The rank-one generator map to the
Lusztig divided-power specialization is well-defined but kills the nonzero
element $E^\ell$; generic inclusion therefore does not imply injectivity
after specialization.

## Required higher-rank proof branches

### Quantum Frobenius and tilting structure

The intended higher-rank claim is that quantum Frobenius and the relevant
tilting-module structure hold only in a declared Cartan datum, root-order,
divisibility, parameter, and lattice regime. A complete supplier still must
state that regime and prove the Frobenius map and its kernel description,
construct the relevant Weyl and dual Weyl families, and prove the required
tilting closure and tensor-ideal claims. The local interface
[[def-root-of-unity-tilting-and-negligible-quotient-interface]] defines
filtrations and negligible morphism subspaces from those inputs; it asserts
none of those closure theorems. Generic-parameter semisimplicity is not a
root-of-unity premise and cannot be transferred to this branch.

### Verlinde semisimplification

Keep the Verlinde branch restricted to a complex simple simply-laced Lie
algebra, an odd integer $\ell>h$ (with $h$ its Coxeter number), and a chosen
parameter $\zeta$ for which $\operatorname{ord}(\zeta^2)=\ell$. The selected
root/weight lattice and pivotal trace convention must also be stated by the
supplier. The required proof must show that negligible morphisms form a
tensor ideal in the tilting category, that the quotient is finite semisimple,
and that its simple labels and fusion rules are the claimed alcove data.
Modularity requires a separate nondegeneracy proof for the induced braiding.
The books and papers consulted state or use parts of this construction but
refer its foundational quotient proof elsewhere; no such theorem is claimed
by the present carriers.

The small quantum group, the additive tilting category, its negligible
quotient, a fusion category, and a modular category are distinct objects.
No equivalence among them is asserted here.
