## Statement

Let $\ell>2$ be odd and let $\zeta\in\mathbb C$ be a primitive $\ell$-th
root of unity. In the rank-one De Concini–Kac specialization
$D_\zeta=U^{DK}_\zeta(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ of
[[def-root-of-unity-quantum-group-forms]], the ordered monomials
$$F^aK^bE^c\qquad(a,c\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\ b\in\mathbb Z)$$
form a $\mathbb C$-basis. With the local coproduct convention
$$\Delta(K)=K\otimes K,\quad \Delta(E)=E\otimes K^{-1}+1\otimes E,\quad \Delta(F)=F\otimes1+K\otimes F,$$
the two-sided ideal
$$J_\ell=(E^\ell,F^\ell,K^\ell-1)$$
is stable under the coproduct, counit $\varepsilon(K)=1$,
$\varepsilon(E)=\varepsilon(F)=0$, and antipode
$S(K)=K^{-1}$, $S(E)=-EK$, $S(F)=-K^{-1}F$. The quotient
$u_\zeta(\mathfrak{sl}_2)=D_\zeta/J_\ell$ is a Hopf algebra with basis
$$\{\overline F^{\,a}\overline K^{\,b}\overline E^{\,c}:0\le a,b,c<\ell\},$$
and therefore has dimension $\ell^3$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The rank-one root forms of
[[def-root-of-unity-quantum-group-forms]], a primitive odd root $\zeta$ of
order $\ell>2$, and the Hopf-algebra axioms of
[[def-hh-hopf-algebra-and-antipode]].

[F1] The rank-one De Concini–Kac algebra is generated by $E,F,K^{\pm1}$
with relations
$KEK^{-1}=\zeta^2E$, $KFK^{-1}=\zeta^{-2}F$, and
$EF-FE=H$, where $H=(K-K^{-1})/(\zeta-\zeta^{-1})$
([[def-drinfeld-jimbo-quantized-enveloping-algebra]],
[[def-root-of-unity-quantum-group-forms]]).

[F2] Since $\zeta$ is primitive of odd order $\ell>2$,
$\zeta-\zeta^{-1}\ne0$ and $\zeta^2$ is primitive of order $\ell$
([[def-root-of-unity-quantum-group-forms]]).

[F3] A Hopf algebra is a bialgebra with a two-sided convolution inverse
$S$ of its identity map; equivalently, the two antipode equations hold
([[def-hh-hopf-algebra-and-antipode]]).

[F4] In the generic rank-one algebra generated by $E,F,K^{\pm1}$, the
ordered monomials $F^aK^bE^c$ span and are linearly independent
([[thm-quantized-sl-two-string-formulas]], clause (i)).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** prove spanning by a terminating rewriting system, use the choice-free generic rank-one basis for independence, verify the specialized Hopf maps on the presentation, and then descend them to the root-power quotient.

1.1 Work first over $R=\mathbb C[v,v^{-1},(v-v^{-1})^{-1}]$ with $H_v=(K-K^{-1})/(v-v^{-1})$. Combine adjacent powers of $K$ as elements $K^b$ of the group algebra $R[K,K^{-1}]$, and rewrite $K^bF\to v^{-2b}FK^b$, $EK^b\to v^{-2b}K^bE$, and $EF\to FE+H_v$. Count inversions in the order $F<K<E$, with a second measure equal to the number of $E$ and $F$ symbols. The first two rules decrease the inversion count; the $FE$ term of the third decreases it and its $H_v$ term lowers the second measure. Thus every word reduces after finitely many steps to an $R$-linear combination of the ordered monomials $F^aK^bE^c$. The same rules remain valid after $v=\zeta$ by [F2]. [given, F1, F2, construct, algebra]

1.2 Define a unital algebra-map candidate $\Delta$ and a counit candidate $\varepsilon$ by the displayed formulas, with $\Delta(1)=1\otimes1$ and $\varepsilon(1)=1$; define an anti-algebra-map candidate $S$ by the displayed formulas, with $S(1)=1$. For toral conjugation, direct multiplication gives $\Delta(K)\Delta(E)\Delta(K^{-1})=\zeta^2\Delta(E)$ and $\Delta(K)\Delta(F)\Delta(K^{-1})=\zeta^{-2}\Delta(F)$. For the mixed relation, expand the coproduct commutator: the two cross terms cancel since $EK=\zeta^{-2}KE$ and $K^{-1}F=\zeta^2FK^{-1}$, leaving $[E,F]\otimes K^{-1}+K\otimes[E,F]=H\otimes K^{-1}+K\otimes H=\Delta(H)$. The counit assignments preserve each relation. For the antipode assignment, applying the anti-map to the two toral relations gives $-KE+\zeta^2EK=0$ and $-FK^{-1}+\zeta^{-2}K^{-1}F=0$. Applying it to $EF-FE-H$ gives $K^{-1}FEK-EF+H=FE-EF+H=0$. Thus $\Delta,\varepsilon,S$ descend to $D_\zeta$. [given, F1, F2, algebra]

1.3 For $n\ge1$, telescoping gives $E^nF-FE^n=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}E^j[E,F]E^{n-1-j}$; moving $K^{\pm1}$ through $E^j$ by $E^jK=\zeta^{-2j}KE^j$ and $E^jK^{-1}=\zeta^{2j}K^{-1}E^j$ gives $E^nF-FE^n=\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\zeta^{-2j}K-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\zeta^{2j}K^{-1}}{\zeta-\zeta^{-1}}E^{n-1}$. At $n=\ell$ both geometric sums vanish because for $t=\zeta^{\pm2}$ one has $t\ne1$, $t^\ell=1$, and $\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}t^j=(1-t^\ell)/(1-t)=0$ by [F2]; hence $E^\ell$ commutes with $F$ and it commutes with $E,K$ as well. Similarly, $F^nE-EF^n=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}F^j[F,E]F^{n-1-j}$; using $F^jK=\zeta^{2j}KF^j$ and $F^jK^{-1}=\zeta^{-2j}K^{-1}F^j$ gives $F^nE-EF^n=-\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\zeta^{2j}K-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\zeta^{-2j}K^{-1}}{\zeta-\zeta^{-1}}F^{n-1}$, whose sums also vanish at $n=\ell$, so $F^\ell$ commutes with $E$ and already commutes with $F,K$. Finally $K^\ell$ commutes with $E,F$ since $\zeta^{2\ell}=1$. Thus $E^\ell,F^\ell,K^\ell-1$ are central and $J_\ell$ is a two-sided ideal. [given, F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 The map from the rank-one algebra over $R$ presented by the three relations in [F1] with $v$ in place of $\zeta$ onto the $R$-subalgebra generated by $E,F,K^{\pm1}$ is surjective by construction, and step 1.1 shows that its source is spanned by $F^aK^bE^c$. Their images are linearly independent in the generic algebra by [F4]; hence the map is injective and the $R$-source is free with that basis. Tensoring this free module along $v\mapsto\zeta$ preserves the basis. Thus $D_\zeta$ has the stated rank-one presentation and PBW basis. [step 1.1, F1, F4, F2, algebra]

2.2 On $K$ the two iterated coproducts are $K\otimes K\otimes K$. On $E$ they both equal $E\otimes K^{-1}\otimes K^{-1}+1\otimes E\otimes K^{-1}+1\otimes1\otimes E$, and on $F$ they both equal $F\otimes1\otimes1+K\otimes F\otimes1+K\otimes K\otimes F$; hence coassociativity holds on generators. The two counit composites give $K,E,F$ on each generator: for $E$ they are $0+E$ and $E+0$, and for $F$ they are $0+F$ and $F+0$, while $K$ is sent to $1\cdot K$ and $K\cdot1$. Both counit composites and both iterated coproducts are algebra maps, so these identities hold on all of $D_\zeta$. For the antipode equations, on $K$ they are $K^{-1}K=1=KK^{-1}$; on $E$ they are $S(E)K^{-1}+E=0$ and $EK+S(E)=0$; on $F$ they are $S(F)+K^{-1}F=0$ and $F+KS(F)=0$. Since $S(1)=1$, both equations also hold on the unit. If both equations hold for $x$ and $y$, then anti-multiplicativity of $S$ and multiplicativity of $\Delta$ give $\sum S(x_{(1)}y_{(1)})x_{(2)}y_{(2)}=\varepsilon(x)\varepsilon(y)1$ and $\sum x_{(1)}y_{(1)}S(x_{(2)}y_{(2)})=\varepsilon(x)\varepsilon(y)1$. Induction on word length proves the antipode equations on all of $D_\zeta$, so it is a Hopf algebra by [F3]. [step 1.2, F1, F3, algebra]

3.1 If $AB=tBA$, induction starts at $n=0$ with $(A+B)^0=1$ and $C_{0,0}=1$ and gives $(A+B)^n=\sum_{r=0}^n C_{n,r}B^{n-r}A^r$, where $C_{n,0}=C_{n,n}=1$ and, for $0<r<n$, $C_{n,r}=\prod_{j=1}^r(1-t^{n-r+j})/(1-t^j)$. Multiplying the expansion on the right by $A+B$ shows the coefficient recurrence $C_{n+1,r}=C_{n,r-1}+t^rC_{n,r}$. For the product formula, putting those two terms over $\prod_{j=1}^r(1-t^j)$ leaves the common numerator $\prod_{k=n-r+2}^n(1-t^k)$ times $(1-t^r)+t^r(1-t^{n-r+1})=1-t^{n+1}$, exactly the product formula for $C_{n+1,r}$. Thus the formula follows inductively whenever its denominators are nonzero. For $t=\zeta^{2}$ or $t=\zeta^{-2}$ and $0<r<\ell$, the denominator is nonzero and the numerator contains $1-t^\ell=0$, so all intermediate coefficients vanish. In $\Delta(E)$ the summands $A=E\otimes K^{-1}$ and $B=1\otimes E$ satisfy $AB=\zeta^{-2}BA$; in $\Delta(F)$ the summands $A=F\otimes1$ and $B=K\otimes F$ satisfy $AB=\zeta^{2}BA$. Applying the formula to these two pairs gives $\Delta(E^\ell)=E^\ell\otimes K^{-\ell}+1\otimes E^\ell$ and $\Delta(F^\ell)=F^\ell\otimes1+K^\ell\otimes F^\ell$. Also $\Delta(K^\ell-1)=(K^\ell-1)\otimes K^\ell+1\otimes(K^\ell-1)$. The counit kills all three generators. Since $\ell$ is odd, $(EK)^\ell=\zeta^{-\ell(\ell-1)}E^\ell K^\ell$ and $(K^{-1}F)^\ell=\zeta^{\ell(\ell-1)}F^\ell K^{-\ell}$, with $\zeta^{\ell(\ell-1)}=1$, we have $S(E^\ell)=-E^\ell K^\ell$ and $S(F^\ell)=-K^{-\ell}F^\ell$, both in $J_\ell$; also $S(K^\ell-1)=K^{-\ell}-1=-K^{-\ell}(K^\ell-1)\in J_\ell$. The sum $J_\ell\otimes D_\zeta+D_\zeta\otimes J_\ell$ is a two-sided ideal of $D_\zeta\otimes D_\zeta$, so the coproduct inclusions for the three generators extend to all of $J_\ell$ because $\Delta$ is multiplicative. The counit kills the ideal because it kills its generators, and the anti-multiplicative antipode preserves the ideal because it sends each generator into $J_\ell$. Therefore the quotient operations are well-defined and satisfy the Hopf identities inherited from $D_\zeta$. [step 1.2, step 2.2, step 1.3, F2, algebra]

4.1 By the PBW basis in step 2.1 and centrality in step 1.3, the ideal generated by $E^\ell,F^\ell$ is spanned by monomials with $c\ge\ell$ or $a\ge\ell$, leaving a basis indexed by $0\le a,c<\ell$ and $b\in\mathbb Z$. Modulo $K^\ell-1$ one has $K^{-1}=K^{\ell-1}$, so each toral coefficient is represented by a polynomial in $K$; division by the monic polynomial $K^\ell-1$ gives a unique remainder of degree less than $\ell$. Thus the quotient basis is exactly $F^aK^bE^c$ with $0\le a,b,c<\ell$. There are $\ell^3$ such elements, so the quotient has dimension $\ell^3$. [step 2.1, step 1.3, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
