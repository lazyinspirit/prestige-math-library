This is a proposed local proof, not a gate record or an acceptance claim. No canonical item is changed. The original higher-rank claim is retained. The proof uses the ordinary good-order finite-Cartan regime, not a restriction to rank one.

Fix a finite-type Cartan matrix A, with its standard positive integral symmetrizers d_i chosen componentwise with gcd 1. Let P be any semisimple isogeny lattice between the root lattice Qroot and the full weight lattice Pfull, and let P∨=Hom(P,Z) be its dual, containing the simple coroots h_i. Use K_i=K_{d_i h_i}, exactly the existing Drinfeld–Jimbo convention. This retains every semisimple root-datum isogeny type; a central extra torus would require a separately declared target convention. Let ζ have primitive odd order ℓ>1, with gcd(ℓ,d_i)=1 for every i. Thus ζ_i=ζ^{d_i} also has order ℓ, and ord(ζ_i²)=ℓ. In particular a G2 component requires 3∤ℓ. All off-diagonal entries satisfy −a_ij<ℓ: in simply laced type the bound is 1; in B,C,F4 it is 2 while ℓ≥3; in G2 it is 3 while the odd coprime-to-3 order is at least 5. Reducible finite type is allowed. No claim is made here for even order, bad 3-G2 order, or the altered Langlands-dual root datum appearing in more general Frobenius statements.

The proposed theorem is a surjective Hopf map

Fr: U^L_ζ → U(g),

Fr(E_i^(n))=e_i^(n/ℓ) if ℓ|n, zero otherwise;
Fr(F_i^(n))=f_i^(n/ℓ) if ℓ|n, zero otherwise;
Fr(K_h)=1 for every h∈P∨.

Its **algebra kernel** is the two-sided ideal generated by all E_i,F_i,K_h−1. It is not the small quantum group itself. The Hopf-kernel/coinvariant statement is a different assertion and needs a separate PBW argument; do not silently identify these two notions of kernel. The proof below establishes the algebra kernel and that it is a Hopf ideal.

## 1. Quantum Lucas, including negative upper index

For an indeterminate Q define G(N,r;Q) by

P_N(t)=Σ_{r≥0} Q^{r(r−1)/2}G(N,r;Q)t^r,

where P_N(t)=∏_{j=0}^{N−1}(1+Q^j t) for N≥0 and P_N(t)=∏_{j=N}^{−1}(1+Q^j t)^(−1) for N<0, expanded as a formal power series. The q-Pascal recurrence obtained by multiplying/dividing the last factor proves that this agrees with the usual Gaussian product for nonnegative N, and with its generalized product for negative N. In particular each coefficient is a Laurent polynomial in Q. This also follows for N<0 directly from a finite product of geometric power series, whose coefficient of a given degree uses finitely many terms.

Let z be a primitive odd ℓth root and put Q=z². For N=ℓN1+N0, 0≤N0<ℓ, periodicity of the factors and

∏_{j=0}^{ℓ−1}(1+Q^j t)=1+t^ℓ

give P_N(t)=(1+t^ℓ)^{N1}P_N0(t), including N1<0 by formal inversion. For r=ℓr1+r0, 0≤r0<ℓ, coefficient comparison gives

G(N,r;Q)=binom(N1,r1)G(N0,r0;Q).

The factors Q^{r(r−1)/2} and Q^{r0(r0−1)/2} are equal, since their exponents differ by a multiple of ℓ (ℓ is odd). Converting to symmetric quantum binomials by [N choose r]_z=z^{−r(N−r)}G(N,r;z²) introduces no extra factor: the exponent difference from r0(N0−r0) is also divisible by ℓ. Hence

[N choose r]_z = binom(N1,r1)[N0 choose r0]_z. (QL)

This includes every integer N and nonnegative r, with generalized ordinary binomial coefficients for N1<0. In particular [ℓm+c choose t]_z is the polynomial binom(m+c1,t1)[c0 choose t0]_z for c=ℓc1+c0 and t=ℓt1+t0 with residues in [0,ℓ−1].

For a single divided generator X^(n), the existing multiplication identity and (QL) imply

X^(aℓ+r)=(X^(ℓ))^a X^r/(a![r]_z!)   (0≤r<ℓ).

Indeed [aℓ+r choose r]_z=1 and [aℓ+ℓ choose ℓ]_z=a+1. All [r]_z! with r<ℓ are nonzero. Thus after killing X every nonmultiple divided power vanishes, while X^(aℓ)=(X^(ℓ))^a/a!. This proves an actual generation statement for each specialized divided half, not merely a prescription for a map.

## 2. Higher divided Serre without a presentation assumption

Work generically in the existing positive half. Fix i≠j, write z=q_i and a=−a_ij. On a homogeneous element x of i-weight w=〈h_i,deg x〉 set δ(x)=E_i x−z^w xE_i. Direct multiplication gives the skew Leibniz rule

δ(xy)=δ(x)y+z^{wt(x)}xδ(y).

Induction gives

δ^m(x)=Σ_{r=0}^m (−1)^r z^{rw+r(r−1)}G(m,r;z²) E_i^{m−r}xE_i^r.

For the induction, apply δ to each term; its total i-weight is w+2m, so the two resulting coefficients combine by the q-Pascal recurrence. Substituting G(m,r;z²)=z^{r(m−r)}[m choose r]_z gives exponent r(w+m−1).

The ordinary quantum Serre relation is exactly δ^{a+1}(E_j)=0, up to the nonzero scalar (−1)^{a+1} and reversal of r; the factor exponent w+m−1=−a+a+1−1 is zero at this value. Applying δ repeatedly to a product of n copies of E_j, the skew Leibniz rule expands δ^m(E_j^n) into a finite sum of products δ^{r1}(E_j)…δ^{rn}(E_j), where Σr_k=m. If m>an, some r_k>a, and that product is zero. Dividing by the generically nonzero [m]_i![n]_j!, and setting p=m−r in the expansion, proves

Σ_{p+s=m}(−1)^p z^{p(an−m+1)} E_i^(p)E_j^(n)E_i^(s)=0   whenever m>an. (HS)

The same identity holds for negative generators, by replacing E with F in their defining Serre relation. Reversal or bar gives the variant with the opposite exponent if needed. All summands of (HS) lie in the integral divided-power subalgebra. Therefore the identity remains true in its specialization: no division by a vanishing specialized factorial is being performed.

Two consequences are crucial. First in any module on which every small generator E_j kills a vector v, (HS) with n=1,m=ℓ>a implies E_j E_i^(ℓ)v=0: the p=0 term is this expression; p=ℓ has rightmost E_jv=0; all intermediate terms have a rightmost E_i^(s) with 0<s<ℓ and kill v by (QL). The analogous negative statement holds. Second, in a quotient killing all small generators, apply (HS) with n=ℓ,m=(a+1)ℓ. Only p,s divisible by ℓ remain; z^{p(aℓ−(a+1)ℓ+1)}=1 and (−1)^p=(−1)^{p/ℓ}. Using (QL), the remaining relation is precisely the ordinary divided Serre relation on e_i=E_i^(ℓ), e_j=E_j^(ℓ). This directly proves ordinary Serre in the quotient, without claiming that the divided algebra has only ordinary Serre as its defining relations.

## 3. The specialized torus modulo K_h−1

Fix i and write T_t=T_{i,0,t}, K=K_i, z=ζ_i. The existing integral recurrence

(z^t−z^(−t))T_t = T_(t−1)(Kz^(1−t)−K^(−1)z^(t−1))

shows, after K=1, that T_(aℓ+r)=0 for 1≤r<ℓ: at r=1 the numerator factor is zero while the denominator is nonzero, and the remaining residues follow successively. The toral basis supplier shows that all shifted T_c,t are spanned by K powers times the T_s, so only T_(aℓ) survive after every K_h=1.

Its product identity specialized at r=ℓ,s=aℓ has only j=0 and j=ℓ remaining, by (QL). Since z^(ℓ·aℓ)=1 and K=1, it yields

H T_(aℓ)=(a+1)T_((a+1)ℓ)+aT_(aℓ), H=T_ℓ.

Starting with T_0=1, induction gives T_(aℓ)=binom(H,a). Thus the torus quotient is spanned by polynomials in the commuting H_i. It has no additional polynomial relation: evaluate the integral torus at K_h=q^{〈ℓμ,h〉}, then specialize q=ζ. These homomorphisms descend through K_h−1 and send H_i to 〈μ,h_i〉. As μ runs through P these coordinates form a finite-index sublattice of Z^I, containing NZ^I for some positive integer N. A polynomial vanishing on that integer grid is zero, by induction on the finite number of variables and the one-variable root bound. Hence the torus quotient is exactly C[H_i:i∈I]. No flatness inference is made here.

The same evaluations and (QL) prove the exact formula in this quotient

T_(i,c,t)=binom(H_i+c1,t1)[c0 choose t0]_ζi. (TQ)

Both sides are polynomials in H_i by the spanning result, and evaluation on the integer grid proves equality, including c<0. In particular T_(i,−ℓa_ij,ℓ)=H_i−a_ij.

## 4. Ordinary Chevalley presentation maps onto the actual quotient

Let J be the two-sided ideal in Uζ^L generated by E_i,F_i and K_h−1, and put Q=Uζ^L/J. No presentation for Uζ^L beyond its actual integral-subalgebra construction is being assumed. By (QL) every divided power in Q is either zero or an ordinary divided power of e_i=E_i^(ℓ),f_i=F_i^(ℓ). By §3 every toral generator is a polynomial in H_i=T_i0ℓ. Thus e_i,f_i,H_i generate Q.

The existing mixed reordering at exponents ℓ,ℓ has only t=0 and t=ℓ surviving: for 0<t<ℓ, both remaining divided factors have small nonzero residue and vanish. It therefore gives [e_i,f_i]=H_i. For i≠j the mixed generators commute already generically. The toral commutation formula, iterated to an ℓth divided power and followed by (TQ), gives e_j H_i=(H_i−a_ij)e_j and f_j H_i=(H_i+a_ij)f_j. Thus [H_i,e_j]=a_ij e_j and [H_i,f_j]=−a_ij f_j. The H_i commute. The ordinary Serre relations are the second consequence of (HS). Hence the classical Serre presentation gives a surjective homomorphism ψ:U(g)→Q.

The needed new issue is injectivity; checking these relations alone would not prove it.

## 5. Every classical highest-weight simple factors through Q

Use the actual finite free U_A-stable highest-weight lattice of highest weight ℓμ, for μ dominant integral. Its specialization is finite-dimensional, has a nonzero one-dimensional top weight, is generated by its top vector, and retains its actual integral weights and toral Gaussian characters. These are precisely the existing integral highest-weight supplier facts; this step must cite them with their inherited AC rather than infer specialization facts from generic irreducibility.

Take a simple quotient M of this finite-dimensional cyclic highest-weight module. It still has a nonzero top vector v and its top weight ℓμ. Toral characters separate its finite list of weights: K_i records the residue coordinate and T_i0ℓ records the quotient coordinate by (QL). Therefore every toral-stable submodule splits into its actual weight spaces, using finite polynomial spectral projectors, and the quotient inherits the integral weight labels and toral evaluation.

Every E_i^(r),r>0 kills v. Also F_i v=0. To prove the latter, the complete mixed reordering gives E_j^(r)F_i v=0 for every r>0,j: if j≠i commute; if j=i and r=1 its contraction coefficient is [ℓ〈μ,h_i〉]_ζi=0; if r>1 the contraction term contains E_i^(r−1)v=0. Thus F_i v is a highest-weight vector of weight ℓμ−α_i. If it were nonzero, triangular spanning would make the submodule it generates a nonzero proper submodule, because its weights are at most ℓμ−α_i and cannot contain the top. This contradicts simplicity. Consequently all small E_i,F_i kill v.

Let N⊂M be the subspace annihilated by all E_i,F_i. It contains v. It is stable under the torus because M is a sum of actual toral weight spaces and conjugation/shift identities preserve each small-annihilation condition. It is stable under all E_i^(ℓ),F_i^(ℓ): same-sign adjacent-color stability is the first consequence of (HS); same-color same-sign stability is commutativity; opposite colors commute; and for opposite signs of the same color, the rank-one mixed formula has an error factor of degree ℓ−1, which kills N after the toral coefficient acts diagonally. Thus (QL) shows N is stable under every divided-power generator and hence is a Uζ^L-submodule. Simplicity implies N=M.

Since M is cyclic from its top and small generators vanish, its weights differ from ℓμ by multiples of ℓ times roots. Thus every K_h acts as 1, so J kills M. The resulting Q-module, restricted along ψ, is a finite-dimensional simple classical g-module, generated by a highest-weight vector of weight μ: (TQ) sends H_i on v to 〈μ,h_i〉. It is therefore the ordinary classical simple L(μ). This proves that every classical dominant-integral simple whose highest weight belongs to the chosen lattice P occurs as a ψ-pullback. No claim that all specialized root Weyl modules are simple has been used.

## 6. Finite-dimensional classical modules separate U(g)

Here is a local proof of the separation needed for injectivity. For semisimple g, the adjoint representation embeds g into gl(V), V=g. Let P be the polynomial algebra on End(V), with matrix coordinates x_ab. Use the right-multiplication vector fields D_X, defined on matrix coordinates by D_X(M)=M·ad(X). Then [D_X,D_Y](M)=M[ad(X),ad(Y)], so X↦D_X is a Lie homomorphism and extends to U(g). At the identity matrix, D_X has tangent value ad(X), so the map g→T_Id End(V) is injective. The PBW filtration identifies the leading symbol of a nonzero u∈U(g), of degree m, with a nonzero element of Sym^m g. The differential operator D_u has order at most m and at Id has principal symbol the image of that symmetric tensor under the injective Sym^m(ad). It is nonzero. Choose a homogeneous degree-m polynomial in the shifted coordinates x_ab−δ_ab paired nontrivially with that symbol. All its derivatives of order less than m vanish at Id, so (D_u p)(Id)≠0. Thus u acts nontrivially on P.

Each subspace of polynomials of total degree ≤d is finite-dimensional and stable under the linear vector fields D_X. Finite-dimensional complete reducibility for semisimple g decomposes it into classical L(μ), μ dominant integral. The polynomial representation has all weights in Qroot, because the adjoint representation, its dual and their tensor/symmetric powers have this property; hence every such μ is in Qroot⊂P. Thus these polynomial pieces use only simples available from §5, for every chosen isogeny lattice. Hence if u killed all those L(μ), it would kill every finite-degree polynomial subspace and therefore all of P, contrary to the preceding paragraph. The intersection of their annihilators is zero, even if only μ in the root lattice are used. For g=0 this says C acts faithfully on its one-dimensional module.

By §5, ker ψ kills every L(μ); hence ker ψ=0. Therefore ψ is an isomorphism U(g)≅Q. The quotient map followed by ψ^(−1) is the claimed Frobenius map, with exact kernel J. Its explicit divided-power formulas follow from (QL), and its toral formulas are (TQ). Uniqueness follows because all the original integral generators have now been assigned explicit images.

## 7. Hopf compatibility in the local coproduct convention

The local convention is Δ(E_i)=E_i⊗K_i^(−1)+1⊗E_i and Δ(F_i)=F_i⊗1+K_i⊗F_i. The q-binomial theorem gives

Δ(E_i^(n))=Σ_(r+s=n) q_i^(rs) E_i^(r)⊗K_i^(−r)E_i^(s),
Δ(F_i^(n))=Σ_(r+s=n) q_i^(−rs) F_i^(r)K_i^s⊗F_i^(s).

These are integral expressions. Their derivation uses AB=q_i^(−2)BA for A=E_i⊗K_i^(−1),B=1⊗E_i and CD=q_i²DC for C=F_i⊗1,D=K_i⊗F_i, followed by symmetric normalization of the Gaussian coefficient. The antipode formulas are S(E_i^(n))=(−1)^n q_i^(n(n−1))E_i^(n)K_i^n and S(F_i^(n))=(−1)^n q_i^(−n(n−1))K_i^(−n)F_i^(n), obtained by moving the toral factors in the nth power of −E_iK_i and −K_i^(−1)F_i. Counits are zero for n>0.

Toral generators are already generated integrally by the divided powers and K_h: induct on t in the complete reordering of E_i^(t)F_i^(t), isolating its final term T_i0t; all other toral coefficients have smaller last index, and the exact shift recurrence expresses every T_i,c,t of that smaller index through the unshifted ones. Thus the displayed integral coproduct and antipode formulas prove stability of the entire actual U_A, and the generic Hopf identities restrict and specialize. No separate unproved toral Hopf formula is needed.

The ideal J is Hopf: Δ(E_i),Δ(F_i) lie in J⊗U+U⊗J, Δ(K_h−1)=(K_h−1)⊗K_h+1⊗(K_h−1), and the counit/antipode preserve its displayed generators. In Q the interior terms of Δ(E_i^(ℓ)) and Δ(F_i^(ℓ)) vanish, so e_i and f_i are primitive. Their commutator H_i is then primitive as well. Therefore ψ:U(g)→Q and its inverse preserve the classical Hopf structure on its generators; Fr is a Hopf map in the library convention.
