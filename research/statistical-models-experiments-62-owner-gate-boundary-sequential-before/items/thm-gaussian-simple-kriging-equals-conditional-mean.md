## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $X$ be a Gaussian real field, meaning every
finite coordinate vector has a possibly singular multivariate-normal law.
For the target $Y=X(s_0)$ and observations $Z$ of
[[def-simple-ordinary-and-universal-kriging]], suppose $\Sigma$ is positive
definite. Then the simple-kriging predictor
$m_0+(\Sigma^{-1}c_0)^\top(Z-m)$ equals
$\mathbb E[Y\mid\sigma(Z)]$ almost surely, where $\sigma(Z)$ is the
sigma-algebra generated by the observation vector.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a finite jointly Gaussian vector $(Y,Z)$ with finite second moments, and positive-definite observation covariance $\Sigma$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice says every family of nonempty sets has a choice function. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] [[def-gaussian-process]]: under AC, every finite coordinate vector of a Gaussian field has a possibly singular multivariate-normal law.

[F2] [[lem-uncorrelated-jointly-gaussian-blocks-are-independent]]: under AC, jointly Gaussian finite blocks with zero cross-covariance are independent, including when a covariance matrix is singular.

[F3] [[def-independent-random-elements]] and [[def-independent-sigma-algebras-and-events]]: independent random elements generate independent sigma-algebras, so their measurable events factor.

[F4] [[thm-kriging-systems-and-l2-optimality]]: under $\Sigma\succ0$, the simple-kriging weights are $\Sigma^{-1}c_0$.

[F5] [[lem-conditioning-a-known-variable-and-an-independent-variable]]: under AC, an integrable variable whose Borel events are independent of the conditioning sigma-algebra has conditional expectation equal to its mean.

[F6] [[def-conditional-expectation-as-an-ae-class]] and [[def-conditional-expectation-given-a-sigma-algebra]]: the conditional-expectation class consists of measurable integrable versions with the same integrals as the input on every event in the conditioning sigma-algebra.

[F7] [[lem-conditional-expectation-is-unique-almost-surely]]: two versions with the same conditioning sigma-algebra and event integrals agree almost surely.

## Proof

**Given:** AC, the Gaussian field, and the target and observation vector as in the statement.

1.1 Let $\lambda=\Sigma^{-1}c_0$ and $R=Y-m_0-\lambda^\top(Z-m)$. By [F1], $(R,Z)$ is jointly Gaussian because each of its linear combinations is a linear combination of $(Y,Z)$. Its residual mean is zero, and $\operatorname{Cov}(R,Z)=c_0^\top-\lambda^\top\Sigma=0$ by the equation $\Sigma\lambda=c_0$. [A1, F1, F4, given, algebra]

2.1 Applying [F2] to the jointly Gaussian blocks $R$ and $Z$ from step 1.1 shows that $R$ is independent of $Z$. [A1, F2, step 1.1]

3.1 By [F3], independence from step 2.1 gives the Borel-event factorization required by [F5] for $R$ and $\mathcal G=\sigma(Z)$. The residual is integrable because it is a finite linear combination of square-integrable variables. AC is stated in [A1], so [F5] gives $\mathbb E[R\mid\mathcal G]=\mathbb E[R]=0$; by [F6], this means $\mathbb E[R\mathbf 1_B]=0$ for every $B\in\mathcal G$. [A1, F3, F5, F6, step 2.1]

4.1 Put $\widehat Y=m_0+\lambda^\top(Z-m)$. This is $\sigma(Z)$-measurable and integrable. For each $B\in\sigma(Z)$, step 3.1 and $Y=\widehat Y+R$ give $\mathbb E[Y\mathbf 1_B]=\mathbb E[\widehat Y\mathbf 1_B]$. Thus [F6] makes $\widehat Y$ a version of $\mathbb E[Y\mid\sigma(Z)]$, and [F7] gives the asserted almost-sure equality. AC is used only through the Gaussian-law, Gaussian-independence, and conditional-expectation suppliers. [F6, F7, step 3.1] ∎
