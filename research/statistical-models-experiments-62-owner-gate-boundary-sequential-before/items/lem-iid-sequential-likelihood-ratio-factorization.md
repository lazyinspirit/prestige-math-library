## Statement

Assume AC. Let $P_0,P_1$ be probability measures on a common measurable space and let $(X_i)_{i\ge1}$ be random elements of $(S,\Sigma)$ that are IID under each $P_j$, with one-coordinate law $Q_j$. Suppose $Q_1\ll Q_0$, let $\lambda=dQ_1/dQ_0$ be a nonnegative representative, and put $\mathcal F_t=\sigma(X_1,\ldots,X_t)$. For every $t\ge1$,
$$L_t:=\prod_{i=1}^t\lambda(X_i)$$
is a version of $d(P_1|_{\mathcal F_t})/d(P_0|_{\mathcal F_t})$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Assume AC. Let $P_0,P_1$ be probability measures on $(\Omega,\mathcal F)$; under each $P_j$, let $(X_i)_{i\ge1}$ be IID with common law $Q_j$ on $(S,\Sigma)$. Assume $Q_1\ll Q_0$ and let $\lambda$ represent $dQ_1/dQ_0$.

[A1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: every family of nonempty sets has a choice function; AC is the hypothesis used by the supplied Radon–Nikodym likelihood-ratio definition.

[F1] [[def-identically-distributed-and-iid-random-variables]] and [[def-independent-random-elements]]: IID means mutual independence of the coordinate random elements and a common one-coordinate law.

[F2] [[thm-rectangle-criterion-for-independent-random-elements]]: for every finite list of distinct coordinates and measurable coordinate sets, the joint rectangle probability is the product of the marginal probabilities.

[F3] [[def-measurable-rectangle]] and [[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]]: finite products use the sigma-algebra generated by all-coordinate measurable rectangles.

[F4] [[thm-sigma-finite-product-measure-exists-is-rectangular-and-is-unique]]: products of probability measures exist, have the rectangle product formula, and are unique with that formula.

[F5] [[def-law-or-distribution-of-a-random-element]] and [[lem-law-of-a-random-element-is-a-probability-measure]]: the law of a random element is its pushforward probability measure.

[F6] [[def-pi-system]] and [[thm-measure-uniqueness-on-a-sigma-finite-pi-system]]: a generating pi-system with a common finite-measure exhaustion determines two measures.

[F7] [[def-likelihood-ratio-as-rn-derivative]]: under AC, $\lambda$ is a nonnegative measurable representative of the Radon–Nikodym density class.

[F8] [[thm-likelihood-ratio-change-of-measure]]: if $Q_1\ll Q_0$, then $\int g\lambda\,dQ_0=\int g\,dQ_1$ for every nonnegative measurable $g$.

[F9] [[thm-indefinite-integral-of-a-nonnegative-function-is-a-measure]]: $B\mapsto\int_B h\,d\mu$ is a measure for nonnegative measurable $h$.

[F10] [[thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces]]: nonnegative product integrals can be evaluated as iterated integrals.

[F11] [[thm-monotone-convergence-for-the-integral]]: integrals of increasing nonnegative measurable functions converge to the integral of their pointwise limit.

[F12] [[cor-continuous-functions-are-borel-measurable]]: under countable choice, a continuous map between finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces is Borel measurable; AC implies this choice principle.

[F13] [[thm-composition-with-borel-functions-preserves-measurability]]: the composition of measurable maps is measurable.

## Proof

1.1 By [F8] with $g=1$, $\int\lambda\,dQ_0=1$, so $N=\{\lambda=\infty\}$ is $Q_0$-null and hence $Q_1$-null. Set $\lambda=0$ on $N$; this preserves its density class and gives a finite nonnegative representative. Now fix $t\ge1$ and $j\in\{0,1\}$. The vector $Y_t=(X_1,\ldots,X_t)$ is measurable into the finite product sigma-algebra because preimages of all-coordinate rectangles are finite intersections of coordinate events, and those rectangles generate that sigma-algebra by [F3]. Its law $\mu_{j,t}$ is a probability measure by [F5]. On every rectangle $R=\prod_{i=1}^tB_i$, [F1] and [F2] give $\mu_{j,t}(R)=\prod_iQ_j(B_i)$. Repeated application of [F4] constructs $Q_j^{\otimes t}$ with those same rectangle masses. The all-coordinate rectangles form a pi-system by [F3] and [F6], generate the product sigma-algebra, and have a finite exhaustion consisting of the whole space, whose mass is one for both measures. Hence [F6] gives $\mu_{j,t}=Q_j^{\otimes t}$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F8]

2.1 Define $\Lambda_t(x_1,\ldots,x_t)=(\lambda(x_1),\ldots,\lambda(x_t))$. Preimages of product rectangles are finite intersections of measurable coordinate preimages, so [F3] makes $\Lambda_t$ measurable. The finite multiplication map $m_t:\mathbb R^t\to\mathbb R$, $m_t(y)=\prod_{i=1}^ty_i$, is continuous: the two-coordinate multiplication map is continuous by $|ab-a'b'|\le |a||b-b'|+|b'||a-a'|$, and finite products follow by induction. Thus [F12] makes $m_t$ Borel and [F13] makes $h_t=m_t\circ\Lambda_t$ measurable. By [F9], $\nu_t(B):=\int_Bh_t\,dQ_0^{\otimes t}$ is a measure. For each rectangle $R=\prod_iB_i$, repeated Tonelli integration, justified by induction on the finite number $t$ of factors, gives $\nu_t(R)=\prod_{i=1}^t\int_{B_i}\lambda\,dQ_0=\prod_{i=1}^tQ_1(B_i)$, where each factor uses [F8]. This is the rectangle formula for $Q_1^{\otimes t}$ by [F4]. The two measures agree on the generating pi-system and both have total mass one, so [F6] gives $\nu_t=Q_1^{\otimes t}$. [A1, F3, F4, F6, F7, F8, F9, F10, F12, F13, step 1.1]

3.1 If $A\in\mathcal F_t=\sigma(Y_t)$, then $A=Y_t^{-1}(B)$ for some product-measurable $B$: the family of such preimages is a sigma-algebra and is exactly the sigma-algebra generated by $Y_t$. The definition of law [F5] gives the integral change-of-variables identity first for indicators and nonnegative simple functions; increasing simple approximation and [F11] extend it to $h_t\mathbf1_B$. Thus, using steps 1.1 and 2.1, $P_1(A)=Q_1^{\otimes t}(B)=\int_Bh_t\,dQ_0^{\otimes t}=\int_Ah_t(Y_t)\,dP_0$. This is the Radon–Nikodym characterization of $L_t=h_t(Y_t)$. [F3, F5, F7, F11, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎

## Source notes

Wolpert writes the iid likelihood ratio as the product of one-observation density ratios in §1.2, PDF p. 4. Durrett Theorem 2.1.11, printed p. 48, proves the finite iid product-law identity by checking rectangles and using pi-system uniqueness. The density factorization above is proved locally for general one-observation laws with $Q_1\ll Q_0$.
