# Same-owner future-consumer correction proposal

This is a proposed replacement of only the online-learning part of ML-28 and its plan-spec mirror. It leaves finite-class private ERM unchanged. The original plan and plan-spec preimages are archived in `before/research/`. No actual ML-28 item carrier exists at the census; these are future consumer authoring obligations, not proof acceptance or native coverage.

## Exact replacement prose

For online learning, fix integers $k,T\ge1$, loss vectors $\ell_t\in[0,1]^k$, and average regret $R_T=T^{-1}\sum_t\ell_t(a_t)-\min_jT^{-1}\sum_t\ell_t(j)$. Initialize $w_{1,j}=1$, choose $a_t=j$ with probability $p_{t,j}=w_{t,j}/\sum_rw_{t,r}$, observe the entire loss vector, then update $w_{t+1,j}=w_{t,j}e^{-\eta\ell_t(j)}$. Require losses to be nonanticipating: the round-$t$ loss vector is fixed before the fresh action draw, though it may depend on the previous transcript. For every such adversary and every $\eta>0$, $\mathbb E R_T\le\eta+\ln k/(\eta T)$; with probability at least $1-\beta$,
$$R_T\le\eta+\frac{\ln k}{\eta T}+\sqrt{\frac{\ln(1/\beta)}{2T}}.$$
The expected-regret bound gives $2\sqrt{\ln k/T}$ at $\eta=\sqrt{\ln k/T}$ for $k\ge2$; at $k=1$ the regret is identically zero. No computational-efficiency obstruction arises in the finite $k$ update itself.

For privacy, view a fixed loss sequence as the private input and declare two sequences adjacent when one entire time-step vector is arbitrarily replaced. The released output is the full action transcript. Each action kernel is $(2\eta,0)$-DP under that replacement relation, for every previous action transcript. Under AC, for every $\varepsilon>0$ and $0<\delta<1$, put $L=\ln(1/\delta)$ and choose
$$\eta=\min\left\{\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{32TL}},\frac12,\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon}{16T}}\right\}>0.$$
Advanced composition with round error zero and auxiliary error $\delta'=\delta$ gives $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP for the full action transcript. The displayed exact regret bounds apply with this choice for all $\varepsilon>0$ and $0<\delta,\beta<1$. In particular the high-probability bound can be written without an implicit simplification as
$$R_T\le\eta+\frac{\ln k}{T}\max\left\{\frac{\sqrt{32TL}}{\varepsilon},2,4\sqrt{\frac{T}{\varepsilon}}\right\}+\sqrt{\frac{\ln(1/\beta)}{2T}}.$$
The source choice $\eta_0=\varepsilon/\sqrt{32TL}$ is retained whenever $\varepsilon\le\sqrt{8TL}$ and $\varepsilon\le2L$; in this region the bound specializes to $\eta_0+\sqrt{32L}\ln k/(\varepsilon\sqrt T)+\sqrt{\ln(1/\beta)/(2T)}$. Do not replace these bounds by the originally planned unrestricted $O(\sqrt{L\ln(k/\beta)}/(\varepsilon\sqrt T))$: the stated update and proof do not support that simplification for arbitrary $k,\varepsilon,\delta$. The privacy claim for a fixed sequence and the regret claim for a nonanticipating adaptive sequence are distinct quantified claims. For an interactive privacy formulation with loss-generating strategies, require at every common transcript the per-round ordered kernel inequalities under the chosen adjacent-strategy relation, then apply the same general-adjacency composition theorem. Arbitrary response to the current fresh action is not permitted by the regret claim.

Proposed items remain `alg-private-randomized-weighted-majority` and `thm-private-online-learning-regret-under-composition`, with a local potential/martingale lemma if needed. Their composition supplier is `thm-advanced-adaptive-composition-arbitrary-measurable-outputs`, whose Statement permits any symmetric adjacency relation, including replacement of one loss vector. Carry `def-axiom-of-choice` for that invocation. Retain the original ERM items and examples.

## Complete local derivation for the replacement

Write $W_t=\sum_jw_{t,j}$. For $u\ge0$, $e^{-u}\le1-u+u^2/2$: the difference has value and first derivative zero at zero and second derivative $1-e^{-u}\ge0$. Thus
$$\frac{W_{t+1}}{W_t}=\sum_jp_{t,j}e^{-\eta\ell_t(j)}\le1-\eta\langle p_t,\ell_t\rangle+\eta^2/2.$$
The right side is positive because it bounds the positive left side. Since $\log x\le x-1$, summation gives $\log(W_{T+1}/k)\le-\eta\sum_t\langle p_t,\ell_t\rangle+T\eta^2/2$. Meanwhile $W_{T+1}\ge e^{-\eta\min_j\sum_t\ell_t(j)}$. Combining yields, pathwise even for adaptive losses,
$$\sum_t\langle p_t,\ell_t\rangle-\min_j\sum_t\ell_t(j)\le\frac{\ln k}{\eta}+\frac{T\eta}{2}\le\frac{\ln k}{\eta}+T\eta.$$
For a nonanticipating adversary, condition immediately before drawing $a_t$ (including the already selected $\ell_t$). Then $V_t=\ell_t(a_t)-\langle p_t,\ell_t\rangle$ has conditional expectation zero and lies in a predictable interval of length at most one. The partial sums form a martingale, so Azuma with interval length one gives $\Pr[\sum_tV_t\ge s]\le e^{-2s^2/T}$. With $s=\sqrt{T\ln(1/\beta)/2}$ this gives the displayed regret bound. Integrating $V_t$ gives the expectation bound, with no independence claim for adaptive loss sequences. At $k=1$ the realized action and hindsight expert coincide exactly.

For adjacent fixed loss sequences, each cumulative score $q_t(j)=-\sum_{s<t}\ell_s(j)$ changes by at most one. The numerator $e^{\eta q_t(j)}$ and its normalizer each change by at most the factor $e^\eta$ in either direction; consequently every action probability ratio is at most $e^{2\eta}$, and summing gives every event inequality. This argument holds for every prior action transcript and requires no exact attained sensitivity claim at the initial round (whose sensitivity is zero). The general symmetric-adjacency composition supplier therefore applies with common per-round epsilon $2\eta$, per-round delta zero, and auxiliary delta exactly $\delta$, giving
$$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{adv}}=2\eta\sqrt{2TL}+2T\eta(e^{2\eta}-1).$$
Our first cap makes the first term at most $\varepsilon/2$. Since $2\eta\le1$, convexity of $e^x$ on $[0,1]$ gives $e^{2\eta}-1\le2\eta(e-1)\le4\eta$ (for example the exponential series gives $e<3$). The third cap then gives $2T\eta(e^{2\eta}-1)\le8T\eta^2\le\varepsilon/2$. Thus $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{adv}}\le\varepsilon$ for every parameter in the replacement statement. The auxiliary error satisfies $T\cdot0+\delta=\delta<1$, with no beta allocation to privacy. The regret tail uses beta separately. Solving $\eta_0\le1/2$ and $\eta_0\le\sqrt{\varepsilon/(16T)}$ gives exactly the two stated conditions retaining the original eta formula.

## Source reading and correction evidence

Read Dwork–Roth §11.2 in full: printed pp.222–226 (PDF zero-based pages225–229), Algorithms15–16, Theorems11.1–11.4, and all adjoining proof explanations. Screenshot of PDF page229 confirms the source's exact displayed expected-regret term is $\sqrt{32L}\ln k/(\varepsilon\sqrt T)$, with $\ln k$ outside the square root, and its final big-O summary has a smaller logarithmic dependence not established by that displayed calculation. The source invokes Theorem3.20 through its convenient privacy-parameter corollary without spelling out the restrictions in this later section. The proposed correction supplies the full local bound and an all-positive-epsilon safe choice rather than importing either simplification.

The original unrestricted eta prescription already fails the sufficient exact composition calculation: at $T=1$, $\delta=e^{-1}$, $\varepsilon=10$, it gives $\eta_0=10/\sqrt{32}$ and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{adv}}=5+(20/\sqrt{32})(e^{20/\sqrt{32}}-1)>10$. This is a counterexample to the claimed inference from the exact composition bound, not a claim that the one-round RWM transcript itself violates privacy (the first draw is input-independent). The fixed-random-sequence $1/\varepsilon$-only regret rate also cannot be asserted for every epsilon: with $T=1,k=2$ and fixed losses $(0,1)$, RWM's initial uniform action has regret1 with probability1/2 irrespective of epsilon; at fixed beta<1/2 no universal bound tending to zero as epsilon→∞ is valid.
