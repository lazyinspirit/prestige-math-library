## Statement

Assume full AC and the hypotheses of [[lem-getzler-radial-gauge-oscillator-leading-term]]. In the finite-dimensional even exterior-form algebra at $y$, the heat equation for $P_0$ has the normalized Gaussian solution
$$k_t(x,0)=(4\pi t)^{-m}\det^{1/2}\!\left(\frac{t\mathcal R/2}{\sinh(t\mathcal R/2)}\right)\exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{4t}x^T\bigl((t\mathcal R/2)\coth(t\mathcal R/2)\bigr)x\right)e^{-tF^{V/S}}.$$
The square root is the formal branch with constant term $1$. Its degree-$2m$ diagonal supertrace coefficient, interpreted as an oriented top form using [[def-riemannian-volume-form-on-an-oriented-manifold]] and the curvature convention of [[def-ahat-and-relative-chern-character-forms]], is exactly $[\widehat A(TM,\nabla^{LC})\operatorname{ch}(V/S)]_{2m}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The oscillator, curvature normalization and relative supertrace of [[lem-getzler-radial-gauge-oscillator-leading-term]], [[def-ahat-and-relative-chern-character-forms]], and the local spinor module interface.

[F1] $P_0=-\sum_i(\partial_i+\frac14\mathcal R_{ij}x^j)^2+F^{V/S}\wedge$ and in positive dimension the top Clifford supertrace is $(-2i)^m\operatorname{str}_W$ ([[lem-getzler-radial-gauge-oscillator-leading-term]]).

[F2] The characteristic normalizations are $\widehat A=\det^{1/2}((i\Omega^{LC}/4\pi)/\sinh(i\Omega^{LC}/4\pi))$ and $\operatorname{ch}(V/S)=\operatorname{str}_W\exp(-F^{V/S}/2\pi i)$ ([[def-ahat-and-relative-chern-character-forms]]).

[A1] The local complex spinor interface is supplied by [[thm-complex-spin-modules-and-compact-reducibility]]. Full AC is inherited through the analytic and Chern–Weil suppliers ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F3] The oriented Riemannian volume form has value $\operatorname{vol}_g(y)=\varepsilon_y$ in dimension zero, while its unsigned volume density has value $1$ ([[def-riemannian-volume-form-on-an-oriented-manifold]]).

## Proof

**Given:** The formal finite-dimensional even exterior algebra generated by tangent curvature two-forms, the matrix $\mathcal R$, and the relative endomorphism-valued two-form $F=F^{V/S}$. Steps 1.1–4.1 treat positive dimension; step 5.1 treats $m=0$.

1.1 Put $B(t)=(t\mathcal R/2)\coth(t\mathcal R/2)$, $z=t\mathcal R/2$, and $a(t)=(4\pi t)^{-m}\det^{1/2}(z/\sinh z)$. All positive-degree curvature coefficients are nilpotent, so the power series for $\sinh$, $\coth$, determinant and the square root truncate in each exterior degree. Since $\mathcal R$ is skew, $B$ is an even power series in $\mathcal R$, hence symmetric and commuting with $\mathcal R$; $\mathcal R B$ is skew. Direct differentiation gives $$tB'=B-B^2+\tfrac{t^2}{4}\mathcal R^2,$$ using $z\frac d{dz}(z\coth z)=z\coth z-z^2\operatorname{csch}^2z$, and $$\frac{a'}a=-\frac{\operatorname{tr}B}{2t},$$ since $\dim M=2m$. [F1, given, algebra]

2.1 Let $\phi=a(t)\exp(-x^TB(t)x/(4t))e^{-tF}$. For $L_i=\partial_i+(\mathcal R x)_i/4$, one has $L_i\phi=(- (Bx)_i/(2t)+ (\mathcal R x)_i/4)\phi$. Summing $L_i^2$ for $L_i=\partial_i+(\mathcal R x)_i/4$ gives $$\sum_iL_i^2\phi=\left(-\frac{\operatorname{tr}B}{2t}+\frac{x^TB^2x}{4t^2}-\frac{x^T\mathcal R^2x}{16}\right)\phi,$$ because the mixed term is $x^T\mathcal R Bx=0$ and $\operatorname{tr}\mathcal R=0$. The equations in step 1.1 cancel the constant and quadratic coefficients of $(\partial_t-\sum_iL_i^2+F\wedge)\phi$, so $\phi$ solves the oscillator heat equation. As $t\downarrow0$, $B(t)\to I$, $a(t)\sim(4\pi t)^{-m}$, and the normalized Euclidean Gaussian tends to the delta distribution; this fixes the initial value. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 At $x=0$ the Gaussian factor is $1$. The spinor supertrace kills every Clifford degree below $2m$ and multiplies the top term by $(-2i)^m$ from [F1]; combining this with $(4\pi)^{-m}$ contributes $(-i/(2\pi))^m$ to each product of $m$ curvature two-forms. The determinant factor is even in $\mathcal R$, so replacing each tangent-curvature symbol by $-i\Omega^{LC}/(2\pi)$ gives exactly $\widehat A$ in [F2]. The twisting factor $e^{-F}$ becomes $e^{iF/(2\pi)}=e^{-F/(2\pi i)}$ and its trace is the relative supertrace in [F2]. Their degree-$2m$ part is the asserted density. [F1, F2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 No matrix of forms is diagonalized: the calculation is in a finite-dimensional graded-commutative algebra, and all formal series are degree-truncated. Endomorphisms on $W$ need not commute with one another; they commute with the scalar tangent-curvature matrix, which is all the Gaussian calculation uses. The sign is fixed by the positive-frequency symbol $ic(\xi)$ and the curvature convention in [F2]. The local spinor factor is the actual interface in [A1]. [A1, F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 If $m=0$, the determinant of the empty matrix and the empty Gaussian factor are $1$, and all curvature two-forms vanish. Thus the displayed kernel is $I_V$, the normalized heat solution on a point as in [F1]. Its diagonal supertrace with respect to the unsigned counting density is $\operatorname{str}_V I$. Multiplying by the oriented volume form in [F3] gives the degree-zero form $\varepsilon_y\operatorname{str}_V I$. By [F2] and the reference grading, $\operatorname{str}_{W_y}I=\varepsilon_y\operatorname{str}_{V_y}I$, so this oriented form is exactly $[\widehat A\operatorname{ch}(V/S)]_0$. This distinguishes the heat density from the differential form at a negatively oriented point. [F1, F2, F3, given, algebra] ∎
