## Statement

Assume full AC. Let $U_n$, $n\ge1$, be the standard ordered unlink with a marked point and normal vector on its last component, and add its small meridional earring and joining arc. Use determinant-one gauges for the actual singular bundle with second Stiefel–Whitney class dual to that arc. The unperturbed flat critical set is a smooth clean compact manifold diffeomorphic to $(S^2)^{n-1}$. Its critical Hessian kernel is exactly its tangent bundle and its normal Hessian has a uniform gap.

There are arbitrarily small allowed holonomy perturbations with exactly $2^{n-1}$ nondegenerate critical points near this manifold, labelled by the north/south choices on those sphere factors. Their relative cylindrical indices are all even, in every relative path class. Thus there are no index-one trajectories between these generators, and their chosen critical-point cochain group has rank $2^{n-1}$ with zero degree-one differential.

This is the chosen unlink critical and grading calculation. It does not by itself prove multi-component link invariance, a split/excision formula, any saddle cobordism map or a skein exact triangle. In particular the finite fixed-index class assertion for a two-core knot earring is not asserted for these additional orientable-axis cores.

## Proof

**Given:** The standard split unlink, with the marked component and its earring contained in one ball, the remaining $m=n-1$ unknots in disjoint balls, and the specified arc-supported singular bundle.

1.1 **The actual link data and its twisted holonomies.** Remove product collars of the separating spheres between these balls. Their intersections are $S^2\times I$, simply connected, so [F4] gives exterior group $\mathbb Z^2*F_m$: the Hopf exterior contributes the first factor, and each ordinary unknot exterior contributes its meridian generator $c_j$. The latter exterior is a solid torus, as the coordinate-circle complement in [F1] shows. The arc-supported $w_2$ has value one on the Hopf product torus and zero on the other boundary tori. Extend the Hopf orbifold data exactly as in [F1]. Each additional core has the constant meridional half-turn model, with trivial longitudinal axis transport; no arc ends there. Glue those local models to the prescribed exterior bundle by the same boundary two-cell $w_2$ comparison as [F1]. The fixed determinant lift is obtained from the integral arc class by the same explicit finite-cover scalar cocycle construction. In particular the two earring cores still force every determinant-one stabilizer to be central, even though the additional axis lines are orientable. For a flat connection, lifted parallel transport on the universal cover gives quaternion holonomies $a,b,c_1,\ldots,c_m$, with $ab=-ba$ and every $c_j$ a unit imaginary quaternion. This describes the determinant-one quotient, not just adjoint conjugacy: fix one lift at the basepoint and the square-root determinant conventions on the generators; the only two-cell central obstruction is the Hopf square. Conversely these tuples glue the flat projective bundle and the local meridional models. Equal tuples up to one $SU(2)$ conjugator give a determinant-one comparison by parallel transport along paths; the generator and two-cell identities make it single-valued, and its local gauge equation extends it through the branch cores. Thus this description retains the lift sheets which would be lost by replacing every $c_j$ by an unoriented adjoint axis. [F1, F2, F4, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 **The critical manifold and its full Hessian kernel.** Conjugate the ordered perpendicular pair $(a,b)$ to $(i,j)$. The conjugator is unique up to the center, so the remaining $c_j$ are independently points of the unit imaginary sphere. This identifies the actual flat critical set with $C=(S^2)^m$. The parametrization is smooth in the quotient: locally choose smoothly the conjugator of the perpendicular frame; its central ambiguity acts trivially. The inverse is the corresponding normalized holonomy tuple, so this is an embedded compact critical manifold in the Sobolev slice quotient. Compute infinitesimal flat deformations by differentiating these actual word identities. On the Hopf factor, [F1]'s adjoint torus complex is acyclic, so any cocycle there is a unique coboundary. Subtract that global coboundary. It now vanishes on $a,b$. There are no free-product relators involving distinct $c_j$. Differentiating $c_j^2=-1$ gives $(1+\operatorname{Ad}c_j)Z(c_j)=0$, precisely the two-plane perpendicular to its axis. No nonzero remaining coboundary vanishes on both $a,b$, since their adjoint zero space is zero. Hence the deformation space is exactly the direct sum of these $m$ tangent planes. This cocycle computation is the actual flat differential calculation: integrate a closed adjoint one-form on the universal cover to its covariant primitive; its deck differences form this cocycle, and a coboundary is precisely a single-valued gauge primitive. The local meridian relation is what lets that primitive and deformation extend smoothly over the branched core, by the same radial gauge equation as [F1]. Thus the compact selfadjoint critical Hessian has kernel exactly $TC$, not an additional normal null space. The orthogonal normal realization is invertible. Its inverse norms are uniformly bounded over compact $C$: a sequence of normalized normal vectors with Hessian tending to zero would have a strong subsequential kernel limit by the local elliptic estimate and Rellich, perpendicular to the limiting tangent kernel, a contradiction. The finite invariant chart proof of [F2] applies unchanged on each extra component; global irreducibility still comes from the Hopf part in step 1.1. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 **An actual cylinder perturbation with the prescribed restriction.** With the Hopf pair used to choose the frame, put $h(c_1,\ldots,c_m)=\sum_j\langle c_j,i\rangle$. In unnormalized holonomies this is $-\frac12\sum_j\operatorname{tr}(c_ja)$, invariant under simultaneous conjugation. Choose the corresponding finite common-base tuple of loops, and thicken it to the actual immersed cylinder data of [F2]. At a flat connection the nearby loops are homotopic with their common-base transports, so the invariant trace function is constant in the thickening disk. Integrating a weight of total mass one therefore gives a genuine allowed cylinder function whose restriction to $C$ is exactly $h$. Near $C$ take the $L^2$-normal slice and solve the normal component of the critical equation for perturbation $\varepsilon h$, using step 2.1 and [F4]. The local solutions $w_\varepsilon(x)=O(\varepsilon)$ agree on overlaps by the normal inverse's uniqueness. The remaining tangential equation is the derivative of the reduced function $S|_C+\varepsilon h+O(\varepsilon^2)$, with the error bounded in every fixed $C^k$ norm on compact $C$. The unperturbed functional is constant there, since its differential vanishes. The function $h$ has precisely the $2^m$ north/south critical tuples and each Hessian is the direct sum of positive or negative definite two-dimensional sphere Hessians. Stability of these nondegenerate finite critical points and the positive lower bound for $|dh|$ outside their neighborhoods give precisely one perturbed critical point near each tuple and no others near $C$, for all sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$. There are none far from $C$: otherwise the bounded-forcing critical compactness proof in [F2], applied to perturbations tending to zero, would yield a flat critical limit outside its prescribed neighborhood. That proof only uses curvature/holonomy derivative bounds and actual finite cocycle comparisons, so does not require the unperturbed critical set to be discrete. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.2 **The extra gauge components and their actual index periods.** The two earring restrictions have component quotient $(\mathbb Z/2)^2$ as in [F3]. Each additional orientable-axis core instead has restriction space of circle-valued maps on a circle, with component winding in $\mathbb Z$. Do not replace these factors by finite groups. Restriction is onto on components by the same vanishing $\pi_1SU(2)=\pi_2SU(2)=0$ extension over the exterior cells. After removing chosen extensions of those core maps, a gauge is identity near every core. The relative three-cell obstruction represents it by ordinary ball degrees, possibly with relations from loops of the restriction spaces; generation suffices here, and no injection of that relative integer group is needed. An ordinary generator has index eight. For either earring parity component, square its chosen lift and use the ordinary kernel, exactly as in [F3], giving index divisible by four. For an orientable-core winding-one extension, close its interpolation on a small ball centered on that core. Its equivariant clutch has fixed-circle winding one. The elementary clutch calculation in [F3] gives index four modulo the ordinary index-eight ambiguity, since its cover degree is odd. The winding-minus-one case and all powers follow by index additivity. Thus every gauge period is divisible by four. This proves the required grading-period assertion even though infinitely many gauge components can have one fixed index. No finite-class conclusion has been imported from the restricted two-core theorem. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 **All critical relative indices are even.** Along $C$ the normal Hessian remains invertible. Shift its tangent zero block to a small fixed positive block. The resulting selfadjoint family is invertible along any path in connected $C$, so its spectral flow is zero, by the same cylindrical index continuation and constant-neck inverse in [F2]–[F3]. Near a perturbed critical point, the full Hessian has this same normal block up to a small invertible deformation and tangent block $\varepsilon\operatorname{Hess}h+O(\varepsilon^2)$. The Schur complement removes the small mixed blocks without a zero crossing. Relative to the positive tangent reference its index is the negative eigenspace dimension of that finite tangent block, up to the fixed incoming/outgoing sign convention. Each south/north choice contributes zero or two, so these differences are even. Connect any two perturbed critical points through their small normal paths and a path in $C$. Their relative index is therefore even. Every other relative path differs by a quotient gauge loop; all such index periods are divisible by four by step 3.2. Hence the index is even in every class, not merely in the chosen nearby Morse model. This argument uses the actual full Hessians and their finite tangent blocks, not an asserted equality between the infinite-dimensional action and an unrelated finite-dimensional gradient flow. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra]

5.1 **The exact scope of the chosen unlink calculation.** The chosen nondegenerate critical group has one integral orientation module per tuple, hence is free of rank $2^m$. By step 4.1 no relative class has index one, so no index-one solution can contribute to its degree-one differential; that differential is zero. If $m=0$, the product of spheres is one point and this is the four-row unknot calculation [F1]. For $m>0$ the same chosen critical computation is now proved, with its actual additional gauge components retained. Identifying it as a link invariant or identifying saddle maps still requires the link-scope continuation/cobordism construction. Neither the original knot-only analytic Statement nor the cited primary's split formula is silently substituted for that missing operation. [F1, step 3.1, step 3.2, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
