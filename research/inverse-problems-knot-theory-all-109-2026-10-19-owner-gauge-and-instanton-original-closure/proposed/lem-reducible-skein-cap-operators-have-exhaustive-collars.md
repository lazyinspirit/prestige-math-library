## Statement

Assume full AC. On the orbifold pair consisting of a standard four-ball and its standard boundary-parallel annulus, give the annulus trivial sheet data and the flat singular connection whose two boundary meridians agree. Choose the product metric on its branched double cover, a round two-sphere times a disk with a cylindrical end, invariant under the simultaneous reflections on the two factors. Use a small positive exponential weight, fix the limiting critical connection, and allow constant limiting gauge transformations. Its deformation cohomology dimensions are $(h^0,h^1,h^2)=(1,0,2)$. The two infinitesimal framed normal parameters for the reducible endpoint of the unlink critical interval pair isomorphically with $H^2$. After these parameters are included, the only residual symmetry is the circle stabilizer.

For the standard two-disks cap, every point of the unlink critical interval has a unique flat extension. Its obstruction space is zero and its critical deformation restriction is an isomorphism, including the two framed modes at the reducible endpoints. Thus this cap has an invertible normal operator after its actual critical modes and stabilizers are separated.

Gluing this annulus connection to the regular quarter-charge projective-plane connection across their common unknot end, with those two parameters free, therefore has an exhaustive one-parameter collar after quotienting the relative circle matching. Here the one parameter is the neck length. The same construction works when the second boundary meridian has the opposite orientation. It uses no positive lower bound for a critical spectral gap on the entire unlink interval: the two modes that lose their gap at its endpoints are included explicitly.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] The explicit projective-plane quotient, including its positive normal Euler sign, and the unique regular irreducible quarter-charge connection are proved in [[lem-conjugation-quotient-of-the-projective-plane-is-a-smooth-veronese-four-sphere]] and [[thm-quarter-charge-asd-connections-on-the-reversed-projective-plane-are-tautological]]. The two-saddle punctured summand is constructed in [[lem-two-positive-cyclic-skein-saddles-split-off-the-explicit-positive-projective-plane]].

[F2] Local invariant Sobolev estimates and finite-dimensional Fredholm augmentations are the actual constructions in [[lem-order-two-singular-gauge-completions-and-cylindrical-fredholm-operators]]. The compact-core count proof, its nonlocal perturbation truncation and its contraction/exhaustion estimates are in [[thm-admissible-earring-cobordisms-have-finite-oriented-family-counts-and-gluing]]. That theorem excludes reducible cuts; its local estimates alone are used here, with the missing modes computed below.

[F3] Bulk regularity on a nonorientable component with trivial sheet data is [[lem-nonorientable-trivial-delta-orbifold-asd-moduli-have-generic-holonomy-regularity]]. It does not by itself prove the collar claimed here.

## Proof

**Given:** The specified standard annulus, product branched-cover metric, flat connection and quarter-charge projective-plane connection. All deformation spaces below are real.

1.1 **The actual cover and coefficient action.** The map $Q:S^3\to S^3$, $Q(z_1,z_2)=(z_1\sqrt{1+|z_2|^2},z_2^2)$, has norm-one image because $|z_1|^2(1+|z_2|^2)+|z_2|^4=1$. It is the quotient by $(z_1,z_2)\mapsto(z_1,-z_2)$, branched on $z_2=0$: off that circle a square root of its second coordinate determines precisely the two preimages, and the transverse coordinate is literally squared. Remove the exchanged round caps $\Re z_2>a$ and $\Re z_2<-a$, $0<a<1$. Their complement has coordinates $(s,u)\in[-a,a]\times S^2$ by writing $(\Re z_1,\Im z_1,\Im z_2)=\sqrt{1-s^2}\,u$. Its involution is $(s,u_1,u_2,u_3)\mapsto(-s,u_1,u_2,-u_3)$. The quotient is a ball: the removed caps map diffeomorphically to one ball disjoint from the original branch circle, leaving its complementary ball with that standard circle. Taking a product with an interval and rounding invariant product corners gives the cover $S^2\times D^2$ with the simultaneous reflections, and branch annulus $S^1\times I$. The flat connection lifts to the trivial adjoint connection. Its axis is invariant under the deck involution and its two-plane is anti-invariant. Thus its axis-valued forms are invariant ordinary forms on this cover and each off-axis scalar coefficient is anti-invariant. [given, construct]

2.1 **Zero modes on the boundary.** Put $X=S^2\times D^2$ and attach its cylinder, whose cross-section is $Y=S^2\times S^1$. Fix the product orientations and unit area forms $\omega_S,\omega_D$, so $*\omega_S=\omega_D$. On the cylindrical end $\omega_D=dt\wedge d\theta$, up to a fixed positive length normalization. The harmonic zero modes relevant to the ASD complex on $Y$ are constants, $d\theta$, and $\omega_S$ and their duals; this follows directly by expanding on the round sphere and circle. Every other separated mode has absolute eigenvalue bounded below by a positive constant. Reflection acts positively on constants and negatively on each of $d\theta$ and $\omega_S$. Consequently the invariant axis has one constant gauge mode and no critical deformation mode. The anti-invariant two-plane has two critical deformation modes, represented by $d\theta$ times its two constant coefficient vectors. For the unlink these are actual framed normal parameters, not merely formal harmonic vectors: normalize its first meridian to $i$ and write the second as $\cos|q|\,i+(\sin|q|/|q|)(q_1j+q_2k)$, $q\in\mathbb R^2$ small. Its free group has no relation imposing an obstruction on $q$. The residual axis circle rotates this plane, and its quotient is the half-interval at the reducible endpoint. Reversing the second meridian changes which endpoint is used and leaves these dimensions unchanged. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 **The full weighted deformation calculation.** A decaying one-form $b$ in the deformation kernel satisfies $d^*b=0$ and $d^+b=0$. Integration by parts on truncated cylinders gives $\int db\wedge db=0$, whence $db=0$; the boundary term tends to zero exponentially. Since $H^1(X;\mathbb R)=0$, write $b=df$. On the single end $f$ is a constant plus an exponentially decaying function by the scalar separated expansion. Another integration by parts gives $df=0$. Thus $H^1=0$ in both coefficient sectors. Constant parallel sections give only the invariant axis, so $h^0=1$. A dual-weight self-dual harmonic two-form has a bounded constant-mode term and exponentially decaying nonzero modes. Indeed the weight is smaller than the least nonzero boundary eigenvalue; the dual weighted norm excludes every growing nonzero mode. Its constant term is determined by its period on $S^2$. Subtract the corresponding multiple of $\omega_S+\omega_D$, which is closed, self-dual and anti-invariant. The remainder decays and has zero absolute cohomology class, since $H^2(X;\mathbb R)$ is generated by the sphere. It has a primitive that is bounded on the end: a decaying primitive of the exact end form differs from any global primitive by a closed end form, hence by $c\,d\theta+df$, and the exact term can be removed there. Stokes then gives zero for the squared norm of this remainder, so it vanishes. The invariant axis therefore has no $H^2$, while the anti-invariant two-plane has precisely the two forms $(\omega_S+\omega_D)e_j$. These are extended, rather than square-integrable, harmonic forms. This proves $(h^0,h^1,h^2)=(1,0,2)$ without treating a dense range as an invertible operator. [step 1.1, step 2.1, F2, construct]

4.1 **The missing obstruction pairing.** Represent a derivative of an external critical parameter by $\chi(t)d\theta\,e_j$, with $\chi=0$ before the cylindrical collar and $\chi=1$ far out. Its curvature derivative is $\chi'(t)dt\wedge d\theta\,e_j$. Pairing its self-dual part with the extended dual vector $(\omega_S+\omega_D)e_l$ gives $c\,\delta_{jl}$, where $c$ is the positive sphere area times the circle length, with the fixed nonzero normalization of $d^+$. This is also exactly the boundary integral $\int_Y d\theta\wedge\omega_S$. It is independent of the choice of $\chi$. Therefore adjoining $q_1,q_2$ kills the entire two-dimensional obstruction and introduces no new kernel. Project the scalar Coulomb equation off the constant axis gauge mode; on that complement the augmented operator is onto and has zero kernel. Its bounded inverse follows from the local elliptic estimates and separated end inverse: failure of a lower bound would give a normalized sequence, convergent on the core and on each nonzero spectral subspace, with zero limit in the already computed kernel; the zero modes have been included in the finite augmentation and cannot escape down the end. The remaining constant mode is the genuine circle stabilizer, not an additional right-inverse assertion. [step 2.1, step 3.1, F2, construct]

4.2 **The actual two-disks cap.** Its double cover is $S^1\times D^3$ with action $(\theta,s,x_1,x_2)\mapsto(-\theta,-s,x_1,x_2)$. An explicit quotient parameter is obtained from the annulus $z=e^{s+i\theta}$ by $z\mapsto z+z^{-1}$. Its quotient by $z\mapsto z^{-1}$ is the ellipse disk: solving the quadratic gives the two reciprocal roots, and on $|z|=e^{\pm a}$ the image is the same ellipse. Its only branch points are $z=1,-1$, of ordinary square type. Product with a disk, then invariant corner rounding, gives the four-ball with two standard branch disks. The cover retracts onto its circle; its boundary is $S^1\times S^2$ and inclusion induces its circle generator. For external meridians $i$ and $b=\cos\theta\,i+\sin\theta\,j$, cover holonomy is $ib=-\cos\theta+\sin\theta\,k$. In the open interval its adjoint has a parallel $k$ axis and a two-plane with nonidentity holonomy, so the latter's circle cochain differential is invertible. The deck reverses the circle and the $k$ axis, giving one invariant $H^1$ and no invariant $H^0$. At either endpoint cover adjoint holonomy is the identity: its invariant $H^0$ is the meridional axis and its invariant $H^1$ consists of the other two coefficients times the circle form. These are exactly the external critical spaces of step 2.1, and restriction is an isomorphism. In every coefficient sector $H^2$ of the cover vanishes. A bounded extended self-dual harmonic form could have a constant end term only through its period on the boundary two-sphere; that sphere bounds the three-disk with its parallel coefficient, so the period is zero. The remaining form decays, is exact, and has a bounded primitive; the Stokes argument of step 3.1 makes it zero. Thus the obstruction is zero, not merely the ordinary absolute second cohomology. All flat extensions are unique because the exterior meridian presentation is free on the same two generators as the boundary unlink exterior; there is no extra representation relation or extension parameter. Separating the just calculated finite critical spaces and stabilizers leaves a zero-kernel, zero-cokernel normal operator, with the bounded inverse supplied by the local elliptic and end estimates. Near a wall use the actual framed disk $q$, retaining its two modes before quotienting the circle, so no inverse of the disappearing critical eigenvalue is invoked. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, F2, construct]

5.1 **Matching at the internal unknot.** The unknot meridian has fixed trace zero. Its infinitesimal fixed-conjugacy deformation vanishes: its complement group is infinite cyclic, its axis displacement is excluded by the trace condition, and on its off-axis plane $\operatorname{Ad}(i)-1=-2$ makes the one-generator cochain differential onto. Hence its critical $H^1$ is zero and its stabilizer is the axis circle. After the annulus augmentation of step 4.1 the annulus has no normal kernel or obstruction. The punctured quarter-charge solution has no normal kernel or obstruction by [F1]; capping it by the flat disk neither creates one, since that disk has only its constant axis stabilizer. On the long internal neck all nonzero critical modes have a fixed gap. Its only zero mode is a constant gauge mode. Use temporal gauge there: an axis component in the neck direction is removed by its actual integral, leaving the relative circle matching parameter. Thus no inverse of a derivative on a length-$T$ interval with a claimed uniform bound is being used. The annulus stabilizer acts transitively on this relative circle; quotienting it leaves exactly one matching class, since the other component is irreducible. Patch the two normal inverses in these temporal slices. Differential cutoff errors are $O(T^{-1})$ and the tails are exponentially small. For bounded holonomy terms first truncate to finitely many loop supports, put the cutoffs outside them, then make the tail operator norm small, exactly as in [F2]. The finite matching map is the isomorphism of step 4.1. A Neumann series therefore gives a uniformly bounded normal inverse on the quotient matching slice. This is the actual reducible-cut replacement for the inadmissible use of a nondegenerate critical inverse. [F1, F2, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 **Nonlinear collar and its exact scope.** Preglue the two fixed limiting connections with their product critical models. The error is exponentially small in $T$, together with any chosen perturbation terms flat at $T=\infty$. Include $q$ among the unknowns and use the inverse of step 5.1. Sobolev multiplication gives a quadratic remainder with Lipschitz constant bounded by $C(\|b\|+\|b'\|)$ on a fixed small slice ball. Contraction yields one corrected solution for each sufficiently large $T$, with its uniquely determined $q(T)\to0$ and smooth parameter dependence. Any sequence converging to this broken pair is exponentially close on the nonzero modes after temporal neck gauge, and its remaining external critical component is exactly its $q$ coordinate. Its relative circle is the unique matching class described in step 5.1. Thus it lies in this same contraction ball and equals the constructed correction. This proves exhaustion and uniqueness of this end collar. It does not classify other possible limits in a whole triple-saddle family: that additional compactness and index argument, and the two different annular identifications at its two ends, are required before a degree-one cap count or a cube relation can be asserted. [F1, F2, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct] ∎
