## Statement

Assume full AC. Fix the constructed earring data and a holonomy perturbation with finitely many nondegenerate critical points. There is a separable Banach space of further cylinder perturbations whose functions and all connection derivatives vanish at those critical points. Sufficiently small such perturbations retain exactly the same critical points and Hessians. A residual dense set of these perturbations makes every nonconstant finite-energy trajectory in every relative class regular, at every integer Fredholm index. The corresponding translation quotient is a smooth Hausdorff second-countable manifold of dimension $I-1$. In particular there are no nonconstant trajectories with $I\le0$.

Trajectories here are solutions in the actual unweighted cylindrical configuration spaces with critical asymptotics, and finite energy is the flow energy defined above. Establishing that arbitrary finite-flow-energy solutions have those asymptotics, and proving compactifications or gluing collars, remain separate claims. No orientability assertion is made here.

## Proof

**Given:** The fixed finite nondegenerate critical set and the actual cylindrical Sobolev spaces of [F1]. Use an integer order at least four; local bootstrap identifies the same solutions at every higher order.

1.1 **The actual cylindrical quotient and linearization.** Represent a relative class by a smooth connection $A_0$ constant at its critical ends and complete $A-A_0$ in $H^k$. Complete determinant-one gauges tending to central constants in $H^{k+1}$ after subtracting those constants. The gauge Laplacian $d_A^*d_A$ has a bounded inverse on these decaying adjoint zero-forms. Indeed the limiting three-dimensional irreducible gauge Laplacians have a positive gap on the compact $Y$. Their end inequalities, a partition and Rellich on a compact middle show that a normalized sequence with $\|d_Au_n\|_2\to0$ would have an $L^2$ parallel limit with no mass escaping through the ends. Such a parallel limit is zero, whereas the normalization and end coercivity retain norm one. Lax–Milgram and [F1]'s local weak bootstrap give the inverse at every required order. Thus $H^k$ one-forms split into $\ker d_A^*$ and the image of this gauge derivative, and the inverse theorem gives local quotient slices. The arbitrary-gauge equation $du=uB-Au$, its local product bootstrap, and the end gauge gaps give compactness of gauges joining converging representatives: on a compact middle use Rellich, and on the ends the small representative tails and the gap bound $u$ minus its limiting central sign; these tails tend uniformly to zero. Hence the quotient is Hausdorff and has a countable atlas inherited from the separable affine space. The section $F_A^++\widehat V(A)$ is smooth $H^k\to H^{k-1}$. Its gauge-fixed derivative is Fredholm by [F1], with the full perturbed Hessians on the ends. These endpoint Hessians, not unperturbed flat ones, are used. [F1, F2, F3, F4, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 **Cauchy uniqueness and an embedded trajectory arc.** In temporal gauge the equation is $B'=-G(B)$, $G=*F+V$. Its derivative is $H_B=*d_B+DV_B$, symmetric for the $L^2$ metric, since it is the local Hessian of the real function $CS+f$. On the full space of one-forms, the curl $*d_{B_0}$ has a selfadjoint closure even though its gauge directions are not elliptic: local mollification and a partition approximate its adjoint-domain sections in graph norm, because the first-order coefficient commutator tends to zero in $L^2$ by the same differentiated-mollifier calculation as [F1]. Symmetry then identifies the closure with the adjoint. On any compact time interval the difference of two smooth gradient solutions satisfies $w'=-H(t)w$ with $H(t)=*d_{B_0}+R(t)$, where $R(t)$ and $R'(t)$ are bounded selfadjoint $L^2$ operators, obtained by integrating the Hessians along the segment between the two solutions. If $f=\|w\|_2^2>0$, put $a=\langle Hw,w\rangle/f$. Differentiation gives $a'=\langle H'w,w\rangle/f-2(\|Hw\|_2^2/f-a^2)\le\|H'\|$, hence $(\log f)''\ge-2\|H'\|$. Convexity after adding a fixed quadratic term implies that if $w$ vanishes at one time it vanishes identically: interpolate between that time and any positive-norm time and let the first endpoint tend to the zero. This proves Cauchy uniqueness, including for the adjoint equation with reversed time. Consequently a nonconstant trajectory has nonzero velocity at every finite time. If two of its times represented the same gauge orbit, a constant-in-time gauge and this uniqueness would make it periodic modulo gauge. Its nonzero flow energy would then repeat on infinitely many periods, contrary to finite total energy. Thus its quotient path is injective; every compact subarc is embedded, and the critical-end convergence in the defining cylindrical norm makes the rest of its image compact in the $C^0$ holonomy topology. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 **A genuine allowed parameter space preserving the critical jets.** Two inequivalent smooth connections are separated by a finite common-base holonomy tuple. Otherwise compactness of the conjugating unitary group gives one simultaneous conjugator for all tuples; the path-transport construction in [F2] would then give a global determinant-one gauge. Thus a chosen noncritical point on an embedded trajectory arc can be separated, by finitely many tuples, from all critical points and from the compact remainder of that path outside a slightly larger arc. In the finite tuple's smooth conjugation quotient, choose a bump supported in the separating open set. Products of this bump with smooth tuple functions give cylinder functions vanishing on neighborhoods of every critical point. Choose countably many such data dense on all separating tuple charts, and choose weighted $\ell^1$ constants as in [F2]. Their sum space $\mathcal R$ is separable, its functions and every derivative vanish at each critical point, and all the preceding field bounds hold. There need not be one common neighborhood on which every infinite sum vanishes; flat critical jets are the exact condition used. Uniform derivative bounds in fixed critical slices preserve invertibility and uniqueness of their zeros for sufficiently small $\rho\in\mathcal R$. Properness of the critical projection in [F2] excludes any new critical points outside those slices for a sequence $\rho\to0$. Hence these small perturbations retain exactly the stipulated critical points and Hessians. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 **Every cokernel is detected by an actual holonomy variation.** At a smooth solution, let $(\phi,\omega)$ annihilate the universal gauge-fixed linearization. Gauge covariance gives $L_A d_A=0$ for the equation derivative $L_A$ at a zero. Applying $d_A^*$ to $d_A\phi+L_A^*\omega=0$ gives $d_A^*d_A\phi=0$, so step 1.1 gives $\phi=0$. Identify the self-dual two-form $\omega$ in temporal gauge with a one-form $u(t)$. Variations of the time component impose $d_B^*u=0$, and variations of the spatial component give $-u'+H_Bu=0$. Elliptic bootstrap and [F2]'s derivative bounds make $u$ smooth. Its pairing with $v=B'$ is constant, because $u'=H_Bu$ and $v'=-H_Bv$ with a symmetric Hessian. Both are $L^2$ over the cylinder, so this constant is zero. A nonzero $u$ is nonzero at every time by step 2.1; it is therefore transverse to the nonzero $v$ in the quotient tangent. At one selected time, [F2]'s infinitesimal holonomy detection chooses a finite tuple whose quotient differential is injective on their span. After shortening the arc, its tuple image is a smooth embedded arc and the image of $u$ has a nonzero transverse component. Use the separating tuple bump of step 3.1 and tubular coordinates around that finite-dimensional arc to choose an invariant smooth function zero along the arc whose differential pairs positively with $u$ on a smaller interval and is zero outside the larger interval. Its mollified cylinder derivative retains that strict positive integral by taking its transverse disk sufficiently small. Thus $\int\langle u,V_f(B)\rangle dt>0$, contradicting universal annihilation. Density of the countable data in $\mathcal R$ retains this nonzero pairing. Hence the full universal derivative is onto; no arbitrary time-dependent perturbation was substituted for these gradient cylinder functions. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 **All indices are regular at one chosen perturbation.** For any endpoint pair and relative class the universal zero locus is a smooth Banach manifold. To see the required complemented surjection explicitly, the connection-variable derivative is Fredholm; finitely many parameter directions from step 4.1 span its cokernel, giving a bounded right inverse, and the remaining parameter subspace is free. The implicit theorem [F4] applies. Projection to $\mathcal R$ is Fredholm of the trajectory index $I$: its kernel is the connection derivative's kernel and its cokernel is its finite cokernel, by the universal tangent equation. All spaces are Hausdorff and second countable by step 1.1, so Sard–Smale [F4] makes regular parameters residual for this class. There are only finitely many critical pairs and countably many relative classes by [F3]. Intersect their residual sets in the small Banach parameter ball. Its Baire property gives a residual dense set making every trajectory regular, not just the trajectories initially having index one or two. The critical jets remain unchanged by step 3.1. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 **Translation quotient and the actual nonpositive-index exclusion.** At a regular nonconstant solution the kernel has dimension $I$ and contains its nonzero velocity. Thus $I\ge1$. Time translation is free by step 2.1 and is proper on this nonconstant moduli space: if representatives and their translates converge in the strong cylindrical topology while translation lengths tend to infinity, the uniformly small tails of the converging first sequence force the translated middle to converge to a critical constant; it cannot converge to the proposed nonconstant second limit. Hence translation lengths stay bounded. A local slice for the action is obtained by the scalar condition $\langle A-A_*,\partial_tA_*\rangle_{L^2}=0$, whose translation derivative at the center is the nonzero squared velocity norm. The implicit theorem gives smooth slices, and properness makes their quotient Hausdorff; its countable atlas is inherited from the moduli atlas. Its dimension is $I-1$. The inequality $I\ge1$ excludes every nonconstant nonpositive-index solution at the selected perturbation, which is the exact limiting-level regularity input needed later. This proves all assertions without drawing any compactification or orientation conclusion. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
