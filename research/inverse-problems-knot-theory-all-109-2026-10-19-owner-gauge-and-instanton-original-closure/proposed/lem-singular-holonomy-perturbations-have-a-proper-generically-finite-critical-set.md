## Statement

Assume full AC. Supply a compact oriented three-dimensional order-two singular pair $(Y,K)$, compatible orbifold bundle data and determinant-one gauges as in the preceding completion lemma, and a fixed $U(2)$ lift on $Y\setminus K$ with fixed determinant connection. Suppose every connection in this singular configuration space has stabilizer $\{\pm1\}$. Use $H^k/H^{k+1}$, $k\ge3$.

There is a separable Banach space $\mathcal P$ of countable weighted sums of smooth cylinder functions supported away from $K$. For $\pi\in\mathcal P$ its function $f_\pi$ is bounded, smooth and gauge invariant, and its $L^2$ gradient $V_\pi$ is a smooth gauge-equivariant $H^k$ vector field. It is uniformly pointwise bounded by $C\|\pi\|_{\mathcal P}$, independently of the connection; its derivative is bounded on $L^2$ with the same type of uniform bound. Its spatial covariant derivatives have polynomial bounds in lower spatial covariant derivatives of curvature, with constants uniform on bounded perturbation sets.

The critical-point projection for $*F_B+V_\pi(B)=0$ is proper over compact subsets of $\mathcal P$ in the actual $H^k$ quotient. An open dense subset of $\mathcal P$ has only nondegenerate critical points; their set is finite. All critical connections are smooth in orbifold gauges. This result proves the critical set and not transversality, compactness or gluing for nonconstant trajectories. The existence of the stipulated global singular data and $U(2)$ lift is not asserted here.

## Proof

**Given:** The fixed singular data, determinant lift and metric, and the absence of noncentral stabilizers in the Statement.

1.1 **An actual cylinder function and its gradient.** Choose smooth immersions $q_j:S^1\times D^2\to Y\setminus K$, $j=1,\ldots,l$, agreeing on a neighborhood of $\{p\}\times D^2$ for a chosen $p\in S^1$, and a smooth compactly supported two-form $\eta$ on the interior of $D^2$. Their fixed lifted determinant trivializations identify the $l$ holonomies based on that common disk with a tuple in $U(2)^l$, up to simultaneous conjugation. The local Chern–Simons potential has differential $b\mapsto\int\langle *F_B,b\rangle$: in an affine chart its difference from the value at $B$ is $-\int\operatorname{tr}(b\wedge F_B+\tfrac12b\wedge d_Bb+\tfrac13b^3)$ in the fundamental trace normalization. Differentiate this polynomial and integrate by parts on the full lifted charts; invariant partition localization cancels artificial chart boundaries. Thus its local Hessian is symmetric, without assuming a global single-valued Chern–Simons functional. For a smooth conjugation-invariant real $h$, set $f(B)=\int_{D^2}h(\operatorname{Hol}_{q_1,x}B,\ldots,\operatorname{Hol}_{q_l,x}B)\eta(x)$. The transport variation [F2] gives its directional derivative by replacing one holonomy by $-U(1)\int_0^1U(t)^{-1}b(\partial_tq_j)U(t)dt$. On the compact support each $q_j$ is a local diffeomorphism with bounded inverse Jacobian and finite multiplicity. Change variables on a finite collection of its injective pieces. The resulting integral $\int_Y\langle V_f(B),b\rangle$ explicitly defines the gradient: it is a finite sum of transported bounded derivatives of $h$, multiplied by the fixed contraction of $\eta$ with the volume density. This proves $\|V_f(B)\|_\infty\le C_f$, independent of $B$. A second variation has two loop integrals and bounded derivatives of $h$; Cauchy–Schwarz on their finite parameter intervals and change of variables prove $\|DV_f(B)b\|_2\le C_f\|b\|_2$. All constructions are gauge equivariant, since the determinant-one gauges conjugate the entire holonomy tuple at its common base disk. [F2, F4, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 **Covariant spatial estimates and an actual Banach perturbation space.** Differentiate the transported expression of step 1.1 in a spatial parameter. The transport identity gives endpoint covariant derivative equal to the integral of $U^{-1}F_B(\partial_s q,\partial_tq)U$; endpoint connection terms cancel when it is interpreted as a covariant derivative of the output field. Differentiation of the fixed immersion and density adds bounded geometric coefficients. Repeating this identity shows $\|\nabla_B^jV_f(B)\|_\infty\le P_{f,j}(1+\|F_B\|_\infty+\cdots+\|\nabla_B^{j-1}F_B\|_\infty)$, with a finite polynomial for every $j\ge1$. The fixed determinant curvature adds only fixed smooth terms. In a fixed local connection chart, differentiating the original ordinary differential equation instead gives smooth maps $H^k\to H^k$ with all connection derivatives bounded on every bounded $H^k$ set; Fubini and the local-diffeomorphism parameter changes bound each integral of a Sobolev derivative by its spatial Sobolev norm. Products are bounded by the $H^k$ algebra, since $k>3/2$. Choose a countable collection $f_i$ dense among the choices of immersed loop tuples, smooth test two-forms and invariant smooth $h$ in their $C^\infty$ topologies. Such a collection can be made by finitely many coordinate charts, rational meshes and polynomial approximations followed by invariant averaging over unit quaternions; their normalized sphere measure is invariant because quaternion multiplication is orthogonal in $\mathbb R^4$. Immersion and common-base-disk conditions are preserved by making the approximations within their fixed collars. Choose positive $C_i$ at least $2^i$ times one plus every constant for the first $i$ spatial and connection derivatives, first $i$ integer Sobolev orders and connection balls, and their polynomial coefficients. Then $\mathcal P=\{(\pi_i):\sum C_i|\pi_i|<\infty\}$ is a separable Banach space and $f_\pi=\sum\pi_i f_i$, $V_\pi=\sum\pi_i V_{f_i}$ converge in all those seminorms. For each fixed seminorm the tail is bounded by its corresponding weighted $\ell^1$ tail; the finitely many initial terms cause no problem. This proves all mapping and covariance bounds, with uniform constants on bounded perturbation sets. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 **Critical curvature controls every derivative.** At a critical point $F_B=-*V_\pi(B)$, so step 2.1 bounds $\|F_B\|_\infty$ uniformly for bounded $\pi$. Its first covariant derivative is bounded by the same formula for $j=1$, and induction bounds every $\nabla_B^jF_B$. These estimates are valid on the full invariant charts: each cylinder function is supported away from $K$, hence the critical connection is exactly flat near $K$, and the zero extension of its perturbing field is smooth there. For a countable collection whose supports approach $K$, the same assertion is replaced by the already proved uniform covariant estimates, with each summand and all derivatives zero at $K$; the chosen seminorm bounds make their sum smooth with all jets zero there. Initial $H^k$ critical points also bootstrap: $H^k$ in dimension three embeds in $C^1$, so the transport variation first gives $F_B$ with one continuous covariant derivative. In radial gauge the identity $a_i(x)=\int_0^1t x^j(F_B)^g_{ji}(tx)dt$ follows by differentiating $x\cdot a=0$ and the curvature formula. It upgrades the lifted connection by one derivative; differentiation of the transport expression and this radial identity iterate. Thus the critical connections are smooth in invariant gauges and satisfy all stated bounds. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.2 **Cylinder derivatives detect every non-gauge infinitesimal direction.** At a smooth irreducible $B$, suppose a smooth tangent $b$ annihilates the differentials of all cylinder functions. Before mollifying on $D^2$, shrinking its test form to a loop tuple shows that the derivative of every finite holonomy tuple is tangent to its simultaneous conjugation orbit. In detail, choose two based loops whose lifted holonomies do not commute; they exist because otherwise the holonomy group preserves a quaternionic axis and gives a noncentral stabilizer. Their common infinitesimal conjugation stabilizer is zero. On the product of their two conjugacy matrices and any further finite tuple, the conjugation orbit is a compact embedded submanifold. A normal-slice coordinate chart and averaging a bump over the compact conjugation group give invariant smooth functions with arbitrary transverse differential. Annihilation therefore forces the tuple derivative to be a pure simultaneous commutator. The infinitesimal conjugator is unique because of the first two loops, and is the same for every further loop. Subtract the corresponding infinitesimal gauge at the basepoint. All based loop holonomy derivatives now vanish. Differentiate path parallel transport to define a section $\xi$ at each endpoint; changing paths changes it by precisely that zero loop derivative. Hence it is globally well-defined and satisfies $b=d_B\xi$. This section extends over each singular chart by the same smooth ordinary differential equation for the lifted smooth $B,b$ along straight segments; its invariance holds off the singular set and then by continuity everywhere. Conversely a gauge direction annihilates each invariant function. Countable dense cylinder data suffice by continuity of these derivatives under smooth loop, test-form and $h$ approximation. Thus the countable family separates every nonzero vector in any finite-dimensional Coulomb tangent subspace. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 **Properness on the actual determinant-one quotient.** Cover the compact orbifold by finitely many smaller lifted balls. In each ball put a sequence of critical connections with bounded perturbation parameters in radial gauge; the ordered-integral gauge is equivariant because radial paths commute with the constant local isotropy. The identity in step 3.1 and its differentiated version bound all ordinary derivatives of $a$ uniformly: a derivative of the conjugated curvature is its covariant derivative plus a commutator with the already bounded potential, and induction closes. Transition maps take values in compact $SO(3)$, and $dg=g a_j-a_i g$ bounds all of their derivatives. A finite diagonal subsequence gives smooth limits of forms and transition maps, preserving their exact cocycle and isotropy identities. Close smooth cocycles are actually isomorphic: average their local comparison maps with a finite invariant partition, then take $T(T^*T)^{-1/2}$; the positive matrix inverse square root is its convergent power series near the identity. The matrix has positive determinant, so this is an actual $SO(3)$ bundle isomorphism. It converges smoothly and identifies the limiting connection with the fixed orbifold bundle. The possible obstruction to lifting a comparison to the stipulated determinant-one gauges is finite: it is the sign cocycle of local quaternionic lifts on a finite cover of the compact exterior, in finite $H^1(-;\mathbb Z/2)$, with finitely many further deck signs on the components of $K$. On a subsequence the relative obstruction of $\phi_n:P_\infty\to P$ is fixed; fix one comparison $\phi_1$ in that class. Then $\phi_n\phi_1^{-1}$ is an actual determinant-one gauge of $P$, and its pullback connections converge to $(\phi_1^{-1})^*B_\infty$. Thus the subsequence converges in the actual determinant-one $H^k$ quotient, not merely in the larger $SO(3)$ quotient. If $\pi_n$ lies in a compact subset of $\mathcal P$, take a convergent parameter subsequence and use step 2.1's continuous field limits to retain the critical equation. This proves properness. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

4.2 **The actual critical Hessian and finite cokernel directions.** At a critical point, the derivative of the gradient on the Coulomb slice is selfadjoint for $L^2$: this is equality of the two second variations of the explicitly smooth real function $CS+f_\pi$. Gauge invariance makes it vanish on $d_B\xi$, and selfadjointness makes its image orthogonal to all such directions. The extended gauge-fixed block is the operator of [F1] plus the bounded selfadjoint $DV_\pi$ of step 1.1, acting only on one-forms. It is Fredholm and selfadjoint, since the perturbation is bounded on $L^2$ and its $H^k\to H^{k-1}$ realization is compact by Rellich. Split one-forms as $d_BH^{k+1}\oplus\ker d_B^*$. The gauge block $d_B^*d_B$ is invertible by irreducibility and [F1], so the restricted Coulomb Hessian is Fredholm of index zero, with equal smooth kernel and cokernel. For any nonzero member $b$ of that finite cokernel, step 3.2 gives some cylinder differential with nonzero pairing with $b$. Finitely many of the gradients therefore span its dual and hence its quotient target. Varying just their coefficients makes the augmented critical equation onto. The implicit theorem provides a local finite-dimensional universal zero manifold. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra]

5.1 **Finite Sard gives global critical nondegeneracy.** For a fixed $\pi$, its critical set is compact by step 4.1. Finitely many neighborhoods and finitely many coefficient directions from step 4.2 make the universal derivative onto near all of it. For sufficiently small finite parameters there are no other critical points: otherwise a sequence of vanishing parameters and critical points outside these neighborhoods would have, by step 4.1, a limiting original critical point inside them. The resulting universal zero manifold has dimension the number $N$ of finite parameters, because the slice Fredholm index is zero. Projection to the parameter ball is regular exactly when the connection-variable Hessian is onto, by the tangent equation $Tv+Sq=0$ and surjectivity of $(T,S)$. Sard [F3] in its countable finite-dimensional atlas gives regular parameters arbitrarily close to zero. If $N=0$ the original Hessians already are onto. This proves density in $\mathcal P$. Openness follows from the right-inverse Neumann series at its finite-dimensional Fredholm zeros and the same properness argument excluding new zeros for perturbations tending to $\pi$. A nondegenerate critical point is isolated by the implicit theorem, so its compact critical set is finite. This proves the full critical-set assertion, retaining the global singular-bundle construction and every nonconstant-trajectory obligation outside this result. [F1, F3, step 4.1, step 3.2, step 4.2, construct, algebra] ∎

## Source notes

The actual full primary passage read for this result is Kronheimer–Mrowka §3.4, PDF30–32, including its reference to analytic properties in the earlier instanton paper. Those imported properties are reconstructed by the explicit transport, curvature-derivative and finite-parameter arguments above. The argument uses the stipulated $U(2)$ lift to retain the determinant-one holonomy tuples; unrestricted $SO(3)$ Wilson functions would identify additional discrete gauge symmetries and need not give the same local detection argument. The global lift and the earring's no-reducible-configuration hypothesis must be established from the actual knot data before this supplier can close that portion of the original complex.
