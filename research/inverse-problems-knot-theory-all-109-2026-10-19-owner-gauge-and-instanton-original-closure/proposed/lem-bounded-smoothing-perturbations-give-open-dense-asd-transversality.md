## Statement

Assume full AC. Use the unbased $H^3/H^4$ irreducible connection quotient on a closed Riemannian four-manifold and its $H^2$ anti-self-dual curvature bundle. Let $U$ be an open subset covered by finitely many Coulomb slices centered at smooth irreducible connections $A_i$. In each of these charts use a bounded open ball of radius $R_i$ in $S_i=\ker d_{A_i}^*\subset H^3$. Let $V$ be open, with its closure contained in the union of the images of smaller closed balls of radii $r_i<R_i$, all these images lying in $U$.

Allow smooth sections $\sigma$ supported in $U$, whose representatives in the specified charts are smooth maps into $H^3$ and have finite uniform $H^3$ norm on these chart balls. Interpret them in the $H^2$ equation bundle by inclusion. Give this class the topology generated by the uniform $H^3$ section norm on the finitely many chart balls and the $C^1(H^3,H^2)$ seminorms on compact subsets of those balls. Then the perturbations for which $\Phi+\sigma$, $\Phi([A])=F_A^-$, is transverse to zero at every zero in $\overline V$ form an open dense subset. The zero set in $V$ is a smooth finite-dimensional manifold of dimension equal to the unbased ASD index. In particular that dimension is five in the simply connected positive-definite charge $c_2=-1$ case.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] The unbased quotient, smooth equation bundle, gauge action, slice representatives and compactness of connecting gauges are supplied by [[lem-high-sobolev-coulomb-slices-for-irreducible-su-two-connections]]. Its proof gives $H^3$ multiplication, $H^3\cdot H^2\subset H^2$, and the bounded inverse $d_A^*d_A:H^4\to H^2$ at an irreducible center.

[F2] At a smooth center $D_A=d_A^*\oplus\sqrt2d_A^-$ is a geometric Dirac-type operator, with scalar-principal Laplace-type square. Its index and chirality are supplied by [[thm-asd-deformation-complex-index-formula]]. The Green estimates give $\|a\|_{H^4}\le C(\|D_Aa\|_{H^3}+\|a\|_2)$ and compact $H^4\to H^3$ ([[thm-sobolev-compactness-and-elliptic-estimates-give-fredholm-hodge-decomposition]]).

[F3] Compact perturbations preserve Fredholmness and index; Fredholm operators have bounded kernel/range complements. The Banach implicit theorem applies to a surjection with a complemented kernel. [[thm-fredholm-index-is-stable-under-compact-perturbations]] [[lem-fredholm-splitting-and-parametrix]] [[thm-implicit-function-theorem-for-banach-spaces]]

[F4] A smooth map between finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces has a null set of critical values. Apply this in a countable atlas under full AC. [[thm-morse-sard-for-euclidean-maps]] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

## Proof

**Given:** Full AC, the finite bounded slice cover, its smaller radii, $V$, and the specified smoothing perturbation class and topology.

1.1 **Fredholmness in the actual spaces.** The Green identities for the smooth-center Dirac square in [F2] supply a parametrix $H^2\to H^3$ with finite-rank smooth remainders: use $D_A^*G$ and the Green bounds at order two. Thus the $H^3\to H^2$ realization has the same smooth kernel and cokernel and index as its $H^1\to L^2$ realization. Decompose $H^3=S_A\oplus d_AH^4$ by [F1]. In these coordinates the upper block of $D_A$ on the second summand is the invertible $d_A^*d_A$; its lower block there is $\sqrt2[F_A^-,v]$, a compact $H^4\to H^2$ map by smooth multiplication and compact inclusion. Hence the lower block $d_A^-:S_A\to H^2(\Lambda^-\otimes\operatorname{ad}P)$ is Fredholm with index $\operatorname{ind}D_A$. In a fixed slice $\Phi(a)=F_A^-+d_A^-a+(a\wedge a)^-$; its differential differs from $d_A^-$ by multiplication by the $H^3$ form $a$, which maps $H^3$ boundedly to $H^3$ and then compactly to $H^2$. The same is true of $D\sigma$, since $\sigma$ is smooth into $H^3$. Thus $D(\Phi+\sigma)$ is Fredholm of the stated index at every point. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 **A compact set containing all possible nearby zeros.** Fix $M<\infty$. Suppose a permitted perturbation $\tau$ has chartwise $H^3$ norm at most $M$, and $[A_i+a]\in\overline V$ is a zero represented in the smaller $i$th ball. The equation gives $d_{A_i}^-a=-F_{A_i}^--(a\wedge a)^--\tau_i(a)$, whose $H^3$ norm is bounded by a constant depending only on $M$, $r_i$ and the fixed center, since $H^3$ is an algebra. Also $d_{A_i}^*a=0$. [F2] bounds $\|a\|_{H^4}$ uniformly. The closure in $H^3$ of the resulting bounded $H^4$ set intersected with $\|a\|_{H^3}\le r_i$ is compact and lies inside the larger chart ball. Take the finite union of its chart images and intersect with the closed set $\overline V$, obtaining a compact set $K_M\subset U$ containing every such zero. Any sequence of zeros, even with varying $\tau$ subject to this bound, therefore has a subsequence converging in one of these fixed compact chart sets. No compactness of an open subset of an infinite-dimensional manifold has been assumed. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 **Openness.** Let $\sigma$ be transverse at all its zeros in $\overline V$, and choose $M$ greater than its uniform chart norm by one. All zeros of perturbations uniformly within one of $\sigma$ lie in $K_M$. The zeros of $\Phi+\sigma$ there form a compact set $Z$. For a surjective bounded operator choose the right inverse supplied by [F3]; if $\|(T'-T)R\|<1$, the convergent Neumann series inverts $T'R$, so $T'$ remains onto. Continuity and finitely many neighborhoods of $Z$ therefore give an open set $O$ containing $Z$ and a positive derivative tolerance for surjectivity throughout $O\cap K_M$. On $K_M\setminus O$ the continuous norm of $\Phi+\sigma$ has a positive minimum, unless that compact set is empty. Small uniform section change excludes new zeros there. Small compact-set derivative change on the finitely many compact chart representatives of $K_M$ preserves surjectivity at every remaining zero. These are finitely many of the stated seminorm conditions, so the good set is open. If $Z$ is empty, the positive-minimum argument alone suffices. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 **Finite-dimensional perturbation directions.** For arbitrary allowed $\sigma$, its zero set $Z$ in $\overline V$ is compact by step 2.1. At each zero, the range of $D(\Phi+\sigma)$ has finite codimension by step 1.1. Smooth target sections are dense in $H^2$, so choose finitely many $H^3$ target vectors whose images span this quotient. Extend them as constant vectors in that slice trivialization, multiplied by a smooth radial Hilbert-space bump equal to one near the zero and supported in a smaller norm ball with closure inside $U$. The zero extension is smooth. On overlap charts their $H^3$ norms are uniformly bounded: the connecting gauges have uniform $H^4$ bounds on bounded $H^3$ chart balls by [F1], and their action on $H^3$ is bounded. These are therefore allowed sections. The augmented derivative is onto near the chosen zero by the right-inverse argument of step 3.1. Finitely many such constructions cover $Z$, giving sections $s_1,\ldots,s_N$ and an open neighborhood $O$ of $Z$ on which $(a,p)\mapsto\Phi(a)+\sigma(a)+\sum p_js_j(a)$ has surjective derivative for all sufficiently small $p$. By step 2.1 and the positive-minimum argument on $K_M\setminus O$, shrinking the parameter ball ensures that every zero in $\overline V$ lies in $O$. If $Z$ is empty, $\sigma$ already has the desired property. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 **Finite Sard and the conclusion.** The augmented zero locus over $O$ and the small parameter ball is a smooth manifold of dimension $d+N$, where $d$ is the index from step 1.1: its derivative is onto, has a finite-dimensional kernel, and [F3] applies. It is second countable as a subspace of a finite product of the quotient and Euclidean parameter space. The projection to $\mathbb R^N$ is regular at a zero exactly when the derivative in the connection variable is onto. Indeed its tangent equation is $Tv+S q=0$; surjectivity of the full map $(T,S)$ implies that every $q$ admits such a $v$ exactly when $\operatorname{ran}S\subset\operatorname{ran}T$, equivalently $\operatorname{ran}T$ is the whole target. If $N=0$, the augmentation was unnecessary and $\sigma$ is already transverse. If $N>0$, [F4] in a countable atlas gives regular parameters arbitrarily close to zero. The resulting perturbations converge to $\sigma$ in the specified topology, since there are only finitely many fixed smooth bounded sections $s_j$ and their derivatives are bounded on every compact set. Their zeros on $\overline V$ are transverse by step 4.1, proving density. The implicit theorem gives the asserted $d$-manifold on $V$; [F2] gives $d=5$ in the positive-definite simply connected charge-one case. Together with step 3.1 this proves every assertion. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra] ∎

## Source notes

Donaldson's complete Lemma 9 proof, printed p.293, was read together with its perturbation class on p.292. Its compactness mechanism is the extra derivative in the perturbation value, not an assertion that a Hilbert-manifold open set is relatively compact. The proof here makes explicit the finite bounded slice collars and the uniform section control used for openness. It does not infer transversality for arbitrary compact-derivative perturbations in a critical Sobolev quotient.
