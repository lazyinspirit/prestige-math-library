## Statement

Assume full AC. Let $X$ be a closed connected simply connected oriented smooth Riemannian four-manifold with positive-definite intersection form, and fix a smooth $SU(2)$ bundle $P$ with $c_2(P)[X]=-1$. The unperturbed self-dual moduli space has a neighborhood of its ideal stratum consisting precisely of the flat connection and one charge-one point, and this neighborhood, including the ideal stratum, is diffeomorphic in its interior and homeomorphic up to that stratum to $X\times[0,\lambda_0)$. Its interior consists of irreducible regular solutions. The parameter on the second factor is a smooth positive concentration scale tending to zero at the ideal stratum. Every sufficiently concentrating solution occurs, and occurs once. In particular this is a complete end theorem, not merely existence of a five-parameter family. It does not assert that arbitrary perturbations preserve this end.

## Proof

**Given:** The manifold, bundle, charge and unperturbed equation of the Statement. All smallness choices depend on this fixed metric, not on a particular concentrating solution.

1.1 **All ideal limits and a normalized bubble.** The energy is $8\pi^2$. By [F2], a noncompact sequence has a positive integer atom. There can be exactly one atom, of charge one, and the remaining smooth connection has zero energy, hence is flat. A flat connection on the simply connected $X$ has a global parallel frame: transport from a fixed fiber along paths is independent of the path, by the transport variation identity with zero curvature and a contracting homotopy; the local radial path formula makes this frame smooth. Thus the flat orbit is unique. Fix a nonnegative radial bump $\beta$, equal to one near zero, zero outside $(-1,1)$ and strictly decreasing on its nonconstant positive-radius part. Put $R_A(x,t)=\int_X\beta(d(x,y)/t)|F_A(y)|^2$, for $t$ smaller than a fixed normal radius. Let $K$ be the value of $t$ for which $R_I(0,t)=4\pi^2$ for the scale-one BPST connection. Define $\lambda(A)=K^{-1}\min\{t:R_A(x,t)=4\pi^2\text{ for some }x\}$, and choose a minimizer $x_A$. This definition is used on the neighborhood where some ball within the fixed normal radius has weighted energy greater than $4\pi^2$, as holds near every ideal point. On this neighborhood the minimum exists by compactness and continuity, and is positive for each smooth connection. Along a noncompact sequence it tends to zero; conversely a sequence with scale tending to zero cannot converge smoothly. Rescale about $x_A$ by $\lambda(A)$. There is no curvature atom on a bounded rescaled region: any such atom has the full energy $8\pi^2$, and a ball of arbitrarily small rescaled radius would then contain weighted energy $4\pi^2$, contradicting minimality of the scale. Compactness [F2] therefore gives smooth convergence on every bounded region to a nonflat Euclidean finite-energy connection. Nonflatness follows from $R(0,K)=4\pi^2$. Point removal at infinity and quantization show its energy is $8\pi^2$, so no energy is lost at infinity. Classification [F3] makes the limit a BPST connection. [F1, F2, F3, F7, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 **Unique smooth center and scale.** For the BPST density $h(y)=48(1+|y|^2)^{-4}$, $R_I(x,t)$ is strictly decreasing in $|x|>0$ at fixed $t$: write each radial decreasing factor as an integral of ball indicators; translating a ball away from the origin strictly decreases the integral against the strictly decreasing density when their boundaries overlap. Such overlapping levels form a positive interval for each nonzero displacement. Moreover $\partial_tR_I(0,t)>0$. Its Hessian in each center direction at zero is $-\tfrac{|S^3|}{4}\int_0^\infty t^{-1}\beta'(r/t)h'(r)r^3\,dr<0$, by integrating the radial Laplacian of $h$. Thus the implicitly defined radius $t(x)$ has its unique nondegenerate minimum at $0$. Any two minimizing centers for an actual connection are at distance at most $2K\lambda$: their radius-$K\lambda$ balls each contain strictly more than half the total energy, since normalized BPST convergence makes the curvature energy on the bump transition annulus positive for every sufficiently concentrating solution. They therefore intersect. In the normalization of step 1.1 all centers stay in a bounded ball. Smooth convergence of the densities makes $R_A$, its center derivatives and its radius derivatives converge there to those of the normalized BPST density. The limiting unique minimum fixes its center and scale to $0,1$; its nondegenerate Hessian and the strict absence of any other critical minimum on the remaining compact set imply that every sufficiently small-scale solution has one minimizing center. Consequently $p(A)=(x(A),\lambda(A))$ is smooth on a neighborhood in the configuration quotient. Differentiating the two equations $R_A(x,K\lambda)=4\pi^2$ and $\partial_xR_A(x,K\lambda)=0$ gives $|Dp_A(v)|\le C\lambda\|d_Av\|_2$: in normalized coordinates the inverse finite Jacobian is uniformly bounded, and the first variations are integrals of $2\langle F_A,d_Av\rangle$ against fixed bounded bump derivatives. Smooth coordinate changes give the same estimate on $X$. [F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 **Strong and weighted comparison with the actual preconnection.** In the preceding normalization the convergence to BPST is uniform in every fixed $C^j$ norm on a ball of radius $2R$. Otherwise a violating subsequence would have the same normalized BPST limit by steps 1.1–2.1. The exterior energy at radius $R\lambda$ tends, for fixed $R$, to the BPST exterior energy, which is $O(R^{-4})$. Apply [F4] with $s=R\lambda$. It gives a neck frame in which the potential, its derivative and the Green-weighted square integral are bounded by $C E(R\lambda)^{1/2}$ in their scale-invariant norms. On $X\setminus B_\rho(x)$ its pointwise curvature and all fixed covariant derivatives are $O(E(R\lambda)^{1/2}(R\lambda)^{7/4})$, by the same exterior-energy estimate and the fixed-radius balls of [F2]. There is a global frame there with connection coefficients and derivatives bounded by this quantity. Here is the needed global-frame construction: the complement of a ball is simply connected by van Kampen; choose a fixed finite coordinate cover, paths from a common point to its chart centers, and radial paths within each chart. Their transport frames have covariant derivative bounded by $C\|F\|_\infty$. At finitely many reference points of the overlaps the relative loops contract; smooth finite homotopies have finite area, so the transport variation formula bounds their holonomy by $C\|F\|_\infty$. The derivative formula then gives that same bound throughout the overlaps. A partition-weighted quaternionic average, divided by its positive scalar quaternionic norm, is a global frame, just as in [F4]. Differentiating the variation formula gives its higher coefficient bounds. A finite collar-chart refinement makes the number of overlaps finite; constants are uniform for the compact family of ball centers, using finitely many supported coordinate transports. Begin the radial neck frame with this exterior frame. On $R\lambda<r<2R\lambda$ its transition to the core BPST singular frame has derivative $O((E(R\lambda)^{1/2}+R^{-2})/(R\lambda))$; this follows from $dh=h a_{\mathrm{neck}}-a_{\mathrm{core}}h$ and the two small singular potentials. After multiplying one frame by its value at a point, the transition is uniformly close to one. Write it as $\exp u$ and interpolate $u$ with a fixed annular cutoff. This gives an actual bundle isomorphism from the preconnection bundle to $P$, with cutoff derivative bounded by $C/(R\lambda)$. In that isomorphism $A=T_{x,\lambda}+b$ and $\|b\|_{C_T}\to0$, uniformly as $\lambda\to0$. Explicitly, the core errors tend to zero after scaling for each fixed $R$, the transition errors in all three terms of $C_T$ are $O(E(R\lambda)^{1/2}+R^{-2})$, the neck errors obey [F4], and the exterior errors tend to zero by the fixed-frame bound. First let $\lambda\to0$, then $R\to\infty$. The cutoff BPST potential itself has the same summable neck estimates. The fixed scalar kernel satisfies $G\le C(1+d^{-2})$, so [F4]'s Euclidean endpoint bound is exactly the needed manifold bound up to a uniform constant. This proves weighted comparison, not merely critical Sobolev comparison. [F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 **Uniform regularity for every solution in the end.** For any such self-dual $A$, $2P_A=\nabla_A^*\nabla_A+s/3-2W^-$ on adjoint-valued anti-self-dual two-forms; the $F_A^+$ terms cancel exactly. There is a uniform positive gap for all sufficiently concentrating solutions. Indeed, if $\|\xi_n\|_2=1$ and $\|(d_{A_n}^-)^*\xi_n\|_2\to0$, this identity bounds $\nabla_{A_n}\xi_n$ in $L^2$. The scalar Kato inequality and fixed Sobolev estimate bound $\xi_n$ in $L^4$, so their $L^2$ mass in a ball of radius $r$ is at most $Cr^2$, uniformly. In the exterior frames from step 3.1, Rellich gives a nonzero ordinary anti-self-dual harmonic two-form off the limiting point. A cutoff across the point has bounded gradient $L^4$ norm; multiplying that by the $L^4$ norm of the limiting form on its shrinking annulus tends to zero. Thus the limiting equation holds across the point. Positive definiteness gives $b^-=0$, a contradiction. This is the same no-mass argument as the forward proof, applied to every actual solution. Also all such solutions are irreducible: otherwise a unit parallel adjoint section on their normalized core would converge to a unit BPST-parallel section, impossible since the three curvature components at its center span $\mathfrak{su}(2)$. Now [F5] gives onto $d_A^-$ with a uniformly bounded $Q_A$ in the displayed norms. On a self-dual irreducible connection $d_A^-d_A=0$, and the images of $d_A$ and $(d_A^-)^*$ are orthogonal; the gauge-fixed cokernel is therefore zero, including its zero-form part. Index five and the slice theorem [F1] make this end a smooth five-manifold. [F1, F2, F3, F5, F6, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 **The center-scale map is a local diffeomorphism.** On the normalized BPST model take the four translation variations in curvature gauge and the dilation variation, $v_j=\iota_{e_j}F_I$ and $v_0=\iota_yF_I$. Cartan's covariant identity, the Bianchi identity and the self-dual equation show $d_I^-v_j=d_I^-v_0=0$; these are the derivatives of translated and dilated connections after subtracting their infinitesimal gauge terms. They have normalized center-scale derivatives forming the identity after fixing their nonzero constant normalizations. Cut them off at radius $R$, transplant them to the actual core, and use the normalized convergence of step 3.1. Their defects $f_i=d_A^-v_i$ satisfy $\|f_i\|_B\le o_\lambda(1)+C R^{-2}$ for each fixed $R$. To check the tail term, the slowest model variation is the dilation, with $|v_0|\le C r^{-3}$ in its singular frame; its cutoff derivative on $R<r<2R$ gives $|f_0|\le C R^{-4}$. Its $L^2$ norm and the supremum of its $|z-y|^{-2}$ convolution are both at most $C R^{-2}$ by scaling. Translation variations decay faster. All other error terms are bounded by normalized smooth convergence on this fixed core. Correct them by $\tau_i=v_i-Q_A f_i$. They solve $d_A^-\tau_i=0$. The bound [F5] and the derivative estimate in step 2.1 show that their normalized center-scale derivatives still form an invertible matrix, first choosing $R$ large and then $\lambda$ small. Hence $Dp$ has rank five on the actual tangent quotient. Its dimension is five by step 4.1, so $p$ is a local diffeomorphism. [F1, F3, F5, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 **Properness and the covering, without assuming inverse gluing.** Restrict to $0<\lambda<\lambda_0$ small enough for all preceding uniform conclusions. A sequence whose images lie in a compact subset of $X\times(0,\lambda_0)$ has an ideal subsequence by [F2]. An ideal point would force $\lambda\to0$ by step 1.1, which the compact image excludes. Thus it converges smoothly up to gauge; its limiting small-scale solution is still irreducible and is in this same open scale region. Therefore $p$ is proper. Its image is open by step 5.1 and closed by properness, hence is all the connected product, since [F6] gives a nonempty small-scale image. A fiber is compact and discrete, hence finite. Choose disjoint local-diffeomorphism neighborhoods at its points. Properness excludes any further sheets entering a sufficiently small common image neighborhood, by taking a converging violating sequence. This proves directly that $p$ is a finite covering. No assertion that the constructed family already exhausts the moduli space has been used. [F2, F6, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 **A genuine projected path joins any two points in a fiber.** Let $p(A)=p(B)=(x,\lambda)$. Step 3.1 identifies both bundles with the same actual $T_{x,\lambda}$ bundle and makes $b=B-A$ small in $C_A$, uniformly as $\lambda\to0$. The $C_A$ and $C_T$ derivative norms are equivalent here by $\nabla_Av-\nabla_Tv=[A-T,v]$ and Holder, and the other two norm terms are unchanged. Put $C_t=A+t b$. Since both endpoints are self-dual, $E_t=F_{C_t}^-=(t^2-t)(b\wedge b)^-$. Seek a self-dual path $D_t=C_t+Q_A f_t$. Its exact equation is $f_t=-E_t-2(tb\wedge Q_Af_t)^--(Q_Af_t\wedge Q_Af_t)^-$. All products have the actual $B$ estimates of [F5], so this is a uniform contraction on a ball of radius $C\|b\|_{C_A}^2$ when $\|b\|_{C_A}$ is sufficiently small. Its unique fixed point depends continuously on $t$, is zero at $0,1$, and gives a smooth connection by the same coupled weak bootstrap as [F5], with the additional fixed smooth coefficient $tb$. It is a continuous path in the strong configuration topology: locally in $t$, differentiating this fixed-point equation gives an invertible $B$ operator by the same contraction bound; its solutions bootstrap with their smooth coefficient and right side, giving the inverse on each higher Sobolev space. The Banach implicit theorem there identifies these smooth branches with the unique $B$ branch. The path stays close to $A$ in $C_A$, and its curvature variation in $L^2$ tends uniformly to zero. The finite bump equations from step 2.1, or their uniform inverse-Jacobian estimate, give $d_X(x(D_t),x)+|\lambda(D_t)-\lambda|=o(\lambda)$. Thus the whole projected path lies in a product coordinate ball of radius $o(\lambda)$ about $(x,\lambda)$, with positive scale. All $D_t$ remain irreducible by their BPST core and lie in the same end region. [F1, F3, F5, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 **One sheet and the ideal collar.** The product ball in step 7.1 is simply connected, after choosing $\lambda_0$ small. Under the covering of step 6.1 a path joining two points in its central fiber would lift a nullhomotopic loop in that ball; path and homotopy lifting [F7] force the endpoints of the lift to agree. Hence $A=B$ in the quotient. Every fiber has one point, so $p$ is a diffeomorphism from the entire small-scale moduli region to $X\times(0,\lambda_0)$. Extend it by assigning the unique ideal connection with atom at $x$ to $(x,0)$. Compactness and step 1.1 identify convergence to these ideal points exactly with convergence of these coordinates, in both directions. The extension is therefore a homeomorphism of ideal neighborhoods, supplying a smooth collar structure at the new $X$ boundary by these coordinates. This proves actual exhaustion, uniqueness and the compactified end, while making no claim about arbitrary perturbations. [F2, F7, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, construct, algebra] ∎
