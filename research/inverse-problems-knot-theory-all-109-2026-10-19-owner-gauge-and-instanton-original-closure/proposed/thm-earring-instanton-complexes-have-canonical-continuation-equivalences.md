## Statement

Assume full AC. Integral earring instanton complexes formed from any two regular orbifold metrics and allowed regular holonomy perturbations of the fixed based earring data are related by continuation chain maps. A map depends only on its endpoints up to chain homotopy and the declared determinant-orientation sign choices. Continuation maps compose up to chain homotopy, and the maps in reverse directions are inverse chain homotopy equivalences. Transport under an isotopy of the based knot and its earring data gives the corresponding homology isomorphism. This statement concerns the singular instanton complex; it makes no assertion about sutured-closure independence, a knot-detection theorem or an instanton surgery formula.

## Proof

**Given:** Two regular endpoint metrics and holonomy perturbations on the same constructed earring orbifold and determinant-one bundle data.

1.1 **The actual continuation equation and its uniform budget.** Join the metrics by a smooth path constant outside a finite time interval, using convex combinations of their positive invariant metric tensors. Join the perturbations in their actual weighted Banach space, also with stationary ends, and allow further compact-time-supported cylinder perturbations. The equation is $B'+\operatorname{grad}_{g(t)}(CS+f_t)(B)=0$. All metrics in a fixed compact parameter family have equivalent Sobolev norms and uniform elliptic constants on the finite chart cover. The principal symbol on the middle may vary; the compact elliptic parametrix is constructed for that actual smooth metric, with the same invariant difference-quotient proof as [F1]. The stationary end inverses remain the endpoint full perturbed Hessians. This gives a Fredholm operator, independent of any claim that the middle connection is a flat one. Differentiating the functional gives $\frac d{dt}(CS+f_t)(B)=-\|B'\|_{g(t)}^2+\partial_t f_t(B)$. The functions and their parameter derivatives are uniformly bounded, and $\partial_t f_t$ has a common finite time support. Hence flow energy is bounded by its endpoint topological difference plus a uniform constant. The gauge periods of that difference are still $\pi^2$ times the index periods of [F2], since neither gauge period depends on the metric or compact-time deformation. Fixing reference paths for the finitely many endpoint pairs therefore gives $E\le\pi^2I+C$ and a uniform lower index bound. There are finitely many classes in every index band, by the same component calculation in [F2]. These estimates are uniform for compact one- or two-parameter families. [F1, F2, F3, A1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 **Regular families relative their stationary collars.** Use a countable dense space of time-dependent cylinder functions supported in the middle, weighted to control all finite parameter derivatives; require the fixed data on prescribed parameter-boundary collars. The universal cokernel argument of [F3] still applies, now with arbitrary time localization: a nonzero adjoint solution at a time in a free interval is horizontal, and finite holonomy derivatives detect that non-gauge vector. There is no velocity-orthogonality requirement, so constant solutions cause no exception. The needed Cauchy uniqueness also holds for varying metrics. Trivialize the time-varying $L^2$ metric by its smooth positive square root. The adjoint equation is then a symmetric first-order compact-$Y$ operator plus bounded terms. Remove its bounded skew part by its unitary ODE; its conjugated selfadjoint operator $H(t)$ has common graph domain and $|\langle H'u,u\rangle|\le C(\|Hu\|\|u\|+\|u\|^2)$ by the uniform first-order elliptic estimate. For a nonzero solution put $a=\langle Hu,u\rangle/\|u\|^2$. The differentiation in [F3], followed by $C\sqrt{\|Hu\|^2/\|u\|^2-a^2}\le(\|Hu\|^2/\|u\|^2-a^2)+C^2/4$, gives $a'\le C'(1+|a|)$. Thus $p=(\log\|u\|^2)'=-2a$ satisfies $p'\ge-C''(1+|p|)$. On every positive segment of $p$, comparison bounds $p$ above toward the left by its value at any later interior time; integrating forbids $\log\|u\|^2\to-\infty$ at a finite left endpoint. Reverse time for a right endpoint. Bounded symmetric terms are absorbed into $H$, and the skew conjugation retains the same relative estimate, since its spatial commutator is first order with bounded coefficients and the holonomy terms preserve all graph orders. This proves the required time uniqueness. Therefore universal surjectivity holds unless the solution lies wholly on a fixed regular parameter boundary, where it is already stipulated. Finite cokernel complements and Sard–Smale, over the countable classes, choose simultaneous regular data for all indices and compact parameter families relative their boundary collars. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 **Compact parameter moduli and all their low-dimensional faces.** On the compact middle use [F2]'s bounded-forcing proof with the actual smooth varying metric: its local coefficient, invariant-gauge, energy-atom and conformal blowup arguments are uniform on this compact metric family; rescaling freezes the metric and kills the bounded forcing. On the ends use [F3]'s stationary exponential estimates. Subsequence extraction gives one continuation level, possibly with endpoint autonomous broken levels and sphere bubbles. The continuation level cannot disappear by time translation, since its varying data have a fixed compact time support; if its solution is constant it is retained as that level. For a parameter space of dimension $p$, its regular moduli have dimension $I+p$, so every existing main level has $I+p\ge0$. Every nonconstant autonomous end level has positive index by [F3], and every bubble costs at least four by [F2]. Thus parameter moduli of total dimension zero or one have no bubbles, including those whose main level is constant. Their only noncompactness is autonomous breaking at the two ends or the stipulated parameter-boundary degeneration. Exact index addition is the same constant-neck block rotation as [F3]. Finite classes follow from step 1.1. The normal-inverse construction of [F3] works for a continuation level and an autonomous level as well: on the fixed middle its regular operator has a complemented inverse, on the glued stationary end use the same full critical inverse, and there is one neck parameter after removing only the autonomous translation. Its residual is exponentially small and its nonlinearity has the same uniform bounds. The same finite-interval inverse proves exhaustion. These give all actual collars of the zero- and one-dimensional parameter compactifications, with no extra face supplied merely by an assumed formal degeneration. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 **Chain maps and parameter homotopies with integral signs.** Count the finitely many index-zero continuation solutions, with the determinant identifications of [F4], to define a degree-zero map $F$. The compactification of an index-one continuation moduli space has the two kinds of endpoint autonomous breaking. Its oriented boundary formula from [F4] gives $d_1F-Fd_0=0$, with the fixed outgoing/incoming convention. Two choices of continuation data are joined by a compact one-parameter interpolation, constant on its endpoint parameter collars. The metric space is convex and the perturbation space affine, so such an interpolation exists without a middle nondegeneracy requirement. Choose it regular as in step 2.1. Count its index-minus-one solutions, of total dimension zero, to define $K$. The total-dimension-one compactification at index zero has exactly the two parameter-boundary counts and the two end-breaking types from step 3.1. Their signed boundary sum is $F_1-F_0=d_1K+Kd_0$. Orient parameter directions first and then the determinant factor; the sign is the exterior-product boundary sign from [F4], not a mod-two count. Thus the induced homology map is independent of the interpolating data with the declared orientation convention. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 **Composition and the actual inverse.** For two continuation paths insert a stationary interval of length $T$ at their common regular endpoint. Its exact critical inverse, and the regular inverses on the two continuation cores, give the same uniform normal inverse as [F3]. No overall time quotient is taken, since the cores are fixed by their nonautonomous data. Consequently the large-$T$ index-zero solutions correspond bijectively, with product determinant signs, to the two index-zero continuation solutions. Compactness and the finite-interval inverse prove exhaustion just as in [F3]. Letting $T$ run to infinity gives a compact one-parameter problem with that broken boundary; joining its finite-$T$ data to the composite continuation data and applying step 4.1 proves $F_{12}F_{01}\simeq F_{02}$. For the constant continuation at a fixed regular datum, nonconstant solutions have index at least one by [F3], while each constant critical solution has invertible full-line critical operator and index zero. Its determinant is the canonical invertible line, so the index-zero count is exactly the identity on each critical orientation module. A path followed by its reverse is homotopic, relative its ends, to that constant path in the convex metric/affine perturbation spaces. Steps 4.1 and the composition identity therefore make forward and reverse continuation maps inverse chain homotopy equivalences. [F1, F3, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 **Transport of the actual based knot data.** Extend a smooth isotopy of the based knot, normal vector and earring arc to an ambient isotopy of the closed $S^3$ using [F4]. Transport the branch charts, determinant-one gauge data, bundle and metric by that actual diffeomorphism. Pullback conjugates the curvature, holonomy functions, full gauge-fixed operators and their finite-dimensional determinant stabilizations; it thus gives a literal chain identification for the transported regular data, including their integer orientation modules. Any different auxiliary regular metric or perturbation at the endpoint is related by the continuation equivalence just proved. The choices of bases in the critical modules act by diagonal signs; a global orientation convention has its declared overall sign ambiguity. This proves the stated isotopy invariance and independence, without deriving a sutured-closure map, surgery relation or knot-detection input from the analytic complex alone. [F1, F3, F4, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
