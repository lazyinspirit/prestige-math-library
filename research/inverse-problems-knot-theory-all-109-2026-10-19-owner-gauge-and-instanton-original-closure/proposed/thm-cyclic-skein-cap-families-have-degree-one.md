## Statement

Assume full AC. Use the actual positive cyclic three-saddle model and marked backgrounds, with its twice-punctured positive projective-plane cap, two-component unlink boundary, and interval of metrics stretching its two specified internal unknot cuts. Give this cap trivial sheet data, and leave the external unlink equation unperturbed. Its flat critical interval is identified with $[0,\pi]$ using the ordered oriented meridians. Choose the product annular metrics and quarter-charge projective-plane metrics at the two parameter ends. There are perturbations relative to those ends for which the one-dimensional cap moduli space over all classes has an oriented compactification with precisely two boundary points, the prescribed broken annulus/projective-plane configurations. Its restriction to the open critical interval is proper, and its evaluation there has degree $+1$ or $-1$.

For an admissible earring cobordism exterior with this unlink end, its zero-dimensional free-end moduli can be chosen to avoid the reducible endpoints. Gluing the cap therefore gives, up to one overall orientation sign, the same count as gluing the two standard disks. The latter replacement is the actual product replacement in the geometric model. The other two projective-plane faces, cut along a single unknot, have no zero-dimensional boundary contribution. These assertions concern the specified low-dimensional skein families and do not assert a gluing theorem for arbitrary reducible Floer data.

## Proof

**Given:** The specified geometric family and its actual background, with fixed regular admissible data on any exterior earring ends. All moduli dimensions are real, and stabilizers are accounted for before quotienting.

1.1 **Critical slices including both walls.** A trace-zero representation of the free two-meridian group is an ordered pair of unit imaginary quaternions. Conjugate its first entry to $i$; the second entry is determined, modulo the residual circle, by its angle $\theta\in[0,\pi]$ from $i$. At either endpoint its framed slice is the disk $q\in\mathbb R^2$ written explicitly in [F2], with the circle acting by rotation. At an interior point its normal critical parameter is just $\theta$ and its stabilizer is central. The dimensions are $(h^0,h^1)=(0,1)$ in the interior and $(1,2)$ at the endpoints. In all three charts the quotient virtual critical dimension $h^1-h^0$ is one. Retain these finite-dimensional modes and use the spectral inverse only on their common orthogonal complement. On a small endpoint chart the nonzero spectrum off this retained space has a uniform gap; on the remaining compact interior the ordinary critical gap does. Thus no estimate uniform over a disappearing eigenvalue is asserted. An irreducible core makes the residual circle action on its framed configuration slice free, including at $q=0$. Requiring $q=0$ then imposes two real equations on that slice and still imposes two after taking its free circle quotient. Relative bulk transversality therefore avoids this stratum in a zero- or one-dimensional cap family and in zero-dimensional exterior moduli. This is the actual endpoint avoidance used below. [F2, F3, given, construct]

2.1 **The relevant cap index and energy sectors.** For an unbased core write its virtual dimension as $h^1-h^0-h^2$, and adjoin each allowed critical parameter before imposing matching. Finite augmentation at a common cut subtracts its critical deformation space and adds its stabilizer: these are the kernel and cokernel terms of the difference-of-limits map. At the internal unknot the terms are $h^1=0,h^0=1$, as calculated in [F2]. The full annulus has virtual dimension $-3$; its two free external normal modes change this to $-1$. The quarter-charge piece has dimension zero. Removing a flat disk does not alter that number: the flat disk has dimension $-1$, and regluing it adds the same one-dimensional stabilizer. Thus their glued cap has free-end virtual dimension $-1+0+1=0$, and the metric interval gives dimension one. Its endpoint stratum, where those two normal parameters are fixed to zero, has dimension minus one. Other local charge sectors differ by the singular clutch with index four and energy $4\pi^2$, or by its ordinary double; the actual finite-cell calculation in [F3] applies with the cap's fixed external background as well. Consequently the dimension-one union has one common energy bound. This does not assert finiteness of its abstract set of labels. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 **Possible limits on an external critical cylinder.** The unperturbed Chern–Simons functional is constant on the connected flat critical interval. A nonconstant finite-energy cylinder between its points has strictly positive flow energy. Fix actual lifts of the meridians $i$ and $\cos\theta\,i+\sin\theta\,j$. This is a smooth family of representations on the entire interval. Their flat exterior bundles form a bundle over that interval, and a parameter-direction connection identifies its fibers, retaining the explicit meridional models. A cylinder following this family has zero topological energy: off the surface its curvature has only the temporal component, and the meridional boundary term is the same constant Chern–Simons value. Retain the finite critical modes when computing its reference index. Its gapped normal operator deforms to the constant reference; the endpoint change of kernel is balanced by the displayed stabilizer and critical augmentation. For any other class compare the actual determinant-one transition maps with this reference. At a reducible end remove a limiting axis rotation by the gauge which is that parallel rotation on the end and identity before its collar. At an irreducible end a limiting central sign is an unbased framing value, not a nonliftable $SO(3)$ gauge. On the finite relative exterior cells the remaining comparisons have fiber $SU(2)$, whose first and second homotopy groups vanish; on the singular cells their fixed-axis maps and free-cell extensions are the equivariant clutches of [F3]. The local quaternion clutch has index four and energy $4\pi^2$, its reversed-axis version has the same pair, and the free ordinary insertion has pair $(8,8\pi^2)$. The parity relation between the fixed-circle winding and the ordinary degree excludes a half quaternion clutch. Distinct central framing signs do not alter this computation: simultaneous half turns on the two meridians have integral combined degree, while the reducible end rotation just removed is a gauge of zero energy. Thus every relative period has energy/index pair $(4\pi^2d,4d)$ for an integer $d$. A positive-energy cylinder has $d\ge1$. This uses the actual determinant-one convention and retained end modes, rather than importing the earring-bearing gauge-component Statement onto this unmarked cut. In a framed critical slice the extra limiting modes are exactly those in step 1.1. At an endpoint the additional stabilizer is retained rather than discarded; $h^1-h^0=1$ remains the same. After quotienting translation, a positive-period cylinder therefore consumes at least four dimensions in the matching problem. It cannot occur in the dimension-one cap union or its zero-dimensional exterior counts: their remaining irreducible core would have negative expected dimension under the simultaneous regularity of [F3]. Zero-energy cylinders are flat and constant in the critical interval modulo gauge, by the energy identity, so are not extra levels. On an end near a fixed critical slice the remaining modes decay exponentially, while the critical coordinate has derivative quadratic in these modes; integrating that derivative gives an actual limiting critical point. This follows by projecting the ASD equation onto the finite critical modes and their gapped complement. Thus it supplies convergence to a point of the interval, rather than assuming nondegenerate critical points on that interval. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 **Limits at the two metric ends.** On either closed projective-plane piece the branched cover is the reversed projective plane. Its adjoint characteristic class is $w_2=H$ and $p_1\equiv-1\pmod4$. Hence its nonnegative charge is $1/8+n/2$, $n\ge0$, and its index is $4n$, calibrated by the actual quarter-charge index zero. Only $n=0$ can occur in the low-dimensional matching: increasing $n$ consumes four dimensions. This minimum piece is exactly the unique regular irreducible solution of [F2]. A reducible annulus connection has globally oriented parallel axis. Its complex off-axis curvature class on $S^2\times D^2$ is deck-invariant, whereas the deck transformation reverses $H^2$; its integral class is therefore zero. Its decaying abelian ASD curvature has a bounded end primitive, and Stokes gives zero energy, so it is the flat annulus connection. Any nonflat irreducible annulus sector has positive clutch degree and adds at least four to the dimension calculation of step 2.1; generic bulk regularity excludes it from this limit. The same argument applies to positive-period internal unknot-cylinder levels. Thus each metric end has exactly the broken pair in [F2]. Its exhaustive collar is the actual augmented annulus inverse and contraction there. There is one point in that limiting matching quotient, since the annulus circle acts transitively on the relative circle and the projective-plane solution is irreducible. Its external critical value is $0$ at one metric end and $\pi$ at the other, by [F1]. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 **Compactness and the degree.** Use the common energy bound of step 2.1 and the local bounded-forcing compactness of [F3]. Every bubble costs at least four dimensions, so the remaining simultaneously regular irreducible core would have negative expected dimension. No bubble is possible. Steps 3.1–4.1 exhaust the external and metric breaks; the endpoint strata inside the parameter interval are empty by step 1.1. The all-class union is therefore a smooth oriented compact one-manifold after adjoining precisely its two exhaustive end collars. Relative labels are locally constant on these collars by actual bundle comparison and gluing, as in [F3], so no extra finiteness assumption on labels is needed. Its two boundary points lie on the same interval component, since each compact interval has two boundary points; all other components are circles. The oriented evaluation of that interval goes from one endpoint of $[0,\pi]$ to the other. Every regular interior value consequently has algebraic inverse-image number $+1$ or $-1$, independently of backtracking; a circle component contributes zero. Preimages of compact subsets of the open interval avoid the two collars and are compact. This proves the stated proper degree. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 **Exterior matching and the other faces.** Choose the finitely relevant zero-dimensional exterior moduli transverse to interior cap evaluation, using its bulk perturbations and actual free-end critical parameter; endpoint avoidance is step 1.1. Match the finite-dimensional critical coordinates, retain the common stabilizer before quotienting, and use [F2]'s augmented collars at the walls and [F3]'s ordinary inverse in the interior. All-class compactness and the same uniqueness argument exhaust the matching family. Its signed count is the exterior count times the single degree of step 5.1. Each interior unlink critical representation extends uniquely flatly across two standard disks: their exterior has the same free meridian group, so the restriction is the identity on the representation slice. The flat extension has no additional normal obstruction: [F2] constructs its actual double cover $S^1\times D^3$, proves that the critical-mode restriction is an isomorphism and that its extended $H^2$ vanishes, including at the two walls. It therefore supplies the actual cap normal inverse, rather than inferring it from two separate disk neighborhoods. Thus replacing the cap by these disks gives the same count up to the cap's overall orientation sign, and [F1] makes that replacement the actual product. For either separate single-projective-plane face the common cut is the unknot, with no critical deformation and one circle stabilizer. The generic exterior and closed projective-plane pieces have nonnegative dimensions, while matching adds one. They therefore cannot contribute to a zero-dimensional boundary face. This is the dimension computation with the actual stabilizer included, not an inference of vanishing from an irreducible solution's existence. These are exactly the assertions claimed. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct] ∎
