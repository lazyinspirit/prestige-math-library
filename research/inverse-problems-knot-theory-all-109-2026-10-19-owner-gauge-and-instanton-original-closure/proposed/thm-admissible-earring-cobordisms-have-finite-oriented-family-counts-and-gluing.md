## Statement

Assume full AC. Fix a marked earring cobordism made from the specified saddles and standard disk births or deaths, initially with every surface component meeting a temporal boundary, or any finite composite of such cobordisms with its actual glued background. Fix the actual orbifold and exterior determinant background, fixed regular nondegenerate critical data on its based-link ends, and a compact finite-dimensional parameter manifold with corners $P$. Boundary faces may prescribe broken metrics only along admissible based-link cuts carrying the same product data. Assume the prescribed face data already have regular counts and collars and agree on their corners. Then one can choose gauge-covariant bulk holonomy perturbations, product with the fixed gradient perturbations on the ends and relative to those face data, so all finite-energy fixed-endpoint cobordism moduli spaces are regular, for every marked relative class. In total expected dimensions zero and one, their unions over ALL relative classes are finite oriented sets and compact oriented one-manifolds with the expected broken ends and metric-face ends. Every such end has an exhaustive nonlinear collar and the determinant boundary sign is the ordered composition sign. Thus these counts give integral chain maps and relative chain homotopies; compositions are represented by the admissible stretching families with their actual orientation identifications.

No cut with a reducible critical point is included in this theorem. It does not prove the reducible unknot-cut vanishing, the triple-saddle degree-one cap count, or the identification of saddle maps with the Khovanov Frobenius operations.

## Facts & Assumptions

## Proof

**Given:** The specified compact family, marked four-dimensional background and admissible end/face data, with full AC.

[F1] Actual saddle backgrounds and their irreducibility are supplied by [[lem-earring-skein-cobordisms-admit-compatible-marked-orbifold-backgrounds]]. For a composite use the actual restriction maps to glue these backgrounds. Even if a surface component closes under composition, its orbifold atlas and determinant bundle already exist by this gluing; no normal-section existence theorem for a new arbitrary closed surface is invoked. The vertical earring remains present, so its quaternionic stabilizer argument still proves irreducibility. The based-link critical sets, cylindrical differentials and endpoint orientation modules are supplied by [[thm-based-link-earring-complexes-and-resolution-unlink-groups-are-well-defined]].

[F2] The invariant local Sobolev estimates, slices, finite-rank Fredholm augmentations and exponential cylindrical inverses are the actual constructions of [[lem-order-two-singular-gauge-completions-and-cylindrical-fredholm-operators]], [[lem-regular-earring-trajectories-have-exponential-ends-and-low-index-broken-compactness]] and [[lem-regular-earring-broken-trajectories-have-exhaustive-nonlinear-gluing-collars]]. These local estimates are used below; their cylindrical global conclusions are not asserted for a new cobordism without proof.

[F3] The four-dimensional non-gradient holonomy perturbations and local onto argument are constructed in [[lem-nonorientable-trivial-delta-orbifold-asd-moduli-have-generic-holonomy-regularity]]. Its local construction needs irreducibility, not a global orientation of the singular surface. The present background has its own irreducibility from [F1]. Parametric regularity is [[thm-sard-smale-residual-regular-values-for-fredholm-maps]].

[F4] Bounded forcing, local weak convergence and point removal are the local proofs in [[lem-bounded-holonomy-forced-orbifold-asd-trajectories-have-bubble-compactness]]. The ordinary and order-two bubble index losses, with their actual equivariant clutch calculations, are [[lem-order-two-asd-bubbles-have-relative-fredholm-index-loss-at-least-four]].

[F5] Finite-augmentation determinant excision, local complex clutch orientations, supported ordinary gauge transformations and their relations are proved in [[lem-earring-trajectory-determinants-have-coherent-integral-orientations]]. The additional nonorientable-surface gauge loops are treated below rather than inferred from its cylindrical Statement.



1.1 **The actual four-dimensional operator.** Complete connections and gauges in invariant $H^k$ and $H^{k+1}$, $k\ge3$, with small exponential weights on the product ends and fixed critical limits. The Coulomb-slice construction of [F2] uses its local $H^{k+1}\to H^k$ gauge inverse and multiplication estimates; a compact cobordism core needs only finitely many of those charts. On the core, patch the local elliptic parametrices with a partition and retain their compact lower-order errors. On each end use the actual critical spectral inverse, whose gap is positive by nondegeneracy. The commutator errors have compact support and gain a derivative there, so the resulting global operator is Fredholm. Its kernel, cokernel and inverse on the augmented normal complement are therefore genuine finite-dimensional objects. All stabilizers are central by [F1], so the slice quotient has no additional positive-dimensional isotropy. On a parameter-compact set the end gaps have a common positive lower bound. [F1, F2, given, construct]

2.1 **Bulk fields and universal surjectivity.** Use holonomy families $q:S^1\times D^3\to W\setminus\Sigma$ with compact image, whose loops and their transverse thickening are immersions, and output their trace-free holonomy multiplied by an arbitrary self-dual form in a small ordinary chart. Fix the determinant connection and a smooth square root of its loop holonomy over the parameter disk; conjugation then makes the trace-free output gauge-covariant. The four-dimensional Fubini estimate for $q$ gives $\|DV(A)a\|_2\le C\|a\|_2$, and Sobolev multiplication gives all higher derivative bounds. The ordinary loop neighborhoods are compact and disjoint from the singular locus, so these are actual bounded operators. As in [F3], list a countable dense set of such loops, output charts and forms, and weight its $i$th term below $2^{-i}$ times the inverse of all its first $i$ derivative constants and the $i$th power of its distance from the surface. This makes the perturbations and their adjoints smooth in the orbifold charts, with bounded derivative norms and flat tails at the surface. Given a nonzero adjoint cokernel vector, it cannot vanish on the entire output core: otherwise the adjoint bulk term vanishes everywhere, since its output is paired only with that core. On each end its equation is $u_t+B_0u=K(t)u$, where $B_0$ is a fixed self-adjoint first-order operator and $K$ is bounded on $L^2$ on every finite interval; the connection difference is zeroth order and the time-local end holonomy derivative has the bounded Fubini norm. This has zero-Cauchy-data uniqueness without a local unique-continuation claim for a nonlocal operator. On an interval where $f=\|u\|^2>0$, put $q=\langle B_0u,u\rangle/f$. Differentiating gives $q'\le-\|(B_0-q)u\|^2/f+\|K\|^2\le C^2$. Therefore for $t<t_1$, $q(t)\ge q(t_1)-C^2(t_1-t)$, and $f'/f=-2q+2\Re\langle Ku,u\rangle/f$ has a uniform upper bound. Integrating backwards from $t_1$ bounds $f(t)$ away from zero at any finite earlier endpoint, contradicting a first zero there. Smooth bootstrap justifies these differentiations, or finite spectral projections followed by the local estimates give the same inequalities. Thus a solution zero on the core collar stays zero along both ends. At an ordinary point where it is nonzero, two noncommuting holonomies and their product span the adjoint three-plane; such holonomies exist by the earring irreducibility, and embedded small perturbations of their loops retain this spanning property. An output form and coefficient bump therefore pair nontrivially with that vector. This proves universal surjectivity for the actual bulk equation, not just for a cylindrical gradient equation. Apply [F3] with parameter bumps supported away from prescribed faces. The relative data are already regular on the faces, and the perturbation directions in their interior may be made flat at every boundary face. Countably many relative bundle/gauge labels and Sobolev orders may be imposed simultaneously: finite local cells and countable rational mapping-space charts give countably many homotopy labels; the weighted coefficient Banach space is separable and Baire. Thus a single smooth perturbation regularizes every label and every parameter stratum. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, construct]

2.2 **Orientation for the new surface gauge loops.** Use the actual finite-augmentation determinant and collar-excision construction of [F5]. A loop of cobordism configurations closes by a determinant-one gauge central at its ends. The vertical earring annulus forces its central signs at both ends to agree, as in the explicit anti-periodic angle argument of [F5]; remove their common sign. The gauge is now the identity at the ends. On $\Sigma$ its axis-circle sections have orientation local system $a=o(\Sigma)\otimes\Delta$. The obstruction to homotoping such a section to one relative the boundary is its integer angle cocycle in $H^1(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma;\mathbb Z_a)$: lift its angles on the finite cells, and their integer differences on edges give that cocycle; zero cocycle lets the lifts contract. To obtain its dual cycle directly, subdivide the finite surface polygons, draw one transverse dual arc across each edge with its integer cocycle coefficient, and join them in each face; the cocycle condition is precisely cancellation of their signed endpoints. Reversing a face orientation multiplies its dual coefficient by $o(\Sigma)$, so the coefficients of this one-cycle are in $o(\Sigma)\otimes a=\Delta$. Trace its finitely many integer strands to decompose it into loops with consistent coefficient transport, so every resulting loop has trivial $\Delta$ monodromy. Represent each by the local singular clutch of [F5] transported around that loop. Its determinant transport line is $\det(T\Sigma\oplus\mathbb R\oplus\mathbb R_a)$; its orientation local system is exactly $o(\Sigma)\otimes a=\Delta$, hence trivial on this loop. It has positive monodromy. The residual gauge is identity near the surface and the ends; its finite relative ordinary cells give the ordinary instanton-transport curves and supported four-cell gauges of [F5], both of positive monodromy. These exhaust the gauges by the same angle/three-cell/four-cell construction. Thus the actual determinant family is orientable even for nonorientable saddle surfaces. [F1, F2, F5, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 **Uniform energy for all relative classes.** On the core the forced ASD identity is the ordinary Chern–Weil identity $\|F\|_2^2=T+2\|F^+\|_2^2$ with the fixed trace normalization, while the forcing has a uniform $L^2$ bound. On each end use flow energy, not the possibly infinite raw curvature integral: it is the perturbed Chern–Simons drop, and the endpoint perturbation values are bounded. The resulting total core-plus-flow energy satisfies $E\le T_{\rm rel}+C$, with $C$ uniform on $P$. Its topological period and Fredholm index have the same ratio $\pi^2$. Here is why this holds across the actual labels. Use the finite diagram exterior and the finitely many disk and saddle boxes as a relative cell model: subdivide the supplied disk-and-band pieces and their product normal blocks. A disk birth or death contributes its explicit product disk neighborhood and its complementary dual handle, and each saddle adds its dual exterior handle. This is an explicit finite model of these cobordisms, not a triangulation theorem for arbitrary TOP four-manifolds. Fix the boundary identifications and the singular background characteristic classes. Over the ordinary relative three-skeleton, equal $w_2$ data have no additional obstruction, since $\pi_2SO(3)=0$; their relative four-cell changes are ordinary degree clutches. Along the surface the equal orientation/sheet and characteristic data differ by relative two-plane Euler clutches of even degree: odd degree would change their fixed mod-two obstruction. The finite two-cell obstruction calculation represents their half-degrees by point insertions. These are the ordinary and singular local clutches of [F4] and [F5], respectively, with energy/index pairs $(8\pi^2,8)$ and $(4\pi^2,4)$; reversed surface charts have the same pair. Remaining changes are gauge labels, which carry these same local degree periods. Finite excision therefore gives $T_{\rm rel}-\pi^2 I=C_{\beta\alpha}$, independent of the label. Only finitely many endpoint critical pairs occur. On any face or end neck its same identity uses the fixed gradient flow instead of a core volume growing with neck length. Hence $E\le\pi^2 I+C_0$ uniformly on all allowed families. For total dimension at most one, $I+\dim P\le1$, so this is one common energy bound over ALL classes, without an assertion that an abstract set of classes with a fixed index is finite. [F1, F2, F4, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 **Compactness and exclusion of bubbles.** Cover each core by the finite ordinary and invariant charts of [F4]. Its bounded-forcing energy estimate supplies small-energy Coulomb gauges off finitely many energy atoms, weak compactness, strong convergence on smaller charts and the actual limiting cocycle. Holonomies pass to the limit because almost every transverse loop misses the finite bad points; the Fubini bound and the absolutely summable tails give dominated convergence. Point removal and smooth bootstrapping are therefore the same local arguments as [F4], now applied on this compact four-dimensional core. On the product ends use the flow-energy compactness and exponential estimates of [F2]. End critical values in a bounded energy window lie in finitely many translates of their discrete index/energy period, so a nonconstant level has a uniform positive energy quantum. There are finitely many levels. Every bubble has positive degree and drops relative index by at least four at the surface and eight ordinarily, by [F4]. The limiting core and the limiting trajectories are among the already simultaneously regular classes of step 2.1; a nonconstant trajectory uses an additional translation direction before quotienting. A bubble in a total-dimension-zero or -one family would leave the core/broken family with negative expected dimension, even before its independent bubble-position parameters are restored. No such regular base solution exists. Thus bubbles are absent. The same argument on every prescribed parameter stratum excludes hidden bubbling there. The remaining convergence is smooth on the full core, with only the stated finite broken end levels or prescribed admissible metric breaks. [F1, F2, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 **The nonlocal neck estimate.** On each prospective broken configuration impose the finitely many kernel slices supplied by the core and level augmented inverses. Patch those inverses with long cutoffs on the critical cylinders. For differential terms the commutator is bounded by $C/T$, and the exponential tails of the two levels give $Ce^{-\lambda T}$; the stable/unstable splitting in [F2] excludes additional normal kernel. A bulk holonomy derivative does not commute with such cutoffs in general. First retain only finitely many loop families so that the operator norm of the omitted tail is below $\varepsilon$. Their images lie in fixed compact pieces, so put the cutoffs outside all of them; these retained terms now commute exactly with the cutoff. The two omitted-tail commutators have norm at most $2\varepsilon$. For a stretching family choose the fields whose loops cross a new long neck with coefficients flat in $1/T$, for example bounded by $e^{-T^2}$ times every required derivative constant; they remain available and nonzero at every finite $T$, but have vanishing operator-norm limit. This is compatible with the weighted countable construction in step 2.1. Choose the finite truncation first, then $T$. The patched normal inverse error is $C/T+Ce^{-\lambda T}+2\varepsilon$, below one half, and a Neumann series gives the actual uniform right inverse on the normal complement. This addresses the nonlocal terms rather than assuming that time cutoffs commute with them. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 **Existence and exhaustion of collars.** Preglue the limiting smooth solutions with the actual fixed-endpoint bundle identifications; the product critical collars make the error $O(e^{-\lambda T})$ plus the flat cross-neck forcing error. The four-dimensional Sobolev multiplication estimate gives $\|N(a)-N(b)\|_{H^{k-1}}\le C(\|a\|_{H^k}+\|b\|_{H^k})\|a-b\|_{H^k}$ on a uniform small normal ball. The inverse of step 5.1 and its differentiated equation therefore give a unique small correction by contraction, smoothly in the kernel and neck parameters. Conversely any sequence tending to this broken limit is, by step 4.1 and the end spectral splitting, close in the weighted slice norms after choosing its neck lengths and its finite-dimensional kernel coordinates. The local gauge inverse fixes those coordinates up to the central signs, which act trivially. The remaining normal difference satisfies the same small contraction equation, so it is the correction already constructed. This proves all-sequence exhaustion and local uniqueness. Multiple admissible necks use the same estimates in their finite product and the same uniqueness proof, so their collars agree at corners. [F1, F2, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct]

7.1 **No finiteness assumption on labels.** In a fixed smooth slice, sufficiently close connections have the same marked relative bundle/gauge label: use their small logarithmic comparison on the finite core cover and the fixed critical product identifications. At a broken collar this label is the concatenation of its actual cut-and-glued labels; the unique small correction of step 6.1 is a homotopy within that same marked configuration component. Thus labels are locally constant on the entire compactification, including its broken collars. Step4.1 gives compactness of their union, not merely compactness inside one label. In total dimension zero the regular union is a compact discrete space and hence finite. In dimension one, only the finite zero-dimensional broken factors and finite parameter-face factors can occur; their exhaustive collars and the smooth compact interior make a compact one-manifold with corners. A cover by finitely many such charts also gives finitely many connected components. This proves finiteness of the actual contributing classes without claiming a false finite bound on all topological classes of fixed index. [F1, F2, step 4.1, step 6.1, construct]

7.2 **Comparisons and boundary signs.** Across relative classes use [F5]'s local complex clutch orientations. Reversing a local singular chart conjugates a complex line of even index, and the relation between two singular insertions and an ordinary insertion is complex linear; all relevant insertion indices are divisible by four. These comparisons agree on the degree and four-cell relations from step 3.1, and the gauge-loop monodromy of step 2.2 is positive. They therefore identify every determinant orientation line with the ordered incoming and outgoing critical orientation modules and one fixed cobordism orientation module. Gluing uses the finite exact-sequence exterior products of [F5]; a common finite augmentation makes both associations the same ordered wedge product. Parameter orientations and the outward neck coordinate give the usual boundary Koszul signs. The normal inverse in step 5.1 is a continuous invertible factor, so its nonlinear collar has exactly that determinant sign. Fix a basis in the cobordism orientation module, and use the glued product basis for a composite. This proves the ordered composition rule rather than assuming independent arbitrary signs for each zero-dimensional set. [F1, F2, F5, step 3.1, step 6.1, step 2.2, construct]

8.1 **Counts and relations.** Count the finite dimension-zero sets with these integral orientation-module maps. A compact oriented dimension-one family has algebraic boundary zero. Its exhausted boundary is exactly the ordered broken trajectory terms, the prescribed admissible metric-face terms and the ordinary parameter faces, by steps 4.1–7.1. Their determinant signs are precisely step 7.2. For a point parameter this is the chain-map identity; for an interval it is the relative chain-homotopy identity; stretching an admissible cut gives the chain-homotopy composition rule with its actual glued orientation module. Applying the same construction over parameter corners gives the higher relations needed by finite admissible metric cubes. This completes the asserted cobordism-count construction at its exact admissible-cut scope. Reducible cuts and nonvanishing statements were excluded in the Statement and have not been inferred. ∎ [F1, step 7.1, step 7.2, construct]
