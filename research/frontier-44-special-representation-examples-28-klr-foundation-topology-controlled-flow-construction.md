# A locally bounded controlled-lift route to continuous flows

This is a fully expanded conditional local construction. It does not assert that Whitney stratifications already supply its tube data or locally bounded lifts. Those two existence constructions remain explicit open prerequisites; neither ordinary ODE nor this argument discharges them.

Let W be a closed subset of Euclidean space with a finite smooth frontier stratification. Let f:W→B be proper and smooth on each stratum. Let v be a smooth vector field on B with a complete flow β. Suppose a stratumwise smooth field V has actually been constructed with df(V)=v∘f. Assume V is locally bounded in the ambient Euclidean norm. For every stratum R suppose an open tube T_R carries continuous maps π_R:T_R→R and ρ_R:T_R→[0,∞), smooth on each stratum, with ρ_R^(-1)(0)=R and π_R|R=id. Impose dπ_R(V)=V_R∘π_R and dρ_R(V)=0 on incident strata in T_R. Require local tube properness: over compact base neighborhoods C in R there is δ>0 such that the inverse image of C×[0,δ] under (π_R,ρ_R) is compact and contained in T_R. Tube overlaps may be restricted as needed to ensure these conditions. Assume V_R is the actual restriction of V to R, not an independently chosen field.

The field has a complete stratum-preserving flow φ on W, and φ is jointly continuous across strata.

To prove completeness, fix x in one stratum and solve its ordinary smooth ODE there on a maximal interval. The chain rule gives f(φ_t(x))=β_t(f(x)) wherever defined. Over any bounded time interval the base trajectory has compact image, so the full trajectory lies in its compact proper inverse image K. Local boundedness gives one uniform ambient bound for V on K by a finite neighborhood cover. Therefore the trajectory is Lipschitz in ambient Euclidean coordinates on that bounded interval. If its maximal interval had a finite endpoint T, the Lipschitz bound would give a limit y∈K as t→T. If y is in the same stratum, the ordinary smooth ODE extends past T. Otherwise y is in a strictly smaller incident stratum R by frontier. On a terminal interval the trajectory lies in T_R, hence ρ_R is constant there; it is strictly positive since the trajectory remains in the upper stratum. Continuity of ρ_R and convergence to y∈R instead give ρ_R→0, a contradiction. The negative-time argument is identical. This proves completeness and stratum preservation without treating a discontinuous field as a globally continuous ODE.

For joint continuity, fix a compact time interval along the lower-stratum trajectory t↦φ_t(x) with x∈R. The smooth flow on R depends continuously on its initial point and time. Choose a compact neighborhood C in R with the trajectory in its relative interior; shrink the initial neighborhood in R so all corresponding projected trajectories remain in the interior of C. Local tube properness supplies δ with K_R=(π_R,ρ_R)^(-1)(C×[0,δ]) compact and contained in T_R. If initial points x_n→x lie in incident strata, then eventually x_n∈T_R, π_R(x_n)→x and ρ_R(x_n)→0. As long as their trajectories remain in T_R, the control identities and uniqueness of the smooth ODE on R give π_R(φ_t(x_n))=φ_t(π_R(x_n)), while ρ_R(φ_t(x_n))=ρ_R(x_n). They cannot exit T_R on the selected interval: the projected paths stay in interior C and the radii stay strictly below δ; any first-exit limit lies in the compact subset K_R⊂T_R and thus is not an exit. Finally, compactness of K_R gives uniform collapse to the projected point as radius tends to zero. Explicitly, if z_n∈K_R, ρ_R(z_n)→0 and π_R(z_n) lies in C, any subsequence has a convergent subsubsequence z_n→z∈R, and continuity gives π_R(z_n)→π_R(z)=z. Hence the ambient distance ||z_n−π_R(z_n)|| tends to zero uniformly on K_R. Apply this to z_n=φ_(t_n)(x_n) for t_n→t. The projected smooth trajectories tend to φ_t(x), and the collapsing distance tends to zero. This proves joint continuity. Finitely many strata let every sequence be reduced to an incident-stratum subsequence; the conclusion holds for the original sequence. Flow inversion φ_(-t) proves each φ_t is a homeomorphism.

For a base coordinate box B⊂R^m, suppose one has actually constructed m locally bounded controlled lifts V_i of the coordinate fields. Cut off the base fields outside a slightly larger box, using cutoff functions pulled back along f; this preserves dρ=0 and projected control wherever f is compatible with the tube projections. Their flows exist and are continuous by the preceding argument. Starting at the center fiber, successively flow for the m coordinate increments. Each flow changes only its own base coordinate, so the resulting map F×B→f^(-1)(B) lies over B. Its inverse flows in reverse order for the negative increments. Thus this produces a stratum-preserving local trivialization; commutation of the different V_i is not required. This closes local triviality from the specifically stated lifts.

For radial cone charts, the same method needs radial lifts with dπ_S(V_rad)=0 and dρ_S(V_rad)=ρ_S, and compatibility with other controls on annuli. A point of radius r>0 flows to a reference-radius ε link by time log(ε/r). Properness on closed annuli gives existence there. Negative-time flow has ρ_S=e^t ε→0 and fixed π_S; local tube properness gives convergence to the base stratum, uniformly over compact links and base patches. The radial transport therefore extends at the collapsed cone end continuously. Horizontal local trivialization of the reference-radius link bundle then yields B×c(L), with an inverse given by normalized radial transport. This is an explicit proof reduction, but existence of those compatible radial lifts and their local boundedness is still a new upstream obligation. An arbitrary submersion right inverse cannot be assumed to satisfy these controls simultaneously.
