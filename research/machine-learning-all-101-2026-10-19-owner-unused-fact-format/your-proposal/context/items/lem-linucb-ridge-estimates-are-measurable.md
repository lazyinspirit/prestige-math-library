## Statement

In the finite-dimensional linear contextual bandit model, put

$$G_t:=\sum_{s=1}^t A_sA_s^{\mathsf T},\qquad S_t:=\sum_{s=1}^t A_s\eta_s\qquad(0\le t\le n).$$

Give each finite real matrix space its entrywise Euclidean topology and corresponding Borel sigma-algebra. Then $V_t$, $b_t$, $G_t$, $S_t$, and $D_t:=\det(V_t)$ are $\mathcal F_{t+1}$-measurable matrix, vector, or real random elements, and the ridge estimate $\widehat\theta_t=V_t^{-1}b_t$ is an $\mathcal F_{t+1}$-measurable $\mathbb R^d$-valued random element for every $0\le t\le n$.

[[def-linear-contextual-bandit-model]]: the model's features, rewards, filtration, and ridge notation.

[[lem-linucb-ridge-information-matrix]]: $V_t$ is invertible and $\det V_t\ge\lambda^d>0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite-dimensional bandit model's measurable feature vectors, reward noises, and rewards through the finite horizon.

[F1] [[def-linear-contextual-bandit-model]]: $A_s$ is $\mathcal F_s$-measurable, $\eta_s$ is $\mathcal F_{s+1}$-measurable, $X_s=\langle\theta^*,A_s\rangle+\eta_s$, and $V_t$, $b_t$, and $\widehat\theta_t$ have the displayed formulas.

[F2] [[def-matrix-space]] and [[def-matrices-over-a-commutative-ring]]: a matrix is the finite function of its real entries; the entrywise Euclidean coordinates identify $M_d(\mathbb R)$ with $\mathbb R^{d^2}$.

[F3] [[def-measurable-function-between-measurable-spaces]] and [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]: a map is measurable when preimages of Borel sets are measurable, and the Borel sigma-algebra is generated by open sets.

[F4] [[thm-arithmetic-and-lattice-operations-preserve-measurability]]: finite pointwise sums and products of real-valued measurable functions are measurable whenever the operations are defined.

[F5] [[cor-determinant-is-a-polynomial-in-the-matrix-entries]]: for each fixed finite matrix size, determinant is a finite polynomial in the entries.

[F6] [[def-matrix-minors-cofactors-and-adjugate]]: each entry of $\operatorname{adj}(V)$ is a signed deleted-row-and-column determinant; for size $1$, the empty minor has determinant $1$.

[F7] [[thm-adjugate-identity-over-a-commutative-ring]], [[thm-reals-field]], and [[lem-field-is-a-commutative-ring]]: $V\operatorname{adj}(V)=\det(V)I$ for real square matrices.

[F8] [[lem-linucb-ridge-information-matrix]]: each $V_t$ is invertible and $D_t:=\det(V_t)\ge\lambda^d>0$.

[F9] [[thm-continuous-preimages-of-borel-sets-are-borel]]: a continuous real function has Borel preimages of Borel sets.

[F10] [[def-matrix-product-and-identity-matrix]]: each coordinate of a matrix-vector product is a finite sum of products of entries.

## Proof

1.1 For $s\le t$, each coordinate of $A_s$ is $\mathcal F_s$-measurable and each coordinate of $\eta_s$ is $\mathcal F_{s+1}$-measurable; the filtration gives $\mathcal F_s,\mathcal F_{s+1}\subseteq\mathcal F_{t+1}$. The reward equation and [F4] make $X_s$ measurable at $\mathcal F_{t+1}$. Applying [F4] coordinatewise to the finite sums and products defining $G_t$, $S_t$, $V_t$, and $b_t$ proves their $\mathcal F_{t+1}$-measurability. At $t=0$ all four are constant. [given, F1, F2, F4]

2.1 The determinant $D_t$ is $\mathcal F_{t+1}$-measurable by step 1.1, [F4], and the polynomial formula [F5]. By [F8] its range lies in $J=[\lambda^d,\infty)$. It is also measurable as a map into $J$: for every relatively open set $J\cap U$, with $U\subseteq\mathbb R$ open, $D_t^{-1}(J\cap U)=D_t^{-1}(U)$ because $D_t$ takes values in $J$. [step 1.1, F3, F5, F8]

3.1 The reciprocal $r:J\to\mathbb R$, $r(x)=1/x$, is continuous since $\lambda^d>0$, hence Borel measurable by [F9]. The definition of measurability in [F3] then makes $D_t^{-1}=r\circ D_t$ measurable. By [F5]–[F6], every entry of $\operatorname{adj}(V_t)$ is measurable; for $d=1$ it is the constant $1$. Since $V_t$ is invertible, [F7] gives $\operatorname{adj}(V_t)=D_tV_t^{-1}$, so $V_t^{-1}=D_t^{-1}\operatorname{adj}(V_t)$ has measurable entries by [F4]. [step 2.1, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9]

4.1 By [F10], every coordinate of $\widehat\theta_t=V_t^{-1}b_t$ is a finite sum of products of measurable entries from steps 1.1 and 3.1; [F4] makes it measurable. At $t=0$, $\widehat\theta_0=0$ is constant. Thus every stated vector and matrix is an $\mathcal F_{t+1}$-measurable random element. [step 1.1, step 3.1, F2, F4, F10] ∎
