## QA22 — Block encodings

For a finite square matrix `A` with `||A||≤α`, an `(\alpha,a,\varepsilon)` block encoding names a unitary `U` on an ancilla register and the system register for which the all-zero ancilla block is within `\varepsilon` of `A/\alpha` in operator norm. The equivalent projector statement is `||(\Pi U\Pi)|_{ran(\Pi)}-A/\alpha||\le\varepsilon`, where `\Pi=|0^a\rangle\langle0^a|\otimes I`; the compression is not itself asserted to be unitary. Rectangular matrices require an explicit finite padding/isometry and declared domain/codomain. This is the normalization convention used by Childs, Ch. 29, and Gilyén et al., Def. 43.

The access contract names `U`, `U†`, controlled-`U`, and controlled-`U†` separately and records their costs. If a circuit is synthesized from entries of `A`, the data access and construction are charged. The LCU construction takes component block encodings of `A_j`, coefficients `\beta_j`, and `\Lambda=\sum_j|\beta_j|\alpha_j`. It prepares amplitudes `\sqrt{|\beta_j|\alpha_j/\Lambda}`, uses `SELECT=\sum_j|j\rangle\langle j|\otimes e^{i\arg\beta_j}U_j`, and applies `PREPARE† SELECT PREPARE`. Expanding the projected coefficient register gives `\sum_j\beta_jA_j/\Lambda`. The state preparation, its inverse, SELECT, controlled versions when needed, coefficient precision, and every component call remain explicit costs. If the coefficient amplitudes are supplied as an explicit table, QC24’s `def-qc-state-preparation-cost-model` and `thm-qc-state-preparation-cost` apply; the detailed `thm-qc-state-preparation-rotation-level-upper-bound` gives O(D) continuous rotations and O(nD) bounded-arity gates/depth for D=2^n entries, with O(n) clean ancillas. Table storage, reading, coefficient arithmetic, and angle precision are still charged separately. If PREPARE is instead supplied as a circuit oracle, its input cost is recorded directly.

For products, put the two encodings on separate ancillas and compose them in the declared matrix order. The product normalization is `\alpha_A\alpha_B`. If the normalized compressed blocks have errors `E_A,E_B`, the product error obeys

`||B_AB-AB/(\alpha_A\alpha_B)|| \le \varepsilon_A||B||/\alpha_B+\varepsilon_B||A||/\alpha_A+\varepsilon_A\varepsilon_B \le \varepsilon_A+\varepsilon_B+\varepsilon_A\varepsilon_B`.

This bound follows by expanding the product and using operator-norm submultiplicativity. Query and gate costs add; normalizations multiply. A longer product uses the same error recurrence rather than silently treating approximate blocks as exact. QA22's B page works a two-term LCU and a two-factor product error calculation.

## QA23 — Quantum signal processing

Fix the signal matrix `W(x)=[[x,\sqrt{1-x^2}],[\sqrt{1-x^2},-x]]` on `[-1,1]`, the phase matrix `Z(\phi)=diag(e^{i\phi},e^{-i\phi})`, and the ordered sequence `Z(\phi_0)\prod_{j=1}^d(W(x)Z(\phi_j))`. A degree-`d` admissible polynomial pair has the required degree/parity and satisfies the polynomial identity

`P(x)P^*(x)+(1-x^2)Q(x)Q^*(x)=1`,

where `*` conjugates coefficients. The constructive factorization theorem states that such a pair arises from a real phase list and that every phase list produces the corresponding degree/parity pair. Its proof route is local: multiply the elementary matrices for the forward implication; for the converse, factor the complementary polynomial, pair reciprocal-conjugate roots, peel off one phase and degree at a time, and verify the base case and termination. The polynomial identity, degrees, and product order are part of the contract, not conventions that can be switched mid-proof.

For a real degree-`d` target `p` with parity `d mod 2` and `|p(x)|\le1` on the full interval, the real-QSP completion produces phases whose real polynomial component approximates `p` uniformly to the requested `\delta`. Gilyén et al. Corollary 10 provides classical phase construction in `poly(d,log(1/\delta))` arithmetic operations. The circuit uses `d` signal calls and `d+1` phase rotations. The local implementation is OP1’s explicitly named efficient-numerical variant: exact interval validation, bounded-height coefficient rounding, bounded-grid exact rational roots, counted quadratic tower completion/peeling and buffered certified atan2. Its polynomial arithmetic theorem selects unit-charged exact-algebraic field/equality/order operations and finite extension tower/DAG intermediates. Original coefficient access/encoding, expanded minimal-polynomial conversion, operation implementation bit costs and optional finite-basis compilation are separate coordinates. Literal complete exact/complete numerical modes remain available with finite termination; the efficient variant preserves the ideal δ0/8 and implemented δ0/4 accuracy guarantees, δ0=min(δ,8). If each compiled phase has operator error `\zeta_j`, a product hybrid bound adds at most `\sum_j\zeta_j`; finite-basis compilation remains a separate QC cost. The B page multiplies a degree-one sequence explicitly.

## QA24 — Quantum singular-value transformation

Let `U` be unitary and `\Pi_R,\Pi_L` orthogonal projectors. The projected matrix `A=\Pi_LU\Pi_R`, restricted between their ranges, is a contraction and has an SVD `A=\sum_j\sigma_j|u_j\rangle\langle v_j|`, with `0\le\sigma_j\le1`. For `0<\sigma_j<1`, the complementary vector `(I-\Pi_R)U†u_j/\sqrt{1-\sigma_j^2}` forms a two-dimensional invariant singular plane. In its singular bases, the unitary has the scalar signal block with entries `\sigma_j` and `\sqrt{1-\sigma_j^2}`. At `\sigma=1` the plane collapses; zero singular values, unmatched nullspaces, and the orthogonal complement are handled separately. This is the substantive SVD argument in Gilyén et al. Defs. 11–16 and Lemma 14, and is required before the scalar QSP theorem can be lifted to a matrix result.

For an odd function `p`, define `p^{(SV)}(A)=\sum_jp(\sigma_j)|u_j\rangle\langle v_j|`. For even `p`, use the right singular space `\sum_jp(\sigma_j)|v_j\rangle\langle v_j|`, including the domain nullspace with singular value zero; the left-space version is analogous. The decomposition is basis independent because repeated singular values determine canonical subspace projections. QA24 alternates `U,U†` with phases about `R_{\Pi_R}=2\Pi_R-I` and `R_{\Pi_L}=2\Pi_L-I`; the QC08 supplier also records the alternative sign `S_\Pi=I-2\Pi=-R_\Pi` and its controlled-sign relation. Compression to the left projector for odd degree and to the right projector for even degree gives the desired singular-space action. A real bounded parity polynomial uses Gilyén et al.'s extra one-qubit control combining the `\Phi` and `-\Phi` sequences. The proof is blockwise application of the scalar QSP identity to each invariant plane, followed by separate endpoint/nullspace checks.

The robustness route uses Gilyén et al. Lemma 22: for degree `d`, the transformed-operator difference is at most `4d\sqrt{||A-\widetilde A||}` under its contraction and phase hypotheses. Thus an approximate input block, a scalar approximation error, and phase compilation error contribute at most `4d\sqrt{\varepsilon_{BE}}+\eta_{poly}+\sum_j\zeta_j`. The normalization is essential: the polynomial acts on `A/\alpha`; the target must be expressed as a function of the normalized singular value. The abstract sequence uses `d` alternating calls to `U` and `U†` and `O(d)` projector-controlled and phase operations. Gate/query translation and controlled variants are stated separately. The examples calculate a rank-one singular plane and show why assigning a nonzero cross-space coefficient at σ=0 is basis-dependent; changing both positive singular vectors’ signs leaves the map unchanged.

## QA26 — HHL and phase-estimation linear systems

The QLSP output is a state proportional to `A⁺ b`, not a printed list of all classical coordinates. The HHL access model supplies a unitary preparing `|b\rangle`, controlled Hamiltonian simulation `e^{itH}` to declared times and operator error, and any sparse-entry oracle used to build that simulation. Each input preparation, inverse, controlled call, data-loading step, bit precision, and gate cost is explicit. No state-preparation or simulation circuit follows from a matrix description.

For Hermitian `H` with `||H||\le1` and supported eigenvalues satisfying `1/\kappa\le|\lambda|\le1`, HHL prepares `|b\rangle`, estimates eigenvalues coherently, applies an ancilla rotation with success amplitude `c/\widetilde\lambda` for `c\le1/(2\kappa)`, uncomputes the estimate, and postselects a flag. In the ideal case, the flagged component is `cH^{-1}|b\rangle` and succeeds with probability `c^2||H^{-1}|b\rangle||^2`. With `c=1/(2\kappa)` and unit `|b\rangle`, this probability is between `1/(4\kappa^2)` and `1/4`. Phase-resolution, controlled-rotation, simulation, and input-preparation errors must be combined before normalization; an `O(\varepsilon/\kappa)` flagged-vector error is enough for an `\varepsilon` normalized output error under the displayed lower norm bound. The original HHL analysis obtains an `O(\kappa/t_0)` state error with simulation time scale `t_0` and uses amplitude amplification for the postselection cost.

For the sparse QLSP model, Childs–Kothari–Somma Theorem 2 gives the phase-estimation HHL query bound `O(d\kappa^2\varepsilon^{-1} poly(log(d\kappa/\varepsilon)))` to the matrix-entry oracle and `O(d\kappa poly(log(d\kappa/\varepsilon)))` uses of the state-preparation circuit. The gate-efficient claim retains the declared sparse-simulation and circuit costs. A non-Hermitian matrix can be embedded in the finite Hermitian dilation `[[0,A],[A†,0]]`; squaring the dilation recovers `AA†` and `A†A`, so its nonzero singular values and condition number are inherited. Nullspaces and the support of `b` must still be handled. The example works out a diagonal two-eigenvalue system; the counterexample page separates state preparation from classical vector output.

## QA27 — Block-encoded linear systems and variable-time amplification

If `A` has singular gap `\gamma` and is encoded with normalization `\alpha`, the normalized matrix `\bar A=A/\alpha` has gap `\delta=\gamma/\alpha`. For `0<\varepsilon\le\delta\le1/2`, Gilyén et al. Theorem 41 supplies an odd polynomial of degree `O(\delta^{-1}log(1/\varepsilon))` that approximates `(\delta/2)\bar A⁺=(\gamma/2)A⁺` on singular values in `{0}\cup[\delta,1]`. The transition band must be excluded or its behavior specified. QSVT then yields one attempt at a normalized inverse state. If its success probability is `p_succ`, ordinary amplification costs `O(p_succ^{-1/2})`; under only the gap and norm promise the crude `p_succ\ge\delta^2/4` gives another `O(\delta^{-1})` factor. A fixed-time QSVT inverse plus standard amplification is therefore not a linear-condition-number theorem.

Variable-time amplitude amplification has a separate contract. A coherent algorithm has stopping times `t_1<...<t_m=T_max`, an outcome register recording unfinished/good/bad branches, and an orthogonal step register. For branch masses `p_i`, define `T_av=(\sum_i p_it_i^2)^{1/2}` and let `p_succ` be its good-state probability. Ambainis' Theorem 1 amplifies to success at least `1/2` in time

`O(T_max\sqrt{log T_max}+(T_av/\sqrt{p_succ})log^{3/2}T_max)`.

Repeating `O(log(1/\delta_{fail}))` times raises success to `1-\delta_{fail}`. The proof must retain reversible stopping and inverse/coherent-access requirements; `T_av` is not an informal average runtime.

Childs–Kothari–Somma give a modern sparse QLSP algorithm using Fourier or Chebyshev inverse approximations on `D_\kappa=[-1,-1/\kappa]\cup[1/\kappa,1]`, coherent dyadic eigenvalue resolution, and variable-time amplification. Under their `P_A` row-location/value oracle, `P_B` preparation oracle, Hermitian `d`-sparse normalized matrix and supported condition number `\kappa`, Theorem 5 gives `O(d\kappa poly(log(d\kappa/\varepsilon)))` queries to `P_A` and `P_B` for an `\varepsilon`-close normalized solution state, with gate efficiency as defined by the paper. A better supported-subspace condition number may replace `\kappa`; singular matrices are allowed when the input has no nullspace component. `P_A`, its reverse-location logic, `P_B`, inverse/coherent access, gate cost, and state output are explicit. The QA27 inventory also gives a fixed-time block-QSVT inverse bound and compares it with the variable-time expression without claiming every QLSP instance achieves a speedup. The input-state preparation is charged through the supplied B/P_B access cost; QC24 table synthesis is not silently substituted for an arbitrary input oracle.

## QA28 — Spectral estimation and matrix functions

For a finite Hermitian `H=\sum_\lambda\lambda P_\lambda` and a unit input `|\psi\rangle`, define the finite spectral law `\mu_\psi(\lambda)=||P_\lambda|\psi\rangle||^2`. Its weights are nonnegative and sum to one, and the functional calculus gives `\langle\psi|f(H)|\psi\rangle_D=\sum_\lambda\mu_\psi(\lambda)f(\lambda)`. This reuses the published finite spectral theorem, finite functional calculus, and finite probability suppliers rather than importing physics measurement claims.

The spectral-estimation input includes a supplied state-preparation circuit for |ψ⟩, with its inverse and preparation cost charged for every independent repetition. If |ψ⟩ is supplied as an explicit amplitude table, QC24’s cost model and `thm-qc-state-preparation-cost` give a rotation/gate upper bound after table encoding costs are included; otherwise the preparation circuit and its cost remain a supplied input. Its access model supplies either controlled powers of a unitary with a known injective eigenphase encoding, or controlled Hamiltonian-simulation calls with their times, precisions, inverses, and costs. For `U=e^{iH}` and spectrum in `[-1,1]`, the phase is `\lambda/(2\pi)`, so the interval is injective and no phase wrap occurs. QPE on a superposition samples eigenvalues according to `\mu_\psi`; the QA2 phase-tail theorem gives error under a finite grid. Taking `K=3` in that tail bound gives a one-run estimate within `3/Q` in phase with probability at least `3/4`; for `e^{iH}`, this is eigenvalue error below `6\pi/Q`. Set `Q\ge6\pi/\varepsilon` for a single spectral sample. A median confidence wrapper requires repreparing the same fixed eigenstate/eigenspace; fresh superposition preparations sample independent eigenvalues and their median has no one-eigenvalue guarantee. A controlled `U^{2^j}` is charged as repeated unitary access or as an explicitly supplied long-time simulator; the phase-register size alone is not the simulation cost.

For a Hermitian matrix whose spectrum lies in `I`, a uniform scalar polynomial bound transfers directly: if `sup_I|p-f|\le\eta`, then `||p(H)-f(H)||\le\eta` by the spectral projections. To use QSVT, normalize both function and matrix, provide a bounded parity polynomial on the full interval, and charge any extension needed to meet parity. The total block error is the scalar approximation error plus the QA24 robustness and phase-synthesis terms. This inventory states a general function interface, not an unproved efficient polynomial approximation for every `f`. The examples calculate a two-level spectral law and check a supplied polynomial approximation to a scaled exponential.

## QA29 — Sampling, expectation, and Monte Carlo

A coherent finite sampler is a supplied unitary `A` with `A|0\rangle=\sum_x\sqrt{p_x}|x\rangle|g(x)\rangle|work_x\rangle`, its inverse, controlled calls, value precision, and a reversible controlled rotation/predicate. Measurement gives the finite law, but the circuit and coherent preparation are explicit inputs. For `g\in[a,b]`, return `a` directly if `a=b`; otherwise, for target `0<\varepsilon\le b-a`, rescale `q(x)=(g(x)-a)/(b-a)`, rotate a good qubit with conditional probability `q(x)`, and obtain total good probability `(\mu-a)/(b-a)` for `\mu=\sum_xp_xg(x)`. QA29 consumes QA13's unique amplitude-estimation algorithm and theorems; it defines no duplicate AE supplier.

QA13's finalized median interface uses the least power-of-two `M\ge4\pi/\epsilon` and a least positive odd repetition count `r\ge\ln(1/\delta)/(2(8/\pi^2-1/2)^2)`. Its estimator has additive amplitude error `\epsilon` with probability at least `1-\delta` and uses exactly `r(M-1)` controlled Grover calls in the repeated-power model. Rescaling the range gives `O((b-a)\varepsilon^{-1}\log(1/\delta))` coherent sampler/reflection rounds for an additive `\varepsilon` expectation estimate. All state preparation, inverse/controlled sampling, good-state phase flips, reflections, controlled value rotations, arithmetic, and compiled gate costs are multiplied in separately.

The classical comparison uses the sample mean: for nondegenerate range `[a,b]`, its one-sample variance is at most `(b-a)^2/4`, so `n` independent samples have Chebyshev failure at most `(b-a)^2/(4n\varepsilon^2)`. Montanaro's bounded-variance Algorithm 3 and Theorem 5 give a stronger variance-sensitive route: with variance at most `\sigma^2`, coherent output/inverse/reflection access, including the reflection `A(2|0\rangle\langle0|-I)A^\dagger`, and `\sigma>\varepsilon`, additive error `\varepsilon` is achieved with success at least `2/3`, `O(log(\sigma/\varepsilon)loglog(\sigma/\varepsilon))` output-state copies, and `O((\sigma/\varepsilon)log^{3/2}(\sigma/\varepsilon)loglog(\sigma/\varepsilon))` state-reflection uses. Each reflection is implemented and charged through the sampler and its inverse; the local proof obligations reproduce the coarse mean, centering, positive/negative split, bounded second moments, error allocation, and recombination; variance alone does not provide coherent sample access. Quantum counting is recorded only as QA13's Boolean-indicator specialization.

## QA30 — Quantum optimization, SDPs, and variational algorithms

For finite Hermitian data, the primal SDP maximizes `Tr(CX)` subject to `Tr(A_jX)\le b_j` and `X\succeq0`; the dual minimizes `b\cdot y` subject to `y\ge0` and `\sum_jy_jA_j-C\succeq0`. Weak duality is proved directly: the difference of dual and primal objectives is the sum of nonnegative scalar slack terms and `Tr(PX)` with `P,X\succeq0`; write the latter as `Tr(P^{1/2}XP^{1/2})\ge0`. This does not assert strong duality or attainment without hypotheses.

The finite MMW game uses Hermitian losses `-I\preceq M_t\preceq I`, `W_t=exp(-\eta\sum_{s<t}M_s)`, and `\rho_t=W_t/Tr(W_t)`. The regret bound is `\sum_tTr(M_t\rho_t)\le\lambda_{min}(\sum_tM_t)+\eta T+\log(n)/\eta` under the stated logarithm convention. Its proof telescopes the partition function, uses the scalar exponential bound and Golden–Thompson, and lower-bounds the final trace by the least eigenvalue. Because its Golden–Thompson supplier is a draft, the whole result carries that review hold.

The quantum SDP proposal follows van Apeldoorn–Gilyén–Gribling–de Wolf Theorem 1. Under normalized `||A_j||,||C||\le1`, `A_1=I`, `b_1=R`, primal optimal trace bound `R`, attained dual optimum with an optimal `\ell_1` bound `r\ge1`, maximum sparsity `s`, and their finite-precision sparse index/value oracles, its algorithm returns a feasible dual vector within additive `\varepsilon` of optimum with bounded failure. The source reports `\widetilde O(\sqrt{nm}s^2(Rr/\varepsilon)^8)` input-matrix queries and the same order of other gates; the diagonal LP case has exponent 5. The proposed proof route names the Gibbs-state purification, matrix expectation oracle, MMW feasibility implication, and binary-search correctness as separate local suppliers. It also exposes and charges preparation of the maximally entangled reference state. For d=2^q, prepare `|Φ_d⟩` with q Hadamards and q CNOTs; for arbitrary d, keep the supplied preparation cost C_Φ as an explicit term. The QC24 amplitude-table upper bound is not used to claim the source solver’s gate count for this reference state. It tracks the whole error budget and returns a classical dual vector, not a dense primal matrix.

The source's general sparse-sum access construction explicitly assumes a unit-cost QRAM gate in its computational model. Thus the stated theorem is an oracle/query bound under that model; it is not an ordinary standard-gate count until the QRAM and data-structure operations are implemented and charged. Matrix-entry precision, inverse index access, sparse-sum creation, QRAM gate/data memory, classical control, C_Φ reference-state and Gibbs state preparation, and output-bit cost are explicit parameters. The reported source gate bound applies to the decomposed implementation only after these costs are shown to fit within it; otherwise retain the query guarantee and add their actual costs. No free QRAM or state preparation is presumed. Brandão–Svore's older theorem remains a source comparison with its own assumptions; it is not used as a substitute for the later theorem or its proof.

The convex-oracle result is narrower still. For a convex body with `B(0,r)\subseteq K\subseteq B(0,R)`, van Apeldoorn et al. Theorem 23 gives a quantum membership-to-weak-separation procedure with `\eta\in(0,R]`, `\rho\in(0,1/3]`, membership precision `\varepsilon\le\eta(58n)^{-9/2}(r/R)^3(\eta/R)^2\rho`, and `O(log(n/\rho)log((n/\rho)(R/\eta)(R/r)))` quantum membership queries to the oracle and its inverse. The proof uses the stated smoothing and gradient route. Composing with classical separation-to-optimization requires its own oracle, radius, precision, and complexity proof; it is not inferred from the weak-separation theorem alone.

A VQA has a finite parameter domain, supplied parameterized circuit, explicitly prepared/charged input states and observables, and a declared objective such as `C(\theta)=\sum_k f_k(Tr[O_kU(\theta)\rho_kU(\theta)^†])`. The optimizer, measurement estimator, precision, stopping rule and output are part of the algorithm. For VQE, the Rayleigh–Ritz inequality gives `E(\theta)\ge E_0`; equality holds precisely when the ansatz state lies in the ground eigenspace. This is an upper-bound certificate, not an optimizer convergence theorem. For a Pauli generator `P^2=I` in a single parameter gate `e^{-i\theta P/2}`, the exact parameter-shift formula is `C'(\theta)=(C(\theta+\pi/2)-C(\theta-\pi/2))/2`; finite-shot estimates carry separate sampling costs.

For MaxCut QAOA, `C_G=\sum_{(u,v)\in E}(I-Z_uZ_v)/2`, `B=\sum_vX_v`, and the depth-`p` circuit alternates the declared cost and mixer exponentials from `|+\rangle^{\otimes|V|}`. Its objective is the expected feasible cut value. The expectation lies between the minimum and maximum cut scores and a measurement returns an actual cut; no universal approximation ratio, global optimum, optimizer convergence, or quantum advantage follows without additional assumptions. The B page works small VQE/QAOA and SDP instances and gives an ansatz-misspecification counterexample.
