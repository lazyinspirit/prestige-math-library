## QA11: Grover search and integer rotations

For `N=2^n` and marked set `M⊆[N]` with `t=|M|`, the task supplies the standard XOR bit oracle for `1_M`. QA02 phase kickback implements `O_M|x⟩=(-1)^{1_M(x)}|x⟩` with one bit query per Grover iterate; measuring a candidate and verifying membership takes a separate bit query because a bare uncontrolled phase query cannot reveal `1_M(x)`. The uniform state is prepared exactly as `|s⟩=H^⊗n|0^n⟩=N^{-1/2}Σ_x|x⟩`, using n Hadamard gates under the QC07/QC09 gate-resource contract. When `0<t<N`, the normalized marked and unmarked states span an invariant plane. In ordered basis `(|B⟩,|G⟩)`, the iterate matrix is `[[cos(2θ),−sin(2θ)],[sin(2θ),cos(2θ)]]`, and

`|s⟩=sin(θ)|G⟩+cos(θ)|B⟩`, with `sin²θ=t/N` and `0<θ<π/2`.

The iterate `G=(2|s⟩⟨s|−I)O_M` is a rotation on this plane. After exactly `k∈ℕ` iterations, the probability of measuring a marked item is `sin²((2k+1)θ)`. For `t=0` and `t=N`, define the behavior by explicit one-dimensional branches; never normalize a nonexistent marked or unmarked vector. For known `t`, the exact-maximizer procedure chooses an integer with maximum success probability among a finite range: a nearest integer to `π/(4θ)−1/2` is within `ceil√(N/t)` when `t/N≤1/2` and achieves success at least `1−t/N`; for `t/N>1/2`, direct uniform sampling already succeeds with probability greater than one half. The maximization uses the rational recurrence `T_0=1`, `T_1=q`, `T_{m+1}=2qT_m−T_{m−1}` for `q=1−2t/N`, with `p_k=(1−T_{2k+1}(q))/2`. Its schoolbook bit cost is bounded by `O(K²n²)` for `K=ceil√(N/t)`; because `K` can be exponential in `n`, the query theorem makes no end-to-end polynomial-time claim.

For known `t`, a separate randomized variant avoids the exact finite maximization: for `0<t≤N/2`, binary-search the least integer `K` with `tK²≥N`, set `b=ceil(log₂K)`, and in each of nine rounds draw `j` from `0,…,2^b−1`, reject if `j≥K`, otherwise run `j` iterates and verify the measured candidate. The conditional average success over accepted `j` is `1/2−sin(4Kθ)/(4K sin(2θ))≥1/4`; rejection sampling accepts with probability at least `1/2`, so each round succeeds with probability at least `1/8` and nine rounds exceed `2/3`. If `t>N/2`, nine direct uniform samples suffice; if `t=0`, return `none`. The randomized algorithm uses `O(√(N/t))` bit queries and `O(n³)` classical bit work to compute `K`. Uniform preparation uses `H^⊗n`; each reflection about `|s⟩` is `H^⊗n R_0 H^⊗n`, with the exact `R_0` gate cost charged under the QC reflection model.

For unknown `t`, the BBHT schedule prepares its uniform state with `H^⊗n|0^n⟩`, tests a uniformly sampled candidate, and then chooses a random Grover iteration count from a geometrically growing range. It verifies every candidate before returning it; the n Hadamards and reflection gates are charged separately from oracle queries. For `t>0` it halts almost surely with expected `O(√(N/t))` oracle queries. If `t=0`, it does not halt; a finite-time emptiness decision is a different problem. De Wolf’s notes give only a sketch of this unknown-count analysis, while BHMT provides the algorithm and an outline. The item inventory leaves the random-iteration average and the expected-cost sum as local proof obligations.

## QA12: amplitude amplification and phase robustness

Supply a measurement-free unitary `A` and an orthogonal success projection `Π_good` such that `A|0⟩=√a|G⟩+√(1−a)|B⟩`. The standard iterate is `Q=−A(I−2|0⟩⟨0|)A†(I−2Π_good)`. In ordered basis `(|B⟩,|G⟩)`, its matrix is `[[cos(2θ),−sin(2θ)],[sin(2θ),cos(2θ)]]`; after `k` calls the success probability is `sin²((2k+1)θ)`, where `sin²θ=a`. Boundary values `a=0,1` are handled directly. Each iterate uses one A, one A†, and one good-subspace phase operation, while initial preparation and verification are counted separately.

When `a` is known, integer rounding gives constant success in `O(1/√a)` calls. A separate exact-success procedure dilutes the good amplitude with a clean ancilla to an angle exactly hit by an integer number of rotations; its theorem depends on the exact controlled predicate and exact ancilla rotation used. When `a` is unknown, the randomized geometric schedule has expected `Θ(1/√a)` access cost for `a>0` and does not halt at `a=0`. Both the almost-sure guarantee and expected-cost proof remain distinct results.

Høyer’s complete six-page paper supports two further proposed results. For arbitrary phase reflections, equal diagonal entries of the two-dimensional iterate occur exactly when `tan(ϕ/2)=tan(φ/2)(1−2a)`, with phase branches and the `φ=π` case stated separately. If the implemented phase differs from the matched phase by `δ`, Høyer’s Theorem 3 gives failure at most `4a+ε` under the explicit bound `δ≤ε√3/[2π²(√3+π)φ√a]`. The equal-phase corollary has failure `O(√a/φ)` over its stated range. This is a bounded-error robustness result, not a claim that arbitrary uncalibrated phases succeed.

## QA13: amplitude estimation and counting

The main estimator starts with the same `A`, `Π_good`, and Grover iterate `Q`. On the good/bad plane in ordered basis `(|B⟩,|G⟩)`, its matrix is `[[cos(2θ),−sin(2θ)],[sin(2θ),cos(2θ)]]`, so its eigenphases are `±2θ` and `a=sin²θ`. With `m` phase qubits, set `M=2^m`, apply the controlled powers `Q^{2^j}` for `j=0,…,m−1` using the declared access mode, perform inverse Fourier measurement, and map outcome `y` to `ã=sin²(πy/M)`. BHMT’s theorem gives

`|ã−a| ≤ 2πk√(a(1−a))/M + π²k²/M²`

with probability at least `8/π²` for `k=1` (with the theorem’s `M>2` condition) and greater than `1−1/[2(k−1)]` for `k≥2`. At constant confidence, additive `ε` precision therefore uses `O(1/ε)` Grover/oracle-scale calls. For an explicit confidence wrapper, choose power-of-two `M≥4π/ε` and an odd number `r≥ln(1/δ)/(2(8/π²−1/2)²)` of independent runs; the lower median has error at most `ε` with probability at least `1−δ`, using `O(ε⁻¹log(1/δ))` controlled-iterate accesses. In the repeated controlled-`Q` model, the powers cost `M−1` calls; in a supplied-power model, count each declared power query and its stated cost. Bare uncontrolled phase-oracle access does not provide controlled powers.

For counting on `N=2^n` addresses, prepare the exact uniform state as `H^⊗n|0^n⟩`, use the membership predicate as `Π_good`, and multiply the amplitude estimate by `N`. QC07 fixes H and QC09 counts the n Hadamard gates separately from controlled membership queries. The query contract explicitly supplies controlled membership-reflection access; a bare phase query is not enough for QPE. The direct error statement is `|t̃−t|≤2πk√(t(N−t))/M+π²k²N/M²`, with the same confidence and the appropriate endpoint conditions. BHMT’s separate finite schedule gives relative error `εt` with probability at least `2/3` using expected `O(ε⁻¹√(N/t))` queries when `t>0`; when `t=0` it outputs zero after a finite cutoff, with `O(√N/ε)` queries for the displayed ε-dependent final estimator (the source Θ(√N) phrase treats ε as fixed). The inventory keeps these relative-counting branches and their rounding/success proof separate from the raw amplitude estimator.

As a no-QFT variant, I included a finite-grid maximum-likelihood procedure inspired by Suzuki et al. It measures several uncontrolled powers of `Q`, combines the finite sample likelihoods on an algebraic grid, and has an exact access-count theorem. The inspected paper establishes an asymptotic Cramér–Rao analysis and numerical behavior, but does not give a finite-sample `ε,δ` tail theorem for the discretized procedure. The proposed variant is therefore not a source of a worst-case success claim; a complete finite-sample theorem must be proved before that variant is advertised with a guarantee.

The finalized QA13 interface for QA29 is `alg-qa-13-phase-estimation-amplitude-estimation`, `thm-qa-13-amplitude-estimation-guarantee`, `thm-qa-13-amplitude-estimation-query-complexity`, and the explicit median-confidence algorithm/theorems `alg-qa-13-median-confidence-estimation`, `thm-qa-13-median-confidence-guarantee`, and `thm-qa-13-median-confidence-query-cost`. QA29 owns its coherent sampler, value rotation, and input/data-access accounting; this section does not duplicate those contracts.

## QA14: the BBBV hybrid lower bound

Use the standard XOR bit oracle, a query-address decomposition with projectors `Π_i`, and input-independent unitaries between queries. The per-query hybrid estimate is proved directly for this bit-oracle model. Replacing the all-zero oracle by the oracle with marked index `i` changes one query step by at most `2||Π_iψ_t||`. Telescoping over `T` queries gives

`||ψ_T^i−ψ_T^0|| ≤ 2Σ_{t<T}√q_{t,i}`.

Since the address probabilities sum to at most one per time, Cauchy–Schwarz yields the aggregate bound `Σ_i||ψ_T^i−ψ_T^0||²≤4T²`. A bounded-error decision algorithm has an acceptance gap of at least `1/3` for every one-marked input, forcing a constant state distance for every `i`; hence `T=Ω(√N)`. A finder yields a decision procedure with one additional bit query to verify its candidate. The known-count Grover procedure gives the matching `O(√N)` upper bound, so decision and finding both have `Θ(√N)` bit-query complexity.

The full BBBV paper was read. Its Theorem 3.3 and Corollary 3.4 provide the query-magnitude hybrid argument, while Theorem 3.5 proves a random-oracle complexity-class separation. The direct single-marked-search lower bound above is a local specialization and must be proved as such; the random-oracle theorem is not substituted for it.

## QA15: the polynomial method

In the bit-query model, every basis amplitude after `T` queries is a polynomial of degree at most `T`; the final acceptance probability is a real multilinear polynomial of degree at most `2T`. Thus `Q_ε(f)≥ceil(approxdeg_ε(f)/2)`. The root library supplies formal polynomial degree and the unique multilinear reduction on the Boolean cube. The finite-grid inequality needed for OR is not present in the root library: `items/thm-markov-inequality.md` is probability Markov, and `items/thm-chebyshev-markov-inequality-for-the-integral.md` is an integral inequality.

For OR, symmetrize to a univariate polynomial `q(k)`. Its endpoint change gives a derivative lower bound; the discrete Markov/Ehlich–Zeller route controls the continuous range using the integer-grid values `q(k)∈[0,1]`, then the Chebyshev-based Markov derivative bound gives degree `Ω(√N)`. The inventory splits the grid-return estimate from the continuous Markov step so the proof does not hide the key issue that off-grid values can be large. Exact OR degree is `N`, and parity has approximate degree `N` for error below one half by alternating signs at the integer weights. Promise-domain approximations are always quantified only on the promised set.

## QA16: general adversary bounds and tightness

For finite `D⊆{0,1}^N`, define mismatch masks `Δ_i`, the same-output mask `F`, and a real symmetric adversary matrix `Γ` satisfying `Γ∘F=0`. The value is `Adv±(f)=max_Γ ||Γ||/max_i||Γ∘Δ_i||`; negative entries are allowed. The lower bound is decomposed into a progress-function endpoint estimate and a one-query change estimate. For bounded error `ε`, the proposed progress normalization gives `Q_ε(f)≥(1/2−√(ε(1−ε)))Adv±(f)` subject to the stated constant convention.

For the upper bound, the proposed local chain is explicit: (1) prove the finite SDP dual vector formulation and its strong-duality hypotheses; (2) prove the Boolean filtered-`γ₂`/`Adv±` equality using the finite Schur-product lemma; (3) construct the input-consistency and witness-span reflections from a dual certificate; (4) prove the effective spectral gap by Jordan’s two-projection decomposition; (5) use phase detection; each controlled input-consistency reflection is implemented with two ordinary bit queries, and repeated controlled powers are counted explicitly. Correctness/success, query cost, and the final `Q(f)=Θ(Adv±(f))` theorem are separate items. Gate synthesis of the dual-span reflection is not bounded by the query theorem.

The source distinction matters. Reichardt’s 2009 Theorem 10.1 proves an upper bound with a logarithmic factor and its §11 explicitly conjectures removing it. The later Lee–Mittal–Reichardt–Špalek–Szegedy state-conversion paper states `Q(f)=Θ(Adv±(f))` and supplies a full finite-dimensional reflection/effective-gap/phase-detection proof route for the Boolean function specialization. Childs’s complete course notes give the dual-vector algorithm and Jordan-lemma gap proof. No open conjecture is used as a supplier.
