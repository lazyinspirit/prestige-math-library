## Shared conventions and supplier boundary

All state spaces used here are finite complex inner-product spaces. The root convention is linear in the first argument; the QC item `def-qc-dirac-notation-conversion` fixes the Dirac convention `⟨x|y⟩_D = ⟨y,x⟩_root`, so translate the two conventions in matrix-entry calculations. A Markov transition matrix is row-stochastic: `P_xy` is the probability of moving from `x` to `y`. Walks and matrix exponentials use that convention consistently.

A statement about a transition matrix, a Hermitian matrix, or its exponential is mathematical data. It does not posit a physical process. A quantum-walk step or Hamiltonian evolution is an explicitly named unitary in a finite circuit/access model. Any unitary, inverse, controlled unitary, row-state preparation, stationary-state preparation, reflection, coefficient preparation, and state output needed by an algorithm is named and charged separately. A matrix description alone does not give an efficient circuit for any of those operations.

The root audit checked the actual statements of `def-promise-problem`, `def-transition-matrix-and-n-step-transition-probabilities`, `def-invariant-and-stationary-distribution-for-a-markov-kernel`, `def-reversible-measure-and-detailed-balance`, `lem-detailed-balance-implies-invariance`, `thm-every-finite-transition-matrix-has-a-stationary-distribution`, `ex-random-walk-on-a-finite-undirected-graph-is-reversible`, `def-adjoint-of-a-linear-map-between-inner-product-spaces`, `def-self-adjoint-and-normal-endomorphism`, `thm-complex-spectral-theorem-for-normal-endomorphisms`, `def-functional-calculus-for-a-normal-endomorphism`, `def-operator-norm-on-a-finite-dimensional-inner-product-space`, `thm-operator-norm-is-the-largest-singular-value`, and `cor-operator-norm-submultiplicative-and-t-star-t-identity`. ## QA17 — Discrete-time and Szegedy walks

The core walk input is any finite state set `X` and row-stochastic matrix `P`. The reflection walk below is defined for this general input. Reversible-chain results additionally supply a strictly positive stationary distribution `π` with `πP=π` and detailed balance `π_x P_xy = π_y P_yx`.

\[
D_{xy}=\sqrt{P_{xy}P_{yx}},\qquad
|\psi_x\rangle=|x\rangle\sum_y\sqrt{P_{xy}}|y\rangle,
\]

Let `T|x⟩=|ψ_x⟩`, `Π=TT†`, and let `S|x,y⟩=|y,x⟩`. Then `T†T=I` and `T†ST=D`. For every finite `P`, `D_xy=sqrt(P_xy P_yx)` is real symmetric and `||D||≤1`, since `T` is an isometry and `S` is unitary. Under detailed balance, `D=diag(sqrt(π)) P diag(1/sqrt(π))`; Jensen and stationarity also give `||P||_{L²(π)}≤1`. The walk convention is `U=S(2Π−I)`; its two-reflection square is `W=U²=(2SΠS−I)(2Π−I)`. This keeps phases `±arccos(λ)` distinct from `±2 arccos(λ)`.

For an eigenpair `Dv=λv`, `span{Tv,STv}` is invariant. In that ordered, generally nonorthogonal basis, `U(Tv)=STv` and `U(STv)=−Tv+2λSTv`, so the characteristic polynomial is `z²−2λz+1`. For `−1<λ<1`, the eigenvalues are `exp(±i arccos λ)`; `W=U²` has `exp(±2i arccos λ)`. At `λ=±1`, the span may collapse and is handled as a one-dimensional eigenspace. On the remaining orthogonal complement `U=−S`, so its spectrum lies in `{−1,1}`.

The access contract supplies row preparation `V_P: |x,0⟩ ↦ |x⟩Σ_y sqrt(P_xy)|y⟩`, its inverse, and controlled versions only when requested, for any finite row-stochastic `P`. The reflection about `ran(T)` is `V_P(2|0⟩⟨0|−I)V_P†`; charge the listed `V_P` and `V_P†` calls and gates. A controlled walk step needs controlled preparation and controlled inverse access. The swap is a finite register permutation with its own gate cost. Matrix entries alone do not provide efficient row preparation.

The B companion calculates a two-state reversible chain and a periodic/bipartite chain with eigenvalue `−1`, showing why a gap must specify absolute value or laziness. It distinguishes `U` from `W=U²` and shows that zero stationary weights invalidate the reversible similarity transform.

## QA18 — Search, hitting time, and element distinctness

For the elementary classical compression bound, assume a finite, uniform, symmetric lazy chain, so the spectrum of `P` is nonnegative and its second eigenvalue is at most `1−δ`. Let `M⊆X`, `ε=|M|/|X|`, and `P_M=Π_(X\M) P Π_(X\M)`. For a unit vector supported off `M`, the squared component parallel to the uniform vector is at most `1−ε`; its orthogonal component has Rayleigh quotient at most `1−δ`. Hence `||P_M||≤1−δε`. Starting uniformly on unmarked states, the no-hit probability after `t` steps is at most `||P_M||^t`; constant success takes `O(1/(δε))` steps. For arbitrary reversible chains, use the absolute gap `δ=1−max_{λ≠1}|λ|` and the separate reversible-chain theorem; do not use the nonnegative-spectrum Rayleigh proof on a non-lazy chain.

Detection and finding are distinct promise tasks. For detection, promise either `M=∅` or `π(M)≥ε₀` and output one bit. For finding, under the nonempty case output a marked label; the MNRS procedure below supplies that contract. In the detector take a finite symmetric lazy chain with uniform stationary law and spectral gap `δ(P)=1−λ₂(P)≥δ₀` with `δ₀∈(0,1]`; laziness makes its spectrum nonnegative. Make marked states absorbing by defining `P′_xy=1` for `x=y∈M`, `P′_xy=0` for `x∈M,y≠x`, and `P′_xy=P_xy` for `x∉M`. This `P′` is row-stochastic but generally is not symmetric or reversible. The general row-stochastic Szegedy reflection walk from QA17 still applies. Its discriminant is exactly `D′=P_{UU}⊕I_M`, where `U=X\M`; the symmetric-chain compression bound gives `spec(P_{UU})⊆[0,1−δ₀ε₀]` whenever `M` is nonempty. Use a supplied uniform-state circuit `A_unif` of cost `C_unif` for the original symmetric chain, then apply `V_{P′}` to obtain `T′|√π⟩` in `ran(T′)`. The chain matrix does not supply `A_unif`. Measure the marked predicate on the first register. A marked outcome immediately returns “nonempty,” which also covers `M=X`; only the complementary branch is normalized and passed to phase estimation. That conditioned state is `T′|√π_U⟩/sqrt(1−ε)`, lies in `T′(C^U)⊆ran(T′)`, has no overlap with the marked `+1` eigenspace, and has no component in the orthogonal complement of the signal planes. In the empty case the initial state is an exact zero-phase eigenstate. In the nonempty no-mark branch every phase has circular angular distance at least `γ₀=arccos(1−δ₀ε₀)>0` from zero.

For a finite detector, set `Q=2^m≥32π/γ₀`, `m=ceil(log₂(32π/γ₀))`, and run the QA07 `alg-qa-quantum-phase-estimation` on the one-reflection walk `U′` using its controlled powers. If the measured integer is `y`, set `d(y)=min(y/Q,1−y/Q)` and declare “nonempty” when `d(y)>γ₀/(4π)`. The QA07 `thm-qa-phase-estimation-success-and-tail` at fractional accuracy `η=γ₀/(8π)` has `K=floor(Qη)≥4`, so the failure probability is at most `1/[2(K−1)]≤1/6` on every eigenphase outside the gap; `thm-qa-phase-estimation-exact-grid` makes the empty case return `y=0` exactly. The superposition law `thm-qa-phase-estimation-superposition-law` extends the eigenphase bound to the conditioned no-mark state. Check the marked predicate first and declare “nonempty” if a marked label is observed. Repeat this fresh-state run `k=ceil(log(1/β)/log 6)` times and return “nonempty” if any run accepts; then the empty-case error is zero and the nonempty-case false-empty probability is at most `β`. Since `γ₀=Θ(sqrt(δ₀ε₀))` in the small-gap regime, controlled one-step walk access costs `O(1/sqrt(δ₀ε₀))` per run (or exactly `Q−1` controlled `U′` calls when powers are built from a single-step oracle); repeat for the stated confidence. If `C_init=C_unif+C_row` covers the supplied uniform-state circuit and applying `V_{P′}`, `C_mark` is the marked check, `C_walk` is one controlled `U′` implementation, and `G_QPE(m)` covers the phase register and inverse QFT, then the total is at most `k(C_init+C_mark+(Q−1)C_walk+G_QPE(m))` in a single-step access model; controlled-power access replaces `(Q−1)C_walk` by the sum of its `m` supplied power costs. One controlled walk call includes its controlled `V_{P′}`, `V_{P′}†`, reflection and swap charges. Finite-gate synthesis and coefficient bits are additional. This decision procedure does not output a marked vertex.

For finding, the MNRS theorem uses setup/update/check costs. Let `S₀` prepare the stationary-distribution state and its data, `U₀` implement one transition update and inverse, and `C` perform a coherent marked-state check. For a finite ergodic reversible chain of absolute spectral gap `δ` and stationary marked mass at least `ε`, a one-sided-error procedure finds a marked element with constant probability at cost

\[
S_0+O\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt\epsilon}\left(\frac{U_0}{\sqrt\delta}+C\right)\right).
\]

The proof is approximate diffusion from phase estimation followed by recursive amplitude amplification; the recursion removes the logarithmic factor in the simpler detector. To reduce error to `η`, independent repetition costs an additional `O(log(1/η))` factor. State preparation is part of `S₀`; reverse-chain/update access and its inverse are assumptions, not free operations.

For element distinctness, the input is a value oracle `f:[N]→[M]` with coherent reversible forward and inverse access. Use the lazy Johnson walk on `J(N,r)` with uniform stationary measure: with probability 1/2 stay put, otherwise replace one selected index by one outside index. For `1≤r≤N/2`, its gap is `δ=N/[2r(N−r)]`. Mark a subset when two distinct stored indices have equal values; a single collision pair marks fraction `ε=r(r−1)/[N(N−1)]`. Setup queries `r` values (`S₀=r`); one update erases the old value and loads the new one (`U₀=2` queries); checking costs zero additional queries when the values are stored. The search cost is `r+O(N/sqrt(r))`, minimized at `r=Θ(N^(2/3))`, for `O(N^(2/3))` value queries. The measured marked subset can be scanned reversibly to output a pair. Query complexity excludes scans and gates. Ambainis’s polylogarithmic gate-time result assumes a random-access quantum-memory gate model, canonical set storage, bounded-time skip-list operations and extra memory; it is not an ordinary two-qubit gate bound. If f is instead given by a uniform Boolean circuit with G gates, depth D, maximum per-wire layer fanout F, and m output bits, the QC10 reversible-embedding bridge uses fresh-target X/CNOT and round-based copy/unfanout; its stated depth is `2D(2ceil(log₂(F+1))+3)+2ceil(log₂(m+1))+1` per compiled query. Multiply by sequential query uses and keep this gate depth separate from the value-oracle query count.

The B companion works out `J(N,r)` for `r=2,3`, the unique-collision marked fraction, multiple collisions, boundary cases `r=0,N`, and the separation between query cost, comparison/sort gates, QRAM assumptions, and qubit memory.

## QA19 — Continuous-time walks as finite matrix algorithms

For a finite-dimensional complex inner-product space and Hermitian `H`, define `U_H(t)=f_t(H)` by the finite normal functional calculus, where `f_t(λ)=exp(−itλ)`. The spectral resolution gives `U_H(t)†U_H(t)=I`, `U_H(t+s)=U_H(t)U_H(s)`, and `dU_H/dt=−iHU_H`. These are finite-matrix theorems, not physical postulates. For a finite undirected weighted graph, adjacency `A=A†` is a generator; the positive Laplacian `D−A` and Childs’s `A−D` convention differ by a sign, which reverses time.

A continuous-time walk initialized at `|x⟩` is `U_H(t)|x⟩`; the basis measurement probability of `y` is `|⟨y|U_H(t)|x⟩|²` under the QC measurement interface. For the Boolean hypercube, `A=Σ_j X_j`, so `exp(−iAπ/2)|0^n⟩=(−i)^n|1^n⟩`.

A second example is continuous-time search on the complete graph with arbitrary finite `N≥2`. Encode the `N` labels in an active register subspace and supply a uniform-state preparation isometry/circuit `A_s` with `A_s|0⟩=|s⟩=N^(−1/2)Σ_x|x⟩` and charged cost `C_s`; the matrix description does not construct `A_s`. For `N=2^n`, the special preparation is `Had^⊗n` at cost `n` Hadamard gates. Given a marked vertex `w`, set `H=−A/N−|w⟩⟨w|`, charging the marked rank-one term through its oracle access and the known adjacency term through its gate or simulation implementation. On `span{|w⟩,|r⟩}`, with `|r⟩` uniform on unmarked vertices, the two-dimensional restriction gives exact marked probability `1` at time `πsqrt(N)/2`. If the implemented evolution has operator-norm error `ρ`, the success probability is at least `1−2ρ`. Preparation, input oracle, simulation precision, graph gates, and classical bits for `N,w` are separate costs. No universal spatial-graph speedup is asserted.

The resource-accounting theorem keeps the two algorithms’ correctness results separate from costs. It sums the supplied initialization, evolution/simulation, and readout query, gate, and description-bit costs componentwise; the hypercube factorization uses `n` one-qubit evolution gates and measures `n` output bits, while complete-graph search adds the declared `A_s` cost (or `n` Hadamard gates when `N=2^n`). No gate bound follows from the finite matrix description alone.

The B page verifies hypercube factorization for `n=1,2`, evaluates the complete-graph 2×2 matrix for general `N`, checks the supplied `A_s` versus the power-of-two Hadamard construction, and compares adjacency, normalized-adjacency, and Laplacian generators on an irregular graph.

## QA20 — Product formulas and finite-matrix simulation

Simulation error is operator norm: `V` simulates `H` for time `t` to precision `ε` when `||V−U_H(t)||≤ε`. A term-list model supplies a uniform description `H=Σ_j H_j`, Hermitian `H_j`, and implementations of `exp(−iH_j s)` plus any controlled/inverse form used. Query, gate, and coefficient-bit costs are separate parameters `q_j,g_j,b_j`. A sparse-matrix model instead supplies row-location and entry-value oracles with finite-precision complex-entry encoding; sparsity alone does not give an efficient term list.

For `h=t/r`, use the first-order formula `S₁(h)=Π_j exp(−ihH_j)` in a fixed announced order. For skew-Hermitian `A_j=−iH_j`, the two-term Duhamel identity gives

\[
\|e^{h(A+B)}-e^{hA}e^{hB}\|\le \frac{h^2}{2}\|[A,B]\|.
\]

Splitting off terms and telescoping unitary factors gives the local `m`-term bound `h² Σ_(j<k)||[H_j,H_k]||/2`. Telescoping across `r` steps gives

\[
\|S_1(t/r)^r-U_H(t)\|\le \frac{t^2}{2r}\sum_{j<k}\|[H_j,H_k]\|.
\]

The query cost is `r Σ_j q_j`; gate cost is `r Σ_j g_j` plus uniform control and arithmetic overhead. Coefficient bits and classical construction time are separate.

The symmetric second-order formula is `S₂(h)=exp(−ihH₁/2)…exp(−ihH_(m−1)/2) exp(−ihH_m) exp(−ihH_(m−1)/2)…exp(−ihH₁/2)`. Its Taylor coefficients through order two agree with `exp(−ihH)`. Bounding both third derivatives by `(Σ_j||H_j||)^3` gives local error `|h|³(Σ_j||H_j||)^3/3` and global error `|t|³(Σ_j||H_j||)^3/(3r²)`. Higher-order Suzuki formulas need a separate full order-condition/nested-commutator proof and term-access cost derivation.

The B page computes commuting and noncommuting two-term cases, checks the first-order commutator bound, and shows why choosing `r` from `||H||` and `ε` alone can be costly.

## QA21 — LCU, oblivious amplification, and Taylor simulation

An LCU input is an explicit finite sum `M=Σ_j c_j V_j`, with unitary `V_j` and explicit `PREPARE`, `PREPARE†`, `SELECT`, plus each controlled/inverse form used. Let `α=Σ_j |c_j|`. `PREPARE|0⟩=Σ_j sqrt(|c_j|/α)|j⟩`; coefficient phases go in `SELECT`. Then `W=PREPARE† SELECT PREPARE` has projected block `M/α`. This is a block encoding and postselected application; it does not by itself give deterministic implementation.

For `H=Σ_j a_jV_j` with Hermitian-unitary `V_j`, let `Λ=Σ_j|a_j|`. For a segment of duration `h`, define `M_K(h)=Σ_(k=0)^K (−ihH)^k/k!`. Its exact LCU coefficient 1-norm is `s_K=Σ_(k=0)^K (Λ|h|)^k/k!`. If `Λ|h|≤ln 2`, then `s_K≤2`. Pad with cancelling terms `+(2−s_K)I/2` and `−(2−s_K)I/2`; the represented matrix is unchanged and the normalization is exactly 2.

For `R=I−2|0⟩⟨0|⊗I`, the three-use circuit `WRW†RW` has exact top block `−3M_K/2 + M_K M_K† M_K/2`. If `M_K` is unitary this is `−M_K`. For truncated Taylor, robust OAA must prove the full ancilla-cleanup/output error from near-unitarity. A global minus sign is harmless only for an uncontrolled output; under control it is a relative phase and needs an explicit correction.

The power-series remainder gives

\[
\|M_K(h)-e^{-ihH}\|\le e^{\Lambda|h|}(\Lambda|h|)^{K+1}/(K+1)!.
\]

Choose `r=ceil(Λ|t|/ln 2)` (and `r=1` for the identity/short-time branch), so every segment meets the normalization condition. Choose `K` for per-segment Taylor/OAA error at most `ε/(c r)`, with `c` supplied by the robust theorem. Repeat segment preparation, `SELECT`, OAA, required phase correction, and cleanup. Query, gate, state-preparation, controlled/inverse, and coefficient-description costs are distinct; `rK` `SELECT` calls do not imply `rK` gates when `SELECT` is oracle access.

The B page expands a short two-term LCU by hand, checks the cancellation padding, and exhibits the failure of ordinary amplitude amplification if an unknown input register is reflected about as though its state were known.

## QA25 — Qubitization and near-optimal simulation

This page consumes the frozen QA22–24 interfaces from qa_r5: block-encoding and access definitions; LCU/product construction and error/cost theorems; QSP admissible polynomial-pair, factorization, synthesis and query-cost results; and QSVT singular-value-transform, SVD invariant-space, action and resource results. The access model names `U`, `U†`, controlled variants, reflections, `PREPARE`/`SELECT`, and phase synthesis individually.

For Hermitian `H` with normalized block encoding `H/α`, isolate each eigenvector `|ψ_λ⟩` and the orthogonal ancilla components `(I−Π)U|0,ψ_λ⟩` and `(I−Π)U†|0,ψ_λ⟩`. Alternating `U/U†` with the projector reflection preserves these two-dimensional signal spaces and induces the scalar QSP signal with `x=λ/α`. QA24 handles zero singular directions and rectangular padding; the Hermitian specialization must also retain the sign of `λ`. A real parity-bounded scalar theorem alone does not implement complex non-parity `exp(−itλ)`. The proof must explicitly combine even/odd parts (or use the full Gilyén construction) with normalization and error accounting.

The scalar approximation is the Jacobi–Anger/Chebyshev truncation of `exp(−iτx)`, where `τ=αt`. Prove a uniform error on `[-1,1]` and degree

\[
d=O\!\left(\tau+\frac{\log(1/\epsilon)}{\log(e+\log(1/\epsilon)/\tau)}\right)
\]

for `τ>0`, with the identity branch at `t=0`. The full Gilyén construction, not only Childs’s scalar theorem sketch, is the proof locator for conversion to a unitary simulator. It uses `O(d)` signal accesses and phase/reflection operations; controlled/inverse calls, coefficient and phase precision, phase-list computation, and block-combination overhead remain explicit. Gate/bit cost sums those implementations; it is not the query count.

For `d_H`-sparse Hermitian input with row-location and entry-value oracles and `||H||_max≤h_max`, construct a block encoding with normalization `α=d_H h_max` (or prove a sharper bound). Each block-encoding call charges sparse-oracle calls, reversible row/column computation, and amplitude preparation. The simulator uses `O(αt+log(1/ε)/loglog(1/ε))` signal queries in the standard regime; gate precision and oracle circuit costs are additional.

The lower-bound route is separated into the path-transfer lemma, the parity query lower bound, and the sparse-oracle reduction. For `x∈{0,1}^n`, set `w_i=sqrt(i(n+1−i))/n` and

\[
H_x=\sum_{i=1}^n w_i\sum_{b\in\{0,1\}}\bigl(|i-1,b\rangle\langle i,b\oplus x_i|+|i,b\oplus x_i\rangle\langle i-1,b|\bigr).
\]

Each row has at most two nonzero entries. Let `p_i=x_1⊕⋯⊕x_i` and `V_x|i,b⟩=|i,b⊕p_i⟩`. This basis permutation conjugates `H_x` to `H_0⊗I`, where `H_0=(2/n)J_x` on the symmetric `n`-qubit subspace and `J_x=(1/2)Σ_jX_j`. Its norm is at most one, and `exp(−iH_0πn/2)|0^n⟩=(−i)^n|1^n⟩`; hence `exp(−iH_xπn/2)|0,0⟩=(−i)^n|n,parity(x)⟩`. For the finite-precision sparse oracle, return symmetric entry approximations with error `η≤1/(6πn)` per entry; the degree-two row/column bound gives `||H_x−Ĥ_x||≤2η`, so Duhamel changes the time-`πn/2` unitary by at most `1/6`. This takes `b=O(log n)` fractional bits. Each row-location or entry query for `Ĥ_x` asks only a constant number of input bits. An operator-error-`1/3` simulator for the encoded matrix therefore computes parity with error at most `1/4` by measuring the final clock/bit, still a bounded-error algorithm. The parity query lower bound follows because a `q`-query algorithm's signed output expectation is a polynomial of degree at most `2q`, while any polynomial approximating the degree-`n` parity sign function to error below one must have nonzero parity correlation; orthogonality forces `2q≥n`. Therefore simulation at `t=πn/2` uses `Ω(n)=Ω(t)` sparse-oracle queries. The family has sparse normalization `α_sparse=2||Ĥ_x||_max=Θ(1)`, so this matches `Ω(α_sparse t)` for this sparse-oracle family. This does not establish a lower bound for an unrestricted block-encoding oracle or for the additive precision term.

The B companion tracks a 2D block-encoding invariant plane, checks zero singular subspaces, computes a low-degree Chebyshev truncation, contrasts sparse-oracle and block-encoding normalizations, and verifies the parity reduction's norm/time scaling.

## QA36 — Finite-matrix adiabatic computation and circuit equivalence

An adiabatic instance is finite-dimensional data `C^d`, a `C²` path `H:[0,1]→Herm(C^d)`, an isolated spectral projection `P(s)` with uniform full gap `g(s)>0`, and a supplied initial preparation/isometry `A_0` of declared cost `C_init`. For rank one, `A_0|0⟩=ψ_0∈ran(P(0))`; for a rank-m input subspace, `A_0:C^m→C^d` is an isometry with its declared image contained in `ran(P(0))`. The matrix `H(0)` or projector `P(0)` alone does not supply `A_0`. The slow-time propagator solves `i ∂_s U_T(s)=T H(s)U_T(s)`, `U_T(0)=I`. Path/derivative data, coefficient precision, preparation access and simulation access are explicit. A degenerate followed band requires coherent local Riesz continuation and a stated rank bound.

The Jansen–Ruskai–Seiler finite specialization assumes a `C²` self-adjoint family and a uniformly isolated spectral band of constant rank `m`; finite dimension makes all operators and derivatives bounded and removes domain issues. With `g(s)` the distance from the followed band to the remaining spectrum, define

For rank one this gives

\[
A_1(s)=\frac1T\left(\frac{\|H'(0)\|}{g(0)^2}+\frac{\|H'(s)\|}{g(s)^2}+\int_0^s\left(\frac{\|H''(r)\|}{g(r)^2}+\frac{7\|H'(r)\|^2}{g(r)^3}\right)dr\right).
\]

For constant-rank `m`, the conservative bound is

\[
A_m(s)=\frac1T\left(m\left(\frac{\|H'(0)\|}{g(0)^2}+\frac{\|H'(s)\|}{g(s)^2}\right)+\int_0^s\left(\frac{m\|H''(r)\|}{g(r)^2}+\frac{7m\sqrt m\|H'(r)\|^2}{g(r)^3}\right)dr\right).
\]

Writing `P_T(s)=U_T(s)P(0)U_T(s)†`, JRS gives `||P_T(s)−P(s)||≤A_m(s)` and, for each unit `ψ∈ran(P(0))`, transition probability out of the band at most `A_m(s)^2`. The constants, endpoint contributions and gap powers are part of the theorem. A circuit compiler discretizes `s`, evaluates finite-precision Hamiltonian terms at each sample, and charges the Hamiltonian simulator. If `||H′||≤L₁` and the coefficient oracle has matrix error `δ_mat`, path variation costs at most `T L₁/(2q)+Tδ_mat` across `q` intervals, before segment simulation errors.

The source proves the perturbation lemma but imports conductance/Cheeger and Perron facts; keep those as local suppliers and do not mark the route closed until they are proved.

Pad with `Θ(L/ε)` identity gates. The final-time component then has weight `1−O(ε)` in the history state; tracing out the clock gives output trace distance `O(ε)` from the circuit output. This is a conservative polynomial bound from the stated JRS theorem, not the sharper runtime in Aharonov et al. The 3-local and 2-local nearest-neighbor constructions have separate, more technical gap proofs and are outside this claim.

Without uniform efficient term/access hypotheses, a finite matrix family alone gives no circuit-equivalence claim.
