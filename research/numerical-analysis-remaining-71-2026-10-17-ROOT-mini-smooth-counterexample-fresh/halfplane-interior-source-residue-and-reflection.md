# Actual half-plane interior-source residues and base matching

Research proof, 2026-10-18. This is the exact normalized interior-layer-source subtask. It uses the complete actual MINI symbol and actual zero velocity AND pressure base values. It does not replace induced pressure loads from a bubble source by arbitrary independent data, and retains the actual base pressure reaction. No canonical/runtime edits, eigenvalue grids, tests or gates.

Notation c,h,k,L,r_t,r_n,k_n and the stable boundary matrix B(theta) are those of `halfplane-characteristic-and-stable-boundary-amplitudes.md`. Let the source layer be m>=1. Throughout this note F=(F_t,F_n) and Q denote NORMALIZED row impulses:

    c v+hP=F delta_(j,m),
    -h^T v-(7/10)kP=Q delta_(j,m),
    P=p/d.

For actual assembled row moments f_v,f_p, the normalization is

    F=f_v/d²,       Q=f_p/d.                              (1)

The base condition is v_0=0, P_0=0. Its mixed reactions are outputs, not additional zero conditions.

## 1. Exact full-line physical residues

Elimination on the full vertical line gives the pressure multiplier

    P(z,r)=[-c(z,r)Q-h(z,r) dot F]/L(z,r).                (2)

Let r_+(theta) be the stable physical root and r_-(theta) the unstable physical root. For real theta near zero,

    r_-=1/conjugate(r_+).

Put D_+=r_+ partial_r L(z,r_+). The exact pressure residues above and below the source are

    A_+=[-c(z,r_+)Q-h(z,r_+) dot F]/D_+,

    A_-=-[-c(z,r_-)Q-h(z,r_-) dot F]
                                      /[r_- partial_r L(z,r_-)]. (3)

The corresponding contributions are A_+ r_+^(j-m) for j>=m and A_- r_-^(j-m) for j<=m. The signs follow directly from the Laurent coefficient contour; for the positive scalar model 2-r-r^(-1), its stable coefficient is +1/[r partial_r L].

Write t=|theta| and sigma=sign(theta). The already proved physical root and symbol expansions give

    c(z,r_+)=3/10+O(t²),
    h(z,r_+)=(3/10)(i theta,-t)+O(t³),
    D_+=(3/5)t+O(t³).

Consequently

    A_+=-Q/(2t)-(i sigma/2)F_t+(1/2)F_n
                                       +O(t|Q|+t²|F|),
    A_-=-Q/(2t)-(i sigma/2)F_t-(1/2)F_n
                                       +O(t|Q|+t²|F|). (4)

All constants in these expansions are independent of m, because m enters only the propagating powers. Thus a scalar pressure-row impulse has pressure order t^(-1); a velocity-row impulse has pressure order zero. For the PHYSICAL pressure residues, multiply (4) by d:

    a_+ =-f_p/(2t)-(i sigma/2d)f_v,t+(1/2d)f_v,n
                              +O(t|f_p|+t²|f_v|/d),     (5)

and similarly with the negative normal term below. These are per-node row moments, not density-normalized functions.

## 2. Exact reflected physical coefficient

Let b(theta) be the last row of B(theta)^(-1): it extracts the stable physical pressure amplitude from a base vector (v_t,v_n,P). Let e_-(theta)=(-h(z,r_-)/c(z,r_-),1) denote the downward physical mode, with pressure normalized to one. Define the EXACT reflection coefficient

    R(theta)=b(theta)e_-(theta).                         (6)

Since b e_+=1 for the upward physical column and

    e_--e_+=(0,-2t,0)+O(t²),
    b(0)=(0,-k_n,1),

one obtains

    R(theta)=1+2k_n t+O(t²).                              (7)

Thus imposing both zero velocity and pressure base values is NOT identical to scalar pressure-only odd reflection. Its leading Dirichlet reflection is one, but its first correction is fixed by the normal MINI mesh layer.

Let X_m(theta) be the COMPLETE full-line base vector generated by the source at layer m. It is the sum of the downward physical vector A_- r_-^(-m)e_- and all downward mesh contributions. Write

    J_m(theta)=b(theta)[X_m-A_- r_-^(-m)e_-].             (8)

No mesh component is dropped here. For j>=m the exact outgoing PHYSICAL pressure of the zero-base half-plane solution is

    P_ph,j=A_+ r_+^(j-m)
          -[R A_- r_-^(-m)+J_m]r_+^j.                   (9)

This is full-line Green minus the actual homogeneous half-plane response matching its complete base vector. Mesh pressure branches in the solution are additional to (9), as required to recover both base conditions.

The source's full-line velocity also has a pressure-free scalar-mass branch. At a c-root its residue vector is obtained from

    v=hQ/L + [F+h(h dot F)/L]/c.

Thus its c-root numerator is the exact projection

    F-h(h dot F)/(h dot h),

divided by the appropriate radial derivative. A scalar pressure source never excites this pressure-free branch. A velocity source can excite it, and its velocity enters the matching functional (8). Omitting it would give the wrong tangential first-order coefficient below.

## 3. Dirichlet cancellation and exact fixed-layer source moments

For the scalar source Q, the leading part of (9), after factoring r_+^(j-m), is

    -Q/(2t) [1-R(theta)(r_+/r_-)^m]-J_m r_+^m.          (10)

Since log(r_+/r_-)=-2t+O(t³), (10) has the claimed Dirichlet cancellation

    [1-exp(-2mt)]/t,

with its actual reflection and exponentially localized mesh corrections retained. In particular the bare full-line t^(-1) singularity cancels for every FIXED source layer m as t tends to zero. One must not replace the m-dependent exponential by its Taylor series if m t is not small.

Here are the exact first moments for fixed m. They give a particularly sharp check of signs and matching. Put

    q_t=r_t-r_t^(-1),     q_n=r_n-r_n^(-1),
    s_t=-25,             s_n=-862/109.

Then

    q_t²=621,
    q_n²=1120*621/109²,
    k_n=-109 q_n/5040.                                  (11)

At theta=0 the downward full-line NORMAL mesh coefficients are:

    scalar Q:       v_n=-(Q/2)r_n^m,
                    P=(k_n Q/2)r_n^m;
    normal force:   v_n=-[F_n/(2k_n)]r_n^m,
                    P=(F_n/2)r_n^m.                    (12)

Applying b(0)=(0,-k_n,1) therefore gives

    J_m^Q(0)=k_n Q r_n^m,
    J_m^Fn(0)=F_n r_n^m.                                (13)

The tangential source needs first order. The downward tangential mass mode has velocity amplitude (90/q_t)F_t r_t^m at theta=0. Its contribution to b e_t^- has derivative 14i k_n/q_t. The downward normal pressure residue generated by F_t has derivative

    -i(s_n+4)F_t/(6q_n).

Consequently

    J_m^Ft(theta)
      =i theta F_t [1260 k_n r_t^m/q_t²
                                -(s_n+4)r_n^m/(3q_n)]
                                +O(theta²|F_t|).        (14)

For clarity, these derivative identities follow from h_x(theta=0,r)=0 for every r, so its total branch derivative is simply i(s+4)/20; the radial-root derivative term vanishes. The stable tangential mode's normal-velocity derivative is +7i/q_t, and its downward partner's is -7i/q_t. Their difference under b supplies the factor 14i k_n/q_t. The normal pressure coefficient in (14) follows from r_n partial_r L=3q_n/10.

Define the outgoing physical coefficient after factoring r_+^(j-m) in (9) by A_eff,m. Equations (4),(7),(13)--(14) give, for each FIXED m,

    scalar source:  A_eff,m -> -Q H_Q(m),
    normal force:   A_eff,m -> F_n H_n(m),
    tangent force:  A_eff,m =-i theta F_t H_t(m)
                                       +O(theta²|F_t|), (15)

where

    H_Q(m)=m-k_n(1-r_n^m),
    H_n(m)=1-r_n^m,
    H_t(m)=m-k_n[1-(140/69)r_t^m+(71/69)r_n^m].          (16)

The scalar and normal limits in (15) have O_m(t) errors. The tangential coefficient uses the exact identities in (11), which convert 1260/q_t² to 140/69 and the normal contribution to -71k_n/69. All three H-functions vanish at m=0, as a consistency check of exact boundary matching; m=0 is not an imposed interior source in this problem.

Thus the scalar effective height is NOT exactly m: the normal MINI mismatch layer shifts it by -k_n(1-r_n^m). The normal force has a nonzero order-zero pressure moment; the tangent force has a tangential derivative moment with coefficient H_t. These statements concern Fourier pressure amplitudes, not a scalar replacement of the coupled operator.

## 4. Base-neutral mesh corrections and the orders needed by the owner

The half-plane correction of a downward physical input e_- with unit pressure differs from the stable physical column only by a velocity mismatch O(t). Its stable mesh amplitudes are therefore O(t). Multiplying by the full-line physical source amplitudes (4) gives the reflected NORMAL mesh-pressure amplitude orders

    scalar row source:   O(|Q|) exp(-c m t),
    velocity row source: O(t|F|) exp(-c m t).              (17)

The stable tangential mesh mode itself has pressure identically zero. Direct full-line source-mesh contributions are exponentially small with the source-to-base layer distance and are included in (8); for low frequencies their physical-row orders are bounded by O(|Q|+|F|)gamma^m with gamma<1. They must not be confused with the reflected physical contribution in (17).

Restoring physical pressure and actual row units, (17) is

    scalar:   O(|f_p|) exp(-c m|theta|),
    velocity: O(|theta||f_v|/d) exp(-c m|theta|).          (18)

This is precisely the scalar order-zero and velocity order-one boundary-layer input needed for the structured owner's all-port pressure VALUE estimate. A thin pressure mesh layer need not have a small Hessian; its value order (18), not a false uniform second-derivative estimate, is the relevant input.

## 5. Pressure reaction, compatibility and source scope

The base is zero in v and P, but its pressure reaction is not generally zero. At zero tangential frequency, summing every pressure row (equivalently testing by the pressure partition of unity and taking a far-layer limit) gives

    R_p,base(0)=-Q                                       (normalized),
    actual R_p,base(0)=-f_p.                             (19)

For a pure velocity row source Q=0 this net reaction is zero. For a scalar pressure impulse it exactly balances the imposed interior pressure moment. The theta=0 solutions in (15) have a constant limiting pressure at large height, with decaying velocity/pressure differences; a pressure gauge does not remove the reaction (19).

An assembled source with zero net weak pressure load may cancel this channel after summing its actual pressure impulses and boundary/interface reactions. It may NOT be removed from each individual impulse calculation by assumption. For an actual compact bubble velocity source, static elimination supplies BOTH a velocity load and induced pressure-row loads with their exact coefficients; (1)--(19) apply by linear superposition to that coupled source. They do not authorize independent choice of its pressure load or omission of its mass-potential subtraction in an eventual evolutionary pressure observation.

## 6. Uniform source-layer versions of the leading amplitudes

The reflected formulas can also be stated uniformly for ALL integers m>=1, which avoids using a fixed-m Taylor expansion when the physical source height m d is large. With t=|theta|, sigma=sign(theta), and the zero-frequency constants r_t,r_n,k_n, equations (3),(6),(8)--(14) give

    A_eff,m^Q
       =-Q[1-exp(-2mt)]/(2t)
          +k_n Q[exp(-2mt)-r_n^m exp(-mt)] +O(t|Q|),

    A_eff,m^Fn
       =(F_n/2)[1+exp(-2mt)]
          -F_n r_n^m exp(-mt) +O(t|F_n|),

    A_eff,m^Ft
       =-(i sigma F_t/2)[1-exp(-2mt)]
          +i theta k_n F_t[exp(-2mt)
                -((140/69)r_t^m-(71/69)r_n^m)exp(-mt)]
          +O(t²|F_t|).                                  (20)

The error constants are independent of m. To see this directly, replacing (r_+/r_-)^m by exp(-2mt) costs at most C m t³ exp(-cmt)<=C t². The scalar coefficient has one factor 1/t, leaving O(t|Q|). Replacing a mesh root by its zero-frequency value costs at most C t m gamma^(m-1), uniformly bounded after increasing gamma<1. Its amplitude is O(Q), O(F_n), or O(tF_t), respectively. Likewise r_+^m differs from exp(-mt) by O(t²) uniformly. The expansions of the source residues and reflection coefficient have the orders stated earlier. These observations prove each remainder in (20), with no source-depth restriction.

In particular |A_eff,m^Q|<=C|Q|[min(m,t^(-1))+1]. Its singular full-line factor is reduced by the actual base reflection, while the two explicit bounded corrections retain the base-neutral mesh contribution. Equations (15)--(16) are recovered by fixing m in (20).

## 7. Velocity residues, source-height contacts and shallow reflected mesh inputs

This section independently checks the structured owner's velocity/neutral-layer use of the pressure residues. It corrects a possible overly strong reading of Section 4: a DIRECT mesh input reflected from a shallow source does not have a purely order-one velocity-force coefficient. There is also an analytic order-zero coefficient. It is harmless at far horizontal separation, but must be separated explicitly.

On the physical branches, the leading velocity vectors are (-i theta,+t) above and (-i theta,-t) below. Multiplying (4) gives the leading DIRECT full-line velocity residues:

    scalar Q:
      v_+=(i sigma Q/2,-Q/2),
      v_-=(i sigma Q/2,+Q/2);

    velocity force:
      v_+=(-t F_t/2-i theta F_n/2,
                            -i theta F_t/2+t F_n/2),
      v_-=(-t F_t/2+i theta F_n/2,
                            +i theta F_t/2+t F_n/2).    (21)

Thus the scalar jump is the smooth constant vector (0,-Q). The velocity-force jumps are the smooth polynomials (-i theta F_n,-i theta F_t). A first vertical difference across j=m produces these CONTACT terms plus scalar order-one or velocity order-two nonsmooth remainders. A smooth frequency cutoff times a polynomial has arbitrarily rapidly decaying horizontal Fourier coefficients, so the contacts do not change the distant-source difference orders. Away from the source-height crossing, a first target difference directly adds one power of t. The exact higher symbol corrections have the same one-sided analytic structure and the displayed higher orders.

In actual row units, for a target separated from the source support by R with d<=R, the physical velocity therefore obeys the precise first-difference scale

    |D v_ph| <= C [A/R²+B/R³] = C K_*,                  (22)

where A is the sum of absolute actual pressure-row moments and B the corresponding actual velocity-row moments. Here D is any spatial first lattice difference divided by d. For example the scalar coefficient is Q times order one; division by d and inverse Fourier decay (d/R)² give (f_p/d²)(d/R)²=f_p/R². The velocity coefficient is F times order two; its scaling is (f_v/d³)(d/R)³=f_v/R³. Smooth contacts satisfy the same estimates by taking sufficiently many integrations by parts. At large vertical separation exponential factors supply the same bounds; at a source-height crossing the required separation is horizontal.

The discrete gradient relation likewise yields

    |v_ph+grad_d p_ph|<=C d K_*.                         (23)

Indeed h/c equals the central discrete gradient symbol through first order, with order-three error; a forward gradient incurs an additional order-two term. After multiplication by the source pressure orders t^(-1) and order zero, these give the required orders one and two. Contact discrepancies are smooth Fourier polynomials and are rapidly localized horizontally. Thus (23) holds also across a source-height crossing in the distant-source region, without imposing a false continuous global C2 extension.

Now let X_m,mesh(theta) be the downward DIRECT source-mesh base vector in (8). Every mesh root and mode is analytic THROUGH theta=0, whereas the physical branch is only one-sided analytic there. For each fixed derivative order k, the direct mesh vector and its derivatives satisfy

    ||partial_theta^k X_m,mesh||
                    <=C_k gamma_1^m (|Q|+|F|),          (24)

with gamma_1<1; factors polynomial in m are absorbed into gamma_1. The pure velocity C-root contribution is included. Multiplying (24) by the stable boundary inverse gives

    reflected source-mesh coefficient
      = an analytic O((|Q|+|F|)gamma_1^m) part
        + a piecewise analytic O(t(|Q|+|F|)gamma_1^m) part. (25)

To interpret "analytic part" for Fourier estimates, subtract a common finite Taylor jet and multiply it by a smooth cutoff. Its Fourier coefficients decay faster than any prescribed finite power; the remaining one-sided term vanishes to order one at zero. This is sufficient and avoids asserting a globally analytic cutoff. For a shallow VELOCITY source the analytic O(F gamma_1^m) term in (25) can be nonzero. It must not be claimed absent. Its nonanalytic remainder is, however, exactly order one, as required.

Taking a tangential first difference of that remainder produces order two. Replacing the actual analytic mesh roots/vectors by their theta=0 values multiplies by an analytic O(theta) factor: the common analytic contribution remains rapidly localized, while the nonanalytic remainder becomes order two. Consequently the velocity root/vector replacement error is bounded by

    C d K_*                                             (26)

at distant ports. In detail, the shallow velocity term gives F(d/R)³=d B/R³; the scalar term gives Q(d/R)²=d A/R² (or a better power for the secondary term in (25)). Factors j gamma^j from root differentiation are uniformly bounded. The physical-input reflected mesh terms in (17) have scalar order zero and velocity order one and obey the same bounds. Their first tangential amplitude variation is at most K_*.

DIRECT source-mesh fields themselves use r_beta^(|j-m|), with the proper opposite-side eigenvectors, rather than one common r_beta^j profile across the source height. At far horizontal separation they are smooth Fourier multipliers with the bounds (24), and hence are an O(dK_*) velocity remainder after sufficiently many integrations by parts. At large vertical separation their exponential decay gives the same conclusion. Their physical pressure and its first differences are respectively O(d²K_*) and O(dK_*). They may therefore be put in this explicit remainder instead of being incorrectly factored into a single target-base layer amplitude.
