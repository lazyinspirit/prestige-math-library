## Mathematical checks and corrections

- The first Rauch form uses higher radial sectional curvature to make the normal Jacobi norm **smaller** up to the higher-curvature field's first conjugate time. The nonzero initial-value form now requires the same full scalar initial shape `D_tJ_i(0)=lambda J_i(0)`, and stops at the **first focal time of the full Jacobi tensor**. Equal initial norms and scalar logarithmic derivatives alone are false as sufficient hypotheses: in a flat plane, `J_1=e_1+t e_2` and `J_2=e_1` have those equal scalar data but unequal lengths. A selected field staying nonzero also cannot justify passage through another tensor direction's focal time: take a diagonal nonnegative curvature ODE with one mode of curvature 100 up to `pi/20` and zero afterward, a second flat mode of small constant size, and zero initial derivatives. After `pi/20` the first mode grows in negative magnitude while the full selected vector never vanishes. Eschenburg Theorems 3.1/3.3 justify the tightened tensor interval; the piecewise example can be smoothed. The design's broader wording and Eschenburg's printed Rauch II interval are therefore not used without qualification.
- For `K>=k`, fixed-leg hinges have opposite side `c<=c_k(a,b,theta)`, while fixed-three-side triangles have actual angles at least the model angles and corresponding side-point distances at least the model distances. Eschenburg Theorem 6.1 display (6.10) prints `<=` for distance from the third vertex to a point on the opposite side, but its proof defines `delta=f(d_M)-f(d_k)` and proves `delta>=0`, hence `d_M>=d_k`. Lang Definition 5.7 independently confirms the latter fixed-side sign. The scaffold follows the arguments, not the inconsistent display. The strict barrier requires `k'>k`; an ordinary negative-minimum test would be insufficient when `k>0`.
- Positive-curvature comparison triangles require each side `<pi/sqrt(k)` and perimeter `<2pi/sqrt(k)`; the model cotangent and polar formulas stop at their positive model endpoint. The cut locus is excluded from pointwise Hessian/Laplacian comparisons. Toponogov's cut-point argument uses an upper smooth support from an interior point of a minimizing segment and declares the cut-point characterization and smooth-distance prerequisites explicitly.
- Bonnet–Myers assumes a **uniform** positive Ricci lower bound and completeness. Bishop–Gromov's ball ratio is nonincreasing and has limit one at zero; cut time, cut-locus nullity, Radon volume, and sigma-finite Tonelli are used in the polar integral. Cheng's proof uses equality of all these inequalities, rather than assuming the sphere conclusion from diameter alone. The sectional lower-bound rigidity item remains separately escalated for its requested Toponogov endpoint route.
