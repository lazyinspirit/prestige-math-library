## Definition

Let $d,q,B\ge1$, $N\ge0$, and $\Theta\subseteq\mathbb R^q$ be open. Let $\lambda$ be a sigma-finite Borel measure on $\mathbb R^d$, let $\pi$ be a Borel probability measure on $\mathbb R^d$, and let $\rho:[0,1]\times\mathbb R^d\times\mathbb R^d\to[0,\infty]$ and $u^c:[0,1]\times\mathbb R^d\times\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^d$ be jointly measurable. Assume $\int\rho_t(x\mid x_1)\,\lambda(dx)=1$ for every $t$ and for $\pi$-almost every $x_1$, and that for $\pi$-almost every $x_1$, $\rho_t(\cdot\mid x_1)$ and every component of $\rho_tu^c_t(\cdot\mid x_1)$ are continuously differentiable in $(t,x)$ on $(0,1)\times\mathbb R^d$ and satisfy $$\partial_t\rho_t(x\mid x_1)+\sum_{j=1}^d\partial_{x_j}\bigl(\rho_t(x\mid x_1)(u^c_t)^j(x\mid x_1)\bigr)=0.$$ For every compact $K\subset(0,1)\times\mathbb R^d$, suppose there is a nonnegative $G_K\in L^1(\pi)$ bounding on $K$, for $\pi$-almost every $x_1$, the absolute values of $\rho_t$, $\partial_t\rho_t$, each component of $\rho_tu^c_t$, and each corresponding spatial flux derivative.

Define $p_t(x):=\int\rho_t(x\mid x_1)\,\pi(dx_1)$ and $J_t(x):=\int\rho_t(x\mid x_1)u^c_t(x\mid x_1)\,\pi(dx_1)$. Assume $0<p_t(x)<\infty$ and $J_t(x)$ is finite on $[0,1]\times\mathbb R^d$, and put $u^m_t(x):=J_t(x)/p_t(x)$. Let $v:[0,1]\times\mathbb R^d\times\Theta\to\mathbb R^d$ be jointly measurable and continuously differentiable in $\theta$, with jointly measurable parameter derivative. Define $$\mathcal L_C(\theta):=\int_0^1\!\int\!\int\rho_t(x\mid x_1)\|v(t,x;\theta)-u^c_t(x\mid x_1)\|_2^2\,\pi(dx_1)\lambda(dx)dt$$ and $$\mathcal L_M(\theta):=\int_0^1\!\int p_t(x)\|v(t,x;\theta)-u^m_t(x)\|_2^2\,\lambda(dx)dt.$$ Assume, almost surely, that $\mathcal L_C(\theta_k)$ and $\mathcal L_M(\theta_k)$ are finite for every $k=0,\ldots,N$, and that $$C:=\int_0^1\!\int\!\int\rho_t(x\mid x_1)\|u^c_t(x\mid x_1)-u^m_t(x)\|_2^2\,\pi(dx_1)\lambda(dx)dt$$ is finite. Write $D_\theta v$ for the matrix with entries $(D_\theta v)_{ij}=\partial_{\theta_j}v^i$. For every compact $H\subset\Theta$ and coordinate $j$, suppose nonnegative integrable majorants $G^C_{H,j}$ on $dt\,\lambda(dx)\,\pi(dx_1)$ and $G^M_{H,j}$ on $dt\,\lambda(dx)$ satisfy, for every $\theta\in H$ outside fixed null sets, $$2\rho_t(x\mid x_1)\left|\left\langle\partial_{\theta_j}v(t,x;\theta),v(t,x;\theta)-u^c_t(x\mid x_1)\right\rangle\right|\le G^C_{H,j}(t,x,x_1)$$ and $$2p_t(x)\left|\left\langle\partial_{\theta_j}v(t,x;\theta),v(t,x;\theta)-u^m_t(x)\right\rangle\right|\le G^M_{H,j}(t,x).$$ Let $Q(dt,dx_1,dx):=dt\,\pi(dx_1)\rho_t(x\mid x_1)\lambda(dx)$ be the probability law of a training triple, with $dt$ Lebesgue probability measure on $[0,1]$. Take $\theta_0$ as fixed and let $\mathcal F_0$ be the trivial history. At iteration $k$, let $\mathcal F_k$ be the history before the batch, so $\theta_k$ is $\mathcal F_k$-measurable; draw $B$ independent triples with law $Q$, independently of $\mathcal F_k$, and set $\mathcal F_{k+1}$ to be the sigma-algebra generated by $\mathcal F_k$ and that batch. For a triple $w=(t,x_1,x)$, define $$\zeta(\theta,w):=2\,D_\theta v(t,x;\theta)^T\left(v(t,x;\theta)-u^c_t(x\mid x_1)\right).$$ For each $k,b$, set $\zeta_{k,b}:=\zeta(\theta_k,(T_{k,b},X_{1,k,b},X_{k,b}))$ and require it to be integrable on the joint training probability space.

Given the fixed initial parameter $\theta_0\in\Theta$ and deterministic step sizes $\eta_0,\ldots,\eta_{N-1}\ge0$, for each $k=0,\ldots,N-1$ set
$$g_k:=\frac1B\sum_{b=1}^{B}\zeta_{k,b}, \qquad \theta_{k+1}:=\theta_k-\eta_k g_k.$$
Assume all iterates $\theta_0,\ldots,\theta_N$ remain in $\Theta$ almost surely. Training uses only conditional samples and does not solve the neural ODE during these updates. After training, condition on the history $\mathcal F_N$, draw $X_0$ from a supplied initial probability law $\mu_0$ independently of $\mathcal F_N$, and return $X_1=\Phi^{\theta_N}_{1,0}(X_0)$, where $\Phi^{\theta_N}_{t,s}$ solves $\dot z(t)=v(t,z(t);\theta_N)$ with $z(s)=X_0$. This last step is well-defined if the field extends to an open time interval containing $[0,1]$, is jointly $C^\infty$ in time, state, and parameter, and on every compact parameter set $H\subset\Theta$ has uniform constants $L_H,M_H\ge0$ satisfying
$$\|v(t,z;\theta)-v(t,w;\theta)\|_2\le L_H\|z-w\|_2, \qquad \|v(t,0;\theta)\|_2\le M_H$$
for $t\in[0,1]$, $\theta\in H$, and $z,w\in\mathbb R^d$. Conditional on $\mathcal F_N$, the output law assigns mass $\mu_0(\{x:\Phi^{\theta_N}_{1,0}(x)\in A\})$ to each Borel set $A\subseteq\mathbb R^d$. The procedure gives no convergence guarantee for the parameter updates or equality of this output law with a desired data law.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The conditional probability path and velocity, endpoint sampler, parameterized vector field and its sampled gradients, initial parameter, batch size, step sizes, supplied initial law, and exact-flow regularity hypotheses in the Algorithm.

[F1] [[def-probability-path-and-velocity-field]]: a conditional density path is normalized for the endpoint law almost everywhere, and its conditional velocity is defined by the conditional continuity equation.

[F2] [[thm-conditional-flow-matching-gradient-identity]]: under finite loss, positive finite marginal density, local endpoint domination for the conditional continuity equation, and dominated parameter derivatives, the conditional and marginal squared-loss gradients agree.

[F3] [[def-neural-ode]]: for a fixed parameter, the neural ODE is the initial-value problem $\dot z(t)=v(t,z(t);\theta)$ with the stated initial condition.

[F4] [[thm-neural-ode-global-smooth-flow]]: a jointly smooth field on an open time interval containing $[0,1]$, with uniform state-Lipschitz and origin bounds on compact parameter sets, has a unique global flow on $[0,1]$, and its terminal map is jointly smooth in initial state and parameter.

[F5] [[def-euclidean-inner-product]]: the squared Euclidean loss is $\|a-b\|_2^2=\langle a-b,a-b\rangle$.

[F6] [[thm-linearity-of-the-lebesgue-integral-on-l-one]]: the expectation of a finite sum of integrable random vectors is the sum of their expectations, componentwise.

[F7] [[def-law-or-distribution-of-a-random-element]]: the law of a measurable map $S$ under $\mu_0$ assigns a Borel set $A$ the measure $\mu_0(S^{-1}(A))$.

[F8] [[def-conditional-expectation-given-a-sigma-algebra]]: for an integrable real random variable, a conditional-expectation version is measurable, integrable, and has the same integrals on every event in the conditioning sigma-algebra; vector statements apply coordinatewise.

[F9] [[thm-independent-random-elements-have-product-joint-law]]: independent random elements have the product of their marginal laws as their joint law.

[F10] [[thm-change-of-variables-for-expectation]]: the expectation of an integrable function of a random element equals its integral against the law of that element.

[F11] [[thm-fubini-theorem-for-l-one-on-sigma-finite-product-spaces]]: an integrable function on a sigma-finite product can be integrated in either order with equal results.

[F12] [[thm-integral-triangle-inequality]]: the absolute value of an integrable scalar function's integral is bounded by the integral of its absolute value.

[F13] [[thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces]]: a nonnegative product-measurable function has a measurable section-integral function and equal iterated and product integrals.

[F14] [[cor-mean-value-theorem]]: a real function continuous on a closed interval and differentiable in its interior has each difference quotient equal to an interior derivative.

## Verification

**Given:** The hypotheses in the Algorithm.

1.1 Fix $k$ and a history for which $\theta_k=\theta\in\Theta$ and both losses at $\theta$ are finite. Choose a compact parameter box $H\subset\Theta$ with $\theta$ in its interior. Changing coordinates one at a time, the mean value theorem [F14] and the derivative majorants in [F2] bound the differences between each loss integrand at $\theta'$ and at $\theta$ by the corresponding sums of $|\theta'_j-\theta_j|G^C_{H,j}$ and $|\theta'_j-\theta_j|G^M_{H,j}$. Integrating these bounds proves local finiteness of both losses throughout the interior of $H$, so [F2] applies there and gives $\nabla_\theta\mathcal L_C(\theta)=\nabla_\theta\mathcal L_M(\theta)$. The sample gradient is the parameter gradient of the conditional squared loss by [F5]; since its triple has law $Q$, the expectation change-of-variables formula [F10] identifies its $Q$-mean with $\nabla_\theta\mathcal L_C(\theta)$. Thus its $Q$-mean is $\nabla_\theta\mathcal L_M(\theta)$. [given, F1, F2, F5, F10, F14, algebra]

2.1 Each $\zeta_{k,b}$, and hence $g_k$, is integrable by the explicit batch-integrability assumption. For fixed $\theta$, put $m(\theta):=\int_Q\zeta(\theta,w)\,Q(dw)$. Joint measurability, Tonelli [F13], and the local derivative majorants make $m$ measurable and finite; at each visited parameter step 1.1 gives $m(\theta_k)=\nabla_\theta\mathcal L_M(\theta_k)$. Fubini [F11] and the integral triangle inequality [F12] show that each coordinate of $m(\theta_k)$ is integrable, with its absolute expectation bounded by that of the corresponding sample-gradient coordinate. For every $A\in\mathcal F_k$, independence and the product-law fact [F9], the change-of-variables formula [F10], and Fubini [F11] give $$\mathbb E[\mathbf1_A g_k]=\mathbb E\!\left[\mathbf1_A\int_{Q^{\otimes B}}\frac1B\sum_{b=1}^B\zeta(\theta_k,w_b)\,Q^{\otimes B}(d(w_1,\ldots,w_B))\right]=\mathbb E[\mathbf1_A\nabla_\theta\mathcal L_M(\theta_k)],$$ where step 1.1 identifies each fixed-parameter $Q$-mean and [F6] gives linearity. Thus [F8] makes $\nabla_\theta\mathcal L_M(\theta_k)$ a version of $\mathbb E[g_k\mid\mathcal F_k]$; the batch estimate is unbiased conditionally on its pre-batch history. The arithmetic update is defined for every nonnegative step size when $\theta_k\in\Theta$. [given, F6, F8, F9, F10, F11, F12, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For the realized $\theta_N$ satisfying the exact-flow bounds, [F3] identifies the sampling equation as a neural ODE and [F4] gives its unique solution on the full interval and joint continuity of $(x,\theta)\mapsto\Phi^\theta_{1,0}(x)$. The terminal output is therefore measurable as a function of the independent initial draw and the training history. Conditional on $\mathcal F_N$, $\theta_N$ is fixed, so [F7] identifies the conditional output law by $\mu_0(\{x:\Phi^{\theta_N}_{1,0}(x)\in A\})$ for each Borel set $A$. This verifies that the exact-flow output is defined; it makes no claim that the learned field realizes the target path. [given, F3, F4, F7] ∎

## Source notes

The cited papers specify the conditional sample, squared regression loss, parameter update, and ODE sampling steps. This algorithm makes the sampler law, batch gradient, exact global-flow conditions, and output law explicit. Its parameter updates carry no optimization convergence guarantee, and the exact flow is a mathematical solution rather than a numerical solver with an error bound.
