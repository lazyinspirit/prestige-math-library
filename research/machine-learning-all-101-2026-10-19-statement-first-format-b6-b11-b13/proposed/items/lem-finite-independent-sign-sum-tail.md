## Statement

Let $\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_m$ be independent random variables with values in $\{-1,+1\}$, let $c_1,\ldots,c_m\in\mathbb R$, and put $V=\sum_{i=1}^m c_i^2$ and $S=\sum_{i=1}^m c_i(\xi_i-\mathbb E\xi_i)$. For every $t>0$, if $V>0$ then
$$\mathbb P(S\ge t)\le e^{-t^2/(2V)},\qquad \mathbb P(S\le-t)\le e^{-t^2/(2V)},\qquad \mathbb P(|S|\ge t)\le2e^{-t^2/(2V)}.$$
If $V=0$, then $S=0$ almost surely and all three tail probabilities are zero. The empty sum $m=0$ is included with $V=S=0$.

## Statement references

[Schmidt et al., Appendix F.2, Lemma 29 and Theorem 31 proof, PDF pp. 33–37](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.11285v2).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite independent family of sign-valued random variables, real coefficients, and $t>0$.

[A1] No choice axiom is used; every construction and product is finite.

[F1] Independence means independence of the sigma-algebras generated by the random variables. [[def-independent-random-elements]].

[F2] Expectations are Lebesgue integrals against the probability law. [[def-expectation-of-a-nonnegative-or-integrable-random-variable]].

[F3] For a nonnegative random variable $U$ and $a>0$, $\mathbb P(U\ge a)\le\mathbb E[U]/a$. [[cor-markov-inequality-for-random-variables]].

[F4] The natural logarithm differentiates as $1/x$, and $\exp(x+y)=\exp(x)\exp(y)$. [[thm-logarithm-derivative-and-integral]], [[thm-exponential-addition-formula]], [[thm-derivative-of-exponential]].

[F5] A finite union of events has probability at most the sum of its event probabilities. [[thm-finite-union-bound]].

## Proof

1.1 For one sign variable $\xi$ with $p=\mathbb P(\xi=+1)$ and $\mu=\mathbb E\xi=2p-1$, put $\psi(s)=\log\mathbb E e^{s(\xi-\mu)}$. The two-point formula gives $\psi(s)=\log(pe^s+(1-p)e^{-s})-s\mu$, so $\psi(0)=\psi'(0)=0$ and $\psi''(s)=\frac{4p(1-p)}{(pe^s+(1-p)e^{-s})^2}\le1$. The inequality follows from $4ab\le(a+b)^2$ with $a=pe^s$ and $b=(1-p)e^{-s}$. Integrating the second-derivative bound twice from $0$ to $s$, in either direction, gives $\psi(s)\le s^2/2$ for every real $s$; if $p=0$ or $p=1$, $\psi$ is identically zero and the same bound holds. [given, F2, F4, algebra]

2.1 Apply step 1.1 to each $\xi_i$ with $s=\lambda c_i$. By independence [F1] and the exponential addition formula [F4], $\mathbb E e^{\lambda S}=\prod_{i=1}^m\mathbb E e^{\lambda c_i(\xi_i-\mathbb E\xi_i)}\le \exp\!\left(\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\sum_{i=1}^m c_i^2\right)=e^{\lambda^2V/2}$ for every real $\lambda$. [step 1.1, F1, F4, algebra]

3.1 Suppose $V>0$ and choose $\lambda=t/V>0$. Markov's inequality [F3] applied to $e^{\lambda S}$ and the MGF bound in step 2.1 give $\mathbb P(S\ge t)\le e^{-\lambda t+\lambda^2V/2}=e^{-t^2/(2V)}$. Applying the same argument to $-S$ gives $\mathbb P(S\le-t)\le e^{-t^2/(2V)}$. [F3, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 By [F5], $\mathbb P(|S|\ge t)\le\mathbb P(S\ge t)+\mathbb P(S\le-t)$, which gives the stated two-sided bound from step 3.1. If $V=0$, every $c_i=0$, so $S=0$ almost surely; the empty-sum case is the same. [F5, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
