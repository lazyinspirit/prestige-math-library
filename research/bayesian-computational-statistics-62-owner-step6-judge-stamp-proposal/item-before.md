## Statement

Assume countable choice. Use the wavelet product prior and white-noise model from [[lem-wavelet-product-prior-is-l2-valued]] and [[lem-white-noise-is-borel-gaussian-on-negative-sobolev-space]], with $\alpha>0$, $\sigma_l=2^{-l(\alpha+1/2)}$, and a common coefficient density $\varphi$ having finite second moment $m_2$. Let $f_0\in L^2([0,1])$. For each $n\ge1$ and $\delta>1/2$, let $Q_n$ be the prior-predictive law on $H(\delta)$ of $X=f+n^{-1/2}W$, with prior draw $f$ independent of canonical white noise $W$, and let $P_{f_0}^{(n)}$ be the law of $f_0+n^{-1/2}W$. Then $Q_n$ and $P_{f_0}^{(n)}$ are mutually absolutely continuous as Borel probabilities on $H(\delta)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The product-prior and white-noise laws, independence between the prior draw and observation noise, $\alpha>0$, finite coefficient second moment $m_2$, and $f_0\in L^2([0,1])$.

[F1] [[def-negative-sobolev-space-for-white-noise]]: $H(\delta)$ is the weighted coefficient Hilbert space, its finite-coordinate projections converge in norm, and $L^2([0,1])$ embeds continuously into it.

[F2] [[lem-wavelet-product-prior-is-l2-valued]]: The prior coefficients are independent with common density $\varphi$, and the prior law is supported on $L^2$ under its stated scale and moment conditions.

[F3] [[lem-white-noise-is-borel-gaussian-on-negative-sobolev-space]]: The noise law is a Borel probability on $H(\delta)$ with independent standard normal coordinates.

[F4] [[thm-bessel-inequality-for-an-arbitrary-orthonormal-family]]: The squared coefficient sum of $f_0$ is at most $\|f_0\|_2^2$.

[F5] [[thm-independent-random-elements-have-product-joint-law]]: A finite independent family of random elements has the product of its marginal laws as its joint law.

[F6] [[lem-finite-measure-uniqueness-on-a-pi-system]]: Finite measures agreeing on a generating pi-system and on the whole space agree on the generated sigma-algebra.

[F7] [[thm-indefinite-integral-of-a-nonnegative-function-is-a-measure]]: For a nonnegative measurable density $L$, the set function $A\mapsto\int_A L\,dQ_n$ is a measure.

[F8] [[thm-riesz-fischer-completeness-of-l-p]]: Under countable choice, $L^2(Q_n)$ is complete for the probability measure $Q_n$.

[F9] [[thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces]]: Nonnegative integrands may be integrated in either order under the product of the prior coordinate law and Lebesgue measure.

[F10] [[thm-gaussian-integral]]: The Gaussian integral normalizes the displayed one-dimensional normal densities.

**Given:** All probabilities in the statement are defined on the specified Borel space; only their equivalence is proved below.

## Proof

1.1 Enumerate the wavelet coefficients as $j=1,2,\ldots$ and let $P_m$ retain the first $m$ coordinates. The sigma-algebra generated by all coordinate maps is the Borel sigma-algebra of $H(\delta)$. Indeed, the coordinate maps are continuous, so their sigma-algebra is contained in the Borel sigma-algebra. Conversely, for any closed set $C\subseteq H(\delta)$, $x\mapsto d(P_mx,C)$ is measurable with respect to the coordinate sigma-algebra and converges to $d(x,C)$ by [F1]. Thus $C=\{x:d(x,C)=0\}$ belongs to that sigma-algebra. [F1, given, algebra]

1.2 Write $p_{a,n}(x)=(n/2\pi)^{1/2}e^{-n(x-a)^2/2}$ and let $\pi_j(dt)=\sigma_j^{-1}\varphi(t/\sigma_j)dt$, where $\sigma_j=\sigma_l$ for $j=(l,k)$. The true and prior-predictive coordinate densities are $p_j=p_{\theta_{0,j},n}$ and $q_j(x)=\int p_{t,n}(x)\,\pi_j(dt)$; both are positive probability densities by [F9, F10]. Put $h_j^2=\int(\sqrt{p_j}-\sqrt{q_j})^2dx$ and $\rho_j=\int\sqrt{p_jq_j}\,dx=1-h_j^2/2$. Concavity of square root and [F9] give $\rho_j\ge\int\!\left[\int\sqrt{p_{\theta_{0,j},n}(x)p_{t,n}(x)}\,dx\right]\pi_j(dt)$. Completing the square and using [F10] evaluates the inner integral as $e^{-n(t-\theta_{0,j})^2/8}$. Thus $h_j^2\le\int 2(1-e^{-n(t-\theta_{0,j})^2/8})\,\pi_j(dt)\le\frac n4\int(t-\theta_{0,j})^2\,\pi_j(dt)\le\frac n2(\theta_{0,j}^2+\sigma_j^2m_2)$. By [F2], [F3], and [F5] the prior-predictive and true coordinates are each independent across $j$. By [F4] and the geometric-series formula, $\sum_j\theta_{0,j}^2<\infty$ and $\sum_j\sigma_j^2=\sum_{l\ge0}2^{-2\alpha l}<\infty$. Hence $\sum_jh_j^2<\infty$ and $\sum_j(1-\rho_j)<\infty$. [F2, F3, F4, F5, F9, F10, given, algebra]

2.1 Let $\mathcal F_m$ be the sigma-algebra of the first $m$ coordinates on $H(\delta)$ and set $L_m=\prod_{j\le m}p_j(X_j)/q_j(X_j)$. Under $Q_n$, $L_m$ is the density of the first $m$ true coordinates relative to the first $m$ prior-predictive coordinates, and $\mathbb E_{Q_n}L_m=1$. By [F5], finite coordinate laws are products; hence for $r>m$, independence gives $\mathbb E_{Q_n}\!\left[\sqrt{L_rL_m}\right]=\prod_{m<j\le r}\rho_j,$ so $\|\sqrt{L_r}-\sqrt{L_m}\|_{L^2(Q_n)}^2=2\bigl(1-\prod_{m<j\le r}\rho_j\bigr)\le2\sum_{m<j\le r}(1-\rho_j)\to0$ uniformly in $r>m$. By [F8] and countable choice, $\sqrt{L_m}$ has an $L^2(Q_n)$ limit $G$; since each term is nonnegative, $\|G^-\|_2\le\|G-\sqrt{L_m}\|_2\to0$, so $G\ge0$ almost surely. The norms $\|\sqrt{L_m}\|_2=1$ give $\|G\|_2=1$. Put $L=G^2$. Then $\|L_m-L\|_1\le\|\sqrt{L_m}-G\|_2\,\|\sqrt{L_m}+G\|_2\le2\|\sqrt{L_m}-G\|_2\to0$, and $L\ge0$ with $\int L\,dQ_n=1$. [F5, F8, step 1.2, given, algebra]

3.1 For every event $A\in\mathcal F_m$ and $r\ge m$, the finite-coordinate density identity and independence of the remaining coordinates give $\int_A L_r\,dQ_n=P_{f_0}^{(n)}(A)$. Passing to the $L^1$ limit gives $\int_A L\,dQ_n=P_{f_0}^{(n)}(A)$. By [F7], the nonnegative density L with total integral one defines a probability measure. Such finite-coordinate cylinder events form a generating pi-system by step 1.1; [F6] therefore gives $P_{f_0}^{(n)}(A)=\int_A L\,dQ_n$ for every Borel $A\subseteq H(\delta)$. Thus $P_{f_0}^{(n)}\ll Q_n$. Interchanging $p_j$ and $q_j$ gives the same argument and proves $Q_n\ll P_{f_0}^{(n)}$. [F5, F6, F7, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 1.2] ∎
