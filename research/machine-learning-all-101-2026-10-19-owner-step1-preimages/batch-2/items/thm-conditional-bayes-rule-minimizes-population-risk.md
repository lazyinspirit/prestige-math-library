## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $(\mathsf X,\Sigma_X)$ and $(\mathsf Y,\Sigma_Y)$ be standard Borel spaces, let $(\mathsf A,\Sigma_A)$ be a measurable action space, and let $P$ be a probability measure on $\mathsf X\times\mathsf Y$. Write $P_X$ and $P_Y$ for its coordinate marginals. There are regular conditional probability kernels $Q(y,dx)$ for $X$ given $Y$ and $K(x,dy)$ for $Y$ given $X$, with
$$P(C\times D)=\int_D Q(y,C)\,P_Y(dy)=\int_C K(x,D)\,P_X(dx)\qquad(C\in\Sigma_X,\ D\in\Sigma_Y).$$
Let $\ell:\mathsf A\times\mathsf Y\to[0,+\infty]$ be measurable with respect to $\Sigma_A\otimes\Sigma_Y$ and define $q(x,a):=\int_{\mathsf Y}\ell(a,y)K(x,dy)$. If a measurable predictor $f^*:\mathsf X\to\mathsf A$ has a measurable set $E\in\Sigma_X$ with $P_X(E)=1$ such that $q(x,f^*(x))\le q(x,a)$ for every $x\in E$ and every $a\in\mathsf A$, then
$$R_P(f^*)\le R_P(g)$$
for every measurable predictor $g:\mathsf X\to\mathsf A$. All risks may be $+\infty$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; standard Borel instance and label spaces; a joint probability law $P$; a measurable action space; a nonnegative product-measurable loss; and a measurable predictor $f^*$ that minimizes conditional risk on one measurable $P_X$-full-measure set.

[A1] Assume the Axiom of Choice. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] A standard Borel space is measurably isomorphic to a Polish space. [[def-standard-borel-space]]

[F2] Under AC, a joint law on a product of standard Borel spaces admits a probability kernel in either coordinate direction with the rectangle disintegration identity. [[thm-disintegration-of-a-joint-law-on-standard-borel-spaces]]

[F3] The disintegration identity also holds for every nonnegative product-measurable integrand, including value infinity. [[thm-disintegration-of-a-joint-law-on-standard-borel-spaces]]

[F4] Under AC and standard Borel parameter/data hypotheses, a measurable rule attaining posterior minimum almost everywhere minimizes prior-integrated risk among all randomized rules. [[thm-bayes-rule-minimizes-prior-risk]]

[F5] Population risk is the extended nonnegative integral of the loss under the joint law. [[def-population-and-empirical-risk]]

[F6] A regular conditional distribution is a probability kernel whose evaluations satisfy the conditional-event integral identity. [[def-regular-conditional-distribution]]

[F7] Integrating a nonnegative product-measurable function against a probability kernel gives a measurable extended-valued function. [[thm-measurability-of-integration-against-a-kernel]]

[F8] Product sigma-algebras are generated by measurable rectangles, so the coordinate swap between finite products is measurable. [[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]]

[F9] A map is measurable when preimages of measurable sets are measurable; this criterion is preserved by composition. [[def-measurable-function-between-measurable-spaces]]

## Proof

1.1 The spaces satisfy the standard-Borel hypothesis [F1]. Apply [F2] to $P$ and to its coordinate swap. This gives $Q(y,dx)$ and $K(x,dy)$ with the two displayed disintegrations; [F6] identifies them as regular conditional distributions. With prior $\pi=P_Y$ and sampling kernel $Q$, the joint prior-data law is $P$ and the posterior is $K$. The loss $L(y,a):=\ell(a,y)$ is jointly measurable because the coordinate swap on product spaces has rectangle preimages that are rectangles by [F8], and composition preserves measurability by [F9]. The lifted kernel $\widetilde K((x,a),D):=K(x,D)$ is a probability kernel, since its evaluations are measurable by projection from $x$; [F7] therefore makes $q(x,a)$ measurable. [A1, F1, F2, F6, F7, F8, F9]

2.1 For any measurable $g:\mathsf X\to\mathsf A$, the point-mass rule $\delta_g(x,B):=\mathbf1_B(g(x))$ is a probability kernel, since each section is a Dirac probability measure and each evaluation is measurable by [F9]. The function $(y,x)\mapsto\ell(g(x),y)$ is product-measurable by the rectangle-preimage criterion [F8, F9]. Its prior-integrated risk is $\int_{\mathsf Y}\int_{\mathsf X}\ell(g(x),y)Q(y,dx)P_Y(dy)=R_P(g)$ by [F3, F5], with the same identity for $f^*$. Under [F4], use parameter $y$, prior $P_Y$, data $x$, model kernel $Q$, posterior $K$, action space $\mathsf A$, loss $L$, and rule $f^*$. Its posterior risk is $q$, so the common full-measure minimizing premise applies; the theorem gives prior risk of $f^*$ no larger than that of $\delta_g$. Thus $R_P(f^*)\le R_P(g)$. Since $g$ was arbitrary, the claim follows. [A1, F3, F4, F5, F8, F9, given, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
