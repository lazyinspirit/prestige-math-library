## Example

For $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}_2$ and $W=\{123,213\}$, write a weight as its
coordinate $l=\lambda(h)$, so $\rho=1$, the dot action is $s\cdot l=-l-2$, and
the dominant regular integral weights are $l=n\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ with regular
integral block $C_n$ on the labels $\{n,-n-2\}$. Then for $n=0$ and $n=1$ the
two blocks $C_0$ and $C_1$ have identical multiplicity data: with $e=123$ and
$s=213$,
$$(P(e\cdot l):\Delta(e\cdot l))=1,\quad (P(s\cdot l):\Delta(e\cdot l))=1,\quad (P(s\cdot l):\Delta(s\cdot l))=1,\quad (P(e\cdot l):\Delta(s\cdot l))=0,$$
$$[M(e\cdot l):L(e\cdot l)]=[M(e\cdot l):L(s\cdot l)]=[M(s\cdot l):L(s\cdot l)]=1,\quad [M(s\cdot l):L(e\cdot l)]=0,$$
for $l=0$ and $l=1$, matching the Kazhdan–Lusztig values
$P_{123,123}=P_{123,213}=P_{213,213}=1$ and $P_{213,123}=0$ of $S_2$. In each
block $P(e\cdot l)=\Delta(e\cdot l)$ is the projective cover of $L(e\cdot l)$
and $P(s\cdot l)$ fits into the nonsplit sequence
$0\to\Delta(e\cdot l)\to P(s\cdot l)\to\Delta(s\cdot l)\to0$, while
$\Delta(s\cdot l)=L(s\cdot l)$; the character formula gives
$\mathrm{ch}\,L(e\cdot l)=\mathrm{ch}\,M(e\cdot l)-\mathrm{ch}\,M(s\cdot l)$
and $\mathrm{ch}\,L(s\cdot l)=\mathrm{ch}\,M(s\cdot l)$, and for the dominant
label $e$ the first identity telescopes to the Weyl character
$e^{n}+e^{n-2}+\cdots+e^{-n}$ of the $(n+1)$-dimensional simple module. The
actual equivalence
$F=\operatorname{pr}_{\chi_1}(L(1)\otimes-)\operatorname{incl}_{\chi_0}$, with
inverse
$G=\operatorname{pr}_{\chi_0}(L(1)^*\otimes-)\operatorname{incl}_{\chi_1}$,
sends the sequence $0\to M_{-2}\to M_0\to L_0\to0$ of the principal block to
$0\to M_{-3}\to M_1\to L_1\to0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice (inherited from the projectivity and transport suppliers below), the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ with Cartan element $h$, positive root $\alpha$ and $\rho=\alpha/2$, weights written as coordinates $l=\lambda(h)$; for $n\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ the block $C_n$ on the dot orbit $\{n,-n-2\}$, with labels $e=123$ and $s=213$.

[F1] Block conventions ([[def-regular-integral-dot-orbit-labelling-convention]], [[cor-central-characters-are-dot-weyl-orbits]]): $\Delta(w\cdot l)=M(w\cdot l)$, $L(w\cdot l)$, $P(w\cdot l)$ labelled by $W$, the central character depends only on the dot orbit, and a Verma is killed by the projection to a block exactly when its weight is outside the orbit of that block.

[F2] Verma modules and the PBW model ([[def-verma-module]], [[thm-pbw-model-of-a-verma-module]]): $M(l)$ is generated by the highest weight vector $v_l$, and multiplication $u\mapsto uv_l$ identifies $U(\mathfrak n^-)$ with $M(l)$; in particular the $v_k:=f^kv_l$ ($k\ge0$) form a basis and generate $M(l)$.

[F3] Antidominant Vermas are simple ([[cor-antidominant-verma-modules-are-simple]]): if $\langle l+\rho,\alpha^\vee\rangle<0$ then $M(l)$ is simple.

[F4] The maximal-label projectivity ([[lem-maximal-verma-is-projective-in-a-finite-truncation]]): in a finite downward-closed ideal of a linkage class, the Verma at a maximal weight is projective.

[F5] Projective covers ([[thm-category-o-has-enough-projectives]]): every simple admits a projective cover, unique up to isomorphism and indecomposable, and every object is a quotient of a finite direct sum of covers.

[F6] BGG reciprocity ([[thm-bgg-reciprocity]]): $(P(l):\Delta(l'))=[M(l'):L(l)]$ for all weights, the left side being the Verma-flag multiplicity.

[F7] Factors of a Verma ([[lem-jordan-holder-factors-of-verma-modules-lie-above-the-head]]): if $L(l')$ occurs in a composition series of $M(w\cdot l)$ for dominant integral $l$, then $l'=u\cdot l$ with $u\ge w$, and $L(w\cdot l)$ occurs exactly once.

[F8] Translation functors and their transport ([[def-translation-functor-between-o-blocks]], [[prop-translation-identifies-regular-integral-blocks]]): for dominant regular integral weights the functors $T_\lambda^\mu=\operatorname{pr}_{\chi_\mu}(E\otimes-)\operatorname{incl}_{\chi_\lambda}$ and $T_\mu^\lambda=\operatorname{pr}_{\chi_\lambda}(E^*\otimes-)\operatorname{incl}_{\chi_\mu}$ (with $E$ the finite-dimensional simple whose highest weight dominates $W(\mu-\lambda)$) are inverse exact equivalences carrying $\Delta(w\cdot\lambda)\mapsto\Delta(w\cdot\mu)$, $L(w\cdot\lambda)\mapsto L(w\cdot\mu)$ and $P(w\cdot\lambda)\mapsto P(w\cdot\mu)$.

[F9] Finite-dimensional tensoring preserves Verma flags ([[lem-tensoring-with-a-finite-dimensional-module-preserves-verma-flags]]): $E\otimes\Delta(\mu')$ has a finite Verma flag with factors $\Delta(\mu'+\eta)$, the factor occurring $\dim E_\eta$ times.

[F10] Kazhdan–Lusztig data ([[def-kazhdan-lusztig-polynomials-in-the-classical-q-normalization]], [[thm-the-kazhdan-lusztig-character-formula-in-regular-integral-category-o]]): for $S_2$ the two comparable-pair polynomials are $P_{e,e}=P_{e,s}=P_{s,s}=1$ and $P_{s,e}=0$; hence the inverse coefficient matrix at $v=1$ is $(q'_{x,w}(1))=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$, and in each block $\mathrm{ch}\,L(e\cdot l)=\mathrm{ch}\,M(e\cdot l)-\mathrm{ch}\,M(s\cdot l)$, $\mathrm{ch}\,L(s\cdot l)=\mathrm{ch}\,M(s\cdot l)$.

[F11] The Weyl character formula ([[thm-weyl-character-formula]]): for dominant integral $l$ the finite-dimensional simple $L(l)$ has character $A(l+\rho)A(\rho)^{-1}$, which for $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ is the finite geometric sum $e^{l}+e^{l-2}+\cdots+e^{-l}$.

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **The two blocks.** With $\rho=1$ the dot action on coordinates is $s\cdot l=s(l+1)-1=-l-2$, so the orbit of $n$ is $\{n,-n-2\}$; a weight $l$ is dominant regular integral exactly when $l\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$, and then $-n-2<n$ in the dominance order. Hence $C_n$ is a regular integral block with the two labels $e=123\mapsto n$ and $s=213\mapsto-n-2$; for $n=0$ and $n=1$ the abstract labelled data are the same, which is what makes the two blocks directly comparable. [F1, algebra]

1.2 **The PBW computation.** Fix $n\ge0$ and put $v_k:=f^kv_n$, a basis of $M(n)$ by [F2]; the highest weight vector $v_0$ generates $M(n)$ and spans the $n$-weight space, which is one-dimensional. From the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ relations $[h,f]=-2f$, $[e,f]=h$ one computes $h\cdot v_k=(n-2k)v_k$, $f\cdot v_k=v_{k+1}$, and, by induction on $k$ from $e\cdot v_0=0$, $e\cdot v_k=k(n-k+1)v_{k-1}$. If $M(n)=A\oplus B$ with $v_0=a+b$, then $a$ and $b$ lie in the one-dimensional weight space $M(n)_n=\mathbb Cv_0$; if $b\ne0$ then $b$ is a nonzero multiple of $v_0$, so $v_0\in B$ and $M(n)=U(\mathfrak g)v_0\subseteq B$, while if $b=0$ then $v_0=a\in A$ and $M(n)\subseteq A$; either way one of the summands is zero. So $M(n)$ is indecomposable. [F2, algebra]

2.1 **The submodule $W$.** Let $W:=\operatorname{span}\{v_k:k\ge n+1\}$. By step 1.2 it is stable under $f$ and $h$; and $e\cdot v_{n+1}=(n+1)(n-(n+1)+1)v_n=0$, while for $k\ge n+2$ one has $e\cdot v_k=k(n-k+1)v_{k-1}$ with $k-1\ge n+1$, so $e$ maps $W$ into $W$. Hence $W$ is a nonzero submodule. Its highest weight vector $v_{n+1}$ has weight $n-2(n+1)=-n-2$ and is killed by $e$; the linear map $M(-n-2)\to W$ sending the basis element $f^kv_{-n-2}$ of the PBW model of $M(-n-2)$ to $v_{n+1+k}$ is a $\mathfrak g$-module isomorphism by the action formulas of step 1.2 (both sides have the same $h$-eigenvalues and the same $e,f$-action). Since $-n-2+1=-n-1<0$, the Verma $M(-n-2)$ is simple by [F3], so $W\cong M(-n-2)=L(-n-2)$. [F2, F3, step 1.2]

2.2 **Characters.** By step 1.2 the weight multiplicities of $M(n)$ are $1$ in each weight $n-2k$, $k\ge0$, so $\mathrm{ch}\,M(n)=\sum_{k\ge0}e^{n-2k}$ and $\mathrm{ch}\,M(-n-2)=\sum_{k\ge0}e^{-n-2-2k}$; the character formula of [F10] therefore gives $\mathrm{ch}\,L(n)=\mathrm{ch}\,M(n)-\mathrm{ch}\,M(-n-2)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}e^{n-2k}=e^{n}+e^{n-2}+\cdots+e^{-n}$, in agreement with the Weyl character of the finite-dimensional simple $L(n)$ by [F11], and $\mathrm{ch}\,L(-n-2)=\mathrm{ch}\,M(-n-2)$. [F10, F11, step 1.2]

3.1 **The quotient is $L(n)$.** By step 1.2 the images of $v_0,\dots,v_n$ form a basis of $Q:=M(n)/W$, with weights $n,n-2,\dots,-n$, each occurring once; in particular $Q$ is finite-dimensional and nonzero with a one-dimensional $n$-weight space. By [F7] every composition factor of $M(n)$ is $L(n)$ or $L(-n-2)$, and $L(n)$ occurs exactly once. The simple $L(-n-2)=M(-n-2)$ is infinite-dimensional, because the PBW basis $\{f^kv_{-n-2}:k\ge0\}$ of $M(-n-2)$ is infinite by [F2]; hence no composition factor of the finite-dimensional module $Q$ is $L(-n-2)$, and the only remaining factor is $L(n)$, occurring once. Therefore $Q\cong L(n)$, and $M(n)$ has exactly the two composition factors $L(n)$ and $L(-n-2)$, each once. [F2, F7, step 1.2, step 2.1]

4.1 **The projective structure.** The block $C_n=\{n,-n-2\}$ is a finite downward-closed ideal with maximum $n$; by [F4] the Verma $\Delta(n)=M(n)$ is projective in the block. Its proper submodules are contained in the sum $\bigoplus_{k\ge1}M(n)_{n-2k}$ of the lower weight spaces, because a submodule containing a nonzero weight-$n$ vector contains $v_0$ and hence all of $M(n)$ by step 1.2; therefore $M(n)$ has the unique maximal submodule $M(n)_{<n}$ and simple head $L(n)$, and the epimorphism $M(n)\twoheadrightarrow L(n)$ is essential. By uniqueness of projective covers [F5], $P(n)=P(e\cdot l)$ is isomorphic to $\Delta(n)$ and is the projective cover of $L(n)$. For the other label apply BGG reciprocity [F6]: $(P(-n-2):\Delta(n))=[M(n):L(-n-2)]=1$ and $(P(-n-2):\Delta(-n-2))=[M(-n-2):L(-n-2)]=1$ by steps 2.1 and 3.1, while all other flag multiplicities vanish since the block has only these two labels. So $P(s\cdot l)=P(-n-2)$ has a flag with the two factors $\Delta(n)$ and $\Delta(-n-2)$, each once, and sits in the short exact sequence $0\to\Delta(n)\to P(-n-2)\to\Delta(-n-2)\to0$. This sequence is nonsplit: a splitting would exhibit $P(-n-2)\cong\Delta(n)\oplus\Delta(-n-2)$ as a direct sum of two nonzero objects, contradicting the indecomposability of projective covers in [F5]. Finally $\Delta(-n-2)=M(-n-2)=L(-n-2)$ by step 2.1. [F4, F5, F6, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 **The multiplicity data for $n=0,1$.** The four flag multiplicities of the Example are those just computed and do not depend on $n$: $(P(e\cdot l):\Delta(e\cdot l))=1$, $(P(s\cdot l):\Delta(e\cdot l))=1$, $(P(s\cdot l):\Delta(s\cdot l))=1$, $(P(e\cdot l):\Delta(s\cdot l))=0$ (the last because $P(n)\cong\Delta(n)$ has only the factor $\Delta(n)$). The composition multiplicities are likewise independent of $n$: $[M(n):L(n)]=[M(n):L(-n-2)]=[M(-n-2):L(-n-2)]=1$ and $[M(-n-2):L(n)]=0$ by steps 2.1 and 3.1, since $L(-n-2)$ is not among the weights of $M(-n-2)$ other than itself. For $n=0,1$ these are exactly the displayed matrices; they match $P_{e,e}=P_{e,s}=P_{s,s}=1$, $P_{s,e}=0$ by [F10], as asserted by the multiplicity theorem on the A page. [F10, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1]

5.2 **The transported sequence.** Take the principal block $n=0$ and the target $n=1$, both dominant regular integral, and note that for $\lambda=0$, $\mu=1$ the difference $\mu-\lambda=1$ has dominant member $\nu=1$; here $E=L(1)$ is the two-dimensional simple with weights $+1,-1$, each once, and $E^*\cong E$. By [F9] the tensor $E\otimes M(0)$ has Verma flag factors $M(1)$ and $M(-1)$, and $E\otimes M(-2)$ has factors $M(-1)$ and $M(-3)$. The projections to the blocks $\chi_1$ and $\chi_0$ keep exactly the factors lying in the respective dot orbits $\{1,-3\}$ and $\{0,-2\}$ by [F1]: hence $F(M(0))=\operatorname{pr}_{\chi_1}(E\otimes M(0))\cong M(1)$ and $F(M(-2))\cong M(-3)$, while $G(M(1))=\operatorname{pr}_{\chi_0}(E\otimes M(1))\cong M(0)$ from the factors $M(2),M(0)$ and $G(M(-3))\cong M(-2)$ from the factors $M(-2),M(-4)$. By the transport theorem [F8] $F(L(0))=L(1)$, $G(L(1))=L(0)$, $F(P(0))=P(1)$, $F(P(-2))=P(-3)$ and conversely. Since $F$ is exact, applying it to the nonsplit sequence $0\to M(-2)\to M(0)\to L(0)\to0$ of the principal block produces the exact sequence $0\to F(M(-2))\to F(M(0))\to F(L(0))\to0$, that is $0\to M(-3)\to M(1)\to L(1)\to0$ in the block $C_1$; it is nonsplit because an exact inverse equivalence $G$ would carry a splitting of its image back to a splitting of the original sequence. The original middle module is $M(0)=P(0)$, not $P(-2)$; it is indecomposable by step 1.2, and its submodule $M(-2)$ and quotient $L(0)$ are both nonzero by steps 2.1 and 3.1, so such a splitting is impossible. [F1, F5, F8, F9, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1]

6.1 The claims of the Example are therefore all verified: the two blocks $C_0$ and $C_1$ share the displayed multiplicity matrices (steps 4.1 and 5.1), the projective cover $P(e\cdot l)$ equals $\Delta(e\cdot l)$ and $P(s\cdot l)$ carries the nonsplit two-step sequence with $\Delta(s\cdot l)=L(s\cdot l)$ (step 4.1), the character identities telescope to the Weyl character of the $(n+1)$-dimensional simple (step 2.2), and the actual inverse equivalences $F$ and $G$ transport the principal-block Verma sequence to $0\to M_{-3}\to M_1\to L_1\to0$ (step 5.2). The two blocks use different weight series; only the labels and the combinatorial data agree. [step 4.1, step 5.1, step 2.2, step 5.2] ∎
