These examples apply the connected-cover classification in low dimensions. In $D=4$, symmetric powers of the defining $SU(2)$ representation distinguish massive spin one-half from spin one descent, while the null little group separates helicity from nonzero-translation continuous spin and retains both central parities on the double cover. The $D=3$ cases exhibit real massive spin that descends to no finite cover, plus two inequivalent null-translation signs and their independent central phases. The $D=2$ cases retain two inequivalent future null rays and a nontrivial Lorentz character at zero momentum.

- [[ex-d4-helicity-and-continuous-spin-central-parities]] distinguishes helicity and both central parities of continuous-spin representations.
- [[ex-d4-nonzero-null-translations-are-continuous-spin]] checks directly that a positive-radius null little-group representation has nontrivial translations.
