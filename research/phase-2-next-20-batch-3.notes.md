Independence uses thm-factorization-of-expectations-for-independent-variables through finite real sin/cos products; the older finite-range thm-expectation-of-products-of-independent-random-variables is not adequate for arbitrary inputs and is not used. Cauchy and triangle examples first compute transforms of known probability densities before invoking inversion, avoiding circular existence arguments. Every B item is a leaf.

The tightness bound uses the probability weight (1-|t|/delta)_+/delta and its squared-sinc transform; at |x|>=4/delta the latter is <=1/4. The converse applies DCT for fixed delta and then handles finitely many initial laws. It does not infer equicontinuity from pointwise convergence. AC is explicit for Prokhorov and for the countable-choice Riemann/Lebesgue bridge; choice-free basic identities are not strengthened just because later statements need choice.

Kernel composition uses an indicator/simple/MCT proof of the iterated-kernel identity: product-measure Tonelli alone does not establish it. Rational conditional versions enforce bounds, monotonicity, both tails, and rational right-limits on one measurable full-measure set. Coding transport repairs the measurable null set where the real kernel fails to give the coded Borel target full mass. Factorization similarly repairs good rational-CDF conditions and target support on the conditioning space. Uniqueness unions exceptional sets only over a countable determining algebra. No completion of the conditioning sigma-algebra is silently assumed. Density and Bayes normalizers require 0<m<infinity; both m=0 and m=infinity get a fixed measurable probability filler.
