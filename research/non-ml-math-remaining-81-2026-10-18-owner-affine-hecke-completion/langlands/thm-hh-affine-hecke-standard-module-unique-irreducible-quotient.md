## Statement

Let $(P,\sigma)$ be a Langlands datum for the complex affine Hecke algebra of any reduced based root datum with positive real parameters, as in [[def-hh-affine-hecke-weight-temperedness-and-positive-position]], and let $\nu\in C_P^{++}$ be its real exponent. Then $I_P(\sigma)$ has a unique maximal proper submodule and therefore a unique irreducible quotient $L(P,\sigma)$.

With $p,r$ as in [[lem-hh-langlands-dominant-projection]], the largest value of $r(\lambda(t))$ among the weights of $I_P(\sigma)$ or $L(P,\sigma)$ is $\|\nu\|$. The sum of the generalized weight spaces attaining that value is canonically the injected copy of $\sigma$ as an $H_P$-module. Every such weight satisfies $p(\lambda(t))=\nu$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A Langlands datum $(P,\sigma)$, exponent $\nu$, and $M=I_P(\sigma)$.

[F1] All logarithmic weights of $\sigma$ lie in $\nu+K_P$ and $\sigma$ is irreducible ([[def-hh-affine-hecke-weight-temperedness-and-positive-position]]).

[F2] For these weights the dominant projection is $\nu$ and its norm strictly decreases after every $w\in W^P\setminus\{1\}$ ([[lem-hh-langlands-dominant-projection]]).

[F3] Induction is finite dimensional, injects $\sigma$ as $1\otimes\sigma$, and has multiset of weights $\coprod_{w\in W^P}w\operatorname{Wt}(\sigma)$. Generalized weight spaces are exact in invariant-subspace sequences ([[lem-hh-affine-hecke-parabolic-weight-filtration]]).

## Proof

**Given:** These data. Write $S=1\otimes\sigma\subset M$ and $R=\|\nu\|$.

1.1 By [F1], [F2] and [F3], all weights of $S$ have $r$-value $R$ and projection $\nu$, whereas every weight contributed by a nonidentity coset has strictly smaller $r$-value. The $A$-stable quotient $M/S$ has exactly those smaller weights by the induction filtration and weight-space exactness. It follows that the sum $M^{\mathrm{top}}$ of all generalized weight spaces in $M$ with $r$-value $R$ is exactly $S$: the inclusion $S\subset M^{\mathrm{top}}$ holds by its weights, and the quotient has none of these weights. In particular $M^{\mathrm{top}}$ is an irreducible $H_P$-submodule. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 If an $H$-submodule $U\subset M$ contains a nonzero vector of a generalized weight space with $r$-value $R$, then $U\cap S\ne0$. This intersection is $H_P$-stable, so irreducibility gives $S\subset U$. The induced module is generated by $S$ under $H$, whence $U=M$. Consequently every proper $H$-submodule has all its weights of $r$-value strictly below $R$, and is contained in the sum of the lower generalized weight spaces as a vector subspace. The sum $J$ of all proper submodules is therefore contained in that same lower-weight subspace, is proper, and contains every proper submodule. Finite dimensionality ensures this sum is an ordinary finite sum of subspaces after a basis is selected. Thus $J$ is the unique maximal proper submodule and $M/J$ is irreducible. [F1, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Since $J$ has no top weights, exactness of generalized weight spaces identifies the top subspace of $M/J$ with $S$, including its $H_P$-action. It is nonzero, its projection vector is $\nu$, and every remaining weight has smaller $r$-value. Any irreducible quotient has a maximal kernel, necessarily $J$, proving uniqueness and all the claimed intrinsic top-space properties. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
