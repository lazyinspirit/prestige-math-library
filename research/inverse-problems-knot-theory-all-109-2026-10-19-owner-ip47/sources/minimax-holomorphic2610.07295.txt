Minimax Operator Learning for Holomorphic Maps between
Hilbert Spaces
Ly Duc Hoang∗, Sven Wang†, Jakob Zech‡
October 7, 2026
Abstract
We consider the problem of learning nonlinear operators G0 : X →Y from finitely many noisy
observations. While finite-regularity classes such as Lipschitz- or Ck-operators suffer from the curse
of sample complexity (i.e. algebraic sample complexity is impossible), algebraic convergence rates can
be obtained for holomorphic operators. In this paper we study information-theoretic minimax rates
for estimating holomorphic operators. We provide algebraic upper and lower bounds of the metric
entropy of holomorphy classes (matching up to an arbitrarily small loss in the exponent) as well
as statistical minimax lower bounds in both the random-design and a Gaussian white noise model.
For the mean squared error, our novel lower bounds match our upper bounds (up to an arbitrarily
small loss in the exponent) and coincide with the convergence rates obtained previously for related
holomorphy classes [55]. Finally, we apply the theory to the parameter-to-solution map and the
parameter-to-quantity-of-interest map arising from the elliptic Darcy flow equation.
Keywords: operator learning, holomorphic operators, minimax estimation, metric entropy, polyno-
mial chaos expansions, parametric partial differential equations

Which structural assumptions on an operator are sufficiently strong to break the curse of
sample/parametric complexity, while being large enough to contain relevant parameter-to-
solution operators arising in the context of PDEs?
One possible such structure is to consider maps which depend analytically/holomorphically on their
input parameters. Several works in approximation theory and uncertainty quantification have shown that
the parameter-to-solution map of many parametric PDEs admits holomorphic extensions with respect to
their anisotropically weighted input coordinates [14, 15, 13, 16, 22, 23, 26]. These works have in common

of the holomorphic map and leads to algebraic approximation rates despite both the input and output
being infinite-dimensional.
The goal of this paper is to establish analogous statistical results as well as to provide minimax lower
bounds, complementing the upper bounds derived in [55].

Metric entropy of classes of holomorphic operators
Following the classical notion of metric
entropy due to Kolmogorov and Tikhomirov [35], we will first quantify the size of the class of holomorphic
operators through its metric entropy [35] with respect to the L2(X, Y, γ)-norm and with respect to the
supremum norm. We show that, despite infinite-dimensional inputs and outputs, the metric entropy only
scales algebraically with ε →0. For the L2-case, the effective complexity is determined by the parameter
κ2 = min{s −1/2, t}.
Up to an arbitrarily small loss in the exponent, the metric entropy then scales like ε−1/κ2. Analogously,
for the supremum loss, the effective complexity is determined by the parameter
κ∞= min{s −1, t}
and the metric entropy scales like ε−1/κ∞.
The same combinations of input and output regularity parameters s and t appear in the exponent of
the L2- and L∞- approximation rates for neural and spectral operator surrogates [24]. More details can
be found in Section 3.
Minimax Lower Bounds for Operator Learning of Holomorphic Maps
The class of holo-
morphic operators allows for sparse approximation in the polynomial chaos expansion [14, 15, 11]. The
summability of the coefficients yields coefficient bounds and algebraic N-term approximation rates, which
are used in the proof of the upper bounds of the metric entropy and to control the bias of the statistical
projection estimators based on the polynomial chaos expansion. Balancing this against the stochastic
error arising from the observation noise yields the upper bound for the statistical estimation rates.

1.2
Related Literature
Approximation Theory for Operator Learning and Parametric Partial Differential Equa-
tions
Recent successful applications of deep learning methods in the sciences have motivated the de-
velopment of neural network architectures which are able to approximate solutions of partial differential
equations (PDEs). Popular examples include for example the Deep Ritz method [63], the Deep Galerkin
method [58] and physics-informed neural networks [54]. While these methods approximate individual
solutions of a PDE, operator learning seeks to approximate maps between infinite-dimensional spaces,
with a particular focus on parameter-to-solution maps of parametric PDEs. Neural-network based sur-
rogates are commonly referred to as neural operators[37], with prominent examples including Fourier
neural operators (FNOs) [43], Graph Neural Operators [44], DeepONet [46] and PCANet [6, 25]. Uni-
versal approximation results have been established for several such neural operator architectures [19, 41,
36, 37].

Introduction
For broad classes of Lipschitz or finitely Fr´echet differentiable operators, quantitative approximation
rates suffer from the curse of parametric complexity [40, 42]. Finding classes of operators for which this
curse can be circumvented, remains a central challenge in Operator Learning. One possible solution to
this problem arises from the approximation theory of parametric PDEs. For broad classes of operator
equations, the parameter-to-solution maps admit holomorphic extensions with respect to the countably
many expansion coefficients of the parameter. Examples include linear and nonlinear elliptic equations
[14, 15, 11], parabolic equations [27], and PDEs on parametrized domains [32, 16, 26, 23]. The holomor-
phic dependence of the solution on the parameters implies the sparsity of the corresponding generalized
polynomial chaos expansions and dimension-independent N-term approximation rates. Building on this
approximation theory, Schwab and Zech [57] studied neural network approximation of holomorphic maps
and Herrmann, Schwab, and Zech [24] established a unified framework for neural and spectral operator
surrogates with algebraic expression-rate bounds with respect to the L2 and L∞- norm. Related ap-
proximation results on Gaussian spaces are based on Wiener–Hermite polynomials, with holomorphic
extensions to complex strips [20, 47, 48].
Statistical Learning Theory in Operator Learning
In the noise-free setting, several works have
studied the sample complexity of recovering operators from finitely many exact observations. Going back
to earlier work by Mhaskar and Hahm [49] on the nonlinear n-width of Lipschitz operators with respect to
L2, these results have been generalized to general classes of Lipschitz or finite-regularity operators,[38, 2].
Therein, the authors show the prevalence of a curse of sample complexity for these classes, which states
that the reconstruction of such operators with algebraic sample complexity is in general impossible.
The statistical learning theory for operator learning with noisy observations has received less atten-
tion. For the estimation of linear operator from noisy samples, statistical errors have been studied in
[28] and lower bounds were studied in [10]. For nonparametric regression of Lipschitz operators from
noisy data, Liu et al. [45] were able to prove generalization bounds for neural-network based estimators.
For Banach space valued holomorphic operators and noisy observations, Adcock, Dexter, and Moraga
Scheuermann [1] and Adcock et al. [4] derive upper and lower bounds for the approximation in L2.
Results on minimax lower bounds for the nonparametric estimation of nonlinear operators have been
sparser. Adcock, Maier, and Parhi [3] derived statistical lower bounds for Lipschitz operators in fixed- and
random-design observation model, which were however only tight for exponentially decaying eigenvalues
of the design measure. In particular, they show that algebraic rates cannot be achieved for such classes of
operators. By contrast, Reinhardt, Wang, and Zech [55] established algebraic, statistical upper bounds
for empirical risk minimizers over classes of so-called FrameNets if the operator is holomorphic.
A recent survey by Brugiapaglia, Franco, and Nelsen [9] highlighted the open question of sharp lower
bounds for operator learning under statistical observation noise and ask whether the rates by Reinhardt,
Wang, and Zech [55] are minimax-optimal. Our results address this open question both in the random-
design white noise model, with our lower bounds matching the upper bounds up to an arbitrarily small
loss in the exponent. Furthermore, we establish the same minimax rates in the generalized Gaussian white
noise model, which are attained by certain projection estimators based on the generalized polynomial
chaos expansion.

As we will see in Section 2.2 and Section 4, Hs,t(L; Y) is the appropriate class of holomorphic

the anisotropic dependence on the input variables and the compactness of σs(Q) in X. The parameter

conditions are common in the context of parametric PDEs.
Since Yt embeds compactly into Y, the operator class is pointwise relatively compact. Together with
the equicontinuity, which follows from the holomorphy assumption, this yields relative compactness in
the relevant Bochner-space topology by the Arzel`a-Ascoli theorem, see Appendix A.1 for more details.
This compactness is essential for information-theoretic arguments based on packing and covering
numbers. Without such compactness in the input and output space, uniformly consistent statistical
estimation becomes impossible. This follows for example from [33, Theorem 5.7], see also [30, 29] for the
original statement.

Metric Entropy Upper and Lower Bounds
We first establish quantitative metric entropy estimates for the class of holomorphic functionals Hs(L; C)
and the class of holomorphic operators Hs,t(L; YC), in both the corresponding L2- and L∞-losses. The
upper bounds will follow from the polynomial approximation results and the Legendre coefficient esti-
mates of Section 2.2, whereas the lower bounds are obtained from the construction of explicit packings
of the corresponding classes.
The relative compactness of Hs,t(L; YC) with respect to L∞(supp(γ), Y) and L2(γ, Y) is proved in
Theorem A.1 and ensures that the metric entropy is finite. Although the metric entropy estimates in
Proposition 3.1, Proposition 3.2, Proposition 3.3 and Proposition 3.4 are of independent interest, they
will be used to derive the minimax lower bounds in Section 4.2, 4.3 and 4.4.

7
Conclusion and Further Directions
In this paper, we derived information-theoretic lower bounds for the sample complexity for estimating
holomorphic functionals g0 : X →R and operators G0 : X →Y under the random design white noise
model and the operator white noise model. In the latter model, the projection-type estimators developed
in this paper attain L2-convergence rates that match, up to an arbitrarily small loss in the exponent, the
corresponding lower bounds over a class of holomorphic operators. In the random-design setting, these
algebraic convergence rates were previously established by Reinhardt, Wang, and Zech [55] for empirical
risk minimizers over classes of Sparse FrameNets for a related class of holomorphic operators.
Therefore, our results establish algebraic minimax rates for the estimation of holomorphic operators in
the presence of observation noise, which complements earlier lower bounds and optimal recovery results
for operator learning of holomorphic maps without observation noise, as well as recent information-
theoretic lower bounds for noisy operator learning of Lipschitz and H¨older operators.
In particular, this resolves, for the class of holomorphic operators, the open question highlighted by
Brugiapaglia, Franco, and Nelsen [9], whether the rates obtained in [55] are minimax optimal.
We also demonstrated the applicability of our results by considering examples of coefficient-to-solution
maps as well as coefficient-to-quantity-of-interest maps arising from linear elliptic partial differential
equations.
We note that the holomorphy assumption is well-established for a wide array of models
including general elliptic PDEs [11, 14, 15], parabolic PDEs [27], Maxwell’s equations [32], the Calderon
projector [23], the Helmholtz equation [26] and Navier-Stokes-equations [16]. Several questions remain
open. It would be natural to extend the minimax theory of this paper to fixed-design regression, since
sparse-grid collocation methods are known to achieve comparable L∞- approximation rates, and to other
Lp-type losses for p ̸= 2 and p ̸= ∞. Another possible direction is to investigate whether the asymptotic
equivalence in the sense of Le Cam holds for the random design white noise model and the generalized
Gaussian white noise model. Finally, proving lower bounds for holomorphic maps on Gaussian spaces
would be of particular interest. In that setting, the relevant expansions are based on Wiener-Hermite
polynomials, and the corresponding complex extension domains are complex strips. First results on the
approximation theory of such operators have recently been obtained in [20, 47, 48].
