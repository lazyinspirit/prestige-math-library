## Exact contract needed by Theorem 8.5

The application assumes regular `κ<λ=p_s<t_s` and a `(κ,λ)` cut. On a suitable nontrivial pseudofinite order `X_a` with bounded arithmetic and internal-size comparison, construct a cut `(⟨d_ε:ε<κ⟩,⟨e_α:α<λ⟩)` such that `2e_{α+1}<e_α` and, for each `ε<κ`, some later `δ<κ` has `|[0,d_ε)|<_s|[0,d_δ)|`. The source states Claim 8.2 more generally for `κ≤p_s` and an `a∈Or(s)` on which “multiplication by 2” is written as a total `f:X_a→X_a`. In §5 arithmetic is explicitly partial near the order ceiling; a local contract must either choose/restrict an arithmetic-capable order and state the domain of each doubling iterate, or define a bounded total operation with the same laws on every value used. The printed phrase alone does not prove total doubling on all of `X_a`.

The direct prerequisite chain is: ESTT and pseudofiniteness (Defs. 2.3, 2.5, 2.8–2.11, pp. 9–12); definable-subtree treetops and below-ceiling bounds (Claim 2.14, Lem. 2.15, p. 14); uniqueness/existence of the other cut cofinality in any chosen nontrivial order and definable initial-segment closure (Lem. 3.1, Thm. 3.2, Cor. 3.10, pp. 15–18); countable `Or`-saturation in the uncountable-`p_s` case (Thm. 4.2, p. 19); and internal injections, corrected bounded arithmetic, strict interval-cardinality laws, and enough codes for the definable subtree `T_4` (Conv. 5.1, Lem. 5.3, Cor. 5.7, Concl. 5.8/5.15, pp. 20–24). For this particular claim, the §5 requirement is narrower than the six-coordinate coding used later in Theorem 8.5, but it remains a theorem obligation. Its `|A|<_s|B|` means an internally coded injection one way and no internally coded injection back, not an external comparison of infinite sets.

## Source-to-local proof reconstruction

1. **Tree and base.** In an arithmetic-capable `X_a`, let `T_4⊆T_a` contain sequences whose values satisfy, whenever `i<j`, `2c(i)<c(j)` and `|[0,c(i))|<_s|[0,c(j))|`. The tree predicate must be definable in `M_1^+`, using the §5 internal-injection codes; its initial segments stay in `T_4`. The one-point node `⟨0_a⟩` is below the ceiling, and it has arbitrarily long finite future chains using finite successors of `0_a`, provided bounded arithmetic and the finite internal-pigeonhole law are established. This is the paper's invariant (d): every value at every coordinate has finite chains of every standard length above it.

2. **Successor: one point must retain *all* finite growth.** From invariant (d) at `d_β`, for every standard `N` one can choose a finite chain of length `N+1`. Its first new point witnesses `x>2d_β`, strict internal size growth, and `N` remaining steps. It does **not** follow that one point works for every `N`; the printed “by inductive hypothesis (d), we may choose” skips this quantifier exchange. In the Theorem 8.5 application `p_s=λ>κ≥ℵ₀`, hence `ℵ₀<min(p_s,t_s)`. Define the `Or`-type in `X_a` consisting of `x>2d_β`, `|[0,2d_β)|<_s|[0,x)|`, and `P_N(x)` for all `N<ω`, where `P_N(x)` says there is an `N`-step spaced, strictly size-growing chain above `x`. Every finite subtype is realized by the first new point of a sufficiently long chain, **if** order increase gives strict internal-size increase and all finite operations are in domain. Theorem 4.2 realizes the countable type. Append that point; the old-coordinate invariants persist by transitivity. If `κ=ℵ₀` and `p_s` might itself be countable, no limit stage occurs before `κ`; choose each `d_n` as a sufficiently large finite successor instead, which itself has arbitrarily long finite future chains. This case split is a possible repair of the printed general Claim, but it still depends on the §5 laws.

3. **Limit `ε<κ`: below-ceiling bound and index pre-cut.** Because `cf(ε)<min(p_s,t_s)`, Lemma 2.15 bounds the earlier strictly increasing nodes by a `c∈T_4` below the ceiling. Put `d_∞=c(lg(c)−1)`. For each standard `k`, define `m_k` as the greatest index with `c(m_k)≤⌊d_∞/2^k⌋`, equivalently by a bounded definable predicate involving `2^k c(m_k)≤d_∞`. This needs the local bounded-arithmetic interpretation; `f^{-k}` is shorthand, not a primitive global inverse. Every earlier endpoint `n_β` lies **strictly below** each `m_k`: take at least `k+1` later endpoints before `ε` and apply the `2`-spacing in `T_4`. The left endpoints have cofinality at most `cf(ε)`, while the descending right family has coinitiality at most `ℵ₀`. In the uncountable case their sum is `<p_s`. If the right indices eventually stabilized at `m`, the predecessor of `m` would already lie strictly above every endpoint of the infinite left sequence; otherwise thin the right indices to a decreasing `ω`-sequence. Thus an unfilled pre-cut would yield a `(cf(ε),ℵ₀)` cut, prohibited by the definition of `p_s`. Choose `n^{**}` strictly between all `n_β` and all `m_k`, and restrict `c` through `n^{**}` (with an explicit inclusive/exclusive length convention). It remains below ceiling and extends each old node.

4. **The limit growth invariant needs an arithmetic lemma.** Let `u=c(n^{**})`. Since `n^{**}<m_k` for each standard `k`, every finite iterate `2^k u` is bounded by `d_∞` on its stated domain. For `u>0`, a chain such as `u,2u+1,2(2u+1)+1,…` of any fixed standard length fits below `d_∞` by using a sufficiently larger `k`; for `u=0`, use finite successors, noting that `d_∞` is above arbitrarily long finite chains. Each strict order increase must imply strict *internal* size growth for the corresponding initial intervals. Thus `u` has arbitrary finite future growth, and all earlier `c(i)` inherit it by prepending `u` to a future chain. This fills the source's “Clearly `c_ε` satisfies (d)” only **conditional** on a proved finite-iteration/interval-cardinality lemma and explicit closure below `d_∞`.

5. **Final cut and spacing.** As `κ<t_s`, Claim 2.14 gives an upper node `c∈T_4` for the full chain. Its endpoint indices `n_ε` are strictly increasing. Restrict the index order to `n<lg(c)`; Corollary 3.10 puts the relevant definable initial segment into `Or(s)`, and Theorem 3.2/uniqueness gives a decreasing `λ`-sequence `m_α` forming a cut with the `n_ε`. Set `d_ε=c(n_ε)`, `e_α=c(m_α)`. The tree predicate gives `2e_{α+1}<e_α` and strict internal-size growth along the left sequence. To see that the image pair is still a cut in `X_a`, suppose `z` lies between all `d_ε` and `e_α`. By pseudofiniteness, the bounded definable set `{n<lg(c):c(n)≤z}` has a greatest index `n`; monotonicity gives `n_ε≤n<m_α` for all `ε,α`, contradicting the index cut. This last transfer is implicit in the printed final sentence and is locally authorable from the stated definitions.

## Current verdict and exact hold

Claim 8.2 has a **plausible locally authorable route for its Theorem 8.5 use**: the countable-type argument repairs the successor, the `<p_s` index pre-cut repairs the limit, and pseudofinite maxima transfer the final cut. I have not verified a complete local proof. The unresolved load-bearing steps are (i) constructing an arithmetic-capable order and a domain-safe multiplication/iteration predicate despite the printed partial-arithmetic and length-formula issues, (ii) proving strict internal interval-cardinality growth and finite pigeonhole for the specific encoded injections, including the `2u+1` finite chains, and (iii) authoring the full earlier Lemma 2.15, Theorem 3.2/Corollary 3.10, and Theorem 4.2 suppliers with the same tree-length convention. The proof of Theorem 8.5 and the generic-ultrapower half remain separate open obligations. This addendum changes no selected scope, plan, manifest, readiness receipt, or engine state; `p=t` remains escalated.
