## Statement

Assume AC ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

Let $K$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero and let $(\mathcal I,E,\mu)$ be a marked ideal with $\mu\ge1$ on a smooth $K$-scheme $X$ of finite type ([[def-marked-ideal]]), where $\mathcal I$ is not identically zero on any irreducible component of $X$ (equivalently, its stalk at each component's generic point is nonzero).
Then $(\mathcal I,E,\mu)$ admits a canonical resolution: a resolution $(X_i)_{0\le i\le m}$, $X_0=X$, satisfying the four conditions of [[def-canonical-resolution-invariants]]:
(1) there are upper semicontinuous invariants $\operatorname{inv}$, $\nu$ and $\rho$ on the supports, with values in $\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\times\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^{\infty}$, $\mathbb Q_{\ge0}$ and $\operatorname{Sub}(E_i)$ respectively, defined successively in the course of the algorithm;
(2) the centers $C_i$ of the blow-ups are regular, are contained in the supports, have SNC with $E_i$, and are the locus where the pair $(\operatorname{inv},\rho)$ attains its maximum (components of the maximal locus of $\operatorname{inv}$);
(3) after blowing up $C_i$, (a) for $x\in\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal I_{i+1},E_{i+1},\mu)$ with $\sigma_{i+1}(x)\in C_i$ either $\operatorname{inv}(x)<\operatorname{inv}(\sigma_{i+1}(x))$ or $\operatorname{inv}(x)=\operatorname{inv}(\sigma_{i+1}(x))$ and $\nu(x)<\nu(\sigma_{i+1}(x))$; (b) for $\sigma_{i+1}(x)\notin C_i$ the three invariants are unchanged;
(4) for every etale morphism $\varphi\colon X'\to X$ the induced sequence $\varphi^*(X_i)$ is an extension of the canonical resolution of $\varphi^*(\mathcal I,E,\mu)$ and the invariants agree.
The construction is an induction on $\dim X$ and proceeds in two steps: Step 1 resolves marked ideals of maximal order by first replacing the ideal with the equivalent coefficient ideal of its homogenization, then moving marked ideals and normal-crossings strata apart (Step 1a), removing codimension-one components (Step 1ba) and reducing to hypersurfaces of maximal contact (Step 1bb), where the glueing lemma makes the invariant independent of the chosen tangent direction and the inductive hypothesis on the hypersurface provides the next center; Step 2 resolves a general marked ideal by decreasing the maximal order of its non-monomial part through the companion ideal (Step 2a), reducing to the monomial case (Step 2b), whose resolution is finite because $\nu$ takes values in the discrete set $\frac1\mu\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Assume AC. Let $K$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, let $X$ be a smooth finite-type $K$-scheme, and let $(\mathcal I,E,\mu)$ be a marked ideal with $\mu\ge1$ and $\mathcal I$ nonzero at the generic point of every component.

[A2] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: AC is assumed for the marked-sum, completion, and regular-local UFD suppliers used in the construction.

[A1] Encode each source invariant coordinate by the order embedding $q\mapsto(0,q)$ for $q\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}$ and $\infty\mapsto(1,0)$ into $\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^2$. Write $\widehat{\operatorname{inv}}$ for applying this map coordinatewise. Thus, for example, the source values $(s,\infty,0,0,\ldots)$ and $(0,\infty,0,0,\ldots)$ are compared using the rational tuples $((0,s),(1,0),(0,0),(0,0),\ldots)$ and $((0,0),(1,0),(0,0),(0,0),\ldots)$, respectively. Flatten each displayed pair and each grouped tuple into a single rational sequence, identified with its first coordinate and its remaining infinite tail in the stated codomain. This preserves lexicographic comparisons and uses only rational coordinates.



[F1] [[def-maximal-order-and-tangent-directions]], [[lem-derivative-ideals-have-the-same-support]]: a marked ideal is of maximal order when $\mathcal D^\mu(\mathcal I)=\mathcal O_X$; its support is $V(\mathcal D^{\mu-1}\mathcal I)$, and tangent directions are multiplicity-one sections of $T(\mathcal I)=\mathcal D^{\mu-1}\mathcal I$.

[F2] [[lem-homogenized-ideal-is-equivalent]], [[lem-coefficient-ideal-is-equivalent]]: $(\mathcal J,\mu)\simeq(H(\mathcal J),\mu)\simeq C(H(\mathcal J),\mu)$; replacing a marked ideal by an equivalent one does not change its supports, its admissible centers or the resolution process.

[F3] [[lem-glueing-homogenized-ideals]], [[lem-completion-automorphisms-for-tangent-directions]]: homogenized ideals look the same from all tangent directions, and the étale neighbourhoods constructed from two tangent directions identify the two pullbacks; hence invariants defined via different tangent directions agree.

[F4] [[lem-coefficient-ideal-restriction-support]], [[lem-coefficient-ideal-disjoint-centres]], [[lem-refined-giraud-maximal-contact]]: restriction of the coefficient ideal to a smooth subvariety computes the support on that subvariety, and multiple test blow-ups of the restriction define ones of the ambient marked ideal with centers in the strict transforms.

[F5] [[lem-codimension-one-maximal-order-components]]: in a maximal-order marked ideal whose support has codimension one, the codimension-one components are smooth and isolated, and blowing one up removes the support from the exceptional divisor.

[F6] [[lem-order-semicontinuity-and-snc-strata]]: the order function and normal-crossings count $s_E$ are upper semicontinuous; finite-range lexicographic assembly preserves this property. Upper semicontinuity of the algorithm's branchwise invariants must be proved in the induction below; it is not supplied by the definition.

[F7] [[def-companion-ideal-and-monomial-part]], [[lem-addition-and-multiplication-of-marked-ideals]]: if the support is empty, Step 2 is terminal and no companion is used. If $\operatorname{ord}_N(\mathcal I)=0$, the marked ideal is handled directly by the monomial Step 2b; when the input is of maximal order, $(M(\mathcal I),\mu)$ is an equivalent maximal-order input for Step 1. If $\operatorname{ord}_N(\mathcal I)>0$, the companion $O(\mathcal I,\mu)$ is of maximal order with support $\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal I,\mu)\cap\{x:\operatorname{ord}_x N(\mathcal I)=\operatorname{ord}_N(\mathcal I)\}$; its sum branch has positive marks and uses the AC clause of the addition lemma. It is considered on the open neighbourhood of the original support where $\operatorname{ord}(N)\le r$. A positive-residual-order companion is not asserted to be equivalent to the entire original input.

[F8] [[def-canonical-resolution-invariants]], [[def-equivalence-of-marked-ideals]], [[def-multiple-test-blowup-and-controlled-transform]]: a canonical resolution is a resolution whose invariants satisfy conditions (1)–(4) of the definition; equivalence preserves supports and multiple test blow-ups at every stage.

[F9] [[def-locally-finite-type-and-finite-type-morphism]], [[def-dimension-noetherian-topological-space]]: $X$ is Noetherian of finite dimension, so an induction on $\dim X$ is available, and closed subsets have finitely many irreducible components.

## Proof

The following construction repairs the domain of the companion invariant but does not supply a proof of the complete Statement: the standalone upper semicontinuity of $\nu$ and $\rho$ remains unresolved, as detailed in the Remarks.

1.1 Induction setup. We argue by induction on $d=\dim X$, componentwise on the disjoint irreducible components of the smooth scheme. For $d=0$, $X$ is a finite disjoint union of $K$-points; the generic-nonzero hypothesis makes $\mathcal I=\mathcal O_X$ on every component, so the support is empty and the trivial sequence is the canonical resolution. Assume the proposition for all smooth schemes of dimension $<d$ and all marked ideals on them; by [F2] we may replace any marked ideal of maximal order by its equivalent $C(H(\mathcal J,\mu))$, and we do so without changing the supports or the admissible centers. [A2, F2, F9]

1.2 Step 1a, eliminating strata contained in the support. At the start of each nonterminal Step 1a pass at stage $i$, compute and hold fixed for that pass $s_i=\max\{s_i(x):x\in\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal J_i,E_i,\mu)\}$, where $s_i(x)$ counts strict transforms of the boundary family $E$ fixed when this maximal-order Step 1 begins; new exceptional divisors created during Step 1a remain in the full $E_i$ but are not added to this boundary count. If the support is empty, terminate; if $s_i=0$, finish Step 1a and proceed to Step 1b without restricting to $H^0=X$. For $s_i>0$, use only irreducible components $H^{{s_i}}_{\alpha,i}$ of intersections of exactly $s_i$ boundary components that meet the support. These strata are smooth and have finitely many pairwise disjoint irreducible components; taking them componentwise ensures that each retained restricted ideal is generically nonzero unless the stratum belongs to the contained-support branch. Their intersections with the support are pairwise disjoint: a point in two distinct such pieces would lie on more than $s_i$ boundary components. The ambient intersections can overlap away from the support, where the induced test blow-ups are identities. If $H^{{s_i}}_{\alpha,i}\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal J_i,E_i,\mu)$, blow up this stratum. For $s_i\ge2$, the boundary count drops at every support point above it; for $s_i=1$, [F5] shows that the controlled transform has empty support above the codimension-one stratum. Assign points of this stratum the source invariant $(s_i,\infty,0,0,\ldots)$, represented by $((0,s_i),(1,0),(0,0),(0,0),\ldots)$ under [A1], with $\nu=0$ and $\rho=\varnothing$. This is lexicographically above the finite lower-dimensional invariant values on the other support pieces. Recompute $s$ only at the start of the next pass. [A2, A1, F5, F6]

1.3 Step 1b, codimension-one components. After Step 1a reaches $s=0$, no strict transform of the inherited Step 1 boundary family meets the support. If the support has codimension-one components, [F5] shows they are smooth and isolated from the remaining support; assign them the source invariant $(0,\infty,0,\ldots)$, represented under [A1] by $((0,0),(1,0),(0,0),\ldots)$, with $\nu=0$ and $\rho=\varnothing$. This is above all finite lower-dimensional invariant values, so these components are the next centers. Blowing them up makes the controlled-transform support empty above them. The remaining support has codimension at least two. [A2, A1, F5]

1.4 Step 2b, the monomial case. If the support is empty, terminate. Otherwise, for a monomial ideal $\mathcal I=M(\mathcal I)=\prod_D x_D^{a_D}$, set $\operatorname{inv}=(0,0,\ldots)$ and $\nu(x)=\operatorname{ord}_x(\mathcal I)/\mu$. At each point, let $A$ be the inclusion-minimal threshold subset selected by the source's maximal $\rho$ rule: $\sum_{D\in A}a_D\ge\mu$, while $\sum_{D\in A\setminus\{D_0\}}a_D<\mu$ for every $D_0\in A$. The maximal locus of $(\operatorname{inv},\rho)$ gives the regular SNC center locally defined by the divisors in this selected $A$. In a blow-up chart indexed by any $D_j\in A$, the new exceptional exponent is $a=\sum_{D\in A}a_D-\mu<a_{D_j}$, by the minimality condition with $D_0=D_j$. Thus at every support point above the center the total monomial order drops, so $\nu$ strictly drops while the monomial-case $\operatorname{inv}$ stays fixed; equivalently, $(\operatorname{inv},\nu)$ strictly descends. There are finitely many initial maximal support components. Each chosen component is replaced by finitely many maximal components above it, and the maximum monomial order on each new component is strictly smaller, as shown by the chart inequality. This is a finitely branching replacement tree whose path length is bounded by the initial integer monomial-order maximum. It is finite, so the process terminates with empty support; the discreteness of $\nu\in\frac1\mu\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ provides this integer bound. [A2, F6, F7, F8]

2.1 Step 1ab, moving support away from maximal boundary strata. This branch occurs only for the pass-start value $s_i>0$, after all strata contained in the support have been blown up. Resolve each lower-dimensional restricted marked ideal on $H^{s_i}_{\alpha,i}$. The restricted support pieces are disjoint, so their canonical sequences glue; on ambient overlaps outside the support there are no centers and the induced maps are identities. By [F4], each restricted support equals the corresponding ambient support intersection, and the glued sequence is a multiple test blow-up of the ambient ideal. On a support piece use $\operatorname{inv}(x)=((0,s_E(x)),\widehat{\operatorname{inv}_{\mathcal J_i|_{H^{s_i}_{\alpha,i}}}(x)})$, encoding the finite leading coordinate by [A1], with the restricted $\nu$ and $\rho$; the support piece determines the stratum uniquely. By induction, above each center the pair $(\operatorname{inv},\nu)$ strictly descends, while off the center the induced map is the identity and the invariants are unchanged. Once all these restrictions are resolved, no support point remains on any maximal stratum from the pass-start boundary count, so the maximum boundary count drops before the next pass; recompute it only at that next pass. As the count is a nonnegative integer, finitely many passes reach $s_i=0$, and then Step 1b begins. [A2, F4, F6, step 1.2]

2.2 Step 1b, hypersurfaces of maximal contact. When the inherited Step 1 boundary misses the support, first remove codimension-one support components as in 1.3. For the remaining codimension-at-least-two support, cover $X$ near the support by finitely many affine opens admitting a tangent direction $u\in T(\mathcal J)$; each $V(u)$ is a smooth hypersurface of maximal contact. By [F4], its restricted support is the ambient support intersection, and the inductive resolution on $V(u)$ transfers to the ambient ideal; off the restricted support, this transfer is an isomorphism. Set $\operatorname{inv}(x)=((0,0),\widehat{\operatorname{inv}_{\mathcal J|_{V(u)}}(x)})$, with $\nu,\rho$ from the restriction. By [F3], etale neighborhoods from two tangent directions identify the pulled-back homogenized ideals, so the values, centers, and local resolutions agree on overlaps. Thus above every transferred center $(\operatorname{inv},\nu)$ strictly descends by induction, and off the center all invariants are unchanged. The local sequences glue to the canonical resolution. [A2, A1, F1, F3, F4, F6, step 1.3]

3.1 Step 2, the companion reduction and monomial case. If $\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal I,E,\mu)=\varnothing$, the resolution is complete. Otherwise factor $\mathcal I=M(\mathcal I)N(\mathcal I)$ and compute $r=\operatorname{ord}_N(\mathcal I)$ as in [F7]. If $r=0$, $N(I)$ is a unit on a neighbourhood of the support, so the marked ideal agrees there with $(M(I),\mu)$: resolve this neighbourhood by Step 2b and extend by the identity outside its support, without forming a companion. The maximal-order Step 1 may use $(M(\mathcal I),\mu)$ only when the original input is already of maximal order. If $r>0$, form the maximal-order companion $O(\mathcal I,\mu)$ and apply Step 1. For $r<\mu$, a companion test sequence is simultaneously admissible for $(N,r)$ and $(M,\mu-r)$, and their product transforms to $(I_i,\mu)$ by the product rule. For $r\ge\mu$, the controlled transform of $I$ is the transformed $(N,r)$ times a monomial factor, with each new exponent containing $r-\mu\ge0$. Thus every companion center is admissible for $(I,\mu)$ in either case. Write $N_i^*$ for the transform of $(N,r)$; it remains of maximal order by [[lem-maximal-order-preserved-by-controlled-transform]]. Factoring its exceptional powers gives $N(I_i)$, so $\operatorname{ord}N(I_i)\le\operatorname{ord}N_i^*\le r$. At a point with $\operatorname{ord}N(I_i)=r$, every exceptional factor removed from $N_i^*$ is a unit there, so $\operatorname{ord}N_i^*=r$. If that point is in the original marked support, it is in the transformed companion support by the sum-support rule (or directly when $r\ge\mu$). Consequently clearing the companion support lowers the maximum order of $N(I_i)$ on the remaining original support, so the first source coordinate $r/\mu$, represented by $(0,r/\mu)$ under [A1] in $\operatorname{inv}=((0,r/\mu),\widehat{\operatorname{inv}_O})$ on the transformed companion support, strictly drops between companion passes. This formula is used only where $\operatorname{inv}_O$ is defined; values at other original-support points are assigned by the successive-transfer construction below. Within a companion pass, Step 1 gives strict descent of $(\widehat{\operatorname{inv}_O},\nu_O)$ above every center; therefore $(\operatorname{inv},\nu)$ strictly descends there, and off the center the induced map is the identity and all invariants are unchanged. Repeat only while $r>0$; the finite sequence of strictly decreasing nonnegative integer values of $r$ ends with empty support or $r=0$. In the latter case, Step 2b has constant monomial-case $\operatorname{inv}$ and strictly lowers $\nu=\operatorname{ord}_x(M(\mathcal I))/\mu$ above every center, so $(\operatorname{inv},\nu)$ strictly descends until the support is empty. [A2, A1, F7, F8, step 2.2]

4.1 Define the invariants on the entire support by successive assignment, rather than evaluating a companion invariant outside its domain. During a positive-$r$ pass assign the tuple in 3.1 only on the transformed companion support; within Step 1 assign boundary-stratum and maximal-contact values only on their stated domains. Each point not yet assigned lies off every center in that pass, so it has a unique unchanged lift through the pass, with the same local ideal and boundary germs. After that pass continue the algorithm on the remaining support. When its residual-order stratum, boundary-count stratum, or final monomial branch is reached, assign the corresponding invariant there and transfer it back through these unchanged lifts. Termination proved in the preceding steps guarantees a finite first assignment for every point still in a support: otherwise an unassigned point would survive to the empty final support. Use the same transferred value at all earlier stages where the point was untouched. In particular a point of residual order below $r$ receives its own later residual-order coordinate, not the current $r/\mu$ and not an undefined $\operatorname{inv}_O$. The analogous convention fills lower boundary-count pieces during Step 1. Thus the active maximum is unchanged, and the invariants at points outside every intervening center are unchanged by construction. [A2, A1, F6, F7, F8, step 1.4, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1]

5.1 Descent, termination, and canonicity. In a boundary pass the fixed pass-start count makes each maximal-stratum blow-up lower $s_E$ above its center; on restricted strata, induction strictly lowers $(\operatorname{inv},\nu)$ above centers, and the ambient maps are identities off the support intersections. The codimension-one branch assigns the top source value $(0,\infty,0,\ldots)$, rationally encoded as $((0,0),(1,0),(0,0),\ldots)$; blowing up that component makes the controlled-transform support empty above it. In the hypersurface branch, induction strictly lowers $(\operatorname{inv},\nu)$ above every transferred center and the maps are identities off the restricted support. In the companion branch, the first source coordinate $r/\mu$ (encoded as $(0,r/\mu)$) strictly decreases between passes and the companion invariant pair strictly decreases within a pass. In the monomial branch, $\operatorname{inv}$ is constant and $\nu$ strictly decreases above each center by 1.4. Thus in every branch the pair $(\operatorname{inv},\nu)$ strictly decreases above centers, while off centers the morphism is an isomorphism and all invariants are unchanged; each loop terminates by the decreasing boundary count, the decreasing integer $r$, induction in lower dimension, or the discrete nonnegative values of $\nu$. The values and maximal centers are canonical because they are determined by boundary counts, orders, the source's threshold-subset rule, and lower-dimensional canonical invariants; upper semicontinuity of the successively assigned primary invariant is checked branchwise as follows. On each closed maximal boundary-count piece the lower-dimensional invariant is upper semicontinuous by induction, and distinct pieces are disjoint on the support; points specializing to a larger boundary count receive a larger leading coordinate. In the nonboundary branch the finitely many maximal-contact charts identify their invariants étale-locally by [F3], so their closed superlevel loci descend and glue. For the successive assignment in 4.1, each residual-order or boundary-count stratum is unchanged until its own pass, so its transferred subsidiary invariant is upper semicontinuous on that stratum. Its leading coordinate is the pointwise residual order divided by the fixed mark, or the pointwise boundary count, respectively. The superlevel sets of these leading coordinates are closed by [F6]. Since only finitely many values occur at each finite inductive depth, a lexicographic superlevel set is a finite union of higher leading-coordinate superlevels and closed subsidiary superlevels on the corresponding closed leading-coordinate piece. Specialization to a higher leading coordinate therefore preserves the superlevel condition. In the monomial branch $\rho$ has closed superlevel loci given by finite unions of boundary intersections, and $\nu$ is a finite sum of fixed nonnegative exponents on such intersections. The local finite ranges and finite depths are obtained from the finite marked ideals in each terminating inductive pass. The finite-lexicographic lemma [F6] gives closed primary superlevel sets and closed chosen maximal loci in every branch; it does not give standalone upper semicontinuity of the auxiliary functions across different primary strata. Under an etale morphism, the tracked Step 1 boundary components, orders, monomial/non-monomial factors, threshold subsets, companion supports, and maximal-contact restrictions pull back compatibly; [F3] identifies the choices of maximal-contact direction, and [F4], [F8] preserve the test sequences and equivalences. Hence the induced sequence is an extension of the canonical resolution with agreeing invariants. These arguments construct the sequence and primary invariant comparisons, but do not prove the standalone upper semicontinuity of $\nu$ and $\rho$ demanded by [[def-canonical-resolution-invariants]]; that remaining obligation prevents closure of the complete Statement. [A2, A1, F3, F4, F6, F7, F8, step 1.2, step 1.4, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 4.1] ∎



## Remarks

The complete Statement is not supplied by the written algorithm. On $X=\mathbb A^2_K$ take $I=(x(x+y))$, marking $1$, and ordered boundary $E=\{V(x)\}$. Its monomial part is $(x)$ and its residual part is $(x+y)$. At points of $V(x)$ away from the origin, the residual order is zero, so successive assignment reaches Step 2b with $\nu=1$ and $\rho=\{V(x)\}$. At the origin the companion is $(x+y,1)$ and Step 1a restricts it to $V(x)$, giving $(y,1)$ with empty restricted boundary. The lower-dimensional codimension-one branch assigns $\nu=0$ and $\rho=\varnothing$. Thus the superlevel locus $\{\nu\ge1\}$ is $V(x)\setminus\{0\}$, which is not closed in the support $V(x)\cup V(x+y)$; the corresponding positive-$\rho$ superlevel locus is the same. The source's Proposition 3.0.8 makes the same standalone upper-semicontinuity assertion, but its displayed branch formulas give this counterexample. Semicontinuity on fixed-primary-invariant strata does not imply the stated independent semicontinuity on the whole support. A corrected interface or a different auxiliary-invariant construction is required; neither is supplied here.
