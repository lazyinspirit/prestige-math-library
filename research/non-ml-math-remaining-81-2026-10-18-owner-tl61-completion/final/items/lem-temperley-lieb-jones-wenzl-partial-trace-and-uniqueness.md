## Statement

Let $R$ be a nonzero commutative ring with unit, $\delta\in R$, and suppose $[2],\ldots,[n]$ are units. The projector $p_n$ of [[def-temperley-lieb-jones-wenzl-recursion-over-unit-denominators]] has coefficient $1$ on the identity diagram, is nonzero and idempotent, and is annihilated on both sides by each $U_i$ for $1\le i<n$. Its corner is $p_n\mathrm{TL}_n p_n=R p_n$, so it is the unique idempotent with identity coefficient $1$ and those two-sided annihilation conditions. For $n\ge1$,

$$\operatorname{ptr}_n(p_n)=\frac{[n+1]}{[n]}p_{n-1},\qquad \operatorname{Tr}_n(p_n)=[n+1].$$

Only the displayed denominator units are assumed; $\delta$ need not be a unit.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A nonzero commutative ring $R$ with unit, loop parameter $\delta$, the recursive elements $p_r$, and the diagram product convention.

[F1] The recursion is $p_{r+1}=P_r-([r]/[r+1])P_rU_rP_r$, with $P_r=p_r\otimes1$ and the stated unit conditions ([[def-temperley-lieb-jones-wenzl-recursion-over-unit-denominators]]).

[F2] The generators satisfy $U_i^2=\delta U_i$, $U_iU_{i+1}U_i=U_i$, and distant commutation ([[lem-temperley-lieb-generators-satisfy-local-relations]]).

[F3] Quantum integers satisfy $[r+1]=\delta[r]-[r-1]$ ([[def-temperley-lieb-quantum-integers-and-quantum-characteristic]]).

[F4] Every basis endomorphism diagram is generated by the $U_i$; every nonidentity basis diagram has fewer than $n$ through strands ([[thm-temperley-lieb-diagram-generators-span]], [[def-temperley-lieb-diagram-category]]).

[F5] The partial closure and unnormalized trace close the last strand and all strands, respectively ([[def-temperley-lieb-closure-trace-and-partial-trace]]).

[F6] Closing a last identity strand gives a factor $\delta$, closing $U_{n-1}$ on its last strand gives the straight identity on the remaining strands, and full closure factors through the partial trace ([[lem-temperley-lieb-closure-trace-component-identities]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** induction on the strand number.

1.1 At rank $2$, the recursion gives $p_2=1-\delta^{-1}U_1$ only when $[2]=\delta$ is a unit. Then $p_2^2=p_2$ follows from $U_1^2=\delta U_1$, $U_1p_2=p_2U_1=0$, and its identity coefficient is $1$; it is nonzero because the identity and cup-cap diagrams are distinct basis vectors. For the strong induction step, assume the stated assertions for all projectors through $p_r$ [IH]. For $r\ge2$, put $Q=p_{r-1}\otimes1\otimes1$, $a=U_{r-1}$, $b=U_r$, and $\mu=[r-1]/[r]$. The recursion gives $P_r=Q-\mu QaQ$. The supports of $Q$ and $b$ are disjoint, so they commute; using $b^2=\delta b$ and $bab=b$ from [F2] gives $bP_rb=\delta Qb-\mu QbabQ=(\delta-\mu)Qb=([r+1]/[r])Qb$, where [F3] proves the last equality. Since $QP_r=P_r$ and $Qb=bQ$, this is the absorption identity needed below. [F1, F2, F3, base, IH, given, algebra]

2.1 Set $X=P_rbP_r$ and $c=[r]/[r+1]$. Step 1.1 gives $X^2=P_r(bP_rb)P_r=([r+1]/[r])X$, hence $p_{r+1}^2=(P_r-cX)^2=P_r-2cX+c^2X^2=P_r-cX=p_{r+1}$. Also $bp_{r+1}=bP_r-cbP_rbP_r=0$, because $bP_rb=([r+1]/[r])Qb$ and $QbP_r=bP_r$. By [IH], every earlier generator annihilates $P_r$ on both sides. Horizontal reflection fixes the recursion and interchanges left and right, so $p_{r+1}U_r=0$ too. The correction term has fewer than $r+1$ through strands, while $P_r$ has identity coefficient $1$; hence $p_{r+1}$ has identity coefficient $1$ and is nonzero. [F1, F2, IH, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 If $D$ is any nonidentity basis diagram, [F4] writes it as a nonempty word in the generators. Its rightmost letter annihilates $p_n$ on the left and its leftmost letter annihilates $p_n$ on the right, by step 2.1. Thus $Dp_n=p_nD=0$. Expanding any $x\in\mathrm{TL}_n$ in the diagram basis gives $p_nxp_n=\lambda_xp_n$ for a scalar $\lambda_x\in R$, hence $p_n\mathrm{TL}_np_n=Rp_n$. If $p_n'$ is another idempotent with identity coefficient $1$ and the same annihilation, write $p_n=1+m$, $p_n'=1+m'$ with $m,m'$ linear combinations of nonidentity diagrams. The equalities $p_n'p_n=p_n$ and $p_n'p_n=p_n'$ follow respectively from $m'p_n=0$ and $p_n'm=0$; therefore $p_n=p_n'$. [F4, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 For $n\ge2$, write $p_n=P-([n-1]/[n])P U_{n-1}P$ with $P=p_{n-1}\otimes1$. Closing the last identity strand gives $\operatorname{ptr}_n(P)=\delta p_{n-1}$ by [F6]. Closing the last strand of $U_{n-1}$ gives the straight identity, so $\operatorname{ptr}_n(PU_{n-1}P)=p_{n-1}^2=p_{n-1}$. Therefore $\operatorname{ptr}_n(p_n)=(\delta-[n-1]/[n])p_{n-1}=([n+1]/[n])p_{n-1}$ by [F3]. For $n=1$, $\operatorname{ptr}_1(1_1)=\delta1_0=[2]p_0$, the same formula. [F1, F3, F5, F6, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 By [F6], $\operatorname{Tr}_0(p_0)=1=[1]$. Inductively, the partial-trace factorization gives $\operatorname{Tr}_n(p_n)=\operatorname{Tr}_{n-1}(\operatorname{ptr}_n(p_n))=([n+1]/[n])\operatorname{Tr}_{n-1}(p_{n-1})=[n+1]$. Together with steps 1.1–3.2, this completes the strong induction and proves all assertions. [F3, F5, F6, step 1.1, step 3.2, induction, discharge-induction, algebra] ∎
