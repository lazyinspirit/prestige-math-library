## Statement

Let $R$ be a commutative ring with unit, $\delta\in R$, $n\ge1$, and let $t$ be allowed for $W_{n,t}(R,\delta)$. Include $\mathrm{TL}_{n-1}(R,\delta)$ into $\mathrm{TL}_n(R,\delta)$ by adding a rightmost vertical strand, and restrict the right action along this inclusion. Set $W_{m,s}=0$ when $s<0$, $s>m$, or $m-s$ is odd. Then there is a short exact sequence of right $\mathrm{TL}_{n-1}(R,\delta)$-modules

$$0\longrightarrow W_{n-1,t-1}\longrightarrow \operatorname{Res}^{TL_n}_{TL_{n-1}}W_{n,t}\longrightarrow W_{n-1,t+1}\longrightarrow0.$$

The sequence gives a two-step filtration; it is not asserted to split.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $R$, $\delta$, $n\ge1$, $t$, and the link-state quotient $W_{n,t}(R,\delta)$.

[F1] Link states are basis half-diagrams; the right action stacks the state above the algebra diagram and kills lower-propagating terms ([[def-temperley-lieb-link-state-standard-modules]], [[lem-temperley-lieb-link-state-module-action-is-well-defined]]).

[F2] The inclusion $\mathrm{TL}_{n-1}\to\mathrm{TL}_n$ is horizontal juxtaposition with the identity strand, and composition is associative ([[def-temperley-lieb-diagram-category]], [[thm-temperley-lieb-diagram-category-laws]]).

[F3] Every basis diagram is a product of the elementary generators $d_i$; hence $\mathrm{TL}_{n-1}$ is generated by $d_1,\ldots,d_{n-2}$ ([[thm-temperley-lieb-diagram-generators-span]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $U$ be the span in $W_{n,t}$ of link states in which the rightmost bottom site $n$ is a defect. Removing that defect and its top endpoint gives a link state in $W_{n-1,t-1}$; conversely, adding a new rightmost bottom site joined to a new rightmost top defect gives the inverse map. These basis maps define an $R$-module isomorphism $W_{n-1,t-1}\to U$. [F1, given, construct]

2.1 Under the inclusion in [F2], the rightmost strand of every diagram from $\mathrm{TL}_{n-1}$ is vertical and does not meet the other $n-1$ sites. Stacking such a diagram below a link state in $U$ leaves the external site $n$ connected to its same defect. Thus $U$ is a $\mathrm{TL}_{n-1}$-submodule, and the isomorphism in step 1.1 intertwines the restricted action. [F1, F2, step 1.1]

2.2 The quotient by $U$ has basis the link states in which $n$ is paired to a site $m<n$. Cut the arc $(m,n)$ and turn $m$ into a defect. Noncrossing forces every defect to lie outside this arc, so this new defect is the rightmost one; the result is a link state with $n-1$ sites and $t+1$ defects. The inverse attaches a new site $n$ to the rightmost defect. These operations are inverse bijections of bases, so they give an $R$-module isomorphism of the quotient's underlying module with $W_{n-1,t+1}$. [F1, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Check the quotient map on a basis state whose rightmost site $n$ is paired to $m<n$. A generator $d_i$ with $i\notin\{m-1,m\}$ does not meet the endpoint $m$; stacking and cutting therefore give the same exterior matching and the same loop count. If $i=m-1$, the site $m-1$ is either a defect or is paired to a site $k<m-1$ (it cannot be paired to a site inside $(m,n)$, since that would cross the arc $(m,n)$). In the paired case, stacking joins the distinct arcs $(k,m-1)$ and $(m,n)$ and replaces them by the bottom arc $(m-1,m)$ and the arc $(k,n)$; cutting either result at the arc ending at $n$ gives the same link state, with no loop removed. In the defect case, stacking makes $n$ a defect, so the result lies in $U$ and is zero in the quotient. Cutting first makes $m-1$ and $m$ two defects; stacking $d_{m-1}$ joins their two top endpoints, leaving fewer than $t+1$ propagating strands, so its class in $W_{n-1,t+1}$ is also zero. If $i=m$, then $m+1$ lies inside the arc $(m,n)$ and must be paired to some $k$ with $m+1<k<n$. Stacking joins the distinct arcs $(m,n)$ and $(m+1,k)$, replacing them by $(m,m+1)$ and $(k,n)$; cutting at the arc ending at $n$ before or after this operation gives the same state, again with no loop removed. These cases exhaust generators touching $m$; hence the quotient map intertwines every $d_i$. By [F3] it intertwines the whole $\mathrm{TL}_{n-1}$-action. The basis descriptions in steps 1.1 and 2.2 give exactness at every term. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2] ∎
