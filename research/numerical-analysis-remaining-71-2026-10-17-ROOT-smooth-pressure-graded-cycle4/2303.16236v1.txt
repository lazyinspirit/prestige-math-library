The result of the present paper is the ﬁrst result on the estimate of the form (1.6)
for q ̸= 2, to the best of our knowledge. When q = 2, Ah has maximal regularity in
L2 since Ah generates an analytic semigroup and L2 is a Hilbert space. However, for
general q ̸= 2, a diﬀerent approach is necessary, as in the literature on Lq-theory of ﬁnite
element methods. For the parabolic case, discrete counterpart of the maximal regularity
is well-studied for both spatial and temporal discretization (e.g. [10, 25, 30, 19, 26, 18,
28, 24, 22, 31, 2]). To establish the spatially discrete maximal regularity, the Gaussian
estimate for the parabolic Green’s function plays an essential role (see e.g. [10, 25, 18]).
For the Stokes case, however, such an estimate is unavailable since the Stokes operator
does not generate an analytic semigroup in L1 (see [7]). Because of this diﬃculty, (1.6)
for q ̸= 2 has not been established, and only Lq-resolvent estimate for Ah was established
recently [20].
It would be possible to apply our result to the fully discretized case.
Indeed, an
L∞(J; L2)-error estimate for full discretization of the non-stationary Stokes problem
was recently established in [4]. To show the error estimate, the authors used maximal
regularity for Ah in L2. Thus our result makes it possible to generalize the results in the
Lq-setting. Namely, an L∞(J; Lq)-error estimate would be obtained for q ∈

Moreover, it is expected that the discrete maximal regularity (1.6) can be applied
to numerical analysis of the non-stationary Navier–Stokes equations. Indeed, for the
parabolic problems, the results of discrete maximal regularity mentioned above are suc-
cessfully applied to numerical analysis of nonlinear parabolic equations (e.g. [11, 29,

Therefore, it would be interesting to proceed numerical analysis for the
Navier–Stokes problem in this direction.
As possible applications of our result, we would like to introduce related studies. In
[15], it is shown that Ah is stable in fractional Sobolev spaces. This result is applied to
show boundedness in the space-time fractional Sobolev space of the numerical solution
to the Navier–Stokes equations in [14]. In these estimates, fractional Sobolev spaces of
L2-type are addressed, since Lq-type estimates for the non-stationary Stokes problems
were not developed at that time. Therefore, it would be possible to reﬁne the results
by using our estimate (1.6). In [27], it is shown that, if a numerical solution of the
three-dimensional Navier–Stokes problem is bounded in L∞(J; L4(Ω)), then there exists
a unique strong solution to the original problem. Although the setting of discretization is
diﬀerent from ours, discrete maximal regularity may be helpful to show the boundedness
assumption. We here remark that the required regularity is stronger than the Serrin
class.
The outline of the proof of (1.6) is totally diﬀerent from that of the parabolic problems.
In the literature on (spatially) discrete maximal regularity for parabolic equations, the
estimate is reduced to an L1-type estimate of the so-called regularized Green’s function
(cf. [10, 25, 18]), and to show such an estimate, the Gaussian estimate for the parabolic
Green’s function is used as mentioned above, which is unavailable in our case.
Instead of this, we introduce the “temporally regularized” Green’s function. Let η =
ηa,ε ∈C∞
0 (J) be an ε-approximation of the delta function with respect to a time a ∈J.
Then, we consider the dual Stokes problem with an external force ηφh for arbitrary
φh ∈Vh,σ, where Vh,σ is the discrete counterpart of Lp
σ(Ω) (precise deﬁnition will be
given later). We call the solution to this problem the temporally regularized Green’s
function and denote it by g = ga,ε. We also consider the ﬁnite element approximation of
g and denote it by gh. Then, the desired estimate (1.6) is reduced to a weighted estimate
for g −gh. In order to show the weighted estimate, we use the Lq-resolvent estimate for
Ah repeatedly, and this is why we assume q ∈

We ﬁnally mention the temporal discretization. There are several results stating that,
roughly speaking, (continuous) maximal regularity implies temporally discrete maximal
regularity (cf. [3, 5, 22, 19, 24, 28, 2]). Since some of these results are established in an
abstract setting, it is possible to establish full discretization of maximal regularity for
the Stokes problem straightforwardly.
The remainder of the present paper is as follows. In Section 2, we present the precise
settings of this study and state our main results. In Section 3, we collect some preliminary
estimates related to the discrete Stokes operator and the discrete Helmholtz projection.
The proof of discrete maximal regularity (1.6) is given in Section 4, and ﬁnally we show
error estimates for (1.5) in Section 5.
