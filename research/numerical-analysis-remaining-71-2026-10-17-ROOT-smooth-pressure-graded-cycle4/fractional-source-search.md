# Actual fractional projection source search

## Outcome

The desired fractional discrete Leray/Stokes theory has a direct literature antecedent, but the retrieved proofs use mesh and domain assumptions stronger than the original claim. No theorem establishing the required uniform bound on every matching shape-regular graded MINI family was found or proved in this search. This is a source-search result, not a claim that the extension is false or an open problem.

The conditional initialization reduction in `fractional-initialization-reduction.md` remains conditional. In particular, its additional estimate (F_alpha) has not been inserted into any canonical hypothesis or proof.

## Sources actually retrieved and inspected

1. **Guermond and Pasciak, Stability of Discrete Stokes Operators in Fractional Sobolev Spaces**, J. Math. Fluid Mech. 10 (2008), 588–610, DOI 10.1007/s00021-007-0244-z. Author-hosted complete PDF: <https://people.tamu.edu/~guermond/PUBLICATIONS/guermond_pasciak_2008.pdf>. Saved as `guermond_pasciak_2008.pdf` and extracted text. Inspected the introduction, domain assumptions, complete discrete assumptions in section 2, complete discrete-Leray proof in section 3, and the discrete-Stokes norm comparison in section 4.

   Lemma 3.1 proves uniform physical H^s stability of the discrete Leray projection for 0<=s<1/2. Theorem 4.1 compares the fractional discrete Stokes scale with physical fractional Sobolev norms. These are directly relevant to (F_alpha), with physical order s=2alpha. However, assumption (2.3) is the global inverse bound using the same h as global approximation, and (2.4) assumes full mass-projector stability. Section 2.1 explicitly discusses quasi-uniform finite elements as the setting supplying these assumptions. The proof of Lemma 3.1 first bounds the mixed mass-projection error by C h^s and then multiplies by h^(-s), exactly in display (3.2). On the original graded meshes, replacing this last bound by h_min^(-s) introduces forbidden grading dependence. The proof does not supply a local replacement.

   In addition, the paper assumes domain elliptic regularity in (1.1) and Stokes regularity in (1.9), and Lemma 4.1 uses these and the global inverse bound. Thus even independently solving the mass-projector part would not automatically validate every step of its discrete-Stokes scale comparison on the original Lipschitz polyhedral domains. Lemma 2.4 identifies MINI as satisfying its L2-gradient compatibility, but that compatibility alone is not its fractional theorem.

2. **Guermond, The LBB condition in fractional Sobolev spaces and applications**, IMA J. Numer. Anal. 29 (2009), 790–805, DOI 10.1093/imanum/drn028. Author-hosted complete PDF: <https://people.tamu.edu/~guermond/PUBLICATIONS/Guermond_IMA_JNA_2009.pdf>. Saved PDF and extracted text. Inspected the introduction, continuous fractional inf-sup hypotheses, discrete hypotheses in section 3, and the locations of the stationary/time-dependent applications.

   The discrete setting explicitly assumes a global inverse inequality (3.3), described as available for quasi-uniform mesh families. Theorem 3.2 uses that inequality in its proof. Its pressure estimates therefore cannot simply be imported for the permitted arbitrary graded families. The continuous gradient left-inverse discussion is informative but does not supply uniform discrete fractional projection stability.

3. **Li and Zhou, Numerical Analysis of 2D Stochastic Navier–Stokes Equations with Transport Noise: Regularity and Spatial Semidiscretization**, arXiv:2512.03483v3, PDF dated 12 December 2025: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.03483>. Saved PDF and extracted text. Inspected the continuous Helmholtz discussion and the complete relevant discrete estimates/proofs in sections 4.1–4.2, together with the numerical problem's stated mesh assumptions.

   This source uses the actual MINI pair and introduces J_(h,alpha)=A_h^alpha P_h A^(-alpha). It explicitly assumes a convex two-dimensional polygon and a **quasi-uniform** triangulation at the beginning of section 4 (PDF p.10). Its fractional scale equivalence (4.3), global inverse inequality (4.4), and projection approximation (4.13) feed into the resolvent proof of Lemma 4.3. It is a useful blueprint, but no original-scope supplier follows without replacing those steps. In particular, its treatment of loss of boundary conditions under the continuous Helmholtz projection is consistent with the upper physical-order restriction s<1/2 and does not assert full H1_0 projector stability.

4. **Karkulik, Pfeiler and Praetorius, L2-orthogonal projections onto lowest-order finite elements in R^d are H1-stable**, arXiv:1307.0917v1: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1307.0917v1>. Saved this explicitly versioned PDF and extracted text. Inspected the introduction and the fractional-space discussion in section 4.

   The title must not be read as an assertion for all shape-regular meshes. The introduction makes the newest-vertex-bisection/refinement structure explicit, and its fractional stability consequences follow from its established H1 stability under that structure. This is also a scalar/full Courant-space projection, not the MINI weak kernel projection. The most recent API metadata described a later version, but only v1 was successfully retrieved as a PDF in this search; no later-version contents are claimed to have been read.

5. **Kashiwabara and Kemmochi, Discrete maximal regularity for the finite element approximation of the Stokes operator and its application**, arXiv:2303.16236v1: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.16236v1>. Saved this explicitly versioned PDF and extracted text. Inspected the introductory scope, hypotheses (H1)–(H4), discrete Helmholtz section, and references identifying Guermond–Pasciak.

   Hypothesis (H1) explicitly requires shape regularity and quasi-uniformity. Its maximal regularity theorem is therefore not an arbitrary-graded-mesh result. No assertion about the inaccessible later-version full text is made.

6. **Meinlschmidt, Nonautonomous maximal parabolic regularity for nonsmooth quasilinear parabolic systems**, arXiv:2312.13852v1: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.13852v1>. Saved this complete versioned PDF and extracted text; inspected Theorem 2.1, the Sobolev interpolation discussion/Lemma 2.8, and their source references. Theorem 2.1 states an explicit quantitative Sneiberg principle: if an operator has endpoint norms at most gamma and an inverse of norm beta at an interior interpolation parameter theta_0, its inverse remains bounded by 8 beta whenever the parameter changes by at most min(theta_0,1-theta_0)/[6(1+2 beta gamma)]. The paper quotes this theorem from Auscher et al., *Nonlocal self-improving properties: a functional analytic approach*, Tunisian J. Math. 1 (2019), Appendix. That published appendix was subsequently retrieved and read as described in entry 7 below.

   The resulting actual-MINI near-2 proof is now written in `actual-mini-leray-lp-near-two.md`, including the discrete retraction construction and a full finite-dimensional quantitative openness proof. It retains standard ambient Sobolev complex interpolation, retraction and duality as explicit analytic inputs needing locally authored suppliers for a canonical version. Even that consequence alone does not establish physical H^s or pressure-semigroup smoothing.

7. **Auscher, Bortz, Egert and Saari, Nonlocal self-improving properties: a functional analytic approach**, Tunisian J. Math. 1 (2019), 151–183, published complete PDF <https://msp.org/tunis/2019/1-2/tunis-v1-n2-p01-p.pdf>. Saved `auscher2019.pdf` and extracted text. Read the complete Appendix, printed pp.177–181, including Theorem A.1 and all its supporting proofs. This is the actual source of the quantitative openness proof used in the new near-2 note. Also retrieved arXiv:1707.06294v1/v2; those versions do not contain the published appendix and are not substituted for it. The new note needs only the lower-bound part of the theorem because its domain and codomain have equal finite dimensions, so injectivity supplies surjectivity directly.

## What remains mathematically useful

The exact target is now more sharply identified: a local/graded replacement for the physical H^s discrete Leray estimate and the needed portion of the discrete Stokes norm comparison, or a restricted estimate for the actual smooth-solenoidal initialization defect that bypasses generic projection stability. Existing MINI L2-gradient compatibility is necessary context but is already available and does not close that interface.

The supplier owner's separate smooth helper has found additional actual MINI structure: a scalar mass-Schur representation, an exact cellwise weighted-average left inverse of R_h grad, and a strictly diagonally dominant condensed mass matrix after bubble elimination. These are not generic inf-sup arguments. None has yet been converted here into the required fractional Stokes estimate. A generic small positive interpolation shift, even if proved, must be quantified: the symmetric reduction in the companion note needs alpha>1/8, equivalently physical order greater than 1/4. An unspecified arbitrarily small shift would not suffice.

No canonical content, metadata, controls, manifests, hashes, tests or gates were changed or run. The only additions in this continuation are research sources, their extracted text and this source note.
