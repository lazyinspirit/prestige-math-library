L2-ORTHOGONAL PROJECTIONS ONTO LOWEST-ORDER
FINITE ELEMENTS IN Rd ARE H1-STABLE
MICHAEL KARKULIK, CARL-MARTIN PFEILER, AND DIRK PRAETORIUS
Abstract. We prove that the use of newest vertex bisection ensures that the L2-
orthogonal projection onto the space S1(T ) of lowest-order Courant ﬁnite elements
is uniformly H1-stable even on adaptively reﬁned meshes in Rd, d ≥2.
The same
holds for the respective L2-orthogonal projections onto certain subspaces of S1(T ) which
incorporate zero Dirichlet boundary conditions resp. an integral mean zero property, and
these results essentially also transfer to other mesh-reﬁnement strategies.
1. Introduction & Main Result
The leading actor of this work is the L2-orthogonal projection Π(T ) onto the ﬁnite element
space S1(T ) of piecewise aﬃne and globally continuous functions (2), based on a simplicial
mesh T of a d-dimensional domain (or manifold) Ω. Some of the various theoretical and
practical applications of Π(T ), cf. [BY13, BPS02, KPP13], require uniform H1-stability,
i.e.,
∥Π(T )v∥H1(Ω) ≤C1 ∥v∥H1(Ω)
for all v ∈H1(Ω)
(1)
with a constant C1 > 0 independent of T . While the proof is well-known in the case
of globally quasi-uniform meshes [BX91], it is rather demanding in the case of locally
reﬁned meshes. The decay properties of the oﬀ-diagonal part of Π(T )’s inverse mass
matrix might be eaten up by the growth of T ’s elements. Existing results are therefore
based on two diﬀerent approaches, where we will follow the second one:
• Imposing global or local growth-conditions on the mesh. Works based on this
approach include [BPS02, Car02, CT87, EJ95, Ste02].
• The use of a level-function to deﬁne an equivalent local mesh-size function with
good behaviour. This approach was carried out in [BY13, Car04, KPP13].
Both approaches are substantial: The ﬁrst one can be used for arbitrary sequences of
meshes, as long as they fulﬁl these growth conditions. The second one does not impose
growth conditions (they may be violated for a ﬁxed mesh!), but needs a nested sequence
of meshes in order to deﬁne the level-function. Clearly, the advantage of either approach
is the drawback of the other. As mentioned before, we focus on the second approach from
now on and start with a brief discussion of the available works.
In the recent work [BY13], the authors consider meshes in two and three dimensions
and assume that the level of elements that share a vertex diﬀers at most by one. Their
result can be applied to diﬀerent local reﬁnement strategies, as long as they fulﬁl the
above assumption. For a ﬁxed strategy, this assumption needs to be ensured either by
the (maybe cumbersome) deﬁnition of a consistent level-function or by additional and
therefore artiﬁcial local reﬁnements (which is proposed in [BY13]). The work [BY13] is

The current work is the ﬁrst one in this context to consider simplicial meshes in Rd with
arbitrary d ≥2 and subspaces which account for, e.g., Dirichlet or Neumann boundary
conditions. We show that the L2-orthogonal projection onto the space S1(T ) of lowest-
order ﬁnite elements and certain subspaces thereof is H1-stable if T is a reﬁnement of
an initial mesh T0. As the analysis is technical anyhow, we keep things as simple as
possible in the ﬁrst place: (i), we focus on NVB in d dimensions, and (ii), we impose
some well-known restrictions on the choice of the reﬁnement edges which are also required
for the termination of a recursive implementation of NVB (see Section 2). Under these
assumptions, we show that the L2-orthogonal projection onto the space of lowest-order
ﬁnite elements with Dirichlet boundary conditions is H1-stable (Theorem 1) and discuss
immediate consequences like stability of Π(T ) in positive and negative fractional order
Sobolev spaces, weighted L2-spaces, and all the spaces that are used in basic ﬁnite element
theory, e.g., S1-functions with vanishing integral mean (Section 4.2–4.5).
We note that (i) and (ii) do not enter the proof directly. Instead, only the asser-
tions (11)–(12) of Lemmas 2–3 below turn out to be crucial, and it is known that (i)
and (ii) imply these. It is immediately obvious that (i) is not necessary, i.e., other re-
ﬁnement strategies may be used as long as the assertions of Lemma 2–3 remain valid.
However, it is not obvious how to generalize (ii) (for d = 2, this was done in [KPP13]).
We therefore discuss a stronger assumption, namely the possible diﬀerence of the levels
of elements sharing a (d −1)-dimensional face, depending on the underlying dimension d
(Section 4.6).

2. Bisection of simplicial meshes
A d-simplex T in RD is the convex hull T = conv{x0, . . . , xd} of d+1 vertices x0, . . . , xd ∈
RD that do not lie on a (d −1)-dimensional hyperplane. A ﬁnite set T of d-simplices in
RD is said to be a partition of a d-dimensional manifold Ω⊂RD, if Ωis the (relative)
interior of the union of these d-simplices, and if the intersection of two diﬀerent simplices
T, T ′ ∈T has d-dimensional measure zero. A partition T is said to be conforming,
if the intersection of two diﬀerent simplices T, T ′ ∈T is either empty or a hyperface of
both T and T ′. Throughout this work, a conforming partition T is called a mesh. A
mesh T of d-simplices is said to be γ-shape regular if

Bisection is a binary reﬁnement rule and thus gives rise to some level function:
For a coarse-mesh simplex T ∈T0, we deﬁne level(T) = 0. If T is bisected into two
sons T ′, T ′′, we deﬁne level(T ′) = level(T) + 1 = level(T ′′). In this case, it also
holds |T ′| = |T|/2 = |T ′′| and hence level(T) = log2(| bT|/|T|) if bT ∈T0 is the unique
coarse-mesh element with T ⊆bT.
Note that bisection does not lead to conforming meshes in general. By recursive reﬁne-
ment, called newest vertex bisection (NVB) in the following, it is usually guaranteed
that the reﬁned partition is in fact conforming and hence a mesh. To ensure that this
recursion terminates, one requires properties on the initial mesh T0. Following Steven-
son [Ste08], we call the initial mesh T0 admissible if
• T0 is conforming,
• for all T, T ′ ∈T0 which share a (d−1)-dimensional face T ∩T ′, one of the following
assertions holds:
(a) e(T) = e(T ′) ⊂T ∩T ′ and T, T ′ are reﬂected neighbours
(b) there exist son simplices t ⊂T and t′ ⊂T ′ with T ∩T ′ = t ∩t′ which are
reﬂected neighbors.
Here, reﬂected neighbors means that the ordered vertices of T ′ coincide with either
T := (x0, . . . , xd)γ or TR := (xd, x1, . . . , xγ, xd−1, . . . , xγ+1, x0)γ on all but one position,
and we note that T and TR are equivalent in the sense that bisection leads to the very
same two sons (9). Due to [Ste08, Theorem 4.3], admissibility of T0 ensures that, for all
n ∈N, each partition T obtained from bisection of T0 with level(T) = n for all T ∈T
is already conforming. Moreover, the admissibility condition is not only suﬃcient, but
also necessary to ensure this. Finally, admissibility of T0 guarantees that the recursive
implementation of NVB terminates.

4. Extensions
This section brieﬂy collects some additional observations and generalizations of Theo-
rem 1.
The Sections 4.2–4.3 generalize works of Steinbach [Ste01, Ste02] on the L2-
orthogonal projection in fractional-order Sobolev spaces H±s(Ω) and eH±s(Ω) for 0 <
s ≤1.
In Section 4.4, we prove that stability in Hs(Ω) and eH−s(Ω) is preserved if
the L2-orthogonal projection onto S1(T )-functions with integral mean zero is used. Sec-
tion 4.5 shows that stability of the L2-orthogonal projection is also guaranteed for locally
weighted L2-norms. The ﬁnal Section 4.6 addresses the validity of Theorem 1 and thus
the consequences of Section 4.2–4.5, for other mesh-reﬁnement strategies than NVB.
The main analytical tool in Section 4.2–4.5 is interpolation between Hilbert spaces
which is brieﬂy recalled in the following subsection.
