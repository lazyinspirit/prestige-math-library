## Statement

Let $r,N\ge1$ be integers and let $g_1,\ldots,g_N\in\mathbb R^r$. Define
$$
C=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^N\lambda_i g_i:\lambda_i\ge0\text{ for every }i\right\}.
$$
Then $C$ is closed in the Euclidean topology, and for all $x,y\in C$ and all real $\alpha,\beta\ge0$, one has $\alpha x+\beta y\in C$.

## Statement references

[Wouter Kager, A Short Simple Proof of Closedness of Convex Cones and Farkas' Lemma, Lemma and complete proof, pp. 1–2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.11678v2)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Integers $r,N\ge1$, a list $g_1,\ldots,g_N\in\mathbb R^r$, and the set $C$ in the Statement.

[F1] On $\mathbb R^r$, coordinates are indexed by $k<r$, and $\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_{k<r} x_k y_k$ and $\|x\|_2^2=\langle x,x\rangle$; in particular $\langle x,x\rangle=0$ implies $x=0$ ([[def-euclidean-inner-product]]).

[F2] A finite list is linearly independent exactly when every linear combination equal to zero has all coefficients zero ([[def-linear-independence]]).

[F3] For a square matrix over a field, having trivial kernel is equivalent to being invertible ([[thm-invertible-matrix-theorem]]).

[F4] Every nonempty finite set of real numbers has an attained minimum ([[lem-finite-set-has-max]], [[def-max-min]]).

[F5] On $\mathbb R^r$, $d_2(x,y)=\sqrt{\sum_{k<r}(x_k-y_k)^2}$ is a metric and $\|x-y\|_2=d_2(x,y)$ ([[lem-metrics-on-rn]], [[def-euclidean-inner-product]]).

[F6] A set is closed exactly when its complement is open, and a set is open when every point has a positive-radius metric ball contained in it ([[def-metric-topology]]).

[F7] Every nonzero real has positive square, $0^2=0$, and for $c\ge0$, $\sqrt c\ge0$ with $(\sqrt c)^2=c$; finite sums of nonnegative reals dominate each term, termwise inequalities may be summed, finite sums scale by a common scalar, and a zero sum of nonnegative terms forces every term to be zero ([[lem-of-square-positive]], [[def-integer-power]], [[lem-of-zero-mult]], [[thm-of-square-roots]], [[lem-finite-sum-laws]]).

[F8] Absolute values are nonnegative, vanish exactly at zero, satisfy $|ab|=|a||b|$ and $|a+b|\le |a|+|b|$, and obey $|a|^2=a^2$; squaring is monotone on nonnegative reals, and multiplication by a nonnegative real preserves nonstrict inequalities ([[def-abs-value]], [[lem-of-abs-value]], [[lem-of-triangle-inequality]], [[lem-of-square-monotone]], [[lem-of-sign-rules]], [[lem-of-zero-mult]]).

[F9] A metric is nonnegative ([[lem-metric-nonnegativity]]).

[F10] If a property holds at $0$ and passes from $n$ to $n+1$, it holds for every natural $n$ ([[thm-induction-principle]]).

## Proof

1.1 Take a representation $x=\sum_{i=1}^N\lambda_i g_i$ with every $\lambda_i\ge0$ and let $S=\{i:\lambda_i>0\}$. If the sublist indexed by $S$ is dependent, [F2] gives coefficients $c_i$, not all zero, with $\sum_{i\in S}c_i g_i=0$. Reverse all signs if needed so $J=\{i\in S:c_i>0\}$ is nonempty, and set $t=\min_{i\in J}\lambda_i/c_i>0$ using [F4]. Define $\lambda'_i=\lambda_i-tc_i$ for $i\in S$ and $\lambda'_i=0$ for $i\notin S$. For $i\in S$ with $c_i>0$, the minimum choice makes $\lambda'_i\ge0$ and makes at least one such coefficient zero; when $c_i\le0$, $\lambda'_i\ge\lambda_i>0$. Thus every $\lambda'_i\ge0$, the positive support strictly shrinks, and $\sum_{i=1}^N\lambda'_i g_i=x$. Repeating this finite support reduction at most $N$ times gives a representation of $x$ whose positive support is linearly independent. [given, F2, F4, choose, construct]

1.2 Fix a nonempty independent sublist indexed by $S=\{i_1,\ldots,i_s\}$, let $A:\mathbb R^s\to\mathbb R^r$ have these columns, and let $G=A^{\mathsf T}A$. If $Gz=0$, then $0=z^{\mathsf T}Gz=\|Az\|_2^2$, so [F1] gives $Az=0$ and [F2] gives $z=0$. Thus $G$ is invertible by [F3]. Put $B=G^{-1}A^{\mathsf T}$ and $P=AB$, so $BA=I$. The cone $C_S$ generated by this sublist is exactly $\{x\in\mathbb R^r:Px=x,\ (Bx)_k\ge0\text{ for }k<s\}$: if $x=Az$ with $z\ge0$, then $Bx=z$ and $Px=x$; conversely those displayed conditions give $x=P x=A(Bx)$ with nonnegative coefficients. Each coordinate of $Bx$ and $Px-x$ is a finite linear form $\ell(x)=\sum_{j<r}c_jx_j$. For $u\in\mathbb R^r$ and $k<r$, [F7] gives $u_k^2\le\sum_{j<r}u_j^2$; by [F5] and [F8], $|u_k|^2=u_k^2\le\|u\|_2^2$, and both $|u_k|$ and $\|u\|_2$ are nonnegative, so [F8] gives $|u_k|\le\|u\|_2$. For any finite list $a_0,\ldots,a_{n-1}$, induction using the two-term triangle inequality in [F8] gives $|\sum_{j<n}a_j|\le\sum_{j<n}|a_j|$: the empty case is equality and the successor step is the two-term triangle inequality. Applying this, the absolute-value product law, multiplication by nonnegative coefficients, and finite-sum monotonicity in [F7,F8] gives $|\ell(x)-\ell(y)|\le\sum_{j<r}|c_j|\,|x_j-y_j|\le(\sum_{j<r}|c_j|)\|x-y\|_2$. If an equation $\ell(x)=0$ fails at $x_0$, then $C_\ell:=\sum_{j<r}|c_j|>0$: otherwise [F7,F8] force every $c_j=0$, so $\ell$ is identically zero. For $\|x-x_0\|_2<|\ell(x_0)|/(2C_\ell)$, the estimate gives $|\ell(x)-\ell(x_0)|<|\ell(x_0)|/2$, hence $\ell(x)\ne0$. The same positivity of $C_\ell$ holds when an inequality $\ell(x)\ge0$ fails; throughout the ball of radius $-\ell(x_0)/(2C_\ell)$, the estimate gives $\ell(x)<\ell(x_0)/2<0$. Thus every point outside $C_S$ has a ball in its complement; by [F5,F6] that complement is open and $C_S$ is closed. For the empty sublist, $C_\emptyset=\{0\}$: if $x\ne0$, then $d_2(x,0)>0$ by [F5,F9], and every $y$ with $d_2(x,y)<d_2(x,0)/2$ is nonzero, since otherwise the triangle inequality would give $d_2(x,0)<d_2(x,0)/2$. Thus the complement of $\{0\}$ is open by [F6], so $C_\emptyset$ is closed. [given, F1, F2, F3, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, F10, algebra]

2.1 By step 1.1, every $x\in C$ belongs to $C_S$ for some independent subset $S$ of the $N$ generators; conversely every such $C_S$ is contained in $C$. There are only finitely many subsets $S$, and the empty subset is included. For any $x\notin C$, each closed $C_S$ has an open complement containing $x$; choosing one positive ball radius for each of the finitely many such complements and taking their positive minimum gives a ball about $x$ disjoint from their union. Thus $C$ has open complement and is closed. For $x,y\in C$, choose coefficients $\lambda_i,\mu_i\ge0$ with $x=\sum_{i=1}^N\lambda_i g_i$ and $y=\sum_{i=1}^N\mu_i g_i$. Then $\alpha x+\beta y=\sum_{i=1}^N(\alpha\lambda_i+\beta\mu_i)g_i\in C$ for all $\alpha,\beta\ge0$, proving the stated closure under nonnegative linear combinations. [step 1.1, step 1.2, given, F4, F6, choose, algebra] ∎
