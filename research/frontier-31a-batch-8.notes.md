## Conventions, proof route, and limits

For HA-8, Ext is first defined separately from supplied injective and
projective resolution data.  The balance theorem is not definitional.  Its
proof uses the first-quadrant double complex
`K^(p,q) = Hom(P_p, I^q)`, commuting pre-sign differentials, a direct-sum total
complex on finite diagonals, and finite-truncation acyclic assembly; it does
not conceal a spectral-sequence argument.  Only after independence, naturality,
and cocycle compatibility are proved does `def-balanced-ext-bifunctor` allow
the unqualified notation.  The two long exact sequences retain their distinct
variance, and dimension shifting prints its low-degree boundary separately.

For HA-9, an extension “of M by N” is `0 -> N -> E -> M -> 0` and therefore
represents `Ext^1(M,N)`.  Baer addition uses the diagonal pullback followed by
the codiagonal pushout.  Higher extension equivalence is the equivalence
generated by maps of exact sequences, not an assertion of one isomorphism of
all middle terms.  Splicing supplies the Yoneda product; associativity and
unitality are proved before comparison with derived composition.  No generic
graded-commutativity claim is made.  Ring global dimensions retain handedness;
the integer calculation uses a free-subgroup lemma plus nonzero
`Ext^1_Z(Z/n,Z)`, and does not invoke depth or regular-sequence machinery.

These pages assume the library's supplied resolution data and the indicated
projective/injective/abelian-category prerequisites.  The exact-functor item
is deliberately conditional on an explicit adjoint Hom identification and
projective/injective preservation.  Higher Yoneda/derived comparison is kept
under the resolution and smallness hypotheses stated in its item.  Infinite
sum/product Ext, localisation calculations, Tor/flatness, and derived-category
splitting are explicitly kept out of this pair with their coverage dispositions.
