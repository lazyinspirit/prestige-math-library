Frequently, a proof involves a sequence of constructions, each of which 
in turn involves alterations of some things. To avoid an orgy of notation in 
such cases, we often denote the altered things by the old symbols. The reader 
might adopt the point of view that such a proof proceeds by "induction on 
niceness." After having convinced ourselves that there is no obstruction to 
achieve some more niceness, we take up the same problem again, but with an 
improved induction hypothesis.
