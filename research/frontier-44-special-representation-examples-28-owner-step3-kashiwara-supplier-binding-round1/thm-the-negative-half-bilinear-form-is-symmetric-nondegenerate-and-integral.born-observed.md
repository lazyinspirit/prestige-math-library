## Statement

Let $U_q^-=\bigoplus_{\nu\in Q_+}U_q^-[-\nu]$ be the negative half of the
Drinfeld-Jimbo algebra of a finite symmetrizable Cartan datum with $a_{ii}=2$
([[def-drinfeld-jimbo-quantized-enveloping-algebra]],
[[def-positive-negative-and-toral-quantum-subalgebras]]), with skew derivations
$e_i',e_i''$ ([[def-kashiwara-skew-derivations-on-the-negative-quantum-half]]),
and let $(\ ,\ )$ be a bilinear form on $U_q^-$.

(i) **Existence and symmetry.** A bilinear form with $(1,1)=1$ and
$(F_iP,Q)=(P,e_i'(Q))$ for all $i,P,Q$ exists on $U_q^-$; every normalized
homogeneous form satisfying these conditions is symmetric; and by the
uniqueness of [[def-the-lusztig-kashiwara-bilinear-form-on-the-negative-quantum-half]]
there is exactly one such form. No separate right-adjunction formula for
$(P,QF_i)$ is asserted: in the rank-one subalgebra a formula
$(P,QF_i)=c\,(e_i''P,Q)$ with constant $c$ is false, as the explicit values in
(iv) show.

(ii) **Orthogonality.** $(U_q^-[-\nu],U_q^-[-\mu])=0$ whenever $\nu\ne\mu$.

(iii) **Nondegeneracy.** For every $0\ne x\in U_q^-$ there is $y\in U_q^-$ with
$(x,y)\ne0$; equivalently, the restriction of the form to each
finite-dimensional graded piece $U_q^-[-\nu]$ is nondegenerate.

(iv) **Integrality on the word lattice.** Let $A=\mathbb Z[q,q^{-1}]$
([[def-the-laurent-polynomial-ring]]) and let $W\subseteq U_A^-$ be the
$A$-span of the monomials $F_{i_1}\cdots F_{i_k}$ ($k\ge0$). Then
$W\subseteq U_A^-$ (because $F_i^k=[k]_i!F_i^{(k)}$ and $[k]_i!\in A$), and
$(W,W)\subseteq A$. On the larger divided-power lattice $U_A^-$ the form is
*not* $A$-valued: in the rank-one subalgebra generated by a single $F_i$ one
has, for all $a,b\ge0$,
$$(F_i^{(a)},F_i^{(b)})=\delta_{ab}\frac{q_i^{-a(a-1)/2}}{[a]_i!}=\delta_{ab}\frac{1}{[a]_{q_i^2}!},$$
in particular $(F_i^{(2)},F_i^{(2)})=1/(1+q_i^2)\notin A$; here
$[a]_{q_i^2}!=\prod_{m=1}^a(1+q_i^2+\cdots+q_i^{2(m-1)})$. Globally, with
$A_{\mathrm{loc}}=A[s^{-1}:s\in S]$, the localization of $A$ inverting the
multiplicative set $S=\{[r]_i!:i\in I,r\ge1\}$ (equivalently inverting the
Gaussian factorials $[r]_{q_i^2}!$), one has
$(U_A^-,U_A^-)\subseteq A_{\mathrm{loc}}$: divided-power words are word
vectors divided by finite products of these factorials. This is an exact
denominator bound, not an $A$-integrality assertion on $U_A^-$ in this
normalization.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The negative half with its grading, the derivations $e_i',e_i''$ and
the integral form $U_A$ with $W\subseteq U_A^-\subseteq U_q^-$
([[def-lusztig-divided-power-integral-form]]), and the axioms (H), (N), (R) of
[[def-the-lusztig-kashiwara-bilinear-form-on-the-negative-quantum-half]].

[F1] The separation lemma supplies a bilinear form $B$ on $U_q^-$ that is
homogeneous ((H): $B(U_q^-[-\nu],U_q^-[-\mu])=0$ for $\nu\ne\mu$), normalized
($B(1,1)=1$), satisfies $B(F_iP,Q)=B(P,e_i'Q)$ for all $i,P,Q$, is symmetric,
has zero left and right kernels on every finite-dimensional graded piece, and
whose values on the classes of words are given by the matching-permutation
formula; its construction uses AC only through the published generic braided
pairing
([[lem-the-negative-half-is-separated-by-iterated-skew-derivations]],
[[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F2] Axioms (H) and (N) together with (R) determine a form uniquely: peeling
first letters writes every positive-height $P$ as $P=\sum_iF_iP_i$ and
evaluates $B(P,Q)=\sum_iB(P_i,e_i'Q)$, reducing the height, with base
$B(1,Q)=\varepsilon(Q)$
([[def-the-lusztig-kashiwara-bilinear-form-on-the-negative-quantum-half]]).

[F3] $e_i'$ is $\mathbb Q(q)$-linear, and on words
$e_i'(F_{j_1}\cdots F_{j_m})=\sum_{t:j_t=i}q_i^{-a_{ij_1}-\cdots-a_{ij_{t-1}}}F_{j_1}\cdots\widehat{F_{j_t}}\cdots F_{j_m}$
with coefficients in $A$; also $e_i'(F_i^{(n)})=q_i^{-(n-1)}F_i^{(n-1)}$ and
$e_i'F_j=q_i^{-a_{ij}}F_je_i'+\delta_{ij}$
([[def-kashiwara-skew-derivations-on-the-negative-quantum-half]]).

[F4] $F_i^{(n)}=F_i^n/[n]_i!$, $F_i^n=[n]_i!F_i^{(n)}$, and
$[m]_i=q_i^{-(m-1)}[m]_{q_i^2}$ with
$[a]_{q_i^2}!=\prod_{m=1}^a(1+q_i^2+\cdots+q_i^{2(m-1)})$
([[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]).

[F5] $1+q_i^2\notin A^{\times}$ in $A=\mathbb Z[q,q^{-1}]$: its value at
$q=1$ is $2$, and no Laurent polynomial of the form $(1+q_i^2)^{-1}$ lies in
$A$ (an inverse in the domain $\mathbb Q[q,q^{-1}]$ would force
$1=(1+q_i^2)\cdot\text{Laurent}$).
[[def-the-laurent-polynomial-ring]] is used for the Laurent-ring arithmetic.



## Verification

**Proof technique:** read existence, symmetry, orthogonality and nondegeneracy off the separation lemma's constructed form; recover uniqueness by first-letter peeling; and compute integrality by height induction on word vectors, with the rank-one recursion for the exact divided-power denominators.

1.1 Existence, homogeneity, symmetry and nondegeneracy. Take the form $B$ of [F1]. It is bilinear, homogeneous by construction, normalized with $B(1,1)=1$, satisfies $(F_iP,Q)=(P,e_i'(Q))$, and is symmetric by the matching-permutation formula; its restriction to each finite-dimensional graded piece $U_q^-[-\nu]$ has zero left and right kernel because the piece is perfect under the underlying generic pairing. This proves (i)'s existence clause, (ii) and (iii). [F1, algebra]

1.2 Uniqueness and forced symmetry. If $B'$ satisfies (H), (N) and (R), then by [F2] its values are determined recursively from $\varepsilon$, so $B'=B$; hence there is exactly one normalized homogeneous form with the adjunction, and every such form is symmetric because $B$ is. [F1, F2, algebra]

1.3 Integrality on the word lattice. We show $B(u,v)\in A$ for classes of words $u,v$ by induction on the total height of $u$ plus $\deg v$ (in fact on the height of the first argument): if $u=1$, then $B(1,v)=\varepsilon(v)\in\{0,1\}\subseteq A$; if $u=F_iw$ then $B(u,v)=B(w,e_i'v)$, and $e_i'v$ is an $A$-linear combination of classes of words of strictly smaller height by [F3], so the induction hypothesis applies. By bilinearity $B(W,W)\subseteq A$, and $W\subseteq U_A^-$ because $F_i^k=[k]_i!F_i^{(k)}$ with $[k]_i!\in A$ by [F4]. [F2, F3, F4, algebra]

1.4 The rank-one divided-power values. Fix $i$ and assume $I=\{i\}$ for the computation, so that the rank-one subalgebra is the free algebra on $F_i$ and $e_i'(F_i^m)=q_i^{-(m-1)}[m]_iF_i^{m-1}$ by inducting on $m$ from $e_i'F_i^m=q_i^{-2}F_ie_i'F_i^{m-1}+F_i^{m-1}$. Dividing by $[m]_i!$ gives $e_i'(F_i^{(m)})=q_i^{-(m-1)}F_i^{(m-1)}$. Put $b_m:=B(F_i^{(m)},F_i^{(m)})$. Using $F_i^{(m)}=[m]_i^{-1}F_iF_i^{(m-1)}$ and (R), $b_m=[m]_i^{-1}B(F_i^{(m-1)},e_i'(F_i^{(m)}))=q_i^{-(m-1)}[m]_i^{-1}b_{m-1}$ with $b_0=1$, so $b_m=q_i^{-m(m-1)/2}/[m]_i!$. By [F4], $[m]_i!=q_i^{-m(m-1)/2}[m]_{q_i^2}!$, hence $b_m=1/[m]_{q_i^2}!=\prod_{j=1}^m(1+\cdots+q_i^{2(j-1)})^{-1}$; for $m\ne n$ the two divided powers have different heights, so $B(F_i^{(m)},F_i^{(n)})=0$ by homogeneity. At $m=2$ this is $1/(1+q_i^2)\notin A$ by [F5]. This proves the rank-one clause of (iv) and shows that the form is not $A$-valued on $U_A^-$. [F1, F3, F4, F5, algebra]

2.1 The right-adjunction failure and the global denominator bound. First, no constant $c$ makes $(P,QF_i)=c(e_i''P,Q)$ hold on the rank-one subalgebra: $(F_i,F_i)=B(F_i,F_i)=1$ and $(F_i,F_i\cdot1)=1$ force $c=1$ from $(e_i''F_i,1)=(1,1)=1$, but $(F_i^2,F_i^2)=[2]_i^2b_2=(q_i+q_i^{-1})^2/(1+q_i^2)=1+q_i^{-2}$ while $(e_i''F_i^2,F_i)=(1+q_i^2)(F_i,F_i)=1+q_i^2$, which differs; this justifies the parenthetical in (i). Second, every element of $U_A^-$ is an $A$-linear combination of divided-power words $\prod_iF_i^{(r_i)}$ with coefficients in $A$ (the divided powers generate $U_A^-$ by definition), and each such word is its underlying ordinary word divided by the finite product $\prod_i[r_i]_i!$; by bilinearity of $B$ and $B(W,W)\subseteq A$ from step 1.3, $B(U_A^-,U_A^-)\subseteq A_{\mathrm{loc}}=A[s^{-1}:s\in S]$. Since $[r]_i!=q_i^{-r(r-1)/2}[r]_{q_i^2}!$ by [F4] and $q_i^{\pm1}\in A^{\times}$, inverting the $[r]_i!$ is the same as inverting the Gaussian factorials $[r]_{q_i^2}!$; the bound is exact in rank one by step 1.4. This completes (iv). [step 1.3, step 1.4, F1, F3, F4, algebra] ∎
