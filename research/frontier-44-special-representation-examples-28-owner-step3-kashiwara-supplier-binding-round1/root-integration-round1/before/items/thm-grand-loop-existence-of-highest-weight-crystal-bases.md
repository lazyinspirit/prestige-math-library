## Statement

Let $\lambda\in P^+$ and let $V(\lambda)=L(\lambda)$ be the simple integrable highest weight module with highest weight vector $v_\lambda$ ([[thm-integrable-quantum-highest-weight-module-exists-and-is-unique]]). Let $\mathcal L(\lambda),\mathcal B(\lambda),\mathcal L(\infty),\mathcal B(\infty)$ be the lattices and generating residue sets of [[def-kashiwara-lattices-lambda-and-infinity]], let $\tilde e_i,\tilde f_i$ be the Kashiwara operators, let $\pi_\lambda:U_q^-\to V(\lambda)$, $\pi_\lambda(P)=Pv_\lambda$, and let $\Phi_{\lambda,\mu},\Psi_{\lambda,\mu}$ be the tensor maps of [[lem-highest-weight-tensor-maps-and-their-adjunction]]. For $\alpha=\sum_in_i\alpha_i\in Q_+$ write $|\alpha|:=\sum_in_i$ and $Q_+(r):=\{\alpha\in Q_+:\ |\alpha|\le r\}$.

(i) For every integer $r\ge0$ the following interlocking statements are all true.

- A(r): $\tilde e_i\mathcal L(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}\subseteq\mathcal L(\lambda)$ for all $i\in I$ and $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$.
- B(r): $\tilde e_i\mathcal B(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}\subseteq\mathcal B(\lambda)\cup\{0\}$ for all $i$ and $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$.
- C(r): for all $i$, $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$, $b\in\mathcal B(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha+\alpha_i}$ and $b'\in\mathcal B(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}$, one has $\tilde f_ib=b'$ if and only if $b=\tilde e_ib'$.
- D(r): for all $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$, $\mathcal B(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}$ is a $\mathbb Q$-basis of $\mathcal L(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}/q\mathcal L(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}$.
- E(r): for all $\lambda,\mu\in P^+$ and $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$, $\Phi_{\lambda,\mu}(\mathcal L(\lambda+\mu)_{\lambda+\mu-\alpha})\subseteq\mathcal L(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal L(\mu)$.
- F(r): for all $\lambda,\mu\in P^+$ and $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$, $\Psi_{\lambda,\mu}((\mathcal L(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal L(\mu))_{\lambda+\mu-\alpha})\subseteq\mathcal L(\lambda+\mu)$.
- G(r): $\Psi_{\lambda,\mu}((\mathcal B(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal B(\mu))_{\lambda+\mu-\alpha})\subseteq\mathcal B(\lambda+\mu)\cup\{0\}$ for all $\lambda,\mu$ and $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$.
- H(r): $\tilde e_i\mathcal L(\infty)_{-\alpha}\subseteq\mathcal L(\infty)$ for all $i$ and $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$.
- I(r): $\tilde e_i\mathcal B(\infty)_{-\alpha}\subseteq\mathcal B(\infty)\cup\{0\}$ for all $i$ and $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$.
- J(r): for all $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$ and $b\in\mathcal B(\infty)_{-\alpha}$ with $\tilde e_ib\neq0$, one has $b=\tilde f_i\tilde e_ib$.
- K(r): $\mathcal B(\infty)_{-\alpha}$ is a $\mathbb Q$-basis of $\mathcal L(\infty)_{-\alpha}/q\mathcal L(\infty)_{-\alpha}$ for all $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$.
- L(r): $\pi_\lambda(\mathcal L(\infty)_{-\alpha})=\mathcal L(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}$ for all $\lambda\in P^+$ and $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$.
- M(r): for all $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$, $i\in I$, $\lambda\in P^+$ and $P\in\mathcal L(\infty)_{-\alpha+\alpha_i}$, one has $\tilde f_i(Pv_\lambda)\equiv(\tilde f_iP)v_\lambda\bmod q\mathcal L(\lambda)$.
- N(r): for all $\lambda\in P^+$ and $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$, $\pi_\lambda$ induces a bijection $\{b\in\mathcal B(\infty)_{-\alpha}:\pi_\lambda(b)\neq0\}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal B(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}$.
- O(r): for all $\lambda\in P^+$, $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$ and $b\in\mathcal B(\infty)_{-\alpha}$ with $\pi_\lambda(b)\neq0$, one has $\pi_\lambda(\tilde e_ib)=\tilde e_i\pi_\lambda(b)$.

(ii) Consequently $(\mathcal L(\lambda),\mathcal B(\lambda))$ is a crystal basis of $V(\lambda)$ in the sense of [[def-crystal-lattice-and-crystal-basis]]: $\mathcal L(\lambda)$ is a crystal lattice of $V(\lambda)$, $\mathcal B(\lambda)$ is a $\mathbb Q$-basis of $\mathcal L(\lambda)/q\mathcal L(\lambda)$ with $\mathcal B(\lambda)=\bigsqcup_\mu\mathcal B(\lambda)_\mu$, the operators satisfy $\tilde e_i\mathcal B(\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal B(\lambda)\cup\{0\}$ and the partial-inverse axiom, and $\dim_{\mathbb Q(q)}V(\lambda)_\mu=\#\mathcal B(\lambda)_\mu$ for all $\mu$. The lattices $\mathcal L(\lambda)_\mu$ and $\mathcal L(\infty)_{-\alpha}$ are free $A_0$-modules.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Dominant integral weights $\lambda,\mu$, the simple integrable highest weight modules and the lattices $\mathcal L(\lambda),\mathcal L(\infty)$ generated by the lowering words, the tensor maps $\Phi_{\lambda,\mu},\Psi_{\lambda,\mu},S_{\lambda,\mu}$ and the operators with their string decompositions.

[F1] $V(\lambda)$ is simple and universally integrable cyclic of highest weight $\lambda$, with finite-dimensional weight spaces and support $\lambda-Q_+$ ([[thm-integrable-quantum-highest-weight-module-exists-and-is-unique]], [[def-quantum-verma-module-and-simple-highest-weight-quotient]]).

[F2] $\mathcal L(\lambda)=\bigoplus_\mu\mathcal L(\lambda)_\mu$, $\mathcal L(\infty)=\bigoplus_\nu\mathcal L(\infty)_{-\nu}$ are generated over $A_0$ by the lowering words, with the recursive spanning $\mathcal L(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}=\sum_i\tilde f_i\mathcal L(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha+\alpha_i}$ for $\alpha\neq0$ and the same for $\mathcal L(\infty)$; each component is finitely generated, torsionfree and free over the DVR $A_0$ ([[def-kashiwara-lattices-lambda-and-infinity]], [[cor-finitely-generated-torsion-free-modules-over-a-pid-are-free]]).

[F3] Module operators: $\tilde f_i\tilde e_i=\mathrm{id}-p_{\mathrm{top}}$, $\tilde e_i\tilde f_i=\mathrm{id}-p_{\mathrm{bottom}}$, $\ker\tilde e_i=\ker E_i$, $\ker\tilde f_i=\ker F_i$, and the string of an $E_i$-highest vector of rank-one weight $a$ has the positions $0,\dots,a$; the negative-half operators satisfy $\tilde e_i\tilde f_i=\mathrm{id}$, $\tilde e_iu=0$ iff $u\in\ker e_i'$, and $\tilde e_i$ lowers the root height by one ([[def-kashiwara-crystal-operators]], [[thm-the-i-string-decomposition-of-the-negative-half]]).

[F4] The maps $\Phi_{\lambda,\mu},\Psi_{\lambda,\mu}$ are the unique module maps with the prescribed top images, $\Psi_{\lambda,\mu}\Phi_{\lambda,\mu}=\mathrm{id}$, $\Phi_{\lambda,\mu}$ is an isometry onto the cyclic highest summand, they are adjoint for the product form, and $S_{\lambda,\mu}$ is the unique $U_q^-$-linear retraction with $S_{\lambda,\mu}\Phi_{\lambda,\mu}(v_{\lambda+\mu})=v_\lambda$ ([[lem-highest-weight-tensor-maps-and-their-adjunction]]).

[F5] Rank-one tensor rule: for tensors of rank-one divided strings, modulo $q$ the operator $\tilde f_i$ acts on the first factor when $\varphi_i(\text{first})>\varepsilon_i(\text{second})$ and on the second factor otherwise, $\tilde e_i$ acts on the first factor when $\varphi_i(\text{first})\ge\varepsilon_i(\text{second})$ and on the second otherwise, with the partial-inverse property on the resulting chains; the chains partition the rectangle of divided positions ([[lem-rank-one-tensor-strings-have-the-crystal-limit-rule]]).

[F6] Finite-support regularization: there is an auxiliary regular Cartan datum with a finite coordinate summand containing the finitely many top labels used and all simple roots, with integral fundamental weights $\Lambda_i$, independent simple roots, the same $d_i$ and Cartan matrix, and $K_i=K_{d_iH_i}$; it identifies the graded support categories and all operations used below, so auxiliary fundamental or large weights act as labels inside the same category ([[lem-finite-support-regularization-of-quantum-label-weight-modules]]).

[F7] Negative-half form: the bilinear form on $U_q^-$ is symmetric, nondegenerate and integral on the divided lattice, the form on $V(\lambda)$ restricted through $\pi_\lambda$ is the contravariant form of [F4], and the nondegeneracy and rank comparison inherit the axiom of choice declared in [[def-axiom-of-choice]] ([[thm-the-negative-half-bilinear-form-is-symmetric-nondegenerate-and-integral]], [[def-the-lusztig-kashiwara-bilinear-form-on-the-negative-quantum-half]]).

[F8] Finite matrix algebra: over a commutative ring a square matrix whose determinant is a unit is invertible, and over a local ring a finitely generated module is zero when its reduction modulo the maximal ideal is zero, by Nakayama ([[lem-determinant-trick-for-nakayama]], [[def-the-local-ring-at-q-zero]]).

[F9] The axiom of choice may be used as declared; all other arguments below are finite and choice-free ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** induction. Prove the interlocking clauses A(r)-O(r) simultaneously by induction on $r=|\alpha|$, in the order of the JKK grand loop: field spanning and the base cases, then string-component extraction, the rank-one tensor computations, the large-weight microlocal congruences, the lattice projections and the pairing steps, closing each clause at height $r$ before the next.

**JKK clause map.** The stages below follow [JKK §7] in the owner-corrected order: string components (7.3)-(7.4), rank-one tensors (7.5), E (7.6), the last-color-change splitting (7.7), the tensor-lattice decomposition (7.8), the large-weight microlocal congruences (7.10)-(7.11), M and L (7.12)-(7.14), A and H (7.15)-(7.17), I, O and B (7.21)-(7.24), the module pairing (7.25), the large-weight normalization (7.26, corrected), the dual transfer and special F (7.27)-(7.28), J (7.29), C (7.30), D and K (7.31), N (7.34), and general F and G (7.33 and 7.36, with Lemmas 7.32 and 7.35).

1.1 Field spanning by lowering words. For $\alpha\in Q_+\setminus\{0\}$ the module $V(\lambda)$ is generated over $U_q^-$ by $v_\lambda$, and the root decomposition gives $V(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}=\sum_{i\in I}F_iV(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha+\alpha_i}$; by induction on the height, $V(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}=\sum_w\mathbb Q(q)wv_\lambda$ over the words $w$ of total degree $\alpha$, and the same holds for $U_q^-[-\alpha]=\sum_iF_iU_q^-[-\alpha+\alpha_i]$. Since the $\tilde f_i$-words span the same spaces as the $F_i$-words by [F3], the $\mathbb Q(q)$-span of $\mathcal L(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}$ is $V(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}$, and the span of $\mathcal L(\infty)_{-\alpha}$ is $U_q^-[-\alpha]$. [F1, F2, F3]

1.2 The base of the induction. [base] For $r=0$ and $r=1$ all clauses hold: the only $\alpha$ are $0$ and the simple roots; the string of $v_\lambda$ has positions $0,\dots,\lambda(h_i)$, so $\tilde e_iv_\lambda=0$, $\tilde e_i\tilde f_i^nv_\lambda=\tilde f_i^{n-1}v_\lambda$ for $n\ge1$, and the top position of the $\tilde e_i$-string is detected by the finiteness of the module string; the unit $\mathbf 1\in U_q^-$ is killed by $\tilde e_i$ and satisfies $\tilde e_i\tilde f_i=\mathrm{id}$; on the one-dimensional spaces $\mathbb Q(q)v_\lambda$ and $\mathbb Q(q)\mathbf 1$ the residue sets are $\{v_\lambda\}$ and $\{\mathbf 1\}$; and the tensor clauses are checked on top positions, where $v_\lambda\otimes v_\mu$ is the highest vector by [F4]. [base, F2, F3, F4]

1.3 Induction hypothesis. [ih] Fix $r\ge1$ and assume all clauses A-O for all heights $<r$; the induction then proves them at height $r$ in the steps below. All statements are made in the auxiliary regular datum of [F6] with its fundamental and arbitrarily large dominant weights, and are transported back to the original labels at the end. [ih, F6, given]

2.1 String components. Let $u\in\mathcal L(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}$ with $|\alpha|<r$ and let $u=\sum_{n=0}^NF_i^{(n)}u_n$ be its admissible $i$-string decomposition with $u_n\in\ker E_i$. Then every $u_n$ lies in $\mathcal L(\lambda)$: by [F3], $\tilde e_i^NF_i^{(n)}u_n=0$ for $n<N$ and $\tilde e_i^NF_i^{(N)}u_N=\gamma u_N$ with $\gamma\neq0$; A$(r-1)$ makes $\tilde e_i^Nu\in\mathcal L(\lambda)$, so $u_N=\gamma^{-1}\tilde e_i^Nu\in\mathcal L(\lambda)$, and subtracting $F_i^{(N)}u_N$ iterates the argument. Consequently, if $b\in\mathcal B(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}$ is the residue of a lowering word, then modulo $q$ exactly one nonzero string component of a lift survives, by the lower-height clauses B and C, and that survivor is the residue of a unique top word; this is the residue form of the same statement. [F2, F3, step 1.3]

2.2 The rank-one tensor computations. Let $b\otimes b'$ be a tensor of two rank-one divided strings whose factor heights are $<r$. By [F5] the tensor lattice is stable under $\tilde e_i,\tilde f_i$ on these inputs, the comparisons are strict for $\tilde f_i$ ($\varphi_i(\text{first})>\varepsilon_i(\text{second})$) and non-strict for $\tilde e_i$ ($\varphi_i(\text{first})\ge\varepsilon_i(\text{second})$), and where both factor heights are below $r$ the partial-inverse property holds for the tensor basis; this includes the endpoint cases, where one factor is at its top or bottom and the other is not, because the vanishing is decided by the comparison of the two string lengths. [F5, step 1.3]

2.3 E(r). For $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$, the component $\mathcal L(\lambda+\mu)_{\lambda+\mu-\alpha}$ is spanned by the images $\tilde f_i$ of the components at height $\alpha-\alpha_i$ (for $\alpha\ne0$) and by $A_0v_{\lambda+\mu}$ for $\alpha=0$; the map $\Phi_{\lambda,\mu}$ is a module map, hence commutes with the $\tilde f_i$, and the induction hypothesis at height $<r$ gives $\tilde f_i\Phi_{\lambda,\mu}(\mathcal L(\lambda+\mu)_{\lambda+\mu-\alpha+\alpha_i})\subseteq\tilde f_i(\mathcal L(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal L(\mu))$; the tensor lattice is stable under $\tilde f_i$, so $\Phi_{\lambda,\mu}(\mathcal L(\lambda+\mu)_{\lambda+\mu-\alpha})\subseteq\mathcal L(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal L(\mu)$. [F2, F4, step 1.3]

2.4 Large-weight microlocal congruence. On the $n$-th string position the operator $q_i^{-1}K_iE_i$ has the rank-one coefficient $q_i^{a-2n+1}[a-n+1]_i$, whose inverse has nonnegative $q$-valuation at admissible positions; if $q_i^{-1}K_iE_iu\in q^NL$, extracting string components places all positive top components in $q^NL$. For $P=F_i^{(n)}Q$ with $e_i'Q=0$ the exact identity $q_i^{-1}K_iE_i(Qv_\lambda)=-(1-q_i^2)^{-1}q_i^{2\langle h_i,\lambda+\alpha_i+\mathrm{wt}Q\rangle}(e_i''Q)v_\lambda$ shows that the error terms have arbitrarily high valuation once every $\lambda(h_j)$ is large, uniformly on the finitely many words of a fixed degree; hence both $\tilde e_i$ and $\tilde f_i$ commute with $\pi_\lambda$ modulo $q$ for large weights. [F2, F3, F6, step 1.1]

3.1 Splitting at the last color change. Let $w=\tilde f_{i_1}\cdots\tilde f_{i_r}v_\lambda$ be a lowering word of height $r$ whose last color change is at position $t$, so $i_t\neq i_{t+1}=\cdots=i_r$, and let $\lambda_0=\Lambda_{i_t}$, $\mu$ large with $\lambda=\lambda_0+\mu$. Applying the coproduct to $v_{\lambda_0}\otimes v_\mu$, the first occurrence of $i_t$ lowers the first factor, while the repeated tail lowers the second factor; explicitly $E_{i_t}$ kills the repeated different-color tail and $F_{i_t}$ sends it to $F_{i_t}v_{\lambda_0}\otimes\text{tail}+q_{i_t}v_{\lambda_0}\otimes F_{i_t}\text{tail}$, whose second term lies in $q$ times the tensor lattice. Both factor degrees of the resulting residue are strictly between $0$ and $r$, so step 2.2 controls it and the prefix inputs have both heights below $r$. [F4, F5, F6, step 2.2]

3.2 M(r) and L(r). For arbitrary $\lambda$ add a sufficiently large $\mu$, apply $\Phi_{\lambda,\mu}$ and the large-weight congruence of step 2.4 to the tensor top, use E$(r-1)$ and the lower-height projection, and apply the retraction $S_{\lambda,\mu}$, whose $\tilde f_i$-compatibility modulo $q$ follows by checking its top-second and positive-second string positions; this gives M(r). Iterating M(r) along a finite word gives $\pi_\lambda(\mathcal L(\infty)_{-\alpha})\subseteq\mathcal L(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}$ and equality of word residues; recursive spanning gives $\mathcal L(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}=\pi_\lambda(\mathcal L(\infty)_{-\alpha})+q\mathcal L(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}$, and the finitely generated quotient vanishes by [F8], proving L(r). Moreover $\pi_\lambda$ induces a surjection of residue spaces at each height $\le r$. [F2, F4, F8, step 2.4]

4.1 Tensor-lattice decomposition. Let $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$. Then $(\mathcal L(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal L(\mu))_{\lambda+\mu-\alpha}=\sum_{i\in I}\tilde f_i(\mathcal L(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal L(\mu))_{\lambda+\mu-\alpha+\alpha_i}+\mathcal L(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}\otimes A_0v_\mu$ modulo $q$: apply all raising operators $\tilde e_i$ to a tensor residue; if every one kills the residue, its first factor must be the top $v_\lambda$ by the lower-height partial inverse of step 2.2, giving the boundary term; otherwise take the predecessor given by the partial-inverse rule at lower height, and the first-boundary terms are congruent, at each prefix, to the same word applied to $v_\lambda\otimes v_\mu$ by the top-second tensor rule, with only the previous degree involved. [F4, F5, step 2.2, step 3.1]

5.1 Nakayama upgrade at lower height. The decomposition of step 4.1, together with the finite generation of the components in [F2], upgrades the generated residue span of $\mathcal L(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal L(\mu)$ to lattice equality at height $<r$ without assuming a basis at height $r$: a finitely generated $A_0$-module whose reduction modulo the maximal ideal is spanned by the residue classes of the given elements equals the module generated by those elements, by [F8]. [F2, F8, step 4.1]

5.2 A(r) and H(r). Choose a common pole bound $N$ for $\tilde e_i$ on the finite set of labels and components under consideration; at large weight the large-weight isomorphism and the previous-degree bounds give the same bound. A tensor component of total degree $\alpha$ has the same bound: interior factor degrees are lower and already integral, and boundary degrees are handled by extracting positive string components in $q^{-N}$ and applying the rank-one tensor rule. If $N>0$, a pure-color word is checked exactly by rank one; every other word splits at its last color change into an interior tensor residue plus $q$ times the bounded tensor lattice by steps 3.1 and 4.1, so raising improves the bound to $q^{-N+1}$. Applying $\Psi_{\lambda,\mu}$ in raised degree uses F$(r-1)$, not F(r); comparing a word on $v_\tau\otimes v_\mu$ with its first-factor word times $v_\mu$, the projection $\Phi$ in raised degree uses E$(r-1)$ and extracting the second top improves the bound by one power. Transfer to $U_q^-$ by its large-weight lattice isomorphism and microlocal congruence. Hence $N$ decreases by one at each round, terminates at $0$, and A(r), H(r) hold. [F2, F4, step 2.2, step 4.1, step 2.4]

6.1 I(r), O(r) and B(r). For a nonpure negative-half word use the interior tensor splitting of step 3.1, apply the rank-one tensor raising rule and the lower-height clauses B and G, then transport through the large-weight projection of step 2.4; the pure-color case is rank one, giving I(r). For O(r), take a surviving word residue in $V(\lambda)$: tensoring with a large second top gives the residue $u\otimes v_\mu$; raising on a top-second tensor is congruent to $\tilde e_iu\otimes v_\mu$ by the rank-one rule, and comparing with the large-weight negative-half raising calculation, applying $\Phi$ and then $S$, gives O(r). Then I(r), O(r) and the residue-image statement of step 3.2 give B(r), with no same-height C or F premise. [F4, F5, step 3.1, step 2.4, step 3.2, step 5.2]

6.2 The module lattice pairing. String extraction is justified by A(r). Homogeneous top string components of different positions are orthogonal, because repeated contravariance reduces their pairing to a highest vector paired with a positive $F_i$-power, which is zero; at equal position $n$ with top rank-one weight $a$, the exact norm factor is $q_i^{n(a-n)}\binom{a}{n}_i\in1+qA_0$. For a positive-height pair, recursively write one input as $\tilde f_i$ of a lower-height input, extract both strings and apply these unit norm ratios; this gives $\langle\mathcal L(\lambda),\mathcal L(\lambda)\rangle\subseteq A_0$ and $\langle\tilde f_iu,v\rangle\equiv\langle u,\tilde e_iv\rangle\bmod q$. At $n=0$ or $a=0$ the endpoint terms are zero, so no finite-string identity is misused. [F3, F4, step 5.2, algebra]

7.1 The large-weight pairing normalization, corrected. If $Q$ has degree $-\nu$ and $P=F_iR$ with actual weight $-\nu+\alpha_i$, the exact commutator gives $\langle F_iRv,Qv\rangle=(1-q_i^2)^{-1}[\langle Rv,(e_i'Q)v\rangle-q_i^{2\langle h_i,\lambda-\nu+\alpha_i\rangle}\langle Rv,(e_i''Q)v\rangle]$; inducting on height with $\langle F_iR,Q\rangle=\langle R,e_i'Q\rangle$ in the half shows that, for $\nu=\sum_in_i\alpha_i$, the constant term is $C_\nu\langle P,Q\rangle$ with $C_\nu=\prod_i(1-q_i^2)^{-n_i}\in1+qA_0$, so $\langle Pv,Qv\rangle\equiv C_\nu\langle P,Q\rangle$ modulo any prescribed $q$-order once every $\lambda(h_i)$ is large. The other term at each stage carries a large positive power of $q_i$, and its remaining lower-degree pairing is a finite polynomial in the variables $q_j^{2\lambda(h_j)}$ with bounded coefficients, uniformly for the finitely many vectors of a component; Kashiwara's constant-term lemma in §4 has the consistent constant term, and the printed factor direction of the corresponding grand-loop clause is not used. [F2, F7, step 1.1, step 6.2]

8.1 Field-dual lattice transfer and special F(r). Choose an $A_0$-basis $P_k$ of $\mathcal L(\infty)_{-\nu}$ and let $Q_j$ be its field dual under the nondegenerate field form of [F7], $\langle P_k,Q_j\rangle=\delta_{kj}$; define $\mathcal L(\infty)^\vee_{-\nu}:=\sum_jA_0Q_j$; the $Q_j$ need not lie in $\mathcal L(\infty)$, and this is the only place where the inherited axiom of choice of [F7] is used. The corrected comparison of step 7.1 gives $\langle P_kv,Q_jv\rangle\equiv\delta_{kj}\bmod q$ because $C_\nu\equiv1$; the pairing matrix is therefore invertible over $A_0$ by [F8], the large-weight field/lattice isomorphism gives $\pi_\lambda(\mathcal L(\infty)^\vee)=\mathcal L(\lambda)^\vee$, and $(\mathcal L(\lambda)^\vee)^\vee=\mathcal L(\lambda)$. For $\mu$ large, decompose the tensor lattice as in step 4.1; the lowering-image part passes through $\Psi$ by F$(r-1)$. Pair the remainder $v_\lambda\otimes\mathcal L(\mu)$ with every $u\in\mathcal L(\lambda+\mu)^\vee$; writing $u=Pv_{\lambda+\mu}$ with $P\in\mathcal L(\infty)^\vee$, only the extreme toral term of $\Delta P$ can pair with a first top, and its scalar is $q^{\sum_in_id_i\lambda(h_i)}\in A_0$ while $Pv_\mu\in\mathcal L(\mu)^\vee$; adjointness therefore places $\Psi$ of that remainder in the double dual $\mathcal L(\lambda+\mu)$, proving special F(r) for large $\mu$ without assuming unimodularity. [F4, F7, F8, step 4.1, step 7.1]

9.1 J(r). For a nonpure lowering word use its fundamental/large-weight interior tensor residue of step 3.1, whose two factor heights are $<r$. If its raised negative-half residue is nonzero, large-weight raising compatibility and the lower clause G make the tensor raised residue nonzero, and the lower tensor partial inverse gives $b_{\mathrm{tensor}}=\tilde f_i\tilde e_ib_{\mathrm{tensor}}$; applying $\Psi$ to this degree-$r$ congruence using special F(r) of step 8.1, and using the large-weight lattice isomorphism and operator congruences of step 2.4, gives $b=\tilde f_i\tilde e_ib$ in $U_q^-$. 10.1 C(r). For a surviving module edge, string extraction and the lower partial-inverse clauses give C(r) in one direction; for its converse, lift through the lower clauses L and O and apply J(r) of step 9.1. [step 2.1, step 3.2, step 6.1, step 9.1]

11.1 D(r) and K(r). Take a finite linear relation among the residue classes of the lowering words at height $r$ and apply each $\tilde e_i$: below $r$ the raised residues form an independent basis, and the partial-inverse statement makes the surviving preimages under each $\tilde e_i$ distinct, so every positive-degree word residue has a nonzero raising edge — in $V(\lambda)$ it is a $\tilde f_i$-predecessor and the string-component argument gives the inverse, in $U_q^-$ the identity $\tilde e_i\tilde f_i=\mathrm{id}$ on the lattice does. Hence every coefficient of the relation vanishes; recursive definitions give spanning, so D(r) and K(r) hold. [F2, F3, step 2.1, step 6.1, step 10.1]

12.1 General F(r) and G(r). Once D$(r)$ and K$(r)$ and the partial inverse are proved, a residue killed by all $\tilde e_i$ in positive height is zero, by independence of the raised predecessor residues. If a field vector has all raisings in the lattice, take its minimal $q$-pole $N$; if $N>0$, its normalized nonzero residue is killed by all raisings, a contradiction, so the vector lies in the lattice. Applying this to $\Psi$ of an arbitrary tensor lattice element, whose raisings lie in the lattice by tensor stability at height $r$ and F$(r-1)$, proves general F(r). For a tensor basis residue, if every raising vanishes then its $\Psi$-image vanishes by the same residue detection; otherwise the tensor partial inverse, the lower clause G and the commutation of $\Psi$ with the operators produce a basis word or zero, which is G(r). [F4, F5, step 11.1]

13.1 Conclusion. Steps 1.2, 2.3, 3.2, 5.2, 6.1 and 9.1-12.1 prove A(r)-O(r) for every $r$ by induction, so all clauses hold at all heights. Then $\mathcal L(\lambda)$ is a free $A_0$-module by [F2] with $\mathbb Q(q)\otimes_{A_0}\mathcal L(\lambda)=V(\lambda)$ by step 1.1, stable under both operators by A(r); $\mathcal B(\lambda)$ is a $\mathbb Q$-basis of $\mathcal L(\lambda)/q\mathcal L(\lambda)$ with the graded decomposition and operator stability by B, D and the definition, and the partial-inverse axiom is C(r); hence $(\mathcal L(\lambda),\mathcal B(\lambda))$ is a crystal basis in the sense of [[def-crystal-lattice-and-crystal-basis]]. The rank equality $\dim_{\mathbb Q(q)}V(\lambda)_\mu=\#\mathcal B(\lambda)_\mu$ follows from the lattice splitting and D(r), and the negative-half statements give $\mathcal B(\infty)$ with the same properties, with the formal $\varphi_i$ of [[def-crystal-graph-epsilon-phi-and-weight]].
