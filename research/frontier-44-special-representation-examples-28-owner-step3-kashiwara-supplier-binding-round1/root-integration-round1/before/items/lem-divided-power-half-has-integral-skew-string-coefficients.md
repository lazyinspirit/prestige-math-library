## Statement

Let $U_q^-$ be the negative half of the Drinfeld–Jimbo algebra with its
Kashiwara skew derivations $e_i',e_i''$
([[def-kashiwara-skew-derivations-on-the-negative-quantum-half]]), let
$A=\mathbb Z[q,q^{-1}]$ and let $U_A^-$ be the divided-power half generated by
the $F_i^{(r)}$ ([[def-lusztig-divided-power-integral-form]]). Then:

(i) $e_i'$ preserves $U_A^-$: $e_i'(U_A^-)\subseteq U_A^-$.

(ii) Define, for $x\in U_q^-$,
$$P_ix:=\sum_{n\ge0}(-1)^nq_i^{-n(n-1)/2}\,F_i^{(n)}(e_i')^n x,$$
the sum being finite on each $x$ because $e_i'$ is locally nilpotent, and the
powers $(e_i')^n$ being ordinary, not divided, powers of the derivation. Then
$P_i$ is a well-defined $\mathbb Q(q)$-linear map with $e_i'P_i=0$ that fixes
$\ker e_i'$ pointwise and annihilates $F_iU_q^-$; hence
$U_q^-=\ker e_i'\oplus F_iU_q^-$ and $P_i$ is the projection onto $\ker e_i'$
along $F_iU_q^-$.

(iii) Every $x\in U_A^-$ has a unique finite expansion
$$x=\sum_{n\ge0}F_i^{(n)}x_n,\qquad e_i'x_n=0,$$
with $x_n=q_i^{n(n-1)/2}P_i(e_i')^nx\in U_A^-$ for every $n$; the sum is finite
because $(e_i')^nx=0$ for large $n$. Consequently the string operators
$\tilde e_i\bigl(\sum_nF_i^{(n)}x_n\bigr):=\sum_{n\ge1}F_i^{(n-1)}x_n$ and
$\tilde f_i\bigl(\sum_nF_i^{(n)}x_n\bigr):=\sum_{n\ge0}F_i^{(n+1)}x_n$
preserve $U_A^-$.

No global $A$-basis and no freeness of $U_A^-$ over $A$ is assumed or asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The negative half $U_q^-=\bigoplus_{\nu\in Q_+}U_q^-[-\nu]$, the maps
$e_i'$, $e_i''$ of [[def-kashiwara-skew-derivations-on-the-negative-quantum-half]],
the divided powers $x^{(m)}=x^m/[m]_i!$, and the $A$-algebra $U_A^-$.

[F1] $e_i'(F_j)=\delta_{ij}$, $e_i'$ is $\mathbb Q(q)$-linear, and $e_i'$
satisfies the twisted Leibniz rule $e_i'(xy)=e_i'(x)y+q_i^{\langle\operatorname{wt}(x),h_i\rangle}xe_i'(y)$
for homogeneous $x$, with the operator identity
$e_i'F_j=q_i^{-a_{ij}}F_je_i'+\delta_{ij}$; in particular
$e_i'(F_iy)=q_i^{-2}F_ie_i'(y)+y$ and $e_i'(F_jy)=q_i^{-a_{ij}}F_je_i'(y)$ for
$j\ne i$ ([[def-kashiwara-skew-derivations-on-the-negative-quantum-half]]).

[F2] $e_i'$ is locally nilpotent: for every homogeneous $x$ of degree $-\nu$ and
every $k>\operatorname{ht}(\nu)$ one has $(e_i')^kx=0$
([[def-kashiwara-skew-derivations-on-the-negative-quantum-half]] (d)).

[F3] Every word in the $F_j$ of degree $-\nu$ has exactly $\nu_i$ letters
$F_i$; $U_A^-$ consists of the finite $A$-linear combinations of the products
$F_{j_1}^{(r_1)}\cdots F_{j_m}^{(r_m)}$ of divided powers, and these are
$\mathbb Q(q)$-linear combinations of words
([[def-lusztig-divided-power-integral-form]],
[[def-positive-negative-and-toral-quantum-subalgebras]]).

[F4] Divided-power multiplication: $F_i^{(n)}F_i^{(m)}=\frac{F_i^{n+m}}{[n]_i![m]_i!}=\binom{n+m}{n}_iF_i^{(n+m)}$,
and the Gaussian coefficients are Laurent polynomials in $q_i$ with integer
coefficients ([[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]],
[[lem-quantum-pascal-recurrence-and-gaussian-integrality]] (iv)).

[F5] Gauss product identity and its inversion: for $N\ge0$,
$\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}(1+q_i^{2j}z)=\sum_{r=0}^Nq_i^{r(N-1)}\binom{N}{r}_iz^r$; hence
$\sum_{r=0}^{N}(-1)^rq_i^{r(N-1)}\binom{N}{r}_i=0$ for $N\ge1$, and applying the
field involution $q_i\mapsto q_i^{-1}$ (which fixes every Gaussian coefficient)
gives $\sum_{r=0}^{N}(-1)^rq_i^{-r(N-1)}\binom{N}{r}_i=0$ for $N\ge1$, while the
sum equals $1$ for $N=0$ ([[lem-quantum-pascal-recurrence-and-gaussian-integrality]] (iii),
[[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]).



## Verification

**Proof technique:** compute $e_i'$ and its powers on divided-power words, use
the resulting explicit q-binomial sum for the projector, and read the
decomposition and the integrality of its coefficients off that computation.

1.1 The deletion rule: for $r\ge1$, $e_i'(F_i^{(r)})=q_i^{-(r-1)}F_i^{(r-1)}$ and $e_i'(F_j^{(r)})=0$ for $j\ne i$. Indeed $e_i'(F_i)=1$ and $e_i'(F_j)=\delta_{ij}$ by [F1]; the operator identity $e_i'(F_i^{(r)})=e_i'(F_i^{(1)}F_i^{(r-1)})=e_i'(F_i^{(1)})F_i^{(r-1)}+q_i^{-2}F_i^{(1)}e_i'(F_i^{(r-1)})$ together with the induction hypothesis gives $(1+q_i^{-r}[r-1]_i)F_i^{(r-1)}=q_i^{-(r-1)}[r]_iF_i^{(r-1)}=q_i^{-(r-1)}F_i^{(r)}/F_i$ step, i.e. $e_i'(F_i^{(r)})=q_i^{-(r-1)}F_i^{(r-1)}$ using $1+q_i^{-r}[r-1]_i=q_i^{-(r-1)}[r]_i$; for $j\ne i$ the same induction gives $e_i'(F_j^{(r)})=q_i^{-a_{ij}}F_je_i'(F_j^{(r-1)})+\delta_{ij}F_j^{(r-1)}=0$, since $a_{ij}\ne0$ terms only multiply by the unit $q_i^{-a_{ij}}$ and the base case $e_i'(F_j)=0$ vanishes. [given, F1, algebra]

2.1 $e_i'$ preserves $U_A^-$: by [F3] it suffices to check a divided-power word $w=F_{j_1}^{(r_1)}\cdots F_{j_m}^{(r_m)}$, by induction on $m$ using the twisted Leibniz rule of [F1] and the deletion rule of step 1.1: $e_i'(w)=e_i'(F_{j_1}^{(r_1)})w'+q_i^{\langle\operatorname{wt}(F_{j_1}^{(r_1)}),h_i\rangle}F_{j_1}^{(r_1)}e_i'(w')$, where $w'$ is the restricted word, the first factor is $0$ or $q_i^{-(r_1-1)}F_i^{(r_1-1)}\in U_A^-$ by step 1.1, the scalar $q_i^{\langle-r_1\alpha_{j_1},h_i\rangle}=q_i^{-r_1a_{ij_1}}\in A$, and $e_i'(w')\in U_A^-$ by induction; products of divided powers lie in $U_A^-$. By [F1] linearity extends this to all of $U_A^-$. [step 1.1, F1, F3, algebra]

2.2 Powers of the derivation on a string: if $u\in\ker e_i'$ then for $0\le n\le m$, $(e_i')^nF_i^{(m)}u=q_i^{-nm+n(n+1)/2}F_i^{(m-n)}u$. For $n=0$ this is clear; for the induction step, $e_i'(F_i^{(m-n)}u)=e_i'(F_i^{(m-n)})u+q_i^{-2(m-n)}F_i^{(m-n)}e_i'(u)=q_i^{-(m-n-1)}F_i^{(m-n-1)}u$, where the second summand vanishes because $e_i'u=0$; multiplying by $q_i^{-nm+n(n+1)/2}$ gives the exponent $-(n+1)m+(n+1)(n+2)/2$ as required. [step 1.1, F1, algebra]

3.1 The projector: $P_i$ is well defined by [F2], and $\mathbb Q(q)$-linear. Consecutive terms cancel in $e_i'P_i$: applying $e_i'$ to the $n$-th term gives $(-1)^nq_i^{-n(n-1)/2}[e_i'(F_i^{(n)})(e_i')^n+q_i^{-2n}F_i^{(n)}(e_i')^{n+1}]=(-1)^nq_i^{-n(n-1)/2}[q_i^{-(n-1)}F_i^{(n-1)}(e_i')^n+q_i^{-2n}F_i^{(n)}(e_i')^{n+1}]$ by steps 1.1, 2.2, and the contribution of the second part of the $n$-th term cancels the first part of the $(n+1)$-st term because $-n(n-1)/2-2n=-n(n+1)/2-(n)$; the $n=0$ contribution of the second part is $(e_i')^1$ and equals the first part of the $n=1$ term, so $e_i'P_i=0$. If $e_i'x=0$ then $(e_i')^nx=0$ for $n\ge1$, so $P_ix=F_i^{(0)}(e_i')^0x=x$: $P_i$ fixes $\ker e_i'$ pointwise. [step 2.2, given, algebra]

3.2 $P_i$ annihilates $F_i\ker e_i'$: for $u\in\ker e_i'$ and $m\ge1$, step 2.2 gives $(e_i')^n(F_i^{(m)}u)=q_i^{-nm+n(n+1)/2}F_i^{(m-n)}u$ for $n\le m$, and $(e_i')^n(F_i^{(m)}u)=q_i^{-n(m-1)}F_i^{(m-1)}(e_i'u)=0$ for $n>m$ (the surviving term of the Leibniz expansion at the first step where the derivative reaches $u$); hence $P_i(F_i^{(m)}u)=\sum_{n=0}^{m}(-1)^nq_i^{-n(n-1)/2}F_i^{(n)}q_i^{-nm+n(n+1)/2}F_i^{(m-n)}u=\sum_{n=0}^{m}(-1)^nq_i^{-nm+n}\binom{m}{n}_iF_i^{(m)}u=\bigl[\sum_{n=0}^{m}(-1)^nq_i^{-n(m-1)}\binom{m}{n}_i\bigr]F_i^{(m)}u$, using [F4] for $F_i^{(n)}F_i^{(m-n)}$. By [F5] the bracket is $1$ for $m=0$ and $0$ for $m\ge1$; hence $P_i$ fixes $\ker e_i'$ and annihilates $F_i^{(m)}\ker e_i'$ for every $m\ge1$. [step 2.2, F4, F5, algebra]

4.1 Decomposition and uniqueness: for $x\in U_q^-$ let $m$ be maximal with $(e_i')^mx\ne0$ (finite by [F2]) or $m=0$ if $e_i'x=0$, and put $x_m:=q_i^{m(m-1)/2}P_i(e_i')^mx$; then $e_i'x_m=q_i^{m(m-1)/2}e_i'P_i(e_i')^mx=0$ by step 3.1, and $x':=x-F_i^{(m)}x_m$ satisfies $(e_i')^mx'=(e_i')^mx-q_i^{-m(m-1)/2}x_m=0$ by step 2.2 and step 3.1, so $\operatorname{ord}(x')\le m-1$. Descending induction on $m$ therefore writes $x=\sum_{n}F_i^{(n)}x_n$ with $e_i'x_n=0$ and $x_n=q_i^{n(n-1)/2}P_i(e_i')^nx$: at each stage the coefficient of the remainder agrees with the claimed formula because $P_i$ annihilates the subtracted strings $F_i^{(m-n)}x_m$ by step 3.2, so $P_i(e_i')^nx'=P_i(e_i')^nx$ for $n<m$. If also $x=\sum_nF_i^{(n)}y_n$ with $e_i'y_n=0$, let $N$ be maximal with $y_N\ne0$; applying $(e_i')^N$ and using step 2.2 gives $0=(e_i')^Nx=q_i^{-N(N-1)/2}y_N$, so all $y_n=0$: the expansion is unique. [step 2.2, step 3.1, step 3.2, F2, algebra]

5.1 Integrality and conclusion: each $x_n=q_i^{n(n-1)/2}P_i(e_i')^nx$ lies in $U_A^-$, because $e_i'$ preserves $U_A^-$ by step 2.1, every $(e_i')^nx$ is therefore in $U_A^-$, the coefficients $q_i^{\pm m(m-1)/2}$ and $(-1)^m$ lie in $A$, and each $F_i^{(m)}$ is a generator of $U_A^-$; hence the finite sum $P_i(\,\cdot\,)$ of the definition lies in $U_A^-$ on $U_A^-$. Consequently every string coefficient $x_n$ of an $x\in U_A^-$ is integral. For the projection claim, apply step 4.1 to the expansion of an arbitrary $y$: $F_iy=\sum_{n\ge1}F_i^{(n)}y_{n-1}$ decomposes with all string coefficients in $\ker e_i'$ and zero $n=0$ coefficient, so $P_i(F_iy)=0$ by the formula; hence $U_q^-=\ker e_i'\oplus F_iU_q^-$ with $P_i$ the projection along $F_iU_q^-$, and $x_0=P_ix\in U_A^-$ whenever $x\in U_A^-$. Finally the string operators of (iii) are well defined by the uniqueness of the expansion, and they preserve $U_A^-$ because they only shift the divided-power labels $n\mapsto n\pm1$ of the integral coefficients $x_n\in U_A^-$. No global $A$-basis and no freeness of $U_A^-$ was used anywhere. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, step 4.1, F2, F3, algebra] ∎
