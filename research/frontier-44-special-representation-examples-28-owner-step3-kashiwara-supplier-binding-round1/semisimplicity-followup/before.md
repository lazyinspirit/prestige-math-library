## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $M$ be a
$U_q(\mathfrak g)$-module over $\mathbb Q(q)$ for a finite symmetrizable
Cartan datum such that

(a) $M$ is a weight module with $\dim_{\mathbb Q(q)}M_\mu<\infty$ for every
$\mu\in P$ ([[def-quantum-weight-module-and-integrability]]);

(b) the weight set $\mathrm{wt}(M)$ is bounded above: it is contained in a
finite union $\bigcup_{j=1}^s(\lambda_j-Q_+)$;

(c) $M$ is integrable, i.e. $E_i$ and $F_i$ act locally nilpotently for every
$i$.

Then $M$ is isomorphic to an algebraic direct sum of simple integrable highest
weight modules $L(\lambda)$, $\lambda\in P^+$
([[thm-integrable-quantum-highest-weight-module-exists-and-is-unique]]);
concretely, every nonzero submodule of $M$ contains a maximal vector $v$ whose
cyclic submodule $U_q(\mathfrak g)v$ is isomorphic to $L(\lambda)$ for some
$\lambda\in P^+$, and $M$ is the direct sum of the submodules generated by the
vectors of any maximal independent family of simple submodules. AC is used
exactly to select a maximal independent family of simple submodules
simultaneously; restricted to any module generated by one finite-dimensional
$U^{\ge0}$-submodule the decomposition is obtained by a finite induction and is
choice-free. The finite-cone modules use the explicit label grading, and the
regularization supplier is used when the original pairing is not separating
([[lem-finite-support-regularization-of-quantum-label-weight-modules]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The module $M$ satisfying (a)-(c), the subalgebra
$U^{\ge0}=U_q^{\ge0}$ generated by $U_q^0$ and the $E_i$
([[def-positive-negative-and-toral-quantum-subalgebras]]), and the rank-one
subalgebras $A_i=\langle E_i,F_i,K_i^{\pm1}\rangle$.

[F1] Rank-one structure: $A_i$ is the standard rank-one algebra; the divided
powers satisfy
$E_iF_i^{(b)}=F_i^{(b)}E_i+\frac{F_i^{(b-1)}}{q_i^b-q_i^{-b}}\sum_{s=0}^{b-1}(q_i^{-2s}K_i-q_i^{2s}K_i^{-1})$,
so $E_iF_i^k=F_i^kE_i+\sum_{t\ge1}c_tF_i^{k-t}K_i^{m_t}$ with coefficients in
$\mathbb Q(q)$; and for a vector $y$ with $E_iy=0$, $K_iy=q_i^Ny$ one has
$E_iF_i^ky=[N-k+1]_iy$-multiples, with $[N-k+1]_i\ne0$ for $k\le N$ and
vanishing exactly at $k=N+1$; also $F_i^{N+1}y=0$ precisely when the string
has length $N+1$ ([[thm-quantized-sl-two-string-formulas]] (i)-(iii),
[[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]).

[F2] For every $x\in M$ there are integers $A,N\ge0$ with $F_i^{A}x=0$ and
$E_i^{N}x=0$ (local nilpotence); weight spaces are finite-dimensional and
weights are bounded above as in (b) ([[def-quantum-weight-module-and-integrability]]).

[F3] Simple integrable highest weight modules: for $\lambda\in P^+$, $L(\lambda)$
is the unique (up to isomorphism) integrable cyclic highest weight module of
highest weight $\lambda$, realized as the Serre quotient of the Verma module and
simple also as an ordinary module; it carries the normalized nondegenerate
contravariant form
([[thm-integrable-quantum-highest-weight-module-exists-and-is-unique]],
[[lem-cartan-projection-constructs-the-quantum-verma-contravariant-form]]).

[F4] The restricted dual $M^*=\bigoplus_\mu\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Q(q)}(M_\mu,\mathbb Q(q))$
is a $U_q(\mathfrak g)$-module under $(u\cdot f)(x):=f(\sigma(u)x)$ for the
linear anti-involution $\sigma$ with $\sigma(K_h)=K_h$, $\sigma(E_i)=q_iF_iK_i^{-1}$,
$\sigma(F_i)=q_i^{-1}K_iE_i$, and the evaluation pairing is invariant; for a
functional supported on finitely many weights the shifted supports remain
finite, so restricted duals and their submodules are honest weight modules with
finite-dimensional weight spaces
([[def-contravariant-form-on-an-integrable-quantum-highest-weight-module]]).

[F5] Every nonzero submodule of an integrable module is integrable with the same
weight bounds, and submodules and quotients of weight modules are weight modules
with finite-dimensional weight spaces and the same upper bounds
([[def-submodule]], [[def-quantum-weight-module-and-integrability]]).

[F6] The regularization supplier replaces the datum by an auxiliary one with
the same labels, preserving gradings, actions and weight dimensions, so all
statements proved for separating labels transport back
([[lem-finite-support-regularization-of-quantum-label-weight-modules]],
[[lem-local-q-one-deformation-proves-integrable-highest-weight-universality]]).

[F7] A module that is the sum of a family of simple submodules that is maximal
with respect to having a direct sum is the algebraic direct sum of that family:
if a simple submodule is not contained in the sum, it intersects it in zero and
enlarges the independent family. The family {L(\lambda):\lambda\in P^+} is a set
and every simple submodule of $M$ is isomorphic to one $L(\lambda)$ by [F3];
AC is invoked exactly here, in the form of a maximal independent family in the
set of simple submodules ([[def-direct-sum-of-a-family-of-modules]],
[[def-axiom-of-choice]]).



## Verification

**Proof technique:** every vector generates a finite-dimensional U^{>=0}-submodule; for such a submodule pick a maximal weight, split off its simple highest weight module by a duality retraction, and iterate the splitting by finite induction; then express the whole module as a sum of simple submodules and use AC only for the maximal independent family.

1.1 Finiteness lemmas. (i) For $x\in M$, the $A_i$-submodule $U_ix$ is finite-dimensional: let $N$ with $E_i^Nx=0$ and $A$ with $F_i^Ax=0$; setting $x_b:=E_i^bx$ for $0\le b\le N$, the finite-dimensional space $V:=\operatorname{span}\{F_i^ax_b:0\le a\le A,\ 0\le b\le N\}$ is stable under $F_i$ (obvious) and under $E_i$: by [F1], $E_iF_i^ax_b=F_i^aE_ix_b+\sum_{t\ge1}c_tF_i^{a-t}K_i^{m_t}x_b=F_i^ax_{b+1}+\sum_{t\ge1}c_t'F_i^{a-t}x_b$ (the torals act diagonally on $x_b$), which lies in $V$; hence $U_ix\subseteq V$ is finite-dimensional. (ii) For $x\in M$, $U^{\ge0}x$ is finite-dimensional: its weights lie in the intersection of $(\mu+Q_+)$ for the finitely many weights $\mu$ of $x$ with the finite union $\bigcup_j(\lambda_j-Q_+)$, a finite set (the simple roots are independent, so the coefficients in $\nu-\mu$ are bounded by those of $\lambda_j-\mu$); since $U^{\ge0}x\subseteq\bigoplus_{\nu}M_\nu$ over these finitely many $\nu$, each finite-dimensional by (a), it is finite-dimensional. (iii) Label bound: every weight $\nu$ of $M$ satisfies $-m_i\le\langle\nu,h_i\rangle\le m_i$ where $m_i:=\max_j\langle\lambda_j,h_i\rangle$: namely for $0\ne z\in M_\nu$ the finite-dimensional $U_iz$ of (i) has a vector $y$ of maximal $h_i$-label $N\le m_i$ with $E_iy=0$; by [F1] the string from $y$ has $F_i^{N+1}y=0$ and $F_i^ky\ne0$ for $k\le N$, and $z\in\operatorname{span}\{F_i^ky:0\le k\le N\}$ has label $N-2k\ge-N\ge-m_i$; the upper bound is (b). [F1, F2, F5, algebra]

1.2 Maximal vectors in finite-dimensional $U^{\ge0}$-submodules. Let $F\subseteq M$ be a nonzero finite-dimensional $U^{\ge0}$-stable submodule, and choose a weight $\mu$ that is maximal in $\mathrm{wt}(F)$; pick $0\ne v_\mu\in F_\mu$. Then $E_iv_\mu=0$ for all $i$, since $E_iv_\mu\in F_{\mu+\alpha_i}$ would contradict maximality; moreover $\mu\in P^+$: for each $i$, $E_iv_\mu=0$ and $F_i$ locally nilpotent force $\langle\mu,h_i\rangle\ge0$, because if $A$ is minimal with $F_i^{A+1}v_\mu=0$ then applying $E_i$ to $F_i^{A+1}v_\mu=0$ and using [F1] gives $[\langle\mu,h_i\rangle-A]_iF_i^Av_\mu=0$ with $F_i^Av_\mu\ne0$, hence $\langle\mu,h_i\rangle=A\ge0$. Therefore $U_q(\mathfrak g)v_\mu$ is an integrable cyclic highest weight module of highest weight $\mu\in P^+$, and by [F3] $L:=U_q(\mathfrak g)v_\mu\cong L(\mu)$ is simple. [F1, F3, F5, algebra]

2.1 The duality retraction. Let $N:=U_q(\mathfrak g)F=U_q^-F$ (because $U^{\ge0}F=F$); its weights lie in $\mathrm{wt}(F)-Q_+\subseteq\mu-Q_+$, so $\mu$ is maximal in $N$, and $N_\mu=F_\mu$. Choose a linear functional $\psi\in N^*$ with $\psi(N_\nu)=0$ for $\nu\ne\mu$ and $\psi(v_\mu)=1$. Then $E_i\cdot\psi=0$: for $x$ of weight $\nu$, $(E_i\cdot\psi)(x)=\psi(q_iF_iK_i^{-1}x)$ has weight $\nu-\alpha_i$, which equals $\mu$ only if $\nu=\mu+\alpha_i$, impossible since $N$ has no weight above $\mu$ by step 1.1(iii) applied in $N$. Let $W:=U_q(\mathfrak g)\psi=U_q^-\psi$. Then $W$ is integrable: for $E_i$, $E_i^N(F\text{-word}\cdot\psi)=(E_i^NF\text{-word})\psi$ and for $N$ larger than the word length the normal form of $E_i^NF\text{-word}$ contains only positive powers of $E_i$ or toral factors, which annihilate $\psi$ (using $E_j\psi=0$ for all $j$); for $F_i$, $(F_i^k\psi)(x)=c\,\psi(E_i^kx)$ vanishes for $k>\langle\mu,h_i\rangle$ because $\mathrm{wt}(E_i^kx)=\mathrm{wt}\,x+k\alpha_i=\mu$ would require the label $\langle\mu,h_i\rangle-2k\ge-\langle\mu,h_i\rangle$ by step 1.1(iii). Hence $W$ is an integrable cyclic highest weight module of weight $\mu\in P^+$, so $W\cong L(\mu)$ by [F3]. The evaluation pairing $N\times W\to\mathbb Q(q)$ is $U_q$-invariant by [F4], giving a $U_q$-module map $\Phi:N\to W^*_{res}$, $\Phi(x)(w)=w(x)$; since $W\cong L(\mu)$ carries its nondegenerate contravariant form [F3], $W^*_{res}\cong W$, and composing with this isomorphism and with a fixed isomorphism $W\cong L$ (rescaled so that the generator corresponds to $v_\mu$) yields a $U_q$-module map $\pi:N\to L$ with $\pi(v_\mu)=v_\mu$; as $v_\mu$ generates $L$ and $L$ is simple, $\pi|_L=\mathrm{id}$, so $\pi$ is a retraction and $N=L\oplus\ker\pi$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, F1, F3, F4, F5, algebra]

3.1 Finite induction. Let $F$ be a nonzero finite-dimensional $U^{\ge0}$-stable submodule and construct $L,\pi$ and $G:=(\mathrm{id}-\pi)(F)$ as in steps 1.2 and 2.1. Then $G$ is finite-dimensional and $U^{\ge0}$-stable ($U^{\ge0}G=(\mathrm{id}-\pi)(U^{\ge0}F)=(\mathrm{id}-\pi)(F)=G$), $U_q(\mathfrak g)G=(\mathrm{id}-\pi)(U_q(\mathfrak g)F)=\ker\pi$, and $\dim G<\dim F$ because $\pi(F)\ni v_\mu\ne0$. By induction on $\dim F$, $U_q(\mathfrak g)G$ is a direct sum of modules $L(\lambda)$, and hence so is $U_q(\mathfrak g)F=L\oplus\ker\pi$. [step 1.2, step 2.1, F3, F5, algebra]

4.1 The general module. Every $x\in M$ lies in $U_q(\mathfrak g)(U^{\ge0}x)$, and $U^{\ge0}x$ is finite-dimensional by step 1.1(ii), so step 3.1 shows that $U_q(\mathfrak g)(U^{\ge0}x)$ is a direct sum of simple modules $L(\lambda)$; hence $M$ is a sum of simple submodules. If a nonzero submodule $V\subseteq M$ is given, choose $0\ne x\in V$: the maximal vector $v_\mu$ of step 1.2 lies in $U^{\ge0}x\subseteq V$ and generates $U_q(\mathfrak g)v_\mu\cong L(\mu)$ inside $V$, proving the concrete clause. Finally, order the nonempty set of independent families of simple submodules of $M$ by inclusion and choose a maximal one, which exists by AC as stated in [F7]; its sum $\bigoplus_\alpha S_\alpha$ is direct, and if some simple submodule $S$ were not contained in it, then $S\cap\bigoplus_\alpha S_\alpha$ is a submodule of the simple module $S$, hence $0$, and adjoining $S$ would produce a strictly larger independent family, a contradiction. Therefore $M=\bigoplus_\alpha S_\alpha$ is an algebraic direct sum of simple modules, each of which is some $L(\lambda)$ by [F3]. The separating-labels bookkeeping is transported by the regularization supplier when needed [F6], and no step used anything but the local nilpotence, the finite-dimensional weight spaces and the upper bounds, so the proof is complete. [step 1.1, step 3.1, F3, F5, F6, F7, choose, algebra] ∎
