## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $M$ be a
$U_q(\mathfrak g)$-module over $\mathbb Q(q)$ for a finite symmetrizable
Cartan datum such that

(a) $M$ is a weight module with $\dim_{\mathbb Q(q)}M_\mu<\infty$ for every
$\mu\in P$ ([[def-quantum-weight-module-and-integrability]]);

(b) the weight set $\mathrm{wt}(M)$ is bounded above: it is contained in a
finite union $\bigcup_{j=1}^s(\lambda_j-Q_+)$;

(c) $M$ is integrable, i.e. $E_i$ and $F_i$ act locally nilpotently for every
$i$.

Then $M$ is isomorphic to an algebraic direct sum of simple integrable highest
weight modules $L(\lambda)$, $\lambda\in P^+$
([[thm-integrable-quantum-highest-weight-module-exists-and-is-unique]]);
concretely, every nonzero submodule of $M$ contains a maximal vector $v$ whose
cyclic submodule $U_q(\mathfrak g)v$ is isomorphic to $L(\lambda)$ for some
$\lambda\in P^+$, and $M$ is the direct sum of the submodules generated by the
vectors of any maximal independent family of simple submodules. AC is used
exactly to select a maximal independent family of simple submodules
simultaneously; restricted to any module generated by one finite-dimensional
$U^{\ge0}$-submodule the decomposition is obtained by a finite induction and is
choice-free. The finite-cone modules use the explicit label grading, and the
regularization supplier is used when the original pairing is not separating
([[lem-finite-support-regularization-of-quantum-label-weight-modules]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The module $M$ satisfying (a)-(c), the subalgebra
$U^{\ge0}=U_q^{\ge0}$ generated by $U_q^0$ and the $E_i$
([[def-positive-negative-and-toral-quantum-subalgebras]]), and the rank-one
subalgebras $A_i=\langle E_i,F_i,K_i^{\pm1}\rangle$.

[F1] Rank-one strings. For a highest vector $y$ of integral rank-one weight $a$, $E_iF_i^{(n)}y=[a-n+1]_iF_i^{(n-1)}y$; local nilpotence of $F_i$ forces $a\ge0$, and the string has the nonzero positions $0\le n\le a$, with distinct $K_i$-eigenvalues. Every homogeneous vector of an integrable chosen-label module has a unique finite admissible expansion into these strings ([[thm-quantized-sl-two-string-formulas]], [[thm-unique-kashiwara-i-string-decomposition]], [[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]).

[F2] For every $x\in M$ there are integers $A,N\ge0$ with $F_i^{A}x=0$ and
$E_i^{N}x=0$ (local nilpotence); weight spaces are finite-dimensional and
weights are bounded above as in (b) ([[def-quantum-weight-module-and-integrability]]).

[F3] Simple integrable highest weight modules: for $\lambda\in P^+$, $L(\lambda)$
is the unique (up to isomorphism) integrable cyclic highest weight module of
highest weight $\lambda$, realized as the Serre quotient of the Verma module and
simple also as an ordinary module; it carries the normalized nondegenerate
contravariant form
([[thm-integrable-quantum-highest-weight-module-exists-and-is-unique]],
[[lem-cartan-projection-constructs-the-quantum-verma-contravariant-form]]).

[F4] The restricted dual $M^*=\bigoplus_\mu\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Q(q)}(M_\mu,\mathbb Q(q))$
is a $U_q(\mathfrak g)$-module under $(u\cdot f)(x):=f(\sigma(u)x)$ for the
linear anti-involution $\sigma$ with $\sigma(K_h)=K_h$, $\sigma(E_i)=q_iF_iK_i^{-1}$,
$\sigma(F_i)=q_i^{-1}K_iE_i$, and the evaluation pairing is invariant; for a
functional supported on finitely many weights the shifted supports remain
finite, so restricted duals and their submodules are honest weight modules with
finite-dimensional weight spaces
([[def-contravariant-form-on-an-integrable-quantum-highest-weight-module]]).

[F5] Local nilpotence is inherited by every ordinary submodule. Graded submodules and graded quotients inherit the chosen label grading, finite-dimensional label spaces and the finite upper-cone bound. Taking the label components of a finite-dimensional subspace gives a finite-dimensional graded hull; if the original subspace is $U^{\ge0}$-stable, its graded hull is too, since $E_i$ shifts labels and the torus acts diagonally ([[def-submodule]], [[def-quantum-weight-module-and-integrability]]).

[F6] The regularization supplier replaces the datum by an auxiliary one with
the same labels, preserving gradings, actions and weight dimensions, so all
statements proved for separating labels transport back
([[lem-finite-support-regularization-of-quantum-label-weight-modules]],
[[lem-local-q-one-deformation-proves-integrable-highest-weight-universality]]).

[F7] A module that is the sum of a family of simple submodules that is maximal
with respect to having a direct sum is the algebraic direct sum of that family:
if a simple submodule is not contained in the sum, it intersects it in zero and
enlarges the independent family. The family {L(\lambda):\lambda\in P^+} is a set
and every simple submodule of $M$ is isomorphic to one $L(\lambda)$ by [F3];
AC is invoked exactly here, in the form of a maximal independent family in the
set of simple submodules ([[def-direct-sum-of-a-family-of-modules]],
[[def-axiom-of-choice]]).



## Verification

**Proof technique:** finite upper-cone support makes each positive cyclic span finite. The rank-one string decomposition bounds each fixed simple-root line, proving integrability of the restricted dual. A maximal label in a finite graded generating space gives a duality retraction; induction splits its finite generated module, and AC selects a maximal independent family only at the final algebraic direct-sum step.

1.1 Finiteness and the restricted dual. (i) For homogeneous $x$, the finite string expansion of [F1] places $x$ in the sum of finitely many finite-dimensional simple $A_i$-strings, so $A_ix$ is finite-dimensional; a general vector has finite label support, giving the same conclusion. (ii) The labels of $U^{\ge0}x$ lie above one of the finitely many labels of $x$ and below one of the finitely many upper labels $\lambda_j$. Each interval contains finitely many labels because the simple roots freely generate $Q$, and each label space is finite-dimensional, so $U^{\ge0}x$ is finite-dimensional. (iii) Fix a label $\mu$ and $i$. Choose an integer $T\ge0$ so that $M_{\mu+t\alpha_i}=0$ for all integers $t>T$: each possible upper-cone expression $\lambda_j-\mu-t\alpha_i\in Q_+$ bounds $t$ by its $\alpha_i$ coefficient. Put $a:=\langle\mu,h_i\rangle$. Any nonzero string position on this line has a top label $\mu+t\alpha_i$ with $t\le T$, highest rank-one integer $a+2t\ge0$ and position $0\le n\le a+2t$. Its label is $\mu+(t-n)\alpha_i$, with $-a-T\le t-n\le T$. By [F1] every vector on the line is a sum of such positions, so the entire support on the line is finite. Under [F4] an $E_i$-action on a functional supported at $\mu$ shifts its support to $\mu+\alpha_i$, and an $F_i$-action shifts it to $\mu-\alpha_i$; the finite line bounds therefore kill both sufficiently high powers. Taking finite maxima over a functional's finite label support proves that $M^*$ is integrable. The same argument applies to any graded submodule with the same support bound. No global bound on all coroot values is used. [F1, F2, F4, F5, algebra]

1.2 Maximal vectors. Let $F\subseteq M$ be a nonzero finite-dimensional graded $U^{\ge0}$-stable subspace and choose a maximal label $\mu$ in its finite support. Pick $0\ne v_\mu\in F_\mu$. Since $F_{\mu+\alpha_i}=0$ for each $i$, every $E_i$ kills $v_\mu$. By [F1], its finite $i$-string has highest integer $\langle\mu,h_i\rangle\ge0$, so $\mu\in P^+$. The cyclic module $L:=U_qv_\mu$ is integrable and isomorphic to the ordinary-simple $L(\mu)$ by [F3]. [F1, F3, F5, algebra]

2.1 The duality retraction. Put $N:=U_qF=U_q^-F$. Its support lies in the finite union $\bigcup_{\nu\in\mathrm{wt}(F)}(\nu-Q_+)$; maximality of $\mu$ implies $N_{\mu+\alpha_i}=0$ for each $i$ and $N_\mu=F_\mu$, without requiring the other maximal labels to lie below $\mu$. Choose $\psi\in(N_\mu)^*$ with $\psi(v_\mu)=1$ and extend it by zero on the other labels. Since $\sigma(E_i)$ lowers a label by $\alpha_i$, $E_i\psi=0$. By step 1.1(iii), the whole restricted dual $N^*$ is integrable, so $W:=U_q\psi$ is the integrable highest module $L(\mu)$ by [F3]. Evaluation gives a grading-preserving module map $\Phi:N\to W^*_{\mathrm{res}}$, with $\Phi(x)(w)=w(x)$: a finite-label vector $x$ defines a finite-label functional, and invariance follows from [F4]. The homogeneous nondegenerate contravariant form on $W$ identifies $W$ with its restricted dual on each finite-dimensional label space. Choose the unique normalized highest-vector isomorphism $W\cong L$ and rescale the composite so that $\pi(v_\mu)=v_\mu$; this is possible since evaluation on $(v_\mu,\psi)$ is $1$. The grading-preserving module map $\pi:N\to L$ restricts to the identity on the cyclic simple $L$, hence $N=L\oplus\ker\pi$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, F3, F4, F5, algebra]

3.1 Finite induction. For the graded $F$ of steps 1.2 and 2.1 put $G:=(\mathrm{id}-\pi)F$. The grading-preserving retraction makes $G$ graded, finite-dimensional and $U^{\ge0}$-stable, and $U_qG=\ker\pi$. Since $v_\mu\in F\cap L$ is killed by $\mathrm{id}-\pi$, $\dim G<\dim F$. Induction on $\dim F$ therefore decomposes $U_qF$ as a finite direct sum of simple highest modules. This also covers an arbitrary finite-dimensional $U^{\ge0}$-stable generating space $F_0$: its finite graded hull $F$ is stable by [F5], and $U_qF_0$ is an ordinary submodule of the finite direct sum $U_qF$. Every submodule of a finite direct sum of ordinary simple modules is itself a finite direct sum of simples: induct on the number of summands, splitting off the first summand if it is contained in the submodule, and otherwise inject the submodule into the remaining summands by projection. Each resulting simple is isomorphic to a summand by a nonzero coordinate projection. This argument uses only finite choices. [step 1.2, step 2.1, F3, F5, algebra]

4.1 The general module. For $x\in M$, the finite graded hull of $U^{\ge0}x$ is finite and stable by step 1.1(ii) and [F5], so step 3.1 places $x$ in a finite sum of ordinary-simple highest modules. Hence $M$ is a sum of simple submodules. By AC choose a maximal independent family in the set of simple submodules; an ordinary simple not contained in its sum has zero intersection with that sum and would enlarge the family, so maximality gives $M=\bigoplus_\alpha S_\alpha$. Every $S_\alpha$ is isomorphic to some $L(\lambda)$ by a nonzero coordinate projection to one of the finite highest-module sums containing a nonzero vector of $S_\alpha$. For a nonzero ordinary submodule $V\subseteq M$, choose $0\ne x\in V$ and a finite simple sum containing $x$. Its intersection with $V$ is a nonzero submodule of that finite sum, so step 3.1 gives a simple highest-module copy inside $V$; the image of its highest vector is a maximal vector whose cyclic submodule is that copy. This proves the concrete clause as an ordinary-module assertion, without assuming an ordinary submodule respects the chosen label grading. The original finite label grading and [F6] retain the datum convention throughout. AC occurs only at the maximal-family step; all finite generated cases were choice-free. [step 1.1, step 3.1, F3, F5, F6, F7, choose, algebra] ∎
