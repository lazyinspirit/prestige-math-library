The theorem states pointwise fixed-data well-posedness on each closed bounded data ball in separable Banach spaces. Its assumptions include a Borel full-measure forward domain, positive prior mass on a bounded subset, joint continuity, a common data-ball lower envelope, a data-Lipschitz potential bound, and the exact weighted integrability `exp(M1)(1+M2^2) in L1(mu0)`. The proof first invokes the actual batch-16 `thm-ip-function-space-posterior-propriety` supplier for pointwise propriety, then obtains a uniform lower normalizer bound from one support-point neighborhood and the data-Lipschitz envelope. It bounds both square-root likelihood and normalizer differences by the data increment and normalizes in root-L2, giving the stated constant. The Hellinger proof is choice-free; only the final optional TV comparison assumes AC.

Direct in-run dependencies are the level-0 support lemma and `thm-ip-function-space-posterior-propriety` (actual current body, batch 16 item level 3, with a hash-stale Step-1 record; the current body and complete proof were inspected directly), so this theorem is level 4. Their statement interfaces and the IP-16 proof's support convention were checked. Sources: Dashti–Stuart §4.1, Theorems 4.3 and 4.5, PDF pp. 36–39; Latz §3.1 Example 3.1 and §3.4 Lemma 3.7 plus complete proof. ## Item checkpoint: `thm-ip-posterior-potential-discretization-error`

## Item checkpoint: `cex-ip-nonintegrable-potential-error-envelope`

The counterexample uses $X=\mathbb R$, $D=\mathbb N_{\ge1}$, prior masses $c/n^2$, base potential $0$, and discretized potentials $n/N$. The shared lower envelope is zero and the potential error is exactly $M_2(|n|)\psi_N$ with $M_2(s)=s$ and $\psi_N=1/N$, while the weighted envelope diverges because its $M_2^2$ term has constant mass at each integer. The evidence $Z_N=\sum_n(c/n^2)e^{-n/N}$ tends to one by an explicit finite-head/tail argument. On the block $N\le n<2N$, the normalized likelihood ratio stays uniformly below one, so root-L2 Hellinger distance is bounded below by a positive constant times $N^{-1/2}$; this is not $O(N^{-1})$. Direct in-run dependency: the level-4 potential-discretization theorem, so this counterexample is level 5. Its finite potentials are continuous on the discrete subspace and all normalizers are positive finite. Sources: Dashti–Stuart §4.2 Theorem 4.9, and Cotter–Dashti–Stuart §2 Theorems 2.4 and 2.6 with full proofs. Placement is the B page. Its strict proof contract, source-fetch stamps, scoped checks, and ready Step-1 record are complete.
