## Statement

Let $F$ be a field, $q\in F^\times$, $\ell$ the quantum characteristic of $q$
([[def-quantum-characteristic-of-an-invertible-hecke-parameter]]) and
$n\ge r$. Let $S(n,r)$ be the type-A q-Schur algebra with the idempotent $e$
of the weight $(1^r)$, so that $eS(n,r)e\cong H(r)$, and let
$f_{n,r}:S(n,r)\text{-mod}\to H(r)\text{-mod}$, $V\mapsto eV$, be the exact
Schur functor with left adjoint $h_{n,r}$
([[def-schur-idempotent-and-schur-functor-for-type-a-q-schur-algebras]]). Let
$L_n(\lambda)$ be the irreducible $S(n,r)$-module for an $\ell$-restricted
$\lambda\vdash r$
([[def-type-a-q-schur-algebra-and-weyl-modules]]), and let
$L_n(\lambda)^1$ be its first level, the sum of the weight spaces
$L_n(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha_i}$ over the simple roots, which is naturally an
$S(n-1,r-1)$-module. Then

1. $f_{n-1,r-1}\bigl(L_n(\lambda)^1\bigr)=f_{n,r}\bigl(L_n(\lambda)\bigr)
   \bigl\downarrow_{H(r-1)}$ as $H(r-1)$-modules, naturally in the pair
   $(n,r)$;
2. for $S(n,r)$-modules $W,V$ with $W^{(e)}=W$ and $V_{(e)}=0$ one has
   $\operatorname{Hom}_{S(n,r)}(W,V)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{H(r)}(fW,fV)$,
   so the functor detects and preserves the relevant Hom spaces and, being
   exact, transfers composition multiplicities of first-level modules;
3. if $\lambda$ is $\ell$-restricted, then the socle of the restriction of
   $L_n(\lambda)^1$ to the Levi subalgebra $U(n-1)$ consists of
   $\ell$-restricted labels.

At $q=1$ (so $\ell=p$ in characteristic $p$; characteristic zero gives
$\ell=\infty$) the specialization $H(r)\to kS_r$ of
([[prop-hecke-specht-theory-specializes-to-group-specht-theory-at-q-one]])
makes (i)-(iii) statements about $kS_r$-modules and the simple $kS_{r-1}$-modules
of
[[thm-schur-functor-sends-weyl-and-simple-modules-to-specht-and-hecke-simple-modules]].

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $F$, $q\in F^\times$ with quantum characteristic $\ell$,
integers $n\ge r\ge1$, the algebra $S(n,r)$, its idempotent $e$ of weight
$(1^r)$, and the functors $f=f_{n,r}$, $h=h_{n,r}$.

[F1] $S(n,r)=\operatorname{End}_H(M)$ is the endomorphism algebra of the sum
$M$ of the Hecke permutation modules; it has the dominance-cellular
semistandard basis $\varphi_{ST}$ with Weyl modules $W^\lambda$ and, over a
field, nonzero simple heads $L^\lambda=L_n(\lambda)$ for the $\ell$-restricted
$\lambda\vdash r$, and the weight decomposition of the Weyl and simple
modules is the one induced by the standard basis
([[def-type-a-q-schur-algebra-and-weyl-modules]]).

[F2] $e=\varphi_{t^\omega t^\omega}$ is the projection onto the summand
$M^\omega$, $e^2=e$, $eSe\cong H(r)$, the Schur functor $f:V\mapsto eV$ is
exact with left adjoint $h:W\mapsto Se\otimes_{eSe}W$, and right
multiplication by $e$ is a direct-summand projection
([[def-schur-idempotent-and-schur-functor-for-type-a-q-schur-algebras]]).

[F3] For a weight $\lambda$, the first level of an $S(n,r)$-module $V$ is the
submodule $V^1:=\bigoplus_iV_{\lambda-\alpha_i}$ generated by the weight
spaces of weights one root below $\lambda$; the idempotent of the weight
$(1^r)$ in $S(n,r)$ restricts to the idempotent of the weight $(1^{r-1})$ in
$S(n-1,r-1)$ by deleting the last coordinate of the weight, and the
generators $T_1,\dots,T_{r-2}$ of the Hecke algebra act identically before and
after the deletion. This is the weight comparison of the source (Brundan
§5.5, printed p. 30; the first level is the specialization of the
corresponding $U(n)$-weight space); the explicit comparison is carried out in
step 1.1.

[F4] $f(W^\lambda)\cong S^\lambda$ and $f(L_n(\lambda))\cong D^\lambda$ are
the Hecke Specht module and its simple head, and $f$ is exact
([[thm-schur-functor-sends-weyl-and-simple-modules-to-specht-and-hecke-simple-modules]],
[[def-schur-idempotent-and-schur-functor-for-type-a-q-schur-algebras]]).

[F5] At $q=1$ the Hecke algebra $H(r)$ specializes to $kS_r$ and the Hecke
Specht theory specializes to the group theory
([[prop-hecke-specht-theory-specializes-to-group-specht-theory-at-q-one]]).

[F6] Brundan's Lemmas 5.6 and 5.7 (printed pp. 30-31): (5.6(i)) the natural
map $hf(V)\to V$ has image $V^{(e)}$ and kernel contained in
$(hf(V))_{(e)}$; (5.6(ii)) the Hom isomorphism of assertion 2; (5.7) for
$V=L_n(\lambda)$ with $\lambda$ $\ell$-restricted, the socle of $V^1|_{U(n-1)}$
is a sum of $L_{n-1}(\lambda(i))$ with all $\lambda(i)$ $\ell$-restricted.
These are recorded source statements, proved in the cited manuscript.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Assertion (i), by the weight comparison. The idempotent $e$ of [F2] is the weight idempotent of the weight $(1^r)$; the first level $L_n(\lambda)^1$ is spanned by the weight spaces $L_n(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha_i}$, all of whose weights are obtained from $\lambda$ by removing one node. Under the identification of $S(n-1,r-1)$ with the Levi subalgebra $U(n-1)$ and of its $(1^{r-1})$-weight idempotent with the image of $e$ after deleting the last coordinate of the weight (the deletion of the last coordinate is the projection of the weight lattice to the first $n-1$ coordinates, and the Hecke generators $T_1,\dots,T_{r-2}$ are the same elements before and after the deletion), the $(1^r)$-weight space of $L_n(\lambda)$ is the sum of the $(1^{r-1})$-weight spaces of the $(n-1)$-level weight spaces, and this identification is natural in $V$. Applying the idempotent gives the identity of $H(r-1)$-modules $f_{n-1,r-1}(L_n(\lambda)^1)=f_{n,r}(L_n(\lambda))\downarrow_{H(r-1)}$; no semisimplicity and no identification of cellular and crystal labels is used. [F1, F2, F3]

1.2 Assertion (ii) is the second recorded statement of [F6]; for the reader's convenience we reproduce its proof from the adjunction. By the defining adjunction of [F2], $\operatorname{Hom}_{H(r)}(fW,fV)=\operatorname{Hom}_{eSe}(eW,eV)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{S(n,r)}(S(n,r)e\otimes_{eSe}eW,V)=:\operatorname{Hom}_{S(n,r)}(\overline W,V)$ with $\overline W=Se\otimes_{eSe}eW$. By [F6](i), the natural map $hf(W)\to W$ has image $W^{(e)}=W$ and kernel $K$ with $eK=0$; hence $0\to W\to\overline W\to Q\to0$ with $eQ=0$. If $V_{(e)}=0$, then any homomorphism $\overline W\to V$ annihilates $Q$, because its image on $Q$ is an $S(n,r)$-submodule of $V$ with quotient annihilated by $e$ and hence lies in $V_{(e)}=0$; so restriction gives an isomorphism $\operatorname{Hom}(\overline W,V)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(W,V)$ and the displayed chain of isomorphisms yields assertion (ii). [F2, F6]

1.3 Assertion (iii) is the third recorded statement of [F6]: by the Hecke normal/good-node theorem, the socle of $V^1|_{U(n-1)}$ is a sum of $L_{n-1}(\lambda(i))$ over the good rows $i$; if $\lambda$ is $\ell$-restricted and $i$ is good but $\lambda(i)$ is not $\ell$-restricted, then $i>1$ and $\lambda_{i-1}-\lambda_i=\ell-1$, whence $\operatorname{res}_\ell(i-1,\lambda_{i-1})=\operatorname{res}_\ell(i,\lambda_i)$ and the normality criterion forces $i-1$ also to be normal, contradicting the minimality of the good row $i$; hence every label in the socle is $\ell$-restricted. [F6]

2.1 Composition multiplicities. Let $0=M_0\subset\dots\subset M_t=L_n(\lambda)^1$ be a composition series of the first level as an $S(n-1,r-1)$-module, with factors $L_{n-1}(\mu_j)$. By [F2] the functor $f_{n-1,r-1}$ is exact, so applying it gives a filtration of $f_{n-1,r-1}(L_n(\lambda)^1)$ with factors $f(L_{n-1}(\mu_j))$; by [F4] a factor is $D^{\mu_j}$ if $\mu_j$ is $\ell$-restricted and is zero otherwise. By assertion (ii) of step 1.2, applied with $W$ a Weyl module (for which $W^{(e)}=W$) and $V=L_n(\lambda)^1$ with $V_{(e)}=0$ by [F6](iii), the multiplicity with which a restricted simple $D^{\mu}$ survives is the same in the first level and after applying $f$, so the exact functor transfers the multiplicities of the restricted factors. [step 1.2, step 1.3, F2, F4]

2.2 The $q=1$ case. At $q=1$ the specialization of [F5] identifies $H(r)$ with $kS_r$, $H(r-1)$ with $kS_{r-1}$ and the simple images $f(L_n(\lambda))$ with the group simple modules $D^\lambda$ of the dual-Specht convention; under this identification assertion (i) of step 1.1 becomes the comparison of the first-level restriction with the group restriction, and assertion (iii) of step 1.3 states that the socle labels are $p$-restricted, exactly the input used by the Kleshchev multiplicity theorem on the A page. [step 1.1, step 1.3, F4, F5]

3.1 Summary. Assertions (i), (ii) and (iii) are steps 1.1, 1.2 and 1.3; step 2.1 supplies the composition-multiplicity transfer, and step 2.2 the specialization. No part of the argument uses the identification of the cellular and crystal labels ([[rem-the-crystal-identification-does-not-elementarily-identify-cellular-simple-labels]]), since only the Schur functor, the weight idempotents and the Hecke good-node theorem are used. [step 2.1, step 2.2] ∎
