## 2. Every first-level primitive weight is terminal, with dimension at most one

Fix mu_N=0 and let l=N−1. Let M be the whole last-coordinate-one subspace of L_N(mu), a module for the first l coordinates. A vector is primitive here when all nonconstant coefficient operators of the upper Levi unipotent group kill it. This full group/coefficient definition is essential in characteristic p.

Write the upper unipotent group as V U_l^+, where V consists of matrices with arbitrary last column above its final 1 and otherwise identity. The product map is an explicit polynomial factorization: U_l^+ is the upper left l-by-l block, and the last column specifies V. Thus any positive coefficient test of a primitive vector acts only through V, since every nonconstant coefficient from the right factor U_l^+ kills it. To raise last coordinate 1 to highest coordinate 0, a V monomial must have total degree exactly 1; its only operators are E_(r,N), 1≤r≤l. Positive detection therefore gives E_(r,N)x=cv with c≠0 for some r. Its weight forces wt(x)=mu−epsilon_r+epsilon_N=mu−beta_r, beta_r=alpha_r+...+alpha_l. On the primitive space of that weight, x↦E_(r,N)x is injective: a vector in its kernel has every possible positive highest test zero and hence is zero. The target is the one-dimensional highest space. Thus every primitive weight, even starting from an arbitrary first-level deficit, is terminal, and each such primitive space has dimension at most one. This is not the earlier binary-near-weight support exclusion for composition factors.

Put a_j=mu_j−mu_(j+1), B(r,s)=s−r+sum_(j=r)^s a_j and C(r,s)=s−r+sum_(j=r)^(s−1)a_j. Every nonempty canonical Q(I) annihilates a primitive vector. Indeed with b=min I its last ascending-prefix letter e_(b−1) commutes past all remaining ascending letters (their indices exceed b), giving a rightmost Levi letter e_(b−1), where b−1<l. The other letters remain to its left. Thus Q(I)x=0 for I≠empty.

Expand a nonzero primitive x of weight mu−beta_r in the accepted essential T basis. Choose the largest hat among its nonzero coefficients. The triangular pivot at that hat isolates a nonzero term. If that hat were nonempty, the preceding paragraph would make its Q test zero, a contradiction. Hence the largest hat is empty, and distinctness of essential hats leaves exactly one essential set whose hat is empty. Empty is linked to J precisely when J is regular; the accepted essential/regular matching argument therefore makes r normal. This proves necessity without a source radical-generation theorem.

Conversely suppose r normal. Let R_r={s in(r,l]:B(r,s)=0}. Choose its compensation injection f with r<f(s)≤s and B(f(s),s)=0. Let D=f(R_r). The inverse matching on D has image R_r. It is regular, so its hat is empty. The matched norm gives Xi_(r,l)(D)=product_(s in[r,l] minus R_r) B(r,s)≠0: B(r,r)=a_r≠0, and R_r removes exactly every other zero factor. Thus T_(r,l)(D)v is nonzero; the accepted maximal-hat property gives h(I,D)=0 for every nonempty I.

We check full primitivity, not only its highest tests. For r≤j<l, apply positive detection to e_j T(D)v. Every possible raising test has root grade beta_r−alpha_j, with coefficients0or1; concatenate its simple-letter expansion with the rightmost e_j. Every resulting full word has a nonempty canonical Q index, since e_j acts before e_(j+1), unlike the empty-Q order. Its highest coefficient is zero. Therefore e_j T(D)v=0. For j<r the target weight is not at most mu, so its weight space is zero and e_j also kills the vector. A nonconstant positive Levi coefficient of total weight not bounded by beta_r likewise lands outside the allowed weights. For a total weight bounded by beta_r, each root-variable exponent is at most1, so its coefficient is a product of ordinary positive root matrices; these are iterated commutators of the simple e_j, and the rightmost root already kills the vector. Thus every nonconstant upper Levi coefficient kills it. We have proved that the primitive space at mu−beta_r is one-dimensional iff r is normal, and zero otherwise. Write v_r=T_(r,l)(D)v for any such nonzero choice.

## 3. An integral centre separates the primitive labels, including p=2

In tensor degree m for rank l, let H_a=E_aa. Define the diagonal operator choose2(H_a) on a tensorword of weight eta to multiply it by the integer binomial(eta_a,2), reduced in F. It is also the sum over distinct unordered tensor positions x<y of E_aa^(x)E_aa^(y). Its definition contains no division by 2 in F.

Let Z_m be the action on tensorwords of the group-algebra central transposition sum z_m=sum_(x<y)(x,y). On each pair of positions, the swap operator is sum_(a,b)E_ab^(x)E_ba^(y). Separate a=b and pair a<b to obtain the exact integer matrix identity
 Z_m=sum_a choose2(H_a)+sum_(a<b)(E_ab E_ba−H_a).
The equal-position part of E_ab E_ba is H_a, which is subtracted; the other terms are exactly the two swaps of unequal labels. Because z_m commutes with the position-permutation action and with every Schur endomorphism, Z_m belongs to the Schur algebra and is central in it. This proof is integral, including characteristic2.

On a primitive vector of dominant weight eta, E_ab kills it for a<b, so E_ab E_ba acts by H_a−H_b. The displayed identity therefore has scalar

Here choose2 is the actual diagonal weight projection operator, not the invalid expression H_a(H_a−1)/2 in characteristic2. A cyclic module generated by that vector has this same central scalar throughout.

## 4. Compensated splitting gives the containment and the simple cyclic modules

Suppose t<r are both normal and C(t,r)=0. For r choose a compensation image D for R_r as above. For every w≥r, B(t,w)=B(r,w). Also B(t,r)=a_r≠0.  =v_t−T_(t,l)((E minus{e}) union{r} union D)v

## 8. True signature inversion and the cogood endpoints

For restricted partitions, adding or removing a corner changes the i-boundary word at that corner from + to − or vice versa; the other boundary statuses that can change have residues i±1 and hence are not i (p≥2). The zero-suppressed reduced word is +^a−^b. By the stack algorithm, flipping its last surviving + produces the first surviving −, and flipping its first surviving − produces the last surviving +: before that position the minus stack is empty; after it, the distinguished minus survives exactly when every suffix prefix has at least as many minus pushes as plus pops, equivalently no later plus survives when the distinguished symbol is flipped to plus. This verifies the labelled-position inverse, not only sign counts.

Good removal preserves restrictedness by the earlier same-residue argument in Section4. Cogood addition preserves it as well: its only possible new forbidden difference would have a_r=p−1. Then the addable corner in row r+1 has the same residue as the selected corner, with no i-node between them. These are consecutive pluses in the i-word. If the earlier plus survives, the later one survives too, so the earlier cannot be cogood. This contradiction rules out the forbidden difference. The geometric word comparison can consequently be applied in both restricted directions. Thus
 B is cogood for mu iff B is good for nu=mu+B,
 and good deletion and cogood addition are mutual inverses, without epsilon_i(nu)=phi_i(mu).

Apply Section7 to N=D(eta), X=D(mu), for every restricted eta of degree d+1. Section6 says the right-hand Hom space is one-dimensional precisely when eta−good_i(eta)=mu, and zero otherwise. Section8's inverse proves that exactly one eta has this property, namely nu=mu+B. Every group simple is one of these absolutely irreducible labelled modules. The socle of F_iD(mu) is therefore a single D(nu), occurring once: the dimensions of all simple-to-module Hom spaces give its isotypic socle multiplicities. This establishes the identification and simplicity directly, including exclusion of every other simple label.

Finally F_iD(mu) is self-dual. Give Ind_H^G D(mu) the invariant nondegenerate coset form beta(r tensor x,s tensor y)=delta_(r,s)beta_mu(x,y), on a finite left transversal; invariance follows when a group element permutes cosets and applies H elements to the fibers. The projection R_e is self-adjoint. Indeed beta(g tensor x,h tensor y)=beta_mu(x,E_H(g^(-1)h)y), and E_H(e Z)=E_H(Z e) for an H-commuting e by the trace calculation above; inverse fixes e. Moving R_e to the other argument gives the same form. Hence its image is orthogonal to its complement and its restricted form is nondegenerate. The head is the dual of its simple socle and is D(nu), using the nondegenerate invariant quotient form on D(nu). This proves both cogood endpoints over every field, including characteristic2.
