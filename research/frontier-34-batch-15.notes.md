## Design comparison and mathematical decisions

- The design's broad “DT-2--DT-5; DG; AT where cited” requirement is not fully realized by the current plan: the CW gluing supplier identified below is outside its requires closure. The current plan remains authoritative and unchanged; this conflict blocks the consumer.
- Interpret the design's cocore-collapse shorthand as a finite CW **homotopy model of a pair** with one cell per handle. It is not an assertion that independently collapsing all cocores defines a global deformation retraction. Moved this lemma after rearrangement and added cellular approximation and gluing support. The closed-manifold construction starts with an empty base and supplies the finite model of M0 before the relative induction, avoiding circular use of a boundary CW model.
- A normalized adapted function has exact endpoint inverse images and linear collar germs. Duality uses 1-f, which has the same derivative as -f and preserves the [0,1] convention. Reverse W's orientation in the oriented reversed triad. No orientability assumption is imposed in the unoriented results.
- Self-indexing levels are (k+1)/(n+2), strictly inside (0,1), retaining the critical points and indices. Equal-index groups can share a value; excellence is a separate condition. Nicolaescu Lemma 2.4.12's printed target interval [0,1] cannot literally permit endpoint critical values with fixed boundary data: the proposal uses strictly interior targets.
- The rearrangement lemma spells out a compact isolating regular slab, only two critical groups, and disjoint closed critical trajectory sets. The design's abbreviated “no trajectory” condition needs that context. Arbitrary unequal indices alone do not license rearrangement. Prove adjacent inverted-index separation using framed level spheres of dimensions n-k-1 and l-1, whose sum is less than n-1 when l<=k. Then realize the isotopy in a regular band and sort the finite index word. This is Milnor's local proof route, not an invocation of a stronger unproved relative Morse–Smale result.
- Published relative Morse–Smale transversality is stated for closed manifolds. It does not directly supply the boundary-relative field perturbation needed here. The proposal therefore explicitly proves the finite regular-band perturbation instead. Flow use is confined to compact bands and boundary hitting; completeness on a manifold with boundary is not asserted. Ascending normalization is X/df(X); multiply by the negative function df(X) when returning to a downward field.
- The converse handle-to-function direction has its own framed trace construction and rounding comparison. It is not inferred from the forward attachment theorem.
- Endpoint elimination retains connected W and the named nonempty connected boundary. The spanning-tree argument uses a directly proved disk-and-collar absorption lemma, never the later general cancellation theorem at order 529. The upper-end theorem is a separate dual existence statement, not an unproved simultaneous normalization claim.
- The connecting-orbit counterexample fixes the field. A convention audit found that the unscaled round gradient has the wrong local linearization for the library's exact gradient-like definition. It was replaced by a positive rescaling, 8/(1+z) near north and 8/(1-z) near south, yielding exactly +2u/-2v in Morse coordinates. The meridian orbits persist; g=f is a witness that the admissible class is nonempty. This preserves the intended obstruction without a vacuous hypothesis.
- Empty boundary faces, endpoint handle indices, dimension zero, the cylinder, genus-g band presentation, and independent attachments are retained. Full Cerf theory and general cancellation remain assigned to their later destinations, not used as suppliers.
