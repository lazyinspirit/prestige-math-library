## Conventions and existing-result agreements

- The authoritative existing definition is `def-absolutely-continuous-function`: for every `ε>0` there is `δ>0` such that every finite family `[u_j,v_j]` with pairwise disjoint open interiors and total length below `δ` has total endpoint increment below `ε`; on a degenerate interval every function is AC.  `rem-absolute-continuity-conventions-and-hierarchy-agreement` records this exact convention and cites, rather than re-proves, `thm-c1-lipschitz-ac-bv-hierarchy`.
- `thm-first-fundamental-theorem-of-calculus-for-l-one` already proves the a.e.-derivative portion for the `L^1` indefinite integral.  The new corollary supplies only its AC portion, so the sharp FTC theorem combines rather than duplicates them.  It will discharge the statement-only `rem-ftc-absolutely-continuous`.
- The new Banach--Zarecki theorem will discharge statement-only `rem-banach-zarecki`.  Its hard direction has the required growth-estimate intermediate lemma and uses property (N) for **images** of null sets, never preimages.
- The one HK/Lebesgue comparison item is deliberately distinct from the gauge page: it proves the nonnegative-HK and `L^1` directions, equality on the overlap, and the two strictness qualifications.  It cites the existing gauge definitions and unconditional derivative theorem but does not re-mint them.  It upgrades the earlier statement-only `rem-henstock-kurzweil-vs-lebesgue` without treating that unproved remark as a dependency.
- The nonmonotone substitution statement is intentionally conditional: the primitive composed with the AC map must itself be AC.  The monotone increasing theorem is separate.  This prevents the known false naive nonmonotone claim.

## Source repair: bounded-derivative false-statement conflict

The design's final FS says: “A differentiable function with bounded derivative on `[a,b]` need not be absolutely continuous.”  This is false under the stated everywhere-differentiable reading.  Heil §3.3 explicitly says such a function is Lipschitz; the published `cor-bounded-derivative-implies-lipschitz` gives the same mean-value-theorem conclusion, and the published hierarchy then gives AC.  Accordingly the scaffold does **not** create an `fs-` whose assertion is true.  `rem-bounded-derivative-design-correction` records the exact correction and its dependencies.  If the design meant “differentiable only almost everywhere with an essentially bounded derivative,” the Cantor function supplies the different, true false-statement; that is already covered by `fs-continuity-almost-everywhere-differentiability-and-an-integrable-derivative-imply-newton-leibniz` and its companion examples.
