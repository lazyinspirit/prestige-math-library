## Definition

Let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ be open. For the fixed-support spaces of [[def-fixed-support-test-function-frechet-space]], let $\mathcal P$ be the set of all seminorms $q:\mathcal D(\Omega)\to[0,\infty)$ such that $q|_{\mathcal D_K}$ is continuous for every compact $K\subseteq\Omega$. A seminorm means $q(av)=|a|q(v)$ and $q(v+w)\le q(v)+q(w)$.

The **test-function topology** is the topology generated by translated finite intersections of sets $\{\varphi:q(\varphi)<\varepsilon\}$, where $q\in\mathcal P$ and $\varepsilon>0$. Equivalently it is the finest locally convex topology making every inclusion $\mathcal D_K\hookrightarrow\mathcal D(\Omega)$ continuous; the universal-property lemma below proves this equivalence and the topology assertions. This is the locally convex inductive-limit or **LF topology**, not the unrestricted final topology of arbitrary spaces.

For precision, a locally convex topology here is a vector topology generated by a family of seminorms, and boundedness means absorption by every zero-neighborhood: for each such neighborhood $U$ there is $t>0$ with $B\subseteq tU$. Finite intersections may be empty, giving the whole space. When $\Omega$ is empty there is only the zero vector and its unique topology. No convergence criterion for sequences is used in the definition; that criterion will be a theorem.
