137 cor-cauchy-theorem-convex-domain
CONTRACT Let $U$ be a complex domain whose image in $\mathbb R^2$ is convex in the sense of [[rem-plane-star-shaped-and-convex-dictionary]]. If $f:U\to\mathbb C$ is holomorphic and $\gamma$ is a closed rectifiable contour in $U$, then

$$\int_\gamma f(z)\,dz=0.$$

1.1 By [L1], choose any $a\in U$ and regard $U$ as star-shaped with respect to $a$. [given, L1, choose]


138 cor-cauchy-theorem-for-null-homotopic-loops
CONTRACT Let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb C$ be open, let $f:\Omega\to\mathbb C$ be
holomorphic, and let $\gamma:[0,1]\to\Omega$ be a closed rectifiable contour. If
$\gamma$ is path-homotopic relative to the endpoints to a constant loop in
$\Omega$, then

$$\int_\gamma f(z)\,dz=0.$$





SUPPLIER def-complex-contours-reversal-concatenation-and-closedness
[L4] A closed contour has the same initial and terminal point, and constant paths are legitimate contours ([[def-complex-contours-reversal-concatenation-and-closedness]]).
2.1 Because $c$ is constant, one has $f(c(t))=f(c(0))$ for every $t$. Since [L4] makes $c$ a closed contour, [L3] gives $$\int_c f(z)\,dz=\int_c f(c(0))\,dz=f(c(0))\bigl(c(1)-c(0)\bigr)=0.$$ Combining this with step 1.1 proves $\int_\gamma f(z)\,dz=0$. [step 1.1, L3, L4, algebra] ∎


142 cor-closed-contour-integral-of-a-derivative-is-zero
CONTRACT If $F$ is holomorphic on an open set, $F'$ is continuous there, and $\gamma$ is a closed rectifiable contour in that set, then $\int_\gamma F'(z)\,dz=0$.





SUPPLIER def-complex-contours-reversal-concatenation-and-closedness
[L2] A contour is closed exactly when its endpoint values agree ([[def-complex-contours-reversal-concatenation-and-closedness]]).
1.1 Apply [L1] and then [L2]: the endpoint increment is $F(\gamma(a))-F(\gamma(a))=0$. [L1, L2, algebra]

184 cor-holomorphic-function-ring-integral-domain
CONTRACT The holomorphic functions on a complex domain form an integral domain under pointwise addition and multiplication.

More explicitly, for a complex domain $\Omega$, the set $H(\Omega)$ of holomorphic functions $\Omega\to\mathbb C$ is a commutative subring of the function ring $\mathbb C^\Omega$ ([[def-ring-of-functions]], [[def-subring]]), its constant zero and one functions are distinct, and $fg=0$ implies $f=0$ or $g=0$ ([[def-zero-divisor-and-integral-domain]]).





SUPPLIER def-complex-domain
**Given:** A complex domain $\Omega$ ([[def-complex-domain]]); the field $\mathbb C$ ([[thm-complex-numbers-form-a-field]]); and the facts from [[thm-algebra-of-complex-derivatives]] that constants, sums, differences, and products of holomorphic functions are holomorphic, and that a holomorphic function nonzero at a point remains nonzero on some neighbourhood of that point.
