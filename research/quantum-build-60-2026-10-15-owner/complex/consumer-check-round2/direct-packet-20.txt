
2083 thm-weierstrass-convergence-holomorphic-functions
CONTRACT Let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb C$ be open, let each $f_n:\Omega\to\mathbb C$ be holomorphic, and suppose $f_n\to f$ locally uniformly on $\Omega$ in the sense of [[rem-locally-uniform-convergence-dictionary]]. Then $f$ is holomorphic and

$$f_n^{(k)}\longrightarrow f^{(k)}$$

locally uniformly on $\Omega$ for every natural $k$, with $f^{(0)}=f$.

A locally uniform limit of holomorphic functions is holomorphic, and for every natural $k$ the $k$th derivatives converge locally uniformly to the $k$th derivative of the limit.

2093 thm-weierstrass-zeta-sigma-quasi-periodicity
CONTRACT Let $\Lambda=\mathbb Z\omega_1+\mathbb Z\omega_2\subseteq\mathbb C$ be a full
complex lattice with oriented basis $(\omega_1,\omega_2)$
([[def-complex-lattice-and-complex-torus]]), let
$\wp=\wp_\Lambda$, $\zeta=\zeta_\Lambda$ and $\sigma=\sigma_\Lambda$ be its
Weierstrass functions
([[def-weierstrass-elliptic-p-function]],
[[def-weierstrass-zeta-and-sigma-functions]]), and put
$\eta_j:=2\zeta(\omega_j/2)$ for $j=1,2$, so that $\omega_1,\omega_2$ are
primitive and $\eta_j=\eta_{\omega_j}$. Then:

1. the finite-subset net defining $\zeta$ converges normally on
   $\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$ (uniformly on every compact subset), the
   unordered limit being independent of the exhaustion of
   $\Lambda\setminus\{0\}$ by finite subsets; the function $\zeta$ is meromorphic on $\mathbb C$,
   holomorphic exactly on $\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$, odd, and has at each
   lattice point $\lambda\in\Lambda$ a simple pole with principal part
   $(z-\lambda)^{-1}$ and residue $1$, with no other poles; moreover
   $$\zeta_\Lambda'(z)=-\wp_\Lambda(z)\qquad(z\in\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda);$$
2. the finite-subset net defining the product of the elementary factors
   $E_2(z/\omega)$ over $\omega\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}$ converges normally on
   $\mathbb C$, independently of the exhaustion, so that $\sigma$ is entire; $\sigma$ is odd, its zero set is
   exactly $\Lambda$ and every zero is simple, $\sigma'(0)=1$, and
   $$\frac{\sigma_\Lambda'(z)}{\sigma_\Lambda(z)}=\zeta_\Lambda(z)\qquad(z\in\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda);$$
3. the **quasi-period laws** hold, for $j=1,2$ and all
   $z\in\mathbb C$ (with poles matched),
   $$\zeta_\Lambda(z+\omega_j)=\zeta_\Lambda(z)+\eta_j,\qquad \sigma_\Lambda(z+\omega_j)=-\exp\!\left(\eta_j\Bigl(z+\frac{\omega_j}{2}\Bigr)\right)\sigma_\Lambda(z),$$
   and the **Legendre relation** holds:
   $$\eta_1\omega_2-\eta_2\omega_1=2\pi i .$$





SUPPLIER def-complex-contours-reversal-concatenation-and-closedness
[F11] For a piecewise-$C^1$ contour $\gamma$ and a function $f$ continuous on its trace, $\int_\gamma f(z)\,dz=\sum_j\int f(\gamma_j(t))\gamma_j'(t)\,dt$ over the smooth pieces ([[thm-riemann-stieltjes-and-parametric-contour-integrals-agree]], [[def-complex-line-integral-over-a-rectifiable-path]]); the reversal satisfies $\int_{\gamma^-}f\,dz=-\int_\gamma f\,dz$ and concatenation is additive, the concatenation and reversal of paths being those of [[def-complex-contours-reversal-concatenation-and-closedness]] ([[prop-reversal-and-concatenation-of-complex-line-integrals]]); and $\int_{\gamma\circ\phi}f\,dz=\int_\gamma f\,dz$ for a strictly increasing continuous bijection $\phi$ ([[thm-invariance-of-complex-line-integrals-under-increasing-reparametrization]]).
5.4 (A translated parallelogram with one pole.) Put $\tau:=\omega_2/\omega_1$, which has $\operatorname{Im}\tau>0$ by [F1], and define the closed parallelogram $T:=\{s+t\tau:0\le s,t\le1\}$ of the boundary lemma with $\alpha(y):=(\operatorname{Re}\tau/\operatorname{Im}\tau)y$ and $\beta(y):=\alpha(y)+1$ on $[0,\operatorname{Im}\tau]$; define also $a:=-\tfrac12(\omega_1+\omega_2)$, the orientation-preserving similarity $\sigma(w):=a+\omega_1w$, and $P:=\sigma(T)=\{a+s\omega_1+t\omega_2:0\le s,t\le1\}$ with interior $P^\circ$. The boundary contour $\gamma_T$ of $T$ is the concatenation of the bottom segment, the graph of $\beta$ with $y$ increasing, the top segment and the graph of $\alpha$ with $y$ decreasing; its image under $\sigma$ traverses the four sides of $P$ as the paths $\gamma_1(t):=a+t\omega_1$, $\gamma_2(t):=a+\omega_1+t\omega_2$, $\gamma_3(t):=a+\omega_1+\omega_2-t\omega_1$, $\gamma_4(t):=a+\omega_2-t\omega_2$ for $t\in[0,1]$, up to strictly increasing reparametrizations of the two graph pieces. By [F10] applied to $\sigma$, the index of the contour $\Gamma:=\sigma\circ\gamma_T=\gamma_1*\gamma_2*\gamma_3*\gamma_4$ (with the reparametrizations of [F11]) is $n(\Gamma,q)=1$ for $q\in P^\circ$ and $0$ for $q\notin P$. A lattice point $\lambda=m\omega_1+n\omega_2$ lies in $P$ iff $s=m+\tfrac12\in[0,1]$ and $t=n+\tfrac12\in[0,1]$, that is iff $m=n=0$; hence $0\in P^\circ$ is the only lattice point in $P$ and every other lattice point lies outside $P$, with $n(\Gamma,\lambda)=0$ for $\lambda\ne0$. Consequently $\Gamma^\ast$ avoids $\Lambda$; since $\zeta$ is holomorphic on $\Omega\setminus\{0\}=\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$ and $0$ is a pole of $\zeta$ by step 4.1, $\zeta$ is meromorphic on the domain $\Omega:=\mathbb C\setminus(\Lambda\setminus\{0\})$ of step 2.2 with pole set $\{0\}$, and $\Gamma$ is admissible for the residue theorem in $\Omega$ because $n(\Gamma,p)=0$ for every $p\notin\Omega$ by the preceding index computation. 6.2 (The four sides and the Legendre relation.) With $\Omega$ as in step 5.4, the residue theorem [F9] applied to $\zeta$, whose only pole in $\Omega$ is $0$ with $n(\Gamma,0)=1$ and residue $1$ by step 4.1, gives $\int_\Gamma\zeta(z)\,dz=2\pi i$. On the other hand the parametric formula and additivity of [F11] give $\int_\Gamma\zeta=\sum_{k=1}^4\int_{\gamma_k}\zeta$, and $\gamma_3$ is the reversal of the translated path $\delta_1(t):=\gamma_1(t)+\omega_2$ while $\gamma_4$ is the reversal of $\delta_2(t):=\gamma_2(t)-\omega_1$. By step 5.3, $\zeta(\delta_1(t))=\zeta(\gamma_1(t))+\eta_2$ and, using $\zeta(w-\omega_1)=\zeta(w)-\eta_1$ for $w=\gamma_2(t)\in\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$, also $\zeta(\delta_2(t))=\zeta(\gamma_2(t))-\eta_1$; the parametric formula applied to each translated path (with derivative $\omega_1$, respectively $\omega_2$) yields $\int_{\delta_1}\zeta=\int_{\gamma_1}\zeta+\eta_2\omega_1$ and $\int_{\delta_2}\zeta=\int_{\gamma_2}\zeta-\eta_1\omega_2$. Since reversal negates integrals, $$\int_\Gamma\zeta=\int_{\gamma_1}\zeta+\int_{\gamma_2}\zeta-\Bigl(\int_{\gamma_1}\zeta+\eta_2\omega_1\Bigr)-\Bigl(\int_{\gamma_2}\zeta-\eta_1\omega_2\Bigr)=\eta_1\omega_2-\eta_2\omega_1 .$$ Comparing with $\int_\Gamma\zeta=2\pi i$ gives $\eta_1\omega_2-\eta_2\omega_1=2\pi i$.
