This page defines a total product of extended-real-valued functions on any set.
The zero-factor rule is part of the function operation: it assigns zero even
when the other value is infinite. Finite products agree with real arithmetic,
and nonzero infinite values use their signs. The definition is available before
measurability is introduced and asserts no measurability of the output.

The partial arithmetic on the extended real line keeps its original domain.
Later the measurable arithmetic theorem proves that this separate pointwise
operation preserves measurability.
