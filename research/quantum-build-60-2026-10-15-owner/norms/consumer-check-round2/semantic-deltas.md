## Common domain and predicates

Fix K equal to the library real field or complex field. Write Norm_K(V,N) for: V is a K-vector space; N maps V into finite reals; N(v)=0 iff v=0; N(lambda v)=|lambda|N(v) for **every** lambda in K; and N(u+v)<=N(u)+N(v). The old norm convention's first paragraph states exactly these clauses for C, while the real norm definition states exactly these clauses for R. The new complex norm definition is therefore extensionally equivalent on its entire declared domain to that old explicitly stated convention, not merely on finite-dimensional spaces or on inner-product norms. Both derive nonnegativity from N(-v)=N(v) and 0<=2N(v), so adding its explicit derivation changes no admissible N.

Addition and subtraction are the same operations on V and its realification. The old convention explicitly fixes d(u,v)=N(u-v); the proposed definition fixes precisely the same real-valued function, without rescaling. Consequently every ball, metric-open set, sequence limit, Cauchy sequence, closed set and completeness condition is extensionally identical for the two contracts. This uses the displayed metric, not an unproved equivalence of topologies or a choice principle.

For every real r, the embedded r+0i has modulus sqrt(r squared). Its real absolute value is nonnegative with square r squared, so unique nonnegative square roots give the same number. Restricting full complex homogeneity therefore yields exactly real homogeneity. Separation and the triangle inequality restrict verbatim. This proves the norm-realification supplier over all complex normed spaces, including the singleton, and proves the completeness identity used below. Restriction does not forget or weaken the original full complex homogeneity on V.
