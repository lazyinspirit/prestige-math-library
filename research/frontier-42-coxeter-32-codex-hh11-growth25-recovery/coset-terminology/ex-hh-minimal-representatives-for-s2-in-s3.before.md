## Example

Let $W\cong S_3$ be the type-A group of [[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]] (4) with $S=\{s_1,s_2\}$, and let $J=\{s_1\}$, so that $W_J=\{1,s_1\}\cong S_2$. Then $W$ has three left cosets $W_Ja=\{ua:u\in W_J\}$,

$$\{1,s_1\},\qquad \{s_2,\,s_1s_2\},\qquad \{s_2s_1,\,s_1s_2s_1\},$$

with unique minimal elements $1$, $s_2$ and $s_2s_1$ of lengths $0$, $1$ and $2$. Each minimal element $d$ satisfies $\ell(s_1d)>\ell(d)$ (namely $\ell(s_1)=1$, $\ell(s_1s_2)=2$, $\ell(s_1s_2s_1)=3$), and length additivity holds: $\ell(s_1\cdot s_2)=1+1=2$ and $\ell(s_1\cdot s_2s_1)=1+2=3$. The corresponding right cosets $aW_J=\{au:u\in W_J\}$ have the minimal representatives $1$, $s_2$, $s_1s_2$, and for these $\ell(ds_1)=\ell(d)+1$ holds.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The type-A Coxeter matrix on $S=\{s_1,s_2\}$ with $m(s_1,s_2)=3$; the presented group $W$ with its length $\ell$ of [[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]; the parabolic subgroup $W_J=\langle\{s:s\in J\}\rangle$ and the coset theorem of [[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]]; the element list and length table of [[ex-hh-type-a-reduced-words-and-inversions]]; and the coset vocabulary of [[def-coset]].

[F1] [[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]] (3): "Every left coset $W_J a:=\{ua:u\in W_J\}$ ($a\in W$, [[def-coset]]) has a unique element $d$ of minimal length; it is characterized by $\ell(sd)>\ell(d)$ for all $s\in J$, and it satisfies $$\ell(ud)=\ell(u)+\ell(d)\qquad\text{for all }u\in W_J .$$"; and "every right coset $aW_J:=\{au:u\in W_J\}$ has a unique minimal element $d$, characterized by $\ell(ds)>\ell(d)$ for all $s\in J$ and satisfying $\ell(du)=\ell(d)+\ell(u)$ for all $u\in W_J$".

[F2] [[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]] (1),(2): "$W_J=\langle J\rangle=\{w\in W:S(w)\subseteq J\}$", and the canonical map is an isomorphism onto $W_J$, so "its intrinsic length function $\ell_J$ agrees with the ambient length $\ell$ on $W_J$, and $W_J\cap S=J$".

[F3] [[ex-hh-type-a-reduced-words-and-inversions]]: the six elements of $W$ are $1,s_1,s_2,s_1s_2,s_2s_1,s_1s_2s_1=s_2s_1s_2$ with lengths $0,1,1,2,2,3$ and $\ell(w)=\operatorname{inv}(\varphi(w))$; and $s_1s_2s_1=s_2s_1s_2$ is the longest element of $W$.

[F4] [[def-coset]]: "For $g\in G$, the **left coset** and **right coset** of $H$ represented by $g$ are $$gH:=\{gh:h\in H\},\qquad Hg:=\{hg:h\in H\}.$$", so the sets $W_Ja=\{ua:u\in W_J\}$ and $aW_J=\{au:u\in W_J\}$ are the two coset families. This example labels $W_Ja$ left and $aW_J$ right, following the Coxeter-theory convention of [F1]; the displayed sets specify each use.

[F5] [[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]: $W_J:=\langle\{s:s\in J\}\rangle\le W$ for $J\subseteq S$, and $\ell(w)$ is the least length of a word in $S$ representing $w$; in particular $\ell(1)=0$.

[F6] [[lem-hh-dihedral-root-recurrence-and-root-sign]] (1),(4): "$\ell(s)=1$" for the generators, and "$\sigma_s^2=\mathrm{id}_E$ for every $s\in S$"; in particular $\ell(s_1)=1$ implies $s_1\ne1$.

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct enumeration of the six elements of $W$, with the coset, minimality and additivity statements of the parabolic theorem applied to $J=\{s_1\}$.

1.1 **The parabolic subgroup.** $W_J=\langle J\rangle=\langle s_1\rangle=\{1,s_1\}$: the definition of $W_J$ as the subgroup generated by $J$ is [F5] and its identity with $\langle s_1\rangle$ is immediate for $J=\{s_1\}$ [F2]; the relator $s_1^2$ of the presentation gives $s_1^2=1$ [F5], so $\langle s_1\rangle\subseteq\{1,s_1\}$, and $s_1\ne1$ because $\ell(s_1)=1$ [F6], so $\langle s_1\rangle$ has the two distinct elements $1,s_1$; it is a group of order $2$, hence isomorphic to $S_2$. [F2, F5, F6]

1.2 **The right cosets.** The right cosets $aW_J$ are $\{1,s_1\}$, $s_2W_J=\{s_2,s_2s_1\}$ and $s_1s_2W_J=\{s_1s_2,s_1s_2s_1\}$, disjoint and exhausting $W$ by the six distinct elements listed in [F3]; their minimal elements are $1$ (length $0$), $s_2$ (length $1$, versus $\ell(s_2s_1)=2$) and $s_1s_2$ (length $2$, versus $\ell(s_1s_2s_1)=3$), unique by the right-coset half of [F1]. For these three elements $d$ the right-handed additivity of [F1] gives $\ell(ds_1)=\ell(d)+\ell(s_1)=\ell(d)+1$, namely $\ell(s_1)=1$, $\ell(s_2s_1)=2=1+1$ and $\ell(s_1s_2s_1)=3=2+1$, as asserted; equivalently the right coset representatives satisfy the characterisation $\ell(ds_1)>\ell(d)$ of [F1]. [F1, F3, F4]

2.1 **The left cosets and their minimal elements.** For $W_J=\{1,s_1\}$ the sets $W_Ja=\{ua:u\in W_J\}$ of [F4], with $a\in W$, can be enumerated using the six-element list of [F3] they are $W_J\cdot1=\{1,s_1\}$, $W_Js_2=\{s_2,s_1s_2\}$ and $W_Js_2s_1=\{s_2s_1,s_1s_2s_1\}$, which are disjoint and exhaust $W$. Reading the lengths off [F3], the minimum of $\ell$ is $0$ on $\{1,s_1\}$ (attained at $1$), $1$ on $\{s_2,s_1s_2\}$ (attained at $s_2$) and $2$ on $\{s_2s_1,s_1s_2s_1\}$ (attained at $s_2s_1$); by [F1] each coset has a unique element of minimal length, so the minimal representatives are $1$, $s_2$ and $s_2s_1$ with lengths $0$, $1$ and $2$. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 **The descent characterisation and length additivity.** For the three minimal representatives $d=1$, $d=s_2$ and $d=s_2s_1$, the table of [F3] gives $\ell(s_1\cdot1)=\ell(s_1)=1>0=\ell(1)$, $\ell(s_1s_2)=2>1=\ell(s_2)$ and $\ell(s_1s_2s_1)=3>2=\ell(s_2s_1)$, so each of them satisfies the characterisation $\ell(s_1d)>\ell(d)$ of [F1]. The additivity identity of [F1] for $u=s_1\in W_J$ reads $\ell(s_1d)=\ell(s_1)+\ell(d)$ for the minimal $d$; at $d=s_2$ this is $\ell(s_1s_2)=1+1=2$ and at $d=s_2s_1$ it is $\ell(s_1s_2s_1)=1+2=3$, the two values asserted in the statement. [F1, F3, step 2.1]

4.1 **Collected.** The example enumerates the three left cosets and the three right cosets of $W_J=\{1,s_1\}$ in $W\cong S_3$, identifies their unique minimal representatives with the lengths predicted by the parabolic theorem, verifies the descent characterisations on both sides and the length-additivity identities $\ell(ud)=\ell(u)+\ell(d)$ and $\ell(du)=\ell(d)+\ell(u)$ for $u=s_1$ (steps 2.1, 3.1, 1.2). [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 1.2] ∎
