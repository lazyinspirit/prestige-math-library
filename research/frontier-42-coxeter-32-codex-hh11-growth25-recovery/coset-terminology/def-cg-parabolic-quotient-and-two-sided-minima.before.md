## Definition

Let $(S,m)$ be a finite Coxeter matrix, let $W$ be the presented group with
length function $\ell$ ([[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]), and
let $T=\{wsw^{-1}:w\in W,\ s\in S\}$ be the set of reflections of $W$
([[def-cg-canonical-reflection-homomorphism]]). Throughout, $I$ and $J$ denote
subsets of $S$.

**(1) Standard parabolic subgroups.** For $I\subseteq S$ put

$$W_I:=\langle s:s\in I\rangle\le W,$$

the subgroup generated by $I$ ([[def-generated-subgroup]]); $W_I$ is the
**standard parabolic subgroup** of $(W,S)$ of type $I$. Thus
$W_\emptyset=\{1\}$ and $W_S=W$. By
[[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]] (1),(2): for
every $w\in W$ one has $w\in W_I$ if and only if $S(w)\subseteq I$, where
$S(w)$ is the (well-defined) set of letters of any reduced expression of $w$;
$(W_I,I)$ is a Coxeter system; its intrinsic length function agrees with
$\ell|_{W_I}$; and $W_I\cap S=I$.

**(2) Descents and the one-sided quotients.** For $w\in W$ put

$$D_L(w):=\{s\in S:\ell(sw)<\ell(w)\},\qquad D_R(w):=\{s\in S:\ell(ws)<\ell(w)\};$$

each of $\ell(sw)-\ell(w)$ and $\ell(ws)-\ell(w)$ lies in $\{\pm1\}$
([[thm-hh-coxeter-exchange-deletion-and-faithfulness]] (1)). Define

$$W^I:=\{w\in W:\ell(ws)>\ell(w)\text{ for all }s\in I\}=\{w\in W:D_R(w)\cap I=\emptyset\},$$

$${}^IW:=\{w\in W:\ell(sw)>\ell(w)\text{ for all }s\in I\}=\{w\in W:D_L(w)\cap I=\emptyset\}.$$

Conventions are fixed here: in ${}^IW$ the subset $I$ is tested on the left, in
$W^I$ it is tested on the right; $\ell(sw)$ means left multiplication by $s$
and $\ell(ws)$ right multiplication. The interpretation of these sets as sets
of coset representatives is not part of the definition; it is quoted from
[[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]] (3) and
re-examined in [[thm-cg-parabolic-intersections-and-coset-factorization]] (3):
every $w\in W$ has a unique factorization

$$w=u\,d\qquad(u\in W_I,\ d\in{}^IW),\qquad \ell(w)=\ell(u)+\ell(d),$$

where $d$ is the unique element of minimal length in the left coset $W_Iw$, and
it satisfies $\ell(ud)=\ell(u)+\ell(d)$ for all $u\in W_I$; symmetrically every
$w\in W$ has a unique factorization

$$w=d\,v\qquad(d\in W^I,\ v\in W_I),\qquad \ell(w)=\ell(d)+\ell(v),$$

where $d$ is the unique element of minimal length in the right coset $wW_I$, and
it satisfies $\ell(dv)=\ell(d)+\ell(v)$ for all $v\in W_I$. Thus ${}^IW$
consists exactly of the elements of minimal length in the left cosets
$W_Iw=\{uw:u\in W_I\}$ and $W^I$ consists exactly of the elements of minimal
length in the sets $wW_I=\{wv:v\in W_I\}$. Inversion gives
$W^I=({}^IW)^{-1}$, because $\ell(x)=\ell(x^{-1})$ and $(sw)^{-1}=w^{-1}s$.

**(3) The two-sided quotients.** Define

$${}^IW^J:={}^IW\cap W^J=\{w\in W:\ell(sw)>\ell(w)\text{ for all }s\in I\ \text{ and }\ \ell(ws)>\ell(w)\text{ for all }s\in J\};$$

thus ${}^\emptyset W^J=W^J$, ${}^IW^\emptyset={}^IW$ and ${}^\emptyset
W^\emptyset=W$, and the members of ${}^IW^J$ are exactly the elements of $W$
with no left descent in $I$ and no right descent in $J$. Whether ${}^IW^J$ is a
set of representatives of the double cosets $W_IwW_J$ is again a theorem, not a
definition; it is proved in
[[thm-cg-double-coset-unique-minimum-and-normal-form]].

**(4) Parabolic subgroups and reflection subgroups.** A subgroup $H\le W$ is a
**parabolic subgroup** of $(W,S)$ if

$$H=wW_Iw^{-1}\qquad\text{for some }w\in W,\ I\subseteq S;$$

a parabolic subgroup with $w=1$ is *standard*. A subgroup $H\le W$ is a
**reflection subgroup** if $H=\langle H\cap T\rangle$, i.e. if $H$ is generated
by the reflections it contains. Every parabolic subgroup is a reflection
subgroup, since $wW_Iw^{-1}=\langle wsw^{-1}:s\in I\rangle$ and
$wsw^{-1}\in T$ for all $s\in S$, $w\in W$. No converse is asserted: it is not
claimed here that a subgroup generated by reflections is parabolic, and the
companion examples page computes test cases for the two distinctions created by
this definition --- standard parabolic versus parabolic, and reflection subgroup
versus parabolic subgroup.

## Remarks

- **Well-definedness.** $W_I$ is the subgroup generated by a subset of the group
  $W$ and exists by [[def-generated-subgroup]]; $W_\emptyset=\{1\}$ is the
  trivial subgroup and $W_S=W$. The sets $W^I$, ${}^IW$ and ${}^IW^J$ are
  subsets of $W$ specified by a predicate and are therefore well-defined sets.
  The two displayed factorizations of (2) and the description of $W^I$, ${}^IW$
  as sets of minimal representatives are quoted from
  [[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]] (1)-(3),
  which proves them from the deletion and exchange properties before they are
  used here; the inversion identity $W^I=({}^IW)^{-1}$ uses
  $\ell(x)=\ell(x^{-1})$, $(sw)^{-1}=w^{-1}s$ ([[def-group]]) and the
  equivalence $\ell(sw)<\ell(w)\Leftrightarrow\ell(w^{-1}s)<\ell(w^{-1})$. The
  only genuinely two-sided assertion is deferred: this item introduces
  ${}^IW^J$ as a set of descent-free elements and asserts nothing about double
  cosets; the property that every double coset $W_IwW_J$ contains exactly one
  element of ${}^IW^J$, and that this element is its minimum, is established in
  [[thm-cg-double-coset-unique-minimum-and-normal-form]], the later proof that
  justifies the definition for its applications.
- **Left and right conventions are not symmetric by accident.** In ${}^IW$ the
  descent is tested by left multiplication, in $W^I$ by right multiplication;
  the left coset $W_Iw$ is represented by ${}^IW$ and the right coset $wW_I$ by
  $W^I$. Reversing either convention produces false statements already in
  $S_3$ with $I=\{s_1\}$ (for example $s_2s_1$ has no factorization $ud$ with
  $d\in W^I$), which is why they are recorded here once and used consistently.
- **No converse and no Choice.** The inclusion "parabolic subgroups are
  reflection subgroups" in (4) is immediate from the definitions, since a
  conjugate $wsw^{-1}$ of a simple reflection is a reflection; nothing is
  asserted in the other direction. No use of the Axiom of Choice is made: all
  constructions are subgroups and subsets of the fixed group $W$, and the
  minima in (2) are minima of nonempty subsets of the natural numbers.
