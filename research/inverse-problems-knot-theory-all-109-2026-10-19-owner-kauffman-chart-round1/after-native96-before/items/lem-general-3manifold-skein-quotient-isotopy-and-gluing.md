## Statement

Let $M$ and $N$ be smooth oriented $3$-manifolds, and let
$f:M\to N$ be an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism. Then
$[L]\mapsto[f(L)]$ induces an $R$-module isomorphism
$f_*:\mathcal K_A(M)\to\mathcal K_A(N)$. These maps respect identity
diffeomorphisms and composition.

Let $\Sigma$ be a compact oriented surface with a specified product collar
$\iota:\Sigma\times[0,1]\hookrightarrow M$ whose lower face lies in
$\partial M$. The stacking product on
$\mathcal K_A(\Sigma\times[0,1])$ and collar gluing define a unital left
action on $\mathcal K_A(M)$: a closed framed link in the surface product is
placed in a sufficiently thin outer subcollar and united with a framed link
in the interior of $M$. The action is independent of the thin-collar width,
associative, and natural under orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms that
respect the specified collars. Only closed framed links are glued; no relative
boundary endpoints are part of this action.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Smooth oriented $3$-manifolds $M,N$, an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism $f:M\to N$, an oriented surface collar in $\partial M$, and the Kauffman bracket skein quotients defined above.

[F1] The free module is generated by framed unoriented link isotopy classes; its relation submodule is generated by the local crossing relation and the zero-framed trivial-circle relation with $R=\mathbb Z[A^{\pm1}]$ and $\delta=-A^2-A^{-2}$ ([[def-kauffman-bracket-skein-module-of-a-three-manifold]], [[def-kauffman-bracket-smoothing-and-loop-value]]).

## Proof


1.1 Define $\widetilde f:\mathcal F_R(M)\to\mathcal F_R(N)$ on basis elements by $\widetilde f([L])=[f(L)]$ and extend $R$-linearly. A diffeomorphism sends ambient-isotopic framed annuli to ambient-isotopic framed annuli and sends the framing annuli by its derivative, so this is well-defined on the free modules. [F1, given, construct]

1.2 Fix a framed link representative $P$ in $\Sigma\times(0,1)$ and a framed link representative $L$ in the interior of $M$. Compactness of $L$ gives a boundary collar layer disjoint from $L$; rescale $P$ into that layer and glue it to $L$ by disjoint union. A change of $P$ by either defining relation becomes the identical local relation after the collar embedding, and a change of $L$ by either defining relation remains the identical relation with the disjoint collar link outside its ball. Hence the operation descends to a bilinear action on the quotients. If the collar width is decreased, an isotopy supported in the product collar carries one rescaled copy of $P$ to the other while fixing its outer boundary, so its skein class is unchanged. [F1, given, construct]

2.1 The image of an oriented crossing ball is an oriented ball. Its transported chart preserves the over/under crossing and both endpoint pairings, so $\widetilde f([L_\times]-A[L_A]-A^{-1}[L_B])$ is the corresponding defining relation in $N$. A zero-framed trivial circle bounding a disk in the ball maps to another zero-framed trivial circle bounding the image disk. Thus $\widetilde f(\mathcal R_A(M))\subseteq\mathcal R_A(N)$ and $\widetilde f$ descends to $f_*$. Applying the same construction to $f^{-1}$ gives the inverse; applying it to a composite gives the composite map on every link generator. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Stacking three product-collar links in successive subintervals gives the same union in either parenthesization, up to an orientation-preserving product rescaling; the empty link is the unit. Thus the action is associative and unital. If an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism carries one specified collar productwise to another, it sends each stacked or glued link union to the corresponding union, so its module maps intertwine the actions. [F1, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
