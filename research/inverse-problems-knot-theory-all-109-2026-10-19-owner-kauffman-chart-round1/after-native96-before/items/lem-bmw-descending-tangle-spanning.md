## Statement

For each integer $n\ge0$, let $S=\mathbb Z[a^{\pm1},z,d^{\pm1}]/(z(d-1)-(a-a^{-1}))$ and $T=S[q^{\pm1}]/(q-q^{-1}-z)$, with $z_D=q-q^{-1}$. Let $\mathcal{KT}_n(T)$ be the ordinary Dubrovnik tangle skein algebra formed from regular-isotopy classes with crossing rule $D_+-D_-=z_D(D_0-D_\infty)$, curl factors $a,a^{-1}$, and free-circle factor $d$, where $D_0$ is through and $D_\infty$ is cup-cap. Number its $2n$ boundary endpoints in cyclic order as $1,2,\ldots,n,\bar n,\ldots,\bar2,\bar1$. A connector is a perfect matching of these endpoints. Orient each open strand from its lower-numbered endpoint to its higher-numbered endpoint and order the open strands by their initial endpoints. Place the finitely many closed strands after the open strands in any fixed order, with a chosen orientation and initial point for each. A diagram is totally descending if, in this traversal order, the first branch encountered at each crossing is the overpassing branch. Then $\mathcal{KT}_n(T)$ is spanned by one loop-free, self-crossing-free totally descending tangle for each connector. These representatives can have crossings between distinct strands; their crossing data are fixed by the total traversal order. For $n=0$ the single representative is the empty tangle, and for $n=1$ it is the straight strand.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite rectangular tangle diagrams, their fixed endpoint order, the Dubrovnik skein and curl relations, and the generator interpretation from the preceding map.

[F1] A tangle diagram is a generic projection of finitely many arcs with fixed boundary endpoints and closed curves; regular isotopy is generated by planar isotopy and Reidemeister II and III ([[def-dubrovnik-tangle-skein-algebra]]).

[F2] The tangle coefficient rules are $D_+-D_-=z_D(D_0-D_\infty)$, positive and negative curls multiply by $a,a^{-1}$, and a disjoint trivial circle multiplies by $d$ ([[def-kauffman-regular-isotopy-and-dubrovnik-conventions]]).

## Proof

1.1 If a tangle diagram has no crossings, every closed component is a disjoint trivial circle and the remaining arcs form a crossingless connector; removing each circle by [F2] leaves a scalar multiple of its standard matching tangle. This is the base case for induction on the lexicographically ordered pair (crossing count, bad-crossing count). [F1, F2, base]

1.2 For a diagram with crossings, choose a finite traversal order of its components and orient each arc from its smaller boundary label to its larger one; for closed components use any finite encoded orientation and basepoint. A crossing is bad when its first encountered branch is under. If there is a bad crossing, the skein relation expresses the diagram as its switched diagram plus two smoothing terms. Switching preserves the crossing count and lowers the bad-crossing count by one; each smoothing lowers the crossing count by one. Thus every term is lower in the stated lexicographic order, and the induction hypothesis expresses each as a linear combination of standard descending representatives. The choices here are finite choices made from the encoded diagram, not an appeal to an infinite choice principle. [F1, F2, ih, induction]

1.3 If there is no bad crossing, the diagram is totally descending. Place its components in their traversal order as successive height layers. For each closed component, lifting its first traversed branch over all later branches makes it an unknotted circle disjoint from the remaining layers; any framing curl is removed by the corresponding curl relation, and the free-circle rule removes it with a factor $d$. For the remaining arcs, each arc’s first encounter is over at every self-crossing, so lifting each already traversed segment removes its self-crossings while fixing the endpoints; crossings between distinct arcs remain in their ordered layers. The result is a standard loop-free, self-crossing-free descending tangle with the same connector. [F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 The base case, induction step, and descending terminal case together show by well-founded induction that every diagram reduces to a linear combination of standard representatives. Two descending representatives with the same connector have the same ordered height layers; moving the highest arc to its corresponding arc while fixing lower layers, then proceeding downward, gives a boundary-fixing regular isotopy. Thus each connector has one representative. There are $(2n-1)!!$ perfect matchings on $2n$ endpoints, obtained by pairing the first endpoint in $2n-1$ ways and repeating; for $n=0$ the empty matching is unique and for $n=1$ the straight strand is unique. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, discharge-induction: step 1.2] ∎
