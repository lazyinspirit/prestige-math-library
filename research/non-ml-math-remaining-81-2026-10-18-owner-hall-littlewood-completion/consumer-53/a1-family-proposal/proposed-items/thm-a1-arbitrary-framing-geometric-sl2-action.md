## Statement

For $W=\mathbb C^w$, $w\ge0$, and the no-arrow one-vertex quiver with
moment equation $IJ=0$ and stability $J$ injective, let
$$\mathcal V_w=\bigoplus_{v=0}^w A_{a_v}(L_v)_{\mathbb Q},\qquad b_v=[L_v],\qquad a_v=v(w-v).$$
Use the algebraic quotient and conormal Hecke cycles $Z_v$ of
[[lem-a1-arbitrary-framing-cotangent-hecke-model]] and the supported
convolution action of [[lem-a1-supported-chow-convolution-and-action]].
Define $F$ on the $v$-summand by $[Z_v]$ for $v<w$ and zero at $v=w$.
Define $E$ from the $(v+1)$-summand by the reversed cycle with coefficient
$(-1)^{a_v-a_{v+1}}=(-1)^{w-1}$, and zero at $v=0$.
Let $H$ act by $w-2v$ on the $v$-summand. Then
$$F b_v=(-1)^v(v+1)b_{v+1},\qquad E b_{v+1}=(-1)^v(w-v)b_v,\qquad H b_v=(w-2v)b_v.$$
The nonexistent endpoint terms are zero. These geometrically defined
operators satisfy $[E,F]=H$, $[H,E]=2E$, $[H,F]=-2F$ and give the
irreducible integrable highest-weight-$w$ representation of
$\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb Q)$. Scalar extension gives the corresponding
complex representation. All geometric maps and the action on ordinary
Chow groups are $\operatorname{GL}(W)$-equivariant. This theorem concerns
ordinary top zero-fiber Chow groups; it does not assert an action on the
entire equivariant Chow groups with coefficient parameters retained.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The family, gradings, cycles, sign convention and supported
operations in the statement, with the inherited Axiom of Choice.

[F1] $L_v=X_v$ is smooth irreducible projective of dimension $a_v$, and
$Z_v$ has dimension $a_v+a_{v+1}$ with projective projections
([[lem-a1-arbitrary-framing-cotangent-hecke-model]]).

[F2] Raw forward and reverse zero-section coefficients are
$(-1)^v(v+1)$ and $(-1)^{w-v-1}(w-v)$, respectively
([[lem-a1-hecke-zero-section-excess-coefficients]]).

[F3] Supported Chow convolution is associative and acts by composition;
its diagonals are units, its stated degree shifts hold, and its maps
intertwine $\operatorname{GL}(W)$
([[lem-a1-supported-chow-convolution-and-action]]).

## Proof

1.1 The top-dimensional Chow group of an irreducible $a_v$-dimensional variety is freely generated by its fundamental class: there is only one integral subvariety of dimension $a_v$, and rational equivalences in this degree would require an $(a_v+1)$-dimensional subvariety, which does not exist. Thus $\mathcal V_w$ has basis $b_0,\ldots,b_w$. By [F1]–[F3] the Hecke operators have exactly the required degree to preserve these top summands, and their actual push-pull actions are the raw coefficients of [F2]. Since $a_v-a_{v+1}=2v+1-w$, the reverse sign has parity $w-1$; multiplying it into the raw reverse coefficient gives $(-1)^{w-1+w-v-1}(w-v)=(-1)^v(w-v)$. This proves all displayed coefficient formulas. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 For $0\le v\le w$, the formulas in step 1.1 give $EF b_v=(v+1)(w-v)b_v$ and $FE b_v=v(w-v+1)b_v$, with the same equalities at the endpoints by zero extension. Their difference is $(w-2v)b_v=Hb_v$. Since $E$ raises the $H$-weight by $2$ and $F$ lowers it by $2$, the same formulas give $[H,E]=2E$ and $[H,F]=-2F$ on every basis vector. They are the defining brackets of the trace-zero two-by-two Lie algebra, so these operators extend to its enveloping-algebra representation. This is a correspondence action: [F3] identifies every product of its geometric kernels with the corresponding composite operator, so the computed relations are relations of that action rather than formal dimension matching. [F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The vector $b_0$ is killed by $E$ and has weight $w$. Successive lowering gives $F^v b_0=(-1)^{v(v-1)/2}v!b_v$, which is nonzero for $0\le v\le w$; thus it generates the whole space. Both $E$ and $F$ are nilpotent since they move among finitely many summands. If a nonzero invariant subspace is given, a polynomial in the diagonal $H$ projects a nonzero vector onto one of its distinct weight components $\mathbb Q b_v$; applying $E^v$ gives a nonzero multiple of $b_0$, and then the powers of $F$ give all basis vectors. This proves irreducibility and integrability, including the trivial one-dimensional module at $w=0$. Replacing $b_v$ by $(-1)^{v(v-1)/2}b_v$ gives the usual divided-power coefficients $F c_v=(v+1)c_{v+1}$ and $E c_v=(w-v+1)c_{v-1}$. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Equivariance follows from [F1]–[F3]; in fact each $h$ sends the entire $L_v$ to itself and hence fixes $b_v$, so it acts trivially on the ordinary top Chow representation. At $w=1$ all spaces and the Hecke correspondence are points and the formulas reduce to the existing fundamental two-dimensional example. This preserves that example as a special case and completes the arbitrary-framing construction. [F1, F3, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
