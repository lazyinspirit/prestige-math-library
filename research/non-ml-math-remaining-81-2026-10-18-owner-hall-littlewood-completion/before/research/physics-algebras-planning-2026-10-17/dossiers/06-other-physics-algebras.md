# Other physics-connected algebras: source-grounded route and repository delta

**Snapshot:** 2026-10-17. **Research status:** selected primary and textbook reading, plus a focused local-carrier audit. This is a tiered map, not an exhaustive survey or a claim that every deep theorem named below has been independently proved.

This lane covers algebra families whose physics use depends on additional analytic, homological, geometric, or categorical structure beyond the core Lie/Hopf/Hecke/TL/Clifford routes. The central lesson is to preserve the carrier: an abstract algebra, its module category, a completed algebra, an unbounded-operator realization, and a physical model are not interchangeable merely because they share commutation relations or vocabulary.

## 1. Weyl, Heisenberg, CCR, and operator-algebra carriers

### 1.1 Keep four levels separate

For a real symplectic vector space ((V,sigma)), a Weyl algebra is generated by unitary symbols (W(f)) with a convention such as

\[
W(f)W(g)=e^{-i\sigma(f,g)/2}W(f+g),\qquad W(f)^*=W(-f).
\]

Changing the sign in the phase changes the convention and must be translated consistently to the infinitesimal CCR. The algebraic CCR quotient generated by \(Q_j,P_j\) with \([Q_j,P_k]=i\hbar\delta_{jk}I\) is a separate object: in a Hilbert-space representation the \(Q_j,P_j\) are generally unbounded, so their products and commutators must be stated on a common invariant dense domain. An equality on the Schwartz or finite-particle core is not an equality on the entire Hilbert space.

The Weyl relations avoid writing unbounded generators in the defining relations, but a representation need not be **regular**. Regularity means appropriate strong continuity of \(t\mapsto\pi(W(tf))\); only then can Stone’s theorem produce self-adjoint infinitesimal generators. A state on the universal Weyl C*-algebra can be positive and normalized and still be nonregular. It therefore need not define the usual position/momentum observables. The Fewster–Rejzner source gives a concrete nonregular tracial Weyl state; the local physics counterexample `physics/items/cex-qft-nonregular-weyl-state.md` already supplies the corresponding library carrier.

For finitely many canonical degrees of freedom, the Stone–von Neumann uniqueness statement is an irreducible regular-representation theorem (with the nonzero central character / fixed Planck-scale convention). It is not uniqueness for arbitrary algebraic modules, arbitrary Weyl states, or infinitely many field modes. Infinite-dimensional CCRs have inequivalent regular representations, with the van Hove model and AQFT examples illustrating physically material inequivalence. Conversely, Wintner-type arguments rule out bounded operators satisfying the usual nontrivial CCR; this is a helpful failure test, not a substitute for domain control.

## 2. Poisson structures and deformation quantization

### 2.1 Conventions and general Poisson delta

The local symplectic convention is fixed in `items/def-poisson-bracket-on-a-symplectic-manifold.md`: \(i_{X_H}\omega=dH\), \(\{F,G\}=\omega(X_F,X_G)=X_G(F)\), so Hamiltonian evolution is \(X_H(F)=\{F,H\}\). Preserve this exact sign when connecting to mechanics or to a star commutator. Existing coordinate, bilinearity/derivation, Jacobi, and smooth-function Poisson-algebra carriers are `items/prop-coordinate-formula-for-the-poisson-bracket.md`, `items/prop-poisson-bracket-is-bilinear-skew-and-a-derivation-in-each-entry.md`, `items/thm-poisson-bracket-satisfies-the-jacobi-identity.md`, and `items/thm-smooth-functions-form-a-poisson-algebra.md`.

The actual gap is broader than symplectic geometry: on a smooth manifold a Poisson bivector \(\pi\) may be degenerate, and the bracket on functions is \(\{f,g\}=\pi(df,dg)\). Jacobi is equivalent to the Schouten condition \([\pi,\pi]=0\). A symplectic-only definition cannot serve as a proof for a general Poisson manifold. The smallest added math route is a general bivector/bracket definition, the coordinate Jacobi criterion and equivalence to \([\pi,\pi]=0\), and examples that distinguish symplectic from degenerate Poisson structures.

### 2.2 Formal star products and proof contracts

A star product on \(C^\infty(M)[[\hbar]]\) is a **formal**, \(\hbar\)-adically interpreted associative product

\[
f\star g=fg+\sum_{r\ge1}\hbar^r B_r(f,g),\qquad
B_1(f,g)-B_1(g,f)= i\{f,g\}
\]

(or use a convention with a factor of \(1/2\), written explicitly). The first-order antisymmetric term must recover the named Poisson bracket. The series is not claimed to converge at a physical value of \(\hbar\). Equivalence means conjugation by an invertible formal differential operator \(T=\mathrm{id}+O(\hbar)\); equivalent products need not have identical higher coefficients.

For a constant Poisson tensor, the Moyal formula gives a direct finite-dimensional example and a short associativity proof via commuting constant-coefficient bidifferential operators. Fedosov gives a geometric construction for symplectic manifolds with a connection and Weyl-bundle recursion. Kontsevich’s theorem is stronger: an \(L_\infty\) formality quasi-isomorphism from polyvector fields to Hochschild cochains sends a Poisson Maurer–Cartan element to a formal star product on a general Poisson manifold. The graph formula and configuration-space boundary/Stokes argument are a major proof obligation; localizing a citation as though this were a routine calculation is not sufficient.

The physics connection through the Poisson sigma model is a **formal perturbative** route to graph weights and the star product. The cited lecture notes do not construct a rigorous path-integral measure. Likewise, the existing `physics/items/thm-qft-regular-wick-star-associativity.md` proves a local regular-functional Wick result and `physics/items/ex-qft-regular-quadratic-star-product.md` supplies an example; this is not Kontsevich formality and does not automatically justify products of singular distributions.

### 2.3 Minimal new suppliers

1. Define general Poisson manifolds using a bivector and Schouten/Jacobi criterion, with the existing symplectic sign dictionary.
2. Define formal associative star products, equivalence, and the first-order commutator with an explicit \(i\hbar\) convention; prove order-by-order that associativity at order \(\hbar^2\) forces the Jacobi identity.
3. Prove Moyal associativity and compute its first commutator on a polynomial/symplectic-plane example.
4. Use Fedosov and Kontsevich as literature context for the advanced route. An active local theorem must state complete hypotheses and contain a complete proof, or be split into locally proved steps. If the formality proof is outside scope, record it as a deferred authoring target rather than a logical supplier; the graph compactification, signs, boundary strata, and formal domain are part of that obligation.

## 3. Hall algebra: mandatory substantive route

### 3.1 Untwisted categorical counting, with normalization

Start with a small or essentially small abelian category \(\mathcal A\) whose Hom sets and Yoneda \(\operatorname{Ext}^1\) sets are finite. Work on a skeleton or the set \(\mathrm{Iso}(\mathcal A)\); do not assume enough projectives/injectives in order to define Ext. The local library has `items/def-n-fold-yoneda-extension.md`, extension and Baer-sum definitions, `items/def-extension-of-an-object-by-an-object-in-an-abelian-category.md`, `items/def-grothendieck-group-of-an-essentially-small-abelian-category.md`, and `items/thm-yoneda-ext-one-is-naturally-isomorphic-to-derived-ext-one.md`. Reuse these with the theorem’s extra resolution hypotheses visible. An untwisted Hall construction does not require \(\mathcal A\) to be \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear; this preserves classical categories such as finite abelian p-groups.

Let \(a_X=|\operatorname{Aut}(X)|\), and let \(P_{MN}^R\) be the finite set of short exact sequences \(0\to N\to R\to M\to0\) with the endpoint and middle objects fixed. Let \(g_{MN}^R\) count subobjects \(N'\subseteq R\) with \(N'\cong N\) and \(R/N'\cong M\). The endpoint automorphism group acts freely on \(P_{MN}^R\), giving \(g_{MN}^R=|P_{MN}^R|/(a_Ma_N)\). Thus the untwisted product is

\[
[M]*[N]=\sum_R g_{MN}^R[R]=\sum_R\frac{|P_{MN}^R|}{a_Ma_N}[R].
\]

This exact-sequence normalization should not be confused with a count of extension **classes** with fixed middle object: those classes have a different automorphism weighting. Schiffmann’s twisted convention multiplies the displayed coefficient by the square-root multiplicative Euler factor; a local page must choose and stick to one basis and convention.

Associativity of the untwisted subobject product is a finite flag count: both (([L]*[M])*[N]) and ([L]*([M]*[N])) count two-step flags in a common middle object, grouped by the intermediate quotient/subobject in the two possible orders. Finitarity ensures finite structure constants and finite extension sums. The general definition is supported by finite Hom/Ext¹ and a set of isoclasses, without presuming a field-linear Euler form.

The checkable normalization example is \(\mathcal A=\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbb F_q}^{\mathrm{fd}}\), simple \(S=\mathbb F_q\):

\[
[S]*[S]=(q+1)[\mathbb F_q^2],
\]

because the two-dimensional space has \((q^2-1)/(q-1)=q+1\) one-dimensional subspaces. In Schiffmann’s square-root-Euler normalization the coefficient is \(q^{1/2}(q+1)\). This difference is a normalization, not a disagreement in a counting theorem.

### 3.2 Euler twist, Hall–Littlewood, and quantum groups

Only after the untwisted construction, specialize to an \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear finitary abelian category. An Euler form \(\langle M,N\rangle=\prod_{i\ge0}|\operatorname{Ext}^i(M,N)|^{(-1)^i}\) needs finite all-Ext data (finite global dimension with the relevant Ext groups finite is one source hypothesis; hereditary categories are a common sufficient case). A square root and its multiplicative/bicharacter coherence must also be fixed before twisting. Merely having finite Hom and Ext¹ does **not** justify the Euler product or a half-power twist.

The classical Hall algebra of finite-dimensional nilpotent \(\mathbb F_q[t]\)-modules is the Jordan-quiver Hall algebra. Indecomposables are Jordan blocks, isoclasses are indexed by partitions, and its Hall numbers give Hall polynomials in this case. The generic classical Hall algebra identifies, after the source’s normalization, with the ring of symmetric functions; the partition basis maps, up to normalization, to Hall–Littlewood polynomials and the source identifies the corresponding Hall–Littlewood scalar product. This gives a concrete combinatorial module: the symmetric-function algebra acts on itself by multiplication, and its coproduct/pairing supplies adjoint operators. Identifying normalized power-sum creation and annihilation operators with a Heisenberg Fock representation is a further normalization theorem, not a consequence of the bare ring isomorphism. This is a substantive combinatorial interface, not a claim that finite abelian p-groups and nilpotent \(\mathbb F_q[t]\)-modules are the same category: their common partition/Hall-polynomial formulas require the relevant identification/parameter argument. The repository already has `items/def-hall-inner-product-on-symmetric-functions.md` and `items/cor-power-sums-are-orthogonal-for-the-hall-inner-product.md`; they are useful downstream, not a Hall multiplication proof.

For a finite quiver without loops, over a finite field and with Schiffmann’s nilpotent-representation convention when oriented cycles are present, Ringel’s map sends each simple class to a quantum-group generator. Every object has only finitely many subobjects, so here the Hall Hopf structure uses the ordinary tensor carrier rather than the completed Green target. After adjoining the Cartan group-like elements and fixing ν=√q, Green’s coproduct/pairing compatibility makes this an embedding of Hopf algebras from the extended positive Borel for the symmetric Kac–Moody algebra of the underlying graph; its image in the Hall algebra is the **composition subalgebra** generated by simple classes. Removing the Cartan part gives the positive half. The theorem states that this map is an isomorphism precisely in finite type, where the full Hall algebra equals the composition algebra. For affine and wild quivers, do not identify the full Hall algebra with the positive half: extra indecomposables contribute, and Schiffmann’s tame examples exhibit regular/central pieces. The source also notes versions for quivers with loops (Borcherds-type algebras) and species, but they are outside this route. Root/quantum-group items supply reusable presentations, not the missing Hall comparison proof.

### 3.3 Green coproduct: hereditary, abelian, completed

For a hereditary finitary abelian category, one standard twisted coproduct convention has

\[
\Delta([R])=\sum_{M,N}\epsilon(M,N)\,\frac{|P_{MN}^R|}{a_R}[M]\otimes[N]
=\sum_{M,N}\epsilon(M,N)\,\frac{a_Ma_N g_{MN}^R}{a_R}[M]\otimes[N],
\]

where \(\epsilon\) is the chosen square-root multiplicative Euler factor, with a grading by \(K_0(\mathcal A)\). The tensor product multiplication is twisted by the symmetrized Euler bicharacter; its exact order/sign must be written alongside this formula. The theorem is for **abelian** categories in the cited treatment; exact categories admit useful coproduct variants but that remark does not extend Green’s bialgebra compatibility theorem to every exact category.

The target is often a completed tensor product \(\widehat H\widehat\otimes\widehat H\), because one object may have infinitely many subobjects (for instance coherent sheaves on a projective curve). Finitary Hom/Ext¹ does not imply finitely many subobjects. The finite-subobjects condition is a separate hypothesis that can make this coproduct land in the ordinary tensor product. Before saying “bialgebra,” a supplier must specify the grading topology, coefficientwise finite sums, allowed completed products, and compatibility map.

This carrier is also distinct from the repository’s ordinary Hopf tensor product and finite double: `items/def-graded-bialgebra-and-hopf-algebra.md` uses the algebraic tensor product; `items/def-hh-leg-notation-and-quasitriangular-hopf-algebra.md` requires algebraic (R\in H\otimes H); `items/def-hh-finite-double-cross-relations.md` forms a finite-dimensional dual double. A Hall Green completion cannot be silently treated as any of these. Likewise generic quantum-group universal R-matrices and quiver CoHA/Yangian completions require their own completed carrier and action statements. The local `items/def-root-height-completion-for-weightwise-quasi-r-intertwiners.md` and `items/thm-the-weightwise-quasi-r-intertwiner-exists-and-is-unique.md` give a separate typed completion for weightwise quasi-R intertwiners; this does not turn the Hall Green completion into an ordinary Hopf tensor product.

Proof obligation: the local Green proof must expand the two counting identities and the “crosses to diagrams” bijection that Schiffmann imports or leaves as exercises, establish coassociativity and compatibility coefficientwise, and prove that the chosen topology supports each operation. Do not claim those omitted steps are supplied by merely linking the source.

### 3.4 Modules and actions are a separate representation theory

Three meanings of “representation” must remain clear:

1. An object M of category A is an input category object, not automatically a module for its Hall algebra.
2. A Hall module is a vector space with a separately defined action; in quiver cases it can arise by incidence correspondences or from a category action, and its formula/conditions need independent proof.
3. A geometric representation on homology/cohomology of a framed moduli space is yet another action, usually defined by correspondences and proper pushforward/suitable pullback.

A source-backed combinatorial example is the Jordan-quiver Hall algebra acting on the symmetric-function space by its regular action under the Hall–Littlewood identification above. To call this a Heisenberg Fock representation, also prove the normalized creation/annihilation commutators. A geometric example is Nakajima’s quiver variety construction: double a quiver, impose the moment-map equation, add framing and stability, form the smooth/projective resolution under hypotheses, and use Hecke correspondences to obtain a highest-weight Kac–Moody representation. The cited introduction assumes no edge loops for its ordinary Kac–Moody action. Yang–Zhao construct a preprojective CoHA and an action on oriented-cohomology homology of Nakajima varieties; the Yangian identification has no-edge-loop and, for the stated isomorphism, type-ADE/sign-twist/specialization conditions. Schiffmann–Vasserot’s advanced COHA/Yangian comparison uses equivariance and stable envelopes, and should be cited as a deep theorem, not treated as a consequence of ordinary Hall associativity.

Existing local reuse includes `items/thm-signed-klr-algebra-is-the-equivariant-ext-algebra-of-quiver-flag-complexes.md`, cyclotomic KLR induction/restriction and quotient items, quantum-group and quantum-shuffle definitions, and the shifted-Yangian type-A suppliers. These provide categorification and convolution techniques with their own stated rings, orientations, and grading conventions; a KLR Ext-convolution algebra is not automatically the COHA of a preprojective algebra.

### 3.5 Derived, motivic, cohomological Hall, and DT/BPS

**Derived Hall.** Toën’s construction uses a dg enhancement over a finite field with finiteness of total Hom cohomology and locally finite homotopy types. Multiplication counts homotopy cones with higher homotopy weights; associativity follows from homotopy-cartesian diagrams. Its formula involves shifted Hom groups, not just ordinary triangles weighted only by automorphism cardinalities. The heart recovers ordinary Hall under the stated t-structure conditions. Kapranov’s lattice Hall algebra is related but is not Toën’s derived Hall algebra; higher Hom factors are decisive. Neither is obtained by naïvely counting triangles in an unenhanced triangulated category.

**Motivic Hall.** Stack-function or motivic integration requires a chosen coefficient ring/measure, treatment of stabilizers, localization, and often completion. Orientation data and vanishing-cycle conventions matter when passing to 3-Calabi–Yau Donaldson–Thomas invariants. Formal wall-crossing is organized in a completed graded/pro-nilpotent Lie or quantum torus algebra, with support/stability assumptions; it is a separate structure from the categorical category-O wall-crossing functors already in the library.

**Cohomological Hall algebra (CoHA).** For representations of a quiver, a typical construction takes equivariant cohomology of representation stacks/varieties and defines a product by a correspondence of short exact sequences. Grading shifts, equivariance, pullback/pushforward, and completion are essential to associativity. A potential introduces a critical-locus/vanishing-cycle theory and extra orientation/monodromic data. The Kontsevich–Soibelman source explicitly distinguishes off-shell/rapid-decay from on-shell critical/vanishing-cycle versions; do not collapse these into one algebra.

**BPS and physical interface.** In a supersymmetric quiver quantum mechanics model, charge lattice, central charge, stability condition, potential/superpotential, Calabi–Yau dimension, and boundary/orientation data select a problem. Hall/CoHA/DT structures can encode wall crossing and BPS invariants. The mathematical wall-crossing identity under its stated stability/completion assumptions is not by itself a theorem that a particular algebra object equals the physical BPS Hilbert space, nor does it identify category-O wall crossing with DT wall crossing. State model assumptions separately and label physical identifications as conjectural/proposed when the source does.

## 4. Graded, A∞, L∞, BV, and BRST structures

The local graded/homological base is already useful: `items/def-category-of-chain-complexes.md`, `items/def-tensor-product-total-complex-of-chain-complexes.md`, `items/def-bounded-complex-of-graded-bimodules-and-signed-tensor-totalization.md`, `items/def-graded-ext-algebra-of-an-object.md`, bar/Hochschild items and `library/homological-algebra/hochschild-homology-and-diagonal-koszul-resolutions.md`, differential-form/wedge pages, `items/ex-the-sign-braiding-on-supervector-spaces.md`, `items/def-graded-bialgebra-and-hopf-algebra.md`, and `items/def-bigraded-matrix-factorization-with-potential.md`.

The smallest new graded convention contract must declare cohomological or homological grading, the Koszul sign, suspension convention, and whether structure maps are completed. Keller’s convention treats an A∞ algebra as operations \(m_n\) of degree \(2-n\) obeying the signed Stasheff relations; \(m_1^2=0\), the \(m_1,m_2\) identity is the differential Leibniz law, and the \(n=3\) relation makes associativity hold up to the homotopy \(m_3\). An ordinary dg algebra is the special case with \(m_{n>2}=0\). Minimal models transfer structure rather than simply erase it, and the transferred \(m_3\) records triple-Massey information.

For an \(L_\infty\) algebra, shift/sign choices matter. In the coderivation model, a degree-one square-zero coderivation on the cofree cocommutative coalgebra packages brackets \(l_n\) with the Koszul-shifted Jacobi identities. \(l_1\) is the differential, \(l_2\) the bracket up to homotopy, and \(l_3\) witnesses its Jacobiator. Maurer–Cartan elements solve \(\sum_{n\ge1}l_n(x^n)/n!=0\); using this equation requires nilpotence or a stated filtration/completion for the infinite sum. The phrase “\(L_\infty\)” must not be confused with the false-positive `items/def-l-infinity-on-a-measure-space.md`, which is an \(L^\infty\) function space.

A BV algebra needs a graded-commutative product and an odd square-zero operator of a declared degree; the derived bracket is controlled by the second-order condition on the operator. In field theory, the classical/quantum master equation depends on the odd symplectic or BV Laplacian setup and regularization; the formal identity alone is not anomaly cancellation. BRST is a cochain/constraint model whose nilpotence and cohomology require the relevant gauge algebra and quantum correction hypotheses.

No dedicated A∞, L∞, or BV algebra carrier was found by the focused search; the `lem-a-infinity-power-decay` filename is unrelated. Suggested new items are (a) convention-locked A∞ definition + dg example + one minimal-model/Massey example; (b) coalgebra/coderivation L∞ definition + filtered MC gauge example; (c) BV operator/order-two identity and derived bracket + finite-dimensional odd-symplectic example; (d) separate BRST/master-equation physics application with regularization caveats. Deep homotopy-transfer, operadic, and quantum BV theorems should be source-linked and marked for later proof audit.

## 5. Vertex, chiral, Virasoro, and W algebras

A vertex algebra is not merely an associative algebra of modes. It is a vector space with a vacuum, translation operator, and state-field map Y(a,z)=sum over n in Z of a_(n) z^(-n-1), with formal lower-truncation and locality/Jacobi constraints. Formal delta-function expansions and the ordering region for fields must be fixed. The equivalent Lie-conformal lambda-bracket language has its own sesquilinearity, skewsymmetry, and Jacobi identities. A Virasoro algebra is the canonical central-extension example; free boson/fermion constructions provide checkable field examples.

A W algebra can be defined as a quantum Drinfeld–Sokolov/BRST cohomology of an affine vertex algebra with nilpotent-orbit data and level assumptions. Classical Hamiltonian reduction, finite W algebras, affine W vertex algebras, Zhu algebras, and their module categories are related but distinct. Lisse/rationality and modular tensor category conclusions require their hypotheses; not every vertex algebra has semisimple or finite module theory, and logarithmic examples lie outside the rational route.

Focused search found no affine-vertex-algebra/VOA state-field or chiral-algebra core. Finite type-A W-algebra carriers do exist—`items/def-the-type-a-finite-w-algebra-and-whittaker-category.md`, `items/def-deformed-tensor-space-for-the-type-a-finite-w-algebra.md`, and the type-A shifted-Yangian definition/PBW suppliers—so reuse them with their exact type and scope restrictions. They do not provide the affine vertex-algebra vacuum/state-field/locality axioms. Reuse affine Lie algebra, graded complexes, BRST-adjacent, and braided/fusion-category infrastructure for that missing layer. The read sources give bounded frontier evidence: Arakawa’s introduction reviews principal W reductions and a spectral-sequence proof segment; the 2025 boundary-W paper establishes selected q-character/modular-data and type-A fusion conclusions under rational/lisse/admissible hypotheses and warns that equality of modular data does not imply isomorphism of vertex algebras; the September 2026 Arakawa–Dai preprint announces classical freeness for the indicated boundary-admissible affine sl_2 levels, proved via Hilbert-series/character comparison. The latter proof was not audited here. See the manifest for exact versions and read pages.

## 6. Brauer, BMW, and partition algebras

These diagram algebras extend Schur–Weyl centralizers beyond the symmetric/Hecke setting. The Brauer algebra has pairings of (2k) boundary points with concatenation and a loop parameter; it is the orthogonal/symplectic tensor centralizer in the stable range with exact dimension/parameter hypotheses. BMW is a deformation with braid/cup-cap generators and a tangle interpretation; its basis, cellular/canonical basis, and parameter specialization need dedicated proofs.
