## Statement

Assume AC. Let $M$ be a compact connected oriented irreducible PL three-manifold with a supplied finite manifold triangulation. Irreducible means that every locally flat PL sphere in its interior bounds a PL ball in its interior. Suppose at least one component of $\partial M$ has positive genus. No boundary-incompressibility or boundary disk-ball property is assumed.

There is a compact connected orientable two-sided properly embedded locally flat PL surface $F$ with
$$[\partial F]\ne0\quad\text{in }H_1(\partial M;\mathbb Z),$$
with a finite compatible triangulation and product collar, having all the following properties. It has no compressing disk and no boundary-compressing disk. Every component of $\partial F$ is essential in its component of $\partial M$. Either $F$ is a disk with essential boundary, or $\chi(F)\le0$. It has no embedded locally flat PL compressing annulus: this means an annulus $A$ with $A\cap F$ equal to one boundary circle $\gamma$, its other boundary circle on $\partial M$, $\operatorname{int}A\subset\operatorname{int}M\setminus F$, injected core group in $\pi_1(M)$, and $\gamma$ not isotopic in $F$ to a component of $\partial F$. End circles initially touching the boundary of $F$ are allowed in this definition. Thus $F$ is superincompressible with essential disks allowed.

The surface group injects into $M$; cutting along its collar gives irreducible pieces whose groups inject into $M$, and both labeled cutting faces inject into their incident pieces. This proves existence of one actual cut, including on boundary-compressible intermediates. It asserts neither normalization relative to a handle decomposition or a prescribed pared locus nor termination of a hierarchy.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC and the finite oriented irreducible PL manifold with a positive-genus boundary component.

[F1] Cap with the relative fundamental class identifies $H^1(M;R)$ with $H_2(M,\partial M;R)$ for $R=\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q$. Its boundary identity identifies $\partial(u\cap[M,\partial M])$ with $\pm i^*u\cap[\partial M]$; cap duality also holds on the compact oriented boundary. [[thm-poincare-lefschetz-duality]]

[F2] The homology sequence of the pair is exact, and the finite cellular chain and cochain complexes compute homology and cohomology for constant coefficients. [[thm-long-exact-sequence-of-a-pair-in-singular-homology]] [[thm-cellular-chains-compute-homology-with-local-coefficients]] [[thm-cellular-cochains-compute-cohomology-with-local-coefficients]]

[F3] A connected compact orientable surface with genus $g$ and $b$ boundary circles has $\chi=2-2g-b$, obtained by capping those circles and applying closed-surface classification. A nullhomotopic polygonal circle bounds a PL disk, and each boundary circle of a finite oriented surface other than a disk injects as an infinite cyclic subgroup. [[thm-classification-of-compact-connected-surfaces]] [[lem-nullhomotopic-circles-on-finite-oriented-pl-surfaces-bound-pl-disks]]

[F4] Polygonal disks have PL parameters extending prescribed boundary parameters. Proper polygonal arcs have disk-and-strip neighborhoods. Compact locally collared PL faces have PL collars, and all finitely many embedded PL polyhedra and maps used below admit finite compatible subdivisions. [[lem-finite-polygonal-disk-and-collar-surgery]] [[lem-compact-locally-collared-pl-subpolyhedron-has-pl-collar]] [[lem-pl-embedded-polyhedron-admits-compatible-subcomplex-triangulation]]

[F5] For finite compatible two-sided PL surfaces, absence of a compressing disk is equivalent to injectivity of every surface component; face and piece injections follow. Injective cuts with no closed simply connected component preserve irreducibility. [[lem-finite-pl-two-sided-incompressibility-is-pi-one-injectivity]] [[lem-finite-pi1-injective-surface-cuts-preserve-three-manifold-irreducibility]]

[A1] Full AC is retained for the duality and surface-classification interfaces. All new minimizations below are over nonnegative integers. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

## Proof

1.1 Work first over $\mathbb Q$. The finite chain complex has the perfect evaluation pairing $H^1(M;\mathbb Q)\times H_1(M;\mathbb Q)\to\mathbb Q$: a functional on cycles vanishing on boundaries extends to the finite chain space, and a cocycle vanishing on cycles is a coboundary by extending its induced functional from the boundary image. Suppose the boundary map $\partial:H_2(M,\partial M;\mathbb Q)\to H_1(\partial M;\mathbb Q)$ were zero. By [F1], for every $u\in H^1(M;\mathbb Q)$ one would have $i^*u\cap[\partial M]=0$, and boundary duality would give $i^*u=0$. Perfect evaluation would then make $i_*:H_1(\partial M;\mathbb Q)\to H_1(M;\mathbb Q)$ zero. Exactness in [F2], together with the supposed zero boundary map, makes this same map injective. This contradicts the positive-genus boundary component, whose first rational homology has rank $2g>0$ by [F3]. Thus choose a relative rational class with nonzero boundary. Clear its finitely many chain denominators to obtain an integral relative class $\alpha$ whose boundary is nonzero even rationally. [A1, F1, F2, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Represent the cap-dual integral class by a simplicial one-cocycle $u$ using [F1]–[F2]. Order the vertices. On a simplex $[v_0,\ldots,v_q]$ put $a_0=0$, $a_j=u([v_0,v_j])$, and send barycentric coordinates $(t_j)$ to $\sum_jt_ja_j$ modulo $\mathbb Z$. The triangle cocycle identity says $a_j-a_i=u([v_i,v_j])$, so formulas on common faces differ by integral constants. They define a finite piecewise-affine map $f:M\to\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$. The inverse image of $1/2$ consists in each simplex of finitely many affine level sections. No section meets a face where the affine function is constant, since such a constant is an integer. At a level point, the smallest containing simplex therefore has a direction on which that affine function is nonconstant. Its finite star gives a transverse level coordinate and a local PL product; in a boundary half-star it gives the corresponding half-product. Consequently the level set is a finite, oriented, properly embedded locally flat PL surface. Its signed intersections with an oriented edge count the integer $u$ on that edge. On a subdivided loop the same counts telescope to the evaluation of $u$. The local cap description in [F1] therefore identifies its relative class with $\alpha$, and its boundary represents $\partial\alpha\ne0$. Some connected component has nonzero boundary class. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Consider all connected finite oriented proper locally flat PL surfaces $S\subset M$ with $[\partial S]\ne0$. Step2.1 makes this collection nonempty. No such surface is closed. For each put $D(S)=\max(0,-\chi(S))$, $G(S)=g(S)$ and $a(S)=1$ for an annulus and zero otherwise. Choose a surface minimizing the lexicographic triple $(D,G,a)\in\mathbb N^3$. Three successive least-element choices give an attained minimum. Denote a minimizer by $F$. Whenever a surgery below gives a disconnected surface with the same total boundary homology class, at least one connected component has nonzero boundary class; it is again eligible. The sum of the triples of the new components bounds the triple of any retained component coordinatewise. The entries are all nonnegative and additive under disjoint union. [F3, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 A compressing disk replaces an annular strip by two disks; a boundary-compressing disk replaces a strip along the surface arc by two parallel copies of the disk. Small product neighborhoods give embedded oriented PL surgeries by [F4]. The total boundary homology class is preserved: the first surgery leaves the boundary unchanged, and in the second the boundary cycles differ by the boundary of the thin strip along the disk's arc on $\partial M$. A connected component with nonzero boundary class therefore survives. If $\chi(F)<0$, an ordinary compression increases total Euler characteristic by two. An essential loop cannot split off a sphere; if a disk is split off, the other component gains one in Euler characteristic. After omitting zero-boundary-class sphere components, the total $D$ strictly decreases. A boundary compression increases total Euler characteristic by one; an essential separating arc cannot split off a disk, whereas a nonseparating arc leaves a connected result. Again total $D$ strictly decreases. If $F$ is an annulus, an ordinary or essential boundary compression gives disks, whose summed triple is $(0,0,0)$ instead of $(0,0,1)$. The remaining orientable possibilities from [F3] are a disk or a torus; a torus is ineligible because its boundary class is zero, and every proper arc of a disk cuts off a disk. A disk's boundary is essential in $\partial M$ since its class there is nonzero. Thus a minimizer admits neither kind of compression. [F3, F4, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 We detail the annular surgery, including its end normalization. A collar of $\partial M$ compatible with $F$ has $F=(\partial F)\times[0,a]$ in it. To construct such a collar, at a boundary vertex the link of the proper surface is a polygonal arc in the ambient half-link disk. Each side of that arc is a polygonal disk: adjoin the corresponding boundary interval and apply [F4]. Prescribe the same parameter along the shared arc and extend over both disks. Coning straightens the link to a marked diameter and supplies a simultaneous surface/half-space chart. In the finite collar construction from [F4], subdivide with these marked strata as subcomplexes; the cone-vertex pushes preserve the marked surface and produce the compatible collar. If the inner end $\gamma$ of a putative compressing annulus meets $\partial F$, choose a PL function $\lambda$ on $\partial M$ positive near that compact intersection and zero near the disjoint outer end. On a smaller collar replace each vertical interval $[0,a]$ by $[\epsilon\lambda(x),a]$, fixing its top, where $\epsilon\lambda<a$. Over each base simplex the target is a convex prism between an affine lower graph and a constant upper graph. Boundary parameters extend by coning compatible subdivisions, giving a PL inward embedding preserving vertical fibers. Extend it by identity beyond the collar. This carries $F$ into itself and its complement off $F$, moves $\gamma$ into $\operatorname{int}F$ and fixes the outer annulus end. Reducing the lower graph to zero gives a homotopy to inclusion and an isotopy of $\gamma$ in $F$, preserving core injection and its non-boundary-parallel isotopy class. [F3, F4, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Extend the collar height by the constant $a$ outside it and restrict it to the new annulus. It is a finite PL function zero exactly on its outer boundary circle. Choose a positive level $\delta$ below all positive vertex heights and below the inner end. In every boundary edge strip the level is one arc, and the finite boundary vertex fans connect successive arcs once in their circular order. It is therefore one circle parallel to the outer end. That circle lies in the ambient collar level $\delta$, whose projection to $\partial M$ is a homeomorphism and misses $\partial F$. Truncate the annulus there and append the vertical annulus down to $\partial M$. The retained part has height at least $\delta$, the appended part at most $\delta$, so the replacement is embedded and its interior still misses $F$. Its core is homotopic to the old one. At the inner end the annulus link is a proper polygonal arc in the hemisphere cut by $F$; at the outer end it is such an arc in a boundary half-link disk. The two disk parameters from step 5.1 straighten these links relative their endpoint and equator strata. Cone the maps and take finite compatible subdivisions. The annulus sides are now locally collared faces, including their specified end strata. Applying [F4]'s collar theorem and restoring the end charts yields a two-sided product neighborhood $A\times[-\eta,\eta]$ meeting $F$ in the inner standard strip and $\partial M$ in the outer strip. Orientability makes the two normal-side labels consistent around the annulus. [F4, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 In that product remove the strip of $F$ around $\gamma$ and replace it by the two annular side faces running to $\partial M$. Finite subdivisions give embedded oriented proper surfaces. The core injection of the annulus makes $\gamma$ nontrivial in $\pi_1(M)$, hence in $\pi_1(F)$; thus it does not bound a disk in $F$. Topologically the new surface is $F$ cut along $\gamma$, with boundary collars appended. The two new outer end curves are parallel on $\partial M$ with opposite orientations. Their homology classes cancel, while every old boundary curve is unchanged. Total boundary homology is therefore still $[\partial F]\ne0$. If $\gamma$ is nonseparating, the connected result has the same Euler characteristic and genus one less. Its triple is smaller. If $\gamma$ separates, neither side is a disk by essentiality and neither side is an annulus, since that would make $\gamma$ boundary-parallel in $F$. Both sides have negative Euler characteristic by [F3]. Their total negative Euler characteristic equals $D(F)$ and each contributes a positive integer. The component with nonzero boundary class consequently has strictly smaller $D$. On an annulus every essential circle is boundary-parallel: cutting its two capped disks from the sphere and using [F3] shows that it separates the two caps and cobounds an annulus with each original boundary. A disk has no injected essential core, and a torus is ineligible. Thus every possible compressing annulus contradicts the minimum. [F3, F4, step 3.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 Every boundary circle of $F$ is essential in $\partial M$. Otherwise take an innermost one among boundary circles bounding disks there, with a boundary disk $E$ whose interior misses $\partial F$; every circle inside a disk also bounds a disk, so this finite innermost choice is possible. In the compatible collar, push $E$ to a small positive level. Its interior misses $F$, and its boundary is a circle parallel to the chosen boundary circle in $F$. If $F$ is not a disk, [F3]'s boundary subgroup assertion makes that circle essential in $F$, producing a compressing disk contrary to step 3.1. If $F$ is a disk, its single boundary has nonzero boundary homology and cannot bound $E$. Finally [F3] excludes a connected orientable positive-Euler surface other than a disk or a sphere, and a sphere has zero boundary class. Thus $F$ has exactly the disk-or-nonpositive-Euler alternatives stated. [F3, F4, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 The level constructions and finite surgeries retain compatible finite PL triangulations. The verified local product charts and [F4] supply the two-sided proper collar of $F$. Steps 4.1 and 7.1 prove superincompressibility, including the initially unnormalized annulus ends. By [F5], absence of a compressing disk gives injection of $\pi_1(F)$ and the incident face maps. The irreducibility-preserving cut theorem applies: $F$ is proper with nonempty boundary, so it has no closed simply connected component. It gives irreducibility and piece-group injection for each resulting piece. This completes one actual cutting-surface construction without a boundary disk-ball premise, a handle-normal-position premise, or a supplied hierarchy. [F4, F5, step 2.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 7.1, step 8.1, construct] ∎

## Limits for hierarchy use

Selecting this surface again in a later non-ball piece does not by itself prove that the resulting process terminates. A separately proved normalization and a strictly decreasing global handle complexity are required. A prescribed pared locus has not been normalized or transported here; no conclusion about the pared axioms of a cut pair or about peripheral marking equivalences is asserted.
