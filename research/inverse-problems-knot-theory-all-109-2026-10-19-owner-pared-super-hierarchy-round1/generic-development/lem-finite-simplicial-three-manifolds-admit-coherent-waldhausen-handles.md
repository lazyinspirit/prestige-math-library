## Statement

Let $T$ be a supplied finite simplicial manifold triangulation of a compact PL three-manifold $M$, possibly with boundary. Thus vertex links are PL two-spheres or PL disks, edge links are PL circles or PL intervals, and triangle links are two points or one point, with the boundary cases precisely the boundary simplices. Then $M$ has a finite parametrized ball–beam–plate handle decomposition satisfying all incidence and product-agreement requirements in [[lem-normal-handle-cuts-strictly-decrease-finite-complexity]]. All handles, faces and parameters are finite PL after compatible subdivisions. Each vertex supplies one zero-handle, each edge one beam, each triangle one plate, and each tetrahedron one three-handle. The three-handles are disjoint from $\partial M$ and are attached along their entire boundary. No triangulation or smoothing of an arbitrary topological manifold is asserted, and no surface-normalization or hierarchy-existence claim is included.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite simplicial manifold triangulation and its stated standard PL links.

[F1] Finite convex cell complexes have compatible finite triangulations by coning compatible boundary subdivisions from relative interior points. [[lem-finite-convex-cell-complexes-admit-compatible-triangulations]]

[F2] The ball–beam–plate incidence and product conventions are those explicitly stated in [[lem-normal-handle-cuts-strictly-decrease-finite-complexity]]. Only those conventions, not that lemma's cutting conclusion, are used here.

## Proof

1.1 Fix $a=1/10$, $b=1/100$ and $c=1/1000$. On each tetrahedron use barycentric coordinates $\lambda_v$. Start by assigning to a vertex $v$ the regions $\lambda_v\ge1-a$ in all its incident tetrahedra. They agree on common faces and form the truncated cone on its link. The supplied PL sphere or disk link parameter makes this cone a parametrized PL ball: coning a subdivided sphere parameter gives a standard ball, and coning a disk parameter gives a standard half-ball, also a PL ball. Distinct vertex regions are disjoint because $2(1-a)>1$. Declare their boundaries to be retained faces of both adjacent handles whenever applicable. [F1, given, construct]

2.1 For an edge $uv$, take the remaining region $\lambda_u+\lambda_v\ge1-b$ in its incident tetrahedra, excluding interiors of the vertex handles. Write $\rho=\sum_{w\notin\{u,v\}}\lambda_w$, so $0\le\rho\le b$ and $a-\rho\le\lambda_v\le1-a$. Its transverse base is the cone of radius $b$ on the edge link, a PL disk whether the link is a circle or an interval. The interval in the edge direction has positive length $1-2a+\rho$. These regions form a beam over that transverse disk. Here is a finite PL product construction, without using the generally non-PL division by that length. Triangulate the transverse disk so $\rho$ is affine on each simplex. Over a base simplex the two endpoint graphs of $\lambda_v$ are affine, and the region between them has the face lattice of that simplex times an interval. Triangulate its boundary compatibly and cone as in [F1], matching it to the analogous subdivided product prism face by face. This gives a PL product relative to the base faces, and the choices agree over adjacent base simplices. Its two ends are exactly its intersections with the $u$ and $v$ zero-handles. Two edge regions sharing a vertex could meet only where that vertex coordinate is at least $1-2b>1-a$, already removed; disjoint edges cannot both have coordinate sum at least $1-b$. Thus distinct beams are disjoint. [F1, step 1.1, given, construct]

3.1 For a triangle $uvw$ in a tetrahedron with remaining vertex $z$, put $r=\lambda_z$. Its plate consists of $0\le r\le c$ outside the interiors of the vertex and edge handles. On a section at height $r$ its three other coordinates obey $$\lambda_u+\lambda_v+\lambda_w=1-r,\qquad b-r\le\lambda_i\le1-a\quad(i=u,v,w).$$ Constraints from edges involving $z$ are redundant since $\lambda_i+r\le1-a+c<1-b$. The section is a hexagon: at a vertex where $\lambda_u=1-a$ and $\lambda_w=b-r$, the remaining coordinate is $a-b$; all six permutations occur. The inequalities $c<b$, $a>2b$ and $1-a>a-b>b-r>0$ verify that the six vertices are distinct and give three alternating upper and three lower sides. Upper sides belong to zero-handles; lower sides belong to beams. The hexagon vertices move affinely with $r$. Its prism is consequently a PL disk product over $[0,c]$, obtained by the same compatible face-prism construction as in step2.1. For an interior triangle join its two incident prisms across $r=0$ and use the signed normal interval $[-c,c]$; a boundary triangle has just $[0,c]$, with its bottom disk on $\partial M$. If two different triangle regions in a tetrahedron met, two coordinates would be at most $c$, so the other two would have sum at least $1-2c>1-b$, already in a beam or a zero-handle. Therefore distinct plates are disjoint. [F1, step 2.1, given, construct]

4.1 Check the required product agreement, rather than only the topology of the handles. On the intersection of the $uv$ beam and the $uvw$ plate, $\lambda_w+\lambda_z=b$, so $\rho=b$, and the edge coordinate satisfies $a-b\le\lambda_v\le1-a$. The other coordinate is the plate's signed normal coordinate: $\lambda_z\in[0,c]$ on one tetrahedron, with the negative coordinate on the other side if present. Thus this common rectangle has actual coordinates $(\lambda_v,\text{signed }r)$, with constant independent bounds. The marked arc on the beam's transverse disk is this interval of normal directions at $\rho=b$. Different marked arcs have nonempty gaps: in any link sector two endpoint bands of size $c$ cannot meet because $2c<b$. At a boundary endpoint of the link the marked arc can have an endpoint on the radial boundary of the transverse disk; its neighboring gap is the corresponding free radial side. In the product constructions of steps2.1 and3.1 prescribe precisely this rectangle parameter on every such rectangle. These prescriptions agree on their edges and vertices and are product parameters on the abstract beam and plate models. Apply [F1]'s boundary-first coning construction to extend the prescriptions over each remaining face and then each cell: matching face subdivisions and matching interior cone points define a PL homeomorphism of the two finite cell models. Hence both handle parameters induce literally the same two product coordinates on every common rectangle. The three upper and three lower hexagon sides are the required nonempty alternating zero/one attachment arcs of a plate. [F1, step 2.1, step 3.1, given, construct]

5.1 The remaining region in a tetrahedron is the convex polytope $$\lambda_i\ge c,\qquad\lambda_i\le1-a,\qquad\lambda_i+\lambda_j\le1-b\quad(i\ne j),\qquad\sum_i\lambda_i=1.$$ It has nonempty interior, since the barycenter satisfies all inequalities strictly. A compact full-dimensional convex polytope is a parametrized PL ball: triangulate its boundary and cone from an interior point, matching this cone to a standard convex ball cone by [F1]. This is its three-handle. It misses every original tetrahedron face, since every $\lambda_i\ge c>0$, so distinct three-handles are disjoint and none meets $\partial M$. Each facet is on an equality belonging to a zero-handle, beam or plate. Thus its entire boundary is attached to the lower handles. The construction covers each tetrahedron: assign first its vertex regions, then edge regions outside them, then face regions outside those, and finally the displayed residual region. The defining inequalities are identical on common original faces, so these assignments agree globally. [F1, step 4.1, given, construct]

6.1 Cut every constituent polytope along all its finitely many indicated supporting planes and take their common face subdivision. Use [F1] to triangulate this finite cell system compatibly, including the product subdivisions fixed in step4.1. This makes every described handle and every face map finite PL. Near a point of a seam the local model is the corresponding subdivided linear sector in the original triangulation; joining the sectors recovers its original ball or half-ball chart. Hence the face gluing has the required manifold corner charts. Since a three-handle has no free face, a boundary arc of a three-handle region on a zero-handle can leave that zero-handle only across a beam or plate seam: in the subdivided boundary cell system any transition is precisely through one of these lower attachment regions. This gives the last three-handle seam requirement in [F2]. The end faces of beams, alternating plate sides and coherent common products were checked in steps2.1–4.1, and step5.1 checked the three-handles and full coverage. These verify every asserted incidence condition. All choices concern finitely many link parameters and cell points, so no choice axiom or unprovided manifold conversion is required. [F1, F2, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, given, construct] ∎

## Scope

This supplies an initial finite handle system for a given PL triangulation. The strict-cut lemma applies only after actual cutting surfaces have the stated normal position in this system and its inherited successors. Constructing that position for the required superincompressible surfaces and essential disks remains a separate authoring obligation. No paired-boundary marking, pared cut update, or original hyperbolization consumer is certified here.
