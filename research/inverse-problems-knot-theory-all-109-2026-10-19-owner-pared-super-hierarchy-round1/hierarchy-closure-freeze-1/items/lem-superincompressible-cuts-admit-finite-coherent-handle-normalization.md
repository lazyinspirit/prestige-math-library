## Statement

Let $M$ be a compact PL three-manifold with a supplied finite coherent ball–beam–plate decomposition in [[lem-normal-handle-cuts-strictly-decrease-finite-complexity]]. Assume every interior locally flat PL sphere bounds an interior parametrized PL ball. Either of the following inputs supplies an actual normal cutting surface in this same handle system.

* If $M$ has a proper locally flat PL disk with essential boundary in $\partial M$, there exists such a disk $G$ in the normal position specified in that lemma. The new disk need not be isotopic to the initially available disk.
* Suppose every proper locally flat PL disk cobounds a parametrized PL ball with a disk in $\partial M$. Let $S$ be a connected compact oriented two-sided proper locally flat PL surface, neither a sphere nor a disk, which is injective on fundamental groups, has no boundary-compressing disk with essential proper arc, has nonempty boundary, and all its boundary circles are essential in $\partial M$. Then an ambient PL isotopy preserving $\partial M$ setwise takes $S$ to that normal position. In particular it preserves the absence of an injective compressing annulus and every other incompressibility property of $S$.

Normal position means: miss three-handles; full disk layers in plates; product strips $I\times K$ in beams, with no circle components of $K$ and no arc having endpoints on the same marked arc, the same gap, or an adjacent marked arc and gap; and disjoint proper disk pieces in zero-handles. All moves are finite. The handle system is never replaced by a new triangulation. No pared-boundary or hierarchy assertion is included.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The fixed coherent handles and one of the two surface inputs in the Statement.

[F1] The handle coordinates and common rectangle parameters are those in [[lem-normal-handle-cuts-strictly-decrease-finite-complexity]].

[F2] Empty full folds remove two plate layers; empty adjacent half-folds at a free plate end remove one, by ambient PL isotopy. [[lem-empty-plate-folds-admit-finite-pl-removal]]

[F3] Proper disks cut a parametrized ball into parametrized balls. Interior cap surgeries on an injective nondisk surface in an irreducible manifold are empty-ball isotopies after discarding their trivial sphere. In a boundary-disk-ball manifold, a boundary surgery with an inessential surface arc is an empty-boundary-ball isotopy after discarding the boundary-parallel disk. The complete local constructions are proved in Steps1.1,1.3 and2.2 of [[lem-finite-pl-incompressible-boundary-incompressible-surfaces-admit-normal-isotopies]]; only those local ball arguments are used, not an inference from simplicial normality to handle normality.

[F4] Nullhomotopic simple PL circles on finite oriented PL surfaces bound disks. Polygonal disks, strips, boundary windows and their prescribed PL maps have finite compatible parameters. [[lem-nullhomotopic-circles-on-finite-oriented-pl-surfaces-bound-pl-disks]] [[lem-finite-polygonal-disk-and-collar-surgery]] [[lem-parametrized-pl-balls-absorb-standard-boundary-windows]]

[F5] Compact locally collared PL faces have product collars; finite PL polyhedra have compatible subdivisions, and finite convex cells have compatible conical triangulations. [[lem-compact-locally-collared-pl-subpolyhedron-has-pl-collar]] [[lem-pl-embedded-polyhedron-admits-compatible-subcomplex-triangulation]] [[lem-finite-convex-cell-complexes-admit-compatible-triangulations]]

## Proof

1.1 Work with a surface $F$ of the chosen type. In the disk branch it is any available essential disk, and later surgeries may select a different essential disk. In the other branch all later surgeries will be actual isotopies of the original $S$. Put the finite PL surface and all handle faces in general position by finite compatible subdivisions and small vertex moves in their linear ball or half-ball charts. To avoid a three-handle choose a point of its interior outside $F$, and a small closed ball there disjoint from $F$. A PL radial expansion of that ball across the parametrized three-handle and a small outer collar, fixed on the outer collar boundary, moves $F$ outside the three-handle. Its parameter is obtained by extending concentric boundary-disk parameters and using [F4]'s finite ball extension; the radial breakpoints give a finite PL isotopy. The three-handles are disjoint interior balls, so their collars can be disjoint. Perform these moves successively. The surface now misses all three-handles. [F1, F4, F5, given, construct]

2.1 A plate end is either a free disk in $\partial M$ or a face incident to a three-handle. No boundary circle of $F$ is contained in a free plate-end disk, because every such circle is essential. Move $\partial F$ off these finitely many free disks by innermost arc pushes: a proper arc in a disk cuts off a disk whose interior misses the other arcs after choosing an innermost one; push it just outside that disk across its collar. Each push removes two intersections with the disk rim. After finitely many pushes no curve enters a free plate end. Extend each boundary isotopy over the boundary collar of $M$, tapering it to the identity at the inner collar face. The three-handle ends were already avoided. Thus $F$ misses the closed plate-end disks. [F4, F5, step 1.1, given, construct]

3.1 Straighten one plate $D\times I$ as follows. Choose its interval axis $\{p\}\times I$ transverse to $F$, with finitely many intersection points at distinct interior levels; finite PL general position provides this choice. A sufficiently small disk $D_0$ about $p$ meets the surface only in finitely many disjoint sheets transverse to the interval fibers, because the rest of the compact surface avoids the axis. Triangulating their linear graph patches straightens these sheets to $D_0\times\{t_j\}$ by an interval-fiber isotopy fixed near the two ends. Expand the small disk column to fill the plate. More explicitly, add a thin lateral collar to $D$, available in the adjacent zero-handles and beams; choose nested polygonal disks $D_0\subset\operatorname{int}D\subset\operatorname{int}D_+$ there. A radial PL disk homeomorphism taking $D_0$ to $D$ and fixed on $\partial D_+$ extends as a product on the levels containing $F$, and tapers to the identity near the two end disks, where a whole neighborhood misses $F$ by step2.1. The radial annuli have finite prism subdivisions by [F5]. This moves all old surface outside the small column into the adjacent handles, leaving exactly the full layers $D\times\{t_j\}$ inside the plate. The lateral collar misses three-handles; its end taper fixes their faces. Distinct plates have disjoint collars after shrinking, so repeat for all plates. [F1, F4, F5, step 2.1, given, construct]

4.1 Straighten a beam $I\times D$ by choosing an interior cross-section transverse to $F$. In a small interval $I_0$ about it the surface is a product of $I_0$ with a finite union of disjoint proper arcs and circles in $D$: use its local transverse sheet charts, including the half-space charts at boundary endpoints, and compactness. Stretch $I_0$ to $I$ through a slightly longer interval extending into collars of the beam's two zero-handle ends. A monotone piecewise linear change of the interval coordinate, fixed at the ends of that longer interval, pushes the nonproduct portions into those zero-handles. On a marked rectangle the old surface is already a union of longitudinal lines, supplied by the full plate layers. Extend the change over a collar of the corresponding boundary arc of the plate disk, layer by layer, and then over that disk by [F4]. This preserves the full plate layers as sets. The extension is confined to this attaching arc and small neighboring zero-handle arcs, so it misses the other beam attaching arcs of the plate. On free gap rectangles it preserves $\partial M$; on three-handle gap rectangles it preserves the incident three-handle face. Finite common rectangle parameters ensure these extensions match. Thus no plate count changes and no previously treated beam is disturbed. Repeat over all beams. Write $L$ for the total number of full plate layers and $N$ for the total number of beam arcs and circles. [F1, F4, F5, step 3.1, given, construct]

5.1 Make the zero-handle pieces disks by the finite cap procedure, spelling out the needed interface. On a zero-handle boundary sphere, an innermost component of $F$'s boundary trace cuts off a boundary disk. Push that disk slightly into the handle, with its rim moved a little onto $F$, to obtain a surgery disk missing the remaining surface. Existing disk pieces are placed in nested thin cap collars; choose each enclosing cap deeper than the previously treated ones. There are finitely many boundary circles, so this ends with disk pieces and possible closed components. This is precisely the local procedure of [F3]; its disks do not enter any plate or beam interior. In the nondisk branch injectivity and [F4] make each interior surgery circle bound a disk on $F$, and [F3]'s empty-ball argument makes the retained surface isotopic to $F$: a different proper component cannot be inside an interior ball, and the punctured remainder of a nondisk injective component has an essential loop and cannot be inside it. In the disk branch every interior surgery splits off a sphere and leaves a disk with the same essential boundary; retain that disk. Any closed piece left in a zero-handle would be a whole component of the retained connected surface, impossible since it still has proper boundary. These operations and discarded components can only delete old plate layers or beam strips, never add them. [F3, F4, F5, step 4.1, given, construct]

6.1 No beam circle can now remain. Its product with the beam interval is a cylinder, and each of its end circles is the entire boundary of one of the adjacent zero-handle disk pieces. These two disks and the cylinder form a whole sphere component, including when the two ends attach to the same zero-handle. The retained surface is connected with nonempty boundary, so it cannot have this component. Thus every beam component is a product strip on a proper arc. The finite pair $(L,N)$ now counts only plate disks and beam strips. [step 4.1, step 5.1, given, construct]

7.1 If some beam arc returns to the same free gap, choose an innermost such arc. The disk $E$ between that arc and the gap interval contains no other arc: any arc inside would have both endpoints on that same gap and provide a smaller choice. The disk at an interior beam cross-section has boundary one proper arc $\alpha$ on $F$ and one arc on $\partial M$; its interior misses $F$. The gap is free, not incident to a three-handle, since its surface endpoint lies on $\partial M$ and the beam face incidence is constant across a gap. It is therefore an actual boundary-compression disk. In the nondisk branch $\alpha$ is inessential by hypothesis, so [F3]'s empty boundary ball makes this surgery, after deleting its boundary-parallel disk, an ambient isotopy. In the disk branch surgery splits the disk into two disks. At least one has essential boundary: the original boundary loop is the band product of their two boundary loops, with conjugating paths along the surgery band. If both were nullhomotopic in $\partial M$ then this product would be nullhomotopic, contrary to essentiality. Retain an essential disk. [F1, F3, F4, step 6.1, given, construct]

8.1 After this surgery the cut strip has two short end rectangles. Push them along the beam interval into its two zero-handle collars; this is the same interval stretching used in step4.1, supported just in a thin neighborhood of $I\times E$. No other strip enters this neighborhood by the choice of $E$. Thus at least the chosen strip disappears and no new beam strip or plate layer is introduced. Restore the zero-handle disk condition by step5.1. Any component deleted during these operations can only remove further strips or layers. Consequently either $L$ drops, or $L$ stays fixed and $N$ strictly drops. This procedure terminates on its own until there is no same-gap return. [F5, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 7.1, given, construct]

9.1 If an arc returns to one marked arc, choose an innermost return. The disk between it and its marked subinterval contains no other beam arc, since an enclosed arc would itself return to that same marked subinterval. It is an empty full fold as in [F2]. Apply that isotopy; it removes two plate layers. If there is no same-marked return and no same-gap return but there is an arc from a marked arc to an adjacent free gap, choose one whose corner disk contains a minimal number of beam arcs. An enclosed arc can only return to the marked side, return to the gap side, or join the two sides. The first two cases have been excluded; the last gives a smaller adjacent corner disk. Thus the chosen corner disk is empty. [F2, step 8.1, given, construct]

10.1 In this adjacent case the corresponding plate end really is a free boundary disk. Its beam gap is incident to $\partial M$, since $F$ ends there and misses three-handles. Across the corner between that gap and the plate end, the supplied manifold product chart identifies their free boundary sides. The interior of the plate end disk has no lower-handle seams and is connected, so its incidence is constant: it is all free boundary or all incident to a three-handle. The corner shows it is free. The half-fold hypotheses of [F2] are therefore satisfied, not merely presumed. Remove the half-fold by that isotopy, losing one plate layer. In either fold case the other plate layers remain full and unchanged. [F1, F2, step 9.1, given, construct]

11.1 After a fold, reapply beam straightening and zero-handle capping, steps4.1–6.1. These operations preserve or decrease the new plate count; they can give a new but finite strip count. Thus a fold macro strictly decreases $L$, regardless of its effect on $N$. A free-gap macro from steps7.1–8.1 either decreases $L$ or keeps it fixed and strictly decreases $N$. The initial straightening was finite, each capping pass has finitely many original boundary circles, and every macro strictly lowers the nonnegative integer pair $(L,N)$ in lexicographic order. Induction on $L$, with ordinary induction on $N$ between successive decreases of $L$, proves that only finitely many macros can occur. This does not assume one uniform bound on the newly obtained strip counts. [step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 8.1, step 10.1, given, induction]

12.1 At termination every beam arc satisfies all three endpoint exclusions: same free gap was removed by step8.1, same marked arc by the full-fold case, and adjacent marked/free endpoints by the half-fold case. There are no beam circles by step6.1. Plate layers and avoidance of three-handles are preserved throughout, and the last capping pass gives proper disk pieces in zero-handles. These are exactly the stated normal-position conditions. In the nondisk branch all retained changes were ambient isotopies and hence preserve every compressing-annulus or incompressibility condition by transport. In the disk branch the surface retained at every surgery was an actual essential disk, so the final disk has the asserted property without any claim of fixed isotopy class. No handle system was replaced, and the finite descent in step11.1 proves the required finiteness. [step 1.1, step 6.1, step 8.1, step 10.1, step 11.1, given, construct] ∎

## Scope

This is the normal-position bridge for the two distinct branches needed in disk-first hierarchy construction. It asserts neither pared-boundary minimality nor the pared axioms after a cut. A subsequent hierarchy proof must use the strict complexity of the coherently inherited handles, rather than restart a triangulation argument at every stage.
