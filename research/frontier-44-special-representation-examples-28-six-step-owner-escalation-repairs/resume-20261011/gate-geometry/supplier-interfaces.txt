
lem-classical-variety-noetherian-components
Every classical variety is Noetherian and has finitely many irreducible components. Every open or closed subvariety has a finite affine cover.

Work over a fixed algebraically closed field $k$, with the Axiom of Choice. Classical varieties are separated and admit finite affine covers; they may be reducible or empty unless irreducibility is specified. Irreducible means nonempty. All fibres and points below are classical closed-point fibres and points.



lem-dimension-nonempty-open-subset
If $U$ is a nonempty open of an irreducible classical variety $X$, then $\dim U=\dim X$. Every proper closed subvariety $Z\subsetneq X$ has $\dim Z<\dim X$.

Work over a fixed algebraically closed field $k$, with the Axiom of Choice. Classical varieties are separated and admit finite affine covers; they may be reducible or empty unless irreducibility is specified. Irreducible means nonempty. All fibres and points below are classical closed-point fibres and points.



thm-nonempty-regular-locus-reduced-variety-perfect-field
Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $k$ be a perfect field
([[def-perfect-field]]) and let $X$ be a reduced $k$-scheme of finite type over
$k$. Then:

1. the regular locus $X_{\mathrm{reg}}=\{x\in|X|:\mathcal O_{X,x}\text{ is a regular local ring}\}$
   ([[def-singular-and-regular-loci-variety]]) is open in $X$;
2. for every irreducible component $Z$ of $X$
   ([[def-irreducible-component-of-a-topological-space]]) the intersection
   $X_{\mathrm{reg}}\cap Z$ is a dense open subset of $Z$; in particular every
   irreducible component contains a nonempty dense open subset of points
   regular on $X$;
3. if $X\ne\varnothing$, then $X_{\mathrm{reg}}\ne\varnothing$.

No separatedness, irreducibility or equidimensionality hypothesis is imposed,
and $X$ may be empty, in which case the second clause is vacuous and the third
is not asserted.



thm-regular-equals-smooth-over-perfect-field
Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $k$ be a perfect
field and let $X$ be a finite-type $k$-scheme. Here **regular** means that $X$
is locally Noetherian and every local ring $\mathcal O_{X,x}$ is regular local;
**smooth over $k$** means that $X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is smooth under the
local-standard-smooth convention of
[[def-smooth-morphism-classical]]. Then
$$X\text{ is regular}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad X\to\operatorname{Spec}k\text{ is smooth}.$$
No reducedness, irreducibility, or closed-point restriction is imposed.



def-smooth-morphism-classical
Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $k$ be a field and
$f:X\to Y$ a morphism of finite-type $k$-schemes. The morphism $f$ is
**smooth** if every point $x\in X$ has affine neighborhoods
$x\in V=\operatorname{Spec}B$ and $f(x)\in U=\operatorname{Spec}A$, with
$f(V)\subseteq U$, for which the induced map $A\to B$ is standard smooth at
the prime corresponding to $x$ ([[def-ag-standard-smooth-algebra]]). Thus the
condition is imposed at every source point, including nonclosed points; it is
local on the source and target. In this condition, standard smoothness at a
prime means that after a further principal shrinking around that prime the
ring map has a standard smooth presentation.

The pointwise and global equivalences in
[[thm-ag-standard-smooth-geometric-regularity]] identify standard smoothness
for finite-presentation affine charts with flatness and geometrically regular
scheme-theoretic fibres. Applied after the local shrinkings above, this gives
the corresponding local criterion for $f$. The local-presentation clause
itself is choice-free; AC is assumed here for that proved equivalence, through
the cited theorem.


def-ag-standard-smooth-algebra
Let $R$ be a commutative ring. A **standard smooth presentation** of an
$R$-algebra $S$ consists of integers $n\ge c\ge0$, elements
$f_1,\dots,f_c$ of the polynomial ring $P=R[x_1,\dots,x_n]$
([[def-polynomial-ring-on-a-family-of-indeterminates]]) and an element $g\in P$
such that
$$S\cong\bigl(R[x_1,\dots,x_n]/(f_1,\dots,f_c)\bigr)_g$$
as $R$-algebras, and such that the Jacobian matrix of
([[lem-ag-polynomial-quotient-differentials]])
$$\Bigl(\frac{\partial f_j}{\partial x_i}\Bigr)_{\substack{1\le j\le c\\1\le i\le n}}$$
has a $c\times c$ minor whose image in $S$ is a unit, that is, an invertible
element. The integer $n-c\ge0$ is the **relative dimension** of the
presentation. The case $c=0$ is allowed and is exactly a localisation of a
polynomial ring, $S\cong(R[x_1,\dots,x_n])_g$; the case $n=c=0$ presents
$S=R[\,]_g$ for $g\in R$, that is a localisation of $R$ itself.

For a homomorphism $R\to S$ of commutative rings that is finitely presented as
an $R$-algebra ([[def-finitely-presented-module-and-algebra]]) and a prime
$\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}S$, the map is **standard smooth at
$\mathfrak q$**, or has a standard smooth presentation at $\mathfrak q$, when
there is $h\in S\smallsetminus\mathfrak q$ such that $S_h$ admits a standard
smooth presentation over $R$. It is **locally standard smooth** when this holds
at every prime of $S$; equivalently, when every point of $\operatorname{Spec}S$
has an affine open neighbourhood on which $S$ is presented by a single
standard smooth presentation.

Two conventions are part of the definition. First, the invertible minor may be
assumed to sit in the first $c$ columns: if a minor on columns
$i_1<\dots<i_c$ is a unit, the automorphism of $R[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ permuting the
variables so that these become the first $c$ variables carries the presentation
to one whose minor in the first $c$ columns is that unit, and the relative
dimension $n-c$ is unchanged. Second, a further principal localisation can be
absorbed into the presentation: adjoining a variable $z$ with the single
equation $zg-1$ to a presentation produces a standard smooth presentation of
the localisation, the new equation contributing a diagonal entry that keeps a
block minor invertible, and it changes $n$ and $c$ by the same amount, so that
the relative dimension is again unchanged.


thm-generic-smoothness-characteristic-zero
Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $k$ be an algebraically
closed field of characteristic $0$, let $X$ and $Y$ be irreducible classical
varieties over $k$
([[def-classical-algebraic-prevariety-regular-maps-and-varieties]]), and let
$f\colon X\to Y$ be a morphism of classical varieties. Assume $X$ is smooth
over $k$, that is, the structure morphism $X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is smooth
in the locally-standard-smooth sense of [[def-smooth-morphism-classical]]
(equivalently, since $k$ is perfect, $X$ is regular). Then:

1. there is a dense open subvariety $U\subseteq Y$ such that the restriction
   $$f^{-1}(U)\longrightarrow U$$
   is a smooth morphism of finite-type $k$-schemes; when $f$ is not dominant
   one may take $U$ with $f^{-1}(U)=\varnothing$, the empty morphism being
   smooth;
2. if in addition $f$ is dominant, there is a nonempty open (hence dense)
   $V\subseteq U$ such that for every closed point $y\in V$ the
   scheme-theoretic fibre
   $$X_y=X\times_Y\operatorname{Spec}k(y)$$
   ([[def-scheme-theoretic-fibre]]) is nonempty, smooth over $k$, and of pure
   dimension $r=\dim X-\dim Y$.

Neither $Y$ nor $f$ is required to be smooth or flat, the fibres are not
required to be irreducible or connected, and no statement is made about the
size of $U$ or $V$. The characteristic-$0$ hypothesis enters through the
critical-locus dimension bound of
[[lem-critical-locus-image-dimension-bound]] in claim 1 and through the
perfectness of $k$; the failure of the target-open statement for a
non-smooth source and the positive-characteristic failure of the
corresponding source-side statement are recorded on the counterexample page of
this pair.



lem-smooth-map-tangent-surjectivity-criterion
Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $k$ be algebraically closed and let $X,Y$ be
smooth classical varieties over $k$, with their finite-type $k$-scheme
structures. Assume their structure morphisms are smooth in the sense of
[[def-smooth-morphism-classical]]. Let $f:X\to Y$ be a finite-type morphism
of these $k$-schemes ([[def-locally-finite-type-and-finite-type-morphism]])
and let $x\in X$ be a classical
closed point with $y=f(x)$. All such points have residue field $k$. Here
“smooth at $x$” means that the induced scheme morphism is locally standard
smooth at $x$ ([[def-smooth-morphism-classical]]); the structural morphisms
$X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ and $Y\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ are locally
standard smooth at $x$ and $y$, respectively. Then $f$ is smooth at $x$ if
and only if its differential
$$d_xf:T_xX\longrightarrow T_yY$$
is surjective. If these equivalent conditions hold, the scheme-theoretic
fibre $X_y=X\times_Y\operatorname{Spec}k$ has a regular local ring at $x$
of dimension $\dim_xX-\dim_yY$.
For every such $f$ (whether or not it is smooth at $x$), its fibre tangent
space is canonically
$$T_x(X_y)=\ker(d_xf).$$



thm-holomorphic-implicit-function-theorem
Let $m,n\ge1$, let $U\subseteq\mathbb C^m\times\mathbb C^n$ be open, let
$f:U\to\mathbb C^n$ be holomorphic, and let $(a,b)\in U$ satisfy $f(a,b)=0$.
Assume the complex Jacobian with respect to the second block is invertible:

$$\det\left(\frac{\partial f_j}{\partial w_k}(a,b)\right)_{1\le j,k\le n}\ne0.$$

Then there are neighbourhoods $A$ of $a$ and $B$ of $b$, and a unique
holomorphic map $\varphi:A\to B$, such that

$$f(z,\varphi(z))=0\qquad(z\in A),$$

and, after shrinking $A\times B$ if needed,

$$f(z,w)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad w=\varphi(z).$$



thm-holomorphic-constant-rank-theorem
Let $m,n\ge1$, let $U\subseteq\mathbb C^m$ be open, let $F:U\to\mathbb C^n$ be
holomorphic, and suppose the complex rank of $DF$ is the constant value $r$ on a
neighbourhood of $a\in U$. Then there are biholomorphic coordinate changes
$\alpha$ near $a$ and $\beta$ near $F(a)$ such that

$$\beta\circ F\circ \alpha^{-1}(u,v)=(u,0)$$

for $(u,v)\in\mathbb C^r\times\mathbb C^{m-r}$ near $0$. Empty blocks are
omitted when $r=0$, $r=m$, or $r=n$.



def-axiom-of-choice
The **Axiom of Choice** (AC) is the following statement.

> Every family of nonempty sets has a choice function
> ([[def-choice-function]]).

Written out: for every set $\mathcal{F}$ all of whose members are nonempty,
there exists a function $g$ with domain $\mathcal{F}$ satisfying $g(S) \in S$ for
all $S \in \mathcal{F}$.

An equivalent formulation is that a product of nonempty sets is nonempty: if
$X_i \ne \emptyset$ for every $i \in I$, then $\prod_{i \in I} X_i \ne \emptyset$.
Here $\prod_{i \in I} X_i$ is the set of functions $f$ with domain $I$ such that
$f(i) \in X_i$ for every $i \in I$; when a family of nonempty sets is indexed by
itself, such an $f$ is precisely a choice function for it.



lem-irreducible-complex-varieties-are-analytically-connected
Assume AC. Every nonempty irreducible separated complex algebraic variety of
finite type is connected in its analytic topology. For every locally closed
algebraic subset $A$ of a separated finite-type complex variety $X$, the closure
of $A$ in the analytic topology equals its Zariski closure as a set of classical
complex points. In particular smooth irreducible algebraic strata are connected
complex manifolds once their holomorphic charts have been supplied.



def-finite-complex-algebraic-whitney-stratification-and-control-data
Let $X$ be a reduced quasi-projective complex algebraic variety, regarded as a
classical variety over $\mathbb C$
([[def-classical-algebraic-prevariety-regular-maps-and-varieties]]) with its
analytic topology, its smooth points (regular local rings) and its finite
smooth algebraic frontier stratifications
([[def-analytic-topology-and-finite-smooth-algebraic-stratifications]]).

(a) **Whitney conditions.** Let $X=\coprod_{\alpha\in A}S_\alpha$ be a finite smooth algebraic frontier stratification whose strata are pure-dimensional, and let $R\subseteq\overline S\setminus S$ be incident strata. In local analytic charts of an ambient smooth manifold, the pair $(S,R)$ satisfies **Whitney (a)** at $y\in R$ if for every sequence $x_n\in S$ with $x_n\to y$ whose tangent planes $T_{x_n}S$ converge to a plane $T$, one has $T_yR\subseteq T$; it satisfies **Whitney (b)** at $y$ if additionally, whenever $y_n\in R$ with $y_n\to y$ and the real secant lines through $x_n,y_n$ converge to a line $\ell$, one has $\ell\subseteq T$. A **finite complex algebraic Whitney stratification** of $X$ is a finite smooth algebraic frontier stratification satisfying (a) and (b) at every point of every incident pair. Coordinate independence of the limits and the implication (b)$\Rightarrow$(a), commonly used in the literature, are not assumed as proved here; requiring both conditions makes the intended interface explicit.

(b) **Tube control data.** A system of **tube control data** on the analytic stratified space $X$ consists of an open neighbourhood $T_R$ of each stratum $R$, a continuous retraction $\pi_R:T_R\to R$ and a continuous radius $\rho_R:T_R\to[0,\infty)$ such that $\rho_R^{-1}(0)=R$, both maps are smooth on each stratum, and for every incident upper stratum $S$ the pair $(\pi_R,\rho_R)$ restricts to a submersion $S\cap T_R\to R\times(0,\infty)$. On the overlap neighbourhoods where the compositions are defined one requires $\pi_R\pi_S=\pi_R$ and $\rho_R\pi_S=\rho_R$ for $R<S$; every compatibility assertion must name the common domain on which the composition is defined. Tubes may be shrunk. For local conical constructions one requires local properness over compact base slices: sufficiently small closed radius ranges over a compact neighbourhood in $R$ have compact inverse image inside the tube.

(c) **$f$-compatible tubes and controlled lifts.** Let $f:W\to B$ be a stratified submersion onto a smooth manifold. A tube system is **$f$-compatible** if $f\pi_R=f$ on the tubes used. A **controlled lift** of a smooth base field $v$ on $B$ is a field $V$ that is smooth on each stratum and satisfies $df(V)=v\circ f$, $d\pi_R(V)=V|_R\circ\pi_R$ and $d\rho_R(V)=0$ on specified controlled tube neighbourhoods. **Locally bounded** means the ambient norm is bounded on a neighbourhood of every point; this is an additional hypothesis, not a consequence of the definition. **Radial controlled lifts** instead satisfy $d\rho_R(V)=\rho_R$ with fixed $\pi_R$ together with the appropriate annular overlap controls.

(d) **Conical product neighbourhoods.** A **conical product neighbourhood along $R$** is a stratum-preserving homeomorphism from a neighbourhood of a point of $R$ to $B^{2\dim_{\mathbb C}R}\times c(L)$ carrying $R$ to the ball times the cone apex, with compact finitely Whitney-stratified $L$. The radial and tangential transports must be compatible with the actual coefficient data when cohomological local systems are deduced; topological existence alone does not supply canonical derived-sheaf transport.

(e) **Finite triangulation of a noncompact space.** In the compactification convention used here, a **finite triangulation** of a noncompact space $X$ consists of finite simplicial complexes $D\subseteq K$ together with a homeomorphism $|K|\setminus|D|\to X$; compatibility with prescribed subsets means they are unions of interiors of retained simplices. This does not assert that algebraic morphisms become simplicial, and real simplicial dimensions do not replace the complex dimensions of the algebraic strata.



lem-complex-algebraic-morphisms-admit-compatible-finite-smooth-stratifications
Assume AC. Let $f:X\to Y$ be one morphism of reduced separated complex
algebraic varieties of finite type
([[def-classical-algebraic-prevariety-regular-maps-and-varieties]]), and
prescribe finitely many closed algebraic subsets of each. There are finite
partitions of $X$ and $Y$ into irreducible smooth locally closed algebraic
strata such that every prescribed subset is a union of strata, every stratum
closure is a union of strata of no larger dimension with its boundary of
strictly smaller dimension, and each source stratum maps surjectively and
smoothly onto one target stratum. On analytic spaces each such restriction is a
submersion. The source may be empty. This assertion imposes no Whitney
condition, conical control or topological triviality.



lem-generic-complex-algebraic-whitney-regularity
Assume AC. Let $S,Y\subseteq\mathbb C^n$ be disjoint irreducible smooth locally
closed complex algebraic varieties with $Y\subseteq\overline S$ and
$\dim Y<\dim S$. There is a proper closed algebraic subset $E\subsetneq Y$ such
that $(S,Y)$ satisfies Whitney (a) and (b) at every point of $Y\setminus E$.
Finite prescribed incident upper strata can be treated with one common
exceptional subset by taking a finite union. The same result holds in the
affine charts of a quasi-projective algebraic ambient manifold.



thm-heine-borel-rn
Let $n \in \mathbb{N}$ with $n \ge 1$, let $\mathbb{R}^n$ be the set of functions
$n \to \mathbb{R}$ and let $d_2$ be the Euclidean metric on it
([[lem-metrics-on-rn]]). Then:

1. **Closed boxes are compact.** For reals $a_k \le b_k$ $(k < n)$ the box
   $Q = \{\, x \in \mathbb{R}^n : a_k \le x_k \le b_k \text{ for every } k < n \,\}$
   is a compact subset of $(\mathbb{R}^n, d_2)$ ([[def-metric-compactness]]).
2. **Heine-Borel.** A subset $K \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ is a compact subset of
   $(\mathbb{R}^n, d_2)$ if and only if $K$ is closed in $\mathbb{R}^n$
   ([[def-metric-topology]]) and bounded
   ([[def-metric-bounded-diameter]]).
3. **The real line.** A subset $K \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ is a compact subset of
   $(\mathbb{R}, d_{\mathbb{R}})$, the usual metric $d_{\mathbb{R}}(x,y) = |x-y|$
   ([[lem-real-line-is-a-metric-space]]), if and only if $K$ is closed in
   $\mathbb{R}$ and bounded.

**No choice principle is used.** The bisection below halves one coordinate at a
time and takes the **left** half whenever the left half still fails to be finitely
covered, the right half otherwise: a rule with two outcomes, decided by a
property of the box, not a selection. That is the whole reason the theorem is
available in ZF, while the general "complete and totally bounded implies compact"
([[thm-complete-and-totally-bounded-implies-compact]]) is not.

The hypothesis $n \ge 1$ is inherited from [[lem-metrics-on-rn]], which defines
$\mathbb{R}^n$ and its metrics only there; the last remark below records what
happens at $n = 0$.



thm-compactness-under-continuous-maps
Let $(X, \mathcal{T}_X)$ and $(Y, \mathcal{T}_Y)$ be topological spaces
([[def-topological-space]]), and let $\mathbb{R}$ carry its usual topology, the
metric topology of $d_{\mathbb{R}}(s,t) = |s-t|$
([[lem-real-line-is-a-metric-space]], [[def-metric-topology]],
[[def-metrizable-space]]). Then:

1. **Continuous images.** If $f : X \to Y$ is continuous
   ([[def-continuous-map-top]]) and $(X, \mathcal{T}_X)$ is compact
   ([[def-compact-space]]), then $f[X]$ is a compact subset of $Y$. More
   generally, if $K \subseteq X$ is a compact subset of $X$ then $f[K]$ is a
   compact subset of $Y$.
2. **Extreme values.** If $(X, \mathcal{T}_X)$ is compact and nonempty and
   $g : X \to \mathbb{R}$ is continuous, then $g[X]$ has a maximum and a minimum
   ([[def-max-min]]): there are $x_{\max}, x_{\min} \in X$ with
   $$g(x_{\min}) \;\le\; g(x) \;\le\; g(x_{\max}) \qquad \text{for every } x \in X .$$
3. **Compact to Hausdorff.** If $(X, \mathcal{T}_X)$ is compact, $(Y,
   \mathcal{T}_Y)$ is Hausdorff ([[def-hausdorff-space]]) and $f : X \to Y$ is a
   continuous bijection, then $f$ is a homeomorphism
   ([[def-homeomorphism-and-open-maps]]).

Nonemptiness in claim 2 is a hypothesis and not an oversight: for
$X = \varnothing$ the image is empty and has neither a maximum nor a minimum. **No
choice principle is used**: the one selection made below is over a finite index
set, where [[lem-finite-choice]] is a theorem of ZF.



thm-finite-products-of-compact-spaces
For every $n \in \mathbb{N}$ ([[def-natural-numbers]]) and every family
$(X_k)_{k < n}$ of compact topological spaces ([[def-compact-space]],
[[def-topological-space]]), the product

$$\prod_{k < n} X_k$$

with the product topology ([[def-product-topology]]) is compact. In particular a
binary product $X \times Y$ of compact spaces is compact, and the empty product,
a one-point space, is compact.

**No choice principle is used beyond [[lem-finite-choice]]**, which is a theorem
of ZF. That is what separates the finite case from the arbitrary one, where the
Axiom of Choice is genuinely spent.



thm-algebraic-whitney-strata-have-controlled-local-conical-neighborhoods
Assume AC. Let $X$ be a reduced quasi-projective complex algebraic variety with
a finite smooth complex algebraic Whitney stratification. Its analytic
stratified space admits compatible tube control data. At every point $y$ of a
stratum $R$ there is a stratum-preserving neighborhood homeomorphism
$$U_y\cong B^{2\dim_{\mathbb C}R}\times c_{<1}(L_y),$$
where $L_y$ is a compact finitely Whitney-stratified real semialgebraic normal
link. The ball times cone apex is $R\cap U_y$, and upper strata correspond to
their link strata times positive radius and the ball. The local construction
can preserve a chosen ordinary normal projection and radius at $R$. Links are
locally constant in stratified homeomorphism type along $R$. Proper stratified
algebraic submersions, with their actual analytic properness, have whole-fiber
local stratified bundle trivializations; local submersions have
source-neighborhood products. No compatible triangulation or finite
sheaf-cochain model is assumed or asserted here.



lem-compact-semialgebraic-families-admit-compatible-finite-triangulations
Assume AC. A compact finite controlled Whitney-stratified subset of a smooth
ambient space admits a finite triangulation of dimension at most the largest
real stratum dimension, compatible with every stratum closure and any
prescribed union of strata. Each open stratum is a union of open simplices. The
homeomorphism is smooth on the pieces within each stratum. The construction may
make the finitely many normal link-bundle projections between triangulated
compact stratum cores piecewise linear. It does not make an arbitrary algebraic
map globally simplicial. In particular it applies individually to
compactifications of quasi-projective complex varieties and to their compact
controlled links, once the stated Whitney/control hypotheses have been
supplied.



lem-finite-simplicial-open-stars-have-explicit-conical-product-charts
Let $K$ be a finite simplicial complex and let $\sigma$ be a nonempty simplex.
Let $\operatorname{lk}_K\sigma$ be the subcomplex of simplices $\tau$ disjoint
from $\sigma$ with $\sigma\cup\tau\in K$, and let $\operatorname{st}_K(\sigma)$
be the open star of $\sigma$
([[def-simplicial-subcomplex-star-closure-and-link]]). Then
$$\operatorname{st}_K(\sigma)\cong\operatorname{relint}|\sigma|\times c_{<1}(|\operatorname{lk}_K\sigma|),$$
where $c_{<1}(L)=(L\times[0,1))/(L\times\{0\})$ with the quotient topology, and
the cone on the empty space means a single apex. The homeomorphism takes each
simplex interior containing $\sigma$ to the product with the corresponding
link-simplex interior and positive radial coordinate; the simplex $\sigma$
itself maps to the apex. The link is compact and has finitely many simplices.
If $D\subseteq K$ is a subcomplex and $\sigma\notin D$, this star lies in
$|K|\setminus|D|$.



lem-finite-cell-local-system-cohomology-is-finite-and-commutes-with-field-extension
Assume AC. Let $(X,A)$ be a finite CW pair of dimension $d$, let $k$ be a field
and let $L$ be a $k$-vector-space local system with finite-dimensional fibres
([[def-local-system-of-r-modules-and-its-pullback]]). Then $H^q(X,A;L)$ is
finite-dimensional, vanishes for $q<0$ and $q>d$, and for every field extension
$k\subseteq k'$ the natural map is an isomorphism:
$$H^q(X,A;L)\otimes_k k'\cong H^q(X,A;L\otimes_k k').$$
The assertion holds separately on every component, including the empty pair.



lem-finite-simplicial-constructible-sheaves-have-a-derived-incidence-model
Assume AC. Let $K$ be a finite simplicial complex, $D$ a subcomplex and $X=|K|\setminus|D|$. Let $P$ be the face poset of the open simplices of $K$ not in $D$, ordered by inclusion of faces. The exact realization of finite-dimensional covariant $k$-diagrams on $P$ induces a fully faithful functor into $D^b(\operatorname{Sh}_k(X))$, with essential image the complexes having bounded finite-rank locally constant cohomology on each open simplex. Ordinary and compact-support cohomology, ordinary Hom and internal derived Hom have bounded finite cochain models and commute with every coefficient field extension. For a local product projection $\operatorname{pr}:V\times F\to V$ with supplied finite deleted-boundary models and $B$ in the stated constructible subcategory on the base, there is a natural local comparison $R\operatorname{pr}_*\operatorname{pr}^*B\simeq B\otimes_k C^*(F;k)$, taking the unit to $B\otimes(k\to C^*(F;k))$. This does not assert that an arbitrary algebraic map is simplicial.



lem-finite-poset-diagrams-have-bounded-finite-bar-models
Let $P$ be a finite nonempty poset, regarded as a category with one arrow $p\to q$ exactly when $p\le q$, and let $h$ be the largest number of strict inequalities in a chain of $P$. Let $\operatorname{Diag}_k(P)$ be the abelian category of covariant diagrams of $k$-vector spaces on $P$, i.e. the functor category of $k$-vector spaces indexed by $P$, with its cochain complexes as in [[def-cochain-complex-in-an-abelian-category]]. If $M$ is a bounded complex of diagrams in $\operatorname{Diag}_k(P)$ whose vector spaces are finite-dimensional and vanish outside internal degrees $[a,b]$, then the derived inverse limit $\mathbf R\lim M:=\mathbf R\operatorname{Hom}(\underline k,M)$ ([[def-derived-hom-in-the-bounded-setting]]) has a finite-dimensional cochain model concentrated in $[a,b+h]$. If $A,B$ are such complexes, concentrated in $[a,b]$ and $[c,d]$ respectively, then their derived Hom has a finite-dimensional cochain model concentrated in $[c-b,d-a+h]$. These models commute with every field extension. For the empty poset, the diagram sections and the derived Hom model are zero by convention.



thm-complex-algebraic-varieties-and-maps-admit-compatible-finite-stratifications
Assume AC. Let $f:X\to Y$ be one morphism of reduced quasi-projective complex
algebraic varieties, and prescribe finitely many closed or locally closed
algebraic subsets in source and target. There exist finite smooth complex
algebraic Whitney (a),(b) stratifications with frontier on $X$ and $Y$
respecting all prescribed subsets, such that each source stratum maps
surjectively and smoothly onto one target stratum. A further finite algebraic
stratification may be prescribed and refined. The analytic strata are smooth
complex manifolds and the restrictions are smooth submersions. If $f$ is proper
in the analytic topology, in particular if it is a projective algebraic
morphism, the whole restriction $f^{-1}(T)\to T$ over any target stratum is
proper; no restriction of $f$ to a nonclosed individual source stratum is
asserted proper.



thm-constructible-sheaves-admit-finite-stratified-triangulations-and-finite-local-cohomology
Assume AC. Let $X$ be a quasi-projective complex algebraic variety, $k$ a
field, and $F$ a bounded complex of analytic $k$-sheaves whose cohomology is
locally constant of finite rank on a finite complex algebraic stratification.
Fix finitely many algebraic supports and locally closed inclusions to be
respected. There is a finite compatible smooth complex algebraic Whitney

def-inverse-image-presheaf-and-sheaf
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a continuous map, and let $\mathcal G$ be a presheaf on $Y$.
The **inverse-image presheaf**
$$
f_{\mathrm p}\mathcal G
$$
on $X$ is defined by
$$
(f_{\mathrm p}\mathcal G)(U):=\varinjlim_{f(U)\subseteq V}\mathcal G(V),
$$
where $V$ runs over the open neighbourhoods of $f(U)$ in $Y$, ordered by
reverse inclusion. If $U' \subseteq U$, then every neighbourhood of $f(U)$ is a
neighbourhood of $f(U')$, so there is a natural restriction map
$(f_{\mathrm p}\mathcal G)(U)\to(f_{\mathrm p}\mathcal G)(U')$.

The **inverse image sheaf** of a sheaf $\mathcal G$ on $Y$ is the sheafification
of this presheaf:
$$
f^{-1}\mathcal G:=a(f_{\mathrm p}\mathcal G).
$$

When $U$ is open in $X$ and $V$ is an open set of $Y$ with $f(U)\subseteq V$,
there is a canonical map
$$
\mathcal G(V)\longrightarrow(f_{\mathrm p}\mathcal G)(U)
$$
into the colimit class represented by $V$.


def-direct-image-sheaf
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a continuous map, and let $\mathcal F$ be a presheaf on $X$.
The **direct image presheaf**
$$f_*\mathcal F$$
on $Y$ is defined on an open set $V \subseteq Y$ by
$$
(f_*\mathcal F)(V):=\mathcal F(f^{-1}(V)).
$$
If $V' \subseteq V$, the restriction map
$$
(f_*\mathcal F)(V)\longrightarrow(f_*\mathcal F)(V')
$$
is the restriction map of $\mathcal F$ for the inclusion
$f^{-1}(V') \subseteq f^{-1}(V)$.

Thus direct image is just precomposition of the presheaf with the inverse-image
map on open sets.


thm-inverse-direct-image-adjunction
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a continuous map, let $\mathcal G$ be a sheaf on $Y$, and let
$\mathcal F$ be a sheaf on $X$. Then there is a natural bijection
$$\operatorname{Hom}_X(f^{-1}\mathcal G,\mathcal F) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_Y(\mathcal G,f_*\mathcal F).$$



thm-exactness-of-sheaves-stalkwise
Let
$$
\cdots\longrightarrow \mathcal F_{i-1}\xrightarrow{d_{i-1}}\mathcal F_i\xrightarrow{d_i}\mathcal F_{i+1}\longrightarrow\cdots
$$
be a sequence of sheaves of abelian groups on a topological space $X$. Then the
sequence is exact if and only if, for every point $x\in X$, the stalk sequence
$$
\cdots\longrightarrow (\mathcal F_{i-1})_x\xrightarrow{(d_{i-1})_x}(\mathcal F_i)_x\xrightarrow{(d_i)_x}(\mathcal F_{i+1})_x\longrightarrow\cdots
$$
is exact.



def-extension-by-zero-abelian-sheaf
Let $j:U\hookrightarrow X$ be the inclusion of an open subspace, and let
$\mathcal F$ be a sheaf of abelian groups on $U$.

For an open set $V \subseteq X$, define
$$(j_!\mathcal F)(V) := \left\{ s\in \mathcal F(V\cap U): \operatorname{Supp}(s)\text{ is closed in }V \right\}.$$
Equivalently, $s$ lies in $(j_!\mathcal F)(V)$ exactly when for every
$x\in V\setminus U$ there exists an open neighbourhood $W_x \subseteq V$ with
$$
s|_{W_x\cap U}=0.
$$

Restriction maps are inherited from $\mathcal F$, so $j_!\mathcal F$ is a
subsheaf of $j_*\mathcal F$. When $V \subseteq U$, the support condition is
vacuous and
$$
(j_!\mathcal F)(V)=\mathcal F(V).
$$

This is the **extension by zero** of $\mathcal F$ along $j$. It is distinct in
general from the ordinary direct image $j_*\mathcal F$.


thm-extension-by-zero-adjunction-exactness
Let $j:U\hookrightarrow X$ be the inclusion of an open subspace.

1. For every sheaf of abelian groups $\mathcal F$ on $U$ and every sheaf of
   abelian groups $\mathcal G$ on $X$, there is a natural bijection
   $$\operatorname{Hom}_X(j_!\mathcal F,\mathcal G) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_U(\mathcal F,j^{-1}\mathcal G).$$
2. The functor $j_!$ is exact on sheaves of abelian groups.



lem-extension-by-zero-short-exact-sequence
Let $j:U\hookrightarrow X$ be the inclusion of an open subspace
([[def-topological-space]]) with closed complement $Z=X\setminus U$, let

([[def-subspace-topology-top]]), and let $\mathcal F$ be a sheaf of abelian groups

def-derived-category-of-an-abelian-category
Let $\mathcal A$ be an abelian category. We use cochains as in [[def-cochain-complex-in-an-abelian-category]], with $X[k]^n=X^{n+k}$ and $d_{X[k]}^n=(-1)^kd_X^{n+k}$. Thus $H^n(X[k])=H^{n+k}(X)$. The cochain category $K(\mathcal A)$ is the reindexed published homotopy category. Put

$$D(\mathcal A)=K(\mathcal A)[\mathrm{qis}^{-1}],\qquad Q:K(\mathcal A)\longrightarrow D(\mathcal A).$$

Likewise $D^-,D^+,D^b$ initially mean the localizations of the termwise bounded variants of [[def-bounded-bounded-below-and-bounded-above-complex]]. Roof morphisms exist by [[lem-quasi-isomorphisms-admit-the-roof-calculus-in-the-homotopy-category]] and [[thm-the-calculus-of-fractions-constructs-the-localization]] under its standing size hypothesis: a small category of complexes, or supplied small cofinal denominator families. Every assertion of Hom sets is under that hypothesis. In the bounded module models, supplied replacements will separately exhibit those Hom sets. No general local-smallness theorem for unbounded $D(\mathcal A)$ is asserted.

The cone convention is $\operatorname{Cone}(f)^n=Y^n\oplus X^{n+1}$, $d(y,x)=(d_Yy+fx,-d_Xx)$, and its triangle ends in $X[1]$.


def-derived-hom-in-the-bounded-setting
Let $M\in D^-(\mathcal A)$ and $N\in D^+(\mathcal A)$, with termwise bounded representatives. With supplied bounded-above projective models under [[thm-projective-complexes-model-the-bounded-above-derived-category]], define $\mathbf R\operatorname{Hom}(M,N)=Q\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(P_M,N)$. Alternatively, with supplied bounded-below injective models under [[thm-injective-complexes-model-the-bounded-below-derived-category]], use $Q\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(M,I_N)$. The target is $D^+(\mathbf{Ab})$.

Here the cochain form of [[def-hom-complex-of-chain-complexes]] has degree-$r$ term $\prod_i\operatorname{Hom}(M^i,N^{i+r})$ and differential $du=d_Nu-(-1)^ru d_M$. If $M^i=0$ above $b$ and $N^j=0$ below $a$, nonzero factors require $a-r\leq i\leq b$, a finite interval, and the whole term is zero for $r<a-b$.

This construction is a bifunctor on the declared derived categories. Indeed homotopies in either variable induce Hom-complex homotopies. Replacing a projective model by a homotopy equivalent one therefore changes its Hom complex by a homotopy equivalence. A quasi-isomorphism in the target has acyclic cone, whose Hom from $P_M$ is acyclic by K-projectivity; hence the Hom map is a quasi-isomorphism. This also follows degree by degree from [[prop-morphisms-from-a-homotopically-projective-complex-need-no-roof]], which identifies each Hom-complex cohomology with the corresponding derived Hom. The injective argument uses [[prop-morphisms-into-a-homotopically-injective-complex-need-no-roof]] and reverses the roles of source and target. Thus both variables descend through localization. When both systems exist, the quasi-isomorphisms $\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(P_M,N)\to\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(P_M,I_N)\leftarrow\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(M,I_N)$ give their natural identification. Either one-sided resolution hypothesis suffices.

lem-sections-near-a-compact-set-are-sections-on-that-set
Let $K\subseteq X$ be a compact subset ([[def-compact-space]]) of a locally
compact Hausdorff space ([[def-locally-compact-space]], [[def-hausdorff-space]]),
let $\mathcal F$ be a sheaf of abelian groups on $X$
([[def-sheaf-on-topological-space]]) and let $i:K\hookrightarrow X$ be the
inclusion. Then the canonical map
$$\varinjlim_{U\supseteq K,\ U\ \text{open}}\mathcal F(U)\longrightarrow\Gamma(K,i^{-1}\mathcal F)=(i^{-1}\mathcal F)(K)$$
is an isomorphism of abelian groups (of $k$-vector spaces when $\mathcal F$ is a sheaf of $k$-vector spaces). Moreover, for pairwise disjoint compact
subsets $K_1,\dots,K_m\subseteq X$ and sections $s_j\in\mathcal F(U_j)$ over open
neighbourhoods $U_j$ of the respective $K_j$, there are an open neighbourhood $U$
of $K_1\cup\dots\cup K_m$ and a section $s\in\mathcal F(U)$, together with, for
every $j$, an open neighbourhood $W_j\subseteq U_j$ of $K_j$ with
$s|_{W_j}=s_j|_{W_j}$; in particular the zero section can be prescribed on any
compact set disjoint from the given one. The isomorphism is $k$-linear in the vector-space case, and no
choice principle is used: every selection in the proof is made from finitely
many nonempty sets.



def-c-soft-sheaf
Let $X$ be a locally compact Hausdorff space ([[def-locally-compact-space]],
[[def-hausdorff-space]]) and let $\mathcal F$ be a sheaf of abelian groups on
$X$ ([[def-sheaf-on-topological-space]]); this covers in particular every sheaf
of $k$-vector spaces over a field $k$, which is the case used by the
compact-support calculus, and for such a sheaf the groups below are $k$-vector
spaces and the maps $k$-linear. Global sections are written
$\Gamma(X,-)$ ([[def-global-sections-functor-sheaves]]). For a compact subset
$K\subseteq X$ ([[def-compact-space]]) let $i:K\hookrightarrow X$ be the
inclusion and consider the canonical restriction map
$$\Gamma(X,\mathcal F)\longrightarrow\Gamma(K,i^{-1}\mathcal F)=(i^{-1}\mathcal F)(K),\qquad s\longmapsto s|_K,$$
which sends a global section ([[def-section-restriction-and-global-section]]) to
the section of the inverse image sheaf ([[def-inverse-image-presheaf-and-sheaf]])
that it induces on $K$. Then $\mathcal F$ is **c-soft**, also **soft for
compact supports**, when this map is surjective for every compact subset
$K\subseteq X$, that is: every section of $\mathcal F$ over a compact subset of
$X$ extends to a global section of $\mathcal F$. For sheaves of abelian groups
$\Gamma_c$ and $H^q_c$ of
[[def-compactly-supported-global-sections]] are the compactly supported
analogues of $\Gamma$ and $H^q$.

Two consistency facts hold; both are immediate from the definitions and from
the extension lemma for sections on a compact set, and they are recorded here
because the compact-support calculus uses the first constantly and the second
for injective sheaves.

1. **Neighbourhood formulation.** By
   [[lem-sections-near-a-compact-set-are-sections-on-that-set]], the target of
   the restriction map is the defining colimit of the
   inverse-image presheaf,
   $(i^{-1}\mathcal F)(K)=\varinjlim_{U\supseteq K}\mathcal F(U)$, so that
   $\mathcal F$ is c-soft if and only if for every compact $K$ and every open
   neighbourhood $U$ of $K$, every section $s\in\mathcal F(U)$ agrees with a
   global section of $\mathcal F$ on some open neighbourhood of $K$. It
   therefore suffices, for c-softness, to test the extended sections only
   locally near $K$.
2. **Flasque implies c-soft.** If $\mathcal F$ is flasque
   ([[def-flasque-sheaf]]) then $\mathcal F$ is c-soft: a class in
   $(i^{-1}\mathcal F)(K)$ is represented by a section $s$ over an open
   neighbourhood $U$ of $K$, and flasqueness extends $s$ to a global section
   $t\in\mathcal F(X)$ with $t|_U=s$, so $t$ is a preimage of that class.

C-softness is a property of the pair $(X,\mathcal F)$ and not of the sheaf
alone: the compact subsets of the base space are part of the test. Nothing is
assumed about the support of the extended global sections, and c-softness is
not the same condition as flasqueness, only implied by it.



lem-c-soft-field-sheaves-are-compact-support-acyclic
Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $X$ be a locally compact Hausdorff space and
let $\mathcal F$ be a sheaf of abelian groups on $X$
([[def-sheaf-on-topological-space]]); every clause applies in particular to
sheaves of $k$-vector spaces, and the tensor clause of (4) is stated only
there. Then:

1. if $\mathcal F$ is c-soft ([[def-c-soft-sheaf]]), every section of
   $\mathcal F$ over a compact subset $K\subseteq X$ extends to a compactly
   supported global section whose support is contained in any prescribed open
   neighbourhood of $K$;
2. every injective abelian sheaf on $X$ is c-soft;
3. if $\mathcal F$ is c-soft, then $H^q_c(X,\mathcal F)=0$ for every $q>0$
   ([[def-compactly-supported-global-sections]]); when $X$ is compact this is
   the vanishing of the sheaf cohomology $H^q(X,\mathcal F)$
   ([[def-sheaf-cohomology-derived-global-sections]]);
4. c-softness is preserved by restriction to open subsets, by restriction to
   closed subsets, by extension by zero along an open inclusion, and by finite
   direct sums of sheaves of abelian groups; for sheaves of $k$-vector spaces
   it is also preserved by tensor product with a constant $k$-vector space;
5. if $0\to\mathcal F'\xrightarrow{\alpha}\mathcal F\xrightarrow{\beta}\mathcal F''\to0$
   is a short exact sequence of sheaves of abelian groups and $\mathcal F'$ is
   c-soft, then every section of $\mathcal F''$ over a compact subset lifts to
   a section of $\mathcal F$ over an open neighbourhood of it, and the induced
   sequence
   $0\to\Gamma_c(X,\mathcal F')\to\Gamma_c(X,\mathcal F)\to\Gamma_c(X,\mathcal F'')\to0$
   is exact; if $\mathcal F'$ and $\mathcal F$ are c-soft then
   $\mathcal F''$ is c-soft.



def-compactly-supported-global-sections
Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $X$ be a locally
compact Hausdorff space, let $\mathrm{Ab}(X)$ be the category of sheaves of
abelian groups on $X$ ([[def-sheaf-on-topological-space]]) and let
$$\Gamma(X,-):\mathrm{Ab}(X)\longrightarrow\mathbf{Ab}$$
be the additive left exact global-sections functor
([[def-global-sections-functor-sheaves]],
[[def-section-restriction-and-global-section]]). For a sheaf $\mathcal F$ on
$X$ define the **compactly supported global sections** of $\mathcal F$ to be the
subgroup
$$\Gamma_c(X,\mathcal F):=\{\,s\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal F):\operatorname{Supp}(s)\ \text{is a compact subset of }X\,\}\subseteq\Gamma(X,\mathcal F),$$
where the support of a section is the set of points at which its germ is
nonzero ([[def-support-of-a-sheaf-section]], [[def-germ-of-section]],
[[def-compact-space]]). This is a subgroup: the zero section has empty support, negation preserves
support, and the support of a sum is closed in $X$ and contained in the finite
union of the two compact supports, hence compact
([[def-support-of-a-sheaf-section]],
[[thm-closed-subspace-of-a-compact-space-is-compact]]).
A morphism $\varphi:\mathcal F\to\mathcal G$ of abelian
sheaves induces a map $\Gamma_c(X,\varphi)$ by restriction of
$\Gamma(X,\varphi)$, and this makes $\Gamma_c(X,-)$ an additive functor
$\mathrm{Ab}(X)\to\mathbf{Ab}$ ([[def-additive-functor]],
[[def-morphism-of-presheaves]]). Six consistency facts hold; all are immediate
from the definitions, and they record the interface used by the compact-support
calculus.

1. **Well-definedness of $\Gamma_c(X,\varphi)$.** If $x\notin
   \operatorname{Supp}(s)$ then $s_x=0$ in the stalk $\mathcal F_x$
   ([[def-stalk-of-presheaf]]), and since the stalk maps of a morphism commute
   with the germ maps, $\varphi(s)_x=\varphi_x(s_x)=0$, so
   $\operatorname{Supp}(\varphi(s))\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}(s)$. A support
   $\operatorname{Supp}(t)$ is closed in $X$: if $x\notin
   \operatorname{Supp}(t)$ then $t$ agrees with the zero section on some open
   neighbourhood of $x$, by the description of equality of germs
   ([[def-support-of-a-sheaf-section]]). Hence
   $\operatorname{Supp}(\varphi(s))$ is a closed subset of $X$ contained in the
   compact set $\operatorname{Supp}(s)$; being closed in $X$ it is closed in the
   subspace $\operatorname{Supp}(s)$, so it is a compact subset of that compact
   space ([[thm-closed-subspace-of-a-compact-space-is-compact]]) and therefore
   a compact subset of $X$ ([[lem-compactness-of-a-subspace-is-ambient]]), and
   $\Gamma_c(X,\varphi)$ maps $\Gamma_c(X,\mathcal F)$ into
   $\Gamma_c(X,\mathcal G)$. Composites and identities are computed
   componentwise, so $\Gamma_c(X,-)$ is a functor; it is additive because
   $\Gamma(X,-)$ is.
2. **Sheaves of $k$-vector spaces.** If $\mathcal F$ is a sheaf of
   $k$-vector spaces, then $\Gamma_c(X,\mathcal F)$ is a $k$-vector subspace of
   $\Gamma(X,\mathcal F)$: the support of $c\cdot s$ is
   $\operatorname{Supp}(s)$ for $c\ne0$ and $\varnothing$ for $c=0$, and the
   zero section has empty support. For a morphism $\varphi$ of sheaves of
   $k$-vector spaces the map $\Gamma_c(X,\varphi)$ is $k$-linear, since
   $\Gamma(X,\varphi)$ is. The higher derived functors below are computed from
   an injective resolution datum on abelian sheaves, so they are abelian
   groups; the degree-zero part is the $k$-vector space
   $\Gamma_c(X,\mathcal F)$.
3. **Left exactness.** For every short exact sequence
   $0\to\mathcal F'\xrightarrow{\varphi'}\mathcal F\xrightarrow{\varphi}\mathcal F''\to0$
   of abelian sheaves the sequence
   $$0\to\Gamma_c(X,\mathcal F')\xrightarrow{\Gamma_c(\varphi')}\Gamma_c(X,\mathcal F)\xrightarrow{\Gamma_c(\varphi)}\Gamma_c(X,\mathcal F'')$$
   is exact. Injectivity: a monomorphism of sheaves has injective stalk maps
   ([[thm-exactness-of-sheaves-stalkwise]]), so
   $\operatorname{Supp}(\varphi'(s'))=\operatorname{Supp}(s')$ and a section
   killed by $\varphi'$ has all germs zero, hence is zero. Exactness at the
   middle: the kernel of $\Gamma(X,\varphi)$ is the image of
   $\Gamma(X,\varphi')$ by left exactness of global sections
   ([[lem-global-sections-left-exact]]); if $s=\varphi'(s')$ has compact
   support, then $s'$ has the same support,
   $\operatorname{Supp}(s')=\operatorname{Supp}(\varphi'(s'))=
   \operatorname{Supp}(s)$, because $\varphi'$ is stalk-injective, so $s'$ lies
   in $\Gamma_c(X,\mathcal F')$, and the reverse inclusion is immediate from
   $\varphi\circ\varphi'=0$. Thus $\Gamma_c(X,-)$ is an additive left exact
   functor on $\mathrm{Ab}(X)$ ([[def-left-exact-and-right-exact-functor]]).
4. **The derived functors.** Abelian sheaves on $X$ have enough injectives, and
   the embeddings of [[thm-abelian-sheaves-have-enough-injectives]] supply one
   functorial injective resolution datum $\mathcal F\mapsto I^\bullet(\mathcal F)$
   on the whole category $\mathrm{Ab}(X)$, under the Axiom of Choice
   ([[def-right-derived-object-relative-to-injective-resolution-data]]). The
   **compactly supported cohomology** of $\mathcal F$ is
   $$H^q_c(X,\mathcal F):=R^q\Gamma_c(X,\mathcal F):=H^q\bigl(\Gamma_c(X,I^\bullet(\mathcal F)_{\mathrm{del}})\bigr)\qquad(q\ge0),\qquad H^q_c(X,\mathcal F):=0\quad(q<0),$$
   the cohomology of the deleted resolution, as in the pattern of
   [[def-sheaf-cohomology-derived-global-sections]]; for a morphism $\varphi$ we
   write $H^q_c(X,\varphi):=R^q\Gamma_c(X,\varphi)$ for the induced map.
5. **Independence of the datum.** If $J$ is another supplied injective
   resolution datum on $\mathrm{Ab}(X)$, then $R^q\Gamma_c(X,-)$ computed from
   $J$ is naturally isomorphic to $R^q\Gamma_c(X,-)$ computed from $I$
   ([[thm-right-derived-functors-from-two-supplied-injective-resolution-data-are-naturally-isomorphic]]);
   the Axiom of Dependent Choice needed for that comparison follows from AC
   ([[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]]). The notation
   $H^q_c(X,\mathcal F)$ therefore does not depend on the datum up to this
   canonical natural isomorphism.
6. **Delta functor and the compact case.** Since $\Gamma_c(X,-)$ is additive
   and left exact and the datum is defined on all of $\mathrm{Ab}(X)$, the
   functors $R^q\Gamma_c(X,-)$, $q\ge0$, carry connecting maps that make them a
   cohomological delta functor with $R^0\Gamma_c(X,-)\cong\Gamma_c(X,-)$, and
   every short exact sequence of abelian sheaves has a long exact
   compact-support cohomology sequence, natural in the sequence
   ([[thm-right-derived-functors-form-a-cohomological-delta-functor]]). If $X$
   is compact, then for every global section $s$ its support is closed in $X$ by
   fact 1, hence a closed subset of the compact space $X$ and therefore
   compact, so every global section is compactly supported; hence
   $\Gamma_c(X,-)=\Gamma(X,-)$ and
   $H^q_c(X,\mathcal F)=H^q(X,\mathcal F)$ for every $q$, where $H^q(X,-)$ is
   the sheaf cohomology of [[def-sheaf-cohomology-derived-global-sections]]
   computed from the same datum.



lem-compact-support-calculus-of-extension-by-zero-and-closed-pushforward
Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $X$ be a locally compact Hausdorff space, let
$j:U\hookrightarrow X$ be the inclusion of an open subspace with closed
complement $Z=X\setminus U$
([[def-subspace-topology-top]], [[def-locally-compact-space]],
[[def-hausdorff-space]]), and let $i:Z\hookrightarrow X$ be the inclusion of
$Z$. All sheaves below are sheaves of abelian groups
([[def-sheaf-on-topological-space]]); every clause applies in particular to
sheaves of $k$-vector spaces.

1. For every sheaf $\mathcal A$ on $U$ the canonical map
   $\Gamma_c(X,j_!\mathcal A)\to\Gamma_c(U,\mathcal A)$ --- the restriction of
   the identification of $(j_!\mathcal A)(X)$ with a subgroup of
   $\mathcal A(U)$ ([[def-extension-by-zero-abelian-sheaf]],
   [[def-compactly-supported-global-sections]]) --- is an isomorphism, natural
   in $\mathcal A$; the functor $j_!$ carries c-soft sheaves on $U$ to c-soft
   sheaves on $X$ ([[def-c-soft-sheaf]]); and the induced canonical
   isomorphisms $R^q\Gamma_c(X,j_!\mathcal A)\cong R^q\Gamma_c(U,\mathcal A)$
   hold for all $q\ge0$ and are natural in $\mathcal A$.
2. For every sheaf $\mathcal B$ on $Z$ the canonical map
   $\Gamma_c(X,i_*\mathcal B)\to\Gamma_c(Z,\mathcal B)$ --- the restriction of
   the identification of $(i_*\mathcal B)(X)$ with $\mathcal B(Z)$
   ([[def-direct-image-sheaf]], [[lem-direct-image-is-sheaf]]) --- is an
   isomorphism, natural in $\mathcal B$; the functor $i_*$ carries c-soft
   sheaves on $Z$ to c-soft sheaves on $X$; and the induced canonical
   isomorphisms $R^q\Gamma_c(X,i_*\mathcal B)\cong R^q\Gamma_c(Z,\mathcal B)$
   hold for all $q\ge0$ and are natural in $\mathcal B$.
3. If $Z$ is compact ([[def-compact-space]]), then
   $\Gamma_c(X,i_*\mathcal B)\cong\Gamma(Z,\mathcal B)$ and
   $R^q\Gamma_c(X,i_*\mathcal B)\cong H^q(Z,\mathcal B)$ for all $q\ge0$
   ([[def-sheaf-cohomology-derived-global-sections]]).

All isomorphisms are the canonical ones induced by the extension-by-zero
adjunction [[thm-extension-by-zero-adjunction-exactness]] and by the
acyclic-resolution comparison
[[thm-acyclic-resolution-theorem-for-right-derived-functors]]; no choice is
used beyond the injective resolution data and their comparison theorems.



thm-locally-compact-hausdorff-basics
Let $(X, \mathcal{T})$ be a topological space ([[def-topological-space]]). Then:

1. **A neighbourhood base of compact sets.** If $X$ is locally compact
   ([[def-locally-compact-space]]) and Hausdorff ([[def-hausdorff-space]]), then
   every neighbourhood $N$ of a point $x \in X$ ([[def-neighbourhood-top]])
   contains a compact neighbourhood of $x$; so the compact neighbourhoods of $x$
   form a neighbourhood base at $x$.
2. **Heredity along open and closed subspaces.** If $X$ is locally compact and
   Hausdorff and $S \subseteq X$ is open, then the subspace $S$ is locally
   compact ([[def-subspace-topology-top]]). If $X$ is locally compact and
   $F \subseteq X$ is closed, then the subspace $F$ is locally compact; no
   Hausdorff hypothesis is used for this half.
3. **Shrinking inside an open set.** If $X$ is locally compact and Hausdorff,
   $O \subseteq X$ is open and $x \in O$, there is an open $V$ with
   $$x \in V \subseteq \overline{V} \subseteq O$$
   and $\overline{V}$ a compact subset of $X$.
4. **Compact sets sit in open sets with compact closure.** If $X$ is locally
   compact and Hausdorff and $K \subseteq X$ is compact, there is an open
   $V \subseteq X$ with $K \subseteq V$ and $\overline{V}$ a compact subset of
   $X$ ([[def-interior-closure-boundary-top]]).

**No choice principle is used**; every cover produced below is defined by a
formula and thinned by [[lem-compactness-of-a-subspace-is-ambient]], which returns
members rather than indices.



thm-compact-subset-of-a-hausdorff-space-is-closed
Let $(X, \mathcal{T})$ be a Hausdorff topological space
([[def-hausdorff-space]], [[def-topological-space]]), with compact subsets as in
[[def-compact-space]]. Then:

1. **A point and a disjoint compact set are separated.** If $K \subseteq X$ is
   compact and $x \in X \setminus K$, there are $U, V \in \mathcal{T}$ with
   $$x \in U, \qquad K \subseteq V, \qquad U \cap V = \varnothing .$$
2. **Two disjoint compact sets are separated.** If $K, L \subseteq X$ are compact
   and $K \cap L = \varnothing$, there are $U, V \in \mathcal{T}$ with
   $$L \subseteq U, \qquad K \subseteq V, \qquad U \cap V = \varnothing .$$
3. **Compact implies closed.** Every compact subset of $X$ is closed in $X$.
4. **In a compact Hausdorff space the two classes coincide.** If in addition
   $(X, \mathcal{T})$ is compact, then a subset of $X$ is compact if and only if
   it is closed.

**The proof is written choice-free, and that is not a stylistic preference.** The
textbook argument says "for each $y \in K$ choose disjoint open $U_y, V_y$",
which is a selection over an arbitrary index set and therefore an appeal to the
full Axiom of Choice. What is done below instead is to take the family of **all**
open $V$ that admit some open $U \ni x$ disjoint from them — a family cut out by
a formula, with nothing selected — extract a finite subcover from it, and only
then make finitely many selections, which [[lem-finite-choice]] supplies as a
theorem of ZF.



thm-closed-subspace-of-a-compact-space-is-compact
Let $(X, \mathcal{T})$ be a topological space ([[def-topological-space]]), with
subspaces as in [[def-subspace-topology-top]] and compactness as in
[[def-compact-space]]. Then:

1. **Closed in compact is compact.** If $(X, \mathcal{T})$ is compact and
   $F \subseteq X$ is closed in $X$, then $F$ is a compact subset of $X$.
2. **Finite unions.** If $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and $K_0, \dots, K_n$ are compact
   subsets of $X$, then $K_0 \cup \dots \cup K_n$ is a compact subset of $X$. The
   union of the empty list is $\varnothing$, which is a compact subset of every
   space.

Claim 1 needs $X$ to be compact and claim 2 does not; no hypothesis of any kind
is placed on $X$ in claim 2. **No choice principle is used**: claim 1 selects
nothing, taking a least index where a selection would be natural, and claim 2
makes finitely many selections through [[lem-finite-choice]], a theorem of ZF.



lem-filtered-colimits-of-abelian-groups-are-exact
Let $\mathcal J$ be a small filtered category
([[def-filtered-category-and-filtered-colimit]]). The filtered colimit functor
$$\operatorname*{colim}_{\mathcal J}:\mathbf{Ab}^{\mathcal J}\to\mathbf{Ab}$$
on abelian groups is exact ([[def-exact-functor-between-abelian-categories]]).
Equivalently:

1. for a $\mathcal J$-indexed diagram of short exact sequences
   $0\to A_j\to B_j\to C_j\to 0$ of abelian groups, the colimit sequence
   $$0\to\operatorname*{colim}_jA_j\to\operatorname*{colim}_jB_j\to\operatorname*{colim}_jC_j\to 0$$
   is short exact;
2. for a $\mathcal J$-indexed diagram of cochain complexes of abelian groups
   $K_j^\bullet$, the canonical map
   $\operatorname*{colim}_jH^p(K_j^\bullet)\to H^p(\operatorname*{colim}_jK_j^\bullet)$
   is an isomorphism for every $p\in\mathbb Z$; in particular the colimit of a
   diagram of exact complexes is exact in every degree.



cor-noether-normalisation-module-finiteness
Let $k$ be a field and let $A$ be a nonzero finite-type $k$-algebra. Then
there exist algebraically independent elements $z_1,\ldots,z_d\in A$ such that
$A$ is a module-finite algebra over the polynomial ring
$k[z_1,\ldots,z_d]$.



lem-polynomial-algebras-over-fields-are-integrally-closed
For every field $K$ and every finite $d\ge0$, the ring $K[x_1,\ldots,x_d]$ is
an integrally closed domain.



lem-integrally-closed-minimal-polynomial-coefficients
Let $A$ be an integrally closed domain with field of fractions $K$, let $L/K$ be a field extension, and let $u\in L$ be integral over $A$. Then the minimal polynomial of $u$ over $K$ has coefficients in $A$.

In particular, if $f(T)\in A[T]$ is monic and factors in $K[T]$ as
$$ f(T)=(T-a)h(T) $$
with $a\in K$, then $h(T)\in A[T]$.



cor-fields-of-characteristic-zero-and-finite-fields-are-perfect
Every field of characteristic zero is perfect. Every finite field is perfect,
and every algebraically closed field is perfect.



thm-primitive-element-theorem-for-finite-separable-extensions
Let $E=F(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r)$ be a finite extension. If all but possibly
one of the generators are separable over $F$, then $E/F$ is simple. In
particular, every finite separable extension is simple.



thm-bezout-identity-for-polynomials
Let $F$ be a field and let $f,g\in F[x]$ be not both zero. Repeated polynomial division terminates at a last nonzero remainder, whose monic associate is $d=\gcd(f,g)$. There are $A,B\in F[x]$ such that

$$ Af+Bg=d. $$

Moreover, $d$ divides both $f$ and $g$, and every common divisor of $f$ and $g$ divides $d$.



thm-fundamental-theorem-of-algebra-liouville-proof
Every nonconstant complex polynomial has a complex root.

This proof uses Liouville's theorem and is independent of the minimum-modulus proof cited in the accompanying agreement remark.



thm-riemann-extension-across-hypersurface-zero-sets
Let $U\subseteq\mathbb C^m$ be a domain, let $g:U\to\mathbb C$ be holomorphic
and not identically zero, and let
$f:U\setminus Z(g)\to\mathbb C$ be holomorphic. Assume that $f$ is locally

extension $F:U\to\mathbb C$ with
$F|_{U\setminus Z(g)}=f$.



prop-holomorphic-functions-are-continuous-and-separately-holomorphic
Let $U\subseteq\mathbb C^m$ be open and let $f:U\to\mathbb C$ be holomorphic
([[def-holomorphic-function-in-several-complex-variables]]). Then $f$ is
continuous on $U$ and separately holomorphic on $U$
([[def-separately-holomorphic-function]]). Moreover, for $a\in U$ and $k<m$ the
slice $f_{a,k}$ is complex differentiable at $a_k$ with derivative
$Df(a)e_k$, and

$$Df(a)e_k=\partial_{z_k}f(a),\qquad Df(a)h=\sum_{k<m}\bigl(\partial_{z_k}f(a)\bigr)h_k .$$



thm-power-series-expansion-in-several-complex-variables
Fix $m\ge1$, $a\in\mathbb C^m$ and a polyradius $\rho$, let
$f:\Delta_\rho(a)\to\mathbb C$ be continuous and separately holomorphic, let $r$
be a polyradius with $r_k<\rho_k$ for every $k<m$, and let
$C_k(t)=a_k+r_k\exp(it)$ on $[0,2\pi]$. For each multi-index $\alpha$ set

$$c_\alpha:=\frac1{(2\pi i)^m}\int_{C_0}\!\cdots\!\int_{C_{m-1}} f(\zeta)\prod_{k<m}(\zeta_k-a_k)^{-\alpha_k-1}\,d\zeta_{m-1}\cdots d\zeta_0 ,$$

an iterated integral as in the polydisc Cauchy formula. Then, with
$M=\sup_{\Gamma_r(a)}|f|$,

$$|c_\alpha|\le M\prod_{k<m}r_k^{-\alpha_k},$$

and for every real $\theta$ with $0<\theta<1$ the series
$\sum_\alpha c_\alpha(z-a)^\alpha$ converges absolutely and uniformly on
$\overline\Delta_{\theta r}(a)$ with

$$f(z)=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha(z-a)^\alpha\qquad(z\in\overline\Delta_{\theta r}(a)).$$

Since every $z\in\Delta_r(a)$ lies in $\overline\Delta_{\theta r}(a)$ for some
$\theta<1$, the expansion holds throughout $\Delta_r(a)$.

**The coefficients are asserted here only as those iterated integrals.** That
$c_\alpha$ equals $\partial^\alpha f(a)/\alpha!$ needs termwise differentiation
and is not claimed by this statement.



cor-uniqueness-of-multivariable-power-series-coefficients
Let $m\ge1$, $a\in\mathbb C^m$ and let $r$ be a polyradius. Suppose
$c,c':\mathbb N^m\to\mathbb C$ both satisfy a bound
$|c_\alpha|\le M\prod_{k<m}r_k^{-\alpha_k}$ and
$|c'_\alpha|\le M'\prod_{k<m}r_k^{-\alpha_k}$, and suppose

$$\sum_\alpha c_\alpha(z-a)^\alpha=\sum_\alpha c'_\alpha(z-a)^\alpha \qquad\text{for every }z\in\Delta_r(a).$$

Then $c_\alpha=c'_\alpha$ for every multi-index $\alpha$. In particular a
function has at most one such power-series representation about a given centre,
and its coefficients are $\partial^\alpha_zf(a)/\alpha!$.



lem-complex-algebraic-boundary-points-admit-normalized-curve-arcs
Assume AC. Let $V$ be an irreducible complex algebraic variety of positive
dimension ([[def-classical-algebraic-prevariety-regular-maps-and-varieties]]),
let $O\subseteq V$ be a nonempty Zariski open subset and let $z\in V$. There is
an irreducible algebraic curve $C\subseteq V$ through $z$ not contained in
$V\setminus O$. There is also a holomorphic map $\gamma:\Delta\to V$ from a
sufficiently small complex disk, with $\gamma(0)=z$ and $\gamma(s)\in O$ for
all $s\ne0$; here $\Delta$ is a disk centred at $0$ and a map into $V$ is
holomorphic when its coordinate functions in an affine model containing its
image are holomorphic. The arc can be obtained by analytically parametrizing a
point over $z$ in the finite normalization of $C$.



def-classical-algebraic-prevariety-regular-maps-and-varieties
Fix an algebraically closed field $k$. The affine models used here are polynomial zero sets $Z\subseteq k^n$, including empty and reducible sets. Their closed subsets are common zero loci of polynomial families, relative to $Z$. Intersections combine equations, and finite unions use products of equations, so these are the closed sets of a topology.

For an open $U\subseteq Z$, let $\mathcal O_Z(U)$ consist of functions $U\to k$ locally equal to $a/b$ for polynomials $a,b$ with $b$ nonvanishing on the chosen neighbourhood. Pointwise operations and restriction make these sets a sheaf of $k$-algebras: quotient expressions persist on restriction, and functions that agree locally glue uniquely with the same local quotient property. At a point z its stalk is the ring of germs of such functions. Evaluation at z maps it onto k, with kernel the germs vanishing at z. A germ nonzero at z is invertible: shrink to where its numerator and denominator are both nonzero and interchange them. Thus the kernel is the unique maximal ideal. These affine models are locally ringed spaces in the sense of [[def-locally-ringed-space]], with residue fields canonically k.

A **classical algebraic prevariety** over k is a quasi-compact locally ringed space with a structure sheaf of k-algebras, covered by open subspaces isomorphic over k to these affine models. Here quasi-compact means every open cover has a finite subcover. Equivalently it admits a finite affine-model cover. To justify the converse, each affine model is quasi-compact: polynomial principal opens $D_Z(h)=\{z:h(z)\ne0\}$ form a basis. Given an open cover, take all h whose principal opens lie in a member. Their principal opens cover Z. The ideal generated by these h in $k[t_1,\ldots,t_n]$ is finitely generated by [[thm-hilbert-basis-theorem]], iterated from the field k. Express finitely many generators using finitely many of the original h. Those finitely many h have the same common zero locus as the whole ideal, hence their principal opens still cover Z and yield a finite subcover. The empty model has the empty subcover. A finite union of quasi-compact subspaces is quasi-compact.

A map is **regular** when it is a morphism of locally ringed spaces as in [[def-morphism-locally-ringed-spaces]] whose sheaf maps respect k. For these function sheaves its sheaf map is necessarily pullback of functions: the local stalk map induces a k-map between residue fields k, hence the identity, so the value of a pulled-back section at x is its value at the image of x. Conversely, continuous pullback of local regular functions gives a local stalk map because a function vanishing at the image vanishes after pullback.

Regularity can therefore be checked on affine charts: near every source point choose a target affine chart containing its image and an open neighbourhood in a source affine chart mapped into it; the target coordinate functions must pull back to locally polynomial quotients. This condition also implies continuity. Indeed zero sets of locally polynomial quotients are locally polynomial zero sets, hence closed; pulling back polynomial equations consequently gives closed sets. Substitution then pulls every local quotient on the target back to a local quotient on the source, using this continuity to restrict to its domain and its nonvanishing denominator. Thus the chart condition and the locally ringed-space definition agree.

A prevariety is **separated** when the equalizer of every pair of regular maps into it, from any classical prevariety, is closed. A **classical algebraic variety** is a separated prevariety. Here varieties may be reducible or empty; an **irreducible classical variety** is nonempty and irreducible in the sense of [[def-irreducible-topological-space-and-subset]]. These definitions and the local verifications above use no Axiom of Choice.


thm-integrality-and-finite-module-equivalences
Let $A\subseteq B$ be commutative rings with $A\ne0$, and let $b\in B$. The following are equivalent: $b$ is integral over $A$; $A[b]$ is finitely generated as an $A$-module; and there exists a faithful $A[b]$-module that is finitely generated over $A$, where faithful means that $rM=0$ implies $r=0$ for $r\in A[b]$. See [[def-integral-element-and-algebraic-integer]].
