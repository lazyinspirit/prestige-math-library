99: 1.1 **The two charts are mutually inverse.** By [[def-dual-canonical-canonical-and-quasi-canonical-fock-bases]] the vectors $L_B$ and $M_A$ are two $\mathbb Z[s,s^{-1}]$-bases of the finite weight spaces of the Fock module, so the transition matrices $R(s)=(p_{A,B}(-s))$ with $L_B=\sum_AR_{A,B}(s)M_A$ and $D(s)=(d_{A,B})$ with $M_B=\sum_Ad_{A,B}(s)L_A$ satisfy $R(s)D(s)=D(s)R(s)=1$; both are triangular with diagonal entries $1$ on each weight block, and the quasi-canonical vector is $P_A=\sum_Bd_{A,B}(s)M_B$. The polynomial entries and the chart conventions are those of [[def-dual-canonical-canonical-and-quasi-canonical-fock-bases]]. [given, algebra]

105: 3.1 **Transfer to the Hecke side.** By [F2] the Schur quotient is exact, sends $N(B)$ to $S(B)$, $L(A)$ to $D(A)$ for standard $A$ and kills the off-standard simples; applying it to a composition series of $N(B)$ and using [F1] gives $[S(B):D(A)]=[N(B):L(A)]=d_{A,B}(1)$ for $A\in\mathrm{Std}^\Lambda$ and every $B\in\mathrm{Col}^\Lambda$, the sum being finite on each weight block. 107: 4.1 **Shape of the matrix.** The full matrix $D=(d_{A,B})$ is unitriangular on $\mathrm{Col}^\Lambda$ by step 1.1; restricting rows to the simple labels $A\in\mathrm{Std}^\Lambda$ (all of which occur, and which index the simples by [[thm-degenerate-ariki-categorification]]) leaves a rectangular matrix, whose square submatrix on the standard columns is again unitriangular with diagonal entries $1$. The supported weight blocks and labels agree under the actual arbitrary-$I$ cut maps of the $G_0$ theorem, so this same coefficient calculation applies to every admitted consecutive interval. Off standard columns the equation $D(B)=0$ says precisely that $D(B)$ is not a simple module label, so the identity $[S(B):D(B)]=1$ is asserted only for $B\in\mathrm{Std}^\Lambda$. This proves (ii). The statement is not that the rectangular matrix is invertible, and no off-standard simple is created. [step 3.1, given, algebra]
109: 5.1 **The polynomial coefficient is not a transposed inverse.** In the coefficient convention used in the proof, the decomposition array has rows the simple labels and columns the Specht labels, and its entries are the coefficients of $D(s)$ at $s=1$; since $R(s)=D(s)^{-1}$, its transpose is $D(s)^{-t}$, which is in general different from the $L$-in-$M$ chart $R(s)$. On the two-factor space with labels $01<10$ one has $L_{10}=M_{10}-sM_{01}$, so $R_{01,10}=-s$ is the negative-entry chart, whereas its inverse has $D_{01,10}=s$, while the true decomposition entry is $d_{01,10}(1)=1$: $S(10)$ is the simple $D(01)$ and not a formal negative combination. Hence with simple rows and Specht columns the decomposition matrix is the standard-row restriction of $D$; with Specht rows and simple columns it is its transpose. Neither convention uses the transpose of $R$. This completes (iii) together with steps 1.2, whose entry stabilizer $K$ and column Levi $J$ remain distinct and whose multiplicities are the finite Levi cancellation values at parameter one; all statements are ungraded.
