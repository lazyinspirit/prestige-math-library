84: 1.1 **Construction from actual exact sequences.** Let $\bar P(A)$ be the labelled opposite projective cover. Its highest-weight sequence is $0\to\bar K(A)\to\bar P(A)\to\bar\Delta(A)\to0$, with $\bar K(A)$ standard-filtered by labels strictly above $A$. Applying $\Psi$ gives $0\to M(A)\to X(A)\to\Psi\bar K(A)\to0$, where $X(A)=\Psi\bar P(A)$ and the quotient has a finite standard flag with labels strictly below $A$. Its endomorphism algebra is the opposite of the local endomorphism algebra of $\bar P(A)$, so $X(A)$ is indecomposable. For an arbitrary extension $0\to X(A)\to Z\to M(B)\to0$, extension closure makes $Z$ standard-filtered. Inverting $\Psi$ turns this into an extension with source projective $\bar P(A)$ as quotient, which splits; exact equivalence transfers the splitting back. Hence $\operatorname{Ext}^1(M(B),X(A))=0$ for every $B$. The proved homological criterion of [F1] gives a costandard flag of $X(A)$, so it is the required actual tilting object with normalized bottom-standard inclusion. [F5, F1, F2, F3, algebra]
86: 2.1 **Uniqueness.** Let $T,T'$ be two indecomposable tilting objects with $M(A)$ once at the bottom and all other labels $\prec A$. Extending the common normalized bottom inclusion both ways uses $\operatorname{Ext}^1$ vanishing against standard flags, and the resulting composites restrict to the identity on the bottom standard, hence are not nilpotent; finite-dimensional local $\operatorname{End}$ and Fitting's lemma force them to be isomorphisms. So $T\cong T'$, and [F1] identifies its unique maximal support label as $A$. [F1, step 1.1]
88: 3.1 **Every tilting is a sum of these.** Let $T\ne0$ be an arbitrary tilting object and let $\Gamma$ be the lower ideal generated by its entire composition support. Choose one maximal support label $A$, which is maximal in $\Gamma$, and put $m=[T:L(A)]\ge1$. All standard and costandard sections of $T$ have labels in $\Gamma$, so it is tilting in that truncated highest-weight category. The proved maximal-standard trace in [F1] injects $\Delta(A)^{\oplus m}$ into $T$ with standard-filtered quotient. Choose one copy $i:\Delta(A)\hookrightarrow T$; its quotient is still standard-filtered, since the other $m-1$ copies and the trace quotient have such flags. Let $j:\Delta(A)\hookrightarrow X(A)$ be the actual normalized inclusion of step 1.1. Since $X(A)/\Delta(A)$ is standard-filtered and $T$ is tilting, $\operatorname{Ext}^1(X(A)/\Delta(A),T)=0$ extends $i$ to $f:X(A)\to T$ with $fj=i$. Similarly $\operatorname{Ext}^1(T/\Delta(A),X(A))=0$ extends $j$ to $g:T\to X(A)$ with $gi=j$. The composite $gf$ fixes the nonzero bottom standard of the indecomposable $X(A)$, so it is invertible by the finite local-End/Fitting argument. Hence $f$ splits with retraction $(gf)^{-1}g$, and $T\cong X(A)\oplus T'$. The complement is tilting by summand closure and has strictly smaller length; induction proves the stated finite decomposition. Incomparable maximal support labels and multiplicities $m>1$ are both allowed: no normalized flag of the entire $T$ was assumed. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]
90: 3.2 **Duality, ideals and Grothendieck basis.** Restricted duality makes $DT(A)$ an indecomposable tilting object with the same composition support $\le A$ and $[DT(A):L(A)]=1$. In the lower highest-weight ideal of labels $\le A$, the maximal-standard trace proved in [F1] gives an inclusion $M(A)\hookrightarrow DT(A)$ with standard-filtered quotient of strictly smaller labels, since its multiplicity space has dimension one. This supplies an actual normalized standard flag, so uniqueness identifies $DT(A)\cong T(A)$. If $\Gamma$ is a lower ideal containing $A$, every support label of $T(A)$ lies in $\Gamma$, so $T(A)$ computed in the ambient block lies in the Serre subcategory of $\Gamma$ and coincides with the partial tilting there; the classes $[T(A)]$ are unitriangular in the standard basis by the flag multiplicities, hence form a Grothendieck basis. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1]
92: 4.1 **The projection kernel and standard embeddings.** The projection $\pi$ of [F4] has kernel spanned by the $L$-vectors off $\mathrm{Std}^\Lambda$ and is $U$-stable; the residue functors have nonnegative composition coefficients, so iterating them on an off-standard simple never produces a standard composition factor. For this embedding argument, enlarge a finite upper alphabet if necessary while keeping the lower endpoint and the Levi column heights fixed. The blocks of the original labels are unchanged, because their entry multisets are unchanged; the established Fock projection and its off-standard kernel apply on the enlarged alphabet, where the standard predicate for those labels is the same. Every generalized standard $M(B)$ then embeds into an iterated $e$-word applied to an irreducible standard $M(C)$: add successively huge offsets to the later columns so that all later entries lie above the earlier ones, making $C$ the unique minimum in its fixed-multiset tableau order and hence $M(C)=L(C)$ simple; then lower the later-column entries back, column by column and bottom to top, with the explicit residue word. A later column stays above the active residues, so a wrong move can only occur in an earlier column and strictly increases an earlier prefix weight; hence $B$ is the unique maximal final standard section and occurs once, and the canonical maximal trace embeds $M(B)$ at the bottom. If $L(A)$ embedded into $M(B)$ with $A$ off standard, adjunction would give the standard $L(C)$ as a quotient of an $f$-word applied to $L(A)$, impossible by kernel stability. [F4, step 3.1]
