72: 1.1 **The standard-class map is an isomorphism.** In each finite block the standard classes form a basis of the Grothendieck group and characters are injective, while the residue functors act on the standard basis by the explicit single-entry moves; the Fock monomials satisfy the identical relations and have the same degrees. Hence $[M(A)]\mapsto M_A$ is a degree-preserving isomorphism in each block, and it extends to the direct sum over the blocks because the direct sum decomposition is by central character on both sides. [F1, F3, algebra]
74: 2.1 **The transition matrix and duality at $1$.** Write $D_{A,B}=d_{A,B}(1)=[M(B):L(A)]$ with simple rows and standard columns. Then $[M(B)]=\sum_A D_{A,B}[L(A)]$, so it is the simple-to-standard expansion that uses $D^{-1}$. BGG reciprocity instead gives $[P(A):M(B)]=D_{A,B}$, hence $[P(A)]=\sum_BD_{A,B}[M(B)]$; this matrix is the transposed standard/simple multiplicity array when rows are projective labels, not its inverse. Under the standard-class map these are exactly the defining $P_A$ and $L_A$ expansions. On the actual finite type-$A$ cuts, each nonconstant term of the quasi-$R$ root-factor expansion has a factor $s-s^{-1}$ and coefficients regular at $s=1$ (the quantum-factorial denominators have nonzero ordinary-factorial values there). Thus the tensor bar matrix specializes to the identity; passage to the primitive wedge factors preserves this specialization. The source monomial Gram matrices, which are its inverse, are consequently the identity at $1$. Multiplying the $D$ projective array by the $D^{-1}$ simple array yields $\langle P_A,L_B\rangle=\delta_{AB}$. No inverse BGG multiplicity matrix is asserted. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, algebra]
76: 3.1 **The general Hom pairing.** For a projective $P$ and a finite-length $N$, the functor $\operatorname{Hom}(P,-)$ is exact, so its dimension on $N$ is additive on a composition series and equals the pairing of $[P]$ with $[N]$ under the $P/L$ duality of step 2.1; in particular $\langle P_A,L_B\rangle=\delta_{AB}$. This is a projective/simple statement: the standard/simple matrix is the unitriangular mixed multiplicity matrix, not the identity, so no standard/simple orthogonality is claimed. [F2, step 2.1]
