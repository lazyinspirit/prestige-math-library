107: 2.2 **The supported blocks.** Set $x=x_1$, $y=x_2$, $u=s_1$. The defining relations of [[def-degenerate-cyclotomic-hecke-algebra]] give $u^2=1$, $[x,y]=0$, $ux=yu-1$, $uy=xu+1$ and $x^2=0$ in the relevant degree; from $ux^2=0$ and the cross relations one obtains $y^2u=x+y$, hence $y^2=(x+y)u$, and reducing the relation $y^2=(x+y)u$ decreases the $y$-exponent while killing $x$-exponents at least $2$, so the eight words $1,x,y,xy,u,xu,yu,xyu$ span the degree-$2$ algebra. Map these generators to $(N,-N,-J)$ on $M_2(\mathbb C)$ with $N=E_{12}$ and $J$ the swap matrix, to $(\epsilon,1+\epsilon,1)$ on $B_+=\mathbb C[\epsilon]/\epsilon^2$, and to $(\epsilon,-1+\epsilon,-1)$ on $B_-=\mathbb C[\epsilon]/\epsilon^2$; direct matrix computation verifies $u^2=1$, $[x,y]=0$, $x^2=0$, $ux=yu-1$ and $uy=xu+1$ in all three factors. The central element $z=x+y$ has images $0$, $1+2\epsilon$ and $-1+2\epsilon$, and the Hermite polynomials $E_0=(1-z^2)^2$, $E_\pm=(1-E_0\pm(3z-z^3)/2)/2$ map to the three separate identities; each factor is generated by its images of $x$ and $u$, so the map from the span of the eight words onto the eight-dimensional product is surjective and hence, by the spanning bound, an isomorphism. The $I=\{0\}$ spectral content cut keeps the factor $M_2(\mathbb C)$ at content $2\alpha_0$ and discards the adjacent-content factors, so the supported degree-$2$ block is $M_2(\mathbb C)$ with simple dimension $2$; degree one is $\mathbb C[x]/x^2$ with simple dimension $1$ at content $\alpha_0$, and degree zero is $\mathbb C$. To verify vanishing independently of categorification, suppose an all-zero-residue supported module exists in degree at least three. Its restriction to the first two strands lies in the displayed $M_2$ factor, hence $x_2=-x_1$ and $x_1^2=0$. Since $s_2$ commutes with $x_1$, it also commutes with $x_2$. The affine recursion gives $x_3=s_2x_2s_2+s_2=x_2+s_2$. But a commuting sum of a square-zero operator and an involution has generalized eigenvalues only $1,-1$: on the two $s_2$ eigenspaces it is $\pm1+x_2$, each invertible with inverse $\pm1-x_2$. This contradicts the nilpotence required by residue zero for $x_3$. Every nonzero finite-dimensional algebra has its nonzero regular module, so no such supported algebra exists in degree three or higher. [step 1.1, given, algebra]
