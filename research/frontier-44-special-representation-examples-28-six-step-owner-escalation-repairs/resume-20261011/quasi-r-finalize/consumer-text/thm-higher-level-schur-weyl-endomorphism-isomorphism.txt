82: 1.1 The Hecke action lies in the g-endomorphisms. For the empty pyramid, the empty tensor in degree zero and its ground object give $T_0=U_0=H_0=\mathbb C$. In positive degree $V=0$ gives $T_d=U_d=0$, and the level-zero cyclotomic relation is the empty product $1$, so $H_d=0$. The stated endomorphism identities and faithfulness follow directly, with the unique zero-algebra map injective; the remaining argument assumes a nonempty pyramid. Each generator $x_j$ of the truncated tensor action is built from the trace Casimir and place permutations and commutes with the diagonal $\mathfrak g$-action; the place permutations are natural in the module variable, so the resulting right $H_d(\mathbf a)$-action on $T$ consists of $\mathfrak g$-endomorphisms, and its image is $B_d$ by definition. Hence there is a surjection $H_d(\mathbf a)\twoheadrightarrow B_d$ and an inclusion $B_d\subseteq\operatorname{End}_{\mathfrak g}(T)^{\mathrm{op}}$, with the opposite convention fixed by the right action. [F1, algebra]
84: 1.2 The Whittaker comparison is injective. Let $\varphi:T\to T$ be nonzero and put $X=\operatorname{im}\varphi$, a nonzero submodule of the finite-length object $T$. Choose a simple submodule $L(A)\subseteq X$; because $\operatorname{soc}X\subseteq\operatorname{soc}T$, [F2] gives $A\in\mathrm{Std}$. Exactness gives an injection $V(L(A))\hookrightarrow V(X)$, and the first module is nonzero, so $V(X)\ne0$. Exactness also identifies $V(X)$ with the image of $V(\varphi)$, hence $V(\varphi)\ne0$. This proves injectivity of $\operatorname{End}_{\mathfrak g}(T)^{\mathrm{op}}\to\operatorname{End}_{W_d}(V_c^{\otimes d})^{\mathrm{op}}$. No restriction on all composition factors of $T$ is used. [F2, algebra]
86: 2.1 The image of the comparison. On $V(T)=V_c^{\otimes d}$ the induced map lands in the centralizer of the finite-W action, $\operatorname{End}_{W_d}(V_c^{\otimes d})^{\mathrm{op}}$; by the filtered double centralizer [F3] this centralizer equals $B_d$; and the induced map contains $B_d$ because every Hecke generator on $V_c^{\otimes d}$ arises from the corresponding endomorphism of $T$ by [F1]. Since $B_d$ is the whole target, the injective map of step 1.2 is also surjective, so $\operatorname{End}_{\mathfrak g}(T)^{\mathrm{op}}=B_d$. [F1, F3, step 1.2, algebra]
90: 3.1 Central components. The central idempotents $f_\alpha$ of the finite-dimensional algebra $B_d$ decompose $T=\bigoplus_\alpha T_\alpha$ and $B_d=\bigoplus_\alpha B_\alpha$; applying the isomorphism of step 2.1 componentwise gives $\operatorname{End}_{\mathfrak g}(T_\alpha)^{\mathrm{op}}=f_\alpha B_df_\alpha=B_\alpha$. The component $B_\alpha$ sits inside the cyclotomic block as the corner $f_\alpha H_\alpha(\mathbf a)f_\alpha$ by [F4]; it is not the entire degree-$d$ algebra. [F4, step 2.1, algebra]
92: 4.1 Failure of raw faithfulness below the stable range. For a nonempty pyramid, suppose $d>q_l$ and use an enlargement by full ground rows with smallest height at least $d$. In its last column, raise the first $q_l+1$ entries above their ground values by $1$; raise its top entry further by $d-q_l-1$, and leave every other entry ground. This is column strict, has total degree $d$, and is standard: for columns with the same charge, the changed rightmost entry is at least every unchanged entry to its left in that row; different charge cosets are incomparable. The first $q_l$ rows of this column are retained, but its next row is deleted, so this standard label is noncontractible. Its nonzero large finite-W simple belongs to a complementary central block. Hence the supporting idempotent $f$ of [F4] is proper; the faithful large tensor progenerator makes $1-f$ nonzero also in the large Hecke algebra. The small raw action has the nonzero kernel $(1-f)H_d(\mathbf a)$ and is not faithful. Together with step 2.2 this proves the asserted precise stable-range criterion. [F2, F4, step 2.2, construct, algebra] ∎
