69: 1.1 **The actual normalized projective image.** Fix one finite highest-weight central summand and a label $B$, and put $X(B)=\Psi(P^{\bar p}(B))$. The source projective has its highest-weight exact sequence $0\to\bar K(B)\to P^{\bar p}(B)\to\bar\Delta(B)\to0$, with a finite standard flag on $\bar K(B)$ using strictly higher source labels. Exact contravariance of $\Psi$ gives $0\to\Delta(B)\to X(B)\to\Psi\bar K(B)\to0$. Order reversal makes the quotient standard-filtered by labels strictly below $B$. Thus this is an actual normalized bottom inclusion, and $\Delta(B)$ occurs once. The endomorphism ring of $X(B)$ is opposite to the local ring of its source projective cover, so $X(B)$ is indecomposable. [F1, F2, F5]
71: 1.2 **The CWZ parameter, coproduct and ordered labels.** In the two-wedge convention of Cheng–Wang–Zhang, write $q$ for their parameter and $K_a$ for their toral generator. The exact transport to [F4] is $q\mapsto s^{-1}$, $K_a\mapsto D_a^{-1}$, $E_a\mapsto E_a$, $F_a\mapsto F_a$, and $K_f\mapsto M_f$ for the same two decreasing columns in the same left/right order. Their natural eigenvalue $q^{\delta_{ab}}$ becomes $s^{-\delta_{ab}}$, the eigenvalue of $D_a^{-1}$. The toral relations and the $E/F$ commutator agree because both numerator and denominator of the toral fraction reverse sign. Their coproducts are $\Delta E_a=1\otimes E_a+E_a\otimes K_{a+1}K_a^{-1}$ and $\Delta F_a=F_a\otimes1+K_aK_{a+1}^{-1}\otimes F_a$; these become exactly $1\otimes E_a+E_a\otimes D_aD_{a+1}^{-1}$ and $F_a\otimes1+D_a^{-1}D_{a+1}\otimes F_a$ of [F4]. Thus reciprocal parameter substitution alone is accompanied by toral inversion, and no reversal of tensor factors or tableau entries is used. The quantum Serre coefficients are unchanged under reciprocation, and the two bars agree on coefficients and generators: both fix $E_a,F_a$ and invert the torus. The displayed source coproduct and ordered factor actions are those of [[def-the-fock-convention-dictionary-for-finite-type-a-quantum-modules]]. The ordered wedge basis has coefficient-one adjacent-entry replacement actions, so it identifies the elementary wedge factors; their relation on unordered wedges becomes $-s$, consistently with this transport. A swap of exclusive left $b>$ right $a$ gives a strictly smaller label in both orders, since the left prefix changes by the positive root $\delta_a-\delta_b$. Consequently the CWZ positive-$q$ triangular chart maps to the negative-$s^{-1}$ chart of $T$, not to the positive-$s$ chart of $L$. [F4, algebra]
73: 2.1 **Tilting and an actual normalized dual flag.** For an extension $0\to X(B)\to Z\to\Delta(A)\to0$, the middle object is standard-filtered by extension closure. Inverting $\Psi$ turns it into an extension with the opposite projective as quotient, which splits. Hence $\operatorname{Ext}^1(\Delta(A),X(B))=0$ for every $A$, and the criterion of [F1] makes $X(B)$ costandard-filtered and therefore tilting. The object $DX(B)$ is also tilting and indecomposable; duality fixes simple classes, so all its composition labels are $\le B$ and $[DX(B):L(B)]=1$. In the lower highest-weight ideal of labels $\le B$, $\Delta(B)$ is the maximal-labelled projective. The maximal standard trace proved in [[def-standard-costandard-filtered-and-tilting-objects-in-a-highest-weight-category]] injects $\Delta(B)$ into the standard-filtered $DX(B)$ with quotient standard-filtered by strictly smaller labels: its multiplicity space has dimension $[DX(B):L(B)]=1$. Thus the dual has an actual normalized bottom-standard sequence. Uniqueness of such indecomposable tiltings in [F1] gives $X(B)\cong DX(B)$. This normalization uses the proved maximal trace and does not follow from exchanging the kinds of flags alone. [F1, F2, step 1.1]
77: 3.1 **The opposite BGG coefficient identity.** From the tilting nature and maximal support of $X(B)$, every multiplicity can be computed as $(X(B):\Delta(A))=\dim\operatorname{Hom}(X(B),\nabla(A))$. Applying the exact contravariant $\Psi$ to the finite standard flag of $\bar P(B)$ reverses its order but preserves the multiplicities of its corresponding standard sections. Opposite BGG reciprocity therefore gives $(X(B):M(A))=(\bar P(B):\bar M(A))=[\bar M(A):\bar L(B)]$, the opposite parabolic composition coefficient. [F1, F2, step 2.1]
79: 3.2 **Bar-fixed pair formula and coefficient.** The predecessor procedure in step 2.2 terminates: $\sum L-\sum R$ decreases by one at each predecessor, all entries remain in the finite interval between the original smallest and largest entries, and both column sizes are fixed. At its terminal tuple there is no positive exclusive pair; in the fixed union/intersection fiber this is the unique minimal label, so its monomial is fixed by the triangular bar of [F4]. Apply the recorded $E_a,F_a$ in reverse predecessor order; they are bar-fixed. The subset calculation of step 2.2 proves inductively that the resulting bar-fixed vector is $U_f=\sum_{\Sigma\subseteq\Sigma_f^+}s^{-|\Sigma|}M_{f_\Sigma}$. The empty subset gives $M_f$, every nonempty subset gives a lower label, and disjoint exclusive endpoints make distinct subsets give distinct tableaux. Thus $U_f$ has precisely the negative-chart triangular condition of $T_f$, and uniqueness in [F4] gives $U_f=T_f$. In particular every single greedy-pair coefficient is $s^{-1}$ and specializes to $1$. This supplies the local proof in the correct chart; it does not identify a positive-$s$ fixed vector with the negative-chart canonical family. [F4, step 1.2, step 2.2, algebra]
81: 4.1 **The independent tensor/wedge bridge and opposite flag.** Apply [[lem-finite-type-a-tensor-hecke-bars-and-complementary-coefficient-charts]] before making any categorical identification. Its tensor generators act at $v=s^{-1}$, its positive chart transports to the sign module by $G_i=-H_i$ at $v=s$, and its complementary representative is $w_Kd(A)w_0$. The proved inverse tensor coefficient is $s^{\ell(w_C)-\ell(w_A)}P_{d(C)w_0,d(A)w_0}(s^{-2})$, so its value at $1$ is exactly the ordinary singular composition number in [F3]. The primitive ordinary-bar exterior embedding has coefficients $(-s)^{\ell(B,C)}$ on column permutations $C$ and sends the wedge $L_A$ to the ambient $L_{a(A)}$. Its signed inverse formula at $1$ therefore agrees term for term with finite Levi Weyl cancellation of the genuine generalized-standard character: $d_{A,B}(1)=[\Delta(B):L(A)]$. This uses character injectivity, and it keeps the equal-entry stabilizer separate from the column Levi. The helper proves the same transport for reversed factors; hence $\widetilde d_{B,A}(1)=[\bar\Delta(A):\bar L(B)]$. At $s=1$ the ordinary tensor bar matrix is the identity (each adjacent exchange has correction $s^{-1}-s=0$), so the reverse form of [F4] has monomial Gram matrix $I$. The identity $C(\widetilde L_A,T_B)=\delta_{AB}$ now says that the monomial coefficient matrix of $T$ is the transpose inverse of the coefficient matrix of $\widetilde L$, namely $\widetilde d^{\mathsf T}$. Thus the coefficient of $M(A)$ in $T_B$ at $1$ is $\widetilde d_{B,A}(1)=[\bar\Delta(A):\bar L(B)]=(X(B):\Delta(A))$ by step 3.1. All sign, longest-element and reverse-factor transports have been proved independently of this linkage conclusion. [F6, F2, F3, F4, step 3.1, algebra]
83: 4.2 **Isolation of adjacent columns in a larger tensor.** If two tableaux have the same entries outside two adjacent columns, every tableau in the Bruhat interval between them has the same outer prefix weights, since dominance is antisymmetric on fixed total weight; parenthesizing the pair as one tensor factor, a positive transfer across an outer boundary strictly changes a prefix weight in the positive cone and cannot be cancelled by further positive transfers. Hence the bar submatrix and, by triangular correction, the canonical coefficient between the two labelled monomials equal the two-column values of step 3.2. [F1, F4, step 3.2]
85: 5.1 **Genuine edges at $1$.** Specializing step 4.2 at $s=1$, a greedy-pair swap gives $(X(B):M(A))=1$ for the two labels connected by the swap: the corresponding standard factor occurs with multiplicity $1$ in the indecomposable tilting object $X(B)$. Since an indecomposable finite-length object cannot have composition factors in two different linkage blocks, and $M(A)$ has simple head $L(A)$ while the actual $\Delta(B)$ section of $X(B)$ supplies $L(B)$ as a composition factor, the labels $A$ and $B$ lie in the same actual linkage block. Opposite BGG gives $[\bar M(A):\bar L(B)]=1$, and $\bar M(A)$ has head $\bar L(A)$, so the same edge connects the opposite labels. [F1, F2, step 3.1, step 4.2]
