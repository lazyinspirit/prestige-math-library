137: 2.3 **Divided-power integral forms.** The lower library global vectors $G_{\mathrm{low}}(A)=T_{A^\uparrow}=s^{-a(\alpha)}P_A$ and the form-dual upper vectors $G_{\mathrm{up}}(A)=s^{a(\alpha)}D_A$ with $a(\alpha)=(2\Lambda-\alpha,\alpha)/2$ span integral forms on which the divided powers of the Chevalley generators act integrally, by [[thm-the-highest-weight-fock-submodule-has-the-qg-global-bases]]; at parameter one the standard lattice is therefore generated over $\mathbb Z$ by its ground vector $P_{A_\Lambda}$ under the divided-power operators $f_i^{(n)}$, while the costandard lattice is its full weightwise $C_0$-dual: at $s=1$, $C_0(P_A,D_B)=\delta_{AB}$ identifies it with $\bigoplus_\alpha\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(V(\Lambda)_{\mathbb Z,\alpha},\mathbb Z)$. 143: 4.1 **Crystal reachability and composition factors.** The source upper ground component is exactly $\mathrm{Std}^\Lambda$ by [[thm-standard-tableau-monomials-form-a-basis-of-the-highest-weight-fock-submodule]]. The normalized highest form is nondegenerate on each finite weight space, so over $\mathbb Q$ it identifies the full costandard lattice's scalar extension with the irreducible highest module. Its highest vector therefore generates that module under lowering divided powers. Consequently every basis vector $D_A$ is a rational linear combination of lowering words applied to the ground vector. By step 3.1 each ordinary lowering word is the class of the corresponding exact residue-word module, with nonnegative simple-class coefficients by [F1]. If $D(A)$ occurred in none of these modules, every such word and every rational linear combination would have zero $D_A$ coefficient, contrary to the coefficient one in $D_A$. Thus some residue word contains $D(A)$ as a composition factor. This proves (iv) without requiring integral costandard generation or transport of head and socle through the quotient. 145: 4.2 **The maps for arbitrary consecutive $I$.** Fix a finite set of contents and residue words. Choose $J=[k,r]$ so that $[k,r+1]$ contains every charge and $J$ contains every intermediate root support; decrease $k$ further to reach the needed stable range. The Hecke-side map is literally the identity on $H_d(m)e_\alpha$: its spectral idempotent is the same sum over words of content $\alpha$, independently of whether the ambient supported interval is $I$ or $J$. It therefore fixes the intrinsic residue corner $u_{\alpha,i}$ and the bimodules defining $e_i,f_i$. On tableaux the lower transition adjoins the prescribed ground entries to every column, and the upper transition regards the same entries in a larger alphabet with no new top entries. Removing that common tail is the inverse on the selected weight. Column ground differences, hence intrinsic Specht partitions, are unchanged. The actual finite-cut compatibility of [[thm-standard-tableau-monomials-form-a-basis-of-the-highest-weight-fock-submodule]] and [[thm-the-highest-weight-fock-submodule-has-the-qg-global-bases]] gives $D_{\jmath A}=\jmath D_A$, $S_{\jmath B}=\jmath S_B$, $P_{\jmath A}=\jmath P_A$ and commuting normalized highest projections. Thus both upper and lower lattices are identified by their full bases, not just their rational spans. On overlapping cuts these maps compose by adjoining/removing the same ground entries and by the intrinsic Hecke identity; the normalized projections agree by highest-vector uniqueness. Every content, vector and finite residue word fits such a cut. Applying the bounded-below identifications and the rational-cyclicity argument of steps 1.1–4.1 there, and taking the compatible weightwise direct sum, proves all clauses for arbitrary admitted $I$. No full degree algebra is identified with a proper supported cut. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.3, step 3.1, given, construct, algebra]

149: 6.1 **The rank-one index-two witness.** Take $I=\{0\}$ and $\Lambda=2\Lambda_0$. The supported degree-one block is $B_0=\mathbb C[x]/x^2$ and the supported degree-two block is $M_2(\mathbb C)$, with its simple represented by $x_1=N=E_{12}$, $s_1=-(E_{12}+E_{21})$ and $x_2=-N$. For completeness the whole degree-two presentation gives an isomorphism $H_2(0,0)\cong M_2(\mathbb C)\oplus B_+\oplus B_-$ with $B_\pm=\mathbb C[\epsilon]/\epsilon^2$: send $(x_1,x_2,s_1)$ to $(N,-N,-J)$ in the matrix factor and to $(\epsilon,\pm1+\epsilon,\pm1)$ in the other factors. The eight PBW spanning words are $1,x_1,x_2,x_1x_2,s_1,x_1s_1,x_2s_1,x_1x_2s_1$. The central sum $z=x_1+x_2$ has spectra $0,1,-1$ on the three factors; its Hermite polynomials $(1-z^2)^2$ and $\{1-(1-z^2)^2\pm(3z-z^3)/2\}/2$ project onto the three factors. Each factor is generated by the images, making the map onto an eight-dimensional product surjective and hence an isomorphism by the spanning bound. The all-zero-residue cut is exactly the matrix factor. In degree three an all-zero-residue module would have $x_2=-x_1$, $x_1^2=0$ from the matrix factor; $s_2$ commutes with $x_1$ and therefore with $x_2$, so $x_3=s_2x_2s_2+s_2=x_2+s_2$ has eigenvalues $\pm1$, contradiction. The same argument excludes all higher degrees. The first dot is not zero on the matrix simple. Induction from degree zero gives the regular $B_0$-module, with two simple factors. The matrix algebra is right-free of rank two over $B_0$ under $x\mapsto N$, so induction of its one-dimensional simple has dimension two and is the matrix simple; restricting that matrix simple gives the regular $B_0$-module. Restricting the degree-one simple to degree zero has dimension one. Thus the residue Ind/Res computations give $fD_{00}=2D_{01}$, $fD_{01}=D_{11}$ and $eD_{01}=D_{00}$, $eD_{11}=2D_{01}$, with the end actions zero. Their divided squares give $f^{(2)}D_{00}=D_{11}$ and $e^{(2)}D_{11}=D_{00}$. Therefore $\mathbb ZD_{00}\oplus2\mathbb ZD_{01}\oplus\mathbb ZD_{11}$ is stable under $e,f$ and all their divided powers, contains the ground vector and excludes $D_{01}$. This is a proper integral costandard submodule, whereas over $\mathbb Q$ division by two recovers $D_{01}$. Degree one is not called a corner sitting inside degree two. [step 2.3, step 5.1, algebra]
