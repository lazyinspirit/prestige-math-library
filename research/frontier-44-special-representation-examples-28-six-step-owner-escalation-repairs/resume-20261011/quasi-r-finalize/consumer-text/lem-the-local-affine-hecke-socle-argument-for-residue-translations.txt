89: 1.3 **The height filtration.** Fix one residue, write $E,F$ for its biadjoint functors and $e,f$ for their class operators, and put $h_+(b)=\max\{j:e^jb\ne0\}$, $h_-(b)=\max\{j:f^jb\ne0\}$ and $d(b)=h_+(b)+h_-(b)+1$ for a simple class $b$. These are finite by [F1]; at weight $\lambda$, ordinary $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ string theory gives $d(b)=2h_+(b)+\lambda+1=2h_-(b)-\lambda+1$. Positivity implies that every simple constituent $c$ of $eb$ has $h_+(c)\le h_+(b)-1$, so $d(c)\le d(b)$; the $f$ argument is identical. Hence the Serre subcategory generated by simples with $d\le r$ is stable under $E,F$. If $T$ is a simple subobject of $E^jM$, adjunction gives a nonzero map $F^jT\to M$; its image contains a simple subobject $S$ of $M$, and the preceding bound gives $d(S)\le d(T)$. The dual argument applies to simple quotients. Consequently translates of an object of height at most $r$ whose simple head and socle constituents all have height $r$ have the same property. [F1, algebra]

97: 3.1 **An extremal simple.** Suppose $FS=0$ and put $n=h_+([S])$. Positivity and $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ string theory imply $[E^{(n)}S]=m[T]$ for a single simple $T$ and an integer $m>0$: the positive extremal classes give a bijection between the lowest and highest simple classes. Indeed $f^ne^n[S]=(n!)^2[S]$; each positive summand of $e^n[S]$ must return to a multiple of $[S]$, and $e^nf^n$ is the same nonzero scalar on the highest weight space, forcing all these summands to be the same simple class. Since $[F^{(n)}E^{(n)}S]=[S]$, integrality and positivity force $m=1$. Thus $E^{(n)}S$ is simple. The full Hecke multiplicity module in $E^nS\cong n!E^{(n)}S$ is the unique simple module $K_n$ of the central fibre, of dimension $n!$, by step 2.1. [F1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 1.3, algebra]
99: 3.2 **Head, socle and the maximal-height constituent.** More generally suppose $E^{(n)}S$ is simple, where $n=h_+([S])$, and let $0\le j\le n$. The complement-idempotent restriction of $K_n$ in step 2.2 has simple head and socle over the remaining $(n-j)$-step algebra. Applying $E^{n-j}$ to $\operatorname{soc}E^{(j)}S$ embeds its image in that restriction tensored with the extremal simple. Step 1.3 ensures that each simple socle summand has height $d([S])$ and survives this translate. Two such summands, or two copies of one, would give a direct sum of two nonzero submodules inside a module with simple socle. Thus $E^{(j)}S$ has simple socle $T_j$; the opposite-category argument gives a simple head. Its socle multiplicity module in $E^jS\cong j!E^{(j)}S$ is $K_j$. Applying $\operatorname{Hom}(-,E^{(j)}S)$ along a composition series bounds the endomorphism dimension by the composition multiplicity of $T_j$, since Hom from a simple to this object is one-dimensional for $T_j$ and zero otherwise. This multiplicity is at most $\binom nj$, since $E^{(n-j)}E^{(j)}S\cong\binom nj E^{(n)}S$. On the other hand the Hecke action has dimension at least $(j!)^2\binom nj$: its kernel lies in the intersection of the first-$j$ algebra with the maximal central ideal of the full completed matrix algebra. Step 2.2 identifies the corresponding quotient as $\mathrm{Mat}_{j!}(Z_{j,n})$, of precisely that dimension. The two bounds force equality and show that $T_j$ occurs $\binom nj$ times and is the only constituent surviving $E^{n-j}$. The head has the same height and survives, so it is also $T_j$. All other constituents have strictly smaller height. [F1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 1.3, algebra]
