85: 1.1 **Definition of the functors.** Tensoring with $V$ or $V^{*}$ preserves $\mathcal O$ and parabolic local finiteness: tensor a finite parabolic span of a vector with the finite tensor factor. Exact central-character projection therefore defines the stated functors. Replacing $i$ by $i+1$ changes the Fock weight by $-\alpha_i$, so a source weight $\Lambda-\alpha$ becomes $\Lambda-(\alpha+\alpha_i)$; the reverse move gives $\alpha-\alpha_i$. A target with no admitted tableau is the zero category and its projection is zero. [F1, F4, construct, algebra]
87: 2.1 **Exactness and biadjunction.** On the direct sum of blocks, exactness is inherited from the exactness of tensoring with a finite-dimensional module and of the block projections [F1]. Biadjunction holds because the general clause of the published translation supplier applies to the finite modules $V$ and $V^{*}$ without any regular-wall crossing: the unit and counit are those of the tensor/block-projection adjunction, and they restrict to the stated blocks by construction of the projections. [F1, F5, step 1.1]
89: 2.2 **The Casimir description.** The well-definedness supplier [F6] computes the tensor-Casimir scalars on generalized-standard flag sections: a valid $\lambda\mapsto\lambda+\varepsilon_j$ section has scalar $a_j$, and a valid $\lambda\mapsto\lambda-\varepsilon_j$ section has scalar $-a_j-n+1$. In particular the reverse move from entry $i+1$ has scalar $-n-i$. Its extension from these flag sections to arbitrary finite-length objects proves that the generalized $i$-eigencomponent is $f_i$ and the generalized $-n-i$-eigencomponent is $e_i$, with the block shifts of step 1.1. [F4, F6, step 1.1]
93: 3.2 **Commutation with duality.** Restricted transpose duality fixes $V$ and $V^{*}$ with their respective forms and commutes with tensoring with a fixed finite-dimensional module and with the character projections; therefore $D f_i\cong f_i D$ and $D e_i\cong e_i D$ on the direct sum, which is the stated compatibility. [F3, step 2.1]
95: 4.1 **Conclusion.** The residue translation functors $f_i,e_i$ on the direct sum of finite classical blocks are exact, biadjoint, preserve projectives and injectives, commute with restricted duality, and agree with the stated Casimir eigencomponents; they are special residue translations with the Fock root label shifts, not ordinary regular-wall translations and not classical highest-weight shifts.
