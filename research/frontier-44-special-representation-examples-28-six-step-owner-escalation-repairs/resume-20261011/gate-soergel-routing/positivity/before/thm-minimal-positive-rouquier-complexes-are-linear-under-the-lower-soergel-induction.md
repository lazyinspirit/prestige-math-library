## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A symmetric group $S_n$ with simple reflections $s_i$, the real type-$A$ Soergel category of [[def-real-type-a-soergel-category-and-scalar-extension]] with its normalized indecomposables $B_w^R$ and balanced tensor products; the positive Rouquier generator complexes $F_s=[B_s^R\to S(1)]$ with inverses $F_s^{-1}=[S(-1)\to B_s^R]$ ([[def-positive-and-negative-rouquier-generator-complexes]]) and the Rouquier complex $F_{\underline x}$ of a word ([[def-rouquier-complex-of-a-braid-word]]); an element $x\in S_n$ with a fixed reduced expression $\underline x=s_1\cdots s_m$ of length $m=\ell(x)$, the minimal subcomplex $F_x\subseteq^{\oplus}F_{s_1}\cdots F_{s_m}$ ([[def-minimal-complexes-and-perverse-truncations-in-the-type-a-soergel-category]]), and the hypothesis $\mathrm S(y)$ for every $y\le x$.

[F1] $F_s$ is invertible in $K^b$ with two-sided homotopy inverse $F_s^{-1}$, so tensoring with $F_s$ is an equivalence of $K^b$; the normalized indecomposable $B_w^R$ is indecomposable and $\operatorname{End}^0(B_w^R)$ is a finite-dimensional local $\mathbb R$-algebra; for a reduced word the Rouquier complex is well defined up to canonical homotopy equivalence and all its terms are finite free on both sides; if $x=ys$ with $\ell(x)=\ell(y)+1$ then $F_yF_s$ is a Rouquier complex for $x$ ([[lem-opposite-rouquier-generator-complexes-are-homotopy-inverse]], [[thm-rouquier-complex-is-well-defined-up-to-canonical-homotopy-equivalence]], [[lem-rational-and-real-type-a-soergel-characters-and-indecomposable-labels-agree]], [[def-rouquier-complex-of-a-braid-word]]).

[F2] If $xs>x$ and $\mathrm S(y)$ holds for all $y<xs$, then $B_xB_s=B_{xs}\oplus\bigoplus_{y<xs}B_y^{a_y}$ with $a_y\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ and no internal shifts, the top summand $B_{xs}$ occurring with multiplicity one; if $xs<x$ and $\mathrm S(x)$ holds then $B_xB_s=B_x(1)\oplus B_x(-1)$; and $\operatorname{End}^0(B_w^R)=\mathbb R$ for every $w$ with $\mathrm S(w)$ ([[lem-rational-and-real-type-a-soergel-characters-and-indecomposable-labels-agree]]).

[F3] The locally proved support character is additive and has $\operatorname{ch}(M(d))=v^d\operatorname{ch}(M)$; its right generator recursion is $\operatorname{ch}(MB_s)=\operatorname{ch}(M)\underline H_s$ ([[lem-type-a-character-recursion-under-simple-soergel-tensoring]]). Top-support classification gives the unique unshifted $B_x$ summand of a reduced word for $x$. A positive word-complex term in cohomological degree $i>0$ is a sum of words obtained by omitting $i$ letters, hence has no $x$ graph ([[lem-indecomposable-type-a-soergel-bimodules-are-classified-by-top-support]], [[def-rouquier-complex-of-a-braid-word]]).

[F4] The standard basis $\{H_w\}$ and the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis $\{\underline H_w\}$ are $\mathbb Z[v^{\pm1}]$-bases of the Hecke algebra of [[def-normalized-type-a-hecke-algebra-and-its-bar-involution]]; right multiplication by a generator is $H_wH_s=H_{ws}$ when $\ell(ws)=\ell(w)+1$ and $H_wH_s=H_{ws}+(v^{-1}-v)H_w$ when $\ell(ws)<\ell(w)$; the expansion $\underline H_w=\sum_{y\le w}h_{y,w}H_y$ has $h_{w,w}=1$, $h_{y,w}=0$ unless $y\le w$, and $h_{y,w}\in v^{\ell(w)-\ell(y)}\mathbb Z[v^{-2}]$, so that every monomial $v^d$ occurring in $h_{y,w}$ satisfies $d\equiv\ell(w)-\ell(y)\pmod2$ and $h_{y,w}\in v\mathbb Z[v]$ for $y<w$ ([[thm-existence-and-uniqueness-of-the-kazhdan-lusztig-basis]], [[def-kazhdan-lusztig-polynomials-in-the-classical-q-normalization]]).

[F5] Perverse bimodules are those whose character is a nonnegative integer combination of the $\underline H_w$; p-split bimodules are the finite direct sums of internal shifts of perverse bimodules; summands of perverse (resp. p-split) bimodules are perverse (resp. p-split); a minimal complex lies in $pK^b_{\ge0}$ (resp. $pK^b_{\le0}$) if and only if every indecomposable summand $B_w(k)$ in cohomological degree $i$ satisfies $k\le i$ (resp. $k\ge i$); both classes are closed under extensions in distinguished triangles; and the positive Rouquier complexes are $\Delta$-split, with $\Gamma_{\ge y}F_x/\Gamma_{>y}F_x\cong\Delta_x$ for $y=x$ and $0$ for $y\ne x$ in the homotopy category of graded bimodules ([[def-minimal-complexes-and-perverse-truncations-in-the-type-a-soergel-category]], [[lem-radical-minimal-complexes-and-perverse-filtrations-in-the-soergel-category]], [[thm-positive-rouquier-complexes-are-delta-split]]).

[F6] Under $\mathrm S(z)$ and $\mathrm S(u)$ the graded rank of $\operatorname{Hom}(B_z^R,B_u^R)$ is the trace-form value $(\underline H_z,\underline H_u)$, which lies in $v\mathbb Z[v]$ for $z\ne u$ and in $1+v\mathbb Z[v]$ for $z=u$; hence $\operatorname{Hom}^0(B_z^R,B_u^R)=0$ for $z\ne u$, $\operatorname{Hom}^0(B_z^R,B_z^R)=\mathbb R$, and every nonzero degree-zero endomorphism of $B_z^R$ is an isomorphism ([[thm-the-type-a-soergel-hom-formula]], [[lem-hecke-trace-bilinear-form-and-its-values-on-the-kazhdan-lusztig-basis]]).

## Statement

Assume $\mathrm S(y)$ for all $y\le x$, and let $F_x$ be a minimal Rouquier complex of a reduced expression of $x$. Then:

(1) $^0F_x\cong B_x^R$;

(2) for every $i\ge1$, $^iF_x\cong\bigoplus_{z<x}B_z^R(i)^{m_{z,i}}$ with $m_{z,i}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$, so that $F_x$ lies in $pK^b_{\le0}\cap pK^b_{\ge0}$ and is linear: every indecomposable summand of $^iF_x$ carries the internal shift exactly $i$;

(3) the multiplicities satisfy the parity vanishing $m_{z,i}=0$ unless $i\equiv\ell(x)-\ell(z)\pmod2$, and consequently, writing the standard basis element as $H_x=\sum_{z\le x}g_{z,x}\underline H_z$ (the inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig coefficients), one has $g_{x,x}=1$, $g_{z,x}=\sum_i(-1)^im_{z,i}v^i$, and $(-1)^{\ell(x)-\ell(z)}g_{z,x}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}[v^{\pm1}]$.

The statement is conditional exactly on $\mathrm S(y)$ for $y\le x$; it uses neither $\mathrm S(x')$ for $x'>x$ nor the final character theorem, and it is the induction step of the simultaneous induction.

## Proof
**Proof technique:** induction on the length; the perverse lower bound is propagated through the stupid filtration, the upper bound and the labels through the shift-propagation lemma, and the coefficients through inversion of the unitriangular Kazhdan-Lusztig transition matrix.

1.1 **Base of the induction.** [base] For $x=e$ the Rouquier complex is the unit $S$ concentrated in cohomological degree $0$, so $^0F_e=S=B_e$, there is no term of index $i\ge1$, both perverse bounds hold trivially, and the only nonzero inverse coefficient is $g_{e,e}=1$; for $x=s_i$ the complex $F_{s_i}=[B_{s_i}^R\to S(1)]$ has $^0F_{s_i}=B_{s_i}$ and $^1F_{s_i}=S(1)=B_e^R(1)$ and no other term, so $m_{e,1}=1$ with $\ell(s_i)-\ell(e)=1\equiv1\pmod2$, $B_{s_i}$ is perverse by $\mathrm S(s_i)$ and $S(1)$ is the shift of the perverse unit, so $F_{s_i}\in pK^b_{\ge0}\cap pK^b_{\le0}$ and $g_{s_i,s_i}=1$, $g_{e,s_i}=-v$. [given, F1, F3, F5]

2.1 **Induction hypothesis and the last letter.** [ih] Fix $x\ne e$, write $\underline x=s_1\cdots s_m$ with $m\ge1$ and put $y=s_1\cdots s_{m-1}$, $s=s_m$, so that $x=ys$ with $\ell(x)=\ell(y)+1$; the hypothesis $\mathrm S(\le x)$ contains $\mathrm S(\le y)$, and we assume the three assertions of the statement for $y$: $^0F_y=B_y$, $^iF_y=\bigoplus_{z<y}B_z(i)^{m^y_{z,i}}$ for $i\ge1$, and $F_y\in pK^b_{\ge0}\cap pK^b_{\le0}$. By [F1], $F_x\cong F_yF_s$ in $K^b$. [given, step 1.1, F1]

3.1 **Descent contraction.** Let $z$ and a simple reflection $s$ satisfy $zs<z$ and $\mathrm S(z)$; then $B_zF_s\cong B_z(-1)$ in $K^b$. Indeed, by [F2] the two-term complex $B_zF_s$ has terms $B_zB_s=B_z(1)\oplus B_z(-1)$ in degree $0$ and $B_z(1)$ in degree $1$, with differential $d=(\delta,\eta)$ where $\delta\in\operatorname{End}^0(B_z(1))=\operatorname{End}^0(B_z)=\mathbb R$ by [F2] and [F6]. If $\delta=0$ then $B_zF_s$ is the direct sum of the two nonzero complexes $[B_z(1)\to0]$ and $[B_z(-1)\xrightarrow{\ \eta\ }B_z(1)]$, contradicting the indecomposability of $B_zF_s$, which is the image of the indecomposable $B_z$ under the equivalence $-\otimes F_s$; hence $\delta\ne0$ is an isomorphism, homological Gaussian elimination cancels the contractible two-term complex $B_z(1)\cong B_z(1)$, and the remainder is $B_z(-1)$ concentrated in degree $0$. [step 2.1, F1, F2, F6]

3.2 **Top support and the Euler character.** Every positive-degree term of the word complex omits at least one letter, so no such term has the $x$ graph by [F3]. The degree-zero term is its reduced word, whose top summand is exactly $B_x$ unshifted and once. Gaussian minimalization cannot remove it, since no target term has its label. Thus in the minimal complex $m^0_{x,0}=1$ and every other $m^i_{x,k}$ is zero, by actual support rather than alternating cancellation. Character additivity makes its Euler character unchanged by removing a contractible pair. The generator recursion and shift rule in [F3] give $\operatorname{ch}(F_zF_s)=\operatorname{ch}(F_z)(\underline H_s-v)$ degree by degree. Iterating along the reduced word therefore gives Euler character $H_x$. Under the stated $S(\le x)$, expanding each actual summand character in the KL basis yields $\sum_{i,k}(-1)^im^i_{w,k}v^k=g_{w,x}$. This equality is used only for the Euler coefficients, after the support proof of the top multiplicity. [step 2.1, F1, F3, F4]

4.1 **Ascent perverse bound.** Let $z\le y$ and a simple reflection $s$ satisfy $zs>z$; then $B_zF_s\in pK^b_{\ge0}$. Indeed, $\mathrm S(u)$ holds for every $u<zs$, because $u<zs\le x$ implies $u<x$; so [F2] gives the unshifted decomposition $B_zB_s=B_{zs}\oplus\bigoplus_{u<zs}B_u^{a_u}$, whose summands are perverse by $\mathrm S(zs)$ and $\mathrm S(u)$, while the second term $B_z(1)$ of $B_zF_s=[B_zB_s\to B_z(1)]$ is the internal shift of the perverse $B_z$ sitting in cohomological degree $1$; thus the chosen representative has p-split terms and satisfies $\tau_{<0}(B_zB_s)=0$ and $\tau_{<-1}(B_z(1))=0$, which is membership in $pK^b_{\ge0}$ by [F5]. [step 3.1, F2, F5]

4.2 **Shift propagation.** Assume $\mathrm S(\le x)$ and let $F_x$ be minimal. If $^iF_x$ contains a summand $B_z(j)$ with $z<x$, then $^{i-1}F_x$ contains a summand $B_{z'}(j')$ with $z'>z$ and $j'<j$. First, components of the differential into summands of shift $k<j$ vanish: a degree-zero map $B_z(j)\to B_u(k)$ is an element of $\operatorname{Hom}^{k-j}(B_z,B_u)$, which is zero for $k<j$ since the graded Hom space has no negative-degree part by [F6]; components with $k=j$ are zero for $u\ne z$ by [F6] and are scalars for $u=z$, and a nonzero scalar component would make $B_z(j)$ a contractible summand of the minimal complex, so all such components vanish as well; hence $d(B_z(j))$ lies in the sum of the summands of $^{i+1}F_x$ with shift $k>j$. Second, if a summand $B_u(k)$ of $^{i-1}F_x$ maps nontrivially to $B_z(j)$, then $k<j$: a degree-zero map $B_u(k)\to B_z(j)$ lies in $\operatorname{Hom}^{j-k}(B_u,B_z)$, which vanishes for $j<k$, and for $j=k$ it vanishes for $u\ne z$ and is a scalar for $u=z$, the nonzero case again being excluded by minimality. Third, apply the support cutoff $\Gamma_{\ge z/>z}$ and reduce its free graph modules modulo $R_+$. Since $z<x$, its complex is contractible by [F5], hence this reduced finite-dimensional complex is exact. The chosen top layer $\Delta_z(j)$ of $B_z(j)$ is a nonzero homogeneous basis vector. Every outgoing target has shift $k>j$, and its $z$ layer has shift $k+d$ with $d\ge0$ by KL triangularity. A map from the chosen generator into it consequently has positive-degree coefficient, so vanishes after reduction. The chosen vector is a cycle and must therefore be an incoming boundary. Some incoming component has a nonzero constant coefficient onto it, so some $B_{z'}(j')$ in degree $i-1$ has a $z$ layer with $j'+d=j$. The underlying differential component is nonzero, and the preceding Hom estimate gives $j'<j$. Since $d=0$ for label $z$ itself, this requires $z'>z$. This proves shift propagation without using an Euler identity to recover multiplicities. [step 3.2, F2, F4, F5, F6]

5.1 **The perverse lower bound for $F_x$.** Use the stupid filtration $w_{\ge k}F_y$ of the minimal complex $F_y$, with distinguished triangles $w_{\ge k+1}F_y\to w_{\ge k}F_y\to{}^kF_y[-k]\to$ and $w_{\ge\ell(y)+1}F_y=0$; tensoring with the invertible $F_s$ preserves distinguished triangles. We prove $(w_{\ge k}F_y)F_s\in pK^b_{\ge0}$ by descending induction on $k$: for $k=\ell(y)+1$ the object is $0$; assume it for $k+1$. By [ih] and step 2.1, $^kF_y$ is a direct sum of summands $B_z(k)$ with $z\le y$ (with $z=y$ only for $k=0$), so $(^kF_y)F_s[-k]$ is a direct sum of copies of $(B_zF_s)(k)[-k]$. If $zs>z$ then $B_zF_s\in pK^b_{\ge0}$ by step 4.1, and its internal shift $(k)$ and homological shift $[-k]$ place every summand $B_u(\ell)$ occurring in cohomological degree $i$ into degree $i+k$ with internal shift $\ell+k\le i+k$, so $(B_zF_s)(k)[-k]\in pK^b_{\ge0}$; if $zs<z$  then step 3.1 gives $B_zF_s\cong B_z(-1)$, so $(B_zF_s)(k)[-k]\cong B_z(k-1)[-k]$, a perverse object with internal shift $k-1$ in cohomological degree $k$, again in $pK^b_{\ge0}$. Extension closure of $pK^b_{\ge0}$ in the tensored triangles gives $(w_{\ge k}F_y)F_s\in pK^b_{\ge0}$, and at $k=0$ we obtain $F_yF_s\in pK^b_{\ge0}$; by step 2.1 this is $F_x\in pK^b_{\ge0}$. [step 4.1, step 2.1, F1, F5, ih]

6.1 **The top term and the perverse upper bound.** If $^0F_x$ contained a summand $B_z(j)$ with $z<x$, then step 4.2 with $i=0$ would produce a summand of $^{-1}F_x=0$, which is impossible; hence every summand of $^0F_x$ has label $x$, and by step 3.2 ($m^0_{x,0}=1$ and $m^0_{x,k}=0$ for $k\ne0$) we get $^0F_x=B_x$, which proves (1). Now suppose $i\ge1$ and $^iF_x$ has a summand $B_z(k)$ with $k<i$; if $z=x$ this contradicts $m^i_{x,k}=0$ from step 3.2, so $z<x$, and step 4.2 produces a summand $B_{z'}(k')$ of $^{i-1}F_x$ with $k'<k<i$. This summand is again of the forbidden type: if $z'=x$ then $m^{i-1}_{x,k'}=0$ whenever $i-1\ge1$, while for $i-1=0$ it contradicts $^0F_x=B_x$ because $k'<0$; otherwise $z'<x$ and step 4.2 applies again. Iterating this descent in the homological degree we reach a summand of $^0F_x$ with negative internal shift, contradicting $^0F_x=B_x$. Hence every summand of $^iF_x$ has internal shift $\ge i$ for $i\ge1$, which together with $F_x\in pK^b_{\ge0}$ from step 5.1 gives $F_x\in pK^b_{\le0}$; since for $i\ge1$ the label $x$ does not occur by step 3.2, we obtain $^iF_x=\bigoplus_{z<x}B_z(i)^{m_{z,i}}$ with $m_{z,i}:=m^i_{z,i}$, which is (2). [step 4.2, step 5.1, F5]

7.1 **Inversion of the Kazhdan-Lusztig transition matrix.** Let the two bases be related by $\underline H_w=\sum_{y\le w}h_{y,w}H_y$ and $H_w=\sum_{y\le w}g_{y,w}\underline H_y$ as in [F4]; substituting the first expansion into the second gives $\sum_{y\le z\le w}h_{y,z}g_{z,w}=\delta_{y,w}$. Hence $g_{w,w}=1$ and, for $y<w$, $g_{y,w}=-\sum_{y<z\le w}h_{y,z}g_{z,w}$. We prove $g_{y,w}\in v^{\ell(w)-\ell(y)}\mathbb Z[v^{-2}]$ by descending induction on $\ell(w)-\ell(y)$: for $y=w$ this is $1\in v^0\mathbb Z[v^{-2}]$, and for $y<w$ each $h_{y,z}$ lies in $v^{\ell(z)-\ell(y)}\mathbb Z[v^{-2}]$ by [F4] and each $g_{z,w}$ with $y<z\le w$ lies in $v^{\ell(w)-\ell(z)}\mathbb Z[v^{-2}]$ by the hypothesis of the induction, so each product lies in $v^{\ell(w)-\ell(y)}\mathbb Z[v^{-2}]$ and the finite sum does too. [step 6.1, F4, algebra]

8.1 **Parity and positivity of the inverse coefficients.** By step 3.2 and step 6.1 the multiplicities appearing in $g_{z,x}=\sum_i(-1)^im_{z,i}v^i$ are exactly the multiplicities of the linear complex $F_x$, and by step 7.1 we have $g_{z,x}\in v^{\ell(x)-\ell(z)}\mathbb Z[v^{-2}]$; therefore the coefficient $(-1)^im_{z,i}$ of $v^i$ can be nonzero only when $i\equiv\ell(x)-\ell(z)\pmod2$, which is the parity clause of (3). For such $i$ the sign $(-1)^{\ell(x)-\ell(z)+i}$ equals $+1$, so $(-1)^{\ell(x)-\ell(z)}g_{z,x}=\sum_i(-1)^{\ell(x)-\ell(z)+i}m_{z,i}v^i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}[v^{\pm1}]$, and the coefficients are the nonnegative multiplicities $m_{z,i}$. This proves (3) and, with (1) and (2), completes the induction. [step 7.1, step 3.2, discharge-induction: step 2.1] ∎

## Remarks

- The theorem is the linearity statement inside the simultaneous induction of the page: it is proved under $\mathrm S(\le x)$ only and is never used to justify an earlier item. Its role downstream is to supply the linear minimal complex whose first differentials drive the hard-Lefschetz theorems.
- Step 4.2 is the local rendering of Elias-Williamson Lemma 6.11; the two shift estimates and the cancellation in the $\Delta$-split cutoff complex are its complete argument, and the positivity clause of (3) is the inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig positivity of Remark 6.10, obtained here without assuming positivity of the Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials.
