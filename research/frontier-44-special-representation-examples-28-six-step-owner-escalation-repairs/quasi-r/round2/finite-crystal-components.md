# A finite crystal component does not yet supply a finite based envelope

This is a proved intermediate result in final logical repair round 2. It uses the round-1 finite-type local-U-orbit proof and the actual unique admissible string interface. It does not assert the unresolved tensor globalization claim.

Let $(M,B)$ satisfy the four based axioms and be integrable in finite Cartan type. Fix a homogeneous raw basis vector $b\in B$. Round-1 supplier Proof1.3 proves that $W:=Ub$ is finite dimensional, without any weight-multiplicity or support hypothesis on $M$. It is a graded submodule: the algebra is root graded and $b$ is homogeneous. It is stable under each Kashiwara operator. Indeed restriction to $W$ is an integrable graded module; its finite admissible string decomposition exists by `thm-unique-kashiwara-i-string-decomposition`, and uniqueness in $M$ identifies its coefficients with those in $W$. The shifts along those same strings therefore preserve $W$.

Choose a finite field basis of $W$. Each basis vector has finite support in the original field basis $B$; their union is a finite subset $S\subset B$ and $W\subset\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q(q)}S$. Let $w$ be any finite raising/lowering word whose value on the crystal class of $b$ is nonzero. The corresponding Kashiwara word on the raw lift $b$ belongs to $W\cap L_0$, since the lattice is stable. Its reduction is exactly the one nonzero vertex specified by that crystal word. Its field coordinates are supported in $S$, and its lattice coordinates belong to $A_0$, so its residue vertex belongs to $S$. Raising/lowering paths generate the connected component of the initial vertex. Hence every vertex in that component belongs to $S$, and the component is finite.

This result does not say that $W$ is a based subobject or that its raw coordinate projections lie in $W$. It bounds one crystal component; $U_A$ matrix coefficients or bar correction can connect distinct components. A finite raw-basis envelope requires a further compatibility argument. The source finite-dimensional filtration does not provide that argument automatically: its $B[\lambda]$ lies in $M[\ge\lambda]$, allowing upward actual-type contributions, and its induction begins at a globally maximal type.

In particular, substituting “each crystal component is finite” for “the raw correction-support graph has bounded depth” would leave the current gap unresolved.
