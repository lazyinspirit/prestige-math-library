This is a complete local algebraic construction for the coefficient part of the Brundan lowering argument, not a claim that the missing quantum Weyl/cellular representation is supplied. Source: authentic cached Brundan manuscript, printed19–27, read together with rendered pages19,25–26 to retain the complement bars lost by text extraction.

Fix integers i<j, put I={i+1,...,j−1}, and work with independent integer polynomial variables x_i,...,x_(j−1),y_(i+1),...,y_j. For D⊆I let p_D(t)=max({i}∪{d∈D:d<t}). For A,B⊆I define the rational expression

$$H_{i,j}(A,B)=\sum_{D\subseteq B\setminus A}(-1)^{|D|}\frac{\prod_{t\in A}(x_t-x_{p_D(t)})}{\prod_{t\in B}(x_t-x_{p_D(t)})}.$$

Common factors remove A∩B, giving H(A,B)=H(A\B,B\A). The following recursion proves integrality and is an exact prescription for evaluating at colliding coordinates. For disjoint A,B, let h=min(A∪B). If h∈A, multiply H(A\{h},B) by x_h−x_i. If h∈B, set P=H(A,B\{h}) and take (P−P|_(x_i=x_h))/(x_h−x_i). Start with H(empty,empty)=1. Partitioning D according as h∈D gives precisely these two formulas; in the second formula, H(A,B\{h}) is the polynomial with initial variable x_i, and its substitute x_i=x_h is the same interval expression with initial index h. The numerator is divisible integrally: on a monomial x_i^m its divided difference is −sum_(a=0)^(m−1) x_i^a x_h^(m−1−a). Induction on |A|+|B| therefore proves H∈Z[x], without dividing any specialized coordinate difference. All evaluations below mean evaluation of this polynomial, not its rational presentation.

Every H is translation invariant in the x variables and homogeneous of degree |A|−|B| (or zero). This follows directly in the rational expression and hence as a polynomial identity. In particular it is an integral polynomial in differences x_t−x_i. Its coefficient at B=empty is exactly product_(t∈A)(x_t−x_i).

For k∈A and h=max([i,k)\A), the same D-sum gives the full lowering recurrence

$$H_{i,j}(A,B)=(x_k-x_h)H_{i,j}(A\setminus\{k\},B)+\mathbf1_{h\ne i}H_{i,j}((A\setminus\{k\})\cup\{h\},B)+\mathbf1_{k\in B}H_{i,k}(A\cap(i,k),B\cap(i,k))H_{k,j}(A\cap(k,j),B\cap(k,j)).$$

Here (h,k)⊆A. Remove common A/B factors there first. Split x_k−x_(p_D(k)) into x_k−x_h and x_h−x_(p_D(k)). The first term is the first summand. In the second, D containing h has zero factor; for the remaining D, p_D(k)=p_D(h), giving the second summand, absent when h=i. If k∈B, the extra terms with k∈D have sign −1 and factor as independent sums over D∩(i,k) and D∩(k,j); subtraction of these extra terms gives the third, positive, product. This accounts for every term and proves the recurrence before specialization.

Define the norm polynomial

$$K_{i,j}(A)=\sum_{B\subseteq I}H_{i,j}(A,B)\prod_{t\in\{i\}\cup B}(y_{t+1}-x_t).$$

Writing C=I\A, expansion of each numerator factor y_(t+1)−x_(p_D(t+1)) as (y_(t+1)−x_t)+(x_t−x_(p_D(t+1))) gives the equivalent alternating expression

$$K_{i,j}(A)=\sum_{D\subseteq C}(-1)^{|D|}\frac{\prod_{t=i}^{j-1}(y_{t+1}-x_{p_D(t+1)})}{\prod_{t\in C}(x_t-x_{p_D(t)})}.$$

In this expansion the terms vanish unless the selected numerator indices are disjoint from D. On the remaining indices p_D(t+1)=p_D(t); collecting by the selected y−x factors gives H(complement B,complement A)=H(A,B), directly from their numerator/denominator sets, and proves the formula. The complement is essential: this does not assert H(A,B)=H(B,A), which is false already on the one-point interval.

Now let θ:C→B be any bijection onto B⊆I satisfying θ(c)≥c, and let J be the ideal generated by y_(θ(c)+1)−x_c, c∈C. The matched-product identity is

$$K_{i,j}(A)\equiv\prod_{t\in[i,j)\setminus B}(y_{t+1}-x_i)\pmod J.$$

Prove it by induction on j−i. If C is empty, K(A)=product_(t=i)^(j−1)(y_(t+1)−x_i), so it holds. Otherwise take h=min C, C'=C\{h}, B'=B\{θ(h)}. All C' and B' lie strictly after h, since θ(c)≥c. The alternating expression, split by h∈D, gives

$$K_{i,j}(A)=\prod_{t=i}^{h-1}(y_{t+1}-x_i)\frac{K_{h,j}(A\cap(h,j))|_{x_h=x_i}-K_{h,j}(A\cap(h,j))}{x_h-x_i}.$$

By induction the tail K equals P_h plus an element of J', where P_h=product_(t∈{h}∪((h,j)\B'))(y_(t+1)−x_h) and J' is generated by the matches for c∈C'. Every generator of J' is independent of x_h, so the remainder is sum_s a_s x_h^s with a_s∈J' and divided-differencing keeps it in J'. The set of factors of P_h contains θ(h): if θ(h)>h it is the removed target, and if θ(h)=h it is the initial h. Modulo its last match, y_(θ(h)+1)=x_h. Hence P_h=0 and P_h|_(x_h=x_i) contains the factor x_h−x_i. Cancellation of that integral factor leaves exactly the desired product on the tail, deleting θ(h). All indices below h are unmatched because every target is at least its source and every source is at least h. The prefix product therefore completes [i,j)\B. This proves the congruence without specializing a denominator.

For the quantum application at q≠1, evaluate x_k=−q^(λ_k−k)/(q−1) and y_(k+1)=−q^(λ_(k+1)−k)/(q−1). Then c_(i,k)=x_k−x_i and b_(i,k)=y_(k+1)−x_i vanish precisely at equal residue positions. If B is the zero-b set, C⊆the zero-c set, and θ:C→B is a bounded compensation bijection, all generators of J vanish. The norm is exactly product_(t∈[i,j)\B)b_(i,t), nonzero by its definition. These are polynomial evaluations with q−1 a field unit. The q=1 branch uses x_k=k−λ_k and y_(k+1)=k−λ_(k+1), giving the integer difference formulas instead; it does not specialize an inverse of q−1. This closes the norm-polynomial part in both regimes, including coincident residues and arbitrary field characteristic. It does not yet establish the quantum operator action, its radical criterion or the highest-cell identification.
