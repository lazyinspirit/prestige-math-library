## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The rational type-A Soergel category $\mathrm{SBim}_n$ over $\mathbb Q$ with its indecomposables $B_w$, its real scalar extension $\mathrm{SBim}_n^R$ over $S=\mathbb R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ with the extended objects $M^R=M\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb R$ ([[def-real-type-a-soergel-category-and-scalar-extension]]), and the degree-zero endomorphism algebra $A_w:=\operatorname{End}^0_{\mathbb Q}(B_w)$.

[F1] $\mathbb Q\to\mathbb R$ is flat; scalar extension is an exact additive monoidal functor on graded bimodules, commutes with finite direct sums and internal shifts, and on a finite right-free bimodule $M$ satisfies $\overline{M^R}=(\overline M)\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb R$ for the reduction $\overline M=M/MS_+$ ([[def-real-type-a-soergel-category-and-scalar-extension]]).

[F2] $\operatorname{SBim}_n$ is equivalent to $\operatorname{Kar}(D_n)$ and is Krull-Schmidt with one indecomposable $B_w$ for every $w\in S_n$, unique up to internal shift; every object of $\mathrm{SBim}_n$ is a finite direct sum of shifts of the $B_w$ ([[thm-indecomposable-type-a-diagrammatic-soergel-objects-are-indexed-by-permutations-and-shifts]], [[thm-type-a-diagrammatic-and-bimodule-soergel-categories-are-equivalent]]).

[F3] Bott-Samelson bimodules and their summands carry $\Delta$- and $\nabla$-flags with layers of the form $R_x\{a\}=R_x(d)$; all edges take place in the graded free right $R$-module categories, the multiplicities $(M:\Delta_x(d))$, $(M:\nabla_x(d))$ are intrinsic functions of $M$, and the characters $h_\Delta,h_\nabla$ are additive on direct sums; the top support layer $\Gamma_w B_w$ is a single copy of $\Delta_w$ ([[lem-bott-samelson-bimodules-have-delta-and-nabla-support-filtrations]], [[lem-type-a-support-filtration-multiplicities-are-intrinsic]], [[def-type-a-standard-graph-bimodules-support-filtrations-and-character]], [[lem-type-a-top-support-layers-are-controlled-by-reflection-localization]]).

[F4] The $\Delta$-character $h_\Delta$ is a homomorphism of $\mathbb Z[v,v^{-1}]$-algebras on the split Grothendieck group with $h_\Delta(vX)=v\,h_\Delta(X)$, the $\nabla$-character satisfies $h_\nabla=d\circ h_\Delta$ for the semilinear involution $d$ with $d(v)=v^{-1}$, and both are multiplicative on tensor products: $h_\Delta(B_xB_s)=h_\Delta(B_x)h_\Delta(B_s)$ and $h_\nabla(B_xB_s)=h_\nabla(B_x)h_\nabla(B_s)$; the simple generator has $h_\Delta(B_s)=h_\nabla(B_s)=\underline H_s$, the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis element of the simple reflection ([[thm-split-grothendieck-group-of-the-soergel-category-is-the-type-a-hecke-algebra]], [[lem-the-type-a-standard-character-is-multiplicative]]).

[F5] The trace form $(-,-)$ on $H_v(n)$ has orthonormal standard basis $(H_x,H_y)=\delta_{xy}$, is $A$-bilinear, and on the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis satisfies $(\underline H_x,\underline H_y)\in v\mathbb Z[v]$ for $x\ne y$ and $(\underline H_x,\underline H_x)\in 1+v\mathbb Z[v]$ ([[lem-hecke-trace-bilinear-form-and-its-values-on-the-kazhdan-lusztig-basis]]).

[F6] The Kazhdan-Lusztig basis of the frontier Hecke page satisfies $\underline H_x\underline H_s=\underline H_{xs}+\sum_{zs<z<x}\mu(z,x)\underline H_z$ when $xs>x$, and $\underline H_x\underline H_s=(v+v^{-1})\underline H_x$ when $xs<x$, with integer $\mu(z,x)=[v]p_{z,x}$ and the classical normalization $P_{z,x}(v^{-2})=v^{-(\ell(x)-\ell(z))}p_{z,x}$, the coefficients satisfying $p_{z,x}=0$ unless $z\le x$, $p_{x,x}=1$ and $p_{z,x}\in v^{\ell(x)-\ell(z)}\mathbb Z[v^{-2}]$; under $\mathrm{S}(w)$ both characters of $B_w$ equal $\underline H_w=\sum_up_{u,w}H_u$ in the normalized standard basis $\{H_u\}$ of the trace-form lemma ([[thm-kazhdan-lusztig-basis-multiplication-formula]], [[def-kazhdan-lusztig-polynomials-in-the-classical-q-normalization]], [[thm-existence-and-uniqueness-of-the-kazhdan-lusztig-basis]], [[lem-hecke-trace-bilinear-form-and-its-values-on-the-kazhdan-lusztig-basis]]).

[F7] For objects $M,N$ of $\mathrm{SBim}_n$ the Hom space is graded free over $R$ with graded rank $\operatorname{rk}\operatorname{Hom}(M,N)=\langle h_\Delta(M),h_\nabla(N)\rangle$, the standard pairing with $\langle H_x,H_y\rangle=\delta_{xy}$ on the normalized standard basis ([[thm-the-type-a-soergel-hom-formula]], [[lem-hecke-trace-bilinear-form-and-its-values-on-the-kazhdan-lusztig-basis]]).

## Statement

Let $B_w$ be the rational indecomposable type-A Soergel bimodule and let $B_w^R$ be its real scalar extension. (1) $B_w^R$ is indecomposable; its finite-dimensional degree-zero endomorphism algebra is local with residue field $\mathbb R$. If $S(w)$ holds, its degree-zero endomorphism algebra is $\mathbb R$, but no rank-one assertion for the full graded endomorphism module is made. (2) Real extension preserves the labels and shifts of all finite Krull-Schmidt decompositions of rational Soergel bimodules, and every real indecomposable is a unique $B_w^R(d)$. (3) $\Delta$/$\nabla$ support-filtration multiplicities, characters, reduction by positive-degree right polynomials, and Hom spaces commute with scalar extension; thus character identities over $\mathbb R$ descend to $\mathbb Q$. (4) If $xs>x$ and $S(y)$ holds for all $y<xs$, then $B_xB_s=B_{xs}\oplus\bigoplus_{y<xs}B_y^{a_y}$ with $a_y$ nonnegative integers and no grading shifts; the top summand occurs once. If $xs<x$ and $S(x)$ holds, then $B_xB_s=B_x(1)\oplus B_x(-1)$. All assertions concern the normalized self-dual indecomposables.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; locality, flat base change and multiplicity comparison.

1.1 **Base change of Hom and reduction.** For rational finite right-free graded bimodules $M,N$ and every degree $j$, the space $\operatorname{Hom}^j(M,N)$ is the kernel of the finitely many $\mathbb Q$-linear equations expressing left $R$-linearity, right $R$-linearity and degree-$j$ homogeneity on the finitely many coordinates of $M$; tensor product with the flat ring $\mathbb R$ preserves these kernels, so $\operatorname{Hom}^j(M^R,N^R)=\operatorname{Hom}^j(M,N)\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb R$, and this identification is compatible with composition, with shifts and with finite direct sums. Moreover $\overline{M^R}=\overline M\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb R$, because the right augmentation ideal of $S$ is the $\mathbb R$-span of the rational one and tensoring the right presentation $M\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb R$ with $S/S_+=\mathbb R$ gives $(\overline M)\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb R$; also $\Delta_x(d)^R\cong R_x(d)$ for the graph bimodules, since these are free of rank one. [given, F1]

1.2 **Support cuts and top layers.** The support submodules $\Gamma_x$, $\Gamma_{\le x}$, $\Gamma_{\ge x}$ of [F3] are defined by kernels and images of multiplication by invariant elements and hence are compatible with scalar extension in the sense $\Gamma_x(M^R)=(\Gamma_xM)^R$; the top support layer of an indecomposable $B_w$ is, after extension, the single graph layer $\Delta_w$. [given, F1, F3]

2.1 **Local endomorphism algebra over $\mathbb Q$.** The graded Hom space $\operatorname{End}(B_w)$ is finite-dimensional over $\mathbb Q$ in each degree by [F7], hence $A_w=\operatorname{End}^0(B_w)$ is a finite-dimensional unital $\mathbb Q$-algebra; since $B_w$ is indecomposable in the idempotent-complete category $\mathrm{SBim}_n$, every idempotent of $A_w$ is $0$ or $1$, so $A_w$ is local. Restricting $\mathbb Q$-linear endomorphisms of $B_w$ to the top support layer $\Delta_w$ (a rank-one free module over the field $\mathbb Q$) gives a unital algebra homomorphism $A_w\to\operatorname{End}^0(\Delta_w)=\mathbb Q$ that is surjective, because scalars act as scalars on the layer; its kernel is therefore the unique maximal ideal $J_w$ of $A_w$, so $A_w/J_w=\mathbb Q$. [step 1.2, F2, F3, F7]

3.1 **Real endomorphism algebras and indecomposability.** By [F1] applied to the graded endomorphism space, $\operatorname{End}^0(B_w^R)=\operatorname{End}^0(B_w)\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb R=A_w\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb R$, and this algebra is finite-dimensional over $\mathbb R$; since $J_w$ is nilpotent (a proper two-sided ideal of the finite-dimensional local algebra $A_w$), so is $J_w\otimes\mathbb R$, and every element of $A_w\otimes\mathbb R$ outside $J_w\otimes\mathbb R$ is invertible by a finite geometric series after lifting its nonzero residue class; hence $A_w\otimes\mathbb R$ is local with residue field $\mathbb R$. A nonzero idempotent endomorphism of $B_w^R$ would be $0$ or $1$ in the local algebra $\operatorname{End}^0(B_w^R)$, so $B_w^R$ is indecomposable. [step 2.1, F1]

4.1 **Labels and shifts over $\mathbb R$.** Every object of $\mathrm{SBim}_n^R$ is a direct summand of a finite sum of shifts of real Bott-Samelson products, and each real Bott-Samelson product is the scalar extension of the corresponding rational product by [F1] and the monoidality of scalar extension; decomposing the rational object by [F2] and extending the finite decomposition gives that every real object is a summand of a finite sum $\bigoplus B_w^R(d)$. Since each direct summand has local endomorphism algebra by step 3.1, the Krull-Schmidt theorem in this idempotent-complete additive category gives that the object is a finite direct sum of copies of the $B_w^R(d)$, uniquely up to permutation, so real extension preserves labels and shifts; in particular every real indecomposable is a unique $B_w^R(d)$. [step 3.1, F1, F2]

4.2 **Degree-zero endomorphisms under $S(w)$.** Assume $S(w)$, so $h_\Delta(B_w)=\underline H_w$ and $h_\nabla(B_w)=d(\underline H_w)=\underline H_w$ by [F4] and the bar-invariance of the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis; by [F7] and [F5] the graded rank of $\operatorname{End}(B_w^R)$ is $\langle\underline H_w,\underline H_w\rangle=\sum_{u\le w}p_{u,w}^2$, a polynomial in $v$ with constant term $1$ by [F6] since $p_{u,w}\in v\mathbb Z[v]$ for $u<w$. Hence $\dim_{\mathbb R}\operatorname{End}^0(B_w^R)=1$, and since the unital algebra $\operatorname{End}^0(B_w^R)$ contains the scalars $\mathbb R$ as a subfield and is one-dimensional over $\mathbb R$, it equals $\mathbb R$; no statement is made about the full graded endomorphism module, whose graded rank is the displayed polynomial, generally not $1$. [step 3.1, F5, F6, F7]

5.1 **Characters and reduction under scalar extension (3).** Let $M$ be rational with a $\Delta$-flag in the sense of [F3]; each layer $\Delta_x(d)=R_x(d)$ has $R$-rank one, hence the same multiplicity function over $\mathbb Q$ and over $\mathbb R$, and by step 1.1 and step 1.2 the extension of the flag is a flag of $M^R$ with the same shifts, so $(M^R:\Delta_x(d))=(M:\Delta_x(d))$ and $h_\Delta(M^R)=h_\Delta(M)$, $h_\nabla(M^R)=h_\nabla(M)$; additivity on direct sums from [F3] gives the same for every $\mathbb Q$-defined object, and step 1.1 gives the reduction identity and the Hom identity. If two character identities hold after scalar extension, their coefficients lie in $\mathbb Z[v^{\pm1}]$ and scalar extension of the free $A$-module $A=\mathbb Z[v^{\pm1}]$ is injective, so the identities hold over $\mathbb Q$ as well. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 4.1, F1, F3, F4]

6.1 **The ascent decomposition (4): character comparison.** Let $xs>x$ and assume $\mathrm{S}(y)$ for all $y<xs$ (so also $\mathrm{S}(x)$, and $\mathrm{S}(s)$ when $x\ne e$); by steps 4.1 and 5.1 decompose $M=B_xB_s=\bigoplus_{w,e}B_w^R(e)^{m_{w,e}}$ in $\mathrm{SBim}_n^R$. By [F4] and step 5.1, $h_\Delta(M)=h_\Delta(B_x)h_\Delta(B_s)=\underline H_x\underline H_s$ (with $h_\Delta(B_e)=1$ in the case $x=e$), and by [F6] $\underline H_x\underline H_s=\underline H_{xs}+\sum_{y<xs}\mu_y\underline H_y$ in the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis, where $\mu_y=\mu(y,x)$ if $y<x$ and $ys<y$, and $\mu_y=0$ otherwise; all other coefficients vanish. On the other hand $h_\Delta(M)=\sum_{w,e}m_{w,e}v^e\underline H_w$ because under $\mathrm{S}(w)$ the character of $B_w^R(e)$ is $v^e\underline H_w$. Comparing the two expansions in the basis $\{v^e\underline H_w\}$ of the free $A$-module $H\otimes_A A[v^{\pm1}]$, using that the $\underline H_w$ form an $A$-basis, yields $m_{xs,0}=1$, $m_{xs,e}=0$ for $e\ne0$, and $\sum_e m_{y,e}v^e=\mu_y$ for every $y<xs$. [step 4.1, step 5.1, F4, F5, F6]

7.1 **No shifts on the lower summands.** For $y<xs$ we have $S(y)$, so $h_\Delta(B_y)=\underline H_y$ and $h_\nabla(B_y)=d(\underline H_y)=\underline H_y$, while $h_\nabla(M)=h_\nabla(B_x)h_\nabla(B_s)=\underline H_x\underline H_s$ by [F4] and step 5.1; by [F7] together with the symmetry of the standard pairing $\operatorname{rk}\operatorname{Hom}(B_y,M)=\langle\underline H_y,\underline H_x\underline H_s\rangle=\langle\underline H_x\underline H_s,\underline H_y\rangle=\operatorname{rk}\operatorname{Hom}(M,B_y)$. Expanding $\underline H_y=\sum_u p_{u,y}H_u$ and $\underline H_x\underline H_s=\sum_u c_uH_u$ in the normalized standard basis, and using [F6], this polynomial equals $\sum_u p_{u,y}\,c_u$ with $c_u\in\mathbb Z[v]$ and $p_{u,y}\in\mathbb Z[v]$, hence lies in $\mathbb Z[v]$; therefore $\operatorname{Hom}^j(B_y,M)=\operatorname{Hom}^j(M,B_y)=0$ for $j<0$. A summand $B_y(e)$ with $e>0$ would give a nonzero inclusion $B_y(e)\to M$ of degree $0$, hence a nonzero element of $\operatorname{Hom}^0(B_y(e),M)=\operatorname{Hom}^{-e}(B_y,M)$ in negative degree $-e<0$, a contradiction; a summand with $e<0$ would give a nonzero projection $M\to B_y(e)$ of degree $0$, hence a nonzero element of $\operatorname{Hom}^0(M,B_y(e))=\operatorname{Hom}^{e}(M,B_y)$ in negative degree $e<0$, again a contradiction. Hence $m_{y,e}=0$ for $e\ne0$ and every $y<xs$. [step 6.1, F5, F6, F7]

8.1 **The ascent decomposition, conclusion.** By steps 6.1 and 7.1, $M=B_{xs}\oplus\bigoplus_{y<xs}B_y(0)^{a_y}$ with $a_y:=m_{y,0}$ nonnegative integers, and the character identity of step 6.1 gives $a_y=\mu_y$ for every $y<xs$; the top summand $B_{xs}$ occurs exactly once. [step 6.1, step 7.1]

8.2 **The descent case.** Let $xs<x$ and assume $S(x)$; by the right-descent clause of the multiplication formula [F6], $\underline H_x\underline H_s=(v+v^{-1})\underline H_x$, and by the same decomposition-and-character comparison as in step 6.1 we write $B_xB_s=\bigoplus_{w,e}B_w^R(e)^{m_{w,e}}$ and equate $h_\Delta(B_xB_s)=h_\Delta(B_x)h_\Delta(B_s)=\sum_{w,e}m_{w,e}v^e\underline H_w$ with $(v+v^{-1})\underline H_x$; since the $\underline H_w$ form a basis and $v^e\underline H_w$ equals a term of $(v+v^{-1})\underline H_x$ only for $w=x$, the multiplicities satisfy $m_{x,1}=m_{x,-1}=1$ and $m_{w,e}=0$ otherwise, so $B_xB_s=B_x(1)\oplus B_x(-1)$. [step 7.1, F4, F6]

9.1 **Conclusion.** Step 3.1 proves the indecomposability and locality in (1), and step 4.2 the assertion under $S(w)$; step 4.1 proves (2); step 5.1 proves (3); steps 8.1 and 8.2 prove both parts of (4). No positivity of Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials is assumed, no shift of a lower summand is produced, and no choice principle is used. [step 3.1, step 4.1, step 4.2, step 5.1, step 8.1, step 8.2] ∎

## Remarks

- The endomorphism algebra is local over $\mathbb R$ with residue field $\mathbb R$ because the residue-field extension $A_w/J_w=\mathbb Q$ is unchanged by $\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb R$; this is the reason the argument restricts to the degree-zero part and to the top support layer rather than asserting anything about the whole graded Hom module.
- In (4) the coefficients $a_y$ are the actual multiplicities and equal the Kazhdan-Lusztig $\mu$-coefficients appearing in the generator multiplication formula; no positivity theorem is used to obtain them, they are read off from the character identity.
