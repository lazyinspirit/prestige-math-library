## Statement

Let $H^\Lambda_\alpha$ be a central content sector (possibly zero) as in
[[def-cyclotomic-hecke-central-content-sector-notation]], with the residue
tuples $i=(i_1,\dots,i_d)$ contained in a fixed index set $I_\alpha$.

1. Set $y_r:=\sum_{i\in I_\alpha}(x_r-i_r)e(i)$ in the degenerate case and
   $y_r:=\sum_{i\in I_\alpha}(1-q^{-i_r}X_r)e(i)$ in the nondegenerate case
   ($1\le r\le d$). Then $y_1,\dots,y_d$ are commuting nilpotent elements of
   $H^\Lambda_\alpha$; consequently every rational expression displayed below,
   expanded as a power series in the nilpotent $y$'s, is a finite and
   well-defined element of $H^\Lambda_\alpha$.
2. The power series
   $$p_r(i):=\begin{cases}1,&i_r=i_{r+1},\\ (i_r-i_{r+1}+y_r-y_{r+1})^{-1},&i_r\ne i_{r+1}\end{cases}$$
   (degenerate case), respectively
   $$P_r(i):=\begin{cases}1,&i_r=i_{r+1},\\ (1-q)\bigl(1-y_r(i)y_{r+1}(i)^{-1}\bigr)^{-1},&i_r\ne i_{r+1},\end{cases} \qquad y_r(i):=q^{i_r}(1-y_r)$$
   (nondegenerate case), satisfy (3.23)–(3.25) respectively (4.24)–(4.26),
   (4.28)–(4.30) and (4.32); and the intertwining elements
   $$\varphi_r:=\sum_i\bigl(s_r+p_r(i)\bigr)e(i),\qquad \Phi_r:=\sum_i\bigl(T_r+P_r(i)\bigr)e(i)$$
   satisfy all identities (3.14)–(3.20) of the source's Lemma 3.1,
   respectively (4.14)–(4.20) of its Lemma 4.1.
3. There exist invertible power series $q_r(i)\in F[\![y_r,y_{r+1}]\!]$
   (respectively $Q_r(i)$) satisfying (3.27)–(3.29), respectively
   (4.33)–(4.35); the explicit candidates (3.30) respectively (4.36) do, and
   for them the identity (3.43) respectively (4.44) holds.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The cyclotomic Hecke algebra $H^\Lambda=H^\Lambda_d$ of [[def-affine-and-cyclotomic-type-a-hecke-algebras]] with its central content sectors $H^\Lambda_\alpha$ and projectors $e(i)$ ([[def-cyclotomic-hecke-central-content-sector-notation]]), and the notation of the Statement.

[F1] On the summand $e(i)$ the polynomial generator has the single generalized residue $i_r$: in the degenerate branch $x_r-i_r$ is nilpotent on $e(i)$, and in the nondegenerate branch $q^{-i_r}X_r-1$ is nilpotent on $e(i)$; the $y_r$ are commuting sums of these, hence commuting nilpotents ([[def-cyclotomic-hecke-central-content-sector-notation]]).

[F2] The divided-difference convention is $\partial_r(f)=(s_rf-f)/(y_r-y_{r+1})$, and on equal colours $i_r=i_{r+1}$ the intertwiners reduce to $\varphi_r=s_r+1$ respectively $\Phi_r=T_r+1$ ([[def-affine-and-cyclotomic-type-a-hecke-algebras]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** The sector data and the generators $X_r^{\pm1},T_r$

(respectively $x_r,s_r$) of $H^\Lambda$.

1.1 Nilpotence and finite evaluation. By [F1] each $y_r$ is a finite sum of nilpotents supported on the orthogonal summands $e(i)$, so $y_r^N=\sum_iy_r^Ne(i)=0$ for $N\gg0$; the $y_r$ commute because the polynomial generators do. Hence the algebra generated by the $y_r$ is finite over $F$, and any rational function in the $y_r$ whose denominators have invertible constant coefficient evaluates to a finite sum of monomials: expand $(c+u)^{-1}=c^{-1}\sum_{k\ge0}(-c^{-1}u)^k$ and terminate at $N$. In particular all divisions below by differences with a unit constant term are well defined in $H^\Lambda_\alpha$. [F1, algebra]

2.1 The local power-series identities. The displayed definitions are the source's two local power series; on unequal colours the denominators $i_r-i_{r+1}+y_r-y_{r+1}$ and $1-y_r(i)y_{r+1}(i)^{-1}$ have invertible constant terms, and on equal colours the value $1$ is used. Expanding in $F[\![y_r,y_{r+1}]\!]$ and clearing denominators verifies the local power-series identities: each is an equality of power series with unit denominators, so it is a polynomial identity in the finitely many nilpotent variables after using $y_r(i)=q^{i_r}(1-y_r)$, equivalently $y_r=1-q^{-i_r}y_r(i)$. The intertwiner identities of the source's Lemma 3.1 and Lemma 4.1 are then checked by the same case-by-case computations, using the divided-difference convention [F2] and the equal-colour values $\varphi_r=s_r+1$, $\Phi_r=T_r+1$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The invertible series and the final identity. For the explicit candidates $q_r(i)$ and $Q_r(i)$, the normalizing identities are finite computations in $F[\![y_r,y_{r+1}]\!]$ using the identities of step 2.1: every quotient has a numerator divisible by the displayed dot difference, so the series has a nonzero constant coefficient and is invertible; on unequal colours the spectral-value denominators are units, and on equal colours the fixed values are $q_r=1+y_{r+1}-y_r$ (degenerate) and $Q_r=1-q+qy_{r+1}-y_r$ (nondegenerate), with no extra denominator. The final compatibility identity is the source's displayed consequence of the quadratic relation and the normalizing identities, and holds for these candidates by substitution. All identities are polynomial or rational-unit identities, so no algebraic closure or square root is used, and finite nilpotent evaluation is valid over $F$ itself. [step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Conclusion. Step 1.1 gives the commuting nilpotents $y_r$ and the finite evaluation of all displayed power series; step 2.1 gives the local power-series and intertwiner identities (i)–(ii); and step 3.1 gives the invertible series together with the compatibility identity, completing (iii). Thus the sector $H^\Lambda_\alpha$ carries the full local intertwiner calculus of the source, over the original field and without any choice principle. [step 1.1, step 3.1] ∎

## Remarks

The lemma is the local computational core of the Hecke-to-KLR comparison: the only non-invertible denominators are the differences of spectral values, and these are handled by the nilpotence of the $y_r$ inside a fixed content sector. The case of a zero sector is included because all statements are supported on the projectors $e(i)$, which may vanish.
