## Statement

Let $F$ be a field and $q\in F^{\times}$ with quantum characteristic $e$ ([[def-quantum-characteristic-of-an-invertible-hecke-parameter]], [[def-q-integer-q-factorial-and-q-multinomial]]), let $n\ge0$ and $d\ge n$, and let $W^{\lambda}_F$ be the Weyl modules of $S_F(d,n)$ ([[def-type-a-q-schur-algebra-and-weyl-modules]]). Then for partitions $\lambda,\mu\vdash n$ with at most $d$ parts, $W^{\lambda}_F$ and $W^{\mu}_F$ lie in the same block of $S_F(d,n)$ if and only if $\lambda$ and $\mu$ have the same $e$-core ([[def-e-core-e-quotient-and-e-weight]]). Consequently, for finite $e$, the blocks of $S_F(d,n)$ are indexed by the $e$-cores $\gamma$ with $|\gamma|\le n$ and $|\gamma|\equiv n\pmod e$, and the block of $W^{\lambda}_F$ is determined by $C_e(\lambda)$. For $e=\infty$ we set $C_\infty(\lambda):=\lambda$; the algebra is semisimple and its blocks are indexed by the partitions of $n$.

No choice principle is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $F$, parameter $q$, quantum characteristic $e$, integer $d\ge n$, and the Weyl modules in the statement. Write $S=S_F(d,n)$ and $W^{\lambda}=W^{\lambda}_F$.

[F1] The semistandard cellular basis has cells $W^{\lambda}$, nonzero absolutely simple heads $L^{\lambda}$, and dominance-unitriangular decomposition multiplicities $d_{\lambda\mu}$ ([[def-type-a-q-schur-algebra-and-weyl-modules]]). The cellular anti-involution induces contragredient duality fixing the simple isomorphism classes, through the nondegenerate cell form on each simple head ([[lem-cell-form-is-associative-and-its-radical-is-a-submodule]], [[thm-nonzero-cell-heads-classify-the-simple-modules-of-a-cellular-algebra]]).

[F2] The balanced hook-ratio Jantzen formula and alternating beta symbols ([[thm-q-schaper-virtual-radical-layer-formula]], [[def-alternating-weyl-and-specht-symbols-from-beta-sequences]]) use conjugate beta numbers $\beta_j=\lambda'_j+N-j$, with $N$ sufficiently large, and read $$\sum_{r>0}[W^{\lambda}(r)]=\sum_{(a,b),(a,c)\in[\lambda],\ b<c}\bigl(v(h^{\lambda}_{ab})-v(h^{\lambda}_{ac})\bigr)[W^{\beta+h^{\lambda}_{ac}\varepsilon_b-h^{\lambda}_{ac}\varepsilon_c}],$$ where $W^{\lambda}(r)$ are filtration submodules, $W^{\lambda}(1)=\operatorname{rad}W^{\lambda}$, and the right hand symbols are zero on repeated or negative entries and otherwise have the sign of sorting the beta sequence. The suppliers must carry this balanced form; the earlier displayed unbalanced manuscript indices do not preserve degree.

[F3] Core abaci are obtained by packing beads upwards on each runner, so their shapes depend only on runner bead counts; rim-hook removal is a bead-to-gap move ([[def-e-core-e-quotient-and-e-weight]], [[thm-e-core-is-independent-of-the-rim-hook-removal-order]], [[def-beta-set-and-e-runner-abacus-of-a-partition]]). Conjugation reverses dominance ([[def-dominance-order-on-partitions]]). Transposing every strip in a maximal $e$-hook removal sequence gives such a sequence for the conjugate partition; hook lengths are preserved by transposition, so its terminal core is the conjugate core. Hence $C_e(\lambda')=C_e(\lambda)'$.

[F4] Primitive central idempotents decompose the identity, and every finite-dimensional module splits into their block summands ([[def-blocks-of-a-finite-dimensional-algebra-by-primitive-central-idempotents]]). Weyl classes are linearly independent in the Grothendieck group, so a nonzero accumulated Weyl coefficient in the class of a module supported in one block forces that Weyl module into the block ([[lem-virtual-weyl-combinations-detect-q-schur-block-separation]], [[lem-semistandard-weight-dimensions-detect-virtual-weyl-classes]]).



[F5] The radical of a finite-dimensional algebra is nilpotent and its quotient semisimple ([[thm-jacobson-radical-is-nilpotent-and-the-quotient-is-semisimple]]); the Wedderburn decomposition is a product of matrix rings over division rings ([[thm-wedderburn-artin-theorem]]). Cellular ideal layers are the tensor products of the contragredient left cell and the right cell ([[def-cellular-algebra-basis-and-cell-module]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Lift and valuation. Use $R=F[X]_{(X-q)}$ with $\hat q=X$ and uniformizer $X-q$. The element $X$ is a unit since $q\ne0$, and every polynomial $[m]_X=1+X+\cdots+X^{m-1}$ is nonzero, so the generic factorial is nonzero. For $n=0$, $S_F(d,0)=F$, with its one empty-label module, so all assertions hold directly; hence assume $n>0$. Its valuation $v(m)$ is positive exactly when $[m]_q=0$, equivalently $e\mid m$ for finite $e$; for $e=\infty$ all valuations vanish. In characteristic $p$ the positive valuation may exceed1, which causes no difficulty below. [given, F2, algebra]

1.2 Block support and Jantzen edges. A finite-dimensional module with simple head is indecomposable: every nonzero summand has nonzero head, so two summands would make the head nonsimple. Thus $W^{\lambda}$ has just one nonzero block summand, and every Jantzen submodule is supported in that block. By [F4], any nonzero accumulated coefficient $J_{\lambda\mu}$ of $[W^{\mu}]$ in [F2] puts $W^{\lambda},W^{\mu}$ in the same block. This uses the sum of submodule classes, without identifying it with the radical class. [F1, F2, F4]

2.1 Balanced terms preserve the core and descend in dominance. Put $s=h_{ab}$ and $t=h_{ac}$, so $s-t=\beta_b-\beta_c$. If a hook coefficient is nonzero, at least one of $s,t$ is divisible by $e$. If $e\mid t$, the shifted bead residues are unchanged. Otherwise $e\mid s$, so $t\equiv\beta_c-\beta_b\pmod e$ and the two shifted residues exchange. Thus every surviving symbol has the same runner counts and core as $\lambda$. Moreover replacing the two conjugate beta entries $u>v$ by $u+t,v-t$ strictly increases conjugate majorization; the sorted target therefore strictly decreases original dominance. Accumulating equal target labels preserves both conclusions. [F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 No outgoing edge implies a binary row condition. Suppose every accumulated outgoing coefficient from $\mu$ is zero. For row $i$, put $g_i=N+i-1-\mu_i$, a gap in its conjugate beta set; its row hooks are exactly $\beta_j-g_i>0$. If that row contained a divisible hook $s$ and a nondivisible hook $t$, repeatedly replace $t$ by $s+t$ while their sum is another row hook. This finite climb ends with $s+t$ absent from the hook set. The balanced term for the nodes of hook lengths $s,t$ then has lower shifted bead $g_i$, which is a gap, and upper shifted bead $g_i+s+t$, which is absent from the beta set. Its symbol survives and its coefficient is $\pm v(s)\ne0$. There is no cancellation: any surviving balanced term replaces two lost values by a new value larger than both and a new value smaller than both, so those four values determine the pair and its row gap uniquely; the gaps $g_i$ strictly increase with $i$. This contradicts the zero accumulated coefficient. Therefore each row has either all hook lengths divisible by $e$ or none; call this the binary row condition. [F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Composition-factor cores. An off-head simple occurs in $W^{\lambda}$ exactly when it occurs in its radical, equivalently in the positive Jantzen sum: the first submodule is the radical and every later one is contained in it. Its nonzero multiplicity in that sum implies it occurs in some $W^{\mu}$ with $J_{\lambda\mu}\ne0$, since otherwise its coefficient in the sum would be zero. Step 2.1 gives the same core and strict dominance descent. Induction on the finite dominance poset proves that every factor $L^{\nu}$ of $W^{\lambda}$ has $C_e(\nu)=C_e(\lambda)$. No identity equating the radical class with the positive sum is asserted. [F1, F2, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 Vertical core removal under the binary row condition. A removable $e$-hook has hook length $e$ at its defining node. Every hook in that row is then divisible, so its last-column hook, a positive multiple of $e$ no larger than $e$, equals $e$. Hook lengths strictly decrease across the row, forcing the defining node to be at the row end: the strip is vertical on $e$ equal-length rows $i,\ldots,i+e-1$ in column $j$. Removing it decreases that column height by $e$. In unaffected rows hook divisibility is unchanged. For an affected row $i+t$, hooks in the retained columns were all congruent to $-t$ modulo $e$, because row $i$ had all hooks divisible and those rows had equal length. Their new hook lengths are all congruent to $-t-1$, so the binary row condition persists. Induction shows every core-removal strip is vertical. [F3, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 The Cartan graph locally. Choose a complete orthogonal primitive idempotent system of $S$, with one representative $f_\mu$ for each simple label and repeated representatives as necessary. By [F5] and the absolute simplicity in [F1], the semisimple quotient is a product of full matrix rings over $F$. Lift its matrix diagonal idempotents: the radical is nilpotent and the iteration $u\mapsto u+(1-2u)(u^2-u)$ changes an error $a=u^2-u$ to $-3a^2+4a^3$, so finite iteration lifts an idempotent; successively working in complementary corners lifts an orthogonal system. Absolute simplicity gives $\dim L^{\lambda}f_\mu=\delta_{\lambda\mu}$, hence exact multiplication by $f_\mu$ gives $\dim W^{\lambda}f_\mu=d_{\lambda\mu}$. The successive cellular ideal bimodules are the contragredient cell module tensor the right cell module. Apply the exact projection $f_\mu(-)f_\nu$ and add their dimensions; [F1]'s duality fixes the simple labels, giving $\dim f_\mu Sf_\nu=\sum_\lambda d_{\lambda\mu}d_{\lambda\nu}$. This is the Cartan entry of the primitive projective $f_\mu S$. A disconnected graph on the full primitive idempotent system, with edges whenever either mixed corner is nonzero, yields central idempotents by summing each component: all mixed corners vanish in both directions. Conversely a central block idempotent forbids mixed corners. Thus simple labels in one block are joined by pairs sharing a Weyl factor, and step 3.1 proves that labels in the same block have the same core. [F1, F4, F5, step 3.1, algebra]

4.2 The minimum label and the bridge shapes. Assume $e<\infty$. Fix core $\gamma$ and weight $w$, with $l=\ell(\gamma)$, and set $\rho=(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_l,1^{ew})$. It has core $\gamma$ by removing its bottom vertical $e$-strips, and is dominance-minimal in the class: every same-core partition contains $\gamma$ as a diagram, and appending all additional boxes as rows of size1 minimizes each dominance partial sum. Let $\mu\ne\rho$ satisfy the binary row condition. Choose the first row $i$ with divisible hooks and put $j=\mu_i$, $h=h^{\mu}_{ij}=\mu'_j-i+1$, a positive multiple of $e$. If $j=1$, every divisible hook is in column1, so step 3.2 would give $\mu=\rho$; thus $j>1$. Remove the vertical $h$-strip, obtaining $\sigma$, then add $h$ boxes horizontally to its first row to get $\nu$, and vertically to its first column to get $\eta$. All have the same core, since $h$ is a multiple of $e$; $\nu$ dominates $\mu,\eta$, and $\eta$ is strictly below $\mu$ because the removed nodes are moved into new bottom rows. [F3, step 3.2, construct, algebra]

5.1 The bridge coefficients are nonzero. Write $l=\ell(\mu)=\ell(\sigma)$. If $i>1$, then $\sigma_1=\mu_1$ and the row1 hook pair in $\nu$ at columns $j,\mu_1+1$ has lengths $A=h^{\mu}_{1j}$ and $h$: the first is nondivisible because row1 precedes the first divisible row, and the second is divisible. Its balanced symbol sorts to $\mu$, so its coefficient is nonzero. If $i=1$, then $\sigma_1=j-1$, and the row1 pair at columns $j,j+1$ has lengths $h,h-1$; its balanced transfer $h-1$ sorts to $\mu$, again giving a nonzero coefficient. For $\eta$, the pair at columns $1,\sigma_1+1$ has lengths $A'=\sigma_1+h+l-1$ and $h$. When $i>1$, $A'-h^{\mu}_{i1}=A$, so $A'$ is nondivisible; when $i=1$, $A'=h^{\mu}_{11}+h-1$, also nondivisible. Its balanced symbol sorts to $\eta$. Each claimed target has precisely two lost and two gained beta values, so the uniqueness argument in step 2.2 rules out cancellation. Thus both $J_{\nu\mu}$ and $J_{\nu\eta}$ are nonzero. [F2, step 1.1, step 2.2, step 4.2, algebra]

6.1 Connectivity and sufficiency. Induct upwards from $\rho$ in the finite dominance poset. For $\mu\ne\rho$, if an outgoing coefficient $J_{\mu\tau}$ is nonzero, then $\tau$ is strictly below $\mu$ in the same core class by step 2.1, so induction connects $\tau$, and hence $\mu$, to $\rho$. If no outgoing coefficient exists, steps 2.2, 3.2, 4.2 and 5.1 connect $\mu$ to $\nu$ and then $\eta$, with $\eta$ strictly below $\mu$, and induction again connects it to $\rho$. Every such edge is a same-block implication by step 1.2. Thus every pair of labels of the same core lies in the same block. Together with step 4.1 this proves the equivalence. [step 1.2, step 2.1, step 4.1, step 2.2, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 Parameterization and infinite characteristic. Every simple is the head of a Weyl module, so every block has a partition label. The core sizes are $n-ew$, and each admissible core occurs via the partition $\rho$ of step 4.2; step 6.1 therefore gives the stated indexing. If $e=\infty$, all coefficients vanish by step 1.1, so positivity forces every first Jantzen submodule, hence every radical, to vanish. All Weyl modules are simple; their decomposition matrix is the identity. Step 4.1 then gives identity Cartan matrix, so every primitive projective is simple and $S$ is semisimple, with each partition its own block and core. This proves all assertions. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 4.1, step 4.2, step 6.1] ∎

## Remarks

The connectivity argument follows the corrected dominance-minimum route of Lyle–Mathas Proposition 3.7 and Corollary 4.3, while proving the needed elementary row properties locally. Their final Remark explicitly corrects the earlier first-row-only q-Schur argument; the old direct hook move can have zero coefficient.
