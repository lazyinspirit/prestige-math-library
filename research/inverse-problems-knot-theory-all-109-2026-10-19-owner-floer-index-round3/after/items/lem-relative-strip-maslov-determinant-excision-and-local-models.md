## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Consider an actual smooth complex rank-$m$ strip bundle, totally real horizontal boundary subbundles, specified end frames identifying these subbundles with $\mathbb R^m,i\mathbb R^m$, and a smooth real first-order operator with Cauchy–Riemann symbol and exact expression $\partial_s+i\partial_t$ in those frames outside a compact set. Its index means the real Fredholm index before source translation, on fixed-end $H^{q+1}\to H^q$ spaces, for an integer $q\ge0$.

Take a compact strip rectangle $Q$ containing the nonconstant part. Complete the horizontal boundary subbundles to a totally real loop along $\partial Q$ using the vertical end caps
$$L_\pm(t)=\tau_\pm e^{i\pi\rho(t)/2}\mathbb R^m,$$
where $\rho:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ is smooth, zero near zero, one near one, and increases from zero to one. The boundary orientation is that induced by $ds\wedge dt$. In any smooth global complex frame on $Q$, a local real basis $v_1,\ldots,v_m$ of this loop defines
$$\zeta=\frac{(\det_{\mathbb C}(v_1,\ldots,v_m))^2}{|\det_{\mathbb C}(v_1,\ldots,v_m)|^2}\in S^1.$$
These local expressions agree on overlaps. Its integer winding $M$ is independent of real bases, global complex frame, sufficiently large rectangle, and cap cutoff $\rho$, and
$$\operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb R}P=M.$$
The equality also holds with smooth two-sided weights below the end spectral gap $\pi/2$. This specifies actual caps and signs, rather than defining an unexplained topological index.

The following finite-dimensional excision and model calculations hold with the same squared determinant convention.

* Direct sums add $M$. Gluing two such strip problems with matching product end frames and operators exactly constant near the cut adds their actual real indices. This is a statement about the glued linear boundary problem.
* On a rectangle with finitely many disjoint round interior disks removed, suppose a continuous totally real subbundle in a trivial complex bundle is defined on the remaining compact surface. Then the determinant winding along the outer boundary is the sum of its windings along the positively counterclockwise hole circles. It suffices that the bundle be smooth near the boundary circles; for a smooth bundle the local windings can be computed by the following models.
* For the symmetric-coordinate map $q(z_1,z_2)=(z_1+z_2,z_1z_2)$, transport the two real lines generated by the same nonzero constant $v\in\mathbb C$ along $z_1=-\sqrt w,z_2=\sqrt w$, $w$ traversing a small counterclockwise circle. Their image under $dq$ has squared determinant winding $+1$. More generally an order-$r$ source-cover model consisting of the $r$ roots of $w$, with the same constant source vector on each sheet and elementary symmetric target coordinates, has winding $r-1$.
* A scalar target map $f(z)=z^{r+1}$, on a sheet unbranched over the parameter disk, sends a nonzero constant real direction to a line of squared phase winding $2r$ along a positive source circle.
* For two graph branches $(z,Az),(z,Bz)$ in $\mathbb C\times\mathbb C$, with $A,B:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^2$ real linear invertible and $A-B$ invertible, choose source directions $A^{-1}v,B^{-1}v$ for a fixed nonzero $v\in\mathbb C$. Push their target directions to the symmetric coordinates of $Az,Bz$. The resulting two-plane has squared determinant winding $2\operatorname{sign}\det_{\mathbb R}(A-B)$. This sign equals the oriented intersection sign of the two graph sheets, both oriented by their $z$ coordinate and the product oriented by base then target.
* A scalar source direction winding $k$ on an otherwise nonsingular sheet contributes $2k$ to the squared determinant winding. Extra fixed nonzero product directions change none of these local contributions.

These assertions do not identify the domain Euler measure or corner multiplicities of an arbitrary Whitney class. In particular, no representativity theorem, collapsed-tube calculation, corner rounding formula, or branched-cover Euler identity is included.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice, the actual strip problem and its stated exact end data, and, for each local calculation, exactly the model matrices and directions specified in the Statement.

[F1] An invertible smooth complex matrix loop extends across a disk exactly when its complex determinant winding vanishes. In particular the determinant winding of the restriction of a disk frame change is zero ([[lem-complex-frame-loop-extension-determinant-winding]]).

[F2] The actual strip operator has a smooth global complex frame $\Phi$ with lower boundary $\mathbb R^m$, upper boundary $e^{i\vartheta(s)}\mathbb R\times i\mathbb R^{m-1}$, where $\vartheta(-\infty)=\pi/2$, $\vartheta(+\infty)=\pi/2+\ell\pi$, and actual real index $-\ell$. The end frames of $\Phi$ are $\tau_-$ and $\tau_+S_+$ with $S_+$ real diagonal ([[lem-geometric-strip-linearization-frame-and-fredholm-index]]).

[F3] The actual index in [F2] agrees on all integer $H^{q+1}\to H^q$ realizations with only the zeroth real boundary trace, also with smooth two-sided weights below the limiting spectral gap ([[lem-strip-fredholm-index-agrees-in-higher-sobolev-spaces]]).

## Proof

1.1 **Squared phase is well defined, including unoriented boundary bundles.** A real basis of a totally real $m$-plane in $\mathbb C^m$ is complex independent: a complex dependence would express a vector in the plane also in its $i$ multiple, and splitting the dependence into real and imaginary parts forces both to vanish. A real basis change multiplies its complex determinant by a nonzero real determinant; its squared normalized phase is therefore unchanged. Local frames give one continuous map $\zeta$ even when a global oriented real basis does not exist. For a continuous loop into $S^1$, subdivide its parameter interval into finitely many subintervals whose images lie in open arcs of length less than $2\pi$. Each arc has an explicit continuous argument, for instance by the local arctangent after a fixed rotation. Add integer multiples of $2\pi$ successively so that neighboring arguments agree. The final argument differs from the initial one by $2\pi$ times an integer; this is winding. Refinement does not change that integer, so neither does the initial subdivision. Products add their argument changes. A homotopy of loops preserves this integer: uniform continuity permits a finite subdivision in both parameters with all relevant pointwise ratios in an arc about one, so adjacent loops have continuously chosen ratio argument with zero endpoint difference. This proves homotopy invariance by finitely many comparisons. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 **Frame invariance and the actual index sign.** A change of global complex frame on $Q$ multiplies $\zeta$ by the normalized square of an inverse complex determinant of a matrix defined on all of $Q$. Its boundary determinant has winding zero by [F1], so $M$ is unchanged. Work now in the actual frame $\Phi$ of [F2]. On the bottom $\zeta=1$. On the top it is $e^{2i\vartheta(s)}(-1)^{m-1}$. The right end cap has squared phase change $m\pi$ as $t$ increases from zero to one; the real factor $S_+^{-1}$ has squared normalized determinant one. The left end cap, traversed downwards, has change $-m\pi$. The top is traversed from positive to negative $s$, with phase change $-2\ell\pi$, while the bottom is constant. Total phase change is thus $-2\ell\pi$, giving $M=-\ell=\operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb R}P$. Near each corner $\rho$ is constant and the horizontal boundary planes are constant in their end frame, so this boundary loop is smooth after rounding the parameter corners and has the computed winding. Altering $\rho$ gives a homotopy fixing its endpoint planes; enlarging $Q$ only inserts constant horizontal portions and identical product caps in the exact end frame. Step 1.1 proves the claimed independence. Finally [F3] gives the stated equality on all the higher Sobolev and weighted realizations; the end gap is $\pi/2$. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 **Finite planar excision.** Let $K$ be the punctured rectangle in the Statement. Squared phase of the subbundle defines a continuous map $\zeta:K\to S^1$ by step 1.1. Uniform continuity gives a positive mesh size such that the image of $K$ intersected with any square of smaller diameter lies in one proper open arc of $S^1$; choose a point of this nonempty intersection and use a rotated arctangent on the arc about its image. Lay down finitely many horizontal and vertical lines at this mesh size. Shift them slightly, if necessary, to avoid tangencies to the finitely many hole circles and their topmost, bottommost, leftmost and rightmost points; each line has only finitely many excluded values. Choose the vertical lines first, then choose the horizontal ones also avoiding the finitely many heights of circle intersections with vertical lines, so no grid vertex lies on a hole circle. Their intersections with the circles and the outer rectangle are finite. Each cell intersected with $K$ is a finite planar region bounded by finitely many straight segments and circle arcs. Its boundary can be traversed as finitely many closed cycles by following those arcs and segments, with the region on the left; this is a finite graph operation, using the two incident edges at an ordinary boundary vertex and keeping separate the cycles of disconnected components. Every such cycle lies in a single square where $\zeta$ has a continuous real argument. Hence its total argument change is zero by telescoping endpoint differences over its segments. Add over the finitely many cells. On internal square segments the opposite traversals have opposite changes; the change is independent of the selected local argument because any two lifts along a segment differ by a constant multiple of $2\pi$. Only the outer boundary and clockwise hole circles remain. Thus outer winding minus the sum of counterclockwise hole windings is zero. Smoothness is unnecessary for this identity. In a smooth model its winding may equivalently be obtained by integrating the derivative of a local argument, since the one-dimensional fundamental theorem of calculus gives the same endpoint differences. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.3 **A source-cover branch.** For the displayed double cover the vectors $v$ on the two source sheets are sent by $dq$ to $(v,\sqrt w\,v)$ and $(v,-\sqrt w\,v)$, in the ordering $z_1=-\sqrt w,z_2=\sqrt w$. Their determinant is $-2\sqrt w\,v^2$. It never vanishes on the circle, and its square is $4w v^4$, whose phase winds once. The sheet ordering interchanges after a full circuit but this changes determinant by a real sign only, leaving the squared phase unchanged. More generally write the roots as $z_j=\omega^j a$, $a^r=w$, $\omega=e^{2\pi i/r}$. The derivative of the elementary symmetric coordinate map has determinant, up to a fixed nonzero sign, $\prod_{j<k}(z_j-z_k)$. To verify that identity, its determinant is an alternating polynomial in the $z_j$ because exchanging variables exchanges columns. It is divisible by each $z_j-z_k$, has their total degree $r(r-1)/2$, and is not the zero polynomial: at distinct roots, a variation leaving all elementary symmetric coefficients fixed would give the zero polynomial $\sum_j\delta z_j\prod_{k\ne j}(t-z_k)$; evaluation at $t=z_j$ forces every $\delta z_j=0$. Thus the determinant is a nonzero constant times that product. Sending each source vector to the same $v$ multiplies determinant by $v^r$. Its square is a nonzero constant times $a^{r(r-1)}=w^{r-1}$. The squared phase consequently winds $r-1$, also for $r=1$, when the empty product is one. Distinct additional sheets separated from this cluster introduce nonzero factors extending across the small disk and hence winding zero; in the stated fixed-product version they simply multiply by a nonzero constant. [step 1.1, algebra]

2.4 **Target ramification and source vector zeros.** On an unbranched parameter sheet with source coordinate $z$, the derivative of $f(z)=z^{r+1}$ sends $v$ to $(r+1)z^r v$. The square of its normalized phase has argument change twice that of $z^r$, namely $4\pi r$ along a positive circle; its winding is $2r$. If instead a scalar source vector $v(z)$ is merely nonzero along the circle and winds $k$, multiplication of an otherwise nonsingular sheet direction by it multiplies the determinant phase by $v/|v|$, and its square contributes $2k$. The nonsingular factor has zero winding when it extends nonvanishing over the disk, by the argument-lift homotopy of step 1.1. A constant extra product basis contributes a constant nonzero determinant factor and does not alter winding. [step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 **Direct sum and actual strip gluing.** In a direct-sum frame the determinant of concatenated bases is the product of the two determinants. Their squared phases multiply, so step 1.1 proves additivity of $M$. For gluing at matching ends, truncate in their exact constant regions and identify the end fibers by the matching product frames. The complex bundles and operators glue smoothly because all coefficients there have the same exact constant expressions; their horizontal totally real boundary data match. The glued bundle has a global complex frame by [F2], and its restriction is a global frame for each of the two cut rectangles. The squared phase along the inserted shared cap is identical for the two rectangles and traversed in opposite directions. All its argument changes cancel. The remaining changes are precisely those on the boundary loop of the glued problem, so its $M$ is the sum of the two separate $M$ values. Frame invariance from step 2.1 allows the restricted global frame in this computation instead of the individual normal-form frames. Applying step 2.1 to all three actual operators proves index additivity. No assertion about gluing nonlinear solutions has been used. [F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 **A transverse graph node, with its oriented sign.** In the graph model the two target points are $Az,Bz$. The chosen source directions map to $v,v$ respectively. In symmetric target coordinates the two columns are therefore $(v,(Bz)v)$ and $(v,(Az)v)$, and their determinant is $v^2(A-B)z$. The real invertible map $A-B$ sends a positive circle to an ellipse traversed with degree $\operatorname{sign}\det_{\mathbb R}(A-B)$. One direct verification uses real Gram–Schmidt to deform a positive determinant matrix through invertibles to a positive diagonal matrix and then to the identity; a negative determinant matrix is reduced in the same way to a reflection. Identity and reflection have degrees $+1$ and $-1$. Squaring the determinant phase gives twice that degree. For the oriented intersection sign, put an oriented basis for the first graph followed by one for the second into the ambient base-then-target orientation. The resulting real matrix has block form $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&I\\A&B\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, whose determinant is $\det(B-A)=\det(A-B)$ since the blocks have dimension two. This proves precisely the claimed sign. Along the circle the columns are complex independent because $(A-B)z\ne0$, so the image plane is totally real and the calculation applies. Together with the preceding excision and branch calculations this completes all the claimed excision and local model assertions. No global cover or corner classification has entered this proof. [step 1.1, step 2.2, step 2.3, step 2.4, algebra] ∎
