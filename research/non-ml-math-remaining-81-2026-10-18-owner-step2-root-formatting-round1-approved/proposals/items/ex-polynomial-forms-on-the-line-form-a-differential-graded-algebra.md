## Example

Let $S=\mathbb Z[t,\epsilon]=(\mathbb Z[t])[\epsilon]$ and
$A=S/(\epsilon^2)$, where $(\epsilon^2)$ is the ideal generated by
$\epsilon^2$. Write $\epsilon$ also for its class in $A$. Each class has a
unique representative $f(t)+g(t)\epsilon$, so
$A=\mathbb Z[t]\oplus\mathbb Z[t]\epsilon$, with $|t|=0$, $|\epsilon|=1$,
$A^0=\mathbb Z[t]$, $A^1=\mathbb Z[t]\epsilon$, and $A^n=0$ for
$n\notin\{0,1\}$. Multiplication is induced by polynomial multiplication;
equivalently, $\epsilon^2=0$ and $t\epsilon=\epsilon t$. The unit is the class
of $1$ in degree zero. Define the map $d:A\to A$ by
$$d(f(t)+g(t)\epsilon)=f'(t)\epsilon,$$
where the formal derivative of $f(t)=\sum_{i=0}^n a_i t^i$ is
$f'(t)=\sum_{i=1}^n i a_i t^{i-1}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The graded algebra and map above. Verify that they form a cohomological DGA.

[F1] [[thm-int-comm-ring]]: The standard addition and multiplication on $\mathbb Z$ make it a commutative ring with multiplicative identity.

[F2] [[def-ring]] and [[def-commutative-ring]]: A commutative ring has an abelian additive group, an associative unital multiplication, both distributive laws, and commutative multiplication.

[F3] [[thm-polynomial-ring-is-a-commutative-ring]]: If $R$ is a commutative ring, then the coefficientwise and convolution operations make $R[x]$ a commutative ring.

[F4] [[thm-generated-ideal-description-in-a-commutative-ring]]: In a commutative ring, $(a)$ consists of the multiples of $a$.

[F5] [[thm-quotient-ring-laws]]: For a ring $R$ and a two-sided ideal $I$, $R/I$ is a ring with identity.

[F6] [[def-differential-graded-algebra]]: For homogeneous $a\in A^p$ and $b\in A^q$, require the signed Leibniz rule
$$d^{p+q}(ab)=d^p(a)b+(-1)^p a d^q(b).$$

[F7] [[def-differential-graded-algebra]]: The defining cochain differential satisfies $d^{n+1}d^n=0$.

## Verification

1.1 By [F1] and [F2], $\mathbb Z$ with its standard operations is a commutative ring with unit, so it is an admissible base for [F6]. [F1, F2]

2.1 Applying [F3] first to $\mathbb Z$ and then to $\mathbb Z[t]$ shows that $S=(\mathbb Z[t])[\epsilon]$ is a commutative ring. By [F4], $(\epsilon^2)=S\epsilon^2$ is a two-sided ideal; [F5] therefore gives the quotient ring $A$ with its associative multiplication and unit. For cosets, $[s][t]=[st]=[ts]=[t][s]$, so $A$ is commutative. The constant classes define a unital ring homomorphism $\mathbb Z\to A$; its induced scalar action is central, and associativity and distributivity make multiplication $\mathbb Z$-bilinear. [F3, F4, F5, step 1.1]

3.1 Every polynomial in $\epsilon$ with coefficients in $\mathbb Z[t]$ has a unique form $f_0(t)+f_1(t)\epsilon+\epsilon^2 h(t,\epsilon)$. By [F4], the last term is in $(\epsilon^2)$, and a polynomial of degree at most one in $\epsilon$ lies in $(\epsilon^2)$ only when both its coefficients vanish. Thus every quotient class has a unique representative $f+g\epsilon$, giving the stated direct sum of $\mathbb Z$-modules. The quotient multiplication is $(f+g\epsilon)(h+k\epsilon)=fh+(fk+gh)\epsilon$; this preserves the grading and gives $\epsilon^2=0$ and $t\epsilon=\epsilon t$. [F3, F4, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The unique expression in step 3.1 makes the formula for $d$ well defined. Coefficientwise differentiation is additive and commutes with multiplication by each integer, so $d$ is $\mathbb Z$-linear; it sends $A^0$ to $A^1$ and $A^1$ to $A^2=0$, hence has degree $+1$. [given, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 For every $f+g\epsilon$, $d^2(f+g\epsilon)=d(f'\epsilon)=0$, because $d$ vanishes on $A^1$. Thus [F7] holds. [F7, step 4.1, algebra]

5.2 If $f=\sum_i a_i t^i$ and $h=\sum_j b_j t^j$ have degree zero, the finite coefficient sums give $(fh)'=\sum_{i+j\ge1}(i+j)a_i b_jt^{i+j-1}=f'h+fh'$. Hence $d(fh)=d(f)h+f\,d(h)$. [given, F6, algebra, step 4.1]

5.3 If $a=f$ and $b=g\epsilon$ have degrees zero and one, both $d(ab)$ and $d(a)b+a\,d(b)$ vanish because $d(g\epsilon)=0$ and $(f'\epsilon)(g\epsilon)=0$. If $a=g\epsilon$ and $b=f$, then both sides of $d(ab)=d(a)b-a\,d(b)$ vanish because $d(g\epsilon)=0$ and $(g\epsilon)(f'\epsilon)=0$. If both have degree one, their product and both terms on the right vanish. These are all remaining homogeneous degree pairs, so [F6] holds. [F6, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Steps 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1 establish the graded unital associative $\mathbb Z$-algebra structure, and steps 4.1, step 5.1, step 5.2, step 5.3 establish the degree-one, square-zero differential and signed Leibniz rule. Thus all conditions of [F6] hold, so $A$ is a cohomological DGA. [F6, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 5.2, step 5.3] ∎
