## 2. Authoring notes for the step-5 author (no scaffold anchor)

### 2.1 The two proof routes that are load-bearing and easy to get wrong

**`lem-order-of-element-exists`.** Do NOT argue "an injection $\mathbb{N} \to G$
contradicts finiteness" — that needs "a subset of a finite set is finite", which
the library does not have. Use `lem-pigeonhole` **claim 1** directly: $G$ finite
means $G \approx m$ for some $m \in \mathbb{N}$ (`def-countable`); the map
$\sigma(m) \to G$, $k \mapsto g^k$, composed with a bijection $G \to m$, is a map
$\sigma(m) \to m$, hence not injective by claim 1; so $g^i = g^j$ for some
$i < j \le m$; `lem-group-cancellation` gives $g^{j-i} = e$, and $j - i \ge 1$ is
a natural by `def-nat-order` ($i < j$ means $i + k = j$ for some $k$, and
$k \ne 0$).

**`def-order-in-a-group`, the $|G|$ half.** `lem-pigeonhole` **claim 3** ("if
$n \approx m$ with $n,m \in \mathbb{N}$ then $n = m$") is exactly what makes "the
number of elements" well defined. Cite it; do not assert it.

### 2.2 `deps` vs `justified_by` on the two definitions with obligations

`lem-order-of-element-exists` and `thm-well-ordering-principle` are stated BEFORE
`def-order-in-a-group` and do not mention $\operatorname{ord}$, so they are
genuine prerequisites and go in **`deps`**. Putting them in `justified_by` will
fail `depcheck`, which enforces that a `justified_by` target transitively depends
on the item. The same holds for `lem-identity-unique` under
`def-semigroup-and-monoid` and `lem-inverse-unique` under `def-group`.
`lem-order-characterisation` is a consequence, not a justification: `deps`.

### 2.3 The one-sided/two-sided discipline (the trap the brief named)

`def-group` is written as "a monoid in which every element is invertible", and
that is fine ONLY because `lem-inverse-unique` is proved first and is stated in
the sharp form: in a monoid, if $h g = e$ and $g h' = e$ then $h = h'$. Prove it
before `def-group`, cite it from `def-group`, and never write "the inverse"
before it. `lem-identity-unique` does the same job one level down, and is what
licenses "the identity" inside `def-semigroup-and-monoid`.

### 2.4 Index and first-value checks specific to this page pair

- `def-group-power`: $g^0 = e$, by definition, for **every** $g$ including
  $g = e$. $\mathbb{N}$ contains $0$; the recursion starts there.
- `def-monoid-finite-product`: the empty product ($n = 0$) is the identity. State
  it; it is what makes generalised associativity's induction start.
- `ex-integers-mod-n-additive-group`: **$n \ge 1$**. At $n = 0$ the relation is
  equality, $\mathbb{Z}/0 \cong \mathbb{Z}$, and "of order $n$" is false. The
  title and the Statement both carry the hypothesis.
- `lem-symmetric-group-is-a-group`: the non-abelian clause needs $X$ to have
  **three distinct elements**, not "at least three" in a cardinality sense —
  phrase it with three named elements so no finiteness machinery is invoked.
- `lem-order-characterisation`: the distinctness claim is about
  $g^0, \dots, g^{n-1}$, i.e. $n$ powers starting at exponent $0$.

### 2.5 Two different $\iota$'s are already in the library

`lem-nat-embeds-int` writes $\iota(n) = [(n,0)]$ for $\mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{Z}$;
`def-canonical-natural` writes $\iota(n) = n \cdot 1_F$ for $\mathbb{N} \to F$.
Page 20 uses the first and never the second. Where the page writes $\iota$, say
which one. Note that the exponent in $g^n$ is an integer INDEX, not an element of
the group, so no canonical-natural machinery is involved at all — but the case
split defining $g^n$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ does run through
`lem-nat-embeds-int` and `lem-nat-trichotomy`, and that is a real proof
obligation, not bookkeeping.
