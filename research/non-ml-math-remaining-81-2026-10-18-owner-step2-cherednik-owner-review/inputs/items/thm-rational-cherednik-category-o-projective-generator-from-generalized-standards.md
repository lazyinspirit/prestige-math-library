## Statement

For every finite complex reflection group and every parameter, rational Cherednik category $\mathcal O_c$ has a finite projective generator which is a direct summand of a generalized standard $G_{n,c}=H_{1,c}\otimes_{S(\mathfrak h)\rtimes\mathbb CW}(\mathbb CW\otimes S(\mathfrak h)/S(\mathfrak h)_{>n})$ for sufficiently large $n$. It is equivalent to finite-dimensional modules over a finite-dimensional algebra. Every indecomposable projective cover $P(E)$ of the simple head $L(E)$ has a standard filtration and an exact sequence $0\to K(E)\to P(E)\to\Delta(E)\to0$, where the standard labels $F$ in $K(E)$ satisfy $a_E-a_F\in\mathbb Z_{>0}$. The standard labels give a finite highest-weight structure for $E<F$ when $a_E-a_F\in\mathbb Z_{>0}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite complex reflection group $W$ on $\mathfrak h$ and an arbitrary conjugacy-invariant parameter $c$, with the current minus-sign Cherednik normalization.

[F1] PBW and standards give the triangular decomposition and induced modules; the Euler/finite-length proof gives the commutators, generalized eigenspaces, and unique simple heads for standards indexed by irreducible $W$-modules ([[thm-rational-cherednik-pbw-theorem]], [[def-rational-cherednik-standard-module]], [[lem-rational-cherednik-category-o-has-finite-length]]).

[F2] Every finite-dimensional module over a finite-dimensional algebra has a projective cover, unique up to isomorphism ([[thm-projective-covers-exist-and-are-unique-for-finite-dimensional-algebras]]). This is used only after the finite-algebra model is constructed.

## Proof

1.1 Write $H=H_{1,c}$, $X=S(\mathfrak h^*)$, $Y=S(\mathfrak h)$, and $B=Y\rtimes\mathbb CW$. Let $a_E$ be the scalar of $z=\dim(\mathfrak h)/2-\sum_s c_s s$ on $E$, and put $\Sigma=\{a_E:E\in\operatorname{Irr}W\}$. The Euler element is $\mathrm{eu}=\sum_i x_i y_i+z$. The Euler and finite-length argument in [F1] gives locally finite Euler action, finite generalized eigenspaces, and spectra contained in $\Sigma+\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$: the spectral assertion holds on standards and their simple quotients, then on every finite composition series. Fixed generalized eigenspaces are exact, since one can decompose a preimage into its finitely many generalized eigencomponents and retain the desired component. Put $b=\min_E\operatorname{Re}(a_E)$ and choose $n$ such that $\operatorname{Re}(\beta)-(n+1)<b$ for every $\beta\in\Sigma$. Every degree-$(n+1)$ monomial in $Y$ sends $M[\beta]$ to $M[\beta-(n+1)]=0$, for every object $M$. Higher monomials also vanish by factoring them. Thus $Y_{>n}M[\beta]=0$ uniformly over all objects and all $\beta\in\Sigma$. [F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 Let $E_n=\mathbb CW\otimes(Y/Y_{>n})$, with diagonal $W$-action and $Y$ multiplying the second factor, and put $G_n=H\otimes_B E_n$. Filtering the truncated $Y$-factor by descending degree gives a finite standard filtration: PBW makes induction exact, and each layer $S^d(\mathfrak h)\otimes\mathbb CW$ is a finite semisimple $W$-module, since averaging a projection over $W$ splits each submodule. Hence $G_n\in\mathcal O_c$. PBW identifies its vector space with $X\otimes\mathbb CW\otimes(Y/Y_{>n})$. Give $x$ degree $1$, $y$ degree $-1$, and $W$ degree $0$. Each total-degree space is finite-dimensional because the $Y$-degree is at most $n$. The operator $Z=\mathrm{eu}-\mathrm{degree}$ commutes with every homogeneous generator, since both commutators equal its degree times that generator. It preserves total degree. The finite degree-zero space contains the finite generating set $\mathbb CW\otimes1$, so $Z$ is locally finite with a finite set of generalized eigenvalues on all of $G_n$. Therefore the generalized $Z$-eigenspaces $G_n^\beta$ are genuine $H$-direct summands. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Induction adjunction is explicit: an $H$-map $G_n\to M$ is determined by $m=f(1\otimes1\otimes1)$, and this may be any vector killed by $Y_{>n}$. The value on $w\otimes p(y)$ is $p(y)wm$. This is $Y$-linear, and its $W$-linearity follows from the diagonal action; equivalently, $w\otimes p$ corresponds to $pw$ in the regular $B$-quotient. On the degree-zero generator, evaluation intertwines precomposition by $Z$ with $\mathrm{eu}_M$, because $f(Zv)=\mathrm{eu}_Mf(v)$. Consequently $\operatorname{Hom}_H(G_n^\beta,M)=M[\beta]\cap\operatorname{Ann}_M(Y_{>n})$. For $\beta\in\Sigma$, step 1.1 makes this exactly $M[\beta]$. Thus $P_\beta=G_n^\beta$ represents an exact functor and is projective. The finite direct sum $P_\Sigma=\bigoplus_{\beta\in\Sigma}P_\beta$ detects every nonzero object: a nonzero vector has a finite $Y$-orbit, whose commuting nilpotent operators have a nonzero common kernel; that singular space contains an irreducible $W$-module, on which $\mathrm{eu}$ acts by one of the scalars in $\Sigma$. [F1, F2, step 2.1, algebra, step 1.1]

4.1 Hom spaces are finite-dimensional: choose finitely many generators of the source decomposed into generalized Euler eigenvectors; their images lie in finitely many finite-dimensional target eigenspaces. The evaluation map $P_\Sigma\otimes\operatorname{Hom}(P_\Sigma,M)\to M$ is therefore a map from a finite direct sum. If its cokernel were nonzero, a nonzero map from $P_\Sigma$ to that cokernel would lift by projectivity and contradict the definition of the evaluation image. Thus evaluation is surjective. Its kernel belongs to $\mathcal O_c$, so each object has a presentation $P_\Sigma^b\to P_\Sigma^a\to M\to0$. Put $\Gamma=\operatorname{End}(P_\Sigma)^{\mathrm{op}}$, a finite-dimensional algebra. Applying $\operatorname{Hom}(P_\Sigma,-)$ to these presentations proves equivalence with finite-dimensional $\Gamma$-modules: a presentation by finite free $\Gamma$-modules lifts to a map between finite sums of $P_\Sigma$, and its cokernel is the inverse construction; the same presentations identify morphisms. This argument requires only finite presentations, not cocompleteness of $\mathcal O_c$. [F1, F2, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 The standard filtration of $G_n$ in step 2.1 is graded. A section $\Delta(F)$ arising from $Y$-degree $d$ has its generating copy of $F$ in total degree $-d$. On that entire section, $Z$ acts by the scalar $a_F+d$, since $\mathrm{eu}$ increases by one with each multiplication by $x$ and the grading does the same. Applying the exact generalized $Z$-projection to the filtration keeps a whole standard section if $a_F+d=\beta$ and kills it otherwise. Hence $P_\beta$ and $P_\Sigma$ have finite standard flags. Since the $\beta$ are distinct, their sum is itself a direct summand of the single $G_n$. [F1, F2, step 4.1, algebra, step 2.1]

6.1 If $\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathcal O}(\Delta(E),\Delta(F))\ne0$, then $a_E-a_F\in\mathbb Z_{>0}$. Indeed, in an extension lift the generating $E$ into its generalized Euler space of eigenvalue $a_E$ and split the resulting $W$-surjection by averaging. Each $y$ sends this lift into $\Delta(F)[a_E-1]$. Unless $a_E-a_F$ is a positive integer, that space is zero. The lift is then singular and induction adjunction gives a splitting from $\Delta(E)$. This includes the case $a_E=a_F$. Also $\operatorname{End}(\Delta(E))=\mathbb C$, because an endomorphism is determined on the irreducible generating copy of $E$. A map $\Delta(E)\to\Delta(F)$ vanishes when $\operatorname{Re}(a_E)<\operatorname{Re}(a_F)$, or when those real parts agree and $E\ne F$: the target either has no space of weight $a_E$ or has only its degree-zero $F$ there. [F1, F2, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 Direct summands of standard-filtered objects are standard-filtered. To prove this, sort a standard flag by increasing $\operatorname{Re}(a)$ from bottom to top. An adjacent inversion splits by step 6.1, allowing its two factors to be swapped, so finitely many swaps suffice. Extensions among factors with equal real part split too. The bottom group of minimal real part is therefore a direct sum of standards. It is canonical: it is the image of $\bigoplus_E\Delta(E)\otimes\operatorname{Hom}(\Delta(E),M)\to M$, with $E$ ranging over those minimal labels. Every such map factors through this bottom, because its map to the larger-real-part quotient vanishes by step 6.1 and induction along that quotient's flag. Evaluation into the bottom is injective, since distinct bottom labels have zero Hom and each has scalar endomorphisms. Every endomorphism of $M$ preserves this submodule and acts there by matrices on its multiplicity spaces. Thus an idempotent's image in the bottom is again a sum of standards. Its induced idempotent on the quotient has standard-filtered image by induction on the number of distinct real parts. Combining the two images gives the required flag of the original idempotent image. [F1, F2, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 The finite-algebra model from step 4.1 has projective covers by [F2]. Transporting them back gives $P(E)\twoheadrightarrow L(E)$ with simple head $L(E)$ once. Each is a summand of a finite sum of $P_\Sigma$, hence is standard-filtered by step 7.1. Sort its flag as there. Its top real-part group is a quotient which is a direct sum of standards, so its simple heads are quotients of $P(E)$. The simple head condition forces this group to be exactly one $\Delta(E)$. Thus $0\to K(E)\to P(E)\to\Delta(E)\to0$, where all standard labels $F$ in $K(E)$ have $\operatorname{Re}(a_F)<\operatorname{Re}(a_E)$. Euler spectra split every object into congruence classes modulo $\mathbb Z$, because generators shift Euler eigenvalues by integers. The indecomposable $P(E)$ lies in its head's class, so every such $F$ also has $a_E-a_F\in\mathbb Z$ and hence $a_E-a_F\in\mathbb Z_{>0}$. Other composition factors of $\Delta(E)$ have $a_F-a_E\in\mathbb Z_{>0}$, since a proper submodule misses degree zero. These are precisely the standard-support and projective-kernel conditions for the stated finite highest-weight order. [F1, F2, step 7.1, algebra, step 4.1]

9.1 The simple labels are distinct and have scalar endomorphisms. If $L(E)\cong L(F)$, the two lower Euler bounds force both $a_E-a_F$ and $a_F-a_E$ to be nonnegative integers, so they are zero; the common lowest $W$-space then identifies $E=F$. An endomorphism of $L(E)$ is determined on its irreducible generating lowest space and is scalar. Every nonzero map from a projective cover $P(E)$ to a simple has its unique maximal kernel, so $\operatorname{Hom}(P(E),L(F))=\delta_{EF}\mathbb C$. Exactness of $\operatorname{Hom}(P(E),-)$ along composition series gives $\dim\operatorname{Hom}(P(E),M)=[M:L(E)]$. This proves the projective-generator and highest-weight assertions with the multiplicity formula used below. [F1, F2, step 8.1, algebra] ∎
