## Two-end exhaustion without conformal uniformization

Let A be a plane domain whose complement in the sphere has exactly two connected components K₀,K₁. They are disjoint compact continua. Each is nonseparating on the sphere: A, being connected, lies in one component of the open complement of K₀. Any other component U is disjoint from A, hence U⊂K₁. Since K₁ is closed, closure(U)⊂K₁. But every boundary point of a component U of the open complement of K₀ belongs to K₀: a point of that open complement has a connected small ball contained there and cannot be a boundary point of a distinct component. The nonempty proper component U has nonempty boundary on the connected sphere. This gives a point in K₀∩K₁, a contradiction. The same argument applies with the indices exchanged.

The needed constructive topology lemma is that a nonseparating compact planar continuum K has nested closed Jordan neighborhoods N_j with K⊂int N_j and intersection N_j=K. A finite-disk construction avoids grid-contact ambiguity. Cover K by finitely many small open disks with centers in K. Their union is connected: if it had two disjoint open components, each would meet K (every disk center is in K), contradicting connectedness of the covered K. Compactness gives a positive cover margin. Perturb the finitely many radii within that margin so boundary circles have no tangencies and no triple intersections; at each choice only finitely many radii are forbidden. The resulting finite union is still connected, still covers K, and remains in an arbitrarily small metric neighborhood of K.

Its boundary consists of finitely many circular arcs. At a transverse two-circle crossing an exposed boundary has exactly two incident arcs; tangencies and triple vertices were excluded. Thus every boundary component is a finite embedded degree-two graph, hence a simple cycle/Jordan curve (a circle with no vertices is already one). The connected union lies on one side of each boundary cycle. There is exactly one outer cycle: two disjoint nonnested outer cycles would place the connected union in two distinct bounded sides, while a nested cycle inside the outer one bounds a hole rather than another outer side. Filling all bounded holes therefore gives the closed Jordan disk bounded by that outer cycle. To obtain nesting choose every next disk union inside the previous Jordan neighborhood's interior. Filling its holes cannot leave the previous disk, since every point outside the previous disk is joined to infinity in its exterior without meeting the next disk union. Hence the filled neighborhoods remain nested.

Why they shrink to K: for x outside K, the connected open complement is polygonally connected, so a path from x to infinity avoids K. Its compact relevant portion has positive distance from K. Small enough disk unions miss the path, and x lies on their exterior side, not in a filled hole. Thus x is omitted from sufficiently small filled neighborhoods. No smooth uniformization or unproved simple outer grid boundary is used.

Normalize each sphere continuum into a finite planar chart avoiding a point in the other one. Apply the neighborhood lemma to both and transport back. Choose their sufficiently small neighborhoods disjoint and nested. The regions between their boundary Jordan curves are compact annular cores C_j inside A, nested with interiors covering A. The complement A\C_j has exactly two connected collar components. To check connectedness of a Jordan neighborhood minus its inner continuum, join any point through the complement of the continuum to a point outside the neighborhood; stop at its first boundary hit, then connect all such hits along a narrow inner collar of the Jordan boundary, disjoint from K. Each initial path remains in the neighborhood until its first hit. This gives polygonal connectedness of the punctured neighborhood. The sphere version applies to the exterior collar as well.

Thus every sufficiently far tail of a proper (0,1) path lies in one of the two collars for each C_j. Nesting labels the two ends consistently. A homeomorphism A→A' is proper as a map of these locally compact spaces and transports a compact exhaustion to a compact exhaustion; it induces a bijection of the two ends and maps the end-path families bijectively. The identification with complement-component tails is therefore topological, not a claimed extension of f to every physical boundary point.

They are focused elementary topology, not content already supplied by a round-annulus theorem. No blanket citation exception is applied.
