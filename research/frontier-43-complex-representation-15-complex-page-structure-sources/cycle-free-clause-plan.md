## Independent geometric ACL and sharp derivative route

The core's two quadrilateral bounds suffice for inverse regularity through geometric ACL; circular/annular bounds are unnecessary for this step. Here is the exact ACL estimate, avoiding an unsupported whole-source application. Fix a compact rectangle [0,1]×[a,b] in the domain and the finite increasing image-area function A(y)=area(f((0,1)×(a,y))). At almost every y its derivative is finite. For finitely many disjoint horizontal intervals I_j=(u_j,v_j), put l_j=v_j−u_j and d_j=|f(v_j,y)−f(u_j,y)|. Consider the strips R_j=I_j×(y,y+t). Uniform continuity on the fixed compact rectangle ensures that the minimum Euclidean length of a path in f(R_j) connecting the two image vertical sides is at least d_j−ε for sufficiently small t. Constant density1 and the geometric **lower** bound give

`(d_j−ε)_+² <= K*(l_j/t)*area(f(R_j))`.

Cauchy–Schwarz and disjointness of the strip images yield

`(sum_j(d_j−ε)_+)² <= K*(sum_j l_j)*(A(y+t)−A(y))/t`.

Let t↓0 and ε↓0 (the interval family is finite): `sum_j d_j <= sqrt(K*A'(y)*sum_j l_j)`. This is exactly the usual absolute-continuity condition for f restricted to the horizontal line, including arbitrary finite disjoint interval families. Repeat vertically, and use a countable interior rectangle cover. No null-set transport or inverse regularity is used.

The differentiability lemma then applies. At a differentiability point with invertible derivative, use source squares aligned with its singular vectors; rescaled boundary maps converge uniformly to the corresponding nonsingular linear rectangle. The earlier proved quadrilateral-modulus continuity and the geometric bounds force its aspect ratio, hence derivative singular-value ratio, to be at most K. Rank one is ruled out separately, without invoking continuity at a degenerate quadrilateral: align a square with the nonzero singular direction. Its rescaled image lies in an o(1)-width bounded neighborhood of a segment, hence has area o(1), while every joining path between the sides perpendicular to that direction has length bounded below by a positive constant. Constant density1 forces that image modulus to zero, contradicting its geometric lower bound1/K. Rank zero satisfies the Beltrami inequality. This proves the sharp differential bound a.e. Lower area then gives L2 energy; ACL identifies the weak derivatives. Thus the geometric inverse is analytically K-QC independently of circular/quasisymmetry and inverse-null.

The image-area function estimate must use open strips so there is no hidden area-zero image-boundary assumption: the images of the finitely many open strips are disjoint and lie in the open full strip. Their endpoints' uniform approximation uses continuity on the closure, while their area comparison is on interiors.
