## Differentiability of planar homeomorphisms with finite partials almost everywhere

Let `f:U→V` be a homeomorphism of planar domains and suppose its two finite classical partial derivatives exist a.e. Restrict to compact rectangles inside U. For any positive tolerance, Egorov applied to directional difference quotients and Lusin applied to the derivative functions provide a compact set E with complement as small in area as desired, on which the partial derivative functions are continuous and the directional difference quotients converge uniformly. To apply Egorov with the real step parameter, use the measurable functions

`G_n(p)=sup_{0<|h|<1/n} max(|(f(p+h)-f(p))/h-f_x(p)|, |(f(p+ih)-f(p))/h-f_y(p)|)`.

The supremum can be restricted to rational nonzero h by continuity of f in h, hence is measurable; it tends to zero pointwise where partials exist. Ensure points and increments stay in a fixed interior rectangle. Thus no countable-sequence quotient limit is silently substituted for the full derivative limit.

By one-dimensional Lebesgue differentiation and Fubini, at almost every p in E, both coordinate slices of E have density1 at p. Translate such a p to0. Write `A(w)=f(0)+a*x+b*y`, with `a=f_x(0), b=f_y(0)`. Uniform quotients on E and continuity of derivatives on E imply, for any ε>0 and sufficiently small w=u+iv, that

`|f(w)-A(w)|<=3ε|w|`

whenever u belongs to E on the real axis. Indeed split the increment through u: the vertical difference quotient at u approximates f_y(u), f_y(u) approximates b, and the horizontal quotient at0 approximates a. The same conclusion holds when iv belongs to E on the imaginary axis, by splitting through iv.

For a fixed arbitrary nonzero z=x+iy near0, put r=|z|. Density1 on the real slice implies its missing length in [-2r,2r] is o(r). Consequently, for sufficiently small r there are real-slice points u_- and u_+ in `(x-εr,x)` and `(x,x+εr)` respectively; each interval has length εr and cannot be entirely missing. Likewise select imaginary-slice coordinates v_- in `(y-εr,y)` and v_+ in `(y,y+εr)`. These four coordinates bound a rectangle R containing z, with diameter at most `2sqrt(2)εr`. Every boundary point w lies on a vertical side based at real-slice E or a horizontal side based at imaginary-slice E. The preceding estimate therefore bounds `|f(w)-A(w)|` on all of ∂R.

For any fixed c, `w↦|f(w)-c|` attains its maximum over the compact closure of R on its boundary: an interior positive maximum would contradict openness of f, since an open neighborhood of f(w) contains points farther from c; the zero-maximum case is impossible on a nonempty open rectangle for an injective map. Apply this with the **constant** `c=A(z)`. Then

`|f(z)-A(z)|<=max_{w∈∂R}|f(w)-A(z)|<=3ε(1+sqrt(2)ε)r+sqrt(2)ε(|a|+|b|)r`.

Let ε tend to0. This proves total differentiability at almost every point of E. Taking compact sets E whose excluded area tends to0 proves differentiability a.e. in the rectangle, then in U by a countable rectangle cover. The maximum is applied to f minus A(z), never f minus a variable affine map. This resolves the apparent maximum-principle objection to Bishop's pp94–95 proof. It does not by itself prove the reverse area equality, inverse absolute continuity, or modulus distortion.

## Finite-length continuous-analytic removability without the false disjoint-square input

Let E be compact in the interior of a large square S, with finite Euclidean H¹(E), and let g be continuous on S and holomorphic on S\E. Fix z in S\E and d=dist(z,E)>0. For arbitrarily small η, the Hausdorff definition gives a countable cover of E by sets of diameter below η and sum of diameters bounded by H¹(E)+1. Replace each nonempty set meeting E by an open axis-parallel square containing it, of side at most three times its diameter plus an arbitrarily small positive summable error. The total side sum is bounded independently of η. Compactness selects a finite subcover. Every selected square lies within O(η) of E, and hence inside S and away from z for sufficiently small η. They can overlap.

Let O be their union. Its closure lies in S, E lies in O, and ∂O misses E. A finite union of squares has polygonal boundary. Partition O by assigning each point to the first square containing it, successively subtracting earlier squares. This creates finitely many polygonal cells, each contained in an original square of diameter O(η), with disjoint interiors. Edges of the cell partition are subsegments of the original square edges. Each original edge is counted at most twice as a cell edge (once on each side); coincident edges may be consolidated before counting. Thus total cell perimeter is at most twice the sum of original square perimeters, uniformly bounded. Cells may have holes or split into multiple polygonal components; each is treated with its oriented full boundary, and the same edge accounting applies. Degenerate zero-area pieces can be omitted.

Apply Cauchy's theorem in S\(closure(O)∪small-disc-about-z), using a finite triangulation and the circle Cauchy formula. This gives

`2πi*g(z)=∫∂S g(ζ)/(ζ-z)dζ − ∫∂O g(ζ)/(ζ-z)dζ`.

Internal partition edges cancel, so the last integral is the sum of the oriented integrals over all small cells. Put `F(ζ)=g(ζ)/(ζ-z)`, which is continuous near closure(O). On each cell choose a point a. The integral of the constant F(a) over its full closed polygonal boundary is zero. Uniform continuity therefore bounds that cell's integral by its perimeter times `omega_F(Cη)`. Summing gives `|∫∂O F|<=C_0*omega_F(Cη)→0`. It follows that g(z) equals the outer Cauchy integral for every z off E. This integral is holomorphic throughout S's interior. E has empty interior because finite H¹ implies planar area zero (use diameter-square cover cost <=Cη times the bounded sum of diameters), so continuity extends the equality across E. Hence g is holomorphic across E.

This proves the local finite-length result with an overlapping Hausdorff cover; individual square boundaries may cross E, which causes no problem because F is continuous there and no Cauchy theorem is applied inside those cells. Cauchy's theorem is applied only outside the UNION, whose boundary misses E. For the sphere Statement, apply the existing chordal-to-Euclidean comparison after a Möbius normalization whose pole avoids E; compactness gives boundedness and Liouville gives constancy.

The original strengthened cover was false for a connected segment: disjoint square interiors with each square boundary avoiding the segment force the segment into one square. That failed condition is entirely unnecessary in this replacement.

## Residual area equality: repair signed multiplicity, then separate inverse regularity

Lyubich's printed p182 argument for arbitrary smooth approximants incorrectly equates the signed integral of the Jacobian to unsigned fibre cardinality. Smooth approximants need not preserve orientation. A correction can retain the intended conclusion.

First prove the lower area bound `∫_E J_f<=area(f(E))` by differentiability and differentiation of the positive image-area measure; singular derivatives give zero density and the nonsingular case follows by trapping images of small balls between ellipses inflated/deflated by o(r). For a homeomorphism with W¹,² derivatives and bounded distortion, this is independent of geometric/analytic QC equivalence. Its Beltrami inequality makes J_f nonnegative.

On an interior square D, mollification gives smooth f_n uniformly converging to f on closure(D) and in W¹,² on a neighborhood; therefore J_n→J_f in L¹(D), and the integral of the negative part of J_n tends to zero. Fix any compact target subset T of f(D). For sufficiently large n the straight homotopy between the boundary values of f_n and f avoids every point of T. The local degree of f on D is1 at each point of f(D), so f_n has degree1 on T. Sard and inverse branches imply that almost every y in T has at least one preimage with positive determinant. Apply nonnegative C¹ change of variables on a countable disjoint measurable partition of the open set {J_n>0} into inverse-branch pieces (a countable rational-basis inverse-branch cover suffices). Then `∫_D (J_n)_+>=area(T)`. Consequently

`∫_D J_n >= area(T)-∫_D(J_n)_-`.

Pass to the limit and exhaust f(D) by compact subsets to get `∫_D J_f>=area(f(D))`. Combined with the opposite bound this is equality on all relatively compact squares. Uniqueness of locally finite Borel measures then gives equality for all relatively compact Borel E and a compact exhaustion gives the general local formula. This proves f's forward null-set property. It does **not** prove the inverse-null property by itself.

The existing inverse proof acquires local inverse Sobolev regularity from inverse quasisymmetry before it differentiates the inverse. Once that independently gives g=f⁻¹ analytic QC with some finite local bound, apply this same forward area formula to g: this supplies its forward null-set property, i.e. f's inverse-null property. One can then transport differentiability and differentiate g∘f=id to obtain nonvanishing derivatives a.e. and the sharp same-K inverse coefficient. No need to assume inverse-null in advance.

There is a clause-order dependency issue: the current full analytic-distortion lemma combines modulus bounds and inverse-null in one supplier, while circular dilatation uses its modulus bounds, and the inverse lemma uses its inverse-null. To preserve the full original claim without a cycle, establish an earlier **modulus-only core** (using only the lower area inequality), then circular/quasisymmetry regularity and a preliminary inverse regularity argument, then area equality for each map and the sharp inverse conclusion. Keep the current full distortion lemma as a later wrapper that combines these proved clauses; direct consumers needing only modulus bounds should depend on the core. Do not mechanically add an inverse dependency to the early lemma.

Precise published supplier limitation: `thm-regular-value-formula-for-compact-support-degree` concerns proper smooth maps of boundaryless manifolds. It is not directly the local degree theorem for f_n on a square. A local winding/degree argument on the square (with excision and boundary homotopy) must be supplied explicitly or mapped to an exact appropriate local-homology supplier. This is a small topology bridge, not license to pretend the proper-map theorem has different hypotheses.

## Jordan boundary extension without full prime-end theory

Here is a focused alternative to [F10]'s whole prime-end package. Let f:D→Ω be a conformal bijection onto a bounded Jordan domain, with base point f(0). For any ξ on the unit circle, the half-circle/circular-crosscut length–area estimate already in Step7 selects radii ρ_j↓0 such that the image arcs C_j=f({|z−ξ|=ρ_j}∩D) have lengths tending to zero. Each finite-length image arc has two endpoint limits. These endpoints belong to Γ=∂Ω: an interior endpoint would, under the continuous inverse, make a sequence tending to ∂D converge to an interior point. The arc interior is embedded. It is either a genuine crosscut with distinct endpoint limits, or a simple Jordan loop with its two endpoint limits equal.

Parameterize Γ by a homeomorphism of the circle. Its inverse is uniformly continuous, so two sufficiently close points of Γ are joined by one of the two Γ subarcs of arbitrarily small diameter. For distinct endpoints concatenate that small subarc with C_j. For coincident endpoints use C_j itself. This is a Jordan loop of arbitrarily small diameter. Its bounded Jordan interior has diameter no greater than that loop's diameter (it lies in the convex hull of the loop, as follows from the maximum principle for affine real coordinates). The other boundary subarc, or Γ minus the one endpoint, is connected and has points far outside this small loop, so lies on its exterior side. Thus the bounded interior lies in Ω. For large j, f(0) is outside this small loop. Jordan separation therefore identifies the component of Ω\C_j not containing f(0) with that small interior. This component is the image of the source cap adjacent to ξ, because f preserves the two complementary components of the source crosscut and the base point lies in the other one. Its diameter tends to zero. Hence f has a unique limit at ξ. The same caps give continuity of the extension there, and at every boundary point.

For injectivity, suppose two distinct boundary points ξ₁,ξ₂ have the same image a. The straight source chord between them maps to a Jordan loop with its only boundary point a. Γ\{a} is connected and lies in one complementary component of that loop. The other complementary component lies in Ω and corresponds to one of the two source chord caps. By continuity, the boundary circle arc of that cap must map entirely to a. In a Möbius coordinate flattening a subarc of the source circle to a real interval, f−a is holomorphic on one side, continuous to that interval, and identically zero there. The published holomorphic Schwarz reflection principle extends it across the interval; the identity theorem forces f≡a, a contradiction. This proves boundary injectivity. Every boundary point is reached by compactness applied to inverse images of a sequence in Ω, so the continuous extension is bijective, hence a homeomorphism of compact Hausdorff closures.

## Quadrilateral rectification and modulus continuity

Lyubich Exercise2.77 gives the explicit remaining rectification map: on H choose an analytic square-root branch and a primitive of `1/sqrt(z(z−1)(z−a))`, for a<0. The derivative is nonzero. At a,0,1 the inverse-square-root singularities are integrable; at infinity it is O(|z|⁻³/²), so the primitive has a finite common limit at infinity. Boundary intervals (−∞,a),(a,0),(0,1),(1,∞) map monotonically to four straight edges whose directions alternate by π/2. Closing at infinity makes the opposite edge lengths equal and gives a nondegenerate rectangle. The argument principle on indented upper half-disks proves that every point of the rectangle interior has exactly one preimage, and exterior points have none: the image boundary winds once around an interior point and zero times around an exterior point. This proves conformal rectification, without assuming a general Schwarz–Christoffel theorem. Transport a Jordan quadrilateral to H using its now-proved Jordan boundary extension and a Möbius normalization of three marked points.

Continuity in a follows directly for the two adjacent edge integrals. Substitute t=a+(−a)s on [a,0] and t=s on [0,1]. On compact a-ranges inside (−∞,0), the resulting integrands are bounded by a constant times `[s(1−s)]⁻¹/²`. Dominated convergence makes both finite positive edge lengths continuous, hence their ratio continuous. No monotonicity or endpoint degeneration claim is needed for the present continuity conclusion.

For varying Jordan domains Ω_n with parameterizations σ_n converging uniformly to a Jordan parameterization σ, normalized Riemann maps f_n:D→Ω_n converge locally to the normalized map f of Ω. This normal-family/kernel step needs its exact proof or appropriate published supplier: all Ω_n lie in a fixed bounded set; a fixed disk about the normalized interior point lies in Ω_n eventually, since the boundary winding remains1; inverse normal families exclude a constant limit and identify the image kernel with Ω. Boundary convergence can then be proved by a uniform version of the preceding short-crosscut argument, rather than citing Bishop Lemma5.3 wholesale.

In fact Γ_n have uniform local connectivity: uniform convergence to the homeomorphism σ and uniform continuity of σ and σ⁻¹ control small subarcs uniformly for large n. The image areas of all f_n are bounded by a common constant A. For circles centered at ξ∈∂D, radii ρ∈(δ,sqrt(δ)), Cauchy–Schwarz yields

`∫ L_n(ρ)² dρ/ρ <= 2π∫ |f_n'|² dA <=2πA`.

Thus one such crosscut has image length at most `sqrt(4πA/log(1/δ))`. This tends uniformly to zero. Their caps shrink uniformly by the Jordan argument, since the normalized interior points remain a positive distance from Γ_n. This gives a common continuity modulus for f_n on the closed disk. Local convergence and that modulus imply uniform convergence on the closed disk. The four distinct corner preimages therefore converge, and Möbius cross ratios plus the explicit rectangle-ratio continuity give quadrilateral-modulus continuity.
