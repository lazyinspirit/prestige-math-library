## Statement

Assume AC. A compact finite controlled Whitney-stratified subset of a smooth ambient space admits a finite triangulation of dimension at most the largest real stratum dimension, compatible with every stratum closure and any prescribed union of strata. Each open stratum is a union of open simplices. The homeomorphism is smooth on the pieces within each stratum. The construction may make the finitely many normal link-bundle projections between triangulated compact stratum cores piecewise linear. It does not make an arbitrary algebraic map globally simplicial. In particular it applies individually to compactifications of quasi-projective complex varieties and to their compact controlled links, once the stated Whitney/control hypotheses have been supplied.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC and the compact finite Whitney-stratified $W$, with every prescribed subset a union of its strata.

[F1] Compatible control data and commuting normalized radial collars, including the actual positive-radius domain and zero-end continuity proofs, are supplied by [[lem-compact-controlled-whitney-spaces-admit-commuting-radial-collars]].

[F2] Finite simplicial maps, allowing vertex identifications, have an explicit finite triangulated mapping cylinder fixing both ends, with PL retraction and subcylinder compatibility ([[lem-finite-simplicial-maps-have-explicit-compatible-triangulated-mapping-cylinders]]).

[F3] Compact manifolds with corners have finite admissible smooth triangulations relative to supplied compatible boundary or closed-neighbourhood data ([[thm-compact-smooth-manifolds-with-corners-admit-relative-finite-triangulations]]).

[F4] Smooth compact bundles with genuine finite face-compatible product charts admit relative finite triangulations over a fixed smooth base, provided the protected piece has an actual projection-preserving diagram on a CLOSED collar neighbourhood; finite fitting preserves that diagram and protected smaller collars ([[thm-smooth-bundles-admit-relative-finite-triangulations-over-a-fixed-base-triangulation]], [[lem-relative-smooth-triangulations-can-be-fitted-while-preserving-a-bundle-projection]]).

[F5] Constant-rank charts, smooth subordinate partitions and smooth local ODE solutions with uniqueness and parameter dependence are available ([[thm-constant-rank-theorem-for-manifolds]], [[thm-smooth-partitions-of-unity-exist-on-manifolds]], [[thm-smooth-dependence-of-ode-solutions-on-parameters]]).

[A1] AC carries the actual inherited supplier assumptions ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

## Proof

1.1 Let `W` be compact, with finite compatible tube data and the actual commuting radial collars supplied in the new radial lemma. Retain outer maps while using inner normalized domains. Fix a stratum `X` and a level `0<a<1`. Write `A={p in T_X : rho_X(p)=a}` and `L=closure_W(A)`. This is compact. A limit of points of `A` that leaves `T_X` must land on the frontier of `X`: in tube parameters either the bases have an interior limit, which retains the tube because the normalized radius is the fixed `a<1`, or the bases approach the frontier, where the imposed displacement bound forces the point and its base to have the same limit. Consequently `L` meets only upper incident strata, where it is the smooth hypersurface `rho_X=a`, and lower frontier strata of `X`; it misses `X` itself and incomparable strata. [F1, F5, A1]

2.1 Membership in `L` on a lower frontier stratum `Y<X` is locally constant along that stratum. Here is the required transport, rather than an appeal to a Whitney property of `L`. On a small `Y`-base coordinate box, construct smooth lifts of each base coordinate field in increasing dimension, with the maximal incident tube controller as in the radial lemma. The field on `Y` is the given coordinate field; upper fields satisfy `d pi_Z V=V_Z pi_Z`, `d rho_Z V=0` on the retained tube of every lower `Z`. On the `X` tube, therefore, the lift preserves `rho_X=a`. The compact-trapping proof of the radial lemma gives a jointly continuous short flow, even if ambient boundedness on an entire outer tube is unavailable. It maps `A` to itself wherever defined, hence maps its closure `L` to itself, as does the inverse flow. Ordered coordinate transports along `Y` prove local constancy of membership. Thus `L∩Y` is an open and closed union of components of `Y`. [F1, F5, A1, step 1.1]

3.1 The same horizontal-lift construction, with the level constraints imposed in the chain of controllers, shows that the closure-incidence label of an upper level piece is locally constant on each lower level piece. If a disconnected level piece has different incidence labels, split it by the finitely many Boolean labels recording membership in the closures of the finitely many upper pieces. These subsets are open and closed smooth pieces. There are at most finitely many labels, so this makes a finite frontier stratification; it does not require a finite number of connected components by assumption. The maps and collars below restrict to these pieces, since the transports preserve each closure and hence its incidence labels. [F1, F5, A1, step 2.1]

4.1 For a lower frontier `Y<X`, the restricted `Y` tube retracts `L` into `L∩Y`. Indeed, radial `Y` transport preserves `rho_X` on `A` by the commuting collar construction, and its limit as `rho_Y→0` is `pi_Y`. Hence `pi_Y(A∩T_Y)⊂L`; continuity gives the same inclusion for `L∩T_Y`. For an upper `Y>X`, the old identity `rho_X pi_Y=rho_X` shows directly that `pi_Y` preserves the level hypersurface. Therefore all inherited projections have their actual target in the corresponding stratum of `L`. [F1, F5, A1, step 3.1]

5.1 The inherited radius/projection maps are submersive on the smooth strata of `L`. There are two cases. If `Y<X<Z`, on `Z` the map `(pi_X,rho_X)` is submersive and `(pi_Y,rho_Y)` factors through `pi_X`; the latter pair is submersive on `X`. The joint map `(pi_Y,rho_Y,rho_X)` is therefore submersive on `Z`. Restricting to `rho_X=a` gives the full rank of `(pi_Y,rho_Y)` there. If `X<Y<Z`, the map `(pi_Y,rho_Y)` is submersive on `Z` and `rho_X` factors through `pi_Y`. Its restriction to the kernel of `d rho_X` surjects onto the tangent space of `(Y∩{rho_X=a})×(0,1)`: lift a target vector with `d rho_X=0` through the original pair, and the factorization forces its lift to satisfy the same equation. This is precisely the needed level-pair submersion. [F1, F5, A1, step 4.1]

6.1 All control equations and incomparable-domain disjointness restrict. Local properness restricts because `L` is closed in compact `W`. The radial families restrict too: they preserve `A` where defined, preserve its closure by continuity, and preserve the new incidence labels. Their semigroup, commutation and projection equations are the original equations on actual retained domains. Their zero-end convergence still follows from the compact tube estimate. Thus `L` is a compact finite smooth controlled stratified space with commuting collars. Its maximal stratum dimension is at most `dim(W)-1`: upper level strata lose one dimension, and lower frontier strata have dimension strictly less than `dim(X)≤dim(W)`. This is the exact dimension drop needed by the interior triangulation induction. Nothing in this argument identifies `L` with an algebraic or semialgebraic set. [F1, F5, A1, step 5.1]

7.1 For each stratum `Z` define its core by removing the small open tubes of lower-dimensional strata. Compactness is literal. Any sequence in this core that tends to the frontier of `Z` would eventually lie in the open tube of its limiting lower stratum, contradicting its definition. Thus the core is a closed subset of `W` contained in `Z`, hence compact. The same argument applies to a level link after lower-stratum tubes are removed. [F1, F5, A1, step 6.1]

8.1 At a core point the active lower controllers form a chain. The differentials of their radius functions are independent on the stratum. For a chain `Y_1<...<Y_k<Z`, use the submersion `(pi_Yk,rho_Yk)` on `Z`; the earlier radius functions factor through `pi_Yk`, and inductively their differentials are independent on `Y_k`. Lifting first through the earlier joint radius map and then through the maximal pair proves surjectivity of the joint radius map on `Z`. In constant-rank coordinates, inequalities `rho_Yj≥d_j` are independent half-space coordinates. The core is therefore a smooth compact manifold with corners, and every designated face or face intersection is a genuine corner face. This checks [F3]–[F4]'s corner-face compatibility premise. [F1, F5, A1, step 6.1, step 7.1]

9.1 The normal level-core map to a fixed base core, `pi_X: Z∩S_X(a)→X`, is a proper corner submersion with compatible faces. For `Y<X`, the boundary coordinate `rho_Y-d_Y` is the pullback of the same base boundary coordinate by `pi_X`. For `X<Y<Z`, it is an extra source face coordinate. The pair-map factorization proves surjectivity of the joint differential `(d pi_X, d rho_Y1,...,d rho_Yk)` for the extra source faces. The preceding kernel argument applies also to the fixed equation `rho_X=a`. Thus local smooth constant-rank charts put the map and all active faces in the form `(b,u)→b`, with base inequalities in coordinates `b`, the corresponding source inequalities equal to those base inequalities, and extra source inequalities in coordinates `u`. Properness follows from the already proved compactness of the truncated cores. [F1, F5, A1, step 6.1, step 8.1]

10.1 To obtain bundle charts, choose a sufficiently small corner box in the base. For each base coordinate `b_i`, choose local smooth horizontal fields satisfying `d pi_X V_i=∂/∂b_i` and `d h_j V_i=0` for every active extra source face-defining coordinate `h_j`. The joint differential just proved has a smooth right inverse, giving these fields. Choose patches avoiding every inactive closed face and retaining every active one. A smooth subordinate partition preserves all these linear equations. No derivative of a partition coefficient enters. For a corresponding source face pulled back from the base, its derivative is automatically the prescribed derivative of that base coordinate; this face must not incorrectly be required tangent to an outward base field. [F1, F5, A1, step 6.1, step 9.1]

11.1 On a compact inverse image, finitely many extended smooth corner charts give a common short flow time. Flow each coordinate in a fixed order from a selected base point (possibly on a boundary face). The coordinate segment remains in the base orthant. Extra source faces are invariant; corresponding source faces follow the nonnegative base coordinates, so the source path remains in the corner manifold. The ordinary smooth field is temporarily extended across the coordinate hyperplanes in its chart to define the one-sided boundary flow; only the part with nonnegative base and fibre face coordinates is retained. Compactness and the face constraints exclude finite-time exit. Reversing the coordinate flows returns to the selected fibre and supplies the smooth inverse. These products preserve every named face and the projection exactly. A finite base cover gives genuine finite smooth bundle product trivializations, which are the additional hypothesis required by the relative-smooth bundle-fitting author. [F1, F5, A1, step 6.1, step 10.1]

12.1 For a finite controlled space `W`, let `W^(p)` be its union of strata of real dimension at most `p`; these are closed by the frontier axiom. Put `T_p(d)=union_{dimY≤p}{rho_Y<d}`, and for a stratum `X` of dimension `i` put `X_d^0=X\T_(i−1)(d)`. Every such core is a compact manifold with corners, as proved in the core construction below. For a top stratum `Z` of dimension `n` put `S_d^p(Z)=Z_d^0∩union_{dimY≤p}{rho_Y=d}`. Intersections of these sets are genuine corner faces: incomparable tube domains are disjoint, and the joint rank of the radii in every active chain has already been proved. [F1, F3, F4, F5, A1, step 11.1]

13.1 An interior `d` triangulation triangulates all cores `X_d^0`, retains every intersection with a lower normal level as a subpolyhedron, and makes the level-to-base projections PL. A partial `(d,p)` triangulation does the same on all lower-dimensional cores and only on `S_d^p(Z)` for each top stratum. At every stage the prescribed faces and their intersections are full subcomplexes after one common barycentric subdivision. Smooth-core pieces and positive-width collar pieces carry [F3]–[F4]'s nondegenerate local inverse/tangent-star contract. This is not asserted at a singular collapsed end; singular ends arise only in the final topological gluing. [F1, F3, F4, F5, A1, step 11.1, step 12.1]

14.1 The induction retains buffers. If `d'<d`, first choose `d'<d''<d`; prescribed pieces are retained on a closed collar, and fitting is performed within the larger open collar. All objects are compact and there are finitely many strata and flags. Thus each supplier's local fine approximation bounds have positive minima on each of the finitely many compact pieces, and a finite subdivision satisfies them. There is no infinite process and no claim that an arbitrary preassigned incompatible smooth boundary triangulation can be extended. [F1, F3, F4, F5, A1, step 11.1, step 13.1]

15.1 Given an already triangulated level `J=f^{-1}(S_X(d))`, attach the product `J×[d',d]` along `J×{d}` and map `(q,t)` to `R_X(t)f(q)`. This is an embedding onto the annular `X` collar: its inverse records the outer-level point and `rho_X`. The product has a finite triangulation, for example by ordering simplex vertices and decomposing each simplex prism into the finitely many monotone staircase simplices; on shared faces the same vertex order gives the same subdivision. Restrict this subdivision to every marked face. If several collars are extended, the radial commutation identity makes their two product parameterizations agree on overlap, including their corner parameters. A common subdivision of the finitely many product faces therefore glues them without changing their actual maps. Every radius preserved by the transport has the same marked level set before and after extension, and every lower projection transported equivariantly retains its induced PL coordinate expression. This is the precise meaning of extension being independent of the order of incident collar operations. [F1, F3, F4, F5, A1, step 11.1, step 14.1]

16.1 The mutually inductive assertions are: `B_n`, extend an interior triangulation of the lower skeleton to an interior triangulation of an `n`-dimensional space after decreasing `d`; and `C_(n,p)`, extend a partial top-boundary triangulation through the faces whose lower controlling stratum has dimension `p`, after decreasing `d`. The order is `B_(n−1)`, then `C_(n,0),...,C_(n,n−1)`, then `B_n`. The base consists of compact zero-dimensional cores, which are finite: each point is open in its zero-manifold, and compactness gives a finite subcover by singleton neighborhoods. The relative strengthening retains the prescribed finite collar faces and their inherited projections. Every application below is to boundary data produced by the preceding collar/core construction, with the star contract and compatible corners; it is not an unrestricted relative triangulation theorem for arbitrary subsets. [F1, F3, F4, F5, A1, step 11.1, step 15.1]

17.1 For `C_(n,p)`, process the `p`-dimensional lower strata `X` separately; their tubes are disjoint. Work at the original outer level `S_X(d)`, reserving `d'<d''<d`. The already triangulated portion consists of its intersections with the lower skeleton and the top boundary faces previously handled by `C_(n,q)` for `q<p`. Extend those prescribed portions along every other lower collar to the intermediate parameter `d''`. Their intersections with `S_X(d)` are still subpolyhedra by radius preservation. Pass to the compact closure of the level link, whose controlled structure and strict dimension drop were proved in the preceding link and core constructions. Apply the already completed dimension-`n−1` interior/partial extension induction on that link, retaining the prescribed collar intersections. This triangulates all lower link pieces and every required boundary of the still untriangulated top link core. [F1, F3, F4, F5, A1, step 11.1, step 16.1]

18.1 The map to the fixed triangulated `X` core is PL on this prescribed portion. On a portion controlled by a higher stratum `Y>X`, it factors as the inherited link projection to `Y∩S_X(d)` followed by the already triangulated map to `X`; both factors are PL by the induction data. On portions near a lower `Y<X`, it is equivariant with the previously defined collar transports, so the product coordinate maps are the transported old PL maps. All these factorizations are on actual tube overlaps; the radius equations ensure that their source and target remain in the retained cores. [F1, F3, F4, F5, A1, step 11.1, step 17.1]

19.1 The relative bundle prerequisite is a diagram on an entire CLOSED collar, not merely on its boundary. This is supplied before fitting: extend every already triangulated boundary face by a positive-width radial product collar. For a controller `Y>X`, `pi_X R_Y=pi_X`, so the affine diagram map ignores that collar coordinate. For `Y<X`, `pi_X R_Y=R_Y|X pi_X`, and the fixed target triangulation is extended by the identical `Y` collar operation; the diagram map carries that collar coordinate identically to the base. On multiple-face intersections the radial families commute, giving the same product coordinate maps in either order. Thus `pi_X F=h r` holds throughout the closed collar and `r` is PL after common product subdivision. A smaller collar is protected; the positive-width buffer separates every later fitting support from it. This is the actual collar-neighbourhood hypothesis of the bundle theorem, and not an assertion that its manifold boundary-extension theorem automatically preserves a bundle projection. [F1, F3, F4, F5, A1, step 11.1, step 18.1]

20.1 What remains in the top link stratum is a smooth compact manifold-with-corners core. Its map to the fixed `X` core has the actual finite face-compatible bundle charts proved in the core construction below. The new relative smooth bundle theorem triangulates it over that fixed base, making the projection PL. The projection-preserving fitting supplier fits it to the retained positive-width boundary collar, fixes the prescribed subcomplex, and retains the projection exactly. The nondegenerate local star premise is satisfied on both pieces: it is supplied for the smooth core by [F3] and for the collar by the product/radial construction. Full-subcomplex intersections follow from the fitting contract and a common subdivision. Transport the resulting entire link triangulation radially from `rho_X=d` to `rho_X=d'`. Its projection to `X` does not change, and its intersections with all previously treated collar faces agree with the previous partial extension, because the `X` radial transport commutes with those transports and preserves their radii. Glue along those common full subcomplexes. This constructs `C_(n,p)` using only smaller-dimensional induction and the proved smooth bundle-fitting interface. [F1, F3, F4, F5, A1, step 11.1, step 19.1]

21.1 For `B_n`, use the completed `C_(n,p)` in order to triangulate every top-stratum boundary at an intermediate parameter `d''`. Extend this boundary triangulation to parameter `d'` by the collar operator. The remaining portion of each top stratum is a smooth compact manifold with corners. Apply [F3]'s relative manifold triangulation theorem and [F4]'s ordinary finite-fitting clause to that core, retaining its already triangulated closed boundary collar; no bundle structure on the generic top core is required. The shared collar has the star contract and a buffer, and every face intersection is a genuine corner face. A finite simultaneous subdivision makes the overlaps full subcomplexes. Gluing the finite core and collar complexes gives the interior triangulation. The already lower-dimensional pieces and their boundary projections retain their maps. This completes the mutually inductive construction. Only finitely many `(n,p)` assertions occur, so finitely many decreases of `d` leave a positive final parameter. [F1, F3, F4, F5, A1, step 11.1, step 20.1]

22.1 Fix the resulting interior triangulation `g_n:J_n→W`. Its image is the disjoint union of all stratum cores. Descend in `i`. The invariant is that the image of `g_i` is this union plus `W\T_(i−1)(d)`, all stratum portions and future lower level sets are subpolyhedra, and the projection from every future lower level to its current base portion is PL. Only these future projections need be retained; no claim is made that every old higher projection becomes a PL map on the completed space. [F1, F2, step 21.1]

23.1 For a stratum `X` of dimension `i−1`, let `A_X=g_i^{-1}(S_X(d))` and `B_X=g_i^{-1}(X)`. The base portion is precisely `X_d^0`: the smaller-dimensional frontier tubes have not yet been filled. The normal level portion is correspondingly truncated over this base. It is compact. Its projection `f_X:A_X→B_X` is PL by the invariant. Finite subdivision makes it simplicial by an explicit finite polytope construction. Cut the target by the affine-hull and facet hyperplanes of the images of every face of every source simplex on which the map is affine; every such image is a union of the resulting target cells. Cut each source simplex by the inverse images of these target cells. Every face of a source cell is of the form of a source face intersected with the inverse image of a target face, so its image is one target cell (or it is empty): the target refinement has already made every original source-face image a union of cells. For each nonempty source cell choose an interior point mapping to the chosen interior point of its image target cell. Such a lift exists in its relative interior: an affine surjection of a convex polytope onto its image sends relative interior onto relative interior, as seen by taking a small positive convex combination with an interior point of the source. Cone the face subdivisions using these points, and barycentrically cone the target cells. Nested source faces have nested image target faces, with repetitions allowed, so the resulting map is simplicial. Mark all intersections with future lower levels and stratum closures. One further barycentric subdivision makes all these marked subcomplexes full. [F1, F2, step 21.1, step 22.1]

24.1 Use the actual mapping-cylinder helper for `f_X`, allowing simplex vertex identifications. Its finite domain and explicit homeomorphism have already been constructed with the threshold inverse. Map its topological cylinder to `W` by `(q,t)↦R_X(td)g_i(q)` for `t>0`, with the zero end mapped to `pi_X g_i(q)`. The radial lemma supplies continuity at that zero end and the homeomorphism onto the closed `X` collar over `X_d^0`. The new collar meets the old image only in its outer level `A_X` and base `B_X`: every old higher collar began at a core with `rho_X≥d`, and its radial transports preserve `rho_X`. Therefore no old point has `0<rho_X<d` over this base. Two distinct same-dimensional `X` collars are disjoint because their tubes are incomparable. Thus gluing along the two boundary copies is injective. The domain is finite compact, the target `W` is Hausdorff, and the radial inverse proves surjectivity onto exactly the newly filled collar. The map onto the new image is consequently a homeomorphism. [F1, F2, step 21.1, step 23.1]

25.1 For a future lower stratum `Y<X`, `rho_Y` and `pi_Y` factor through `pi_X` on the `X` collar. Hence the part of `S_Y(d)` inside this new cylinder is exactly the cylinder over the corresponding marked source/base subcomplex pair. The mapping-cylinder helper makes it a subpolyhedron. Its projection to the future `Y` base is the cylinder retraction followed by the existing base projection `pi_Y|X`; both are PL. This proves the descending invariant. Stratum-closure intersections are retained by the same subcylinder compatibility: the radial maps preserve strata, so an upper stratum or its closure contributes its marked top subset together with the appropriate zero-end base subset. No dimension used here is a complex perversity dimension. [F1, F2, step 21.1, step 24.1]

26.1 After all `i` have been processed the image is `W`, and all strata and prescribed unions of strata are unions of open simplices. The complex dimension is at most the maximal real stratum dimension: smooth core complexes have their manifold dimension, link dimensions drop by one, and the mapping-cylinder dimension bound of [F2] adds at most one to the source-link dimension while retaining the smaller base dimension. The finite simplicial cylinder maps are smooth inside positive-height cells, and radial transport is smooth on each smooth stratum. At a zero end the lower stratum already has its smooth core triangulation; no ambient smooth immersion through a singular end is asserted. The finite list of glued complexes therefore supplies the claimed finite compatible stratified triangulation by the preceding interior construction and exact relative-supplier premises. [F1, F2, step 21.1, step 25.1] ∎
