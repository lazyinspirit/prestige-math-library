## Statement

Assume AC. Let $W$ be a compact finite smooth Whitney-stratified subset of a smooth Euclidean ambient space, with the frontier condition. After shrinking compatible tube data and smoothly rescaling their squared radii by positive functions of their base projections, there are normalized tubes $T_X=\{\rho_X<1\}$ and stratum-preserving radial projections $R_X(s):T_X\setminus X\to\{\rho_X=s\}$ for $0<s<1$. On every incident overlap $Y<X$ they satisfy
$$R_X(s)R_Y(t)=R_Y(t)R_X(s),\qquad \rho_YR_X(s)=\rho_Y,\qquad \rho_XR_Y(t)=\rho_X,$$
whenever the source and target points lie in the indicated retained tubes. They also satisfy $\pi_XR_X(s)=\pi_X$, $R_X(s)R_X(t)=R_X(s)$, and $\pi_ZR_X(s)=R_X(s)|_Z\pi_Z$ for $X<Z$ on overlaps. They are continuous on their stratified domains and smooth on individual strata. For a fixed reference $a\in(0,1)$, $(R_X(a),\rho_X)$ identifies the punctured tube with $S_X(a)\times(0,1)$, where $S_X(a)=W\cap\{\rho_X=a\}$; adjoining $X$ identifies it with the corresponding open mapping cylinder of $\pi_X:S_X(a)\to X$. This is a controlled topological collar; no semialgebraicity of the resulting flows is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, compact $W$ and its finite Whitney stratification.

[F1] Compatible tubes with chain overlaps, arbitrary positive base-dependent shrinking, frontier-distance displacement bounds and local properness over compact base slices exist ([[lem-whitney-stratifications-admit-compatible-control-data]]).

[F2] Smooth partitions and ordinary smooth ODE uniqueness and parameter dependence exist ([[thm-smooth-partitions-of-unity-exist-on-manifolds]], [[thm-smooth-dependence-of-ode-solutions-on-parameters]]).

[A1] AC supplies the choices of smooth partitions and the inherited tube assumptions ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

## Proof

1.1 Retain the outer tube maps of [F1], and write their parameter domains as $\|v\|<b_X(x)$ with smooth positive $b_X$. Replace $\rho_X$ by $\|v\|^2/b_X(\pi_X)^2$. Every outer tube now has the fixed normalized domain $\rho_X<1$. The old equalities survive: $\pi_Y\pi_X=\pi_Y$ implies that both the numerator and the positive base factor of the new $\rho_Y$ are preserved by $\pi_X$. Rank of $(\pi_X,\rho_X)$ survives because the target-coordinate change $(x,r)\mapsto(x,r/b_X(x)^2)$ is a diffeomorphism for positive $r$. Local properness survives over compact base slices, where $b_X$ has positive minimum and finite maximum. Use closed middle bands $\rho_X\le1/2$ and inner bands $\rho_X<1/4$ throughout the following field construction. Their relative closures in higher strata lie inside the outer tube by [F1]'s frontier-displacement bound. Incomparable tubes remain disjoint. [F1, F2, A1, construct, algebra]

2.1 Fix $X$. On $W\cap T_X\setminus X$ the map $g_X=(\pi_X,\rho_X)$ is submersive on every stratum. Construct a field lifting the target field $(0,r)$, in increasing dimension of its incident strata. On a minimal stratum use any smooth right inverse of $dg_X$, given explicitly by the adjoint minimum-norm matrix inverse. On a higher stratum inside a lower incident $Z$ tube, lift $(V_Z\pi_Z,0)$ through $(d\pi_Z,d\rho_Z)$. This also lifts $(0,r)$ because $g_X\pi_Z=g_X$. Active middle-band controllers form a chain; the maximal one implies all lower constraints by differentiating the control factorization. Patches avoiding inactive closed middle bands and lying inside the active outer domains, followed by a smooth subordinate partition, give $dg_XV=(0,\rho_X)$ everywhere and $d\pi_ZV=V_Z\pi_Z$, $d\rho_ZV=0$ in every inner $Z$ band. These are linear equations in the vector field, so partition derivatives never enter. Do this for each of the finitely many $X$, using the same constant bands; no bound on the field norm on the entire outer tube is needed. Now retain only the inner tube of every stratum and multiply all radii by four. This constant rescaling preserves every field equation and makes every retained tube again $\rho_Z<1$. The retained outer maps and fields remain available on larger bands. For $Y<X$, the field also preserves $\pi_Y$ and $\rho_Y$ wherever defined, since both factor through $\pi_X$ and $d\pi_XV=0$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Here is the compact-trapping integrability argument for the fields just constructed; it does not assume that an arbitrary smooth field on the open strata is ambiently bounded. Induct on stratum dimension. Near a point $q$ of a lower stratum $Z$, choose a compact base slice $C\subset Z$ containing its smooth field trajectory for a common short time, and a closed small radius band whose inverse image $K$ is compact and lies inside the retained tube. For initial upper points sufficiently close to $q$, the projected trajectory follows the already constructed smooth $Z$ flow, and its radius is constant. Hence, while the upper ODE exists, it stays in the interior of $K$: a first exit would have a compact limit with its base still in the interior of $C$ and its radius strictly below the band's outer boundary, contradicting exit. If an upper trajectory had a finite maximal endpoint inside this common interval, compactness gives a cluster point. A cluster point in its own stratum permits ordinary ODE extension: its smooth field is bounded on a relatively compact coordinate ball, so a sufficiently late visit cannot leave the ball during the remaining short time, and the resulting path is Cauchy there. For a cluster point $q'$ in a lower stratum $Z'$, apply the same base-flow and compact-tube trap at $q'$ on a short interval covering the remaining time. Choose one sufficiently late visit of the trajectory to that trap. It then cannot leave before the endpoint, and $\rho_{Z'}$ is a fixed positive value on that terminal segment. A subsequence of the visits defining the cluster point still converges to $q'$, forcing this value to tend to zero, a contradiction. Only incident lower strata can be cluster strata by the frontier condition. This proves a common short flow interval near each lower point. The same argument backward gives inverse flows. Projection and constant small radius also prove joint continuity: on the compact tube slice, distance to projection tends uniformly to zero as the radius tends to zero, and the projected lower flow depends continuously on initial point and time. Thus every incident-stratum sequence approaching a lower initial point has the correct flow limit. Finite stratification and time subdivision complete the induction. [F1, F2, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 The initial radial family is obtained by flowing the $X$ field for time $\log(s/\rho_X(p))$, since $\dot\rho_X=\rho_X$ and $\dot\pi_X=0$. It exists for all positive source and target levels inside the retained domain. Indeed, over the fixed base $\pi_X(p)$ and any closed positive radius interval inside the outer tube, the full inverse image in compact $W$ is compact; normal-bundle coordinates prove this directly. The endpoint argument of step 3.1 therefore extends the flow for this finite time. Ordinary uniqueness gives its semigroup identity and its inverse transport between any two levels. Controls give preservation of every other incident radius, preservation of lower projections, and equivariance with higher projections. A trajectory starting in a retained higher tube cannot leave it during a finite radial transport: its normalized radius there is constant, its projected higher trajectory exists by the same compact endpoint argument, and a first exit is excluded by local properness over the compact projected trajectory. The same argument applies to transport in a lower tube across a higher tube. Consequently all transports used below stay in their actual normalized tube domains; this is the reason for step 1.1's normalization. [F1, F2, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Construct the final families in increasing dimension of $X$. Suppose the lower families are fixed, and modify the initial $X$ family by a decreasing-dimensional induction on $Y<X$. For the currently processed $Y$, retain a small outer $Y$ band $0<\rho_Y<c$ lying inside all previously needed $Y$ commutation domains; finitely many earlier corrections give such a positive constant $c$. Put $a=c/2$. Write $F^Y_{u\to v}$ for transport along the already fixed $Y$ family between radius levels $u$ and $v$. Choose a smooth function $\phi$ with $\phi(t)=a$ for $t\le a$, $\phi(t)=t$ for $t\ge2c/3$, and $a\le\phi(t)<c$ for $0<t<c$. For a point $p$ with $t=\rho_Y(p)<c$, replace the current $X$ family by $\widetilde R_X(s)(p)=F^Y_{\phi(t)\to t}\,R_X(s)\,F^Y_{t\to\phi(t)}(p)$. All terms are defined by step 4.1, and the intermediate $X$ operation preserves the $Y$ radius. This is conjugation on each fixed $Y$ slice. It is equal to the old family near the outer boundary because $\phi(t)=t$ there, so patch it with the old family outside the $Y$ band. The formula is continuous and stratumwise smooth, including across the patch boundary. [F2, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 The correction preserves the $X$ semigroup law because both $X$ operations preserve $t$, so the middle $Y$ transports cancel. It preserves normalization $\rho_X\widetilde R_X(s)=s$ because the $Y$ transports preserve $\rho_X$. It preserves $\pi_X$: higher-projection equivariance of the $Y$ transports moves the $X$ base forward and then backward, while the middle $X$ operation fixes its base. For any other incident radius $\rho_Z$, each $Y$ transport preserves it and the middle $X$ operation also preserves it; for $Z=Y$ the two transports cancel its change. Lower projection preservation and higher projection equivariance survive by exactly the same composition calculation: when the $Y$ transport moves that projection, its equivariance moves it forward and backward around an $X$ operation that fixes it, or yields the identical corrected operation on the projected higher stratum. Hence all statements about domains and actual controls in step 4.1 remain true after correction. [step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 For $t<a$ the correction is obtained by transporting to the same reference level $a$, performing the same $X$ operation there, and transporting back. Thus in $Y$ coordinates $(q,t)=(R_Y(a)p,\rho_Y(p))$ it has the form $(q,t)\mapsto(A_s(q),t)$, independent of $t$. It therefore commutes with every $Y$ transport whose source and target lie in this inner band. Shrink the final $Y$ commutation domain to $t<a$, retain the outer maps for the next correction, and if desired rescale this domain by the constant $a$. For a previously treated $Z$ with $Y<Z<X$, the $Z$ family commutes with the $Y$ transports by the already fixed lower-family construction, it preserves $t$, and it commutes with the current $X$ family by the inner induction. Moving it through the three factors in step 5.1 proves that it commutes with the corrected $X$ family. Incomparable $Y,Z$ have disjoint tubes. Equal-dimensional strata are incomparable. Hence all previously imposed commutations survive. The finite nested induction terminates with every incident pair commuting. All further domain rescalings are by constants, so radius preservation and control equalities survive. [step 5.1, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 Fix a retained normalized reference $a$. The inverse of $p\mapsto(R_X(a)p,\rho_X(p))$ is $(q,t)\mapsto R_X(t)q$, by the semigroup law and step 4.1's invertible level transport. Both maps are continuous and smooth on each incident stratum. Adjoin the zero end by sending $(q,0)$ to $\pi_X(q)$. Continuity is local on compact base slices: in the normal-bundle tube model, the parameter-vector norm is $b_X(\pi_X(p))\sqrt{\rho_X(p)}$. The smooth tube embedding has bounded fibre derivative on a smaller compact parameter slice, so physical displacement is at most a fixed constant times this norm; $b_X$ is also bounded there. Thus transport to radius zero converges uniformly to its base projection. The same estimate proves inverse continuity at the adjoined stratum; compactness of each closed link fibre supplies the uniform quotient neighborhoods. These give the claimed open mapping-cylinder model and finish all assertions. [F1, step 4.1, step 6.1, step 7.1] ∎
