## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and the standing hypothesis (H) of
[[def-elementary-predictable-brownian-integrand]]. Let
$X_t=X_0+\int_0^tb_s\,ds+\int_0^t\sigma_s\,dB_s$ be a real continuous Brownian
Ito process [[def-continuous-brownian-ito-process]], under its usual-filtration
conditions, and let
$f\in C^{1,2}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb R)$, meaning that $f$, its first time
derivative $\partial_tf$ and its first two space derivatives
$\partial_xf,\partial^2_{x}f$ exist and are continuous on
$[0,\infty)\times\mathbb R$. Then, up to indistinguishability, for every
$t\ge0$
$$f(t,X_t)=f(0,X_0)+\int_0^t\Bigl(\partial_tf+b\,\partial_xf+\tfrac12\sigma^2\partial^2_xf\Bigr)(s,X_s)\,ds+\int_0^t\sigma_s\,\partial_xf(s,X_s)\,dB_s,$$
For the displayed integrands use the following representative convention.
Intersect the measurable full events of continuity of $X$, validity of its
decomposition, and local integrability of its coefficients at integer horizons.
Its complement is an $\mathcal F_0$-measurable null event by the usual
conditions. Replace $X,X_0,b,\sigma$ by zero there. This preserves the
decomposition up to indistinguishability and all coefficient classes, and
makes $X$ continuous everywhere and its coefficient path integrals locally
finite everywhere. All integrands below refer to these representatives.
The stochastic integral is the localized Ito integral of the predictable
locally square-integrable process $\sigma_s\partial_xf(s,X_s)$
[[thm-localized-ito-integral]] and the Lebesgue integral is the pathwise
integral of the progressively measurable process
$s\mapsto(\partial_tf+b\partial_xf+\tfrac12\sigma^2\partial^2_xf)(s,X_s)$,
which is pathwise integrable and finite almost surely for every $t$. In differential notation,
$df(t,X_t)=(\partial_tf+b\partial_xf+\tfrac12\sigma^2\partial^2_xf)(t,X_t)\,dt+\sigma_t\partial_xf(t,X_t)\,dB_t$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, (H), a real continuous Brownian Ito process $X=X_0+A+M$ with $A_t=\int_0^tb_s\,ds$ and $M=\int\sigma\,dB$, a function $f\in C^{1,2}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb R)$, a finite horizon $T>0$, and an arbitrary deterministic partition sequence $(\pi_n)$ of $[0,T]$ with mesh $\delta_n\to0$. The stopping time $\rho_c$ is defined as in [F7] below.
 
[F1] **Paths, measurability and local boundedness.** After the statement's $\mathcal F_0$-null-event normalization, $X$ is adapted with everywhere continuous paths, hence predictable, and on each finite time interval a continuous path is bounded. Every continuous function of $(s,X_s)$ is predictable; its product with the predictable coefficient $\sigma$ is predictable, while its product with the progressively measurable drift $b$ is progressively measurable. [[def-continuous-brownian-ito-process]] [[lem-adapted-continuous-processes-are-progressively-measurable]] [[def-progressively-measurable-and-predictable-process]]
 
[F2] **Localized-integral interfaces.** For a predictable $G$ with finite energy: $E(\int_0^TG\,dB)^2=E\int_0^TG^2ds$, the integral over a subinterval is the integral of the restriction, the stopping identity holds, the elementary sums converge to the integral, and the Doob maximal bound $E\sup_{t\le T}(\int_0^tG\,dB)^2\le4E\int_0^TG^2ds$ holds. A locally square-integrable predictable $G$ has a localized integral whose stopped pieces are the finite-energy integrals of $G1_{(0,\rho]}$; and if $c$ is bounded and $\mathcal F_u$-measurable then the localized integral of $cG$ over an interval inside $(u,\infty)$ equals $c$ times that of $G$, because the finite-energy case follows from the elementary case and the $L^2$ isometry and the general case by stopping and uniqueness. [[thm-localized-ito-integral]] [[thm-stopping-an-ito-integral]] [[thm-ito-isometry-and-linearity-in-predictable-l2]] [[thm-doob-maximal-bound-for-the-ito-integral]] [[thm-ito-integral-process-has-a-continuous-martingale-version]] [[def-ito-integral-for-square-integrable-predictable-processes]] [[def-locally-square-integrable-predictable-brownian-integrand]]
 
[F3] **Quadratic variation and covariation of the class.** $[X]_t=\int_0^t\sigma_s^2\,ds$ and, more generally, $\sum_j\Delta_jX\Delta_jY\to[X,Y]=\int\xi\eta\,ds$ uniformly in probability for class processes; in particular with $\Delta_jX=X_{t_{j+1}}-X_{t_j}$ the step-convention sums $Q_n^X(t)=\sum_{j:\,t_{j+1}\le t}(\Delta_jX)^2$ and $Q_n^M(t)=\sum_{j:\,t_{j+1}\le t}(\Delta_jM)^2$ each satisfy $\sup_{t\le T}|Q_n^X(t)-\int_0^t\sigma_s^2ds|\to0$ and $\sup_{t\le T}|Q_n^M(t)-\int_0^t\sigma_s^2ds|\to0$ in probability for every deterministic vanishing-mesh sequence and both conventions. [[thm-quadratic-covariation-of-brownian-ito-processes]] [[def-quadratic-covariation-of-brownian-ito-processes]] [[def-quadratic-variation-along-a-partition-sequence]]
 
[F4] **Taylor expansion with a third-order remainder.** Let $f\in C^3$ on an open set containing the closed segment from $a=(t_0,x_0)$ to $a+h=(t_0+h_1,x_0+h_2)$. Then $f(a+h)=f(a)+\partial_tf(a)h_1+\partial_xf(a)h_2+\tfrac12\bigl(\partial^2_{tt}f(a)h_1^2+2\partial_t\partial_xf(a)h_1h_2+\partial^2_{xx}f(a)h_2^2\bigr)+R$ with $|R|\le M_3|h|^3$, where $M_3$ bounds the third partial derivatives on a ball containing the segment: apply the one-variable Taylor formula with remainder bound to $u\mapsto f(a+uh)$ on $[0,1]$. [[cor-second-order-taylor-expansion-with-the-hessian]] [[cor-multivariable-taylor-formula-with-peano-remainder]] [[def-multivariable-taylor-polynomial]] [[def-taylor-polynomial-and-remainder]] [[cor-taylor-remainder-bound]]
 
[F5] **Staircase comparison and the weighted pullback of quadratic variation.** (a) If $D\in\mathcal F_0$, $G$ is continuous adapted and bounded on $D\times[0,T]$, $G^{(n)}$ is its left-endpoint staircase on $\pi_n$, and $\int_0^TH^2ds\le c$ on $D$, then the integrals of $1_DG^{(n)}H$ converge to that of $1_DGH$ in $L^2(P)$. Indeed the isometry bounds the squared distance by $E[1_D\sup_s|G^{(n)}_s-G_s|^2\int_0^TH^2ds]$, which tends to zero by dominated convergence. (b) If $w$ is continuous adapted with $|w|\le K$ and $M=\int\sigma\,dB$ is a finite-energy integral, then $\sum_jw(t_j)\Delta_jM^2\to\int_0^Tw_s\sigma_s^2\,ds$ in probability; this terminal-time weighted pullback is proved in steps 1.4--2.1 below. [[thm-ito-isometry-and-linearity-in-predictable-l2]] [[thm-quadratic-covariation-of-brownian-ito-processes]] [[def-ito-integral-of-an-elementary-predictable-process]]
 
[F6] **Bounded Riemann integrals.** If $Z$ is continuous on $[0,T]$ and $h$ pathwise Lebesgue-integrable, then $\sum_jZ_{t_j}\int_{t_j}^{t_{j+1}}h_s\,ds\to\int_0^TZ_sh_s\,ds$ along vanishing meshes, the error being at most $\max_j\sup_{I_j}|Z-Z_{t_j}|\int_0^T|h|$. A continuous function on the compact set $[0,T]$ and a continuous function on a compact cylinder are uniformly continuous, so maximal oscillations on the mesh intervals vanish. [[thm-heine-cantor-r]] [[def-continuity-real]]
 
[F7] **Localization of the class and of the coefficients.** For $c>0$ put $D_c:=\{|X_0|\le c\}\in\mathcal F_0$ and $\rho_c:=\inf\{t:\max(|X_t|,\int_0^t|b|,\int_0^t\sigma^2)\ge c\}\wedge T$. Then $\rho_c$ is a stopping time: for $t<T$, the event $\{\rho_c\le t\}$ is the event that the running supremum of the continuous adapted process $\max(|X|,\int|b|,\int\sigma^2)$ on $[0,t]$ is at least $c$. This supremum is the supremum over rational times together with $t$, hence is $\mathcal F_t$-measurable; for $t\ge T$ the stopping event is the whole space. The process $\bar X^{(c)}:=1_{D_c}X^{\rho_c}$ is a continuous Brownian Ito process with initial value $1_{D_c}X_0$ and coefficients $1_{D_c}b1_{[0,\rho_c]}$, $1_{D_c}\sigma1_{[0,\rho_c]}$. It is bounded by $c$, its drift variation and diffusion energy on $[0,T]$ are at most $c$, and on $D_c\cap\{\rho_c\ge T\}$ it and all its integrals coincide with those of $X$. These events increase to a probability-one event as $c\to\infty$, because $X$ is continuous and $\int|b|,\int\sigma^2$ are finite and continuous in the upper limit almost surely. [[def-continuous-brownian-ito-process]] [[thm-localized-ito-integral]] [[thm-stopping-an-ito-integral]] [[def-continuous-time-stopping-time]] [[thm-heine-cantor-r]]
 
[F8] **Cutoff and mollification on a cylinder.** Reflection across $t=0$ by $g(t,x):=f(t,x)$ for $t\ge0$ and $g(t,x):=2f(0,x)-f(-t,x)$ for $t<0$ extends $f$ to a $C^{1,2}$ function on a negative-time collar and agrees with $f$ on the nonnegative half-space. Choose compact cylinders $K\subseteq\operatorname{int}K'$ inside a bounded open set on which $g$ is defined. The cutoff lemma gives a continuous compactly supported cutoff equal to $1$ on $K'$; convolution with a sufficiently small compactly supported mollifier gives $\chi\in C_c^\infty$ equal to $1$ on $K$ and supported in that open set. Then $g_\varepsilon:=(\chi g)*\rho_\varepsilon$ is smooth and compactly supported, and on the inner cylinder the functions $g_\varepsilon,\partial_tg_\varepsilon,\partial_xg_\varepsilon,\partial^2_{xx}g_\varepsilon$ converge uniformly to $f,\partial_tf,\partial_xf,\partial^2_{xx}f$. To justify the derivative convergence, write the convolution as $\int \rho(z)(\chi g)(y-\varepsilon z)dz$. Difference quotients and the fundamental theorem in each variable move each available derivative ($\partial_t$, $\partial_x$, $\partial_{xx}$) onto $\chi g$, dominated on the fixed compact support by the corresponding continuous derivative bound times $|\rho(z)|$. On the inner cylinder $\chi=1$ on a neighbourhood; uniform continuity of each derivative bounds its convolution error by its modulus on shifts of size at most $\varepsilon R$ times $\int|\rho|$, where $R$ bounds the mollifier support. This tends to zero. [[def-c-c-and-c-c-infinity-on-rn]] [[def-mollifier-family-generated-by-a-unit-mass-smooth-bump]] [[thm-convolution-with-a-mollifier-is-smooth-and-differentiates-under-the-integral-sign]] [[lem-a-compact-set-inside-a-bounded-open-set-admits-an-explicit-compactly-supported-cutoff]] [[thm-c-c-infinity-rn-is-dense-in-l-p-of-rn]]
 
[F9] **Convergence tools and estimates.** Cauchy--Schwarz for sums and expectations; dominated convergence for pathwise Lebesgue integrals; Fatou's lemma; and the fact that a sequence bounded by $R_n$ with $ER_n\to0$ converges to $0$ in probability. [[cor-cauchy-schwarz-for-random-variables]] [[thm-dominated-convergence]] [[thm-fatou-lemma]] [[def-convergence-in-probability]]
 
[F10] **AC bookkeeping.** Choice is declared for the ambient conditional-expectation, completeness and density interfaces; all stopping levels, partitions and mollification scales used below are canonical functions of the given data. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]
 
 
 
 

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.
 
1.1 Reduction to a bounded localized problem: fix $c>0$ and replace $X$ by $\bar X^{(c)}=1_{D_c}X^{\rho_c}$ as in [F7]. This is globally bounded by $c$, its drift variation and diffusion energy on $[0,T]$ are at most $c$, and on $D_c\cap\{\rho_c\ge T\}$ both sides of the desired formula agree with those for the original process. Thus the stochastic integrand has finite energy bounded by $c\sup|\partial_xf|^2$, and every continuous function of $(s,\bar X^{(c)}_s)$ is bounded on $[0,T]$. It remains to prove the identity for this bounded process; localization is removed at the end. To simplify notation, call the bounded process and its coefficients again $X,b,\sigma$. [F1, F2, F7, given]
 
1.2 Setup of the $C^3$ case: assume first that $f\in C^3$ on a neighbourhood of the cylinder $[0,T]\times[-c,c]$ with finite bounds $M_0,M_1,M_2,M_3$ for its partial derivatives of orders $0,1,2,3$ there. For the partition $\pi_n$ write $\Delta t_j=t_{j+1}-t_j$, $\Delta X_j=X_{t_{j+1}}-X_{t_j}=\Delta A_j+\Delta M_j$. [F1, F3, given]
 
1.3 Weighted pullback setup: for continuous adapted $w$ with $|w|\le K$ put $H=\sigma$. In steps 1.4 and 2.1 we prove the weighted limit needed for the second-order term. Write $Q_n=Q_n^M$ from [F3] throughout the remainder of the proof; then $Q_n(T)$ is bounded in probability and converges to $\int_0^TH^2ds$. [F3, given]
 
1.4 Weighted pullback, elementary weights: let $w=\sum_a\lambda_a1_{(u_a,u_{a+1}]}$ be elementary with bounded $\mathcal F_{u_a}$-measurable coefficients and fixed deterministic block points. For the cumulative sums $Q_n(v)$ on the original partition, [F3] gives uniform-in-$v$ convergence in probability to $\int_0^vH^2ds$. The sum over those original intervals lying wholly in $(u_a,u_{a+1}]$ is $Q_n(u_{a+1})-Q_n(u_a)$ up to the at most two boundary intervals. Their contribution is bounded by $2\|\lambda_a\|_\infty\max_j|\Delta_jM|^2$, which tends to zero almost surely by continuity. Summing over the finitely many blocks gives $\sum_jw(t_j)\Delta_jM^2\to\sum_a\lambda_a\int_{u_a}^{u_{a+1}}H^2ds=\int_0^TwH^2ds$ in probability. [F3, F6, given]
 
1.5 Remainder of the Taylor expansion: with $h_j=(t_{j+1}-t_j,X_{t_{j+1}}-X_{t_j})$ the Taylor expansion [F4] gives, for each $j$, $f(t_{j+1},X_{t_{j+1}})-f(t_j,X_{t_j})=\partial_tfh_1+\partial_xfh_2+\tfrac12(\partial^2_{tt}fh_1^2+2\partial_t\partial_xfh_1h_2+\partial^2_{xx}fh_2^2)+R_j$ with $|R_j|\le M_3(|h_1|+|h_2|)^3\le4M_3(|\Delta t_j|^3+|\Delta X_j|^3)$. Summing: $\sum_j|\Delta t_j|^3\le\operatorname{mesh}(\pi_n)^2T\to0$ and $\sum_j|\Delta X_j|^3\le\max_j|\Delta X_j|\sum_j|\Delta X_j|^2\le\max_j|\Delta X_j|(2\sum_j\Delta A_j^2+2Q_n(T))$, where $\max_j|\Delta X_j|\to0$ almost surely by continuity of the paths of $A$ and $M$ and $Q_n(T)-\int_0^T\sigma_s^2ds\to0$ in probability by [F3], so the sum of remainders tends to $0$ in probability. [F3, F4, F6]
 
1.6 First-order terms: $\sum_j\partial_tf(t_j,X_{t_j})\Delta t_j\to\int_0^T\partial_tf(s,X_s)ds$ and $\sum_j\partial_xf(t_j,X_{t_j})\Delta A_j\to\int_0^T\partial_xf(s,X_s)b_s\,ds$ both almost surely by the Riemann estimate [F6] applied with the continuous bounded weights $\partial_tf(\cdot,X)$ and $\partial_xf(\cdot,X)$; and the stochastic part satisfies $\sum_j\partial_xf(t_j,X_{t_j})\Delta_jM=\int_0^Tg^{(n)}\sigma\,dB$ for the left-endpoint staircase $g^{(n)}$ of $s\mapsto\partial_xf(s,X_s)$, which converges in $L^2(P)$ to $\int_0^T\partial_xf(s,X_s)\sigma_s\,dB$ by [F5](a), now with $D=\Omega$, and [F2]. [F2, F5, F6, given]
 
2.1 Weighted pullback, continuous weights: for continuous adapted $w$ with $|w|\le K$ and its left-endpoint staircase $w^{(m)}$ on the deterministic grid of mesh $2^{-m}T$, uniform continuity of $s\mapsto w_s$ gives $\sup_s|w^{(m)}_s-w_s|\to0$ almost surely. Fix $m$ first. Step 1.4 gives the asserted convergence with $w^{(m)}$ as $n\to\infty$. Put $R_m=\sup_s|w^{(m)}_s-w_s|$. The error in the sums is at most $R_mQ_n(T)$. For every $L>0$ its probability of exceeding $\varepsilon$ is at most $P(R_m>\varepsilon/L)+P(Q_n(T)>L)$. First take $L$ large using tightness from [F3], then $m$ large; this bound needs no independence of the two factors. the error in the limiting integrals tends to zero as $m\to\infty$ by dominated convergence with integrable bound $2K\int_0^TH^2ds$. Taking these two limits successively proves [F5](b). [F5, F6, step 1.4]
 
3.1 Second-order terms: by step 2.1 applied to $w=\partial^2_{xx}f(\cdot,X)$, $\sum_j\tfrac12\partial^2_{xx}f(t_j,X_{t_j})\Delta_jM^2\to\tfrac12\int_0^T\partial^2_{xx}f(s,X_s)\sigma_s^2ds$ in probability. The mixed drift--martingale term satisfies $\bigl|\sum_j\partial^2_{xx}f\Delta_jA\Delta_jM\bigr|\le M_2(\sum_j\Delta_jA^2)^{1/2}(\sum_j\Delta_jM^2)^{1/2}\le M_2\bigl(\max_j|\Delta_jA|\int_0^T|b|\bigr)^{1/2}Q_n(T)^{1/2}\to0$ in probability, because $A$ is continuous and hence $\max_j|\Delta_jA|\to0$ by [F6] and $Q_n(T)$ is bounded in probability; and the two purely drift/time terms satisfy $\bigl|\sum_j\tfrac12\partial^2_{xx}f\Delta_jA^2\bigr|\le\tfrac{M_2}{2}\max_j|\Delta_jA|\int_0^T|b|\to0$ and $\bigl|\sum_j\partial_t\partial_xf\Delta t_j\Delta X_j\bigr|+\bigl|\sum_j\tfrac12\partial^2_{tt}f\Delta t_j^2\bigr|\le M_2T\max_j|\Delta X_j|+\tfrac{M_2}{2}T\delta_n\to0$. [F3, F6, step 2.1]
 
4.1 Assemble the $C^3$ case at $T$: summing the exact expansion [step 1.5] over $j$, the left side telescopes to $f(T,X_T)-f(0,X_0)$, while the right side is the sum of the terms controlled in steps 1.5, 1.6 and 3.1; passing to the limit along $\pi_n$ gives $f(T,X_T)-f(0,X_0)=\int_0^T(\partial_tf+b\partial_xf+\tfrac12\sigma^2\partial^2_{xx}f)(s,X_s)ds+\int_0^T\sigma_s\partial_xf(s,X_s)dB_s$ in probability, and uniqueness of limits in probability makes the difference of the two fixed random variables zero almost surely. For an arbitrary $t\in[0,T]$ apply the same argument on $[0,t]$ with the restricted, $t$-augmented partition sequence; both sides are continuous in $t$, so the identity holds for all $t$ up to indistinguishability. [F2, F3, step 1.5, step 1.6, step 3.1]
 
5.1 Reduction to $C^{1,2}$ by cutoff and mollification: let $f\in C^{1,2}$ and take the reflected extension, smooth cutoff and mollifications $g_\varepsilon$ of [F8]. On the nonnegative inner cylinder $[0,T]\times[-c,c]$, where the extension equals $f$, the functions $g_\varepsilon$ and their derivatives $\partial_tg_\varepsilon,\partial_xg_\varepsilon,\partial^2_{xx}g_\varepsilon$ converge uniformly to the corresponding derivatives of $f$. Applying step 4.1 to the smooth $g_\varepsilon$ and passing to the limit, the drift term converges by dominated convergence with bound $K'(1+|b_s|+\sigma_s^2)$, whose integral is at most $K'(T+2c)$; the stochastic term converges in $L^2(P)$ by [F2], since its squared norm is bounded by the uniform squared derivative error times $E\int_0^T\sigma_s^2ds\le c$; and the left side converges uniformly on the inner cylinder. Hence the identity holds for $f$ at $T$, and then for every $t$ by the same continuity argument. [F2, F5, F7, F8, F9, step 4.1]
 
6.1 Removal of the localization and conclusion: the identity for $\bar X^{(c)}$ agrees with the desired identity on $D_c\cap\{\rho_c\ge T\}$. For each path with the normalized local bounds, every integer $c$ larger than $\sup_{t\le T}|X_t|$, $\int_0^T|b|$ and $\int_0^T\sigma^2$ has $D_c$ true and $\rho_c=T$. Thus these events increase to a probability-one event by [F7], so the identity holds almost surely at every deterministic time, and by continuity of both sides up to indistinguishability. The stochastic integrand $\sigma\partial_xf(\cdot,X)$ is predictable and locally square-integrable by [F1] and [F2]. The Lebesgue integrand is progressively measurable and integrable almost surely on every finite horizon: after localization its continuous derivative factors are bounded, while $|b|$ and $\sigma^2$ are integrable. Thus the displayed statement follows. [F1, F2, F7, step 5.1]
 
7.1 Boundary and consistency cases: for $t=0$ both sides equal $f(0,X_0)$; for $f$ independent of $x$ the formula reduces to $f(t)=f(0)+\int_0^t\partial_tf(s)ds$, the fundamental theorem for the deterministic continuous function $\partial_tf$; for $f(t,x)=x$ it reduces to the definition of $X$; for $f(t,x)=x^2$ and $X=B$ (so $X_0=0$, $b=0,\sigma=1$) it gives $B_t^2=2\int_0^tB_sdB_s+t$; if $\sigma\equiv0$ then $X$ is pathwise absolutely continuous and the formula is the chain rule with the second-order term absent, consistent with the vanishing covariation of finite-variation parts; if $X\equiv X_0$ is deterministic the formula is the fundamental theorem along the deterministic time variable; and if the diffusion coefficient is unbounded the localization of step 1.1 is what makes every integral finite, with no additional hypothesis. AC enters only through [F10], and all localization and mollification parameters are canonical. [F2, F3, F10, step 6.1] ∎

## Source notes

Van der Vaart states Theorem 5.79 for a continuous local martingale and a continuous finite-variation process; its proof discussion refers the direct Taylor argument to Chung and Williams and presents a polynomial proof of the more general Theorem 5.85. Lawler, Section 3.3, treats Brownian motion and smooth test functions. Neither attribution substitutes for the explicit local argument below. The $C^3$-first route of steps 1.2 through 4.1 is written out in full because the sources present the argument only for their own bounded or stopped settings; the passage to $C^{1,2}$ by reflection, cutoff and mollification in step 5.1 is the standard smoothing argument, included here so that the stated $C^{1,2}$ hypothesis is proved rather than asserted.
