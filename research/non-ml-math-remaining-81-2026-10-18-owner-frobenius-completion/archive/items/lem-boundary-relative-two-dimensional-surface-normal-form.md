## Statement

Let $S$ be any set and let $M,N$ be compact oriented $S$-colored surfaces in $\mathsf{Cob}^{\mathrm{oc}}_2(S)$ with the same marked source and target words. Then $M$ and $N$ represent the same morphism if and only if they have the same partition of marked black boundary slots into components, the same genus and colored-window multiplicities on every component meeting a marked slot, and the same cyclic open-boundary slot cycles with their complete endpoint-color sequences, together with the same multiset of genus/window data for components meeting no marked black slot. For each connected component, the common morphism is equal to the candidate word $N(M)$ of [[def-relative-open-closed-surface-normal-form]]. For disconnected surfaces the word is the tensor product of the component words, with the marked slot permutations restored.

The claim includes components with no black boundary: their data are genus and the finite multiplicities of colored free-boundary circles, and their word is $\epsilon_C H^g W_M\eta_C$. No color sequence may be discarded, and the theorem makes no semisimplicity or characteristic assumption.

## Proof

1.1 Use the snakes in [F4] to bend each outgoing interval into an incoming interval with the reversed endpoint order and each incoming circle into an outgoing circle. Bending back cancels the inserted pairing and copairing, so it preserves the relative surface class and every marked slot. By [F2], the resulting connected surface has a finite expression in the ten generators. Regard it as a finite directed expression tree, ignore identity strands, and assign height $0$ to a source or unit; at a binary vertex define the height to be the sum of the two input heights plus $1$. Let $G$ be the number of generator vertices in the expression. Then each upstream height is an integer between $0$ and $G$. [given, A1, F2, F4, construct]

1.2 A local rewrite below either removes a distinguished generator, strictly raises or lowers its upstream height, or moves it by a relation without changing $G$. A phase that strictly raises height terminates because the height remains at most $G$; a phase that strictly lowers height terminates because height is a nonnegative integer. When a phase removes a generator, the finite number of generators supplies the outer induction. All rewrites are instances of [F3] or [F4], so they are equality-preserving and keep the endpoint colors on every seam. [step 1.1, F3, F4, algebra]

2.1 **Stage I: remove open units and counits.** Replace every open unit by $i_a\eta_C$ using equation 6 of [F1], and replace every open counit by $\epsilon_Ci_a^*$ using cozipper counit preservation in [F4]. These replacements remove the corresponding open cup/cap and replace it with a closed generator and a zipper/cozipper; no open unit or counit remains. The number of open extrema strictly decreases at each replacement, so the process stops after finitely many occurrences. [given, F1, F4, step 1.2, algebra]

2.2 **Stage II: normalize each open comultiplication.** Fix one occurrence of $\Delta_{abc}$. Its two output legs have only the finitely many possible immediate neighbors among the generators of [F2]; cases ending in an open cap or at an exterior closed-only target have already been excluded by Stage I and the bent boundary convention. If a leg meets a cozipper, use the two cozipper/copairing transports of [F4] to move the cozipper past the coproduct; if the resulting pair of open legs closes through a zipper, apply Cardy and remove the marked coproduct. If a leg meets a zipper, use the reflected zipper transport and the two snake cancellations in [F4]. If an output leg enters an open multiplication, apply the left or right typed Frobenius equation, according to which of the two output legs enters it, to move the multiplication below the marked coproduct; if this gives two legs entering a common multiplication, use the open handle/window rotation and Cardy move to remove the coproduct. If a colored open window occurs on either leg, slide it to the opposite side by the open-window identity in [F4] and then apply the preceding cozipper or Frobenius case. These are the five local cases: cozipper transport, zipper/Cardy cancellation, the two distinct left/right Frobenius attachments, and either-side window transport. Every remaining configuration is one of the three reduced forms obtained by repeating those cases: a coproduct immediately above a reconverging open product, a coproduct with one branch in a cozipper/product chain and the other in a free open branch, or the terminal open-coproduct tail. In each residual form, every further move either removes the marked coproduct or strictly lowers its upstream height. The height bound in step 1.1 proves that the stage terminates with every open coproduct in a reduced form. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, F1, F2, F4, algebra]

2.3 **Stage III: normalize open multiplications, cozipper sources, and open windows.** Fix an open multiplication. Apply indexed associativity to reassociate the finite chain above it; each reassociation either removes the marked product or increases its height, and no move adds a generator. Use the open-window slide to move any colored window above the product to the other side, recording its color. Replace adjacent zippers by one zipper using the algebra-map equation; transport any cozipper at the source through a product using the pairing/coproduct identities in [F4]. A cozipper source is thereby reduced to an incoming interval or one open product. Each open window in a product chain is slid toward the nearest cozipper and absorbed there by $i_a^*O_a=W_ai_a^*$; if it lies on the other side, first use the reflected slide. A terminal zipper followed by cozipper is the composite $i_a^*i_a=W_a$ by definition; when the reverse composite $i_ai_a^*$ occurs, use Cardy to replace it by $\mu_{aaa}\tau\Delta_{aaa}$. Each open-window move removes that open window or lowers its height; height zero cannot be an open window because a window has a nonempty open input/output incidence. The bounded-height argument from step 1.2 terminates the product and cozipper normalizations, and the open-window count terminates the absorption phase. Applying these operations to every occurrence yields the three reduced coproduct configurations of step 2.2 without increasing the total number of open coproducts. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.2, F1, F2, F4, algebra]

3.1 **Stage IV: eliminate all open coproducts.** If one remains, choose an occurrence of minimum upstream height. Its upstream subtree contains no open coproduct. The normalized open products of step 2.3 leave two cases, up to reflection. In the first, both branches of the marked coproduct reconverge through the same open product/handle. Rotate the product by the cyclic cozipper identity, reassociate, rotate back, apply the copairing crossing, cancel the resulting snake, and then use Cardy; this composite removes the marked coproduct. In the second, one branch ends at a cozipper. Rotate the cozipper around the cycle, use naturality of the symmetry to restore the marked slots, apply the typed Frobenius equation and the cozipper transport from step 2.2, and cancel the snake; this also removes the marked coproduct. These are the two exhaustive attachments because any additional open product in the intervening subtree would contradict the minimum-height choice and Stage III. [step 1.1, step 2.2, step 2.3, F3, F4, algebra]

3.2 The eliminations in step 3.1 can disrupt the normal configurations established in Stages II–III. Reapply Stages II–III after each elimination. Neither restart increases the number of open coproduct vertices, while the chosen elimination strictly decreases it by one. Induction on that finite count terminates with no open coproducts and with the Stage II–III normal configurations restored. [step 1.2, step 2.2, step 2.3, step 3.1, F4, induction]

3.3 **Stage V: record the remaining open configurations.** Steps 2.1–3.2 leave no open units, counits, or comultiplications, and the open-window phase of step 2.3 removes open windows. Hence every remaining open multiplication has two interval inputs supplied by external source intervals, earlier normalized open multiplications, or zippers; all other generator types that could have occupied an input were removed in Stages I–IV. Every cozipper source is an external interval or a normalized open multiplication output, by step 2.3. A multiplication output can be an external interval target, a later normalized multiplication input, or a cozipper source. The endpoints in each case must match the adjacent color labels by [F1]. These are the source/target configurations needed in Stage VI; the source figures (3.110)–(3.111) subdivide them by left/right input order. The complete typed subdivision into those diagrams is retained as an open local case check rather than inferred from a drawing. [step 2.1, step 2.2, step 2.3, step 3.1, step 3.2, F1, F2, F4]

3.4 **Stage VI: remove remaining zippers and package open cycles.** With the configuration inventory of step 3.3, a zipper can only lie on an open-product branch feeding a cozipper or a closed product. Push it through successive open products using centrality and associativity. When the terminal composite is cozipper after zipper, it is $i_a^*i_a=W_a$ by definition; when the reverse composite occurs, apply Cardy to replace $i_ai_a^*$ by $\mu_{aaa}\tau\Delta_{aaa}$. If the branch is the reflected configuration, first rotate the cozipper cyclically and use its coalgebra preservation. Each completed elimination removes one zipper and creates no new zipper; between eliminations the remaining distance in the finite open-product tree to a cozipper strictly decreases. Thus the lexicographic measure (number of zippers, then sum of their distances) terminates. The surviving open products are exactly the indexed boundary cycles in [F5], so each becomes $i_a^*\mu^{(q)}$ with its full endpoint-color sequence. The local algebraic moves are justified by [F4]; the full colored left/right incidence ledger remains open for owner review. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 3.3, F1, F4, F5, algebra]

4.1 **Stage VII: normalize closed multiplications.** Fix a closed multiplication. Its source has only the configurations listed by the finite generator decomposition: closed products, a closed window, a handle, cozipper outputs, or open-to-closed boundary-cycle outputs. The configurations with an external incoming closed circle or an unnormalized zipper have already been excluded by the bent boundary convention and Stage VI. Apply closed associativity to join closed product chains; use the closed Frobenius relation to move a product past a closed coproduct; use the window slide to move each $W_a$ to the closed chain; and use the handle slide to collect each $H$. A tracked multiplication either disappears into a closed cycle already packaged in [F5] or moves above the next factor, strictly increasing its upstream height. No rewrite increases $G$, so the bound in step 1.1 proves termination. The result has only the closed-product sources and the two terminal forms that are the inputs to closed comultiplication or to an empty-boundary unit/counit case. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 3.4, F3, F4, F5, algebra]

4.2 **Stage VIII: normalize closed comultiplications.** Fix a closed comultiplication. The closed multiplication normal form of step 4.1 excludes a handle or window feeding a product in the wrong order; Stage VI excludes zipper configurations. Apply closed coassociativity to reassociate the output tree, cancel a counit whenever it meets a coproduct, and slide $W_a$ and $H$ through the coproduct using [F4]. Each move either removes the tracked coproduct or strictly lowers its source height. Since source heights are nonnegative and there are finitely many coproducts, this process terminates with the closed coproducts in the output tree $\Delta_C^{[n]}$. The excluded configurations are exactly those that would otherwise reintroduce a closed multiplication into this tree. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 3.4, step 4.1, F3, F4, algebra]

4.3 **Stage IX: order windows and handles.** At this point the remaining closed operators are the window maps $W_a$ and handle map $H$. If any window lies after a handle in the closed chain, the pair is an inversion. The equality $W_aH=HW_a$ from [F4] exchanges the adjacent pair and lowers the finite number of such inversions by one. No other stage is now allowed to create a new inversion. Thus finitely many swaps put all colored windows before all $g$ handles, giving $H^gW_M$ in [F5]. [step 1.1, step 3.4, step 4.1, step 4.2, F4, F5, induction]

5.1 **Stages X–XI: settle the empty source and target cases.** If a closed counit remains, the connectedness and configurations already excluded in steps 4.1–4.3 force its source to be the empty word; it is then exactly the convention $\Delta_C^{[0]}=\epsilon_C$ in [F5]. If a closed unit remains, the same exclusions force its target to be empty; it is exactly $\mu_C^{[0]}=\eta_C$. Therefore the resulting connected composite is precisely $N(M)$, including when the component has no black boundary. Unbend the intervals and circles; the snakes cancel and restore the original marked source and target slots. [step 1.1, step 4.1, step 4.2, step 4.3, F4, F5, algebra]

5.2 For any connected $M$, steps 1.1–5.1 give $[M]=[N(M)]$. If $M$ and $N$ have the same listed invariants, the candidate words in [F5] coincide, so their morphisms agree. Conversely, a boundary-relative diffeomorphism preserves the component partition, genus, free-boundary colors and multiplicities, and the marked cyclic open-boundary slot/color sequences by [F1]; hence equality of morphisms implies equality of those data. For disconnected surfaces, apply the connected reduction to each component and restore the original ordering by the specified symmetry maps; symmetric monoidal interchange proves the tensor formula. [step 1.1, step 5.1, F1, F5, induction] ∎
