# A4 local replacement proposal

Draft proposal only; no native carrier was edited. Replace steps 3.1–3.4 by the following argument, inserting the finite table between 3.2 and 3.3. The table uses strict tensor notation solely to suppress the canonical associators and unitors; the insertion convention of A2 applies.

3.1 An interior critical point of index zero or two has a disk birth or death neighborhood by the Morse lemma, giving $\eta_C$ or $\epsilon_C$. An interior saddle is a rectangular band attached to two disjoint subintervals of the preceding regular level. This level is a compact one-manifold, so each affected component is a circle or an interval. The band is untwisted, because its orientation extends that of the surface. If both affected level components are circular, or both feet lie on one circular component, the usual two circle configurations give $\mu_C$ or $\Delta_C$. If an interval occurs, the only possibilities are two distinct intervals, two feet on one interval, or one interval and one circle. Indeed these exhaust whether the two feet belong to the same or different level components and whether those components have endpoints. Two feet on a single interval split it into an interval and a circle; reversing the slab gives the interval/circle merge. Two different intervals are reconnected into two intervals. A twisted reconnection would give a nonorientable band and is excluded. [F1, F5, step 2.2, construct]

3.3 Here is a boundary-relative verification of the cuts in that table. Write $X^-$ for an incoming black interval read in induced surface-boundary orientation, $U^+$ for an outgoing interval, and $F_s$ for an intervening free arc of color $s$. In rows 1–2, call the inputs $X=I_{ab},Y=I_{cd}$ and outputs $U=I_{ad},V=I_{cb}$. Cutting the one-saddle slab along the band core and extending the cut through its product-level collars produces a disk whose boundary word is
$$
U^+F_dY^-F_cV^+F_bX^-F_a.

For row 1 cut the $\Delta_{acb}$/$\mu_{acd}$ seam $I_{ac}$; for row 2 cut the $\Delta_{cbd}$/$\mu_{abd}$ seam $I_{bd}$. Each composite consists of two disks joined along that seam. Reading the remaining edges gives precisely the displayed word. The apparent crossing in each formula is a permutation of black slots; it does not identify any free edges. In rows 3–4 the slab and composite are annuli. Their boundary words are $(U^+F_bX^-F_a)$ and the incoming closed circle $Z^-$. Cut an arc from $Z$ to $F_a$ for row 3 or to $F_b$ for row 4, and denote its sides by $s,s^{-1}$. The resulting disk word is $(U^+F_bX^-F_a)sZ^-s^{-1}$, with its basepoint cyclically shifted to the indicated free arc. The cut passes through the zipper and product pieces; cutting the composite along its interval seam gives the same disk and the same pairing of the $s$ sides. Rows 5–6 are the height reversals of these annular cut models, with the original interval parameterizations retained. Matching these disk words gives diffeomorphisms relative to black collars: after rounding their nonblack corners, an orientation-preserving circle map extends across the disk by interpolating its lifted angular map to the identity near the disk center; on a prescribed boundary collar use its product extension. Regluing each pair $s,s^{-1}$ recovers the annular diffeomorphism. Thus these are actual local factorizations, including the two interval reconnections and all four interval/circle orientations. 3.4 At a free critical point the tangential restriction has a nondegenerate extremum and the inward normal derivative is nonzero by construction. In coordinates $t\ge0$ inward and $s$ tangential its chart can be taken as $f=f(p)\pm s^2\pm t$: use the inverse-function theorem in the normal variable, followed by the one-dimensional Morse coordinate on the boundary. A free minimum with positive inward normal derivative births an interval and gives $\eta_a$; a free maximum with negative inward derivative kills one and gives $\epsilon_a$. A free maximum with positive inward derivative joins the two local level ends: if they close the one interval into a circle the slab is $i_a^*$ (two corners), and otherwise they join two intervals into one, giving $\mu_{abc}$ (six corners). A free minimum with negative inward derivative is the reverse: $i_a$ has two corners and $\Delta_{abc}$ has six. The label at the extremum is the label of the single free face through that point; all other endpoint labels are carried by product collars. There is no zero-normal-derivative case in the function constructed in steps 1.2–2.3, and no assertion about a full boundary Hessian is needed. [F1, step 1.2, step 2.3, construct]

## Additional required correction to step 4.1

When constructing the regular-slab field, choose its boundary values tangent to every free face, rather than an arbitrary local field satisfying $df(X)=1$. On a free face set $X=(\partial_sf)^{-1}\partial_s$, which exists at every regular value, and extend to its collar. Near black level faces use the collar direction. A finite partition of unity preserves both the equation $df(X)=1$ and tangency to each free face. The resulting flow carries free endpoints along their own faces and therefore identifies colored regular levels, including their corner collars. An arbitrary field with $df(X)=1$ does not establish that fact.

## Scope of this proposal

These replacements address the mixed factorization, the reversed corner counts, the invalid full-Hessian inference, and the regular-slab boundary tangency. They do not independently certify the earlier collar interpolation or global range-preserving extension steps. Each row is a geometric typed cobordism equality, with $I$ replacing $A$ in algebraic notation; no assumption of a knowledgeable datum is needed here.
