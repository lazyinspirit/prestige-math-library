## Statement

Every finitely generated torsion-free abelian group $A$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb Z^r$ for some finite integer $r\ge0$. Here torsion-free means $na=0$ with $n\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$ implies $a=0$. More precisely, a fixed finite generating list yields an embedding of $A$ into a finite-rank integer lattice, and every subgroup of $\mathbb Z^r$ has a basis of at most $r$ elements. These conclusions require no form of the Axiom of Choice and no general PID factorization or structure theorem.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An abelian group $A$ generated by a finite list $a_1,\ldots,a_m$, with no nonzero integer torsion.

[F1] The rationals with their fraction operations form a field ([[thm-rat-field]]); the vector-space axioms are those of [[def-vector-space]].

[F2] Every subgroup of the additive integers is $d\mathbb Z$ for a unique $d\ge0$, with $d$ the least positive element when the subgroup is nonzero ([[lem-subgroups-of-z-are-cyclic]]). Its proof uses the integer division algorithm and the well-ordering of the natural numbers, without a choice hypothesis.



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** The data in the Statement and the fixed generating list.

1.1 On pairs $(a,n)$ with $a\in A$ and positive integer $n$, put $(a,n)\sim(b,m)$ when $ma=nb$. This is an equivalence relation. Reflexivity and symmetry are immediate. If $ma=nb$ and $pb=mc$, then $m(pa-nc)=0$, so torsion-freeness gives $pa=nc$, proving transitivity. Denote the class of $(a,n)$ by $[a,n]$ and its quotient set by $V$. Define $[a,n]+[b,m]=[ma+nb,nm]$, $-[a,n]=[-a,n]$, $0=[0,1]$, and $(r/s)[a,n]=[ra,sn]$ for integer $r$ and positive integer $s$. These are independent of representatives: if $n'a=na'$ and $m'b=mb'$, multiplying the first relation by $mm'$ and the second by $nn'$ gives the cross-multiplied equality for the two proposed sums. If also $r/s=r'/s'$, then $rs'=r's$ and $s'n'ra=snr'a'$, which gives the scalar equality. Negation and zero respect the relation as well. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 For completeness, every subgroup $H\le\mathbb Z^d$ is free on at most $d$ elements, by induction on the finite integer $d$. The case $d=0$ has the empty basis. For $d>0$, project to the first coordinate. Its image is $c\mathbb Z$ by [F2]. The kernel is identified by the remaining coordinates with a subgroup of $\mathbb Z^{d-1}$, so the induction hypothesis gives it a basis of at most $d-1$ elements. If $c=0$, this kernel is all of $H$. If $c>0$, take one element $h\in H$ with first coordinate $c$. Every $x\in H$ has first coordinate $nc$ for a unique integer $n$, and $x-nh$ is in the kernel. Hence the kernel basis together with $h$ generates $H$. It is independent, because the first coordinate of a relation first forces the coefficient of $h$ to be zero, and then independence of the kernel basis forces all remaining coefficients to be zero. This proves the induction step for every subgroup, without assuming it is finitely generated in advance. [F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The operations make $V$ a rational vector space. Addition is commutative because $ma+nb=nb+ma$, and both associations of a three-term sum have representative $[mpa+npb+nmc,nmp]$ for inputs $[a,n],[b,m],[c,p]$. Zero is an identity and the negative sums to $[0,n^2]=[0,1]$. The identities $(r/s)([a,n]+[b,m])=(r/s)[a,n]+(r/s)[b,m]$ and $(r/s+t/u)[a,n]=(r/s)[a,n]+(t/u)[a,n]$ follow by cross multiplication, reducing respectively to $r(ma+nb)=mra+nrb$ and $(ru+ts)a=rua+tsa$. Scalar associativity follows from $[rta,sun]$ for $(r/s)(t/u)[a,n]$, and scalar $1$ acts as the identity. These are all axioms in [F1]. The map $\eta:A\to V$, $\eta(a)=[a,1]$, is additive and injective, since $[a,1]=[0,1]$ forces $a=0$. Every $[a,n]$ equals $(1/n)\eta(a)$, so $V$ is spanned over $\mathbb Q$ by the fixed finite list $\eta(a_1),\ldots,\eta(a_m)$. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Process that finite spanning list in its given order, retaining a vector exactly when it does not belong to the span of those previously retained. The resulting list $b_1,\ldots,b_r$ spans $V$, because each discarded vector was already in the span of an earlier retained sublist. It is independent: in a nonzero finite relation, the last vector with nonzero coefficient would belong to the span of the earlier ones, contrary to its retention rule. Thus it is a basis, constructed by a finite recursion rather than by an arbitrary basis-selection theorem. Each $\eta(a_j)$ has unique rational coordinates in this basis. Take $D>0$ to be the product of the positive denominators of the finitely many coordinates, using $D=1$ for the empty product. For $a\in A$, define $\Phi(a)$ as $D$ times the coordinate vector of $\eta(a)$. Every $a$ is an integer linear combination of the $a_j$, so $\Phi(a)\in\mathbb Z^r$. The map $\Phi:A\to\mathbb Z^r$ is additive and injective, since $D\ne0$, coordinates are unique and $\eta$ is injective. [F1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Apply step 1.2 to $H=\Phi(A)\le\mathbb Z^r$. Its finite basis and the injective map in step 3.1 identify $A$ with $\mathbb Z^t$ for some $t\le r$, giving the asserted finite freeness (rename $t$ to $r$ in the first assertion). The only selections were a single finite generating list, finitely many unique coordinate tuples, and at most one lift at each of finitely many integer-coordinate induction stages. Existence of such finitely many witnesses does not require any choice axiom. The explicit rationalization and integer-subgroup proof used no general PID structure or factorization theorem. [step 1.2, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
