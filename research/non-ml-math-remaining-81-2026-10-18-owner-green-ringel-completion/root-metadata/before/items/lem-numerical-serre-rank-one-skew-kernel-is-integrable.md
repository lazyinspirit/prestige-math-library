## Statement

Let $I$ be finite, $A=(a_{jk})$ a symmetric generalized Cartan matrix, and $q>1$ a real number. Let $S$ be the complex associative algebra on generators $e_j$ for $j\in I$, with only the numerical quantum Serre relations
$$ \sum_{s=0}^{N+1}(-1)^s{N+1\brack s}_q e_j^{N+1-s}e_ke_j^s=0,\qquad j\ne k,\quad N=-a_{jk}. $$
Here $[r]_q=(q^r-q^{-r})/(q-q^{-1})$, divided powers are $e_j^{(n)}=e_j^n/[n]_q!$, and $S$ is graded by $\deg e_j=\alpha_j$. Fix $i\in I$ and write $m(\beta)=\sum_j a_{ij}\beta_j$ for a root degree $\beta=\sum_j\beta_j\alpha_j$.

The operators on words specified by
$$ D(e_j)=D'(e_j)=\delta_{ij},\quad D(xy)=D(x)y+q^{m(\deg x)}xD(y),\quad D'(xy)=q^{m(\deg y)}D'(x)y+xD'(y) $$
descend to $S$, commute, and are locally nilpotent. Set $B_i=\ker D$. Then
$$ S=\bigoplus_{n\geq0}e_i^{(n)}B_i. $$
For homogeneous $b\in B_i$ put
$$ R(b)=e_i b-q^{-m(\deg b)}b e_i,\qquad F(b)=-\frac{D'(b)}{q-q^{-1}},\qquad K(b)=q^{m(\deg b)}b. $$
These define an integrable type-one $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ action on $B_i$, with conventions $KRK^{-1}=q^2R$, $KFK^{-1}=q^{-2}F$, and $RF-FR=(K-K^{-1})/(q-q^{-1})$. The algebra $B_i$ is generated by the finite root strings $R^r(e_j)$, $j\ne i$, $0\leq r\leq-a_{ij}$. In particular, if $0\ne z\in S_\beta$ satisfies $D(z)=D'(z)=0$, then $m(\beta)\leq0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Exactly the symmetric numerical Serre presentation in the statement, with $q>1$. No presentation comparison, pairing nondegeneracy, triangular decomposition, Casimir operator, or ambient quantum-group realization is assumed.

[F1] The numbers $[r]_q$ are nonzero for nonzero integers $r$, and the symmetric quantum binomials satisfy $[r-s]_q{r\brack s}_q=[s+1]_q{r\brack s+1}_q$ and $\sum_{s=0}^r(-1)^s{r\brack s}_qz^{r-s}=\prod_{t=0}^{r-1}(z-q^{r-1-2t})$. The factorial definition gives the first identity and the recurrence ${r\brack s}_q=q^{-s}{r-1\brack s}_q+q^{r-s}{r-1\brack s-1}_q$. The product on the right satisfies the same recurrence for its signed coefficients: it equals $(z-q^{r-1})q^{-(r-1)}\prod_{t=0}^{r-2}(qz-q^{r-2-2t})$. Induction from $r=0$ therefore proves the second identity.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 First define $D,D'$ on the free algebra by the displayed word rules. Write $e=e_i$. Both operators lower the number of letters $e$ in a word by one, so are locally nilpotent there. For each Serre polynomial on letters $e_j,e_k$, put $r=1-a_{jk}$. If $i$ is distinct from $j,k$, its image under $D$ is zero. If $i=j$, set $a=a_{jk}=1-r$. The two terms in $D(e^{r-s}e_ke^s)$ have respective coefficients $q^{r-s-1}[r-s]_q$ and $q^{2(r-s)+a+s-1}[s]_q$ on the words with the appropriate one fewer $e$. On a fixed resulting word $e^{r-s-1}e_ke^s$, the contributions from indices $s$ and $s+1$ cancel: their common $q$-power is $q^{r-s-1}$ and their remaining coefficients cancel by [F1]. If $i=k$, removing its single letter gives $e_j^r$ times $\sum_s(-1)^s{r\brack s}_q q^{a_{ij}(r-s)}$. Symmetry gives $a_{ij}=a_{kj}=a_{jk}=1-r$, so this sum is zero by [F1]. Thus $D$ kills every generating Serre polynomial. Its skew product rule then preserves their two-sided ideal, so it descends to $S$. Reversing word order interchanges $D,D'$ and preserves that ideal, since reversal multiplies each Serre polynomial by $(-1)^r$. Hence $D'$ also descends. The degree-lowering argument proves local nilpotence on $S$. [given, F1, construct, algebra]

2.1 The operators commute. To check this without any presentation comparison, induct on word length in the free algebra. For homogeneous words $x,y$ of $i$-weights $m_x,m_y$, both $DD'(xy)$ and $D'D(xy)$ expand to $q^{m_y}DD'(x)y+D(x)D'(y)+q^{m_x+m_y-2}D'(x)D(y)+q^{m_x}xDD'(y)$, using the inductive commutation on $x,y$. They agree on generators, so agree on all words and on $S$. In particular $D'$ preserves $B_i$. The skew product rule shows that $B_i$ is a graded subalgebra. [step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For homogeneous $b\in B_i$ of $i$-weight $m$, $D(eb)=b$ and $D(be)=q^m b$, so $R(b)=eb-q^{-m}be$ belongs to $B_i$. For homogeneous $b,c\in B_i$ one has $R(bc)=R(b)c+q^{-m_b}bR(c)$. Moreover direct application of the second skew rule gives $D'(R(b))=(q^m-q^{-m})b+R(D'(b))$, since $D'(b)$ has weight $m-2$. With $F=-D'/(q-q^{-1})$, this is $RF(b)-FR(b)=[m]_q b$. The grading gives $KRK^{-1}=q^2R$ and $KFK^{-1}=q^{-2}F$, and $K$ is invertible. Thus these operators satisfy every defining relation of $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ on $B_i$. Their product rules are $R(bc)=R(b)c+K^{-1}(b)R(c)$, $F(bc)=F(b)K(c)+bF(c)$, and $K(bc)=K(b)K(c)$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 For $j\ne i$ put $N=-a_{ij}$. Iterating the definition of $R$ gives $R^r(e_j)=\sum_{s=0}^r(-1)^s q^{-s(a_{ij}+r-1)}{r\brack s}_q e^{r-s}e_je^s.$ Indeed the case $r=0$ is immediate, and passing from $r$ to $r+1$ uses $R(R^r e_j)=eR^r e_j-q^{-a_{ij}-2r}(R^r e_j)e$ and the quantum-binomial recurrence. At $r=N+1$ the extra $q$-factor is $1$, so the numerical Serre relation is exactly $R^{N+1}(e_j)=0$. Since $F(e_j)=0$ and its weight is $-N$, the commutator in step 3.1 gives by induction $F(R^r e_j)=[r]_q[N-r+1]_qR^{r-1}e_j$ for $r\geq1$. The needed scalar identity is $\sum_{t=0}^{r-1}[m+2t]_q=[r]_q[m+r-1]_q$, obtained by summing the defining geometric series. Therefore $V_j=\operatorname{span}\{R^r(e_j):0\leq r\leq N\}$ is a finite-dimensional invariant subspace with weights $-N,-N+2,\ldots,N$. [given, F1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Let $B_0$ be the subalgebra generated by all these $V_j$, $j\ne i$. It is contained in $B_i$ and stable under $R$, by its product rule and the terminating strings of step 4.1. The normal-order identity $be=q^m eb-q^mR(b)$ for homogeneous $b\in B_0$ shows that $\sum_{n\geq0}e^nB_0$ is stable under right multiplication by $e$. It is also stable under right multiplication by every $e_j$ with $j\ne i$, since these lie in $B_0$. It contains $1$, hence every word in the generators and all of $S$. Thus $S=\sum_{n\geq0}e^nB_0$. [step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 This sum is direct. For $b\in B_i$, iterating $D$ gives $D(e^n b)=q^{n-1}[n]_q e^{n-1}b$ and $D^n(e^n b)=q^{n(n-1)/2}[n]_q!b$. If $\sum_{n=0}^d e^n b_n=0$ with $b_n\in B_i$ and $b_d\ne0$, application of $D^d$ gives $q^{d(d-1)/2}[d]_q!b_d=0$, impossible by [F1]. Consequently the expression in step 5.1 is unique. Applying $D$ to that expression and using the directness just proved shows that an element in $\ker D$ has $b_n=0$ for every $n>0$. Hence $B_i=B_0$, and rescaling $e^n$ by the nonzero $[n]_q!$ gives the asserted divided-power direct sum. [F1, step 1.1, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 The action on $B_i$ is integrable. Each $V_j$ in step 4.1 is finite-dimensional with a finite set of integer weights, so its raising and lowering operators are nilpotent. The product formulas of step 3.1 make multiplication $V_{j_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{j_t}\to B_i$ equivariant for the coproducts $\Delta(R)=R\otimes1+K^{-1}\otimes R$, $\Delta(F)=F\otimes K+1\otimes F$, $\Delta(K)=K\otimes K$. These coproduct formulas satisfy the same rank-one relations, as is verified directly using $KR=q^2RK$ and $KF=q^{-2}FK$. Each finite tensor product has only finitely many weights; $R$ increases weight by two and $F$ decreases it by two, so both are nilpotent on it. Every element of $B_i=B_0$ is a finite sum of products of root-string generators, hence lies in a finite sum of such invariant images. Thus $R,F$ are locally nilpotent and $K$ acts with eigenvalues $q^m$, $m\in\mathbb Z$. No complete reducibility theorem for rank-one representations is used. [step 3.1, step 4.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 Finally let $0\ne z\in S_\beta$ satisfy $D(z)=D'(z)=0$, and put $m=m(\beta)$. Then $z\in B_i$ and $Fz=0$. By step 7.1 there is an integer $n\geq0$ with $R^n z\ne0$ and $R^{n+1}z=0$. The same commutator induction as in step 4.1 yields $F R^r z=-[r]_q[m+r-1]_q R^{r-1}z$. At $r=n+1$ it follows that $[n+1]_q[m+n]_qR^n z=0$. Both $[n+1]_q$ and $R^n z$ are nonzero, so $[m+n]_q=0$. Since $q>1$, this forces $m=-n\leq0$, as required. [F1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 7.1, algebra] ∎
