## Statement

Let $Q=p^a>1$, $\nu=\sqrt Q$, and $K=\mathbb Q(\nu)\subset\mathbb R$. Let $\mathcal H$ be the real twisted Hall algebra of the finite-dimensional nilpotent representations of a finite loopless quiver over $\mathbb F_Q$; an acyclic quiver needs no nilpotence restriction. Write $u_i=[S_i]$ for its vertex simples and $C_K$ for the $K$-algebra they generate. Let $A=\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}]$, and let $U_A^-$ be the generic divided-power integral half for the symmetric matrix $a_{ij}=2\delta_{ij}-(\text{number of arrows joining }i,j)$.

There is a surjective graded algebra map
$$\Psi:K\otimes_{A,t\mapsto\nu^{-1}}U_A^-\longrightarrow C_K,\qquad F_i\longmapsto u_i.$$
It preserves the homogeneous form $(Q-1)^{-\operatorname{ht}\alpha}B_{\nu^{-1}}$. In particular $\dim_K C_K[\alpha]$ is at most the classical PBW rank. This conclusion requires only positive Green pairing and the generic word-form identity, and does not require specialized nondegeneracy or any numerical Serre-presentation comparison.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The indicated quiver Hall algebra, its simple classes and composition algebra, the integral half and the specialization parameter.

[F1] The selected quiver category is a finite-field hereditary Hall category by [[lem-finite-loopless-quiver-nilpotent-category-is-hereditary-finitary]]. Its simple Euler values symmetrize to the displayed Cartan matrix. The Green coproduct in [[def-green-hall-completion-and-coproduct]] makes each vertex simple primitive, since it has only the zero subobject and itself. The Green form is $([M],[N])_H=\delta_{[M],[N]}/|\operatorname{Aut}M|$, so its extension to finite real sums is positive definite. Its homogeneous adjunction is compatible with the primitive braided coproduct on words in vertex simples, with twist $\nu^{(\alpha,\beta)}$, vacuum norm $1$ and simple norms $1/(Q-1)$ ([[prop-green-hall-pairing-is-nondegenerate]], [[thm-green-hall-coproduct-is-a-twisted-bialgebra-map]]). Here the primitive assertion refers to the root-graded braided Hall coproduct, before adjoining the torus.

[F2] The normalized generic half form $B_t$ has primitive tensor twist $t^{-(\alpha,\beta)}$ and simple norms $1$. Its divided-power values have only Gaussian-factorial denominators, and all ordinary-word values are Laurent polynomials ([[thm-the-negative-half-bilinear-form-is-symmetric-nondegenerate-and-integral]]).

[F3] $U_A^-$ is a graded algebra generated by divided powers $F_i^{(n)}=F_i^n/[n]_t!$, and its components are finite free of the classical PBW ranks ([[thm-integral-triangular-decomposition-for-the-lusztig-form]]).

## Proof

1.1 Let $\mathcal T$ be the free $A$-algebra on formal symbols $X_{i,n}$ with $i\in I,n\ge1$, graded by $\deg X_{i,n}=n\alpha_i$. Send $X_{i,n}$ to $F_i^{(n)}$ in the generic half, and denote its image algebra $U_A^-$ by [F3]. Define a second map on $\mathcal T$ by specializing $t\mapsto\nu^{-1}$ and sending $X_{i,n}$ to $u_i^n/[n]_{\nu^{-1}}!$ in $C_K$. All denominators are nonzero because $\nu>1$. The latter map has image $C_K$, since $X_{i,1}$ maps to $u_i$. [given, F3, algebra]

2.1 For homogeneous $P,R\in\mathcal T$ of degree $\alpha$, the Hall pairing of their second images is $(\Phi(P),\Phi(R))_H=(Q-1)^{-\operatorname{ht}\alpha}\left.B_t(P(F),R(F))\right|_{t=\nu^{-1}}$. Indeed first take ordinary words: both sides are determined by the same primitive coproduct and tensor twist, the same simple pairings and the vacuum value, by peeling a first letter through the adjunction. The twists coincide because $(\nu^{-1})^{-(\alpha,\beta)}=\nu^{(\alpha,\beta)}$, and the height factor changes each generator norm from $1$ to $1/(Q-1)$. The formula then extends to divided words by their scalar denominators and to $A$-linear combinations by bilinearity and evaluation of coefficients. All expressions are evaluable by [F2]. Unequal degrees pair to zero on both sides. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 If $P$ belongs to the kernel of $\mathcal T\to U_A^-$ and is homogeneous, then $P(F)=0$ in the generic half. Therefore $B_t(P(F),P(F))=0$ as a rational function. Step 2.1 makes $(\Phi(P),\Phi(P))_H=0$, so positivity in [F1] gives $\Phi(P)=0$. The kernel is graded, and applying this to its finitely many homogeneous components proves that the entire kernel is killed. Thus $\Phi$ factors through an $A$-linear algebra map $U_A^-\to C_K$ (with $A$ acting by the stated evaluation). The tensor universal property gives the asserted map $\Psi$, which is surjective by step 1.1. This factorization used no nondegeneracy assertion and no finite-parameter Serre-presentation theorem. Formula 2.1 passes through the factorization and gives the stated isometry. Surjectivity and [F3] give the PBW upper bound. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The source of $\Psi$ is the actual integral specialization. The numerical Serre-only algebra maps surjectively to this source; proving that additional map injective is a separate obligation. Neither this proof nor the dimension consequence silently identifies those two algebras.
