## Q: the one exact quantum supplier still requiring an actual proof

For the finite symmetric Cartan matrix A attached to Q, let T_ν(A) be the algebra over Q(ν) generated by e_i with the symmetric quantum Serre relations and no other relations, where ν=√q>1. The required supplier can be stated narrowly as:

1. The natural map T_ν(A) → Q(ν)⊗_{Z[z,z^{-1}]} U_A^+ is an isomorphism.
2. The braided form on T_ν(A), characterized by (1,1)=1, (e_i,e_j)=δ_ij/(ν²−1), homogeneous orthogonality and the Hall-compatible braided Hopf adjunction, is nondegenerate in every root degree.

The torus is not part of the nondegeneracy assertion. The symmetric Cartan matrix can be singular, so the toral bicharacter itself need not be nondegenerate.

The integral-form supplier already gives all rank conclusions once (1) is proved. Nonzero divided factorials alone do not prove (1) or (2). A valid proof may instead prove the fixed-real quantum shuffle/Nichols theorem: the kernel of the specialized shuffle map from the free algebra is exactly the Serre ideal, then identify its word form with the adjoint Hall pullback. But that kernel assertion must be proved at this ν; the existing formal shuffle theorem cannot be specialized by fiat.

I have **not reconstructed a full local proof of Q from the source arguments actually read**. In particular I am not claiming the standard quantum Gabber–Kac/non-root-of-unity theorem, a quantum Shapovalov determinant formula, or an all-parameter Serre presentation theorem as a proved supplier. Those are possible established routes, but would need their full proofs read and written, or exact existing local suppliers discovered. This is the remaining mathematical authoring gap in the embedding. It is not an open research problem.

## E: complete embedding argument once Q and current Hall inputs are proved

Pull the Green form back under the already-defined simple-generator algebra map ψ:T_ν(A)→H_Q. Its value on generators is δ_ij/(q−1), and its braided adjunction follows from Green multiplicativity. Uniqueness of the form follows by induction on total height: move the first letter of a word to the iterated coproduct on the other argument, whose smaller-height factors determine every value. Thus this pullback equals the form of Q. If x∈kerψ then it pairs to zero with every argument, so x=0 by Q(2). This proves positive-half injectivity.

The extended Borel is the crossed product of the positive half with the group algebra of Z^{Q_0}: normal-order all K's using K_i e_j K_i^{-1}=ν^{a_ij}e_j, and the inverse crossed-product construction proves uniqueness of this normal form. The extended Hall algebra likewise has its **declared free** torus normal form. Therefore ∑_α K_α x_α maps to zero only if every ψ(x_α)=0, giving Borel injectivity. Its image is exactly the torus extension of the simple-generated composition algebra, by definition. No nondegenerate toral pairing is needed.

## F1: a full finite-Dynkin classification constructor, avoiding full Kac

This supplier is only for a finite ADE forest, in arbitrary orientation, over F_q. Every such orientation is acyclic. At a sink i let

    f_M : ⊕_{a:j→i} M_j → M_i.

If M is indecomposable and M≠S_i, f_M is onto: a complement to its image in M_i would be a direct S_i summand, because there are no outgoing arrows. Define the sink reflection by replacing M_i with ker f_M and reversing the arrows incident with i, using the components of the kernel inclusion. At a source use the dual cokernel construction. The kernel/cokernel exact sequences show directly that these functors are inverse on the full subcategories where f is onto / its dual is injective. They preserve Hom spaces and indecomposability there. They are exact on short exact sequences inside these subcategories: the snake lemma applied to the onto incoming maps gives the exact kernel sequence, and the dual statement holds at a source. These are finite-dimensional matrix constructions, so hold over the finite base field and after field extension. Dimensions transform by the simple reflection s_i.

Fix an admissible sink order and repeat it. Its product C is a Coxeter element of the finite Weyl group, so has finite order h. If E is the triangular Euler matrix for that order, the direct multiplication of simple-reflection matrices gives C=−E^{-1}E^t (or its inverse according to the order convention), hence 1−C=E^{-1}(E+E^t). The finite ADE Cartan matrix E+E^t is nonsingular, so 1−C is invertible and ∑_{r=0}^{h−1}C^r=0.

An indecomposable that never becomes the simple at a reflected sink would have all reflected dimension vectors nonnegative forever. In particular C^r dim M would be nonnegative for 0≤r<h. Their sum is zero, impossible for dim M≠0. Thus every indecomposable reaches a simple in finitely many steps. Reflecting backwards proves its dimension is a real positive root and its endomorphism ring is F_q, with self-Ext¹ zero. Conversely every positive root reduces to a simple by this same sink sequence: a simple reflection takes a positive root to a negative one only for that simple root, and the Coxeter sum prevents all C^rα remaining positive. Reverse the reflection functors to construct its indecomposable. Since the sequence and terminal simple are determined by α, the indecomposable is unique.

This proves exactly the needed finite-Dynkin Gabriel result over F_q, including absolute indecomposability, with no theorem about arbitrary imaginary-root quiver representations.

## F2: full Hall generation in finite Dynkin type without Hall polynomials

Order the indecomposables M_1,…,M_m by the time at which the preceding repeated sink process kills them. Ties correspond to nonadjacent commuting sink operations and can be broken arbitrarily. If M_i dies before M_j, transport both to the moment M_i becomes S_s at a sink. The other object has onto incoming map and no S_s summand. Therefore Hom(M_j,S_s)=0 (a functional vanishing on the onto incoming image is zero), and Ext¹(S_s,M_j)=0 because a sink simple is projective. The exact reflection equivalences transport these statements back:

    Hom(M_j,M_i)=0 and Ext¹(M_i,M_j)=0 for i<j.

Each Ext¹(M_i,M_i)=0 as in F1. Hence the ordered Hall product of any multiplicities of M_1,…,M_m has only their direct sum in its support, with nonzero scalar coefficient. There are no nonsplit extensions in that order.

Induct on total dimension to prove every indecomposable class lies in the simple-generated algebra. Order vertices topologically so all arrows go from earlier to later vertices. The product [d_1 S_1]⋯[d_r S_r] has the same nonzero twisted coefficient on every representation of dimension d: its descending subrepresentation filtration supported on the successive terminal vertex sets is unique. The factors [d_i S_i] are nonzero scalar multiples of powers of [S_i]. If d is a positive root, F1 gives precisely one indecomposable M_d in this degree. Every other object in the degree is a direct sum of smaller-height indecomposables, and its class is already generated by induction and the preceding ordered split-product argument. Subtract these finitely many classes from the simple word and divide its nonzero coefficient to isolate [M_d]. All arbitrary decomposable classes are then split products. Thus the composition algebra equals the full Hall algebra.

For disconnected ADE forests tensor the separate component constructions: representations and flags split by connected components, the cross Euler form vanishes, and Hall multiplication is the ordinary tensor product. This preserves the exact componentwise finite-type scope.

## N1: complete nonfull obstructions for multiedges and cycles, requiring no Q

A degree supported on a vertex subset receives no word from vertices outside that subset. Therefore full generation of the Hall algebra would imply full generation for every full induced subquiver. This reduction also works for nilpotent categories.

If two vertices have r arrows one way and s the other, r+s≥2, use degree e_i+e_j. Nilpotence forces all scalar arrows in at least one direction to be zero, since any nonzero arrow in each direction would give a nonzero two-cycle. The orbits are the zero representation and the projective nonzero scalar vectors in either direction. Thus the Hall degree has dimension

    1+(q^r−1)/(q−1)+(q^s−1)/(q−1) > 2.

The composition degree is spanned by the two words [S_i][S_j] and [S_j][S_i]. Hence it is proper. This treats arbitrary edge multiplicities and bidirected pairs, independently of specialized quantum injectivity.

Next take an induced chordless cycle with n≥3 vertices and degree (1,…,1). For every proper subset of its n edges, set precisely those arrows nonzero. The support is a forest, hence nilpotent in any orientation; vertex scalings normalize its arrows to 1. The resulting 2^n−1 supports give pairwise nonisomorphic representations. On the other hand the simple-word span has dimension at most 2^n−2. Indeed nonadjacent letters commute; permutations modulo swapping adjacent nonneighbors correspond to acyclic orientations of the undirected cycle, by recording the relative order along each edge. A fixed acyclic orientation gives a poset; all its linear extensions are connected by swaps of adjacent incomparable elements, proved by moving the first element of one extension left in the other and inducting. There are exactly 2^n−2 acyclic orientations of an n-cycle. Thus full generation fails. This includes the cyclically oriented nilpotent case; no ordinary-representation Kac theorem is used.

## N2: explicit affine D/E Schur configurations (candidate full nonfinite-tree supplier)

A connected simple tree that is not finite ADE contains an affine D/E induced subtree. Here is the elementary graph reduction. Degree≥4 contains affine D4. Two distinct degree-3 vertices, with a shortest path between them and two outward edges at each endpoint, contain affine D_n. Otherwise there is one branch vertex with three arms; writing the arm lengths plus one as p≤q≤r, finite ADE corresponds to 1/p+1/q+1/r>1. Every remaining triple contains (3,3,3), (2,4,4), or (2,3,6), the affine E6,E7,E8 triples. A path is finite A. Along with N1 this reduces every nonfinite graph to affine D/E.

For affine D use a central chain of 2-dimensional spaces with isomorphism maps, and four one-dimensional outer spaces mapping to its endpoints. Contracting the isomorphisms leaves four lines in a 2-space. Three distinct lines force a scalar endomorphism, while the fourth varies. Their configuration space has dimension 4, one more than dim PGL_2.

For affine E use the star with arm types (p,q,r)=(3,3,3),(2,4,4),(2,3,6), central dimension N=3,4,6, and arm dimensions N/p,2N/p,…,(p−1)N/p, similarly for q,r. Orient all arms toward the center and use injections, so representations are triples of partial flags. The configuration variety F has dimension

    Σ_s N²(s−1)/(2s)=N²,

because 1/p+1/q+1/r=1. Scalar stabilizers exist, as follows.

* E6: take two opposite full flags in a 3-space. Their common endomorphisms are diagonal. Choose the third flag's first line spanned by (1,1,1); this forces every common diagonal endomorphism to be scalar.
* E7: two opposite full flags in a 4-space again force diagonal endomorphisms. Choose the remaining plane spanned by (1,1,1,1) and (λ_1,λ_2,λ_3,λ_4) with distinct λ_i over an algebraic closure. If a diagonal endomorphism preserves this plane, its diagonal entries are a+bλ_i; preserving the second vector says bλ_i² is a linear polynomial in λ_i at four distinct points, forcing b=0. Hence it is scalar.
* E8: a full flag in a 6-space and the opposite partial flag of dimensions 2,4 force endomorphisms to be block diagonal with three upper triangular 2×2 blocks. Choose three full flags ℓ_i⊂P_i in a 3-space whose common endomorphisms are scalar as in E6. Embed that 3-space into ⊕_{i=1}^3 (C³/ℓ_i) by the three quotient maps, choosing in each quotient the first coordinate line to be P_i/ℓ_i. It is injective since the three ℓ_i have zero intersection. An endomorphism of the 6-space with those three upper triangular blocks preserving this image induces an endomorphism of C³ preserving all ℓ_i and P_i, hence is scalar; the surjective quotient maps then force each block to be that same scalar. This image is the remaining 3-plane. The configuration works in every characteristic after allowing a finite field extension.

These scalar-endomorphism conditions are nonvanishing minors of the linear endomorphism equations, so they define a nonempty open Schur locus F^s over F_q. The preceding constructions show nonemptiness over the algebraic closure in every characteristic. Grassmann/flag big cells and the elementary polynomial-zero bound show |F^s(F_{q^m})| is between positive constants times q^{mN²} for large m. Every rational orbit in this locus has stabilizer F_{q^m}^×, so its orbit has order |GL_N(F_{q^m})|/(q^m−1). Thus the number of geometric isomorphism classes defined over F_{q^m} is bounded above and below by positive constants times q^m. Rational and geometric isomorphism coincide here: the intertwiner space between geometrically isomorphic Schur representations is one-dimensional, is defined over the finite field, and any nonzero rational intertwiner is invertible.

To pass from these degree-δ objects over extensions to degree-mδ objects over F_q, one must include finite-field descent, not silently count rational orbits as Frobenius orbits. A Frobenius-stable geometric Schur class descends to its field of definition: choose an intertwiner with its Frobenius transform, rescale it so its cyclic product is 1 using surjectivity of the finite-field norm, and use the fixed-vector basis of the resulting semilinear descent operator. The fixed-vector basis can be proved by the usual averaging Σ_σ c_σ σ(v) and independence of distinct field automorphisms; this is matrix Hilbert 90, not a quiver classification theorem. Therefore classes whose Frobenius orbit is shorter than m contribute at most Σ_{d|m,d<m} O(q^d)=O(mq^{m/2}). There are at least c q^m/m Frobenius orbits of exact length m for large m.

Restriction of scalars of an exact-orbit-length-m Schur representation gives an indecomposable F_q representation of dimension mδ. After extending to the algebraic closure it is the direct sum of its m pairwise nonisomorphic Frobenius conjugates. Any decomposition over F_q would partition those indecomposable summands into Frobenius-stable sets by Krull–Schmidt, impossible for a transitive orbit. Distinct Frobenius orbits give nonisomorphic restrictions by the same uniqueness. This yields at least c q^m/m indecomposables in degree mδ.

For another orientation of the affine tree, use finite source/sink reflections. Orientations of a tree are connected under such flips: inductively remove a leaf, implement a sequence on the smaller tree, and flip the leaf as needed before a flip of its neighbor so that the latter remains a source or sink. Reflections preserve Schur indecomposables and δ because s_iδ=δ, and no δ-dimensional object is a simple; they preserve the extension-field and Frobenius constructions. Every representation of a tree is nilpotent. Thus this obstruction respects every approved orientation.

Finally, **conditional only on Q(1)**, the quantum degree mδ has the already supplied classical PBW rank. The affine-root multiplicity supplier shows there is a uniform bound C on the total dimension of positive root spaces of any given total height. Hence the height generating function of U(n+) is bounded coefficientwise by ∏_{h≥1}(1−t^h)^{-C}. For t=e^{-s},

    log ∏(1−e^{-sh})^{-C}
      =C Σ_{k≥1} 1/[k(e^{sk}−1)] ≤ (C/s) Σ_{k≥1}1/k².

The coefficient of height L is therefore O(exp(C'√L)), choosing s proportional to L^{-1/2}. In degree mδ it is at most exp(C''√m), while the Hall degree already contains c q^m/m indecomposables. The Ringel map cannot be onto. No general Kac indecomposable theorem is used.

For canonical authoring it should be separated into (a) reflection and finite ADE classification/generation; (b) graph obstruction plus affine Schur configurations/descent; (c) the finite-Dynkin iff corollary. The matrix-Hilbert-90 descent paragraph should be written as its own short proved helper if no exact existing vector-space descent supplier is reused.
