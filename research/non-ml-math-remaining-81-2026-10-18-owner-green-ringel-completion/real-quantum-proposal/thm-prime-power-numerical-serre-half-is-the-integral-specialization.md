## Statement

Assume the inherited Choice hypothesis of the integral/global-basis suppliers. Let $A=(a_{ij})$ be a symmetric generalized Cartan matrix on a finite index set; finite, affine and indefinite type are all allowed. Let $\nu=\sqrt{p^a}>1$, $K=\mathbb Q(\nu)\subset\mathbb R$, and $\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}$. The algebra over $K$ on generators $E_i$ with only the symmetric numerical quantum Serre relations at parameter $\nu^\epsilon$ is canonically isomorphic, by $E_i\mapsto E_i$, to the actual Lusztig integral-half specialization $K\otimes_{\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}],t\mapsto\nu^\epsilon}U_A^+$. Its finite homogeneous components have the classical PBW ranks and the nondegenerate specialized half form. For the primitive braided tensor twist $\nu^{(\alpha,\beta)}$, the form with $G(E_i,E_j)=\delta_{ij}/(p^a-1)$ is nondegenerate and satisfies both Hopf adjunctions.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite symmetric datum, the prime-power parameter, the numerical Serre half $S_\nu$, and the actual integral specialization $H_\nu^+$.

[F1] The actual integral specialization has nondegenerate homogeneous half forms at $\nu$ and $\nu^{-1}$, with classical PBW component ranks and the stated Green normalization at the inverse parameter ([[thm-prime-power-specializations-of-the-integral-quantum-half-have-a-nondegenerate-green-form]]).

[F2] For the kernel of the map from the free negative tensor algebra to the actual half, modulo the numerical Serre ideal, a nonzero least-height residual degree $-\beta$ satisfies $(\beta,\beta)=2\operatorname{ht}\beta>0$ ([[lem-a-minimal-numerical-serre-kernel-obeys-the-quantum-casimir-constraint]]). Its quantum Casimir is constructed on the actual nondegenerate integral specialization only ([[lem-the-nondegenerate-integral-specialization-has-a-quantum-casimir]]).

[F3] In the numerical Serre half at a real parameter $\nu>1$, a nonzero homogeneous element $z$ killed by both $i$-skew derivatives has $\sum_j a_{ij}\deg_j z\leq0$ ([[lem-numerical-serre-rank-one-skew-kernel-is-integrable]]).

[F4] The negative and positive separate Serre presentations are identical after renaming generators. The two skew derivatives on words can be read from the mixed commutator, and its expression has distinct toral factors ([[lem-the-generic-borel-half-and-opposite-half-form-a-dj-crossed-double]]). The actual specialized torus and triangular factors are independent by [[lem-the-nondegenerate-integral-specialization-has-a-quantum-casimir]], step 1.1.

## Proof

1.1 There is a canonical surjection $\pi:S_\nu\to H_\nu^+$. The actual half satisfies every numerical Serre relation by evaluation of their generic polynomial identities. It is generated by the images of the $E_i$, since the divided-power generators equal $E_i^n/[n]_\nu!$ and the denominators are nonzero. Suppose for contradiction that $\ker\pi\ne0$, and choose a nonzero homogeneous $z\in\ker\pi$ of least positive height, of degree $\beta=\sum_i n_i\alpha_i$. The degree-zero map is injective, so this height is positive. Renaming $E_i$ as $F_i$ identifies this kernel with the residual kernel of [F2]; hence $(\beta,\beta)=2\operatorname{ht}\beta>0$. [given, F1, F2, F4, assume-contra, choose, algebra]

2.1 Both skew derivatives of $z$ lie in $\ker\pi$. Here is a direct justification that requires no numerical presentation comparison. For homogeneous $x$ in the actual positive half, with $i$-weight $m$, define the two word deletions $D_i,D_i'$ with product rules from [F3]. The source mixed relations give $[x,F_i]=(D_i'(x)K_i-K_i^{-1}D_i(x))/(\nu-\nu^{-1})$; it is true on a simple generator and its extension to a product follows by expanding the commutator and crossing the torus. The deletion formulas also define $D_i,D_i'$ on $S_\nu$ by [F3]. Apply the mixed identity to the word expression $z$ whose image in the actual half is zero. Independence of the toral factors $K_i,K_i^{-1}$ in [F4], after moving them to one side, forces $\pi(D_i z)=\pi(D_i'z)=0$. Each derivative lowers height, so minimality of $z$ gives $D_i z=D_i'z=0$ in $S_\nu$, for every $i$. [F3, F4, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Extend scalars from $K$ to $\mathbb C$; the nonzero vector stays nonzero because this is a field extension. Apply [F3] for every $i$ to the common-kernel vector of step 2.1. It gives $\beta(h_i)=\sum_j a_{ij}n_j\leq0$. Symmetry of the Cartan matrix and its normalization $(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)=a_{ij}$ yield $(\beta,\beta)=\sum_i n_i\beta(h_i)\leq0$. This contradicts the strictly positive value in step 1.1. Therefore the assumed kernel vanishes, and the canonical surjection is an isomorphism. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, contradiction]

4.1 The same statement holds at $\nu^{-1}$: the symmetric quantum Serre coefficients and factorials are invariant under parameter inversion, so the two numerical presentations identify by their generators. Integral bar gives a semilinear isomorphism of the integral halves under $t\mapsto t^{-1}$, fixing divided powers, and therefore a $K$-linear isomorphism between their specializations at inverse parameters. The resulting square with the two canonical maps commutes on every generator. The already established injectivity at $\nu$ consequently gives injectivity at $\nu^{-1}$. Transport [F1] across these isomorphisms: its ranks, specialized forms, primitive braided coproduct and Green normalization give all remaining assertions. No arbitrary specialization of a generic determinant was used. [F1, step 3.1, algebra, discharge-contradiction] ∎
