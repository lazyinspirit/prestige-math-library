## Statement

For symmetrizable $A$, let $\overline{\mathfrak g}$ be the quotient of $\widetilde{\mathfrak g}$ by the ideal generated by both families of Serre elements. The natural surjection $\overline{\mathfrak g}\to\mathfrak g(A)$ has zero kernel. Before this identification, finite adjoint exponentials on $\overline{\mathfrak g}$ implement simple reflections and preserve the root multiplicities of its kernel.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Serre quotient and its Q-grading, before identifying it with g(A).

[F1] The relation ideals have homogeneous generators satisfying the Casimir constraint. ([[lem-kac-moody-relation-module-embeds-in-verma-modules-and-obeys-the-casimir-constraint]]).

[F2] The Serre elements vanish in g(A). ([[lem-serre-elements-vanish-before-serre-generation]]).

[F3] The simple reflection is lambda minus its coroot coordinate times alpha_i. ([[def-simple-reflections-and-the-kac-moody-weyl-group]]).

[F4] The root form obeys the symmetrizer convention. ([[thm-invariant-bilinear-form-for-a-symmetrizable-kac-moody-algebra]]).

## Proof

1.1 F2 puts the Serre ideal $S$ inside $\mathfrak r$, yielding the surjection and kernel $\overline{\mathfrak r}=\mathfrak r/S$. Thus the Cartan embeds in $\overline{\mathfrak g}$; its other weights have one sign because it is a homogeneous quotient of $\widetilde{\mathfrak g}$. Pure multiples of a simple root are absent beyond $\pm\alpha_i$, since each free half has that property. The simple components map injectively to $\mathfrak g$, so the kernel has no simple or zero weights. [F2, given]

2.1 On $\overline{\mathfrak g}$, $D=\operatorname{ad}e_i$ is nilpotent on every $e_j$ by the defining Serre relations, on $f_j$ by $Df_j=\delta_{ij}h_i$, $D^2f_i=-2e_i$, $D^3f_i=0$, and on $h$ by $D^2h=0$. The binomial identity $D^m[x,y]=\sum_k\binom mk[D^kx,D^{m-k}y]$ proves local nilpotence on all bracket words. The same calculation holds for $\operatorname{ad}f_i$ and in $\mathfrak g$. The finite exponentials and their negative-exponent inverses preserve brackets. Their product $T_i=\exp(\operatorname{ad}f_i)\exp(-\operatorname{ad}e_i)\exp(\operatorname{ad}f_i)$ sends $(e_i,h_i,f_i)$ to $(-f_i,-h_i,-e_i)$ and fixes $\ker\alpha_i$ in the Cartan, by the three-term simple-triple expansions. Thus $T_i h=h-\alpha_i(h)h_i$, and $[h,T_ix]=(s_i\beta)(h)T_ix$ for $x$ of weight $\beta$. The quotient map commutes with these finite polynomials, so $T_i$ and its inverse preserve the kernel. [F2, F3, step 1.1]

2.2 If the positive kernel is nonzero, let $\alpha=\sum k_i\alpha_i$ have the smallest height among its nonzero weights. By F1 every element of $\mathfrak r^+_\alpha$ is a finite sum of iterated positive adjoints of constrained homogeneous generators. A generator of smaller height maps to zero in the positive kernel by minimality, as do all of its adjoints. Any surviving term at degree $\alpha$ must therefore be a generator in degree $\alpha$ itself. Consequently $(\alpha,\alpha)=2(\rho,\alpha)=2\sum_i k_i d_i>0$. [F1, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 For every $i$, step 2.1 produces a nonzero kernel vector at $s_i\alpha$. Since $\alpha$ is not simple, it has a positive coefficient at some index other than $i$; otherwise it would be a forbidden pure multiple. That coefficient is unchanged by $s_i$, so the one-sign property forces $s_i\alpha>0$. Minimality gives $\operatorname{ht}(s_i\alpha)=\operatorname{ht}(\alpha)-\alpha(h_i)\ge\operatorname{ht}(\alpha)$, hence $\alpha(h_i)\le0$. F4 now gives $(\alpha,\alpha)=\sum_i k_i d_i\alpha(h_i)\le0$, contradicting step 2.2. The positive kernel vanishes. The sign-changing involution preserves S and r and interchanges signs, so the negative kernel also vanishes. There is no zero kernel component by step 1.1. [F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2] ∎
