On the Bernstein-Gelfand-Gelfand resolution
for Kac-Moody algebras and quantized
enveloping algebras
Istv´an Heckenberger and Stefan Kolb∗
May 16, 2006
Abstract
A Bernstein-Gelfand-Gelfand resolution for arbitrary Kac-Moody
algebras and arbitrary subsets of the set of simple roots is proven.
Moreover, quantum group analogs of the Bernstein-Gelfand-Gelfand
resolution for symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebras are established. For
quantized enveloping algebras with ﬁxed deformation parameter q ∈
C \ {0} exactness is proven for all q which are not a root of unity.
1
Introduction
In this note we consider the Bernstein-Gelfand-Gelfand resolution (BGG-
resolution) of a maximal integrable highest weight module over an arbitrary
Kac-Moody algebra g and its generalization for a standard parabolic subalge-
bra pS ⊂g (which we will abbreviate BGGL-resolution). These resolutions,
which for symmetrizable g are classical results in representation theory by
now, lead to conceptual insight into the Weyl-Kac character formula and the
n−-homology results of Kostant and Garland-Lepowsky.
In the ﬁnite case the BGG-resolution was introduced in [BGG75] and ex-
tended to parabolic subalgebras by J. Lepowsky in [Lep77]. Generalizations
for symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebras were established soon after [GL76],
[RCW82]. Finally S. Kumar proved exactness of the BGG-complex for ar-
bitrary Kac-Moody algebras [Kum90] using geometric methods. The exact-
ness of the BGGL-complex for arbitrary subsets of simple roots, however,
remained open [Kum02, 9.3.19].

Here we consider two problems related to the BGG-resolution. On the
one hand we answer Kumar’s question as anticipated in [Kum90, 3.28]. On
the other hand, we are interested in analogs of both the BGG-resolution
and the BGGL-resolution for quantized enveloping algebras. Such analogs
appear in various places in the literature [Ros91], [FF92], [Mal92], [Cal93],
[Jos95, 4.5.5]. However, these presentations are either rather short, treat only
special cases, or rely on specialization arguments. The present note grew out
of the authors’ desire to obtain an easily accessible proof of the exactness of
a quantum analog of the BGGL-resolution, avoiding specialization.
The construction of the BGG-complex can be translated into the quan-
tum group setting word by word without further ado. The usual proof of the
exactness of the BGG-complex, however, relies on the standard resolution in
Lie algebra homology (cp. also [Kum02, Chapter 9.1]). This standard reso-
lution is written in terms of exterior powers of g/pS which are not available
in the quantum case (note however that the SL(N) case is treated in [Ros91]
in terms of Koszul resolutions). At this point other authors revert to special-
ization [Mal92], [Cal93]. In his book [Jos95, 4.5.5] A. Joseph also suggests
specialization or refers to the paper [GJ81] which in a more general setting
relies on more advanced homological and representation theoretic methods.
Here we give an elementary proof of the exactness of the BGG-resolution
which does not depend on the standard resolution and which also works in
the quantum group setting avoiding specialization. Hence we prove exactness
not only for ‘almost all q’ as in [Mal92, Theorem 3.3], or for all q but certain
algebraic numbers as in [Cal93, IV.2.3], but for all q ∈C \ {0} which are
not a root of unity.
The main idea is to show that the homology group
Hn(C∗) of the BGG-complex (C∗, d∗) is an integrable U(g)-module. Hence for
symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebras Hn(C∗) vanishes for n ≥1. The BGGL-
resolution (CS
∗, dS
∗) of a parabolic subalgebra pS ⊂g is then constructed
as a quotient complex of (C∗, d∗). Its exactness is veriﬁed by showing that
the kernel of the canonical surjection π : C∗→CS
∗admits a ﬁltration such
that the associated graded complex is isomorphic to a direct sum of BGG-
resolutions for the Levi factor lS of pS.
The proof of the exactness of the BGG-complex given here does not work
for nonsymmetrizable Kac-Moody algebras. However, in view of Kumar’s re-
sult [Kum90], our construction of a quotient complex establishes the BGGL-
resolution for arbitrary Kac-Moody algebras and arbitrary subsets S of the
set of simple roots.

3.4
The quantum BGGL-resolution
In this section we discuss the quantum versions of Proposition 3.4 and The-
orems 3.2 and 3.6. Let Uq(g) denote the quantized enveloping algebra of a
symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebra g. The main advantage of our approach
in this paper is that the following remarks are valid for both, the deﬁnition of
Uq(g) in [Jos95, 3.2.9] over the ﬁeld k(q), where k is a ﬁeld of characteristic
zero, and the deﬁnition in [KS97, 6.1.2] over the ﬁeld of complex numbers,
where q ∈C\{0} is not a root of unity. Note that in [KS97] the algebra Uq(g)
is deﬁned only for semisimple g. However for a symmetrizable Kac-Moody
algebra one can consider the same presentation in terms of generators and
relations. Note that the equivalence of the relations [Jos95, 3.2.9(vi)] with
the quantum Serre relations follows from [Jos95, 4.1.17] analogously to the
proof of [Kac90, Theorem 9.11].
Along the lines of Section 3.1 one obtains a sequence C∗of Uq(g)-modules
analogous to (3). To this end one has to verify that the relation
dim HomUq(g)(V w.µ, V w′.µ) = 1
if w ≤w′
holds for all µ ∈P +. This can be seen following [Jos95, 4.4.7, 4.4.15]. The
proof also holds for q ∈C \ {0} not a root of unity. Now one can take the
proof of Proposition 3.4 literally to obtain the integrability of the homology
Hn(C∗). The necessary quantum analog of Lemma 3.3 can be found in [Jos95,
4.3.5]. The proof given there works for q ∈C \ {0} not a root of unity.
Note that by [Jos95, 4.3.6(i)] which implies the second half of [Jos95,
Theorem 4.3.10] and by [Jos95, 3.4.9] the proof of Theorem 3.2 also holds for
Uq(g). Again the argument also works for q ∈C \ {0} not a root of unity.
Note that [Kum02, 9.2.14] translates into the quantum setting. Hence
one may construct quantum analogs of the complexes Ker(π∗) and CS
∗as in
Section 3.2. Finally, the proof of Theorem 3.6 also applies literally in the
quantum setting.
