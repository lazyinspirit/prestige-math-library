## Review and evidence

- The gentle-measurement proof uses the squared-overlap calculation on the span of a purification and its filtered vector, then contracts the raw trace norm under partial trace. Its support, normalization, and endpoint cases agree with the cited interfaces.
- The square-root order proof first proves inverse order for positive-definite operators and then obtains the square-root comparison through a finite spectral resolvent integral. Its limiting argument treats zero eigenvalues by regularization and establishes the improper-integral constant as finite and positive.
- The Hayashi–Nagaoka proof derives the quadratic inequality from positivity of `(A-cB)^*(A-cB)` and treats the kernel of `S+T` separately. The packing proof constructs a complete POVM, bounds each competing codeword term using the compressed average-state assumption, and applies the gentle and Hayashi–Nagaoka bounds to the transmitted term.
- The Schmidt-rank bound uses Kraus families for the encoder and decoder, bounds each composite Kraus branch through the code space, and sums its reference-side overlap bound. At `lem-it-schmidt-rank-compression-fidelity-bound` step 2.1, the omitted rank comparison closes by expanding the vector in a basis of `ran(K)`; its Schmidt support has dimension at most `rank(K)≤M`.
- The entropy-continuity proof controls each ordered eigenvalue by operator norm and applies continuity of `-t ln t` on `[0,1]`, including zero eigenvalues.
- The typical-projector proof handles the IID spectral source by its finite-alphabet AEP. For cq conditional words, it separately controls empirical averages and applies Chebyshev to independent, potentially nonidentically distributed spectral measurements. It supplies one uniform vanishing error bound over the selected typical words, along with the rank and compressed-state bounds used by the packing lemma.
- The Schumacher direct construction gives explicit CPTP encoder and decoder maps through the typical subspace and a flag; its fidelity bound tends to one. Its converse bounds the sum of the `M` largest source eigenvalues by atypical mass plus the typical eigenvalue ceiling, then invokes the Schmidt-rank bound.
- The HSW direct proof conditions the random codebook on a finite typical input set, transfers the average-state operator bound to that conditional law, and derives a vanishing expected average error. Its converse applies the measurement Holevo bound and Fano, then uses strong subadditivity to bound the output mixture entropy and finite spectral calculus to add product-state entropies. Compactness and entropy continuity justify the maximizing input law. The proof remains within a fixed finite classical alphabet and does not use a general quantum-input single-letter assertion.
