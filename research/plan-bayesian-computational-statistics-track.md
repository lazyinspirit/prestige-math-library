# Bayesian and Computational Statistics — prose scaffold

**Status: all six source dossiers integrated.** This is Category 3. Pair and
item identifiers below are proposed interfaces, not authored library content.
The proposed category directory is
`bayesian-computational-statistics/`; every B page is an examples companion
and never supplies another pair.

This category may use existing mathematics, independent probability/numerical
suppliers, and Categories 1–2. It has no direct or transitive dependency on
Category 4. Bayesian learning applications may be consumed by Category 4 after
their Category 3 interfaces are stable, but Category 3 proofs and algorithms
must stand without them. Category 1 (`MSF-*`) and Category 2 (`SME-*`) are
earlier draft interfaces; proposed dependencies on them are provisional until
the corresponding plans are reconciled. In particular, BCS owns the generic
optimization and Monte Carlo machinery needed for its own computation. It may
reuse the already published matrix factorizations and conditioning results.

Algorithms are proposed as `kind: algorithm` items with `alg-` IDs and a
`## Definition` body. Correctness, convergence, bias, and error claims are
separate result items with their own hypotheses. Statistical assumptions are
contracts, not conclusions: identifiability, correct specification, tail
conditions, mixing, and regularity must appear where used. Posterior
probability, algorithmic approximation, numerical stability, and frequentist
coverage are distinct claims.

### BCS-02 — Posterior propriety and dominated Bayes formula
**Pages:** posterior-propriety-and-dominated-bayes-formula / posterior-propriety-and-dominated-bayes-formula-examples. **Scope:** In a dominated model define $Z(y)=\int L_y(\theta)\Pi(d\theta)$ for a prior measure and measurable nonnegative likelihood. The normalized posterior is a probability exactly when $0<Z(y)<\infty$. For a proper prior and normalized likelihood kernel, Tonelli gives $\int Z(y)\nu(dy)=1$, so the posterior normalization holds on the prior-predictive full-measure set where $Z>0$; exceptional zero/infinite fibers are assigned only the existing RCD filler version, not a pointwise learned posterior. For sigma-finite improper priors, prove the finite-positive normalizer statement locally and distinguish it from a prior-predictive probability. Give flat-prior known-variance Normal mean posterior $N(\bar y,\sigma^2/n)$ for $n\ge1$; show improper Beta$(0,0)$ Binomial posterior diverges at an endpoint when $y=0$ or $y=n$. **Proof route:** Reuse the existing Bayes-kernel theorem for proper priors, Tonelli for almost-everywhere normalization, and explicit integration/completing the square for examples. Never form a quotient at zero/infinite evidence. **Examples:** valid finite evidence, prior-predictive null fiber, improper flat prior yielding proper posterior, improper Beta endpoint divergence. **Requires:** BCS-01; `MSF-04` `def-dominated-statistical-experiment`, `def-likelihood-density`; `thm-conditional-density-formula`, `thm-bayes-formula-for-dominated-kernels`, `thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces`. **Sources:** Gelman et al., *Bayesian Data Analysis*, §§1.3, 2.8; Johnson, Ott & Dogucu, *Bayes Rules!*, §§2.1.3–2.1.4, 2.3.4–2.3.6. Do not duplicate the generic proper-prior posterior-kernel theorem.

### BCS-03 — Conjugate families, Gaussian conditioning, and posterior prediction
**Pages:** conjugate-families-and-posterior-prediction / conjugate-families-and-posterior-prediction-examples. **Scope:** State the sufficient exponential-family update direction: likelihood $h(y)\exp\{\eta(\theta)^TT(y)-A(\theta)\}$ and conjugate prior kernel $\exp\{\eta(\theta)^T\chi-\nu A(\theta)\}$ update $\chi$ by $\sum_iT(y_i)$ and $\nu$ by $n$, only if prior/posterior normalizers are finite. Give exact proper Beta$(\alpha,\beta)$–Binomial$(n,p)$ update Beta$(\alpha+y,\beta+n-y)$ for $\alpha,\beta>0$; Gamma$(a,b)$ shape–rate prior with Poisson counts/exposures $x_i$ updates to Gamma$(a+\sum_i y_i,b+\sum_i x_i)$ when updated shape/rate are positive; for $Y_i\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)$, known $\sigma^2>0$, prior $\mu\sim N(\mu_0,\tau_0^2)$ with $\tau_0^2>0$, posterior variance $V_n=(\tau_0^{-2}+n\sigma^{-2})^{-1}$ and mean $V_n(\mu_0/\tau_0^2+n\bar y/\sigma^2)$; and Dirichlet$(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k)$–multinomial update $\alpha_j+y_j$ with all $\alpha_j>0$. Define only those probability laws locally needed, including Poisson and Dirichlet normalization; use iterated Beta integrals for the Dirichlet normalizer. Do not claim the converse characterization of regular exponential families. Include the reusable Gaussian conditioner: if $(X,Y)$ has mean $(a,b)$ and PSD block covariance $\begin{pmatrix}A&C\\C^T&B\end{pmatrix}$ with observed block $B\succ0$, then $X|Y=y$ is Gaussian with mean $a+CB^{-1}(y-b)$ and covariance $A-CB^{-1}C^T\succeq0$; $A$ and the conditional covariance may be singular. Exclude singular-$B$ pseudoinverse formula unless proved separately. Integrate posterior-predictive kernels against the posterior. **Proof route:** Likelihood-kernel multiplication, positive-parameter Beta/Gamma integrals, iterative Beta normalization, Gaussian residual $X-a-CB^{-1}(Y-b)$ independent of $Y$, and Tonelli for posterior prediction. **Examples:** Beta–Binomial, exposure-adjusted Gamma–Poisson, normal precision pooling, multinomial Dirichlet, Gaussian conditioning with singular state covariance and SPD observed block, posterior predictive tail versus plug-in. **Requires:** BCS-01, BCS-02; `MSF-04` `def-likelihood-density`; `MSF-05` `def-exponential-family`; `def-real-beta-integral`, `thm-real-beta-gamma-identity`, `thm-change-of-variables-for-expectation`, `thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces`, `def-multivariate-normal-law`, `lem-characteristic-function-of-a-multivariate-normal-law`. **Sources:** Gelman et al., *Bayesian Data Analysis*, 3rd ed., §§2.1, 2.4–2.6, 3.3–3.6, App. A; Johnson, Ott & Dogucu, *Bayes Rules!*, Ch. 3 §§3.1–3.5, Ch. 5 §§5.2.1–5.2.4/5.3.1–5.3.4; Bayesian-04 Särkkä App. A. The normal-model example with a textbook arithmetic typo is not reused.

### BCS-04 — Posterior prediction, credible sets, and Bayes actions
**Pages:** posterior-prediction-and-bayesian-decision / posterior-prediction-and-bayesian-decision-examples. **Scope:** For posterior $\Pi_y$ and future sampling kernel $Q_\theta$, define posterior predictive $Q_y^{pred}(A)=\int Q_\theta(A)\Pi_y(d\theta)$. Under finite second moments prove $E(\tilde Y|y)=E_{\Pi_y}E(\tilde Y|\theta)$ and $Var(\tilde Y|y)=E_{\Pi_y}Var(\tilde Y|\theta)+Var_{\Pi_y}E(\tilde Y|\theta)$. Define credible sets by posterior mass; central quantiles are one option and HPD sets need a density and may be disconnected/nonunique. Given action $a$ and loss $L(\theta,a)$ define posterior risk and a Bayes action only when risk is defined and a measurable minimizer is attained. Under finite posterior second moment squared loss uniquely gives posterior mean; absolute loss gives any median; finite-class zero–one loss gives maximal posterior mass/mode. Do not assert pointwise zero–one MAP optimality for a continuous parameter. Posterior credibility is not a frequentist coverage theorem. **Proof route:** Tonelli/conditional integration for prediction; expand conditional second moments; squared-loss posterior mean follows from the checked L2 projection theorem; median and finite-class actions follow by direct loss comparison; use MSF-03 only under its measurable attained-minimizer contract. **Examples:** predictive variance decomposition, asymmetric loss, nonunique posterior median, continuous density mode without point mass, credible interval with nonnominal frequentist coverage. **Requires:** BCS-01, BCS-02; `MSF-03` `def-loss-function-and-risk`; `thm-conditional-expectation-is-the-l2-orthogonal-projection`, `thm-conditional-integration-through-a-regular-conditional-law`, `thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces`. **Sources:** Gelman et al., *Bayesian Data Analysis*, §§1.3, 2.5, 9.1; Johnson, Ott & Dogucu, *Bayes Rules!*, Ch. 8 §§8.1–8.3, §16.5. Neither source gives generic arbitrary-loss minimizer existence.

### BCS-05 — Hierarchical Bayes and partial pooling
**Pages:** hierarchical-bayesian-models-and-partial-pooling / hierarchical-bayesian-models-and-partial-pooling-examples. **Scope:** Define group model with conditionally independent observations $Y_j|\theta_j\sim f_j$, $\theta_j|\eta\stackrel{iid}{\sim}G_\eta$ and proper hyperprior $\eta\sim\Pi_\eta$; state joint posterior factorization only on a positive finite evidence fiber. For known-variance normal random effects with $s_j^2>0$ and $\tau^2>0$, derive $\theta_j|\mu,\tau,y_j\sim N(\hat\theta_j,V_j)$ where $\hat\theta_j=(\bar y_j/s_j^2+\mu/\tau^2)/(1/s_j^2+1/\tau^2)$ and $V_j=(1/s_j^2+1/\tau^2)^{-1}$. This is conditional shrinkage; when hyperparameters are unknown, integrate their posterior rather than plug them in silently. Distinguish current-group and new-group prediction. Finite exchangeability is a modeling assumption and does not imply a mixture-of-iid representation; no de Finetti theorem is claimed here. **Proof route:** Product-kernel factorization and BCS-03 Normal–Normal update conditional on hyperparameters; check posterior normalizer. **Examples:** complete/no/partial pooling; full posterior versus plug-in hyperparameters; improper variance hyperprior causing impropriety. **Requires:** BCS-01, BCS-02, BCS-03; `MSF-01` `def-parametric-statistical-model`; `def-conditional-independence-given-a-sigma-algebra`, `thm-independent-random-elements-have-product-joint-law`, `thm-existence-of-regular-conditional-distributions-for-standard-borel-targets`, `thm-conditional-integration-through-a-regular-conditional-law`. **Sources:** Gelman et al., *Bayesian Data Analysis*, §§5.1–5.4, 5.7; Johnson, Ott & Dogucu, *Bayes Rules!*, Chs. 15–16. Improper scale-prior examples are warnings, not generic prior recommendations.

### BCS-54 — Statistical, resampling, simulation, and numerical error ledger

### BCS-55 — Multilevel Monte Carlo and cost theorem
**Pages:** multilevel-monte-carlo / multilevel-monte-carlo-examples. **Scope:** Define geometrically refined levels $h_l=M^{-l}T$, coupled corrections and telescoping expectation. Require independent level estimators and source rates: bias $O(h_L^\alpha)$, $\alpha\ge1/2$; correction variance $O(N_l^{-1}h_l^\beta)$, $\beta>0$; cost $O(N_lh_l^{-1})$. State complexity for MSE $\epsilon^2$: $O(\epsilon^{-2})$ if $\beta>1$, $O(\epsilon^{-2}\log^2\epsilon)$ if $\beta=1$, and $O(\epsilon^{-2-(1-\beta)/\alpha})$ if $\beta<1$. **Proof route:** Telescoping identity, variance allocation $N_l\propto\sqrt{V_l/C_l}$, bias/variance budget and geometric sums. Separate proven abstract theorem from Euler-SDE application rates and adaptive bias stopping heuristic. **Examples:** source-covered Euler rates; discontinuous payoff; missing weak/strong rate. **Requires:** BCS-25, BCS-54; planned Cat3 `thm-euler-maruyama-weak-and-strong-error-rates-under-source-conditions` only for a concrete SDE application; otherwise retain abstract MLMC rates. **Sources:** Giles (2008), “Multilevel Monte Carlo Path Simulation,” Theorem 3.1 and full proof, *Operations Research* 56(3), 607–617, PDF pp. 1–11. SDE rates are cited externally; adaptive stopping is heuristic.
