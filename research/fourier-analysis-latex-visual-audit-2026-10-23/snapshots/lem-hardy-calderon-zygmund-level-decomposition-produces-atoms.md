## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Fix the admissible kernel $\varphi$ defining the $H^p$ quasi-norm. Let $n\ge1$, $0<p\le1$, and let
$f\in H^p(\mathbb R^n)$ with the space and quasi-norm of
[[def-real-hardy-space-by-a-radial-maximal-function]]. Put
$s=\lfloor n(1/p-1)\rfloor$, let $K\ge n/p$ be an integer and let
$\Phi,\psi,\widetilde\psi$ be the Calderon reproducing pair of flatness $K$
from [[lem-calderon-reproducing-formula-for-the-hardy-decomposition]]. Fix an
admissible grand-maximal order
$N\ge\max\{N_0(n,p,\varphi),\,n,\,\lfloor n/p\rfloor+1\}$. For
$r\in\mathbb Z$ put $\Omega_r=\{x:M_Nf(x)>2^r\}$. Then there are a countable
family $(a_B)$ of $(p,\infty,s)$-atoms and positive coefficients $\lambda_B$
such that

1. $\sum_B\lambda_B^p\le C\|f\|_{H^p}^p$ with
   $C=C(n,p,N,K,\varphi,\Phi)<\infty$;
2. $f=\sum_B\lambda_Ba_B$ with convergence in $\mathcal S'(\mathbb R^n)$;
3. each atom $a_B$ is supported in a fixed dilation $B^\star=7B$ of a ball $B=B(\xi,\rho(\xi)/2)$ of the Whitney-type ball cover of $\Omega_r$ for the corresponding level $r$, with $\|a_B\|_{L^\infty}\le|B^\star|^{-1/p}$ and vanishing moments through order $s$, and the balls $B$ cover $\Omega_r$ with multiplicity at most $K(n)=785^n$;
4. the centres and radii are those of [[lem-whitney-type-ball-cover-of-a-proper-open-set]] applied to each nonempty $\Omega_r$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice, $n\ge1$, $0<p\le1$, admissible $N\ge\max(N_0(n,p,\varphi),\max(n,\lfloor n/p\rfloor+1))$, $f\in H^p$, $s=\lfloor n(1/p-1)\rfloor$, an integer $K\ge n/p$, and the fixed reproducing pair $\Phi,\psi,\widetilde\psi$ with $\psi_k(x)=2^{kn}\psi(2^kx)$, $\widetilde\psi_k(x)=2^{kn}\widetilde\psi(2^kx)$.

[F1] Maximal characterisation: $\|M_Nf\|_{L^p}\le C_0(n,p,N,\varphi)\|f\|_{H^p}$ and $M_Nf$ is Borel and lower semicontinuous, so each $\Omega_r$ is open ([[thm-maximal-function-characterisations-of-real-hardy-spaces]], [[lem-smooth-maximal-functions-of-tempered-distributions-are-borel-measurable]]).

[F2] Layer-cake bound: $\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z}2^{pr}|\Omega_r|\le C_p\|M_Nf\|_{L^p}^p$, since $\sum_r2^{pr}|\Omega_r|=\sum_\nu|\Omega_\nu\setminus\Omega_{\nu+1}|\sum_{r\le\nu}2^{pr}\le C_p\sum_\nu2^{p\nu}|\Omega_\nu\setminus\Omega_{\nu+1}|\le C_p\int(M_Nf)^p$ ([[lem-smooth-maximal-functions-of-tempered-distributions-are-borel-measurable]], [[def-real-hardy-space-by-a-radial-maximal-function]]).

[F3] Reproducing identity: $f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\psi_k*\widetilde\psi_k*f$ in $\mathcal S'$, $\operatorname{supp}\psi_k\subseteq B(0,2^{-k})$, and $\int\psi(x)x^\alpha dx=0$ for $|\alpha|\le K$; the identity and its justification are in [[lem-calderon-reproducing-formula-for-the-hardy-decomposition]].

[F4] For every $k\in\mathbb Z$ one has $\|\widetilde\psi_k*f\|_{L^\infty}\le c2^{kn/p}\|f\|_{H^p}$: indeed $|\widetilde\psi_k*f(x)|^p\le\inf_{|x-y|\le2^{-k}}\sup_{|y-z|\le2^{-k}}|\widetilde\psi_k*f(z)|^p\le c\inf_{|x-y|\le2^{-k}}M_Nf(y)^p\le c|B(x,2^{-k})|^{-1}\int_{B(x,2^{-k})}(M_Nf)^p\le c2^{kn}\|M_Nf\|_p^p$ ([[def-grand-maximal-test-class-of-order-n]], [F1]).

[F5] Whitney-type ball cover of each nonempty $\Omega_r$: points $\xi_j$, radii $\rho_j=\rho(\xi_j)$, pairwise disjoint balls $B(\xi_j,\rho_j/8)$ with $\bigcup_jB(\xi_j,\rho_j/2)=\Omega_r$, comparison $\rho_j/7\le\rho_\nu\le7\rho_j$ for meeting $3\rho/4$-balls and bounded overlap $K(n)$ of the dilated balls $B(\xi_j,3\rho_j/4)$ ([[lem-whitney-type-ball-cover-of-a-proper-open-set]]).

[F6] Every $x$ with $M_Nf(x)>2^r$ lies in $\Omega_r$; the sets $\Omega_r$ decrease in $r$ and are open, so $\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,\Omega_r^c)$ is continuous and positive on $\Omega_r$ ([[lem-smooth-maximal-functions-of-tempered-distributions-are-borel-measurable]]).

[F7] Moment-tail estimate: if $\Psi\in C_c^\infty$ with $\operatorname{supp}\Psi\subseteq B(0,1)$ and $\int\Psi(z)z^\alpha dz=0$ for $|\alpha|\le K$, then for every $\chi\in\mathcal S$ and $\sigma>n$ there is $c_{\sigma}$ with $|\int_{\mathbb R^n}\Psi_k(x-y)\chi(x)dx|\le c_\sigma2^{-k(K+1)}(1+|y|)^{-\sigma}$ for $k\ge0$. This is the Taylor estimate (using [[thm-multivariable-taylor-formula-with-lagrange-remainder]] separately on real and imaginary parts): expand $\chi$ about $y$, use the vanishing moments, bound the remainder by $C2^{-k(K+1)}(1+|y|)^{-\sigma}$ with the Schwartz decay of $\chi$ and of its derivatives ([[def-schwartz-space-and-its-seminorms]], [[lem-schwartz-dilations-preserve-schwartz-space]]).

[F8] Ball volumes: with $\omega_n=|B(0,1)|$, one has $0<\omega_n<\infty$ and $|B(x,R)|=\omega_nR^n$ for $R>0$, by [[lem-euclidean-balls-have-positive-finite-lebesgue-measure]] and [[thm-lebesgue-measure-under-dilations-and-reflections]].



[F9] A tempered distribution $u$ satisfying $|\langle u,\chi\rangle|\le D\|\chi\|_1$ for all Schwartz tests is represented by an $L^\infty$ function bounded by $D$. Indeed, density of complex $C_c^\infty$ in $L^1$ ([[thm-complex-finite-simple-and-smooth-compact-support-density-for-finite-p]]) uniquely extends $u$ to a bounded complex-linear functional on $L^1$. Restrict it to $L^2([-m,m]^n)$, extending inputs by zero; $\|h\|_1\le(2m)^{n/2}\|h\|_2$. Apply [[thm-arbitrary-measure-duality-for-l-p-when-one-less-p-less-infinity]] at exponent two separately to the real and imaginary parts on real inputs, then combine by complex linearity, to obtain a complex density $g_m$. Testing with $\overline{g_m}/|g_m|$ times indicators of measurable subsets (zero where $g_m=0$) gives $\int_E|g_m|\le D|E|$, hence $|g_m|\le D$ a.e. Uniqueness of the $L^2$ densities makes them agree on nested cubes; choosing representatives under Countable Choice and discarding their countable union of disagreement null sets glues a globally bounded density. Density and truncation recover the functional on all of $L^1$. All hypotheses of these suppliers hold under the assumed Countable Choice.

**Proof technique:** level-set decomposition, telescoping cancellation, Whitney covering and atom normalisation.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** constructive.

1.1 The level sets and scale shells. If $f=0$, the empty atomic family gives the conclusion, so assume $f\ne0$. Put $M=M_Nf$ and $\Omega_r=\{M>2^r\}$. By [F1], [F2], and layer cake (summing nonnegative indicators using [[thm-monotone-convergence-for-the-integral]]), $$\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z}2^{pr}|\Omega_r|\le C_p\|M\|_p^p\le C\|f\|_{H^p}^p,$$ so every $\Omega_r$ has finite measure. For $k\in\mathbb Z$ set $a_k=2^{-k+1}$, $$U^r_k=\{x\in\Omega_r:d_r(x)>a_k\},\quad V^r_k=\{x\in\Omega_{r+1}:d_{r+1}(x)>a_k\},\quad E^k_r=U^r_k\setminus V^r_k,$$ where $d_r(x)=\operatorname{dist}(x,\Omega_r^c)$. The sets $E^k_r$ are disjoint in $r$. At a fixed $k$, they cover every $y$ with $0<M(y)<\infty$ for which $\widetilde\psi_k*f(y)\ne0$: indeed, with $\eta=4^n\widetilde\psi(4\,\cdot)$ and $t_k=2^{-k+2}$ one has $\eta_{t_k}=\widetilde\psi_k$. After division by $P_N(\eta)$ this is a test in $\mathcal F_N$, so a nonzero value at $y$ gives a positive lower bound for $M(z)$ for all $|z-y|\le t_k$. Choosing $r$ below that bound puts $B(y,t_k)\subset\Omega_r$, hence $y\in U^r_k$. Since $M\in L^p$ is finite a.e., for a.e. such $y$ it is outside $\Omega_r$ for all sufficiently large $r$; the nested sets $U^r_k$ therefore give a unique last index $r$ with $y\in E^k_r$. Points where $M=\infty$ form a null set, and where $M=0$ the displayed convolution is zero by the grand-maximal definition. Thus the $E^k_r$ partition the integrand $\widetilde\psi_k*f$ up to a null set, which is all the integral and distributional identities below require. Finally, if $\Omega_r\ne\emptyset$, choose $s_r$ so negative that $\omega_n a_k^n>|\Omega_r|$ for $k<s_r$. Then $U^r_k=E^k_r=\emptyset$ for $k<s_r$, since each point of $U^r_k$ would force a ball of radius $a_k$ inside $\Omega_r$. If $\Omega_r=\emptyset$, set all its pieces to zero. [F1, F2, F4, F6, F8, algebra, construct]

2.1 Local bounds and the two-boundary scale split. For every measurable $A\subseteq E^k_r$, $$\left\|\int_A\psi_k(\,\cdot-y)(\widetilde\psi_k*f)(y)\,dy\right\|_\infty\le C2^r,\qquad \left\|\int_A\Phi_m(\,\cdot-y)(\Phi_m*f)(y)\,dy\right\|_\infty\le C2^r\quad(m=k,k+1). \tag{1}$$ To see this, if $y\in E^k_r$ is outside $\Omega_{r+1}$, use $z=y$. Otherwise $d_{r+1}(y)\le a_k<d_r(y)$; choose $z\in\Omega_{r+1}^c$ with $|z-y|<d_{r+1}(y)+\epsilon<d_r(y)$ and $|z-y|<2a_k$. Then $z\in\Omega_r\setminus\Omega_{r+1}$ and $M(z)\le2^{r+1}$. Each inner kernel here is exactly a grand-maximal test dilation at cone scale $2a_k$: use $4^n\widetilde\psi(4\cdot)$ for $\widetilde\psi_k$, $4^n\Phi(4\cdot)$ for $\Phi_k$, and $8^n\Phi(8\cdot)$ for $\Phi_{k+1}$. The cone definition of $M_N$ therefore gives $|\widetilde\psi_k*f(y)|+|\Phi_m*f(y)|\le C2^r$. Multiplication by the fixed $L^1$ norms of the outer kernels $\psi_k$ and $\Phi_m$ proves (1). For $s_r\le q\le m$ write $$F_{r,q,m}(x)=\sum_{k=q}^{m}\int_{E^k_r}\psi_k(x-y)(\widetilde\psi_k*f)(y)\,dy.$$ Its $L^\infty$ norm is at most $C2^r$, uniformly in $r,q,m$. Here are the scale details. If a summand can be nonzero at $x$, then $x\in\Omega_r$. Choose $\ell$ with $x\in U^r_{\ell+1}\setminus U^r_\ell$, so $2^{-\ell}<d_r(x)\le2^{-\ell+1}$. If also $x\in\Omega_{r+1}$, choose $\nu$ with $x\in V^r_{\nu+1}\setminus V^r_\nu$; since $d_r(x)\ge d_{r+1}(x)$, $\nu\ge\ell$. For $\nu\ge\ell+3$, the Lipschitz property of distance gives $$B(x,2^{-k})\cap E^k_r=\emptyset\ (k\le\ell-1\text{ or }k\ge\nu+2),\qquad B(x,2^{-k})\subset E^k_r\ (\ell+2\le k\le\nu-1).$$ Thus, in any finite interval $[q,m]$, at most four non-full boundary terms remain; the consecutive full terms $k=u,\ldots,v$ telescope to $$\sum_{k=u}^{v}\psi_k*\widetilde\psi_k*f(x)=\Phi_{v+1}*\Phi_{v+1}*f(x)-\Phi_u*\Phi_u*f(x).$$ The support balls of the two endpoint convolutions lie in $E^v_r$ and $E^u_r$, respectively, so (1) bounds both endpoints by $C2^r$. If $\ell\le\nu\le\ell+2$, only the at most four indices $\ell,\ldots,\nu+1$ can contribute, and (1) applies directly. If $x\in\Omega_r\setminus\Omega_{r+1}$, choose $\ell$ as above. There is no interaction for $k\le\ell-1$, while $B(x,2^{-k})\subset E^k_r$ for every $k\ge\ell+2$. The latter tail in any finite interval telescopes, with both endpoint convolutions localized in the corresponding $E^k_r$ and bounded by (1); only the two scales $\ell,\ell+1$ are left. These cases prove the uniform bound for every finite partial sum. For $\chi\in\mathcal S$, the moment estimate [F7] and [F4] give, for $k\ge0$, $$\sum_r|\langle g_{rk},\chi\rangle|\le C_\chi2^{-k(K+1-n/p)}\|f\|_{H^p},\quad g_{rk}(x)=\int_{E^k_r}\psi_k(x-y)(\widetilde\psi_k*f)(y)\,dy.$$ For $k<0$, the same sum is at most $C_\chi2^{kn/p}\|f\|_{H^p}$, using $\|\psi_k\|_1=\|\psi\|_1$ and $\|\widetilde\psi_k*f\|_\infty\le C2^{kn/p}\|f\|_{H^p}$. The first bound is summable because $K\ge n/p$, and for each fixed $r$ only finitely many negative $k$ occur because $k\ge s_r$. Thus $F_r=\sum_{k\ge s_r}g_{rk}$ converges in $\mathcal S'$. The uniform bounds for finite partial sums imply $|\langle F_r,\chi\rangle|\le C2^r\|\chi\|_1$; by the bounded-distribution representation [F9], $F_r$ is represented by an $L^\infty$ function with $\|F_r\|_\infty\le C2^r$. No pointwise convergence of the infinite scale series is needed. [step 1.1, F3, F4, F6, F7, F9, algebra]

3.1 Whitney localization, overlap, and atoms. Fix a nonempty $\Omega_r$ and take the cover [F5], writing $B_j=B(\xi_j,\rho_j/2)$ and $W_j=B(\xi_j,3\rho_j/4)$. Then $B_j\subset W_j$, so the $B_j$ cover $\Omega_r$ with multiplicity at most the exact constant $K(n)=785^n$ from [F5]. For each $j$, let $k_0(j)$ be the unique integer with $2^{-k_0(j)}<\rho_j\le2^{-k_0(j)+1}$. For $k\ge s_r$, put $N_{j,k}=\{y:\operatorname{dist}(y,B_j)<a_k\}$ and $$A^k_{rj}=\begin{cases}E^k_r\cap N_{j,k},&k\ge k_0(j),\\ \emptyset,&k<k_0(j).\end{cases}$$ Put $R^k_{rj}=A^k_{rj}\setminus\bigcup_{m>j}A^k_{rm}$ and $g_{rjk}(x)=\int_{R^k_{rj}}\psi_k(x-y)(\widetilde\psi_k*f)(y)\,dy$. These sets are disjoint and cover $E^k_r$: if $y\in E^k_r$, choose a covering ball $B_j$ containing $y$. Then $d_r(y)>a_k=2\cdot2^{-k}$ and $d_r(y)\le d_r(\xi_j)+|y-\xi_j|<3\rho_j/2$, so $2^{-k}<3\rho_j/4<\rho_j$ and $k\ge k_0(j)$. Hence $y\in A^k_{rj}$, and the greatest eligible index assigns it to exactly one $R^k_{rj}$, once the finite overlap below is established. The actual localization neighborhoods $N_{j,k}$ with $k\ge k_0(j)$ have uniformly finite overlap. For such an index $2^{-k}<\rho_j$, so $a_k<2\rho_j$ and $|y-\xi_j|<\rho_j/2+a_k<5\rho_j/2$ whenever $y\in N_{j,k}$. If two eligible neighborhoods meet, the 1-Lipschitz property of $d_r$ gives, for $R=\max(\rho_j,\rho_m)$ and $r_0=\min(\rho_j,\rho_m)$, $$R-r_0\le|\xi_j-\xi_m|<\tfrac12(R+r_0)+4r_0,$$ so $R<11r_0$. For any finite collection of eligible neighborhoods containing one point $y$, the disjoint balls $B(\xi_j,\rho_j/8)$ therefore have radii at least $R/88$ and lie in $B(y,21R/8)$. Comparing volumes gives multiplicity at most $231^n$ at every fixed scale. If $A^k_{rj}\ne\emptyset$, then $k\ge k_0(j)$ and the support of $\psi_k*[(\widetilde\psi_k*f)1_{R^k_{rj}}]$ lies in the ball of radius $\rho_j/2+3\cdot2^{-k}<7\rho_j/2$ about $\xi_j$, hence in $7B_j$. It remains to prove a uniform $L^\infty$ estimate for $F_{B_j}:=\sum_{k\ge s_r}\int_{R^k_{rj}}\psi_k(\cdot-y)(\widetilde\psi_k*f)(y)\,dy$. We use the following explicit localization estimate. For any set $S\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ and integers $s_r\le q\le m$, replacing $E^k_r$ by $E^k_r\cap\{y:\operatorname{dist}(y,S)<a_k\}$ in $F_{r,q,m}$ still gives an $L^\infty$ norm at most $C2^r$. If $S=\emptyset$, the sum is zero. If $S\ne\emptyset$ and $\operatorname{dist}(x,S)=0$, all kernel-support balls $B(x,2^{-k})$ lie in these neighborhoods and the sum is the full bounded partial sum. If $\delta=\operatorname{dist}(x,S)>0$, choose $\ell$ with $2^{-\ell}<\delta\le2^{-\ell+1}$. Then $B(x,2^{-k})$ lies in the neighborhood for $k\le\ell-1$ and is disjoint from it for $k\ge\ell+2$, leaving only two boundary scales, each bounded by (1). The same estimate holds for the infinite tail: the preceding absolute pairing bounds give distributional convergence, and the uniform finite-sum bounds pass to the limit by the same bounded-distribution representation [F9] used in step 2.1. Let $J_j$ be all later indices $m>j$ for which $W_m\cap W_j\ne\emptyset$. By [F5], $\#J_j\le K(n)$ and $\rho_m\ge\rho_j/7$. Put $\rho_* =\min(\{\rho_j/7\}\cup\{\rho_m:m\in J_j\})=\rho_j/7$, let $k_1$ be the least integer with $a_{k_1}<\rho_*/4$, and set $S=\bigcup_{m\in J_j}B_m$. Then $\rho_j/7\le\rho_*\le\rho_j$, while $\rho_j/2\le2^{-k_0}<\rho_j$ and $\rho_*/16\le2^{-k_1}<\rho_*/8$. Hence $k_0<k_1$ and $2^{k_1-k_0}=2^{-k_0}/2^{-k_1}<112<2^7$, so $k_1-k_0\le7$. For every $k\ge k_1$ each neighborhood $N_{m,k}$ with $m\in J_j\cup\{j\}$ lies in $W_m$. To exclude other later indices without presupposing their radii, a meeting point gives $|\xi_m-\xi_j|<(\rho_m+\rho_j)/2+2a_k$. Here $a_k<\rho_*/4\le\rho_j/28$, so this distance is less than $3(\rho_m+\rho_j)/4$. Thus $W_m\cap W_j\ne\emptyset$ and $m\in J_j$. All indices in $J_j$ are eligible at these scales because $2^{-k}<\rho_*/8\le\rho_m/8$. Consequently the high-scale part is exactly the difference of the two localized sums associated with $S\cup B_j$ and $S$, each bounded by the localization estimate. There are at most seven lower scales $k_0\le k<k_1$, each bounded by (1). Thus $\|F_{B_j}\|_\infty\le C_\sharp2^r$ uniformly in $r,j$. Absolute convergence against Schwartz tests follows by summing [F7] over the disjoint sets $R^k_{rj}$; for $k<0$ the bound is $C_\chi2^{kn/p}\|f\|_{H^p}$ as above. Each summand is compactly supported in $7B_j$ and has zero moments through order $K$ by the moments of $\psi$. Choose the representative of $F_{B_j}$ to vanish outside its compact support. That support lies in the closed ball of radius $\rho_j/2+3\cdot2^{-k_0(j)}<7\rho_j/2$, so it is contained in the open ball $7B_j$. For fixed $j,k$, $R^k_{rj}\subset N_{j,k}$ is bounded, has finite measure, and $\widetilde\psi_k*f$ is bounded by [F4], so absolute integrability and [[thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces]] applied to the absolute values justify each moment integral. The distributional limit is supported in $7B_j$ and has the same moments, by testing against a smooth compactly supported test equal to each monomial on a neighborhood of the closed ball $\overline{7B_j}$ (construct the cutoff from [[def-the-standard-smooth-step-function]]). Finally, the sets $R^k_{rj}$ partition the $E^k_r$, and the absolute pairing bounds summed over all $r,j,k$ show $$f=\sum_{r,j}F_{B_j}\quad\hbox{in }\mathcal S'.$$ Indeed, for $k\ge0$ the full sum of absolute pairings is bounded by $C_\chi2^{-k(K+1-n/p)}\|f\|_{H^p}$ using [F7]; for $k<0$ it is bounded by $C_\chi2^{kn/p}\|f\|_{H^p}$. Sum in $k$ and apply the Calderon reproducing identity [F3]. [step 2.1, F3, F4, F5, F7, F8, F9, algebra]

4.1 Atom normalization. Choose an axis-parallel cube $Q_j$ centered at $\xi_j$ with side length $7\rho_j$; it contains $7B_j$, and $|Q_j|/|7B_j|=2^n/\omega_n$. Enlarge $C_\sharp$ if needed so that $C_\sharp\ge C_0(2^n/\omega_n)^{1/p}$, where $C_0$ is the constant in the preceding $L^\infty$ estimate. Put $$a_{rj}=C_\sharp^{-1}|7B_j|^{-1/p}2^{-r}F_{B_j},\qquad \lambda_{rj}=C_\sharp|7B_j|^{1/p}2^r.$$ Then $\operatorname{supp}a_{rj}\subseteq7B_j$, $\|a_{rj}\|_\infty\le|7B_j|^{-1/p}$, and the choice of $C_\sharp$ gives $\|a_{rj}\|_\infty\le|Q_j|^{-1/p}$. Its moments vanish through order $K$, hence through $s$, because $s=\lfloor n(1/p-1)\rfloor<n/p\le K$. Thus $a_{rj}$ is a $(p,\infty,s)$-atom in the cube-supported convention of [[def-hp-atom-with-moment-order]], and $f=\sum_{r,j}\lambda_{rj}a_{rj}$ in $\mathcal S'$. [step 3.1, F8, algebra]

5.1 Coefficient bound. Since $|7B_j|=7^n|B_j|$ and $B_j\subset W_j$ with multiplicity at most $K(n)$, $$\sum_{r,j}\lambda_{rj}^p=C_\sharp^p7^n\sum_r2^{pr}\sum_j|B_j|\le C_\sharp^p7^nK(n)\sum_r2^{pr}|\Omega_r|\le C\|f\|_{H^p}^p$$ by the layer-cake estimate and [F1]. This proves the coefficient bound. [step 4.1, F1, F2, F5, F8, algebra]

6.1 Conclusion. The zero case was handled at the start. For $f\ne0$, steps 3.1 and 4.1 produce the atoms and coefficients, step 5.1 gives the $\ell^p$ estimate, and the absolutely convergent distributional sum in step 3.1 equals $f$. Hence all four claims hold. [step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, discharge-construct] ∎
