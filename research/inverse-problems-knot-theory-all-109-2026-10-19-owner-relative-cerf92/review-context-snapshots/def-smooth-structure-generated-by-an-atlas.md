## Definition

Let $\mathcal A$ be a smooth atlas on a topological manifold $M$
([[def-smooth-atlas]]). The **smooth structure generated by** $\mathcal A$ is
the set

$$[\mathcal A]:=\bigl\{\,(U,\varphi):(U,\varphi)\text{ is a chart on }M\text{ and }(U,\varphi)\text{ is smoothly compatible with every chart of }\mathcal A\,\bigr\}.$$

By [[lem-all-charts-compatible-with-a-smooth-atlas-form-a-smooth-atlas]],
$[\mathcal A]$ is again a smooth atlas on $M$, and since every chart of
$\mathcal A$ is compatible with itself and with the other charts of
$\mathcal A$, the inclusion $\mathcal A\subseteq[\mathcal A]$ holds. A chart is
said to be **compatible with the atlas** $\mathcal A$ exactly when it belongs to
$[\mathcal A]$. In the notation $[\mathcal A]$ the brackets name the whole
generated structure, not a quotient; distinct atlases may generate the same
structure, and
[[thm-each-smooth-atlas-is-contained-in-a-unique-maximal-smooth-atlas]] records
exactly when that happens.

## Remarks

- **The construction is formulaic, not a choice.** $[\mathcal A]$ is the single
  family of all charts satisfying the stated compatibility condition; no
  representative is selected from it, and no choice principle is used.

- **The bracket is the maximal atlas.** By its definition $[\mathcal A]$ already
  contains every chart compatible with $\mathcal A$, so nothing can be added to
  it without losing pairwise compatibility; the maximal-atlas theorem states
  this precisely.
