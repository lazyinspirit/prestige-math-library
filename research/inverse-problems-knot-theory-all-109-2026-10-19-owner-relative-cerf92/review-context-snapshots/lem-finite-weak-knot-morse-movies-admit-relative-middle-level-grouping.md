## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Supply on a compact closed oriented three-manifold a stationary-ended function/field family $(f_t,X_t)$ from the finite weak-field construction, including its ordinary Morse models, split product folds, uniform broken-trajectory compactness and fixed-fold incidence exclusions. Its fixed region is a neighborhood of an invariant marked knot $K$, carrying only its fixed minima of value zero and maxima of value one. Suppose $0\le f_t\le1$ and all unprotected critical values have a uniform positive margin from zero and one. At both stationary endpoints suppose every unprotected critical point of index at most one has value below $1/2$, and every one of index at least two has value above $1/2$.

There is a smooth stationary-ended function family $h_t:M\to[0,1]$ with the SAME fields $X_t$, the same spatial critical curve, indices, folds and same-index connecting-trajectory events, equal to $f_t$ near the parameter endpoints and on one fixed smaller neighborhood of $K$. Every regular critical point of index at most one is below $1/2$ and every one of index at least two is above $1/2$. Near a fold of indices $(0,1)$ the whole product model has inflection value $1/5$ and lies below $1/2$; near a fold of indices $(2,3)$ it has inflection value $4/5$ and lies above $1/2$. Near an index-$(1,2)$ fold it is the original product model plus one scalar translation placing its inflection value at $1/2$; on its occupied side the index-one point is below $1/2$ and the index-two point above it. Thus $1/2$ is a regular level except at these finitely many index-$(1,2)$ fold parameters.

All spatial critical models are retained up to parameter-dependent constant value shifts, and all fields and their connecting orbits are retained. No assertion of finiteness of OTHER new critical-value equalities is made or needed. This is function grouping relative to the knot; translation into a based attaching-disk movie is a separate assertion.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual finite weak-field family and stationary grouped endpoint data in the Statement.

[F1] The weak-field construction has finitely many fold and same-index events, no index-increasing trajectories, no same-index event in a whole birth neighborhood, no same-index event at an original value crossing, the product fold models and uniform broken-trajectory compactness. Its regular branch models are normalized Morse models, except for the explicitly split scalar positive-speed interpolation in compact annuli near folds. ([[lem-cc-relative-descending-paths-have-finite-same-index-events]], full Proof1.1–7.1)

[F2] Two ordinary adjacent clusters without connecting trajectories can have their heights independently reassigned, smoothly on a local parameter interval, with the same field and with one smaller knot neighborhood fixed. ([[lem-two-regular-critical-clusters-rearrange-parametrically-relative-a-knot]])

[F3] A fold block and an ordinary cluster inverted relative to index order have the same local-parameter reassignment property, with ONE scalar translation on the whole fold block. ([[lem-a-simple-fold-block-rearranges-relative-an-inverted-ordinary-cluster]])

[F4] Finite compact-set cutoffs exist, including finite smooth partitions of a compact parameter interval by normalizing a finite sum of supported bumps. ([[lem-manifold-bump-for-a-compact-set-inside-an-open-set]])

[A1] Countable Choice is inherited from the supplied weak-field and rearrangement constructions. Only finitely many local operations and a finite parameter cover are used below. ([[def-countable-choice]])

## Proof

**Given:** The data in the Statement.

1.1 Specify target values on the finite critical curve. Put $(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3)=(1/10,3/10,7/10,9/10)$ and $e=1/100$. Away from small disjoint birth intervals give an ordinary index-$i$ point $p$ target $a_p=c_i+ef_t(p)$. Near each stationary endpoint interpolate this with $f_t(p)$ by a common scalar cutoff, making the target exactly $f_t(p)$ near that endpoint; take the cutoff transition entirely inside its stationary interval. This keeps the middle-level separation there and keeps the target difference for any orbit a positive combination of its old height difference and its index difference. At a fold choose its interval inside the whole no-same-index-event neighborhood of [F1], away from all other events and stationary ends. Write its inflection value $b(t)=g(0,t)$ and set $B_0=1/5$, $B_1=1/2$, $B_2=4/5$. On the two newborn branches replace the targets by $B_\lambda+f_t(p)-b(t)$ on a smaller fold interval, blending with their ordinary targets by one common smooth parameter cutoff on the larger interval. Shrink that interval so $|f_t(p)-b(t)|<1/100$ on both branches. These target prescriptions are smooth along ordinary branches and are the SAME scalar translation on a whole neighborhood of the fold. [F1, F4, given, construct]

2.1 Every actual nonconstant connecting orbit strictly respects these target heights. Away from a fold interval, a positive index drop gives positive difference of the $c_i$ and a nonnegative contribution of the old strict height gap; an equal-index orbit keeps $e$ times its old positive gap. The endpoint blend has the same property. Inside a fold interval there are no same-index orbits. If $\Delta=f_t(p_{\rm upper})-f_t(p_{\rm lower})>0$ and $\chi$ is the birth cutoff, their target difference is $(1-\chi)(c_{\lambda+1}-c_\lambda+e\Delta)+\chi\Delta>0$. Against an old point of strictly lower or higher index the numerical ranges give strict separation: the fold ranges are within $1/100$ of $1/5,1/2,4/5$, between the adjacent old index ranges $(c_i,c_i+1/100)$. Convex blending preserves the necessary inequalities. The protected minima and maxima stay at zero and one. Target equalities can therefore occur only between points having no connecting orbit. Moreover each target-equality group has no connection between any of its members on a whole local parameter neighborhood: for old equal-index points equality is an original critical-value equality, disjoint from the finite same-index event list; any equality involving a newborn occurs inside a no-same-index birth interval. Shrink the neighborhood to avoid those finitely many event parameters. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For any finite group isolated in one small band and having no mutual connecting orbit on this parameter neighborhood, perform the following assignment. In particular it applies to a tied target group from step 2.1. Their joint truncated critical-trajectory sets are pairwise disjoint compact sets, so choose lower-exit bumps $b_j$ with disjoint supports, equal to one near the exits of the $j$th set, and zero near every other set and the knot. Extend them along orbits, setting $\beta_j=1$ on its own critical-trajectory set and zero on the others. The compactness/exit argument of [F2] proves joint smoothness and orbit constancy. They satisfy $\beta_j\ge0$ and $\sum\beta_j\le1$, since their lower-level supports are disjoint; no-exit attracting sets have the same constant assignments on their open basins. For each point choose an increasing interval map $\phi_j$ translating its current value to its assigned smooth target, fixed near the band ends. Then $$f_{\rm new}=(1-\sum_j\beta_j)f+\sum_j\beta_j\phi_j(f)$$ has the exact targets, unchanged critical models up to translations, and positive derivative factor along $X$. It equals $f$ near the knot and the band ends. The same formula supplies arbitrary tiny initial tie splitting when the current critical values coincide. There is no requirement that the actual target order inside a tied group remain fixed as the parameter varies. [F2, F4, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 First realize the targets locally about a parameter that is not a fold. If there is no unprotected critical point, take the original function. Initial critical-value equalities can be split by arbitrary tiny distinct heights because their points have no connecting orbit on a small neighborhood. To see the neighborhood assertion, a sequence of connections with old height gap tending to zero would cross a fixed exterior Morse annulus and hence incur a fixed positive height loss, as in [F1]'s broken-compactness proof, a contradiction. The finite multi-center exit-cutoff construction of step 3.1 supplies the splitting if more than two points share a value. Now order the distinct current critical heights at the central parameter, and order the target groups there, refining ties arbitrarily. An adjacent inversion has no orbit from its higher current point to its lower one: such an orbit would violate step 2.1's target ordering; within a tied target group the stronger no-connection-neighborhood conclusion applies. The reverse direction is impossible by current strict descent. Apply [F2] to swap these two heights within a small regular-ended band containing just that pair. Each swap lowers the finite inversion number. After finitely many swaps the critical points are ordered by their target groups; shrink the parameter neighborhood to retain all chosen bands and orders. The same argument works on the compact regular scalar-interpolation annuli of [F1]: there the only changed kernel equation is $\dot v=\pm d_t(v)v$ with $d_t$ bounded above and below by positive constants. Its integral gives the same uniform exponential entrance/exit bounds, and the invariant coordinate disks and finite-time hitting maps are unchanged. Thus the compactness and constant-exit-cutoff proof of [F2] applies directly there; no exact normalization through a fold is being assumed. [F1, F2, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 Realize the final numerical locations without moving the knot. After the sorting in step 4.1 choose distinct intermediate node values in disjoint small intervals about the target GROUP values, retaining the chosen within-group order. A smooth increasing interval map moves all current nodes to these intermediate nodes and is a translation near each. Explicitly move the ordered nodes linearly to their ordered destinations, and integrate the sum of scalar velocities equal to each node displacement on disjoint moving neighborhoods; compactness supplies a uniform positive separation and the flows used in [F2] give this map. Implement it relatively to $K$ as follows. In a regular-ended band containing all these ordinary critical points, take the union of all their truncated critical trajectories, including all pieces of broken chains. It is compact jointly near the central parameter by [F1]; a break adds another included critical trajectory, and there are finitely many strictly decreasing critical heights. This union is disjoint from $K$ by invariance, since no point of $K$ has an end at one of these critical points in this band. Choose a lower-exit cutoff equal to one near all its exits and zero near the knot intersections, and extend along orbits, as in [F2]. It is a smooth orbit-constant $\alpha$, one near all these critical models and zero near a common smaller knot neighborhood. Constancy of the exit cutoff and broken compactness prove smoothness at critical-ended trajectories, including an attracting basin with no lower exit. Replace $f$ by $(1-\alpha)f+\alpha\phi(f)$. Its derivative along $X$ is the positive factor $1-\alpha+\alpha\phi'(f)$ times $df(X)$. It has exactly the prescribed intermediate shifts and is unchanged near $K$ and the band ends. [F1, F2, F4, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 At a fold parameter treat its entire small product block as one ordered element between indices $\lambda$ and $\lambda+1$. Its target is $B_\lambda$, strictly between the old target index groups, and all other critical values are distinct at this parameter by the supplied event list. Sort the finitely many ordinary points and this block. Ordinary inversions use step 4.1. A block/ordinary inversion is precisely one of [F3]'s cases: an index $i\le\lambda$ point currently above the block, or an index $i\ge\lambda+1$ point currently below it. Apply [F3] to exchange them; its weak-section exclusions supply the required no-connection assertion even at the fold. Shrink the block and parameter interval finitely as needed to keep its height interval disjoint from the other nodes. After sorting, the simultaneous interval-node flow of step 5.1 can be chosen to be a TRANSLATION on the entire fold height interval: use a velocity constant on that small moving interval and separate velocities near the other nodes. The source and target gaps are positive, so their linear interpolations keep those intervals disjoint. For its knot-relative implementation include the closed saturation of the fold block together with ALL ordinary critical trajectories in step 5.1's compact set. Broken limits remain in this union; it is disjoint from $K$ by invariance and [F1]. The orbit cutoff is exactly one on a neighborhood of the whole fold block, so no singular fold hitting-time differentiability is asserted. The resulting local function is exactly $f_t+B_\lambda-b(t)$ there and has all the specified ordinary targets. [F1, F2, F3, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct]

7.1 Cover the parameter interval by finitely many such local realization intervals. Choose smaller fold intervals on which only the fold realizations of step 6.1 are used, and use regular intervals with closures away from those fold parameters for the rest. Include stationary endpoint intervals where the local realization is simply the original function, and keep all other supports away from smaller endpoint intervals. Take a finite smooth partition of unity in the parameter subordinate to this cover and average the local functions. At every noncritical point each local function has negative $X_t$ derivative, so the average does too. At every ordinary critical point ALL local functions are the original function plus the SAME specified target shift in a neighborhood of that point; their average has that exact critical germ. On every smaller fold block they likewise have the same translation $B_\lambda-b(t)$, so preserve its critical curve, fold and product model. The finite intersection of the local protected neighborhoods contains one fixed smaller neighborhood of compact $K$, on which every function is unchanged. The endpoint average equals the original stationary functions. Each local function has values in $[0,1]$ because its increasing interval maps preserve the band ends, so their average does as well. [F4, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 3.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 The target ranges in step 1.1 put every index-at-most-one point below $1/2$ and every index-at-least-two point above it, including on the birth transition annuli. On the smaller $(1,2)$ fold block, reflect its scalar coordinate if needed so $g_{uuu}>0$. On the occupied side $g_u(0,t)<0$ and its two roots satisfy $u_-<0<u_+$. The integrals of $g_u$ from zero to those roots show $g(u_-,t)>g(0,t)>g(u_+,t)$; the upper point has index two and the lower point index one. Translation to inflection value $1/2$ therefore gives the strict asserted straddling. For the other two fold indices their whole sufficiently small block lies within $1/100$ of $1/5$ or $4/5$. The middle level consequently has no critical point except the actual $(1,2)$ fold points at their finitely many parameters. The fields never changed, so all their connecting orbits and finite same-index events are exactly retained. Other new value equalities do not affect this conclusion and are not asserted finite. This establishes the stated grouping under Countable Choice, with no based disk-movie inference. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 7.1, algebra] ∎
