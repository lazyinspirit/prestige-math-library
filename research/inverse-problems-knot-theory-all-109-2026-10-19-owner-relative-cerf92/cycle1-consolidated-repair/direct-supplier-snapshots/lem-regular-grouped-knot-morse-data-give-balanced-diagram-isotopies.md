## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. On $S^3$ supply a smooth Morse function $f$ and a strictly descending field $X$ with the ordinary product critical models of the finite weak-field construction. A fixed neighborhood of an invariant oriented marked knot $K$ carries its protected minima of value zero and maxima of value one, one on each alternating arc, and its marked crossings are exactly the regular level $\Sigma=f^{-1}(1/2)\cap K$. All other critical points of index at most one are below $1/2$ and those of index at least two above it. Suppose no trajectory increases index and no same-index connecting trajectory exists. Scalar positive-speed regular product models from the fold interpolation are allowed. Let $k$ be the number of protected minima and maxima, with $k\ge1$.

Then $\Sigma$ is a connected Heegaard surface. The truncated stable disks of its lower index-one points and the truncated unstable disks of its upper index-two points give extended compression systems. Each lower cut ball contains one protected knot arc or is empty, with exactly $k$ occupied balls and $e_0$ empty balls, where $e_0$ is the number of unprotected minima. The analogous upper system has $k$ occupied and $e_3$ empty balls. The occupied arcs have simultaneous disjoint bridge disks missing all disk faces. If $h$ is the genus of $\Sigma$, the system sizes are $h+k+e_0-1$ and $h+k+e_3-1$. Rooted-forest deletion gives balanced systems of size $h+k-1$ on each side with the original ordered marks, oriented arcs and pairing.

For a smooth family of these data over a closed regular parameter interval, with no fold or same-index event, the extended disks vary smoothly after parameterized local critical-model identifications. After ambient transport of the middle levels fixed near $K$, and continuously transported forest choices, the balanced diagrams differ only by finitely many curve isotopies avoiding all marks. Arbitrary alternative endpoint forest choices are related by finitely many marked disk slides. This is a regular-interval assertion; a same-index event or a birth requires its own actual local translation.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The grouped function, actual local field models, protected knot data, and, in the family clause, a closed interval with no exceptional trajectory or fold.

[F1] Adjacent ordinary clusters with no connecting trajectory can be rearranged smoothly in a local parameter, with the field and a smaller knot neighborhood fixed; its proof constructs the required increasing interval maps and orbit cutoffs. ([[lem-two-regular-critical-clusters-rearrange-parametrically-relative-a-knot]])

[F2] The actual local quadratic handle has core $v=0$ and cocore $u=0$, with relative comparisons preserving those coordinate disks and regular complementary collars. Prescribed framed bands glue along those collars. ([[lem-local-morse-sublevel-pair-is-a-handle-pair]], [[lem-gluing-handle-morse-models-along-collars]])

[F3] An extended system with one actual trivial arc per occupied ball and empty other balls admits rooted-forest reductions to balanced systems; different forests give finite disk slides avoiding the marks and actual arcs. ([[lem-empty-cut-ball-births-erase-to-balanced-disk-slides]])

[F4] Finite-time regular-level and collar holonomies depend smoothly on the parameter, preserve marked fibers when their fields do, and give marked curve isotopies. Supported time-dependent fields have smooth global evolution. ([[lem-neat-marked-morse-endpoints-glue-across-a-heegaard-surface]], [[thm-compactly-supported-time-dependent-vector-fields-have-global-evolution-on-a-compact-time-interval]])

[A1] Countable Choice is inherited from these suppliers. All handle and graph operations below are finite. ([[def-countable-choice]])

## Proof

**Given:** The data in the Statement.

1.1 We may use an auxiliary function with the SAME field and middle level, ordering all lower minima below all lower index-one points, and dually all upper index-two points below all maxima. Indeed an inversion between lower indices zero and one has no connecting orbit in either direction: the only height-allowed direction would increase index. Apply [F1] finitely to adjacent inversions inside regular bands strictly below $1/2$. The inversion number decreases. An increasing interval reparametrization inside that half assigns separated target ranges, equal to translations near the critical nodes, using [F1]'s scalar node-moving flows. Make it knot-relative by the orbit cutoff equal to one near all the finitely many critical-trajectory sets and zero near the knot; the union is compact after including its broken pieces and is disjoint from invariant $K$, whose endpoints are the protected extrema outside those regular bands. The identical dual procedure works strictly above $1/2$. Thus the middle level and its neighborhood, field, actual stable/unstable sets and protected knot are unchanged. In a family this procedure is local in the parameter; use the SAME targets on the finite critical branches and average finitely many local realizations with a time partition. Descent is convex, and all critical germs have the same translations, so this gives the needed smooth auxiliary family. On regular scalar-interpolation models the equation $\dot v=\pm a(v)v$, $a>0$, has the same invariant coordinate disks and uniform exponential estimates on compact regular intervals; replacing $a$ by its normalized signed constant in a smaller local model retains those disks and their full exterior stable/unstable sets. Thus these models supply the same local handles, without normalizing through a fold. [A1, F1, given, construct]

2.1 Choose a regular value between the lower zero- and one-handle heights. Its lower sublevel is a disjoint union of standard minimum balls: expand their small quadratic balls along regular trajectories. No other critical point lies in any such component before its boundary. Every lower index-one unstable core then reaches this lower level at its two feet. The hitting is finite and the feet are distinct: an infinite limit would end at an index-one point in the intervening band, a prohibited same-index connection, while an actual intersection of two cores would give the same orbit and hence such a connection by uniqueness. Choose disjoint small framed attaching disks about these finitely many feet. [F2]'s local quadratic bands, transported through the regular complement, give a finite one-handle presentation of the actual lower sublevel $U=f^{-1}[0,1/2]$. At each index-one point its stable set in the model is exactly the cocore plane $u=0$. Its full disk extends upwards to $\Sigma$: an unbounded reverse hitting sequence could only break at another index-one point, since all index-two and higher critical points are above $\Sigma$; this is excluded. Thus its disk and boundary circle are compact and smooth. Different stable disks are disjoint because a point has only one forward limiting critical point. The relative local comparison [F2] identifies them with the actual cocores, including their boundary collars. This proves that cutting them leaves the minimum balls with half-one-handle caps attached along their feet. Each such cap is an absorbable boundary disk collar, so each cut component is a ball. [F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 The knot avoids all these stable disks: every lower knot half-arc descends to its protected minimum, never to an index-one point. Its two branches at that minimum are opposite radial rays in the protected exact quadratic chart. Each occupied zero-handle therefore contains exactly the corresponding one arc; ordinary minima contain none. Supply an actual bridge disk as follows. On a small protected minimum sphere the two knot points are antipodal. Choose a great semicircle between them missing the finitely many pulled-back one-handle feet. Such a choice exists because each nonpolar foot excludes only finitely many meridian angles; no foot is a pole, since its core would then coincide with the knot trajectory by uniqueness. Choose the attaching disk radii small enough to miss this entire semicircle. Its flat radial half-disk in the minimum ball is a smooth bridge witness; transport its outer collar through the regular complement, avoiding those feet disks. The half-handle caps do not meet it. This gives a simultaneous bridge system, since different occupied balls are disjoint. The ordered pairing is exactly the fixed knot pairing, unchanged on its protected tube. The time-reversed argument gives the upper index-two cocores and its $k$ occupied and $e_3$ empty balls. [F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 The lower handlebody is connected. Handles of index at least two have connected attaching regions and cannot join two components of the preceding sublevel, whereas attaching them all completes the connected manifold $S^3$. Thus its zero/one-handle graph is connected. A connected collection of balls and one-handles has connected boundary: a handle joining two components joins their boundary surfaces, and a handle with both ends on one component adds a genus without disconnecting its boundary. Therefore $\Sigma$ is connected. The upper handlebody is connected by the same reversed argument. For $k+e_0$ balls and $n_1$ one-handles, a spanning-tree set of $k+e_0-1$ joining handles merges the balls to one ball; every remaining handle adds one to the genus. Hence $h=n_1-(k+e_0)+1$. Dually $h=n_2-(k+e_3)+1$. Apply [F3] to their actual cut-ball/arc data from step3.1, deleting rooted forests with $e_0$ and $e_3$ edges. Both retained systems have exactly $h+k-1$ disks and exactly one paired knot arc in every ball. [F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Over a closed parameter interval without folds or same-index events, the critical labels and their product charts vary smoothly. For the stable-disk boundaries at the middle level, hitting times are uniformly bounded: a sequence with unbounded transit has a broken limit through a lower index-one point and would give a same-index connection, exactly as in step2.1. The compact local crossing circles and finite-time flow therefore parametrize the full boundary circles smoothly, and the local disks and their annular transports vary smoothly as well. The upper assertion is dual. The finite bridge-foot choices of step3.1 can be made on a local parameter neighborhood and then on a finite cover; existence of bridge witnesses at every slice is sufficient, and no globally fixed meridian angle is asserted. The dual graphs are transported by these labelled disk families, so a chosen rooted forest is transported on the whole interval. Its reduced balanced disks therefore give smooth disjoint curve-system families. [F2, F3, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 Identify the middle levels by integrating $V_t=-(\partial_tf_t)\operatorname{grad}f_t/|\operatorname{grad}f_t|^2$ on a regular neighborhood of their compact union, with a cutoff equal to one there. Its parameter derivative identity preserves the level, as in [F4]. It is zero near $K$, because the family is fixed there, so this ambient transport fixes all marks and knot germs. In the identified surface the balanced attaching curves from step5.1 form a smooth isotopy of disjoint systems avoiding those marks. Realize it by finitely many one-curve isotopies: on a sufficiently small parameter interval each curve stays in its own annular neighborhood disjoint from every other curve and every mark; its nearby slices are graphs in that annulus and interpolate through embedded graphs. Move the curves one at a time there. Compactness of the parameter interval gives finitely many such subintervals. Each operation is a permitted curve isotopy. If a prescribed endpoint reduction uses a different forest, [F3] gives the additional finite marked disk-slide sequence there. All assertions are therefore consequences of the actual regular flow and cut-ball construction; no same-index-event or birth translation has been inferred. [F3, F4, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct] ∎
