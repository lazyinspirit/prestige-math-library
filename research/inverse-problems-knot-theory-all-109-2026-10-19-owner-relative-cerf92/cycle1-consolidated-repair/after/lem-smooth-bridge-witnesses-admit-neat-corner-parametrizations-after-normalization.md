## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let a smooth marked knot cross a fixed smooth splitting surface by side membership as in the marked-crossing normalization lemma, permitting tangential crossings. Supply pairwise disjoint bridge witnesses on each side, disjoint from the specified compression disks. Each witness is the image of a smooth rank-two embedding of a closed planar Jordan disk whose boundary is smooth except possibly for transverse corners at its two marked endpoints. Its two smooth boundary arcs are the knot arc and an auxiliary arc on the splitting surface, and its remaining points are interior to that side. Thus an ordinary smooth disk at a mark is permitted, including collinear boundary tangents; an initial right-corner parametrization is NOT required.

After an arbitrarily localized smooth knot normalization fixing all marks and retaining the same side membership and compression systems, there are new simultaneous bridge witnesses, agreeing with the old ones outside those marked neighborhoods, with supplied smooth neat parametrizations by $H=\{(u,v):u^2+v^2\le1,v\ge0\}$. Their semicircles are the normalized knot arcs and their bottom edges lie in the fixed splitting surface. Their auxiliary surface arcs may change near the marks. The normalized knot is ambient isotopic to the original oriented knot, fixing the marks and with support in the same neighborhoods. The final witnesses may be chosen disjoint from one another on each side and from the compression disks.

This statement is in the smooth embedded-disk category just specified, not the category of arbitrary wild topological disks. It does not claim a smooth rank-two family on ONE fixed corner domain through an initially smooth or degenerate boundary point, and it does not claim that an ambient extension preserves the splitting surface.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The fixed splitting, smooth knot and actual parametrized smooth or already-cornered bridge disk witnesses in the Statement.

[F1] The normal-graph knot isotopy and its supported ambient velocity extension use only the smooth knot and its side signs, not an initial corner parametrization of its witnesses. ([[lem-marked-knot-crossings-can-be-neatened-in-a-fixed-splitting]], the independent knot construction in Proof1.1–2.1 and4.1)

[F2] A bridge half-disk for a NEAT arc can be made transverse along its bottom edge by choosing a new witness, with boundary fixed. ([[lem-bridge-half-disks-can-be-neatened-while-preserving-their-arcs]])

[F3] Smooth planar Jordan regions are smooth disks; orientation-preserving boundary maps extend with prescribed boundary collars. The complete proof also compares rounded corner choices in graph charts. ([[lem-smooth-jordan-disk-boundary-maps-extend-to-diffeomorphisms]])

[F4] Smooth local inverse maps and compact-set cutoffs exist. ([[lem-choice-free-smooth-inverse-function-theorem-in-euclidean-space]], [[lem-manifold-bump-for-a-compact-set-inside-an-open-set]])

[A1] Countable Choice is inherited from these suppliers; only finitely many corners, charts and disk replacements occur. ([[def-countable-choice]])

## Proof

**Given:** The data in the Statement.

1.1 Use one ambient chart at each mark, with splitting surface $r=0$ and oriented knot parameter $u$, to perform the SAME global knot displacement $r\mapsto r+\epsilon\sigma u\chi(u)$ of [F1]. Supports are disjoint and avoid the compression disks and every nonincident witness. The side sign is unchanged and the new normal derivative is nonzero for $\epsilon>0$. At a previously transverse corner its incident witnesses already have ordinary corner charts, so the explicit $\epsilon v h$ extension of the normalization lemma applies there. We must prepare the ordinary smooth-boundary endpoints for which no such corner chart exists. At such an endpoint the smooth boundary of the witness has the knot arc and the auxiliary arc as its two halves. The auxiliary arc lies in $r=0$, so their common tangent is tangent to that surface; in particular the knot is tangential there. Choose the tangential coordinate $u$ regular along that tangent, and flatten the tangential knot projection by the other tangential coordinate. Thus the knot has coordinates $(u,0,r_a(u))$. In each side use its inward normal $r$ and its inward arc parameter $u\ge0$; the prescribed change is then $r_a(u)+\epsilon u$ near the endpoint, the two sides still being restrictions of the one global knot displacement. [F1, F4, given, construct]

2.1 First suppose the witness surface plane at that endpoint is transverse to the splitting surface. Projection to $(u,r)$ is then nonsingular on its surface germ: its tangent contains the nonzero $u$ direction and a vector with nonzero normal component. By [F4] write that germ as $y=Y(u,r)$, with $Y$ smooth on a full open planar neighborhood. Near the mark replace it by the graph $$Y_\epsilon(u,r)=Y(u,r-\epsilon u).$$ On the NEW knot boundary $r=r_a(u)+\epsilon u$ it has $y=Y(u,r_a(u))=0$, exactly the prescribed normalized knot. On its new auxiliary edge $r=0$ it has $y=Y(u,-\epsilon u)$, so that edge lies on the fixed splitting surface and is free to change. The source domain is bounded by those two new planar boundary arcs, with the same outside continuation as the old domain. At the mark their tangents are independent: the auxiliary edge has normal component zero and the normalized knot has a nonzero one. It is therefore a genuine planar corner. Choose its same interior side as before; the graph parametrization is embedded, and points with $r>0$ lie in the correct piece. Away from this smaller corner graph, extend the prescribed boundary values along their ordinary planar collars and patch with the old graph by cutoffs, equal to it near the outer patch edge. A graph stays embedded under this interpolation; its fixed $(u,r)$ coordinates enforce the side condition independently of $Y$. This constructs a local witness replacement, not an impossible diffeomorphism of the initial collinear and final independent boundary tangents. [F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 Suppose instead the witness plane is the splitting tangent plane. Projection to its two tangential coordinates is nonsingular, so the germ is $r=R(u,y)$ with $DR(0,0)=0$, and the old knot edge is $y=0,u\ge0$. Alter only the auxiliary planar boundary edge near the endpoint so it has an independent tangent there. An explicit choice is as follows. Parametrize that old edge by its regular tangential $u$ coordinate, with $u=-s$ for its inward parameter $s\ge0$ at an ordinary smooth-boundary endpoint. Replace this near zero by $u=-s^2/d$, joined monotonically to its old parametrization away from zero, and replace its $y$ component by its old smooth graph value plus $s\eta(s)$, with $\eta=1$ near zero and zero outside a small interval. Its $u$ coordinate is strictly decreasing for $s>0$, so it is embedded, misses the positive $u$ knot edge, and joins the old edge smoothly outside that interval. Its tangent at the mark is the $y$ direction. It bounds with the unchanged knot projection a planar corner domain. Near the mark it is $u=\varphi(y)$ with smooth $\varphi(0)=\varphi'(0)=0$. Set $q=u-\varphi(y)$, so the new domain has $q\ge0,y\ge0$ near the corner. In those coordinates define $$R_\epsilon(q,y)=R(q+\varphi(y),y)-R(\varphi(y),y)+\epsilon q.$$ Shrink the patch until $|\partial_uR|<\epsilon/2$. Then $R_\epsilon\ge\epsilon q/2$, strictly positive for $q>0$. On the new auxiliary edge $q=0$ it is zero; on the knot edge $y=0$ it is exactly $r_a(q)+\epsilon q$. Its graph is a smooth rank-two embedding with a genuine corner and the required boundary. The same construction with the other transverse $y$ sign handles the opposite choice of interior orientation. [F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Complete the positive graph replacement of the tangent-plane case on its whole modified planar patch. Near the new auxiliary edge away from the corner use a positive inward boundary coordinate as the normal height, so it vanishes exactly on that edge. Near the knot edge away from the corner extend the prescribed positive knot height along a narrow source collar; it remains positive there. Use the old graph near the outside of the patch, where the source domain is unchanged. On the remaining interior use any positive smooth height. Choose a finite nonnegative partition by [F4], subordinate to these regions and equal to the old-graph choice near the outside seam. Along either boundary every active choice has the SAME required boundary value, and in the interior every active choice is positive. Their convex combination therefore has precisely those boundary values and remains strictly inside the piece. Near the corner keep that case's explicit graph unchanged. The new source boundary is simple: its altered auxiliary arc has monotone negative $u$ coordinate and all its moving portion misses the positive knot edge; the rest is the old boundary. It still bounds a planar disk. Replacements can be made in arbitrarily small marked neighborhoods. There is just one incident witness on each side at a mark; all other witnesses and compression disks are disjoint compact sets, so choose those neighborhoods disjoint from them. This gives simultaneous embedded disjoint witnesses, matching the SAME globally normalized knot arcs. [F3, F4, step 2.1, step 2.2, construct]

4.1 Produce actual corner parametrizations rather than infer them from an ordinary disk parametrization. The original witnesses have planar source disks by hypothesis. The projection charts in the two graph cases extend to planar local diffeomorphisms of their source charts; hence the local domain replacements define new planar Jordan source regions, unchanged outside the endpoint patches. Their boundaries have exactly two transverse marked corners and are otherwise smooth. Any two such two-corner regions, with labelled boundary arcs, are diffeomorphic as manifolds with corners. To prove the needed assertion, choose corner charts and use the SAME fixed smooth monotone rounding profile in both sets of charts. The resulting rounded regions have smooth Jordan boundaries. Specify an orientation-preserving boundary map equal to the corner-chart identification on neighborhoods of both rounding arcs, and extend it over the remaining two face arcs by smooth increasing interval maps. By [F3] it extends over the rounded disks and agrees with these chosen collar identifications near their boundaries. Since the two rounding profiles and their collars were selected in the corner coordinates, their identifications are the identity in those coordinates. Restore the deleted corner patches by those same coordinate identifications; they agree on open collars of the joining arcs, so glue smoothly with smooth inverses. This proves the assertion for the original cornered regions. Apply it with the standard half-disk $H$ as source. Compose with the graph witness embeddings to obtain smooth rank-two parametrizations whose semicircle is the normalized knot arc and whose bottom is the new auxiliary surface arc. [F3, F4, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 The normalized knot is neat. Apply [F2] to these actual half-disk parametrizations, choosing new witnesses transverse along their bottom edges and retaining both boundary arcs. Its perturbations can avoid the other compact witness images and compression disks, so simultaneous disjointness persists. The ambient knot-isotopy extension from [F1] fixes marks and is supported in the chosen neighborhoods; that construction depends only on the knot track and was already available before witness preparation. It need not preserve the splitting surface, which has remained fixed as background throughout the witness replacements. No smooth fixed-$H$ family through an originally noncornered endpoint is claimed: each final positive-$\epsilon$ witness has just been constructed and parametrized separately. This proves exactly the smooth-category normalization and parametrization asserted. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 4.1, construct] ∎
