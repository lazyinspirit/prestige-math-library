## Precise finite lemmas

**Unprotected retained-output lemma.** In a finite channel schedule let X be a retained classical register of dimension at least two. Assume every allowed one-location fault leaves its output marginal equal to the same prescribed ideal marginal on an admissible nonempty input domain. Assume X is an actual register factor, the comparison does not decode X, and a faulty slot may be replaced by every CPTP map on its declared support. Then no slot may have X in its declared support. Indeed, choose the last such slot t. The ideal channel at t followed by resetting X to either of two distinct basis states gives two allowed replacements, each supported on the same declared factors. All slots after t act identically on X. Their two X marginals are distinct constants, contradicting equality with the same ideal marginal on any one admissible input. This works equally for a newly initialized output factor. A one-dimensional factor carries no information and may be dropped.

Apply this only to retained logical classical ports of an elementary EC: take the admissible input domain to be the quantum code sector P_0 with the allowed classical input registers. The identity D E_1 P_0 = D P_0 supplies the premise because D is identity on X. Syndrome-processing records discarded inside EC are not retained outputs. If a record is carried in an encoded quantum block until a later logical measurement, it remains part of that block edge rather than a separate classical output. A protected classical output decoder would invalidate the lemma's premise and would require a different supplier; none is supplied here.

**Terminal-transfer lemma.** Let a finite directed circuit be partitioned into owners with acyclic quotient Q. Let S be a subcircuit of owner A, and let B be a distinct owner. Assume (i) no edge from S enters A minus S, (ii) every edge from S to a different owner enters B, and (iii) there is such a continuing edge from S into B. Reassign S to B, leaving its locations and all wires fixed. The new quotient is acyclic.

Proof: Q has A-to-B because of (iii). The transfer creates no new outgoing edge from B: (i) excludes B-to-A, and (ii) excludes B-to-a-third-owner. Its only new nontrivial edges enter B. Such a new edge X-to-B with X different from A,B came from X-to-S, so the old quotient had X-to-A. If it closed a new directed cycle, choose the first new edge on that cycle. The rest of the cycle, from B back to X, uses only old edges: every new edge enters B, so another such edge would revisit B earlier and give a shorter cycle. But the old Q then had B-to-...-X-to-A-to-B, a contradiction. A new edge A-to-B already existed. A new B self-edge is suppressed as internal. This proves the assertion. Empty pieces are ignored. A final terminated output can be treated as an explicit sink; shared ECs being claimed have an actual following input outlet, so condition (iii) holds in the application.

This lemma permits multiple input edges, arbitrary predecessors and external reference correlations. It needs no total-time cut or convexity assumption beyond (i)–(iii), which must be proved for each moved piece.

## Proposed Step 2.2 replacement

Split into 2.2–2.4 if this improves readability, updating later proof references mechanically. The following is mathematical prose for the native author to integrate with the existing phase tags.

> First normalize retained logical classical bypass wires. On any nontrivial retained classical factor X fixed by D, the one-fault EC identity on P_0 excludes every supported EC location: at the last such location replace its ideal channel by that channel followed by reset of X to either of two different basis values. These are allowed one-location CPTP faults. Later EC locations do not support X, so the two retained X marginals are different, whereas both must equal the same marginal of D P_0. This contradiction also excludes a newly emitted retained classical factor. Therefore every such external classical wire is a structurally untouched bypass. Draw its original source and all fan-out edges outside EC ownership. No wire, channel or location is erased, and no record is discarded. Internal syndrome/ancilla records remain internal. A record decoded from an encoded block is still in that block bundle until its declared measurement. Recursive refinement replaces supported locations, so these bypasses remain bypasses at every scale.
>
> Contract ordinary recursively replaced cores. By the refinement hypothesis their quotient is the original acyclic parent circuit. Maintain this quotient while assigning claimed collars. A one-block trailing EC has, after bypass normalization, just its continuing quantum block outlet as an external output; this outlet enters the immediately following root on that block. Other outputs are internal EC ancillas. Start at the outer shared EC being assigned to the later bad root. It is terminal in its former rectangle: no retained operation of that rectangle follows it on its output block, and it has no other non-bypass outlet. Thus it satisfies the terminal-transfer lemma and can be reassigned without making a cycle.
>
> Continue along the finite expanded-support construction, one collar scale down at a time. Each next piece is a leading lower EC of an opening child of the piece already assigned. It is the trailing EC of the preceding lower rectangle on that block. Its only non-bypass external outlet enters that opening child, which is already inside the same claiming owner. It cannot enter a retained gate of its former owner: such a gate would lie between this trailing EC and its declared immediately following child on the same block. Its ancillas close internally, and all retained classical fan-out already bypasses it. Hence conditions (i)–(iii) of the terminal-transfer lemma hold again. If the elementary addresses are already claimed, no second transfer occurs. Never split a primitive multi-register operation: transfer the entire EC core with all its internal slots. The shared-EC-only overlap hypothesis identifies each removed portion as just these terminal collar pieces; no other removal is permitted.
>
> Each transfer removes finitely many addresses from an earlier owner and assigns them to the prescribed claimant. Every scale descends strictly in a collar step, and both the depth and schedule are finite. Applying the terminal-transfer lemma after every nonempty move gives a final acyclic owner quotient. Its owners have disjoint elementary supports, with exactly the erasures specified by the scan. External ports may be staggered in the underlying elementary schedule, but the acyclic quotient now supplies a topological ordering in which each owner is an ordinary channel on its exposed input/output bundles. Whole block bundles, descending to their child bundles at exposed collars, define its quantum ports. No elementary operation is cut, no boundary is moved across a retained gate, and every classical bypass retains its original source and destinations.

This proof uses only existing A1/A2, the explicit arbitrary same-support fault supplier, and the existing parent-refinement hypothesis.
