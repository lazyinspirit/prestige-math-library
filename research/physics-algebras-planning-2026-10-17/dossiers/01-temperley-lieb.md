# Temperley–Lieb algebras and representation theory

**Lane:** 01  
**Evidence cut-off:** 2026-10-17  
**Status:** source research and local library audit complete; this is a planning dossier, not a published library item.  
**Scope:** classical finite diagram algebras, their representation theory and selected categorical/physics connections. Nothing in this dossier changes the library, items, build, or autopilot state.

## Findings that should govern future item design

The sources support a cohesive mathematical core: define the finite diagram algebra over a ring with loop parameter δ; prove the equivalent local-generator presentation and Catalan diagram basis; construct link-state standard modules with their reflection/Gram form; identify them as a cellular family; then separate generic semisimplicity, fixed-rank root-of-unity behavior, Jones trace degeneracy, and positive-characteristic behavior. Jones–Wenzl projectors and the Jones quotient depend on further nonvanishing hypotheses. The physics and categorical branches should be presented afterward, with their own assumptions and evidence.

Conventions must be fixed before any statements are reused. In the diagram convention used here, a closed loop evaluates to δ and generators u_i satisfy u_i²=δu_i, u_i u_{i±1}u_i=u_i, and distant commutation. Some sources instead use idempotents e_i²=e_i and e_i e_{i±1}e_i=τe_i, while others use f_i²=−(q+q⁻¹)f_i or the opposite sign. The common quantum convention writes δ=q+q⁻¹, but this parameterization and its quantum integers require a suitable scalar extension and do not erase the special case δ=0. A statement about one normalization cannot be moved to another without recording the rescaling and any inverted scalars.

The fixed-rank warning is substantive. Over C with δ=q+q⁻¹, the common threshold statement from the Jones-trace literature applies to nontrivial orders ℓ≥3 of q²: TL_n is semisimple for n<ℓ and nonsemisimple for n≥ℓ, while the Jones trace is already degenerate at n=ℓ−1. Two low-order parameter families must be separated. At q²=1 (δ=±2), TL_n is semisimple for every n. At q²=−1 (δ=0), TL_n is semisimple for odd n and nonsemisimple for even n; the Jones trace normalization used in the trace-quotient source is undefined here. Thus “q is a root of unity” alone is not a fixed-n nonsemisimplicity criterion. In particular TL₂(δ)≅k[u]/(u²−δu) is split semisimple over a field exactly when δ≠0; at δ=0 it is the dual-number algebra. For a direct field-independent rank-three check at δ=0, write a=u₁,b=u₂. The relations give a²=b²=0, aba=a, bab=b, so words reduce to 1,a,b,ab,ba. The map to k⊕M₂(k) sending 1↦(1,I), a↦(0,E₁₂), b↦(0,E₂₁) sends ab↦(0,E₁₁) and ba↦(0,E₂₂); these five images are independent and span the five-dimensional target. Hence TL₃(0)≅k⊕M₂(k), semisimple over every field. Normalization by δ is invalid at δ=0. These fixed-rank statements are from the complex theory plus the explicit TL₂/TL₃ calculations; Spencer’s arbitrary-field analysis uses quantum characteristic and characteristic-p digits instead.

For a cellular algebra, a nonzero cell form gives the simple head and the full simple classification follows from the cellular axioms. A zero cell form means that the form quotient is zero; it does not prove the underlying cell module has zero head. For TL, statements about a particular standard module’s radical, simple quotient, or vanishing must be sourced from TL-specific representation theory.

## Mathematical coverage and supplier evidence

| Topic | Evidence-backed content | Source and limits |
|---|---|---|
| Diagram and generator presentations | Finite TL_n has a basis of planar noncrossing pairings of n top and n bottom points; multiply by vertical stacking and replace each closed component by δ. The local generators are elementary cup-cap diagrams. Ridout–Saint-Aubin prove the diagram/generator equivalence using Jones normal form and a Catalan count, rather than merely asserting the familiar relations. | Ridout–Saint-Aubin §§2–3, full article pp. 1–50. They work over C and choose a nonstandard diagram orientation for left-action convenience. The proof is a good exact supplier for the ordinary algebra, not arbitrary base rings. |
| Catalan basis and link states | The diagram count is C_n=(1/(n+1)) binom(2n,n). For a standard-module label p (or a through-string count t in other sources), link states have dimension binom(n,p)−binom(n,p−1), after conventions are translated. Link states are partial noncrossing matchings with prescribed defects/through-lines. | Ridout–Saint-Aubin §§2–3; Chen §§2.1–2.4. Existing Catalan library items count Dyck paths/parenthesizations, but do not prove the bijection with TL diagrams. A new proof must state that bijection and indexing convention. |
| Cellular/standard modules | The standard module is obtained by taking link states with fixed defect number and quotienting the link-state representation by the next-through-line filtration. Reflection gluing defines an invariant bilinear form. When nonzero, its radical is the unique maximal proper submodule and the quotient is simple. Restriction has a two-step filtration, with a split exception on critical labels. | Ridout–Saint-Aubin §§3–4, 6–8; Graham–Wenzl roots paper for block structure. The TL-specific cell basis and form should be proved or quoted explicitly; the existing generic cellular item is not a substitute. For δ=0 and n=2p, the usual form on one standard module vanishes identically and requires special treatment. |
| Gram determinants and generic regime | The link-state form’s determinant is expressed through quantum integers. Its nonvanishing gives irreducibility of standards and generic semisimplicity. For q not a root of unity, the complex TL_n family is semisimple for every finite n. | Ridout–Saint-Aubin §4 and source discussion of Westbury’s determinant method. Their article says Westbury’s recursion contains a mistake and supplies its own corrected derivation; cite the read source rather than relying on the older formula without checking it. |
| Root-of-unity representation theory | For nontrivial q²-order ℓ≥3, critical labels and reflection/linkage patterns describe nonzero radicals; the cited pedagogical paper derives radical dimensions, standard-module composition factors, inter-standard homomorphisms, projectives, and simples. It separately proves β=±2 semisimplicity for all n, and at β=0 proves semisimplicity for odd n and nonsemisimplicity for even n. Goodman–Wenzl give root-of-unity blocks, with critical blocks simple and noncritical blocks organized by affine-reflection orbits; in their stated range the radical is nilpotent of exponent at most 3. | Ridout–Saint-Aubin §§4–8 is over C and includes β=0. Goodman–Wenzl assume characteristic zero, a primitive ℓ-th root and ℓ>3, with normalized Jones projections. Translate conventions before importing any formula; their paper does not cover all small orders. |
| Jones trace versus semisimplicity | With the Iohara–Lehrer–Zhang convention and its defined-trace range q²≠−1, the Jones trace is nondegenerate for generic q and, at nontrivial root orders ℓ≥3, for n≤ℓ−2. It is degenerate at n=ℓ−1 although the algebra is semisimple there; the algebra is nonsemisimple for n≥ℓ and the trace radical is generated by E_{ℓ−1}. The q²=1 (β=±2) and q²=−1 (β=0) families require separate treatment. | Iohara–Lehrer–Zhang §1.2 and §2, plus Ridout–Saint-Aubin Cor. 4.8 and §8 for low-order exceptions. Their convention is f_i²=−(q+q⁻¹)f_i; the normalized Jones trace is undefined when q²=−1. Trace degeneracy and algebra nonsemisimplicity are different tests. |
| Arbitrary characteristic | The modular source defines diagram TL over a pointed ring and computes decomposition data over fields using quantum characteristic and (p,ℓ)-digits, and gives simple-dimension and Jones–Wenzl descent results beyond the complex root-of-unity setting. For finite p, Spencer sets p(i)=ℓp^{i−1} for i>0 (p(0)=1); §10.3 gives the criterion that the projector of index n exists when n<ℓ or n=a p(i)−1 for some i>0 and 1≤a<p. | Spencer, whole article, especially the decomposition-theorem sections and §10.3. Here ℓ is the least positive quantum integer index with [ℓ]=0 (or ∞), and p is the characteristic parameter from the pointed-ring setup; when p=∞ and ℓ<∞, the criterion is n<ℓ or n=aℓ−1 for a positive integer a (the i=1 term survives). When ℓ=∞ every finite n satisfies n<ℓ. This is a field criterion, not an existence theorem over arbitrary integral coefficient rings. Use the source’s exact definitions. The ordinary U_q(sl₂) tensor-centralizer argument is not a replacement for this treatment. |
| Centers | A 2026 characteristic-zero result computes dim Z(TL_n(δ))=1+⌊n/2⌋ for every n and every δ≠0, including root-of-unity nonsemisimple cases. | Giaquinto–Mastnak, full article. This does not cover δ=0 or arbitrary characteristic; their Gram-leading-term specialization is posed as a question. |
| Jones basic construction and trace | A subfactor tower uses conditional expectation and Jones projections, with a trace fixed by finite-index II₁ hypotheses. Planar-algebra cap/cup operations encode the same local relations. A finite diagram algebra tower and a von Neumann subfactor tower are related but are not interchangeable constructions. | Jones, “Planar Algebras, I,” §§3.1, 4.1–4.2, including the proof of Theorem 4.2.1; Jones, “The Jones Polynomial,” §§3–6. The subfactor result assumes finite index and extremality where stated. Survey p.2 has a skein normalization inconsistency; do not use that line. |
| Jones–Wenzl idempotents | Away from vanishing quantum integers, the projector is recursively built by p_{r+1}=p_r−([r]/[r+1])p_r U_r p_r in the diagram convention of Chen; it is idempotent, annihilated by adjacent cups/caps, and has quantum trace [r+1]. The denominator condition is part of the theorem. At a root where [ℓ]=0, the recursion reaches an obstruction; the negligible/Jones quotient is a different construction. | Chen §§3.1–3.3 and §5; Jones survey §3; Spencer §10.3 for mixed-characteristic descent. Chen’s q=e^{πi/r} convention has p_{r−1} negligible and quotient simples p_0,…,p_{r−2}. Do not conflate “projector fails to exist,” “trace becomes zero,” and “quotient by negligible morphisms.” |
| Hecke and braid connections | The braid group maps to a TL algebra by a linear expression in adjacent TL generators after choosing a quadratic Hecke normalization; the Hecke relation and braid relation are normalization-sensitive. The existing published quotient item proves a specific Hecke quotient’s generator relations after inverting v+1 and adjoining a square root, and specializes a Hecke-trace invariant to Jones. It explicitly does not prove diagram/generator equivalence or descent of that trace through the quotient. | Existing item `def-temperley-lieb-quotient-and-jones-specialization`; Jones survey §§3–4; Chen’s category/braiding discussion. New proof obligations remain for a diagram-algebra isomorphism to the presented Hecke quotient and for any claim that the Ocneanu trace descends. |
| U_q(sl₂) and centralizers | At generic q, the two-dimensional U_q(sl₂) tensor action and Hecke action commute. The library’s type-A quantum Schur–Weyl theorem is stated for every N≥1, so its N=2 instance already gives the generic matrix-image double-centralizer result. The remaining bridge is to identify the N=2 Hecke tensor-action kernel/image with the TL diagram quotient under an exact parameter map; after that bridge, the TL diagram action inherits the centralizer conclusion. Root-of-unity semisimplification gives truncated SU(2)-type fusion, while root-of-unity tilting centralizers are a separate result. | Chen §§5–6 and Jones/Goodman–Wenzl context; local `thm-hh-generic-quantum-schur-weyl-mutual-centralizers`. This local theorem’s image equalities are valid for N=2; the abstract Hecke action is faithful only in its stated N≥n range, so retain its image-versus-abstract-algebra distinction. It does not itself identify the TL diagram quotient or cover roots of unity. |
| XXZ and Potts/loop models | Local TL generators act on spin-½ chains; the nearest-neighbor Hamiltonian is a scalar/sign-normalized sum of generators. Nichols identifies Potts representations at Q=2,3,4 with q=e^{πi/4}, e^{πi/6}, and 1, respectively. Boundary/periodic models add parameters and can involve blob or periodic TL algebras instead. | Nichols §§2, 2.2, 3.1, 5 and selected periodic-model portions. Several periodic and two-boundary claims are numerical, sketched, or explicitly open; finite-size scaling and CFT identifications are physical results with assumptions, not universal algebra theorems. |
| Anyons, fusion categories, and TQFT | A fusion category with a noninvertible self-dual object gives a local projection representation of TL when the F-symbol gauge and vacuum-channel normalization satisfy the stated hypotheses: δ=1/(F^{aaa}_a)_{1,1}, and in the unitary case δ=κ_a d_{FP,a} for the source’s sign κ_a. TLJ at the appropriate root has truncated fusion, and Jones–Wenzl idempotents produce the corresponding negligible quotient. | Blakeney et al. (2026), Chen §§5–6, Jones surveys. The current integrable-model article proves the F-symbol/TL relation (with gauge details) but its δ>2 gap statements rely on Bethe ansatz/numerics and finite-size behavior; the authors leave a general rigorous sector decomposition open. Turaev–Viro’s general surface construction should not be narrowed to Chen’s direct skein proof domain. |
| Logarithmic CFT boundaries | Root-of-unity TL standards may become indecomposable and participate in tilting bimodules; lattice induction/fusion has a useful representation-theoretic formulation. In boundary models, blob/one-boundary TL modules are related to Virasoro Kac/Verma modules in scaling limits. | Gainutdinov et al. §§5–9. The authors identify general scaling-limit and Virasoro-mode claims as heuristic/conjectural outside special cases. Periodic/bulk LCFT remains difficult. Treat this as a frontier/review boundary, never as a proved general equivalence. |

## Regime and normalization checklist

| Setting | Safe claim | Do not infer |
|---|---|---|
| Commutative ring R with δ∈R | Diagram multiplication is defined by loop removal; local diagram generators satisfy the TL relations. | A basis theorem or an isomorphism to another presentation over every R without a normal-form argument and coefficient-ring conditions. |
| Field k, TL₂(δ) | k[u]/(u²−δu)≅k×k if δ≠0; it is dual numbers if δ=0. | Division by δ in the exceptional case. |
| C, δ=q+q⁻¹, q² not a root of unity | Every finite TL_n is semisimple. | A statement over every field or every specialization. |
| C, q² of order ℓ≥3 | TL_n is semisimple for n<ℓ and nonsemisimple for n≥ℓ; Jones trace is already degenerate at n=ℓ−1. | “Root of unity means nonsemisimple at every rank,” or “trace degenerate iff algebra nonsemisimple.” |
| C, q²=1 (δ=±2) | TL_n is semisimple for every n. | Treating order ℓ=1 as a plug-in to the ℓ≥3 trace threshold. |
| C, q²=−1 (δ=0) | TL_n is semisimple for odd n and nonsemisimple for even n; TL₂ is dual numbers and TL₃≅k⊕M₂(k). | Applying the Jones-trace normalization, which divides by q+q⁻¹ and is undefined here. |
| Arbitrary field / mixed characteristic | Spencer’s quantum-characteristic framework supplies the relevant decomposition and projector criteria. | Substitution of the characteristic-zero ℓ criterion without verifying the modular definitions. |
| Root-of-unity quotient / fusion category | A quotient by the appropriate negligible ideal can be semisimple with truncated labels. | That the unreduced TL algebra is semisimple, or that an individual JW recursion is defined at a vanishing denominator. |
| Jones subfactor tower | Finite-index II₁ conditional expectation, projections, and normalized trace give the basic construction under the source’s hypotheses. | Identifying it literally with the finite diagram-algebra tower without specifying the representation/planar-algebra passage. |

## Exact repository reuse map

### Direct TL and categorical carriers

- `def-temperley-lieb-quotient-and-jones-specialization` (published): defines the Hecke quotient J(n) by the three-strand parabolic symmetrizers. It sets e_i=(T_i+1)/(v+1), λ=v/(v+1)², then after adjoining s with s²=v rescales to f_i=(v+1)s⁻¹e_i, so f_i²=(s+s⁻¹)f_i and f_i f_{i±1} f_i=f_i. It also gives the Jones specialization and a Hecke-tower trace normalization. Its caveat is decisive: no trace descent to the quotient is claimed, and no diagram/generator equivalence, TL standard modules, or TL cellularity is proved. Its Jones-polynomial proof uses the Hecke trace construction, not the trace on the quotient.
- `thm-khovanov-seidel-u-functors-satisfy-temperley-lieb-relations` (published): proves square, adjacent triple, and distant-zero relations for specific graded Khovanov–Seidel quiver bimodule functors. It is a categorified action on a particular category, not a definition/classification of classical TL_n modules.
- `ex-the-jones-specialization-of-a-two-strand-closure` and `ex-the-hecke-trace-skein-calculation-for-a-three-crossing-braid`: examples consuming the existing Jones specialization; they add no diagram-algebra or representation-theory supplier.
- Khovanov–Seidel consumers: `lem-khovanov-seidel-generator-complexes-are-mutually-inverse`, `lem-khovanov-seidel-curve-complexes-intertwine-the-braid-generators`, `lem-khovanov-seidel-complexes-satisfy-far-commutativity`, `lem-khovanov-seidel-complexes-satisfy-the-three-term-braid-relation`, and `prop-khovanov-seidel-decategorification-is-the-unreduced-burau-action`. These belong to `library/braid-groups/graded-quiver-algebras-and-derived-tensor-functors.md` and should only be reused for that categorified lane.

## Independent audit correction (2026-10-17)

The auditor corrected the characteristic-zero Jones–Wenzl interpretation after checking Spencer §§2.3,8.4 discussion and10.3. For p=∞, p(1)=ℓ and all finite a satisfy a<p, so the n=aℓ−1 branch remains. Directly in TL₃(0), with a=u₁,b=u₂, the normalized projector f₃=1−ab−ba satisfies af₃=bf₃=f₃a=f₃b=0 and f₃²=f₃ because ab and ba are orthogonal idempotents. Thus failure of the usual f₂ denominator does not rule out a normalized f₃. Projector normalization includes identity coefficient1; annihilation alone would also allow the zero idempotent. Over a coefficient ring, required inverses/descent must be checked separately (e.g. rational JW₂ at δ=2 has coefficient1/2 and is not integral over Z). Exact pre-correction dossier/manifest bytes are retained under audit/preimages/.
