# Lie algebras and their representations in theoretical physics

**Status:** source and carrier audit complete; planning/research only. **Cutoff:** 2026-10-17. No canonical library, run, or source-content changes are proposed in this dossier.

## Finding and scope

The repository already supplies a broad, proof-bearing ordinary Lie-theory spine: enveloping algebras and PBW, finite root systems and highest weights, Weyl characters, BGG category O and its geometric/categorical interfaces, real forms and global integration, and affine/Kac–Moody highest-weight theory including Weyl–Kac. Those subjects should be reused through their existing carriers. The actual additions are narrower: Lie-superalgebra signs and representation theory; Virasoro and affine-current/vertex structures; the Beilinson–Bernstein flag-localization theorem; a Hall-algebra construction/Green–Ringel bridge; and a careful physics-facing account of unitary Poincaré representations, gauge groups, CFT, and integrability.

The local Hall inner product on symmetric functions is not a Ringel–Hall algebra. Generic Hopf, Drinfeld-double, quantum-group, and KLR suppliers are useful prerequisites, but none constructs Hall multiplication or Green’s coproduct. The finite-dimensional local Drinfeld-double theorem also cannot be applied directly to the generally infinite-dimensional completed Hall bialgebra. The Hall source below supplies this mathematical delta without claiming that a Hall algebra is itself a physical observable or symmetry in every model.

The dossier distinguishes algebraic modules from continuous/unitary group representations, finite-dimensional from infinite-dimensional modules, and integrable from unrestricted highest-weight modules. The physics examples illustrate interfaces under stated hypotheses; they do not infer a gauge theory, a full CFT, or an integrable model from a Lie algebra alone.

## Mathematical delta and proof obligations

### Lie superalgebras, their highest weights, and supersymmetry

An ordinary PBW relation does not prove the super statement. A future superalgebra foundation must define the Z/2Z grading and parity, prove the signed super-Jacobi identity for the stated bracket, and derive the ordered monomial basis with even exponents unrestricted and odd exponents at most one in characteristic zero; for odd x, the relation is x² = [x,x]/2. It must state the ground field and the enveloping-superalgebra convention. Finite-dimensional Lie-superalgebra representation categories are not generally semisimple. Typical/atypical character statements, odd reflections, and Weyl-groupoid arguments require their specific basic-classical or contragredient hypotheses; ordinary Weyl-group and Weyl-character arguments cannot be copied without proof.

Kac’s 2002 classification survey supplies finite-dimensional simple Lie-superalgebra classifications, the linearly compact simple-superalgebra list, and the list of linear superconformal algebras; its introductory Standard-Model comments are speculative and excluded. Serganova’s super-Kac–Moody text defines integrability for weight modules using local nilpotence for principal roots; odd reflections alter highest-weight labels and generally produce a Weyl groupoid rather than an ordinary Weyl group. The cited results are for regular quasisimple contragredient/Kac–Moody superalgebras, not a universal super Weyl–Kac theorem.

Kac–Wakimoto’s affine-superalgebra work shows why “integrable” must be defined per class. Their affine superalgebra setup assumes a finite-dimensional Lie superalgebra with an even invariant symmetric form and reductive even part. Their (J)-integrability imposes ordinary integrability only on selected affine even components together with weight/local-finiteness conditions; they explain that the ordinary all-root integrability condition is too strong for most affine superalgebras. Their classification is for non-twisted affine superalgebras and chosen integrability notion, with level-one free-field/character results in special families. No claim is made for all affine superalgebras, all levels, or arbitrary modules.

Lai–Nakano–Wilbert (2025, v3) establishes, for a finite-dimensional quasi-reductive Lie superalgebra with a triangular decomposition and a principal parabolic p = l ⊕ n+, an equivalence between category-O Ext and relative Lie-superalgebra cohomology, finite generation results, standard stratification in their stated generalization (which can allow infinitely many simples), and a complexity bound by dim(g_1). Those are not assertions that every super category O is a finite highest-weight category or semisimple.

For the physics example, the four-dimensional N=1 super-Poincaré anticommutator (up to convention) relates Q_alpha and Qbar_dotalpha to translations P_mu; N >= 2 permits central charges. A super-Yang–Mills transformation example closes on shell and modulo a field-dependent gauge transformation; it is not an off-shell representation without additional structure/auxiliary fields. Wigner supermultiplets describe one-particle representation data under the stated little-group and positivity assumptions. Spin-statistics is a theorem of relativistic QFT with locality/positivity hypotheses, not a consequence of the abstract superalgebra alone.

The October 2026 affine vertex-superalgebra preprint is deliberately narrow: at boundary principal admissible levels, for basic classical g except D(2|1;a), its ordinary-module category is shown finite semisimple and then a rigid braided/ribbon fusion supercategory. It separately assumes away psl(n|n), where that family has no such boundary level; a remark covers finite ordinary simples at boundary subprincipal levels for osp(1|2n), not a general theorem for those levels. Neither rationality nor semisimplicity is inferred at arbitrary level.

### Virasoro, affine currents, vertex algebras, and W-algebra links

The Witt algebra’s central extension is Virasoro. In one common mode convention,
\[
[L_m,L_n]=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\frac{c}{12}(m^3-m)\delta_{m,-n}.
\]
The adjoint convention in some sources swaps m,n; fix it before translating formulas. Highest-weight conditions L₀v = hv and Lₙv = 0 for n > 0 permit Verma modules that are generally infinite-dimensional and reducible at determinant-zero parameters. The familiar unitary positive-energy highest-weight classification is not a classification of all Virasoro modules: the source gives c >= 1, h >= 0, or the discrete unitary minimal-series values c = 1 - 6/[m(m+1)] and their allowed weights (with the trivial c = 0 case). A full 2D CFT adds locality, OPE/associativity, spectrum, and global consistency such as modular invariance; algebraic highest-weight data alone do not classify it.

For a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra with a chosen normalized invariant form, affine-current modes obey a central extension of the loop bracket, conventionally [Jᵃₘ,Jᵇₙ] = fᵃᵇ_c Jᶜₘ₊ₙ + k m δᵃᵇ δₘ,₋ₙ. For a compact simple group and a positive-energy unitary representation, Wassermann’s notes give the positive integral level k ∈ Z≥0 (long roots normalized to square length 2) and allowed finite-dimensional lowest weights satisfying (λ, θ) ≤ k. This is a unitary positive-energy theorem; algebraic affine modules and universal affine vertex algebras can be considered at general complex levels and need not be integrable or unitary.

The Sugawara conformal vector has denominator k + h∨ and central charge c = k dim(g)/(k + h∨) for a simple Lie algebra with this normalization. The construction excludes the critical level k = -h∨; the formula is not a definition there. Universal affine vertex algebras, their simple quotients, positive-energy unitary modules, and rational/modular tensor categories are different levels of structure. Vertex-algebra OPEs and locality in these sources are formal algebraic statements; they do not assert analytic convergence.

Nozaradan supplies locality, vertex-algebra, Virasoro, and affine-current definitions; De Sole–Kac–Valeri develop vertex algebras, Poisson vertex algebras, and Hamiltonian PDE examples. Their KdV/Lenard–Magri and homogeneous Drinfeld–Sokolov constructions require their own Poisson data and stated finite-dimensional reductive Lie algebra, invariant-form, and semisimple-element hypotheses. The later current paper of Creutzig–Dhillon–Nakatsuka (2026) proves a braided tensor equivalence for the Kazhdan–Lusztig categories of W-algebras for simple simply-laced ADE Lie algebras, arbitrary nilpotent f, and irrational level κ ∈ C minus Q. Its restriction is part of the theorem; it is not a result for arbitrary type or rational level. Existing type-A finite-W and shifted-Yangian carriers elsewhere in the repository remain in force and are outside this delta.

### Hall algebras, quantum groups, and current/Yangian bridges

Schiffmann’s lecture notes define the Ringel–Hall algebra of a small finitary abelian category over a finite field by counting short exact sequences. Finiteness of Hom and Ext¹ makes the product well-defined; associativity counts two-step filtrations. Green’s coproduct is generally valued in a completed tensor product. Under the hereditary hypothesis (global dimension at most one), Green’s theorem gives the twisted bialgebra compatibility. Xiao’s antipode applies when the finite-subobjects condition holds; then the extended Hall bialgebra is an ordinary Hopf algebra, while absent that condition the completion/topological qualification must remain. The quiver realization is specifically for a finite quiver with no loops and the category Rep_nil(F_q Q) of finite-dimensional nilpotent representations; oriented cycles are allowed, but nilpotence is part of the category (and is automatic when there are no oriented cycles). Its vertex simples then exhaust the simple objects, and this category is hereditary with the finite-subobjects condition. With the fixed finite field F_q and a chosen ν = sqrt(q), Theorem 3.16 maps the correspondingly specialized quantum positive Borel U_ν(b′_+) into the extended Hall algebra by E_i -> [S_i], K_i -> k_{S_i}; it is an embedding, and is an isomorphism exactly in finite/Dynkin type. The image generated by the vertex simples is the composition algebra. This is not a generic-parameter statement over C(v), nor a theorem about unrestricted representations of an oriented-cycle quiver. A reduced Drinfeld double recovers the full quantum-group structure with the stated pairing and quotient. The quiver orientation, field-size parameter, twist, toral extension, and completion are part of the result.

The current bridge is a distinct, later theorem. Schiffmann–Vasserot (arXiv:2312.15803v2, 2024) construct preprojective cohomological Hall algebras for an arbitrary finite quiver and prove an isomorphism with a positive half of the corresponding Maulik–Okounkov Yangian, intertwining actions on Nakajima-quiver-variety cohomology; the associated graded recovers an enveloping algebra of a polynomial-loop Lie algebra in their equivariant setup. This does not assert that every ordinary Hall algebra is a Yangian or that every quiver yields the finite-type Kac–Moody algebra. The authors’ source itself distinguishes their preprojective COHA from the Kontsevich–Soibelman 3-Calabi–Yau COHA. Geometric Hall, DT/CoHA, and derived Hall constructions therefore need separate sources and statements.

The local repository has no direct Ringel–Hall/Green-bialgebra carrier. The nearest exact suppliers are the generic Hopf pages and the Drinfeld–Jimbo quantum-group/KLR pages in the table above. This is a genuine delta, not a claim that quantum groups, Hopf algebras, or the Hall inner product are absent. The future proof obligations are: set up the finitary category and field; prove product finiteness and associativity; state the twist/Euler form; prove Green’s coassociativity and bialgebra identity in the appropriate completed tensor product under the hereditary hypothesis; state when an antipode exists; establish the simple-generator map and quantum Serre relations; prove injectivity/finite-type surjectivity; and handle the infinite-dimensional/topological double separately from the local finite-double theorem.

### Flag localization and category O geometry

MIT 18.757 Lecture 29 sets U_lambda with infinitesimal-character convention chi_(lambda-rho), defines lambda-twisted differential operators on the flag variety, and states that localization and global sections are inverse for the lecture’s antidominant convention. It records the zero-character U_0 ~= D(F) statement and the associated graded Springer map. Its Theorem 29.2 explicitly does not provide a proof of the global equivalence. Remark 29.8 says global sections can fail to be faithful outside antidominance, and for integral parameters a singular lambda-rho can also obstruct the converse equivalence; the sl_2 example at lambda = 1 is a failure. A local theorem must preserve the source’s parameter shift and regularity conventions, then prove exactness, essential surjectivity, and the two unit/counit isomorphisms for the stated module categories; a citation to a source whose proof was not read does not discharge that local proof obligation. The local repository already has substantial category O, Soergel, Hodge, and type-A geometric bridges; the missing item is the direct Beilinson–Bernstein/TDO theorem and its proof, not “geometry of category O” wholesale.

### Noncompact/unitary representations and the Poincaré example

The Poincaré group is a noncompact semidirect product of translations with a Lorentz group or its spin cover. Positive-energy irreducible unitary representations are infinite-dimensional as group representations, although their compact spin/helicity data are finite-dimensional in the appropriate cases. The Mackey supplier gives the abstract induction/imprimitivity route, but a Poincaré specialization still has to compute the momentum orbits and stabilizers under the chosen cover and positivity assumption: massive spin has an SU(2) little group; the massless little group is the double cover of E(2), whose translation subgroup may act trivially (helicity) or nontrivially (continuous-spin representations). Projective representation and central-extension conventions matter. No Poincaré classification or spin-statistics theorem is claimed by the local Lie pages. A specialist source and explicit orbit/stabilizer proof are prerequisites before publishing that application.

Likewise, a gauge algebra does not specify the global gauge group, principal bundles, allowed representations, or constraints. Figueroa-O’Farrill’s supersymmetric Yang–Mills example explicitly uses a compact group and a principal-bundle connection; the gauge transformation and on-shell closure clauses should remain with that model. In 2D CFT, affine currents give a central extension of loop modes and a Virasoro action under Sugawara’s hypotheses. In integrability, a Drinfeld–Sokolov hierarchy is built from additional Poisson/Lax data. These are interfaces, not implications from the abstract Lie algebra.

The Frenkel–Ben-Zvi *Vertex Algebras and Algebraic Curves* book page was inspected only as metadata; no full text was available and no book section is claimed read. The downloaded 1994 Kac–Wakimoto number-theory version is not relied on for the claims in this dossier. No full-text source for the explicit Wigner positive-energy Poincaré classification was read here; that application remains a source/proof obligation rather than a completed local theorem. The source scan and paper set are a selected, verifiable frontier through the cutoff, not an exhaustive bibliography of every 2026 result.

## Recommended reuse and planning boundary

Keep the existing finite-dimensional Lie, category-O, real/global, affine Kac–Moody, finite-W and shifted-Yangian, quantum-group, and geometric-representation suppliers as the canonical mathematical core. Route any later additions through those items. The highest-value genuinely new proof paths are super-PBW/basic superalgebra representations; a narrowly stated affine-superalgebra integrability branch; Virasoro/current/Sugawara plus formal vertex-algebra foundations; direct Beilinson–Bernstein localization with proof/source recovery; and Hall algebra with finite-field/hereditary/completion assumptions. Treat the Poincaré unitary example as an explicit Mackey specialization only after its orbit/stabilizer proof and specialist source are supplied. Keep gauge/CFT/integrability prose as assumption-bearing applications. No local content or run transition is made by this dossier.
