# Hopf algebras, quantum groups, tensor categories, and physical models

**Lane:** 04 — Hopf/quantum-group source research  
**Coverage cutoff:** 17 October 2026  
**Purpose:** sourced planning/reuse audit for the physics-facing algebra scaffold. This is not native library authoring, proof validation, or a claim that every repository prerequisite has been audited.

## Recommendation

Build a route from coalgebra and Hopf foundations through module tensor products, rigid duals, quasitriangular structures, quantum groups, doubles, and selected physical applications. Reuse the large published Hopf–Hecke and quantum-group branches. Put new work only at explicit boundaries: Hall/Green constructions and their completed tensor products; finite versus completed/topological and quasi-/weak-Hopf variants; root-of-unity representation categories and modular data; and model-specific integrability or topological-symmetry applications.

The route must not treat every “quantum symmetry” as an ordinary Hopf action. An algebraic Hopf algebra has a coassociative coproduct into the algebraic tensor square. A generic quantum-group universal R-matrix can lie only in a completion and act only on a selected module class. A Green coproduct can be topological for an entirely different finiteness reason. A quasi-Hopf algebra changes the associativity constraint; a weak Hopf algebra changes the tensor unit/base structure. Lattice models add geometry, spectral parameters, transfer matrices, or physical conditions that are not consequences of the bare Hopf axioms.

Hall algebra is a required route, not an aside. It is a concrete bridge from extension-counting in quiver representations or coherent sheaves to positive quantum groups, Green’s coproduct and pairing, and Drinfeld doubles. Its completed coproduct is a central planning boundary: local ordinary Hopf, finite-double, and algebraic R-matrix carriers do not automatically supply it.

## Mathematical spine and exact hypotheses

1. **Coalgebra, bialgebra and antipode.** Define a coalgebra over the stated base with coassociative Δ and counit ε; define a bialgebra by compatibility of algebra and coalgebra structures; define an antipode as the two-sided convolution inverse of the identity. State whether the base is a field or a commutative ring. The standard algebraic definition has Δ:H→H⊗H. It does not itself require the antipode to be bijective. Local def-hh-hopf-algebra-and-antipode has that non-bijective generality. By contrast, nonzero finite-dimensional Hopf algebras over a field have bijective antipode under the finite-dimensional theorem in the local finite-Hopf branch.

2. **Modules, comodules, tensor products and duals.** A bialgebra coproduct gives the diagonal action on V⊗W; the unit/counit give the tensor unit. The module associativity constraints are inherited from vector spaces. Finite-dimensional modules admit a left dual using S; a right dual uses S⁻¹ and therefore requires a bijective antipode. Do not call the entire module category rigid when only its finite-dimensional objects have duals. Full linear duality for comodules becomes an equivalence only in finite-dimensional settings; for an infinite coalgebra the full dual sees only rational modules, with the finite dual or coefficient coalgebra handling the appropriate restriction.

3. **Quasitriangular and ribbon cases.** The ordinary algebraic quasitriangular datum is an invertible R∈H⊗H, with the coproduct-intertwining and two coproduct identities. It induces a braiding on the category of left modules. An invertible solution of the Yang–Baxter equation alone does not give an H-module braiding or the two hexagons. A ribbon Hopf algebra adds a compatible central ribbon element; categorically, a ribbon category supplies duality, braiding and twist and therefore a framed-tangle evaluator. A writhe-normalized unframed link statement needs additional hypotheses (in the checked local result, the object is absolutely simple with scalar twist).

4. **Finite versus topological or completed quantum groups.** The generic Uq(sl₂) universal R-matrix is an infinite root-height series in a completed tensor product, not an element of algebraic Uq⊗Uq. Its action is locally finite/terminating on modules where the positive root generator acts locally nilpotently; this includes finite-dimensional type-I modules in the cited source. The local root-height completion is a weightwise formal product with explicitly checked coefficientwise multiplication and intertwining on specified module pairs. It does not assert a universal R in the uncompleted tensor square or a braiding on all modules. Quantum-affine spectral-parameter R(u) has another interface: u-dependent/meromorphic intertwiners, poles, and evaluation modules; a typical evaluation homomorphism is not Hopf, and the finite-dimensional affine module category is not genuinely braided as an ordinary category.

5. **Finite Drinfeld double and Yetter–Drinfeld modules.** A finite-dimensional Hopf algebra H has a finite dual Hopf algebra, a canonical element, and a finite Drinfeld double. In the local convention, D(H)=H^{*op}⊗H, with a fixed normal order; its left modules are identified with left-left Yetter–Drinfeld modules. This convention must be translated before importing formulas: EGNO and other sources use different factor order, dual coproduct/opposite conventions, and canonical R-tensor order. For infinite-dimensional H, the full linear dual is not an algebraic substitute; a restricted dual, topology, and completed tensor product need separate definitions and proofs.

6. **Hall and Green.** For a small finitary abelian category over a finite field, finite Hom and Ext¹ make the subobject/short-exact-sequence Hall multiplication meaningful. Euler-twisted formulas require extra assumptions: finite global dimension and finite higher Ext groups for the Euler form; the hereditary hypothesis is central to Green’s bialgebra compatibility. The Green coproduct can take values in a completed tensor product because a fixed object may have infinitely many subobjects. Green’s theorem also requires a twisted multiplication on the tensor product, or an extended Hall algebra with a Grothendieck-group torus. Multiplication after completion is defined only on a coefficientwise locally finite domain and must be shown convergent; arbitrary completion is not automatically an algebra. The ordinary Hopf/double pages below are reusable once these precise tensor and pairing hypotheses are met, not as a replacement for them. Schiffmann’s 2009 notes connect quiver Hall algebras to positive quantum groups and curve Hall algebras to quantum loop groups; their introduction explicitly excludes geometric Hall theory, most derived/cluster versions, and DT counting theory from its survey.

7. **Generic and root-of-unity Uq(g).** The local Drinfeld–Jimbo carrier is generic over Q(q), for a fixed symmetrizable Cartan datum, with a stated coproduct normalization. Its Serre halves, PBW ranks, Hopf pairing and triangular decomposition are already developed. The local divided-power integral form supports integral and based-module statements but does not amount to the distinct representation theories at roots of unity. At roots, distinguish at least the De Concini–Kac form, Lusztig divided-power form, and small quantum group; fix order, parity, Cartan-lattice and rank assumptions before asserting quantum Frobenius or semisimplicity. For suitable restricted root-order assumptions, a tilting-module quotient by negligible morphisms yields a semisimple Verlinde category; this is a specific construction, not a theorem about arbitrary root specializations or small quantum groups.

8. **Quasi-Hopf and weak Hopf are different variants.** A quasi-bialgebra has an algebra-homomorphism coproduct that is coassociative only up to conjugation by an invertible reassociator Φ satisfying a pentagon identity; Φ acts as the associator in its module category. A quasi-Hopf algebra adds an antipode and distinguished elements α, β satisfying the quasi-antipode axioms. A weak bialgebra relaxes unitality of Δ and the ordinary counit identities; its source/target counital subalgebras give a nontrivial base, and its representation tensor product is relative over that base (equivalently, the image of Δ(1) in a suitable convention). The weak antipode is a separate axiom. These variants need separate definitions, axioms and examples; a quasi-Hopf associator does not encode a weak-Hopf base algebra.

9. **Finite tensor, fusion, modular and nonsemisimple categories.** In the usual finite tensor convention, the category is a rigid k-linear finite abelian category with finite-dimensional Hom spaces, finitely many simple objects, enough projectives and simple tensor unit (the precise field/closure convention must be stated). A fusion category adds semisimplicity; it is not a synonym for finite tensor category. A modular tensor category is braided ribbon/fusion with nondegenerate S-matrix under the stated conventions; the Verlinde formula depends on that structure and nondegeneracy. A finite nonsemisimple tensor category may be pivotal, spherical, factorizable or admit modified traces without being modular in the semisimple sense. Keep finite tensor, fusion, modular, factorizable/nonsemisimple and weak-Hopf statements separate.

## Hall algebra route: required new bridge, not duplicated foundations

Schiffmann’s complete lecture-note survey is a suitable source for the first finite-field route, subject to its declared limits. Its §1.1 finitary hypotheses require finite Hom and Ext¹; §1.2’s multiplicative Euler form requires finite global dimension and finite higher Ext groups and includes a square-root convention; §1.3 proves Hall multiplication by counting short exact sequences/flags; §§1.4–1.5 explain that Green’s coproduct is generally completed and prove bialgebra compatibility for hereditary categories using the twisted tensor product. The “finite subobjects” condition is the separate condition that makes the coproduct land in the ordinary tensor product. The proof’s multiplication on a completed tensor square is locally coefficient-finite only for the indicated image elements. It is not a general completion theorem.

Recommended smallest route:

- Define finitary Hall multiplication with the automorphism normalization and a fixed convention for extension direction. Give the simplest finite-vector-space/Jordan-quiver and a small noncommutative quiver example.
- State the hereditary Euler-twisted Green coproduct, its completion and counit, the coefficientwise convergence criterion, and the exact bialgebra compatibility/twist. Separate the finite-subobjects special case.
- Compare the composition subalgebra generated by simple objects with generic Uv(n⁺), not the entire Hall algebra without hypotheses. Keep v, finite-field cardinality q, and square-root or integral-form conventions explicit.
- Add the Grothendieck-group torus and Green pairing where needed, then explain when a double realizes the full quantum group. Translate Schiffmann’s double convention to the local finite-double H^{*op}⊗H convention explicitly; curve/coherent-sheaf Hall algebras are an important source of completions.
- Treat derived Hall, motivic/cohomological Hall, quantum-loop, and DT/BPS constructions as separate later routes, with dg finiteness, geometric push/pull, vanishing-cycle, orientation, or Calabi–Yau hypotheses as appropriate. Schiffmann explicitly says those fields are mostly omitted from his 2009 notes.

An ordinary finite Hopf Δ:H→H⊗H is not the Green map into H-hat⊗H-hat; an ordinary finite Drinfeld double assumes a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra and canonical finite-dual tensor; an algebraic quasitriangular R assumes R∈H⊗H. None is a drop-in proof of a Hall coproduct, Hall double, completed universal R, or topological Hopf algebra. The new route must declare its completion, topology and coefficientwise domain and show that the relevant maps/multiplications are well-defined there.

The handoff from the other-algebras lane records a complementary Hall/derived Hall source reading: Toën, Derived Hall Algebras, has dg-enhanced hypotheses over Fq, finite total Hom cohomology, and higher-Ext homotopy factors. That is a distinct construction from the ordinary finitary Ringel–Hall algebra and should be cross-referenced rather than merged into its axioms; see research/physics-algebras-planning-2026-10-17/root-topic-and-reuse-review.md and sources/06-other-physics-algebras/.

## Quantum groups, roots of unity, and categorical consequences

The local QG branch is already deep at generic q: it fixes a symmetrizable Cartan datum and normalization, constructs the Drinfeld–Jimbo algebra and coproduct, proves stability of the quantum Serre ideals, triangular decomposition and PBW-rank/pairing statements for the positive and negative halves, and develops generic highest-weight/integrable modules and crystal bases. The relevant theorem proof was checked at the local item. The coproduct convention is a practical integration constraint: compare it explicitly to external Uq(g) sources that use Δ(E_i)=E_i⊗K_i+1⊗E_i (or an opposite coproduct) before quoting any R, double, or tensor formula.

The local divided-power and crystal pages establish useful integral lattices and based modules, but do not give the root-of-unity classification. A future root page should begin with sl₂ and put three forms side by side: De Concini–Kac, Lusztig divided-power, and the small quantum group. Then state whether q has odd order, what divisibility/parity conditions apply to the Cartan matrix, what weight lattice is used, and which Frobenius map/module category is in view. Only after that should it construct the tilting module tensor ideal and negligible quotient. For the checked EGNO example the modular/Verlinde claim is in a specified simply-laced, sufficiently large odd root-order regime; it should not be broadened to arbitrary ℓ, non-simply-laced root data, or to the small quantum group by analogy.

The categorical route can reuse the local tensor/fusion and pivotal/spherical inputs. A genuine modular-category statement additionally needs a braided ribbon structure and nondegenerate S-matrix; a Verlinde formula is downstream of that condition. A nonsemisimple finite tensor category may require factorizability, modified traces or nonsemisimple modular functor constructions instead. Do not smuggle finite semisimplicity into “finite tensor category.”

## Integrable systems and physical/topological interfaces

**Algebraic Bethe ansatz.** Faddeev’s note supplies a concrete periodic spin-½ XXX chain: N sites, R(u)=uI+iP, a compatible Lax operator, monodromy entries satisfying the RTT/FCR relation, transfer matrices whose commutator vanishes, a pseudovacuum, Bethe states and Bethe equations. This provides the algebraic reason for commuting transfer matrices. A physical Hamiltonian is selected from a transfer-matrix derivative under the model’s regularity/normalization; Hermiticity, a real spectrum and continuum QFT are separate assumptions or arguments. A general integrability page should present this finite-chain example first and label exactly which identity follows from Yang–Baxter/RTT.

**Quantum-affine modules and spectral R.** Etingof–Semenyakin’s §§4–5 show evaluation representations and spectral-parameter R(u) for affine Uq(sl₂-hat), and explain why the evaluation map generally fails to be a Hopf map, why the category is not genuinely braided due to poles and nonsplit tensor products, and why “meromorphic braiding” is the more accurate phrase in that setting. Local finite-type R-matrix claims and ordinary categorical braiding must not erase those qualifications.

**Topological symmetries.** A finite-group Kitaev quantum-double lattice model is a rigorous example in which Hopf/Drinfeld-double data controls excitations and topological operators. A selected decoherence channel can have strong or weak higher-form non-invertible symmetry, with conclusions tied to that lattice model, cellulation, finite nonabelian group, and channel. It does not prove a universal theorem that arbitrary Hopf module categories classify physical symmetry defects. Fusion categories, MPOs, boundary module categories, anomalies, higher-categorical and mixed-state settings each require their own data.
