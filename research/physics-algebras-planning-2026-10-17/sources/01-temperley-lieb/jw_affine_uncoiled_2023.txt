Abstract
Aﬃne and periodic Temperley–Lieb algebras are families of diagrammatic algebras that ﬁnd diverse
applications in mathematics and physics. These algebras are inﬁnite dimensional, yet most of their
interesting modules are ﬁnite. In this paper, we introduce ﬁnite quotients for these algebras, which
we term uncoiled aﬃne Temperley–Lieb algebras and uncoiled periodic Temperley–Lieb algebras. We
study some of their properties, including their deﬁning relations, their descriptions with diagrams, their
dimensions, and their relations with aﬃne and skew sandwich cellular algebras. The uncoiled algebras
all have ﬁnitely many one-dimensional modules. We construct a family of Wenzl–Jones idempotents,
each of which projects onto one of these one-dimensional modules. Our construction is explicit and
uses the similar projectors for the ordinary Temperley–Lieb algebras, as well as the diagrammatic
description of the uncoiled algebras in terms of sandwich diagrams. We also discuss the Markov traces
for the uncoiled algebras and their evaluations on the newly deﬁned projectors, and ﬁnd expressions
involving Chebyshev polynomials of the ﬁrst kind.

Introduction
The Temperley–Lieb algebra TLn(β) is an algebra that describes the product of connectivity diagrams
made of curves drawn inside a rectangle, in a way that keeps track of the non-local connectivity
properties of those curves. It has a long history. This algebra of diagrams was ﬁrst discovered by
physicists in the context of percolation problems [71], and subsequently used to describe ice-type
models [2], XXZ spin chains [59] and the Q-state Potts model [46]. It then reappeared in the work of
Jones on knot invariants [36], and its representation theory was worked out over the next years [24,
76, 67]. The algebra TLn(β) is also in Schur–Weyl duality with the quantum group Uq(sl2), an idea
that has continued to develop as it was later applied to study the representation theory of other
diagrammatic algebras like the Brauer algebra [7], the BMW algebra [57, 6, 12, 11] and the partition
algebra [38, 49, 47, 30]. The Temperley–Lieb algebra is, in fact, a subalgebra of the partition algebra.
Many results about the representation theory of this larger algebra were obtained using combinatorial
methods which are also applicable to its subalgebras [10]. Another approach is to view TLn(β) as
a quotient of the Hecke algebra of type A. From the latter, one can generalise the Temperley–Lieb
algebra in various ways, either by studying other quotients of this Hecke algebra, or quotients of other
types of Hecke algebras; see for example [27,29].
One generalisation of the Temperley–Lieb algebra is obtained by studying connectivity diagrams
endowed with periodic boundary conditions. The precise names and the choices of relations used to
deﬁne these algebras vary in the literature. In this paper, the periodic Temperley–Lieb algebra pTLn(β)
refers to the algebra deﬁned with only the generators e0, e1, . . . , en−1, whereas the aﬃne Temperley–
Lieb algebra aTLn(β) also involves the translation generators Ωand Ω−1. These algebras were initially

inﬁnite-dimensional algebras. Nonetheless, many of the methods employed to study TLn(β) go through

aTLn(β) [48,28,26,17,61] allowed for a full characterisation of its irreducible modules, but also revealed
a much richer panorama of indecomposable yet reducible modules than for the ordinary Temperley–Lieb
algebra. The classiﬁcation of these indecomposable modules remains an open problem.
The Temperley–Lieb algebra and its aﬃne and periodic versions ﬁnd further applications in
statistical mechanics to describe special families of models deﬁned in terms of non-local observables [60,
34], with the Temperley–Lieb diagrams conveniently encoding the connectivity properties of these
objects.
Figure 1 gives examples of conﬁgurations of two statistical models on the square lattice,
namely the model of bond percolation and the Ising model, and their maps to connectivity diagrams
of the algebra aTLn(β). Interestingly, the representations of aTLn(β) and pTLn(β) relevant in physics
are ﬁnite-dimensional, marking a clear contrast with the algebras’ inﬁniteness. The reasons behind
this are clear. First, on a periodic geometry, the curves that form the Temperley–Lieb diagrams may
wind arbitrarily many times along this axis. Second, such curves may close after winding along this
periodicity axis and thus form non-contractible loops, of which there can also be arbitrarily many. It
thus makes sense to deﬁne and study quotients of aTLn(β) and pTLn(β), with extra relations that
render the resulting algebras ﬁnite-dimensional. A ﬁrst objective of this paper is to study and deﬁne
such algebras, where (i) the non-contractible loops are removed with weights α, and (ii) the winding
of the curves around the full n nodes is equivalent to no winding at all, times an unwinding factor γ.
We refer to these quotient algebras as uncoiled aﬃne Temperley–Lieb algebras and uncoiled periodic
Temperley–Lieb algebras.
These algebras turn out to depend crucially on the parity of the system
size n. In particular, it is natural to deﬁne two types of uncoiled algebras for n even compared to just
one type for n odd. This will thus lead to a total of six algebras, half of them associated with aTLn(β)
and the other half with pTLn(β).
In a sense, by studying these quotients, we will be fragmenting
the inﬁnite-dimensional algebras aTLn(β) and pTLn(β) into families of ﬁnite-dimensional ones, whose
representation theory can be expected to be simpler. In particular, we hope that this fragmentation
will help make the study of the indecomposable modules more amenable.

The Temperley–Lieb algebra is also a prime example of a unital associative algebra falling under
the umbrella of cellular algebras [25,27], and whose representation theory can be completely unravelled
using this framework. The notion of cellularity was originally introduced for ﬁnite-dimensional algebras,
but was already extended in [26] to an inﬁnite-dimensional case: the aﬃne Temperley–Lieb algebra.
The extension to other inﬁnite-dimensional cases was formalised by the notion of aﬃne cellularity
given by K¨onig and Xi [43], which adjoins commutative algebras to the cells. The notion of aﬃne
cellularity can still be weakened, notably by removing the requirement of the commutativity of the
associated algebras and by removing the involution from the cellular datum, which gives rise to sandwich
cellularity [51,73,72]. We refer to [72] for more on sandwich cell theory. Notably, the algebra aTLn(β)
is aﬃne cellular in all cases. To the best of our knowledge, the same question for the algebra pTLn(β)
has not been previously addressed. We will show in this paper that it is aﬃne cellular for n odd.
Our construction, however, does not carry over to the case with n even. We will also show that the
uncoiled algebras are aﬃne cellular, except in one special case. For this special case and for pTLn(β)
with n even, the ﬁrst three axioms deﬁning sandwich cellularity hold, but not the fourth axiom that
dictates the action of the anti-involution. This motivate us to modify the fourth axiom and introduce
the notion of skew sandwich cellular algebras, building on Hu, Mathas and Rostam’s notion of skew
cellularity [31]. The sandwich cellular and skew aﬃne cellular algebras are, in fact, special subcases of
skew sandwich cellular algebras. We will then show that the two problematic algebras described above
are skew sandwich cellular.
There is another reason, from a physics standpoint, to launch the study of these quotient algebras.
The Temperley–Lieb algebra is particularly useful to study the continuum scaling limits (n →∞) of
critical statistical models and their description with conformal ﬁeld theories (CFTs). The connection
between the fusion of modules over TLn(β) and the fusion of ﬁelds for boundary CFTs is by now well
established [60,64,63,23], and has proven very useful to compute conformal correlation functions. The
search for a similar connection tying the fusion of modules over aTLn(β) and the fusion of bulk ﬁelds is
less immediate. Despite some recent progress [22,20,4,32], many questions remain unanswered, again
leading us to wonder whether aTLn(β) is really the ideal algebraic structure to probe the scaling limit.
We hope that the uncoiled algebras deﬁned in this paper will provide a new tool to tackle these issues.
One of the main results that we provide for the uncoiled algebras is the deﬁnition of a family
of idempotents mimicking the famed Wenzl–Jones projectors of TLn(β). The Wenzl–Jones projectors
P1, P2, . . . , Pn [36,75,40] form an important family of elements of TLn(β). They are deﬁned recursively
and play a crucial role for the representation theory. In particular, Pn projects on the unique one-
dimensional module of TLn(β). In the study of Kazhdan–Lusztig theory [41], Wenzl–Jones projectors
encode particularly important idempotent endomorphisms projecting to indecomposable summands,
allowing for explicit expressions for the bases dual to the canonical bases in the tensor products of
ﬁnite-dimensional simple modules of Uq(sl2) [19]. They have recently resurfaced in the trail of Soergel
bimodule theory [16], as slight generalisations of these projectors were used to give a categorical link
between the Temperley–Lieb algebra and Bott–Samelson bimodules, speciﬁcally for the inﬁnite dihedral
group [14] and for the universal Coxeter group [15]. This categoriﬁcation should also hold for the
p-Kazhdan–Lusztig basis [35].
Recently, Martin and Spencer [50] studied this correspondence and
provided a construction, building on [8], of the Wenzl–Jones projectors in positive characteristic intrinsic
to the diagrammatic construction and to the Temperley–Lieb category without resorting to the tilting
theory of Uq(sl2) [70].
The Wenzl–Jones projectors also ﬁnd multiple applications in physics. For
instance, they arise in the construction of fused R-matrices for integrable models [68,54]. They are also
used to deﬁne loop models with thick boundary conditions and thus to gain insight on the boundary
conformal ﬁeld theory describing the continuum scaling limit of these models [60,55,44].
Diﬀerently from TLn(β), the algebra aTLn(β) has a one-parameter family of one-dimensional

representations, labelled by the twist parameter z ∈C×. Deﬁning a projector on each representation in
this continuous family is not possible. This can, however, be achieved for the uncoiled algebras, as they
turn out to have ﬁnitely many one-dimensional representations. We will construct these projectors
by expressing them as linear combinations of connectivities sandwiched between the Wenzl–Jones
projectors of TLn(β), seen as a subalgebra of aTLn(β), and we will give closed-form formulas for the
coeﬃcients arising in these sums.
One of the tools used by Jones in his construction of the Jones invariant of knots [37] was the
Markov trace. It is originally a trace on braid groups amenable to Markov moves [5]. It has multiple
interpretations, and can be extended to other types of quotients of the braid group [74]. The Wenzl–
Jones projectors for TLn(β) behave nicely with respect to this trace: they evaluate to Chebyshev
polynomials of the second kind. This fact shows up in the categoriﬁcation of the integer polynomial
ring by the Temperley–Lieb category where the Wenzl–Jones projectors and these polynomials are in
direct correspondence [42]. In contrast, for the uncoiled algebras, the Markov traces of the Wenzl–Jones
projectors evaluate to Chebyshev polynomials of the ﬁrst kind and sums thereof.
We mention in passing that the quotients that we study are not the only possible quotients of
aTLn(β) and pTLn(β). For instance, the quotient considered in [62, 3] involves an extra relation that
equates the braid transfer matrix (or hoop operator) to a constant. These quotients then fragment
aTLn(β) into ﬁnite-dimensional algebras that are isomorphic to certain one-boundary Temperley–Lieb
algebras [3]. The corresponding dimensions, projectors and representations are then diﬀerent from the
ones that we consider here. The interest of [62] was also to categorify the integer polynomial ring by
a diagrammatic category. The projectors that they deﬁne also correspond via their Markov trace to
Chebyshev polynomials of the ﬁrst kind. It will be interesting to interpret the uncoiled algebras in a
similar framework, to study the resulting categoriﬁcation, and compare it with the one studied in [62].

Outline.
The paper is structured as follows. In Section 2, we review the deﬁnitions of aTLn(β) and
pTLn(β), describe their standard modules Wn,d,z and present their description with sandwich diagrams.
A short discussion on central elements of these two algebras is presented in Section A. In Section 3,
we deﬁne the uncoiled aﬃne and periodic Temperley–Lieb algebras, both in terms of generators and
connectivity diagrams. We study the basic properties of these algebras, namely their dimensions, their
standard representations, and their description as aﬃne cellular algebras and skew sandwich cellular

algebras, are relegated to Section B. In Section 4, we deﬁne the Wenzl–Jones projectors, ﬁrst for the

between the periodic and aﬃne projectors and present their explicit construction. The construction
involves some constants Γk,ℓ, whose computation is non-trivial. In Section 5, we derive linear relations
satisﬁed by these constants that determine them uniquely and use them to ﬁnd closed forms for the
constants Γk,ℓ, for each of the six uncoiled algebras. The proof uses contour integral expressions for
these constants and is given in Section C. In Section 6, we discuss the Markov trace and its evaluation
on the Wenzl–Jones projectors. We conclude with some remarks in Section 7.

To compute the numbers nβ(c) and ni,j(c), we ﬁrst connect the loop segments attached to the top and

7
Conclusion
In this paper, we deﬁned and studied quotients of the aﬃne and periodic Temperley–Lieb algebras:
the uncoiled aﬃne and periodic Temperley–Lieb algebras. We described them in terms of sandwich
diagrams, computed their dimensions, and showed that they are aﬃne cellular, except in one special
case where skew sandwich cellularity is needed. We also constructed Wenzl–Jones projectors for these
algebras in terms of the sandwich diagrams and the similar projector of the ordinary Temperley–Lieb
algebra.
The construction involves certain coeﬃcients Γk,ℓ, for which we derived linear recurrence
relations; see Proposition 5.1. The closed forms for these constants are then given in Propositions 5.3
to 5.5.
Finally, we computed the homogeneous Markov trace of the Wenzl–Jones projectors and
obtained simple formulas in terms of Chebyshev polynomials of the ﬁrst kind for the uncoiled aﬃne
algebras uaTL(1)
n (β, α) and uaTLn(β, γ ̸= 1), and for their periodic counterparts.
This work marks a ﬁrst step in the investigation of these algebras and leaves open a number of
questions. A ﬁrst step is to investigate the representation theory of the uncoiled algebras, as a way to
approach the same investigations for the aﬃne and periodic Temperley–Lieb algebras. To illustrate,
let us focus on the aﬃne case for n odd.
To be precise, we wish to study a functor between the
categories of ﬁnite-dimensional modules of the aﬃne Temperley–Lieb algebra and of the uncoiled aﬃne

theory of the aﬃne Temperley–Lieb algebra, for which the classiﬁcation of the indecomposable modules
is an open problem.
A second direction is to explore the characteristic p case. The work done here always assumed that
the variables β, α, γ and z are complex numbers. However, most of the deﬁnitions and results can be
extended to positive characteristics and thus ﬁt in the framework of the recent work of Spencer [50,69].
We note, however, that the expressions in Propositions 5.3 to 5.5 for the constants Γk,ℓare written in
terms of q instead of β. To study the similar objects for the modular uncoiled Temperley–Lieb algebra,
one would need to rewrite these constants purely in terms of β.
We note that ﬁnding closed-form formulas for the Wenzl–Jones projectors as sums of connectivities
is a hard problem, even for the Temperley–Lieb case. In this case, recursive formulas for the coeﬃcients
in these sums were found long ago [75,19] and closed-form formulas were conjectured by Ocneanu [58].
However, their proofs are known only in a few limited cases [65]. These coeﬃcients have deep relations
with Kazhdan–Luzstig theory.
Indeed, it was recently shown that they can be expressed as sums
of Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials [1], for which no closed formulas are known. Our results directly
allow us to obtain closed formulas for the coeﬃcients of the projectors for the uncoiled algebras in
the basis of connectivities, as sums of the coeﬃcients conjectured by Ocneanu for the Temperley–Lieb
case. Moreover, it would be interesting to see if our methods with contour integrals can be adapted to
also give a complete proof of Ocneanu’s formulas for the coeﬃcients of the Wenzl–Jones projectors of
TLn(β).
After the completion of this project, we discovered that it is possible to construct a proper cellular
basis for the uncoiled aﬃne algebras following the arguments from Propositions 3.4.2 and 3.5.6 of [28],
with minor adaptations for the cases with n even. In short, the sandwich diagram basis is replaced by
certain linear combinations of diagrams. This is not a big change for the purposes of this paper; in
particular, the cell modules are the same. In this work, we presented the sandwich basis, as it is the
simplest and because it also works with the uncoiled periodic algebras. For further investigations of
the representation theory of the uncoiled algebras, it could be useful to use the proper cellular bases.
