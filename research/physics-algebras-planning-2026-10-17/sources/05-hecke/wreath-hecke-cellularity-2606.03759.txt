ON CELLULARITY OF HECKE ALGEBRAS FOR WREATH PRODUCTS
BERTA HUDAK AND CHUN-JU LAI
Abstract. The (generalized) Hu algebra is a nontrivial quantization of the wreath product Σm≀Σd
between symmetric groups, whose representation theory controls the Hecke algebra of the complex
reflection group G(d, d, md).
In this paper, we construct a unified basis for this algebra and
establish its cellular algebra structure in the case d = 2.
As an application, our construction
provides an elementary realization of the simple modules for the Hecke algebra of type D2m that
are parameterized by bipartitions of size (m, m).
1. Introduction
1.1. Background. Introduced by Graham and Lehrer in [GL96], the notion of the cellular algebras
has proved to be foundational in modular representation theory of finite dimensional algebras over a
field K. The framework provides a systematic construction and classification of the irreducibles via
non-zero heads of cell modules W(λ), generalizing the construction of irreducible modules in terms
of the Specht modules Sλ for the non-semisimple Hecke algebra Hq(Σd) of the symmetric group
Σd. Geck [Gec07] proved that all Hecke algebras of finite Coxeter groups are cellular, provided
they either have equal parameters or certain unequal parameters. For the complex reflection group
G(m, 1, d), the (integral) cellular structures of the associated Hecke algebras have been studied
intensively in the past decade (see [Bow22] and the references therein).
It is important to better understand the cell modules for the Hecke algebras of complex reflection
group G(r, p, n) as they play a crucial role in the second named author’s program regarding the
conjecture of Ginzburg, Guay, Opdam, and Rouquier (see Section 6.2).
However, even in the
smallest nontrivial case G(2, 2, n) = Dn, the known construction of cell modules relies heavily on
thorough understanding of Kazhdan–Lusztig cells [KL79], Lusztig’s a-function, and asymptotic
Hecke algebras [Lus03].
The approach we are taking utilizes the following Morita equivalence theorem [Hu02] due to Jun
Hu, under a natural invertibility condition, i.e. qi ̸= −1 for 0 ⩽i ⩽2m −1:

is simpler as it is similar to types B and C.
Hu’s original construction of A(m) can be considered of type B as it relies on deep properties of
the Jucys–Murphy elements in the type B Hecke algebra of unequal parameters (1, q). Recently,
a “type A” construction of the Hu algebra A(m) was made available using the framework of the
quantum wreath products [LNX24].
1.2. Quantum wreath products. One should think of the Hu algebra A(m) as a nontrivial
quantization of the wreath product Σm ≀Σ2, which is not a Coxeter group unless m ≤2. The
realization of A(m) as a quantum wreath product Hq(Σm) ≀H(2) (see Definition 2.5.1 for details)
allows us to study its analog Hq(Σm) ≀H(d) for d > 2, called the generalized Hu algebra. The
representation theory of Hq(Σm) ≀H(d) is closely tied to the representation theory of the Hecke
algebra for the complex reflection group G(d, d, dm).
It is natural to investigate the potential cellular algebra structure of Hq(Σm) ≀H(d) for an ar-
bitrary d. The main difficulty arises from the key feature that each generator Hi in the quantum

1.4. Cell modules versus Specht modules. The cell modules constructed by Geck are intrin-
sically tied to geometry. However, their structures remain notoriously elusive for general Hecke
algebras – with one exception of type A, where these modules admit a realization [MP05] via the
tableaux-based approach of Dipper, James, and Murphy [DJM98, Mur92].
Such an identification remains only partially understood for type B (with unequal parameters)
and is unknown beyond types A and B. For example, the complex reflection group G(m, 1, d) has a
quantization known as the Ariki-Koike algebra [AK94] (or the cyclotomic Hecke algebra [DJM98])
denoted by Hq(Cm ≀Σd). Under certain assumptions on the parameters q, one can combine results
on rational Cherednik algebras by Shan [Sha11, Lemma 3.1] and by Chlouveraki, Gordan, and
Griffeth [CGG12, Proposition 4.6] to deduce that [W(λ)] = [Sλ] ∈K(Hq(Cm ≀Σd)). In other
words, the best we know is that at the Grothendieck group level, Geck’s cell modules agree with
the Specht modules over Hq(Cm ≀Σd).
Our cellularity result extends such a module identification to the case of the Hu algebras (and
hence of type D).
Theorem B (Theorem 5.1.2). For λ ∈Ω, the cell module W(λ) is isomorphic to the Specht module
Sλ over the Hu algebra A(m).
Furthermore, we compute the canonical bilinear form ϕλ for all λ ∈Ωand then obtain represen-
tation theoretic consequences in Theorem 5.4.1.
1.5.
The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we review the necessary background on
cellular algebras, the Hu algebra, and its generalization via the quantum wreath products.
In Section 3, we construct the combinatorial data required for our main results for an arbitrary
d ≥2.
This includes the poset consisting of poset-indexed multipartitions and the associated
multitableaux sets. We then explicitly construct our new cellular basis elements, and verify that
this basis successfully recovers established cellular bases in the extremal cases d = 1 and m = 1.
Section 4 is devoted to the proof of Theorem A. We provide explicit formulas for the basis
elements and rigorously verify the cellular algebra axioms. We also briefly discuss how the resulting
cellular datum naturally yields a generalized Robinson-Schensted correspondence.
In Section 5, we analyze the structure of the resulting cell modules to prove Theorem B. We
demonstrate that these cell modules are isomorphic to the Specht modules constructed by Hu
(using the language of wreath modules), and we explicitly compute their canonical bilinear forms.

Theorem 5.4.1. Consider the Hu algebra A(m). Then,
(a) The set of absolutely irreducible A(m)-modules, up to isomorphisms, is given by
Ω0 = {eν
λ ∈Ω| λ is ℓ-restricted} ⊔{[λ, λ′] ∈Ω| λ, λ′ are both ℓ-restricted},
where ℓ:= ord(q) is the quantum characteristic of q ∈K.
(b) The Hu algebra A(m) is semisimple if and only if Hq(Σm) is semisimple.
(c) The Hu algebra A(m) is quasi-hereditary if and only if Hq(Σm) is quasi-hereditary.
Proof. The Corollary follows from applying Proposition 2.1.2. For (a), it follows from Proposi-
tion 5.2.1 that ϕeν
λ is nonzero if and only if ϕλ is nonzero, which is equivalent to that λ ⊢m is
ℓ-restricted. On the other hand, it follows from Proposition 5.3.1 that the form ϕ[λ,λ′] is nonzero
if and only if both ϕλ and ϕλ′ are nonzero.
For (b), by combining Corollary 5.2.2 and Corollary 5.3.2, one has rad(ϕλ) = 0 for all λ ∈Ωif
and only if rad(ϕλ) = 0 for all λ ⊢m, which is equivalent to that Hq(Σm) is semisimple.
For (c), by combining Proposition 5.2.1 and Proposition 5.3.1, one has that ϕλ ̸= 0 for all λ if
and only if ϕλ ̸= 0 for all λ. This completes the proof of the theorem.

6.1. Modular Representations of Hecke Algebras of type D2m. Let H(Q,q)(Bn) be the
Hecke algebra of type Bn with unequal parameters (Q, q) where Q = qb ∈K× for some integer
b. When b is sufficiently large, we are in the asymptotic case (cf. Bonnafe and Iancu [BI03]). In
this asymptotic regime, Geck, Iancu, and Pallikaros [GIP08] constructed a non-trivial isomorphism
between the Specht module S(λ,µ) := Sλ⊗Sµ and the cell module W(λ, µ). However, there remains
a significant and missing special case: the parameter choice (1, q) corresponding to b = 0.
The Hecke algebra HB := H(1,q)(Bn) is closely related to type D, as it contains a subalgebra

now outline the findings of Geck [Gec00] and Hu [Hu02, Hu09], regarding the irreducibles for type

6.2. Rational Cherednik Algebras. For a complex reflection group W, a family of quasi-
hereditary covers of the Hecke algebra Hq(W) were constructed by Ginzburg, Guay, Opdam, and
Rouquier [GGOR03]. Their method utilizes the rather implicit Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov functor
of the rational Cherednik algebra HW corresponding W:
KZW : O(H(W)) →Hq(W)-Mod,
where O(H(W)) is the associated category O, which is a highest weight category whose standard
objects are denoted by ∆H
λ . It is natural to seek for a more direct construction of quasi-hereditary
covers which generalizes the Schur functor of Dipper–James’ q-Schur algebra in type A [DJ86].
For the wreath product Σm ≀Σd, Nakano, Xiang, and the second named author constructed
(1-faithful) quasi-hereditary covers of the corresponding Hecke algebra Hq(Σm ≀Σd) by utilizing
the theory of wreath modules [LNX25]. In particular, for the case of the Hu algebra Hq(Σm ≀Σ2) ∼=
A(m), the Specht module denoted by Sλ therein coincides with the cell modules W(λ) with respect
to the cellular algebra structure in theorem A.
Let S(n, m) be the associated Schur algebra for A(m) as in [LNX24, LNX25]. The algebra
S(n, m) is quasi-hereditary and can be thought of as the analog of the q-Schur algebra.
The
representation theory of S(n, m) has been studied therein using homological methods such as
spectral sequences and the Schur functor Sch : S(n, m)-Mod →A(m)-Mod. In particular, S(n, m)-
Mod is a highest weight category in which the standard module is denoted by ∆S
λ , λ ∈Ω. Moreover,
under certain conditions which guarantee both KZD and Sch are 1-faithful quasi-hereditary covers,
one has
F ◦KZD(∆H
λ ) = Sch(∆S
λ )
for all λ ∈Ω.
It is anticipated that one can lift the cellular basis for A(m) constructed in this paper to a
basis of S(n, m) that plays the role of the semistandard tableaux basis of the q-Schur algebra.
Consequently, one obtains an explicit description of the standard modules ∆S
λ in the highest weight
category S(n, m)-Mod.
