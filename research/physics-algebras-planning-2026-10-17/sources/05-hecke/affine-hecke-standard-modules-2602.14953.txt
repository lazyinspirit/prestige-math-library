Standard modules of affine Hecke algebras
Stefan Dawydiak ∗
April 21, 2026
Abstract
Let G be a connected reductive group defined and split over a non-archimedean local field F. We give
a new geometric proof of a special case of a recent theorem of Solleveld. Namely, we show that the class
of standard Iwahori-spherical G(F)-representations, a notion a priori dependent on the coefficient field
being the complex numbers, is actually defined over ¯Qℓ.
An unpublished theorem of Clozel, proven with global techniques, says that the class of essentially
square-integrable representations is also defined over ¯Qℓ. As an application of our main result, we give a
local proof of this theorem for inner forms of GLn, as well as showing that standard representations of
these groups are defined over ¯Qℓ.
Keywords— Standard representation, essentially square-integrable representation, simple type, Iwahori-
Hecke algebra, Chow group

Introduction
1.1
Representations over ¯Qℓ
Let G be a connected reductive group defined and split over a non-archimedean local field F. Let π be a
complex admissible representation of G(F), let γ be a field automorphism of C, and define a representation
γ · π of G(F) by declaring that its matrix coefficients are of the form γ(f(g)), for matrix coefficients f of
π (see Section 2.2 for a precise definition). It is clear that γ · π is irreducible when π is, and likewise for
supercuspidality. In [KSV25] and [Sol25], less obvious heritability from π to γ · π is studied. Recall that the
standard representations of G(F) are by definition parabolic inductions iG
P (σ ⊗ν) for irreducible tempered
representations σ and positive unramified characters ν of MP (F), respectively, where MP is the Levi subgroup
of P (see [Sol25, Def. 4.1, Thm. 4.3 (a)]). Thus the standard modules occur in algebraic families.
As pointed out in [Sol25], this occurrence in families is one reason to think that the standard representations
may be better behaved in the context of the categorical Local Langlands correspondence of [FS21] than
simple representations. For this to make sense, though, the notion of “standard representation” should be
defined over ¯Qℓ, i.e., be stable under the above action of Aut(C). To this end, Solleveld has recently shown
Theorem 1 ([Sol25], Prop. 5.5). Let G be a connected reductive group over F and π a standard representation
of G(F). Then γ · π is also a standard representation of G(F).
The first result of this note is a new geometric proof, for representations in the principal block of split
groups, of Theorem 1. Precisely, we give a geometric proof, as our Corollary 1, that if K(s, N, ρ) is a standard
module for the Iwahori-Hecke algebra H of G, as constructed in terms of G∨and the enhanced L-parameter
(s, N, ρ) in [KL87], [CG97], then γ ·K(s, N, ρ) ≃K(γ(s), N, ργ), as one would expect upon considering central
characters. Here, ργ is the pullback of ρ along the isomorphism π0(ZG∨(s, N)) ≃π0(ZG∨(γ(s), N)) induced
by γ, see (10) below. This implies that the Deligne-Langlands correspondence of [KL87] is defined over ¯Qℓ,
see Corollary 2.

is γ · π. In general, the functor Cγ ⊗C −does not preserve Bernstein blocks (consider G = GL1), but still

The main input to our argument is the main result of [CLP88], which with [KL87], [CG97] implies that, up
the central character, representations K(s, N, ρ) can be constructed on rationalized Chow groups of certain
varieties defined over Q. This may be viewed as further suggestion that (at least for the principal block or
G = GLn) the standard modules admit a natural interpretation in terms of [FS21].
After to appealing to [CLP88], the argument is very formal, amounting essentially to various intersection-
theoretic constructions commuting with isomorphisms of schemes, i.e., being well-defined. Nevertheless,
we have tried to include careful references for these constructions beyond the usual references for singular
homology, because it is conceptually important that we may work morphisms of schemes that are not
morphisms of C-schemes.
Recall that a Langlands parameter
WF × SL2(C) →G∨(C)
is called essentially discrete if it does not factor through any proper Levi subgroup of G∨. Clearly, this
property is stable under post-composition with field automorphisms of C. The corresponding property for
representations is of course not obvious. Our Corollary 1 also shows that when K(s, N, ρ) is essentially
square-integrable, then so is γ · K(s, N, ρ), with the same formal degree.
Combining Corollary 1 with the results of [BK93], [BK99], [S´ec05], [SS12], [SS08], we obtain the second
result of this note: We give a local proof, valid for function fields, for inner forms G of GLn, of
Theorem 2 (Clozel, unpublished). Let G be a connected reductive group over a p-adic field F, and let π be
an essentially square-integrable representation of G. Then γ · π is again essentially square-integrable, with the
same formal degree as π.
In fact, using [Sol19], we treat general standard modules, giving a proof of Theorem 1 for inner forms of
GLn, compatible with 2.
An exposition of Clozel’s proof is given in [KSV25], where the corresponding result for function fields is
then deduced from Theorem 2 by Kazhdan’s method of close fields.
Finally, note that irreducible tempered and discrete series representations are standard, but that within
the class of standard representations, the tempered representations are not stable under Aut(C). Thus the
standard representations are a class of representations defined over ¯Qℓcontaining the tempered representations.
Our proof of Theorem 1 will show that the standard representations form essentially the minimal enlargement
of the tempered representations, at least in the case of the principal block and inner forms of GLn.
In [BK18], Braverman-Kazhdan introduce a certain subalgebra J of the Harish-Chandra Schwartz algebra
of G(F). By [Suz24], Theorem 1 implies that the class of simple J -modules is defined over ¯Qℓ. Our proof
in this note provides an independent argument for the principal block, and for inner forms of GLn. The
idempotented piece JI of J corresponding to the principal block is isomorphic to Lusztig’s asymptotic Hecke
algebra J [BK18] (see the proofs in [Daw26], [Daw23], [BKK23]), and [Daw26] then implies that JI has a
C-basis of Q-valued functions.
1.2
Prior work for discrete series of inner forms of GLn
Theorem 2 can be deduced from a local Langlands correspondence for G satisfying the properties of [Ta¨ı25, Conj.
6.1], see Proposition 6.3 of op. cit.. Of course, this is an extreme approach: all the known proofs [LRS93],
[LH22] (for function fields), [Hen00], [HT01], [Sch13] (for p-adic fields) of the local Langlands correspondence
for GLn and its inner forms use far more global input than does Clozel’s proof.
However, there are also prior local proofs of Theorem 2 for GLn and its inner forms. After finishing a
first version of this paper, we happened upon [Dat07, §2.1.1 (ii)], where it is explained that for GLn(F),
Theorem 2 is known to follow the Bernstein-Zelevinski classification, specifically [Zel80, Thm. 9.3], or
alternatively from [Rog83] for p-adic fields (which again uses global techniques). Presumably the case of
GLn(D) then follows from the (purely local) proof of a Bernstein-Zelevinski-style classification for these

1.3
Other groups
In principle, our approach to Theorem 2 based on affine Hecke algebras from types should adapt more readily
to general groups than approaches based on the Bernstein-Zelevinski classification. However, the techniques
of the present note work only for equal-parameter affine Hecke algebras. While such algebras can appear
for types beyond the principal block or inner forms of GLn, it seems that the only groups that can be dealt
with via types using equal-parameter algebras exclusively are again inner forms of GLn. Using more general
Morita equivalences, Aubert-Baum-Plymen-Solleveld showed that all Bernstein blocks of inner forms of SLn
may be treated by more complicated algebras built out of equal-parameter affine Hecke algebras [ABPS17],
but Theorem 7 connecting preservation of standardness to our geometric statement does not seem to be
available for these algebras, see the discussion in [Sol19, p.43].
In future work, we hope to expand our techniques to unipotent blocks of classical groups, using [Kat09]
and [CK11].
