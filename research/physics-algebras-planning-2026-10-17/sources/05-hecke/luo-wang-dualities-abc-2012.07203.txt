Abstract. In these lecture notes for a summer mini-course, we provide an
exposition on quantum groups and Hecke algebras, including (quasi) R-matrix,
canonical basis, and q-Schur duality. Then we formulate their counterparts in
the setting of ıquantum groups arising from quantum symmetric pairs, includ-
ing (quasi) K-matrix, ı-canonical basis, and ıSchur duality. As an application,
the (ı-)canonical bases are used to formulate Kazhdan-Lusztig theories and
character formulas in the BGG categories for Lie (super)algebras of type A-D.
Finally, geometric constructions for q-Schur and ıSchur dualities are provided.

1. Introduction
1.1. A quick overview. The classic Schur duality relates the representation
theories of general linear Lie algebras and the symmetric groups as well as Young
tableau combinatorics of Schur functions. On one hand, it can be reformulated
and then generalized to Howe dualities between classical groups. On the other
hand, it admits a remarkable q-deformation, which allows further generalizations
and interpretations. These notes address q-Schur dualities of type ABC(D) as
well as their categorical and geometric incarnations.
We ﬁrst review some basic constructions for Hecke algebras and quantum
groups of ﬁnite type, including (quasi) R-matrix, canonical bases, q-Schur du-
ality and its geometric interpretation. We further use the canonical basis in a
(generalized) setting of q-Schur duality to formulate the Kazhdan-Lusztig theory
for the BGG category O of (super) type A. The materials on quantum groups
and Hecke algebras are widely known with a possible exception of the canonical
basis formulation of (super) Kazhdan-Lusztig theory.
Then we present the parallel developments in the past few years on ıquantum
groups arising from quantum symmetric pairs of ﬁnite type, including (quasi)
K-matrix, ı-canonical bases, and ıSchur duality. For the sake of simplicity, we
have restricted ourselves to the quasi-split ıquantum groups in these notes. We
use the ı-canonical basis in a (generalized) setting of ıSchur duality to formulate
the Kazhdan-Lusztig theory of (super) classical type.
The notes contain no original results. We provide or sketch proofs in the notes
whenever it is practical to do so. For several major theorems, we refer to the
references for complete proofs. For subjects like super Kazhdan-Lusztig theories
or geometric realizations of (ı-) quantum groups, our sketchy exposition hopefully
helps to provide some roadmap to read the original papers.
These notes can serve as a quick introduction for graduate students to quantum
groups in action; they may also help to guide researchers who are already familiar
with quantum groups into the area of ıquantum groups, which has been expanding
rapidly in recent years.
1.2. Quantum groups. Since their introduction by Drinfeld and Jimbo [Dr86,
Jim86] independently, quantum groups have played a central role in represen-
tation theory, quantum topology, and mathematical physics. The universal R-
matrix introduced by Drinfeld [Dr86] provides solutions to the Yang-Baxter equa-
tion.
Just as (Iwahori-) Hecke algebras are deformations of Weyl group algebras,
quantum groups are deformations of universal enveloping algebras of semisimple

Lie algebras. As a profound analogue of Kazhdan-Lusztig basis for a Hecke alge-
bra [KL79] and motivated by Ringel’s Hall algebra realization of half a quantum
group [R90], Lusztig introduced canonical bases arising from quantum groups
[Lus90].
Further generalizations and diﬀerent approaches toward canonical bases for
halves of quantum groups and integrable modules have been developed [Ka91,
Lus91]. Canonical bases for tensor products of modules and modiﬁed quantum
groups were constructed in [Lus92], where a key component of R-matrix, known
as quasi R-matrix, is used to deﬁne a bar involution on the tensor products.
Schur duality between a general linear Lie algebra and a symmetric group
admits a q-deformation [Jim86], where the Lie algebra is replaced by the corre-
sponding quantum group and the symmetric group by the Hecke algebra of type
A. A diﬀerent formulation of the q-Schur duality via q-Schur algebra was given in
[DJ89]; also cf. [GL92]. As an analogue of Iwahori’s realization of Hecke algebras
[Iw64], a geometric realization for q-Schur algebras and their canonical bases via
partial ﬂag varieties of type A is given in [BLM90], and it further leads to a con-
struction of the type A quantum group and its canonical basis from the family of
q-Schur algebras.
Our exposition on KL basis for Hecke algebras, (quasi) R-matrix for quantum
groups, and q-Schur duality is largely self-contained. On the other hand, we only
give a very rough outline of a construction of canonical bases on U−and ﬁnite-
dimensional simple U-modules. Then we present in detail the construction of a
bar involution (via quasi R-matrix) and the canonical basis on a tensor product
of based U-modules; this in turn leads to a construction of the canonical basis
on the modiﬁed quantum group ˙U.
The comultiplication ∆on U here (which is the same as [Ka91, Br03, CLW15,
BW18a] but diﬀerent from [Lus93]) is chosen in these notes to make the canonical
basis on the tensor power ﬁt most naturally with the partial ordering arising
from the BGG category; we have also chosen to work with Z[q]-lattices instead
of Lusztig’s choice of Z[q−1]-lattices for canonical bases. These choices have led
to diﬀerent conventions in the notes, e.g., on quasi R-matrix, than those used in
[Lus93, Jan95].
1.3. ıquantum groups. A theory of quantum symmetric pairs (U, Uı) was
systematically developed by Gail Letzter [Let99, Let02], with Satake diagrams
(which classify the real forms of simple Lie algebras) as inputs. A Satake dia-
gram is encoded in a pair (I = I◦∪I•, τ) subject to some natural constraints,
where I = I◦∪I• is a 2-colored partition of the vertex set of a Dynkin diagram

regarded as examples of quasi-split ıquantum groups.
Indeed, the embedding
(ω ⊗1) ◦∆: U →U ⊗U makes U a coideal subalgebra of U ⊗U, and hence
(U ⊗U, U) is a quantum symmetric pair of diagonal type. This is in the same
spirit as viewing a complex Lie group as a real group.
The ıquantum groups come with parameters, which correspond to a family
of embeddings of Uı into U. A Serre presentation for an arbitrary ıquantum
group of ﬁnite type was given by Letzter [Let02]; however Letzter’s conven-
tion for quantum groups is not quite standard and diﬀers from those used by
[Lus93, CP94, Jan95]. A Kac-Moody generalization of ıquantum groups, which
is consistent with Lusztig’s convention on braid group symmetries, was provided
by Kolb [Ko14].
An ı-program was initiated in 2013 by Huanchen Bao and the second-named
author [BW18a], aiming at generalizing “all” fundamental (algebraic, geometric,
and categorical) constructions from quantum groups to ıquantum groups. A new
q-Schur duality (called ıSchur duality) between Hecke algebra of type B and the
ıquantum group Uı of type AIII (with suitable parameters) was constructed loc.
cit.
As an ı-analogue of quasi R-matrix, a notion of quasi K-matrix (initially called
an intertwiner) was formulated in [BW18a] for the ﬁrst time, and this further gives
rise to a Uı-module isomorphism T on a U-module (called a K-matrix later). The
ı-canonical basis on a based U-module (a.k.a. a U-module with canonical basis)
was constructed loc. cit., where a new bar involution on a based U-module is
constructed via the quasi K-matrix. The notion of ı-canonical basis was in turn
motivated by the formulation of a super Kazhdan-Lusztig theory of classical type;
see below.
The (quasi) K-matrix is a key new notion in the program of ı-canonical basis
announced in [BW18a], and it is expected to be valid in general. The quasi K-
matrix Υ lies in a completion of U−, and its formulation via a bar involution ψı
on Uı (which is not a restriction of the bar involution ψ of U) is uniform for all

of ıquantum groups is more than suﬃcient for the applications to ıSchur duality,
KL theory, and a geometric realization via ﬂag varieties.
1.4. Applications to super Kazhdan-Lusztig theory. Kazhdan-Lusztig (KL)
basis (and respectively, parabolic KL) of a Hecke algebra Hq(W) has been used
to formulate a character formula for the principal block (and respectively, inte-
gral singular blocks) of the BGG category O of a semisimple Lie algebra with
Weyl group W. This is the celebrated Kazhdan-Lusztig conjecture [KL79], now
a theorem proved in [BB81, BK81].
However, this formulation does not extend to Lie superalgebras such as gen-
eral linear or ortho-symplectic Lie superalgebras, as the Weyl groups of these
superalgebras do not control the linkage in the corresponding BGG category O.
Consider U = Uq(slN) and its natural representation V (and its dual represen-
tation V∗); we allow N = ∞. Then V⊗m, or more generally V⊗m ⊗V∗⊗n, admits
a standard basis (which is given by the tensor products of the natural basis of
V and V∗) as well as a canonical basis. A basic fact here [FKK98] is that the
canonical basis on V⊗m coincides with the KL basis on V⊗m when viewed as a
direct sum of permutation modules over the Hecke algebra Hq(Sm).

plosive activity on ıquantum groups in the last few years, including many works

The ı-analogue of Yang-Baxter equation is known as the reﬂection equation.
Just as R-matrices arising from quantum groups provide solutions to Yang-Baxter

A geometric realization of the modiﬁed ıquantum groups and ı-canonical bases

the aﬃne type A construction in [DF15].
As an ı-analogue of Nakajima quiver varieties, a class of “ıquiver varieties”
(corresponding to quasi-split ıquantum groups of ﬁnite type) has been constructed
in [Li19]; it includes the cotangent bundles of partial ﬂags of type B/C as a basic
example (compare [BKLW18]).
The ıSchur duality has been extended to the aﬃne type [FLW20] (also cf.
[FL+20]).
A further extension and formulation via aﬃne q-Schur algebras of
arbitrary type can be found in [CLW20b].
A (global) crystal basis theory of ıquantum groups Uı of type AIII (under some

recent advance in type AI see [Wat21]. Letzter [Let19] has constructed a Cartan
subalgebra in any ıquantum group of ﬁnite type (which gives the right classical

theory for a class of ıquantum groups; see [Wat19].
There has been an ıHall algebra realization of the quasi-split ıquantum groups
in a program developed by Lu and the second author, cf. [LuW19a, LuW20].
The universal ıquantum groups were introduced therein with additional central
elements, and ıquantum groups with various parameters `a la Letzter are recovered
by central reductions.

1.6. Organization. Sections 2–6 contain more standard materials on quantum
groups, Hecke algebras, and their canonical bases; we put a special emphasis on
type A and q-Schur duality. Sections 7–10 cover the ıquantum groups (in partic-
ular, of type AIII), ıSchur duality, and applications to KL theory and character
formulas in super category O of type ABCD.
The (classical) Schur duality between the general linear Lie algebra and the
symmetric group is described in Section 2. In Section 3, we formulate the quasi
R-matrix as an intertwiner between the comultiplication ∆and its bar conjugate
∆. Then we upgrade it to the R-matrix and show that it induces a U-module
isomophism on tensor products and provides a solution to Yang-Baxter equation.
In Section 4, we construct the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis for a Hecke algebra;
detailed proofs are provided.
In Section 5, we formulate the canonical bases for halves of quantum groups,
ﬁnite-dimensional simple U-modules, their tensor products, and the modiﬁed
quantum groups. The quasi R-matrix is used in deﬁning the bar involution on a
tensor product. Some main steps of proofs are outlined.
In Section 6, we formulate the q-Schur duality via explicit actions of quantum
group and Hecke algebra of type A, and explain that the Hecke algebra action
can be realized by R-matrix. We show the bar involution on the tensor power
V⊗m of the natural representation of U can be interpreted from either quantum
group or Hecke algebra side. Then we show the canonical basis on V⊗m as a
tensor product of U-modules coincides with the type A KL basis from the Hecke
algebra side.

7. ıQuantum groups and ı-canonical basis
The ıquantum groups arising from quantum symmetric pairs are introduced,
and ı-canonical basis on U-modules regarded as Uı-modules is formulated. A key
ingredient is the formulation and construction of the quasi K-matrix.
7.1. Quantum symmetric pairs and ıQuantum groups. The theory of quan-
tum symmetric pairs of ﬁnite type was systematically formulated by G. Letzter
[Let99], though examples of ıquantum groups (of classical type) were known ear-
lier. It has been extended to Kac-Moody setting by Kolb [Ko14]. Unlike Letzter’s
papers, Kolb’s formulation of quantum symmetric pairs and ıquantum groups is
compatible with Lusztig’s book, which makes it a standard working reference in
the ﬁeld.

(1) A symmetric pair (g, gθ) consists of a simple Lie algebra (over C) and its

(2) A quantum symmetric pair (QSP) (U, Uı), as formulated by G. Letzter
(see [Let99]), consists of a quantum group U and a coideal subalgebra Uı

(3) A quantum group is an ıquantum group (just as a complex Lie group

diagonal type, where the embedding (ω ⊗1) ◦∆: U →U ⊗U makes
U a coideal subalgebra of U ⊗U, and ω is the Chevalley involution in
Lemma 3.4.
