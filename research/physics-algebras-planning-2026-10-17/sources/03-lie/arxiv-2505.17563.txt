CATEGORY O FOR LIE SUPERALGEBRAS

CHUN-JU LAI, DANIEL K. NAKANO, AND ARIK WILBERT

Abstract. The authors define a Category O for any quasi-reductive Lie superalgebra g with respect
to a triangular decomposition. This much needed approach unifies many important constructions in
the existing literature in a rigorous fashion. Our Category O encompasses all highest weight categories
for Lie (super)algebras as well as specific examples which may not be highest weight categories. When
the decomposition arises from a principal parabolic subalgebra p of g, the Category O exhibits rich
homological properties. For one, the authors show that in contrast to the case of a semisimple Lie
algebra, the Category O is standardly stratified.
Furthermore, the categorical cohomology of O is a finitely generated ring. This provides a first
step towards developing a support variety theory for Category O. It is shown that the complexity of
modules in Category O is finite with an explicit upper bound given by the dimension of the subspace of
the odd degree elements in g. This upgrades results known for glpm|nq to the more general setting. Our
arguments are based on foundational connections between the categorical cohomology and the relative
Lie superalgebra cohomology as well as the interplay between Category O for g and the Category O
for its corresponding Lie algebra g¯0.

1. Introduction

1.1.
The BGG Category O introduced by Bernstein, I. Gelfand, and S. Gelfand in the early 1970’s
has been a central object of study in modern representation theory for nearly fifty years. The BGG
Category O is associated to a complex semisimple Lie algebra g¯0 together with a choice of Borel
subalgebra b¯0 Ď g¯0. More precisely, it consists of certain possibly infinite-dimensional Upg¯0q-modules
subject to certain finiteness conditions. Category O has many remarkable structural properties. For
example, it is a highest weight category as defined by Cline, Parshall and Scott [CPS88], and it
admits a Kazhdan–Lusztig theory.
Deep connections to geometry (flag varieties), topology (knot
invariants and categorification), as well as combinatorics (via the Kazhdan–Lusztig conjectures) have
been established (cf. [BB81, BK81, EW14, KL79, Str05, Su07]).
Given a parabolic subalgebra p¯0 containing b¯0, one can define a parabolic Category Op¯0 generalizing
Category O. Many of the results for Category O have analogs in the parabolic setting, in particular
Category Op¯0 is also a highest weight category. Moreover, the representation type of the associated
quasi-hereditary algebras was studied in [BN05]. For other axiomatic approaches via BGG algebras,
see Irving [I90].
For maximal parabolics in type A, the situation is particularly nice. In this case, these algebras can
be constructed diagrammatically via so-called arc algebras (cf. [BrS11, CK14]). Much of the highest
weight category structure, such as the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials, can be encoded combinatorially
in the diagrams (cf. [BrS08]). Slight variations of the arc algebras can then be used to make con-
nections between parabolic Category Op¯0 and perverse sheaves on Grassmannians [Str09], Springer
fibers [SW12], knot homology [Kho02], as well as Lie superalgebras [BrS12].

1.2.
Given the prominence of parabolic Category O for complex semisimple Lie algebras, it is natural
to develop the theory of parabolic Category O for quasi-reductive Lie superalgebras g “ g¯0 ‘ g¯1.

Category O for Lie superalgebras is not a new concept. It has been developed on a case by case basis
(cf. [Br03, Br04, CLW11, CLW12]), and introduced more generally in [Maz14, AM15].
For this reason, it is our goal to develop a general theory that provides a unified picture encompassing
different versions of Category O for Lie superalgebras that have appeared in the literature. When
g “ g¯0, this includes the Category O for a complex semisimple Lie algebra. We remark that some
of these categories, e.g. Brundan’s Category On [Br04] for the isomeric Lie superalgebras, are not
necessarily highest weight categories. Blocks of certain parabolic Category O which are highest weight
categories with finitely many irreducibles have previously been studied in [Maz14, CCC21]. One of our
goals is to highlight the major differences between parabolic Category O for complex semisimple Lie
algebras as opposed to parabolic Category O for quasi-reductive Lie superalgebras. For semisimple Lie
algebras, the blocks are highest weight categories, and we demonstrate that the general behavior for
quasi-reductive Lie superalgebras is that the blocks are standardly stratified categories with possibly
infinitely many irreducible representations. In terms of the homological algebra, modules for parabolic
Category O for semisimple Lie algebras have finite projective dimension, thus the cohomology lives in
finitely many degrees, and have complexity equal to zero. On the other hand, modules for Category
O for quasi-reductive Lie superalgebras in general have infinite global dimension and have complexity
which can be strictly positive.

1.3.
In this paper, for a finite-dimensional quasi-reductive Lie superalgebra g that admits a triangular
decomposition g “ n` ‘ a ‘ n´, where p “ a ‘ n`, we develop a general theory of Category Op, which
is a certain full subcategory of Upgq-modules. Our Category Op encompasses numerous variations of
Category O in the literature. This includes the parabolic Category O for Lie algebras (by setting
a “ l to be a Levi subalgebra), and the category F of finite-dimensional modules which are completely
reducible over g¯0, by setting a “ g¯0 as the even part of a Lie superalgebra of Type I. Section 4 contains
a compendium of examples covered by our theory.
One can then specialize our definition to the case where p is a parabolic subalgebra as defined by
Grantcharov and Yakimov [GY13]. In fact, in several cases our main results will be stated in the case
of p being a principal parabolic subalgebra (see [GY13, Section 1]). With this setup, one can rely on
using known results from the parabolic Category Op¯0 for g¯0 to prove fundamental theorems for Op. A
precursor to our approach in which the parabolic Category O for Lie algebras was used to study Lie
superalgebra cohomology appeared in Ph.D dissertation work of Maurer [Ma19].
From a historical viewpoint, a similar setting via triangular decompositions for finite-dimensional
modules was developed by Bellamy and Thiel [BT18].
They generalized the ideas of Holmes and
Nakano [HN91] by considering the module category over a finite-dimensional algebra A admitting
a triangular decomposition.
The authors were certainly inspired by the module constructions in
[HN91, BT18]. However, many of the arguments cannot be directly applied in our context because of
the need to work with infinite-dimensional representations. A more elaborate setup using triangular
decompositions can be found in work of Brundan and Stroppel [BrS24].

In the original definition of the standardly stratified categories [CPS96, Definition 2.2.1], there can
only be finitely many simple modules, which does not capture the general behavior of our Category
Op. Furthermore, the conditions involve using projective covers instead of injective hulls, and hence
cannot be used to describe categories without enough projectives. This means that this definition of
standardly stratified does not generalize the concept of a highest weight category. For instance, the
category of rational G-modules where G is a reductive group in positive characteristic is a highest
weight category, but not standardly stratified.

modules (see Theorem 2.4.1). Moreover, our definition allows for the category to have infinitely many
simples. Using Theorem A, we are able to establish an orthogonality result between standard and
costandard type modules. This enables us to prove that our Category Op is standardly stratified with
our new definition.

2. Categories with Filtered Objects

2.1.
In the representation theory of algebraic groups, Lie algebras, and finite groups, the modules need
not be completely reducible. An effective method to study the structure of various modules involves

the use of filtrations. The notion of a highest weight category, as introduced by Cline, Parshall, and
Scott in [CPS88], has become a ubiquitous concept in providing a fundamental framework for studying
filtrations whose subquotients are natural classes of representations.
In this section, we present a definition of a standardly stratified category that differs from the one
that was first given by Cline, Parshall, and Scott [CPS96]. There are several reasons for stating this
definition precisely. First, our definition of standardly stratified is a true generalization of highest
weight categories as first defined by Cline, Parshall, and Scott [CPS88]. For example, the categories of
rational G-modules where G is a reductive algebraic group is a highest weight category. This category
has infinitely many simple modules and does not have enough projectives. Thus, this category would
not be considered a standardly stratified category under the definitions given in [CPS96] and its
variants in [ADL98, D00, Fr07, LW15, BrS24]. However, with our definition (see Definition 2.3.1), the
category of rational G-modules is indeed standardly stratified.
Second, our definition will include blocks for Category O for Lie superalgebras where there are
infinitely many simple modules. Moreover, our definition does not need the restricted assumptions
with the poset Λ having to be finite, and objects of C being of finite length. The definition given in
the following sections are much more compatible with our setting, and can be used in our prototypical
examples such as Brundan’s Category On [Br04] for the queer Lie superalgebra qpnq.

2.4.
Originally, Cline, Parshall, and Scott (CPS) severely relaxed the requirements of the highest
weight category to introduce standardly stratified categories [CPS96, Definition 2.2.1]. The definitions
therein considered only the case when Λ is finite (i.e., there are only finitely many simple modules), and
used projective covers instead of injective hulls. Moreover, our treatment differs from other approaches
(see [ADL98, D00, Fr07, LW15, BrS24]), in that it is not assumed that one is working with a module
category for an algebra. With the definition in the prior section, we can present a definitive statement.

Theorem 2.4.1. If C is a highest weight category (as defined by CPS in Definition 2.2.1) then C is
standardly stratified as defined in Definition 2.3.1.

Example 2.4.2. Let G be a reductive algebraic group over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p ą 0. It is well-known the category of rational G-modules, C “ ModpGq, is a prototype for a highest
weight category. The category C has enough injective modules, but does not have enough projective
modules. Furthermore, blocks can have infinitely many simple modules.
From Theorem 2.4.1, C is standardly stratified, however, under older definitions as above, C would
not be viewed as standardly stratified. It is anticipated that Definition 2.3.1 will be useful in the study
of the representation theory for quasi-reductive algebraic supergroups.

Example 2.4.3. The prototype for a standardly stratified category C is the category of graded mod-
ules, C “ CgrpAq for A a finite-dimensional graded algebra over a field k with a triangular decomposition
A – A´ b A0 b A` (as vector spaces).

Again, note that C can have infinitely many simple modules.

Appendix A. Almost Simple Lie Superalgebras

In the appendix, we compare common setups in the study of finite-dimensional Lie superalgebras
over C.

A.1. Quasi-reductive Lie Superalgebras. The complete list (see Table A.1.1 below) of simple
Lie superalgebras consists of the Cartan series and the classical simple Lie superalgebras. A finite-
dimensional (not necessarily simple) Lie superalgebra g is quasi-reductive (also called classical) if g¯0 is
reductive and g¯1 is semisimple as a g¯0-module. A classical simple Lie superalgebra g is basic if it has
a nondegenerate invariant supersymmetric even bilinear form; while it belongs to the strange series if
it is not basic. A basic classical Lie superalgebra g is said to be of Type I if g is Z-graded with g¯0 “ g0
and g¯1 “ g1 ‘ g´1; otherwise, g is said to be of Type II.
