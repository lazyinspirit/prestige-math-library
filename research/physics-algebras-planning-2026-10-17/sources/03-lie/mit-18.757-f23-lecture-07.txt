7.2. Dixmier’s lemma and infinitesimal character. The following
is an infinite-dimensional analog of Schur’s lemma.

Lemma 7.2. (Dixmier) Let A be a countable-dimensional C-algebra
and M a simple A-module. Then EndA(M) = C. In particular, the
center Z of A acts on M by a character χ : Z →C.

Note that the condition of countable dimension cannot be dropped.
Without it, a counterexample is A = M = C(x) (the field of rational
functions in one variable), then EndA(M) = C(x).

Proof. Let D := EndA(M). By the usual Schur lemma, D is a division
algebra. Assume the contrary, that D ̸= C. Then for any x ∈D \C, D
contains the field C(x) of rational functions of x (as C has no finite field
extensions). But C(x) has uncountable dimension (contains linearly
independent elements

Now let g be a countable-dimensional complex Lie algebra and M a
simple g-module. By Lemma 7.2, the center Z(g) of U(g) acts on M by
a character, χ : Z(g) →C. This character is called the infinitesimal
character of M.
In particular, for semisimple groups we obtain

Corollary 7.3. (Schur’s lemma for (g, K)-modules) Any endomor-
phism of an irreducible (g, K)-module M is a scalar. Thus the center
Z(g) of U(g) acts on M by a infinitesimal character χ : Z(g) →C.

The character χ is often also called the infinitesimal character of
M.
