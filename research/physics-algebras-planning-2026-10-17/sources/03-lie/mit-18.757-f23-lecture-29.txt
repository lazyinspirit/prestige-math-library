29. The Beilinson-Bernstein Localization Theorem

29.1. The Beilinson-Bernstein localization theorem for the zero
infinitesimal character. Let g be a complex semisimple Lie algebra
and U0 be the maximal quotient of U(g) corresponding to the infini-
tesimal character χρ = χ−ρ of the trivial representation of g. Recall
that gr(U0) = O(N). Let G be the corresponding simply connected
complex group and F the flag variety of G; thus F ∼= G/B for a Borel
subgroup B ⊂G. Let D(F) be the algebra of global differential opera-
tors on F; it is clear that grD(F) ⊂O(T ∗F). Also, we have a natural
filtration-preserving action map a : U(g) →D(F), induced by the Lie
algebra homomorphism g →Vect(F).

Theorem 29.1. (Beilinson-Bernstein, [BB]) (i) The homomorphism
a : U(g) →D(F) factors through a homomorphism a0 : U0 →D(F).
(ii) One has gr(a0) = p∗where p is the Springer map T ∗F →N.
(iii) grD(F) = O(T ∗F) and a0 is an isomorphism.

Proof. (i) Let z ∈Z(g) be an element acting by zero in the trivial
representation of g. Our job is to show that for any rational function
f ∈C(F) we have a(z)f = 0. Writing F as G/B, we may view f as
a rational function on G such that f(gb) = f(g), b ∈B. The function
a(z)f on G is the result of action on f of the right-invariant differential
operator Lz corresponding to z: a(z)f = Lzf.
Since z is central,
this operator is also left-invariant: Lz = Rz.
Since z acts by zero
on the trivial representation, using the Harish-Chandra isomorphism,
we may write z as �

Thus Rz = �

translations by B. Thus Rzf = 0 and we are done.
(ii) It suffices to check the statement in degrees 0 and 1, where it is
straightforward.
(iii) The statement follows from (i), (ii) and the fact that p∗is an
isomorphism (Theorem 27.16).
□

The isomorphism a0 gives rise to two functors: the functor of global
sections Γ : M(F) →D(F) −mod ∼= U0 −mod and the functor
of localization Loc : U0 −mod ∼= D(F) −mod →M(F) given by
Loc(M)(U) := D(U) ⊗D(F) M for an affine open set U ⊂F. Note that
by definition the functor Loc is left adjoint to Γ.
The following theorem is a starting point for the geometric represen-
tation theory of semisimple Lie algebras (in particular, for the original
proof of the Kazhdan-Lusztig conjecture).

Theorem 29.2. (Beilinson-Bernstein localization theorem, [BB]) The
functors Γ and Loc are mutually inverse equivalences. Thus the cate-
gory U0 −mod is canonically equivalent to the category of D-modules
on the flag variety F.

We will not give a proof of this theorem here.
Theorem 29.2 motivates the following definition.

Definition
29.3. A smooth algebraic variety X is said to be
D-affine if the global sections functor Γ : M(X) →D(X) −mod
is an equivalence (hence Loc is its inverse).

It is clear that any affine variety is D-affine. Also we have

Corollary 29.4. Partial flag varieties of semisimple algebraic groups
are D-affine.

29.3. The localization theorem for non-zero infinitesimal char-
acters. We are now ready to generalize the localization theorem to
non-zero infinitesimal characters. Let Uλ be the minimal quotient of
U(g) corresponding to the infinitesimal character χλ−ρ. Recall that
gr(Uλ) = O(N).
Let �F := G/[B, B]. We have a right action of T := B/[B, B] on
this variety by y �→yt, defining the structure of a principal T-bundle
�F →F. Thus for every λ ∈P ⊗Z C = h∗we have a sheaf of λ-twisted
differential operators Dλ,F = Dλ on F. For example, if λ ∈P then Dλ
is the sheaf of differential operators acting on sections of the line bundle
Lλ appearing in the Borel-Weil theorem (Theorem 27.3). Let Dλ(F)
be the algebra of global λ-twisted differential operators on F; it is clear
that grDλ(F) ⊂O(T ∗F). Also, we have a natural filtration-preserving
action map a : U(g) →Dλ(F).

Theorem 29.6. (Beilinson-Bernstein) (i) The map

a : U(g) →Dλ(F)

(ii) One has gr(aλ) = p∗where p is the Springer map T ∗F →N.

Proof. The proof is completely parallel to the proof of Theorem 29.1.

As in the untwisted case, the isomorphism aλ gives rise to two func-
tors: the functor of global sections

Γ : Mλ(F) →Dλ(F) −mod ∼= Uλ −mod

and the functor of localization

Loc : Uλ −mod ∼= Dλ(F) −mod →Mλ(F)

given by Loc(M)(U) := Dλ(U)⊗Dλ(F) M for an affine open set U ⊂F.
Moreover, as before, Loc is left adjoint to Γ.
Let us say that λ ∈h∗is antidominant if −λ is dominant (cf.
Subsection 16.1).

Theorem 29.7. (Beilinson-Bernstein localization theorem) If λ is an-
tidominant then the functors Γ and Loc are mutually inverse equiva-
lences. Thus the category Uλ −mod is canonically equivalent to the
category of Dλ-modules on the flag variety F.

Remark 29.8. 1. As explained above, for β ∈P we have an equiv-
alence Mλ(F) ∼= Mλ+β(F) defined by tensoring with Lβ.
On the
other side of the Beilinson-Bernstein equivalence this corresponds to
translation functors defined in Subsection 24.1.
2.
The first statement of Theorem 29.7 fails if λ is not assumed
antidominant. Indeed, if λ is integral but not antidominant then by
the Borel-Weil theorem (Theorem 27.3) Γ(F, Lλ) = 0, so the functor
Γ is not faithful. The second statement of Theorem 29.7 also fails if
λ ∈P and λ −ρ is not regular.
For example, for g = sl2 and λ ∈Z, the localization theorem holds
for λ ≤0. For λ ≥2 the first statement fails but we still have an
equivalence Mλ(F) ∼= Uλ −mod (as Uλ ∼= U−λ+2), albeit not given
by Γ. But for λ = 1 there is no such equivalence at all; in fact, one
can show that the category Uλ −mod, unlike Mλ(F), has infinite
cohomological dimension.
