QUASI-QUANTUM GROUPS ARISING FROM COTENSOR
COQUASI-HOPF ALGEBRAS

JING YU AND XIANGJUN ZHEN

Abstract. In this paper, we show that for any coquasi-Hopf algebra H and any H-Majid bi-
module M, the cotensor coalgebra CoTH(M) admits a graded coquasi-Hopf algebra structure.
We then define the standard filtration of a coquasi-Hopf algebra and show that the associated
graded coquasi-Hopf algebra admits an embedding into a cotensor coquasi-Hopf algebra that
preserves degrees zero and one. If, in addition, this coquasi-Hopf algebra is coquasitriangu-
lar (respectively, coribbon), then the cotensor coquasi-Hopf algebra is also coquasitriangular
(respectively, coribbon), and the above embedding is a morphism of coquasitriangular (re-
spectively, coribbon) coquasi-Hopf algebras. We also prove that if a coquasi-antipode S maps
the coradical into itself, then S is bijective if and only if its restriction to the coquasi-Hopf
subalgebra generated by the coradical is bijective. As a byproduct, we prove that the coquasi-
antipode of a coquasi-Hopf algebra with the dual Chevalley property is bijective. Moreover, we
establish graded quasi-coquasi-Hopf pairings between tensor quasi-Hopf algebras and cotensor
coquasi-Hopf algebras.

1. Introduction

The notions of tensor and cotensor Hopf algebras were introduced by Nichols in his founda-
tional work on bialgebras of type one [47]. Given a Hopf algebra and a Hopf bimodule, these
constructions yield a systematic method for constructing graded Hopf algebras and studying
their algebraic and coalgebraic structures. In this direction, Rosso [50] realized the positive
Borel part of a quantized enveloping algebra as the subalgebra generated by the degree-zero
and degree-one components of a suitable cotensor Hopf algebra (namely, the quantum sym-
metric algebra). Covering quivers [36] and Hopf quivers [19] were also studied within the
framework of tensor and cotensor Hopf algebras. Later, Chen [18] established a link between
the two structures via a Hopf pairing. Further developments include Fang-Rosso’s multi-brace
cotensor Hopf algebras [34], realizing quantum groups for symmetrizable Kac-Moody alge-
bras and their highest-weight representations, and Fang-Jian’s [33] Hopf algebra structures
on cotensor coalgebras from Hopf bimodule algebras, related to quantum quasi-shuffle alge-
bras and Rota-Baxter structures, and encompassing quantum groups and universal Clifford
algebras.
Meanwhile, quasi-Hopf algebras were introduced by Drinfeld [25] in connection with the
Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov equations of conformal field theory, while their dual counterparts,
coquasi-Hopf algebras, were developed by Majid in the context of Tannaka-Krein reconstruc-
tion [45]. Following Drinfeld’s philosophy of quantum groups [24], both mutually dual struc-
tures can be understood within the framework of quasi-quantum groups. By allowing coasso-
ciativity or associativity to be controlled by a (re)associator, these structures extend the Hopf
algebra framework and provide algebraic models for tensor categories, conformal field theory,

struction of Nichols [47] to the quasi-Hopf algebra context. This naturally raises the question
of whether an analogous cotensor construction can be developed for coquasi-Hopf algebras.
Moreover, Etingof and Ostrik [31] showed that a finite tensor category in which all objects
have integer Frobenius-Perron dimensions, called a finite integral tensor category, can be real-

tensor categories reduces to that of the associated quasi-quantum groups. Current classifi-

Chevalley case, that is, the case in which the coradical is a coquasi-Hopf subalgebra. Examples
include the pointed case [39], which extends the classification results for Hopf algebras [4], and
the cotriangular case with the dual Chevalley property [27, 30]. In our previous work [57], we
developed a quiver approach to coquasi-Hopf algebras with the dual Chevalley property and
studied their corepresentation type.
However, in certain cases, the dual Chevalley property does not hold. For instance, the
tensor product structure of the symplectic fermion category shows that the finite-dimensional
ribbon quasi-Hopf algebras constructed by Farsad, Gainutdinov, and Runkel [35] fail the
Chevalley property; consequently, their duals are coribbon coquasi-Hopf algebras without the
dual Chevalley property. From a Hopf-algebraic point of view, Andruskiewitsch and Cuadra [2]
introduced a new filtration on Hopf algebras, termed the standard filtration, which generalizes
the coradical filtration and is designed to investigate more general Hopf algebras. A central
idea in their work is to consider the algebra generated by the coradical. This approach was
also highlighted in Angiono’s report at the 2026 International Congress of Mathematicians [5],
where it was noted that the classification of Hopf algebras without the dual Chevalley property
remains an open problem, and that the framework of Andruskiewitsch and Cuadra is expected
to play a significant role in future developments. It is therefore natural to ask whether this
approach extends to coquasi-Hopf algebras. Moreover, if it does, can cotensor coquasi-Hopf
algebras provide an effective framework for studying the resulting graded structures?
In this paper, we first show that for any coquasi-Hopf algebra H and any H-Majid bimodule

Proposition 2.10). Next, inspired by [2], for a coquasi-Hopf algebra H we define its Majid

then define the standard filtration {H[n]}n≥0 of H by

H[n+1] := H[0] ∧H[n] = ∆−1(H[0] ⊗H + H ⊗H[n]), n ≥0.

It is well known that the corepresentation category of a coquasitriangular (respectively,
coribbon) coquasi-Hopf algebra forms a braided (respectively, ribbon) tensor category.
In
the finite-dimensional setting, these constructions are dual to those for quasitriangular and
ribbon quasi-Hopf algebras. A concrete example of ribbon quasi-Hopf algebras is the twisted
quantum double Dω(G) of a finite group G, constructed by Dijkgraaf, Pasquier and Roche
[22]. Note that the representation category of the fixed-point subalgebra V G of a holomorphic
vertex operator algebra V is equivalent to the module category of some twisted Drinfeld double
Dω(G) [26]. Altsch¨uler and Coste [1] constructed invariants of links and three-manifolds from
ribbon quasi-Hopf algebras.
Creutzig, Gainutdinov, and Runkel [20] gave a ribbon quasi-
Hopf algebra U, whose underlying algebra is that of the restricted quantum group for sl(2)
at a 2pth root of unity, in connection with the triplet VOA W(p).
By taking duals, we
obtain examples of coribbon coquasi-Hopf algebras. These connections provide motivation for
studying coquasitriangular and coribbon structures on cotensor coquasi-Hopf algebras. We
first study coquasitriangular structures on cotensor coquasi-Hopf algebras (see Propositions
4.1 and 4.4). We then prove that if H is a coquasitriangular (respectively, coribbon) coquasi-
Hopf algebra, then CoTH[0](H[1]/H[0]) is also coquasitriangular (respectively, coribbon), and
the embedding (1.1) is a morphism of coquasitriangular (respectively, coribbon) coquasi-Hopf
algebras; see Propositions 4.6 and 4.13.
We also give the definition of a quasi-coquasi-Hopf pairing (see Definition 5.3), which gener-
alizes that of a Hopf pairing [41], and establish a connection between quasi-Hopf algebras and
coquasi-Hopf algebras. Let A be a quasi-Hopf algebra and H a coquasi-Hopf algebra. Let M
be a two-sided two-cosided quasi-Hopf A-bimodule and N an H-Majid bimodule. We establish
a connection between the tensor quasi-Hopf algebra TA(M) constructed by Bulacu [13] and
the cotensor coquasi-Hopf algebra CoTH(N) via a quasi-coquasi-Hopf pairing. Specifically,
we show that a graded quasi-coquasi-Hopf pairing

ψ : TA(M) × CoTH(N) →k

exists if and only if there exists a pairing (ψ0, ψ1) between (A, M) and (H, N); see Theorem
5.8. This result generalizes that of Chen [18].
The organization of this paper is as follows: In Section 2, we recall the definitions of coquasi-
Hopf algebras, Majid bimodules, Yetter-Drinfeld modules, and cotensor coalgebras, along with
some of their basic properties. We prove that, for a coquasi-Hopf algebra H and a Majid bi-
module M, the cotensor coalgebra CoTH(M) can be endowed with a graded coquasi-Hopf alge-
bra structure. In Section 3, we first give the definitions of the Majid coradical and the standard
filtration. We then construct a graded coquasi-Hopf algebra associated with this filtration and
show that it admits an embedding into a cotensor coquasi-Hopf algebra that preserves degrees
zero and one. As an application, we investigate the bijectivity of the coquasi-antipode. In Sec-
tion 4, we show that if H is a coquasitriangular (respectively, coribbon) coquasi-Hopf algebra,
then CoTH[0](H[1]/H[0]) is also coquasitriangular (respectively, coribbon), and the embedding
is a morphism of coquasitriangular (respectively, coribbon) coquasi-Hopf algebras. Section 5
is devoted to establishing quasi-coquasi-Hopf pairings between tensor quasi-Hopf algebras and
cotensor coquasi-Hopf algebras.

Recall that a ring category over k is a locally finite k-linear abelian monoidal category C
with a bilinear and biexact tensor product such that EndC(1) ∼= k. Here, by a locally finite
category [29, Definition 1.8.1], we mean one whose morphism spaces are finite-dimensional
and in which every object has finite length. A locally finite category is said to be finite [29,
Definition 1.8.6] if it has enough projective objects and finitely many isomorphism classes of
simple objects. It is clear that the category of finite-dimensional comodules of a coquasi-Hopf
algebra ( not necessarily finite-dimensional) forms a ring category whose objects all possess
left duals.
Let C be a ring category, and let

0 →X
ι−→Z
π−→Y →0

be a short exact sequence in C . Etingof and Penneys [32, Lemma 4.2] proved that, assuming
C is braided, if X and Y have both left and right duals, then so does Z. They also noted that
the use of the braiding in the proof of [32, Lemma 4.2] is not essential (see [32, Remark 4.4]).
For details on braided categories, see [29]. In [55, Appendix A], Shimizu and Yadav showed
that [32, Lemma 4.2] remains valid in the above form without assuming a braiding, by means
of a careful examination of the original proof. Later, they [55, Theorem A.2] proved that if
C is a ring category in which every object has finite length and every simple object of C has
both left and right duals, then so does every object of C .
From the above analysis, we obtain the following proposition.

Theorem 3.10. Let H be a coquasi-Hopf algebra over k with a coquasi-antipode (S, α, β) such
that S(H0) ⊆H0. Then S is bijective if and only if S |H[0] is bijective.

Proof. Since the “only if” part is trivial, it remains only to prove the “if” part. By Lemma
3.9, it suffices to prove that S is surjective. Note that the category M H of finite-dimensional
right H-comodules is a ring category in which every object has a left dual. We claim that
every object of M H has a right dual. In fact, since S |H[0] is bijective, it follows that M H[0]
is a tensor category. By Lemma 3.1(2), we have H0 ⊆H[0]; consequently, every simple right
H-comodule has a right dual. According to [55, Theorem A.2], every finite-dimensional right
H-comodule has a right dual. If we regard every finite-dimensional subcoalgebra C of H as a
right H-comodule via the comultiplication, then, by [29, Remark 2.10.3], we have

(∗C)∗∼= C.

This implies that C ⊆S(H). By the fundamental theorem of coalgebras [23, Theorem 1.4.7],
every element of H is contained in a finite-dimensional subcoalgebra. Thus S is surjective;
together with its injectivity, this implies that S is bijective.
□
