We show that a compact rigid balanced braided monoidal category with enough com-
pact projective objects gives rise to a system of mapping class group representations
compatible with the gluing along marked intervals.
A motivation to consider non-
finite tensor categories is the theory of vertex operator algebras where such categories
arise as categories of modules. The mapping class group representations presented in
this article admit a factorization homology description. In other words, they are of
three-dimensional origin and hence obey a holographic principle. A compact projective
symmetric Frobenius algebra endows the representations with a pointing that is map-
ping class group invariant and compatible with the gluing along intervals. This shows
that, at least to some extent, many of the tools for the construction and study of spaces
of conformal blocks and correlators remain available in a non-finite, but rigid setting.

1 Introduction and summary

The monodromy data of a conformal field theory can be described by the notion of a modular
functor [Seg88, MS89, Tur94, Til98, BK01]. An open-closed modular functor assigns vector spaces
that are called spaces of conformal blocks to compact oriented surfaces with parametrized intervals
and circles in their boundary. The spaces of conformal blocks are endowed with an action of the
mapping class group, i.e. the group of isotopy classes of diffeomorphisms preserving the boundary
parametrizations and the orientation. These vector spaces are compatible with the gluing along
intervals and circles, see e.g. [MSWY23]. An open modular functor is the restriction to the open
sector, in other words, we only allow surfaces with marked intervals. This notion is studied in
[MW25] as a modular algebra in the sense of [GK98] over a modular operad of surfaces with
marked intervals, see also [Laz01, MS06, Cos07] for more background on open-closed field theories.
In this note, we define a class of open modular functors in a non-finite setting. We also show
that this open modular functor can be given in terms of factorization homology.
More precisely, let A be a framed E2-algebra in Pr, where Pr denotes the symmetric monoidal
bicategory of k-linear presentable categories, cocontinuous functors and linear natural isomorph-
isms. This means that A is equipped with a cocontinuous monoidal product ⊗: A ⊠A −→A

Open correlators.
A modular functor describes the monodromy data of a chiral conformal field
theory. To describe the full conformal field theory, one needs to exhibit, after the so-called holo-
morphic factorization, a collection of mapping class group invariant vectors inside the spaces of
conformal blocks. These vectors are called correlators, see e.g. [FSWY25] for an overview. The
same notion considered for the open sector of a modular functor leads to the so-called open cor-
relators. For the open modular functor constructed in this note, we show that compact projective
symmetric Frobenius algebras F ∈A give rise to a system of open correlators, i.e. to a collection
of vectors

2 Preliminaries

In this section, we give a reminder on open modular functors and factorization homology. We fix
an algebraically closed field k.

2.1 Open modular functors.
A cyclic operad [GK95] has a structure that allows to consistently
exchange the inputs and the output of the operations. A modular operad [GK98] furthermore
includes self-compositions of operations. An open modular functor is a modular algebra over a
certain modular operad, the so-called open surface operad. We will use a groupoid-valued model
for the open surface operad O. We refer to [MW23, MW25] for the precise definitions and details.
The groupoid of operations O(n) of total arity n+1 ≥0 consists of connected surfaces Σ (always
compact and oriented) with at least one boundary component and n + 1 parametrized intervals on
the boundary. Here a parametrization means an orientation-preserving embedding [0, 1]⊔(n+1) �→
∂Σ. The morphisms of O(n) are isotopy classes of orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms that also
preserve boundary parametrizations. In particular, the automorphism group of Σ is the mapping
class group that will be denoted by Map(Σ). The operadic composition is given by gluing surfaces
along the boundary intervals.
The algebras that we consider in this note take values in Pr, the bicategory of presentable k-
linear categories, cocontinuous functors and linear natural isomorphisms, see e.g. [BCJF14, BJS21,
BJSS21] for more background. The Kelly-Deligne tensor product ⊠endows Pr with a symmetric
monoidal structure, the monoidal unit is the category of vector spaces Vect. We refer to [SP09] for
the precise definition of a symmetric monoidal bicategory.
Cyclic and modular algebras are defined via a self-duality structure on the underlying category.
Let A ∈Pr be equipped with a cocontinuous pairing κ : A ⊠A −→Vect that is

• non-degenerate, i.e. there exists a copairing ∆: Vect −→A⊠A such that the snake relations
hold up to isomorphism,

• symmetric, i.e. κ is a Z2-homotopy fixed point with respect to the action of the symmetric
braiding of Pr.

An open modular functor with label category A amounts to a collection of cocontionuous functors
F(Σ; −) : A⊠n −→Vect together with a Map(Σ)-action, where Σ is a surface with n marked
intervals on its boundary. Furthermore, these functors respect the gluing in the following way:
Given a surface Σ′ obtained by gluing Σ along two intervals on ∂Σ, we have a Map(Σ)-equivariant
natural isomorphism

for

where ∆= ∆′ ⊠∆′′ is inserted in the slots belonging to the intervals that we glue together. This
is called excision. Note that we use Sweedler notation for the copairing ∆; it is a priori not a pure
tensor.

2.2 Factorization homology.
Inspired by [BD04], factorization homology is introduced in [Lur,
AF15] as a homology theory for n-dimensional (topological) manifolds that takes an En-algebra as
input. The relevant case in this note is the factorization homology of two-dimensional manifolds,
we refer to [BZBJ18].
Recall that a framed E2-algebra in Pr is a balanced braided monoidal category A in Pr, i.e. A
is equipped with

• a cocontinuous monoidal product ⊗: A ⊠A −→A with a monoidal unit I, together with
the usual associativity and unit isomorphisms that satisfy coherence conditions,

• a braiding, i.e. a natural isomorphism cX,Y : X ⊗Y ∼= Y ⊗X that satisfies the usual hexagon
relations,

• a balancing, i.e. a natural isomorphism θX : X ∼= X such that

2.3 Cyclic framed E2-algebras.
The framed E2-operad has a cyclic structure [Wah01, SW03,
Bud08]. If A is moreover a cyclic framed E2-algebra, then one can define an open modular functor
in the following way: A cyclic framed E2-algebra is in particular a cyclic associative algebra. By
the results in [MW25], which builds on [Cos04, Gia11, MW23, MW24b], a cyclic associative algebra
in any symmetric monoidal bicategory can be uniquely extended to an open modular functor that
is denoted by A!. This establishes an equivalence between cyclic associative algebras and open
modular functors.

3 Factorization homology construction of non-finite open modular functors

In this section, we introduce a class of cyclic framed E2-algebras in Pr and describe its associated
open modular functor via factorization homology.

3.1 Cyclic framed E2-algebras from non-finite tensor categories.
Let A ∈Pr.
Recall that an
object X ∈A is called

• compact if HomA(X, −) preserves filtered colimits,

• compact projective if HomA(X, −) preserves arbitrary colimits.

We say that A has enough compact projective objects if every object of A is a colimit of compact
projective objects and that A is compact rigid if its a monoidal category whose compact objects
are dualizable. In the following, we will denote by c(A) the full subcategory of compact objects,
and by cp(A), the full subcategory of compact projective objects of A.
The following fact is possibly well known, but we state and prove it here in lack of a reference.

Lemma 3.1. Suppose that A ∈Pr has enough compact projective objects and is compact rigid
with a compact monoidal unit. Then, the left (and right) dual of a compact projective object is
also compact projective.

Proof. Let P ∈A be compact projective and P ∨be its left dual. We will show that P ∨is compact
projective, i.e. HomA(P ∨, −) is cocontinuous. The proof for the right dual is analogous.

• By (2.1), we have HomA(P ∨, −) ∼= HomA(I, P ⊗−). The monoidal unit I is compact and
the monoidal product ⊗is cocontinuous, hence the functor HomA(P ∨, −) preserves filtered
colimits.

• The category A has enough compact projective objects. In particular, it is compactly gen-
erated, i.e. it is the ind-completion of c(A).
Consequently, the functor HomA(P ∨, −) is
cocontinuous if its restriction to c(A) preserves finite colimits. As it is additive, it preserves
finite coproducts, therefore it is sufficient to prove that HomA(P ∨, −) ∼= HomA(I, P ⊗−)
preserves coequalizers.
Let C ⇒C′ →C′′ be a coequalizer in c(A).

Example 3.3. Let C be a linear abelian ribbon category. In particular, C is finitely cocomplete
hence is a framed E2-algebra in Rex, the symmetric monoidal bicategory of finitely cocomplete
linear categories, right exact functors and linear natural isomorphisms. Its ind completion ind C is
a framed E2-algebra in Prc, the subcategory of Pr that consists of compactly generated presentable
linear categories, cocontinuous and compact preserving functors and linear natural isomorphisms
[BZBJ18, Section 3.1].
If furthermore C has enough projective objects, then ind C has enough
compact projective objects. By Proposition 3.2, ind C is therefore a cyclic framed E2-algebra in Pr.
As a technical subtlety, we should highlight that C, however, is generally not a cyclic framed E2-
algebra in Rex or Prc because the copairing ∆might only exist as an ind object, see also [BJS21,
Remark 2.11] for a related problem.
For this reason, passing to the ind completion is not an
artificial step, but necessary to obtain the cyclic structure. We will discuss more specific examples
falling into this example class in Example 3.7 and 3.8.

4 Open correlators

Let us fix a category A ∈Pr which satisfies the conditions spelled out in Proposition 3.2. In
this last section, we exhibit a class of open correlators for the open modular functor A!. Recall
that a system of open correlators is a collection of vectors ξF
Σ ∈A!(Σ; F, . . . , F) that are invariant
with respect to the Map(Σ)-action and that respect gluing. The notion of an open correlator is
formalized in [Woi25b, Section 8] by extending the microcosm principle in the sense of [BD98]
to cyclic and modular algebras. The microcosm principle provides a systematic way to construct
operadic algebras inside an algebra over the same operadic structure. Within this framework, an
open correlator is defined as a modular O-algebra inside A!.
The main result of this section is a characterization of modular O-algebras in A! whose under-
lying object is compact projective. We will first characterize compact projective cyclic associative
algebras inside A. Then we will use the fact that this can be uniquely extended to A!.

4.2 Frobenius structures.
The characterization of self-duality on compact projective objects leads
to the following description of compact projective cyclic associative algebras in terms of symmetric
Frobenius algebras.

Proposition 4.2. A compact projective cyclic associative algebra F in A is a symmetric Frobenius
algebra in A, i.e. F is

• a unital associative algebra with the unit η : I −→F and the multiplication µ : F ⊗F −→F,

• equipped with a non-degenerate symmetric pairing β : F ⊗F −→I in the sense of Proposition
4.1 that is invariant, i.e. β(η, µ) = µ.

Proof. The non-degenerate symmetric pairing β endows F with a self-dual structure by Proposition
4.1. By [Woi25b, Remark 5.4], the cyclic associative algebra structure on F is determined by the
generating operations and relations of the cyclic associative operad.
Namely, each generating
operation of the associative operad induces a structure map on F, while the relations tell us the
conditions that need to be satisfied by the structure maps. By following the presentatation of the
cyclic associative operad given in [MW23, Definition 4.1], we see that the two generating operations
of the associative operad, namely the product and the unit, define two morphisms µ : F ⊗F −→F
and η : I −→F. The associativity and unit isomorphisms of ⊗entails that µ and η endow F
with a unital associative algebra in A. Lastly, the relation (Z) in [MW23, Definition 4.1], i.e. the
isomorphism κ(I, −⊗−) ∼= κ(−, −), implies that β is invariant.

With this result, we obtain the following examples of open correlators:

Corollary 4.3. A compact projective symmetric Frobenius algebra F ∈A defines a consistent
system of open correlators ξF
Σ ∈A!(Σ; F, . . . , F). Conversely, any such system with underlying
compact projective object F defines a symmetric Frobenius algebra structure on F.

Proof. This follows from a microcosmic version of the equivalence between cyclic associative algeb-
ras and open modular functors. More precisely, the collection of cyclic associative algebras in A
forms a groupoid [Woi25b, Remark 5.3]. Likewise, the collection of open correlators for A! forms
a groupoid and these two groupoids are equivalent [Woi25b, Theorem 6.1]. Now the result follows
from Proposition 4.2.
