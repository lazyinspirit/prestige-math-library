Strong-to-weak spontaneous symmetry breaking of higher-form non-invertible
symmetries in Kitaev’s quantum double model

Zijian Song (宋子健)1, ∗and Jian-Hao Zhang (张健豪)2, †

1C. N. Yang Institute for Theoretical Physics, Stony Brook University, Stony Brook, NY 11794, USA
2Department of Physics and Center for Theory of Quantum Matter,
University of Colorado, Boulder, CO 80309, USA

Topological orders can be understood as spontaneous symmetry breaking of higher-form symme-
tries. In the non-abelian case, the broken higher-form symmetries are notably non-invertible. In
this work, we extend this framework to mixed states, where symmetries can be either strong or
weak. In particular, we investigate the strong-to-weak spontaneous symmetry breaking (SWSSB) of
non-invertible higher-form symmetries in non-abelian Kitaev’s quantum double models under deco-
herence. We further show that the resulting decohered quantum double mixed states form a locally
indistinguishable set, which also constitutes an information convex set. Importantly, we emphasize
that the number of extremal points of this convex set equals the ground-state degeneracy of the
corresponding pure state, highlighting that the quantum information encoded in the ground-state
subspace is degraded into classical information captured by the convex set of decohered density
matrices.

Introduction – Topological phases of matter with differ-
ent types of symmetry have been extensively studied over
the past few decades [1–5], including the stable ground-
state degeneracy, long-range entanglement, quasiparticle
excitations with exotic braiding statistics, etc. In par-
ticular, the stability of topological order (TO) under lo-
cal perturbations highlights its potential for advancing
fault-tolerant quantum computation [2, 6, 7]. Recently,
the concept of symmetry has been generalized in many
senses: On the one hand, a distinctive feature of mixed
quantum states is the distinction between strong (exact)
symmetry and weak (average) symmetry. On the other
hand, recent developments in quantum field theory reveal
that the mathematical structure of symmetry is not lim-
ited to groups. Symmetry operators can be generalized
to topological operators in spacetime, which may be non-
invertible. This extends the concept of symmetry from
groups to fusion categories [8–16].
Furthermore, even
topological quantum phases once thought to lie beyond
Landau’s symmetry-breaking classification have been in-
corporated into the traditional paradigm through a gen-
eralized notion of symmetry [17, 18].

Nevertheless, in realistic settings, physical systems are
inevitably coupled to their environments and must be de-
scribed by mixed states rather than by pure states, as in
isolated systems. This makes the study of phases of mat-
ter in mixed states a subject of both fundamental and
practical significance. Recent work has uncovered a vari-
ety of mixed-state quantum phases, including strong-to-
weak spontaneous symmetry breaking (SWSSB) phases,
average symmetry-protected topological (ASPT) phases,
finite-temperature topological phases, and intrinsic topo-
logical phases that arise only in open quantum sys-
tems [19–64].

Previous studies of mixed-state topological orders have
primarily focused on abelian cases. Recently, decoher-
ence of Kitaev’s quantum double model attracted con-

siderable attention [45, 46, 61].
However, a lattice re-
alization of the SWSSB mechanism on these systems
remains unidentified.
In this work, we investigate the
(untwisted) Kitaev’s quantum double model D(G) with
a general finite group G, subject to external decoher-
ence.
We show that the decohered quantum-double
state exhibits an SWSSB of a strong electric 1-form
non-invertible symmetry and weak-to-trivial spontaneous
symmetry breaking (WTSSB) of a dual weak magnetic
1-form non-invertible symmetry. We further prove that
the decohered quantum-double density matrices form a
locally indistinguishable set. At maximal decoherence,
these density matrices further constitute an information
convex set. The number of its extremal points equals the
ground-state degeneracy of the original quantum-double
model, so the quantum information stored in the ground-
state subspace is reduced to classical information stored
in the convex set.

Symmetries of density matrices – Before studying the
decoherence of general non-abelian quantum double mod-
els, we first introduce the types of symmetries that a
density matrix can have.

One perspective from which to study phases of mat-
ter and their phase transitions is through symmetry and
symmetry breaking, an approach known as the Landau
paradigm. Topological phases of matter were historically
believed to lie outside the Landau paradigm. However,
recent studies of generalized symmetries have extended
the Landau paradigm to a broader form that incorpo-
rates higher-form symmetries as well as non-invertible
symmetries. Passing from pure states to density matri-
ces extends the paradigm in a further direction, since a
symmetry may now be realized in two inequivalent ways.

For density matrices, there are accordingly two types
of symmetries, namely strong symmetry and weak sym-
metry. A strong invertible symmetry Us is defined by the

Conclusion and outlook – In this work, we system-
atically study Kitaev’s quantum double model for an
arbitrary finite group G under certain types of local
decoherence, together with the resulting spontaneous
symmetry-breaking patterns. We show that under max-
imal Z-type decoherence, the ground state codespace
C[D(G)] is mapped to a locally indistinguishable set
Q{ρcl}. We further define strong and weak 1-form non-
invertible symmetries of the decohered density matrices
ρcl via closed ribbon operators, and demonstrate that
ρcl exhibits SWSSB of the strong 1-form symmetry and
WTSSB of the weak 1-form symmetry. Finally, we prove
that the locally indistinguishable set Q{ρcl} indeed forms
an information convex set. A basis of ground states la-
beled by definite global holonomy sectors is mapped to
the distinct extremal points of the information convex set
Q{ρcl}, while a general coherent superposition of these
states is mapped to a classical convex combination of the
extremal points. This highlights that the quantum in-
formation originally stored in C[D(G)] is decohered into
classical information stored in Q{ρcl}.
We conclude this work with several open questions. We
have shown that maximal Z-type decoherence maps the
quantum information stored in the ground-state subspace
C[D(G)] to classical information stored in the locally in-
distinguishable convex set Q{ρcl}. A key open problem
is to determine the error threshold of this decodability
transition in Kitaev’s quantum double model under Z-
type noise [77]. We conjecture that for an error channel
E with parameter p below a critical value pc, the error
is correctable in the sense that there exists a quasi-local,
low-depth recovery channel ˜E such that ˜E ◦E[ρ] ≃ρ. By
contrast, for p > pc, no such recovery channel exists.
Another open problem is to extend the paradigm of
SWSSB to string-net models [78, 79]. For instance, one
may consider decohering the doubled Ising anyon the-
ory by incoherently proliferating the ψ ¯ψ anyon, as dis-
cussed in Ref. [45]. Following our analysis, the resulting
decohered density matrix exhibits strong symmetries as-
sociated with the ψ, ¯ψ, ψ ¯ψ, and σ¯σ anyons, while the
remaining 1-form symmetries become weak. Neverthe-
less, a systematic understanding of SWSSB in string-net
models is still lacking, and we leave a detailed study of
this problem to future work.
