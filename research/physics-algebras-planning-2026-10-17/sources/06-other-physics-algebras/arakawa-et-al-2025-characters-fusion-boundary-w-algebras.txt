Characters and fusion rules of boundary W-algebras
TOMOYUKI ARAKAWA1, IGOR ALARCON BLATT2, JETHRO VAN EKEREN2,
AND WENBIN YAN3
Abstract. We study the q-characters and modular data of exceptional W-algebras and
give several examples and applications. We establish equality of q-characters and modu-
lar data between certain boundary W-algebras, leading in particular to a largely complete
determination of fusion rules of exceptional W-algebras in type A.
1. Introduction
The affine W-algebras constitute a large and important class of vertex algebras, with appli-
cations in integrable systems, representation theory, enumerative geometry, and theoretical
physics [24, 31, 11, 43, 1]. Categories of representations of exceptional W-algebras yield
examples of modular tensor categories, and it is an interesting problem to determine the
structure of these categories [26, 7].
Among all exceptional W-algebras, the so called boundary W-algebras are particularly im-
portant for several reasons. It is these W-algebras which arise in the 4d/2d correspondence
[10], and the q-characters of these algebras and their irreducible modules are given by in-
teresting infinite product formulas [48, 38]. In this article the importance of the boundary
W-algebras is due to their role in controlling the fusion rules of exceptional W-algebras more
broadly [7], as we now briefly explain.
Let g be a simple Lie algebra, O ⊂g a nilpotent orbit and k = −h∨+ p/u an admissible
level for g (see Section 2 below for definitions and background material). We denote by
W(O, p/u) the simple quotient of the quantum Drinfeld-Sokolov reduction of the universal
affine vertex algebra V k(g) relative to a nilpotent element f ∈O. There is a certain nilpotent
orbit O = Ou, depending on the denominator u, for which W(O, p/u) is lisse [4] and rational
[7, 40]; we refer to such vertex algebras as exceptional W-algebras. The boundary W-algebras
are those for which p assumes its smallest possible value p = h∨(we assume for simplicity
here that g is simply laced and gcd(h∨, u) = 1). The general pattern is that the fusion
rules of W(Ou, p/u) are obtained as products of fusion rules of the corresponding boundary
W-algebra W(Ou, h∨/u) and of the simple affine vertex algebra Lp−h∨(g). The fusion rules
of the simple affine vertex algebras are well known, so the determination of fusion rules of
exceptional W-algebras in general, largely reduces to the boundary case. See Corollary 1.2
and Section 4.3 below for precise statements and details.
In Proposition 3.6 we give a formula for the S-matrix of a boundary W-algebra, under some
additional hypotheses explained in Section 2.3 below. These hypotheses are satisfied for all
nilpotent orbits Ou in type A (and outside type A for some nilpotent orbits but not others).

We briefly review the description of nilpotent orbits and auxiliary data in terms of the
combinatorics of pyramids [15, 21, 12].
For a partition λ = (λ1, λ2, . . . , λk) of n, with λi ≥λi+1 for each i, a pyramid of λ is an
arrangement of k rows of boxes, λi boxes in the ith row (conventionally with the first row at
the bottom), in which the centre of each box lies over either the centre or the edge of the
box below it, and there are no overhangs.
Nilpotent orbits in g = sln are well known to be parametrised by partitions of n [15]. We
are particularly interested in the nilpotent orbit Ou, which is known [4] to correspond to the
partition [um, s] where n = mu + s and 0 ≤s < u. We consider only left adjusted pyramids,
though the constructions described below are general.
The boxes of the pyramid are labeled with the integers 1, 2, . . . , n, starting at the top left,
running down each column, and filling the columns sequentially from left to right.

The equality of q-characters has technical consequences that will be useful for us below.
Corollary 4.9. The exceptional W-algebras W(sln[um, s], n/s) are self dual.
Proof. By a theorem of Li [39] the vector space of invariant bilinear forms on a conformal
vertex algebra V is isomorphic to the vector space V0/L1V1. For m = 0 our W-algebra is
of principal type, hence strongly generated by vectors of conformal weight 2 and higher, so
V0 = C1 and V1 = 0. By equality of q-characters we have V0 = C1 and V1 = 0 in all the
other cases too. So in all cases there is a 1-dimensional space of invariant bilinear forms.
These vertex algebras are simple, so any nonzero invariant bilinear form is non degenerate,
and so we are done.
□

Remark 4.13. Let V be a rational vertex algebra, and suppose the conformal dimensions of
