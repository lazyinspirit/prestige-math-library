## Precise scope for the planned interface

Choose the actual group first: translations R^D semidirect the proper orthochronous Lorentz group or a specified covering group. Use strongly continuous unitary representations on a specified Hilbert space. A representation of the Lie algebra alone does not select the global group, its covering, or the spectral domains of its unbounded generators.

With mostly-plus metric, translation spectral support lies on momentum orbits. The relevant positive-energy nonzero orbits are timelike mass shells p²=−m²,p⁰>0 and the future null cone p²=0,p⁰>0. Zero momentum is a separate orbit; spacelike or negative-energy orbits should not be smuggled into a positive-energy particle statement. Orbit labels alone do not classify representations: choose an irreducible unitary representation of the appropriate little group and prove the induction/equivalence assertions.

For the connected Lorentz group, a nonzero timelike stabilizer is SO(D−1); a nonzero null stabilizer is ISO(D−2). Their appropriate preimages are used for covering groups. The null little group's translation subgroup distinguishes helicity-type representations (trivial translation action) from continuous/infinite-spin representations (nontrivial translation orbit). Massless does not imply helicity without an added finite-component or little-translation condition. For D=3 the universal Lorentz cover is not the familiar double cover, and arbitrary real spin on the universal cover must not be excluded by a blanket half-integrality claim. Descent to a specified quotient imposes a separate central-kernel condition.

The translation generators should be treated using the joint spectral theorem and induced-representation/Mackey machinery. Momentum plane waves in the source are generalized vectors or distributional notation; they are not an orthonormal basis of ordinary normalizable Hilbert vectors. Section3.2 supplies a little-algebra computation, but a Lie-algebra stabilizer calculation alone does not prove the exact global stabilizer/cover or Mackey's measurable-classification hypotheses.

The existing local general unitary induction, Mackey imprimitivity/uniqueness, spectral/Stone, and group-cover suppliers are reuse candidates. The new mathematical delta is their explicit application: classify orbits/stabilizers, choose measure and measurable sections, form the induced Hilbert space and cocycle, prove strong continuity/unitarity, verify irreducibility and equivalence/classification under the actual local theorem's hypotheses, and handle cover descent. Exact source IDs and full statement hypotheses must be checked before native authoring.

## Source-proof cautions

PDFp.5's proposed proof that a connected noncompact simple group has no nontrivial finite-dimensional unitary representation uses the assertion that a compact group cannot contain a noncompact subgroup. That inference needs closedness and is false for arbitrary subgroups. It also treats injectivity as guaranteeing a continuous inverse onto the image. The stated theorem has a sound Lie-theoretic route, but this source proof should not be copied. Use an independently checked local representation/Lie-algebra proof and its connectedness/simple/noncompact hypotheses. Low-dimensional simplicity claims likewise require the actual real form, rather than assuming every dimension-four orthogonal real form has the same splitting behavior.

The source's one-particle/free-field interpretation is a physical modelling choice. A unitary Poincaré representation is not by itself a proof of an interacting QFT, local covariant field, spin-statistics, or experimental particle realisation. These connections remain separate physics/model statements.
