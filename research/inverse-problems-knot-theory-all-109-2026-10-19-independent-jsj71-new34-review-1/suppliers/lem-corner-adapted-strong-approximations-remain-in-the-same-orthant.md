## Statement

Let $F:K\to[0,\infty)^c\times\mathbb R^{n-c}$ be a finite whole-star smooth
embedding, and write $\rho_iF$ for its first $c$ coordinate functions. Assume
for each original simplex $\sigma$ and each $i$ that
$Z_{i,\sigma}=\{x\in\sigma:\rho_iF(x)=0\}$ is a union of faces and that, at
every $b\in Z_{i,\sigma}$, the linear form $d(\rho_iF)_b$ on the tangent cone of
$\sigma$ is nonnegative with zero set precisely the tangent cone of
$Z_{i,\sigma}$. These are the adapted face/tangent conditions, not merely
simplex rank conditions.

There is $\delta>0$ such that every strong $\delta$-approximation $G$ preserving
each of these coordinate planes on the specified zero subcomplexes maps all of
$K$ into the same closed orthant and has exactly the same coordinate zero sets.
It is an embedding for this choice of $\delta$. Thus plane-preserving fitting
and extension can be used in corner charts when the initial pieces satisfy these
adapted face conditions.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the finite whole-star smooth embedding $F:K\to[0,\infty)^c\times\mathbb R^{n-c}$, its coordinate functions $\rho_1F,\dots,\rho_cF$, the zero sets $Z_{i,\sigma}=\{x\in\sigma:\rho_iF(x)=0\}$ (unions of faces), the adapted face/tangent hypotheses, and a strong $\delta$-approximation $G$ that preserves each coordinate plane on the specified zero subcomplexes ([[def-relative-smooth-triangulation-and-projection-preserving-fitting]]).

[F1] Whole-star smooth embeddings are stable under sufficiently small strong approximation: there is $\delta_0>0$ such that every strong $\delta_0$-approximation of $F$ is again a whole-star embedding ([[lem-piecewise-smooth-embeddings-are-stable-under-fine-c-one-approximation]]).

[F2] Segment integration bounds the change of a smooth function by the supremum of its directional derivative times the segment length, and every continuous positive function on a nonempty compact set has a positive minimum ([[cor-vector-valued-ftc-and-lipschitz-bound]], [[thm-extreme-value-metric]]).

[F3] Orthant preservation means $\rho_iG\ge0$ for every $i$ and equality of the zero sets; the coordinate planes are preserved in the sense that $\rho_iG=\rho_iF=0$ on the given zero subcomplexes. All these notions are those of [[def-relative-smooth-triangulation-and-projection-preserving-fitting]].

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Structure of a zero set. Fix a coordinate and an original simplex, writing $u=\rho_iF$ and $Z=u^{-1}(0)\cap\sigma$. The zero set is a finite union of faces. Its maximal faces are pairwise disjoint: if two met at $b$, the hypothesis would identify their union of tangent cones with the zero face of the convex cone $C_b$ under the supporting form $du_b$. This zero face is convex and corresponds to a face $\tau$ of $\sigma$ containing $b$: the supporting form vanishes on the direction space of the minimal face containing $b$, is nonnegative on every vertex direction $v-b$, and its zero cone is generated by that direction space and precisely the zero-valued vertex directions. A relative interior direction of that face must belong to one of the finitely many zero-face cones, forcing that face to contain $\tau$; hence $\tau\subset Z$, contrary to maximality of two distinct intersecting faces. Thus, near any maximal zero face $\tau$, the only zero face is $\tau$ itself. If $Z=\sigma$, plane preservation already forces $\rho_iG=0$ throughout $\sigma$; assume otherwise that $Z$ is nonempty and proper. For every $z\in\tau$ and every vertex $v$ outside $\tau$, the tangent hypothesis gives $du_z(v-z)>0$. Their finite minimum over the compact face $\tau$ is a number $\kappa_\tau>0$. This is a bound on those transverse vertex directions, not on all unit directions outside the zero cone. [F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 Distance lower bound. Near a proper maximal zero face $\tau$, write $x=(1-t)z+ty$ by barycentric projection, with $z\in\tau$, $y$ in the opposite face and $t$ the sum of coordinates outside $\tau$. For $x$ sufficiently near $\tau$, $t<1/2$ and $|x-z|\le t\operatorname{diam}\sigma$ is arbitrarily small, uniformly along $\tau$. Step 1.1 gives $du_z(y-z)\ge\kappa_\tau$ by expressing $y$ as a convex combination of outside vertices. Uniform continuity of $du$ on $\sigma$ follows directly by covering it with half-radius continuity balls, taking a finite subcover, and using the triangle inequality on each corresponding full-radius ball. It gives $du_{z+s(x-z)}(y-z)\ge\kappa_\tau/2$ for $0\le s\le1$, after choosing the neighbourhood small enough. Segment integration yields $u(x)\ge t\kappa_\tau/2\ge[\kappa_\tau/(2\operatorname{diam}\sigma)]\operatorname{dist}(x,Z)$, since $z\in Z$ and $|x-z|\le t\operatorname{diam}\sigma$. Take the finite minimum of these positive constants over the maximal zero faces. If the compact remainder outside their neighbourhoods is nonempty, the continuous positive ratio $u(x)/\operatorname{dist}(x,Z)$ has a positive minimum there by [F2]; if it is empty, omit this bound. Consequently there is $a>0$ with $u(x)\ge a\operatorname{dist}(x,Z)$ on the whole simplex. No such bound is needed when $Z$ is empty or the whole simplex. [F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Perturbing the coordinate functions. For $x\in\sigma$ choose a closest point $z\in Z_{i,\sigma}$ when the zero set is nonempty and proper; it exists by compactness of $Z_{i,\sigma}$ and the segment $[z,x]$ lies in the convex simplex. Since $\rho_iG(z)=\rho_iF(z)=0$ on the zero subcomplex, segment integration of the piecewise smooth difference along $[z,x]$, split into the finitely many refined pieces and added, gives $|\rho_iG(x)-\rho_iF(x)|\le\delta|x-z|=\delta\operatorname{dist}(x,Z_{i,\sigma})$, using the derivative bound of the strong approximation. Choose $\delta<a/2$ with $a$ from step 2.1. Then $\rho_iG(x)\ge(a/2)\operatorname{dist}(x,Z_{i,\sigma})$, so $\rho_iG\ge0$ on $\sigma$ with equality exactly on $Z_{i,\sigma}$. If $Z_{i,\sigma}=\sigma$, plane preservation gives $\rho_iG=0$ there. If $Z_{i,\sigma}$ is empty, then $\rho_iF$ has a positive minimum $m_\sigma$ on the compact simplex by [F2], and the value error $|G-F|<\delta$ with $\delta<m_\sigma/2$ keeps $\rho_iG$ positive on all of $\sigma$. [F2, F3, step 2.1]

4.1 Conclusion. Choose $\delta>0$ below $\delta_0$ of [F1], every $a/2$ for a proper nonempty zero set, and every $m_\sigma/2$ for an empty zero set, omitting nonexistent cases. Every strong $\delta$-approximation $G$ preserving the zero subcomplexes then has $\rho_iG\ge0$ with zero set exactly $Z_{i,\sigma}$ on every simplex, for every $i$: all coordinate functions are nonnegative and the coordinate zero sets are exactly the prescribed ones, so $G$ maps $K$ into the same closed orthant with the same boundary structure. By [F1] $G$ is again a whole-star embedding. This supplies the side control that affine-plane preservation alone does not give, as the half-plane warning in the cited Munkres exercise requires. [F1, step 3.1] ∎
