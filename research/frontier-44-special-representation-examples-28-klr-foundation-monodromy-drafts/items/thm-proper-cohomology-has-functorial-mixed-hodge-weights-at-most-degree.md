## Statement

Assume AC. The rational cohomology $H^n(Y,\mathbb Q)$ of every proper complex algebraic variety has a functorial mixed Hodge structure with weights at most $n$. For a morphism $g:Y\to U$ with $Y$ proper and $U$ smooth quasiprojective, its pullback $H^n(U)\to H^n(Y)$ is a mixed Hodge morphism for the logarithmic structure on $U$. If $Y\to X$ is proper with $X$ smooth projective of dimension $d$, the proper homological class map
$$H_i^{\mathrm{BM}}(Y,\mathbb Q)=H_i(Y,\mathbb Q)\longrightarrow H^{2d-i}(X,\mathbb Q)(d)$$
is a mixed Hodge morphism after the indicated Tate twist. Its image, and consequently the untwisted image in $H^{2d-i}(X)$, is a pure Hodge substructure. No smoothness of $Y$ is assumed.

## Proof

1.1 Choose $Y_\bullet\to Y$. Resolve the constant rational sheaves and the holomorphic de Rham complexes functorially on this simplicial scheme, so derived global sections form a cosimplicial rational/complex comparison. Every term is a pure weight-zero Hodge complex: its degree-$q$ cohomology is smooth projective cohomology, pure of weight $q$, and its Hodge filtration is strict by compact Kähler $E_1$ degeneration. The simplicial weight lemma thus puts a mixed Hodge structure on the simple complex's cohomology, with $E_1^{p,q}=H^q(Y_p,\mathbb Q),\qquad \operatorname{Gr}^W_qH^n(sR\Gamma(Y_\bullet))=E_2^{n-q,q}.$ Descent identifies this cohomology with derived constant-sheaf cohomology on $Y$. As $p=n-q\ge0$, all its weights are at most $n$. There are finitely many terms on each relevant diagonal and they have finite-dimensional cohomology. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 To identify the derived constant-sheaf cohomology in step 1.1 with ordinary singular cohomology, check the hypotheses of [[lem-locally-contractible-metric-spaces-have-singular-constant-sheaf-cohomology]] on $Y^{\mathrm{an}}$. Choose a finite algebraic affine-open atlas. Each chart is quasi-projective, so [[thm-constructible-sheaves-admit-finite-stratified-triangulations-and-finite-local-cohomology]] applies on that chart. Its deleted-boundary finite simplicial model and [[lem-finite-simplicial-open-stars-have-explicit-conical-product-charts]] give a contractible-open neighborhood basis: at a point in the interior of a simplex, use a small ball in that simplex and a sufficiently small radial cone neighborhood of the compact link; these product neighborhoods form a basis and stay outside the closed boundary subcomplex. Local contractibility therefore holds on $Y$ without requiring a global quasi-projective embedding. Separation makes the analytic diagonal closed on every pair of affine charts, so $Y^{\mathrm{an}}$ is Hausdorff. The degree-zero projective hyperresolution map $Y_0^{\mathrm{an}}\to Y^{\mathrm{an}}$ is a continuous surjection from a compact space, proving compactness. To construct a metric, first observe that compact Hausdorff spaces are normal: separate each pair of points in disjoint closed sets by disjoint open neighborhoods, take finite covers of each compact closed set, and intersect/union these neighborhoods to separate the two sets. For each point choose a smaller chart neighborhood with closure inside an original affine chart, using normality; compactness selects finitely many of these smaller neighborhoods covering $Y$. For each of their closed closures $A_i$, choose an intermediate open $V_i$ with $A_i\subset V_i$ and $\overline V_i$ compactly contained in the original chart $U_i$. Normality constructs continuous cutoffs $f_i$ equal to one on $A_i$ and zero outside $V_i$: choose nested open neighborhoods of $A_i$ indexed by dyadic rationals with each closure contained in the next, and take one minus the infimum of their indices, with the infimum of the empty set defined as one; nesting makes both its strict sublevel and strict superlevel sets open. Each $f_i$ thus has compact support inside $U_i$. Let $\phi_i:U_i\to\mathbb R^{2N_i}$ be the injective affine coordinate map. The map $y\mapsto(f_i(y),f_i(y)\phi_i(y))_i$, with the products extended by zero outside $U_i$, is continuous because $\phi_i$ is bounded on the compact cutoff support. It is injective: some $f_i(y)=1$, and equality of the cutoff coordinate and the coordinate product forces the other point to lie in $U_i$ with the same $\phi_i$ coordinates. A continuous injection from compact $Y$ into Hausdorff Euclidean space is an embedding, since it sends closed sets to compact closed sets. The induced Euclidean metric metrizes $Y$. The declared natural singular/constant-sheaf comparison now applies to every proper $Y$. It identifies the cohomology in step 1.1 with $H^n(Y,\mathbb Q)$, carrying its finite-dimensionality and mixed Hodge structure. [step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 Common refinement induces filtered comparison maps on the simple complexes. By descent the induced ordinary cohomology map is the identity of $H^n(Y)$. It is a mixed Hodge morphism by the mixed-complex theorem and therefore an isomorphism by strictness. This makes the structure independent of its hyperresolution. The relative construction lifts a morphism of proper varieties to hypercovers and gives a filtered pullback; independence makes its induced cohomology map the canonical one. Thus proper pullbacks are mixed Hodge morphisms. [step 1.1, step 2.1, given, construct, algebra]

4.1 Let $g:Y\to U$ and take an SNC smooth projective compactification $U\hookrightarrow\overline U$. Every $Y_p$ maps into the open $U$. Pullback therefore sends logarithmic forms on $\overline U$ to regular forms on $Y_p$: locally all boundary equations pull back to nonvanishing holomorphic functions. Pullback preserves $F$ and sends every nonnegative pole-count weight into the weight-zero ordinary de Rham complex; negative pole-count terms are zero. Choose functorial resolutions so pullbacks along equal composites agree as chain maps. The degree-zero simplicial component is the pullback to $Y_0$, and its alternating face differential vanishes because the two composites $Y_1\to Y_0\to U$ agree. Thus this component defines a chain map from the logarithmic complex into the simplicial simple complex, supported in simplicial degree zero. It preserves weight: the source has only nonnegative pole-count weights, while the target simplicial $W_a$ contains degree zero for every $a\ge0$. This is consequently a morphism of mixed Hodge complexes, without appealing to an unproved general theorem about totalizing cosimplicial mixed complexes. It agrees with rational pullback under the singular comparisons, so it induces the actual map $g^*$ as a mixed Hodge morphism. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, given, construct]

5.1 For proper $Y$, ordinary and Borel–Moore homology coincide, and [[cor-cohomology-over-a-field-is-dual-to-homology-over-that-field]] and finite-dimensionality from the finite-diagonal hypercover computation identify $H_i(Y,\mathbb Q)$ with the dual of $H^i(Y,\mathbb Q)$. Give it that dual mixed Hodge structure. The homological pushforward to $X$ is dual to cohomological pullback, hence is a mixed Hodge morphism by step 3.1. Poincaré duality on smooth projective $X$ identifies $H_i(X)$ with $H^{2d-i}(X)(d)$ as pure Hodge structures: cup product pairs complementary $(p,q)$ types and the integral trace has type $(d,d)$, so the Tate twist removes it. The target is pure of weight $-i$, and the image of a mixed Hodge morphism is a Hodge substructure, hence is pure of that weight. Untwisting gives a pure Hodge substructure in $H^{2d-i}(X)$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, given, algebra] ∎
