## Statement

Let $A\subseteq\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb Q}(V)$ be a finite-dimensional rational algebra. The socle of the $A_{\mathbb C}$-module $V_{\mathbb C}$ is defined over $\mathbb Q$: it is $W\otimes\mathbb C$ for a rational $A$-submodule $W\subseteq V$. If $V\ne0$, then $W\ne0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $A,V$ as stated; the socle is the sum of all simple submodules. Finite-dimensional radicals and their semisimple quotients are [[thm-jacobson-radical-is-nilpotent-and-the-quotient-is-semisimple]]. Complex semisimple algebras are products of complex matrix algebras and their simple modules are column modules ([[thm-wedderburn-artin-theorem]], [[thm-simple-modules-over-semisimple-rings]], [[lem-trace-form-nondegeneracy-characterizes-semisimple-finite-dimensional-algebras]]). Finite sum-of-simples characterizations are [[thm-finite-length-semisimple-module-characterizations]].

## Proof

1.1 Write $B=A_{\mathbb C}$ and $J=J(B)$. Choose a finite composition series of the regular $B$-module by choosing a submodule of minimal positive dimension in each quotient. Its simple factors are killed by $J$: for a simple $T$, $JT=T$ would imply $J^rT=T$ for all $r$, contradicting nilpotence. Their action therefore factors through $B/J=\prod_iM_{d_i}(\mathbb C)$. Every simple factor type of $B/J$ occurs among these composition factors, since a composition series of the quotient lifts to the regular module. If its multiplicities in the regular module are $m_i>0$, the trace of left multiplication on the regular module is $\operatorname{Tr}_B(L_b)=\sum_i m_i\operatorname{tr}(\overline b_i).$ Indeed left multiplication preserves the chosen series, so in an adapted basis its diagonal blocks are the simple-factor actions. Consequently the radical of the regular trace pairing $\operatorname{Tr}(L_{bc})$ is exactly $J$: it is zero on $J$, while on $B/J$ it is $\sum_i m_i\operatorname{tr}(\overline b_i\overline c_i)$, nondegenerate by matrix units and $m_i\ne0$. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The regular-trace Gram matrix in a rational basis of $A$ has rational entries, so its kernel is $J_0\otimes\mathbb C$ for its rational kernel $J_0$. Step 1.1 identifies that complex kernel with $J$. Define $W=\{v\in V:J_0v=0\}$. This is a rational linear kernel, and hence $W_{\mathbb C}=\{v\in V_{\mathbb C}:Jv=0\}$. This latter subspace is a $B/J$-module, so it is a sum of simples; explicitly it is generated by finitely many vectors and therefore is a quotient of a finite direct sum of the semisimple regular $B/J$-module. Images of its simple summands are zero or simple, and the finite sum splits by the finite-length characterization. Every simple $B$-submodule is killed by $J$ as in step 1.1. Thus $W_{\mathbb C}$ is exactly the socle. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 If $V_{\mathbb C}\ne0$, choose the least $r\ge1$ such that $J^rV_{\mathbb C}=0$. Then $J^{r-1}V_{\mathbb C}\ne0$ and is killed by $J$, so the socle is nonzero; the rational kernel $W$ is nonzero because its complexification is. Rational $A$-invariance follows either from the equations defining $W$ and the two-sidedness of $J$, tested over $\mathbb C$, or directly from its complex socle. [step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
## Source notes

This explicit regular-trace argument supplies the rational descent which Deligne II 4.2.6 abbreviates as the complex simple-summand sum being defined over $\mathbb Q$. It is separate from the characteristic-zero scalar-extension theorem and does not assume semisimplicity of $V$.
