Thus this reconciliation includes the scalar centre and proves that the **original** tensor ground is the one used in $E(A)$. ## 2. Exact dimensional and graded information

Each selected row name occurs once in $e_S$. Assigning the names to wedge positions and choosing, in a position of original column $i$, any column in $\{i,\ldots,l\}$ gives the quotient basis after antisymmetrization. Each wedge block has distinct row names, so the signed orbit sums are independent, and there is no vanishing from a repeated row name. Hence
$$\dim Q_A=\frac{d!}{\prod_{i,r}\nu^{(i)}_r!}\prod_{i=1}^l(l-i+1)^{d_i}.$$
The top polynomial symbol of $g_A$ is $X^{\mathbf b}a_-$, where $b_j=i-1$ for a position belonging to the original $i$th factor, and $a_-$ is the Young antisymmetrizer in the reversed factor order. These exponents are constant inside each wedge subgroup. In the truncated polynomial/permutation graded algebra, the left ideal generated by this symbol has the same dimension as the displayed formula: choose minimal Young coset representatives, and in positions of type $i$ choose each additional exponent from $0,\ldots,l-i$. Distinct cosets have disjoint permutation supports, and distinct bounded monomials have distinct polynomial supports. Thus these graded vectors are independent. Lifting their multipliers shows
$$\dim Hg_A\ \ge\ \dim Q_A.$$
This is a lower bound only. It does not show the exact annihilator or give the desired intertwiner in the filtered algebra.

## 3. Smallest remaining operator/fibre map

The natural candidate is
$$F_A:Q_A\longrightarrow Hg_A,\qquad q_A(\mathrm{reg}(b))\longmapsto bg_A.$$
It is automatically left-Hecke-linear and surjective **if it is well defined**. The exact still-unproved calculation is
$$q_A(\mathrm{reg}(b))=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad bg_A=0,$$
i.e. $K_A\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{\mathrm{left}}(g_A)$. Together with the verified dimension inequality of §2, this inclusion would make $F_A$ an isomorphism and prove the stronger equality $K_A=\operatorname{Ann}_{\mathrm{left}}(g_A)$. Therefore the remaining proof can be concentrated on this single kernel-vanishing calculation. It must cover the forbidden-column relations and all their row-corner Hecke translates, as well as the wedge relations, for repeated integral charges. A proof valid only when charge differences are invertible is insufficient.

## 4. Why the full read permutation proof does not fill this calculation

BK0605217 Theorem6.9, read in full, computes symmetric-power **submodules** with columns at most the prescribed columns. Its key identity (6.16) uses a product of factors associated to roots in columns strictly to the right. Its prescribed target columns are nondecreasing, because the tableau boxes are numbered down columns. During the ordered application, earlier positions have already moved left of the currently active position and later positions have not moved; this suppresses the place-exchange terms in Lemma3.3. The rest of the proof is a valid triangular independence argument once that precise inclusion is known.

Exterior factors are reversed and impose lower column bounds. Neither their product order nor their quotient map is the symmetric submodule calculation. For example with $l=2,d=2$ and both entries of $w$ initially in column2, Lemma3.3 gives
$$w(x_2-m_2)=L_2(w)+ws_1.$$
Thus the naive mirror of (6.16) has an extra swap term. If the two positions belong to different exterior groups, there is no wedge relation that removes it. Similarly, with first position of lower bound2 and second of lower bound1, $g_A=x_1-m_1$ and
$$q_A(\mathrm{reg}(g_A))=(m_2-m_1)q_A(w).$$
For repeated charges this is zero even though $Q_A$ is nonzero and $g_A$ has nonzero PBW leading symbol. This prevents using multiplication followed by the quotient as a universal invertible comparison; it does not refute the candidate $F_A$, whose direction is the reverse quotient-to-ideal direction and whose kernel-vanishing remains open.

The symmetric trace converts a **known** regular quotient kernel into an orthogonal ideal, but it does not compute $K_A$ or identify that orthogonal ideal with the specific $Hg_A$. Self-duality of $E(A)$ and commutation of $\pi$ with duality must also be proved in their actual scope before using that alternate conversion. Neither the new coset pairing nor source6.9 supplies this missing kernel calculation.
