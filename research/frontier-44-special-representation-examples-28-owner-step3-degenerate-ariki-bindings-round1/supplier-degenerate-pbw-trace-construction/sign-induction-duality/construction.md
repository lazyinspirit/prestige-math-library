## Exact claim

For nonnegative integers $d_1,\ldots,d_l$ with sum $d$, let $P=H_{d_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes H_{d_l}\subset H_d$ be the affine Young subalgebra on consecutive blocks. For every finite-dimensional left $P$-module $M$, its induced module $H_d\otimes_P M$ is finite-dimensional, and its linear dual with action $(h\varphi)(z)=\varphi(h^*z)$ is canonically isomorphic, by the pairing below, to induction from the reversed consecutive blocks of the correspondingly transported $M^*$. Here $*$ is the anti-involution fixing each $x_i,s_i$. In particular, for $M=\bigboxtimes_j\mathrm{ev}_{m_j}^*S^{\mu^{(j)}}$, this gives
$$S(A)^*\cong \mathrm{ev}_{m_l}^*S^{\mu^{(l)}}\circ\cdots\circ\mathrm{ev}_{m_1}^*S^{\mu^{(1)}}=\widetilde S(A).$$
No induction/coinduction isomorphism or unproved Frobenius-extension theorem is assumed.

## Full local proof

**Given:** The affine ordered PBW basis and the native cross relations, the generator-fixing anti-involution, and finite-dimensional $M$ as above.

1.1 **Finite parabolic coset PBW.** Write $W=S_d$, $W_P=S_{d_1}\times\cdots\times S_{d_l}$ and let $U$ be the permutations $u$ whose values are increasing on each consecutive original block. These are the minimal representatives for the right cosets $W/W_P$: every permutation has a unique factorization $u p$ with $u\in U$, $p\in W_P$, obtained by sorting its values inside each block. The affine PBW basis can equivalently be written with polynomials on the right: moving a variable past a permutation preserves the leading polynomial-degree term and has corrections of smaller polynomial degree, so the change from $x^{\mathbf a}w$ to $w x^{\mathbf a}$ is triangular and invertible degree by degree. Factoring $w=u p$ now gives
$$H_d=\bigoplus_{u\in U}uP$$
as a right $P$-module. Thus $H_d\otimes_PM=\bigoplus_{u\in U}u\otimes M$ has dimension $|U|\dim M=d!\dim M/\prod_jd_j!$. This is finite coset PBW, although $H_d$ and $P$ themselves are infinite-dimensional. [given, algebra]

1.2 **The longest block-reversing shuffle.** Let $r\in U$ send the first original block to the last new block, the second to the penultimate new block, and so on, preserving the order inside every block. It has one inversion for each pair from two different original blocks, hence length $L=\sum_{j<k}d_jd_k$. Every $u\in U$ has no inversions inside original blocks and at most these $L$ cross-block inversions; only $r$ has all $L$, so it is the unique longest representative. Its conjugate subgroup $W_Q=rW_Pr^{-1}$ is the Young subgroup on reversed consecutive block sizes $(d_l,\ldots,d_1)$. Let $Q=H_{d_l}\otimes\cdots\otimes H_{d_1}$ be the corresponding affine subalgebra, and define $\theta:Q\to P$ by $x_j\mapsto x_{r^{-1}(j)}$ and by mapping each adjacent transposition within a new block to the corresponding adjacent transposition within its original block. All internal orders are preserved, so these images satisfy the native affine relations; the inverse is the opposite block relabeling. Thus $\theta$ is an algebra isomorphism and commutes with $*$. [step 1.1, algebra]

2.1 **Coefficient extraction with the correct affine correction terms.** Define $E:H_d\to P$ by $E(\sum_{u\in U}u p_u)=p_r$. It is right $P$-linear. We claim $E(qh)=\theta(q)E(h)$ for $q\in Q$. For a polynomial generator, straighten $x_j u$ through any reduced word of $u$. The native cross relations yield
$$x_j u=u x_{u^{-1}(j)}+\text{terms with permutation length strictly smaller than }\ell(u).$$
Indeed every correction deletes at least one simple reflection from that reduced word; its resulting permutation has length strictly smaller than $\ell(u)$. After factoring into a minimal coset representative and a permutation in $W_P$, a correction cannot have minimal representative $r$, because that would require permutation length at least $L$. If $u\ne r$ even the leading term has the wrong representative. Hence $E(x_jh)=x_{r^{-1}(j)}E(h)$. For a simple reflection $s_j\in W_Q$, $s_jr=r\theta(s_j)$ holds as an exact permutation identity. Left multiplication by $W_Q$ stabilizes the right coset $rW_P$, so no different coset can be sent to this coset; consequently $E(s_jh)=\theta(s_j)E(h)$. These generator identities extend by linearity and multiplication to all $q\in Q$, proving the claimed bimodule identity. The affine correction terms were explicitly excluded by permutation length, not ignored. [given, step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra]

2.2 **The balanced contragredient pairing.** Give $M^*$ its left $P$-action $(p\varphi)(m)=\varphi(p^*m)$, and let $M^\vee$ denote the same vector space made into a left $Q$-module by $q\varphi=\theta(q)\varphi$. Set $I=H_d\otimes_PM$ and $J=H_d\otimes_QM^\vee$. Define
$$\langle a\otimes m,b\otimes\varphi\rangle=\varphi\bigl(E(b^*a)m\bigr).$$
Right $P$-linearity of $E$ gives $\langle ap\otimes m,b\otimes\varphi\rangle=\langle a\otimes pm,b\otimes\varphi\rangle$. On the other side, $(bq)^*=q^*b^*$ and step 2.1 give $E((bq)^*a)=\theta(q^*)E(b^*a)$; this is exactly the equality with $\langle a\otimes m,b\otimes q\varphi\rangle$, since $\theta$ commutes with $*$. Therefore the pairing descends to both tensor products. Also
$$\langle h(a\otimes m),b\otimes\varphi\rangle=\langle a\otimes m,h^*(b\otimes\varphi)\rangle$$
for every $h\in H_d$, because $(h^*b)^*=b^*h$. Thus the map $J\to I^*$ sending $z$ to the functional $y\mapsto\langle y,z\rangle$ is an $H_d$-module map for the generator-fixing duality. [step 2.1, algebra]

3.1 **Perfectness, with an explicit permutation matrix.** Let $V$ be the minimal right coset representatives for $W/W_Q$, equivalently the permutations increasing on each reversed block. The map $v\mapsto vr$ is a bijection $V\to U$: $r$ preserves order inside each original block and sends it onto a new block, so $vr$ is increasing inside each original block; its inverse sends $u$ to $ur^{-1}$, which is increasing on every new block. On pure group elements $v^*=v^{-1}$. The coefficient $E(v^{-1}u)$ is nonzero exactly when $v^{-1}u\in rW_P$, equivalently $u\in vrW_P$. Since $u$ and $vr$ are both the unique minimal representative of that coset, this occurs exactly when $u=vr$; then $v^{-1}u=r$ and $E(v^{-1}u)=1$. Hence
$$\langle u\otimes m,v\otimes\varphi\rangle=\begin{cases}\varphi(m)&u=vr,\\0&u\ne vr.\end{cases}$$
The coset pairing matrix is a permutation matrix tensored with the ordinary perfect pairing $M\times M^*\to\mathbb C$. It is therefore perfect, and the map in step 2.2 is an isomorphism. This proves $I^*\cong J$ for every finite-dimensional $M$. Empty blocks cause no difficulty: omit them, or interpret their affine algebra and module as $\mathbb C$. [step 1.1, step 2.2, algebra]

3.2 **Evaluation Specht factors are self-dual for the required anti-involution.** On $H_n$, $\mathrm{ev}_m(s_i)=s_i$ and $\mathrm{ev}_m(x_j)=m+\sum_{k<j}(k\ j)$, by the recursion $x_{j+1}=s_jx_js_j+s_j$. Each transposition is self-adjoint because it is an involution preserving the form. Hence every evaluated $x_j$, being a scalar plus a sum of transpositions, is self-adjoint too. Thus this form identifies $\mathrm{ev}_m^*S^\mu$ with its affine-Hecke dual for $*$, not merely with its group dual. Tensor the forms on the finite factors. Under $\theta$, the resulting $M^\vee$ is exactly the external product with the factors in reversed order. Applying step 3.1 proves the reversed induction product assertion for $S(A)$. If the original $S(A)$ has already been proved to factor through $H_d(\mathbf m)$ and its content block, then the isomorphic dual/reversed product factors through the same quotient/block, since the defining ideal and the central content projector are $*$-stable. The proof does not assume cyclotomic factorization for every individual dot. [step 3.1, algebra]
