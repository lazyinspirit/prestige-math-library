## Definition

Let $H_d$ be the degenerate affine Hecke algebra of
[[def-degenerate-affine-hecke-algebra]] and let
$\mathbf a=(a_1,\dots,a_l)$ be a multiset of complex numbers, $l\ge1$. The
**degenerate cyclotomic Hecke algebra** is the quotient
$$H_d(\mathbf a):=H_d\Big/\Bigl(\prod_{j=1}^l(x_1-a_j)\Bigr),$$
the two-sided ideal being the one generated by the displayed polynomial
([[def-left-right-and-two-sided-ideal]], [[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]],
[[thm-quotient-ring-laws]]).

The verification below records the structure of $H_d(\mathbf a)$ used on this
page. A $\mathbb C$-basis is given by the ordered monomials
$$x_1^{r_1}\cdots x_d^{r_d}w,\qquad 0\le r_1,\dots,r_d<l,\ w\in S_d,$$
so that $\dim H_d(\mathbf a)=l^dd!$; the polynomial-degree filtration has
associated graded the algebra
$\mathbb C[x_1,\dots,x_d]/(x_1^l,\dots,x_d^l)\rtimes\mathbb C[S_d]$. For
integral roots $a_j\in\mathbb Z$ this is the cyclotomic quotient $H_d(\Lambda)$
attached to the dominant weight $\Lambda=\sum_j m_j\Lambda_j$ of the integer
type-A root system, with $m_j=\#\{i:a_i=j\}$; for general complex roots one
uses a separate integer chain in each coset of $\mathbb C/\mathbb Z$, and the
symbol $\Lambda_a$ denotes a formal fundamental weight on that chain, not an
index of the integer-only lattice. The pyramid roots of the finite-W-algebra
constructions are $a_j=c_j+q_j-n$ for the origin $c=(c_1,\dots,c_l)$ and the
pyramid columns $q_1\ge\cdots\ge q_l$. If $l=1$, then
$x_j=a_1+\sum_{k<j}(k\ j)$ in the quotient. The generator-fixing
anti-involution of $H_d$ descends to $H_d(\mathbf a)$. The top coefficient
functional of the monomial basis is a nondegenerate symmetric trace on
$H_d(\mathbf a)$, and it restricts to a nondegenerate trace on every central
block corner.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The algebra $H_d$ with its basis and relations, a multiset $\mathbf a=(a_1,\dots,a_l)$ of complex numbers, and the quotient $H_d(\mathbf a)$.

[F1] $H_d$ has the ordered monomial basis $x_1^{r_1}\cdots x_d^{r_d}w$ and the cross relations $s_ix_{i+1}=x_is_i+1$, $x_{i+1}s_i=s_ix_i+1$; the generator-fixing anti-involution exists ([[def-degenerate-affine-hecke-algebra]]).

[F2] The finite W-algebra and Skryabin constructions provide concrete realizations: for an admissible pyramid with roots $a_j=c_j+q_j-n$ there is a filtered $(W(\lambda),H_d(\mathbf a))$-bimodule structure on the truncated tensor space, whose associated graded is the Vust tensor space ([[thm-the-loop-filtration-on-the-type-a-finite-w-algebra-has-associated-graded-u-of-g-e]], [[thm-skryabins-equivalence-for-the-type-a-whittaker-pair]], [[def-a-type-a-nilpotent-pyramid-good-grading-and-whittaker-character]]).

[F3] The Vust double centralizer: on $V^{\otimes d}$ with individual $e$-operators and places, the right truncated polynomial/permutation algebra $B$ and the enveloping image of $U(\mathfrak g^e)$ are mutual centralizers, and the right action is injective when $e$ has at least $d$ strings of maximum length ([[thm-vust-double-centralizer-for-the-type-a-nilpotent-centralizer]]).

[F4] The Hecke operators $x_j$ on tensor space satisfy the cross relations, and the Casimir operator on the first tensor factor is $e$ in the associated graded ([[thm-the-casimir-and-place-permutation-operators-satisfy-degenerate-affine-hecke-relations]]).

[F5] PBW and the associated graded: ordered monomials in a filtered basis compute the associated graded algebra ([[thm-pbw-ordered-monomial-basis-for-the-enveloping-algebra]], [[def-associated-graded-algebra-of-a-filtered-algebra]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** The quotient $H_d(\mathbf a)$ and the constants of the Definition.

1.1 Straightening. Using the cross relations, every word in the generators of $H_d$ can be rewritten as a linear combination of ordered monomials $x^rw$ with $r\in\mathbb N^d$, $w\in S_d$; in the quotient the relation $x_1^l=-c_1x_1^{l-1}-\cdots-c_l$ (the expansion of $\prod_j(x_1-a_j)$, where the $c_i$ are the elementary symmetric functions of $\mathbf a$) reduces every exponent $r_1$ to the range $0\le r_1<l$ by induction on $r_1$, and the other exponents are unrestricted so far. Hence the bounded monomials span $H_d(\mathbf a)$. [F1, algebra]

1.2 The admissible realization. Choose an integer coset decomposition of the multiset $\mathbf a$ and, in each coset, let $c_\kappa$ be a representative; pick $n$ so large that $q_j:=n+a_j-c_{\kappa(j)}\ge d$ for every $j$ and the $q_j$ can be ordered decreasingly. Then the numbers $a_j=c_j+q_j-n$ are the pyramid roots of an admissible pyramid, and by [F2] the truncated tensor space carries commuting actions of the finite W-algebra and of $H_d$ in which the individual operator $x_i$ has leading term the diagonal $e$-operator on the $i$-th factor. [F2, F4, construct]

2.1 Independence. If $e$ has at least $d$ strings of maximum length $l'=\max_jq_j$, the Vust longest-string vector argument [F3] makes the right action of the truncated polynomial/permutation algebra injective; reading the bounded monomials $x_1^{r_1}\cdots x_d^{r_d}w$ with $r_i<l$ through this faithful action, their images are linearly independent because the leading term of $x_i$ is the individual $e$-operator and the leading symbols are exactly the Vust basis monomials. Hence the bounded monomials are independent in $H_d(\mathbf a)$, and with step 1.1 they form a basis; in particular $\dim H_d(\mathbf a)=l^dd!$. [F3, step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 The filtration and the quotient identifications. Since the defining polynomial $\prod_j(x_1-a_j)$ is monic of degree $l$ with lower-degree lower terms, the polynomial-degree filtration of $H_d$ descends to $H_d(\mathbf a)$ and its associated graded is $\mathbb C[x_1,\dots,x_d]/(x_1^l,\dots,x_d^l)\rtimes\mathbb C[S_d]$: in the associated graded the inhomogeneous terms vanish and the relation becomes $x_1^l=0$. The monomial basis of step 2.1 is compatible with this filtration, so the two descriptions (quotient of $H_d$ and the filtered algebra) are the same algebra. [F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Integral roots, cosets, and the pyramid dictionary. If all $a_j$ are integers, the quotient is the cyclotomic quotient attached to the dominant weight $\Lambda$ of the integer type-A root system with $m_i=\#\{j:a_j=i\}$; the cyclotomic polynomial is $\prod_j(x_1-a_j)=\prod_i(x_1-i)^{m_i}$ in the notation of the source. For nonintegral roots the quotient is defined by the same polynomial, but the polynomial's roots do not lie in the integer weight lattice; one therefore retains the integer-chain bookkeeping in each coset separately, as recorded in the Definition, and no assertion is made that the general roots index the integer-only Chevalley lattice. The pyramid roots satisfy the same dictionary through $a_j=c_j+q_j-n$. [F5, step 3.1, algebra]

4.2 The level-one and anti-involution statements. If $l=1$ the polynomial is $x_1-a_1$, and the recursion $x_{j+1}=s_jx_js_j+s_j$ of the Hecke algebra gives $x_j=a_1+u_j$ with $u_j=\sum_{k<j}(k\ j)$, exactly as in the evaluation isomorphism of [[def-degenerate-affine-hecke-algebra]]; the quotient is $\mathbb C[S_d]$ with the Jucys-Murphy action. The generator-fixing anti-involution of $H_d$ preserves the two-sided ideal generated by $\prod_j(x_1-a_j)$ because it fixes $x_1$ and reverses products, so it descends to an anti-involution of $H_d(\mathbf a)$. [F1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 The trace form. Define $\tau\colon H_d(\mathbf a)\to\mathbb C$ as the coefficient of the monomial $x_1^{l-1}\cdots x_d^{l-1}\cdot1$ in the bounded monomial basis. For monomials $u=x^rw$, $v=x^{r'}w'$ the product $uv$ has top coefficient concentrated at the complementary exponents and the inverse permutation: expanding $uv$ in the monomial basis, the coefficient of $x_1^{l-1}\cdots x_d^{l-1}$ is nonzero exactly when $r+r'= (l-1,\dots,l-1)$ componentwise and $ww'=1$, in which case it equals the product of the leading coefficients of the cross relations along the reduced word of $w$, a nonzero number; all other products contribute to strictly smaller exponents. Hence $\tau(uv)=\tau(vu)$ (the two conditions $r+r'=(l-1,\dots)$ and $w'w=1$ are symmetric) and the Gram matrix of $\tau$ in the monomial basis is upper triangular with nonzero diagonal, so $\tau$ is a nondegenerate symmetric trace. A central idempotent block corner is cut out by a central idempotent, and the restriction of a nondegenerate symmetric trace to such a corner remains nondegenerate, because the complementary idempotent acts as zero and the corner is a direct summand. [step 2.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

- The integer and nonintegral cases are kept separate on purpose: the integral case is the classical cyclotomic quotient of the type-A root system, while the general complex roots are handled through the pyramid realization whose columns are chosen large enough to make the tensor action faithful.

- The trace of step 1.7 is the top PBW coefficient functional; it is the tool that rules out nonzero ideals in the radical in the double centralizer arguments on this page.
