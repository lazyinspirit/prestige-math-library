## Scope and finding

This repair changes only `items/cor-ring-of-integers-is-a-dedekind-domain.md`

The prior proof applied the integral-closure corollary with base ring
`Z` and extension `K/Q` without establishing either that `Z` is Dedekind or
that `K/Q` is finite separable. The statement already assumes Choice; that
qualifier and the statement text are preserved.

The repaired proof establishes the supplier hypotheses explicitly:

1. `lem-subgroups-of-z-are-cyclic` classifies every ideal of `Z` as `nZ`, so
   every submodule of the left regular module is generated by one element and
   `Z` is Noetherian.
2. `cor-rational-algebraic-integers-are-integers`, together with the canonical
   identification `Frac(Z) = Q`, proves `Z` is integrally closed.
3. Every nonzero prime ideal is `pZ`; primality gives the Euclid property, so
   `p` is prime. `Z/pZ` is then a field, making each such ideal maximal.
   For the strict chain, the proof now establishes `2` prime inline: for a
   positive divisor `d | 2`, the divisor-bound lemma gives `d <= |2| = 2`,
   and its integer-discreteness argument gives `1 <= d`. If `d != 1`,
   translation of the strict inequality gives `0 < d - 1`; discreteness then
   gives `1 <= d - 1`, hence `2 <= d` and `d = 2`. The natural successor
   facts establish `0 < 1 < 2`, so the prime definition applies. Thus `2Z`
   is maximal and prime, and `(0) < 2Z` gives `dim Z = 1`. These three
   properties prove that `Z` is Dedekind.
4. The number-field definition gives finite-dimensional `K/Q`. Linear
   dependence of powers proves algebraicity; characteristic zero makes `Q`
   perfect, and the published perfect-field separability corollary gives
   separability.
5. With `R=Z`, `Frac(R)=Q`, `L=K`, and integral closure `O_K`, the
   AC-qualified integral-closure corollary now applies exactly.

The first repair draft's Fact F15 had asserted that `2` was prime by citation
to the definition alone. Supplier review showed that this left the divisor
case unproved. The final item replaces that assertion with the explicit
`1 <= d <= 2` argument above, cites the divisor-bound, integer-discreteness,
absolute-value, and order suppliers, and applies the prime definition only
after proving its hypotheses.

No Choice is used in the `Z` or separability arguments. The inherited AC
assumption is retained because the integral-closure supplier explicitly uses
the AC-qualified dimension-preservation result. No B/examples supplier is
used.
