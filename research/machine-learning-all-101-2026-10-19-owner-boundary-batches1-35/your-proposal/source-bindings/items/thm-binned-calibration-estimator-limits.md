## Statement

Let $Y$ be a random variable into $\{1,\ldots,K\}$ with its full power-set sigma-algebra, $K\ge2$, and let $G\in\Delta_K$ be a random forecast with measurable calibration function $r:\Delta_K\to\Delta_K$. Let $d:\Delta_K\times\Delta_K\to[0,\infty)$ be continuous and satisfy $d(p,p)=0$. For a finite data-independent Borel partition $\mathcal P$ of $\Delta_K$, draw an iid sequence $(G_i,Y_i)$ from the joint law of $(G,Y)$, let $V_i=e_{Y_i}$ be the corresponding standard basis vector, and form the empirical estimator
$$\widehat E_{n,\mathcal P}=\sum_{B\in\mathcal P}\frac{N_B}{n}\,d(\bar V_B,\bar G_B),$$
where $N_B=\#\{i\le n:G_i\in B\}$ and, when $N_B>0$, $\bar V_B$ and $\bar G_B$ are the sample averages in bin $B$; when $N_B=0$, set both to any fixed $q_0\in\Delta_K$. Define
$$E_{\mathcal P}=\sum_{B\in\mathcal P:P(G\in B)>0}P(G\in B)\,d\!\left(\mathbb E[r(G)\mid G\in B],\mathbb E[G\mid G\in B]\right).$$
For each fixed $\mathcal P$, $\widehat E_{n,\mathcal P}\to E_{\mathcal P}$ almost surely. If $d$ is additionally jointly convex, then $E_{\mathcal P}\le\mathbb E[d(r(G),G)]$. Without requiring convexity, if $\mathcal P_m$ is a sequence of finite data-independent Borel partitions whose mesh $\max_{B\in\mathcal P_m}\operatorname{diam}_2(B)$ tends to zero, where $\operatorname{diam}_2(B)=\sup_{u,v\in B}\|u-v\|_2$, and $r$ is Lipschitz with respect to the Euclidean metric on $\Delta_K$, then $E_{\mathcal P_m}\to\mathbb E[d(r(G),G)]$. Thus the iterated limit $\lim_{m\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\widehat E_{n,\mathcal P_m}$ equals the expected calibration error almost surely; no diagonal rate is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite-label random forecast with calibration function $r$, a continuous discrepancy $d$ (additionally jointly convex for the underestimation conclusion), and the iid sample and finite partition specified above.

[F1] For every label $j$ and Borel bin $B$, $\mathbb E[\mathbf1_{\{G\in B\}}\mathbf1_{\{Y=j\}}]=\mathbb E[\mathbf1_{\{G\in B\}}r_j(G)]$. [[def-probability-calibration]]

[F2] A measurable simplex-pair random vector and continuous jointly convex function satisfy $\psi(\mathbb EU,\mathbb EV)\le\mathbb E\psi(U,V)$, and the mean pair lies in $\Delta_K\times\Delta_K$. [[lem-jensen-for-simplex-pairs]]

[F3] Measurable coordinate functions of independent observations remain independent; applying the same measurable map to identically distributed observations gives identically distributed outputs by the preimage identity. Averages of iid integrable real variables converge almost surely to their means. [[def-identically-distributed-and-iid-random-variables]] [[lem-measurable-functions-preserve-independence]] [[thm-kolmogorov-iid-l1-strong-law]]

[F4] The simplex and its product are compact subsets of finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces, and a continuous function on a compact metric space is uniformly continuous. [[thm-heine-borel-rn]] [[thm-heine-cantor-metric]]

[F5] The Euclidean distance $d_2(u,v)=\|u-v\|_2$ is a metric on $\mathbb R^K$; its formula gives $\|\lambda z\|_2=|\lambda|\|z\|_2$. [[lem-metrics-on-rn]]

## Proof

1.1 Fix a partition $\mathcal P$. For every bin $B$ and each coordinate, the variables $\mathbf1_{\{G_i\in B\}}$, $\mathbf1_{\{G_i\in B\}}(V_i)_j$, and $\mathbf1_{\{G_i\in B\}}(G_i)_j$ are bounded iid real variables: the same measurable coordinate map is applied to each iid pair, so the outputs have the same law by taking preimages, and their independence follows from [F3]. Their sample averages therefore converge almost surely to their expectations. On the event $P(G\in B)>0$, division by $N_B/n\to P(G\in B)$ gives convergence of the bin means to $\mathbb E[V\mid G\in B]$ and $\mathbb E[G\mid G\in B]$. On a zero-probability bin every sample has count zero almost surely. Since the partition and label set are finite, these convergences hold together almost surely; continuity of $d$ then gives $\widehat E_{n,\mathcal P}\to E_{\mathcal P}$. By [F1], $\mathbb E[V\mid G\in B]=\mathbb E[r(G)\mid G\in B]$ coordinatewise. [F1, F3, given, algebra]

1.2 Let $\delta_m$ be the maximum Euclidean diameter of a cell of $\mathcal P_m$. For any fixed $v\in\Delta_K$, the function $\psi(u,w)=\|u-w\|_2$ is continuous and jointly convex: the metric triangle inequality gives continuity in $(u,w)$, and
$$\|\lambda(u-w)+(1-\lambda)(u'-w')\|_2\le\lambda\|u-w\|_2+(1-\lambda)\|u'-w'\|_2$$
for $0\le\lambda\le1$ by the triangle inequality and homogeneity in [F5]. For a positive-probability bin $B$, define $P_B(A)=P(A\cap\{G\in B\})/P(G\in B)$. If $G(\omega)\in B$, apply [F2] under $P_B$ to the simplex pair $(G,G(\omega))$. It gives $\|\mathbb E[G\mid G\in B]-G(\omega)\|_2\le\mathbb E[\|G-G(\omega)\|_2\mid G\in B]\le\delta_m$. Apply the same argument to $(r(G),r(G(\omega)))$ to obtain $\|\mathbb E[r(G)\mid G\in B]-r(G(\omega))\|_2\le L\delta_m$. Thus the Euclidean distance between the ordered pairs is at most $\sqrt{1+L^2}\,\delta_m\le(1+L)\delta_m$. By [F4], $d$ is uniformly continuous on the compact product simplex, so the absolute difference between the bin value and $d(r(G(\omega)),G(\omega))$ is bounded by a modulus tending to zero with $\delta_m$, uniformly over all bins and $\omega$. Integrating over bins yields $E_{\mathcal P_m}\to\mathbb E[d(r(G),G)]$. [F2, F4, F5, given, algebra]

2.1 For the underestimation conclusion assume additionally that $d$ is jointly convex. For each positive-probability bin define the conditional probability measure $P_B(A)=P(A\cap\{G\in B\})/P(G\in B)$. Under $P_B$, the pair $(r(G),G)$ takes values in $\Delta_K\times\Delta_K$. Apply [F2] to $\psi=d$ and multiply by $P(G\in B)$ to obtain $P(G\in B)d\!\left(\mathbb E[r(G)\mid G\in B],\mathbb E[G\mid G\in B]\right)\le\mathbb E[\mathbf1_{\{G\in B\}}d(r(G),G)].$ Summing over the partition proves $E_{\mathcal P}\le\mathbb E[d(r(G),G)]$. [F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For each fixed $m$, step 1.1 gives the almost-sure limit in $n$. There are countably many $m$, so the intersection of these probability-one events still has probability one; step 1.2 then gives the iterated limit as stated. This order takes the sample-size limit before the partition refinement and makes no claim about a jointly chosen diagonal sequence. [step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
