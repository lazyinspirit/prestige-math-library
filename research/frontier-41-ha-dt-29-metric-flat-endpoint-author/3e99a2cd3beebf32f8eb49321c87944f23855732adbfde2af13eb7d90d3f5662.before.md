## Statement

Let $A_s$ and $A_u$ be self-adjoint matrices whose eigenvalues are respectively negative and positive. Suppose the smooth vector field near the origin in $\mathbb R^{l}\times\mathbb R^{k}$ is
$$s'=A_ss+R_s(s,u),\qquad u'=A_uu+R_u(s,u),$$
where $R(0)=DR(0)=0$ and the coordinate axes are invariant:
$$R_s(0,u)=0,\qquad R_u(s,0)=0.$$
There are $r>0$, $\beta>0$ and $C>0$, independent of $T>0$, such that for every $|a|,|b|<r/2$ there is a unique solution on $[0,T]$ staying in $|s|,|u|\le r$ with mixed boundary values $s(0)=a$, $u(T)=b$. It depends smoothly on $(T,a,b)$ for $T>0$. The endpoint maps
$$\alpha_T(a,b)=u(0),\qquad \zeta_T(a,b)=s(T)$$
satisfy
$$|\alpha_T(a,b)|+|\zeta_T(a,b)|\le (|a|+|b|)e^{-\beta T},\qquad \|D_{(a,b)}\alpha_T\|+\|D_{(a,b)}\zeta_T\|\le Ce^{-\beta T}.$$
Here the domain norm is $|\delta a|+|\delta b|$. Thus both endpoint maps converge to zero in $C^1$ uniformly over this boundary-data ball. This is a passage estimate for invariant-axis coordinates; it does not assert a collar chart for a moduli-space compactification.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The smooth field and invariant-axis conditions in the statement.

[F1] The spectral theorem gives $\lambda>0$ such that $\|e^{tA_s}\|,\|e^{-tA_u}\|\le e^{-\lambda t}$ for $t\ge0$ ([[cor-real-spectral-theorem-for-self-adjoint-endomorphisms]]).

[F2] A contraction on a complete metric space has a unique fixed point ([[thm-banach-fixed-point]]). A smooth fixed-point equation with invertible derivative has smooth parameter dependence ([[thm-implicit-function-theorem-for-banach-spaces]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct, by a mixed boundary integral equation and weighted estimates.

1.1 Work in the product norm $\max(|s|,|u|)$. Shrink a coordinate ball so that each of the four derivative blocks of $R$ has norm at most $\varepsilon$, with $\varepsilon<\lambda/8$. Multiply $R$ by a smooth cutoff supported in a slightly larger ball and equal to one on $|s|,|u|\le r$. Since $R(0)=DR(0)=0$, this extension can still have all four derivative blocks at most $\varepsilon$ after shrinking the ball further. It preserves the invariant axes. Its second derivative is bounded by a constant $K$ independent of sufficiently small $r$: Taylor's estimates $|R(x)|=O(|x|^2)$ and $|DR(x)|=O(|x|)$ bound the cutoff derivative terms as well. In particular $\|D_uR_s(s,u)\|\le K|s|$ and $\|D_sR_u(s,u)\|\le K|u|$, since these blocks vanish on the corresponding invariant axes. Choose $r$ further so that $Kr/\lambda<1/8$. [given, construct, algebra]

1.2 On continuous paths over $[0,T]$, define
$$\mathcal T_s(s,u)(t)=e^{tA_s}a+\int_0^t e^{(t-v)A_s}R_s(s(v),u(v))\,dv,$$
$$\mathcal T_u(s,u)(t)=e^{(t-T)A_u}b-\int_t^T e^{(t-v)A_u}R_u(s(v),u(v))\,dv.$$
The supremum product norm gives Lipschitz constant at most $2\varepsilon/\lambda<1/4$, uniformly in $T$. The closed path ball of radius $r$ maps into itself for $|a|,|b|<r/2$, since each integral has norm at most $2\varepsilon r/\lambda<r/4$. By [F2] it has a unique fixed point in that ball. Differentiating its integral equations gives the required solution and boundary values. Every solution staying in the ball satisfies the same variation-of-constants equations, so uniqueness holds in the asserted class. The cutoff equals the original field there. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.1 Invariance and the diagonal derivative bound give $|R_s(s,u)|\le\varepsilon|s|$ and $|R_u(s,u)|\le\varepsilon|u|$. The integral equations then give $|s(t)|\le |a|e^{-(\lambda-\varepsilon)t}$ and $|u(t)|\le |b|e^{-(\lambda-\varepsilon)(T-t)}$. For example, multiplying the first integral inequality by $e^{\lambda t}$ and iterating its scalar integral term gives the convergent series $|a|\sum_{j\ge0}(\varepsilon t)^j/j!$; the second inequality uses reversed time. Consequently the endpoint value bound holds with $\beta=\lambda/2$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 For fixed $T$, the derivative of the integral map has norm below $1/4$, so the Neumann series for $I-D\mathcal T$ and [F2] give smooth dependence on $(a,b)$. Dependence jointly on $T>0$ follows on the fixed path space $C^0([0,1])$ after rescaling $t=T\theta$: the integral kernels and limits define a smooth map in $T,a,b$ and the same invertibility holds at each fixed positive $T$. Let $(\sigma,\upsilon)$ be the derivative solution for boundary variation $(\delta a,\delta b)$, and put $P=\sup e^{\beta t}|\sigma(t)|$, $Q=\sup e^{\beta(T-t)}|\upsilon(t)|$. The diagonal terms in its differentiated integral equations contribute at most $\varepsilon P/(\lambda-\beta)$ and $\varepsilon Q/(\lambda-\beta)$. For a cross term, step 2.1 and step 1.1 bound $|D_uR_s\,\upsilon|$ by $K|a|Qe^{-\beta T}e^{-(\lambda-\varepsilon-\beta)t}$. Integration with the stable kernel and multiplication by $e^{\beta t}$ contribute at most $K|a|Q/\lambda$. The other cross term contributes at most $K|b|P/\lambda$ by reversing time. Thus $P\le|\delta a|+\varepsilon P/(\lambda-\beta)+K|a|Q/\lambda$ and $Q\le|\delta b|+\varepsilon Q/(\lambda-\beta)+K|b|P/\lambda$. Our choices make the sum of the coefficient bounds less than $1/2$, whence $P+Q\le2(|\delta a|+|\delta b|)$. At the endpoints this gives $|\upsilon(0)|+|\sigma(T)|\le2e^{-\beta T}(|\delta a|+|\delta b|)$, proving the sum of the two operator-norm bounds with $C=4$ and the uniform $C^1$ convergence. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
