## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $n\ge1$, permutations $x,y\in S_n$, their RSK pairs $(P(x),Q(x))$ and $(P(y),Q(y))$, and the left, right, and two-sided Kazhdan–Lusztig cell relations.

[F1] The RSK map is a bijection between permutations and pairs of standard tableaux of the same shape ([[thm-robinson-schensted-correspondence]]).

[F2] Equality of recording tableaux implies left-cell equivalence, and equality of insertion tableaux implies right-cell equivalence ([[prop-same-insertion-or-recording-tableaux-imply-cell-equivalence]]).

[F3] Left-cell equivalence implies equality of recording tableaux ([[lem-left-cell-equivalence-forces-equality-of-recording-tableaux-in-type-a]]).

[F4] Inversion interchanges left and right cell relations, while RSK interchanges insertion and recording tableaux: $a\sim_L b\iff a^{-1}\sim_Rb^{-1}$ and $(P(w^{-1}),Q(w^{-1}))=(Q(w),P(w))$ ([[def-left-right-and-two-sided-kazhdan-lusztig-preorders-and-cells]], [[cor-rsk-symmetry-under-inversion]]).

[F5] If $a\le_L b$, then $Q(b)$ dominates $Q(a)$ in tableau-dominance order. He–Hu–Sun's Theorem 1.3 proves this for the standard Kazhdan–Lusztig left preorder; their Definition 3.1 records the coefficient definition and identifies it with the original preorder used by [[def-left-right-and-two-sided-kazhdan-lusztig-preorders-and-cells]]. Their tableau order compares the shapes of all initial-label subtableaux, so at $k=n$ the shape of the target dominates the shape of the source. The right-preorder assertion follows from the left one by inversion, using [F4].

[F6] The two-sided preorder is generated by left- and right-preorder steps, and two-sided cell equivalence means mutual two-sided comparability ([[def-left-right-and-two-sided-kazhdan-lusztig-preorders-and-cells]]).

## Statement

For $x,y\in S_n$, with the RSK tableaux $P,Q$ of [[thm-robinson-schensted-correspondence]] (insertion tableau and recording tableau of the one-line word): $$x\sim_Ly\iff Q(x)=Q(y),\qquad x\sim_Ry\iff P(x)=P(y),\qquad x\sim_{LR}y\iff \operatorname{sh}(Q(x))=\operatorname{sh}(Q(y)).$$ Here $\sim_L,\sim_R,\sim_{LR}$ are the cell equivalence relations of [[def-left-right-and-two-sided-kazhdan-lusztig-preorders-and-cells]]; the chosen convention is displayed: **left cells are the fibers of the recording tableau**, **right cells are the fibers of the insertion tableau**, and **two-sided cells are the fibers of the shape map** (note $\operatorname{sh}(P(w))=\operatorname{sh}(Q(w))$). Consequently the left cells of $S_n$ are in bijection with the standard tableaux of size $n$, the right cells likewise, and the two-sided cells with the partitions of $n$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** use the one-sided classifications, RSK inversion symmetry, and tableau-dominance monotonicity along the two-sided preorder.

1.1 **Left cells are exactly the $Q$-fibers.** If $x\sim_Ly$, [F3] gives $Q(x)=Q(y)$. Conversely, if $Q(x)=Q(y)$, [F2] gives $x\sim_Ly$. [F2, F3]

1.2 **Two-sided equivalence forces equal shape.** By [F6], each step in an $LR$ chain is a left or right preorder step. Fact [F5] shows that along either kind of step the RSK shape weakly increases in partition dominance: use $Q$ for a left step and $P$ for a right step, whose shapes agree by [F1]. Thus $x\le_{LR}y$ implies $\operatorname{sh}(Q(y))$ dominates $\operatorname{sh}(Q(x))$. If $x\sim_{LR}y$, [F6] gives the reverse preorder, so the two shapes dominate each other. Antisymmetry of partition dominance gives equality. This uses monotonicity along preorder steps, not constancy of $P$ or $Q$ along an individual step. [F1, F4, F5, F6]

2.1 **Right cells are exactly the $P$-fibers.** If $x\sim_Ry$, [F4] gives $x^{-1}\sim_Ly^{-1}$, so step 1.1 gives $Q(x^{-1})=Q(y^{-1})$ and [F4] gives $P(x)=P(y)$. Conversely, if $P(x)=P(y)$, [F2] gives $x\sim_Ry$. [F2, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 **Equal shape implies two-sided equivalence.** Suppose $\operatorname{sh}(Q(x))=\operatorname{sh}(Q(y))$. By [F1], there is a unique permutation $z$ whose RSK pair is $(P(x),Q(y))$. Then $P(z)=P(x)$ gives $x\sim_Rz$ by step 2.1, and $Q(z)=Q(y)$ gives $z\sim_Ly$ by step 1.1. By [F6], these equivalences give chains in both directions using left and right preorder steps, so $x\sim_{LR}y$. [F1, F6, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 **Count the cells.** For each standard tableau $T$ of size $n$, fill the boxes of its shape from left to right across each row, starting with the top row, by consecutive integers to obtain a canonical standard tableau $P_{\operatorname{sh}(T)}$. By [F1], the pair $(P_{\operatorname{sh}(T)},T)$ comes from a permutation, so every $Q$-fiber is nonempty; step 1.1 identifies distinct such fibers with distinct left cells. The same argument with the pair $(T,P_{\operatorname{sh}(T)})$ and step 2.1 counts right cells. For every partition $\lambda\vdash n$, [F1] gives a permutation with RSK pair $(P_\lambda,P_\lambda)$, so every shape fiber is nonempty; steps 3.1 and 1.2 identify exactly one two-sided cell for each shape. Hence the stated bijections hold. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 1.2] ∎

## Remarks

The scaffold's proposed justification that an elementary two-sided preorder step fixes $P$ or $Q$ is false: the coefficient of $C_{s_1}$ in $C_{s_1}C_e$ is $1$, so the relation $s_1\leftarrow_L e$ in $S_3$ changes shape from $(3)$ to $(2,1)$. This follows from the unit property and the basis multiplication formula [[thm-kazhdan-lusztig-basis-multiplication-formula]]. Step 4.1 instead uses the published monotonicity theorem for the one-sided preorders. He, Hu, and Sun's Theorem 1.3 is proved using the seminormal/Murphy basis comparison; with their parameter $t=v^{-1}$, their Hecke quadratic relation and KL basis agree with this page's normalization.

The one-sided cell arguments use the locally proved star-cell transport, Knuth-fiber and inverse-cell suppliers; coefficientwise positivity is not required. The separate shape-monotonicity premise [F5] in step 1.2 invokes He–Hu–Sun Theorem 1.3 without a proved local supplier. That exact prerequisite remains open; the positivity citation authorization does not cover it. No Choice is used.
