For n≥3 and any good weighting, a minimum weight m exists in the nonempty finite vertex set. Perfection of the deletion of that vertex gives A≤1 for the remaining sum; nonnegativity and minimality give A≥(n−1)m and each remaining weight ≤A−(n−2)m. Multiplying by each nonnegative weight and summing yields sum of squares ≤A²−m((n−2)A−m)≤A²≤1. The last sign follows from (n−2)(n−1)≥1, and includes m=0. This is choice-free and does not use the pending general bull-free theorem.

Each C5 vertex deletion is P4. Its induced subgraphs are unions of paths: edge-containing graphs have χ=ω=2, nonempty edgeless graphs have χ=ω=1, and the empty graph has χ=ω=0. Thus the new lemma applies. Constant weight 1/4 is good on C5 because all perfect induced subgraphs are proper and have at most four vertices, while C5 itself is nonperfect. Total weight is 5/4. The two-narrow/not-one-narrow witness then directly refutes the universal implication.
