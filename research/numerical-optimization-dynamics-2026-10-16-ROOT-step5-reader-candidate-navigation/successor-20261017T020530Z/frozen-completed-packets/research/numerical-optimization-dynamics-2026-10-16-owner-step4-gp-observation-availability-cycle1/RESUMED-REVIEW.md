The genuine concern is the first concern in `research/numerical-optimization-dynamics-2026-10-16-batch-69-step5-context.json`, also archived under `before/research/`. The concern is confirmed. With one candidate, independent f and ξ both N(0,1), and an oracle returning Y=f+ξ iff f>0, success reveals the sign of f. The ordinary kernel is N(y/2,1/2), but the actual successful-record density is

p(f | Y=y,A=1) = 1_{f>0} φ_{1/2}(f-y/2) / Φ(y/√2).

The denominator is the positive-tail probability of N(y/2,1/2). At y=0 its mean is 1/√π and variance 1/2−1/π, and its support is positive, so it cannot equal the computed ordinary Gaussian. The y=0 comparison uses the natural continuous conditional-density version; failure of equality also holds for every y, hence on a set of successful histories of full conditional probability. On failure, the actual law is the negative half-normal, while the algorithm retains its prior. This is a genuine model-information defect, not a numerical solve defect.

The primary proposed repair is q_t(H_t), conditionally independent of the **joint** latent candidate vector and **all** unused noises given the observed past. The assumption allows adaptive selection, repeated sites, history-dependent success probabilities and always available observations. It preserves the true-posterior interpretation on successful histories and after an unavailable flag. It does not require availability independent of the past. Fixed initial observations cannot themselves have been chosen by a hidden informative inclusion event.

The theorem proof strengthens induction to retain the product law of unused Gaussian errors, conditions on the selected site only after conditioning on history, cancels both success and failure flag factors by conditional expectation, applies the scalar conditional-Gaussian update, and gives the explicit block-inverse identity recovering the selected-list batch formulas. It does not assume an adaptively selected observation vector is jointly Gaussian without conditioning. It handles singular latent/predictive covariance, positive noise at repeated sites, and zero-noise SPD observed blocks. Conditional laws are versions and statements are almost sure for realized histories. A failed solve after an appended observation retains a state valid only for its previous history, rather than claiming that an old state conditions on the newly returned value.

A weaker optional extension, not installed in the primary source proposal, permits q(H,Y) with availability conditionally independent of latent quantities given H,Y. On success the factor q(H,y) cancels after Y=y is observed, whenever that success has positive probability. On failure the factor is instead E[1−q(H,f(x)+ξ) | H,f], generally dependent on f. For example, q(H,Y)=1_{Y>0}, with f and ξ independent N(0,1), gives failure density 2φ(f)Φ(−f); the pre-call prior is not its posterior. Therefore this extension supplies only successful-record guarantees, and could not silently replace the primary failed-flag guarantee. The primary q(H) proposal avoids this loss.
