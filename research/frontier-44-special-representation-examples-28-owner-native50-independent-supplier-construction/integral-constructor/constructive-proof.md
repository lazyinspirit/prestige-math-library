## 1. Opposite dominance annihilation, with no cancellation

**Lemma.** For partitions λ,ν, if λ is not dominated by ν', then xλ H yν=0 over every R.

Decompose permutations into minimal `(Sλ,Sν)` double-coset representatives d. The standard length-additive double-coset decomposition reduces xλH yν to the products xλT_d yν: left Sλ factors act on xλ by powers of q and right Sν factors act on yν by powers of −1. To justify the needed double-coset facts directly, sort the images of each right block and the inverse images of each left block; the resulting unique minimal representative is increasing within each block in both directions. Its block-incidence matrix a_ij counts the positions of right block j sent to left block i. Each nonempty intersection block is contiguous within the two corresponding blocks: the simultaneous increasing orders place the entries grouped by the other block in consecutive order. Consequently Sλ∩dSνd^{-1} is a standard parabolic subgroup inside Sλ, generated by adjacent transpositions of these contiguous intersections; its conjugate inside Sν is likewise standard. This also gives the usual length-additive transversal fact by counting inversions inside and between the blocks.

The incidence matrix has row sums λ_i and column sums ν_j. If all a_ij≤1, then for every k, Σ_{i≤k}λ_i≤Σ_j min(k,ν_j)=Σ_{i≤k}ν'_i. Thus the hypothesis forces an intersection block of size at least2. Choose its adjacent generators s in Sλ and t in Sν with sd=dt; minimality gives ell(sd)=ell(d)+1=ell(dt), hence T_sT_d=T_dT_t. Factor xλ=X(1+T_s) by minimal right representatives of <s> in Sλ and yν=(1-q^{-1}T_t)Y by minimal left representatives of <t> in Sν. These factorizations follow by pairing the length-additive standard-basis terms in the defining sums, including their (-q)^{-ell} weights. Therefore

xλT_dyν=X T_d(1+T_t)(1-q^{-1}T_t)Y=0,

because `(1+T_t)(1-q^{-1}T_t)=0` follows exactly from the quadratic relation. No division or cancellation of q+1 occurs. This proves the lemma.

## 3. Explicit unit triangular minor: independence and split inclusion

Order row-standard λ-tableaux by dominance of every initial-label subtableau. Write r≤s if each initial-label row composition of r is dominated by that of s. Claim:

`e_t = q^{ell(dμ(t))} b_{t'} + Σ_{r<t'} a_{r,t} b_r`.  (†)

First take t=t^μ. Here t'=t_λ. The permutation wλ is minimal on the right for Sμ: the inverse images of consecutive labels in any μ-row occur in increasing row-reading λ order, because these are the nodes down a single λ-column. Thus ell(wλu)=ell(wλ)+ell(u) for every u∈Sμ. Expanding z gives row tabloids of t_λu with coefficients from (-q)^{-ell(u)} and row sorting. For each λ-column, t_λ contains its labels in increasing order down that column. Among all permutations of those labels, its first k nodes contain the maximum possible number of labels≤m, for every k,m. Summing over the columns proves that each row tabloid in the expansion is dominated by t_λ. The same tabloid as t_λ occurs only for u=1: each λ-row intersects each column in at most one node, so preserving all row sets forces every column entry fixed. Its coefficient is1. This proves (†) initially.

Now take a standard ascending μ step t→t s_i. The two nodes are incomparable; i lies in an upper μ-row and to the right of i+1. Transposing gives s=t' with i in a lower λ-row a and i+1 in an upper λ-row b<a. The tableau s'=s s_i is standard and more dominant, and ell(dλ(s'))=ell(dλ(s))-1 (swapping the adjacent labels removes one inverse inversion). The row-module action therefore gives b_sT_i=q b_{s'}+(q-1)b_s.

For every r≤s, both the row tabloid r and its swapped row tabloid r s_i are ≤s'. Only the prefix m=i needs checking; other prefixes are unchanged by the swap. For a row prefix k<a use `count_{≤i}(r s_i;first k rows) ≤ count_{≤i+1}(r;first k rows) ≤ count_{≤i+1}(s;first k rows)=count_{≤i}(s';first k rows)`. For k≥a use `count_{≤i}(r s_i;first k rows) ≤ count_{≤i-1}(r;first k rows)+1 ≤ count_{≤i-1}(s;first k rows)+1=count_{≤i}(s';first k rows)`. The same bounds hold for the unswapped r, or follow from r≤s≤s'. Row sorting changes none of these counts. Thus no term escapes below s'. Moreover only r=s can produce the tabloid s' under this multiplication: a swapped source must be s's_i=s, and an unswapped source s' was absent because s'>s. Hence the coefficient at b_{s'} is q times the previous coefficient, proving (†) by induction along the standard chain.

Distinct standard μ-tableaux have distinct transposes. Select in the row-module matrix exactly the rows indexed by these standard λ-tableaux. Formula (†) gives a triangular square minor with diagonal q^{ell(dμ(t))}. Its determinant is a unit. Therefore {e_t} is independent and its span is a split direct summand of M over A and over every R: project to the selected coordinates, invert this triangular matrix, and subtract the resulting combination of e_t. To finish spanning without a generic dimension argument, let U=C_M(μ)^θ and let F:U→P be the surjection from Section2. The coordinate construction gives a retraction π:M→E. Since E⊂P, πF:U→E is surjective. Both U and E have explicit free bases of the same finite size f^μ. Choose preimages of each of the finitely many basis vectors of E; their coordinate columns form a right inverse of the square matrix of πF. Taking determinants shows det(πF) is a unit, so πF is an isomorphism over every commutative R. Therefore, if p∈P and π(p)=0, choose u with F(u)=p; injectivity of πF gives u=0 and p=0. For arbitrary p∈P, subtract π(p)∈E⊂P; this difference is in that kernel and is zero. Hence P=E and F is an isomorphism. This proves the exact standard basis, split inclusion, rank f^λ, and compatibility of P with every base change. It uses a unit matrix inverse over the ground ring, not generic semisimplicity or a field dimension count.
