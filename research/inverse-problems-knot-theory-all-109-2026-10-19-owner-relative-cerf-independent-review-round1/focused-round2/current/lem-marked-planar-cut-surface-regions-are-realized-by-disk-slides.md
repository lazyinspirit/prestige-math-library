## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $U$ be either handlebody of a balanced multipointed knot diagram in the sense of the balanced pointed-diagram definition, with its specified compression disks $D_1,\ldots,D_m$, marked endpoints, and disjoint simultaneously boundary-parallel knot arcs. Cutting $U$ along all these disks gives $k$ balls. Each disk gives two distinguished disk faces on the boundaries of these balls.

Suppose that one cut-ball boundary contains a compact planar subsurface $R$, supplied with a disk-with-holes parametrization, whose boundary consists of an outer circle $c$ and $r+1$ inner circles bounding disk faces $E_0,\ldots,E_r$. These faces come from distinct disks $D_{i_0},\ldots,D_{i_r}$. The interior of $R$ misses every other disk face, its closure misses all marked points, and the faces have disjoint interiors from $R$. Then $D_{i_0}$ can be replaced by a disk with boundary isotopic to $c$ by exactly $r$ disk slides over $D_{i_1},\ldots,D_{i_r}$ in some order, followed by a curve isotopy. The slide bands and the final isotopy miss all marks and all other attaching curves of this system. The other disks remain unchanged, and at each completed slide the disks cut $U$ into $k$ balls, each containing the same one trivial arc under an identification fixing the actual arcs. Thus every resulting full diagram is balanced and represents the same oriented knot. The assertion concerns completed slides; it does not require the sliding curves to be disjoint during a band-sum operation.

For $r=0$ only an isotopy is needed. Opposite-system curves may cross these bands; a small perturbation makes the two systems transverse again without crossing marks. No relative generic-family existence assertion is part of this lemma.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The specified disks, actual arcs, faces, and parametrized marked-point-free region $R$.

[F1] [[def-admissible-pointed-heegaard-floer-data]] specifies disjoint compression disks, one trivial arc in each cut ball, the simultaneous boundary-parallelism disks, and the cyclic marking.

[F2] A smooth planar Jordan disk is a standard smooth disk, with prescribed boundary-collar extension, under Countable Choice ([[lem-smooth-jordan-disk-boundary-maps-extend-to-diffeomorphisms]]).

[F3] Smooth collars and supported extension of compact embedding isotopies are available under Countable Choice ([[thm-collar-neighborhood-theorem]], [[thm-isotopy-extension]]).

[F4] The critical values of a smooth real function on a smooth curve form a null set under Countable Choice ([[thm-morse-sard-for-smooth-manifolds]]).

[F5] Neat proper disk isotopies, with interior in the manifold interior and boundary in its boundary, extend to boundary-preserving ambient isotopies with prescribed support ([[lem-supported-neat-proper-isotopies-extend-boundary-preservingly]]). Before applying the smooth-boundary extension theorem to a cut manifold, round its finitely many disk-face corners in small product collars, away from marks and actual arcs, transporting the band and face coordinates through this rounding. The local rounding is a monotone smoothing of the quadrant corner in its two normal coordinates and is the identity outside those collars. All proper compression-disk isotopies below are chosen product-neat in the resulting boundary collars. For a bridge-boundary path change, first extend its isotopy on the boundary surface and then through a thin product collar; it is stationary near its marked endpoints, so that collar support can miss the actual knot arcs.

[F6] Broad original compression witnesses are first compared with neat disks by a boundary-pointwise-fixing homeomorphism, smooth away from the old rim curves. Compact objects off those rims have a genuine smooth comparison. Completed parallel band sums may miss the ENTIRE old family, so their smooth pullbacks restore retained ORIGINAL disks exactly. Bridge witnesses are first cleaned of all disk intersections, including for extended empty-ball systems. Neat proper-disk isotopies below occur ONLY in that auxiliary comparison. ([[lem-broad-compression-comparisons-preserve-retained-disks-and-smooth-local-moves]], [[lem-simultaneous-bridge-witnesses-can-avoid-a-compression-disk-system]])

## Proof

1.1 First apply [F6] to the actual broad disk family and bridge witnesses. Keep all attaching curves, actual arcs and the surface fixed; only auxiliary witnesses are prepared. Work in its neat cut balls with rounded face collars. Transport the supplied region and its labelled faces through the boundary-fixed cut correspondence; their surface portion and bands are unchanged. Cap the inner boundaries of $R$ by its supplied faces. The result $Q=R\cup E_0\cup\cdots\cup E_r$ is a parametrized disk in the cut-ball boundary and contains no marked point. Construct disjoint embedded joining arcs between the faces, with interiors in $R$, forming a tree on those $r+1$ faces. Here is a finite construction in the supplied planar parametrization. A disk with finitely many disjoint disk holes is path connected: a polygonal path between interior points can be modified at each hole it meets by a short path on that hole's exterior collar; a finite subcover of its compact image gives finitely many such modifications. Choose a path joining two holes, erase loops in a sufficiently fine finite polygonal approximation, and smooth the finitely many corners in disjoint chart disks. Thicken this simple joining arc to a rectangle. Joining the two holes by this rectangle replaces them by one disk hole: two disk parametrizations joined by a rectangle form a disk, as is seen by straightening the two attaching intervals in collar coordinates; equivalently its planar Jordan boundary bounds that union by [F2]. Repeating with the remaining holes constructs the required tree. The arcs can have distinct endpoints on every face boundary, and all rectangles can be made disjoint except in their prescribed face collars. Only finitely many choices occur. [F6, F2, F3, given, construct]

2.1 Root the tree at $E_0$. Take a small regular neighborhood $N$ in $Q$ of all faces and joining rectangles. It is a disk: add its leaf faces and their rectangles one at a time, each time joining a disk to a disk along a single boundary interval. Its boundary is isotopic to $c$ in $R$. Indeed the remaining region in the supplied planar disk coordinates is the region between two nested Jordan disks. Choose a simple smooth arc between its two boundary components as in step1.1. Slit along this arc and round its four endpoint corners. The slit region is connected: a path meeting the slit can be rerouted along either side to an endpoint and around the corresponding boundary collar. Its boundary is one Jordan circle, so [F2] makes the slit region a disk. Parametrize its boundary by the four successive sides of a rectangle, its two opposite sides being the two copies of the slit; [F2] extends this parametrization with prescribed collars. Regluing these opposite sides gives a smooth annulus. Its circle levels supply the stated isotopy. All of this lies in $R$ and its arbitrarily thin collars, so misses every mark and other face. [F5, F2, F3, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Process the edges in any order for which the already processed vertices form a connected subtree containing the root. Initially the moving curve is $\partial E_0$. If the next edge joins this subtree to $E_j$, extend its rectangle a short distance along the boundary of the already thickened subtree. Its interior misses the other faces, the previously constructed boundary and every unused rectangle after a small collar displacement. The band sum of the moving boundary with $\partial E_j$ is exactly the boundary of the enlarged subtree neighborhood. In the original surface the two ends are on the current curve and $\partial D_{i_j}$, since the chosen faces belong to distinct disks. The rectangle descends to an embedded band disjoint from all the other curves of this system and all marks. Hence it is precisely an allowed handleslide. Use parallel copies on the chosen cut side of BOTH source disks, joined by a thin inward push of this band. As in [F6], the new disk is the inward frontier of the cap over the two faces and band. Choose its exterior rim disjoint from EVERY current old rim and its interior disjoint from the ENTIRE current old prepared family, including the old moving disk. This is the same completed boundary band sum, with an arbitrarily small curve displacement. Its smooth pullback under [F6] is therefore a genuine new disk disjoint from all retained ORIGINAL disks; the latter are kept exactly. For the following cap calculation work in this auxiliary neat comparison, and repeat this preparation at each completed slide. After $r$ repetitions its boundary is $\partial N$, and step2.1 gives the required final curve isotopy. [F6, F5, F1, F3, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 We check the ball and arc assertion for one slide in the auxiliary NEAT comparison, rather than infer it from a homological disk count. No boundary-preserving proper-disk isotopy of an original tangential disk is asserted. Cut $U$ along all retained disks, leaving the moving disk uncut. In this cut manifold the copied slide disk is parallel to the boundary face of the retained disk. The new moving disk is the old one with a boundary finger running along the band and around this face. The finger retracts across that boundary face by an isotopy of proper disks: first shorten its two parallel sides in the band rectangle, then flatten its parallel disk cap in the product collar of the face. In coordinates these are the translations of a rounded rectangle and a parallel disk in their product collar, constant off a slightly larger collar. The face, band and their collars miss the actual arcs, because the compression disks and bands do, and compactness gives a common positive separation from the arcs and their endpoints. The isotopy consequently misses the arcs. Choose this proper-disk isotopy product-neat along its moving boundary collar; by [F6, F5] it extends to the cut manifold fixing every actual arc. Cutting it along the moving disk before or after this isotopy gives diffeomorphic cut manifolds, fixing those arcs. The original cut manifold was $k$ balls with one trivial arc each, so the new cut manifold has exactly that property. Carry the original boundary-parallelism disk in each ball by this diffeomorphism. Its boundary path can be made to avoid the distinguished disk faces: make it transverse to their finitely many rims and, in each face, replace its finitely many entering subarcs by disjoint parallel paths in the exterior rim collar, using innermost subarcs first. Each replacement is a boundary isotopy across an empty disk, extended to the bridge disk by [F3]. There is only one knot arc in each ball, so no other bridge disk in that ball obstructs this operation. Thus these bridge disks are again simultaneous and have their boundary paths in the attaching-complement surface. Choose these witnesses outside the empty cap before completing the operation, using the boundary-subarc and inward-collar construction in [F6]. Then they miss all OLD faces, and their bottom intervals miss all old rims. Their compact smooth pullbacks under [F6] give simultaneous witnesses for the actual new system. Its cut components correspond by the homeomorphism to these neat cut balls, with each actual arc fixed. The other actual compression disks are restored literally; the smooth replacement is used only near the completed new disk and these witnesses, away from the old rim curves. [F6, F5, F1, F3, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 Iterate step4.1 and apply the final SURFACE-curve isotopy of step2.1. In each auxiliary comparison extend it inward by [F6, F3], keeping its completed curve and new witness off the retained old rims; use [F6] to pull the completed data back smoothly and restore the retained original disks. The auxiliary preparation added no diagram move, because every old attaching curve and the actual knot were fixed throughout. Every endpoint is fixed. Thus the cyclic endpoint pairing, the actual oriented knot, and simultaneous boundary-parallelism are preserved, and the number of disks and balls is unchanged. Each complementary surface component still has exactly the endpoints of its one cut-ball arc; the endpoint pairing therefore remains the required one. Opposite-system curves were never required to avoid a slide band. If a completed curve is not transverse to that finite system, choose an annular collar of it missing all other curves of its own system and the marks. By [F4], the normal coordinate restricted to the finitely many opposite curves inside that annulus has arbitrarily small common regular values. Moving the completed curve to one such circle level gives transverse intersections with that system. The perturbation preserves the compression and arc properties by its supported isotopy. Countable Choice is used only by [F2]–[F4]; all constructions above are finite. This proves the assertion, including the empty tree when $r=0$. [F6, F5, F1, F2, F3, F4, step 2.1, step 4.1, construct] ∎
