## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $M$ be a closed compact smooth $n$-manifold. Supply a smooth function path $f_t$, stationary near its parameter endpoints, with the finite simple fold and separated critical-value event list of the compact relative function-event lemma among its unprotected critical points. It may be fixed on a prescribed open neighborhood $N$; assume the only critical points in $\overline N$ are finitely many fixed nondegenerate points of indices $0$ or $n$, with fixed local Morse models, and every other critical branch and fold lies outside $\overline N$. The fixed index-zero points have one common value $c_-$ and the fixed index-$n$ points one common value $c_+>c_-$; every unprotected critical value stays strictly between them. Either fixed set may be empty, in which case its bound is simply supplied. Thus equal values among the protected minima or maxima are permitted, and are not counted as crossing events. Supply a smooth strictly descending field on a neighborhood of $\overline N$, descending for every $f_t$ there, fixed with the parameter and standard at these fixed critical points. Prescribe stationary descending endpoint fields, standard at their critical points, agreeing with this field on $N$, and with no trajectory of nonpositive index drop.

There is a smooth strictly descending family $X_t$ with those endpoint and $N$ values, hyperbolic at each regular critical point, with the following properties. No trajectory increases index. There are only finitely many same-index connecting trajectories; each occurs at a distinct parameter, away from every fold and critical-value crossing, and is transverse in the parameter: its source-sphere directions of dimension $i-1$ together with the parameter direction map isomorphically onto the $i$ normal directions of the destination stable section. A whole neighborhood of each birth parameter has no same-index connecting trajectory. The newborn pair has its single local geometrically cancelling trajectory. Near folds the family is the product descending field for a smooth split scalar fold and a fixed nondegenerate quadratic form.

This is a weak descending-field assertion: it does not require slice-wise Morse–Smale transversality at positive index drops, nor exact eigenvalue normalization of newborn Morse points uniformly through their fold. It does not yet assert a based attaching-disk movie or the balanced knot-diagram move-generation theorem. The protected region may be a fixed knot neighborhood carrying its marked minimum/maximum models, provided the stated hypotheses hold.

Finite-dimensional parameter transversality applies to explicit transverse evaluation families under Countable Choice ([[thm-parametric-transversality]]).

[F3] Smooth partitions, compact cutoffs and an auxiliary smooth Riemannian metric exist under Countable Choice ([[thm-smooth-partitions-of-unity-exist-on-manifolds-with-boundary]], [[lem-manifold-bump-for-a-compact-set-inside-an-open-set]], [[cor-every-smooth-manifold-admits-a-riemannian-metric]]).

## Proof

1.1 Construct the initial admissible family and protect its local models. Split each fold in smooth parameter coordinates as $$f_t=g(u,t)-|z_-|^2+|z_+|^2,$$ with $g_u=g_{uu}=0$, $g_{uuu}\ne0$ and $g_{ut}\ne0$ at the event. Keep the exact product field $-g_u\partial_u+2z_-\partial_{z_-}-2z_+\partial_{z_+}$ on a smaller fixed fold chart. Outside these charts each critical branch is regular and projects to a parameter interval. Smooth parameter Morse charts can be chosen along each such interval, by finite patching of the signed-square charts. Here is the transition detail: a germ $h$ preserving the signed quadratic form $Q$ is joined to its derivative by $h_a(z)=a^{-1}h(az)$, smoothly continued at $a=0$. Its derivative lies in the group preserving $Q$. In coordinates $Q(z)=z^TJz$, a matrix $A$ in that group has polar form $A=RP$ with $P>0$, $JPJ=P^{-1}$, and $R$ orthogonal commuting with $J$. The path $RP^a$ stays in the same quadratic-form group and joins $A$ to $R$; finitely many plane rotations in the positive and negative blocks join $R$ to coordinate sign changes in its component. These sign changes commute with the normalized model field. Thus smaller chart germs can be patched across finitely many overlapping parameter subintervals while retaining a normalized model field; there is no monodromy because the branch base is an interval. On a compact parameter annulus outside a smaller fold neighborhood, use scalar square completion at each newborn root. In its scalar Morse coordinate the product field has a component $c_t(v)v\partial_v$ with the appropriate fixed nonzero sign. Interpolate this coefficient to the normalized signed constant, leaving the transverse signed-square coordinates unchanged; strict descent and the invariant coordinate disks persist. This matches the fold model to the regular charts without trying to normalize its eigenvalue at the fold. The supplied endpoint and $N$ fields and these local models patch with a background negative gradient by [F3]; strict descent off the critical curve is convex, and every fixed smaller model is kept unchanged. Choose the union $Q$ of the protected model neighborhoods, $N$, and stationary parameter-end intervals so that distinct critical models have disjoint spatial closures at any ordinary parameter, except the two newborn points in their one fold chart. Let $\mathcal A$ be the nonempty open set of descending families differing from this initial family only outside $Q$. It is open by [F4]. All later perturbations lie there. [F1, F2, F3, F4, given, construct]

2.1 Set up actual finite-flow incidence problems. For regular critical branches $p_i(t),q_j(t)$ choose compact outgoing unstable and incoming stable sphere sections in their protected local models, of dimensions $i-1$ and $n-j-1$. Their finite-flow equation is $$\Phi^T_{X_t}(A_p(t,\xi))=B_q(t,\eta),\qquad T>0.$$ Its source variables $(t,\xi,T,\eta)$ have dimension $n+i-j$, while equality to the diagonal has codimension $n$. Thus the expected dimension is $i-j$. Source sections are fixed for all candidates in $\mathcal A$. Near a fold their regular sections are considered only on compact parameter subintervals avoiding the fold, where the scalar roots and charts are smooth. Every nonpositive-drop connection has an exterior regular subsegment outside $Q$. Indeed indices0 and $n$ give empty outgoing or incoming sections whenever they could participate in a nonpositive drop; the remaining endpoints are outside $\overline N$. A trajectory between their distinct protected models must cross their exterior gap. The two points in one fold model have adjacent indices and their internal connection has positive drop, so they are not an exception. In a short exterior flowbox, a supported shear $$\partial_r\longmapsto\partial_r+\beta(r)\chi(z)w\cdot\partial_z,\qquad\int\beta=1,$$ changes its exit to first order in every transverse direction $w\in\mathbb R^{n-1}$. The time variable supplies the remaining flow direction. Subsequent flow has invertible differential, so the universal incidence map is transverse to the diagonal. For two distinct trajectories at the same parameter choose one flowbox on each, avoiding the other compact segment; distinct orbits have disjoint interiors by uniqueness. These shears prescribe the two exits independently, even if the trajectories share a critical endpoint. Two same-index incidences with one shared parameter consequently have expected dimension $-1$. At a fixed value-crossing parameter a single nonpositive-drop incidence has expected dimension $i-j-1<0$. Smooth parameter cutoffs make all shears permitted family perturbations. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Impose the incidence conditions by actual Countable Choice genericity. Exhaust the finite-flow source domains by countably many compact pieces, using finite sphere charts, bounds $1/m\le T\le m$ and parameter distance at least $1/m$ from folds and stationary ends. For double incidences also exhaust away from their coincidence diagonal, so one orbit is not counted twice. Closedness and compactness of $M$ make every bounded-time flow defined; no moving finite-flow domain must be assumed. On one compact piece, at any candidate field its coincidence set is compact. Select finitely many of the flowbox shears of step2.1 covering that set. The union of their parameters makes one finite-dimensional family transverse near all coincidences; the compact complement has positive distance from the diagonal and acquires none under small changes. [F2] therefore gives arbitrarily small perturbations transverse on this piece. Transversality there is open in a sufficiently high finite derivative topology, by compactness of the source and continuous derivatives of finite flows. For negative expected dimension this is avoidance, also open on the compact piece. Include every single nonpositive-drop problem, every double same-index problem and every fixed-value-event problem. Include as well the fixed-fold problems described in step6.1, with all smooth face and corner strata of their explicit sections. This is a specified countable list of relatively open dense subsets of $\mathcal A$. By [F4] they have a simultaneous solution. This invocation uses the proved separable complete metric field space and its ZF Baire conclusion, not a recursion selecting successively dependent perturbations. Dimension now excludes every index-increasing ordinary trajectory, makes each same-index incidence isolated and parameter-transverse, excludes two distinct such trajectories at a common parameter, and excludes them at value-crossing parameters. [F2, F3, F4, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 Prove uniform broken-trajectory compactness, including the folds, without imposing Morse–Smale assumptions. In a protected regular Morse chart, $|X|\le C|z|$, $-df(X)\ge c|z|^2$ and $|f-f(p)|\le C|z|^2$, uniformly on a compact regular parameter interval. The height-parametrized speed is consequently at most $C|a-f(p)|^{-1/2}$. Outside the critical charts $|X|/|df(X)|$ is uniformly bounded by compactness and strict descent. In a fold chart shift the scalar coordinate so its inflection is at0, with $g_{uuu}$ bounded above and below by positive constants and $g_u(0,t)=b(t)$, $b'(0)\ne0$. For $b(t)\le0$ choose the critical root $r$ of the same sign as $u$. Integral Taylor estimates give $$|g_u(u,t)|\asymp|u-r|\,|u+r|,\qquad |g(u,t)-g(r,t)|\le C|u-r|^2|u+r|\le C'|g_u(u,t)|^{3/2}.$$ These estimates use $g_{uu}(0,t)=0$ and $0<c\le g_{uuu}\le C$; for negative coordinates integrate with reversed signs. For $b(t)>0$, the elementary bounds $|g_u|\asymp b+u^2$ and $|g(u,t)-g(0,t)|\le C(b|u|+|u|^3)$ give the same last inequality with reference value $g(0,t)$. Add the transverse quadratic variables. On a small fixed chart $|z|^2\le C|z|^{3/2}$, so, if $D_t$ is the set of its two critical values or its single inflection value, $$\operatorname{dist}(f_t,D_t)\le C|\nabla f_t|^{3/2}.$$ The product field there is the negative Euclidean gradient, hence its height speed is at most $C\operatorname{dist}(a,D_t)^{-2/3}$. Integration over any height interval of length $\delta$ gives a uniform length bound $$L\delta+C\delta^{1/2}+C'\delta^{1/3},$$ with finite sums over the finitely many charts understood. Isolated singular reference heights do not affect the integral. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 For parameters converging to a limit, parametrize any connecting trajectories by height. The bound of step4.1 gives uniform equicontinuity and bounded length. They admit a uniformly convergent subsequence on a common compact height interval. To keep the choice cost precise, choose by Countable Choice a finite $1/m$ net of the compact metric manifold for each integer $m$. Equicontinuity and a finite rational height grid give finitely many $1/m$ codes for these curves. Select the least code occurring infinitely often, refining previously retained indices, and then the least new index in that class. This is a definable natural-number recursion, so gives a uniform Cauchy subsequence without Dependent Choice. Compactness makes its pointwise limits exist and the uniform Cauchy bounds give a continuous uniform limit. On every interval of regular limiting heights, the curves satisfy the height equation $h'=X_t/df_t(X_t)$ with uniformly convergent smooth coefficients. Passing to its integral equation identifies the limiting piece with a single ordinary flow line. A nonconstant orbit cannot reach a critical point in finite ordinary time by uniqueness. There are finitely many critical points at the limiting parameter, so the pieces form a finite broken trajectory, with strictly decreasing heights on its nonconstant components. Equal-height distinct endpoints at a value crossing cannot arise as a zero-height disappearing orbit: a joining orbit must cross an exterior annulus of a fixed critical chart, on which $-df(X)/|X|$ has a uniform positive lower bound. The positive length of that annulus costs a fixed positive height, contradicting a vanishing endpoint gap. No transversality or index bound was used in this compactness extraction. [F1, F2, F3, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 At a fold of indices $\lambda,\lambda+1$, its product phase line at the event has $\dot u=-u^2h(u)$ with $h>0$. Its weak stable set is $z_-=0,u\ge0$, its strong stable set has also $u=0$; its weak unstable set is $z_+=0,u\le0$, and its strong unstable set has also $u=0$. These follow directly from the scalar sign and $z_-(s)=e^{2s}z_-(0)$, $z_+(s)=e^{-2s}z_+(0)$. Intersect with small entry/exit block faces and their corner strata. Weak/strong unstable sections have dimensions $\lambda,\lambda-1$; weak/strong stable sections have dimensions $n-\lambda-1,n-\lambda-2$. Empty sections at extreme indices are omitted. The fixed-parameter finite-flow incidence dimensions, after subtracting diagonal codimension, are: ordinary $p_i\to q_j$, $i-j-1$; ordinary $p_i$ into weak/strong fold sections, $i-\lambda-1$ and $i-\lambda-2$; weak/strong fold sections to $q_j$, $\lambda-j$ and $\lambda-j-1$. Their exterior shears are those of step2.1, so they are among the countable conditions of step3.1. Negative dimension therefore excludes ordinary nonpositive-drop connections at the fold parameter. Entry into the weak fold requires $i\ge\lambda+1$, entry into the strong fold requires $i\ge\lambda+2$; exit from the weak fold requires $j\le\lambda$, and exit from the strong fold requires $j\le\lambda-1$. Now suppose same-index trajectories accumulated at this fold. The broken limit of step5.1 either avoids the fold, in which case every component strictly drops index at this fixed parameter, or passes through it, in which case the entry/exit inequalities give a strict net drop there as well. A newborn upper source tends to the weak unstable section and can only reach index at most $\lambda$; a newborn lower source tends to the strong unstable section and can only reach at most $\lambda-1$. Dually, a newborn lower target requires entry from index at least $\lambda+1$, and an upper target from at least $\lambda+2$. These section limits follow from the scalar roots and invariant transverse planes in the product chart. None permits equal source/target indices. A chain cannot revisit the fold because its nonconstant components strictly lower height. Thus some whole parameter neighborhood of every fold has no same-index trajectory. The product chart also retains the unique newborn cancelling trajectory and its full crossing spheres by [F5]; all admissible perturbations vanish there. [F1, F2, F4, F5, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Finally suppose there were infinitely many same-index trajectories. Finitely many critical branches and compact parameter space give a sequence with fixed source and target branches and a convergent parameter. The stationary endpoint intervals contain none; step6.1 excludes fold limits. At an ordinary limiting parameter step5.1 gives a broken chain. No component increases index by step3.1. Equal endpoint indices force every component to have index drop zero. A chain with two or more components would then give two distinct same-index trajectories at one parameter, excluded by the double-incidence condition. The limit is therefore unbroken. Its transit between the fixed source and destination sections is finite, and smooth dependence and transverse section hitting give convergence of its incidence coordinates, including transit time. The transverse zero-dimensional incidence set is locally isolated, contradicting accumulation of distinct events. Value-crossing parameters present no exception by step3.1 and the annulus bound in step5.1. This proves global finiteness and distinctness of the event parameters, with the parameter-normal isomorphism already given by step3.1. All fixed models, endpoint fields and the prescribed $N$ field were retained in $\mathcal A$. The only countable selections were the explicit Countable Choice uses in [F3]–[F4] and the independent finite-net family of step5.1; no genericity at positive drops or based handle-movie completeness has been inferred. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 3.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct] ∎
