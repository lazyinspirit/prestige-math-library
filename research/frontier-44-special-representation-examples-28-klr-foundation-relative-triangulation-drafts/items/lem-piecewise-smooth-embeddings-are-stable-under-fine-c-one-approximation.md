## Statement

Let $K$ be finite and $f:K\to\mathbb R^q$ a whole-star smooth immersion in the sense of [[def-relative-smooth-triangulation-and-projection-preserving-fitting]]. There is $\delta>0$ such that every strong piecewise $C^1$ $\delta$-approximation $g:K'\to\mathbb R^q$, on any finite subdivision $K'$, is a whole-star immersion. If $f$ is also an embedding, $\delta$ may be chosen so that $g$ is an embedding. The subdivision need not have a mesh or shape bound.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the finite complex, map and whole-star hypothesis in the Statement.

[F1] The approximation contract controls the derivatives on every tangent cone, even after subdivision ([[def-relative-smooth-triangulation-and-projection-preserving-fitting]]).

[F2] A continuous positive function on a compact set has a positive minimum, and integration of a continuous derivative along a segment gives the endpoint difference ([[thm-extreme-value-metric]], [[cor-vector-valued-ftc-and-lipschitz-bound]]). For a piecewise smooth map on a finite subdivided segment, apply the latter on each subinterval and add.

## Proof

1.1 Each simplex differential has full rank, because whole-star injectivity includes its cone and linearity then includes its affine tangent space. On the unit sphere in each such tangent space, $|D(f|\sigma)(b)v|$ has a positive minimum as $b$ ranges over the closed simplex. Take the minimum $\mu>0$ over the finitely many positive-dimensional simplices. If the complex has only vertices the immersion condition is empty. Derivative changes of norm less than $\mu/2$ preserve these ranks. Furthermore, if $|V|\ge\mu$ and $|W-V|<\delta$, the angle between $V$ and $W$ tends uniformly to zero as $\delta\to0$: indeed $|W|\ge\mu-\delta$ and $|W/|W|-V/|V||\le2\delta/(\mu-\delta)$. Thus strong derivative approximation gives arbitrarily small uniform angle approximation. [F1, F2, given, algebra]

1.2 Consider two simplices $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$ with common face $\tau$ proper in both. Let $c$ be the barycentre of $\tau$. A point $p\in\sigma_1\setminus\tau$ has a unique expression $p=(1-t)x+ty$, with $x\in\tau$, $y$ in the opposite face, and $t\in(0,1]$; for $t=1$ choose $x=c$. Define its projection to $\tau$ instead by $x_p=p+t(c-y)$, so $p-x_p=t(y-c)$ and $x_p\in\tau$. This projection is continuous, affine-natural, and fixes $\tau$: the expression for $x_p$ is $(1-t)x+tc$, which also proves continuity at $t=1$. For any point $z\in\sigma_2$ with $z\ne x_p$, the angle between $p-x_p$ and $z-x_p$ is bounded below by the angle from $\sigma_1$ to $\sigma_2$ defined by minimizing the angle between $y-c$ and $z'-c$, with $y$ in the opposite face and $z'\in\sigma_2\setminus\{c\}$. To see this, translate the ray $x_p z$ to start at $c$; its direction is in the cone generated by $\sigma_2-c$, since $x_p\in\tau$ and $c$ is relatively interior to $\tau$. Extend the ray to the boundary of this cone's truncated simplex star. Its endpoint ranges over a compact set not containing $c$, so the minimum exists. It is positive: equality of ray directions would give a ray in both simplices outside their common face, contrary to the simplicial intersection property. The same argument applies to any linear injective image of this two-simplex complex. [F2, algebra]

2.1 For $b\in\tau$, the map $d f_b$ is a linear embedding on each simplex of its star and injective on their union. Apply step 1.2 to its images of $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$. The resulting minimum angle varies continuously with $b$: all vertex images vary smoothly and remain nondegenerate, and minima over the fixed compact parameter spaces are continuous by uniform continuity. Take the positive minimum over $b\in\tau$ and the finitely many ordered simplex pairs, denoting it by $\alpha>0$; if there are no such pairs set $\alpha=\pi/2$. Reduce it if necessary to have $\alpha\le\pi/2$. These are actual finite-star separation angles, not merely rank bounds on separate simplices. [F2, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 Fix $b\in|K|$. In a small star neighbourhood $U_b$, continuity of the finitely many original simplex derivatives makes $f$ an angle approximation to the frozen piecewise linear $d f_b$ with tolerance $\alpha/8$. Step 1.1 permits a uniform $\delta$ making $g$ an angle approximation to $f$ with tolerance $\alpha/8$, hence to that frozen map with tolerance $\alpha/4$ on $U_b$. Choose a smaller neighbourhood $V_b$ such that for every pair of points $p,q\in V_b$ the two-segment path $p x q$ lies in $U_b$, where $x$ is the midpoint when the points belong to the same original simplex and is the projection from step 1.2 otherwise. Such shrinking is possible because the finitely many face projections are continuous and fix $b$. Integrate each subdivided segment using [F2]. Its endpoint difference lies in the closed cone of vectors making angle at most $\alpha/4$ with its frozen image direction: projection onto that direction is positive, and transverse projection is bounded by its tangent times the longitudinal projection, an inequality preserved under finite sums and integration. The two frozen rays from $x$ toward $p$ and $q$ have angle at least $\alpha$ (or $\pi$ in the midpoint case). Their perturbed endpoint differences have angle at least $\alpha/2$ and cannot be equal. Thus $g(p)\ne g(q)$ for distinct $p,q\in V_b$, independently of the subdivision. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 The same two-segment argument proves that $d g_b$ is injective on its tangent star: replace $g$ by this piecewise linear map, whose derivatives on each refined cone differ from $d f_b$ by at most $\delta$ by [F1]. Distinct cone vectors can be scaled simultaneously into the neighbourhood where the projected paths are defined; the angle argument then forbids equality of their images. The positive rank bounds cover the same-cone case. Compactness and a finite cover by the neighbourhoods of step 3.1 allow all derivative continuity tolerances and resulting $\delta$ to be chosen simultaneously. This proves stability of immersion. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 Suppose $f$ is an embedding. Retain a finite cover of $|K|$ by the $V_b$. The closed set of pairs $(p,q)$ which do not belong together to any member of this cover is compact and disjoint from the diagonal. On it $|f(p)-f(q)|$ has positive minimum $\eta$; if it is empty no such constraint is needed. Requiring also $\delta<\eta/3$ gives $|g(p)-g(q)|\ge\eta-2\delta>0$ on these pairs. Step 3.1 separates all other distinct pairs. Thus $g$ is continuous and injective on a compact space and is therefore a homeomorphism onto its image in the Hausdorff target. Together with step 4.1 this proves the embedding assertion. [F1, F2, step 3.1, step 4.1] ∎
