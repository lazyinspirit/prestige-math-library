## Statement

Let $(W,S)$ be an irreducible finite-type Coxeter system with $|S|=n\ge2$, with bipartition $J\sqcup K=S$ and data $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n$, $s_1,\dots,s_n$, $a,b,c=ab$, $h=\operatorname{ord}(c)$, $\beta_i,\rho_i,\mu_i$ and conditional map $\mu$ as in [[def-cg-bipartite-coxeter-element-and-root-recursion]]. Put $r:=|J|$, so $J=\{s_1,\dots,s_r\}$ and $K=\{s_{r+1},\dots,s_n\}$. Let $C_V:=\rho(c)\in\mathrm{GL}(V)$ denote the linear action of $c$, let $\mathcal C$ be the primal chamber in $V$ and $\mathcal C^\circ$ its interior, and let $H_\alpha=\{x\in V:B(x,\alpha)=0\}$ be the root hyperplanes ([[def-cg-real-coxeter-form-and-reflection]], [[def-cg-canonical-reflection-homomorphism]], [[def-cg-finite-reflection-arrangement-and-spherical-chambers]]). Write $w_0$ for the longest element ([[thm-cg-finite-parabolic-longest-element-and-opposition]]). Then:

**(1) The Coxeter-plane eigenvector.** The matrix $A=\bigl(2(\delta_{st}-B(e_s,e_t))\bigr)_{s,t\in S}$ has nonnegative entries, zero diagonal, and connected nonzero off-diagonal pattern. There are $p\in\mathbb R_{>0}^S$ and $\lambda\in(0,2)$ with $Ap=\lambda p$, and $\lambda=2\cos(\pi/h)$.

**(2) The invariant plane and chamber sector.** Put $u:=\sum_{s\in J}p_s\alpha_s$, $v:=\sum_{t\in K}p_t\alpha_t$, $d:=\sum_{s\in J}p_s^2$ and $P:=\mathrm{span}(u,v)$. Then $d>0$, $B(u,u)=B(v,v)=d$, $B(u,v)=-\lambda d/2$, the plane $P$ is invariant under $\rho(a)$, $\rho(b)$ and $C_V$, and

$$\rho(a)u=-u,\qquad \rho(a)v=v+\lambda u,\qquad \rho(b)v=-v,\qquad \rho(b)u=u+\lambda v.$$

Thus $C_V|_P$ is a rotation through $2\theta$, where $\theta:=\arccos(\lambda/2)=\pi/h$. The intersection $\mathcal C\cap P$ is a sector of angle $\theta$ whose relative interior lies in $\mathcal C^\circ$; it contains a point with trivial $W$-stabiliser. The restrictions of $\rho(a)$ and $\rho(b)$ generate a dihedral group of order $2h$ on $P$. Its $2h$ translates of $\mathcal C\cap P$ are exactly the full-dimensional chamber sections $w\mathcal C\cap P$: they are the sectors cut out by the $h$ root-hyperplane traces, with pairwise disjoint relative interiors. The set of traces $\{H_\alpha\cap P:\alpha\in\Phi\}$ is exactly the set of $h$ reflection lines of this dihedral action.

**(3) Enumeration of the roots.** Every $\rho_i$ is a root, $\rho_{i+n}=C_V\rho_i$ for all $i\ge1$, and

$$\Phi_+=\{\rho_1,\dots,\rho_{nh/2}\},\qquad \Phi_-=-\Phi_+=\{\rho_{nh/2+1},\dots,\rho_{nh}\},$$

with all $nh$ listed roots pairwise distinct; hence the sequence has exact period $nh$. Moreover $|\Phi_+|=|T|=nh/2=\ell(w_0)$. If $h$ is even then $w_0=c^{h/2}$. If $h$ is odd then $n$ is even, $r=|J|=|K|=n/2$, and $w_0=c^{(h-1)/2}a$. Accordingly, the word

$$(s_1\cdots s_n)^{h/2}\qquad\text{or}\qquad(s_1\cdots s_n)^{(h-1)/2}s_1\cdots s_r$$

is a reduced expression of $w_0$ of length $nh/2$, with prefix roots exactly $\rho_1,\dots,\rho_{nh/2}$ in this order.

**(4) Invertibility of the linear action minus the identity.** The operator $C_V-\mathrm{id}_V$ is invertible. Consequently the conditional map from the preceding definition is defined on all of $V$ and satisfies $\mu(\rho_i)=\mu_i$ for every $i\ge1$.

For $n=1$ the conclusions are direct: $\Phi_+=\{\alpha_1\}=\{\rho_1\}$, $c=s_1$ and $h=2$. Reducible systems are handled componentwise using the preceding definition's clause (5), and no uniform $nh/2$ count is asserted for unequal component Coxeter numbers. No Choice is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An irreducible finite-type Coxeter system $(W,S)$ with $|S|=n\ge2$, the bipartite data $\alpha_i,s_i,R_i,a,b,c,h,\beta_i,\rho_i,\mu_i$ of [[def-cg-bipartite-coxeter-element-and-root-recursion]], the operator $C_V=\rho(c)$, the positive definite space $(V,B)$ with root system $\Phi=\Phi_+\sqcup\Phi_-$ and reflection set $T$, and the transferred primal chamber $\mathcal C$ and root hyperplanes $H_\alpha$.

[F1] The defining data have $\Gamma$ connected, $J=\{s_1,\dots,s_r\}$, $K=\{s_{r+1},\dots,s_n\}$, pairwise orthogonal simple roots within each class, and $\rho_{i+n}=C_V\rho_i$, $\mu_{i+n}=C_V\mu_i$ for every $i\ge1$. The vectors $\beta_i$ satisfy $B(\beta_i,\alpha_j)=\delta_{ij}$. [[def-cg-bipartite-coxeter-element-and-root-recursion]] (1)-(3)

[F2] $B$ is positive definite, $\rho$ is faithful and preserves $B$, and every root satisfies $B(\alpha,\alpha)=1$; for each $s$, $R_s=\rho(s)$ is the reflection $x\mapsto x-2B(x,\alpha_s)\alpha_s$. [[thm-cg-finite-type-positive-definite-criterion]], [[lem-cg-reflection-representation-descends-and-root-norms]] (1)-(3), [[thm-cg-root-length-criterion-and-faithfulness]] (3)

[F3] The roots split as $\Phi=\Phi_+\sqcup\Phi_-$ with $\Phi_-=-\Phi_+$ and $\Phi_+$ consists of the nonzero vectors with nonnegative simple-root coordinates; each $R_s$ sends $\alpha_s$ to $-\alpha_s$ and permutes $\Phi_+\setminus\{\alpha_s\}$. [[thm-cg-root-sign-and-simple-reflection-positivity]] (1)-(3)

[F4] The transferred chamber is $\mathcal C=\{x\in V:B(x,\alpha_s)\ge0\ \forall s\}$ with interior defined by strict inequalities; the chambers $w\mathcal C$ tile $V$ with disjoint interiors, and a point in $w\mathcal C_I$ has stabiliser $wW_Iw^{-1}$. In particular a point of $\mathcal C^\circ$ has trivial stabiliser, and a point in the relative interior of $\mathcal C_J$ has stabiliser $W_J$. The interior of every chamber is disjoint from every root hyperplane, and $H_{\rho(w)\alpha}=\rho(w)H_\alpha$. [[def-cg-finite-reflection-arrangement-and-spherical-chambers]], [[thm-cg-finite-chamber-tiling-and-coset-face-identification]] (1),(3)

[F5] For every $w\in W$ and $s\in S$, $\ell(ws)>\ell(w)$ if and only if $\rho(w)\alpha_s\in\Phi_+$. [[thm-cg-root-length-criterion-and-faithfulness]] (1)

[F6] The longest element satisfies $\rho(w_0)\Phi_+=\Phi_-$, $\ell(w_0)=|\Phi_+|=|T|$, $w_0^2=1$, and it is the unique element of length $\ell(w_0)$. [[thm-cg-finite-parabolic-longest-element-and-opposition]] (1)(ii)-(iv)

[F7] The map $\Phi_+\to T$, $\alpha\mapsto t_\alpha$, is a bijection, $\rho(t_\alpha)=r_\alpha$, and $t_\alpha=t_\beta$ exactly when $\alpha=\pm\beta$. Thus root hyperplanes are in bijection with $T$. [[thm-cg-root-inversion-formulas-and-strong-exchange]] (1)(i)-(iv)

[F8] The coordinate norm $\|\cdot\|_0$ and inner product $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_0$ are the standard Euclidean ones on $\mathbb R^S$. For $n\ge1$ the coordinate unit sphere is nonempty (it contains each simple basis vector $\alpha_s$) and compact. The identity map is continuous, and a linear map $L$ is continuous because $\|Lx-Ly\|_0\le K\|x-y\|_0$ for some $K\ge0$. The inner product of continuous vector-valued maps is continuous, so $q(x)=\langle x,Ax\rangle_0$ is continuous; every continuous real-valued function on a nonempty compact subset of $\mathbb R^n$ attains a maximum. [[def-cg-real-coxeter-form-and-reflection]], [[def-euclidean-inner-product]], [[lem-euclidean-linear-maps-have-matrices-and-are-bounded]], [[thm-componentwise-limits-and-continuity]] (3), [[cor-euclidean-closed-balls-and-spheres-are-compact]], [[thm-euclidean-heine-borel-pseudocompactness-and-extreme-values]] (4)]

[F9] For a reducible diagram with components $S_i$, $W$ is the direct product of the $W_{S_i}$ and $V$ is their orthogonal direct sum; the root, reflection and bipartite constructions are componentwise. [[lem-cg-diagram-products-and-invariant-form-comparison]], [[def-cg-coxeter-diagram-components-and-finite-type]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** maximize the nonnegative adjacency quadratic form, build the invariant plane, and use the chamber tiling to determine its angle and the roots crossing its walls.

1.1 Write $\|x\|_0^2:=\sum_{s\in S}x_s^2$ for the standard coordinate norm and set $q(x):=x^{\mathsf T}Ax$. If $m(s,t)=\infty$ for distinct $s,t$, then $B(e_s+e_t,e_s+e_t)=0$, contradicting positive definiteness; hence every off-diagonal Coxeter exponent is finite. Therefore $A_{ss}=0$ and $A_{st}=-2B(e_s,e_t)=2\cos(\pi/m(s,t))\ge0$ for $s\ne t$, with $A_{st}>0$ exactly on the edges of the connected diagram. Also $q(x)=2\|x\|_0^2-2B(x,x)<2\|x\|_0^2$ for every nonzero $x$. [F1, F2, algebra]

1.2 For $1\le i\le n$, the first $n$ prefix vectors satisfy $\rho_i=\alpha_i$ for $i\le r$ and $\rho_i=\rho(a)\alpha_i=\alpha_i+\sum_{s\in J}A_{si}\alpha_s$ for $i>r$: the earlier reflections in the same color class fix $\alpha_i$, while each reflection in the other class adds its $A_{si}\alpha_s$ and fixes the other roots of that class. Hence the coordinate matrix of $(\rho_1,\dots,\rho_n)$ relative to the ordered basis $(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n)$ is block upper triangular with identity diagonal blocks, so these vectors form a basis. The cyclic definitions give $\rho_{i+n}=C_V\rho_i$ and $\mu_{i+n}=C_V\mu_i$ for every $i\ge1$, because each block of $n$ reflections has product $c$. Each $\rho_i$ is a root, being the image of a simple root by an element of $W$. [F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 The unit sphere for $\|\cdot\|_0$ is compact, so $q$ attains a maximum $\lambda$ there; an edge $\{s,t\}$ gives $q(e_s+e_t)=4\cos(\pi/m(s,t))>0$, so $\lambda>0$, and the strict inequality of 1.1 at a maximizer gives $\lambda<2$. Let $p$ be a unit maximizer. For $y$ with $\langle p,y\rangle_0=0$, the curve $\gamma(t)=(p+ty)/(1+t^2\|y\|_0^2)^{1/2}$ stays on the unit sphere, and the derivative at $t=0$ of $q(\gamma(t))$ is $2\langle Ap,y\rangle_0$; maximality makes this zero for every such $y$, so $Ap$ is a multiple of $p$, and pairing with $p$ shows $Ap=\lambda p$. [F2, F8, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 For $1\le i\le n$, duality gives $B(\beta_i,\alpha_j)=0$ for every $j\ne i$, so $R_j\beta_i=\beta_i$ for every $j\ne i$ and in particular $\mu_i=R_1\cdots R_{i-1}\beta_i=\beta_i$. Also $R_i\beta_i=\beta_i-2\alpha_i$. Thus $C_V\beta_i=R_1\cdots R_n\beta_i=R_1\cdots R_{i-1}(\beta_i-2\alpha_i)=\mu_i-2\rho_i=\beta_i-2\rho_i$, so $(C_V-\mathrm{id}_V)\beta_i=-2\rho_i$. [F1, F2, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Since every entry of $A$ is nonnegative, $q(|p|)\ge q(p)=\lambda$, while $\||p|\|_0=1$, so $|p|$ is also a maximizer and satisfies $A|p|=\lambda|p|$ by the stationary-point argument in 2.1. Put $r:=|p|$. If $r_s>0$ and $A_{st}>0$, then $\lambda r_t=\sum_uA_{tu}r_u\ge A_{ts}r_s>0$; since $\lambda>0$ and the edge graph of $A$ is connected, positivity propagates to every coordinate. Thus we may take $p\in\mathbb R_{>0}^S$, proving the existence claim in (1). [F1, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 The vectors $\beta_1,\dots,\beta_n$ form a basis by the construction in the preceding definition, and $\rho_1,\dots,\rho_n$ form a basis by 1.2; thus 2.2 shows that $C_V-\mathrm{id}_V$ maps one basis to the other up to the nonzero scalar $-2$, so it is invertible. If $i=mn+j$ with $m\ge0$ and $1\le j\le n$, then the cyclic recursions in F1 and the identity in 2.2 give $(C_V-\mathrm{id}_V)\mu_i=C_V^m(C_V-\mathrm{id}_V)\mu_j=-2C_V^m\rho_j=-2\rho_i$. Applying the conditional map of the preceding definition now yields $\mu(\rho_i)=\mu_i$ for all $i\ge1$, proving (4) and the definition's well-definedness justification. [F1, step 1.2, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 Put $d':=\sum_{t\in K}p_t^2$. Since distinct simple roots in either color class are orthogonal, $B(u,u)=d$ and $B(v,v)=d'$. Multiplying the eigenvector equations $\sum_{t\in K}A_{st}p_t=\lambda p_s$ by $p_s$ and summing over $s\in J$ gives $\sum_{s\in J,t\in K}p_sA_{st}p_t=\lambda d$; summing the equations indexed by $K$ gives the same left side equal to $\lambda d'$, hence $d=d'>0$. Finally $B(u,v)=-\frac12\sum_{s\in J,t\in K}p_sA_{st}p_t=-\lambda d/2$. The Gram matrix on $(u,v)$ is $d\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-\lambda/2\\-\lambda/2&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with determinant $d^2(1-\lambda^2/4)>0$, so $u,v$ are independent and $P$ is a plane. [F1, F2, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 For $s\in J$, $R_s$ fixes $\alpha_{s'}$ for $s'\in J\setminus\{s\}$ and negates $\alpha_s$; the corresponding statement holds in $K$. For $t\in K$, the commuting reflections in $a$ give $\rho(a)\alpha_t=\alpha_t+\sum_{s\in J}A_{st}\alpha_s$, and for $s\in J$, $\rho(b)\alpha_s=\alpha_s+\sum_{t\in K}A_{ts}\alpha_t$. Summing with coefficients $p_t$ and $p_s$ and using $Ap=\lambda p$ yields $\rho(a)u=-u$, $\rho(a)v=v+\lambda u$, $\rho(b)v=-v$, and $\rho(b)u=u+\lambda v$. Thus $P$ is invariant under $\rho(a),\rho(b)$ and $C_V=\rho(a)\rho(b)$. [F1, F2, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 In the basis $(u,v)$, $\rho(a)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&\lambda\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, $\rho(b)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\\lambda&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, and $C_V|_P=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\lambda^2-1&-\lambda\\\lambda&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. The vectors $f_1:=u/\sqrt d$ and $f_2:=(v+\frac\lambda2u)/\sqrt{d(1-\lambda^2/4)}$ are $B$-orthonormal, and direct substitution gives $C_Vf_1=(\lambda^2/2-1)f_1+\lambda\sqrt{1-\lambda^2/4}\,f_2$. With $\theta:=\arccos(\lambda/2)\in(0,\pi/2)$ these coefficients are $\cos(2\theta)$ and $\sin(2\theta)$; since $C_V$ preserves $B$ and has determinant $1$ on $P$, it is the rotation through $2\theta$. [F2, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 For $x=\xi u+\eta v\in P$, one has $B(x,\alpha_s)=p_s(\xi-\frac\lambda2\eta)$ for $s\in J$ and $B(x,\alpha_t)=p_t(\eta-\frac\lambda2\xi)$ for $t\in K$. Thus $\mathcal C\cap P$ is given by $\xi\ge\frac\lambda2\eta$ and $\eta\ge\frac\lambda2\xi$; because $0<\lambda/2<1$, these inequalities imply $\xi,\eta\ge0$, and their strict versions place the relative interior in $\mathcal C^\circ$. Its boundary rays are $x_J:=\lambda u+2v$ (the $J$ simple-root hyperplanes) and $x_K:=2u+\lambda v$ (the $K$ simple-root hyperplanes). Their squared $B$-norms are both $d(4-\lambda^2)$ and $B(x_J,x_K)=\lambda d(2-\lambda^2/2)$, so the sector angle has cosine $\lambda/2$ and equals $\theta$. The restrictions of $\rho(a)$ and $\rho(b)$ are reflections in the lines $\mathbb R x_J$ and $\mathbb R x_K$: they fix those lines, are involutions, and are nonidentity on $P$ by 4.1. [F1, F2, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 Let $m_0$ be the order of $C_V|_P$. Since it rotates through $2\theta\in(0,\pi)$, $m_0\ge3$ and $2\theta=2\pi j/m_0$ for an integer $j$ with $1\le j<m_0/2$ and $\gcd(j,m_0)=1$. The subgroup $D:=\langle\rho(a)|_P,\rho(b)|_P\rangle$ consists of the $m_0$ distinct rotations $(C_V|_P)^k$ and the $m_0$ distinct reflections $\rho(a)|_P(C_V|_P)^k$, so $|D|=2m_0$. Distinct elements of $D$ have lifts $\tilde g\in\langle a,b\rangle$ with distinct restrictions to $P$, hence are distinct elements of $W$; by F4 their chamber interiors are distinct and disjoint. Thus the orbit of $S_0:=\mathcal C\cap P$ consists of $2m_0$ distinct sectors of angle $\theta$, each of the form $\rho(\tilde g)\mathcal C\cap P$, with pairwise disjoint relative interiors. Hence $2m_0\theta\le2\pi$, so $j=1$ and $\theta=\pi/m_0$; equality shows the sectors cover $P$. As $m_0\mid h$ by F1, the element $c^{m_0}$ fixes every point of $P$, in particular a point of $\mathcal C^\circ\cap P$; its stabiliser is trivial, so $c^{m_0}=1$ and $h\mid m_0$. Thus $m_0=h$ and $\lambda=2\cos(\pi/h)$, completing (1) and the angle claims of (2). [F1, F2, F4, step 6.1, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 For every root $\alpha$, the trace $H_\alpha\cap P$ is a line: if $P\subseteq H_\alpha$, the nonidentity reflection $t_\alpha$ fixes a point of $\mathcal C^\circ\cap P$, contradicting its trivial stabiliser; otherwise the nonzero linear functional $x\mapsto B(x,\alpha)$ restricts to a nonzero functional on the two-plane $P$, whose kernel is a line. Each such line is a root-hyperplane trace and cannot meet the relative interior of any sector $\rho(\tilde g)S_0$, because that relative interior lies in the chamber interior $\rho(\tilde g)\mathcal C^\circ$, which is disjoint from the arrangement. Conversely the two boundary lines of $S_0$ are traces of simple-root hyperplanes by 7.1, and their images under $D$ are root-hyperplane traces because $\rho(W)$ permutes roots. These images are all the sector boundary lines: the $2h$ sectors from 8.1 tile $P$ and have $2h$ distinct boundary rays, while every root trace must be one of those lines because it cannot meet a sector interior. Opposite rays define the same line, so the traces are exactly $h$ distinct lines. The connected components of the complement of these lines are the sector interiors. Each component is connected and the ambient chamber interiors form a disjoint open cover of it, so it lies in one chamber interior. Conversely, if a full-dimensional chamber section contained points from two components, choose them in its relative interior; their segment lies in that relative interior by convexity and crosses a root trace, contradicting disjointness of chamber interiors from the arrangement. Hence the full-dimensional chamber sections are exactly the $2h$ sectors. [F2, F4, step 7.1, step 8.1, algebra]

10.1 The ray $\mathbb R_{>0}x_J$ lies in the relative interior of the face $\mathcal C_J$, so its stabiliser is $W_J$; similarly the stabiliser of $\mathbb R_{>0}x_K$ is $W_K$. Every element of $W_J$ reduces to a product of a subset $I\subseteq J$ because its simple generators are commuting involutions; the subset products are distinct because their $\rho$-images have different signs on the independent simple roots and $\rho$ is faithful by [F2]. The image of the product for $I$ acts by $-1$ on $\operatorname{span}\{\alpha_s:s\in I\}$ and by $+1$ on its $B$-orthogonal complement, so its $(-1)$-eigenspace has dimension $|I|$; therefore it is a reflection exactly when $|I|=1$, and the reflections in $W_J$ are precisely its $|J|=r$ simple generators. The same reasoning gives exactly $|K|=n-r$ reflections in $W_K$. The $2h$ distinct boundary rays from 8.1 alternate between the two types, so there are $h$ of each; conjugating the stabiliser count around them gives $hr+h(n-r)=hn$ incidences between rays and ambient root hyperplanes. Each hyperplane contributes exactly two incidences because its trace is a line. Hence the arrangement has $hn/2$ hyperplanes, so $|T|=hn/2$ by the root-reflection correspondence of [F7]. [F1, F2, F4, F7, step 6.1, step 7.1, step 8.1, step 9.1, algebra]

11.1 If $h$ is odd, a semicircle from a point of $\mathcal C^\circ\cap P$ to its antipode crosses each trace line once; since every root-hyperplane trace is one of these lines, it meets each ambient root hyperplane exactly once. The boundary-ray types alternate, so one type occurs $(h+1)/2$ times and the other $(h-1)/2$ times. Counting the hyperplanes met using 10.1 gives $hn/2=((h+1)/2)r+((h-1)/2)(n-r)$ if the more frequent rays have type $J$, and the same equation with $r$ and $n-r$ interchanged if they have type $K$. Either equation gives $r=n/2$. Thus $n$ is even and $|J|=|K|=n/2$. [step 9.1, step 10.1, algebra]

12.1 Choose the direction around the circle so that the first boundary ray of $S_0$ is $x_J$. The rays then occur in the cyclic order $p_{2k}=C_V^k\mathbb R_{>0}x_J$ and $p_{2k+1}=C_V^k\rho(a)\mathbb R_{>0}x_K$ for $k=0,\dots,h-1$: $\rho(a)$ reflects across the $J$ boundary, while $C_V$ advances by two sector angles. The hyperplanes through $p_{2k}$ are exactly $H_{C_V^k\alpha_s}$ for $s=s_1,\dots,s_r$, and those through $p_{2k+1}$ are exactly $H_{C_V^k\rho(a)\alpha_t}$ for $t=s_{r+1},\dots,s_n$, by the stabiliser counts and the description of the reflections in $W_J,W_K$ from 10.1. These are precisely the blocks $H_{\rho_{kn+1}},\dots,H_{\rho_{kn+r}}$ and $H_{\rho_{kn+r+1}},\dots,H_{\rho_{(k+1)n}}$ by the cyclic prefix definition in 1.2. The first $h$ rays carry $hn/2$ hyperplanes: this is $hn/2$ from $h/2$ complete color pairs when $h$ is even, and $n(h-1)/2+r=hn/2$ when $h$ is odd by 11.1. The opposite semicircle carries the other $hn/2$ hyperplanes. 13.1 Fix $k\le hn/2$. The first half-turn list of hyperplanes in 12.1 is pairwise distinct, so $\rho_k\ne\pm\rho_j$ for every $j<k$. 14.1 Let $q:=hn/2$. The word $w=c^{h/2}$ for even $h$, or $w=c^{(h-1)/2}a$ for odd $h$, has exactly $q$ letters; in the odd case this uses $r=n/2$ from 11.1. Its prefix roots are $\rho_1,\dots,\rho_q$ by the cyclic definition, and these are positive by 13.1. At each step the root-length criterion therefore increases the prefix length by one, so the word is reduced and $\ell(w)=q=\ell(w_0)$. By uniqueness of the longest element, $w=w_0$, proving the formulas for $w_0$ in (3). [F5, F6, step 11.1, step 13.1, algebra]

15.1 The full word $(s_1\cdots s_n)^h$ represents $1$ and splits after its first $q$ letters, whose product is $w_0$ by 14.1. Its remaining $q$-letter suffix also represents $w_0^{-1}=w_0$, so it is reduced. Every prefix of a reduced word is reduced; applying the root-length criterion at each next letter shows that every prefix root of this suffix is positive. Its corresponding global prefix root is obtained by applying $\rho(w_0)$ and is therefore negative by [F6]. The second half in 12.1 has pairwise distinct hyperplanes, so these negative roots are pairwise distinct as well. Hence the first $q$ roots are all of $\Phi_+$ because $|\Phi_+|=|T|=q$ by [F6] and 10.1, and the second $q$ are all of $\Phi_-=-\Phi_+$. The first and second lists are disjoint by sign, giving pairwise distinctness of all $nh$ roots; $C_V^h=\mathrm{id}_V$ gives period $nh$, and that distinctness makes it exact. This proves (3).
