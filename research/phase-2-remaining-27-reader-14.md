**(1) `thm-dmc-tree-and-successor-menu-formulations`, step 3.1.** The step
claimed that for $x \in F_n$ realised by $t \in T'_{n+1}$ "the node $t$ has an
extension in $T'_{n+2}$ by the nonemptiness of that level together with [F2]".
That is false: the tree form of DMC yields a subtree with nonempty finite levels
but not a pruned subtree (the definition item says so explicitly). Explicit
counterexample to the claim: for $A=\{0,1\}$ and $R$ the equality relation,
$T' = \{\varnothing\}\cup\{(0)\}\cup\{(1)\}\cup\{(0,0)\}$ is a subtree of $T_R$
with finite nonempty levels, but the node $(1)$ has no extension in $T'$, and
the entry $1 \in F_0$ has no $R$-successor in $F_1 = \{0\}$. Repair: prune $T'$
to $T'' := \{t \in T' : t \text{ extends to every higher level of } T'\}$,
prove $T''_n \ne \varnothing$ (otherwise the finitely many least dying levels of
the members of $T'_n$ have a maximum $j^{*}$, and no node of $T'_{j^{*}}$
extends any member of $T'_n$, although each does extend its own initial
segment), and prove every $t \in T''_n$ has an extension in $T''_{n+1}$ (the
finite set of non-$T''$ one-step extensions has a maximum dying level $j^{+}$,
and $t \in T''$ has an extension $w$ at that level whose $(n+1)$-th initial
segment then lies in $T''$). $T''$ is downward closed with finite levels, so
$F_n := \{t(n) : t \in T''_{n+1}\}$ satisfies the menu condition as claimed; the
downstream steps 4.2 and 5.2 keep their references to step 3.1.

**(2) `thm-dc-iff-products-compact-hausdorff-are-baire`, steps 4.1, 5.1, 7.1.**
The recursion relation $\rho$ was ill-formed and inverted: it required
$B'_i = B_i$ *and* $\overline{B_i} \subseteq B'_i$ for $i \in F$ (forcing the
old sets to be closed), and the nesting direction $\overline{B^n_i} \subseteq
B^{n+1}_i$ is inconsistent with step 6.2, which uses the opposite inclusion
$\overline{B^{n+1}_i} \subseteq B^n_i$, and with step 8.1, which needs
$b_i \in B^n_i$ for all $n$. Repair: $\rho$ now requires only $F \subseteq F'$
and $\overline{B'_i} \subseteq B_i$ for $i \in F$; the entireness argument
chooses a basic cylinder inside $C \cap U_{n+1}$ (with $C = \bigcap_{i \in F}
\pi_i^{-1}[B_i] \subseteq B \cap U_n$), adds $C$'s coordinates where omitted and
then shrinks every coordinate by regularity of the compact Hausdorff factor
$X_i$ to sets whose closures lie in the cylinder; step 5.1 records
$\overline{B^{n+1}_i} \subseteq B^n_i$; step 7.1 chooses $b_i$ in the nested
closed intersections $\bigcap_{n \ge n_i}\overline{B^n_i}$, which compactness
makes nonempty and which step 6.2 embeds in $\bigcap_{n\ge n_i}B^n_i$, so
step 8.1's use "$b_i \in B^n_i$ for $i \in F_n$" is licensed.

**(5) `thm-effective-metacompact-discrete-metrics-implies-ac`, [F2] and steps
1.1-4.1.** The extraction repeated the source's printed proof
(Good–Tree–Watson, Proposition 5), whose inference "a member of $\mathcal{U}$
that contains $F$ is one of the pairs $\{x,F\}$ with $x \in F$" fails for a
disjoint family whose members can be elements of other members. Counterexample
to the inference: $F = \{A,B\}$ with $A = \{x\}$, $B = \{A,y\}$; then
$\{A,B\} \in \mathcal{U}$ contains $A$ but is not of the form $\{z,A\}$ with
$z \in A$, and the effective point-finite refinement
$\mathcal{V} = \{\{A\},\{x\},\{y\},\{A,B\}\}$ with $a(\{A\}) = a(\{A,B\}) =
\{A,B\}$ gives $f(A) = \varnothing$. Repair: the space is replaced by the
tagged disjoint union $X := (F\times\{0\}) \cup (\bigcup F\times\{1\})$ with the
cover $\mathcal{U} := \{\{(x,1),(F,0)\} : x \in F \in F\}$; then for
$V \in C(F)$ one has $(F,0) \in V \subseteq a(V) = \{(x,1),(F',0)\}$, and
$(F,0) \in a(V)$ forces $F' = F$ (the alternative $(F,0) = (x,1)$ would give
$0 = 1$), so $a(V) = \{(x,1),(F,0)\}$ with $x \in F$ and the extraction is
nonempty. This is the standard tagged-union repair; the sets $F$ and
$\bigcup F$ are kept apart by the tags.

**(6) `thm-bing-q-set-moore-space-is-normal-and-nonmetrizable`, step 2.5,
steps 3.1 and 4.1, first Remark.** The old step 2.5 asserted that a relatively
$G_\delta$ subset $A \subseteq E$ is of the form $E \cap S$ with $S$ open (and
dually $E\setminus A = E\cap G$ with $G$ closed); the Q-set property as stated
in `def-q-sets-and-heath-moore-space-interface` supplies only
$G_\delta$ witnesses $A = E\cap\bigcap_n S_n$, and for e.g. $E = \mathbb{Q}$,
$A = \mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1)$ there is no open $S$ with $A = E\cap S$ and
$S \cap (E\setminus A) = \varnothing$. The old step also separated $A$ from $B$
with one radius $k(a)$ per point, which needs a common positive distance
$\operatorname{dist}(a,\mathbb{R}\setminus S)$ that need not exist. Repair:
step 2.5 now follows the cited source (Burke, Example 4.1) exactly in stages —
decreasing open sequences $(S_n)$, $(T_n)$ with $A = E\cap\bigcap S_n$,
$B = E\cap\bigcap T_n$, the sets $B_n = B\setminus S_n$, $A_n = A\setminus T_n$,
the criterion "tangent disks of radii $1/k$ and $1$ are disjoint iff the axis
distance is $\ge 2/\sqrt{k}$", and the combination
$V = \bigcup_n (V_n \setminus \bigcup_{k<n}\operatorname{cl}W_k)$,
$W = \bigcup_n(W_n \setminus \bigcup_{k<n}\operatorname{cl}V_k)$, which is
open, disjoint, contains $B$ and $A$. Steps 3.1 and 4.1 were adjusted to the
complementary form ($A$ closed in $E_0$, $B := E_0\setminus A \supseteq K\cap
E_0$), and the first Remark was corrected: its sentence "$\rho \ge 2/\sqrt{k}$
... because $2/\sqrt{k} \ge 1/k+1$ fails only for small $k$" is false for every
$k \ge 2$; the correct equivalence is
$\sqrt{\rho^2+(1-1/k)^2} \ge 1+1/k \iff \rho \ge 2/\sqrt{k}$.

**N3. `lem-ladder-separation-from-hyp` — unlicensed inference in step 2.3.**
