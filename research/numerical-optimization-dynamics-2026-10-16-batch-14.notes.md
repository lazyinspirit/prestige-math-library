## Mathematical conventions and caveats

All finite real arithmetic endpoints are exact rational expressions followed by directed dyadic rounding. Subnormal and overflow behavior is explicit, and an overflow sentinel never becomes a finite endpoint. Elementary functions use exact rational series, locally proved range reductions and explicit tails; no system `libm` accuracy claim is used. Real intervals and complex rectangles have inclusion-isotone formulas; ball operations return conservative origin-centered balls with monotone radii, and ball division returns a non-finite status when the coordinate-box lower bound cannot certify separation from zero. The finite width procedure shares one total box-split counter, covers each parent box by two closed halves, and returns `inconclusive` whenever a branch or the final hull misses the requested width.

The finite-format rounding lemma handles values below the subnormal quantum with an absolute bound, and overflow with infinite error. The square-root endpoint theorem now states the exact-width result before storage conversion and proves monotonicity of the computed endpoints across exponent-bin transitions. The complex logarithm definition states all quadrant and axis cases and excludes zero and the negative real branch cut. Taylor coefficients are supplied as interval enclosures, so non-dyadic derivatives are not silently treated as machine values.
