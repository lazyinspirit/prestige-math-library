## Correct side and direct-consumer impact

Novikov Theorem 8.1, printed p.26, says: “The closed leaf A₀ obtained in Theorem 7.1 is the only boundary leaf of the connected component of the foliation on M³ which lies to the right of A₀, and the limit set of any leaf A_t, for small t > 0, is precisely A₀.” Immediately before it he fixes j=1; j=2 reverses right/left. Thus `t>0` means the Π-side. Lemma 8.2, pp.26–27, likewise concerns only those displaced leaves.

A concrete opposite-side configuration is a flat-boundary Reeb solid torus glued to a product collar `T²×[0,ε]`, then capped at the other end by a second flat-boundary Reeb solid torus (choose the torus gluing to obtain a closed oriented 3-manifold, for instance a lens-space gluing). A Reeb model whose spinning coefficient vanishes to all orders at its boundary glues C∞ to the product foliation `T²×{s}`. Coorientations match by choosing the sign on each piece. Interior Reeb meridians give a transverse vanishing-cycle family supported on the first boundary torus. On its opposite side, arbitrarily close leaves `T²×{s}`, s>0, are compact and disjoint from it. Their ambient closure is themselves; they do not accumulate on the supporting torus. Whether “limit set” excludes the compact leaf itself or means its closure, that torus is absent.

Recommended precise clause: “On the side j approached by the vanishing cycle (or the nonzero Π^j₁ class), L₁ is the limit set of each sufficiently nearby leaf.” State the one-sided collar or fence used to mean “nearby.” This preserves the sourced theorem, rather than narrowing a valid two-sided theorem.

The statement needing correction is `lem-the-compact-leaf-produced-by-a-vanishing-cycle-bounds-a-reeb-component`. Its sole direct dependency consumer across the current F41 page manifests is `thm-novikov-reeb-component-theorem`; that theorem only uses existence of a Reeb component, so its stated conclusion needs no corresponding side qualifier, but its supplier use must be rechecked. ## Route B: embedded cycle and synchronized first return

Brittenham class 9 supplies a significantly shorter architectural alternative. It can prove the compact leaf and Reeb component together, without §1 component theory or Euler classification. The exact proof obligations are as follows.

**B0 — embedded input on L.** Obtain a C² embedded essential loop γ in L with an embedded compact transverse annulus γ_t, whose t>0 levels are embedded null loops. For a supplied simple loop the fence embedding follows by finite local charts and compactness after shrinking. For the current arbitrary Π input, a same-leaf embedded-cycle reduction remains required. Brittenham class11/Novikov Lemma7.2 reduce immersed loops but may change leaves. Their separate return-to-original-leaf argument cannot be omitted.

**B1 — embedded disk theorem and collar cleanup.** Prove that every embedded null loop in an oriented C² leaf bounds an embedded C² disk, with uniqueness unless the leaf is a sphere; dispose of the sphere case at C²/ACω. Then choose one cap D_ε whose intersection with the fence is only its boundary, possibly by the innermost-cap adjustment in class9 p.1. Prove finiteness/transversality of intersections before taking “innermost”; arbitrary cap–annulus intersections need not be a finite collection just from compactness.

One sufficient disk-descent argument is precise but conditional: in a planar universal cover, the lifted simple boundary encloses a Jordan **disk**, not just a separated region. Other lifts have disjoint boundaries. Their disks are disjoint or nested. If a deck map takes a disk into itself (use its inverse for the reverse inclusion), Brouwer gives a fixed point, contradicting the free deck action. Hence all nontrivial translates are disjoint and the projected disk is embedded. This proves descent once a planar-cover theorem and restricted C² Schoenflies disk theorem are supplied. The current finite-corner plane-separation item proves separation/winding, not a disk parametrization, disk-in-disk annulus theorem, or C² disk filling. No general Jordan–Schönflies citation is an ACω substitute.

**B2 — positive-parameter synchronized transport.** Construct `f:D_ε×(0,ε]→M`, each level a cap bounded by γ_t, and each point track an oriented transverse trajectory with leaf-synchronized parameter. Prove extension at every positive parameter using cap uniqueness, finite flow boxes, and exact boundary control. Failure to extend at zero must produce an infinite-flow-time trajectory: prove that if all times are finite, their endpoint map is continuous and leafwise and extends γ, contradicting essentiality. Ambient completeness of the transverse field alone proves none of these synchronization claims.

**B3 — recurrent crossing and simultaneous first return.** A complete infinite trajectory in compact M revisits a fixed flow box. Select actual crossings of its central plaque using the nonzero transverse field and the intermediate value theorem; chart visits alone are insufficient. Use a fixed compact reverse-flow segment from that plaque to D_ε to produce actual returns to D_ε. Show the first positive return parameter exists, and **all point tracks return at the same leaf parameter t₀**. Class9 p.2 explicitly leaves this last assertion as an exercise. Prove no earlier level collisions, and that D_ε lies strictly inside D_t₀; disjoint fence boundaries plus the disk nesting lemma give only part of this argument. A trap cannot be used to prove the injectivity needed to construct that same trap without an additional argument.

**B4 — embedded solid-torus core.** Factor the first-return sweep through the quotient of a 3-ball `D²×[t₀,ε]` which identifies its upper disk with a proper subdisk of its lower disk. Prove the factor map injective, including boundary/seam points. Prove locally that a disk minus the interior of a smaller embedded disk is an annulus, matching its two given boundary parametrizations. Then an explicit relative disk/annulus straightening makes the quotient a 3-ball with one 1-handle, hence a solid torus. This is a finite topology construction; the needed disk-in-disk annulus extension is an actual supplier duty, not the general 3-dimensional annulus theorem hidden by the phrase “solid torus.”

**B5 — outside annulus and limiting torus.** Let B=D_t₀ minus int(D_ε). Prove that outward-pointing transverse trajectories through B cannot re-enter the **already embedded** core, and hence have finite limiting flow times as t→0. Prove continuity of that endpoint map, identify its two boundary circles by exactly the prescribed return gluing, and prove no additional endpoint identifications. Uniqueness of finite-time ODE trajectories excludes collisions only after the orientation and finite-time hypotheses are established. The resulting embedded closed torus is locally open in the intrinsic leaf, and closed there by compactness; connectedness then makes it the entire original L. This directly identifies L without Euler characteristic. Obtain C² rather than merely continuous embedded boundary charts if the claimed diffeomorphism of L is retained.

**B6 — full shell and Reeb conjugacy.** Prove the limiting shell is the annulus mapping cylinder with the explicit shift/return gluing, injectively attached to the core and covering the whole compact region R. Match local C² collars and round the two core seams inside ambient charts to justify a solid-torus **diffeomorphism**, not just a homeomorphism. Show R is saturated, its interior leaves are disks followed by an infinite sequence of annuli, and each is a plane with end accumulating on L. Parametrize the annuli and give compatible leafwise maps to standard Reeb disks/annuli, proving uniform continuity and inverse continuity at L. A return-map conjugacy alone is not a foliated homeomorphism of the whole region.

All B stages use local C² charts, at least C¹ ODE uniqueness, finite compactness arguments, and countably many iterates. ACω is a plausible upper bound, but only after the missing surface/disk and synchronized-flow arguments have been proved with that bound. The source outline's smooth category is not an automatic finite-regularity adapter.

## Small complete algebraic pieces and their limits

If an increasing one-sided germ h has h^m=id, shrink so the finitely many iterates are defined. h(x)>x would give x<h(x)<…<h^m(x)=x, and h(x)<x gives the reversed contradiction. Thus h is the identity germ. This supplies root extraction in the holonomy kernel, but nullity of β_t^m still requires a separate leaf-group argument to infer nullity of β_t.

For a primitive torus class (p,q), gcd(p,q)=1, the parametrization `s↦(ps,qs) mod Z²` is embedded: equal values imply p(s−t),q(s−t) are integers, and Bézout implies s−t is an integer. This supplies a slope only **after** a torus parametrization and primitive reduction are proved.

For a strictly contracting return germ h, choose a fundamental interval [h(a),a], map it monotonically onto [1/2,1], and extend by `ψ(h^n(x))=2^(−n)ψ(x)`. Endpoints agree and h^n(a)→0 since any positive limiting value would be a fixed point. This gives a conjugacy near the boundary, on the union of forward fundamental intervals, with ψ(0)=0. h is locally invertible on its image; it need not be onto the entire initial closed interval. This does not construct the global return chart or fill the meridional disks.
