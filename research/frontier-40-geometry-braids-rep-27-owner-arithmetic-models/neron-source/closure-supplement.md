## Local foundations, with proofs

### Strict henselization and enough sections (F01)

Fix a separable closure ks of k. Use pointed local etale R-algebras with residue embeddings into ks; their fibre products and the chosen residue point make a directed system. Each such local algebra is flat over R; its maximal ideal is generated by pi, because its residue fibre is a separable field. Its local ring is regular of dimension one, hence a DVR. The transition maps are injective local maps and preserve pi. In the union Rsh, every nonzero element is pi to an integer power times a unit, as already true at a finite stage. Every nonzero ideal has a least such power, so is principal. Thus the union itself is a Noetherian DVR, without excellence. Filtered colimits of flat modules are flat, and this local flat extension is faithfully flat. Its residue field is ks. The etale-local universal property and henselian lifting follow directly: an etale neighbourhood and residue point involve finitely many coefficients and descend to a finite stage; adjoining that pointed neighbourhood to the directed system supplies its section in the limit. Consequently the union is strictly henselian.

For smooth V/Rsh and a special ks-point, choose differential coordinates at the point. Cutting out the coordinate differences gives an etale slice through it, with unchanged residue field (BLR 2.2/14, whose proof was read). The defining henselian property gives a section of that slice, and hence a section of V. Every nonempty smooth finite-type open over the separably closed field has a rational point by the exact published `lem-nonempty-smooth-scheme-finite-separable-point`; therefore these section specializations are dense. For the generic fibre of a smooth integral model, a dense generic open also contains a section: the complement closure is horizontal and cannot contain a special-fibre component, so a special section outside that closure works. Equivalently one can use the relative dimension upper semicontinuity supplier below. This also proves the DVR specialization of BLR 5.3/7 needed in finite translate enlargement.

### Rational-map domains and base change (F02)

Use relative fibre-dense opens, not arbitrary absolute dense opens, for the birational functor. On a smooth scheme over a DVR, a fibre-dense open meets every generic point of the regular total space and is schematically dense. This remains true after the flat base changes actually used. For arbitrary base changes in strict-law functor calculations, smoothness gives geometrically reduced fibres and flatness; the affine associated-prime calculation in BLR 2.5/1(b) proves universal schematic density. For a separated target, local representatives agree by the closed diagonal and schematic density and glue to the maximal domain.

The graph closure of a rational map is calculated on a finite affine cover by kernels of restriction maps. Flat tensor products preserve those kernels and finite equalizers; hence graph closure commutes with flat base change. The domain is exactly the open where graph projection is an isomorphism. If a faithfully flat pullback of that projection is an isomorphism, affine ring descent gives an isomorphism before pullback. Thus domains descend through faithfully flat source maps and commute with flat base changes, exactly BLR 2.5/5–6. The target-separated hypothesis is essential. For maps between Noetherian finite-type schemes, finite presentation spreads the stalk statement; the explicit spreading computation is given below. This is sufficient for the separated smooth graph and completion applications and the tensor-product bases in the completion cocycle.

### Regularity, divisors and scheme Hartogs (F03–F04)

The exact published smoothness criterion gives flatness and geometric regular fibres. `lem-ag-flat-local-regularity-ascent-descent` then gives regular local rings of every smooth DVR total space. `thm-nonaffine-regular-local-ring-is-ufd` applies to **every regular local ring**, including these total spaces; its subsequent field example does not restrict that first assertion. Thus their Weil divisors are locally Cartier and extend generic Cartier divisors by closing their prime supports. The rational section correspondence is precisely `thm-line-bundle-rational-section-cartier-divisor`.

Here is the required scheme Hartogs proof. Let A be a Noetherian normal domain and a/b a fraction outside A. The nonzero cyclic submodule generated by a modulo b in A/bA has an associated prime p, by `thm-existence-of-associated-primes` (AC implies its DC assumption). Then (A/bA)p has depth zero. Since b is regular, `lem-depth-quotient-by-regular-element` gives depth Ap equal to one. Normality implies S2 by `lem-normal-domain-implies-s-two`, so height p is at most one. It cannot be zero since b belongs to p. Moreover a/b remains outside Ap: localizing its nonzero cyclic module at its associated prime does not kill it. This proves A is the intersection of its height-one localizations. Apply it to coefficients in a local frame to extend a line-bundle section defined in codimension one. A nonzero section's zero set has pure codimension one because locally it is a nonzero principal element in a regular domain.

For a separated quasi-finite birational morphism Y to a normal integral X with Y reduced, apply `lem-scheme-zariski-main-factorization-quasi-finite` locally to get an open immersion into a finite X-scheme. Take the reduced closure of the generic Y image in that finite scheme. Its affine ring B is integral over A and embeds in Frac A; normality forces B=A. The open Y image lies in this closure because Y is reduced and its generic point is dense. Therefore Y to X is an open immersion. The published classical-variety-only finite-birational result is not used.

### Relative etale criterion and defect-zero criterion (F05–F06)

BLR 2.2/8–10 was read in full. Its criterion has a direct standard-chart proof: for a map from smooth V to affine n-space, embed the graph into a relative affine space and append equations Ti-fi to a standard smooth presentation of V. Independence of dfi together with the already independent relation differentials gives a unit Jacobian minor. For a general smooth target W, take etale coordinates on W and extend their pulled-back differentials to coordinates on V. The resulting coordinate map is etale by the graph calculation; etale formal lifting (published `thm-etale-formally-etale-finite-presentation`) or the same standard presentations proves the map into W times affine space is etale. Thus a map between smooth schemes of equal relative dimension is etale exactly when its differential determinant is a unit.

For a section a of X with smooth generic fibre of dimension d, delta zero means a*Omega is free of rank d, hence the differential vector space at its rational specialization has dimension d. The exact published `lem-local-fibre-dimension-bound-via-polynomial-quasifiniteness` makes local fibre dimension upper semicontinuous. Since the generic section specializes to the special section, special local fibre dimension is at least d. Tangent dimension bounds local fibre dimension above, so equality holds. In a polynomial ambient n-space choose n-d actual ideal equations with independent differentials. They define a standard smooth ambient Z of relative dimension d. Locally on the special fibre X lies in Z with the same dimension at the rational point; the regular local domain of Z admits no proper quotient of the same dimension, so X and Z coincide there. The same argument at the generic point of the section gives generic coincidence. As Z is regular near the special section it has one local component, and that generic equality makes the remaining ideal zero; an ideal vanishing after pi inversion in a pi-torsion-free ring is zero. Hence X is smooth along a. In the actual construction X is flat (generic schematic closure, then blowups), which also rules out vertical residual equations immediately. Conversely smoothness makes a*Omega free, so delta vanishes. This supplies the precise criterion used by the defect induction, without applying the absolute rational-point submersion theorem to a DVR base.

### Dense geometrically reduced centres and free loci (F07)

For a centre Y with schematically dense ks-points, each affine coordinate ring B injects into a product of copies of ks via evaluation. This remains injective after tensoring with an arbitrary field extension l/k: every relation uses finitely many coefficients, and on their finite-dimensional k-span tensoring commutes with the coordinate evaluations. Since ks is separable algebraic over k, each ks tensor l is reduced; therefore B tensor l is reduced. This proves geometric reduction rather than merely reduction over ks.

To prove dense smoothness, extend to a perfect closure of k. The scheme remains reduced. At each generic point its function field is separably generated over that perfect field (`thm-ag-separating-transcendence-basis-perfect-field`); `thm-ag-field-differentials-separable-rank` gives differential rank equal to transcendence degree. The relative Jacobian criterion and actual defining ideal produce a smooth neighbourhood of that generic point, as in the ambient comparison above. Smoothness descends through field extension by the exact `lem-ag-geometric-regularity-field-tests`, flatness over a field and the smoothness criterion. Thus the original smooth locus is dense. A coherent sheaf is free on a dense open of each reduced component: choose a basis over its fraction field, clear denominators in finitely many generators and relations, then remove the other components and intersect the finitely many resulting opens. Alternatively `lem-generic-freeness-finite-type-algebra-module` supplies this exact step with B=A and M the coherent module.

### Blowups and finite algebra spreading (F08–F09)

Published `thm-affine-blowup-standard-charts` and `lem-affine-blowup-algebra-properties` give the actual chart algebra A[I/pi] as the quotient of A[tj]/(pi tj-gj) by pi-power torsion. Thus the dilatation chart is flat over the DVR. The Rees-Proj construction is proper by `thm-blowup-projective`; centres contain pi so the generic fibre is unchanged. For a flat source B, the special-factorization condition makes each gj image divisible by pi, uniquely. Sending tj to gj/pi kills all torsion and proves the universal property. Flat base change preserves the relevant torsion-free chart description by tensoring the finite kernel/equalizer or by this universal property; products and closed immersions follow by the same universal property and affine coordinate equations. Unique section lifting under the proper blowup follows from the exact `thm-valuative-criterion-properness`.

The essential finite-stage stalk argument is explicit. Suppose A and B are finite-type R-domains in a common field L, A included in B, and Ap=Bq via the map at p,q. Write B=A[b1,...,bm]. Each bj is aj/sj in Ap. Taking s the product of sj, outside p, gives Bs=As as subrings of L. Consequently Spec Bs to Spec As is an isomorphism on neighbourhoods of q,p. This applies to generic graph projections, since graph closures are integral and have the same generic fraction field. For finite-presentation schemes over a filtered colimit ring, choose finite affine covers and finite covers of their overlaps, record the finitely many equations, generators, gluing maps and inverse identities. All these coefficients occur at one finite stage; finitely many equality identities hold at a later common stage. This proves the object/morphism spreading used for etale neighbourhood limits. Generic closure over a DVR is pi-torsion-free by its kernel definition, hence flat.

### Affine neighbourhood of finitely many codimension-one points (F10)

This is a complete proof of the valuation step in BLR 6.4/4, not a citation to an approximation theorem. Reduce to a connected normal component and let Vi=O_X,xi be distinct DVRs in its rational function field L. Distinctness follows from separatedness: two centres of the same valuation would violate valuative uniqueness. Two distinct rank-one DVRs in L are incomparable. Indeed if V is included in W and its uniformizer is a unit in W, then L=V[1/pi] is included in W, so W is a field; otherwise every x outside V has inverse in pi V and cannot belong to W. Thus inclusion would force equality.

For each pair i,j choose y in Vi outside Vj. Then vi(y)>=0 and vj(y)<0; y to a sufficiently large power times a uniformizer of Vi has vi>0 and vj<0. For fixed i construct xi with vi(xi)>0 and vj(xi)<0 for every j different from i. Inductively add a large power of a pairwise element for a newly included index: at a previous index its valuation is either nonnegative (so the previous negative valuation remains) or negative (choose the exponent so it strictly dominates); at the new index the new term strictly dominates; at i both terms have positive valuation. Strict domination prevents cancellation. Put ei=1/(1+xi to the N). Then vi(ei-1) can be arbitrarily large and vj(ei) can be arbitrarily large for all j different from i. Given prescribed finite targets ci in L, the sum of ei ci approximates ci at every vi to any prescribed finite accuracy, by choosing N sufficiently large. This proves the finite approximation needed here.

Let C be the intersection of Vi. Its maximal ideals are mi={c:vi(c)>0}: an element is invertible in C exactly when all valuations vanish, so all nonunits form the union of mi; finite prime avoidance makes every maximal ideal one of these. The approximation just proved separates them and realizes arbitrary residues. Also C localized at mi is Vi. For x in Vi, approximate 1 at i and 0 to sufficiently high order at the other indices by a t in C; then t is outside mi and tx belongs to C. Thus x=(tx)/t in Cmi. The reverse inclusion is immediate.

Here are all finite equations behind the common quasi-affine neighbourhood. Choose Wi=Spec Bi around xi with Bi finite type over R. Every generator bil of Bi belongs to Vi=Cmi, so write bil=ail/sil with ail,sil in C and sil outside mi. Choose a finite-type R-subalgebra C0 of C containing every numerator and denominator for every i. Its finitely many generators are regular near each xi; shrink Wi to a principal open Wi,ti containing xi on which all these generators are regular. Since ti is in Bi, it already belongs to (C0)si for si the product of sil; write ti=ui/si to some power with ui in C0 outside mi. Then Bi[1/ti] is included in (C0)siui, while C0 is included in Bi[1/ti]. Localizing both sides by siui gives equality of the coordinate rings. Hence a neighbourhood of xi in Wi,ti is isomorphic to D(siui) in Spec C0. The rational map from X to Spec C0 is defined near all xi and is an open immersion on this union. Its inverse maps on the D(siui) agree: they agree on the common generic point and X is separated, so schematic dense agreement gives equality on their overlaps. Thus this union is a quasi-affine open W of X containing all xi. Embed W as an open in Spec C0. The complement ideal is contained in none of the finitely many primes corresponding to xi; finite prime avoidance gives f in that ideal outside all of them. D(f) is an affine open contained in W and contains all xi. Include generic points at the beginning or simply work componentwise; this gives the required R-dense affine open.

### Ampleness, quasi-affine loci and effective pair descent (F11–F12)

For a qcqs scheme and a section s of a line bundle L, sections over Xs are localized sections: Gamma(Xs,F) is the colimit of Gamma(X,F tensor L to the n) under multiplication by s. Prove this on a finite trivializing affine cover: fractions represent sections locally, finitely many denominators clear, and finitely many overlap equalities clear after raising the power once more. This is an actual finite equalizer argument. In particular sections defining principal opens in a quasi-affine Xs extend after multiplying by a power of s. Multiplying once more by s makes their nonvanishing loci stay inside Xs. Choose principal affine opens of its affine ambient scheme containing each point. Their pulled-back functions give sections of powers of L with affine nonvanishing loci. Thus a finite cover by quasi-affine section loci implies the repository's affine-locus ampleness definition.

Conversely, if L is ample, every positive-power section s has quasi-affine nonvanishing locus. Choose a finite affine section-locus cover Xtj of X and raise powers so the degrees of tj and s agree. On Xs the functions tj/s define affine principal opens: their nonvanishing loci are Xtj intersect Xs, principal opens in affine Xtj. These cover Xs. The canonical map Xs to Spec Gamma(Xs,O) is an open immersion because localization of global sections identifies each such principal open with the corresponding distinguished open in that spectrum. This proves quasi-affineness.

For a fixed line bundle on a Noetherian separated finite-type R-scheme X and any faithfully flat DVR extension R to B, finite affine-cover equalizers give Gamma(X,F) tensor B=Gamma(XB,FB) for every quasi-coherent F, using flatness. If LB is ample, apply published `thm-serre-criterion-ampleness` to each coherent FB. The evaluation map for F tensor L to the n becomes surjective for all sufficiently large n after tensoring B. Its cokernel therefore vanishes by `thm-faithfully-flat-descent-vanishing`. Serre's criterion on X gives ampleness of L. This is the full DVR extension argument; the field-only published ampleness lemma is not silently widened.

For effective descent of an ample pair (Xprime,Lprime) from Rsh to R, form the graded global section algebra. Flat base change of its graded pieces and the pair cocycle give algebra descent; `lem-nonaffine-effective-affine-algebra-descent` supplies the actual invariant algebra A and identifies Rsh tensor A with the section algebra. Each section upstairs is a finite sum of scalar multiples of descended sections of the same degree. If that section generates Lprime at a point, at least one descended section does too. Every such nonvanishing open is quasi-affine by the preceding argument and stable under the datum. Quasi-affine descent is direct: embed that open in Spec of its global sections, descend the algebra, and take its image under the faithfully flat spectrum map. Stability makes the inverse image of that image exactly the original open; `lem-fpqc-cover-submersive` makes the image open. These descended opens glue because compatible morphisms descend by the same affine algebra equalizer. The invertible sheaf descends on the resulting scheme by module descent on each affine chart; rank-one freeness is checked faithfully flat, or via dual evaluation and finite-generator descent. This establishes effective ample-pair descent, including actual scheme representability.

For the descended group G, exact `lem-fpqc-descent-properness-components` supplies finite type and separatedness. Since R is Noetherian, finite type gives local finite presentation. Flatness descends by `thm-faithfully-flat-descent-of-flatness`. For each residue field, geometric regularity descends by `lem-ag-geometric-regularity-field-tests`. Thus the smoothness criterion supplies smooth G. Multiplication, inverse and unit descend as compatible morphisms; their identities hold after the faithfully flat base change, hence hold below. This proves every geometric-property use needed here.

### Strictification density (F13)

In BLR 2.5/1(a), take the closed complement A of a quasi-compact open V in a smooth Y/S. Locally fix relative dimension d. The locus F in A where the A/S fibre has local dimension at least d is closed by exact `lem-local-fibre-dimension-bound-via-polynomial-quasifiniteness`; all these dimensions are at most d. A closed subset of an equidimensional smooth fibre has dimension d precisely when it contains a component, so V is not dense in that fibre exactly on the image of F. That image is constructible by `lem-finite-presentation-image-constructible`, covering F and the target by finitely many affine charts. This proves the density-locus assertion, without an unexamined EGA semicontinuity input.

Apply this with S=X and Y=X times_R X. The bad loci for each projection are constructible. They omit every generic point of both R-fibres because the original domain/image opens are R-dense. Over a DVR a constructible subset whose closure contained a fibre generic point would itself contain that generic point (a dense constructible set contains an open in its closure; use generization at the generic point). Therefore remove their closures and obtain an R-dense open good in both projections. The remaining strictification argument is exactly the read BLR 5.2/2 universal-translation argument.

### Affine complements and the identity component (F14–F15)

If a dense affine open U of a normal separated Noetherian X had a minimal boundary component of codimension at least two, shrink at its generic point away from all other boundary components. Every function on U extends across that neighbourhood by scheme Hartogs. These extensions give the rational identity map X to the affine U as an actual map near that point. Its composite with U to X agrees with the identity on the dense intersection, hence everywhere by separatedness. The point would belong to U, contradiction. Thus the boundary has pure codimension one. In the regular case its prime support gives an effective Cartier divisor. If U is fibre-dense over a DVR, none of those prime divisors is a special-fibre component; its complement is therefore the closure of its generic complement.

For smooth G with abelian generic fibre over a DVR, define G0 as G_K together with the identity connected component of G_k. The other finitely many special connected components are closed in G, so this is open. Products and inverse preserve it: on the generic fibre this is automatic, and on the special fibre it is the ordinary identity component subgroup. Checking preimages of the open gives actual factorization, not merely pointwise identities. The connected smooth field-group identity component is geometrically integral by exact `lem-nonaffine-connected-group-geometrically-connected`. Hence G0 has geometrically connected and irreducible fibres. On each geometric fibre, its orbits on G are the connected components, because translation identifies each with the identity component. This proves the only component theorem needed for the square/divisor ampleness argument without a general component scheme theorem.

## Two construction details that must remain explicit

In the square reduction, the residual obstruction line bundle on G0 times G0 times G has a vertical divisor. Projection to G has geometrically integral fibres on the special fibre, so each vertical prime divisor upstairs is the inverse image of a special component of G. Thus it is pulled back from G. Restrict to (unit,unit,idG): the square obstruction becomes trivial there and the extended generic base line bundle pulls back from R. This identifies the residual G line bundle with one from R, which can be absorbed in the G0 times G0 base line bundle. The generic cube identity is the exact published cube theorem. No Picard scheme, dual abelian variety, Chevalley decomposition or Raynaud theorem is used.

In Weil extension, near a diagonal point choose an affine neighbourhood of the group unit. Scheme Hartogs says its affine rational-map pole set is pure codimension one. The source Z times Z is regular. Locally a pole divisor has one equation h; restricting to the smooth diagonal gives one equation in its regular local ring. Its zero locus is empty or has pure codimension one (and cannot be the whole diagonal because the rational map is the unit on its dense defined diagonal). This contradicts the diagonal bad locus having codimension at least two. Hence the difference map is defined near the diagonal. Intersect its domain with Z times dom(u); the first projection is smooth surjective because a fibre-dense open meets each nonempty open in a fibre. Recover u upstairs by the difference map times u on the second coordinate, and use rational-domain descent with separated target. This closes the diagonal-intersection step without an unexamined intersection-dimension theorem.

## Universal density and strictification refinements

For the universal schematic-density step, the associated-prime calculation can be proved locally, rather than treating BLR's EGA reference as a supplier. Over a Noetherian base ring C, take a finite prime filtration of C (`thm-prime-filtration-of-a-finite-module`) and tensor with the flat smooth algebra B. The resulting filtration of B has factors B/pB. Each factor is flat over the domain C/p, so injects into its generic fibre. That generic fibre is geometrically reduced, hence reduced; consequently B/pB is reduced. A reduced Noetherian ring has only minimal associated primes: it injects into the finite product of its minimal-prime domain quotients, and the annihilator of any element is the intersection of the minimal primes where its image is nonzero; a prime such intersection equals one of them. The associated primes of B are therefore contained in the union of the minimal primes of these factors. Each such factor-minimal prime lies over p: flatness prevents a nonzero C/p element from being a zero divisor. A fibre-dense open meets all of these generic fibre-component points. The kernel of restriction to that open, if nonzero, has an associated prime by `thm-existence-of-associated-primes`; as a submodule of B this prime is one of the just listed primes, where restriction is injective, contradiction. Thus restriction is injective. After an arbitrary affine base change, write its base ring as the filtered colimit of finite-type algebras over the original DVR. Each stage is Noetherian, and the open remains fibre-dense by geometric density over field extension. Restriction injectivity passes to the colimit by exactness of filtered colimits and the finite affine-cover equalizer. This supplies universal schematic density for the arbitrary T-valued strict-law calculations.

For the closure of the bad locus in strictification, use the smooth DVR total space explicitly. If a constructible bad locus omitted all generic points of the generic and special R-fibres but its closure contained a generic special point eta, an irreducible component of that closure containing eta has generic point q specializing to eta. The local ring at eta is a DVR, so the only possible q are eta and the total-space generic point. A constructible set dense in this irreducible closure component contains its generic point (published `lem-constructible-dense-contains-open`). Both possibilities contradict the stated omissions. Thus removal of the closure preserves fibre density. This is the exact dimension-one-base argument; it does not assert the analogous claim over an arbitrary base.

For the divisor-ampleness covering argument, the field-extension descent is also explicit. Given a closed point x in a fibre of G, put l=k(x), a finite extension of the fibre field k. On the geometrically integral (G0)l, the inverse images of the affine fibre-dense U under g to g x and g to g inverse x are nonempty opens, so their intersection W is dense open. The finite projection (G0)l to G0 takes its closed complement to a proper closed subset: the source is integral with the same-dimensional generic point, and that generic point is not in the complement. Remove this image to obtain a dense open Z over k; every g in Z(k) has both g x and g inverse x in U. The section-density argument above supplies such a section g of G0 over the strictly henselian DVR in either fibre. Therefore the section loci gU intersect g inverse U cover all closed points of each fibre. Every fibre point specializes to a closed fibre point and opens contain its generalizations, so they cover all of G. This supplies the “methods of descent” sentence in BLR 6.4/2 with a finite projection argument, including inseparable residue extensions.

## Parent review: finite-type ample schemes embed without properness

For quasi-projectivity of a finite-type separated R-scheme X with an ample line bundle L, choose finitely many affine section opens X_si covering X and raise the sections to a common positive degree. Each Γ(X_si,O) is a finite-type R-algebra; choose finitely many R-algebra generators f_ij. The section-extension lemma extends each f_ij si^n to a global section of L to a sufficiently large common power. After multiplication by further si powers, their ratios to si^n are exactly f_ij on X_si. Include each si^n in the finite global section list; they have no common zero and define a map X→P_R^M. On the projective chart for si^n its preimage is X_si, and the coordinate-ring map is surjective because its ratios include all f_ij. Thus the map is a closed immersion into the union of these projective charts, by closed-immersion locality on the target. That union is open in projective space, so the map to projective space is a locally closed immersion. This proof uses no properness of X.

This replaces the integrating manifest's inapplicable use of `thm-ample-powers-very-ample-proper-base` for a potentially nonproper group model. Exact suppliers: `lem-extend-sections-from-nonvanishing-open`, `thm-line-bundle-sections-define-projective-map`, `lem-closed-immersion-local-on-target`, `def-locally-closed-immersion` and `lem-ample-stable-positive-power`.
