## Construction, with arbitrary-test universal property

Work first over an algebraically closed field. For proper geometrically integral A, universal constants satisfy p_*O_(A×T)=O_T for every T. On affine tests, compute by a finite affine Čech cover and tensor the field cohomology complex; H0 is k. Normalize L by tensoring with p_T^*(e_T^*L)^−1. A rigidified line bundle has no nontrivial automorphism, since each automorphism is a unit from T and restriction along e determines it. Thus isomorphisms on an fppf overlap are unique and satisfy the cocycle; effective module descent gives a line bundle with rigidification. Consequently the rigidified functor is already an fppf sheaf, without invoking representability.

Fix a very ample H on A. The relative effective-divisor functor is an open of the published Hilbert scheme: on a flat finitely presented family, being cut out by a fibrewise regular element with invertible ideal is open, by the local flatness criterion plus Nakayama and proper projection of the bad locus. For a fixed Hilbert polynomial, this divisor scheme D is finite type and quasi-projective. Use positive charts with a specified Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity bound: impose the finitely many higher-cohomology vanishings in that regularity definition, using the universal finite cohomology complex. The published relative-regularity/base-change supplier propagates these finitely many vanishings to all required nonnegative twists. After twisting by a sufficiently high H power every individual test family is locally on its base in such a chart, by Serre vanishing. The chosen chart is an open D^+, with fixed polynomial and fixed finite regularity conditions; do not define openness by an unproved infinite intersection of vanishings. A line bundle satisfying these conditions has finite locally free sections, compatible with every base change, with positive rank.

The map D^+ to the line-bundle sheaf is relatively represented by projective spaces: for an arbitrary test family L, divisors with class L are fibrewise nonzero sections up to units from the test base. Integral fibres make every nonzero fibre section regular. Hence the fibre is P((p_*L)^∨), scheme theoretically, and is smooth projective surjective. The linear-equivalence relation R on D^+ is therefore a represented smooth projective relation, of finite type, with both projections flat. Its map to D^+×D^+ is a monomorphism. The source is separated and finite type, so the published generic quotient theorem applies exactly, yielding a nonempty saturated open W and Y=W/R_W.

The image V of W in the Picard sheaf is an **open subfunctor**, not just a subset of geometric classes. For any T→Pic, first restrict to the open T+ where its line bundle has the fixed polynomial and finite positive-regularity conditions of the chosen chart. Polynomial local constancy and the finite-complex vanishing conditions make T+ open; finite-presentation descent treats arbitrary tests. Over T+ the divisor map is the faithfully flat open projective-space bundle of sections. Saturation makes the preimage of W invariant under its kernel pair, so that open descends to an open subset of T+, and therefore of T. On that descended open the pullback is represented by the quotient Y×_Pic T. The fppf sheaf quotient equality identifies V with Y. This proves an open immersion of represented functors V→Pic.

Translate V by every rigidified class over the algebraically closed base field k. These translates contain every k-valued class: choose v∈V(k), which exists since V is nonempty and locally of finite type, and for x∈Pic(k) use translation by x−v. To prove coverage on arbitrary tests, pull their union back to each finite-type positive divisor chart D for every polynomial and every sufficiently high twist. This pullback is open and contains every closed point of D, since closed points have residue field k. Its closed complement is therefore empty by the Nullstellensatz. The positive divisor charts, with twists reversed, cover the Picard sheaf fppf-locally on every test: locally a sufficiently positive twist has locally free nonzero sections and a fibrewise nonzero section exists after the projective-space cover. Hence every test pulls back to the union of the translates. Their represented open overlaps and identity transition maps glue to a scheme representing the full functor on all tests, including nonreduced and non-Noetherian tests. This chart argument does not assume that every geometric class descends to k.

Separatedness can be checked on these locally finite-type charts. If a rigidified line bundle on A_R, for a DVR R over the field, is trivial generically, its Cartier divisor is supported on the irreducible special fibre of the smooth regular A_R. On a regular scheme Cartier and Weil divisors agree, and this divisor is an integer multiple of the special fibre, the principal divisor of the uniformizer. Thus its class is trivial modulo a base line bundle. For diagonal representability and quasi-compactness, on the Noetherian product T of two finite-type divisor charts put M=L1^−1⊗L2 with the prescribed rigidification. A universal finite free cohomology complex has H0(M_T′)=ker(d0:K0⊗OT′→K1⊗OT′) on every test. Evaluation at the unit is a linear functional on this kernel; choosing a free representative of the degree-zero evaluation chain map gives its linear extension to K0. Sections with d0(s)=0 and e(s)=1 are therefore represented by a closed subscheme of the finite-rank affine vector bundle K0, defined by finitely many linear equations. Restrict to the open where the section is invertible everywhere on A: the zero locus of the universal section is closed, and its proper image in the parameter scheme is closed. This open is Noetherian, finite type, and quasi-compact. Such sections are exactly rigidified isomorphisms L1→L2, uniquely if they exist. Thus the diagonal is representable and quasi-compact on these covering chart products. The Noetherian DVR valuative uniqueness criterion now gives separatedness. Chain evaluation can be constructed by an affine Čech model and transferred to the finite projective complex; this should be written explicitly in authoring.

## Dual, dimension, and descent

For coherent cohomology over a field, the finite affine Čech complex on A×A is the tensor product of the two complexes; field Künneth gives the cohomology tensor product, with the Koszul signs. Thus H^*(A,O_A) is a connected graded Hopf algebra via addition. Every element of H1 is primitive. If v1,…,vr are independent in H1, apply the r-fold coproduct to v1⋯vr and project to (H1)^⊗r. The result is the signed sum over permutations of the distinct tensors v_(σ1)⊗⋯⊗v_(σr). These tensors are independent; each coefficient is ±1, so the result is nonzero even in characteristic 2. Therefore H^r≠0 and r≤g. **No Borel structure theorem is needed for this bound.**

The exact sequence 1→1+εO_A→O^*_(A[ε])→O_A^*→1 identifies the rigidified Picard tangent space with H1(A,O_A). It follows that tangent dimension≤g. For ample L, the already available cube/square and ample-kernel argument defines φ_L into Pic with finite scheme kernel, hence image dimension g. Since Pic is separated, the morphism from proper A is proper. Its image is closed, connected, and has dimension g. At the identity, local dimension g and embedding dimension≤g force a regular local ring; translation makes the whole identity neighbourhood smooth. The proper image contains an open neighbourhood of the identity, and translations give open image everywhere. It is open and closed and therefore equals Pic0. Hence B=Pic0 is a smooth proper finite-type connected group of dimension g, and φ_L:A→B is an isogeny. This avoids assuming projective Picard components or separately invoking a compactness theorem for the identity component.

The universal rigidified bundle exists by evaluation at the identity map of the representative; restrict to A×B and normalize on both axes. B is projective by the published field-level abelian projectivity theorem.

For arbitrary k, construct over kbar and spread the **finite data** B, group operations, and the rigidified Poincaré bundle to some finite K/k. The induced natural transformation Hom(−,B_K)→the fibrewise degree-zero rigidified functor becomes an isomorphism after base change to kbar. Both functors are fppf sheaves, so it is an isomorphism already over K on every test scheme. This is a standard sheaf-local argument: existence descends after local lift, and uniqueness descends after comparing overlaps.

Thus B_K obtains a canonical descent datum over K⊗_kK from uniqueness of representatives of the **same** base-changed functor; this includes its entire nonreduced tensor algebra, and satisfies the cocycle by uniqueness. B_K is a smooth proper connected group, projective over K; as an underlying k-scheme it is projective too, since Spec K→Spec k is finite/projective. Every finite descent orbit lies in an affine open of this projective scheme: choose a closed specialization of each of its finitely many points; for a high power of a very ample line, Serre vanishing makes the map to the fibres at those finitely many closed points surjective; take a section nonzero at each, whose nonvanishing locus is affine. Nonvanishing at a specialization implies nonvanishing at the original point. The exact published finite-field descent lemma now descends B_K, including inseparable K. Module descent gives P; compatible morphism descent gives the group law and rigidifications. The sheaf isomorphism descends and proves the full desired universal property over k.

## Finite Cartier foundation, with exactness and annihilator

For a finite commutative group H over a field, its finite locally free Hopf algebra C has dual C*=Hom(C,k). Dualize multiplication and comultiplication and swap them; the unit/counit and antipode also dualize. Each Hopf identity dualizes to the corresponding identity of C*, so Spec C* is a finite commutative group of equal rank, and evaluation C→C** is a Hopf isomorphism. For every test algebra R, maps C*⊗R→R correspond to elements b of C⊗R; multiplicativity and unit mean Δb=b⊗b and εb=1. The antipode makes b invertible, so these are exactly characters H_R→Gm,R. This is the complete all-test construction in EGM3.22, including its exercise of checking the dual identities explicitly.

Exactness can be obtained categorically without importing a Hopf freeness theorem. Finite commutative group schemes form a full additive category of fppf abelian sheaves. Kernels are finite. The **published normal-subgroup quotient** gives H/ker f as a separated finite-type group scheme; it has dimension zero because H→H/ker f is fppf onto, so it is finite over k. Its induced monomorphism into the target is finite (a morphism between finite k-schemes is finite), hence a closed immersion. The quotient of the target by this finite image is finite by the same argument. Thus the finite category has the same kernel/cokernel factorization as fppf sheaves and is abelian. Cartier duality is an additive contravariant equivalence, so it exchanges kernels/cokernels and preserves short exact sequences. This proves the exactness needed in the theta-extension argument.

A rank-d finite commutative H is killed by d, using the same character description rather than a separate Lagrange theorem. On an arbitrary affine test R, let h∈H(R) and χ:H_R→Gm,R. In the rank-d free algebra after localization, multiplication by χ is invertible. Translation by h conjugates this multiplication operator, so its determinant (norm) is unchanged. But t_h*χ=χ(h)χ, giving χ(h)^d Norm(χ)=Norm(χ), hence χ(h)^d=1. This holds functorially for the universal character parametrized by H^D; evaluation biduality therefore says [d]h=0. The argument glues on arbitrary tests.

## Homogeneous bundles, dual isogenies, biduality

For a bundle M with φ_M=0, the square/cube plus rigidification gives m^*M=p1^*M⊗p2^*M. If H0(M)≠0, also H0(M^−1)≠0 by inversion; their product is a nonzero constant, forcing M trivial. If i is the least index of nonzero cohomology of a nontrivial M, pullback by m followed by restriction along (id,0) is the identity on H^i(M). Künneth says the intermediate cohomology is ⊕_(a+b=i)H^a(M)⊗H^b(M), zero by minimality and H0=0. This contradiction proves H^i(M)=0 for all i.

For fixed ample L, if no x satisfies M=t_x^*L⊗L^−1, set Q=Λ(L)⊗p2^*M^−1. Horizontal fibres are nontrivial homogeneous bundles, so Rp1_*Q=0 by the universal cohomology complex. Vertical fibres are homogeneous and vanish except over finite K(L); thus every R^ip2_*Q has finite support. Leray gives H^*(Q)=0 via p1 and then R^ip2_*Q=0 via p2, contradicting the trivial vertical fibre at zero and derived base change. Therefore φ_L exhausts the homogeneous geometric classes. Since the Picard scheme is smooth and φ is constant on connected families by rigidity, ker φ=Pic0 as a sheaf, not only on points. Write this family argument against the universal bundle and then pull back to arbitrary tests.

For an isogeny f:A→C with kernel H, f is the fppf quotient. A rigidified bundle M on C_T with f^*M trivial descends the trivial bundle on A_T with H_T-action. Universal constants force any such action to be a character H_T→Gm,T. Trivializations differ only by a base unit, so the character is independent of choice; conversely character descent produces M. Tensor and base change commute with the construction. All these bundles are in Pic0: the finite-flat norm makes M^(deg f) trivial, and a torsion class has φ_M=0 because [deg f] is an epimorphism. Hence ker f^∨=H^D **as group schemes**, with no reduced-kernel restriction. Finite rank equality gives deg f^∨=deg f; dimension and finite kernel give an isogeny.

Switching the normalized Poincaré bundle defines κ_A:A→B^∨. Symmetry of Λ(L) gives φ_L=φ_L^∨κ_A on every test. Its finite kernel makes κ_A an isogeny. Degree equality for φ_L and its dual then gives deg κ_A=1, so κ_A is an isomorphism. Pullback of normalized bundles gives contravariance and composition; the cube identity gives addition compatibility.

## Symmetric maps and polarizations, including characteristic 2

For λ:A→B, set M=(id,λ)^*P. Biextension identities give φ_M=λ+λ^∨. If λ is symmetric, N=M² has φ_N=4λ. Thus A[4]⊂K(N). The theta commutator e_N on A[4] has values in μ4, and e_N(2y,2z)=e_N(y,z)^4=1. Since [2]:A[4]→A[2] is fppf onto, A[2] is isotropic scheme theoretically even in characteristic 2.

Here is a local replacement for EGM's deferred Ext(H,Gm)=0 fact, once finite Cartier exactness is proved. Let 0→Gm→E→H→0 be a commutative extension over an algebraically closed field and n annihilate H. Then [n]:E→Gm restricts to the nth-power map on Gm, and F=E[n]→H is a finite μn-torsor. Cartier exactness makes F^D→(μn)^D=Z/n fppf onto. A nonempty finite scheme over an algebraically closed field has a rational point, so choose χ:F→Gm restricting to the tautological μn character. The map Gm×F→E, (t,h)↦th, is a μn-torsor; (t,h)↦tχ(h) is invariant under its kernel and descends to a retraction E→Gm. Its kernel maps isomorphically to H, so the extension splits. Applying this to the theta extension over an isotropic subgroup supplies a linearization, and ordinary flat descent gives N=[2]^*L. Then 4λ=4φ_L and cancellation through the fppf epimorphism [4] gives λ=φ_L.

To obtain finite **separable** realization, represent the locus of bundles with φ_L=λ inside Pic. Equality of these two morphisms A×Pic→B is a closed condition represented by the universal vanishing locus of the pulled-back diagonal ideal (proper flat A permits the Hilbert vanishing-locus supplier). This locus is a torsor under B; after an algebraically closed extension, one realization identifies it with B. It is therefore smooth over k, nonempty, and locally finite type; a nonempty smooth scheme over a field has a point over a finite separable extension. The universal bundle at that point realizes λ there. This avoids needing a separate represented Hom-scheme theorem.

For ample L, φ_L is symmetric by Λ(L), and (id,φ_L)^*P=L⊗[-1]^*L, which is ample. Every A has such L by field-level projectivity. If φ_M=0 and H is ample, surjectivity of φ_H makes M=t_x^*H⊗H^−1 over an algebraically closed extension; hence H⊗M=t_x^*H is ample. Thus ampleness is invariant under Pic0 twists. For symmetric λ=φ_L, the bundle (id,λ)^*P has φ equal to 2λ, so it differs from L² by a Pic0 bundle; it is ample exactly when L is. This proves the definition's polarization criterion.

## Square degree without HRR or a tame-kernel assumption

For P on A×B, nontrivial homogeneous fibre bundles have no cohomology. Thus all R^ip2_*P are supported at zero. At R=O_(B,0), take a finite free universal cohomology complex K in degrees 0,…,g: fibre cohomology vanishes outside that interval, so split off the acyclic tails using invertible differential minors and Nakayama. All its cohomology modules have finite length because their support is the closed point. The shifted dual C=Hom_R(K,R)[−g] (so C^i=Hom_R(K^{g−i},R), with the usual dual signs) is also concentrated in 0,…,g and has finite-length cohomology: away from the closed point K is a split exact complex of finite free modules, and so is C. Apply the following local acyclicity argument to K and to C, obtaining vanishing below degree g for both. This supplies the concentration argument directly, without an unsupported global Leray/duality inference. For completeness, a bounded nonnegative free complex C over a g-dimensional regular local ring R, all of whose cohomology has finite length, has H^i(C)=0 for i<g. Choose a regular parameter system x1,…,xg. Let E be the augmented localization Čech complex R→⊕R[xi^−1]→⋯→R[(x1⋯xg)^−1] in degrees 0,…,g. The complex E is the filtered direct limit of cohomologically oriented Koszul complexes on x1^n,…,xg^n; each power sequence is regular, so E has no cohomology below g. This uses exactness of filtered colimits of modules and the ordinary Koszul regular-sequence theorem. Apply the two spectral sequences of the bounded double complex C⊗E. Computing E first yields no total cohomology in degrees below g since C starts in degree zero. Computing C first, flatness of each localization gives H^i(C) tensored with E. Every finite-length R-module is killed by a power of the maximal ideal, so all its nonempty parameter localizations are zero; its E-cohomology is itself in degree zero. This second spectral sequence degenerates and identifies total cohomology in degree i with H^i(C). Therefore H^i(C)=0 for i<g. Apply this to the dual cohomology complex. This explicit proof avoids incorrectly applying a length-g induction hypothesis to a length-g complex over dimension g−1.

Split off any remaining differential unit entries to choose a minimal finite free complex over R. Its differential vanishes after reduction to k, so dim H0(A,O)=dim Hg(A,O)=1 makes K0 and Kg rank one; the latter equality uses Serre duality and the trivial canonical bundle. Hence both terminal cokernels, for K and C, are cyclic. Write the dual terminal cokernel R/J. Its dual description of H0 after reduction shows that the section 1 on the zero fibre lifts over Spec R/J; Nakayama makes the induced section of P invertible there. The **full** universal property forces Spec R/J→B to factor through zero, so J is the maximal ideal. The dual complex is therefore a free resolution of k. Compare it by chain lifting with the Koszul resolution on a regular parameter system; resolutions by projectives are homotopy equivalent. Dualizing back yields terminal cokernel k. Hence Rp2_*P=k(0)[−g]. This is EGM's second proof, with its unproved references replaced by ordinary complex, regular-local and Koszul suppliers; it needs neither the Borel theorem nor general relative Grothendieck duality.

For any nondegenerate L, pull back this equality along the finite flat isogeny φ_L. It gives χ(Λ(L))=(−1)^g deg φ_L, retaining the full scheme length of K(L), including characteristic-dividing degree. Since all direct images of Λ(L) have support on the finite kernel, tensoring by L does not change their lengths or Euler characteristic. The automorphism (m,p1) of A×A identifies m^*L⊗p1^*L^−1 with the pullback of L⊠L^−1. Künneth and Serre duality give χ(Λ(L))=χ(L)χ(L^−1)=(−1)^gχ(L)². Therefore deg φ_L=χ(L)². Realize a polarization by ample L after finite separable extension; degree is unchanged by field extension, so it is a perfect square over the original field.

## Pitfalls that must not reappear

- A/K(L) plus a geometric-point bijection does not prove the rigidified Picard universal property. The open-functor gluing and fpqc descent above are essential.
- Finite group schemes cannot be replaced by their geometric points. EGM §8.31's printed sentence that the k-characters span the coordinate algebra is false for arbitrary nonreduced group schemes; any representation proof must be rewritten functorially.
- A perfect alternating pairing alone does **not** force square rank in characteristic 2: α2 has rank 2 and the perfect alternating pairing e(x,y)=1+xy. Use the theta-extension argument or the Poincaré cohomology degree proof.
- Do not claim the AK80 quotient proof was read. The attempted MIT paths returned 403, a CORE candidate returned 403, and the publisher text endpoint returned INVALID_INPUT; no full AK80 text was obtained. The generic published quotient route is the substantive replacement.
