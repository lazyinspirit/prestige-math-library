## Statement

Assume countable choice. Fix $\infty\in S^3$, put $A=\{\infty\}\times[0,1]$, and identify its complement with $\mathbb R^3\times[0,1]$. Let $S_0,S_1$ be compact smooth oriented link cobordisms disjoint from $A$, with the same fixed boundary parametrizations and product collars. If they are ambient isotopic in $S^3\times[0,1]$ relative to those collars, then their comparison factors into finitely many ambient isotopies supported in the Euclidean complement and finitely many local sheet passages through $A$. Each sheet passage occurs at a regular surface-height point and gives the sweep-around move: in planar movies, a closing arc for a one-interval tangle travels over the tangle to the opposite side and returns underneath, or the inverse, retaining its orientation. All end collars remain fixed. No assertion that a sweep-around is generated by the fifteen Euclidean Carter–Saito moves, or that any homology map respects it, is made.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The fixed missing arc $A$, the two compact collared cobordisms, their given ambient isotopy and countable choice.

[A1] [[def-countable-choice]] is used in the following transversality and extension suppliers, with no full AC.

[F1] [[thm-parametric-transversality]] and [[thm-transverse-preimage-theorem]] give a generic parameter whose incidence with a submanifold is transverse, with its preimage of the expected dimension; negative expected dimension makes the preimage empty.

[F2] [[thm-isotopy-extension]] extends a compact embedding isotopy in a boundaryless target with support in a prescribed neighbourhood. A source collar fixed by the isotopy has zero velocity and can be protected by choosing the extension cutoff away from a smaller collar.

## Proof

1.1 Parametrize the given isotopy by embeddings $e_\lambda:S\to S^3\times[0,1]$, stationary near $\lambda=0,1$ after a smooth parameter change. Write $e_\lambda(x)=(a_\lambda(x),h_\lambda(x))$. Product collars are fixed, and both endpoint surfaces avoid $A$. Consequently there is a compact subset of $S^\circ\times(0,1)$ outside of which the spatial map $a(x,\lambda)$ cannot equal $\infty$. Work in a spatial chart about $\infty$ and finitely many source charts covering the possible incidence set. Bumps supported in these charts and in the free parameter interval allow arbitrary spatial value perturbations and first jets of $a$ and $h$. These perturbations are supported off all protected collars and parameter ends. Small perturbations remain families of embeddings: compactness of $S\times I$ makes the local immersion and distant-source separation bounds uniform in $\lambda$. Their straight interpolation in these coordinate neighbourhoods is again a family of embeddings. Thus the endpoints and their comparison isotopy are retained. [given, A1, F1, construct]

2.1 Apply [F1] to the spatial map $a:S^\circ\times(0,1)\to S^3$ and the point $\infty$, using the finite family in step 1.1. Its transverse incidence has dimension $3-3=0$. Exclude at an incidence the additional condition $d_xh_\lambda=0$: it imposes two independent first-jet equations, independently controllable together with the three spatial value equations. The total codimension is five on a three-dimensional source, so this extra incidence is empty. Exclude also two different source points with $a_\lambda(x)=a_\lambda(y)=\infty$ at one parameter: their six spatial equations have codimension six on $(x,y,\lambda)$ of dimension five; independent values at two points exclude this. Cover the free off-diagonal configurations by countably many chart pieces as in [F1]. There can be no accumulation of such pairs on the diagonal at an individual transverse incidence, since there the full map $a(x,\lambda)$ is a local diffeomorphism. Therefore all incidences are single, occur at regular height points, and have distinct parameter values. [A1, F1, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 The incidence set is closed in the compact free set of step 1.1 and discrete by transversality, hence finite. Choose small disjoint parameter intervals about its values. On the complementary intervals the complete compact surface track avoids $A$, so lies in a compact subset of $\mathbb R^3\times[0,1]$. The given family there is an embedding isotopy in that Euclidean complement. Extend it by [F2], using the boundaryless carrier $\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R$ and velocity cutoffs away from the fixed end collars. Its support may be kept in a compact subset of the open strip, so the extension restricts to an ambient isotopy of the original product fixing those collars. Thus these pieces contribute precisely the asserted Euclidean ambient isotopies. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 At one incidence, $d_xh\ne0$. Use height $t=h$ as one surface coordinate, with the other coordinate $u$. Transversality says that the derivative of $(u,t,\lambda)\mapsto a(u,t,\lambda)$ is invertible at the incidence. Its inverse function chart therefore identifies the track locally with one sheet crossing the missing vertical arc once. Distinctness and embeddedness permit a small four-ball meeting no other surface piece at that parameter. The boundary of that sheet disk and all other surface pieces remain disjoint from $A$. In a spatial ball about $\infty$, replace its crossing by either of the two detours on the boundary of the ball. Under stereographic projection these become the two ways to carry the same local arc past the outside of a large ball containing the rest of the diagram. A meridional disk to that arc shows their difference explicitly: its boundary passes from the front of the complementary one-interval tangle to its back, first over every strand and then underneath on the return. Shrink the crossing disk to obtain the standard closing-arc movie; expanding it back gives the original sheet passage, with its unchanged boundary. The front/back choices give the move and its inverse. The sphere-boundary detours and their annular interpolations supply actual smooth isotopies off $A$ outside this one passage, so no additional local event has been suppressed. The orientation of the arc is the transported boundary orientation of the same sheet disk. This is exactly the sweep-around, rather than a compactly supported Euclidean isotopy. [given, F2, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 Cut the original parameter interval into the finitely many crossing neighbourhoods and their complement. Apply step 3.1 on each complementary piece and step 4.1 at each crossing. Their boundary identifications are those of the original family; the local adjustments fix their boundaries and all original end collars, so the pieces concatenate. This gives the finite factorization, retaining all orientations and boundary parametrizations. Empty incidence gives only Euclidean isotopies. The construction separates geometric sufficiency from any algebraic sweep-around identity, as stated. [given, A1, step 3.1, step 4.1] ∎
