## Statement

Assume countable choice. Let two finite generic planar movies represent compact smooth oriented surfaces in $\mathbb R^3\times[0,1]$, with the same parametrized boundary and product collars. If their embedded surfaces are ambient isotopic with compact support relative to those collars, then the two movies are related by a finite sequence of the fifteen Euclidean Carter–Saito local types, distant interchanges of disjoint elementary movie events, and planar isotopies. All orientations and omitted-coordinate crossing orders are retained. This statement concerns Euclidean isotopies; it supplies no sweep-around relation.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The two generic movies, a compactly supported smooth isotopy between their embedded traces fixed on the end collars, and countable choice.

[A1] [[def-countable-choice]] is used in finite-dimensional transversality on the countably covered configuration strata and in isotopy extension. It is not full AC.

[F1] [[thm-parametric-transversality]] and [[thm-transverse-preimage-theorem]] give transverse incidence and exclude strata whose codimension exceeds the source dimension.

[F2] [[thm-euclidean-inverse-function-theorem]], [[thm-morse-lemma]] and [[lem-one-variable-completion-of-the-square-with-a-smooth-parameter]] supply local graph coordinates, nondegenerate quadratic forms, and square completion with parameters.

[F3] [[def-carter-saito-euclidean-local-movie-moves]] specifies the local types, including all reflected, reversed and compatible oriented variants.

[F4] [[thm-isotopy-extension]] extends the compact embedding isotopies produced by the supported perturbations, with the fixed end collars protected by zero velocity there.

## Proof

1.1 **Independent finite jets of the actual family.** Write the given family as embeddings $f_\lambda:S\to\mathbb R^4$, $f=(x,y,z,t)$, stationary near its parameter ends and fixed on the surface end collars. Embed the compact source $S\times I$ in Euclidean space using its given initial embedding and the coordinate $\lambda$. With smooth cutoffs supported off the protected collars, perturb the four coordinates of $f$ by coordinate polynomials of degree at most $89$. This prescribes jets through order five independently at any at most eight free source points. Indeed at point $X_i$ multiply a suitable degree-five Taylor polynomial by $\prod_{j\ne i}|X-X_j|^{12}$; this vanishes to order at least twelve at every other point, is nonzero at $X_i$, and has degree at most $12\cdot7+5=89$. Invert the cutoff's nonzero jet at each free point. Finitely many source charts suffice; on fixed regular collar pieces no perturbation is needed. Sufficiently small parameters retain every $f_\lambda$ as an embedding, by uniform local immersion bounds and compact separation of distant source points. Interpolation gives an embedding deformation fixing the endpoint surfaces, hence [F4] preserves the original comparison. We may therefore apply [F1] to all the finite-jet conditions below. [given, A1, F1, F4, construct]

2.1 **The possible multiple-point strata.** Put $p_\lambda=(x,y,t)\circ f_\lambda$. For $k$ distinct surface points, equality of their projected images has $3(k-1)$ independent equations on dimension $2k+1$, including $\lambda$. It is empty for $k\ge5$; double, triple and quadruple configurations have dimensions two, one and zero. Projection rank at most one has codimension two in the space of $3$-by-$2$ derivatives, so its locus in $S\times I$ has dimension one; rank zero has codimension six and is absent. At rank one the omitted $z$ derivative on its kernel is nonzero because $f_\lambda$ is an immersion. For height-critical points, $d_St=0$ has codimension two and hence gives a one-dimensional locus. Conditions of a second independent degeneracy add another equation; two extra conditions at one of these isolated events are avoided. Independent jets also make distinct local degeneracies have distinct parameter values, except for the single equality of heights of two disjoint ordinary events considered in the disjoint-event analysis below. Higher multiple points, rank-zero points and the excluded extra degeneracies cannot be missing cases. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 **Double curves and MM3, MM4, MM9.** Away from projection branch points and height criticals, a sheet is a moving projected curve parametrized by one spatial coordinate and $t$. At a tangency of two such curves choose their common tangent as $x$ and write their normal difference as $q(x,t,\lambda)$. The equations for a double-curve height critical are $q=q_x=0$. Ordinarily $q_tq_{xx}\ne0$, giving one R2 event by [F2]. If $q_t=0$, transversality makes the Hessian in $(x,t)$ nonsingular and the deformation derivative nonzero. Completing squares with parameter gives the two level-set types $x^2+t^2-\lambda=0$ and $x^2-t^2-\lambda=0$, up to signs and smooth changes of coordinates. These are exactly MM3 and MM4 of [F3]. If instead $q_{xx}=0$ while $q_t\ne0$, solve $q=0$ for $t=\varphi(x,\lambda)$. The generic conditions are $\varphi_x=\varphi_{xx}=0$, $\varphi_{xxx}\ne0$ and $\partial_\lambda\varphi_x\ne0$. Apply square completion to $\varphi_x$: its two simple zeros are created or removed as $\lambda$ crosses zero. Their two critical heights give the cubic double-curve picture $x^3+\lambda x-t=0$, namely MM9. The root and height ordering follows from the nonzero derivatives, so higher terms do not add a local event. Vanishing of both alternatives simultaneously, a rank-zero Hessian, a zero cubic derivative, or a zero unfolding derivative is an extra independent condition and is absent. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct]

3.2 **Branch points and MM1, MM2, MM7.** At a branch point with regular surface height use source coordinates $(v,t)$ and write its planar projection as $g(v,t,\lambda)$. Its defining equations are $g_v=0$. Generically $g_{vv}\ne0$; otherwise there are two further equations and no such point. Square completion in one planar coordinate and subtraction of the moving basepoint put that coordinate in the form $v^2$. Equality of two nearby projected points then pairs $v$ with $-v$. Divide the difference of their other coordinates by $2v$. It extends smoothly and evenly in $v$, including $v=0$, by the integral formula for a divided difference. Writing $r=v^2\ge0$ gives a smooth function of $(r,t,\lambda)$ up to $r=0$: Taylor's formula to arbitrary order gives all one-sided derivatives there. At an ordinary branch point the relevant first derivatives are nonzero, and this is the usual R1 unfolding. There are two possible additional isolated degeneracies. First the branch locus $g_v=0$ has a quadratic turn in its projection to $\lambda$; after solving the first equation its second equation has a nondegenerate critical point in $t$. Square completion gives $t^2-\lambda$ and the local projection $(v^2,(t^2-\lambda)v+c v^3)$, $c\ne0$, with the two relative signs giving MM1 and MM2. Second solve the divided-difference equation for $t=\varphi(r,\lambda)$. At $r=0$ a critical point of this double-curve height enters its half-line when $\varphi_r(0,0)=0$, $\varphi_{rr}(0,0)\ne0$ and $\partial_\lambda\varphi_r(0,0)\ne0$. Thus $\varphi_r$ has one simple zero on $r>0$ on exactly one side of the parameter; this is $t=-\lambda r-r^2$ up to signs, giving MM7. A fifth-order term of $g$ supplies the nonzero $r^2$ term. These derivative tests also cover higher-order-looking planar cusp representatives without introducing another topological move. Extra vanishing derivatives have higher codimension and are excluded by step 1.1. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct]

3.3 **Triple and quadruple points.** At an ordinary projected triple point all pairwise sliced tangents are transverse. Continue the intersection of two strands as the reference point; their transverse coordinates and [F2] reduce the third-strand condition to a signed displacement $q(t,\lambda)=0$. A simple zero in $t$ is an R3 event. Its quadratic creation or cancellation, $q=q_t=0$ with $q_{tt}q_\lambda\ne0$, is MM5. If instead one pair of sliced tangents becomes parallel, its quadratic contact is nonzero and the third strand is transverse to that tangent. The corresponding local picture is two graphs with difference $x^2-t$ and a third strand $x=\lambda$. Its two resolutions give MM6. Further pairwise tangency or a coincident branch or Morse degeneracy is extra codimension and is absent. A projected quadruple point is isolated by step 2.1. Its four sheets are transverse in the generic sense that any three independent sheet normals span the projected three-space; their omitted heights are four distinct values. Replace each sheet by its tangent affine plane in a small ball. The perturbation moving the fourth plane across the meeting point of the other three resolves the point in the two directions; reading the four triple-point heights gives the tetrahedral R3 pair MM10. A singular determinant of three normals is an extra equation and is excluded. Higher Taylor terms retain these transverse intersections and their order on the ball boundary, so the affine resolution is an isotopy of the same local configuration. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct]

3.4 **Morse births and MM11.** At a height critical point the spatial derivative is injective. For invertible planar tangent projection the height is a function of the two planar variables. Its ordinary nonsingular Hessian gives an ordinary birth, saddle or death. At an isolated degeneracy the Hessian has a one-dimensional kernel: rank zero imposes three Hessian equations and cannot occur here. Split off its nonzero square by [F2]. In the remaining coordinate $v$, the generic derivative has a nondegenerate quadratic zero with nonzero parameter derivative, so its two critical points are created or removed exactly as for $v^3-\lambda v$. Their indices differ by one, and the square's sign gives the two adjacent-index possibilities. This is MM11, including its reverse. Its local critical values are distinct off the one parameter. The cubic derivative or unfolding derivative cannot vanish as well, by the independent-jet condition. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 **A branch point or a Morse point meeting another sheet.** A branch point on one projected sheet coinciding with another sheet imposes the three projected value equalities and the two branch equations on dimension five, hence is isolated in the family. All further degeneracies are absent. The other sheet meets transversely, so put its sliced strand in the form of a line independently displaced by $\lambda$ across the cusp unfolding. The two signs of the displacement give precisely the two ways to pass a strand across an R1 branch picture: MM8. Likewise a height critical on one sheet coinciding with another sheet is isolated. When its planar tangent projection is invertible, [F2] gives its height as $u^2+v^2$, $-u^2-v^2$ or $u^2-v^2$ in planar coordinates. The other sheet is a transversely displaced strand. Passing it across zero gives the extremum passage MM14 or the saddle passage MM15. These identifications retain the full neighbouring strands, not merely the numbers of crossings. At these configurations the two omitted heights are different, since the original surface is embedded; hence their over/under order is fixed. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.2, construct]

4.2 **Morse points with rank-one planar projection: MM12 and MM13.** At a height critical with rank-one planar tangent projection choose the nonzero projected tangent as $x=u$; the hidden $z$ derivative in the other direction is nonzero. The height Hessian is nonsingular generically, and its restriction to that other direction is nonzero; the exceptions are extra equations. Completing the square in that direction while retaining $u$, then rescaling, gives height $u^2+v^2$, its negative, or $u^2-v^2$. Subtract multiples of this height and of $x^2$ from the second planar coordinate. Its remaining generic quadratic term is a nonzero multiple of $uv$, while the parameter moves its linear $v$ coefficient through zero. Thus the models are $(u,uv+\lambda v,v,u^2\pm v^2)$. For the definite form, a positive-height slice has $u^2+v^2=c$; equality of the two points with the same $u$ requires $u=0$ when $\lambda=0$, giving the single crossing of the small circle in MM12. For the indefinite form, the zero-height slice has $v=\pm u$, giving two tangent parabolic branches whose pairings change on the two sides of the saddle; displacement of the projection singularity to one side or the other is MM13. The nonzero mixed coefficient and the definite or indefinite quadratic form control these pairings on a sufficiently small boundary, so their higher-order remainders do not change the movie type. The missing simultaneous branch-height case of step 3.2 is exactly this configuration. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.2, step 3.4, construct]

5.1 **Exhaustiveness near all source configurations.** At a single point the alternatives are an immersed projected sheet, its height critical, or a rank-one branch point. The branch-point and height-critical calculations in steps 3.2, 3.4 and 4.2 list every extra equation that can occur in their one-parameter families. At two distinct points the generic double-curve description fails only by a tangent height condition, a cubic contact, a branch point or a height critical on one sheet, or nontransverse projected tangent planes. The first four were handled in steps 3.1 and 4.1; equality of two rank-two tangent planes is the two-variable critical case of $q$ in step 3.1. Rank-one coincidence with nontransverse other tangent plane imposes one further equation on the isolated branch-sheet event and is absent. At three points the ordinary R3 description can fail only by the displacement derivative or by one sliced tangency, as in step 3.3. Four points give MM10; five are absent. Hidden-height coincidences would be spatial self-intersections and are forbidden by the embedding. Thus neither a seam stratum nor a framing stratum exists in this ordinary-surface problem, and none is being omitted by specializing the foam calculation. [F1, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, step 4.1, step 3.3, step 3.4, step 4.2, construct]

6.1 **Finiteness and distant events.** The isolated event loci just listed cannot accumulate at deleted source diagonals. Near a regular sheet a projected coordinate chart is injective. Near a branch point the divided-difference half-line in step 3.2 is the entire local double locus; its ordinary or quadratic-root alternatives have only the listed finite changes. If some points stay separate while a pair coalesces, the limit is the branch-sheet case already treated; analogous limits with a height critical are step 4.1. Triple or quadruple clusters reduce to these single- or double-cluster alternatives; the listed nonzero derivatives exclude further degeneration. On compact configuration sets separated from those neighbourhoods the event conditions are closed and transverse zero-manifolds, hence finite. The same reasoning on the finitely many boundary-collar charts excludes accumulation at the protected faces. For two disjoint ordinary events at the same height, their independently continued heights $t_1(\lambda),t_2(\lambda)$ have a transverse difference zero; equality of three heights, a zero derivative of that difference, or equality simultaneously with one of the preceding degeneracies is higher codimension and is avoided by the independent jets at up to eight points. Their isolated exchanges are finite by the same compactness argument and are exactly the distant interchanges of [F3]. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, step 4.1, step 3.3, step 4.2, step 5.1, construct]

7.1 **Assembly of the comparison.** Choose disjoint small parameter intervals around the finitely many event values. On the intervening intervals every finite projected incidence pattern continues smoothly, with unchanged omitted-coordinate inequalities and surface orientations. Inverse-function charts at its crossings and branch or Morse generators, followed by normal-coordinate cutoffs on the intervening edges and sheets, identify the movies by planar isotopy and changes of stationary frames. At an event the corresponding local calculation above replaces one movie by its other side with all external strands fixed. Supported embedding perturbations and these local comparisons preserve the boundary parametrizations and collars by [F4]. Compose the finitely many comparisons in parameter order. By the preceding exhaustive local classification and finiteness argument there are no other events; the composition is the required finite sequence of the fifteen types and distant interchanges. This proves the Euclidean assertion only. [given, A1, F3, F4, step 5.1, step 6.1] ∎
