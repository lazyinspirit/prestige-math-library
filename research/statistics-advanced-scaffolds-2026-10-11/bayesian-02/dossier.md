# Bayesian-02 source dossier

Assignment: asymptotic and nonparametric Bayes—posterior consistency and contraction, Bernstein–von Mises limits and failures, misspecification and generalized/robust updating, Gaussian-process and nonparametric priors, and uncertainty calibration. This dossier proposes theorem interfaces and proof routes; it does not create items, pages, or canonical-plan edits. Pair labels A01–A11 are local reader labels, not final BCS IDs.

Category 3 dependency boundary: every proposed pair uses existing mathematics, a planned Category 1/2 supplier, or an earlier Category 3 pair. None has a direct or indirect Category 4 dependency. In particular, GP priors are probability models on function spaces; their contraction proofs do not require GP prediction/learning interfaces from Category 4.

### Distance convention needed by the asymptotic pairs

Use MSF-21 for KL and Pinsker rather than duplicating its intended definitions. Add only the missing local Hellinger contract, with
\[
h(P,Q)^2=\int(\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2\,d\mu,
\]
for densities with respect to a common dominating measure. Prove invariance under a change of common dominating measure and the comparisons
\[
\|P-Q\|_{TV}\le h(P,Q),\qquad h(P,Q)^2\le 2\|P-Q\|_{TV}.
\]
These constants match the library TV convention. Ghosal–Ghosh–van der Vaart (2000) and the DP-mixture paper use the same root-density L2 Hellinger distance but call the full L1 density distance “total variation”; their TV statements therefore differ by a factor of two from the library’s `sup_A` distance. Use their Hellinger formulations where possible and translate any L1 conclusion explicitly. Kleijn–van der Vaart’s BvM statement is a supremum over measurable sets and matches the library convention.

## Source-grounded results and proof routes

**IID posterior contraction.** For a dominated IID density model with true density `p0`, Ghosal, Ghosh and van der Vaart (2000), Theorem 2.1, assume `εn→0`, `n εn²→∞`, a sieve `Pn`, entropy `log D(εn,Pn,d) ≤ n εn²`, prior sieve tail `Π(Pnᶜ) ≤ exp(-n εn²(C+4))`, and local prior mass at least `exp(-C n εn²)` in the set where both `-P0 log(p/p0) ≤ εn²` and `P0(log(p/p0))² ≤ εn²`. Then posterior mass outside `M εn` in the selected metric tends to zero in `P0ⁿ`-probability for sufficiently large `M`. Theorem 2.4 gives the annular-entropy/testing generalization, including `εn→0` with `n εn²` bounded away from zero, prior mass in KL/second-moment balls, and a tail summability condition. Section 7, Theorems 7.1 and 7.3, constructs/tests annuli; §8, Lemma 8.1 and the proof of Theorem 2.1, lower-bounds the posterior denominator by Jensen and Chebyshev, controls test errors on the numerator, and sums over shells. This is convergence in probability. Their almost-sure results require stronger prior-mass and likelihood-ratio control (Theorems 2.2–2.3), so do not upgrade the conclusion automatically. Their density-TV convention is L1, not the library supremum convention.

**Misspecified consistency and LAN/BvM.** Kleijn and van der Vaart (2012), §4, Lemma 4.1 and Corollary 4.2, give a misspecified Schwartz route. Define `Qθ(A)=P0[(pθ/pθ*)1A]` relative to a KL minimizer `θ*`; this is the source’s likelihood-tilted measure convention and need not be a probability. Positive prior mass in every KL neighbourhood of the minimizer set, plus tests of `P0` against the `Qθ` products outside any neighbourhood, implies almost-sure posterior concentration on that minimizer set. The proof uses exponential test errors, a strong-law lower bound on the integrated likelihood denominator over a KL set, Fubini, and Borel–Cantelli. For a compact parameter space, a unique KL minimizer, and the source’s continuity/integrability assumptions, Theorem 3.2 constructs consistent tests; the authors explicitly say that qualitative continuity alone does not handle distant alternatives on a noncompact parameter space.

For the parametric LAN result, Kleijn–van der Vaart Theorem 2.1 assumes a finite-dimensional interior point `θ*`, stochastic LAN at rate `δn` with bounded-in-probability central sequence and nonsingular matrix `V`, a prior density continuous and positive near `θ*`, and posterior concentration `Πn(||θ-θ*||>δn Mn|X)→0` for every `Mn→∞`. It concludes TV convergence of the locally recentered/rescaled posterior to `N(Δn,V⁻¹)`. In the IID misspecified case, Lemma 2.1 supplies sufficient LAN conditions: differentiability of `log pθ(X)` at `θ*` in `P0`-probability; a square-integrable local Lipschitz envelope; a positive-definite second-order expansion of the KL contrast. Lemma 2.2 gives the MLE expansion under consistency and an approximate likelihood-maximization condition. Its asymptotic covariance is the sandwich `V⁻¹ P0(\dotℓ\dotℓᵀ) V⁻¹`, whereas the posterior covariance is `V⁻¹`. Example 2.1, fitting `N(θ,1)` to a mean-zero truth of variance `σ²`, shows credible widths based on `1/n` and sampling widths based on `σ²/n`; coverage can approach zero or one. LAN and contraction imply a BvM approximation, but ordinary Bayesian credible coverage is not automatically robust to misspecification.

**Gaussian-process support and contraction.** Van der Vaart and van Zanten (2008), Theorem 2.1, assume a centered, tight Borel Gaussian random element `W` in a separable Banach space, its RKHS `H`, and a target `w0` in the Banach closure of `H`. With concentration function
`φw0(ε)=inf{||h||H²: ||h-w0||<ε} - log Pr(||W||<ε)`, if `φw0(εn)≤n εn²`, the theorem constructs measurable sieves with entropy `O(n εn²)`, exponentially small Gaussian tail, and local prior mass at least `exp(-n εn²)`. The proof (§5, proof of Theorem 2.1) uses Borell’s inequality and RKHS translations to bound the sieve tail, cover its RKHS component, and lower-bound prior mass. Combined with the Ghosal et al. posterior-contraction theorem, this yields a contraction rate only when the statistical likelihood distances and KL/variance quantities are controlled by the chosen Banach norm. Theorem 3.1 gives one such result for log-Gaussian density priors, and Theorems 3.2–3.3 treat binary regression and fixed-design Gaussian regression under their displayed norm/link/design conditions. The paper states the support-equals-RKHS-closure theorem as known in its introduction; it does not prove that identity. A local support theorem needs a Cameron–Martin/Hahn–Banach proof or an explicit earlier supplier. A finite-dimensional Gaussian process or cylinder construction is insufficient.

**Dirichlet normal-mixture contraction.** Ghosal and van der Vaart (2007), Theorem 1, consider IID data from compactly supported `p0`, Gaussian mixtures `pF,σ=F*φσ`, `F~DP(α)` with `0<α(ℝ)<∞`, and independent `σ/σn~G`; `n^-a1≲σn≲n^-a2`, `0<a2<a1<1`, and the scale cdf satisfies `G(s)≲exp(-βs^-γ)` as `s↓0` and `1-G(s)≲exp(-βs^γ)` as `s→∞`, for `β>0`, `γ>1`. The base measure has a continuous positive density on an interval containing `supp(p0)`, `a2≥(4+γ)^-1`, and `h(p0,p0*φσ)=O(σ²)`. A sufficient smoothness/integrability condition is twice continuous differentiability together with `∫[((p0''/p0)^2)+((p0'/p0)^4)]p0 dλ<∞`. The displayed Hellinger rate is the maximum of the kernel/entropy term `(nσn)^-1/2 log n`, the scale-tail term `n^-1/2 (σn^-1)^[γ/(2(γ-1))]_+`, and `σn² log n`; under the additional interval-mass condition the last term is `σn²`, and a compactly supported scale hyperprior removes the middle term. Section 9 proves the result from the paper’s sieve theorem 5 (§4): normal-mixture entropy (§5), convolution approximation (§6), Dirichlet simplex prior-mass bounds (§7), and posterior scale-tail control (§8). The compact-support and tail assumptions are real restrictions; an inverse-Gamma scale prior has polynomial upper tail and is explicitly outside this theorem. The paper says this does not prove inconsistency for inverse-Gamma priors.

**Generalized Bayes and calibration.** Bissiri, Holmes and Walker (2016), equations (7)–(8), define the update by minimizing `∫ℓ(θ,x)dν + KL(ν||π)`. When `0<∫exp(-ℓ(θ,x))π(dθ)<∞`, the minimizer is `ν(dθ) ∝ exp(-ℓ(θ,x))π(dθ)`. Additive losses give sequentially coherent updates in their framework. For observations `x1,…,xn`, the loss update is proportional to `exp(-w Σi ℓ(θ,xi))π(dθ)` for a chosen temperature `w>0`. Sections 2.2–2.3 give proxy-likelihood and robust M-loss motivations. Section 3 discusses subjective, unit-information, hierarchical, and operational calibration of `w`; it explicitly does not prescribe one universal method. These formulas alone imply no posterior consistency, contamination robustness, sandwich correction, or credible coverage theorem. The local normalization/variational identity is elementary once the normalizer is finite; any frequentist guarantee needs separate assumptions and proof.

**Nonparametric BvM and credible sets.** Castillo and Nickl (2013) study white noise `dX⁽ⁿ⁾(t)=f(t)dt+n^-1/2dW(t)` and define a weak BvM after centering/scaling in `H(δ)=H^{-1/2,δ}_2`, `δ>1/2`. The white-noise Gaussian is tight there; it is not an `L²`-valued Gaussian. Theorem 1 transfers weak BvM to an exact asymptotic `1-α` credible-ball coverage theorem with radius `OP(1)` in that weak norm. Theorem 4 transfers it to smooth nonlinear functionals with a quadratic remainder and a posterior `L²`-contraction condition `rn=o(n^-1/2)`; Theorem 3 treats linear functionals with Riesz representer in `Hs`, `s>1/2`. For product priors, Theorems 5, 7–9 and their proofs use coordinatewise second-moment control, finite-dimensional TV convergence, and tightness to obtain weak BvM. Theorem 9 assumes `f=Σlk σl αlk ψlk`, iid coefficient density `ϕ(x)≤C exp(-a x²)`, `σl=2^{-l(α+1/2)}`, `f0∈L²`, the coefficient bound `sup_lk |⟨f0,ψlk⟩|/σl≤M`, and a coefficient density continuous and bounded below on a neighborhood containing the normalized true coefficients. The theorem also assumes continuity of each coordinate density near its true coefficient. If `f0∈Cβ` and `α≤β`, that smoothness is one sufficient way to obtain the coefficient bound; it is not an extra hypothesis of Theorem 9. These results do not state full `L²` or all-Borel-set total-variation BvM. The source points to Leahu for restrictions on strong BvM; that paper was not read, so no broad impossibility theorem from it is imported. A direct local failure proof is available: independent standard-normal coefficients do not have square-summable norm almost surely, so the white-noise limit has no probability law on `L²`.

Castillo and Nickl (2014) extend weak BvM results to IID density sampling. Theorem 3 assumes `ρ0≤f0≤D0` and either: (S) a truncated log-density prior `f=exp(T-c(T))`, with cutoff `Ln=jn` or `ln` as defined in equations (19)–(20), `log f0∈C^α`, `α>1`; coefficient scales `σl=2^{-l(α+1/2)}` for a log-Lipschitz coefficient density, or `σl=2^{-l(r+1/2)}` for Gaussian coefficients with `α>1/2` and `0<r<α-1/4`; or (H) dyadic histograms at that cutoff, Dirichlet weights satisfying `c1 2^{-Ln a}≤αk≤c2` for fixed positive `a,c1,c2`, and `f0∈C^α`, `1/2<α≤1`. In both cases the multiscale weight is admissible (`wl/√l→∞`). The theorem proves weak BvM in `M0(w)` centered at the truncated empirical measure `Pn(Ln)`. Theorem 4 transfers this to posterior CDFs and Brownian-bridge limits; Corollary 2 gives exact asymptotic coverage of posterior CDF bands. Theorem 5 gives exact coverage of multiscale credible bands under the weak BvM premise; Proposition 5 adds a Hölder truth and a diverging undersmoothing bound to obtain the stated diameter. These are specific sampling models and prior classes, not universal coverage statements.

**Adaptive empirical-Bayes credible sets.** Szabó, van der Vaart and van Zanten (2015) study the inverse Gaussian sequence `Xi=κi θ0,i+n^-1/2 Zi`, independent standard normal `Zi`, with mildly ill-posed `C^-2 i^-2p≤κi²≤C² i^-2p`, and independent prior coordinates `θi~N(0,i^-1-2α)`. The marginal-likelihood maximizer `αhat` over `[0,A]` selects the empirical-Bayes posterior. Its `1-γ` posterior `ℓ²` ball is inflated by `L`. The polished-tail class requires `Σ_{i=N}∞ θi² ≤ L0 Σ_{i=N}^{ρN} θi²` for every `N≥N0`. Theorem 3.6 states that for each fixed polished-tail class, a sufficiently large `L` gives uniform coverage tending to one (conservative, not exact `1-γ`); for growing `A=An≤√log(n)/(4√log(ρ∨e))`, the source gives a slowly varying inflation sequence. Theorem 3.1 constructs a fixed sparse-gap truth inside a hyperrectangle `Θβ(M)`: for `ρj↑∞` and integers `nj+1≥(2ρj²)^{1+2β+2p}nj`, its squared coefficients alternate zero gaps with `M i^{-1-2β}` blocks, and coverage tends to zero along `nj` whenever the inflation `Lnj` is at most the theorem's order `√M ρj^{(1+2p)/(8+8β+8p)}` (up to the source's displayed constant convention). Section 5 proves coverage by Theorem 5.1's bracket on `αhat`, then compares posterior radius, bias and centered noise; Section 9 proves the sparse-gap failure by showing that the fitted regularity is too smooth and the posterior radius is smaller than the bias. Adaptivity and coverage are specific to this model, prior, hyperparameter selection, and parameter class.
