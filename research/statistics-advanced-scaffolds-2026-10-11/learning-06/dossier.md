The citations below distinguish theorem statements from proofs actually shown. The source ledger in [sources.json](sources.json) gives URLs, exact PDF pages and printed ranges when available, full-text paths, and PDF/text SHA-256 hashes.

### Online convex optimization and bandits

- **Hazan, *Introduction to Online Convex Optimization*, author-posted arXiv v3.** Complete selected units: PDF pp. 23–38 (printed pp. 1–16, online protocol, static regret, Hedge); 39–62 (17–40, convex optimization/projection background); 63–76 (41–54, OGD); 93–122 (71–100, regularized FTL, mirror descent, adaptive regularization/AdaGrad); 123–143 (101–121, bandit convex optimization, EXP3 and SCRiBLe); 191–206 (169–184, dynamic and adaptive regret). OGD's bounded-diameter/subgradient proof is shown; its minimax lower-bound derivation is left as an exercise. Several convex optimization steps are exercises or citations (including a projection-existence exercise and AdaGrad optimizer proof). The adaptive-regret chapter defers parts of the FLH/FLH2 proof to exercises. Keep theorem assumptions attached: convex losses, bounded diameter and subgradient for OGD; comparator class and loss range for Hedge; path variation for dynamic regret; exp-concavity/strong convexity for stronger adaptive rates. Hazan's “adaptive regret” is regret on changing intervals/comparators, not sequential statistical validity.
- **Lattimore & Szepesvári, *Bandit Algorithms*, author-hosted free edition.** Complete selected units: PDF pp. 65–82 (printed pp. 56–73, stochastic protocol/regret decomposition); 83–97 (74–88, concentration); 100–110 (91–101, ETC); 111–125 (102–116, UCB); 157–173 (148–164, adversarial Exp3); 233–246 (224–237, adversarial contextual bandits and Exp4); 247–262 (238–253, stochastic linear contextual bandits/LinUCB); 263–275 (254–266, time-uniform linear confidence sets). The UCB proof handles its random arm-count issue by a union bound over fixed reward-table prefixes; do not replace it with an unjustified concentration inequality at a random sample count. Chapter 11's Exp3 theorem is expected regret under its stated adversary/reward model; it does not give a high-probability guarantee and explicitly notes high variance. Exp4-IX high-probability analysis is an exercise. In Chapter 5, the Markov/Chebyshev and Hoeffding-lemma proofs and one sub-Gaussian-sum proof are exercises; use the existing local concentration suppliers or prove the specific needed form. Chapter 20 gives a mixture-supermartingale proof for a time-uniform linear-regression ellipsoid, assuming conditionally sub-Gaussian noise, predictable designs, positive ridge parameter, and a bound on the unknown parameter norm; keep this stronger assumption visible in LinUCB.

### MDPs, exploration, and function-approximation limits

- **Sutton & Barto, *Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction*, 2e official “complete draft” (22 March 2018).** Complete selected chapters: PDF pp. 67–92 (printed pp. 47–72, finite MDP/returns/Bellman equations); 93–110 (73–90, dynamic programming); 111–138 (91–117, Monte Carlo); 139–160 (119–140, TD/Sarsa/Q-learning); 179–213 (159–193, planning, Dyna, RTDP); 217–262 (197–241, function approximation); 263–278 (243–257, control with approximation/average reward); 279–308 (259–288, off-policy approximation); 343–361 (323–341, policy gradients and actor–critic). Policy-improvement and policy-gradient-theorem derivations are shown. General value-iteration convergence and the standard linear TD/Q convergence results are cited or sketched rather than proved in full. Exploring-starts Monte Carlo control convergence is explicitly left as a fundamental open proof question in the book. The book gives concrete instability counterexamples, including Baird's seven-state construction and a two-state divergence calculation, and explicit continuing average-reward ergodicity requirements. Do not turn these examples into a claim that all approximation or deep RL diverges. The online author PDF was retrieved over HTTP after its HTTPS endpoint failed; it is the authors' complete draft, and some figures were still being remade.
- **Strehl, Li & Littman, “Reinforcement Learning in Finite MDPs: PAC Analysis.”** Full JMLR article PDF pp. 1–32 (printed pp. 2413–2444), including R-MAX and Delayed Q-learning algorithms, general escape-event theorem, full upper-bound derivations, lower-bound derivation, appendices/references. The discounted finite-MDP model has rewards in [0,1], known finite S/A and discount, plus an admissible upper heuristic. PAC-MDP counts non-ε-optimal-policy steps with high probability; it does not mean the algorithm stops making all mistakes after a fixed learning phase. The reward Hoeffding and transition L1 concentration lemmas are stated/proved through cited inequalities; the authors note that adaptively selected online samples need a martingale argument, which they refer to another paper for. Reprove the adaptive concentration step or supply an earlier result. R-MAX and Delayed-Q bounds are worst-case upper bounds and are not practical sample predictions without the stated model/heuristic assumptions. Their lower-bound theorem assumes deterministic policies and a one-state-at-a-time policy-change condition.
- **Jaksch, Ortner & Auer, “Near-optimal Regret Bounds for Reinforcement Learning.”** Full JMLR article PDF pp. 1–38 (printed pp. 1563–1600), including UCRL2, extended value iteration, all main regret/sample-complexity proofs, the lower bound, changing-MDP result, and appendices. Assumptions: finite state/action sets, rewards from [0,1], communicating finite-diameter MDP, unknown model, and average-reward benchmark independent of the initial state. The main high-probability bound is Õ(DS√(AT)) with logarithmic factors; a gap-dependent sample-complexity bound is also proved. The lower bound is Ω(√(DSAT)) only under the explicit size/diameter/horizon restrictions in Theorem 5. The source's changing-MDP claim assumes a bounded number of changes and diameter bounded throughout. This is not a discounted-return theorem and does not cover function approximation.

### Partial observation, imitation, safety, and adaptive inference

- **Kaelbling, Littman & Cassandra, “Planning and Acting in Partially Observable Stochastic Domains,” Brown CS-96-08.** Full report PDF pp. 1–29; PDF p. 2 is blank, body is pp. 3–27, references pp. 27–29. The scan's text extraction loses many mathematical symbols and subscripts; use it as a locator and verify formulas in the cached PDF. It treats finite-state/action/observation known-model POMDPs, Bayesian belief updates, the belief MDP, finite-horizon piecewise-linear convex value functions, witness pruning, and finite-memory policy graphs. The report does not learn the world model; exact witness/policy-tree counts can grow exponentially, and the paper acknowledges practical intractability.
- **Ross, Gordon & Bagnell, “A Reduction of Imitation Learning and Structured Prediction to No-Regret Online Learning” (DAgger).** Full AISTATS/JMLR W&CP 15 PDF pp. 1–9 (printed pp. 627–635). The main guarantee depends on no-regret over strongly convex bounded surrogate losses, expert labels for learner-induced states through interaction, the stated decay of expert-action mixing, and a surrogate-loss-to-task-cost assumption; the finite-sample result additionally uses Azuma. The paper proves a reduction/iteration bound, not that any arbitrary expert or supervised learner yields low task cost. Reported benchmarks are empirical, not part of the theorem.
- **Achiam et al., “Constrained Policy Optimization.”** Full PMLR 70 main-paper PDF pp. 1–10. It defines discounted CMDP cost constraints and proves the average-divergence performance-bound statement by referring to supplemental material; the main paper does not include that proof. Its idealized trust-region update has a worst-case constraint-violation bound; practical CPO linearizes, estimates from samples, uses conjugate gradients/line search, and adds cost shaping. The method's empirical near-satisfaction is not a hard pathwise safety guarantee. Pair proofs should either derive the performance-difference inequality locally or cite a proof source explicitly and retain the approximation gap.
- **Howard et al., “Time-uniform, nonparametric, nonasymptotic confidence sequences,” arXiv v9 (9 August 2022).** Complete PDF pp. 1–48, main text, proofs, appendices, tables, and references. The mixture-bound proof uses a product-measurability condition and Ville's inequality for nonnegative supermartingales; the stitching proof is in Appendix A.1; the empirical-Bernstein result has its self-normalization proof in Appendix A.8 and requires bounded observations and predictable forecasts. The matrix martingale claims rely on cited matrix supermartingale facts from Howard et al. (2020), not proved anew here; do not import that matrix theorem without its conditions/proof. Confidence sequences yield validity at all times and arbitrary stopping, not OCO adaptive regret and not automatic validity under an unmodeled distribution shift.
