# Bayesian-01 source dossier

Assignment: Bayesian foundations—posterior existence and propriety, conjugacy, decisions and prediction, hierarchical models, empirical Bayes, marginal likelihood and Bayes factors.

The current library already supplies a general posterior kernel for a proper prior and a dominated likelihood. The main new work is to delimit fixed-data posterior propriety, prove named conjugate updates with their normalization conditions, define prediction and posterior decision objects, and distinguish full hierarchical Bayes from empirical Bayes. The proposed Category 3 interfaces below use published mathematics/probability, the planned earlier MSF statistical foundations where appropriate, and earlier Bayesian-01 pairs. None depends directly or transitively on Category 4.

| **BCS-01 — Posterior propriety and normalizers**; `posterior-propriety-and-normalizers` / `posterior-propriety-and-normalizers-examples`; proposed interfaces `def-posterior-normalizer-and-propriety`, `thm-posterior-probability-when-evidence-is-finite-positive`, `ex-flat-prior-normal-mean-posterior`, `cex-improper-beta-zero-zero-posterior-diverges-at-boundary` | Define a posterior from a prior measure $\Pi$ and nonnegative measurable likelihood section $L_y(\theta)$. If $Z(y)=\int L_y\,d\Pi\in(0,\infty)$, then $\Pi_y(A)=Z(y)^{-1}\int_A L_y\,d\Pi$ is a probability; prove by countable additivity and normalization. For a proper prior and a normalized likelihood kernel $q(\theta,dy)=f(y\mid\theta)\nu(dy)$, Tonelli gives $\int Z(y)\nu(dy)=1$, so $Z$ is finite almost everywhere and the posterior is normalized on the prior-predictive full-measure set where $Z>0$. Keep the local existing filled-fibre version for $Z=0$ or $Z=\infty$ separate from a posterior for a specified impossible/exceptional observation. Give a flat-prior known-variance normal-mean example: for $n\ge1$, completing the square gives $N(\bar y,\sigma^2/n)$. Give the improper $\mathrm{Beta}(0,0)$ binomial counterexample: $y=0$ or $y=n$ leaves a $\theta^{-1}$ or $(1-\theta)^{-1}$ endpoint divergence. | Reuse `thm-bayes-formula-for-dominated-kernels`, `thm-conditional-density-formula`, and `thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces`, plus planned MSF-01/04; do not restate the general proper-prior kernel theorem. New proof obligation: extend the finite-positive normalizer statement to a sigma-finite improper prior and prove the two explicit examples. Sources: BDA §1.3 pp. 6–8 and §2.8 pp. 51–54; Bayes Rules §§2.1.3–2.1.4 and 2.3.4–2.3.6. |
| **BCS-02 — Conjugate Bayesian updates**; `conjugate-bayesian-updates` / `conjugate-bayesian-updates-examples`; proposed interface `thm-conjugate-update-for-exponential-family-kernel` with Beta–Binomial, Gamma–Poisson, Normal–Normal, and Dirichlet–Multinomial examples | State the natural exponential-family update only in the sufficient direction: for $f(y\mid\theta)=h(y)\exp\{\eta(\theta)^TT(y)-A(\theta)\}$ and a conjugate prior kernel $\exp\{\eta(\theta)^T\chi-\nu A(\theta)\}$, multiplication updates $\chi$ by $\sum T(y_i)$ and $\nu$ by $n$, **provided both prior and posterior normalizers are finite**. Give explicit proper cases: Beta$(\alpha,\beta)$ with binomial count $y$ gives Beta$(\alpha+y,\beta+n-y)$ for $\alpha,\beta>0$; Gamma$(\alpha,\beta)$ in shape–rate form with Poisson counts and exposures $x_i$ gives Gamma$(\alpha+\sum y_i,\beta+\sum x_i)$ for positive updated shape/rate; Normal$(\mu_0,\tau_0^2)$ for iid Normal$(\mu,\sigma^2)$ data with known $\sigma^2>0$ gives variance $(\tau_0^{-2}+n\sigma^{-2})^{-1}$ and the corresponding precision-weighted mean; Dirichlet$(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k)$ plus multinomial counts gives parameters $\alpha_j+y_j$, all $\alpha_j>0$. | Require BCS-01 and planned MSF-04/05. Proofs: multiply kernels, complete the square for Normal–Normal, and use the local Beta/Gamma integrals and Beta–Gamma identity; prove Dirichlet normalization by iterated Beta integrals. Do not claim the converse that only regular exponential families have fixed-dimensional conjugates unless its regularity hypotheses and proof are separately supplied. Sources: BDA §§2.1, 2.4–2.6, 3.3–3.6, Appendix A pp. 577–586; Bayes Rules ch. 3 §§3.1–3.5 and ch. 5 §§5.2.1–5.2.4, 5.3.1–5.3.4. |
| **BCS-03 — Marginal likelihoods and Bayes factors**; `marginal-likelihoods-and-bayes-factors` / `marginal-likelihoods-and-bayes-factors-examples`; proposed interfaces `def-marginal-likelihood-and-bayes-factor`, `thm-finite-model-posterior-from-evidences` | For a model $M_i$ with proper parameter prior $\Pi_i$, define evidence $m_i(y)=\int f_i(y\mid\theta_i)\Pi_i(d\theta_i)$. For finite model space and positive prior model weights $w_i$, if $0<m_i(y)<\infty$, prove $P(M_i\mid y)=w_im_i(y)/\sum_j w_jm_j(y)$ and $P(M_2\mid y)/P(M_1\mid y)=[w_2/w_1]\,BF_{21}$ with $BF_{21}=m_2(y)/m_1(y)$. Explain that a continuous prior assigns zero mass to a point null, so a point-null comparison needs an explicit atom/model indicator (or a different positive-mass hypothesis); for an improper prior, its arbitrary multiplicative constant makes the marginal evidence and generic Bayes factor undefined. Include the diffuse-prior sensitivity example rather than a universal BF threshold. | Requires BCS-01 and planned MSF-04. Proof: integrate the within-model likelihood under each proper prior and apply finite Bayes to the model indicator; ensure evidence is finite/positive. Algorithm for numerical evidence evaluation is not supplied by these sources and belongs with an earlier Category 3 numerical method if authored. Sources: BDA §7.4 pp. 182–184; Bayes Rules ch. 8 §§8.2.1–8.2.2. Do not absorb cross-validation or predictive model comparison here; those are assigned to Bayesian-06. |
| **BCS-04 — Posterior prediction and credible sets**; `posterior-prediction-and-credible-sets` / `posterior-prediction-and-credible-sets-examples`; proposed interfaces `def-posterior-predictive-distribution`, `def-posterior-credible-set`, `thm-posterior-predictive-moments`, and algorithm `alg-posterior-predictive-simulation` | For posterior $\Pi_y$ and sampling kernel $Q_\theta$, define $Q_y^{\rm pred}(A)=\int Q_\theta(A)\Pi_y(d\theta)$. For dominated conditionally independent future data, state $p(\tilde y\mid y)=\int p(\tilde y\mid\theta)\Pi_y(d\theta)$. Under finite second moments, prove $E(\tilde Y\mid y)=E_{\Pi_y}E(\tilde Y\mid\theta)$ and $\mathrm{Var}(\tilde Y\mid y)=E_{\Pi_y}\mathrm{Var}(\tilde Y\mid\theta)+\mathrm{Var}_{\Pi_y}E(\tilde Y\mid\theta)$. Define a credible set by posterior mass; central quantile intervals are one choice. State that credible mass is conditional on the prior/model and is not a frequentist coverage theorem. HPD regions require a density and may be disconnected or nonunique for multimodal posteriors. | Requires BCS-01/02 and the existing conditional-integration/kernel suppliers. | **BCS-05 — Bayesian decision theory and Bayes actions**; `bayesian-decision-theory-and-bayes-actions` / `bayesian-decision-theory-and-bayes-actions-examples`; proposed interfaces `def-posterior-risk-and-bayes-action`, `thm-bayes-actions-for-squared-absolute-and-zero-one-loss` | Given a measurable action space $\mathcal A$, loss $L(\theta,a)$ and posterior $\Pi_y$, define posterior risk $r_y(a)=\int L(\theta,a)\Pi_y(d\theta)$. A Bayes action minimizes $r_y$ only when the risk is defined and the minimum is attained; guarantee existence for finite action sets, or state the compactness/lower-semicontinuity assumptions for any continuous-action theorem. Under $E_{\Pi_y}\theta^2<\infty$, squared loss uniquely yields $a=E_{\Pi_y}\theta$; absolute loss yields any posterior median; finite-class zero–one loss yields a posterior mode/class of maximal posterior mass. Do not assert that a density mode is optimal for a continuous parameter under pointwise zero–one loss. Distinguish choosing a predictive distribution from choosing a point action under loss. | Reuse planned MSF-03 `def-loss-function-and-risk`, BCS-04, and the published L2 projection theorem for squared loss. Proofs: finite action comparison; expand squared risk into posterior variance plus squared distance; use the absolute-deviation/median split and finite-class loss sum. BDA §9.1 pp. 237–239 supplies the four-step decision framework (actions/outcomes, conditional posterior distribution, utility, maximize expected utility) but does not prove general existence of optimal actions. Continuous numerical optimization must be a Category 3 prerequisite, never Category 4. |
| **BCS-06 — Hierarchical Bayes and partial pooling**; `hierarchical-bayesian-models-and-partial-pooling` / `hierarchical-bayesian-models-and-partial-pooling-examples`; proposed interfaces `def-hierarchical-bayesian-model`, `thm-normal-normal-partial-pooling` | Under an explicitly stated group model, e.g. $Y_j\mid\theta_j\sim f_j(\cdot\mid\theta_j)$ conditionally independent, $\theta_j\mid\eta\stackrel{iid}{\sim}G_\eta$, and proper hyperprior $\eta\sim\Pi_\eta$, write the joint prior and posterior $\pi(\eta,\theta_{1:J}\mid y)\propto\pi_\eta(\eta)\prod_j g_\eta(\theta_j)f_j(y_j\mid\theta_j)$. In the known-variance normal random-effects case, $\bar y_j\mid\theta_j\sim N(\theta_j,s_j^2)$ and $\theta_j\mid\mu,\tau\sim N(\mu,\tau^2)$ imply $\theta_j\mid\mu,\tau,y_j\sim N(\hat\theta_j,V_j)$, with $\hat\theta_j=(\bar y_j/s_j^2+\mu/\tau^2)/(1/s_j^2+1/\tau^2)$ and $V_j=(1/s_j^2+1/\tau^2)^{-1}$. This is conditional shrinkage; if $\mu,\tau$ are unknown, full Bayes integrates their posterior rather than replacing them silently by point estimates. Distinguish current-group from new-group prediction. Finite exchangeability is a model assumption and does **not** by itself imply a mixture of iid distributions; de Finetti mixture claims need the infinite-exchangeability theorem and its conditions. | Requires BCS-01/02/04 and ordinary product-measure/Normal suppliers. Proof: factor the hierarchical joint model under the stated conditional independence and apply Normal–Normal conjugacy conditionally on hyperparameters. State hyperprior propriety and check posterior normalizers; BDA’s improper $\tau$ examples are a warning, not a general prior recipe. Sources: BDA §§5.1–5.4 pp. 102–119, §5.7 pp. 128–132; Bayes Rules ch. 15 §§15.1–15.5 and ch. 16 §§16.3–16.6. |
| **BCS-07 — Empirical Bayes and plug-in inference**; `empirical-bayes-and-plug-in-inference` / `empirical-bayes-and-plug-in-inference-examples`; proposed interface `def-empirical-bayes-plug-in-procedure`, plus a negative ANOVA moment-estimate example | For the hierarchical model in BCS-06, define marginal likelihood $m(y\mid\eta)=\int\prod_j f_j(y_j\mid\theta_j)G_\eta(d\theta_{1:J})$. A method-of-moments or marginal-likelihood estimate $\hat\eta(y)$ followed by $\Pi(\theta\mid y,\hat\eta)$ is a plug-in EB procedure. Contrast with full Bayes $\Pi(\theta,\eta\mid y)$, which retains $\eta$ uncertainty and generally induces cross-group posterior dependence. State no generic credible-coverage, risk, consistency, or dominance theorem for EB without separate assumptions. Show the normal one-way ANOVA moment estimate $\hat\tau^2=(MSB-MSW)/n$ may be negative; truncating at zero changes the method and does not prove $\tau=0$. | Requires BCS-03/06. Algorithm items can define method-of-moments and marginal-likelihood plug-in workflows; theorem items must separately state any properties. Sources: BDA §5.1 pp. 102–104 (plug-in hyperparameters, data reuse and ignored uncertainty), §5.3 pp. 108–113 (full posterior for hyperparameters), §5.4 pp. 117–119 (variance-component point estimate and limitations). Bayes Rules chs. 15–16 help explain full hierarchies but do not develop EB theory. |

## Source reading record and limitations

I downloaded the full 677-page PDF and extracted its full text with PyMuPDF 1.27.2.3. Complete units read: Ch. 1 §§1.1–1.3 (printed pp. 3–8); Ch. 2 §§2.1–2.6 (pp. 29–46), §2.8 (pp. 51–54), §2.9 (pp. 55–56); Ch. 3 §§3.1–3.6 (pp. 63–74); Ch. 5 §§5.1–5.4 (pp. 102–119), §5.7 (pp. 128–132); Ch. 7 §7.4 (pp. 182–184); Ch. 9 §9.1 (pp. 237–239); Appendix A §§A.1–A.3 (pp. 577–586). The chapters are an applied textbook: many claims are derived algebraically, but several details are assigned to exercises or standard-distribution facts. The local proof routes above supply exact integrability and hypothesis checks; the source citation alone is not a proof. The text extraction preserves PDF text and formulas but not the original figures as images.
- **Johnson, Ott & Dogucu, *Bayes Rules!*** The author site lists the 2021-12-01 web edition and a Creative Commons BY-NC-SA 4.0 license at `/preface#license`. Complete selected units read: Ch. 2 §§2.1.1–2.1.4 and §§2.3.1–2.3.6; Ch. 3 §§3.1–3.5; Ch. 5 §§5.2.1–5.2.4, §§5.3.1–5.3.4, §5.5; Ch. 8 §§8.1–8.3; Ch. 15 §§15.1–15.5; Ch. 16 §§16.3.1–16.6. Full chapter HTML and Pandoc plain-text extracts are cached and hashed. This is an applied textbook; only the optional Normal–Normal section gives a derivation, and it assumes the proper Normal prior/data model. Its plots are represented by captions/alt descriptions in the plain-text extraction. In the Normal–Normal numerical example, the prose once prints $\bar y=5.375$ while the data/formula use $5.735$; use only the general formula and independently checked arithmetic, not that typo. The book’s discussion of Bayes factors correctly flags zero prior mass under a purely continuous prior for a point null, but it is not a general proof of model-evidence propriety.

No claim below depends on an unread proof. BDA’s general claim that regular exponential families are the only families with natural conjugate priors (apart from irregular cases) is not proposed as a theorem: the excerpts state it but do not prove the classification. Prove only the sufficient kernel-update direction under explicit normalizability conditions. Similarly, no generic finite-sample calibration for credible intervals, arbitrary-loss Bayes-action existence, hierarchical exchangeability beyond stated models, or empirical-Bayes frequentist guarantee is inferred from these texts.
