# Learning-03 source dossier: neural architectures and approximation

**Scope.** This role covers feedforward-network definitions and approximation, quantitative ReLU rates and depth/region expressivity, convolutional and group-equivariant networks, message-passing graph networks, recurrent/state-space architectures, attention/transformers, neural operators, and physics-informed neural networks (PINNs). These are proposed local labels `ML-03`–`ML-09`; the scaffold author will renumber globally. This is source-based planning, not a claim that any new repository item has been proved or published.

**Coordination.** Learning-05 owns representation and generative guarantees. I retain architecture definitions, sequence/attention expressivity, and transformer limits here; move autoencoder, contrastive/self-supervised, flow, diffusion/score, and foundation-model guarantees to learning-05. Learning-04 owns training dynamics, optimization, implicit bias, interpolation, NTK/mean-field, and generalization; the pairs below make no such claims. `ML-09` PINN convergence is an idealized consistency theorem and not an optimization result.

## Proposed dependency-ordered pairs

### ML-03 — Feedforward networks and single-hidden-layer universality

**Pages:** `feedforward-networks-and-universal-approximation` / `feedforward-networks-and-universal-approximation-examples`.

**Scope and interfaces:** `def-feedforward-neural-network` defines a finite directed acyclic computation graph and the usual layered MLP specialization; `def-single-hidden-layer-network` fixes the affine offsets and output coefficients in
\[
x\longmapsto c+\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_j\sigma(w_j\cdot x+b_j).
\]
`def-activation-class-locally-essentially-bounded-ae-continuous` records the Leshno class: locally essentially bounded and with the closure of the discontinuity set of Lebesgue measure zero. `thm-single-hidden-layer-universal-approximation` states that the span of affine ridge units is dense in `C(R^d)` in the topology of uniform convergence on each compact set iff \(\sigma\) is not an algebraic polynomial almost everywhere. Separately state the paper's compactly supported finite measure result: for \(\mu\ll dx\), density in \(L^p(\mu)\), \(1<p<\infty\), has the same iff condition. The theorem is about existence of sufficient finite width, without a quantitative width bound.

**Proof route:** polynomial activations yield only polynomials of bounded degree and fail density. For smooth nonpolynomial activations, finite differences of translates approximate derivatives; a nonzero derivative of some order then generates all univariate monomials and Weierstrass gives compact approximation. For the stated rough activation, convolution against a smooth compactly supported kernel is approximated by finite Riemann sums of translates. If every such convolution is polynomial, Baire category gives a common degree bound on each test-function Fréchet space; the resulting distributional derivative identity implies that the activation itself is polynomial a.e. The dimension-reduction step uses density of ridge functions \(\operatorname{span}\{f(a\cdot x)\}\) in `C(K)`; Leshno et al. cite this step rather than prove it. The local item should discharge it using Stone–Weierstrass on polynomials plus polarization (each homogeneous polynomial is a finite linear combination of powers of linear forms), or mark that sublemma as an explicit proof obligation. Existing `thm-real-stone-weierstrass-general` and `cor-weierstrass-approximation-on-the-unit-interval` support this route.

**Boundary conditions and limitations:** the offsets \(b_j\) are essential in general (the paper gives the no-offset sine family, which remains odd and cannot approximate even functions). The topology is compact-uniform, not uniform over all of \(\mathbb R^d\). Do not state continuity as necessary: the theorem allows discontinuous activations under the exact measure-zero discontinuity-closure condition. Do not infer trainability or statistical generalization from density. Goodfellow's overview independently states the classical squashing and Leshno/ReLU universality results but does not supply this proof.

**Requires:** existing `thm-real-stone-weierstrass-general`, `thm-lattice-stone-weierstrass`, and/or `cor-weierstrass-approximation-on-the-unit-interval`; include the ridge-density/polarization sublemma locally if it is used. No Category-3 dependency.

### ML-04 — ReLU approximation rates, depth separation, and linear regions

**Pages:** `relu-approximation-rates-and-depth-expressivity` / `relu-approximation-rates-and-depth-expressivity-examples`.

**Scope and interfaces:** `def-relu-network` and `def-relu-linear-region` set the architecture and the connected activation-pattern regions on which the represented map is affine. `thm-yarotsky-fixed-architecture-sobolev-approximation-rate` covers the unit ball of \(W^{n,\infty}([0,1]^d)\) (all weak derivatives of order at most \(n\) bounded by one), error in the sup norm, and a target-independent ReLU architecture of depth at most \(c(\log(1/\varepsilon)+1)\) and at most \(c\varepsilon^{-d/n}(\log(1/\varepsilon)+1)\) weights and computation units, with \(c=c(d,n)\). `thm-yarotsky-adaptive-one-dimensional-lipschitz-approximation` records the distinct target-dependent architecture result: for a unit Lipschitz function on `[0,1]`, depth 6 and at most \(c/(\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon))\) weights, connections, and units. `thm-yarotsky-approximation-lower-bounds` separates (i) the \(\Omega(\varepsilon^{-d/n})\) lower bound under continuous selection of the fixed architecture's parameters, (ii) the fixed-architecture arbitrary-selection lower bound \(\Omega(\varepsilon^{-d/(2n)})\), improved to \(\Omega(\varepsilon^{-d/n}\log^{-2p-1}(1/\varepsilon))\) when depth is at most \(O(\log^p(1/\varepsilon))\), and (iii) the fixed-depth curvature lower bound \(\Omega(\varepsilon^{-1/(2(L-2))})\) for each nonlinear \(C^2([0,1]^d)\) target at fixed depth \(L\ge3\). `thm-telgarsky-depth-separation-l1` records the actual theorem's one-family separation: for every integers \(k,d\ge1\), there is a ReLU target with \(2k^3+8\) layers, \(3k^3+12\) nodes, and \(4+d\) distinct parameters whose Lebesgue-\(L^1([0,1]^d)\) distance from every at-most-\(k\)-layer semi-algebraic network with at most \(2^{k/(t\alpha\beta)}\) nodes, or every sum of at most \(t\) decision trees of at most \(2^{k^3/t}\) nodes each, is at least \(1/64\). Here the gate class has the paper's fixed parameters \((t,\alpha,\beta)\); ordinary ReLU is a special case.

For region counts, `thm-relu-linear-region-upper-bound` gives Serra–Tjandraatmadja–Ramalingam's exact upper expression for widths \(n_1,\ldots,n_L\) and input dimension \(n_0\):
\[
\sum_{(j_1,\ldots,j_L)\in J}\prod_{\ell=1}^L {n_\ell\choose j_\ell},\quad
J=\{0\le j_\ell\le\min(n_0,n_1-j_1,\ldots,n_{\ell-1}-j_{\ell-1},n_\ell)\},
\]
which is tight for one hidden layer. `thm-relu-linear-region-lower-bounds` includes the exact one-dimensional count \(\prod_\ell(n_\ell+1)\) for all widths \(n_\ell\ge3\), and for \(n_\ell\ge3n_0\) the lower bound
\[
\left[\prod_{\ell=1}^{L-1}(\lfloor n_\ell/n_0\rfloor+1)^{n_0}\right]
\left[\sum_{j=0}^{n_0}{n_L\choose j}\right].
\]
State also their comparison caveat: with input dimension \(n_0\ge Ln\), an equal-width deep network has fewer maximal regions than a single hidden layer of width \(Ln\), and the ratio tends to zero as depth grows. Region counts are a combinatorial expressivity proxy, not a performance, accuracy, or generalization theorem.

**Proof route:** Yarotsky approximates local Taylor polynomials on a grid using a partition of unity, then implements products by an approximate squaring network built from repeated tent maps; the number of grid cells gives \(\varepsilon^{-d/n}\) and the product/squaring precision gives the logarithmic factor. The adaptive one-dimensional construction caches quantized local residual shapes and changes the architecture with the target. Nonlinear-width lower bounds need continuous parameter selection; the other lower bounds use VC/shattering or restrict the target to a line segment with fixed curvature and count linear pieces. Telgarsky composes a triangle/sawtooth ReLU map to make exponentially many oscillations and bounds crossings of shallower semi-algebraic gates; the PDF was visually checked for the exponent in Theorem 1.1. Serra's upper bound recursively counts hyperplane cuts in the rank-dimensional image of each activation region; the lower bounds compose one-dimensional zigzags and a general-position final hyperplane arrangement.

**Requires:** `ML-03` network notation and the existing Stone–Weierstrass suppliers for polynomial approximation; local definitions of activation patterns/regions. Yarotsky's results concern approximation complexity, not learning algorithms. Do not claim that depth always increases the number of regions or improves every function's approximation rate.

### ML-05 — Convolutions and finite-group equivariance

**Pages:** `convolution-and-group-equivariance` / `convolution-and-group-equivariance-examples`.

**Scope and interfaces:** `def-discrete-convolutional-layer` gives local receptive fields, channel mixing, tied weights, and stride/padding; note that common libraries implement cross-correlation under the name convolution. `def-group-action-on-feature-maps` and `def-equivariant-neural-layer` define representations \(T_g,T'_g\) and the identity \(\Phi(T_gx)=T'_g\Phi(x)\). `def-group-convolution` specifies the group correlation on functions over a group. `thm-group-convolution-equivariance` proves equivariance by substitution in the group product. Cover translation equivariance of standard discrete convolution and the Cohen–Welling construction for the discrete wallpaper groups p4 (integer translations and quarter-turns) and p4m (plus reflections), with orientation-indexed feature maps and group convolution across layers. Pointwise nonlinearities commute with the group action; pooling without subsampling preserves equivariance, while subgroup/coset pooling may reduce it to subgroup equivariance or invariance.

**Proof route:** spell out the left/right action convention, then change variables in the group sum; verify equivariance for the first image-to-group layer and subsequent group-to-group layers. State where the finite-grid implementation inherits the group action. Exact equivariance assumes a compatible lattice and boundary convention (the paper models finite-support maps as functions on the infinite lattice); crop, padding, or stride can break translation/rotation symmetry. This paper establishes algebraic equivariance, not universal approximation or empirical superiority.

**Requires:** existing `def-group-action`, `def-equivariant-map-of-group-actions`, `def-graph-automorphism-and-group-action-on-a-simplicial-graph`; the local item must specify a discrete group/action and its boundary convention. No need to depend on a continuous-rotation theorem.

### ML-06 — Message-passing graph networks and the 1-WL ceiling

**Pages:** `message-passing-graph-neural-networks` / `message-passing-graph-neural-networks-examples`.

**Scope and interfaces:** `def-multiset` and `def-message-passing-gnn` define
\[
a_v^{(k)}=\operatorname{AGGREGATE}_k(\{h_u^{(k-1)}:u\in N(v)\}),\quad
h_v^{(k)}=\operatorname{COMBINE}_k(h_v^{(k-1)},a_v^{(k)}),
\]
and a permutation-invariant graph readout. `alg-message-passing-forward-pass` spells out the layer loop, aggregation, update, and readout. `def-one-dimensional-weisfeiler-lehman-test` states color refinement. `thm-message-passing-gnn-no-more-powerful-than-1wl` and `thm-injective-message-passing-matches-1wl` state Xu et al.'s two directions: any aggregation-based message-passing GNN cannot distinguish a graph pair that 1-WL cannot; with enough layers and injective multiset aggregation/update and injective graph readout, a GNN distinguishes every pair that 1-WL distinguishes. `def-graph-isomorphism-network` gives the GIN update \(\operatorname{MLP}((1+\epsilon)h_v+\sum_{u\in N(v)}h_u)\); the sum over bounded-size multisets from a countable feature universe is injective for appropriate scalar \(\epsilon\) (the paper gives irrational choices).

**Hypotheses and limitations:** finite graphs, countable input-feature universe, bounded neighborhood-multiset size in the injective sum construction, enough message-passing layers, and injective graph readout. The paper leaves continuous/un-countable node features for further work. Mean aggregation loses multiplicity information and max aggregation can lose set distinctions; 1-WL itself fails on some non-isomorphic regular graphs. Do not say GIN solves graph isomorphism or that a finite practical MLP automatically realizes every ideal injective map without the paper's hypotheses.

**Proof route:** induction on the WL color-refinement rounds. Injective aggregation/update preserves the exact multiset label, so equal node embeddings coincide with equal WL colors; injective readout preserves the multiset of final node colors. For the upper limit, prove by induction that every message-passing update is a function of the current 1-WL colors. The countable bounded-multiset sum encoding gives the constructive GIN witness. Empirical graph-classification tables are not theorem evidence.

**Requires:** existing group-action/graph-automorphism definitions as notation only; include a precise multiset definition and the countable/bounded-size argument in the pair. `ML-05` is not mathematically necessary.

### ML-07 — Recurrent, gated, and linear state-space sequence layers

**Pages:** `recurrent-and-state-space-networks` / `recurrent-and-state-space-networks-examples`.

**Scope and interfaces:** `def-recurrent-neural-network` defines a shared-parameter recurrence \(h_t=F(h_{t-1},x_t;\theta)\) and its time-unrolled DAG. `def-lstm-cell` gives input, forget, output, and candidate gates and the cell/hidden recurrences (Goodfellow equations 10.40–10.44). `def-linear-state-space-model` gives \(x'(t)=Ax(t)+Bu(t),\;y(t)=Cx(t)+Du(t)\), its bilinear discretization and the equivalent recurrence and convolution kernel \(K=(CB,CAB,\ldots,CA^{L-1}B)\). `def-s4-dplr-parameterization` and `alg-s4-state-space-recurrence-and-convolution` cover HiPPO's normal-plus-low-rank representation and the recurrence/convolution implementation.

**Exact proved algorithmic claims:** Gu et al.'s Theorem 1 places the stated HiPPO-LegS/LegT/LagT matrices in NPLR form, rank 1 or 2. For any DPLR state matrix and step size, Theorem 2 computes one discretized recurrent step in \(O(N)\) operations. Theorem 3 reduces computation of the length-\(L\) SSM convolution filter to four Cauchy multiplies with \(\widetilde O(N+L)\) operations and \(O(N+L)\) space, where the paper's Cauchy subroutine has stated exact-arithmetic and finite-precision alternatives. The appendices C.1–C.3 were read for these proofs. Do not extrapolate these kernel costs to full model training or claim a theorem about long-context accuracy.

**Proof route:** unroll the recurrence to identify the convolution kernel. For S4, conjugate NPLR matrices to DPLR form; use the Woodbury identity for bilinear discretization and matrix-vector recurrence; for the parallel filter, evaluate the truncated generating function on roots of unity, use the matrix resolvent and Woodbury identity to reduce to diagonal-plus-low-rank Cauchy products, then apply the Cauchy algorithm. The state-space equations and architecture are mathematical definitions; long-range-dependency motivation and benchmarks are empirical/contextual.

**Requires:** no upstream neural result; the pair itself should include the discretization and kernel derivation. Goodfellow Chapter 10 gives the recurrent/LSTM architecture; Gu et al. supplies S4. Backpropagation through time and vanishing/exploding gradient analyses belong to learning-04, so do not include them here.

### ML-08 — Attention and transformer architecture, universality, and limits

**Pages:** `attention-and-transformer-expressivity` / `attention-and-transformer-expressivity-examples`.

**Scope and interfaces:** `def-scaled-dot-product-attention` fixes \(\operatorname{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt{d_k})V\); `def-multihead-self-attention`, `def-transformer-encoder-decoder-layer`, and `def-positional-encoding-and-causal-mask` define projections, residual/feed-forward blocks, encoder-decoder cross-attention, positions, and autoregressive masking. `alg-transformer-forward-pass` records the ordered query/key/value, mask, softmax, value aggregation, residual, normalization, and tokenwise-MLP steps. Vaswani's complexity comparison is for dense full attention: per-layer attention cost \(O(n^2d)\), constant sequential depth and path length versus sequential recurrent processing; do not extend that to sparse/linear attention or include hardware claims.

`thm-transformer-permutation-equivariance-without-position` states the unmasked self-attention/tokenwise-FFN block is permutation equivariant. `thm-fixed-width-transformer-lp-universality` reports Yun et al.'s exact regime: for a fixed sequence length \(n\) and embedding dimension \(d\), continuous compactly supported permutation-equivariant maps \(\mathbb R^{d\times n}\to\mathbb R^{d\times n}\) are approximable in integrated Lebesgue \(d_p\) error, any \(1\le p<\infty\), by a composition of blocks with two heads, head size one, and tokenwise ReLU FFN width four; with trainable positional embeddings the corresponding result covers continuous maps on compact domains without the permutation restriction. The theorem as stated gives existence, not an efficient size bound; the proof's grid/quantization constructions can be exponential in the number of entries and accuracy. Yun's block omits layer normalization, which must be stated.

`thm-transformer-formal-language-limits` keeps Hahn's hard and soft attention claims separate. For fixed-depth hard attention under the paper's formal model, a restriction leaves a linear number of free input bits while the output depends on only a bounded number of symbols; the stated corollaries rule out PARITY and 2DYCK for unbounded input length. For softmax attention, the bounded-parameter, fixed-depth, smooth/Lipschitz positionwise setting gives an \(O(1/n)\) influence bound for changing one input symbol and cross-entropy lower bounds for specified parity/2DYCK next-token distributions. This is not an exact-classification impossibility theorem. Both limits are asymptotic and do not contradict fixed-length universal approximation with size/depth allowed to grow.

**Proof route:** derive permutation equivariance by permuting token columns through shared query/key/value maps and softmax; causal mask changes the allowed position action and is not permutation symmetric. Yun quantizes the compact domain, uses attention to identify contextual token configurations and a ReLU tokenwise map to select outputs, then integrates the grid error; positional tokens break the symmetry. Hahn's hard-attention proof counts dependence after restricting inputs; the soft result bounds one-symbol influence and compares conditional distributions/loss. Keep parameter sharing, finite sequence length, positional encodings, and the exact error topology visible in every statement. No empirical language-model performance claim belongs here.

**Requires:** `ML-03` MLP notation/UAT for the construction, plus local linear algebra and finite-dimensional compact approximation. Learning-05 owns the generative/representation guarantees; learning-04 owns optimization/training results.

### ML-09 — Neural operators and PINN approximation/consistency

**Pages:** `neural-operators-and-physics-informed-learning` / `neural-operators-and-physics-informed-learning-examples`.

**Scope and interfaces:** `def-neural-operator` gives lifting, iterated kernel-integral/nonlinearity layers and projection, e.g.
\[
v_{t+1}(x)=\sigma\!\left(W_tv_t(x)+\int_D\kappa_t(x,y)v_t(y)\,dy+b_t(x)\right).
\]
`def-discretization-invariant-operator-family` formalizes fixed parameters acting on refinements of the input mesh with convergence to the function-space operator; `alg-neural-operator-mesh-discretization` makes the Riemann-sum evaluation explicit. `thm-neural-operator-discretization-invariance` gives Kovachki et al.'s Theorem 8: continuous embeddings of the input/output Banach function spaces into `C` on the compact closures, continuous activations, and discrete refinement/Riemann-sum convergence imply discretization invariance. `thm-neural-operator-compact-uniform-universality` gives Theorem 11 on Lipschitz domains: input \(A=L^{p_1}\) (`1≤p_1<∞`), `W^{m1,p1}` (`1≤p1<∞`), or `C`; output \(U=L^{p_2}\) (`p2<∞`), `W^{m2,p2}` (`p2<∞`), or `C^{m2}`; continuous target operator; compact input set; and the paper's activation classes \(\sigma_1\in A^L_0,\sigma_2\in A_0,\sigma_3\in A_{m_2}\). Then some neural operator uniformly approximates the target over that compact set in the output norm. ReLU is in \(A^L_0\cap BA\) in the paper's cited lemma. If the output is Hilbert and the target is globally bounded, the theorem gives a globally bounded approximant under the additional \(BA\) activation condition. Include Theorem 12's `C^{m1}` input case only if defining the derivative-augmented input architecture. The theorem is an existence/density statement with no parameter/sample/mesh rate.

`def-fourier-neural-operator-layer` may define the periodic Fourier multiplier, mode truncation, and FFT implementation. **Do not attach Theorem 11 to the FNO:** the source explicitly says its generic-kernel theorem does not apply to the FNO formulation; the analogous Hilbert-space result is only cited there. Likewise, standard grid-tied CNNs and standard DeepONet branch inputs are not discretization-invariant without a modified function-space construction. The full-text source proves generic-kernel operator approximation by finite-dimensional factorization, approximation of functionals by smooth-kernel integrals, an MLP-like middle map, and finite-rank output expansion. It does not prove efficient rates or advantages from nonlinear operator structure.

`def-physics-informed-neural-network-loss` defines residual and boundary/initial collocation penalties (and the paper's Hölder-regularized empirical version). `alg-empirical-pinn-objective` specifies iid interior/boundary sampling and regularized empirical minimization. `thm-pinn-linear-elliptic-consistency` and `thm-pinn-linear-parabolic-consistency` state Shin et al.'s sample-limit results only under their full conditions: bounded Lipschitz domain and sampling densities/space-filling bounds bounded below; network classes `H_m` with PDE residual and boundary residual in the required Hölder spaces; for each sample size, an exact zero-empirical-loss witness with uniformly bounded Hölder seminorms; a global minimizer of the Hölder-regularized empirical loss with regularizers satisfying (3.2); and the classical PDE hypotheses. In Theorem 3.2, with \(m_r=O(m_b^{d/(d-1)})\), expected PINN loss is \(O(m_r^{-\alpha/d})\) with the stated high probability and residual/boundary residuals converge uniformly almost surely. The elliptic solution theorem is for a uniformly elliptic second-order linear divergence-form Dirichlet problem: \(a_{ij}=a_{ji}\), ellipticity lower bound \(\lambda_0>0\), \(a_{ij},b_i\in C^{1,\alpha}\), \(c_i,d,f\in C^{0,\alpha}\), exterior sphere condition, the displayed coefficient bounds in Assumption 3.3, and \(\tilde c=\sum_iD_ib_i+d\le0\); it yields almost-sure uniform convergence to the unique classical solution. The parabolic solution theorem has \(\tilde b_i=\sum_jD_ja_{ij}+b_i+c_i\), \(\tilde c=\sum_iD_ib_i+d\), a uniform ellipticity bound, Hölder bounds on \(a_{ij},\tilde b_i,\tilde c\) and their stated derivatives, compatible Hölder initial/boundary data and forcing, an exterior-ball condition, coefficient bounds, the sign \(\tilde c\le0\), and the paper's condition \(\theta^2a_{11}+\theta\tilde b_1\ge1\) for some \(\theta>0\). With \(m_r=O(m_b^{(d+1)/d})\), it yields almost-sure uniform convergence on the space-time cylinder. If each minimizer exactly satisfies boundary data (and initial data in the parabolic case), the paper additionally proves the respective \(H^1\) or \(L^2(0,T;H^1)\) convergence.

**Proof route and gap:** the PINN proof first uses a covering/space-filling estimate to control expected residual loss from the regularized empirical loss, then Hölder bounds and compactness to get uniform residual/boundary convergence, then a classical PDE stability estimate (maximum principle / energy estimate) to identify the limit with the unique solution. It assumes a global minimizer and an exact empirical interpolant with uniform Hölder control; the paper explicitly says there is no guarantee that gradient methods find global minima. Its proof cites external Schauder existence/regularity and variable-coefficient maximum principles. The repository's `thm-global-schauder-estimate-and-classical-dirichlet-solvability` has different nondivergence-form/injectivity hypotheses, and `thm-weak-parabolic-maximum-principle` is only for the heat operator. A locally proved PDE existence/stability lemma matching Shin's exact elliptic/parabolic sign conventions is therefore an open supplier/proof obligation; do not silently treat those current items as identical. A safe scaffold can state the source theorem with its full conditions and include the needed PDE lemma in this pair, or explicitly put that lemma in an earlier mathematical supplier plan. No Category-3 prerequisite is needed.

## Source limitations and remaining proof obligations

1. Leshno et al.'s ridge-function-density citation in proof Step 2 is not proved there. Supply a local Stone–Weierstrass/polarization proof or mark the sublemma explicitly.
2. The DeVore–Hanin–Petrova survey was read only in the indicated partial ranges; it is context/cross-check only. No claim in this dossier relies on its unread sections or proofs.
3. The visual PDF check resolved Telgarsky's exponent despite extraction loss: the correct gate-network size is \(2^{k/(t\alpha\beta)}\), not a polynomial bound. Preserve the gate parameters in the statement.
4. The neural-operator universal approximation theorem is for a simplified integral-kernel operator subclass. It supplies no explicit rates in parameter count, training samples, or mesh size; the generic-kernel theorem does not automatically prove FNO universality.
5. The transformer universal-approximation result is fixed-length, integrated-Lp and worst-case large; it is distinct from uniform approximation and from finite-depth/finite-parameter formal-language impossibility. The hard and soft attention limits have different assumptions.
