## 1. Exact integral lattice, with the boundary retained

Let $\Sigma$ be the actual compact connected sutured Heegaard surface, and let the compressed positive and negative surfaces be connected. Write $H=H_1(\Sigma;\mathbb Z)$, with its possibly degenerate algebraic intersection form. Let $A,B\subset H$ be the spans of the ordered alpha and beta circles. Supply the actual homology-product condition, equivalently the unimodular relative handle matrix $I=(\alpha_i\cdot\beta_j)$ proved in the earlier reduction. The restricted form on $A\oplus B$ has matrix

$$\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\-I^{\mathsf T}&0\end{pmatrix}.$$

This gives an **integral** direct summand. To see it explicitly, use $I^{-1}$ to subtract from any $h\in H$ the unique alpha/beta combination with the same pairings against every alpha and beta circle. The remainder is orthogonal to both. Thus $H=(A\oplus B)\oplus W$, where $W$ is this orthogonal complement. It contains the entire radical, including all boundary-circle classes. No quotient by the boundary radical is taken. In particular $A\cap B=0$ integrally, not just over a field.

Let gamma and delta be actual same-handlebody disk-system moves of beta. A disk slide replaces one basis vector by that vector plus or minus another; disk isotopies leave the classes fixed. Hence the homology spans of all three systems equal the **same integral submodule** $B$, with unimodular basis-change matrices. For $v\in B$ let $\beta(v),\gamma(v),\delta(v)$ denote the corresponding integral full-curve chains, using those three bases.

An integral region chain with a specified full-curve boundary, if it exists, is unique: the difference of two is an absolute two-cycle in $\Sigma$, and $H_2(\Sigma;\mathbb Z)=0$. It has zero boundary-region coefficient exactly as required for a sutured domain. Conversely, a full-curve chain homologous to zero bounds such a chain: it bounds in the surface's cellular complex, and its coefficient at each actual boundary component is zero because its boundary has no boundary edges. Thus for each $v$ there are unique domains

$$\partial P_{\beta\gamma}(v)=\beta(v)-\gamma(v),\qquad\partial P_{\gamma\delta}(v)=\gamma(v)-\delta(v).$$

The same assertion defines $P_{\beta\delta}(v)$, and uniqueness gives the literal chain identity

$$P_{\beta\delta}(v)=P_{\beta\gamma}(v)+P_{\gamma\delta}(v).$$

For any quadruply periodic domain, its boundary has an alpha combination plus a combination in $B$. Since $A\cap B=0$, the alpha combination is zero, and the beta/gamma/delta coefficients sum to zero in $B$. Therefore the full quadruply periodic-domain group is

$$\mathcal Q=\{P_{\beta\gamma}(v)+P_{\gamma\delta}(w):v,w\in B\}\cong B\oplus B.$$

Injectivity follows by reading beta and delta boundary coefficients. Surjectivity follows by subtracting this expression with those coefficients; the remaining chain has zero boundary, hence is zero. This explicitly tracks all punctures. A hypothetical additional boundary/radical relation would require a nonzero alpha/beta combination lying in $W$, contrary to the integral direct sum, or an additional absolute two-cycle, which the boundary of $\Sigma$ excludes.

The auxiliary periodic domains are genuinely represented by Whitney classes, rather than merely formal two-chains. For a handleslide $d\ge2$. In that case $\pi_1(\operatorname{Sym}^d\operatorname{int}\Sigma)=H_1(\Sigma;\mathbb Z)$. One direct construction is as follows. Moving a single point while holding the other points at a basepoint gives generators from the surface's disk-band spine. A loop of unordered configurations can be decomposed into these motions by joining its initial roots to the basepoint and letting the joining paths coalesce at the diagonal; the interchange of two roots contracts across the diagonal in a coordinate disk. These are actual homotopies because collisions are allowed in the symmetric product. Two one-root motions commute by performing them in the two factors of $\Sigma\times\Sigma\to\operatorname{Sym}^d\Sigma$, so the resulting fundamental group is generated by commuting spine loops. There are no extra relations: map the surface spine to its product of circles, inducing its homology basis, and send a configuration to the coordinatewise product of those circle values. This maps the generator loops to an independent basis of the torus fundamental group. Hence the claimed fundamental group isomorphism follows.

Now take the beta torus loop with winding vector representing $v$ and the reversed gamma torus loop representing $v$. Their concatenation is zero in that fundamental group, so it has an actual disk filling in the symmetric product. With the two boundary arcs on their tori this is a periodic Whitney class. Its domain has the prescribed beta/gamma boundary, hence equals $P_{\beta\gamma}(v)$ by uniqueness. The same applies to the other auxiliary pairs. If $d=1$ there is no handleslide between distinct disk curves; for the small-isotopy comparison the sole auxiliary periodic generator is explicitly the difference of its two bigons, which represents it. If $d=0$ the lattice is zero. Thus the represented/domain distinction is resolved in the exact auxiliary moves used here, while sphere classes are retained separately.

## 2. Compute the exact orientation-extension condition

## 3. Distinguish coherent compatibility from the auxiliary unit normalization

One cannot yet infer move inverses merely from section 2. The coherent beta/delta system forced there must have its top generator as a cycle. The following finite local count is the appropriate way to test that normalization.

In a small Hamiltonian beta/delta pair, each coordinate has two intersections and two empty bigons. Their signed counts are units. The top tuple is a cycle precisely when the two units cancel in every coordinate. The differences of these bigons are a basis of the beta/delta periodic lattice. Thus this is a specific orientation class, rather than an arbitrary choice among its $2^d$ characters.

For a beta/gamma handleslide pair, choose the local two-handle model: gamma-one is the band sum of beta-one and beta-two, gamma-two is a small translate of beta-two, and the remaining pairs are separate small translates. The slide band joins two different boundary components of the surface cut along beta. Since that cut surface is connected, cutting along the band keeps its exterior connected. Consequently all boundary/radical topology lies in the single exterior region with zero sutured multiplicity; the nonzero domains in the two-handle patch obey the same local region equations independently of the genus of that exterior.

Label the four active regions as $D_1,D_2,D_3,D_4$, with periodic basis

$$P_1=D_1-D_2-D_3,\qquad P_2=D_3-D_4.$$

For the top tuple $y^+$ and the tuple obtained by lowering its first coordinate, every domain is $D_1+nP_1+mP_2$. Its four coefficients are $(1+n,-n,-n+m,-m)$, so positivity leaves exactly $D_1$, $D_2+D_3$, $D_2+D_4$. Lowering the second coordinate leaves exactly $D_3,D_4$. These domain equations, rather than an inference from rank, isolate the actual unit-cancellation problem.

The first, fourth and fifth domains are embedded empty bigons, each with one regular strip modulo translation. The two remaining domains are annular matching problems. Cut the annular domain from a specified stationary corner by a length parameter $t\in(0,1)$. Uniformize its boundary components as concentric circles and let $f_1(t),f_2(t)$ be the angles of the two required boundary arcs. A holomorphic double-cover disk exists exactly when $F(t)=f_1(t)-f_2(t)=0$: the required annulus involution has form $z\mapsto c/z$, with $|c|=1$, and is unique when the angles agree. The linearized problem consists of the invertible normalized conformal problem and this scalar matching equation. Its determinant therefore differs from the orientation of $F'(t)$ by one constant sign on that disk class. At regular zeros the signed count is consequently the signed degree of $F$, up to that constant sign.

For one cut, $F(0)<0$ and $F(1)=\nu_1-\nu_2$; for the other, $G(0)>0$ and $G(1)=\nu_1-\nu_2$, where the two limiting annular angles are unequal after a generic choice. The endpoint signed-degree formula gives one family count zero and the other a unit. This statement concerns signed degrees: it does not falsely assert a zero-degree family contains no actual solutions. Hence the two annular signed counts have sum a unit, regardless of their individual reference signs. Choose the orientation on $P_2$ to cancel the two second-coordinate bigons, and on $P_1$ to cancel the first bigon's unit with that annular unit sum. The top generator is then a cycle. Spectator coordinates have the separate two-bigon cancellation. The reverse slide pair has the same construction with curve labels reversed.

To finish the normalization, use the auxiliary triple $(\beta,\gamma,\delta)$ in its actual small local position. If its top-to-top triangle count is a single regular product of small triangles and no other nonnegative index-zero domain contributes, its signed coefficient is a unit. Orient the triangle reference so it is $+1$. The signed triangle boundary identity then forces the resulting beta/delta top generator to be a cycle, because both input top generators are cycles. The characterization by the individual beta/delta bigon cancellations shows that the forced beta/delta system is the required distinguished one. Thus a **genuine top-triangle domain exhaustion** is still required at this point; the lattice alone does not supply it.

## 4. Signed quadrilateral boundary and actual inverse formulas

For the following identities supply the actual regular compactified zero/one-dimensional polygon problems on the chosen sutured multidiagrams, their stated admissibility/energy bounds and absence of all additional boundary strata. An accepted closed-diagram theorem is usable only when those diagrams satisfy its actual hypotheses. Choose corner orientation lines in homological convention, so the triangle operator $T$ has degree zero and the quadrilateral operator $H$ degree one. At an input strip end put the strip translation before the polygon determinant factor; at the output end use its dual orientation line. Contract each matching corner with its dual in boundary order. The finite determinant-splicing convention then gives the signed triangle identity $dT=T(d\otimes1+(-1)^{|x|}1\otimes d)$, and for the quadrilateral, on cycle inputs $\theta_{\beta\gamma},\theta_{\gamma\delta}$, gives

$$GF-U=dH+Hd,$$

where $F=T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(-,\theta_{\beta\gamma})$, $G=T_{\alpha\gamma\delta}(-,\theta_{\gamma\delta})$, and $U=T_{\alpha\beta\delta}(-,\theta_{\beta\delta})$. The two conformal endpoint signs are opposite because the oriented interval parameter for quadrilateral shapes has boundary $\{+\infty\}-\{-\infty\}$. Strip-breaking signs are the endpoint translation/corner contractions above; there is no characteristic-two cancellation in this formula. The auxiliary product $T_{\beta\gamma\delta}(\theta_{\beta\gamma},\theta_{\gamma\delta})=\theta_{\beta\delta}$ must be the actual normalized count from section 3.

If $U$ is an actual signed chain isomorphism, a left homotopy inverse of $F$ is $L=U^{-1}G$, with

$$LF-1=d(U^{-1}H)+(U^{-1}H)d.$$

Here is an explicit reverse-side construction rather than an appeal to equality of ranks. Choose a small translate eta of gamma, let $V:C_\gamma\to C_\eta$ be its actual small-isotopy isomorphism, let $B:C_\gamma\to C_\beta$ be the reverse-slide triangle map, and let $F':C_\beta\to C_\eta$ be the forward-slide map with that translated endpoint. The reverse-slide quadrilateral gives $F'B-V=dH_2+H_2d$. The endpoint-isotopy quadrilateral gives $VF-F'=dJ+Jd$. Both use the normalized auxiliary top products described in section 3. Consequently

$$FB-1=dK+Kd,\qquad K=V^{-1}(JB+H_2).$$

Thus $R=B$ is an actual right homotopy inverse. Left and right inverses agree up to the explicit homotopy

$$L-R=L(1-FR)+(LF-1)R=d(-LK+(U^{-1}H)R)+(-LK+(U^{-1}H)R)d.$$

Thus the construction produces honest signed inverse homotopies once its actual polygon counts and the two small-isotopy isomorphisms have been supplied. It does not replace an isomorphism proof by equality of mod-two ranks.

For a finite sutured small-isotopy complex with zero periodic domains, the usual area-triangular argument is integral as well. After identifying nearest generators, the actual small-triangle map is $U=D+N$, where $D$ is diagonal with entries $\pm1$ and $D^{-1}N$ strictly lowers a finite area order. This implies $(D^{-1}N)^N=0$ for some finite $N$, and

$$U^{-1}=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}(-D^{-1}N)^k\right)D^{-1}.$$

The finite signed expansion is an inverse over $\mathbb Z$. Its premise is an actual area order and actual leading-unit count; neither is inferred from the formal expression. All of these formulas remain on the full original integral/general-manifold branch.

$$\Phi(x)=\sigma(x)\,p^{-1}(x)$$

$$\Phi\,\partial_{\mathrm{top}}=\partial_{\mathrm{cut}}\,\Phi.$$
