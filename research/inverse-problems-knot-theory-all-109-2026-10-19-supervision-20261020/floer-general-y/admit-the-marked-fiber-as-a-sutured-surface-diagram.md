# Admit the marked fiber as an actual sutured surface diagram

New topological authoring segment, 2026-10-20. Compose this with the binding/bridge construction. The output is an adapted surface diagram of the actual exterior and its actual fiber; no old doubled-page intersections are retained or counted.

## Exact geometric problem

Let $(M,\gamma)$ be the meridionally sutured exterior constructed in the binding donor, and let $S=-F'$ be its carried, cooriented fiber with the sign needed for top Alexander grading. Its boundary crosses each of the two meridional sutures once. In particular its single boundary component has a nonempty arc on each of $R_+$ and $R_-$. The construction below gives a finite balanced diagram $(\Sigma,\alpha,\beta)$ and a quasi-polygon $P\subset\Sigma$, with $\partial P=A\cup B$, such that $\alpha\cap B=\beta\cap A=\varnothing$ and the rounded surface

$$P\times\{1/2\}\ \cup\ A\times[1/2,1]\ \cup\ B\times[0,1/2]$$

is equivalent to the actual marked $S$ through decomposing surfaces. The vertices of $P$ are on $\partial\Sigma$. The construction is allowed to change the diagram and its genus; it retains the exterior, peripheral marking and fiber isotopy class.

## Construct the middle polygon and its creases

Straighten the two meridional boundary annuli as products of their core circle and a height interval, with height increasing from $R_-$ to $R_+$. Along the longitude $\partial S$, the two crossing arcs are vertical in these annuli. Choose a narrow collar of $\partial S$ inside $S$. For the arc of $\partial S$ lying on the positive side, join its two suture-crossing points by an interior parallel arc; the region between the original arc and this parallel arc is a boundary bigon $D_+$. Do the same on the negative side, giving $D_-$. Retain the endpoints at the two actual suture crossings. The remainder $P$ has one edge $A$ adjacent to $D_+$ and one edge $B$ adjacent to $D_-$. Its vertices are those crossing points, and its orientation is the given orientation of $S$.

Give $P$ constant function value $c=1/2$. Parametrize $D_+$ by an interval across its collar so the function increases from $c$ at $A$ to $1$ at $R_+$, matching the already specified boundary height on the sides. On $D_-$ it increases from $0$ at $R_-$ to $c$ at $B$. At $A$ crease the surface so its positive middle normal points toward $D_+$, and at $B$ crease it in the opposite direction. These are the two local graph models of an oriented right-angle crease. On a small normal neighborhood of each bigon extend the function constantly in that normal coordinate; near $P$ extend it by $c+\varepsilon n$, where $n$ is its positive normal coordinate. The crease choices make these extensions agree on their overlaps, after using smaller collar rectangles and a monotone interpolation. In particular the gradient is nonzero there and has the specified coorientation through the middle polygon.

Extend this locally defined function, together with the sutured boundary heights, over the remaining compact manifold. Use any finite relative smooth handle presentation to obtain a Morse extension, taking perturbations supported outside the prescribed neighborhood. Such a presentation can be obtained from a finite relative triangulation by thickening its simplices in increasing dimension; rounding their collars supplies the local Morse handle charts. All choices here are finite. Call the resulting function $f_0$.

## Reorder handles and remove the unwanted polygon feet

Reorder the relative handles so index-one critical values lie below $c$ and index-two values above it. At the middle level this has the following explicit effect. Every index-one handle moved from above $c$ inserts a tube into that level, whose belt circle is an alpha curve. Every index-two handle moved from below $c$ inserts a tube with beta belt circle, as seen by reversing the handle chart. Minima and maxima crossing the middle add separate spherical level components. The actual surface $S$ is not discarded: intersect it with the new middle level, retaining the boundary bands. Its middle part $P'$ can acquire holes at the tube feet. The additional alpha belt circles themselves miss $P'$, and similarly for additional beta belt circles.

The following finite removal performs the required geometric repair. On each component of $P'$ choose a finite rooted tree from its nonempty $A$ edge meeting every component of its intersection with the old alpha circles. Push parallel alpha fingers along this tree to the root. After this, every alpha portion meeting $P'$ has an endpoint on $A$, with no closed alpha component confined to $P'$. The fingers are parallel inside narrow tree ribbons, so alpha circles remain mutually disjoint and miss $B$. Use a tree rooted on $B$ for beta circles. Its beta fingers likewise remain disjoint and miss $A$. In particular every component of $P'\setminus\bigcup\beta$ meets $B$: the beta arcs have both endpoints on $B$, and no beta circle remains in the interior. The corresponding statement holds with alpha and $A$ exchanged.

For a foot of an additional alpha-belt tube, choose an arc from that foot to $B$ inside $P'\setminus\bigcup\beta$. First slide every old alpha curve crossing this arc over the tube's alpha belt circle, using parallel copies of that arc. This clears the chosen corridor and preserves the complete disk systems. Then push the $B$ edge through the corridor and across the tube foot. In the actual manifold this last push is an isotopy of the negative boundary band of $S$: follow its negative gradient collar through the tube chart. Thus the surface remains equivalent as a decomposing surface, and the foot ceases to be a boundary component of its middle polygon. The push creates no beta intersection because the corridor misses beta. For a beta-belt foot, use the symmetric corridor to $A$, clear crossing beta curves by beta disk slides, and push the positive boundary band. Each operation removes one unwanted foot. There are finitely many, so the procedure terminates.

After this removal the middle polygon misses all auxiliary tubes introduced by handle reordering. Remove index-zero handles by a rooted spanning tree of the one-handle incidence graph, rooted at the nonempty incoming boundary; cancel each terminal zero/one pair in its actual handle chart. The manifold is connected to that incoming boundary, so such a tree reaches every zero handle. Use the dual rooted tree from the outgoing boundary to remove index-three handles with two-handles. The polygon and its boundary bands miss the auxiliary tube neighborhoods just cleared, so these cancellations can be made away from them. The remaining middle level and attaching circles give a relative diagram with only index-one and index-two handles.

Its two compressed boundary surfaces are exactly the original $R_-$ and $R_+$, and no closed compressed component remains. Since $\chi(R_-)=\chi(R_+)$, the two numbers of handles agree. Every component of either cut attaching surface meets the sutured boundary, so the diagram is balanced. By construction alpha misses $B$, beta misses $A$, and the rounded middle polygon with its bands is the original marked $S$ up to the explicit band isotopies. This gives the asserted surface diagram.

## What the cut represents

Replace $P$ by the two copies $P_A,P_B$, retaining alpha only on $P_A$ and beta only on $P_B$. This is the literal diagrammatic cut along the rounded surface just constructed: below the middle level use the $P_A$ sheet, above it use $P_B$, and on the boundary bands use the same collar-height coordinate. The remaining exterior of $P$ keeps its old interval coordinate. Locally these descriptions meet by monotone interval rescaling, so the cut manifold and its sutures retain their actual marking. Since $S$ is the carried fiber, the resulting sutured manifold is the marked product already supplied in the binding donor.

This is a topological identification of the cut manifold. It does not identify its possibly nonempty attaching-curve diagram with the empty product diagram by signed chain maps. Nor does it make the diagram admissible, make it nice, classify its disk counts, construct coherent integral orientations, or prove the product computation is invariant. Those are still actual analytic and move operations. In particular no new claim of one outer generator follows from this construction.
