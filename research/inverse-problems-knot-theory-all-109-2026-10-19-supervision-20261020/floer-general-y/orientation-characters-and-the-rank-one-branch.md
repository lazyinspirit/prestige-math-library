# Orientation characters on the exact rank-one branch

New construction, 2026-10-20. No canonical source or accepted supplier is changed or rechecked. This file concerns the difference between existing coherent orientations. It does not assume existence, coherence, or signed diagram invariance merely from orientability of individual determinant lines.

## 1. Derive the actual character group

Fix a finite transverse balanced sutured diagram $\mathcal H=(\Sigma,\alpha,\beta)$ and put $X=\operatorname{Sym}^d(\operatorname{int}\Sigma)$. Treat the empty diagram separately: its generator and orientation are canonical. For $d>0$, let $\mathcal G$ be the groupoid of Whitney-disk homotopy classes with its actual generators as objects and splicing as composition. Work separately on each connected component of this groupoid. Fix a base generator $x_0$ and reference paths $\theta_x:x_0\to x$ for its finitely many objects. Let $\Pi=\mathcal G(x_0,x_0)$, and let $\mathcal P$ be the integral periodic-domain group. Both endpoints and the ordered attaching circles are retained throughout.

The domain homomorphism $D:\Pi\to\mathcal P$ has kernel exactly the image of sphere insertion. Here is the needed topological proof. The coefficient of $\alpha_i$ in the boundary of a periodic Whitney domain is the winding number of its alpha boundary loop in the $i$th factor of the product torus; the beta coefficients have the same interpretation. This follows by taking a regular point on the relevant attaching circle: its signed boundary preimages count that winding, and are precisely the boundary incidence coefficient of the region chain. If $D(u)=0$, all these windings are zero. Both torus loops are therefore nullhomotopic, since a product of circles has universal cover $\mathbb R^d$. Cap them inside the respective tori; the capped map is a sphere in $X$. Removing the caps expresses the original class as insertion of that sphere into the constant Whitney disk. Conversely, a sphere has zero domain. Indeed its intersection number with $E_p=\{p\}+\operatorname{Sym}^{d-1}(\Sigma)$ is independent of $p$ along any path in $\operatorname{int}\Sigma$: the moving divisors give the homology between these intersection tests. The union of all coordinates of a compact sphere image is compact in $\operatorname{int}\Sigma$, so choose $p$ in a boundary collar outside it. That intersection number is zero, hence it is zero for every region test point. Thus sphere insertion lies in the kernel. No assertion that $\pi_2(X)=0$ has been made.

Write $\Lambda=D(\Pi)\subseteq\mathcal P$. Suppose $o,o'$ are coherent orientation systems, with the same determinant-splicing convention and the canonical complex orientation for sphere insertions. Their ratio $r(u)\in\{\pm1\}$ is constant on each disk class, multiplicative under splicing, and one on sphere insertions. Therefore its restriction to $\Pi$ factors through a unique character $\chi:\Lambda\to\{\pm1\}$. Put $\sigma(x)=r(\theta_x)$. For $u:x\to y$, the class $\theta_x*u*\theta_y^{-1}$ is periodic at $x_0$, and multiplicativity gives the exact formula

$$r(u)=\sigma(x)\sigma(y)\,\chi\bigl(D(\theta_x)+D(u)-D(\theta_y)\bigr).$$

Conversely every character of $\Lambda$ produces a coherent twist by this formula: the two reference-domain terms at an intermediate endpoint cancel under composition, and sphere insertions have zero domain. Thus, **if at least one coherent system exists**, their equivalence classes modulo generator sign changes are a torsor for $\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda,\mathbb Z/2)$. This is the exact group justified here. It is not silently replaced by $\operatorname{Hom}(H_2(M),\mathbb Z/2)$ or by all of $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal P,\mathbb Z/2)$; surjectivity of disk classes onto periodic domains has not been assumed. Different connected components of the disk groupoid carry their own such groups.

## 2. On the rank-one homology-product branch the domain group vanishes

Use the actual sutured intersection matrix $I$ in the preceding homology-product donor. Its proof derives $\det I=\pm1$ from an actual integral complex with homology $\mathbb Z$, without an $S^3$ normalization. Let $Q$ be any periodic domain, with

$$\partial Q=\sum_i a_i\alpha_i+\sum_j b_j\beta_j.$$

Intersect this homologically zero boundary with each $\alpha_i$. The alpha curves are mutually disjoint, so $I b=0$ up to the fixed overall intersection convention. The integer inverse of $I$ gives $b=0$. Hence $\partial Q$ is supported on alpha curves alone. Its coefficient function is constant on each component of $\Sigma\setminus\bigcup\alpha$. Every such component meets $\partial\Sigma$ by the balanced condition, and the coefficient there is zero by the sutured-domain condition. Therefore $Q=0$. This proves $\mathcal P=0$, and hence $\Lambda=0$ in every generator component.

It follows that any two **existing** coherent systems on this branch differ only by generator signs. The map $x\mapsto\sigma(x)x$ identifies their actual integer differential matrices: for an index-one class from $x$ to $y$ its count changes by exactly $\sigma(x)\sigma(y)$. This eliminates orientation-character ambiguity on precisely the homology-product branch required by Ni. It does not construct the first coherent system, signed continuation maps, or signed surface-decomposition maps. Nor does it justify using this conclusion before the top-group/decomposition comparison has put the actual cut-manifold complex on that branch.

## 3. Explicit ambiguity away from that branch

Take a flat torus $\mathbb R^2/\mathbb Z^2$, let $\alpha$ be $y=0$, and let $\beta$ be $y=\varepsilon\sin(2\pi x)$, with $0<\varepsilon<1/10$. Remove a small open disk centered near $(1/4,1/2)$, disjoint from the two curves and their thin bigons. The resulting once-punctured torus is a balanced sutured diagram: each nonseparating attaching curve has connected complement meeting the boundary. There are two transverse generators, call them $x,y$, and two embedded empty bigons $B_1,B_2$ from $x$ to $y$. The complement of these thin bigons is the single region meeting the puncture.

The periodic-domain group is $\mathbb Z(B_1-B_2)$. To see this directly, its puncture-region coefficient is zero. At either crossing the absence of boundary endpoints forces the two thin coefficients to be opposite. These are the only two remaining coefficients, and every such opposite pair has boundary a sum of the full alpha and beta curves. In particular the diagram is admissible. Every positive disk domain from $x$ to $y$ is $B_1+n(B_2-B_1)$, and nonnegativity forces $n=0$ or $1$. For the reverse endpoint order the analogous coefficients cannot both be nonnegative. With the integrable surface structure each bigon is a single translation class of regular holomorphic strips: a conformal map from the standard strip onto the open Jordan bigon is unique up to translation, and the linearized scalar boundary problem has its one translation kernel and zero cokernel. Thus these are exactly the two differential contributions.

Here the nontrivial periodic class is actually realized by $B_1*B_2^{-1}$, so $\Lambda=\mathcal P=\mathbb Z$. Given any coherent orientation system, write the two signed strip counts as $s_1,s_2\in\{\pm1\}$. Its complex is $\partial x=(s_1+s_2)y$, $\partial y=0$. Twist it by $(-1)^{n_p(u)}$, for a test point $p$ in the interior of $B_1$. Domain multiplicity is additive under splicing and zero on spheres, so this is a coherent orientation twist. It changes $s_1$ to $-s_1$ and leaves $s_2$ unchanged. Exactly one of the two coefficients $s_1+s_2$ and $-s_1+s_2$ is zero, and the other is $+2$ or $-2$. The two resulting homologies are therefore $\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z$ and $\mathbb Z/2$, respectively. They cannot be isomorphic.

For existence in this specific example, no free-abelian commutator compatibility is needed: the periodic group is infinite cyclic, the surface has no sphere classes, and the determinant lines of individual strip components are orientable. Choose an orientation for its periodic generator and one reference disk. Extend to their integer powers and composites by the actual splicing determinant maps, orient inverses so their product is the constant operator's orientation, and use associativity of splicing. There are no further group relations to impose. The same construction gives the twisted system above. This is also the genus-one case of the explicit two-disk construction in Ozsvath--Szabo Lemma 9.1, whose complete proof was newly read here from `/tmp/moise-fibered/os-foundations.txt`, printed pp.79--80. The present calculation exhibits the individual domains, the character, and both differential matrices; it does not infer the ambiguity from a count of possible conventions alone.

The example concerns a general balanced sutured manifold, not the irreducible homology-product branch of the Ni original. It therefore obstructs an invariance assertion independent of **arbitrary** orientation systems, not the original fiberedness theorem. The correct continuing construction must transport specified classes off the homology-product branch; on that branch section 2 removes the ambiguity. Canonical definitions remain untouched.
