# Signed exterior realization: cap barriers, area, and framed periodic classes

New construction, 2026-10-20. Start with an actual admissible balanced sutured diagram. The surface may have arbitrary genus and several boundary components, and its attaching systems need not be complete systems on a closed surface. The target below is closed only to obtain a compact analytic ambient space. No closed-manifold invariance assertion is imported for the incomplete attaching systems.

## 1. Construct the actual compact target and its area identity

Cap every boundary circle of $\Sigma$ by a disk, obtaining a closed Riemann surface $\bar\Sigma$. Retain a point $z_j$ in each cap and a small open collar of the old boundary in the cap-plus-surface. All attaching circles miss these collars. Put $X=\operatorname{Sym}^d\bar\Sigma$, with the actual $d$ of the sutured diagram, not the genus of $\bar\Sigma$. Its product tori are compact, embedded and disjoint from the discriminant. They have their ordered global tangent frames.

Choose positive region areas annihilating the sutured periodic-domain subspace. The finite positive-area separation argument applies exactly because admissibility excludes nonzero nonnegative periodic chains; it imposes no rational-homology-sphere assumption. Realize the areas by a smooth positive surface form: start with a sufficiently small positive background form and add a nonnegative smooth bump inside each of the finitely many regions to reach its prescribed integral. Extend it positively over the caps. Call the result $\omega_\Sigma$.

The product form on $\bar\Sigma^d$ descends to a smooth Kähler form away from the discriminant and to a continuous-potential positive current $T$ everywhere. To construct a smooth form while preserving the tori, use the following local smoothing. In a cluster coordinate disk take a smooth strictly subharmonic potential $f$ for $\omega_\Sigma$. The symmetric potential is $F(s)=\sum_i f(z_i)$ in the elementary symmetric coordinates $s$ of its roots. It is continuous and plurisubharmonic; on a compact cluster chart it is strictly so. Indeed the quotient polynomial map has bounded derivative there, so $\sum f(z_i)-\varepsilon|s(z)|^2$ remains plurisubharmonic for sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$. Positivity descends through the finite quotient, including its branch locus by continuity. Local differences of these potentials are smooth pluriharmonic functions: they are sums of real parts of holomorphic functions of the roots, hence symmetric holomorphic functions of $s$.

Mollify these strictly plurisubharmonic potentials on smaller charts. Paste replacements using a smooth regularized maximum, chosen to equal the larger entry when their difference exceeds its smoothing width. Strict positivity has a positive margin on each compact chart, so choose the mollification error and maximum width below that margin. Give each replacement a small positive offset on its inner chart and a negative offset on its outer collar; the maximum then selects the smooth replacement inside and the old potential near the collar. A finite nested chart cover of the compact discriminant makes these replacements smooth everywhere. Outside that cover leave the old potential unchanged. The resulting smooth Kähler form $\omega$ equals the product form on neighborhoods of **all** attaching tori; its difference from $T$ is $dd^c\psi$ for a global continuous potential supported in that cover. Thus the attaching tori remain Lagrangian. This construction keeps the actual target dimension $d$ throughout.

For a strip or polygon standard-holomorphic near the discriminant and with boundary on those tori, the area is

$$\int u^*\omega=\sum_R n_R(u)\int_R\omega_\Sigma.$$

Here is the relative, rather than purely absolute, reason. The incidence cover records the individual roots of the configuration; its evaluation to the surface pushes its oriented fundamental chain to the region domain, so integration against $\omega_\Sigma$ is the right-hand side. Off the branch locus this is literally the product-form integral. Near the branch locus the map is standard holomorphic, and the continuous symmetric potential pulls back with positive Hölder exponent; excising small disks there produces boundary integrals tending to zero. Stokes therefore computes the correction by $\psi$ only on the strip/polygon boundary and its ends. That correction is zero because $\psi$ vanishes on neighborhoods of all tori. The same argument on a sphere gives $\int v^*\omega=\operatorname{area}(\bar\Sigma)\,n_z(v)$ for any point divisor. It does not assert that all zero-domain sphere homotopy classes are trivial.

## 2. Impose every cap condition and exclude the extra degenerations

Use almost complex data equal to the symmetric integrable structure near the discriminant, near the finitely many point divisors of one test point in **each** complementary region, and near every point-divisor $E_p$ with $p$ in the cap-plus-boundary collars. Count only classes with $n_{z_j}=0$ for **every** cap. These conditions are additive under breaking. A divisor crossing in those standard neighborhoods has positive local multiplicity. The degree is constant as $p$ moves within a complementary boundary region, so zero cap multiplicity forces zero multiplicity for every collar test point. If a coordinate entered a collar, the standard-holomorphic incidence evaluation there would have positive local degree at some test point. Thus every counted map stays in the fixed compact subtarget with no coordinate in those collars. This gives the actual sutured boundary barrier, rather than merely placing one marked point on a capped drawing.

The domain coefficients are nonnegative by point-divisor positivity, with zero coefficient on all boundary regions. For fixed endpoints their differences are sutured periodic domains. The finite integer-domain bound follows from admissibility: an unbounded sequence of nonnegative domains, normalized by its norm, would converge to a nonzero nonnegative vector in the real periodic subspace; rational approximation in that rational subspace would give a forbidden integral periodic domain. Hence the domains and, by the displayed area identity, their energies are bounded.

Every nonconstant sphere bubble is excluded in the compactification. Its cap degrees are nonnegative and sum to zero, so they vanish. Point-divisor classes are homologous on the closed surface, so its degree at every point divisor is zero; the actual sphere area identity makes its energy zero. A tame holomorphic map with zero energy is constant. A one-torus boundary bubble has zero sutured domain by the balanced complementary-component argument already supplied. The relative area identity likewise makes it constant. These arguments treat the actual smoothing and its actual target, so they do not extend a zero-area statement to an unrelated background.

## 3. Supply coherent orientations with nonzero periodic domains

The previous determinant-splicing construction gives actual associative signed line maps using finite cokernel columns, cutoff/projection kernel maps and the exterior-algebra factor order. Individual disk components are orientable because the two product-torus tangent bundles have global ordered frames. To extend from the zero-periodic branch to the actual exterior, one additional commutator calculation is required.

Let $\Lambda$ be the actual image of the periodic Whitney group in the finite integral periodic-domain lattice. It is a finitely generated free abelian group. Choose a basis and represented periodic classes $p_i$ lifting it. All periodic classes have even index, by the actual endpoint intersection-sign parity identity at the same generator. The kernel of the domain map is exactly sphere insertion, with its canonical complex orientation.

The orientation commutator of two lifted periodic classes is positive. An explicit comparison uses their boundary loops in each product torus. Homotope those loops to their linear winding representatives in $\mathbb R^d/\mathbb Z^d$. Their commutator contracts through the square $(s,t)\mapsto x+s a+t b$ in that torus. The global tangent frame extends across this **actual** square. Cut the four disk operators and these two torus squares into common collar pieces. The constant real-boundary square operator has its canonical ordered $\mathbb R^d$ determinant factor; the matching real-fiber factors cancel with their ordered duals. The two ways to reassemble differ by exchanging the two disk determinant factors and by the capped sphere operator. The exchange sign is $(-1)^{\operatorname{ind}p_i\operatorname{ind}p_j}=+1$, and the sphere factor has its positive complex orientation. There is no additional framing loop: both torus squares were filled in their global frames. This computes the commutator sign, rather than assuming a free abelian domain lattice automatically splits an arbitrary sign extension.

Choose orientations for the finitely many $p_i$ and the finitely many reference generator paths in each Whitney-groupoid component. For a general class, subtract its reference paths and ordered powers of the $p_i$; the remainder is a unique sphere insertion. Orient using those factors and the canonical sphere factor. Any reordering uses exactly the positive commutator just computed, inverses contract to the constant orientation, and the determinant associator is already fixed. Therefore the construction is independent of the word and compatible with every disk splice. It supplies an actual coherent strip orientation system for this chosen admissible exterior diagram. Changing the periodic choices has exactly the character ambiguity previously derived; this construction does not assert all such choices yield the same group.

## 4. Realize the signed finite complexes and parameter boundaries

At this point the analytic problem has a compact target, compact Lagrangian tori, actual transverse generator ends, bounded energies, a compact boundary barrier, and no nonconstant sphere or one-torus boundary bubbles. Apply the compact-target Fredholm/regularity, compactness and strip/polygon gluing constructions to these **actual** operator problems, with the stated integrable collars retained. Completeness of a closed Heegaard disk system is not substituted for any of those analytic hypotheses: it is not used in the new area, barrier or degeneration proofs above. The remaining analytic input used here is the operator-level compact-target theory with these totally real tori, not the closed-manifold invariance theorem.

Choose generic actual strip data and polygon/parameter data inductively, preserving already chosen regular ends. The parametric regularity construction is applied relative to the prescribed ends and the frozen divisor/diagonal neighborhoods. ## 5. Align actual strip choices, including their generator jets

For two chosen tame strip data on the same capped target, first keep the same symplectic form and the same attaching tori. Join their almost complex data through tame data fixed on the divisor/discriminant neighborhoods. Such a path can be chosen pointwise in the Cayley ball based at a fixed compatible structure: the anti-complex-linear Cayley parameter has norm less than one, and radial contraction to zero followed by expansion gives the required path. The common protected values stay fixed. The data need not initially have identical generator jets.

Every constant continuation strip at a generator is regular. Its linearization is $\xi_s+J_{s,t}(x)\xi_t$, with the two fixed transverse linear Lagrangian planes and zero limits. The zeroth-order derivative-of-$J$ term is zero because the underlying map is constant. A kernel section has linear symplectic area zero: the standard linear Liouville form vanishes on either plane, and the end terms vanish. The equation gives $\xi_t=J_{s,t}(x)\xi_s$, so tameness makes this area the integral of a strictly positive quadratic form in $\xi_s$. Hence $\xi=0$. Its Fredholm index is zero by deformation to the constant-coefficient transverse operator, so its cokernel is zero as well. This is an actual kernel argument, not an assumption that arbitrary endpoint jet changes preserve regularity.

Use actual generic continuation data relative to these regular constant strips. The cap barrier, area bound and bubble exclusions apply unchanged. Orient their index-zero operators by determinant continuation from the chosen endpoint systems; the constant strip has coefficient $+1$ with the identity convention. Index-one continuation compactifications give the signed chain-map identity. For a reverse path, stretch and then contract the concatenation through a two-parameter continuation family. Its compact oriented one-dimensional boundary has the composite, the constant-path identity, and broken strips. The same determinant/corner orientation convention therefore gives actual chain homotopies $RC-1=dK+Kd$ and $CR-1=dK'+K'd$. Every map and parameter count in this argument is on the chosen sutured compact-target problem of sections 1--4, not a use of a closed-system invariance conclusion.

With an annihilating area form there is also a finite explicit inverse for the continuation matrix. In each Whitney-groupoid component assign a generator the area of a reference disk from a base generator. Periodic domains and zero-cap sphere insertions have zero area, so differences of those assigned values are unambiguous. A nonconstant continuation has strictly positive area and moves strictly in that finite action order. The constant solutions give the diagonal identity. Thus its matrix is $1+N$ with $N$ strictly triangular, and its integer inverse is the finite alternating sum $1-N+N^2-\cdots$. This supplements, rather than replaces, the parameter-family homotopy proof.

To align choices constructed using different positive surface forms, compare each endpoint at its **own** form to a sufficiently small perturbation of the symmetric integrable structure. For a finite list of forms, those reference perturbations can be chosen tame for all of their smoothed Kähler forms, since the common integrable structure has a positive tameness margin on their compact targets. At a shared diagram fix one such form and use the preceding continuation construction to compare the reference data there. This does not require an arbitrary original endpoint to be tame for a foreign form. If target complex charts are held fixed, the construction aligns the actual forms and generator jets needed at the junctions of a finite move word.
