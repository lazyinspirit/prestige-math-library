# Construct coherent orientations on zero-periodic sutured data

New signed construction, 2026-10-20. This concerns an actual chosen diagram whose periodic-domain lattice is zero. It applies to the actual product-cut diagram, and to the rank-one homology-product branch after the earlier determinant reduction. It is not a claim of signed invariance on every general sutured diagram.

## Analytic input and the new sign operation

Supply the actual strip Fredholm realizations with transverse ends, orientability on each map-space component from the framed attaching-torus construction, and the actual linear sewing estimates for sufficiently long matching ends. These are operator/gluing inputs, not integer count identities. In particular a finite cokernel can be killed by finitely many compactly supported target sections; the resulting onto operators have bounded right inverses, and sewing their kernels by cutoff and projection is an isomorphism for long necks. No orientation of that isomorphism is presumed. The following defines it explicitly and checks the needed coherence.

For $D_i$ choose an ordered finite-dimensional space $V_i$ of added domain columns making $\widetilde D_i$ onto, and write $K_i=\ker\widetilde D_i$. Use the determinant model

$$\det D_i=\det K_i\otimes(\det V_i)^*.$$

For a sewn operator append $V_1\oplus V_2$ in that order. Let $Q_T$ be a right inverse of the augmented sewn operator, and let $\operatorname{pre}_T$ be cutoff sewing on sections and the identity on the finite variables. Its actual kernel comparison is

$$G_T(k_1,k_2)=(1-Q_T\widetilde D_T)\operatorname{pre}_T(k_1,k_2).$$

The supplied sewing estimate makes this an isomorphism $K_1\oplus K_2\to\ker\widetilde D_T$ for large $T$. Transport the wedge orientation through this map, with all $K$ factors in component order and then all dual $V$ factors in component order. When moving $V_1^*$ past $K_2$, insert the actual exterior-algebra sign $(-1)^{\dim V_1\dim K_2}$. This defines the determinant-splicing map, including its sign. It is not obtained from an unsigned count.

The construction is independent of finite stabilization. Adding an extra column space $W$ changes the kernel determinant by the exact-sequence factor $\det W$ and adds its dual $\det W^*$; contract these adjacent factors using the ordered evaluation pairing. A finite block decomposition into the old kernel, its invertible complement, and the new columns proves this identity even when some new columns land in the old range. Passing to a common stabilization therefore compares any two choices. Varying right inverses causes no sign ambiguity: their space is affine, and the cutoff/projection maps remain isomorphisms for long enough necks uniformly on each compact interpolation path. A continuous nonzero real determinant has constant sign.

For three components, use disjoint matching necks and append $V_1,V_2,V_3$ once in that order. Both parenthesizations tend, in the common kernel coordinates, to the same cutoff-sewn direct sum $K_1\oplus K_2\oplus K_3$. Their relative finite-dimensional operator tends to the identity; hence for both necks sufficiently long it lies in the identity component of the invertible matrices. Both exterior-algebra reorderings also give the same final factor order, so their Koszul signs agree. This proves associativity of the actual determinant-splicing maps. The same argument works for any finite number of necks. Shortening a neck within a fixed component transports this identification by determinant-line continuation; it is not a new arbitrary sign choice.

For sphere insertion one can construct the needed determinant identification by compact operator excision, without assuming a regular holomorphic sphere gluing theorem. Choose an interior disk on each component, identify their collar bundles by complex frames, and deform their zeroth-order coefficients there to the same standard complex-linear coefficients. This is an affine deformation of actual Cauchy--Riemann operators with unchanged principal symbol and strip ends; determinant orientations transport along it. Cut across the common collars in the two pairs $P\oplus A_S$ and $P\#S\oplus A_0$, where $A_S$ is the closed-sphere operator and $A_0$ the trivial-bundle sphere operator. Rotate the two matching exterior traces by the real two-by-two rotation $R_\theta$, from identity to the exchange at $\theta=\pi/2$. Real multiplication by $R_\theta$ commutes with the common complex symbol. A collar cutoff identifies all transmission domains; after this identification the operator changes only by compactly supported zeroth-order terms. Thus this is an actual continuous Fredholm family, furnishing the orientation excision identification between the two displayed direct sums. Stabilizing by finite target sections gives its determinant model exactly as above. The sphere factors have canonical complex orientations: homotope their real-linear zeroth-order terms to a complex-linear Cauchy--Riemann operator in the contractible affine space of such terms. Its kernel and cokernel are complex vector spaces, and complex orientations cannot change along a complex-linear family. The factor $A_0$ is the complex matching-fiber factor, of real index $2m$.

For several sphere cuts choose disjoint collars. Their trace rotations commute, giving the same determinant orientation on either route around the parameter square. Exchanging sphere factors likewise has positive Koszul sign because their real indices and the real index $2m$ of each matching factor are even. Consequently insertion of spheres commutes with strip splicing, and successive insertions use the canonical complex orientation of their sum. This is an operator-orientation construction, not an assertion that a holomorphic sphere bubble or regular nonlinear sphere-gluing chart exists.

## Assign the orientations without assuming a general-Y normalization

Assume now the periodic-domain lattice is zero. In each connected component of the finite-generator Whitney groupoid choose a base generator $x_0$ and a reference class $\theta_x:x_0\to x$ for every object, with $\theta_{x_0}$ constant. Choose an orientation of each reference determinant component. These are finitely many choices. Their extensions over each component are well-defined by the supplied framed-torus orientability construction.

For any class $u:x\to y$, the composite $\theta_x*u*\theta_y^{-1}$ has zero domain because it is periodic and the periodic-domain group vanishes. By the explicit capping argument in the orientation-character donor, this composite is a sphere insertion. Its sphere class is unique: the two boundary caps lie in product tori, and $\pi_2$ of those tori is zero, so changing the caps cannot add a sphere class. Equivalently, the sphere-to-Whitney-class map is injective by this same cap construction. Therefore write

$$u=\theta_x^{-1}*S(u)*\theta_y.$$

Orient its determinant using the chosen reference orientations, the inverse reference orientation determined by the constant class, and the canonical complex orientation of $S(u)$. The splicing map constructed above makes this an orientation of the actual determinant component. There is no hidden orientation assigned by a formal sign of a domain: the domain is used only to identify the remaining class as a sphere, and the determinant orientation is transported through actual operator splicing.

For composable classes $u:x\to y$, $v:y\to z$, the two adjacent reference factors $\theta_y$ and $\theta_y^{-1}$ contract to the positive constant factor. Associativity and the even complex sphere factors then give $o(u*v)=o(u)\wedge o(v)$ under the actual splicing map. Sphere insertions retain their canonical orientation. This is a coherent strip orientation system on the zero-periodic diagram. A change of reference choices gives only generator sign changes, by the earlier exact character classification. Neither $S^3$ nor an absolute Maslov normalization appears.

## What follows for a supplied compactified strip problem

Suppose the actual zero/one-dimensional strip moduli for this diagram are regular and compactified, and the only boundary strata of the index-two translation quotients are broken pairs of index-one strips. The new coherent orientations orient those compact one-manifolds. In a gluing chart choose the global translation coordinate $s$ and neck separation $T$ so the two component translations are $s_1=s-T/2$, $s_2=s+T/2$. The Jacobian is $+1$, so $ds_1\wedge ds_2=ds\wedge dT$. After quotienting by the positively oriented translation, the endpoint sign of a broken pair is one fixed convention factor times the product of its two rigid-strip signs. That convention factor is the same at every broken pair in the coefficient under consideration. The signed boundary count of a compact oriented one-manifold is zero. Consequently every integer coefficient of $\partial^2$ is zero.

One-torus degenerations have zero domain by the separate balanced-domain calculation. They are absent wherever the actual zero-domain energy identity applies. Sphere bubbles must likewise be excluded using the actual analytic hypotheses, rather than by falsely asserting $\pi_2(\operatorname{Sym}^d\Sigma)=0$. The construction above is compatible with canonical sphere orientations but does not replace that exclusion. Thus this paragraph supplies the signed boundary identity once the stated compactification hypotheses have actually been met.

## Extend over a triangle with two zero-periodic boundary pairs

There is a further specific extension relevant to a move between two product-cut diagrams. Suppose a triple $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ has zero periodic-domain groups for the main pairs $(\alpha,\beta)$ and $(\alpha,\gamma)$. Supply a coherent system on its auxiliary pair $(\beta,\gamma)$, as well as the systems just constructed on the main pairs. Supply orientability of the triangle determinant components and the actual linear sewing problems at their three strip ends. Their determinant identifications are given the finite-stabilization sign convention above.

Fix any orbit of triangle classes under splicing boundary disks. Its stabilizers have no nontrivial domain relation: if periodic boundary disks $u_{\alpha\beta},u_{\beta\gamma},u_{\alpha\gamma}$ return a triangle to the same class, then their signed domain sum is zero. The first and third domains are zero by hypothesis, so the auxiliary domain is zero as well. The capping argument makes all three disks sphere insertions. The residual sphere sum acts trivially only when it is the zero sphere class: cap the three boundary loops in their aspherical product tori, whose second homotopy groups vanish. Its determinant action therefore has positive complex orientation, by the sphere excision construction. Thus no stabilizer can reverse a triangle orientation.

Choose an orientation of one determinant component per orbit and extend it by the three boundary-disk systems, using the actual splicing maps. The stabilizer calculation proves that this extension is independent of the representative. Associativity of the finite kernel sewing maps proves compatibility with all further disk insertions. The choices require at most Countable Choice: homotopy classes of maps between the finite CW models in use are countable, and the set of generator triples is finite. This constructs compatible triangle orientation systems under the stated actual operator-orientability/sewing inputs. It does not claim the auxiliary orientation system exists merely because the two main pairs have no periodic domains.

In particular this shows why the general-Y strip orientation-character issue does **not** by itself obstruct a triangle joining two homology-product cut diagrams. It also locates the next problem more narrowly. For the inverse-homotopy quadrilateral one must align the three auxiliary pair systems and auxiliary triangle system, not simply orient another isolated triangle component. A relation among those auxiliary periodic classes can remain even though every main pair has zero periodic domains. Such a relation is absent from the two-main-pair stabilizer calculation just proved; it cannot be removed by repeating that calculation for the two triangulations separately.

The present construction supplies coherent strip orientations on that cut diagram, and the two-main-pair triangle extension under its explicit auxiliary-system and operator inputs.
