# Admit the actual binding and fiber before counting generators

New topological construction, 2026-10-20. This replaces the unverified two-marking of the unchanged doubled-page diagram. It constructs a diagram of the actual meridionally sutured exterior and retains the actual fiber. It makes no claim that the resulting attaching systems have the old upper-page intersection pattern.

## Input and output

Let $F$ be a compact connected oriented genus-$g$ surface with one boundary component, and let $\phi:F\to F$ be an orientation-preserving return diffeomorphism equal to the identity in a specified boundary collar. Use the actual open-book manifold: start with the mapping torus of $\phi$ and attach $\partial F\times D^2$ so the angular coordinate of $D^2$ is the mapping-torus coordinate. Its binding is $K=\partial F\times\{0\}$. The completed pages at angles $0$ and $\pi$ form the doubled-page splitting surface $\Sigma_0$ of genus $2g$. Denote its two actual handlebodies by $H_\alpha,H_\beta$. Their complete disk systems are the doubled cocores of a disk-band presentation of $F$. All these statements refer to the supplied open-book model, not merely to an arbitrary diagram with the right number of curves.

The construction produces a surface $\Sigma=\Sigma_0\setminus(\operatorname{int}D_p\cup\operatorname{int}D_q)$, with two boundary components, and $2g$ complete attaching circles in each family. Its balanced sutured manifold is exactly $Y\setminus\operatorname{int}N(K)$ with two oppositely oriented meridional sutures. The original fiber, carried through the specified isotopy, is retained as a properly embedded surface whose boundary meets these sutures once each. Cutting along that surface is the marked product $F\times[0,1]$ with the return map retained on its ends.

## 1. Bridge the actual seam

Choose distinct points $p,q$ on the binding seam in $\Sigma_0$. They divide the seam into two closed arcs $a_\alpha,a_\beta$ with common endpoints and disjoint interiors. In a two-sided product collar of $\Sigma_0$, push the interior of $a_\alpha$ a small distance into $H_\alpha$ and the interior of $a_\beta$ into $H_\beta$. Use a positive smooth displacement vanishing at the endpoints; round in disjoint coordinate balls at $p,q$. The result is an embedded knot $K'$ ambient isotopic to $K$: in the collar, interpolate the displacement linearly from zero, and extend that explicit small graph isotopy using the collar's normal coordinate and a compact cutoff. Carry the pages and their normal framing by this same isotopy.

Each pushed arc $k_\alpha\subset H_\alpha$ and $k_\beta\subset H_\beta$ has an actual bridge disk: sweep its original boundary arc through this collar displacement. The swept rectangle, with its two endpoint sides collapsed and rounded, is an embedded disk whose boundary consists of the pushed arc and the original arc on $\Sigma_0$. Thus the arcs are boundary parallel in the specified handlebodies. This is an isotopy of the actual binding, not an identification inferred from basepoint positions.

## 2. Construct disk systems missing the bridge arcs

Here is the finite disk-patch operation needed on either handlebody $H$. Let $a\subset\partial H$ be its boundary bridge arc. Choose a smooth disk patch $D\subset\partial H$ containing $a$ in its interior, and choose a slightly larger disk patch $D^+$. Start with the supplied complete compressing disks. Select a point in $\operatorname{int}D$ outside their finitely many boundary circles and a small disk $D_0$ about that point disjoint from those circles.

There is an explicit boundary isotopy shrinking $D$ into $D_0$. Choose a disk coordinate on $D^+$, first carry its chosen center to the origin through a small coordinate flow, and then use radial maps $r\mapsto f_t(r)$ with $f_t'>0$, fixed near $\partial D^+$, shrinking the smaller disk into $D_0$. The increasing functions can be chosen by integrating a positive smooth radial derivative and normalizing its total integral; leave it equal to one near the outside boundary. This gives a surface diffeomorphism isotopic to the identity. Extend its time-dependent vector field to a collar of $\partial H$, multiply by a cutoff in the normal coordinate, and integrate it on the compact handlebody. This gives an actual handlebody isotopy with that boundary value.

Pull the old complete disk system back by its endpoint diffeomorphism. Its boundary circles now miss $D$, and its complement remains the original cut ball carried by a diffeomorphism. Choose a sufficiently thin boundary collar and straighten the disks there as products of their boundary curves. Their interiors away from that collar are compact, so the bridge disk and arc can be chosen in the collar over $D$, disjoint from every compressing disk. Equivalently, first make this collar choice and then perform the arbitrarily small bridge push of section 1. No endpoint of the final binding is moved by an unrecorded identification: the auxiliary boundary isotopy only constructs the new disks, while the bridge arcs remain the specified arcs of $K'$.

Perform this operation independently in $H_\alpha$ and $H_\beta$. Let the new disk boundaries be $\boldsymbol\alpha,\boldsymbol\beta$. Both systems miss small common disks $D_p,D_q$ around the endpoints, because their boundary curves miss their respective bridge patches containing both endpoints. Make the two attaching systems transverse by small boundary perturbations away from these endpoint disks; carry their disk witnesses at the same time. This preparation need not preserve any old intersections.

## 3. Drill and read the actual sutures

Delete a small product neighborhood of each pushed bridge arc from its handlebody. The two deleted neighborhoods fit at the endpoint disks to the actual tubular neighborhood $N(K')$ with the carried page framing. Each new complete disk system misses its arc neighborhood. Cutting its drilled handlebody along those disks gives a ball with a boundary-parallel arc neighborhood removed, hence a solid torus. This last identification is explicit in a bridge-disk neighborhood: a ball with the neighborhood of a diameter-parallel proper arc removed is an annulus times an interval, after rounding. The meridian disks that were cut remain the feet of the original $2g$ one-handles.

Consequently each drilled handlebody is the compression body obtained from an annulus times an interval by attaching $2g$ one-handles. Its positive boundary is precisely $\Sigma$; its negative annulus $A_\alpha$ or $A_\beta$ is the tube around its bridge arc. Compressing $\Sigma$ along the corresponding attaching circles gives that annulus. In particular every component of the complement of a single attaching system meets $\partial\Sigma$, so the diagram is balanced. This can also be read from the original cut sphere with the two endpoint disks deleted: it is an annulus, and cutting the attaching curves only adds the retained disk faces.

Glue the two drilled compression bodies along their original punctured splitting surface. Since the bridge isotopy and tube matching were explicit, the result is literally the exterior of $K'$, hence is ambient diffeomorphic to the exterior of the original binding. Its torus boundary is $A_\alpha\cup A_\beta$, joined along the two endpoint meridian circles. Choose the two sutured annuli as narrow collars of these joining circles. The remaining two annuli are $R_-$ and $R_+$. Each joining circle bounds a normal disk in the deleted tube at $p$ or $q$, so it is an actual meridian of $K'$. Their induced suture orientations are opposite. This proves the required peripheral marking; a longitude has not been substituted for a meridian.

## 4. Retain the actual fiber class

Carry a completed open-book page through the bridge isotopy and delete its boundary collar inside $N(K')$. The resulting surface $F'$ is properly embedded in the constructed exterior. In the carried page framing of $N(K')$, its boundary is the longitude with fixed normal angle. Each endpoint meridian circle meets it once, with the two sutured crossings having opposite boundary-region directions. Thus $F'$ is the actual decomposing Seifert surface, not an inferred polygon on $\Sigma$.

The complement of a binding tube in the original open book is the supplied mapping torus of the truncated page. Carry this model by the same ambient isotopy. Cutting at $F'$ opens the base circle to an interval and yields the marked product $F\times[0,1]$. At its two ends the original return map is still $\phi$; no assertion that $\phi$ is isotopic to the identity is used. The construction covers $g=0$ as well: the doubled splitting is a sphere, there are no attaching circles, and drilling the two bridge arcs gives the annular product diagram of the disk-open-book binding exterior.

## What this does and does not admit

This operation supplies the actual binding, peripheral meridian and fiber marking needed before an Alexander calculation can be made. It deliberately replaces the old attaching systems. The old all-periodic-domain sign proof and the old unique upper tuple therefore cannot be reused for this new diagram without a new argument. The next required operation is a genuine adapted-surface construction and signed comparison to the product diagram; a geometric diagram of the correct exterior alone does not imply the top Alexander group is $\mathbb Z$.
