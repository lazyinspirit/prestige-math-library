# New relative winding and separating-cut tensor operation

This continues the larger Ni analytic original. It does not inspect the completed forward theorem or its seven suppliers. Sources actually read for this operation: Ni, arXiv:math/0507440v2, complete Lemma 3.2 and Proposition 3.3; Ni, arXiv:math/0607156v4, complete §§4–5; Juhász, arXiv:math/0609779v3, the complete positive-finger normalization and the separating-diagram argument of Proposition 8.6. The signed analytic and nice-disk constructions already authored are used directly.

## Uniform relative winding, not a domain-dependent choice

Let R be a compact surface with a nonempty prescribed set of zero-multiplicity test points. Write its curve set as Ξ∪Θ, with Ξ={ξ₁,…,ξ_n}. Assume circles τ_i are algebraically dual to Ξ, geometrically meeting their own ξ_i once, and that those intersections lie in a connected open U containing a zero test point and disjoint from Θ. All winding corridors avoid the protected boundary marks. Over Q let X and Y be the spans of Ξ and Θ, and let K be the kernel of X⊕Y→H₁(R,∂R;Q), with the zero test-point normalization. A relative periodic domain is uniquely determined by its boundary in K: the difference of two fillings is constant on R and its value is fixed to zero at the test point.

Put X₀=proj_X K. Row-reduce a basis so ζ_i=ξ_i+Σ_{j>m}a_ij ξ_j, i=1,…,m, and choose a relative domain Q_i filling ζ_i plus a Θ-chain. A domain with zero Ξ-boundary is zero on U: its boundary misses U, so its coefficient is constant there and vanishes at the test point. This kernel fact is the required control of arbitrarily large Θ-components; a bound depending only on the Ξ-coefficients would otherwise be invalid.

Wind simultaneously along τ_i and a parallel oppositely oriented copy. Along one turn the successive strip coefficients differ by the ξ_i boundary coefficient. Therefore at the two exit ports the coefficient on Q_i is increased and decreased by the number of turns. Put those ports in U outside all other winding annuli. The off-diagonal coefficients of Q_j at these ports, j≠i, remain unchanged. Choose finitely many turns so the positive and negative diagonal port coefficients each strictly dominate the sum of the absolute off-diagonal coefficients in that row. If D=Σc_i Q_i+D_Θ and |c_l| is maximal, the two l-ports have opposite signs: the l-term strictly dominates Σ_{i≠l}c_i Q_i, and D_Θ vanishes on U. Thus every relative periodic domain with nonzero Ξ-boundary has both signs after one fixed finite winding operation. Clearing denominators shows that this single operation applies to the integral lattice as well; no rational coefficient change is made in the Floer complex.

The winding is performed on all parallel strands of the same attaching family. Since τ_i has zero algebraic intersection with ξ_j for j≠i, the other strands return with their original net jump and can be kept on parallel tracks. Thus the family remains embedded and pairwise disjoint. Dual circles are constructed by cutting along the independent ξ-arcs and the protected meridional arc: the cut surface is connected, so join the two sides of each cut arc by a path in the complement, and close it through that arc. Move its crossing toward the boundary U without crossing Θ. This is the actual geometric dual construction used on the closure surfaces below.

## Positive fingers and good surface diagrams

For a closed boundary component of a decomposing surface which is boundary-coherent in R(γ), choose a path from its exterior side to a suture. Push a short oriented boundary arc along that path and through the suture at a positive tangency. Attach the trace of this isotopy in a new boundary collar; collapsing the collar identifies the cut sutured manifold before and after the move. In the tangency chart the normal vector can become the positive surface normal, never its negative. Hence the outer vector-field condition is unchanged. For several parallel oriented curves use parallel tracks and the same positive tangency. For a homologically nonzero boundary curve, cut along it; both sides can be joined to sutures, and exactly one path orientation gives the positive tangency. Finitely many fingers make every boundary component meet both R₊ and R₋. This is a literal collar operation preserving the cut manifold and the outer set, not an arbitrary isotopy through negative tangencies.

For the resulting good surface, here is the relative Morse operation, without a fiber assumption. Remove narrow boundary fingers from S and round their ends so the remaining P has alternating crease arcs A,B ending on the true boundary. The removed strips on the A side run to R₊, and those on the B side to R₋. Set f₀=3/2 on P and prescribe its normal derivative so its gradient is never minus the oriented surface normal; in the strip collars extend the gradient to R₊ along A and to R₋ in negative time along B. Extend f₀ generically to the remaining compact manifold, fixing these collars and the boundary heights. Reorder its critical values by exchanging disjoint handle levels. The new middle surface is the old P surface with finitely many critical-handle tube feet. Clear those feet as follows. On each connected P component choose a rooted tree to A meeting every α arc, and push the α arcs along its corridors until each meets A. Do the same for β and B. For an α-belt tube foot connect it to B through the complement of the β arcs (every such component meets B), slide every crossed α disk over that belt, and move the B crease along the same corridor. This removes that foot. Use the color-reversed operation for β feet. All corridors are parallelized before the slides, so each attaching family remains disjoint. Repeat for the finite foot set; finally absorb the relative zero/one and two/three pairs by the rooted ball forests. The resulting balanced diagram carries the actual good S and its A/B creases. Cutting and doubling P literally cuts the prescribed gradient-thickened S.

To make it admissible while remaining adapted, choose a complete system of proper arcs in Σ\B relative to its true boundary, doubled into opposite parallel winding tracks. Every component of ∂(Σ\B) meets ∂Σ because B consists of arcs with boundary endpoints there. Intersection with these arcs detects every nonzero α-boundary combination in H₁(Σ\B); if a combination were zero there it would be zero in Σ, contradicting independence of the complete α disk system. Apply the port construction above in successive cut components, using a boundary zero region as U and making all Θ-only contributions zero on its ports. Every nonzero periodic domain has a nonzero α-boundary combination: if its α-boundary were zero, the balanced β-complement and its zero boundary coefficient would force the entire domain to be zero. Thus every nonzero periodic domain has both signs. All windings avoid B and carry P, so the result remains an actual good surface diagram. The completed permissible niceification then applies.

## All generators are outer for a separating balanced cut

Suppose the connected good S separates M into balanced M₁ and M₂. In the actual cut/double diagram let P_A lie on the M₁ side and P_B on the M₂ side. Partition its attaching families into α¹,β¹ and α²,β². Balance gives |α^i|=|β^i|=d_i. Project back to the uncut surface. An α² circle misses P because its permitted copy P_A is on side one; a β¹ circle misses P because its permitted copy P_B is on side two. Their images cannot intersect outside P either, since the projection there is injective and the cut components are disjoint. Thus there are no α²β¹ intersection points.

An inner intersection, if present, has type α¹β². A generator is a perfect matching of α and β circles. The number of α¹β² matches must equal the number of α²β¹ matches, by subtracting the d₁ row and column counts. The latter is zero, so every generator avoids P. This is the exact cardinality argument; it does not claim that individual inner intersections are absent. It also does not use taut nonvanishing to assume that an outer generator exists: if no generator exists, both sides are the zero complex.

## Signed cut comparison with nonzero periodic domains

Use the already constructed nice-disk exhaustion and literal local Fredholm conjugacies. They give a bijection of every differential disk with its cut lift. The remaining orientation question is not solved by asserting zero periodic domains. Choose the cut orientation character to be the pullback of the specified exterior character along the actual pushforward of periodic domains. The reference orientations are obtained by the same ordered real tangent caps on each copied intersection chart. For a loop of lifted rigid disks, the product of determinant-conjugacy ratios is the exterior character on its pushed domain minus the cut character on its original domain. It is zero by this pullback choice; the zero-domain sphere factor is complex oriented on both sides. A spanning forest of the finite generator graph now gives generator signs making every differential coefficient agree. Hence the full complex is integrally isomorphic to the cut complex with its specified induced class. This proves compatibility even if the cut has nonzero H₂.

For a separating open S, the homological pushforward is also concrete: H₂(S)=0, so the Mayer–Vietoris map H₂(M₁)⊕H₂(M₂)→H₂(M) is injective. The construction therefore retains all cut periodic classes; it has not quotiented out an invisible characteristic ambiguity.

## Actual tensor differential and the coefficient convention

The cut diagram is a disjoint union of the two actual component diagrams. Its generators are pairs. A holomorphic disk splits into the two target components. Under a regular product choice, each nonconstant factor has positive index. An index-one disk consequently has precisely one nonconstant factor and a constant factor on the other side. Order the real determinant factors with side one first. Passing the odd differential operator past the orientation line of the first generator gives

    ∂(x₁⊗x₂)=(∂₁x₁)⊗x₂+(−1)^|x₁| x₁⊗(∂₂x₂).

This supplies an actual integral chain isomorphism with the graded tensor product, including its Koszul sign. It yields Ni's homology tensor formula over every field. Over Z it instead yields the integral Künneth short exact sequence, with the Tor summands shifted by one parity; a plain homology tensor formula requires vanishing of those Tor groups. Finite free chain groups justify this directly by reducing each differential matrix to Smith normal form and decomposing into one-generator free blocks and two-generator multiplication-by-n blocks. Tensoring the blocks produces precisely the tensor and Tor terms. No torsion-freeness assumption is silently inserted.

For a horizontal surface, its class [R₊] gives the separating sides (the signed intersection of S with any closed loop is zero); its boundary and Euler equality make both cuts balanced. If S has several components, use the complementary-region adjacency graph and the same balanced matching counts, or apply the connected operation successively. In the usual two-piece horizontal case this is exactly Ni's horizontal chain operation. It uses neither a hierarchy nor a characteristic-product region.
