# Simultaneous move admission by positive area and local action balance

This is the new geometric admission left open in the nonzero-periodic orientation-transport construction. It retains the actual boundary radical. It uses the earlier local auxiliary models and signed exterior area identity directly; it does not reread or certify them. No claim of equality between arbitrary orientation classes is made.

## Keep a positive area form for the actual old pair

Start with an actual weakly admissible (Σ,α,β). There is a positive area form ω annihilating every old periodic domain: separate the finite region coefficient subspace from the nonnegative orthant, then realize the resulting strictly positive region areas by positive smooth bumps and a sufficiently small positive background form. Fix this ω. Since ∂Σ≠∅, write ω=dλ. For a periodic domain Q, Stokes gives

    area(Q)=∫∂Q λ.

This is an equality for signed two-chains, not a claim that every attaching circle is exact. Put A=span α, B=span β, C=A∩B as in the preceding construction. For u∈C the equality of α and β λ-periods follows exactly from old periodic area zero.

It suffices to construct γ and δ so that their λ-period functionals, expressed in the actual handle-slide bases of B, equal the β functional. Then every auxiliary periodic domain has area zero. Since the full triangle and quadrilateral domain groups are generated by the old C-domains and the auxiliary B-domains, every periodic domain of every required multidiagram has area zero. Positive ω then excludes a nonzero nonnegative periodic domain. This proves simultaneous weak admissibility, including auxiliary stabilizers, rather than inferring it from pairwise admissibility.

## An actual balanced slide patch

Choose the slide band thin, with its interior disjoint from the other β circles. Its ribbon neighborhood is the actual planar two-handle patch used in the auxiliary unit construction. The four adjustable positive lobes have periodic domains

    P₁=D₁−D₂−D₃,     P₂=D₃−D₄;

each spectator has P_i=E_i⁺−E_i⁻. Their span is all of the auxiliary B-domain group, not just the span of the sliding circle. This last fact follows also from the exact relation-group calculation for two copies of the same B: a domain is uniquely determined by its coefficient on one disk basis.

Construct the patch in area coordinates for the fixed ω. First make the band and all lobes so small that they lie in the chosen ribbon neighborhood. Prescribe

    area(D₃)=area(D₄)=ε,
    area(D₂)=ε,
    area(D₁)=2ε,
    area(E_i⁺)=area(E_i⁻)>0.

These conditions are geometric choices, not a change of ω. To achieve them, reserve one subarc on each lobe boundary away from the intersections and the band junction. A normal displacement on that subarc changes its enclosed area continuously with nonzero derivative. The reserved subarcs have disjoint supports. Choose ε and the band width small first, then tune the remaining lobe areas in these subarcs. The D₃ band contribution is positive and can be made arbitrarily small; tune it first and then tune D₄, D₂, D₁ in that order. None of these displacements changes the crossing pattern, the slide-band isotopy class, or the strict convex corners. Spectator lobes are balanced by the same one-variable adjustment. The outside component continues to meet the actual boundary of Σ.

Thus ∫P₁ω=∫P₂ω=∫P_iω=0. Equivalently, the γ λ-periods are exactly the β λ-periods in the slid basis. The old C-periods have not changed: neither ω nor the old α,β circles was altered. Even if the ribbon patch crosses α circles, this statement remains true; its construction concerns the β-family only and the old periodic domains are still the original two-chains.

Choose δ as the small normal graph with the three-port placement used for the actual auxiliary triangle unit. Balance each positive and negative normal-graph lobe in ω-area. Then the βδ B-domains also have zero area. By subtraction so do the γδ domains. The three-port inequalities and the strict unit triangles remain valid: the area corrections occur along reserved open sides, away from the three ports and corners, and can be made arbitrarily small. Hence the same actual unit calculation is available on these simultaneously admissible diagrams.

For an elementary isotopy, use the same construction with a normal graph over the original circle: one positive lobe, one negative lobe of equal ω-area. It is an actual Hamiltonian deformation. Indeed, the normal velocity v_t along the moving circle satisfies ∫ι_{v_t}ω=0, because its λ-period is constant. Hence ι_{v_t}ω is the differential of a single-valued function on that circle. Extend that function to a thin annulus and cut it off outside; its Hamiltonian field has precisely the prescribed normal component. Different circles use disjoint annuli.

## Reaching prescribed diagrams rather than only specially balanced representatives

A prescribed disk slide is represented by a slide band and a sufficiently small push-off; the balanced representative above uses that same band. Any other representative with the same band is isotopic to it. Thus it remains to realize a prescribed isotopy, not to assume that every representative is already action balanced.

Put the finite isotopy of one attaching family in generic position relative to the other. Its exceptional times are isolated ordinary tangencies creating or deleting a pair of intersections; between them the colored transverse curve graph has constant combinatorics. A compact isotopy can be approximated with finitely many such events. On an interval without an event, extend the velocity of the colored graph to an ambient vector field: at a crossing use the two transverse tangents as a basis, assigning their prescribed crossing-point velocity, then extend on the edges and patch in the complementary regions. Its flow carries the diagram exactly and carries the other family setwise. The induced surface diffeomorphism gives literal conjugacy of symmetric-product maps, operators, orientations, and domains after transporting J and ω. This step has no Hamiltonian or flux assumption.

At an exceptional event use its small disk normal form. Realize the creation/deletion by a sufficiently small normal bump of the moving circle. Add an opposite signed-area compensating bump on a different open subarc disjoint from the stationary family and every marked point. Such an arc exists because the intersection set is finite. Make the event disk small enough that its area is less than the capacity of the compensating strip. Vary that strip continuously so the total λ-period remains constant at every time. The compensating bump creates no intersection with the stationary family. Join the positive and negative graph portions with exactly two simple zeros. The resulting elementary move is Hamiltonian by the preceding primitive construction, and has exactly the required tangency event. Its endpoint and the prescribed local endpoint have the same colored graph, so the ambient-conjugacy step joins them.

This decomposes any prescribed finite isotopy into literal conjugacies and the admitted balanced elementary isotopies. Apply it also to the isotopy from a prescribed slide representative to the balanced representative of its band. No assertion that arbitrary non-Hamiltonian Lagrangian isotopies preserve Floer homology has been used.

## Actual consequence for the whole move word

For each balanced elementary move, the complete finite triangle/quad configurations are weakly admissible by the fixed-positive-area argument. The signed exterior construction supplies their compactification. The transported C-character and the explicit K₄ extension supply their compatible signs. The actual auxiliary triangle is a unit, so the signed inverse and quadrilateral homotopies already constructed apply. Literal conjugacies transport the specified character. Ordinary stabilization is first converted by the relative geometry to the true boundary region and is then the literal old complex times its single invertible constant factor.

Consequently the relative compressionbody move word yields integral chain homotopy equivalences for the specified transported orientation class on general balanced sutured diagrams. The argument does not say that other C-characters produce the same homology. On the product/homology-product branch C=0, the character ambiguity disappears as previously proved. This supplies the new exterior move-admission operation required to use the nice cut diagram as an actual integral invariant, rather than only as a favorable chosen diagram.
