# Signed local disk transfer under a supplied sutured surface cut

Research donor, 2026-10-20. This is a local operation, not integral sutured invariance or the full surface-decomposition theorem. No canonical interface is changed.

## Precise input and conclusion

Let $(\Sigma,\alpha,\beta,P)$ be a balanced surface diagram with $\partial P=A\cup B$, $\alpha\cap B=\beta\cap A=\varnothing$, as in Juhasz Definition 4.3. Form $\Sigma'$ by replacing $P$ with copies $P_A,P_B$, attaching the exterior of $P$ to $P_A$ along $A$ and to $P_B$ along $B$. Lift alpha curves to $P_A$ and beta curves to $P_B$. Write $p:\Sigma'\to\Sigma$ for the resulting map, a local diffeomorphism on the interior. Equip $\Sigma'$ with the pulled-back complex structure.

An **outer generator** has no coordinate in $P$. Its unique preimage is a generator upstairs. Conversely every generator upstairs avoids both copies of $P$: an intersection in $P_A$ cannot lie on a beta curve, and an intersection in $P_B$ cannot lie on an alpha curve. Thus $p$ gives a bijection of the upstairs generators with the outer generators, with the original ordered attaching-curve labels retained.

Consider an embedded empty bigon or rectangle $D$ between outer generators. Assume every connected component $C$ of $D\cap P$ has its boundary entirely in $(\bigcup\alpha)\cup A$ or entirely in $(\bigcup\beta)\cup B$, and in the former case meets $A$, in the latter meets $B$. This is an explicit hypothesis here; its derivation for all differential domains in a good nice diagram is a separate operation. Then:

* $D$ has exactly one lift $D'$ with zero multiplicity at the new boundary of $\Sigma'$. It is an embedded empty bigon or rectangle.
* For an actual regular holomorphic strip supported on this disk, with unchanged stationary coordinates, the lifted strip has a canonically conjugate Fredholm operator. Transporting the determinant orientation through that conjugacy preserves its signed count, including the orientation of the real translation orbit.
* Consequently, if the entire outer differential is already known to consist of these disks and their lifts, its integer differential matrix is identical to the upstairs matrix when the upstairs disk orientations are transported from downstairs. This conclusion is about these matrices; it does not assert a global coherent orientation system on all upstairs disk classes or any diagram-change map.

The assertion applies to any ambient three-manifold and uses integers throughout.

## Proof of the operation

Outside $P$, the lift of $D$ is forced because $p$ is one-to-one. Lift an alpha-type component $C$ of $D\cap P$ into $P_A$ and a beta-type component into $P_B$. Along an $A$ edge the unique exterior sheet attaches to $P_A$, and along a $B$ edge it attaches to $P_B$, so these prescriptions agree on every seam. They define a continuous map $h:D\to\Sigma'$ satisfying $p h=\operatorname{id}_D$. Since $p$ is a local diffeomorphism at every image point, $h$ is smooth and conformal after rounding domain corners. It is injective because $p h$ is the identity. Its boundary alpha and beta arcs lie on the specified lifted attaching curves. Its corner generators lift uniquely, and its interior contains no generator coordinate because $D$ is empty.

Uniqueness uses the new boundary condition, not a drawing. A lift of an alpha-type component into $P_B$ would place its nonempty $A$ edge on $\partial\Sigma'$; its coefficient would be one at that boundary, contrary to the defining condition for a sutured disk domain. A beta-type component is excluded from $P_A$ in the same way. Since each $C$ is connected, it cannot switch between the two disjoint copies. All components and the exterior portion are therefore forced. The same reasoning applies to a positive upstairs disk whose pushforward is the indicated embedded disk; no two distinct positive sheets can occur over one point because that pushforward has multiplicity one.

Here are the analytic identifications actually needed for the sign statement. The compact disk $h(D)$ is disjoint from $\partial\Sigma'$. The local inverses of $p$ chosen by $h$ agree on overlaps; by compactness they extend $h$ to a neighborhood of $D$ after shrinking it. For a bigon the moving coordinate lies in that neighborhood. For a rectangle the two moving coordinates lie there, and the induced map on their symmetric square is holomorphic also across its diagonal: in local complex coordinates this follows by expressing unordered pairs through their elementary symmetric functions. All stationary coordinates have disjoint small neighborhoods on which the unique chosen inverse of $p$ is defined. Emptiness and the attaching-curve labels exclude collisions with a stationary coordinate. Taking the corresponding symmetric-product neighborhoods gives a biholomorphism $H$ near the entire strip image and the constant coordinates. It takes the lifted attaching-torus boundary conditions to the original ones and preserves their order.

For the pulled-back almost complex structure, composition with $H$ conjugates the nonlinear Cauchy--Riemann sections. At a zero its derivative $dH$ therefore conjugates the linearized operators. On Sobolev spaces along the strip, these multiplication operators and their inverses are bounded: their coefficients are smooth on the compact disk neighborhoods, and at either strip end they converge to the fixed derivative at the corresponding transverse intersection. Thus this is an isomorphism of the actual Fredholm problems, not just equality of their indices. It induces maps on kernel and cokernel, and hence a canonical map on

$$\det D_u=\Lambda^{\max}\ker D_u\otimes(\Lambda^{\max}\operatorname{coker}D_u)^*.$$

Assign the lifted determinant line the pullback of the given downstairs orientation. Regularity is preserved. Composition with $H$ commutes with translation in the source variable, so $dH(\partial_su')=\partial_su$. Quotient orientations on zero-dimensional translation quotients therefore agree. This proves equality of the signed contributions. Summing the contributions proves the matrix assertion whenever its stated exhaustive-disk hypothesis holds. No choice of signs modulo two has been substituted for this orientation transport.

For completeness, admissibility also transfers in the required direction without an orientation argument. If an upstairs periodic domain $Q'$ has all coefficients nonnegative, its pushforward $Q=p_*Q'$ is a downstairs periodic domain with nonnegative coefficients. An admissible downstairs diagram forces $Q=0$. Each coefficient of $Q$ is the sum of one or two nonnegative coefficients of $Q'$, so all coefficients of $Q'$ vanish. Apply the same argument to $-Q'$ to exclude nonpositive nonzero periodic domains. This proves upstairs admissibility from downstairs admissibility. It does not prove that the initial diagram can be made admissible by permissible moves.

The operation deliberately requires a supplied signed downstairs strip problem. It cannot create coherent orientations merely by declaring the upstairs individual signs equal: other homotopy classes, higher-index moduli, gluing, and diagram changes still require construction. In particular this donor alone does not establish the integral $SFH$ used in the repository's Alexander-grading definition.
