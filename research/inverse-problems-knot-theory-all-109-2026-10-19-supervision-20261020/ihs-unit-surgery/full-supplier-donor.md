# Integral homology spheres are connected by preferred unit knot surgeries

## Statement

Every closed connected oriented integral homology three-sphere Y can be obtained from S³ by a finite sequence of knot surgeries of coefficient +1 or -1 relative to the preferred Seifert framing in the current manifold. Every intermediate manifold is an integral homology sphere. Reversing the sequence has the same property.

More precisely, if a framed-link presentation of Y has symmetric integral surgery matrix A, then A⊕〈1,-1〉 is integrally congruent to a signed-unit diagonal matrix. Thus adding just one isolated +1 and one isolated -1 unknot, followed by finitely many handle slides, supplies a presentation whose components can be surgered in any order to give the required sequence.

## Proof

1.1 Let L be an integral lattice with nonsingular symmetric Gram matrix of determinant ±1. For an odd prime p, its extension to Z_p has an orthogonal basis of unit-norm vectors. Indeed, if a diagonal entry is a unit use that basis vector. If all diagonal entries vanish modulo p, unimodularity gives a unit off-diagonal entry, and the sum of its two basis vectors has unit norm, since 2 is a unit. Orthogonally project using division by that unit and continue in its unimodular complement. In the first three unit coordinates over F_p, write the norm as ax²+by²+cz². The sets {ax²:x∈F_p} and {-c-by²:y∈F_p} each have (p+1)/2 elements, so they intersect. Their common value gives a zero with z=1. Its gradient has a nonzero coordinate. Correct that coordinate successively modulo p²,p³,…: if q(x) is zero modulo p^k, adding p^k t in a coordinate with unit gradient changes the next residue by an arbitrary multiple of p^k. The corrections converge in Z_p, retaining a nonzero residue vector and giving a nontrivial p-adic zero. Thus every unimodular integral form of rank at least three is isotropic at every odd prime. [construct, algebra]

1.2 Here is the full 2-adic argument needed in rank at least five. If L over Z₂ is even, q/2 is an integral polynomial whose reduction Q modulo2 has polar form equal to the unimodular alternating form of L/2L. In n≥3 variables a polynomial over F₂ of degree at most2 has an even number of zeros: sum 1+Q(x) over all 2^n inputs, and each monomial misses a variable, so its sum is zero modulo2. Since zero is one zero, there is a nonzero zero x. Nondegeneracy of the polar form makes some derivative of Q at x a unit. The one-coordinate lifting construction just given, now for the integral polynomial q/2 and p=2, produces a primitive zero in Z₂. The even unimodular rank is even (split alternating planes modulo2), so any even case of rank at least five indeed has rank at least six. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

1.3 If L over Z₂ is odd, first split an odd-unit line. Any even unimodular complement splits into even planes: modulo2 choose e,f with e·f odd; their Gram determinant is odd, so their integral inverse gives the actual Z₂-orthogonal projection and a unimodular complement, allowing induction. An odd-unit line generated by t, orthogonal to an even plane generated by e,f with e·f odd, can be replaced by three odd-unit lines. Put v=t+e and w=t+f. Both have odd norm, while v·w is even. Thus w'=w-(v·w/v²)v has odd norm and is orthogonal to v. The vectors v,w,t form a basis (its determinant in t,e,f is a unit); projecting the third vector off these first two unit lines leaves a final unit line. Repeating converts every even plane, so the whole odd lattice is diagonal with odd 2-adic units. For any five such coefficients a₁,…,a₅, find a primitive zero modulo8 as follows. If some pair sums to0 modulo8, use ones in those coordinates. If a pair sums to4, use ones there and a2 in any third coordinate. Otherwise all pair sums are2 or6 modulo8, so the coefficients all agree modulo4. Four ones then have norm0 or4 modulo8; in the latter case put2 in the fifth coordinate. In every case some coordinate is odd and q is divisible by8. Starting at precision 2^k with k≥3, alter an odd coordinate by 2^{k-1}t, t∈{0,1}. The norm change modulo2^{k+1} is 2^k t(Ax)_j; the quadratic correction has valuation at least k+1. Since the diagonal coefficient and chosen coordinate are odd, this toggles the next bit. These corrections converge, preserve primitivity, and give a nontrivial zero in Z₂. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

1.4 Consequently an indefinite unimodular integral lattice of rank at least five has a primitive integral isotropic vector. The real zero follows from one positive and one negative direction. Steps1.1–1.3 supply every finite completion, so the supplied rational Hasse–Minkowski interface gives a nontrivial rational zero. Clear denominators and divide the integer coordinates by their greatest common divisor. This proves the required integral isotropy; no lattice classification or Meyer theorem is being assumed. It also makes the step algorithmic: enumerate primitive integer vectors in increasing coordinate boxes until a zero is found, which terminates by the existence just proved. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, construct, algebra]

2.1 We supply the small-rank base without an unproved classification. For a nonsingular symmetric real matrix A with |det A|=1, diagonalize it orthogonally over R and replace its eigenvalues by their absolute values to obtain a positive definite matrix B with det B=1 and |xᵀAx|≤xᵀBx. For n≤4 the ellipsoid E={x:xᵀBx<19/40} has volume greater than1. The unit-ball volumes in dimensions1,2,3,4 are 2,π,4π/3,π²/2, respectively, so its volume is that value times (19/40)^{n/2}; each exceeds1 (π>3 already suffices). Only finitely many integer translates of the unit cube meet E. Integrating their indicator functions over that cube gives vol(E)>1, so some two distinct points x,y of E differ by a nonzero integer vector v. The B-norm triangle inequality gives vᵀBv<19/10<2, hence the integer vᵀAv is 0,1,or-1. If it is zero divide v by its coordinate gcd. Thus every unimodular integral lattice of rank at most four has a primitive isotropic vector or a unit-norm vector. This is the usual volume pigeonhole argument, proved here in the exact range required. [construct, algebra]

2.2 A unit-norm vector u splits integrally: x↦x-(x·u/u²)u projects onto its integral unimodular orthogonal complement. If instead v is primitive isotropic, complete it to an integer basis and take the dual basis; unimodularity makes that dual basis integral. Its first vector w has v·w=1. The plane spanned by v,w has matrix [[0,1],[1,a]], determinant -1, so its inverse is integral and it too splits orthogonally over Z. If a is even, replace w by w-(a/2)v to obtain the hyperbolic plane H. If a is odd, replace w by w-((a-1)/2)v, whose norm is1; that vector and v minus it are orthogonal, with norms1,-1, and form an integral basis. Recursive splitting in rank at most four therefore gives only unit lines and hyperbolic planes. If the original form is odd, at least one unit line occurs. For σ=±1, H⊕〈σ〉 is explicitly a unit diagonal form: with e²=f²=0,e·f=1,t²=σ, use e+t, f-σt, e-σf+t. They form a unimodular basis with norms σ,σ,-σ and pairwise zero products. Combine each hyperbolic plane with a unit line until none remains. Hence every odd unimodular lattice of rank at most four is diagonalizable, and every even such lattice is a sum of hyperbolic planes. The recursion terminates by rank descent. [step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Now let L be odd, indefinite, unimodular, of rank n>4. Find a primitive isotropic v by step1.4 and complete it to an integer basis x₁=v,x₂,…,x_n. Write x_i* for its integral dual basis. If (x₁*)² is odd, take w=x₁*. If it is even, some x_k* has odd norm because L is odd; necessarily k>1, and w=x₁*+x_k* has odd norm and v·w=1. The resulting odd plane splits as 〈1,-1〉 by step2.2. Write L=〈1〉⊕〈-1〉⊕K, where K is unimodular of rank n-2. Retain just one of the two unit lines with K so that their sum is indefinite: retain the negative line when K is positive definite, the positive line when K is negative definite, and either when K is indefinite. This retained lattice is odd, unimodular, indefinite, and has rank n-1. Apply induction to it, putting the unused signed line aside. The base n≤4 was proved in step2.2. Thus every odd indefinite unimodular integral lattice is integrally diagonalizable. The decreasing measure is the retained rank n→n-1; no stabilizations are inserted during the induction and no indefinite complement is assumed without the specified retained line. All basis completions, dual inverses and orthogonal projections are integral finite operations. [step 1.4, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

3.2 Given any symmetric integral unimodular A, add the two signed unit lines 〈1,-1〉. The enlarged lattice is odd and indefinite, regardless of the parity or signature of A, so step3.1 diagonalizes it. A diagonal unimodular integral matrix has only±1 diagonal entries, since their nonzero integer product is±1. The proof gives a terminating construction of an integral unimodular congruence P: isotropic vectors are found by the terminating bounded-box search of step1.4; in the small-rank branch search for norm0/±1, guaranteed by step2.1; basis completion and primitive gcd operations are ordinary integer Euclidean algorithms; split each plane by its integral inverse; choose which sign to retain by exact rational inertia computation; and compose the recorded basis changes. The final matrix is Pᵀ(A⊕〈1,-1〉)P with signed-unit diagonal. This is precisely the arithmetic stabilization needed for surgery, not a two-sided row/column reduction of an unrelated handle matrix. [step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Take the supplied framed-link presentation of Y in S³. Its exterior first homology is freely generated by the meridians μ_i, and the preferred longitude of component i has class Σ_{j≠i}lk(K_i,K_j)μ_j. Filling each framed slope imposes the rows of its symmetric integer linking/framing matrix A, so H₁(Y;Z)=coker A. Since Y is an integral homology sphere, A is unimodular. Add one isolated+1 and one isolated-1 framed unknot in disjoint balls. The retained Kirby blow-up interface preserves the oriented filled manifold and adds exactly〈1,-1〉 to A. Each elementary integer basis addition is a handle slide; signed permutations are relabelings or orientation changes. The Euclidean algorithm generates every integral unimodular basis change from these operations, so the retained Kirby interface realizes the P of step3.2. The final link has pairwise linking numbers zero and all framings±1, while still presenting Y. [step 3.2, construct, algebra]

4.2 Perform surgery on the resulting components in any order. The first k have diagonal linking matrix with±1 entries, hence their surgery has H₁=0 by the same meridian presentation. These closed connected oriented three-manifolds have H₂=H¹=0 by duality and H₃=Z by orientation, so all are integral homology spheres. The still-unfilled knot's original preferred framing remains preferred after the earlier fillings. Indeed its algebraic linking number with every earlier component is zero. Starting from a Seifert surface, pair its opposite-sign intersections with each earlier component and tube along thin parallel intervals of that component to remove the pairs. Finitely many nested tubes with disjoint radii give an oriented embedded surface with the same boundary, disjoint from all earlier components. It survives their surgeries and induces the unchanged longitude. Thus the next specified coefficient is genuinely±1 relative to the Seifert framing in the current homology sphere. No simultaneous disjointness of the different components' Seifert surfaces is required. [step 4.1, construct, algebra]

4.3 The full ordered sequence starts at S³ and ends at Y. For its reverse, if a step has coefficient ε=±1 with old basis (μ,λ), the core of the filling has meridian μ'=εμ+λ and preferred longitude λ'=ελ. They have intersection one and λ' is the primitive kernel of boundary homology into the unchanged exterior. The slope -εμ'+λ' is -μ, so coefficient-ε on that core restores the old manifold. Hence all reversed steps also have preferred coefficients±1. This proves the stated connection in either direction, with every intermediate an integral homology sphere. [step 4.1, step 4.2, construct, algebra] ∎
