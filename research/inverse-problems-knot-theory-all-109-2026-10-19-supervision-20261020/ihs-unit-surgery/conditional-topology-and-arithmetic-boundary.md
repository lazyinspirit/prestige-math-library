## A complete conditional topology passage

Suppose the following arithmetic input is supplied:

For every symmetric A∈M_n(Z) with det(A)=±1, there are r,s≥0 and P∈GL_{n+r+s}(Z) for which

    Pᵀ(A⊕I_r⊕(-I_s))P = diag(ε₁,…,ε_N),   ε_i∈{1,-1}.

Then every oriented integral homology sphere Y is connected to S³ by finitely many surgeries of coefficient ±1 relative to the preferred Seifert framing of a knot in the current integral homology sphere. All intermediates are integral homology spheres. Here is the complete deduction, including the framing issue.

Take the retained framed-link presentation of Y in S³, with symmetric linking/framing matrix A. The exterior has first homology freely generated by link meridians μ_i. Its preferred longitudes represent Σ_{j≠i}lk(K_i,K_j)μ_j; filling along each framed slope imposes exactly the rows of A. Thus H₁(Y;Z)=coker A. Since this group vanishes, the square integer matrix is unimodular.

Add r isolated +1 and s isolated -1 framed unknots in disjoint balls. The retained Kirby blow-up interface preserves the oriented surgered manifold, and their matrix contribution is exactly the indicated signed unit summands. Every integer unimodular P is a product of signed permutations and elementary basis additions: the Euclidean algorithm reduces a primitive first column to e₁, then induction reduces the remaining unimodular block. Signed permutations relabel/reorient components; an elementary addition is the corresponding handle slide. The retained Kirby interface therefore realizes the full congruence PᵀAP on the surgery matrix and preserves Y. The resulting link has pairwise linking numbers zero and each framing ε_i=±1.

Order the resulting link components arbitrarily and do their surgeries in that order. Surgery on the first k components has presentation matrix diag(ε₁,…,ε_k), so its first homology is zero by the same meridian presentation. It is a closed connected oriented three-manifold, hence an integral homology sphere (Poincare duality gives H₂=H¹=0, and orientation gives H₃=Z). Thus every intermediate is within the required category.

It remains to identify the *preferred framing* of each still-unfilled knot. In S³ choose a Seifert surface for it. Its algebraic intersection number with each previously filled component is the corresponding linking number, namely zero. Pair opposite-sign intersections and tube the surface along a thin parallel of that component between the paired points. The oriented tubing removes the chosen pair without changing the surface boundary. Thin nested tubes with disjoint radii handle finitely many pairs; the component neighborhoods are mutually disjoint and miss the boundary knot. Thus one obtains an embedded oriented Seifert surface disjoint from all previously filled components. It persists after their fillings and still induces the original Seifert longitude on the next knot. Consequently its specified coefficient ε_i is genuinely ±1 relative to the preferred framing in the *current* integral homology sphere, not merely relative to a remembered S³ longitude. This also agrees with the Schur-complement formula, whose correction is zero because all intervening linking vectors vanish.

After all components are filled the result is Y. Reversing gives the requested Y-to-S³ direction. To check the inverse coefficient, let the old knot basis be (μ,λ), with λ its preferred longitude and ε=±1. The new core's meridian is μ′=εμ+λ, and its preferred longitude can be taken as λ′=ελ; these have intersection one and λ′ is the primitive kernel of boundary homology into the exterior. Then μ=εμ′-λ′, so the filling that restores the old manifold is coefficient -ε on the new core, up to reversing the unoriented slope. Hence every reversed step also has preferred coefficient ±1.

## Elementary arithmetic pieces actually supplied here

A primitive isotropic v in a unimodular integral lattice has a partner w with v·w=1. Indeed unimodularity identifies the lattice with its integer dual, and the functional defined by a primitive vector is primitive. The plane spanned by v,w has matrix [[0,1],[1,a]] and determinant -1. Its orthogonal complement is an integral direct summand: subtract from each x its projection using the integral inverse of this 2×2 matrix. Thus splitting the plane really drops rank by two.

If a is even, replace w by w-(a/2)v to obtain H=[[0,1],[1,0]]. If a is odd, first arrange w²=1 by replacing w with w-((a-1)/2)v; then w and v-w are orthogonal with squares 1,-1 and form a unimodular basis. Finally H⊕〈1〉 is explicitly diagonal: for e²=f²=0,e·f=1,t²=1, take

    u=e+t,  v=f-t,  w=e-f+t.

Their norms are 1,1,-1, they are pairwise orthogonal, and their basis determinant is -1. Thus an *already established finite isotropic-plane decomposition* has a completely explicit signed-unit stabilization and diagonalization.

For example, A⊕(-A) has its primitive diagonal isotropic submodule {(x,x)}. Splitting successive primitive isotropic planes by the preceding construction yields a terminating decomposition for this doubled form, and hence a signed-unit stabilization diagonalizes the doubled form. This does NOT diagonalize A alone: one may not cancel the unrelated -A summand from a stabilization allowed to use only signed units.

## The unsupplied arithmetic operation

No argument above proves the displayed stable diagonalization input for an arbitrary unimodular A. In particular E₈ cannot be diagonally reduced without stabilization, and the existing two-sided integer handle boundary-matrix lemma is not a symmetric congruence theorem.

One tempting extension is to represent a positive norm by four squares, add four negative units, and obtain a primitive isotropic vector. Adding one extra unit of the other sign can make the resulting plane odd. That produces a legitimate plane split, but its new complement has larger rank than the starting lattice; repeating it has no established decreasing measure. It is therefore not a completed stable-diagonalization algorithm.

Another route is the classification of odd indefinite unimodular integral forms, after adding both signs. No such classification or primitive-isotropic-existence supplier is retained in this task. Merely naming that theorem, or applying rational diagonalization, does not supply its integral arithmetic proof. Accordingly the unconditional homology-sphere ±1-surgery supplier remains held at exactly this arithmetic input. The conditional topology passage and the elementary plane calculations above are complete; they are not represented as completion of the unconditional theorem.
