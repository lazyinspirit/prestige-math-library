# Actual rainbow-band completion of a good decomposing surface

This fills the topological boundary-pairing operation left explicit in the earlier decomposition note. It consumes the newly constructed positive boundary fingers and literal product-handle/auxiliary-band invariance; it makes no all-genus instanton spectrum assumption.

## Preparing an odd number of pairs

Let $S$ be a connected good decomposing surface. Each boundary component $C$ meets the signed boundary regions and has a positive even number $2k_C$ of vertical arcs through the annular sutures. Along the oriented $C$ these arcs alternate between passages from the positive region to the negative region and the reverse passages. This alternation follows from actually following the region containing $C$ between consecutive crossings.

A positive finger across a suture adds exactly two such vertical arcs on that same boundary component. The collar-and-ball construction preserves the cut sutured manifold. Thus, independently for each $C$, add one positive finger if necessary so that $k_C$ is odd. The cyclic list of vertical arcs is still alternating. Write $k_C=2l_C+1$.

## The explicit nested pairing

Choose a base point on $C$ outside the sutures, enumerate its $2k_C$ vertical arcs in cyclic order, and pair the arc numbered $i$ with that numbered $2k_C+1-i$, for $1\le i\le k_C$. Their traversal signs are opposite, since their indices have opposite parity. Attach a product one-handle at each pair, and extend $S$ by the rectangle through that handle. Opposite traversal signs make that rectangle orientation-compatible. Choose a separate three-dimensional handle for every pair and a separate collar for every original boundary component. No handles need intersect; if auxiliary compensating bands require extra genus, reserve a private auxiliary handle for each pair before closing.

The boundary effect is a finite ribbon calculation. Cut the boundary circle at its chosen base point and draw the paired intervals as nested chords of an abstract disk, with pairings $(1,2k_C),(2,2k_C-1),\ldots,(k_C,k_C+1)$. The dual adjacency graph of its complementary regions is a path with $k_C$ edges and $k_C+1$ vertices. This can be proved inductively: the outermost chord separates one end region, and removing it leaves the nested diagram with one fewer chord. Each vertex is the boundary of one region obtained after the oriented band surgeries; following its perimeter alternates an original segment of $C$ with an edge of a new rectangle. Thus these vertices are exactly the new boundary circles, not an auxiliary count of abstract faces.

Every original segment lies in one signed boundary region. The two long edges of a band lie respectively in the positive and negative boundary bands of its product handle. Hence the complementary-region signs alternate along the dual path. Since $k_C$ is odd, its $k_C+1$ vertices split into precisely $l_C+1$ positive and $l_C+1$ negative vertices. All vertical arcs have been used as rectangle attaching edges, so no new boundary curve still crosses an annular suture. The resulting boundary lies wholly in the two signed surfaces, with equal numbers of closed curves on each side.

The construction is local to the collar of $C$ and its private handles. It applies to an arbitrary good boundary circle, without requiring that $C$ bound a disk in $S$. The abstract disk records only the ribbon surgery of that single boundary circle; the remainder of $S$ is not replaced by that disk.

## Actual dual arcs and independence

Each vertex of the path is incident to at least one edge. For each positive new boundary curve choose one incident handle edge. Its positive boundary band contains a co-core arc. This arc is proper in the enlarged positive boundary surface: its endpoints lie on the two free sides of that band, hence on the new sutures. It intersects the new boundary curve once, transversely, and misses every other positive new boundary curve. Indeed each handle rectangle has exactly one positive long edge, so that boundary band belongs to only one positive curve. Two distinct positive vertices cannot choose the same edge of the path, because each edge has only one positive endpoint. Thus the chosen arcs have the identity intersection matrix with all positive boundary curves, up to individual signs. Repeat on the negative bands for the negative curves.

This proves homological independence by intersection, rather than merely asserting that each curve is individually nonseparating. Handles attached at different original boundary components are disjoint, so the union of these intersection matrices is still diagonal. Let the two collections be $C_1^+,\ldots,C_n^+$ and $C_1^-,\ldots,C_n^-$.

Now choose a connected auxiliary surface for the enlarged sutured manifold. The co-core arcs have endpoints at its attaching boundary circles. Join the two endpoints of each co-core by a path in the auxiliary surface and push slightly to get a closed cycle in the actual closed preclosure boundary. The joining path is disjoint from every $C_i^\pm$, which all lie on the sutured side. The same diagonal intersection matrix therefore proves independence in the homology of each CLOSED preclosure boundary, not only before the auxiliary surface was attached. The connecting paths need not be disjoint from one another for this argument.

Both closed preclosure boundaries have the same genus by balance. Cutting an oriented closed surface along a homologically independent disjoint curve family is connected: a disconnected complement would give an oriented boundary relation among a nonempty subfamily, contradicting the dual intersection matrix. Thus cutting the two boundaries along these collections gives connected surfaces of equal genus with $2n$ labeled boundary circles. Classification by cutting them into disks and paired oriented handle bands gives an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism between them respecting the labels. Regluing its matching boundary circles gives an end identification $h$ carrying $C_i^+$ to $-C_i^-$. Individual desired curve orientation reversals can be realized by reversing both coordinates of its handle band, which preserves the surface orientation.

## The actual same-manifold cut closure

The surface obtained by extending $S$ through these handles is still connected. Closing its paired boundary curves under $h$ gives a connected closed surface $\bar S$. The distinguished preclosure surface $\bar R$ meets it in exactly the $n$ identified circles. Smoothing $\bar R\cup\bar S$ gives a connected $F$, with $[F]=[\bar R]+[\bar S]$ and $\chi(F)=\chi(\bar R)+\chi(\bar S)$.

Cut the enlarged sutured manifold along the extended $S$. In its auxiliary closure add one annulus for each matched pair of new boundary annuli and glue by $h$ on the cut $\bar R$ part and by identity on the two copies of $S$. The annuli are the collars of the paired circles; attaching them reconstructs the same original end identification. The resulting closed three-manifold is literally the same $Y$, and its distinguished surface is the smoothed $F$. The connected-complement property above ensures $F$ is connected. If this auxiliary surface is disconnected, join its components by the already constructed compensating product-handle bands, starting at sufficiently large auxiliary genus; no new instanton count is asserted by this topological redistribution.

The newly constructed product-handle invariance applies before and after the surface cut. Therefore this enlargement has not changed either selected sutured group. The operator comparison is the actual incidence identity $\mu(F)=\mu(\bar R)+\mu(\bar S)$ in this same $Y$. Once the actual universal surface bounds and extreme simplicity are supplied at these genera, the finite joint-eigenspace argument in `seifert-cut-closure-and-genus-sector.md` gives the decomposition summand. This final spectral input is still separate; the rainbow construction supplies its missing boundary and gluing geometry, not the all-genus polynomial itself.
