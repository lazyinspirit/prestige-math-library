# Product-cap action on arbitrary ordinary closure groups

This authors the geometric passage from a proved product calculation to actual arbitrary-manifold surface bounds. It supplies no missing product polynomial by assumption. The ordinary count and insertion constructions are the newly authored ordinary-end operations; frozen earring scopes are not changed.

Let $R\subset Y$ be a closed connected oriented surface with odd determinant restriction. Put a copy $R_0$ in the interior of the product four-cobordism $[0,1]\times Y$. Its normal bundle is the oriented plane given by its normal line in $Y$ and the interval direction, hence is trivial. Remove its open disk normal bundle. The resulting actual cobordism has incoming boundary $Y\sqcup(R\times S^1)$ and outgoing boundary $Y$. The determinant on its new incoming component is precisely the pullback of its odd restriction on $R$. Its ordinary count defines

$$m_R:I(R\times S^1)\otimes I(Y)\longrightarrow I(Y).$$

The relative product cap $R\times D^2$ defines a vector $e_R$ in the first factor. Gluing that very removed disk bundle back into the cobordism returns the product cylinder. Thus the actual gluing formula gives

$$m_R(e_R\otimes v)=v.$$

There is no scalar ambiguity in this equation if the orientation of $e_R$ and of the punctured cylinder is fixed by this cylinder gluing. Changing a product trivialization changes both choices together. In particular $m_R$ is surjective, with an explicit right inverse $v\mapsto e_R\otimes v$.

The copy $R_0$ can be moved to a parallel surface on the new product end or to the corresponding surface on the outgoing $Y$ end. The homotopy of the universal surface-incidence class across this movement has boundary equal to the difference of those two incidence cycles. Counting the resulting one-parameter cutdowns gives a chain homotopy and therefore the identity

$$m_R(\mu(R)a\otimes v)=\mu(R)m_R(a\otimes v).$$

For the point class, join the two insertion points by an arc in the punctured cylinder. Moving its codimension-four adjoint incidence along this arc gives the same conclusion for $\mu(\mathrm{pt})$. The rational weights and bubble exclusions are exactly those of the new adjoint-incidence construction. This argument uses the actual compactly supported incidence homotopy, not an identification of unrelated formal generators.

Consequently every polynomial relation $P(\mu(R),\mu(\mathrm{pt}))=0$ on the whole ordinary product group gives the identical relation on $I(Y)$. Indeed,

$$P(\mu(R),\mu(\mathrm{pt}))v
=m_R(P(\mu(R),\mu(\mathrm{pt}))e_R\otimes v)=0.$$

This is enough to transfer actual joint spectral bounds, by the primary decomposition of a finite-dimensional rational module after extending scalars to $\mathbb C$. It also transfers top semisimplicity. For example, on the point-$+2$ sector suppose the actual product polynomial is

$$P(t)=(t-a)Q(t),\qquad Q(a)\ne0.$$

The same polynomial annihilates the arbitrary closure operator $A=\mu(R)$. On its generalized $a$-block, $Q(A)$ is invertible, so $(A-a)Q(A)=0$ implies $A-a=0$. This proves ordinary-eigenspace selection there, not merely a bound on eigenvalues. If the product relation is given only after imposing the point eigenvalue, the exact sufficient input is a relation modulo the image of $\mu(\mathrm{pt})-2$, not merely an eigenvalue list on its kernel. The insertion movement also gives $m_R((\mu(\mathrm{pt})-2)a\otimes v)=m_R(a\otimes(\mu(\mathrm{pt})-2)v)$. Thus for $v$ in the actual point-$+2$ kernel the map factors through the product quotient by that image; a polynomial annihilating this quotient transfers. Equivalently, a polynomial identity $P(\mu(R))=(\mu(\mathrm{pt})-2)T$ on the actual product cap module transfers to zero on that kernel. No semisimplicity of the point operator on an arbitrary admissible three-manifold is assumed. The ordinary torus operation supplies point semisimplicity only when an odd embedded torus is actually available, as in the annular knot closure.

All selected-factor versions of $m_R$ are obtained by the commuting spectral projectors. One should not assert that the entire cap vector is already in the surface extreme sector. For universal annihilators it is the full unit $e_R$ that gives surjectivity; for higher-genus saddle inversion it is the separately proved nonzero extreme projection of this actual unit that matters. Those are distinct uses of the same geometric cap.

Thus the only product premises needed downstream are actual polynomial annihilators with their simple extreme factors and the nonzero extreme unit projection. A proved product eigenvalue list alone would transfer eigenvalue bounds but would not supply the simple factor or the cap nonvanishing. The genus-two nondegenerate pairing matrix alone supplies none of these all-genus premises.
