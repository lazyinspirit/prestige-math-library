# Boundary normalization and actual product-handle invariance

The primary texts actually read for this new local construction are Juhász, arXiv:math/0609779v3, Lemma 4.5 and its proof (PDF pp.12–14), and Kronheimer–Mrowka, arXiv:0807.4891v2, the proof of Proposition 6.9 and Lemma 6.10 (PDF pp.47–52). The hierarchy assertion in Juhász Theorem 8.2 is a citation to Gabai, not a proof supplied here. The following operations do not assert hierarchy existence.

## Positive boundary fingers

Write an annular suture as $[-1,1]\times S^1$ and its oriented core as $\{0\}\times S^1$. A positive tangency is one at which the oriented boundary curve of the decomposing surface and the oriented suture have the same tangent direction. In a small oriented boundary chart take the core to be $y=0$, directed by $\partial_x$, and the moving boundary arc to be $y=x^2-t$, also directed by increasing $x$. Passing through $t=0$ creates the required pair of transverse intersections with the core.

Keep track of the actual surface during this move by attaching the collar $\partial M\times[0,1]$ and adding the trace annulus of its moving boundary. The trace annulus has its old boundary on level zero and its moved boundary on level one; glue its old boundary to the original surface. Cutting the collar along this annulus gives the old cut manifold with an attached boundary collar. At the tangency the two new corners bound a three-ball whose incoming and outgoing face are disks; sliding one disk across that ball removes the two corners. Their suture orientations agree precisely for the positive tangency. Thus the sutured cut manifold is unchanged up to diffeomorphism. A negative tangency has the opposite suture attachment and is not included in this assertion.

Here is a terminating normalization for a boundary-coherent family. First move any closed curves lying in an annular suture into its adjacent negative boundary region, with the normal of the decomposing surface pointing out of the manifold. An arc transverse to all those parallel curves, continued across the suture to the positive region, gives simultaneous positive fingers because the curves have the same orientation.

Next let $C_1,\ldots,C_k$ be the parallel oriented closed intersection curves in a component $V$ of the positive or negative boundary. A short transverse arc crosses each once. If their homology class in $H_1(V)$ is nonzero, the two sides of this family can be connected to the sutures without crossing the family: the family does not cut off a compact subsurface away from the boundary. Continue the transverse arc on its two sides to obtain the finger path. Exactly one orientation of that path gives positive tangencies for every curve, since all their orientations agree.

If their homology class is zero, boundary coherence says that each is the oriented boundary of the compact side it cuts off. Continue the transverse arc from its exterior side to a suture. This gives a one-ended finger path. The boundary orientation of that compact side and the boundary orientation of $V$ again give positive tangencies. No assertion for oppositely oriented boundaries of compact sides is needed.

Choose the continuation arcs successively in the complement of the other boundary arcs. At each stage use an outermost parallel family. The components already meeting both signed boundary regions need not be moved. Each operation reduces the number of boundary components lying wholly in one signed region; no new wholly contained component is created. Hence finitely many moves give a good decomposing surface: every component of its boundary meets both signed regions. The actual cut sutured manifold is preserved by the collar-and-ball construction at every move. This establishes the normalization step rather than treating boundary coherence as an analytic nonvanishing theorem.

## A product one-handle and its compensating auxiliary band

A product one-handle is $[-1,1]\times D^2$ attached along its two end disks to small disks centered on the sutures. Each attaching disk meets both signed boundary regions in half-disks. The positive and negative boundaries each acquire the same rectangular band, so the new manifold remains balanced. The co-core disk $\{0\}\times D^2$ is a product decomposing disk; cutting it undoes this handle.

Its effect on an ordinary closure can be canceled geometrically, before taking any homology. Choose the auxiliary surface sufficiently large and select a properly embedded band joining the two attaching suture arcs. Reserve this band from all determinant markings and from the gluing curves of interest. In the preclosure, a neighborhood of the band times the interval is a three-dimensional one-handle on the auxiliary side of those very disks. Move that neighborhood across the common attaching annuli to the sutured side. On the sutured side it is exactly the displayed product one-handle; on the auxiliary side it has been removed. The union is the same preclosure, merely with a different dividing boundary between its two named pieces.

The two distinguished closed boundary surfaces of the preclosure are unchanged by this redistribution. Their end identification can therefore be chosen identically. The resulting closed three-manifold, its distinguished surface, and its determinant bundle are the same. In particular the selected ordinary closure groups agree by the identity map, after choosing connected auxiliary surfaces of sufficiently large genus. Removing the reserved band may otherwise disconnect an auxiliary surface; starting with an extra unused handle joining the two sides prevents that, and the newly constructed auxiliary-stabilization operation removes dependence on this preliminary choice. This is why arbitrary-small auxiliary genus is not assumed in this argument.

The same redistribution proves invariance under the inverse product-disk cut. Applying it on disjoint reserved bands proves the result for any finite collection of product one-handles. If those handles meet a decomposing surface in the standard rectangular bands joining paired vertical suture arcs, first cut that surface. Each rectangle becomes a product half-handle on the cut boundary, and the same neighborhood redistribution applies on each side. Thus the before-cut and after-cut handle additions both preserve the selected group. This justifies their use in putting a given good surface into a closure-adapted form.

The remaining combinatorial assertion in the full reduction is that the chosen paired bands can make all resulting closed boundary curves independent in each signed boundary surface. That assertion requires its own explicit pairing/dual-arc construction; it is not a consequence of product-handle invariance alone. Likewise these finite local operations do not supply a finite taut hierarchy for an arbitrary taut sutured manifold.
