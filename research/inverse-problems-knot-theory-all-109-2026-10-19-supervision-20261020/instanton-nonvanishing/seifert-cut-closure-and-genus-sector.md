## The actual simultaneous closure surfaces

Take a genus-$g$ Seifert surface $S$ for the knot. In the ordinary annular closure write

$$Y_1(K)=E_K\cup(F_{1,1}\times S^1),$$

with its actual longitude identified with $\partial F_{1,1}$ and meridian with the circle factor. Choose curves $\alpha,\beta$ in the punctured torus intersecting once. The distinguished torus is $R=\alpha\times S^1$. Close the Seifert surface by the actual punctured-torus slice:

$$\widehat S=S\cup(F_{1,1}\times\{q\}).$$

It is closed of genus $g+1$. It meets $R$ in the single circle $\alpha\times\{q\}$. Put the determinant marking on $\beta\times\{q'\}$, $q'\ne q$. Its evaluation is one on $R$ and zero on $\widehat S$.

Smooth the transverse intersection of $R$ and $\widehat S$ respecting orientations. Locally these are two oriented coordinate sheets; replacing their crossing by the oriented hyperbola glues their cut edges. Call the resulting connected surface $F$. Its homology class and Euler characteristic are

$$[F]=[R]+[\widehat S],\qquad \chi(F)=\chi(R)+\chi(\widehat S)=-2g.$$

Thus $F$ has genus $g+1$, and the determinant is odd on it. These assertions retain the actual marking circle, not only an Euler-characteristic calculation.

Let $M$ be the knot exterior cut along $S$, with its induced sutured boundary. The same $Y_1(K)$ is a closure of this sutured manifold with distinguished surface $F$. This can be seen by cutting the torus block along the displayed circle. Cutting $F_{1,1}$ along $\alpha$ gives a pair of pants; cutting $R$ along $\alpha\times\{q\}$ gives an annulus. The oriented smoothing glues that annulus to the two new circles of the pair of pants, giving a punctured torus again. On either side of the cut exterior, its copy of $S$ attaches to that punctured torus to give a closed genus-$g+1$ boundary. Identifying the two copies by the identity on the copy of $S$ reconstructs the original exterior, while the annular gluing reconstructs $F_{1,1}\times S^1$. This identifies the ambient closed three-manifold, not just its surface genus. The determinant marking stays in the punctured-torus region and crosses the resulting distinguished surface once.

The ordinary universal adjoint class is additive on these homologous surfaces. Hence its actual operators satisfy

$$\mu(F)=\mu(R)+\mu(\widehat S).$$

On the selected point-$+2$ closure group, $\mu(R)=0$ by the odd-torus cap-cut operation. Therefore the subgroup with $\mu(\widehat S)=2g$ is precisely the subgroup selected by the top surface eigenvalue $2\operatorname{genus}(F)-2=2g$ in this cut closure. This identifies the intended top genus sector by an actual cut-and-smoothed closure, without assuming a taut-nonvanishing theorem. The bottom sector similarly uses the oppositely oriented Seifert surface and eigenvalue $-2g$.

For $g>0$ these eigenvalues are distinct. If both actual sectors are nonzero, their sum already has dimension at least two. Since the newly constructed entire KHI has odd dimension, its dimension is then at least three. This is the exact remaining nontrivial-knot rank implication.

## The cut topology and what tautness does and does not supply

A minimal-genus Seifert surface is incompressible. A compression along a nonseparating essential curve lowers its genus while retaining its one knot boundary. A separating compression splits off a closed positive-genus component and again lowers the genus of the component with knot boundary. Either contradicts minimality. Thus cutting the irreducible knot exterior along this surface gives an irreducible cut manifold: an embedded sphere bounding a ball before the cut can be made disjoint from the incompressible surface by successively removing innermost intersection circles; its ball then lies in the cut component.

The cut sutures have two copies of $S$, one on each side, so the balance equation is literal equality of their Euler characteristics. Norm-minimization is a further precise topological input: the Seifert class has Thurston norm $2g-1$ for a nontrivial knot. Its usual elementary reduction takes a representative, cancels oppositely oriented longitudinal boundary components by annuli and joins the remaining positively oriented components along boundary bands, without increasing negative Euler characteristic, to obtain a one-boundary Seifert surface. Minimal genus then gives the lower bound. The cut copies inherit this minimal class under inclusion into the exterior. These facts give the taut cut manifold used in the source route.

Tautness is not itself a proof that its selected instanton group is nonzero. The required next construction is a finite sutured hierarchy for this actual cut manifold, with decomposing surfaces normalized to the boundary-coherent conditions and terminal product pieces, together with actual decomposition inclusions. A citation to the existence of such a hierarchy would leave the central local authoring obligation unresolved.

## The exact decomposition algebra, once the spectral geometry is supplied

The local smoothing construction for a general normalized decomposing surface works the same way. Close its positive and negative boundary curves in pairs; its closed completion $\bar S$ meets the old closure surface $\bar R$ in circles. Smoothing gives $F$ with $[F]=[\bar R]+[\bar S]$ and $\chi(F)=\chi(\bar R)+\chi(\bar S)$. The cut-open manifold closes to the same $Y$ with distinguished surface $F$; the added annuli are exactly the collars inserted along the cut boundary curves. Thus there are actual commuting operators $A=\mu(\bar R)$, $B=\mu(\bar S)$ and $A+B=\mu(F)$.

Suppose the actual product/cap geometry has established the real surface spectral bounds $A\le a=-\chi(\bar R)$, $B\le b=-\chi(\bar S)$ on the point-$+2$ group, and the simplicity of the top generalized blocks for $\bar R$ and $F$. Their joint generalized decomposition then gives an actual inclusion of the cut selected group into the old one. Indeed an eigenpair summing to $a+b$ must be $(a,b)$; otherwise one of its two real bounds is violated. On this joint block, the nilpotent of $A$ is zero by the top simplicity for $\bar R$, and the nilpotent of $A+B$ is zero by that for $F$, so the nilpotent of $B$ is zero as well. Hence the $F$ top space is exactly that joint block, which is a direct summand of the $\bar R$ top space.

The simplicity hypothesis matters: if the two top nilpotents were allowed to cancel, an ordinary eigenspace of their sum need not lie in either ordinary eigenspace separately. The new finite extremal algebra proves the needed simplicity conditional on its actual product-sector presentation; a nondegenerate pairing matrix alone does not supply that presentation or its geometric containment.
