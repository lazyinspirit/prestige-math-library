## The inverse composition is a product, after a rank-one cap replacement

The composite of the two saddle disks is an annulus: the two disks are joined along two output edges, so its Euler characteristic is $1+1-2=0$. Its product with the torus contains a central $T^3$. Cutting the annulus along its core and capping the two resulting circles by disks turns it into the two strips of the product cobordism. In dimension four this replaces the internal $T^3$ cut by two copies of $T^2\times D^2$. Outside this product block the manifold, bundle and singular surface are unchanged.

On that $T^3$ the quotient subgroup restricts to the two-element group dual to its torus fiber. Its group is rank one by the newly constructed torus seed, and its disk-cap relative invariant is a unit. The cap self-pairing is also a unit: gluing two caps gives $T^2\times S^2$ with odd determinant and zero index/energy; its projective flat tuple is the unique anticommuting torus pair, with acyclic deformation system. Thus the basis element and its dual differ by one orientation sign, not a nonunit numerical scalar.

Gluing across the central cut is contraction with the identity in this one-dimensional space. Filling its two ends with the cap unit gives exactly that contraction, with its determinant sign fixed by the product complex structure. The cut-and-capped composite therefore induces the product map. The same holds for the opposite composite. If orientations have initially left a common overall sign, change one saddle orientation; associativity forces the two inverse-composition signs to agree. Hence the two maps are inverse isomorphisms.

Surfaces and points that extend through the cobordism have the same universal adjoint class on its ends. Their insertion cycles consequently intertwine the corresponding operators, by moving the cycle through the compactified one-parameter count. In particular the torus and point operator conventions are retained.

## Point operator at an odd torus in an arbitrary closure

There is a useful immediate operation that avoids assuming the full point spectrum for a new closed manifold. In its product cylinder remove a small normal disk tube of an odd torus $R$. The boundary is $R\times S^1$, and capping it by $R\times D^2$ restores the product. The cap selects one of the two flat lifts. On that boundary the point operator sends this cap vector to minus twice its other lift by the actual torus seed.

The deck twist changing the cap lift extends over the punctured cylinder as the projective gauge transformation dual to $R$ on its exterior end. With the signed orientation convention this gives the actual equality $\mu(\mathrm{pt})=-2\tau_R$ on the closed Floer group (or the simultaneous sign reversal if the other quotient lift convention is chosen). It follows that $\mu(\mathrm{pt})^2=4$, so its $\pm2$ eigenspaces are ordinary eigenspaces, without hidden nilpotent blocks. The surface operator of $R$ similarly vanishes because its torus cap operator vanishes. The degree-four grading rotation interchanges the two point eigenspaces. All these assertions follow from the actual cap-cut insertion identity, not from a bare rank comparison.

## The singular knot to ordinary closure operation

For the based earring knot in $S^3$, take the boundary torus of a small tube of $K$ and the oppositely oriented boundary torus of its meridional earring $L$. The marking arc crosses each once. Identify longitude of $K$ with meridian of $L$ and meridian with longitude, preserving their specified intersection point. This is precisely the odd-bundle torus cut just constructed.

Here the input dual subgroup is zero, since both tori bound their tubes in $S^3$. The output is the disjoint union of a standard Hopf-link pair and the ordinary closure

$$T^3_K=(T^3\setminus\operatorname{int}N(C))\cup(S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}N(K)),$$

where $C$ is a coordinate circle of $T^3$ and meridian/longitude are interchanged. This topology is visible without a diagram: the earring exterior in a meridional ball is the product annular block, whose two new tube components close to the Hopf pair; its remaining punctured-torus times circle piece attaches to the exterior of $K$. Its determinant marking becomes the Hopf marking arc and a transverse coordinate circle in $T^3_K$. The output quotient subgroup is zero on the Hopf sphere and the two-element subgroup dual to the coordinate torus on $T^3_K$.

The Hopf pair contributes a rank-one integral group by its quaternionic critical point and even grading. The disconnected quotient normalization above therefore gives an actual isomorphism from the singular based-earring group to the ordinary closure quotient group. The cut-and-cap operator identity identifies the latter with one of the two point eigenspaces; grading rotation identifies it with the $+2$ one. The torus surface operator is zero. Thus over $\mathbb Q$ this is the desired singular-to-torus-closure bridge, with no invented canonical element or sign-independent generator.
