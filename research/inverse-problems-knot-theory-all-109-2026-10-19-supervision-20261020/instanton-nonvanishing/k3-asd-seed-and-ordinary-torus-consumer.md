## The product torus cap and point eigenspaces

Take the ordinary determinant-one torus closure $Y=S^1\times T^2$ with determinant degree one on the $T^2$ factor. Its retained flat lifts are exactly two points: $U=i,V=j$ on the torus and circle holonomy $Z=\pm1$. Their adjoint local systems are the three rank-one systems with sign monodromies $(1,-1,1)$, $(-1,1,1)$ and $(-1,-1,1)$; the explicit Koszul contraction in the first operations note makes each acyclic. Thus both points are nondegenerate.

The actual degree-four quotient bridge gives their relative grading four. The ordinary complex therefore has zero differential: all possible relative degrees between its two generators, including any repeated degree-eight ordinary period, are even and never the trajectory differential degree. Its rational homology is two-dimensional. The surface operator has degree two modulo eight, so is zero on this two-degree support.

The cap $T^2\times D^2$ with its fixed determinant has one zero-energy solution and selects the circle lift $+1$. Its adjoint deformation groups are zero by the same surface Koszul calculation, so the actual cap solution is regular and has signed count a unit. Other relative ordinary classes change its index by eight; they cannot contribute to its index-zero count. Negative-energy classes are empty by the curvature identity, and energy zero forces flatness. Thus the cap relative invariant is nonzero.

For the source's signed quotient sector, use the actual determinant orientation and quotient-gluing construction in the cube author's lem-kahler-orientation-and-signed-product-quotient-gluing.md. It identifies the quotient cap pairing with the sum of the two determinant extensions $\sigma$ and $\sigma+F$, each counted once. Its transfer normalization cancels the simultaneous duplication of the two lifts; it is not an arbitrary factor of two. This quotient group is one-dimensional and its cap invariant is nonzero. $$\mu(\mathrm{pt})=\frac{D_X^{(\sigma,F)}(\mathrm{pt})}{D_X^{(\sigma,F)}(1)}=-2.$$

$$\begin{pmatrix}0&-2\\-2&0\end{pmatrix}.$$
