# Proper normal families: the new finite-block reduction and the hierarchy trace boundary

The exact Scharlemann complexity definition was inspected in the source image at PDF27, printed583: its first coordinate uses the length of the **longest partial hierarchy** having incompressible, boundary-incompressible nondisk cuts. These are sequential surfaces in successive cut manifolds. A bound for a simultaneously disjoint family of surfaces in one manifold is not that statement.

The retained normal-position interface, supplied by the hyperbolization owner without source reinspection, allows an injected boundary-incompressible proper nondisk surface with essential boundary to be normalized after the ambient boundary has been made injective by disk-first cutting. It does not by itself assert a coherent inherited rank under arbitrary later cuts. The following local reduction concerns an actually supplied simultaneous normal family only.

## Finite local block data, with boundary faces retained

Let $\mathcal F$ be a finite union of pairwise disjoint two-sided proper normal surfaces in a fixed finite triangulated manifold. In each tetrahedron only the four triangle types and at most one quadrilateral type occur. Disks of one type have a linear nesting order. Consecutive disks in such an order bound explicit disk-times-interval prisms. Retain these prisms, with their actual interval directions, rather than replacing them by a formal statement that the complement is a product.

There are at most two exposed levels per occurring disk type in each tetrahedron. Thus at most fourteen outer disk levels need be recorded per tetrahedron even if the family contains arbitrarily many disks. On a triangular face there are three normal-arc directions. For a fixed direction, the list of intersection arcs is the concatenation of the triangle block at its vertex and the possible quadrilateral block. Their ordering is forced by disjointness. The two adjacent tetrahedra therefore induce two partitions of that ordered list, each with at most two blocks. Their common refinement has at most four blocks. Only the endpoints of those blocks can stop an interval matching; all interior consecutive levels match an interval prism on the other side.

For a boundary triangular face there is only the one partition, and the interval matching is retained as a boundary rectangle. It is not discarded as though the surface were closed. Small neighborhoods of edge endpoints are handled by the same cyclic ordered lists; their subdivisions are determined by the face-block endpoints, so there is no additional quantity proportional to the number of normal disks. Consequently all failures of a prism-to-prism continuation are concentrated in finitely many explicitly marked level endpoints and the complementary tetrahedral regions incident to them. The number of marked endpoints is bounded by a constant times the number of tetrahedra and boundary triangles, independently of the disk multiplicities.

This is an actual finite encoding. An implementation can enumerate the at most seven formal disk types, the three arc directions on each face, and their exposed levels. It marks every first or last level in a common-refinement block and every boundary rectangle. The remaining levels carry only an integer multiplicity and an order-preserving prism matching. No enumeration through all intermediate disks is required. This local statement alone is sufficient to explain which geometric information a later global parallelity argument must retain.

## Propagation through genuine product blocks

Glue the unmarked prism interiors across matching face rectangles. Each gluing preserves interval fibers, possibly reversing their chosen direction. Thus their union is an actual interval bundle over a surface, with possible twisted components. Its horizontal boundary lies in $\mathcal F$ and the retained boundary faces; its vertical frontier is precisely where continuation stopped at the marked block endpoints. The possible reversal is recorded as a $\mathbb Z/2$ transition cocycle; it is not silently set to zero.

If a connected horizontal component avoids that vertical frontier and all nonproduct blocks, its interval fibers continue around its entire surface. An untwisted component with two distinct horizontal components gives a literal product region and therefore a parallel mate. A twisted component has connected horizontal boundary, a two-fold cover of its one-sided base, and is the one-sided interval-bundle exception. Boundary rectangles give the corresponding properly embedded versions with vertical boundary in $\partial M$.

To obtain a quantitative nonparallel-family bound, one still has to charge every horizontal component to a bounded set of nonproduct frontiers, or classify the components that have only twisted-bundle sides. Merely counting disk types does not prove that charging assertion: global prism monodromy and repeated visits to one frontier must be handled. This note retains that exact global step rather than inventing a numerical bound from the local fourteen-level count.

## Why even a completed simultaneous bound would not yet be C1

A later hierarchy surface may end on a copy of an earlier cut surface. Its inverse image in the original manifold therefore has boundary in the interior, rather than on the original boundary. Pushing that boundary off the earlier surface does not automatically create a disjoint proper surface; it may create tubes, intersection curves or a branched trace. Incompressibility and boundary incompressibility are statements in the current cut manifold, and do not automatically survive such a trace replacement in the original manifold.

Thus two further operations are explicitly separate: finish the global parallelity/frontier charging for an actual simultaneous proper family, and construct a finite hierarchy-trace carrying argument whose replacement surfaces satisfy exactly that family's hypotheses while retaining a strict sequential rank. The accepted finite closed-family result and the existence of one finite unpared hierarchy supply neither operation at this scope. The central taut-cut and six-case sutured-complexity arguments are authored in `finite-taut-hierarchy-by-sutured-complexity.md`; their first-coordinate finiteness remains held until this sequential rank is actually supplied.

## Completing the simultaneous-family bound by exposed disks

There is a sharper charging choice than propagating arbitrary frontier strips. Charge a surface if it contains a disk which is first or last among the disks of its own normal type in some tetrahedron. There are at most fourteen such disk occurrences per tetrahedron. A disk occurrence belongs to exactly one surface, so at most $14t$ components can be charged this way. Repeated visits to a frontier cannot multiply this count, because the charge is the original disk occurrence, not its propagated image.

Suppose a connected component $S$ of the family is uncharged. Every one of its normal disks has a predecessor and a successor of the identical type. On each side of each disk, take the prism up to its immediate neighbor. Across a face, the corresponding arcs of $S$ are interior to their normal-type blocks on both adjacent tetrahedra. The immediate-neighbor intervals match: an intervening arc would contradict immediacy on one side, while a block transition would make the incident disk of $S$ an exposed disk of its type. Thus the prisms on a fixed side continue across every arc of $S$. At boundary arcs they continue to vertical rectangles in $\partial M$ instead of stopping at an unrecorded interior frontier.

Accordingly that entire side of $S$ is horizontal boundary of an actual interval bundle with no vertical frontier in the interior of $M$. This is a global statement for this uncharged component, rather than for a general component of the partial prism union. It follows by the disk-by-disk continuation just described, including every boundary arc. The other side has the same property.

If either resulting bundle is untwisted, its other horizontal boundary is a distinct family component and is diffeomorphic to $S$. Its vertical boundary lies in $\partial M$. It is therefore a proper product region between the two surfaces, contradicting that the family is pairwise nonparallel. If both endpoints of an interval belong to the same connected $S$, its interval-bundle component is twisted, with connected horizontal boundary $S$ and one-sided base. This is exactly the exception that must be retained.

For an uncharged nonparallel $S$, both sides must therefore be twisted bundles. They have no interior vertical frontier; their vertical boundaries, when present, lie on $\partial M$. Their union along their common horizontal boundary $S$ is open and closed relative to the interior of the connected ambient component. Thus it is that entire ambient component. It is a surface semibundle, obtained by gluing the horizontal boundaries of two twisted interval bundles. There cannot be another family component in it: the two bundles were constructed from prisms between immediate neighboring disks, so their interiors contain no disk of the family. Their only horizontal boundary is the displayed $S$, and their vertical boundaries are on the ambient boundary. Their union therefore leaves no location for another family component. In particular there cannot be two distinct uncharged components in one ambient component.

Hence there is at most one uncharged nonparallel component per connected component of the ambient manifold. The actual simultaneous-family estimate is

$$|\mathcal F|\le14t+|\pi_0(M)|.$$

Here every surface is a two-sided proper surface in the **one fixed triangulated manifold**, with the simultaneous normal position supplied as data. Boundary rectangles and twisted interval bundles have both been included. This completes that new finite-family operation. It leaves untouched the separate sequential-hierarchy trace problem stated above: surfaces whose boundaries lie on earlier cuts have not thereby been made a simultaneous proper family in the original manifold, so this estimate is still not a claimed Haken-length bound.
