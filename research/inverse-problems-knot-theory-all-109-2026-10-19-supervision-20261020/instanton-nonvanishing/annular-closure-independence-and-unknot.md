## An odd torus mapping torus has a rank-one selected group

Let $\phi:T^2\to T^2$ be an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism fixing the chosen determinant point, and form its mapping torus. Give it determinant degree one on the fiber, with its specified extension across the fixed-point circle. Its flat projective torus tuple is an anticommuting pair $(U,V)$, unique up to conjugacy; write $(U,V)=(i,j)$. The monodromy sends the pair to the words prescribed by the integral matrix of $\phi$. Their commutator is again $-1$ because its determinant is one, so the resulting pair is again conjugate to $(i,j)$. A conjugating lift of the mapping-torus circle is consequently determined up to their common centralizer $\{\pm1\}$. There are exactly two retained lifts.

The same conclusion permits the additional specified signs from the determinant gluing: changing the signs of an anticommuting pair is implemented by conjugation with one of $i,j,k$. Thus the actual determinant extension is respected, rather than replaced by a bundle chosen only by its fiber degree.

The adjoint local system on every torus fiber is acyclic. Its product cellular complex is the explicit direct sum of the three contractible sign Koszul complexes. The mapping-torus cochain complex is the cone of the induced map minus the identity on this fiber complex. A cone of a map between contractible complexes is contractible: contract its long exact sequence, or use the two explicit fiber contractions. Therefore the full three-dimensional adjoint complex has $H^0=H^1=0$, and both critical points are nondegenerate.

The actual degree-four deck transformation interchanges these two points. With the relative degree-eight periods retained, no differential of odd degree connects them, and the ordinary rational group has dimension two. Quotienting by the signed fiber-dual deck gives dimension one. The odd-torus cap-cut identity supplies $\mu(\mathrm{pt})=-2\tau$ in the chosen quotient convention, and grading rotation supplies the other sign. Thus its $(\mu(T^2),\mu(\mathrm{pt}))=(0,+2)$ space is one-dimensional. This uses the constructed point operator, not a claim that two critical points alone determine that operator.

## Comparing two actual annular gluing maps

Fix the same preclosure with two torus boundaries and two gluing diffeomorphisms $h,h'$, with their point and determinant identifications. Let $\phi=h^{-1}h'$ in the resulting marked boundary coordinates. Take the disjoint union of the $h$ closure and the torus mapping torus of $\phi$, and perform torus excision along one fiber in each component. The cut preclosure is the same in both cases. Cross-gluing sends one component to the $h'$ closure and the other to the identity mapping torus $T^3$.

This is an actual cancellation of gluing maps: on a common torus coordinate, traversing the first component applies $h$, then the mapping-torus transition $h^{-1}h'$, so its total transition is $h'$. The unused pair of collars has the identity transition. Determinant lifts compose by the same equation, and the fixed-point circles are carried to the fixed-point circles. The quotient subgroups include the Mayer–Vietoris central signs as in the excision construction.

The actual excision isomorphism therefore identifies the selected group of the $h$ closure tensored with a one-dimensional vector space with that of the $h'$ closure tensored with a one-dimensional vector space. Both additional factors are nonzero by the mapping-torus calculation and the torus seed. Choosing either nonzero factor gives an isomorphism of the two closure groups. The ambiguity is a nonzero scalar, fully compatible with the original assertion of an isomorphism type; no distinguished vector is manufactured.

All oriented annuli with the specified sutured boundary collars are diffeomorphic. ## The actual unknot closure

For the unknot the exterior is a solid torus and its compressing slope is the preferred longitude. The punctured-torus closure piece is $F_{1,1}\times S^1$, with its boundary-circle slope glued to that preferred longitude and its $S^1$ factor to the knot meridian. Attaching the solid torus therefore fills the boundary of $F_{1,1}$ by a disk in the product framing:

$$Y_1(U)=(F_{1,1}\cup D^2)\times S^1=T^2\times S^1.$$

Its odd determinant and distinguished torus are precisely those of the constructed torus seed. Its selected rational closure group has dimension one. Annular closure independence gives this calculation for every allowed choice of its genus-one closure, not just one convenient model.
