## Statement

Let $L=K_1\cup\cdots\cup K_r$ be a nonempty labeled oriented link in $S^3$,
let $G=\pi_1(E_L)$, and choose one based positive meridian $m_i\in G$ for each
component. For every integer $q\geq1$, the homomorphism from the free group
$F_r=\langle x_1,\ldots,x_r\rangle$ defined by $x_i\mapsto m_i$ induces a
surjection
$$F_r\longrightarrow G/\Gamma_qG.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The link, its chosen product tubes, and based component meridians.

[F1] The positive component meridians form a basis of $H_1(E_L;\mathbb Z)$. [[lem-string-link-exterior-homology-and-meridian-maps]]

[F2] The degree-one Hurewicz map identifies $G/\Gamma_2G$ with $H_1(E_L;\mathbb Z)$. [[prop-the-first-hurewicz-map-in-degree-one-is-abelianization]]

[F3] The lower-central series is $\Gamma_1G=G$ and $\Gamma_{k+1}G=[G,\Gamma_kG]$. [[def-lower-central-series-and-magnus-expansion-for-links]]

[F4] Commutators add lower-central weights, and their induced pairings on graded quotients are biadditive. [[lem-hall-malcev-lower-central-commutators-add-weights]]

## Proof

**Given:** $G$ and its selected meridians $m_1,\ldots,m_r$.

1.1 Let $H=\langle m_1,\ldots,m_r\rangle$. By [F1] and [F2], the images of these meridians generate $G/\Gamma_2G$, so $G=H\Gamma_2G$. [F1, F2, given]

2.1 For every $k\geq2$, the quotient $\Gamma_kG/\Gamma_{k+1}G$ is generated by iterated commutators of weight $k$ in the selected meridians. For $k=2$, [F4] makes the pairing $G/\Gamma_2\times G/\Gamma_2\to\Gamma_2/\Gamma_3$ biadditive, so [F1] implies this layer is generated by brackets of the $m_i$. If the claim holds at weight $k$, then [F3] writes the next layer as generated by brackets of a degree-one class with a weight-$k$ class; [F4] makes that pairing biadditive, so [F1] again reduces the degree-one entries to the $m_i$, and the induction hypothesis reduces the other entries to brackets of those meridians. Each such iterated commutator is a word in $H$ lying in $\Gamma_{k+1}G$, so its class is in the image of $H\cap\Gamma_{k+1}G$ in the graded layer. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1, induction]

3.1 The conclusion of step 2.1 implies $\Gamma_kG\subseteq H\Gamma_{k+1}G$ for every $k\geq2$, because each generator of the graded layer is represented by a word in the $m_i$ that lies in $H$. Starting from $G=H\Gamma_2G$ in step 1.1 and applying these inclusions successively gives $G=H\Gamma_qG$ for every finite $q$. Therefore the image of $H$, and hence of the free group map in the statement, is all of $G/\Gamma_qG$. For $q=1$ the quotient is trivial. [step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra, induction] ∎
