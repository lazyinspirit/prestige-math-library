## Statement

Assume full AC and let $K\subset S^3$ be a smooth knot. For a nonzero Laurent polynomial $f(M,L)$, its Newton polygon is the convex hull in $\mathbb R^2$ of exponent pairs $(a,b)$ for which the coefficient of $M^bL^a$ is nonzero. The enhanced A-polynomial is **thin** when this polygon is contained in an affine line; the constant polynomial $1$ has a one-point Newton polygon and is thin. Then the enhanced A-polynomial of $K$ is thin if and only if $K$ is a torus knot, including the unknot.

## Proof

1.1 The unknot and all positive nontrivial torus knots are thin by [F1]: their enhanced curves have respectively the unit equation and the two explicitly calculated monomial terms. 1.2 Suppose now that the knot exterior has the complete finite-volume hyperbolic metric. We first construct the necessary SL2 lift on this EXTERIOR; no lift of the holonomy of a closed filling is assumed. Alexander duality in [F3], in degrees one and two, gives $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$ and $H_2(E_K;\mathbb Z)=0$, since the knot is a connected circle. The universal coefficient exact sequence therefore gives $H^2(E_K;\mathbb Z/2)=0$. On a finite triangulation choose a spanning tree and the associated finitely many vertex base paths. A PSL2 representation labels each oriented edge by its based transport; the labels multiply to identity around every triangle. Lift each edge label to SL2, choosing the reverse lift as its inverse. A triangle product is a central sign. The four triangle signs around each tetrahedron sum to zero in $\mathbb Z/2$: after conjugating to one vertex the intermediate edge factors cancel and the remaining signs commute. They are therefore a simplicial two-cocycle. Its cohomology class is zero, so it is the coboundary of a one-cochain. Multiply each edge lift by its corresponding sign. Every triangle product is now identity, and edge-path homotopy gives an actual SL2 representation lifting the specified PSL2 one. This finite argument uses the space cohomology directly and inserts no spin or closed-three-manifold parallelizability theorem. [F2, F3, given, construct, algebra]

1.3 We also need global image control before the SU2 filling operation, since the enhanced Definition alone omits rather than excludes higher-dimensional images. Take any irreducible component of the finite algebraic representation variety together with one common peripheral eigenline, and its actual generic matrices over its finitely generated function field $F$. Cover the eigenline projective coordinate by its two affine charts. If its peripheral image were two-dimensional, $M,L$ would be algebraically independent by [F5]; in particular a peripheral matrix generically has distinct eigenvalues. Its adjoint system is self-dual by trace. The explicit nonparabolic torus computation in [F5] gives boundary homology dimension two. Here is the rank computation over this arbitrary $F$, without assuming that its transcendence degree is one. Dualizing chains over the field, Lefschetz cap identifies restriction on $H^1$ with the pair connector on $H_2$, up to its degree sign, while restriction is transpose to inclusion on $H_1$. Exactness therefore gives $2=\dim\ker i_*+\operatorname{rank}i_*=2\operatorname{rank}i_*$; both image and kernel have dimension one. The extension of functionals needed to dualize is permitted by the standing full AC, exactly as in [F5]. For any $\mathbb C$-derivation $D:F\to F$, differentiating actual word matrices gives the cocycle $Z(g)=D\rho(g)\rho(g)^{-1}$. The finite word pairing with $u=\rho(\delta)-\operatorname{tr}\rho(\delta)I/2$ annihilates every relator boundary and has peripheral values one common nonzero factor times $(DL/L,DM/M)$, as the explicit moving-eigenbasis calculation in [F5] shows. Hence ALL these pairs annihilate the SAME one-dimensional boundary kernel. But extend $M,L$ to a finite transcendence basis of $F/\mathbb C$ and use the two coordinate derivations on that rational function field. Each extends over its finite algebraic extension to $F$: for a minimal polynomial $p(\alpha)=0$, set $D\alpha=-(Dp)(\alpha)/p'(\alpha)$; characteristic zero ensures $p'(\alpha)\ne0$, and the product/quotient rules verify the extension. Their resulting logarithmic pairs are independent, contradicting that common annihilator line. Thus no image component is two-dimensional. Components with generically reducible representations have $L=1$, because their diagonal characters factor through the meridian abelianization. The central-peripheral case has $M,L\in\{1,-1\}$ and is already zero-dimensional. This proves the dimension bound for the entire eigenvalue image. [F3, F4, F5, construct, algebra]

Write $X=V\setminus\operatorname{Int}\nu P$ for the pattern exterior and $E_K=X\cup_T E_C$ for the satellite exterior, with outer meridian $\mu_V$ identified with $\mu_C$ and preferred outer longitude with $\lambda_C$. Let $w\ne0$ be the winding number, and let $\Lambda=\mathbb Z[t^{\pm1}]$ act on covers by the satellite's positive-meridian deck transformation. Total linking restricts to $w$ times companion linking on $E_C$, to $w$ on $\mu_V$ and zero on the outer longitude. The pulled-back cover of $T$ has $|w|$ cylinder components, so $H_1(\widetilde T)=\Lambda/(t^w-1)$ generated by a lift of $\lambda_C$; its $H_0$ is the same module. The cover of $E_C$ has $|w|$ components, with module $H_1=\Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb Z[s^{\pm1}]}A_C$, where $s$ acts by $t^w$. Its $H_0$ map from $T$ is an isomorphism, because the two linking images are the same subgroup $w\mathbb Z$. Its longitude class is zero in $H_1$: a Seifert surface for $C$ lifts, since linking vanishes on all its loops by normal push-off, and its boundary is that lifted longitude. Replace $C$ by the unknot to obtain the pattern closure $P(U)$. Its companion-side cover has $|w|$ contractible components, the same $H_0$ and zero $H_1$. In both lifted Mayer–Vietoris sequences the $H_0$ map is injective, since its companion-side coordinate is an isomorphism. The only $H_1(\widetilde T)$ image is consequently in the SAME pattern-side module. Quotienting that image yields, respectively, $A_K\cong A_{P(U)}\oplus\bigl(\Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb Z[s^{\pm1}]}A_C\bigr)$. All gluings and the chosen longitude bounds are in the actual peripheral marking; no companion character transfer is used in this module calculation. [F3, F6, construct, algebra]

2.1 This proves the forward direction for every signed torus knot as well. Reflection carries its oriented meridian to the inverse positive meridian of the reflected knot: it preserves the carried knot orientation and reverses the ambient normal-plane orientation. It carries its preferred longitude to the preferred longitude, because the degree along the knot remains one and its exterior homology class remains zero; uniqueness in [F3] excludes any meridian shift. Composition of representations with this exterior diffeomorphism therefore identifies the peripheral loci by $(M,L)\mapsto(M^{-1},L)$, preserving irreducibility and all closure dimensions. Their reduced curve equations differ by that Laurent substitution and a unit. Reversing the knot orientation instead inverts both peripheral generators and gives $(M,L)\mapsto(M^{-1},L^{-1})$. These invertible integral changes of exponents preserve containment in an affine line. Finally swapping the two complex coordinates of the standard $S^3$ interchanges the two torus parameters and preserves ambient orientation; the radial torus parameter may be changed by a smooth radial isotopy. Thus, using the mirror and orientation conventions in [F1], every coprime torus knot reduces to the positive case, or to the unknot if one parameter has absolute value one. 2.2 The lift works for the local holonomy family, not merely its central point. Use the actual local slice representations constructed in [F2], not just the abstract quotient coordinate. The augmented-coordinate supplier cited in [F2] proves that three conditions transverse to the conjugation orbit give a holomorphic slice, with no large-conjugator identifications, and then gives its holomorphic family after the branched substitution $s=4\cosh^2(u/2)$. The complete cusp group is nonelementary. Indeed, if the full discrete group fixed its cusp point, put that point at infinity. It contains a rank-two translation lattice. An element with dilation of modulus different from one would conjugate a nonzero translation to translations tending to identity, contrary to discreteness. The translations in the group form a discrete rank-two lattice containing the cusp lattice; conjugation preserves it. The linear parts therefore belong to the finite orthogonal symmetry group of this lattice, and the Euclidean quotient of each horizontal horosphere has the same finite positive area. Its hyperbolic volume is the area times $\int_0^\infty z^{-3}\,dz=\infty$, contradicting finite volume. Thus the holonomy has no common invariant line and its SL2 lift is irreducible. The same is true in a neighborhood: otherwise invariant lines for a convergent sequence of reducible tuples have a convergent subsequence in compact $\mathbb{CP}^1$, giving an invariant line at the complete point. Now choose the local edge lifts holomorphically, with their values at the center equal to those of step 1.2. Each triangle product is a continuous central sign, hence stays identity. This yields the actual local family of irreducible SL2 representations. On the augmented curve its chosen common peripheral line has eigenvalues $M(u)=\epsilon_\mu e^{u/2}$ and $L(u)=\epsilon_\lambda e^{v(u)/2}$ for fixed signs, because their squares are the eigenvalue ratios in [F2]. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 This nonconstant peripheral arc lies in the enhanced curve of [F4]. To check the dimension condition rather than assume it, take the algebraic irreducible-character component through the complete irreducible point. The ordinary local quotient in [F2] is a smooth complex curve, so that component has dimension one; the local finite lift from PSL2 to SL2 does not change this dimension. 3.2 We now complete the nonvertical thinness-to-SU2-averse filling operation. Suppose the thin polynomial has nonconstant support direction $(p,q)$ with $q\ne0$, so its curve components are $M^pL^q=c_j$. Fact [F7] supplies finite multiplicative orders for ALL the constants $c_j$, using the entire thin segment. Let $N$ be divisible by all of them and by $|q|$. For every positive multiple $n$ of $N$, the slope $(np+1)/(nq)$ is primitive: a common divisor of its coefficients divides $q$, while $np+1$ is congruent to one modulo every divisor of $q$. The slopes are distinct and tend to $p/q$. If an irreducible SU2 representation extends over such a filling and lies on a curve component, its filling equation gives $1=M^{np+1}L^{nq}=M c_j^n=M$, impossible: a unitary determinant-one matrix with eigenvalue one is the identity, and a meridian normally generates the knot group, so its representation would be trivial. For any of the finite exceptional pairs from step 2.3 put $c=M^pL^q$. If $c$ is a root of unity, enlarge $N$ by its order; then its filling equation again forces $M=1$. If $c$ is not a root of unity, the equation $M c^n=1$ holds for at most one integer $n$, since two would give $c^{n-n'}=1$. Delete those finitely many integers. All remaining infinitely many primitive fillings have no irreducible SU2 representation. Every reducible unitary representation is abelian: an invariant line has invariant orthogonal complement, so all matrices are simultaneously diagonal. Hence these are actually SU2-abelian fillings. This proves the entire infinite-filling reduction on the stated nonvertical nonconstant branch. A vertical support direction $q=0$ produces no such sequence; ruling out that case for a nontrivial knot remains among the genuine representation/instanton operations in [L1], rather than being hidden by division by $q$. [F3, F4, F7, step 2.3, construct, algebra]

3.3 The finite torus-pattern obstruction is also elementary once the stated coefficient restrictions in [L1] have been supplied. For the nonzero signed winding $w$ put $v=|w|>0$. Symmetric normalization gives $\Delta_C(t^w)=\Delta_C(t^v)$, so the satellite product of step 2.4 uses this positive exponent. Write $a>b\ge2$ coprime and $g=(a-1)(b-1)/2$. The formula in [F6], expanded at infinity, is $\Delta_{T_{a,b}}(t)=t^g(1-t^{-ab})(1-t^{-1})/((1-t^{-a})(1-t^{-b}))$. Modulo terms of degree at most $g-a$, its expansion is $\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor a/b\rfloor}(t^{g-ib}-t^{g-ib-1})$. Thus, for $0\le v<a$, its coefficient $c_{g-v}$ is $1$ if $v=0\pmod b$, $-1$ if $v=1\pmod b$, and zero otherwise. Suppose the companion's normalized polynomial has leading terms $t^h-t^{h-1}+O(t^{h-2})$ and the satellite's nonzero coefficients alternate with absolute value one, exactly the two externally supplied instanton conclusions in [L1]. By step 2.4 its coefficient in degree $g+hv-v$ is $c_{g-v}-1$. If $v=1\pmod b$ this is $-2$, impossible. If $v=qb+r$ with $2\le r<b$, it is $-1$, while the preceding nonzero coefficient is the $-1$ in degree $g+hv-qb-1$: all intervening torus coefficients vanish and no lower companion term reaches those degrees. These consecutive negative coefficients contradict alternation. Consequently $b$ divides $v$ whenever $0<v<a$. This argument is a proved finite polynomial implication and uses the externally supplied instanton properties, without proving their gauge-theoretic foundations. [F6, L1, step 2.4, algebra]

4.1 A nonzero Laurent polynomial with collinear integral support has the form $M^aL^b P(M^rL^s)$, where $(r,s)$ is a primitive nonzero integral vector and $P$ is a one-variable Laurent polynomial; subtract one support point and divide all support differences by their common primitive direction. If the support is a single point, it has no zero in $(\mathbb C^*)^2$ and already cannot vanish on the arc in step 3.1. Otherwise $P$ has finitely many nonzero complex roots. A connected local arc on its zero set has $M^rL^s$ equal to one fixed root. Applying step 2.2 and differentiating at $u=0$ gives $r+s\tau=0$. Since $\operatorname{Im}\tau\ne0$, this forces $s=0$ and then $r=0$, a contradiction. Thus the enhanced polynomial of a finite-volume hyperbolic knot is not thin. This proves the complete hyperbolic exclusion without treating a PSL2 filling representation as an already lifted SL2 representation. [F2, F4, step 2.2, step 3.1, algebra]

4.2 In particular the torus-pattern configuration required by the minimal-satellite argument is incompatible with those coefficient restrictions.
