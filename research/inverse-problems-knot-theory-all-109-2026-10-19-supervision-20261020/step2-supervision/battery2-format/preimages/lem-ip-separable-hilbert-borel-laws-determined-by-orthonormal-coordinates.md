## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice. Let $H$ be a real Hilbert space with a
finite or countable complete orthonormal basis $(e_j)_{j\in J}$. For each finite
$F\subseteq J$, define the coordinate map
$\pi_F:H\to\mathbb R^F$ by
$\pi_F(x)=(\langle x,e_j\rangle)_{j\in F}$. If $\nu$ and $\nu'$ are Borel
probability measures on $H$ and
$\nu(\pi_F^{-1}(B))=\nu'(\pi_F^{-1}(B))$ for every finite $F\subseteq J$ and
every Borel $B\subseteq\mathbb R^F$, then $\nu=\nu'$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice, a real Hilbert space $H$, a finite or countable
complete orthonormal basis $(e_j)_{j\in J}$, and two Borel probability measures
$\nu,\nu'$ whose finite-coordinate pushforwards under the maps $\pi_F$ agree.

[A1] Countable Choice is assumed, as required by the Parseval supplier.
[[def-countable-choice]]

[F1] Each coordinate map $x\mapsto\langle x,e_j\rangle$ is a continuous linear
functional by Cauchy–Schwarz; continuous maps have Borel preimages of Borel
sets; the inner-product norm satisfies the triangle inequality; a Borel
probability law is a probability measure on the Borel sigma-algebra.
[[def-hilbert-space]] [[thm-cauchy-schwarz-in-an-inner-product-space]]
[[thm-continuous-preimages-of-borel-sets-are-borel]]
[[cor-inner-product-induces-a-norm]] [[def-norm-and-normed-space]]
[[def-borel-sigma-algebra]] [[def-borel-probability-law-on-a-polish-space]]
[[def-probability-measure]]

[F2] For a complete orthonormal family in a Hilbert space, Countable Choice
implies Parseval's identity
$\|x\|^2=\sum_j|\langle x,e_j\rangle|^2$ for every $x\in H$.
[[thm-parseval-equivalences-for-a-complete-orthonormal-family]]

[F3] The rationals are countable and dense in the reals, finite products of at
most countable sets are at most countable, and under Countable Choice a
countable union of at most countable sets is at most countable.
[[thm-rationals-countable]] [[lem-q-and-irrationals-dense-r]]

[F4] Finite measures agreeing on a generating pi-system and on the whole space
agree on the generated sigma-algebra.
[[lem-finite-measure-uniqueness-on-a-pi-system]]

[F5] The norm topology is the induced metric topology: every open set contains
a metric ball about each of its points, and positive-radius metric balls are
open.
[[def-metric-ball]] [[def-metric-topology]] [[thm-metric-open-set-algebra]]

[F6] Finite sums, products, and lattice operations of Borel real functions are
Borel measurable.
[[thm-arithmetic-and-lattice-operations-preserve-measurability]]

## Proof

**Given:** The hypotheses in the Statement.

1.1 If $H=\{0\}$, then it has only one Borel probability measure and the
conclusion holds. Otherwise, let $\mathcal C$ be the family of all finite
coordinate cylinder sets $\pi_F^{-1}(B)$, where $F\subseteq J$ is finite and
$B\subseteq\mathbb R^F$ is Borel. Each cylinder is Borel by continuity in [F1].
The family contains $H$, and the intersection of two cylinders is the
finite-coordinate cylinder obtained on the union of their index sets. Hence
$\mathcal C$ is a pi-system. [F1, given, construct]

1.2 Enumerate the basis if it is infinite, and in the finite case repeat the
last partial sum after all basis vectors have appeared. For $x_0\in H$ and
$r>0$, put $S_N(x)=\sum_{j\le N}|\langle x-x_0,e_j\rangle|^2$. By [F2],
$\|x-x_0\|^2=\sup_N S_N(x)$, so
$$B(x_0,r)=\bigcup_{\substack{q\in\mathbb Q\\0\le q<r^2}}\ \bigcap_{N\ge1}\{x:S_N(x)\le q\}.$$
Indeed, if $\|x-x_0\|^2<r^2$, choose a rational strictly between these two
numbers; conversely, membership in one intersection gives
$\|x-x_0\|^2\le q<r^2$. By [F1] and [F6], each $S_N$ is Borel, and
$\{S_N\le q\}$ is a cylinder over a Borel subset of $\mathbb R^F$. Thus every
open ball belongs to $\sigma(\mathcal C)$.
[A1, F1, F2, F3, F6, step 1.1, choose, construct]

2.1 Let $D$ be the set of all finite rational linear combinations of basis
vectors. If the basis is finite, $D$ is at most countable by [F3]. If it is
countably infinite, enumerate it and write $D$ as the union over $N$ of the
sets of rational linear combinations of the first $N$ vectors. Each of those
sets is at most countable by [F3], so their union is at most countable by the
Countable Choice clause of [F3]. The real span of the basis is dense by
completeness. Each finite real linear combination is approximated by a
rational one: approximate each of its finitely many coefficients by a rational
and use the triangle inequality and $\|e_j\|=1$. Thus $D$ is dense in $H$.
By definition, this makes $H$ separable. [[def-separable-space]]
The balls $B(d,s)$ with $d\in D$ and positive rational $s$ form an at most
countable family by [F3], and they are open by [F5]. They are a base: given
$x\in U$ with $U$ open, choose
$r>0$ with $B(x,r)\subseteq U$, then $d\in D$ with $\|d-x\|<r/3$, and a
rational $s$ with $\|d-x\|<s<r-\|d-x\|$. The triangle inequality gives
$x\in B(d,s)\subseteq B(x,r)\subseteq U$. By step 1.2 every such ball lies in
$\sigma(\mathcal C)$, so every open set, as a union of a subfamily of this
countable base, lies in $\sigma(\mathcal C)$. [F3, F5, step 1.2, choose, construct]

2.2 Every coordinate map is continuous by [F1], so
$\sigma(\mathcal C)\subseteq\mathcal B(H)$. Step 2.1 and the definition of
the Borel sigma-algebra give the reverse inclusion. Thus
$\sigma(\mathcal C)=\mathcal B(H)$. [F1, F3, step 2.1]

3.1 The assumed equality of finite-coordinate measures makes $\nu$ and
$\nu'$ agree on every cylinder in $\mathcal C$. They are probability measures,
so they agree on $H$ as well. The pi-system $\mathcal C$ generates
$\mathcal B(H)$ by step 2.2; [F4] therefore gives $\nu=\nu'$.
[F4, step 1.1, step 2.2, given] ∎
