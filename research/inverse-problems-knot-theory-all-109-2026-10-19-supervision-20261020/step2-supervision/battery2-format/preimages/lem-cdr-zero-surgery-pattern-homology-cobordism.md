## Statement

Let P be an oriented pattern of winding n≠0 and K an oriented knot. If P(U) bounds a smooth proper disk in a compact smooth oriented 4-manifold B whose oriented boundary is identified with the standard oriented S³ and H_i(B;R_n)≅H_i(B⁴;R_n) for every i, then for every K the zero surgeries M(P(K)) and M(K) are smoothly R_n-homology cobordant. For the positive meridians determined by the supplied orientations, their images in the cobordism satisfy [mu_K]=n[mu_{P(K)}]; in particular they differ by a unit of R_n. This is a winding-unit meridian comparison, not a claim of literal equality for arbitrary winding. If P has strong winding number ±1 and P(U) is pseudo-slice (respectively locally flat topologically slice), then M(P(K)) and M(K) are smoothly (respectively topologically) homology cobordant through a 4-manifold whose fundamental group is normally generated by the image of the fundamental group of either boundary component. The category and closure hypotheses are separate and cannot be inferred from winding alone.

## Proof

0.1 The smooth disk tube uses [F5] directly. In the TOP branch the input is a single locally flat proper disk in the standard topological ball with its given end collar. Encode that standard ball, disk and finitely many pair charts as in [F7], apply the normal existence assertion of [F6] in $L[r]$, and export a compact tube with a wider domain and its actual boundary-collar certificates. Its zero-framing calculation and all subsequent Mayer–Vietoris and group pushout calculations are performed on the resulting actual objects in $V$. Thus no full-AC hypersurface supplier and no general homology-absoluteness assertion enters the no-choice Statement. [F5, F6, F7, given, construct]


1.1 Write $P_0=P(U)$ and $R=\mathbb Z[1/|n|]$. First take the supplied smooth disk $D\subset B$. Apply the finite smooth disk-tube construction [F5] to this actual disk and its boundary collar. They give $N=D^2\times D^2$, product-shaped at its boundary. The framing at $P_0$ is zero. Indeed the small parallel disk is disjoint from $D$; boundary linking equals their intersection over $R$ in the $R$-homology ball. To check the latter identity here, two choices of bounding two-chain differ by a two-cycle, which bounds since $H_2(B;R)=0$; the chain intersection boundary formula makes their intersections with a proper relative cycle equal. Compare with the pushed-in boundary Seifert chain to get boundary linking. Thus the integral boundary linking is zero in $R$, hence zero in $\mathbb Z$. Put $Z=\overline{B\setminus N}$. Its boundary is the actual preferred-longitude filling $M(P_0)$. In the excisive collared decomposition $B=Z\cup N$, the overlap is $D^2\times S^1$. Mayer–Vietoris, with its connecting map represented by the meridian disk, gives $H_0(Z;R)=R$, $H_1(Z;R)=R[\mu_0]$ and $H_j(Z;R)=0$ for $j\ge2$. This uses only the stated $R$-homology of $B$. For a pseudo-slice disk the same construction applies smoothly with $R=\mathbb Z$. For a TOP disk this calculation is valid if its boundary-compatible zero-framed product normal neighborhood is actually supplied; this paragraph does not construct that neighborhood from local flatness. [F4, F5, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Use the accepted actual standard infection cobordism [[lem-standard-infection-cobordisms-have-boundary-generated-second-homology]] for $P_0\cup\eta$ and $K$, with $\operatorname{lk}(P_0,\eta)=n$, and call it $E$. Its construction glues the two surgery products along the axis tube and companion filling solid torus, exchanging $\lambda_\eta=\mu_K$ and $\mu_\eta=\lambda_K$. Its third boundary is exactly $M(P(K))$, with its zero slope and oriented exterior marking, as proved in that supplier's2.1. Glue $Z$ to the $M(P_0)$ end, with the reversed boundary orientation needed to form the oriented cobordism $V$. Product collars make $V$ smooth for smooth input and TOP for supplied TOP tube input. The two remaining ends are $M(K)$ and $M(P(K))$. In $H_1(E;R)$ the incoming meridians and outgoing meridian have coordinates $(1,n,1)$, respectively for $P_0,K,P(K)$. In $H_2(E;R)=R^2$ the incoming classes are the coordinate basis and the outgoing class is $(1,n)$. These are the actual inclusion maps of the accepted supplier, not inferred solely from abstract ranks. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Here is the full gluing homology calculation. The common surgery $M(P_0)$ has $H_j=R$ in degrees0,1,2,3 and zero otherwise. The product-union spine defining $E$ has the Mayer–Vietoris sequence for the common solid torus; it gives $H_3(E;R)=R^2$ with the two incoming oriented fundamental classes as basis, $H_4(E;R)=0$, and no homology above4. This is the same finite sequence as the accepted supplier's3.1, now in degree3: the solid torus has no degree2 or3 homology. In the sequence for $V=E\cup_{M(P_0)}Z$, the degree3 map sends $1$ to the first basis vector of $R^2$; the degree2 map sends $1$ to the first basis vector of $R^2$; the degree1 map is the diagonal inclusion $R\to R\oplus R$ with unit coordinates, since $\mu_0$ generates both groups. All three are injective and the degree0 difference map is injective. Exactness in every degree therefore gives $H_j(V;R)=R$ for $j=0,1,2,3$ and zero otherwise. The $M(K)$ inclusion is an isomorphism in degree3 by the second fundamental-class coordinate, degree2 by the second basis coordinate, and degree1 by multiplication by $n$, a unit in $R$. For the outgoing end, degree2 is multiplication by $n$ because $(1,n)$ becomes $n$ in the quotient by the first basis vector; degree1 is the unit1. In degree3 the oriented boundary of a relative fundamental chain of $E$ is the sum of its incoming components minus the outgoing component. Consequently its outgoing degree3 class is the sum of the incoming classes; after gluing $Z$, it becomes the second generator. Degree0 is an isomorphism for both connected ends. Thus BOTH boundary inclusions are homology isomorphisms in every degree. The exact marked degree1 relation is $[\mu_K]=n[\mu_{P(K)}]$, with the signs determined by the supplied peripheral orientations. It is equality up to the unit $n$, not literal equality for general $n$. This is the precise meaning of the cited CFHH 'rel meridians' convention. [F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Suppose now that the supplied disk lies in a simply connected ball (standard TOP or pseudo-smooth) and that $P$ is strong winding $\pm1$. Van Kampen for $B=Z\cup N$ identifies $\pi_1B$ with $\pi_1Z/\langle\!\langle\mu_0\rangle\!\rangle$, so $\mu_0$ normally generates $\pi_1Z$. Strong winding says that $\eta$ normally generates the closure knot-complement group; its image therefore normally contains $\mu_0$ in $\pi_1Z$, since the knot exterior maps into $Z$. Hence $\eta$ normally generates $\pi_1Z$ too. The actual product-union construction of $E$ and van Kampen give
$$\pi_1V=(\pi_1Z*\pi_1M(K))/\langle\!\langle\eta\mu_K^{-1}\rangle\!\rangle.$$
No injectivity of the gluing maps is assumed. Killing $\mu_K$ kills $\pi_1M(K)$ and then $\eta$, hence $\pi_1Z$; so $\mu_K$ normally generates $\pi_1V$. Killing $\mu_0$ instead kills $\pi_1Z$, hence $\eta=\mu_K$ and the other factor, so $\mu_0$ normally generates as well. The same $\mu_0$ is the outgoing satellite meridian in the actual infection boundary. Thus the image of either boundary group normally generates $\pi_1V$. The integer homology calculation is step3.1 with $n=\pm1$. The smooth pseudo-slice branch and the conditional supplied-neighborhood TOP branch follow with their actual categories; no smooth identification of a homotopy ball is used. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]
