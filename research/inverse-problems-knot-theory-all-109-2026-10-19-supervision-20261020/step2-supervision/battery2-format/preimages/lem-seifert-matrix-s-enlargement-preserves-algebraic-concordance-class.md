## Statement

Assume full AC for any knot-derived Seifert matrix. Let $V$ be an integral Seifert matrix and let $E_0(V;x,y,z)$ or $E_1(V;x,y,z)$ be either elementary enlargement in the definition of S-equivalence, where $x,y\in\mathbb Z^{2g}$ and $z\in\mathbb Z$. Then $E_0(V;x,y,z)$ and $E_1(V;x,y,z)$ are integral Seifert lattices, and each represents the same element of $G_{\mathbb Z}$ as $V$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integral Seifert matrix $V$ of size $2g$, integers $x,y,z$ of the stated sizes, and full AC only when $V$ is instantiated as a knot pairing.

[A1] Full AC is required by the knot Seifert-pairing interface; the matrix argument below is choice-free. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] The elementary enlargement matrices are
$$E_0=\begin{pmatrix}V&x&0\\y^T&z&0\\0^T&1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad E_1=\begin{pmatrix}V&x&0\\y^T&z&1\\0^T&0&0\end{pmatrix}.$$
[[def-seifert-matrix-s-equivalence]]

[F2] An integral Seifert lattice has even rank and unimodular skew part; it represents zero in $G_{\mathbb Z}$ when it has a rank-half direct summand on which its bilinear form vanishes, and direct sum is the group operation. [[def-metabolic-seifert-form-and-algebraic-knot-concordance]]

[F3] The negative lattice $-V$ represents the additive inverse of $V$ in $G_{\mathbb Z}$. [[lem-negative-seifert-form-represents-algebraic-inverse]]

## Proof

1.1 For each displayed enlargement, its skew part has block form
$$E_i-E_i^T=\begin{pmatrix}J&u&0\\-u^T&0&s_i\\0^T&-s_i&0\end{pmatrix},$$
where $J=V-V^T$, $u=x-y$, $s_0=-1$, and $s_1=1$. If $V$ is knot-derived, [A1] is the choice hypothesis for its Seifert-pairing convention; the following lattice calculation itself is choice-free. Since $J$ is unimodular, choose the integral vector $c=-J^{-1}u$ and replace the penultimate basis vector by itself plus the old-basis combination $c$. This integral basis change removes the $u$ block and leaves $J\oplus\begin{pmatrix}0&s_i\\-s_i&0\end{pmatrix}$. Both blocks are unimodular, so each enlargement is an integral Seifert lattice. [A1, F1, F2, given, algebra, construct]

1.2 In $E_i\oplus(-V)$, take the subgroup generated by $(e_j; e_j)$ for the old basis vectors $e_1,\ldots,e_{2g}$ and by $(b;0)$, where $b$ is the last basis vector of the enlarged lattice. Its rank is $2g+1$, half the total rank. The two displayed enlargement matrices have zero old-to-$b$, $b$-to-old, and $b$-to-$b$ entries. The $V$ and $-V$ blocks cancel on the diagonal generators, while all pairings involving $(b;0)$ vanish. Therefore the form vanishes on this subgroup. [F1, given, algebra]

2.1 The subgroup in step 1.2 is a direct summand: its generators, together with $(a;0)$ and $(0;e_j)$ for every old basis vector, form an integral basis because subtracting $(0;e_j)$ from $(e_j;e_j)$ gives $(e_j;0)$. It is therefore a metabolizer. By [F2], $[E_i]+[-V]=0$, and [F3] gives $[E_i]=[V]$ in $G_{\mathbb Z}$. [F2, F3, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
