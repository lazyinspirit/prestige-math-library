## Statement

Let $K$ and $J$ be smooth oriented knots, and use the epsilon sign convention from the preceding definition. If their knot Floer complexes are stably filtered equivalent, meaning that there are totally acyclic bifiltered complexes $A$ and $B$ with $\mathrm{CFK}^{\infty}(K)\oplus A$ filtered-chain-homotopy equivalent to $\mathrm{CFK}^{\infty}(J)\oplus B$, then $\epsilon(K)=\epsilon(J)$. The acyclic summands here need not be acyclic after taking either associated-graded filtration.

The invariant satisfies $\epsilon(-K)=-\epsilon(K)$, $\epsilon(K)=0\Rightarrow\tau(K)=0$, and the tensor rules
$$ \epsilon(K)=\epsilon(J)\Rightarrow\epsilon(K\#J)=\epsilon(K),\qquad \epsilon(K)=0\Rightarrow\epsilon(K\#J)=\epsilon(J). $$
The converse of $\epsilon(K)=0\Rightarrow\tau(K)=0$ fails: the knot $T_{2,5}\#-T_{4,5}\#T_{2,3;2,5}$ has $\tau=0$ and $\epsilon=1$, where $T_{p,q}$ is the positive $(p,q)$ torus knot and $T_{2,3;2,5}$ is the $(2,5)$ cable of the right-handed trefoil with longitudinal winding two. Epsilon is not an integer-valued homomorphism on the smooth concordance group: the right-handed trefoil has $\epsilon=1$ and its connected sum with itself also has $\epsilon=1$, not $2$.

## Proof

1.1 In the finite-rank free Maslov-graded knot Floer category, [F4] proves the stable-equivalence conclusion directly. Its finite endomorphism matrices strictify homotopy idempotents and give a unique non-acyclic indecomposable core up to filtered homotopy. The rank-one vertical homology of each original knot complex forces its internal acyclic complementary summands to have acyclic vertical complexes. Consequently its vertical threshold and the zero/nonzero patterns of $F_\tau,G_\tau$ are precisely those of that core. Stable equivalence identifies the two cores even if the externally added acyclic summands have nonzero vertical or associated-graded homology. Thus the three-case epsilon values in [F1] agree. This argument does not apply the epsilon definition to the stabilized complexes themselves. [given, F1, F4, algebra]

2.1 First reduce a finite based Laurent complex by explicit filtered contractions. If a differential coefficient preserves both coordinates, homogeneity makes it a single Laurent monomial; translate its target so that it is $1$. Write $da=b+r$ with $a,b$ at the same bifiltration and replace $b$ by $b'=da$. This is a filtered homogeneous basis change with inverse $b=b'+r$. For every other basis element $c$, subtract the coefficient of $b'$ in $dc$ times $a$ from $c$; this is filtered since the relevant copy of $a$ has the coordinates of the corresponding copy of $b'$. The new differentials contain no $b'$, and their $a$ coefficients vanish by $d^2=0$. Thus the pair $a\to b'$ splits off. Inclusion $I$, projection $P$ and $H(b')=a$, zero on all other generators, satisfy $PI=1$ and $IP+1=dH+Hd$, with $H$ preserving both filtrations and of Maslov degree $+1$. Finitely many repetitions give a reduced complex with no equal-bifiltration arrow. Tensoring these identities with another complex preserves them: the extra differential terms cancel over $\mathbb F_2$. Dualizing likewise preserves them. This supplies all reduction inverse maps and homotopies used below, including their tensor compatibility. [given, F1, construct, algebra]

2.2 Let $s=\tau(C)$ and choose a Maslov-zero vertical cycle $z$ at $j\le s$ representing its distinguished class. Its corner part $z_c$ is nonzero, or the threshold would be smaller. If $F_s=0$, choose a Maslov-one northeast-hook chain $b$ with $d_Nb=z_c$ and split it into strictly upper, corner and strictly right parts $b_+,b_0,b_r$. The strictly upper part of $d_vb_+$ is zero, since the other parts cannot reach it. Hence $z'=z+d_vb_+$ is another distinguished vertical cycle supported at $j\le s$. Put $q=b_0+b_r$. Its horizontal boundary $h=d_hq$ has no strictly right component, and its corner is $z'_c$, by the corner equation of $d_Nb=z_c$. Then $x=z'+h_{\mathrm{left}}$ is supported in $i\le0,j\le s$, its vertical projection represents the distinguished class, and its horizontal projection is the boundary $h$. Conversely, if such an $x$ has horizontal part $d_hq$, project $q$ to the northeast hook: its strictly left part cannot reach the corner or right ray, and the remaining boundary is precisely $x_c$, with no upper component. Thus $F_s=0$. This proves the exact boundary-representative alternative, rather than assuming a simultaneous simplification theorem. [F1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

2.3 If $G_s\ne0$, choose a Maslov-zero southwest-hook cycle mapping to the distinguished vertical class. In the reduced complex it is $x=z+\ell$, with $z$ on the lower vertical ray, including the corner, and $\ell$ strictly left at $j=s$. The two retained boundary equations are exactly $d_vz=0$ and $d_h(z_c+\ell)=0$: a lower ray cannot reach the other ray, and the corner has no equal-bifiltration differential. Conversely these two equations give a southwest-hook cycle mapping to the vertical class. Therefore $G_s\ne0$ is exactly the existence of this simultaneous vertical/horizontal cycle representative. If also $F_s\ne0$, its horizontal cycle is not a boundary, by step 2.2. Each constructed $x$ extends to a homogeneous filtered Laurent basis: choose a nonzero original corner coordinate $e$, whose coefficient is $1$, and replace $e$ by $x$. Homogeneity excludes any other power of that same basis element, and all other terms are no higher in either filtration, so the inverse is the same filtered elementary substitution. These are the needed distinguished representative normal forms. A chosen horizontal noncycle alone is not used as an epsilon test. [F1, step 2.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

2.4 The dual vertical threshold is $-s$. Indeed, if $B$ is the vertical boundary space, the distinguished cycle $z$ is not in $B+V^{\le s-1}$, since such an expression would give a lower-level cycle representing it. Finite-dimensional basis extension gives a cocycle functional annihilating that subspace and taking $z$ to $1$; its dual filtration is at most $-s$. A dual cocycle at level at most $-s-1$ vanishes on every cycle at level $s$ and hence on the one-dimensional vertical homology, so it is a dual boundary. The finite perfect pairing identifies the dual of the northeast hook at $s$ with the southwest hook of $C^*$ at $-s$. The transpose of $F_s(C)$ is $G_{-s}(C^*)$, and the transpose of $G_s(C)$ is $F_{-s}(C^*)$: quotient/inclusion maps reverse under this pairing. Duality of finite-dimensional homology preserves zero/nonzero. Thus $\epsilon(C^*)=-\epsilon(C)$; [F2] identifies this with $\epsilon(-K)=-\epsilon(K)$. [F1, F2, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Normalize Laurent bases at $i=0$. The vertical slice of $C\otimes D$ is the ordinary field tensor of the vertical slices, with differential $d_v\otimes1+1\otimes d_v$; no term lowering algebraic level can survive there. Similarly horizontal slices at levels $s,t$ tensor to the horizontal slice at $s+t$. Splitting a finite field complex into boundary pairs and homology representatives proves the field Kunneth assertion directly: a boundary pair tensored with anything is contractible by its pair homotopy. In particular the tensor of distinguished vertical cycles represents the distinguished tensor class. Its threshold is $s+t$ by [F3]. All representatives used in steps 2.2–2.3 are supported southwest, so their vertical and horizontal projections tensor exactly to the projections of their tensor. [F3, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 2.3, construct, algebra]

3.2 If both epsilon values are $1$, choose the boundary representatives of step 2.2. Both horizontal parts are cycles; if $x_h=d_ha$, then $(x\otimes y)_h=d_h(a\otimes y_h)$. Its vertical part is the distinguished tensor class, so the converse in step 2.2 gives $F_{s+t}=0$, and epsilon of the tensor is $1$. The same proof works when one factor has epsilon zero and the other has epsilon $1$, using a cycle representative from step 2.3 for the zero factor. If both factors have epsilon zero, tensor their southwest-hook lifts to obtain $G_{s+t}\ne0$. Their duals also have epsilon zero by step 2.4; applying the same lift construction to the dual tensor gives its $G_{-(s+t)}\ne0$, hence $F_{s+t}\ne0$ on the original tensor. Its epsilon is therefore zero. Dualizing the already proved positive cases proves the negative/negative and zero/negative cases. Together with [F3], these are exactly both tensor rules in the Statement, with actual representatives and compatible homotopies. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.2, step 2.3, step 2.4, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

The horizontal and vertical intervals meet away from their endpoints at exactly three crossings: $c_1=(\text{row }1,\text{column }2)$, $c_2=(\text{row }2,\text{column }1)$, and $c_3=(\text{row }3,\text{column }3)$. Traversing the component from $O_{0,4}$ encounters $c_1$ over, $c_2$ under, $c_3$ over, $c_1$ under, $c_2$ over, and $c_3$ under. At $c_1$ and $c_2$ the oriented projected tangents $(v_{\rm over},v_{\rm under})$ are $((0,1),(-1,0))$; at $c_3$ they are $((0,-1),(1,0))$. 5.1 Compute the tilde groups of these three new grids by the following finite exact procedure. Enumerate the $n!$ state permutations $p$. For each ordered pair $a\ne b$, take the positive cyclic row interval $a\to b$ and column interval $p(a)\to p(b)$. Its initial corners are southwest and northeast, and the two row orders give the two directed rectangles for each unordered pair. Retain the rectangle exactly when its cell support contains no $O$ or $X$, and no state point lies in the product of its strict interior intervals. Its target swaps $p(a),p(b)$; equal targets cancel over $\mathbb F_2$. Compute each state's degrees directly from $M_O=I(p,p)+I(O,O)-I(p,O)-I(O,p)+1$ and $A=(M_O-M_X-(n-1))/2$. In a column, use the largest target permutation index as pivot. If that pivot has an existing stored column, take symmetric difference with it; otherwise store the column there. A replacement is an invertible field-column operation and strictly lowers the pivot, so distinct stored pivots give precisely the rank. For each grade, homology dimension is basis count minus outgoing rank minus incoming rank. The completed exact computation over all $5040$, $362880$, $3628800$ states respectively gives tilde dimensions $320$, $1792$, $2560$ and the graded polynomials recorded in Remarks. The implementation and full pivot checkpoints are finite arithmetic certificates of this explicit calculation; its square-zero input is the already supplied grid differential, not an unproved reconstruction theorem.
