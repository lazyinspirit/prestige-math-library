## Definition

For any group $G$, define its lower-central series by $\Gamma_1G=G$ and
$\Gamma_{q+1}G=[G,\Gamma_qG]$, where $[G,H]$ is the subgroup generated by
$ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$ for $g\in G$ and $h\in H$. For $r\ge1$, let
$F_r=\langle x_1,\ldots,x_r\rangle$ be the free group on the formal meridian
symbols $x_i$, with the free-group convention of its stated generators.

Let $\mathcal A_r=\mathbb Z\langle\!\langle X_1,\ldots,X_r\rangle\!\rangle$
be the ring of formal integer sums $\sum_w a_wX_w$ over all finite words $w$
in the letters $X_i$, with multiplication given by concatenation of words.
This is the degree-complete ring for the word-length filtration; a series with
invertible constant coefficient is a unit, with inverse determined recursively
degree by degree.
Filter it by total degree (word length), and let $\mathfrak m$ be the ideal of
series with zero constant term.
The Magnus expansion is the group homomorphism
$$
E:F_r\longrightarrow
\mathbb Z\langle\!\langle X_1,\ldots,X_r\rangle\!\rangle^\times,
\qquad E(x_i)=1+X_i.
$$
It sends $x_i^{-1}$ to $(1+X_i)^{-1}=\sum_{k\ge0}(-1)^kX_i^k$. For an
integer $d\ge0$, calculations through degree $d$ use the truncated ring
$R_d=\mathbb Z\langle X_1,\ldots,X_r\rangle/\mathfrak m^{d+1}$. As a
$\mathbb Z$-module it has the finite monomial basis of words of length at most
$d$. In $R_d$, the inverse of $1+A$ for $A\in\mathfrak m$ is the finite sum
$1-A+A^2-\cdots+(-A)^d$.

For a based string link, $x_1,\ldots,x_r$ may be used as formal symbols
corresponding to the positive bottom-end meridians. No freeness or
lower-central quotient isomorphism is asserted here; those properties require
separate proofs.

## Remarks

The commutator convention is $[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}$. Truncation to degree $d$
retains the constant and homogeneous terms of degrees at most $d$.
