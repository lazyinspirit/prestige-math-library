## Statement

For an actual weakly admissible balanced sutured diagram on a surface with boundary, the integral disk complex exists for each specified coherent orientation class. Its disk, triangle and quadrilateral moduli spaces admit finite signed counts and the corresponding oriented boundary identities on actual weakly admissible multidiagrams. Sphere and one-torus boundary bubbles are excluded by the zero boundary-region multiplicities. Orientation classes are transported rather than declared equivalent.

## Proof

**Given:** The actual diagrams, markings, orientation classes, and admissibility hypotheses in the statement.

1.1 New operation, 2026-10-20. The argument below is a domain calculation. It is independent of determinant orientation choices and uses no manifold restriction. [construct]

1.2 Let $\mathcal H=(\Sigma,\alpha,\beta)$ be a balanced sutured diagram. Let $Q$ be the region chain of a disk bubble whose whole boundary lies on $\mathbb T_\alpha$, rather than on alternating alpha and beta tori. Its boundary on the Heegaard surface is a sum of full alpha circles. There is no beta boundary: each beta segment therefore has equal coefficients on its two sides. Consequently the coefficient function of $Q$ is constant on each connected component of $\Sigma\setminus\bigcup\alpha$. Every such component meets $\partial\Sigma$ by balance, and a sutured domain has zero coefficient there. Thus every coefficient of $Q$ vanishes. The identical argument with alpha and beta exchanged handles beta bubbles. Nonnegativity and admissibility are not required for this calculation. [construct]

1.3 This also describes exactly why a closed-diagram one-torus disk cannot simply be carried into the sutured count: a positive component meeting a cap or deleted boundary region is not a sutured disk domain. The argument must be applied after imposing zero multiplicity at **every** sutured boundary component, not just at one selected closed-diagram basepoint. [construct]

1.4 On any actual sutured analytic construction for which the area identity for the operator and symplectic background in use gives energy zero to the zero domain, the above calculation excludes nonconstant alpha and beta bubbles. Compatibility with the almost complex structure then makes zero energy equivalent to constancy, since the energy density is nonnegative and strictly positive wherever the derivative is nonzero. Therefore those bubbles contribute no signed boundary strata at all. No comparison of alpha-bubble and beta-bubble signs is necessary in that setting. [construct]

1.5 The scope of this last implication is deliberate. The domain calculation does not itself construct an area identity for a new symmetric-product background, control sphere classes invisible to the domain map, or prove compactness of sutured moduli spaces. An accepted zero-domain area theorem can be invoked only when its actual target, area-form, divisor and boundary hypotheses hold. It must not be extended from complete closed Heegaard data to arbitrary incomplete sutured systems merely because the same curves can be drawn on a capped surface. Signed strip breaking, triangle/rectangle compatibility, and signed move maps remain separate constructions. [construct]

2.1 New signed construction, 2026-10-20. This concerns an actual chosen diagram whose periodic-domain lattice is zero. It applies to the actual product-cut diagram, and to the rank-one homology-product branch after the earlier determinant reduction. It is not a claim of signed invariance on every general sutured diagram. [construct]

2.2 **Analytic input and the new sign operation.** Supply the actual strip Fredholm realizations with transverse ends, orientability on each map-space component from the framed attaching-torus construction, and the actual linear sewing estimates for sufficiently long matching ends. These are operator/gluing inputs, not integer count identities. In particular a finite cokernel can be killed by finitely many compactly supported target sections; the resulting onto operators have bounded right inverses, and sewing their kernels by cutoff and projection is an isomorphism for long necks. No orientation of that isomorphism is presumed. The following defines it explicitly and checks the needed coherence. [construct]

2.3 For $D_i$ choose an ordered finite-dimensional space $V_i$ of added domain columns making $\widetilde D_i$ onto, and write $K_i=\ker\widetilde D_i$. Use the determinant model [construct]

2.4 $$\det D_i=\det K_i\otimes(\det V_i)^*.$$ [construct]

2.5 For a sewn operator append $V_1\oplus V_2$ in that order. Let $Q_T$ be a right inverse of the augmented sewn operator, and let $\operatorname{pre}_T$ be cutoff sewing on sections and the identity on the finite variables. Its actual kernel comparison is [construct]

2.6 $$G_T(k_1,k_2)=(1-Q_T\widetilde D_T)\operatorname{pre}_T(k_1,k_2).$$ [construct]

2.7 The supplied sewing estimate makes this an isomorphism $K_1\oplus K_2\to\ker\widetilde D_T$ for large $T$. Transport the wedge orientation through this map, with all $K$ factors in component order and then all dual $V$ factors in component order. When moving $V_1^*$ past $K_2$, insert the actual exterior-algebra sign $(-1)^{\dim V_1\dim K_2}$. This defines the determinant-splicing map, including its sign. It is not obtained from an unsigned count. [construct]

2.8 The construction is independent of finite stabilization. Adding an extra column space $W$ changes the kernel determinant by the exact-sequence factor $\det W$ and adds its dual $\det W^*$; contract these adjacent factors using the ordered evaluation pairing. A finite block decomposition into the old kernel, its invertible complement, and the new columns proves this identity even when some new columns land in the old range. Passing to a common stabilization therefore compares any two choices. Varying right inverses causes no sign ambiguity: their space is affine, and the cutoff/projection maps remain isomorphisms for long enough necks uniformly on each compact interpolation path. A continuous nonzero real determinant has constant sign. [construct]

2.9 For three components, use disjoint matching necks and append $V_1,V_2,V_3$ once in that order. Both parenthesizations tend, in the common kernel coordinates, to the same cutoff-sewn direct sum $K_1\oplus K_2\oplus K_3$. Their relative finite-dimensional operator tends to the identity; hence for both necks sufficiently long it lies in the identity component of the invertible matrices. Both exterior-algebra reorderings also give the same final factor order, so their Koszul signs agree. This proves associativity of the actual determinant-splicing maps. The same argument works for any finite number of necks. Shortening a neck within a fixed component transports this identification by determinant-line continuation; it is not a new arbitrary sign choice. [construct]

2.10 For sphere insertion one can construct the needed determinant identification by compact operator excision, without assuming a regular holomorphic sphere gluing theorem. Choose an interior disk on each component, identify their collar bundles by complex frames, and deform their zeroth-order coefficients there to the same standard complex-linear coefficients. This is an affine deformation of actual Cauchy--Riemann operators with unchanged principal symbol and strip ends; determinant orientations transport along it. Cut across the common collars in the two pairs $P\oplus A_S$ and $P\#S\oplus A_0$, where $A_S$ is the closed-sphere operator and $A_0$ the trivial-bundle sphere operator. Rotate the two matching exterior traces by the real two-by-two rotation $R_\theta$, from identity to the exchange at $\theta=\pi/2$. Real multiplication by $R_\theta$ commutes with the common complex symbol. A collar cutoff identifies all transmission domains; after this identification the operator changes only by compactly supported zeroth-order terms. Thus this is an actual continuous Fredholm family, furnishing the orientation excision identification between the two displayed direct sums. Stabilizing by finite target sections gives its determinant model exactly as above. The sphere factors have canonical complex orientations: homotope their real-linear zeroth-order terms to a complex-linear Cauchy--Riemann operator in the contractible affine space of such terms. Its kernel and cokernel are complex vector spaces, and complex orientations cannot change along a complex-linear family. The factor $A_0$ is the complex matching-fiber factor, of real index $2m$. [construct]

2.11 For several sphere cuts choose disjoint collars. Their trace rotations commute, giving the same determinant orientation on either route around the parameter square. Exchanging sphere factors likewise has positive Koszul sign because their real indices and the real index $2m$ of each matching factor are even. Consequently insertion of spheres commutes with strip splicing, and successive insertions use the canonical complex orientation of their sum. This is an operator-orientation construction, not an assertion that a holomorphic sphere bubble or regular nonlinear sphere-gluing chart exists. [construct]

In each connected component of the finite-generator Whitney groupoid choose a base generator $x_0$ and a reference class $\theta_x:x_0\to x$ for every object, with $\theta_{x_0}$ constant. Choose an orientation of each reference determinant component. These are finitely many choices. Their extensions over each component are well-defined by the supplied framed-torus orientability construction. [construct]

2.13 For any class $u:x\to y$, the composite $\theta_x*u*\theta_y^{-1}$ has zero domain because it is periodic and the periodic-domain group vanishes. By the explicit capping argument in the orientation-character donor, this composite is a sphere insertion. Its sphere class is unique: the two boundary caps lie in product tori, and $\pi_2$ of those tori is zero, so changing the caps cannot add a sphere class. Equivalently, the sphere-to-Whitney-class map is injective by this same cap construction. Therefore write [construct]

2.14 $$u=\theta_x^{-1}*S(u)*\theta_y.$$ [construct]

2.15 Orient its determinant using the chosen reference orientations, the inverse reference orientation determined by the constant class, and the canonical complex orientation of $S(u)$. The splicing map constructed above makes this an orientation of the actual determinant component. There is no hidden orientation assigned by a formal sign of a domain: the domain is used only to identify the remaining class as a sphere, and the determinant orientation is transported through actual operator splicing. [construct]

2.16 For composable classes $u:x\to y$, $v:y\to z$, the two adjacent reference factors $\theta_y$ and $\theta_y^{-1}$ contract to the positive constant factor. Associativity and the even complex sphere factors then give $o(u*v)=o(u)\wedge o(v)$ under the actual splicing map. Sphere insertions retain their canonical orientation. This is a coherent strip orientation system on the zero-periodic diagram. A change of reference choices gives only generator sign changes, by the earlier exact character classification. Neither $S^3$ nor an absolute Maslov normalization appears. [construct]

2.17 **What follows for a supplied compactified strip problem.** Suppose the actual zero/one-dimensional strip moduli for this diagram are regular and compactified, and the only boundary strata of the index-two translation quotients are broken pairs of index-one strips. The new coherent orientations orient those compact one-manifolds. In a gluing chart choose the global translation coordinate $s$ and neck separation $T$ so the two component translations are $s_1=s-T/2$, $s_2=s+T/2$. The Jacobian is $+1$, so $ds_1\wedge ds_2=ds\wedge dT$. After quotienting by the positively oriented translation, the endpoint sign of a broken pair is one fixed convention factor times the product of its two rigid-strip signs. That convention factor is the same at every broken pair in the coefficient under consideration. The signed boundary count of a compact oriented one-manifold is zero. Consequently every integer coefficient of $\partial^2$ is zero. [construct]

2.18 One-torus degenerations have zero domain by the separate balanced-domain calculation. They are absent wherever the actual zero-domain energy identity applies. Sphere bubbles must likewise be excluded using the actual analytic hypotheses, rather than by falsely asserting $\pi_2(\operatorname{Sym}^d\Sigma)=0$. The construction above is compatible with canonical sphere orientations but does not replace that exclusion. Thus this paragraph supplies the signed boundary identity once the stated compactification hypotheses have actually been met. [construct]

2.19 **Extend over a triangle with two zero-periodic boundary pairs.** There is a further specific extension relevant to a move between two product-cut diagrams. Suppose a triple $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ has zero periodic-domain groups for the main pairs $(\alpha,\beta)$ and $(\alpha,\gamma)$. Supply orientability of the triangle determinant components and the actual linear sewing problems at their three strip ends. Their determinant identifications are given the finite-stabilization sign convention above. [construct]

2.20 Fix any orbit of triangle classes under splicing boundary disks. Its stabilizers have no nontrivial domain relation: if periodic boundary disks $u_{\alpha\beta},u_{\beta\gamma},u_{\alpha\gamma}$ return a triangle to the same class, then their signed domain sum is zero. The first and third domains are zero by hypothesis, so the auxiliary domain is zero as well. The capping argument makes all three disks sphere insertions. The residual sphere sum acts trivially only when it is the zero sphere class: cap the three boundary loops in their aspherical product tori, whose second homotopy groups vanish. Its determinant action therefore has positive complex orientation, by the sphere excision construction. Thus no stabilizer can reverse a triangle orientation. [construct]

2.21 Choose an orientation of one determinant component per orbit and extend it by the three boundary-disk systems, using the actual splicing maps. The stabilizer calculation proves that this extension is independent of the representative. Associativity of the finite kernel sewing maps proves compatibility with all further disk insertions. The choices require at most Countable Choice: homotopy classes of maps between the finite CW models in use are countable, and the set of generator triples is finite. This constructs compatible triangle orientation systems under the stated actual operator-orientability/sewing inputs. 2.22 In particular this shows why the general-Y strip orientation-character issue does **not** by itself obstruct a triangle joining two homology-product cut diagrams. It also locates the next problem more narrowly. For the inverse-homotopy quadrilateral one must align the three auxiliary pair systems and auxiliary triangle system, not simply orient another isolated triangle component. A relation among those auxiliary periodic classes can remain even though every main pair has zero periodic domains. Such a relation is absent from the two-main-pair stabilizer calculation just proved; it cannot be removed by repeating that calculation for the two triangulations separately. [construct]

3.1 New construction, 2026-10-20. Start with an actual admissible balanced sutured diagram. The surface may have arbitrary genus and several boundary components, and its attaching systems need not be complete systems on a closed surface. The target below is closed only to obtain a compact analytic ambient space. No closed-manifold invariance assertion is imported for the incomplete attaching systems. [construct]

3.2 **1. Construct the actual compact target and its area identity.** Cap every boundary circle of $\Sigma$ by a disk, obtaining a closed Riemann surface $\bar\Sigma$. Retain a point $z_j$ in each cap and a small open collar of the old boundary in the cap-plus-surface. All attaching circles miss these collars. Put $X=\operatorname{Sym}^d\bar\Sigma$, with the actual $d$ of the sutured diagram, not the genus of $\bar\Sigma$. Its product tori are compact, embedded and disjoint from the discriminant. They have their ordered global tangent frames. [construct]

3.3 Choose positive region areas annihilating the sutured periodic-domain subspace. The finite positive-area separation argument applies exactly because admissibility excludes nonzero nonnegative periodic chains; it imposes no rational-homology-sphere assumption. Realize the areas by a smooth positive surface form: start with a sufficiently small positive background form and add a nonnegative smooth bump inside each of the finitely many regions to reach its prescribed integral. Extend it positively over the caps. Call the result $\omega_\Sigma$. [construct]

3.4 The product form on $\bar\Sigma^d$ descends to a smooth Kähler form away from the discriminant and to a continuous-potential positive current $T$ everywhere. To construct a smooth form while preserving the tori, use the following local smoothing. In a cluster coordinate disk take a smooth strictly subharmonic potential $f$ for $\omega_\Sigma$. The symmetric potential is $F(s)=\sum_i f(z_i)$ in the elementary symmetric coordinates $s$ of its roots. It is continuous and plurisubharmonic; on a compact cluster chart it is strictly so. Indeed the quotient polynomial map has bounded derivative there, so $\sum f(z_i)-\varepsilon|s(z)|^2$ remains plurisubharmonic for sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$. Positivity descends through the finite quotient, including its branch locus by continuity. Local differences of these potentials are smooth pluriharmonic functions: they are sums of real parts of holomorphic functions of the roots, hence symmetric holomorphic functions of $s$. [construct]

3.5 Mollify these strictly plurisubharmonic potentials on smaller charts. Paste replacements using a smooth regularized maximum, chosen to equal the larger entry when their difference exceeds its smoothing width. Strict positivity has a positive margin on each compact chart, so choose the mollification error and maximum width below that margin. Give each replacement a small positive offset on its inner chart and a negative offset on its outer collar; the maximum then selects the smooth replacement inside and the old potential near the collar. A finite nested chart cover of the compact discriminant makes these replacements smooth everywhere. Outside that cover leave the old potential unchanged. The resulting smooth Kähler form $\omega$ equals the product form on neighborhoods of **all** attaching tori; its difference from $T$ is $dd^c\psi$ for a global continuous potential supported in that cover. Thus the attaching tori remain Lagrangian. This construction keeps the actual target dimension $d$ throughout. [construct]

3.6 For a strip or polygon standard-holomorphic near the discriminant and with boundary on those tori, the area is [construct]

3.7 $$\int u^*\omega=\sum_R n_R(u)\int_R\omega_\Sigma.$$ [construct]

3.8 Here is the relative, rather than purely absolute, reason. The incidence cover records the individual roots of the configuration; its evaluation to the surface pushes its oriented fundamental chain to the region domain, so integration against $\omega_\Sigma$ is the right-hand side. Off the branch locus this is literally the product-form integral. Near the branch locus the map is standard holomorphic, and the continuous symmetric potential pulls back with positive Hölder exponent; excising small disks there produces boundary integrals tending to zero. Stokes therefore computes the correction by $\psi$ only on the strip/polygon boundary and its ends. That correction is zero because $\psi$ vanishes on neighborhoods of all tori. The same argument on a sphere gives $\int v^*\omega=\operatorname{area}(\bar\Sigma)\,n_z(v)$ for any point divisor. It does not assert that all zero-domain sphere homotopy classes are trivial. [construct]

3.9 **2. Impose every cap condition and exclude the extra degenerations.** Use almost complex data equal to the symmetric integrable structure near the discriminant, near the finitely many point divisors of one test point in **each** complementary region, and near every point-divisor $E_p$ with $p$ in the cap-plus-boundary collars. Count only classes with $n_{z_j}=0$ for **every** cap. These conditions are additive under breaking. A divisor crossing in those standard neighborhoods has positive local multiplicity. The degree is constant as $p$ moves within a complementary boundary region, so zero cap multiplicity forces zero multiplicity for every collar test point. If a coordinate entered a collar, the standard-holomorphic incidence evaluation there would have positive local degree at some test point. Thus every counted map stays in the fixed compact subtarget with no coordinate in those collars. This gives the actual sutured boundary barrier, rather than merely placing one marked point on a capped drawing. [construct]

3.10 The domain coefficients are nonnegative by point-divisor positivity, with zero coefficient on all boundary regions. For fixed endpoints their differences are sutured periodic domains. The finite integer-domain bound follows from admissibility: an unbounded sequence of nonnegative domains, normalized by its norm, would converge to a nonzero nonnegative vector in the real periodic subspace; rational approximation in that rational subspace would give a forbidden integral periodic domain. Hence the domains and, by the displayed area identity, their energies are bounded. [construct]

3.11 Every nonconstant sphere bubble is excluded in the compactification. Its cap degrees are nonnegative and sum to zero, so they vanish. Point-divisor classes are homologous on the closed surface, so its degree at every point divisor is zero; the actual sphere area identity makes its energy zero. A tame holomorphic map with zero energy is constant. A one-torus boundary bubble has zero sutured domain by the balanced complementary-component argument already supplied. The relative area identity likewise makes it constant. These arguments treat the actual smoothing and its actual target, so they do not extend a zero-area statement to an unrelated background. [construct]

Supply coherent orientations with nonzero periodic domains.** The previous determinant-splicing construction gives actual associative signed line maps using finite cokernel columns, cutoff/projection kernel maps and the exterior-algebra factor order. Homotope those loops to their linear winding representatives in $\mathbb R^d/\mathbb Z^d$. Their commutator contracts through the square $(s,t)\mapsto x+s a+t b$ in that torus. The global tangent frame extends across this **actual** square. Cut the four disk operators and these two torus squares into common collar pieces. The constant real-boundary square operator has its canonical ordered $\mathbb R^d$ determinant factor; the matching real-fiber factors cancel with their ordered duals. The two ways to reassemble differ by exchanging the two disk determinant factors and by the capped sphere operator. The exchange sign is $(-1)^{\operatorname{ind}p_i\operatorname{ind}p_j}=+1$, and the sphere factor has its positive complex orientation. There is no additional framing loop: both torus squares were filled in their global frames. This computes the commutator sign, rather than assuming a free abelian domain lattice automatically splits an arbitrary sign extension. [construct]

3.21 Every constant continuation strip at a generator is regular. Its linearization is $\xi_s+J_{s,t}(x)\xi_t$, with the two fixed transverse linear Lagrangian planes and zero limits. The zeroth-order derivative-of-$J$ term is zero because the underlying map is constant. A kernel section has linear symplectic area zero: the standard linear Liouville form vanishes on either plane, and the end terms vanish. The equation gives $\xi_t=J_{s,t}(x)\xi_s$, so tameness makes this area the integral of a strictly positive quadratic form in $\xi_s$. Hence $\xi=0$. Its Fredholm index is zero by deformation to the constant-coefficient transverse operator, so its cokernel is zero as well. This is an actual kernel argument, not an assumption that arbitrary endpoint jet changes preserve regularity. [construct]

3.22 Use actual generic continuation data relative to these regular constant strips. The cap barrier, area bound and bubble exclusions apply unchanged. Orient their index-zero operators by determinant continuation from the chosen endpoint systems; the constant strip has coefficient $+1$ with the identity convention. Index-one continuation compactifications give the signed chain-map identity. For a reverse path, stretch and then contract the concatenation through a two-parameter continuation family. 3.23 With an annihilating area form there is also a finite explicit inverse for the continuation matrix. In each Whitney-groupoid component assign a generator the area of a reference disk from a base generator. Periodic domains and zero-cap sphere insertions have zero area, so differences of those assigned values are unambiguous. A nonconstant continuation has strictly positive area and moves strictly in that finite action order. The constant solutions give the diagonal identity. Thus its matrix is $1+N$ with $N$ strictly triangular, and its integer inverse is the finite alternating sum $1-N+N^2-\cdots$. 3.24 To align choices constructed using different positive surface forms, compare each endpoint at its **own** form to a sufficiently small perturbation of the symmetric integrable structure. For a finite list of forms, those reference perturbations can be chosen tame for all of their smoothed Kähler forms, since the common integrable structure has a positive tameness margin on their compact targets. At a shared diagram fix one such form and use the preceding continuation construction to compare the reference data there. This does not require an arbitrary original endpoint to be tame for a foreign form. If target complex charts are held fixed, the construction aligns the actual forms and generator jets needed at the junctions of a finite move word. [construct] ∎
