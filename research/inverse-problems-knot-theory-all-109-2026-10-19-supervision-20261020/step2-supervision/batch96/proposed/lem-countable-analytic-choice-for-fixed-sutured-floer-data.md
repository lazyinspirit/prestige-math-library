## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Fix a finite collection of admitted compact marked sutured diagrams, a finite sequence of diagram moves or surface cuts, and finite-dimensional parameter families of dimension at most two. Suppose the local Fredholm, finite-dimensional Sard, compactness and gluing constructions are supplied for their actual Cauchy–Riemann operators, including the prescribed endpoint and protected-neighborhood conditions. All selections needed to apply those constructions, choose coherent determinant orientations, and obtain simultaneous regular data can then be made under Countable Choice. In particular no uncountable choice of analytic charts or regular data is needed for these fixed finite integral Floer constructions.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] [[def-countable-choice]] supplies choices from a countable family of nonempty sets. Finite choices require no additional axiom.

[F2] The supplied local operator constructions are used pointwise, not as an assumption that one has already chosen a family over an uncountable index set. Their Fredholm normal forms and finite-dimensional Sard conclusions are part of the hypotheses of this selection lemma.

## Proof

**Given:** The finite admitted geometric data in the statement and their pointwise local analytic constructions.

1.1 A finite surface cellulation gives finitely many regions and finitely many generator tuples. Polygon domains have integer coefficients in that finite region set; periodic groups are subgroups of finite free integer groups and can be given bases by integer row operations. Zero-domain sphere insertions are indexed by integers. Thus all relevant classes, corner tuples, strip/triangle/quadrilateral types and parameter types form a countable set. The compact targets have finite coordinate atlases. Choose rational Sobolev exponents and rational weights in the finitely many endpoint spectral gaps. On compact strip exhaustions, rational piecewise polynomial functions in the coordinate charts, with the prescribed asymptotic references, form countable dense sets in the finite-order Sobolev spaces. The required reference paths between the finite generator sets are finite choices. If reference maps are used separately for homotopy classes, their index set is countable and F1 selects them. These constructions give actual second-countable mapping spaces, rather than an assertion of arbitrary Banach-space separability. [given, construct]

1.2 For almost-complex structures, use a fixed admitted reference and its local Cayley coordinates. Allowed anti-commuting tensor perturbations are supported away from the protected region and satisfy the specified endpoint conditions. Rational coordinate bump functions and rational polynomial tensors on a countable exhaustion of that open support give countable dense subsets. The smooth space has the complete metric obtained by summing 2 to the minus k times the truncated C-to-the-k seminorm distances. The fixed boundary/family-jet conditions define closed affine subspaces of the parameter tensors. Tameness is enforced by working in an open Cayley chart and taking the first closed metric ball strictly inside it. For a family of dimension zero, one or two, take the product with its compact finite-dimensional parameter cube and impose its fixed boundary jets in the same tensor description. No unrestricted product of nonempty sets is used. [step 1.1, construct]

1.3 The universal solution spaces inherit countable bases from these mapping and parameter spaces. Their pointwise Fredholm charts form an open cover. For each basic open set contained in some such chart, choose one chart containing it, with its finite kernel, finite cokernel and finite stabilizing perturbations. This is a countable family of nonempty choices, so F1 applies. The chosen charts cover every solution: every solution has a basic neighborhood inside its pointwise chart. Finite-dimensional bases, cokernel columns and local frames are then chosen only over this countable family. This is the exact place where a casual choice of one chart for every solution would unnecessarily invoke full Choice. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 1.2]

1.4 In one chosen Fredholm normal form the projection to perturbation data is $(x,y)\mapsto(x,g(x,y))$, with $x$ in a Banach space and $y$ in a finite-dimensional kernel coordinate space. Criticality is failure of $D_yg$ to surject onto the finite-dimensional second target factor. Shrink the chart and take a closed product $B\times Q$ contained in its domain, where $B$ is a closed Banach ball and $Q$ is a closed finite-dimensional cube. Its critical-value image is closed relative to the target chart: if $(x_j,g(x_j,y_j))$ converges to $(x,z)$, then $x_j\to x$ because $x_j$ is the FIRST TARGET COORDINATE; no compactness of the Banach ball is used. Compactness of $Q$ gives a subsequence $y_j\to y$, and closedness of $B$ and continuity give $(x,y)\in B\times Q$, $g(x,y)=z$. The nonsurjective finite-dimensional derivative condition is closed, so the limit is critical. Intersect this image with a closed inner target ball lying in the coordinate chart to obtain an exclusion closed in the whole perturbation space. It has empty interior: any target open product has some fixed first coordinate $x$, and finite-dimensional Sard for $y\mapsto g(x,y)$ supplies a second coordinate outside its critical values. Countable-basis centers and rational radii give countably many such domain products and inner target balls covering every chart point and its image. Their critical-value images are thus only countably many closed nowhere-dense exclusions. Taking the union over the countable class and family list remains countable. [F2, step 1.3, construct]

1.5 For smooth perturbations the same exclusions are closed in the stronger smooth topology, and their complements are dense there. Indeed, the local surjectivity directions can be taken smooth and supported in the allowed open region. Their span is finite dimensional. In that span the finite-dimensional Sard perturbation can be made arbitrarily small in any specified finite list of C-to-the-k seminorms; it therefore meets every basic smooth neighborhood. At parameter boundaries the endpoint regularity is already supplied, and gluing collars are fixed; the perturbations act only in the remaining interior. Thus the same countable residual construction applies simultaneously with every prescribed relative condition. [F2, step 1.2, step 1.4]

1.6 Here no appeal to an unrestricted form of the Baire theorem is needed. Enumerate a countable basis of metric balls and enumerate the dense open complements U_n of the exclusions. Having chosen a closed ball B_n, choose the least-index basic ball with positive radius less than 2 to the minus n minus one whose closure lies in the interior of B_n intersected with U_n. Such a ball exists by density and openness. This is recursion on the natural numbers with a unique least-index choice, not a use of Dependent Choice. Its centers form a Cauchy sequence; completeness gives a limit in every B_n, hence in every U_n. Starting with a closed ball inside the permitted Cayley chart keeps the limit tame and preserves the fixed boundary data. The result is one actual simultaneous regular choice for all the fixed problems. [step 1.2, step 1.5, construct]

1.7 For compactness, retain the supplied energy bounds and the actual cap-divisor or basepoint barriers, so every sequence lies in the same compact target region. Index the local elliptic estimates by the countable operator charts, integer energy bounds, integer derivative orders, rational error tolerances and compact strip intervals with integer endpoints. Whenever an estimate needs a constant, take the least integer larger than a valid positive constant. A gradient blow-up center may be taken as the least-index rational grid point where the gradient exceeds half its relevant supremum, and its rescaling factor as a rational within the required factor-two interval. Thus no selection over all points of a limiting curve is needed. On every compact interval and in every derivative order, use the supplied local precompactness and the countable Sobolev bases to extract a subsequence by least-index balls containing infinitely many terms. The diagonal subsequence is indexed by the paired natural-number list of intervals and derivative orders. Its limits are consistent because the subsequences are nested. Bubble and end limits are obtained by this same countable construction; the supplied domain-area identity, positivity and bubble exclusion apply to these actual limits. After the given compactification bounds the number of nonconstant levels, only finitely many breaking levels are retained. This is the complete selection scope of the compactness application, not a new compactness theorem for arbitrary targets. [F2, step 1.1, step 1.3, construct]

1.8 For gluing, use the finite local coordinate covers and the already supplied operator gluing estimates on each broken-curve chart. Work in higher Sobolev Hilbert spaces with the fixed finite-atlas inner products. The inverse on the orthogonal complement of the finite kernel is canonical; for an onto stabilized operator D it is D* times the inverse of DD*. Thus it needs neither a Hahn–Banach extension nor a choice of bases in an arbitrary infinite-dimensional space. The glued approximate inverse is corrected by its convergent Neumann series. Choose the neck threshold as the least integer beyond the valid local estimate, and choose all contraction radii as smaller rational numbers. The correction is the limit of the contraction iterates starting at zero, a specified recursion. The broken-curve spaces have the countable chart covers already constructed in step 1.3; at a bounded compact end a finite subcover supplies a uniform estimate if needed. Exhausting the finitely many parameter types and the countable energy/domain indices therefore introduces only countably many chart choices. The uniqueness in the contraction neighborhoods and the supplied inverse gluing construction give the actual local charts for the compactification, with no new selection over an uncountable family of broken curves. [F1, F2, step 1.3, step 1.7, construct]

1.9 Coherent-orientation construction introduces no larger selection. Ordered tangent frames are finite data on the finite attaching tori. Reference paths are finite, periodic bases are finite, and sphere determinants are complex oriented. The local finite-kernel determinant splices are chosen on the countable chart family from step 1.3, and their compatibility is an algebraic equality of finite exact-sequence determinants. The finite positive region-area separation and finite matrix operations in the admitted geometric construction use finite-dimensional choices. By steps 1.7–1.8, compactness and gluing also involve only countable charts and specified limits. Thus every selection in the stated fixed finite application is finite or countable. This proves the asserted Countable Choice branch without weakening a separate theorem declared under full Choice. [F1, F2, step 1.3, step 1.6, step 1.7, step 1.8] ∎
