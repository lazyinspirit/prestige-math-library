## 1. The literal normal predicate

MT requires a round Euclidean boundary sphere whose equator is a Conway
circle, cutting the diagram into T and U, such that EACH diagram contains
at least one classical crossing and NEITHER represented tangle is trivial.
These are three distinct conditions. A rigid vertex is an actual flat
square disk, and a graph morphism maps this disk isometrically. The given
map is part of the triple throughout.

For the graph proof the distinction between a single graph vertex and a
trivial two-arc tangle matters. MT 4.10 explicitly allows the reduction of
the chosen side to one or more graph vertices with no classical crossings,
and handles that branch separately. Thus one cannot replace its normal
predicate by the assertion "every reduced side contains a crossing".

Likewise "neither tangle trivial" is not, by itself, an actual concordance
or a fundamental-group injection. For application of the newly authored
Menasco-1984 supplier, geometric incompressibility and the literal
once-punctured-disk pairwise condition must be derived, in the actual finite
PL exterior, rather than silently imported under the word Conway.

## 2. What is genuinely formal once the local decoration is fixed

Let J be an ACTUAL marked four-port link tangle in a vertex ball. Suppose
the source graph map has been changed by a specified pair isotopy so that
it is a specified rigid isometry on a neighborhood of every flat vertex
disk. Choose pairwise disjoint sufficiently small closed vertex balls
inside these neighborhoods; choose the target balls as their images. Put
J in each source ball and its image under that same isometry in the
corresponding target ball. The given map now extends to the decorated
link/tangle map simply by restriction of that very map. Away from the
balls neither the embedded graph nor the map is changed.

This uses a SINGLE normalized map. It does not imply that an arbitrary
later isotopy through weak disk-rigid morphisms is normalized on a common
bicollar. The bridge owns that separate parametric germ obligation.

If each inserted diagram has exactly five crossings, disjointness of the
vertex balls gives

    c(T^J) = c(T) + 5 nu(T).

If the given graph map takes the tangle T_1 to T_2, it gives a bijection of
their actual singular disks, so nu(T_1)=nu(T_2). This equality is topological
node bookkeeping; it must not be inferred by subtracting two unknown
crossing counts. Labels on nodes and on the boundary ports are transported
by the actual normalized map.

These formal facts require no essential Conway sphere, no visibility
isotopy, and no claim that an arbitrary link isotopy lifts to graphs.

## 3. Boundary-isometry consumer: only a count transfer is needed

MT 4.9 uses Theorem 3.1 of the 1991 paper. Its two reduced tangle diagrams
represent the same graph tangle under the given map. Conditional on the
following finite J property, the crossing-count transfer is complete:

    J-count property: inserting J in every node of a reduced alternating
    four-port graph-tangle diagram yields a reduced alternating LINK-tangle
    diagram with exactly c+5nu crossings. Transport of J by an arbitrary
    rigid neighborhood isometry yields a competing diagram of the image
    tangle with the same five crossings per node, even if that competitor
    is not alternating.

Choose the alternating insertion on side 1 and transport it through the
given map. One-boundary tangle minimality gives

    c(T_1)+5nu(T_1) <= c(T_2)+5nu(T_2).

Choose the alternating insertion independently on side 2 and use the
inverse given map for the reverse inequality. Node equality cancels the
five-crossing terms, giving c(T_1)=c(T_2). Both directional comparisons
preserve the actual map for that comparison. They do NOT presume that the
same transported decorations are alternating at both ends.

This last distinction is explicit in MT 4.1: page 138 says the transported
resolved diagram D_2 need not be alternating. Any extension that omits
this sentence may introduce an unjustified simultaneous-decoration
hypothesis.

The remaining braid/closure contradiction in 4.9 can therefore use this
count transfer, the actual boundary mapping-class representative, and the
specified Figure-4.5 closure. It has no logical need for graph Conway
visibility or marker-return isotopies. Its additional finite reducedness
and closure conditions remain the boundary-recognition author's scope.

## 4. Normal sphere essentiality: the smallest new local geometry

For a literal visible P^4 sphere in a prime alternating link diagram,
Menasco 1985 provides the relevant missing geometric analysis, rather than
a blanket nontriviality implication. Its Theorem 1 analyzes an actual
ordinary or once-punctured compressing disk after normalizing that DISK
relative to the specified sphere. The two possible terminal patterns are:

* a circle revisits one complementary projection region;
* upper and lower circles have three common-bubble-adjacent arcs as in
  Figure 1/11(b).

The disk normalizations are finite surgeries of the helper compressing
disk, not substitutions of an arbitrary surface. Lemma 4 gives the exact
no-empty-region arcs, no-repeated-bubble, disk-piece, and no-surface/bubble
return conditions. Lemma 5 excludes closed intersection circles by the
Menasco-1984 grouped-word calculation. The remaining disk tiling has an
outermost cap or an adjacent pair of one-bubble caps (Figure 10), giving
the two displayed obstructions.

For a P^4 sphere Theorem 2(c) and Figure 15 reduce the common-bubble case
to one actual sphere slide across that bubble. Its proof explicitly uses
the Figure-11(b) product isotopy and the Figure-3/4 bubble-cancellation
isotopies. This is the smallest source-backed finite geometry that can
connect MT's actual normal predicate to the ordinary plus pairwise
incompressibility input. A complete local derivation still has to bind
failure of its test to an actually trivial tangle on the corresponding
side, with all rigid-marker balls retained; it is NOT proved by naming
Theorem 2(c) here.

The repeated-region obstruction has a useful planar reduction under the
MT hypotheses: join two visits within the region, selecting a cut-off
circle with two diagram intersections. Diagram primeness forces an arc
on one side. The nonzero-crossing conditions on BOTH original sides must
be retained when identifying which side is the empty arc disk. This
reduction by itself does not dispose of the common-bubble pattern.

## 5. Visibility consumer: additional requirements remain

MT 4.10 uses 1991 Proposition 5.1. A proof by resolving graph vertices
would need all of the following, with the actual SAME marked J:

1. The two relevant resolved link diagrams satisfy the hypotheses used in
   the link visibility proof. In particular, either their transported
   decorations really are both alternating, or one supplies a different
   valid count argument. The two-directional count proof of section 3
   alone does not establish this.
2. The normal sphere in the resolved link is geometrically and pairwise
   incompressible, by the preceding local geometry and an actual argument
   that inserting J retains the required nontrivial sides.
3. The resulting visibility isotopy of that resolved link can be returned
   to an isotopy of the GIVEN rigid graph map, retaining the labelled
   vertex disks and their isometric germs. A generic link isotopy is
   allowed to move the J-marker balls through the sphere. Its final
   sphere being visible does not say that the transported marker balls
   are the original target marker balls, on their required sides.

Consequently the minimal additional finite supplier should have this
joint output: a normalized given graph map extends over the explicit J
decoration; the relevant sphere is essential there; and the specific
link-sphere visibility construction can be chosen/modified to preserve
and return the labelled J-marker balls with their actual boundary germs.
The weak-to-strong germ lemma alone cannot recognize a transported J ball
or move it back across a Conway sphere.

No arbitrary rigid-graph normal form, generic TOP surface normalization,
or unrestricted lifting of link isotopies is proposed as a substitute.
