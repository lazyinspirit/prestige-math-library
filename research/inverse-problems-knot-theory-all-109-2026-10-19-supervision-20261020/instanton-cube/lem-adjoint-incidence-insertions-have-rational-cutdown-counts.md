## Statement

Assume full AC. Fix a smooth oriented four-dimensional cobordism with product ends and an actual determinant-one ASD background. In the singular case all insertion supports below lie in its ordinary locus. Supply finite nondegenerate irreducible critical data at its temporal ends, oriented determinant modules, and the following analytic data: regular moduli in all relative classes and parameter strata under consideration; a common energy bound at each fixed upper Fredholm index over those classes; Uhlenbeck subsequential compactness on the compact core with ordinary bubble index loss eight and singular bubble index loss at least four; exponential ends, exhaustive broken collars and associative determinant gluing. In the ordinary case these are data for that ordinary theory, and are not consequences of an earring theorem by a change of terminology.

Choose finitely many distinct interior points and oriented compact embedded interior surfaces. The points avoid the surfaces; the surfaces meet transversely in double points and have no triple points, and avoid any singular surface. Then there are rational geometric insertions representing
$$\mu(z)=-\tfrac14p_1(\mathbb E)/z,$$
where $\mathbb E$ is the universal real adjoint bundle. A point insertion has real codimension four and a surface insertion has real codimension two. They can be chosen coherently for the stated finite families and their prescribed broken faces. For cutdown total dimensions zero and one the counts are finite rational numbers and the compactified cutdowns are oriented zero- and one-manifolds with the usual broken and parameter-face terms. No bubble contributes to either cutdown. Moving these insertions through prescribed compact paths or surface bordisms gives the corresponding parameterized relations whenever those supports and analytic data are supplied. Gluing marked cores takes the product of insertions and their rational weights; the insertions have even degree.

This assertion constructs insertion cycles and the additional cutdown compactness argument from the stated analytic data. It does not construct ordinary closed Floer homology, prove its quotient-gauge orientation bridge, identify a product-cap unit or insertion basis, or evaluate a nonzero Donaldson invariant. In particular it supplies no K3, elliptic-surface, or genus-two gluing evaluation.

## Proof

1.1 **The actual universal bundle.** On the space of irreducible connections, divide pairs $(A,v)$, with $v\in(\operatorname{ad}P)_x$, by the same determinant-one gauge transformations that act on $A$. The only stabilizers are central and act trivially on $v$. The local Coulomb slice therefore identifies this quotient with a genuine oriented real rank-three vector bundle over the irreducible configuration quotient times the ordinary locus of the cobordism. Changes of slice act by the adjoint representation and satisfy the actual gauge cocycle. This constructs $\mathbb E$ without assuming a global universal rank-two lift. Its complexification $E$ is a complex rank-three bundle, with its usual complex orientation. Restrict it to the actual moduli and marked supports; no quotient containing a reducible connection is used. [given, F1, construct]

2.1 **A point cycle with its coefficient, not a named formal operator.** On a finite-dimensional regular moduli chart $M$ take two smooth sections $s_0,s_1$ of $E$ at a chosen point. Over $M\times\mathbb{CP}^1$ their linear combination is a section $S=s_0z_0+s_1z_1$ of $E\otimes\mathcal O(1)$. A local change of projective representative multiplies this formula by exactly the transition of $\mathcal O(1)$. Perturb the two sections so $S$ is transverse to zero and their common zero is avoided in the cutdown dimensions at issue. Its zero set has codimension six upstairs. Where the pair has rank one its kernel is one projective line point, so projection gives the usual codimension-four rank-drop incidence downstairs, with multiplicity one. Keeping the projective incidence itself also works and avoids choosing a kernel coordinate. To calculate its class write $u=c_1(\mathcal O(1))$, with $\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1}u=1$ and $u^2=0$. The top Chern class upstairs is $c_3(E\otimes\mathcal O(1))=c_3(E)+u c_2(E)$. Thus its oriented pushforward represents $c_2(E)=-p_1(\mathbb E)$. Give this geometric cycle weight $1/4$. Its class is exactly $\mu(\mathrm{pt})$, including the sign and denominator. This calculation can be checked in local complex frames by the splitting polynomial $\prod_{j=1}^3(u+x_j)$; the symmetric polynomial identity is independent of those frames. [F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 **A surface cycle.** Restrict the same universal bundle to $M\times\Sigma$ and choose its two sections there. Take the same projective incidence on $M\times\Sigma\times\mathbb{CP}^1$. Its zero section has codimension six; the two oriented auxiliary factors have total dimension four. Projection to $M$, retaining incidence multiplicities if it is not embedded, has codimension two. Its class is $c_2(E)/[\Sigma]=-p_1(\mathbb E)/[\Sigma]$, by the identical projective integration. Weight it by $1/4$. Orient its zero set by the determinant orientation of $M$, the given surface orientation, the complex projective orientation and the complex normal orientation, in that order. For several insertions use separate copies of their marked support and projective parameters and intersect the actual zero sections. If there are $a$ point and $b$ surface insertions their combined rational weight is $4^{-(a+b)}$ and their codimension is $4a+2b$. This is not an assertion that arbitrary pointwise degeneracy equations in a nontrivial universal bundle have integral quarter-classes. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 **Sections at limits away from an insertion collision.** An Uhlenbeck limit consists of a smooth lower-charge connection and finitely many bubble points. On a compact ball not containing those points choose its actual Coulomb comparison gauges; convergence on smaller balls is smooth. Evaluation of those gauges gives the transition maps for the restriction of $\mathbb E$ there. Consequently this restriction is a bundle also over each limiting chart in which the evaluation point stays away from the bubbles. This does not extend the universal bundle through a bubble at the evaluation point. Choose the incidence sections first on those lower strata and on the prescribed broken faces, then extend in these local trivializations; a locally finite partition of unity glues extensions. Use the prescribed face product identifications when two temporal breaks occur, so they agree on their common corners. Local changes of a section in its complex fiber span the full finite-dimensional target at a zero, so the universal zero-section problem is onto. Finite-rank section perturbations on a chart, relative to its already fixed closed subsets, make its intersection transverse. Exhausting the countable moduli charts and countable class/stratum labels with decreasing perturbations gives simultaneous transverse choices. Countability comes from the finite cell model of the fixed smooth core and its product ends, not from a claim of finitely many classes at fixed index. The same construction applies with compact parameter factors. At the rank-zero subset of a pair, the two independent complex sections impose twelve real equations; in the incidence resolution this has six more codimensions than the ordinary rank-one incidence. Its projected singularity is consequently absent in cutdown dimensions zero and one, also in their one-parameter relations, after these simultaneous choices. [F1, F3, given, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 **Dimension accounting for a bubble at a marked support.** Write $N$ for the ambient total index, including metric parameters, $q=4a+2b$ for the imposed codimension, and $d=N-q\le1$. First suppose an ordinary bubble of charge $k\ge1$ occurs at a point away from all insertions. Its lower connection has index $N-8k$, and its position contributes four. All insertions persist by step 4.1, so the limiting cutdown dimension is $d-8k+4\le d-4$. At a marked point the whole point insertion may be lost, so do not impose its four equations on the lower connection. But the bubble position is now fixed, removing its four position parameters. The same upper bound $d-8k+4$ results. At a point of one marked surface one may lose its two equations, but the position now has dimension two, again giving $d-8k+4$. At a double intersection of two marked surfaces one may lose four equations, but its position is discrete, with the same bound. There are no other possibilities: distinct marked points avoid all surfaces and the surfaces have no triple points. This accounts for every incidence condition that may disappear, rather than claiming it extends across its own bubble. Several bubbles repeat the argument at their distinct positions; a higher charge at one position loses no additional insertion conditions and improves the bound. A singular bubble has position dimension two and meets no insertion support, so loses at least four in index and contributes only two position parameters: its bound is $d-2$. All these limiting strata have negative dimension for $d=0,1$ and are empty by simultaneous regularity and the transverse surviving incidences of step 4.1. Thus bubbles cannot occur in the stated cutdowns. Bubble scales, framings and the bubble's own internal moduli are not added to an Uhlenbeck limiting stratum; its compactification records its charge and position. The negative dimension is that of the actual lower connection and retained data. [F2, given, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 **Compactness over classes and temporal breaks.** The ambient index in this construction is bounded by $q+1$ plus the fixed finite parameter dimension. The assumed index-energy bound is therefore a single bound on the whole cutdown union, including all relative classes; one cannot substitute the old uncut dimension-one bound. Apply the assumed Uhlenbeck subsequential extraction on this budget. Step 5.1 rules out every bubble. All insertions lie in a fixed compact core. They stay on that core when trajectories escape down temporal ends. Their constraints therefore persist on the limiting core factor. End convergence and the discrete positive index/energy periods give finitely many nonconstant temporal levels. Regularity makes every nonconstant level have its actual positive pre-translation index; the usual translation quotient and matching then leave, in cutdown dimension one, only the single ordinary differential break, prescribed metric breaks and parameter faces. Dimension zero has no break. The supplied exhaustive nonlinear collars apply: the incidence sections on broken faces were the pullbacks of those on the core, and section transversality combines their finite-dimensional normal inverse with the analytic normal inverse. The implicit function theorem therefore cuts each such collar by the same incidence equations, with no additional branch. Smooth closeness and the unique normal correction keep its relative bundle label constant. The dimension-zero union is compact and discrete, hence finite. The dimension-one union is an oriented compact one-manifold with its exhausted boundary and finitely many charts. This proves finiteness of contributing classes without assuming that all abstract classes of fixed index form a finite set. [F1, F2, given, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct]

7.1 **Boundary signs, gluing and moving insertions.** The normal bundle of each incidence equation is complex oriented, and its auxiliary surface and projective factors were explicitly oriented in step 3.1. Cutting down the supplied determinant gluing exact sequence by these factors gives the same exterior-product orientation on a collar as first cutting down its core factor and then gluing. All inserted codimensions are even; interchanging independent insertions introduces no extra sign. The outward normal to the gluing or parameter coordinate consequently gives precisely the differential, composition and parameter-face signs of the original oriented count. A compact oriented one-manifold has algebraic boundary zero. Multiplying by the common weight $4^{-(a+b)}$ preserves this identity over the rationals. A compact motion of a point, or a supplied oriented surface bordism with its product boundary collars, adds its actual support parameter to the incidence construction. The same counting argument applies: its generic support intersections have the listed dimensions, and there are no excess multiple coincidences after relative transversality. It gives the claimed parameterized identities. For a composition, put the insertion supports in the specified separate compact cores, use their sections there, and prescribe their product on the broken face. Their auxiliary orientations and rational weights multiply exactly. No split-link identity or nonzero cap value is used. [F1, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra] ∎

## Authoring boundary

This supplies the incidence realization, coefficient normalization and the new cutdown bubble argument. Its analytic hypotheses are explicit because the accepted earring saddle/disk theorem does not define a nonsingular closed determinant-one theory or its product relative invariants. To apply this donor to $\Sigma\times D^2$, the owner must supply that theory's actual nondegenerate boundary data, all-index regular moduli, index-energy bound, Uhlenbeck compactness, determinant orientations and broken gluing. A nonzero product or K3 witness is a further enumerative assertion. No such assertion follows from these geometric representatives or from the existence of a codimension-four point cycle.
