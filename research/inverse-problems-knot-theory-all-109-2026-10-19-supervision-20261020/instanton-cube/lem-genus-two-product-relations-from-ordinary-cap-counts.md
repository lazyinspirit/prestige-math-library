## Statement

Assume full AC and the separately constructed ordinary count, K3 ASD seed, exceptional-end, and genus-two cap-basis interfaces below. In the signed degree-four quotient theory of $Y=\Sigma_2\times S^1$, write $m=\mu(\Sigma_2)$ and $p=\mu(\mathrm{pt})$. Its even algebra, with the actual product cap as unit, is
$$A_{\mathrm{even}}=\mathbb Q[m]/(m^2(m^2-4)),\qquad p=m^2-2.$$
On its odd homology $m=0$ and $p=-2$. Consequently the $p=2$ sector has precisely the two simple one-dimensional surface eigenspaces $m=2,-2$. The unit has nonzero projection to each, and their oriented inverse pairings are respectively $-32,+32$. In particular on the entire product group
$$m^2(m^2-4)=0,\qquad p=m^2-2.$$
This statement concerns genus two. It supplies neither a higher-genus recurrence nor a surface-with-boundary insertion construction. All named new analytic suppliers remain author-stage premises, without an assertion of independent acceptance.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Rational coefficients, the signed quotient conventions, and the actual K3 ASD seed used by the exceptional-end construction.

[F1] Ordinary finite oriented cobordism counts, insertion sliding and their compactified gluing are [[thm-odd-admissible-ordinary-cobordisms-support-insertion-counts]]. Full AC is used there. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F2] The actual genus-two cap classes $e_i$ for $0\le i\le3$ form the entire even basis. Their pairing matrix is $N$ with anti-diagonal entries $-1/2$ and additional entries $N_{23}=N_{32}=-2$, all other entries zero. The actual small perturbation has eight generators, with Morse indices $0,3,2,3,3,4,3,6$ relative to its common normal shift. [[lem-genus-two-trace-morse-perturbation-gives-the-cap-class-basis]]

[F3] For the actual twice-blown-up K3 complement, its even relative vector $v$ has pairings $\langle v,e_1\rangle=\langle v,e_3\rangle=0$ and $\langle v,e_2\rangle=2$. Both ordinary exceptional flat sectors give the identity, both minimal odd sectors together give $E_1E_2\mapsto1$, and higher local charges with at most three exceptional insertions have negative exterior cut dimension. The K3 seed gives $D^F(x)=2$ and its determinant/reflection identifications. [[lem-two-ordinary-exceptional-ends-give-the-needed-low-insertion-counts]]

[F4] The ordinary odd torus quotient is one-dimensional with actual point action $-2$ and nonzero flat product cap. Cutting and regluing an odd square-zero torus gives the closed sum $D^w+D^{w+R}$ with each extension counted once. [[lem-kahler-orientation-and-signed-product-quotient-gluing]]

## Proof

1.1 **The algebra is defined by actual cobordisms.** Use $\Sigma_2$ times a pair of pants, oriented as two incoming and one outgoing circles, with determinant pulled from the odd surface bundle. The ordinary all-class counts of [F1] define its product. Gluing the actual product cap into either incoming boundary gives the cylinder, with the determinant-line identity orientation, so $e_0$ is its unit. Gluing two pairs of pants in the two ways and using the compactified one-parameter neck family proves associativity. The same family interchanging incoming ends gives the graded commutativity sign. Insertion sliding across the glued collar identifies multiplication by $e_i$ with $m^i$, and multiplication by the point-inserted cap with $p$ on every input. Thus this is a geometric product, not a presentation inferred from a pairing alone. By [F2] the even algebra is generated by the unit and $m$, with basis $1,m,m^2,m^3$. In the quotient grading modulo four, $m$ has degree two and $p$ degree zero. Therefore
$$m^4=bm^2+\delta,\qquad p=A+Bm^2.$$
Let $\tau(a)=\langle a,1\rangle$. The actual cap matrix gives $\tau(m^3)=-1/2$, $\tau(m^5)=-2$, and $\tau(m)=0$. Taking $\tau(m\,m^4)$ proves $b=4$. At this point $\delta$ is genuinely undetermined. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 **Additional low closed counts retain their exterior constraints.** For the twice-blown-up K3 $B$ of [F3], put $S=\sigma+2F$ and $\Sigma=S-E_1-E_2$. In the no-exceptional-insertion sector, each even exceptional end is its flat identity. A nonminimal even local class raises the relative index by eight; after all exterior insertions have already been included, the required exterior cut dimension is then negative. The two odd minimal ends have total relative dimension four. With no exceptional insertion their exterior cut dimension is minus four, again independently of how many fixed exterior point constraints are imposed; higher local charges only decrease it. These are the dimension conventions of [F3], applied to the entire external cutdown, not a charge-zero truncation. Hence
$$D_B^{(w,\Sigma)}(1)=D_{K3}^{F}(1)=0,\qquad D_B^{(w,\Sigma)}(x)=D_{K3}^{F}(x)=2.$$
The first equality vanishes because the K3 determinant $F$ has index four modulo eight. For $x\Sigma^2$, the even local sectors leave $xS^2$, which has codimension eight and the same incompatible index four modulo eight. A single odd exceptional factor or a squared factor integrates to zero by the actual exceptional pushforwards. The only surviving term is $2E_1E_2$; it leaves the K3 point insertion of value two. Consequently
$$\langle v,1\rangle=0,\qquad \langle pv,1\rangle=2,\qquad \langle pv,m^2\rangle=4.$$
The stated index exclusions include the other exterior insertions in each term; they do not assert that all higher-charge exceptional invariants vanish in other degrees. [F3, construct, algebra]

3.1 **The point relation has no guessed constant.** Together with [F3], the first equality in step 2.1 gives the full dual coordinate vector $(0,0,2,0)$ for $v$. Solving against the nondegenerate actual matrix $N$ gives $v=-4m$. This is containment and equality in the entire even homology, supplied by [F2]. For $p=A+Bm^2$, the second equality of step 2.1 is $2B=2$, so $B=1$. The third is $2A+8B=4$, because $\langle v,m^4\rangle=4\langle v,m^2\rangle+\delta\langle v,1\rangle=8$. Thus $A=-2$ and
$$p=m^2-2,\qquad p^2=(4+\delta)1.$$
Neither $v_0=1/2$ from a point-eigenspace projection nor a point-specialized source formula has been substituted for this full relative vector. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 **An actual torus cut determines the remaining coefficient.** The cotangent Dehn twist in the K3 sphere $\sigma$, already used in [F3], takes a smooth elliptic fibre to an embedded square-zero torus $R$ of class $\sigma+F$. Its intersection with $w=F$ is one. Remove its normal product tube, keeping all point incidences in the exterior. By [F4] the quotient boundary group is one-dimensional, its cap is nonzero, and the point operator is exactly $-2$. Ordinary relative gluing therefore gives, for every required finite degree,
$$D_{K3}^{(F,R)}(x^{n+1})=-2D_{K3}^{(F,R)}(x^n).$$
The two extension determinants have squares $F^2=0$ and $(F+R)^2=2$. Their indices are respectively four and zero modulo eight. Thus for $n=1$ and $n=3$ in the closed evaluations only determinant $F$ contributes. Applying the displayed recurrence twice gives
$$D_{K3}^{F}(x^3)=4D_{K3}^{F}(x)=8.$$
This is an actual closed count obtained by ordinary torus cutting; it assumes no classification of the higher-dimensional K3 moduli. The exceptional-end dimension argument of step 2.1, now with all three point constraints retained, gives $\langle p^3v,1\rangle=8$. But step 3.1 gives this same number as $(4+\delta)\langle pv,1\rangle=2(4+\delta)$. Hence $\delta=0$. [F3, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 **The odd group and the operator identities.** The perturbed chain group of [F2] has four generators of either parity. Its even homology has dimension four, so both differentials between its even and odd parts have rank zero. The differential is therefore zero and all four odd generators survive. The common normal grading shift is even: the product cap has flat dimension six and its successive codimension-two cuts give the nonzero even classes of [F2]. Equivalently, an odd shift would put all four even generators in the single residue of the four Morse-index-three generators, contradicting the nonzero degree-two-separated cap basis. Thus all four odd generators have one residue modulo four. Since $m$ changes that residue by two, it vanishes on odd homology. Step 3.1's equality of point-inserted cap classes transfers to an equality of operators on the full group by the actual pants product and its unit from step 1.1. Thus $p=m^2-2=-2$ on odd homology, and the asserted polynomial identities hold everywhere. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 **The normalized extreme vectors are actual nonzero cap projections.** In the proved even algebra set
$$P_+=\frac{m^2(m+2)}{16},\qquad P_-=\frac{m^2(2-m)}{16}.$$
Reduction modulo $m^2(m^2-4)$ verifies that these are the idempotents of the simple roots $2$ and $-2$. They are nonzero because $1,m,m^2,m^3$ are independent. Each is precisely the corresponding spectral projection of the actual unit. They have $p=2$, and no odd class has $p=2$. The cap trace gives
$$\tau(P_+)=-\frac1{32},\qquad\tau(P_-)=\frac1{32}.$$
Different idempotents pair to zero by the actual Frobenius identity from gluing pants. Each extreme is one-dimensional, so these nonzero numbers establish its perfect oriented pairing and inverse coefficients $-32,+32$. This identifies a genuine product-sector normalization, rather than merely multiplying an undetermined scalar double count. For arbitrary manifolds the identities may be transferred only through an independently supplied actual product-cap module/unit construction; for higher genus a new product calculation is still required. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra]
