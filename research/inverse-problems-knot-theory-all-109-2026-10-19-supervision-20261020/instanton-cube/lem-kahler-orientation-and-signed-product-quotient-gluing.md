## Statement

Assume full AC and the actual ordinary relative-count and geometric insertion construction. On a smooth projective surface $X$, use its Kahler homology orientation, an actual determinant line with class $w$, and the lift-dependent Donaldson orientation calibrated at trivial determinant by that homology orientation. Wherever a regular ASD moduli space is identified with a regular moduli space of stable bundles by the differential of the unitary-connection correspondence, the Donaldson orientation relative to the complex orientation is
$$\epsilon_X(w)=(-1)^{(w^2+K_X\cdot w)/2}.$$
This is an orientation comparison, not an existence or compactness theorem for that correspondence.

Suppose $X=M\cup_{\Sigma_g\times S^1}(\Sigma_g\times D^2)$, the surface has square zero and odd pairing with $w$, and the boundary quotient adds exactly the projective gauge class dual to $\Sigma_g$. The quotient determinant-line lift must be specified: relative to the complex-symbol-positive lift $\tau_{\rm cx}$, the Donaldson-oriented lift is $\tau_D=(-1)^g\tau_{\rm cx}$. With that lift the quotient pairing of the relative invariants is
$$\langle\phi(M,z),\phi(\Sigma_g\times D^2,z')\rangle_Q=D_X^w(zz')+D_X^{w+\Sigma_g}(zz').$$
There is no extra factor of two or one half in this formula. Changing the sign of the lift changes the plus to a minus and interchanges its two full-theory sectors.

For a K3 elliptic torus $F$, assume separately an actual regular ASD realization and incidence-compatible correspondence for the two following algebraic moduli: the determinant $\sigma$ zero-dimensional moduli is one complex-oriented point, and the determinant $\sigma+F$ real-four-dimensional moduli has geometric point-insertion degree two. Then the two closed quotient counts are $D^{(\sigma,F)}(1)=-1$ and $D^{(\sigma,F)}(x)=2$. Consequently the Donaldson-oriented quotient torus point operator is minus two, the full determinant-one point operator has one eigenline of eigenvalue plus two and one of eigenvalue minus two, and the surface operator is zero. The asserted ASD realization is an explicit remaining hypothesis, not supplied by this lemma or by a spherical moduli point.

## Proof

1.1 **The relative determinant orientation sign.** At a split reference $E=\mathcal O\oplus L$ fix the diagonal generator $i\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ and take the upper-right line $\operatorname{Hom}(L,\mathcal O)=L^{-1}$ as the complex off-diagonal orientation. This is the lift convention in the Donaldson determinant orientation. Compare it with the complex orientation of the Dolbeault deformation complex. The comparison is the identity on the $(0,1)$ off-diagonal term belonging to $L$, and is complex conjugation on the complementary term belonging to $L^{-1}$: a unitary one-form is determined by its $(0,1)$ part, while its $(1,0)$ part is minus the Hermitian adjoint. In the range, combine the real scalar and Kahler-form components into the complex $(0,0)$ component and retain the $(0,2)$ component. Thus the conjugated finite-dimensional virtual factor is the Dolbeault cohomology determinant of $L^{-1}$, relative to the trivial-line factor in the homology-orientation calibration. Complex conjugation on a complex vector space of dimension $n$ has real determinant $(-1)^n$. In a determinant line the exponent is consequently $\chi(L^{-1})-\chi(\mathcal O_X)$ modulo two: replacing an alternating sum by the sum changes its parity by an even integer. Finite Fredholm augmentation cancels matching image factors, so this calculation applies even if the reference is not an ASD solution. By [F2] the exponent is $(w^2+K_Xw)/2$. It is integral, since the Riemann--Roch index is integral. Determinant continuation over the affine connection space and the gauge-compatible determinant construction in [F1] transport the comparison to every regular moduli chart. At a regular bundle/ASD identification its differential is the same Dolbeault-to-unitary deformation map just used, so the comparison has this sign. No statement about existence of such an identification was used. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 **Which extensions the boundary quotient adds.** Let $t$ be the positive boundary meridian of the normal disk. With the actual local determinant splitting, the extra boundary map is represented projectively by $\operatorname{diag}(e^{it},1)$. In the Mayer--Vietoris description its determinant winding contributes the connecting class $\delta[dt]=PD[\Sigma_g]$ in $H^2(X;\mathbb Z)$. This can also be seen by trivializing the line on the disk and its exterior: a transition of winding one around that disk is exactly the Thom clutch of its zero section. Thus the even and odd clutches give the determinant extensions $w$ and $w+\Sigma_g$. Changing the clutch by two tensors the rank-two bundle by the line of class $\Sigma_g$; its adjoint is unchanged. Since the normal square is zero, the corresponding lift-orientation change is $(-1)^{\Sigma_g^2}=1$, also obtained by substituting $w+2\Sigma_g$ in step 1.1. Hence there are exactly these two parity sectors for the specified extra order-two quotient. Other boundary projective gauge classes have not been added, and their extension data remain the fixed data of the relative problem. [F1, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 **Why the signed lift is necessary.** Compute the boundary comparison on the local doubled product $S=\Sigma_g\times\mathbb{CP}^1$, with $B=\mathbb{CP}^1$ and $\Sigma_g$ its fiber class. Here $B^2=\Sigma_g^2=0$, $B\Sigma_g=1$ and $K_S=(2g-2)B-2\Sigma_g$. Step 1.1 gives $\epsilon_S(B)=-1$ and $\epsilon_S(B+\Sigma_g)=(-1)^{g-1}$, so their ratio is $(-1)^g$. The difference between the two clutch comparisons is supported on the fixed normal-disk product. Finite determinant excision cancels the same exterior operator on both sides; therefore this local doubled-product calculation gives the boundary orientation character for any supplied exterior, not only a complex exterior. If the original surface is a complex curve, the same result follows directly from adjunction $K_X\Sigma_g=2g-2$ and odd $w\Sigma_g$. Complex determinant gluing is complex linear and has positive comparison on the odd clutch before this Donaldson lift correction. The two closed sectors, however, have precisely the relative correction displayed above. Thus the quotient orientation line that glues to the Donaldson orientations must identify the odd boundary lift with $(-1)^g\tau_{\rm cx}$. This is an actual sign in the orientation-line action, not a change of the underlying free involution on configurations. Its square is still the positive determinant-one action, so it defines a valid quotient orientation system. It commutes with insertion operators because their normal orientations are complex and their codimensions are even. In particular for a torus it is the negative of the complex-symbol-positive lift; omitting this choice silently interchanges the two point-eigenvalue sectors. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 **Normalization of transfer and gluing.** For one free critical orbit choose a basis $a$ and its oriented partner $\tau_Da$, and let $\bar a$ denote the quotient generator. Projection sends each of these oriented basis elements to $\bar a$, and transfer sends $\bar a$ to $a+\tau_Da$. On the dual orbit use the dual bases. The ordinary pairing of two transferred generators is two, so the quotient pairing is one half of that ordinary pairing. This does not insert one half into the closed gluing formula. For ordinary relative vectors $u,v$, their quotient vectors are their projections, whose coefficients are the sums of the two oriented coefficients on each orbit. Equivalently, transferring a projected relative vector applies $1+\tau_D$. Thus the one-half factor cancels the duplication from the two simultaneous lifts, leaving the two DIFFERENT relative matching sectors once each. More explicitly, their sum is $\langle u,v\rangle+\langle u,\tau_Dv\rangle$, with any remaining ordinary matching labels summed in the usual way. The first gluing is the even clutch of step 2.1 and the second is its odd clutch. Step 3.1 gives exactly their Donaldson signs. Exhaustive neck collars and the incidence gluing from [F1] therefore identify these two terms with $D_X^w(zz')$ and $D_X^{w+\Sigma_g}(zz')$. The same argument with the other quotient orientation character subtracts the odd term. This proves the formula with its coefficient and its orientation system specified. [F1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 **The K3 signs and the two different dimensions.** Now take $K_X=0$, $\sigma^2=-2$, $F^2=0$ and $\sigma F=1$. Step 1.1 gives $\epsilon_X(\sigma)=-1$ and $\epsilon_X(\sigma+F)=+1$. Under the separately supplied ASD/bundle identifications, the spherical determinant-$\sigma$ moduli contributes one point with Donaldson count minus one. The determinant-$\sigma+F$ moduli has real dimension four and geometric point degree two, so its point cutdown has count plus two. The other contributions to these two particular insertions vanish by dimension: the four-dimensional sector does not contribute to the zero-dimensional no-insertion invariant, and a degree-four insertion has negative expected dimension on the zero-dimensional sector. The resulting quotient pairing from step 4.1 has $D^{(\sigma,F)}(1)=-1$ and $D^{(\sigma,F)}(x)=2$. This uses the four-dimensional moduli for the second count; it does not reinterpret a single spherical point as a point-operator count. [F1, given, step 1.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 **What the ratio proves in the full torus theory.** For the admissible torus write its lifted surface holonomies as $X,Y$ with $XY=-YX$. Conjugation normalizes them to $i,j$; their common determinant-one centralizer is $\{\pm1\}$, so the third holonomy is exactly $+1$ or $-1$. These are precisely its two central-lift critical points. Their adjoint local system splits into three real characters, each nontrivial on at least one of the first two circle generators. The torus cellular Koszul complex of each character contracts using the inverse of that generator minus one. Thus all its adjoint cohomology, including the critical first space, vanishes. Both critical points are nondegenerate and are exchanged by the free degree-four action. Their degree difference is four, so both have the same parity and its differential is zero; the quotient complex is one-dimensional. For the actual product cap the disk meridian contracts, so its flat extension has third holonomy $+1$ and is unique. The same contracting coefficient complex on the surface factor gives no cap kernel or obstruction. Its zero-index topological energy is zero; the other boundary lift has relative index four modulo eight and cannot occur in that zero-index count. Thus the no-insertion cap selects the first lift with coefficient one in its oriented module, and projects to a nonzero quotient generator. Since its pairing with the K3 exterior is minus one by step 5.1, neither relative vector vanishes. The quotient point operator is a scalar $c$. Moving the geometric point insertion from the exterior to the cap by the insertion homotopy in [F1] gives $c(-1)=2$, so $c=-2$. In the full complex choose the homogeneous basis $a,\tau_Da$. The point operator has degree four and commutes with $\tau_D$, so its matrix is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&c\\c&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Its eigenlines are $a+\tau_Da$ with value minus two and $a-\tau_Da$ with value plus two. The surface operator has degree two and the complex has no degree-two target, so it is zero. Thus the desired full-theory simultaneous eigenspace $(\mu(F),\mu(x))=(0,+2)$ is one-dimensional. The sole additional enumerative input in this deduction is exactly the separately stated ASD realization of the two K3 moduli and their insertion-compatible degrees. [F1, given, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
