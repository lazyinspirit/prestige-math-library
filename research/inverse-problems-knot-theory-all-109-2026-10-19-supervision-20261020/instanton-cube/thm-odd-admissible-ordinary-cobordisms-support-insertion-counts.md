## Statement

Assume full AC. Let $Y$ be a closed connected oriented smooth three-manifold, let $E\to Y$ be an actual rank-two Hermitian bundle with a fixed determinant connection, and suppose an oriented closed surface $R\subset Y$ has odd pairing with $c_1(E)$. Use determinant-one gauges and the trace-free ASD equation. There are small holonomy perturbations defining a finite rational Floer complex with oriented critical modules, all-class differential, relative grading modulo eight and continuation. For smooth oriented cobordisms carrying such bundles on their nonempty product ends, every connected component meeting an end, homology orientations and fixed end orientation modules give finite rational relative counts with any fixed finite collection of the point and surface incidence insertions below. Counts are defined in cutdown dimensions zero and one, include all relative classes, obey the oriented boundary and gluing identities, and give the corresponding relative invariant, pairing under neck stretching, and commuting even-degree insertion operators. A product cylinder gives the identity continuation map.

For $Y=S^1\times\Sigma_g$, $g\ge1$, and $c_1(E)=PD[S^1\times\{p\}]$, the extra projective gauge class dual to $\Sigma_g$ can be implemented by a free degree-four chain involution $\tau$ commuting with point and surface insertions. Over the rationals its quotient complex identifies with the invariant subcomplex. The full determinant-one theory retains both sectors; this statement does not identify their point or surface eigenvalues. Here the insertion normalization is exactly $\mu(z)=-p_1(\mathbb E)/4z$; a point has degree minus four and a surface has degree minus two.

The pairing assertion is the neck-stretching pairing of these relative counts with their actual compatible end backgrounds and orientations. It asserts no numerical nonvanishing, no product insertion basis, no ring presentation, and no K3 or elliptic-surface Donaldson evaluation.

## Proof

1.1 **No reducible flat limit and a compact finite critical set.** A positive-dimensional determinant-one stabilizer gives a parallel oriented real axis in $\operatorname{ad}E$. Its orthogonal oriented two-plane is a complex line $L$ whose first Chern class reduces to $w_2(\operatorname{ad}E)=c_1(E)\bmod2$. If the trace-free connection is flat, its induced connection on $L$ is flat, so $\langle c_1(L),[R]\rangle=0$, contradicting oddness. The same argument excludes reducible solutions of the sufficiently small perturbed critical equation: its curvature is bounded in supremum norm by a constant times the perturbation size, whereas Chern--Weil on $R$ gives $|\int_R F_L|\ge2\pi$. Take that bound smaller than $2\pi$. Thickened holonomy loops $S^1\times D^2\to Y$, invariant trace functions and smooth transverse two-form densities give gauge-invariant cylinder functions with this bounded gradient. Choose a countable dense list and summable coefficients controlling every derivative on bounded Sobolev sets. Their gradient and adjoint derivatives have the three-dimensional Fubini bounds. For a sequence of critical connections the uniform curvature bound gives small curvature on uniformly small balls. Ordinary Coulomb gauges from [F1], elliptic bootstrap of the critical equation and compact Sobolev inclusions give a smoothly converging subsequence modulo gauges and compatible limiting transition maps. There is no three-dimensional point atom: the uniform curvature bound makes the integral on shrinking balls tend to zero. Gauge normalization on the finite ball cover retains the actual determinant lift. Thus the perturbed critical quotient is compact and irreducible. Holonomy coordinates separate infinitesimal connection variations modulo gauge: a variation with zero first variation of all parallel transports is an infinitesimal gauge, obtained by transporting its path integral from a basepoint; path independence is exactly zero loop variation. Finitely many cylinder-function differentials therefore span each finite critical cokernel. Sard--Smale, with their dense jet list, makes the critical Hessians invertible on Coulomb slices. A compact zero-dimensional critical quotient is finite. [F1, F4, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 **The ordinary period and all-class energy bound.** A determinant-one gauge has fiber $SU(2)$, with conjugation transitions. On a finite three-dimensional cell model its first two homotopy obstructions vanish. A relative two-skeleton trivialization leaves its three-cell degree, with untwisted coefficients because inner conjugation has degree one. Thus component periods are generated by an ordinary degree-one gauge in a ball. Its energy period is $8\pi^2$ by quaternionic cubic transgression, and its relative index is $5-(-3)=8$ by [F2]'s ordinary comparison sphere. Stabilizer signs act trivially and have zero period. Concatenation and finite determinant excision make both quantities additive. For any fixed critical pair, therefore, $\mathcal E_z-\pi^2I_z$ is independent of its relative class. The finite list of critical pairs bounds this constant. The flow identity $\mathcal E_z=2\int\|\partial_tB\|^2$ gives a common energy bound at every upper index bound, not merely at index at most two. For a four-dimensional core the Chern--Weil identity and uniformly bounded bulk forcing give $E_{\rm core}+E_{\rm ends}\le T_{\rm rel}+C$. With the determinant fixed, relative bundle changes are ordinary four-cell degrees; their energy/index pair is again $(8\pi^2,8)$. A finite relative cell construction leaves only torsion labels, which change neither real characteristic number nor index. Thus $T_{\rm rel}-\pi^2I$ depends only on the fixed end pair and core background. This gives the required uniform all-class bound at every fixed upper core-plus-parameter index. On a stretching family use the flow identity on the neck, so its length does not enter the constant. No claim of a curvature-energy integral on an infinite cylinder at a nonflat perturbed critical point is needed. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 **Trajectory regularity at all indices.** Complete the Coulomb domains with exponential weights smaller than the finitely many positive critical spectral gaps. The end spectral inverses and compact-core parametrices make their actual operators Fredholm. Vary cylinder-function jets away from the critical jets. A nonconstant finite-energy trajectory has no repeated point in the configuration quotient: by autonomous uniqueness a repeated point would make the orbit periodic, giving infinite flow energy. A compact small embedded arc of its quotient path may therefore be isolated from the rest of that path. If an adjoint cokernel vector annihilates every gradient perturbation localized on such arcs, integrating the differential of arbitrary cylinder functions along that arc forces its transverse component to vanish; infinitesimal cylinder functions are dense in the cotangent slice by the holonomy separation in step 1.1. It is consequently tangent to the velocity there. But its pairing with the velocity is constant: the velocity solves $v'+H(t)v=0$, the adjoint vector solves $-u'+H(t)u=0$, and the Hessian is self-adjoint. Their decay at both ends makes this constant zero. Tangency then makes the cokernel vector zero on the arc. Time uniqueness for the first-order end and local slice equations makes it zero everywhere. The universal derivative is onto. The countable class, Sobolev-order and parameter-stratum list permits simultaneous Sard--Smale choices, relative to the fixed critical jets. This proves all-index trajectory regularity in the ordinary gradient theory, not only bulk transversality. [F1, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 **Bulk regularity and actual end extraction.** A cobordism connection with a positive-dimensional stabilizer would restrict to a reducible critical point on an end, impossible by step 1.1. Thickened four-dimensional loops and arbitrary supported self-dual output forms thus detect every nonzero adjoint bulk cokernel vector: irreducibility supplies two noncommuting holonomies, whose imaginary parts and product span the adjoint fiber. The nonlocal derivative and its adjoint have the four-dimensional Fubini bounds. If an adjoint vector vanished on the output core, zero-Cauchy-data uniqueness on the two ends would make it zero. One direct estimate writes its end equation as $u_t+B_0u=K(t)u$, with self-adjoint $B_0$ and bounded $K$, and differentiates $q=\langle B_0u,u\rangle/\|u\|^2$ to obtain $q'\le\|K\|^2$; integrating the corresponding upper bound on $(\log\|u\|^2)'$ rules out a first zero. Thus relative parameter bumps and Sard--Smale regularize every core class. For any energy-bounded sequence cover the core by small Coulomb balls off its finitely many atoms, extract actual limiting transition maps and remove points as in [F1]--[F2]. On an end a translated window of arbitrarily small flow energy converges to a critical slice; the finite critical set and local spectral inverse force eventual residence in one slice, then exponential convergence. A first exit otherwise extracts another positive-energy trajectory. The discrete period and finite endpoint list give a positive minimum for the energy of a nonconstant level in the bounded window, so only finitely many levels occur. This proves all-class Uhlenbeck subsequential extraction and exponential ends on the fixed upper-index budget. It allows higher-index bubbles at this stage; they are excluded only after the actual incidence calculation. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 **Ordinary orientations for a possibly nonliftable adjoint bundle.** The given rank-two bundle is an actual $U(2)$ bundle with determinant $w$; an $SU(2)$ lift of its adjoint is not assumed. Stabilize it to the determinant-trivial rank-three bundle $E'=E\oplus w^{-1}$. Its real adjoint splits as $\operatorname{ad}E'=\operatorname{ad}E\oplus\mathbb R\oplus(E\otimes w)_{\mathbb R}$. The last summand has its complex structure and its deformation operator is complex linear; its determinant has the complex orientation. The middle summand is the untwisted real operator, oriented by the homology orientation and the supplied end data. To orient the remaining factor, use [F3]'s finite ordinary-cell clutch construction on $E'$. Its gauge fiber is $SU(3)$. After lower cells are filled, closing gauges are products of ordinary degree clutches transported around curves and supported four-cell gauges. The latter contract since $\pi_4(SU(3))=0$; the former transport their ordinary framed charge mode with the ambient oriented four-plane and the connected unitary framing action, whose real determinant is positive. This is the ordinary part of the local determinant construction [F3], now with an actual global determinant-trivial stabilization. It gives positive determinant transport for these gauge generators. Dividing that oriented determinant by the complex factor and the homology-oriented real factor orients $\operatorname{ad}E$ and proves positive determinant-one gauge monodromy. Use finite augmentations at the ends. A common augmentation identifies both orders of a gluing with the same ordered wedge product, so the orientations glue associatively. This constructs the ordinary orientation extension rather than applying the earring orientation Statement to a different gauge group. [F1, F3, given, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 **Exhaustive ordinary collars and incidence counts.** At each bubble-free broken configuration separate its finite kernels and patch the normal core and end inverses over the long collars. Differential cutoff errors are $O(T^{-1})$ and the decaying tails are exponentially small. For nonlocal holonomy terms retain finitely many loop supports first, place the cutoffs outside them, and make the remaining operator-norm tail small. The resulting normal parametrix has a Neumann-series inverse uniformly for large $T$. Pregluing error is exponentially small, and Sobolev multiplication makes its nonlinear remainder quadratically Lipschitz on a uniform slice ball. Contraction gives a unique small correction. Conversely the smooth core convergence and the stable/unstable end splitting put every sequence with this broken limit into that same slice ball after choosing its neck and kernel coordinates, so uniqueness proves exhaustion. Multiple necks use the product inverse and the same uniqueness, giving compatible corner collars. Steps 1.1--5.1 now supply every analytic and orientation hypothesis of [F4] for these ordinary moduli at the required ambient indices. Impose its actual universal-adjoint point and surface incidences, with their quarter-weights. Its dimension calculation treats bubbles hitting insertion points or surfaces separately, giving codimension at least four for an ordinary ideal stratum; thus cutdown dimensions zero and one are bubble-free. Its finite-dimensional transverse incidence equations cut these exhaustive collars with the determinant boundary signs just proved. This gives finite rational counts over all classes and the ordinary boundary relations. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct]

7.1 **Complex, continuation, relative invariant and pairing.** Sum the finite critical orientation modules over the rationals and count all index-one cylinders. The compact cutdown-free one-manifolds at the next index give $d^2=0$. Metric and perturbation paths use a fixed end collar; their core energy estimate is step 2.1, not a time-independent gradient identity on the varying part. Bulk regularity and the preceding compactness/collars give continuation and its composition homotopies. A constant cylinder has only its constant solutions in zero dimension, since any nonconstant solution has a translation direction; its ordered determinant comparison is the identity. Inserting the finite cycles of [F4] into the cylinder gives chain operators of degrees minus four and minus two. Moving and interchanging their marked supports gives their even commutation relations on homology. A one-ended core gives its relative invariant by counting cutdowns to a critical orientation module; the end boundary formula makes it a cycle. Stretching a common admissible end and applying step 6.1 identifies the count with the sum of products of the relative counts over matching critical modules, including all orientation and quarter-weight factors. This is the asserted neck-stretching pairing. The rational numbers so obtained may all vanish; no existence of a nonzero invariant follows from this construction. 8.1 **The actual product deck transformation.** On $Y=S^1_t\times\Sigma_g$ choose the topological Hermitian splitting $E=\mathbb C\oplus w$ pulled back from $\Sigma_g$, where $\deg w=1$. Such a splitting is the supplied concrete model for the determinant class, not a splitting of a flat connection. The unitary automorphism $g(t)=\operatorname{diag}(e^{it},1)$ acts projectively on trace-free connections. Restore the fixed determinant connection by subtracting its scalar trace change. Its square is the determinant-one gauge $\operatorname{diag}(e^{it},e^{-it})$ on the trace-free bundle. Thus it induces an actual involution $\tau$ of the determinant-one quotient. At a projectively flat point, restriction to $\Sigma_g$ is irreducible by oddness, so its determinant-one centralizer is $\{\pm1\}$. Holonomy around $S^1$ is therefore one of these two central lifts. The action of $g$ changes that lift's sign and leaves the surface representation fixed. It swaps the two disjoint flat critical sectors and has no fixed critical point. Compactness of the flat quotient gives disjoint invariant neighborhoods of the two sectors. Choose the critical perturbations in step 1.1 sufficiently small to stay in them, and choose all perturbations equivariantly by pairing their supports under $\tau$; the infinitesimal detector on either neighborhood is unchanged. The critical set remains free. The same action on trajectories is free because its end critical points are exchanged. [F1, F4, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 7.1, construct, algebra]

9.1 **Its relative degree is four.** Form the projective mapping torus of $g$ with clutch parameter $s$. In the indicated rank-two model its two line summands have Chern classes $u=[ds\wedge dt]$ and $w$, with $\int_{T^2}u=\int_{\Sigma_g}w=1$. Thus $c_1=u+w$, $c_2=uw$, and $p_1(\operatorname{ad}E)=(u+w)^2-4uw=-2uw$. The adjoint ASD relative-index term is $-2p_1$, so its value on this mapping torus is four. To see this coefficient directly in the supplied index convention, the two formal Chern roots of the actual rank-two bundle give adjoint weights $0,x_1-x_2,x_2-x_1$ and degree-four Chern character $(x_1-x_2)^2=p_1$. The local Clifford multiplicity of the deformation symbol is two, as in [F2]'s ordinary calculation, with the ASD sign yielding $-2p_1$. The untwisted Euler/signature term vanishes on the product mapping torus. Hence this is an actual projective-gauge spectral shift of four modulo eight, while determinant-one gauges have the period eight of step 2.1. Incidence operators are natural for this actual adjoint-bundle automorphism and have the degrees given in step 7.1. [F2, F4, step 2.1, step 8.1, construct, algebra]

10.1 **Quotient orientations and transfer.** Choose the critical orientation modules in paired neighborhoods so the determinant map induced by $g$ identifies each first module with its partner. Applying it twice is the determinant-one gauge of step 8.1, whose orientation action is positive by step 5.1; this prescription is consistent. For any trajectory, naturality of finite determinant augmentation and its ordered gluing identifies its transformed determinant with the transformed two end modules. For this product quotient one can check the extra orientation directly, rather than extrapolate a determinant-one gauge claim to $g$. Give $\mathbb R_s\times S^1_t\times\Sigma_g$ its product complex structure. The real ASD deformation symbol, written in $(0,1)$ forms, is the complex Dolbeault symbol from $(0,1)$ forms to $(0,0)\oplus(0,2)$ forms with trace-free endomorphism coefficients; identifying the real scalar and Kahler-form components gives the complex $(0,0)$ factor. The unitary connection and the product metric change this identification only by zeroth-order terms. Finite augmentation makes their straight interpolation a Fredholm determinant comparison. Thus the relative orientation can be taken from that complex symbol. The projective unitary action of $g$ is complex linear in these coordinates; restoring the fixed trace is an affine zeroth-order connection change and preserves this comparison. Its determinant transport is positive. The same complex comparison on the closed product mapping torus gives no extra orientation character. The incidence equations retain their separate complex orientations. It gives an orientation-compatible free chain action with $\tau^2=1$ commuting with differential and insertions. A quotient generator maps to its two-element orbit sum. Each quotient trajectory has precisely its two lifts, with these same signs, so this identifies the quotient differential with the invariant differential. The rational projector $(1+\tau)/2$ splits the full chain complex; it follows directly that the quotient homology equals the invariant homology. The degree-four action exchanges the two grading sectors, and the full theory still contains both. No value $+2$ or $-2$ for the point operator, nor an extremal surface value, has been inferred from this transfer. This proves the claims at their stated analytical and coefficient scope.
