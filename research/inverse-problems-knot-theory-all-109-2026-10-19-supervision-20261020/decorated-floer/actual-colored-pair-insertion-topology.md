# Actual local pair insertion, with arbitrary link matching

This constructs the geometry for a chosen colored quasi-stabilization. The analytic differential block is a separate obligation. No existing S3-knot or V=1 theorem is promoted to arbitrary links.

Let an actual balanced multipointed link diagram and its embedded arcs and compression disks be supplied. Take an ordered adjacent pair $w',z'$ belonging to one alpha arc. Near $w'$ choose a ball disjoint from every old attaching curve and compression disk. Normalize the arc to be neat in this ball, retaining its boundary-parallel witness; this is an ordinary local smooth arc isotopy. In coordinates $(s,y,t)$ put the surface at $t=0$, the alpha side at $t<0$, and the old oriented knot segment at $(s,0,-s)$. Its orientation goes from the beta side through $w'$ into the alpha side.

Choose $0<a<b$ inside this coordinate interval. Replace this segment by the embedded graph $(s,0,-s h(s))$, with $h(s)=(s-a)(s-b)$ near $[a,b]$ and $h=1$ near the interval ends. Choose the interpolation positive outside a small neighborhood of $[a,b]$, so the only zeros of $h$ are $a,b$. The graph has exactly three surface crossings in the ball: the old $w'$ at zero, a new $z$ at $a$, and a new $w$ at $b$. Its successive sides are beta, alpha, beta, alpha. The linear interpolation between the old and new graph is an isotopy of embedded arcs because its $s$ coordinate is always injective, even at the instant the two surface intersections are born. A supported velocity extension in the ball supplies the ambient isotopy. Thus the represented oriented link is unchanged; this argument makes no assumption that the global matching permutation has one cycle.

The short alpha arc from $w'$ to $z$ and the short beta arc from $z$ to $w$ have explicit graph-to-surface boundary-parallel disks in the coordinate plane $y=0$, with collars rounded. The remaining alpha arc from $w$ to $z'$ inherits its old witness with the initial graph strip replaced. Its replacement support is disjoint from all old compression faces. The new beta arc is in a small ball adjacent to the surface; the old beta arc ending at $w'$ is unchanged off its endpoint collar. Hence all witnesses remain embedded and pairwise disjoint after taking their product neighborhoods sufficiently thin.

On the small surface disk choose a simple alpha circle enclosing $w',z$ and excluding $w,z'$, and a simple beta circle enclosing $z,w$ and excluding $w',z'$. Their two boundaries cross twice. They can be chosen as boundaries of overlapping round disks after a smooth disk-coordinate change. Both are disjoint from every old attaching curve. Each bounds a boundary-parallel compressing disk in its corresponding old cut ball, separating off the child ball containing the short arc of that type. Choose these disks outside the small graph-to-surface witness disks. Cutting the old alpha ball by the new alpha disk gives precisely two balls: one contains the short $w'$-to-$z$ arc, the other contains the remaining $w$-to-$z'$ arc. Cutting the relevant beta ball by the new beta disk separates off precisely the new short $z$-to-$w$ arc; its other child contains the old beta arc. The unaffected cut balls and their arcs remain the old ones. Thus the new genus is the old genus, the number of w/z pairs increases by one, and each system has exactly one new compression disk and exactly one new cut ball. Completeness and the balanced rank $d=g+n-1$ are preserved with $d$ replaced by $d+1$.

Equivalently choose the new alpha circle first, very small about $w'$ in its actual old region, and choose an unmarked point $p$ on it. At $p$ insert a disk with a proper alpha arc and a beta circle crossing that arc twice; the circle encloses the new $w,z$. This is the special-connected-sum picture. The planar disk coordinates above identify the two descriptions with their actual compression disks and bridge witnesses. The new alpha circle can therefore be chosen disjoint from ALL old beta curves for this particular model. A more general separating alpha circle may cross old beta curves, but comparison with those choices is an additional naturality problem, not required to construct this chosen model.

The new beta circle meets only the new alpha circle. Any generator of the new diagram must pair that beta circle with the new alpha circle. The remaining factors then pair old alpha and beta circles exactly as before. Consequently the module generators are literally $x\otimes\theta_w$ and $x\otimes\xi_w$, with no new mixed generator assignments. All old marks, including the remote adjacent $z'$, stay at their old points. Their independent variables cannot be identified during this construction. In particular the remote $V_{z'}$ differential term is not contained in the two small new-point bigons; its coefficient requires the new planar cut-region degeneration count and gluing operation.

The construction supplies no regularity, finite disk count, arbitrary-old-link endpoint existence, or identification of the differential with a tensor or a two-by-two block. Those are the actual colored-neck analytic obligations being developed separately.
