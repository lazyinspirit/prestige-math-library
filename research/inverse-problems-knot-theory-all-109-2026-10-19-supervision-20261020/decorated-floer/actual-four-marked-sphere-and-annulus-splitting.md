## Actual colored saddle-top model

Take a sphere with two transverse round circles meeting at $a,b$. Put one of $w,w',z,z'$ in each of its four bigon regions, alternating in that order. The two corresponding compression disks cut each three-ball into two balls, and the boundary-parallel marked arcs join to a four-marked unknot. It can be realized directly by taking their boundary shadows around a small planar quadrilateral and pushing alternate sides into the two three-balls. The resulting simple circle bounds the quadrilateral disk with its corners rounded. The symmetric-product rank is $0+2-1=1$, so the actual target is this sphere, and the two actual generators are $a,b$.

Choose a sphere coordinate sending its two crossings to $0,\infty$. The circles become lines through zero. Each open bigon is an angular sector, and its strip parametrization is $u(s,t)=\exp(c(s+it)+i\theta_0)$, with $c$ its positive angular width and with the boundary labels selecting its direction. This is the unique conformal parametrization modulo real translation: composing any two such parametrizations gives an automorphism of the strip fixing its ordered ends, hence a translation. For its real linearized operator, divide a section by $u$ and reflect across the two real boundary conditions. The end decay conditions leave precisely the real scaling mode, which is the strip translation; all nonconstant reflected Fourier modes violate an end condition. Its Fredholm index is one, so its cokernel is zero. Thus each bigon has actual regular mod-two count one.

For completeness no additional index-one domain is hidden in this count. Write the multiplicities in the four bigons as $d_1,\ldots,d_4$. The actual scalar boundary index is $\mu=\sum d_i$. To verify this without a domain/class bijection, start with one of the displayed strip classes. Changing the two boundary paths changes their integer circle windings; attach the corresponding signed hemispheres of the two circles. Each hemisphere has two regions and boundary index two. The remaining change is an actual sphere class: its degree is the common residual region coefficient, and its Chern contribution to the disk index is four times that degree. These changes prove the formula for every actual class. A zero-domain remaining sphere has degree zero and zero index; injectivity of the domain map was not assumed. Positivity makes all $d_i\ge0$ for an actual holomorphic strip. At index one exactly one is one, hence the four bigons are exhaustive. The sphere degree is fixed by these multiplicities, so it supplies no further same-domain classes. The same reflection argument identifies the representatives in each basic bigon class.

Label the crossings $\Theta_w,\Theta_z$ so that the two $z$-marked bigons run from $\Theta_w$ to $\Theta_z$. On the genuinely UNCOLORED free module over $P=\mathbb F_2[U_w,U_{w'},V_z,V_{z'}]$ the actual count matrix is therefore
$$D\Theta_w=(V_z+V_{z'})\Theta_z,\qquad D\Theta_z=(U_w+U_{w'})\Theta_w.$$
Its square is $(U_w+U_{w'})(V_z+V_{z'})I$. This is a curved model before coloring. Setting the two $w$ variables equal to $U$ and the two $z$ variables equal to $V$ makes BOTH generators actual cycles and makes the whole model differential zero. The relative degrees satisfy $\operatorname{gr}_w(\Theta_w)-\operatorname{gr}_w(\Theta_z)=1$ and $\operatorname{gr}_z(\Theta_w)-\operatorname{gr}_z(\Theta_z)=-1$. Thus the two types of decorated band use different tops: the $z$-type uses $\Theta_w$ and the $w$-type uses $\Theta_z$. They cannot silently be replaced by a single common top in both gradings.

The two-variable coloring also gives actual local maps from the two-marked genus-zero unknot module $R=\mathbb F_2[U,V]$ to this four-marked module $R\Theta_w\oplus R\Theta_z$:
$$S^+(1)=\Theta_w,\quad S^-(\Theta_w)=0,\quad S^-(\Theta_z)=1,$$
$$T^+(1)=\Theta_z,\quad T^-(\Theta_z)=0,\quad T^-(\Theta_w)=1.$$
All are chain maps on these explicit models. Matrix multiplication gives the strict identities
$$S^-S^+=T^-T^+=0,\quad T^-S^+=S^-T^+=I,\quad S^+T^-+T^+S^-=I.$$
Differentiate the uncolored matrix BEFORE specializing the colors. The basepoint operators $\Phi_w=\partial D/\partial U_w$ and $\Psi_z=\partial D/\partial V_z$ have matrices $\Phi_w\Theta_z=\Theta_w$, $\Phi_w\Theta_w=0$, $\Psi_z\Theta_w=\Theta_z$, $\Psi_z\Theta_z=0$. Therefore $\Phi_w^2=\Psi_z^2=0$ and $\Phi_w\Psi_z+\Psi_z\Phi_w=I$ on this model. Differentiating the already colored zero differential would incorrectly give zero operators.

These are actual local scalar counts and local identities. They do not prove insertion of this factor beside an arbitrary old link complex. That further target-neck operation must identify its full colored differential with the block matrix having old differential on both diagonal entries and $U_w+U_{w'}$, $V_z+V_{z'}$ off the diagonal. A full-minus operation after forgetting the $z$ variable is insufficient to identify this two-color block. Likewise one must transport the appropriate top homology class between subordinate band triples, not infer that transport from the rank-two model alone.

## Actual local forget-z edge cancellation

The same scalar count also computes the relative-edge operator, rather than identifying it with a basepoint operator by assertion. Forget the z variables by setting both to one, retaining independent $U_w,U_{w'}$. In the ordered basis $(\Theta_w,\Theta_z)$ the actual differential becomes
$$D=\begin{pmatrix}0&U_w+U_{w'}\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.$$
Choose a simple arc $\lambda$ from $w$ to $w'$ on the sphere, transverse to the alpha circle once and avoiding its two crossings. Weight each counted disk by the mod-two intersection of that arc with the disk's alpha boundary. The alpha circle has two arcs between the crossings, and each is a boundary edge of exactly one w bigon and one z bigon. Choose the crossing of $\lambda$ on the edge incident to the $w$ bigon. Precisely those two bigons acquire weight one. Consequently this actual edge action is
$$A_\lambda=\begin{pmatrix}0&U_w\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.$$
Direct multiplication gives
$$DA_\lambda+A_\lambda D=(U_w+U_{w'})I,\qquad A_\lambda^2=U_wI.$$
After identifying the w variables, the differential is zero and
$$A_\lambda=\Psi_z+U\Phi_w,\qquad S^-A_\lambda S^+=I.$$
The last identity has coefficient exactly one and no positive power of $U$: $S^+$ picks the top generator, the selected z bigon sends it to the bottom generator with weight one, and $S^-$ picks that bottom generator. This supplies the actual genus-zero scalar cancellation underlying a graph arc's cup/cap pair. It does not prove insertion of this local word in an arbitrary old complex, or that a general surface movie reduces to the graph word. In particular $\Psi_z$ alone cannot replace $A_\lambda$ in the full graph algebra: the $U\Phi_w$ term is required for $A_\lambda^2=U I$.

## Exact finite stable-slice algebra

Let $\Lambda=\mathbb F_2[U,U^{-1}]$ with $\deg U=-2$ and generator of degree and bifiltration $(0;0,0)$. Suppose the actual annulus and reverse-annulus constructions have supplied degree-zero $\Lambda$-linear bifiltered chain maps
$$i:\Lambda\longrightarrow C(K),\qquad p:C(K)\longrightarrow\Lambda,$$
which induce isomorphisms on total homology. No assumption about the geometric composite of the two annuli is needed.

A degree-zero $\Lambda$-endomorphism of $\Lambda$ is multiplication by a scalar in $\mathbb F_2$, since every nonconstant Laurent monomial has nonzero Maslov degree. The differential of $\Lambda$ is zero, so its map on homology is that same endomorphism. Since $p_*i_*$ is an isomorphism, $pi=I$ STRICTLY. In particular $i$ is an injection, $p$ is a surjection, and $q=I-ip$ is an actual bifiltered degree-zero chain projection onto $B=\ker p$.

The maps $\Lambda\oplus B\to C$, $(a,b)\mapsto i(a)+b$, and $C\to\Lambda\oplus B$, $c\mapsto(p(c),q(c))$, are strict inverse chain maps. Give $B$ the inherited bifiltration. Both inverse maps preserve it: $p,q,i$ are bifiltered, and the filtration of a sum is bounded by the maximum of its terms. In particular the splitting is a splitting of the actual filtrations, not just an unfiltered module decomposition. The kernel is finite rank free: $p$ is a split surjection between finite free modules over the PID $\Lambda$, so its kernel is a finite free direct summand. It has a homogeneous free basis as well: multiply each homogeneous input basis generator by a Laurent unit to put its degree in $\{0,1\}$. On the degree-zero generators $p$ is a row of binary scalars, and it vanishes on the degree-one generators. A finite binary basis change isolates this nonzero row; its remaining even vectors together with all odd vectors form a homogeneous free basis of the kernel. This basis change need not be filtered, and none is asserted to be. Its filtration is generated by the finite family $q(x_j)$ with the inherited bounds of a finite filtered basis $x_j$ of $C$; no filtration-preserving contraction is asserted. The total homology splitting is $H(C)=H(\Lambda)\oplus H(B)$, and $p_*$ is an isomorphism, so $H(B)=0$. This proves precisely the original slice stable-equivalence conclusion, with the left acyclic summand zero, PROVIDED the two displayed actual geometric maps have been constructed.

The remaining geometric premise is explicit: both annulus maps must have absolute Maslov degree zero, not merely relative degree zero, and preserve both normalized filtrations. Their action on the localized total tower must be nonzero. This nonvanishing is not implied by the rank of either tower, a tau inequality, the existence of triangle chain maps, or the assertion that reversing an arbitrary concordance gives an isotopy inverse. For the expected genus-$g$ extension the analogous map from the unknot needs Maslov degree zero, algebraic filtration shift zero and Alexander upper shift $g$, with tower action $1\mapsto1$ after forgetting $z$.
