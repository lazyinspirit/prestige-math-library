## Statement

Let a balanced attaching system on a closed oriented surface have at least two complementary regions. Retain its actual attaching circles, marked points, and chosen intersection asymptotics. Select a complementary region $R$ and the multiplicity-one cylindrical alpha-boundary-degeneration class supported on its closure. This includes either new region obtained by the actual separating-circle insertion in a marked alpha cut ball. A circle may occur twice on the boundary of $R$; such occurrences are retained, rather than treated as different attaching circles.

For suitable regular geometric data, the normalized count of this class is one modulo two. More precisely, fix its distinguished boundary attachment at infinity, and normalize its simple interior evaluation at one point of $R$ to a prescribed point of the upper half-plane. The resulting moduli space is a compact zero-manifold of odd cardinality. Its unnormalized interior evaluation has onto derivative at every regular representative. Allowing the distinguished boundary attachment point to vary also gives an onto real boundary matching map. Thus the punctured local bigon can be glued using the actual finite matching modes; no transverse-strip inverse is substituted for the stationary alpha sheet.

The assertion concerns this new one-system boundary-degeneration operation. It constructs neither arbitrary-link curved disk ends nor their curvature. It does not assert count one for a singly pointed attaching system with only one complementary region.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The attaching circles and geometric data above. The upper half-plane is denoted $H$. A stationary sheet has its actual fixed alpha asymptotic; its constant mode is not an unbased gauge freedom.

[F1] The complemented implicit-function theorem applies to the Sobolev normal slices used below. [[thm-implicit-function-theorem-for-banach-spaces]] Parameter transversality uses [[thm-sard-smale-residual-regular-values-for-fredholm-maps]]. Neither theorem is used as a boundary-count supplier.

## Proof

1.1 **The surface and the actual stationary sheets.** Cut along all attaching circles. Every cut region is planar, because the circles span the handlebody's full genus lattice. In the selected region glue back each pair of boundary circles which are the two sides of the SAME attaching circle. The resulting surface $R'$ has genus $p$ and $m$ genuine boundary circles, where the $p$ reglued attaching circles lie in its interior and form a cut system for its handles. One has $m\ge1$: the dual graph of the original cut system is connected and has at least two vertices, so the vertex of $R$ has an edge to a different region. Each interior attaching circle retains its one chosen asymptotic point and its stationary alpha sheet. Each genuine boundary circle retains its chosen boundary asymptotic. The remaining global asymptotic points supply disjoint stationary sheets outside this support. Choose separating curves inside $R'$ exhibiting its $p$ handles as punctured tori, each containing its interior attaching circle and asymptotic point, and then separating its genuine boundary holes as disks. This is obtained by cutting $R'$ along the interior cut system, joining its paired faces by disjoint dual arcs and taking small neighborhoods of each handle and of a tree joining the remaining boundary circles. All cuts and collars avoid the finite marks and asymptotic points. Old beta curves may cross them: there is no beta boundary condition in this one-system problem, so those crossings impose no new constraint. The original circles, marks and their collars are retained throughout. In particular a repeated alpha face is now an interior handle WITH a stationary sheet, rather than a spurious new free boundary. [given, construct]

2.1 **The disk response and its normalization.** Start with one genuine boundary disk and its distinguished boundary asymptotic $x$. A conformal coordinate sends its interior to $H$ and $x$ to infinity. A multiplicity-one curve is its graph. If $a_0$ is the chosen ordinary interior matching point, all these graphs have second coordinate $f=sf_0+b$, where $s>0$ and $b\in\mathbb R$ are affine parameters and $f_0$ is one fixed conformal map. For any prescribed $q\in H$ there is exactly one such pair, since $s=\operatorname{Im}q/\operatorname{Im}f_0(a_0)$ and $b=\operatorname{Re}q-s\operatorname{Re}f_0(a_0)$. Its evaluation derivative is invertible: the two vectors $f_0(a_0)$ and $1$ are real-linearly independent. Fixing the end at infinity leaves precisely this affine target-automorphism group; fixing the interior evaluation removes it. Equivalently, one can first mark the boundary attachment in the usual disk-automorphism quotient and take this same slice. There is no unexplained division by an isotropy order. The disk real-boundary graph operator, with its allowable affine end modes retained, is onto: reflection and the Cauchy integral give its inverse modulo the real affine solutions at that end. Its kernel is exactly the displayed affine variation of the graph, and prescribing its interior evaluation removes it. This graph problem is distinct from the decaying stationary-sheet normal problem used below. Adding another genuine boundary disk by target connected sum uses this very response, with evaluation at its ordinary connected-sum point prescribed by the old sheet. It contributes exactly one matched curve, with onto finite matching. This is true-hole peeling; it has not removed any interior attaching circle. [given, construct, algebra]

3.1 **The interior-handle model has TWO nodes.** Choose its flat torus $T=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+\tau\mathbb Z)$, with its alpha circle straight, its asymptotic point $x$, and a distinct ordinary target-plumbing point $p$. The other piece already has a moving sheet through its plumbing point, with physical projection $f(\xi)=q+A\xi+O(\xi^2)$, where $q\in H$ and $A\ne0$. The attachment point is chosen away from the finitely many branch points of the previously constructed curves. Target plumbing $\xi\eta_p=\varepsilon$ prescribes the principal part $A\varepsilon/\eta_p$ in the torus physical projection. There is also the stationary alpha half-plane at $x$, with physical projection the identity. Its source plumbing to the horizontal torus at $(x,q)$ has a second, unknown complex parameter $\lambda$, and prescribes the torus principal part $\lambda/\eta_x$. These are distinct nodes: the first is forced by actual target connected sum, while the second joins the stationary sheet to the moving divisor. Omitting the pole at $p$, or introducing an arbitrary forcing in its place, would describe the wrong problem. For the flat coordinate the torus residue equation is
$$\lambda+A\varepsilon=0.$$
More invariantly it is $\lambda\omega(x)+A\varepsilon\omega(p)=0$, with coordinate derivatives included in the two coefficients, where $\omega$ is a nonzero elliptic one-form. This coefficient never vanishes. Consequently $\lambda=-A\varepsilon\omega(p)/\omega(x)\ne0$ for small nonzero target plumbing. On a flat torus the corresponding physical-projection correction is explicitly $A\varepsilon(\zeta(z-p)-\zeta(z-x))+c$, with $\zeta$ the Weierstrass zeta function and $c$ its additive matching constant. The two zeta quasi-periods cancel. This is a degree-two meromorphic dipole on the torus, with two simple poles, not a degree-one map from a genus-one surface to $H$. On the torus with small disks around its nodes removed it remains close to $q$ and lies in $H$; its pole neighborhoods are glued to the old moving sheet and the stationary disk. Thus the whole source has acquired both a handle and one extra stationary-sheet degree, as its actual branched-cover degree requires. [step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.2 **The normal operator and its missing mean mode.** Here is the actual torus solvability calculation. On the torus punctured at $p,x$, prescribe the pole at $p$ and allow the residue $\lambda$ at $x$. Fix the additive constant by the old root-matching value. The normal operator is $\bar\partial$ on functions, with those two principal parts. Pairing its right-hand side with $\omega$ gives the only mean obstruction: the boundary integrals on the two deleted circles are $2\pi i$ times the two principal coefficients paired with $\omega$, with the common orientation convention. It is exactly the equation of step 3.1. Once that equation is imposed, the zero-mean torus inverse is obtained by dividing each nonzero Fourier coefficient by its nonzero $\bar\partial$ symbol. Its Sobolev bound follows from the linear growth of those symbols. Fixing one value removes its constant kernel. Thus adjoining the ACTUAL source-plumbing residue makes this normal operator onto with zero kernel. For the stationary alpha disk, in a coordinate with alpha equal to the real axis, the required principal part at its interior attachment $b$ has the unique decaying real-boundary response
$$r(v)=\frac{\lambda}{v-b}+\frac{\overline\lambda}{v-\overline b}.$$
Its value is real for $v\in\mathbb R$, its principal coefficient is $\lambda$ at $b\in H$, and it tends to zero at infinity. The difference of two such responses has no pole, has real boundary values and vanishes at infinity; reflection makes it zero. For inhomogeneous terms the same reflected Cauchy inverse applies. The pole location $b$ is fixed by constant matching to $q$; its derivative is the identity, not an unaccounted disk kernel. The torus tangent operator adds no obstruction: varying the source elliptic modulus maps onto its tangent $H^{0,1}$ through the degree-one target projection, and fixing the node removes translations. The disk tangential variations are its source reparametrizations and the explicitly separated end normalization. These give the full, rather than only formal, normal and finite matching blocks. [step 3.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 **Index and actual smoothing.** The torus scalar $\bar\partial$ has a complex constant kernel and complex mean cokernel. Fixing its matching constant removes the kernel, and its unknown source residue supplies exactly the complex dimension needed for the cokernel. Thus the fixed-old-root handle response has real index zero and is onto. The stationary disk response has no additional fixed-end normal kernel or cokernel; the pole location and matching value give equal two-dimensional real finite blocks. A genuine disk-hole response likewise has index zero after its two evaluation constraints. Before prescribing the root, its two real evaluation modes remain. Consequently the fully normalized unit-domain problem has index zero, while its unnormalized interior evaluation has two independent real modes. To smooth the two-node handle, use the displayed principal parts as its pregluing terms and retain the residue, root location and constant matching variables. In cylindrical plumbing coordinates the nonconstant normal Fourier modes have a gap; their Cauchy inverses, the torus mean-zero inverse and the disk reflection inverse give parametrices on the pieces. Cutoffs supported in the long annuli have derivative $O(1/L)$, and the matching principal parts remove the nondecaying errors. Their retained finite block is the nonzero coefficient $\omega(x)$ and the identity constant-matching block. The remaining inverse error is $O(1/L)+O(e^{-\delta L})$ in the weighted plumbing norms, so a Neumann series supplies the actual right inverse. Cauchy-product estimates give a quadratic nonlinear remainder on the normal slice. Contraction, with the finite matching equations included, gives the corrected curve. The residue equation has leading term $\lambda\omega(x)+A\varepsilon\omega(p)$ and higher terms of smaller order; hence its solution has the nonzero leading coefficient in step 3.1 and both source nodes really smooth. The same inverse shows regularity. This construction uses the residue-augmented stationary-sheet operator, rather than asserting that the split horizontal-torus operator was regular. [F1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra]

4.2 **Exhaustion at a handle or disk hole.** The unit domain fixes the energy and allows no multiple cover of a target piece. At a handle limit the torus projection has degree one and the additional alpha end supplies one stationary half-plane sheet. In split coordinates any component with nonconstant physical projection in that packet has degree one onto $H$ and is therefore a disk. Its torus projection, with boundary on a straight essential alpha circle, is constant: lift it to the plane, where the imaginary part has zero boundary values, and use the bounded finite-energy reflection. The remaining torus degree is exactly one horizontal torus at height $q$. The old simple incidence fixes that height; the torus meets the stationary disk exactly at $(x,q)$. Thus the only limit is the two-node configuration of step 3.1, not an arbitrary family of new split solutions. A root at $q\in H$ rules out loss of the whole torus to the boundary of $H$. A second nontrivial boundary level or another torus would require another positive unit of domain or another end degree. Ordinary sphere components are excluded by positivity: a closed component has the same projection degree in every original cut region, whereas the present class has zero multiplicity in at least one other region. For a true disk hole, the only limit is its biholomorphic disk response with the prescribed simple root. Compactness on the thick pieces and exponential control of the nonconstant neck modes therefore put every sequence with these limits in the normal slices of step 4.1. Its finite residues and root locations obey the same matching equations, so contraction uniqueness makes it one of the constructed curves. This proves exhaustion as well as existence; the argument is not a count obtained solely from an index. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, step 4.1, construct]

5.1 **Induction, count and matching.** The starting disk has exactly one normalized curve by step 2.1. Attach each of the finitely many interior handles using the two-node response, and each of the remaining genuine boundary disks using its disk response. Choose successive target-neck lengths large enough for steps 4.1–4.2; at each finite stage choose the next ordinary attachment away from the finitely many critical values on the constructed sheets. Each step has one corrected representative for each existing representative and no others, so the final normalized count is one modulo two. Generic small data changes make all required root evaluations regular. The count is independent of such a change while the actual curves, marks and end collars are retained: the normalized one-parameter moduli is compact. Its unit domain cannot split into two positive one-system classes; a sphere would have positive multiplicity in an excluded complementary region; and fixing an interior evaluation prevents the entire unit class from escaping in target automorphism directions. Consequently its only boundary consists of its two parameter ends. This allows the neck computation to supply the count for the desired regular cut-region data, without changing the physical attaching system. The same response blocks show that varying the ordinary interior root has onto derivative. For the distinguished boundary attachment retain its boundary-point parameter before taking the end slice: in the starting disk it is its actual boundary coordinate, and its derivative is one. Neither the handle residue block nor disk evaluation block removes this coordinate. The implicit construction transports it with the same nonzero derivative, giving the onto real boundary matching needed by the punctured local bigon. There is no additional stabilizer, constant alpha mode or root constraint to discard. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.2, step 4.1, step 4.2, construct, algebra] ∎

## Scope and source notes

This is a new author-stage proof of the particular one-system operation used at the two new alpha cut regions. The essential repeated-face step is the two-node meromorphic dipole and its residue-augmented normal inverse. A previous exploratory one-pole model with a manufactured forcing is not used: it misses the prescribed target-plumbing pole and would obscure the source's actual degree. With only one complementary region there need be no genuine boundary disk to seed a nonconstant old incidence; this proof therefore makes no extension to that case. It also makes no arbitrary-link old-end, curvature, full quasi-stabilization, cobordism or naturality acceptance claim.
