We now provide the description of both the type-S and type-T quasi-stabilization maps on the
level of intersection points and Heegaard diagrams.
Suppose we are given a Heegaard diagram H = (Σ, α, β, w, z) for (Y, L), and w and z are new
basepoints on L, which are contained in a single component of L \ (w ∪z). Furthermore, suppose
that w comes after z. Write w′ and z′ for the two basepoints of L which are adjacent to w and
z. Since w appears immediately after z on L, if follows from Deﬁnition 3.1 that the component of
L \ (w ∪z) which contains w and z is contained in the Uα handlebody of Y \ Σ.
Let A denote the component of Σ\α containing w′ and z′. We pick a point p ∈A\(α∪β ∪w∪z)
and a choice of new αs curve in A passing through p which cuts A into two pieces, one of which
contains w′ and one of which contains z′. Note that αs can in general intersect many diﬀerent β
curves.
Let H0 = (D2, α0, β0, w, z) be a piece of a Heegaard diagram, where D2 is a disk, α0 is an arc
with both endpoints on ∂D2, which cuts D2 into two pieces, one of which contains w and one of
which contains z. Furthermore β0 is a closed curve which intersects α0 twice and bounds a disk

Remark 4.2. The quasi-stabilization maps are only deﬁned when w and z are not the only basepoints
on their link component, since link Floer homology is only deﬁned when all components of a link
have at least two basepoints.
