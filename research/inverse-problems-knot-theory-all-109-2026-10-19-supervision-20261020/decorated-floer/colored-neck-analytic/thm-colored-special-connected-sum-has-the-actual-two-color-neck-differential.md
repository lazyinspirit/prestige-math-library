## Statement

Supply a strongly admissible good balanced multipointed LINK diagram, on its actual intermediate three-manifold, with its finite regular full-marked curved disk count $D$ and the low-dimensional compactness and fixed-end Fredholm realizations needed for that count. No restriction to knots or to the three-sphere is made; existence of these old good ends is an explicit input, not a consequence of this theorem.

Let old adjacent marks $w',z'$ lie in the same alpha cut component. Perform the actual special connected sum: add a separating alpha circle through an ordinary point $p$ of that component, paste the local disk with one new beta circle and marks $w,z$, and use the actual disk-and-arc gluing collars. The new beta circle meets only the new alpha circle, in points $\theta_w,\xi_w$. Then sufficiently stretched regular data give the ACTUAL full-marked count
$$D_+=\begin{pmatrix}D&U_w+U_{w'}\\ V_z+V_{z'}&D\end{pmatrix}$$
on the literal generator module with ordered basis $\theta_w,\xi_w$, over the polynomial ring containing every old marking variable and the two new variables. The four displayed variables remain independent. Every listed term arises from actual index-one curves and their exhaustive matching construction; the formula is not an abstract tensor differential.

The relative grading differences are $\operatorname{gr}_w(\theta_w)-\operatorname{gr}_w(\xi_w)=1$ and $\operatorname{gr}_z(\theta_w)-\operatorname{gr}_z(\xi_w)=-1$. As an operator identity,
$$D_+^2=\bigl(D^2+(U_w+U_{w'})(V_z+V_{z'})\bigr)I_2,$$
where $D^2$ acts separately on each old factor. In particular, if the supplied old operator has scalar curvature $\omega$, the new scalar curvature is $\omega+(U_w+U_{w'})(V_z+V_{z'})$. No variable coloring, square-zero specialization, quasi-stabilization naturality or arbitrary-link old-end existence is inferred.

## Proof

1.1 **Generators, weights and compact domain control.** The new beta circle has no intersection with any old alpha circle. Every generator must therefore use one of its two intersections with the new alpha circle. The other coordinates are exactly one old generator, even if the new alpha circle crosses old beta circles. This proves the literal module identification. Split the local sphere piece into its two new-mark bigons, of multiplicities $n_1,n_2$, and its two neck-side regions, of multiplicities $m_1,m_2$. All old marks remain on the old piece. Strong admissibility is retained in this special insertion: collapsing the new local piece turns a periodic domain into an old periodic domain plus the two new unit alpha cut-region domains; the latter have index two and positive multiplicity in their respective separated marked regions. A nonnegative zero-index periodic ray would therefore have zero new-unit coefficients and give the forbidden old nonnegative zero-index ray. Equivalently, normalize an unbounded sequence of positive domains of fixed endpoint and index by its largest coefficient; its limit is precisely such a ray. Thus the fixed index-one positive-domain inventory is finite. This standard finite-cell argument is about actual domains and indices, not a specialization of marking variables. It permits one common large neck length after the finitely many gluing problems below have been solved. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 **The entire index-one limiting list.** Stretch the target neck at $p$. A weak limit consists of old strips, one-system boundary degenerations, closed components and local curves. The old strip portions cannot have an endpoint on an intersection of the new alpha circle with an old beta circle, by step 1.1. A nonconstant old strip component using only the new alpha circle on one boundary would project to a strip with a boundary extremum or with incompatible two-sided infinite values at its sole boundary puncture. Reflection and the first nonzero term of the holomorphic function rule out the extremum: a function taking an upper half-disk to an upper half-plane has first-order, not higher-order, nonconstant boundary behavior at such a point. Thus additional components with that alpha boundary are one-system degenerations. Write $A$ for their domains and $\phi'_{\Sigma}$ for the remaining old components. The local corner formula gives $\mu(\phi_0)=n_1+n_2+m_1+m_2$. Cut-and-paste of the real Cauchy–Riemann symbols subtracts $m_1+m_2$ for the neck root matching. An alpha-plus degeneration has index contribution $m_1(A)+m_2(A)$ in the two regions meeting the new circle, and twice its multiplicity in every old alpha region disjoint from that circle. Hence
$$\mu(\phi)=\mu(\phi'_{\Sigma})+n_1+n_2+m_1(A)+m_2(A)+2\sum_{Q\cap\alpha_s=\varnothing}n_Q(A).$$
Every summand is nonnegative for a regular positive limit; the old-strip summand is zero only for constants. At index one the last sum vanishes, and exactly one of the five preceding terms equals one. The complete list is consequently an old index-one strip, one new $w$ bigon, one new $z$ bigon, or one of the two unit alpha-plus region degenerations. Extra closed components require positive full-surface degree, contradicting this list and its zero new-mark or disjoint-region multiplicities. Multiple nonconstant old levels also violate its index budget. This classifies all possible limits without assuming the desired matrix. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 **The old-strip response is an actual divisor problem.** In the old-index-one case the local domain has $n_1=n_2=0$ and $m_1=m_2=k$, where $k$ is the old intersection multiplicity with the neck slice. The old strip gives a divisor $\rho_p(u)$ of $k$ points in the physical strip. Choose the ordinary plumbing point generically so these roots are distinct and simple for the finite old inventory. The local returning class has real index $2k$. Prescribing its divisor $\rho_{p_0}$ imposes exactly $2k$ real conditions; its matched response has dimension zero. Its parity is one, as follows directly. For a generic path of distinct interior divisors the fiber union is one-dimensional. Since its two new-mark multiplicities are zero, a nontrivial local strip break must be a returning break and has positive index. It leaves the matched component below the $2k$ remaining interior-root constraints, so the constrained problem is empty there. A boundary degeneration cannot carry a fixed interior root in such a limit: its attaching physical value is on the corresponding boundary of the strip. A sphere would have positive multiplicity at a new mark. Thus the fiber union has only its two prescribed endpoint fibers. Now separate its roots far in the translation direction and move them toward the beta edge. In that limiting family each root supports one multiplicity-one beta-circle disk degeneration, together with the constant returning strip. The actual count and onto response of each such disk are [F1]; they apply to the local two-region sphere, not to an old singly pointed end. The separated matched count is their product, one. The same root-mode gluing described in step 4.1 exhausts this end of the divisor family. Therefore the original local fiber has parity one. It follows that every old strip contributes its old full monomial to each diagonal entry, with no new variable power. This proves the diagonal contribution by the actual $2k$-root response; a constant two-generator tensor factor would miss those modes. [F1, F2, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.2 **All four off-diagonal monomials.** When $n_1=1$, respectively $n_2=1$, the old component is constant and the local component is its ordinary new-mark bigon. Its conformal disk has one regular representative modulo translation, and it contributes $U_w$ from $\xi_w$ to $\theta_w$, respectively $V_z$ from $\theta_w$ to $\xi_w$. In either remaining case the old component is precisely a unit alpha-plus cut-region degeneration and the local component is the corresponding punctured bigon. The alpha-plus system has one more complementary region than the old one, so [F1] supplies its count one, its actual stationary-sheet operators and its onto matching. One cut region contains $w'$ and the other $z'$, since the original balanced alpha region contained just that old adjacent pair. The local bigon has $n_1=n_2=0$. These two contributions are consequently $U_{w'}$ from $\xi_w$ to $\theta_w$ and $V_{z'}$ in the other direction, each with coefficient one. Every other old or new marking multiplicity is zero in these classes. In particular the remote $z'$ term has not been manufactured from a new local bigon or from a preemptive identification $V_z=V_{z'}$. [F1, F2, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 **The actual matching operators and exhaustive gluing.** For the divisor response retain its $2k$ real root positions before taking normal complements. For the unit-region response retain its actual boundary attachment and stationary-sheet finite modes from [F1]. In its interior-handle charts write the source parameter as $\lambda=\varepsilon L$, where $\varepsilon$ is the prescribed nonzero target plumbing. Divide its torus mean equation by $\varepsilon$. The finite equations have leading form $\omega(x)L+A\omega(p)=0$ and $b=q$, with complex unknowns $L,b$. Their Jacobian is $\operatorname{diag}(\omega(x),I)$, hence invertible. The old normalization root is at a separate retained ordinary point; $q=f(p)$ and $A=f'(p)\ne0$ come from its actual old sheet. No normalization root has been silently identified with the plumbing point. The two interior evaluation modes account for the affine part of target reparametrization. Before imposing the distinguished end slice, the remaining real stabilizer of a fixed interior root is the third real disk-automorphism mode; retain it for the boundary attachment matching, and remove it only once. It is not a genuine extra kernel after the full disk-automorphism quotient. These finite blocks, together with [F1]'s torus mean-zero Fourier inverse and real-boundary alpha-sheet Cauchy inverse, replace an inapplicable transverse-strip inverse. On the complements use the supplied old normal inverse and the local Cauchy inverses. In logarithmic annular coordinates the nonzero Fourier modes have gap one; choose weights below that gap. Insert complete principal parts and constant matching before cutting off, so cutoff derivatives act only on their decaying remainders. For example, buffers of radius $|\varepsilon|^{1/4}$ make the old Taylor remainder $O(|\varepsilon|^{3/2})$ and the regular local-pole remainder $O(|\varepsilon|^{5/4})$; after the stated mean rescaling both tend to zero. The normal parametrix error is therefore $O(1/L_{\rm neck})+o(1)$; its mean block is the exact residue calculation, not a constant mode divided by that small error. A Neumann series gives a bounded inverse in these retained-mode plumbing norms. Quadratic Cauchy estimates give the contraction for the preglued error and smooth dependence on all finite parameters. Each regular matched solution gives one corrected curve, with the same monomial and index. Conversely the compactness and list in step 2.1 put any long-neck index-one sequence in one of these weighted slices. Root coordinates and finite residues are its actual matching parameters; the normal remainder solves the same small equation. Contraction uniqueness proves exhaustion. This also supplies the gluing used in the divisor deformation of step 3.1. [F1, F2, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra]

5.1 **The matrix, gradings and curvature before coloring.** Summing the exhaustive five cases yields exactly the displayed matrix over the full independent-variable ring. Positivity and the finite domain inventory justify its polynomial coefficients and a single sufficiently large regular neck length. For the two new bigons the relative formulas are $\operatorname{gr}_w(x)-\operatorname{gr}_w(y)=\mu-2\sum n_w$ and $\operatorname{gr}_z(x)-\operatorname{gr}_z(y)=\mu-2\sum n_z$. Applying them with $\mu=1$ gives $\operatorname{gr}_w(\theta_w)-\operatorname{gr}_w(\xi_w)=1$ and the opposite difference for $\operatorname{gr}_z$. These are relative local comparisons; they invent no absolute grading on an arbitrary intermediate link. Finally square the actual matrix. Its off-diagonal terms are $D(U_w+U_{w'})+(U_w+U_{w'})D$ and their $V$ analogues, which vanish in characteristic two because all variables are central. Both diagonal entries are $D^2+(U_w+U_{w'})(V_z+V_{z'})$. If old curvature is scalar $\omega$, this is the asserted new scalar. Geometrically its increment replaces the old adjacency monomial $U_{w'}V_{z'}$ by $U_wV_{z'}+U_wV_z+U_{w'}V_z$, with every other old adjacency untouched. Neither this multiplication nor the count has set the increment to zero. Old-end existence, its curvature proof, coloring-compatible chain maps and later naturality remain distinct operations. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
