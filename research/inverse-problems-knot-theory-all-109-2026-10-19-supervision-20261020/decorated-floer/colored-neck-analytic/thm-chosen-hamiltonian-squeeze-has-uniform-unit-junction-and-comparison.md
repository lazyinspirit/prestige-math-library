## Statement

Use the actual chosen band triple in the three-sphere after setting every $z$ variable equal to one. Retain its marks, actual auxiliary top cycle, and supplied good regular end data. The new area-balanced chosen-triple construction is supplied: all normalized triply periodic domains in this actual three-sphere trace have zero area and zero Chern evaluation. The actual auxiliary pair, after forgetting $z$, is Hamiltonian-isotopic to the old alpha torus. Its unmarked equal-area bigons are squeezed to the graph of $\epsilon df$, where $f$ is a sum of circle Morse functions with unique maximum tuple. Choose the critical points so the finitely many old generator tuples lie in that maximum's unstable cell. The actual constant-strip end operators at those old generators are transverse.

For sufficiently small positive $\epsilon$, every index-zero small-graph triangle with auxiliary maximum input is the unique corrected junction of its gradient half-trajectory with the constant strip at one old generator. Its coefficient is one and its $w$ multiplicities are zero. The normal inverse is uniform in the rescaled mean and weighted fast norms described below; the assertion includes actual curve exhaustion. The resulting triangle map is the generator-by-generator ordinary near-diagonal comparison.

The chosen finite-angle auxiliary top cycle is carried to this small-graph top class by actual fixed-end Hamiltonian continuation. Parameterized moving-boundary triangles compare the finite-angle band triangle, with that very top input, to the small-graph unit followed by the actual output continuation. Both comparisons use compact source transitions and fixed transverse asymptotic diagrams. No transversality of every intermediate static alpha-prime/beta pair, generic naturality theorem, absolute half-degree anchor or surface-tower theorem is assumed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The supplied NEW chosen-model Hamiltonian squeeze, balanced area and good-end interfaces. The old diagrams and finite-angle auxiliary cycle have not been replaced by unrelated models. All remaining $w$ variables are retained with their actual marking transport.

[F1] On each forgotten-$z$ packet the two unmarked bigons have equal area. Area coordinates and a foliation across the two bigons give a cotangent annulus in which alpha is the zero section and alpha-prime an exact graph. Normalize its primitive to have maximum zero. It is nonpositive. Scaling that primitive is an actual mark-avoiding Hamiltonian squeeze, keeping its maximum intersection fixed. Parallel auxiliary packets have the same graph model. This is chosen local geometry, not an invariance assertion.

[F2] The supplied strict chosen-triple and good-end operations provide actual finite fixed-index domains, core Fredholm estimates and ordinary strip compactness on the thick pieces. Their uniform use during the squeeze is justified in step 4.1. The complemented implicit theorem is [[thm-implicit-function-theorem-for-banach-spaces]]. The source-dependent regularization is relative to the fixed end tensors.

## Proof

1.1 **The mean response, including its endpoint condition.** Put $\tau=\epsilon s$ on the thin auxiliary strip. Its leading map is $q=q(\tau)$, $p=\epsilon t\,df(q(\tau))$, with $q_\tau=-\nabla f(q)$. The normal residual is $O(\epsilon^2)$ in physical coordinates. For an old generator coordinate tuple $q_x$ the unique trajectory from the maximum to $q_x$, ending at $\tau=0$, is defined because $q_x$ lies in its unstable cell. Linearization gives $L_x v=v_\tau+\operatorname{Hess}f(q(\tau))v$ on $(-\infty,0]$. With $v(0)$ specified, this operator is invertible: a fundamental matrix $G$ gives $v(\tau)=G(\tau)v(0)-G(\tau)\int_\tau^0G(r)^{-1}h(r)\,dr$. On the far negative tail the Hessian is negative definite; choose an exponential weight smaller than its smallest positive unstable rate. The finite remaining trajectory segment has bounded coefficients. This gives the actual weighted $W^{1,p}_\tau$ bound for $v$ in terms of its rescaled right-hand side and endpoint, uniformly over the finite tuple set. The old constant-strip operator is $\partial_s+B_x$, where the transverse Lagrangian angles give spectrum $\theta_j+\pi\mathbb Z$, with $\theta_j\notin\pi\mathbb Z$. Its whole-strip inverse is bounded. The boundary attachment mark fixes the strip source translation; the constant map has no map-translation vector, since $u_s=0$. Its boundary evaluation $E_x$ is a bounded trace. The limiting junction block is therefore $(a,v)\mapsto(D_xa,L_xv,v(0)-E_xa)$, with the endpoint condition imposed once. Solve for $a$ first and then $v$; this is a genuine triangular inverse, not a missing slow-mode kernel. [given, F1, F2, construct, algebra]

2.1 **Uniform junction norms and the mixed-mode estimate.** Fix $p>4$, a slow exponential weight below the maximum rate, and physical overlap length $L=|\log\epsilon|$. Write $\delta_\epsilon=\epsilon L$. Use the genuine small spectral cluster $P_\epsilon(q)$ of the transformed auxiliary boundary operator as the mean bundle. It converges to the Fourier constants; all other eigenvalues have a gap tending to $\pi$. Spectral projection diagonalizes the frozen boundary operator. Since $P_\epsilon-P_0=O(\epsilon)$ and $q_s=O(\epsilon)$, its off-diagonal connection derivative on the long strip is $O(\epsilon^2)$; dividing the mean equation by $\epsilon$ therefore leaves a small coupling, rather than an unjustified order-one Fourier coupling. Measure the mean in $W^{1,p}_\tau$, with its equation in $L^p_\tau$ AFTER division by $\epsilon$. Measure the fast and old-core remainders in $\epsilon^{-1/2}W^{1,p}$ in physical coordinates, and their equations and endpoint mismatch with the corresponding $\epsilon^{-1/2}$ factor. The mean endpoint is supplied by the old-core trace and is thus small in this norm.
Do not cut off the slow mean. Solve its one-dimensional equation THROUGH the overlap and use its exact endpoint transfer in the triangular block. That transfer is $I+O(\delta_\epsilon)$ and an $L^p_\tau$ source changes its endpoint by at most $C\delta_\epsilon^{1-1/p}$ times its norm. This handles concentrated sources as well as bounded ones. Only fast normal complements are cut off. Their commutator errors are $O(1/L)$. The difference of the mean from its matched endpoint is at most $C\delta_\epsilon^{1-1/p}\|v\|_{W^{1,p}_\tau}$; its old-core error, with the stated fast scaling, is bounded by $C\epsilon^{1/2-1/p}L^C$. All other junction coefficient errors multiplying bounded traces are supported in a rescaled interval of width $\delta_\epsilon$, so their mean $L^p_\tau$ bound is $C\delta_\epsilon^{1/p}$, not a false uniform small $C^0$ bound. The fast Fourier inverse, old strip inverse and mean transfer consequently give an approximate right inverse with error
$$C/L+C\epsilon^{1/2-1/p}L^C+C\delta_\epsilon^{1/p}+o(1).$$
It tends to zero. A Neumann series gives a right inverse uniformly bounded in precisely these norms. If extra normal kernel survived, a normalized sequence would either have a nonzero fast limit, contrary to the Fourier gap and transverse old inverse, or a mean limit in the fixed-end gradient kernel, which step 1.1 excludes. Thus this is the full normal inverse at the junction. [step 1.1, F2, construct, algebra]

3.1 **Actual correction, without a rescaled nonlinear loss.** Preglue the constant old strip and its unique gradient half-trajectory, retaining the attachment point and the exact mean transfer. The thin residual $O(\epsilon^2)$ becomes $O(\epsilon)$ in the mean equation. The physical junction residual $O(\epsilon)$ may become order one after mean division, but has mean norm $O(\delta_\epsilon^{1/p})$. Its fast/core norm, including the $\epsilon^{-1/2}$ factor, is $O(\epsilon^{1/2}L^C)$. Thus its full norm tends to zero. Use coordinates consisting of the actual mean point $q$ and its complementary fast vector in $\ker P_\epsilon(q)$, rather than a fixed Fourier splitting. This prevents a spurious mean term of size $v\partial_sw/\epsilon$. Tangential nonlinear terms are the smooth gradient-ODE nonlinearity in $\tau$; fast-to-mean terms are either the small spectral-connection terms or quadratic fast terms. The fast remainder has physical size $O(\epsilon^{1/2}\rho)$ in a ball of radius $\rho$. A quadratic fast term divided by $\epsilon$ therefore has mean $L^p$ bound $C\epsilon^{1/p}\rho^2$ on the long strip, and a junction contribution $C\delta_\epsilon^{1/p}\rho^2$. The analogous mixed and fast/core estimates have the positive powers from step 2.1. Sobolev multiplication gives a uniformly small Lipschitz bound on a sufficiently small fixed ball. The uniform inverse and contraction give one corrected index-zero triangle near each limiting configuration and smooth parameter dependence. There is no additional translation quotient: the three-puncture/source attachment normalization has already fixed that position. The correction avoids the $w$ divisors, since its limiting constant point and thin path lie in the given marked-free charts; consequently its actual monomial is one. [F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 **Uniform compactness and all-sequence exhaustion.** A finite bound at each separate $\epsilon$ would not suffice. In every shrinking auxiliary packet its two signed-bigon coefficients change by $(m,-m)$ under its pure periodic generator. Their SUM is fixed by the old thick-region coefficients and the endpoint corners. Positivity therefore bounds both coefficients independently of $\epsilon$; no number of extra turns can be hidden in bigons whose area tends to zero. For a compact moving-boundary transition, cap its boundary by the actual Hamiltonian trace before comparing these coefficients. In the chosen monotone graph squeeze each reference patch is crossed at most the fixed packet number of times; the trace changes the two nonnegativity lower bounds by a fixed constant, while their sum remains fixed. Thus the same bound holds after translating or backtracking that compact transition. The old thick coefficients have the supplied chosen-sector formal-domain bound. This gives a common domain/energy bound for the actual fixed-index squeeze families. Any sequence of index-zero triangles has a thick old limit and a slow mean limit. Its index splits as the old strip index plus the matched gradient-leg index; the maximum's free unstable dimension is canceled by the endpoint matching. A nonconstant regular old strip has index at least one and cannot occur. The old part is thus a constant strip at one of the finite old tuples. The slow projected equation converges to the gradient equation, while its fast component is controlled by the spectral gap. A broken slow leg through a lower critical point would put its final endpoint in that critical point's lower unstable cell. The selected finite tuples avoid the union of those cells, with positive separation from its walls; this excludes such an escaping break. The endpoint condition fixes any possible slow translation. Bubbles consume index at least two and would leave a negative-index regular principal triangle. The only limits are therefore the configurations of step 1.1. The exact mean transfer, fast spectral estimates and thick convergence put every such sequence in the contraction slice of step 3.1. Its unique correction exhausts all triangles. Their full count is consequently the generator-by-generator coefficient-one near-diagonal comparison. [F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 **Transport of the actual auxiliary top.** Keep the maximum intersection fixed in the chosen Hamiltonian squeeze of [F1]. For the REVERSE path, from the small graph to the finite-angle graph, write its primitives as $h_s=\lambda(s)f$, with $\lambda$ increasing, $f\le0$ and $f(q_{\max})=0$. The moving-boundary energy identity for top-to-top strips, with zero periodic area, is $E=\int\partial_sh_s(q(s,1))\,ds\le0$ after the common endpoint action is zero. Hence only the constant maximum strip occurs. Its fixed-end linearization is regular: the mean maximum Hessian keeps the same nonzero sign along the path, the other modes have their gap, and the two-ended first-order inverse has zero kernel and index zero. It gives coefficient one. No static intermediate alpha-prime/beta transversality is required; this auxiliary continuation and the output continuation use a compact moving-boundary transition and FIXED transverse diagrams at their asymptotic ends. Relative source-dependent perturbations give their actual low-index regularity. The energy identity has a uniformly bounded compact transition term. The domain control in step 4.1 and fixed-index compactness give their collars. Reversing the path and contracting its compact backtrack, still with fixed ends, gives inverse continuations up to the actual one-parameter chain homotopies. In particular the forward continuation carries the finite-angle top class to the small maximum class. This is not a nonvanishing conclusion from rank alone: the reverse top coefficient is the explicit constant count, and the inverse identities come from those compactified families. All continuation degree comparisons use their actual constant top reference and the relative gradings, with no absolute half-degree anchor asserted. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 **The parameterized finite-angle triangle comparison has fixed ends.** Put the entire Hamiltonian transition on the alpha-prime boundary of a triangle, constant near its three punctures. Its auxiliary asymptotic pair is the fixed small-graph pair, its old input is fixed, and its output is the fixed finite-angle pair. Translate this compact transition toward the auxiliary puncture or toward the output puncture. At one end the broken configuration is auxiliary continuation followed by the finite-angle triangle; at the other it is the small-graph triangle followed by output continuation. Intermediate static intersections of alpha-prime with beta may be born or die, but no such intermediate diagram is an asymptotic end. The boundary problem remains elliptic, since its different real boundary conditions lie on different source edges. Source-dependent tensors, fixed at the good ends, regularize this actual compact-transition family. Its transition energy bound is invariant under translating the compact segment, and step 4.1 supplies uniform domain control; index-two bubbles would leave a negative-index principal problem in this one-parameter family. The normal inverses give exhaustive collars at its two prescribed broken limits. The boundary-zero count is thus the chain homotopy between the two described compositions, including the ordinary differential terms. Step 5.1 identifies the actual transported top input, and steps 3.1–4.1 identify the small-graph map by actual coefficient-one triangles. Since output continuation has the explicitly constructed inverse, the finite-angle forgotten-$z$ band map is this chosen ordinary comparison on the normalized tower. This asserts only the chosen comparison needed here, not invariance under arbitrary band-triple changes or a generic decorated theory. [F1, F2, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
