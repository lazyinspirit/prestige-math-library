## Statement

Supply a finite regular full-marked old LINK disk count $D$ on its actual good balanced diagram, with low-index Fredholm compactness and the finite positive formal-domain bound through index three supplied by strictly admissible old data. Do not assume an old curvature identity. Suppose the actual split-unknot insertion is represented in a disk of a common alpha and beta region containing just the old pair $w',z'$. Retain its actual old bridge witnesses. Draw the two new overlapping disk-boundary circles $\alpha_s,\beta_0$, crossing twice, with cyclic regions $r,s,t,k$: left lune $r$ contains the OLD pair $w',z'$, overlap lens $s$ is empty, right lune $t$ contains the NEW pair $w,z$, and the outer region $k$ is empty in this disk. The actual old exterior is retained through $k$. Thus $\alpha_s$ bounds $r+s$, and $\beta_0$ bounds $s+t$. The two new bridge arcs in the right-lune ball give the supplied split unknot, while the old bridges remain in the left-lune ball and old exterior.

For suitable sufficiently stretched regular data the literal module on new intersections $\theta_{\rm top},\theta_{\rm bottom}$ has ACTUAL count
$$D_{\sqcup}=\begin{pmatrix}D&P\\0&D\end{pmatrix},\qquad P=U_wV_z+U_{w'}V_{z'}.$$
All four displayed variables and every other old marking variable are independent. The common top intersection is higher by one in both relative $w$ and $z$ Maslov gradings; its Alexander difference from the bottom is zero. The two empty bigons cancel over $\mathbb F_2$, and both product monomials arise from actual curves. In particular the old product comes from the paired alpha-PLUS region, not from an assumed singly pointed old boundary count.

Consequently, if $\Theta$ is an actual old common top cycle, then $\Theta\otimes\theta_{\rm top}$ is an actual new common top cycle. Under uniform coloring $U_w=U_{w'}=U$ and $V_z=V_{z'}=V$, the same count is exactly $\operatorname{diag}(D,D)$. The top inclusion and projection are literal chain maps whose composite is the identity. Thus this split factor preserves a primitive common top class whenever the old one is primitive; its bottom factor remains present. This is an actual chosen-model top-cycle transfer, not arbitrary unlink naturality or an arbitrary link-cobordism theorem.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual local disk/bridge insertion, the old good disk-count data, and the full independent-variable polynomial ring. The split-component topology and old bridge identifications are part of the specified geometric insertion, not inferred merely from the number of marked points.

[F1] The supplied NEW one-system normalization and actual punctured matching are [[lem-new-alpha-cut-region-degenerations-have-unit-count-and-onto-matching]]. It applies to alpha-PLUS with at least two cut regions, including a region with no physical marking variables, and to the local two-region beta-circle disk. Ordinary reference points used for positivity in an empty region introduce no new weighting variable. No singly pointed zero-count interface is used.

[F2] Old-strip Fredholm normal operators, regularity and compactness are explicit supplied good-end data. The normal matching operators for this new placement are supplied below; existence of arbitrary-link old good ends is not deduced from a knot theorem. The complemented implicit theorem is [[thm-implicit-function-theorem-for-banach-spaces]].

## Proof

1.1 **The new geometry and a genuine finite-domain bound.** Use target neck stretching at an ordinary point $p$ ON the circle $\alpha_s$ enclosing BOTH old marks. This is the special connected sum, not an ordinary connected sum at a point off that circle. On the old side $\alpha_s$ splits its old cut region into a paired inner region and an empty outer region. The local inserted alpha arc and beta circle have a paired new bigon and an empty new bigon. The new beta circle meets no old alpha circle, so every generator uses one of its two intersections with $\alpha_s$; the remaining coordinates are precisely one old generator. For a positive formal domain write its four local coefficients as $r,s,t,k$ in the order of the Statement. Collapsing the local disk to coefficient $k$ gives a positive old formal domain $D_0$. At the two new corners, the difference relation is $\kappa=r+t-s-k\in\{-1,0,1\}$, determined by the two endpoint intersections. Euler measure changes by $(r+s+t-3k)/2$ and the new corner averages contribute $(r+s+t+k)/2$. Consequently the actual formal index is $\mu_{\rm new}=\ell_{\rm old}(D_0)+r+s+t-k=\ell_{\rm old}(D_0)+2s+\kappa$. If $\mu_{\rm new}\le2$, then $\ell_{\rm old}(D_0)\le3$. The supplied old formal-domain bound leaves finitely many $D_0$, hence finitely many $k$ and a lower bound $L$ for their old indices. Now $s\le(3-L)/2$ and $r+t=s+k+\kappa$ bound all remaining nonnegative coefficients. This proves actual index-at-most-two positive-domain finiteness and its energy consequence; no blanket transfer of a strict-admissibility criterion is asserted. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 **The five index-one cases for THIS marking placement.** Let $n_1,n_2$ be multiplicities of the two local new bigons and $m_1,m_2$ the local neck-side multiplicities. In a stretched limit the old strip components cannot have an endpoint on an unused intersection of $\alpha_s$ with an old beta curve, because no generator makes that assignment. A further component with boundary only on $\alpha_s$ is therefore a one-system degeneration: a nonconstant strip alternative would have a forbidden extremum of its physical boundary projection at its sole target puncture, by reflection. Let $A$ denote these alpha-plus domains and let $\phi'_{\Sigma}$ denote the remaining old components. The corner formula gives local index $n_1+n_2+m_1+m_2$; root matching subtracts $m_1+m_2$. Thus
$$\mu=\mu(\phi'_{\Sigma})+n_1+n_2+m_1(A)+m_2(A)+2\sum_{Q\cap\alpha_s=\varnothing}n_Q(A).$$
All terms are nonnegative for a regular positive limit. At index one exactly one of the first five terms is one and the last sum vanishes. The list is an old index-one strip, the two new local bigons, and the two unit alpha-plus cut-region degenerations. The formula depends on the geometry of the cut and its real matching, not on putting one color in each bigon. This is why it must be applied anew with the paired and empty placement of this Statement. The alpha-plus system has one more cut region than the old system, so it has at least two even when the old diagram had just one pair. Its paired and EMPTY unit counts are both [F1]. There is no appeal here to the old one-region degeneration count. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 **Diagonal divisor response.** For an old index-one strip the local return domain has $n_1=n_2=0$ and $m_1=m_2=h$, where $h$ is its intersection multiplicity with the neck slice. This statement constrains the empty bigon domain as well as the paired one; an absence of marks in a bigon does not permit its index contribution to be dropped. The local operator has index $2h$ and its actual divisor evaluation has $2h$ real root coordinates. Fix the divisor of the old strip. The regular local fiber is zero-dimensional, and its mod-two count is one. To see this, vary its distinct interior roots along a generic path. A nonconstant returning strip break lowers the matched component's index while all its interior root constraints remain, making that fiber empty. A boundary degeneration cannot carry a fixed interior root at a strip-boundary attaching value. A sphere has positive multiplicity in the paired new bigon and is also excluded. Move the roots far apart and toward the beta edge. The limit consists of the constant return and one local beta-circle unit degeneration at each root. Its actual normalization and onto root response are [F1], with product count one. The retained root modes and normal inverses in step 4.1 glue and exhaust this end of the root family. Compactness therefore keeps the parity one for the original divisor. Every old strip contributes its original full monomial to BOTH diagonal entries, with no new marking factor. [F1, F2, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.2 **The two empty arrows and the two PRODUCT arrows.** In the new paired bigon, its degree-one conformal disk contributes $U_wV_z$ from bottom to top. The other new bigon is empty and contributes one from top to bottom. On the old side the unit region INSIDE $\alpha_s$ contains BOTH $w',z'$ and hence contributes $U_{w'}V_{z'}$ from bottom to top. The unit outer alpha-plus region contains no old mark from that cut component and contributes one from top to bottom. Each old region contribution is matched to its actual punctured local bigon, with coefficient one by [F1]; these are not extra new local bigons. All other old marks lie in other cut components and have multiplicity zero in these unit domains. Thus the two top-to-bottom arrows sum to $1+1=0$, while the reverse arrows sum to $P$. Their opposite degrees are consistent: an empty index-one bigon makes the top higher by one in BOTH $w$ and $z$ Maslov gradings; an index-one bigon through one $w$ and one $z$ has both grading differences reversed. In particular the two intersections have the SAME Alexander grading. The independent product monomials do not cancel before coloring. [F1, F2, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 **Actual matching, nonlinear gluing and exhaustion.** In the old-strip case retain all $2h$ divisor modes. In each region-degeneration case retain the supplied boundary attachment mode and the actual stationary-sheet response of [F1]. For an interior handle of that response the old target-node incidence at $p_0$ is $q+A\xi+O(\xi^2)$ with $A\ne0$. The prescribed target plumbing $\varepsilon$ gives the torus principal part $A\varepsilon/\eta_{p_0}$, while its stationary alpha sheet supplies the second principal part $\lambda/\eta_x$. Set $\lambda=\varepsilon L$ and divide the torus mean equation by $\varepsilon$: its finite block is $\omega(x)L+A\omega(p_0)=0$, together with the identity constant/pole-location matching at $q$. The old normalization root is a different retained ordinary point; it is not identified with $p_0$. The nonzero elliptic one-form and the real-boundary stationary-disk Cauchy response give an onto, index-zero fixed-root normal problem. Keep the third real disk-automorphism mode for boundary attachment BEFORE the distinguished end slice; it is not an additional kernel after quotienting it. On the remaining modes use the mean-zero torus Fourier inverse, the real-boundary disk inverse and the supplied old-strip normal inverse. Insert the exact principal parts before cutting off along logarithmic annuli. Nonzero Fourier modes have a gap; cutoff errors are $O(1/L_{\rm neck})$ and the buffered analytic remainders tend to zero, also after the stated mean rescaling. A Neumann series gives the matched normal inverse. Quadratic Cauchy estimates then give a unique small correction for every regular preglued solution, with the same full monomial. Conversely positive-domain finiteness and compactness give one of the five limits in step 2.1 for every index-one long-neck sequence. Its actual roots, residues and boundary matching parameters put it in the corresponding same normal contraction slice. Uniqueness exhausts that end. Consequently there is no missing cancellation or additional zero-mark curve beyond the two empty cases already counted. Taking one large regular neck length works for the finite inventory. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra]

5.1 **Actual common-top transfer after the correct specialization.** The exhausted counts give the displayed triangular matrix. Hence an old cycle $\Theta$ has $D_{\sqcup}(\Theta\otimes\theta_{\rm top})=0$ already before identifying the four variables, because its entire top-to-bottom coefficient is the actual cancellation $1+1$. Uniformly color the two pairs only AFTER establishing that matrix: $P=UV+UV=0$. The complex is then literally two copies of the old colored complex, with the inclusion into and projection from its top copy satisfying their chain-map identities and projection-inclusion equal to one. An old primitive top class therefore remains nonzero and primitive in that copy. The other copy is retained; relative to the common product normalization its local degrees are $(0,0)$ and $(-1,-1)$, not an acyclic factor to discard. Since top is higher in both gradings, this is the simultaneous $w$- and $z$-top factor needed when extending a chosen band auxiliary top cycle by a standard unlink component. Every claim concerns this actual supplied split-component diagram and its chosen local data. No general split-link excision, arbitrary cobordism, unlink-motion invariance or alternative coefficient restriction has been inferred. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
