## Statement

Take the actual chosen band-subordinate triple $(\Sigma,\boldsymbol\alpha',\boldsymbol\alpha,\boldsymbol\beta,\mathbf w,\mathbf z)$ built from its relative exterior handles and actual compression disks. Retain its chosen auxiliary top cycle and all its local pair, unlink and split-handle packets. In this chosen model, the all-$\mathbf w$-normalized pure auxiliary periodic domains have their disjoint signed-bigon-difference basis, with protected positive and negative witness patches, and the auxiliary restriction of the chosen triangle Spin-c sector is torsion. The actual alpha and beta cut-region caps bound the specified cut balls. Fix finitely many such compatible triangle sectors, each with this auxiliary restriction.

There is a mark-avoiding isotopy of the BETA system alone, supported outside the protected band disks and witness patches, after which the triple is strictly admissible in each chosen sector: every nonzero all-$\mathbf w$-normalized triply periodic integral domain $P$ with $\langle c_1, H(P)\rangle=2N\ge0$ has some coefficient greater than $N$. Both alpha systems, their actual auxiliary cycle, the marks, the band and its capping geometry are unchanged. The same construction gives the required admissibility of the two beta-containing paired ends in the restricted sectors; good regular end data are then chosen by the supplied arbitrary-link end operation. Thus the actual triple meets the strict-domain and good-end hypotheses of the accepted full-marked low-dimensional polygon operation, rather than borrowing a two-ended conclusion.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual chosen triple and packet/cut-ball data in the Statement. All coefficient spaces below are finite-dimensional real extensions of their actual integral periodic-domain lattices. All-$\mathbf w$ normalization means zero coefficient at EVERY $w$ mark.

[F1] The chosen auxiliary packet geometry supplies signed periodic generators $Q_j$, each with one positive and one negative bigon, and all zero near the $w$ marks. For the distinguished band packet these are its two $z$ bigons; for an unlink packet they are its two empty bigons. Its chosen auxiliary sector has zero Chern evaluation on every $Q_j$. These are supplied actual packet and compression identifications, not an inference from a top-cycle rank.

[F2] The actual old/new link-end construction supplies good regular disk data for the resulting admissible beta-containing paired diagrams. The accepted full-marked polygon operation is used only after the triple hypotheses below and those paired good ends have been supplied; it is not used to manufacture admissibility.

## Proof

1.1 **A positive form adapted to the fixed auxiliary pair.** Let $n=|\mathbf w|$. Choose a positive smooth area form $\Omega$ of total area one with $\int Q_j\Omega=0$ for every signed auxiliary generator and area $1/n$ on each alpha cut region. Here is an explicit construction. Give the positive and negative bigon of each disjoint packet the same very small area, with positive density everywhere; distribute an arbitrarily small positive area on all the remaining unmarked pieces and prescribed collars. These choices make the integral of every $Q_j$ zero. Each alpha cut component contains its one $w$ mark. Add the remaining area needed to reach $1/n$ in a small pad at that mark. Every $Q_j$ vanishes on those pads, so these additions retain its zero integral. The initial small areas can be chosen so every addition is positive. For ANY pure $(\alpha',\alpha)$ periodic chain $C$, not necessarily normalized, subtract $\sum_i n_{w_i}(C)A_i$, where $A_i$ are the alpha cut-region chains. The difference is a combination of the normalized $Q_j$. Consequently
$$\int C\Omega=\frac1n\sum_i n_{w_i}(C).$$
This also gives area $1/n$ on every alpha-prime cut region. Nothing has moved either alpha system or its auxiliary cycle. [given, F1, construct, algebra]

2.1 **The relative mark directions are balanced by beta-only area transfer.** First isotope beta, fixing all marks and protected band/witness patches, so each beta cut region has area $1/n$. This requires no unbounded winding along a separating beta curve. To construct the isotopy choose a positive form $\Omega_1$, equal to $\Omega$ on small protected patches, whose integral on each current beta cut region is $1/n$. Each contains its one $w$ pad and has ordinary unprotected interior, so positive bump densities there give these prescribed masses; the protected masses were chosen smaller than $1/n$. Since both forms have total one and agree on the protected disks, their difference has a primitive vanishing near those disks. One may obtain it by joining ordinary region charts by a tree of arcs outside the disks, transferring the finite excess masses along that tree, and using compactly supported disk primitives on the remaining zero-integral charts. Put $\Omega_t=(1-t)\Omega+t\Omega_1$ and solve $\iota_{X_t}\Omega_t=-\eta$, with $d\eta=\Omega_1-\Omega$. The field vanishes near all protected data; its finite-time flow gives $F^*\Omega=\Omega_1$. Move ONLY the beta curves by $F$. Their regions now have the required masses. This is an actual curve isotopy relative the marks and band, with no mark crossing beta. The transfer arcs include the relative directions between different $w$ pads. They handle the multipointed separating directions which closed homology-dual winding would miss. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 **The enlarged undetected kernel has both signs AND Chern zero.** Let $\Pi$ be the normalized triple-periodic space, and send $P\in\Pi$ to the homology class $h(P)=[\partial_\beta P]\in H_1(\Sigma;\mathbb R)$. If $h(P)=0$, the beta boundary bounds an integer or real combination $B=\sum_i m_i B_i$ of beta cut-region chains: the homology relations among a complete meridian system are precisely these region boundaries. Then $C=P-B$ is pure auxiliary and has $n_{w_i}(C)=-m_i$. Steps 1.1–2.1 give $\int B\Omega=\sum_i m_i/n$ and $\int C\Omega=-\sum_i m_i/n$, hence $\int P\Omega=0$. A nonzero such domain has both signs. Its Chern evaluation is also zero in the actual chosen triangle sector. Indeed each $B_i$ cap bounds its beta cut ball. Subtract the corresponding alpha cut-region chains from $C$ to normalize its $w$ coefficients; those alpha caps also bound actual cut balls. What remains is a normalized pure auxiliary class, whose cap lies in the actual auxiliary boundary with its specified torsion restriction. All these Chern evaluations are zero by the supplied capping geometry and [F1]. This uses the actual restrictions of the SAME triangle Spin-c structure; it makes no substitution of a $z$-based restriction for a $w$-based one. Thus the Chern functional factors through $h$. For finitely many chosen sectors there is a common constant $C_c$ with $|c(P)|\le C_c\|h(P)\|$. The unit sphere in $\ker h$ is compact, so finitely many ordinary positive and negative coefficient patches witness a common contrast $\delta>0$. Protect smaller patches there too. They can be chosen outside the band disks, except for already protected auxiliary witnesses inside the $z$ bigons. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 **Actual paired fingers control the remaining homology quotient.** Choose a genus cut basis among the complete beta meridians and pairwise disjoint closed dual curves $\gamma_j$. Their intersection functionals $\ell_j$ detect the beta Lagrangian, hence $h(\Pi)$. They can be constructed in the surface cut along that basis by joining the two copies of each cut through disjoint standard handle corridors. Small detours avoid the finite protected disks and marks while retaining disjointness. Thus the winding corridors are disjoint and lie in the exterior. Along a thin corridor for $\gamma_j$, replace the beta strands by paired opposite fingers: one branch runs $M$ turns along the corridor and its return runs $M$ opposite turns in adjacent parallel tracks. The tracks have distinct ordered radial levels, so each strand remains embedded and different beta strands remain disjoint. Canceling consecutive outgoing/returning turns gives an explicit isotopy of these arcs relative their entrance/exit intervals; extend it across their thin rectangles and by identity outside. The resulting beta system is isotopic to the original one, not a Dehn twist changing its meridians. Choose the two opposite strips with equal swept area; this makes the signed area trace of each beta strand zero. Tiny width adjustments away from the protected patches give exact equality. Thus normalized domains in $\ker h$ still have zero $\Omega$ integral and retain their protected coefficient witnesses. In the winding corridor a periodic-domain coefficient jumps across its fixed alpha/alpha-prime traces by their oriented boundary multiplicities. In one turn their total is $-\ell_j(h(P))$, because $\partial_\alpha P+\partial_{\alpha'}P+\partial_\beta P=0$ in homology. Recording the coefficients in the two terminal finger patches after $M$ turns gives
$$P(q_j^\pm)=\pm M\ell_j(h(P))+E_j^\pm(P),\qquad |E_j^\pm(P)|\le C_0\|P\|.$$
The error is the coefficient in the fixed entrance/exit charts, so its bound is independent of $M$. The different dual corridors are disjoint, so no later winding changes these entrance estimates. This is the actual coefficient estimate, not a claim that a separating mark-relation coefficient could be amplified without bound. Those relative directions were instead balanced in steps 2.1–3.1. [step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 **Strict estimates and the actual polygon hypotheses.** Fix a norm on the original finite periodic space and identify it with its transported domains after the isotopies. Choose $\eta>0$ small enough that $\|h(P)\|\le\eta\|P\|$ implies a protected positive coefficient at least $\delta\|P\|/2$, and $C_c\eta/2<\delta/2$. This follows by compactness from the contrast on $\ker h$, since the protected coefficient functions do not change in the finger moves. Such a $P$ has a coefficient greater than $c(P)/2$. On the remaining unit directions, $\|h(P)\|>\eta\|P\|$. The detecting functionals have $\max_j|\ell_j(h)|\ge\sigma\|h\|$ for some $\sigma>0$. The two opposite signs in step 4.1 therefore give a positive coefficient at least $M\sigma\|h(P)\|-C_0\|P\|$. Choose $M$ so that $M\sigma-C_0/\eta>C_c/2$. Again this exceeds $c(P)/2$, simultaneously in all chosen sectors. For integral $P$ with $c(P)=2N\ge0$ this proves the strict coefficient condition. Periodic domains of either beta-containing paired end are among the triple-periodic domains, and their capped Chern evaluations are the actual sector restrictions, so the same strict condition holds there. The auxiliary end has not moved and retains its actual chosen cycle and signed periodic model. Apply the supplied arbitrary-link good-end construction to those beta-containing ends, choose its coherent regular tensors on them, and then use the accepted full-marked polygon interface at its genuine strictly admissible triple scope. It supplies finite relevant index-zero and index-one triangle lists, their energy bounds and the actual compactified counts on these supplied good ends. This last application is justified by the NEW triple construction and Chern estimates above, rather than by the two-ended domain bound or by a generic auxiliary-pair comparison. The beta isotopies extend their actual compression disks through the exterior, so the represented links, band and triangle cobordism Spin-c transport are unchanged. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra] ∎
