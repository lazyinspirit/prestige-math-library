## Statement

Supply actual good full-marked disk-count data $D$ for an arbitrary old based link diagram, with its finite positive formal-domain inventory through index three and its low-dimensional Fredholm compactness. Choose an ordinary attachment point $p$ in ANY old alpha-beta face, away from curves and marks. It need not share that face with an old $w$ or $z$ mark.

Attach a sphere by an ordinary target connected sum at $p$ and a point $p_0$ OFF both new curves. On the sphere draw two overlapping disk-boundary circles $\alpha_0,\beta_0$ meeting twice. Put the newborn $w,z$ pair in their overlap lens $s$, put $p_0$ in the opposite outer region $k$, and leave the two intervening lunes $r,t$ empty. The actual new bridges lie in the lens ball and form the split newborn unknot; the old link and its marked data are retained outside that ball. This is a birth attachment, not a same-component quasi-stabilization or the same-region unlink-neck operation.

For sufficiently stretched regular data, in the ordered new intersection basis $\theta_+,\theta_-$, the ACTUAL count has the form
$$D_B=\begin{pmatrix}D&H\\0&D\end{pmatrix}$$
for a finite polynomial matrix $H$ over all old and new independent marking variables. No formula for $H$ is required. The two empty local bigons go from $\theta_+$ to $\theta_-$ and cancel; the $w$ and $z$ relative Maslov differences are both one. Thus
$$B(x)=x\otimes\theta_+,\qquad E(x\otimes\theta_-)=x,\qquad E(x\otimes\theta_+)=0$$
commute with the actual disk operators. They are the chosen split-birth and death chain maps when the supplied ends are square-zero. Their definition does not identify a birth with the alternating same-component map.

After setting ALL $z$ variables equal to one, this very diagram, its neck, its $w$ weights and its top/bottom maps are the ordinary OFF-CURVE free-point creation and removal model. The comparison is literal at the same geometric data and counts. No choice of old marks in the attachment face or silent identification of different colored links is used for this equality. After identifying the ordinary $w$ variables, the actual relative-edge chain identity supplies a triangular change of basis to $\operatorname{diag}(D,D)$ which preserves BOTH these maps; in particular the born top is primitive in the retained two-dimensional factor.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** For the final ordinary normalization, also supply the actual relative-edge index-two identity on the new good target: $D_B A_\lambda+A_\lambda D_B=(U_{\rm old}+U_{\rm new})I$. This is the genuine weighted boundary-end identity, with all its compactification hypotheses, not an inference from the triangular strip count. The main old-link owner supplies this new interface. The actual off-curve connected sum, the supplied old good ends and their marking transport. The chosen birth/death cap is the local disk minimum/maximum with one new $w$ arc and one new $z$ arc; its two Heegaard bridge arcs lie in the newborn lens ball.

## Proof

1.1 **Index and finiteness for the new off-curve placement.** Every generator uses exactly one of the two new intersections; the other coordinates are the old generator. Write $s$ for the marked-lens multiplicity, $k$ for the outer multiplicity, and $r,t$ for the empty-lune multiplicities. The local corner relation is $r+t=s+k+\kappa$, where $\kappa=1,0,-1$ for top-to-bottom, diagonal and bottom-to-top endpoints. The local sphere index is $2k+2s+\kappa$; ordinary target connected sum subtracts $2k$ for the $k$ complex roots. Consequently
$$\mu_{B}=\ell_{\rm old}(D_0)+2s+\kappa.$$
Here $D_0$ is the positive old formal domain obtained by collapsing the local disk to coefficient $k$, and $k=n_p(D_0)$. This follows as well by the Euler-measure change $(r+s+t-3k)/2$ and the additional corner averages $(r+s+t+k)/2$. For index at most two, $\ell_{\rm old}\le3$. The supplied old formal-domain bound fixes finitely many $D_0$, hence bounds $k$ and gives a lower bound for $\ell_{\rm old}$. The displayed formula bounds $s$, and the corner relation bounds $r,t$. All relevant positive-domain and energy bounds are therefore finite independently of the target neck length. This argument involves no location of an old mark in the face of $p$. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 **The lower-left entry vanishes for actual geometric reasons.** In a long-neck limit old components have nonnegative index. With $\kappa=1$ and total index one, the index equation forces old index zero and $s=0$. The old representative is constant, so $k=n_p(D_0)=0$. The corner relation leaves exactly the two empty bigons, one in $r$ and one in $t$. Each conformal disk has one regular representative modulo translation. All its marking multiplicities are zero, old and new, so their contributions are literally $1+1=0$ over $\mathbb F_2$, before any variable specialization. The ordinary bigon matching has no nonzero old root in this case and its contraction is the elementary disk one. Thus no remote weighted correction can appear in the top-to-bottom entry. The empty index-one bigons also give the relative top-minus-bottom differences $+1$ in both $w$ and $z$ gradings. [F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 **The diagonal requires a NEW off-curve return calculation.** For a diagonal index-one curve the old component has index one and $s=0$. Local return domains satisfy $r+t=k$ and have index $2k$. The old simple divisor $\rho_p(u)$ has $k$ distinct interior roots for generic $p$ and regular data; the local divisor constraint has real codimension $2k$. Define the local return count by summing ALL such local classes with $s=0$ and that prescribed divisor. Its parity is one for either returning intersection. Here is the essential difference from an alternating marked model: empty index-one local strip breaks ARE allowed. Along a generic path of distinct divisors, a breaking strip cannot carry any prescribed root, since those remain in a compact subset of the strip interior while its attaching physical value escapes to an end. Such a strip has $k=s=0$ and is exactly one of the TWO empty bigons. These broken ends pair and cancel, with identical remainder and monomial. This holds at either end of the returning curve. Sphere components are excluded because they have positive marked-lens multiplicity; a boundary component through the connected-sum point cannot carry a fixed interior divisor in that boundary limit. Thus the only uncanceled ends are the two prescribed divisor fibers, whose parities agree. Now separate the roots far in the translation direction and move them toward the beta edge. The resulting matching consists of one local beta-region unit degeneration at each root and the constant strip. The actual beta-region count and its onto matching are [F1] and their product is one. The root-mode inverse glues this separated limit and exhausts its collars. It follows that the original return count is one. This argument does not incorrectly exclude empty strip breaks by an index-only assertion. The actual newborn $w,z$ are BOTH in the zero-multiplicity lens, so each diagonal old strip retains exactly its old full $U,V$ monomial and no new factor. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.2 **Compactification, including tube components and ghosts.** For completeness consider an index-one diagonal sequence as the OFF-CURVE neck grows. On the old side an unmarked boundary or closed component has index at least two and would leave a negative-index regular old principal part, since its total old index is one. After trimming constant ghosts the old part is a single embedded regular index-one strip. Its divisor is simple. The tube projection has constant physical coordinate by the maximum modulus principle, so its nonconstant components are exactly $k$ once-covered cylinders at those distinct roots. On the local sphere the matched component has index at most $2k$. A component with a double point has expected dimension lowered by twice its singularity number. The old divisor locus has dimension one before translation, hence codimension $2k-1$; the matched expected dimension is at most $1-2\operatorname{Sing}$, and after the common translation it is nonnegative only with no singularities and the full index $2k$. No positive-domain local component can be omitted. Stabilize any remaining constant ghost components and smooth nodes between ghosts. A smoothed ghost cannot lie at a puncture, has at most one attachment to the embedded nonconstant pieces, and has no unpunctured boundary component; otherwise an embedding or the physical maximum principle fails. Stability then makes each such ghost have genus at least one. The cylindrical index identity $\operatorname{ind}=d-\chi+2e$ and $e(D_B)=e(D_0)+e(D_{\rm sphere})-2k$ force
$$\chi(S_B)=\chi(S_{
m old})+\chi(S_{
m sphere})-2k.$$
Adding any of those positive-genus ghosts lowers the right gluing Euler characteristic by at least two, contradicting this identity. Thus no ghost survives. Every diagonal limit is exactly the regular old/local matched pair counted in step 3.1. This deals with the actual off-curve compactification rather than borrowing an on-alpha degeneration collar. 4.1 **Actual normal gluing and the unspecified upper entry.** Retain the $2k$ root coordinates on both pieces and their finite difference-of-evaluations map. At a regular constrained local representative it is onto. The local response at separated beta degenerations has the supplied onto one-system block, and generic path/root data give regular fibers elsewhere. On the normal complements use the genuine old critical strip inverse and the local Cauchy–Riemann inverse. Their punctured normal Fourier operators have their constant root modes already retained and a gap on the remaining modes. Exact root matching cancels the nondecaying terms; long logarithmic cutoffs give $O(1/L)$ error and exponentially small tails. The finite onto block and a Neumann series give the normal right inverse. Quadratic Sobolev estimates and the implicit theorem give one correction per regular matched pair. Conversely step 3.2, its root coordinates and the end splitting put every diagonal sequence into one of these same contraction slices. Uniqueness proves exhaustion and validates the local divisor deformation counts. Step 2.1 already exhausts the other controlled column/row. For the reverse entry $\kappa=-1$, the old index may be two with $s=0$, or zero with $s=1$; both may contribute. The finite domain bound and regular full-marked count make their sum a polynomial matrix $H$. No claim that this is a local scalar or that distant old-region contributions vanish is needed. Thus the controlled entries give exactly the stated triangular count. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra]

5.1 **Birth/death and the ordinary free-point comparison.** The triangular matrix gives $D_BB=BD$ and $ED_B=DE$ by multiplying its top column and bottom row. These identities hold over the full independent-variable ring, even before any supplied curvature is specialized to square zero. They therefore define actual birth and death chain maps on the chosen good complexes. Now set every $V_z$ equal to one on the SAME geometric diagrams and perturbation data. Each actual curve keeps its $w$ monomial and its count; only its $z$ weight is forgotten. The result is precisely the ordinary multipointed free-point sphere joined at the off-curve point $p$, with the two empty bigons, the new $w$ lens and the remote old region. Its top has the ordinary relative Maslov difference one, and the maps are the literal ordinary top inclusion and bottom extraction at that neck. This proves the ordinary free-point comparison for this actual birth/death model, not a comparison with an alternating colored link. It does not require computing the residual ordinary $H$. In particular no two-dimensional auxiliary factor is discarded and no chosen-variable restriction is replaced by a simultaneous collapse. The following actual edge computation supplies the common-color direct-sum normalization without erasing $H$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 **The NEW ordinary primitive-top comparison.** Choose an embedded relative arc $\lambda$ from any retained old $w$ to the newborn $w$, avoiding other marks and transverse to the curves; its final segment enters the marked lens across $\alpha_0$ once. Define the actual ordinary edge action by weighting every index-one strip by its alpha-boundary intersection with $\lambda$. The lower-left entry again has ONLY the two empty local bigons of step 2.1. The alpha-boundary difference of those two disks is the full new circle $\alpha_0$ modulo two. Its intersection with the final segment of $\lambda$ is one. Therefore their weighted sum is $I$, even though their unweighted sum is zero. Old components are constant in this entry, so crossings of $\lambda$ elsewhere contribute nothing. All other entries are finite actual counts. Thus $A_\lambda=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\I&c\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. After the ordinary variables are identified, the supplied actual edge identity gives $D_BA_\lambda+A_\lambda D_B=0$. Its upper-left block reads $Da+H+aD=0$. In particular $H=Da+aD$. Set $Q=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&a\\0&I\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Over $\mathbb F_2$ it satisfies $Q^2=I$ and direct multiplication gives $QD_BQ=\operatorname{diag}(D,D)$. Moreover $QB=B$ and $EQ=E$. This is an actual polynomial change of basis preserving the chosen top inclusion and bottom extraction. It proves that the retained ordinary factor has primitive born top and the required top/bottom free-point normalization, without a Laurent inverse, removal of the factor, a formula for the colored $H$, or a claim that $H$ vanishes at the original basis. It also yields $EA_\lambda B=I$ directly. [given, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
