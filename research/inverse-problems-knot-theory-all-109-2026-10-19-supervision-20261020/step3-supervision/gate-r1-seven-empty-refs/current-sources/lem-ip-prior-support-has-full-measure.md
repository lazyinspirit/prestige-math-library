## Statement

Let $X$ be a separable real Banach space with its norm metric $d_X(x,x')=\|x-x'\|_X$ and let $\mu$ be a Borel probability measure on $X$. Define
$$\operatorname{supp}\mu:=\{x\in X: \mu(U)>0\text{ for every norm-open neighborhood }U\text{ of }x\}.$$
Then $\operatorname{supp}\mu$ is Borel and $\mu(\operatorname{supp}\mu)=1$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A separable real Banach space $X$ and a Borel probability measure $\mu$ on its norm Borel sigma-algebra.

[F1] Separability supplies an at most countable dense subset of $X$. [[def-separable-space]]

[F2] The norm metric satisfies the triangle inequality, and its positive-radius open balls form open sets in the metric topology. [[def-banach-space]], [[def-norm-and-normed-space]], [[def-metric-space]], [[def-metric-topology]], [[def-metric-ball]], [[thm-metric-open-set-algebra]]

[F3] The positive rationals form an at most countable dense subset of the positive reals, and a product of two at most countable sets is at most countable. [[thm-rationals-countable]], [[lem-q-and-irrationals-dense-r]], [[thm-product-of-countable]], [[def-countable]], [[lem-subset-of-countable]]

[F4] Every nonempty at most countable set is the range of a sequence indexed by $\mathbb N$. [[lem-countable-iff-surjection-from-n]]

[F5] The Borel sigma-algebra is generated by the open sets and is closed under countable unions. [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]

[F6] A measure is zero on the empty set, is monotone, and is countably subadditive. [[def-measure]], [[prop-measure-monotonicity]], [[thm-finite-and-countable-subadditivity-of-measures]]

[F7] A probability measure assigns mass one to its whole space; together with zero mass on the empty set this makes $X$ nonempty. [[def-probability-measure]], [[def-measure]]

## Proof

**Given:** The hypotheses in the Statement.

1.1 By [F1], choose an at most countable dense set $C\subseteq X$. Since $\mu(X)=1$ and $\mu(\varnothing)=0$ by [F7], $X$ is nonempty, so $C$ is nonempty. Let $\mathbb Q_{>0}$ be the positive rationals. By [F3], $C\times\mathbb Q_{>0}$ is a nonempty at most countable set, so [F4] gives a surjection $e:\mathbb N\to C\times\mathbb Q_{>0}$. The composite of $e$ with $(c,q)\mapsto B(c,q)$ is a surjection onto $\mathcal B_0:=\{B(c,q):c\in C,\ q\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\}$, so $\mathcal B_0$ is at most countable. Each of its members is open by [F2]. It is a base: if $x\in U$ with $U$ open, choose $r>0$ so $B(x,r)\subseteq U$, choose $c\in C$ with $\|x-c\|_X<r/3$, and choose rational $q$ with $\|x-c\|_X<q<r-\|x-c\|_X$ using density in [F3]. Then $x\in B(c,q)$, and for every $z\in B(c,q)$ the triangle inequality gives $\|z-x\|_X<q+\|x-c\|_X<r$, so $x\in B(c,q)\subseteq U$. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F7, choose, construct]

2.1 Let $\mathcal N:=\{B\in\mathcal B_0:\mu(B)=0\}$ and $N:=\bigcup\mathcal N$. By [F3], $\mathcal N$ is at most countable; by [F5] $N$ is Borel, and by [F6] its measure is zero (with the empty-family case giving $N=\varnothing$). If $x\in N$, a zero-measure open basis ball contains $x$, so $x\notin\operatorname{supp}\mu$. If $x\notin N$, every open neighborhood $U$ of $x$ contains some $B\in\mathcal B_0$ with $x\in B\subseteq U$ by step 1.1; since $B\notin\mathcal N$, $\mu(B)>0$, and [F6] gives $\mu(U)\ge\mu(B)>0$. Thus $X\setminus N=\operatorname{supp}\mu$, which is Borel and has measure one. [F3, F5, F6, step 1.1, given] ∎
