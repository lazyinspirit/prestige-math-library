Abstract
In this paper we propose a novel regularisation strategy for structured linear inverse problems of
the form Ax = F ◦Gx = z. Instead of using the operator A or a complex scheme involving both F
and G simultaneously, combining already existing and simpler regularisations for F and G allows
the use of established and optimised programs for high-speed computations. As classical parameter
choice rules fail in the scenario when no source conditions are known because intermediate error
bounds may be unavailable, we provide both a-priori and a-posteriori rules with a convergence
speed analysis given source conditions. We test the proposed method on the pre-clinical medical
imaging technique of magnetic particle imaging with different choices of regularisation and on a
convolution problem for an analysis of the convergence speed.

Introduction
Considering a linear inverse problem, we are interested in solving the operator equation
Ax = z,
(1)
with a linear map A : X →Z between Hilbert spaces X and Z. However, the right hand side z is not
readily available but only a noisy version zδ with
z −zδ ≤δ with noise level δ. The operator is often
times ill-posed and naive solutions are thus heavily corrupted by the noise. Regularisation methods
are necessary to stabilise the inversion process. Therefore, the reconstruction error is split in two
parts resulting in the data error and the approximation error. It is necessary to balance both types of
error against each other. Classical regularisation approaches include variational or iterative methods.
Variational methods are based on minimisation of functionals consisting of a data term which ensures
that the minimising solution solves the operator equation (1) approximately and a regularisation
term penalising undesired behaviour such as oscillations or enforcing desired behaviour like sharp
edges, depending on the application. Machine learning can be included in modern approaches, see for
example [Arr+19] for an overview and further [Zha+22; Hur+24; Can+26; REM17]. Regularisation
methods include a parameter describing how well the unstable inverse of A is approximated and
which therefore influence data and approximation error. For high-quality reconstructions it is not
only important to choose an appropriate regularisation, but also to pick the regularisation parameter
properly in dependence of the noise level. Additional information about the forward operator can be
used to choose or design the regularisation, for example by including PDE constraints on the solution
[Bra+24; AR26; MDR26].
In this manuscript, we assume that the operator A
A = G ◦F
(2)
is composed of two linear maps F : X →Y and G : Y →Z with an additional Hilbert space Y .
This type of additional information was already used to design regularisation terms, see for example
[Bra+24] or [BSW24]. Instead of applying one adapted but complicated regularisation scheme, we
want to split the regularisation process into multiple regularisation steps which may be easier to
compute, i.e. we want to design regularisations for the operator equation (1) using regularisations for
the operators F and G.
