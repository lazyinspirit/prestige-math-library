Conclusion
In this paper, we proposed a method to design regularisation methods for structured inverse problems
with applications in imaging and machine learning. By combining existing regularisations, it is possible
to use optimised methods. We further derived a-priori parameter choice rules if source conditions are
known and the double discrepancy principle, an a-posteriori choice rule if they are not known. All
parameter choice rules were analysed with regard to convergence speed, resulting in the expected, but
suboptimal result of O

We tested the double discrepancy principle on a simulated MPI problem comparing the reconstruc-
tions with different regularisation methods to a benchmark regularisation using a system matrix and
the Kaczmarz-algorithm. This gave visually great results, regardless of the regularisation methods
and showed stability of the second chosen regularisation parameter independent of the regularisation
method used in the step before. Moreover, the method performed well even for regularisation methods
not covered in the Assumptions 5 used for the proof of the regularisation parameter, leading to future
research angles.
The convergence speed of the double discrepancy principle given source condition was explored con-
firming the regularisation property derived theoretically and mostly confirming the convergence speed.
In case of the Tikhonov regularisation with finite qualification, the method showed superior perfor-
mance than expected, which needs further investigation.
