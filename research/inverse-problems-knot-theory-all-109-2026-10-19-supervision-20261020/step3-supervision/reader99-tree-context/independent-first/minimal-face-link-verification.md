Fix a midpoint m and a point p of its inverse image outside the already regular boundary-product neighborhood. Continuity restricts a small neighborhood of p to the interior of the target edge containing m. In that edge coordinate the actual piecewise-affine map is a PL scalar h, with h(p)=0. Work in its stated common finite subdivision. Let τ be the least domain face containing p. Since p is in the relative interior of τ and all its vertices avoid the level, h|τ is nonconstant (indeed some vertex values are negative and some positive). Choose two vertices u,v∈τ with d=h(v)−h(u)≠0. The u,v barycentric coordinates at p are positive. On a sufficiently small neighborhood their coordinates remain >η>0. Local finiteness permits restricting to the union of cofaces containing τ.

For x in any such coface, replace λ_u(x) by λ_u(x)−t/d and λ_v(x) by λ_v(x)+t/d, leaving every other barycentric coordinate unchanged. For sufficiently small |t| this remains in the same coface and defines Φ_t. The affine h formula gives h(Φ_t(x))=h(x)+t. All cofaces use the SAME u,v and d; their formulas coincide on intersections. Φ_−t is the inverse. Thus (y,t)↦Φ_t(y) is a common local product with inverse x↦(Φ_−h(x)(x),h(x)). It is not a separate simplex-by-simplex sheet construction; in particular it cannot branch at a shared edge.

One must additionally identify the zero slice as a surface, rather than infer factor-manifold cancellation from a product. The compatible whole-star triangulation supplies an explicit link description near an interior point of the k-face τ: R^k×cone(link(τ)). Since dimN=3 and k≥1, link(τ)=S^(2−k) for k=1,2, while k=3 has no transverse factor. The displayed barycentric shear removes one τ-coordinate from the scalar equation. Therefore the pair has coordinates (R^(k−1)×cone(S^(2−k)))×R, with the zero slice at normal coordinate0. At k=1 the first factor is cone(S1)=R2 near its cone point; at k=2 it is R×cone(S0)=R2; at k=3 it is R2 directly. This is an actual locally flat plane-pair chart, not an unsupported dimension count.

The boundary is handled by the specified already smooth product collar, which supplies half-disk/neat charts. This must mean a genuine neighborhood of the protected points, as the text says, not only product behavior on one closed side of an unprotected seam. The declared relative corner-triangulation interface explicitly places a smaller protected subcomplex in the relative interior of the retained neighborhood piece; thus the existing buffered-collar interpretation supplies those open regular neighborhoods. A generic closed seam with a midpoint-valued seam vertex would require a separate argument, and is not used here. No new collar condition or transversality theorem is introduced.

Each connected lifted midpoint sheet maps to a single midpoint. An element preserving that sheet stabilizes the target edge. The no-edge-inversion property proved in1.1 prevents it from reversing the scalar normal direction, so the local two-sided structure descends. Finite quotient triangulation gives finitely many affine level cells and compactness downstairs. These are precisely the local regularity facts subsequently used by smoothing3.1. No change to the equivariant map or new choice is needed for this local verification.
