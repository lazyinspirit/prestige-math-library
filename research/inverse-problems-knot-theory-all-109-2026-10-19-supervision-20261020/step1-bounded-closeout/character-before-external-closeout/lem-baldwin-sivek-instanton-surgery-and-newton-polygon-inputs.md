## Statement

Assume full AC and let $K\subset S^3$ be a knot. The input package required by the Baldwin–Sivek thin-knot and infinitely-many-abelian-surgeries arguments consists of the following conclusions with their stated hypotheses: (i) a nontrivial $\mathrm{SU}(2)$-averse knot has a unique rational accumulation slope and either it or its mirror is an instanton L-space knot; (ii) a nontrivial instanton L-space knot is fibered and its symmetrized Alexander polynomial has coefficients in $\{-1,0,1\}$ with nonzero coefficients alternating in sign; (iii) the L-space satellite restrictions and the pattern/companion surgery-transfer statements exclude the satellite cases used in §§3–5; and (iv) the hyperbolic Dehn-filling theorem and the knot-exterior JSJ classification reduce the remaining cases to torus knots. This carrier records the exact imported result interfaces and does not assert that these conclusions have been proved locally.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The full original input-package scope. The argument below proves the finite representation/pillowcase operation conditional on the exact missing instanton alternative [L1]; it does not assume that alternative has already been established locally.

[F1] Filling with primitive slope $p[\mu]+q[\lambda]$ imposes the actual group relation $\mu^p\lambda^q=1$. Abelianization of a knot group is generated by its meridian. A unitary reducible two-dimensional representation is simultaneously diagonal: the orthogonal complement of its invariant line is invariant as well. Consequently its image is abelian, and a representation with abelian image factors through the cyclic abelianization of the filled group. Thus SU2-abelian and SU2-cyclic are equivalent here, including the zero-slope case. [[def-sl-two-abelian-surgery]] [[thm-knot-exterior-homology-and-longitude]]

[F2] The actual finite representation and common-eigenline incidence varieties have finitely many irreducible components. For each component, dimension of its affine image is the transcendence degree of its coordinate function field. The generic adjoint trace pairing, cap identity, pair exact sequence and word derivations apply over the actual finitely generated characteristic-zero field. [[lem-sl2-representation-variety-is-affine]] [[lem-classical-affine-algebraic-set-finite-irreducible-components]] [[thm-affine-domain-dimension-transcendence-degree]] [[lem-generic-adjoint-peripheral-kernel-is-the-logarithmic-conormal-line]]

[F3] The ordinary A-polynomial is a nonzero square-free Laurent equation for the one-dimensional peripheral eigenvalue-image components, including the abelian component. It omits isolated image components but not points on a curve component. [[def-knot-a-polynomial]]

[F4] The actual marked pattern/companion filling transfers, including reducible nonabelian SL2 cases, and the closed hyperbolic holonomy lift are already supplied. They do not establish the instanton alternative [L1], L-space surgery, or Alexander coefficient restrictions. ## Proof

1.1 **The exact full slope set.** Write $\mathcal S(K)=\{p/q\in\mathbb Q:\gcd(p,q)=1,\ q>0,\ E_K(p/q)\text{ is SU2-abelian}\}$. Its elements are DISTINCT slopes, not a selected sequence. The meridional slope $\infty$ is isolated and is not a finite-surgery parameter in this set. A simultaneous peripheral diagonalization of an SU2 representation gives $\rho(\mu)=\operatorname{diag}(e^{i\alpha},e^{-i\alpha})$ and $\rho(\lambda)=\operatorname{diag}(e^{i\beta},e^{-i\beta})$. The simultaneous inversion $(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto(-\alpha,-\beta)$ is the pillowcase quotient. For any continuous lifted path through peripheral images of irreducible representations, a slope in $\mathcal S(K)$ satisfies $p\alpha(t)+q\beta(t)\notin2\pi\mathbb Z$: equality would give an actual irreducible representation of the filled group, contradicting [F1]. Continuity puts this entire real function in one component of $\mathbb R\setminus2\pi\mathbb Z$. Thus for ANY two points of that path, $|p(\alpha_1-\alpha_0)+q(\beta_1-\beta_0)|<2\pi.$ This is a full-set exclusion, valid for positive, negative and zero numerators. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 **The exact instanton interface selects a nonvertical path.** Under [L1], its first alternative makes $\mathcal S(K)$ empty: for any finite $p/q$ choose $\beta=-p\pi/(2q)$ modulo $2\pi$ on that full vertical circle, giving an actual irreducible filling representation. Hence an SU2-averse knot, for which $\mathcal S(K)$ is infinite, must satisfy the second alternative. On a smaller closed subinterval its meridian is noncentral and already diagonal. Its commuting longitude is diagonal too; its eigenvalue is a continuous circle-valued function, so subdivision into finitely many open semicircle charts gives a continuous real lift $\beta$. The meridian coordinate $\alpha=s$ has distinct endpoint values. This is the ONLY use of [L1] in steps3.1–7.1; no bare nontrivial-knot representation-existence assertion is silently substituted for its varying-meridian conclusion. [F1, L1, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 **Boundedness of ALL slopes.** Fix such a nonvertical path and endpoints, put $d=\alpha_1-\alpha_0\ne0$, $r_0=-(\beta_1-\beta_0)/d$ and $C=2\pi/|d|$. Step1.1 gives $|p/q-r_0|<C/q\le C$ for EVERY $p/q\in\mathcal S(K)$. Thus the full set is contained in the fixed bounded interval $[r_0-C,r_0+C]$; an unbounded exceptional subsequence is excluded. In a bounded interval there are only finitely many reduced fractions with denominator at most any fixed $Q$: for each $1\le q\le Q$ only finitely many integers $p$ satisfy the interval bound. Consequently, along EVERY sequence of pairwise distinct elements of $\mathcal S(K)$, $q\to\infty$ and the displayed inequality forces $p/q\to r_0$. The infinite set therefore has exactly one accumulation point in $\mathbb R\cup\{\infty\}$, namely the finite $r_0$. This proves both quantifiers needed when passing to arbitrary infinite subsets. [step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 **All lifted paths are straight and parallel.** Apply step1.1 to any two points of any continuous lifted irreducible peripheral path and to a sequence of distinct elements of the FULL set. Divide by $q$ and use step3.1 to obtain $r_0(\alpha_1-\alpha_0)+(\beta_1-\beta_0)=0.$ Thus $\beta+r_0\alpha$ is constant on each path, and any nonconstant one lies on a line of slope $-r_0$. In particular the varying-meridian path of step2.1 contains every point of a nontrivial interval of that line: its $\alpha$ coordinate covers the whole endpoint interval by continuity. No finite-component or semialgebraic path-selection assertion is required for this implication. [step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 **A nonzero Laurent equation actually vanishes on this arc.** For completeness the needed image-dimension operation is the accepted field-linear adjoint calculation [F2], not an assumption that the A-polynomial Definition excludes larger images. If a generic irreducible eigenline-incidence component had algebraically independent $M,L$, its function field would admit two derivations independently differentiating $M,L$; extend these through its finite separable algebraic part by the minimal-polynomial derivative formula. The actual boundary homology has dimension two. Self-duality and the pair exact sequence make both boundary inclusion and cohomological restriction rank one. The actual word/eigenbasis pairing therefore puts ALL logarithmic derivative pairs $(DL/L,DM/M)$ in the same line, contradicting those two independent derivations. Reducible components have $L=1$ by knot abelianization. All peripheral images thus have dimension at most one. The finitely many zero-dimensional image components give only finitely many points. The nonconstant interval of step4.1, after discarding these points, lies in the union of the one-dimensional image curves; hence the nonzero Laurent polynomial $A_K$ in [F3] vanishes on it. By continuity it vanishes on the entire interval. This argument allows arbitrary dimensions of the underlying character components and does not confuse a one-parameter family with a maximal image component. [F1, F2, F3, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 **Rationality by a finite exponential calculation.** Write $A_K(M,L)=\sum_{(a,b)\in T}c_{ab}M^aL^b$, with finite distinct integral pairs and nonzero coefficients. On the interval $M=e^{i\alpha}$, $L=e^{i(c-r_0\alpha)}$, step5.1 gives $\sum_{(a,b)\in T} c_{ab}e^{ibc}e^{i(a-r_0b)\alpha}=0.$ If $r_0$ were irrational, all real numbers $a-r_0b$ would be distinct: equality for different pairs would either force identical pairs or express $r_0$ as a ratio of integers. Differentiate this finite identity $0,1,\ldots,|T|-1$ times at an interior point. Its coefficient matrix is a Vandermonde matrix in the distinct numbers $i(a-r_0b)$, multiplied by nonzero diagonal exponentials. Its determinant is nonzero, forcing every $c_{ab}e^{ibc}=0$, contrary to their choice. Thus $r_0\in\mathbb Q$. This proves rationality without importing a transcendence-number theorem or claiming arithmetic algebraicity of isolated pillowcase points. [F3, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 **Original-facing disposition.** Steps1.1 and3.1–6.1 provide the full elementary representation/curve operation: a single nonvertical irreducible peripheral path plus infinitude of the FULL SU2-abelian set implies boundedness, a unique finite rational accumulation slope, convergence of EVERY distinct slope sequence, and the same limit for EVERY infinite subset. Step2.1 derives the needed path from precisely [L1], whose actual knot/instanton proof remains owed. In particular the geometric argument is not a proof of [L1] and does not establish the instanton L-space or Alexander conclusions [L2]. No nonzero bound such as $|r_0|>2$ is inferred from the elementary argument; any use of that additional conclusion remains with its actual missing instanton input. The full original package Statement remains unchanged and unproved as a whole. The separate BFR exceptional actual-kernel specialization issue remains exactly as previously recorded and is not reopened or used here.
