## Statement

Let $T$ be an oriented torus and let $([\mu],[\lambda])$ be an ordered basis of $H_1(T;\mathbb Z)$. An unoriented essential simple closed curve on $T$ has homology class $p[\mu]+q[\lambda]$ for a unique pair $(p,q)$ up to simultaneous sign, and that pair is primitive: $\gcd(p,q)=1$. Conversely, every primitive pair $(p,q)$ determines an essential simple closed curve, unique up to isotopy and simultaneous reversal of orientation.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An oriented torus $T$ and an ordered integral basis $([\mu],[\lambda])$ of its first homology.

[A1] Identify $T$ with $\mathbb R^2/\mathbb Z^2$ so the coordinate loops represent $[\mu]$ and $[\lambda]$; the chosen basis fixes this coordinate convention.

## Proof

1.1 Let $p,q\in\mathbb Z$ satisfy $\gcd(p,q)=1$. Bézout's identity gives $r,s\in\mathbb Z$ with $ps-qr=1$. The matrix with columns $(p,q)$ and $(r,s)$ lies in $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb Z)$, so it induces a homeomorphism of $\mathbb R^2/\mathbb Z^2$. The image of the first coordinate circle is embedded, essential, and represents $p[\mu]+q[\lambda]$. Reversing its parameter negates both coefficients, so the unoriented curve is unchanged. [given, A1, construct, algebra]

1.2 If $\gamma$ is an essential simple closed curve on $T$, it is nonseparating; cutting along it gives an annulus. An arc joining the two annulus boundary circles closes to a simple curve $\delta$ meeting $\gamma$ once, so the algebraic intersection is $[\gamma]\cdot[\delta]=\pm1$. Writing $[\gamma]=p[\mu]+q[\lambda]$ and $[\delta]=a[\mu]+b[\lambda]$ gives $pb-qa=\pm1$ because the ordered basis has intersection form $\pm1$. Any common divisor of $p,q$ would divide $pb-qa$, so $\gcd(p,q)=1$. [given, A1, construct, algebra]

2.1 Suppose $\gamma_0,\gamma_1$ are essential simple closed curves whose primitive classes agree up to sign. Reverse one orientation if necessary so their classes agree, and isotope them to be transverse. Let $v$ be their common primitive class and let $A=\mathbb R^2/\langle T_v\rangle$ be the cyclic cover of $T$ corresponding to the subgroup generated by $v$. A lift of each curve to $A$ is an embedded essential circle representing the generator of $H_1(A;\mathbb Z)$. Their algebraic intersection in the annulus $A$ is zero, so if they meet the signed crossing count in a fundamental period has both signs. Lift these circles to the universal cover $\mathbb R^2$ of $A$. Their chosen lifts are proper lines invariant under the same translation $T_v$; they have the same two ends at infinity. Opposite-sign crossings on this pair of lines have a bounded complementary disk between subarcs, by the Jordan curve theorem. Among all such disks choose one with the fewest interior intersections with lifted curves, and among ties one of least transverse width modulo the deck translations. Any lifted arc entering its interior cuts off a disk with fewer interior intersections; any nontrivial translate meeting it yields a disk of smaller transverse width. Thus its interior misses every lift and translate, so it projects injectively to an embedded bigon in $T$. Isotoping one curve across this bigon removes exactly two intersections and creates none. Since the original intersection set is finite, repetition terminates and makes the curves disjoint. Cutting $T$ along $\gamma_0$ gives an annulus, and the disjoint essential curve $\gamma_1$ is parallel to its core, so the two curves cobound a subannulus. Isotopy across that annulus carries $\gamma_0$ to $\gamma_1$. Thus the primitive class modulo simultaneous sign determines exactly one unoriented isotopy class. [given, A1, step 1.1, step 1.2, construct] ∎
