[F5] Bare locally flat proper arcs with specified product end collars can be straightened by [[thm-locally-flat-codimension-three-arc-unknotting]]. This retained local result is not being strengthened into a relative transverse pair-tube or complement theorem.

[F6] **External TOP input, initially in ordinary ZFC:** connected sum is well-defined on the concordance classes of smooth oriented knots represented by proper locally flat annuli in $S^3\times I$. In particular, the two given annulus concordances imply an oriented locally flat annulus concordance between their endpoint connected sums, in the usual collared-end convention of [F1]. This is the TOP connected-sum operation in Ray, §3, Definition 3.1, Definition 3.5 and the discussion through Proposition 3.6, printed II–11–II–12. It is imported here, not presented as a locally reconstructed pair-tube/complement proof.

[F7] **External set-theoretic input:** for a real $r\subseteq\omega$, the relativized constructible class $L[r]$ is a transitive inner model of ZFC containing $r$ and all natural numbers. Karagila, §8.3, closing remarks, printed p.42, gives the $L[A]$ construction and its inner-ZFC conclusion; for a real parameter, $r\subseteq\omega\subseteq L[r]$, so the parameter belongs to that model. No ambient full AC is assumed by this input. The compact coding argument below, rather than an implication from ambient full AC, supplies the Countable Choice specialization of [F6].

4.1 For the TOP implication, first code the actual input as compact metric data. Parametrize the two annuli by the standard compact annulus, and choose finitely many local pair charts covering each, together with their inverses and the given end-collar maps. Such a finite selection is possible by compactness and finite choice; using countably many relatively compact chart restrictions instead would require only $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$. Include the endpoint knots and the chosen connected-sum representatives from [F2]. For these smooth knots and the finite smooth coordinate maps used in their sums, also code the derivatives of every finite order, their continuity moduli and rational difference-quotient remainder bounds; thus the inner model sees smooth knots, not merely continuous embeddings. Continuous maps on these standard metric domains are coded by their values on fixed countable rational dense sets and uniform continuity moduli on compact exhaustions; include inverse moduli for embeddings and chart inverses. All this countable numerical data is one real $r$. [F1, F2, F6, F7, given, construct, choose]

4.2 We use certificates, not an unqualified assertion that homeomorphism existence is absolute. For every open chart domain record its rational-ball code (relative rational half-balls at a boundary chart), compact exhaustion, and positive-distance margins putting the images of exhaustion pieces in the corresponding open image. Record both map/inverse identities on dense sets. For the pair condition record the parametrization of the linear plane section and its identities with the annulus parametrization; on smaller charts also record positive separation from the compact remainder of the annulus outside that patch. A finite parameter-patch cover and a Lebesgue-number certificate cover the entire annulus. Properness is certified on the compact subannuli $S^1\times[1/j,1-1/j]$ by positive separation from the ambient end levels. Equalities with the oriented endpoint parametrizations and the product collars are dense-set equalities with their continuity moduli. The smooth derivative identities are certified by rational difference quotients and their coded remainder bounds at each finite order. These certificates express input validity using only rational inequalities, integer indices and quantification over natural numbers. They therefore have the same truth in $L[r]$ and in the ambient universe; in particular the coded input is valid in $L[r]$. [F1, F2, F7, step 4.1, construct]

4.3 Apply [F6] inside $L[r]$, which satisfies full Choice by [F7]. It gives a locally flat annulus for the two connected sums. In that model choose its finite pair-chart cover, inverse maps, compact-exhaustion moduli, open-image margins and end-collar certificates of the same form as in step 4.2; collect them into a real $s\in L[r]$. Decode $s$ in the ambient universe by completion from the rational dense domains. The coded forward and inverse moduli give continuous inverse maps on their coded domains; the dense-set identities extend to all points, and the open-image and separation certificates make these actual local pair charts with no additional surface branch. The finite cover certificate, properness margins, and endpoint/collar identities likewise persist. Thus the decoded object is an actual oriented proper locally flat annulus in the ambient $S^3\times I$, with the required endpoint connected sums and product collars. This transfer uses arithmetic certificates for the particular compact objects, not preservation of arbitrary sets or homeomorphisms between inner models. [F1, F2, F6, F7, step 4.1, step 4.2, construct]

4.4 Steps 4.1–4.3 earn the required compact specialization of the external TOP input under the stated $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, without assuming ambient full AC. Together with the retained smooth construction in steps 1.1–3.1, this proves both implications in the unchanged Statement.
