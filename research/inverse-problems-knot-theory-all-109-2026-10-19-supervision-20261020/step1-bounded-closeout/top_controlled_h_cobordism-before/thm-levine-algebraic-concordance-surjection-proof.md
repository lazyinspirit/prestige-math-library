## Statement

Assume full AC. Let $\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sm}}$ and $\mathcal C_{\mathrm{top}}$ denote, respectively, the smooth and locally flat topological concordance groups of smooth oriented knots in the standard oriented $3$-sphere, with connected sum as operation. Let $G_{\mathbb Z}$ be the integral algebraic concordance group generated by integral Seifert lattices modulo direct-sum and metabolic relations. In either category $\mathcal C$, the rule $[K]\mapsto[(H_1(F;\mathbb Z),V_F)]$, where $F$ is any connected oriented Seifert surface of $K$ and $V_F$ is its positive-push Seifert pairing, defines a surjective group homomorphism $\Phi_{\mathcal C}:\mathcal C\to G_{\mathbb Z}$.

## Proof

1.1 For a smooth oriented knot $K$, choose a surface and basis from [F1] and define $a(K)=[V_F]\in G_{\mathbb Z}$. The class is independent of this choice: [F4] relates any two such matrices by finitely many congruences and elementary enlargements or their inverses. A congruence is an isomorphism of the integral lattice, and each enlargement preserves the class. This also handles every integral basis choice. Applied to a transported surface under an oriented ambient isotopy, the same surface-independence statement and the pairing convention give a function on oriented knot types. This part is proved without a slice-disk premise. [A1, F1, F2, F4, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Put representatives of $K,J$ and their chosen surfaces in separated balls, and join the surfaces by the connected-sum band away from the old basis curves. Their old first-homology bases together form a basis of the boundary-connected sum: collapsing the connecting band gives the wedge of the two surfaces, so first homology is their direct sum. Positive push-offs and the curves from opposite balls have linking number zero, since any curve in one ball bounds a rational, indeed integral, singular two-chain there, disjoint from the other ball. Thus the matrix is $V_K\oplus V_J$, and [F2] gives $a(K\#J)=a(K)+a(J)$. The unknot has its disk surface with empty matrix and hence $a(U)=0$. By step 1.1 these calculations are independent of the separated representatives and chosen surfaces. [F1, F2, F5, F7, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Work first in the smooth category. A knot smoothly concordant to $U$ bounds a smooth proper disk by [F6], so [F8] gives $a(K)=0$. The inverse of $[J]$ is represented by $J^\dagger$, and therefore $J\#J^\dagger$ is concordant to $U$. Step 2.1 gives $a(J^\dagger)=-a(J)$. If $K$ and $J$ are smoothly concordant, their group difference $K\#J^\dagger$ is concordant to $U$, so $0=a(K\#J^\dagger)=a(K)-a(J)$. Hence $a$ descends to a well-defined map $\Phi_{\mathrm{sm}}$ on smooth concordance classes. Step 2.1 proves that this map is a group homomorphism. These are actual smooth conclusions using [F8], not conditional uses of its unproved TOP branch. [A1, F2, F6, F8, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 The smooth homomorphism is onto. Each positive generator $[V]$ of $G_{\mathbb Z}$ is the image of the realizing knot from [F3]. The image of a group homomorphism is a subgroup, so it also contains the negative of every such generator. Every element of the presented integral group is a finite sum of signed generators, hence is in the image. This proves the entire smooth surjective-homomorphism assertion. [F2, F3, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 For the locally flat category the remaining descent argument has the same algebra but still requires exactly [L1] and [L2]. Once the topological group law is supplied, its zero class is represented by a collared concordance to $U$. Cap its lower boundary by the standard unknot disk in a standard ball. Gluing that ball to $S^3\times I$ gives a standard ball with an added radial collar, and the matching annulus/disk product charts give a locally flat proper disk. The TOP slice-metabolizer assertion [L1], if proved, then gives zero algebraic class. The inverse and difference argument of step 3.1 would prove topological concordance invariance, and the realization argument of step 4.1 would prove surjectivity. At the exact current supplier bindings neither [L1] nor [L2] is established, so this is a precise remaining implication, not a proof of the TOP conclusion. [F2, F3, L1, L2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]
