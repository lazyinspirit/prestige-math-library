SUPPLIER alg-seifert-surface-from-diagram
Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice. Let $D$ be a finite regular oriented link diagram with exactly one component, and let $c$ be its number of crossings. Perform the following construction in its projection sphere $S^2$.

1. Choose a point $q\in S^2$ outside the finite diagram. In pairwise disjoint small disks about the crossings, replace each crossing by the unique noncrossing smoothing compatible with the strand orientations. Let the resulting oriented circles be the Seifert circles and let $s$ be their number.
2. For each Seifert circle $C$, take the closure of the component of $S^2\setminus C$ that does not contain $q$. Under the stated choice axiom, each such region is an embedded compact disk. The finite family of disk regions is nested or disjoint. Orient each disk to induce the given orientation of its boundary circle, then place nested disks at distinct nearby heights ordered by inclusion; the resulting disk interiors are pairwise disjoint in $S^3$.
3. For every original crossing, attach one rectangular band along short boundary intervals on the two smoothing arcs. A half-twist means a single $180$-degree turn of the band across the crossing neighborhood. Choose its direction, endpoint orientations, and over/under order to match the original crossing.
4. Return the finite disk-and-band construction data together with $(s,c)$.

The returned data are a finite attached two-dimensional complex. This algorithm's definition alone does not assert that the resulting image is embedded, is a surface, is connected, or has the original knot as boundary; the separate correctness lemma establishes those properties.

SUPPLIER def-coprime-torus-knot
Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice. Identify the standard oriented three-sphere with $S^3=\{(z_1,z_2)\in\mathbb C^2:|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2=1\}$ and choose $0<r<1$. The standard unknotted Heegaard torus is $T_r=\{|z_1|=r,\ |z_2|=\sqrt{1-r^2}\}$; its two sides are solid tori. In the angular coordinates $(u,v)\in(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)^2$ given by $(u,v)\mapsto(r e^{2\pi i u},\sqrt{1-r^2}e^{2\pi i v})$, let $\alpha$ and $\beta$ be the first and second coordinate classes in $H_1(T_r;\mathbb Z)$. For coprime nonzero integers $p,q$, the **oriented torus knot** $T(p,q)$ is the image of $s\mapsto(r e^{2\pi i p s},\sqrt{1-r^2}e^{2\pi i q s})$, for $s\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$, oriented by increasing $s$; it represents the primitive slope $p\alpha+q\beta$. The mirror convention is the reflection $(z_1,z_2)\mapsto(z_1,\overline{z_2})$, which sends $T(p,q)$ to $T(p,-q)$ with image orientation, and reversing the knot orientation gives $T(-p,-q)$. For nonzero $p,q$ with $d=\gcd(|p|,|q|)>1$, the corresponding torus link is the union of $d$ disjoint parallel copies of the primitive slope $(p/d,q/d)$; it is not a single embedded curve of class $p\alpha+q\beta$. The exterior is $E_K=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}N(K)$ for a chosen closed tubular neighborhood. A positive meridian is the oriented boundary of a normal disk, and a preferred longitude is a degree-one class on $\partial E_K$ whose image in $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)$ is zero; this definition alone asserts neither existence nor uniqueness.

SUPPLIER def-ideal-tetrahedron-shape-and-cusp-holonomy
Use the upper half-space model
$$
\mathbb H^3=\{(x+iy,t):x,y\in\mathbb R,\ t>0\},\qquad
ds^2=t^{-2}(dx^2+dy^2+dt^2),
$$
whose ideal boundary is $\mathbb C\cup\{\infty\}$. An oriented ideal tetrahedron in normalized position has ideal vertices $0,1,\infty,z$, where $\operatorname{Im}z>0$. Its three edge-shape parameters are
$$
z,\qquad z'=\frac1{1-z},\qquad z''=\frac{z-1}{z},
$$
each assigned to one pair of opposite edges; the parameter at a corner of a truncated ideal vertex triangle is the parameter of the incident edge. For an oriented path $\gamma$ in a triangulated torus link of ideal vertices, its cusp multiplier $H(\gamma)$ is the product of the corner parameters encountered along $\gamma$, using the parameter when the path cuts off that corner on its left and its reciprocal when it cuts off the corner on its right.

SUPPLIER def-incompressibility-seifert-fibration-and-jsj-system
Let $M$ be a compact smooth $3$-manifold. A properly embedded surface is **two-sided** if it has a product neighbourhood $F\times[-1,1]$ in $M$. A **compressing disk** for a two-sided surface $F$ is an embedded disk $D\subset M$ with $\operatorname{int}(D)\cap F=\varnothing$ and $\partial D$ an essential simple closed curve in $F$; $F$ is **incompressible** if it has no compressing disk. A **boundary-compressing disk** is an embedded disk $D$ with $\partial D=\alpha\cup\beta$, where $\alpha=D\cap F$ is an arc not cutting off a disk in $F$, $\beta=D\cap\partial M$, and $\operatorname{int}(D)$ misses $F\cup\partial M$; $F$ is **boundary-incompressible** if it has no such disk. A surface is **boundary-parallel** if it cobounds a product with a subsurface of $\partial M$, and it is **essential** if it is incompressible, boundary-incompressible, and not boundary-parallel. $M$ is **irreducible** if every embedded $2$-sphere in its interior bounds an embedded $3$-ball in $M$. An incompressible torus is boundary-parallel if it cobounds a product $T^2\times[0,1]$ with a component of $\partial M$; $M$ is **atoroidal** if every incompressible torus is boundary-parallel. A **Seifert fibration** is a decomposition into circles with local models given by fibered solid tori; in the model $(D^2\times S^1)/\langle(z,w)\mapsto(e^{2\pi i/q}z,e^{2\pi ip/q}w)\rangle$, where $\gcd(p,q)=1$, the core fiber has multiplicity $q$. Fibers of multiplicity $1$ are **regular** and those of multiplicity greater than $1$ are **exceptional**. A finite family of pairwise disjoint incompressible tori is a **JSJ system** if every component obtained by cutting $M$ along the family is atoroidal or Seifert-fibered. It is **inclusion-minimal** if no proper subfamily is a JSJ system. Atoroidal does not mean hyperbolic.

SUPPLIER def-knot-connected-sum-and-prime-knot
Fix a coordinate orientation-reversing reflection $r:S^3\to S^3$ for the mirror convention. Let $K$ and $J$ be smooth oriented knots in the standard oriented three-sphere. A connected-sum datum consists of one smoothly embedded closed three-ball around a short subarc of $K$ and one around a short subarc of $J$, each meeting its knot in a single trivial properly embedded arc, together with the fixed standard orientation-reversing boundary identification of the two standard oriented ball boundaries that joins the oriented arcs into one circle. Choose this standard map once in the standard two-ball model so that it commutes with $r$ and an orientation-preserving half-turn interchanging the two balls preserves the joining convention. Here a trivial arc is an embedded interval in a ball that is carried by an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism of ball-arc pairs to a straight diameter. Remove the interiors of the two balls and glue the remaining boundary spheres using this fixed identification; the resulting oriented knot in the standard oriented three-sphere is a connected sum of $K$ and $J$, denoted $K\#J$ for the specified datum.

The **standard unknot** is the oriented unit circle in a fixed coordinate two-plane of $S^3\subset\mathbb R^4$. A knot is **nontrivial** if it is not ambient isotopic through orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms of $S^3$ to the standard unknot. A nontrivial knot is **prime** if it has no connected-sum presentation whose two summand knots are both nontrivial.

SUPPLIER def-knot-exterior
Assume countable choice, as required for the smooth normal-bundle structure in the tubular-neighbourhood interface. Let $K:S^1\to S^3$ be a smooth oriented knot, let $\nu(K)=TS^3|_K/TK$ be its quotient normal bundle, and let $\Phi:\Omega\to S^3$ be a tubular-neighbourhood map from an open neighbourhood $\Omega$ of the zero section in $\nu(K)$ that restricts to the inclusion of $K$. Choose a radius $\epsilon>0$ whose closed disk subbundle $D_\epsilon\nu(K)$ is contained in $\Omega$, and set $N(K)=\Phi(D_\epsilon\nu(K))$. The **exterior** of this chosen tube is
$$
E_K:=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}_{S^3}N(K).
$$
Retain its boundary $T_K:=\partial E_K=\partial N(K)$, orient $E_K$ by restriction of the fixed orientation of $S^3$, and orient $T_K$ as the boundary of $E_K$. The choice of tubular map and radius is part of the input to this definition.

SUPPLIER def-knot-meridian-and-preferred-longitude
Assume countable choice as in the chosen tubular-neighbourhood data. Let $K$ be a smooth oriented knot with chosen exterior $E_K$ and boundary torus $T_K=\partial E_K$, and let $p:T_K\to K$ be the projection of the chosen normal-disk tube. Orient each normal disk so that its positive tangent direction followed by its oriented normal basis agrees with the orientation of $S^3$. A **meridian** $\mu$ is the oriented boundary of one such normal disk. A **degree-one parallel curve** is a simple closed curve $\lambda$ in $T_K$ for which $p|_\lambda:\lambda\to K$ has degree $+1$. A **preferred longitude** is a degree-one parallel curve satisfying $i_*[\lambda]=0$ in $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)$, where $i:T_K\hookrightarrow E_K$ is inclusion. This is a condition on a curve; this definition alone asserts neither existence nor uniqueness. Reversing the orientation of $K$ reverses the orientations of both $\mu$ and any degree-one parallel curve. If a subgroup $P\le\pi_1(E_K,x)$ is specified using a path from $x$ to $T_K$, record it only up to replacing it by $gPg^{-1}$ for $g\in\pi_1(E_K,x)$.

SUPPLIER def-knot-seifert-pairing-and-matrix
Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $K:S^1\hookrightarrow S^3$ be a smooth oriented knot in the standard oriented three-sphere, and let $F\subset S^3$ be a connected compact smooth oriented embedded surface with boundary $K$, oriented so that its boundary orientation is the given orientation of $K$. Let $g$ be the nonnegative integer determined by $\chi(F)=1-2g$. Choose an ordered integral basis $a_1,\ldots,a_{2g}$ of the first singular homology group $H_1(F;\mathbb Z)$, represented by integral one-cycles on $F$. Orient the normal line of $F$ by requiring $(n,u,v)$ to be positively oriented in $S^3$ whenever $(u,v)$ is a positive tangent basis of $F$. The positive push-off $a_i^+$ is the cycle carried to a sufficiently small positive normal level in a bicollar of $F$.

For disjoint oriented closed curves $J,L\subset S^3$, orient a meridian $\mu_L$ of $L$ so that a positive tangent to $L$ followed by a positive basis of its normal disk gives the ambient orientation. The linking number $\operatorname{lk}(J,L)$ is the integer determined by $[J]=\operatorname{lk}(J,L)[\mu_L]$ in $H_1(S^3\setminus L;\mathbb Z)$, using the Alexander-duality identification that sends $[\mu_L]$ to $1$. Extend linking additively over integral one-cycles. The Seifert pairing is $\theta_F(x,y)=\operatorname{lk}(x^+,y)$, and its matrix in the chosen basis is $V=(V_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{2g}$, where $V_{ij}=\theta_F(a_i,a_j)=\operatorname{lk}(a_i^+,a_j)$. The surface and basis are part of the data; this definition asserts no knot-only matrix invariant.

SUPPLIER def-metabolic-seifert-form-and-algebraic-knot-concordance
Assume the full Axiom of Choice when a knot's Seifert pairing is formed. An integral Seifert lattice is a pair $(H,V)$ in which $H$ is a free abelian group of rank $2g$ for some $g\geq0$, and $V:H\times H\to\mathbb Z$ is bilinear with $V-V^T$ unimodular. The rank-zero lattice is allowed, with the determinant of its empty skew matrix equal to $1$. A metabolizer is a primitive direct summand $M\subset H$ of rank $g$ such that $V(x,y)=0$ for every $x,y\in M$; the lattice is metabolic when it has a metabolizer. A chosen Seifert surface of an oriented knot gives such a lattice through the positive-push pairing. Its form is algebraically slice when that lattice is metabolic.

An isomorphism of integral Seifert lattices $(H,V)$ and $(H',V')$ is an integral group isomorphism $P:H\to H'$ satisfying $V'(Px,Py)=V(x,y)$ for all $x,y\in H$. Let $\mathcal S$ be the commutative monoid of these isomorphism classes under orthogonal direct sum, where $(H,V)\oplus(H',V')$ has underlying group $H\oplus H'$ and pairing $V\oplus V'$, with the rank-zero lattice as identity. The integral algebraic concordance group $G_{\mathbb Z}$ is the abelian group generated by symbols $[V]$ for $[V]\in\mathcal S$, subject to $[V\oplus W]=[V]+[W]$ and $[M]=0$ for every metabolic lattice $M$. Two selected Seifert forms are algebraically concordant when they represent the same element of $G_{\mathbb Z}$. This form relation does not assert the existence of a smooth or locally flat topological concordance annulus between the knots.

SUPPLIER def-oriented-seifert-surface-and-knot-genus
Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice. For an oriented smooth knot $K\subset S^3$, define

$$\mathcal G(K):=\{n\in\mathbb N_0: \text{there is a Seifert surface }F\text{ for }K\text{ with }g(F)=n\}.$$

Every element of $\mathcal G(K)$ is the genus of a connected compact oriented surface. The existence theorem makes $\mathcal G(K)$ nonempty, and the well-ordering of $\mathbb N_0$ gives its least element. The **smooth knot genus** is $g(K):=\min\mathcal G(K)$. This genus is the minimum over embedded spanning surfaces; it is not the number of Seifert circles or the genus of every surface produced from a diagram.

SUPPLIER def-oriented-slope-and-knot-dehn-filling
Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$. Let $K$ be a smooth oriented knot in the oriented $3$-sphere, let $N(K)$ be a chosen closed tubular solid-torus neighborhood, let $E_K=\overline{S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}N(K)}$, and let $T_K=\partial E_K=\partial N(K)$. Let $\mu$ be the positive oriented meridian, meaning the boundary of a positive normal disk in $N(K)$, and let $\lambda$ be a preferred longitude, meaning a degree-one parallel of the core $K$ in $N(K)$ that is null-homologous in $E_K$. Assume that $[\mu],[\lambda]$ are an ordered basis of $H_1(T_K;\mathbb Z)$, with the boundary-orientation convention $\mu\cdot\lambda=-1$. A slope is the unoriented isotopy class of an essential simple closed curve on $T_K$ whose homology class is $p[\mu]+q[\lambda]$ for coprime integers $p,q$ not both zero; $(p,q)$ and $(-p,-q)$ represent the same slope. For a slope represented by $p[\mu]+q[\lambda]$, the Dehn filling $E_K(p/q)$ is obtained by gluing an oriented solid torus $D^2\times S^1$ to $E_K$ by an orientation-reversing boundary homeomorphism that sends $[\partial D^2\times\{1\}]$ to that slope; the filled orientation agrees with the fixed orientation on $E_K$. If $q\ne0$, the coefficient is written $p/q$; the meridional slope is $1/0$, and integer $n$-framed filling means slope $n[\mu]+[\lambda]$. The oriented homeomorphism type is independent of the gluing map once its meridian image is fixed.

SUPPLIER def-pared-hyperbolic-structures-and-volume
A compact orientable three-manifold $M$ is **irreducible** if every embedded $2$-sphere in its interior bounds an embedded $3$-ball in $M$. A properly embedded surface is **incompressible** if it admits no embedded disk whose interior misses the surface and whose boundary is an essential simple closed curve in it. A boundary subsurface is **$\pi_1$-injective** if the inclusion of each component induces an injection on fundamental groups. A **pared pair** is a pair $(M,P)$ in which $M$ is compact, orientable and irreducible, and $P$ is a possibly empty compact subsurface of $\partial M$ whose pairwise disjoint components are $\pi_1$-injective annuli or tori, every noncyclic abelian subgroup of $\pi_1(M)$ is conjugate into $\pi_1$ of a component of $P$ up to base-path conjugacy, and every map of pairs
$$
(S^1\times I,S^1\times\partial I)\longrightarrow(M,P)
$$
that induces injections on fundamental groups is homotopic through maps of pairs into $P$, so its boundary remains in $P$ during the homotopy. Write $\partial_0M=\partial M\setminus\operatorname{int}P$. A **horoball** is a region bounded by a horosphere, a Euclidean plane or sphere tangent to the ideal boundary at one point. The **peripheral subgroup** for a component of $P$ is the image of its fundamental group in $\pi_1(M)$, recorded up to conjugacy. A **hyperbolic structure** on $(M,P)$ is a curvature $-1$ metric on $\operatorname{int}M$ described by an atlas of charts into $\mathbb H^3$ whose transition maps are in $\operatorname{Isom}^+(\mathbb H^3)$. Its developing map $\operatorname{dev}$ from the universal cover and holonomy homomorphism $\rho:\pi_1(M)\to\operatorname{Isom}^+(\mathbb H^3)$ satisfy $\operatorname{dev}(gx)=\rho(g)\operatorname{dev}(x)$. The metric is **complete** when it is geodesically complete. A **cusp** corresponding to a component of $P$ is a quotient of a horoball by the associated peripheral subgroup. The hyperbolic volume is the integral of the Riemannian volume density over $\operatorname{int}M$; the structure has **finite volume** when this integral is finite.

SUPPLIER def-ribbon-disk-and-ribbon-knot
Let $D^2$ be the oriented closed disk and let $K\subset S^3=\partial B^4$ be a smooth oriented knot, meaning the image of an orientation-preserving smooth embedding of a circle. A **ribbon immersion** for $K$ is a smooth immersion $f:D^2\to S^3$ whose boundary restriction is an embedding onto $K$ and whose only multiple points form finitely many pairwise disjoint transverse double arcs $\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_m$. For each $i$, the full preimage of $\gamma_i$ consists of two disjoint embedded arcs $\alpha_i$ and $\beta_i$ in $D^2$: $\alpha_i$ is properly embedded, with its two endpoints on $\partial D^2$ and its interior in $\operatorname{Int}D^2$, while $\beta_i$ is compact and lies in $\operatorname{Int}D^2$. There are no triple points or other self-intersections. A **ribbon disk** for $K$ is such a ribbon immersion, and $K$ is a **ribbon knot** if it admits a ribbon disk.

A **band** on a link in $S^3$ is the image of a smoothly embedded rectangle whose two opposite attaching edges lie on the link and whose remaining points are disjoint from it. A **band move** removes the attaching edges, inserts the other two edges, and rounds the corners; the embedded band, including its half-twist data, is part of the move. A **no-maximum disk movie** is the family of regular level links of a smooth proper disk $D\subset B^4$ for which the squared radial function $\rho(x)=\lVert x\rVert^2$ is Morse in the disk interior and has only index-$0$ minima and index-$1$ saddles. A minimum creates a split unknot and a saddle performs an oriented band move; there are no maxima. The no-maximum disk movie is a second presentation convention used by the design for explicit ribbon examples. This movie model and the ribbon-immersion definition above describe the same class of knots: a finite birth-and-band induction converts the movie to an immersion, and cutting the proper double-arc preimages converts an immersion to a disk-and-band movie. The local resolution lemma proves only that a ribbon immersion pushes rel boundary to a smooth proper disk in $B^4$. The slice-ribbon conjecture asks whether every smooth slice knot admits a ribbon immersion as defined above.

SUPPLIER lem-complete-curvature-minus-one-manifolds-are-kleinian-quotients
Assume AC. Every nonempty connected oriented complete Riemannian three-manifold of constant sectional curvature $-1$ is isometric to $\Gamma\backslash\mathbb H^3$ for a discrete torsion-free subgroup $\Gamma\le\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)$. The covering is a universal Riemannian covering and identifies $\Gamma$ with the fundamental group up to the usual basepoint conjugacies. Its completeness and quotient presentation are conclusions from the stated metric hypotheses. The orientation-preserving isometry group of the model is $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)$ with its usual Möbius boundary action.

SUPPLIER lem-finite-volume-hyperbolic-three-manifolds-have-finite-standard-cusp-decompositions
Assume AC. Let $M$ be a nonempty connected oriented complete Riemannian three-manifold of constant curvature $-1$ and finite volume. Then $M$ is a compact core together with finitely many pairwise disjoint standard torus cusp ends. Each end is an embedded quotient $\{y>R_i\}/\Lambda_i$ of a horoball by a rank-two translation lattice, and the complement of these open ends is compact. The number of cusps may be zero. In particular, the standard cusp decomposition in the general rigidity construction follows from the complete finite-volume metric, with no knot or triangulation assumption.

SUPPLIER lem-framed-knot-push-off-self-linking-well-defined
Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice. Let $K:S^1\hookrightarrow S^3$ be an
oriented smooth embedded circle and let $s$ be a smooth nowhere-zero section of
its normal bundle. For any tubular neighbourhood chart that is the inclusion
on the zero section and identifies each vertical normal fibre with the ambient
normal quotient by the identity, the curve $K_{\varepsilon s}$ obtained by
mapping the graph of $\varepsilon s$ into $S^3$ is an embedded oriented circle
disjoint from $K$ for every sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$. The integer
linking number of $K$ with $K_{\varepsilon s}$ is independent of the sufficiently
small radius, of the representative section in its homotopy class, and of the
normalized tubular chart. It therefore defines a self-linking integer of the
framing class $[s]$.

SUPPLIER lem-horocusp-truncation-has-finite-volume
Let $\Gamma\subset\mathbb C$ be a rank-two lattice acting on $\mathbb H^3$ by horizontal translations, and let
$$
C=\{(x+iy,t):t\ge1\}/\Gamma
$$
be the quotient of the horoball $t\ge1$. If $A$ is the Euclidean area of a fundamental parallelogram for $\Gamma$, then the end is at infinite hyperbolic distance and $\operatorname{Vol}(C)=A/2$.

SUPPLIER lem-ideal-tetrahedron-shape-parameter-relations
For an oriented ideal tetrahedron with normalized vertices $0,1,\infty,z$ and $\operatorname{Im}z>0$, its three edge-shape parameters are $z$, $z'=1/(1-z)$, and $z''=(z-1)/z$. They satisfy $zz'z''=-1$, and their arguments in $(0,\pi)$ are the three dihedral angles, whose sum is $\pi$. For $\omega=e^{i\pi/3}$, the choice $z=\omega$ gives $z=z'=z''=\omega$ and all six dihedral angles equal to $\pi/3$.

SUPPLIER lem-ideal-torus-cusp-holonomy-criterion
Let an orientable finite ideal triangulation have exactly one ideal vertex, whose link is a torus. Suppose its tetrahedra carry positive shapes whose edge products equal $1$ and whose dihedral angles sum to $2\pi$ around every edge. If the cusp multipliers $H(\alpha)$ and $H(\beta)$ are $1$ for a basis $\alpha,\beta$ of the torus fundamental group, then the glued hyperbolic metric is complete at that cusp.

SUPPLIER lem-knot-exterior-has-peripheral-torus

SUPPLIER lem-relative-transversality-for-embedded-surfaces

SUPPLIER lem-seifert-bands-embedded-orientable-boundary-correct

SUPPLIER lem-seifert-matrices-of-a-knot-are-s-equivalent
Assume full AC. Let $K$ be a smooth oriented knot in the oriented standard $3$-sphere, and let $F,F'$ be any connected compact smooth oriented spanning surfaces for $K$. For any ordered integral bases of $H_1(F;\mathbb Z)$ and $H_1(F';\mathbb Z)$, the matrices of the positive-push Seifert pairing are related by a finite sequence of integral unimodular congruences and the two rank-two elementary Seifert enlargements or their reverses.

SUPPLIER lem-seifert-matrix-s-equivalence-under-isotopy
Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $(K,F)$ and $(K',F')$ be oriented knot and spanning-surface pairs in the oriented standard three-sphere, and let $h_t:S^3\to S^3$ be a smooth ambient isotopy with $h_0$ the identity, $h_1(K)=K'$, and $h_1(F)=F'$, preserving the given orientations. If $V$ and $V'$ are their Seifert matrices in any integral bases, then the basis transported by $h_1$ has matrix $V$, and any other target basis with transition matrix $P$ has matrix $V'=P^TVP$. Hence $V$ and $V'$ are S-equivalent, meaning they are related by a finite sequence of integral unimodular congruences and the two elementary rank-two enlargement moves or their reverses.

SUPPLIER lem-smooth-jordan-disk-boundary-maps-extend-to-diffeomorphisms
Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice. The bounded region of a smooth embedded
circle in the oriented plane, together with its boundary, is a smooth manifold
with boundary diffeomorphic to the closed standard disk. Let $D_0,D_1$ be two
such closed regions. Every orientation-preserving smooth boundary
diffeomorphism $b:\partial D_0\to\partial D_1$ extends to an
orientation-preserving diffeomorphism $B:D_0\to D_1$. More precisely, for
specified smooth inward collars $c_i:\partial D_i\times[0,\epsilon)\to D_i$
with $c_i(p,0)=p$, the extension may be required to satisfy
$B(c_0(p,r))=c_1(b(p),r)$ for every sufficiently small $r\ge0$. In addition, given interior points $p_i\in D_i$ and an orientation-preserving smooth diffeomorphism $q$ from a neighborhood of $p_0$ onto a neighborhood of $p_1$, the extension can agree with $q$ near $p_0$ while retaining the prescribed boundary collars.

Assume the full Axiom of Choice. $$\pi_1(E_K)=\langle x,y\mid x^p=y^q\rangle.$$

The fibration has two distinguished core fibers of multiplicities $|p|$ and $|q|$; a fiber of multiplicity one is regular, and a fiber of multiplicity greater than one is exceptional. Thus there are exactly two exceptional fibers when $|p|,|q|>1$, and one is regular when one absolute parameter is $1$.

$$\mu=y^\alpha x^\beta,\qquad \lambda=h\mu^{-pq}.$$

Here the positive meridian is the oriented boundary of a normal disk, and the preferred longitude has degree one around $K$ and maps to zero in $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)$. Their homology classes form a basis of $H_1(\partial E_K;\mathbb Z)$, so both classes are primitive.

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $E_K$ be the compact connected orientable exterior of a smooth knot in $S^3$, with one torus boundary component. No conclusion is asserted for a torus or satellite knot unless its exterior satisfies all four topological hypotheses.
