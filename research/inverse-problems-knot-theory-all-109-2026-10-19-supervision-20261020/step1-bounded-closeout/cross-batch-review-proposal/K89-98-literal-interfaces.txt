SUPPLIER alg-khovanov-chain-complex-from-diagram
**Input:** a nonempty finite regular oriented link diagram $D$ and a total order $c_1<\cdots<c_n$ on its crossing set. A diagram object is supplied as mathematical input; this procedure does not parse a serialized string.

**Output:** the normalized bigraded Khovanov cochain module $C^{i,j}(D)$ and its candidate differential $d:C^{i,j}(D)\to C^{i+1,j}(D)$, constructed by the following finite instructions.

1. Enumerate every state $v:C(D)\to\{0,1\}$ once. Form the crossingless resolution by using the A-smoothing at $v(c)=0$ and the B-smoothing at $v(c)=1$.
2. For each state, create one tensor factor of the integral Frobenius algebra for each resolution circle and place the resulting tensor product in cohomological degree $|v|-n_-$ and quantum degree $|v|+n_+-2n_-+\sum\deg(a)$ for its circle labels $a$.
3. For each state and each crossing $c_j$ with $v(c_j)=0$, create the edge to $v+e_j$. Apply multiplication if that smoothing change merges two circles, comultiplication if it splits one circle, the identity on other tensor factors, and the sign $(-1)^{\sum_{i<j}v(c_i)}$. Sum the edge maps out of every state to obtain $d$.
4. Return the finite direct sum of the state modules with these degrees and maps. The square-zero property is the separate local lemma named by `justified_by`.

SUPPLIER def-colored-framed-knot-evaluation-from-ribbon-category
Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice. Let $\mathcal C$ be a ribbon category with chosen left duals and tensor unit $\mathbf 1$. A **finite oriented framed link colored in $\mathcal C$** is a finite disjoint union $L$ of oriented smoothly embedded circles in $S^3$, a homotopy class of nowhere-zero normal vector field on each component, and an assignment of an object of $\mathcal C$ to each component. A framed colored isotopy carries orientations, framings, and component colors. If $F_{\mathcal C}$ is the ribbon-tangle evaluation functor, define
$$\operatorname{RT}_{\mathcal C}(L):=F_{\mathcal C}(L)\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal C}(\mathbf 1).$$
This value depends only on the framed colored isotopy class. It is not automatically a field scalar; if $\mathcal C$ is $k$-linear and $\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal C}(\mathbf 1)=k$, it is a scalar in $k$. The empty link is assigned $1_{\mathbf 1}$.

SUPPLIER def-coprime-torus-knot
Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice. Identify the standard oriented three-sphere with $S^3=\{(z_1,z_2)\in\mathbb C^2:|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2=1\}$ and choose $0<r<1$. The standard unknotted Heegaard torus is $T_r=\{|z_1|=r,\ |z_2|=\sqrt{1-r^2}\}$; its two sides are solid tori. In the angular coordinates $(u,v)\in(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)^2$ given by $(u,v)\mapsto(r e^{2\pi i u},\sqrt{1-r^2}e^{2\pi i v})$, let $\alpha$ and $\beta$ be the first and second coordinate classes in $H_1(T_r;\mathbb Z)$. For coprime nonzero integers $p,q$, the **oriented torus knot** $T(p,q)$ is the image of $s\mapsto(r e^{2\pi i p s},\sqrt{1-r^2}e^{2\pi i q s})$, for $s\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$, oriented by increasing $s$; it represents the primitive slope $p\alpha+q\beta$. The mirror convention is the reflection $(z_1,z_2)\mapsto(z_1,\overline{z_2})$, which sends $T(p,q)$ to $T(p,-q)$ with image orientation, and reversing the knot orientation gives $T(-p,-q)$. For nonzero $p,q$ with $d=\gcd(|p|,|q|)>1$, the corresponding torus link is the union of $d$ disjoint parallel copies of the primitive slope $(p/d,q/d)$; it is not a single embedded curve of class $p\alpha+q\beta$. The exterior is $E_K=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}N(K)$ for a chosen closed tubular neighborhood. A positive meridian is the oriented boundary of a normal disk, and a preferred longitude is a degree-one class on $\partial E_K$ whose image in $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)$ is zero; this definition alone asserts neither existence nor uniqueness.

SUPPLIER def-finite-volume-hyperbolic-knot-exterior
For a smooth knot $K$ with chosen compact exterior $E_K$ and boundary torus $T_K$, a finite-volume hyperbolic knot exterior structure is a complete Riemannian metric of constant sectional curvature $-1$ on $\operatorname{int}(E_K)$ with finite volume and exactly one cusp whose torus cross-section is isotopic in $\operatorname{int}(E_K)$ to a parallel copy of $T_K$. The definition asserts no existence.

SUPPLIER def-generic-movie-of-an-oriented-knot-cobordism
Assume countable choice. Let $K_0,K_1\subset S^3$ be smooth oriented knots. A smooth oriented knot cobordism from $K_0$ to $K_1$ is a compact smooth properly embedded oriented surface $\Sigma\subset S^3\times[0,1]$ whose boundary is $(-K_0\times\{0\})\sqcup(K_1\times\{1\})$, with product collars at both ends. A **generic movie** for $\Sigma$ is obtained by choosing a generic projection of the slices $S^3\times\{t\}$ to the plane and a Morse height function on $\Sigma$, then recording the oriented link diagram at each regular level. Intermediate regular levels may be links with any finite number of components. After choosing the projection and height generically, the finitely many exceptional levels are isolated and distinct: between them the diagrams change by planar isotopy or one oriented Reidemeister move, and at them the surface height has one nondegenerate minimum (a birth), one nondegenerate maximum (a death), or one index-one critical point (a saddle band move). A movie includes its ordered list of frames and these elementary changes; no claim that two movies of the same cobordism are equivalent is part of this definition.

SUPPLIER def-kauffman-bracket-smoothing-and-loop-value
Let $A$ be an indeterminate and let $\mathbb Z[A^{\pm1}]$ be the Laurent
polynomial ring in $A$. A **nonempty unoriented regular link diagram** is the
finite planar projection and over/under data of a regular oriented link diagram
after its strand arrows are forgotten. Its finite crossing set is denoted
$C(D)$, and at least one closed component is required. The empty link diagram is
outside this definition.

At a crossing use the following local chart: the over-strand runs from the
southwest endpoint to the northeast endpoint and is drawn continuously over the
under-strand, which runs from northwest to southeast with a gap at the
intersection. Horizontal-right followed by vertical-up is the positive
orientation of this displayed chart. In this chart the **A-smoothing** joins the
two left endpoints and joins the two right endpoints; the **B-smoothing** joins
the two top endpoints and joins the two bottom endpoints. These labels are
attached to this displayed chart and are transported by orientation-preserving
changes of local coordinates. Thus turning right from an entering underpassing
half-strand gives the A-smoothing.

```tikz
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.72, line cap=round, line join=round]
  \begin{scope}
    \draw[thick] (-1,1)--(-0.14,0.14);
    \draw[thick] (0.14,-0.14)--(1,-1);
    \draw[very thick] (-1,-1)--(1,1);
    \node at (0,-1.45) {crossing};
  \end{scope}
  \begin{scope}[xshift=3.4cm]
    \draw[thick] (-1,1) .. controls (-0.45,0.35) and (-0.45,-0.35) .. (-1,-1);
    \draw[thick] (1,1) .. controls (0.45,0.35) and (0.45,-0.35) .. (1,-1);
    \node at (0,-1.45) {$A$-smoothing, weight $A$};
  \end{scope}
  \begin{scope}[xshift=6.8cm]
    \draw[thick] (-1,1) .. controls (-0.35,0.45) and (0.35,0.45) .. (1,1);
    \draw[thick] (-1,-1) .. controls (-0.35,-0.45) and (0.35,-0.45) .. (1,-1);
    \node at (0,-1.45) {$B$-smoothing, weight $A^{-1}$};
  \end{scope}
\end{tikzpicture}
```

For a state $S:C(D)\to\{A,B\}$, let $a(S)$ and $b(S)$ be the numbers of
A- and B-smoothings, and let $|S|$ be the number of closed circles in the
crossingless diagram obtained by performing all smoothings. Set
$$\delta:=-A^2-A^{-2}$$
and define the **Kauffman bracket** of $D$ by
$$\langle D\rangle:=\sum_{S:C(D)\to\{A,B\}} A^{a(S)-b(S)}\delta^{|S|-1}\in\mathbb Z[A^{\pm1}].$$
Because $D$ is nonempty, every complete smoothing has $|S|\ge1$, so the loop
exponent is nonnegative. A crossingless $k$-component unlink with $k\ge1$ has
bracket $\delta^{k-1}$.

SUPPLIER def-khovanov-frobenius-algebra-and-cube-of-resolutions
Let $D$ be a nonempty finite regular oriented link diagram in an oriented $3$-sphere, with finite crossing set $C(D)$. Fix a unit viewing normal $n$ and orient the projection plane $P$ so $(u,v)$ is positive exactly when $(n,u,v)$ is positive in the ambient $3$-space. At a crossing $c$, define its sign as the sign of $\det_P(v_{\rm over},v_{\rm under})$, where the two nonzero projected tangent vectors follow the oriented over- and under-strands. Let $n_+$ and $n_-$ be the numbers of positive and negative crossings.

Set $\mathcal A=\mathbb Z[X]/(X^2)$, with $\deg(1)=1$ and $\deg(X)=-1$. Its unit, multiplication, counit, and comultiplication are
$$
\eta(1)=1,\qquad m(1\otimes1)=1,\quad m(1\otimes X)=m(X\otimes1)=X,\quad m(X\otimes X)=0,
$$
$$
\epsilon(1)=0,\quad\epsilon(X)=1,\qquad \Delta(1)=1\otimes X+X\otimes1,\quad\Delta(X)=X\otimes X.
$$
Thus $m$ and $\Delta$ have quantum degree $-1$, while $\eta$ and $\epsilon$ have degree $+1$.

Choose a total order $c_1<\cdots<c_n$ on $C(D)$. A resolution state is a function $v:C(D)\to\{0,1\}$; at $v(c)=0$ use the A-smoothing and at $v(c)=1$ use the B-smoothing from the Kauffman source chart. Write $|v|=\sum_c v(c)$, let $D_v$ be the crossingless resolution, and let $k(v)$ be its number of circles. Assign the state module $\mathcal A^{\otimes k(v)}$. For an edge $v\to v+e_j$ changing crossing $c_j$ from $0$ to $1$, use $m$ if the local smoothing merges two circles and $\Delta$ if it splits one circle, tensored with the identity on all other factors, and multiply this map by $(-1)^{\sum_{i<j}v(c_i)}$. The resulting direct sum has cohomological degree
$$i(v)=|v|-n_-$$
and the generator with circle labels $1$ or $X$ has quantum degree
$$j(v)=|v|+n_+-2n_-+\sum_{\text{circle labels }a}\deg(a).$$
This defines the normalized integral Khovanov cube and its candidate degree-zero cochain differential; the assertion that the differential squares to zero is a separate lemma.

The Frobenius identity that controls a merge-split face is represented by the two equal composites below. The diagram records the maps only; the proof verifies the equality on the basis $\{1,X\}^{\otimes2}$ and does not rely on the drawing.

```tikzcd
\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal A \arrow[r,"m"] \arrow[d,"\Delta\otimes\mathrm{id}"'] & \mathcal A \arrow[d,"\Delta"] \\
\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal A \arrow[r,"\mathrm{id}\otimes m"'] & \mathcal A\otimes\mathcal A
```

SUPPLIER def-knot-exterior
Assume countable choice, as required for the smooth normal-bundle structure in the tubular-neighbourhood interface. Let $K:S^1\to S^3$ be a smooth oriented knot, let $\nu(K)=TS^3|_K/TK$ be its quotient normal bundle, and let $\Phi:\Omega\to S^3$ be a tubular-neighbourhood map from an open neighbourhood $\Omega$ of the zero section in $\nu(K)$ that restricts to the inclusion of $K$. Choose a radius $\epsilon>0$ whose closed disk subbundle $D_\epsilon\nu(K)$ is contained in $\Omega$, and set $N(K)=\Phi(D_\epsilon\nu(K))$. The **exterior** of this chosen tube is
$$
E_K:=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}_{S^3}N(K).
$$
Retain its boundary $T_K:=\partial E_K=\partial N(K)$, orient $E_K$ by restriction of the fixed orientation of $S^3$, and orient $T_K$ as the boundary of $E_K$. The choice of tubular map and radius is part of the input to this definition.

SUPPLIER def-knot-floer-filtration-and-tau
Let $K$ be a smooth oriented knot in the standard oriented three-sphere. Let $F$ be a compact connected oriented Seifert surface with boundary $K$, and let $\mathcal H=(\Sigma,\boldsymbol\alpha,\boldsymbol\beta,w,z)$ be an admissible doubly pointed Heegaard diagram representing $(K,F)$. The associated one-pointed hat chain complex $\widehat{CF}(\mathcal H)$ is the finite-dimensional chain complex over $\mathbb F_2$ obtained from the minus complex by setting its basepoint variable to zero; its differential counts index-one disks with zero multiplicity at $w$. Assume for this specified data that the differential is nonincreasing in the Alexander filtration, that the absolute Alexander formula below takes integer values, and that $H(\widehat{CF}(\mathcal H))\cong\mathbb F_2$. Give a generator $\mathbf x$ its absolute Alexander filtration
$$A_F(\mathbf x)=\frac12\left\langle c_1(\mathfrak s_{w,z}(\mathbf x)),[\widehat F]\right\rangle,$$
where $\mathfrak s_{w,z}(\mathbf x)$ is the homology class of the nowhere-vanishing vector field assigned to $\mathbf x$ by the pointed Morse construction, with its prescribed boundary data near the two basepoints; $c_1$ is the first Chern class of the oriented plane field perpendicular to that vector field. To form the zero-framed surgery, remove a tubular neighborhood of $K$ and glue in a solid torus whose meridian is the longitude induced by $F$. The surface $\widehat F$ is obtained by deleting a boundary collar of $F$ and capping its longitude by the meridian disk of that solid torus. This is the standard integer normalization, with the one-generator diagram of the standard unknot at filtration zero. The subcomplex $F_s\widehat{CF}(\mathcal H)$ is spanned by generators of Alexander filtration at most $s$.

More generally, a finite integer-filtered chain complex is a finite-dimensional chain complex $C$ over $\mathbb F_2$ with subcomplexes $F_sC$ such that $F_sC=0$ for all sufficiently small integers $s$ and $F_sC=C$ for all sufficiently large integers $s$. If $H(C)\cong\mathbb F_2$, define
$$\tau(C):=\min\{s\in\mathbb Z:\operatorname{im}(H(F_sC)\longrightarrow H(C))\ne0\}.$$
For the fixed diagram and surface above, write $\tau_{\mathcal H,F}(K)=\tau(\widehat{CF}(\mathcal H),F_*\widehat{CF}(\mathcal H))$. Once diagram and surface independence is proved, their common value is the knot invariant $\tau(K)$. Until that comparison is proved, $\tau_{\mathcal H,F}(K)$ denotes only the value for the specified data.

SUPPLIER def-knot-meridian-and-preferred-longitude
Assume countable choice as in the chosen tubular-neighbourhood data. Let $K$ be a smooth oriented knot with chosen exterior $E_K$ and boundary torus $T_K=\partial E_K$, and let $p:T_K\to K$ be the projection of the chosen normal-disk tube. Orient each normal disk so that its positive tangent direction followed by its oriented normal basis agrees with the orientation of $S^3$. A **meridian** $\mu$ is the oriented boundary of one such normal disk. A **degree-one parallel curve** is a simple closed curve $\lambda$ in $T_K$ for which $p|_\lambda:\lambda\to K$ has degree $+1$. A **preferred longitude** is a degree-one parallel curve satisfying $i_*[\lambda]=0$ in $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)$, where $i:T_K\hookrightarrow E_K$ is inclusion. This is a condition on a curve; this definition alone asserts neither existence nor uniqueness. Reversing the orientation of $K$ reverses the orientations of both $\mu$ and any degree-one parallel curve. If a subgroup $P\le\pi_1(E_K,x)$ is specified using a path from $x$ to $T_K$, record it only up to replacing it by $gPg^{-1}$ for $g\in\pi_1(E_K,x)$.

SUPPLIER def-locally-flat-proper-surface-and-concordance-collars
Let $X$ be a four-dimensional manifold with boundary and let $W$ be a compact surface with boundary. An embedding $e:(W,\partial W)\to(X,\partial X)$ is proper when $e^{-1}(\partial X)=\partial W$. It is locally flat at an interior point of $W$ when there are neighborhoods of the point and its image and a homeomorphism of pairs from those neighborhoods to $(\mathbb R^4,\mathbb R^2)$, with the standard linear plane $\mathbb R^2\subset\mathbb R^4$. It is locally flat at a boundary point when there are such neighborhoods and a homeomorphism of pairs to $(\mathbb R^4_+,\mathbb R^2_+)$, where $\mathbb R^4_+=\{x_4\geq0\}$ and $\mathbb R^2_+=\{x_2=x_3=0,\ x_4\geq0\}$; the line $\partial\mathbb R^2_+$ lies in $\partial\mathbb R^4_+$. A collar of a boundary circle of $W$ is an embedding of that circle times $[0,\varepsilon)$ into $W$ that is the identity on the circle at parameter $0$; a concordance collar is required to agree with the corresponding product collar of the ambient end. A smooth proper surface has the analogous smooth charts and collars, which are also topological locally flat charts.

SUPPLIER def-quantum-sl2-fundamental-ribbon-module
Let $\omega$ be an indeterminate, set $A=\omega^{-2}$, and put $k'=\mathbb Q(\omega)$, $q_K=A^2$, and $q_L=A^{-2}$. Work over the scalar extension to $k'$ of the library Hopf algebra at $q_L$; $U_{q_K}$ in Korinman's convention is identified by $q_L=q_K^{-1}$ and $K_L=K_K^{-1}$. Use its generic two-dimensional module $V$ over $k'$ with ordered basis $(v_+,v_-)$ and generator matrices
$$E=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad F=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad K_K=\begin{pmatrix}q_K&0\\0&q_K^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.$$
Equivalently, for the library parameter $q_L=q_K^{-1}$ use $K_L=K_K^{-1}=\operatorname{diag}(q_L,q_L^{-1})$ and the library coproduct convention $\Delta(E)=E\otimes K_L^{-1}+1\otimes E$, $\Delta(F)=F\otimes1+K_L\otimes F$. This parameter and Cartan-generator conversion is part of the definition; the two $q$ symbols are not identified.

The object $V$ is absolutely simple in the $k'$-linear representation category.

Equip $V$ with the Kauffman-bracket half-ribbon structure. The half-ribbon operator $\Omega$ is specified by
$$C:=\operatorname{Mat}_{(v_+,v_-)}(\Omega^{-1})=\begin{pmatrix}0&\omega\\-\omega^5&0\end{pmatrix},$$
and its associated twist is $\theta_V=\Omega^2=-A^3\operatorname{id}_V$. In the ordered basis $(v_+\otimes v_+,v_+\otimes v_-,v_-\otimes v_+,v_-\otimes v_-)$, the positive braiding is
$$c_{V,V}=\begin{pmatrix}A&0&0&0\\0&0&A^{-1}&0\\0&A^{-1}&A-A^{-3}&0\\0&0&0&A\end{pmatrix}.$$
Its ribbon quantum dimension is $\delta=-A^2-A^{-2}$. The half-ribbon and universal $R$-matrix are those of the topological half-ribbon representation category; the selected ribbon element is the Kauffman-bracket one, not the usual $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ ribbon element.

Let $v_+^*,v_-^*$ be the ordinary dual basis. The left-dual action on $V^*$ is the contragredient action $(x\cdot f)(v)=f(S(x)v)$ for the antipode $S$ of the stated library Hopf convention. Identify the displayed copy of $V$ with this left dual $V^\vee$ by $v_+\mapsto v_-^*$ and $v_-\mapsto -A^{-2}v_+^*$. In this identification the evaluation and coevaluation maps are
$$B(v_+\otimes v_-)=1,\quad B(v_-\otimes v_+)=-A^{-2},\quad B(v_+\otimes v_+)=B(v_-\otimes v_-)=0,$$
$$U(1)=-A^2v_+\otimes v_-+v_-\otimes v_+.$$

SUPPLIER def-reduced-khovanov-cohomology-of-a-knot
Let $(D,p)$ be a finite regular oriented diagram of a knot with a basepoint $p$ away from the crossings, and let $C(D)$ be its normalized integral Khovanov cochain complex. At each resolution state, let $X_p$ act by multiplication by $X$ on the tensor factor for the circle containing $p$, and by the identity on every other circle factor. The basepoint action is the direct sum of these statewise maps. Define the reduced cochain complex by
$$\widetilde C(D,p):=C(D)/\operatorname{im}(X_p),$$
with the same cohomological grading and with reduced quantum degree $\widetilde j=j-1$ for the class represented by a state whose basepoint circle is labelled $1$. Its cohomology is the reduced integral Khovanov cohomology $\widetilde{Kh}^{i,\widetilde j}(D,p)$. The unknot is normalized so that its reduced group lies in bidegree $(0,0)$.

Equivalently, cut the based knot at $p$ to a one-interval tangle. Put $M=\mathbb Z$ in quantum degree $0$ with $X\cdot1=0$. A resolution with $k$ closed circles and the distinguished interval is assigned $\mathcal A^{\otimes k}\otimes M$. An edge that merges a circle into the interval uses $m_M(1\otimes1)=1$ and $m_M(X\otimes1)=0$; an edge that splits the interval uses $\Delta_M(1)=X\otimes1$, where the circle factor is written first and the interval factor second. Edges not meeting the interval use $m$ or $\Delta$. This based tangle cube is the quotient complex above, with the stated quantum shift.

SUPPLIER lem-khovanov-cube-differential-squares-to-zero
Let $D$ be a finite regular oriented link diagram, order its crossings as $c_1<\cdots<c_n$, and form the direct sum of state modules $\mathcal A^{\otimes k(v)}$ over all functions $v:C(D)\to\{0,1\}$. Here $\mathcal A=\mathbb Z[X]/(X^2)$, each crossing is changed from its A-smoothing at $0$ to its B-smoothing at $1$, and the edge map is the multiplication $m$ for a merge or comultiplication $\Delta$ for a split, multiplied by $(-1)^{\sum_{i<j}v(c_i)}$ on the edge at $c_j$. Then the sum $d$ of all edge maps satisfies $d^2=0$.

SUPPLIER lem-lee-cobordism-filtration-genus-bound
Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice. Let $S\subset S^3\times[0,1]$ be a connected compact oriented smoothly properly embedded surface of genus $g$, with product collars and oriented boundary $(-K_0\times\{0\})\sqcup(K_1\times\{1\})$ for oriented knots $K_0,K_1$ in the standard three-sphere. A **finite movie** for $S$ is a finite ordered sequence of regular-level link diagrams whose changes are planar isotopies, oriented Reidemeister moves, births, saddles or deaths. Such a movie defines a map on rational Lee homology that preserves the induced decreasing filtration with degree $\chi(S)=-2g$. The map is an isomorphism; consequently
$$
|s(K_1)-s(K_0)|\le 2g.
$$

SUPPLIER lem-lee-filtered-spectral-sequence-and-two-orientation-classes
Let $D$ be a finite regular oriented diagram of a link with $c$ components, and form its rational Lee cochain complex using the normalized crossing cube and decreasing quantum filtration. The associated bounded spectral sequence has the rational Khovanov cohomology of $D$ on its homology page and converges to the associated graded of Lee homology with its induced filtration. For each of the $2^c$ orientations $o$ of the link, the oriented resolution and the parity-of-nesting label rule define a canonical Lee cycle $s_o$; these cycles represent a basis of Lee homology. In that rule, a Seifert circle contributes $1$ when counterclockwise and $0$ when clockwise. For a knot, the two canonical classes $s_o,s_{\bar o}$ have equal least filtration level, while $s_o+s_{\bar o}$ and $s_o-s_{\bar o}$ are nonzero classes in the two distinct quantum-degree congruence summands modulo $4$.

SUPPLIER lem-meridian-preserving-torus-homeomorphism-extends-over-solid-torus
Let $V=D^2\times S^1$ and $V'=D^2\times S^1$ be solid tori, with meridian curves $\mu=\partial D^2\times\{1\}$ and $\mu'=\partial D^2\times\{1\}$. Suppose $h:\partial V\to\partial V'$ is a homeomorphism and there is an isotopy $H_t:\partial V'\to\partial V'$ through homeomorphisms, with $H_0=\mathrm{id}$, such that $H_1(h(\mu))=\mu'$. SUPPLIER lem-puncturing-smooth-four-ball-surfaces-gives-genus-preserving-knot-cobordisms
Assume countable choice. Let $F\subset B^4\subset\mathbb R^4$ be a compact connected oriented smoothly properly embedded surface of genus $g$, with its supplied smooth boundary collar and oriented boundary the knot $K\subset S^3$. There is an orientation-preserving ambient diffeomorphism $\Psi:B^4\to B^4$ equal to the identity on a neighborhood of the outer boundary, and $0<\varepsilon<1$, such that $\Psi(F)\cap B^4_\varepsilon$ is exactly a flat two-disk in a plane through $0$. The ambient diffeomorphism $\Psi$ fixes a sufficiently small restriction of the supplied outer collar; the shell coordinates may be adjusted there to make that surface collar a product. No assertion about the complement of an arbitrary embedded four-ball is used.

SUPPLIER lem-quantum-sl2-ribbon-trace-matches-hecke-and-bracket
