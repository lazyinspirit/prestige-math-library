SUPPLIER alg-seifert-surface-from-diagram
Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice. Let $D$ be a finite regular oriented link diagram with exactly one component, and let $c$ be its number of crossings. Perform the following construction in its projection sphere $S^2$.

1. Choose a point $q\in S^2$ outside the finite diagram. In pairwise disjoint small disks about the crossings, replace each crossing by the unique noncrossing smoothing compatible with the strand orientations. Let the resulting oriented circles be the Seifert circles and let $s$ be their number.
2. For each Seifert circle $C$, take the closure of the component of $S^2\setminus C$ that does not contain $q$. Under the stated choice axiom, each such region is an embedded compact disk. The finite family of disk regions is nested or disjoint. Orient each disk to induce the given orientation of its boundary circle, then place nested disks at distinct nearby heights ordered by inclusion; the resulting disk interiors are pairwise disjoint in $S^3$.
3. For every original crossing, attach one rectangular band along short boundary intervals on the two smoothing arcs. A half-twist means a single $180$-degree turn of the band across the crossing neighborhood. Choose its direction, endpoint orientations, and over/under order to match the original crossing.
4. Return the finite disk-and-band construction data together with $(s,c)$.

The returned data are a finite attached two-dimensional complex. This algorithm's definition alone does not assert that the resulting image is embedded, is a surface, is connected, or has the original knot as boundary; the separate correctness lemma establishes those properties.

SUPPLIER def-admissible-pointed-heegaard-floer-data
Work in the smooth category inherited from the oriented-link convention. In particular, an embedded bridge witness disk means the image of a smooth rank-two embedding of a planar disk, with ordinary smooth boundary or smooth transverse corners at its two marked endpoints; smoothness at a boundary or corner means local smooth extension in its charts. Witness parametrizations can be chosen for the finite disk families. This category convention does not require the knot arcs to meet the Heegaard surface transversely: tangential smooth crossings remain permitted.

Let $K\subset S^3$ be a smooth oriented knot. Let $\Sigma$ be a closed oriented surface of genus $h$, let $k\ge1$, and put $m=h+k-1$. A **handlebody** is a compact three-manifold that is a regular neighborhood of a finite connected graph. A **$2k$-pointed Heegaard diagram for $S^3$** consists of a splitting $S^3=U_{\alpha}\cup_{\Sigma}U_{\beta}$ into handlebodies, two systems $\boldsymbol\alpha=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_m)$ and $\boldsymbol\beta=(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_m)$ of pairwise disjoint simple closed curves on $\Sigma$ that bound pairwise disjoint properly embedded disks in $U_{\alpha}$ and $U_{\beta}$, respectively, cutting each handlebody into exactly $k$ three-balls, and two ordered sets of distinct points $\mathbf w=(w_1,\ldots,w_k)$ and $\mathbf z=(z_1,\ldots,z_k)$ disjoint from the curves. Curves from different systems intersect transversely in finitely many points. Each component of $\Sigma\setminus\boldsymbol\alpha$ and of $\Sigma\setminus\boldsymbol\beta$ contains exactly one $w$-point and exactly one $z$-point. The diagram also includes trivial arc systems in the two handlebodies: the $\alpha$-arcs join $w_i$ to $z_i$, and the $\beta$-arcs join $z_i$ to $w_{i-1}$, with indices modulo $k$. Each system is disjoint from its compression disks and is simultaneously boundary parallel: its arcs cobound pairwise disjoint embedded disks with arcs in the corresponding components of the attaching-system complement on $\Sigma$. Thus each ball obtained by compression contains one unknotted arc. Together the arcs form an oriented knot, oriented from $w_i$ to $z_i$ in $U_{\alpha}$ and from $z_i$ to $w_{i-1}$ in $U_{\beta}$. The diagram is **compatible with $K$** when this knot is orientation-preservingly ambient isotopic to $K$. For a grid diagram, compatibility uses the row and column orientation fixed in the grid definition, with $w$-points at $O$ and $z$-points at $X$. For a grid representing one knot, the basepoint pairs can be numbered cyclically in reverse order of travel along the oriented knot so that $w_i,z_i$ lie in the same component $A_i$ of $\Sigma\setminus\boldsymbol\alpha$ and $w_i,z_{i+1}$ lie in the same component $B_i$ of $\Sigma\setminus\boldsymbol\beta$.

Comparisons of these embedded diagrams allow **orientation-preserving diagram isomorphisms**: an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism of $S^3$ carrying the two handlebodies, their oriented Heegaard surface, the two attaching systems, the ordered $w,z$ points, and the two simultaneously boundary-parallel arc systems to the corresponding data. Equivalently one may specify the surface isomorphism together with its compatible collared extensions over both handlebodies carrying those arcs. The attaching systems are carried as systems; reordering their curve factors induces the corresponding product identification. Comparisons also allow **arc normalizations in the fixed splitting**: a smooth isotopy of the represented oriented knot, supported near the ordered marks and fixing each mark, during which every open connecting arc stays in its original handlebody and the two simultaneous boundary-parallel witness systems are retained or replaced by embedded witnesses for those same arcs. The surface, attaching curves, marked points and compression disks stay fixed. Such a normalization may replace a tangential marked crossing by a transverse one; the original data are not required to have neat arcs. This is a change of knot representative relative to the fixed surface, not a diagram isomorphism carrying both objects. Analytic and assigned grading data depending on the fixed surface diagram can be kept identical; the corresponding generator and disk-count identification is then the identity. Existence of normalizations with the required witnesses is a separate geometric assertion, not part of this definition. Such a comparison transports the represented oriented knot and any specified Seifert surface as well. For analytic data, it transports $j$, the surface area form, all chosen divisor neighborhoods and $J_t$ by the induced symmetric-product diffeomorphism. The comparison maps on generators and disk classes are their pushforwards under this map; compatibility with the count differential is a mathematical property to be proved. No identity action is part of this convention, even when the ambient diffeomorphism is isotopic to the identity.

The $m$-fold symmetric product $\operatorname{Sym}^{m}(\Sigma)=\Sigma^m/S_m$ is the space of unordered $m$-tuples of points of $\Sigma$, where $S_m$ permutes coordinates. Let $q:\Sigma^m\to\operatorname{Sym}^{m}(\Sigma)$ be the quotient map. The product tori are the images $\mathbb T_{\alpha}=q(\alpha_1\times\cdots\times\alpha_m)$ and $\mathbb T_{\beta}=q(\beta_1\times\cdots\times\beta_m)$. Their intersection points are the generators. A **Whitney disk class** $\phi\in\pi_2(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)$ is a relative homotopy class of maps from a disk to $\operatorname{Sym}^{m}(\Sigma)$ whose two boundary arcs lie on $\mathbb T_{\alpha}$ and $\mathbb T_{\beta}$ and whose endpoints are $\mathbf x$ and $\mathbf y$. Its domain is the integral two-chain of complementary regions with coefficients equal to local multiplicities. Write $n_{w_i}(\phi)$ and $n_{z_i}(\phi)$ for its local multiplicities at the marked points. An almost complex structure $J$ on $\operatorname{Sym}^{m}(\Sigma)$ is a smooth endomorphism of the tangent bundle with $J^2=-1$. Fix a complex structure $j$ and an area form on $\Sigma$. For $m>0$, choose a smooth path $\mathbb J=(J_t)_{t\in[0,1]}$ of nearly symmetric almost complex structures, constant near $t=0,1$: each $J_t$ agrees with the induced integrable structure $\operatorname{Sym}^{m}(j)$ on a fixed neighborhood of the diagonal and of every divisor $\{p\}+\operatorname{Sym}^{m-1}(\Sigma)$ for a chosen interior point $p$ of every complementary region, including every marked point, and tames the form descended from the product area form off that neighborhood. The path is a sufficiently small perturbation in this class. For $m=0$ the symmetric product is a point. Local multiplicities are intersection numbers with these divisors; positivity is a mathematical property to be proved, not an additional choice of disk counts. A $\mathbb J$-holomorphic strip representative is a smooth map $u:\mathbb R\times[0,1]\to\operatorname{Sym}^{m}(\Sigma)$ satisfying $\partial_{\tau}u+J_t(u)\partial_tu=0$, with $u(\tau,0)\in\mathbb T_{\alpha}$, $u(\tau,1)\in\mathbb T_{\beta}$, and limits $\mathbf x,\mathbf y$ as $\tau\to-\infty,+\infty$. Translation in $\tau$ defines an $\mathbb R$-action. The Maslov index $\mu(\phi)$ is the Fredholm index of the linearized Cauchy–Riemann operator at a representative of $\phi$; the relative Maslov grading convention is $M(\mathbf x)-M(\mathbf y)=\mu(\phi)-2\sum_{i=1}^{k}n_{w_i}(\phi)$, up to one overall additive constant.

A **periodic domain** is an integral two-chain in the complementary regions whose boundary is an integral sum of full $\alpha$- and $\beta$-curves; its local multiplicity at a marked point is the coefficient of the containing region. The pointed diagram is **admissible** when every nonzero periodic domain with $n_{w_i}=0$ for all $i$ has at least one positive and at least one negative region coefficient. A path $\mathbb J$ is **regular for a set of disk classes** when the linearized Cauchy–Riemann operator at every holomorphic-strip representative in those classes is surjective. For a path regular on the classes used, let $\mathcal M_{\mathbb J}(\phi)$ be the space of strip representatives. If, for each generator $\mathbf x$, only finitely many pairs $(\mathbf y,\phi)$ with $\mathcal M_{\mathbb J}(\phi)\ne\varnothing$, $\mu(\phi)=1$, and $n_{w_i}(\phi)\ge0$ for every $i$ occur, and each quotient is finite, the multipointed full filtered minus module is the free module over $\mathbb F_2[U_1,\ldots,U_k]$ generated by $\mathbb T_{\alpha}\cap\mathbb T_{\beta}$, with differential
$$\partial^-\mathbf x=\sum_{\mathbf y}\sum_{\substack{\phi\in\pi_2(\mathbf x,\mathbf y),\;\mathcal M_{\mathbb J}(\phi)\ne\varnothing\\\mu(\phi)=1,\; n_{w_i}(\phi)\ge0\ \forall i}}\#_2\bigl(\mathcal M_{\mathbb J}(\phi)/\mathbb R\bigr)\,U_1^{n_{w_1}(\phi)}\cdots U_k^{n_{w_k}(\phi)}\mathbf y.$$
Here $\#_2$ is the count modulo two of the zero-dimensional quotient when it is finite. Quotienting by all $U_i$ leaves the fully blocked multipointed chain complex (the hat chain complex when $k=1$), with terms only from classes satisfying $n_{w_i}=0$ for every $i$; its Alexander-filtered associated-graded complex further retains only classes with every $n_{z_i}=0$. The relative Alexander grading convention is $A(\mathbf x)-A(\mathbf y)=\sum_i n_{z_i}(\phi)-\sum_i n_{w_i}(\phi)$, with an overall additive constant. Each $U_i$ lowers Maslov grading by two and Alexander filtration by one. In a grid diagram, $\Sigma=T^2$, $h=1$, $k=n$, and $m=n$; the horizontal circles are the $\alpha$-curves, the vertical circles are the $\beta$-curves, and the $w$-points and $z$-points are the $O$- and $X$-markings respectively.

SUPPLIER def-based-string-link-and-labeled-link-peripheral-system
Let $D^2$ be the oriented closed disk and give $C=D^2\times[0,1]$ the product orientation. An $r$-component string link is an ordered family of pairwise disjoint smooth proper embeddings $\tau_i:[0,1]\to C$, for $1\le i\le r$, with fixed distinct endpoints $\tau_i(0)=(p_i,0)$ and $\tau_i(1)=(p_i,1)$ and fixed product collars at the endpoints. Orient each interval from bottom to top. The exterior $X_\tau$ is obtained by removing the relative interiors of chosen pairwise disjoint product tubes around the intervals. The bottom and top punctured disks $P_-$ and $P_+$ are the copies of $D^2$ with the interiors of the endpoint tube disks removed.

Choose a basepoint on the outer boundary of $P_-$, a path in the side boundary of $C$ to the corresponding top basepoint, and basing paths from these points to the component meridians. Each end inclusion, with the chosen connecting path for the top end, induces a based homomorphism $\iota_\pm:\pi_1(P_\pm)\to\pi_1(X_\tau)$. For each string component choose a parallel path on its tube boundary from the bottom endpoint to the top endpoint, and choose access paths that close it to a based loop using the fixed side connector. The resulting element of $\pi_1(X_\tau)$ is its based string-link longitude; the parallel and access paths are part of the peripheral datum. The positively oriented boundaries of the endpoint tube disks are the designated meridian loops. Stacking two string links means rescaling them into the lower and upper halves of $C$ and identifying their matching endpoint collars; closure joins each top endpoint to the matching bottom endpoint outside $C$ and retains the component labels.

For a nonempty labeled oriented link $L=K_1\cup\cdots\cup K_r$ in the oriented three-sphere, choose pairwise disjoint closed product tubes $N(K_i)$, put $E_L=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}\bigl(\bigcup_iN(K_i)\bigr)$, and write $T_i=\partial N(K_i)$. A positive meridian $\mu_i$ is the oriented boundary of a normal disk, with the disk orientation fixed by the rule that the oriented tangent of $K_i$ followed by the disk orientation gives the ambient orientation. A degree-one parallel is a simple closed curve on $T_i$ whose projection to $K_i$ has degree $+1$. A based longitude is the class of such a parallel transported to the common basepoint by a specified path. When the positive meridians form a basis of $H_1(E_L;\mathbb Z)$, the self-meridian coordinate of a longitude is its coefficient on $[\mu_i]$. A preferred zero-self-meridian longitude is a chosen degree-one parallel whose self-meridian coordinate is zero. The choice, when it exists, is part of the peripheral datum; this definition asserts neither existence nor uniqueness. Changing a common basing path conjugates the peripheral group elements simultaneously.

SUPPLIER def-knot-exterior
Assume countable choice, as required for the smooth normal-bundle structure in the tubular-neighbourhood interface. Let $K:S^1\to S^3$ be a smooth oriented knot, let $\nu(K)=TS^3|_K/TK$ be its quotient normal bundle, and let $\Phi:\Omega\to S^3$ be a tubular-neighbourhood map from an open neighbourhood $\Omega$ of the zero section in $\nu(K)$ that restricts to the inclusion of $K$. Choose a radius $\epsilon>0$ whose closed disk subbundle $D_\epsilon\nu(K)$ is contained in $\Omega$, and set $N(K)=\Phi(D_\epsilon\nu(K))$. The **exterior** of this chosen tube is
$$
E_K:=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}_{S^3}N(K).
$$
Retain its boundary $T_K:=\partial E_K=\partial N(K)$, orient $E_K$ by restriction of the fixed orientation of $S^3$, and orient $T_K$ as the boundary of $E_K$. The choice of tubular map and radius is part of the input to this definition.

SUPPLIER def-knot-meridian-and-preferred-longitude
Assume countable choice as in the chosen tubular-neighbourhood data. Let $K$ be a smooth oriented knot with chosen exterior $E_K$ and boundary torus $T_K=\partial E_K$, and let $p:T_K\to K$ be the projection of the chosen normal-disk tube. Orient each normal disk so that its positive tangent direction followed by its oriented normal basis agrees with the orientation of $S^3$. A **meridian** $\mu$ is the oriented boundary of one such normal disk. A **degree-one parallel curve** is a simple closed curve $\lambda$ in $T_K$ for which $p|_\lambda:\lambda\to K$ has degree $+1$. A **preferred longitude** is a degree-one parallel curve satisfying $i_*[\lambda]=0$ in $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)$, where $i:T_K\hookrightarrow E_K$ is inclusion. This is a condition on a curve; this definition alone asserts neither existence nor uniqueness. Reversing the orientation of $K$ reverses the orientations of both $\mu$ and any degree-one parallel curve. If a subgroup $P\le\pi_1(E_K,x)$ is specified using a path from $x$ to $T_K$, record it only up to replacing it by $gPg^{-1}$ for $g\in\pi_1(E_K,x)$.

SUPPLIER def-lee-deformed-khovanov-complex
Let $D$ be a finite regular oriented link diagram in the standard oriented $3$-sphere. Use the oriented Khovanov resolution cube, crossing order, signs, and shifts fixed in the integral Khovanov construction. Over $\mathbb Q$, replace its circle algebra by $A_L=\mathbb Q[X]/(X^2-1)$, with $\deg_q(1)=1$ and $\deg_q(X)=-1$, and structure maps
$$
\eta_L(1)=1,\quad \epsilon_L(1)=0,\quad \epsilon_L(X)=1,
$$
$$
m_L(1\otimes1)=1,\quad m_L(1\otimes X)=m_L(X\otimes1)=X,\quad m_L(X\otimes X)=1,
$$
$$
\Delta_L(1)=1\otimes X+X\otimes1,\quad \Delta_L(X)=1\otimes1+X\otimes X.
$$
For a resolution state $v$ with $k(v)$ circles, assign $A_L^{\otimes k(v)}$ and cohomological degree $i(v)=|v|-n_-$. On a tensor basis element whose circle labels are $a_1,\ldots,a_{k(v)}\in\{1,X\}$, set
$$
q(v;a_1,\ldots,a_{k(v)})=|v|+n_+-2n_-+\sum_{j=1}^{k(v)}\deg_q(a_j).
$$
The differential is the signed sum of the edge maps $m_L$ and $\Delta_L$. The resulting finite cochain complex is the **Lee-deformed Khovanov complex** $C_L(D;\mathbb Q)$. Its decreasing quantum filtration $F^pC_L(D;\mathbb Q)$ is spanned by basis elements of quantum degree at least $p$. Its homology with the induced filtration is the Lee homology of the diagram. The diagram-level definition does not by itself assert invariance under changing $D$.

The two equal composites in the diagram below are the Frobenius compatibility maps used on merge-split faces. The drawing names maps only; the proof checks the equality on the basis $\{1,X\}^{\otimes2}$.

```tikzcd
A_L\otimes A_L \arrow[r,"m_L"] \arrow[d,"\Delta_L\otimes\mathrm{id}"'] & A_L \arrow[d,"\Delta_L"] \\
A_L\otimes A_L\otimes A_L \arrow[r,"\mathrm{id}\otimes m_L"'] & A_L\otimes A_L
```

SUPPLIER def-lens-space-surgery-orientation-convention
Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$. An oriented lens space is an oriented closed $3$-manifold obtained by gluing two solid tori by an orientation-reversing boundary homeomorphism. For coprime integers $p>0$ and $q$, let $L(p,q)$ denote the oriented union of the exterior of the unknot in the oriented $3$-sphere and a solid torus whose meridian is identified with the slope $p[\mu_U]+q[\lambda_U]$ on the unknot exterior. Thus this library uses the convention $L(p,q)=E_U(p/q)$; no symbol $L(0,q)$ is assigned.

SUPPLIER def-locally-flat-proper-surface-and-concordance-collars
Let $X$ be a four-dimensional manifold with boundary and let $W$ be a compact surface with boundary. An embedding $e:(W,\partial W)\to(X,\partial X)$ is proper when $e^{-1}(\partial X)=\partial W$. It is locally flat at an interior point of $W$ when there are neighborhoods of the point and its image and a homeomorphism of pairs from those neighborhoods to $(\mathbb R^4,\mathbb R^2)$, with the standard linear plane $\mathbb R^2\subset\mathbb R^4$. It is locally flat at a boundary point when there are such neighborhoods and a homeomorphism of pairs to $(\mathbb R^4_+,\mathbb R^2_+)$, where $\mathbb R^4_+=\{x_4\geq0\}$ and $\mathbb R^2_+=\{x_2=x_3=0,\ x_4\geq0\}$; the line $\partial\mathbb R^2_+$ lies in $\partial\mathbb R^4_+$. A collar of a boundary circle of $W$ is an embedding of that circle times $[0,\varepsilon)$ into $W$ that is the identity on the circle at parameter $0$; a concordance collar is required to agree with the corresponding product collar of the ambient end. A smooth proper surface has the analogous smooth charts and collars, which are also topological locally flat charts.

SUPPLIER def-oriented-crossing-sign-writhe-and-blackboard-framing
Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ for the smooth normal-bundle structure used in the
framing clause. For a knot component $K$, its normal bundle is the fibrewise
quotient $\nu K=TS^3|_K/TK$, and a normal section is a smooth section of this
bundle.

Let $D$ be a regular oriented link diagram in a projection plane $P$ of an
oriented $3$-sphere, after moving the link off the point at infinity. Fix the
unit viewing normal $n$ pointing toward the viewer. Orient $P$ by the rule that
an ordered basis $(u,v)$ of $P$ is positive exactly when $(n,u,v)$ is a positive
basis of the ambient $3$-space. At a crossing $c$, let $v_{\rm over}$ and
$v_{\rm under}$ be the nonzero projected tangent vectors following the
orientations of the overpassing and underpassing strands. Define
$$\operatorname{sgn}(c):=\operatorname{sgn}\det_P(v_{\rm over},v_{\rm under})\in\{+1,-1\}.$$
Reversing the orientation of $P$ reverses every crossing sign.

This convention makes the positive geometric Artin half twist positive. In
the braid chart $(x,y,t)$ with ambient orientation $(x,y,t)$, view along $+y$;
the induced positive orientation of the $(x,t)$ projection plane is the ordered
basis $(\partial_t,\partial_x)$. For the positive half twist, label $i$ moves
from $x=-h$ to $x=h$ below the midpoint, while label $i+1$ moves from $x=h$ to
$x=-h$ above it, with $h>0$. Thus the over and under projected tangent vectors
in coordinates $(t,x)$ are $(1,-2h)$ and $(1,2h)$, whose determinant is
$4h>0$.

The **writhe** of $D$ is
$$\operatorname{wr}(D):=\sum_{c\text{ a crossing of }D}\operatorname{sgn}(c).$$
For a link component $K_i$, its **self-writhe**
$\operatorname{wr}_i(D)$ is the sum over crossings whose two branches belong to
$K_i$; the total writhe also includes all mixed crossings between distinct
components.

The **blackboard framing** of a component of $D$ is the homotopy class of the
normal section represented by the positive quarter-turn in $P$ of the projected
oriented tangent. The projection is an immersion, so this vector never
vanishes; it is perpendicular to the projected tangent, hence also to the
spatial tangent, and therefore represents a nonzero normal section of the
component in the ambient $3$-sphere.

```tikz
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.8,>=stealth]
  \draw[->] (-1,0) -- (1,0) node[right] {$v_{\rm over}$};
  \draw[->] (0,-1) -- (0,1) node[above] {$v_{\rm under}$};
  \node[above left] at (0,0) {$+1$};
\end{tikzpicture}
```

In the displayed local chart, the over tangent is the positive horizontal
vector and the under tangent is the positive vertical vector, so the determinant
is $+1$. This figure illustrates the determinant rule only; no proof below
depends on a picture.

SUPPLIER def-pl-embedding-and-subpolyhedron
Let $P$ and $Q$ be compact piecewise-linear polyhedra. A **piecewise-linear map** $f:P\to Q$ is a map that is simplicial after suitable finite subdivisions of triangulations of $P$ and $Q$. A **subpolyhedron** $A$ of $Q$ is a subset represented by the realization of a subcomplex in some finite linear triangulation of $Q$. A map $f:P\to Q$ is a **piecewise-linear embedding** if it is piecewise-linear, its image $f(P)$ is a subpolyhedron of $Q$, and $f:P\to f(P)$ is a piecewise-linear homeomorphism. A finite simplicial triangulation of a closed piecewise-linear $n$-manifold is **combinatorial** if the link of every $k$-simplex is a piecewise-linear sphere of dimension $n-k-1$. Thus in a closed combinatorial $3$-manifold every triangular face has link $S^0$ and is incident to exactly two tetrahedra.

SUPPLIER lem-complete-curvature-minus-one-manifolds-are-kleinian-quotients
Assume AC. Every nonempty connected oriented complete Riemannian three-manifold of constant sectional curvature $-1$ is isometric to $\Gamma\backslash\mathbb H^3$ for a discrete torsion-free subgroup $\Gamma\le\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)$. The covering is a universal Riemannian covering and identifies $\Gamma$ with the fundamental group up to the usual basepoint conjugacies. Its completeness and quotient presentation are conclusions from the stated metric hypotheses. The orientation-preserving isometry group of the model is $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)$ with its usual Möbius boundary action.

SUPPLIER lem-explicit-unknot-and-figure-eight-oriented-wirtinger-codes
There are the following explicit valid finite planar codes, with the standard right/up orientation of the plane and positive based meridians using compatible common upper-collar access paths.

The counterclockwise square with vertices $(0,0),(1,0),(1,1),(0,1)$ is a crossing-free unknot code. It has one under-arc and gives $\pi_1(S^3\setminus U)=\langle x\mid\ \rangle\cong\mathbb Z$, with $x$ the positive meridian.

An oriented figure-eight code has crossings $C_1,C_2,C_3,C_4$. The letters $O,U$ mean over and under, and $i,o$ mean incoming and outgoing. Its integer plane-drawing certificate is

| $r$ | $C_r$ | $rOi$ | $rOo$ | $rUi$ | $rUo$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $1$ | $(-423,0)$ | $(-627,102)$ | $(-363,-30)$ | $(-363,30)$ | $(-627,-102)$ |
| $2$ | $(0,-9)$ | $(-33,-6)$ | $(99,-18)$ | $(-99,-18)$ | $(33,-6)$ |
| $3$ | $(0,9)$ | $(33,6)$ | $(-99,18)$ | $(99,18)$ | $(-33,6)$ |
| $4$ | $(423,0)$ | $(627,-102)$ | $(363,30)$ | $(363,-30)$ | $(627,102)$ |

The complete cyclic visit list is $$1O,\ 2U,\ 3O,\ 1U,\ 4O,\ 3U,\ 2O,\ 4U.$$ For every successive pair $rB,sD$ in this list, including the last-to-first pair, use the three straight segments of the route $C_r\to rBo\to sDi\to C_s$. At each crossing pair $rOi$ with $rOo$, and $rUi$ with $rUo$; the first pair is over. Smooth the degree-two bends in disjoint small disks. The cyclic orders at crossings are the counterclockwise lists $(rOo,rUi,rOi,rUo)$ for $r=1,4$ and $(rOo,rUo,rOi,rUi)$ for $r=2,3$.

Label the under-arcs $x_1:U4\to U2$, $x_2:U2\to U1$, $x_3:U1\to U3$, $x_4:U3\to U4$. In each oriented over-branch chart, let $L$ denote its left under-side (target of the positive in-plane normal), and $R$ its right under-side (source). The exact crossing table is

| crossing | over | $L$ (target) | $R$ (source) | under traversal | $\operatorname{sign}\det(\text{over},\text{under})$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $R_1$ | $x_1$ | $x_2$ | $x_3$ | target to source | $-1$ |
| $R_2$ | $x_4$ | $x_2$ | $x_1$ | source to target | $+1$ |
| $R_3$ | $x_2$ | $x_4$ | $x_3$ | source to target | $+1$ |
| $R_4$ | $x_3$ | $x_4$ | $x_1$ | target to source | $-1$ |

Thus its actual positive-meridian presentation is $$\pi_1(S^3\setminus 4_1)=\langle x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4\mid x_1x_2=x_3x_1,\ x_1x_4=x_4x_2,\ x_3x_2=x_2x_4,\ x_3x_4=x_1x_3\rangle.$$ The last relation follows from the first three. Starting on $x_1$, the underpasses occur at $R_2,R_1,R_3,R_4$, and the self-writhe is zero. All drawing data, validations and algebra in this statement are finite and use no choice axiom.

SUPPLIER lem-knot-exterior-h1-is-generated-by-meridian
Let $K\subset S^3$ be a smooth oriented knot with a chosen closed product
tubular neighborhood $N(K)$, exterior $E_K=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}N(K)$,
and positive meridian $\mu_K$. Then
$$H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[\mu_K].$$
The assertion uses integral singular homology and the stated tube convention.

SUPPLIER lem-knot-genus-zero-iff-unknot
Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice. For every smooth oriented knot $K$ in $S^3$, its Seifert genus is zero if and only if $K$ is ambient isotopic through orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms to the standard unknot.

SUPPLIER lem-knot-normal-circle-bundle-trivialization
For every smooth oriented embedding $K:S^1\to S^3$, let $\nu_K=K^*TS^3/dK(TS^1)$ be the quotient normal bundle. Orient its two-dimensional fibres so that a positive tangent to $K$ followed by a positive basis of the normal fibre gives the fixed orientation of $S^3$. Then $\nu_K$ admits a smooth orientation-preserving bundle isomorphism $\nu_K\cong K\times\mathbb R^2$.

SUPPLIER lem-lee-cobordism-filtration-genus-bound

   $W_j=\prod_c (Y_{o(c)}^{(d)})^{\epsilon_c}$, where the factors are in
   traversal order and $\epsilon_c=+1$ for source-to-target and $-1$ for
   target-to-source. In string-link mode set $\Lambda_j=W_j$. In closed-link
   mode set
   $$\Lambda_j=(1+X_j)^{-\operatorname{wr}_j(D)}W_j,$$
   where $\operatorname{wr}_j(D)$ is the sum of the determinant signs at
   self-crossings of component $j$.

4. In the arbitrary-choice branch evaluate each supplied transport word by the strand series from step 2. Write $G_e$ for the resulting unit series, and $H_{0,j},H_{1,j}$ for the endpoint transport series. In string-link mode replace the compatible $\Lambda_j=W_j$ by
   $$H_{0,j}(1+X_j)^{k_j}W_jH_{1,j}^{-1}.$$
   In closed-link mode replace the compatible preferred series by
   $$H_j(1+X_j)^{-\operatorname{wr}_j(D)}W_jH_j^{-1},$$
   where $H_j=H_{0,j}=H_{1,j}$ is the actual longitude access difference, independently of the initial-meridian difference $G_j$. The arbitrary parallel winding cancels in the preferred correction. All powers and inverses are finite truncated operations. To express this result in the actual component-meridian symbols, form
   $$G_j(1+X_j)G_j^{-1}=1+X_j+R_j(X),\qquad \deg R_j\ge2.$$
   With new letters $V_j$, initialize $Z_j^{(1)}=V_j$ and iterate
   $$Z_j^{(s+1)}=\operatorname{trunc}_d\bigl(V_j-R_j(Z^{(s)})\bigr),\qquad 1\le s<d.$$
   Substitute $X_j=Z_j^{(d)}(V)$ in the corrected longitude series. This is its expansion in the chosen actual meridians. For the compatible branch this step is the identity. Rename the final letters $V_j$ as $X_j$.

5. For every $I=(i_1,\ldots,i_k,j)$ with $1\leq k\leq d$, read the coefficient
   of $X_{i_1}\cdots X_{i_k}$ in $\Lambda_j$. In string-link mode return this
   integer. In closed-link mode, also compute the gcd of the coefficients for
   all proper subsequences of cyclic permutations of $I$ of lengths at least
   two, with empty gcd equal to zero, and return the corresponding residue
   class.

All scans, including the supplied transport words and the $d-1$ inverse-substitution iterations, are over finite crossing, strand, degree, and index sets. The
procedure makes no polynomial-time claim. If a finite-input validation fails,
return `invalid-input`.

CONSUMER def-hat-heegaard-floer-complex-of-a-closed-three-manifold
Supplier edges: def-admissible-pointed-heegaard-floer-data
(No named supplier use in literal Facts; declared-context edge only at this scope.)
Literal Definition/Statement in absence of Facts:
Let $Y$ be a closed connected oriented smooth three-manifold. A pointed genus-$g$ Heegaard diagram for $Y$ consists of an embedded splitting $Y=U_\alpha\cup_\Sigma U_\beta$, where $\Sigma=\partial U_\alpha$ is a closed oriented genus-$g$ surface and each $U$ is a handlebody, meaning a regular neighborhood of a finite connected graph; ordered systems $\boldsymbol\alpha=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_g)$ and $\boldsymbol\beta=(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_g)$ of pairwise disjoint smooth simple closed curves bounding specified disjoint proper compression disks in the respective handlebodies, each disk system cutting its handlebody into one ball; and one point $z$ off the curves. Opposite-system curves meet transversely in finitely many points. This is closed-manifold data; it includes neither a knot nor a second marked point.

Write $q:\Sigma^g\to\operatorname{Sym}^g(\Sigma)=\Sigma^g/S_g$ for the permutation quotient. The product tori are the images
$$T_\alpha=q(\alpha_1\times\cdots\times\alpha_g),\qquad T_\beta=q(\beta_1\times\cdots\times\beta_g).$$
The group $S_g$ acts on the ambient product, rather than on a fixed ordered product of distinct attaching circles. A generator is an intersection $x\in T_\alpha\cap T_\beta$; equivalently it selects one intersection point on each curve of each system. There are finitely many generators. For $g=0$ the symmetric product and both empty products are one point, with the empty generator.

A Whitney class $\phi\in\pi_2(x,y)$ is a relative homotopy class of maps of a closed disk with two marked boundary points into the symmetric product, taking its two boundary arcs into $T_\alpha,T_\beta$ and its marked points to $x,y$. Its domain is the integral two-chain of complementary regions; its coefficient at an interior point $p$ is the intersection multiplicity $n_p(\phi)$ with $\{p\}+\operatorname{Sym}^{g-1}(\Sigma)$. In particular $n_z(\phi)$ is the basepoint coefficient. A periodic domain is an integral region two-chain whose boundary is an integral sum of full attaching circles. The domain of a class $\psi\in\pi_2(x,x)$ is periodic. Such a class is normalized when $n_z(\psi)=0$. Write $\mu(\phi)$ for the real Fredholm index of the disk linearization, before quotienting longitudinal translation; well-definedness and its topological index formula are properties to be proved in the analytic and index theory.

For a periodic domain P, cap its full attaching-circle boundary with the specified compression disks, using the boundary multiplicities and orientations, to obtain the integral class $H(P)\in H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)$. A Spin-c structure $\mathfrak s$ is a lift of the oriented tangent frame bundle through $\operatorname{Spin}^c(3)\to SO(3)$; $c_1(\mathfrak s)$ is the first Chern class of its determinant line, with determinant character $[s,u]\mapsto u^2$. **Weak admissibility in the specified Spin-c sector $\mathfrak s$** means that EVERY nonzero integral periodic domain P with $n_z(P)=0$ and $\langle c_1(\mathfrak s),H(P)\rangle=0$ has both positive and negative region coefficients. This condition is imposed even when the sector has no diagram generators. It is a condition on domains and the intended sector, not only on periodic classes based at existing generators.

The ordinary sector module $\widehat{CF}(\mathcal H,\mathfrak s)$ uses generators with Morse Spin-c label $\mathfrak s_z(x)=\mathfrak s$. The label is defined by compatible handle Morse data: replace the gradient in disjoint tubes around the generator's index-one/index-two trajectories and the basepoint's index-zero/index-three trajectory by nonsingular fields, and take the Spin-c lift of their oriented perpendicular planes. Its actual construction, independence properties and periodic-index formula are obligations of the Spin-c and index theory. In particular the equality between the index of a REALIZED normalized periodic class based at x and the Chern evaluation in $\mathfrak s_z(x)$ is not assumed as its own proof. A realized-generator test alone is not substituted for the stated domain condition.

An **admissible diagram for the full single-diagram hat module** satisfies this sector condition for EVERY Spin-c structure on Y, including the sectors with no generators. The full module below uses all generators and hence all intended sectors; an empty sector contributes a zero summand but is not omitted from the admissibility test. Equivalently this full-diagram condition is global weak admissibility: every nonzero periodic domain with $n_z=0$ has both signs. The equivalence uses the actual zero-Chern Spin-c sector constructed from complete Heegaard data in [[lem-complete-heegaard-data-have-a-zero-chern-spin-c-sector]]; it is not inferred from a generator existing in that sector. For a SINGLE specified sector, ordinary weak admissibility remains the stated Chern-kernel condition and need not imply global weakness. No assertion of the full group from a diagram tested only at its represented sectors is made.

For $g>0$ choose a complex structure $j$ and positive area form on $\Sigma$. Analytic data include a smooth path $J_t$ on the symmetric product, constant on actual neighborhoods of $t=0,1$. On a fixed neighborhood of the diagonal and the point-divisors for a chosen interior point of every complementary region, including $z$, it agrees with the integrable structure $\operatorname{Sym}^g(j)$; away from that neighborhood it is tame for the form descended from the product surface area form. The path is chosen in a sufficiently small nearly symmetric class. Product-integrable normal forms near the finitely many transverse generators may be included among its specified auxiliary choices. Existence of such choices and of the regular perturbations needed below remains a mathematical obligation. A holomorphic strip is an actual smooth finite-Dirichlet-energy map $u:\mathbb R\times[0,1]\to\operatorname{Sym}^g(\Sigma)$ satisfying
$$u_s+J_t(u)u_t=0,\qquad u(s,0)\in T_\alpha,\quad u(s,1)\in T_\beta,$$
with uniform generator limits at both longitudinal ends. The space in class $\phi$ is $\mathcal M_J(\phi)$, and translation in $s$ acts on it. Regular means that its actual linearized operator is surjective at the solutions being counted. Local multiplicity positivity, Fredholmness, the class index and compactness are results to be proved, not choices of which maps to count.

The hat diagram-level module $\widehat{CF}(\mathcal H;\mathbb F_2)$ is free over $\mathbb F_2$ on the generators. For regular data for which the displayed quotient sets are finite and, for each $x$, only finitely many nonempty pairs $(y,\phi)$ occur, its candidate count operator is
$$\widehat\partial x=\sum_y\ \sum_{\substack{\phi\in\pi_2(x,y)\\\mu(\phi)=1,\ n_z(\phi)=0}}\#_2\bigl(\mathcal M_J(\phi)/\mathbb R\bigr)y.$$
Here $\#_2$ is parity of an actual finite zero-dimensional quotient. For $g=0$ the operator is zero on the empty generator. That the required regular finite counts exist, that $\widehat\partial^2=0$, and that its homology is invariant under changing pointed admissible diagrams and auxiliary choices are separate analytic and move theorems. Once these hold, the common homology is denoted $\widehat{HF}(Y;\mathbb F_2)$. No filtered chain-homotopy invariant, integral orientations or absolute grading is asserted by this definition.

Comparisons of embedded diagrams permit orientation-preserving isomorphisms carrying $Y$, its two handlebodies, their oriented surface, the attaching systems and $z$ to the specified counterparts, equivalently compatible collared extensions of the surface map. Curve-factor reordering gives the corresponding product identification. Analytic data are transported by the induced symmetric-product map, including $j$, the area form, protected neighborhoods and $J_t$. The intended count identification is the actual pushforward of generators and disk classes with these transported data; its compatibility with the operator requires proof. An ambient isotopy is not assumed to act as the identity. Comparison of independently chosen perturbations likewise requires the continuation theorem.

CONSUMER def-lower-central-series-and-magnus-expansion-for-links
Supplier edges: def-based-string-link-and-labeled-link-peripheral-system
(No named supplier use in literal Facts; declared-context edge only at this scope.)
Literal Definition/Statement in absence of Facts:
For any group $G$, define its lower-central series by $\Gamma_1G=G$ and
$\Gamma_{q+1}G=[G,\Gamma_qG]$, where $[G,H]$ is the subgroup generated by
$ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$ for $g\in G$ and $h\in H$. For $r\ge1$, let
$F_r=\langle x_1,\ldots,x_r\rangle$ be the free group on the formal meridian
symbols $x_i$, with the free-group convention of its stated generators.

Let $\mathcal A_r=\mathbb Z\langle\!\langle X_1,\ldots,X_r\rangle\!\rangle$
be the ring of formal integer sums $\sum_w a_wX_w$ over all finite words $w$
in the letters $X_i$, with multiplication given by concatenation of words.
This is the degree-complete ring for the word-length filtration; a series with
invertible constant coefficient is a unit, with inverse determined recursively
degree by degree.
Filter it by total degree (word length), and let $\mathfrak m$ be the ideal of
series with zero constant term.
The Magnus expansion is the group homomorphism
$$
E:F_r\longrightarrow
\mathbb Z\langle\!\langle X_1,\ldots,X_r\rangle\!\rangle^\times,
\qquad E(x_i)=1+X_i.
$$
It sends $x_i^{-1}$ to $(1+X_i)^{-1}=\sum_{k\ge0}(-1)^kX_i^k$. For an
integer $d\ge0$, calculations through degree $d$ use the truncated ring
$R_d=\mathbb Z\langle X_1,\ldots,X_r\rangle/\mathfrak m^{d+1}$. As a
$\mathbb Z$-module it has the finite monomial basis of words of length at most
$d$. In $R_d$, the inverse of $1+A$ for $A\in\mathfrak m$ is the finite sum
$1-A+A^2-\cdots+(-A)^d$.

For a based string link, $x_1,\ldots,x_r$ may be used as formal symbols
corresponding to the positive bottom-end meridians. No freeness or
lower-central quotient isomorphism is asserted here; those properties require
separate proofs.
