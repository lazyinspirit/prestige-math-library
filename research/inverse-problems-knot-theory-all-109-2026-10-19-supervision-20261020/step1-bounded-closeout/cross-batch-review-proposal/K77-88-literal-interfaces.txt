SUPPLIER def-blanchfield-pairing-and-higher-order-rho-invariant
Let $M$ be a closed oriented three-manifold with a specified coefficient homomorphism $\phi:\pi_1(M_j)\to\Gamma$ on each component, where $\Gamma$ is PTFA. The integral coefficient ring is $\Lambda=\mathbb Z[\Gamma]$ with its fraction division ring $K\Gamma$. More generally let $k$ be $\mathbb Z$ or a commutative field, let $K$ be the fraction division ring of $k[\Gamma]$, and let $k[\Gamma]\subseteq R\subseteq K$ be an involution-stable unital intermediate ring. The involution fixes $k$ and sends $g$ to $g^{-1}$; on fractions it is the involutive anti-automorphism extending this formula. Taking $k=\mathbb Z$ and $R=\Lambda$ recovers the integral convention.

If $H_1(M;K)=0$, the coefficient Bockstein, oriented Poincaré duality and evaluation define the Blanchfield linking pairing
$$B_{M,\phi,R}:TH_1(M;R)\times TH_1(M;R)\longrightarrow K/R.$$
Here $T$ denotes the submodule of elements with a nonzero right annihilator, the first variable is conjugate-linear, and the second is right-linear. Nonsingularity means that the adjoint
$$\overline{TH_1(M;R)}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_R(TH_1(M;R),K/R)$$
is an isomorphism of left $R$-modules, where $r\cdot\bar x=\overline{x\bar r}$ on the conjugate module. This is equivalent to the conjugate-dual formulation in the right-module convention. For $\Gamma=\mathbb Z$ with generator $t$, $R=\mathbb Z[t^{\pm1}]$ and $K=\mathbb Q(t)$ give the classical coefficient convention. Nonsingularity is a separate property of the defined pairing, with its coefficient hypotheses supplied by the nonsingularity theorem.

For a homomorphism φ:π₁(M)→G and an extension ψ:π₁(W)→G over a compact oriented 4-manifold W with ∂W=M, define the signature defect by ρ(M,φ;W,ψ)=sign_G^(2)(W,ψ)−sign(W). It is an invariant of (M,φ) only when a separate bordism-independence theorem applies.

SUPPLIER def-framed-chord-diagram-and-four-term-relations
For a commutative ring $R$, a **degree-$n$ chord diagram** is an oriented circle with $n$ disjoint unordered pairs of distinct points, called chords, considered up to orientation-preserving homeomorphism of the circle. A chord records a double point by pairing its two preimages. The diagrams used for symbols of both framed and unframed invariants are these ordinary chord diagrams. In a framed singular-knot homotopy, each simple zero of the normal section may be marked by an auxiliary dot and counted as one singularity; these auxiliary dots are not generators of the chord-diagram modules below.

For $n\ge2$, choose any degree-$(n-2)$ background chord diagram and choose three pairwise distinct new points $p,q,r$ on its circle, disjoint from all background endpoints. Choose disjoint endpoint-free neighborhoods of $p$ and $q$ that avoid $r$ and every background endpoint. Let $s_1,s_2,s_3,s_4$ lie, respectively, immediately before $p$, immediately after $p$, immediately before $q$, and immediately after $q$ in the circle orientation, each within the corresponding neighborhood. Let $D_i$ be the degree-$n$ diagram obtained by adding the fixed chord $pq$ and moving chord $rs_i$ to the background. The **4T relation** is
$$
D_1-D_2+D_3-D_4=0.
$$
Two chords **intersect** if their endpoints alternate around the oriented circle. A chord is **isolated** if it intersects no other chord. The **1T relation** sets every unframed diagram with an isolated chord equal to zero. The framed degree-$n$ chord-diagram module is the free $R$-module on degree-$n$ chord diagrams modulo all 4T relations. The unframed degree-$n$ chord-diagram module is the free $R$-module on degree-$n$ chord diagrams modulo 4T and 1T relations. A framed weight system is an $R$-linear functional on the framed module; an unframed weight system is an $R$-linear functional on the unframed module.

SUPPLIER def-integral-half-solutions-strebel-and-l2-signatures
Assume full AC, matching the chosen finite twisted-chain and maximal-abelian-cover conventions. Let K be a smooth oriented knot, M(K) its preferred-longitude zero-framed surgery, W a compact connected oriented smooth 4-manifold with boundary M(K), and π=π₁(W). Put π^(0)=π and π^(j+1)=[π^(j),π^(j)]. A right Zπ-chain complex with coefficients in a left Z[π/π^(j)]-module is formed by tensoring over Zπ with that module.

For n≥0, W is an integral n-solution for K when H₁(M(K);Z)→H₁(W;Z) is an isomorphism, H₂(W;Z) is free of rank 2r for some finite r, and there are classes x₁,…,x_r,y₁,…,y_r in H₂(W;Z[π/π^(n)]) whose equivariant intersection pairings satisfy λ_n(x_i,x_j)=0 and λ_n(x_i,y_j)=δ_ij. It is an integral (n+1/2)-solution when, in addition, each x_i is the image of a class x̃_i in H₂(W;Z[π/π^(n+1)]) and λ_{n+1}(x̃_i,x̃_j)=0 for all i,j. Here λ_j is the equivariant intersection pairing induced by the orientation of W.

For a commutative ring R with 1≠0, a group G belongs to Strebel's class D(R) if every homomorphism between projective RG-modules is injective whenever its tensor with the trivial G-module R is injective. In this page R is Q or F_p for a prime p. For a countable discrete group G, its group von Neumann algebra N G is the weak-operator closure of CG in the left regular representation on ℓ²(G), with trace τ_G(T)=⟨Tδ_e,δ_e⟩. The von Neumann dimension of a finitely generated projective N G-module represented by a projection E is τ_G(E); for an arbitrary module it is the supremum of the dimensions of its finitely generated projective submodules.

For a compact oriented 4-manifold W with boundary, sign(W) is the inertia difference of the symmetric intersection form on H₂(W;R) modulo its radical (the subspace pairing to zero with every class), equivalently on the image of H₂(W;R)→H₂(W,∂W;R). Given a closed oriented 3-manifold M, a homomorphism φ:π₁(M)→G, a compact oriented 4-manifold W with ∂W=M, and an extension ψ:π₁(W)→G of φ, the L²-signature sign_G^(2)(W,ψ) is the von Neumann signature of the nonsingular part of the N G-coefficient intersection form. When such a W and extension exist, the signature defect is sign_G^(2)(W,ψ)−sign(W). It is called the rho invariant of (M,φ) only after its independence of the bounding data is established by a separate bordism argument.

SUPPLIER def-kauffman-bracket-smoothing-and-loop-value
Let $A$ be an indeterminate and let $\mathbb Z[A^{\pm1}]$ be the Laurent
polynomial ring in $A$. A **nonempty unoriented regular link diagram** is the
finite planar projection and over/under data of a regular oriented link diagram
after its strand arrows are forgotten. Its finite crossing set is denoted
$C(D)$, and at least one closed component is required. The empty link diagram is
outside this definition.

At a crossing use the following local chart: the over-strand runs from the
southwest endpoint to the northeast endpoint and is drawn continuously over the
under-strand, which runs from northwest to southeast with a gap at the
intersection. Horizontal-right followed by vertical-up is the positive
orientation of this displayed chart. In this chart the **A-smoothing** joins the
two left endpoints and joins the two right endpoints; the **B-smoothing** joins
the two top endpoints and joins the two bottom endpoints. These labels are
attached to this displayed chart and are transported by orientation-preserving
changes of local coordinates. Thus turning right from an entering underpassing
half-strand gives the A-smoothing.

```tikz
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.72, line cap=round, line join=round]
  \begin{scope}
    \draw[thick] (-1,1)--(-0.14,0.14);
    \draw[thick] (0.14,-0.14)--(1,-1);
    \draw[very thick] (-1,-1)--(1,1);
    \node at (0,-1.45) {crossing};
  \end{scope}
  \begin{scope}[xshift=3.4cm]
    \draw[thick] (-1,1) .. controls (-0.45,0.35) and (-0.45,-0.35) .. (-1,-1);
    \draw[thick] (1,1) .. controls (0.45,0.35) and (0.45,-0.35) .. (1,-1);
    \node at (0,-1.45) {$A$-smoothing, weight $A$};
  \end{scope}
  \begin{scope}[xshift=6.8cm]
    \draw[thick] (-1,1) .. controls (-0.35,0.45) and (0.35,0.45) .. (1,1);
    \draw[thick] (-1,-1) .. controls (-0.35,-0.45) and (0.35,-0.45) .. (1,-1);
    \node at (0,-1.45) {$B$-smoothing, weight $A^{-1}$};
  \end{scope}
\end{tikzpicture}
```

For a state $S:C(D)\to\{A,B\}$, let $a(S)$ and $b(S)$ be the numbers of
A- and B-smoothings, and let $|S|$ be the number of closed circles in the
crossingless diagram obtained by performing all smoothings. Set
$$\delta:=-A^2-A^{-2}$$
and define the **Kauffman bracket** of $D$ by
$$\langle D\rangle:=\sum_{S:C(D)\to\{A,B\}} A^{a(S)-b(S)}\delta^{|S|-1}\in\mathbb Z[A^{\pm1}].$$
Because $D$ is nonempty, every complete smoothing has $|S|\ge1$, so the loop
exponent is nonnegative. A crossingless $k$-component unlink with $k\ge1$ has
bracket $\delta^{k-1}$.

SUPPLIER def-knot-seifert-pairing-and-matrix
Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $K:S^1\hookrightarrow S^3$ be a smooth oriented knot in the standard oriented three-sphere, and let $F\subset S^3$ be a connected compact smooth oriented embedded surface with boundary $K$, oriented so that its boundary orientation is the given orientation of $K$. Let $g$ be the nonnegative integer determined by $\chi(F)=1-2g$. Choose an ordered integral basis $a_1,\ldots,a_{2g}$ of the first singular homology group $H_1(F;\mathbb Z)$, represented by integral one-cycles on $F$. Orient the normal line of $F$ by requiring $(n,u,v)$ to be positively oriented in $S^3$ whenever $(u,v)$ is a positive tangent basis of $F$. The positive push-off $a_i^+$ is the cycle carried to a sufficiently small positive normal level in a bicollar of $F$.

For disjoint oriented closed curves $J,L\subset S^3$, orient a meridian $\mu_L$ of $L$ so that a positive tangent to $L$ followed by a positive basis of its normal disk gives the ambient orientation. The linking number $\operatorname{lk}(J,L)$ is the integer determined by $[J]=\operatorname{lk}(J,L)[\mu_L]$ in $H_1(S^3\setminus L;\mathbb Z)$, using the Alexander-duality identification that sends $[\mu_L]$ to $1$. Extend linking additively over integral one-cycles. The Seifert pairing is $\theta_F(x,y)=\operatorname{lk}(x^+,y)$, and its matrix in the chosen basis is $V=(V_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{2g}$, where $V_{ij}=\theta_F(a_i,a_j)=\operatorname{lk}(a_i^+,a_j)$. The surface and basis are part of the data; this definition asserts no knot-only matrix invariant.

SUPPLIER def-oriented-crossing-sign-writhe-and-blackboard-framing
Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ for the smooth normal-bundle structure used in the
framing clause. For a knot component $K$, its normal bundle is the fibrewise
quotient $\nu K=TS^3|_K/TK$, and a normal section is a smooth section of this
bundle.

Let $D$ be a regular oriented link diagram in a projection plane $P$ of an
oriented $3$-sphere, after moving the link off the point at infinity. Fix the
unit viewing normal $n$ pointing toward the viewer. Orient $P$ by the rule that
an ordered basis $(u,v)$ of $P$ is positive exactly when $(n,u,v)$ is a positive
basis of the ambient $3$-space. At a crossing $c$, let $v_{\rm over}$ and
$v_{\rm under}$ be the nonzero projected tangent vectors following the
orientations of the overpassing and underpassing strands. Define
$$\operatorname{sgn}(c):=\operatorname{sgn}\det_P(v_{\rm over},v_{\rm under})\in\{+1,-1\}.$$
Reversing the orientation of $P$ reverses every crossing sign.

This convention makes the positive geometric Artin half twist positive. In
the braid chart $(x,y,t)$ with ambient orientation $(x,y,t)$, view along $+y$;
the induced positive orientation of the $(x,t)$ projection plane is the ordered
basis $(\partial_t,\partial_x)$. For the positive half twist, label $i$ moves
from $x=-h$ to $x=h$ below the midpoint, while label $i+1$ moves from $x=h$ to
$x=-h$ above it, with $h>0$. Thus the over and under projected tangent vectors
in coordinates $(t,x)$ are $(1,-2h)$ and $(1,2h)$, whose determinant is
$4h>0$.

The **writhe** of $D$ is
$$\operatorname{wr}(D):=\sum_{c\text{ a crossing of }D}\operatorname{sgn}(c).$$
For a link component $K_i$, its **self-writhe**
$\operatorname{wr}_i(D)$ is the sum over crossings whose two branches belong to
$K_i$; the total writhe also includes all mixed crossings between distinct
components.

The **blackboard framing** of a component of $D$ is the homotopy class of the
normal section represented by the positive quarter-turn in $P$ of the projected
oriented tangent. The projection is an immersion, so this vector never
vanishes; it is perpendicular to the projected tangent, hence also to the
spatial tangent, and therefore represents a nonzero normal section of the
component in the ambient $3$-sphere.

```tikz

  \draw[->] (-1,0) -- (1,0) node[right] {$v_{\rm over}$};
  \draw[->] (0,-1) -- (0,1) node[above] {$v_{\rm under}$};
  \node[above left] at (0,0) {$+1$};

```

In the displayed local chart, the over tangent is the positive horizontal
vector and the under tangent is the positive vertical vector, so the determinant
is $+1$. This figure illustrates the determinant rule only; no proof below
depends on a picture.

SUPPLIER def-poly-torsion-free-abelian-group
A group G is poly-torsion-free abelian (PTFA) if it admits a finite subnormal series
1=G_0 ◁ G_1 ◁ ⋯ ◁ G_m=G
in which every quotient G_{j+1}/G_j is torsion-free abelian. The trivial group is PTFA, using the length-zero series. The term “subnormal” means each G_j is normal in G_{j+1}; normality in all of G is not required.

SUPPLIER def-singular-knot-and-vassiliev-derivative
Fix the round metric and orientation on $S^3$. An **$m$-singular oriented knot** is a smooth immersion $K:S^1\to S^3$ with exactly $m$ distinct transverse double points and no other self-intersections; each double-point image has exactly two preimages with linearly independent tangent vectors, and the preimage pairs are disjoint. The knot has no framing. Choose disjoint small balls around its double points, each containing no other part of the singular knot. At each double point there are two **resolutions**, obtained by separating the two local branches in the ball; label them $+$ and $-$ so the resolved crossing signs are respectively $+1$ and $-1$ under the convention that, in a diagram whose positive viewing normal agrees with the ambient orientation, the sign is $\operatorname{sgn}\det(v_{\mathrm{over}},v_{\mathrm{under}})$ and the positive Artin half twist has sign $+1$.

A **framed oriented knot** is a smooth oriented embedding $K:S^1\hookrightarrow S^3$ together with a homotopy class through nowhere-zero smooth sections of its oriented rank-two normal bundle. For an immersion $K:S^1\to S^3$, including one with double points, define its branchwise normal bundle over the parameter circle by $\nu_K:=K^*TS^3/dK(TS^1)$, oriented so that a positive tangent followed by a positive normal basis gives the ambient orientation. Because $dK$ has rank one at every parameter, its image is locally a smooth line subbundle and this quotient is locally a rank-two bundle. Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ for the framed clauses below, so these normal quotient bundles have their smooth vector-bundle structures. A **framed $m$-singular knot** has finitely many transverse double points and finitely many simple zeroes of a smooth section of $\nu_K$, all disjoint; its number $m$ of singularities is the number of double points plus the number of zeroes. A simple zero is one at which the derivative of the section in an oriented local trivialization is nonzero. Resolve double points as above, extending the normal section over the local resolution balls; each resulting section determines a framing homotopy class. To resolve a framing zero at parameter $t=0$, choose the oriented normal trivialization so the section is $t e_1$ near $0$, with $e_2$ the positive quarter-turn of $e_1$; replace it by $t e_1+\delta e_2$ or $t e_1-\delta e_2$ for sufficiently small $\delta>0$. These are the $+$ and $-$ resolutions of that zero and determine the framing classes on the resolved knots.

Let $R$ be a commutative ring. For an $R$-valued ambient-isotopy invariant $V$ of unframed oriented knots and an $m$-singular knot $K$, its **finite difference** is
$$
D_mV(K):=\sum_{\epsilon\in\{+1,-1\}^m}\left(\prod_{i=1}^m\epsilon_i\right)V(K_\epsilon),
$$
where $K_\epsilon$ resolves singularity $i$ positively when $\epsilon_i=+1$ and negatively when $\epsilon_i=-1$. For $m=0$, set $D_0V(K)=V(K)$. The same formula defines $D_mV$ for an invariant of framed knots on framed singular knots, counting both double points and framing zeroes among the $m$ singularities. For one double point it reads $V(K_+)-V(K_-)$; the zero resolutions use the local normal-section model just specified. An invariant has **type at most $n$** if $D_{n+1}V$ vanishes on every $(n+1)$-singular object in its category. It has **exact type $n$** if it has type at most $n$ but not type at most $n-1$. Type at most $-1$ means the zero invariant, so a nonzero type-at-most-zero invariant has exact type zero. For an unframed link, replace $S^1$ by a finite disjoint union of oriented circles, allow marked self- or mixed double points, and use the same resolution sum. For a framed link, take a normal section on each component and count its simple zeroes together with all double points.

SUPPLIER def-zero-surgery-on-a-knot
Assume countable choice as required by the chosen tubular-neighborhood and peripheral-system data. Let K be a smooth oriented knot with compact exterior E_K, positive meridian mu_K, and preferred longitude lambda_K on the boundary torus. The zero-framed surgery manifold M(K) is the closed oriented 3-manifold obtained by gluing D²×S¹ to E_K so that the meridian boundary circle of D²×{1} is identified with lambda_K and the core {0}×S¹ is identified with mu_K. The orientation extends the orientation of E_K across the filling. Thus the filling slope is the preferred zero-linking longitude; the definition asserts no 4-manifold bounded by M(K).

SUPPLIER lem-choice-free-wall-chain-finiteness-from-explicit-domination
Work in ZF. Let $A$ be any associative unital ring and let $C$ be a chain complex of unital right $A$-modules with $C_n=0$ for $n<0$. Supply a chain complex $F$ with $F_n=0$ outside $0\le n\le m$, each $F_n$ supplied as a finite free right $A$-module, chain maps $f:C\to F$ and $g:F\to C$, and right $A$-linear maps $h_n:C_n\to C_{n+1}$ such that
$$1_C-gf=d_Ch+hd_C.$$
Then there is a chain complex $E$ of finitely generated projective right $A$-modules, zero outside $0\le n\le N$ for some finite $N$, together with explicitly constructed chain maps $a:C\to E$, $b:E\to C$ and chain homotopies
$$1_C-ba=dH+Hd,\qquad 1_E-ab=dL+Ld.$$
The modules of $E$ below its top degree can be taken finite free, and its top module is the image of an explicit idempotent on $\bigoplus_{j=0}^mF_j$.

Neither freeness nor projectivity nor finite generation of the modules of $C$ is required. Thus the result applies in particular to the nonnegative singular or cellular free chain complexes with specified bases. More generally it applies after reindexing whenever $C$ and $F$ have a common finite lower bound. It makes no assertion for inputs unbounded below and no assertion that $E$ is finite free or that any underlying space has finite CW type. No form of the Axiom of Choice is assumed.

SUPPLIER lem-compact-connected-manifold-fundamental-group-has-finite-generators-in-zf
Let $M$ be a compact connected Hausdorff topological manifold and $o\in M$. There is a finite based graph mapped continuously into $M$ such that its induced fundamental-group map is onto. Consequently $\pi_1(M,o)$ has a finite generating list, without a choice principle. No triangulation or smooth structure on $M$ is assumed.

SUPPLIER lem-compact-manifold-twisted-singular-chains-admit-one-vertex-reduction
Let $M$ be a compact connected Hausdorff manifold, $o\in M$, and $R$ a unital ring. One can construct paths $\gamma_x$ from $o$ to every $x\in M$, with $\gamma_o$ constant, by fixed rules from one finite atlas and finitely many connecting paths. Let $\mathcal R$ be a right-$R$ local system with a supplied identification $\mathcal R_o=R$ and transport given by left multiplication by units, with monodromy image $H\le R^\times$. Its twisted singular complex has an explicit chain decomposition $C_*(M;\mathcal R)=C'_*\oplus Q_*$ after a change of free bases, where $C'_0=R$, $C'_* $ is free, and $Q_*$ is contractible with its only nonzero differential an identity from degree one to degree zero. Moreover
$$\operatorname{im}(d'_1)=\sum_{h\in H}(h-1)R.$$
The decomposition and its dual cochain homotopy equivalences require no choice principle. In particular this image is principal if $H$ is cyclic, if it equals $R$, or if every right ideal of $R$ is principal.

SUPPLIER lem-equivariant-intersection-pairings-are-preserved-by-collared-bordism-gluing
Let $W,X$ be compact oriented topological four-manifolds with supplied boundary collars and decompositions $\partial W=A\sqcup D$, $\partial X=A\sqcup B$. Glue their copies of $A$ by a supplied orientation-reversing identification, obtaining $Y=W\cup_A X$ with $\partial Y=D\sqcup B$. Supply one ambient rank-one right-$R$ local system on $Y$, where $R$ is an involutive unital ring. On each connected component supply a base-fiber identification with $R$ and unitary monodromy, and use its transported standard Hermitian form. Use its restrictions and the induced orientations on $W,X$.

For the intersection pairings defined by inverse relative cap duality, passage to absolute cohomology and evaluation,
$$\lambda_Y(i_*p,i_*q)=\lambda_W(p,q)\qquad(p,q\in H_2(W;R)).$$
In particular all isotropic and dual-pair identities for homology classes in $W$ persist under their inclusion in $Y$. The result applies to topological manifolds and requires no geometric surface transversality or choice principle.

SUPPLIER lem-finite-von-neumann-module-dimension-and-dense-quotients
Assume AC. Let $G$ be a discrete group, let $A=\mathcal N G\subseteq\mathcal B(\ell^2(G))$ have its canonical faithful normal trace $\tau$, and give matrices the unnormalized trace $\tau_r((a_{ij}))=\sum_{i=1}^r\tau(a_{ii})$. For a projection $p\in M_r(A)$ put $\dim_A(pA^r)=\tau_r(p)$; for any right $A$-module take the supremum of dimensions of its finitely generated projective submodules. These dimensions are independent of the projective representation. If $L\subseteq pA^r$ is any submodule and $q$ is the projection onto its Hilbert closure in $p\ell^2(G)^r$, then $q\in M_r(A)$, $q\le p$, and
$$\dim_A L=\tau_r(q),\qquad \dim_A(pA^r/L)=\tau_r(p)-\tau_r(q).$$
In particular a Hilbert-dense submodule has dimension-zero quotient. Modules of dimension zero are closed under submodules, quotients, extensions and filtered colimits. For any matrix $B\in M_{r,s}(A)$, its kernel and Hilbert-closed image are projection modules, its algebraic image is isomorphic to its support projection module, and
$$\tau_s(P_{\ker B})+\tau_r(P_{\overline{\operatorname{im}B}})=s.$$

SUPPLIER lem-hecke-bracket-trace-dictionary-and-local-descent
Let $A$ be an indeterminate, $R_A=\mathbb Z[A^{\pm1},(A^{-4}+1)^{-1}]$,

A knot is a smooth embedding $K:S^1\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\times\mathbb R$ with the orientation of $S^1$. Let $\mathcal A_n^{\mathrm{fr}}$ be the rational vector space on these diagrams modulo 4T, and let $\mathcal A_n^{\mathrm{un}}$ be its quotient by 1T as well. A 4T relation fixes one chord $pq$ and another chord endpoint $r$, moves the other endpoint of the second chord to just before or after $p$ and just before or after $q$, and takes the alternating sum of those four diagrams. A chord is isolated when it intersects no other chord, where intersection means that chord endpoints alternate around the circle; 1T sets every such diagram to zero. $$
Z(K):=\sum_{m\ge0}\frac1{(2\pi i)^m}\int_{t_{\min}<t_m<\cdots<t_1<t_{\max}}\ \sum_{P}(-1)^{\downarrow(P)}[D_P]_{\mathrm{un}}\bigwedge_{j=1}^m\frac{dz_j-dz'_j}{z_j-z'_j}\ \in\widehat{\mathcal A}^{\mathrm{un}}_{\mathbb C}.
$$
The ordered simplex is divided into chambers by the critical values of $t|_K$. In each chamber, $P$ ranges over choices, at each level $t_j$, of an unordered pair of distinct points $(z_j,t_j),(z'_j,t_j)$ of $K$. The diagram $D_P$ joins the corresponding preimages on the oriented parameter circle. The integer $\downarrow(P)$ counts endpoints at which $t$ decreases along the orientation of $K$. The differential form is pulled back to the chamber by the selected local branches. The degree-zero term is the empty diagram with coefficient $1$; the displayed integrals are interpreted as improper integrals on each chamber.

$$\Omega_q=\frac1{2\pi i}\sum_{1\le i<j\le q}t_{ij}\,d\log(z_i-z_j).$$

$$1+\sum_{m\ge1}\int_{0<t_m<\cdots<t_1<1}\gamma^*\Omega_q(t_1)\cdots\gamma^*\Omega_q(t_m).$$
A tangential endpoint is a limiting collision configuration together with a specified nonzero first-order direction of approach for each colliding cluster. When endpoints have such clusters prescribed by the parenthesizations, the holonomy is the constant term after removing the local logarithmic factors $\exp(t_{ij}\log\varepsilon/(2\pi i))$ at each tangential endpoint. A change between the two parenthesizations of three consecutive strands is encoded by the regularized transport $\Phi_{\mathrm{KZ}}$.

Literal claim:
Let $W$ be a compact connected oriented topological four-manifold with a supplied boundary collar, and put $M=\partial W$. Let $R\subseteq K$ be a unital involution-stable subring of an involutive division ring. Supply a based rank-one right-$R$ coefficient system $\mathcal R$ on $W$, with its base fiber identified with $R$, unitary monodromy and the transported standard Hermitian form. Write $H_*(W;R)$ and $H_*(W;K)$ for its homology and scalar-extended homology.

Define the equivariant intersection pairing $\lambda_R$ by sending $\bar p\in\overline{H_2(W;R)}$ through inverse relative cap duality to $H^2(W,M;\mathcal R^\vee)$, then into $H^2(W;\mathcal R^\vee)$, then evaluating on $q\in H_2(W;R)$. Here $\mathcal R^\vee$ is the intrinsic dual coefficient system, identified with the conjugate system by the supplied Hermitian form. Thus $\lambda_R(p,q)$ is conjugate-linear in $p$ and right-linear in $q$. Define $\lambda_K$ by the same construction over $K$.

Suppose $m\ge0$ and classes $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_m,d_1,\ldots,d_m\in H_2(W;R)$ satisfy
$$\lambda_R(\ell_i,\ell_j)=0,\qquad\lambda_R(\ell_i,d_j)=\lambda_R(d_j,\ell_i)=\delta_{ij},$$
and suppose
$$\dim_K\bigl(H_2(W;K)/\operatorname{im}H_2(M;K)\bigr)\le2m.$$
Let $M_0$ be one connected boundary component with $H_1(M_0;K)=0$. Its coefficient Bockstein, oriented cap duality and evaluation define the linking form $B_0$ with values in $K/R$. Then
$$B_0(x,y)=0\quad\text{for all }x,y\in\ker\{H_1(M_0;R)\longrightarrow H_1(W;R)\}.$$
No vanishing condition on the other boundary components is imposed. No choice axiom, finite CW structure, or general noncommutative universal-coefficient theorem is assumed.
Relevant declared Facts:
**Given:** The manifolds, collar, rings, unitary coefficient system, finite list of intersection pairs and dimension bound in the Statement.

[F1] Oriented relative cap duality holds for a compact collared manifold with arbitrary abelian-group local systems and commuting scalar actions; closed cap duality holds on its boundary. [[thm-compact-collared-manifold-local-coefficient-lefschetz-duality-in-zf]], [[thm-compact-oriented-manifold-local-coefficient-duality-in-zf]]

[F2] Supplied standard parallel Hermitian structures identify intrinsic dual coefficient systems with conjugate right systems. The cochain identification is simplexwise, so restriction to a subspace and the relative cochain subcomplex preserve it. [[lem-involutive-twisted-chain-duals-realize-local-coefficient-duality]]

[F3] A compact connected closed manifold has a choice-free one-vertex right-chain model with degree-zero term its coefficient ring. The low-degree coefficient Bockstein is natural and exact, without lifting an infinite product of quotient fibers. [[lem-compact-manifold-twisted-singular-chains-admit-one-vertex-reduction]], [[lem-choice-free-low-degree-coefficient-bockstein-on-compact-manifolds]]
