SUPPLIER def-ip-gaussian-sequence-inverse-model
Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice. Let $X$ and $Y$ be real Hilbert spaces and let $K:X\to Y$ be a compact linear operator of infinite rank, viewed as an inverse model with exact datum $Kx$. Here compact means that the image of every bounded subset has compact closure. Write $N(K)=\{x\in X:Kx=0\}$ and $H_0=\overline{\operatorname{ran}K}$. Fix a positive singular system $(s_j,v_j,u_j)_{j\in\mathbb N}$ for $K$, where $\mathbb N=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}$, $s_1\ge s_2\ge\cdots>0$, $s_j\to0$, $Kv_j=s_ju_j$, $(v_j)$ is a complete orthonormal basis of $N(K)^\perp$, and $(u_j)$ is a complete orthonormal basis of $H_0$. A complete orthonormal basis is an orthonormal family whose closed linear span is the whole indicated subspace. These are the compact SVD conventions; in particular, this model concerns minimum-norm representatives in $N(K)^\perp$ and does not estimate a kernel component.

For a truth $x\in N(K)^\perp$, let $\theta_j=\langle x,v_j\rangle_X$. The complete-basis coefficient map identifies $N(K)^\perp$ bijectively and isometrically with $\ell^2(\mathbb N,\mathbb R)$, so every $\theta\in\ell^2(\mathbb N,\mathbb R)$ is a model parameter. For $\epsilon>0$, take the functional white-noise experiment on the separable Hilbert space $H_0$ with observations $Y_x(g)=\langle Kx,g\rangle_Y+\epsilon W(g)$, where $W$ is centered, has covariance $\mathbb E[W(g)W(h)]=\langle g,h\rangle_Y$, and has independent standard-normal values on every finite orthonormal tuple. The countable complete basis makes $H_0$ separable. Define $\xi_j=W(u_j)$ and $Z_j=Y_x(u_j)/s_j$. The sequence sample space is $\mathbb R^{\mathbb N}$ with the product Borel sigma-algebra, meaning the sigma-algebra generated by its coordinate projections. The coordinate law is the pushforward of the functional experiment under $y\mapsto(y(u_j)/s_j)_{j\in\mathbb N}$; this map is measurable because each coordinate is a cylinder evaluation followed by scalar multiplication. The compact SVD expansion gives $\langle Kx,u_j\rangle_Y=s_j\theta_j$, and the positive-noise white-noise coordinate theorem gives the independent standard-normal coordinates $\xi_j$. Hence the normalized coordinates satisfy $Z_j=\theta_j+\epsilon s_j^{-1}\xi_j,\qquad j\in\mathbb N.$

For the zero-noise boundary $\epsilon=0$, define the sequence experiment separately by the point mass at $\theta$, so $Z_j=\theta_j$. This boundary is not an instance of the positive-noise functional experiment.

SUPPLIER def-ip-isonormal-white-noise-experiment
Let $H$ be a separable real Hilbert space, where separable means having a countable dense subset, and let $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)$ be a probability space. A real isonormal Gaussian process on $H$ is a family $Z(g)$ of real random variables with finite second moments, indexed by $g\in H$, such that $g\mapsto[Z(g)]$ is linear into $L^2(\mathbb P)$, $\mathbb E Z(g)=0$, and $\mathbb E[Z(g)Z(h)]=\langle g,h\rangle_H$. It is also required that for every finite orthonormal tuple $(e_1,\ldots,e_m)$ the variables $Z(e_1),\ldots,Z(e_m)$ are independent and each has density $\varphi(t)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-t^2/2}$. For any finite list of vectors, expressing them in an orthonormal basis of their finite-dimensional span shows that their process values have the corresponding linear-image Gaussian law. Here $L^2(\mathbb P)$ identifies square-integrable random variables that agree almost surely. When $H\ne\{0\}$, all unit-vector marginals have the same law; write $N(a,\sigma^2)$ for the law of $a+\sigma Z(e)$ for any unit vector $e\in H$ and $\sigma\ge0$.

Let $X$ be a set, let $\Theta\subseteq X$ be a nonempty parameter set, let $K:X\to H$ be a signal map, and let $\epsilon>0$. For $x\in\Theta$ and $g\in H$, define the observation functional

$$Y_x(g)=\langle Kx,g\rangle_H+\epsilon Z(g).$$

The associated statistical inverse experiment has sample space $\mathbb R^H$ with the cylinder sigma-algebra generated by the evaluation maps $y\mapsto y(g)$, and its sampling law $P_x$ is the law of the random function $g\mapsto Y_x(g)$. Each sample is indexed by test functionals on $H$; it is not asserted to be a vector in $H$ or to have a continuous path. If $H=\{0\}$, linearity forces $Z(0)=0$ and the experiment is deterministic.

SUPPLIER def-ip-learned-reconstruction-estimator
Let $(X,d_X)$ and $(Y,d_Y)$ be metric parameter and data spaces, let $U\subseteq X$ be a nonempty admissible truth class, let $F:U\to Y$ be a forward map, and let $(Q,d_Q)$ be a metric target space with a measurable target map $q:U\to Q$. The target $q(x)$ is the recovery object assigned to truth $x$; $Q$ may be the parameter space itself or a quotient solution-class space. All metric spaces in this definition carry their Borel sigma-algebras. A **point reconstruction rule** is a Borel map $g:Y\to Q$, and its output at data $y$ is $g(y)$. A **learned point estimator** is such a map $\widehat q_\phi$ with a fitted parameter $\phi$ in a specified parameter set. A complete training description names the probability law $P_{\rm fit}$ of all variables used to fit $\phi$; those variables may be paired or unpaired. A complete test description names a probability law $P_{\rm test}$ for evaluation pairs $(Y,q(X))$. No equality between fitting and test laws is assumed.

A finite **unrolled learned update** consists of an integer $K\geq1$, a Borel initialization $u_0:Y\to Q$, and Borel maps $T_{\phi,k}:Q\times Y\to Q$ for $0\leq k<K$, with $u_{k+1}(y)=T_{\phi,k}(u_k(y),y)$. Its output $u_K(y)$ is a point reconstruction rule. An **amortized inverse map** is a learned point estimator applied to multiple data inputs after a single fit; this term adds a reuse convention, not an accuracy guarantee.

For a real Hilbert signal space $H$, a **denoiser** is a Borel map $D_\phi:H\to H$ trained under a specified signal-corruption law and used to transform a corrupted signal into a signal estimate or an update. A denoiser is the exact minimum mean-square error map only when it equals the conditional mean for the specified signal and corruption laws. A **learned prior** is a supplied Borel probability measure $\rho_\phi$ on $H$; one construction is the pushforward law $\rho_\phi(A)=\nu(G_\phi^{-1}(A))$ of a specified probability $\nu$ under a Borel generator $G_\phi:Z\to H$. The law or generator alone is not a data-to-target estimator.

In a finite-dimensional signal space $\mathbb R^d$, a **score field model** is a Borel map $s_\phi:[0,T]\times\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^d$ intended to approximate $\nabla_x\log p_t(x)$ for a specified family of positive continuously differentiable densities $p_t$. A score field is not itself a posterior law, posterior sample, or point reconstruction rule. A **posterior sampler** is a probability kernel $S:Y\to Q$; relative to a supplied posterior target kernel $\Pi_y$, it is exact on a data domain $D$ when $S_y=\Pi_y$ as probability measures for every $y\in D$. A draw from $S_y$ is random; obtaining a point estimate from it requires an additional summary rule. The name “learned” by itself asserts no exact recovery, stability, convergence, calibration, or train-to-test guarantee.

SUPPLIER def-ip-misfit-evidence-and-posterior-law
Let $X$ be a separable real Banach space with its norm topology, $D\subseteq X$ a Borel forward-map domain, and $\mu_0$ a Borel probability measure on $X$ with $\mu_0(D)=1$. Write $\mu_{0,D}$ for its restriction to $D$, so $\mu_{0,D}(A)=\mu_0(A)$ for $A\in\mathcal B(D):=\{D\cap B:B\in\mathcal B(X)\}$. Let $(\mathsf Y,\mathcal Y)$ be a measurable data space. A likelihood potential is a function $\Phi:D\times\mathsf Y\to(-\infty,+\infty]$ that is measurable from $\bigl(D\times\mathsf Y,\mathcal B(D)\otimes\mathcal Y\bigr)$ to $\bigl((-\infty,+\infty],\mathcal B((-\infty,+\infty])\bigr)$, where $\mathcal B((-\infty,+\infty])$ is the trace of $\mathcal B(\overline{\mathbb R})$ on $(-\infty,+\infty]$. Viewing $\Phi$ via the inclusion $(-\infty,+\infty]\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb R}$, it is also product-measurable as an extended-real-valued map because for every Borel set $B\subseteq\overline{\mathbb R}$, $\Phi^{-1}(B)=\Phi^{-1}(B\cap(-\infty,+\infty])$ is measurable by the definition of the trace sigma-algebra. Here $+\infty$ is allowed and $-\infty$ is excluded; set $e^{-\infty}=0$. The scalar map $t\mapsto e^{-t}$ on this range is continuous at finite $t$, and is continuous at $+\infty$: given $\varepsilon>0$, the limit supplies $a\in\mathbb R$ such that $t>a$ implies $e^{-t}<\varepsilon$, and the order-topology neighborhood $(a,+\infty]$ maps into $[0,\varepsilon)$. Hence it is Borel, so $L_\Phi:D\times\mathsf Y\to[0,+\infty)$, $L_\Phi(u;y):=e^{-\Phi(u;y)}$, is product-measurable by composition, and each section $u\mapsto L_\Phi(u;y)$ is nonnegative and measurable. Define its evidence by
$$
Z_\Phi(y):=\int_D L_\Phi(u;y)\,\mu_{0,D}(du)\in[0,+\infty].
$$
For each fixed $y$ with $0<Z_\Phi(y)<+\infty$, define the normalized fiberwise update on $A\in\mathcal B(D)$ by
$$\Pi^y(A):=\frac{\int_A L_\Phi(u;y)\,\mu_{0,D}(du)}{Z_\Phi(y)}.$$
No normalized fiberwise update is defined by this formula when $Z_\Phi(y)=0$ or $Z_\Phi(y)=+\infty$.

For a data-indexed claim, an observation model and its version convention must also be specified. In the dominated case, let $Q$ be a proper probability kernel from $(D,\mathcal B(D))$ to $(\mathsf Y,\mathcal Y)$, let $\nu$ be a sigma-finite measure on $(\mathsf Y,\mathcal Y)$, and suppose $Q(u,B)=\int_B L_\Phi(u;y)\,\nu(dy)$ for every $u\in D$ and $B\in\mathcal Y$. Then $Z_\Phi$ is the density of the prior-predictive data law with respect to $\nu$; a data-indexed conditional-law version uses $\Pi^y$ when $0<Z_\Phi(y)<\infty$ and the prior $\mu_{0,D}$ when $Z_\Phi(y)$ is zero or infinite. These filled fibers have zero prior-predictive mass, and their chosen values are not normalized fiberwise updates. More generally, for a proper Bayesian model with standard-Borel parameter space $(D,\mathcal B(D))$ and data space $(\mathsf Y,\mathcal Y)$ (each measurably isomorphic to the Borel space of a Polish space), the Axiom of Choice guarantees a data-indexed posterior kernel; its values are unique as probability measures outside one data-marginal-null set, and values on that null set remain version choices. The dominated construction is choice-free; the separate standard-Borel existence branch assumes the Axiom of Choice.

SUPPLIER def-ip-nonlinear-tikhonov-functional
Let $X$ and $Y$ be real Hilbert spaces, let $D\subseteq X$ be a nonempty domain, and let $F:D\to Y$ be a forward map. For a regularization parameter $\alpha>0$, reference element $x_0\in X$, and datum $y\in Y$, the **nonlinear Tikhonov functional** is the map $J_{\alpha,y,x_0}:D\to[0,\infty)$ defined by

$$
J_{\alpha,y,x_0}(x):=\frac12\|F(x)-y\|_Y^2+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|x-x_0\|_X^2\qquad(x\in D).
$$

A **global minimizer** is a point $x_\alpha\in D$ such that $J_{\alpha,y,x_0}(x_\alpha)\le J_{\alpha,y,x_0}(x)$ for every $x\in D$. This definition asserts neither existence nor uniqueness.

SUPPLIER def-ip-power-source-set
Assume Countable Choice. Let $K:X\to Y$ be compact between real or complex Hilbert spaces over the same scalar field and let $\nu,\rho>0$. The power source ball is $X_{\nu,\rho}(K):=\{|K|^\nu w:w\in X,\ \|w\|_X\le\rho\}$, where $|K|^\nu=(K^*K)^{\nu/2}$ is defined by compact spectral calculus. It lies in $N(K)^\perp$ and consists of minimum-norm representatives of the exact data $Kx$. In the convention $S=K^*K$ this is the source condition $x=S^{\nu/2}w$.

SUPPLIER def-ip-simple-surface-and-geodesic-xray-transform
Let $(M,g)$ be a compact, connected, nonempty smooth two-dimensional Riemannian manifold with smooth boundary. For $x\in\partial M$, let $\nu_{\mathrm{out}}(x)$ be the outward unit normal. The boundary is **strictly convex** if
$$
g(\nabla_w\nu_{\mathrm{out}},w)>0
$$
for every $x\in\partial M$ and every nonzero $w\in T_x\partial M$. A **Jacobi field** along a unit-speed geodesic $\gamma$ is a smooth vector field $J$ along $\gamma$ satisfying
$$
D_t^2J+R(J,\dot\gamma)\dot\gamma=0,
$$
where $R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_X\nabla_YZ-\nabla_Y\nabla_XZ-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z$. The manifold has **no conjugate points** if, on every unit-speed geodesic segment $\gamma:[a,b]\to M$, a Jacobi field with $J(a)=J(b)=0$ must be identically zero. It is **nontrapping** if every maximal unit-speed geodesic starting in $M^\circ$ reaches $\partial M$ in finite forward and backward time. The pair $(M,g)$ is a **simple surface** if its boundary is strictly convex, it has no conjugate points, and it is nontrapping.

The unit tangent bundle is $SM=\{(x,v):x\in M,\ v\in T_xM,\ g_x(v,v)=1\}$. Write $\nu_{\mathrm{in}}=-\nu_{\mathrm{out}}$ and
$$
\partial_+SM=\{(x,v)\in SM:x\in\partial M,\ g_x(v,\nu_{\mathrm{in}})>0\}
$$
for inward-pointing unit boundary vectors. For $(x,v)\in\partial_+SM$, let $\gamma_{x,v}$ be the unit-speed geodesic with $\gamma_{x,v}(0)=x$ and $\dot\gamma_{x,v}(0)=v$, and let $\tau_+(x,v)$ be its first positive exit time from $M$. For $f\in C^\infty(M)$, its scalar **geodesic X-ray transform** is the function on $\partial_+SM$ defined by
$$
I_0f(x,v)=\int_0^{\tau_+(x,v)} f(\gamma_{x,v}(t))\,dt.
$$
The geodesic equation is interpreted up to first boundary exit; local smooth extensions of the metric are used only to solve the coordinate ODE at boundary initial data.

SUPPLIER def-ip-statistical-ellipsoid-class
Let $Q\in[0,\infty)$ and let $a=(a_j)_{j\in\mathbb N}$ be a nondecreasing sequence of positive real numbers, so $0<a_1\le a_2\le\cdots$. The statistical ellipsoid of radius $Q$ and weights $a$ in the Gaussian sequence inverse model is
$$\Theta_a(Q)=\left\{\theta\in\ell^2(\mathbb N,\mathbb R):\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_j^2\theta_j^2\le Q^2\right\}.$$
No assumption that $a_j\to\infty$ is included. In particular, this definition does not assert that the ellipsoid is compact in $\ell^2$.

SUPPLIER lem-ip-bv-coarea-formula
Assume Countable Choice. Let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ be open, let $u\in BV(\Omega)$, and for $t>0$ set $E_t^+:=\{x\in\Omega:u(x)>t\}$ while for $t<0$ set $E_t^-:=\{x\in\Omega:u(x)<t\}$. Then for almost every applicable $t$ these sets have finite perimeter relative to $\Omega$, and $$TV(u;\Omega)=\int_0^\infty P(E_t^+;\Omega)\,dt+\int_{-\infty}^0P(E_t^-;\Omega)\,dt.$$

SUPPLIER lem-ip-bv-compactness-lower-semicontinuity
Assume Countable Choice. Let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ be a bounded Lipschitz domain, meaning a bounded open set whose boundary has a finite cover by Lipschitz graph neighborhoods with $\Omega$ on one side of each graph. Total variation is lower semicontinuous under $L^1(\Omega)$ convergence of BV functions. Every sequence $(u_k)\subset BV(\Omega)$ satisfying $$\sup_k\bigl(\|u_k\|_{L^1(\Omega)}+TV(u_k;\Omega)\bigr)<\infty$$ has a subsequence converging in $L^1(\Omega)$ to a function in $BV(\Omega)$.

SUPPLIER lem-ip-bv-extension-and-translation
Assume Countable Choice. Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^n$ be a bounded Lipschitz domain, meaning a bounded open set whose boundary has a finite cover by Lipschitz graph neighborhoods with $\Omega$ on one side of each graph. The zero-extension map is bounded from $BV(\Omega)$ to $BV(\mathbb R^n)$, with support in $\overline\Omega$, and there is a finite $C_\Omega$ such that $$\|\widetilde u\|_{L^1(\mathbb R^n)}+TV(\widetilde u;\mathbb R^n)\le C_\Omega\bigl(\|u\|_{L^1(\Omega)}+TV(u;\Omega)\bigr)$$ for every $u\in BV(\Omega)$. For every $v\in BV(\mathbb R^n)$ and $h\in\mathbb R^n$, $$\|v(\cdot+h)-v\|_{L^1(\mathbb R^n)}\le |h|TV(v;\mathbb R^n).$$

Here $\mathcal H^m$ on a boundary denotes normalized Hausdorff measure: the repository's unnormalised $m$-dimensional Hausdorff measure divided by $c_m=H^m((0,1]^m)$ for $m\ge1$, and counting measure for $m=0$. SUPPLIER lem-ip-calderon-boundary-trace-quotient-identification
Assume the Axiom of Choice and Countable Choice. Let $n\ge2$ and let $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^n$ be a nonempty bounded connected open set whose boundary is covered by finitely many $C^1$ graph charts. Give $H^1(\Omega;\mathbb K)=W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb K)$ its Sobolev norm, let $H_0^1(\Omega;\mathbb K)$ be the $H^1$-closure of $C_c^\infty(\Omega;\mathbb K)$, and let $W^{1/2,2}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb K)$ have the finite-chart Slobodeckij norm. Define $\mathcal D(\Omega):=H^1(\Omega;\mathbb K)/H_0^1(\Omega;\mathbb K)$ with its quotient norm. The trace induces a bounded linear bijection $\overline T:\mathcal D(\Omega)\to W^{1/2,2}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb K)$ with bounded inverse, so these two norms are equivalent under the induced identification. The induced map after quotienting by constants is also a bounded linear bijection $\mathcal D(\Omega)/\mathbb K\mathbf1\to W^{1/2,2}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb K)/\mathbb K$, with equivalent quotient norms. A continuous linear functional on $W^{1/2,2}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb K)$ descends to the constant quotient exactly when it annihilates constants, and its descended functional has the quotient dual norm.

SUPPLIER lem-ip-lipschitz-graph-area-and-calculus-under-countable-choice
Assume Countable Choice. Let $m\ge1$, let $Q\subseteq\mathbb R^m$ be open, and let $h:Q\to\mathbb R$ be locally Lipschitz. Then $h$ is differentiable almost everywhere, its partial derivatives are measurable and are its weak derivatives. On every open region where $h$ is $L$-Lipschitz, $|Dh|\le L$ almost everywhere. Let $H^m$ denote the repository's unnormalised Hausdorff measure, put $c_m=H^m((0,1]^m)$, and write $\sigma_m=c_m^{-1}H^m$ also on Euclidean spaces containing $m$-dimensional subsets. The graph map $F(y)=(y,h(y))$ satisfies, for every nonnegative Borel $a$,
$$\int_{F(Q)}a(x)\,d\sigma_m(x)=\int_Qa(F(y))\sqrt{1+|Dh(y)|^2}\,dy.$$
For the epigraph $G=\{(y,z):y\in Q,\ z>h(y)\}$, a smooth vector field compactly supported away from the chart sides satisfies
$$\int_G\operatorname{div}\phi\,dx=\int_Q\phi(y,h(y))\cdot(Dh(y),-1)\,dy.$$
Thus its outward normal is $(Dh,-1)/\sqrt{1+|Dh|^2}$ almost everywhere. The sign reverses for a subgraph. The graph surface measure is locally finite and Radon on $F(Q)$ with its relative topology, and its null sets coincide with graph images of Lebesgue-null subsets of the base.

SUPPLIER thm-ip-2d-simple-surface-xray-injectivity

SUPPLIER thm-ip-function-space-posterior-propriety

Let the input be an initial coefficient $q_{\rm init}\in Q_h$, data $y^\delta\in Y_h$, noise bound $\delta\ge0$, penalty $\alpha>0$, state and adjoint residual tolerances $\tau_u,\tau_p\ge0$, discrepancy factor $\kappa\ge1$, stationarity tolerance $\tau_g\ge0$, scaling parameter $L>0$, and positive integer budgets $N_{\rm iter}$, $N_{\rm solve}$, $N_{\rm eval}$, and $N_{\rm backtrack}$. The global budget $N_{\rm solve}$ counts state and adjoint solves; $N_{\rm eval}$ counts exact projections and objective-interval evaluations. The scaling parameter $L$ is only a positive step-size scale; it is not assumed to be a Lipschitz constant. The algorithm returns a coefficient, one status from `converged`, `discrepancy-stopped`, `linear-solve-failed`, `inadmissible`, or `budget-exhausted`, and any diagnostics certified at that coefficient.

1. If $q_{\rm init}\notin Q_h$ or the model's exact admissibility predicate is false at $q_{\rm init}$, return status `inadmissible` without solving a PDE. Otherwise set $q:=q_{\rm init}$.

2. For $k=0,\ldots,N_{\rm iter}$, first check the finite-time admissibility predicate, which includes membership in $Q_h$. If it is false, return `inadmissible`. If no global solve or evaluation budget remains, return `budget-exhausted`.

3. Call the state/adjoint solver at $q$ with tolerances $\tau_u,\tau_p$ and the remaining solve budget. If it returns `failure`, if either certified residual bound exceeds its declared tolerance, or if any required state/adjoint error estimate or derivative bound is missing or invalid, return `linear-solve-failed`. Otherwise receive $\widetilde g_h$, $\widetilde F_h$, $\eta_u,\eta_p,P,L_{J,u},L_{R,u},M_q,L_{G,u}$ and set $\varepsilon_g=(L_{J,u}+L_{R,u}P)\eta_u+M_q\eta_p$ and $\varepsilon_F=L_{G,u}\eta_u$.

4. Use the exact projection routine to compute $P_{Q_h}(q-\widetilde g_h/L)$. If the routine fails or the evaluation budget is exhausted, return `budget-exhausted`. Set $\widetilde\rho:=L\|q-P_{Q_h}(q-\widetilde g_h/L)\|_2$. If $\widetilde\rho+\varepsilon_g\le\tau_g$, return `converged` with stationarity upper bound $\widetilde\rho+\varepsilon_g$. This status means only that the exact projected-gradient residual of the discrete objective is within tolerance; it is not a claim that an iterate sequence converges or that $q$ is a global minimizer.

5. Compute $d^+:=\|\widetilde F_h-y^\delta\|_{Y_h}+\varepsilon_F$. If $d^+\le\kappa\delta$, return `discrepancy-stopped` with data-residual upper bound $d^+$. This status certifies only the discrete model's data discrepancy.

6. If $k=N_{\rm iter}$, return `budget-exhausted` at the current coefficient with its current diagnostics. Otherwise, evaluate the current objective using its certified interval $[\ell_h(q),u_h(q)]$. If the evaluation fails because a required state or adjoint solve fails its contract, return `linear-solve-failed`; if the global solve or evaluation budget is exhausted, return `budget-exhausted`.

7. For $j=0,\ldots,N_{\rm backtrack}-1$, set $t_j=2^{-j}/L$ and use the exact projection routine to compute $q_j=P_{Q_h}(q-t_j\widetilde g_h)$. If projection fails or the evaluation budget is exhausted, return `budget-exhausted`. Reject this trial and continue to the next $j$ if $q_j=q$ or its admissibility predicate is false. Otherwise evaluate a certified interval $[\ell_h(q_j),u_h(q_j)]$. If evaluation fails because a required linear solve fails its contract, return `linear-solve-failed`; if it only exhausts the finite evaluation budget, return `budget-exhausted`. Accept the first trial with $u_h(q_j)\le\ell_h(q)$, set $q:=q_j$, and continue to the next outer iteration. This interval test guarantees $\Phi_h(q_j)\le\Phi_h(q)$.

8. If no trial is accepted within the backtracking budget, return `budget-exhausted` at the current coefficient. Each accepted trial uses one of the at most $N_{\rm iter}$ updates; the inclusive outer loop evaluates the final coefficient before returning budget exhaustion.

The global solve budget counts all primal and adjoint solves used both at iterates and in objective trials. The evaluation budget counts every projection and objective-interval call. Each status is returned after finitely many tests because all four budgets are finite and every model routine returns in finite time or reports failure. Any continuous-to-discrete mesh bound is reported separately by the model contract and is never inferred from the algebraic state or adjoint residuals.

Assume the Axiom of Choice and Countable Choice. Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a nonempty bounded connected smooth domain, and let $\gamma\in\Gamma_{m,M}(\Omega)$ be a real almost-everywhere class with $0<m\le\gamma\le M<\infty$. Well-posedness, independence from the representative $V_g$, boundedness and the induced quotient map are established by the well-posedness result cited below.

CONSUMER def-ip-simple-riemannian-background
Supplier edges: def-ip-simple-surface-and-geodesic-xray-transform
(No named supplier use in literal Facts; declared-context edge only at this scope.)
Literal Definition/Statement in absence of Facts:
Let $n\ge2$ and let $(M,g)$ be a compact, connected, nonempty smooth $n$-dimensional Riemannian manifold with smooth boundary. Let $\nu_{\mathrm{out}}$ be the outward unit normal and let $\nabla$ be the Levi–Civita connection of $g$. The boundary is **strictly convex** when
$$
g(\nabla_w\nu_{\mathrm{out}},w)>0
$$
for every boundary point and every nonzero boundary-tangent vector $w$. Use the curvature convention
$$
R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_X\nabla_YZ-\nabla_Y\nabla_XZ-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z.
$$
A Jacobi field along a unit-speed geodesic $\gamma$ is a smooth vector field $J$ satisfying $D_t^2J+R(J,\dot\gamma)\dot\gamma=0$, where $D_tJ=\nabla_{\dot\gamma}J$. The manifold has **no conjugate points** if every positive-length unit-speed geodesic segment $\gamma:[a,b]\to M$ has no nonzero Jacobi field with $J(a)=J(b)=0$. It is **nontrapping** if every maximal unit-speed geodesic starting in $M^\circ$ reaches $\partial M$ in finite forward and backward time. The pair $(M,g)$ is a **simple Riemannian background** if its boundary is strictly convex, it has no conjugate points, and it is nontrapping.

When $n=2$, this is the simple-surface convention used for the scalar geodesic X-ray transform on the required inverse-problems page.
