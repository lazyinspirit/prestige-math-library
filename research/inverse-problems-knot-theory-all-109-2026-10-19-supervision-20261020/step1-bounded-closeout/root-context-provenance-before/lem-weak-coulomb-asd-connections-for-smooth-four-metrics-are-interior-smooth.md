## Statement

Assume full AC. There are constants $\eta,\delta>0$ with the following property. Let $g$ be a smooth Riemannian metric on the Euclidean unit four-ball with $\|g-I\|_{C^0}<\eta$. Let $a\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(B_1;\mathfrak{su}(2)\otimes\mathbb R^4)$ satisfy the EUCLIDEAN Coulomb condition $d_e^*a=0$ and either $F_a^{+,g}=0$ or $F_a^{-,g}=0$ distributionally. Then $a$ has a smooth representative in $B_1$.

There is also a uniform interior conclusion. If a family of such metrics is bounded in every fixed $C^j$ norm on compact subballs and the forms satisfy $\|a\|_{L^4(B_1)}\le\delta$, the forms are bounded in every fixed $C^j$ norm on each compact subball, by constants depending only on that subball and the corresponding finite metric bounds. No initial bound on higher derivatives of $a$ is required.

There are also $\epsilon>0$ and constants $C_M$ such that every smooth connection $A$ near $\overline B_1$ with $F_A^{\pm,g}=0$, $\|F_A\|_{L^2(B_1)}\le\epsilon$, and $\|g\|_{C^1(B_1)}\le M$ satisfies $\|F_A\|_{L^\infty(B_{1/2})}\le C_M\|F_A\|_{L^2(B_1)}$. Curvature norms in this assertion are Euclidean coefficient norms; $\epsilon$ is independent of $A$. On a ball of radius $r$ the corresponding bound is $r^2\|F_A\|_\infty\le C_M\|F_A\|_2$, with $M$ a bound for the rescaled metric.

Consequently the assertion applies in sufficiently small normal-coordinate balls for ANY smooth Riemannian four-manifold, after dilation, whenever its small-curvature construction supplies the Euclidean Coulomb gauge in those coordinates. It supplies local regularity and interior estimates in that given gauge, not point removal, charge quantization or global compactness.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Full AC, the stated metric and weak connection. All Sobolev norms in a coordinate ball are Euclidean coefficient norms.

[F1] The full proof1.1–3.1 of the Euclidean weak-ASD theorem constructs a constant first-order real system $D=\sum C_j\partial_j$, with $D^*D=DD^*=-\Delta I$, and $K=D^*N$, where $Nf=(4\pi^2|x|^2)^{-1}*f$. For compact data the actual potential bounds give $K:L^2\to L^4$ and $K:L^3\to L^{12}$. Its differentiated multipliers are finite sums of products of Riesz transforms, so $\|\nabla Kf\|_p\le A_p\|f\|_p$ for $1<p<\infty$. In particular $\|\nabla v\|_p\le A_p\|Dv\|_p$ for smooth compact $v$, and compact $W^{1,2}$ functions satisfy $v=K(Dv)$. These are actual proved operator formulas, not an application of a Euclidean ASD Statement to a curved metric. ([[lem-weak-euclidean-asd-coulomb-connections-are-interior-smooth]], [[lem-small-critical-drift-poisson-gradient-inverse]], [[cor-riesz-transforms-are-bounded-on-lp]])

[F2] Holder, completeness, contraction, absolute continuity of the integral and the actual four-dimensional Sobolev embeddings are available. They include $W^{1,2}\hookrightarrow L^4$ and $W^{1,6}\hookrightarrow C^{0,\alpha}$ on smaller balls. ([[thm-holder-inequality-for-integrals]], [[thm-riesz-fischer-completeness-of-l-p]], [[thm-banach-fixed-point]], [[thm-absolute-continuity-of-the-integral]], [[thm-higher-order-sobolev-embedding]], [[thm-extension-theorem-for-bounded-smooth-domains]])

[F3] Compact cutoffs, smooth Sobolev approximation and convolution approximate identities, including commutation with distributional constant derivatives, are proved. [F4] The actual small-curvature ball construction gives a Euclidean Coulomb gauge $a=A^u$ with $\|a\|_4+\|a\|_{W^{1,2}}\le C_0\|F_A\|_2$, and $F_a=u^{-1}F_Au$. It applies to every smooth connection, not just Euclidean ASD ones. Unitarity preserves pointwise coefficient norms and commutes with the Hodge projection of any fixed base metric. ## Proof

**Given:** Full AC and the data in the Statement. It suffices to prove the plus-projection case: reflecting one Euclidean coordinate preserves Coulomb gauge and all coefficient norms, reverses orientation and interchanges the two projections.

1.1 Express the variable equation without assuming a curved parametrix. For $g$ near $I$, restriction of the $g$-self-dual projection to the fixed Euclidean self-dual three-space is invertible onto its image: at $I$ it is the identity and its matrix inverse is a convergent finite Neumann series nearby. Use this restriction to identify its target with that fixed space. The three component equations for $\pi_{+,g}(da+a\wedge a)=0$, together with $d_e^*a=0$, become $$Da=\sum_j E_j(x)\partial_j a+B_g(x,a)a.$$ Here $D$ is exactly [F1]'s Euclidean system, $E_j=0$ at $g=I$, and the finite coefficient formulas for $g^{-1}$, its volume density and Hodge star show $\sum_j\|E_j\|_\infty\le C\|g-I\|_\infty$ and $|B_g(x,a)v|\le C|a||v|$. The constants are uniform in a fixed small matrix neighborhood of $I$. All derivatives of $E_j$ and of the coefficients of $B_g$ are bounded by finite metric derivative bounds there. No derivatives of $g$ enter this first-order equation itself. The products are meaningful because $a\in L^4_{\mathrm{loc}}$ and $\nabla a\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}$. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 Construct simultaneous spaces for the principal perturbation. For $p=2,3$ put $p^*=4p/(4-p)$ and let $Y_p$ consist of classes $v\in L^{p^*}(\mathbb R^4)$ with distributional gradient in $L^p$, with norm $\|v\|_{p^*}+\|\nabla v\|_p$. It is complete: Cauchy sequences converge in both component spaces by [F2], and distributional derivative tests identify the second limit as the gradient of the first. By [F1], $K$ maps compactly supported $L^p$ boundedly to $Y_p$, with one consistent distributional value on $L^2\cap L^3$. If $E_j$ are bounded with common compact support and $B\in L^4$ has that support, Holder gives $$\left\|\sum E_j\partial_j v+Bv\right\|_p\le\left(\sum\|E_j\|_\infty+\|B\|_4\right)\|v\|_{Y_p}.$$ Thus, with these two coefficient sizes sufficiently small, the map $v\mapsto K(f+\sum E_j\partial_jv+Bv)$ is a contraction with norm at most one half on BOTH $Y_2,Y_3$, for compact $f\in L^2\cap L^3$. Starting at zero gives the same iterates in both spaces. Both limits agree distributionally on bounded sets and hence as classes. The existing compact $W^{1,2}$ solution, if present, belongs to $Y_2$ and satisfies this fixed-point equation by [F1], so uniqueness identifies it with the common limit in $Y_3$. [F1, F2, construct, algebra]

1.3 Supply weak variable-coefficient regularity, rather than differentiate an unproved strong solution. Let $u\in L^p$ have compact support and solve $(D-\sum E_j\partial_j)u=f\in L^p$ weakly, where the $E_j$ are smooth with bounded first derivatives. For the fixed unit mollifier $\rho_\epsilon$, write $u_\epsilon=\rho_\epsilon*u$. Its equation is $$Du_\epsilon=\sum E_j\partial_j u_\epsilon+f_\epsilon+R_\epsilon.$$ The commutator $E_j\partial_j u_\epsilon-(E_j\partial_j u)*\rho_\epsilon$ is the sum of $\int(E_j(x)-E_j(y))\partial_{x_j}\rho_\epsilon(x-y)u(y)\,dy$ and $\int\rho_\epsilon(x-y)(\partial_jE_j)(y)u(y)\,dy$, with the opposite sign if included in $R_\epsilon$. Its $L^p$ norm is at most $C\|\nabla E_j\|_\infty\|u\|_p$, because $|E_j(x)-E_j(y)|\le\|\nabla E_j\|_\infty|x-y|$ and $\int|z||\nabla\rho_\epsilon(z)|\,dz$ is independent of $\epsilon$. It tends to zero in $L^p$: this is direct for smooth compact $u$, and the uniform bound plus dense smooth approximation proves it for every $u\in L^p$. Apply [F1]'s constant-coefficient gradient estimate to $u_\epsilon$. If $A_p\sum\|E_j\|_\infty<1/2$, absorb the principal term. Applying the same estimate to two mollification radii shows their gradients are Cauchy, since $f_\epsilon\to f$ and $R_\epsilon\to0$ in $L^p$. Their weak derivative limits prove $u\in W^{1,p}$ and $\|\nabla u\|_p\le2A_p\|f\|_p$. All convolutions and density statements are those in [F3]. [F1, F2, F3, construct, algebra]

2.1 Obtain the actual critical gain. Choose $\eta$ so step 1.1's principal coefficient norm is small enough for both contractions in step 1.2. At a fixed point choose $B_{2r}\Subset B_1$ with $C\|a\|_{L^4(B_{2r})}$ below their remaining smallness threshold, using [F2]. Let $v=\chi a$ for a compact cutoff equal to one on $B_r$. Extend $E_j$ from a neighborhood of its support by multiplying with another cutoff, without increasing their sup norms, and extend $B=B_g(x,a)$ by zero from $B_{2r}$. The product equation is $$Dv=\sum E_j\partial_jv+Bv+f,\qquad f=\sum(C_j-E_j)(\partial_j\chi)a.$$ The forcing is compactly supported in $L^2\cap L^3$, since it is in $L^4$ there. Step 1.2 applies to the actual compact $W^{1,2}$ map $v$, giving $a\in W^{1,3}\cap L^{12}$ on $B_r$. If $\|a\|_{L^4(B_1)}\le\delta$ for one fixed sufficiently small $\delta$, the same cutoff argument works on prescribed nested subballs without a form-dependent radius; the contraction bound controls both gained norms by the forcing norm, hence uniformly by that fixed $L^4$ bound and the cutoff. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 Shrink the single universal $\eta$ further to satisfy step 1.3 also for $p=6$. By step 2.1, $a\in L^{12}_{\mathrm{loc}}$, so $B_g(x,a)a\in L^6_{\mathrm{loc}}$. For a further compact cutoff $u=\psi a$, the equation from step 2.1 has right side in $L^6$, including its cutoff term, and $u\in L^6$. Step 1.3 therefore gives $a\in W^{1,6}_{\mathrm{loc}}$. It is locally bounded and continuous by [F2]. Under the small global $L^4$ hypothesis, step 2.1's controlled $L^{12}$ norm bounds this forcing and hence the obtained $W^{1,6}$ norm on each smaller fixed subball. This already gives the uniform zeroth derivative bound. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 1.3, construct, algebra]

4.1 Suppose $a\in W^{m,6}_{\mathrm{loc}}$, $m\ge1$. Derivatives of order at most $m-1$ are bounded on smaller balls by [F2]. The finite Leibniz formula, justified by smooth approximation [F3], consequently puts the smooth-coefficient quadratic expression $B_g(x,a)a$ in $W^{m,6}_{\mathrm{loc}}$: a factor of order $m$ is in $L^6$ with its other factor bounded; all other terms use two bounded lower derivatives. For a compact cutoff $u=\psi a$ and $|\alpha|=m$, commute derivatives in its distributional equation. The result for $z=\partial^\alpha u\in L^6$ has the same principal part $D-\sum E_j\partial_j$. Every new commutator term has a positive derivative on $E_j$ and therefore at most $m$ derivatives on $a$; it is in $L^6$. The differentiated quadratic and cutoff terms are in $L^6$ as just proved. Step 1.3 with $p=6$ gives $z\in W^{1,6}$, hence $a\in W^{m+1,6}_{\mathrm{loc}}$. All norm bounds use only previously controlled norms, finitely many cutoff derivatives and finitely many metric derivatives. Induction proves every finite Sobolev order and the uniform bounds asserted in the Statement. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 1.3, step 3.1, induction, algebra]

5.1 The higher embeddings in [F2] give a smooth representative locally. Continuous representatives agree on overlaps because a nonzero continuous difference is nonzero on an open set of positive measure; thus they give one smooth representative on $B_1$. Its classical and weak derivatives agree, so both equations hold classically. A smooth metric in coordinates centered at a point can first be put in the form $g(0)=I$ by a positive linear coordinate change; continuity then makes $\|g-I\|_{C^0}<\eta$ after shrinking. Under dilation of this ball to $B_1$, the pulled-back connection is $a_r(y)=r a(ry)$, the four-dimensional $L^4$ norm is unchanged, and the normalized metric is $g(ry)$, still $\eta$-close to $I$. Consequently the precise local assertion applies to the Euclidean Coulomb gauges constructed in these charts. The proof used the existing constant-coefficient inverses and a proved small principal perturbation argument; it used no flat-metric ASD regularity assertion outside its scope. Full AC is inherited only through [F1]–[F3]. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 1.3, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Prove the stated quantitative curvature consequence. Put $t=\|F_A\|_2$ and choose $\epsilon\le1$ below [F4]'s threshold with $C_0\epsilon\le\delta$. That construction gives the actual weak Euclidean Coulomb form $a$ for the same curved-metric equation. On fixed nested subballs the two consistent contractions above give $\|a\|_{12}\le C\|a\|_4\le Ct$, because their cutoff forcing is linear in $a$ and their norm is at most one half. The weak variable-coefficient estimate at exponent six then gives $\|a\|_{W^{1,6}}\le C(t+t^2)\le Ct$ on a smaller fixed subball. In particular $a$ is bounded there. Differentiate its equation once, with another fixed cutoff. Its principal part is unchanged; its right side consists of derivatives of the quadratic expression, cutoff terms and $(\partial E_j)\partial_j a$. The first is bounded in $L^6$ by $C_Mt^2$ and the others by $C_Mt$, since metric coefficients and their first derivatives are bounded by $M$. Apply the same exponent-six estimate to the first weak derivatives, as in the derivative induction, giving $\|a\|_{W^{2,6}(B_{5/8})}\le C_Mt$. Sobolev embedding now gives $\|a\|_{C^1(B_{1/2})}\le C_Mt$. Therefore $\|F_a\|_\infty\le C_M(t+t^2)\le C_Mt$. The unitary curvature identity in [F4] gives this identical bound for $F_A$. Under $x=x_0+ry$, the normalized metric is $g(x_0+ry)$, the connection coefficients scale by $r$, the curvature by $r^2$, and its four-dimensional $L^2$ norm is unchanged. This proves the claimed scaled form for the corresponding rescaled metric bound, without a Euclidean-ASD substitution. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
