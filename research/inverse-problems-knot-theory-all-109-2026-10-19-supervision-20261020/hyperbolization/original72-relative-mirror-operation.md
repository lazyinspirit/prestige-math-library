## New construction: finite cancellation retaining the pure-color kernel

The folding homomorphism's kernel need not be guessed or deleted. The following finite normal form keeps it. This section constructs an algebraic operation needed by the original proof; it does not assume that the unproved geometric compatibility at its end has been obtained.

Use ACTUAL lifted connected mirror panels as letters. Distinct lifts with the same downstairs color are distinct letters. Let V be this set and let L be their incidence graph: two different letters commute precisely when the two panels meet along an actual old corner edge. For a word in V, permit two operations: interchange consecutive different letters joined by an edge of L, and delete consecutive equal letters. The interchange is the actual four-sector corner square. A deletion is a crossing of one panel followed immediately by the reverse crossing of THAT SAME lift, not of another panel with equal finite color. Denote the presented group by W(L). Finite color is a separate homomorphism from W(L); a word in its kernel is not in general the identity of W(L).

Here is an exhaustive identity algorithm. Search a finite word for two occurrences of a letter v with no v between them and with every intervening letter adjacent to v in L. Choose the first such pair in lexicographic order of its two positions. Move its left occurrence rightward through that intervening block, using its actual successive corner squares, and delete the pair. Repeat. Each macro deletes two letters. Thus at most half the original length many macros occur; the total number of square swaps is finite. A final word has no pair with only commuting letters between. Call such a word reduced.

For completeness, this criterion detects the identity, rather than merely terminating. Regard reduced words up to swaps of consecutive adjacent letters. For a letter v, there is at most one occurrence of v which can be moved to the right end: two such occurrences would be separated only by letters commuting with v, contrary to reduction. If it exists, remove that occurrence; if it does not, append v. The resulting word is reduced. In the removal case a newly exposed reducible pair would have had the removed v between its occurrences. Its right occurrence was to the right of v, so it commuted with v; hence the same pair was already reducible before removal, a contradiction. In the append case the only possible new reducible pair would end at the appended v, exactly the movable occurrence that was excluded. These operations are independent of the representative under swaps: a swap not involving v does not change whether any intervening letter fails to commute with v; a swap involving v moves only its position among letters with which it commutes. Removal of a movable v is consequently a well-defined operation on these classes.

Applying this end-operation twice with v is the identity: appending creates precisely the movable occurrence just appended, and removing a movable occurrence leaves no earlier movable v by reduction. End-operations for adjacent different letters u,v commute. Indeed the movable-status of u is unaffected by appending or removing v, since u and v commute; the same holds with u,v exchanged. If both operations append, the two outcomes differ by their final allowed swap. If both remove, they remove the two specified occurrences regardless of order. If one appends and the other removes, they affect their specified independent occurrences. Thus these involutions satisfy every defining relation of W(L) and act on the reduced-word classes. Starting with the empty word and applying a reduced word gives its own swap class, since each prefix of a reduced word is reduced and no appending step cancels. A nonempty reduced word therefore cannot represent the identity: its action sends the empty class to a nonempty class. This proves the algorithm's completeness. It also proves uniqueness of reduced length and reduced letter support, since the resulting class is the image of the empty word under the group action.

Suppose an actual reflected surface has one connected trace for each mirror panel that meets a chosen developed surface component. Let A be that set of panel lifts. Suppose its intrinsic adjacency graph is the FULL subgraph L[A]: two such traces meet at a surface corner if and only if the ambient panels meet at an old corner. Then the same algorithm proves W(L[A]) injects into W(L). A reduced intrinsic word is still reduced ambiently, since every commutation between its letters is already intrinsic. Equivalently, killing letters outside A defines a retraction W(L) to W(L[A]); all commutation and involution relations remain valid. This statement concerns actual panel lifts, not their finite-color images.

This has a literal marked diagram interpretation. Retain a basepoint, the first surface face and the paths to each crossed panel lift. A square interchange occurs only at the corner for those two selected incident lifts, with its four side paths, so it changes the path by the boundary of that actual square and retains its endpoints. The deletion crosses the same selected lift out and back and contracts in its actual product collar. Thus all the macros are based homotopies; projecting to finite colors at the end retains the entire kernel word and its actual paths. If the initial word is a loop in a finite-color cover, the homotopy starts and ends at the same color sheet; no intermediate color path is silently reset. This explicitly handles words whose projection to the chamber group is trivial.

A further useful exact fact follows without an additional diagram argument. For two full vertex subsets A,B, the intersection W(L[A]) intersect W(L[B]) equals W(L[A intersect B]). If a group element belongs to either subgroup it has a reduced expression supported there, and reduced support is invariant by the construction above. Belonging to both therefore puts its support in their intersection; the reverse inclusion is immediate. This is the first-face, zero-transverse-conjugator case needed when the face and the cusp are based at their actual common window. ## New construction: the pure-color annulus with its transverse word

The arbitrary transverse word also has a finite reduction in the same actual-panel word group. Write WA and WB for the full-subgraph groups belonging to a developed surface and a selected developed window carrier. Given the ACTUAL transverse word d, repeatedly move a letter from A to its left end when all preceding letters commute with it, or a letter from B to its right end when all following letters commute with it; remove that end letter and record it in the corresponding endpoint factor. First reduce the word by the earlier identity algorithm. Every end-strip decreases its length by one; intervening square swaps are finite. The output retains an EXACT equality d=a*t*b with a in WA, b in WB, and t reduced with no A-letter movable to its left end and no B-letter movable to its right end. The endpoint factors are actual paths, not changes of basepoint made without a record.

This t is shortest in its double coset. In reducing a word u*t*v, with u in WA and v in WB, an initial cancellation of a t-letter with u would exhibit that t-letter movable to the left end and in A; an initial cancellation with v gives the forbidden right-end B-letter. Cancellation between u and v across t can remove their letters only by commuting through the intervening t letters, and cannot remove a t-letter. Thus at least all the letters of t survive. This argument can be repeated after each cancellation between the endpoint factors, and proves minimality without an unbounded search over words.

The exact intersection is

    WA intersect d*WB*d^(-1) = a*WJ*a^(-1),
    J = A intersect B intersect link(support(t)),

where link(support(t)) consists of those panel letters adjacent to EVERY letter of t. For an empty t this last condition is vacuous. Here a and t are the ACTUAL factors just constructed, not freely selected substitutes.

To prove the nontrivial inclusion, take a reduced x in WA with t^(-1)*x*t in WB. There can be no first cancellation between an x-letter and a t-letter. Such an x-letter lies in A. All earlier t letters either commute past it or have already cancelled with their opposite copies across x; in the latter case they too commuted with that x-letter. Hence the proposed t-letter could have been moved to the left end of t and would be in A, contrary to its reduced-end property. Thus every x-letter survives. A surviving t-letter is impossible as well. If any remain, take the rightmost surviving letter in the right-hand t. Membership in WB forces it to be in B. Every original t-letter to its right has disappeared by cancellation with its opposite copy: it could pass this surviving letter only if it commuted with it. The surviving B-letter was therefore movable to the right end of the original t, contradicting the other reduced-end property. Consequently all t letters cancel with their opposite copies across x. Each x-letter must commute with each of those cancelled letters; no x-letter disappeared. The remaining word is x itself, so reduced-support invariance puts its letters in B too. Thus x is in WJ. Conversely each J-letter belongs to both endpoint groups and commutes with every t-letter, proving t^(-1)*WJ*t=WJ inside WB. Conjugating by the recorded a and b proves the displayed formula.

This is an exhaustive annular reduction for PURE MIRROR words. It retains the original transverse path as a*t*b. If the surviving subgroup WJ is the actual infinite-dihedral pair for a window span, its finite-color kernel is the exact period subgroup generated by a_window to the power e computed earlier. No smaller exponent is introduced by taking a primitive generator in the orbifold word group. The a endpoint factor gives the actual surface path to that span, t records the old intervening corner path, and b is the terminal path inside the selected carrier. If instead the survivor is a genuine common-I block, retain that WHOLE marked block and its two window labels as in the preceding explicit example. The formula does not identify different endpoint labels.

The formula has a strict scope. It does not yet handle chamber-group coefficients interleaved with these letters. Those require the actual semidirect/development construction and the cut-side retained factorization, not a projection that throws the chamber coefficient away. ## New terminal realization supplier: source and compact existence construction

The new source used here is Roeder--Hubbard--Dunbar, *Andreev's Theorem on hyperbolic polyhedra*, arXiv:math/0601146v2, full 47-page author PDF, acquired locally as `hyperbolization/primary/roeder-hubbard-dunbar-andreev.pdf` with its complete page-labelled text extraction. Here is the compact input with the proof operations made explicit. Let C be a trivalent abstract sphere polyhedron with more than five faces and with no prismatic three-circuit. Assign angles in (0,pi/2] with sum greater than pi at each vertex, sum less than 2pi on each prismatic four-circuit, and the two quadrilateral-face sums less than 3pi (the sum of alternate two face edges and all four outgoing edges). Then C has a compact convex realization with those angles. The construction proceeds as follows.

Represent a marked polyhedron by its outward unit spacelike face normals in Minkowski space of signature (-,+,+,+), modulo hyperbolic isometries. For N faces this is an open manifold of dimension 3N-6. The number E of edges is 3N-6 by trivalence and Euler's formula. On the open subset whose face polygons have disjoint supporting lines for disjoint sides, the angle map to R^E is injective. To prove this, label each edge by the sign of the change in its length between two proposed equal-angle polyhedra. In the simple planar dual graph some vertex has at most two changes of sign: otherwise total changes are at least 4V, while summing around faces gives at most sum(2 degree(face)-4)=4E-4F=4V-8. A bridge contributes a repeated equal sign and does not invalidate this bound. At that vertex all positive lengths occur in one consecutive block after deleting zero signs.

The hyperbolic polygon comparison needed there is elementary. Put the one side being lengthened on x2=0 in the hyperboloid plane and apply the translation matrix with 2 by 2 block (cosh(t),sinh(t);sinh(t),cosh(t)) to all subsequent sides. If v and w are outward normals of the two end sides, the derivative of their inner product is

    sinh(t)(w1 v1-w0 v0)-cosh(t)(w1 v0-w0 v1).

For the consistently oriented disjoint-support polygon, D=w1 v0-w0 v1 is positive, and w1 v1-w0 v0<D: the two end halfspaces contain the appropriate opposite ideal endpoints of the translated side. Thus this derivative is negative for t nonnegative. Lengthening the consecutive positive block strictly decreases the end-normal inner product, whereas shortening the complementary negative block weakly increases it. If the two end sides meet, their fixed angle already fixes that inner product. In either case the two values cannot agree. All edge lengths therefore agree, and the equal face angles (computed from the same dihedral angles) make all faces and then the entire polyhedra congruent. Nonobtuse face polygons lie in this disjoint-support open subset.

The angle map is proper over the indicated strict inequalities. Normalize three vertices of one face to the positive coordinate axes in the ball model and take a subsequence of all vertices in the closed ball. For a cluster at an ideal point, translate a plane halfway between it and the other vertices to the equator. The edges crossing that plane approach perpendicularity and form a Euclidean link polygon with nonobtuse angles, so there are three or four of them. A three-edge cluster boundary would be a prismatic three-circuit; a prismatic four-edge boundary would have limiting angle sum 2pi. Both are excluded. A nonprismatic four-edge boundary isolates exactly two vertices; its joining edge would have angle tending to zero, also excluded here. The last assertion follows directly from the spherical cosine relation: the two outgoing right-angle limits force the joining dihedral angle to equal the vanishing face angle.

No face can collapse to a segment or point: its area tends to zero only if it has three or four sides. A triangular collapse violates the three-circuit inequality. A quadrilateral collapse has all four face angles tending to pi/2; the cosine formula then gives all four outgoing dihedral angles and an alternate pair of face dihedral angles tending to pi/2, violating its quadrilateral sum. Consequently the whole polyhedron does not collapse to a polygon or line. A finite cluster is then enclosed by a small spherical link; nonobtuse angles force a triangle of angle sum greater than pi, again an excluded prismatic three-circuit unless the cluster is one vertex. Thus every vertex has a distinct finite limit. The needed cosine formula is

    cos(beta_i)=(cos(alpha_i)+cos(alpha_j)cos(alpha_k))
                    /(sin(alpha_j)sin(alpha_k)).

These arguments prove properness, rather than assuming bounded edge lengths from bounded angles.

Injectivity and invariance of domain make the angle image open in the admissible angle polytope; properness makes it closed. The polytope is convex. It remains to construct ONE nonobtuse realization for every such combinatorial C, which is the genuinely substantive existence step.

For a prism, take a small-angle regular polygon in an equatorial hyperbolic plane, erect its perpendicular side planes, and cap by two planes sufficiently close to the equator. All dihedral angles are positive and nonobtuse. Thus the open-and-closed argument gives every admissible angle assignment on that prism. The N-face split prism has an explicit seed too: take an (N-3)-gonal prism. Set its longitudinal angles to pi/2, the angles at one base to pi/3, and the angles at the other base to pi/2 except ONE angle pi/4. These satisfy the compact inequalities when N>7. Double across the latter base. Its pi/2 edges disappear into merged faces; the pi/4 edge doubles to pi/2. The result has N faces and exactly the split-prism dual with two interior endpoints, one connected to three consecutive vertices of the outer polygon.

The corrected finite Whitehead procedure connects every no-prismatic-three C to such a seed, preserving absence of prismatic threes. Here are its actual termination and exclusion rules. In the dual choose a maximum-degree vertex at infinity and call its link the outer polygon P. The interior graph is connected. The following moves introduce no separating three-cycle: (i) absorb an interior vertex attached to exactly two consecutive P vertices by flipping their common P edge; (ii) for a nonendpoint, shorten a component of M>2 consecutive attachments by flipping its last attachment repeatedly, retaining two; for an endpoint retain THREE; (iii) delete an unwanted attachment component only when TWO distinct P vertices remain attached outside it. The exclusion proof uses the Jordan curve through the retained vertices: any new three-cycle would have to contain a vertex on that separating curve adjacent to both ends of the new diagonal. In these moves only the two vertices of the new facial triangles have that property. The two-retained-vertices condition in (iii) is necessary and is the correction to Andreev's original erroneous rule.

If an interior nonendpoint has a component with at least two attachments, (ii),(iii),(i) increase the length of P. If an endpoint has more than three, transfer one attachment to its neighboring nonendpoint and use that case. Otherwise every endpoint has exactly three attachments and each other attachment component is a singleton. If the interior graph is a line, this is the prism. If it branches, start at an endpoint I1 and follow its degree-two chain I2,...,Im to the first branching vertex. The chain has the same two outside attachments P1,P3, and I1 also has P2 between them. At Im retain P1,P3 and delete all other components by (iii); flip ImP3; then flip Im-1 P1,...,I1 P1 in that order. The separating curves P1,Ik,P3,infinity show that only the new facial three-cycles occur, because Im no longer attaches to P3. The resulting Im attaches exactly to consecutive P1,P2, so (i) increases P. Each macro strictly increases its integer length, bounded by N-2. Stop at N-3, when the interior graph has two endpoints; (ii) makes one endpoint have exactly three attachments, giving the split prism. The prism case has already been constructed.

Existence transfers across each such flip. Give the edge to be flipped angle epsilon, its four outgoing edges pi/2, and all remaining edges 2pi/5. The compact inequalities hold. Let epsilon decrease to zero. The preceding cluster analysis leaves precisely the two endpoint vertices converging to ONE ideal point; all other vertices remain distinct and finite. At r_x=a,r_y=b both opposite pairs are tangent at the origin and adjacent circles are orthogonal. Increasing r_x slightly and decreasing r_y accordingly resolves one diagonal; the opposite change resolves the other. The other dihedral angles had the strict 2pi/5 margin, so a sufficiently small perturbation remains nonobtuse. This gives a seed on BOTH sides of the flip. Open-and-closed continuation then gives all admissible assignments on each side. Reversing the finite flip sequence constructs the desired compact C. This establishes the compact existence input rather than merely naming a theorem or assuming the angle polytope has nonempty image.
