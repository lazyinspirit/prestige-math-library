Application to 3-manifolds

A key tool in Thurston’s construction of hyperbolic structures on 3-manifolds
is the skinning map, an iteration on Teichmiiller space whose fixed point yields
a geometric structure [Th 1].

The skinning map factors through canonical maps between Teichmiiller
spaces whose contraction is controlled by a refinement of the geometric bound
above (Theorem 11.1). In a sequel [Mc], this refinement is coupled with the
theory of geometric limits of quadratic differentials (developed in the Appendix
below) to give a new, analytic proof of the existence of a fixed point.

Outline of the paper

We begin with a sketch of the proof of Theorem 1.1 for a nonamenable covering.

Suppose O(¢)=y. To prevent loss of mass, the phase of @ must nearly
agree with that of the pull-back of w to Y, at least over a region Yy which
contains most of the mass of ||. But agreement of phase implies the mass
distribution of @ mimics that of y, which in the large is determined by the
combinatorics of the covering YX. For a nonamenable covering, most of
the mass of Yo will be near its boundary, where the pairing is inefficient by
a definite amount; this forces ||@|| <1.

§§ 2 and 3 develop the combinatorial theory of amenable and nonamenable
graphs and coverings, and establish the global features of the argument above.
Explicit ‘rates of nonamenability’ are estimated in § 4; these estimates provide
many examples of nonamenable coverings and also play a role in the proof
of Theorem 1.4.

§5 is a study of the shape of the unit ball in Q(A4), which justifies the local
part of the argument: quadratic differentials with nearly synchronized foliations
have similar mass distributions.

In § 6, we formulate a ‘fiberwise’ version of the statement ||O||<1; this
is established in § 7, and we deduce the nonamenable half of Theorem 1.1 in
§ 8. §9 treats the case of amenable coverings, completing the proof of Theo-
rem 1.1.

In §§ 10 and 11 we analyze the proof further to obtain the dependence
on moduli (Theorem 1.4 and its refinements). The discussion in § 10 draws on
classical results in Teichmiiller theory, while § 11 employs a geometric limit
argument.

Finally § 12 sketches a generalization to Poincaré series for higher order
differentials and quotients of bounded domains in C".

The Appendix develops the theory of geometric limits of quadratic differen-
tials, for use in § 11 and in a sequel on the skinning map [Mc].
