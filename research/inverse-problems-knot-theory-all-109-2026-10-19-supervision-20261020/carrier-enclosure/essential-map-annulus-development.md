## Exact theorem required

Let M be a compact orientable finite PL three-manifold. Let f:(A,boundary A)->(M,boundary M) be a proper PL annulus map. Suppose its core map on fundamental groups is injective and the image of a spanning arc is not homotopic relative its two endpoints to a path in boundary M. Then SOME properly embedded annulus in M has injected core and an endpoint-essential spanning arc.

The boundary maps of f may have arbitrary nonzero degrees. Neither an embedded representative of its whole relative class nor preservation of its original transverse path d is asserted. For an ORIENTABLE ambient M the conclusion is an ANNULUS: Cannon–Feustel1976 Theorem3, printed pp230–233. Their annulus-or-Möbius alternative is Theorem4 for possibly NONorientable ambient manifolds. That alternative is not being suppressed in the present orientable application.

## Pair-homotopy failure and the exact spanning-arc hypothesis

Use the already supplied initial asphericity interface pi2(M)=0, and assume T is the actual injected boundary torus. No asphericity source is reread here. Then an annulus map f:(A,boundary A)->(M,T) is homotopic through maps of that pair into T if and only if one spanning path is homotopic relative its endpoints into T.

The forward implication records the endpoint tracks, rather than pretending the homotopy fixes them. If f_u ends in T, take the original first endpoint to its final point along its boundary track, follow the final spanning path in T, and return along the other endpoint track. This concatenation is a T-path with the ORIGINAL endpoints. The restriction of f_u to the spanning square gives its endpoint-fixed homotopy to the original span.

Conversely give A the finite CW structure consisting of its two boundary circles, a joining spanning edge and one two-cell. The hypothesized endpoint-fixed homotopy of that edge into T extends over A by the finite CW homotopy-extension construction, fixing the boundary circles. The resulting one-skeleton lies in T. The attaching loop of the two-cell is null in M; injection pi1(T)->pi1(M) makes it null in T, so choose its disk filling there. Compare the old two-cell with this T-filling along their common boundary. Their union is a sphere map in M and is null by pi2(M)=0. The disk-pair homotopy-extension construction therefore homotopes the old filling to the T-filling relative that entire boundary. The complete map now lies in T. All steps are finite and the old endpoint labels remain distinct data.

Thus failure of the ORIGINAL pair homotopy supplies exactly the endpoint-essential span in the cited theorem, without a malnormality assumption and without replacing d by an embedded witness path. A collar push makes maps proper during intermediate steps if needed: use a nonnegative finite collar-height function zero on the source boundary and at terminal homotopy times, positive on interior boundary contact. For an embedded annulus in the irreducible injected-boundary setting, this essentialness is the usual essential-annulus condition. In every auxiliary stage G is pulled back by that actual map; it is not replaced by the whole new boundary groupoid.

The elementary selection identity used in circle cut-and-paste is the following. Let a,b,c be composable path classes, with b a loop at the common double-curve point. Put u=ac and v=ab^-1 c. Then

    abc = u v^-1 u.

Consequently if abc is outside G, u and v cannot both belong to G. This is a statement in the actual groupoid with its original endpoints. It needs no normality of G. It explains why the two adjacent-arm circle resolutions can retain essentialness while changing which embedded witness is chosen.
