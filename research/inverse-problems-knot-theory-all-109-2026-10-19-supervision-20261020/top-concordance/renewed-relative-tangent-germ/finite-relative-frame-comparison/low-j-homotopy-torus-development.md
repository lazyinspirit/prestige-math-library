# The low-j homotopy-torus construction

This is NEW mathematical development, not an accepted classification theorem. Target: geometric finite stabilized nullconcordances in degrees j=0,1,2. The manifold used for the surgery comparison is X_j=I^j×T^(6−j), with the boundary germ fixed. The result below constructs the concluding concordance and the below-middle surgery operations. It does not assert the missing middle-dimensional classification.

## 1. Exact transfer algebra of normal invariants

For a pointed homotopy-commutative group-like H-space H, the splitting of the suspension of Y×S¹ gives

    [Y×S¹ relative boundary,H] = [Y relative boundary,H] ⊕ [Σ(Y/boundary),H].

Indeed restriction to Y×{1} has the projection-induced right inverse. Its kernel is the reduced smash-product term, since collapse of Y×{1} identifies the remaining quotient with the suspension. These two operations give mutually inverse maps after applying the H-space group operation. Iterating gives a summand π_(j+|S|)(H) for each subset S of the r=6−j circle factors. A degree-λ cover in a circle acts as λ on summands containing that circle and as the identity on the others: on the suspension smash factor it is the suspension of the degree-λ circle map. This is a literal integer action on each summand, including torsion summands.

Consequently an element fixed by every standard cover has zero component for every nonempty S. It suffices to use λ=2 in each such factor, since 2x=x implies x=0 in any abelian group. The invariant subgroup is exactly π_j(H), not the full collection of torsion components.

Applied to H=G/O, this is the explicit algebraic reduction used in KS V Appendix B. The additional low input is π₀(G/O)=π₁(G/O)=0 and π₂(G/O)=Z/2 with surgery obstruction the Arf isomorphism. These low values are stated and explained by KS at printed267; their geometric surgery consequence is not automatic. In particular j=0,1 have zero invariant normal class as soon as the normal-invariant classification and its transfer naturality are genuinely supplied. The j=2 case retains one potentially nonzero Arf class.

No decomposition of a structure set or of a Wall L-group has been deduced merely from this normal-invariant splitting.

### Finite surface calculation of the low normal input

The particular low input can be obtained without higher stable stems. In stable rank, π₁(O)=Z/2 and π₂(O)=0: SO(3)=RP³ gives these values in rank three; the successive orthogonal quotients are spheres, so the values stabilize. The stable degree-±1 sphere equivalences G have components ±1, and stable Pontryagin–Thom identifies π_i(G), i>0, with framed i-bordism. Only i=1,2 is needed below.

A stable framing on an oriented surface determines, on an embedded oriented circle c, a parity q(c): zero when its induced stable circle framing bounds a disk and one otherwise. Define the induced circle framing using the surface-normal line along c together with the ambient stable normal frame. Resolving one transverse crossing in two circles changes this parity by one in the local oriented two-disk; tracing the two normal vectors through the smoothing gives one full rotation. Disjoint union contributes the sum. Thus

    q(x+y)=q(x)+q(y)+(x·y) mod 2

on H₁(F;F₂). Stable surface framings with the same q are homotopic: their difference is a map F→SO in stable rank, and on a two-complex this is determined by its H¹(F;Z/2) class because π₁(SO)=Z/2 and π₂(SO)=0. This also shows that all quadratic refinements are obtained by changing the framing along finitely many dual loops.

Let a be a nonseparating embedded curve with q(a)=0. Its actual normal framing in F extends across the three-dimensional two-handle precisely by that condition: the boundary comparison is a loop in stable SO; its parity is q(a), so the zero loop extends over the handle core disk. The remaining disk directions are trivial. Framed surgery on a therefore realizes the quotient quadratic space a^⊥/⟨a⟩. A symplectic partner b can be replaced by b+a to arrange q(b)=0, since q(a)=0 and a·b=1. Removing this hyperbolic plane leaves Arf unchanged. A separating curve instead splits the quadratic form as a direct sum, retaining the sum of the Arf invariants.

Finite symplectic linear algebra now provides the bordisms. Whenever Arf(q)=0, choose a symplectic basis with every a_i of q-value zero and realize the a_i by disjoint standard handle curves. Surgery on them gives S², whose stable framing is unique because π₂(SO)=0; it bounds the standard framed D³. When Arf(q)=1, remove hyperbolic planes until the remaining surface is a torus with q(a)=q(b)=1. Conversely every compact framed three-bordism admits a finite relative Morse handle decomposition. At an index-two handle the changed boundary surface is the q=0 surgery just described; index one is its reverse, and index zero/three creates or removes a framed sphere. Thus the total boundary Arf invariant is zero. This proves that the odd framed torus does not bound, whereas two copies do. Hence Ω₂^fr=Z/2 with generator the odd torus, by an actual finite framed surgery construction.

For completeness, the one-dimensional calculation needed for the adjacent exact-sequence term is equally finite. Stable circle framings have two parity classes. Cut any compact framed surface into disks and oriented bands. The induced framings on its boundary have an even number of nonbounding circles: disk boundaries are bounding, and each band changes that number by zero or two, as its two boundary arcs inherit opposite normal-line directions. A disk kills the bounding circle; a framed pair-of-pants identifies two nonbounding circles with a bounding one. Thus Ω₁^fr=Z/2. Rotating a stable normal frame once represents its nonzero class. Therefore J:π₁(O)→π₁(G) is an isomorphism, not merely a surjection.

The homotopy sequence of O→G→G/O now yields π₀(G/O)=π₁(G/O)=0 and an isomorphism π₂(G)→π₂(G/O). The framed odd torus represents its generator. Map that torus to S² by the degree-one handle-collapse map and use its stable framing as the normal datum (the stable normal bundle of S² is trivial). Its kernel quadratic form is exactly the odd rank-two form, so its two-dimensional surgery obstruction is Arf=1. Unimodular alternating integral forms have symplectic bases by the integer Euclidean algorithm; their quadratic refinements reduce to these mod-two symplectic forms. Hyperbolic stabilization and the same basis changes show that their stable Witt obstruction is precisely this Arf parity. Consequently the required map π₂(G/O)→L₂(Z) is the isomorphism Z/2→Z/2.

This proves the small normal-input calculation. It does not show that the restriction of a six-dimensional homotopy equivalence to a transverse two-dimensional fiber is a homotopy equivalence. The latter is still the distinct geometric splitting obligation below. No π₃(G/O) calculation is actually needed for the final odd cokernel in degree three: its target L₃(Z) is zero.

## 2. The concluding geometric nullconcordance, given a marked smooth identification

Suppose an actual smooth structure σ on X_j is supplied, standard near its boundary, together with an actual diffeomorphism

    h:(X_j,standard) → (X_j,σ)

homotopic to the identity relative that boundary. For j=0 choose the identity action on π₁ for the marking; for j=1,2 the relative homotopy gives the same marking. Choose the lift h̃ on I^j×R^r whose difference from the identity is bounded. Such a lift exists: h is homotopic to the identity, hence its lift commutes with integer translations, so the displacement on a compact fundamental domain bounds displacement everywhere. Boundary normalization lets this lift be the identity near the side boundary for j>0. Extend it by the identity to R^j×R^r outside I^j×R^r. Use a centered cube I^j.

For t>0 put H_t(x)=t h̃(x/t), and put H₀=id. If |h̃(x)−x|≤B, then |H_t(x)−x|≤tB. The same bound holds for the inverse family, since the inverse displacement is bounded by B. These inequalities prove continuity of both families at t=0 uniformly on compact sets. For t>0 they are homeomorphisms, and (x,t)↦(H_t(x),t) is therefore a genuine TOP isotopy, not a formal homotopy of maps. For j>0 its support lies inside tI^j×R^r⊂I^j×R^r, so it fixes the prescribed side boundary.

Pull back the product σ structure by this actual isotopy. At t=0 the induced structure is σ and at t=1 it is h̃*σ, which is the standard smooth structure. This is an actual concordance of smooth reductions, including its actual tangent microbundle identification. Restricting its reduction section to I^j×{0} gives the required nullconcordance of a reduction family that was originally pulled back from I^j. There is no need for this Euclidean isotopy to descend to the torus. There is also no invocation of local contractibility of homeomorphism groups in this step. Finite tangent rank for the concordance is obtained from its actual seven-dimensional product microbundle, followed by the finite stabilization in the prescribed realization.

Thus the genuinely needed preceding result is a marked smooth identification, or a smooth relative h-cobordism from σ to standard together with its relative product cancellation. The bounded-displacement stage does not itself manufacture that h-cobordism.

## 3. Explicit geometric normal surgery below the middle

Here is the finite local operation available before the middle obstruction. Let (F,B) be an actual smooth normal map of dimension m=6 or 7, with its bundle datum and prescribed boundary collars. Let a finite family of relative homotopy classes in π_(p+1)(F), p≤2 and 2p+2≤m, be supplied, along with the normal nullhomotopies defining those classes. Place their boundary p-spheres in the interior by relative general position, pairwise disjoint. The dimension estimate is 2p<m; the prescribed boundary collars are avoided by choosing the perturbations in the interior. The canonical relative-Hurewicz/general-position Statement supplies these representatives and the stable triviality of their normal bundles.

The normal rank is q=m−p≥p+2. A stable normal frame can be converted into an actual q-frame over S^p: successively remove its extra orthogonal vectors. At each removal the obstruction to homotoping that vector to a fixed coordinate vector is a map from S^p to a sphere S^q (or a sphere of larger dimension), and p<q makes it null; the homotopy is relative to a chosen basepoint. Complete by orthogonal projection and Gram–Schmidt. The same finite procedure on a cylinder makes the resulting unstable frame represent the specified stable framing. This last requirement retains the normal-map datum, rather than choosing an arbitrary trivialization of a stably trivial bundle.

Attach D^(p+1)×D^q to the trace along S^p×D^q using that frame. Define the trace map over the core disk by the supplied nullhomotopy and extend constantly over its normal disk. Over the core disk, the target bundle is trivial; its prescribed boundary comparison is exactly the stable frame just retained. It therefore extends over the handle and gives the actual stabilized normal bundle isomorphism B on the trace. On the old cylinder retain the original B. The two isomorphisms agree on the attaching region, so they glue. This explicitly supplies the b-framed trace datum required by the normal-bordism interface; a trivial normal bundle alone would not have supplied it.

Carry this out in the disjoint interior neighborhoods for the finite family. The known below-middle homotopy-effect Statement says exactly that the selected relative classes are killed and lower groups preserved. The local traces and bundle extensions leave all prescribed boundary germs unchanged. In dimension six, p=2 is the final such operation: it uses embedded S² with normal rank four. The middle kernel requires S³ surgery and does not satisfy this inequality. In dimension seven the same p≤2 steps leave the odd-dimensional middle formation still to be dealt with.

Finiteness of a family is a genuine hypothesis in this operation. For a compact finite CW normal problem with π₁ already Z^r, successive low relative kernels are finitely generated when their identification with the appropriate kernels of finite group-ring chain complexes has been supplied: Z[Z^r] is Noetherian. This observation does not manufacture the middle lagrangian or imply that a pre-existing homotopy equivalence has become a fiberwise homotopy equivalence.

### Actual middle surgery when a hyperbolic quadratic basis is supplied

There is also a concrete six-dimensional middle operation, with a more substantial algebraic hypothesis. Suppose the completed below-middle normal map f:M⁶→X has its remaining quadratic kernel supplied with a hyperbolic basis a₁,…,a_c,b₁,…,b_c over Λ=Z[π₁X]. The datum includes the normal-map quadratic refinement, λ(a_i,a_l)=0, μ(a_i)=0, and λ(a_i,b_l)=δ_il. It also includes the ordinary kernel/Poincaré identifications: the universal mapping cone has no homology except this middle kernel. If a stable hyperbolic basis is supplied, its extra free hyperbolic pairs can first be realized by trivial framed S² surgeries in disjoint interior balls. On a ball this replaces the punctured S⁶ by punctured S³×S³, and the two product S³ classes are the standard hyperbolic pair. The trace core nullhomotopy is constant, so its normal-map extension is literal. This is a finite stabilization, not an assertion that the original kernel was already hyperbolic.

Represent each a_i by a based S³ map, using the supplied kernel Hurewicz identification after the low connectivity steps. The target nullhomotopy and normal data give a stable framing of its pulled-back tangent data. Realize it at actual rank six, since O(6)→O is an isomorphism on π₃ (successive quotients are spheres of dimension at least six). With the standard parallelization of S³ this gives an actual formal immersion with a framed rank-three complement. Apply the accepted countable-choice Smale–Hirsch Statement to this finite disjoint union of S³ sources, targeting the interior of M. Its formal homotopy supplies framed immersions representing the given stable normal data. Make them pairwise self-transverse, with finitely many double points and no triple points; dimension counting excludes triple points. Keep the chosen whiskers.

The quadratic refinement μ is, here, explicitly the group-labelled signed self-double-point count of these normally framed immersions, with branch interchange identifying g with −g⁻¹ in dimension three. The hypothesis μ(a_i)=0 therefore permits a finite pairing of the double points after choosing branch orderings. Each pair has equal group label and opposite signs. Its Whitney circle is nullhomotopic. The accepted framed Whitney-disjunction Statement removes that pair by a regular homotopy, retaining the stable framing. A generic filling disk has interior disjoint from every three-dimensional sheet because 2+3<6. Repeating finitely often makes each sphere embedded. Next λ(a_i,a_l)=0 permits equal-label opposite-sign pairings of all intersections between different spheres. Apply the embedded Whitney Statement, again with the disks avoiding every other sphere. The resulting spheres are disjoint framed embeddings, and their stable normal data still agree with the supplied quadratic data: all modifications were regular homotopies with transported normal frames.

Attach the c framed four-handles along these S³×D³ neighborhoods. The target nullhomotopies and the retained stable normal frames extend the normal map over each trace exactly as in the previous local construction. For the universal-cover kernel homology, the surgery exact sequence has the two maps

    Λ^c → K₃ → Λ^c,

where the first sends the i-th basis vector to a_i and the second is intersection with the a_i. In the supplied hyperbolic basis the second map sends the b_i to the standard basis (up to the chosen sign convention) and the a_i to zero. Thus the first map is injective, its image is the kernel of the second, and the second is surjective. The outgoing middle kernel, and the two neighboring surgery kernels, are zero. The other kernel groups were zero and are unaffected. The π₁ is unchanged because the surgery spheres have dimension three. Hence the outgoing map is a homotopy equivalence: its universal-cover mapping cone is acyclic and simply connected, so relative Hurewicz followed by Whitehead applies. All operations lie in the interior and preserve the boundary germs.

This is an actual finite geometric realization of a **supplied hyperbolic quadratic basis**, not a computation of whether the Wall class vanishes. It does not identify an arbitrary normal kernel with such a basis, prove the group-ring L-decomposition, or resolve the odd-dimensional normal-bordism action.

## 4. The exact remaining geometric steps in the KS route

The freshly read primary passages are KS IV10.12 printed200–202, V5.3 printed247–250, and V Appendix B printed264–271. Two places require more than the operations above.

First, j=2 needs exclusion of the surviving Arf normal invariant for a transfer-invariant homotopy equivalence. KS B.1 does this by crossing with P=(CP²)² and then deforming P×f to a bundle map over the torus by Farrell's fibering construction. Its inverse image over a torus point is then a homotopy equivalence of fibers, so its Arf obstruction vanishes. The ambient dimension in that argument is fourteen, with fiber dimensions 8+j. Merely taking a transverse inverse image of the original six-dimensional map does not make that inverse image a homotopy equivalence. No such fibering construction has been proved in this development.

Second, even for j=0,1 after the normal class vanishes, an actual normal bordism must be modified to an h-cobordism. KS B.3 uses the geometric odd-dimensional surgery action; B.4 splits its obstruction group when adding a circle. Its proof again uses fibering to arrange the needed transverse restriction as a homotopy equivalence before cutting along it. The normal-invariant splitting in §1 cannot replace this geometric splitting. After it is proved, transfer-invariant cokernels reduce to the base surgery maps in degrees 1+j; those are zero cokernels for j=0,1,2. This last algebra is not a proof of the preceding geometric step.

The cut-and-paste part can be made explicit once those missing transverse data are supplied. Let a representative normal bordism F over I×Y×S¹ have a two-sided inverse-image hypersurface V over I×Y×{1}, with the required homotopy-equivalence restrictions. Suppose its restricted normal problem is supplied with a relative normal bordism to the identity. Choose a product neighborhood V×[−δ,δ] by transversality. Take the product of the supplied normal bordism with [−δ,δ] and glue it to the original normal bordism in that neighborhood. The old and new stable bundle maps agree on V at the gluing face and extend as products in the normal coordinate. Rounding its finite corners leaves all boundary collars untouched. The outgoing representative is the identity on a smaller neighborhood of the cut hypersurface. Cutting there now gives a normal problem over I×Y×I; identifying its two product end faces recovers the circle representative. This proves the geometric kernel-to-cut-representative step when its nullbordism is supplied, with actual normal data retained.

Conversely a cut representative is inserted in a small interval of S¹ and is the identity outside that interval. In a degree-λ circle cover there are λ disjoint copies of this interval. Their associated normal bordisms concatenate in order, giving λ times the cut representative. The product-circle representative has no distinguished interval and pulls back to the same product representative. These literal finite gluing operations explain the transfer action once the geometric splitting exists. They neither guarantee the required transverse homotopy equivalence nor prove that every representative admits it.

At the geometric middle stage, equal-label Whitney moves can remove pairs after a suitable lagrangian/formation and framed representatives have actually been constructed. The accepted Whitney Statements do not create those objects. Similarly accepted Wh(Z^r)=0 can cancel an actual finite smooth h-cobordism; it does not convert a normal bordism into one. The existing smooth s-cobordism Statement is formulated for closed ends, so its relative-boundary version must be realized by the actual interior handle cancellation, not silently asserted as a larger contract.

The finite lower surgery construction and final bounded isotopy are therefore genuine completed operations here. They bracket, but do not prove, the missing middle surgery/fibering bridge. This is an established-theorem authoring obligation, not an open mathematical conjecture and not a false claim that a characteristic-class obstruction survives.
