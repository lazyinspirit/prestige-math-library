# Countable triangulation packet for an actual boundaryless smooth source

This is a new noncompact assembly, using only the Statements of the published suppliers `thm-every-smooth-manifold-admits-a-smooth-proper-exhaustion-function`, `thm-morse-sard-for-smooth-manifolds`, and `thm-compact-smooth-manifolds-with-corners-admit-relative-finite-triangulations`. Their proof bodies are not reviewed here. The first two assume Countable Choice. The third Statement explicitly says its declared ambient Choice hypothesis is dispensable for its finite construction and supplies finite whole-star triangulations, relative to admissible finite boundary triangulations, with compatible subdivisions allowed.

Under those exact contracts and Countable Choice, every actual boundaryless smooth m-manifold X has a countable locally finite triangulation of dimension m. This supplies a packet, rather than merely using the adjective smooth as a packet. No triangulation of an unsmoothed TOP target is asserted.

Choose a nonnegative smooth proper exhaustion ρ on X by the first contract. For each j≥1 choose a regular value aⱼ∈(2j,2j+1), independently by Countable Choice and the Sard contract. Then P₀={ρ≤a₁} and Pⱼ={aⱼ≤ρ≤aⱼ₊₁} are compact smooth manifolds with boundary, and Σⱼ={ρ=aⱼ} is a compact smooth (m−1)-manifold. Empty pieces cause no difficulty. Distinct frontiers are disjoint and have actual smooth collars by their nonvanishing exhaustion derivative.

Choose finite whole-star smooth triangulations Tⱼ of all Σⱼ simultaneously. Their union on the two boundary components of each Pⱼ is admissible boundary data for the relative finite contract: every boundary component has its ordinary halfspace collar, and there are no intersecting boundary faces to match. Choose all finite relative slab triangulations simultaneously; likewise triangulate P₀ relative to T₁. These choices are independent after the first frontier table is fixed, so only two finite iterations of Countable Choice have occurred.

Two adjacent slab outputs may induce different compatible subdivisions of Tⱼ. Identify both on the fixed abstract finite complex Tⱼ. Their common refinement is finite: intersect their finitely many affine subdivided simplices inside each original simplex and triangulate the resulting finite convex cells, agreeing on faces. On each finite slab extend these prescribed boundary subdivisions over its finite complex. The elementary subdivision extension is finite: process simplices in increasing dimension; once their boundary subdivision is fixed, cone it from one interior point of each remaining simplex. It changes only the triangulation, not the underlying map to X. Restriction of smooth simplex maps to affine subdivisions preserves the whole-star smooth property. All new boundary subdivisions are now identical across each Σⱼ. These are independent finite operations on the already selected table; any finite choices can again be selected simultaneously by Countable Choice.

Glue the resulting slab complexes along their identical frontier subcomplexes. The result is countable. It is locally finite: every compact subset of X lies in {ρ≤R}, hence meets only finitely many slabs, and each slab has finitely many simplices. Its map onto X is a homeomorphism, since it is the given finite triangulation on every slab, these maps agree on their closed collared frontiers, and the family of slabs is locally finite. Its dimension is m.

The same construction can be selected simultaneously for any predetermined countable family of boundaryless open smooth restrictions. Their source triangulations need not agree with one another; each proper lifting argument uses one supplied packet. Product with I is triangulated by the finite prism subdivision on each simplex, with both endpoint copies subcomplexes.

This statement does not supply a relative triangulation at an arbitrary wild protected set or at a boundary that has not been earned as a smooth product. Those extra clauses remain separate in the proper lifting interface. In the actual desuspension application the larger target and the completion source are boundaryless; local open restrictions of the actual source inherit its smooth structure and therefore meet the stated scope.
