Once such a tube is supplied, the remaining line-removal homotopy calculation has the following explicit form. Choose compacta C_j which include a central tube segment [−r_j,r_j]×D⁴_(1/r_j), with C_j outside this central part missing a smaller full tube, and with N\C_j having its two unbounded end components U_j^+,U_j^−. Remove the smaller tube from C_j to obtain compact C'_j⊂N\L. The complement (N\L)\C'_j consists of U_j^+,U_j^− joined through the punctured tube. Its tube cross-section is S³ and is simply connected. Van Kampen therefore gives the free product of the two end groups, with the injections induced by the actual inclusions U_j^±⊂(N\L)\C'_j. Choose the exhaustion cofinally so that the two end bonding maps are zero by 1-LC. The induced maps on their free products are then zero. Thus N\L is 1-LC at its single joined end.

The inclusion N\L→N induces a fundamental-group isomorphism: loops and their two-dimensional nullhomotopies can be put off the locally flat line in its product charts, since 2+1<5, using finite chartwise relative perturbations and subdivision. Its group is therefore zero. The local relative homology of removing the line is the Thom homology of its oriented rank-four normal disk tube. The only possible new reduced homology is the linking class in degree three. The connecting map H₄(N)→H₃(N\L) sends a generator to its intersection number with the proper line. That intersection number is ±1 when the line joins the two ends: the compact generator is dual to the difference of the two ends, and its signed crossing number with a path from one end to the other is one. This is the compact-support Poincaré–Lefschetz duality calculation, with the local D⁴ fiber generator specifying the sign. Consequently N\L is acyclic. Manifold CW type and simple connectedness make it contractible. The newly authored R5-recognition proof, if accepted, would then identify N\L with R⁵.

This calculation is conditional on the actual line/product tube and does not designate that input as already proved. In particular the product tube is what makes the cofinal compacta and the van Kampen overlap S³ explicit.

## 2. The meridian-disk compactification

In the given tube take the meridian disk D={0}×D⁴. Its punctured part lies in N\L. After identifying N\L with R⁵ and compactifying by ∞, the set

    D* = ({0}×(D⁴\{0})) ∪ {∞}

is topologically a 4-disk: inversion of the radial coordinate replaces its puncture by its center. The compactification topology has this property because approaching the missing line leaves every compact subset of N\L. It is locally flat except possibly at its new center ∞. Its outer boundary is the original meridian S³ and remains locally flat by the actual tube chart.

The step “therefore D* is flat” is the genuine additional operation. It does not follow from R⁵ recognition or Brown's bicollared-sphere theorem. Kirby's isolated-point corollary, printed 614, establishes precisely that an isolated nonflat interior point cannot occur for a codimension-one manifold embedding when n>3. To use it locally here one must actually supply the proof of that corollary and its imports. If supplied, extend the resulting flat disk D* to a standard locally flat S⁴ in the compactified S⁵. Its complementary cap A meets D* only on their common outer S³ and avoids ∞, which lies in the interior of D*. Thus A is an actual compact disk in N\L. Glue A to the ORIGINAL meridian disk D in N, rather than treating the compactification point ∞ as a point of N. Near their common boundary D and D* coincide and the cap has the matching locally flat germ, so D∪A is the desired actual locally flat sphere in N. Its crossing of the tube and the original end separation give a separating sphere. The inclusion/half homotopy-equivalence calculation must then be recorded before applying the two relative half-products.

## 3. What Kirby's four-page proof actually supplies

The main theorem says that two locally flat (n−1)-balls β₁,β₂ in Rⁿ, meeting exactly in an (n−2)-ball in their boundaries that is locally flat in both boundaries, have a flat union. Kirby says his proof avoids engulfing and works in all dimensions, but expressly requires detailed Cantrell–Lacher straightening constructions.

The geometric normalization in printed 615–616 is concrete. Parametrize the two halves by opposite half-disks D_π,D₀ in R^(n−2)×R², with their common equatorial disk fixed. Use their local flat parametrizations to extend to ambient open embeddings. In one chart write f=f₁^−1f₂. Rotate its two half-disks into a thin wedge W(0,π/4), keeping their common equatorial disk fixed. Open the complementary wedge by an angular homeomorphism which fixes W(π,2π). Cutting along f(D_(π/2)) and applying that angular map on one component produces a map h, with h f fixed on D₀ and h f(D_π) lying in W(π/2,π). Shrink the working ball to remove extraneous components and make the piecewise map injective; its compact inner half-disk is retained.

Kirby then chooses ε>0 away from that retained half-disk and an angular map g₁ moving D_(2π−ε) to D_π. Conjugate g₁ by h f to define g₂. Pasting g₂ to the identity gives g on a narrowed wedge, after a further shrinking to keep its boundary pieces disjoint. Pasting g f i on one half and h f on the other gives an ambient ball embedding F which is fixed on D₀ and puts the other half in the opposite wedge. These explicit formulas reduce the problem to an embedding transverse to the two indicated separating half-hyperplanes.

The last paragraph on printed 617 is a further proof input, not a derived local formula in this article. It invokes the latter part of Lemma 5.2 of Cantrell–Lacher to isotope F(D₀) tangent to every half-hyperplane H_θ along its equator, then invokes their Theorem 6.1 to produce an ambient homeomorphism fixed on D_π taking that half-disk to standard D₀. Without those actual constructions, the preceding wedge normalization has not yet flattened the union.

The isolated-point corollary in printed 614 has a second input. Split a neighborhood of the isolated point in the embedded manifold into two half-balls meeting along a disk through that point. Each half-ball is locally flat except at one point of its **boundary**. Kirby invokes Lacher's half-string theorem to flatten each such boundary point; only then does the union theorem apply. Boundary-point flatness is not an immediate consequence of the hypothesis “locally flat away from that point”.

## 5. Renewed derivation using the now accepted raw engulfing and R5 input

The special compactified meridian has stronger complement control than an arbitrary disk with one apparently bad point. Let p=∞ and Δ=D*. In the adapted cofinal neighborhoods W_j of p with p removed, section 1's tube description gives two flank sets A_j^+,A_j^− after deleting Δ. Each consists of one end component together with its adjacent half of the thin punctured tube. Thicken both flank sets slightly across the central meridian level to open sets B_j^+,B_j^− covering W_j. Their intersection is the central product S³×(0,ε_j)×(−δ_j,δ_j), hence has the homotopy type of S³. Each B_j^± deformation retracts onto its own flank, by pushing the small added central interval back into that half of the product tube. These retractions and the exhaustions can be chosen compatibly at the cofinal levels.

Van Kampen gives π₁(W_j)=π₁(A_j^+) * π₁(A_j^−), with the two vertex-group injections equal to the actual inclusion maps. There are also injections π₂(A_j^±)→π₂(W_j). For the latter claim, take the universal cover of this union. Because the intersection is simply connected, its incidence pattern is a tree, with vertex spaces the universal covers of the B_j^± and edge spaces copies of the simply connected overlap. The overlap has H₁=H₂=0, so Mayer–Vietoris on each finite subtree injects every vertex H₂ into the union's H₂. A spherical cycle uses only finitely many pieces. Hurewicz for the simply connected vertex and total covers identifies these injections with the asserted π₂ maps. This argument does not assume that an individual deep flank is already simply connected.

Under N\L≅R⁵, the W_j form a cofinal system of neighborhoods of infinity. The pro-systems π₁(W_j) and π₂(W_j) are zero, since standard neighborhoods outside balls in R⁵ have homotopy type S⁴. Fix j and choose j′ sufficiently deep so the maps π_i(W_j′)→π_i(W_j) are zero for i=1,2. The inclusion squares commute. Injectivity at level j therefore implies that the maps π_i(A_j′^±)→π_i(A_j^±) are zero for i=1,2 as well. Thus the TWO individual local complement flanks of Δ at p are pro-2-connected.
