# New one-exceptional-endpoint arc construction in dimension five

This is new research mathematics, not yet an adopted supplier. Its finite locally-flat compact-arc conversion is consumed only at root's adopted Statement contract, without a proof reread. The endpoint/annular assembly below is new and requires its own review.

## Input and desired output

Let K be an actual parametrized TOP 5-ball in a 5-manifold. Let A⊂K be an embedded compact arc, with one endpoint q∈∂K and its other endpoint p∈intK. Assume A\{p} is locally flat, its interior misses ∂K, and near q an ACTUAL supplied boundary collar identifies the pair with a straight boundary-normal arc. The output is a homeomorphism of K, preserving a smaller supplied collar at q, which carries A to a standard boundary-attached straight arc. No isotopy of the limiting homeomorphism is claimed.

Use a ball parameter compatible with the smaller actual boundary collar. It can be obtained from any boundary-ball parameter by truncating the given collar, applying the accepted Brown ball contract to its inner parallel sphere, adjusting that sphere's parameter radially, and pasting back the actual collar. This uses a supplied annulus, not an arbitrary annulus identification. Work henceforth in the interior's actual standard PL ball coordinates. The supplied boundary-normal germ is PL in these coordinates.

## 1. Making the punctured arc locally polyhedral

Root adopted `renewed-relative-arc-polyhedralization/root-arc-operation-adoption.json`. The exact contract, clarified by its author, permits the protected PL germ set L to be EMPTY, or to consist of only one existing overlap. It requires a whole TOP pair chart for the compact subarc, extended along its axis past the compact endpoints. It yields an arbitrarily small compactly supported ambient homeomorphism taking that subarc to an ambient PL arc, fixed on smaller neighborhoods of the protected actual PL germs. No exceptional endpoint or limit isotopy is part of that provider.

Choose a locally finite partition of the parameter interval (0,1] of A\{p}, subordinate to the supplied local pair-chart neighborhoods; order its compact intervals from q toward p. Such a partition is obtained by finite rational subdivision on each dyadic compact parameter interval, choosing the least subdivision whose closed enlarged pieces lie in the chosen local charts. Consecutive pieces overlap on nontrivial smaller subintervals, and only finitely many pieces meet any compact parameter interval away from zero. Refine so their ambient neighborhoods have diameter tending to zero toward p and meet A only in the corresponding enlarged parameter intervals. Compactness and injectivity of A guarantee that a compact central piece is separated from the closed remaining arc, including p.

Apply the finite provider to the first compact charted piece, preserving the actual PL collar germ at q if it meets it; an empty protected set would also be permitted for an interior seed. Apply it successively to the next pieces, protecting the already PL overlap and all old portions lying in its support neighborhood. The latter are just finitely many PL subarcs because the subdivision is locally finite away from p. Choose the new support disjoint from every earlier piece except those overlaps. Outside that support, earlier PL pieces are unchanged.

At stage i choose both forward and inverse displacements below 2^−i times a positive control function tending to zero at p, and below the corresponding inverse-continuity tolerance of the current finite composition. These choices are part of the finite provider's stated arbitrary-smallness contract. Each support lies in an ambient neighborhood of diameter tending to zero at p; outside p the family is locally finite. Thus every compact subset away from p sees only finitely many stages, and the product and inverse product stabilize locally there. At p their displacements tend to zero uniformly, since all sufficiently late supports lie in any prescribed neighborhood of p. The resulting maps are inverse homeomorphisms, fix p, preserve the smaller boundary germ, and make A locally PL at every point except possibly p. This is an actual countable assembly, not an assertion that a forward Cauchy sequence alone has a homeomorphic limit.

## 2. Closing the arc near its exceptional endpoint

Take a small coordinate ball B around p contained in intK. The already polyhedral punctured arc is a countable locally finite union of compact PL segments away from p. Choose a ray from p whose direction avoids their radial projections. Each segment's forbidden directions form a closed subset of S⁴ with empty interior; a nested sequence of closed rational balls of decreasing diameters, avoiding the next finite list at each step, selects such a direction without arbitrary choice. The resulting ray misses A except at p.

Use a short initial piece of that ray and a finite generic polygonal connector to a point q' of A outside a smaller ball. The connector can be confined away from p and made disjoint from the rest of A: there only finitely many PL arc pieces occur on its compact working set, and the dimension bound for two finite 1-complexes is 1+1<5. It can also be kept disjoint from the ray except at its own endpoint. The connector together with the subarc A[p,q'] forms a simple closed curve C, locally PL except at p. Only a small ball about p will be used to tame C, so q', the remainder of A and the original boundary collar can be protected.

## 2A. The finite circle exchange in a four-dimensional PL boundary

The boundary disk used next is a special ONE-DIMENSIONAL unknot construction, not a four-dimensional disk-embedding theorem. Let e be a finite PL embedded circle in a PL 4-sphere and J a finite PL 1-complex disjoint from e. There is an embedded, locally flat PL disk E with ∂E=e and intE disjoint from J.

Here are finite geometric details. Remove a point outside e∪J and identify the remaining standard PL 4-sphere with R⁴. Choose a generic linear projection to R³ which is injective on e: the finite secant direction sets have dimension at most two in the space of projection directions S³, so after a finite generic subdivision a projection outside its finite bad strata works. More directly, for distinct 1-simplices the projected images have expected intersection dimension 1+1−3<0, and the finite incident-simplex conditions are open inequalities. The circle is the graph of its fourth-coordinate height over a PL knot in R³. Flatten that height by moving its finitely many vertices vertically, one at a time, subdividing each short vertex path as needed. Every intermediate edge still projects to the same edge of the projected knot, so nonincident edges remain disjoint. Each edge's track is an explicit polygonal strip over that edge. Subdivide the strips and implement their elementary triangle moves by the product/cone construction in the next paragraph; all finitely many intermediate common-star cone inequalities are open, so sufficiently short path pieces have the same kernel point and oriented face pattern. If J is present, choose the projection injective on the disjoint finite complex e∪J. Its projected e and J are then disjoint compact sets; the strip supports may be chosen disjoint from J. Thus this height flattening is a finite ambient PL homeomorphism and preserves J. No bilinear variable shear or unproved arbitrary-germ extension is needed.

A generic planar diagram of the projected knot has finitely many crossings. Change them to a descending diagram, starting at a chosen point; following the diagram then explicitly pulls the curve successively off the plane into a standard circle. Each crossing change is an ambient PL motion in R⁴: lift the short moving strand into the fourth coordinate, pass it across the stationary strand there, and lower it on the opposite third-coordinate side. Its trace is an embedded polygonal strip: the along-strand coordinate is retained, while the other two coordinates trace a simple polygonal arch around their common origin. For a completely finite realization, triangulate this strip and move the strand across its triangles in order. A triangle move replaces two boundary edges of a planar triangle by the third edge. In a small product box around that triangle, triangulate the two boundary-arc neighborhoods identically, cone them from a common interior point, and extend the resulting boundary-vertex map over those cones. The cones have the same orientation and intersect only on their named faces, so this is a PL homeomorphism. It is identity on the box boundary. Finite subdivision realizes the entire strip motion by these moves, without a theorem about arbitrary two-disk embeddings.

The strips can be made disjoint from J. Their parameter dimension is two, so their finite generic perturbations miss a fixed 1-complex in dimension four (2+1<4). The explicitly embedded strip remains embedded under sufficiently small finite perturbation in its product charts; this is an open condition on the finitely many nonincident closed simplices, together with the oriented cone conditions at incident faces. Take its small product boxes disjoint from J. Thus the ambient changes fix J. Their inverse carries a planar spanning disk of the final circle back to an embedded locally flat PL disk E. If that disk meets J, perturb its finitely many vertices in its actual product-star charts, fixed on its boundary, to miss J; the same 2+1<4 inequality gives simultaneous finite choices while the open embedding conditions are retained. This proves the finite circle exchange, using only finite PL moves and no removal of double points of an immersed 2-disk in dimension four.

## 3. Shrinking cubical annuli: the finite geometric step

The following is the actual single-point construction from Cantrell–Edwards1963 printed452–453, reconstructed at n=5. Choose nested closed coordinate cubes N_i centered at p, with diameters tending to zero. By continuity of the inverse curve parameter, choose N_i so every EXCESS excursion of C outside N_i whose two endpoints lie on ∂N_i is contained in intN_(i−1). Exclude the distinguished outer component containing the fixed connector through q′; it is retained, not claimed small. The remaining excursions lie in the short intrinsic subarc about p, where inverse-parameter continuity gives exactly the asserted bound. A small finite PL perturbation of each cube boundary makes its intersections with C finite; arrange this on alternating cube boundaries so the resulting support annuli are locally finite away from p.

For each outside excursion u in intN_(i−1)\N_i, join its endpoints by a PL boundary arc w in ∂N_i missing the other curve intersections. The circle u∪w bounds an embedded PL 2-disk in intN_(i−1): first take a nullhomotopy in that coordinate ball, triangulate it relative the PL boundary, and use general position 2+2<5 to make it embedded while keeping the boundary. The same perturbation makes its interior miss all other finite curve pieces, since 2+1<5. Prescribe its boundary collar along w to lie on the OUTSIDE of N_i. The relative general-position construction may retain this collar. Its transverse intersections with ∂N_i are then finitely many closed circles; no arcs can end on w because a whole collar of w has been fixed outside. Choose an innermost such circle e on the disk. Section 2A gives an embedded PL disk E in ∂N_i spanning e, missing the other finite intersection circles, the boundary arc w and all curve intersections on ∂N_i. Push intE slightly to the outside of N_i in its actual PL normal collar, retaining its boundary. It can be made disjoint from the retained portion of the original ambient disk: relative general position of two 2-disks in the FIVE-dimensional ambient chart has 2+2<5. A small perturbation in E's actual product-star charts retains its embedding and the outside condition. Replace the subdisk bounded by e by this pushed disk, matching its boundary collar by the explicit product push. The result is an embedded PL disk with one fewer intersection circle and with unchanged boundary and curve-avoidance. Repeat finitely. There is no four-dimensional Whitney call: the sole four-dimensional embedded disk was constructed by the finite circle unknot moves of section 2A.

The resulting disk meets ∂N_i only in w, lies otherwise outside N_i, and misses C except on u. Finite elementary triangle moves across the disk carry u onto w and then just inside N_i, with ambient support in a small disk neighborhood disjoint from the rest of C. Repeat over all excursions, protecting the two first-entering curve pieces. The resulting curve meets ∂N_i in exactly two points. Apply the construction in alternating annuli so supports can be pasted on their fixed outer and inner cube boundaries.

Once every cube boundary has exactly two intersections, finite PL disk moves in each annulus straighten the two curve pieces to radial segments, matching at the already fixed endpoints. Even/odd annular products have locally finite supports away from p and supports shrinking to p. Their maps and inverses are continuous at p. This would make C, and hence A, PL at p while preserving the chosen outer boundary germ.

## 4. The final finite straightening and candidate scope

After section 3 the compact arc A is PL everywhere in intK, with its actual boundary-normal germ retained. A finite PL disk now joins it to a chosen standard boundary-attached arc: join their interior endpoints by a short generic PL path first, choose the standard arc disjoint from A except at the retained boundary segment, and fill the resulting PL loop in the ball. Relative general position makes this TWO-dimensional filling embedded in dimension five and keeps it disjoint from the protected initial collar. Move one arc across the triangles of this disk in order, using the explicit product/cone triangle moves described in section 2A, now with three normal coordinates. The resulting PL homeomorphism of the ball is identity near the original boundary germ and carries A to the chosen standard arc. All choices are finite least-code choices in rational subdivisions; the locally finite preparations use the previously displayed natural-number recursion.
