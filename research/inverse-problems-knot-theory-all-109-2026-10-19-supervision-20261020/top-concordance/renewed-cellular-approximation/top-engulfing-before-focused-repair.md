## Exact finite primitive

Let O be an open subset of a metrizable TOP n-manifold without boundary, n≥5. A closed subset X of O is **p-dominated** if it has a covering by Euclidean charts in each of which its intersection is contained in a locally finite linear polyhedron of dimension at most p. This is chart data near X, not a PL structure on O. Let V⊂O be open. We use p=0,1,2, always p≤n−3, and assume π_i(O,V)=0 for 0≤i≤p, interpreted componentwise with chosen paths. Let X be p-dominated with X\V compact. Let Γ be a locally finite polyhedron of dimension at most n−3, f:Γ→O continuous, H⊂Γ a closed subpolyhedron of dimension at most p, and L⊂Γ a closed subpolyhedron on which f is a locally PL embedding. Assume common smoothing charts near X and f(L): whenever these data overlap, the same chosen chart makes f(L) locally linear and contains X in a linear p-polyhedron. Assume f(H)\V compact, and that the active source part can be taken in a finite subpolyhedron. Equivalently, for the compact version used below take Γ finite from the outset. Given a positive continuous error bound ε on Γ, there are a homeomorphism h of O, isotopic to the identity with compact support, and a map g:Γ→O such that

    X ∪ g(H) ⊂ h(V),       g|L=f|L,       d(g(z),f(z))<ε(z).

The changes of g occur on the same finite active source neighborhood. The construction does not assume that f is a global embedding or that O admits a PL structure. The case Γ=H=L and f the inclusion gives ordinary engulfing of a closed locally tame p-polyhedron: then g=f, so that polyhedron itself lies in h(V).

For a prescribed closed protected region F⊂M, carry out this construction with O=M\F and require its actual homotopies, charts and active sets to lie in O. Its support is compact in O and hence disjoint from an open neighborhood of F in M. Extending by the identity on F gives an ambient isotopy preserving that neighborhood pointwise. This is the precise relative support assertion. Merely having the absolute groups π_i(M,V)=0 does **not** assert the corresponding groups after removing F. Applications must establish the stated groups in the work region or exhibit the required membrane homotopies there. In particular this note does not infer preservation of an arbitrary open K from an absolute engulfing conclusion.

## 1. The actual Euclidean push

Here is the local move replacing a regular-neighborhood engulfing assertion. Work in a chart with coordinates Y×Z×R, with distinguished 0∈Y. Let D⊂Z be closed, and let a:D→R be bounded upper semicontinuous and b:D→R continuous, a≤b and a=b on the frontier of D. Write Π(a,b) for the closed vertical prism over D between them; when a is not continuous include the entire interval from its lower to its upper limit in the graph of a. Suppose an open set U contains that base graph, and an open set W contains every point of the prism strictly above the upper limit of a. Then a compact portion of {0}×Π(a,b) can be engulfed by a homeomorphism supported in W. It is an actual variable-speed parallel-line push; the following construction also gives its isotopy.

First take continuous a, with the whole compact prism contained in W. Choose a continuous roof b_+≥b, equal to b where b=a, with Π(a,b_+)⊂W. Choose a continuous lower level c with a≤c≤b, equal to b where b=a, and whose graph lies in U, with c>a wherever b>a. These choices follow by taking locally admissible small positive intervals, a locally finite partition of unity, and shrinking near the equality set. In a thin Y-neighborhood extend the levels to functions of (y,z). Choose a continuous level c′ there with a≤c′≤b: on y=0 it equals c, and on the outer boundary of that thin neighborhood it equals b. For each vertical line use the increasing piecewise linear map θ which sends [a,c′] to [a,b] and [c′,b_+] to [b,b_+], and is the identity outside [a,b_+]. If an interval has zero length, the equality conditions make the entire map the identity on that line. The formula and its inverse are continuous, including on the equality set. Its displacement is bounded by b_+−a, which tends to zero at a pinched boundary. Thus (y,z,t)↦(y,z,θ(t)) extends by the identity to a homeomorphism of the chart. On the central line it sends the portion of U below c to a set containing Π(a,b). Replacing b in the image level by (1−u)c′+u b, 0≤u≤1, gives an isotopy with the same support. Shrink the thin neighborhood and the roof beforehand so that its closed active support is a compact subset of W.

For upper semicontinuous a, choose a continuous level a<c_0 wherever a<b, whose intervening prism lies in U; then replace it by min(c_0,b). On the closed region where c_0<b, this is a continuous floor strictly above a, so the preceding continuous-floor construction applies to its prism. On its complement the original prism already lies in U. The push is the identity below c_0, so those already covered portions stay covered. This proves the claimed version with an upper semicontinuous floor. The continuous intermediate levels exist by the elementary insertion construction: for upper semicontinuous l and lower semicontinuous r with l<r, choose local constants between them, refine to a locally finite cover and take a partition-of-unity average; enforce equality on a closed pinching set by successively smaller local intervals. No ambient smoothing theorem is used.

There is a useful guard version. Suppose a closed set A already lies in U, and its intersection with the prism is contained in the base graph. Replace W by W\A throughout. The strict-above-floor prism misses A, so the preceding construction still applies. Its support misses A and therefore fixes A pointwise, while engulfing the remaining prism. In the application below, raising the floor to the roof on a closed collection of vertical columns makes that collection part of the protected base. This is Newman's Theorem 2 in the form actually needed.

## 2. Chartwise optimal approximation

A finite polyhedral map into one Euclidean chart can be made locally linear and in general position there by an arbitrarily small change, fixed on a specified locally linear embedded source subpolyhedron. Subdivide so that the relevant preimages and a smaller concentric cube are finite subcomplexes. Extend linearly over the simplexes meeting the smaller cube, and interpolate by coning in the intervening annulus to the unchanged map outside the larger cube. Perturb the new vertex images one at a time. At each step the prohibited choices lie in finitely many proper affine subspaces: spans of at most n old vertices together with the prescribed faces. Their complement meets every small ball. The fixed embedded subpolyhedron has no unwanted mutual identifications. If linearization perturbs its vertex images in an intermediate construction, apply the inverse of the small PL vertex-displacement homeomorphism of the larger cube, fixed near its boundary, to restore it; the resulting map is optimal for principal simplexes. Such a displacement is obtained by linear extension on a sufficiently fine common stellar subdivision, choosing the vertex displacement small enough that the finitely many determinants retain their signs, followed by linear interpolation of the displacement. Thus the restoration itself is an explicit local ambient homeomorphism.

The exact consequence used is this: for a new r-simplex τ and an already specified linear polyhedron of dimension at most p, the part of its image meeting the old image other than along common prescribed faces is contained in a polyhedron of dimension at most r+p−n. To see the bound, write each simplex as the join of its fixed common face and its remaining vertices. The latter vertices were chosen with independent spans modulo the common face. Intersecting the two affine spans gives the dimension bound; their common-face intersection is the only forced exception. For the finite unions, take the maximum over pairs of principal simplexes. This is a calculation in R^n, not an appeal to general position in a globally triangulated TOP manifold. Only the active membrane and the already engulfed H-skeleton enter this intersection estimate. Passive source cells, including a larger locally embedded L, are not asserted to be engulfed or avoided.

Repeat on finitely many charts covering the active image. At a later chart a previously processed image need not be linear in that chart; only the prescribed embedded L and the original p-dominating set require compatible charts. Other previously processed images may move slightly, since their containment in the already engulfed open set is an open condition. Choose each error within that open margin. This distinction is why the argument does not manufacture a common PL structure on O.

## 3. The finite membrane induction

We give the induction explicitly, including the dimension-five bad-locus step. First reduce to X⊂V. Cover the compact X\V by finitely many of its dominating charts. In one such chart enlarge X to a closed linear p-polyhedron P in the chart. Adjoin P to the source Γ and identify points of P and L having the same image; the identifications are simplicial after subdivision because of the common-chart hypothesis. The quotient is again a polyhedron, and the map on the identified copy of L∪P is locally PL embedded. Remove the portion of X in this chart from the unprocessed set. If the operation is proved when no charts remain, induction on the finite number of these charts proves it for X: the enlarged protected subpolyhedron forces g(P)=P, so the conclusion engulfs that piece of X as well. In each finite cover choose smaller chart cores first, so the residual closed set and the finite chart count remain well-defined. This is a reduction of the active set, not a smoothing of X outside its given charts.

For X⊂V prove the assertion by induction on q=dim(H\f^−1(V)), q≤p, after a subdivision. For q=−1 use the identity. To pass from q−1 to q, first apply the induction to the (q−1)-skeleton of the finitely many active q-simplexes, obtaining a new engulfed open set h(V) and a sufficiently close map. The finitely many remaining q-simplexes have boundaries mapping inside that open set. Subdivide further so that any simplex whose image leaves h(V) misses X; this is possible because X is closed, contained in h(V), and the active source is compact. It remains to handle one such q-simplex σ while keeping the already handled pieces engulfed.

The relative nullhomotopy condition π_q(O,h(V))=0 supplies a membrane a*σ, with a fresh cone vertex a, whose side a*∂σ maps into h(V) and whose base σ has the current map. For q=0 this is a path from the new point to h(V); for q=1 it is a disk filling the path together with a path in h(V); for q=2 it is a three-dimensional relative membrane. These are continuous membranes, not claims that a 3-disk embeds in a 5-manifold. Extend the domain by this finite cone. It meets the old protected L only where σ already did, and that image was arranged to miss X. Subdivide the membrane into pieces each mapping inside one Euclidean chart and use the local approximation of section 2. A relative derived subdivision of the elementary collapse of a*σ onto a*∂σ orders these pieces as finitely many elementary expansions. Concretely begin with the pair consisting of the cone simplex and its free face σ; stellar subdivisions of a free pair replace it by free pairs ordered from the new vertex outward, so repeating the subdivisions gives the required finite order. The already included faces of each piece form its lower boundary; its opposite face is the new part to be engulfed.

Consider one (q+1)-simplex τ in that order, lying linearly in a chosen chart. The old source K includes all the earlier pieces, with images already in the current engulfed set, and the new map on K may vary within that open margin. The excess intersection of g(τ) with X∪g(K), outside its prescribed lower boundary, is contained in a linear polyhedron T of dimension at most

    (q+1)+p−n ≤ q−2,

since p≤n−3. This is the calculation from section 2. Choose linear coordinates so τ is a vertical prism with its old boundary as lower floor and its free face as roof. Let J be the projection of T to its floor, and let ΠJ be the union of the vertical segments through J. Then dim J≤q−2 and dim ΠJ≤q−1. In particular for q=1 the excess set is empty; for q=2 in n=5 the obstruction is at most zero-dimensional, and its columns are at most one-dimensional. There is no appeal to four-dimensional Whitney disks.

Apply the q−1 induction to engulf those columns together with the previously handled data. To keep τ itself unchanged during this lower induction, adjoin its current linear image in the chart to the protected source and identify points having the same current image. The quotient is polyhedral because the maps in the chart are finite PL maps; adjoining and identifying the old L uses the same common local charts as before. Its protected image is locally PL embedded. The active part to be newly engulfed is precisely the inverse image of ΠJ, of dimension at most q−1; the rest of the adjoined source is passive or is already engulfed. The inductive invariant allows passive source cells of dimension p+1: changes on the active subpolyhedron extend to them by homotopy extension, relative to L, and they do not enter the bad-intersection estimate. This matters in dimension five, where the membrane itself has dimension three. What is fixed on L is the MAP g, not the ambient homeomorphism on its entire image; a later membrane can cross the passive protected image without creating an additional forbidden intersection. Thus the lower induction does not need to avoid a three-dimensional passive membrane in a five-manifold. Only X and the already engulfed active K form its guard, and they have dimension at most p. This is genuinely the smaller-q induction, not a circular use of the result in dimension q. Pulling the new map back to the old source keeps its prescribed L fixed and keeps the membrane linear in this chart. At this point the old active image and all ΠJ are inside the new engulfed open set.

Now raise the floor of τ to its roof exactly over J. That floor is upper semicontinuous: it jumps up on the closed set J, and agrees with the old continuous floor elsewhere. The part not yet engulfed is the prism above this new floor. Its intersections with X and the old image all lie on the floor, since every excess intersection was put into ΠJ. Section 1, with its closed guard A equal to the old image and ΠJ inside a slightly smaller chart, supplies an actual homeomorphism which fixes those guarded points and engulfs the rest of τ. It extends by the identity outside a compact subset of the chart. Compose it with the preceding engulfing homeomorphism. This completes the elementary expansion, then the membrane, then σ. Repeat for the finite list of active simplexes. The induction terminates because both q and each finite list decrease. The map perturbations can be chosen inside the prescribed ε margins at each stage; their finite sum is bounded by ε. Each ambient operation is one of section 1's explicit isotopies, so their finite composition is an isotopy with compact support in O. This proves the finite primitive.

For locally finite data with compact active part, take the finite union of all source and target stars meeting that compact part, with a smaller open buffer on which the unprocessed data are already in V. Preserve this buffer as part of the old guarded image. The proof above then acts only on finitely many simplexes. Outside those stars extend both maps and the ambient isotopy by their unchanged values. Local finiteness and the open buffer make this continuous. This is the limited noncompact version asserted in the statement; no infinite composition is hidden inside this finite primitive.

## 4. The two-set 2-complex refinement actually used at infinity

Here is the extra refinement needed when only 1-LC, rather than 2-LC, at infinity is supplied. Let P be a closed subpolyhedron of dimension ≤2 inside an open PL region O₀ of a TOP n-manifold M, n≥5. Let V⊂U be connected open subsets, with P\V compact, π₁(M,V)=0, and π₂(U,V)→π₂(M,V) onto. Then a compactly supported ambient homeomorphism h satisfies P⊂h(U).

Choose a closed subpolyhedron P₀⊂P∩V leaving compact Q=closure(P\P₀). The two relative conditions give a homotopy F:P∪(Q×I)→M, starting at the inclusion of P, fixed on P₀ and on the frontier of Q, with F(Q×{1})⊂U. Indeed extend across the vertices and edges by relative 0- and 1-connectivity; for each 2-simplex the obstruction lies in π₂(M,V), and its chosen preimage from π₂(U,V) subtracts it, leaving the relative extension. All this is a finite relative cellular obstruction computation on Q. Near the initial P the map is the inclusion in its actual supplied PL region.

Subdivide Q×I into finitely many chart-small prisms and process them from the final-time U end toward time zero, with faces in collapse order. In dimension at least seven their three-dimensional images have no excess intersections after chartwise approximation, so section 1 pushes each prism into the current engulfed region. In dimension six the intersection of the two-dimensional frontier with a new three-dimensional simplex has dimension at most 2+3−6<0, so the same procedure works on the frontier. In dimension five that dimension is zero. The exceptional points in a new 3-simplex project to finitely many vertical segments of dimension one. Apply the p=1 finite primitive of section 3 to engulf those segments together with the old frontier, keeping the current prism fixed by the same PL-image quotient construction. Then use the guarded upper-semicontinuous-floor stretch of section 1 to engulf the remainder. At each step the old frontier, not the potentially self-intersecting three-dimensional interior of the complete homotopy, is what must stay engulfed. The terminal frontier is the original P itself, so the final ambient image of U contains P. All alterations of F are relative to P at time zero and P₀, so P's actual location is retained. The compactness of Q gives compact support. This supplies the genuine five-dimensional refinement instead of assuming general position embeds a three-dimensional trace in dimension five.

## 5. Exact present scope for the approximation program

The construction above supplies finite raw TOP engulfing operations in dimensions ≥5, and the two-set two-dimensional refinement, with support in the **actual specified work region**. It does not use the original W⁵ smoothing, global TOP product structure, the new cellular approximation conclusion, a TOP4 pair operation, or the smooth S handle theorem. It uses elementary finite polyhedral subdivision, relative homotopy extension, the Euclidean vertex calculation, normality/partitions of unity, and the explicit parallel-line stretch. Countable locally finite choices can be made under the already permitted CC; no unrecorded stronger choice axiom is introduced.

The carrier-enclosure owner has identified three applications still requiring assembly:

* E1: contractible TOP5 open manifold, 1-LC at infinity, is R⁵. Siebenmann's §3 proves this by finite two-set engulfing, dual-skeleton stretching, and the monotone open-cell union argument, **after** a union-of-two-open-cells construction. That initial construction is not proved in this note. No R⁵ recognition conclusion is therefore asserted here merely from the finite primitive.
* E2: a compact TOP5 h-cobordism with prescribed standard torus-product side has overlapping closed collars of its two frontiers whose union is the cobordism. This is an invertibility conclusion, not a product conclusion. Siebenmann's §4.1 requires a 2-connectivity assumption or a PL structure on boundary×R. The latter may be available from the actual standard frontier in the special case; it is not inferred by smoothing the unknown W⁵. The infinite collar construction and its proper/end controls still need to be assembled from these finite moves. No invertibility conclusion is asserted here without that assembly.
* E3: the relative CE-preimage engulfing homeomorphisms in the Main Lemma. The carrier-enclosure owner reports that the particular source interior already inherits an actual DIFF structure from the independently supplied P∞; if so an actual PL buffer may be chosen there without the circular PL-extension argument for an unknown TOP5 source. The hypotheses and proper compact-support iteration, including the actual fixed side/open germ, still belong to that application. The finite theorem alone supplies neither a family bundle theorem nor the zero-handle family engine.

In particular a request to preserve an end or K-open germ must bind the actual exhaustion/work regions and verify the relative homotopies there. Compact support of each finite move permits extension across a protected germ; it does not by itself prove properness or convergence of an infinite sequence. Those are explicit remaining application duties, not silent consequences of this local construction.

## 6. Added assembly: two open cells in a contractible TOP5 manifold

Sections 6–9 are new applications of the finite primitive and require their own review. They supersede the E1 assembly hold in section 5 once accepted; E2 and E3 remain separate application duties.

**Accepted Brown contract, authorized by root for this new assembly:** an actual bicollared S^(n−1) in S^n bounds actual topological n-balls, with ball parametrizations, including n=5; a specified boundary homeomorphism then extends by the explicit radial Alexander extension in those parametrizations. This is the exact generalized TOP Schoenflies subargument already independently accepted within the earlier new proper-tube-complement construction. Its proof is not reread or copied here. It is not an arbitrary relative annulus or half-product theorem.

First note a consequence of this contract. An embedding e:R^5→R^5, restricted to any compact coordinate closed ball, extends to a self-homeomorphism of R^5 agreeing with e on that closed ball. Its boundary is bicollared by the open embedding. Compactify the target to S^5, and use the contract on this boundary. Parametrize the exterior ball and extend the required boundary map by Alexander. Adjust the image of the exterior interior point ∞ to ∞ by a self-homeomorphism of the exterior ball fixed on its boundary: join the two interior points by a polygonal path in a ball chart and move them along that path by finitely many small ball-supported translations with radial cutoffs. Gluing the given map of the inner ball to this exterior extension gives a self-homeomorphism of S^5 fixing ∞. Removing ∞ proves the consequence. This supplies a compact-chart extension, with no fixed outer sphere asserted.

It follows that a manifold exhausted by nested coordinate open cells is R^5. Here is the actual chart construction. Let U_j≅R^5 be an increasing open-cell cover, and choose compact exhaustion sets C_j. Given a chart φ_j on U_j and a coordinate closed ball D_(j−1) on which earlier charts agree, choose a larger φ_j-coordinate closed ball D_j containing D_(j−1)∪C_j and with φ_j(D_j) containing the Euclidean ball of radius j. By the preceding compact-chart extension, a chart on U_(j+1) can be adjusted to agree with φ_j on D_j. Iterate. The consistent limit is a homeomorphism: every point has a neighborhood on which it is one fixed chart; the compatible coordinate balls cover M, and their images contain balls of arbitrarily large radius. Thus the limit and its inverse are continuous and its image is all R^5. This proves the monotone-open-cell fact in the exact form needed here.

Now let M be contractible. Given a compact set C, finitely many chart cores cover it. We explain how to reduce three such cores to two open coordinate cells while retaining a specified compact core in each of the first two cells. Choose nested concentric cores K_i^α⊂int K_i^β in the first two charts, and an inner and outer core in the third chart. In the first work region M_1=M\K_1^β, the open set V_1=(first chart)\K_1^β is an annulus S^4×(0,1). Both M_1 and V_1 are 2-connected. Indeed van Kampen applied to M_1 and the first chart gives π₁(M_1)=π₁(M)=0, since their overlap is this simply connected annulus; Mayer–Vietoris gives H₂(M_1)=0; Hurewicz gives π₂(M_1)=0. The same statements for V_1 are immediate. Hence π_i(M_1,V_1)=0 for i≤2.

Triangulate the outer core of the third chart, keeping its inner core as a subcomplex and subdividing so no simplex meets both K_1^α and M\int K_1^β, or both K_2^α and M\int K_2^β. In its derived subdivision use the primal 2-skeleton and the complementary dual 2-skeleton. The primal skeleton outside K_1^β is a closed 2-dominated subset of M_1. Its part outside V_1 is compact; the possible accumulation on K_1^β occurs inside the first chart and hence inside V_1. The finite primitive engulfs it into V_1 by a compactly supported homeomorphism of M_1. Extend by the identity across K_1^β. Its image of the first open chart, call it U_1, contains the whole primal skeleton and K_1^β. Repeat in M\K_2^β for the dual skeleton, giving an open cell U_2 containing that skeleton and K_2^β.

In each simplex of the derived subdivision every point away from the two skeletons is uniquely on a join segment from the primal to the dual skeleton. The open sets U_1,U_2 contain neighborhoods of its two respective ends. Along each such segment use the increasing piecewise linear map which expands a sufficiently short initial segment in U_1 to the complement of a sufficiently short terminal segment in U_2. Choose these lengths continuously on simplex faces, using the finite minima of their open margins; equivalent vertical-floor coordinates are those of section 1. The formulas agree on common faces. Taper this stretching to the identity in the annular buffer between the third inner and outer cores. This gives a homeomorphism θ supported in the third outer chart core, preserving every simplex as a set, with the third inner core contained in θ(U_1)∪U_2. Also K_1^α⊂θ(U_1): the inverse image of a point of K_1^α stays in its same simplex, and that entire simplex lies in K_1^β⊂U_1 by the mesh choice. The second core K_2^α remains in U_2. Thus these two open cells cover the three prescribed inner cores while retaining the first two individually.

Partition the compact third core into two compact subsets lying respectively in θ(U_1) and U_2; such a finite closed refinement exists by normality. Enlarge the coordinate core inside each open cell to contain its assigned subset and its retained earlier core. This lets the same three-to-two reduction absorb the remaining chart cores one at a time. It proves that C is contained in two open cells, with any initially prescribed compact first and second chart cores retained individually.

Apply this relative-core version to a compact exhaustion of M. At stage j, choose large compact coordinate balls in the two current cells containing all the portions of the j-th exhaustion set assigned to them. At stage j+1 use them as the first two prescribed cores in the reduction of a finite chart cover of the next exhaustion set. The new pair of cells contains those two old balls individually. Choose new coordinate balls containing them in their interiors. For each of the two labels we obtain an increasing sequence of coordinate open balls; their two unions cover M. By the compact-chart-extension argument above, each union is an open cell. Thus

    M=O₁∪O₂,        O₁≅R⁵≅O₂.

This proves the previously missing union-of-two-open-cells construction from the actual finite primitive, not from a global PL structure or the recognition conclusion.

## 7. Added assembly: contractible and 1-LC at infinity implies R5

Let M be contractible and 1-LC at infinity. Given an arbitrary compact C⊂M, choose a connected open neighborhood U of infinity disjoint from C. Choose a smaller connected neighborhood V⊂U so that π₁(V)→π₁(U) is zero. Contractibility of M gives π₁(M,V)=0. The exact sequences for (U,V) and (M,V) show that π₂(U,V)→π₂(M,V) is onto: the latter boundary identifies π₂(M,V) with π₁(V), while every loop of V bounds a disk in U by the chosen zero map. These are exactly the two-set hypotheses of section 4.

Use M=O₁∪O₂ from section 6. Choose in the standard PL coordinates of O₂ a closed full-dimensional polyhedral neighborhood L of M\O₁, with its interior containing that closed set and L closed in M. This can be done by locally finite cubical stars subordinate to O₂: first choose a closed neighborhood of M\O₁ contained in O₂ by normality, then refine the chart mesh locally so its selected closed stars lie there. Triangulate O₂ with L a subcomplex. Apply section 4 to its closed 2-skeleton P=L^(2), obtaining a compactly supported homeomorphism h with P⊂h(U).

Choose compact X containing supp(h) and M\U. Add all closed triangulation simplexes meeting both X and L, and their finitely many incident stars, to obtain a compact enlargement X̄. Local finiteness makes this finite. Choose a coordinate closed ball D⊂O₁ and a finite full-dimensional subcomplex L′⊂L so that their interiors together cover X̄. This is possible because int L∪O₁=M, and X̄ is compact. In selecting the finite stars use the same triangulation on L and L′.

Let Q be the dual 2-skeleton in L′ complementary to P. In the open manifold M\D, the set Q\D is closed and 2-dominated; its part outside O₁\D is compact. As in section 6, M\D and O₁\D are 2-connected, so the finite primitive supplies a compactly supported homeomorphism g₀ of M\D with Q\D⊂g₀(O₁\D). Extend g₀ by the identity on D. Thus g₀(O₁) contains D and the full dual skeleton Q.

Stretch h(U) toward g₀(O₁) along the primal-dual join segments of the triangulation, as in section 6, so that a simplex-preserving homeomorphism θ satisfies

    L′ ⊂ θh(U) ∪ g₀(O₁).

Use an outer finite star buffer in L for the taper. The enlargement X̄ was chosen as a union of whole affected stars, so θ preserves X̄ as a set: any simplex on which θ acts is either contained in X̄ or disjoint from its interior, and outside the buffer θ is the identity. Since h(U) contains M\X, θh(U) contains M\X̄. Combining this with the covering of X̄ by int L′ and int D gives

    M = θh(U) ∪ g₀(O₁).

Pull back by θh. The open cell (θh)^−1g₀(O₁) contains M\U and therefore C. Every compact subset of M is consequently contained in a coordinate open cell. Choose these cells successively to contain a compact exhaustion and a larger closed coordinate ball in the previous cell. The compact-chart-extension limit from section 6 identifies their union with R⁵. This is the actual recognition conclusion.

All homeomorphisms before this last chart limit have compact support, and the final limit is justified by exact agreement on an exhaustion of coordinate closed balls, with image balls of radii tending to infinity. No unproved assertion that an arbitrary infinite product of compactly supported maps converges is used.

## 8. Added assembly: the exact marked S4 half-product

Let E be a connected metrizable TOP5 manifold with compact boundary supplied with a parametrized collar c:S⁴×[0,ε)→E. Assume the inclusion of its boundary is a homotopy equivalence, E has one end, and that end is 1-LC. The exact conclusion is a homeomorphism

    Φ:E→S⁴×[0,∞)

which equals c(x,t)↦(x,t) on a smaller actual collar. This is the special relative half needed for the B0 endpoint reduction; it is not asserted for arbitrary nonsimply-connected ends.

Attach a standard 5-ball along the given boundary parametrization. The result M has no boundary and is an open TOP5 manifold, using the actual given collar at the seam. Van Kampen and Mayer–Vietoris give π₁(M)=0 and reduced H_*(M)=0, because ∂E→E is a homotopy equivalence and the attached ball kills that boundary sphere. Manifold CW type and Hurewicz then show that M is contractible. Its end is unchanged by the compact attached ball, so it is 1-LC. Section 7 gives an actual homeomorphism M→R⁵.

The image D of the attached closed ball has an actual bicollared boundary in R⁵, by its interior ball collar and the supplied exterior collar in E. Compactify to S⁵. The accepted Brown contract gives a parametrization q of the complementary closed ball S⁵\int D. The point ∞ is an interior point of that ball. Adjust q by a ball homeomorphism fixed on its boundary to send ∞ to its center, using the finite interior point-moving construction of section 6. Correct the remaining boundary parametrization by an explicit radial Alexander extension. We now have a homeomorphism of E=(S⁵\int D)\{∞} to D⁵\{0}, with exactly the prescribed boundary map. The radial coordinates x,r↦(x,−log r) give the marked half-product S⁴×[0,∞).

This first map need not preserve the prescribed collar. Choose δ strictly inside that collar. Remove its part with t<δ, apply the same capping/recognition construction to the truncated E_δ with its transported boundary parametrization, and target S⁴×[δ,∞). Define Φ there by that construction and on c(S⁴×[0,δ]) by Φ(c(x,t))=(x,t). They agree on the full common boundary t=δ. Pasting gives a homeomorphism, with inverse pasted in the same way, and it agrees with the actual original collar on [0,δ]. A homeomorphism between these locally compact spaces is proper. This completes the marked half-product construction, rather than deducing it from an unspecified homeomorphism of the unmarked open interior.
