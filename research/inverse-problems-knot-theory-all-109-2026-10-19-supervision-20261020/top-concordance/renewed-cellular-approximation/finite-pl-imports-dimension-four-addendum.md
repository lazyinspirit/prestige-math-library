# Narrow dimension-four addendum to the finite PL imports

This answers the E1 author's new scope query. It does not modify the independently reviewed source receipt, credit the old endpoint draft, or consume a review of that draft.

## Actual finite disk primitive

The affine cone proof in `finite-pl-imports-source-receipt.md` §1 works in R4 as well as R5: the finitely many forbidden affine spans of two polygonal edges, or of one circle edge and one protected one-complex edge, have dimension at most THREE. Their union therefore has empty interior in R4. A generic apex in the interior of a chosen convex four-ball gives an embedded finite PL two-cell with the specified polygonal boundary and disjoint from a given disjoint finite protected one-complex. The same elementary intersection argument proves this statement. No four-dimensional Whitney move is involved. No local flatness at the apex is asserted or required for the output “polyhedral two-cell.”

Consequently a polygonal circle in a SUPPLIED standard PL four-sphere bounds an embedded finite PL two-cell in that four-sphere, avoiding any supplied disjoint finite protected one-complex. Choose a point outside the finite one-complexes and use a standard punctured PL-sphere coordinate to R4; the compact images lie in a convex four-ball and the construction pulls back. This is a standard PL-sphere input, not a claim that an arbitrary TOP four-sphere or arbitrary locally flat sphere has an available PL chart. In the actual cube-boundary use the standard PL structure is already displayed.

For precision, a punctured standard PL sphere has the required locally finite PL coordinate without a PL stereographic formula: express it as the double of a standard PL four-ball, put the omitted point at the center of the second ball, and map concentric polyhedral annuli in that punctured second ball onto successively larger polyhedral annuli outside the first ball. Triangulate corresponding annuli by the same finite product triangulations and extend linearly on their simplices. The first ball maps identically to the central ball. This is a proper locally finite PL homeomorphism to R4. Every compact set away from the omitted point meets only finitely many of these annuli, so the circle and retained finite complex still have finite polygonal images. The coordinate is used only to construct an intrinsic finite disk in the displayed sphere.

If the intended disk is subsequently moved into one selected side of a standard sphere collar, the actual collar S4×[0,eta) gives this directly: take a continuous PL height on a disk triangulation, zero on its boundary and positive on its interior, and use its graph in the collar. Graph injectivity gives an embedded disk on that chosen side, with its original boundary. Matching a prescribed seam or avoiding additional disks is separate actual geometric data, not automatic from this graph construction.

## Actual finite ambient move in dimension four

Zeeman Lemma 3 is stated for every k>n. The fully read proof pp354–355 therefore applies with n=1,k=4. In its join model the perpendicular simplex Z has dimension k−n−1=2, and N=Y*boundary(Z)*boundary(B)*boundary(C) is four-dimensional. The same join-linear extension fixes its entire boundary and realizes the legal triangle move with arbitrarily small ambient PL support. The finite protected-complex/small-neighborhood qualifications from the original receipt remain unchanged. It is not restricted to the k=5 instance.

Printed 355's example explicitly treats a polygonal circle in E3 inside E4 and explains the difference between its cone disk (possibly locally knotted at the apex) and the locally unknotted disk obtained by the Lemma1/Lemma3 construction. Thus the primary itself warns against silently equating those two disk outputs. This addendum needs only the embedded polyhedral disk and actual legal finite triangle slides, not the stronger local-flat-disk conclusion.
