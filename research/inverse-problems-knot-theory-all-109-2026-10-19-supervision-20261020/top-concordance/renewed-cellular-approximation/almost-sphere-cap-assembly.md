## 1. A boundary-attached straight arc has a displayed ball quotient

In the coordinate cylinder C=D⁴×[0,2] let L={0}×[0,1]. Choose a continuous function δ:D⁴→[0,1] with δ(0)=0, δ(u)>0 for u≠0, and δ=1 near ∂D⁴. Define F:C→C by F(u,t)=(u,f_u(t)), where

    f_u(t)=δ(u)t                      (0≤t≤1),
    f_u(t)=δ(u)+(2−δ(u))(t−1)         (1≤t≤2).

The formulas agree at t=1 and vary continuously with u. For u≠0, f_u is a strictly increasing homeomorphism [0,2]→[0,2]. For u=0 its sole non-singleton fiber is [0,1], sent to 0. The map fixes the whole boundary of C, except that the attached arc's interior also maps to its boundary endpoint; on ∂C itself it is the identity. It is onto and has exactly L as its non-singleton point inverse. Compactness and the Hausdorff quotient property show C/L≅C. It is a homeomorphism off L, with the inverse obtained from the two linear formulas.

Consequently if an arc in an actual parametrized 5-ball is ambiently standardized, relative its supplied boundary endpoint collar, to such a boundary-attached straight arc, its quotient is an actual ball. This finite quotient operation uses neither a four-dimensional embedded filling disk nor an annulus theorem. The required standardization of the arc is a separate input.

This is a ball-plus-supplied-annulus construction, not a claim that an arbitrary annulus between two balls is standard.

Choose a smaller parallel sphere Σ₀ inside H. Its opposite complementary closed ball K contains the other side G, the original Σ and the collar between Σ₀ and Σ. The chosen one-sided collar contains an actual arc L from a point of Σ₀ to p, with its initial endpoint normal to Σ₀. In collar coordinates it is the straight normal fiber over p. It lies in K, starts on ∂K and otherwise lies in intK. Its interior is locally flat. Its endpoint p can still be wild in the ambient manifold; no flattening is inferred from the intrinsic collar alone.

The owed arc operation is exactly to standardize this L in K relative a smaller initial boundary germ. If supplied, section 1 identifies K/L with a ball. The remaining map from K/L to ClG must be constructed using a cusped collar on the OUTSIDE of Σ away from p and the displayed fiber-collapse on the inside. A genuine cusped annular embedding across Σ away from p, continuous at p, remains to be written. Thus even the one-sided almost-sphere lemma is not yet being claimed complete by this draft.

## 3. Subsequent finite interfaces and exact scope

Once the one-sided almost-sphere lemma is actually proved, Cantrell's chamber construction (Transactions1963 printed188–190) removes the one-sided-collar requirement at the exceptional point. It first takes an actual cusped annular neighborhood of Σ\{p}, cuts out a curvilinear simplex with vertex p inside that neighborhood, applies the one-sided lemma to its image sphere, and forms alternating ball chambers. Every chamber is then a parametrized ball; its common boundary maps extend by radial Alexander maps. The chamber diameters tend to zero at the one shared point, which must be checked for both the glued map and its inverse. This is a candidate route, not a missing proof replaced by the phrase “Mazur swindle”. The curvilinear simplex, cusp compatibility and exact gluing are owed local constructions.

The resulting actual almost-sphere theorem would standardize a proper locally flat hyperplane R⁴⊂R⁵: compactify at infinity, apply the theorem to its sphere closure, then choose the ambient sphere parameter to send the compactification point to infinity. Each closed side is then an actual half-space with its supplied boundary parameter adjusted by the product extension of a homeomorphism R⁴→R⁴.

In that coordinate neighborhood, choose a closed half-space-shaped neighborhood P of H⁴ with boundary a proper hyperplane, narrowing toward H⁴ at infinity. The narrowing must be chosen using the actual inverse coordinate map's uniform continuity on compact annular pieces, so h⁻¹(P) is closed in ambient R⁵. If both boundary hyperplanes have already been standardized by the almost-sphere theorem, extend h across the other half-spaces with the product extension of its boundary map. The result is an actual ambient half-string standardization. The closedness of h⁻¹(P), not merely closedness of the boundary alone, is the needed gluing condition.
