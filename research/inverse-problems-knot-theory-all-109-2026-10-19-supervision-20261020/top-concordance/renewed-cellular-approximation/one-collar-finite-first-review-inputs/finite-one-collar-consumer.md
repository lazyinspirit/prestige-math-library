Put A=M−O₁. If A is empty, O₁=M already contains C. Otherwise A is closed in M and lies in N. We now earn the closed PL buffer needed by the finite placement argument. Fix a compatible metric d on M and put

    G={x∈M:dist(x,A)<dist(x,M−N)/10}.

It contains A and its closure in M is contained in N: a point outside N in that closure would have distance zero from A and hence belong to A, a contradiction. Define on N

    ℓ(x)=max(dist(x,M−G),dist(x,A))/4.

It is positive and 1/4-Lipschitz. Choose the following actual locally finite PL triangulation T of N with every simplex image of d-diameter less than one thousandth of the minimum of ℓ on that simplex.

Here is the elementary mesh construction, including its countable-choice scope. Start with the transported countable product triangulation of Rˢ×Tⁿ×(0,∞); give its abstract simplices compatible unit regular Euclidean metrics. For each compact original simplex σ, uniform continuity supplies δσ>0 such that parameter subsets of diameter below δσ have d-image diameter below minσℓ/1000. The δσ may be least sufficiently small dyadic numbers. For a face τ assign

    aτ=min{δσ·10^{−(dimσ−dimτ)}:σ contains τ}.

The minimum is over finitely many cofaces, by local finiteness. It is positive, and a proper face τ of σ has aτ≤aσ/10. Induct in dimension over original simplices. The already triangulated boundary of σ has mesh≤aσ/10. Extend it by finitely many concentric homothetic copies toward the barycenter, with radial spacing less than aσ/10, triangulating the intervening simplex prisms by a common vertex order; stop at an inner copy of diameter below aσ/10 and cone it to the barycenter. All new simplices have diameter below aσ. Shared original faces retain exactly their previously assigned triangulations. This is a finite subdivision construction, not a claim about an adapted collapse. The number of dimension stages is finite; choices on the countably many independent simplices at one stage need at most ACω. Each original simplex is subdivided finitely, so the result is locally finite in N and has the required image mesh.

Write St_T(S) for the union of closed simplices meeting S and all their faces. Then St_T⁶(A)⊂G. To see this, a point in that iterated star can be connected to a point a∈A by a chain of at most six simplex diameters. If S is the sum of those diameters, the mesh bound and Lipschitz bound give S≤(6/1000)(ℓ(a)+S/4), hence S<ℓ(a)<dist(a,M−G). Thus the chain stays in G. Each iterated star is a subcomplex closed in N; since it is contained in G and closure_M(G)⊂N, it is also CLOSED IN M. Put

    L₁=St_T(A),    L₂=St_T⁴(A),    P=L₂^(2).

## The finite dual complex and the join stretch

For this finite consumer use the ACTUAL E2 source W. Choose an actual V₁-collar neighborhood U⁺ in M, disjoint from C, and a deeper such V⁺. Their complements in M are compact. Put U=U⁺∩int M and V=V⁺∩int M. These interior inclusions into int M are homotopy equivalences: the interior V₁-face collar maps to int W by the given h-cobordism face equivalence and the actual face/boundary collar pushes. Thus (int M,V) is 2-connected (in fact all its relative homotopy groups vanish). The present unit does not replace this input by a generic proper-homotopy argument.

P is closed in int M, and P−V is compact because P is closed in M, disjoint from B, and M−V⁺ is compact. The adopted p≤2 finite engine in int M therefore gives h, with compact support there, such that P⊂h(V)⊂h(U). Extend it by identity to M. Choose compact X containing C, supp(h), and M−U⁺. Then h(U⁺) contains M−X. The actual U⁺ is disjoint from C and will be retained in the final complement argument; it is not replaced by its interior when boundary coverage is used.

Adjoin to X all whole simplices of L₂ meeting X, with their faces, to form X̄. Only finitely many occur: X∩L₂ is compact because L₂ is closed in M, and T is locally finite in N. Thus X̄ is compact. The compact set X̄−int L₁ is contained in O₁, since M−O₁⊂int L₁. Choose a standard ambient-closed parallel collar D⊂O₁ whose INTERIOR contains that set, with a larger closed parallel buffer still in O₁. Such levels are available by the adopted ray-reparametrization interface applied to this actual collar. Consequently

    J=X̄−int D

is compact and lies in int L₁. Let L′ be the union of all closed simplices of T meeting J and their faces. It is finite and lies in L₁: if a simplex meets the interior of a subcomplex, that simplex belongs to the subcomplex, since its relative interior otherwise lies outside and approaches the meeting point. We have X̄⊂D∪L′. If L′ is empty, set g=θ=id; then X̄⊂D⊂O₁ and M=h(U⁺)∪O₁ already. The final containment argument below applies directly. In the remaining construction assume L′ is nonempty.

Take the finite outer star region R=St_T²(L′). It lies in L₂, and L′ lies in its interior in N. Its frontier F=Fr_N(R) is a subcomplex: any simplex incident to a missing coface is on that frontier, together with all its faces. These explicit closed-star neighborhoods provide the inner/outer buffer used below. We do NOT infer that R is a PL ball or invoke a regular-neighborhood straightening theorem.

In βR split the vertices by ORIGINAL T-face rank: low rank≤2, high rank≥3. Its low subcomplex lies in P. Let Q be its high full subcomplex. A chain has at most the ranks 3,…,m, so dim Q≤m−3. This certificate is for this actual fixed Q; no dual of a later refinement is assigned that dimension. The finite Q is PL in the transported structure on N. In each of its own PL charts, intersecting with a small Euclidean cube inside M−D directly proves that Q−D is CLOSED locally (m−3)-dominated in M−D. Also

    (Q−D)−(O₁−D)=Q−O₁

is compact there. The raw pair is ONLY (M−D,O₁−D). It is a homotopy-equivalence pair by the actual collar-height cutoff already displayed in `selected-e2-higher-top-consumer.md`, since B→M is a homotopy equivalence.     Q⊂g(O₁),    g=id on an open neighborhood of D.

The points of Q∩D were already covered and fixed. The raw support may range throughout M−D; it is NOT required to lie in O₂. Q and the PL structure of O₂ remain fixed chart data while only the open set O₁ moves.

Here is the actual finite join operation. On βR every point outside its low/high endpoint subcomplexes has its unique expression (1−t)a+tb in a simplex, with a low and b high. The parameter t is the sum of its high-vertex barycentric weights. The endpoint-pair space E is the finite union of products of the corresponding low/high faces. Its t≤δ and t≥1−δ blocks are explicit join neighborhoods, with their mapping-cylinder collapse to the endpoint faces. The low endpoints lie in h(U⁺), and the high endpoints lie in g(O₁). Compactness gives ε∈(0,1/4) so that t≤2ε lies in the former and t≥1−2ε in the latter.

The endpoint pairs in simplices of L′ form a compact E_in⊂E; those in the frontier subcomplex F form a compact E_out. They are disjoint since L′⊂int_N R. Choose a continuous λ:E→[0,1], one on E_in and zero on E_out, by the distance formula. Let T be the increasing piecewise linear interval homeomorphism fixing 0,1 with T(ε)=1−ε. Replace t by (1−λ)t+λT(t) on each join segment. This defines θ on R. The low/high partition agrees on shared faces; endpoints are fixed, and the uniform positive finite slope bounds give continuity and inverse continuity even at endpoint degeneracies. Every original T-simplex is preserved setwise. On F the map is identity (either an endpoint is fixed or its pair lies in E_out). Extend θ by identity outside R. Closed-set pasting on R and M−int_N R proves that this is an actual compactly supported ambient homeomorphism; replace λ by τλ to obtain its actual isotopy. Its support is compact in N⊂int M.

On every inner segment, λ=1, so θ expands the low neighborhood up to t≥1−ε, while g(O₁) already covers t≥1−2ε. Hence

    L′⊂θh(U⁺)∪g(O₁).

The map θ preserves all original T-simplices, including those outside R where it is identity. Also D⊂g(O₁), and X̄⊂D∪L′. Thus

    M=θh(U⁺)∪g(O₁).

Apply the ACTUAL homeomorphism (θh)⁻¹ to that covering equality. It gives

    M=U⁺∪(θh)⁻¹g(O₁).

Now use the original U⁺∩C=∅ to conclude

    C⊂(θh)⁻¹g(O₁).

### Compression into an arbitrary specified initial collar

The O₁ just used can be the collar produced by stretching, not a global ambient image of the original input collar I. To supply the finite ambient-image conclusion claimed at the start, let N_C be the actual collar just containing C. Its coordinate projection of compact C to B is compact and its height is bounded by T. Choose a compact base neighborhood B₁ and a>0 such that the N_C strip B₁×[0,a] lies in I. Choose T>a larger than the heights of C. A continuous base cutoff λ is one on that compact projection and supported in int B₁. Choose an increasing height homeomorphism φ fixing [0,a/2] and [2T,∞), with φ(T)<a. The ray map (b,t)↦(b,(1−λ(b))t+λ(b)φ(t)) is a homeomorphism supported in the compact strip B₁×[a/2,2T], extends by identity, and sends C into I. Its inverse sends I to a collar containing C, with compact ambient support and the same fixed boundary germ. This is a finite compression, not an assertion that arbitrary entire open collars are globally ambient equivalent.
