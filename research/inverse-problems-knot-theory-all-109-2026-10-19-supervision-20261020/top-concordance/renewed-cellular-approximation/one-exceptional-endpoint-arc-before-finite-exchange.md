# New one-exceptional-endpoint arc construction in dimension five

This is new research mathematics, not yet an adopted supplier. Its finite locally-flat compact-arc conversion is consumed only at root's adopted Statement contract, without a proof reread. The endpoint/annular assembly below is new and requires its own review.

## Input and desired output

Let K be an actual parametrized TOP 5-ball in a 5-manifold. Let A⊂K be an embedded compact arc, with one endpoint q∈∂K and its other endpoint p∈intK. Assume A\{p} is locally flat, its interior misses ∂K, and near q an ACTUAL supplied boundary collar identifies the pair with a straight boundary-normal arc. The output is a homeomorphism of K, preserving a smaller supplied collar at q, which carries A to a standard boundary-attached straight arc. No isotopy of the limiting homeomorphism is claimed.

Use a ball parameter compatible with the smaller actual boundary collar. It can be obtained from any boundary-ball parameter by truncating the given collar, applying the accepted Brown ball contract to its inner parallel sphere, adjusting that sphere's parameter radially, and pasting back the actual collar. This uses a supplied annulus, not an arbitrary annulus identification. Work henceforth in the interior's actual standard PL ball coordinates. The supplied boundary-normal germ is PL in these coordinates.

## 1. Making the punctured arc locally polyhedral

Root adopted `renewed-relative-arc-polyhedralization/root-arc-operation-adoption.json`. The exact contract, clarified by its author, permits the protected PL germ set L to be EMPTY, or to consist of only one existing overlap. It requires a whole TOP pair chart for the compact subarc, extended along its axis past the compact endpoints. It yields an arbitrarily small compactly supported ambient homeomorphism taking that subarc to an ambient PL arc, fixed on smaller neighborhoods of the protected actual PL germs. No exceptional endpoint or limit isotopy is part of that provider.

Choose a locally finite partition of the parameter interval (0,1] of A\{p}, subordinate to the supplied local pair-chart neighborhoods; order its compact intervals from q toward p. Such a partition is obtained by finite rational subdivision on each dyadic compact parameter interval, choosing the least subdivision whose closed enlarged pieces lie in the chosen local charts. Consecutive pieces overlap on nontrivial smaller subintervals, and only finitely many pieces meet any compact parameter interval away from zero. Refine so their ambient neighborhoods have diameter tending to zero toward p and meet A only in the corresponding enlarged parameter intervals. Compactness and injectivity of A guarantee that a compact central piece is separated from the closed remaining arc, including p.

Apply the finite provider to the first compact charted piece, preserving the actual PL collar germ at q if it meets it; an empty protected set would also be permitted for an interior seed. Apply it successively to the next pieces, protecting the already PL overlap and all old portions lying in its support neighborhood. The latter are just finitely many PL subarcs because the subdivision is locally finite away from p. Choose the new support disjoint from every earlier piece except those overlaps. Outside that support, earlier PL pieces are unchanged.

At stage i choose both forward and inverse displacements below 2^−i times a positive control function tending to zero at p, and below the corresponding inverse-continuity tolerance of the current finite composition. These choices are part of the finite provider's stated arbitrary-smallness contract. Each support lies in an ambient neighborhood of diameter tending to zero at p; outside p the family is locally finite. Thus every compact subset away from p sees only finitely many stages, and the product and inverse product stabilize locally there. At p their displacements tend to zero uniformly, since all sufficiently late supports lie in any prescribed neighborhood of p. The resulting maps are inverse homeomorphisms, fix p, preserve the smaller boundary germ, and make A locally PL at every point except possibly p. This is an actual countable assembly, not an assertion that a forward Cauchy sequence alone has a homeomorphic limit.

## 2. Closing the arc near its exceptional endpoint

Take a small coordinate ball B around p contained in intK. The already polyhedral punctured arc is a countable locally finite union of compact PL segments away from p. Choose a ray from p whose direction avoids their radial projections. Each segment's forbidden directions form a closed subset of S⁴ with empty interior; a nested sequence of closed rational balls of decreasing diameters, avoiding the next finite list at each step, selects such a direction without arbitrary choice. The resulting ray misses A except at p.

Use a short initial piece of that ray and a finite generic polygonal connector to a point q' of A outside a smaller ball. The connector can be confined away from p and made disjoint from the rest of A: there only finitely many PL arc pieces occur on its compact working set, and the dimension bound for two finite 1-complexes is 1+1<5. It can also be kept disjoint from the ray except at its own endpoint. The connector together with the subarc A[p,q'] forms a simple closed curve C, locally PL except at p. Only a small ball about p will be used to tame C, so q', the remainder of A and the original boundary collar can be protected.

## 3. Shrinking cubical annuli: the finite geometric step

The following is the actual single-point construction from Cantrell–Edwards1963 printed452–453, reconstructed at n=5. Choose nested closed coordinate cubes N_i centered at p, with diameters tending to zero. By continuity of the inverse curve parameter, choose N_i so every excursion of C outside N_i whose two endpoints lie on ∂N_i is contained in intN_(i−1). A small finite PL perturbation of each cube boundary makes its intersections with C finite; arrange this on alternating cube boundaries so the resulting support annuli are locally finite away from p.

For each outside excursion u in intN_(i−1)\N_i, join its endpoints by a PL boundary arc w in ∂N_i missing the other curve intersections. The circle u∪w bounds an embedded PL 2-disk in intN_(i−1): first take a nullhomotopy in that coordinate ball, triangulate it relative the PL boundary, and use general position 2+2<5 to make it embedded while keeping the boundary. The same perturbation makes its interior miss all other finite curve pieces, since 2+1<5. The disk may initially cross ∂N_i. Take an innermost closed intersection curve on the disk, replace its enclosed disk by a close parallel copy of its spanning disk in the PL cube boundary, and push to the required side. Here the boundary has dimension four; an embedded intersection circle bounds a PL disk there after general position of a 2-disk, since 2+2=4 can have double points. THIS LAST EMBEDDED BOUNDARY DISK HAS NOT BEEN PROVIDED BY THAT DIMENSION COUNT. Accordingly the boundary-exchange claim requires further construction, rather than being inferred from the ambient five-dimensional inequality.

If those boundary exchanges are actually supplied, the resulting disk meets ∂N_i only in w, lies otherwise outside N_i, and misses C except on u. Finite elementary triangle moves across the disk carry u onto w and then just inside N_i, with ambient support in a small disk neighborhood disjoint from the rest of C. Repeat over all excursions, protecting the two first-entering curve pieces. The resulting curve meets ∂N_i in exactly two points. Apply the construction in alternating annuli so supports can be pasted on their fixed outer and inner cube boundaries.

Once every cube boundary has exactly two intersections, finite PL disk moves in each annulus straighten the two curve pieces to radial segments, matching at the already fixed endpoints. Even/odd annular products have locally finite supports away from p and supports shrinking to p. Their maps and inverses are continuous at p. This would make C, and hence A, PL at p while preserving the chosen outer boundary germ.

## 4. Current exact obstruction and alternative finite operation

The finite disk-boundary exchange in section 3 cannot be claimed merely from the printed source's phrase “similar process”: the relevant disk is sought IN the 4-dimensional cube boundary. No four-dimensional Whitney theorem or disk-embedding theorem has been supplied. The source's construction uses a join of an interior point to the intersection curve's boundary pieces, followed by a small PL general-position perturbation. Its exact disjointness and support have to be reconstructed before this draft earns the exceptional-point conclusion.

A sufficient alternative is an ambient five-dimensional finite disk exchange which removes all intersections with ∂N_i while keeping the ambient disk embedded and missing the curve, without embedding a new 2-disk into the four-dimensional boundary. This is a precise local raw operation, and the accepted p≤2 TOP finite engulfing may be relevant to its proof. It is not the forbidden four-dimensional meridian filling from the original cap problem.

Thus section 1 is a completed countable propagation at the adopted finite arc contract, but sections 3–4 explicitly hold the remaining exceptional-point taming claim. The standard boundary-attached arc quotient is already written independently in `almost-sphere-cap-assembly.md` section 1; it can be applied only after the actual tame-arc standardization has been completed.
