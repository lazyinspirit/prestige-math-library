# Singular and partial parameterization, and the common quotient

This is new research authoring for the renewed TOP tower obligation. It does not rereview an accepted supplier, change a canonical item, supply a native review, or authorize a gate. Source read: Freedman–Quinn, *Topology of 4-Manifolds*, §§4.2–4.4, printed pp. 62–75 (PDF pp. 68–81). The PNGs in `sources/` were read visually; the separately generated OCR in `../renewed-controlled-disk/sources/` is navigation only. In particular §4.4 is not complete at printed p. 73.

**Input interface.** Let C be a pinched regular neighborhood of a connected convergent infinite disk-like tower; the first story has grope height at least three. Retained tower construction supplies finite height raising, insertion of a two-story tower in a sufficiently high top story, and identification of a pinched regular neighborhood with the endpoint compactification of a regular neighborhood of its body. These are the §3.5/3.7/3.8 inputs assigned to the controlled-disk owner. They are hypotheses here, not re-proved or rechecked here. Convergence means that the diameters of components in story n tend to zero. The attaching region is A=S¹×D², with its given product normal germ. All finite replacements below occur away from a fixed smaller product collar of A.

**Finite-base convention.** Section 0 below supplies explicit full-boundary-collar coordinates for the otherwise implicit empty-word block and cap. This is essential: treating the collar as only the V-side radial collar leaves no room for the root cap disjoint from B. The source's displayed `T_empty=S²×S¹` is read as the ambient parent V=D²×S¹, consistent with the surrounding definition; the nonempty-word tori remain genuine interior solid tori. No proof rests on the inconsistent printed S².

**Output interface.** A smaller convergent tower neighborhood C'⊂C, a closed partial parameterization domain P⊂D²×D², an embedding q:P→C', and onto quotient maps α:D⁴→Q and β:C'→Q, where Q is compact metrizable. Both inverse-image families are null; the closure of the set of their nontrivial inverse-image values has empty interior. Each nontrivial α-fiber is either a tame four-ball, or a flat embedded disk joined to a regular neighborhood S¹×D³ of its boundary. On the reserved attaching collar, q uses the original product coordinates, α is injective, and βq=α. This is precisely the common-quotient input to the separate §4.5/4.6 approximation proof. No claim that the latter disk-with-boundary-neighborhood fiber is already a four-ball is made.

## 0. The finite initial block, with coordinates

Use a rounded D⁴ and a full boundary collar, not just the collar of one solid-torus face. Explicitly identify D⁴ with the unit round ball in C², put B={||z||<r₀} for 0<r₀<1, and write its complement as

F:S³×[0,1]→D⁴\B,  F(u,t)=(r₀+(1−r₀)t)u.

On S³ choose its standard genus-one splitting V∪W with V≅D²×S¹ and W≅S¹×D². Thus the longitude λ of V is the meridian of W. The attaching face is W (the rounded version of S¹×D²); their shared boundary is a torus. Product corner coordinates can be rounded in a small neighborhood of that torus, without changing any compact set used below. Let T_empty=V and J_empty=[1/3,2/3]. The root block is R=F(V×J_empty), a copy of S¹×D³ after rounding its product corners. Choose a meridian disk Δ={a}×D² in W, with ∂Δ=λ⊂∂V, and put

Δ₀=F(Δ×{4/9}),  E_empty=R∪Δ₀.

The intersection R∩Δ₀ is precisely the attaching circle ∂Δ₀. In coordinates on ∂V×[0,1], that circle is the core longitude λ at time 4/9 and at a fixed meridian coordinate. It therefore has the product framing and is a section S¹×{y} in the boundary of R=S¹×D³. Hence E_empty is exactly a flat disk union a regular neighborhood of its boundary circle, the exceptional-fiber type in §4.4. It is not asserted to be a ball. The closed inner ball B̄ meets neither R nor Δ₀, since its collar time is 0 and both sets have time at least 1/3. All nonempty-word tori and their spanning disks lie in int V; their graph pieces therefore miss Δ₀. The root height function is simply the constant 4/9 on Δ.

For 0<η<1/3, the attaching product germ G=F(W×[1−η,1]) is disjoint from E_empty and B̄. The later nonempty-word pieces lie over interior tori in V, so also avoid G. Their first-level tori are compactly contained in int V and all descendants stay there; consequently one may also retain a small neighborhood of the corner of G. This gives a single protected product germ, not merely pointwise fixing of the attaching boundary.

The first-story dual handles have attaching tori compactly contained in int V. Choose the W-side collar sufficiently thin that it misses these finitely many handles; their interiors are away from the attaching face, and their attaching regions avoid the shared torus ∂V. Extend the V collar to this W collar. The collar can initially have variable radial width: choose a continuous function a:S³→(0,1), close enough to 1 on W to avoid the finitely many handles there, and use F_a(u,t)=(a(u)+(1−a(u))t)u. The complementary region {ρu:0≤ρ<a(u)} is a ball. To obtain the displayed uniform F coordinates, conjugate by the explicit radial homeomorphism which is linear from [0,r₀] to [0,a(u)] and from [r₀,1] to [a(u),1] on each ray u. It fixes S³ and transports all the handles, collars and maps simultaneously. Thus disjointness from the W shell is retained; no unsupported equality of two independently chosen collar systems is used. Consequently W×I is contained in the initial first-story body L, the complement of the interiors of the first-story dual handles. This placement, rather than boundary agreement alone, is the support fact needed below.

Here is the relative construction used every time a new singular parameterization is inserted. Keep the bottom-story body L unchanged, with its existing map. Contract only the upper stories: they attach through the cap neighborhoods, modeled by those first-story dual handles, so these contractions take place inside the handles. Height raising replaces caps inside the same handles. Every following convergent-tower replacement and every collar adjustment in §1 takes place in those handles or in their descendants. On each handle use the new singular parameterization with its prescribed boundary collar; on L retain the old map. The definitions agree on the whole common collar, not just the attaching circle, so they glue continuously. Their images meet only along that collar: the new pieces are constructed inside the old images of their respective handles, whereas the old map on the complement is injective and its only nonsingleton fibers lie in nested subhandles. Thus the glued map agrees with the old map on L, and in particular on W×I. This is a first-story-body relative construction; it asserts nothing about arbitrary interior protected sets that meet the handles.

At the empty-word insertion this gives p_{1,empty}=p₀ throughout W×I. It also proves the required separation: portions of the new lower-third region outside the first dual handles retain p₀ and hence are disjoint from p₀(W×I); portions inside a handle have image inside its old image, again disjoint from p₀(W×I). The root cap Δ₀ and a neighborhood of it lie in the retained W collar, so its q-coordinate is unchanged while the lower-third tower is inserted. The same first-body argument applies to later insertions within their separate parent handles.

Retain W×I as an initial regular piece of q. All new V-side definitions agree with it on their overlap because the insertion just described fixes L containing that overlap. The domain P below is therefore the whole boundary collar minus the root middle-third block and all nonempty-word deleted blocks; ∂B is included. Its V part has the lower/upper-third union description; its W part is retained throughout. This supplies the finite base together with the support and image-separation checks, rather than merely the geometry of the root cap.

## 1. Dual models and singular parameterization

Write D⁴=D²×D². A finite disk-like capped tower has a dual description as the complement of finitely many disjoint embedded two-handles H_{i,m}≅D²×D². Their boundary solid tori ∂₀H_{i,m}=D²×S¹ lie on D²×S¹. Replacing its caps by another story puts the new dual handles inside the old ones. Thus the body of an infinite model is D⁴ minus K, where K=⋂_i⋃_m H_{i,m}. Endpoint compactification identifies each component of K to one point. The resulting quotient p:D⁴→C' fixes A and is a homeomorphism on D²×S¹ onto the outer frontier of C'. The issue is the shape of the components of K.

Here is the finite geometric operation used to control that shape. A surface obtained by one zero-surgery on a disk has two dual disks: trace the two sides of the surgery band through the normal disk product. In the boundary solid torus their attaching circles form its Bing double. Further grope stages iterate that replacement inside the preceding dual handle. For a core circle whose angular projection consists of an outgoing and a returning monotone interval of length r, with the remaining joining arc projecting into an interval of length ε, a Bing replacement can place the two clasps at the two endpoints of the outgoing interval. Slide one clasp to distance ε from the other end, and use the parallel outgoing and returning strands between them. Each component then has an outgoing and returning interval of length at most r−ε; its remaining strand turns in an ε interval. These slides occur in a tubular neighborhood of the core, with opposite normal coordinates at a crossing, so the components remain disjoint and the replacement remains its Bing double. The initial Bing replacement has r less than half the angular circumference. After k replacements, kε greater than that half-circumference leaves every projected component in an ε interval. Compress the normal disks radially; its whole boundary solid torus then lies in an arbitrarily small ball centered on {0}×S¹. Finitely many components allow one common height. The additional finger moves making the tower caps, rather than grope caps, take place inside those handles, so their dual tori inherit the same size bound. This proves the finite small-boundary-dual operation used below. It is the operation drawn and described on printed pp. 65–66; the argument uses the angular monotone intervals, not a conclusion inferred merely from the drawing.

Let d:D²×S¹×I→D⁴ be the V-side product collar, ending at D²×S¹ at time 1. It may be obtained from the source’s radial collar of inner normal radius 1/2 by a change of its finite collar coordinates; use its extension F over W from §0. The radius 1/2 in the following estimates is a normal coordinate inside each dual handle and is unaffected by this global collar choice. Inductively choose the dual handles and collars c_i so that:

1. On ∂₀H_{i,m}, c_i is the standard radial collar in H_{i,m}.
2. c_{i+1}=c_i except in ⋃_m∂₀H_{i,m}×[0,2^{-i}], and c_{i+1}(∂₀H_{i,m}×I)⊂H_{i,m}.
3. Each ∂₀H_{i,m}, and each H_{i,m}(D²×(1/2+2^{-i})D²), has diameter less than 2^{-i}.

To carry out the step, first retain n old stories. In each of its finitely many last dual handles choose δ so that a model set of diameter below δ maps into a set of diameter below 2^{-n-1}. Raise the replacement grope height until the finite operation gives boundary tori below δ. In C replace the top story by the supplied two-story tower, taking its first grope height this large and its second at least four; choose its components sufficiently small for convergence in C. Its first story supplies the next model dual handles. Match their collars to the old collars for times [2^{-n},1]. Compress their inner normal disks until the larger half-radius disks also have diameter below δ. Interpolate the collar only below time 2^{-n}. The interpolation lies in the old dual handle, giving (1)–(3). None of these changes meets the reserved collar of A.

Every nested sequence of boundary tori has one intersection point y, by (3). Conditions (2)–(3) give a continuous limit collar c, including its inner endpoint over y. Distinct sequences have disjoint nested inner half-handles, so their endpoint values differ and are disjoint from the other collar image. Thus c is an embedding. Condition (1) identifies ⋂_iH_{i,m(i)} exactly with c({y}×I). Consequently p has only singleton fibers and these collar arcs. The collar need not be collared on its inner side: this is the singular parameterization, not yet a product parameterization of C'.

## 2. Partial parameterization

Use finite words s in {0,2}; ds deletes the last symbol, |s| is the word length, and .s is the corresponding ternary endpoint. Put J_s=[.s1,.s2], the middle third of [.s,.s(0:2)]. Put J_s^-=[.s,.s1] and J_s^+=[.s2,.s(0:2)]. For each s choose a finite family T_{s,m} of pairwise disjoint solid tori in D²×S¹. Set T_∅=D²×S¹. For s≠∅ require:

* T_{s,m} is a regular neighborhood of the boundary of a framed immersed disk D_{s,m} in the interior of a component of T_{ds}; the disks have only disjoint double arcs and no triple points;
* diam T_{s,m}<2^{-|s|};
* the image under the eventual q of T_{ds,m}×J_s has diameter below 2^{-|ds|}.

The disk description has a finite model explanation. For the dual handles of a one-finger disk, trace the two parallel boundary sheets of that disk into the boundary collar. They form two immersed spanning disks; the only intersections are the arcs across the finger band. Different finger bands have disjoint product neighborhoods, so their intersection arcs are disjoint and there are no triple points. Replacing a cap by a capped surface inserts copies of this construction inside disjoint previous solid tori; parallel sheets in its separate band neighborhoods give the same description at every finite grope height. Thus the finite families just used have the required framed disks. This is the first exercise following §4.2, used in §4.3; it is a property of these dual families, not a claim about arbitrary links in a solid torus.

Include the empty-word root block of §0 among the deletions; its associated cap is Δ₀, while all D_{s,m} below refer to nonempty words. Define

P=D⁴\(B∪⋃_{s,m}int(T_{s,m}×J_s)),

where B is the open inner four-ball in the full-collar coordinates of §0. This is the domain of the §4.3 proposition with its otherwise implicit W-side collar retained explicitly. Its proof's abbreviated union description omits B: throughout this note P excludes B as the proposition requires. The V-collar part of P is the closure of the pieces

(T_s\T_{s2})×J_s^+ and (T_s\T_{s0})×J_s^-.

The W-collar part is retained as in §0. Here and below a difference of families means the union in the parent component in question.

Choose levels n(i) with n(i+1)≥n(i)+2 such that the boundary tori of level n(i), and their p-images, have diameter below 2^{-i}. First use only words with no zero. Put T_{2^i} equal to these level n(i) boundary tori. The restriction of p to the resulting upper-third region is injective: each collapsed collar arc meets that region only at its top endpoint.

There is an important endpoint in the source formula: set H'_s=H_{n(i),m}\(T_s×(.s1,1]), for s=2^i. This removes the OUTER collar beyond .s1, not the initial interval [.s,.s1]. The remaining handle is a copy of D⁴ with an exterior collar T_s×J_s on its new outer face. Initialize n(0)=0, H₀=D⁴, and T_empty=V; then H'_empty is the ambient disk product with the outer V collar V×(1/3,1] shaved off. Rounding the corner gives a disk product: in the full shell this is an inward displacement of the V part of its boundary, joined continuously to the unchanged W part by the collar corner. It contains the V lower third, the inner ball, and the retained first-story-body portion of W. Choose n(1)≥2, as well as sufficiently large for the size estimates. Thus H'_empty\H'_2 is the initial uncapped tower with at least two stories. Contract its upper stories to cap the first, retaining its first-story body L, and apply the relative construction in §0. This gives p_{1,empty} on H'_empty, equal to p₀ on L, with singularities confined to the first dual handles. Choose deep dual levels of this new map and place their tori in the lower-third intervals: they are T_{0 2^j}. The corresponding upper-third pieces of p_{1,empty} define q on all the first-zero pieces in the root lower third. Their sizes satisfy the dyadic bounds, their old-boundary values match q₀, and their images avoid the retained W image by §0.

For the other s=2^i the difference H'_{ds}\H'_s is likewise a regular neighborhood of an uncapped, unconnected tower of height n(i)−n(i−1)≥2, on which p₀ is a homeomorphism. Perform the same upper-story contraction and relative insertion in each component, retaining its first-story body. These maps p_{1,s} extend p₀ on the common outer faces; their deep dual tori give all remaining words with one zero. This explicitly includes the empty-word case instead of hiding its lower-third domain in the induction.

Repeat inside these inner tower segments. At stage m there is a map q_m on the region indexed by words with exactly m zeros and their predecessors. Its new pieces lie inside mutually disjoint inner tower components, away from the previous image, and the singular map restricted to the corresponding upper-third pieces is injective. Thus the union q_∞ is injective and respects all earlier definitions. The dyadic image bounds imply that q_∞ extends continuously to the closure P: a new point is represented by nested tori and nested ternary intervals, and the images have diameters tending to zero.

For completeness the extension is injective at its new points as well. A word with only finitely many zeros is already in some finite-stage domain. A genuinely new point therefore has an infinite word containing infinitely many zeros. At its first zero it lies in an inner tower component disjoint from the previous image. Repeating at its next zeros proves that its image is outside every finite-stage image. If two such words first differ at their j-th zero insertion, their images lie in the disjoint components supplied at that insertion. If they share that prefix but their tori differ later, apply the same argument at the next zero after that difference. Distinct nested addresses therefore have distinct images. Compactness of P and Hausdorffness of C' show that q:P→q(P) is a homeomorphism. On the attaching collar no deletion or insertion has occurred, so q retains the specified product coordinates there.

## 3. Separate the disk graphs

A candidate exceptional set is T_{s,m}×J_s joined to D_{s,m}×{.s11}. At this level the spanning disks may intersect or self-intersect. We replace their constant levels by continuous heights e_{s,m}:D²→(.s0,.s22), applied to the parameter disks, so their graphs in the four-dimensional collar are disjoint embedded disks. Require e=.s11 along the sheet meeting its own boundary torus, and a constant nonendpoint Cantor value on intersections with every other boundary torus.

Induct on |s|. Over a given parent torus all preceding graphs have constant nonendpoint Cantor heights. Only finitely many previous graphs enter it, because at each previous length there are finitely many disks. There are consequently numbers .s01<a<.s1 and .s2<b<.s22 enclosing no such old height. Make the new graphs take values in (a,b). Their interiors can then meet no old graph.

At a double arc of the new immersed disks there are exactly two preimage arcs, with disjoint small sheet neighborhoods; triple points are absent. Give one sheet height .s11+ηχ and the other .s11−ηχ, where χ is positive on the double-arc segment and tapers off in the small neighborhood, and η is smaller than the distance to a,b. These choices are independent for different double arcs. At endpoints near boundary tori use the prescribed constants and taper only after the sheets have become disjoint. Boundary tori are disjoint, so these finitely many constant prescriptions have disjoint neighborhoods on the disks and extend continuously. Away from the double-arc neighborhoods the sheets were disjoint already. Thus the lifted disk graphs are embedded, mutually disjoint, and meet their own boundary-neighborhood blocks in precisely the required attaching annulus; the prescribed nonendpoint levels avoid every other block. Their finite local product coordinates show that they are flat. This completes the induction.

Put E_{s,m}=(T_{s,m}×J_s)∪graph(e_{s,m}). Each is a flat disk union a regular neighborhood of its boundary circle. These compact sets are pairwise disjoint and disjoint from the closed interior ball B̄ and from the reserved attaching collar. They form a null family: each lies over its parent torus, whose diameter is bounded dyadically, and in a ternary interval of length tending to zero; there are finitely many exceptions at every bounded word length.

## 4. Quotient topology and the second quotient map

Let α:D⁴→Q identify B̄, the root set E_empty of §0, and each nonempty-word E_{s,m} separately to a point, with every other fiber a singleton. Its decomposition is upper semicontinuous. Here is the needed verification. For an open neighborhood U of one compact fiber, remove every other fiber that meets U without being contained in U. Each removed fiber meets D⁴\U. A sequence of distinct such fibers has diameters tending to zero, hence any limit point of their union lies outside U. Their union is therefore relatively closed in U; the remaining set V is open, contains the chosen fiber, is contained in U, and is saturated. Two distinct compact fibers first have disjoint ordinary neighborhoods and then disjoint saturated neighborhoods. Thus Q is Hausdorff and compact.

Metrizability requires an extra sentence beyond “compact Hausdorff.” Let V range over finite unions from a countable basis of D⁴. The sets W(V)=Q\α(D⁴\V) are open, because α is a closed map. For q∈O open, its compact fiber is covered by a finite such union V⊂α^{-1}(O). Then q∈W(V)⊂O. Thus these W(V) give a countable base. The compact Hausdorff second-countable metrization theorem applies.

On q(P) define β=αq^{-1}. Each component of C'\q(P) has frontier corresponding either to ∂B or to ∂(T_{s,m}×J_s), whose image under α is one point. Extend β over that component by the same constant. This defines a continuous map: across its frontier the old map has that constant value, and an infinite succession of distinct components has diameters of the image-controlling parent pieces tending to zero, so there is no additional discontinuity at an accumulation point. More formally, an inverse image of a closed set consists of its closed inverse image in q(P) together with whole complementary components whose frontiers lie there; a limit point outside these components lies in q(P), and the dyadic bound forces its β-value into the same closed set. Surjectivity follows because each fiber of α meets P, except B̄, and ∂B⊂P gives its value too. We have βq=α on P. The boundary is saturated and regular: let ∂Q=α(∂D⁴). Every exceptional α-fiber lies in int D⁴, so α^{-1}(∂Q)=∂D⁴ and these fibers are singletons. The map q takes ∂D⁴ homeomorphically onto ∂C': on the attaching face this is the prescribed identity, and on V it is the boundary homeomorphism of the singular parameterization. Each complementary component and each graph piece used to make a nontrivial β-fiber has compact closure in the interior of C'; its frontier corresponds to an interior deleted block or ∂B. Therefore β^{-1}(∂Q)=∂C', also with singleton fibers. Distinct such fibers may accumulate at the boundary, but no individual fiber includes a boundary point. These are the saturation statements needed by the subsequent relative approximation proof.

A nontrivial β-fiber consists of the closure of the corresponding complementary component, together with the q-image of the part of its disk graph in P. It is contained in the controlling image q(T_{ds}×J_s), apart from finitely many initial exceptional sets. Its diameter is therefore dyadically small. This proves nullity for β; α-nullity was proved above. The common exceptional values are the countably many values associated with B and the E_{s,m}.

Finally their closure has empty interior, not merely the exceptional set itself. Every open quotient set meeting an exceptional value has preimage containing its whole E_{s,m}. In the parent product block, remove the finitely many same-level disk projections and tori and the finitely many earlier graphs. A small open ball remains arbitrarily near the given exceptional set: the child tori are disjoint proper interior sub-tori, the disk projections have dimension two in the three-dimensional parent, and earlier graphs have empty interior. Choose it away from their closed union. Descendant exceptional sets are confined to the removed child blocks, so this ball misses every exceptional fiber. A smaller ball has closure missing them as well. Its image is a nonempty open quotient subset avoiding the closure of the exceptional values. If an open quotient set meets only an accumulation value, it also meets an exceptional value and the preceding argument applies. The isolated ball fiber causes no change: an open neighborhood of it meets its exterior collar, where the same argument applies. Thus every nonempty open subset of Q contains an open subset avoiding all exceptional values and their closure.

With the finite base of §0, the construction proves the stated common-quotient interface relative to the reserved attaching collar. It does not prove relative behavior on an arbitrary interior disk or arbitrary bottom-stage dual product: such a set can intersect the deleted blocks. The subsequent approximation owner must use the attaching collar as the protected germ, or supply a separate avoidance construction for any larger protected set.

## 5. A possible geometric-dual interface using only the proved relative region

This paragraph is a conditional local reduction, not a claim that arbitrary marked interior charts are preserved by §§4.2–4.4. Suppose a dual sphere S meets the bottom-stage surface Σ transversely once at x, is otherwise disjoint from the tower body, and there is an embedded arc γ⊂Σ from x to y in the interior of the reserved attaching collar, disjoint from every higher-stage attachment. Then S can be ambiently isotoped, away from the actual attaching boundary, so that its unique intersection occurs at y. Choose a closed disk neighborhood U of γ in the interior of Σ, avoiding all attachments. In coordinates U×D² for its normal neighborhood, choose a compactly supported time-dependent tangent vector field on U whose flow takes x to y; take its product with a bump function of the normal radius. Its ambient flow preserves U×{0}, fixes the actual attaching boundary, and transports the transverse sphere intersection with its normal product chart. Choose the ambient neighborhood small enough that S has no other part in it: the compact arc away from x is disjoint from S. Consequently the moved S is still embedded and meets the tower only once, now inside the protected collar. The isotopy can be extended by the identity in the ambient four-manifold and does not change the framed attaching data. A later disk extraction fixed on that collar retains the geometric dual. For a standard surface stage with its symplectic attaching curves, cutting along their union gives a connected polygonal surface, so a point outside the curves can be joined to the outer boundary without crossing the attachments. The controlled-disk owner must verify this actual arc hypothesis for the chosen finite input and confirm that isotoping the dual is allowed by its contract.

## 6. Choice ledger for this construction

The countably many stages above depend on earlier stages. Merely calling those choices “countable” would not justify them from Countable Choice. The mechanism here is functional recursion on natural numbers after fixing countable model data; it does not invoke Dependent Choice.

The input tower is supplied as its actual countable family of finite, parameterized tower pieces, with attaching maps, product handle charts and normal framings. This is the form constructed by the controlled-disk owner. We use the standard finite PL models in those charts, transported to the ambient topological manifold by their specified characteristic maps; no triangulation of the ambient topological four-manifold is asserted. If the charts were not included as data, one may first use Countable Choice once to choose a finite chart/model package independently for each piece of the already given tower. The index set is the fixed countable set of input pieces, not a succession of towers chosen in response to earlier choices. Their given attaching maps remain part of the data; independent chart selections do not assert that ambient transition maps are PL. All further constructions take place in the standard models and are transported by the fixed charts and the explicitly composed previously constructed maps.

Fix an enumeration of finite words, integers, positive dyadic numbers, rational points in each finite model simplex, and finite rational PL templates for the local moves used here. Include standard templates for the zero-surgery dual disks, Bing clasps and their strand slides, parallel-sheet finger bands, the two local sheet-height perturbations, product collar interpolation, radial compression, and rounding a product corner. Prescribed boundary values and prescribed unchanged first-story bodies are imposed structurally in these templates: a template is the identity on those faces, and an interpolation agrees with the existing map there. Those equalities are not obtained by a rational approximation to an arbitrary boundary map. A template in a new piece is composed with its fixed chart and the existing maps on that piece. Finite compositions and finite subdivisions have finite integer codes, so this is a countable list even after earlier maps have been composed into it.

For each finite operation in the proof, the free choices can be made within this list. The separation of finitely many disjoint compact sets leaves a positive clearance; uniform continuity on a compact model gives a positive diameter tolerance; collar and normal radii can therefore be chosen dyadic below those tolerances. The height-raising parameter and the number of Bing replacements are integers. The strand slides, compression and interpolation just described are given by the fixed finite templates with those rational parameters. The immersed-disk double arcs come from the finite finger-band templates, so their disjoint sheet neighborhoods can be chosen from rational subdivisions in those same model coordinates. Piecewise linear bump functions on these subdivisions give the graph separations; their small positive amplitudes may again be dyadic. Exact prescribed values are retained as symbolic previous constants, not rounded. For Cantor constants use ternary words followed by the periodic tail 020202…; these form a fixed countable dense set of nonendpoint Cantor points. At a given stage there are only finitely many earlier forbidden constants in the relevant parent torus, so a suitable gap and the required constants can be taken from the fixed enumeration. The base collar uses the explicit radial formulas of §0; its variable width can be chosen from rational PL functions on a fixed finite subdivision, sufficiently close to the boundary over W to miss the finitely many first dual handles. Thus no arbitrary choice of a continuous function, embedded disk or new chart is hidden in that collar step.

At stage n, enumerate the finite tuples of such template codes. A tuple is admissible if it satisfies the exact support, collar-matching, containment, no-intersection and strict diameter inequalities already required in §§0–3 for the previously fixed finite-stage map. The geometric arguments prove that an admissible tuple exists: first perform the finite template operation, then make its rational or dyadic free parameters sufficiently small; strict separation and diameter inequalities persist. Choose the least admissible code. This is a mathematical least-element definition; decidability of the admissibility predicate is not being claimed or needed. The new finite-stage map is consequently a uniquely specified function of the preceding finite-stage map and n. Ordinary recursion on N constructs the whole sequence in ZF. It is not a dependent-choice relation with an unspecified successor.

The same convention applies to subsidiary sequences: n(i) is the least integer above its prescribed previous bound satisfying the two diameter tests; a common grope height is the least integer exceeding the finitely many required heights; each auxiliary dyadic radius is the first one in the fixed enumeration satisfying its finite constraints. Finite families at each stage and the word/address enumeration fix the order in which the operations are performed. There is no additional choice of an infinite branch in the nested-handle arguments: those arguments quantify over all addresses, and define a value at an address by the unique intersection or limit. The continuous extensions of c and q, the exceptional sets, the quotient α and the extension β are uniquely determined by the constructed sequence. To give Q a metric, its countable base in §4 can be used in the usual metrization construction with least finite-basis codes for all normal shrinkings; its countable recursive choices likewise have specified least successors.

Accordingly this branch needs no general Dependent Choice and no arbitrary-index Choice.
