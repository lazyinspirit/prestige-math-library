# Actual DIFF5 proper line and closed product tube

This NEW operation supplies only the proper-line/tube portion of the two-ended argument. It does not supply isolated-point flatness, Euclidean recognition or an unmarked S4 cross-section. The construction is independent of those missing operations.

## Data and conclusion

Let M be a connected, second-countable DIFF5 manifold without boundary. Supply two distinct ends with nested connected open neighborhoods U_n^+,U_n^-, n≥1, and a compact exhaustion K_n, such that U_n^+ and U_n^- are disjoint, U_{n+1}^±⊂U_n^±, and U_n^±∩K_n is empty. Assume these neighborhoods are cofinal at their indicated ends. Fix a countable smooth chart atlas and countable dense coordinate lists. Such an atlas can be chosen using Countable Choice from a second-countable smooth manifold; no arbitrary-index choice is used below.

There is a smooth proper embedding ell:R→M joining these ends and a proper smooth embedding Phi:R×closed(D4)→M with Phi(t,0)=ell(t). Its restriction to the open product is a smooth chart neighborhood of the line, and its full image is closed. Thus the line has an actual closed product tube with a specified meridian disk Phi({0}×closed(D4)). If a specified closed set is disjoint from the constructed line, the tube can be chosen disjoint from that set as well.

The actual annular CE preimages in B0 satisfy the end-neighborhood hypothesis through the hereditary-HE/end-control input: their two target annular ends have connected simply connected cofinal preimages. No conclusion about a topological or smooth compactification of a meridian disk is included here.

## Proper embedded line

1. Choose x_1^± in U_1^± and join them by a compact smooth embedded arc. In a connected smooth manifold an open path component is also closed, so connectedness gives a finite coordinate path. Approximate it by a piecewise smooth path in finitely many charts, perturb its finitely many free vertices and round its corners. In dimension five a one-dimensional arc can be made embedded relative its two endpoint germs. More explicitly, the forbidden incidences of two nonadjacent compact path segments are contained in finite-dimensional parameter subsets with at least three remaining transverse coordinates; using a free local vertex or a small supported perturbation avoids them. In a chart the bad endpoints for intersection with a prescribed compact smooth arc lie in the image of its cone parameters, of dimension at most two. On compact pieces these images have empty five-dimensional interior, as follows by covering a Lipschitz two-dimensional parameter rectangle with O(epsilon^-2) boxes of image diameter O(epsilon). Their five-dimensional volume bound tends to zero. Thus finitely many such incidences can be avoided with arbitrarily small perturbations. The same argument works relative fixed endpoint subarcs.

2. Having constructed finitely many embedded arcs, choose the next point x_{n+1}^+ in U_{n+1}^+ away from their images and join x_n^+ to it inside U_n^+. Perturb relative the old endpoint germ so the new arc meets the previous finite arc collection only at its prescribed common endpoint. Its interior and endpoint can be kept outside K_n because U_n^+ is open and disjoint from K_n. Perform the identical construction in U_n^- on the negative side. At each stage only a finite union of compact arcs is being avoided. The finite-parameter argument in step 1 makes its complement path connected and supplies the required embedded arc. The positive and negative new arcs can be chosen disjoint from each other in that same finite step.

3. This does not require Dependent Choice. Enumerate the coordinate points and the finite coordinate-path/perturbation codes once. For each finite history, the admissible next points and arcs exist by steps 1–2 and contain a nonempty open set of the finite perturbation parameters. A fixed countable dense parameter set meets that open set. Choose the least successful code. These codes include finitely specified endpoint collars and corner-rounding data; existence of a good code follows by C1 approximation of an embedded compact arc with positive separation from the previous nonincident pieces. Primitive recursion on these least natural-number codes constructs the sequence. Countable Choice is confined to obtaining the initial countable atlas and standard countable auxiliary data, not to selecting an infinite dependent sequence of otherwise unstructured choices.

4. Parametrize the middle arc and successive end arcs on adjacent integer intervals of R. The choices can prescribe matching nonzero tangent germs at their common endpoints. Alternatively round the corners in pairwise disjoint small chart neighborhoods of the joints. Those neighborhoods can be chosen locally finite and outside the corresponding K_{n-1}; the same least-code procedure chooses them. The resulting union is a smooth injective immersion. It is proper: every compact subset of M lies in some K_N, and all end arcs with index n≥N miss K_N. Therefore that compact set meets only finitely many parameter intervals. The preimage of the compact set is closed and bounded in their finite union and is compact. A proper injective immersion is an embedding: its map is closed, and local immersion coordinates give the inverse topology. Call it ell.

## Proper closed product tube

5. Choose a smooth Riemannian metric using the countable atlas and a locally finite partition of unity. Along ell, the orthogonal normal bundle has rank four. Choose an orthonormal normal frame at t=0 and transport it along R using a metric normal connection. The transport equation on each finite t interval is a linear smooth ODE preserving orthonormality; its solutions agree on overlaps and extend through all finite intervals. This gives a smooth trivialization of the entire normal bundle. No statement about a normal bundle over any compactified line or disk is being made.

6. In this trivialization let E(t,v)=exp_{ell(t)}(v). The inverse function theorem at every (t,0) shows that E is a diffeomorphism on some neighborhood of that point in the normal bundle. On each compact parameter block [n-2,n+3], choose a uniform positive radius small enough for E to be defined and injective near that compact zero section. This follows from finitely many inverse-function neighborhoods plus compactness away from the diagonal of pairs of distinct parameters: a hypothetical sequence of collisions with radii tending to zero would limit to either two different points of the embedded zero section, which is impossible, or one diagonal point, where the inverse-function chart excludes collisions.

7. Also choose a uniform positive separation of ell([n,n+1]) from ell(R minus (n-1,n+2)). The latter is closed by properness of ell, and it is disjoint from the first compact set, so its distance from that compact set is positive. Require the normal displacement over [n,n+1] to be less than one tenth of that separation. Two points from nonneighboring parameter blocks cannot then have the same image: each displacement is less than one tenth of their two zero-section blocks' separation. Neighboring parameter blocks are handled by step 6. These are countably many positive block requirements, with only finitely many relevant bounds near each fixed parameter.

8. Choose a smooth positive function r(t) below all the local radius/separation bounds just obtained. Impose in addition that the image of the radius-r(t) normal disk misses K_N whenever the zero-section parameter is sufficiently far out in either U_{N+1} end. This is possible block by block because each relevant compact segment of ell is disjoint from K_N by a positive distance. A smooth minorant can be constructed from a locally finite partition of unity on R, taking the coefficient on each support below the minimum of the finitely many required positive bounds there. If a specified closed set A is disjoint from ell, add the positive local bound dist(ell(t),A)/4. Use also the uniform continuity of the exponential map on each compact block to convert radius bounds to these displacement bounds.

9. Define Phi(t,z)=E(t,r(t)z) for z in the closed unit four-disk, making r smaller by a factor two so its closed disks lie strictly inside every open inverse-function radius. Fiberwise multiplication by the positive r(t) is a smooth diffeomorphism of the open normal bundle; steps 6–7 therefore show that Phi is a smooth injective immersion on a neighborhood of R×closed(D4). Step 8 makes it proper: for each K_N, all sufficiently far parameter disks miss K_N, and the remaining bounded parameter slab times closed(D4) is compact. Thus Phi is a proper embedding and its image is closed in M. Its restriction to R×int(D4) is an open diffeomorphic neighborhood of ell. The disk at t=0 is a specified smooth meridian disk; its center is the point ell(0), and its boundary is a smooth embedded S3 in the tube boundary.

This completes the new line/tube operation. No flatness conclusion at the point added when M minus ell(R) is compactified follows from it. In particular, the Kirby isolated-point flatness bridge remains owned by the separate two-end-recognition author.
