## Elementary geometric operations, including size bookkeeping

1. **Contraction and push-off.** On one symplectic pair of caps contract the handle using two parallel copies of each cap and the central square. An intersection with a cap then occurs twice with opposite signs. An arc in that cap from the intersection to its attaching circle gives the ribbon Whitney disk joining the two copies; choose these arcs disjoint except for their prescribed endpoints. Pushing another surface across these disks removes its intersections with the contracted surface. The only new intersections occur where arcs belonging to dual caps cross the central square. The associated loop is gh^{-1}, where g,h are the two old cap-intersection labels. Parallel copies do not alter the labels. All moves lie in an arbitrarily small regular neighborhood of the old caps and central squares. Contraction itself increases no projected diameter; a push-off across a disk of diameter a can increase the recipient diameter from a to at most 3a.

2. **Push-down and tubing.** An intersection with stage j is moved along an arc in that stage through its attaching curve to stage j−1. It becomes two opposite fingers. Iterating k times gives 2^k fingers at the bottom stage. Paths may be chosen disjoint because cutting a surface along a symplectic attaching system leaves a connected complement. If a framed transverse sphere meets the bottom stage once and avoids its remaining body, tube each finger into a separate parallel of that sphere. This removes those intersections. Tubes, finger moves and their framings are performed in product neighborhoods of the specified paths. An even pair introduced by a push-down carries its visible ribbon Whitney disk. Thus new algebraic cancellations are witnessed geometrically, not deduced from disappearance of integer intersection numbers.

3. **Transverse gropes.** Divide the second-stage surfaces into + and − symplectic sides. Two parallels of the entire − side joined by its boundary annulus give a sphere-like capped grope transverse to the + side, and conversely. It has one intersection with the corresponding second-stage surface and otherwise only cap intersections. Its support lies in an arbitrarily small neighborhood of that side. To move this transverse grope off the grope caps of a tower, replace its Whitney arcs by two parallel copies of the corresponding Whitney disk/grope and the intervening ribbon. Repeat story by story. Its eventual caps meet only tower caps. Stopping before the tower caps gives a transverse grope carried by a neighborhood of the tower body, hence π1-null wherever that body is π1-null.

4. **Height raising with exact growth bounds.** Form the transverse grope for the + side from the − side, contract its top stage, and push the original caps off it. At each + cap intersection push the other sheet down to the second stage and tube to a parallel of the now disjoint transverse grope. This replaces + caps by higher capped gropes without changing the existing body. Alternate sides. If a_n is the height just raised, a_n=a_{n−1}+a_{n−2}−2 with initial heights3, so after n steps the height is at least 2^{n/2} (a smaller harmless constant works for all initial indices). Every new double-point loop uses at most five old generators: two from the initial push-off, one inside a transverse parallel, and two on its return. Thus after n steps word length is at most 5^n. The new projected diameter is at most 7 times the old one: 3a for the first push-off plus two further displacements bounded by 2a. Consequently n steps give the bounds 5^n and 7^n. Arbitrarily many steps inside one already separated regular neighborhood never leave that neighborhood.

These constructions use ordinary framed finger/Whitney/tubing models. A TOP input requires those models to have been supplied on the particular operating neighborhood; no arbitrary ambient smoothing is inferred here.

## Controlled separation proved geometrically

Given a small locally finite cover of X, choose compact inner sets K_i still covering the relevant region. For a compact base with k sets choose a>0 so that the enlargement of each K_i by 3^{k+3}a stays inside the designated small open control set. Assign each grope component to the first K_i which its image meets. Separate the k collections by raising height once on both sides, contracting the first collection and pushing all later collections off it, and continuing in this order. Each push-off costs at most a factor3; the two height-raising steps cost7²<3⁴. Hence the displayed enlargement bound suffices after an additional harmless shrink of a. Each collection is now separated from all others.

Take pairwise disjoint regular neighborhoods of the separated collections, with images still in their prescribed control sets. Inside each neighborhood repeat the same ordering procedure with individual components instead of the initial finite collections. There is no uniform bound on the number of these inner operations, but every one remains in its fixed small neighborhood. Thus no number-of-components factor is needed in the final estimate.

For locally compact X first use a compatible locally proper metric on the relevant components and choose a locally finite relatively compact cover. Only finitely many cover members meet the compact star of K_i. Choose the initial control minorant on that star using that finite number in place of k. Processing the collections in a locally finite order cannot create an intersection outside the designated star, since the preceding enlargement bound prevents a surface from leaving it. The construction is therefore locally finite and has the same conclusion. In the proper-q application projected compact stars have compact preimages; finite collar data there require only finitely many operations. Output: new caps for unchanged bodies, distinct grope components disjoint, each entire component lying over one prescribed small neighborhood.

## Cyclic image killing inside a separated neighborhood

Let U be one separated regular neighborhood of a height≥3 disk-like capped grope. Suppose the image of π1(U) in π1(E_O), for some slightly larger control neighborhood O⊂X, is trivial or infinite cyclic. Use this image group as Γ and α:π1(U)→Γ.

If Γ=0 nothing is required. If Γ≅Z, let B bound the absolute α-labels of the finitely many double-point generators. Raise height by2k elementary steps. The new labels have absolute value at most N=5^{2k}B, and there are at least 2^k stages. Contract one top stage and push off intersections with labels in (N/2,N]; contract a second stage and push off those in [−N,−N/2). Old remaining labels have absolute value at most N/2. Each new label is a difference of two labels from the same half interval, also of absolute value at most N/2. Repeat using the new bound. After i rounds all labels have absolute value ≤2^{-i}N. Choose i=floor((2^k−4)/2), which leaves the original lower body untouched. Since 2^{-floor((2^k−4)/2)}5^{2k}B→0, a finite k makes this <1. Integral labels must then all be zero.

All these operations stay inside U. Height can be initially raised by a fixed extra amount to retain bottom height4. The final cap loops generate the fundamental group of the disk-like capped-grope image: the abstract capped grope is simply connected, and each plumbing adds its double-point generator. Thus the entire image fundamental group maps trivially into E_O. The transverse spheres are retained by choosing all upper-stage neighborhoods disjoint from them except at their designated bottom-stage transverse points.

By local cyclicity, a sufficiently small projected control neighborhood O' about any x has image π1(E_{O'})→π1(E_O) cyclic or trivial. Separation is performed first, so each U is contained over such an O'. This is why unbounded height raising cannot exhaust the control budget.

## Accessory disks and controlled one-story towers

A loop in the final grope image is now nullhomotopic in E_O. Its nullhomotopy is a map of a disk with boundary already lifted to M. The (δ,1)-connected input extends this particular lift with projection δ-close to that filling. If O is small and δ is subordinate to its slightly larger allowed neighborhood, the lifted filling remains over that neighborhood and misses the fixed product. Approximate to a generic immersed disk relative to its boundary and framing in the supplied local smooth models. Boundary twists correct the accessory framing; the new intersections are among the eventual tower caps and are allowed.

First make the grope caps nice: raise height by one, contract top components in order, and push all later components off each contraction. Distinct resulting grope caps are disjoint; their self-intersections have the explicit ribbon Whitney disks from contraction. Choose accessory circles for those standard Whitney pairs and fill them as just explained. Push all intersections of Whitney/accessory tower caps with the grope down to the bottom stage, then remove them by tubing to the original transverse spheres. This yields a properly immersed one-story capped tower.

For constant initial nullity scale a, the accessory fillings can have diameter≤3a (their chosen base paths can go across one grope component before and after the filling), and the whole first tower has diameter≤7a. In the function case choose successive neighborhood stars instead of using this numerical shorthand.

Different tower components can still meet among tower caps. Separate them by the following two-sided procedure, which is the actual content of FQ3.6. Build second-stage transverse gropes from the opposite side, with caps meeting only tower caps. Push intersections of + tower caps down to that second stage and tube to these transverse gropes. The + tower caps become properly immersed second-story gropes. Contract the − second-story gropes, pushing the + side off. Apply the already proved controlled separation procedure to the + side second-story gropes. Their components now have disjoint small neighborhoods.

Reverse the signs. Build the − transverse gropes from parallels of the separated + side. Contract + second-story gropes, pushing these new transverse gropes off. The + side is back to tower caps, pairwise disjoint and disjoint from these transverse gropes. Push − tower-cap intersections down and tube to their transverse gropes; this makes − second-story gropes without touching the separated + caps. Apply separation within their available neighborhoods, and contract. The − tower caps are now mutually disjoint and miss the + tower caps. Consequently distinct complete one-story towers are disjoint.

There are only finitely many outer size transitions in this two-sided procedure. Work backward from any prescribed final control cover, applying the separation minorants and then the7a and height-raising estimates. This supplies a positive continuous initial nullity scale depending only on that cover. Every unbounded repetition occurred inside an already fixed regular neighborhood. The resulting one-story towers have bottom grope height≥4 and pairwise disjoint actual regular neighborhoods N_i with q(N_i) of diameter<ε and N_i contained in the allowed region. These are the supports needed for the subsequent extraction.

## Raising tower height without leaving its actual neighborhood

Fix one of the disjoint N_i. All subsequent constructions occur inside N_i; q-control no longer enters their possibly arbitrarily long recursions.

Contract these transverse gropes and push the old tower caps off the contractions. This gives transverse spheres for the portion above the first stage, disjoint from that portion except at their designated points. Remove a neighborhood of the first stage; the remaining tower is now a union-of-disks tower with these transverse spheres.

Construct transverse gropes E_+ to its second-stage + surfaces whose caps are parallel only to the − top-story grope caps. They lie near the existing tower body and are π1-null: every body double-point loop is capped by one of the existing higher tower disks. Contract one top stage of E_+ and push − caps off it; both remain in this null body neighborhood. Raise height in E_+ as much as required. Repeated contraction of a near parallel, followed by pushing off the remaining caps, supplies arbitrarily many pairwise disjoint near parallels of these π1-null gropes.

At each + tower-cap intersection push one sheet down to that second stage and use a separate such near parallel for each finger. These tower caps become new-story capped gropes. Loops in their images have trivial π1-image: a loop created in one new cap belongs to the corresponding single π1-null near parallel; its two push-down paths are homotopic along the finger cascade. On the unchanged − side nullity persists because it stayed near the old body. ## Graph squeezing by an actual isotopy

Raise to three stories using the preceding standard-model construction, with the required height in the second story. Discard the third-story tower caps and contract the third-story gropes. The resulting two-story tower has disjoint upper-story components. The discarded tower caps supply nullhomotopies, in the complement of the first-story body, for their double-point generators.

A regular neighborhood H of one upper-story component collapses to a finite graph K: collapse each surface to its next attaching curves joined by a tree, and the top caps to their double-point loops and connecting arcs. Its intersections with the lower-story cap sheets form circles L. As a pair, H with these circles is a relative regular neighborhood of the mapping cylinder L×I→K. Write C_f for the portions of those sheets outside H. The carried lower caps, including C_f and the mapping-cylinder sheets, are BODY; they are not permitted any new intersections.

Choose small disjoint balls inside the original N(T), off the fixed first-story body. Nullhomotope K into these balls in the complement of that fixed body. General position makes its auxiliary trace K×I a two-complex with isolated self-intersections and isolated intersections with the actual carried sheets. This trace is auxiliary; it is not an extra tower body sheet.

Resolve an offending point using an arc in its chosen trace sheet to the free K×{1} edge. The arc avoids K×{0}, the fixed first-story body, every other trace/carried-sheet intersection, and previously chosen arcs. There are finitely many points, so choose narrow disjoint strip neighborhoods, with their terminal pieces still inside the chosen small ball. Each strip has a product normal two-disk neighborhood. A carried-sheet intersection is pushed along one side of this strip, around its free end, and back along the other side. Since the free end is not an actual sheet of the original tower, this motion passes around a free auxiliary edge rather than through another actual sheet. Its support misses every other carried sheet. It is an ambient isotopy of that carried sheet in the product strip neighborhood, fixed at its attaching boundary; hence it creates no actual body/body or body/tower-cap intersections. For an auxiliary trace self-intersection make the same free-edge modification on the auxiliary sheet only. It does not change an actual tower sheet. Choose later strips off the already cleared actual sheets. Thus the finite sequence yields an embedded auxiliary trace disjoint from the interiors of the carried sheets while retaining the tower's precise original plumbing pattern and all its normal products.

Sliding a normal neighborhood of K along this embedded trace is now an ambient isotopy into the small balls. Taper it along L×I and fix C_f and the first-story body. It carries the entire upper-story regular neighborhood with its gluing collars and framings. This is the source's free-end isotopy, not a permission to introduce new intersections among body caps. Every support is inside the fixed original N(T).

## Coherent model neighborhoods and finite-point/end separation

Here is the avoidance condition that prevents an end from becoming a finite point. An infinite branch of target containers has one limit point. It misses each image P_j because its tail is contained in a later closed container disjoint from that image. Different branches first separate into disjoint closed containers at a finite level and therefore have different limits.
