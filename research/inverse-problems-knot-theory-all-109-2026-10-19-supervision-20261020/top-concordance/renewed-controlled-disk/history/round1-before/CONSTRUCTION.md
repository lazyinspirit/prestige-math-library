## Relative and infinite-family input which the h-cobordism application supplies

Use an open allowed region Ω in X. The fixed product is contained in q^{-1}(X\Ω), and all input data lie over control neighborhoods whose closures are in Ω. Thus a small lift cannot enter the fixed product. Merely requiring the original disks to miss a product region in M would not imply this.

For the noncompact application q is proper on the region in which operations occur. All input disks, chosen Whitney disks, dual spheres, their attaching arcs and collars form a locally finite family. Control neighborhoods are subordinate to a locally finite relatively compact cover of Ω. These conditions ensure that every locally finite family of constructions with projected supports in these neighborhoods is locally finite in M. For a finite disk system these extra properness conditions are unnecessary.

The general FQ5.4 statement is broader than these sufficient infinite-family hypotheses. No claim is made here that local finiteness of A alone establishes local finiteness of arbitrary accessory fillings when q is nonproper. The present sufficient interface is the one needed by the owner’s punctured-end application.

The input normal information is an actual framed product germ on a collar of each boundary circle. Every operation below is supported off a smaller such collar. The final handle parameterization must be relative to the entire attaching solid torus; that is the specific fact which transfers the full boundary normal germ.

## Elementary geometric operations, including size bookkeeping

1. **Contraction and push-off.** On one symplectic pair of caps contract the handle using two parallel copies of each cap and the central square. An intersection with a cap then occurs twice with opposite signs. An arc in that cap from the intersection to its attaching circle gives the ribbon Whitney disk joining the two copies; choose these arcs disjoint except for their prescribed endpoints. Pushing another surface across these disks removes its intersections with the contracted surface. The only new intersections occur where arcs belonging to dual caps cross the central square. The associated loop is gh^{-1}, where g,h are the two old cap-intersection labels. Parallel copies do not alter the labels. All moves lie in an arbitrarily small regular neighborhood of the old caps and central squares. Contraction itself increases no projected diameter; a push-off across a disk of diameter a can increase the recipient diameter from a to at most 3a.

2. **Push-down and tubing.** An intersection with stage j is moved along an arc in that stage through its attaching curve to stage j−1. It becomes two opposite fingers. Iterating k times gives 2^k fingers at the bottom stage. Paths may be chosen disjoint because cutting a surface along a symplectic attaching system leaves a connected complement. If a framed transverse sphere meets the bottom stage once and avoids its remaining body, tube each finger into a separate parallel of that sphere. This removes those intersections. Tubes, finger moves and their framings are performed in product neighborhoods of the specified paths. An even pair introduced by a push-down carries its visible ribbon Whitney disk. Thus new algebraic cancellations are witnessed geometrically, not deduced from disappearance of integer intersection numbers.

3. **Transverse gropes.** Divide the second-stage surfaces into + and − symplectic sides. Two parallels of the entire − side joined by its boundary annulus give a sphere-like capped grope transverse to the + side, and conversely. It has one intersection with the corresponding second-stage surface and otherwise only cap intersections. Its support lies in an arbitrarily small neighborhood of that side. To move this transverse grope off the grope caps of a tower, replace its Whitney arcs by two parallel copies of the corresponding Whitney disk/grope and the intervening ribbon. Repeat story by story. Its eventual caps meet only tower caps. Stopping before the tower caps gives a transverse grope carried by a neighborhood of the tower body, hence π1-null wherever that body is π1-null.

4. **Height raising with exact growth bounds.** Form the transverse grope for the + side from the − side, contract its top stage, and push the original caps off it. At each + cap intersection push the other sheet down to the second stage and tube to a parallel of the now disjoint transverse grope. This replaces + caps by higher capped gropes without changing the existing body. Alternate sides. If a_n is the height just raised, a_n=a_{n−1}+a_{n−2}−2 with initial heights3, so after n steps the height is at least 2^{n/2} (a smaller harmless constant works for all initial indices). Every new double-point loop uses at most five old generators: two from the initial push-off, one inside a transverse parallel, and two on its return. Thus after n steps word length is at most 5^n. The new projected diameter is at most 7 times the old one: 3a for the first push-off plus two further displacements bounded by 2a. Consequently n steps give the bounds 5^n and 7^n. Arbitrarily many steps inside one already separated regular neighborhood never leave that neighborhood.

These constructions use ordinary framed finger/Whitney/tubing models. A TOP input requires those models to have been supplied on the particular operating neighborhood; no arbitrary ambient smoothing is inferred here.

## Controlled separation proved geometrically

Given a small locally finite cover of X, choose compact inner sets K_i still covering the relevant region. For a compact base with k sets choose a>0 so that the enlargement of each K_i by 3^{k+3}a stays inside the designated small open control set. Assign each grope component to the first K_i which its image meets. Separate the k collections by raising height once on both sides, contracting the first collection and pushing all later collections off it, and continuing in this order. Each push-off costs at most a factor3; the two height-raising steps cost7²<3⁴. Hence the displayed enlargement bound suffices after an additional harmless shrink of a. Each collection is now separated from all others.

Take pairwise disjoint regular neighborhoods of the separated collections, with images still in their prescribed control sets. Inside each neighborhood repeat the same ordering procedure with individual components instead of the initial finite collections. There is no uniform bound on the number of these inner operations, but every one remains in its fixed small neighborhood. Thus no number-of-components factor is needed in the final estimate.

For locally compact X first use a compatible locally proper metric on the relevant components and choose a locally finite relatively compact cover. Only finitely many cover members meet the compact star of K_i. Choose the initial control minorant on that star using that finite number in place of k. Processing the collections in a locally finite order cannot create an intersection outside the designated star, since the preceding enlargement bound prevents a surface from leaving it. The construction is therefore locally finite and has the same conclusion. In the proper-q application projected compact stars have compact preimages; finite collar data there require only finitely many operations. Output: new caps for unchanged bodies, distinct grope components disjoint, each entire component lying over one prescribed small neighborhood.

## Cyclic image killing inside a separated neighborhood

Let U be one separated regular neighborhood of a height≥3 disk-like capped grope. Suppose the image of π1(U) in π1(E_O), for some slightly larger control neighborhood O⊂X, is trivial or infinite cyclic. Use this image group as Γ and α:π1(U)→Γ.

If Γ=0 nothing is required. If Γ≅Z, let B bound the absolute α-labels of the finitely many double-point generators. Raise height by2k elementary steps. The new labels have absolute value at most N=5^{2k}B, and there are at least 2^k stages. Contract one top stage and push off intersections with labels in (N/2,N]; contract a second stage and push off those in [−N,−N/2). Old remaining labels have absolute value at most N/2. Each new label is a difference of two labels from the same half interval, also of absolute value at most N/2. Repeat using the new bound. After i rounds all labels have absolute value ≤2^{-i}N. Choose i=floor((2^k−4)/2), which leaves the original lower body untouched. Since 2^{-floor((2^k−4)/2)}5^{2k}B→0, a finite k makes this <1. Integral labels must then all be zero.

All these operations stay inside U. Height can be initially raised by a fixed extra amount to retain bottom height4. The final cap loops generate the fundamental group of the disk-like capped-grope image: the abstract capped grope is simply connected, and each plumbing adds its double-point generator. Thus the entire image fundamental group maps trivially into E_O. The transverse spheres are retained by choosing all upper-stage neighborhoods disjoint from them except at their designated bottom-stage transverse points.

By local cyclicity, a sufficiently small projected control neighborhood O' about any x has image π1(E_{O'})→π1(E_O) cyclic or trivial. Separation is performed first, so each U is contained over such an O'. This is why unbounded height raising cannot exhaust the control budget.

## Accessory disks and controlled one-story towers

A loop in the final grope image is now nullhomotopic in E_O. Its nullhomotopy is a map of a disk with boundary already lifted to M. The (δ,1)-connected input extends this particular lift with projection δ-close to that filling. If O is small and δ is subordinate to its slightly larger allowed neighborhood, the lifted filling remains over that neighborhood and misses the fixed product. Approximate to a generic immersed disk relative to its boundary and framing in the supplied local smooth models. Boundary twists correct the accessory framing; the new intersections are among the eventual tower caps and are allowed.

First make the grope caps nice: raise height by one, contract top components in order, and push all later components off each contraction. Distinct resulting grope caps are disjoint; their self-intersections have the explicit ribbon Whitney disks from contraction. Choose accessory circles for those standard Whitney pairs and fill them as just explained. Push all intersections of Whitney/accessory tower caps with the grope down to the bottom stage, then remove them by tubing to the original transverse spheres. This yields a properly immersed one-story capped tower.

For constant initial nullity scale a, the accessory fillings can have diameter≤3a (their chosen base paths can go across one grope component before and after the filling), and the whole first tower has diameter≤7a. In the function case choose successive neighborhood stars instead of using this numerical shorthand.

Different tower components can still meet among tower caps. Separate them by the following two-sided procedure, which is the actual content of FQ3.6. Build second-stage transverse gropes from the opposite side, with caps meeting only tower caps. Push intersections of + tower caps down to that second stage and tube to these transverse gropes. The + tower caps become properly immersed second-story gropes. Contract the − second-story gropes, pushing the + side off. Apply the already proved controlled separation procedure to the + side second-story gropes. Their components now have disjoint small neighborhoods.

Reverse the signs. Build the − transverse gropes from parallels of the separated + side. Contract + second-story gropes, pushing these new transverse gropes off. The + side is back to tower caps, pairwise disjoint and disjoint from these transverse gropes. Push − tower-cap intersections down and tube to their transverse gropes; this makes − second-story gropes without touching the separated + caps. Apply separation within their available neighborhoods, and contract. The − tower caps are now mutually disjoint and miss the + tower caps. Consequently distinct complete one-story towers are disjoint.

There are only finitely many outer size transitions in this two-sided procedure. Work backward from any prescribed final control cover, applying the separation minorants and then the7a and height-raising estimates. This supplies a positive continuous initial nullity scale depending only on that cover. Every unbounded repetition occurred inside an already fixed regular neighborhood. The resulting one-story towers have bottom grope height≥4 and pairwise disjoint actual regular neighborhoods N_i with q(N_i) of diameter<ε and N_i contained in the allowed region. These are the supports needed for the subsequent extraction.

## Raising tower height without leaving its actual neighborhood

Fix one of the disjoint N_i. All subsequent constructions occur inside N_i; q-control no longer enters their possibly arbitrarily long recursions.

For an n-story tower, construct second-stage transverse gropes with caps meeting only tower caps, as described above. Contract these transverse gropes and push the old tower caps off the contractions. This gives transverse spheres for the portion above the first stage, disjoint from that portion except at their designated points. Remove a neighborhood of the first stage; the remaining tower is now a union-of-disks tower with these transverse spheres.

Construct transverse gropes E_+ to its second-stage + surfaces whose caps are parallel only to the − top-story grope caps. They lie near the existing tower body and are π1-null: every body double-point loop is capped by one of the existing higher tower disks. Contract one top stage of E_+ and push − caps off it; both remain in this null body neighborhood. Raise height in E_+ as much as required. Repeated contraction of a near parallel, followed by pushing off the remaining caps, supplies arbitrarily many pairwise disjoint near parallels of these π1-null gropes.

At each + tower-cap intersection push one sheet down to that second stage and use a separate such near parallel for each finger. These tower caps become new-story capped gropes. Loops in their images have trivial π1-image: a loop created in one new cap belongs to the corresponding single π1-null near parallel; its two push-down paths are homotopic along the finger cascade. On the unchanged − side nullity persists because it stayed near the old body. Raise height once more and contract top components in order, pushing later ones off, to make distinct new grope caps disjoint. This operation takes place near the null image and preserves nullity.

Their contraction intersections have the canonical Whitney disks. Fill the accessory circles by the known nullhomotopies in the tower body neighborhood, push all intersections with the grope to its bottom stage, and tube to the transverse spheres constructed in the first paragraph. This gives new tower caps disjoint from the body. Thus one story has been added on the + side. Exchange + and − and repeat to obtain a whole additional story, with arbitrary height in its grope. This is the two-sided replication mechanism of FQ3.5, now located explicitly in N_i.

## Graph squeezing and the actual nested limit

Raise to three stories by the preceding procedure, with the desired height in the second story. Discard the third-story tower caps and contract the third-story gropes completely. Their contractions are the tower caps of the remaining two-story tower. Distinct upper-story components are disjoint. The discarded top tower caps provide nullhomotopies, in the complement of the first story, for the double-point generators of each upper-story image. Hence that image is π1-null in that complement.

A regular neighborhood H of an upper-story component collapses to a finite graph K: each surface collapses to the next-stage attaching curves joined by a tree; iterate to top caps, then collapse each top cap to its double-point loops and connecting arcs. The intersections of ∂H with the first-story cap sheets form circles L. With these sheets retained, the pair is a relative regular neighborhood of the mapping cylinder L×I→K. Write C for the portions of those sheets outside H. Thus the relevant lower caps are C∪(L×I)∪K, with L×{1} attached to K.

Choose disjoint small balls in N_i off the first-story body, one per upper-story component. Since K is nullhomotopic in the complement of that body, choose a nullhomotopy taking each K into its assigned ball. General position makes its trace K×I an immersed two-complex with finitely many point intersections with itself, C, and the mapping cylinders. Resolve these point intersections toward the free K×{1} end: choose disjoint arcs in the trace from the offending points to that end and push the intersecting sheets along the arcs. In the graph’s thickened normal product these are finger displacements of the carried sheets; the first-story body and C outside these narrow fingers remain fixed. Intersections introduced among the carried caps are permitted tower-cap intersections. The resulting trace of K is embedded and misses the first-story body, C, and the interiors of the mapping cylinders. Its terminal graph still lies in the chosen balls.

Sliding along the I-coordinate of this embedded trace gives an isotopy of a thin regular neighborhood of K into those balls. Use its normal product structure to carry the entire upper-story neighborhood, tapering along the mapping cylinders and fixing C and the first-story body. This gives a two-story tower whose upper-story components, including tower caps, lie in disjoint arbitrarily small balls. The operation is relative to the bottom attaching collar and therefore to the protected dual point described below. This reproduces FQ3.7; the trace resolution, not a bare claim that a nullhomotopic two-complex is isotopic to a point, is essential.

Now use squeezing recursively. At the first step choose upper-story balls of diameter<1/2 and with closures in N_i. Remove a small open neighborhood of the fixed first story and continue separately inside the disjoint upper-story neighborhoods. At the jth step the new story and its regular neighborhood are modified only inside the preceding designated balls; choose new balls with closures inside them and diameters<2^{-j}. The bottom attaching collar of each stage is unchanged. Every finite initial collection of stories is eventually fixed. Along an infinite branch the nested balls have a unique limiting point. Distinct branches either separate at a finite stage into disjoint closed balls, or define the same branch. This proves continuity and injectivity of the endpoint extension of the embedded union of finite neighborhoods. Compactness of its abstract endpoint compactification then makes the extension an embedding. It is the embedded pinched regular neighborhood C_i of a convergent tower. In particular C_i⊂N_i, including every limit point. The bottom-stage attaching solid torus has its original parameterization.

Countable Choice is used to select the successive finite height choices, generic perturbations, nullhomotopies and shrinking neighborhoods along this recursion. With a locally finite family of N_i the recursions are independent in pairwise disjoint sets. Their union remains locally finite in M under the proper-q/local-control hypotheses already stated.

## Exact output retained by the consumer

The actual h-cobordism consumer needs framed embedded Whitney disks in the complement of its A∪B, and a small DIFF/PL regular neighborhood of the finite tower containing those disks. Thus one cannot shrink a neighborhood of the whole tower off the original sphere except at the bottom transverse point without further work. The boundary-relative chapter-4 parameterization is enough for the consumer’s embedded Whitney disks. ## Actual Whitney-disk input and the small linking-torus construction

The new helper needed by the h-cobordism consumer has the following precise input. The surfaces whose extra intersections are to be removed have framed geometric transverse spheres. Their undesired intersections are paired by small immersed framed Whitney disks W_j. All disks, Whitney disks, spheres, collars and chosen incidence paths are locally finite and lie in the q-separated allowed control neighborhoods already specified. No zero intersection form on the original geometric dual spheres is assumed.

Work in M_0, the complement of a small open normal neighborhood of the original surfaces; its new boundary has the inherited smooth product structure. The interior of each W_j is made disjoint from those surfaces by tubing at each intersection into a parallel of their given geometric duals. The tubes follow the appropriate surface sheet, miss all other surfaces, and preserve the specified Whitney boundary frame. If a boundary twist is needed first to make the Whitney framing extend, its extra surface intersections are removed in exactly the same way. The W_j are now proper framed immersed disks in M_0, with the intended Whitney attaching circles on its boundary.

At one of the original paired intersection points use coordinates R²×R² in which the two sheets are the coordinate planes and the Whitney disk approaches in the positive quadrant. The linking torus S¹×S¹ meets the Whitney disk in one point. Its two standard caps are D²×{p} and {p}×D², where p∈S¹ has nonzero second coordinate. Their single intersections with the two original sheets are removed by tubing to the corresponding original geometric transverse spheres. The resulting sphere-like capped torus lies in M_0 and has its torus body meeting the designated W_j once. It misses the other W attaching arcs. Its cap interiors may meet the W disks, each other, and other such caps.

Contract this capped torus to a framed immersed sphere E_j. The contraction uses two oppositely oriented parallel copies of each cap and the central square. Its algebraic pairing with W_j is1 and with W_k, k≠j, is0: the original torus contributes the single distinguished point, while every cap/W intersection occurs in the two parallel copies with opposite signs and the same group label. For two E spheres, every cap/cap intersection produces the four signs (+,−,−,+), with identical labels after identifying the parallel attaching paths; their sum is0. For a self-intersection the same pairing applies in the Wall quotient identifying the two sheet orders. The parallel self-pairing terms have the identical cancellation because each cap has total coefficient1−1=0. The central-square contribution has its standard product framing and no additional twist. Therefore λ(E_j,E_k)=0 and μ(E_j)=0, with actual small immersed Whitney pairings supplied by the strips between these parallel copies. This argument does not use λ or μ of any original transverse sphere: its contributions also occur in the same opposite parallel pairs.

All objects just constructed lie in small regular neighborhoods of W_j, its two source sheets, their given transverse spheres, and the specified incidence paths. A cap/sphere tube or Whitney strip can enlarge that neighborhood by at most one incident input piece at each of this fixed number of passes. Consequently a continuous initial control minorant makes each whole linking-torus/Whitney-disk package as small as prescribed, independently of the number of its intersections.

## Turning those data into a small height4 grope

Here is the finite construction, including the simultaneous intersection bookkeeping from FQ1.10. Whenever a collection D has algebraic duals E with chosen small Whitney pairings for all extra D/E intersections, first push every E sheet off the interiors of those Whitney disks along arcs exiting on their E-side boundary arcs. The exit arcs avoid all disk/disk intersection points and all other Whitney boundary arcs. The finger displacements may create E/E intersections, but no new D/E intersections: the exits lie on E sheets, not on D. They preserve all Wall forms by regular homotopy. Now perform the immersed Whitney moves on D. The resulting D sheets are assembled from old D and two parallel copies of the Whitney disks, whose interiors miss E. Hence only the distinguished D/E points remain. This is a geometric conversion, rather than an appeal to the vanishing of algebraic sums.

For the W_j/E_j data above, first correct all W self and mutual intersection numbers by tubing one sheet at each unwanted intersection into the appropriate algebraic dual E_j along the other sheet. Since E_j has the unit pairing with its designated W_j and zero self/mutual forms, that tubing cancels the selected contribution without adding an algebraic error elsewhere. For a tube along a path with group label g, bilinearity gives λ(D_i#(±gE_j),D_k)=λ(D_i,D_k)±gδ_{jk}; the quadratic identity μ(x+y)=μ(x)+μ(y)+λ(x,y) gives the corresponding self correction. Since λ(E_j,E_k)=μ(E_j)=0, these corrections retain the dual pairings and introduce no secondary algebraic error. Each selected old intersection is paired with the new intersection at the designated unit point by the strip following the tube and the two local sheet arcs. The extra intersections of that tube’s sphere parallel with D are paired using the already specified D/E Whitney disks, attached along parallel strips to the tube. Thus these are actual immersed small Whitney disks. The other cap intersections occur in the opposite parallel pairs described above. Convert E_j to geometric transverse spheres by the preceding simultaneous procedure, changing W_j by immersed regular homotopies. All existing small Whitney disks are transported across these homotopies; newly created intersections carry the move’s parallel-strip Whitney disks. The new W_j thus have small Whitney pairings for all their self and mutual intersections.

At each of those Whitney pairs, self-add the W sheet along the other boundary arc of its Whitney disk. This trades the two transverse intersections for a handle on an embedded body surface. Its two caps are the old Whitney disk and the small meridian disk through the tube. Remove cap/body intersections by tubing caps into the geometric E spheres. The result is a properly immersed one-stage capped grope with the same framed boundary.

To repeat on its caps, first make the E spheres disjoint from those caps. Push an E/cap intersection down to the bottom stage and remove the resulting opposite pair by tubing to two parallel E spheres. This changes E to E+E−E along the two homotopic push-down paths, preserves its Wall forms, and introduces only paired intersections with explicit finger/ribbon Whitney disks. The new E spheres are still algebraically transverse to the bottom stage and algebraically disjoint from the caps. Use the simultaneous Whitney procedure to make them geometrically disjoint from the caps. Next cancel algebraic cap self/mutual intersections by pushing down and tubing into those spheres, exactly as for W in the preceding paragraph. Perform the resulting Whitney self-additions on the caps and move their new caps off the existing body by the same sphere tubing. This raises grope height by one.

Repeat a total of three further times. At the end the W boundaries bound properly immersed height4 capped gropes, with framed transverse spheres meeting the bottom stage once and otherwise disjoint from them. Every operation is one of the finger, Whitney, self-addition, contraction, or tubing models already described. There are four rounds, each with a fixed finite list of passes. If one pass traverses at most c incident packages, use a minorant subordinate to the c-fold star of the preceding control cover. A finite composition of these star refinements gives a continuous δ depending only on the requested final scale, not on the number of intersections or components. This is the required size estimate for the initial “exercise” in FQ2.10, with the geometry supplied rather than the exercise cited as proof.

Apply controlled separation, cyclic image killing, controlled tower construction, and the nested tower procedure above to these W gropes in M_0. The geometry and source maps restrict to M_0 only after cap/body intersections have been removed. Accessory lifts are originally made in M and then made disjoint from the original surfaces by the same transverse-sphere tubing, with its explicitly reserved control margin. This avoids asserting that M_0→E inherits (δ,1)-connectivity.

## Original surface cleanup for the actual consumer

After the framed embedded W_j have been extracted in M_0, perform the embedded Whitney moves on the original A sheets across them. Their interiors miss A∪B, their Whitney boundary arcs are the prescribed ones, and distinct W_j and their chosen small product neighborhoods are disjoint. Therefore the moves remove exactly the undesired intersections and retain the distinguished A/B unit points. The final disks and the moves need no retained dual spheres; those spheres were a construction resource.

All changed points lie in the original small Whitney-disk packages and the actual tower neighborhoods inside their prescribed control neighborhoods. The fixed q-separated product is untouched. The original surface boundary maps and full product normal germs have been fixed throughout.
