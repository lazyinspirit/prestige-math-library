## Input and exact conclusion

Let M=S³×[0,1]. Suppose H:D³×[0,1]→M is a proper embedding with an open radial extension around its side: more precisely it is the restriction of an embedding of D³_(1+η)×[0,1], for some η>0, with the evident relative openness at the ambient boundary. Suppose on actual end collars H(x,t)=(h_i(x),t), where each h_i is the supplied smooth ball chart. Set T=H(D³×I), B_i=h_i(D³), and E_i=closure(S³ minus B_i). Let e_i:E_i→D³ be chosen endpoint complementary-ball charts satisfying

    e_i(h_i(x))=x  for x∈S².

These are exactly the consistency conditions for a marked complement. They are necessary, not additional geometric claims: endpoint chart and side chart must agree on their common boundary. The smooth endpoint-ball normalization module supplies E_i≅D³ and allows this choice. Arbitrarily preassigned e_i with incompatible boundary values cannot be preserved simultaneously with the fixed side parametrization.

Then C=closure(M minus T) has a homeomorphism F:C→D³×I such that

    F(H(x,t))=(x,t)  for x∈S²,
    F(y,t)=(e_i(y),t)  on smaller actual end collars.

Thus it identifies **the whole complement**, both marked end balls, their product collars, and the entire parametrized side. Combining F with the inverse tube chart gives an ambient homeomorphism from M to the union of two standard D³×I's along S²×I, hence to S³×I. The tube is carried to one standard ball tube. If H is a pair tube for an annular strip, the pair condition is retained on that tube. No assertion about the remainder of the annulus is needed.

## Brown's ball theorem, with the proof supplied

Only the ordinary foundational facts used below are implicit: invariance of domain, the Jordan separation theorem for a topologically embedded sphere, compact-to-Hausdorff quotient maps, and radial homeomorphisms of a ball. All arguments apply in dimension n=4; no four-dimensional smooth Schoenflies assertion occurs.

Call a compact set K cellular if K=∩Q_j, where Q_j are embedded closed n-balls and Q_(j+1) lies in int Q_j. A nontrivial fiber is a fiber containing more than one point.

**1. Collapsing a cellular set inside a ball.** If K⊂int Q is cellular, a continuous surjection q:Q→Q fixed on ∂Q exists whose only nontrivial fiber is K (when K is a singleton, use a homeomorphism instead). Choose the defining balls inside int Q. Construct homeomorphisms u_j:Q→Q, fixed on ∂Q, so that diam u_j(Q_j)<2^(-j), and u_(j+1)=u_j off int Q_j. To make the next choice, compress the compact subset u_j(Q_(j+1)) of the interior of the ball u_j(Q_j) into a ball of the desired small diameter by a radial homeomorphism of that ball fixed on its boundary, using any ball coordinate on it. The supports of all later changes lie in u_j(Q_j); consequently sup|u_k-u_j|≤diam u_j(Q_j) for every k≥j. This proves uniform convergence to q. Surjectivity follows by compactness, since every u_j is onto. Every point outside K is eventually fixed by all subsequent changes; two points not both in K have distinct images by injectivity of the appropriate u_j and the fact that later images of K remain inside u_j(Q_j). The diameters show q(K) is one point. This proves the assertion without a summability mistake in the convergence estimate.

**2. Finite-fiber ball image.** Suppose f:Q→S^n has finitely many nontrivial fibers, all contained in int Q. Then f(Q) is the closure of one complementary domain of f(∂Q). Indeed f|∂Q is an embedding and f(int Q) avoids f(∂Q), hence lies in one domain D by connectedness. Let P be the finite set of exceptional image values in D. At any value of f(Q)\P lying in D, f is locally a homeomorphism by invariance of domain: away from the finitely many compact exceptional fibers its restriction is an injective map on an open neighborhood. Such a value exists, since otherwise the connected image f(int Q) would be a point, contradicting continuity and the injective boundary map. Thus f(Q)∩(D\P) is nonempty and both open and closed in D\P. For n≥2 that punctured domain is connected, so it is all of D\P. Closedness gives D⊂f(Q), and the reverse containment in closure(D) was already proved. This establishes the finite-fiber image claim.

**3. A single exceptional fiber is cellular.** Suppose f:Q→S^n has only one nontrivial fiber K⊂int Q. Put a=f(K). By step 2, f(Q)=closure(D). Given an open neighborhood U of K inside int Q, f(U) is a neighborhood of a in D: its complement in f(Q) is the compact image of Q\U, which misses a. There is a homeomorphism v:S^n→S^n carrying closure(D) into f(U) and equal to the identity on a smaller neighborhood V of a. To construct it, choose p outside closure(D), use S^n\{p}≅R^n, and radially compress a large Euclidean ball containing closure(D) into a small ball about a contained in f(U), fixing an even smaller ball about a; extend the radial function increasingly to infinity. Define w on Q by w(x)=x for x∈K and w(x)=f^(-1)(v(f(x))) for x∉K. This expression uses the unique inverse away from a. On f^(-1)(V) it is the identity, so it is continuous across K. It is injective; compactness makes it a homeomorphism onto its image. Its image is an n-ball contained in U, and K lies in its interior because w is the identity on a neighborhood of K. Repeating within successively smaller such neighborhoods constructs nested balls with intersection K. This proves cellularity.

**4. Two exceptional fibers are cellular.** Suppose f:S^n→S^n is onto and has just two nontrivial fibers A,B, with images a,b. Choose a small round closed ball disjoint from A∪B, and let Q be the complementary closed ball. Then A∪B⊂int Q. By step 2, f(Q)=closure(D), where a,b∈D. Choose an open neighborhood U of a with closure(U)⊂D\{b}. As in step 3, choose v carrying closure(D) into U and fixing a small neighborhood V of a. Define w:Q→Q by w(x)=x on A and w(x)=f^(-1)(v(f(x))) off A. Since v(f(Q)) avoids b, the inverse is unique except at a; away from A the image avoids a as well. Near A the map is the identity. The only nontrivial fiber of w is B: B maps to the unique point over v(b); all other points are distinct, including the separate points of A. Step 3 applied to w proves B cellular; interchange A and B to prove A cellular. This is the corrected reference in Brown's printed Theorem 4.

**5. Recovering a ball from a cellular collapse.** Suppose X is the closure of a complementary domain of a sphere, and f:X→R is onto a closed n-ball, with only nontrivial fiber K⊂int X cellular there. Choose a defining ball Q⊂int X containing K. Step 1 supplies q:Q→Q collapsing just K and fixed on ∂Q; extend q by the identity on X\int Q. The maps f and q have precisely the same fibers, so f factors through q as a continuous bijection X→R. Compactness makes this induced map a homeomorphism. Thus X is a ball.

**6. Bicollared sphere theorem.** Let j:S^(n-1)×[-1,1]→S^n be an embedding. Let A,B be the two closed exterior sets beyond its two boundary spheres. Collapse A and B to the north and south poles; on the collar use its actual coordinates and the usual latitude quotient S^(n-1)×[-1,1]→S^n. This defines a continuous surjection S^n→S^n having only the two nontrivial fibers A,B. Step 4 shows both cellular. The closures X_+,X_- of the complementary domains of j(S^(n-1)×{0}) contain A,B in their respective interiors; their defining cellular balls can therefore be chosen inside those interiors. The quotient map restricted to either X is onto a hemisphere, a closed n-ball, with just that one exceptional cellular fiber. Step 5 shows each X is a ball. This is the required generalized TOP Schoenflies theorem.

## Applying the theorem to the actual tube, including all markings

**7. A concrete boundary sphere and its bicollar.** Cap both ends of M with standard 4-balls to obtain the standard S⁴. The boundary of C in this capped ambient space is

    Σ = (E_0×{0}) ∪ H(S²×I) ∪ (E_1×{1}).

The e_i and the side coordinates identify Σ with ∂(D³×I), by maps that agree pointwise on each seam. Thus Σ is a topologically embedded S³, with no inference from the core alone. In the interior of the side, the radial extension of H gives a two-sided collar. In the interiors of E_i the ambient cap/product collar gives a two-sided collar. Near either seam, the given exact end product germ identifies the geometry with a corner of two coordinate half-planes crossed with R². Replacing that planar corner by a straight line through an elementary planar piecewise-linear homeomorphism makes the local boundary flat on both sides. A global bicollar can be specified directly by rounding this corner inside the exact product neighborhood and joining the resulting product collars; equivalently the standard collaring theorem for a compact locally flat hypersurface yields the same bicollar. This uses the actual radial extension and the actual end germ; a naked embedded closed product, with no side local-flatness information, would not justify this step.

For clarity, the bicollar can be constructed without invoking a global TOP collaring supplier. Transport the product smooth atlas through the open radial tube H onto a neighborhood of the side. Retain the original smooth atlas on neighborhoods of the interiors of the two end complementary balls in the capped ambient space. Arrange all overlaps of these two kinds of neighborhoods to lie inside the genuine supplied endpoint product germs. There H(x,t)=(h_i(x),t), with h_i the actual smooth ball chart, so the transition maps are smooth. No smooth compatibility is asserted outside these overlaps. This gives an auxiliary smooth atlas on a neighborhood of Σ, not on the original annulus or the whole ambient manifold. In that atlas Σ is smooth except for the standard end corners. Near the bottom seam its model is the positive u-axis joined to the positive t-axis in the (u=r-1,t)-plane, crossed with S², and likewise at the top. Round those planar corners in the product neighborhoods. A planar homeomorphism supported in the same neighborhoods carries the unrounded boundary to the rounded smooth boundary. The latter has a smooth collar: take a transverse vector field in its local smooth charts, patch with a smooth partition of unity using its consistently chosen outward side, and use its flow for a uniform short time by compactness. Pull this collar back through the corner homeomorphism. This constructs the required bicollar from the actual tube extension and endpoint overlaps alone.

Σ bounds two domains in S⁴. One domain has closure C. To justify this identification, ∂C=Σ is connected; every component of C must have nonempty boundary, since a compact boundaryless component would be open and closed in S⁴ by invariance of domain. Hence C is connected. Its interior is connected as well, by pushing paths off its boundary in the just-constructed collar. Thus it is one complementary domain of Σ. Brown's theorem in step 6 supplies an actual homeomorphism q:C→D⁴. The argument does not assume that a boundary homeomorphism already extends, and does not infer a ball from simple connectedness alone.

**8. The entire marked boundary map.** Define b:∂C→∂(D³×I) by b(y,i)=(e_i(y),i) and b(H(x,t))=(x,t). The consistency condition on e_i proves agreement on the seams. Each piece is a homeomorphism; the finite closed-piece pasting lemma and compactness prove b is a homeomorphism of the entire boundary. Choose any fixed homeomorphism r:D³×I→D⁴. Then

    k = (r|∂(D³×I)) ∘ b ∘ (q|∂C)^(-1)

is an actual self-homeomorphism of S³. Its radial Alexander extension is K(0)=0 and K(ρz)=ρk(z) for 0<ρ≤1, z∈S³. The map r^(-1)∘K∘q:C→D³×I extends **exactly** b. This is the requested extension, with its formula and hypotheses supplied.

**9. Preserving actual collar germs, not merely end faces.** Choose δ>0 inside both exact input product collars. Repeat steps 7–8 for M_δ=S³×[δ,1-δ], its restricted tube, and C_δ. Its end complementary balls remain precisely E_0,E_1. On its boundary use the same side rule (x,t)↦(x,t), the same e_i at t=δ,1-δ, and target D³×[δ,1-δ]. The result F_δ extends that entire boundary map. Define F=F_δ on C_δ and F(y,t)=(e_i(y),t) on the two exterior collars. These maps agree on the complete shared end faces and on the side seams; pasting gives the claimed homeomorphism, with continuous inverse obtained by the same pasting. It is exactly product on those smaller collars. No collar-preserving property is attributed to an arbitrary Alexander extension.

**10. Ambient assembly.** Glue F on C to H^(-1) on T, using a second copy of D³ for the tube ball. They agree on S²×I. Their union is a homeomorphism M→(D³∪_(S²)D³)×I=S³×I. It preserves all the prescribed smaller product collars through the specified endpoint pair-ball and complementary-ball charts. In particular the complement parametrizations used for connected-sum gluing are actual parametrizations of the entire complements, compatible on the whole gluing side and at both ends.

## What is and is not discharged

Steps 7–10 prove the required complement standardization **for every actual open proper pair tube matching the supplied endpoint product germs**. This removes a separate need for an ambient arc-to-thick-tube extension theorem once that tube has been constructed. The remaining annulus task is still the existence of the actual proper pair tube with its radial extension and endpoint germs. This note does not establish that existence or the unrelated marked disk/angular-fiber construction.
