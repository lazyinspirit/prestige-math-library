## The actual published inputs and outputs

**Quinn I 2.1, printed282 (PDF9), is the precise supplier for this plan:** a map e:M→X from an actual manifold of dimension at least six, with a given completion on its boundary and near D, and an onto, 0-LC, tame end with locally constant local fundamental group π near C and Wh(π×Z^k)=0 for all finite k, has a completion near C retaining the given one on the boundary and D. X is locally compact, locally 1-connected, metric. The category is DIFF/PL when M is, as in the prototype1.4. **Exact-source warning:** visually inspected printed280/PDF7 confirms that the displayed absolute statement1.4 literally omits “tame”; this is not an OCR loss. Relative2.1 and the proof2.8 printed284 explicitly require tameness. Therefore this plan uses2.1 (or the absolute route with tameness explicitly retained as an additional hypothesis); it does not silently quote1.4 as containing that word. These published interfaces do **not** assume an externally supplied globally index-sorted source filtration, raw geometric ancestor package, or whole-end deformation H_i.

Tameness is the actual small homotopy in Definition1.1, printed279. Definitions1.2–1.3, printed280, specify 0/1-LC and the locally constant covering system. These cannot be replaced by an abstract weak equivalence. Conversely, in the literal product application θ:P≈B×R, vertical pushes and local charts give these original end hypotheses directly, with all remaining parameters retained in B. Whether a protected θ-product germ is smooth is a separate literal relative datum; it is not supplied by θ merely being a homeomorphism.

**Approximate completion is an output with a real deformation:** Definition2.2, printed282, requires a manifold neighborhood N and a controlled relative deformation into its frontier (with the stated W1/W2 allowances). The approximate end theorem2.5, printed283, produces this from a sufficiently long finite tame structure2.4 and the specified existing relative completion. For W=empty its finite length is n+2; the general W version has a larger finite length. Strong H_i are therefore genuine intermediate outputs, not minimal assumptions on the relative end theorem2.1. Naming a neighborhood an approximate completion does not create its deformation.

## How the proof obtains the output, and where control is chosen

1. **Finite tame structures, before improving frontiers.** Proposition5.5, printed305–307, produces arbitrarily long finite tame structures from the end hypotheses (the displayed proof uses a finite universal 2-complex, its controlled equivariant lift, then compactness to choose a deeper cutoff). Proposition5.3 and the eventual Hurewicz theorem5.2, printed302–305, turn the homological data into controlled relative homotopies. The relative-connectivity definition5.1, printed302, is already a statement about actual homotopies of relative k-complexes, not just vanishing groups.

2. **Actual frontier paths and null disks.** Section7.1, printed314–315, first interposes manifold neighborhoods between the open tame neighborhoods, preserving the collar over the protected base. Section7.2, printed315–317, takes finite local manifolds G_j, component points and arcs, then generating loops. It obtains actual small arcs/disks from the tame-structure lifting property, embeds them, cuts and adds their neighborhoods, and proves the new frontiers are controlled (δ,1)-connected. Thus later reference channels are earned on these particular operated frontiers; no assertion is made that the original θ can be pushed freely through arbitrary later surgeries.

3. **Small index handlebodies.** Definition preceding6.1, printed308, is an index filtration N_-1⊂N0⊂…⊂Nn; the initial collar is controlled as a homotopy and each WHOLE handle has small image. The base case k=-1 of6.1, printed309, says explicitly that an arbitrary handlebody is subdivided and adjusted by small isotopies so handles attach to lower-dimensional ones; its details are omitted in the primary source. The remaining proof, printed309–312, removes low handles by actual controlled disks, small isotopies and disjoint matched-pair absorption. This is a published internal existence step, not a premise of1.4. If the repository requires a full local reconstruction, the precise omitted primitive is this finite small handlebody subdivision/index correction. A generic global Morse–Smale sorting theorem with arbitrary tracked backgrounds is a stronger implementation choice, not a separate published end-theorem hypothesis.

4. **Higher relative connectivity and the kernel.** Section7.3, printed317–319, uses6.1 and controlled Whitney operations to embed selected small disks and remove their neighborhoods, increasing relative connectivity. Section7.4, printed319–320, obtains only n−3/n−2 handles, derives a controlled kernel projection from the tame homotopies, applies stable algebra to make the kernel geometric, and removes the corresponding handles. Its upper-side argument explicitly uses the already controlled frontier and the fact that the intermediate level is obtained from it by trivial 2-handles. The eventual Hurewicz conclusion supplies the n-dimensional relative homotopies and hence the approximate completion. A separate new theorem assuming preexisting H_i at the start would invert this logic; the published route constructs them here.

5. **Tolerance order and the whole completion.** Section2.8, printed284–285, chooses summable output tolerances ε_i first. The thin h-cobordism theorem2.7 then supplies δ_i; Proposition5.4 supplies the smaller γ_i needed to recognize the intervening slabs. Successively deeper γ_i/2 approximate completions are obtained from2.5 and5.5. Each slab is then a δ_i h-cobordism, so2.7 produces an ε_i product. Summability gives Cauchy tracks and the completion. This is the published noncircular order; one should not first assume all whole-end H_i and use their products to prove their existence.

## The ordinary controlled product and the raw-support issue

Theorem2.7, printed284, is the controlled smooth/PL product theorem for source dimension at least six, with actual controlled h-cobordism deformations, local-cover lifting, and Wh hypotheses. Its proof6.3, printed312–314, uses6.2 to reduce to two adjacent indices, obtains a controlled boundary matrix and inverse from the actual homotopies, applies8.4, realizes elementary operations by small bands, chooses actual small arcs/disks using the cover lifting and local 1-connectivity of X, and simultaneously absorbs disjoint matched handle pairs. This directly bounds product tracks by three times the final whole-handle bound (printed314). It does not use an uncontrolled global gradient flow through a long ordered filtration.

Definition8.2, printed321, controls the ENTIRE forward and inverse compositions of the UNDERLYING SET functions of elementary factors. This is stronger than the reduced matrix product support. Printed313 explicitly invokes that definition when saying that the algebraic deformation never alters a handle by anything farther away than its deformation diameter. Therefore global raw support is a real part of the published algebra contract, but an additional external arbitrary “raw ancestor package” hypothesis is not a minimal end theorem input. Our package bookkeeping was introduced to justify that geometric realization in a reconstruction. It must be derived from the actual global set-composition bound, guide arcs and whole handle carriers, rather than demanded as unrelated initial data.

The finite-head reduction is printed322 (not after328). It transfers geometric MODULE locations through a finite-dimensional Euclidean head; it does not furnish a source triangulation, source normal bundle, or actual path lifts. Those remain supplied by the actual controlled manifold/cover in6.3 and by the constructed frontiers in7.2–7.4. The backward tolerance/subdivision choice in8.4 is printed329–330. Finite head dimension may increase when accuracy changes; no fixed dimensional replacement B≃N^3 is justified for the smoothing/desuspension problem.
