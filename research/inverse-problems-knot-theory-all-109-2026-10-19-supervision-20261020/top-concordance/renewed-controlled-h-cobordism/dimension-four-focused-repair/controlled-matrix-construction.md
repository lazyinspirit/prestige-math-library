# Controlled matrix construction over a control manifold of dimension at most four

This is a detailed construction extracted from the actual proof of Quinn, Ends of maps I8.4–8.7, printed321–330. It is not yet an accepted local supplier. The required algebraic input is Wh(π×Z^m)=0 for every torus dimension m≤d, with d≤4. This is a hypothesis of this matrix construction, not a new proof or review of the ring-theoretic supplier. The finite punctured-torus input in Step2 is supplied, under Countable Choice, by the retained1≤n≤3 contract and the separately root-reviewed NEW finite4-torus construction. The ring-theoretic condition is explicit throughout; its consumer availability is recorded by root. The ring-theoretic draft is owned by `/root/top_free_abelian_torsion`. The existence of the immersion is not inferred from a PL atlas. ##1. Geometric modules and permitted stabilization

A finite geometric R-module is a finite free module with a chosen basis and a location in X for each basis vector. The support of a matrix column consists of the input location together with the locations of every nonzero output coefficient. A matrix has propagation≤δ if each such set has diameter≤δ. A controlled isomorphism includes an inverse with the same bound. An elementary deformation is a sequence of basis additions and allowed basis signs/group elements whose entire forward and reverse underlying set compositions have small displacement; bounding each individual factor alone is insufficient.

For a partial isomorphism h:P→Q over a buffered region C, choose a small partial inverse g, meaning gh=1 and hg=1 after restriction to the smaller basis submodules whose neighborhoods stay in C. The handle chain complex and its controlled contraction supply this data by the parity-isomorphism calculation in `completed-controlled-algebra-steps.md`.

Permitted stabilization adds a geometric small isomorphism, not an arbitrary invertible block. Such an added block is geometrically already a cancelling handle pair. The equal-rank chain construction proved in the companion note identifies the two stabilized bases with arbitrarily small displacement; if h is only defined as an isomorphism over C, rank discrepancies may be corrected outside C. This reduces to a partial automorphism. No inverse-torsion block is introduced at this reduction stage.

### Proper basis identification before using flux

For the proper case the finite equal-rank lemma is not silently applied to an infinite module. The two-sided small inverse supplies the needed proper matching directly. Augment Z[π] to the real numbers. Make a bipartite graph of input/output basis vectors, joining a pair when the corresponding augmented matrix entry is nonzero. It is locally finite because the based modules and bounded-control stars are locally finite. Every finite set of input vertices has at least as many neighbors: otherwise the augmented matrix would map their independent columns to too small a vector space, contrary to its left inverse. The transpose argument using the right inverse gives the corresponding condition for finite output sets.

These two finite Hall conditions give a matching covering both sides in a countable locally finite graph. Here is the required countable compactness argument. Any finite prescribed set of vertices can be covered by a finite matching: first saturate the finitely prescribed input vertices by the usual finite alternating-path argument and their Hall inequalities. Then augment from any uncovered prescribed output vertex, allowing an alternating path to terminate either at an unmatched input vertex or by releasing a matched output vertex that is not prescribed. If neither exit exists, all reachable input vertices are prescribed and all reachable output vertices are prescribed; the finite reachable alternating component has one more output than input and contains every neighbor of its outputs, contradicting their Hall inequality. Finiteness follows because only the finite current matching can occur before an exit. Thus previously prescribed vertices stay covered. Enumerate the vertices. At level n choose the finite edge decisions incident with the first n vertices, requiring those vertices to be matched; their neighborhoods are finite and the preceding argument makes every finite constraint system satisfiable. Compatible decisions form a finitely branching tree with nodes of arbitrarily large depth. Recursively choose the least child having arbitrarily deep extensions in a fixed finite ordering. This produces a full matching without an arbitrary choice principle. Each matching edge has the original short displacement. Choose one actual reference path occurring in its nonzero group-ring entry; that path gives a unit-labelled geometric basis isomorphism γ with short inverse.

Replace h by γ^{-1}h and locate the output basis at its matched input positions. This is now an automorphism of the SAME located module, with at most twice the original propagation; both the geometric matching and its inverse are retained in the final control budget. If inverse identities are available only over a buffered controlled set, impose matching constraints only on the basis vertices in a smaller buffered set. The identical finite Hall proof covers all those vertices and every neighbor needed by its local operations; no perfect matching outside the controlled buffer is required. Unmatched vertices are kept as exterior ports outside the next buffer, where no automorphism identification or vertex balance will be asserted. All subsequent controlled vertices and operation supports are chosen inside the matched buffer. Thus the finite vertex rank balance used below is actual, not an inference from equality of infinite cardinalities.

##2. Filling a puncture in a torus

Use the cube model D^m=[−1,1]^m throughout; scaled discs in the matrix argument mean these cubes. Fix a dimension m≤d≤4 and such a coordinate chart. The torus puncture is a separate small ball in a source chart and need not be cubical. The exact geometric input is a marked local homeomorphism, defined on an OPEN neighborhood of the following compact torus complement,

j:O→int((2/3)D^m),   O⊃T^m−int((1/2)B),

which is the identity on the designated central (1/2)D^m, where B is a ball in the torus disjoint from that central disc. Here is the actual construction from the retained accepted interface. Assuming Countable Choice, `thm-punctured-standard-tori-admit-relative-pl-immersions` supplies an orientation-preserving PL immersion a_m:T^m minus {p}→R^m, m=1,2,3, exactly the identity on a fixed central coordinate cube, with a subdivision finite on compact subsets. Rescale that cube to (1/2)D^m and restrict the domain to the complement of a small ball about p. Compose each target coordinate with an odd strictly increasing PL homeomorphism ρ:R→(−2/3,2/3) that fixes [−1/2,1/2]: for example set ρ(1/2+k)=2/3−1/(6·2^k), k≥0, and interpolate linearly, extending oddly. On the compact restricted domain only finitely many cells and breakpoints occur. The resulting j is the required finite marked local homeomorphism and retains the exact central marking. This uses the accepted contract only, not its proof. For m=4, use the NEW root-reviewed `items/lem-finite-marked-local-homeomorphism-from-a-punctured-four-torus.md` contract: its local homeomorphism is defined on an open neighborhood of the compact punctured torus, fixes[-2,2]^4, and has bounded cube image. Rescale by1/4 to obtain the displayed half-cube marking and two-thirds-cube range. The old1≤n≤3 statement is unchanged. No smoothness or PL assertion about j beyond this local-homeomorphism contract is needed by the matrix lifting. Choose a compact annulus outside the removed ball and choose γ below the separation of distinct sheets of j on a finite immersion-chart cover. Compactness gives δ>0 such that a δ-small matrix entry lifts uniquely within distanceγ from any chosen lift of its input basis point, as long as that input stays outside a slightly enlarged puncture. Uniqueness of these local lifts makes matrix products lift correctly on the smaller region: the lifted partial inverse identities are still identities there. The finite family of original basis points has finitely many lifts in this compact immersion domain, because every point inverse is discrete and compact.

Let P be the free submodule supported outside a larger annular buffer. Set a=h i_P, b=p_P g. The local inverse relation gives ba=1. The explicit completion

F(x,y)=(ax+(1−ab)y,by)

from P⊕G to G⊕P is an isomorphism, with inverse (u,v)↦(bu,(1−ab)u+av), as checked in the companion note. Rewrite its free summands so that the original matrix is retained away from the annulus, and place the added free basis at the puncture center. Here is the locality calculation with actual buffers. Put the basis vectors in three bands G0,G1,G2, where G0 lies between the puncture radius1/2 and1/2+γ, G1 between1/2+γ and1/2+3γ, and G2 outside1/2+3γ, using scaled ball radii and makingγ smaller than the available gap. Take P=G1⊕G2. The partial inverse relations give ba=p_P g h i_P=1. For q=1−h i_P p_P g, locality gives p_2 h i_P p_P=p_2 h, because h cannot carry a G0 generator across the two-band gap to G2. Likewise the outer partial inverse identity and propagation give p_2 h g=p_2. Hence p_2q=0: all new q-images lie in the two inner bands. Rewrite P⊕G=(P⊕G0)⊕P=G⊕P; the original G2 columns are mapped by h unchanged, while the added P-columns have original-module output only in the inner bands. Placing the added P copy at the puncture center therefore confines all uncontrolled entries to the puncture ball and annulus, not to the central fixed disc. Its possible large entries all stay inside the puncture ball together with its annulus.

Collapse the puncture ball to its center by a radial map of the torus fixed on the central disc and outside a slightly larger puncture ball. The important point is that the puncture completion was an actual invertible block matrix, not a declaration that a partial inverse extends.

##3. Making the uncontrolled factorization small without assuming its factors were small

Lift H to the universal cover R^m of T^m. Its based module is infinite as an R=Z[π]-module but finite free over R[Z^m], and the lifted automorphism is deck-periodic. The Whitehead-vanishing input for π×Z^m expresses its INVERSE, after identity stabilization, as a finite product of elementary matrices and allowed diagonal group units. Write explicitly H^{-1}=E_s⋯E_1E_0. Collect all diagonal deck translations into the rightmost diagonal factor E_0; conjugating an elementary factor by a diagonal unit leaves it elementary. The remaining factors E_1,…,E_s are triangular basis additions on the infinite R-module. All have bounded propagation because the factorization is finite and each Laurent polynomial has finite support. This bound need not have been known when δ was chosen.

Choose a radial map q:R^m→(2/3)D^m, identity on (1/2)D^m, whose radial derivative tends to zero at infinity. Outside a sufficiently large disc, every bounded-propagation entry of every elementary factor projects under q to displacement<γ/(s+1). Replace each triangular elementary factor by the identity on generators inside that large disc and keep it unchanged outside. It remains invertible: its off-diagonal map goes between two different free summands and squares to zero, even after this column restriction.

The deck-translation diagonal needs separate treatment. Each diagonal shift decomposes into orbits of basis vectors arranged along parallel lines. For m≥2, each orbit meeting the chosen disc can be rerouted outside it: remove its generators from the moving orbit inside the disc, retain those original generators as fixed identity summands, join its two entry/exit points by a path on the boundary sphere, place finitely many new generators along that path, and shift along the resulting ordered orbit. Extend the original liftedH and every other factor by the identity on the new generators. The finitely many such orbits may be treated independently because distinct module basis vectors may occupy the same control-space location. Choose spacing and the large radius so every modified shift becomes small after q. For m=1 the boundary sphere has two points, and rerouting a single shift is impossible; pair a positive shift with a negative shift and splice their entering/exiting tails. This succeeds exactly when the integer flux, the number of positive minus negative crossing orbits, is zero. The flux correction in Step5 ensures this condition before using a one-dimensional chart.

The modified factors E'_i are the identity on a large inner cube, agree with E_i off a larger cube, and are arbitrarily small after q. Form P=E'_s⋯E'_1E'_0H. Outside a sufficiently large cube this is exactly E_s⋯E_1E_0H=H^{-1}H=I. Inside the protected central cube every E'_i is identity, so P agrees with H there. This is the factorization orientation needed for truncation, not a product factoring H followed by another H. The inverse is P^{-1}=H^{-1}(E'_0)^{-1}(E'_1)^{-1}⋯(E'_s)^{-1}; bound its displacement in this reverse order by the same finite-factor budget. Enlarge the outer disc to contain the support of every nonidentity row and column, and delete the exterior identity generators. Only finitely many basis vectors remain. Applying q gives a finite automorphism extending the original h on (1/2)D^m. Both it and its inverse have arbitrarily small propagation by the finite-factor estimates. The new factors themselves were not required to be small near the center: they were made the identity there. This is the precise step that repairs an uncontrolled ordinary Whitehead factorization.

##4. Splitting off a small-disc piece by allowable elementary operations

Let h now be a partial small automorphism over (3/4)D^m. Step2–3 gives a global small extension H equal to h, after identity stabilization, over (1/2)D^m. Add an identity block of twice the extension rank. The explicit six-factor identity in the companion note transforms this identity block into diag(H,H^{-1}) by a small deformation. Reorder the three summands so that the old h and the new H^{-1} block are adjacent, leaving the new H block separate. Apply the explicit cancellation factors to the first two summands, restricting each off-diagonal block to input basis points in (1/2)D^m. A restricted off-diagonal block still squares to zero, so every such cut-off elementary factor is globally invertible. On (1/3)D^m the entire factors and their inverse compositions agree with the unrestricted cancellation: chooseγ so their fixed finite propagation budget is less than the gap between1/3 and1/2. Since h=H on the larger region, the first two summands are consequently the identity on all rows and columns of the central submodule. The assertion about rows uses the same gap and the inverse propagation, not only an identity on columns. Split off that central identity submodule. The unmodified third H block is already a globally defined separate summand. Thus the result splits as h_out⊕h_in, where h_out has no generators in (1/3)D^m, while h_in consists of H together with the central identity summands and is supported in (2/3)D^m. The construction stabilizes only by initially geometric identity blocks; the later H/H^{-1} pair is produced by the displayed elementary identity. It does not assume either H or H^{-1} is already elementary in the small disc.

##5. Handlewise splitting, its control loss, and flux

For a compact target region in a d-manifold X, d≤4, choose a sufficiently small finite handle decomposition of that region with extended charts D^j×D^{d−j}. The control proof cannot simply process one skeleton after another with a single fixedδ: projecting onto the normal D^{d−j} factor and pulling back can lose control along the D^j direction.

Choose the parameters backwards instead. Start with the desired final bound on the d-handles. Choose the allowed matrix bound on the(d−1)-handles so Step4 in their one-dimensional normal factors produces the needed d-handle bound. Then proceed backwards: subdivide each tangential j-disc into pieces narrower than half the already selected(j+1)-level bound, and choose its input bound so the normal(d−j)-dimensional splitting uses the other half. Continue down to the0-handles. There are exactly d+1 skeletal levels, hence at most five. Process them forwards. At levelj the outgoing part has no basis in the processed j-skeleton; the incoming pieces are confined to individual small handles. The backwards choices ensure the next level has the bound it requires. This supplies the missing control estimate in a naive induction.

The integer flux used here has an actual finite-dimensional definition. Augment R=Z[π] to the real numbers and denote the resulting finite real matrix by h_R, with its small partial inverse g_R. On an inner interval around0, the row identity p_inner g_R h_R=p_inner follows from the buffered inverse identities and propagation: columns outside the larger interval cannot reach the inner one in two short steps. Consequently every kernel vector of h_R vanishes on that inner interval. Its left and right parts are separately kernel vectors, because their h_R-images have disjoint supports. Apply the same argument to the transposes to split ker(h_R^t), hence the cokernel of h_R, into left and right parts. Define flux=dim ker_left(h_R)−dim coker_left(h_R). This is zero for the identity and is additive under direct sum. A one-step shift on a finite chain has one missing target at one end and one kernel generator at the other, so its flux is±1 according to orientation. More general diagonal shifts decompose into these crossing orbits. The source's printed323 formula says image in place of cokernel; that literal formula fails already for the identity and is not used here.

On a(d−1)-handle the normal factor is one-dimensional and its partial automorphism may have nonzero flux. If a circle in the remaining region crosses that handle once, stabilize by a close basis shift around that circle with the opposite flux. If a path crossing once has both ends outside the target region, use a shift along that path; any failure to be globally invertible occurs only at its endpoints, where no conclusion is required. If neither exists, the handle separates the remaining region and bounds a side disjoint from the uncontrolled exterior. On that side the buffered partial inverse identities leave kernel and cokernel supported only at the separating cut. Their total difference is the Euler characteristic of the finite two-term real complex, which is zero because its domain and range have equal rank. Thus the cut's flux is zero. This argument uses rank conservation and does not assume a geometric cancellation theorem for that side. These cases exhaust the component graph obtained by cutting across the remaining(d−1)-handles. Therefore every one-dimensional normal splitting may be arranged to have zero flux.

After the d-handles are reached, h is a direct sum of a piece outside the target and finitely many automorphisms each confined to one small handle. Wh(π)=0 deforms each confined piece to a geometric isomorphism after stabilization. These deformations can have arbitrarily many factors: all their basis points stay in the same small handle, so their total forward and reverse set compositions remain controlled by that handle's diameter. This is why ordinary Whitehead vanishing is sufficient only after the torus splitting, not before it.

### Actual proper schedule, including the annular supports

Assume a countable locally finite control-handle atlas, finite on compact sets, with compact handle closures and the chart-local reference data just specified. This is the actual supplied atlas for N²×(0,1), or for a neighborhood of a finite complex in the4-dimensional application. Build each index-j operation in an EXTENDED handle O_H≅D^j×int D^{d−j}, not merely in its core. At a fixed index the extended neighborhoods are chosen pairwise disjoint, including every annular operation support. Here is how to choose them. The index-j attaching footprints in the previous level boundary are pairwise disjoint compact product neighborhoods. Local finiteness lets their boundary collars have disjoint closed smaller extensions; take the positive minimum of the allowed collar widths on each footprint. Extend each handle only through its own such collar into the already processed lower-index part. The normal enlargement inside the handle uses its own product coordinates. Thus two same-index extended charts cannot meet either in their handle interiors or in their attaching collars. Perform the entire finite local block for all handles of a fixed index SIMULTANEOUSLY. Earlier split-off blocks remain separate direct summands and are untouched when the residual block is processed at the next index. ### Proper flux without a fictitious finite noncompact side

Before the index(d−1) normal-one-dimensional splitting, use the actual locally finite dual component graph: vertices are the compact residual d-handle pieces, and edges are the(d−1)-handle crossing regions. The atlas is locally finite with compact closures, so this is a locally finite graph, proper over X. To see this, cut the incident edge tubes in their buffered middle portions and apply the finite two-term augmented complex to the resulting compact vertex piece. All its basis sets are finite. Its equal source/range rank makes its Euler characteristic zero; the only residual kernel/cokernel terms are its incident cut ports, and their signed indices are exactly those fluxes. This is a FINITE vertex calculation. No Euler characteristic is assigned to an infinite side of a separating edge.

Orient each edge by the sign of its flux and replace it by its finitely many integer unit strands. At every controlled vertex pair incoming and outgoing ports. Choose the pairing canonically from fixed enumerations of the countable atlas and the finite ports. Uncontrolled exterior vertices may retain endpoints. The strands decompose simultaneously into finite cycles, proper double rays, finite relative paths, and relative rays to an end. A noncyclic strand cannot return infinitely often to a compact set: it would revisit a vertex infinitely often, but that vertex has only finitely many distinct ports, and repeating a port closes a cycle. Every compact set meets finitely many strands because it meets finitely many graph edges and each has finite multiplicity. This rules out the invalid construction that independently sends infinitely many cancelling lines through one compact core.

Every entry has its actual short reference-path label. Subdivide each compact graph edge and each finite vertex connector so a shift and its inverse have displacement at most b(x).
