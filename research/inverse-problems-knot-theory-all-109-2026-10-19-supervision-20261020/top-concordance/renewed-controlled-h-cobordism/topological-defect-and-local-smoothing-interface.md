# Topological defect and the local smoothing interface

This records the weaker technical refinement actually sufficient for the topological-isotopy branch of FQ8.1. It does not claim the smooth regular-homotopy negligibility refinement for a capped tower. The controlled-disk owner explicitly has not yet supplied whole-tower transverse spheres after every accessory-cap construction, and those spheres must not be invented here.

## Defect contained in a small regular neighborhood of a1-complex

Start after the controlled elementary handle deformation, with framed attaching spheres A and belt spheres B in the intermediate4-manifold. Let D be the actual small framed embedded Whitney discs provided by the new controlled Whitney helper, whose interiors avoid A∪B. Their pairwise disjoint product neighborhoods contain all the topological Whitney moves. Away from these neighborhoods and the original attaching region, the product constructed by handle cancellation remains in the original category M=DIFF/PL.

Write A' for the result of the Whitney moves. Its component A'_i intersects B_i once and misses every other B_j. Reversing a Whitney move is a finger move guided by one arc c from a sheet back to the other. The two descriptions give the same regular-neighborhood handle picture: in the framed Whitney-disc chart D²×D², collapsing toward the disc on the old side, or toward the finger arc on the new side, gives thickenings of the same elementary handle configuration. Therefore a neighborhood of A∪B∪D may instead be described as a neighborhood of A'∪B∪c, with one guide arc for each Whitney disc. The neighborhoods may be chosen inside the original small control balls, since this comparison takes place in the given Whitney charts.

Pass to the incoming boundary by undoing each2-handle. In local coordinates this replaces its belt sphere neighborhood S²×D² by D³×S¹; equivalently, each B_i is replaced by its attaching circle. The sphere A'_i, punctured at its single B_i intersection, becomes a disc whose boundary runs once along that circle. The disc together with its circle has a ball neighborhood: its radial product coordinates give an explicit0-handle. The guide arcs c then attach1-handles between these0-handles. Thus the entire region where the product can fail to be M is contained in a topological regular neighborhood of a1-complex. The graph itself may have many vertices and edges; its mapping-cylinder radial arcs stay in the individual small0/1-handle neighborhoods and therefore have small projected diameter. No claim that a whole graph component has small diameter is required.

This is the actual topological neighborhood argument on FQ printed106/PDF112, after the Whitney construction on printed104–105. It needs only the framed embedded Whitney discs and their actual product neighborhoods. It does not require a geometric dual sphere for the entire capped tower after disk construction.

For a relative product over V, all attaching regions and Whitney-disc neighborhoods used here have control-space closure outside V, by the same V^ε/V^{2ε} buffers as the handle and disk constructions. The defect-neighborhood comparison consequently preserves the protected product. For a proper control map and locally finite handle system, the graph and its regular neighborhood are locally finite, and every comparison takes place in a locally finite family of neighborhoods.

## What FQ8.1 actually consumes

Freshly read printed114–116/PDF120–122:

* Given a homeomorphism h between smooth4-manifolds and a smooth complex K of dimension≤2, choose a regular neighborhood U0 of K and put U1=h(U0).
* The mapping cylinder W of h:U0→U1 is a topological5-manifold with prescribed boundary smooth structures. The obstruction to extending those structures is in H⁴(W,U0∪U1;Z/2)=H³(U0;Z/2)=0. The implication from this vanishing to an actual smoothing is the independent5-dimensional theorem8.3B; it is not proved by calling7.2C on an unsmoothed W.
* Over U1 the topological mapping cylinder is an exact product, so it is a δ-h-cobordism with the controlled connectivity required for everyδ. Once its independent DIFF/PL structure is supplied, the controlled handle construction gives a nearby boundary homeomorphism with its nonsmooth set contained in the small topological graph neighborhood just described.
* First establish Case1 for a smooth1-complex: the nonsmooth locus is also contained in the actual DIFF/PL regular neighborhood of the finite2-complex K0∪T, supplied by the attaching-region and tower models. A small smooth isotopy moves a smooth1-complex off that smooth2-complex (1+2<4), and radial pushing moves it off the neighborhood. This step needs no transverse sphere for the whole tower and no regular-homotopy negligibility.
* For Case2, a graph's topological regular neighborhood from the explicit0/1-handle construction has an abstract smooth0/1-handlebody model. Apply the already established Case1 to its inclusion homeomorphism into the old smooth4-manifold: this topologically ambient-isotopes the graph to a smooth graph. A smooth small isotopy then moves that graph off the original smooth2-complexK (again1+2<4). Taking the inverse ambient isotopy moves K off the original graph. Radial mapping-cylinder pushing then moves K off the graph neighborhood. This derives the needed topological displacement without invoking an unproved4-dimensional TOP transversality theorem. The smooth regular-homotopy alternative would still require the separate whole-tower negligibility property.
* Turning the nearby homeomorphism into an isotopic replacement of h uses the separate approximation-implies-isotopy lemma8.1B (Cernavskii/Edwards–Kirby). The source states and invokes it from outside the development. No local proof of that general lemma is supplied in this work.

Thus basic controlled-product existence alone does not close local smoothing. The independently realized5-dimensional smoothing and the exact ambient-isotopy/displacement interfaces must also be supplied. This file is an explicit interface record and a proof of the graph-neighborhood refinement, not an assertion that those independent smoothing/isotopy suppliers have already been proved or accepted.


## Control-dimension hold for the local-smoothing application

The new controlled matrix construction currently proves the torus argument only for control-space dimension≤3, using the literal retained punctured-torus immersion1≤n≤3. This is sufficient for the actual normal-neighborhood cobordism controlled overN²×(0,1). FQ8.1 instead controls its mapping cylinder overU1, an open4-manifold. Applying the present3-dimensional control theorem there would be invalid. A new4-dimensional controlled matrix extension, or a rigorously constructed lower-dimensional proper reference map with good local groups, is still needed for that8.1 application. A radial collapse to a2-complex cannot be assumed to preserve good local fundamental groups: complement groups at singularities of a2-complex may be nonabelian free groups. The independent EK shell proof avoids a punctured4-torus for its own deformation theorem, but does not automatically fill this different controlled algebra input.
