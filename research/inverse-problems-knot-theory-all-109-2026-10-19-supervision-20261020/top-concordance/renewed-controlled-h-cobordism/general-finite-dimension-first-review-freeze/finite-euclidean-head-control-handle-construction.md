# Finite Euclidean-head PL control handles

NEW construction for the Quinn finite-head reduction, not an intrinsic atlas theorem for a TOP manifold. Only the control-base manifold is PL here; the geometric source remains the independently supplied smooth source. No smoothing of an arbitrary PL boundary is asserted or needed by the algebra.

Fix a compact K in R^n and an open neighborhood G of K, together with a prescribed positive handle-diameter bound a and finitely many nested compact control buffers. There is a compact codimension-zero PL neighborhood U of K contained in G with a finite PL handlepresentation whose handles and private collar extensions have diameter below a. The construction is relative a prepared collar when one is supplied. Every choice below is finite; a countable locally finite list of such finite constructions requires at most CountableChoice.

Choose a large finite rectangular box containing K and its positive compact G-buffer. Subdivide its Euclidean grid into cubes of mesh h, then use the standard compatible permutation triangulation of each cube: a simplex is obtained by increasing the coordinates in one of the n! orders. This is an explicit finite Euclidean triangulation, not a triangulation of the original TOP control base. Take the finite subcomplex A consisting of the grid cubes meeting K and all their faces. With h small enough, its closed star and two additional mesh collars lie in G, and every such local star has diameter below a after a fixed dimension-dependent subdivision. Nested buffer sets are handled by choosing h below their finitely many separation margins and taking the corresponding nested stars.

Here is an explicit regular neighborhood construction in this Euclidean triangulation L. In its barycentric subdivision, label the vertex b_sigma by zero if sigma is a simplex of A and by one otherwise. Let f be the affine extension and set U={f<=1/2} in the compact closed star. It contains A and its actual open neighborhood. On every barycentric simplex, partition its vertices by their zero/one labels. A point with 0<f<1 has the unique form (1-t)*z+t*w, where t=f, z lies in the zero-face and w in the one-face. Changing t while retaining z,w agrees on all faces. It gives an actual product collar across f=1/2; no arbitrary level-set regularity theorem is assumed. For a local PL chart, a vertex of the triangulated half-level lies in the interior of an edge with endpoint labels zero and one. The affine f coordinate along that edge solves the half-level equation; the normal fan is the link of that edge in the ambient Euclidean triangulation, a standard PL sphere. Thus the half-level has a PL (n-1)-ball chart, and U has PL half-space boundary charts. This verifies the codimension-zero manifold assertion. The outer box boundary is avoided by the chosen buffer.

A finite handlepresentation is obtained by thickening the simplices of A in increasing dimension. Give vertices disjoint small Euclidean n-balls. Suppose the faces of a j-simplex sigma have already been thickened. Remove their private collars from sigma; its remaining truncated simplex is a PL j-ball. In the actual affine Euclidean chart take a small (n-j)-normal ball along this truncated ball. At its boundary this product joins the already supplied face collars in the attaching product S^(j-1) times D^(n-j). The normal fan around an interior point of sigma is a triangulation of S^(n-j-1): orthogonal normal directions in the actual R^n are partitioned by the finitely many incident affine simplices and cover the whole normal sphere. Its cone is the actual normal ball. Consequently this is a j-handle, not merely an abstract cell thickening.

For distinct j-simplices their truncated compact cores are disjoint; their only original intersections were faces already assigned lower indices. Choose the finitely many normal radii and collar widths below half their positive separation and inside their individual Euclidean stars. They have disjoint CLOSED extended products, including collars. Their remaining boundary corners are straightened by the elementary PL product-coordinate shear in each corner quadrant, within smaller collars. This can be done with the previous face collars fixed, since all choices take place in the residual truncated products. Continue through j=n, where the normal ball is D0. All handles are thereby actual products with their full attaching collars; their cores contain all of A. Their union is a codimension-zero regular neighborhood, contained in the two-star neighborhood just chosen. It may be used as U instead of the displayed half-level model. The same zero/one weight product across nested face collars identifies their boundary germs, so changing to this handle-adapted neighborhood does not change the prepared compact buffer conditions.

All truncated cores, products and private extensions lie in one dimension-bounded collection of adjacent original cubes. Their diameters are bounded by C_n*h for a finite constant depending only on n (for instance take a deliberately larger finite multiple of the closed-star diameter). Choose h<a/C_n initially. Subsequent backward tangential subdivision is carried out before the forward matrix schedule, assigning every l-face separator index l as in this same construction. Relative prescribed products are left fixed in a smaller collar and all choices are made outside its closed buffer; there is no requirement to represent an arbitrary protected closed set exactly by the grid.

This supplies a finite PL Euclidean control handlebody with small actual products under finite choice. It supplies no geometric-source handles, no reference lifts, no theta-product channel inside an arbitrary later slab, and no global triangulation or smoothing of the original TOP base. Its role is solely to replace the previously unbound finite-head control-atlas hypothesis. The general finite-dimensional matrix and metric-transfer proof must still be independently reviewed with this lemma and the arbitrary-finite torus input.
