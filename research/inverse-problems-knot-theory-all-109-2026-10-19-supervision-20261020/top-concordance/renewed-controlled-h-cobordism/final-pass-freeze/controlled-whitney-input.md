Controlled deformation retraction to M0, combined with the controlled1-connectivity of W→E, gives controlled lifting of relative2-complexes into M0: lift to W and compose with that retraction, using the sum of the two track bounds. The analogous statement holds for M1.

The same low-dimensional lifting property holds for L after a further fixed small enlargement. Here is a concrete surgery argument, rather than an inference from abstractπ1 equality. L is obtained from M0 by replacing finitely or locally finitely many S¹×D³ attaching tubes by D²×S². A loop in a new D²×S² piece can be moved to its boundary by making its D²-coordinate avoid0 and pushing radially to S¹; a generic1-dimensional map avoids a codimension2 central set. This gives a small annular homotopy, supported in that2-handle, from any new attaching loop to a loop in the old complement. Its reference image changes only within that small handle. Fill the resulting loop in M0 by the controlled lifting property. A generic2-disc in the4-manifold M0 misses the1-dimensional attaching circles, since2+1<4. The filling then lies in the common part of M0 and the changed L. Glue back the annular homotopy to recover the original loop exactly. All changes take place in the finitely many small handle tubes met by the input; for a locally finite input family the shrinkings are chosen locally finitely. This also applies cell by cell to relative2-complexes, keeping the already supplied subcomplex fixed.

## Separate transverse spheres for the two collections

Write A_i for the attaching2-spheres of the3-handles and B_i for the belt2-spheres of the2-handles. The collections A and B are separately disjoint, framed, embedded spheres. The controlled matrix construction has put their based intersection numbers into λ(A_i,B_j)=δ_ij in the appropriate local group ring.

For B_i the2-handle contains a core disc D²×{point} meeting its belt sphere once. Its boundary is a small parallel of the attaching circle in M0. That circle bounds a small disc in W via the2-handle core; composing with the controlled retraction to M0 gives a small nullhomotopy in M0. Approximate this disc generically, keeping its collar fixed, and off every attaching circle. Shrinking tube radii as above lets the disc join the handle core to form an immersed sphere meeting B_i once and every other B_j zero times. Its normal framing is not needed at this intermediate stage. The dual handlebody and the retraction to M1 supply analogous unframed geometric transverse spheres for A.

## Framed geometric transverse spheres for A∪B

Take small framed parallel copies A' and B'. The matrix condition makes A'_i an algebraic dual to B_i and B'_i an algebraic dual to A_i, with extra intersections in opposite-sign equal-label pairs. Choose Whitney boundary arcs for those pairs and small immersed Whitney fillings V for A'∩B, W for B'∩A using the preceding lifting argument. Their basing paths and arcs stay in the selected package, and the compared auxiliary labels have the ACTUAL local reference-null witnesses in its allowed O2, as specified above. On each original sphere the arcs can be chosen pairwise disjoint and away from the distinguished unit points: a finite embedded forest of intervals does not separate S², so add the arcs one at a time in its connected complement. Different spheres are already disjoint; local finiteness reduces each compact sphere to finitely many arcs.

Tube the interiors of V off B using the separate geometric transverse spheres of B. These are ordinary connected sums guided along arcs of B to the distinguished transverse point. Correct a possible Whitney framing defect by spinning the boundary edge lying on A'. Do the dual operation for W, using the transverse spheres of A and spinning on B'. Now V misses B and W misses A in their interiors. They may still meet the other original collection.

For an interior A∩V point choose an arc in V to its boundary edge on B. Push the A sheet along this arc and through B by a finger move. This removes that A∩V point and adds only a cancelling pair of A∩B points. Similarly push B off W by fingers through A. The guiding arcs can be chosen pairwise disjoint and away from A'∪B' and every other finite disk package already cleaned, by1-dimensional general position in the4-manifold. Within a guiding disc avoid its finitely many intersections with those other discs before choosing the arc; then choose its transverse neighborhood sufficiently narrow. Thus a later finger does not undo an earlier cleanup. Local finiteness gives the same finite choice in each compact region. Interpreting the inverse of a finger of B through A as a finger of A through B allows B to be fixed throughout, by applying the inverse ambient isotopy to the whole configuration. Consequently these moves are realized by an isotopy of the attaching spheres A; the handlebody itself remains DIFF/PL.

The interiors of V∪W now miss A∪B. Perform the immersed Whitney moves on A'∪B' across these discs. The resulting spheres are framed immersions, and each meets its corresponding original sphere once and every other original sphere zero times. They are therefore geometric framed transverse spheres for the total collection A∪B. No zero self/mutual intersection forms of these auxiliary spheres are asserted. Their self-intersections are permitted by the helper's linking-torus construction.

Every tubing, spin and finger is confined to the original small handle spheres and their selected small filling neighborhoods. The parallel copies and guiding arcs introduce no excursion outside those packages. There are finitely many types of operations; choose the initial positive control function backwards through those finite bounds. An unbounded number of intersection points does not create an unbounded chain of control-space excursions, since all operations associated to one small sphere package remain within its already chosen neighborhood.
