# Controlled matrix construction over a three-dimensional control manifold

This is a detailed construction extracted from the actual proof of Quinn, Ends of maps I8.4–8.7, printed321–330. It is not yet an accepted local supplier. The vanishing of Wh(Z^r), r≤4, used in Step3 remains under separate construction/review. The finite punctured-torus immersion in Step2 is now supplied by the exact retained interface described below, under Countable Choice. The ring-theoretic draft is owned by `/root/top_free_abelian_torsion`. The existence of the immersion is not inferred from a PL atlas. On root authorization, only the Statement of the accepted frozen punctured-torus supplier was consulted; its Facts/Proof/current carrier and verification were not opened or checked. The purpose of spelling out the construction is to expose exactly what those inputs must support.

##1. Geometric modules and permitted stabilization

A finite geometric R-module is a finite free module with a chosen basis and a location in X for each basis vector. The support of a matrix column consists of the input location together with the locations of every nonzero output coefficient. A matrix has propagation≤δ if each such set has diameter≤δ. A controlled isomorphism includes an inverse with the same bound. An elementary deformation is a sequence of basis additions and allowed basis signs/group elements whose entire forward and reverse underlying set compositions have small displacement; bounding each individual factor alone is insufficient.

For a partial isomorphism h:P→Q over a buffered region C, choose a small partial inverse g, meaning gh=1 and hg=1 after restriction to the smaller basis submodules whose neighborhoods stay in C. The handle chain complex and its controlled contraction supply this data by the parity-isomorphism calculation in `completed-controlled-algebra-steps.md`.

Permitted stabilization adds a geometric small isomorphism, not an arbitrary invertible block. Such an added block is geometrically already a cancelling handle pair. The equal-rank chain construction proved in the companion note identifies the two stabilized bases with arbitrarily small displacement; if h is only defined as an isomorphism over C, rank discrepancies may be corrected outside C. This reduces to a partial automorphism. No inverse-torsion block is introduced at this reduction stage.

##2. Filling a puncture in a torus

Fix a dimension m≤3 and a disc chart D^m. The exact geometric input is a PL immersion

j:T^m−int((1/2)B)→(2/3)D^m

which is the identity on the designated central (1/2)D^m, where B is a ball in the torus disjoint from that central disc. Here is the actual construction from the retained accepted interface. Assuming Countable Choice, `thm-punctured-standard-tori-admit-relative-pl-immersions` supplies an orientation-preserving PL immersion a_m:T^m minus {p}→R^m, m=1,2,3, exactly the identity on a fixed central coordinate cube, with a subdivision finite on compact subsets. Rescale that cube to (1/2)D^m and restrict the domain to the complement of a small ball about p. Compose each target coordinate with an odd strictly increasing PL homeomorphism ρ:R→(−2/3,2/3) that fixes [−1/2,1/2]: for example set ρ(1/2+k)=2/3−1/(6·2^k), k≥0, and interpolate linearly, extending oddly. On the compact restricted domain only finitely many cells and breakpoints occur. The resulting j is the required finite PL immersion and retains the exact central marking. This uses the accepted contract only, not its proof. Choose a compact annulus outside the removed ball and choose γ below the separation of distinct sheets of j on a finite immersion-chart cover. Compactness gives δ>0 such that a δ-small matrix entry lifts uniquely within distanceγ from any chosen lift of its input basis point, as long as that input stays outside a slightly enlarged puncture. Uniqueness of these local lifts makes matrix products lift correctly on the smaller region: the lifted partial inverse identities are still identities there. The finite family of original basis points has finitely many lifts in this compact immersion domain, because every point inverse is discrete and compact.

Let P be the free submodule supported outside a larger annular buffer. Set a=h i_P, b=p_P g. The local inverse relation gives ba=1. The explicit completion

F(x,y)=(ax+(1−ab)y,by)

from P⊕G to G⊕P is an isomorphism, with inverse (u,v)↦(bu,(1−ab)u+av), as checked in the companion note. Rewrite its free summands so that the original matrix is retained away from the annulus, and place the added free basis at the puncture center. Here is the locality calculation with actual buffers. Put the basis vectors in three bands G0,G1,G2, where G0 lies between the puncture radius1/2 and1/2+γ, G1 between1/2+γ and1/2+3γ, and G2 outside1/2+3γ, using scaled ball radii and makingγ smaller than the available gap. Take P=G1⊕G2. The partial inverse relations give ba=p_P g h i_P=1. For q=1−h i_P p_P g, locality gives p_2 h i_P p_P=p_2 h, because h cannot carry a G0 generator across the two-band gap to G2. Likewise the outer partial inverse identity and propagation give p_2 h g=p_2. Hence p_2q=0: all new q-images lie in the two inner bands. Rewrite P⊕G=(P⊕G0)⊕P=G⊕P; the original G2 columns are mapped by h unchanged, while the added P-columns have original-module output only in the inner bands. Placing the added P copy at the puncture center therefore confines all uncontrolled entries to the puncture ball and annulus, not to the central fixed disc. Its possible large entries all stay inside the puncture ball together with its annulus.

Collapse the puncture ball to its center by a radial map of the torus fixed on the central disc and outside a slightly larger puncture ball. The ball and annulus were chosen small enough that this map has a fixed finite Lipschitz bound (a bound2 is obtainable with concentric radii). Moving basis locations by this map turns F into a global small torus automorphism H. It still agrees, up to the explicitly added identity basis, with the input h on (1/2)D^m. The important point is that the puncture completion was an actual invertible block matrix, not a declaration that a partial inverse extends.

##3. Making the uncontrolled factorization small without assuming its factors were small

Lift H to the universal cover R^m of T^m. Its based module is infinite as an R=Z[π]-module but finite free over R[Z^m], and the lifted automorphism is deck-periodic. The Whitehead-vanishing input for π×Z^m expresses it, after identity stabilization, as a finite product of elementary matrices and allowed diagonal group units. Collect all diagonal deck translations into one diagonal factor E_0; conjugating an elementary factor by a diagonal unit leaves it elementary. The remaining factors E_1,…,E_s are triangular basis additions on the infinite R-module. All have bounded propagation because the factorization is finite and each Laurent polynomial has finite support. This bound need not have been known when δ was chosen.

Choose a radial map q:R^m→(2/3)D^m, identity on (1/2)D^m, whose radial derivative tends to zero at infinity. Outside a sufficiently large disc, every bounded-propagation entry of every elementary factor projects under q to displacement<γ/(s+1). Replace each triangular elementary factor by the identity on generators inside that large disc and keep it unchanged outside. It remains invertible: its off-diagonal map goes between two different free summands and squares to zero, even after this column restriction.

The deck-translation diagonal needs separate treatment. Each diagonal shift decomposes into orbits of basis vectors arranged along parallel lines. For m≥2, each orbit meeting the chosen disc can be rerouted outside it: remove its generators from the moving orbit inside the disc, retain those original generators as fixed identity summands, join its two entry/exit points by a path on the boundary sphere, place finitely many new generators along that path, and shift along the resulting ordered orbit. Extend the original liftedH and every other factor by the identity on the new generators. The finitely many such orbits may be treated independently because distinct module basis vectors may occupy the same control-space location. Choose spacing and the large radius so every modified shift becomes small after q. For m=1 the boundary sphere has two points, and rerouting a single shift is impossible; pair a positive shift with a negative shift and splice their entering/exiting tails. This succeeds exactly when the integer flux, the number of positive minus negative crossing orbits, is zero. The flux correction in Step5 ensures this condition before using a one-dimensional chart.

The modified factors are the identity on a large inner disc, agree with the old factors off a larger disc, and are arbitrarily small after q. Their product with the original lifted H agrees with the identity off a sufficiently large disc, since there the original factorization still applies. Enlarge the outer disc to contain the support of every nonidentity row and column, and delete the exterior identity generators. Only finitely many basis vectors remain. Applying q gives a finite automorphism extending the original h on (1/2)D^m. Both it and its inverse have arbitrarily small propagation by the finite-factor estimates. The new factors themselves were not required to be small near the center: they were made the identity there. This is the precise step that repairs an uncontrolled ordinary Whitehead factorization.

##4. Splitting off a small-disc piece by allowable elementary operations

Let h now be a partial small automorphism over (3/4)D^m. Step2–3 gives a global small extension H equal to h, after identity stabilization, over (1/2)D^m. Add an identity block of twice the extension rank. The explicit six-factor identity in the companion note transforms this identity block into diag(H,H^{-1}) by a small deformation. Reorder the three summands so that the old h and the new H^{-1} block are adjacent, leaving the new H block separate. Apply the explicit cancellation factors to the first two summands, restricting each off-diagonal block to input basis points in (1/2)D^m. A restricted off-diagonal block still squares to zero, so every such cut-off elementary factor is globally invertible. On (1/3)D^m the entire factors and their inverse compositions agree with the unrestricted cancellation: chooseγ so their fixed finite propagation budget is less than the gap between1/3 and1/2. Since h=H on the larger region, the first two summands are consequently the identity on all rows and columns of the central submodule. The assertion about rows uses the same gap and the inverse propagation, not only an identity on columns. Split off that central identity submodule. The unmodified third H block is already a globally defined separate summand. Thus the result splits as h_out⊕h_in, where h_out has no generators in (1/3)D^m, while h_in consists of H together with the central identity summands and is supported in (2/3)D^m. The construction stabilizes only by initially geometric identity blocks; the later H/H^{-1} pair is produced by the displayed elementary identity. It does not assume either H or H^{-1} is already elementary in the small disc.

##5. Handlewise splitting, its control loss, and flux

For a compact target region in a3-manifold X choose a sufficiently small finite handle decomposition of that region with extended charts D^j×D^{3−j}. The control proof cannot simply process one skeleton after another with a single fixedδ: projecting onto the normal D^{3−j} factor and pulling back can lose control along the D^j direction.

Choose the parameters backwards instead. Start with the desired final bound on the3-handles. Choose the allowed matrix bound on the2-handles so Step4 in their one-dimensional normal factors produces the needed3-handle bound. Subdivide the tangential directions of each1-handle into pieces narrower than half that2-handle bound; then choose the input bound on those1-handles so their two-dimensional normal splitting uses the other half. Finally subdivide the0-skeleton charts as required and choose the initialδ so the three-dimensional normal splitting produces that1-handle bound. There are only four skeletal levels. Process them forwards. At levelj the outgoing part has no basis in the processed j-skeleton; the incoming pieces are confined to individual small handles. The backwards choices ensure the next level has the bound it requires. This supplies the missing control estimate in a naive induction.

The integer flux used here has an actual finite-dimensional definition. Augment R=Z[π] to the real numbers and denote the resulting finite real matrix by h_R, with its small partial inverse g_R. On an inner interval around0, the row identity p_inner g_R h_R=p_inner follows from the buffered inverse identities and propagation: columns outside the larger interval cannot reach the inner one in two short steps. Consequently every kernel vector of h_R vanishes on that inner interval. Its left and right parts are separately kernel vectors, because their h_R-images have disjoint supports. Apply the same argument to the transposes to split ker(h_R^t), hence the cokernel of h_R, into left and right parts. Define flux=dim ker_left(h_R)−dim coker_left(h_R). This is zero for the identity and is additive under direct sum. A one-step shift on a finite chain has one missing target at one end and one kernel generator at the other, so its flux is±1 according to orientation. More general diagonal shifts decompose into these crossing orbits. The source's printed323 formula says image in place of cokernel; that literal formula fails already for the identity and is not used here.

On a2-handle the normal factor is one-dimensional and its partial automorphism may have nonzero flux. If a circle in the remaining region crosses that handle once, stabilize by a close basis shift around that circle with the opposite flux. If a path crossing once has both ends outside the target region, use a shift along that path; any failure to be globally invertible occurs only at its endpoints, where no conclusion is required. If neither exists, the handle separates the remaining region and bounds a side disjoint from the uncontrolled exterior. On that side the buffered partial inverse identities leave kernel and cokernel supported only at the separating cut. Their total difference is the Euler characteristic of the finite two-term real complex, which is zero because its domain and range have equal rank. Thus the cut's flux is zero. This argument uses rank conservation and does not assume a geometric cancellation theorem for that side. These cases exhaust the component graph obtained by cutting across the remaining2-handles. Therefore every one-dimensional normal splitting may be arranged to have zero flux.

After the3-handles are reached, h is a direct sum of a piece outside the target and finitely many automorphisms each confined to one small handle. Wh(π)=0 deforms each confined piece to a geometric isomorphism after stabilization. These deformations can have arbitrarily many factors: all their basis points stay in the same small handle, so their total forward and reverse set compositions remain controlled by that handle's diameter. This is why ordinary Whitehead vanishing is sufficient only after the torus splitting, not before it.

##6. Relative and proper form required by7.2C

All charts, puncture additions, factor modifications, stabilizations and slides in the construction must lie in the allowed buffered control region. For a supplied product over V^{2ε}, choose the target and chart supports so their closures avoid V. The relevant smallness functions are bounded also by the distance to V on those supports. Then their entire preimages avoid the protected product; mere disjointness of the starting spheres in the cobordism would not suffice. This is the relative support mechanism used by the disk-construction agent as well.

The local coefficient calculation must not assume that the entire cobordism has fundamental groupZ or admits a global regularZ-cover. Refine the control-space handle charts so every extended chart lies in a contractible trivializing neighborhood for the reference system p:E→X. In such a neighborhood pull back the universal cover of the reference fiber model to the operating manifold; the controlled1-connectivity identifies the required deck coefficients with the local groupπ=0 orZ after shrinking to its buffered inner chart. All partial matrices, inverse identities and elementary factors in Steps2–4 lie in that one chart. Their coefficients are computed in its actual local cover. On an overlap a change of lift conjugates group labels by a unit and transports the local group by its given isomorphism; the matrix identities and permitted basis changes are invariant under both operations. Since each operation is the identity outside its own buffered chart, it defines an operation on the original path-labelled handle matrix without choosing a global cover. The split-off piece stays in that chart and is trivialized there. The handlewise algorithm is therefore an algorithm for the local groupoid of labelled paths, using the constant-ring proof only where that local groupoid has been trivialized. In particular base loops or a circle-bundle extension inπ1(W) have not been discarded.

For the actual oriented disk/annulus application the local group system is constant, not merely a system with abstract fibers isomorphic toZ. In an oriented locally flat pair chart, the ambient local homology generator is the cross product of the tangent surface generator and the normal2-plane generator. Both the ambient and surface generators transport with sign+1. Therefore the normal generator also transports with sign+1, and its positively oriented boundary meridian induces the same generator ofπ1=Z in every chart. Since Aut(Z)={±1}, every group transport is the identity. This computes the sign in pair charts without assuming a global normal bundle. Doubling changes both ambient and tangent orientations together and preserves this normal sign. The alternative trivial group system needs no such argument. This actual geometry was supplied by the normal-bundle owner; a nonorientable surface with sign monodromy is outside this constant-group specialization.

For X=N²×(0,1), use a locally finite handle decomposition with four index levels and apply the same backwards choices locally. Properness makes the based handle modules locally finite over X. Before choosingδ, ensure each selected chart and every operation using it stays in a relatively compact control neighborhood, and choose positive continuous minorants of the finitely many requirements meeting each compact patch. The operations within a fixed level have locally finite disjoint handle-chart cores; the annular supports are chosen inside their extended charts. Every compact set meets finitely many operations. Thus all matrix compositions and their geometric slides are defined locally finitely; there is no infinite product of moves accumulating at an interior point. At the deleted radial end, the prescribed output boundε(n,t)=t/10 supplies the shrinking needed for the eventual compactification, which remains owned by the normal-bundle assembly.
