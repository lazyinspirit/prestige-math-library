# Five-dimensional geometric stage

This is the geometric portion of the intended controlled proof. It assumes an independently supplied small DIFF/PL handlebody and the controlled deformation retractions. It does not assume TOP handlebody existence and therefore does not invoke the later normal-bundle conclusion. The controlled matrix trivialization and controlled embedded Whitney-disk construction remain separate suppliers under construction.

## Removing indices0,1,4,5

Here is the five-dimensional specialization of the argument actually read in Quinn I6.1–6.3, printed308–314. Work first over a compact control region with a buffer, and keep the existing collar fixed over the smaller protected region. All new paths and homotopies below are chosen inside that buffer; tracks are estimated before choosing the input smallness constant.

For each0-handle, the deformation to the incoming boundary gives a small path to the incoming collar. Push this path into the0/1-skeleton. It traverses only handles whose image meets its track, so its control diameter increases by at most a fixed dimension-dependent multiple of the handle size. Delete loops and terminate the path at its first meeting with the collar. The resulting paths can share0- and1-handles. Resolve this combinatorially: if m paths traverse a0-handle, replace its ball by m ordered0-handles joined by m−1 short1-handles; if p paths traverse a1-handle, subdivide its interval direction into p parallel ordered1-handles connected by p−1 short2-handles. Associate each path with its own copy in the same order at both ends. The order agreement makes the resolved paths pairwise disjoint. Higher attaching maps can be pushed off the new subdivision strips by small isotopies.

Each resolved path together with its0/1-handles is a thickened tree meeting the incoming collar in one boundary ball. Its regular neighborhood is a ball: remove leaf vertices inductively, at each stage gluing two balls along a boundary ball, using the collar coordinates on that gluing ball to straighten the union to one ball. Absorb these pairwise disjoint balls into the collar. This removes the original0-handles and changes only the chosen small neighborhoods. The same description estimates the enlarged collar tracks by the diameter of the resolved paths. Quinn's displayed bound at this stage is (n+4) times the input size.

For a1-handle, choose its core interval and use the deformation retraction to obtain a small relative nullhomotopy of that interval in the cobordism, with its two ends carried to the incoming collar. Push its interior into the2-skeleton. Because a2-disc and the1-handle cores have dimensions2 and1 in dimension5, a small general-position perturbation makes the disc disjoint from those cores. Push the remaining portions radially out to the intermediate boundary. This gives the relative nullhomotopy in the union of the intermediate boundary and the2-handles.

Intersect the disc with the2-handles in finitely many small discs after transverse perturbation. In its domain join those discs by a finite tree of arcs, avoiding double points and the boundary. In the four-dimensional intermediate boundary, the images of the arcs can be made embedded and pairwise disjoint: two1-dimensional arcs have negative expected intersection dimension, and their2-dimensional strips may be chosen narrow after the arcs are fixed. A regular neighborhood of the resulting tree and discs in the domain gives one embedded2-disc B in the upper boundary; the remainder of the original nullhomotopy describes a homotopy of its boundary loop to a loop crossing the belt sphere of the original1-handle exactly once and all other belt spheres zero times. A loop homotopy in a4-manifold can be perturbed, keeping its ends fixed, so that every time slice is embedded and distinct loops remain disjoint: the potential coincidence locus has dimension1+1+1−4<0. Thus it is an isotopy of the attaching circles. Its ambient extension is obtained in their normal tubes and cut off before the tube boundary.

Introduce a cancelling2/3-handle pair along B, choosing the2-handle framing induced by B. Apply the isotopy just constructed to its attaching circle. The new2-handle now cancels the original1-handle. The remaining3-handle is allowed. Repeating removes all1-handles. Every part of this construction uses a fixed number of traversals of the input nullhomotopy and handle neighborhoods; consequently choosing their size sufficiently small gives any prescribed output control bound. More importantly, the finite count is independent of how many handles occur: the resolving subdivisions and surgeries are simultaneous in disjoint neighborhoods. On a proper control map the construction is locally finite when applied to a locally finite handlebody.

Turn the handlebody upside down and repeat the same0/1 procedure from the outgoing face. In the original indexing this removes5/4-handles. Removing an indexk handle in the described procedure changes only indicesk,k+1,k+2; hence the outgoing operation does not reintroduce the removed0/1-handles. The result has only2- and3-handles. It still agrees with the supplied product on the protected region, since all neighborhoods were chosen outside its buffered preimage.

This specializes the source's general geometrical-connectivity induction to the only low indices needed here. It avoids its higher-index Hurewicz/excision step and does not apply the four-dimensional Whitney trick at this point.

## Actual cancellation once embedded Whitney discs have been supplied

In the intermediate four-manifold let A_i be the framed attaching2-spheres of the3-handles and B_i the belt2-spheres of the2-handles. Suppose that small handle slides and allowable identity stabilizations have already put the based intersection matrix into the identity, and suppose small pairwise disjoint framed embedded Whitney discs have been constructed for every extra A∩B pair, with interiors disjoint from all A and B.

Each Whitney disc has a product neighborhood fixed by its framing. In that neighborhood the two sheets meeting its boundary arcs can be replaced by the opposite pair of faces of the product disc; the replacement is an isotopy of the attaching sphere, keeping its boundary attachments and every B-sphere fixed. Its support lies in that product neighborhood. Disjoint Whitney discs permit these moves simultaneously. They eliminate exactly the paired intersections; the distinguished identity intersection of A_i with B_i remains. Thus each3-handle attaches across precisely one belt sphere of a2-handle, once, and misses all other belt spheres. The attaching framing extends over the Whitney move by its given normal product, so no new twist is introduced.

A2/3 pair with this geometrical intersection pattern has a standard cancellation chart. In that chart the two critical points are represented by the one-variable birth/death model u³−su plus a nondegenerate quadratic form in the four transverse variables. Changing s from positive to negative through the chart and using a cutoff before its boundary removes the two critical points while retaining the boundary collar. This gives the local product after cancellation, with tracks inside the union of the two handle neighborhoods and their attaching collar. Applying this to all disjoint pairs leaves a product over the target control region.

The smallness estimate here is obtained from the actual supports, not from the statement that the manifolds happen to be h-cobordant. Choose a positive continuous subordinate control function so the union of each handle pair and every Whitney neighborhood it uses projects into one prescribed small ball. In a compact control patch take the minimum of the finitely many required constants; in a locally finite patch system choose a positive continuous minorant of the finite minima. Both forward and reverse product tracks lie in those same balls. The resulting product is consequently controlled in both directions. The same argument preserves the product over V when every selected control ball has closure disjoint from V.

Neither the existence of the controlled elementary matrix deformation nor the existence of these small embedded Whitney discs is asserted by this geometric stage. Those are the two independent difficult inputs, not consequences of cancellation.
