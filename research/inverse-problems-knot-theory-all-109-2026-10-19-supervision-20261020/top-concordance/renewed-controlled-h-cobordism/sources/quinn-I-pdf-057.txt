Suppose then that the process is complete over Uc X, which consists

intersects the handle in exactly one point. Then there are small geometric
isomorphisms with arbitrary flux through the handle obtained by closely
spacing generators along the circle and shifting one way or the other.
Cancel the flux by stabilizing by one of these.

In the second case suppose there is a path (in X — U with ends in
X — D) which intersects the handle in a single point. Again we can cancel
the flux by adding a shift along the path. This will fail to be an isomorphism
at the endpoints, but this is acceptable since they lie in X — D.

Finally if neither of these holds, then the handle separates X — U and
the component on one side is disjoint from X — D. The homomorphism h is
therefore an isomorphism over this component, and the flux is automatically

zero. So in any case we can arrange the flux to be zero, complete the process
on the handle, and add it to U.

This completes the proof of Theorem 8.4.
