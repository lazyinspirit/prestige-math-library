Section 5 contains some e-homotopy theory over a space X, in particular
a version of the Hurewicz theorem. This is used, among other places, to
find homotopy classes of handles to delete in the improvement of the neigh-
borhoods of the end.

Section 6 begins the handlebody theory. It gives an ¢ version of a
geometrical connectivity lemma of Wall [1]. This is used to reduce the proof
of the thin h-cobordism theorem to a stable algebra problem.

Section 7 continues the handlebody theory. It employs the previous
sections to reduce the approximate end theorem (roughly the outline above
except for the last application of the h-cobordism theorem) to a stable
algebra problem.

Finally Section 8 provides solutions to the stable algebra problems
encountered in Sections 6, 7. ‘These questions are set in the geometric group
context developed by Connell and Hollingsworth [1], and the theorem can
be described roughly as a solution of the conjectures advanced in that paper.
The central ingredient of the proof is a Kirby torus argument, as might
have been expected.

Historical note

The end theorem was first formulated as a conjecture in December 1969,
in the course of an investigation of cell-like maps of manifolds (see
Siebenmann [2], p. 276 line 12). Most of the proof given was worked out at
that time except for the geometric group material of Section 8. The appli-
cations of Sections 3.1, 3.2, 3.3 were also observed to follow from the
theorem.
