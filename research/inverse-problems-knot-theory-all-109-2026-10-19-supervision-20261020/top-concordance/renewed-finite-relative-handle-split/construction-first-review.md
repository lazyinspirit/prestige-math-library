# Finite relative five-dimensional handle split

New finite authoring for the relative side-coverage construction. No adopted proof, acceptance, provider hash, native carrier or gate is checked or changed. In particular this note does not reopen the raw TOP recognition proof. It concerns an actual compact smooth/PL product-collared triad and actual finite handle coordinates; it does not place a PL structure on an unknown TOP5 manifold.

## Input and exact finite output

Let C be a compact smooth or PL5-manifold with corners. Its boundary has incoming face H, outgoing face J, and vertical side S. Assume S=T×[c,d] with the given collars, H∩S=T×c, J∩S=T×d, and the two corners have their actual product quadrant coordinates. More generally the following argument applies componentwise, with the SAME chosen c,d and collar widths for the finite side components. An open positive set O+ contains J∪S and a product collar of it, and an open negative set O− contains H. A neighborhood of the negative corner T×c is supplied inside O+∩O−. All occurrences of “contains” include an open neighborhood of the compact set in question. A finite compatible handle presentation is supplied, or is chosen from the standard smooth/PL relative Morse construction described below.

There are finite lower/upper handlebodies A,B in C, overlapping only in a chosen small collar of their common interior frontier, and a small removed corner region G⊂O+∩O−, such that C=G∪A∪B. They have the following concrete interfaces.

* The lower spine is Z−=H0∪Y, with H0 the corner-trimmed incoming face, Y a finite polyhedron of dimension≤2 outside H0. Its moving part misses the original side S and the new small vertical face; its attaching trace Y∩H0 has dimension≤1. Thus, when the collar/contact region is restored, the primary part is H∪Y and its intersection with S is contained in H∩S.
* The upper spine is Z+=P0∪D, with P0 the rounded/trimmed outgoing face J∪S. The moving D has dimension≤2, misses the new vertical face and the protected boundary collars except for its attaching trace, and dim(D∩P0)≤1. P0 and its contact collars are already in O+. No star incident to the4-dimensional side is counted as part of D.
* A and B are actual finite relative regular neighborhoods of their respective spines, with the small vertical collar treated separately as an already covered contact collar. Their finite collapse/compression models are given by product-handle collapses and mapping-cylinder prisms. They do not assert that a side4-manifold collapses to a two-complex.
* If an open set O contains the appropriate base face, a collar of the small vertical/contact face, and the respective finite moving spine, then a finite compactly supported ambient isotopy enlarges O to contain the entire corresponding closed handlebody. One may enlarge the handlebody by a small outer frontier collar before applying this assertion. The isotopy fixes a neighborhood of the base face and the entire prescribed contact collar. It also fixes any original side collar that has been retained in that contact region. This last clause requires that collar to be covered by O; it is not an assertion that arbitrary unengulfed side points can be covered while fixed.

The last assertion is the finite compact-set interface used by coverage. Equivalently, if a compact set K is disjoint from B and the contact region after the upper capture, it lies in int A and the lower compression engulfs it after Y is engulfed. No infinite composition, end hypothesis, group substitution or choice of an unknown TOP collar is part of this lemma.

## 1. Separate the corner before assigning boundary values

One cannot put Morse value0 on all H and value1 on all J∪S at their common negative corner. Use the actual negative quadrant chart

    T×[0,η)_u×[0,η)_v,

where H is u=0 and the original side is v=0. Choose a small constant δ>0 so the closed region u+v≤2δ is contained in the supplied common open buffer and actual original-side collar. Remove the corner u+v<δ from C and call the resulting compact manifold C0. Its new vertical face is R={u+v=δ}, parametrized by T×[0,1]; H0 is u=0,v≥δ, and P0 is the remainder of J∪S, with its positive corner rounded using its given product chart. H0 and P0 are now disjoint and meet the two ends of R. The removed corner and a collar of R stay in O+∩O−. Replacing the diagonal by a smooth monotone arc flat at its ends gives the smooth version; retain the diagonal for PL coordinates. All changes occur inside the actual common buffer.

All handle moves below take place in C0 and are zero near R. They extend by identity over the removed corner. Here is an actual predetermined guard, not an assumption that an arbitrary h will preserve an initially covered interior region. On the known original-side collar write coordinates(z,t,v), with t the given common height and v the inward normal. The two clean cuts have t=c,d there and the two opens have their actual specified height germs. Choose a nonnegative smooth ρ(t), supported in a compact height interval around[c,d], and a smooth χ(v), equal to1 near0 and0 near the collar's outer edgeη. Choose ρ small enough that ρ_max||χ′||<1. The maps

    κ_s(z,t,v)=(z,t,v+sρ(t)χ(v)), 0≤s≤1,

are diffeomorphisms onto the collar-trimmed regions with boundary v=sρ(t); extend by identity outside this collar. Their v-derivative is positive. They preserve t, the actual end cuts, and the input open sets on their support, since those are height products there. Thus they identify the pairs(Q±,U±) with their trimmed pairs and give the corresponding pair homotopy equivalences. In PL use piecewise linear increasing versions with matching end intervals. The cut-off ρ may be chosen constant on the compact negative-corner height interval.

Choose δ afterwards so the entire closed triangular region u+v≤2δ, and R and a collar of it, lie inside the removed normal collar Nρ. Perform both finite engulfings in the trimmed work regions. Their active spine pieces outside the relevant opens are compact and lie outside this known collar; its covered attaching pieces are stationary. Compact supports inside the trimmed regions extend by identity over Nρ, fixing an open neighborhood of it. In particular the first h fixes the whole triangular corner and R, so these remain in h(O+) and the negative compact complement cannot enter them. This proves the required G/R guard, instead of discarding the corner because it was initially covered. Relative connectivity is transported by these actual product-collar identifications; no connectivity after deletion of an arbitrary4-dimensional interior guard is inferred.

## 2. Actual finite handle split relative to R

Choose a relative Morse/PL handle presentation of C0 from H0 to P0, with the product coordinate on R and no critical point in its collar. The boundary values are now compatible:0 on H0,1 on P0, and the height on R. In the smooth case a function with these collar values is perturbed only in finitely many interior coordinate charts to have finitely many nondegenerate critical points. In the PL case use the given finite collared triangulation and its relative derived-block handle presentation. These standard finite constructions concern the supplied smooth/PL C0, not a TOP smoothing argument.

Order the finite handles by index. The elementary interchange has no dimension-five obstruction: when a later index-q handle must pass below an earlier index-p handle, q≤p, its attaching sphere has dimension q−1 and the earlier belt sphere has dimension4−p in the4-dimensional intermediate boundary. Their sum is q−p+3≤3<4. A finite small general-position perturbation makes them disjoint; the product collars then commute the handles. The perturbation and commutation miss R because all attaching/belt data are compact in the interior of that boundary. This orders the handles without a Whitney disk or any π1 assumption.

Take a regular intermediate level between indices2 and3. The lower part A is H0×[0,1] with only0-,1-,2-handles, with its lower part of the R collar. The upper part B, viewed backwards, is P0×[0,1] with the reversed original3-,4-,5-handles, hence only2-,1-,0-handles, with the upper part of that collar. Choose a small overlapping level collar so A∪B=C0 and their relative interiors cover C0. This overlap is internal and is not required to be fixed by either capture. The two pieces of R are already covered by their respective input open sets. Product charts near R stay unchanged.

No top-index handle has been omitted: an original5-handle is precisely an upper0-handle. An original0-handle is a lower0-handle. No assumption that C0 is an h-cobordism or has special fundamental group is needed for this finite split.

## 3. Finite low-index handlebody spine, including its base trace

We record the elementary relative handlebody fact with its construction. Suppose V is a finite m-dimensional0/1/2-handlebody over a compact(m−1)-dimensional base P, with a product side/contact collar Rv. The handles and their attaching maps are disjoint from a fixed neighborhood of Rv. Then V has a finite relative polyhedral spine P∪Z with dim Z≤2, Z disjoint from Rv, and dim(Z∩P)≤1. With the collars and an arbitrarily small outer frontier collar included, V is its relative regular neighborhood. “Spine” here permits branching along the attaching tracks; it does not claim that the cores have disjoint smooth closures or that their attaching loops embed in an earlier graph.

Here are the finite collapses. Triangulate each handle D^a×D^{m−a}, a≤2, and its attaching product, compatibly with the finite previous stage. A disk D^{m−a} has an explicit finite collapse to its center (triangulate it as a cone on its boundary, then remove paired cone/base faces). The elementary product-collapse rule gives

    D^a×D^{m−a} ↘ (∂D^a×D^{m−a}) ∪ (D^a×{0}).

For clarity, this rule is relative to the attaching product. For each free pair of the normal disk, process its products with the nonboundary faces of D^a in decreasing face dimension; the corresponding product free pairs remove exactly the product interior, leaving ∂D^a unchanged. A staircase triangulation of each product cell converts these into ordinary simplicial free pairs. Thus this displayed collapse is finite and does not collapse an attaching band that belongs to the previous body.

To combine it with earlier collapses, retain the trace of the new attaching CORE boundary, rather than the whole high-dimensional attaching band. If λ:∂D^a→V_old is that boundary and r_t is an earlier elementary-collapse deformation, retain

    Track(λ)={r_t(λ(z)): z∈∂D^a, 0≤t≤1}.

Subdivide its source and the finitely many collapse prisms so the maps are PL on each piece. The image is a finite polyhedron of dimension≤a. Its core boundary at the terminal end lies in the earlier spine. The new spine is the old spine, this track, and the new core D^a. The attaching map is not replaced by a putative embedded loop in the old spine: all its actual branching is retained in this image. Every operation occurs in the previous handlebody, and the normal thickening follows the original attaching product, so its framing/embedding information is retained.

Here is the relative regular-neighborhood verification behind this tracking operation. For one free pair of the old collapse, cut its standard collapse prism along the finitely many PL pieces of λ. Thicken those pieces in the original normal product coordinates. On each resulting product prism, remove the free pairs not incident to the retained track first, and then the product free pairs incident to the attaching band's normal factor. The remaining set is exactly the earlier collapsed face together with the thickened track and the new core thickening. The two boundary parametrizations are the original product parametrizations; on their common faces they are equal, so the operations glue. This is the usual prism mapping-cylinder construction, here on the actual finite attaching data. Iterating through the old finite free-pair sequence proves that the thickening of the tracked spine is the original attached handlebody, relative to P and Rv. It also supplies its finite relative collapse/prism compression sequence. No homeomorphism of a side star to a2-dimensional set is asserted.

The dimension estimate follows at every step: the old moving spine has dimension≤2; ∂D^a×I has dimension a≤2; the new core has dimension a≤2. Retain no part of the attaching band except its inclusion in the existing body and these core-boundary tracks. Hence the moving spine remains at most2-dimensional even though its base P has dimension m−1.

For the base trace, each collapse deformation is stationary once it reaches P. Thus a trajectory's contact point with P equals its terminal point in P, and the union of all such points is the PL image of the original ∂D^a, of dimension≤a−1≤1, together with earlier attaching traces. The new core has boundary dimension a−1 and interior off P. There are finitely many handles, so dim(Z∩P)≤1. All tracks and core interiors can be kept away from Rv: the old deformation is product/stationary in that collar, and all attaching data started outside a fixed smaller one. The initial collar P×[0,1] collapses normally onto P, stopping at P. Its side/contact part is treated separately as a covered product collar, not included in a moving2-polyhedron. A0-handle has no attaching trace and begins with its center point; disconnected0-handles join by later1-handles if the given body is connected.

Apply this construction to A over H0 to obtain Y, and to B over P0 to obtain D. Their attaching traces have dimension≤1. Restoring the removed corner adds only the original incoming base/collar and the already covered outgoing side/collar. In particular Y has no new intersection with the original side S, and no high-dimensional star adjacent to P0 is counted as part of D.

## 4. From a finite collapse to actual ambient capture

We spell out the extra operation used after a spine has been engulfed. It is stronger than a bare deformation retraction, which would not itself give an ambient isotopy.

Let Z=P∪Z0 be one of the preceding finite spines and let V be its finite regular neighborhood/handlebody, with contact collar Rv. Choose a slightly larger relative regular neighborhood V+ by adding an outer collar along its internal frontier, and take a compact cutoff region inside V+ outside that frontier collar. The base/contact collars remain fixed. Suppose an open set O contains Z and all the contact collar. Compactness gives a smaller finite neighborhood N(Z) contained in O, together with the already covered contact collar. The finite product-collapse/prism sequence just constructed has the following explicit ambient realization.

For a single free pair, use the product coordinates of its thickened prism. There is a longitudinal coordinate from the retained face to its free face; the other coordinates range over a compact polyhedral footprint and normal disk. The retained face and its chosen small collar are inside the current covered neighborhood. Choose four levels a<c<b<b+ on the active longitudinal fibers, constant on a smaller footprint and pinching to equality at the footprint boundary. The increasing fiber map taking [a,c] onto[a,b] and[c,b+] onto[b,b+], identity outside[a,b+], expands the current covered collar across the prism. Interpolate its image level from c to b to obtain an isotopy. Use nested compact footprints so the active support misses the fixed base/contact collars and the outside frontier of V+. When some part is already covered near a fixed collar, terminate the footprint there and use that open margin for the pinching region. Thus no unengulfed point on a fixed side is asked to move into O.

Reverse the finite collapse order, applying this push for every prism/free pair. At each step only finitely many collars must be retained; their positive open margins allow the next parameters and support cutoffs. The resulting finite composition is an ambient isotopy supported inside V+, fixing a neighborhood of P and the prescribed contact collar, whose final image of O contains V. For a compact target K⊂int V+ the same argument stops after the finitely many prisms meeting it; choose V with K⊂int V and with its outer collar still in V+. In the smooth version carry out each active product-prism push in the actual smooth handle charts, refining into finitely many smaller chart prisms when a tracked face crosses a chart boundary. The collar margin permits rounding the finite polyhedral footprint and its free faces before the push. Choose smooth increasing fiber diffeomorphisms with these endpoint values and positive derivative, using a smaller inner prism to preserve the strict engulfing inclusion and smooth cutoffs on its outer buffer. On overlaps first leave a buffer fixed and perform the smaller prisms successively, retaining the already covered open margins. Each such map is equivalently a compactly supported smooth longitudinal flow in that Euclidean chart. There is no attempt to smooth an arbitrary PL homeomorphism of a5-ball or transport a field through an unsmoothed PL coordinate change. The finite composition is a smooth isotopy when C and the supplied handle charts are smooth; the PL construction supplies the PL version.

The existence of the outer frontier collar is why capturing the entire closed B, including its internal frontier, does not require an unsupported endpoint-limit argument. Capture takes place in B+ and stops at the smaller B. The outgoing base and original side stay fixed pointwise. The same statement for A fixes H0 and its contact collar. Neither capture fixes the shared A/B interface, which is an interior auxiliary surface and need not be protected.
