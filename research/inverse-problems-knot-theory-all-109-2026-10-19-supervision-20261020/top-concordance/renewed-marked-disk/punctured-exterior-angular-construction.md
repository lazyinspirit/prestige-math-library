## Exact input

An actual embedded marked tube `T:D²×D²_η→B⁴` must extend the prescribed positive zero-longitude knot tube on the boundary collar, with equality after restriction. Put `X=B⁴\Int(T)`, with its product corners straightened. Its capped boundary Seifert surface is `Σ=F∪D²`, and its actual angular fiber germ is an embedding `Σ×(-a,a)→∂X`, with the circle map `exp(iθ)` on this germ. The meridional map `f:X→S¹` is constructed by the exterior homology and covering-space argument, agreeing with the given angular map near `Σ`. Before using a regular-fiber approximation we arrange the stronger boundary condition `f^{-1}(1)∩∂X=Σ`, as follows; merely agreeing near `Σ` would not imply it.

Cut `∂X` along the actual angular bicollar of `Σ`. On the two retained edge collars prescribe a real coordinate in `(0,1)` agreeing respectively with the positive small angular coordinate and with one plus the negative small angular coordinate. Extend these real functions across the compact cut core with values in `[δ,1−δ]`: an elementary partition of unity subordinate to the two edge collars and their complement does this, taking value `1/2` on the complement. Exponentiation gives a boundary map `b:∂X→S¹` which equals the prescribed angular map near `Σ` and has no other inverse image of `1`. Following a loop through the cut pieces shows that its degree is its signed crossing number with `Σ`. The positive meridian crosses `Σ` once. The marked zero-surgery decomposition has `H₁(∂X;Z)=Z` on this meridian, so `b` induces exactly the restriction of the exterior meridional class. The quotient `b/(f|∂X)` therefore has a continuous real lift, by zero winding on every loop. On the connected prescribed angular germ this lift is a constant integer; choose it to be zero there. On an actual TOP boundary collar, multiply this lift by a cutoff which is one near the boundary and zero at the inner edge, and multiply the exterior map by its exponential. After restriction this makes the map equal to `b` on the whole boundary collar, retaining the prescribed angular germ. This is a continuous operation on the actual given collar. These inputs still do not assert an interior regular fiber.

## Why puncturing resolves the angular seam

A smoothing of all of the compact exterior relative its prescribed boundary smoothing is unnecessary. Choose an interior point `p` away from the protected boundary fiber germ. A smooth structure on `X\{p}` extending a chosen product smoothing on the actual TOP boundary collar suffices. This is a smoothing on the same underlying space: the prescribed TOP angular coordinate is made smooth by choice of structure, rather than being declared smooth in the ambient standard smoothing. The final construction is pulled back to the original point set and all equalities of germs are literal.

Here is the precise construction assuming the local smoothing operation described below.

1. Give `Σ` its smooth structure from the smooth Seifert surface and the capping disk, matching their longitude collar. Give its prescribed angular neighborhood the product smoothing. Extend this to a smoothing of the TOP three-manifold `∂X`, without changing a smaller angular neighborhood; retain its actual product collar in `X`. This requires the relative three-dimensional smoothing interface, not just the assertion that an abstract three-manifold has some triangulation. One way to obtain that interface is to cut along the product neighborhood, smooth the remaining three-manifold relative its surface-boundary collars, and reglue using those actual collars.

2. Choose `p∈Int X` with `f(p)≠1`, and a small coordinate ball `U` whose closure is disjoint from the protected collar and whose image lies in a contractible arc avoiding `1`. Such a point exists, for the meridional map on the boundary takes values different from `1`, and the same is true on nearby interior collar points. Lift `f|U` to the real interval parametrizing this arc. Multiply its difference from the value at `p` by a cutoff which is zero on a smaller ball and one near `∂U`. Exponentiating gives a homotopic circle map, equal to the original near `∂U` and the boundary germ, and constant with value different from `1` near `p`.

3. Smooth `X\{p}` relative the product boundary smoothing. Approximate the circle map in this smoothing, keeping it equal to the product angular map on a smaller neighborhood of `Σ` and constant near `p`. Outside the protected angular neighborhood its boundary map may first be approximated relative that neighborhood. It remains disjoint from `1` there: the compact complement of a smaller angular neighborhood has image a positive distance from `1`. Hence its entire boundary fiber is still exactly `Σ`. Relative smooth approximation on the manifold with boundary is obtained in finitely many charts on the compact region where a change is needed, using lifts to short circle arcs, a partition of unity, and an arbitrarily small target error. The map is already smooth on the two protected open sets, so no approximation is required there.

4. Make `1` a regular value while fixing these protected open sets. On the compact region possibly mapping near `1`, take finitely many smooth bumps `β_j` supported away from the protected sets, with some `β_j>0` wherever the current map is not already transverse. In local circle coordinates consider `g_b(x)=g(x)exp(iΣb_jβ_j(x))`. At every point mapping to `1`, either the source derivative is already surjective on a retained product neighborhood, or a parameter derivative `β_j` is nonzero. The combined map is therefore transverse to `1` for sufficiently small parameters. Sard applied to its inverse-image manifold and projection to parameter space supplies an arbitrarily small `b` for which `g_b` is transverse to `1`. No perturbation occurs near `p` or on the fixed fiber germ. This is relative smooth transversality in the newly chosen smoothing, not an assumption of TOP transversality.

5. Its inverse image `W=g_b^{-1}(1)` is compact: it is closed in `X` and misses a whole neighborhood of `p`. It is a smooth three-manifold in `X\{p}`, with boundary exactly `Σ`; thus it is locally flat in the original TOP exterior. Its normal orientation is the positive circle direction. Choose a smooth vector field `v` with `d(arg g_b)(v)=1` near `W`, tangent to `∂X`, and equal to the prescribed angular coordinate field on the retained boundary product germ. Local solutions patch by partitions of unity because this equation is affine. Compactness gives a common flow time, and uniqueness of the flow makes `W×(-ε,ε)` an embedded actual bicollar. On the retained germ this bicollar is exactly the prescribed angular one. Delete closed components. Since `Σ` is connected, exactly one remaining component meets it.

The procedure constructs an actual compact bicollared fiber. A continuous circle representative alone does not do this. It also does not claim that the given disk or its tube is smooth in the standard ball.
