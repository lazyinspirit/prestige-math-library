# Free abelian integral group rings: detailed local algebra draft

Author: `/root/top_free_abelian_torsion`, 2026-10-20. Research draft, not an accepted
library item and not native review coverage. No canonical supplier was inspected
for proof content or changed. This note develops the low-dimensional algebra
rather than invoking Bass–Heller–Swan as a black box. The argument is supplied for independent mathematical review before any
canonical integration; it supplies no audit or acceptance stamp.

Put R_r=Z[x_1^{±1},...,x_r^{±1}]. The desired interfaces are
K_0(R_r)=Z[R_r] and K_1(R_r)=C_2⊕Z^r, generated by the units -1,x_1,...,x_r.
They imply reduced K_0(R_r)=0 and Wh(Z^r)=0. Finite arbitrary r is required by
Quinn's locally constant controlled theorem; the torus normal-bundle construction
currently needs r≤6. None of these assertions supplies Wh(Z^r×F_2)=0.

We work with unital modules and the usual axiom of choice. Choice is used in
free resolutions of arbitrary modules and the well-ordering argument below.
Only finitely generated modules occur in Grothendieck groups.

## 1. Ring-theoretic foundations and the Euler-resolution lemma

A category's K_0 is generated by isomorphism classes [M], with relation
[M]=[M']+[M''] for every admissible short exact sequence. For projective modules
the sequences split. G_0(A) means K_0 of finitely generated A-modules when A is
noetherian. K_1(A)=GL(A)/E(A), with stabilization by adjoining identity matrices.
The stable elementary subgroup is the commutator subgroup: elementary matrices
are commutators, since e_ij(a)=[e_ik(a),e_kj(1)] after increasing size; conversely
block interchange and elementary block operations show diag(U,V) and diag(V,U)
have the same class and that diag(U,V) has the class of diag(UV,I). Thus this
quotient is the stable abelianization. In particular block triangular matrices
have class equal to the sum of their diagonal classes.

**Finite global dimension for these rings.** A submodule of an arbitrary free
Z-module is free. To see this, well-order a basis, intersect with its increasing
initial spans, and observe that each successive quotient embeds in Z and is
therefore a principal ideal, hence free. Split each successor exact sequence;
at a limit take the union of the already chosen bases. Every element has finite
support, so these choices give a basis of the whole submodule. Consequently
any Z-module has projective dimension at most one.

If A has global dimension ≤d, any A[t]-module M has the exact sequence

0 → A[t]⊗_A M → A[t]⊗_A M → M →0,

where the first map sends t^i⊗m to t^{i+1}⊗m−t^i⊗tm and the second sends
t^i⊗m to t^im. Injectivity follows by taking the greatest nonzero coefficient;
exactness in the middle follows by reducing a polynomial tensor using this
relation. Inducing an A-projective resolution gives projective dimension at
most d for each induced term, hence at most d+1 for M (splice the resolutions,
or apply Hom and its long exact sequence). Localizing a projective resolution
of an A-module M which already is an S^{-1}A-module proves that localization
does not increase this bound. Thus R_r and R_r[t] have global dimension at
most r+1 and r+2, respectively.

These rings are noetherian. The polynomial induction is the leading-coefficient
proof of the Hilbert basis theorem: in an ideal collect the ascending ideals of
leading coefficients through degree n; after their stabilization choose finite
polynomials realizing generators, reduce the degree of every larger polynomial,
and choose finite generators of the finitely many lower-degree coefficient
modules. Localization preserves finite generation of ideals by clearing
finitely many denominators. Every finitely generated module therefore has a
finite resolution by finitely generated projectives: construct finite free
surjections; kernels stay finitely generated; the dimension bound makes the
last kernel projective.

**Euler-resolution lemma.** Let P⊂C be full additive subcategories of an abelian
category. Suppose C is closed under kernels of epimorphisms between its objects,
P is closed under kernels of epimorphisms between its objects, and every object
of C has a finite P-resolution. Then K_0(P)→K_0(C) is an isomorphism.

Here is the proof used below. For any morphism f:C'→C and any finite
P-resolution P_*→C there is a finite P-resolution P'_*→C' and a map to P_*.
Prove this by induction on the resolution length. At length zero any resolution
of C' maps to P_0=C. In positive length let Z=ker(P_0→C) and form the pullback
B=P_0×_C C'. Choose a P-epimorphism P'_0→B, let Y=ker(P'_0→C'), and apply the
induction hypothesis to Y→Z and the truncated resolution of Z. Splicing gives
the required map. All the kernels stay in C by hypothesis.

A finite acyclic complex with all terms in P has Euler sum zero in K_0(P):
starting at the last nonzero differential, every cycle stays in P by the kernel
hypothesis, and split it into its short exact cycle sequences. The cone of a
quasi-isomorphism between finite P-complexes is such an acyclic complex.
Apply the preceding comparison construction to C→C⊕C and the direct sum of two
resolutions of C. Its two projection maps are quasi-isomorphisms, so the two
Euler sums agree. Hence χ(C)=Σ(-1)^i[P_i] is independent of resolution. For
0→C'→C→C''→0 use the comparison construction on C'→C; the cone resolves C'',
so χ(C'')=χ(C)−χ(C'). Thus χ induces an inverse on K_0, as claimed.

Applying this to projectives and finitely generated modules over a noetherian
finite-global-dimensional ring gives K_0(A)≅G_0(A). The proof, and not merely
its theorem name, is now available for the uses below.

## 2. G_0 localization and polynomial invariance

**Localization.** For a commutative noetherian A and multiplicative set S, let
T_S be the category of finitely generated S-torsion modules. Then

K_0(T_S) → G_0(A) → G_0(S^{-1}A) →0

is exact. A finite presentation over the localization descends after clearing
finitely many denominators, proving surjectivity. If two descended modules
become isomorphic, lift the isomorphism to a map after first multiplying it by
an element of S; its kernel and cokernel are S-torsion, so the two modules have
the same class modulo torsion. A short exact sequence over S^{-1}A descends to
a complex of finite modules whose homology is S-torsion; taking image and kernel
sequences shows that its additivity relation already holds modulo torsion.
This constructs the inverse from G_0(S^{-1}A) to the displayed cokernel and
proves exactness. The same reasoning shows that the torsion contribution is
generated by finite modules killed by some s∈S.

For S={1,t,t²,...} in A[t], a module killed by t^N has a finite filtration by
t^iM with successive factors A-modules. Its class in G_0(A[t]) is a sum of
classes of these factors. Each factor N has zero class there, by
0→N[t] --t-->N[t]→N→0. Hence

G_0(A[t]) ≅ G_0(A[t,t^{-1}]).

**Polynomial invariance.** The flat extension map i:G_0(A)→G_0(A[t]) is an
isomorphism for every commutative noetherian A. Its left inverse is

[M] ↦ [M/tM]−[ker(t:M→M)].

These are finite A-modules; the kernel-cokernel long exact sequence for
multiplication by t makes the displayed difference additive. On N[t] it is [N].

For surjectivity argue by noetherian induction on quotients of a fixed A.
If there is a counterexample choose an inclusion-maximal ideal J in the set of ideals for which
A/J fails surjectivity, and replace A by A/J. Every nonzero quotient now
satisfies surjectivity. The nilradical I of A is nilpotent, since it is finitely
generated by nilpotent elements. Filtration by its powers expresses every
finite module, over A or A[t], as a sum of A/I or (A/I)[t] modules. Thus if I≠0,
surjectivity for A/I gives it for A, a contradiction. We may assume A reduced.

A reduced noetherian ring has finitely many minimal primes p_1,...,p_s and its
total quotient ring Q is ∏Frac(A/p_i). Here is the algebra behind the use of Q.
The map A→∏A/p_i is injective. By prime avoidance choose, for each i, an element
a_i in all other p_j but outside p_i. The sum a=Σa_i lies outside every p_i,
is a nonzerodivisor, and the elements a_i/a are the mutually orthogonal
idempotents selecting the factors in Q. In factor i every nonzero b modulo p_i
can be inverted after multiplying by the selector and clearing the other
factors: choose representatives and add a sum of the other selectors to make
a denominator outside all p_j. This proves the stated product decomposition.
An element outside all minimal primes is a nonzerodivisor because A embeds in
the product of the domains A/p_i; conversely elements of p_i kill the nonzero
a_i after multiplying by suitable elements of the other minimal primes, or
use the displayed selectors in Q. Only the first direction and the product
construction are needed here.

Apply localization to A and A[t] using the nonzerodivisors S of A. Any torsion
module is killed by a single s∈S. Since (s)≠0, the induction hypothesis for
A/sA says its class over A[t] comes from A/sA, and hence its transfer class comes
from A. On the localized end, G_0(Q)→G_0(Q[t]) is an isomorphism: for each field
F, F[t] is a PID, finite modules split into free and cyclic torsion modules,
and each torsion module F[t]/(f) has zero G_0-class by its two-term free
resolution. So both groups are generated by the rank-one free module in each
factor. Given a class of G_0(A[t]), subtract a lift from G_0(A) of its image in
G_0(Q[t]); the remainder is torsion and also comes from A. This proves
surjectivity and completes the induction.

Consequently G_0(R_r)=G_0(Z)=Z. For Z, a finite module splits into its free and
finite cyclic parts and each cyclic torsion part has zero class. The rank is
the inverse. Section 1 therefore yields, with its explicit generator,

K_0(R_r)=Z[R_r], for every finite r.

## 3. Nilpotent endomorphisms and polynomial K_1 invariance

Let Nil_P(A) be finite projective A-modules with a nilpotent endomorphism ν;
let Nil_M(A) be finite A-modules with a nilpotent endomorphism. Exact sequences
must respect ν and be exact on underlying modules. Assume A noetherian of
finite global dimension d.

Every (M,ν) has a finite Nil_P-resolution. If ν^N=0 and P→M is a finite free
surjection, take P^N with the shift endomorphism σ, and send the ith summand to
ν^i times the chosen map P→M. Orient σ to send summand i to i+1 and the last
to zero. This is an equivariant surjection. Its kernel has underlying projective
dimension at most max(pd M−1,0); continue, stopping once the kernel is
projective. Nilpotence persists on kernels. Both categories are closed under
the relevant kernels, so the Euler-resolution lemma gives

K_0(Nil_P(A)) ≅ K_0(Nil_M(A)).

The forgetful map K_0(Nil_M(A))→G_0(A) is an isomorphism with inverse sending
M to (M,0). A proof of injectivity is needed here: ν^iM/ν^{i+1}M carries zero
endomorphism, so in K_0(Nil_M(A))

[(M,ν)]=Σ[(ν^iM/ν^{i+1}M,0)]=[(M,0)].

The second equality is additivity for the same filtration on the underlying
module with zero endomorphism. Hence K_0(Nil_P(A))→K_0(A) is an isomorphism.

For (P,ν) the automorphism 1−sν of P[s] defines an element of K_1(A[s]):
choose a complement Q with P⊕Q free and extend by the identity on Q[s]. This
is independent of the complement because adding complements makes the two
representatives conjugate after stabilization. A short exact sequence of
nilpotent projectives splits as A-modules; in a splitting, 1−sν is block
triangular, so its K_1-class is the sum of the diagonal classes. This therefore
defines an additive homomorphism τ:K_0(Nil_P(A))→K_1(A[s]). On (P,0) it is zero.
As every nil class is the class of its underlying projective with zero
endomorphism, τ vanishes identically.

**Higman linearization, with polynomial variable only.** If g(s)∈GL_m(A[s])
and g(0)=I, stabilize and reduce its degree. Write

g=I−s h(s)+C s^n, deg h≤n−2, n≥2.

Elementary block row and column additions transform diag(g,I) into

[[I−s h(s), C s^{n−1}], [−sI,I]] = I−s [[h(s),−C s^{n−2}],[I,0]].

Indeed first add C s^{n−1} in the upper right and then subtract s times the
second block column from the first block column. The degree decreases by one;
iterate until g has the K_1-class of I−sν with ν a constant matrix. This matrix
is polynomially invertible; if its inverse has degree q, comparison of the
coefficients of (I−sν)(ΣB_i s^i)=I gives B_i=ν^i and ν^{q+1}=0. Thus ν is
nilpotent, and the preceding paragraph gives [g]=0.

For arbitrary g, multiply by g(0)^{-1}; hence evaluation induces

K_1(A[s])≅K_1(A).

This proves the exact nil-vanishing input locally. It uses no Laurent version
of Higman's trick (whose polynomial-inverse argument does not apply to an
inverse that contains negative powers).

## 4. Low K_1 localization by explicit presentations

Fix A=R_r, B=A[t], C=A[t,t^{-1}], and let H be the exact category of finite
B-modules killed by a power of t and of projective dimension at most one over B.
Let T be all finite t-power-torsion B-modules. A module of T is a finite A-module
with nilpotent endomorphism t. The categories of Section 3 apply.

There is an equivalence H≅Nil_P(A). For a projective A-module P and nilpotent ν,
the characteristic sequence

0→P[t] --(t−ν)-->P[t]→(P,ν)→0

is exact: injectivity follows from its leading coefficient, and the quotient
map sends Σp_i t^i to Σν^i p_i; successive subtraction of (t−ν)-multiples
proves its kernel is exactly the image. Conversely let M∈H. Choose
0→P→Q→M→0 with P,Q finite B-projective and t^NM=0. Reduction modulo t^N and
the Tor sequence give

0→M→P/t^NP→Q/t^NQ→M→0.

The two middle terms are A-projective, since B/t^NB is A-free. Thus an
A-projective resolution becomes periodic after two stages. M has finite
A-projective dimension by Section 1; dimension shifting in this periodic
sequence forces Ext_A^1(M,N)=Ext_A^{2j+1}(M,N)=0 for all sufficiently large j
and all N, so M is A-projective. This proves the equivalence.

In particular K_0(H)≅K_0(A)=Z, with the isomorphism given by underlying
A-rank. Every M∈T has a finite H-resolution: choose a surjection
(B/t^N)^m→M, whose source has B-projective dimension one; the kernel remains
t-power torsion and has smaller projective dimension as long as pd M>1.
The finite bound from Section 1 gives termination. The Euler-resolution lemma
then also gives K_0(H)≅K_0(T).

For a square B-matrix α invertible over C, α is injective and coker α∈H.
If g=α/t^q∈GL_m(C), with q≥0 chosen to clear denominators, set

∂[g]=[coker α]−m[B/t^q B] in K_0(H).

The second term is qm[A] under the identification above. The formula is
independent of denominator: the exact cokernel sequence for products of
injective matrices gives
[coker(αβ)]=[coker α]+[coker β]. Applying it to β=t^kI proves invariance under
replacing (α,q) by (t^kα,q+k). The same product sequence proves that ∂ is a
group homomorphism on GL(C). Under adjoining an identity, use the presentation
diag(α,t^q) with denominator t^q; the added cokernel [B/t^q B]
cancels the added denominator term. Thus it is stable, so it
factors through the abelianization K_1(C). It kills GL(B).

The map n↦[diag(t^n,1,...)] splits ∂, because ∂[t]=[A]. If exactness at K_1(C)
is established, Section 3 immediately gives the required decomposition.

We prove exactness directly by building an inverse on the quotient
D=K_1(C)/im(K_1(B)). First every M∈H has a square free presentation. Choose
0→P→Q→M→0 with P,Q finite B-projective. Their ranks agree over the fraction
field, and K_0(B)=Z by Sections 1–2; hence [P]=[Q]. The group completion of the
monoid of projectives says P⊕T≅Q⊕T for some projective T (add the same terms on
both sides in a finite equality of split-sum relations). Add the identity
presentation of T, then the identity presentation of a common complement to
make both modules free. We obtain 0→B^m --α-->B^m→M→0, and α is invertible
over C.

**Independence of presentation.** Suppose α,β are two such square free
presentations with isomorphic cokernels. By padding them with identity blocks
we may suppose their free ranks equal, say F=B^m. Write M_α=coker α,
M_β=coker β and let γ:M_α→M_β be the given isomorphism. Set Q=F⊕F,
L=M_α⊕M_β and let π:Q→L be the direct sum quotient map. Define θ on L by
θ(u,v)=(γ^{-1}v,γu).

For any automorphism θ of any quotient π:Q→L with Q free, choose endomorphisms
f,g of Q lifting θ,θ^{-1}; lifts exist by freeness. The product of elementary
block matrices

Γ=E_12(f) E_21(−g) E_12(f) [[0,−I],[I,0]]

is a Q⊕Q automorphism. The final signed interchange matrix is itself
elementary: [[0,−I],[I,0]]=E_12(−I)E_21(I)E_12(−I). On L⊕L the product
induces diag(θ,θ^{-1}), because multiplying
the four displayed factors with f=θ and g=θ^{-1} gives that diagonal matrix.
Thus for e:Q⊕Q→L defined by e(q,q')=π(q), we have eΓ=θe.

Apply this construction to the swap θ above. Regard Q⊕Q as F^4. If π_1:F^4→M_α
is q_α on the first component and π_2:F^4→M_β is q_β on the second component,
the equation gives π_2Γ=γπ_1. Consequently Γ restricts to an isomorphism of
the kernels. The kernels have the explicit free identifications

D_α=diag(α,I,I,I):F^4→ker π_1,
D_β=diag(I,β,I,I):F^4→ker π_2.

The induced kernel isomorphism is an automorphism Γ_1 of F^4 and satisfies
ΓD_α=D_βΓ_1. After localization, taking K_1-classes gives
[α]−[β]=[Γ_1]−[Γ], which lies in im K_1(B). Thus the class a(M)=[α]∈D is
independent of every presentation choice and of the chosen isomorphism.

**Additivity.** For 0→M'→M→M''→0 in H choose square free presentations α'
and α'' of the endpoints. Lift the free surjection onto M'' through M.
Together with the free surjection onto M' this gives a free surjection onto M.
Its kernel fits into an exact sequence of the two endpoint free kernels; since
the right endpoint kernel is free, that sequence splits. In these bases the
presentation matrix of M has diagonal blocks α',α'' and an off-diagonal block.
After localization its K_1-class is [α']+[α''], since its off-diagonal block can
be removed by a single elementary block multiplication. Hence
a(M)=a(M')+a(M''). We obtain a well-defined homomorphism

a:K_0(H)→D.

For a square free presentation α of M, the boundary formula gives
∂a[M]=[M]. Conversely if g=α/t^q, its class in D is

[g]=a[coker α]−qm[t].

The presentation t:B→B has cokernel A with t acting as zero, so a[A]=[t].
The filtration of B/t^q B has q factors A, giving a[B/t^q B]=q[t]. Thus the
last display is exactly a(∂[g]). The maps a and ∂ are inverse isomorphisms
D≅K_0(H). This proves ker ∂=im K_1(B) without an unproved normal-form or
simultaneous-stabilization claim. The already constructed splitting by [t]
therefore gives

K_1(C)=im K_1(B) ⊕ Z[t].

Section 3 says K_1(B)≅K_1(A); its map into K_1(C) is injective, since the
composite K_1(A)→K_1(C)→K_1(A) induced by t=1 is the identity. We have proved

K_1(R_r[t,t^{-1}])≅K_1(R_r)⊕K_0(R_r),

with the last summand represented by the scalar unit t.

## 5. The exact vanishing conclusion

Integer Euclidean row operations reduce any unimodular integer matrix to a
diagonal matrix with entries ±1: reduce the first column by the Euclidean
algorithm to a primitive basis vector, eliminate the remaining first row by
column additions, and continue on the smaller unimodular block. A pair of −1
entries is elementary after stabilization (use the elementary factorization
of diag(u,u^{-1}) given in Section 4 with u=−1); determinant detects the
remaining sign. Thus K_1(Z)={±1}.

Induction using Section 4 now gives

K_1(R_r)=C_2⊕Z^r,

with generators represented by −1,x_1,...,x_r. They are independent: at each
induction the new exponent is detected by ∂ and the preceding summand by
x_r=1; the sign is detected at the initial integer stage. Section 2 already
proved K_0(R_r)=Z[R_r].

The group-ring identification Z[Z^r]≅R_r sends the chosen basis of Z^r to the
Laurent variables. The reduced projective class group is the quotient of K_0
by the free rank-one class and is zero. The Whitehead group is the quotient
of K_1(Z[Z^r]) by the classes of ±g for g∈Z^r. These units include precisely
the generators just exhibited, so the quotient is zero. Therefore

reduced K_0(Z[Z^r])=0 and Wh(Z^r)=0, for every finite r≥0.
