# Specific Laurent-ring proof under countable choice

Author: `/root/top_free_abelian_torsion`, 2026-10-20. This supplement refines the
choice assumption of the new local-algebra-proof.md only. The original full-AC
argument and its independent review remain preserved. The refinement below is
submitted for a separate focused review; no full-AC acceptance is silently
relabelled as countable-choice acceptance.

## Exact assertion and convention

In classical ZF plus countable choice (CC), for every finite r≥0, put
R_r=Z[x_1^{±1},...,x_r^{±1}]. Then the argument in the new local algebra draft,
with the replacement and restricted intermediate statements below, proves
K_0(R_r)=Z[R_r], K_1(R_r)=C_2⊕Z^r generated by -1 and the variables, and hence
reduced K_0(R_r)=Wh(Z^r)=0.

CC means that every countable family of nonempty sets has a choice function.
No dependent-choice principle or arbitrary-index choice is assumed. The result
is a theorem about these particular countable rings, not an assertion about
all rings or all modules under CC.

A countable module in this supplement comes with a surjection from N, allowing
repetitions and zero, or equivalently a specified finite/countable generating
list over an enumerated countable ring. Finite sets and finitely generated
modules are included by repeating zero. Polynomial and Laurent rings here have
canonical enumerations by finite integer coefficient/exponent codes. A quotient
module of a countably generated module inherits an enumeration. A submodule of
an enumerated module has an enumeration by selecting the least unused index
whose element lies in the submodule, repeating zero if that set becomes empty.
This definition uses classical excluded middle to distinguish the cases; it
uses no choice. Alternatively list zero at indices not representing members.
Thus kernels of maps between these countable modules are countable without
selecting a fresh enumeration by a choice axiom.

## Replacement for the arbitrary-module dimension argument

We use the following bounded statement, proved by induction on polynomial and
Laurent variables:

For the particular enumerated ring A, every countably generated A-module has
a finite resolution of length at most d by countably generated projective
A-modules, where d=1 plus the number of variables introduced. No statement about
uncountably generated modules is needed.

A countably generated free module is projective under CC. Indeed, to lift a
map from it through any surjection, choose a preimage for each of the countably
many basis images; CC applies to these nonempty fibers, which can be arbitrary
sets. A summand of a projective module is projective by composing its inclusion,
a lift, and the projection. Conversely a countably generated projective module
is a summand of a countably generated free module: take its countable free
surjection and lift its identity. These statements justify the unqualified
word projective for the countably generated projectives used below. Countable
choice is explicitly sufficient here; the fibers are not presumed countable.

**Base A=Z, d=1.** If K⊂⊕_{n∈N}Ze_n, set K_n=K∩⊕_{i<n}Ze_i. Projection to the
new coordinate identifies K_{n+1}/K_n with an ideal d_nZ of Z. If d_n>0 choose
the vector of least natural-number code in K_{n+1} whose new coordinate is d_n;
if d_n=0 add no vector. The code exists because the finite-rank free group has
a fixed enumeration. This gives

K_{n+1}=K_n ⊕ Zv_n when d_n>0, and K_{n+1}=K_n otherwise.

Every element lies in some finite initial span, so the v_n that were added
form a basis of K, a subset of an explicitly countable list. This construction
uses no arbitrary well-order or choice of witnesses. For a countably generated
Z-module take its prescribed countable free surjection; its kernel is such a
free K. This supplies the length-one projective resolution under CC.

**Polynomial step.** Let M be countably generated over A[t], with a specified
list m_i. As an A-module it is generated by t^j m_i, indexed by N×N. Hence the
induction hypothesis supplies a resolution of the underlying A-module of length
≤d by countably generated projectives. The exact sequence

0 → A[t]⊗_A M --δ--> A[t]⊗_A M --ε--> M →0,

δ(t^j⊗m)=t^{j+1}⊗m−t^j⊗tm, ε(t^j⊗m)=t^jm,

has the same leading-coefficient injectivity and reduction-to-constants
exactness proof as in Section 1 of the original draft. Induce the chosen
A-projective resolution of M to A[t]. Induction is exact because A[t] is
A-free; induced projectives are countably generated projectives, as witnessed
by their induced summand decompositions. Thus both copies of A[t]⊗_A M have
such resolutions of length ≤d. Lift δ to a chain map between them. At every
one of the finitely many degrees projectivity gives the needed lift; its
justification is precisely the countable-fiber choice argument above, or a
summand of it. The mapping cone has length ≤d+1, has terms that are finite
direct sums of countably generated projectives, and resolves M by the
homology exact sequence. This proves the bounded statement for A[t].

There is no countably infinite succession of unspecified choices in this
induction: for a fixed finite number of variables, one uses finitely many
resolutions and finitely many lifting constructions. The only possibly
infinite simultaneous selection within a lifting construction is indexed by
a countable free basis and is covered by CC.

**Laurent/localization step.** Suppose S is one of the specified countable
multiplicative sets (in particular powers of a variable). A module countably
generated over S^{-1}A is countably generated over A: if m_i generate it over
the localization, the list m_i/s, indexed by N×S, generates over A. Apply the
bounded A-resolution statement to this underlying module and localize the
resolution. Localization is exact, preserves summand decompositions, and sends
countably generated projectives to countably generated projectives. Also
S^{-1}M=M. This yields the same length bound for the localized ring.

Consequently all particular mixed polynomial/Laurent rings used in the proof
have the required bounded projective resolutions for countably generated
modules under CC.

## Passing to finite resolutions in the K-groups

The noetherian argument in the original draft is about finitely generated
ideals and finite coefficient modules. It is unchanged and requires no
arbitrary choice. These enumerated rings are noetherian. To resolve a finitely
generated module, take a finite free surjection and then finite free
surjections onto successive kernels, which are finitely generated by
noetherianity. After d kernels, the last kernel is projective.

For precision, the last assertion follows from the bounded countable resolution
statement without using any uncountable module hypothesis. Compare the finite
free partial resolution with a length-d countably generated projective
resolution of the same module. For two projective epimorphisms onto a module,
their pullback splits over both projectives, giving Schanuel's isomorphism
K⊕P'≅K'⊕P. Iterating this construction d times shows that the last kernel of
the finite free partial resolution becomes isomorphic, after adding projective
summands, to a direct sum of projectives from the bounded resolution. It is
therefore a summand of a projective and hence projective. All the compared
modules are countable. Only finitely many projective splittings occur, each
justified under CC as above. Since the last kernel is already finitely
generated, this is the required finite resolution by finitely generated
projectives.

The same comparison shows the dimension bound on kernels used in the Nil
resolution argument: for an epimorphism P→M with P countably generated
projective, if M has projective dimension e>0 in these bounded resolutions,
the kernel has a resolution of length at most e−1. One can either apply the
same pullback/Schanuel construction to a length-e resolution or split off the
first projective from that resolution. If e=0, the kernel is projective by
splitting. No projectivity test on an uncountable module is required beyond
the CC-based projectivity of countably free modules already established.

## Choice audit of the remaining proof

The following are restrictions of the original intermediate statements, not
blanket weakenings of their full-AC versions.

1. G_0 localization and polynomial invariance are needed only for countable
commutative noetherian rings A (and their quotients and localizations). Every
finite presentation and every cleared denominator is finite data. The total
quotient ring of a reduced countable noetherian A is countable, and its finitely
many field factors are countable. Prime avoidance and the selectors use only
finitely many choices.

2. The noetherian induction on quotients does not hide dependent choice.
Every ideal of a countable noetherian ring has a finite generating tuple and
therefore has a least such tuple in the fixed enumeration of finite ring
lists. Its collection of ideals is thus countably enumerable. If a nonempty
set of counterexample ideals lacked an inclusion-maximal member, repeatedly
choose the strict larger counterexample with least generating code. Ordinary
recursion on N gives a strictly increasing chain with no arbitrary choices.
Its union ideal is finitely generated, so that chain must stabilize, a
contradiction. Thus the exact maximal counterexample used in Section 2 exists
under these hypotheses.

3. The Euler-resolution lemma uses finite resolutions and finite pullback and
cone constructions. In its applications all module objects are countable.
Projective lifts are justified by CC as above. No well-ordering of an
uncountable set is used.

4. Nil_M(A) consists of finitely generated modules over a countable A, so each
underlying module is countable. The shift cover P^N→M in Section 3 is finite
free, and its iterated kernels are finite by noetherianity. The bounded
countable resolution statement supplies the finite termination bound just
proved. The Nil_P-to-Nil_M K_0 argument therefore survives unchanged.

5. The Laurent torsion module M in Section 4 is finite over A: it is finite
over A[t] and killed by t^N. The periodic exact sequence used to show it is
A-projective has countable middle terms and M has a finite A-projective
resolution by the preceding finite-resolution argument. To avoid any appeal
to Ext against arbitrary modules, let Ω²M be the two-step syzygy in that
periodic sequence; it is M itself. Choose an even number 2q at least the finite resolution length d. Repeat
the two-term period q times; its 2qth syzygy is M. Pad a length-d finite
projective resolution by zero terms to length 2q. The iterated Schanuel
construction compares these two resolutions and identifies M plus projective
summands with a projective module. Thus M is a summand of a projective
module and is projective. This
replaces the unrestricted Ext phrasing of the original paragraph. The same
conclusion also follows from Ext dimension shifting under CC, but that broader
phrasing is unnecessary here.

6. Square free presentation stabilization, the four-block lift, horseshoe
additivity, Higman matrix operations and the final induction on r involve
only finitely generated free/projective modules and finitely many matrices.
They use no additional choice principle. Choosing a complement to a finite
projective requires only its individual finite free surjection and a splitting.
Integer Euclidean reduction is finite.

All required countability statements are thus established inside this specific
proof. With these replacements the freeabelian vanishing theorem has a genuine
CC proof. This supplement does not assert that general polynomial invariance,
general global-dimension theorems, or arbitrary-ring projective lifting become
choice-free, and it does not address the extra F_2 interface.
