## Exact compact contract

Let M,N be metric topological four-manifolds, h:M→N a homeomorphism, K⊂M compact, U⊂M open containing K, D⊂M closed, and V an open neighborhood of D. If M has boundary assume a boundary collar is contained in V and the data below agree there; alternatively assume K⊂int M. For every continuous positive ε:N→(0,∞), there is a neighborhood P of h|U in the compact-open topology on proper open embeddings U→N, and a fixed compact L⊂U, such that every g∈P agreeing with h on U∩V admits an ambient isotopy h_t:M→N with h_0=h, h_1=g on a neighborhood of K, h_t=h on M∖U and on D, and d(h_t(x),h(x))<ε(h(x)) for all x,t. "Ambient" here means h_t h^{-1} is an isotopy of N by homeomorphisms, beginning at identity. The isotopy can be chosen continuously in g near h, and stationary when g=h.

For a finite compact application P contains a uniform δ-closeness condition on a fixed compact neighborhood of K; there is no assertion that a single constant δ suffices on an arbitrary noncompact K. Agreement just on D, with no neighborhood agreement or locally flat pair hypothesis, is not asserted.

The neighborhood conclusion is obtained by performing the construction for a compact neighborhood K^+⊂U of K instead of K. The shape/dimension of K itself is immaterial; in particular a compact two-complex is allowed.

## Elementary coordinate correction (the actual isotopy-creation step)

Write B^j=[−1,1]^j and T^n=(R/8Z)^n. For k+n=4, n≥1, consider a proper embedding

f:B^k×4B^n→B^k×R^n

sufficiently near inclusion, equal to identity on a neighborhood of ∂B^k×4B^n. We construct a compactly supported homeomorphism G of B^k×R^n, equal to f on B^k×B^n, continuously in f and equal to identity when f is identity. Its Alexander isotopy produces a deformation f_t from f to an embedding equal to identity on B^k×B^n, fixed on the outside boundary and the attaching collar. This creates an isotopy from closeness; it is not an application of isotopy extension.

**A. A torus completion without a punctured T^4 immersion.** Construct a shell embedding α:(−6,6)×T^{n−1}→int4B^n having α|2B^n=identity in the standard local parameter patch. It is an embedding, not an immersion of a punctured n-torus. For n=1 use an interval. Inductively place the preceding shell in the positive first-coordinate halfspace of R^n and rotate that halfspace about its codimension-two boundary axis in R^{n+1}. Positive radius ensures injectivity; distinct angles have distinct nonzero radial vectors, and the inverse is given by radius and angle. This appends one circle coordinate. Here are details of the normalization, so no annulus theorem is hidden in it. Begin with smooth circular rotations, and translate and linearly transform the target so the shell map has value0 and derivative identity at the central parameter point. On a sufficiently small parameter box it is C^1-close to identity. The map Ψ(x)=x+χ(x)(α(x)−x), with a bump χ equal to1 on a smaller box and zero outside twice the box, has derivative uniformly close to identity when the box is small. It is a global diffeomorphism: the perturbation has Lipschitz constant<1, giving injectivity, and properness plus invariance of domain gives surjectivity. Replace α by Ψ^{-1}α, now exactly identity on a small r-box. In the standard parameter patch (all circle coordinates in(−3,3), so no seam is encountered), a monotone radial coordinate change sends2B^n linearly onto that r-box and is identity outside3B^n. Precompose with this change and postcompose with the inverse linear rescaling of the target. The resulting shell is identity on2B^n. Finally a radial embedding of R^n into int4B^n, identity on2B^n, compresses the rest of the image. These operations use explicit smooth/Euclidean maps only. Multiply everything by B^k. Put

u=α^{-1}fα:B^k×[−4,5]×T^{n−1}→B^k×(−6,6)×T^{n−1}.

Compactness makes this defined and close to identity for all sufficiently close f.

Here is the complete cut/stretch/glue formula. Set a0=−11/3, b0=−10/3, a1=13/3, b1=14/3. Let w:R→R be increasing piecewise linear, w(s)=s+8 for s≤a0, w(s)=s for s≥b1, and affine from [a0,b1] onto [a1,b1]. On the cylinder let λ(s,z)=(s−8,z), ω=w×id, and W=λω^{-1}λ^{-1}. Thus W is translation by−8 below a0 and identity above b0. Put S=Wu(B^k×[−4,5]×T^{n−1}). On S define

R=(λu)ω(λu)^{-1} on S∩λu(B^k×[−4,5]×T^{n−1}), and R=id elsewhere.

The first formula is defined on this intersection when u is sufficiently close to identity: the lower boundary of S is λu(−4), and its upper boundary lies above the entire translated cylinder. The overlap has an upper collar on which ω=id (the parameter there is above b1); hence the two definitions glue. The lower part is taken monotonically, in the collar coordinates supplied by λu, to the strip above λu(4); the complementary upper part is fixed. Thus R is an embedding of S, with lower boundary λu(4) and upper boundary u(5). This also proves injectivity across the gluing region, rather than merely continuity there. Define

v=RWu|B^k×[−4,4]×T^{n−1}.

For the middle region [−2,2], W and R are identity, so v=u there. At the lower boundary W u(−4)=λu(−4), and ω(−4)=4; consequently v(−4)=λu(4). At the upper boundary v(4)=u(4). The image of v is the region between these two translated boundary copies. Identifying them by λ therefore gives a homeomorphism of B^k×T^n. Equivalently

F=(id×e×id) v (id×e×id)^{-1}, e:R→R/8Z.

This formula is independent of the representative at the seam by the endpoint equality. Interior points cannot be identified accidentally: they lie strictly between a boundary and its period translate. The quotient map is an injective local homeomorphism; its image is open and closed in each component of the compact target, and its degree is one because it is close to identity. Hence it is onto. The construction preserves every k-parameter value at which f is identity and therefore fixes the attaching collar. Every formula has a common fixed open collar where a piece changes, so F depends continuously on f and F=id for f=id.

**B. Lift, compress, and apply Alexander.** Lift F to the unique homeomorphism \widetilde F:B^k×R^n→B^k×R^n close to identity on one fundamental region. The lift exists by the local covering inverse since F is uniformly sufficiently close to identity. It commutes with the period translations, has bounded displacement, fixes the attaching collar, and agrees with f wherever both the point and its f-image lie in B^k×2B^n (in particular on a smaller inner box for f close to identity). Extend it by identity to R^k×R^n; this is continuous across ∂B^k because it fixes a neighborhood of that boundary. Let γ:int3B^4→R^4 be a radial expansion fixed on2B^4. Conjugate the extended lift by γ inside3B^4 and put identity outside. Restrict the resulting map to B^k×R^n, calling it G. Radial expansion leaves points outside B^k outside B^k; there the extended lift is identity, so the conjugate fixes the complement of B^k and the restriction is a homeomorphism. Bounded displacement of the lift makes the conjugate approach identity continuously at ∂3B^4, as does its inverse. Its support is contained in B^k×3B^n. For f near identity its image of B^k×B^n remains in B^k×2B^n; γ is identity on these points and their images, so G=f there. The construction is continuous in f, uniformly on its fixed compact support, and G=id for f=id.

Extend G by identity to R^4. Put A_0=id and A_s(x)=sG(x/s) for 0<s≤1. These are homeomorphisms; their inverses have the same formula with G^{-1}. The support lies in s(B^k×3B^n), so both maps tend uniformly to identity as s→0. Thus A_s is an ambient isotopy from identity to G, fixed on the attaching collar and the outside boundary. Now f_s=A_s^{-1}f starts at f and ends at identity on B^k×B^n. Choose f sufficiently close that f(∂(B^k×4B^n)) misses the support, which makes this deformation stationary on that outer boundary. Also the support lies in f(B^k×4B^n): on every interior point of the smaller box, the straight homotopy between f and inclusion misses that point on the outside boundary, so its local degree remains one. Thus that point has a preimage. The attaching-face boundary is fixed; the same conclusion there follows from the fixed collar. Consequently each deformation is postcomposition by a homeomorphism supported inside the current embedding image, and it leaves that image unchanged. If a neighborhood rather than equality on the inner box is needed, use inner2B^n and the smaller handle B^k×B^n. There is no n≥5 hypothesis anywhere. For n=0 the residual embedding of B^4 into B^4, fixed on its boundary collar, is a homeomorphism: its interior image is open and closed in int B^4 (compactness excludes an interior limit having only a boundary preimage), hence equals int B^4. Apply the Alexander formula directly to this homeomorphism. This handles the top-index handle and does not assume its interior was already fixed.

The quantitative feature follows from continuity at f=id, uniformly in s on a fixed compact region: for every η>0 one can choose a neighborhood of inclusion on which both the entire deformation and its inverses move points by less than η. No estimate obtained solely from a nearby boundary homeomorphism is substituted here.

## Relative finite assembly in coordinate charts

The remaining input is finite PL geometry inside individual Euclidean coordinate charts, not a triangulation of the topological four-manifold. Given compact C, closed D, open V⊃D on which f=id, first replace C by a compact neighborhood C^+ and work only on a compact neighborhood of C^+ in U. Cover its part outside V by finitely many coordinate charts contained in int M∩U. A boundary collar already fixed by f is excluded from these supports.

Here is the finite relative handle construction used in each chart. Choose a compact neighborhood E of C^+∩D inside V. Take a sufficiently fine standard Euclidean triangulation. Let K0 be generated by simplices meeting C^+∪E, and L0 by simplices meeting E. Use the closed derived neighborhoods in the second barycentric subdivision, K=N(K0), L=N(L0). Small mesh ensures C^+⊂int K, C^+∩D⊂int L⊂V, and (K∖L)∩D=∅. A derived block associated to a p-simplex is a p-handle: its tangential factor is the simplex with corners rounded or subdivided, and its normal factor is its dual block. Distinct handles of the same index have disjoint interiors after taking smaller collars; the attaching region of each p-handle is ∂B^p×B^{4−p}. Product collars in the subdivided Euclidean triangulation give a chart B^p×R^{4−p} for a thickening, disjoint from D and from all already treated handles except at the attaching collar. This is the only finite PL handle fact used. It concerns an explicit standard triangulation of R^4 and has no TOP four-dimensional triangulation or normal bundle premise.

Order handles by index. The embedding is already identity on a neighborhood of the attaching region. Reparameterize its normal factor so that ∂B^p×4B^{4−p} lies in that fixed neighborhood, then apply A–B in the thickening. Extend by the unchanged embedding outside the thickening, using the stationary outside boundary. This fixes a neighborhood of the new handle while preserving a neighborhood of D and every preceding handle. Compactness and finiteness allow neighborhoods to be shrunk after every step. When moving to the next coordinate chart include all previously completed compact cores in the protected closed set, with their already fixed open neighborhoods. This is precisely why the relative input requires neighborhood agreement. After finitely many charts and handles the resulting deformation φ(f,s) starts at f, ends at identity near K, is constant outside one fixed compact L⊂U, fixes D, and is stationary at f=id. Each local construction is continuous; their finite composition is continuous. Shrink the initial neighborhood of inclusion so every successive restriction lies in the domain of the next local construction and the whole deformation satisfies any prescribed positive displacement tolerance on L.

## Conversion into the required ambient correction

Apply the preceding construction in M to f=h^{-1}g. Write p_s=φ(f,s). It is important to convert the deformation in the correct order. Define on f(U)

J_s=p_s f^{-1},

and put J_s=id off f(U). Since p_s=f outside the fixed compact L, this glues continuously across the outside boundary; the inverse is f p_s^{-1} on p_s(U). The images p_s(U) and f(U) agree: their difference would have to lie inside a relatively compact chart region with fixed outside boundary, where injectivity and invariance of domain imply surjectivity onto the same region. Equivalently every local stage was composition with an ambient compactly supported homeomorphism, and so is their finite composition. Thus J_s is an ambient isotopy of M, J_0=id, and J_1 f=id near K. Consequently B_s=J_s^{-1} has B_1=f near K. Define h_s=h B_s.

Choose the initial neighborhood small enough that f(L)⊂U; then J_s and B_s have support in U. If x∈D∩U, f(x)=x and p_s(x)=x, so J_s(x)=B_s(x)=x; outside U they are also fixed. The relative assertion follows. Smallness follows by first prescribing a tolerance on the fixed compact set containing all supports, using uniform continuity of h there and the positive minimum of ε after a slight enlargement, and then shrinking the initial embedding neighborhood using continuity of the finite deformation and inversion. This proves the compact contract above, including near-K equality and the protected fixed region.

## Noncompact extension status

No noncompact assertion is needed for a finite compact two-complex. For a closed noncompact K, a single compact-open neighborhood is insufficient in general (EK printed77 gives a twisting-at-infinity example). EK Corollary6.2 and its following remark, printed78–79, use a fine/majorant tolerance and an alternating locally finite cover to extend the result. That distinct extension must retain a positive function δ and local finiteness; it is not supplied by writing a constant δ. The compact proof here does not certify a noncompact use.

## Choice and dependency ledger

All geometric choices above are finite once the compact core and finitely many charts are fixed. Sequential choices of finitely many tolerances need no countable or arbitrary choice. The contract can therefore be used under the existing CC standing regime without adding full AC. A noncompact locally finite assembly may require a countable cover/choices and should be ledgered separately. The alternative shell proof invokes neither full annulus4 nor a punctured T4 immersion nor isotopy extension nor FQ Chapter9. It uses finite Euclidean PL derived-block geometry, elementary circle covering maps, invariance of domain, and explicit radial/Alexander formulas.
