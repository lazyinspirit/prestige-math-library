# Relative local-to-global assembly on N×R

This is a conditional assembly proof, with two specific geometric/algebraic interfaces remaining external to this note: the KS codimension-two handle smoothing device in ambient dimension five, and relative reduction of an embedded rank-two microbundle to a vector bundle. The separate source reconstruction identifies how the device is built; naming it here is not a proof of its outstanding inputs. The purpose of this note is to supply the relative gluing argument, including the actual embedding germ, rather than to replace it by the phrase “the usual patching argument.”

Write B=N×R⊂Q=M×R. The given product vector tube exists on an open base neighborhood U_K of K×R. Choose nested smaller open product neighborhoods U_0,U_1 with K×R⊂U_0⊂closure(U_0)⊂U_1⊂closure(U_1)⊂U_K. All closures here are in B; the inclusions are obtained from nested collars on compact N and then taking product with R.

## Comparison of two existing tubes on an open overlap

Suppose ν and μ are already embedded normal vector bundles over an open set O⊂B. The base O has a smooth structure inherited from the chosen product smoothing of B. Their continuous vector bundles can be made smooth without changing their actual vector-bundle isomorphism classes, by the following explicit finite-dimensional construction. Subdivide the base triangulation subordinate to vector charts. The barycentric-star cover has four colors, indexed by simplex dimension, with disjoint stars of each color. With a locally finite partition φ_i subordinate to these charts, send a fiber vector to its four pairs of coordinates √φ_i·v_i, combining charts of the same color (at most one contributes). This embeds the bundle into the trivial R^8-bundle. Let P(x) be the orthogonal projection onto its image plane. Choose a countable coordinate cover on which P varies by less than ε=1/100, and a SMOOTH subordinate partition ω_i. The finite local sum Q(x)=Σω_i(x)P(x_i) is a smooth symmetric matrix with ||Q−P||<ε. Its two eigenvalues near one are separated from the others near zero, so its spectral projection P'(x) onto eigenvalues greater than 1/2 is smooth and has rank two. This projection may be written locally by the resolvent contour integral around the two high eigenvalues, proving smoothness. The restriction of P' to the old image plane is injective: its component in the low eigenspace has norm at most ε/(1−ε) times the vector's norm, since (Q−I) has norm ≤ε on that plane and I−Q is bounded below by 1−ε on the low eigenspace. It is therefore an actual fiberwise linear isomorphism onto the new smooth plane bundle. Transport this smooth structure back along that isomorphism. Thus their abstract total spaces are smooth manifolds and their zero sections are smooth; no ambient smoothing of Q is asserted. All covers and partitions here are countable and locally finite.

Restrict the radius of μ until its image lies in the image of ν over a neighborhood of the zero section. This is possible pointwise because both images are open neighborhoods of O, and a positive continuous radius is chosen by a locally finite refinement. The comparison h=ν^{-1}μ is then a pairwise open embedding into the abstract smooth pair (E_ν,O), fixing its zero section pointwise. Its restriction to that section is the identity and is therefore smooth. This is precisely the input type of the KS local smoothing device; the surrounding manifold Q is not being assumed smooth.

Take a locally finite handle presentation of O. Process all handles of index zero simultaneously, then index one, then index two, holding fixed previously smoothed attaching neighborhoods. For a fixed index, interiors of distinct handles are disjoint, and the supports avoid their attaching collars, so the choices are independent. Choose the countable family of device witnesses for that index with ACω after the lower indices have been fixed. Each individual device uses a single preselected countable E-table and finitely many S/F and extension witnesses; the union of these requests over the countably many handles is still countable. There are only three dependent index stages. Thus this is not an infinite sequence of fresh dependent selections. Normal-coordinate trivializations over the individual handles identify the local problem with the device's standard model. Each modification has compact support in its chart. Choose supports inside the domain where h is defined and inside ν's image, with size bounded by a positive continuous function tending to zero at the frontier of that domain. The resulting isotopy extends by the identity outside the comparison neighborhood. Local finiteness and fixed collars of previously secured compact subsets imply that both the isotopy and its inverse are continuous at every point, including the frontier; smallness there follows from the prescribed bound on the displacement of maps and inverses. The terminal comparison h' is smooth near the zero section.

For our open overlaps one can avoid top-index handles: an open subset O of N×R has no compact connected component, so its smooth locally finite handle presentation can be chosen with indices ≤2. One way to obtain this is to triangulate locally finitely and push each top-dimensional handle out along a locally finite forest in the dual graph directed to infinity, pairing it with the next codimension-one face. Every compact subset sees only finitely many changes. Thus in dimension three the device is needed only for k≤2, with n=3−k≥1. This elementary open-handle presentation fact is a specified topological input to this assembly; the formula containing T^{n−1} is never applied at n=0.

A smooth embedding fixing a zero section can now be straightened to a bundle isomorphism near that section. In local bundle coordinates write h'(x,v)=(a(x,v),b(x,v)). For s>0 define h'_s(x,v)=(a(x,sv),s^{-1}b(x,sv)); define h'_0(x,v)=(x,D_vb(x,0)v). The formula extends smoothly at s=0 by the integral Taylor formula for b, and h'_0 is a fiberwise linear isomorphism. Each s>0 map is an embedding by fiberwise dilation conjugation; compactness on each protected chart supplies a common smaller radius including s=0. A locally finite choice gives a neighborhood of the entire zero section on which this is an isotopy. Its velocity field on its open image, multiplied by a cutoff equal to one on a smaller neighborhood of the zero section and supported in the comparison domain, extends by zero. Integrating that field, in finitely many time intervals on each compact chart, gives an ambient isotopy realizing the straightening. Control the support by the same positive displacement function as above. Thus the two original tubes can be made to agree as normal bundle GERMS on O, after identifying their vector bundles by the resulting normal derivative. The base remains fixed pointwise throughout.

The needed assertion here is equality as germs, not equality on the entire original-radius fiber disk. Different radii are immaterial and may always be reduced. This avoids trying to align globally incompatible boundaries of arbitrary disk domains.

## Adding one flat chart

Assume an actual normal microbundle germ is already defined over an open set U containing U_1. For comparison only, convert it to an R²-bundle and reduce its structure group; this changes the provisional linear structure but leaves its embedded projection and normal microbundle germ unchanged. Take a relatively compact locally flat chart V⊂B−closure(U_0); it comes with a standard trivial vector tube. On O=U∩V both the old tube and this flat tube exist. Apply the comparison argument to the two tubes on O, modifying the NEW tube only. The old tube itself is never moved or redefined. The comparison isotopy is supported in the common open normal neighborhood of O, and controlled to extend by the identity at its frontier. Consequently the modified new tube agrees, as a normal bundle germ, with the old tube on all of O, and is still defined near the zero section on V.

Glue these two germs over U∪V. More explicitly, for each point of O equality of germs supplies an open total-space neighborhood on which the two zero sections and retractions agree; shrink the representative domains to their union of such neighborhoods before identifying. This is a microbundle atlas over U∪V: each chart is a bundle chart, the projections coincide on their overlaps, and the zero section is the original embedding into Q. The total space is an actual open neighborhood of that zero section in Q; it is not an abstract glued manifold with a merely homotopic map to Q. There are no Hausdorff quotient issues because the gluing is by equality of subspaces of Q.

Apply Kister conversion and vector reduction provisionally to this microbundle if necessary before the next comparison step. A conversion or a fiberwise reparametrization changes neither the embedded projection nor its microbundle germ. At intermediate stages only that microbundle germ is retained; no claim is made that every old provisional linear structure remains fixed on an entire nonclosed open set. The final conversion and reduction are relative to the closed protected collar and restore its originally prescribed vector structure literally. The vector-bundle identification on the new overlap is allowed to be nontrivial; no independent normal framing on V was part of the data.

## Iteration and preservation of the supplied germ

Fix a countable locally finite triangulation fine enough that each closed vertex star lies in one given flat chart, refining away from the protected collar. In its barycentric subdivision vertices have four colors, the dimensions of their original simplices. Write λ_v for its continuous barycentric coordinate. At most one coordinate of each color is positive at a point. The closed cores C_v={λ_v≥1/4} cover the base, because every point lies in a simplex with at most four vertices. Use the larger opens V_v={λ_v>1/8}; distinct V_v of one color have disjoint closures {λ_v≥1/8}. Each is relatively compact in a supplied flat chart, and the family is locally finite. For the relative construction use a triangulation of the complement collar region and include the original U_1 as the initial protected member; take only cores away from closure(U_0). The refinement may be chosen so these cores together with U_1 cover B.

Process the four colors, not a countable ordered list of charts. At the start of a color an old microbundle germ over U is fixed. Its overlaps U∩V_v for that color are mutually disjoint. Separate their ambient supports as follows: the closed sets closure(V_v)={λ_v≥1/8} of that color form a locally finite disjoint family; metric neighborhoods, chosen with radii below one third of distance to the union of the other closure(V_w) of the same color and decreased on compact exhaustion shells, give pairwise disjoint ambient neighborhoods at B. Reduce all tube domains into these neighborhoods. Near their frontiers use the displacement/inverse bounds of the comparison construction. Consequently the extensions for that entire color are independent. ACω chooses their countably many witnesses simultaneously, using the finite index schedule above on each overlap. Apply those isotopies simultaneously, then glue the new germs. The same-color disjoint supports and their local finiteness give an actual continuous map and inverse. Repeat for the next color. There are only four dependent color stages.

At every color the already supplied NORMAL MICROBUNDLE germ is retained; no provisional vector structure outside the protected collar is required to remain fixed. Each point has a neighborhood meeting finitely many chosen chart supports, so the final atlas on that neighborhood is obtained by finitely many actual gluings. Its retraction is continuous and its total space is an open neighborhood of B in Q. Assigned ambient neighborhoods ensure fibers over distant base points do not accumulate at that point. This proves that the union has a genuine representative, rather than merely pointwise germs. The construction never needs a fresh witness for the output of an infinite preceding sequence of chart choices.

Every new base chart avoids closure(U_0), and the original germ is kept at each step. The resulting microbundle agrees with the prescribed product vector germ on U_0, an open neighborhood of K×R. Final relative conversion and reduction can be fixed on an even smaller product neighborhood. Thus an actual prescribed boundary collar is retained; no collar is inferred from an abstract relative homotopy class.

## Domain and base-product controls

After the embedded vector bundle F→B has been obtained, choose positive continuous fiber radii 0<r(x)<R(x) inside its embedding domain. This follows by selecting local admissible radii, taking a locally finite subordinate partition of unity, and lowering the local constants before averaging. Compactness on each local chart ensures every smaller interpolated radius remains admissible. Reparametrize each entire fiber into the open radius-r(x) disk by r(x)ψ(|v|/r(x)), where ψ is strictly increasing, equals its argument near zero, and tends to one at infinity. In particular this reparametrization is the IDENTITY on a neighborhood of the zero section, so it preserves the actual prescribed germ, not merely its fiber projection. The original embedding on the radius-R(x) disk is the required radial extension of the new germ. On the prescribed product collar retain its original radial coordinate and choose the new radii within the prescribed domain.

The radii may additionally enforce |pr_R f(v,(x,t))−t|<1/10 throughout the chosen wider disk domain: this difference is zero on the zero section and is continuous, so the same locally finite positive-radius selection applies. The prescribed product germ has difference identically zero, so this restriction preserves it. After the identity-near-zero radial reparametrization, the entire new fiber lies in this controlled image. Consequently the eventual restriction f|_{1E×R} is proper: a compact ambient set bounds its R coordinate, hence bounds t by this estimate; the unit disk bundle 1E is compact over compact N, and the preimage is a closed subset of that compact disk bundle times a bounded closed interval. Thus f(1E×(−∞,a]) has no spurious finite accumulation coming from the negative base end. This control is available before the FQ neighborhood W is formed; no controlled cobordism theorem is used for it.

Here is an actual relative base-product isomorphism, avoiding an opaque invocation of bundle homotopy invariance. Give the abstract vector bundle F a smooth structure by the finite-dimensional projection construction above, now RELATIVE to its prescribed product structure on a smaller closed collar: choose local frames smooth there and squared smooth partition weights φ_i=η_i²/Ση_j², so the factors √φ_i in the R^8 embedding are smooth there and P is smooth on that collar. Approximate this continuous projection P by a smooth matrix Q fixed where P is already smooth. A smooth cutoff equal to one on that smaller collar combines P with the previous smooth approximation, preserving the same spectral gap. The resulting linear bundle isomorphism is the identity there. Construct a connection on this smooth bundle using a countable smooth partition of unity and the ordinary local matrix connections. On the protected product collar choose all local connections to have zero t-component in the supplied product coordinates; their affine partition average then has that same t-component there.

Let E=F|_{N×0}. Parallel transport this connection along the fixed path s↦(x,s), from s=0 to s=t. It gives an actual fiberwise linear isomorphism E_x→F_{(x,t)}, depending smoothly on (x,t), and it is the prescribed identity on the product collar. Existence along every finite t-interval is explicit: in a local frame solve U'=−A_tU by the iterated-integral series, whose kth term has norm at most M^k|t|^k/k! on a compact coordinate interval. The uniformly convergent series solves the equation; solving the inverse equation proves invertibility. Frame changes give the same transport by uniqueness. Finite subdivisions cover each compact path, and countable local data suffice over the σ-compact base. Thus F≅pr_N^*E relative to the supplied product germ, with no global choice of unrelated fiber isomorphisms. This changes only abstract bundle coordinates, not the embedded zero section. The resulting neighborhood embedding is the stable tube f:E×R→M×R needed by FQ9.3A.

## Honesty boundary

The assembly reduces the exact target to (a) the m=3 codimension-two handle smoothing device, (b) rank-two microbundle conversion/vector reduction, and (c) elementary smooth/paracompact bundle and open-handle constructions described above. The deeper torus operation, pairwise circle cancellation, and TOP(2)/O(2) contractibility must actually be supplied; this note is not a citation-based replacement for any of them. It does not establish the separate smoothing-realization theorem needed later for the five-dimensional cobordism W.

## Choice use

The construction is under ACω. First choose the countable cover, coordinate data and triangulation. The finitely colored cover above requires four dependent stages; each overlap device has three dependent handle-index stages. At any such stage select all independent witnesses simultaneously from one countable family. Their E-table entries can be indexed by (chart,handle,j,m,sign), a countable set, before the deterministic stretching recursion begins. S/F and finite ambient extensions require only finitely many further independent requests per handle. There is a fixed finite number of these selection phases; a finite iteration of ACω is available in ZF+ACω and does not imply or invoke DC. Cellwise vector reduction has four skeletal stages and Kister's explicit deformation uses finitely many dimension/color stages as specified in its note. All residual countable recursions (radius bounds, least-integer stretching and fixed formula compositions) are deterministic from the data already chosen. This scheduling retains the actual protected germ and local finiteness. Each external premise must itself have its declared ACω proof; this ledger does not weaken a stronger supplied choice contract.
