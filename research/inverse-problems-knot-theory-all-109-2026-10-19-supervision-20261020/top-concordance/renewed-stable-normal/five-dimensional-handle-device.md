# The compact five-dimensional handle device and its assembly from torus operations

This note supplies the initial insertion, the finite-cover descent and marking argument, and the final compression/isotopy. It uses the adopted S/F and relative circle-cancellation contracts stated below; its geometric inputs are now bound at their adopted interfaces: pair E for the initial BASE/core furl, the separately adopted ordinary normal-refurl bridge for the two NORMAL refurls, and S6-proper for the second split. The six-module focused final PASS and the later bridge PASS retain their separate review histories. Root owns canonical packaging and any original-target disposition. No broad smoothing, normal-bundle, or four-dimensional theorem is used as an unproved substitute.

## Needed compact interface

Let k+n=3 with n≥1. A pairwise open embedding h from a neighborhood of B^k×B^{n+2} in R^k×(R^{n+2},R^n) into a smooth pair (P^5,A^3) is given. Its restriction to the zero/core R^3 is smooth, and it is smooth in an ambient neighborhood of the attaching part (the outer first-factor collar). After enlarging coordinates slightly, assume it is defined on the source coordinates used below. Required conclusion: a pairwise isotopy, compactly supported in these coordinates, fixed on the core and the attaching collar, makes h smooth on an ambient neighborhood of the COMPACT core box B^k×B^n.

This compact-box conclusion is all relative handle gluing uses. It is also exactly the conclusion obtained in the last paragraph of KS §5 (printed318, image `ks-09.png`). A statement requiring smoothness near every point of the noncompact R^3 core while changing h only on a compact set would additionally need smoothness already near that core outside the compact set. We do not need or assert that stronger version. A locally finite iteration supplies the noncompact result when required.

Pull back the target smooth structure by h, calling the resulting structure Σ. Its core and its attaching collar have their prescribed standard smooth structures. The problem is to construct a compactly supported core-fixing homeomorphism G of the source which is smooth as a map from the standard structure to Σ near the compact core box. Then hG is smooth there.

## A concrete toroidal insertion

For each r≥0 there is a standard two-sided embedded hypersurface T^r⊂R^{r+1} with a flat coordinate patch of any prescribed size. Start with T^0 a point in R. Given a two-sided T^r in R^{r+1}, in R^{r+2} its old normal line plus the new coordinate gives a trivial normal 2-plane bundle. The boundary of a small disk tube is T^r×S¹=T^{r+1}, again two-sided. Smooth its product corners if any. Near one point a smooth ambient coordinate change flattens its patch; scale before flattening to obtain the desired protected coordinate box. These are finite standard smooth constructions.

Set Γ=T^{n−1}⊂R^n. Its normal line is trivial. Adding the two normal coordinates of R^{n+2} gives a trivial normal 3-plane tube Γ×R³ in R^{n+2}, after radially compressing each R³ into an admissible open disk fiber. In R³ choose a standard two-sided embedded 2-torus with a chosen point p and a flat patch. Its normal line at that patch is chosen to be the first coordinate axis, and its p-normal interval lies on that axis. Its tubular neighborhood is T²×R. Choose the tube coordinates to be the identity on a protected local 3-box. Inserting this tube into Γ×R³ produces

(T^{n+1},T^{n−1})×R=(Γ×T²,Γ×{p})×R → (R^{n+2},R^n).

The marked Γ×{p}×R maps into Γ's old normal line tube in R^n. By using the flat patch on Γ and the flat patch on T², and reordering coordinates, the insertion is the identity on the protected local (n+2)-box. Multiplying by B^k gives the insertion i used in the KS tower. Pulling Σ back along i gives Σ₀ on B^k×(T^{n+1},T^{n−1})×R. It agrees with Σ in that box and has the standard marked core and outer first-factor collar.

Here an embedding of marked pairs means that the specified source submanifold maps into the specified target submanifold. There may be additional unmarked intersections with R^n elsewhere in the toroidal neighborhood. They are irrelevant: only the chosen marked core is used for pulling back its smooth structure or for subsequent furling. Requiring the entire inverse image of R^n to be precisely the marked T^{n−1}×R would be an extra and generally false demand on this simple insertion (already a line meets a compact torus an even number of transverse times). The device needs no such demand; on the protected local box the pair identification is exact.

## Actual compact furl and its comparison

Let Q be a smooth manifold with underlying proper topological product X×R, X compact, with a smooth marked submanifold and the given product side collar. Let U_−,U_+ be disjoint signed end neighborhoods. The stretching module gives smooth diffeomorphisms φ_±:U_±→Q, identity on smaller corresponding tails and isotopic to the inclusions there. For the original-dimensional pair construction they preserve the marked core and are the specified same product compressions on the side. Define τ=φ_+⁻¹φ_−:U_−→U_+ and identify x with τ(x). This is an equivalence relation with classes of size at most two, since U_− and U_+ are disjoint. The quotient projection is open (the saturation of an open set is its union with its τ and τ⁻¹ images) and locally injective. Thus its local charts are the original smooth charts of Q, and the transition across a seam is the actual smooth τ.

Here is a compact Hausdorff model, so those charts are not only a formal quotient atlas. Choose a compact smooth separating cut S in the positive identity tail of φ_+, using a regular level of a proper smooth height uniformly approximating the topological height, product near the side. Its pullback φ_−⁻¹S lies in U_−. Keep the closed region C between φ_−⁻¹S and S; it contains the protected central box. In coordinates this means keeping the negative representatives with height(φ_−x) at least the cut level, all central representatives, and positive representatives below the cut. Properness of φ_± and identity on their corresponding tails imply that this region is compact. Identify its two boundary faces by φ_−, since φ_+=id near S. A finite collar on each face gives a compact smooth manifold by this gluing, including its side/corner product charts. Every quotient class has a representative in C, uniquely except for the paired boundary faces. Thus this compact Hausdorff model is exactly the displayed quotient. The same argument in TOP charts constructs a compact topological marked pair when the compressions are merely topological.

For uniqueness, first fix φ_− and change φ_+ to another positive compression φ_+*. Choose S in their COMMON positive identity tail. The same C and the same seam φ_− then give BOTH compact quotients; the induced comparison is the identity on C, and in particular on the protected central box. Reverse signs to compare two negative compressions with a fixed positive one. Whenever every negative domain is disjoint from every positive domain, compare the two pairs through the mixed pair (φ_−,φ_+*). This proves smooth uniqueness for such smooth data, and topological uniqueness for such TOP data. Do NOT obtain cross-disjointness by restricting an onto compression to a smaller domain: that restriction need not be onto. In the applications below the comparison pair is constructed with smaller product end domains inside the original domains, so cross-disjointness holds from the outset. If the data on the side are a common prescribed pair of product compressions, these comparisons are identity there: on that side the two quotient maps being compared have the identical one-dimensional relation. If their side coordinates initially differ by an orientation-preserving circle diffeomorphism, lift it to b:R→R with b(t+1)=b(t)+1 and isotope by (1−u)b(t)+ut; a side-collar cutoff of this isotopy makes the final comparison the prescribed identity circle coordinate. It does not alter the protected central region.

The underlying TOP marked-pair identification follows by this SAME comparison with explicit product compressions (x,t)↦(x,r_±(t)) of X×R, with their product end domains chosen inside the given U_± from the outset. The side versions can be chosen to equal the prescribed side compressions as described in the next paragraph. Their quotient is X×S¹, with standard marked core and side. The comparisons are identity on the protected central box. No theorem identifying arbitrary smooth structures with products is used to obtain this topological identification.

There are two covering markings. The preferred furl class is represented by the explicit infinite cyclic atlas with copies {j}×Q, gluing each negative seam in tile j to its positive representative in tile j+1. The compact-cut comparison above is applied in each tile and commutes with j↦j+1; it therefore preserves that class. For any other class γ∈H¹(Q;Z), take its principal Z-cover. The isotopies from the inclusions to φ_± lift starting at the identity and hence determine lifts on every end component, identity over the fixed tails. Apply exactly the same cut construction to these lifted seams. The comparison therefore also lifts to that auxiliary cover and preserves γ's compactly furled class. This deals with possible twists, rather than merely naming a generator. All these constructions commute with taking a product with a compact auxiliary BASE torus.

For clarity the required smaller PRODUCT domains also exist relative to the side. In any actual product F×R with F compact, uniform properness of its signed height puts the graphs r<c_−(x) and r>c_+(x) inside the prescribed end domains. Choose smooth graph functions far enough out in the interior and equal to the prescribed end-frontier constants on the side, using the actual side product collar to interpolate. They can also lie outside the chosen protected compact central region. A smooth fiber-preserving change of coordinate (x,r)↦(x,d_x(r)), equal to the identity on the side and on that central region, straightens both graph frontiers to constant levels. Construct d_x by flows of one-dimensional vector fields equal to translation on the compact interval of frontier values and supported in disjoint negative and positive bands; their times are the smooth differences of the graph functions from the constants. Pull the fixed standard product compressions back by this actual coordinate change. They are onto from the chosen smaller graph domains, equal the prescribed side compressions, and their compact furl is explicitly diffeomorphic to F×S¹ by that coordinate change followed by the fixed one-dimensional quotient. For the TOP product comparison choose continuous graph functions and piecewise-linear increasing coordinate changes instead. This supplies the cross-disjoint comparison data without restricting an onto map or presuming its restriction remains onto.

## Lift, radial compression, and a genuine compact isotopy

Lift g to the universal cover in all torus directions, choosing the lift which fixes the lifted marked core. Because g induces the identity on fundamental groups, the lift commutes with the translation lattice. Thus its displacement is bounded on one compact fundamental domain and, by periodicity, everywhere. The lift is the identity near the first-factor boundary, fixes the lifted core pointwise, and is a diffeomorphism from the standard structure to the lifted target structure. Near the protected core box that target structure is Σ.

Let D=B^k×R^{n+2}. Choose a radial homeomorphism J:R^5→int(5B^5) equal to the identity on 4B^5, with the protected compact box inside 4B^5. The restriction j=J|D embeds D into D. On j(D) define

G=j \widetilde g j^{-1},

and define G to be the identity outside j(D). At the finite boundary corresponding to ∂B^k, the definitions agree since the lift is already the identity near that face. At the compressed sphere at infinity, bounded displacement proves that G and the identity have the same limit: if x_j escapes every compact set then J(x_j+v_j)−J(x_j)→0 whenever v_j stays bounded. The same estimate applies to the inverse lift. Therefore G is a homeomorphism of D, extends by the identity to all of R^5, and has compact support. It fixes the core, since both j and the lift preserve it and the lift is pointwise the identity there.

Near a point of the protected compact core box, j and j^{-1} are the identity and the lift fixes that point. On a sufficiently small neighborhood of that point the lifted target smooth structure equals Σ and both chart identifications are unchanged. Hence G is smooth there as a map from the standard structure to Σ. Compactness gives a neighborhood of the whole core box with this property. It is not necessary that the lift carry the entire larger coordinate box into itself.

The Alexander formula

G_t(x)=tG(x/t) for t>0,   G_0(x)=x

is a compactly supported isotopy from id to G. Its support lies in the same fixed ball, its inverse is tG^{-1}(x/t), and boundedness of the compact support proves joint continuity at t=0. Since the core is linear and G fixes it, every G_t fixes it pointwise. Near the first-factor boundary the maps are the identity: radial scaling either stays in that fixed collar or leaves D, where G was extended by the identity. Restrict back to D and compose with h. The resulting h_t=hG_t fixes the core and attaching collar; h_1 is smooth near B^k×B^n. This proves the compact device once the two marked operations and the initial relative pair furling have been supplied.
