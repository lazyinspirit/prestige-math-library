## Exact interface

Let N be the compact smoothable surface S² or T², locally flat and closed in a topological 4-manifold M. Set B=N×R and Q=M×R. On a neighborhood U of a closed seam collar K⊂N an actual embedded rank-two normal vector-bundle germ is prescribed. Its product with R is prescribed on U×R. Required output: a rank-two vector bundle F→B and a zero-section-preserving open embedding of a neighborhood of its zero section into Q, agreeing with that prescribed germ over a smaller neighborhood of K×R. The embedding must admit a slightly wider disk domain; a mere stable abstract normal class or spherical fibration does not suffice.

A subsequent bundle isomorphism F≅pr_N^*E gives an embedding of E×R into Q after radius restriction. This is a rank-two bundle over a 3-dimensional base; E×R here denotes the product in the BASE direction, not addition of a rank-one normal summand. No normal bundle of the original embedding N⊂M is assumed.

## Local smoothing device to be proved

In a smooth pair (P^{m+2},A^m), write m=k+n. The local device starts with a pairwise open embedding

h:R^k×(R^{n+2},R^n)→(P,A)

which is smooth on the core and near the outer part of the k-handle. It must be changed by a compactly supported pairwise isotopy, fixed on the core and near that outer part, to be smooth near the entire handle. In our use m=3 and ambient dimension is 5. KS §4 reduces microbundle uniqueness to this device, then gets existence by local extension and gluing. The reduction does not start with a global rank-two bundle on B.

There is an additional useful special-case restriction: B=N×R admits a proper handle presentation with indices at most two (use a Morse function on N plus t², and the corresponding relative construction outside the prescribed product collars). Thus n=3−k≥1. The torus construction below then never needs a symbol T^{−1}. This observation does not itself prove the local smoothing device or the gluing step.

## Mechanism of the local device

Pull back the smooth structure of P by h. The resulting structure Σ on R^k×R^{n+2} agrees with the standard structure on the core and in the prescribed outer region. Work on B^k in the first variable. The following sequence is the actual geometric content of KS §§5–6; it is not a proof merely by invoking Theorem A.

* Put a toroidal open subset (T^{n+1},T^{n−1})×R into (R^{n+2},R^n), agreeing with the original local box. Pull back Σ. Furl the R direction by smooth engulfing, relative to the core and boundary, to obtain a smooth structure Σ₁ on B^k×(T^{n+2},T^n). The two complementary torus directions are the NORMAL directions.
* Unroll and refurl the first complementary circle, then the second, keeping the local box and the boundary unchanged. This gives Σ₂. The refurlings are essential: they arrange an ambient smooth manifold to which splitting/fibering can subsequently be applied. They do not provide a vector tube by themselves.
* In sufficiently large base dimension (m≥5), split the two unrolled complementary directions and use uniqueness of furling to obtain a diffeomorphism f:V^m×T²→(B^k×T^{n+2})_{Σ₂}, with the required boundary product behavior. The construction preserves the two distinguished cohomology classes in H¹ corresponding to these circles. Consequently f lifts to V×R²→(B^k×T^n×R²)_{\barΣ₂}. Forgetting this marking would allow an unwanted full twist and would invalidate the lift with the required boundary control.
* In that lift, choose a large standard normal tube around f(V×0) enclosing the core torus and a small smooth normal tube around the core. The intervening cobordism W is an h-cobordism; a proof is below. Its angular map is changed, preserving its homotopy class, so that its boundary maps are circle-bundle projections; smooth fibering over S¹ then makes the entire map a bundle. Each fiber is an h-cobordism between a copy of V and the core torus, of dimension at least six. Vanishing Whitehead torsion and smooth s-cobordism make that fiber a product. Sliding along the resulting products carries f(V×0) to the core by a diffeomorphism with compact support.
* Choose a finite cover in the two normal torus directions so large that this support lies inside one fundamental domain. Extend its translates equivariantly and descend to that cover. Composing with f yields a pair diffeomorphism g between the standard torus pair and Σ₃, fixed on the core and near the boundary. If k=0, the induced map on π₁ may still differ from the identity by an integral linear automorphism; precompose with its inverse. This automorphism fixes the core and the two normal quotient coordinates, so the correction preserves all required markings.
* For m=3, first apply the large-dimensional construction to Σ₁×T², increasing m to five. Then cancel these TWO ADDED BASE circles successively by a marked relative pair cobordism argument, in ambient cobordism dimensions seven and six. This is distinct from the two complementary NORMAL circles used earlier. The separately assigned pair-cobordism agent owns the exact complement and marking proof. Neither a four-dimensional smooth h-cobordism theorem nor an unproved generic pairwise cancellation theorem is admissible here.
* Lift g to the universal cover. Because g is homotopic to the identity, its lift has bounded displacement. Compress R^{m+2} radially into a ball, equal to the identity on the protected local box; conjugate the bounded lift and extend by the identity at the boundary sphere. The displacement tends to zero there, so this is continuous and has a similarly continuous inverse. Its Alexander isotopy has compact support and fixes the core. The transported smooth structure agrees with Σ near the local handle box; composing h with the terminal homeomorphism gives the desired smoothing near that handle.

The final bullet is an actual construction once the marked torus diffeomorphism exists. The preceding deep bullets are NOT justified merely by having named them.

## Filled exercise: the nested-tube cobordism is an h-cobordism

For readability take k=0; the same proof is relative to the product side boundary for k>0. Let X be the underlying topological product T^m×R² with the new smooth structure. Let T=T^m×0, and let f:V×R²→X be the smooth diffeomorphism obtained by splitting. Set N_V=f(V×D²_R) for a sufficiently large R so that T lies in its interior, and choose a small smooth tube N_T of T contained in int(N_V). Write W=N_V−int(N_T).

The inclusion N_V→X is a homotopy equivalence: under f it is the usual inclusion V×D²_R→V×R². The inclusion T→X is the standard deformation retract in the underlying topological coordinates. Thus T→N_V is a homotopy equivalence by the two-out-of-three property. In particular V≃T^m and V is aspherical.

Removing T, the region X−int(N_V) is the explicit infinite exterior collar of ∂N_V supplied by f, whereas N_T−T is the explicit punctured normal disk collar of ∂N_T. Radially retract each onto its finite boundary. These retractions agree with the identity on W, and give a deformation retraction X−T→W. Since X−T is topologically T^m×(R²−0), it is aspherical and its homotopy type is T^m×S¹.

The inner inclusion ∂N_T→X−T is a homotopy equivalence: restrict the ambient T^m projection and the normal angular class. Its base map is the identity on T up to the tube's zero-section contraction, and its normal meridian has degree one. The bundle ∂N_T→T is homotopically trivialized by the angular class, so its total space maps equivalently to T^m×S¹. One can alternatively establish this by the long exact homotopy sequence: the meridian maps isomorphically to the extra Z, and the quotient map to π₁(T) is an isomorphism; higher homotopy groups vanish.

For the outer boundary use ∂N_V=V×S¹. The map to X−T has an isomorphism on the quotient π₁(V)→π₁(T^m), by the first paragraph. Its fiber meridian has degree ±1 under the angular map, by the following relative intersection calculation. Orient V, T and the ambient normal disk consistently. Because T→N_V is a homotopy equivalence, [T] maps to ±[V×0] in H_m(N_V). A transverse perturbation, fixed at its boundary, of the disk D=f({v}×D²_R) therefore has oriented intersection D·T=±1, since its pairing with [V×0] is one. Removing small disks around its finitely many transverse intersections produces a punctured disk in X−T whose boundary relation is [∂D]=Σ ε_i[local meridian]; thus the outer boundary circle has angular degree Σ ε_i=±1. Choose its angular orientation to give +1. For k>0 use [T,∂T] and H_m(N_V,side N_V); the pair equivalence and the disk based at an interior v give the identical relative pairing, while all perturbations avoid the prescribed product side collar. Therefore its map on π₁ is a triangular isomorphism with diagonal blocks an integral isomorphism and 1. Both spaces are aspherical, so this is a homotopy equivalence. Composing with X−T→W proves that both boundary inclusions are homotopy equivalences, as required.

The assertion about the inner angular class uses that the ambient embedding T⊂X is TOP-standard and a local meridian links it once; it does not assume that an arbitrary homotopy equivalence of ambient manifolds preserves complements. The global complement product is used explicitly. Vector reduction of the small smooth tube is available because it is already a SMOOTH tube; no TOP vector-normal theorem is being smuggled into this exercise.
