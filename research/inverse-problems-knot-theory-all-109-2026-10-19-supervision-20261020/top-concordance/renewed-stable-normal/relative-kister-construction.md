# Concrete relative Kister conversion after a normal microbundle is available

This is a reconstruction of the actual construction in J. M. Kister, *Microbundles are Fibre Bundles*, Ann. Math. 80 (1964), 190–199. The entire primary text was visually read. This note is not an acceptance record and supplies no existence proof for the underlying normal microbundle. Its rank-two specialization has no dimension-four topology in it.

Let Emb₀(R^q,R^q) carry the compact-open topology and let Homeo₀ be its onto subspace. We first construct a continuous deformation D(g,t) through embeddings, starting at g and ending in Homeo₀, with the extra property that every D(g,t) is onto whenever g is onto. The deformation need NOT fix Homeo₀ pointwise; this distinction matters in relative gluing.

For an origin-containing embedded compact ball g(D_r), write ρ(g(D_r)) for the largest s such that D_s⊂g(D_r). These positive radii are continuous in g and r. Indeed, uniform closeness of embeddings on D_{r+ε} keeps the smaller image ball inside the perturbed image; their inverse maps on that smaller compact ball are uniformly close. The lower bound follows from the positive distance between g(D_r) and g(∂D_{r+ε}); the inverse estimate follows from the positive distance between images of pairs of source points separated by a fixed ε. Apply this twice to get the two-sided estimate for the inradius.

Here is the elementary move. Suppose h(R^q)⊂g(R^q) and h(D_b)⊂g(int D_c), with 0≤a<b and 0<c<d. There is a continuously chosen ambient isotopy Φ_t, supported in g(int D_d)−h(D_a), with Φ_1(h(D_b))⊃g(D_c). To construct it, let a' and b' be the inradii of g^{-1}h(D_a) and g^{-1}h(D_b), and let a'' be the inradius of h^{-1}g(D_{a'}). Shrink h(D_a) onto h(D_{a''}), by conjugating a piecewise linear radial reparametrization through h, supported inside h(D_b); call the resulting ambient homeomorphism σ. In g-coordinates, radially move radius b' to c while fixing D_{a'} and the complement of D_d, through an isotopy Ψ_t. Then set Φ_t=σ^{-1}Ψ_tσ. The support and fixed-set conditions follow by direct conjugation; the containment follows because h(D_b) contains g(D_{b'}) before the expansion. The same formula works when a=0. Every choice is determined by the inradius functions, so the construction depends continuously on its inputs. Conjugated maps extend by the identity at their outer sphere because each radial reparametrization is already the identity there.

For g, put r_i=ρ(g(D_i)). Define a radial embedding h_g which sends each interval [i,i+1] linearly onto [r_i,r_{i+1}], and [0,1] onto [0,r_1]. The radii strictly increase and the radial map has image contained in g(R^q). On successive time intervals accumulating at t=1, apply the elementary move to produce embeddings α_t with α_0=h_g, α_1(R^q)=g(R^q), and with the restriction on D_i stationary after the ith stage. At stage i, protect D_{i−1}, expand the current image of D_i to contain g(D_i), and work inside g(D_{i+1}). The protected-ball condition makes every compact subset stationary after finitely many stages. Therefore the endpoint exists, is injective, and the map into the embedding space is continuous even at t=1. Surjectivity onto g(R^q) follows because every g(D_i) is eventually covered.

Independently interpolate the radial embedding h_g to id through radial embeddings β_t, correcting the first ball, then the second, and so on, again keeping every previously corrected D_i fixed. The same compact stabilization proves endpoint continuity. If g is onto then r_i→∞, and every α_t and β_t is onto. Now define

D(g,t)=α_{1−2t} α_1^{-1} g for 0≤t≤1/2,

D(g,t)=β_{2t−1} α_1^{-1} g for 1/2≤t≤1.

The formulas agree at 1/2 since α_0=β_0=h_g. At t=0 the result is g; at t=1 the result is α_1^{-1}g, an onto homeomorphism. Composition is well-defined because α_1 and g have the same image. Uniform convergence of inverses on interior compact subsets follows from the image-separation estimates above. This proves the required parameter continuity rather than selecting an unrelated isotopy separately for each g.

Now let ξ be a q-microbundle over a locally finite triangulated base X. The inclusion of the zero section and the projection are fixed. An admissible Euclidean bundle means an OPEN neighborhood of the zero section on which this very projection is an R^q-bundle, not an unrelated abstract bundle stably isomorphic to ξ.

Existence proceeds by induction over skeleta, carrying a bundle already supplied near a protected subcomplex L as initial data. Before beginning, refine the locally finite triangulation until every closed simplex lies inside one ORIGINAL microbundle chart. This is possible by a subdivision subordinate to the locally finite open chart cover. On each top-dimensional simplex σ take the restriction of that chart as its microbundle trivialization. Thus this specialization does not need a separate homotopy theorem asserting triviality of an arbitrary microbundle over a contractible base. Choose an open zero-section neighborhood D whose restriction over each d-simplex lies in that simplex's chosen original chart; local finiteness allows this simultaneous restriction. Apply the ALREADY PROVED existence statement in dimension d−1 to the old-skeleton microbundle restricted to D, relative to the protected initial bundle, obtaining the old-skeleton bundle η inside D. Thus its fibers over each ∂σ lie inside that chart. This is the simultaneous induction X_{d−1},U_{d−1}⇒X_d, rather than an unjustified claim that an arbitrary nonlinear bundle can simply be radially shrunk globally. Before writing a global parameterized embedding family on ∂σ, use U_(d−1), already proved on that boundary, to identify η there with the chart's trivial admissible bundle. This is an actual bundle isomorphism because both are admissible for the same restricted microbundle. Expressing its fiber maps in the chart gives the required continuously varying embeddings g_p:R^q→R^q; a mere fiberwise inclusion would not have supplied a global trivialization. Use the collar ∂σ×[0,1] around a smaller concentric simplex, applying D(g_p,t) along the collar. At t=0 this is exactly the preexisting fiber. At t=1 each image is all of R^q, so it matches the standard local bundle over the smaller simplex. The image union is open by the PARAMETERIZED image-separation estimate used above, not by incorrectly treating a skeleton as a manifold. For a point y in one fiber image choose a compact source ball around its inverse with image boundary separated from y by a positive distance. Nearby embedding parameters still contain a smaller fixed target neighborhood of y, by the same image-containment estimate; their inverses there vary continuously. Hence a continuous fiberwise embedding family gives an open total-space image and a continuous inverse onto that image for an arbitrary base parameter space. For completeness the actual seam trivializations are as follows. Write the collar family in the chosen local chart as F(p,t,q)=D(g_p,t)(q). At its inner edge t=1 it is onto. Reparametrize every fiber along that collar by the homeomorphism e_p=F(p,1,·)^{-1}; then F(p,t,e_p(q)) equals q at the inner edge, so it glues to the INNER simplex's original trivialization. At the outer edge it differs from the old bundle trivialization only by the homeomorphism e_p, which is allowed. Near any point of the outer attaching face, start instead with a chosen old local trivialization on a skeleton neighborhood and use its comparison homeomorphism with F(p,0,·), constant in the collar parameter. This gives a trivialization agreeing with that old one across the outer seam. These local formulas and the parameterized inverse estimate prove local triviality at both seams, including skeleton points shared by several simplices; openness alone would not have been a sufficient assertion of local triviality. Local finiteness allows all simplices in each dimension to be treated simultaneously without accumulating chart changes near any point.

For the relative statement choose a subcomplex L whose interior contains the closed prescribed collar and which is contained in the open region where its bundle was supplied. In our application the closed set is K×R with an actual product collar, so a product triangulation refined inside that collar provides such an L. Carry its admissible bundle unchanged and attach only simplices outside L. The collar formula fixes every existing fiber at its t=0 seam. Thus the resulting admissible bundle literally agrees with the given bundle on a smaller neighborhood of K×R. No assertion that D fixes all homeomorphisms pointwise is needed.

One also needs the following uniqueness to justify the skeletal induction when the old bundle is locally compared to a newly chosen trivial bundle. After existence X_d has been proved, apply it to the common microbundle neighborhood of two given admissible bundles, obtaining a third admissible bundle contained in both. For the relative statement keep their common prescribed germ by beginning with it as the protected data. It is therefore enough to prove uniqueness in the case of an actual fiberwise inclusion of one admissible bundle into the other; applying that inclusion case twice compares the original two. This reduction uses X_d before U_d and is not circular. Choose once a countable locally finite cover subordinate to charts of BOTH admissible bundles, and refine its base triangulation to that cover. In the barycentric subdivision color vertices by simplex dimension, using d+1 colors. If λ_v is a barycentric coordinate, the cores C_v={λ_v≥1/(d+1)} cover the base and the larger supports V_v={λ_v>1/(2d+2)} have disjoint closures within a color. Fix source and target bundle charts over V_v and a bump u_v, zero near its frontier and one on C_v; all these countable independent choices are made once by ACω. In each color express the CURRENT inclusion in those fixed source/target coordinates as g_p and REPLACE it by D(g_p,u_v(p)), leaving it unchanged off V_v. This is a replacement of a map η→ζ, not composition of maps with different bundle domains. Supports in that color are disjoint, so the replacements are simultaneous. On C_v the map becomes onto; a later replacement preserves onto fibers because D preserves that subspace. After d+1 colors every fiber is onto. The parameterized inverse estimate makes the resulting fiber inverse continuous. The fixed coordinate data and explicit D formula make these operations deterministic; no new witnesses are chosen after an infinite preceding list. All replacements fix the zero section. The same construction never changes a protected region by choosing every bump supported outside it. This proves relative uniqueness and closes the simultaneous induction: existence in dimension d uses uniqueness in d−1; existence in dimension d then gives uniqueness in dimension d.

For B=N×R all complexes are locally finite and finite-dimensional, so no transfinite local-convergence issue occurs beyond the locally finite star argument. The output is an embedded R²-bundle in the already existing normal microbundle neighborhood. Turning its structure group Homeo(R²,0) into O(2) remains a different theorem; this construction does not prove vector reducibility.

The elementary prerequisites used above are explicit: invariance of domain and a locally finite triangulation/subdivision of the already SMOOTH 3-dimensional base subordinate to its microbundle-chart cover. Subordination removes any need for a separate contractible-base microbundle triviality theorem in this specialization. This note reconstructs Kister's special conversion argument; it does not claim a new independent proof or repository acceptance of invariance of domain or smooth triangulation.
