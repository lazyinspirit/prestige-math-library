# The free-abelian end construction needed for the two splittings

This is a geometric reconstruction of the actual end argument, rather than a citation to the splitting theorem. Its algebraic inputs are the separately owned vanishing of reduced K₀(Z[Z^r]) and Wh(Z^r). Its differential-topology background consists of Morse functions and their handle models, smooth tubular neighborhoods, and the relative smooth s-cobordism product theorem; the latter is used only after an actual compact h-cobordism has been produced. No original supplier was checked or edited. This note supplies no native acceptance or certification.

The primary proof source is Siebenmann's 1965 thesis, §§2–5, retyped pp.12–37, with relative §10 pp.81–82, all read from the acquired full text `sources/siebenmann-thesis-retyped.pdf`. The retype is hosted at https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/surgery/thesis.pdf and linked as a retype of the original thesis on the host's `surgery/notes.htm`. Wall's 1964 *Differential Topology*, Part IV, printed pp.13–24, actually read, supplies the explicit finite handle slides and the important borderline complement calculation; full scan and text are `sources/wall-handle-decompositions.*`, from https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/surgery/wall.pdf. Milnor's *Lectures on the h-cobordism theorem*, §6, printed pp.71–84/PDF76–89, was read for the Whitney construction and its framing argument, with visually checked formulas at PDF76,79,88,89. Full scan/text are `sources/milnor-h-cobordism.*`, from the host's `surgery/hcobord.pdf`. These reading claims do not cover the rest of Wall or Milnor.

## Exact splitting input

Let Q^q, q=6 or 7, be smooth and properly homeomorphic, by a specified map, to L×R, where L is a compact connected manifold with finite CW type and π₁(L)=Π=Z^r. There may be side boundary, and on a neighborhood of it a smooth product with R is already prescribed. This side product is part of the same specified end marking: its positive and negative tails approach respectively the positive and negative topological product ends, uniformly over its compact cross section. Its height is uniformly proper there. Equivalently, after discarding compact collars, its level slabs escape every compact subset of Q in the same signed direction as the specified L×R height. Opposite end assignments on separate boundary components are excluded; such incompatible products could not extend even continuously to a proper height. The construction below produces a compact smooth (q−1)-manifold F and a diffeomorphism F×R→Q extending that side product, provided reduced K₀(Z[Π])=0 and Wh(Π)=0.

For the first KS split, q=7 and L=B^k×T^(n+1). For the second, q=6 and L=B^k×T^n, after treating the already refurled circle as part of the compact factor. The proof does not presume a smooth copy of either topological cross section L×{t}; finding a smooth cross section is its purpose. In BOTH actual KS applications the required side compatibility is already present, rather than an additional restriction on their output: Σ₁ is standard near ∂B^k×T^(n+2), and the two preliminary refurlings agree with the standard coordinate refurlings on that collar. Unrolling the first distinguished normal circle there gives exactly the canonical signed real coordinate t of that normal circle. Its compact remaining factors give uniform properness on the entire side, including both components when k=1. After the first split and refurl, the boundary diffeomorphism is a product with id on the circle just refurled; unrolling the SECOND distinguished normal circle again gives its own canonical signed real coordinate on every side component. Thus neither split inherits an arbitrary reversed side height. For k=0 there is no side boundary. These are precisely the existing standard-boundary and product-id boundary clauses of the marked refurl input and comparison, not an inferred choice of new boundary data. The normal cohomology classes are retained by the subsequent marked furl comparison in `operation-proof-and-obligations.md`.

## The finite handle operations, including the dimension-six endpoint

A handle of index i is D^i×D^(q−i), attached along ∂D^i×D^(q−i). Its core is the first disk and its belt sphere is {0}×∂D^(q−i). Two successive handles cancel when the attaching sphere of the second meets the belt sphere of the first transversely once, with the matching tubular framing. To see the local cancellation geometrically, take a tubular neighborhood of the unique connecting gradient trajectory. The Morse function there is deformed to the one-variable birth/death model u³/3−εu, plus a nondegenerate quadratic form in the transverse coordinates. Replacing ε>0 by ε<0 removes its two critical points. Outside this neighborhood the function and gradient are fixed. Conversely ε<0→ε>0 inserts an auxiliary pair of successive indices. Thus these operations change no manifold or prescribed side germ.

For handles of the same index, choose a thin embedded band between two attaching spheres. Route the band along a chosen based loop g∈Π, and use its tubular normal coordinates to push a disk of the second attaching sphere across the first handle core and back into the preceding boundary. This is an isotopy in the boundary after the first handle is attached. Its effect on the based lifted core classes is e_j↦e_j±g e_i. Reversing the chosen band orientation changes the sign. Repeating bands realizes a coefficient ∑n_g g∈Z[Π] and hence every elementary basis operation. Finitely many bands and isotopies are involved. The ambient isotopy is extended through a collar by multiplying its generating field by a collar cutoff. This is the actual geometric content of a handle slide, not an assertion that an arbitrary matrix is automatically geometric.

When algebraic intersections of an attaching sphere and a belt sphere cancel, they occur in pairs with opposite signs and the same based Π-label. Join such a pair by arcs in the two spheres. Equality of labels says precisely that the resulting Whitney loop is nullhomotopic in the level manifold. A small annular ribbon pushes this loop off both spheres. A spanning disk can then be chosen embedded, relative to its ribbon, since the level has dimension at least five. If both spheres have dimension at least three, general position puts its interior off both. When one sphere has dimension two, the missing condition is injectivity of π₁(level−other sphere)→π₁(level); it must be verified, not assumed.

Here that condition has a concrete handle-theoretic proof. For an index-two lower handle, gradient trajectories identify the complement of its belt sphere in the level with the preceding boundary minus its attaching S¹. Removing that S¹, of codimension q−2≥4, does not change π₁. The preceding boundary and the relevant handle cobordism have the same π₁ in our applications. Thus the required complement map is injective. For a top index-(q−2) handle, reverse the Morse function and use the same argument from the other boundary. This is the reason q=6 is permitted; merely saying “Whitney trick in dimension at least five” would omit its essential π₁ condition.

There is also no hidden framing assertion in this move. Over the Whitney disk, first choose the (a−1)-frame agreeing with the tangent directions of the a-dimensional moving sphere along one arc and normal directions of the other sphere along the other arc. Opposite intersection signs make these frames compatible at the ends. The extension obstruction is a loop in the Stiefel manifold V_(a−1)(R^(a+b−2)), where the other sphere has dimension b≥3. This manifold is simply connected: successively forgetting frame vectors has sphere fibers of dimension at least b−1≥2. Hence the frame extends over the disk. Complete it to a normal frame and exponentiate. This gives a neighborhood D²×R^(a−1)×R^(b−1) in which the two spheres are the two boundary arcs times their separate coordinate planes. A planar isotopy across D² removes the two intersections. Multiply its time parameter by a smooth radial cutoff in the transverse coordinates and conjugate through the exponential chart. This is the supported smooth ambient Whitney motion. It fixes every other intersection and every prescribed boundary collar.

We will also use the following elementary handle simplification procedure. If a compact cobordism has both boundary inclusions inducing π₁-isomorphisms, its 0- and q-handles are eliminated using spanning trees of the 1-handles and dual 1-handles. A remaining index-one core represents a zero relative π₁-class. Insert a 2/3 auxiliary pair representing a nullhomotopy of that core and cancel the original 1-handle against the new 2-handle; this trades it for an index-three handle. Repeat from the other boundary for index q−1. This leaves only indices 2,…,q−2. If relative homology vanishes through degree d and π₁ is unchanged, the same procedure eliminates indices through d: solve the boundary equation for each lowest core, introduce an auxiliary pair one index higher, perform the elementary slides expressing the solution, use the just-proved Whitney motion to make the algebraically complementary pair geometrically complementary, then cancel it. Each step removes one lowest handle. In the range used below the eliminated indices are at most q−4, so the required Whitney dimensions are covered. This terminates because the handle decomposition is finite.

Here is the precise prescribed-class consequence, also used by the angular packet. Let (C;M,N) be a compact smooth q-dimensional cobordism, q=6 or 7, with both frontier maps inducing the same Π-isomorphism. Let 2≤d≤q−3, suppose M→C is (d−2)-connected, and specify x∈H_d(C̃,M̃), a base path and orientation, and a fixed product collar on the side boundary. Then x is represented by an ACTUAL framed properly embedded d-disk D⊂C, with ∂D⊂M, meeting M transversely and missing the fixed side collar. To construct it, simplify the finite handles to indices max{2,d−1},…,q−2, choose a finite core cycle c representing x, insert a d/(d+1) auxiliary pair and slide its new d-core e₀ to e₀+c. This is a finite sequence of elementary additions, so the modified core represents exactly x, with the specified based lift and orientation. Its boundary coefficient against every (d−1)-belt sphere is zero. Clear the paired intersections by the framed Whitney motion proved above, then follow the gradient to M to add the boundary collar to that core. This yields the required disk and its thickened handle framing. For d=2 there are no lower handles. In q=7 this supplies exactly dimensions 2,3,4; no arbitrary embedded five-disk is inferred from general position. The top-class argument of this packet uses d=q−2 ONLY after its special adapted free-basis construction, with the extra opposite-frontier control stated there; it is not a general disk-trading interface. Deleting/trading a disk in the displayed range has the exact relative-cell effect: lower relative homology below d is unchanged, degree d is quotiented by Rx, and possible new homology is confined to degree d+1. The dual index q−d≥3 and the trivial attaching loop when d=2 preserve all π₁ isomorphisms. These are the exact class, framing, lower-connectivity, fixed-collar and group conclusions required by the angular construction.


## Smooth end neighborhoods with controlled fundamental group

Fix either of the two ends, ε. The specified homeomorphism L×R→Q gives a cofinal sequence of product end neighborhoods. Their π₁ maps are all Π-isomorphisms, so the end group is stable and is the explicitly finitely presented group Π. Each such neighborhood has finite CW type. All subsequent constructions take place beyond an arbitrarily prescribed compact set.

Start with a regular level of a proper smooth function which equals the prescribed R-coordinate near the side boundary. Discard the bounded components on the end side. Connect the finitely many frontier components by disjoint arcs in the end region and delete small open tubular neighborhoods of the arcs. Round corners. The resulting closed end neighborhood V has compact connected frontier M, and agrees with the prescribed product along the side boundary.

Here is the finite π₁ correction. Choose nested such neighborhoods V₁⊃V₂⊃V₃⊃V₄ so that the images of their π₁ maps identify with Π. Represent generators of Π by finitely many based arcs attached to the frontier of V₄, splitting any arc at its finitely many transverse frontier crossings. Trade the small 1-handles around these arcs across the frontier, adding a tube on the outside or deleting it on the inside. The resulting frontier group maps onto Π inside V₂. It is a finitely presented group because its frontier is compact.

Its kernel is normally generated by finitely many loops. Explicitly, write G=⟨x₁,…,x_p:r₁,…,r_u⟩ and Π=⟨y₁,…,y_d:s₁,…,s_v⟩. Choose words ξ_i(y) with θ(x_i)=ξ_i(y), and words η_j(x) with θ(η_j(x))=y_j. The kernel is the normal closure of x_i ξ_i(η₁(x),…,η_d(x))⁻¹, 1≤i≤p, and s_l(η₁(x),…,η_d(x)), 1≤l≤v. Adjoin the typed relations y_j=η_j(x) and x_i=ξ_i(y) and then eliminate the y_j to verify that quotient presentation directly. Represent one such loop on the frontier by an embedded circle and span it by an embedded disk in a slightly larger end neighborhood. This is possible in q≥6 by relative general position. Its interior meets the frontier in finitely many circles. Trade disks bounded by innermost circles first, until the original disk has only its boundary on the frontier, and then trade that disk. A 2-handle trade kills the corresponding kernel element. Its dual index is q−2≥4, so it does not introduce a new π₁ generator on the other side. After finitely many trades the frontier maps isomorphically to Π. Van Kampen, using the stable group in a deeper product end, now shows that M→V and the end group→π₁(V) are both isomorphisms.

Call such a V a 1-neighborhood. Nested 1-neighborhoods have compact intervening slabs whose two frontier inclusions and inclusion into either end neighborhood induce π₁-isomorphisms; apply Van Kampen to their union and the two frontier isomorphisms. This is the π₁ fact needed by the handle operations above.

Every V used here is finitely dominated. One way to check this in this particular product-end situation is to sandwich V between two topological product levels. Retract its far product tail to the larger finite level; the remaining compact manifold with corners is an ANR. Embed that compact ANR in Euclidean space, retract a neighborhood onto it, and choose a finite polyhedron in that neighborhood containing it. Restricting the retraction gives finite domination. No smoothness of the bounding topological level is asserted or needed for this domination step.

## Improving the end to a (q−3)-neighborhood

Say that V is a j-neighborhood if it is a 1-neighborhood and H_i(Ṽ,M̃)=0 for i≤j, with lifts in the Π-cover. We now construct arbitrarily small j-neighborhoods, j=2,…,q−3, by a finite induction for each prescribed compact exclusion.

The first nonzero relative homology is finitely generated over R=Z[Π]. In this special free-abelian case this can be checked directly: R is a localization of the Noetherian polynomial ring Z[t₁,…,t_r], hence Noetherian. Finite domination of V and finite CW type of M make the relative lifted chain complex a chain-homotopy retract of a finite complex of finitely generated free R-modules. Its homology is therefore finitely generated. Only this elementary finiteness is used, not vanishing of any unexplained geometric obstruction.

Suppose V is a (j−1)-neighborhood and x∈H_j(Ṽ,M̃). A finite singular cycle representing x is contained in a compact slab U=V−int V′, where V′ is a sufficiently small (j−1)-neighborhood supplied by the induction. The triple sequence and excision show that M→U is (j−2)-connected. Give U a finite handle decomposition, simplified as above, with indices max{2,j−1},…,q−2. Its based lifted core complex computes H_*(Ũ,M̃).

Insert a j/(j+1) auxiliary pair. If e₀ is the new j-core and c is a chain representing x, then e₀+c is another cycle representing x. The elementary slides e₀↦e₀+c, leaving every old basis element fixed, make that cycle the core of one actual j-handle. Its attaching (j−1)-sphere has algebraically zero intersection with every lower belt sphere. Pair intersections with equal Π-labels and opposite signs and remove them by the supported Whitney moves above. The sphere now misses every lower belt sphere. Following the gradient trajectories from it to M therefore extends its core to a properly embedded based j-disk D in U with ∂D⊂M and [D]=x. For j=2 there are no lower handles, so this last step is immediate; for larger j both Whitney dimensions, or the verified index-two complement exception, apply.

Delete an open tubular neighborhood of D from V, rounding corners. The new V″ remains a 1-neighborhood: the dual handle index q−j≥3 preserves all π₁ maps. Excision and the triple exact sequence identify its relative H_j with the quotient of H_j(Ṽ,M̃) by the generated class Rx, while lower relative homology remains zero. Repeat for a finite generating set, choosing its images after each deletion. This yields a j-neighborhood. The construction avoids the prescribed compact set and the side collar throughout. Induction reaches j=q−3.

## Isolating and removing the projective top class

For a (q−3)-neighborhood V, choose a nested sequence of 1-neighborhoods. Each compact slab has handles of indices 2,…,q−2, so piecing its Morse functions gives a proper handle presentation of (V,M) using only those indices. Its lifted relative chain complex is free, bounded in degrees 2,…,q−2, and exact below q−2. Split the exact sequence upward from its lowest free term. The sole possible homology P=H_(q−2)(Ṽ,M̃) is consequently projective. The preceding finite-domination argument shows it is finitely generated. Reduced K₀(R)=0 implies that P is stably free.

Choose a smaller (q−3)-neighborhood V′ so that the finite generating cycles of P lie in U=V−int V′. Write M=frontier V, N=frontier V′. The triple sequence, together with the same high-degree calculation for V′, gives

H_(q−2)(Ũ,M̃)≅P,     H_(q−3)(Ũ,M̃)≅P′,

where P′ is projective; all other relative homology vanishes. Simplify the finite handle decomposition of (U;M,N) until it has only indices q−3 and q−2. Its free based chain complex is C_(q−2)→C_(q−3), with projective kernel P and projective cokernel P′. If B is its boundary image, splitting gives C_(q−2)=P⊕B and C_(q−3)=B⊕P′. Hence B and P′ are stably free as well.

Stabilization here has a literal geometric realization. Adding auxiliary (q−3)/(q−2) pairs adds free summands to B. Creating such a pair and deleting its lower thickened core from the end adds one free summand to P (and to the corresponding slab class). Thus finitely many operations make P and B actually free. Stabilize once more until an adapted basis for P⊕B is reachable by elementary basis changes. The algebraic reason is explicit: for any invertible matrix A, the block matrix diag(A,A⁻¹) is elementary. Indeed

diag(A,A⁻¹) = [[I,A],[0,I]] [[I,0],[−A⁻¹,I]] [[I,A],[0,I]] [[0,−I],[I,0]],

and the final block rotation is itself a product of three elementary block shears. Each block shear is a finite product of ordinary elementary matrices. The inverse factor acts on auxiliary free coordinates. Therefore the required adapted basis is achieved by the concrete handle slides already described; no arbitrary basis change is silently declared geometric.

The upper handles whose core classes form the chosen basis of P have zero algebraic intersection with all lower belt spheres. Reverse the Morse function, use the verified index-two complement calculation, and perform the framed Whitney motions to remove all these intersections. Their attaching spheres can now be followed to M, yielding disjoint properly embedded based (q−2)-disks representing the basis of P. Lower their critical levels below all remaining q−3 handles. The compact initial region K is M with precisely those thickened disks attached, and its inclusion into V induces an isomorphism on the only possible relative homology. Consequently, for V₀=V−int K,

H_*(Ṽ₀,M̃₀)=0.

All π₁ maps are still isomorphisms. One can check the only potentially delicate map from the new frontier through U−K to U by reversing its remaining handles: the preceding frontier and N each induce Π-isomorphisms, and the handle indices on the original side are at least q−3≥3. Van Kampen and the original N→U isomorphism give the claim. Thus M₀→V₀ is a homotopy equivalence. This has produced an actual acyclic smooth end neighborhood; it has not simply relabeled the obstruction as zero.

## From acyclic ends to a collar, then to a product with R

Repeat the construction beyond successively larger compact sets to obtain nested acyclic end neighborhoods V₀⊃V₁⊃⋯ escaping to ε. Every compact slab A_i=V_i−int V_(i+1) has both frontier inclusions inducing Π-isomorphisms. The triple sequence and excision give acyclicity of its universal-cover relative complex for the first frontier. To obtain the OTHER frontier, take a finite handle presentation of the slab relative to the first frontier. Its finite free R-chain complex is acyclic and therefore contractible: successively split the exact sequence from the lowest free term. Relative Poincare–Lefschetz duality identifies the complex for the opposite frontier, with its side product included on the relative side, with the shifted R-dual of this finite complex, using the orientation involution on R. That dual is contractible as well. Hence both frontier pairs are acyclic in the universal cover; the Π-isomorphisms and the simply connected homology criterion make both inclusions homotopy equivalences. Thus A_i is an actual compact smooth h-cobordism. This finite duality is relative to the unchanged side collar and does not infer opposite acyclicity merely from excision. Its Whitehead torsion lies in Wh(Π)=0. Apply the relative smooth s-cobordism product theorem to A_i, with its existing side product collar fixed. Choose its product to agree with the preceding bottom collar; rescaling collars arranges this without changing the map on that collar. The products concatenate along their matching collars. Local finiteness of the escaping slabs makes their union a diffeomorphism frontier(V₀)×[0,∞)→V₀. This is the desired smooth end collar.

Do this at both ends. Between two smooth collar frontiers lies a compact smooth cobordism A. Each collar includes homotopy equivalently into Q. Here is a useful check of that assertion: a sufficiently deep translation of the smooth collar lies inside a topological product tail, which itself lies inside the original smooth collar. Inclusion of the translated smooth collar into the original one is a homotopy equivalence, as is inclusion of the topological tail into Q. Applying the two-out-of-six property to these nested inclusions proves the intermediate inclusions are homotopy equivalences. Therefore both frontiers include homotopy equivalently into A. This is again an actual compact h-cobordism with group Π, hence a relative product because Wh(Π)=0. Attach the two existing infinite collars to this product. The resulting diffeomorphism F×R→Q has the prescribed side product germ.

All finite moves are performed in the interiors of their compact slabs. On the side boundary, start with the specified smooth product height and retain it through the Morse perturbations. Handle cores, bands, Whitney disks and their cutoff fields remain away from that fixed collar. Thus the final product has the full prescribed boundary behavior, rather than merely some product structure on the boundary.

## Scope and choice accounting

This reconstructs the two q=7 and q=6 end splittings from elementary handle operations and the two separately supplied free-abelian algebra vanishings. It does not prove the original ambient-dimension-five engulf/refurl step, and it does not prove the angular fibering F. Those require separate constructions. No product identity in the two added base circles is promised by this splitting itself; product naturality is required of the earlier original-dimensional refurlings, as recorded in the operation interface.

The ACω bound needs more than a countable list of dependent choices. Here is the actual relative coding argument. Fix once a countable smooth atlas and compatible countable triangulation of Q, a countable compact exhaustion, and a countable collection of chart bump and collar formulas. The given smooth side germ is retained as an EXACT input template in these formulas; it is not assigned a rational code or approximated. The same applies to the exact collars and attaching maps created at an earlier finite history.

At any finite history form a countable family of candidate next configurations as follows. Combinatorial cells, based paths, group elements, ring coefficients, matrices, finite cycles and finite presentations have codes in a countable alphabet. On each of the finitely many moving chart pieces, use rational-coefficient polynomial approximations on rational compact boxes, patched by the fixed bump formulas. On an already fixed collar use the existing exact template, and multiply the moving correction by a fixed cutoff vanishing on a smaller collar. Relative tubular extension and corner rounding use the fixed formulas. Thus the family is countable relative to that history, even when its fixed smooth input functions have no finite rational description.

Every successful finite compact configuration constructed in the proof has a representative among these candidates. A smooth map on a compact moving box admits arbitrarily close polynomial approximation, with derivatives through any prescribed finite order; partitioning into finitely many charts and using the just-described relative cutoff retains the exact matching germs. For disks, tubes, bands, collars and Whitney frames use C¹ closeness of maps and C⁰ closeness of their frames. Compact embeddings have tubular neighborhoods; sufficiently close maps remain embeddings and are homotopic relative to their fixed attaching collar by the tubular interpolation. The finitely many disjoint sets, protected supports and chart boundaries have positive separation after the buffers chosen in the construction. Transverse intersections and their signed labels persist under sufficiently small C¹ perturbations, with no new intersections outside the chosen compact intersection charts. The based homotopy classes are preserved by the same relative interpolation, so the exact homology and group-ring labels do not change. A frame stays in its prescribed component and homotopy class under small interpolation in the frame bundle. For Morse functions use C² approximation on their compact perturbation regions; nondegenerate Hessians and the finitely many distinct regular/critical level gaps are stable. Exact birth/cancellation models and previously fixed boundary germs remain the unaltered templates on their smaller neighborhoods. These facts provide the positive finite-order margins required for an admissible code; no arbitrary smooth germ has been claimed to possess an exact polynomial encoding.

The possible finite histories themselves form a countable tree: append one finite natural-number code to the preceding history, and evaluate the prescribed relative formulas on that history's actual templates. No selection of an uncountable family of functions is involved. The proof above establishes that every history at which a move is owed has at least one admissible child. Define the next state to use the LEAST admissible child, requiring its support to avoid the next prescribed compact/protected collar and requiring the explicitly stated class, framing and separation conditions. Admissibility need not be decidable. Well-ordering of the natural numbers gives a unique child, so ordinary recursion constructs the successive neighborhoods without dependent choice. Initial selections of the fixed countable atlas/templates/exhaustion, if needed rather than supplied, use ACω once. All later stages are this functional recursion. Thus the stated countable-choice bound is retained.
