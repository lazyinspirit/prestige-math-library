## Obtaining the countable analytic background underACω

Second countability supplies a countable topological basis. For each basis element that lies in some smooth chart, choose one such chart; this is a countable family of nonempty sets and usesACω. These chosen charts coverQ. In interior charts enumerate coordinate rational balls with compact closures in the chart domains. In boundary charts also enumerate RELATIVE half-balls meeting the boundary, and for the finite cornered slabs enumerate rational boxes intersected with the coordinate orthants. Closures and interiors here are relative to the halfspace or orthant, so these sets actually cover boundary and corner points. Their pulled-back closed sets are compact. Their relative interiors coverQ and the relevant cornered slabs. Finite unions give compact sets exhaustingQ. Pass to a sequence K_n⊂intK_(n+1) by selecting at each step the LEAST larger finite initial union whose interior contains the current compact set. Compactness guarantees such an index, so this recursion is deterministic.

Cover each compact shell K_n−intK_(n−1) by finitely many enumerated chart balls with closures in intK_(n+1)−K_(n−2). Choose the least finite list satisfying those containment and covering conditions; no decidability is needed. The shell index makes the resulting family locally finite. Fixed Euclidean bump formulas, RESTRICTED to the chart halfspace or orthant for those relative half-balls/boxes, remain smooth up to the boundary and corners and positive where needed. Dividing by their positive locally finite sum produces the smooth partition of unity. Smooth cutoffsχ_n equal to1 onK_n and supported in intK_(n+1) follow from finitely many such bumps. The locally finite sum ∑(1−χ_n) is a proper smooth exhaustion. Thus this background needs oneACω atlas selection followed by least-index recursion and explicit formulas, not dependent choice.

When a signed proper marked height is given, refine the locally finite chart cover so its oscillation on each ball is less than a fixed error bound. Choose the least rational in the corresponding nonempty value interval and average with the partition of unity. The result is a smooth proper signed approximation with that error. Where a product boundary height is prescribed, carry out the approximation off a smaller known collar and interpolate to the exact product height on that collar. The required compatible bounded-height overlap is supplied by the marked exterior construction. This yields the regular-level background used for the finite clean cuts.

Finite triangulations and handle decompositions of a compact smooth slab still belong to the ordinary finite geometric input explicitly listed above. There is no countable geometric selection hidden in one slab: its finite coordinate cover, finite Morse perturbation, finite handle cores and finite common refinements are selected inside that instance's complete finite package.
