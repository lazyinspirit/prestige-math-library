# The marked torus operation in base dimension five

This is a research proof reconstruction, not an accepted supplier. The elementary portions below are proved, but the smooth engulfing, end splitting, and relative fibering constructions remain genuine missing local proofs. Naming their established theorems does not discharge those obligations. No canonical item, manifest, native report, or old evidence was changed.

The primary text actually inspected is Kirby–Siebenmann, *Normal bundles for codimension 2 locally flat imbeddings*, printed 312–315 and 317–322, cached in `../renewed-stable-normal/sources/ks-03.png` through `ks-06.png` and `ks-08.png` through `ks-13.png`. In particular, the primary text itself states the end splitting and fibering theorems as inputs in §2; it does not prove them in §§5–6. Its bibliography attributes splitting to Siebenmann's 1965 thesis and Kervaire's 1969 lectures, and fibering to Farrell's Nice ICM 1970 contribution, vol. 2, pp. 69–72. This note does not claim to have read those additional works.

## Exact interface and dimensions

Write P=B^k×T^n, where k+n=5, 0≤k≤2, and n≥3. The two further torus factors are ordered and distinguished: X=P×T², with core P×{0}. Input is a smooth structure Σ₁ on the underlying topological pair (X,P), standard on the core and in a neighborhood of ∂P×T². The required construction changes Σ₁ by two controlled unfurl/refurl operations to Σ₂. It then chooses a positive integer s and pulls Σ₂ back by

e=id_P×(R²/8sZ²→R²/8Z²)

to obtain Σ₃. Required output is a pair diffeomorphism g:(P×T²_s,P)→(P×T²_s,P)_{Σ₃}, identity on P and near the outer boundary, whose underlying map is homotopic to the identity. In particular g preserves both distinguished normal H¹ classes. It is not a claim that a finite cover of the unmodified Σ₁ is already standard by this specific construction.

The first splitting concerns a 7-manifold and produces a compact 6-manifold. The second concerns a 6-manifold and produces V^5. The nested-tube W has dimension 7, fundamental group Z^(n+1), and prospective angular fibers have dimension 6 and fundamental group Z^n. There is no dimension-five smooth h-cobordism in this part of the proof. The later cancellation of two added BASE circles is a different operation, owned by `../renewed-smooth-pair-cobordism/`; these are not the two NORMAL circles used here.

For the original base-dimension-three consumer, the order is stricter: construct the two normal refurlings Σ₁→Σ₂ in ambient dimension five FIRST, and only then take the product with the two auxiliary BASE circles. Splitting and fibering act on that stabilized input. The finite normal cover is chosen on the original normal factors, so its pullback is Σ₃×T²_base. A generic dimension-five-base refurling that mixes the auxiliary base coordinates would not give this cancellation interface. The finite smooth engulfing motions constructed in `finite-engulfing-construction.md` are natural under product with T²_base: take the vector fields, cutoff and zero-set function independent of the two auxiliary coordinates. Their compact support becomes the product of the old compact support with the compact T²_base; the flows and the furling quotient then commute with that product. The remaining infinite and pairwise constructions must retain the same product condition.

## What furling supplies, with the markings retained

An unfurling is a specified connected infinite cyclic cover. For a two-ended manifold N, choose disjoint end neighborhoods U₋,U₊ and maps φ₋:U₋→N and φ₊:U₊→N onto N which are identity sufficiently far out the corresponding end and isotopic to the inclusions relative to a smaller end neighborhood. Furling identifies x∈U₋ with φ₊⁻¹φ₋(x)∈U₊. Work in the smooth category when the maps are smooth. These hypotheses, including the existence of the compression maps, are essential.

Here is the full uniqueness mechanism once those maps exist. If φ₋ is unchanged and φ₊ is changed, let D be the set on which the two positive maps differ. Since both maps are identity near the positive end, choose a sufficiently large negative end neighborhood O containing both φ₊(D) and φ₊*(D), with a collar of its finite frontier disjoint from these sets. This is a large negative end neighborhood; its complement is a positive tail, so no assertion of compactness of D is needed. In each quotient discard the negative representatives φ₋⁻¹(O), retaining their equivalent positive representatives. On the retained positive representatives use φ₊*⁻¹φ₊, and use the identity on the remaining representatives. Near the frontier of O the positive maps coincide, so these formulas agree on a collar. They also agree on the remaining seam because φ₋ is unchanged. Thus they descend to a smooth isomorphism; the reversed transition gives its inverse. Interchange the signs to change only φ₋. To compare two arbitrary pairs, first shrink the end neighborhoods until every positive neighborhood is disjoint from every negative one, then compare through the mixed pair (φ₋,φ₊*). Shrinking only discards redundant end charts and leaves the quotient canonically isomorphic. Thus the resulting map is identity on the common protected central region. The same construction respects a closed submanifold when every compression does, and is smooth near a protected set when every map there is smooth. In product boundary charts the formulas are products, hence the descended map has the same boundary product property.

This construction also proves the cohomology assertion needed below. Build the preferred infinite cyclic cover as the quotient of Z×N, identifying (j,x) with (j+1,φ₊⁻¹φ₋(x)) on the seam. The preceding comparison formulas apply on every copy of N and commute with (j,x)↦(j+1,x). Thus the comparison map pulls the cover class in H¹(-;Z) back to itself. If another class in H¹(N;Z) is given, take its principal Z-cover. The isotopies of the compression maps lift to this cover (choose the lift starting at the identity), so the same seam construction furls this auxiliary cover and the comparison is covered by its comparison map. Thus it preserves the other class too. Applying this with each of the two ordered normal classes proves preservation of both; a bare unmarked uniqueness assertion would not suffice.

Applied first to the first normal circle and then to the second, smooth end splitting and this uniqueness mechanism give

f:V^5×T²→X_{Σ₂}.

The ordered normal classes pull back to the two torus projection classes. Therefore the covering-space lifting criterion gives a diffeomorphism

f̃:V×R²→(P×R²)_{Σ̄₂}.

For k>0, choose the lift that is a product with id_R² on one boundary component. If k=2 the boundary is connected, hence this is true on all of it. For k=1, the lift on the other component may differ by an 8Z² translation. In a collar of that component compose f with a normal-torus translation which is the identity on the boundary, using a path whose lift runs from that deck translation to zero. This changes the lift there to the required identity translation and changes nothing on the other boundary. Thus requiring a product lift on the whole boundary is a real adjustment, not an automatic consequence of the ordinary lifting criterion.

## An elementary replacement for the circle-bundle trivialization sentence

This part avoids treating the assertion G(2)/O(2) contractible as an extra opaque input. Suppose q:E→A is an oriented smooth circle bundle, and α∈H¹(E;Z) restricts to +1 on every oriented fiber. In the integral Gysin sequence, the restriction H¹(E;Z)→H⁰(A;Z) sends α to 1; exactness says that the Euler class e(q) is zero. Oriented smooth circle bundles reduce to SO(2): choose a fiber metric and arclength, with the normalized fiber volume; the rotation coordinate changes are rotations after choosing local origins. Their isomorphism classes are therefore classified by H²(A;Z), their Euler class. Hence q is smoothly trivial.

Choose a smooth trivialization and let β be its angular class. The difference α−β restricts to zero on every fiber and so, again by the degree-one part of the Gysin sequence, equals q*γ for γ∈H¹(A;Z). Represent γ by a smooth map a:A→S¹ and multiply the chosen angular coordinate by a∘q. The resulting smooth angular projection is a bundle projection and represents α. Since S¹ is K(Z,1), it is homotopic to any original angular map representing α. With specified product data on a boundary collar, use the relative Gysin/relative classification version: the difference class and Euler class must vanish relative to that collar. Absolute vanishing alone must not be silently substituted for this relative condition.

For the KS nested tubes, the normal angular map on P×(R²−0) has degree +1 on each boundary meridian, after the coherent orientation choice. The inner statement follows from local linking number. For the outer boundary it follows by taking a proper normal plane f̃({v}×R²), projecting to R², and taking its degree; the lift preserves the ordered normal end classes and orientations. Both circle bundles therefore admit smooth angular projections homotopic to the original angular map. The remaining task is to extend these boundary projections across W as a smooth fibration: the preceding elementary argument proves only the boundary step.

## Once the angular fibration exists, its fibers really are h-cobordisms

The proof that W=N_V−int(N_T) is an h-cobordism is recorded in `../renewed-stable-normal/source-and-proof-status.md` under the filled exercise. Here the further assertion about a fiber has a short independent proof. Suppose p:W→S¹ is a smooth bundle in the prescribed angular homotopy class, with both boundary restrictions the specified product circle projections. Each boundary inclusion is a homotopy equivalence and preserves the map to Z on π₁. Lift the inclusion and its homotopy inverse to the corresponding infinite cyclic covers; the ordinary lifted homotopies show that the lifted inclusion is still a homotopy equivalence. A bundle over R is trivial, so these three covers are F×R, P×R, and V×R. Under these identifications the lifted inclusions are the products of the fiber boundary inclusions with id_R, up to homotopy. Contracting R shows that P→F and V→F are homotopy equivalences. Thus F is a six-dimensional h-cobordism from P to V, relative to the product side boundary. Its group is π₁(P)=Z^n. Provided the separately owned vanishing Wh(Z^n)=0 and the relative smooth s-cobordism theorem, F is a product.

The product identifies the two normal tube cores by moving successively along three embedded product regions: core V to the chosen angular section of ∂N_V, across its fiber F, and from the corresponding section of ∂N_T to core P. In each region a compact collar translation, extended by a bump function in its normal collar, realizes the indicated motion; the three motions compose to an ambient diffeomorphism ψ of P×R², with compact support, carrying f̃(V×0) onto P×0. In the relative case the translations on the side boundary cancel, and their collar germs must be matched before extending by the identity; treating each of the three individual translations as boundary-fixed is incorrect. The existence of a smoothly varying product fiber and the relative collar matching remain part of the geometric fibering/product construction, not a consequence of abstract homotopy equivalence alone.

## Finite-cover descent and final normalization, proved explicitly

Given the compactly supported ψ just described, choose s so large that its support and its image lie in P×(−4s,4s)², and that ψ is identity in a neighborhood of the boundary of this box. For each a∈8sZ² define ψ_s on the translate of the box by τ_aψτ_a⁻¹, and identity elsewhere. The definitions agree near every box face because ψ is already identity there. Hence ψ_s is a smooth diffeomorphism; its inverse is obtained from the same construction with ψ⁻¹. It commutes with 8sZ² and descends to a diffeomorphism Ψ_s of (P×T²_s)_{Σ₃}. This requires disjoint translates of the whole support, not just pointwise periodic copying without a seam check.

The marked f also lifts to f_s:V×T²_s→(P×T²_s)_{Σ₃}. Because Ψ_s f_s carries V×0 onto P×0, its restriction there is a diffeomorphism a:V→P. Precompose with a⁻¹×id_T²_s. The result u is a pair diffeomorphism fixing P pointwise, and it is identity near the outer boundary after the boundary identifications used in constructing f. Compactly supported ψ preserves the ordered normal cohomology classes: its lift is identity outside a compact set, and those classes on the quotient are determined by the deck translations, with which ψ_s commutes. Thus u preserves both normal classes.

When k>0, a component of the outer boundary surjects onto π₁(P×T²_s); u is identity there, so u induces the identity on π₁. The target is aspherical, hence u is homotopic to the identity. When k=0, core fixation and normal-class preservation imply, in the ordered decomposition π₁(T^(n+2))=Z^n⊕Z², that

u_* = [[I,A],[0,I]].

Let ω be the linear torus automorphism with matrix [[I,−A],[0,I]]. It fixes the core pointwise and preserves the two normal classes. Then g=uω is a pair diffeomorphism with g_*=I, hence homotopic to the identity because the torus is K(Z^(n+2),1). This also shows precisely what the correction is allowed to change: a normal-to-base shear, never a permutation or alteration of the two normal quotient classes.

## Exact unclosed geometric obligations

E. For each of the two unfurled normal directions, construct the required smooth end compression maps by engulfing, equal to the prescribed product compressions near the side boundary and preserving the core where required. Their existence is not proved by the furling uniqueness argument.

S. For the resulting smooth manifolds topologically P×S¹×R (dimension 7) and P×R×S¹ (dimension 6), produce compact smooth cross sections and product diffeomorphisms relative to the specified boundary products. The reduced projective-class obstruction vanishing is necessary, but stating its vanishing is not a proof of the end-completion/handle construction.

F. For the compact 7-dimensional nested-tube h-cobordism W and its specified degree-one angular class, construct a smooth submersion p:W→S¹ agreeing with the already normalized boundary projections, and obtain the collar-compatible product-fiber motion. Wh(Z^(n+1))=0 removes the algebraic obstruction in the established fibering theorem; it does not itself construct the submersion.

The packet therefore does not yet prove the demanded full marked torus operation. E/S/F are established-theorem authoring obligations, not mathematical open problems, and must not be converted into active unproved fallback suppliers. The two later base-circle cancellations additionally have their separately identified exterior-group torsion obligations; this note does not conflate their groups with the free-abelian groups in E/S/F.

## Choice bound

The annular consumer permits at most countable choice, not arbitrary choice. No full-choice inference is licensed by this packet. The displayed finite-cover descent uses a single integer s: once a compact support is given, take the least positive s meeting the two strict box containments. The periodic extension is defined simultaneously by one explicit formula indexed by Z² and requires no choices of maps for individual translates. The shear correction is uniquely determined by the integer matrix of u. The finitely many boundary components and finite compact bundle charts require only finite selections. Infinite cyclic covers and the cyclic-cover fiber argument use explicit Z-actions, not choices of representatives in arbitrary sets. These elementary portions therefore introduce no choice beyond the countable constructions already permitted by the consumer. A future proof of E/S/F must separately control its handle choices along a countable exhaustion and may not import an unrestricted-choice theorem without inspecting its proof. Since E/S/F have not been proved here, their compliance with the ACω bound has not been established merely by this paragraph.
