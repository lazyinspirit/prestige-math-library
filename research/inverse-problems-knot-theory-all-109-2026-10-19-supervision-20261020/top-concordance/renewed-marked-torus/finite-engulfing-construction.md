# Finite smooth engulfing by explicit vector fields

This proves a genuine smooth geometric subsidiary of E. The complete finite engulfing induction is combined with explicit smooth vector fields for its elementary collapse moves. Consequently no approximation of an arbitrary PL homeomorphism by a diffeomorphism is assumed. Compatible smooth triangulation, the ordinary tubular chart of a single embedded simplex, and finite general position are the elementary background; the vertex-perturbation argument is recalled below. The infinite two-ended and codimension-two pair extensions remain separate.

## Exact finite smooth result

Let Q be a smooth q-manifold with a compatible smooth triangulation, without boundary. Let U⊂Q be open, X⊂Q a compact polyhedron of dimension j, and Y⊂U a closed subpolyhedron of dimension s. Suppose the pair (Q,U) is k-connected, with j≤k, j≤q−3, and s≤q−3. Then there is a compactly supported smooth ambient isotopy h_t, starting at the identity, fixing Y, such that X⊂h_1(U).

Here k-connected means that every point of Q can be joined to U and all relative homotopy groups through degree k vanish, for every relevant base point. The result also holds when X is closed rather than compact and X−U is compact, with the same compact-support conclusion. With boundary, assume in addition X∩∂Q⊂U; then the isotopy fixes ∂Q∪Y. The relevant dimensions in the torus application are q=6 or 7. This finite result does not allow a fixed Y of dimension q−2: the core of a codimension-two pair cannot be substituted for Y.

## The collapse move, realized by an explicit smooth flow

Suppose compact subpolyhedra A₀⊂A in a compatible smooth triangulation satisfy A simplicially collapses to A₀, and A∩Y⊂A₀. Given an open O containing A₀, there is a compactly supported smooth ambient isotopy fixing A₀∪Y whose terminal map enlarges O to contain A. It suffices to handle one elementary collapse A=A₀∪σ, with σ=a*Δ, free face Δ, and σ∩A₀=a*∂Δ.

Put r=dim σ. In affine barycentric coordinates (λ_a,λ₁,…,λ_r) on σ, the attached part a*∂Δ is the union of the faces λ_i=0 for 1≤i≤r. Write P=λ₁⋯λ_r. Consider the polynomial tangent field on the affine span of σ given by

λ_i′=−P (1≤i≤r),     λ_a′=rP.

The sum of the barycentric coordinates remains one. Each λ_i stays nonnegative because its derivative has λ_i as a factor. On the free face λ_a=0 the field points inward. On a*∂Δ it vanishes. Its trajectories therefore stay in σ and approach the attached part: the λ_i decrease at the same rate, and if their minimum had a positive limiting value, P would be bounded below and would force them to decrease further.

Here is the required relative ambient extension. A smoothly embedded simplex has a characteristic map extending as an embedding to a neighborhood of the simplex in its affine span. Its normal bundle over a slightly larger contractible disk is trivial. A tubular chart therefore extends the displayed field to a neighborhood of σ in Q. Choose a compactly supported cutoff equal to one near σ. Choose also a smooth nonnegative function ρ whose zero set is A₀∪Y and which is positive elsewhere; only its restriction to the compact chart is needed. Multiply the extended field by this cutoff and by ρ. The resulting global smooth field Z has compact support and vanishes on A₀∪Y. On σ its equations are the displayed equations multiplied by the common nonnegative factor ρ, positive off the attached part. Hence it still preserves σ and approaches a*∂Δ.

The existence of ρ uses no unrestricted choice. In a fixed countable chart basis enumerate the rational balls with closures disjoint from A₀∪Y, put a nonnegative bump positive on a smaller ball in each, and form a positive sum with coefficients chosen to bound every derivative of order at most the index by 2^(−index). The sum is smooth, has exactly the required zero set on the chart, and uses only a countable explicit construction.

Let Φ_t be the complete flow of Z; compact support guarantees completeness for finite positive and negative times. Choose ε>0 so that the subset of σ where min_i λ_i<ε lies in O. On the compact subset min_i λ_i≥ε, the function ρP has a positive minimum c. While a trajectory remains there its least λ_i decreases at rate at least c. Thus after a single finite time T>1/c every point of σ lies in O, and remains there since the λ_i are nonincreasing. Consequently σ⊂Φ_(−T)(O). The inverse flow h_t=Φ_(−tT), 0≤t≤1, is the required smooth engulfing isotopy. It fixes A₀∪Y pointwise and is identity outside one compact set. A finite concatenation handles an arbitrary finite collapse sequence.

This replaces Hudson's PL join homeomorphism by an actual smooth construction. It is essential that each elementary simplex belongs to the compatible smooth refinement; no claim is made that a general PL ambient homeomorphism is itself smooth.

We also need the following dimension-controlled collapse fact, with the auxiliary part distinguished from the retained base. If Z collapses to A and B⊂Z is a subpolyhedron, there is a polyhedron T₀, of total dimension at most dim B+1, such that B⊂A∪T₀ and Z collapses to A∪T₀, which in turn collapses to A. To see this, refine until B is a subcomplex and reorder elementary collapses by decreasing dimension, interchanging independent consecutive moves. Stop immediately before a move would REMOVE an open simplex belonging to B, not merely meet B along a retained face. If its free pair has larger simplex σ and free face τ, either τ orσ belongs to B. Since B is a subcomplex, in the latter case τ belongs to B too. Hence dimσ≤dimB+1. All subsequently retained simplices outside A have no larger dimension. Let T₀ be the closure of their union. Taking that closure adds only their faces, so dimT₀≤dimB+1. The remaining collapse sequence gives A∪T₀↘A, while the performed sequence gives Z↘A∪T₀. No claim that the entire retained base A has this small dimension is made.

## Complete induction for finite smooth engulfing

Fix k and induct on j, the dimension of X, proving the assertion simultaneously for all protected polyhedra of dimension at most q−3. There is one necessary relative preparation. Choose a compact subpolyhedron D⊂X∩U whose relative interior contains X∩Y. Such D exists after refining the finite triangulation using the open cover U and Q−Y. Construct the homotopy Φ:X×I→Q stationary on D×I, with upper face Φ(x,1)=x and lower face in U. The relative construction chooses paths for vertices outside D, then extends simplex prisms using π_d(Q,U)=0; the prescribed D faces already map to U.

Use the relative prism polyhedron C obtained from X×I by identifying each vertical interval {d}×I, d∈D, to its bottom point. Triangulate this finite polyhedron with the upper and lower X copies as subcomplexes. The ordinary relative prism collapse X×I↘(X×0)∪(D×I), followed by those vertical identifications, gives a simplicial collapse C↘X×0 after subdivision. Thus D, including every prescribed intersection of the upper X with Y, belongs to the initially covered base, not a newly added free face. The induced Φ on C is perturbed only on the moving simplices, fixing the upper and lower prescribed faces as needed. Its lower face stays in U.

Here is the actual general-position assertion. For distinct DISJOINT open faces of the moving domain, the extra intersection of their images has dimension at most a+b−q, where a,b are their dimensions. For adjacent faces this bound applies after deleting their prescribed common-face image. The same relative rule holds against fixed upper faces; all prescribed common faces are retained, rather than perturbed away. Against Y, a moving open a-face has extra intersection dimension at most a+s−q. Its possible prescribed fixed intersection with the upper face lies in D and is already in the initial base. Each moving characteristic simplex can be taken nondegenerate and embedded after subdivision.

These assertions follow by finite vertex perturbation in target affine simplex charts, relative to the fixed faces. For a pair of disjoint open source faces, failure of the stated bound is a rank failure of their affine spans: the free vertex coordinates lie in a proper algebraic subset. The complement of the finitely many such failure sets is dense. Choose the moving vertices there in succession, retaining the prescribed common-face images. Refinement puts the finite resulting maps in a common compatible triangulation. No assertion bounds the TOTAL intersection of two adjacent closed simplices by a+b−q.

The relative prism C has the stated simplicial collapse to its lower face. Reverse it, writing R₀⊂R₁⊂⋯⊂R_m, where R₀ is the lower face and R_i=R_{i−1}∪aΔ∪Δ. We construct smooth ambient maps h_i fixing Y with Φ(R_i^(j))⊂h_i(U); here the superscript denotes j-skeleton. At i=0 use the identity.

For the next elementary expansion put V=h_{i−1}(U). Write its new simplex as σ=a*Δ with attached part A=a*∂Δ and free face Δ. Every prescribed common domain face with the old complex is in A. Prescribed intersections with Y on the fixed upper X lie in D, already part of the old base. Thus, after deleting A and those already covered base faces, the bad preimage in σ of Y or Φ(R_{i−1}^(j)) is the union of the EXTRA open-face intersection loci just estimated. Calling its closure Z, the finite polyhedral bounds give

dim Z≤max{j+1+s−q, j+1+j−q}≤j−2.

Taking closures of these extra loci adds only their faces and does not change their dimension. The shared faces deleted above are already attached old faces; they are not smuggled into Z. The relative-prism preparation ensures that no uncontrolled j-dimensional upper face in Y remains a new free face.

Apply the dimension-controlled collapse fact to σ↘A and this Z. It supplies a compact auxiliary polyhedron T₀ of TOTAL dimension≤dimZ+1≤j−1, with Z⊂A∪T₀, and σ↘A∪T₀↘A. The base A can have dimension j; its dimension is not assigned to T₀. Because all extra intersections with Y and the old image have been retained in A∪T₀, the induced collapse of the new image onto the old image together with Φ(T₀) deletes no protected or multiply identified point. This is the precise sense in which that image collapse does not cross Y.

Apply the dimension-(j−1) inductive engulfing assertion to Φ(T₀), protecting Y∪Φ(R_{i−1}^(j)). The protected polyhedron has dimension max{s,j}≤q−3 and already lies in V. The auxiliary has dimension≤j−1, so the induction really applies. The explicit elementary smooth collapse moves then enlarge the resulting open set to contain the new image, fixing the old image and Y. Composition gives h_i.

After the finitely many elementary expansions, X=Φ(X×1) lies in h_m(U). Every new ambient map is smoothly isotopic to the identity with compact support by the explicit vector-field collapse move and the lower-dimensional induction. Their finite concatenation proves the stated isotopy. This proves the induction, including j=0, where general position makes the obstruction empty and only arcs and the collapse move are needed.

If X is closed with X−U compact, choose a compact polyhedral X₀ containing X−U in its relative interior. Apply the compact assertion to X₀, with additional fixed set Y∪closure(X−X₀), which is a closed polyhedron contained in U. Its dimension is at most max{s,j}≤q−3. The produced map fixes the remainder of X pointwise, so it engulfs all X with compact support. For a manifold with boundary remove ∂Q first. A collar pushes any relative homotopy slightly into the interior while leaving its prescribed sphere in U; thus the necessary connectivity persists for (int Q,U∩int Q). Apply the just-proved closed-polyhedron version there. Its compact support stays away from ∂Q, so extending by the identity gives the boundary-fixed conclusion.

## What this has actually closed and what remains

This supplies the complete low-dimensional finite smooth engulfing operation used inside a two-ended argument, including the dimension inequalities, explicit smooth motions, and boundary reduction. It is not a proof that an arbitrary compact full-dimensional set can be engulfed from a single end. The usual two-skeleton argument first engulfs a dual skeleton of dimension two from one end, and then a skeleton of dimension q−3 from the other. Turning that into the required controlled two-ended operation must account for both noncompact ends and any prescribed boundary products. A naive use of the compact-difference corollary on the entire dual skeleton fails: for N=K×R, its difference from a single end need not be compact.

The construction never smooths a completed PL isotopy. The combinatorial homotopy is used only to choose finitely many collapse moves in a common refinement. Each refined simplex is smoothly embedded because it lies in an original smooth characteristic simplex and is obtained there by affine subdivision. Every ambient move is then built by the smooth vector field above. Thus the finite smooth bridge identified in the earlier source discussion is now supplied locally. Infinite two-ended stretching remains a separate task.

The codimension-two pair version requires more still. Hudson's fixed-subpolyhedron bound is dim Y≤q−3, while the required core has dimension q−2. KS printed 317 explicitly distinguishes engulfing motions supported off the core from motions sliding a smooth tubular neighborhood over itself. A proof must arrange the core slide and complement engulfings so that all prescribed germs survive; one cannot claim the pair version by inserting the core in the theorem's Y slot.

All selections in the finite argument are finite. Countable exhaustion and stretching in a future extension must be inspected separately against the consumer's ACω bound.
