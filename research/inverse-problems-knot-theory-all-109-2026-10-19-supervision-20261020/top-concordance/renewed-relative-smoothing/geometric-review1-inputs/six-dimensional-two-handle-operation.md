# The two-handle step in the six-dimensional end construction

This is the needed special geometric-connectivity step in an already smooth
six-manifold. The fresh source is Quinn, *Ends of maps I*, §6.1, printed
pp.308–312, read in full; its index range was visually checked on p.308:
k<n−3, so in dimension six the highest index eliminated by that theorem is
two, not three. The construction below does not claim the full theorem in
every dimension or for an arbitrary control space.

Take a finite small handlebody (V,∂₀V) in a smooth six-manifold, with no
zero- or one-handles in the control region being changed. Suppose each
two-handle core has an actual small relative nullhomotopy into ∂₀V, and that
the required finite Whitney-circle contractions can be made in buffered small
neighborhoods of the intermediate boundary. In the end construction the
relative nullhomotopies come from relative controlled two-connectivity, and
the boundary contractions come from the already constructed controlled
one-connected frontier. These are data, not consequences of the mere names
of the manifolds. Preserve the given product over the closed relative region.

For each core D² of a two-handle, move its boundary through the bottom collar
and use its supplied relative nullhomotopy to obtain a map of D³ into V.
Its boundary consists of the original core and a cap in the bottom. Push
this map into the three-skeleton of the handle presentation. Because
3+2<6, relative general position makes its interior disjoint from the
two-handle cores, so it can be pushed to the boundary of the two-skeleton
together with the three-handles. No middle-dimensional disk-embedding theorem
has been used in this step.

Make the maps transverse to the existing three-handle cores, and push off
their center points. Each map is then in the union of the intermediate
five-dimensional level L and the outgoing boundaries of the three-handles.
Its excursions across those outgoing boundaries are finitely many copies
of the core three-disks. Join their preimages inside its domain D³ by a
tree of arcs. By pushing the images of those arcs into L, and making the
finite trees disjoint from one another and from the core disks, band-sum
these finitely many parallel three-disks. The arcs can be embedded and
kept disjoint in dimension five: 1+1<5 and 1+3<5. Their sufficiently thin
bands are embedded as well. The result is an actual family of disjoint
embedded three-disks C_i in the outgoing boundary of the three-skeleton,
with ∂C_i in L. The remaining parts of the original D³ maps give homotopies
of ∂C_i in L to the reference core-plus-bottom-cap spheres.

The reference sphere for a given two-handle meets that handle's belt S³
once and all the other belts not at all. The cap disks can be embedded and
made disjoint in the five-dimensional bottom, since 2+2<5; they avoid the
other attaching loops because 2+1<5. Thus these reference intersection
numbers are actual geometric data. The homotopies of ∂C_i give the same
intersection matrix with group labels, namely the identity. They also give
the finite Whitney circles needed to pair the excess intersections. Group
labels and their nullhomotopies are retained; they are not replaced by
unlabelled integer cancellation.

The original two-handle attaching loops have small disk maps in the bottom:
restrict the supplied relative core nullhomotopies to their boundary. Choose
a buffered bottom neighborhood containing those disks, the handle tubes,
the relevant homotopy tracks and the required circle contractions. The
preceding belt-complement calculation proves complement injection there.
The preceding controlled borderline Whitney operation then adjusts the
embedded spheres ∂C_i in L so that each meets just its own belt S³ once.
It applies simultaneously to the finite families by its clean-disk
construction in the complement of all belts, preserving the other sheets.
It is essential here to use the actual buffered contractions, not merely
the equality of the algebraic matrix with the identity.

Use the actual three-disks C_i to introduce cancelling three-/four-handle
pairs. A tubular neighborhood of C_i in the outgoing boundary supplies the
standard local model for adding the pair; the newly introduced three-handle
has attaching sphere ∂C_i. The displayed boundary isotopies put these new
three-handles in the geometrically single-point configuration with the old
two-handles. Retained `thm-handle-cancellation` (Statement only consulted;
Countable Choice; support in the affected boundary disk and two handles,
with later attaching data carried along) cancels these two-/three-handle
pairs. The newly introduced four-handles remain. Hence the resulting
presentation has no two-handles in the smaller control region.

Control comes from three finite sources: original handle diameters, the
supplied core nullhomotopies, and the buffered open sets containing the
circle contractions and clean disks. Each band construction or cancellation
adds only the diameters of finitely many such stages. Choose the initial
budget backwards, smaller than the final tolerance divided by the fixed
number of stages and buffer factors. If those supplied contractions cannot
be kept in the buffers, the claimed small presentation has not been proved.
All constructions are on actual smooth finite handlebodies and preserve the
given relative product by working off its protected neighborhood.

This new operation still requires independent review. It supplies the
index-two step once its actual controlled homotopy inputs are supplied;
it does not create a smoothing of a TOP five-manifold, nor assert the full
approximate end theorem without its earlier frontier-connectivity and later
projection-kernel operations.
