# Finite frontier improvement in the actual smooth six-dimensional product end

This reconstructs the needed n=6, W_j=∅ case of Quinn, *Ends of maps I*,
§7.2–7.3, printed pp.315–319, newly read in full. All operations take place
in the actual smooth stabilized manifold. They preserve the already prescribed
product near the closed protected region. The finite relative Hurewicz,
complement, and controlled borderline Whitney operations are the NEW drafts
in this directory; they require independent acceptance before this assembly
is a proved supplier.

Start with a sufficiently long finite tame structure of smooth neighborhoods
U₀⊃U₁⊃⋯ in one of the preassigned height bands of the product end. Its
buffered annular inclusions have the actual approximate extension property
for finite relative two-complexes over the control base. Its homotopies push
U_j into U_j−U_{j+2}, fixed on U_j−U_{j+1}, with the prescribed small track
bound. These are actual maps from the vertical-clamp construction, not an
assertion that the neighborhoods are homotopy equivalent by name.

Whenever U_j⊃V⊃U_{j+r} is constructed, retain V and discard the intervening
levels to interpolate a shorter tame structure. The original homotopies and
extra outer levels supply the buffered hypotheses for that interpolation.
Every improvement below consumes a fixed finite number of levels. Choose
the input length and tolerances backwards from the final requested bound.

## Make the frontier controlled path connected

Cover the compact control region by finitely many small sets S_α, with
larger sets still in the prescribed buffers. In the compact affected slab,
choose smooth codimension-zero neighborhoods G_α of the inverse images of
smaller control sets, contained in e⁻¹(S_α), transverse to the old frontiers.
Smooth separation of compact sets inside an open smooth manifold and regular
values supply these actual neighborhoods. They are not submanifolds of the
TOP control base.

Each G_α∩∂U_j has finitely many components; choose representatives. The
compact relative pieces admit finite handle presentations, and hence finite
sets of paths generating their relative fundamental groupoids. For this
existence only, the retained Statements of adapted excellent Morse existence
and Morse/handle correspondence are used; both explicitly assume Countable
Choice. No proof or check of those providers was read. Choose finite paths
β in (U_j−U_{j+2})∩G_α, with ends on the old frontier, generating the relative
path information after its components have been joined. Embed them disjointly
in the smooth six-manifold and delete their thin one-handle neighborhoods
from the end.

Use local path connectedness of the control sets and the supplied approximate
lifting to choose paths α joining the component representatives, small over
the control base. They may cross the frontier. At every such crossing cut
out the portions lying inside U_j, then add thin neighborhoods of the whole
paths. The α and β arcs and their collars are chosen disjoint by relative
general position; 1+1<6. All these are actual additions and deletions of
one-handles, with finite intersections after transversality.

The new frontier is controlled path connected: subdivide a proposed finite
path in the control base, lift its vertices to the old frontier, and use the
added α handles to join consecutive lifts inside one G_α. The relative pair
is controlled one-connected: push a relative path's endpoints off the new
handles, subdivide it into small pieces, push it through the original tame
homotopy into the outer annulus, and use the β cuts to move each remaining
piece into the new frontier. The paths β were selected to generate exactly
that finite relative groupoid. Each movement stays in the corresponding
larger S_α. This verifies the lifting and relative-connectivity consequences,
not only the connectivity of a graph in the frontier.

## Kill the frontier's small fundamental groups

In each G_α choose finite generating loops of the frontier components.
Their projected loops bound small disks in the locally 1-connected control
base. The supplied approximate two-complex extension property lifts these
to actual disk maps in an outer buffered end neighborhood. Smooth relative
general position in dimension six makes the finite disk family embedded
and disjoint: 2+2<6. They start outward at the old frontier and meet it
transversely elsewhere.

Their intersections with U_j may be planar surfaces rather than disks.
Choose finite arc systems cutting those planar surfaces to disks. The
relative controlled one-connectivity just obtained moves the arc images
into the frontier. Extend each arc homotopy as an embedded disk in dimension
six, and push the original surface across that disk and past the frontier.
The tame homotopy puts these changes inside a larger prescribed annulus.
This produces actual spanning disks whose intersections with U_j are disks.

Delete the neighborhoods of those inside pieces, then add neighborhoods of
the full spanning disks. These are two-handle deletions and additions. They
kill the selected generating loops. They introduce no untracked fundamental-
group generators: a one-complex in the new frontier can be pushed off the
new belt spheres and back into its common part with the old frontier,
since 1+3<5 in that frontier. The two-handle additions then supply the
actual relations killing the old generators. Thus the improved frontier
has the controlled approximate extension property for finite relative
two-complexes, with the same buffer allowances. Interpolate again.

## Obtain relative two-connectivity, then relative three-connectivity

The improved frontier first makes the end/frontier pair relatively controlled
one-connected. For relative degree two, take the finite two-handle cores of
a compact slab. Move its one-skeleton into the frontier using that property,
so its two-cells give small relative disk maps. The tame push moves those
maps into a bounded larger annulus. Relative general position embeds the
finite disk family disjointly in dimension six. Delete their thin tubular
neighborhoods from the end to obtain V. Excision says that the resulting
buffered pair maps kill relative homology in degree two: the generators
represented by those core disks now lie in the new frontier. The new
relative two-connectivity follows from the finite controlled Hurewicz
operation, applied to a sufficiently long subsequence of those buffered
maps. Its count of stages is fixed in advance.

For degree three, repeat with the three-skeleton after relative two-connectivity
has been obtained. Do **not** claim that arbitrary three-disks embed by
general position in dimension six: 3+3=6. Instead eliminate zero- and
one-handles on a sufficiently large compact slab, then put the relative
three-disk maps in its three-skeleton. Their excursions across the actual
three-handle cores can be band-summed along finite disjoint arc trees in
the five-dimensional level, exactly as in the preceding two-handle operation.
This gives actual embedded three-disks with boundary two-spheres in the
intermediate level and homotopies to their original boundary spheres in
the lower frontier.

Those boundary spheres have zero group-labelled intersections with the
dual three-spheres of the intervening two-handles, because their supplied
homotopies end in the lower frontier. The latter two-handle attaching loops
have actual small spanning disks there, supplied by the improved frontier's
approximate two-complex lifting. Hence the finite belt-complement injection
calculation and controlled borderline Whitney construction apply in a
buffered open neighborhood containing these data. They remove the excess
intersections. Radial motion off the belts then gives disjoint cylinders
down to the lower frontier, producing actual properly embedded three-disks
with the required boundaries.

Delete their thin tubular neighborhoods. Excision again gives the relative
homological three-triviality of the buffered pair maps; the finite controlled
Hurewicz operation gives relative controlled three-connectivity after a fixed
finite further thinning. Each lower index and its control allowance is
retained by choosing its budget before performing the next stage.

The output is a shortened finite tame structure whose frontiers are controlled
one-connected and whose end/frontier pair maps are relatively controlled
three-connected. These are the exact n=6 inputs to the final three-/four-
handle normalization and actual kernel-projection realization. The latter
stages are separate, already explicit drafts, not conclusions smuggled into
this improvement step.

Every choice here is finite within one finite tame structure; obtaining all
