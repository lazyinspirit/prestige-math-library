# Actual stable elementary diagonalization of a finite idempotent

This finite algebraic operation uses the retained NEW algebra contract supplied
by root: for R=Z[Z^r], reduced K₀(R)=0, K₁(R) is generated by the classes of
−1 and the Laurent variables, and the contract is proved by finite presentation
and stable elementary operations. Root reports that contract accepted under
full AC; its Countable Choice refinement was pending when this note was
written. No proof or receipt of that provider was opened or checked here.
The operation below adds only finite selections of witnesses and bases to
that supplied contract, so its choice strength is exactly that of the contract.

Let p be an idempotent on R^n. Put I=im(p), J=ker(p). Then I and J are
finitely generated projective modules and R^n=I⊕J. The augmentation R→Z
gives a nonnegative integer rank a for I and b for J, with a+b=n.
The retained K₀ contract gives [I]=a[R] and [J]=b[R]. By the definition of
group completion of the direct-sum monoid of finite projectives, there are
finite projectives T and U and actual isomorphisms

I⊕T ≅ R^a⊕T,   J⊕U ≅ R^b⊕U.

Choose finite complementary projectives T′,U′ with T⊕T′≅R^s and
U⊕U′≅R^t. Adding those complements to the two displayed isomorphisms gives
actual stable trivializations

I⊕R^s ≅ R^(a+s),   J⊕R^t ≅ R^(b+t).

Thus p′=p⊕1_s⊕0_t has genuinely free image and kernel. Choose their finite
bases using those actual isomorphisms. Their concatenation is a basis of
R^(n+s+t). The resulting invertible matrix A sends p′ by conjugation to
d=diag(1_(a+s),0_(b+t)). This is an actual matrix, rather than a K₀ class
asserted to be a basis.

Every K₁ class in the retained contract is represented by a unit
u=±x₁^m₁⋯x_r^m_r. Choose such a unit representing [A]. If n+s+t=0 the
assertion is empty. Otherwise put u⁻¹ in one diagonal entry of a diagonal
matrix D, with 1 in every other entry. Then D commutes with d, and
[DA]=0 in K₁(R). By the definition of stable K₁, after adjoining finitely
many identity coordinates DA is a finite product E of elementary matrices.
Adjoin the same extra coordinates with zero projection to p′ and d.
Conjugation gives

E (p⊕1_s⊕0_(t+v)) E⁻¹
 = (D⊕1_v)(d⊕0_v)(D⁻¹⊕1_v)
 = diag(1_(a+s),0_(b+t+v)).

Every intermediate elementary factor, its inverse, and the final geometric
projection now exist as finite matrices. This is the exact algebraic
diagonalization needed after lifting a finite geometric projection to a torus
cover. It has not yet imposed a propagation bound on those factors; the
controlled proof must use their finite Laurent degree bounds, radial shrinking,
and bounded truncation before projecting back to the small disk. Vanishing
K₀ and K₁ by themselves do not supply that geometric control.

For the annular route this construction may be used only once the retained
algebra input has a genuine Countable Choice contract. For the disk route
its present full-AC contract is compatible with the permitted assumptions.
