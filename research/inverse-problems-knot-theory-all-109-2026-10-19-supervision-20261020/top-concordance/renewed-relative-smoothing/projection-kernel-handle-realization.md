# Actual handle realization of the kernel projection

This is the last finite handle operation of Quinn, *Ends of maps I*, §7.4,
printed pp.319–320, newly read in full, with p.319 also visually checked.
It is performed in an **already smooth six-dimensional end**. No smoothing
of the TOP comparison region is inferred from it.

Assume an actual finite relative three-/four-handle presentation has been
constructed, with boundary d:C₄→C₃. Supply the actual small terminal chain
projection h with h²=h, dh=0 and im(h)=ker(d), as in the preceding tame
domination construction. Put p=1−h, so ker(p)=ker(d). Assume the finite
controlled projection operation has given an actual finite elementary change
of basis, after adding geometric zero and identity summands, making that
kernel a coordinate submodule. This means the finite elementary factors
and their propagation bounds, not merely a vanishing K₀ class.

## Realize the stabilization with the correct sign convention

Adding a cancelling three-/four-handle pair adds a one-dimensional identity
block to d. Its new degree-four direction is not in ker(d). Accordingly it
adds a zero block to h and an **identity** block to p=1−h.

To add a **zero** block to p, first add such a cancelling pair and then delete
the interior of its three-handle from the end neighborhood. Its outgoing
four-handle is then based on the modified frontier, giving a new free
degree-four kernel direction. Thus it adds an identity block to h and a zero
block to p. The relative end neighborhood has changed by the specified finite
handle cut; the manifold outside the compact modified region is unchanged.
This modification is allowed when constructing a new approximate-completion
frontier. Confusing h with p would reverse these two stabilization operations.

An elementary change on the four-handle module is realized by the corresponding
handle slide: band-sum a parallel attaching sphere of one four-handle into
another along the specified arc in the intermediate level. A signed group
element is realized by a choice of lift, orientation, and an actual arc with
that label. A general group-ring coefficient is a finite sum, hence uses
finitely many parallel copies and slides. The arcs and their control bounds
must be supplied by the controlled basis-change construction and the actual
one-connected frontier data. An algebraic word with no controlled arcs is
not a controlled geometric realization.

With those arcs, every elementary factor is an actual finite handle operation.
Their total track bound is the sum of the factor budgets. The resulting
boundary is dE⁻¹ and its kernel is the coordinate submodule carried by the
distinguished four-handles. All of these distinguished columns are zero,
including their fundamental-group labels, not only their integer augmentations.

## Remove excessive geometric intersections in the five-dimensional level

Let L be the intermediate five-dimensional level. From the upper frontier,
L is obtained by adding the dual two-handles corresponding to the four-handles.
The improved upper frontier supplies actual small disk maps for the attaching
loops of these dual two-handles. Thus the preceding finite belt-complement
calculation applies in a buffered open neighborhood containing their tubes
and those disk maps. Its B_j³ are exactly the attaching spheres of the
distinguished four-handles; the A_i² are the belt spheres of the lower
three-handles.

The zero group-labelled columns pair their transverse intersection points
with opposite signs and equal labels. Choose arcs avoiding every other finite
intersection. Equality of labels gives the corresponding nullhomotopy of each
Whitney circle; for a controlled conclusion those actual contractions must
lie in the same buffered neighborhood. The improved frontier's approximate
two-complex lifting data provide these contractions after the control
tolerances have been shrunk. Neither the integer matrix alone nor global
simple connectivity is substituted for them.

Apply the new finite-family controlled borderline Whitney operation. It moves
the lower A_i² spheres off the distinguished B_j³, preserving the whole other
finite family. This is a change of the lower handle presentation: the ambient
isotopy on L extends into a collar of the lower handlebody, and its images
are the new lower belt spheres. The upper attaching spheres remain the fixed
reference data. Therefore the resulting presentation describes the same
smooth slab, rather than an unproved alteration of its diffeomorphism type.

An attaching B_j³ now avoids every lower belt sphere. In a three-handle
D³×D³, the outgoing boundary is D³×S² and its belt is {0}×S². Radial motion
in the punctured D³ factor pushes each portion of B_j away from that
outgoing boundary to the attaching side. Applying this to the finite disjoint
family carries these attaching spheres into the original lower frontier.
The radial motions preserve disjointness because they arise from an ambient
collar isotopy on the complement of the belts, restricted to the compact
family in question.

Delete the interiors of these distinguished four-handles from the end
neighborhood to obtain the new frontier. This is now a geometrically defined
finite modification: their attaching spheres are on that frontier and their
interiors are disjoint. The remaining relative chain complex has no degree-four
kernel. Its degree-three cokernel is already zero in the buffered controlled
sense from the preceding relative three-connectivity. Excision transfers
these two statements to the new end-frontier pair.

Apply the separately reconstructed finite controlled homology-to-homotopy
operation through dimension six, with the required finite sequence of
buffered maps. This gives actual controlled relative nullhomotopies for
the finite cell structure. Extending those cell homotopies by the finite
mapping-cylinder HEP gives the desired approximate-completion deformation.
In the special product-end case there is no hidden infinite-cell argument:
all frontier changes lie in a compact height band, and the untouched deeper
tail is pushed into a finite slab by the explicit vertical clamp. That clamp
is the identity below its chosen level, which can be chosen above all the
removed handles, so it preserves the new frontier.

This completes the specified finite kernel-handle operation once its listed
controlled projection, frontier-lifting and relative three-connectivity data
are actually supplied. Each is a concrete previous finite operation; none
can be omitted when assembling a full proof of approximate completion.
Countable Choice is sufficient for the retained local handle/Whitney inputs;
this finite operation adds no stronger choice principle.
