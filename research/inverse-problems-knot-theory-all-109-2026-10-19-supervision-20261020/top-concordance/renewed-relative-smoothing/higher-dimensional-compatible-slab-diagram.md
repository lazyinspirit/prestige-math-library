# Compatible finite slab diagram in each actual dimension n≥6

This is the separately tracked higher-dimensional counterpart of
`compatible-three-four-slab-diagram.md`. It does not alter the n=6 frozen
review inputs. Its geometric operations are the finite operations enumerated
in `higher-dimensional-handle-extension.md`; their supplied controlled
homotopies and frontier lifting hypotheses remain explicit. No stable
smoothing or unproved product-structure conclusion is used as a handle move.

Put q=n−3. In the product-end problem W_j=∅, so the relative homological
maps after every frontier cut are identities of the same newly cut pair.
The finite controlled Hurewicz operation therefore gives actual relative
q-connectivity of each output (U_i,A_i). The stage count is finite for each
fixed n. This is exactly the special W=∅ alternative in Quinn §7.3,
printed p.318; it is stronger than a map between distinct frontier pairs.

Choose three consecutive retained output neighborhoods U_i⊃U_j⊃U_l
with disjoint reserved height intervals for their frontiers. Write
K=closure(U_i−U_j), J=closure(U_j−U_l). Excision and the actual annular
one-connected lifting data identify the relative homology of (U_i,K)
through degree q with that of (U_j,A_j). Finite controlled Hurewicz then
makes (U_i,K) relatively q-connected. For d≤q−1, contract any relative
d-disk in (U_i,A_i). Its contraction is a relative (d+1)-disk in (U_i,K);
move that contraction into K, fixing its boundary. Thus (K,A_i) is actually
relatively (q−1)-connected. The same reasoning applies to (J,A_j).
Since q−1≥2, the already established bottom relative connectivity makes
π₁(A_i)→π₁(K) an isomorphism with its controlled buffers. The frontier's
approximate two-complex lifting contracts its small loops, hence small
loops in K contract in K. For a path γ in K with endpoints on A_j,
controlled path lifting supplies η⊂A_j with the same endpoints; contract
γη⁻¹ in the same K. This gives actual relative one-connectivity from A_j.
The identical argument applies to J from A_l. A merely buffered annular
nullhomotopy is not assumed to lie in K.

Normalize K from below by eliminating indices through q−1=n−4, using the
higher-index operations. Normalize from above through dual index one.
Every intermediate operation is relative to both actual boundary collars.
The new handles created in these stages have indices at most n−2 from
below and at least n−3 when read from above. Thus precisely indices q and
q+1 remain. Perform the same normalization on J while preserving its actual
A_j collar. Concatenation along that shared collar gives an actual
L=K∪_(A_j)J whose handle presentation extends the chosen one on K and
has no index q+2 handles. Separate abstract decompositions of the same
underlying slab would not supply this compatibility.

Choose the constant-height clamp r_s in U_i with its terminal height c
above every A_i modification and below the lower A_j bound. Then
r(U_i)⊂K and r fixes A_i. Choose A_l beyond the upper A_j bound, which
makes r_s(K)⊂L. Let g_s contract the q-skeleton of K into A_i and extend
it over its finite (q+1)-handles by the explicit controlled cylinder HEP.
The terminal selfmap h=r g_1 has range in K. The homotopy

r_s|K followed by r g_s

lies in L: its first part does by the height bounds and its second part
lies in K regardless of how deeply the original g_s runs. Cellularize h
in K and this homotopy in the compatible L. Since C_(q+2)(L,A_i)=0,
the actual chain identities are dh_(q+1)=0 and T d=1−h_(q+1).
Consequently h_(q+1) is an idempotent with image ker d. The forbidden
higher boundary term is absent because of the actual handle presentation,
not omitted from the chain equation.

For completeness, the regular-value disk normal form used in both the
higher-index normalization and frontier cuts has the same actual model.
For a p-disk map in a p-handle D^p×D^(n−p), with p<n, put the finite maps
in relative general position and choose a regular value of the core
projection outside the projected multiple-point locus. The latter locus
has dimension at most 2p−n<p; hence this avoidance is possible even when
the disk maps themselves have positive-dimensional double sets. The
preimage of the chosen regular value is finite. A sufficiently small core
ball has disjoint inverse-function preimage balls, whose images are
disjoint graphs because their normal coordinates at that value are distinct.
The complementary maps radially move to the attaching side in the core
coordinate. The selected graphs become parallel outgoing core disks.
The finite trees connecting them have disjoint embedded image arcs in the
(n−1)-level because 1+p<n−1 in each use (p≤n−3). Thin bands give actual
disjoint embedded p-disks, with their product normal frames joined along
the bands. This is a normal form for the selected disks, not a claim that
the original entire p-disk maps embed. Carry these disks and frames through
the boundary Whitney isotopies by extending the isotopy over the collar
and carrying the subsequent handle attaching data.

All numbers of handles, disks, homotopies and stages here are finite for
fixed n. The same retained Countable Choice hypotheses and independent
height-band selection apply. Actual matrix/path realization at the dimension of the literal product-end
base, and the controlled two-ended slab-product criterion, are separate
required interfaces. In a one-factor desuspension from W⁵×R^k that base is
W⁵×R^(k−1); a base-five pass does not by itself supply its missing parameter
control. This compatible diagram does not claim to provide either input.
