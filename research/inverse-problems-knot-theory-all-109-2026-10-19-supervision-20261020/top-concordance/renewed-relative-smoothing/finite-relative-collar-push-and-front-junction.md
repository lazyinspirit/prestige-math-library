# Finite collar-push operation and the exact relative-front junction

NEW local construction, not independently reviewed. This does not claim the whole marked product theorem. The actual primary is Mazur III5–6,III9 and IV5, acquired/read in `strong-product-primary-proof-boundary.md`. No accepted proof was reread.

## The finite push that is supplied here

Let L be a compact finite PL manifold, with any boundary faces, and let

    c:L×[-2,2] -> U

be an ACTUAL nonsingular PD bicollar with its actual inverse onto its image in the smooth ambient manifold. All coordinate structures and finite triangulations are given. Let G0 be a compact protected subset and G1 an open neighborhood of it in the bicollar. Suppose a finite subpolyhedral neighborhood Z of its projection to L has been chosen with the whole smaller packet c(Z×[-2,2]) inside G1. This is a local compact-packet condition, not a claim about arbitrary closed open-germ sets. Let b be a nonnegative finite PL function on L, zero on a neighborhood of Z and of any side boundary that is to be fixed. A specified smaller source patch outside these sets can be made b=1 by a finite subdivision and assignment of vertex values.

Choose a finite triangulation of L×[-2,2]×I respecting the zero packets, the levels s=−2,−1,0,1,2, and t=0,1. Give its vertices displacement values t b(x) chi(s), where chi is the PL hat equal1 at s=0 and0 for |s|≥1. Interpolate these FINITELY many values affinely on each simplex; call the resulting jointly PL function h(x,s,t). This is an actual joint PL function, not the generally non-PL product t b(x)chi(s). It is identically0 on the marked and side packets and near the collar's two ends, and on t=0. Along the middle slice s=0,t=1 its values equal b after a common refinement. Let C be the maximum absolute s-direction slope on the finitely many affine simplices. Choose a positive rational delta with delta C<1/2 (if C=0 any sufficiently small positive rational works).

Then

    P(x,s,t)=(x,s+delta h(x,s,t),t)

is a jointly PL embedding, onto the whole product, preserving x and t. To check this without an isotopy theorem: on every line with x,t fixed its second coordinate is continuous, piecewise affine and strictly increasing with slope≥1/2, and it equals s near both ends. It is therefore a bijection of that interval. On each simplex its affine derivative has determinant≥1/2; its inverse is affine on the finite image triangulation. Thus P and its inverse are jointly PL. The compact parameter product makes inverse continuity automatic as well.

Conjugate in the supplied collar and extend by identity outside it. The track is a nonsingular PD isotopy in the actual collar triangulation; it is literally identity on G0 and the side and outside U. Consequently it is genuinely smooth there in ANY prescribed smooth structure, regardless of whether the collar itself was smooth there. Its effect at time1 is the front c(x,delta b(x)) on the middle slice. It retains the actual side and endpoint labels. If the given bicollar is itself a smooth product on some further packet and the PL displacement has a crease in that packet, no smoothness there is asserted: use the identity buffer when exact smooth germs are required.

All ingredients are finite: common subdivision, vertex assignments, slope bounds, a first qualifying rational delta, and a finite image triangulation. This operation uses no arbitrary choice, no PL isotopy-extension theorem, and no smoothing product theorem. A finite list of such pushes, and their inverses, remains a finite construction; parameter-end stationary intervals are obtained by a piecewise-linear time change. For a cylinder the protected time-end collars are additional zero packets. This is the actual finite support/marked push mechanism missing from the shorthand coordinate translation in Mazur III6.

A smooth function used inside a merely PD collar cannot silently replace this PL interpolation: compositions may fail to have a finite linear subdivision. The joint-PL construction above avoids that additional assumption. Smoothness on the prescribed smaller packets follows from exact identity, not from an unsupported PD-composition assertion.

## What is still required to bind Mazur's relative product proof

Mazur III6 moves the ENTIRE boundary to its derived-neighborhood front. The construction above, when it is fixed on an already smooth marked packet, instead leaves the old boundary there. Therefore its target front is a patched front, not the unmodified second-derived front. The next step needs an ACTUAL transverse field on this patched front, agreeing with a fixed smooth normal field on a smaller marked packet. Generic PL collar existence alone does not give it.

The finite interface required from the relative approximation branch is precise: in finitely many smooth marked product charts, arrange that the replacement front and its tangent/secant cones are uniformly close to the original smooth slice on a compact transition annulus, while retaining the exact smaller germ. On that annulus the original smooth normal field is then transverse; outside it use the join-line transverse field from the second-derived front. Choose the outside field in the same coorientation. In a local graph chart, transversality can be expressed by strict positive evaluation on the cooriented normal covectors. Hence a convex combination of the two fields with a finite smooth partition remains transverse, provided BOTH fields lie in this common positive cone on the overlap. The closeness estimate must establish that premise; positivity is not inferred merely from the words “outward field.” Extending/approximating the resulting continuous field smoothly with a fixed smaller germ then feeds Mazur I's graph smoothing.

The low K=S0,S1,S2 and cylinder cases have finite triangulations and finitely many protected end/side packets, so this is a finite interface even for arbitrary fixed finite stabilization r. The required estimate is not a restriction that total stabilized dimension≤3. Relative_smoothing owns the Whitehead finite PD-to-linear approximation proof; no duplicate proof or acceptance is claimed here.

Finally IV5's boundary reduction must retain these packets when extending across a boundary collar and when smoothing the boundary again. A finite corner double with prescribed compatible product collars can convert it to this same local problem, but those collars and their marking comparisons must actually be given/constructed. A full arbitrary open-set-relative product theorem or arbitrary parameter fibration is not exported from this local push. The exact missing supplier is now the finite relative-front/transverse-cone compatibility, followed by its finite boundary-collar gluing—not a new homotopy-sphere or surgery theorem.


## Explicit graph junction once the relative front is supplied

Here is the precise remaining geometric input, and the actual construction after it. In a compact transition packet let the already smooth slice be S, with specified smooth flow coordinates theta:S×(−a,a)→W. Suppose the outside front A is ACTUALLY a one-sheeted graph theta(x,u(x)), with u nonsingular PD on a supplied finite triangulation of S. Its graph identification, including its inverse, is part of the input; C0 proximity alone is insufficient. Give nested finite subpolyhedral packets Z0⊂int Z1 in this graph base, and choose a finite PL function lambda, zero near Z0 and one off Z1. Then replace the graph height u by lambda u. This is PD on a common finite refinement: u and lambda are smooth on each simplex and theta is a genuinely SMOOTH chart. The t-family with height ((1−t)+t lambda)u is jointly PD on the simplex prisms; it is always a graph and hence an embedding with inverse given by projection. On Z0 its endpoint is exactly the old smooth slice. On the outside it remains exactly A. These are actual marked identifications.

The smooth vertical field in theta coordinates is transverse to this entire patched graph: on the finite compact triangulation the height functions have a finite derivative/Lipschitz bound, giving a positive uniform angle from vertical to every secant. On the outside overlap, require the preexisting field of A to have the same coorientation. For a PL/smooth graph each facet has an oriented normal covector; transverse vectors of the positive side have strictly positive pairing with every incident facet normal. These finitely many inequalities define an open convex cone. A finite smooth partition combines the vertical field with the outside field inside this cone, and compactness gives a positive margin. A sufficiently close finite-chart smooth approximation, equal on the smaller marked germ, retains that margin. This proves the actual FIELD-PATCH part; it does not prove the missing graph presentation of the derived front.

A candidate way to discharge the protected-union premise of Whitehead6 at this chosen scope is to use the GIVEN smooth marked codimension-zero product as reference, rather than an arbitrary fixed ambient triangulation. Choose the original PD source subdivision and the marked product grid in common refinement; use a buffer/subdivision so every source simplex meeting cl(V) lies wholly in the larger smooth product packet. Supply the reference ambient chart complexes there by this SAME product triangulation. Choose other finite compact ambient chart packets disjoint from cl(V); the larger product packet covers the boundary of cl(V), so a finite cover of the remaining compact region exists. Then the pieces interacting with cl(V) are literally subcomplexes of one nonsingular product union. Whether the subsequent second-derived collar/front has the required one-sheeted graph in that packet still needs its actual finite combinatorial construction. No such graph claim is inferred from compatible union alone.
