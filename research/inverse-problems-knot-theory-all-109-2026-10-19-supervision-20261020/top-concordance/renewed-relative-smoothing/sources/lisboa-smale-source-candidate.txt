PDFPAGE3
Isometries of the sphere
n-sphere Sn = {x ∈Rn+1 : ||x|| = 1}
Deﬁnition
An isometry is a bijection f : Sn →Sn which preserves distances.
n = 1: rotation by an angle θ (and reﬂection)
n = 2: rotation about a line in R3 by an angle θ (and reﬂection).
Any isometry f : Sn →Sn can be extended to a linear isometry
F : Rn+1 →Rn+1
,
F(x) = f (x/||x||)||x||
i.e. an orthogonal transformation. That is,
Isom(Sn) ∼= O(n + 1)


PDFPAGE4
Smooth symmetries of the sphere
isometries ⊂smooth symmetries
A smooth symmetry – aka diﬀeomorphism – is a smooth map
Sn →Sn with smooth inverse.
O(n + 1) ⊂Diﬀ(Sn)
⇝”Most” smooth symmetries aren’t isometries.

Exotic smooth bundles
Diﬀ(Sn) ≃O(n + 1) × Diﬀ∗(Sn)
So Watanabe’s result is equivalent to
Diﬀ∗(S4) ̸≃∗
Strategy: Test with spheres,
{Sk−1 →Diﬀ∗(S4)} ⇆{smooth Sk-families of (pointed) 4-spheres}

trivial, i.e. a product Sk × S4
