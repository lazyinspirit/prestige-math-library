# Finite relative connectivity-to-index elimination in dimensions14 and15

NEW proof, for first independent review. This is a handle-presentation theorem, not a normal-surgery or controlled-carrier theorem. Its primary source is Wall, *Differential Topology*, PartIV, printed13–25, especially4.1,5.1 and5.5; the actual public207-page scan is cached in `../renewed-marked-torus/sources/wall-handle-decompositions.pdf`. Those source pages were actually read. No accepted proof body was read.

## Claim

Assume ACω. Let W be an actual compact smooth n-dimensional cobordism triad, n∈{14,15}, with connected nonempty incoming face M, outgoing face M′, and a specified product side ∂M×I, with its genuine nested product collars. Suppose W is connected, the incoming map π₁(M)→π₁(W) is an isomorphism, and an actual finite index-ordered smooth handle presentation relative to M is supplied, all attachments away from the fixed side collar. Let0≤q≤6 and suppose π_i(W,M)=0 for1≤i≤q. Then W has a finite index-ordered handle presentation relative to the SAME incoming and side product germs with no handles of index≤q.

The presentation is exported with an actual comparison to the original W, fixed on the incoming and side collars. Outgoing labels and later attaching maps are carried by that comparison. No independent outgoing pointwise-fixing requirement is asserted. Equivalently put the new handle images into the original physical W; then that W and any maps or bundle data already on it are unchanged.

In particular, given an actual map F:W→Z, a vector bundle ξ on Z, and an actual stable bundle isomorphism B:ν_W⊕ε^a≅F*ξ⊕ε^b, the new presentation retains these data by restriction on the physical W, or by pullback under its displayed comparison. This operation constructs no new normal-surgery trace and assumes no extension of B that was not already given.

## 1. The finite relative general-position operation needed in low indices

We use only r=1,2 in this paragraph, in a smooth target P of dimension m=n−1≥13. Compact disk/sphere maps and their homotopies can first be made smooth relative to already smooth collars. One finite construction is to embed a compact target neighborhood into a finite-dimensional Euclidean space using finitely many chart bumps and their coordinate functions, use a tubular retraction near its compact image, and approximate in Euclidean coordinates by a finite source partition of unity. Relative cutoffs keep the given smooth collars. This gives a smooth map and its short homotopy, fixed on those collars. All target neighborhoods and source partitions are finite.

Here are the actual transversality families. For a compact-source map with a free interior region, finitely many local target-coordinate perturbations with source bumps span values and first source derivatives on that region; the coefficients of coordinate-linear source functions supply the derivative directions. The universal1-jet map is transverse to each rank-deficient matrix stratum. Their smallest codimension is m−r+1, which exceeds r for a disk and exceeds r+1 for an r-sphere homotopy when n≥2r+3. Apply the stated finite parametric transversality interface to these finitely many chart families. A good parameter therefore makes every required time slice immersive.

After this perturbation a compact family of immersions has a uniform local injectivity radius in the source coordinates. To obtain global injectivity, restrict distinct-source pairs to distances at least half that radius. This is a compact set. Use finitely many bump pairs which independently vary the two values at such pairs; the universal difference map is transverse to the target diagonal. The expected collision dimension for an r-sphere homotopy is2r+1−m<0. Thus another arbitrarily small good parameter removes all slice double points. For disk maps the bound is still better. Protected pairs have no collisions, and an active/protected pair requires varying only its active value. This constructs an embedding isotopy relative to its stationary end collars whenever the endpoints were embeddings and a homotopy between them was supplied. The same finite construction makes a compact map disjoint from finitely many specified submanifolds when the relevant dimension sum is less than m. Near the fixed time collars, where embeddings and avoidance are already given, perturbations vanish.

Consequently a nullhomotopic embedded S^r⊂Int(P), r=1,2, is isotopic to a small standard sphere: choose a homotopy to that sphere, smooth it relative its embedding endpoints, and use the preceding parameter construction. Apply the actual compact-source isotopy-extension Statement, taking supports inside Int(P). Transporting a standard spanning disk and its standard attaching normal frame gives an ACTUAL spanning disk and ACTUAL framing for that particular embedded sphere. This conclusion concerns the NEW sphere so constructed; it is not the invalid claim that an arbitrary old framing became trivial merely because its core was nullhomotopic.

The same construction permits small attaching normal disks to be shrunk around their cores. The embeddings (u,v)↦f(u,a_tv), a_t>0 stationary at its ends, form an actual compact-source isotopy and carry the attaching framings. This is a presentation change with a displayed diffeomorphism, not an unproved change in the manifold. It lets a finite set disjoint from attaching cores be disjoint from the full smaller attaching neighborhoods when needed below.

## 2. Remove index0 handles geometrically

The0- and1-handles give a finite graph: its vertices are the connected incoming component and the0-handles; each1-handle joins the components containing its two attaching disks. This graph is connected, because handles of index≥2 do not join components and W is connected. Choose a finite rooted spanning tree, rooted at M. Cancel a leaf0-handle with the tree1-handle joining it toward the root. Its0-handle belt is its entire boundary sphere; the chosen1-handle has exactly one end on it. The geometric cancellation Statement therefore applies, with every other attaching map carried along. Repeating removes every0-handle. The operations occur in the interior of the outgoing face and retain the incoming and side collars. This argument uses connectivity, not a nonexistent group-ring π₀ calculation.

## 3. Inductive topology at the beginning of degree r

Assume all handles below r have been removed, for1≤r≤q. Keep the base V=M×I. Let T be the prefix through all r- and(r+1)-handles. Later handles have index≥r+2. The finite relative CW comparison and cellular approximation show that their addition does not change π_i relative to M for i≤r. Hence π_r(T,M)=0, and there are no relative lower cells. All comparisons carry the supplied collars and their base labels.

For r≥3 the incoming cover of M induced from W is its universal cover, since the incoming π₁ map is an isomorphism. Applying finite relative cellular approximation to T and then the choice-free Hurewicz comparison on its simply connected cover gives H_r(T̃,M̃;Z)=0. With no cells below r, this says that the finite handle-chain map

    d:C_(r+1)(T̃,M̃) → C_r(T̃,M̃)

is onto. Thus for each chosen lower generator e_j there is an actual finite chain c with dc=e_j. Every coefficient is a finite sum of signed group elements; no projective-module or preferred-basis theorem is required for this assertion.

Here the group at the geometric level is not silently replaced by a group after killing loops. Before degree r≥3 the base is exactly M×I. Attaching r-handles changes its outgoing boundary by surgery on(r−1)-spheres. These spheres have dimension≥2 and codimension n−r≥3; their complements have the same π₁ as M, and the replacement disk/sphere pieces introduce no generator or relation in π₁, by van Kampen. The same holds for the(r+1)-handles. Hence the partial outgoing levels have π₁=M's π₁, identified with π₁(W). This earns the exact boundary π₁ hypothesis used for the group labels. The original low-index construction below does not require this argument at r=1,2.

## 4. Trade degree1 and2 handles using actual new framed spheres

Fix r∈{1,2}. All same-index lower handles may be permuted, so put the chosen r-handle last. Write its attaching chart as S^(r−1)×D^(n−r) and its body as D^r×D^(n−r). Its relative core class vanishes in π_r(T,M). A nullhomotopy of this actual relative class gives a map g:D^r→M with the same attaching boundary, and a filling in T of the sphere formed by the core and g. Relative boundary homotopies can be placed in the attaching collar, so this assertion keeps the specified boundary parametrization. For r=1 the disk means a path and uses the connected incoming M.

By the finite low-index general-position construction, make g embedded off its prescribed boundary collar, avoiding the other lower attaching core spheres and the chosen attaching sphere except along that collar. The dimension sum is r+(r−1)<n−1. Shrink all lower attaching normal disks, keeping their cores and their actual presentation comparisons, so the compact part of g outside its prescribed rim misses their full attaching neighborhoods. At the chosen rim use the displayed product attaching collar. Give its rim a small nonzero displacement in a chosen normal direction v₀ of the old attaching chart. Extend this displacement over a slightly larger collar with a cutoff, zero on the remaining disk; sufficiently small C¹ displacement preserves its embedding. Joining its displaced rim to the corresponding disk D^r×{v₀} of the new outgoing handle face produces an embedded sphere A⊂P, where P is the level after the r-handles. It crosses the chosen belt {0}×S^(n−r−1) once and misses all other lower belts. These properties are retained in a small neighborhood of the one intersection and by sufficiently small perturbations elsewhere.

Make A disjoint from the finitely many(r+1)-handle attaching core spheres, relative that intersection neighborhood. The dimension estimate is r+r<m. Choose v₀ off their finite transverse intersection set with the belt. Shrink their normal attaching neighborhoods as necessary by the actual attaching isotopy described in Section1, retaining the induced presentation comparisons. Thus A lies in a common part of P and the outgoing boundary P′ of T. Its homotopy class in T is zero, by the supplied relative-core filling; the normal displacement changes only its representative.

There is an elementary way to certify that A is nullhomotopic in P′ without assuming a framing. Reverse T. Its dual presentation has handles of indices n−r and n−r−1. Thus the pair(T,P′) has no relative cells below n−r−1. Finite relative cellular approximation gives π_i(T,P′)=0 for i<n−r−1. Since n≥2r+3, this includes i=r+1. The homotopy sequence then makes π_r(P′)→π_r(T) injective. Therefore A is actually nullhomotopic in P′.

Section1 now supplies an ambient isotopy of Int(P′) between A and a small standard sphere, together with a transported STANDARD framing and actual spanning disk. Introduce the standard new cancelling pair a^(r+1),b^(r+2) in that small ball and carry the whole pair by the isotopy so that a's attaching sphere is exactly A with that transported framing. A's attaching neighborhood lies in the common portion avoiding all old upper attaching neighborhoods. It can therefore be commuted before those old(r+1)-handles. Its single intersection with the chosen old r-belt is still the actual geometric intersection just constructed. Cancel that old r-handle with a, carrying all other handles and b. The remaining new handle b has index r+2. No pre-existing framed attaching sphere was declared trivial; the framing was constructed for the new sphere from a genuine isotopy to a standard model.

Repeat for all degree-r handles. Each trade removes one such handle and creates none of that degree. The number of operations is finite. All sphere maps, tracks, and collars are compact in the interior of the relevant level; normal product collars let their preliminary homotopies be pushed off the fixed side. Ambient isotopy extension is then applied with support in that interior. The incoming and side product germs remain untouched.

## 5. Trade degrees3 through6 by labelled slides and ordinary Whitney moves

Let3≤r≤q. Choose an r-handle h_j and the finite chain c of Section3 with dc=e_j. On the level after the r-handles choose a small ball disjoint from the old upper attaching neighborhoods and the fixed side collar. Such a ball exists after shrinking those finitely many normal neighborhoods: their core spheres have dimension r<m and cannot fill that level. Introduce the actual standard pair a^(r+1),b^(r+2) in this ball. Its support is disjoint from the old upper attachments, so b can be put after the old(r+1)-handles; initially a has zero attaching-belt coefficients against every old r-handle.

Slide a over the old(r+1)-handles according to the finitely many signed group elements in c. Here is the labelled calculation. Fix basepoint whiskers and oriented lifts of the handle cores. Choose a band arc from the attaching sphere of a to that of an old upper handle in the specified relative homotopy class g. The arcs can be embedded and made disjoint from the other attaching cores, with their endpoint product sectors fixed: their dimensions are one, the core spheres have dimension r, and1+r<m. A tubular product thickens the arc, using the actual attaching frames at its two ends. The band sum is the genuine framed handle slide. In the universal cover, the added core chain is the chosen lift of the old core translated by the deck element specified by g, with sign chosen by the band orientation. Hence it adds that signed monomial to the coefficient of the old core in a's chain. Iterating over the finite expression gives new chain e_a+c, so its old lower boundary is exactly e_j. This proves the Λ action geometrically, rather than importing an integer-only matrix Statement with changed coefficients. The new b and all later attaching maps are transported by the supported slide diffeomorphisms.

Put the attaching sphere of a transverse to the lower belts. Against belt j its labelled signed coefficient is1; against every other lower belt it is zero. Pair all unwanted intersections with equal group label and opposite local sign. They define nullhomotopic Whitney circles in the ACTUAL level, because its π₁ identification was earned in Section3. At this stage the spheres have dimensions r and n−r−1, both at least three. The accepted high-dimensional Whitney operation removes each pair. Its disk interior can also avoid all other lower belts and upper attaching core spheres: the relevant dimension sums are2+(n−r−1)<n−1 and2+r<n−1. The finite relative transversality construction supplies this additional avoidance, with the boundary and prescribed corners fixed. Shrink the other attaching normal neighborhoods if needed; the supported isotopies then carry the whole attaching data. Continue until a meets belt j once and every other belt not at all.

Permute equal-index handles to put h_j last among lower handles and a first among upper handles. Cancel h_j and a by the actual one-geometric-intersection theorem. The upper new handle b remains, of index r+2; all other handles are carried along and retain their indices and framings. Its original complementary status with a was used only to create the new pair; after sliding it is not asserted that this status remained unchanged. The cancellation with the OLD h_j is justified independently by the new geometric single intersection.

This decreases the number of degree-r handles by one. Recompute the finite chain for the remaining presentation when necessary; the physical W and its relative connectivity have not changed. Repeat finitely often.

## 6. Iteration, retained normal data, and the normal-map input boundary

Run Section2 and then Sections4–5 for successive r≤q. A trade introduces only index r+2, so it never reintroduces a previously removed degree. The new(r+2)-handle is already after the(r+1)-block; later handles are carried by the displayed comparison. Equal-index permutations preserve index order. Thus after finitely many stages the presentation has no handles of index≤q.

Every pair insertion, slide, isotopy, or cancellation is an actual relative diffeomorphism or actual change of handle images in the same physical W. Keep the maps F and B on that physical W. If P_new is used as an abstract handle domain with comparison φ:P_new→W, set F_new=Fφ and B_new to the differential pullback of B. This is the actual stable bundle comparison; it agrees with the given data on the fixed incoming and side collars because φ is the identity there. There is no appeal to characteristic-class equality and no attempt to extend a bundle isomorphism over a new surgery trace. This is why the theorem does not silently use the normal-surgery proposition outside its stated closed-source scope.

The input can be supplied by a preceding normal-map construction as follows, but that construction is NOT proved here. If F:W→X×I is(q+1)-connected and f₀=F|M is q-connected, the mapping-cylinder triple gives

    π_(i+1)(F) → π_i(W,M) → π_i(f₀) → π_i(F).

Thus its end groups vanish for i≤q and give the required pair connectivity, with the corresponding fundamental-group assertion. In the intended q=6,n14/15 applications, a7-connected normal bordism map with6-connected incoming map is sufficient. Merely naming a normal map6-connected, or invoking a closed normal-surgery theorem on a triad, does not supply these input groups. The extra odd-dimensional handle-subtraction step that can change the outgoing end to kill a remaining relative kernel is also not part of this same-W theorem.

## 7. Choice ledger and exact export

There are finitely many handles at every stage and finitely many stages. The relative homotopy hypotheses supply one witness for each core when used; only finitely many are selected in a given finite presentation. The surjectivity dc=e_j supplies finite group-ring chains. Each such chain has finitely many signed monomials, so only finitely many bands and Whitney pairings are required. No basis of a projective module or arbitrary infinite family of geometric representatives is chosen.

The manifold coordinate data used in each compact track are a finite subcover. Relative smooth approximation, the finite1-jet and two-point parameter constructions, and compact-source isotopy extension have the declared finite/CC scope above. There is no use of the full-AC relative Hurewicz Statement: the simply connected cover comparison uses the separate choice-free weak-model Statement with supplied characteristic maps. Universal-cover handle chains have finitely many Λ generators; individual group-ring elements have finite support even if the deck group is infinite. The remaining existing geometric operations state CC. Thus the proof uses at most ACω on the supplied finite smooth data. It gives no CC theorem for the independent complete Wall surgery/fibering calculation.

The export is precisely finite UNCONTROLLED lower-index elimination with actual incoming/side germs and actual presentation comparisons. It supplies neither quantitative carrier bounds nor upper-index elimination simultaneously, neither generic14-dimensional fibering nor normal-map connectivity/B-trace existence, and neither stable kernel duality nor realization of a preferred kernel basis by middle handles. Those are separate owned inputs/consumers.
