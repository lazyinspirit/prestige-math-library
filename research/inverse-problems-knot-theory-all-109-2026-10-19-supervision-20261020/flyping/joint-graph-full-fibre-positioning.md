## Which side of the lower traces contains the white set

The finite positive-corner state rule keeps every lower return adjacent to ONE black core face, rather than crossing the core of a positive band into another black face. Use that actual face and its component cut by gamma. A type7 guide excludes a second gamma black common-face side there. At a terminal tube end the type8 guide forces incidence with its indicated black common-face side. Following the standard crossing quarter then gives the following closed-ear supports. This is an incidence list on the physical common/tube/crossing faces; arbitrarily many old positive-band boundary arcs may occur in its black-side boundary chain.

| Return endpoints | Gamma-side closure of its ear | Interior support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B+ to B− | the single common black side between the two named tube ends | that black-core component and its boundary collars |
| lower U to W+ | upper overpass-side quarter, the adjacent black side, the terminal ordinary upper interval ending at W+ | the chosen black crossing quarter, black-core component, and ordinary tube collar |
| lower S to W+ | the selected upper saddle-side quarter, its black side, the terminal ordinary upper interval ending at W+ | the first empty black saddle quarter, black-core component, and ordinary tube collar |

The quarter and tube collars in the last two rows terminate AT the W+ endpoint; they do not continue through a common white arc. Their interiors are on the black side of the corresponding W page. Thus the ears miss the entire projected white set W intersect int(T), including the white page's crossing-face traces, not just the open planar white regions. In the U row this is the actual T-side black quarter, not the opposite black sector; in the S row it is the first empty quarter supplied by the saddle ordering. An alleged farther common side or terminal edge would give exactly the type7/8 guide used to name the return, not an additional allowed ear.

These are empty ears for the COMPLETE lower boundary family. The open black-core portion has no F trace because it projects into the innermost upper disk interior. The tube collar contains no second old boundary section over its longitudinal interval. Each positive-band corner collar has just its one fixed common-band trace; another crossing piece would meet the occupied axis. The first saddle quarter is empty by its finite order. These exhaust the physical support cells listed in the table. Hence an additional lower trace cannot lie in an ear. Two ears have disjoint interiors: disjoint proper arcs in a disk have disjoint or nested boundary-side ears, and nesting would put a second return in the interior of an empty ear.

A closed lower trace wholly inside T likewise follows one black core's boundary, by the same corner-state rule. Its bounded disk side is that black core with its small boundary collars and misses the white set. Take the outermost such disks. They are mutually disjoint and disjoint from all the boundary ears: an ear is empty, and a compact inner disk cannot contain the boundary interval of an ear on partial T.

Deleting the interiors of these disjoint boundary ears and inner black disks leaves one connected central region (a disk with finitely many holes) containing EVERY white point of int(T). Choose a white root t there. For any global lower boundary circle Gamma, its intersection with T consists of some of the listed lower returns or a whole inner circle. Every complementary central-region path misses Gamma. Consequently every white point is on the SAME sphere side of Gamma as t. The sphere disk Delta_Gamma avoiding t therefore misses the ENTIRE white set in int(T). This conclusion allows a global Gamma to have several return occurrences. It asserts no one-contact property for its global disk.

## Actual simultaneous graph placement

Now apply the finite actual-disk pocket sweeps of renewed-prism-model.md, relative all boundary traces, using those root-avoiding domains Delta_Gamma. For nested domains choose outer domes below inner domes; use their fixed inward boundary germs and strict finite interior height separation. The actual lower disks become projection-injective graphs over these domains. Since every domain excludes the white set, there is NO lower F point on a vertical fibre through any inner white point. This exclusion is a consequence of the chosen actual domains, so it is preserved by the positioning, not silently imported after an arbitrary isotopy.

For the upper disks, the chosen X has boundary gamma and is positioned as a graph over T. Every other upper boundary circle avoids int(T) by innermostness. On the upper reference sphere choose its side avoiding the same t; that side lies outside int(T). Indeed its boundary does not enter int(T), so membership in either side is constant on the connected interior T, and the chosen side excludes t. The finite upper pocket sweeps, with X's boundary side held and the other pockets on their actual disjoint sides, position all other upper disks over those outside domains. They therefore contribute no point on any inner white fibre. These upper sweeps have disjoint support from the lower sweeps; their common boundary traces remain fixed.

X supplies exactly one point on each inner white fibre by its graph property. All other upper and lower pieces are absent there, and no crossing/tube piece enters such a fibre on the white-set side just excluded by the physical table. Thus the FULL intersection F with that fibre is a singleton lying in X. This earns both parts of the simultaneous normalization used by the renewed prism proof.
