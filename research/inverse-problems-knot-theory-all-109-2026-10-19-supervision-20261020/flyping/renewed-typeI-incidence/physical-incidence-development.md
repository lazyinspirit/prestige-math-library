## 1. A genuine common pole for all lower graph patches

Use the actual joint-graph/full-fibre input supplied by the owner: choose the open WHITE point t in the central Y1, outside A, at which the full F fibre consists of the single upper-X point. If a closed lower projected domain ΔΓ contained t, its lower graph would contribute another F point to this fibre. Hence t is outside EVERY ΔΓ. Also t is outside every rectangle A_i⊂A. Therefore all ΔΓ and A_i are Jordan disks in the SAME plane S²\\{t}, with the actual common omitted point supplied by the geometry, not merely inferred from injective projection.

For a particular Γ, the complement of ΔΓ∩A_i in S² is the union of the two connected complements of ΔΓ and A_i; they intersect at t. It is connected. After the permitted finite transverse cut/corner refinement, the boundary intersection is a finite one-manifold with corners. A component with a hole would have a boundary circle enclosing a component of the global complement, contradicting connectedness. Thus every component of ΔΓ∩A_i is a disk. Its lower graph QΓ⊂F∩H−∩P_i is consequently an ACTUAL disk. This does not imply that it meets a wall once, or that two occurrences on one global Γ are the same local patch.

The common pole is essential: two arbitrary Jordan disks on S² can have annular intersection if their union is S². The full-fibre point is the reason that example is excluded here.

## 2. The upper-saddle star is a disk without an F_i assumption

    K=X∪⋃ D_j
