# The uncut two-chamber union is not the required cap ball

NEW finite local observation, not a check of an accepted proof or a disproof of the Type-I construction. It diagnoses the failure of the proposed local tree-ball shortcut.

Use the actual normalized local lower reference ball, cut by its finite disjoint proper lower graph disks. Keep all face copies distinct. Its component adjacency graph is a tree: one proper disk separates a ball into two balls; each successive proper-disk cut splits one existing component and adds one edge. Induction preserves the tree property. This uses disjoint proper disks with disjoint boundary curves in the actual local reference sphere, not labels of global components.

The short outward-U meridian arc from the first lower attachment face to the second crosses the old peripheral section once and no other lower boundary. Thus in the cut reference sphere it passes directly from component G0 across the face copy of D_U,loc to G1. These are adjacent vertices of the tree, joined by the D_U,loc edge. In particular their uncut closures share this disk face. No additional disk can separate their banks along that face, since the disks are disjoint; its connected collar on each side belongs to one component. No second shared disk edge is possible in a tree.

Now add the actual U-side crossing ball B_U along its two disjoint lower disk faces Δ0 and Δ1, one to G0 and one to G1. If the D_U,loc bank copies remain separated, the three-ball diagram is a tree and defines a cut-open ball. If they are identified as in the uncut ambient prism, the graph has three vertices and THREE edges: the old D_U,loc edge and the two Δ edges. Its first Betti number is one. More explicitly, first join G0 and G1 along D_U,loc; the result is a ball. Attaching B_U along the two disjoint boundary disks Δ0 and Δ1 adds one handle. The actual union, after rounding its product corners, is a genus-one handlebody, hence a solid torus.

This conclusion uses the stated pairwise disjoint attachment disks and no further ambient identifications. Any degeneracy at a common boundary corner must first be separated by the actual small product collars; it cannot be silently declared an additional gluing disk.

A separated-bank buffered ball can still be placed in this solid torus: shrink the two old-disk bank faces into opposite normal collars before gluing the Δ faces. But its boundary has TWO physical displaced copies of D_U,loc, not one literal old-F face. Thus it does not automatically carry the prescribed retained seams. Applying a sphere-fill argument to that buffered ball requires the full cap cycle with exact bank occurrences and the corresponding seam collars. In particular it requires which physical boundary routes cross the meridional cut and how the free inner wall I and the exterior upper curtain complete those routes. The inner-wall boundary chain μ_U+μ_E1 alone does not supply this information.

The supplied upper-positioning interface is also insufficient by itself to decide these routes: nonselected upper disks avoid Y1 and admit projected boundary collars over their actual boundary intervals in A_i. It does not assert a one-contact condition, a dual forest, or an actual auxiliary Xi separating all protected saddle pages. All repetitions and bank occurrences remain part of the missing finite matching problem.

Therefore the cut-open tree calculation cannot be promoted to the requested physical Type-I ball/C/P interface without a new sheet-matching proof. This is a concrete obstacle to that shortcut, not evidence that the intended full Type-I cap is impossible.
