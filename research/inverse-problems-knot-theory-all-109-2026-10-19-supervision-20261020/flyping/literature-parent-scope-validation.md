## What could replace the flyping hypothesis

The word “flyping” is stronger than the particular lower-incidence conclusion explicitly used in this proof. The useful local conclusion is that the lower circle containing the underpass section also abuts the unique saddle, in the empty local quarter used for the depicted lower white push. A direct proof of that coincidence, together with the existing five-good nonincidence exclusions, could replace the global two-circle count. It would be a genuine additional geometric argument, not a correction of a label.

Writing the labels separately exposes the obligation: the saddle has its two lower incident banks, and the outward underpass section has its own lower-circle label. The source's flyping argument forces the latter to agree with a saddle-incident label. The mere existence of all these local pieces does not identify their global lower circles. A cap or current isotopy may join old pieces, so a post-cap connection is not an old-label certificate.

This audit does not claim that an arbitrary two-circle numerical bound alone proves every pictured local guide incidence. The indicated local geometry still has to be bound. It identifies the exact coincidence the printed argument needs, rather than promoting a general new lemma.

## Why the one-child and endpoint information does not presently supply it

Sublemma 5.2(A) constrains proper W-arcs that are **not** isotopic into the white core: such an arc must have an endpoint on the chosen child. It leaves the core-only W-arcs unconstrained in number. Part (B) permits tube-to-tube white arcs on the noninnermost, crossing-ball-free parent. Such arcs can accompany several parent overpass channels. Parts (C)–(D) require a saddle on the child and at least three upper circles; they give neither a numerical bound on the parent's white/core arcs nor an equality of the relevant lower-circle labels.

Even granting the source footnote's stronger nested/single-child situation, the region between parent and child is an annulus. The number of holes is one, but a planar annulus admits arbitrarily many parallel crosscuts. Thus counting children is not a bound of two on lower-circle incidences. This is a demonstration that the proposed elementary counting inference is insufficient, not a construction of a definite nine-good surface or a counterexample to the flyping theorem. Additional definiteness, guide, or actual peripheral geometry could impose restrictions; that additional argument is exactly what remains to be established.

In the current Type-I labels the underpass return and the two saddle banks have separate physical owners. The accepted distinctness assertion only identifies a global lower disk when two occurrences are proved to lie on it; it cannot create that identification from a new cap prefix. Consequently it does not fill the source application by itself.
