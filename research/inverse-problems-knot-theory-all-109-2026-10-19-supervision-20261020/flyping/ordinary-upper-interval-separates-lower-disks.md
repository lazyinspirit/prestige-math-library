# The precise lower-disk distinction needed at E1

Renewed user-authorized original105 development, `/root/flyping_repair`, 2026-10-20. This proves the particular global-disk distinction needed by the new typeIII construction. It does not assume or certify the broader old authored Proposition8.5-style claim about every pair of upper common-face arcs.

The primary reference newly read for this operation is Kindred v2 Proposition5.8, actual PDF25, and its proof; the actual TeX source labels it `P:BadArc`. The argument below supplies its ambient boundary-disk construction and the torus-boundary step explicitly.

## Exact claim

Let $L$ be a nontrivial nonsplit link and let $F$ be an essential spanning surface in the supplied good reference-ball position. In particular, $F$ is boundary-incompressible in the link exterior; every component of $F\cap H_-$ is a proper disk in the actual lower reference ball; and the old boundary is a longitudinal section of each tube. Let $e$ be an arc of $\partial F\cap S_+$ contained in one ordinary edge tube, with one endpoint on each of its two distinct common-face sides. Then the two endpoints of $e$ lie on different **global** disks of $F\cap H_-$.

At the typeIII ordinary interval $E1$, these two disks are precisely the ones carrying $B_1$ and $W_1$. Thus the claim proves their required distinction even if other common-face arcs elsewhere share a global disk.

## The actual complementary lower arc

Use the supplied product chart $I_s\times S^1_\theta$ on this ordinary edge tube. The upper half is $0<\theta<\pi$ after choosing the two common-face sections as its endpoints. The interval $e$ is its old upper graph. It has no other common-face endpoint in its interior, so its angular values stay in this open half-circle. Construct an arc $a$ in the lower half with the same endpoints, longitudinally returning across the same parameter interval. Explicitly, if the forward upper graph runs from angle $0$ to $\pi$, take a forward lower graph with angles from $2\pi$ to $\pi$, keeping its interior in $(\pi,2\pi)$, and traverse it backwards when closing with $e$. Its endpoint collars are the actual lower torus collars. Then

$$
[e\cup a]=\pm\mu\quad\text{in }H_1(T;\mathbb Z),
$$

where $T$ is this link component's actual tube torus and $\mu$ its primitive meridian. The longitudinal winding is zero and angular winding is one. The arc $a$ misses $\partial F$ except at the two endpoints: on this parameter interval the one old section is the disjoint upper graph. There is no pass or crossing-book adapter hidden in this chart.

## Why the tube torus is incompressible here

Suppose an exterior compression disk had essential boundary on $T$. In the meridian/preferred-longitude basis its meridian coefficient is the linking number with the corresponding link component; that coefficient is zero because the disk misses the component. Primitivity of a simple essential torus curve makes its longitude coefficient $\pm1$. An annulus in the actual solid-torus tube joins this zero-linking longitude to its core component. Gluing the compression disk to this annulus gives an embedded spanning disk for that component, disjoint from all other link components. A sufficiently small regular neighborhood of the disk is a ball containing that component and no other component. Thus it is a split unknot component if other components exist; if none exist, the whole link is the unknot. Both contradict the hypothesis. Therefore each such tube torus is incompressible in the exterior. This uses the actual nonsplit nontrivial link, not primeness of a diagram.

## Boundary compression forced by a repeated lower disk

Suppose the two endpoints lie on one global proper lower disk $D$. Cut the actual lower reference ball along all old lower $F$ disks. The interior of $a$ misses all their boundary circles, hence belongs to one boundary region of one of the actual cut balls $C$. Both its endpoints meet the same boundary copy of $D$: a proper disk separates a ball, so the two copies of $D$ border different components, even after the other disks are cut.

Choose a proper arc $b$ in that copy of $D$ with these two endpoints. The interiors of $a$ and $b$ lie in different boundary faces, so $a\cup b$ is a simple circle on $\partial C$. Choose either of its actual sphere disks and push its interior slightly into $C$, keeping the prescribed boundary collars on $D$ and $T$. This is the ordinary relative ball-disk push: the chosen boundary disk is an actual embedded disk on the sphere, and its inward collar supplies the push. It gives an embedded disk $X$ with boundary $a\cup b$ and interior disjoint from $F$ and the tube boundary. Thus $X$ is an exterior boundary-compression disk with its $F$ arc $b$.

Boundary-incompressibility of $F$ makes $b$ boundary-parallel in $F$. Let $E\subset F$ be its boundary-parallelism disk, with other boundary interval $j\subset\partial F$. The circle $a\cup j$ on $T$ bounds a disk in the exterior: glue $X$ to a slight pushed copy of $E$ along $b$ and round their common interval. Its boundary is simple because the interior of $a$ misses the entire $\partial F$. Tube incompressibility therefore makes $a\cup j$ inessential on $T$, so $a$ is parallel on the cut torus to the interval $j$.

Both endpoints lie on the one boundary curve $c=\partial F\cap T$. There are just two intervals of $c$ between them, one being $e$. Thus replacing $a$ by its parallel interval $j$ makes $e\cup a$ homologous either to zero or to $\pm[c]$. But $[c]$ has longitudinal coefficient one, whereas $[e\cup a]=\pm\mu$ has longitudinal coefficient zero and is nonzero. This is impossible. The two endpoints of $e$ therefore lie on distinct global lower disks.

## Use in the new cap construction

Apply the claim only to $E1$. Its endpoints lie on the common black $B_1$ arc and common white $W_1$ arc. Their global lower circles and graph disks are distinct. Consequently their two actual local trace systems cannot cancel as an XOR contribution of one global projected disk. The membership-side readout can now use the actual separate global Jordan domains and the fixed white-fibre outside value, and the strict outer-wall witnesses can establish $\Delta_{B_1}\subset\Delta_{W_1}$ and the immediate pocket needed by the coherent direct construction. No assertion about all repeated global lower-disk labels is used here.
