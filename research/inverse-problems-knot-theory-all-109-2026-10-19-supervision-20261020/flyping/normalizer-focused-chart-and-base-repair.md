## Global page germs replace sign-only chart averaging

Let v be one compact actual reference axis, including its two transverse tube ends. Choose the coorientations of B and W continuously along v; an interval has no normal-line monodromy. Fix a smooth metric. In the sufficiently small two-sided neighborhoods of these actual page patches, let b and w be their respective SIGNED NORMAL DISTANCE FUNCTIONS. The page patches are actual embedded smooth surfaces, so their normal maps are injective on sufficiently thin neighborhoods of the compact axis patch; compactness gives a common size. Hence b,w are single globally defined smooth germs near the entire axis, with B={b=0}, W={w=0}. They are not averages of independently normalized local page equations. Their differentials are independent along v by actual transverse intersection of the pages.

Choose a smooth axis parameter r and a neat tubular parameter near its ends, where the tube torus is a coordinate end face. To construct it, use the endpoint inverse-function charts (rho,b,w), where rho is the actual torus defining function, choose r increasing on v with r=0 or1 at those two faces, and extend the longitudinal derivative along v by a smooth positive function. The middle is a usual tubular interval chart; join its normal frame to the two endpoint frames by paths of oriented normal frames along the interval. The actual endpoint charts and their smaller collars are retained. A thin neighborhood of the zero section in this matched normal bundle maps injectively into the ambient manifold by the metric normal map in the middle and the endpoint collar maps at the ends. On overlaps first choose the middle normal map to agree in first jet with the endpoint maps along the axis. Their differences then have first derivative tending uniformly to zero as the transverse radius shrinks. A finite interpolation through these C1-close maps has nonsingular derivative, fixes the axis and the retained smaller endpoint collars, and is injective on a sufficiently thin tube by local inverses and compact separation. This supplies the neat retraction and r-coordinate; it does not average independent b/w normals.

The triple (r,b,w) now has invertible derivative on v. Indeed db,dw annihilate its tangent line and are independent on its normal plane, while dr is positive on that tangent. Shrink the actual tube once more. The triple is a local diffeomorphism there; finite compactness and the injective axis parameter rule out pairs of distinct points with the same triple in a sufficiently thin tube. It therefore gives the one actual book coordinate system, with both actual torus end disks and both page germs matched. The page independence is established BEFORE any interpolation and remains open under the controlled C1 perturbation. Matching signs alone would not have justified it.

## The zero-upper endpoint need not assume equal Betti numbers

The old endpoint paragraph assumed b1(F)=b1(B). That equality is not an input for an arbitrary essential positive F and must not be inferred from the desired flyping conclusion. Instead use the actual tube/book incidence before counting ranks.

At each crossing the physical overpass has a central link meridian. On the exterior tube above that meridian, the upper shifted reference boundary and the crossing-book cup cover the entire meridian: the outer part of the overpass tube is on S+, while its complementary inner cup lies on the book boundary or in the book. In the standard half-tube/hemisphere chart this is exactly the decomposition of a tube meridian into its outer semicircle and its inner semicircle. The common endpoints are retained in their actual corner collars. If the book radius is changed, the same statement is retained by absorbing the intervening page/tube product rectangles into the reference faces; it is not true for an arbitrary unrelated small axis neighborhood with the old S+ left fixed.

The old boundary is a degree-one longitudinal section and therefore meets this actual central meridian. If a crossing had no common band, fair condition(d) would make boundary F disjoint from its book. Its point on that meridian would consequently lie on S+. Thus F intersect S+ empty implies that EVERY crossing has its common band. Fairness(g) and the absence of upper pieces ensure these are the already specified positive bands, and the remaining lower proper disks have exactly the reference black boundary circuits. The collar-fixed proper-disk comparison in the old endpoint proof therefore identifies the actual pair with (B,W). No Betti equality or Tait writhe statement is used; equality of the Betti numbers follows from the resulting ambient isotopy.

The incidence assertion used here is an explicit requirement on the SAME half-tube/cup S+ construction. If one instead reselects thin books while leaving unrelated reference faces fixed, this argument is not available. The finite reference-chamber collar adjustment in the preparation is precisely what must retain this cover.

## Actual ordinary-edge disk and outermost black trace

Suppose one upper or lower old disk has two distinct boundary intervals in the SAME ordinary edge tube. Select adjacent such intervals and a short intervening torus-strip arc alpha_T, with endpoints in their interiors, whose interior misses boundary F and boundary W. Its route uses the black-side component of that cut tube strip. It contains no crossing cup. The actual side-ball disk supplies the companion proper arc beta in the chosen F disk and an exterior parallelism disk from beta to alpha_T. Boundary-incompressibility of F gives a disk E in F with boundary beta union eta, eta in boundary F. Torus incompressibility applied to the union of these two disks makes alpha_T and eta parallel through a torus disk R.

The arc alpha_T misses boundary W. Every boundary-W arc in R would therefore have both endpoints on eta and would have opposite local intersection signs there. All original F/W boundary signs are positive, so no such arc exists. A whole essential boundary-W circle cannot lie in the disk R. Thus R and eta miss boundary W. The two endpoints project to an ordinary proper link interval. As both boundary F and alpha_T use its actual degree-one link-parameter graphs, choosing eta over the complementary interval would give nonzero longitudinal degree to alpha_T union eta. Hence eta uses the same edge interval and misses every crossing book.

The interior of beta lies in one open side piece and misses W and the books. Every F/W intersection is proper, so an arc in E could not leave through its boundary or reach boundary F; there are no intersection circles. Consequently E misses W. Its boundary misses all crossing books. Any crossing piece meeting E would be wholly contained in E (its frontier cannot cross boundary E), and would meet W, impossible. Thus E misses the crossing pieces as well.

There is at least one BLACK common-face trace in E. Indeed along eta, travel from the interior of the first chosen edge interval to the interior of the distinct second one. To leave and reenter that same chosen upper/lower half-tube the longitudinal section must cross its black side: the white side is excluded by eta disjoint boundary W, and the path never reaches a book. This gives at least two black endpoints on eta. The F/B trace family in E contains only arcs with endpoints on eta, since beta is in the open side piece and closed common-face traces were removed. Choose an outermost such arc lambda. Its E-side disk has interior disjoint B and its other boundary side is a tube interval eta_lambda with interior disjoint B. It is an ACTUAL boundary-compression disk for B, in the old link exterior; it meets B just in lambda. Essentiality of B therefore makes lambda boundary-parallel in B. This is forbidden after the black boundary-cap cleanup. Thus those two distinct edge intervals cannot exist. This replaces the old suppressed successive-intersection circle/compression paragraph entirely.
