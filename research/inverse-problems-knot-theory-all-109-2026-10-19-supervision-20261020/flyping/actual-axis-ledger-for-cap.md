# Actual fixed-axis ledger for the renewed cap

Renewed author-stage development, `/root/flyping_repair`, 2026-10-20. This checks the intrinsic integer used by the original-facing cap step. It grants no acceptance or workflow coverage. The cap body and full seam allocation remain the separately identified construction duties.

Fix the actual reference axes $v_c$, not merely a chosen crossing-ball radius. In prepared position each axis is either contained in one positive common crossing band, or is unused and meets $F$ in a finite number $t_c$ of transverse points. Its contribution to $a=|v\setminus\setminus F|$ is respectively $0$ or $1+t_c$. The endpoint collars are retained, so an unused interval cut at $t_c$ interior points has exactly $1+t_c$ components. Let $U$ be the ACTUAL old shadow: its central part is the upper disk $X\cap Y_0$, and its annular parts are the selected components in the thin prisms.

## No old common band is removed

Use the asymmetric FAIR-position condition, not an arbitrary Menasco placement of a band. In the actual primary v2 Definition5.1 (PDF21–22, fourth bullet on PDF22, TeX1238–1248), its fourth bullet is exactly “each crossing band in $F$ is disjoint from $S_+$.” The ensuing paragraph explicitly identifies the asymmetry between the two shifted spheres. The owner preparation used here retains this condition on the SAME $S_\pm$ and crossing-book boundaries. If a different convention places a band's boundary on the upper bubble, it is not this input and may not be substituted into this ledger.

Every common positive band is also the sole old piece in its prepared crossing book. Indeed the normal crossing pieces other than a common band are transverse saddle pieces, each meeting the actual axis. A second such piece would intersect the axis already contained in the common band, contradicting disjointness of the pieces of the embedded surface. Thus $F\cap S_+$ has no point anywhere in a common-band book: the sole band's whole closed piece misses $S_+$. In particular the upper circle $\gamma$ meets none of those books. Choose the thin projection annulus to miss every crossing footprint not met by $\gamma$; the finite disjoint compact footprints allow this choice while retaining all previously chosen wall collars. Consequently no annular part of $U$ contains any old common crossing band. Its central part lies in the upper chamber and also contains none. A lower return can follow arbitrarily many old positive-band boundary arcs inside $T_-$. Those bands lie on lower old $F$ inside $Y_0$, whereas the OLD central shadow inside $Y_0$ is only the upper disk $X$. The bands are outside $U$ and stay fixed. Replacing the central shadow by a lower-root frontier does not remove the lower old pieces: the new disk is pushed into their complementary root chamber, outside their books.

This distinction is essential: a lower return used to identify the normal table is not itself the entire old shadow.

## Per-axis table

Write $k_c$ for the number of transverse old axis points lying in $U$ at $c$. At a saddle book the cap deletes exactly those selected saddle points. Any extra old pieces in the protected component $Z_i$ remain fixed; the new disk and its inward collars miss them and the axis. At an active overpass book, old $F$ is absent from the whole book, and the new local piece has exactly the one actual positive $B$ band. The other local and central filling disks stay in lower chambers outside book interiors.

| Axis class | Old contribution | New contribution | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| old common positive band | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ |
| saddle book with $k_c$ selected old points | $1+t_c$ | $1+t_c-k_c$ | $-k_c$ |
| active previously empty overpass book | $1$ | $0$ | $-1$ |
| every other unused axis | $1+t_c$ | $1+t_c$ | $0$ |

The first and last rows follow from literal fixed supports, not isotopy invariance of an unsigned crossing count. No saddle book is simultaneously an active empty book. The selected annulus visits each active book in its prescribed single crossing occurrence, so one new band is counted once even though it has two tube ends. If $k=\sum_c k_c$ and $r$ is the number of active empty books, the actual result before re-preparation is

$$
a(F_{\mathrm{new}})=a(F)-k-r.
$$

## Why the decrease is strict

Use the ACTUAL prism input: the upper circle has at least one common white arc $\omega_i$. This is already required to form its finite white-dual subdivision; a circle with no such arc is not an input to the stated strip construction. Each endpoint of a white common-face arc is on a tube or on a saddle crossing face. Suppose $k=r=0$. Then the selected circle has neither a saddle nor an overpass, since every selected saddle contributes to $k$ and every traversed overpass is an active empty book contributing to $r$. Consequently the endpoints of $\omega_i$ are tube endpoints and are $W+$ by the all-positive white normalization.

Choose any such endpoint and follow its actual lower return BACKWARD. In the fully derived endpoint table a return terminating $W+$ starts either at a lower underpass point over an upper overpass (type U) or at a lower point over a selected saddle (type S). The only other initial category is $B+$, and the mixed row $(B+,W+)$ has already been excluded. Thus that actual finite return forces either a selected overpass or a selected saddle. This contradicts $k=r=0$. It proves $k+r\ge1$ without requiring the entire lower return to lie on one edge, without counting an old common band in its chain as removed, and without assuming that ordinary angular graph sections cannot oscillate.

For completeness, the nonempty-white-arc premise in this strictness argument can be earned using the same prepared no-type7 and no-black-boundary-parallel inputs; it need not be inferred from signs. Continue under the contradictory assumption $k=r=0$ and suppose that $\gamma$ has no white arc. It has no crossing-face arc, and every ordinary tube interval then has both endpoints on its black section. Within the upper half-tube such an interval returns to the same black side of the SAME ordinary edge. Thus the entire circle stays beside one black core disk: ordinary returns do not pass through a crossing band or change black faces. Cut that core disk along its actual common-face traces, and consider a component on the innermost $T_+$ side. Any component incident to two distinct common-face intervals of $\gamma$ supplies a type7 guide in that open black core; its companion lies on the one upper disk, its core meets no tube or book, and the two common-face endpoint intervals are distinct. This is forbidden. Hence the component adjacent to an ordinary return has exactly one such interval $\beta$. Its remaining boundary is the edge interval between the two endpoints of that ordinary return: no crossing corner is traversed, since the return lies on one ordinary edge. Consequently $\beta$ and that actual interval of $\partial B$ bound a disk in the black core. This makes the proper common black arc boundary-parallel in the full black surface, contrary to the prepared black-return removal. An entire closed common-face trace is the previously removed closed-trace case. Therefore $\gamma$ has a white arc whenever $k=r=0$, giving the contradiction above.

This argument is confined to the no-saddle/no-overpass case. It does not assert that an arbitrary lower multi-band return is boundary-parallel in the full black surface merely because it abuts one core disk.
Thus the displayed fixed-axis integer strictly decreases. Re-preparation may retain every old and newly common axis and remove further transverse hits; it is the relative preparation already supplied in the owner proof, not the optional perturbation which discards common bands. Its contribution therefore cannot reverse this strict decrease.
