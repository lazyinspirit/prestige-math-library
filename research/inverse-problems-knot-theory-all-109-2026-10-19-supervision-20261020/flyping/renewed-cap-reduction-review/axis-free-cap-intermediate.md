## Precise statement

Let B,W be the actual standard checkerboard pair of a connected reduced alternating diagram which is diagrammatically prime and represents a prime nonsplit oriented link. Assume the retained essentiality and actual checkerboard-ball interfaces. Let V be an actual genuine acceptable spherical plumbing cap for B, with actual disk shadow U, consecutive peripheral interval pairings, completed sphere, radial tube charts and retained seam collars. Assume every retained B seam of V is disjoint from W, equivalently from every reference axis. Put V in standard position by isotopies preserving that property.

Suppose there is no complementary reducing disk X DISJOINT FROM W whose two surgeries are both genuine caps. This is weaker than full irreducibility; no assertion that an arbitrary reducing disk can be moved off W is made. Then V is apparent and its actual marked replacement is a registered flype, up to simultaneous actual checkerboard collar isotopies. The transported oriented component map is the actual strand map, including closed components in either tangle ball.

More generally the same conclusion is a finite flype sequence if a finite reduction tree of actual W-disjoint genuine surgeries and W-free acceptability cleanups is SUPPLIED, with every remaining leaf acceptable and axis-free and with no W-disjoint genuine reducing disk. The existence of such a cleanup/reduction tree is a separate obligation. This statement does not promote a conditional tree to an existence proof.

## Axis-free standardization and what it does not assume

A B-seam missing W lies in one B-core disk and misses every crossing-band axis. Isotope its portions parallel to a crossing-book boundary off that boundary using its actual core-side bigon, within that B-core. Tube boundary arcs are moved off inner cups in their given tube charts. These changes leave the B-seams W-free. Circle intersections of V with W are removed by the actual W-disk/empty-ball exchange from the consolidated proof; it fixes the cap boundary and B. Thus standardization preserves the stated axis-free input. It does not assert an axis-crossing seam can be made axis-free.

Acceptability gives meridional tube intervals with alternating signs. The alternating book rule gives at least one W arc. Every endpoint of a W arc now lies on a tube interval, since there is no seam/axis endpoint. Every outermost cap disk therefore has the positive-port/positive-port row: its boundary a traverses one negative overpass, at c, and joins the two actual black-core regions there. The other rows have genuinely been excluded by the input, not renamed.

## Scope of reduction and preservation still owed

A supplied W-disjoint reducing disk inserts new retained seams consisting of old seam halves joined along epsilon; these are W-free. Old tube intervals are inherited, so their individual acceptability condition survives. HOWEVER a new B seam can acquire a complementary-F parallelism certificate; automatic acceptability of both genuine surgery caps is NOT proved. The argument in Proposition3.8 uses one FAKE factor essentially and cannot be reused to claim automatic preservation when both surgery factors are genuine.

For an actual complementary-F certificate whose seam is W-free, its disk misses W: any reference axis entering the disk would have both ends on its old-boundary arc, and an outermost such axis would be boundary parallel in B, contrary to reduced alternating checkerboard axes being essential. This provides a W-free F-ear push. But the resulting merged tube interval can be inessential; removing that torus ear may merge retained seams along an old boundary interval meeting axes. Therefore a complete W-free cleanup algorithm is STILL NOT supplied by that observation. A caller must supply the actual cleanup preserving axis-free seams, or start with a reduction tree whose caps already have the required acceptable axis-free data. This is why the theorem above is a genuine restricted classification and not yet an original105 proof.
