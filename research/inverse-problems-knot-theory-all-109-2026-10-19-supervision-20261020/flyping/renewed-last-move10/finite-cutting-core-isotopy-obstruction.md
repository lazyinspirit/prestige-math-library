# Finite cutting proof of the required non-core-isotopy criterion

New author-stage argument for the corrected v3 branch; not a provider recheck. Use only the standard proper-arc comparison disk Statement (two isotopic proper arcs admit a bigon, boundary triangle, or boundary rectangle), with isotopies in the actual surface minus its protected common arcs. Alternatively the comparison disk is the elementary proper-arc bigon criterion. No source proof or acceptance of that criterion is newly claimed here.

Let W be the actual checkerboard surface, v its disjoint reference proper axes, and w the protected common subfamily. Let u be the finite actual proper F/W arc system, containing w, with other arcs transverse to v. Require every arc of BOTH u and v to be non-boundary-parallel in W. For the actual application this u condition is already supplied by essentiality of the opposite-definite spans, boundary-torus incompressibility and their all-positive boundary intersections, as proved in the accepted normalizer; it is not inferred from the disk argument below. Assume there is NO complementary disk of W cut along u union v having exactly one u side and one v side (bigons, boundary triangles, and boundary rectangles included). This is the exact retained 6-good normal form, not a generic incompressibility assertion.

Claim: if a component a of u minus w is properly isotopic inside W minus w into a white core, then a misses every reference axis. Consequently an actual proper a which crosses an axis is obstructed from being core-isotopic.

Choose a comparison arc b inside a single white core, properly isotopic to a in W minus w, and minimize its intersections with a among arcs remaining in that core, allowing its boundary endpoints to slide only in that core's actual peripheral boundary intervals. The proper-arc comparison disk criterion supplies a disk D with one a side and one b side, and zero, one, or two peripheral boundary sides. Choose such a disk so that its interior misses a union b; this is the usual innermost comparison disk. If D misses v, moving its b side across D (with boundary slides confined to the same white core) either removes a pair of a/b intersections, or removes their terminal intersection. This contradicts the chosen minimum, unless a and b are disjoint and D is a rectangle. In that remaining case, because a crosses v and b misses v, D meets v. Thus in every relevant case one may use a comparison disk D meeting v. This is the only use of minimality; it must not be inferred solely from the existence of some unrelated intersection elsewhere on a.

Every component of v intersect D has endpoints on the a side or on the peripheral sides, never on the b side. Circles do not occur because v is a proper arc system and D is a disk. Use the planar order of these disjoint arcs to choose a component nearest the a side, and let E be the subdisk between it and that side, with no other v component in its interior. Its boundary has one v side, one subarc of a, and zero, one, or TWO peripheral sides. These are respectively a bigon, a terminal triangle, or a rectangle. An axis whose two endpoints lie on the SAME peripheral side would instead cut off a disk with an actual peripheral arc in W and would be boundary-parallel, contrary to essentiality of reference axes. In contrast an axis joining the TWO DISTINCT peripheral sides is legitimate; it yields the rectangle case and is not discarded. Protected axes cannot occur in D, since D is in W minus w.

Finally E might contain other components of u. They are disjoint from its a side. Any such component has endpoints on the v side and/or the peripheral sides, and no circles. A component joining two points of the v side cuts off a smaller one-u/one-v bigon. A component from v to a peripheral side cuts off a one-u/one-v triangle. A component joining the TWO DISTINCT peripheral sides cuts off, on the v side, a smaller one-u/one-v rectangle. Components with both ends on the SAME peripheral side would be whole proper u arcs bounding disks with intervals of boundary W, contrary to the explicit non-boundary-parallel input; none occur. Choose the component nearest the v side among the remaining types and use its disk. Finiteness, or repeatedly taking a smaller such disk, leaves no further u component inside. The result is a complementary disk with one actual u side, one actual v side, and zero, one, or two peripheral sides. Each type contradicts the stated 6-good normal form. Thus the claimed core-isotopy obstruction follows from exactly those inputs.
