## A rigorous two-leaf reduction

Let C be a nonempty finite disjoint circle system on a sphere. Its dual graph has one vertex for each component of the complement and one edge for each circle, joining its two incident components. The graph is connected: a path between points in two regions, transverse to C, gives a graph path. Each circle separates the sphere, and deleting its edge separates the graph into the regions on its two sides. Thus every edge is a bridge and the graph is a tree. Its leaves correspond exactly to complementary disk regions, hence to global innermost circles, counted by disk sides. When there are at least two circles each such leaf has a distinct boundary circle.

Assume the bad-position endpoint assertion 5.2(A): every non-core-isotopic proper arc a of F intersect W has an endpoint on every global innermost circle. Also assume existence of such an arc, supplied in the intended application by the NEW finite-cutting core-isotopy obstruction, at its exact proper-arc comparison-disk input and retained 6-good cell hypothesis. Because a has two endpoints, there are at most two global leaf circles. A finite tree with an edge has at least two leaves: take endpoints of a longest simple path. Hence there are exactly two. Every vertex has degree at most two. Indeed, a vertex of degree at least three has at least one leaf in each component of its deletion, by following a longest path in that component. The dual tree is consequently a path.

If there are at least three circles, choose a leaf disk D0 and its boundary g0. The adjacent dual vertex has degree two, with other boundary g1. The g1-side containing D0 contains exactly the single circle g0 and no others. Thus the parent disk X used in Lemma 5.3 contains ONE innermost child. This conclusion uses the sphere and the endpoint obstruction; a bound of two endpoints alone does not imply that an arbitrary chosen disk has one child.

This proves the source footnote 57 precisely, contingent only on the separately required physical support statement behind 5.2(A). It does not construct the white push-through corridor.

## Monotonicity needed for the bad-arc support implication

The support implication is valid under the following exact geometric section input: every old proper W arc changed or removed by Move 10 contains a selected saddle or selected gamma-side white arc, and every such arc has an actual peripheral endpoint on g0. Suppose an old obstructed arc a has no endpoint on g0. The section input leaves a pointwise unchanged as an entire proper W arc. Old common axes remain common; new common axes occur only at previously empty crossing books, so a is disjoint from them. Write w for the old common system and w' for the new, with w contained in w'. Any proper isotopy taking a into the white cores inside W minus w' would also be such an isotopy inside W minus w, since the former is a subset of the latter and the white cores miss all reference axes. This contradicts the old obstruction. Thus the Move 10 could not have made every noncommon arc core-isotopic. Bad position therefore forces a to have an endpoint on g0. Enlarging the protected common system does not invalidate this implication; it only restricts the available isotopies.
