definite. Thus Fact 2.23 applies to their actual transverse intersection.
Suppose there is a circle. Choose one innermost on W, bounding a disk
D with interior disjoint from F'. The properly embedded reference axes

collection of proper chords after transverse perturbation. (An axis
contained in F' cannot meet this disk's interior.) If there is no chord,

an outermost one cuts off a subdisk whose interior misses every axis and

## Lower disk pieces from essentiality: NEW proof avoiding O

The outer-contact assertion O is sufficient for the older gluing proof,
but is not necessary for FAIR (h). Here is an independent proof that
requires no outer-wall count or repeated-contact premise.

**Simultaneous compression lemma.** A finite disjoint family of compact
proper surfaces in a ball, each having nonempty boundary, either consists
of disks or has a compression disk whose interior misses the entire family.
First cut along all components which already are disks. A finite disjoint
family of proper disks cuts a ball into balls. The remaining proper
surfaces are unchanged and lie in these balls. If one remains, choose an
innermost circle among their boundary circles on the boundary of one such
ball. It belongs to a nondisk component S. The sphere disk bounded by that
circle has interior disjoint from all remaining boundary circles. Push
its interior into a boundary collar of the ball, and use a small collar
of the circle on S to move its boundary to an interior parallel curve.
The resulting disk is disjoint from all surfaces in its interior; it
also misses the previously cut disk faces after pushing away from those
faces. Its boundary is essential in S: a boundary-parallel interior curve
could bound a disk on the connected surface S only if S itself were a
disk. This proves the assertion, including for nonorientable components.
All corner collars are rounded first.

Apply this to the actual finite family F' intersect H-. No component is
closed: such a component would be a whole connected component of F',
whereas F' is connected with nonempty link boundary. Suppose a lower
component is not a disk, and take the simultaneous compression disk D
from the lemma. It is an actual compression disk for F' unless its boundary
bounds a disk E in F'. Essentiality of the marked re-plumbed F' supplies
exactly such an E. Its boundary lies in int H-, and E cannot contain
actual boundary points of F'.

Assume FAIR (a) has already been established above. The system u=F' intersect
W consists of proper arcs, with endpoints on actual boundary F'. Because
boundary E misses W and E contains no actual boundary of F', an arc of u
cannot enter E, and cannot lie wholly in E. Thus E misses W. Each connected
crossing-ball piece lies wholly on one side of boundary E, since boundary
E is in int H-. Every such piece meets W by its retained saddle/positive
band chart, so no crossing-ball piece enters E. Now suppose E enters an upper component. Its entire boundary lies in E,
since boundary E is in int H-. That boundary cannot meet a crossing-ball
piece (already excluded), W, or actual boundary F'. Since every upper
component is an unchanged OLD upper disk, its boundary would be an OLD
closed trace in the black core S0. This contradicts the old FAIR arcs-on-S0
assertion, without requiring a new black-core trace table. Thus E enters no upper component either. Consequently E
is entirely in H- and in the same lower component as boundary D. This
contradicts essentiality of boundary D on that component. All lower
components therefore are disks.

This proves (h) from (a), essentiality, unchanged old upper components,
old arcs on S0, and every crossing piece meeting W,
without assuming O or the unproved outer-contact half of Proposition 8.5.
It uses actual physical lower pieces, not a quotient adjacency graph.

## A weaker sufficient local certificate for B

The following precise certificate is enough for the no-bigon portion of
L; task C's complete triangle classification is unnecessary here.

1. Every transverse intersection with an unused reference axis is in a
   retained old saddle, with its old half-book germs. New positive bands
   contribute whole common-axis components, not transverse intersections.
2. Any new white-core component having two crossing-boundary endpoints
   is a literally unchanged old arc, including the actual two book
   germs. Every changed white-core component instead is a proper arc
   with an actual tube endpoint. In particular no changed component is
   a white-core circle.

To verify the implication, suppose D is an interior u-v bigon. Its u edge
has no other axis intersection. By item 1 and the standard old saddle
chart, its portions inside endpoint books are retained half-book germs;
any visit to an intermediate saddle would cross another axis. The
remaining portion is consequently a single white-core arc with both
ends on crossing boundaries. By item 2 that arc, and hence the entire
u edge, is old. The old u family may have additional arcs inside D, but
these can enter D only across its v edge. They have no endpoints inside
D, since D misses boundary W, and no old intersection circles occur.
Choose an innermost one of these chords, or D itself if there are none.
It cuts off an interior bigon for the OLD u and v families. The interior
already misses v, since D did. This contradicts old six-good position.
Thus no new interior bigon exists.

The same certificate excludes circles in the white core: a changed
component is a proper boundary-ended arc, while an unchanged component
is an old proper arc. Combined with the preceding bigon argument, this
is exactly what the circle-exclusion proof needs. One need not prove
the stronger all-colors S0 statement first. In the essentiality argument
for lower disks, an upper component is an unchanged old upper disk, so
its hypothetical all-black-core boundary would contradict OLD arcs on
S0. This likewise does not require an independently proved new black-core
arc statement.

The certificate remains an author obligation: 'every changed arc has a
