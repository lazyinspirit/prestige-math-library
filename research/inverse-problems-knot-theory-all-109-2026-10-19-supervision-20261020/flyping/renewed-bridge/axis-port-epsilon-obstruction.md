# Axis-port obstruction to the originally prescribed epsilon

Targeted author development, `/root/flyping_bridge`, 2026-10-21. This diagnoses the remaining cap-classification operation. It is not a counterexample to the flyping theorem or an acceptance of any cap-classification proof.

Use the current named leaf setup: a1 traverses the negative overpass at c; its switching neighbor d traverses the negative underpass at the same c. The shadow U contains the full proper axis chord v_c. The paired old/new tube intervals are sections over the same whole longitudinal interval. Thus the old boundary intervals belonging to those two negative passes each contain just their indicated v_c endpoint, and an old interval paired to a positive ordinary-edge interval has no axis endpoint. Retained seam W points are exactly the shared cap-boundary axis ports.

The switching endpoint x=a1 intersect d must be a positive tube port. Indeed the U boundary route between the two v_c endpoints through x has no other W point. If x were an axis port, its proper shadow-axis component would have one endpoint on that route, could not cross v_c, and would have no other endpoint there. This is impossible for a disjoint proper arc system in the disk cut off by v_c.

Suppose the other endpoint y=a1 intersect d' is an axis port on a different axis v_t. First d' contains no negative tube interval. Between the negative interval in a1 and another negative interval along the cap boundary, signs must alternate through a positive interval. Such an interval has a W endpoint. There is no positive interval between the negative interval in a1 and y, since y is in a retained seam; and an intervening positive interval in d' would give a W point in its interior. Both are impossible. Hence d' consists of a B-seam portion, possibly with the terminal part of a positive tube interval at its far W endpoint.

Let eta be the proper component of U intersect v_t with endpoint y. The distinct axis chords eta and v_c do not intersect. Therefore both v_c endpoints lie in the same component of U minus eta. The boundary portion of a1 from y toward its old overpass endpoint has no other eta endpoint: it has no retained-seam W point, and the old overpass interval contains only the c-axis endpoint. Thus the a1-side germ at y lies in that component. The d'-side germ is on the other side of the proper crosscut eta.

All B portions of d stay on the v_c side of eta: connect them to the old underpass endpoint along their shadow boundary portions, which have no interior t-axis endpoint. All B portions of d' stay on its opposite side. Its seam interior has no W point, its possible terminal positive old tube interval lies on an ordinary edge with no axis endpoint, and a far endpoint on the same axis t is the already excluded same-axis return. Consequently no arc in U minus W joins an interior B point of d to an interior B point of d'. In particular a collar of v_c cannot supply the originally prescribed epsilon for this mixed axis/positive row.

This leaves a concrete authoring obligation: select a different leaf that has two positive endpoint ports, or find another valid pair of retained seam occurrences on one shadow W-complement region and one cap region, or construct a different reducing parallelism. The statement that the half-band alone reaches both original d,d' cannot be used in the axis row. The argument does not say that such an axis leaf cannot exist; it says that this particular epsilon construction needs a replacement there.

The argument depends on the full longitudinal endpoint ledger stated in the first paragraph. It should not be applied if the old/new interval pairing is known only by total slope without the whole-section realization.
