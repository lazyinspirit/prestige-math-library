# Ordinary upper edge separates its two lower disks

Renewed user-authorized development by `/root/flyping_bridge`, 2026-10-20. This is a new proof of the particular distinctness needed for W1 and B1. It avoids the unaccepted general Proposition8.5 homology/framing argument. Actual primary text read for this route: Kindred v2 PDF24–26, especially Proposition5.8 and its proof, and PDF5, Proposition2.2. No accepted repository supplier was reread or recertified.

## Precise scope

Let L be a nontrivial nonsplit link in S3, and let F be an essential spanning surface in its exterior. Use the actual prepared position in which the lower reference region H- is a ball, F cuts H- into finitely many proper disk pieces, and old boundary sections on link tubes are longitudinal graph sections. Let beta be an upper boundary interval of F lying on one ordinary edge tube and traversing no crossing overpass. Its endpoints x0,x1 lie on the two different common-face edges of that tube, as guaranteed by good position. Then the endpoints abut distinct *global* disks of F intersect H-.

The nontriviality condition excludes the unknot base case. For a nonsplit link with more than one component, nonsplitness already excludes a split unknot component. No primeness beyond this nonsplit nontrivial scope is used here.

## The actual complementary tube arc

Use an ordinary tube chart with longitudinal parameter s and angular parameter theta, with upper semicylinder 0<=theta<=pi. The displayed beta is one graph theta=phi(s), a<=s<=b, whose interior is in that open semicylinder and whose endpoints lie on its two distinct edges. After reversing s if needed, phi(a)=0 and phi(b)=pi. Take the complementary lower graph theta=-phi(s), interpreted modulo 2pi, and call it alpha'. Its interior lies in the lower semicylinder and misses beta. No other old F boundary lies over this longitudinal interval, since the old full boundary is one longitudinal section and its piece there is beta. Thus alpha' is an actual arc on the lower reference sphere, with interior disjoint from all old F.

The circle beta followed by alpha' backwards is simple. It has zero longitudinal winding and one meridional turn: lift beta from theta=0 to pi, and the backwards lower graph from pi to 2pi. Hence it is a meridian of the actual tube torus T, with no omitted channel fragment or extra angular turn. The coordinate chart is used only on this ordinary edge, so it does not impose a false plane framing on a crossing cup.

## A genuine boundary-compression disk if the lower disk repeats

Suppose x0,x1 abut the same global lower proper disk D. Cut H- along all actual lower F disks, keeping the two copies of every cut face distinct. The arc alpha', with interior disjoint from their boundary circles, lies on the boundary sphere of one resulting ball C. Its two endpoints lie on the SAME copy of D: an arc in the complement of the boundary circle of a separating proper disk cannot approach its two opposite boundary-sphere sides without crossing that circle. Cutting along the other disks does not alter this fact.

Choose a simple arc alpha on that copy of D from x0 to x1, with interior in D. Then alpha union alpha' is a simple circle on the actual sphere boundary of C. Choose one sphere disk it bounds and push its interior into C, retaining its alpha collar on the D face and its alpha' collar on the actual tube face. The result is an embedded disk E in the link exterior whose boundary is alpha union alpha', whose interior misses F and T. All other old disks are boundary faces of C and are avoided by the push. This is a concrete relative disk construction, not a compression inferred from a dual graph.

If alpha is essential as a proper arc of F, E is a boundary-compression disk, contrary to essentialness. Therefore alpha is boundary-parallel in F: alpha and an arc J of the actual boundary of F cobound a disk in F. Glue this disk to E along alpha and round the seam. The resulting embedded disk R lies in the exterior and has boundary alpha' union J on T. The interiors of alpha' and J are disjoint because alpha' misses the old boundary; its boundary is therefore a simple torus circle.

## Why the boundary torus is incompressible here

For completeness, an essential circle c on the boundary T of one link tube cannot bound an exterior disk. Write its primitive slope as p times the meridian plus q times a preferred longitude. Linking with that link component equals p. Since an exterior spanning disk misses the component, p=0. Primitivity gives q=+1 or -1. A longitude with this slope cobounds an embedded annulus in the solid tube with its core. Gluing that annulus to the exterior disk gives an embedded spanning disk for the component, disjoint from every other link component. It is therefore an unknot component separated from the rest by the boundary of a small disk neighborhood. This contradicts nontriviality in the one-component case and nonsplitness otherwise. Thus T is incompressible.

Apply this to R. Its torus boundary must be inessential, so alpha' is parallel on T to J through the actual disk bounded by alpha' union J.

## The final meridian contradiction

Both beta and J are arcs of the SAME actual boundary component of F with endpoints x0,x1. If J is the same of the two boundary-component arcs as beta, replacing alpha' by J makes beta union alpha' nullhomologous on T. If it is the complementary arc, the replacement gives the full boundary component of F, up to orientation. That component has longitudinal coefficient +1 or -1 because F spans the actual link component. Thus the meridian beta union alpha' would have torus homology either zero or plus/minus the class of that longitude-bearing boundary component. Neither is the nonzero primitive meridian class, whose longitudinal coefficient is zero. This is a contradiction.

Therefore the two endpoints of beta abut distinct global lower disks.
