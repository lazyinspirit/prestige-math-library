# From actual projected face witnesses to cut-ball incidence

Renewed user-authorized development, `/root/flyping_bridge`, 2026-10-20. This is a proof tool for the newly extracted typeIII face table, not acceptance of that table from its picture.

Use the simultaneous lower graph placement in `../renewed-prism-model.md`. Distinct global proper lower disks have disjoint boundary circles on the reference sphere. Their root-avoiding projection disks are nested or disjoint; on a strict nesting the outer graph lies strictly below the inner graph. A *local piece* is a connected intersection of such a global graph disk with the actual prism. Global names are not assumed distinct merely because local names differ.

## Immediate nesting from one physical projection fibre

Suppose two actual local pieces A,C have a common interior projection point p in the prism, and their heights satisfy h_A(p)<h_C(p). Then they belong to distinct global disks: projection injectivity prevents one global graph disk from having two heights over p. Their global root-avoiding disks overlap, hence are nested. The height order forces Delta_C to be strictly inside Delta_A.

Suppose further that the actual fibre over p meets no lower old disk other than those two. Then C is an immediate child of A in the global inclusion forest. Indeed any intermediate global disk D with Delta_C strictly inside Delta_D strictly inside Delta_A would contain p and contribute a third graph height on that physical fibre. This contradicts the stipulated local normal-cell readout. Therefore the upper cut-face copy of A and the lower cut-face copy of C are incident to the same actual chamber after cutting the lower ball along *all* old lower disks.

This last assertion can be checked directly, without treating an abstract tree as marked data. Choose a small vertical segment over p strictly between the two graph heights. It lies in one component of the actual complement. Its endpoints approach the indicated physical face copies. If another proper disk separated these two face copies, its projection domain would be an intermediate domain, just ruled out. Extra disks elsewhere in the chamber can subdivide its boundary, but cannot separate these two faces without creating an intermediate sheet over p.

If A had an ancestor D, that disk also contributes a height over p. Thus in the exact two-sheet situation A is outermost. Its lower face is incident to the actual root chamber. This conclusion uses the actual fibre and every actual sheet on it, rather than a count of local names.

## Disjoint outer pieces from two marked witnesses

Let A,B be two distinct global graph disks. If a marked point of Delta_A lies outside Delta_B and a marked point of Delta_B lies outside Delta_A, nesting is impossible, so the two domains are disjoint. If each has a local interior projection fibre with no covering lower old sheet below its graph, each domain is outermost. Their lower face copies consequently lie on the same actual root chamber boundary. The witnesses must be checked on the actual projected domains; a schematic drawing of a piece 'wrapping' another is not such a check.
