# Mixed new/old rectangle: exclusion of selected targets

2026-10-21. NEW bounded author lemma for the remaining v3 C operation.
It does not assert a Type I body exists. Its input is the actual Type I
pendant primitive once that body has been supplied, including the local
endpoint-placement freedom confirmed by the Type I section author.
No accepted module is rechecked, and no v2 general-classification result
is used.

## Actual input

Let gamma bound the old innermost disk T in S+ cut along F. For each
selected Type I crossing K, let E_in(K) be its protected underpass-side
ordinary edge leading to the common crossing S(K) inside T. The actual
Lemma 4.1 row gives:

* gamma misses E_in(K);
* the other crossing S(K) lies in the inner region;
* the opposite underpass-side ordinary edge is E_out(K);
* the selected crossing has an old saddle, hence NO old F boundary
  on its tube inside the closed crossing book (FAIR (d));
* the constructed pendant ends at a new W port p_K on E_out(K),
  in its actual exterior white/tube collar.

Use the legitimate local freedom to choose p_K just outside the crossing
book, before the FIRST old W port on that branch. To justify this choice,
the boundary-W interval from the underpass axis endpoint through the book
to its exit point is old-port-free, since the selected book has no old
boundary F. The old W ports beyond it form a finite set. Thus there is
an open initial exterior interval free of all old ports. The pendant
corner admits an endpoint anywhere sufficiently close in that interval.
These neighborhoods are disjoint for finitely many selected books.
This is a choice within the eventual body construction, preserving its
fixed seams and retained pieces, not permission to slide an endpoint
past an arbitrary old port after the fact.

## The mixed rectangle

Let A be the selected crossing producing a new endpoint p_A. Suppose
its new whole white-core arc ends at an OLD retained port q and cuts a
complementary one-u/one-v rectangle with an unused axis v_B. In the
actual alternating crossing row, p_A lies on a branch which reaches B
on its overpass side. The other rail reaches the underpass endpoint of
v_B from the old q. Thus q is on an UNDERPASS-side ordinary edge of B.
All ports and crossings below are physical occurrences, not labels of
possibly repeated global faces.

### B cannot equal A

The retained opposite white tail of the selected A saddle is an OLD
white-core arc from the crossing boundary of A to q. If B=A, the q edge
is incident to A. This is exactly the old crossing/incident-edge pattern
forbidden by old six-good position, v3 Lemma 3.23(I)(b). No port sliding
or new surface is used in this contradiction.

### B cannot be another selected Type I crossing

Suppose B is selected and different from A. There are two possibilities
for the underpass branch containing q.

If it is E_in(B), then q is impossible already on OLD F. The ordinary
edge E_in(B) misses gamma, and its inner endpoint lies on S(B) inside T.
The connected edge consequently lies on that same inner side. In
particular an old W port on it is a point of F intersect S+ inside the
innermost complementary disk T, which has no such point. Gamma itself
does not supply an exception because gamma misses this edge.

If it is E_out(B), then q lies beyond p_B in the boundary-W order from
the underpass endpoint of v_B: p_B was chosen before every OLD W port,
and q is an old port. Thus the supposed rectangle's peripheral rail
from that endpoint to q contains the distinct new port p_B in its
INTERIOR. A complementary cell of W cut along u union v cannot have an
additional u endpoint in the interior of one of its peripheral rails.
The point p_B cannot be an endpoint of the same new u arc p_A--q,
which already has its two distinct endpoints; nor can it equal q,
since it was chosen in an old-port-free interval. This contradicts
that the proposed disk was a one-u/one-v complementary rectangle.

So the mixed target B is neither A nor any other selected Type I
crossing.

## Exact consequence and limit

For a mixed new/old rectangle, its target crossing is nonselected.
Therefore an old selected saddle at B cannot be a removed ghost blocking
an OLD guide near the B corner. This is useful for the separate
old-black-port/white-guide argument.

This result does not exclude the remaining NONSELECTED target row.
That row still requires actual old peripheral ownership, rather than
inferring traversal of an entire pass from the initial W sign.

It also does NOT exclude a TWO-NEW rectangle whose target is one of
its own selected crossings. In that case the underpass rail can END
at p_B, instead of containing it in its interior. The mixed argument
cannot be applied to that row. In particular no unsupported general
'no adjacent selected Type I crossings' assertion is made here.
