## 1. A lower disk shared by two intervals must face outward at both

Let a be a common-face interval of gamma and D its incident lower disk. The projected domain side of D is constant along the interior of a: its actual boundary circle is embedded, its domain has a consistent side, and the upper domain T is likewise on one fixed side of this common interval.

If a is white, D faces OUT of T. Otherwise an inner white point arbitrarily close to a would lie in its projected domain, giving a lower F point in addition to X on that white fibre. This contradicts the FULL-fibre singleton condition.

If a has an endpoint on the selected saddle, D again faces OUT of T. In the actual transverse-saddle normal chart the surface is a projection graph, and its two upper/lower branches occupy opposite projection sides of the equatorial seam. The chart and its endpoint collars are retained by the normalizer. At that common-face endpoint the upper branch is the T side and the lower branch is its opposite. The consistent domain side along a propagates this to all of a. This is an actual graph-germ argument; it would not follow merely from the word “saddle” for an arbitrary warped piece. The normalizer's selected-saddle endpoint rule excludes an interval with TWO crossing ends.

The only remaining case is a BLACK interval with both ends on ordinary tube portions. Suppose its lower domain faces INTO T. Follow the actual lower-circle continuation from either endpoint on that inward side. The signed half-tube rule makes these the initial B+ and terminal B− ends of the lower return beside the black core containing a. The finite positive-corner state keeps the whole intervening chain beside ONE black core. The no-type7 condition permits only the one gamma common-face side a in that core region. The named-return derivation therefore joins the OTHER end of that SAME a: the return is precisely its black ear, regardless of how many old positive-band boundary arcs its far side contains. The actual global lower boundary circle is a joined to this return. It contains NO second gamma common-face interval.

Consequently, if D carries two distinct gamma intervals a1,a2, neither can be this isolated inward black-ear disk. The other cases show that BOTH have outward projection germs. The same holds for every additional gamma interval on that D.

## 2. The actual exterior chord exists

Let Delta be the actual projected Jordan disk of such a shared D. Its intersections with int(T) are the finite black-side boundary ears from the accepted joint-positioning proof. None has an inward common gamma interval on its boundary, by step1. Thus each such component is a genuine boundary cap of Delta: its cut side is one proper interval on partial T, while its other side is the actual lower boundary return. Removing its interior and that cut side leaves the opposite connected disk side. The caps have disjoint interiors and their cut intervals have disjoint interiors. A whole inner closed-circle domain cannot occur for this D, whose global boundary meets gamma.

Successively remove these boundary caps. The remaining exterior part of Delta is connected. Choose x_i in int(a_i), away from the finite marked subdivision endpoints. Their outward germs belong to this connected exterior part. A finite disk-with-boundary-caps parameter gives a simple arc between these two germs whose interior lies STRICTLY outside T. Extend it through the two short endpoint collars to x1,x2. It remains simple and misses T except at its endpoints. Lift it by the actual graph of D to obtain alpha_minus in D. This is an arc on the actual old surface, not an abstract tree path.

Choose a proper arc alpha_plus in X joining the same x1,x2, with projection in int(T) apart from its endpoints. Their union tau has a simple Jordan projection: its upper arc is inside T and its lower arc outside. At each common-face seam the two pieces occupy opposite projected sides, so their annular surface collar projects injectively there as well. Compactness provides an annular neighbourhood of the whole tau on F with injective projection.

## 3. The framing is zero, and the homology class is nonzero

First make that projected annular collar a SMOOTH graph where necessary; a projection homeomorphism alone would not make the vertical vector transverse. Near a common-face seam, transversality of F to S0 gives actual parameters (s,z) with F written x=g(s,z), where x is the in-plane normal to the seam, z is height, g(s,0)=0, and g(s,z) has the sign of z because the two domain germs are opposite. Thus partial_z g(s,0) is nonnegative. In a smaller seam collar add epsilon z to this x graph, with epsilon positive. This is an actual supported ambient graph translation, fixes the seam z=0, and is arbitrarily C1-small. On a sufficiently small collar partial_z g+epsilon is positive. Match its outer collars to the existing smooth graph pieces, using their positive graph derivatives; finite compact collars give one embedded annular isotopy. Supports at the two seams are disjoint and miss the tube and books. The loop and annulus are thereby carried to a smooth projection graph, with an isotopic Jordan curve and the homology class transported. No framing is inferred from a merely topological cusp.

Use the vertical transverse direction on this smooth projected annulus as the normal interval framing of F along tau. Each of its two normal translates is a graph over the same projected Jordan curve. Extend that boundary graph height across its planar Jordan disk, using its actual disk parameter and a radial interpolation to a constant near the centre. The graph disk bounding either small translate misses the original tau, whose projection is the boundary circle but whose height there differs. Thus each of the two linking numbers with the normal transfer is zero. The full normal-transfer self-pairing of tau is ZERO.

If a1,a2 have different colours, tau meets the proper relative arc system F intersect W exactly ONCE, at its white common-face interval. Its two graph interiors miss W. Mod-two intersection with that relative cycle proves [tau] nonzero in H1(F;F2), hence its integral class is nonzero.

If they have the SAME colour, use the actual endpoint rule instead of claiming that two visible seams automatically give a graph cycle. Each of the two gamma boundary routes from x1 to x2 contains a nonempty interval on boundary F. Indeed a route with no tube interval could pass only through common-face intervals and saddle-face arcs. Each saddle arc switches black to white. To join two distinct same-colour common-face intervals without a tube would therefore require a common-face interval with TWO crossing ends, excluded by the accepted selected-saddle endpoint rule. A single saddle without an intermediate face interval joins opposite colours and is not this case.

Suppose [tau]=0 in mod-two homology. Since tau has its annular collar, it bounds a compact subsurface G of F with boundary exactly tau and NO boundary-F interval. The proper arc alpha_plus cuts the upper disk X into two disks. At its interior G must contain exactly one of these two sides. Its closure therefore contains the corresponding entire gamma route between x1,x2. That route contains a boundary-F interval by the preceding paragraph. This contradicts boundary G=tau. Thus [tau] is nonzero mod2 also in the same-colour case. Integral H1 of a compact surface with boundary is free, so its integral class is nonzero.

Positive definiteness now contradicts the ZERO self-pairing of this nonzero class. The assumed shared global lower disk cannot exist.
