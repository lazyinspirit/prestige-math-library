## Fixed conventions and boundaries

1. For finite $F\subseteq I$, a cylinder is $\pi_F^{-1}(A)$ with $A$ in the finite product sigma-algebra. All finite-coordinate measurable cylinders form an algebra; rectangle cylinders are separately retained as a pi-system for uniqueness.
2. A finite-dimensional family is indexed by finite coordinate sets and is compatible under projection and reordering. One-dimensional marginals never determine the required joint data.
3. `thm-countable-product-of-probability-spaces` is only the product-marginal theorem for a countable sequence of arbitrary measurable probability spaces. It is not the general consistent-FDD theorem and it does not rely on Polishness.
4. A standard Borel space is a measurable space measurably isomorphic to a Polish Borel space. A chosen topology is not part of the datum.
5. The arbitrary-index theorem is scoped to standard-Borel coordinates and to the generated cylinder/product sigma-algebra. It makes no assertion about every path subset or arbitrary path functional.
6. Equality in law, modification, and indistinguishability remain distinct. For a modification the diagonal must be measurable and equality is almost sure separately at each time; indistinguishability requires one measurable null exceptional set.
7. The arbitrary-product compactness route uses the published `thm-tychonoff`, whose statement assumes full AC; Caratheodory extension also records countable choice. The eventual theorem must expose that choice strength (or replace this dependency with a separately proved weaker route), not silently present an AC proof as choice-free.
