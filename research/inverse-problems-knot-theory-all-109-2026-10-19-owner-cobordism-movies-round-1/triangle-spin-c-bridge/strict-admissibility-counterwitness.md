# The strict admissibility correction

This is the mathematical reason for replacing the original `2N>0 / coefficient at least N` clause by `2N>=0 / coefficient strictly greater than N`. It does not assert infinitely many holomorphic solutions.

Start with the product genus-one splitting of S1×S2, whose meridians are disjoint parallel circles α1,β1 bounding an annulus A in the Heegaard torus. Stabilize in a ball. On the second handle choose α2,β2 intersecting once. Thus [α1]=[β1]=a1, [α2]=a2, [β2]=b2 are three independent classes except for the displayed equality. The added handle is the usual cancelling one/two-handle pair in that ball; it does not change S1×S2.

Make a narrow finger of α2 across β1 and back, ending inside A before α1. Make a narrow finger of β2 across α1 and back, ending inside A before β1. These supports can be chosen disjoint: the complement of a regular neighborhood of the once-intersecting α2∪β2 in the complement of int(A) is a pair of pants. Use disjoint seams from its inner rim to its two other rims, with distinct initial points; extend each seam only a short way into A. Each finger is an isotopy of its own attaching circle, disjoint from the other circle of the same system. It supplies two transverse intersections with the opposite boundary of A. Choose x1∈α1∩β2 and x2∈α2∩β1. Then x=(x1,x2) is a genuine cross-matched generator. Choose w,z close together outside A and all finger supports.

The region chain A has coefficients 0 and 1. Subdivision by the finger arcs does not change its Euler measure: its boundary is straight at each new crossing, so the two quarter-corner contributions cancel the subdivision's edge contribution. Hence e(A)=χ(A)=0. At each of x1,x2 exactly two adjacent quadrants have coefficient 1, so n_x(A)=1/2+1/2=1. Also n_w(A)=0. The capped A is the product S2 generator: the original meridian disks and annulus are unchanged by the two other fingers, and the resulting sphere meets the product S1 once. The actual finite Morse cap calculation in `lem-balanced-periodic-domain-morse-chern-evaluation` gives c1(s_w(x))[A]=0+2·1−0=2.

Every normalized periodic domain is an integer multiple of A. Indeed, writing its boundary as a1'α1+a2'α2+b1'β1+b2'β2 and taking surface homology gives a2'=b2'=0 and a1'+b1'=0. Subtract the resulting multiple of A; the remainder is a constant surface chain, and its zero w coefficient makes that constant zero. Therefore the original criterion is satisfied: the domains with Chern value 2N>0 are tA, t>0, and max(tA)=t=N. The strict criterion rejects A because max(A)=1 is not strictly greater than 1.

The failure affects the intended finite-domain consequence. Q=Σ−A is nonnegative, n_w(Q)=1, and its actual periodic index is −2+2=0. In symmetric degree two every formal periodic domain is realized by the explicit finite fundamental-group filling and unit-divisor-sphere adjustment in `lem-heegaard-polygon-relative-maslov-variation-is-chern-plus-marks`. Thus all tQ, t≥0, are realized nonnegative periodic domains of index zero with unbounded mark degree. This is an actual infinite family of topological Whitney classes and domains under the old clause. It does not say that these classes admit holomorphic representatives. The repaired strict hypothesis excludes precisely this zero-index nonnegative recession direction, as proved in `lem-strong-polygon-admissibility-bounds-indices-and-positive-domains`.

The source distinction is deliberate: OS0101206 Definitions4.10/8.8 use the strict criterion including N=0. The actually read Zemke1610.05207v3 PDF12 Definition3.2 has the nonstrict displayed wording. The latter is not reported as an OCR error or silently substituted for the strict hypothesis used in the proof.
