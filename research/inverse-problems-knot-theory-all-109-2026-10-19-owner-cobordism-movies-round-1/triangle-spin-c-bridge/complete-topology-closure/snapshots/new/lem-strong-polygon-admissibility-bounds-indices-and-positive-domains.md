## Statement

Assume countable choice and the actual finite balanced polygon data, a Spin-c structure $\mathfrak s$ on $W$, and one realized reference polygon $u_0$. Let $L$ be the lattice of formal integral periodic region chains $Q$ with $j\operatorname{PD}H(Q)=0$, and let $G\subset L$ impose zero multiplicity at every $w_l$. On $G$ put $\ell(P)=\langle c_1(\mathfrak s),H(P)\rangle/2$. The values of $\ell$ are integers. Assume the strict strong-admissibility condition
$$0\ne P\in G,\quad\ell(P)=N\ge0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\max_R P(R)>N.$$
Then the relative-index functional
$$\nu(Q)=\langle c_1(\mathfrak s),H(Q)\rangle+2\sum_l n_{w_l}(Q)$$
is strictly positive on every nonzero nonnegative direction in $L$. The nonnegative domains of actual same-corner, same-Spin-c polygons have a finite lower relative-index bound. For every integer $K$, only finitely many such nonnegative integral domains have relative index at most $K$. These statements concern domains, not the number of literal homotopy classes or holomorphic maps with one domain. They apply to actual Fredholm indices when the fixed-cap relative-index identification has been supplied.

For a boundary-pair sector the same conclusions hold with $L$ the FULL pair-periodic lattice, $G$ its all-mark-zero sublattice, and the Chern evaluation taken on its capped class in $Y$. Thus every nonempty fixed-end strip sector has a lower actual index bound $L_0$ and finitely many nonnegative domains of indices in $[L_0,K]$. For fixed attaching curves, marks and labels these bounds and finite sets are independent of the Cauchy–Riemann coefficients, auxiliary family parameters and source conformal parameters satisfying the same index conventions; they depend only on the finite domain lattice and Chern functional. No uniformity under an unspecified change of attaching curves is asserted.

There is also a weaker fixed-index statement. If every nonzero $P\in G$ with $\ell(P)=0$ has both signs, then at a fixed entire mark vector and any fixed finite set of relative indices there are only finitely many nonnegative domains in the fixed Spin-c sector. The pair version may use exactly the ordinary sector-weak condition only on REALIZED normalized index-zero periodic classes, rather than on every formal periodic chain. This proves a finite-domain statement only for supplied actual classes; it neither establishes invariance under moves nor supplies a generator in an intended sector whose generator set is empty. It does not give an unrestricted lower index bound. Any separately supplied uniform positive area bound also bounds every nonnegative component domain and hence its index, by its finite region coefficients.

There is a separate all-Spin-c zero-mark conclusion. If every nonzero formal periodic domain with all $w$ multiplicities zero has both positive and negative coefficients, then for fixed corners and fixed zero $w$ multiplicities there are only finitely many nonnegative domains, without fixing a Spin-c structure or an index. This global multi-periodic hypothesis is not inferred from strong admissibility for one Spin-c structure or from pairwise weak admissibility. No existence or winding construction of either type of diagram is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite region basis, attaching and mark data, actual reference polygon, Spin-c label and the stated strict or global admissibility hypothesis.

[F1] Capped formal domains and their boundary-pair decomposition are finite integral homology calculations. The kernel of the capped-domain map consists of one-system cut domains $A^i_l$, with $n_{w_j}(A^i_l)=\delta_{jl}$ and $H(A^i_l)=0$. Equal Spin-c differences have $j\operatorname{PD}H=0$. [[lem-heegaard-polygon-spin-c-difference-is-the-capped-periodic-domain]]

[F2] The actual same-Spin-c relative polygon Maslov number is $\nu(D(u)-D(u_0))$. Formal pair domains can be realized in symmetric degree at least two; the degree-one case is compared by an extra cancelling Morse pair and constant root. [[lem-heegaard-polygon-relative-maslov-variation-is-chern-plus-marks]]

[F3] The actual periodic strip index is the Morse Chern evaluation plus twice the total mark degree. Unit-divisor spheres and based one-system disks used in [F2] do not supply a homotopy-class/domain bijection. [[lem-realized-periodic-whitney-index-is-morse-chern-plus-marks]] [[lem-finite-heegaard-surface-polygon-supplies-unit-divisor-spheres]]

## Proof

1.1 **The lattice and its integral half-Chern functional.** For the pair version take the full pair-periodic lattice; [F2] uses the product cobordism $Y\times[0,1]$ and gives its actual relative strip index. For the polygon version use the stated $j\operatorname{PD}$ kernel. In either case, in the finite region basis being periodic is an integral kernel condition on edge jumps. The additional capped homology condition is another finite integral kernel condition, allowing the finite torsion congruences of the map $j\operatorname{PD}$. Thus $L$ is a finitely generated subgroup of $\mathbb Z^r$. The proof of [F1] uses only capped chains and their finite exact sequences to decompose a zero-mark member of this kernel into normalized adjacent pair domains; it does not require that member to be a polygon difference. On a pair domain in degree at least two, [F2] gives an actual periodic strip. Its Maslov number is even: orient the product torus by its ordered circle tangents, so its determinant boundary phase on this loop is the square of a globally defined nonzero determinant frame; identical corner caps cancel. With zero marks [F3] therefore makes its Chern evaluation even. In degree one use the extra cancelling Morse pair in [F2], normalize at its new mark by a one-system domain, and realize that normalized degree-two pair domain. This leaves the capped class and its Chern evaluation unchanged. Summing the pair evaluations proves $\ell(G)\subset\mathbb Z$. All the coefficient and Chern maps extend rationally to the rational span of these lattices. [F1, F2, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 **Every positive recession direction has positive index.** Suppose $0\ne Q\in L$ has every coefficient nonnegative. Put $b_l=n_{w_l}(Q)\ge0$ and $P=Q-\sum_l b_lA^0_l\in G$. If $P=0$, then $\nu(Q)=2\sum_l b_l>0$. Otherwise suppose $\nu(Q)\le0$. Then $$\ell(-P)=\sum_l b_l-\nu(Q)/2\ge\sum_l b_l\ge0.$$ This is an integer by step1.1. Since the $A^0_l$ are the disjoint cut components of one system, each region of $\sum_l b_lA^0_l$ has one of the coefficients $b_l$. Consequently every coefficient of $-P=\sum_l b_lA^0_l-Q$ is at most $\max_l b_l\le\sum_l b_l\le\ell(-P)$. This contradicts strict strong admissibility applied to $-P$. Thus $\nu(Q)>0$. The proof uses the sum of ALL mark coefficients; replacing it by a single mark would be incorrect in a balanced multi-based diagram. [F1, step 1.1, given, algebra]

3.1 **Exclude real escaping directions as well.** Let $V$ be the real span of $L$. A nonzero $q\in V$ with $q\ge0$ and $\nu(q)\le0$ would give a nonzero rational vector with the same inequalities. For an explicit rational-face argument, retain the zero-coordinate equations of $q$, add $\nu=0$ if equality holds, and impose all remaining strict inequalities in one small neighborhood. Gaussian elimination over $\mathbb Q$ parametrizes the resulting rational linear subspace; approximate its finitely many free coordinates by rationals within that neighborhood. If $\nu(q)<0$, preserve that inequality too. Clear denominators and multiply by the finite index of $L$ in $V\cap\mathbb Z^r$ to obtain a nonzero member of $L$ forbidden by step2.1. This proves that the closed cone $\{q\in V:q\ge0,\nu(q)\le0\}$ is zero. [step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 **Finite slices and a lower bound.** Fix $K$ and consider real domains $d\in D(u_0)+V$ with $d\ge0$ and $\nu(d-D(u_0))\le K$. If this set were unbounded, take a sequence with norms tending to infinity and a convergent subsequence of its normalized vectors in the finite-dimensional unit sphere. Such a subsequence is obtained by repeatedly taking the lexicographically first subbox containing infinitely many terms. Its limit is a unit vector $q\in V$, $q\ge0$, $\nu(q)\le0$, contradicting step3.1. Hence the set is bounded and contains only finitely many integer vectors. The same argument excludes relative indices tending to minus infinity: their corresponding norms must tend to infinity, since a linear functional is bounded on every bounded set, and normalization again gives the forbidden cone vector. Thus the nonnegative affine domains have a finite lower index bound. Actual polygon domains form a subset, and [F2] supplies their relative-index interpretation. This proves both claims without asserting finiteness of classes over a domain. The pair version is the same argument on its full periodic lattice. There are only finitely many end generators, so taking minima of the finitely many nonempty sectors gives one common lower bound and taking the finite union gives the required finite domain set for every bounded index band. These are purely topological finite sets; changing the operator coefficients or adding specified finite parameter columns does not change them. Source or external parameters contribute their stated finite index shifts, so a fixed finite count-dimension band is still one fixed upper topological-index band. [F2, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 **Distinguish the weak fixed-sector and global branches.** For the sector-weak claim restrict first to the rational kernel $G_0=\ker\ell\cap G$. Domains of one fixed mark vector and relative index have differences in $G_0$ by [F2]. Its real span has no nonzero nonnegative vector by precisely the rational-face argument of step3.1, using the sector-weak hypothesis in place of step2.1. The normalization argument of step4.1 therefore bounds this affine slice, and its integer vectors are finite. For the ordinary pair-sector convention requiring mixed signs only for realized normalized index-zero periodic classes, use their DOMAIN subgroup instead of the full formal kernel. It is a subgroup: concatenate actual periodic classes for addition and reverse their source orientation for negation. Any two actual strips with the same endpoints, index and entire marked vector differ by an actual periodic class, obtained by concatenating the first with the reverse of the second, so their domain difference lies in this subgroup. Every subgroup of $\mathbb Z^r$ is finitely generated: induct on $r$, take the least positive nonzero coordinate in its last-coordinate image and one actual preimage, and reduce to its last-coordinate-zero subgroup. This uses only finitely many such selections. Its rational span consequently has finite index saturation, so the same rational-face and bounded-affine-slice proof applies to this ACTUAL subgroup, including symmetric degree one. Thus the literal ordinary sector-weak input is sufficient, without silently replacing it by global or formal weak admissibility. Take a finite union over the specified indices. No statement about all negative indices follows. If positive region areas are $a_1,\ldots,a_r$ and a nonnegative domain has area at most $E$, then its $i$th coefficient is at most $E/a_i$; this independently gives a finite domain set and a finite index range for all component domains satisfying that same bound. For the all-Spin-c claim use instead the full periodic lattice with zero at every mark and no $j\operatorname{PD}$ condition. The global hypothesis excludes every nonzero nonnegative member. The same rational-face argument excludes every nonzero nonnegative vector in its real span. An unbounded nonnegative affine domain set with the fixed zero marks would, after normalization as in step4.1, supply just such a vector. Therefore that set is bounded and its integral domains are finite, without imposing an index or Spin-c label. This argument uses exactly the stated global multi-periodic hypothesis; it does not replace it by a collection of pair hypotheses or claim that a required globally admissible diagram has already been constructed. [F2, step 3.1, step 4.1, given, algebra] ∎
