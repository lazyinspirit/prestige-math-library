## Statement

Assume AC. Let $M$ be the compact exterior of a smooth fibered knot, equipped with a compatible finite PL structure, and let $G$ be its connected fiber, with nonempty connected boundary. Let $S$ be a connected two-sided properly embedded incompressible finite PL surface. Suppose their boundaries have been aligned, with compatible product collars, and that there is a homeomorphism $h:S\to G$ and a homotopy from the inclusion of $S$ to the inclusion of $G$ composed with $h$, fixed on this aligned boundary through maps into $M$. Then $S$ and $G$ are ambient isotopic relative to $\partial M$. This assertion concerns the actual marked original fiber, not just existence of some fibration with fiber $S$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite structures, proper collars, injections of the two surface groups, and the actual boundary-relative homotopy.

[F1] Half-product normalization gives actual relative graph positions and boundary-parallel products for finite injected surface families with boundary on one end of a compact surface product. [[thm-relative-pl-half-products-have-horizontal-incompressible-surfaces]]

[F2] Prescribed essential arcs homotopic into an injected boundary complement give proper embedded disks. The fixed-priority disk surgeries retain their prescribed arcs, and paired-window ball reconstruction gives the marked product. [[lem-incompressible-boundary-complements-give-controlled-pl-arc-disks]], [[thm-boundary-arc-homotopies-give-finite-pl-surface-products]]

[F3] The actual finite relative preimage proof uses interior compression balls, cap fillings, the target cut pieces' $\pi_2=0$, and the target's $\pi_3=0$ to make injected nonspherical preimages, fixing its specified transverse boundary pattern. Its published Statement requires a boundary local homeomorphism; the exact more general boundary pattern used here is separately justified in step 1.1 rather than silently assumed to satisfy that Statement. [[lem-aspherical-pl-targets-admit-incompressible-surface-preimages]]

[F4] For a supplied finite PL pair, incompressibility is equivalent to group injection, and cutting preserves the face and piece injections. Injected finite surface cuts preserve irreducibility. The proper surface-map classifier gives coverings or annular collapses, fixing actual boundary local homeomorphisms. [[lem-finite-pl-two-sided-incompressibility-is-pi-one-injectivity]], [[lem-finite-pi1-injective-surface-cuts-preserve-three-manifold-irreducibility]], [[thm-pi-one-injective-finite-pl-surface-maps-are-covers-or-annular-collapses]]

[F5] Finite disk-window and surface polygon parameters supply the relative product and empty-ball moves. [[lem-parametrized-pl-balls-absorb-standard-boundary-windows]], [[lem-finite-polygonal-disk-and-collar-surgery]]

[A1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]] is retained throughout.

## Proof

1.1 We verify the relative preimage operation needed here. A homotopy domain is a compact surface times an interval, with its two end maps and side map protected on a finite collar. First make their prescribed boundary maps transverse to the relevant target boundary curves; protect that resulting pattern. The interior inverse image of a collared target surface is made PL product-transverse by varying its finitely many normal vertex values, as in [F3]. This does not require the tangential boundary map to be a local homeomorphism: only the already fixed transverse normal boundary values are used. Compress a noninjective preimage component by an interior disk. Its image boundary contracts in the injected target surface; use that filling for the two new cap faces. The three complementary compression-ball chambers are balls with sphere boundary maps into actual target cut pieces. Their $\pi_2=0$ extends those maps with strictly positive or negative collar heights, creating exactly the two cap preimages. The old and new maps are homotopic relative their ball boundary by target $\pi_3=0$. The nonnegative lexicographic triple consisting of negative Euler sum, total genus and annulus count decreases under every compression. Remove sphere preimages by their domain balls; every other component has boundary outside that interior ball or an injected nontrivial group, so only other sphere components can be deleted with it. The same cap and $\pi_3$ construction gives an actual homotopy. These operations are ALL interior, so preserve the protected boundary map and its transverse pattern. No closed positive-genus component can remain in this surface-times-interval domain: its injection followed by projection to the domain base surface would be a closed-source injective map to a boundary surface, excluded by the actual classifier [F4]. Thus every remaining component has boundary on the protected end. This proves the required extension of [F3]'s actual local argument, without using its stronger boundary hypothesis as though it held here. [F3, F4, F5, given, construct]

2.1 First obtain the surface-group injections from the actual incompressibility hypothesis by [F4]; the fiber injection is also immediate from its product cyclic cover. The target vanishing assumptions in step 1.1 hold on the pieces actually used below. The fibered exterior is aspherical: its cyclic cover is $G\times\mathbb R$, and the universal cover of $G$ is a contractible tree-thickening, since $G$ has a finite graph spine. The same is true for compact products of such surfaces. Further injected cuts have aspherical pieces. To see this, lift their graph-of-spaces decomposition to the universal cover; piece-group injections make the lifted incidence graph a tree and each edge cover is a contractible surface. A cycle in one simply connected piece cover that bounds in the total contractible cover uses only finitely many tree pieces. Prune leaves of that finite tree by Mayer–Vietoris with the contractible edge cover; its positive-degree homology classes already bound in the original piece. Thus every piece cover is acyclic. Hurewicz and Whitehead make this simply connected CW cover contractible. Irreducibility of the products follows from their standard handlebody models, and irreducibility of their injected cuts follows from [F4]. These are actual vanishing and disk-cut verifications, not an imported sphere theorem. [F1, F4, construct]

3.1 We record the relative boundary product operation in the form needed at a cut stage. Let $Q$ be an injected proper surface in one of these irreducible targets, let $B$ be an injected surface in its ambient boundary with $\partial Q\subset\operatorname{Int}B$, and suppose a map $H\times I$ starts with a finite covering onto $Q$ and sends its other boundary into $B$. If $Q$ is a disk, its rim bounds a disk in $B$ by the injection of $B$; irreducibility and the marked ball-window construction give their actual parallelism directly. Otherwise every rim of $Q$ is essential in $B$, since it is infinite order in $Q$ and $Q$ injects in the target. First assume the homotopy interior misses $Q$. Split at $Q$ and lift this map into one component $N$. A copy of $Q$ is a boundary face there; the portions $E$ of $B$ on that side are adjacent to every rim of that face. Their groups inject: each component of $B$ cut along the essential $\partial Q$ curves injects in $B$ by the surface amalgam normal form, and then in the original target, hence in $N$. Any proper arc of the $Q$ face lifts to the covering $H$. Its rectangle in $H\times I$ gives a disk map from that arc to a comparison path in $E$, with endpoints held by adjoining the two side paths in $E$. Thus the exact arc homotopies hold IN $N$, not only before the cut. [F2, F4, step 2.1, construct]

3.2 We next remove the assumption that the homotopy interior misses $Q$, for the boundary case needed below: $B$ is a subsurface of a fiber and therefore has free fundamental group. Push the side collars of the homotopy slightly into $B\setminus\partial Q$, protecting its bottom covering onto $Q$. On its upper end make the map transverse to $\partial Q$, remove contractible preimage circles by the disk-and-strip operation in the surface-map classifier, and minimize the remaining number. Those curves are essential on the upper domain surface. Each maps with nonzero degree to its target boundary circle, because the upper map induces the same injection as the bottom covering. Homotope these circle maps to their nonzero-degree circle coverings and straighten their thin transverse collars, preserving the exact preimage curves and their number. Thus their actual boundary maps are now local homeomorphisms and are protected in the next operation. If no preimage component remains, apply steps 3.1 and 4.1 directly. Otherwise apply step 1.1 to normalize the interior inverse image of $Q$, keeping that entire upper boundary map fixed. Its components have boundary only on the upper end. By [F1], each is parallel to an upper subsurface. Choose one nearest that end, so the region between them contains no other preimage; graph order in [F1] supplies this finite choice. Its map into $Q$ is injective on groups, since the whole homotopy induces the same injected group map as the original covering. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct]

3.3 Return to $S,G\subset M$. Minimize their finite number of interior intersection circles by boundary-fixed isotopies, keeping their common boundary collars on prescribed sides so that no intersection arc ends there. Any circle contractible on either surface is contractible on both by their injections. Choose an innermost disk cap on one surface, whose interior misses the other. The surface disk on the other and that cap bound a ball whose interior misses the rest of the surface being moved, since its boundary lies on the ambient boundary outside the ball. Its other-surface intersections may change during the move, but the final replacement close to the cap removes the selected circle and every trace in the discarded disk, adding none. The marked-ball move [F5] is an ambient isotopy of the moved surface. Thus every remaining interior circle is essential on both. Apply step 1.1 to the actual supplied homotopy from $S$ to $G$, protecting its two end maps and the side collar. Near the final end its preimage is precisely that end; near the side it is the prescribed collar. The remaining preimage components have boundary on the initial end $S$. Apply [F1] with interval direction reversed. If this remaining system is empty, use the whole initial surface and the original homotopy directly in the cut-stage argument below. Otherwise choose a component nearest the initial end and its parallel region with no further preimage. The initial-end patch has no interior intersection curve, since transversality there would extend it into that empty region. Hence it is one whole component of $S$ cut at $G$. [F1, F3, F4, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 The individual prescribed-arc proof [F2] works with these supplied portions $E$, even if they are not initially the whole complement of the $Q$ face on its boundary component. Double $N$ along $E$: all rims of $Q$ are in the gluing boundary, so the two $Q$ copies form the closed doubled-$Q$ boundary component. Extra rims of $E$ form other boundary components and do not meet it. The fold map still injects each $E$ group into the double. Doubling the prescribed arc, applying the Loop theorem in its thin boundary annulus, and removing circle intersections with $E$ therefore gives exactly the same prescribed-arc disk. Use the fixed-priority surgeries of [F2] to make a complete cutting-arc disk system disjoint. A regular neighborhood of the $Q$ face and those disks has one frontier disk, because the arcs cut $Q$ to a disk. Its rim lies in the interior of $E$ and contracts in $N$ via that frontier disk. Injection of $E$ makes it bound a disk there. This disk misses every comparison arc: their endpoints are on the old $Q$ rims and they cannot cross the frontier rim. The frontier disk and this boundary disk bound a ball by irreducibility. Attach that ball through its marked window to the regular neighborhood. The paired-window reconstruction in [F2] gives an actual product between the supplied $Q$ face and a surface in $E$. If $Q$ is a disk, take no cutting arcs and use the same frontier cap. This proves the boundary product operation on the actual cut side and with the actual complement injections. [F2, F4, F5, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 If that component maps to a covering of $Q$, its parallel region gives a new homotopy with no interior preimage of $Q$, reducing to steps 3.1 and 4.1. The disk case cannot be an obstruction: its parallel upper disk would be bounded by a contractible upper preimage curve, already removed in step 3.2. The only other case in [F4] is an annulus collapsing to one boundary circle $k$ of $Q$. Its parallel upper annulus has both rims mapping to $k$. Their comparison map into $B$ is homotopic relative to both rims into $k$. Here is the needed group check, instead of assuming a general annulus theorem. The core has nonzero image in $k$, and its end-to-end track centralizes a nonzero power of $k$ in the free group $\pi_1B$. In the reduced-word tree a nontrivial element translates its unique bi-infinite repeated-word axis. A commuting element preserves that axis and acts on it by an integral translation; the least positive translation is the primitive root. The simple essential curve $k$ is not a proper power, by the surface proof in [F4], so its centralizer is exactly $\langle k\rangle$. Both the core and track therefore lie in that cyclic subgroup. Since $B$ retracts onto a graph, this gives the claimed relative annulus homotopy. The interior of this upper annulus misses every preimage curve by the nearest-component choice, so it approaches the SAME side of $k$ at both rims. After the collapse push it to the common outside side, using its two thin transverse collars. This deletes both rim preimage curves and creates none, contradicting the minimal count of step 3.2. Thus only the covering case remains, proving the boundary product operation without the no-interior-preimage assumption. [F1, F4, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 3.2, construct]

6.1 Split $M$ at $G$. The chosen parallel region maps into one cut component, which is the ACTUAL fiber product $G\times I$. The initial-end patch is an injected proper surface $Q$ in it: otherwise a compression would make one of the retained $S\cap G$ circles inessential, contrary to step 3.3; equivalently use the injections in [F4]. The other end of the region maps into one boundary copy $B$ of $G$. Taking the boundary-fixed level homotopy in its parallelism gives a map of a surface times interval whose bottom is a homeomorphism onto $Q$ and whose other boundary maps into $B$. Thus the exact boundary product operation of steps 3.1, 4.1, 3.2 and 5.1 applies. Notice that it is applied in this CUT product, with $B$ an injected free-group boundary surface; no homotopy before the cut is used to supply an arc homotopy after it. We obtain an embedded product between $Q$ and a subsurface of that one boundary copy. [F1, F2, F4, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 3.2, step 5.1, step 3.3, construct]

7.1 Other components of the cut $S$ may lie in this product. They inject there because a loop killed there is killed in the cut target and hence in that surface by [F4]. Their boundaries lie on its $B$ end, and they have no closed components: cutting the original boundary surface along the retained essential circles cannot create one. Apply [F1] to this finite family inside the actual product. Choose its component nearest the $B$ end, or use $Q$ if the family is empty. Its graph-order product to a subsurface of $B$ is empty of every other cut-surface component. This produces the family-innermost product patch. It meets only the ONE chosen copy of $G$ on the boundary of the cut target, so regluing that target into $M$ is injective on this patch and its product. We therefore have an embedded product DOWNSTAIRS between patches of $S$ and $G$, with interior disjoint from both surfaces. No arbitrary covering-space isotopy has been projected. [F1, F4, step 6.1, construct]

8.1 If the selected patch is proper in $S$, push it across its empty product and slightly beyond $G$, keeping any part of the original boundary fixed in its quadrant collar. Its interior frontier consists of at least one of the retained intersection circles. The move removes those circles and introduces none; finite graph-roof interpolation in the marked product and its collar gives the ambient isotopy [F5]. A disk patch cannot hide additional intersection circles in the opposite patch, since such a circle would be contractible on $G$, already excluded in step 3.3. This contradicts the chosen minimum. Thus the selected patch is all of $S$. Its opposite patch is all of $G$: it has boundary exactly the aligned sole boundary of $G$, and a connected subsurface with no interior frontier in connected $G$ is the whole surface. The actual product gives the required boundary-relative isotopy. If the fiber is a disk, the same conclusion follows directly from the disk-cut and marked-ball case of [F1,F5]. This proves the literal marked-fiber assertion. [F1, F5, step 3.3, step 7.1, construct] ∎
