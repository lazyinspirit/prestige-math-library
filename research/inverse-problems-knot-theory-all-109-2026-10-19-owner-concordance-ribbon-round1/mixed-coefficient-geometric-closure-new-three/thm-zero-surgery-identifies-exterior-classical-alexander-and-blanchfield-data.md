## Statement

Supply a finite polygonal oriented disk-band Seifert-surface model and local ambient smooth roundings for a smooth oriented knot $K\subset S^3$, with compatible closed product tube, positive meridian $\mu$, preferred longitude $\lambda$, collars and peripheral paths. Put $X=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}N(K)$, $T=\partial X$, and let $M=X\cup_T V$ be its actual preferred zero-longitude surgery, oriented to extend $X$. Let $k=\mathbb Z$ or a commutative field, $R=k[t,t^{-1}]$, and let $F$ be its fraction field, so $F=\mathbb Q(t)$ when $k=\mathbb Z$. Use the associated based right coefficient systems sending $\mu$ to $t$ with transport by the inverse unit, and their standard parallel Hermitian form.

The actual maps induce isomorphisms
$$i_*:H_1(X;R)\cong H_1(M;R),\qquad j_*:H_1(X;R)\cong H_1(X,T;R).$$
The closed Bockstein–duality pairing $B_M$ is defined on the whole first homology and is nonsingular. On $X$ use the following explicit classical cocycle rule: take an integral $R^\vee$-valued two-cocycle $a$ whose cap class is $\overline{j_*x}$, take one whole $F^\vee$-valued one-cochain $b$ with $\partial^*b=a$, and set $B_X(x,y)=[b(y)]\in F/R$. Such primitives exist, this rule is independent of all representatives and choices, and
$$B_X(x,y)=B_M(i_*x,i_*y).$$
Thus it is the nonsingular exterior classical Blanchfield form with the same positive-meridian, positive-push and first-variable conjugate-linear conventions. Based axis classes in the exterior are carried to their actual surgery classes. The constructions commute with the rational and prime coefficient maps wherever the displayed forms are compared; no choice principle, arbitrary knot projection theorem or four-dimensional normal-neighborhood claim is assumed.

**Given:** The finite smooth knot model, actual peripheral products and collars, coefficient ring and convention in the Statement.

## Proof

1.1 The finite polygonal knot complement is path-connected by polygonal paths avoiding its finitely many segments: choose an intermediate point off the finitely many forbidden affine incidences. Tube-radial pushing sends such a path to $X$; the supplied ambient roundings transport this statement. The torus and filling solid torus are path-connected too. Their finite local cellular calculation gives $$H_0(T;R)=R/(t-1),\quad H_1(T;R)=(R/(t-1))[\lambda],\quad H_2(T;R)=0,$$ and $H_0(V;R)=R/(t-1)$, $H_1(V;R)=0$. Indeed the torus square has degree-one boundary $(t^{-1}-1,0)$ and degree-two boundary $(0,1-t^{-1})$, up to its harmless overall orientation sign; multiplication by $t-1$ is injective. The filling core is $\mu$, so its circle complex has the single differential $t^{-1}-1$. Degree-zero local homology on $X$ and $M$ is also $R/(t-1)$, since paths identify point fibers and the meridian generates the transport image. Thus the degree-zero inclusions from $T$ are the natural isomorphisms. [F1, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The infinite-cyclic covering subgroup of the exterior is $N=\pi_1(X)^{(1)}$, since its abelianization is $\mathbb Z[\mu]$ by [F1]. The longitude lies in $[N,N]$, so [F1]'s actual cover-homology identification sends its local class to zero, for $\mathbb Z$ and every field $k$. Therefore $H_1(T;R)\to H_1(X;R)$ is zero, as its generator is precisely $\lambda$. In Mayer–Vietoris for $M=X\cup_TV$, the following degree-zero difference map is $(1,-1)$ on $R/(t-1)$ and is injective. Since $H_1(V;R)=0$, exactness gives the actual isomorphism $i_*$. The pair sequence of $(X,T)$, with the same zero degree-one map and the degree-zero inclusion an isomorphism, gives the actual isomorphism $j_*$. [F1, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Ordinary zero surgery has $H_1(M;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[\mu]$: the ordinary torus Mayer–Vietoris calculation uses the zero class of the preferred longitude in $X$ and the filling core $\mu$, with $H_1(X;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[\mu]$ from [F1]. Hence [F2] applies to $M$. Its annihilator is nonzero over every stated coefficient field, so localization gives $H_1(M;F)=0$. Over $F$, the torus and filling complexes of step 1.1 are acyclic because $t-1$ is invertible. Thus Mayer–Vietoris and the pair sequence give $H_1(X;F)=H_1(X,T;F)=0$. Relative cap duality [F4] implies $H^2(X;F^\vee)=0$. Also $H^1(X;F^\vee)=0$ without an infinite-dimensional splitting: use a finite cover by convex coordinate balls and half-balls, a finite overlap spanning tree and center paths, and the least-chart straight segment to prescribe paths $\gamma_x:o\to x$. In those frames $C_0$ has bases $e_x$, and $p_x=\gamma_x$ has boundary $e_x-e_o$. The based meridian has boundary $e_o(t^{-1}-1)$; normalize its chain to $v$ with boundary $e_o$. Then $e_x\mapsto p_x+v$, with $p_o=0$, is an explicit right-linear section of $d_1$ on every basis vector. A dual one-cocycle kills $\ker d_1=\operatorname{im}d_2$ and therefore equals its composition with that section and $d_1$, a coboundary. The unitary identification transfers this to intrinsic dual cochains. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.2 The cap restriction identity in this gluing is $$D_X(i^*u)=\overline{j_*x}\quad\text{if }D_M(u)=\overline{i_*x},$$ where $D_X$ takes absolute cohomology to relative homology. To verify it with actual collars, map the fundamental class of $M$ to the relative pair $(M,V)$ and use collared excision to identify that pair's local chains with those of $(X,T)$. Its fundamental class becomes the relative fundamental class of $X$: both have exactly the same local orientation generator at every interior point of $X$. The cap formula on each small simplex commutes with restriction of its coefficient and with this quotient/excision, so the same is true on the actual finite fundamental cycle and hence on homology. The image of $i_*x$ in this pair is exactly the image of $j_*x$. The orientation of $M$ extends $X$, so this equality has no orientation-reversal sign. This proves the displayed cap identity without geometric transversality in a topological four-manifold. [F3, F4, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 For $x\in H_1(X;R)$, relative cap duality gives the absolute degree-two dual class of $\overline{j_*x}$. Choose a cocycle $a$ for it. Its image over $F$ is zero by step 3.1, so a whole field-valued one-cochain $b$ with $\partial^*b=a$ exists by the definition of zero cohomology. This proves existence in the exterior rule. If $b$ is changed for the same $a$, the difference is a field one-cocycle and is a coboundary by step 3.1, hence evaluates to zero on a cycle. Changing $a$ by an integral coboundary changes $b$ by an integral one-cochain up to such a field coboundary, so changes its evaluation only by an element of $R$. Changing a cycle for $y$ by $\partial c$ changes $b(y)$ by $a(c)\in R$. Thus the quotient value is well-defined. Its additivity and conjugate/right linearity follow by adding or scaling actual cocycles and their primitives, with the conjugation supplied by [F4]. This is the classical inverse-Bockstein rule on its explicitly supplied whole primitives; no infinite product of quotient-lift maps is assumed surjective. [F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Take the closed dual class of $\overline{i_*x}$, with an actual representative cocycle $a_M$ and a whole field primitive $b_M$. These exist by [F5] and step 3.1; equivalently its Bockstein inverse is represented by $b_M\bmod R$. Step 3.2 says that $a_M|_X$ is a valid representative for the exterior dual class. Its primitive is $b_M|_X$. The exterior evaluation rule in step 4.1 therefore gives $$B_X(x,y)=[b_M|_X(y)]=[b_M(i_*y)]=B_M(i_*x,i_*y).$$ Since $i_*$ is an isomorphism, and the closed coefficient image is cyclic, [F5] makes the closed pairing and then its transported exterior pairing nonsingular. This establishes the actual classical linking-form identification, beyond the underlying Alexander-module isomorphism. [F4, F5, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra]

6.1 Every based axis loop in $X$ is taken by $i$ to that very loop in $M$, so its module class and its self-value are identified. All maps in steps 1.1–2.1 are actual inclusion or coefficient maps, hence commute with rationalization and prime coefficient change. Whenever linking-form coefficients are changed, the two copies of the equality in step 5.1 identify exterior values with their respective closed values; the uniform-denominator prime reduction is supplied by [F6], rather than by a nonexistent global fraction-field reduction map. All paths, peripheral products, frames and smooth knot models were supplied or obtained by finite chart constructions. The proof did not select infinitely many representatives, add a choice assumption, or invoke a four-dimensional annulus neighborhood. This proves the full supplied-data exterior-to-surgery comparison. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 3.2, step 5.1, algebra] ∎
