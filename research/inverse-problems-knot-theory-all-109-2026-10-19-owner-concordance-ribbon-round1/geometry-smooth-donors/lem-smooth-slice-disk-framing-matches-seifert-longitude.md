## Statement

Assume full AC. Let $D\subset B^4$ be a smooth neat proper disk with boundary $K$, and let $F\subset S^3$ be a smooth oriented Seifert surface for $K$. A product tube of $D$ may be framed so that its boundary frame consists of the inward conormal of $K$ in $F$ and the positive normal of $F$ in $S^3$. Up to an orientation-preserving ambient isotopy fixing $D$, $F$ is then the positive radial sheet in that tube near $K$.

For a sufficiently small closed tube $N=D\times D^2_\epsilon$, put $W=B^4\setminus\operatorname{int}_{B^4}N$, $X=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}_{S^3}(K\times D^2_\epsilon)$ and $Y=D\times S^1_\epsilon$. Both $H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(W;\mathbb Z)$ and $H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(W;\mathbb Z)$ are isomorphisms, carrying a normal-circle meridian to a generator. The disk's boundary section is the zero longitude of $X$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Full AC, the disk $D$, the surface $F$, and product pair collars.

[A1] Full AC is assumed. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] The smooth disk has a framed product tube, and compact smooth neat cooriented hypersurfaces have boundary-preserving bicollars. [[lem-smooth-disk-product-tubes-and-oriented-bicollars]]

[F2] For a supplied knot tube, $H_1(X;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$ is generated by the fibre meridian, and the boundary torus has a unique zero-longitude class projecting once onto the knot. [[lem-knot-exterior-homology-and-longitude-via-mayer-vietoris]]

[F3] The inverse function theorem supplies the local graph descriptions. [[thm-euclidean-inverse-function-theorem]] Mayer–Vietoris applies to open covers; deformation retractions identify their homology groups. The ball is contractible, and the standard sphere groups used in [F2] vanish in degrees one and two. [[thm-mayer-vietoris-sequence-in-singular-homology]], [[thm-homotopy-equivalences-induce-isomorphisms-on-singular-homology]], [[cor-homology-of-spheres]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Take a product tube of radius $2\epsilon$ by [F1]. The relatively open sets $U=D\times\{|z|<3\epsilon/2\}$ and $V=B^4\setminus(D\times D^2_{\epsilon/2})$ cover $B^4$. The first retracts to $D$, their intersection retracts radially to $Y$, and $V$ retracts to $W$ by pushing its radii between $\epsilon/2$ and $\epsilon$ out to $\epsilon$. Product geometry at $K$ makes the same formulas valid at the ambient boundary. Thus the degree-one Mayer–Vietoris map is an isomorphism $$H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong H_1(W;\mathbb Z),$$ since the adjacent groups of $B^4$ and the first homology of $D$ vanish. Reduced degree-zero exactness similarly gives that $W$ is connected. The circle generator of $Y=D\times S^1$ may be based at any point of $D$, including its boundary, so the meridian in $X$ maps to that same generator. Consequently [F2] makes $H_1(X)\to H_1(W)$ an isomorphism as well. [F1, F2, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The section $s\mapsto(s,\epsilon e_1)$ on the boundary torus bounds $D\times\{\epsilon e_1\}\subset Y$, so it vanishes in $H_1(W)$, hence in $H_1(X)$ by step 1.1. Its projection onto $K$ has degree one. It is therefore the unique zero-longitude class of [F2]. The curve cut out by $F$ on a sufficiently small tube boundary also projects once onto $K$ and bounds the trimmed Seifert surface in $X$, so it is the same class. Its existence and transversality follow in boundary coordinates by the inverse function theorem, since the inward tangent of $F$ has a nonzero normal component to $K$; compactness of $K$ permits one sufficiently small radius. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Compare the disk frame along $K$ with the frame consisting of the inward conormal in $F$ and its positive normal. Orient the disk complement so these frames have the same orientation; the comparison is a smooth loop $A(s)\in\mathrm{GL}^+(2,\mathbb R)$. Gram–Schmidt writes $A=R U$, where $R(s)\in\mathrm{SO}(2)$ is its normalized first-column rotation and $U(s)$ is upper triangular with positive diagonal. The winding of $R$ is the meridian coefficient of the difference between its two section curves in the product boundary torus. That coefficient is zero by step 2.1. To make the ensuing extension explicit, parametrize the circle by $s\in[0,2\pi]$. For the unit complex coordinate $r(s)$ of $R$, integrate $\theta'(s)=\operatorname{Im}(r'(s)/r(s))$ with an initial argument of $r(0)$. Differentiation gives $r(s)=e^{i\theta(s)}$, and $\theta(2\pi)-\theta(0)=2\pi n$ for an integer $n$. Interpolation of this lift with $\theta(0)+ns$ proves that the induced first-homology coefficient is $n$, so $n=0$. Thus $\theta$ is a smooth periodic real lift. With a radial cutoff $\chi$ equal to zero near the disk center and one on its boundary collar, the formula $$\widetilde A(\rho e^{is})=R_{\chi(\rho)\theta(s)}\bigl((1-\chi(\rho))I+\chi(\rho)U(s)\bigr)$$ extends $A$ smoothly over the disk in $\mathrm{GL}^+$. Multiply the disk frame by this matrix; then make it a boundary product by the collar adjustment of [F1], which preserves its prescribed values on $K$. The tube construction in [F1] now gives a product tube with exactly the desired boundary normal frame. [F1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Finally straighten the actual surface germ without altering the disk. In the resulting boundary tube write normal coordinates as $(u,v)$ in the conormal and positive-normal directions. The inverse function theorem makes $F$ near $K$ the graph $v=h(s,u)$ for $u\ge0$, with $h(s,0)=\partial_u h(s,0)=0$. Extend $h$ smoothly to small negative $u$ by its boundary chart extensions. For sufficiently small $\delta$, multiply it by cutoffs in $u/\delta$ and $v/\delta$, equal to one near this graph and zero outside the tube. The map $$ (s,u,v)\longmapsto(s,u,v-\chi_1(u/\delta)\chi_2(v/\delta)h(s,u)) $$ is a diffeomorphism: $h=O(u^2)$ uniformly on compact $K$, so its $v$ derivative differs from one by $O(\delta)$ and is positive after shrinking. It is the identity outside a smaller tube and on the core $K$, and sends the graph to $v=0$ near $K$. The same formula with the subtraction multiplied by a parameter in $[0,1]$ is an isotopy. Extend it into the disk pair collar using an additional cutoff in the inward collar coordinate, constant one at the ambient boundary and zero beyond that collar. It still has positive $v$ derivative, fixes $D$ pointwise because $u=v=0$ there, and is the identity elsewhere. This is an orientation-preserving ambient isotopy of $B^4$ straightening $F$ to the positive radial sheet. Pulling later constructions back by its endpoint restores the original surface. The homology assertions proved before the reframing apply equally to the new tube, so all conclusions follow. [F1, step 3.1, construct, algebra] ∎
