## Statement

Assume full AC. Let $D\subset B^4$ be a smooth neat properly embedded disk bounded by an oriented knot $K\subset S^3$, and let $F\subset S^3$ be a connected compact smooth oriented Seifert surface for $K$. After an explicit inward pushing and adjustment of their boundary collars, the capped surface $F\cup_K(-D)$ bounds a compact connected oriented smooth embedded $3$-manifold $R\subset\operatorname{Int}B^4$. Here the notation means the abstract cap, with its corner rounded in a small collar of the attaching circle; it does not require a closed smooth surface inside $B^4$ to retain the original transverse disk boundary points on $S^3$.

The construction identifies a subsurface of $\partial R$ with $F$ minus an arbitrarily small open boundary collar, and this identification induces the usual isomorphism $H_1(F)\cong H_1(\partial R)$. There is an ambient oriented bicollar $R\times[-\epsilon,\epsilon]\hookrightarrow B^4$ whose positive direction along that subsurface agrees with the positive Seifert normal push-off. The original cycles in $S^3$ are connected to these inward copies by disjoint product annuli in an outer shell; the bicollar and all pushed chains may be kept out of the interior of that shell. Thus collared chains in $R$ may be pushed to one complementary side and reattached to the original boundary cycles, as required for the Seifert-metabolizer calculation. No locally flat topological disk is included in this smooth statement.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Full AC, the smooth proper disk $D$, and the connected Seifert surface $F$.

[A1] Full AC is the ambient hypothesis. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] Smooth disk product tubes, matching pair collars, and bicollars for compact cooriented smooth hypersurfaces are supplied with proofs. [[lem-smooth-disk-product-tubes-and-oriented-bicollars]]

[F2] The disk frame can match the Seifert frame and $F$ can be straightened radially near $K$ by an ambient isotopy fixing $D$. The knot and disk exteriors have first homology generated by the same meridian. [[lem-smooth-slice-disk-framing-matches-seifert-longitude]]

[F3] For a compact connected smooth $4$-manifold with connected boundary, a prescribed smooth boundary circle map whose first-homology map extends admits a smooth collar-preserving extension; if $1$ is regular on the boundary, it can be made regular globally relative to that collar. Its fibre is a compact cooriented smooth $3$-manifold with the exact prescribed boundary. [[lem-relative-smooth-circle-map-extension-and-regular-fiber]]

[F4] Mayer–Vietoris is exact for open covers, including the degree-zero segment. [[thm-mayer-vietoris-sequence-in-singular-homology]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F1] and [F2], use a disk tube $\Psi:D\times D^2_{2a}\to B^4$ with product boundary collar and Seifert boundary frame, and straighten $F$ to its positive radial sheet there. The isotopy fixes $D$ and may be pulled back at the end. Put $N=\Psi(D\times D^2_a)$, $W=B^4\setminus\operatorname{int}_{B^4}N$, $X=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}_{S^3}(N\cap S^3)$, and $Y=\Psi(D\times S^1_a)$. They are compact smooth manifolds with corners, $X\cap Y=T=K\times S^1_a$, and $\partial W=X\cup_TY$. The trimmed surface $F_0=F\cap X$ is neat in $X$ and has its single boundary circle at angle $\theta=0$ on $T$. By [F2], $H_1(X)\to H_1(W)$ and $H_1(Y)\to H_1(W)$ are isomorphisms, taking their meridians to the same generator. All coefficients in this proof's homology calculations are integral. [A1, F1, F2, given, construct]

2.1 Give $F_0$ a boundary-preserving bicollar in $X$ by [F1], positive in its Seifert normal direction. Near $T$ choose its transverse field to be the angular field, so its parameter is a fixed positive multiple of $\theta$. A positive transverse field can be prescribed there and combined with other positive transverse fields by finite bumps, as proved in [F1]. Choose a smooth nondecreasing function $\rho:[-1,1]\to[-1,1]$ equal to the endpoints on endpoint collars, with $\rho^{-1}(0)=\{0\}$ and $\rho'(0)>0$. Such a function results by integrating an even nonnegative smooth bump positive near zero and normalizing its integral. Define the map on the bicollar by $e^{i\pi\rho(t)}$ and on its complement by $-1$. Flatness at the endpoint collars makes this a smooth $g_X:X\to S^1$ with inverse image of $1$ exactly $F_0$, and with positive angular derivative there. Its restriction to $T$ is a degree-one circle function $h(\theta)$, independent of $K$, equal to $-1$ outside a small angular arc, with $h^{-1}(1)=\{0\}$. Set $g_Y(d,e^{i\theta})=h(\theta)$. This agrees with $g_X$ on $T$ and is independent of the two corner-normal coordinates near $T$. [F1, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 We specify the corner rounding used to apply [F3]. Near $T$, write $s=r-a\ge0$ and $t\ge0$ for the exterior radial coordinate and ambient inward coordinate. In $u=s-t$, $v=s+t$ the normal quadrant is $v\ge|u|$. Replace its boundary near the origin by $v=b(u)$, where $b$ is a smooth convolution of $|u|$ with an even nonnegative compactly supported unit-mass bump of small width. Then $b\ge|u|$ and $b=|u|$ outside that width. A continuous homeomorphism from the old to the rounded region is given near the origin by $(u,v)\mapsto(u,v+b(u)-|u|)$; away from the origin insert a cutoff in $v$ so the added displacement vanishes, choosing its width much larger than the bump width so its $v$ derivative remains positive. This gives a homeomorphism equal to the identity away from an arbitrarily small product neighborhood of $T$, and the new boundary is smooth. Write $W'$ for the rounded manifold. The maps in step 2.1 extend over its rounded boundary bridge as $h(\theta)$, independent of $u,v$; hence they give a smooth boundary map $g:\partial W'\to S^1$, with $1$ regular. Its fibre is the connected closed capped surface $F_0\cup(K\times\text{rounded arc})\cup(D\times\{a\})$. The displayed homeomorphism identifies the homology of $W'$ with that of $W$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Let $\lambda:H_1(W')\to\mathbb Z$ send its meridian to $1$. The boundary map satisfies $g_*=\lambda i_*$. Indeed $X$, $Y$ and $T$ are connected, as follows for $X$ also from the degree-zero knot-exterior Mayer–Vietoris calculation in [F2]. Thus the map $H_1(X)\oplus H_1(Y)\to H_1(\partial W)$ is onto, since the next map goes to the kernel of the injective $H_0(T)\to H_0(X)\oplus H_0(Y)$. Product collars justify open-cover Mayer–Vietoris, and rounding leaves these maps unchanged. On both meridian generators, step 2.1 gives winding $1$, exactly the value of $\lambda$ after inclusion by step 1.1. This proves the factorization on every boundary class. [F2, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Apply [F3] to obtain $G:W'\to S^1$ equal to $g$ on a boundary product collar and with $1$ regular. Its fibre $Q$ is a compact cooriented smooth $3$-manifold whose boundary is exactly the connected surface described in step 3.1. Keep its unique component meeting that boundary and discard any closed components. Choose the boundary collar in the rounded product region with $K$ and $\theta$ fixed: its inward field may be chosen in the $u,v$ coordinates there and then extended by finite bumps. Along the remaining boundary near the fibre, an inward field can be made tangent to the circle level by subtracting its angular derivative times a positive tangential transverse field; this does not change inwardness. Consequently $Q$ is the plane $\theta=0$ near the rounded bridge and is a radial product near the $Y$ face. Fill back the discarded small corner quadrant in this same plane. This extends $Q$ to a cooriented $Q_0\subset W$ with corners and boundary faces $F_0$ and $D\times\{a\}$; the gluing is smooth because both germs are the same plane along the bridge. Now attach the radial sheet $P=\Psi(D\times[0,a]e_1)$ along $D\times\{a\}$. Its interior is inside $N$, while $Q_0$ is outside, and the radial product germs agree at their common face, so their union $R_0$ is an embedded connected compact smooth $3$-manifold with corners. Its boundary faces are the original $D$ and $F_0\cup(K\times[0,a]e_1)=F$. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 The coorientation of $Q_0$ extends across $P$: on its attaching face, positive angular derivative is positive on the second disk-frame vector $e_2$, and this nonzero vector extends across all of $D\times[0,a]e_1$. The orientation with positive normal last agrees with the specified boundary orientation on $F$; its other boundary face is the oppositely oriented disk. Push the entire construction into the interior by the linear embedding $j(x)=(1-\delta)x$ of the standard ball, with $0<\delta\ll1$. The shell between $j(S^3)$ and $S^3$ is the explicit product of these spheres under radial scaling. The only boundary corner of $j(R_0)$ lies on $j(K)$ and has local model $u,t\ge0$, $v=0$; round it in that $3$-plane by the same replacement of $t+u\ge|t-u|$ by a smooth upper graph as in step 3.1. This changes only an arbitrarily small attaching collar, gives a compact connected smooth $R\subset\operatorname{Int}B^4$, and preserves the coorientation. Its boundary is precisely the rounded abstract cap, with the indicated copy of $F$ away from its attaching collar. Capping a surface by a disk leaves its first homology unchanged: the attached circle already bounds the oriented fundamental chain of $F$, and the attaching disk adds no one-cells. Thus the stated homology identification holds. [step 3.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Apply the bicollar conclusion of [F1] to $R$, choosing its positive transverse field to equal the scaled positive Seifert normal along the retained $F$ portion. This is compatible with the coorientation by steps 2.1 and 6.1. The ambient extension may be chosen tangent to $j(S^3)$ wherever it meets that sphere: near that portion the field is the tangential Seifert normal, near the rounded corner it is the second normal-disk coordinate, and the remaining choices are supported away from the sphere. Finite bumps preserve tangency and positivity. Its flow therefore preserves the inner ball $j(B^4)$ by uniqueness, and its small-time restriction gives the oriented ambient product $R\times[-\epsilon,\epsilon]$. Every homology class of $F$ can be represented away from its boundary collar. Radial product annuli connect such cycles and their positive Seifert pushes on $j(S^3)$ to the original cycles and positive pushes on $S^3$; these annuli lie outside the inner ball except at their inner boundaries. For any finite choice of disjoint boundary cycles, the annuli are disjoint exactly when their traces on $S^3$ are disjoint, since the shell is a product. Hence pushed chains in the bicollar are disjoint from the interiors of the annuli, and a chain in $R$ can be pushed to the positive complementary side and reattached to the prescribed original boundary cycle. Finally pull back by the isotopy used in step 1.1, if one was used. All smooth embeddings, collars, orientations, and positive-side identifications are preserved. This proves the smooth statement with all claimed compatibility. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The circle extension, relative smoothing and regular-value construction are supplied locally, rather than assumed from the homology computation. The original locally flat TOP-disk assertion remains a separate held obligation; this smooth donor neither changes its Statement nor supplies TOP transversality.
