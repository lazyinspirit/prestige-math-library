## Statement

Fix $n\ge1$ and a finite marked smooth infection sequence $J_{j+1}=P_j(J_j)$, $0\le j<n$, with unknotted axes $\eta_j$ and winding number zero. Supply compatible product tubes, peripheral paths and finite disk-band Seifert-surface models with local ambient roundings for all knots $J_j$ and $P_j(U)$. Let $E_j$ be their actual standard infection cobordisms. Let $W_n$ be any compact connected oriented topological four-manifold with a supplied collared boundary component $M(J_n)$, and form
$$W_j=W_{j+1}\cup_{M(J_{j+1})}E_j\qquad(j=n-1,\ldots,0)$$
using the marked collars. Other boundary components of $W_n$ are allowed. Base paths are supplied coherently through the finite gluings. Write $\mu_j$ for the positive meridian of $J_j$ and $G_j=\pi_1(W_j)$.

For $0\le k<n$, inclusion induces an epimorphism $q_k:G_{k+1}\to G_k$ and a natural isomorphism
$$G_{k+1}/G_{k+1}^{(n-k+1)}\cong G_k/G_k^{(n-k+1)}.$$
Moreover $\mu_k\in G_k^{(n-k)}$. For any supplied finite commutative-ring list $R_0,\ldots,R_n$, define the mixed series by
$$P^0G=G,\qquad P^{i+1}G=\ker\!\left(P^iG\longrightarrow(P^iG/[P^iG,P^iG])\otimes_{\mathbb Z}R_i\right).$$
The same $q_k$ identifies $G_{k+1}/P^iG_{k+1}$ with $G_k/P^iG_k$ for $i\le n-k+1$, and identifies $P^iG_{k+1}/P^{i+1}G_{k+1}$ with $P^iG_k/P^{i+1}G_k$ for $0\le i<n-k+1$. These are assertions for the actual inclusion maps. No nontriviality or order assertion about a meridian in a mixed quotient is included; such an assertion requires the separate Blanchfield argument. No choice principle or smoothing of $W_n$ is assumed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite marked knot models, zero-winding axes, actual product cobordisms, collared $W_n$, paths and finite coefficient-ring list.

[F1] Adjoining the actual standard infection cobordism along its outgoing surgery induces precisely the quotient by the normal closure of the companion longitude, and its companion meridian is the marked pattern-axis core. [[lem-standard-infection-cobordism-adjunction-kills-the-companion-longitude]]

[F2] For the supplied finite knot models, exterior and zero-surgery groups are normally generated by their meridian, and the preferred exterior longitude lies in the exterior group's second derived subgroup. [[lem-finite-disk-band-knot-longitudes-lie-in-the-second-derived-subgroup]]

[F3] Killing a normal subgroup contained in $G^{(r)}$ preserves the ordinary derived quotient at index $r$ and all mixed-series quotients and factors through index $r$, for the actual quotient map. [[lem-killing-deep-derived-normal-subgroups-preserves-mixed-derived-quotients]]

[F4] The standard product-spine markings identify the outgoing satellite meridian with the pattern meridian. The peripheral calculation gives $H_1(M(P_j(U));\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[\mu_{P_j}]$ and the axis class $[\eta_j]=\operatorname{lk}(P_j(U),\eta_j)[\mu_{P_j}]=0$: this is the longitude calculation in the two-component exterior, before filling back the axis tube. [[lem-standard-infection-cobordisms-have-boundary-generated-second-homology]]

[F5] Zero first-homology class is equivalent to membership in the commutator subgroup, without choice. [[prop-the-first-hurewicz-map-in-degree-one-is-abelianization]]

## Proof

1.1 Fix $k<n$ and work first in $G=G_{k+1}$. For $k+1\le j<n$, the piece $E_j$ is already present in $W_{k+1}$. Let $A_j$ be the image of the group of its pattern boundary $M(P_j(U))$ in $G$. Its meridian is the image of $\mu_{j+1}$ by [F4], so [F2] implies $A_j\subseteq\langle\!\langle\mu_{j+1}\rangle\!\rangle_G$. By [F4] and [F5], its axis lies in the commutator subgroup of that pattern-boundary group. Its image is $\mu_j$ by [F1]. Hence $$\mu_j\in[A_j,A_j]\subseteq[\langle\!\langle\mu_{j+1}\rangle\!\rangle_G,\langle\!\langle\mu_{j+1}\rangle\!\rangle_G].$$ Only this inclusion is needed; no equality between a single axis normal closure and an entire commutator subgroup is asserted. [F1, F2, F4, F5, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Starting with $\mu_n\in G^{(0)}$, descend through the finite range $j=n-1,\ldots,k+1$. If $\mu_{j+1}\in G^{(n-j-1)}$, its normal closure is contained in that derived subgroup because derived subgroups are normal: conjugation preserves every finite commutator word. Step 1.1 then gives $\mu_j\in G^{(n-j)}$. Consequently $\mu_{k+1}\in G^{(n-k-1)}$; when $k=n-1$ this just says $\mu_n\in G$. The companion exterior $X_{J_k}$ is an actual subspace of the outgoing boundary $M(J_{k+1})$. By normal generation of that surgery group in [F2], the entire image of its group, and hence the image of the companion-exterior group, lies in $\langle\!\langle\mu_{k+1}\rangle\!\rangle_G\subseteq G^{(n-k-1)}$. Its longitude lies in its second derived subgroup by [F2]. Expanding the finite commutator products under the inclusion homomorphism therefore puts the longitude image $\lambda_k$ in $G^{(n-k+1)}$. [F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 By [F1], the actual inclusion gives $G_k=G/\langle\!\langle\lambda_k\rangle\!\rangle_G$. Derived subgroups are normal, so step 2.1 puts the entire kernel in $G^{(n-k+1)}$. Apply [F3] with $r=n-k+1$ to obtain exactly the ordinary and mixed-quotient isomorphisms in the Statement, for the actual inclusion map and every supplied finite ring prefix. To check the remaining meridian depth, observe in the new piece $E_k$ that the image $A_k$ of its pattern-boundary group lies in the normal closure of $q_k(\mu_{k+1})$, and that $\mu_k\in[A_k,A_k]$, by the same argument as step 1.1. The image of $\mu_{k+1}$ has depth at least $n-k-1$ by step 2.1, because a homomorphism sends each finite commutator word to the corresponding word of its images. Thus $\mu_k\in G_k^{(n-k)}$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 The topological adjunctions used only supplied three-dimensional boundary collars and the explicit smooth product pieces. Finite paths fix all based inclusions; changing any such path conjugates its relevant image and does not affect normal closures or derived-depth statements. The calculation never asserted that a derived meridian is nonzero, of infinite order, or of prime order: those require additional homological and Blanchfield inputs. Likewise positive-characteristic mixed factors are not called PTFA merely because the rational prefixes are. Every commutator, representative and path used above belongs to a fixed finite calculation, and [F3] proves the kernel comparisons without an infinite representative selection. Thus the full stated finite-tower naturality and depth assertions hold in the supplied TOP/smooth scope without a choice axiom. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
