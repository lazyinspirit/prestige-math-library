## Statement

Assume full AC. Let $V$ be a compact connected oriented topological four-manifold with a supplied collar and connected boundary $M$, and choose a based loop $\mu\subset M$ such that
$$H_1(M;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[\mu],\qquad H_1(M;\mathbb Z)\xrightarrow{\cong}H_1(V;\mathbb Z),\qquad H_2(V;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^{2r}.$$
Put $\pi=\pi_1(V)$, take $n\ge0$, and supply classes $x_i,y_i\in H_2(V;\mathbb Z[\pi/\pi^{(n)}])$, $1\le i\le r$, satisfying $\lambda_n(x_i,x_j)=0$ and $\lambda_n(x_i,y_j)=\delta_{ij}$. Supply classes $\widetilde x_i\in H_2(V;\mathbb Z[\pi/\pi^{(n+1)}])$ mapping to $x_i$ with $\lambda_{n+1}(\widetilde x_i,\widetilde x_j)=0$. These are actual lifted half-pair data on the supplied manifold; existence of such a manifold or lifts is not asserted.

Let $k=\mathbb Q$ or $\mathbb F_p$ for any prime $p$. Let $G$ be a discrete amenable group in $D(k)$ with $G^{(n+1)}=1$, and supply $\phi:\pi\to G$ such that $\phi(\mu)$ has infinite order. Use its based regular unitary local system and the finite-projective topological intersection convention. Then
$$\operatorname{sign}^{(2)}_G(V,\phi)=0=\operatorname{sign}(V),$$
so its actual bounding signature defect is zero. This applies to the full supplied locally flat topological category as well as smooth manifolds; no finite CW type, smoothing, closed-manifold ordinary-versus-$L^2$ index equality or rho bordism-independence theorem is assumed. If a separately applicable bordism-independence result identifies a rho invariant with this bounding defect, that invariant is consequently zero.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual manifold, collar, integral homology, finite lifted pair lists, amenable coefficient group in $D(k)$, derived-length bound, infinite-order meridian and standing full AC in the Statement.

[F1] Topological singular complexes over an arbitrary based right ring have explicit finite-free domination and hence Wall bounded finite-projective chain-homotopy models. In the Wall model all terms below its chosen top degree are finite free; padding the domination complex by zero does not alter its input. [[thm-finite-geometric-complex-chains-dominate-compact-manifold-chains-in-zf]], [[lem-choice-free-wall-chain-finiteness-from-explicit-domination]]

[F2] Topological finite-projective models have actual bounded completed cap duality, weak pair exactness, Hermitian middle intersection operators, and the exact algebraic projection-module versus dense completed-homology dictionary. Their signatures are model independent and agree with those for their countable coefficient image. [[thm-topological-finite-projective-l2-duality-and-boundary-gluing]]

[F3] For countable amenable $G\in D(k)$, a bounded-below $\mathbb ZG$ complex finite free through degree $j$ has dimension-zero $A$ homology through $j$ if its augmented $k$ homology vanishes there. Also image rank is at least augmented image rank for every map to finite free chains, and the cycle-quotient inequality holds when $C_i,C_{i-1}$ are finite free, with no freeness requirement on $C_{i+1}$. [[lem-amenable-D-R-l2-dimension-estimate]], [[lem-amenable-strebel-rank-and-cycle-quotient-comparison]]

[F4] Finite von Neumann modules have spectral rank-nullity and projection quotient dimensions. A dimension-zero finite harmonic projection module is zero by trace faithfulness. Inertia is characterized by dimensions of strictly positive or negative closed submodules. [[lem-finite-von-neumann-module-dimension-and-dense-quotients]], [[lem-von-neumann-inertia-and-coisotropic-reduction]]

[F5] Topological local-coefficient cap duality and the standard parallel Hermitian identifications commute with actual coefficient maps. [[thm-compact-collared-manifold-local-coefficient-lefschetz-duality-in-zf]], [[lem-involutive-twisted-chain-duals-realize-local-coefficient-duality]]

[F6] The right-projective augmentation detection property is the meaning of $D(k)$. Amenability means an invariant positive normalized mean. The full AC convention already applies to these analytic suppliers. [[def-integral-half-solutions-strebel-and-l2-signatures]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

## Proof

1.1 First assume $G$ countable and put $A=\mathcal NG$. Apply [F1] over $\mathbb ZG$ to $V$ and $M$, padding the finite domination complex to top index at least two. Its Wall top index is then at least six, so the resulting bounded projective complexes $E_V,E_M$ have finite free terms in degrees zero, one and two. Their augmentations compute ordinary homology over $k$, and their complex scalar extensions are legitimate finite-projective models for [F2]. An actual based singular meridian gives a chain map from the two-term circle complex $S=(\mathbb ZG\xrightarrow{g^{-1}-1}\mathbb ZG)$, $g=\phi(\mu)$, into either model: send its degree-zero generator to the model image of the basepoint and its degree-one generator to the model image of the meridian chain. The boundary equality is precisely $d\mu=e_o(g^{-1}-1)$, so this is an actual chain map, not a map asserted only after augmentation. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 Separately record the final arbitrary-target reduction. Put $G_c=\operatorname{im}\phi$, which is countable by [F2]'s compact-manifold finite-generator construction. Amenability descends under the standing AC: choose representatives $\gamma$ for right cosets $G_c\gamma$ and extend a bounded function $f$ on $G_c$ to $Ef(h\gamma)=f(h)$. This positive unital extension is equivariant for left $G_c$ translations; composing it with the supplied invariant mean on $G$ gives one on $G_c$. The class $D(k)$ descends too. Given a projective right-$kG_c$ map $f:P\to Q$, induction $-\otimes_{kG_c}kG$ preserves projectivity by its restriction adjunction: a lift of an induced map across a surjection is exactly a lift of the restricted map on $P$. Its augmented map is the same $f\otimes_{kG_c}k$. Thus augmented injectivity makes the induced map injective by $D(k)$ for $G$. The coefficient projection $E_c:kG\to kG_c$ retaining only $G_c$-supported terms is a $kG_c$-bimodule map, so $p\otimes a\mapsto pE_c(a)$ splits the unit $p\mapsto p\otimes1$. This injective unit makes $f$ injective. Hence $G_c\in D(k)$; it also has $G_c^{(n+1)}=1$ and the same infinite-order meridian. This observation will be used after the countable calculation. [F2, F6, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The ordinary $k$ homology of each space in degrees zero and one is $k,k[\mu]$. For $k=\mathbb Q$ this follows by rational localization. For $k=\mathbb F_p$ the finite-chain coefficient argument is explicit: lift one mod-$p$ one-cycle $z$ to $\widetilde z$ and write $\partial\widetilde z=pw$. The zero-chain class $[w]$ is killed by $p$ in $H_0=\mathbb Z$, so $w=\partial v$ and $\widetilde z-pv$ is an integral cycle. Conversely an integral cycle bounding mod $p$ differs from a lifted finite boundary by $p$ times an integral cycle. This proves $H_1(-;\mathbb F_p)=H_1(-;\mathbb Z)\otimes\mathbb F_p$; the degree-zero assertion follows from connectedness. Thus the augmented circle map of step 1.1 is an isomorphism on $H_0$ and onto on $H_1$. Its algebraic mapping cone has augmented homology zero in degrees zero and one, and its terms through degree one are finite free. [F1, given, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 The derived-length hypothesis makes $\phi$ factor through $\pi/\pi^{(n+1)}$. Change the supplied lifted $\widetilde x_i$ to $\mathbb ZG$ coefficients and then to $A$. Their intersection pairings remain zero. This naturality is an actual cochain calculation: extend a dual functional by $f(c\otimes a)=\theta(f(c))a$ for the coefficient homomorphism $\theta$; it is balanced, commutes with coboundaries and the finite cap formula, and respects the same standard parallel Hermitian form. Cap maps are isomorphisms over both fibers by [F5], so their inverses commute on the represented classes as well. The ordinary images of these lifted classes pair with the ordinary images of $y_j$ as $\delta_{ij}$ by the same calculation through the height-$n$ augmentation. Thus those $r$ ordinary images are independent over every $k$. Only the isotropic classes are sent to $G$; no $G$-coefficient lifts of the height-$n$ dual classes have been assumed. [F5, given, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Apply [F3] to the mapping cone of step 2.1 through degree one. Its $A$ homology in those degrees is dimension zero. The $A$ circle complex itself has zero degree-one homology and dimension-zero degree-zero homology: left translation by the infinite-order $g$ has no nonzero fixed vector in $\ell^2(G)$, because such a vector is constant along each infinite $\langle g\rangle$ orbit. The adjoint $g-1$ has the same property, so $g^{-1}-1$ is Hilbert-injective with dense image. Its algebraic kernel in $A$ is consequently zero and its cokernel has dimension zero by [F4]. The algebraically exact cone sequence makes $H_1(E;A)$ a submodule of a zero-dimensional module, and $H_0(E;A)$ an extension of a quotient of the circle cokernel by a submodule of the cone homology. These modules are dimension zero by [F4]. The harmonic projection dictionary [F2] and trace faithfulness [F4] give $$b^{(2)}_0(V)=b^{(2)}_1(V)=b^{(2)}_0(M)=b^{(2)}_1(M)=0.$$ Closed three-dimensional cap duality on $M$ then gives zero completed reduced homology in every degree on $M$. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.2 Compute the ordinary middle dimension over $k$. In the prime case lift a mod-$p$ two-cycle $z$ to $\widetilde z$ with $\partial\widetilde z=pw$. Here $w$ is an integral one-cycle with $p[w]=0$ in $H_1(V;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$, so $w=\partial v$. Then $\widetilde z-pv$ is an integral two-cycle lifting $z$. If an integral two-cycle becomes a boundary mod $p$, subtract one lifted finite three-boundary; the difference is $p$ times an integral two-cycle because chain groups have no $p$-torsion. Hence $H_2(V;\mathbb F_p)=H_2(V;\mathbb Z)/p$ has dimension $2r$. The rational case gives the same dimension by localization. The meridian is an isomorphism on ordinary $H_1$ of boundary and interior by step 2.1. Ordinary finite-dimensional dual cochain evaluation and cap duality therefore show $H^1(V,M;k)=0$ and $H^0(V,M;k)=0$: use the cohomology pair sequence with its isomorphisms in degrees zero and one. Thus $H_3(V;k)=H_4(V;k)=0$ by [F5]. Together with $b_0=b_1=1$, this gives ordinary coefficient Euler sum $\chi_k(V)=2r$. [F1, F5, given, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 The finite-projective chain Euler sum has the same $k$ and $A$ value, without a free-top-term assumption. Represent any term by an idempotent $e\in M_s(\mathbb ZG)$. Apply the image-rank inequality in [F3] to $e$ and $1-e$, as maps to $(\mathbb ZG)^s$. It gives $\dim_A eA^s\ge\dim_k(\varepsilon e)k^s$ and the corresponding inequality for $1-e$. The two left dimensions sum to $s$, because $A^s=eA^s\oplus(1-e)A^s$ as modules and [F4] makes finite-projective dimensions invariant under this isomorphism. The two right dimensions also sum to $s$. Both inequalities must therefore be equalities. In each degree, spectral rank-nullity expresses the term dimension as harmonic homology dimension plus the ranks of its incoming and outgoing differentials. Alternating the finitely many identities cancels all boundary ranks, regardless of whether their images are closed. Thus $$\sum_i(-1)^i b_i^{(2)}(V)=\sum_i(-1)^i\dim_A(E_{V,i}\otimes A)=\sum_i(-1)^i\dim_k(E_{V,i}\otimes k)=2r.$$ This supplies the Euler equality for the actual Wall model rather than assuming finite CW type. [F3, F4, step 1.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra]

5.1 Boundary completed homology is zero by step 3.1. Weak pair exactness in [F2] consequently makes $\mathscr H_i(V)\to\mathscr H_i(V,M)$ injective with dense image, so polar rank-nullity gives equality of their trace dimensions. Completed cap and Riesz duality identify the latter dimension with $b_{4-i}^{(2)}(V)$. Hence $b_4^{(2)}(V)=b_0^{(2)}(V)=0$ and $b_3^{(2)}(V)=b_1^{(2)}(V)=0$; cap duality also kills degrees above four. The Euler identity in step 4.1 now gives $$b_2^{(2)}(V)=2r.$$ In cohomological degree two the same weak pair sequence makes restriction from relative to absolute completed cohomology injective with dense image, since the adjacent boundary groups vanish. Inverse relative cap and Riesz duality therefore make the bounded Hermitian middle intersection operator $J_V$ injective with dense image. Its kernel is zero, but no bounded inverse or closed range is asserted. [F2, F4, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Represent the $G$ images of $\widetilde x_1,\ldots,\widetilde x_r$ by actual cycles in the finite free term $E_{V,2}$, using the chain comparison of step 1.1. Apply [F3]'s cycle-quotient inequality in degree two; $E_{V,2}$ and $E_{V,1}$ are finite free, while the term above is allowed arbitrary. The ordinary cycle-image span has dimension $r$ by step 2.2, and the ordinary and $L^2$ middle dimensions are both $2r$ by steps 3.2 and 5.1. Consequently the algebraic $A$ homology submodule generated by these cycles has dimension at least $r$. Its harmonic image has the same dimension, since its kernel is zero-dimensional, and its closed Hilbert span $L$ has that trace dimension by [F4]. The zero equivariant pairings in step 2.2 make this span isotropic: they vanish on all finite $A$ linear combinations of the harmonic classes, and the bounded form extends the equality to their closure by continuity. Thus $\dim_A L\ge r$. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 3.2, step 5.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

7.1 Write $H=\mathscr H_2(V)$, of dimension $2r$, and let $H_+,H_-$ be the positive and negative spectral submodules of $J_V$. Each intersects the isotropic $L$ only in zero. The orthogonal projection from $H_+$ to $L^\perp$ is therefore injective, so polar rank-nullity bounds $\dim_A H_+\le\dim_A H-\dim_A L\le r$; the same argument bounds $\dim_A H_-\le r$. Since $\ker J_V=0$ by step 5.1, its positive and negative spectral projections sum to identity, giving $\dim_A H_++\dim_A H_-=2r$. Both dimensions equal $r$, hence $\operatorname{sign}^{(2)}_G(V)=0$. This inertia argument uses only the zero kernel and the closed isotropic span, not an unjustified uniformly nonsingular boundary form. [F4, step 5.1, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 The ordinary images of $x_i,y_i$ are a basis of $H_2(V;\mathbb R)$: pairing a zero linear combination first with the $x_i$ forces all dual coefficients zero, and then pairing with the $y_i$ forces the others zero; there are exactly $2r$ classes. Their real symmetric matrix is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&I\\I&D\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with $D=D^T$. Replacing the $y$ block by $y-\tfrac12xD$ gives $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&I\\I&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, which has $r$ positive and $r$ negative directions. Thus $\operatorname{sign}(V)=0$, including $r=0$. With step 7.1 the actual bounding defect is zero for countable $G$. [F2, F5, given, step 3.2, step 2.2, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 Apply the countable result to the image group $G_c$ of step 1.2 and the same actual lifted pair data. The $G_c$ and $G$ finite-matrix signatures agree by [F2]'s regular-coset block trace comparison, so $\operatorname{sign}^{(2)}_G(V)=0$ and step 8.1 still gives the ordinary zero signature. The full AC hypothesis was used only within its stated analytic/projective suppliers and in the explicit subgroup mean extension of step 1.2; no stronger normal-neighborhood, smoothing or closed index theorem was used. If a rho invariant is separately known to equal the defect of this particular extending $V$, the just-proved equality makes it zero; no bordism-independence premise was manufactured. This proves all stated supplied-data conclusions in the full topological and smooth categories. [F2, F6, step 7.1, step 8.1, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
