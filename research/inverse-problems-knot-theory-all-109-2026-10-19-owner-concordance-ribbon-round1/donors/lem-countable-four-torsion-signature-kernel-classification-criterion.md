## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $G$ be a countable abelian group and let $\sigma:G\to\mathbb Z^{(\mathbb N)}$ be a homomorphism whose kernel is annihilated by four. Suppose $G$ contains a countably infinite independent family of infinite-order elements $u_i$, elements $v_i$ of order two whose cosets are linearly independent in $G/2G$, and elements $w_i$ of order four whose doubles $2w_i$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb F_2$. Then, as an abstract group,
$$
G\cong\mathbb Z^{(\mathbb N)}\oplus(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^{(\mathbb N)}\oplus(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^{(\mathbb N)}.
$$
Here independence of the $u_i$ means integral linear independence, and every displayed direct sum consists of elements with finite support.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $G$, $\sigma$, and the three families in the Statement, under AC.

[A1] AC is used by the free-subgroup supplier and to choose bases and their lifts. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] Under AC, a subgroup of a free abelian group is free abelian. [[lem-subgroups-of-free-abelian-groups-are-free]]

## Proof

**Given:** The displayed group, homomorphism, and independent families.

1.1 Put $T=\ker\sigma$. Every torsion element lies in $T$ because the codomain is torsion-free, and every element of $T$ is torsion because $4T=0$. Thus $T$ is exactly the torsion subgroup. The image $A=\sigma(G)$ is a countable free abelian group by [F1]. The elements $\sigma(u_i)$ are independent: a finite integer relation among them gives $x=\sum n_i u_i\in T$, hence $\sum4n_i u_i=0$; the assumed independence forces every $n_i=0$. Therefore the free rank of $A$ is infinite. Its basis is at most countable, since it is a subset of a countable group, so $A\cong\mathbb Z^{(\mathbb N)}$. [A1, F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Choose a basis of $A$ and a lift in $G$ of each basis element, using [A1]. Extending these lifts by finite integer linear combinations defines a homomorphic section $s:A\to G$ of $\sigma$. Every $g\in G$ is $g-s(\sigma g)+s(\sigma g)$ with first term in $T$, and $T\cap s(A)=0$. Hence $G=T\oplus s(A)$. [A1, step 1.1, choose, construct]

2.2 We compute the group $T$, using only $4T=0$ from step 1.1. Both $2T$ and $T[2]=\{t:2t=0\}$ are vector spaces over $\mathbb F_2$, with $2T\subset T[2]$. Choose a basis $b_i$ of $2T$ and lifts $x_i\in T$ with $2x_i=b_i$. The subgroup $X$ generated by the $x_i$ is the direct sum of their cyclic groups of order four. Indeed, doubling a finite relation $\sum a_i x_i=0$ first forces all $a_i$ even, by independence of the $b_i$; writing $a_i=2c_i$ then gives $\sum c_i b_i=0$, forcing every $c_i$ even as well. Thus the only relations are $4x_i=0$. Extend the basis of $2T$ to a basis of $T[2]$, and let $Y$ be the span of the added vectors, so $T[2]=2T\oplus Y$. For any $t\in T$, express $2t=\sum\epsilon_i b_i$; then $t-\sum\epsilon_i x_i\in T[2]$, proving $T=X+Y$. Since $X\cap T[2]=2X=2T$, the intersection $X\cap Y$ is zero. Therefore $T\cong(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^{(I)}\oplus(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^{(J)}$, with $I$ a basis index set of $2T$ and $J$ a basis index set of $T[2]/2T$. All these index sets are at most countable. [A1, step 1.1, choose, construct, algebra]

3.1 The independent doubles $2w_i$ lie in $2T$ and force $I$ to be infinite. The classes of $v_i$ in $T[2]/2T$ are independent: any relation there would be a relation in $G/2G$, since $2T\subset2G$, contrary to the hypothesis. Thus $J$ is infinite as well. Both are consequently countably infinite. Combine their decomposition of $T$ from step 2.2 with the splitting and countably infinite free part from steps 1.1–2.1 to obtain the displayed isomorphism. The exact choices are those stated in [A1]; no arithmetic classification is assumed. [A1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra] ∎
