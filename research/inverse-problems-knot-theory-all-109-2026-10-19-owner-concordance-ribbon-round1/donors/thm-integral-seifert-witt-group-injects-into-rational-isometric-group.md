## Statement

Every element of the integral algebraic concordance group has an invertible integral Seifert-matrix representative, allowing the empty matrix for the zero-rank representative. If $V$ is any such representative, the rule
$$
[V]\longmapsto[(\mathbb Q^{\operatorname{rank}V},V+V^T,V^{-1}V^T)]
$$
defines an injective homomorphism into the rational isometric Witt group. An integral Seifert form represents zero in the integral group if and only if it is itself metabolic. These are finite algebraic assertions and require no choice principle; knot-derived forms retain their separately stated choice assumptions.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Integral Seifert lattices with bilinear matrices $V$, whose alternating skew matrices $V-V^T$ are unimodular.

[F1] The integral group is generated by isometry classes, with orthogonal direct sums giving addition and integral metabolic forms zero. [[def-metabolic-seifert-form-and-algebraic-knot-concordance]]

[F2] An elementary Seifert enlargement preserves its integral Witt class. [[def-seifert-matrix-s-equivalence]], [[lem-seifert-matrix-s-enlargement-preserves-algebraic-concordance-class]]

[F3] A rational isotropic half-dimensional subspace for an integral bilinear form intersects its lattice in a primitive rank-half direct summand with zero restriction. [[lem-rational-isotropic-subspace-saturates-to-integral-metabolizer]]

[F4] In the rational isometric group, zero class is equivalent to an actual invariant metabolizer. [[lem-isotropic-reduction-of-rational-isometric-structures]]

[A1] The proof selects only finitely many integer vectors, bases, and polynomial inverses. No choice axiom is needed.

## Proof

**Given:** The displayed integral forms and their finite-rank lattices.

1.1 We first record a rational cancellation argument that also works for singular $V$. On the rationalization put $A=V-V^T$ and $s=A^{-1}V$, so $V(x,y)=A(x,sy)$. The alternating adjoint of $s$ is $s^*=I-s$, since $s^*=A^{-1}s^TA=-A^{-1}V^T=I-s$. A half-dimensional subspace $P$ has $V|_P=0$ exactly when it is an $A$-Lagrangian invariant under $s$. Indeed vanishing of $V$ makes $A|_P=0$, so $P=P^{\perp_A}$, and $A(P,sP)=V(P,P)=0$ implies $sP\subset P$; the converse follows from the same equality. For any invariant $A$-isotropic $L$, its orthogonal $L^{\perp_A}$ is invariant under $s$ because $s^*=I-s$ preserves $L$. Thus $A$ and $V=A s$ descend to $L^{\perp_A}/L$, where the alternating form is nonsingular. Metabolicity is preserved in both directions by this reduction. In one direction lift a quotient Lagrangian. In the other, if $P$ is a metabolizer, then $((P\cap L^{\perp_A})+L)/L$ is invariant and isotropic and has half the quotient dimension: the pairing map $P\to L^*$ has rank $\dim L-\dim(P\cap L)$, hence its kernel has dimension $\dim P-\dim L+\dim(P\cap L)$. This proves the assertion by taking its quotient by $P\cap L$. [A1, F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The diagonal is an integral primitive metabolizer for $V\oplus(-V)$, so $[-V]=-[V]$. The group presentation [F1] implies that a zero class $[V]$ can be expressed as a stable isometry $V\oplus M\oplus C\cong N\oplus C$ with $M,N$ metabolic: being in the subgroup generated by metabolic classes gives a finite difference of their sums in the Grothendieck group, and equality in that group means equality after adding one common monoid summand $C$. Append $-C$ and absorb the metabolic form $C\oplus(-C)$ into both metabolic sides. Thus $V\oplus M'\cong N'$ with $M',N'$ metabolic. Rationalize and reduce by a metabolizer of $M'$. In the alternating description of step 1.1 this quotient is precisely the rationalization of $V$; since $N'$ is metabolic, step 1.1 makes the quotient metabolic. By [F3] it has an integral metabolizer as well. Therefore zero integral class implies the original integral form is metabolic. The reverse implication is one of the defining relations. This also proves that two integral forms represent the same class exactly when their orthogonal difference is metabolic. [F1, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Suppose now that the integral matrix $V$ is singular. Choose a primitive integral vector $x\ne0$ with $Vx=0$, by clearing denominators of a nonzero rational kernel vector and dividing by the greatest common divisor. Since $A$ is unimodular, the functional $A(x,-)$ is primitive, so an integer Bezout identity supplies $y$ with $A(x,y)=1$. The span of $x,y$ is an integral symplectic plane. Its $A$-orthogonal complement $H'$ is an integral direct summand: the projection onto it is $z\mapsto z-A(z,y)x+A(z,x)y$. The restriction of $A$ to $H'$ is unimodular, as its matrix is the complementary block of the unimodular alternating matrix $A$. For $z\in H'$, the kernel relation gives $V(z,x)=0$, and $A(x,z)=0$ then gives $V(x,z)=0$. Also $V(x,y)=1$ and $V(y,x)=0$. Thus in a basis ordered as a basis of $H'$, then $y$, then $x$, the form is exactly an elementary enlargement $E_0(W;u,v,d)$ of the smaller integral Seifert form $W=V|_{H'}$. By [F2] it has the same integral class. Iterating decreases rank by two each time and stops at an invertible form or the empty matrix. Every integral group element is represented by an actual integral form, since negative generators are represented by negative forms and finite sums by block sums. Hence every group element has the asserted invertible representative. [A1, F1, F2, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Take an invertible representative $V$ furnished by step 3.1. Then $B=V+V^T$ is nonsingular: modulo two its determinant equals that of the unimodular $A=V-V^T$, hence is odd. Put $T=V^{-1}V^T$. Then $I-T=V^{-1}A$ is invertible, and direct multiplication gives $T^TBT=B$. Thus this is a rational isometric structure. A rational half-dimensional subspace $L$ with $V|_L=0$ is an invariant $B$-metabolizer: its right orthogonal $\{z:V(x,z)=0\text{ for every }x\in L\}$ under the nonsingular form $V$ has dimension half and equals $L$, while $V(x,Ty)=V(y,x)=0$ for $x,y\in L$ forces $Ty\in L$; also $B|_L=0$. Conversely, if $L$ is an invariant $B$-metabolizer, note that $I+T=V^{-1}B$ is invertible and its inverse preserves $L$ by finite dimension. Since $V=B(I+T)^{-1}$, $V(x,y)=B(x,(I+T)^{-1}y)=0$ on $L$. Therefore the two rational metabolic conditions are equivalent; [F3] turns the latter into an integral metabolizer when needed. [F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Choose invertible representatives $V,W$ of the same integral class. Step 2.1 makes $V\oplus(-W)$ metabolic integrally and hence rationally. Its associated rational isometric structure is the orthogonal difference of the structures associated to $V,W$, so step 4.1 makes their rational Witt classes equal. Thus the displayed rule is independent of the invertible representative. Block sum of invertible representatives is invertible, and its associated structure is their orthogonal direct sum, proving the homomorphism law. If its value on an invertible $V$ is zero, [F4] gives an actual invariant rational metabolizer. Step 4.1 makes it a rational $V$-metabolizer, and [F3] gives an integral metabolizer. Hence $[V]=0$ by [F1]. The kernel is zero, proving injectivity and every assertion. [F1, F3, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra] ∎
