## Statement

Let P⊂S¹×D² be a winding-zero pattern. Assume the axis has nonzero classical Blanchfield self-pairing in the Alexander module of P(U), with the convention that the axis defines the class paired with itself. For either the locally flat topological concordance group C_top or the smooth concordance group C_sm, let Pⁿ be the n-fold composition of the induced satellite set map, with P⁰ the identity. For every n≥0, the quotient ⟨Pⁿ(C)⟩/⟨Pⁿ⁺¹(C)⟩ has infinite rank, where ⟨S⟩ denotes the subgroup generated by S and rank means dim_Q(A⊗_Z Q) for an abelian group A. For n≥1, Pⁿ is not a group homomorphism under the same hypotheses. No pairwise-coprime Alexander-polynomial assumption is part of these Theorems A and B.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A winding-zero pattern P satisfying the nonzero axis self-pairing hypothesis, the induced satellite function on C_top and C_sm, and an integer n≥0.

[F1] The classical Blanchfield self-pairing is the specialization of the linking form defined on the Alexander module ([[def-blanchfield-pairing-and-higher-order-rho-invariant]]).

[F2] The iterated infection cobordisms have the integral solvability and Blanchfield-bordism properties required in the Cha–Kim argument ([[lem-cha-kim-integral-solvability-and-blanchfield-bordism]]).

[F3] The amenable signature theorem converts those bordism data into vanishing rho constraints ([[thm-amenable-signature-vanishing-for-solvable-bordisms]]).

[F4] The finite companion selection separates the higher-order rho terms while keeping all other terms bounded ([[lem-cha-kim-higher-order-rho-bound-and-coprime-separation]]).

## Proof

1.1 For n=0, Cha–Kim choose companions with signature jumps near the unit-circle root detected by the nonzero classical self-pairing. The resulting classical signature homomorphism separates infinitely many cosets in the first graded quotient. [F1, given, choose]

1.2 For n≥1, construct the iterated infection cobordism from [F2]. The amenable coefficient systems and the signature-vanishing constraints in [F3] show that any putative finite relation among the selected iterates has a nonzero rho contribution. [F2, F3, given]

2.1 Choose the finite companion family using [F4]. Its designated signature jumps dominate the bounded rho terms, so no nontrivial integral combination can vanish in the relevant quotient. This yields infinite rank at each stage. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2]

2.2 The non-homomorphism conclusion follows from Cha–Kim’s separate construction showing Pⁿ(K#J) is not concordant to Pⁿ(K)#Pⁿ(J) for a chosen pair when n≥1; this uses the same nonzero-pairing hypothesis but is not implied by infinite rank alone. [F2, F3, F4, step 2.1]
