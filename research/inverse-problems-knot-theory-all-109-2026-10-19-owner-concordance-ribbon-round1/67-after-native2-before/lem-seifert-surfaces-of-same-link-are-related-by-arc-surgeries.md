## Statement

Assume the full Axiom of Choice. Let $L$ be a nonempty smooth oriented link in the oriented $3$-sphere, and let $F_0,F_1$ be connected compact smooth oriented embedded surfaces with the same oriented boundary $L$. There is a finite sequence of such spanning surfaces from $F_0$ to $F_1$ in which each consecutive pair differs either by an ambient isotopy fixed on $L$ or by one embedded arc surgery or its reverse.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Full AC, a nonempty oriented link $L\subset S^3$, and connected compact oriented spanning surfaces $F_0,F_1$ with boundary $L$.

[A1] Full AC implies dependent choice and countable choice. [[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]]

[F1] The one-variable cover of $S^3\setminus L$ is the connected cover classified by the kernel of total linking, which sends every positive meridian to $1$; its deck group is generated by the positive-meridian transformation. [[def-one-variable-alexander-module-of-an-oriented-link]], [[def-covering-map-and-evenly-covered-neighbourhoods]], [[thm-classification-of-connected-covering-spaces]], [[def-deck-transformation-and-deck-group]]

[F2] For any smooth loop in the link complement transverse to the interior of a spanning surface, its signed intersection count equals its total-linking homomorphism. [[lem-total-linking-equals-intersection-with-link-seifert-surface]]


[L1] The available isotopy-extension supplier only treats an $n$-manifold source embedded in an $n$-manifold target. This application needs a compact surface (dimension $2$) embedded in $S^3$ (dimension $3$), with its common boundary fixed. A dimension-correct relative general-position/ambient-isotopy argument is not supplied by that item's statement. [[lem-isotopy-extension-for-a-compact-source-with-boundary]]

[A2] For the conditional reduction only, assume the two surfaces have been put by an ambient isotopy fixed on $L$ into distinct inward boundary collars with transverse interiors. This arrangement is an unresolved obligation, not a supplied result. [[lem-isotopy-extension-for-a-compact-source-with-boundary]], [[thm-transversality-homotopy-theorem]], [[prop-relative-transversality-preserves-a-map-on-a-closed-good-region]]

[F3] A compact smooth manifold with corners has a finite triangulation relative to its already triangulated boundary faces. [[thm-compact-smooth-manifolds-with-corners-admit-relative-finite-triangulations]]

[F4] The arc-surgery operation removes two disks and replaces them with the lateral annulus of an embedded product cylinder, leaving the boundary link fixed. [[def-seifert-surface-arc-surgery]]

[F5] A transverse intersection of two surfaces in a three-manifold is a one-manifold; a compact one-manifold has finitely many components. [[thm-transverse-preimage-theorem]]

## Proof

**Given:** Full AC, the oriented link $L$, and $F_0,F_1$ as in the Statement.

1.1 Temporarily assume [A2]. Distinct inward collar directions make the surfaces disjoint from one another in a sufficiently small collar of $L$. Their remaining intersection is a closed subset of the compact surfaces away from that collar. By transversality and [F5], it is a compact one-manifold with finitely many circle components. Trimming the collar gives a compact exterior $X$ and properly embedded trimmed surfaces $G_0,G_1$ with disjoint boundary curves on each boundary torus of $X$. This is only the conditional reduction; [L1] identifies why the ambient-isotopy step needed for [A2] is unavailable. [A2, L1, F5, given, construct]

2.1 Assuming the conditional arrangement of step 1.1, cut $X$ along $G_0$ and stack the resulting cobordism $Y$ over the integers, gluing the positive face of one copy to the negative face of the next. A lifted loop changes its index by its algebraic intersection with $G_0$, which equals its total linking number with $L$ by [F2]. The same construction using $G_1$ therefore gives the same connected cover $p:\widetilde X\to X$ classified by the kernel in [F1]. Write $P_n=t^nP_0$ for the $G_0$ lifts and $Q_r=t^rQ_0$ for the $G_1$ lifts. The closed slabs $Y_n$ between $P_n$ and $P_{n+1}$ have disjoint interiors, and $p$ is injective on $Y_n\setminus P_{n+1}$. The second stacking description shows that $Q_0$ separates $\widetilde X$ into a negative and a positive component $C_-,C_+$, with $Q_r\subset C_-$ precisely for $r<0$. Indeed the negative slabs in the $G_1$ stacking form $C_-$ and the positive slabs form $C_+$. Both surfaces are connected, so the cut exteriors and these half-stacks are connected. Compactness of $Q_0$ and local finiteness of the slabs imply that $Q_0$ meets only finitely many $P_n$. [A1, F1, F2, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Suppose there is an interior intersection and choose the largest $n$ with $Q_0\cap P_n\ne\varnothing$. The positive end is the same in the two stackings because a positive meridian increases both sheet indices. Every $P_j$ with $j>n$ is disjoint from $Q_0$ and lies on its positive side; in particular $P_{n+1}\subset C_+$. Choose a component of $C_-\cap\operatorname{Int}Y_n$ incident to an intersection curve of $Q_0\cap P_n$, and let $\widetilde M$ be its closure. It lies in $Y_n\setminus P_{n+1}$, so the projection is injective there. No other lift $Q_r$ meets this component: if $r>0$, $Q_r\subset C_+$; if $r<0$, maximality gives $Q_r\cap P_n=\varnothing$, while translating a chosen point of $Q_0\cap P_n$ by $t^r$ shows $Q_r\cap P_{n+r}\ne\varnothing$. Since $n+r<n$, the slab order puts $P_{n+r}$ on the negative side of $P_n$; the connected $Q_r$, disjoint from $P_n$, therefore lies entirely on that side and cannot meet $Y_n$. Thus $M=p(\widetilde M)$ is the closure of a component of $X\setminus(G_0\cup G_1)$. Restore the collars at $L$ when necessary. Locally at an intersection curve it is a quadrant, hence a compact three-manifold with corners, lying on one side of each surface it meets. It has at least one interior intersection curve in its boundary. [F1, F2, step 2.1, choose, construct]

4.1 Here is the finite modification associated to such a one-sided region $M$. Write its two surface faces as $\partial_0M=M\cap F_0$ and $\partial_1M=M\cap F_1$; the small link collars are understood throughout. Triangulate $M$ relative to these faces by [F3]. In the second barycentric subdivision take $A$ to be a regular neighborhood of $\partial_0M$ together with the entire primal one-skeleton, and let $B=\overline{M\setminus A}$. The latter is a regular neighborhood, relative to its part on $\partial_1M$, of the dual graph: its vertices are the tetrahedron barycenters and its edges pass through the triangle barycenters of faces not belonging to $\partial_0M$. The common frontier $S=A\cap B$ is a surface. Starting with $F_0$, move its $\partial_0M$ face through its collar and then over a neighborhood of the primal graph, replacing that face by $\overline{\partial A\setminus\partial_0M}$. Starting with $F_1$, do the analogous replacement across $B$ and its dual graph. Each is a finite succession of allowed moves: take a rooted spanning forest of the graph relative to the attaching face; extending over a forest edge and its new vertex is a ball isotopy, and each remaining edge attaches a solid cylinder to two disks on the current surface, precisely the arc surgery [F4]. All cylinders approach from the same side because they lie in $M$. The construction is relative to the collars and preserves orientability. Every intermediate surface remains connected, since an isotopy or an orientable one-handle attachment to a connected surface preserves connectedness. [F3, F4, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 The two new faces agree on the common frontier $S$. Push one slightly toward $A$ and the other toward $B$, using its product neighborhood. They are then disjoint in $M$. Outside a small neighborhood of $M$ the surfaces have not changed. At the old corners the local quadrant model rounds the new faces on opposite sides; thus the old intersection curves incident to $M$ disappear, while every other old intersection curve is unchanged and no new one is created. In particular the number of interior intersection curves strictly decreases. If $M$ meets $L$, move the shifted boundary back along the fixed link collar; this isotopy is supported near $L$ and leaves every interior intersection curve untouched. Repeat steps 2.1–4.1 for each remaining nonempty intersection. The positive integer count strictly decreases, so after finitely many such modifications the two surfaces have disjoint interiors. [F3, F4, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 In this last case, the disjoint lift $Q_0$ lies in a unique slab $Y_n$ because it is connected and misses every $P_j$. The slab order puts $P_n$ and $P_{n+1}$ on opposite sides of $Q_0$; the closure of the component between $P_n$ and $Q_0$ is compact and projects injectively from $Y_n\setminus P_{n+1}$. Its two surface faces are the full surfaces after the common link collars are restored. Apply the construction of $A$ and $B$ once more. Both modified surfaces are the common frontier $S$ up to opposite small parallel pushes, so they are ambiently isotopic through that product neighborhood, fixed on $L$. This last step does not assert that the original disjoint surfaces were parallel. Concatenate the finite moves from $F_0$ to its last modified surface, this last isotopy, and the reversed finite moves from $F_1$ to its last modified surface. All are ambient isotopies fixing $L$, embedded arc surgeries, or their reverses, and all intermediate spanning surfaces are connected and oriented. This completes the conditional argument once the general-position step in step 1.1 is supplied. [A2, F1, F3, F4, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct] ∎

## Unresolved proof obligation

The proof only gives a conditional reduction. It does not establish [A2]: the cited isotopy-extension supplier assumes equal dimensions for source and target, whereas a spanning surface has dimension $2$ and $S^3$ has dimension $3$. A dimension-correct ambient isotopy extension relative to the common boundary and the required boundary-relative transverse perturbation must be supplied. The later maximal-translate projection argument and the relative primal/dual regular-neighbourhood construction also need complete verification, including the boundary triangulation required by [F3] and a proof that rounding changes only the selected intersection curves. These obligations remain open before the theorem or its S-equivalence consumers can be marked ready.
