RIBBON KNOTS AND ITERATED CABLES OF FIBERED KNOTS
JENNIFER HOM AND JUNGHWAN PARK
Abstract. We define a knot to be γ0-sharp if its Seifert genus is detected by the concordance
invariant γ0, which arises from the immersed curve formalism in bordered Heegaard Floer homology.
We show that a connected sum of γ0-sharp fibered knots is ribbon exactly when it is of the form
K # −K. Consequently, either iterated cables of tight fibered knots are linearly independent in the
smooth concordance group, or the slice–ribbon conjecture fails.
1. Introduction
A knot in the three-sphere S3 is called slice if it bounds a smoothly embedded disk in the four-ball
B4, and ribbon if it bounds an immersed disk in S3 with only ribbon singularities. Since resolving
the ribbon singularities naturally yields a smooth slicing disk, every ribbon knot is necessarily
slice. One of the most significant open problems in low-dimensional topology, originally posed by
Fox [Fox61, Problem 25], asks whether the converse is also true. This question is known as the
slice-ribbon conjecture.
By applying the celebrated theorem of Casson and Gordon [CG83], which states that a fibered
knot in a homology sphere is homotopically ribbon1 if and only if its closed monodromy extends
over a handlebody, many interesting non-ribbon knots have been constructed. For a large number
of them, the sliceness problem remains unresolved to this day.
For instance, Miyazaki [Miy94] proved that a connected sum of iterated cables of torus knots is
not ribbon unless it is of the form K # −K, where K is a knot and −K denotes its mirror image
with reversed orientation. In contrast, the sliceness problem for connected sums of iterated cables
of torus knots remains widely open. In particular, a well-known and long-standing conjecture of
Rudolph [Rud76] states that the set of algebraic knots, which are in particular iterated cables of
torus knots, is linearly independent in the smooth knot concordance group.
About ten years ago, Miyazaki’s result was strengthened by Baker [Bak16], who proved that
the same holds for connected sums of fibered knots supporting the tight contact structure. For
simplicity, we will refer to such knots as tight fibered knots throughout this article. This led to a
stronger version of Rudolph’s conjecture, which states that prime tight fibered knots are linearly
independent in the smooth knot concordance group (see [Bak16, Corollary 5] and [AT16, Lemma
3.1]).
In this article, we provide additional examples of fibered knots that are not ribbon, using the
results of Casson and Gordon [CG83] and Miyazaki [Miy94], combined with the concordance in-
variant γ0, which arises from the interpretation of bordered Floer homology [LOT18] via immersed
curves by Hanselman, Rasmussen, and Watson [HRW22,HRW24,HW23]. We define a knot K to
be γ0-sharp if its Seifert genus agrees with the maximal Alexander grading of the summand of
the knot Floer complex associated to γ0(K); that is, the Seifert genus is determined by γ0. In
particular, every tight fibered knot is γ0-sharp (see Remark 2.2).
Theorem 1.1. A connected sum of γ0-sharp fibered knots is ribbon if and only if it is of the form
K # −K.

Corollary 1.2. Either distinct iterated cables of tight fibered knots are linearly independent in the
smooth knot concordance group, or the slice-ribbon conjecture is false.
In the following corollary, we present a special case of Corollary 1.2, which provides many simple
and interesting new examples of non-ribbon knots whose sliceness is not easily detected. Notably,
all of these knots are algebraically slice. Throughout this article, we use the notation Kp,q to denote
the (p, q)-cable of a knot K, where p indicates the longitudinal winding. We assume p > 1, since
Kp,q is isotopic to K−p,−q and K1,q is isotopic to K.
Corollary 1.3. Let K and J be distinct iterated cables of tight fibered knots. Then, for each pair
of distinct integers q1 and q2 that are relatively prime to p, the knot
P(K, J, p, q1, q2) := Kp,q1 # −Kp,q2 # Jp,q2 # −Jp,q1
is algebraically slice but not ribbon.
We make two remarks on the corollary.
First, we discuss the algebraic sliceness and non-
ribbonness of the knots in question.
The fact that the knot is algebraically slice follows from
work of Livingston and Melvin [LM83] (see also [Kea79]), and holds for any choice of knots K
and J and any integers p, q1 and q2 with q1 and q2 relatively prime to p. The non-ribbonness of
P(K, J, p, q1, q2) follows from work of Miyazaki [Miy94] in the case where K and J are distinct
iterated cables of torus knots, and from work of Baker [Bak16] when K and J are distinct tight
fibered knots and q1, q2 > 0, as each cable remains a tight fibered knot under these conditions.
As mentioned earlier, our contribution lies in extending these results to the case where K and J
are arbitrary iterated cables of tight fibered knots, and in particular, the parameters q1 and q2 are
allowed to be negative integers.
Secondly, while the slice–ribbon conjecture predicts that each knot from Corollary 1.3 is not
slice, proving this remains challenging in general with current techniques in knot concordance.
Nonetheless, notable results by Hedden, Kirk, and Livingston [HKL12], Conway, Kim, and Politar-
czyk [CKP23], and Zhang [Zha23] prove that certain families of such knots can indeed be shown to
be non-slice.
More precisely, sliceness is obstructed in [HKL12, Theorem 3] using Casson–Gordon theory [CG86]
in the case where K is the torus knot T2,3, J is the unknot, p = 2, and q1 and q2 are cho-
sen appropriately.

Ozsv´ath–Szab´o d-invariant [OS03a] of the double branched covers, to the case where K is a

• q1, q2 > 4g(K),

with the Hendricks–Manolescu d- and d-invariants of the double branched covers, vanish.

of L-space knots is much broader than that of torus knots (see, for example, [OS05,ST10]). Since

Question 1.5. Is there a nontrivial L-space knot K and an integer q < −1 such that K2,q is

2. Background on knot Floer complexes and immersed curves
We assume the reader is familiar with the knot Floer homology package of [OS04b] and [Ras03];
for survey articles on these invariants, we recommend [OS06], [Hom17], and [Hom21].

the conventions of [Zem19b] and [DHST21], and view the knot Floer complex CFK(K) as a chain
complex over the ring F[U, V ]. The knot Floer complex can be endowed with the additional structure
of an endomorphism ιK, coming from involutive Heegaard Floer homology [HM17].
If two knots K and J are concordant, then their knot Floer complexes CFK(K) and CFK(J)
are locally equivalent: there exist graded chain maps
f : CFK(K) →CFK(J)
and
g: CFK(J) →CFK(K)
that induce isomorphisms in U−1H∗. We will also say that two knots K and J are locally equivalent
if their knot Floer complexes are locally equivalent.
Local equivalence can be considered in both weaker and stronger settings. The weaker setting
is to quotient by the ideal (UV ), and consider the knot Floer complex over F[U, V ]/(UV ), as
in [DHST21, Definition 2.3]; this is the same as ε-equivalence, considered in [Hom14b, Section
4.1]. The stronger setting is to require that the maps f and g homotopy-commute with ιK, as
in [Zem19a, Definition 2.4], which we will refer to as ιK-local equivalence.
The local equivalence class of CFK(K) over R = F[U, V ] or F[U, V ]/(UV ) admits a unique
minimal (with respect to rank) representative, called the connected complex over R, which has
antecedents in the Pin(2)-monopole setting [Sto20, Section 2.5] and the involutive Heegaard Floer
setting [HHL21, Section 3]; see also [Zho21, Section 6] and [HKPS22, Section 5].
As observed in [HW23, Proposition 2], the connected complex over F[U, V ]/(UV ) is exactly the
data of the component γ0 coming from the immersed curve associated to K [HRW24]. Moreover,
following [DHST21, Section 4], the connected complex over F[U, V ]/(UV ), or equivalently γ0, can
be completely described by a finite sequence of nonzero integers, specifying its standard complex
representative. In Section 4, we will say that a knot K is locally equivalent to a sequence if its
complex CFK(K) is locally equivalent to the standard complex associated to that sequence.
Remark 2.1. The connected complex is a concordance invariant and in fact is an invariant of
concordances in homology cobordisms [DHST24]. It follows from the proof of [HW23, Proposition
2] that γ0 is an invariant of concordances in homology cobordisms as well.
Recall from [OS04a] that the knot Floer complex CFK(K) detects the genus of K, in that the
genus is the maximum Alexander grading in the support of CFK(K). We say that K is γ0-sharp if
the genus of K is the maximum Alexander grading (i.e., the maximum vertical coordinate) in the
support of γ0.
Remark 2.2. Observe that the maximum Alexander grading in the support of γ0 is at least |τ(K)|,
where τ is the Ozsv´ath-Szab´o concordance invariant from [OS03b]. Hence if g(K) = τ(K), then K
is γ0-sharp.

We conclude this section by showing that satelliting with a 1-bridge braid pattern preserves
γ0-sharpness:
Proposition 2.6. Let P be a 1-bridge braid pattern in the solid torus. If K is γ0-sharp, then P(K)
is also γ0-sharp. In particular, any cable of K is γ0-sharp.
Proof. By [CH23, Theorem 1.3], we have that for P a 1-bridge braid pattern in the solid torus,
γ(P(K)) is homotopic to fP (γ(P(K))), where fP is the diffeomorphism from [CH23, Section 6.4].
If the Seifert genus of K is determined by γ0, then the compact components of γ(K) all lie within
the vertical span of γ0(K), and this property is preserved by fP . It follows that the Seifert genus
of P(K) is also determined by γ0, as desired.
□
3. Proof of the main theorem
We now prove the main theorem, whose statement we recall below. The argument follows closely
that of Baker [Bak16, Theorem 3].
Theorem 1.1. A connected sum of γ0-sharp fibered knots is ribbon if and only if it is of the form
K # −K.
Proof. Assume that J is a γ0-sharp fibered knot. We will show that J is minimal with respect to ho-
motopy ribbon concordance among all fibered knots in S3, thereby generalizing [Bak16, Lemma 2].
Suppose that J is homotopy ribbon concordant to a fibered knot J′ in S3.
Then, by [Gor81,
Lemma 3.4], we have either J = J′ or g(J) > g(J′). Although the result is stated for ribbon con-
cordance, the same argument applies to homotopy ribbon concordance. By the assumption that J
is γ0-sharp and by Remark 2.1, we have g(J) ≤g(J′), which implies that J = J′.
We now apply [Miy94, Theorem 5.5], which states that if a connected sum of knots that are
minimal with respect to homotopy ribbon concordance among all fibered knots in homology spheres
is ribbon, then it must be of the form K # −K. On the other hand, the remark following [Miy94,
Lemma 1.3] and the proof of [Bak16, Theorem 3] state that the condition “among all fibered knots
in homology spheres” can be relaxed to “among all fibered knots in S3” using the Geometrization
Conjecture [Per02,Per03a,Per03b]. This completes the proof.
□
Remark 3.2. If we consider ribbon concordance instead of homotopy ribbon concordance, then
each γ0-sharp fibered knot J is minimal not only among all fibered knots in S3, but also among
all knots in S3. This follows from an observation of Miyazaki [Miy18], which builds on results of
Silver [Sil92] and Kochloukova [Koc06], showing that if there is a ribbon concordance from J to J′
and J is fibered, then J′ must also be fibered.
Next, we prove Corollary 1.2. By iterated cables, we mean knots obtained by performing at least
one nontrivial cabling operation. The statement is as follows:
Corollary 1.2. Either distinct iterated cables of tight fibered knots are linearly independent in the

of tight fibered knots, and suppose that

assume that c1 ≥0. It suffices to show that c1 = 0.

Finally, we prove Corollary 1.3, restated below for convenience:
Corollary 1.3. Let K and J be distinct iterated cables of tight fibered knots. Then, for each pair
of distinct integers q1 and q2 that are relatively prime to p, the knot
P(K, J, p, q1, q2) := Kp,q1 # −Kp,q2 # Jp,q2 # −Jp,q1
is algebraically slice but not ribbon.
Proof. By Corollary 1.2 and the fact that P(K, J, p, q1, q2) is known to be algebraically slice
by [LM83], it suffices to show that Kp,q1, Kp,q2, Jp,q1, and Jp,q2 are distinct. This will follow in
particular from showing that Kp,q1 is not isotopic to any of Kp,q2, Jp,q1, or Jp,q2 and then applying
symmetry.
The first case is immediate, since we are assuming q1 ̸= q2, which implies that Kp,q1 and Kp,q2
have distinct Alexander polynomials. For the remaining cases, recall that if Kp,q is isotopic to Jp′,q′
for some p, p′ > 1, then K is isotopic to J. This follows because any homeomorphism between the
complements of the cabled knots induces a homeomorphism between the complements of K and J,
by the uniqueness of the JSJ decomposition [JS78,Joh79], and knot complements determine knot
types [GL89]. This completes the proof.
□
