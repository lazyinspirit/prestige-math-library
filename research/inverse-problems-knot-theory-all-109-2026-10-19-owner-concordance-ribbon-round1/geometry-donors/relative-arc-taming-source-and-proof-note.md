## Source recovery and actual reading

The initial Freedman–Quinn route was unnecessarily general for this one-dimensional locally flat source. I independently inspected the actual scan at printed pp.114–116. Its 8.1A(1) is a theorem about smoothing a homeomorphism near a one-dimensional subcomplex, and its proof does use a five-dimensional smoothing theorem and controlled h-cobordism. This does not prove that every special arc-taming argument needs those ingredients.

The parent identified Gluck's trivial-range work as a lead. The complete 1965 article has DOI `10.2307/1970614`; the 1963 announcement has DOI `10.1090/S0002-9904-1963-11052-6`. I did not retrieve and read either complete article. JSTOR returned challenge HTML and the tested AMS PDF returned HTTP 403. Project Euclid metadata identified announcement `euclid.bams/1183525703`, but its tested pages likewise returned challenge HTML. These are discovery records, not source-reading or proof evidence. The retrieved Ranicki `gluck1.pdf` is a different 1961 note on polyhedral surfaces and was not used as an arc-taming supplier.

The successful actual full source is T. B. Rushing, *Topological Embeddings* (1973), downloaded from https://www.maths.gla.ac.uk/~mpowell/rushing_topological%20embeddings.pdf to `/tmp/arc-rushing.pdf`; its extracted text is `/tmp/arc-rushing.txt`. It has 333 PDF pages. Printed page n is PDF page n+15. I read the actual extracted source at printed pp.118–132 and visually checked pp.119,120,129,130,131 against the PDF. In particular the scan reads the non-strict bound $2k+2\le n$, including $k=1,n=4$; the OCR often drops the lower stroke of the inequality. Theorem 3.6.1 and Corollary 3.6.1 at p.119 supply the relative locally-tame/locally-flat trivial-range statements. Theorem 3.6.3 and its preceding lemmas explain the two-structure back-and-forth construction; the finite patching proof is on pp.129–131. Rushing explicitly notes an earlier solvability-generality difficulty and treats it in this account. I do not use the separately stated unknotting Theorem 3.6.2 as a supplier: its source proof appeals to an unproved folklore solvability claim.

The second full source is Daverman–Venema, *Embeddings in Manifolds*, downloaded from https://www.maths.gla.ac.uk/~mpowell/%5BDaverman-Venema%5D%20Embeddings%20in%20manifolds.pdf to `/tmp/arc-daverman-venema.pdf`, text `/tmp/arc-daverman-venema.txt`. ## Authored proof route

The parent separately authored the elementary controlled smooth-arc suppliers. The actual current bodies were read, including their later choice-free replacements and protected-set clause. The useful input is stronger than density alone: close smooth arcs in Euclidean four-space admit small ambient smooth pushes, with specified parameter and field selectors, preserving fixed source collars and protected closed sets.

The new choice-free meshing donor takes two countable dense families with supplied local small-push rules. It alternates approximations and pushes from the two families. After each cumulative homeomorphism exists, the next motion bound is made small enough to control the next **inverse composition** by its uniform continuity on compact M. Both forward and inverse sequences are then uniformly Cauchy. Their limits are mutual inverses; the concatenated paths extend continuously, with inverse paths, to time one. Every infinite selection is a least-index operation in given enumerations. The separate full-AC general meshing donor is unused by the choice-free arc proof.

The arc donor makes the local-flatness chart parametrically straight by a product change of its first coordinate. It then compares its transported smooth structure with the ambient one. It supplies a countable dense family in each: a fixed extended smooth base plus a flat cutoff times rational polynomials, filtered by the embedding predicate. Cutoff approximation and polynomial approximation give density; the explicit finite-parameter perturbation makes nearby rational-polynomial members embeddings. Thus the repeated push selectors have actual supplied extension germs, without choosing infinitely many unspecified smooth extensions.

There is a four-dimensional subtlety here: a topological chart is not assumed diffeomorphic to Euclidean four-space in the ambient smooth structure. Each chart is placed inside an ambient smooth coordinate neighborhood; the two smooth push constructions use these two different coordinate systems. A protected complement confines each push to a common compact carrier. Small total motion keeps the finitely many later source patches inside their ambient coordinate neighborhoods, where the transported topological charts can be restricted again.

Finite patches proceed along the source interval with overlaps. Each local operation freezes a collar extending strictly beyond the already protected initial source interval. This source buffer separates the moving compact part from the old arc and lets its ambient velocity extension fix the entire old arc. A larger right end collar stays protected until the final patch; the final patch uses the buffer inside that collar to preserve the originally prescribed smaller right end collar. All supports avoid fixed neighborhoods of the ambient boundary levels.
