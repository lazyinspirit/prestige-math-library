## Statement

Let P be an oriented pattern of winding n≠0 and K an oriented knot. If P(U) bounds a smooth proper disk in a compact smooth 4-manifold B with boundary S³ and H_i(S³;R_n)→H_i(B;R_n) is an isomorphism for every i, then for every K the zero surgeries M(P(K)) and M(K) are smoothly R_n-homology cobordant, with preferred meridian carried to preferred meridian. If P has strong winding number ±1 and P(U) is pseudo-slice (respectively locally flat topologically slice), then M(P(K)) and M(K) are smoothly (respectively topologically) homology cobordant through a 4-manifold whose fundamental group is normally generated by the image of the fundamental group of either boundary component. The category and closure hypotheses are separate and cannot be inferred from winding alone.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A pattern of winding n≠0, a knot K, and the stated Z[1/|n|]-slice disk for P(U).

[F1] M(K) is obtained by preferred-longitude zero surgery ([[def-zero-surgery-on-a-knot]]).

[F2] The pattern and its winding number are defined using the solid-torus core and meridional disk ([[def-satellite-induced-concordance-operator]]).

## Proof

1.1 Remove a tubular neighborhood of the slice disk for P(U) and glue in the exterior of K along the zero-framed boundary torus. The boundary components are M(P(K)) and M(K); the meridian identification follows from the gluing slope. [F1, F2, given, algebra]

1.2 The relative homology calculation is made with coefficients R_n=Z[1/|n|]. The pattern exterior contributes the winding multiplication n on the meridional class, which is invertible over R_n; the slice-disk exterior contributes no additional reduced homology. Mayer–Vietoris then gives the required R_n-homology isomorphisms. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

2.1 The complete construction must verify the relative disk complement homology, the boundary inclusions in every degree, and the category-specific local flatness or smoothness of the glued cobordism. These are proved in the cited CFHH and CDR arguments under their precise slice, pseudo-slice, and topological-slice hypotheses. [step 1.1, step 1.2]
