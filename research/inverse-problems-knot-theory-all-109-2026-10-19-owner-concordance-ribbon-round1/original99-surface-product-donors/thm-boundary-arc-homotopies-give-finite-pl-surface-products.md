## Statement

Let $M$ be a compact connected orientable irreducible PL three-manifold with a supplied finite compatible triangulation. Let $G$ be a connected boundary component, let $F\subset G$ be a connected compact PL subsurface with nonempty boundary, and set $E=\overline{G\setminus F}$. Suppose that $F$ and every component of $E$ induce injective fundamental-group maps into $M$.

Suppose every essential properly embedded polygonal arc $k\subset F$ is homotopic in $M$, with its endpoints fixed, to a path in $E$. Then $M$ is PL homeomorphic to $F\times[0,1]$, with its bottom face equal to the specified $F$ and its remaining boundary equal to $E$. In particular $E$ is connected, and $F$ is boundary-parallel by an actual PL product. Comparison paths need not be simple, and no extra restriction on separating arc or disk classes is imposed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite oriented irreducible PL manifold, its boundary decomposition and arc homotopies.

[F1] An essential polygonal arc in $F$, homotopic with endpoints fixed into a $\pi_1$-injective boundary complement, is the exact $F$-trace of a proper embedded PL disk. This theorem needs no irreducibility premise and permits a singular comparison path. [[lem-incompressible-boundary-complements-give-controlled-pl-arc-disks]]

[F2] A connected finite PL boundary surface has an embedded finite graph spine with a graph-thickening presentation. Nullhomotopic simple interior circles bound PL disks; every boundary circle of a compact connected oriented surface other than a disk is infinite order. [[lem-nullhomotopic-circles-on-finite-oriented-pl-surfaces-bound-pl-disks]] Polygonal disk boundary maps extend over disks, finite arcs have strip neighborhoods and the two disk double-arc resolutions join respectively the two outer pieces and all three cut pieces, retaining each chosen piece at most once. [[lem-finite-polygonal-disk-and-collar-surgery]], [[lem-two-sheeted-pl-disk-immersion-has-essential-embedded-disk]]

[F3] Compact PL boundaries have PL collars. [[lem-compact-locally-collared-pl-subpolyhedron-has-pl-collar]]

## Proof

1.1 First suppose $F$ is not a disk. Take the finite graph thickening of [F2], choose a spanning tree in its spine and take co-core arcs across the bands of all edges outside that tree. They are disjoint properly embedded polygonal arcs $k_1,\ldots,k_r$, and cutting $F$ at all of them leaves the thickening of the tree, a disk $F_0$. Every such arc is essential already in $F$: its edge lies on a cycle, and cutting its band reduces the first graph rank without splitting off a disk. Equivalently a disk cobounded by it and a boundary arc would make that band a tree finger, contrary to its being an edge outside the spanning tree. Apply [F1] to obtain proper embedded disks $D_j$ with boundaries $k_j\cup l_j$, where each $l_j$ is a simple arc in $E$. Their interiors miss $\partial M$. [F1, F2, given, construct]

2.1 We make the prescribed disks disjoint by priority, keeping earlier disks embedded and pairwise disjoint throughout. Suppose $D_1,\ldots,D_{j-1}$ have already been fixed. Put only $D_j$ in PL transverse position against their union, fixing its prescribed $k_j$ and allowing its other boundary to move in $E$. At boundary points use the half-plane model; interior vertex shifts avoid vertex-on-sheet and edge-edge incidences and permit transverse edge-face crossings, giving a finite one-manifold of intersections. Every arc has both endpoints on the unmarked $E$ portion of a fixed disk and on the unmarked $E$ portion of $D_j$, since their specified $k_i$ are disjoint. Distinct fixed disks are disjoint, so there are no triple points involving two fixed disks. We keep $D_j$ embedded at every subsequent operation; no self-intersection cleanup theorem is needed. [F2, F3, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 First remove intersection circles. For a circle on a fixed disk $D_i$, choose one whose disk region $A\subset D_i$ is innermost among the circles of $D_i\cap D_j$. No intersection arc meets that region: its endpoints are on $\partial D_i$ and it cannot cross the selected intersection circle. Thus $\operatorname{int}A\cap D_j=\varnothing$. The same circle bounds an interior subdisk $B\subset D_j$, disjoint from its marked boundary segment $k_j$. Replace $B$ by $A$, pushed slightly off $D_i$ toward the side occupied by the retained outside collar of $D_j$, and round the seam. The selected intersection was transverse, so that outside collar has one fixed side. Since $A$ has interior disjoint from $D_j$, the replacement is an embedded disk with the same boundary; a sufficiently small push is disjoint from its retained compact remainder. It misses all other fixed disks because $D_i$ does. It removes the selected circle and any circles discarded with $B$, and adds none. Repeat until no circle remains; the finite component count decreases strictly. [F2, F3, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 If an intersection arc remains on a fixed $D_i$, choose one outermost on $D_i$ on the side avoiding the connected marked segment $k_i$. Such an outermost cap $A$ exists because every endpoint lies on the complementary boundary interval $l_i$: among the finitely nested endpoint intervals in $l_i$, choose an inclusion-minimal one. Its interior misses $D_j$, and its boundary is the selected arc $a$ plus an interval $b\subset l_i\subset E$ whose interior has no further endpoint. Cutting $D_j$ along $a$ gives two disks. Retain the one containing its entire marked segment $k_j$, and cap it with $A$, pushed slightly off $D_i$ on the retained side and rounded. The new boundary is $k_j$ followed by an embedded path in $E$: the old retained boundary portions and $b$ meet only at the joining endpoints, because $\operatorname{int}A\cap D_j=\varnothing$ includes its boundary interval's lack of further intersection endpoints. The new disk is embedded by the same empty-cap argument as step 3.1. It has no new intersections with the fixed disks and loses at least $a$. Iterate to make $D_j$ disjoint from the earlier union. The nonnegative number of intersection components decreases after each operation. Process $j=1,\ldots,r$. This gives a simultaneous pairwise disjoint embedded disk system with all prescribed $k_j$ retained exactly. [F2, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 Cut $M$ along these disks and call the result $M_0$. It is connected. Indeed cutting $F$ along all the $k_j$ leaves the connected disk $F_0$, so all disk shores adjacent to that disk lie in one component of $M_0$. Every component is reached from that component by crossing some cut disk, because $M$ was connected. Both shores of every cut disk abut the same connected $F_0$, so crossing any such disk stays in that component. Thus there are no others. It is irreducible by a direct proper-disk argument. A sphere in its interior maps to a sphere in $M$ disjoint from the disk system, and hence bounds an interior ball there. Each cutting disk is connected and misses that ball boundary, so lies wholly inside or outside it. Its boundary is on $\partial M$ and outside the interior ball, forcing the outside alternative. The ball therefore misses every cutting disk and lifts unchanged into the same cut piece. This proves irreducibility without importing the choice hypothesis of a more general cut-group theorem. Put $E_0$ equal to the cut copy of $E$ together with both copies of every cut disk, regarded as boundary flaps along their $l_j$ shores; their other shores adjoin $\partial F_0$. Adding such a disk flap along an interval changes no fundamental group. Cutting a surface along disjoint proper arcs also injects each piece's fundamental group into the original: choose finite graph spines in the cut surface pieces and reattach the finitely many arc strips as bands. The resulting graph-of-thickenings is the original surface; joining the component spines through those bands gives a graph spine in which every component spine is a subgraph. A reduced loop in a subgraph cannot acquire a cancellation using an edge outside that subgraph. This proves the injection even for separating cut arcs. Consequently every component of $E_0$ is $\pi_1$-injective in $M_0$: a loop nullhomotopic in $M_0$ becomes null in $M$, hence null in $E$ by the given injection, and then null in its cut component; the disk flaps do not affect that conclusion. [F2, step 1.1, step 4.1, given, construct]

6.1 The boundary component of $M_0$ containing $F_0$ is made of that disk and its complementary connected surface $E_0$ with one boundary circle. Other original boundary components are for now left separate. Connectedness of this complement follows directly by removing the interior of one disk from a connected closed surface; any path crossing the removed disk can be replaced by a path along its boundary. The common circle is nullhomotopic in $M_0$ via $F_0$, and hence null in $E_0$ by step 5.1. Its class is trivial only when $E_0$ is a disk, by the boundary-circle assertion in [F2]. Thus this entire boundary component is a sphere. Push it slightly into its boundary collar. Irreducibility makes the pushed sphere bound a PL ball. That ball must be on the side away from the original boundary sphere, because it is contained in the manifold interior and cannot contain any point of that boundary; adjoining the thin collar recovers a ball bounded by the original sphere. Since this ball has no interior frontier in connected $M_0$, it is all of $M_0$. In particular no other boundary component is present. [F2, F3, step 5.1, construct]

7.1 We reconstruct the marked product, rather than concluding it just from the number of disks. Cutting the standard product $F\times[0,1]$ along the rectangles $k_j\times[0,1]$ gives a ball with bottom disk the cut $F_0$. Each rectangle gives two disk windows on its boundary, meeting that bottom disk along the paired $k_j$ shores. In the actual ball $M_0$, the two copies of each $D_j$ are exactly such disk windows in the complementary disk $E_0$, each adjoining $F_0$ along its prescribed shore. Their interiors are disjoint. Removing finitely many such boundary caps from the disk $E_0$ leaves a disk: remove one cap and its attaching interval at a time, and use the finite polygonal disk parametrization in [F2]. This remaining disk is precisely the cut copy of $E$, so it is connected as well. The same finite marked-cap configuration holds in the standard cut product. [F2, step 1.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct]

8.1 Map the bottom disk of the actual ball to the standard $F_0$ by its original identification with the cut surface. For each paired disk window choose the map of one copy matching its prescribed $k_j$ shore and any compatible PL parameter on its $l_j$ shore; define the map on its paired copy using the actual and standard disk gluing identifications. These boundary parameters agree because both identifications recover the same original $k_j$ in $F$. Extend across the windows by polygonal disk extension [F2]. The remaining complementary disks now have prescribed PL boundary homeomorphisms; extend across them by [F2]. This gives a homeomorphism of the two boundary spheres, respecting every paired window and the specified bottom $F_0$. Parametrize both PL balls by a standard ball and cone this sphere map after a common finite subdivision; this extends it to a PL ball homeomorphism. Descending under the paired disk identifications gives a PL homeomorphism $M\to F\times[0,1]$, whose bottom restriction is identity and whose other boundary is exactly $E$. [F2, step 7.1, construct]

9.1 If $F$ is a disk, take no cutting arcs or disks. Its boundary circle is nullhomotopic in $M$, so injectivity of $E$ makes it null in the complementary connected surface $E$. That complement is connected because it is the complement of a disk in the connected closed boundary component $G$. The boundary-circle clause of [F2] forces $E$ to be a disk. The argument of step 6.1 makes $M$ a ball, and the disk-marked boundary extension of step 8.1 identifies it with $F\times[0,1]$. This includes the vacuous arc-homotopy case, completing all stated surface types. [F2, F3, step 6.1, step 8.1, given, construct] ∎
