## Statement

Assume full AC. Let $P,Q$ be compact connected oriented smooth surfaces with nonempty boundary, with $\partial Q$ connected. Let $f:(P,\partial P)\to(Q,\partial Q)$ be a smooth map with $f^{-1}(\partial Q)=\partial P$, equal to a product map on smaller boundary collars, such that each restriction from a boundary circle of $P$ to $\partial Q$ is a diffeomorphism. Suppose $f_*:\pi_1P\to\pi_1Q$ is injective and $f_*[P,\partial P]=\varepsilon[Q,\partial Q]$ for $\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}$. Then $P$ has one boundary component, $f_*$ is an isomorphism, and $f$ is homotopic relative to its boundary to a homeomorphism $P\to Q$. The homotopy keeps interior points in the interior. No three-manifold isotopy or covering-space descent is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the two oriented surfaces, the actual map and collars, injectivity and the stated relative degree.

[F1] Subgroups are realized by connected coverings and maps lift when their induced fundamental-group image is contained in the covering subgroup. [[thm-classification-of-connected-covering-spaces]], [[thm-covering-space-lifting-criterion]]

[F2] Compact oriented surfaces have finite disk-and-band normal forms, free fundamental group of rank $2g+b-1$, and every regular nullhomotopic interior circle bounds a disk. Boundary surfaces retract onto finite graphs; a finite cutting arc system reduces a one-boundary genus-$g$ surface to a disk. These constructions follow from the finite graph thickening and oriented polygon reductions in the supplier. [[lem-finite-chart-surface-normal-forms-supply-jordan-disks-and-torsion-free-groups]]

[F3] Van Kampen and finite free-product normal forms preserve component injections when boundary intervals are joined by one-handles. A handle between two components gives their free product, while a handle on one component adds a free generator, by contracting its interval attaching collars to points. Cellular and singular homology agree on CW complexes; their pair sequences give the same comparison for CW pairs. [[thm-seifert-van-kampen]], [[thm-normal-form-for-free-products-with-amalgamation]], [[thm-cellular-homology-computes-singular-homology]], [[thm-long-exact-sequence-of-a-pair-in-singular-homology]]

[F4] Finite bump perturbations make a smooth surface map transverse to a finite disjoint arc system, fixing its boundary product collars. [[thm-parametric-transversality]]

[A1] Full AC permits the covering and finite surface constructions and implies their countable-choice hypotheses. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

## Proof

1.1 Let $H=f_*\pi_1P$ and take its based connected covering $q:Q_H\to Q$ by [F1]. The map lifts to $\widetilde f:P\to Q_H$; it is still a map of boundary pairs. If this covering had infinitely many sheets, its relative top homology would vanish. Here is the finite-chain verification: lift a finite triangulation of $Q$. A relative simplicial two-cycle has equal oriented coefficients on the two triangles incident to every interior edge. The triangle adjacency graph is connected, also along boundary vertex links, so all coefficients are equal. Infinite sheet number gives infinitely many triangles, whereas a chain has finite support, forcing that coefficient to be zero. The cellular-to-singular comparison for the lifted CW pair in [F3] identifies this computation with relative singular homology. This would force $f_*[P,\partial P]=q_*\widetilde f_*[P,\partial P]=0$, a contradiction. For finite sheet number $d$, the same triangle calculation gives the fundamental relative generator upstairs, and its pushforward is $d[Q,\partial Q]$. Thus $d$ divides the integer $\varepsilon$, so $d=1$ and $H=\pi_1Q$. Together with the supplied injection this makes $f_*$ an isomorphism. [A1, F1, F2, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 If $P$ had more than one boundary circle, the class of each such circle would be nonzero in $H_1(P;\mathbb Z)$. Indeed an oriented proper arc joining that circle to another boundary circle gives signed intersection one with an interior parallel of the first, so detects its nonzero class. Its image under $f$ is plus or minus the sole boundary circle of $Q$, which is zero in $H_1(Q;\mathbb Z)$ since it bounds the oriented fundamental two-chain. This contradicts the $H_1$ isomorphism induced by step 1.1. Hence $P$ has one boundary circle. By [F2] the free group ranks are $2g(P)$ and $2g(Q)$; their abelianizations are free of those ranks and are isomorphic. Thus their genera agree, say $g$. If $g=0$, both are disks; the boundary diffeomorphism extends radially to a disk homeomorphism, and convex interpolation in disk coordinates gives a homotopy relative to the boundary, preserving interiors. The remaining steps handle $g>0$. [F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Choose $2g$ disjoint smooth proper arcs in $Q$ cutting it into one disk, with all their boundary endpoints distinct. Apply [F4], keeping the product collars fixed. Each inverse image is then a compact neat one-manifold. The fixed boundary diffeomorphism gives exactly two boundary endpoints for the inverse image of each target arc. Hence it consists of one proper arc and finitely many interior circles. The inverse images of distinct target arcs are disjoint. All perturbations are small on a compact interior set, so preserve the proper-map condition and the relative homotopy class. [A1, F2, F4, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 Remove the circle components by the following finite homotopy. Each such circle maps into a contractible target arc; injectivity from step 1.1 makes it nullhomotopic in $P$, so [F2] gives a disk that it bounds. Choose a disk innermost among all the inverse-image circles. No inverse-image proper arc lies in its interior, since its endpoints lie on the original boundary of $P$. Thus its interior misses the entire target arc system. In a short outer collar of this circle the map lies on the opposite side of its one target arc, in an interior rectangular neighborhood of a compact subarc. Its outer rim consequently contracts within that one open half-rectangle. Replace the map on the disk plus this collar by such a filling, entirely in that half-rectangle. This replacement is homotopic to the old filling relative to the outer rim: gluing the fillings gives a two-sphere map into $Q$, whose homotopy class is zero because $Q$ retracts onto a graph by [F2]. It creates no new inverse image of any target arc and removes at least the chosen circle. Repeat finitely many times; each operation is in the interior and leaves the fixed boundary untouched. A small relative smoothing and transverse adjustment within the separated half-rectangles keeps all these separations. [F2, F4, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 There remains exactly one proper inverse-image arc for each target arc. Cut $P$ along these $2g$ arcs. Every resulting component injects on fundamental groups into $P$: regluing one cut arc is attachment along an interval, so van Kampen gives a free product if it joins components and adds one free generator if it joins two boundary intervals of one component; the reduced-word normal forms in [F3] preserve all component injections at each finite stage. Each component maps into the disk obtained by cutting $Q$, hence its fundamental group has trivial image in $\pi_1Q$. The injections just checked and step 1.1 make its fundamental group trivial. The compact surface normal form in [F2] therefore makes it a disk. Cutting along one proper arc increases Euler characteristic by one, as seen by removing its rectangle collar. Thus the number of these disks is $\chi(P)+2g=(1-2g)+2g=1$. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 On each remaining inverse-image arc homotope its map, relative to its two endpoints, to the interval homeomorphism onto the target arc with the same endpoints. Use disjoint thin strip collars to extend these homotopies: transversality gives one fixed crossing sign on each connected arc, so in the target strip replace the tangential coordinate by that interval parameter and the normal coordinate by a small signed linear transverse coordinate; interpolate on the outer strip collars. Keep the original boundary product collars fixed. Their endpoint orders agree with the prescribed boundary diffeomorphism. After cutting, the resulting map of the single source disk has a boundary homeomorphism onto the single target disk: its sides are precisely the boundary segments and the two copies of each cut arc, in their matching cyclic order. Parametrize the two disks by convex disks. Extend that boundary homeomorphism radially to a homeomorphism of disks. The old disk map and this homeomorphism are homotopic relative to the boundary by convex interpolation in the target coordinates. Both maps take disk interiors to disk interiors, so the interpolation does also. Reglue paired arc sides; the side maps and their homotopies coincide on both copies, yielding a homeomorphism $P\to Q$ and a homotopy from $f$ fixed on the original boundary. This proves the full assertion, including the disk case from step 2.1, without a general Nielsen theorem or a three-manifold isotopy premise. [F2, step 2.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
