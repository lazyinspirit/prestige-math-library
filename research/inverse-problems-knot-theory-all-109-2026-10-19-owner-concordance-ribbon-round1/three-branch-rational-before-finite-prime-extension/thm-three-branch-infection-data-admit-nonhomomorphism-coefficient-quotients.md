## Statement

Let $P$ be a smooth winding-zero satellite pattern with unknotted axis $\eta$ and nonzero rational classical axis self-pairing. Fix $n\ge1$ and a smooth knot $J$. Put $J^1_0=J^2_0=J$, $J^0_0=J\#J$ and $J^a_{h+1}=P(J^a_h)$ for $0\le a\le2$, $0\le h<n$. Supply finite disk-band models and local ambient roundings, peripheral product tubes and paths for this finite collection and $P(U)$. Put $K=J^1_n\#J^2_n\#(-J^0_n)$, where the inverse knot uses the marked mirror-and-reverse convention. Supply a compact connected oriented topological four-manifold $V$ with collared boundary $M(K)$, with $H_1(M(K);\mathbb Z)\to H_1(V;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$ an isomorphism and $H_2(V;\mathbb Z)=0$.

Let $C$ be the actual standard connected-sum cobordism and put $W_n=V\cup_{M(K)}C$, with other boundary $M(J^1_n)\sqcup M(J^2_n)\sqcup(-M(J^0_n))$. Recursively attach the actual three standard infection pieces, reversing the distinguished branch's orientation:
$$W_h=W_{h+1}\cup E_{1,h}\cup E_{2,h}\cup(-E_{0,h}).$$
All attachments are along their outgoing components, with supplied compatible collars and signed-surgery markings preserving the positive meridian and the zero slope. Fix coherent base paths through these finite pieces.

There is an explicit quotient homomorphism $\phi:\pi_1(W_0)\twoheadrightarrow G$ such that $G$ is PTFA of derived length at most $n+1$, satisfies the right- and left-projective defining property of $D(\mathbb Q)$, and has finite Folner sets. The meridian of $K$ has infinite order in $G$. The restriction of $\phi$ to $\pi_1(M(J^0_0))$ is trivial. Each restriction to $\pi_1(M(J^i_0))$, $i=1,2$, is trivial or has infinite cyclic image, and at least one of these two images is infinite cyclic. This is a theorem for the supplied topological or smooth $V$, without a choice axiom. It asserts neither existence of that $V$ from a locally flat slice disk nor an analytic rho identity.

The quotient is given as follows. Set $G_h=\pi_1(W_h)$, $\Lambda=\mathbb Q[t,t^{-1}]$, and use the positive-meridian abelianization on all the $W_h$. Let $A_h$ be the image in $H_1(W_h;\Lambda)$ of the cyclic submodule generated by the distinguished top companion-meridian class in $H_1(-M(J^0_n);\Lambda)$. Define $P^0G_h=G_h$, $P^1G_h=[G_h,G_h]$, and
$$P^2G_h=\ker\{P^1G_h\to(P^1G_h)_{\mathrm{ab}}\otimes\mathbb Q=H_1(W_h;\Lambda)\to H_1(W_h;\Lambda)/A_h\}.$$
For $i\ge2$ define $P^{i+1}G_h=\ker\{P^iG_h\to(P^iG_h)_{\mathrm{ab}}\otimes\mathbb Q\}$. Then $G=G_0/P^{n+1}G_0$ and $\phi$ is the actual quotient map. The normal-cover identification in the displayed definition is the prescribed based one, with right transport by inverse units.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite smooth knot and peripheral models, nonzero rational axis self-value, actual initial collared $V$ and three-branch adjunctions in the Statement.

[F1] The standard product infection and connected-sum pieces have the stated actual spines and ordinary boundary homology maps. Their outgoing $H_1$ maps are isomorphisms; their incoming components generate ordinary $H_2$. The infection pair retracts to the outgoing surgery with the filling solid torus attached along its boundary. [[lem-standard-infection-cobordisms-have-boundary-generated-second-homology]], [[lem-standard-infection-cobordism-adjunction-kills-the-companion-longitude]]

[F2] Finite-model exterior longitudes lie in the second derived subgroup, and knot exterior and surgery groups are normally generated by their positive meridian. The actual exterior-to-surgery map is a classical Alexander and Blanchfield isometry. [[lem-finite-disk-band-knot-longitudes-lie-in-the-second-derived-subgroup]], [[thm-zero-surgery-identifies-exterior-classical-alexander-and-blanchfield-data]]

[F3] A winding-zero infection gives the actual classical pairing isometry taking its pattern axis to its companion meridian. [[thm-winding-zero-infection-preserves-classical-blanchfield-pairings]]

[F4] Ordinary boundary-cokernel zero on a compact collared four-manifold with Betti-one boundary gives localized boundary-cokernel zero for PTFA coefficients. A nontrivial PTFA system on a Betti-one manifold kills localized first homology. The kernel on any selected finite union of such boundary components is isotropic. [[thm-ptfa-localized-boundary-intersection-rank-is-no-larger-than-ordinary-rank]], [[lem-ptfa-localization-kills-first-homology-of-betti-one-manifolds]], [[lem-zero-localized-intersection-rank-forces-finite-boundary-union-kernel-isotropy]]

[F5] Empty integral pair data on a supplied $V$ with $H_1(V;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$ and $H_2(V;\mathbb Z)=0$ yield boundary-kernel isotropy after all standard adjunctions for PTFA coefficient targets of the needed derived length. [[thm-integral-pair-data-yield-blanchfield-bordisms-after-standard-infections]]

[F6] Subgroup extension preserves actual nonzero Blanchfield self-values. The normal-cover coefficient-one homology class of $u\in N\triangleleft\pi_1(X)$ is $[u]\otimes1\in N_{\mathrm{ab}}\otimes\mathbb Q$, naturally for actual maps. [[lem-ptfa-subgroup-scalar-extension-preserves-blanchfield-self-pairings]], [[lem-normal-cover-local-homology-detects-mixed-derived-kernels-without-choice]]

[F7] PTFA groups satisfy projective augmentation detection over $\mathbb Q$ and the finite Folner condition without choice. [[thm-ptfa-augmentation-detects-projective-injectivity-without-choice]], [[lem-ptfa-groups-have-finite-folner-sets]]

[F8] The actual collared gluings have the local-coefficient Mayer–Vietoris and pair sequences. [[thm-excision-and-mayer-vietoris-with-local-coefficients]], [[thm-pair-long-exact-sequences-with-local-coefficients]]

## Proof

1.1 First establish the precise connected-sum classical form decomposition. Realize a two-knot connected-sum piece as [F1]'s infection along a positive meridian, of winding one. Its spine is $M(L_1)\cup_S M(L_2)$ for the common filling solid torus $S$, and its pair relative to $M(L_1\#L_2)$ has relative homology $H_*(S,\partial S;\Lambda)$. Use on this piece the common positive-meridian system $t$. The core of $S$ also has image $t$, so $H_1(S;\Lambda)=0$, $H_0(S;\Lambda)=\Lambda/(t-1)$. Both incoming $H_0$ groups are the same quotient, and their degree-zero difference map is $(1,-1)$, which is injective. Mayer–Vietoris [F8] therefore identifies the incoming direct sum of $H_1$ with $H_1$ of the piece. For the outgoing map, the torus local chain complex gives $H_1(\partial S;\Lambda)=\Lambda/(t-1)$ generated by the meridian of $S$, while $H_0(\partial S)\to H_0(S)$ is an isomorphism. Hence $H_1(S,\partial S;\Lambda)=0$, and $H_2(S,\partial S;\Lambda)=\Lambda/(t-1)$ is represented by its meridian disk and its local translates. Its boundary on the outgoing knot exterior is the companion longitude. That coefficient-one class is zero by the actual second-derived longitude/normal-cover calculation in [F2,F6]. The pair connector is therefore zero, and the outgoing $H_1$ map is an isomorphism too. The same product-spine equivalence identifies the incoming exterior inclusions with their actual inclusions into the outgoing knot exterior; no formal ordinary-to-twisted inference is made. [F1, F2, F6, F8, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The ordinary degree-two boundary image on this piece is all its second homology by [F1]. Apply [F4] to the cyclic coefficient group to obtain localized boundary-intersection rank zero. All its boundary classical systems are nontrivial and localized-first-acyclic. The finite-union kernel is therefore isotropic. The graph of the isomorphisms from step 1.1 lies in this kernel, so its orthogonal boundary form identity is exactly that the outgoing classical form is the orthogonal sum of the incoming forms. Orientation reversal negates the form, because it negates inverse oriented cap duality and leaves Bockstein and evaluation unchanged. Iterate these actual two-knot product constructions to the signed three-knot list defining $C$. This proves an actual isomorphism and isometry $$H_1(M(K);\Lambda)\cong H_1(M(J^1_n);\Lambda)\oplus H_1(M(J^2_n);\Lambda)\oplus H_1(-M(J^0_n);\Lambda),\qquad B_K=B_1\oplus B_2\oplus(-B_0).$$ All maps are the inclusions into $C$ and its inverse outgoing isomorphism, with the given base paths. [F1, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Ordinary Mayer–Vietoris and [F1] show $H_1(W_h;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$ for every $h$, with every top meridian a generator; the given signed markings make its positive-meridian map the specified one. On classical local homology, gluing $C$ to $V$ preserves $H_1(V;\Lambda)$ because the outgoing $H_1$ map to $C$ is an isomorphism by step 2.1 and its $H_0$ map is an isomorphism. Each subsequent winding-zero piece has its outgoing classical $H_1$ map an isomorphism by [F3], and its $H_0$ map is the isomorphism $\Lambda/(t-1)\to\Lambda/(t-1)$. Mayer–Vietoris thus preserves $H_1$ of the preceding $W$ at each of the finitely many attachments. Consequently $$H_1(V;\Lambda)\xrightarrow{\cong}H_1(W_h;\Lambda)$$ for every $h$, with actual inclusion maps, and each $A_h$ is the image of the same cyclic distinguished top submodule. These facts also identify the based normal-cover maps used in the definition of $P^2$. [F1, F3, F6, F8, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.2 Write $\xi_a$ for the three top companion-meridian classes on $M(J^a_n)$, identified by step 2.1 with their direct summands in $H_1(M(K);\Lambda)$. By the actual winding-zero isometry [F3], their positive-orientation self-values are all the same nonzero $b=B_{P(U)}(\eta,\eta)\in\mathbb Q(t)/\Lambda$. On the distinguished negatively oriented component the sign is negative. Apply [F5] to $V$ with its empty pair list, cyclic coefficients and no attachments. Its inclusion kernel $\mathcal M\subset H_1(M(K);\Lambda)$ is isotropic. If both $\xi_1,\xi_2$ died in $H_1(V;\Lambda)/\operatorname{im}(\Lambda\xi_0)$, there would be $f_1,f_2\in\Lambda$ with $u_i=\xi_i-\xi_0f_i\in\mathcal M$. Set $a_i=\bar f_i f_i$. Orthogonality of the three summands gives $0=B_K(u_i,u_i)=(1-a_i)b$. Also $u_1f_2-u_2f_1=\xi_1f_2-\xi_2f_1\in\mathcal M$ has self-value $(a_1+a_2)b=0$. Adding these three zero identities gives $2b=0$. But $\mathbb Q(t)/\Lambda$ is a rational vector space, so multiplication by two is invertible, contradicting $b\ne0$. Thus at least one $\xi_i$, $i=1,2$, survives in this quotient. This proves the required algebra without a polynomial factorization or a metabolizer equality. [F3, F5, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 All the prescribed $P^iG_h$ are normal and contain $G_h^{(i)}$. This holds for $P^1$; conjugation on $(P^1)_{\mathrm{ab}}\otimes\mathbb Q$ is its meridian $\Lambda$ action, so the $\Lambda$ submodule $A_h$ makes the displayed kernel $P^2$ normal. It contains $[P^1,P^1]=G_h^{(2)}$. Inductively conjugation preserves each later abelianization-and-tensor kernel, giving normality, while $[P^i,P^i]\subseteq P^{i+1}$ gives the derived containment. Each factor $P^i/P^{i+1}$ is torsion-free abelian: the first is $\mathbb Z$, the second embeds in the rational vector space $H_1(W_h;\Lambda)/A_h$, and later factors embed in their rational tensor spaces. Therefore $G_h/P^rG_h$ is PTFA of derived length at most $r$ for every relevant $r$. This argument uses no basis of any of those rational spaces. [given, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 The actual map $q_h:G_{h+1}\to G_h$ is the quotient by the normal closure of the three companion longitudes, and its kernel lies in $G_{h+1}^{(n-h+1)}$. Here is the depth check in the simultaneous three-branch setting. In $G_{h+1}$ every piece at levels $j>h$ is already present. The image of each pattern group lies in the normal closure of its outgoing meridian by [F2], and its axis lies in its commutator by zero winding. Thus finite descent from the top meridians puts $\mu^a_{h+1}$ in $G_{h+1}^{(n-h-1)}$. The whole outgoing surgery group, and hence its companion exterior, lies in that subgroup by normal generation. Its longitude lies in the exterior's second derived subgroup, hence has depth $n-h+1$. Each of the three adjunctions is the actual longitude quotient by [F1], so their composite kernel is exactly the finite normal closure of those deep elements. The same axis-commutator argument in the newly adjoined pieces gives $\mu^a_h\in G_h^{(n-h)}\subseteq P^{n-h}G_h$. The quotient direction is $W_{h+1}\to W_h$. [F1, F2, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 These actual maps preserve the modified quotient factors through index $r=n-h+1$. Write $N=\ker q_h\subseteq G^{(r)}$, $G=G_{h+1}$. Since $r\ge2$, $N\subseteq G^{(2)}=[P^1G,P^1G]$, so the restricted quotient induces an isomorphism of $P^1$ abelianizations and their rational tensor groups. Step 3.1 identifies the $A$ images under that same map; quotienting the rational groups by these images is therefore an isomorphism. Taking its actual kernels proves $q_h(P^2G)=P^2G_h$ and $q_h^{-1}(P^2G_h)=P^2G$. At each next index $i<r$, the already established derived containment gives $$N\subseteq G^{(r)}\subseteq G^{(i+1)}\subseteq[P^iG,P^iG].$$ The quotient by $N$ consequently preserves that abelianization, its rational tensor group and the next kernel, by the same finite element-lifting argument. Induction proves that $q_h$ induces $G/P^iG\cong G_h/P^iG_h$ for $i\le r$ and the corresponding successive factors for $i<r$. This is proved for the modified second stage, rather than inferred from characteristicness or asserted as an isomorphism of the unquotiented subgroups. [step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 The distinguished top companion-meridian class is in $A_n$ by its definition. Under the natural maps of steps 3.1 and 6.1 its actual loop, identified with $\mu^0_{n-1}$ in the first distinguished infection piece, lies in $P^2G_{n-1}$. If $\mu^0_{h+1}\in P^{n-h}G_{h+1}$, its image is in $P^{n-h}G_h$ by step 6.1. That subgroup is normal. The pattern group at the new distinguished piece lies in its normal closure, and its axis $\mu^0_h$ lies in the commutator of that image. Therefore $\mu^0_h\in[P^{n-h}G_h,P^{n-h}G_h]\subseteq P^{n-h+1}G_h$. Descending finitely proves $\mu^0_0\in P^{n+1}G_0$, including $n=1$ directly from the first assertion. The distinguished bottom surgery group is normally generated by this meridian, so its entire image in $G$ is trivial. [F1, F2, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

7.2 Choose one of those two surviving indices, taking $i=1$ if it works and $i=2$ otherwise. By step 3.1 its class is nonzero in $H_1(W_n;\Lambda)/A_n$, hence its actual loop is nonzero in $P^1G_n/P^2G_n$ by the defining kernel and [F6]. The first infection adjunction identifies that loop with $\mu^i_{n-1}$, and step 6.1 preserves the factor through index two. Therefore $\mu^i_{n-1}$ is nonzero in $P^1G_{n-1}/P^2G_{n-1}$ and has infinite order by step 4.1. This is the survival base case; the distinguished meridian was killed by design rather than asserted to survive. [F1, F6, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 6.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra]

8.1 Suppose $h\le n-2$ and the chosen $\mu^i_{h+1}$ is nonzero in $P^{n-h-1}G_{h+1}/P^{n-h}G_{h+1}$. Put $s=n-h$, $\Gamma=G_{h+1}/P^sG_{h+1}$. Its meridian image has infinite order by step 4.1. By step 5.1 the entire outgoing surgery group's image lies in $G_{h+1}^{(s-1)}$, and its commutator lies in $G_{h+1}^{(s)}\subseteq P^s$. Thus its coefficient image in $\Gamma$ is the actual infinite cyclic subgroup generated by this meridian. The new companion meridian $\mu^i_h$ has zero ordinary class on that surgery by zero winding, hence maps into $G_{h+1}^{(s)}\subseteq P^sG_{h+1}$ and is a coefficient-one local cycle. Its classical self-value is the nonzero pattern-axis value by [F3]. Subgroup extension [F6] carries that value injectively to the $\mathbb Q[\Gamma]$ linking form. The target $\Gamma$ is PTFA of derived length at most $s\le n$ by step 4.1. Apply [F5] with the empty initial pair list and all actual standard attachments forming $W_{h+1}$: its restriction to $V$ factors through the required derived quotient since $\Gamma^{(n)}=1$. The selected boundary kernel is isotropic, so this nonzero-self class has nonzero image in $H_1(W_{h+1};\mathbb Q[\Gamma])$. [F3, F5, F6, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 7.2, construct, algebra]

9.1 The normal-cover identification [F6] sends that nonzero image to the actual tensor class of $\mu^i_h$ in $(P^sG_{h+1})_{\mathrm{ab}}\otimes\mathbb Q$. Here $s\ge2$, so this is precisely the ordinary rational tensor kernel defining $P^{s+1}$ after the modified stage. Hence the meridian is nonzero in $P^sG_{h+1}/P^{s+1}G_{h+1}$. Step 6.1 transports that actual factor to $P^sG_h/P^{s+1}G_h$ and takes the loop to its marked $\mu^i_h$. This proves the finite descent to a nonzero infinite-order $\mu^i_0$ in $P^nG_0/P^{n+1}G_0$. When $n=1$ no such further descent is needed, since step 7.2 already is the bottom conclusion. [F1, F6, step 4.1, step 6.1, step 7.2, step 8.1, construct, algebra]

10.1 Put $G=G_0/P^{n+1}G_0$. Step 4.1 supplies its PTFA series and derived-length bound, and [F7] gives $D(\mathbb Q)$ and finite Folner sets without choice. The meridian of $K$ remains infinite order because $G$ surjects onto $G_0/P^1G_0=\mathbb Z$. By step 5.1 each bottom meridian lies in $P^nG_0$, and the corresponding entire surgery image lies in the abelian subgroup $P^nG_0/P^{n+1}G_0$, since that surgery group is normally generated by its meridian. Its image therefore factors through $H_1(M(J^a_0);\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$; it is trivial or infinite cyclic because the target factor is torsion-free abelian. Step 7.1 gives triviality for branch zero, while step 9.1 gives infinite cyclic image for at least one of branches one and two. These are exactly the required restrictions of the single actual quotient map $\phi$. All normal closures and paths came from the supplied finite construction. No slice-disk tube, infinite selection or analytic signature theorem was used, so all the stated coefficient conclusions hold for the supplied topological and smooth $V$. [F2, F7, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 7.1, step 9.1, algebra] ∎
