## Statement

Fix $n\ge1$, $q\ge1$ and finitely many marked smooth infection towers $J_{a,j+1}=P_{a,j}(J_{a,j})$, $1\le a\le q$, $0\le j<n$, whose unknotted axes $\eta_{a,j}$ have winding zero. Supply compatible finite disk-band models, ambient roundings, peripheral product tubes and paths for all their knots and patterns. Let $E_{a,j}$ be their actual standard infection cobordisms. Supply a compact connected oriented topological four-manifold $W_n$ with disjoint marked collared boundary components $M(J_{a,n})$, allowing orientation reversals, such that $H_1(W_n;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$ and each positive meridian $\mu_{a,n}$ represents either generator. Form the actual collared adjunctions
$$W_j=W_{j+1}\cup_{\bigsqcup_aM(J_{a,j+1})}\bigsqcup_aE_{a,j},\qquad G_j=\pi_1(W_j),$$
with each piece oriented to make the attachment oriented; other boundary components are allowed. Based paths through these finite gluings are supplied.

Fix an odd prime $p$. Define the mixed series by $P^0G=G$ and
$$P^{i+1}G=\ker\{P^iG\longrightarrow(P^iG/[P^iG,P^iG])\otimes_{\mathbb Z}R_i\},\qquad R_0=\cdots=R_{n-1}=\mathbb Q,\quad R_n=\mathbb F_p.$$
For $0\le j<n$ set $s_j=n-j$, $\Gamma_j=G_{j+1}/P^{s_j}G_{j+1}$, and $k_j=\mathbb Q$ if $j>0$, $k_0=\mathbb F_p$. Let $A_j=k_j[\Gamma_j]$ and let $K_j$ be its Ore fraction division ring, with standard involution. Use on $W_{j+1}$ the quotient coefficient homomorphism and based standard parallel Hermitian structure.

Assume for every $a,j$ that the classical axis class on $M(P_{a,j}(U))$ has nonzero self-pairing over $k_j[t,t^{-1}]$. Assume also the following actual intersection data for each $j$: there is a finite list $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_{m_j},d_1,\ldots,d_{m_j}\in H_2(W_{j+1};A_j)$ with
$$\lambda(\ell_a,\ell_b)=0,\qquad\lambda(\ell_a,d_b)=\lambda(d_b,\ell_a)=\delta_{ab},\qquad\dim_{K_j}\operatorname{coker}\{H_2(\partial W_{j+1};K_j)\to H_2(W_{j+1};K_j)\}\le2m_j.$$
Existence of these intersection data is a hypothesis. Then for every $1\le a\le q$ and $0\le j\le n$ the actual meridian satisfies
$$\mu_{a,j}\in P^{n-j}G_j,\qquad[\mu_{a,j}]\ne0\text{ in }P^{n-j}G_j/P^{n-j+1}G_j.$$
Its image has infinite order for $j>0$ and order exactly $p$ for $j=0$.

A sufficient condition for all the displayed intersection-data hypotheses is that $W_n$ is obtained by finitely many marked standard connected-sum and infection adjunctions from one compact connected oriented topological manifold $V$ with boundary a knot zero surgery, $H_1(V;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$, $H_2(V;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^{2r}$, and supplied integral zero-and-unit intersection pairs in $H_2(V;\mathbb Z[\pi_1(V)/\pi_1(V)^{(n)}])$. The stipulated meridian generator condition on $W_n$ remains part of the hypotheses. In this sufficient condition no existence of $V$ or of its pairs is asserted.

In particular, if all integral classical axis self-values are nonzero, their rational hypotheses hold automatically, and the bottom prime hypothesis holds for all odd primes outside an explicitly finite exceptional set. The integral self-values may be supplied in the knot exteriors: the actual exterior-to-surgery identifications carry them to the values used here. This theorem applies in the supplied topological and smooth scopes without a choice axiom; it does not assert that a topological concordance relation supplies the hypothesized intersection data.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite marked tower, collared manifolds, meridian homology, odd prime, nonzero classical self-values and actual finite intersection data in the Statement.

[F1] One actual infection adjunction along its outgoing boundary gives the quotient by the normal closure of its companion longitude, and identifies the companion meridian with the marked axis core. Each knot-surgery group is normally generated by its meridian, and each exterior preferred longitude lies in the second derived subgroup. The finite single-branch derived-depth argument uses only these facts and zero winding; its local argument will be applied simultaneously below. [[lem-standard-infection-cobordism-adjunction-kills-the-companion-longitude]], [[lem-finite-disk-band-knot-longitudes-lie-in-the-second-derived-subgroup]], [[lem-finite-winding-zero-infection-towers-preserve-required-derived-quotients]]

[F2] Mixed subgroups are characteristic, contain ordinary derived subgroups, and an all-rational finite prefix gives a PTFA quotient. [[lem-killing-deep-derived-normal-subgroups-preserves-mixed-derived-quotients]]

[F3] The ordinary infection calculation identifies the outgoing meridian with the pattern meridian; the companion meridian maps to winding number times that generator, and the outgoing $H_1$ map to $E_j$ is an isomorphism. The actual classical inclusion isometry carries the pattern-axis class to the companion-meridian class on the outgoing surgery. [[lem-standard-infection-cobordisms-have-boundary-generated-second-homology]], [[thm-winding-zero-infection-preserves-classical-blanchfield-pairings]]

[F4] Subgroup extension to a PTFA coefficient group preserves nonzero Blanchfield self-values. PTFA field group rings have involutive Ore fraction division rings. A nontrivial such coefficient system on a compact Betti-one manifold has zero localized first homology. [[lem-ptfa-subgroup-scalar-extension-preserves-blanchfield-self-pairings]], [[lem-ptfa-integral-and-field-group-rings-have-compatible-ore-involutions]], [[lem-ptfa-localization-kills-first-homology-of-betti-one-manifolds]]

[F5] The displayed finite intersection pairs and localized rank bound imply isotropy of the inclusion kernel on any selected boundary component with localized first homology zero, without assumptions on the other boundary components. [[thm-unimodular-intersection-pairs-force-boundary-blanchfield-kernel-isotropy]]

[F6] On a connected manifold, the actual coefficient-one class of a loop $u\in N\triangleleft\pi_1(W)$ in $H_1(W;k[\pi_1(W)/N])$ corresponds to $[u]\otimes1$ in $N_{\mathrm{ab}}\otimes_{\mathbb Z}k$. [[lem-normal-cover-local-homology-detects-mixed-derived-kernels-without-choice]]

[F7] Nonzero integral closed classical self-values stay nonzero rationally and outside an explicitly finite exceptional prime set. Actual exterior-to-surgery maps preserve these classes and forms. [[thm-integral-classical-blanchfield-self-values-survive-rational-and-prime-reduction-without-choice]], [[thm-zero-surgery-identifies-exterior-classical-alexander-and-blanchfield-data]]

[F8] Integral homology and lifted zero-and-unit pair data on a supplied initial $V$ persist through finitely many actual connected-sum and infection adjunctions, giving the needed localized rank bound for every PTFA coefficient homomorphism factoring through the supplied derived quotient. [[thm-integral-pair-data-yield-blanchfield-bordisms-after-standard-infections]]

## Proof

1.1 Each $\Gamma_j$ uses only the first $s_j\le n$ rational rings, so [F2] makes it PTFA. More explicitly its finite series has successive factors embedded in the additive groups $P^iG/P^{i+1}G\hookrightarrow(P^iG)_{\mathrm{ab}}\otimes\mathbb Q$, which are torsion-free abelian. Thus [F4] supplies precisely the rings and involutions in the Statement, including when $k_j=\mathbb F_p$. This does not call the final mixed quotient $G_0/P^{n+1}G_0$ PTFA. [F1, F2, F4, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Fix $j<n$ and work in $G=G_{j+1}$. For every branch $a$ and $j+1\le h<n$, its piece $E_{a,h}$ is already in $W_{j+1}$. Its pattern-boundary image lies in the normal closure of $\mu_{a,h+1}$ by normal generation in [F1]. Its axis has ordinary class zero by zero winding and [F3], so its image $\mu_{a,h}$ lies in the commutator of that pattern-group image. Starting with $\mu_{a,n}\in G$, finite descent therefore gives $\mu_{a,j+1}\in G^{(n-j-1)}$. The entire image of its outgoing surgery group, hence of its companion exterior, lies in the same derived subgroup by normal generation. Its companion longitude lies in the exterior's second derived subgroup by [F1], so its image $\lambda_{a,j}$ lies in $G^{(n-j+1)}$. Attach the $q$ pieces at level $j$ in their fixed finite order. Each attaches along one connected component of the preceding connected manifold, so [F1] gives the actual quotient by that longitude; finitely composing gives $G_j=G/\langle\!\langle\lambda_{1,j},\ldots,\lambda_{q,j}\rangle\!\rangle_G$. This entire kernel lies in $G^{(n-j+1)}$. By [F2], the actual map $q_j:G\to G_j$ therefore identifies mixed quotients through index $n-j+1$ and their successive factors below that index. In $G_j$ the new pattern-boundary image lies in the normal closure of $q_j(\mu_{a,j+1})$, and its axis image is $\mu_{a,j}$ in its commutator. Consequently $\mu_{a,j}\in G_j^{(n-j)}\subseteq P^{n-j}G_j$ for every branch. This proves the depth and quotient statements for the actual simultaneous finite forest; the additional branches introduce only the explicitly listed deep relations. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 Under the sufficient initial-$V$ condition, each $W_{j+1}$ is obtained from that same $V$ by a finite list of the standard adjunctions. By step 1.1 the derived length of $\Gamma_j$ is at most $s_j\le n$, since a group with a length-$s_j$ normal series of abelian factors has its $s_j$th derived subgroup trivial, as follows by descending its commutator images through the series. Hence the restriction $\pi_1(V)\to\Gamma_j$ kills $\pi_1(V)^{(n)}$ and factors through the specified initial quotient. Apply [F8] with the field $k_j$ to obtain all the displayed pairs and dimension bounds on $W_{j+1}$, with $m_j=r$. All its remaining boundary components are the marked zero surgeries in those finite pieces, exactly as required by [F8]. Thus this sufficient condition genuinely supplies the hypotheses used below, without declaring an infection cobordism itself an integral half-solution. [F8, given, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.3 At $j=n$, the supplied integral homology sends every $\mu_{a,n}$ to one of its two free generators. Consequently its image under $G_n\to(G_n)_{\mathrm{ab}}\otimes\mathbb Q$ is $1$ or $-1$ in $\mathbb Q$, and the kernel of this map is $P^1G_n$. Each meridian class in $G_n/P^1G_n$ is therefore nonzero and of infinite order. This is the base case of descending induction. [given, step 1.1, base, algebra]

3.1 Fix $j<n$ and assume the assertion for every branch at $j+1$. Fix a branch $a$ and abbreviate its $\mu_{a,h},J_{a,h},P_{a,h},\eta_{a,h}$ by $\mu_h,J_h,P_h,\eta_h$ throughout this induction step. Put $s=n-j$, $G=G_{j+1}$, $\Gamma=G/P^sG$ and $k=k_j$. Since $j+1>0$, the induction hypothesis gives infinite order for $\mu_{j+1}$ in $P^{s-1}G/P^sG$ and hence in $\Gamma$. By step 2.1 the image of the entire outgoing surgery group $\pi_1(M(J_{j+1}))$ lies in the normal closure in $G$ of $\mu_{j+1}\in G^{(s-1)}$, and therefore in $G^{(s-1)}$. Its commutator subgroup maps into $G^{(s)}\subseteq P^sG$. Its coefficient homomorphism to $\Gamma$ thus factors through its ordinary abelianization $\mathbb Z[\mu_{j+1}]$. The infinite order just proved makes this factorization an isomorphism onto the actual infinite cyclic subgroup $H=\langle\mu_{j+1}\rangle\le\Gamma$. Identifying $t$ with this element gives the exact classical coefficient system on that boundary, including its positive meridian and standard based Hermitian structure. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.3, ih, construct, algebra]

4.1 Let $u$ be the actual positive companion meridian of $J_j$ viewed in $M(J_{j+1})$. Its ordinary homology class is zero: by [F3] its image in $H_1(E_j;\mathbb Z)$ is the winding number zero, while the outgoing $H_1$ map is an isomorphism. Thus $u\in\pi_1(M(J_{j+1}))^{(1)}$, so step 3.1 puts its image in $G^{(s)}\subseteq P^sG$. It consequently defines an actual coefficient-one local cycle on the outgoing boundary and on $W_{j+1}$ with the quotient $\Gamma$ coefficients. In the classical boundary module its class is, by the actual isometry [F3], the image of $[\eta_j]$ on $M(P_j(U))$. Its classical self-value is therefore the nonzero value assumed in the Statement, with the identical $k[t,t^{-1}]$ convention. [F1, F2, F3, given, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 The classical boundary fraction field is $k(t)$. Its first homology vanishes by the nontrivial cyclic coefficient case of [F4], since the ordinary first Betti number of a zero surgery is one over every stated $k$. Apply the subgroup comparison [F4] to the actual subgroup $H\le\Gamma$ identified in step 3.1. The class of $u$ then has nonzero self-pairing in $H_1(M(J_{j+1});k[\Gamma])$ with values in $K_j/k[\Gamma]$: that theorem gives the exact value as the injective image of its nonzero classical value. The coefficient system is nontrivial, so [F4] also gives $H_1(M(J_{j+1});K_j)=0$. These statements use the coefficient system restricted from the actual $W_{j+1}$, rather than an unrelated representation of its boundary group. [F3, F4, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Apply [F5] on $W_{j+1}$ with $R=A_j$, $K=K_j$, and selected component $M(J_{j+1})$. Its collar and orientation are those in the construction, its finite pairs and dimension bound are exactly the assumed data, and the selected localized first homology vanishes by step 5.1. The inclusion kernel is isotropic. Since the class of $u$ has nonzero self-pairing, it cannot be in this kernel. Its actual image in $H_1(W_{j+1};k[\Gamma])$ is therefore nonzero. Reversing the selected boundary orientation, if required by the marked attachment, only negates its form and does not change this implication. No other boundary component has been assumed localized-acyclic. [F5, given, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Use [F6] with $N=P^sG$. Step 4.1 puts $u$ in this kernel, and step 6.1 says its coefficient-one homology class is nonzero. Its class $[u]\otimes1\in(P^sG)_{\mathrm{ab}}\otimes k$ is consequently nonzero. Here $k=R_s$: if $j>0$ then $s<n$ and both are $\mathbb Q$, whereas $j=0$ gives $s=n$ and both are $\mathbb F_p$. The defining kernel of this tensor map is exactly $P^{s+1}G$. Hence $u$ has a nonzero class in $P^sG/P^{s+1}G$. For $j>0$ this group embeds in a rational vector space and is torsion-free, so that nonzero class has infinite order. For $j=0$ it embeds in an additive $\mathbb F_p$ vector space, where each nonzero element has order exactly $p$. This derives the order from the actual normal-cover class, without a universal-coefficient splitting. [F2, F6, step 4.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 By step 2.1 the actual adjunction epimorphism $q_j:G_{j+1}\to G_j$ identifies $P^sG/P^{s+1}G$ with $P^sG_j/P^{s+1}G_j$, and its image of $u$ is the actual marked $\mu_j$. It therefore preserves the nonzero class and its exact order from step 7.1. Together with step 2.1 this proves the assertion at $j$ for every branch $a$ and completes the finite descending induction from step 2.3. All quotient maps here have the inclusion direction $W_{j+1}\to W_j$; no inverse geometric inclusion is used. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.3, step 3.1, step 7.1, induction, algebra]

9.1 Finally suppose the supplied integral classical axis self-values are nonzero. By [F7] all the rational hypotheses follow, and each of the finitely many bottom values remains nonzero over $\mathbb F_p$ outside the finite divisors of the product of their explicit nonzero exceptional integers. Include $2$ among those divisors to obtain only odd admissible primes. There is such a prime without an infinite choice: if $D\ne0$ is their product, a least prime divisor of $2|D|+1$ is odd and does not divide $D$; existence of a least prime divisor follows by choosing the least integer divisor greater than one and checking it cannot be composite. The exterior values, when used as input, are the same closed values by the actual maps in [F7]. Every induction, path, quotient and exceptional-prime calculation is finite or given by a prescribed normal-cover formula. The intersection data were used as explicit hypotheses throughout, so the proof makes no assertion of their existence from a topological concordance relation. This proves the entire stated topological and smooth conditional theorem without a choice axiom. [F7, step 1.1, step 2.3, step 8.1, algebra, discharge-induction: step 3.1] ∎
