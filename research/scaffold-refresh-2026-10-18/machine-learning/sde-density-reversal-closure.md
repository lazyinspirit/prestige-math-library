# Constructive density and reversal prerequisite closure

This addendum is part of the new probability pair P-SDE-03 and existing machine-learning pair P-SDE-02, preserving P-SDE-01 and all original stable IDs/conditional Anderson contracts. Items 1–4 and 8 belong to the new P-SDE-03 A page; items 5–7 belong to P-SDE-02. The published P-SDE-01 is reused without extension. It supplies an unconditional, nontrivial learning regime without an unspecified parabolic density theorem. It is a complete local prerequisite design and argument, not an authored/accepted proof. The broad conditional theorem does not supply any density assertion unless its full stated hypotheses have been established; the following module is the concrete branch available to ML-60 without that import.

## Exact spaces, hypotheses and interfaces

Fix d>=1, T>0, 0<epsilon<T. Let mu be a Borel probability on R^d supported in the closed ball of finite radius R. Let g:[0,T]->(0,infinity) be C1 and satisfy 0<g_min<=g(t)<=g_max<infinity; put v(t)=integral_0^t g(r)^2 dr. Assume AC exactly as in the published Itô strong-solution and conditional-expectation suppliers. On their usual complete filtered probability space take X0 with law mu independent of the d-dimensional Brownian future. Set X_t=X0+integral_0^t g(r)dW_r. Existing global Lipschitz/linear-growth SDE existence and finite moments are reusable; no new generic SDE existence result is proposed. mu need not have a Lebesgue density, can be singular/discrete and need not be Gaussian. The reverse initial law X_T is unbounded in general.

Proposed local prerequisite items, in this order:

1. `lem-deterministic-ito-integral-has-gaussian-independent-increments`: the integral over [s,t] has independent centered Gaussian law with covariance [v(t)−v(s)]I, independent of the past. Start with step integrands; independent Brownian Gaussian increments give their joint characteristic function. Approximate deterministic continuous g in L2 by step integrands. Itô isometry gives L2 convergence; |exp(iu·z)−exp(iu·w)|<=|u||z−w| gives characteristic-function convergence. The same joint calculation with an arbitrary bounded past measurable variable preserves independence. Uniqueness of finite characteristic functions identifies the Gaussian law; if the library characteristic-function uniqueness theorem cannot be used with its exact finite-law hypotheses, prove this finite Gaussian-convolution specialization locally via Fourier inversion for the smooth Gaussian density. No martingale representation theorem is needed.
2. `def-gaussian-smoothed-data-law-and-transition-density`: let phi_v(z)=(2 pi v)^(−d/2)exp(−|z|^2/(2v)); for t>0 define p_t(x)=integral phi_v(t)(x−z) mu(dz), and q(s,x;t,y)=phi_(v(t)−v(s))(y−x). Positive Gaussian integration gives a strictly positive normalized density by Tonelli and the published multivariate-normal density/change-of-variables construction. Its transition law follows from item 1; finite Gaussian covariance addition gives Chapman–Kolmogorov. This definition discharges density existence for this branch, with no assumption that mu itself has a density.
3. `thm-gaussian-smoothing-density-and-forward-equation`: on every [epsilon,T], p is C1 in time and C-infinity in space and solves partial_t p=(g(t)^2/2) Delta p. For any finite derivative order, the derivative of phi_v is a polynomial times the same Gaussian; with v bounded below and z in a fixed compact ball, its absolute value on each compact x-neighborhood has a uniform integrable constant. Differentiation under the finite measure integral is therefore justified by dominated convergence. The same calculation differentiates the explicit kernel; integrating its Laplacian gives zero by Gaussian tails. On R^d, uniqueness among probability-law-valued weak forward solutions with narrowly continuous t and prescribed initial law follows by testing against the bounded smooth backward solution h(s,x)=integral phi_(v(t)−v(s))(y−x)f(y)dy for f smooth compactly supported. Cut off h spatially, let the cutoff radius increase using bounded derivatives and finite total mass, then obtain integral f dmu_t=integral h(0,x)mu(dx). Thus the explicit heat semigroup identifies the law; no unspecified boundary condition or uniqueness theorem is invoked. Endpoint h(t)=f is obtained by dominated convergence/Gaussian approximate identity.
4. `thm-gaussian-mixture-score-and-global-lipschitz-bound`: define the posterior weighting nu_(t,x)(dz)=phi_v(t)(x−z)mu(dz)/p_t(x). It is a probability kernel by positive p and measurability. Direct differentiation gives score_t(x)=−[x−m_(t,x)]/v(t), m_(t,x)=integral z nu_(t,x)(dz), and D_x score=−I/v(t)+Cov_nu(Z)/v(t)^2. Since |Z|<=R, ||Cov_nu(Z)||<=R^2. Hence uniformly on [epsilon,T], ||D score||<=1/v(epsilon)+R^2/v(epsilon)^2, and |score_t(x)|<=(|x|+R)/v(epsilon). The bound follows from u^T Cov u<=E(u·Z)^2<=R^2|u|^2. This is a verified global state-Lipschitz/linear-growth contract; it is not asserted for arbitrary unbounded data measures.
5. `def-bayes-reversed-gaussian-smoothing-kernels`: for 0<=u<w<=T−epsilon, define K_(u,w)(y,dx)=p_(T−w)(x)q(T−w,x;T−u,y)dx/p_(T−u)(y), and Y0 law p_T. The forward marginal identity normalizes this probability kernel at every y. Multiplication cancels the intermediate p and forward Chapman–Kolmogorov proves reverse Chapman–Kolmogorov. Iterated Bayes proves its finite-dimensional laws coincide with (X_(T−u)); no abstract infinite product construction is necessary, because the forward process already has continuous paths.
6. `lem-reversed-smoothing-generator-and-martingale`: for compactly supported smooth phi and t=T−u, write the small-time reversed conditional expectation as [P^*_(t−h,t)(p_(t−h)phi)](y)/p_t(y). Differentiate the explicit Gaussian kernel, use the preceding forward equation, and Taylor-expand at h=0 to obtain the generator [L_t^*(p_t phi)−phi L_t^*p_t]/p_t=(g(t)^2/2)Delta phi+g(t)^2 score_t·grad phi. The sign is plus score; the drift of the original pure-noise process is zero. To justify this on compact y neighborhoods, p_t has a strictly positive compact minimum and every differentiated Gaussian convolution is locally uniformly bounded on [epsilon,T]; tails have Gaussian decay since mu is compact. Finite difference quotients thus converge locally uniformly. Differentiate K_(u,w)phi under the integral (or integrate the same generator identity) to get K_(u,w)phi−phi=integral_u^w K_(u,r) Ltilde_r phi dr. Conditioning at successive times gives phi(Y_w)−phi(Y_u)−integral_u^w Ltilde_r phi(Y_r)dr as a martingale increment after localization to a bounded ball. This supplies the generator-to-martingale step rather than assuming a separate reverse martingale-problem theorem.
7. `thm-gaussian-smoothing-reversal-on-truncated-time-interval`: localize the coordinate functions x_i and products x_i x_j by smooth cutoffs and use item 6. The continuous local martingale M_u=Y_u−Y0−integral_0^u g(T−r)^2 score_(T−r)(Y_r)dr has brackets [M_i,M_j]_u=delta_ij integral_0^u g(T−r)^2dr. Define B_u=integral_0^u g(T−r)^(−1)dM_r by localized L2 integration; inverse g is bounded. Its brackets are delta_ij u. Prove the required Lévy characterization locally: complex Itô applied to exp(i theta·(B_t−B_s)+|theta|^2(t−s)/2) yields a local martingale whose modulus is bounded by exp(|theta|^2T/2), hence is a true martingale after stopped dominated convergence. Therefore E[exp(i theta·(B_t−B_s))|F_s]=exp(−|theta|^2(t−s)/2); finite characteristic-function uniqueness gives Gaussian increments independent of the reverse past. Thus B is Brownian in the reversed filtration and dY_u=g(T−u)^2 score_(T−u)(Y_u)du+g(T−u)dB_u. Item 4 verifies the global state-Lipschitz/linear-growth drift premise of the published strong existence/pathwise uniqueness theorem on [0,T−epsilon]. The time dependence is Borel/continuous, and Y0 has finite second moment and is independent of future B increments by the same conditional characteristic-function statement. This identifies the unique law of the reversed SDE without imposing Anderson's bounded reverse initial law or an unspecified augmented KFP theorem.
8. `thm-gaussian-smoothing-endpoint-weak-limit`: E|X_epsilon−X0|^2=d v(epsilon)->0. Hence X_epsilon converges in probability and weakly to mu; coupling gives W2 distance at most sqrt(d v(epsilon)). The exact reversed endpoint law at time T−epsilon consequently approaches mu. No regular score, Lipschitz drift, or solver-uniform error constant is asserted at the singular endpoint epsilon=0. Total generative error must separately bound endpoint truncation, learned-score error and numerical discretization; letting step size decrease does not remove either of the first two.

## Dependency closure and breadth boundary

The module needs only published finite Gaussian laws/densities, finite independent products, Tonelli/dominated convergence, characteristic functions/uniqueness or the explicit finite Gaussian-convolution proof above, scalar Itô isometry and formula coordinatewise, continuous local martingales and quadratic covariation (with stopped stochastic integration supplied in item 7 if unavailable), and published global strong Lipschitz SDE well-posedness/moments. Its new local obligations are explicitly enumerated in items 1–8; none calls the broad density theorem or assumes its own reversal conclusion. Measurable posterior kernels are explicit density ratios, so no additional measurable-selection theorem is hidden.

This gives a complete proof design for the additive Gaussian-smoothing branch of ML-60 and actual initial-law weak-limit closure. P-SDE-02's original general state-dependent theorem is retained as a conditional result with its full density/semigroup hypotheses, not applied to every neural drift or nonlinear forward diffusion. For an unconditional theorem for arbitrary smooth uniformly elliptic state-dependent coefficients, a separate parametrix/heat-kernel module is still required; its absence does not justify claiming such a theorem here. Likewise the untouched Wasserstein-gradient-flow and random-matrix prerequisite designs retain their original explicit local obligations; no source reading or proof completion for them is fabricated by this addition.
