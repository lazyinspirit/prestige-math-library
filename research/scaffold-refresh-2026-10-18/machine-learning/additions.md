### ML-84 — Sketched kernel learning and certified approximate solves

**Pages:** sketched-kernel-learning-and-certified-solves / sketched-kernel-learning-and-certified-solves-examples. **Scope and interfaces:** `def-regularized-kernel-prediction-system`, `alg-nystrom-kernel-ridge-fit`, `thm-kernel-ridge-gram-perturbation-bound`, `thm-kernel-ridge-residual-prediction-certificate`, `rem-statistical-and-numerical-kernel-errors`. For a finite real PSD Gram matrix K, response y in R^n and tau=n lambda>0 under mean-square empirical loss plus lambda times squared RKHS norm, define a=(K+tau I)^{-1}y and fitted vector u=Ka=y−tau a. The algorithm takes K,y,tau, a conformal finite sketch S, a solve tolerance epsilon>=0, and a finite solve budget; invoke published PSD Nyström to form Ktilde=KS(S^TKS)^dagger S^TK, solve (Ktilde+tau I)atilde=y, and return the coefficients, Ktilde atilde, and actual residual or `budget-exhausted` with the available residual. A budget exit is not a certified solve; finite comparison always returns a status. Use direct factorization or a separately specified iterative solver, never an instruction merely to “invert accurately.” For approximate coefficient b with residual r=y−(K+tau I)b, ||b−a||<=||r||/tau and ||Kb−Ka||<=||K|| ||r||/tau. For any two PSD K,Ktilde with ||K−Ktilde||<=epsilon, ||a−atilde||<=epsilon ||y||/tau^2 and ||u−utilde||<=epsilon ||y||/tau. Include these precise deterministic guarantees separately from a statistical risk bound.

**Argument:** The PSD spectral theorem gives both inverse norms <=1/tau. The resolvent identity a−atilde=(K+tau I)^{-1}(Ktilde−K)(Ktilde+tau I)^{-1}y gives the coefficient estimate; u−utilde=−tau(a−atilde) gives the fitted-vector estimate. Subtracting linear systems gives b−a=−(K+tau I)^{-1}r. Reuse `thm-psd-nystrom-order-and-exactness` and `thm-nystrom-deterministic-spectral-residual-bound` for PSD and spectral approximation certificates, with its full-row-rank leading sketch premise. Gaussian range-finder expectations in the published randomized low-rank page are only expectations for their exact real/complex Gaussian sketch regimes; do not silently turn them into high-probability or leverage-score results. Out-of-sample prediction additionally needs a cross-kernel approximation certificate; the training-vector estimate alone supplies none.

**Prerequisites:** ML-14/15; published Nyström definition/projector/order/error items, finite SVD and regularized least squares. **Examples:** zero/singular K; exact captured range; small lambda amplifying coefficient error; low rank with poor test-point cross-kernel. **Sources:** actual published Nyström theorem bodies read in this refresh; inherited GPML kernel-ridge normalization. No new statistical effective-dimension theorem is imported.

### ML-85 — Bilevel learning, hypergradients, and implicit layers

**Pages:** bilevel-learning-hypergradients-and-implicit-layers / bilevel-learning-hypergradients-and-implicit-layers-examples. **Scope and interfaces:** `def-smooth-bilevel-learning-problem`, `alg-adjoint-hypergradient-with-residual`, `thm-smooth-bilevel-hypergradient-formula`, `thm-adjoint-hypergradient-linear-solve-error`, `rem-truncated-training-and-exact-hypergradients`. Let theta in an open subset of R^p, w in R^d, inner loss f be C2, outer loss g be C1. At (theta0,w0) suppose grad_w f=0 and H=partial^2_ww f is invertible. The local stationary branch w*(theta) exists and is C1; it need not be a global inner optimizer. With B=partial_theta grad_w f, J(theta)=g(w*(theta),theta), the column-gradient formula is grad J=grad_theta g−B^T H^{-T}grad_w g. If H>=mu I for mu>0 and f is globally strongly convex in w on R^d, this branch is the unique inner optimizer wherever its stationary point exists. Keep that additional regime separate.

The algorithm takes a supplied inner state w, the matrices/oracles H,B, the two derivatives of g, mu>0 with a valid H>=mu I certificate, a tolerance and finite linear-solve budget. Solve H^T v=grad_w g by an admissible SPD solver; return grad_theta g−B^T vtilde, actual residual rho=grad_w g−H^T vtilde and success/budget status. At the exact inner state, the hypergradient error is <=||B|| ||rho||/mu. At an approximate inner state add a separately proved state/sensitivity perturbation bound, requiring a common invertible chart, Lipschitz derivatives and bounded inverse; do not identify a low training residual with a hypergradient guarantee.

**Argument:** Apply published `thm-euclidean-implicit-function-theorem` to R(theta,w)=grad_w f; Dw*=−H^{-1}B. Apply the total-derivative chain rule and transpose to obtain the formula. Solve-error bound follows from v−vtilde=H^{-T}rho. Reuse the full-chart hypotheses of `lem-implicit-sensitivity-error-controlled-by-residual`. Published `thm-finite-iteration-sensitivity-bound-and-residual-limitation` explicitly distinguishes differentiating K training steps from differentiating an exact fixed point: its contraction q<1, derivative-Lipschitz constants and C1 initialization give q^K d0+C K q^(K−1) delta0; zero primal residual alone need not give a correct derivative. The same issue occurs for meta-learning and equilibrium networks. **Prerequisites:** published implicit-function theorem, AD and Hessian-vector interfaces, SPD linear solves; ML-37 for neural specialization. **Examples:** ridge hyperparameter; singular Hessian at a nonisolated optimum; exact state with wrong truncated derivative. **Sources:** actual published sensitivity proof bodies read; no uninspected deep-equilibrium global-convergence theorem is claimed.

### ML-86 — Exact tiled attention and memory-aware differentiation

**Pages:** exact-tiled-attention-and-memory-aware-differentiation / exact-tiled-attention-and-memory-aware-differentiation-examples. **Scope and interfaces:** `def-attention-block-summary`, `alg-exact-tiled-softmax-attention`, `thm-tiled-attention-exactness`, `thm-tiled-attention-workspace-and-work`, `rem-attention-io-model-and-roundoff`. Take finite real Q,K in R^(n times d), V in R^(n times v), scale c, a fixed admissible-key mask with at least one key in every query row, and positive integer key-block size b. Return rowwise softmax(c QK^T+mask)V. For one row initialize an empty summary, not the undefined exponential of minus infinity minus minus infinity. For each nonempty allowed block J form scores s_j, m_J=max_J s_j, l_J=sum_J exp(s_j−m_J), and z_J=sum_J exp(s_j−m_J)V_j. If the old summary is empty, copy these values. Otherwise set m'=max(m,m_J), l'=exp(m−m')l+exp(m_J−m')l_J and z'=exp(m−m')z+exp(m_J−m')z_J. Skip empty masked blocks; return z/l after the last block. Specify the query and key loops and stop after the finite block partition. No all-masked-row distribution is defined without a separate convention.

**Argument:** Induct on processed allowed keys: m is their maximum, l=sum exp(s_j−m), z=sum exp(s_j−m)V_j. The merge equations preserve each invariant by factoring exp(m_old−m_new). A nonempty final row has l>0; z/l is exact dense attention in real arithmetic. Evaluating one query at a time needs O(n(d+v)) stored inputs/output plus O(b+v) extra score/summary workspace, arithmetic O(n^2(d+v)); there is no materialized n by n score/probability array. A blocked query implementation trades SRAM/workspace against rereads. For backward differentiation replay the same fixed mask and recompute blocks, applying published AD/chain rule; if dropout is added its random mask must also be replayed. Exact real arithmetic does not imply bitwise equality of floating-point accumulation orders or a hardware speedup.

**Prerequisites:** ML-35 and published reverse-mode correctness; finite sums, exponentials, matrix multiplication. **Sources:** Dao et al., FlashAttention, full text retrieved; §§2–3 (pp. 3–6) and Appendix C proofs (pp. 22–24) consulted, selected algorithm appendices pp. 20–21. This refresh's provable workspace/work claim is for the explicitly defined algorithm above. The paper’s SRAM/HBM bound Theta(n^2 d^2/M), for its particular tiling/memory model and range d<=M<=nd, is contextual until that exact carrier is authored; its endpoint input-size lower bound is not a universal lower bound at every fixed M. **Examples:** one key; an empty first masked block; two merge orders; replaying a different dropout mask.

### ML-87 — Low-rank adaptation and the geometry of parameter updates

**Pages:** low-rank-adaptation-and-parameter-update-geometry / low-rank-adaptation-and-parameter-update-geometry-examples. **Scope and interfaces:** `def-low-rank-adaptation-of-a-linear-layer`, `alg-finite-budget-low-rank-adaptation`, `thm-low-rank-adapter-merging-and-gradients`, `thm-low-rank-adapter-factorization-ambiguity`, `rem-adaptation-rank-and-empirical-transfer`. Freeze W0 in R^(d times k); choose rank budget 1<=r<=min(d,k), nonzero real scale c, A in R^(r times k), B in R^(d times r), and W=W0+cBA. Define layer output and train only A,B on a supplied differentiable finite empirical loss with fixed minibatch stream, admissible update method/steps and finite horizon T. State initial A (random seed/law if used), B=0, fixed dropout convention if any, and return A_T,B_T and optionally merged W. Each finite update must return a failure status if a supplied primitive is not defined. No neural convergence result is assumed.

**Argument:** rank(W−W0)<=r by image inclusion; trainable scalar count is r(d+k), excluding optional biases and frozen W0 storage. Exact merging preserves every layer output by distributivity, but switching/roundoff/device latency are separate matters. If G=partial L/partial W is the d by k matrix gradient, then grad_B L=c G A^T and grad_A L=c B^T G by the Frobenius inner product and chain rule. With B=0 the initial grad_A is zero, explaining why simultaneously zero A,B stalls this factorization. For any invertible r by r M, BA=(BM)(M^{-1}A), so factors are unidentifiable; this is not convexity of factor training. Any update matrix of rank<=r admits such a factorization by finite SVD; a rank budget below the required update rank is an expressivity obstruction. No theorem says the update needed by every downstream task has small rank or that low rank prevents catastrophic forgetting.

**Prerequisites:** ML-30/35/37, published SVD and matrix chain rule. **Sources:** Hu et al., LoRA arXiv:2106.09685, §§3–4 and beginning of §5, PDF pp. 3–5 read; not a complete-paper reading. Low-intrinsic-rank and measured speed/memory/task results are empirical motivation, not premises of the algebra above. **Examples:** identity adapter change of basis; zero/zero initialization; a full-rank desired linear update; merged versus unmerged arithmetic.

### ML-88 — Preference learning and direct preference optimization

**Pages:** preference-learning-and-direct-preference-optimization / preference-learning-and-direct-preference-optimization-examples. **Scope and interfaces:** `def-bradley-terry-preference-model`, `def-kl-regularized-policy-improvement`, `alg-direct-preference-optimization-finite-budget`, `thm-finite-action-gibbs-policy-optimum`, `thm-dpo-reward-policy-reparameterization`, `rem-preference-coverage-and-model-misspecification`. For a fixed prompt, finite nonempty response set Y, strictly positive reference q, reward r:Y->R and beta>0, maximize F(p)=sum_y p_y r_y−beta sum_y p_y log(p_y/q_y) on the probability simplex, taking 0 log0=0. Let Z=sum_y q_y exp(r_y/beta), p*_y=q_y exp(r_y/beta)/Z. Then p* uniquely maximizes F and F(p*)−F(p)=beta KL(p||p*). Bradley–Terry assumes P(a preferred to b)=sigmoid(r_a−r_b); it is a model for comparisons, not a universal property of human preferences. Substitution gives sigmoid(beta log(p*_a/q_a)−beta log(p*_b/q_b)); adding a prompt-only constant to all rewards leaves both preferences and p* unchanged.

The algorithm takes a finite ordered preference dataset (x,y_w,y_l), a fixed full-support reference, positive differentiable conditional model on every observed response, beta>0, fixed minibatch indices, a stated optimizer/step schedule and finite T. Compute u=beta[log pi_theta(y_w|x)−log q(y_w|x)−log pi_theta(y_l|x)+log q(y_l|x)], evaluate stable loss log(1+exp(−u)), its gradient, perform the specified updates and return parameters/loss/status. This is supervised finite-budget optimization; its termination is separate from attainment or statistical optimality.

**Argument:** Expand beta KL(p||p*)=beta KL(p||q)−sum p r+beta log Z; finite log-sum/Gibbs nonnegativity and equality prove uniqueness. Subtract log p* equations to cancel log Z. Differentiate the finite loss to obtain −beta sigmoid(−u)[grad log pi_w−grad log pi_l]. Finite response support avoids an unproved infinite partition-function assumption; extension to countable strings requires 0<Z<infinity and integrability. Empirical preference MLE recovers p* only under extra identifiability, connected comparison support, realizability and consistency conditions; no such statistical theorem is imported here. A disconnected comparison graph permits component offsets that affect policy mass while preserving observed preferences.

**Prerequisites:** ML-07/13/37, published KL nonnegativity or its elementary finite log-sum specialization. **Sources:** Rafailov et al., DPO arXiv:2305.18290v3 (2024), §§1–5 pp. 1–6 and Appendix A pp. 15–19, implementation p. 20 read; experiments/completion examples not fully read. The appendix gradient has sign/orientation transcription inconsistencies; the local derivative above is derived directly from the displayed preferred-minus-dispreferred loss. **Examples:** two responses; disconnected comparison support; reward offset; zero reference support.

### ML-89 — Discrete masked diffusion and finite-state reverse generation

**Pages:** discrete-masked-diffusion-and-reverse-generation / discrete-masked-diffusion-and-reverse-generation-examples. **Scope and interfaces:** `def-finite-masking-diffusion-chain`, `alg-finite-grid-masked-diffusion-sampler`, `thm-masking-chain-posterior`, `thm-masked-diffusion-kl-reduces-to-cross-entropy`, `thm-finite-chain-reverse-kernel-error-telescope`, `rem-discrete-diffusion-factorization-and-endpoint-limits`. Fix finite clean vocabulary V and separate mask m; length L>=1, grid 0=t0<...<tN=1, survival 1=alpha0>alpha1>...>alphaN=0. Independently mask each surviving token with forward probability 1−alpha_j/alpha_(j−1). For one token x and s<t, an observed unmasked z_t is copied backward; when z_t=m, the true conditional distribution given x is mass (alpha_s−alpha_t)/(1−alpha_t) at x and (1−alpha_s)/(1−alpha_t) at m. These are conditionals on positive-probability events; do not divide by zero at time 0.

The learned reverse sampler starts all masked. At each finite reverse-grid step, for each masked position compute a probability vector h_theta(z_t,t) on clean V with no mask mass; sample an unmask event with probability (alpha_s−alpha_t)/(1−alpha_t) and, if unmasking, sample a token from h; otherwise keep m. Copy already unmasked positions. Declare conditional independent sampling across positions as part of this model, all random seeds/kernels, and stop at t0. At alpha0=1 every remaining mask is removed. This defines a normalized finite generative law. It does not make that law the exact reversal of an arbitrary dependent sequence distribution: joint newly unmasked tokens can be conditionally dependent.

**Argument:** Bayes on the two possible precursor states gives the true single-token posterior. Write c=(alpha_s−alpha_t)/(1−alpha_t). With h(x)>0 the conditional KL of this posterior against the SUBS learned kernel, at a masked state and clean truth x, is −c log h(x): the mask probabilities agree and the only true clean mass is at x. Unmasked states contribute zero. Average over independently forward-masked training examples and sum positions/steps for the finite latent-chain ELBO; reuse published `thm-elbo-kl-decomposition` and its admissibility, retaining reconstruction/prior terms when endpoints are not exactly clean/all-masked. Infinite-time limits need dominated convergence/uniform integrability and are not inferred from the finite identity. If true and learned reverse kernels on the entire finite sequence state space satisfy sup_z TV(Q_j(z,.),P_j(z,.))<=epsilon_j, starting laws differ by epsilon_init, then endpoint TV<=epsilon_init+sum_j epsilon_j. Prove by adding/subtracting one kernel at a time and the TV contraction of a Markov kernel; clamp the bound at one. Marginal token error alone is not a full-sequence kernel error.

**Prerequisites:** ML-01, published finite Markov kernels/law construction and Bayesian ELBO, elementary finite conditional probability; no continuous reverse-SDE supplier is needed. **Sources:** Sahoo et al., Simple and Effective Masked Diffusion Language Models, arXiv:2406.07524; §§2.2–4 pp. 3–6, Appendix A/B pp. 18–22 read. Section 3.3 explicitly calls its parameterization simplification “Rao-Blackwellization” in a looser sense; do not claim a variance-reduction theorem without an actual conditional-expectation estimator pair. **Examples:** one-token exact posterior; two correlated tokens versus factorized reverse draws; model-error accumulation with zero mesh error.

### ML-90 — Monotone risk calibration and finite-sample deployment guarantees

**Pages:** monotone-risk-calibration-and-deployment-guarantees / monotone-risk-calibration-and-deployment-guarantees-examples. **Scope and interfaces:** `def-finite-nested-decision-family-and-bounded-risk`, `alg-finite-grid-conformal-risk-control`, `thm-finite-grid-conformal-risk-control`, `thm-finite-grid-monotone-envelope-risk-control`, `rem-marginal-risk-control-and-adaptive-reuse`. Fix a deterministic finite ordered grid Lambda={lambda1<...<lambdaM}, n>=1, alpha in [0,B), B>0. Let the n+1 random loss vectors (L_i(lambda_j))_j be jointly exchangeable across i, measurable, coordinatewise nonincreasing with values in [0,B], and L_i(lambdaM)<=alpha almost surely. Conditional on an independently trained model the same conditions may be required under an actual regular conditional exchangeable law. Scan j increasingly and return the first j with [sum_(i<=n) L_i(lambda_j)+B]/(n+1)<=alpha; if none, return lambdaM. Output the selected prediction rule and pass/fallback status after at most M tests. Then E L_(n+1)(lambda_hat)<=alpha, with expectation over calibration and test data. The guarantee is not P(risk conditional on the realized calibration set<=alpha)>=1−delta, subgroup coverage, or validity after arbitrary reuse of calibration data.

**Argument:** Let lambda' be the first grid member with sum_(i<=n+1) L_i(lambda)/(n+1)<=alpha; it exists because the maximal member satisfies the endpoint condition. If the calibration scan passes at a member, its true all-n+1 average also passes since L_(n+1)<=B; hence lambda'<=lambda_hat, also on fallback. Monotonicity gives L_(n+1)(lambda_hat)<=L_(n+1)(lambda'). The latter selector is symmetric in all n+1 vectors. Exchangeability therefore makes E L_(n+1)(lambda') equal to E of their average at lambda', which is <=alpha. This finite proof needs no selection theorem, right-continuity, or conditional law given a random multiset. For nonmonotone finite losses define the tail-maximum envelope Ltilde_i(lambda_j)=max_(k>=j)L_i(lambda_k); applying the same algorithm to these monotone exchangeable finite vectors bounds the original test loss by pointwise domination, provided their maximal endpoint obeys the safe bound. The price can be conservative predictions.

**Prerequisites:** published exchangeability and finite measurable-selection/rank interfaces from mathematical statistics; ML-64 for the distinction from conformal miscoverage. **Sources:** Angelopoulos et al., Conformal Risk Control arXiv:2208.02814v4 (13 June 2025), entire 19-page extracted text read. Only the upper-control design is imported, with the finite-grid proof above. Printed Theorem 2's unconditional lower bound is excluded: constant-zero losses satisfy its printed premises but violate alpha−2B/(n+1)>0. Finite grids also avoid additional measurability issues from arbitrary indexing. No lower-tightness theorem is promised without repaired endpoint hypotheses; arbitrary-index robust envelopes additionally need measurable suprema. **Examples:** multilabel false-negative loss with an explicit convention for empty truth sets; all-zero loss; calibration fallback when alpha<B/(n+1); nonmonotone threshold failure.

### ML-91 — Verifiable generation, group-relative updates, and test-time sampling

**Pages:** verifiable-generation-and-test-time-sampling / verifiable-generation-and-test-time-sampling-examples. **Scope and interfaces:** `def-verifier-and-generation-protocol`, `alg-budgeted-verifier-guided-generation`, `alg-finite-group-relative-policy-update`, `thm-independent-generation-success-and-verifier-error`, `thm-group-centered-score-estimator-factor`, `rem-reasoning-training-evidence-and-evaluation-targets`. Fix a prompt, finite truncated response space, sampling policy pi, measurable binary correctness C and verifier A, maximum N independent draws, and a deterministic tie rule for a supplied score. Rejection search returns the first verified response or explicit failure after N attempts; best-of-N samples all N and returns a maximal-score response. Declare the length/token budget and truncation token; these affect the distribution being evaluated. If C has success probability p and the verifier equals C, rejection-search success is 1−(1−p)^N. For an imperfect verifier, the probability of outputting a false acceptance is at most N epsilon when each candidate has P(A=1,C=0)<=epsilon; this union bound does not require independence, whereas the success formula does. An adaptive sampler needs conditional success lower bounds; if P(C_i=1|history)>=p on every history with no previous success, its failure probability is at most (1−p)^N by iterated conditioning.

Define a finite-budget GRPO-style procedure, separately from any guarantee: at each outer iteration freeze old policy with positive probabilities, draw G>=2 responses per prompt, compute finite rewards r_i, mean and population standard deviation s, set A_i=(r_i−mean r)/s if s>0 and A_i=0 if s=0, and keep these values fixed while optimizing a specified clipped importance-ratio empirical objective for K finite inner steps. The clipping threshold, sequence versus token ratio, reference policy, KL-estimator convention, optimizer, step schedule and failure status must be stated. This procedure does not promise monotone policy improvement or unbiased REINFORCE.

**Argument:** Independence yields the success formula by multiplying failure probabilities; false acceptance is a union of per-draw events, giving the verifier bound. For unnormalized group centering and iid responses from a positive differentiable finite policy, score S_i=grad log pi(Y_i), mean rbar, E S_i=0 and independent cross terms imply E[(1/G)sum_i(r_i−rbar)S_i]=(1−1/G)grad E r. Replacing rbar by the leave-one-out mean removes this multiplicative factor. Dividing by random group s, using clipped importance ratios or reusing old-policy samples changes the target again; none inherits unbiasedness from the baseline identity. State exact zero-variance handling to avoid dividing by zero. Correctness under a verifier is distinct from the verifier's own soundness and from mathematical proof checking.

**Prerequisites:** ML-76/79 and published finite policy-gradient/change-of-measure identities, differentiation of finite sums; ML-83 only if making an actual optional-stopping statistical statement. **Sources:** DeepSeek-AI, DeepSeek-R1 arXiv:2501.12948v1 (2025), §§2.2.1–2.2.3 pp. 5–6, §§2.3.2–2.4 pp. 10–11, §§4.2–5 pp. 15–16 read in the original 22-page version; the later 86-page revision was retrieved but not read and supplies no claim. GRPO/clipping, rule-based rewards, rejection filtering, distillation and search limitations are descriptions/empirical evidence. No universal reasoning-scaling or training-convergence law is inferred. The zero-s branch and estimator-factor calculations above are explicit local completions. **Examples:** all group rewards equal; perfect versus false-positive verifier; repeated dependent copies with no sampling gain; empirical pass@1 versus oracle pass@N versus verifier-selected accuracy.

### ML-92 — Active querying, version spaces, and abstention

**Pages:** active-querying-version-spaces-and-abstention / active-querying-version-spaces-and-abstention-examples. **Scope and interfaces:** `def-pool-based-realizable-active-learning`, `alg-finite-pool-version-space-query`, `thm-active-version-space-invariant-and-query-bound`, `thm-disagreement-abstention-correctness`, `rem-active-label-complexity-and-noisy-oracles`. Fix a nonempty finite classifier list H, finite ordered unlabeled pool U, deterministic unknown target h* in H and exact label oracle. The learner starts V=H, scans the smallest unprocessed pool index, predicts its unanimous V label without querying, otherwise queries its oracle label and deletes every disagreeing hypothesis. Stop after all pool points or a supplied query budget; on budget exhaustion return current V and mark unresolved points as abstentions. The output is a version space, labeled pool entries and query log, not a distribution-free population guarantee. It retains h*, never becomes empty, and every unanimous prediction is correct. At most min(|U|,|H|−1) queried disagreements occur because each query removes at least one hypothesis. No logarithmic bound in |H| follows unless every queried label branch is known to shrink the current V by a fixed factor; e.g. branch sizes <=rho|V| for 0<rho<1 gives at most log(|H|)/log(1/rho) disagreement queries.

**Argument:** Induct on oracle updates: realizability retains h*, so a unanimous prediction equals h*. On a disagreement both label sets are nonempty and the wrong set is removed, proving strict cardinality decrease. Iterate the optional rho bound separately, stopping when |V|=1. For a fresh point, predicting only where V is unanimous gives zero error on answered points under the exact target assumption; its abstention probability is P_X(DIS(V)), not a bound automatically controlled by |V|. Without realizability/noiseless labels the invariant fails and a robust learner needs a new model and concentration argument. **Prerequisites:** ML-01–03 and finite sets; no additional unproved disagreement-coefficient theorem. **Examples:** thresholds with binary-search query selection; a finite star class with only one hypothesis eliminated per queried positive target response; empty-pool and one-hypothesis boundaries. **Sources:** inherited finite-class PAC framework; all finite algorithm properties are justified by the complete arguments here. Research connections include selective prediction and adaptive acquisition, with no unaudited optimal active-learning rate claim.

### ML-93 — Semi-supervised graph propagation and pseudo-labeling limits

**Pages:** semi-supervised-graph-propagation-and-pseudolabeling-limits / semi-supervised-graph-propagation-and-pseudolabeling-limits-examples. **Scope and interfaces:** `def-graph-harmonic-label-extension`, `alg-graph-harmonic-label-propagation`, `thm-graph-harmonic-extension-uniqueness-and-maximum-principle`, `alg-finite-round-confidence-pseudolabeling`, `thm-unlabeled-law-alone-does-not-identify-labels`, `rem-semisupervised-cluster-assumptions`. Let W be a finite symmetric nonnegative weight matrix, L=D−W, nonempty labeled subset B, unlabeled subset U and prescribed real labels y_B. Require every connected component containing an unlabeled vertex to meet B; an all-unlabeled component returns `unanchored-component` instead of a singular solve. Minimize E(f)=one-half f^T L f with f_B=y_B. The unique unlabeled solution is f_U=−L_UU^{-1}L_UB y_B. The algorithm validates symmetry/nonnegativity and component anchors, forms the principal linear system, calls an SPD solver with finite budget/residual and returns values/status. Multiclass one-hot labels can be propagated one coordinate at a time; each predicted row lies in the probability simplex under the same exact anchored solve. A residual certificate is numerical; it does not certify a true class label.

**Argument:** For a vector v zero on B, v^T L v=one-half sum_ij w_ij(v_i−v_j)^2. It vanishes only if v is constant on every component; an anchor forces each relevant constant to zero. Thus L_UU is SPD, giving existence/uniqueness and the normal equations. Each unlabeled value equals a nonnegative weighted average of its neighbors. A strict interior maximum above the boundary maximum propagates to every connected neighbor, eventually contradicting an anchor; the minimum is analogous. Apply this to each one-hot coordinate and to their sum, whose unique harmonic boundary data are one, to obtain nonnegative coordinates summing to one. Pseudo-labeling is a separate heuristic: for T finite rounds retrain with a stated learner on true labels plus selected high-confidence predictions, specify threshold, no-relabel rule/ties, weights and stop when no points are added. Finite termination follows from the supplied budget/no-relabel rule, not from improved test risk. Without a structural link between P_X and P_(Y|X), two laws can have identical P_X, identical labels on every observed labeled input and opposite deterministic labels at an unobserved input of positive P_X mass. Any same output predictor has error at least half that unseen mass under one of the two laws. This elementary two-point experiment proves nonidentification; it does not refute gains under explicit cluster/smoothness/low-density-separation assumptions.

**Prerequisites:** ML-20 weighted-Laplacian identity, published SPD solves, ML-01 risk. **Examples:** path with endpoint labels; unanchored component; confident wrong pseudo-label locked in by the no-relabel rule; indistinguishable unlabeled laws. **Sources:** the weighted graph and finite decision-theoretic suppliers already specified in ML-20/MSF-03; harmonic-extension uniqueness, maximum principle and nonidentification have complete supporting arguments above. No continuum graph-limit, semi-supervised minimax improvement or consistency theorem is imported without a separate sampling/scaling/prerequisite design.
