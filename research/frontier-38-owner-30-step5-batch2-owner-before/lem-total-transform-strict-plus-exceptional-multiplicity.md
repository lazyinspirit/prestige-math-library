## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $S$ be a regular surface over a field $k$ and let $C\subseteq S$ be a reduced curve (an effective Cartier divisor) with a closed point $p$ such that $\dim\mathcal O_{S,p}=2$, at which the multiplicity $m=\operatorname{mult}_p(C)$ of a local equation is finite and positive. Let $\pi\colon S'\to S$ be the blowup of the point $p$ with exceptional curve $E$ and let $C'$ be the strict transform of $C$. Then $\pi^*C=C'+mE$ as effective Cartier divisors on $S'$; equivalently, the strict transform is defined by dividing a local equation of the total transform by the $m$-th power of an exceptional equation on each chart, and $C'$ meets $E$ in the $0$-cycle of degree $m$ cut out by the degree-$m$ leading form of a local equation of $C$ at $p$ (its degree over $\kappa(p)$ is $m$; its degree over $k$ is $m[\kappa(p):k]$ when this residue degree is finite).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A regular surface $S$ over $k$, a reduced curve $C\subseteq S$ that is an effective Cartier divisor, a closed point $p\in S$ with $\dim\mathcal O_{S,p}=2$ at which a local equation $f$ of $C$ has finite positive multiplicity, the blowup $\pi\colon S'\to S$ of $p$, the exceptional curve $E$, and the strict transform $C'$ of $C$.

[A1] **Choice.** The Axiom of Choice is assumed as inherited from the blowup and associated-graded constructions used below. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F1] [[def-total-transform-divisor]]: The total transform of an effective Cartier divisor is its effective Cartier pullback, with associated line bundle the pulled-back line bundle.

[F2] [[def-strict-transform-closed-subscheme]]: The strict transform of a closed subscheme is the scheme-theoretic closure of the inverse image minus $E$; when the ideal of $E$ is invertible on a chart, it is the closed subscheme defined by the saturation of the inverse-image ideal by the ideal of $E$.

[F3] [[thm-pullback-center-ideal-invertible]]: The inverse image ideal of the blowup is invertible, and $E=V(\mathcal I\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Bl}})$ is an effective Cartier divisor cut locally by a generator of that ideal.

[F4] [[lem-affine-blowup-algebra-properties]]: For a domain $A$ and $a\ne0$, the affine blowup algebra $A[I/a]$ is a domain with $I\,A[I/a]=a\,A[I/a]$.

[F5] [[thm-associated-graded-ring-of-a-regular-local-ring]]: At the regular local ring $A=\mathcal O_{S,p}$ of dimension two with regular parameters $x,y$, the associated graded ring is $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m}A=\kappa(p)[X,Y]$ with $X,Y$ the initial classes of $x,y$.

[F6] [[lem-cartier-divisor-local-equation-equivalence]]: Two effective Cartier divisors agree where their local equations differ by a unit; effective Cartier data are exactly principal ideals generated by regular sections.

[F7] [[thm-affine-blowup-standard-charts]]: The blowup at $p$ has the two charts $\operatorname{Spec}A[y/x]$ and $\operatorname{Spec}A[x/y]$, glued by inverting the ratio.

[F8] [[cor-exceptional-divisor-smooth-center-normal-bundle]]: At a closed point with two-dimensional regular local ring, the exceptional curve is $\mathbb P^1_{\kappa(p)}$. The quotient chart presentations are established directly in step 1.1.

## Proof

1.1 At $p$ put $A=\mathcal O_{S,p}$, $\mathfrak m=(x,y)$ and $\kappa=\kappa(p)$. The equation $f\in\mathfrak m^m\setminus\mathfrak m^{m+1}$ has nonzero leading form $f_m(X,Y)$ in $\kappa[X,Y]$. Write the finite ideal-power expression $f=\sum_{i+j=m}c_{ij}x^iy^j$ with $c_{ij}\in A$. The chart is $B_x=A[T]/(xT-y)=A[y/x]$: if $xh=(xT-y)q$, reduction modulo $x$ and regularity of $y$ modulo $x$ give $q=xq_1$, and cancellation gives $h=(xT-y)q_1$; hence the incidence quotient has no $x$-power torsion and [F4, F7] identify it with the chart. In this chart, the ideal-power expression gives $f=x^mg$, where $g=\sum c_{ij}T^j$ and $g\bmod x=f_m(1,T)\ne0$. The ring $B_x$ is a domain and $B_x/xB_x=\kappa[T]$, so $x$ is prime and does not divide $g$. [A1, F4, F5, F7, F8]

2.1 If $xh=gq$ in $B_x$, primality of $x$ forces $q=xq_1$, and cancellation gives $h=gq_1$. Therefore $(g):x=(g)$, and iteration gives $(x^mg):x^\infty=(g)$. The strict-transform chart is thus $V(g)$. Since $g$ is a nonzero element of a domain, it is a Cartier equation, and the factorization $f=x^mg$ gives the total-transform identity there. In the second chart $B_y=A[U]/(yU-x)$ the identical argument gives $f=y^mh$, with $h\bmod y=f_m(U,1)\ne0$. On the overlap $y=xT$, so cancellation of $x^m$ gives $g=T^mh$; the equations differ by a unit and glue. Off the exceptional curve the blowup is the identity, as follows by inverting the chart denominators. Hence globally $C'$ is effective Cartier and $\pi^*C=C'+mE$. [F1, F2, F3, F6, F8, step 1.1]

3.1 On $E\cong\mathbb P^1_\kappa$, these equations cut the homogeneous divisor of the nonzero degree-$m$ form $f_m$. To include the entire projective line, set $d=\deg f_m(1,T)\le m$. Its zeros in this affine chart have total degree $d$, since $\kappa[T]/(f_m(1,T))$ has dimension $d$ (zero if $d=0$); this counts local lengths times residue degrees. At the omitted point, $U=0$, the identity $f_m(U,1)=U^mf_m(1,1/U)$ gives order $m-d$. Thus the complete zero-cycle degree over $\kappa$ is $d+(m-d)=m$. When $[\kappa:k]$ is finite, each residue degree over $k$ is that degree times its degree over $\kappa$, giving $m[\kappa:k]$. No splitting or separability assumption is used. [F5, F8, step 2.1] ∎
