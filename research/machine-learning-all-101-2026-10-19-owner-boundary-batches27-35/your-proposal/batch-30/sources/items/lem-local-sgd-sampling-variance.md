## Statement

At a fixed time $t$, suppose the worker points $x_t^1,\ldots,x_t^K$ are
determined by the earlier sampled indices. Define
$$ g_t=\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^K g_t^k(x_t^k),\qquad \bar g_t=\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^K\nabla f(x_t^k), $$
where each $g_t^k$ is the average of $b\ge1$ independent uniform component
gradients from $\{f_1,\ldots,f_n\}$. If, for every worker point $x_t^k$,
$$ \frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n \|\nabla f_j(x_t^k)-\nabla f(x_t^k)\|_2^2\le\sigma^2, $$
then
$$ \mathbb E\|g_t-\bar g_t\|_2^2\le\frac{\sigma^2}{bK}. $$

## Statement references

[Stich, Local SGD Converges Fast and Communicates Little, Lemma 3.2, Remark 2.4 and Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.09767v3)

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** The finite-sum objective, worker iterates, and mutually independent uniform indices in the Statement.

[F1] [[alg-local-sgd]]: each worker averages $b$ independent component gradients at its current point.

[F2] [[def-independent-random-elements]]: independence means independence of the sigma-algebras generated by the random indices.

[F3] [[def-expectation-of-a-nonnegative-or-integrable-random-variable]]: expectation is the integral against the index law; on this finite law it is a finite weighted sum by [[cor-expectation-agrees-with-the-finite-weighted-sum]].

[F4] [[cor-expectation-linearity-monotonicity-and-modulus-bound]]: expectation is linear on integrable variables.

[F5] [[def-euclidean-inner-product]]: the squared norm expands as the inner product of a finite sum with itself.

## Proof

1.1 Fix the earlier indices, so each $x_t^k$ is fixed. For worker $k$ and within-batch draw $q$, put $\epsilon_{kq}=\nabla f_{i_{t,q}^k}(x_t^k)-\nabla f(x_t^k)$. Uniform sampling gives a zero finite mean for every $\epsilon_{kq}$ and $\mathbb E\|\epsilon_{kq}\|_2^2\le\sigma^2$. Distinct draws are independent by [F1]–[F2], so every cross inner product between distinct centered draws has mean zero by [F3]–[F5]. Expanding $g_t-\bar g_t=(bK)^{-1}\sum_{k,q}\epsilon_{kq}$ therefore leaves only $bK$ diagonal terms. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, given, algebra]

2.1 Each diagonal term is at most $\sigma^2$ by the hypothesis, so the expansion in step 1.1 is at most $(bK)^{-2}(bK)\sigma^2=\sigma^2/(bK)$. Averaging over the earlier finite index history proves the unconditional inequality. [step 1.1, given, algebra] ∎
