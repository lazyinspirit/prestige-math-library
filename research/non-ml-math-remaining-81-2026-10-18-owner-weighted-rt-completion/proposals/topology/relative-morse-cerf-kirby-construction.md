## Data and two different meanings of boundary

Let $W$ be a compact oriented smooth 3-manifold, with incoming and outgoing closed surfaces $\Sigma_-$ and $\Sigma_+$, specified collar embeddings, and specified boundary identifications. Components with either or both ends empty are allowed. Let $G$ be a compact embedded ribbon graph, including oriented ribbons and coupon disks, with product ends on these collars. A presentation must remember the outgoing identification as well as the incoming one. A handle description merely up to diffeomorphism relative to the incoming end is insufficient to recover this data.

There are two genuinely different function spaces:

* Adapted functions equal the prescribed noncritical product height on a smaller collar of every boundary component. Their restriction to each end surface is constant. They have no boundary critical events.
* Walker slicing functions restrict to a Morse function on the boundary surface; this restriction has critical points of types $B_i,T_i$. They allow the bottom, top, and vertical portions of the boundary to change. Their boundary restriction cannot be constant on an entire end surface.

Holding the **geometric boundary collar** fixed does not forbid changing the function on it. Holding the **product height function** fixed does forbid these boundary events. Any proof invoking Walker's 19 moves must specify which is fixed; these are not interchangeable assertions.

## Completed local helper H2: collar exclusion for an entire path

**Statement.** Suppose $f_0,f_1$ equal the same product height $h$ on an open neighborhood $C$ of the boundary, and $dh\ne0$ on $C$. Then there is a smooth path joining them, equal to $h$ throughout $C$, and every perturbation supported outside $C$ remains free of critical points on $C$. In particular, none of Walker's boundary births or interior/boundary transitions occurs there.

**Proof.** The affine path $f_s=(1-s)f_0+sf_1$ equals $h$ on $C$ for every $s$. It takes its values in $[0,1]$ if the endpoints do, and has the prescribed endpoint fibers because a convex combination of two strictly interior values is strictly interior. Replace $s$ by a smooth nondecreasing function equal to zero near zero and one near one to make the path stationary near its endpoints. A perturbation supported outside $C$ has zero differential on $C$, so $d_Wf_s=dh\ne0$ there for all parameters. A boundary event requires either degeneration of the boundary restriction in the slicing space or arrival of an interior critical point at the boundary. The first is not an admissible event in this function space (the boundary restrictions are fixed constants), and the second contradicts $d_Wf_s\ne0$. Thus all possible interior critical events are confined to the compact complement of a smaller collar. This proves the exclusion, not the genericity or classification of those interior events. ∎

## Completed local helper H3: actual coordinate models and their event types

The following calculation verifies each model; it does **not** prove that these models exhaust generic families.

**Interior birth.** For $j=0,1,2$, put $Q_j(v,w)$ equal to a diagonal nondegenerate quadratic form of index $j$ and set

$$F_s(u,v,w)=u^3/3-su+Q_j(v,w).$$

The critical equations are $u^2=s$, $v=w=0$. For $s<0$ there are no critical points; for $s>0$ there are exactly two, at $u=\pm\sqrt{s}$. The Hessian is the direct sum of $(2u)$ and the Hessian of $Q_j$, so the indices are $j$ and $j+1$. At $s=0$ the Hessian has one-dimensional kernel, the cubic derivative on that kernel is $2\ne0$, and the mixed derivative $\partial_s\partial_uF=-1\ne0$. The Euclidean negative-gradient equations leave $v=w=0$ invariant and have $\dot u=s-u^2$ there. Between $-\sqrt{s}$ and $\sqrt{s}$ this is strictly positive. The relevant ascending and descending manifolds meet in this unique trajectory, with transverse complementary directions furnished by the positive and negative eigenspaces of $Q_j$. Thus the model is a geometrically cancelling adjacent-index pair. The existing handle-cancellation supplier applies after restricting to an isolating band for this model. This proves the local cancellation; it does not straighten an arbitrary degenerate family into this model.

**Boundary pair births.** In the half-space $z\ge0$, let $j=0,1$, $\eta\in\{1,-1\}$, $\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}$, and put

$$F_s(u,v,z)=u^3/3-su+\eta v^2+\epsilon z.$$

There is no interior critical point because $\partial_zF_s=\epsilon$. On $z=0$ the two critical points appear at $u=\pm\sqrt{s}$ for $s>0$; their boundary indices are $j,j+1$, where $j=0$ for $\eta=1$ and $j=1$ for $\eta=-1$. The inward normal derivative is $\epsilon$, so both are of type B for $\epsilon=1$ and both of type T for $\epsilon=-1$. This verifies Walker's boundary pair models, covering $B_0/B_1$, $B_1/B_2$, $T_0/T_1$, and $T_1/T_2$.

**Interior/boundary passage.** Let $Q_i(x,y)$ have index $i\in\{0,1,2\}$ and put

$$F_s(x,y,z)=Q_i(x,y)+\epsilon(z-s)^2,\qquad z\ge0.$$

The unique boundary critical point is $(0,0,0)$, of boundary index $i$, with inward derivative $-2\epsilon s$. An interior critical point exists exactly when $s>0$, at $(0,0,s)$, of index $i$ when $\epsilon=1$ and $i+1$ when $\epsilon=-1$. Thus $\epsilon=1$ gives $B_i\leftrightarrow T_i+I_i$, while $\epsilon=-1$ gives $T_i\leftrightarrow B_i+I_{i+1}$. These are the six transitions of Walker 7.3(3–4). At $s=0$ the ambient differential vanishes on the boundary; H2 excludes the model in an adapted collar-fixed path. The formulas are smooth on a fixed geometric half-space collar, so moving the *function* while keeping that geometric collar fixed is possible. ∎

## Completed local helper H4: obstruction to using only ordinary Kirby moves in an arbitrary fixed boundary ambient manifold

**Statement.** Let $N$ be a connected compact 3-manifold and $L\subset\operatorname{int}N$ a framed link. Write $J(L)$ for the normal subgroup of $\pi_1(N)$ generated by its component free homotopy classes. Interior isotopies, framed handle slides, and creation/removal of a disjoint $\pm1$ unknot preserve $J(L)$. Therefore a complete fixed-ambient relative calculus cannot in general assert that these are the only required link operations.

**Proof.** Choosing paths from a common basepoint to a component changes its representative only by conjugation. Reversing its orientation replaces it by its inverse, so $J(L)$ is unambiguously defined. Isotopy preserves its conjugacy class. A band slide replacing component $a$ by its band sum with a signed parallel of $b$ replaces a representative $a$ by $a\gamma b^{\pm1}\gamma^{-1}$ for a band-dependent path $\gamma$, up to conjugacy. The new component lies in the old normal subgroup, and conversely $a$ lies in the normal subgroup generated by the new component and $b$. Hence the two normal subgroups agree. A newly added unknot bounding a disk in a ball is nullhomotopic, and so contributes no normal generator. The reverse moves preserve the same subgroup. These arguments are independent of framings.

Roberts' additional operation O3 inserts a framed curve $K$ with a small zero-framed meridian $\mu$ (his reverse circumcision move). The meridian is nullhomotopic in $N$, while $K$ need not be, so this operation can change $J(L)$. His full source proves that its surgery leaves the ambient manifold unchanged relative to its boundary by the corresponding surgery construction; that topological assertion is **not** substituted here for an independently authored local proof. The normal-subgroup invariant alone already proves that a derivation of O3 from O1/O2 is impossible whenever it changes $J$. For example, take the empty link in a solid torus and let $K$ be its essential core. Thus the canonical phrase “boundary/coupon moves forced by the fixed collars” must acquire an explicit operation list, including O3 or a proved equivalent operation if this fixed-ambient presentation is used. Capping into $S^3$ may make O3 derivable by ordinary Kirby moves, but a proof must then track the cap spines and parametrizations through those moves; closed-manifold Kirby completeness does not supply this relative assertion. ∎

## Completed local helper H5: independent critical heights, relative to the boundary

**Statement.** Near a fixed Morse function $f$ on compact $W$, with no boundary critical point and with $q$ interior critical points, the space of functions equal to $f$ on a fixed collar has local product coordinates whose $q$ real coordinates are the labeled critical values. In these coordinates, critical-value coincidence is exactly the union of hyperplanes $a_i=a_j$. This assertion concerns a neighborhood of a Morse function; it does not include births.

**Proof.** Choose mutually disjoint interior coordinate balls about the critical points $p_j$, and smaller balls with closures inside them. On the complement of these smaller balls, $|df|$ has a positive lower bound for any fixed metric. The inverse function theorem applied to the first derivatives in each ball, together with this lower bound and a smaller neighborhood of $f$ in the $C^2$ topology, shows that every nearby $g$ has exactly one critical point $c_j(g)$ near $p_j$, and no others. Each $c_j$ depends continuously (and on finite-dimensional smooth parameter families smoothly) on $g$. In each ball choose a bump equal to one near $p_j$; the map $x\mapsto x+\chi_j(x)(c_j-p_j)$ in that coordinate chart is a diffeomorphism for small $c_j-p_j$, is the identity off the ball, and takes $p_j$ to $c_j$. Its derivative is uniformly close to the identity, and it is a proper local diffeomorphism of the extended coordinate space equal to the identity outside a compact set; its straight-line small-displacement isotopy gives an inverse. Multiplying these disjoint maps gives $\phi_c$, fixed on the boundary collar. Thus $h=g\circ\phi_c$ has critical points exactly $p_j$.

Choose disjoint bumps $\rho_j$ equal to one on neighborhoods of $p_j$, and put $a_j=h(p_j)$ and $r=h-\sum_j a_j\rho_j$. Then $r(p_j)=0$ and $dr(p_j)=0$. Conversely, from nearby $c$, nearby $a$, and nearby $r$ in the fixed affine subspace specified by these value and derivative conditions and by the collar restriction, form $h=r+\sum_j a_j\rho_j$ and $g=h\circ\phi_c^{-1}$. Its critical points are precisely $c_j$ by the same inverse-function and compact-complement argument. These constructions are continuous inverses on sufficiently small open neighborhoods and have smooth dependence on finite-dimensional parameters. Finally $g(c_j)=h(p_j)=a_j$, so critical-value equalities are exactly $a_i=a_j$. This proves the stated local product and explicitly keeps all changes away from the boundary. ∎

The transverse crossing of one such hyperplane reverses the order of exactly two critical values. Avoiding intersections of two independent hyperplanes excludes simultaneous coincidences in this local Morse neighborhood. H5 does not prove that an arbitrary path has finitely many such neighborhoods separated only by H3's birth models, or that crossing these hyperplanes gives disjoint framed attachments; those are separate obligations below.

## What has not been constructed

H1 is a complete finite **handle** existence result. A three-dimensional 1-handle has attaching region $S^0\times D^2$, and a three-dimensional 2-handle has attaching region $S^1\times D^1$ in a surface. An integral Dehn-surgery diagram instead records two-handles in a **four-dimensional surgery trace**, with attaching maps $S^1\times D^2$ in a three-manifold. Simply writing “replace one-handles by dotted circles and cap” does not construct the passage between these dimensions, nor does it prove independence of the fixed cap parametrizations. The current surgery carrier's step 1.2 skips precisely that passage. Its claimed surgery-existence portion therefore has more missing detail than the genuine H1 handle-existence conclusion.

The smallest remaining proof obligations, in order, are:

1. **Constructed in the continuation:** a relative one-parameter finite-jet/multijet transversality argument, parameter-dependent splitting, and supported homotopy to a cubic elementary path. Existing Morse density alone establishes only transversality of one differential section; the continuation supplies the extra fixed finite interpolation family and the relevant strata explicitly. It avoids a smooth preparation theorem by proving an elementary-path homotopy with derivative bounds instead of claiming exact equivalence of every cubic germ.
2. **Constructed for parametrized elementary bands in the continuation:** continuation, independent critical-value interchange, field-choice comparison on one-critical-point bands, and geometric framed birth cancellation with outgoing maps retained. A separately chosen global descending-field path, with attaching handles transported all the way to a single base level, still requires control of broken trajectories and same-index crossings before asserting its usual band-slide description. The elementary-band comparison does not silently assert that additional theorem or suppress its terminal maps.
3. Either a complete relative four-dimensional surgery-trace comparison (including the higher-dimensional Cerf manipulations used to remove unwanted handles), or a complete graph-decorated relative Fenn–Rourke/bridged-link theorem with its exact move set. A 3-dimensional Morse-function comparison by itself is not the Kirby theorem for 4-dimensional traces.
4. If Walker slicing is chosen, the boundary normal-form exhaustiveness is now constructed in `generic-boundary-events-construction.md`; the finite **slicing** reduction giving all 19 moves still needs the actual half-handle and extreme-level identifications. His moves 16–19 also change the chosen slicing extremities. Their interpretation relative to the supplied incoming/outgoing end surfaces must be specified. No blanket claim that these are permitted deformations of an adapted fixed-height function is correct.
5. For either surgery route, show graph lifting into the link complement and completeness with graph/coupon data: isotopies through surgery belts, slides of graph strands over surgery components, behavior of ribbons and coupon disks, and preservation of their product boundary ends. An underlying uncolored relative theorem alone does not prove this decorated statement.
