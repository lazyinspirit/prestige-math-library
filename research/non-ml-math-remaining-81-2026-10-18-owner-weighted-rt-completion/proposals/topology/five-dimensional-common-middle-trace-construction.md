## 1. Local interior trade of a 1-handle in dimension d

Let $d\ge4$. Suppose a relative handle presentation has no 0-handles before a specified 1-handle, and that its two feet lie in the same connected lower stage. Choose an embedded arc in that stage joining the two feet, with its interior away from the feet. Together with the 1-handle core it gives an embedded circle. A small regular neighborhood of the arc together with the handle is the standard 0/1-handlebody $S^1\times D^{d-1}$, with an identified $(d-1)$-ball on its boundary in the lower stage. This is an explicit ball-plus-tube product: thicken the joining arc to a $(d-1)$-disk tube, take the two foot disks as its end disks, and join the second tube supplied by the handle. The resulting circle's normal coordinates are exactly the disk factors of these two tubes; choose their endpoint identifications to match.

Push the circle into the interior of this local piece and perform framed interior surgery, replacing a smaller $S^1\times D^{d-1}$ by $D^2\times S^{d-2}$. The complement of that smaller neighborhood is the product collar $S^1\times S^{d-2}\times I$, so the replaced local piece is $D^2\times S^{d-2}$ with a retained boundary collar and the same external boundary identification. Split $S^{d-2}$ into two hemispheres $D^{d-2}_+\cup D^{d-2}_-$, meeting in $S^{d-3}$. The first product $D^2\times D^{d-2}_+$ is a $d$-ball. The second is a $(d-2)$-handle, with attaching region $D^2\times S^{d-3}$ and outgoing region $S^1\times D^{d-2}_-$. Place the identified lower-stage boundary ball inside $S^1\times\operatorname{int}D^{d-2}_+$, so it lies in the boundary of that first ball. Absorbing the first ball into the lower-stage collar leaves one handle of index $d-2$ in place of the original 1-handle.

This is a **modification of the ambient d-manifold by interior surgery**, not a cancellation or a claim that the d-manifold was unchanged. Its entire boundary is unchanged under the specified boundary identification. Every later handle was attached on that boundary and is carried with that identification. The normal framing for this existence trade is chosen from the displayed product neighborhood. No assertion that every prescribed framing gives the same modified d-manifold is needed. In dimension five this replaces a 1-handle by a 3-handle; in dimension four it replaces a 1-handle by a 2-handle. Reversing a five-dimensional presentation gives the corresponding 4-handle-to-2-handle trade.

## 2. A four-dimensional interior circle surgery is two link handles

This is the local coordinate bridge from an actual four-dimensional surgery to an O3 pair. Let a framed circle lie in a regular three-dimensional level $M\times\{0\}$ inside a four-manifold, with a product neighborhood $M\times[-2,2]$. Choose its rank-three normal coordinates so that its normal ball $D^3$ meets the middle level in an equatorial disk $D^2_{\mathrm{eq}}$, and write the lower/upper hemispherical balls as $D^3_-,D^3_+$. Remove $S^1\times D^3$ and insert $D^2_a\times S^2$, using the supplied rank-three framing. Decompose the inserted sphere as $S^2_-\cup_{S^1_{\mathrm{eq}}}S^2_+$.

The lower product with its lower half-tube removed,

$$\bigl(M\times[-2,0]\bigr)\setminus\bigl(S^1\times D^3_-\bigr),$$

is another product collar with an indented outgoing face. To verify the collar claim, express the removed lower half-ball in the normal coordinates as the region between the original upper face and a rounded inward graph. Move the upper face to that graph by a smooth increasing interval map on each normal line, fixed near the lower end; the graph and its seam are rounded in a smaller collar. These maps match the identity outside the tube. The analogous statement holds for the upper half. The tube complement can therefore be read as a product, followed by the two inserted hemispherical pieces, followed by an absorbable outgoing product.

The first inserted piece $D^2_a\times S^2_-$ is a 4-dimensional 2-handle: its core disk is $D^2_a$, its normal disk is the hemispherical disk $S^2_-$, and its attaching region is $S^1_a\times S^2_-$ on the indented lower face. In the original middle level this is the framed attaching knot $K$ with its induced rank-two framing. Its outgoing surgery region is $D^2_a\times S^1_{\mathrm{eq}}$.

The second piece $D^2_a\times S^2_+$ is also a 2-handle, but **its disk factors are interchanged**: its core disk is $S^2_+$ and its normal disk is $D^2_a$. Its attaching region is $D^2_a\times S^1_{\mathrm{eq}}$, precisely the outgoing region of the first handle. This factor swap is essential; treating both pieces as having the same core factor gives the wrong attaching region.

Its attaching circle is $\{p\}\times S^1_{\mathrm{eq}}$ for $p\in\operatorname{int}D^2_a$. Move $p$ along a radial arc to a point near $S^1_a$, shrinking the normal thickening uniformly before the motion. The corresponding circles and thickenings form an actual compact isotopy in the boundary after the first handle has been attached. Near the seam use the product corner rotation to carry them into the old middle level. The resulting circle is a small meridian of $K$. Its framing is the disk framing: the tangential $S^1_a$ direction is a constant push direction around this meridian, and the other normal direction is the complementary radial direction in $D^2_{\mathrm{eq}}$. Hence there is no turn relative to its meridian disk. The other boundary of the second piece, $S^1_a\times S^2_+$, is exactly where the indented upper product is attached; absorbing that product retains the original upper boundary identification.

Thus the interior circle surgery is represented, relative to the unaffected boundary of the four-manifold, by adding two 2-handles on a framed knot $K$ and a small zero-framed meridian. This is a four-dimensional trace statement; the separately proved local O3 filling lemma identifies the unchanged three-dimensional boundary. The construction uses the explicit disk-factor decompositions, not the unproved assertion that a 3-dimensional handle diagram automatically is a link-surgery diagram.

## 3. Exact normal-framing interface when the circle is moved to a level

Thus §2 applies to every rank-three framing once the circle itself has been isotoped into the level; it does not equate the two parity classes of rank-three framings or drop the integral framing used for $K$.

## 5. Relative Morse handles with a fixed vertical boundary collar

Handles are to attach in the interiors of the horizontal regular faces, away from this vertical collar.

## 6. A relative two-handle surgery presentation from a boundary-marked four-bordism

Assume an actual compact oriented four-bordism $W$ from a connected compact oriented three-manifold $N$ to a connected compact oriented three-manifold $M$ is supplied, with the same boundary $\Sigma$, its vertical collar $\Sigma\times I$, and all boundary identifications fixed. Every successive lower stage remains connected. The trades change $W$ in its interior and preserve its entire parametrized boundary. Reversing back retains only 2-handles. Thus a possibly modified four-bordism $W'$ has exactly the same fully marked boundary and is a two-handle trace from $N$ to $M$.

To see this inductively, suppose the first several handles are already recorded by a framed link $L$ in $N$. The next attaching circle lies in the three-manifold obtained by those surgeries. The corresponding ambient isotopy is supported in that torus and its seam collar; carry the full attaching thickening by its derivative and shrink it uniformly as needed. The new circle is now in the old exterior $N\setminus\nu L$. It is therefore a new framed link component in the original $N$. Isotopy extension in the already attached trace and the retained outgoing boundary comparison identify the resulting total trace with the prescribed successive one. Finite induction records all its handles by one link, with no boundary parametrization dropped.
