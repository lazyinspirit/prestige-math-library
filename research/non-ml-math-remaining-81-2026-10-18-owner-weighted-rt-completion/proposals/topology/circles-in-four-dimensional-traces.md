# Moving framed circles to the base of a four-dimensional two-handle trace

2026-10-18. Local construction for the common-middle-trace step. The full current proofs of `thm-relative-whitney-approximation-for-manifold-valued-maps`, `thm-whitney-approximation-for-manifold-valued-maps`, `thm-seifert-van-kampen`, and `lem-a-normal-summand-of-rank-at-least-two-surjects-on-the-framing-loop-obstruction` were read, in addition to the previously read transversality and compact isotopy-extension suppliers. This proof uses their actual interfaces and supplies its finite-dimensional general-position family explicitly.

## 1. Homotopic circles are isotopic in dimension at least four

Let $Q$ be a smooth manifold of dimension $d\ge4$, and let two smooth embedded circles in $Q$ be freely homotopic through a homotopy whose image lies in a prescribed relatively compact open set $O$. The relative Whitney-approximation supplier, applied after reparametrizing the homotopy to be constant near its parameter endpoints, gives a smooth homotopy $H:[0,1]\times S^1\to O$ with those same stationary endpoint circles. Corners in the compact source cause no issue: first extend the stationary homotopy across its parameter ends, apply the supplied boundaryless relative theorem on an open parameter interval times $S^1$, and then restrict back.

We prove that $H$ can be perturbed relative to those endpoints into a path of embeddings. The complete proof of the manifold-valued Whitney supplier supplies an embedding $j:O\to\mathbb R^N$ and a smooth retraction $R:U\to j(O)$ from an open neighborhood. Along the compact image of $jH$, $dR$ has rank $d$, since $R$ restricts to the identity on $j(O)$. Shrink to a uniform neighborhood of that image on which $dR$ still has rank $d$.

Let $e:S^1\to\mathbb R^2$ be its ordinary round embedding and let $P_\nu$ run over a basis of the polynomials in two variables of degree at most three. Their restrictions prescribe arbitrary value and first derivative at any two distinct circle points simultaneously: the exact interpolation proof of the finite-jet construction applies with $k=2,r=1$, using a square of a separating affine linear function times a linear Taylor polynomial at the other point. Let $\beta(s)$ be zero near $0,1$ and positive elsewhere. For real coefficients $z_{\nu\alpha}$, define

$$H_z(s,t)=j^{-1}R\left(jH(s,t)+\beta(s)\sum_{\nu,\alpha}z_{\nu\alpha}P_\nu(e(t))\,e_\alpha\right),$$

where $e_\alpha$ is the ordinary coordinate basis of $\mathbb R^N$. For small $z$ this is well defined and remains in $O$. Parameter differentiation maps onto the values and first derivatives of the target curve at every one or two distinct points: prescribe the independent ambient value/derivative variations by polynomial interpolation, then apply the surjective $d(j^{-1}R)$. In the derivative variation, the additional term from differentiating $dR$ can be canceled by prescribing the first-derivative variation after the value variation. Thus the relevant jet evaluation is submersive, not just generically hoped to be transverse.

Apply the actual finite-dimensional parametric-transversality theorem to two strata. The zero-velocity stratum in the curve 1-jet bundle has codimension $d$ and the source $(s,t)$ has dimension two, so for a transverse slice it is absent. The diagonal in $Q\times Q$ has codimension $d$ and the same-parameter two-point source $(s,t,t')$, $t\ne t'$, has dimension three, so its inverse image is also absent. Both dimensions are strictly less than $d$. Choose one small parameter outside the union of the two null exceptional sets. On the stationary endpoint neighborhoods smallness retains the embedding property: the nonzero derivative has a uniform local lower bound in finitely many coordinate intervals, and pairs of circle points outside those intervals have a positive image separation by compactness. These are precisely the two requirements for $C^1$ openness of the embedding condition for a compact circle. The resulting homotopy therefore has injective immersive slices everywhere. A continuous injection from the compact circle into a Hausdorff manifold is a homeomorphism onto its image, so every slice is a smooth embedding.

This is a smooth isotopy of circles, stationary at the ends, with compact image in $O$. The actual isotopy-extension theorem gives an ambient isotopy supported in a compact subset of $O$. In particular, when $Q$ is the interior of a manifold with boundary and $O$ avoids its fixed boundary collar, that collar is fixed pointwise. This proves the claimed free-homotopy-to-isotopy statement with its relative support. No four-dimensional Whitney-disk theorem or higher-dimensional s-cobordism theorem was invoked.

The same construction without the parameter $s$ proves that any free homotopy class of loops in a smooth 3-manifold has an embedded representative. Here the double-point source has dimension two and diagonal codimension three, while the zero-velocity source has dimension one and stratum codimension three. Start with a continuous representative, smooth it by the actual approximation supplier, then choose the small finite perturbation. The interpolation and retraction argument is unchanged.

## 2. The base surjects on the fundamental group of a two-handle trace

Let $W$ be obtained from $N\times I$, with $N$ connected, by finitely many four-dimensional 2-handles. The inclusion $N\times\{0\}\to W$ is surjective on fundamental groups. Indeed for one handle use an open thickening of the old stage and an open thickening of the handle, whose overlap retracts onto its attaching $S^1\times D^2$. The handle thickening is contractible and the overlap is connected. The actual Seifert–van Kampen theorem identifies the new group as the quotient of the old group by the normal closure of the attaching-circle class. Thus the old group maps onto the new one. Finite induction proves the assertion; collars change no homotopy classes. Handles of higher index also add no fundamental-group generators, but are not needed for this stated interface.

Consequently any embedded circle in the interior of $W$ is freely homotopic to a loop in the fixed base $N$ (push that base slightly into a collar inside $W$). Choose an embedded representative there by the three-dimensional part of §1. It can also be chosen disjoint from the finite old attaching link: add the finite evaluation condition that the new curve be transverse to each old component, whose codimension is two in $N$ while the curve source has dimension one. The same submersive polynomial family therefore makes the intersection empty. Smooth a free homotopy between the representatives relative to their stationary ends. If its image reaches the external boundary, first push it off the protected boundary strip by an inward collar compression fixing both endpoint loops. More explicitly, if that strip is $r\le a$, choose $\delta>0$ so both loops lie outside $r\le a+2\delta$ and reparametrize $[0,a+2\delta]$ increasingly into $[a+\delta,a+2\delta]$, matching the identity near its upper endpoint; extend by the identity beyond it. This operation changes only the auxiliary homotopy, not the physical bordism or its collar data. Its image is compact and misses the protected strip, and its endpoint loops are unchanged. Apply the four-dimensional part of §1 there. The original circle is therefore ambiently isotopic into the base level, with support disjoint from that protected external collar. This relative conclusion assumes, as in the relative handle application, that the original and chosen endpoint circles already avoid that protected collar; without that hypothesis the same proof gives support away from a sufficiently small boundary strip. This argument is valid even if the group class is nontrivial; nullhomotopy was not assumed.

## 3. Transport the full framing, retaining its parity

Transport the original rank-three normal framing by this ambient isotopy. At the resulting circle in the three-dimensional base the normal bundle splits as its oriented rank-two normal bundle in the base and the level-normal line. An oriented real vector bundle on a circle is trivial: transport a frame along the interval covering the circle, then correct its endpoint holonomy by a path in the orientation-preserving linear group; such a path exists by orthonormalization followed by rotations in coordinate two-planes. Thus choose an initial rank-two normal frame in the base.

The discrepancy between its rank-three extension and the transported prescribed frame is a loop in $GL^+(3,\mathbb R)$, homotopic by polar orthonormalization to one in $SO(3)$. The actual read framing-loop lemma with $N=3,r=2$ proves that this class is the image of a loop in the rank-two block $SO(2)$. Modify the chosen level frame by that loop. This is an integral twist of its rank-two framing and now its rank-three extension is homotopic to the prescribed frame. This realizes both possible rank-three classes; it does not assert that they agree or that all circle surgeries have the same result.

Finally a smooth homotopy of these frames gives an isotopy of sufficiently small tubular product embeddings, with one uniform radius by compactness of the parameter/circle product. In normal coordinates it is just application of the invertible linear frame map to the disk variable; the tubular map remains injective on a common small disk. The compact isotopy-extension supplier then compares the resulting surgeries relative to the ambient boundary. This uses the **full** framed embedding, not just the unframed circle class.

## 4. Consequence for one five-dimensional 2-handle

Attach a five-dimensional 2-handle to a four-dimensional two-handle trace $W$ along a framed embedded circle in its interior. The outgoing four-dimensional level is obtained by the corresponding interior 1-surgery on $W$. Sections 1–3 move its attaching data into the fixed three-dimensional base and represent the prescribed rank-three framing by an integral level framing. The hemispherical disk-factor construction in `five-dimensional-common-middle-trace-construction.md` §2 then represents this surgery by adding the framed $K$ and its small zero-framed meridian as two four-dimensional 2-handles. Thus the new four-dimensional level again has a two-handle presentation relative to the same base. Repeating this finite construction represents any finite sequence of such five-dimensional handles by a finite sequence of O3 insertions.

This consequence requires the actual local disk-factor construction, supplied in the companion proposal; it is not obtained from the topology of the boundary O3 filling alone. It supplies the circle/framing part of the common-middle reduction. It still does not prove that two arbitrary two-handle descriptions of that middle level are related by a complete decorated Kirby movie.
