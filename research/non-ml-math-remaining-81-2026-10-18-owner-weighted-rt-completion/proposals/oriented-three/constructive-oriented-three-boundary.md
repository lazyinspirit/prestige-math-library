# Draft proof: every closed oriented smooth three-manifold bounds

This is an isolated proof proposal, not a published library item or a readiness assertion. Assume AC. Choices made below are auxiliary; no canonical filling is asserted.

**Claim.** For every compact closed oriented smooth three-manifold X there exist a compact oriented smooth four-manifold W and an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism j:∂W→X. X may be disconnected or empty. A specified finite collection of collared submanifolds, parametrizations and embedded decorations in X is retained by pulling it back through j. Consequently Ω³_SO=0, integrally, with a single copy of X.

The proof uses smooth Morse/handle existence, smooth isotopy extension, elementary surface cutting, planar Poincare–Bendixson and turning tangent. It does not use integral characteristic-number detection, a stable-stem computation, or the assertion of the surgery-presentation theorem as a black box. The actual additional arguments are supplied below.

## 1. Smooth disk isotopy, including the smoothness issue

An orientation-preserving smooth disk diffeomorphism equal to the identity on a boundary collar is smoothly isotopic to the identity on a (possibly smaller) boundary collar. Here is the smooth square proof of Li–Watts, §3, with its planar no-trapping argument and the required reparametrization spelled out. Rounding the square corners outside the support gives the disk version.

Put Q=[0,1]². Let F be the diffeomorphisms equal to identity near ∂Q. Enlarge F to E consisting of diffeomorphisms equal to identity near the bottom, left and top edges, and of the form (x,g(y)) near the right edge. For f∈E the nonzero vector field v=f_*e₁ is e₁ near all four edges. It has a smooth complex logarithm ℓ:Q→C, zero near the boundary: lift through exp:C→C× starting with ℓ(0,0)=0. Local inverse branches show smoothness; the boundary collar is connected, hence its lift is zero. Set v_t=exp((1−t)ℓ). Thus v_0=v, v_1=e₁, and all v_t are nonzero and equal e₁ on a common boundary collar.

Every trajectory starting at the left edge exits the right edge in finite positive time, and every interior trajectory meets the left edge in finite negative time. To see this, extend v_t as e₁ beyond Q. A trajectory cannot cross the top or bottom collar and can exit only at the right in forward time. If it never exits, its forward orbit has compact closure in a slightly larger rectangle, with no equilibrium. The local planar Poincare–Bendixson theorem then supplies a periodic orbit. A periodic orbit of a nonzero plane field is a smooth simple closed curve. Its tangent has winding ±1 by turning tangent, whereas the restriction of the globally defined logarithm of v_t has winding zero, a contradiction. The negative-time argument is identical. This also rules out any missing region of Q in the left-to-right trajectory parametrization.

Let Γ_t(s,y) be the flow of v_t from (0,y), and let r_t(y)>0 be its first right-edge exit time. Existence, uniqueness and smooth dependence of ODE solutions apply; the implicit function theorem gives smooth r since ∂_sΓ_t¹=1 at exit. Compactness of [0,1]×[0,1] gives positive lower and finite upper bounds m≤r_t(y)≤M. Choose a smooth function ρ≥0 on [0,1], zero on endpoint collars, positive in between. Make the total length of those collars less than m. The function a↦∫₀¹exp(aρ(u))du is smooth strictly increasing with image (c,∞), where c<m is the measure of {ρ=0}. Therefore there is a unique smooth a(r) for r∈[m,M] with this integral r. Put χ(x,r)=∫₀ˣexp(a(r)ρ(u))du. Then χ(0,r)=0, χ(1,r)=r, ∂_xχ>0, ∂_xχ=1 near either endpoint, and χ(x,1)=x.

The maps E_t(f)(x,y)=Γ_t(χ(x,r_t(y)),y) are smooth diffeomorphisms of Q in E: inverse coordinates are obtained by following a trajectory back to the left edge and inverting χ. Near the horizontal edges they are identity; near the right edge they have form (x,g_t(y)). At t=0 one has Γ_0(s,y)=f(s,y), r_0=1, so E_0(f)=f. At t=1 the result is identity.

This isotopy need not fix the right edge. Correct it as follows. For h∈E write h(x,y)=(x,g_h(y)) near that edge. Choose β(x)=1 near x=0 and 0 near x=1, and define G_s(g)(y)=(1−s)g(y)+sy. All these interval maps are increasing diffeomorphisms. Set Ψ_h(x,y)=(x,G_{β(x)}(g_h^{-1})(y)). It is identity near the left, top and bottom; near the right it is (x,g_h^{-1}(y)). Thus p(h)=Ψ_h∘h lies in F, and p|F=id. For f∈F, p(E_t(f)) is the promised smooth isotopy in F. Compactness in t gives a common collar after shrinking. A time reparametrization constant near both endpoints permits concatenation with other smooth isotopies.

Only the no-equilibrium case of the existing `lem-local-generalized-poincare-bendixson-for-a-precompact-planar-orbit` is used here. The periodic-orbit contradiction uses the existing `thm-hopf-turning-tangent-theorem`, whose full proof was read. The usual topological Alexander shrinking formula is deliberately not used as a smooth isotopy.

## 2. Surface diffeomorphisms are finite words of twists

We need generation by **all** Dehn twists, not a particular finite generating set. Boundary circles are individually fixed on collars. Curves and isotopies are smooth. A twist in a collar A=S¹×[−1,1] is (θ,u)↦(θ+2πb(u),u), with b constant 0 and 1 near its two ends. The inverse uses −b.

We record the elementary cutting facts used in the argument. A compact connected orientable surface has a disk-and-bands model: cut a finite handle decomposition along the cores of its one-handles, and cancel a maximal tree of zero-handles and joining bands. Sliding the ends of bands in oriented boundary collars puts intersecting pairs in the usual one-holed-torus blocks; remove one such block at a time. If there is no such pair, the remaining surface is a disk with disjoint disks removed. These are smooth operations: a band slide is supported in a disk containing its two adjacent attaching intervals, moves them along an embedded arc by a time-dependent vector field, and preserves all other boundary collars. This proves the smooth cutting facts needed here, without converting a general topological surface homeomorphism to a diffeomorphism. In particular a nonseparating curve reduces the genus by one on cutting, and cutting two disjoint nonseparating curves has either connected complement or two components each incident to both curves. This last assertion also follows directly: a component incident only to one of them would make that curve separating.

**Curve connectivity.** On a positive-genus surface any two nonseparating curve classes a,b can be joined by a finite sequence of nonseparating curves with successive geometric intersection zero or one. Here is a direct intersection-reduction proof, including the boundary cases. Put α,β in transverse minimal position. If N=|α∩β|≥2, take a subarc δ of β between consecutive intersections, with interior disjoint from α. If its endpoints are on opposite banks of α, an arc of α between the endpoints joined to δ, with the two corners rounded and one endpoint shifted across α, gives a simple curve γ intersecting α once and β at most N−1 times. Outside the small rectangle at δ it follows a parallel copy of a subarc of α. The single intersection with α makes γ nonseparating. If the endpoints are on the same bank, the boundary surgery of α along a thin band about δ gives two disjoint-from-α curves γ₁,γ₂. With suitable orientations [α]=[γ₁]+[γ₂] in H₁ of the surface with boundary. Since α is nonseparating, its class in the homology of the capped surface is nonzero; at least one γ_i has nonzero capped class and hence is nonseparating. Each γ_i uses one of the two complementary α-arcs and two parallel copies of δ; push these copies away from β and round the endpoints, so |γ_i∩β|≤N−2. This proves the reduction by induction on N; N=0 or 1 is the base. The surgeries avoid the boundary collars. Minimal position can be obtained by repeatedly removing innermost bigons; each removal lowers the finite transverse intersection count by two, using a smooth disk-supported isotopy.

If a,b are disjoint nonisotopic nonseparating curves, there is a curve d crossing each exactly once. Cut along both. If the complement is connected, join one bank of a to one bank of b and join the other banks by disjoint interior arcs; these arcs can be chosen disjoint by first cutting along the first arc (which joins distinct boundary components and leaves the surface connected). If the complement has two components, join the a-bank to the b-bank in each. Regluing and rounding gives d. It is nonseparating because it crosses a once. Thus one may replace every zero-intersection step by two one-intersection steps. The intersection-one graph is connected, including genus one with arbitrarily many boundary components.

For curves a,b with i(a,b)=1, their regular neighborhood is a one-holed torus. The product T_aT_bT_a interchanges their unoriented classes. This can be checked by cutting that torus to a square and following the two coordinate arcs: T_a sends the transverse arc to its concatenation with a, T_b performs the other shear, and the three shears exchange the two primitive coordinate directions, one with reversed orientation. The same square calculation gives (T_aT_bT_a)² acting as −I on these two curves, so it reverses the orientation of a. The representatives are supported inside the one-holed torus and fix its boundary collar. Consequently a word in twists carries any nonseparating curve to any other, and after doing so may correct reversal of its orientation.

**Planar base.** Every diffeomorphism of a disk with n holes, fixed near all boundary circles, is a twist word modulo smooth isotopy. Induct on n. For n=0 use §1. Cap one inner hole by a parametrized disk D. By induction the extended map is a twist word on the capped surface; choose the curves of that word to miss D by a small isotopy, and subtract the word. The resulting extended diffeomorphism has a smooth isotopy to identity on the capped surface. During it, the center and an oriented tangent frame of D trace a loop in the oriented frame bundle. The kernel is generated by moving D along loops of its center and rotating its frame. This assertion can be obtained directly by lifting paths of embedded disks: for a small disk, coordinate translations and positive linear maps give local sections; subdivide a path into finitely many such charts and integrate its velocity with a cutoff. Shrinking disks before making these moves shows that a fixed-center, fixed-frame disk path contributes no further component: after rescaling about its center it deforms to its derivative, and the positive linear maps fixing a given first vector form a contractible half-plane. Here are the compact-family and homotopy-lifting details behind this kernel assertion. For a smooth family e_z of disk embeddings with parameter z in a compact interval or square, fix a smooth Riemannian metric on the surface. The geodesic ODE and the inverse function theorem give an exponential coordinate disk at each center p_z=e_z(0); compactness gives a single radius valid at all these centers. Restrict each embedding to a uniformly small concentric disk, e_z(rw), so all such images are in those exponential disks. Put E_z(w)=exp_{p_z}^{−1}(e_z(w)) for w in this uniformly small source disk. For 0<q≤1 the embeddings

exp_{p_z}(E_z(rqw)/q)

extend smoothly at q=0 to exp_{p_z}(r(de_z)_0w). Indeed E_z(rqw)/q=r∫₀¹(dE_z)_{srqw}w ds. For uniformly small r, all these tangent vectors remain inside the exponential disk. For q>0 the displayed map is a composition of a source restriction, the diffeomorphism exp_{p_z}^{−1}, a positive dilation and exp_{p_z}; at q=0 it is an exponential linear disk, so every map is an embedding. The formula uses the fixed metric and hence agrees on parameter-chart overlaps, including for a two-parameter family. Thus the small-disk family is deformed to its center and positive linear frame. Positive scale and the remaining positive triangular part of an oriented frame are contractible, leaving only a unit tangent direction and its integer winding. Shrinking changes the base disk along a path of embeddings; transport along that path only conjugates endpoint monodromies. Conjugation carries a twist to the twist about the transported curve, and hence preserves the subgroup generated by all twists.

For completeness, the required two-parameter lifting does not assert a new disk-diffeomorphism contraction. If e_{s,t} is a smooth homotopy of disk paths, extend the embeddings slightly beyond the source disk locally in (s,t), and define the spatial velocity on their compact track by V_{s,t}(e_{s,t}(w))=∂_s e_{s,t}(w). The graph is embedded because its first two coordinates are (s,t). Smooth local extensions of V exist in the corresponding product charts. A finite cover and a partition of unity on a neighborhood of this compact graph patch them: on the graph all local extensions have the same value, so the patched field has exactly that value. Multiply by a cutoff avoiding the protected surface collars and integrate in s, with t as a parameter. Smooth ODE dependence gives a smooth two-parameter family of ambient diffeomorphisms carrying e_{0,t} to e_{s,t}. If e_{s,t} is constant in s on a protected parameter edge, its velocity is zero there; use the constant extension on a parameter collar of that edge, so the lift is identity there. The same construction in the t direction lifts paths. For a based loop homotopy constant at t=0,1, the t=1 endpoint diffeomorphisms of its lifts therefore vary by a smooth isotopy fixing the disk pointwise. If two lifts of the same path are used, their quotient fixes the disk at every time, so their endpoints also differ by such an isotopy. This proves exactly the monodromy-independence and homotopy-invariance needed for the kernel calculation. It uses velocity extension and ODEs, not connectivity or contractibility of a disk diffeomorphism group.

A loop of frames projects to a word of simple loops of the center (the planar surface retracts onto a finite graph), and the remaining loop is an integer rotation of the disk.

A center push along a simple loop is a product of two twists on the two boundary curves of its annular neighborhood: in coordinates (θ,u)∈S¹×[0,2], move θ by 2πtu for u≤1 and by 2πt(2−u) for u≥1, smoothed at u=1. At t=1 this is T_left T_right^{-1}. A full frame rotation is a twist around ∂D. These formulas take place away from the other boundary collars and descend to the disk complement. Hence the kernel, and then the original map, is a twist word. Path lifting only needs surjectivity onto the kernel; no injectivity assertion about a Birman sequence, nor contractibility of the whole diffeomorphism group, is being assumed.

**Genus induction.** Let S have positive genus, with any number of boundary circles, and choose a nonseparating oriented curve a. For f∈Diff⁺(S) fixed on boundary collars, the curve-connectivity argument and the one-holed-torus calculation give a twist word h such that hf preserves a and its orientation. First an ambient isotopy carries its image curve exactly to a. Correct the restriction to a by an isotopy in the annular collar: an orientation-preserving circle diffeomorphism has an increasing lift F with F(θ+2π)=F(θ)+2π, and the convex interpolation of F with θ is increasing. Having fixed a pointwise, write the resulting map as k in oriented collar coordinates (θ,u), with a={u=0}. Its derivative there is A(θ)=[[1,a(θ)],[0,b(θ)]], with b(θ)>0. Both the tangential normal shear a and the normal scale b must be removed. Choose the smooth path J_t(θ)=[[1,−t a(θ)/b(θ)],[0,1−t+t/b(θ)]], so J_0=I and J_1=A^{-1}, all with positive normal diagonal. Write J'_tJ_t^{-1}=[[0,c_t],[0,d_t]]. The time-dependent field X_t(θ,u)=ρ(u)(c_t(θ)u,d_t(θ)u), where ρ is one near zero and zero near the collar ends, has a global supported smooth flow ψ_t fixing a pointwise. The variational ODE at u=0 gives dψ_t=J_t. Consequently K=k∘ψ_1 fixes a and has its *whole first jet* equal to identity there.

Here is the remaining collar removal with an actual supported diffeomorphism, rather than a germ assertion. In the collar choose the unique small angular lift of K−id. Taylor expansion uniformly in θ gives K−id=O(u²) and dK−I=O(|u|). Let ρ_δ(u) be one for |u|≤δ and zero for |u|≥2δ, with |ρ'_δ|≤C/δ. For small δ the maps L_s(θ,u)=(θ,u)+sρ_δ(u)(K(θ,u)−(θ,u)) have derivative I+O(δ), including the cutoff derivative term. They equal identity near the collar ends, hence extend by identity to S. For small δ, lift the annulus to R² and extend the displacement by zero in the normal direction. Its derivative has norm less than 1/2. The estimate |L_s(x)−L_s(y)|≥|x−y|/2 gives injectivity; the contraction x↦z−(L_s(x)−x) gives a preimage of every target z. Smooth local inverses and angular-period equivariance give the annulus diffeomorphism, extended by identity on the surface. At s=1 it equals K on |u|≤δ. The isotopy L_s^{-1}∘K therefore ends in a map equal to identity on a smaller annular collar of a. All modifications avoid the original boundary collars. Cutting there gives a diffeomorphism of genus one less with two additional fixed boundary collars. Apply induction, then extend each resulting twist back by identity. This proves the assertion. In the closed genus-zero case fix a disk by moving its image and frame, as in the disk-motion construction above; what remains is the disk case. This also proves smooth isotopy triviality for orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms of S², without using a topological Alexander trick.

All isotopy extensions in this section can be made by the usual explicit velocity construction: extend ∂_t e_t∘e_t^{-1} from a compact track to a neighborhood, multiply by a cutoff avoiding protected collars, and integrate. For curve motions the full existing `lem-a-smooth-isotopy-of-compact-embedded-submanifolds-extends-to-an-ambient-isotopy` supplies exactly this construction (its full proof was read). For moving disks, extend each disk embedding slightly beyond its boundary before applying the same local velocity construction, as in the read disk-arc supplier; compactness and the inverse function theorem give a common slightly enlarged domain along a fixed compact parameter path. Thus the boundaryless-source hypothesis of the named lemma is not silently applied to a disk. No extension over a hypothetical filling is used.

## 3. A smooth Heegaard splitting from the actual Morse suppliers

For connected X, use `thm-adapted-excellent-morse-functions-exist-on-compact-cobordisms` with empty faces, followed by `thm-self-indexing-morse-function-existence` and `lem-handles-of-equal-index-can-be-attached-on-one-level`. These statements apply to arbitrary compact triads in dimension three; no h-cobordism assumption is imported from the title of a downstream proposition. The full authored proofs and hypotheses of these three suppliers were read.

Choose a regular level between indices one and two. Its lower sublevel H is a finite union of zero-handles joined by one-handles. It is connected: handles of index two and three have connected attaching regions and cannot join components, whereas X is connected. Contract a spanning tree in the finite zero/one-handle graph. Each tree edge joins two balls through a tube; straightening its attaching disks in the ball collars identifies their union smoothly with one ball. Inductively H is a ball with g one-handles, a genus-g handlebody. Reading the upper sublevel upside down gives another connected handlebody H′, because the dual indices are zero and one. Their common boundary is the regular surface, so their genera agree (its Euler characteristic is 2−2g). Thus X=H∪_f(−H_g) after choosing oriented diffeomorphisms of both handlebodies with a fixed standard H_g. These choices are disk-and-tube identifications from the constructions above, not applications of three-dimensional classification. The gluing map f:∂H_g→∂H_g is orientation preserving in this convention. By §2 it is smoothly isotopic to a finite word of Dehn twists.

## 4. A twist of the gluing is an oriented two-handle trace

Suppose Y=H∪_q(−H_g), and c is a curve in the splitting surface. Changing q to T_c^{±1}q is obtainable by integral surgery on the corresponding curve pushed into a product collar of the splitting surface, with framing differing by one from the surface framing. Here is the local boundary check, which is all that is needed from surgery theory. Give the pushed-off curve a solid-torus neighborhood with boundary basis (μ,λ), where λ is the surface parallel and μ bounds a normal disk. Cut along the splitting annulus about c and reglue by (θ,u)↦(θ±2πb(u),u). On the two sides of the cut the complementary product coordinates agree away from the annulus. Following a normal meridian through the cut adds one signed circuit of the core longitude, so the meridian of the replacing solid torus is μ±λ. Conversely gluing a solid torus with this meridian removes that shear, since the annular coordinate change is identity at both outer boundaries. This is the meridian relation for coefficient ±1 relative to λ. The precise assignment of the two signs depends on which side carries the oriented collar; both signs occur, and the construction chooses the one giving the specified twist.

Attach a smooth four-dimensional two-handle D²×D² to Y×[0,1] along the pushed-off c with that integer framing. Its outgoing boundary removes S¹×int(D²) and adds D²×S¹, exactly the described solid-torus replacement. Thus its oriented boundary is (−Y)⊔Y′, with Y′ the twisted gluing. This follows directly from the two pieces of ∂(D²×D²); all maps are smooth product maps on the seams and all corners can be rounded away from the incoming boundary. This is existence of a trace, not any claim about completeness of Kirby moves.

The double D(H_g)=H_g∪_id(−H_g) bounds H_g×[0,1], with corners rounded. It is also the boundary of a four-dimensional zero-handle with g one-handles, but this identification is unnecessary. Starting with this product filling and attaching the finitely many two-handle traces for a twist word for f gives a compact oriented smooth W whose boundary is H_g∪_f(−H_g), up to the smooth isotopy of the gluing map. An isotopy of gluing maps gives a diffeomorphism of glued manifolds: use (x,s)↦(f_{β(s)}f_0^{-1}(x),s) in a collar, with β constant near its ends, and identity outside. Composing with the chosen Heegaard identification gives j:∂W→X. For genus zero the starting filling H_0×I is a rounded four-ball; §2 already handled the sphere gluing map.

For disconnected compact X there are finitely many components: components are open in a manifold, hence compactness makes this open cover finite. Perform the construction on each, using its given orientation, and take the finite disjoint union of fillings. For X empty take W empty. Reversing a component orientation reverses its filling orientation, so no convention on a preferred component orientation is required.

## 5. Fixed caps and relative markings

Let M be any compact oriented smooth three-manifold with boundary, with a specified finite system of standard boundary caps C and specified smooth collared gluing maps making X=M∪_∂C a closed oriented smooth three-manifold. Caps may be disconnected. Apply the claim to this actual X, not to an unparametrized diffeomorphism type. The resulting j identifies the two marked submanifolds j^{-1}(M),j^{-1}(C) of ∂W and their common collars with the original ones. In particular the original cap parametrizations, boundary charts and any prescribed decorations in M or C are unchanged by transport. Regarding the seam as a corner yields a four-dimensional manifold with the two specified boundary faces. A product collar of ∂W, pulled back from j, identifies their common face with precisely the prescribed surface collar. No extension of a boundary diffeomorphism across C or across W is asserted or required.

This is the exact fixed-cap existence interface: a bounding W of the *marked closed cap*, with the markings as part of its boundary identification. Additional demands such as presenting W with only two-handles relative to a chosen cap require the separate relative handle-trading proof, and are not silently included here.

## 6. Logical and choice ledger

The integral conclusion follows from an actual single-copy filling, not from rationalization. The local surface proofs use smooth compact collars, finite transverse perturbations, finite graph/spanning-tree choices and ODE flows. The Morse suppliers spend AC_ω. We declare AC throughout so all smooth auxiliary and classifying data are covered, but select only finitely many objects for any individual compact input. A simultaneous filling assignment for a set of objects may be chosen by AC from the nonempty sets of fillings; no canonical or natural choice is claimed. No four-dimensional Poincare theorem, three-dimensional Poincare theorem, integral Wall detection theorem, or unproved surgery-generation assertion occurs.
