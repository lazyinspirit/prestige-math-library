## A. An elementary finite-generation point

Every integral homology group of K(Z,n), n≥2, is finitely generated. Here is a proof sufficient to avoid an implicit finite-type assumption in the coefficient arguments below. Start with K(Z,2)=CP∞, whose cellular homology has this property. Induct on n using the path fibration K(Z,n−1)→P→K(Z,n). Within that induction induct on k, the base homology degree. In the integral homological Serre spectral sequence, E²_{k,0}=H_k(K(Z,n)) has no incoming differential. Each outgoing target has bidegree (k−r,r−1), with base degree smaller than k. Its E² group is finitely generated by the coefficient UCT, the induction on k, and the known finite generation of the fiber homology. It follows that each image of an outgoing differential from the bottom term is finitely generated. Only finitely many differentials can leave that term. Since the positive-degree homology of P is zero, its surviving subgroup is zero. The successive kernels therefore express E²_{k,0} as a finite extension of finitely generated images. This proves the induction. For n=2 the fiber circle can alternatively be used, but CP∞ already supplies that base case. All bases in the argument are simply connected, so the local coefficient systems are constant.

We use the usual cohomological UCT in the following form: for a space with finitely generated homology, the torsion subgroup of H^{j+1}(−;Z) is Ext(H_j(−;Z),Z), and has the same primary cyclic summands as the torsion of H_j. Rational cohomology determines its rank. Thus vanishing of all the primary torsion and of the rational part proves the integral group is zero; no divisible or infinite torsion summand is being silently discarded.

## B. The needed integral Eilenberg–Mac Lane homology vanishing

For n≥8,

\[
\tag{B1}H_{n+5}(K(\mathbb Z,n);\mathbb Z)=0.
\]

### B.1 The prime two

Hatcher's Theorem 1.37(a), with its full proof by transgression and Borel comparison at pp.52–58, gives in the strict range n+j<2n the basis of H^{n+j}(K(Z,n);F₂) consisting of admissible Sq-monomials of degree j which have no Sq¹ factor. At j=6 this basis is

\[
Sq^6\bar\iota_n,\qquad Sq^4Sq^2\bar\iota_n.
\]

The mod-two Bockstein is Sq¹. The Adem relations give

\[
Sq^1Sq^6\bar\iota_n=Sq^7\bar\iota_n,
\qquad
Sq^1Sq^4Sq^2\bar\iota_n=Sq^5Sq^2\bar\iota_n.
\]

The two right sides are distinct admissible basis classes. Their degree is n+7<2n for n≥8, so Sq¹ is injective in degree n+6. Write β_Z for the integral Bockstein and ρ for mod-two reduction. Since Sq¹=ρβ_Z and β_Zρ=0, every reduced integral class in degree n+6 lies in the kernel of this injective Sq¹, hence is zero. By the coefficient exact sequence, the reduction map embeds H^{n+6}(−;Z)/2 into mod-two cohomology. Consequently this quotient is zero. Finite generation and the absence of a rational class in degree n+6 (which is below 2n and above n) imply that H^{n+6}(−;Z) has no two-primary torsion.

### B.2 Primes at least five

Hatcher's Lemma 1.29, whose complete path-fibration proof is on pp.41–42, states that the first p-primary torsion in the integral cohomology of K(Z,n), n≥3, occurs in degree n+2p−1. For p≥5 this exceeds n+6. Thus there is no such torsion in degree n+6. The lemma is used only in its stated range; the next subsection supplies the missing degree at p=3.

### B.3 The prime three: the additional degree actually required

Put R=Z_(3), write K_m=K(Z,m), and use the multiplicative cohomological Serre spectral sequence for K_{m−1}→P→K_m. We prove that H^{m+6}(K_m;R) has no torsion for every m≥3, and is zero except when m=6, when it is R generated by the square of the fundamental class. All rational free parts mentioned below are given by the polynomial/exterior calculation of rational K(Z,m) cohomology. Lemma 1.29 gives the first three-torsion in degree m+5 and no torsion below it.

For m=3 the fiber is CP∞ with H*(−;R)=R[a], |a|=2. The fundamental transgression is d₃a=x, |x|=3, and d₃(a^k)=k a^{k−1}x. In particular a⁴ does not survive d₃, since 4 is a unit of R. Any differential hitting the bottom entry (9,0) must have source (9−r,r−1). For r=9 the source a⁴ has already died. For positive source column below 9 the only possibilities, by Lemma 1.29 and rational cohomology, are column 3 and column 8. Column 3 would require fiber degree 5, which is zero, and column 8 would require r=1, which is not a Serre differential. All other source columns are zero. Thus the bottom group H⁹(K₃;R) has no incoming differential and must itself be zero, since P is contractible. This proves the base case. The earlier nontrivial torsion is the R/3 in H⁸, as stated by Lemma 1.29; it does not enter the above argument.

For m=4 the possible incoming sources to (10,0) have first column fiber degree 9, which just vanished, or positive base columns 4,8,9. Those columns require respectively fiber degrees 5,1,0 for differentials r=6,2,1. The first two fiber groups are zero, and r=1 is unavailable. Hence H¹⁰(K₄;R)=0.

For m=5 the first-column source to (11,0) is H¹⁰(K₄;R)=0. The only other possible positive source column is 5 (the first torsion column 10 would again require r=1); column 5 would require fiber degree 5, whereas K₄ has zero cohomology there. Thus H¹¹(K₅;R)=0.

For m=6 the first-column source to (12,0) is H¹¹(K₅;R)=0. The only possible incoming source is (6,5)=R xa, where x and a are the fundamental base and fiber classes. Its d₆ is x². Connectivity leaves this source equal to R through page six. There is a possible incoming d₆ from (0,10): although a²=0 because a has odd degree and 2 is invertible, H¹⁰(K₅;R)=R/3 is the first torsion group of Lemma 1.29. That incoming homomorphism is zero because its source is three-torsion and its target R is torsion-free. No later differential can enter (6,5). Contractibility forces H¹²(K₆;R) to be generated by x². Its rational rank is one, so the map R→H¹² sending 1 to x² is injective as well as surjective. Therefore H¹²(K₆;R)=R, with no torsion.

For m=7 the only possible first-column source to (13,0) is a²∈H¹²(K₆;R). It has already died by d₇(a²)=2ax, a nonzero map between copies of R. The target (7,6) is R on that page: connectivity eliminates earlier differentials and other incoming sources. Every possible positive-column source to (13,0) requires fiber degree at most 5, below the fiber's first positive degree 6. Hence H¹³(K₇;R)=0.

For m≥8 any positive-column source to (m+6,0) has p≥m, hence r≤6 and fiber degree r−1≤5, which is below m−1. The first-column source is H^{m+5}(K_{m−1};R), which is zero by induction. Thus there is no incoming differential to the bottom group, and H^{m+6}(K_m;R)=0. This completes the prime-three calculation. There are no unmentioned extension questions: each assertion about the bottom group is forced by its entire finite incoming differential list and by the zero positive-degree abutment.

### B.4 Return to integral homology

The preceding primary calculations show that H^{n+6}(K_n;Z) has no torsion. The rational K(Z,n) calculation gives H^{n+5}(K_n;Q)=0 for n≥8. By finite generation and the cohomological UCT, the former assertion kills all torsion of H_{n+5}, and the latter kills its free part. This proves (B1).

## C. The integral fourth homology of BSO and its two-torsion detector

For q≥8,

\[
\tag{C1}H_4(BSO(q);\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2,
\]

and the class w₄ pairs nontrivially with its unique nonzero torsion element.

The lifted Schubert cells give finite generation in each degree. The native theorem `thm-bo-bso-cohomology-away-from-two`, whose full authored proof was read, computes cohomology with every odd-prime field and with Q. The mod-two calculation is F₂[w₂,…,w_q]: derive it directly from the mod-two Gysin sequence of the determinant-line sphere bundle BSO(q)→BO(q). Multiplication by w₁ in H*(BO(q);F₂)=F₂[w₁,…,w_q] is injective, so the pullback onto BSO cohomology is surjective with kernel (w₁).

For clarity the only Sq¹ formulas needed here can be derived by the real splitting principle. On roots t_j of degree one, Sq¹(t_j)=t_j², and Cartan makes Sq¹ a derivation. For the elementary symmetric function w_k, expansion gives

\[
Sq^1w_k=w_1w_k+(k-1)w_{k+1}.
\]

Indeed in w₁w_k the terms with the extra root already in the k-subset are exactly Sq¹w_k, and each (k+1)-subset occurs k+1 times in the remaining terms; k+1≡k−1 modulo two. On BSO, therefore, Sq¹w₂=w₃ and Sq¹w₄=w₅, both nonzero.

Since BSO(q) is simply connected, H₁=0. The UCT and the displayed low mod-two ring show that H₂ has one two-primary cyclic summand, H₃ has none, and H₄ has one two-primary cyclic summand in addition to its rank-one free part; here are the exact details that exclude extra exponents. Rational and odd-prime cohomology first exclude odd torsion and give ranks H₂=H₃=0 and rank H₄=1. Then dim H²(F₂)=1 supplies one two-primary summand of H₂. The nonzero Sq¹w₂ is the reduction of the integral Bockstein, forcing that summand to have order exactly two. Since dim H³(F₂)=1 is already accounted for by Ext(H₂,F₂), H₃/2=0, hence H₃=0. Now dim H⁴(F₂)=2, with H₃=0 and rank H₄=1, supplies one two-primary cyclic summand of H₄. The nonzero Sq¹w₄ similarly makes its exponent exactly two and makes w₄ evaluate nontrivially on it.

The cyclic-group assertion just used is explicit: for Z/2^a, the integral Bockstein sends its nonzero F₂-valued homomorphism to the element 2^{a−1} in Ext(Z/2^a,Z)=Z/2^a. Reduction of that element is nonzero exactly when a=1. A free summand contributes no integral Bockstein. Thus nonzero reduced Bockstein detects precisely the order-two summand and the nonzero evaluation on that summand. This proves (C1) and its detector claim.

## D. The Thom-fiber argument

Let T_q be the Thom space of the universal oriented q-plane bundle. Take q≥8. The integral Thom class represents a based map

\[
f:T_q\longrightarrow K(\mathbb Z,q).
\]

It induces an isomorphism on π_q=Z: both first Hurewicz groups identify the bottom fiber sphere with the normalized Thom generator. The stable Pontryagin–Thom correspondence, together with Ω₁SO=Ω₂SO=Ω₃SO=0, gives

\[
\pi_{q+i}(T_q)=0\quad(1\leq i\leq3),
\qquad \pi_{q+4}(T_q)=\Omega^{SO}_4.
\]

Here is the finite-rank stability check, rather than an assumption that a colimit is already a fixed term. The universal sphere-bundle model makes BSO(q)→BSO(q+1) a q-connected map, its fiber being S^q. A relative CW model consequently has no new cells of dimension at most q. Taking the Thom construction adds q+1 to every relative cell dimension; thus ΣT_q→T_{q+1} is an isomorphism on homotopy in degrees at most 2q. Freudenthal gives π_{q+i}(T_q)≅π_{q+i+1}(ΣT_q) for q+i≤2q−2, since T_q is (q−1)-connected. For i≤4 and q≥8 both comparisons are isomorphisms. Iteration identifies this actual term with the stable colimit. The relative Thom cell statement follows cell by cell by trivializing the bundle over each characteristic disk: a d-cell contributes a (d+q+1)-cell. The same argument applies to the mapping-cylinder relative CW model of the stabilization map, so it does not require an inclusion of particular chosen Grassmannian cell structures.

Let F be the homotopy fiber of f. Its homotopy exact sequence makes F (q+3)-connected and identifies π_{q+4}F with Ω₄SO. Replace F by a CW approximation if needed, preserving both homotopy and singular homology through its weak equivalence; apply the ordinary first-nonzero-degree Hurewicz theorem to that CW approximation. Naturality gives

\[
\Omega^{SO}_4\cong H_{q+4}(F;\mathbb Z).
\]

The homological Serre spectral sequence of F→T_q→K(Z,q) has, in total degrees through q+5, only its two axes: a mixed term would require base degree at least q and fiber degree at least q+4. Its edge/transgression exact sequence is therefore

\[
H_{q+5}(T_q)\longrightarrow H_{q+5}(K(Z,q))
\longrightarrow H_{q+4}(F)
\longrightarrow H_{q+4}(T_q)
\xrightarrow{f_*}H_{q+4}(K(Z,q)).
\]

The middle base group vanishes by (B1). The Thom isomorphism and (C1) consequently embed Ω₄SO as a subgroup of Z⊕Z/2, with image ker f_*.

The kernel has no torsion. Let z be the nonzero order-two element of H_{q+4}(T_q), corresponding under Thom isomorphism to the torsion element of H₄(BSO(q)). Naturality of squares and the native Thom identity give

\[
f^*(Sq^4\bar\iota_q)=Sq^4\bar u_q=w_4\bar u_q.
\]

The right side evaluates to one on z by §C. Thus Sq⁴\barι_q evaluates to one on f_*z, so f_*z≠0. Every torsion element of Z⊕Z/2 lies in {0,z}; none lies in the kernel except zero. Hence Ω₄SO is torsion-free.

Finally the already authored rational oriented-bordism theorem makes Ω₄SO⊗Q one-dimensional. The signature homomorphism has value one on CP², so for any class a the rational class a−σ(a)[CP²] is zero. The integral difference is torsion by exactness of rationalization, and it is zero by the torsion-freeness just proved. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\ [M]=\sigma(M)[\mathbb {CP}^{2}]\text{ in }\Omega^{SO}_4,
\qquad \sigma:\Omega^{SO}_4\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathbb Z.\ }
\]

In particular σ(M)=0 gives an actual compact oriented smooth five-manifold filling M, by the geometric definition of the bordism group. This is (I4), the exact input needed by the relative seam construction. No condition on π₁(M) has entered.
