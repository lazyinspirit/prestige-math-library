Take T=1 and 0<epsilon<1. Let I={*} disjoint union [0,1], V=H=ell2(I), with identity dense continuous embedding. For each t, let A(t)=Id+epsilon(e_t tensor e_* + e_* tensor e_t). Each element of ell2(I) has countable support: for each n, only finitely many coordinates have magnitude >=1/n, and their union contains all nonzero coordinates. Thus for fixed v,w the scalar a(t;v,w)=<v,w>+epsilon(v_* w_t+v_t w_*) differs from the constant <v,w> on a countable measurable subset, hence is scalar measurable. A(t) is uniformly bounded by 1+epsilon, and a(t;v,v)>= (1-epsilon)||v||² by 2|v_* v_t|<=|v_*|²+|v_t|²<=||v||². It meets the generic form hypotheses on this nonseparable real evolution triple. Nevertheless t->A(t)e_*=e_*+epsilon e_t is not strongly measurable. Every strongly measurable ell2(I)-valued function is essentially valued in a separable closed subspace, by its countable simple approximations; such a subspace has a common countable coordinate support by taking a countable dense family and the union of their supports.
Suppose u in W=H1(0,1;H) solves u'+A(t)u=e_* with u(0)=0, with one exceptional null set for the H-vector equation. The strong measurable functions u and u' have a common countable coordinate support S, enlarged to include *. Outside S as a countable subset of time and the exceptional null sets, the t-coordinate of the equation is epsilon u_*(t)=0, because u_t(t)=u'_t(t)=0. Hence u_*=0 a.e. The *-coordinate is u'_*+u_*+epsilon u_t=1; outside S, u_t=0, so u'_*+u_*=1 a.e. But u_* in H1 is identically zero from its a.e. vanishing, and its derivative is zero a.e., a contradiction. Thus even strongly measurable constant forcing and zero initial data need not produce the asserted energy solution under scalar measurability on a nonseparable triple. The example refutes the unsupported generic reading; it is research evidence, not a new library claim. Separability restores the actual proved supplier setting and preserves all substantive typed control, state-adjoint, gradient and comparison-derivative conclusions.
