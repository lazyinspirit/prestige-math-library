## Definition

Fix an integer $d\ge1$ and a bounded Borel set $W\subset\mathbb R^d$. Under
Countable Choice every Borel subset of $\mathbb R^d$ is Lebesgue measurable
([[thm-borel-sets-are-lebesgue-measurable]]), so Lebesgue measure $\ell_d$ is
defined on each such set. Write $|A|:=\ell_d(A)$ for Borel $A$, and
$\mathcal B_W:=\{W\cap A:A\in\mathcal B(\mathbb R^d)\}$ for the trace Borel
sigma-algebra on $W$. Every member of $\mathcal B_W$ is Borel in $\mathbb R^d$
because $W$ is Borel. Define $\ell_W:\mathcal B_W\to[0,+\infty]$ by
$\ell_W(A):=\ell_d(A)$. If $(A_k)$ is pairwise disjoint in $\mathcal B_W$,
then each $A_k$ and their union are Borel in $\mathbb R^d$, so countable
additivity of $\ell_d$ gives
$$\ell_W\!\left(\bigcup_k A_k\right)=\ell_d\!\left(\bigcup_k A_k\right) =\sum_k\ell_d(A_k)=\sum_k\ell_W(A_k),$$
and $\ell_W(\varnothing)=0$. Thus $\ell_W$ is a measure on the trace sigma-
algebra. Since $W$ is bounded, $\ell_W(W)<\infty$
([[prop-lebesgue-measure-is-sigma-finite-and-finite-on-bounded-sets]]).
The only choice axiom used to regard $\ell_d$ as a measure in this library is
Countable Choice, as recorded in
[[thm-lebesgue-measure-is-a-complete-measure]].

The **finite configuration space on $W$**, denoted $\mathcal C(W)$, is the
measurable subspace of locally finite counting measures on $\mathbb R^d$
consisting of those $\eta$ with $\eta(W^c)=0$ and $\eta(\mathbb R^d)<\infty$.
It is measurable in the ambient evaluation sigma-algebra because both
$\eta\mapsto\eta(W^c)$ and $\eta\mapsto\eta(\mathbb R^d)$ are evaluation
maps for Borel sets.
Thus each $\eta\in\mathcal C(W)$ has a representation
$\eta=\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_{x_i}$ for a finite $n\ge0$ and $x_i\in W$; repeated
locations are allowed and represent multiplicity. Its sigma-algebra is the
trace of the evaluation sigma-algebra: it is generated by
$\eta\mapsto\eta(B)$ for Borel $B\subseteq W$.

For a finite ambient configuration $\widetilde\eta$, its observation in $W$
is $\eta=\widetilde\eta|_W$, where
$\eta(B)=\widetilde\eta(B\cap W)$. The observation includes precisely the
locations belonging to $W$: a boundary location is included when and only
when it belongs to $W$. This convention does not replace $W$ by its interior
or its closure.

## Remarks

This definition allows multiplicities and makes no simplicity claim. The
separate finite-intensity Poisson simplicity argument is in
[[lem-diffuse-finite-window-poisson-process-is-simple]].
