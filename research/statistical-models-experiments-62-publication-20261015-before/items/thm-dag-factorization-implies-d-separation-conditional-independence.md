## Statement

Assume AC. Let $(X_v)_{v\in V}$ be random elements in measurable spaces
whose joint law is generated, in a topological order of the finite DAG $G$,
by probability kernels $K_v(x_{\operatorname{pa}(v)},dx_v)$. For pairwise
disjoint $A,B,C\subseteq V$, if $C$ d-separates $A$ and $B$ in $G$, then
$$\sigma(X_A)\perp\!\!\!\perp\sigma(X_B)\mid\sigma(X_C).$$
This is the global Markov implication. Its converse is the separate
faithfulness condition in [[def-faithfulness-of-a-dag-model]].

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite DAG-kernel factorization, measurable vertex spaces, and AC.

[F1] D-separation is equivalent to separation by $C$ in the ancestral moral
graph on $A\cup B\cup C$.
[[lem-d-separation-ancestral-moral-separation]]

[F2] In the moral graph each family $\{v\}\cup\operatorname{pa}(v)$ is a
clique, because every pair of parents of $v$ is joined by a moral edge.
[[def-ancestral-moral-graph-of-a-dag]]

[F3] A probability-kernel extension depending only on one side and the separator preserves conditional independence; conditioning on the extended variable also preserves independence of the two original sides. [[lem-conditional-independence-under-kernel-extension-and-conditioning]]

[F4] Supplied probability kernels have normalized finite associative composition. [[def-composition-of-probability-kernels]], [[lem-kernel-composition-is-well-defined-and-associative]]

[F5] Conditional independence is the bounded-function conditional-product identity. [[def-conditional-independence-given-a-sigma-algebra]], [[def-conditional-expectation-given-a-sigma-algebra]]

[F6] Finite measures agreeing on a generating rectangle pi-system agree on the product sigma-algebra. [[thm-measure-uniqueness-on-a-sigma-finite-pi-system]], [[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** retain the ancestor kernels and prove moral-separator independence by induction deleting a sink, including conditioned sinks.

**Given:** The hypotheses in the statement.

1.1 Put $H=\operatorname{An}_G(A\cup B\cup C)$. Marginalize vertices outside $H$ in reverse topological order: at removal each has no surviving child, so integration of its kernel gives one. The marginal on $H$ is consequently generated by the original kernels at vertices of $H$, with their original parent sets. Reverse-order integration is elimination of final conditional kernels using [F4], not an interchange of arbitrary dependent integrations. [given, F4, construct]

1.2 We prove a stronger finite-graph assertion: if a DAG's full moral graph has a partition of all vertices into $L,R,D$ with no edge from $L$ to $R$, its kernel law satisfies $X_L\perp\!\!\!\perp X_R\mid\sigma(X_D)$. Induct on the number of vertices. For no vertices the variables are constants and the conditional-product identity is immediate. Choose a sink $v$ (the last vertex in a topological order). Marginalizing $v$ retains all other kernels and leaves the DAG with $v$ deleted; its moral graph is a subgraph of the former moral graph, so the remaining partition obeys the induction hypothesis. The original joint law is this marginal extended by $K_v(X_{\operatorname{pa}(v)},dx_v)$: no other kernel depends on $x_v$, as $v$ is a sink. This identity follows first on rectangles from the iterated composition, and then on their generated sigma-algebra by finite-measure uniqueness. [given, F2, F4, F5, F6, construct]

2.1 If $v\in L$, all its parents are in $(L\setminus\{v\})\cup D$, since a parent in $R$ would give a forbidden skeleton edge. Apply the first conclusion of [F3] to the induction hypothesis with left vector $X_{L\setminus\{v\}}$, right vector $X_R$ and separator $X_D$, extending the left vector by $X_v$. This proves the assertion. If $v\in R$, symmetry gives the same result. If $v\in D$, its parents outside $D\setminus\{v\}$ lie wholly in $L$ or wholly in $R$: two parents on opposite sides would be married by a forbidden moral edge. Choose that side (either side when there are no such parents). The induction hypothesis gives independence of $X_L,X_R$ given $X_{D\setminus\{v\}}$, and the second conclusion of [F3], applied to $K_v$, gives independence given $(X_{D\setminus\{v\}},X_v)=X_D$. Thus even a conditioned vertex's kernel is retained as a kernel extension before conditioning. This completes all cases of the induction. [F2, F3, step 1.2, construct]

3.1 By [F1], the moral graph on $H$ with $C$ deleted has no component meeting both $A$ and $B$. Let $L$ be the union of components meeting $A$, and $R=H\setminus(L\cup C)$. Then $B\subseteq R$, $A\subseteq L$, and no moral edge joins $L$ to $R$. Steps 1.2 and 2.1 applied to the marginal law of step 1.1 give independence of $X_L,X_R$ given $X_C$. Restrict its bounded-function identity to functions of $X_A,X_B$ to obtain the claim. Empty endpoint sets yield constant functions and satisfy the same identity. No density, positivity, faithfulness, or regular conditional distribution on an arbitrary measurable space is used. [F1, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct] ∎
