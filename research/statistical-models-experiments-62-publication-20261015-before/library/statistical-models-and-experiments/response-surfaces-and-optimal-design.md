Response-surface models are local polynomial approximations on a declared factor region. The second-order result locates and classifies the stationary point of the fitted polynomial, with no physical or out-of-region guarantee. A central composite design combines factorial or fractional-factorial runs with axial and center runs; estimability and rotatability remain separate checks. For a finite candidate set and a stated linear model, approximate designs assign weights and produce an information matrix. D-, A-, E-, and $c$-optimality optimize different functions of that matrix. The D-equivalence theorem gives a necessary and sufficient leverage bound for nonsingular approximate designs. Exact integer-run optimization is a separate finite problem.
