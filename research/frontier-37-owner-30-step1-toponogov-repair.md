## 1. Corresponding-side-point distance

**Finding: ready after owner reconciliation; no mathematical gap.** The exact result is Lang, *Riemannian Geometry*, Lemma 5.9, printed p.67 / PDF p.71. It says that angle comparison on every subhinge implies chord comparison; its lower-curvature version (actual angles at least model angles, actual corresponding-point distances at least model distances) assumes the two original segments are balanced. Lang defines this chord comparison in Definition 5.7, printed p.66 / PDF p.70. Every geodesic segment in a smooth Riemannian manifold is balanced.

Apply Lemma 5.9 to the hinge at the common vertex of the two sides. For a selected point `u` on the first side, angle comparison on the two subtriangles with vertices `u,p,y` and `u,x,y` gives the model angle inequality at `p`; the model cosine law then gives `d(u,y) >= d(u_tilde,y_tilde)`. Apply the same argument to the reduced hinge with vertex `p` and endpoints `u,y`, selecting any independent point `v` on the second side, to obtain `d(u,v) >= d(u_tilde,v_tilde)`. The two fractions are arbitrary real positions on their sides; no rational subdivision or continuity extension from rational fractions is needed. Endpoint cases are immediate or follow by continuity.

All connector segments required by Lang's lemma exist by completeness and Hopf–Rinow. Every three points on the boundary of the original triangle have a subtriangle perimeter no larger than the original perimeter: each pairwise distance is bounded by the shorter boundary arc, and the three shorter arcs have total at most the boundary perimeter. Thus each subtriangle side is below `pi/sqrt(k)` and its perimeter is below the original strict bound `2*pi/sqrt(k)`. The angle-comparison supplier is therefore used only inside its stated domain, including when the selected fractions are unequal. The original statement's strict triangle inequalities make its model triangle unambiguous. This is the lower-curvature sign; it is the reverse of the fixed-hinge hinge-comparison side inequality.

If an intermediate subtriangle is collinear or has a repeated vertex, its angle or chord case is immediate or is obtained by the continuity cases already required by the planned triangle-comparison item; the strict perimeter and side bounds above still hold.

The page JSON now cites Lang Lemma 5.9 directly and its strategy follows that proof. It also directly depends on `thm-hopf-rinow`, which supplies the connecting segments for every pair of points in the two hinge sides as required by Lang's lemma. Under the current in-run contracts the proposition inherits exactly `AC_omega` through Toponogov comparison; Lemma 5.9 itself makes no stronger choice demand. No full Axiom of Choice is used, and no new prerequisite page or cross-batch edge is needed.

## 2. Diameter rigidity under `K >= k > 0`

**Finding: the updated Toponogov endpoint proof closes the item.** Lang Theorem 5.17, printed p.71 / PDF p.75, supplies the key endpoint perimeter bound: in a complete geodesic Alexandrov space of curvature at least `k`, all triple perimeters are at most `2*pi/sqrt(k)` under its stated midpoint-perpendicular condition. Lang Theorem 5.12, printed p.68 / PDF p.72, identifies a complete Riemannian manifold with `K >= k` as such a space. Here `diam(M)=D=pi/sqrt(k)`, so the extra condition in Theorem 5.17 is vacuous: there is no segment of length greater than `D`. The current manifest does not cite Lang's recursive proof as an `AC_omega`-only derivation; it supplies the endpoint locally by the strict-domain side-point limit below.

The endpoint bound can be obtained without applying the strict comparison theorem at an excluded equality case. Here is the exact reduction through the planned side-point proposition. If a minimizing triangle had perimeter `P > 2D`, its side lengths are at most `D`; hence it is nondegenerate (a degenerate triangle has perimeter twice its largest side). Put `k_*=(2*pi/P)^2 < k`, so `P=2*pi/sqrt(k_*)`. On one side choose `q` so that its distance from an adjacent vertex `p`, plus the other adjacent side length, is `P/2`. For every `k' < k_*` sufficiently close to `k_*`, each side is strictly below `pi/sqrt(k')` and `P < 2*pi/sqrt(k')`; also `K >= k > k'`. Apply the side-point proposition at curvature `k'`. In the model limit as `k'` increases to `k_*`, the perimeter-`2*pi/sqrt(k_*)` spherical triangle lies on a great circle and the selected model chord tends to `P/2`. The actual chord is therefore at least `P/2 > D`, contradicting the diameter. This proves the perimeter bound using only triangles strictly inside the allowed domain. The limiting model statement is Lang's Theorem 5.17 proof, last paragraph on PDF p.75.

Choose diametral points `p,q` with `d(p,q)=D`; they exist since completeness and finite diameter make `M` compact by Hopf–Rinow. For any `x`, triangle inequality gives `d(p,x)+d(x,q) >= D`, while the perimeter bound for `(p,x,q)` gives `D+d(p,x)+d(x,q) <= 2D`. Hence

`d(p,x)+d(x,q)=D`.

Any minimizing segments from `p` to `x` and from `x` to `q` therefore concatenate to a length-minimizing path from `p` to `q`, so their tangents match at `x`. Fixing either segment shows uniqueness of the other by uniqueness for the geodesic initial-value problem. In particular, every sufficiently short radial geodesic from `p` extends along a minimizing geodesic to `q` at time `D`; all radial directions remain minimizing through `D`.

Normalize to `k=1`, `D=pi`. Along any such unit-speed radial geodesic `gamma`, let `E` be any parallel unit normal field and use the fixed-endpoint test field `V(t)=sin(t)E(t)`. Its index form is nonnegative because `gamma` minimizes. The curvature bound gives

`0 <= I_gamma(V,V) = integral_0^pi (cos^2(t) - K(span(E,gamma')) sin^2(t)) dt <= integral_0^pi (cos^2(t)-sin^2(t)) dt = 0`.

Thus `K(span(E,gamma'))=1` throughout the interior. Since the initial normal direction was arbitrary, all radial sectional curvatures from `p` are one. The Jacobi equation gives the radial Jacobi fields `J(t)=sin(t) P_t w`; Gauss's lemma then makes the metric in polar coordinates exactly `dr^2 + sin^2(r) g_{S^{n-1}}`. The exponential map is nonsingular before `pi`, surjective onto `M` minus `{q}` by Hopf–Rinow and the distance-sum identity, and injective by the uniqueness of the minimizing segments just proved. It identifies `M` minus `{q}` isometrically with the round sphere minus its antipode. Both complete spaces are metric completions of these punctured spaces, so the isometry extends to the whole round sphere. Rescale to curvature `k`.

The current manifest names the chord proposition, Hopf–Rinow, second variation/index form, Jacobi and exponential-map interfaces, Gauss lemma, metric completion, and constant-curvature Jacobi model. Its strategy now makes index-form nonnegativity explicit: realize the smooth fixed-endpoint sine field by a small fixed-endpoint variation; the minimizing geodesic minimizes energy among those paths, so its energy Hessian is nonnegative, and second variation identifies that Hessian with `I_gamma`. The listed polar-form corollary is local to a normal neighborhood and can be used on the global normal domain after `exp_p` is shown nonsingular and injective on the punctured domain; Jacobi fields give the angular coefficient `sin^2(r)`. These are already within the pair's published prerequisites or batch-13 inventory; no new pair is needed. The updated diameter dependencies include the chord proposition and remove Cheng/Bonnet–Myers as direct suppliers, in keeping with the design that its spherical equality proof is not conflated with Cheng's Ricci theorem.
