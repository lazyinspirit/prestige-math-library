# Local algebraic Lie bridge — mathematical review evidence

Let G be connected reductive complex affine algebraic group, embedded faithfully and closed in GL(W). Write g for its complex tangent Lie algebra, and r for its Lie radical. G is smooth (local batch16 lemma), integral (published lem-nonaffine-connected-group-geometrically-connected), and a closed matrix Lie group. Complex algebraic smoothness identifies its algebraic tangent with its matrix Lie algebra; exp(tX) belongs to G for X in g and complex t. A Lie-invariant subspace of any rational module is G-invariant: its stabilizer is a closed algebraic subgroup, its Lie algebra contains g, and smoothness plus equal dimensions force the stabilizer to contain the identity component G. This also upgrades Lie-invariant flags. These facts use the published closed subgroup and exponential interfaces and ordinary Jacobian charts; no analytic connectedness theorem is needed.

1. Published Lie triangularization supplies a basis in which r acts upper triangular on W; let lambda_1,...,lambda_N be its diagonal linear forms. The radical is characteristic (published proposition), so Ad(G) preserves r. Conjugation gives equivalent r-representations. More explicitly the characteristic polynomial of A in r is product(T-lambda_i(A)); uniqueness of factorization of this polynomial in C[r][T] shows Ad(h) permutes the finite multiset of linear forms. Each algebraic map h -> lambda_i circ Ad(h) has connected image in a finite subset of r*, hence is constant. Differentiating at identity gives lambda_i([X,A])=0 for X in g and A in r. Thus n=[g,r] is an Ad(G)-stable Lie ideal of g represented by strictly upper triangular matrices.

2. Let N be the subgroup generated by exp(n); each exp(tA) is polynomial in t because A is strictly upper triangular. Its Zariski closure U is a closed subgroup of G, contained in the upper unitriangular group and normal because Ad(G)n=n and conjugation commutes with exponentials. U is connected because N is generated by connected algebraic curves through identity and closure preserves connectedness. To check the definition's fixed-vector characterization of unipotence rather than merely assert it from its matrix realization, Lie(U) is solvable and every finite-dimensional rational U-representation has a Lie-invariant flag by Lie triangularization, hence a U-invariant flag by the stabilizer/dimension argument. Every diagonal character is trivial on exp(tA): its restriction is an algebraic homomorphism A1_add -> Gm, whose coordinate polynomial and inverse are both polynomial, hence constant. It is therefore trivial on dense N and on U. The full flag is consequently unitriangular, so its first line is fixed. U is unipotent in the exact definition and reductivity gives U=1, forcing n=0. Since every centre lies in the radical, r=z(g). Published Levi decomposition gives g=z direct sum s with s semisimple.

3. For X in z, exp(tX) is central in G by the closed-stabilizer/dimension argument. Write X=Xs+Xn as its commuting ordinary matrix Jordan parts; this splitting is in End(W), without assuming either part lies in g. For any regular function P on GL(W) vanishing on exp(tX), substituting the exponential gives a finite sum sum_mu p_mu(t)exp(mu t), where each p_mu is a polynomial; determinant denominators only add exponential factors. Distinct exponential functions exp(mu t) are linearly independent over C[t]: if such a finite relation exists, choose a term mu0 and apply product_mu!=mu0 (D-mu)^(1+deg p_mu), killing all others; on the mu0 term each D+(mu0-mu) is invertible on polynomials of bounded degree, so the result is a nonzero polynomial times exp(mu0 t), contradiction. Hence every p_mu vanishes identically. Substituting exp(tXs)exp(uXn) now gives sum_mu p_mu(u)exp(mu t)=0 for independently chosen t,u, since only Xn contributes polynomial coefficients. Therefore every such P vanishes on exp(uXn), so that subgroup is contained in the Zariski closure of exp(tX), which is central in G. If Xn is nonzero its one-parameter polynomial image is isomorphic to Ga: choose an entry of log(1+M) (the finite polynomial logarithm on unipotent matrices) to recover u. This is a closed image since log identifies it with the closed line C Xn; exp and log are inverse finite polynomials on nilpotent/unipotent matrices. Ga's rational representations have a fixed vector by the flag argument of step2, so it is a nontrivial closed normal unipotent subgroup of G, contradiction. Thus Xn=0. Every X in z is semisimple as a matrix.

4. Published simultaneous diagonalization applies to the commuting family z in End(W). The Zariski closure T of the subgroup generated by exp(z) is consequently contained in the diagonal torus, central in G, and its Lie algebra contains z. It need not be identified abstractly with a torus or placed in a central isogeny. Its coordinate ring is a quotient of C[t1^{+-1},...,tN^{+-1}], and is linearly spanned by the restrictions of monomial characters. Distinct characters of any group are linearly independent: a shortest nontrivial relation, evaluated at hg and subtracting one character(h) times the relation at g, yields a shorter relation unless all characters agree. This proves independence and shows the distinct restricted characters form a basis of C[T]. For a finite-dimensional rational T-module, expand the coaction of v in that basis; coassociativity with Delta chi=chi tensor chi makes the coefficients chi-weight vectors, and the counit makes their sum v. Thus every rational G-module of finite dimension decomposes into T-character spaces, which are G-stable because T is central; its G-submodule W0 decomposes into the intersections with those same spaces. The Lie algebra z acts scalarly on each character space since z is contained in Lie(T). Published Weyl complete reducibility splits the semisimple Lie algebra s on each character space, supplying an s-stable complement of W0 intersect that space. Scalar z action makes those complements g-stable, and the closed-stabilizer/dimension argument makes their direct sum G-stable. This proves complete reducibility for connected G.

5. For possibly disconnected reductive G, first G^circ is reductive: any nontrivial closed normal unipotent subgroup U0 of G^circ has finitely many conjugates Ui under G. Their product is a normal subgroup of G and is unipotent. To substantiate the product assertion using the exact fixed-vector definition, for a rational module V each V^Ui is nonzero and stable under every Uj because each Ui is normal in G^circ. Restrict successively and obtain a common fixed vector; repeat on successive quotients for a full common invariant flag. Thus all Ui are simultaneously unitriangular in a faithful module; the closure of their generated subgroup is normal in G and unipotent by the same flag/character reasoning. Reductivity forces U0=1. Apply step4 to G^circ. If W0 is G-stable choose a G^circ-equivariant projection p onto W0 and average hph^{-1} over one representative of each of the finitely many G/G^circ components; normality makes the resulting projection independent of representatives, G-equivariant and identity on W0. Its kernel is the desired complement.
