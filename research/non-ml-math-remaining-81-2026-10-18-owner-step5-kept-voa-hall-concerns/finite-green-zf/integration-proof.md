## Complete replacement argument

1.1 Let $\mathcal T$ be the free $A$-algebra on $X_{i,n}$, $i\in I,n\ge1$, with degree $n\alpha_i$. The map $X_{i,n}\mapsto F_i^{(n)}$ has image the actual integral half $U_A^-$. Define $\Phi:\mathcal T\to C_K$ by coefficient evaluation $t\mapsto c=\nu^{-1}$ and $X_{i,n}\mapsto u_i^n/[n]_c!$. The denominator evaluations are nonzero by F3. Its image includes every $u_i$, so its $K$-linear span is $C_K$.

2.1 For ordinary words $w,z$ of degree $\alpha$ and height $h$, the primitive Hall coproduct and Green adjunction evaluate their Hall pairing by deleting successive letters. Deleting a letter at position $r$ crosses the preceding letters that remain in the second tensor factor and contributes $\nu^{\sum_{s<r}a_{i,j_s}}$. Each generator pairing contributes $1/(Q-1)$. Thus repeated deletion gives exactly $(w(u),z(u))_H=(Q-1)^{-h}B_t(w(F),z(F))|_{t=c}$, since $c^{-a}=\nu^a$. Equivalently both sides have the same first-letter recursion, vacuum value and generator norms after the height rescaling. By dividing by the evaluated factorials and taking finite linear combinations, for homogeneous $P,R\in\mathcal T$ one obtains
$$ (\Phi(P),\Phi(R))_H=(Q-1)^{-\operatorname{ht}\alpha}B_t(P(F),R(F))|_{t=c}. $$
Every evaluation is legitimate by the actual form's localization bound in F2. Unequal degrees give zero on both sides.

3.1 If homogeneous $P$ is in the actual kernel of $\mathcal T\to U_A^-$, then $P(F)=0$ as an element of the actual generic half. Since F2 has constructed a well-defined bilinear form on that actual half, its norm $B_t(P(F),P(F))$ is the zero rational function. Step 2.1 and positive definiteness of the real Green form imply $\Phi(P)=0$. The kernel is graded, so applying this to its finitely many homogeneous components kills every kernel element. The map $\Phi$ consequently factors through an $A$-algebra map $U_A^-\to C_K$, and extension of scalars gives an algebra map whose image contains $K$ and every $u_i$, hence the surjection $\Psi:K\otimes_{A,t\mapsto c}U_A^-\to C_K$ asserted in the unchanged Statement. The evaluated form on $U_A^-$ is $A$-balanced and induces a $K$-bilinear form on its scalar extension. Step 2.1 passes through the quotient and scalar extension, proving the stated form-preservation assertion, with the factor $(Q-1)^{-\operatorname{ht}\alpha}$.

4.1 Fix $\alpha$. The finite set of ordinary words $W_\alpha$ spans $C_K[\alpha]$, since $C_K$ is generated by the homogeneous simple classes. Let $E:K^{W_\alpha}\to C_K[\alpha]$ be word evaluation. Its pullback Green Gram matrix is $(Q-1)^{-\operatorname{ht}\alpha}G_\alpha(c)$ by step 2.1. The kernel of this matrix is exactly $\ker E$: one inclusion is immediate; if $v$ is in the matrix kernel then $(E(v),E(v))_H=0$, and positivity in the real Hall space gives $E(v)=0$. All coefficients lie in the specified real field $K=\mathbb Q(c)=\mathbb Q(\nu)$. Finite rank-nullity therefore gives
$$\dim_K C_K[\alpha]=\operatorname{rank}_K G_\alpha(c)\le p_\alpha.$$
The final inequality is F2. This proves the dimension consequence without asserting freeness of $U_A^-[\alpha]$, nondegeneracy of the generic or specialized form, or injectivity of any numerical Serre-presentation map.

## Why the helper is sufficient

The helper solves this by proving skew-derivative stability of the Serre ideal, constructing the form on the second quotient argument, then using the explicit matching-permutation symmetry to descend the first argument. $$\operatorname{rank}G(c)\le\operatorname{rank}G(t)\le N-\operatorname{rank}R(t)\le N-\operatorname{rank}R(1)=p_\alpha.$$
In particular it does not infer specialization dimension is bounded by generic dimension for an arbitrary integral lattice. Such a general inference would be false without extra hypotheses. The specialized *Hall image* has dimension equal to its positive Gram rank, which is the relevant quantity.
