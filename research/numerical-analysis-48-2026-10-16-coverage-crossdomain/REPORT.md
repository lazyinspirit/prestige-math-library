## XD-01 — finite-generator master equations and certified transient probabilities

**Pages:** `finite-generator-master-equations-and-uniformization` / companion. **New delta:** finite CTMC generator numerics, not a duplicate discrete Markov kernel definition or generic exponential evaluator.

**Exact scope.** Use column probability vectors: finite Q has Q_ij≥0 for i≠j, 1ᵀQ=0. For ν≥max_j(−Q_jj)>0, P=I+Q/ν is column stochastic and

`p(t)=exp(tQ)p0 = Σ_{k≥0} exp(−νt)(νt)^k/k! P^k p0`.

Define finite Poisson-series accumulation with a certified tail bound and a maximum term budget; ν=0 returns p0. Prove nonnegative substochastic partial sums, l1 omitted probability exactly the scalar Poisson tail for normalized p0, and l1 total transient error ≤tail + accumulated arithmetic/algebraic error. A floating-point subtraction `1−sum` is not automatically a certified tail; use controlled interval sums or a proved geometric/Chernoff tail bound. Large νt must use time splitting or stable Poisson recurrences rather than silently underflowing `exp(−νt)`.

Finite-state projection: for an increasing finite set F in a countable nonexplosive chain, kill the process on first exit, or append an absorbing cemetery state carrying escaped mass. The killed state probability is componentwise ≤the full marginal restricted to F. Under a common-process coupling, l1 error after embedding the killed vector in the full state space equals lost mass; the absorbing projection including the cemetery state instead gives an observable error bound for bounded observables, with oscillation factor stated. Returning trajectories are a reason killed and restricted full probabilities differ. Adaptive enlargement requires a finite budget and certificates from each actual projection; existence of a finite set meeting a tolerance is separate.

**Proof route and local suppliers.** (1) `def-xd-finite-column-generator` and `lem-xd-uniformized-matrix-is-stochastic`; (2) finite matrix power series convergence/term differentiation and coefficient identity, reuse the general matrix-functions supplier if available; otherwise prove these finite-dimensional facts here before use; (3) Poisson law/tail recurrence and a controlled tail majorant; (4) uniformization identity by commuting Q=ν(P−I), uniqueness of finite linear ODE; (5) killed path coupling and first-exit decomposition, using time-agent CTMC construction/nonexplosion, or prove the finite stopped-path construction locally; (6) finite/countable projection monotonicity and loss certificate by summing nonnegative path probabilities; (7) telescoping perturbation error in substochastic l1 contractions. No unproved assertion of arbitrary countable-generator nonexplosion.

**Dependencies.** NA-01/03; independent ODE; time-agent jump construction; general matrix functions optional. Published Markov kernel composition, scalar Poisson law and mass normalization reused under exact hypotheses. The published finite-intensity spatial Poisson theorem alone does not supply all stopping-time CTMC interfaces.

**B examples.** Two-state gating; finite birth–death model with computed Poisson tail; countable immigration/death projected to F with loss monitor; equal mean but different distributions showing that a deterministic concentration ODE is not a master-equation solver.

**Source locators.** Jensen, “Markoff chains as an aid in the study of Markoff processes,” Skandinavisk Aktuarietidskrift 1953, uniformization construction, DOI `10.1080/03461238.1953.10419467`; Munsky–Khammash, “The finite state projection algorithm for the solution of the chemical master equation,” J. Chemical Physics 124 (2006), 044104, finite projection lower bound and error criterion, DOI `10.1063/1.2145882`; Fox–Glynn, “Computing Poisson probabilities,” CACM 31 (1988), pp. 440–445, DOI `10.1145/42404.42409`. These are proposed reading locators, not read receipts.

## XD-02 — certified numerical stationary distributions and hitting statistics

**Pages:** `certified-stationary-distribution-and-hitting-statistic-numerics` / companion. **Disposition:** add numerical guarantees while reusing canonical invariant/discrete-chain results and linear-solver definitions.

**Exact scope.** Finite P stochastic, Q generator. Stationary solve uses `(I−P)x=0, 1ᵀx=1` or `Qx=0,1ᵀx=1`, with a stated irreducible/unique-class hypothesis for uniqueness. Reuse NA/LA factorization/Krylov procedures, never restate them. Reducible chains require explicit closed-class decomposition and initial-law-dependent class weights; no arbitrary unique stationary vector. For a nonnegative normalized candidate x, residual r=P x−x and a certified induced zero-sum l1 contraction `||P^m z||1≤q||z||1`, q<1, give

`||x−π||1 ≤ ||P^m x−x||1/(1−q) ≤ m||r||1/(1−q)`.

Derive a computable q from column-overlap/Dobrushin coefficient of P^m, bounded with controlled arithmetic; if q≥1, return `certificate-unavailable`, not a false accuracy claim. CTMC stationary certificates use any stochastic uniformization P, with r=Qx/ν. A negative/nonnormalized approximate vector needs explicit correction and a corresponding perturbation ledger before this theorem applies.

Transient hitting/absorption probabilities solve `(I−T)h=b` and mean hitting times `(I−T)t=1` only for a substochastic transient block with proved spectral radius <1. Supply a finite-step escape/minorization bound giving inverse norm and residual error. No absorption guarantee from an arbitrary substochastic matrix. Add stationary perturbation conditioning via the same zero-mass inverse, separating generator error from solve residual.

**Proof route / all missing suppliers.** Locally own graph class decomposition algorithms and invariance proof; zero-sum l1 operator coefficient formula; finite-step contraction/geometric Neumann inverse; normalized augmented system uniqueness and solver residual map; transient inverse positivity and finite-step escape bound. Existing stationary existence/uniqueness/detailed-balance and LA inverse identities are canonical suppliers. General Perron–Frobenius is unnecessary for this finite contraction route; if a spectral-gap alternative is promised, its missing PF/reversible weighted-norm proof is owned here. Published unweighted simple-graph Kirchhoff matrix-tree theorem is not a directed weighted Markov-chain tree theorem; do not silently substitute it.

**B examples.** Nearly reducible two-state chain with small residual and large stationary error; reducible two absorbing classes; birth–death detailed balance; finite credit/reliability absorption. Show periodic P can fail ordinary power iteration although stationary solve remains well defined; lazy uniformization or P^m contraction requires the actual hypotheses.

**Dependencies.** XD-01; NA-01/02/03/05 as actual solver interfaces; canonical probability stationary laws. **Sources:** Stewart, *Introduction to the Numerical Solution of Markov Chains* (1994), stationary methods and conditioning chapters, DOI `10.1515/9780691241762`; Seneta, *Non-negative Matrices and Markov Chains*, ergodicity coefficients chapter, DOI `10.1007/0-387-32792-4`; Norris, *Markov Chains*, finite chains/hitting-time sections, DOI `10.1017/CBO9780511810633`. ## XD-03 — conservative positive production–destruction integrators

**Pages:** `positive-conservative-production-destruction-integrators` / companion. **New method:** modified Patankar families, with no claim that A-stability, symplecticity or generic RK order implies positivity.

**Exact scope.** Nonnegative finite compartments y_i with pair transfers p_ij(y)=d_ji(y)≥0 and `y_i′=Σ_j(p_ij−d_ij)` (external sources/sinks separately specified). First-order Patankar Euler uses transfers frozen at y^n with destination/source weights `y_j^{n+1}/σ_j`, where σ_j>0 is declared and, for first-order consistency, σ_j=y_j^n under a uniform positive lower bound. The resulting matrix has nonpositive off-diagonals, positive diagonal and strict column dominance; prove inverse nonnegativity, positivity and exact summed conservation. Handle initially zero compartments by an explicit zero-consistent transfer-factor representation or a separately analyzed positive denominator regularization; `0/0=0` cannot silently preserve the order proof. The specified second-order positive-interior scheme is MPRK22(1): compute the stage z by Patankar Euler with denominator y^n; then solve `y_i^{n+1}=y_i^n+Δt Σ_j[(p_ij(y^n)+p_ij(z)) y_j^{n+1}/(2z_j)−(d_ij(y^n)+d_ij(z)) y_i^{n+1}/(2z_i)]`. Prove second order on a compact smooth positive tube with uniformly positive stage denominators; conservation/positivity hold independently of that Taylor estimate. Zero-stage and denominator-regularized variants have separate declared contracts, rather than borrowing this order theorem. Do not assert all Patankar variants are second order.

**Proof route / local ownership.** Own finite M-matrix inverse positivity by a nonnegative Neumann series after diagonal scaling, strict dominance/invertibility, conservative column-sum identity, admissible zero-state transfer representation, uniform coefficient bounds, local defect and global order on a compact positive tube. These are not supplied by scalar stiff stability. Derive a residual-to-mass/positivity ledger for inexact solves; positivity after rounded solves is conditional on certified component bounds or a separately analyzed conservative correction. A tiny linear residual alone is not positivity.

**B examples.** Two-compartment stiff reversible transfer; three-species stoichiometric conservation, with explicit conversion from number to weighted mass variables; extinction/zero-component corner showing denominator failure; explicit Euler negative concentration despite correct first-order truncation. Stipulate kinetics; no empirical reaction law becomes a theorem.

**Dependencies.** NA-01/02/15/17, independent ODE; XD-01 generator positivity as reusable special case. **Sources:** Burchard–Deleersnijder–Meister, “A high-order conservative Patankar-type discretisation for stiff systems of production–destruction equations,” Applied Numerical Mathematics 47 (2003), pp. 1–30, DOI `10.1016/S0168-9274(03)00101-6`; Kopecz–Meister, “On order conditions for modified Patankar–Runge–Kutta schemes,” Applied Numerical Mathematics 123 (2018), pp. 159–179, DOI `10.1016/j.apnum.2017.09.004`. Read actual stage formulas/order conditions before committing a particular second-order carrier.

## XD-05 — coagulation–fragmentation and population-balance numerics

**Pages:** `coagulation-fragmentation-and-sectional-population-balance-numerics` / companion.

**Exact scope.** Define finite discrete cluster aggregation/fragmentation by binary event rates and stoichiometric increments. Positive event rates and pair identities establish number/mass moments directly. Define conservative sectional/fixed-pivot redistribution on a finite positive mass grid: each product lying between adjacent pivots is split by nonnegative barycentric weights summing to one and reproducing its mass. Product above the cutoff enters a **reported lost-mass reservoir**, not an invented last-bin particle. ## XD-06 — meshfree kernel consistency and conservative SPH

**Pages:** `meshfree-kernel-approximation-and-conservative-sph` / companion. General scattered-data RBF interpolation is routed to linear/approximation owner; this pair does not rename it.

State the joint refinement relation between particle fill distance and ε required for convergence; fixed neighbors/irregular particles do not automatically give consistent derivatives. Boundary truncation destroys unity/linear reproduction: give a specified corrected moment fit with uniformly invertible moment matrix, or restrict theorem to interior/periodic points and identify the boundary solver as a separate obligation. Do not claim arbitrary boundary consistency.

The specified SPH system has `ρ_i=Σ_j m_j W_ε(x_i−x_j)`, `x_i′=v_i`, and `v_i′=−Σ_j m_j(p_i/ρ_i²+p_j/ρ_j²)∇W_ε(x_i−x_j)`, positive masses, a C² radial kernel and a proved lower bound on ρ_i on the controlled tube. Antisymmetry proves total linear-momentum conservation; radial central forces prove angular-momentum conservation. An energy-conserving semidiscrete variant must include exactly matched internal-energy equation; dissipative artificial viscosity must have the stated sign and a separate energy inequality. **Local suppliers.** Kernel moment/Taylor bounds; particle-cell quadrature estimate; derivative kernel scaling; boundary moment-matrix conditioning; pair antisymmetry/central-force identities; smooth regularized-force Lipschitz bounds and time-integrator invariant/error ledger. **Dependencies.** NA-01/10/12/15/22; XD-08 common regularized-force stability optional earlier supplier. **Sources:** Monaghan, “Smoothed particle hydrodynamics,” Reports on Progress in Physics 68 (2005), pp. 1703–1759, DOI `10.1088/0034-4885/68/8/R01`; Quinlan–Basa–Lastiwka, “Truncation error in mesh-free particle methods,” International J. ## XD-08 — interacting particle stability, mean-field approximation and vortex blobs

**Pages:** `interacting-particle-mean-field-and-vortex-blob-error` / companion. This is not the BCS particle-filter algorithm: particles here evolve by coupled forces rather than proposal/resampling weights.

**Exact scope.** Globally Lipschitz K_ε (or controlled compact tube with proved invariance), dynamics `x_i′=N^−1Σ_j K_ε(x_i,x_j)`. Define probability-measure transport/weak solution by characteristics, and coupling-based W1 only on finite-first-moment measures. For any initial coupling, characteristic differences satisfy an integral inequality with constant ≤2Lip(K_ε); derive `W1(μ_t,ν_t)≤exp(2Lip(K_ε)t)W1(μ_0,ν_0)` by taking infima, without assuming an attained optimal coupling. Empirical measures solve the same weak equation exactly. Add temporal and force-approximation perturbations by discrete Gronwall. For finite-support empirical measures and a prescribed initial coupling, avoid a completeness theorem if not needed; if general measure existence is promised, prove the pushforward fixed-point construction and required completeness locally.

Regularized vortex blobs: define a smooth regularized Biot–Savart kernel and weighted particle vorticity for a **finite signed measure**.
