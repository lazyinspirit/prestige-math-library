## Construction-time mathematical content

The A page carries 15 items: the 11 design items plus 4 local prerequisites, all before their consumers.

1. `def-gauss-frame-map-of-an-immersion-into-euclidean-space` (design 1) fixes the convention: the derivative of an immersion into Euclidean space is a section of the twisted Stiefel bundle, and only a trivialisation of the tangent bundle turns it into a map into $V_m(\mathbb R^n)$. This distinction is load-bearing: for $S^2$ the bundle is non-trivial, so "Gauss map into $SO(3)$" is only meaningful after choosing frames.
2. `prop-euclidean-formal-immersions-are-sections-of-a-stiefel-bundle` (design 2) identifies formal Euclidean immersions with sections of $V(TS^m,\varepsilon^n)$, including the trivial special case $V(TS^1,\varepsilon^2)\cong S^1\times S^1$.
3. `lem-the-basepoint-evaluation-of-the-stiefel-section-space-is-a-fibration` (local) is the technical heart of the formal classification: evaluation at the basepoint is a Hurewicz fibration; the based section space is the space of based maps $S^m\to V_m(\mathbb R^n)$, so its $\pi_0$ is $\pi_m(V_m(\mathbb R^n))$ via the **difference (clutching) class**; the long exact sequence then gives $\pi_0$ of the full section space as a quotient of that group, hence a bijection when the fibre is simply connected and a singleton when $\pi_m$ of the fibre vanishes.
4. `lem-stiefel-manifolds-are-connected-and-simply-connected-in-positive-codimension` (local) supplies the connectivity inputs, by induction on $m$ along the fibre bundle $V_m(\mathbb R^n)\to S^{n-1}$ with fibre $V_{m-1}(\mathbb R^{n-1})$.
5. `lem-the-second-homotopy-group-of-so-three-vanishes` (local) computes $\pi_2(SO(3))=0$ from the quaternion double cover and $\pi_2(S^3)=0$, and transfers it to $V_2(\mathbb R^3)\cong O(3)$. This is the algebraic eversion input.
6. `def-rotation-number-of-an-immersed-oriented-circle-in-the-plane` (design 3) and `lem-formal-immersions-of-the-circle-in-the-plane-are-classified-by-the-winding-number` (local) set up the circle case: the trivial tangent bundle makes the formal space $\operatorname{Map}(S^1,S^1)$, and the invariant of an immersion is its rotation number.
7. `lem-regular-homotopy-preserves-the-formal-gauss-class` (design 5) differentiates a regular homotopy and gives the necessity half of every classification.
8. `thm-whitney-graustein-classification-of-plane-circle-immersions` (design 4) is proved as necessity (the invariant lemma) plus sufficiency (the formal classification and the Smale–Hirsch $\pi_0$ bijection); Whitney's deformation proof is the recorded classical treatment.
9. `thm-smale-classification-of-sphere-immersions-in-euclidean-space` (design 6) assembles the three-clause statement described above.
10. `lem-standard-and-reflected-two-sphere-immersions-have-homotopic-formal-data-in-r-three` (design 7), `thm-sphere-eversion` (design 8) and the three remarks (design 9–11) close the page.
11. **B page.** `ex-plane-circle-immersions-of-rotation-number-k`, `cex-the-figure-eight-and-round-circle-are-not-regularly-homotopic-as-oriented-immersions`, `ex-formal-frame-homotopy-behind-sphere-eversion`, `cex-a-homotopy-through-maps-with-a-rank-drop-is-not-a-regular-homotopy`, `ex-a-boy-surface-immersion-of-real-projective-two-space` (design B1–B5).

Honest caveats for Step 3, stated again in the readiness records:

- The classical statements (Smale's sphere classification, Whitney–Graustein, the Boy-surface parametrisation) are literature-derived; the local items isolate the two steps that need the most careful authoring: (i) the evaluation-fibration computation of $\pi_0$ of the section space (the standard mapping-space obstruction argument, expanded from Hatcher and Francis) and (ii) the explicit immersedness verification for the Bryant–Kusner parametrisation and the smooth closing of the three planar ends (Kusner's explicit Weierstrass computation plus Karcher's inversion/closing statement). Neither is hidden.
- The figure-eight rotation number $0$ is verified in the example by the signed-crossing count of the velocity about a ray (two crossings of the positive $x$-axis with opposite signs); the numeric check is not part of the mathematics, the finite count is.
- The B-page counterexample `cex-a-homotopy-through-maps-with-a-rank-drop-…` is a deliberate counterexample: at $t=1$ the slice is constant, so the family is not a regular homotopy although every earlier slice is an immersion.
