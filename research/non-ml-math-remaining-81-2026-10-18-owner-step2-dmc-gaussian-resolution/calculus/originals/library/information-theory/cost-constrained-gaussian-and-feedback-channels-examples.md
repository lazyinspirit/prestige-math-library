These examples compute a binary identity channel's capacity under a unit letter cost, solve both regimes of the two-branch AWGN water-filling allocation, diagonalize an explicit correlated Gaussian noise covariance, and exhibit a countable-input DMC whose finite information-capacity supremum is not attained. The final example uses the separately defined countable-input model and proves directly that its finite information-capacity supremum has no maximizing input law. All examples use nats, verify relevant endpoints, and identify when feedback leaves capacity unchanged.
