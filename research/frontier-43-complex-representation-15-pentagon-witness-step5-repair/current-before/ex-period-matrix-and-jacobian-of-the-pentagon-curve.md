## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $X$ be a compact
connected Riemann surface of genus $2$ admitting an automorphism $T$ of order
$5$, with $H_1(X;\mathbb Z)$ a free rank-one module over
$A:=\mathbb Z[T]/(1+T+T^2+T^3+T^4)$ generated by a class $C$, and suppose there
is a degree-five quotient map $y:X\to\widehat{\mathbb C}$ whose fibres are the
$T$-orbits (for instance the smooth genus-two curve obtained by gluing two
regular pentagons, equivalently a smooth projective model of $y^2=x^5-1$, for
which $T$ is induced by the rotation of the pentagons). Then:

1. The eigenvalues of $T^{*}$ on $\Omega(X)\cong\mathbb C^2$
([[lem-holomorphic-differentials-form-a-g-dimensional-space]]) are primitive
fifth roots of unity; after replacing $T$ by a power and choosing the basis,
there is a basis $\omega_1,\omega_2$ of $\Omega(X)$ with
$T^{*}\omega_i=\zeta^{i}\omega_i$ for $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/5}$ and
$P(C,\omega_i)=1$, $i=1,2$
([[def-period-pairing-and-period-lattice]]).
2. On the cycle $T^{k}C$ one has $P(T^{k}C,\omega_i)=\zeta^{ki}$, so the period
lattice is the rank-four lattice
$$\Lambda=\bigl\{\bigl(p(\zeta),p(\zeta^2)\bigr):p\in\mathbb Z[T]\bigr\} =\bigl\{(a,\sigma(a)):a\in\mathbb Z[\zeta]\bigr\}\subseteq\mathbb C^2,$$
$\sigma$ the Galois conjugation $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^{2}$, and
$\operatorname{Jac}(X)=\mathbb C^{2}/\Lambda$
([[def-jacobian-of-a-compact-riemann-surface]]).
3. The period vectors of the four cycles $C,TC,T^2C,T^3C$ are the four columns
$(1,1),(\zeta,\zeta^2),(\zeta^2,\zeta^4),(\zeta^3,\zeta)$, which generate
$\Lambda$; they are $\mathbb R$-linearly independent, because a real relation
$\sum_kx_k(\zeta^k,\zeta^{2k})=0$ gives $\sum_kx_k\zeta^{jk}=0$ for
$j=1,2,3,4$ (using conjugation for $j=3,4$) and the Vandermonde matrix
$(\zeta^{jk})_{1\le j\le4,\,0\le k\le3}$ has trivial kernel: a polynomial of
degree at most three with four distinct roots is zero
([[thm-root-bound-for-polynomials-over-a-domain]]). Define the real $4\times4$
matrix $M$ by its columns
$$M_{:,k+1}:=(\operatorname{Re}\zeta^k,\operatorname{Im}\zeta^k,\operatorname{Re}\zeta^{2k},\operatorname{Im}\zeta^{2k})^{\mathsf T},\qquad 0\le k\le3.$$
Here $(z_1,z_2)\in\mathbb C^2$ has real coordinates
$(\operatorname{Re}z_1,\operatorname{Im}z_1,\operatorname{Re}z_2,\operatorname{Im}z_2)$.
Hence $\Lambda$ is a full lattice of covolume $|\det M|>0$, so the
period torus is compact ([[def-full-rank-lattice-covolume-and-dual-lattice]],
[[thm-riemann-bilinear-relations]]).
4. Every point of $\operatorname{Jac}(X)$ is represented by a degree-zero
divisor and $\operatorname{Pic}^0(X)\cong\operatorname{Jac}(X)$
([[thm-jacobi-inversion]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Full AC, a genus-two surface $X$ with an order-five automorphism $T$ and a degree-five quotient map $y$, a class $C$ generating $H_1(X;\mathbb Z)$ freely over $A$, and the period pairing $P$.

[F1] $X$ has genus $2$, so $\Omega(X)$ is a two-dimensional complex vector space, and a symplectic basis of $H_1(X;\mathbb Z)$ exists ([[lem-holomorphic-differentials-form-a-g-dimensional-space]], [[def-genus-and-euler-characteristic-compact-riemann-surface]], [[thm-symplectic-homology-basis-compact-riemann-surface]]).

[F2] $H_1(X;\mathbb Z)$ is a free rank-one $A$-module generated by $C$; equivalently the classes $C,TC,T^2C,T^3C$ form a $\mathbb Z$-basis, and $T^4C=-C-TC-T^2C-T^3C$ (the defining relation of $A$).

[F3] The trace of a holomorphic differential along the quotient map $y$ extends holomorphically to the sphere and is zero. For a $T$-invariant differential, all five inverse-branch pullbacks are equal, so pulling the trace back on the regular locus gives $y^*(y_*\omega)=5\omega$; hence such a differential is zero there, and continuity makes it zero everywhere ([[lem-trace-of-a-holomorphic-one-form-under-a-nonconstant-map-to-the-sphere]], [[thm-proper-holomorphic-map-riemann-surfaces-has-degree]], [[thm-local-normal-form-holomorphic-map-riemann-surfaces]], [[def-ramification-index-and-branch-value]]).

[F4] The period pairing $P$ is additive in the cycle class and $\mathbb C$-linear in the differential, is independent of representatives and of the chosen symplectic basis, and for $e(\gamma)(\omega)=P(\gamma,\omega)$ the bilinear relations make $e$ injective with $\Lambda=e(H_1(X;\mathbb Z))$ a full lattice ([[def-period-pairing-and-period-lattice]], [[lem-period-pairing-is-well-defined-and-computed-by-integration]], [[thm-riemann-bilinear-relations]]).

[F5] For a biholomorphic automorphism $T$ and a holomorphic differential $\omega$, $\int_{T\circ\gamma}\omega=\int_\gamma T^*\omega$; hence at the level of homology classes $P(T_*\gamma,\omega)=P(\gamma,T^*\omega)$ ([[def-path-integral-of-a-holomorphic-differential-on-a-riemann-surface]], [[def-meromorphic-differential-on-a-riemann-surface]]).

[F6] The Jacobian is $\Omega(X)^*/\Lambda$, and for degree-zero divisors the Abel-Jacobi map is represented by path integrals; $u$ is surjective by Jacobi inversion, and $\operatorname{Pic}^0(X)\cong\operatorname{Jac}(X)$ ([[def-jacobian-of-a-compact-riemann-surface]], [[def-abel-jacobi-map]], [[thm-jacobi-inversion]], [[cor-picard-zero-is-the-jacobian]]).

[F7] A nonzero polynomial of degree $n$ over an integral domain has at most $n$ distinct roots ([[thm-root-bound-for-polynomials-over-a-domain]]). A square matrix with trivial kernel is invertible ([[thm-invertible-matrix-theorem]]).

[F8] Full AC is inherited from the period, dimension and Jacobian interfaces ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

## Verification

**Given:** The surface, the automorphism and the generating class of the Statement.

1.1 Put $V=\Omega(X)$, of complex dimension two by [F1], and $A=T^*|_V$. Since $A^5=1$, the projections $E_j=\frac15\sum_{k=0}^4\zeta^{-jk}A^k$ satisfy $\sum_{j=0}^4E_j=1$ and $AE_j=\zeta^jE_j$, so $V$ is a sum of eigenspaces; [F3] excludes eigenvalue $1$. By [F4], the real-linear extension of the period map $H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb R\to V^*$ is an isomorphism: it maps the real basis of cycles to the real basis given by their full-lattice period vectors. Naturality [F5] intertwines $T_*$ with the dual operator $\xi\mapsto\xi\circ A$. The cyclic basis of [F2] gives characteristic polynomial $t^4+t^3+t^2+t+1$ for $T_*$, with each of $\zeta,\zeta^2,\zeta^3,\zeta^4$ occurring once. On the underlying real space of $V^*$, its complexification has eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\bar\lambda_1,\bar\lambda_2$, where $\lambda_1,\lambda_2$ are those of $A$. Consequently the exponents $k,l$ of $\lambda_1=\zeta^k,\lambda_2=\zeta^l$ are distinct and nonopposite modulo five. Their ratio $l/k$ is $2$ or $3$ modulo five; by exchanging them if necessary it is $2$. Replace $T$ by $T^r$ with $rk=1$ modulo five. Its eigenvalues on $V$ are then $\zeta,\zeta^2$, and $T^r$ generates the same ring as $T$, so $C$ remains a cyclic generator by [F2]. Choose corresponding eigenvectors $\omega_1,\omega_2$. This proves the required eigenvalue normalization without a separate Hodge-decomposition premise. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, algebra]

2.1 For each eigenvector $\omega_i$, if $P(C,\omega_i)=0$, naturality [F5] gives $P(T^kC,\omega_i)=\zeta^{ki}P(C,\omega_i)=0$ for every $k$. The cyclic basis [F2] then makes every period vanish on $\omega_i$. But [F4] says the real span of period functionals is all of $V^*$, so every complex-linear functional vanishes on $\omega_i$, forcing $\omega_i=0$, a contradiction. Thus rescaling by $P(C,\omega_i)^{-1}$ preserves the eigenvector and normalizes its period to $1$. [F2, F4, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 By naturality [F5] and the eigenvalue relations, $P(T^kC,\omega_i)=P(C,(T^k)^*\omega_i)=\zeta^{ki}P(C,\omega_i)=\zeta^{ki}$ for every $k\ge0$; the case $k=4$ is consistent because $T^4C=-C-TC-T^2C-T^3C$ and $\zeta^{4i}=-\sum_{k=0}^{3}\zeta^{ki}$ for $i=1,2$. [F2, F5, step 2.1]

4.1 Every class in $H_1(X;\mathbb Z)$ is $\sum_k a_kT^kC$ with integers $a_k$ by [F2]; by additivity of $P$ and step 3.1 its period vector is $\bigl(\sum_ka_k\zeta^k,\sum_ka_k\zeta^{2k}\bigr)=\bigl(p(\zeta),p(\zeta^2)\bigr)$ for the polynomial $p=\sum_ka_k t^k$ reduced in $A$. Hence $\Lambda$ is the displayed lattice; the identification with $\{(a,\sigma(a)):a\in\mathbb Z[\zeta]\}$ uses $\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^2$. [F2, F4, step 3.1]

5.1 The four period vectors of $C,TC,T^2C,T^3C$ are $(1,1),(\zeta,\zeta^2),(\zeta^2,\zeta^4),(\zeta^3,\zeta)$ by step 3.1, and generate $\Lambda$ by step 4.1. If $\sum_{k=0}^{3}x_k(\zeta^k,\zeta^{2k})=0$ with real $x_k$, then $p(t):=\sum_{k=0}^{3}x_kt^k$ vanishes at $\zeta,\zeta^2$ and, by conjugation, at $\zeta^4,\zeta^3$. These four complex numbers are distinct, so [F7] forces $p=0$ and every $x_k=0$. The real coordinate matrix $M$ defined in clause 3 therefore has trivial kernel and is invertible by [F7]. Its columns generate $\Lambda$, so the full-rank lattice definition gives $\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)=|\det M|>0$, in agreement with [F4]. [F4, F7, step 3.1, step 4.1]

5.2 By [F6] the Jacobian is $\mathbb C^2/\Lambda$ with $\Lambda$ as in step 4.1, every point of it is represented by a degree-zero divisor, and $\operatorname{Pic}^0(X)\cong\operatorname{Jac}(X)$; this is the last claim. [F6, step 4.1]

6.1 Claims 1-4 are steps 2.1, 4.1, 5.1 and 5.2, under the inherited AC of [F8]. [F8, step 2.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 5.2] ∎


## Source notes

The example is McMullen's pentagon computation, Theorem 15.3 with the preceding paragraph (*Riemann Surfaces*, printed p. 128): $T$ has order five, $y$ is the quotient map, the eigenvalues of $T^*$ on $\Omega(X)$ are $\zeta$ and $\zeta^2$ after replacing $T$ by a power, the periods on $T^kC$ are $\zeta^{ki}$, and $H_1(X;\mathbb Z)=A\cdot C$ gives $\operatorname{Jac}(X)\cong\mathbb C^2/\mathbb Z[(\zeta,\zeta^2)]$. The Vandermonde computation of step 5.1 is the explicit full-lattice verification for this model. No published defect was found in the passage.
