## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $X$ be a compact
connected Riemann surface of genus $g\ge1$, let $p_0\in X$ and let
$u=u_{p_0}:X\to\operatorname{Jac}(X)$ be the Abel-Jacobi map
([[def-abel-jacobi-map]]). Then:

1. The image $u(X)$ is a compact subset of the complex torus
$\operatorname{Jac}(X)$ with a one-dimensional complex-graph chart at each of
its points, and $u$ is a homeomorphism of $X$ onto $u(X)$; for $g\ge2$ the
image has complex dimension one inside the $g$-dimensional torus, so it is
neither open nor a complex subtorus
([[thm-abel-jacobi-embedding-positive-genus]]).
2. The subgroup of $\operatorname{Jac}(X)$ generated by
$u(X)-u(p_0)=\{u(p)-u(p_0):p\in X\}$ is all of $\operatorname{Jac}(X)$: every
class $u((q)-(p))$ is a difference of two image points, every degree-zero
divisor is a finite sum of such classes, and Jacobi inversion gives the whole
group ([[thm-jacobi-inversion]]).
3. For $g=1$ the image is the whole torus: $u:X\to\operatorname{Jac}(X)$ is a
biholomorphism ([[ex-periods-of-a-complex-torus]]). For the genus-two pentagon
curve, $X$ embeds as a compact curve generating the two-dimensional torus
$\operatorname{Jac}(X)=\mathbb C^2/\Lambda$ of
[[ex-period-matrix-and-jacobian-of-the-pentagon-curve]].
4. The map is a bijection onto its image with holomorphic inverse: $u(p)=u(q)$
implies $p=q$ by the embedding theorem, and the inverse is holomorphic because
$u$ is locally biholomorphic onto its image.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Full AC, a compact connected Riemann surface $X$ of genus $g\ge1$, a base point $p_0$, and the Abel-Jacobi map $u$.

[F1] $u$ is injective, its derivative is nonzero at every point, it is a homeomorphism onto its compact image, and every point of the image has a chart of $\operatorname{Jac}(X)$ in which the image is the graph of a holomorphic map ([[thm-abel-jacobi-embedding-positive-genus]], [[def-biholomorphic-map]]).

[F2] On $\operatorname{Div}^0(X)$ the map $u$ is a group homomorphism with $u((q)-(p))=u(q)-u(p)$, and the kernel is the principal divisors ([[def-abel-jacobi-map]], [[lem-abel-jacobi-map-is-well-defined-and-base-point-independent]], [[thm-abels-theorem-for-divisors]]).

[F3] The degree-zero divisor extension of $u$ is surjective, so every class is $u(D)$ for a degree-zero divisor $D$, and every degree-zero divisor is a finite $\mathbb Z$-combination of point differences ([[thm-jacobi-inversion]], [[thm-abels-theorem-for-divisors]]).

[F4] The Jacobian is the complex torus $\Omega(X)^*/\Lambda$ of complex dimension $g$; for $g=1$ the Abel-Jacobi map is a biholomorphism onto the whole torus, and $\operatorname{Pic}^0(X)\cong\operatorname{Jac}(X)$ ([[def-jacobian-of-a-compact-riemann-surface]], [[thm-abel-jacobi-embedding-positive-genus]], [[cor-picard-zero-is-the-jacobian]], [[ex-periods-of-a-complex-torus]]).

[F5] The genus-two pentagon curve has Jacobian $\mathbb C^2/\Lambda$ with a full period lattice $\Lambda$, and every point of it is represented by a degree-zero divisor ([[ex-period-matrix-and-jacobian-of-the-pentagon-curve]], [[thm-jacobi-inversion]]).

[F6] Full AC is inherited from the Jacobian construction ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

## Verification

**Given:** The objects and conventions in the Statement.

1.1 By [F1], $u$ is injective with nonzero derivative, is a homeomorphism onto its compact image, and is locally biholomorphic onto the graph charts of that image. Thus $u(X)$ is a one-dimensional complex submanifold in the stated chart sense. For $g\ge2$, the ambient dimension is $g>1$ by [F4]; in every graph chart a small change in a transverse complex coordinate leaves the graph, so the image has empty interior and is not open. [F1, F4]

1.2 For $g=1$ claim 3 is [F4]: $u$ is a biholomorphism onto the whole torus. For the genus-two pentagon curve, [F5] identifies the Jacobian as a two-dimensional complex torus with full period lattice and every class represented by a degree-zero divisor, so claim 2 applies and the image generates the torus; claim 1 gives the compact curve embedded in it. [F1, F4, F5]

2.1 Every difference $u(q)-u(p)$ is the class $u((q)-(p))$ by [F2]. Finite sums of such differences are images of degree-zero divisors, and conversely every degree-zero divisor is a finite integer sum of point differences. By [F3] the divisor extension is surjective, so the subgroup generated by $u(X)-u(p_0)$ is the whole Jacobian. Since $u(p_0)=0$, if $u(X)$ were a complex subtorus, it would already be a subgroup and would equal the subgroup it generates, hence would equal the Jacobian. This is impossible for $g\ge2$ by the dimension and empty-interior calculation in step 1.1. This completes claims 1 and 2. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Claim 4 is contained in claim 1: $u$ is injective, so it is a bijection onto its image, and the local biholomorphy of step 1.1 gives a holomorphic inverse on the image. [F1, step 1.1]

3.1 Claims 1-4 are steps 1.1, 2.1, 1.2 and 2.2, under the inherited AC of [F6]. [F6, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 1.2, step 2.2] ∎

## Source notes

McMullen's Theorem 15.7 and the surrounding discussion (*Riemann Surfaces*,
printed p. 130) exhibit the curve in its Jacobian as a smooth embedded curve
generating the torus; Looijenga's Ch. 7 §2 (printed pp. 60-63) shows the point
map, its injectivity for positive genus and the genus-one biholomorphism. The
example packages the general embedding theorem with the two explicit models of
claims 3.
