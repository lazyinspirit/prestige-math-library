## Example

In $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,0}$ the lift of the nonidentity reflection in $O(1)$ squares to $+1$, while in $\mathrm{Cl}_{0,1}$ it squares to $-1$. In positive two-dimensional signature, $\operatorname{Spin}(2)$ is the unit circle in the even Clifford algebra and its map to $SO(2)$ doubles the angle.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The conventions Pin$^+(n)\subset\mathrm{Cl}_{n,0}$, Pin$^-(n)\subset\mathrm{Cl}_{0,n}$ and the full-even Spin definition.

[F1] Pin is generated by vectors with $|Q(u)|=1$, full Spin is its even subgroup, and the Clifford norm on vector products is $\prod(-Q(u_j))$ ([[def-real-clifford-group-and-normalized-pin-spin]], [[lem-normalized-pin-spin-product-and-norm-laws]]).

[F2] The twisted action of an anisotropic vector is its hyperplane reflection ([[lem-twisted-clifford-adjoint-reflection-and-scalar-kernel]]).

[F3] Ordered square-free monomials form a basis of the Clifford algebra ([[thm-clifford-pbw-basis-and-associated-graded]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 In $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,0}$, $e^2=1$ and the unit-vector-generated Pin group is $\{1,-1,e,-e\}$; its reflection lift $e$ has square $+1$. In $\mathrm{Cl}_{0,1}$, $f^2=-1$ and Pin is $\{1,-1,f,-f\}\cong C_4$; its reflection lift has square $-1$. In both cases the even subgroup is $\{1,-1\}$. [given, F1, algebra]

1.2 In $\mathrm{Cl}_{2,0}$ put $E=e_1e_2$. Then $E^2=-1$, and by [F3] the even algebra is exactly $\mathbb R\oplus\mathbb R E\cong\mathbb C$. For $a^2+b^2=1$, $a+bE$ is a product of two unit vectors: $e_1(ae_1+be_2)=a+bE$. Thus every unit $a+bE$ belongs to Spin, and conversely every even unit product has norm one, so Spin$(2)=\{a+bE:a^2+b^2=1\}$. [F1, F3, algebra]

2.1 For $\theta\in\mathbb R$, let $g_\theta=\cos(\theta/2)-E\sin(\theta/2)$. Since $g_\theta^{-1}=\cos(\theta/2)+E\sin(\theta/2)$, direct multiplication using $Ee_1=-e_2$ and $e_1E=e_2$ gives $\rho_{g_\theta}(e_1)=\cos\theta\,e_1+\sin\theta\,e_2$ and $\rho_{g_\theta}(e_2)=-\sin\theta\,e_1+\cos\theta\,e_2$. Therefore the angle doubles; $g_{2\pi}=-1$ and $g_{4\pi}=1$. [F2, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
