FILE items/def-qa-37-fibonacci-braid-gate-set.md
Definition

Use the fixed chirality and fusion-tree basis of the three-$\tau$, total-$\tau$
Fibonacci space from [[ex-qc-tqc-fibonacci-qubit-matrices]]. There
$B_1=\operatorname{diag}(1,r)$, $B_2=FB_1F$, and
$r=e^{-3\pi i/5}$. Put $z=e^{3\pi i/10}$ and
$$G_{\rm Fib}=\{zB_1,zB_2,(zB_1)^{-1},(zB_2)^{-1}\}\subset SU(2).$$
The determinants are one because $\det B_j=r$ and $z^2r=1$; the matrices
are unitary by the cited matrix verification. The resulting package is
inverse-closed, and its generated subgroup is dense under the inherited
$\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ assumption by
[[lem-qa-37-fibonacci-su2-lifts-are-dense]]. Its exact entries are algebraic
numbers. For the finite bit implementation, the real and imaginary
coordinates are supplied by minimal polynomials over $\mathbb Q$ and rational
isolating intervals with opposite endpoint signs and exactly one real root,
as required by the finite-precision SK theorem. For the SK recursion one
additionally supplies a fixed finite base-net certificate $(\mathcal N_0,\eta_0,L_0)$ with
$0<\eta_0<64^{-2}$, as defined in
[[def-qc-inverse-closed-dense-su2-gate-set]]. The certificate is input data:
the compactness result [[lem-qc-compact-su2-base-net]] proves existence but
computes neither the list nor $L_0$.

The diagram uses the positive Artin crossing convention: time runs downward,
and the continuous left-input strand passes over the broken right-input
strand. Its word matrix is the representation convention in
[[def-qc-tqc-braid-action-from-fusion-data]]; no proof depends on the drawing.

FILE items/def-qa-37-finite-adiabatic-braid-input.md
Definition

A **finite adiabatic braid input** consists of a finite-dimensional complex
Hilbert space $V$, a $C^2$ Hermitian path $H(s)$ on $0\le s\le1$, a
norm-continuous constant-rank orthogonal spectral projection $P(s)$ onto a
coherently followed band with uniform positive full gap, a slow-time schedule
$T>0$, and an explicit initial isometry $A_0:\mathbb C^m\to\operatorname{ran}P(0)$.
These are the path and propagator hypotheses of
[[def-qa-36-finite-adiabatic-path-model]]; the output propagator $U_T$ solves
$iU_T'(s)=TH(s)U_T(s)$. Also supply an endpoint isometry
$A_1:\mathbb C^m\to\operatorname{ran}P(1)$ and a specified Fibonacci braid
word $b$ with physical action $\rho(b)$ from
[[def-qc-tqc-physical-braid-interface]] in the fixed convention of
[[ex-qc-tqc-fibonacci-data]], and a unitary target
$\rho_{\rm log}(b)\in U(\mathbb C^m)$ in the declared logical input/output
frames. The target $\rho_{\rm log}(b)$ is the selected categorical $F/R$
action written in those frames, including the phase convention recorded by
[[def-qa-37-phase-reference-cost]]; specifying it does not assert that the
physical path realizes it. The **in-band transport condition** is the separate
hypothesis
$$\|P(1)U_T(1)A_0-A_1\rho_{\rm log}(b)\|_{\rm op}\le\epsilon_{\rm tr}.$$
This condition is not a consequence of a projector-leakage estimate. The
model also specifies a finite output measurement and, if needed, its own
implementation-variation bound. The JRS expression and leakage consequence
are handled as a separate result.


FILE items/def-qa-37-fusion-input-encoding.md
Definition

Fix $n\ge1$, ordered simple charges $a_1,\ldots,a_n$, a total charge $c$,
and one parenthesized binary tree. An **encoded fusion input** consists of the
finite tree shape, a simple-charge label on each internal edge, and a
multiplicity-basis index at each trivalent vertex. A label is **admissible**
exactly when every chosen vertex index belongs to the specified basis of its
multiplicity space $V_{xy}^z$; the allowed local vertex data and resulting
orthonormal fusion-tree vectors are those of
[[def-qc-tqc-fusion-tree-basis]]. The input encoding uses fixed self-delimiting
binary codes for charge labels and nonnegative multiplicity indices, with
out-of-range labels rejected as invalid encodings. It also records the total
charge and the selected computational subspace isometry required by
[[def-qa-37-anyon-task-and-promise]], as in
[[def-qc-tqc-fixed-charge-encoding]]. The tree labels and isometry are finite
classical data; they do not themselves specify or prepare the initial encoded
quantum state, which is the separate state/preparation field in the task
record.


FILE items/def-qa-37-fusion-measurement.md
Definition

Let $H$ be a fixed finite fusion space with an orthonormal fusion-tree basis.
For an adjacent pair of leaves or a declared adjacent pair of subtrees, recouple
the tree by its specified unitary $F$-moves so that the pair fuses first. For
each allowed simple total charge $a$ of that pair, let $P_a$ be the orthogonal
projection onto the span of basis vectors whose pair label is $a$, transported
back by the inverse recoupling unitary. Distinct channel subspaces are
orthogonal and their direct sum is $H$, so $(P_a)_a$ is a finite projective
measurement in the sense of [[def-qc-projective-measurement]]. Its outcome is
$a$, its unnormalized branch vector on input $\psi$ is $P_a\psi$, and the
measurement is specified by the braid/F-matrix action convention in
[[def-qc-tqc-braid-action-from-fusion-data]]. The branch vector is unnormalized until its probability is checked.


FILE items/def-qa-37-n-triple-fusion-code.md
Statement

Let $n\ge1$ and $c\in\{1,\tau\}$ with $V(\tau^n\to c)\ne0$, using the
left-associated tree for both fixed-charge spaces. In the ordered list of
$3n$ Fibonacci $\tau$ charges, group consecutive leaves into $n$ triples;
within each triple fuse its first two charges and then its third, and fuse the
$n$ triple outputs by the left-associated $n$-leaf tree to total $c$. Restrict
each triple output to $\tau$; its two local paths are indexed by the charge
$x\in\{1,\tau\}$ after the first two leaves. Fix a rebracketing sequence
of associators from this grouped tree to the fixed left-associated $3n$-leaf
tree, and let $R_{n,c}$ be the induced composite unitary. For each tensor
basis vector $e_{x_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{x_n}\otimes v_\lambda$, where
$e_1,e_\tau$ are the local triple paths and $v_\lambda$ is a basis vector of
$V(\tau^n\to c)$, map it to its grouped fusion-tree basis vector, then
apply $R_{n,c}$. Extend linearly and call the resulting map $J_{n,c}$. Its
image is the code $\mathcal C_{n,c}$ and it is an isometry; set
$P_{n,c}=J_{n,c}J_{n,c}^*$ for the code projector.
The factor $V(\tau^n\to c)$ retains all spectator fusion paths and is not a
physical preparation at zero cost.



[F1] The Fibonacci rule has the only nontrivial fusion channels
$\tau\otimes\tau=1\oplus\tau$, each of multiplicity one.
[[ex-qc-tqc-fibonacci-data]]

[F3] The fixed-charge code projector associated with an isometry is
$JJ^*$. [[def-qc-tqc-fixed-charge-encoding]]

FILE items/def-qa-37-phase-reference-cost.md
Definition

For the fixed Fibonacci convention, write $\rho(b)$ for the physical braid
matrix and $B(b)$ for the normalized word matrix obtained by removing the
common exchange scalar $R_1$ from each Artin generator, as in
[[lem-qc-tqc-determinant-character-phase]]. If $e(b)$ is exponent sum, then
$$\rho(b)=R_1^{e(b)}B(b).$$
On the three-anyon total-$\tau$ block the SK alphabet instead uses the
special-unitary lifts $zB_j$ with $z=e^{3\pi i/10}$, so a word of exponent sum
$k$ satisfies
$$\rho(b)=(R_1z^{-1})^k\widehat B(b),\qquad \widehat B(b)=z^kB(b).$$
A **phase reference record** stores the exact generator scalar, exponent sum,
which normalized convention was compiled, the resulting scalar on each
coherently used branch, and any correction operation and its declared cost
under [[def-qc-circuit-cost-model]]. A standalone fixed-sector computation
may report its output projectively using the metric of
[[def-qc-tqc-projective-gate-and-metric]]. In coherent control the active
branch scalar is relative to the inactive branch and must remain in the
record. No phase correction is assigned zero cost unless its interface says
so. This ledger distinguishes the common $R_1^{e(b)}$ phase from the
additional $z^{-e(b)}$ needed when the compiled alphabet is the SU(2) lift.


FILE items/def-qa-37-physical-locality-boundary.md
Definition

A **physical realization record** for a mathematical braid-compilation
instance contains, in addition to its finite fusion input and braid word, all
of: a specified physical state space and low-energy-sector identification; a
$C^2$ Hermitian path and constant-rank uniformly gapped projection; an
initial preparation isometry and its cost; a slow-time schedule; an endpoint
frame; a separately justified identification of band transport with the
logical-frame Fibonacci $F/R$ target of
[[def-qa-37-finite-adiabatic-braid-input]], including its phase; and a
locality model for exchanges. A local-Hamiltonian model specifies a finite
lattice $\Lambda$ with its metric, an interaction range $R$, and a uniform
per-site interaction bound $J$ for a decomposition
$H(s)=\sum_{X\subseteq\Lambda}h_X(s)$: every $h_X(s)$ is Hermitian and
supported on $X$, $\operatorname{diam}(X)\le R$, and
$$\sup_{s\in[0,1],\,x\in\Lambda}\sum_{X\ni x}\|h_X(s)\|\le J.$$
The record separately states the local-indistinguishability and stability
criteria, including their region scales, norms, tolerances, and the evidence
used to establish them. It also supplies explicit preparation, excitation,
measurement, and control-error terms.
The first spectral-path clauses are the finite interface in
[[def-qc-tqc-physical-braid-interface]] and
[[def-qa-37-finite-adiabatic-braid-input]]. The error ledger is
[[lem-qa-37-total-error-budget]]. A successful SK compilation, projective
density theorem, or JRS leakage estimate supplies none of the material,
locality, preparation, or band-holonomy evidence by itself; those are
independent record fields, not consequences of the mathematical compiler.
Only terms appearing as hypotheses in the stated error ledger contribute to
its measured-output bound; a separately recorded preparation, excitation, or
control error requires an additional bound before it can be included in a
total physical error claim.


FILE items/def-qa-37-resource-cost-model.md
Definition

For a finite anyon solver instance and one run, record separately the tuple
$$\bigl(L_{\rm in},\ N_{\rm any},\ |b|,\ N_{\rm prep},\ N_{\rm anc},\ N_{\rm meas},\ B_{\rm out},\ b_{\max},\ t_{\rm cl},\ T_H,\ \epsilon_{\rm op},\epsilon_{\rm tr},\epsilon_{\rm meas},\ell_{\rm leak},\ p_{\rm leak},\ N_{\rm phase},\ t_{\rm phase}\bigr).$$
Here $L_{\rm in}$ is the complete finite classical input-description bit
length, including the declared task, state/preparation descriptions when
classically encoded, and tolerance descriptions; a directly supplied quantum
input is not assumed to have a finite classical coordinate description;
$N_{\rm any}$ is the number of ordered simple-charge strand labels in the
fusion-space instance; and $|b|$ is the number of braid-generator occurrences
(inverse generators count as one). $N_{\rm prep}$ counts elementary
operations in the declared preparation procedure, while $N_{\rm anc}$ counts
the ancillary anyons or other physical ancilla systems used by preparation or
computation. If the initial state is supplied directly as an abstract quantum
input with no preparation interface, $N_{\rm prep}$ is “not applicable”, not
silently set to zero; $N_{\rm anc}$ still records any declared solver ancillas
(zero if none, or “not applicable” if no physical ancilla model is supplied).
$N_{\rm meas}$ is the number of fusion measurements,
and $B_{\rm out}$ is the total bit length of their classical outcome record
under its declared finite encoding (zero when no measurement occurs). The
largest unary precision query to a certified matrix name is $b_{\max}$; $t_{\rm cl}$
is the classical bit time used by search and postprocessing. $T_H$ is the total
Hamiltonian evolution time over the run in the declared physical time unit;
it is “not applicable” for an abstract braid-only model without Hamiltonian
evolution. The error coordinates $\epsilon_{\rm op},\epsilon_{\rm tr},
\epsilon_{\rm meas},\ell_{\rm leak},p_{\rm leak}$ are respectively a stated
operator-norm approximation bound, an independent in-band transport bound,
an implementation-to-ideal-measurement total-variation bound, and leakage
amplitude and probability bounds. $N_{\rm phase},t_{\rm phase}$ count phase
corrections and their classical control cost. Circuit description and
elementary wire costs follow [[def-qc-circuit-cost-model]], while classical
input bit cost follows [[def-qa-classical-bit-cost]] and finite nonoracle
gates follow [[def-qa-nonoracle-gate-cost]]. An inapplicable coordinate is
marked “not applicable”; it is not assigned value zero. Unless an interface
explicitly states a bound, no braid length, preparation operation, ancilla,
matrix precision, measurement, Hamiltonian time, search step, or phase
correction is free. The tuple is a bookkeeping convention; it does not assert
any of its error or runtime bounds.
