## Statement

Set $P=\mathbb C[\mathfrak h^*]$, choose one nonzero linear form $\alpha_H$ for each reflecting hyperplane, and put $\delta=\prod_H\alpha_H$ and $\mathfrak h_{\rm reg}=\{z\in\mathfrak h:\delta(z)\ne0\}$. The assignment
$$x\mapsto m_x,\qquad w\mapsto w,\qquad y\mapsto T_y:=\partial_y-\sum_{s\in S}c_s\frac{\alpha_s(y)}{\alpha_s}(1-s)$$
defines an injective homomorphism $H_{1,c}(W,\mathfrak h)\to\mathcal D(\mathfrak h_{\rm reg})\rtimes W$, and it induces an isomorphism after inverting $\delta$. Here $\mathcal D(\mathfrak h_{\rm reg})$ is the algebra generated by regular functions and algebraic vector fields, and the crossed product uses the given action of $W$.

For every $M\in\mathcal O_c(W,\mathfrak h)$, $M_{\rm reg}=P[\delta^{-1}]\otimes_P M$ is finite locally free over $P[\delta^{-1}]$ with an integrable connection from the $\mathcal D(\mathfrak h_{\rm reg})$ action. The connection is $W$-equivariant. Localization and then taking a fiber with parallel transport is exact on short exact sequences in $\mathcal O_c$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $H_{1,c}(W,\mathfrak h)$, its PBW basis, the reflection data and parameter $c$, and $M\in\mathcal O_c(W,\mathfrak h)$.

[F1] [[def-rational-cherednik-reflection-data-and-parameters]]: $W$ preserves the finite reflecting arrangement; each $\alpha_s$ defines its fixed hyperplane and $x-sx=\alpha_s^\vee(x)\alpha_s$.

[F2] [[thm-rational-cherednik-pbw-theorem]]: ordered monomials $x^a w y^b$ form a basis; the associated graded for degree in $y$ is $P\rtimes W\otimes\mathbb C[\mathfrak h]$.

[F3] [[lem-rational-cherednik-polynomial-representation-dunkl-formula]]: the displayed $T_y$ preserve $P$ and satisfy the rational Cherednik relations, including $[T_y,T_z]=0$.

[F4] [[def-rational-cherednik-category-o]]: $M$ is finite over $\mathbb C[\mathfrak h^*]$.

[F5] [[lem-determinant-trick-for-nakayama]]: if a finite module $Q$ over a commutative ring satisfies $I Q=Q$ for an ideal $I$, then some $a\in I$ satisfies $(1-a)Q=0$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** [F1]–[F5].

1.1 By [F3], the assignments $x\mapsto m_x$, $w\mapsto w$, and $y\mapsto T_y$ respect every defining relation, so they give an algebra homomorphism into $\mathcal D(\mathfrak h_{\rm reg})\rtimes W$. Filter $H_{1,c}$ by degree in $\mathfrak h$ with $\mathfrak h^*$ and $W$ in degree zero, and filter the target by differential order. The principal symbol of $T_y$ is $\partial_y$, so the associated-graded map is the localization $\mathbb C[\mathfrak h^*]\rtimes W\otimes\mathbb C[\mathfrak h]\longrightarrow\mathbb C[\mathfrak h_{\rm reg}\times\mathfrak h^*]\rtimes W$, which is injective because a polynomial that vanishes after localization by the nonzero $\delta$ is zero. Therefore the original homomorphism is injective: a nonzero element of least filtration degree would have a nonzero leading symbol, contradicting injectivity of the associated-graded map. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 After inverting $\delta$, every $\alpha_s$ is a unit by [F1]. Hence $\partial_y=T_y+\sum_s c_s\alpha_s(y)\alpha_s^{-1}(1-s)$ belongs to the localized image for every $y\in\mathfrak h$. The localized image contains all regular functions, $W$, and all constant coordinate derivations, which generate $\mathcal D(\mathfrak h_{\rm reg})\rtimes W$. It is the whole target; injectivity is preserved by exact localization over $P$. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Localization is exact because it is localization over the commutative ring $P$. By [F4], $M_{\rm reg}$ is finite over $P[\delta^{-1}]$. Step 2.1 makes it a module over the differential operators on that ring. The coordinate derivations satisfy the Leibniz rule and commute, so they define an integrable connection. The crossed-product relations make this connection $W$-equivariant. [F4, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 It remains to prove local freeness. At a closed point $p\in\mathfrak h_{\rm reg}$ let $R=P[\delta^{-1}]_{\mathfrak m_p}$, $\mathfrak m=\mathfrak m_pR$, and $N=(M_{\rm reg})_p$. Choose lifts $m_1,\dots,m_r$ of a basis of the finite-dimensional fiber $N/\mathfrak mN$. The quotient $Q=N/\sum_iRm_i$ is finite and satisfies $\mathfrak mQ=Q$. By [F5], some $a\in\mathfrak m$ has $(1-a)Q=0$; since $1-a$ is a unit in $R$, $Q=0$, so the lifts generate. If $\sum_i f_im_i=0$ is a nonzero relation, basis independence modulo $\mathfrak m$ forces every $f_i\in\mathfrak m$. Let $k$ be the least total order of vanishing of a nonzero $f_i$ at $p$. Some multi-index $\beta$ of order $k$ has $\partial^\beta f_i(p)\ne0$ for at least one $i$, because a nonzero regular germ has a nonzero Taylor coefficient. Apply the ordered sequence of connection derivations specified by $\beta$ to the relation. By the Leibniz rule, every term in which at least one derivation hits an $m_i$ leaves fewer than $k$ derivatives on its coefficient and vanishes modulo $\mathfrak m$. Reducing modulo $\mathfrak m$ therefore gives $\sum_i\partial^\beta f_i(p)\bar m_i=0$, a nontrivial relation among the fiber basis. This contradiction proves $N$ is free. Since this holds at every closed point, $M_{\rm reg}$ is a finite-rank vector bundle. [F4, F5, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Localization sends each short exact sequence in $\mathcal O_c$ to a short exact sequence of vector bundles with connection by step 3.1 and local freeness from step 4.1. A short exact sequence of vector bundles is locally split, so its fiber sequence is exact. Parallel transport commutes with morphisms of connections; hence the fiber monodromy representations form a short exact sequence. [step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
