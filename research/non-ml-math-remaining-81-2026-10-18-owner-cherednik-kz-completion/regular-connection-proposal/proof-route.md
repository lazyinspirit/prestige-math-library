# Elementary full-faithfulness route, replacing only the needed RH implication

This is a proof proposal, not an accepted canonical supplier. The target is full faithfulness of horizontal monodromy on localized Cherednik category O, including algebraic subobjects. Essential surjectivity for arbitrary regular connections is unnecessary and is not claimed.

## Bounded separately rational functions

Let U be a nonempty Zariski open subset of C^n, and f holomorphic on U. Suppose that outside an algebraic exceptional set of coordinate lines, its restriction to each coordinate line is rational of numerator and denominator degrees at most D, uniformly in the line. Then f is rational.

Induct on n. The n=1 assertion is the assumption. Choose 2D+1 distinct constants a_i in the first coordinate such that all nonempty slices U_i inherit the generic-line hypotheses in the remaining variables. There are only finitely many forbidden values: each generic-line exception is specified by finitely many nonzero polynomial coefficients, and a coefficient polynomial can vanish identically after x_1=a at only finitely many a unless it is zero. The induction hypothesis gives rational functions f_i(x')=f(a_i,x'). Over L=C(x'), solve the 2D+1 homogeneous equations P(a_i)=f_i Q(a_i), with deg P,deg Q <=D. There are 2D+2 unknown coefficients. A nonzero solution cannot have Q=0 because then P has 2D+1>D distinct roots. For generic fixed x', the actual rational restriction P_*/Q_* has degree <=D and is regular at all a_i; the polynomial P Q_*-P_* Q has degree <=2D and vanishes at all 2D+1 nodes. It is zero. Thus f=P/Q on a nonempty analytic open subset, and then throughout U by the identity theorem. The n=0 assertion concerns constants.

## Moderate growth for induced nilpotent modules

For M in category O choose finitely many polynomial generators, take their finite W-orbits, then all y-monomials applied to those vectors. Local nilpotence of the commuting y_i makes the resulting space X finite dimensional and stable under W and all y_i. The induced module U=H tensor_(S(h) semidirect W) X surjects onto M, and PBW gives U=P tensor X. The exact connection formula is

partial_y(f tensor v)=partial_y f tensor v + f tensor yv + sum_s c_s alpha_s(y)/alpha_s f tensor (1-s)v.

This follows by subtracting the reflection difference term in the Dunkl identification from the induced y-action; it must be checked with the current minus-sign presentation. The sum gives the standard-module formula when yX=0.

Filter X by powers of the augmentation ideal m=(h), or by their annihilator filtration; W preserves it and every y lowers it. On successive quotients the constant y-part vanishes, so diagonal connection blocks are logarithmic with fixed residue matrices. Along a generic affine line t -> a+tb, finite poles are simple, with residues conjugate to the fixed hyperplane residue matrices. At infinity, z=1/t, the diagonal blocks have simple poles, while off-diagonal blocks from yX have poles of order at most two. There are finitely many blocks, and their number is bounded by the nilpotence length of X.

For a simple-pole system Y'=A(z)Y with ||A(z)||<=C/|z|, solve along a bounded-angle sector, first along a circle and then radially. Gronwall gives ||Y|| and ||Y^{-1}|| <=C' |z|^{-N}, with N any integer larger than C (enlarge for the finite angular segment). For an upper triangular block system, variation of constants expresses each off-diagonal block as a diagonal fundamental solution times integrals of products of previously bounded blocks and coefficients with pole order <=2. Integrating a bound r^{-a} adds a finite power of r^{-1}; a possible logarithm is bounded by one extra inverse power. Induction over block distance bounds both a fundamental matrix and its inverse by an inverse power. The integer exponent depends only on block count, pole-order bound, and diagonal residue norms, not on the generic line. Constants and the permitted radius may depend on that line. This proves the precise uniform-degree consequence required below, without asserting a logarithmic lattice for the full nilpotent module at infinity.

A quotient connection M of U has the same moderate-growth property, as does its dual. Indeed every horizontal section of M locally lifts to a horizontal section of U after choosing its initial lift. Every horizontal section of M^* injects into U^*. In any fixed rational frame, the quotient map and a rational left inverse of its dual embedding have finite pole orders. Such frames exist by choosing a nonzero maximal minor of a finite generating matrix. On generic lines their pole orders, number of poles, and degrees are bounded by those of the finitely many fixed rational matrix entries and minors. Thus the growth exponents remain bounded independently of the generic line. This argument uses no exactness of reduction over a local ring.

## Horizontal maps are algebraic

An intertwiner of the two monodromy representations extends uniquely by parallel transport to a single-valued holomorphic horizontal map F between the analytic localized bundles: changing the path changes the transports by monodromy, and the intertwining identity cancels that change. Local existence of holomorphic horizontal frames follows by successively solving the coordinate linear ODEs on a small polydisc; curvature zero ensures that the successive solution satisfies every coordinate equation. Analytic dependence follows from the uniformly convergent Picard series, bounded by (C length)^r/r! on each smaller polydisc. This is also an elementary proof of the local statement used in defining the monodromy functor.

In rational frames, each entry of F is a holomorphic function on a fixed Zariski open subset. On a generic coordinate line, F is a linear combination of products of horizontal sections of the target and dual source. The preceding uniform growth bounds imply a pole of bounded integer order at each finite puncture and at infinity: a single-valued holomorphic function bounded by |z|^{-N} has no Laurent coefficient of degree below -N by the circle Cauchy formula. Hence each restricted entry is a meromorphic function on the sphere, therefore rational, with degrees bounded uniformly by the finite number of punctures and their pole-order bounds. Bounded separate rationality proves each entry rational in all variables.

These rational maps are regular on all of h_reg. At any point choose local algebraic frames for the two bundles. Their matrix entries are rational and holomorphic analytically. A reduced denominator with an irreducible factor defining a hypersurface through that algebraic neighborhood would produce a pole at a smooth point where its numerator is nonzero; such points are dense in that hypersurface, contradicting analytic holomorphy. Thus every denominator factor cancels except factors invertible in the local ring. This is the elementary UFD argument in the localized polynomial ring, not an appeal to GAGA. It proves that F is an algebraic connection map. Faithfulness is immediate from uniqueness of horizontal continuation.

## Subobjects and quotients of monodromy: determinant-line argument

Let S_b be an invariant r-dimensional subspace of the fiber of M_reg. The cases r=0 and r=rank M are immediate. Its parallel transports form an analytic flat subbundle S. In wedge^r M the determinant of S is a monodromy-invariant line. Choose a nonzero horizontal determinant section s on the universal cover. On a small punctured transverse disc its monodromy is multiplication by one scalar mu, not necessarily 1. Choose lambda with exp(2 pi i lambda)=mu. Then z^{-lambda}s is single-valued. Both it and s have moderate growth on a bounded-angle sector: wedge powers preserve the growth bounds and multiplication by z^{-lambda} changes only a power. Thus every coordinate of z^{-lambda}s is meromorphic at the puncture, by the Laurent/Cauchy argument. All ratios of Pluecker coordinates consequently extend meromorphically there. This is stronger than an unsupported assertion that a ratio of moderately growing functions is moderately growing.

There is a uniform degree bound on these ratios on generic affine coordinate lines. The finitely many local monodromy scalars mu are eigenvalues of the corresponding fixed exterior-power monodromy matrices of M (near each hyperplane and at generic infinity). Their logarithms lambda can be chosen with real part in [0,1); the imaginary parts are bounded because this is a finite eigenvalue set. The induced-module block estimate therefore gives a uniform bound on pole orders of each of the single-valued twisted coordinates. To bound the degree of their ratios on the entire punctured line, use the same determinant line with its rank-one monodromy character: multiply s by a product of factors (t-a_j)^{-lambda_j}, choosing exponents for all finite punctures. The resulting vector is single-valued on the finite punctured line, meromorphic at every finite puncture, and at infinity because its residual monodromy is the inverse product of the finite ones. Its coordinates are rational functions with uniformly bounded degrees. Their ratios have uniformly bounded degrees as well. The number of finite punctures is fixed by the arrangement plus the fixed rational-frame divisor. Scalar exponents at artificial frame divisors can be zero. No nonzero coordinate is asserted never to vanish.

Fix one Pluecker chart nonempty on an analytic open subset. Its coordinate ratios are holomorphic there and satisfy bounded separate rationality, hence are rational in all variables. They satisfy the Pluecker equations by analytic equality, so define an r-dimensional subspace S_K of M tensor K over K=C(h). Let S_alg=M_reg intersect S_K inside M tensor K. This is a finite submodule because M_reg is finite over the Noetherian ring P[delta^{-1}]. Horizontality on a dense open set makes it stable under all connection derivations, by rational equality. Its quotient is finite with a connection as well. The local-freeness proof already supplied for finite modules with a connection in the current Dunkl-localization lemma applies verbatim to both modules (it used only finiteness, a connection, and the polynomial regular local ring, not membership in O). Thus S_alg and the quotient are vector bundles. They coincide with the original analytic S and its quotient: equality on a dense open set propagates by uniqueness of horizontal continuation. This also shows the intersection has the required rank everywhere.

Finally let N={m in M : its localized image belongs to S_alg}. It is an H-submodule of M and finite over P by Noetherianity, and inherits local nilpotence of h; hence N belongs to O. Its localization is S_alg: each element of the latter is a localized element of M and multiplying by a sufficient power of delta puts it in N. The quotient M/N realizes the monodromy quotient. Consequently the essential image of O/O_tor under KZ is closed under subobjects and quotients. Together with horizontal-map algebraicity and the explicit localization roofs below, this is exactly the part of regular RH required by the quotient-equivalence proof; no essential-surjectivity assertion for arbitrary regular connections is used.

## Localization roofs

For any algebraic localized map f:M_reg -> N_reg, form its graph and the inverse image of that graph under M direct-sum N -> M_reg direct-sum N_reg. This inverse image G is an H-submodule of M direct-sum N, hence belongs to O by polynomial Noetherianity and inherited local nilpotence. Its first projection has torsion kernel and torsion cokernel, since it becomes an isomorphism after localization. The second projection gives a roof M <- G -> N in the torsion quotient. Two roofs with equal localization agree after quotienting their torsion difference. Conversely a zero localized map has torsion image and is zero in the quotient. This proves full faithfulness of algebraic localization directly, with no appeal to a general Serre-quotient theorem. Closure of the kernel under subobjects, quotients and extensions follows from exact localization.
