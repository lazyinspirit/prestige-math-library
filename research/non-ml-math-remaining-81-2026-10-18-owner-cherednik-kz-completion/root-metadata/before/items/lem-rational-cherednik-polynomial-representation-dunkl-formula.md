## Statement

For $t=1$, the polynomial representation of [[def-rational-cherednik-polynomial-representation]] has the differential-reflection action
$$x f=xf,\qquad (w f)(z)=f(w^{-1}z),\qquad y f=\partial_y f-\sum_{s\in S}c_s\frac{\alpha_s(y)}{\alpha_s}(1-s)f.$$
Each displayed operator maps $\mathbb C[\mathfrak h^*]$ to itself and these operators satisfy the presentation of [[def-rational-cherednik-algebra]].

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The reflection representation $(W,\mathfrak h)$, its reflection data and parameters, the algebra $H_{1,c}(W,\mathfrak h)$, and the polynomial module $P_c(W,\mathfrak h)$.

[F1] [[def-rational-cherednik-algebra]]: $[y,x]=x(y)1-\sum_s c_s\alpha_s(y)\alpha_s^\vee(x)s$, the $x$'s commute, the $y$'s commute, and $W$ acts by its given linear action.

[F2] [[lem-rational-cherednik-commutator-jacobi-compatibility]]: the commutator parameter $\kappa$ is $W$-equivariant and satisfies cyclic Jacobi compatibility.

[F3] [[thm-rational-cherednik-pbw-theorem]]: $H_{1,c}$ has vector-space basis $\mathbb C[\mathfrak h^*]\otimes\mathbb C W\otimes\mathbb C[\mathfrak h]$; the induced module $P_c$ has polynomial basis generated by $1$.

[F4] [[def-rational-cherednik-reflection-data-and-parameters]]: each reflection fixes the hyperplane $\ker\alpha_s$, and $x-sx=\alpha_s^\vee(x)\alpha_s$ for $x\in\mathfrak h^*$.

[F5] [[def-rational-cherednik-polynomial-representation]]: $P_c(W,\mathfrak h)$ is $\Delta_c(\mathbf1)$, with $x$ acting by multiplication and $1$ killed by every $y\in\mathfrak h$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** [F1]–[F5].

1.1 For $f\in\mathbb C[\mathfrak h^*]$ and $s\in S$, the polynomial $f-sf$ vanishes on $\ker\alpha_s$, since $s^{-1}$ fixes every point of that hyperplane. The ideal of this hyperplane is generated by $\alpha_s$, so $(1-s)f/\alpha_s$ is a polynomial. Thus every displayed $y$-operator preserves $\mathbb C[\mathfrak h^*]$. [F4, construct, algebra]

1.2 Let $R_s=\alpha_s^{-1}(1-s)$ on the rational function field of $\mathfrak h$. For multiplication by $x\in\mathfrak h^*$, $[R_s,x]=\alpha_s^{-1}(x-sx)s=\alpha_s^\vee(x)s$ by [F4]. Using $[\partial_y,x]=x(y)$ therefore gives $[\partial_y-\sum_s c_s\alpha_s(y)R_s,x]=x(y)1-\sum_s c_s\alpha_s(y)\alpha_s^\vee(x)s$, exactly [F1]. The $x$'s commute, and conjugation by $w$ sends the operator for $y$ to that for $wy$ because $c$ and the reflection forms are equivariant. [F1, F2, F4, algebra]

2.1 Write $T_y=\partial_y-\sum_s c_s\alpha_s(y)R_s$. The commutator $[T_y,T_z]$ is a differential-reflection operator on $\mathfrak h_{\rm reg}$, the complement of the reflection hyperplanes. By step 1.2 and Jacobi, $\operatorname{ad}_{[T_y,T_z]}(x)=[T_y,\kappa(z,x)]-[T_z,\kappa(y,x)]=[T_y,\kappa(z,x)]+[T_z,\kappa(x,y)]=0$, since $\kappa(y,z)=0$ for $y,z\in\mathfrak h$ and [F2] applies. The commutator with a group element uses the same formula as in [F1], by the $W$-covariance just checked. [F1, F2, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 In the crossed differential-operator algebra $\mathcal D(\mathfrak h_{\rm reg})\rtimes W=\bigoplus_{w\in W}\mathcal D(\mathfrak h_{\rm reg})w$, the centralizer of all multiplication operators by $x\in\mathfrak h^*$ consists of multiplication by rational functions. Indeed, for a nonidentity $w$, choose $x$ with $wx\ne x$; the top differential-order term of $[D w,x]$ is the top term of $D$ multiplied by $wx-x$, so commutation forces $D=0$. For $w=1$, a differential operator commuting with every coordinate function has order zero, since the commutator of a positive-order operator with a coordinate has nonzero top symbol in some coordinate direction. Thus [T_y,T_z], which commutes with all $x$ by step 2.1, is multiplication by a rational function. Every $T_y$ kills $1$, so $[T_y,T_z]1=0$, and that multiplication function is zero. Hence the $T_y$ commute. [step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The assignments $x\mapsto m_x$, $w\mapsto w$, and $y\mapsto T_y$ now satisfy every relation in [F1]: the $x$'s commute, the $y$'s commute by step 3.1, the mixed commutator is step 1.2, and the $W$-action relation is the covariance in step 1.2. The quotient universal property therefore gives an $H_{1,c}$-module structure on $\mathbb C[\mathfrak h^*]$. [F1, step 1.2, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 The constant polynomial is killed by every $T_y$, and $W$ fixes it, so induction gives an $H_{1,c}$-module map $P_c=\Delta_c(\mathbf1)\to\mathbb C[\mathfrak h^*]$ sending $1\otimes1$ to $1$. It is surjective because multiplication by $\mathfrak h^*$ generates all polynomials. By PBW [F3], both spaces have the same monomial basis and this map is the identity on that basis, hence is an isomorphism. This is the asserted polynomial representation and action. [F3, F5, step 4.1, construct] ∎
