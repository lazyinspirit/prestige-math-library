## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $A$ be a complex C\*-algebra, and let $B$ be
$A$ itself when $A$ is unital and the minimal unitization $A^+$ otherwise;
spectra of elements of $A$ are computed in $B$
([[thm-minimal-c-star-unitization]],
[[def-spectrum-and-resolvent-set-in-a-banach-algebra]]). Then:

1. every self-adjoint $h\in B$ has real spectrum and a continuous functional
   calculus: for every $f\in C(\sigma(h))$ there is an element
   $f(h)\in C^*(1,h)\subseteq B$ with $\|f(h)\|=\sup_{\lambda\in\sigma(h)}|f(\lambda)|$
   and $\sigma(f(h))=f(\sigma(h))$, the assignment $f\mapsto f(h)$ is a unital
   $\ast$-homomorphism extending the polynomial calculus;
2. the algebraically positive elements $a^*a$ $(a\in B)$ are exactly the
   self-adjoint elements with nonnegative spectrum: with
   $P=\{h=h^*:\sigma(h)\subseteq[0,\infty)\}$ one has $P=\{a^*a:a\in B\}$, and
   $P$ is a closed convex cone with $P\cap(-P)=\{0\}$;
3. writing $a\le b$ for $b-a\in P$, conjugation preserves positivity and order,
   $x^*ax\in P$ whenever $a\in P$, and $0\le a\le b$ implies
   $\|a\|\le\|b\|$;
4. every star-homomorphism between C\*-algebras is contractive; a unital
   star-homomorphism $\varphi$ is natural for the calculus on normal elements,
   $\varphi(f(a))=f(\varphi(a))$; and if $f$ vanishes at $0$ while $h$ lies in
   a nonunital $A$, then $f(h)\in A$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; a complex C\*-algebra $A$ with ambient unital C\*-algebra $B$
($B=A$ if $A$ is unital, $B=A^+$ otherwise); the algebraic notion $a=b^*b$ of
positivity; the convention that spectra of elements of $A$ are computed in
$B$.

[F1] $A$ and $B$ are C\*-algebras with $\|a^*a\|=\|a\|^2$; when $A$ is
nonunital, $A^+$ is a unital C\*-algebra containing $A$ as a closed two-sided
$\ast$-ideal of codimension one, its norm extends the norm of $A$, and a
star-homomorphism between C\*-algebras is a bounded complex-linear map
preserving products and the involution, not required to be unital
([[def-c-star-algebra]], [[thm-minimal-c-star-unitization]],
[[def-unital-banach-algebra]],
[[def-self-adjoint-positive-unitary-and-normal-elements-of-a-c-star-algebra]]).

[F2] For a normal $h$ in the unital C\*-algebra $B$, the closed
$\ast$-subalgebra $C^*(1,h)$ generated by $1$ and $h$ is a nonzero commutative
unital C\*-algebra, and the Gelfand transform $\Gamma$ is an isometric unital
$\ast$-isomorphism onto $C(\Delta(C^*(1,h)))$
([[thm-commutative-gelfand-naimark]]).

[F3] In a commutative unital Banach algebra the spectrum of an element is the
set of its character values, and in a unital C\*-algebra spectra are permanent
under passing to a unital C\*-subalgebra with the same identity
([[thm-spectrum-as-character-values]],
[[lem-spectral-permanence-for-unital-c-star-subalgebras]]).

[F4] In a unital C\*-algebra the spectral radius satisfies
$r(x)=\lim_n\|x^n\|^{1/n}$, and $r(x)=\|x\|$ for normal $x$
([[def-spectral-radius]], [[thm-spectral-radius-formula]],
[[lem-c-star-spectral-radius-equals-norm-for-normal-elements]]).

[F5] A point-separating self-adjoint complex function algebra containing the
constants on a compact Hausdorff space is uniformly dense
([[thm-complex-stone-weierstrass-self-adjoint]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** AC and a complex C\*-algebra $A$ with ambient unital C\*-algebra $B$ as in the statement.

1.1 Let $a\in B$ be normal. Then $C:=C^*(1,a)$ is commutative, since $a$ commutes with $a^*$, and unital. By [F2], its Gelfand transform $\Gamma:C\to C(\Delta)$ is an isometric unital $\ast$-isomorphism. By [F3], the range of $\Gamma(a)$ is $\sigma_B(a)$. For $f\in C(\sigma_B(a))$ define $f(a):=\Gamma^{-1}(f\circ\Gamma(a))$. This is a unital $\ast$-homomorphism in $f$, extends polynomials in $a,a^*$, and satisfies $\|f(a)\|=\sup_{\sigma(a)}|f|$ and $\sigma_B(f(a))=f(\sigma_B(a))$ by [F3]. If $a=h=h^*$ then $\Gamma(h)$ is real-valued, so $\sigma_B(h)\subseteq\mathbb R$, and this construction gives the stated self-adjoint calculus. [F1, F2, F3]. [F2, F3, F1]

1.2 For $u,v\in B$, put $c:=(1-vu)^{-1}$ when this inverse exists and $d:=1+ucv$. Then $(1-uv)d=1+u(c-1-vuc)v=1$ because $(1-vu)c=1$, and $d(1-uv)=1+u(c-1-cvu)v=1$ because $c(1-vu)=1$. Thus $1-vu$ invertible implies $1-uv$ invertible. Interchanging $u,v$ and replacing $u$ by $u/\lambda$ gives $\sigma(uv)\setminus\{0\}=\sigma(vu)\setminus\{0\}$. [F1, algebra]

1.3 Every bounded star-homomorphism $\varphi:E\to F$ between C\*-algebras is contractive. If $\varphi=0$ this is immediate. Otherwise let $M>0$ bound $\varphi$. For a self-adjoint $b\in E$, multiplicativity gives $\|\varphi(b)^n\|\le M\|b^n\|$. Taking $n$th roots and using the spectral-radius formula [F4] gives $r(\varphi(b))\le r(b)$. Both $b$ and $\varphi(b)$ are normal, so [F4] yields $\|\varphi(b)\|\le\|b\|$. Apply this to $b=a^*a$ and use the C\*-identity: $\|\varphi(a)\|^2=\|\varphi(a)^*\varphi(a)\|=\|\varphi(a^*a)\|\le\|a^*a\|=\|a\|^2$. The spectral radii for nonunital algebras are computed in their minimal unitizations, whose norms restrict to the original norms; the powers used in the formula lie in the original algebra. [F4, F1, algebra]

2.1 Put $P:=\{h\in B:h=h^*,\ \sigma(h)\subseteq[0,\infty)\}$. For a self-adjoint $h\in B$, one has $h\in P$ if and only if $\|t1-h\|\le t$ for some (equivalently, every) real $t\ge\|h\|$; in particular, $h\in P$ if and only if $\|\|h\|1-h\|\le\|h\|$. Indeed, step 1.1 and [F4] give $\|t1-h\|=\max_{\lambda\in\sigma(h)}|t-\lambda|$. If $h\in P$ and $t\ge\|h\|$, every spectral value lies in $[0,t]$, so this maximum is at most $t$. Conversely, a negative spectral value $\lambda$ gives $|t-\lambda|=t-\lambda>t$ for every such $t$. Nonnegative scalar multiples preserve $P$ by step 1.1. If $h,k\in P$, choose $t>0$ with $t\ge\max(\|h\|,\|k\|)$ and put $w=(h+k)/(2t)$. Then $w=w^*$, $\|w\|\le1$, and $\|1-w\|\le(\|1-h/t\|+\|1-k/t\|)/2\le1$, so the criterion with parameter $1$ gives $w\in P$ and hence $h+k\in P$. If $h_n\in P$ and $h_n\to h$, continuity of the involution gives $h=h^*$, and $\|\|h\|1-h\|=\lim_n\|\|h_n\|1-h_n\|\le\lim_n\|h_n\|=\|h\|$, so $h\in P$. Finally, $h\in P\cap(-P)$ implies $\sigma(h)\subseteq\{0\}$, whence $\|h\|=r(h)=0$. Thus $P$ is a closed convex cone with $P\cap(-P)=\{0\}$. [F1, F4, step 1.1]

2.2 Let $\varphi:E\to F$ be a unital star-homomorphism of unital C\*-algebras and let $a\in E$ be normal. Then $\sigma_F(\varphi(a))\subseteq\sigma_E(a)$: invertibility of $a-\lambda1$ implies invertibility of $\varphi(a)-\lambda1=\varphi(a-\lambda1)$ with inverse $\varphi((a-\lambda1)^{-1})$. Moreover $\varphi(f(a))=f(\varphi(a))$ for every $f\in C(\sigma_E(a))$: choose polynomials $p_n(z,\bar z)$ with $\|p_n-f\|_{\infty,\sigma_E(a)}\to0$, possible because the polynomials in $z$ and $\bar z$ form a point-separating self-adjoint unital complex function algebra on the compact set $\sigma_E(a)\subseteq\mathbb C$ and [F5] applies; then $\varphi(p_n(a))=p_n(\varphi(a))$ by multiplicativity, star preservation and unitality, while $\|\varphi(f(a)-p_n(a))\|\le M\|f-p_n\|_{\infty,\sigma_E(a)}$ for the bound $M$ of the bounded map $\varphi$, and $\|p_n(\varphi(a))-f(\varphi(a))\|=\sup_{\lambda\in\sigma_F(\varphi(a))}|p_n(\lambda)-f(\lambda)|\le\|p_n-f\|_{\infty,\sigma_E(a)}$ by step 1.1 and the spectral inclusion just proved. [F1, F5, step 1.1]. [F5, F1, step 1.1]

2.3 Suppose $A$ is nonunital, $h=h^*\in A$ and $f\in C(\sigma(h))$ vanishes at $0$. Then $f(h)\in A$. First $0\in\sigma_B(h)$: an element of the proper two-sided ideal $A$ of the unital algebra $B=A^+$ cannot be invertible, since an invertible element generates the unit ideal. Given $\epsilon>0$, use [F5] to choose a polynomial $q$ with $\|q-f\|_{\infty,\sigma(h)}<\epsilon/2$; then $p:=q-q(0)$ satisfies $p(0)=0$ and $\|p-f\|_{\infty,\sigma(h)}\le\|q-f\|_{\infty,\sigma(h)}+|q(0)-f(0)|<\epsilon$, since $f(0)=0$ and $|q(0)-f(0)|=|q(0)|\le\|q-f\|_{\infty,\sigma(h)}$. Every such $p$ is a finite combination of powers $h^k$ with $k\ge1$, so $p(h)\in A$; by step 1.1, $\|f(h)-p(h)\|=\|p-f\|_{\infty,\sigma(h)}<\epsilon$, and $A$ is closed in $B$. [F1, F5, step 1.1]. [F5, F1, step 1.1]

3.1 For every $a\in B$ one has $a^*a\in P$. Put $c:=a^*a=c^*$ and let $c_+:=\max(\cdot,0)(c)$ and $c_-:=\max(-\cdot,0)(c)$ be given by the calculus of step 1.1; then $c=c_+-c_-$ and $c_+c_-=0$, while $c_+,c_-\in P$ by the spectral image formula of step 1.1. Put $d:=ac_-$. Then $d^*d=c_-a^*ac_-=c_-(c_+-c_-)c_-=-c_-^3$, and $d^*d+dd^*=2x^2+2y^2=2(x^2+y^2)$, where $d=x+iy$ is the decomposition of $d$ into self-adjoint parts, so $d^*d+dd^*\in P$: indeed $x^2$ and $y^2$ lie in $P$ because their spectra are squares of the real spectra of $x$ and $y$ by step 1.1, and $P$ is a cone by step 2.1. Since $-d^*d=c_-^3\in P$, adding gives $dd^*\in P$; step 1.2 applied to $(u,v)=(d^*,d)$ shows $\sigma(d^*d)\setminus\{0\}=\sigma(dd^*)\setminus\{0\}\subseteq[0,\infty)$, so $d^*d\in P$ as well. Then $c_-^3=-d^*d\in P\cap(-P)=\{0\}$ by step 2.1; the calculus gives $\sigma(c_-)^3=\sigma(c_-^3)=\{0\}$, hence $\sigma(c_-)=\{0\}$ and $\|c_-\|=r(c_-)=0$ by [F4], so $c_-=0$ and $c=c_+\in P$. [F1, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1]. [F4, F1, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1]

4.1 Every $h\in P$ is a square: the continuous function $\sqrt{\cdot}$ on $\sigma(h)\subseteq[0,\infty)$ has $\sqrt{\cdot}(h)$ self-adjoint by step 1.1 and $\sqrt{\cdot}(h)^2=h$. Hence $P\subseteq\{b^*b:b\in B\}$, and step 3.1 gives the reverse inclusion, so $P=\{a^*a:a\in B\}$ is exactly the set of algebraically positive elements. [step 1.1, step 3.1]. [step 1.1, step 3.1]

5.1 If $a\in P$ and $x\in B$, then $x^*ax\in P$: by step 4.1 write $a=y^2$ with $y=y^*$, so that $x^*ax=(yx)^*(yx)\in P$ by step 3.1. In particular the relation $\le$, defined by $a\le b$ iff $b-a\in P$, is compatible with conjugation, and it is reflexive and transitive because $P$ is a cone. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1]. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1]

6.1 If $0\le a\le b$ then $\|a\|\le\|b\|$. For $\delta>0$ the element $b+\delta1$ has spectrum in $[\delta,\infty)$, so it is invertible in $B$; with $z:=(b+\delta1)^{1/2}$ given by step 1.1 and $z^{-1}$ both self-adjoint, put $c:=z^{-1}az^{-1}$. Then $c\in P$ by step 5.1, and $1-c=z^{-1}(b+\delta1-a)z^{-1}\in P$ because $b-a\in P$ and $\delta1\in P$; hence $\sigma(c)\subseteq[0,1]$ by step 2.1, so $\|c\|=r(c)\le1$ by [F4]. Since $a=zcz$, the C\*-identity and [F4] give $\|a\|\le\|z\|^2\|c\|\le\|b+\delta1\|\le\|b\|+\delta$; letting $\delta\downarrow0$ yields $\|a\|\le\|b\|$. [F1, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 5.1]. [F4, F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 5.1]

7.1 AC is inherited from the unitization, Gelfand–Naimark, Stone–Weierstrass and spectral-radius suppliers of [F1]–[F5]; the cone, positivity, order and naturality arguments use no further choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). [F1, F5, given, F4] ∎
