## Statement

For the contract of
[[def-counting-process-intensity-and-compensator-contract]], let
$\mathcal P_T$ be the predictable sigma-algebra on $[0,T]\times\Omega$ and
define the finite measure
$$\mu(E):=\int_EY_s(\omega)\lambda_s(\omega)\,(ds\times P)(d(s,\omega)),\qquad E\in\mathcal P_T.$$
Then bounded predictable step processes are dense in $L^2(\mu)$. The time-zero
slice has $\mu$-measure zero.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite-horizon predictable process $Y\lambda$, its compensator $A_T=\int_0^T Y_s\lambda_s ds$ with $\mathbb E A_T<\infty$, and the predictable sigma-algebra convention.

[F1] The predictable sigma-algebra is generated by time-zero sets and rectangles $(s,t]\times B$ with $B\in\mathcal F_s$, and is contained in the product sigma-algebra. ([[def-progressively-measurable-and-predictable-process]])

[F2] The product measure and Tonelli theorem identify $\int Y\lambda\,d(ds\times P)=\mathbb E A_T$ and give measurability of the section integrals. ([[def-product-measure-on-sigma-finite-spaces]] [[thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces]])

[F3] A pi-system contained in a lambda-system generates only sets in that lambda-system. ([[thm-dynkin-pi-lambda]])

[F4] Finite-measure simple functions are dense in $L^2(\mu)$. ([[thm-simple-functions-with-finite-measure-support-are-dense-in-l-p-for-finite-p]])

[F5] Increasing nonnegative integrals converge to the integral of the limit. ([[thm-monotone-convergence-for-the-integral]])

[A1] AC is inherited through the L² simple-density interface, which supplies the approximating sequence used in step 3.1. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[thm-simple-functions-with-finite-measure-support-are-dense-in-l-p-for-finite-p]])

## Proof

1.1 Put $f(s,\omega)=Y_s(\omega)\lambda_s(\omega)$. By [F1], $f$ is product-measurable, so [F2] defines $\mu$ and gives $\mu([0,T]\times\Omega)=\int f\,d(ds\times P)=\mathbb E\int_0^T f(s,\omega)ds=\mathbb E A_T<\infty$. Countable additivity follows from the product measure; [F5] also gives it by monotone convergence on disjoint unions. Lebesgue measure gives $\mu(\{0\}\times B)=0$ for every $B\in\mathcal F_0$. [F1, F2, F5, given]

2.1 Let $\mathcal G$ contain the empty set, whole space, time-zero generators, and interval rectangles from [F1]. Their finite intersections are empty or generators, so $\mathcal G$ is a $\pi$-system generating $\mathcal P_T$. Let $\mathcal D$ be the sets whose indicators lie in the $L^2(\mu)$-closure of the span of generator indicators. The whole space is in $\mathcal D$ because $\mathbf1_{(0,T]\times\Omega}$ is an elementary step and the time-zero slice is null. Complements preserve $\mathcal D$ by subtraction from $\mathbf1$; disjoint countable unions preserve it because finite partial sums converge in $L^2(\mu)$, with squared tail norm the measure of a decreasing tail tending to zero as $\mu$ is finite. Thus $\mathcal D$ is a $\lambda$-system containing $\mathcal G$, and [F3] gives $\mathcal D=\mathcal P_T$. [F1, F3, step 1.1, given, construct]

3.1 By [F4], every $L^2(\mu)$ class is an $L^2$ limit of finite simple functions on measurable sets. Each indicator in such a simple function belongs to the closure from step 2.1, so each simple function does as well; hence the span of bounded predictable step indicators is dense in $L^2(\mu)$. [A1, F4, step 2.1] ∎
## Source notes

Bass states the $L^2$ approximation of predictable integrands by bounded step
integrands. The proof above supplies the density argument for this precise
predictable sigma-algebra and finite compensator measure using its interval
generators.
