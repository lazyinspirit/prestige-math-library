Asymptotic distributions: Delta method
(See vdV3.)
We have an estimator Tn, and we know Tn
P→θ and √n(Tn −θ) ⇝Z.
Suppose we are interested in the saymptotics of φ(Tn).
The continuous mapping theorem implies that if φ is continuous then
φ(Tn)
P→φ(θ). The delta method gives its asymptotic distribution.

Delta method
Theorem: If φ : Rk →Rm is differentiable at θ,
and √n(Tn −θ) ⇝T, then
√n(φ(Tn) −φ(θ)) ⇝φ′
θ(T)
√n(φ(Tn) −φ(θ)) −φ′
θ(√n(Tn −θ))
P→0.
Here, φ′
θ is the derivative (linear map) satisfying
φ(θ + h) −φ(θ) = φ′
θ(h) + o(∥h∥)
for h →0.

===== PDF PAGE 11 =====
Delta method
Proof:
Consider the remainder function
R(h) = φ(θ + h) −φ(θ) −φ′
θ(h).
Differentiability implies R(h) = o(∥h∥) as h →0. But √n(Tn −θ)
converges in distribution, so it is uniformly tight and since √n →∞,
Tn −θ
P→0. So we can substitute h = Tn −θ and get
R(Tn −θ) = oP (∥Tn −θ∥).
But
R(Tn −θ) = φ(Tn) −φ(θ) −φ′
θ(Tn −θ).

Delta method
Thus,
√n(φ(Tn) −φ(θ)) = φ′
θ(√n(Tn −θ)) + √noP (∥Tn −θ∥).
But √noP (∥Tn −θ∥) = oP (√n∥Tn −θ∥) = oP (1).
This shows that
√n(φ(Tn) −φ(θ)) −φ′
θ(√n(Tn −θ))
P→0.
The continuous mapping theorem and Slutsky’s lemma shows that
√n(φ(Tn) −φ(θ)) ⇝φ′
θ(T).
