## Repairs and evidence

1. **`items/lem-polynomial-algebras-over-fields-are-integrally-closed.md`, step 1.2 and step 4.1.** The old content product ran over irreducible elements rather than associate classes. In a UFD, associate representatives are not unique and can be infinite in number, so that product was not a finite or representative-independent construction. Content is now defined using the finite-support valuations on associate classes and a finite choice of representatives; its associate class is shown independent of those choices. Step 4.1 now uses the same class valuations and handles zero factors before applying the content-product identity.

2. **`items/lem-zmt-one-variable-integral-correction.md`, steps 1.2, 4.1, 5.1, and 6.1.** A corrected element `t'` can be nilpotent even when `t` is not; the proof now closes the monic case immediately in that branch before using the nonzero localization. The transitivity argument now places `t'` in `B[t']` and proves `A → B[t']` integral, instead of applying the finite-module criterion to `t'` as though it belonged to `B`. Coefficients in the subring generated by `φ(R)` and `1/t'` are now written as finite sums of denominator terms; the common-denominator equation and the subsequent kernel-criterion calculation in step 6.1 use all those terms. This matches the explicit finite-sum form and clearing step in [Stacks Project, Lemma 10.123.2, tag 00PI, lines 27–32](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00PI#L27).

3. **`items/lem-zmt-quasi-finite-transfer-through-intermediate-rings.md`, steps 1.4, 2.3, 3.3, and 4.3.** The old assertion that the base-changed local fibre was generated by images of a basis overlooked the inverses introduced by localization. The proof now identifies that local fibre as a localization of the scalar extension of the original finite-dimensional local fibre. Scalar extension preserves finite dimension; localization of a finite-dimensional algebra at a prime remains finite-dimensional, proved by stabilization of the descending principal-ideal chain. Added `lem-tensor-ring-presentations-for-base-change` as a dependency and fact [L9] for the localization/base-change identification. The independent source check is [Stacks Project, Lemma 10.122.7, tag 00PN, lines 24–31](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00PN#L24), whose proof follows the finite part of the fibre through base change.

4. **`items/lem-zmt-conductor-radical-coefficients.md`, step 5.1.** From `u^N φ(a_k)^(Nm) ∈ J` the old text asserted `(u φ(a_k))^(Nm) ∈ J`, which has a different power of `u`. For `m≥1`, the proof now multiplies by `u^(N(m−1))`; for `m=0`, it multiplies `u^N∈J` by `φ(a_k)^N`. It also uses the coefficient at `x^(Nk)` without claiming that `P^N` has degree `Nk`, so zero leading coefficients and `P=0` are included. Compare [Stacks Project, Lemma 10.123.6, tag 00PI, lines 45–46](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00PI#L45); the repaired local argument spells out the power adjustment.

5. **`items/lem-strongly-transcendental-finite-one-variable-algebra-is-nowhere-quasi-finite.md`, steps 2.1 and 3.2.** The normal-domain argument now states the integrally closed-domain hypothesis before using going down over `R[x]`. In the general domain case, `Frac(overline R)=K` follows from `R⊆overline R⊆K` and `K=Frac(R)`; the proof now states this equality and records the denominator-clearing consequence of strong transcendence. The organization agrees with [Stacks Project, Lemma 10.123.9, tag 00PI, lines 54–59](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00PI#L54), which treats the normal base first and then passes to the integral closure. The now-unused transitivity-of-algebraicity dependency was removed; the remaining transitivity-of-integrality fact was renumbered in the item and proof contract.

6. **`items/ex-zariski-main-open-immersion-punctured-affine-line.md`, step 4.1.** The image of `R\p` in `R_t` is not literally every element outside `q` (for example, `1/t` need not come from `R`). The proof now uses the valid localization argument: if `r/t^n` lies outside `q`, then `r` lies outside `p`, so it becomes a unit after localizing by `R\p`; conversely, all elements of `R\p` map outside `q`. The two localizations therefore have the same universal property.
