## Local regular-restriction lemma

Write r for the rho-function and R_H for the right regular representation. Obtain r and the Radon measure mu on X=G/H from [[thm-existence-of-rho-functions-and-quasi-invariant-measures-on-g-mod-h]] and [[thm-weil-quotient-integration-formula-with-rho-function]]. Fix f in C_c(G), S=supp(f), and put a_x(h)=[Delta_G((xh)^(-1))/r(xh)]^(1/2) f((xh)^(-1)). Its support is contained in x^(-1)S^(-1) intersect H, hence compact. Inversion and the Weil formula, applied to the continuous compactly supported integrand Delta_G(y^(-1)) f((yk)^(-1)) overline(f(y^(-1)))/r(y), yield c_f(k)=<lambda_G(k)f,f>=integral_X B(q,k)dmu(q), where B(xH,k)=<R_H(k)a_x,a_x>. Indeed r(xhk)/r(xh)=Delta_H(k)/Delta_G(k), so the square-root factors give precisely Delta_H(k)^(1/2) a_x(hk) overline(a_x(h)). For t in H, a_(xt)(h)=a_x(th); left Haar invariance therefore makes B independent of the representative x. The integrand is continuous in (x,k,h); on compact x-neighborhoods its h-support is confined to C^(-1)S^(-1) intersect H because of its second factor. Compact-kernel continuity gives continuity in (x,k), and openness of the quotient map gives a continuous function B on X times H. B vanishes off the compact T=p(S^(-1)), independently of k. For any compact Q in H, q->B(q,.)|Q is continuous into C(Q) in its sup norm (use a finite subcover of Q for joint continuity). Cover T by finitely many neighborhoods on which this map oscillates by less than delta, take the resulting disjoint Borel partition E_j of T, pick q_j in each nonempty E_j, and lift q_j to x_j. Radon compact finiteness gives mu(T)<infinity, and sup_Q|integral_T B(q,k)dmu - sum_j mu(E_j)B(q_j,k)| <= delta mu(T). If mu(T)=0 use the zero coefficient; otherwise choose delta so this is below the desired tolerance. The sum consists of right-regular diagonal coefficients at eta_j=sqrt(mu(E_j))a_(x_j). Convert them to left-regular coefficients locally: Jb(h)=Delta_H(h)^(-1/2)b(h^(-1)) is an isometry on C_c(H) by [[lem-haar-change-of-variables-under-inversion]], satisfies J^2=I and J lambda_H(k)=R_H(k)J, and extends unitarily by [[lem-complex-haar-l1-and-l2-are-complete-and-cc-dense]]. Thus use J eta_j for lambda_H. Finally approximate any xi in L2(G) by f in C_c(G); unitarity and Cauchy-Schwarz give sup_(k in H)|c_xi(k)-c_f(k)| <= (||xi||_2+||f||_2)||xi-f||_2. This proves the exact finite-sum coefficient definition [[def-weak-containment-of-unitary-representations]]. All choices and supplier choice hypotheses are covered by AC; no measurable section, countability, direct integral, or global sigma-finite product theorem is used.

The local lemma's statement is: assume AC; for any LCH G and closed H, lambda_G restricted to H is weakly contained in lambda_H in the existing finite-sum diagonal-coefficient sense. ## Complete three-clause theorem route

The statement must start with arbitrary LCH G, assume AC, and make amenability of G conditional in (i),(ii); (iii) assumes only amenability of closed normal N and G/N. The current blanket opening “Let G be amenable” makes (iii) vacuous and is replaced with the source-strength extension statement.

Use the repaired pointwise UCB(G), identified isometrically with its L-infinity subspace. An invariant mean on L-infinity restricts to UCB; conversely the local invariant-mean-to-Reiter and Reiter-to-mean lemmas give amenability from an invariant UCB mean, with all AC assumptions carried. (i) By [[thm-hulanicki-weak-containment-criterion-for-amenability]], amenability gives 1_G prec lambda_G. Every compact subset Q of closed H is compact in G, so restriction of the coefficient approximations gives 1_H prec lambda_G|H. The new [[lem-restricted-regular-representation-is-weakly-contained-in-subgroup-regular-representation]] and transitivity proved in [[def-weak-containment-of-unitary-representations]] give 1_H prec lambda_H. Apply the same Hulanicki theorem to the LCH group H. (ii) If pi:G->G/N is the open quotient map, pullback is an isometric positive unital embedding UCB(G/N)->UCB(G), and L_g(psi o pi)=(L_(gN)psi) o pi. Restrict the G-invariant mean along it to obtain a G/N-invariant UCB mean, hence amenability. (iii) For phi in UCB(G) set phi_g(h)=phi(gh) on N and F_phi(g)=m_N(phi_g). For fixed g, ||L_n phi_g-phi_g||_sup <= ||L_(gng^(-1))phi-phi||_sup->0 as n->e in N. Thus phi_g is in UCB(N); the conjugation must be included. Invariance of m_N gives F_phi(gn)=F_phi(g), since phi_(gn)(h)=phi_g(nh)=L_(n^(-1))phi_g(h). Moreover ||L_gamma F_phi-F_phi||_sup <= ||L_gamma phi-phi||_sup->0, so F_phi is continuous and UCB on G and descends through the open pi to psi_phi on G/N. To prove quotient UCB, for any small-error identity neighborhood V of G its image pi(V) is an identity neighborhood of G/N, and each coset in pi(V) has a representative gamma in V; the same bound then holds for L_(gamma N)psi_phi-psi_phi uniformly. Define m(phi)=m_(G/N)(psi_phi). Linearity, positivity and m(1)=1 follow from those of the two means. F_(L_gamma phi)=L_gamma F_phi implies psi_(L_gamma phi)=L_(gamma N)psi_phi, hence m is G-invariant and G is amenable. This proves all clauses in full LCH generality with nets.

The original extension bound omitted conjugation: for the N-function `phi_g(h)=phi(gh)`, its translate is controlled by `L_(gng^(-1))phi`, not `L_n phi`. Continuity of conjugation for each fixed g closes this without any uniform conjugation assumption. The quotient uniform-continuity proof uses openness of pi and small representatives explicitly.
