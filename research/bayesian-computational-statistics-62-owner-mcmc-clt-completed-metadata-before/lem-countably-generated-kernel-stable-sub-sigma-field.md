## Statement

Let $P$ be a probability kernel on an arbitrary measurable space $(X,\mathcal F)$, and let $f:X\to\mathbb R$ be measurable. There is a countably generated sub-sigma-field $\mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal F$ such that $f$ is $\mathcal G$-measurable and $P$, restricted to events in $\mathcal G$, is a probability kernel $P_{\mathcal G}$ on $(X,\mathcal G)$. For every $n\ge0$, $x\in X$, and $B\in\mathcal G$,
$$P_{\mathcal G}^n(x,B)=P^n(x,B).$$
If $\pi$ is invariant for $P$, then $\pi|_{\mathcal G}$ is invariant for $P_{\mathcal G}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The measurable space, probability kernel, and real measurable function in the Statement.

[F1] [[def-measure-kernel-and-probability-kernel]]: $P(x,\cdot)$ is a probability and $x\mapsto P(x,B)$ is measurable for every $B\in\mathcal F$.

[F2] [[thm-dynkin-pi-lambda]]: a lambda-system containing a pi-system contains the sigma-field it generates.

[F3] [[thm-monotone-convergence-for-the-integral]]: increasing nonnegative integrands have increasing integrals with the expected limit.

[F4] [[def-iterated-transition-kernels]]: iterated transitions are defined by kernel integration, with the identity kernel at time zero.

[F5] [[def-invariant-and-stationary-distribution-for-a-markov-kernel]]: invariance means $\int P(x,B)\pi(dx)=\pi(B)$ for every measurable $B$.

[F6] [[def-measurable-function-between-measurable-spaces]]: a real function is measurable when inverse images of Borel sets are measurable.

[F7] [[thm-nonnegative-measurable-functions-admit-increasing-simple-approximations]]: nonnegative measurable functions have increasing nonnegative simple approximations.

## Proof

**Given:** The hypotheses in the Statement.

1.1 Let $\mathcal A_0$ be the algebra generated by $\{\{f<q\}:q\in\mathbb Q\}$. Inductively, let $\mathcal A_{j+1}$ be the algebra generated by $\mathcal A_j$ and all sets $\{x:P(x,A)>q\}$ with $A\in\mathcal A_j$ and $q\in\mathbb Q$. These are subsets in $\mathcal F$ by [F1] and [F6]. Each algebra is countable: enumerate its generators and then all finite Boolean expressions in them. This also explicitly enumerates $\mathcal A=\bigcup_{j\ge0}\mathcal A_j$. Put $\mathcal G=\sigma(\mathcal A)$. Since rational half-lines generate the real Borel sigma-field, $f$ is $\mathcal G$-measurable. [F1, F6, construct, given]

1.2 For $A\in\mathcal A$, all rational superlevel sets of $P(\cdot,A)$ belong to $\mathcal G$ by construction. The class $\mathcal D$ of $B\in\mathcal G$ for which $P(\cdot,B)$ is $\mathcal G$-measurable contains $\mathcal A$ and $X$. It is closed under complements because $P(x,B^c)=1-P(x,B)$ and under countable disjoint unions because $P(x,\bigcup_lB_l)=\sum_lP(x,B_l)$ is a measurable increasing limit. Thus $\mathcal D$ is a lambda-system, and [F2] gives $\mathcal D=\mathcal G$. The restriction $P_{\mathcal G}$ therefore is a probability kernel. [F1, F2, step 1.1]

2.1 For every nonnegative $\mathcal G$-measurable $g$, integration against $P_{\mathcal G}(x,\cdot)$ agrees with integration against $P(x,\cdot)$: this holds for indicators by restriction, for nonnegative simple functions by finite additivity of the integral, and for general $g$ by [F7] and [F3]. Both integrals are allowed to be infinite. Induction in [F4], using this integration identity and step 1.2, gives $P_{\mathcal G}^n(x,B)=P^n(x,B)$ for every $B\in\mathcal G$, including $n=0$. [F3, F4, F7, step 1.2]

2.2 If $\pi$ is invariant, the same restriction identity for integrals gives $\int P_{\mathcal G}(x,B)(\pi|_{\mathcal G})(dx)=\int P(x,B)\pi(dx)=\pi(B)$ for $B\in\mathcal G$. This is invariance of $\pi|_{\mathcal G}$ by [F5]. All generating enumerations in step 1.1 were explicit, so this construction introduces no choice assumption. [F5, step 2.1, step 1.1] ∎
