## Logic proof audit and changes

1. Specify a Hilbert calculus over the published **set-coded** syntax, with tautology instances, quantifier instances and side conditions, equality substitution, MP and generalization. Theories are sets of **sentences**, not automatically deductively closed; domains are nonempty. The primitive connective/quantifier conventions remain those of SET-1. Soundness works for every set-sized signature in ZF.
2. The deduction theorem has its sentence/free-variable restriction. Its generalization case cannot be justified by propositional reasoning alone. The B counterexample uses a two-element structure to exhibit failure of unrestricted discharge.
3. Add a **syntactic** fresh-constant elimination lemma. The published bound-variable-renaming lemma proves semantic invariance; that is inadequate by itself to transform a derivation. The new lemma replaces constants by variables absent from the entire finite proof and checks every rule and side condition.
4. Add one-witness consistency, sentence decision, finite proof support, canonical countable syntax coding, and staged Henkin completion before constructing the quotient. Choices in the countable completion are least natural codes; consistency testing is definable but not claimed effective.
5. Prove equality congruence before interpreting the quotient, and Boolean/witness closure before the truth lemma. Add a seed constant even for an empty signature. Truth induction uses representatives only for a finite tuple, never a choice function on the full quotient. The truth lemma is stated for **any** complete consistent Henkin theory; countability is needed to obtain the choice-free completion, not for the lemma itself.
6. Countable-language compactness is a corollary of countable model existence and finite proof support. No arbitrary-language ZF compactness is inferred. The source Weiss–D’Mello explicitly assumes compactness, so it is independent evidence for the model-theoretic applications, not an independent proof of syntactic completeness.
7. Downward LS for an arbitrary ambient model requires selected witness functions or choice. Its stated cardinal version is ZFC and preserves a parameter set. Merely knowing that the language is countable does not canonically choose witnesses from an arbitrary domain.
8. Upward LS requires an expanded language with κ many fresh constants, even when the original language is countable. Add well-ordered-language Henkin completeness **with an explicit ZFC hypothesis and a cardinal bound** before using it. Add elementary diagrams and their embedding lemma before obtaining elementary extensions of the requested cardinality. A model of the elementary diagram yields an elementary map; a relabeling gives literal inclusion.
9. The nonstandard example uses the externally defined complete theory of `(ω,0,S,<)`. The infinite scheme says `n<c` for each standard numeral; the proof retains the distinction between a set satisfaction relation and an internal truth predicate.
10. Add the coded first-order ZF axiom theory and the lemma that all its set models have externally infinite domains. The coding uses the exact earlier axiom conventions: weak implication-form Power Set, functional-image Replacement, fresh output variables and universal parameter closure. Infinitude follows from the earlier displayed Infinity, Extensionality, Pairing and Foundation sentences without assuming the model is transitive or externally well-founded. Thus `fs-categorical-first-order-zf` is a conditional refutation under **ZFC + Con(ZF)**, not an assertion of Con(ZF).

## Tree proof audit and changes

1. Separate chains from maximal branches, strict tree order from reverse-extension poset order, and incomparability from incompatibility in a general poset. Define κ-trees with their level-size bound. Keep normality and splitting separate; limit-level predecessor uniqueness is explicit.
2. König’s lemma retains **finite** levels, with ZFC stated. Countable levels are insufficient; finite decreasing sequences of naturals give the B counterexample.
3. The rationally labeled Aronszajn construction needs a bound-preserving limit lemma. It is expanded to maintain infinitely many immediate successors below every larger rational bound. Enumerate the countably many `(node,bound)` requests; for request n avoid the finitely many previously chosen branches before continuing below an intermediate rational bound. This gives distinct predecessor branches at a limit level, so adding differently labeled tops does not violate normality. No cofinal branch can inject into the rationals.
4. Prove the finite delta-system lemma at a regular uncountable cardinal and its **indexed** ω1 version. Repeated supports require a separate constant-fibre case. Arbitrary countable sets are not substituted for finite sets: the nested countable ordinals provide the counterexample.
5. Prove finite and finite-support products of **Knaster** posets. Repeated coordinate projections require constant-fibre thinning. This is not an assertion that arbitrary ccc products are ccc.
6. Prove the finite-petals lemma using a cocountable ultrafilter, with the earlier proper-filter convention. The ultrafilter need not be countably complete. Finite primeness chooses a comparison index; intersections of selected large sets provide a node above two chosen heights, yielding the forbidden uncountable chain. This is the reason for the missing filters page edge.
7. For specialization, agree on the root **and** use cross-incomparable petals. Same-petal pairs and root-to-petal pairs are controlled by an original condition; only cross-petal pairs use the new lemma. Source Lemma16.37 omits distinctness in its displayed comparability condition and mishandles the final case in its prose. The scaffold requires `x<_T y`, never `p(x)≠p(x)`, and records the correct case split. A directed family meeting all dense domains yields a specializing function; ccc alone does not supply such a family.
8. Diamond guesses stationarily often; diamond implies CH by least matching indices beyond ω. Clubsuit is the stationary ladder-guessing principle. Square has an **order-type** bound, not merely a cardinality bound; its width-one definition is freshly harvested from Cummings–Magidor. The bound excludes threads. No `V=L ⇒ diamond` premise enters any proof.
9. The diamond-to-Suslin proof includes countable antichain sealing and a club of correct coding/maximality restrictions. The latter uses the already-published `thm-elementary-initial-segments-form-a-club`, whose language-size and regularity hypotheses apply to a countable-language structure on ω1. It does not require a new dependence on the current logic page. Zorn extends an arbitrary antichain to a maximal one before sealing is applied. Coding by consecutive countable blocks must be maintained during construction, rather than recoding afterward and assuming the diamond guesses survived.
10. For a normal splitting Suslin tree, pairs of two distinct immediate successors form an uncountable antichain in the square of its reverse-extension poset. The example retains diamond when using the constructed tree; a Suslin tree is not asserted to exist in ZFC alone.
11. Preserve Kurepa’s full line/tree equivalence as **non-load-bearing orientation**, with the full proof assigned by SET-17 to `suslin-trees-lines-algebras-and-independence`. Its normal-tree, branch-order, completion and nowhere-separable-core reductions still require full source/proof review. The complete rational-specialization/countable-antichain equivalence from Monk9.37 is likewise retained as an explicit downstream obligation; the current coloring definition does not pretend to prove that stronger rational-order statement.
12. Add a proved infinite Ramsey theorem for fixed positive finite arity and number of colors. Retain the general-cardinal Erdős–Rado target, but flag its unread source/proof route as fatal. The resolving ID of the proposed large-cardinal destination does not establish that the destination actually supplies the result.
13. “Finite-tree” in the design means a **finite number of infinite rooted finitely branching height-ω trees without terminal nodes**. Preserve Monk29.28’s exact dense-matrix dichotomy, distinguishing full products and level products. SET-21 owns the full proof; no unproved equivalence with a strong-subtree version is used here.
