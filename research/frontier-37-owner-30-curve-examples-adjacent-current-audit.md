### `cex-rational-map-singular-curve-not-extend-uniquely`

The branch-value obstruction is correct and cleanly separates existence from uniqueness. On `U=D(x)`, `t=y/x` is regular; the parametrization `ν#(x)=t²−1`, `ν#(y)=t(t²−1)` maps `t=1,-1` to the node. If an extension `ψ` existed, `ψ∘ν` and the coordinate map `τ:A¹→P¹` agree on the dense open `A¹\{1,-1}`. Separatedness and reducedness force equality everywhere, while the two preimages of the node would force `ψ(o)=1=-1`, contrary to `char(k)≠2`. Any two extensions agree on dense `U` and so are equal. This proves failure of existence, not uniqueness, over every field of characteristic not two.

Two proof obligations are currently implicit. First, `def-algebraic-curve-over-field` requires geometric integrality, but step 1.1 proves only that `A=k[x,y]/(y²−x²(x+1))` is a domain over `k`. For every field extension `K/k`, the valuation of `x²(x+1)` at `x+1` is odd, so it is not a square in `K(x)`; since `char(K)≠2`, `y²−x²(x+1)` remains irreducible over `K[x,y]`. This proves geometric integrality. Second, to justify “the smooth locus `U`,” note that with `t=y/x`, `A_x≅k[t,(t²−1)^{-1}]`, a localization of a polynomial ring and hence smooth; the node is singular by the already computed embedding dimension two versus local dimension one. The same parameterization is in fact the normalization: `t=y/x` in the function field satisfies the monic equation `t²=x+1`, `A[t]=k[t]` is finite and birational over `A`, and `k[t]` is normal. That identification is useful for the branch language, though the nonextension contradiction only needs the displayed morphism `ν`.

### `cex-degree-zero-line-bundle-no-section`
