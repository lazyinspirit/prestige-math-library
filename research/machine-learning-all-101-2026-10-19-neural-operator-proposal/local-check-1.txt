The direct modulus argument below also handles bounded measurable domains without an extra Jordan-boundary assumption.\n\n\n\n1.1 Write $I(v)=\\int_D\\kappa(\\cdot,y)v(y)\\,dy$ for one compatible Banach layer, and let $C$ be a compact subset of its input space $X$. Its image in $C(\\bar D)$ is compact: pulling an open cover back through the continuous inclusion and using compactness proves this directly. Thus $R=\\sup_{v\\in C}\\|v\\|_\\infty<\\infty$. This family has a common modulus of continuity tending to zero. Indeed a finite $\\eta$-net in the supremum norm and uniform continuity of its finitely many functions give $|v(y)-v(z)|\\le2\\eta+\\max_j|v_j(y)-v_j(z)|$ for every $v\\in C$. Uniform continuity of the finitely many functions follows by the compact-domain subsequence contradiction. Let $B=\\sup_y\\|\\kappa(\\cdot,y)\\|_{\\mathcal L(\\mathbb R^q,Y)}$ and let $\\omega_\\kappa$ be its norm modulus. Compactness similarly proves $B<\\infty$ and $\\omega_\\kappa(h)\\to0$. Therefore the $Y$-valued integrands $\\Phi_v(y)=\\kappa(\\cdot,y)v(y)$ have common modulus bounded by $R\\omega_\\kappa(h)+B\\omega_C(h)$. [given, F1, construct, algebra]\n\n2.1 A continuous $Y$-valued function $\\Phi$ on $\\bar D$ has an integral in $Y$: volume-weighted step sums on partitions of diameter $h$ form a Cauchy family, since comparing two sums on their common refinement bounds their difference by $|D|\\omega_\\Phi(h+h')$. Completeness of $Y$ gives a limit, independent of tags and partitions. Each point evaluation is a bounded functional on $Y$ by the continuous inclusion into $C(\\bar D')$, so applying it to the convergent sums identifies this limit with the pointwise scalar integral. Comparing a fixed tagged sum to refinements and taking the limit gives $\\|Q_n(v)-I(v)\\|_Y\\le |D|[R\\omega_\\kappa(h_n)+B\\omega_C(h_n)]$ uniformly for $v\\in C$. Also $\\|Q_n(v)-Q_n(w)\\|_Y\\le |D|B\\|v-w\\|_\\infty\\le |D|Bc_X\\|v-w\\|_X$, where $c_X$ is the embedding bound. These estimates use only measurable disjoint cells, their volumes summing to $|D|$, and their diameter bound. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]\n\n3.1 Suppose inductively that the discrete inputs $v_n(a)$ converge in $X$ to the continuum inputs $v(a)$ uniformly for $a$ in the original compact set $K$. The continuum map is continuous by the bound for $I$ from step 2.1 and continuity of all other maps, so $C=v(K)$ is compact. Comparing preactivations at $v_n(a)$ and $v(a)$, the quadrature Lipschitz bound and the bounded local-map norm give an upper bound $(|D|Bc_X+\\|W\\|)\\|v_n(a)-v(a)\\|_X+\\|Q_n(v(a))-I(v(a))\\|_Y$, which tends to zero uniformly in $a$. The continuum preactivations form a compact set $P\\subset Y$. A continuous map $T:Y\\to X'$ takes perturbations tending uniformly to zero around $P$ to uniformly vanishing output errors: for a prescribed $\\varepsilon$, cover $P$ by finitely many balls $B(p_i,r_i)$ on each of which $\\|T(z)-T(p_i)\\|<\\varepsilon/2$ for $\\|z-p_i\\|<3r_i$, and use a positive perturbation bound smaller than $\\min_i r_i$. If $p\\in B(p_i,r_i)$ and $\\|z-p\\|<\\min_i r_i$, both images are within $\\varepsilon/2$ of $T(p_i)$. Apply this to the activation. The same argument treats continuous lifting or projection maps. Starting with the identical input and proceeding through the finitely many stages proves uniform convergence in the final Banach norm. [step 2.1, given, construct, algebra]\n\n4.1 For the supremum-norm architecture take every relevant $X$ and $Y$ to be the continuous-function space with its supremum norm. Joint continuity of $\\kappa(x,y)$ on the compact product implies continuity of $y\\mapsto\\kappa(\\cdot,y)$ in that norm, by its uniform modulus on the product. Finite matrix local terms are bounded in that norm. For any fixed continuous scalar activation, its induced map on continuous functions is continuous in the supremum norm: near a function of norm $R$, all scalar arguments lie in $[-R-1,R+1]$, where the activation is uniformly continuous. Pointwise finite networks are finite compositions of these continuous maps. Thus all conditions in steps 1.1–2.1 hold, proving the first clause and then its continuously embedded input-space version. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, F1, F2, algebra] ∎\n\n\n\nFor example, kernels continuous in the output $C^r$ norm and norm-continuous local and nonlinear maps give convergence in $C^r$. The preceding arbitrary Banach formulation is useful beyond such examples. The stronger original assertion is retained verbatim in the proposal's archived preimage and is explicitly refuted by the separate one-neuron $C^1$ counterexample; neither an open-problem label nor an unqualified norm upgrade is warranted."

In particular fix a smooth $\\beta:\\mathbb R\\to[0,1]$, supported in $(-1,1)$, with $\\beta(0)=1$.\n\n[F2] A general integral-kernel neural operator and its volume-weighted tagged quadratures are defined in [[def-neural-operator]] and [[alg-neural-operator-mesh-discretization]].\n\n\n\n1.1 For integers $n\\ge2$ put $t_n=3\\cdot2^{-n-2}$, $w_n=2^{-4n-4}$, $H_n=2^{2n}$ and $\\rho_n(s)=H_n\\beta((s-t_n)/w_n)$. Their supports are disjoint subintervals of $(0,1)$: successive centers differ by $3\\cdot2^{-n-3}$, which exceeds $w_n+w_{n+1}$, and each radius is smaller than its center. Define $\\rho(s)=\\sum_{n\\ge2}\\rho_n(s)$ for $s>0$, and $\\rho(s)=0$ for $s\\le0$. On each compact subinterval of $(0,\\infty)$ only finitely many summands are nonzero. Moreover $m_n=\\int\\rho_n\\le2H_nw_n=2^{-2n-3}$, so $B=\\sum_{n\\ge2}m_n<1$. Define $\\sigma(s)=\\int_0^s\\rho(t)\\,dt$ for $s\\ge0$ and $\\sigma(s)=0$ for $s<0$. The tail mass bound proves continuity at zero and uniform convergence of the primitives $\\sigma_n(s)=\\int_0^s\\rho_n(t)\\,dt$; thus $\\sigma=\\sum\\sigma_n$ is continuous on $\\mathbb R$, bounded by $B$, and smooth on $(0,\\infty)$. It is a legitimate continuous activation. [given, F1, construct, algebra]\n\n2.1 Set $\\kappa(x,y)=\\sigma(x+y)$ and $G(a)(x)=\\int_0^1\\sigma(x+y)a(y)\\,dy$. The kernel is exactly a one-hidden-unit network on $(x,y)$ with weights $(1,1)$, activation $\\sigma$, output weight $1$, and zero biases. The other activations can be the identity. Each $G_n^*(a)(x)=\\int_0^1\\sigma_n(x+y)a(y)\\,dy$ is $C^1$, with derivative $\\int_0^1\\rho_n(x+y)a(y)\\,dy$, and both its function norm and derivative norm are at most $m_n\\|a\\|_\\infty$. Consequently both the function series and derivative series converge uniformly. To verify differentiation without an interchange assumption, the finite partial sums satisfy $S_N(x)-S_N(0)=\\int_0^x S_N'(t)\\,dt$; uniform convergence permits passage to the limit in this equality, and the fundamental theorem of calculus gives a continuous derivative. Hence $G:A\\to U$ is bounded linear, with $\\|G(a)\\|_U\\le2B\\|a\\|_\\infty$. Both $A$ and $U$ embed continuously into $C([0,1])$. [step 1.1, F2, construct, algebra]\n\n3.1 At refinement $n$ partition $(0,1)$ into $2^n$ dyadic cells of length $h_n=2^{-n}$. Choose the first tag to be $t_n\\in(0,h_n)$ and choose the midpoint of every other cell. Let $Q_n(a)(x)=h_n\\sum_{j=1}^{2^n}\\sigma(x+\\xi_{n,j})a(\\xi_{n,j})$. These are compatible tagged Riemann sums, with mesh diameter tending to zero and the same continuum parameters at every refinement. Since every tag is positive, each summand is smooth in a neighborhood of $[0,1]$, so $Q_n(a)\\in U$. For $a=1$, all derivative summands are nonnegative and $Q_n(1)'(0)\\ge h_n\\rho(t_n)=h_nH_n=2^n$. On the other hand, substitution in the continuum integral gives $G(1)(x)=\\int_x^{x+1}\\sigma(s)\\,ds$ and $G(1)'(0)=\\sigma(1)-\\sigma(0)=B$. Thus $\\|Q_n(1)-G(1)\\|_U\\ge2^n-B\\to\\infty$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, F2, construct, algebra]\n\n4.1 The set $K=\\{1\\}\\subset A$ is compact. The continuous integrand $(x,y)\\mapsto\\sigma(x+y)$ on $[0,1]^2$ is uniformly continuous: otherwise pairs at distances tending to zero with a fixed positive function gap have convergent subsequences in the compact square and contradict continuity. Writing its modulus of continuity as $\\omega$, the cellwise estimate gives $\\|Q_n(1)-G(1)\\|_\\infty\\le\\omega(h_n)\\to0$. Thus the genuine supremum-norm conclusion coexists with divergence in the admissible output norm, refuting the asserted inference. [step 3.1, step 1.1, algebra] ∎\n\n\n\nThe defect occurs in Appendix E's opening reduction from continuous embeddings to equality with $C(\\bar D)$. Such an embedding bounds the supremum norm by the Banach norm; it supplies no reverse inequality. This counterexample also uses a network kernel, rather than only an arbitrary continuous kernel. It refutes the specified volume-weighted tagged quadratures in the asserted norm; it does not assert that no other finite-dimensional approximation to this bounded integral operator can exist."
