### ML-31 — ReLU approximation rates, depth separation, and linear regions

**Pages:** relu-approximation-rates-and-depth-expressivity / relu-approximation-rates-and-depth-expressivity-examples. **Scope:** Define ReLU networks and connected activation-pattern regions. State separate results, not a single universal “depth helps” claim. Yarotsky’s target-independent rate: for the unit ball Wⁿ,∞([0,1]ᵈ), sup error ε is achieved with depth at most c(log(1/ε)+1) and at most c ε⁻ᵈ⁄ⁿ(log(1/ε)+1) weights and computation units, c=c(d,n). His target-dependent unit-Lipschitz one-dimensional construction instead uses depth 6 and at most c/[ε log(1/ε)] weights/connections/units. Keep the parameter-selection conditions on the approximation lower bounds: Ω(ε⁻ᵈ⁄ⁿ) under continuous architecture-parameter selection; arbitrary selection gives Ω(ε⁻ᵈ⁄²ⁿ), strengthened to Ω(ε⁻ᵈ⁄ⁿ log⁻²ᵖ⁻¹(1/ε)) at depth O(logᵖ(1/ε)); fixed depth L≥3 has curvature lower bound Ω(ε⁻¹⁄²⁽ᴸ⁻²⁾) for each nonlinear C² target. Telgarsky’s fixed gate-class separation uses the iterated-tent target with 2k³+8 computational layers, 3k³+12 gate nodes and at most 4+d shared parameter slots (counting the computational output gate, without an extra affine readout). Prove the explicit semi-algebraic crossing-budget bound for every positive gate-class triple (t,α,β). In particular the original at-most-k-layer, at-most-2^{k/(tαβ)}-node L¹([0,1]ᵈ) separation ≥1/64 holds under (tαβ)2^{k/(tαβ)}≤2^k, hence for every fixed gate class at sufficiently large k and for all k≥1 for ReLU gates (1,1,1). The sum-of-at-most-t-decision-trees separation with at most 2^{k³/t} total nodes per tree holds for every positive integer t. Preserve the original unrestricted all-parameter assertion as a false statement, with the explicit k=d=t=α=1, β=2^22 one-polynomial-gate Bernstein counterexample. Keep the computational-gate convention distinct from the affine-output ReLU convention; appending that readout adds one layer and one computation unit. Serra et al.’s region upper bound for widths n₁,…,n_L and input dimension n₀ is Σ_(j∈J) Π_ℓ binom(nℓ,jℓ), with J={0≤jℓ≤min(n₀,n₁−j₁,…,nℓ₋₁−jℓ₋₁,nℓ)}; it is tight for one hidden layer. Lower bounds include the exact one-dimensional product Πℓ(nℓ+1) when all widths nℓ≥3 and, when every nℓ≥3n₀, the product/arrangement lower bound $\prod_{\ell=1}^{L-1}(\lfloor n_\ell/n_0\rfloor+1)^{n_0}\sum_{j=0}^{n_0}\binom{n_L}{j}$. Region count is an expressivity proxy, not accuracy or generalization. The adaptive construction caches quantized local residual shapes. Lower bounds use continuous parameter selection, VC/shattering, or restriction to a line and fixed curvature. Telgarsky composes sawtooth maps and counts crossings by shallow semi-algebraic gates; PDF page 2 was visually checked for the exponent. Serra recursively counts hyperplane cuts in each activation region; lower bounds compose one-dimensional zigzags and a general-position final arrangement. **Examples:** tent-map composition; same target with target-dependent vs target-independent architecture; region-rich networks without a learning guarantee. **Requires:** ML-30 — Feedforward networks and single-hidden-layer universality for network notation; finite-dimensional approximation and hyperplane-arrangement counting proved or cited locally. No learning-04 optimization or training theorem is implied. **Sources:** Yarotsky, “Error bounds for approximations with deep ReLU networks,” Theorems 1–6 and proofs; Telgarsky, “Benefits of depth in neural networks,” Theorem 1.1 and §3.4; Serra, Tjandraatmadja, and Ramalingam, “Bounding and Counting Linear Regions of Deep Neural Networks,” Theorems 1, 7, 8, Corollary 3 and appendices.
