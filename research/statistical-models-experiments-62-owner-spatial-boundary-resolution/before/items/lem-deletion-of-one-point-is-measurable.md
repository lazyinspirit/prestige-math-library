## Statement

Let
$$D:=\{(x,\mu)\in S\times\mathcal N_{\mathrm{lf}}(S):\mu(\{x\})\ge1\}.$$
Then $D$ is measurable in the product evaluation sigma-algebra, and the map
$R:D\to\mathcal N_{\mathrm{lf}}(S)$ defined by $R(x,\mu)=\mu-\delta_x$ is
measurable. The subtraction removes one occurrence, so any other occurrences
at $x$ remain.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The topological and measurable structures of $S$ and
$\mathcal N_{\mathrm{lf}}(S)$ from
[[def-random-measure-and-locally-finite-point-process]].

[F1] There is an increasing compact exhaustion $(K_n)$ such that every compact
subset of $S$ is contained in some $K_n$.
([[lem-locally-compact-second-countable-spaces-have-compact-exhaustions]])

[F2] Evaluation maps $\mu\mapsto\mu(B)$ generate the sigma-algebra on
$\mathcal N_{\mathrm{lf}}(S)$, and each $\mu$ is a locally finite measure.
([[def-random-measure-and-locally-finite-point-process]])

[F3] A measure kernel has measurable evaluations, and integration of a
product-measurable nonnegative function against a uniformly sigma-finite
kernel is measurable. ([[def-measure-kernel-and-probability-kernel]],
[[thm-measurability-of-integration-against-a-kernel]])

[F4] The product sigma-algebra is generated by measurable rectangles, and
$\mathcal B(S)$ is generated by the open sets.
([[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]],
[[def-borel-sigma-algebra]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 On the parameter space $S\times\mathcal N_{\mathrm{lf}}(S)$ define $Q((x,\mu),B):=\mu(B)$. For each parameter this is a measure, its evaluations are measurable by [F2], and $(K_n)$ is a common finite-mass exhaustion because $\mu(K_n)<\infty$ for every locally finite $\mu$. Thus $Q$ is a uniformly sigma-finite kernel. [given, F1, F2, construct]

1.2 The diagonal $\Delta=\{(x,y):x=y\}$ is closed in $S\times S$ because $S$ is Hausdorff. The product topology has the countable basis consisting of rectangles from countable bases of the two copies of $S$; every open set is a countable union of these rectangles. Hence the product Borel sigma-algebra equals $\mathcal B(S)\otimes\mathcal B(S)$, so $1_\Delta$ is product measurable by [F4]. [given, F4, algebra]

2.1 For $(x,\mu)$, $\mu(\{x\})=\int 1_\Delta(x,y)\,Q((x,\mu),dy)$. The measurability theorem in [F3] makes $(x,\mu)\mapsto\mu(\{x\})$ measurable; therefore its inverse image $D$ of $[1,\infty]$ is measurable. [step 1.1, step 1.2, F3]

3.1 On $D$, $\mu-\delta_x$ is again a locally finite counting measure: in a representation of $\mu$ as a countable sum of point masses, remove one occurrence at $x$; no compact-set count increases. For every Borel $B$, $$R(x,\mu)(B)=\mu(B)-1_B(x),$$ where $\infty-1$ is interpreted as $\infty$. This evaluation is measurable on $D$, since both terms are measurable and on $D$ the subtraction is nonnegative. The evaluations generate the target sigma-algebra [F2], so $R$ is measurable. [given, F2, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 Steps 2.1-3.1 prove measurability of the domain and deletion map, including the multiplicity convention. [step 2.1, step 3.1] ∎
