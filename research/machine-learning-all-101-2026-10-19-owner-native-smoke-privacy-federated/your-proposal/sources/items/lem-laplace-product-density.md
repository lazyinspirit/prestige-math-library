## Statement

For $b>0$ and a finite integer $d\ge1$, the finite product probability law $\operatorname{Lap}(b)^{\otimes d}$ on $\mathbb R^d$ has Lebesgue density
$$
z\longmapsto (2b)^{-d}\exp\!\left(-\frac{\|z\|_1}{b}\right),
$$
where $\|z\|_1=\sum_{i=1}^d|z_i|$. Its coordinate projections are independent and each has law $\operatorname{Lap}(b)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $b>0$, a finite integer $d\ge1$, and the finite product conventions in the Statement.

[A1] [[def-countable-choice|Countable Choice]] is inherited from the one-dimensional exponential/Laplace density construction; no stronger choice principle is used here.

[F1] [[thm-laplace-density-from-exponential-law|One-dimensional density]]: $\operatorname{Lap}(b)$ has density $f_b(x)=(2b)^{-1}e^{-|x|/b}$.

[F2] [[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras|Finite product sigma-algebra]] is generated by measurable rectangles.

[F3] [[thm-sigma-finite-product-measure-exists-is-rectangular-and-is-unique|Product Lebesgue measure]] exists, is sigma-finite, and has the rectangular product formula.

[F4] [[thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces|Tonelli's theorem]] permits repeated integration of nonnegative product-measurable functions over the finite product.

[F5] [[thm-indefinite-integral-of-a-nonnegative-function-is-a-measure|Density measure]]: integrating a nonnegative measurable function over events defines a measure.

[F6] [[thm-measure-uniqueness-on-a-sigma-finite-pi-system|Measure uniqueness]] identifies sigma-finite measures that agree on a generating pi-system under its stated hypotheses.

[F7] [[def-independent-random-elements|Independent random elements]] have independent generated sigma-algebras; the finite-product law is specified by its rectangular factorization.

[F8] [[thm-exponential-addition-formula|The exponential addition formula]] gives $\exp(x+y)=\exp(x)\exp(y)$ for all real $x,y$; repeated application combines any finite sum in the exponent.

## Proof

1.1 Define $f(z)=\prod_{i=1}^d f_b(z_i)=(2b)^{-d}\exp(-\|z\|_1/b)$; the equality factoring the exponential of a finite sum uses [F8]. The function is nonnegative and product-measurable. Repeated application of [F4] gives $\int_{\mathbb R^d} f(z)\,d\lambda^d(z)=\prod_{i=1}^d\int_{\mathbb R}f_b(x)\,d\lambda(x)=1$, using [F1] for each one-dimensional integral. By [F5], $\nu(A)=\int_A f\,d\lambda^d$ is therefore a probability measure. [A1, F1, F3, F4, F5, F8, given, construct]

2.1 For a measurable rectangle $A=\prod_{i=1}^d A_i$, repeated Tonelli integration [F4] gives $\nu(A)=\prod_{i=1}^d\int_{A_i}f_b\,d\lambda=\prod_{i=1}^d\operatorname{Lap}(b)(A_i)$. The finite product law has this same rectangle value by [F3]. Rectangles form a generating pi-system by [F2]; both measures are finite, hence sigma-finite, so [F6] identifies $\nu$ with $\operatorname{Lap}(b)^{\otimes d}$ on the entire product sigma-algebra. The rectangular product law also gives independence of the coordinate projections and their stated marginals by [F7]. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F6, F7, step 1.1] ∎
