## Statement

Assume countable choice. Let $D$ be a finite connected regular diagram representing a nonsplit smooth link $L$, with at least one crossing. Suppose $L$ admits a transverse two-point sphere with both side tangles nontrivial. Then the following model can be supplied.

Take its usual reference cornered crossing-ball picture $K$: the overpasses and underpasses are exact semicircles in unit crossing coordinates, and the remaining arcs lie on the projection sphere $P$. Its upper and lower open-side pieces $B_+,B_-$ are balls with product edge corners; their faces and the crossing balls cover $S^3$. There are disjoint closed smooth adapter balls $A_e$ about the finitely many corners of $K$, and a smooth link $L'$ ambient isotopic to $L$, equal to $K$ outside their interiors and equal to its semicircle arcs throughout the crossing balls. Inside the adapters the exterior arcs of $L'$ may leave $P$.

There is a smooth sphere $T$ with two transverse punctures on $L'$ outside the crossing and adapter balls and with both side tangles nontrivial, disjoint from every $A_e$, such that its crossing-ball pieces are finite ordered quadrilateral saddle disks. The reference open-side pieces $T\cap B_\pm$ are disks; their intersection circles with the reference shifted spheres have nonempty bubble words and meet each hemisphere at most once. These words use $K$ on the reference common face, where it agrees with $L'$ at every puncture. All incidence statements are taken in the reference face-and-corner model; individual geometric disk applications use inward smooth roundings of the open-side balls. Their intersections with $T$ have exactly the same piece topology and circle incidences.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable choice, the finite regular diagram, its nonsplit represented smooth link and its nontrivial two-point decomposing sphere.

[F1] Finite planar link corners have smooth graph roundings unique up to an ambient isotopy supported in their boxes. They represent the original diagram's smooth link by the positive-height interpolation proof. Exterior-only endpoint roundings retain the exact semicircle inside each crossing ball. Surfaces disjoint from the boxes retain their side-tangle types. ([[lem-finite-planar-link-corners-have-compatible-smooth-roundings]])

[F2] A compact surface avoiding the arc in a supplied trivial one-string ball can be moved off the whole ball by an ambient isotopy fixing the link pointwise. ([[lem-surface-avoiding-a-trivial-ball-arc-can-avoid-its-ball]])

[F3] The explicit crossing-ball foliation places a compact set away from the two semicircle arcs in a middle band. Its ordered quadrilateral disks and outward radial field supply finite saddle position. Its construction can keep every compact set outside the closed middle band fixed. ([[lem-explicit-quadrilateral-saddle-foliation-of-a-crossing-ball]], [[lem-finite-saddle-position-in-protected-crossing-balls]])

[F4] A ball with product edge corners has an inward smooth rounding preserving the topology of transverse surface pieces and retaining chosen compact cores away from the edges. In adapter boxes disjoint from those cores and the surface, extra graph indentations can exclude specified compact subsets. ([[lem-product-edge-corner-ball-has-a-relative-inward-rounding]])

[F5] Simultaneous transversality can be achieved by an arbitrarily small ambient isotopy fixing a protected closed set, if it already holds near the protected surface intersections. ([[lem-simultaneous-surface-transversality-fixed-on-a-protected-closed-set]])

[F6] Natural numbers have minima. A nondisk proper ball-surface component gives a compression disk for the whole surface. In a nonsplit link, a link-free compression boundary on a two-point sphere bounds a puncture-free sphere disk; replacing it by the supplied disk is an ambient isotopy fixing the link and preserving the retained sphere. ([[thm-well-ordering-principle]], [[lem-nondisk-proper-surface-in-a-ball-has-a-compressing-disk]], [[lem-disk-replacement-on-a-nonsplit-two-point-sphere]])

[F7] A repeated hemisphere visit gives an empty rectangle. A narrow spanning rectangle on a smooth side ball supplies a bridge disk avoiding its disk family. Its normal collar can be framed using the same boundary sphere on the surface and rectangle sides; the annulus-complement and two parallel bridge copies then give the two-saddle replacement disk. These constructions are explicit in the corresponding local proofs. ([[lem-empty-rectangle-for-a-repeated-hemisphere-circle]], [[lem-empty-boundary-rectangle-supplies-a-surface-bridge-disk]], [[lem-empty-overpass-free-rectangle-cancels-two-saddles]])

[F8] Meridional splitting of a two-point sphere with nontrivial sides has a nontrivial child. Boundary sums of trivial one-string ball tangles are trivial with an actual glued bridge disk. A punctured disk window in a supplied trivial-arc ball slides to the opposite face, preserving the smooth link setwise and avoiding any closed set disjoint from that face. ([[lem-meridional-splitting-retains-a-nontrivial-decomposing-sphere]], [[lem-boundary-sum-of-one-string-ball-tangles-preserves-nontriviality]], [[lem-punctured-disk-window-slides-across-a-trivial-arc-ball]])

[F9] Smooth spheres bound balls on both sides; attaching or removing a ball along a boundary disk preserves the ball type after local corner smoothing. ([[thm-smooth-schoenflies-for-embedded-two-sphere-in-s-three]], [[lem-smooth-ball-windows-and-boundary-disk-absorption]])

[F10] Compactly supported smooth fields have global unique flows. Finite local extensions of a tangent field along a link, cut off in link-axis charts, preserve that link setwise. ([[lem-the-extended-time-dependent-field-has-a-global-time-one-flow]])

[F11] Bubble words record punctures by $P$ and saddle-hemisphere visits by $S$; the stated nonempty-word and no-repeat conditions are the bubble-standard conditions. ([[def-flype-crossing-ball-and-alternating-tangle-data]])

## Proof

1.1 Use local product crossing coordinates of the diagram to put semicircle arcs into disjoint unit balls, and use its planar edges outside them. This gives the reference cornered link $K$. The upper open-side piece is the standard upper ball of $P$ with the finitely many upper half-crossing balls removed. It is still a topological ball by an explicit collar homeomorphism. In a projection disk let $\psi(x,y)$ be its continuous upper hemisphere height, zero outside that disk. The map $(x,y,z)\mapsto(x,y,z+\psi(x,y)\rho(z))$, with $\rho=1$ near zero and zero farther up the upper collar, carries its boundary to that hemisphere. Choose the crossing balls small enough that $\|\psi\rho'\|_\infty<1$; every vertical fiber is then strictly increasing, with identity at the far end, so the map and its fiber inverse are continuous. Finite disjoint supports give a homeomorphism of the upper ball onto $B_+$. The lower construction is reflected. Their only corners are product circles: near an equator use $a=z$, $b=r^2+z^2-r_0^2$ for the upper outside region, so $a,b\ge0$ and their independent derivatives give a product collar; $u=a-b$, $v=a+b$ give $v\ge|u|$. No smoothness of this collar homeomorphism at the unrounded edge is needed. Choose a smooth exterior-only rounding $L_0$ by [F1], supported in disjoint small endpoint balls $W_e$. Each $L_0\cap W_e$ is a trivial proper arc: in the positive planar graph coordinate it cobounds a strip with the upper boundary path of the box, with the far box corners rounded. It has an actual bridge disk. By [F1], $L_0$ is ambient isotopic to $L$. [given, F1, construct]

2.1 Transport the supplied sphere to $L_0$ and move its two punctures onto edge subarcs outside all crossing balls and $W_e$. This uses a smooth orientation-preserving circle isotopy on each affected component, with the same cyclic order of the two chosen target points; construct its positive derivative by interior bumps with prescribed positive interval integrals and interpolate its lift with the identity. Extending the velocity in finitely many link-axis charts and using [F10] realizes it ambiently preserving $L_0$. Now apply [F2] to move the sphere off every $W_e$, fixing its punctures and retaining both nontrivial sides. Choose the middle foliation bands of [F3] for this sphere. The corner points lie outside the closed bands, since they have $q=\pm1$; choose smaller nested adapter balls $A_e\subset\operatorname{int}W_e$ disjoint from those bands. Replace the rounding by an exterior-only rounding $L'$ supported in their interiors. The relative rounding isotopy of [F1] is supported in $W_e$, which miss the sphere, so its nontrivial side tangles are unchanged. Perform the saddle-position construction of [F3] with the chosen bands; all its moving supports can be confined to their neighborhoods and the axis patches, away from $A_e$. This gives a sphere avoiding the adapters with a finite fixed foliation stack. [step 1.1, F1, F2, F3, F10, construct]

3.1 All further cut circles are defined in the reference face-and-corner model. They can be made transverse outside the crossing balls by [F5]. To apply it literally, first round the two open-side boundaries individually using [F4] with the empty surface; only the local calculation below is needed before global face transversality is arranged. At a saddle corner, transversality to the edge follows from the nonzero pairing of the edge tangent $(-y,x,0)$ with the saddle normal $(2x,-2y,1)$, namely $-4xy\ne0$. Hence the inward rounding preserves the cut-piece topology and is transverse to the surface there. The link punctures are on the unchanged common face outside the adapters and crossing balls; transversality to the link forces transversality to that face. Apply [F5] protecting the link, crossing balls, adapters and their small fixed surface collars. In the remaining face interiors its targets are precisely the reference face, so the resulting model intersections are transverse as required. Thus there is a nonempty class of smooth nontrivial two-point spheres avoiding all adapters, with punctures outside crossing balls, finite subsets of the fixed foliation levels and transverse reference face intersections. [step 2.1, F3, F4, F5, construct]

4.1 Minimize lexicographically over that global class the total saddle count $s$ and then the total upper-plus-lower cut-circle count $c$, using [F6]. Both are finite natural numbers, by the finite patches and compact transverse model intersections. For a candidate $T$, use [F4] with forbidden sets $A_e\cap B_\pm$ inside slightly larger product boxes disjoint from $T$. The resulting smooth balls $B_\pm'$ lie inside the reference pieces, avoid all adapters and have the same surface-piece topology. Their interiors miss $L'$: outside the adapters that link lies on the old faces, and inside the adapters the balls are absent. Every component has boundary: a closed component would be the whole connected sphere, contrary to its two punctures on the unchanged boundary face and transversality there. A nondisk piece therefore has a compression disk in $\operatorname{int}B_\pm'$ by [F6], missing the whole sphere, every crossing ball, every adapter and the actual smooth link. The puncture-free sphere disk it replaces contains whole model cut circles and saddle patches and at least one cut circle; otherwise the compression boundary would bound a disk in the same ball piece. The replacement deletes whole patches and circles and adds none in the protected regions. It gives a member of the same class with smaller $(s,c)$, a contradiction. Consequently all reference open-side pieces are disks. [step 3.1, F4, F6, construct, contradiction]

5.1 An empty-word circle lies on the common projection face, avoids all saddle patches and all punctures, and is the same cut circle on both sides. Its incident upper and lower pieces are disks by step 4.1. They join to a closed component of the sphere, hence the whole sphere. Their interiors miss $L'$ because they miss the adapters and every other link point lies on the reference faces. Their boundary has no puncture. This contradicts the sphere's two link intersections, so no word is empty. [step 4.1, construct, contradiction]

5.2 Suppose a hemisphere is repeated by a cut circle. Its upper arcs span two equatorial intervals, so [F7] supplies an empty rectangle $R$ with its two arc sides on one disk piece $E$. The ordered-stack arc list gives two cases: $R$ joins the same-type arcs of two distinct adjacent saddles and misses the overpass, or it is the central strip between the two upper arcs of the same uppermost saddle. Choose a narrow rectangle $R_0$ around a spanning core of $R$, meeting its two arc sides in small interior subintervals. It is compactly inside the smooth hemisphere face, away from its equatorial edge and every adapter; in the central case its core can be the $x=0$ upper meridian. Choose a core arc $\beta$ in $E$ between those endpoints. Apply [F4] retaining a neighborhood of $R_0\cup\beta$, and excluding the adapters. The resulting smooth side ball has a disk family with the same topology, the same core and the same small rectangle. The bridge construction of [F7] therefore gives a disk inside this ball, disjoint from the whole sphere except along $\beta$. Its boundary may be pushed slightly inward from the rectangle core. Its normal collar is framed by the common side-ball boundary on both ends. Explicitly in its boundary collar the graph $v=f(s,t)$ with $f(s,0)=0$ is straightened by $v\mapsto v-\chi(t)f(s,t)$, retaining that framing. [step 4.1, F3, F4, F7, construct]

6.1 In the overpass-free case the annulus between the two adjacent saddle boundaries is entirely in the fixed middle band and avoids every adapter. Remove $R$ from this annulus and attach two normal parallel copies of the bridge, extending them across the two half-rectangles between $R_0$ and the long sides of $R$. This gives a disk whose boundary is the two-saddle-plus-band patch on $T$: attaching each bridge copy along one interval to the annulus complement preserves a disk, and the common boundary-sphere framing pairs the two long sides correctly. Push its interior outside the crossing ball and round the finitely many seams. It misses the whole sphere and $L'$; the rectangle and annulus are empty, the bridge interior missed the sphere, and all these patches avoid the adapters. The quadrant chart at an attaching arc puts the side ball in $v,w\ge0$ and the region between saddles in $v\ge0,w\le0$, so their collars use the same $v>0$ side. Before the exchange, extend the old patch by a short retained-surface collar out of the crossing ball, and extend the replacement disk across the same collar, pushing its interior off the surface on its existing side. In the stated quadrant coordinates the extension and its rounding stay in $v,w>0$; the new common boundary is strictly outside $C_j$. The disk replacement in [F6] consequently deletes exactly those two saddle disks. Its new disk lies outside all crossing and adapter balls. Restoring exterior transversality by [F5], if needed, produces a competitor with smaller $s$, a contradiction. This uses only the retained small rectangle for the smooth bridge application; the full reference rectangle need not survive the inward boundary rounding. [step 5.2, F4, F5, F6, F7, construct, contradiction]

6.2 In the central case join the $x=0$ planar cap above the uppermost saddle to the bridge from step 5.2. Extend the cap through the short strip to the pushed meridian before gluing, so its north-pole overpass intersection is interior. It crosses $L'$ once there, transversely, and misses every other saddle, the underpass and all adapters. This is a meridional disk with boundary on $T$; parity of a closed link's intersections with a sphere in [F9] forces that boundary to separate the two punctures. Apply [F8] to obtain a nontrivial child. Choose its parallel cap in $x=\delta$ or $x=-\delta$. The region above its half-saddle in the corresponding half-crossing ball is a ball bounded by that half-saddle and cap, together with the curved outer face, by [F9]. It contains no other sphere piece. Extend this outer face slightly outside the crossing ball and extend its surface window along a short actual child-surface collar, as in the local quadrant chart. The remaining endpoint adapter can be included in this window ball without introducing a knot: choose an outer adapter ball $A_e^+$ containing $A_e$, still disjoint from the child sphere and all other saddles. Near that endpoint the graph region occupies the entire inside of $C_j$, so its intersection with $A_e^+$ is one boundary-attached cap ball. Remove this overlap and add the whole $A_e^+$ along the resulting disk window, using [F9]. The opposite face now lies outside $C_j\cup A_e$. The captured arc is the boundary sum of the overpass segment, whose thin outward strip is a bridge disk, and the planar graph arc in $A_e^+$, with its graph bridge disk. The two factors are trivial, so the constructive boundary-sum assertion of [F8] supplies an actual bridge disk for the combined arc. The attaching disk meets it once, at the incoming segment; the outgoing endpoint lies on the outer adapter face. Apply the punctured-window slide of [F8] with protected closed set $\bigcup_jC_j\cup\bigcup_eA_e$. It removes that child's half-saddle and planar cap, moves its puncture beyond the adapter and leaves every other original saddle fixed. The child remains nontrivial, and after [F5] it is a member of the candidate class with at most $s-1$ saddles, a contradiction. The reflected construction treats lower repetitions, with its corresponding endpoint adapters. [step 4.1, step 5.2, F3, F5, F8, F9, construct, contradiction]

7.1 Thus a minimizing sphere has disk pieces, nonempty words and no repeated hemisphere, on both shifted reference spheres. It is a smooth sphere for the actual smooth link $L'$, and it avoids every adapter by definition of the candidate class and by the protected-set choice in the two moves. The link $L'$ represents the original diagram and is ambient isotopic to $L$ by step 1.1 and the relative rounding comparison in step 2.1. All geometric ball arguments were performed in individual smooth inward roundings or in the marked child window; no simultaneous smooth partition, and no smooth link lying in the literal unrounded planar-corner picture, was assumed. This proves the stated transfer and the word conditions of [F11]. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 6.2, F11] ∎
