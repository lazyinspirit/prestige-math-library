## Statement

Assume full AC. Let $F_0,F_1\subset S^3$ be compact smooth embedded surfaces with the same nonempty oriented boundary link $L$, no closed surface components, and supplied radial collars of $L$. Suppose their entire intersection is $L$ together with $n$ disjoint isolated smooth arcs with distinct endpoints on $L$. Each arc is the radial extension of a proper transverse common arc in the link exterior; its two transverse boundary-curve crossings have equal ordered signs on the boundary tori oriented outward toward the exterior. No intersection circles occur. Suppose $c(F_0)=c(F_1)=p$ and $n=\operatorname{rank}H_1(F_0;\mathbb Z)+\operatorname{rank}H_1(F_1;\mathbb Z)$.

There are $p$ disjoint smooth embedded two-spheres and a regular oriented diagram on each, connected as a graph, with exactly $n$ crossings in total, whose simultaneous small-height realization is smoothly ambient isotopic to the original $L$. Every sphere carries a nonempty group of complete link components. The induced coloring by the two original surfaces gives checkerboard surfaces, made of one disk in each region of a color and a half-twisted band at each crossing, in the smooth ambient-isotopy classes of $F_0$ and $F_1$, with their boundary orientations transported. In particular, if $L$ is nonsplit, then $p=1$ and the construction gives a connected regular oriented diagram of $L$ on a single projection sphere. If the full normal-transfer pairings on the original surfaces are definite with opposite signs, all the resulting diagrams are alternating. No primeness assumption is used, so this conclusion includes composite nonsplit links.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Full AC, the actual surface pair with its radial collars and finite standard arcs, the numerical balance assumptions, and the orientation of $L$.

[F1] The original pair admits an ambient quotient with exactly the common arcs as its nonsingleton fibers, giving $p$ topological sphere components with one component from each surface on each sphere. Its marked boundary graph is connected on every sphere, and its four-page vertex chart has alternating surface sectors. ([[lem-finite-standard-arc-collapse-of-balanced-spans-is-a-union-of-spheres]], [[lem-balanced-span-sphere-quotients-have-connected-four-valent-marked-graphs]])

[F2] The actual local arc pair has a flat-first-surface chart and a link-fixed angular isotopy to the ruled half-turn model. Its actual proof preserves the axial coordinate and normal radius. The second strip before straightening is $(x,t\,v(x,t))$ with smooth nonzero $v$ and monotone radius on each transverse half. ([[lem-standard-intersection-arc-pairs-admit-a-relative-ruled-half-turn-model]])

[F3] The explicit smooth-arc quotient can be performed in a prescribed straight-arc chart, preserving its outer ellipsoid and fixing its boundary and outside. Four pages with opposite endpoint matchings collapse intrinsically to the cone on their four-edge outer circle. ([[lem-collapsing-a-smooth-embedded-arc-preserves-the-three-sphere]], [[lem-four-page-arc-collapse-is-a-disk-for-opposite-end-pairings]])

[F4] Finite smooth surface seams with distinct inward rays have graph coordinates and compactly supported vertical fiber rounding maps. Their actual proof uses graph convolution and a displacement $\Delta$ small enough that $1+\chi'\Delta/a>1/2$. The seam-fixed modification is proved below. ([[lem-finite-smooth-surface-creases-admit-relative-ambient-rounding]])

[F5] The actual smooth finite handle normal-form proof reduces a closed connected smooth surface to one zero-disk and one two-disk, with transported attaching collars. Its smooth circle-map extension makes the Euler-characteristic-two case smoothly parametrized by the standard sphere: the handle count leaves zero one-bands. ([[lem-finite-c2-surface-carriers-have-smooth-normal-forms-and-relative-cap-approximations]])

[F6] A smooth isotopy of embeddings of a compact source, stationary near its time endpoints, extends to a compactly supported smooth ambient isotopy. A smooth transverse vector field has a smooth local flow; the inverse function theorem and compactness give a uniform short injective product collar of a compact two-sided surface. ([[lem-choice-free-isotopy-extension-for-a-compact-smooth-source]], [[thm-fundamental-theorem-on-flows]], [[thm-euclidean-inverse-function-theorem]])

[F7] An embedded sphere has two complementary components. A smooth standard-sphere embedding in $S^3$ is carried to the equator by a smooth ambient isotopy. Splitness is invariant under smooth ambient isotopy. ([[thm-jordan-brouwer-separation]], [[thm-smooth-schoenflies-for-embedded-two-sphere-in-s-three]], [[def-split-link]])

[F8] A compact connected smooth surface with nonempty boundary has a finite disk-and-band presentation. Its disks and bands give a finite graph spine. Contracting a spanning tree of that graph by the actual smooth disk-window absorptions leaves one disk and $h\ge0$ bands, so its Euler characteristic is $1-h$ and equals one only for a disk. ([[lem-compact-smooth-boundary-surfaces-have-finite-disk-band-presentations]], [[lem-finite-c2-surface-carriers-have-smooth-normal-forms-and-relative-cap-approximations]])

[F9] A smooth Jordan curve on a two-sphere bounds the two disk regions supplied by plane Jordan–Schönflies. The Tait graph has one vertex for each region of a chosen color and one edge through each crossing joining its two sectors. ([[lem-jordan-schoenflies-extension-for-plane-curves]], [[def-tait-graph-of-a-link-diagram]])

[F10] The full normal-transfer pairing restricts to each surface component and is preserved by smooth ambient isotopy. Standard checkerboard surfaces with opposite definite pairings force their connected diagram to be alternating. ([[lem-normal-transfer-pairing-and-signature-under-component-connecting-tubes]], [[lem-opposite-definite-checkerboard-pairings-force-alternation]])

## Proof

1.1 Apply [F1] to the original pair, obtaining a reference quotient $q_0$, its sphere image and its connected marked graphs. Keep this reference only for their topology and component assignment. Choose disjoint actual arc charts and use [F2] in smaller cylinders. The angular isotopies fix the literal link. Their images of the second surface are still smooth: write the signed normal vector as $t|v(x,t)|(\cos\beta(x,t),\sin\beta(x,t))$, with a smooth angle lift $\beta$ for the nonzero $v$. The formula on that strip is the smooth interpolation from $\beta$ to the ruled angle, with a radius cutoff constant near the axis. It is smooth through $t=0$ and the end faces, has nonzero transverse derivative by monotone normal radius, and is injective by the distinct positive and negative half angles. Outside the supported cylinders it agrees with the old strip. Thus the new pair is smooth, has the same link and the same arc fibers and Betti numbers. In each smaller chart it is exactly the ruled pair with $\theta(x)=\pi(1+\epsilon x)/2$, $\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}$. [given, F1, F2, construct]

2.1 In each such chart take a small ellipsoid $B=\{x^2/(1+e^2)+(y^2+z^2)/e^2\le1\}$, with a slightly larger ellipsoid still inside the ruled region. Rotate every normal plane by $-\theta(x)/2$. This rotation is realized by a global smooth ambient isotopy: multiply its angle by a smooth cutoff in the chart, equal to one on the larger ellipsoid, depending smoothly on $x$ and $y^2+z^2$ and zero near the chart boundary. Every time map preserves $x$ and normal radius and is inverted by the opposite angle. It therefore is a smooth diffeomorphism, including at the axis. The local link becomes $\Lambda_y=\{(-\epsilon,t,0):|t|\le d\}$ and $\Lambda_z=\{(\epsilon,0,t):|t|\le d\}$, where $d=e^2/\sqrt{1+e^2}$. Denote the resulting original-link representative by $\widehat L$. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Perform the coordinate arc collapse [F3] in each ellipsoid before this last rotation, and conjugate it by the rotation. Compose the disjoint collapses, obtaining $q$ for the rotated pair, with each ball preserved and its boundary fixed. Its only nonsingleton fibers are the original arc sets, fixed as sets by all the preceding local maps. Conjugating $q_0$ by those preceding ambient homeomorphisms gives another quotient of the rotated pair with precisely the same fibers. The induced map between the two compact Hausdorff quotient targets is a homeomorphism, since each quotient map is continuous and onto. Hence $q$ has the same $p$ sphere components and the same connected marked graphs. Inside each ball, the four actual pages, truncated by the ellipsoid, are parametrized by $x\in[-1,1]$ and normalized normal radius $u\in[0,1]$; opposite end matchings give their cone disk and four radial marked arms by [F3]. Outside the balls its surface image is exactly the rotated original surface union. [step 1.1, step 2.1, F1, F3, construct]

3.2 Describe its outer page curve in the rotated chart. If $\phi$ is normal angle and $a(\phi)$ its distance from $\pi\mathbb Z$ in $[0,\pi/2]$, put $b(\phi)=\epsilon(4a(\phi)/\pi-1)$. The four edges have angles $\pm\theta/2$ and $\pi\pm\theta/2$, so on every nearby confocal level of parameter $\rho$ their union is $(b(\phi),R_\rho(b(\phi))\cos\phi,R_\rho(b(\phi))\sin\phi)$, where $R_\rho(v)=\rho\sqrt{1-v^2/(1+\rho^2)}$. Replace the cornered function $b$ by the smooth function $b_s(\phi)=-\epsilon\cos(2\phi)$. They have the same four values at angles $0,\pi/2,\pi,3\pi/2$, namely $-\epsilon,\epsilon,-\epsilon,\epsilon$. [step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Make this replacement on a collar of $\partial B$ by an actual link-fixed ambient topological isotopy. On a confocal level, $x$ runs from $-\sqrt{1+\rho^2}$ to $\sqrt{1+\rho^2}$ at fixed $\phi$. Choose a smooth radial cutoff $\lambda(\rho)$ equal to one on $[e/2,5e/4]$ and zero outside $(e/3,3e/2)$; choose $e$ small enough that these larger levels remain inside the ruled region. Use the increasing piecewise-affine meridian interval map fixing its two poles and taking $b(\phi)$ to $(1-t\lambda)b(\phi)+t\lambda b_s(\phi)$. It and its inverse vary continuously; at a pole continuity follows from its unchanged pole and vanishing normal radius. Extend by the identity off that annular region. At each of the four link directions $b=b_s$, the entire meridian map is the identity, so the literal chords and all of $\widehat L$ remain fixed. Near $\partial B$ the outside surface is now the smooth annulus $x=b_s(\phi)$ on the confocal levels. Each original half-page remains smooth up to its seam: on its closed angular quadrant $b$ is smooth, and the displayed interpolation is smooth in the level and quadrant parameters. The modified seam curve is still the literal smooth link. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, construct]

5.1 In the ball set $R_s(\phi)=R_e(b_s(\phi))$ and $\Omega_s=\{(r\cos\phi,r\sin\phi):0\le r\le R_s(\phi)\}$. This is a smooth star-shaped disk, with $d\le R_s\le e$. Choose a smooth nondecreasing $\zeta:[0,1]\to[0,1]$, zero on $[0,1/2]$ and one on $[3/4,1]$. The graph $P=\{(h(y,z),y,z):(y,z)\in\Omega_s\}$, where $h(r,\phi)=\zeta(r/R_s(\phi))b_s(\phi)$, is smooth, including at the origin where it is identically zero. It is properly embedded in $B$: at radius $r\le R_s$ the allowed absolute $x$ height is $A(r)=\sqrt{1+e^2}\sqrt{1-r^2/e^2}\ge|b_s|\ge|h|$, with strict ball inequality for $r<R_s$. Its boundary is the new smooth page curve. On its boundary collar $h=b_s$, exactly the same parametrized annulus as in step 4.1; hence it glues smoothly to the modified outside surface. Projection of the two literal chords along $x$ onto this disk is $(-\epsilon\zeta(|t|/d),t,0)$ and $(\epsilon\zeta(|t|/d),0,t)$. They are smooth regular curves, meet only at the origin in its flat disk chart, and there cross transversely. Their signed heights are $-\epsilon(1-\zeta)$ and $\epsilon(1-\zeta)$; the two heights are opposite at the crossing, and vanish on the outer collar where projection is the identity. [step 2.1, step 3.2, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Replace the collapsed disk in each ball by $P$, leaving the modified outside surface unchanged. This preserves its sphere components and its marked graph topology. Indeed the old disk has the four-page cone chart in step 3.1, transported by the annular homeomorphism. Its four boundary marks are fixed by that homeomorphism. The new disk projects homeomorphically onto $\Omega_s$, with its four marked arms the coordinate radial segments. The prescribed boundary identification extends from the old cone to $\Omega_s$ radially, sending its four marked radii to those four segments; compose with the graph map to $P$. This is a disk homeomorphism agreeing on the entire boundary, so pastes to a homeomorphism of the two global surface-and-graph pairs. Call the new surface $S$ and its projected graph $D$. Thus $S$ has exactly $p$ topological sphere components and $D$ is connected on each, with one vertex for each original arc. Opposite boundary marks are joined by the actual link chords, so these vertices have the correct strand pairing. [step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, F1, F3, construct]

7.1 Round the remaining seams while keeping the literal link fixed. They are finitely many compact link arcs or circles outside the protected cap collars; their arc ends lie in the smooth annuli from steps 4.1–6.1. Their two inward half-sheet rays are distinct. Outside the normalization cylinders this follows from the original distinct radial collars and smooth rotations. In a normalization cylinder the relative radial collar angle has nonzero derivative at the arc endpoint. In the adapted strip this says $Z=(x-x_{\rm end})t c(x,t)$ with $c(x_{\rm end},0)\ne0$, by applying the two vanishing factors and differentiating the relative collar angle; shrink so $c$ remains nonzero. Therefore the end derivative of the lift $\beta$ in step 1.1 is nonzero and has the same sign as the ruled-angle end derivative, since both angles approach the same endpoint of $[0,\pi]$ from its interior. Their convex interpolation retains that sign, and the derivative of its cutoff is multiplied by $\beta-\theta=0$ at the end. Thus distinct inward rays remain distinct off the original arc endpoints, which are removed inside the balls. Inside an annular quadrant chart the two outgoing angular derivatives have opposite nonzero angular components, so cannot coincide, even when their tangent planes agree. Use [F4]'s seam graph $y=f(s,x)$, with $f(s,0)=0$. Replace its rounding displacement by $\Delta=\mu(s)\chi_0(x)(f^\eta(s,x)-f(s,x)-f^\eta(s,0))$, where $f^\eta$ is the transverse convolution, $\chi_0=1$ near zero, and $\mu=1$ near the nonsmooth locus and is supported away from the protected smooth collars. Near a nonsmooth seam the new graph is $f^\eta(s,x)-f^\eta(s,0)$, hence smooth; all cutoff transitions occur where the old graph is smooth. The error is at most twice the usual convolution error, so shrinking $\eta$ retains [F4]'s positive vertical fiber derivative. Crucially $\Delta(s,0)=0$, and therefore the whole fiber isotopy fixes the seam pointwise. Its support misses the caps and lies where $\widehat L$ is exactly that seam. The resulting $S$ is smooth everywhere, with $\widehat L$ and its diagram projection unchanged. [step 1.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, F4, construct, algebra]

8.1 Each smooth surface component is homeomorphic to $S^2$ by step 6.1. To supply an actual standard smooth sphere parameter, apply [F5]'s finite smooth handle normalization. One zero-disk and one two-disk remain; Euler characteristic two forces zero one-bands. The smooth boundary circle gluing extends across a disk with its transported product collars, giving a smooth standard sphere parameter. The map from $\widehat L$ to $S$ is the identity outside the balls and the projections in step 5.1 inside them. It is smooth at the joins because those projections are the identity on open link collars, is an immersion everywhere, and has precisely the $n$ transverse double points just described, with no other multiple points. Orient its strands from the oriented $\widehat L$. This gives the required regular graphs on the smooth spheres. [step 5.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, F5, construct]

9.1 Track the two surfaces themselves through the local changes, without replacing their bands by the projection caps. Apart from the smooth rotation in step 2.1, the angular and seam changes restrict to smooth embedding isotopies of either surface, fixed on its boundary link: this was checked for the angular normalization in step 1.1 and for the quadrant pages in step 4.1; in step 7.1 the graph formula is smooth on either closed half-sheet and has zero displacement on the seam. By [F6] each such source isotopy extends smoothly, and its extension fixes the boundary link because the source isotopy does. The resulting surfaces have disk-and-band pieces on the two colors of $S$, with one band neighborhood for each original common arc. To prove that their cut pieces are disks, cut all $n$ arc strips. The interiors of their components correspond bijectively to the colored faces of $D$, by the marked cone charts and the surface-and-graph homeomorphism in step 6.1. A thin smooth regular neighborhood of a connected graph in a sphere has disk complementary regions: every boundary circle bounds a disk on the side missing the connected neighborhood by [F9], and these disks are disjoint and fill its complement. Its Euler count is $-n_a$ for a graph with $n_a$ four-valent vertices, including the circle case, so it has $n_a+2$ boundary circles. Summing gives $n+2p$ cut pieces. Formula [F8] gives $\chi(F_i)=p-b_i$, where $b_i= \operatorname{rank}H_1(F_i;\mathbb Z)$. Cutting each proper arc adds one to Euler characteristic, by restoring its rectangle along two intervals. Hence the sum of the cut-piece Euler characteristics is $2p-b_0-b_1+2n=2p+n$, equal to their number. Each is at most one by [F8], so every cut piece is a disk. [given, step 1.1, step 4.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, step 8.1, F6, F8, F9, construct, algebra]

9.2 Bring the actual link into an arbitrarily small neighborhood of $S$ by smooth embedding isotopies, without using the arbitrary quotient homeomorphisms on the link. On each chord keep $(y,z)$ fixed and replace its $x$ coordinate by $h(y,z)+k(x-h(y,z))$, with $k$ decreasing smoothly from one to an arbitrarily small positive constant. The displayed chord formulas make this jointly smooth, including the crossing and the stationary outer collars. They remain embeddings: along each branch the nonconstant coordinate is still its parameter, and the only pair of equal projected points is the crossing, where the two nonzero opposite heights remain distinct because $k>0$. They stay inside the convex vertical ball fibers. Extend by the identity on the rest of the link, stationarize time, and apply [F6] to this compact source isotopy. Thus the compressed link is smoothly ambient isotopic to $\widehat L$, hence to the original $L$, and can be made arbitrarily close to its fixed diagram $D$. [step 2.1, step 5.1, step 7.1, step 8.1, F6, construct]

10.1 Check the bands' actual framing. In a rotated ball, the two band cores are the diagonals $x=0$, with signed core parameter $s$ and normal-plane basis $(x,w)$, where $w$ is the positive quarter-turn of that core in the $(y,z)$ plane. Put $\tau_0=1$, $\tau_1=-1$ for the two colors, $c(s)=1+\lambda(|s|)(\pi/2-1)$, and $z(s)=\zeta(|s|/e)$. Differentiating the ruled strips and their meridian modification at the core gives band width vector $(c(s),-\tau_i\epsilon\pi s/4)$, hence normal vector $N_i=(\tau_i\epsilon\pi s/4,c(s))$. Differentiating the smooth projection disk gives normal $N_S=(1,2\tau_i\epsilon z(s)/s)$, extended smoothly at zero since $z$ vanishes there. Whenever $z>0$, the cutoff in step 4.1 has $\lambda=1$. Therefore $\det(N_S,N_i)=c-\pi z/2\ge0$, while $N_S\cdot N_i=\tau_i\epsilon(\pi s/4+2cz/s)$ has sign $\tau_i\epsilon s$ for $s\ne0$. The relative normal angle lies in $[0,\pi]$, equals zero or $\pi$ on the two attaching collars, and crosses $\pi/2$ only at the center, transversely. Thus each band has exactly one half-turn relative to the sphere framing, with no extra full twists; the two colors have opposite half-turn signs. At the center its positive width direction points toward increasing $x$, so its boundary branch there is exactly the actual overpassing chord specified in step 5.1. [step 2.1, step 3.2, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 9.1, construct, algebra]

10.2 Choose an orientation and positive transverse direction on each sphere. Near the compact cap portions used by the projected chords, choose the transverse field to be $\partial_x$ or $-\partial_x$, according to that positive direction; the graph disk is transverse to this field. Positive transverse fields extend over the rest of the sphere by a finite smooth partition, since the positive normal cone is convex. Its flow gives a common short smooth product collar by [F6]: invertibility holds on the zero section, and compactness excludes collisions in successively shorter strips. With $k$ small enough in step 9.2, the compressed link lies in these disjoint collars and projects exactly to $D$; in the balls its flow segments are the same vertical segments, while outside them the link lies on the sphere. Read the overcrossing data from its positive collar coordinate. This is a simultaneous small-height realization of those oriented diagrams, smoothly equivalent to the original link. If there is only one sphere, apply [F7]'s smooth ambient isotopy to the equator to view it as an ordinary diagram on the oriented projection sphere. [step 5.1, step 8.1, step 9.2, F6, F7, construct]

11.1 These data identify the smooth checkerboard surfaces by an explicit finite ribbon straightening. In each disk piece choose a smaller round-parameter disk core and disjoint radial spokes to its band windows, all on the colored sphere region; the band cores from step 10.1 complete the embedded Tait graph on $S$. Thin its surface neighborhood in the supplied disk-and-band coordinates: positive radial compressions on disk sectors and positive transverse rescalings on rectangular strips agree after the positive interval interpolations in their attaching collars. They give a smooth embedding isotopy to an arbitrarily narrow ribbon around this graph, carrying the boundary with it. Near each edge core choose smooth tubular coordinates $(s,u,v)$ with $S=\{v=0\}$. Its band is $(s,tV(s)+O(t^2))$. For sufficiently small width, replacing the remainder by $(1-r)O(t^2)$ is an embedding isotopy: projection onto $V(s)$ has strictly positive derivative uniformly, while the coordinate $s$ and disjoint finite tubes exclude all other collisions. Use the disk's product window coordinates to make this replacement fixed on attaching collars. The resulting linear ribbon is determined by its nonzero width field. Interpolate its positive lengths and the relative angle lift in step 10.1 to the standard single half-turn with the same endpoint values, fixing the collars. Choose the target lift on the same side of $\pi/2$ for each sign of $s$; the interpolation retains the unique transverse middle passage and the same overpassing boundary branch. Every intermediate ribbon remains embedded by its fixed $s$ coordinate. On the vertex disks the finite collar maps match the same windows. Thus the endpoint is precisely disks in the chosen-color regions joined by the standard half-twisted crossing bands with the prescribed over-under data. The finite face collars identify their boundary projection with $D$ up to planar isotopy, without changing any crossing or cyclic order. Apply [F6] to these compact smooth source isotopies. This proves that the two checkerboard surfaces have the asserted individual smooth ambient-isotopy classes and transported boundary orientations. [step 5.1, step 8.1, step 9.1, step 10.1, F5, F6, F8, F9, construct]

12.1 Suppose in addition that the original full pairings are definite with opposite signs. The inclusion of a component is an injection on first homology, since the homology of a finite disjoint union is its direct sum. Its pairing is the restriction of the full pairing by [F10], so the same strict sign holds on each nonzero component class. The individual smooth ambient isotopies of step 11.1 transport these restricted pairings to the two checkerboard surfaces on each projection sphere. Apply [F10]'s converse on that connected diagram. Every diagram is alternating, including the crossingless case. This uses no assumption that the original link or either surface was connected. [given, step 6.1, step 11.1, F10, algebra]


13.1 Finally suppose $p>1$. The continuous projection of each whole link component lies in one sphere, and every sphere has a nonempty graph, so each carries a nonempty group of complete link components. Compress further so each such group lies in a very thin collar of its sphere. Fix one sphere $S_a$. By [F7] its complement has two sides; some other sphere is on one of those sides, since it is connected and disjoint from $S_a$. Push $S_a$ slightly toward that side, beyond its own thin link collar but still within its collar disjoint from all other spheres and their link groups. This parallel smooth sphere has the group carried by $S_a$ on one side and the selected other group on the other, and misses the entire compressed link. It is a smooth splitting sphere. Pulling it back through the smooth isotopies from step 9.2 gives a splitting sphere for the original link. Consequently nonsplitness forces $p=1$. The empty arc list uses the identity collapse and omits all local ball operations; seam rounding, the component argument and the same conclusion still apply. Nothing in the construction used primeness, so composite nonsplit links are included. [step 6.1, step 8.1, step 9.2, step 10.2, F7, construct] ∎

## Remarks

The link remains literal during the topological local pair and seam modifications. The only changes to its smooth link type are explicit smooth rotations and smooth height-compression isotopies. Thus no passage from a general topological ambient isotopy to a smooth one is hidden in the recovery argument. The surface isotopies identify smooth ambient-isotopy classes with transported boundary links; they are not asserted to be simultaneous isotopies fixing the original link exterior pointwise.
