## Statement

Assume full AC. Supply a smooth two-sphere $\Sigma\subset S^3$ with its two parametrized closed ball sides $Y_1,Y_2$, a circle $\gamma\subset\Sigma$ dividing it into closed disks $P,Q_0$, and a closed disk $Q\subset Q_0$, and compact embedded surfaces $A_i\subset Y_i$ satisfying $A_i\cap\Sigma=P$. They may have finitely many compatible smooth corners, and need not be connected or orientable. All their components have nonempty boundary. Along $\gamma$, the portions where $A_1\setminus P$ and $A_2\setminus P$ attach are disjoint unions of finitely many open intervals $I_1,I_2$; their closures may meet at finitely many corner vertices, and gaps with no attachment are allowed. If there is an attaching interval, assume that $Q$ meets $\gamma$ along all the marked windows and is obtained from $Q_0$ by deleting finitely many boundary tabs away from those windows. A boundary tab is a disk bounded by one boundary arc of $Q_0$ and one new arc with the same endpoints; their interiors and the supports of the deletions are disjoint. The case $Q=Q_0$ is included. If there is no attaching interval, $Q$ may instead be any disk in $Q_0$. Supply their actual product attaching collars and compatible corner roundings, with no extra band twists. On the disk-flipped attaching intervals use product semicircle roundings of the folded seams, or supported smooth isotopic variants of those roundings.

Suppose the two sets
$$F=P\cup E_1\cup E_2,\qquad F^\dagger=Q\cup E_1\cup E_2,$$
where $E_i=\overline{A_i\setminus P}$, with those product collars rounded give compact smooth embedded surfaces with nonempty boundary and no closed components. Their boundary links need not be assumed equal. This is a supplied marked spherical disk-flip model, not merely an abstract homeomorphism of surfaces. Then their full rational normal-transfer pairings are isometric, including all cross-component entries. In the direct-sum coordinates obtained by cutting cycles at the attaching intervals and joining them through the disk, the isometry is the identity on the first side and minus the identity on the second. Consequently the component counts, first Betti numbers, signatures and either sign of definiteness are preserved. No primeness assumption is used.

In particular, this conclusion applies to a supplied finite exterior plumbing cap $V$ and shadow $U$ with a completed disk $\widehat U$: assume that $\widehat U\cup V$ has compatible smooth sphere and attaching-collar roundings, meets the exterior surface exactly in the disk $U$, and has the finite boundary-tab pattern just specified. The replacement $(F\setminus U)\cup V$, with its supplied roundings and annular collars back to the boundary link, preserves the full pairing and either definite sign. This last clause requires the actual finite cap data; it does not infer them from a drawing.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Full AC and the actual two parametrized balls, their common sphere disks, the marked attaching intervals, surfaces on their respective sides and the two supplied embedded smooth roundings. For the exterior-cap clause, the actual completed sphere, finite tabs and annular link collars are also supplied.

[F1] Compact boundary surfaces have finite graph presentations and full normal-transfer pairings. Classes have oriented multicurve representatives, allowing integer coefficients. Pairings restrict to subsurfaces, are transported by smooth ambient isotopy, and are extended rationally. ([[lem-normal-transfer-pairing-and-signature-under-component-connecting-tubes]], [[lem-normal-transfer-linking-defines-a-symmetric-surface-pairing]])

[F2] Mayer–Vietoris is exact for a two-set open cover. ([[thm-mayer-vietoris-sequence-in-singular-homology]])

[F3] Smooth ball windows and their disk collars can be normalized and absorbed. The finite smooth surface normalization gives disk parameters with prescribed attaching collars; finite smooth source isotopies extend ambiently. ([[lem-smooth-ball-windows-and-boundary-disk-absorption]], [[lem-finite-c2-surface-carriers-have-smooth-normal-forms-and-relative-cap-approximations]], [[lem-choice-free-isotopy-extension-for-a-compact-smooth-source]])

[F4] Positive-meridian linking is homological and evaluates a positive meridian as one. A compact curve contained in a smaller parametrized ball has linking zero with a disjoint cycle outside that ball, since contraction in the ball supplies its bounding chain. ([[def-one-variable-alexander-module-of-an-oriented-link]])

[F5] Jordan curves on the sphere bound disk regions. A smooth sphere in the three-sphere has two smoothly parametrized ball sides under countable choice. ([[lem-jordan-schoenflies-extension-for-plane-curves]], [[thm-smooth-schoenflies-for-embedded-two-sphere-in-s-three]])

## Proof

1.1 Thicken the two side subsurfaces slightly within $F$ to make an open cover whose overlap retracts to $P$; use their product attaching collars and finite corner charts. The two open sets retract to $A_1,A_2$. Since $H_1(P)=0$ and $H_0(P)\to H_0(A_1)\oplus H_0(A_2)$ is injective, [F2] gives $H_1(F;\mathbb Q)=H_1(A_1;\mathbb Q)\oplus H_1(A_2;\mathbb Q)$. The same cover with the disk $Q$ gives the analogous coordinates for $F^\dagger$, using the same exterior pieces. To identify each old and new side group, cut a cycle at its finitely many passages across $\gamma$ and reconnect its endpoints in the new disk. Two choices of reconnection differ by cycles bounding chains in that disk, so the identification on homology is unambiguous. The identical disk attachment incidences identify the component sets of the old and new side surfaces. The degree-zero sequence also gives $c(F)=c(A_1)+c(A_2)-1=c(F^\dagger)$; the first-Betti-number equality follows from the two direct sums. [given, F1, F2, construct, algebra]

2.1 Choose finitely many multicurve representatives for bases on the sides. Make their passages across $\gamma$ transverse, avoiding its finite vertices; first-side endpoints then lie in $I_1$ and second-side endpoints in $I_2$. Within $P$ the two families of oriented arcs can be made transverse, and each color is itself embedded. The finitely many choices and perturbations are made in their actual disk and rectangle charts. Cycles on a component not meeting $P$ stay entirely inside its ball. Thus all representative ribbons and transfers miss the corner vertices and depend only on the supplied product collars. The calculation may be made before or after their arbitrarily small compatible roundings, which are smooth isotopies of these ribbons. [step 1.1, F1, F3, construct]

3.1 The diagonal pairing on either side is unchanged by replacing $P$ first with $Q_0$. For the first side alone, the other ball $Y_2$ contains no part of $A_1$ except its boundary disk. Normalize this ball and window by [F3], so $P,Q_0$ are its upper and lower hemispheres. Write the upper hemisphere as $(\sqrt{1-u^2}\cos\phi,\sqrt{1-u^2}\sin\phi,u)$, $0\le u\le1$. Choose $\chi=1$ near $u=0$ and zero for $u\ge1/2$, and a small $\delta>0$. Sweep it by $r_t(u)=\sqrt{1-u^2-\delta u\chi(u)\sin(\pi t)}$, $z_t(u)=u\cos(\pi t)$, using the points $(r_t\cos\phi,r_t\sin\phi,z_t)$. The radius is strictly decreasing in $u>0$ after choosing $\delta$ small: on the cutoff transition $2u+\delta(\chi+u\chi')\sin(\pi t)>0$, and near zero the added term is nonnegative. The radicand stays positive on that cutoff support. At the fixed boundary $u=0$ the radial and height derivatives never vanish together, and near the pole $\chi=0$, so the original smooth pole coordinates still apply. Its squared norm is $1-u^2\sin^2(\pi t)-\delta u\chi(u)\sin(\pi t)\le1$, strictly less for $u>0$ and $0<t<1$. Thus this is an actual smooth disk sweep through the ball, fixing its edge and joining $P$ to $Q_0$. Retain the first-side exterior ribbons. At an attaching interval the limiting union at $t=1$ has a folded seam, so do not concatenate the raw limiting tangent rays. Stop the sweep just before that time and join its two branches by a short semicircle in each normal product plane. Keep this radius positive while flattening the remaining cap to $Q_0$. Off that semicircle the two branches remain on their distinct ball sides; on it the arc derivative never vanishes. Smooth cutoff interpolation of their positive gaps joins this fold to the unchanged outer ribbons. This is an embedded smooth product-ribbon family, and its endpoint is exactly the stipulated semicircle rounding. All intervals used by these representatives are disjoint and avoid the corner vertices, so their supports can be chosen disjoint. No other first-side ribbon lies inside the sweeping ball. Extend this compact ribbon isotopy by [F3]; the stipulated supported variants of the roundings may be included at the end. Next indent each supplied boundary tab, keeping all attaching windows fixed. Its old and new boundary arcs bound the actual tab disk; [F3] gives a smooth lens parameter with the fixed endpoint collars. Moving one boundary arc across that lens and carrying its inward collar gives an embedding isotopy from the larger disk to the indented disk. The tab interiors and their endpoint collars miss every exterior attaching ribbon, so the finitely many indentations may be made in disjoint supports and introduce no twist. Their endpoint is $Q$. All normal-transfer linking entries within that side therefore agree by [F1]. Use $Y_1$ for the second side. If there is no attaching interval, all side homology representatives miss the disk, whose homology is zero, so this conclusion needs no disk sweep. Denote the unchanged two full diagonal matrices by $G_1,G_2$. [step 2.1, F1, F3, construct]

3.2 Compute a mixed entry using the orientation of $\Sigma$ as the boundary of $Y_1$. Let $a$ and $b$ be the oriented arcs in $P$ of a first-side and a second-side cycle $c_1,c_2$. Choose the normal-transfer radius of $c_2$ very small. Its portion in $Y_1$ then lies in an arbitrarily thin boundary collar, because $A_2\setminus P$ lies in $\operatorname{int}Y_2$; each arc $b$ has exactly one transferred lift on the $Y_1$ side and one on the $Y_2$ side, both oriented by projection. Push the portions $a$ of $c_1$ into $Y_1$ until the entire pushed cycle lies beyond that thin collar. Outside $P$ this requires only a collar adjustment near its endpoints, away from the disjoint second-side windows. The final cycle has linking zero with the transfer by [F4]. During the push the only crossings of the transfer are its one $Y_1$ lift above each transverse point of $a\cap b$. Removing a small disk from the traced annulus at each crossing shows that the linking change is its signed positive-meridian count. In oriented flat coordinates on $\Sigma$ this sign is one fixed global sign $\eta$ times the oriented intersection of $a$ and $b$; $\eta$ depends only on the chosen side and ambient orientation, not on either cycle. Consequently the old mixed pairing is a fixed sign times their disk intersection count. This argument includes weighted multicurves and gives zero when either cycle misses the disk. [step 2.1, F1, F4, construct, algebra]

4.1 Repeat exactly that calculation on $Q_0$ with the same ball side and ambient orientation, so the fixed sign is unchanged. The common boundary identification reverses disk orientation measured in $\Sigma$: the two disk regions have opposite induced boundary orientations along $\gamma$. Hence reconnection from $P$ to $Q_0$ reverses the intersection number of every two arc families with the same endpoint order. One can also verify this by choosing disk parameters with the same boundary parameter and reflecting one parameter disk; intersection signs reverse pointwise. The tab indentations are orientation-preserving disk embeddings fixing the attachment windows, so they do not alter this arc intersection count when $Q_0$ is replaced by $Q$. Thus every mixed entry changes sign. With the coordinates of step 1.1, the two full matrices are therefore $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}G_1&C\\C^T&G_2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}G_1&-C\\-C^T&G_2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Their congruence by $\operatorname{diag}(I,-I)$ proves the asserted isometry. All cross-component entries have stayed in their original full diagonal blocks or in this explicitly computed mixed block. [step 1.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, F1, construct, algebra]

5.1 An invertible isometry preserves the kernel, signature and strict sign of the quadratic form, including its zero-dimensional case. The component and rank equalities were proved in step 1.1. Nothing used link primeness, connectedness or orientability of the supplied side surfaces. The assertion concerns the actual supplied marked disk-flip data, including its fold and tab collars. [step 1.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 For the exterior-cap clause set $\Sigma=\widehat U\cup V$, using its supplied compatible smoothing, and parametrize its two ball sides by [F5]. The shadow disk is $P=U$; let $Q_0$ be its complementary disk in $\Sigma$. The actual cap $V$ is a subdisk of $Q_0$. The remaining parts of $\widehat U$ are exactly its supplied boundary tabs, so set $Q=V$. Each piece of the old surface off $U$ lies on one ball side, and its attaching intervals on $\partial U$ are assigned to that side. At an interval there is just one exterior half-sheet, because the original surface is embedded; the finite interval and corner data make these precisely the two disjoint marked families. Thus $A_i=U\cup\overline{(F\setminus U)\cap\operatorname{int}Y_i}$ satisfy the hypotheses. Their components have boundary: an untouched closed component would be a closed component of $F$, while every cut component meets a marked cut edge or the old boundary. Apply steps1.1–5.1 to obtain the full pairing isometry for the two exterior surfaces. Finally the supplied annular collars from their boundary curves back to the boundary link give deformation retractions from the completed spanning surfaces to their exterior portions. Interior representatives and their short normal transfers can be chosen literally in those portions, so [F1] identifies their full pairings, including cross-component entries. This proves the finite-cap consequence from its actual supplied data, with no use of a four-dimensional cap-isotopy observation. [given, step 1.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, F1, F5, construct] ∎
