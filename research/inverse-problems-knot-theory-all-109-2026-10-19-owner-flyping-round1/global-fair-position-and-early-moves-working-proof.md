# Global fair position and the first moves

Mathematical working draft. This file is not an accepted supplier or an assertion of the original flyping conclusion. No main Statement is changed. The discussion below separates explicit constructions from the remaining relative-move and composite obligations.

## Reference chambers and actual local ball applications

Start with the finite crossing-ball reference model of the actual regular connected diagram. Overpasses and underpasses are semicircles on the crossing-ball boundaries; the remaining edges lie on the projection sphere. Proof1.1 of `lem-smooth-link-roundings-admit-the-cornered-bubble-normalization` supplies an explicit reference upper-ball homeomorphism, independently of that lemma's later decomposing-sphere hypothesis: in each projection disk it is

`(x,y,z) ↦ (x,y,z+ψ(x,y)ρ(z))`,

where ψ is the continuous hemisphere height and `||ψρ'||<1`. Each vertical fiber map is strictly increasing and has continuous inverse. Reflected maps give the lower reference ball. At an equatorial edge, coordinates `a=z`, `b=r²+z²−r₀²` give `a,b≥0`; putting `u=a−b`, `v=a+b` gives the supplied product corner `v≥|u|`. This is actual finite corner data, not a smooth map from a corner to a smooth surface.

The finite endpoint adapters and the smooth reference link are provided by `lem-finite-planar-link-corners-have-compatible-smooth-roundings`, including exterior-only rounding that retains each entire semicircle. The topology of the upper and lower reference chambers is not affected by choosing a different sufficiently fine smooth rounding inside the adapters. All later disk arguments can be made in individual inward smooth roundings of these reference balls. `lem-product-edge-corner-ball-has-a-relative-inward-rounding` preserves the topology and circle incidences of transverse surface pieces and retains a chosen compact chord and interior joining arc away from the edge collars. Its actual fiber map is monotone and its smooth boundary is a genuine smooth sphere, so smooth Schoenflies supplies the ball parameters. A simultaneous smooth partition of all of three-space is unnecessary.

For use with proper spanning surfaces, a link tube must still be incorporated explicitly. In a regular edge chart, its intersection with either side is the boundary-attached half-tube `y²+z²≤ε²`, with the edge as axis and the projection face `z=0`. In a crossing-face chart the same construction is in the collar of the semicircle. Endpoint adapters are first included as boundary-attached cap balls, and their thin collars are absorbed before the edge half-tubes are joined. The resulting neighborhood is a finite union of vertex balls and edge tubes around each link component; its disk fibers are oriented by the ambient orientation and the link orientation, so it is a solid-torus neighborhood with actual product charts. The half-tube cutouts on a reference side are boundary indentations. Their height profiles may initially be piecewise smooth; on each collar, the map `t ↦ t+d(s)η(t)`, with `d≥0` and `||d η'||<1`, identifies the unindented and indented balls. Subsequent individual inward rounding gives actual smooth ball parameters while retaining the specified interior surface pieces.

The adapter/tube matching can be made before choosing the book coordinates. Choose each adapter as a parametrized ball containing the entire exterior-only rounding and meeting only its two adjacent link segments. The positive graph coordinate of the rounded arc supplies a straight core in that ball: the explicit fiber translation subtracting the smooth graph height, with transverse cutoff and positive fiber derivative, gives the parameter. Join these ball parameters by disk tubes along the regular link segments, retaining product collars at their ends. This is an actual zero/one-handle neighborhood of a finite cycle, not an arbitrary neighborhood of a topological curve. A spanning path merges its zero handles successively by disk-window absorption; its last disk tube closes the two remaining windows. The oriented disk fibers glue to the product solid torus, with any final rotation absorbed by a smooth angular interpolation along the interval before closing. Thus it has a smooth projection onto the actual rounded link. The neighborhood may be made tiny outside the adapters and contains each entire adapter. Consequently its intersections with the reference chambers are boundary-attached caps and half-tubes, even when the rounded link briefly leaves a reference face inside an adapter. Removing the entire cap, rather than just a thin tube around that intrusive arc, is essential: the latter could drill a tunnel.

At a common-axis endpoint the axis is transverse to the tube boundary. Take local defining functions b,w for B,W and a boundary defining function z for the tube. Their differentials are independent because the intersection of B and W is the transverse axis. The Euclidean inverse function theorem therefore makes (z,b,w) actual simultaneous coordinates: the two pages are coordinate planes and the book end is exactly a tube-boundary disk. Along the interval axis, choose its two page defining functions by finitely many overlapping cooriented charts, matching their signs. Their two independent normal derivatives persist under a finite interpolation of the charts. Choose the longitudinal coordinate to agree with the two boundary functions near its ends and have positive derivative along the axis, interpolating the positive derivatives on the middle interval. The map consisting of these three functions has invertible derivative along the axis, hence is injective on a sufficiently thin tube by compactness and local inverses. This supplies a whole book with both actual end disks matched. No arbitrary boundary sphere map is extended.

The remaining chamber corners are finite actual orthant corners, including vertices. They admit individual smooth inward roundings as follows. Let a_i be the finitely many inward face defining functions, smoothly extended positively off their face collars; unrelated collars are disjoint after shrinking. In each incident-face chart their active differentials are independent. For a small ε put f(t)=exp(1−1/(1−t/(2ε))) for t<2ε and f(t)=0 for t≥2ε. Then f(0)=1, f(t)>1 for t<0, and f is strictly decreasing and smooth until its flat zero. Replace the chamber near its boundary by Σ_i f(a_i)≤1. This lies inside the old orthant and equals its old single smooth face wherever only one collar is active. Its boundary is smooth: the gradient is a negative combination of the independent active face gradients, with at least one nonzero coefficient, and so cannot vanish. Finite shrinkings ensure this independence throughout the active collar overlaps.

This rounding preserves the ball type by an actual collar homeomorphism. Choose a smooth inward field in each orthant chart, with da_i(V)>0 on all active faces, and combine the finitely many fields with nonnegative normalized bumps. The inequalities persist. Along a V-flow line, Σ_i f(a_i) decreases strictly until the new boundary is reached. While that sum is at least1, some summand is at least the reciprocal of the finite number of faces, so its derivative has a uniform negative bound on the compact collar. The hitting time is finite and continuous. The old corner collar itself is parameterized by these lines: backward exit time is the minimum of finitely many smooth face hitting times, hence continuous, including at edges and vertices. An increasing change of their interval parameters carries the old boundary to the new one and is the identity beyond the collar. Thus the rounded chamber is homeomorphic to the original ball; its smooth spherical boundary then has actual smooth ball parameters by smooth Schoenflies. This is not a claim that an arbitrary topological ball parameter is smooth.

For a prepared surface, avoid the finitely many vertices first by an arbitrarily small proper perturbation. Choose their rounding neighborhoods disjoint from it. At each remaining edge intersection, transversality gives the local surface equation s=h(a,b), where s is the edge coordinate. The rounding in that surface is exactly the rounding of a quadrant; its topology and its boundary intervals are retained by the monotone fiber calculation in the product-edge supplier. Compactness gives finitely many such charts. Outside those charts the old face transversality persists. Hence the individual rounded ball has the same disk-piece topology and boundary-circle incidences as the reference chamber. Chosen compact interior joining arcs and core-face chords are retained by making the corner collars disjoint from them. The construction is individual to the required chamber and surface; no simultaneous smooth partition is required.

## The finite book normalization is explicit

In a supplied book `(z,x,y)`, `W={y=0}`, `B={x=0}` and the axis is `{x=y=0}`. If F contains the axis and is transverse to W, the inverse function theorem and compactness give one graph `x=f(z,y)` with `f(z,0)=0`. The supported field

`(k y−f(z,y))∂x`

is zero on W and tangent to the end caps. Its cutoff is one on the inner graph track and zero near the outer lateral boundary. Its unique flow realizes `x=(1−t)f+t k y`. Compact separation excludes every other sheet from a sufficiently thin final book. If the axis meets F transversely, each local sheet is `z=h_j(x,y)`. The supported field

`(z_j−h_j(x,y))∂z`

vanishes on the axis and is tangent to W. It realizes the straight interpolation to `z=z_j`. The finite fields have disjoint height supports and extend over the supplied end collars.

The final transverse sheet has four page-seam corners and exactly two arcs on each of the two opposite side pairs. A common-axis band `x=k y`, `k>0`, lies wholly in the designated opposite pair `xy>0`. These coordinate assertions are proved in the new choice-free finite-book source; no endpoint category conversion is involved.

It is permissible for the initial fair-position construction to have no common axes at all. Move the finitely many endpoints of F intersected with W away from the finitely many book endpoints, preserving their positive intersection signs, and perturb the interior arcs transversely to the fixed axes in W. Then F meets each axis in finitely many transverse interior points. This only requires a small proper isotopy of the arc system on W; a relative ambient extension must be tangent to the actual link-tube boundary. Near a proper arc endpoint, use simultaneous coordinates in which W and the boundary torus are coordinate half-planes: extend its tangential velocity in the two normal coordinates with finite bumps. On the overlap their tangencies agree, so a finite normalized bump sum retains both tangencies. Its supported evolution fixes the link core and preserves W and the boundary torus. Interior arc charts are ordinary surface charts. This gives the required proper perturbation without changing the standard-arc signs.

## Disk pieces preserve the prepared crossing chambers

Suppose the actual finite crossing/end-cap data have been matched and the local conditions (a)–(g) hold. Complete the exterior F and W by their supplied radial boundary annuli to the original link. Take G=W and take Q to be the union of the prepared crossing chambers. Every component of F intersected with Q is a disk meeting W. Each Q component itself meets W. The upper and lower open-side ball interiors miss W, Q and the link tube.

A nondisk proper piece has a geometric compression disk D in an individual smooth inward rounding of its chamber. Its boundary c lies in the chamber interior. Geometric incompressibility supplies a disk E in F. Because c misses W and the entire F/W intersection consists of proper arcs, E misses W: a trace arc meeting E would be trapped wholly inside that interior disk and could not reach the link boundary. Likewise E misses Q, since any crossing piece meeting E would be wholly inside E and yet meet W. The product boundary annuli show E misses the link tube as well.

The two-copy sphere from E and D has a link-free ball side. The disk-replaced F, W, every component of Q and every tube component stay outside that ball: they miss its boundary and meet the link or W on the outside. The actual ball-window graph isotopy therefore fixes W, Q and the link tube. It removes the whole crossing-free patch E and inserts a disk wholly inside the same open chamber. It deletes complete reference wall traces and creates none. At least one wall trace is deleted, since otherwise E would be a disk in the original nondisk chamber piece. Thus the finite total wall-trace count decreases strictly.

After finitely many such replacements every chamber piece is a disk, while all prepared crossing pieces and boundary data remain fixed. Conditions (a), (c)–(g) are retained literally. The new disks miss B and W in the chamber interiors, and all retained wall intersections are old transverse ones, so (b) is retained. This proves the disk-piece part (h) from actual prepared input; it does not fill the outstanding adapter/book-end matching by an abstract ball claim.

## First move: the relative white-face case

For a Move1 arc α on the common face, its endpoints lie on the same upper and lower disk boundaries. In individual actual smooth side balls, retaining α and the chosen proper joining arcs, the cut-ball parameters give parallelism disks X_+ and X_−. Glue them along α. Their union is an embedded disk D outside F, with boundary a circle c in F. Incompressibility gives the replacement disk E in F. If α lies in a black face, D misses W and the preceding empty-ball argument applies relative to W.

If α lies in a white face, D intersects W exactly in α and c intersects W twice. Consequently E intersects W in exactly one arc ω: no intersection circle occurs, and all other trace arcs would be trapped in E. The curve δ=α∪ω is a simple circle on W. The sphere E∪D intersects W precisely in δ. Either disk of this sphere bounded by δ has interior disjoint from W, so incompressibility of W gives a disk R in W bounded by δ. Its interior misses F: every other F/W arc would have to enter through δ, which it cannot do, while its endpoints lie on the link outside R. The continuation of the ω arc at either endpoint likewise lies outside R. Thus R is an actual empty surface bigon.

A relative surface isotopy on W slides ω across R while retaining the exterior collars of the proper arc. The endpoint issue differs from the earlier nugatory-crossing defect: here both unchanged continuations lie outside R, as just proved; no reflex corner containing a continuation is permitted. In the two endpoint half-disks use a positive graph coordinate and interpolate the graph; away from them use the actual disk parameter. Extend the surface velocity tangent to W and to the link-tube boundary in the finite product charts. This proves that the new intersection arc is isotopic in W to the old one and retains its standard type.

To complete this Move1 proof, the bigon slide must be matched to the two empty half-balls cut off by R in the link-free ball bounded by E∪D. W\R meets the link and lies outside that ball; transversality along δ forces R inside. Thus R is its proper cutting disk. The joint disk-window matching, including its relative ambient extension, is constructed in the section below. An arbitrary sphere-map extension is not substituted for these parameters.

## A direct strict cancellation for white return arcs

There is a way to replace the neutral Move2 macro by the already constructed two-saddle patch exchange. Assume the prepared disk-piece configuration and no intersection circles. Let ω be an outermost arc of F intersected with one white core face, with both ends on the same crossing chamber. Its bigon with that chamber boundary has interior disjoint from F. Because the diagram is reduced, the two white core sectors at a crossing belong to different core disks. Hence the two ends of ω meet distinct saddle disks, not the two ends of one saddle's W-section. Their levels are adjacent: an intermediate saddle has a W endpoint on the chamber-boundary interval of the empty bigon, whose proper continuation into that white core would have to enter its interior. It could neither cross ω nor leave through another edge, so it would give an additional arc in the supposedly empty bigon.

The two saddle boundaries therefore bound an empty annulus in the lateral crossing-chamber boundary, between their consecutive levels. It misses the link tube, and in particular the overpass. Its upper quadrant contains the empty spanning rectangle R between the two upper saddle arcs. Those arcs lie on one upper disk E, since ω joins their incident ends on the common white face. Retain a small spanning core of R and a proper joining arc β inside E. An individual inward-rounded upper ball retaining these compact sets has the same disk-piece topology. The bridge-disk construction from `lem-empty-boundary-rectangle-supplies-a-surface-bridge-disk` applies literally there.

Use that actual bridge's normal collar on both sides, exactly as in Proof2.1–5.1 of `lem-empty-overpass-free-rectangle-cancels-two-saddles`. The saddle disks joined by a narrow band about β form a disk P_0, regardless of whether the whole F is a sphere. The annulus cut along R, together with the two framed parallel bridge copies, is an embedded disk P_1 with the same boundary and interior disjoint from F and the link. The side-coordinate verification is unchanged: the side ball occupies v≥0,w≥0, the region between saddles v≥0,w≤0, and both collars approach the v>0 side. Thus no extra ribbon twist or crossed long-side pairing is introduced.

The replacement works for the present spanning surface without a two-point-sphere hypothesis. The link-free sphere from P_0 and P_1 has a ball side missing the link. Every component of F with the interior of P_0 deleted is connected to a boundary component of the link, so it lies outside that ball. The tube also lies outside it. The same empty-ball window isotopy replaces this specified disk P_0 by P_1, fixing the link tube and all other crossing chambers. It deletes precisely the selected two saddles. There was no crossing band in this chamber, since a prepared crossing band excludes the other chamber pieces.

Track W explicitly. Before the exchange, its two saddle sections are joined on one side by ω and are part of one proper F/W arc. After the exchange, the two longitudinal W-arcs in the annulus complement pair the four ends differently. On the ω side they give exactly one new intersection circle; on the other side they give the shortcut of the original proper arc. All other intersections are unchanged. The new circle bounds the visibly empty bigon disk on W consisting of the original return bigon and the thin annular collar. It also bounds a disk in F by the opposite-definite common-circle result, since F is ambient isotopic to its original definite representative. That disk contains no other intersection arc, since proper arcs reach the link and cannot be trapped in it. It contains no crossing piece, because every crossing piece meets a proper F/W arc. Thus the circle can be removed by the proved circle-removal disk exchange, with every crossing chamber and the link tube protected. No saddle is reintroduced.

The shortcut proper arc is isotopic to the original in W with the common crossing bands fixed. Their difference bounds the actual disk formed by the white return bigon and the strip between the two adjacent saddle sections in W. That disk can cross the intervening axis segment, but that axis was not a common crossing band. Its boundary endpoints on the link tube are unchanged, so the new arc is still standard. Apply the relative disk-piece completion to repair any nondisk upper/lower piece introduced by the exchange, fixing all crossing chambers, W and the tube. The final configuration is again prepared, its intersection with W consists only of standard arcs isotopic to the previous ones away from the common crossing bands, and the first complexity has decreased by exactly two. This gives a strict cancellation procedure for every white return bigon, with no primeness assumption.

This paragraph uses the local annulus/bridge construction for a general spanning F by repeating its actual proof. It does not cite the old sphere-scoped Statement as if it already stated the generalization. It also does not say the intermediate exchange is ambiently relative to W: it passes through W and creates a temporary circle. W is a fixed background carrier, and the end arc system is isotopic within it. Confusing these two meanings of “fix W” would make the intersection-circle cleanup impossible.

## Relative matching for the white-face disk exchange

The joint window coordinates in the white-face case can be built using only ball windows and a finite proper-arc straightening. The disk R lies in the link-free ball V: W with the interior of R removed is connected to its link boundary outside V, and transversality of W to the sphere δ forces the other local side, namely the interior of R, inside V. Cutting V along R gives two balls. To see this without assuming disk unknotting, cap R with either of the two boundary disks cut off by δ on the boundary sphere. Each capped sphere has a ball side contained in V by smooth Schoenflies; the two contained sides lie on opposite sides of R and exhaust V. Their common R collar is the original two-sided smooth collar of R.

Parametrize R as a disk with its two distinguished boundary arcs ω and α. Standardize it as a boundary window in each of the two cut balls using the same R parameter and the same product collar. The ball-window proof transports a specified window parameter by its actual boundary isotopy and extends that isotopy through a collar; it does not require extending an arbitrary sphere diffeomorphism. Each complementary boundary disk is divided by one proper arc of c into the old and new disk sides.

Here is the needed boundary-disk adjustment. Two proper arcs in an unpunctured disk with the same endpoint collars can be matched by an isotopy fixed near the disk boundary. Put their interiors in finite transverse position. Cutting along the fixed arc gives two disks. An outermost piece of the other arc cuts off a clean disk with a subinterval of the fixed arc; choose an innermost such piece to make the interior empty. If the piece is incident to a common endpoint, use its actual half-disk endpoint sector. The smooth disk parameter supplies an embedded bigon slide, and a positive graph coordinate on the endpoint sector supplies the fixed-collar join. Each slide removes two interior intersections, or the one endpoint-sector intersection. After finitely many slides the arcs are disjoint in their interiors; their union bounds the remaining empty disk, and the same parameter sweep matches them. Extending the arc velocity with finite bumps, with its boundary-normal component divided by the inward coordinate before extension, makes the field vanish on the boundary. The prescribed endpoint collars were already matched, so all supports can miss a whole boundary collar. No assertion about the entire diffeomorphism group of the disk is used.

Apply this to the two complementary boundary disks of the cut balls. At the two common marked corners use the existing transverse product charts to choose the target arc germs equal to the old ones. The boundary isotopies are then fixed near the R boundary and extend over each ball by its boundary collar, with the identity on the R window and its smaller product collar. The two ball maps consequently glue smoothly along the common R collar. This gives joint window parameters for V,R and the old/new disk halves, with all retained outside collars matched. In their common thin-cap coordinates the disk-flattening graph field is tangent to the vertical R plane. It therefore supplies the two halves of one embedding isotopy, whose intersection arc moves from ω to α by the previously described bigon sweep.

The ambient extension can also be made tangent to W, rather than merely fixing W as an unacted-on background. Near the transverse track/W intersection use a defining coordinate r for W. The normal component of the track velocity vanishes when r=0, and on the transverse track it is r times a smooth coefficient by the fundamental theorem of calculus. Extend that coefficient and multiply by r. Extend tangential components normally. A finite normalized bump sum agrees with the velocity on the whole compact track and is tangent to W. The same argument in neat corner charts keeps it tangent to the tube boundary. Its compact support misses the link. The supported flow therefore preserves W setwise, fixes the link, and realizes the required disk exchange. Thus the end intersection arc is isotopic in W to the old one with the retained proper collars.

## Elimination of Move1 after strict white-return cancellation

A black-face Move1 would give an incompressibility disk E as above whose boundary misses W. The proper-arc hypothesis forces E to miss W. Since every crossing piece meets W and the boundary of E misses all crossing chambers, E misses all of them. Its intersection with B has just two boundary endpoints, so one of its arcs joins those two endpoints wholly in one black core face. They would therefore lie on the same component of F intersected with the common face, contrary to the Move1 hypothesis that they lie on distinct such arcs. This rules out a black Move1.

For a white Move1, the preceding relative argument gives the empty W-bigon R between α and a subarc ω of one proper F/W arc. The endpoints are on distinct white common-face arcs, so ω must pass through a crossing chamber and meet an axis. The axes intersect R in proper arcs with all endpoints on ω: the other boundary arc α misses every axis, and the axis endpoints lie on the link torus outside the interior disk R. Choose an outermost axis arc in R. It cuts off a disk bounded by that axis segment and a subarc of ω, with interior missing F and every axis. The subarc leaves and returns to the same crossing chamber and has no intervening chamber visit, since every crossing piece meets its axis. It therefore supplies a white return bigon in a single white core face. This contradicts the completed strict return-arc cancellation. Consequently no Move1 remains after that finite cancellation, for nonsplit definite spans without a primeness assumption.

## Boundary tori and the boundary-parallel arc case

Here is the boundary-torus argument for the full nonsplit link, rather than an import of a knot-only Statement. Assume the link is nonsplit and is not the one-component unknot. Suppose a disk X in its exterior has essential simple boundary c on the component torus T_i. Regard X as a disk in the exterior of the single knot L_i, by filling back the other link tubes. The actual knot-exterior homology interface says its peripheral kernel is generated by the degree-one preferred longitude. Since c is essential and simple on T_i, its homology class is primitive: cut the torus along c, obtaining an annulus, and join its two boundary circles to obtain a dual curve meeting c once. Thus [c] is plus or minus that longitude.

There is an embedded annulus from c to L_i inside its actual product tube. For completeness, isotope c on the product torus to the graph (e^{it},e^{ikt}) representing its core-degree-one primitive class. Realize this circle isotopy at strictly decreasing radii from1 to1/2; the nonzero radial derivative makes its track an embedded annulus even if the isotopy trace on the torus itself overlaps. Make the circle isotopy stationary near its final time. Continue inward by (r,t)↦(e^{it},r e^{ikt}), down to r=0. This is a smooth annulus up to its core boundary and matches the first annulus in their stationary collars. This is the actual construction in `lem-nontrivial-knot-exterior-boundary-incompressible` Proof3.1, not an assertion that the entire torus isotopy trace is embedded. The resulting annulus and X give a smooth embedded disk with boundary L_i, disjoint from every other component.

A sufficiently small smooth thickening of this supplied disk is a ball: use its disk parameter, its trivial normal interval bundle, and rounded product corners. Slightly extend the disk parameter past its boundary before thickening, so L_i lies in the interior of that ball. Compact separation makes the ball disjoint from the other components. If there are other components its boundary is a splitting sphere, contrary to nonsplitting. If there are no other components, the actual disk gives an unknot by the disk-to-unknot supplier. This proves boundary-torus incompressibility in either case. Only actual smooth disk and tube parameters were used. The full-AC main scope supplies the homology and torus-slope interfaces; no claim that this paragraph is choice-free is needed.

Consequently an essential spanning F has the boundary-arc property needed for Move3. Suppose β⊂F is parallel outside F to an arc α⊂T_i whose interior misses ∂F, through an embedded disk X. Boundary-incompressibility of F gives a disk Y⊂F between β and an arc η⊂∂F. Because the interior of X misses F, X and Y meet only along β. Their union, rounded and pushed off the torus along η, gives a proper disk bounded by the simple curve α∪η on T_i. Boundary-torus incompressibility makes that curve inessential. Its torus disk has interior disjoint from ∂F: the complementary arc of the essential circle ∂F∩T_i cannot lie inside that disk, as it would then make the whole circle inessential, and it cannot cross α∪η. There is only one F-boundary circle on T_i. Thus α and η are parallel through the cut boundary annulus, as required, with their actual endpoint collars.

One also obtains the following useful empty-bigon argument without a prime hypothesis or Lemma2.27(C). Suppose W is essential, F∩W has only proper arcs, and every point of ∂F∩∂W has the same positive sign on each boundary torus. Then no intersection arc is boundary-parallel in F. Otherwise choose an outermost intersection arc λ in a boundary-parallel disk of F. It cuts off a disk X⊂F whose interior misses W and whose other boundary arc η_F⊂∂F has no point of ∂W in its interior. Essentiality of W makes λ boundary-parallel in W, through a disk Y with other boundary arc η_W. Since the interior of X misses W, X and Y meet only along λ, and η_F has no other intersection with η_W. Their rounded union is therefore a proper disk with simple boundary η_F∪η_W on one boundary torus. Boundary-torus incompressibility would make this curve inessential, but the endpoint signs make it essential. Cut T along the essential circle ∂W∩T. The interior of η_F misses that circle, and its two endpoints have the same ordered crossing sign. Following η_F in its induced ∂F direction therefore leaves its first endpoint on one side of ∂W and reaches its second endpoint from the other side. Thus its lift joins the two boundary circles of the cut annulus. Closing by η_W and making a small transverse perturbation along that boundary interval gives a curve with algebraic intersection ±1 with ∂W, so η_F∪η_W is essential on T. This contradicts the proper disk just constructed. This annulus argument does not assume that an unchanged continuation of ∂F stays outside a planar bigon; a reflex endpoint is not silently excluded. Thus no such λ, and no original boundary-parallel arc in F, exists. Interchanging F,W gives the analogous conclusion when both are essential.

In particular a Move3 arc lying in a white core is impossible. Its boundary-parallel disk in the common face is an actual boundary-compression disk for F lying in W, with interior disjoint from F. Essentiality of F would make that same intersection arc boundary-parallel in F, which the previous paragraph excludes. Therefore every possible Move3 is in a black core. Its supporting bigon misses W. The torus parallelism disk between its boundary interval α and the interval η on ∂F also misses ∂W: α is contained in a black boundary interval and misses ∂W; any ∂W arc in that torus disk would have both endpoints on η with opposite intersection signs, contrary to the all-positive signs, while an entire essential ∂W circle cannot lie in a disk. Thus η misses ∂W as well, and the resulting thin supported disk sweep can be chosen disjoint from W. In actual product coordinates on the bigon and its collars, move the boundary-parallel patch of F through the appropriate side ball; extend the field tangent to the torus. This retains all crossing chambers and the complete F/W arc system. The move removes the two incident common-face traces in the outermost chosen patch, introduces none, and hence strictly decreases their finite number. Disk-piece completion, if needed after this sweep, fixes W and the crossing chambers and only deletes further wall traces. The first complexity is unchanged, the second decreases, and the absence of white return arcs is preserved literally.

For a crossingless connected diagram, crossing minimality forces every reduced alternating diagram of its link to have zero crossings. The only connected zero-crossing diagram is one circle, with its prescribed orientation transported by a sphere isotopy. This is the separate trivial case; no incompressibility of the unknot boundary torus is asserted above.

## Assembly of the initial fair position

The required initial preparation is available for the actual F and the fixed reference W, not merely for an abstract isotopic pair. Use the actual construction in `thm-opposite-definite-spanning-surfaces-recover-alternating-diagrams-for-nonsplit-links` Proof1.1–3.1, stopping before its reconstruction. Its boundary normalization changes F alone, retaining W at its original location. In a W-adapted normal tube the two radial collars have angles0 and 2πd_i s+δ_i, with d_i≥0. The normal-tube projection is the actual link parameter s, so these boundary curves are graphs over that parameter and are transverse to every meridian. Rotating the normal coordinate by the smooth W framing changes no meridian; thus this is also transversality in the specified reference tube. Choose each phase δ_i outside the finite forbidden set for the axis endpoints. The resulting torus intersections are all positive, have distinct link base parameters, and avoid those endpoints. Subsequent transverse perturbation and circle removal are supported away from smaller collars. They give finitely many proper standard F/W arcs, still retaining the actual reference W and the same meridian-transverse F boundary.

Choose the reference crossing books thin enough to miss ∂F, since ∂F misses their finite axis endpoints. ## Removing black boundary-parallel arcs directly

## The actual diagrammatic exception for a white parallel disk

The parallel-arc obstruction can be localized without silently assuming primeness. Let α_F⊂F\\W and α_W⊂W\\F have common endpoints on two distinct standard common arcs u_j,u_k, and suppose they cobound an embedded parallelism disk outside the two surfaces. Use the actual standard-pair reconstruction, transporting these chosen compact arcs in the local arc collars. The two arcs lie in one black and one white region disk of the reconstructed diagram, respectively. Call these region disks X_F,X_W. The endpoint vertices j,k are incident to both regions.

If these regions have only one common diagram edge e, both endpoint vertices must be the endpoints of e. Move the interiors of α_F,α_W toward that edge inside their actual region disks, retaining the selected common-axis endpoint positions and their transverse collar germs. This is a proper-arc disk isotopy: in each region disk the path and the appropriate boundary interval enclose a disk, and its finite smooth disk parameter gives the supported sweep. In the link-tube chart of e the two paths are now on its two checkerboard sides; the short common-axis end joins pass on opposite sides of that strand. Their union is therefore a meridian of the component containing e. Explicitly, the side-disk collars identify it with a circle in the oriented normal disk to the strand, with normal angle increasing once around that disk. Its linking number with that component is ±1. But the original union bounded the supplied parallelism disk in the link exterior, so its linking number with every component was zero. This is a contradiction. The same argument excludes zero common edges, since j,k cannot both then be incident to both disks. Consequently the two regions share at least two distinct diagram edges.

## Four-page directions and the distinct-arc white saddle

The endpoint pairing of a distinct-arc white saddle can be computed, rather than inferred from a graph flip. Orient the reconstructed sphere and its positive height. In the normalized ruled chart put θ(x)=π(1+εx)/2. After the normal-plane rotation by−θ/2 the two black half-pages have angular coordinates−θ/2 andπ−θ/2; the white ones haveθ/2 andπ+θ/2. With sphere normal in the positive axial direction, its oriented outer circle runs in increasing normal angle. The two black edges therefore both run in axial direction−ε, while the two white edges both run in direction+ε. Passing from that outer boundary to the common-axis boundary of a page reverses these directions. The under and over strands are at x=−ε and x=+ε. Thus the two black page-boundary intervals have the SAME under-to-over axis direction, and the two white ones have the opposite direction. Reversing all orientation conventions reverses both tables together. In particular two black pages never acquire independent sign choices. This is the four-edge end-cycle calculation; it does not orient the whole possibly nonorientable spanning surface.

Let α_F run inside one black disk P between distinct common arcs u_j,u_k, and let α_W run inside one white disk Q with the same endpoints, with actual parallelism disk D outside the pair. Take a rectangle about α_F, with u_j,u_k on its two short ends. The boundary orientations on the two short ends are opposite in these rectangle coordinates, whereas their canonical under-to-over directions agree with the oriented boundary of P. Consequently the over ends are on opposite long sides of the rectangle. The white finger push replaces the two short intersection arcs by the two long ones; its endpoint pairs are therefore

 (O_j,U_k) and (O_k,U_j),

not (O_j,O_k) and (U_j,U_k). The two original arcs were distinct proper arcs, so these are again two proper arcs and no intersection circle is formed. Their endpoint signs remain the original positive ones, since the interior push fixes the boundary curves. Thus they are standard arcs. All other common arcs, radial collars and crossings are unchanged. The new surface is ambient isotopic to F, retains its pairing and Betti number, and remains numerically balanced with W. The actual original-link reconstruction therefore applies at this stage.

The chamber data constrain which branches O_j/U_j and O_k/U_k meet both selected faces. Around a standard axis the four sectors are those between F+,W+,F−,W−. In the ruled half-turn their two opposite sector pairs close at opposite end matchings; they correspond to the two crossing heights. For a fixed incidence sign, the same chamber pair always corresponds to the same height. All black band incidences are equal because the reconstructed diagram is connected alternating (equivalently use the accepted signed cycle/cut-space calculation). Since int(D) lies in one chamber, its selected adjacent-page sectors at j,k choose the SAME over/under phase. The actual smooth chamber truncation above retains exactly these choices and permits replacing D by the canonical marked disk in that chamber.

Round the two selected vertex corners of the quotient boundary curve through their selected common-face branches. In each vertex cap the two page points are on adjacent boundary intervals; the short boundary interval between them contains exactly one of the four marked link endpoints. A cap arc just inside that interval therefore crosses exactly one projected arm. It can replace the original cap arc by an isotopy in the actual cap-disk interior, relative its two page endpoints. Restore the original axis-point germ at smaller radii and interpolate the cap-arc isotopy over a compact positive-radius annulus; each radial level contains one arc, so the annulus is embedded and its lift by q^{-1} is smooth. The smaller untouched corner has the original axis limit. This verifies that the smoothing uses the actual chosen chamber germ, not an arbitrary product-sphere identification. The resulting circle on the marked reconstructed sphere meets the diagram twice. The two edges are distinct: if they were one edge, its endpoint crossing phases would be opposite by alternation, contrary to the same-phase chamber condition.

These two cut points lie on one component C by Jordan parity. Moreover j,k lie on OPPOSITE capped sides of that circle. If both lay on the same side, they would be the first and last encounters of its proper C strand. The encounter count on a proper strand is even, by self-crossings counted twice and mod-two parity with closed strands. In an alternating strand its first and last phases would therefore be opposite, a contradiction. Each capped side consequently has a positive crossing count, is connected reduced alternating, and has a distinguished crossing adjacent to its cut edge: j on H₁ and k on H₂. All orientations and closed components remain in their actual side data.

The local cell changes can now be traced as a connected-sum operation. Read C so j,k are both the first crossings of their respective proper factors; if they are both last, use the reverse reading for the same calculation. Denote the incoming connector before j by p₂ and its outgoing strand into H₁ by r₁; before k the connector is p₁ and its outgoing strand into H₂ is r₂. The other strand at j is unchanged inside H₁, and the other strand at k is unchanged inside H₂. The saddle pairing above replaces the crossing using O_j by one paired with U_k, and the crossing using O_k by one paired with U_j. Hence the crossing retaining H₁'s other strand now has C passage p₁→r₂; the one retaining H₂'s other strand has passage p₂→r₁. Capping each side shows that the closed factors H₁,H₂ have exactly their original decorated diagrams, but each chosen cut edge has moved from just before its distinguished crossing to just after it.

There is no hidden independent mirror in this cell calculation. The cut pieces of P inherit its old orientation. In the merged opposite black pieces, continuing across u_j reverses the old chosen page orientation and continuing across u_k reverses it again; their old orientations therefore agree in the merger. The same two-reversal check holds for the two opposite white pieces. All other page interiors retain their actual orientations. The new standard arcs have the displayed O/U ends and their ruled models have no extra full turn. Thus the two closed factor diagrams retain both their cyclic orders and their crossing heights, up to one simultaneous global normal convention, rather than an untracked mutation of one factor.

This simultaneous cut-edge shift has an explicit flype realization. First pass the isolated H₁ factor across k using the local factor-pass flype: H₁ becomes H₁ʰ, and the cut of H₂ moves past k. Next pass that isolated H₂ factor across j in H₁ʰ: H₂ becomes H₂ʰ, and the cut of H₁ moves past j. Finally perform one entire flype. Both turnovers cancel, while the two cut edges remain shifted. The resulting oriented connected-sum diagram is exactly the preceding saddle port table. All three moves are registered flypes, with sphere planar isotopies only to shorten the crossing-free collars. This is a research proof of the distinct-arc white saddle's local diagram relation; it does not preserve the actual relative isotopy class of the common-arc system.

That last distinction is essential globally. Kindred's bad-position argument needs relative arc-system persistence after normalizing a Move10. Replacing a white saddle by the three flypes establishes a relation of recovered diagrams but does not establish that persistence. No conclusion that all general Move10 caps are flypes, or that the original full theorem is resolved, follows from this local calculation alone.


## Attachment congruence for a supplied component-tracked flype sequence

This is a finite operation on GIVEN diagram moves, not an assertion that every prime-link symmetry has already been realized. Let Q,Q′ be connected reduced alternating diagrams with a supplied finite sequence of registered flypes and sphere planar isotopies between them. Require that its transported component map carries the specified oriented attaching component of Q to the specified attaching component of Q′; if other component labels are supplied, retain them as well. Let R be another connected reduced alternating diagram with a specified oriented attaching component. Choose arbitrary noncrossing cut points on those components, with the local cut charts such that the connected sums Q#R and Q′#R are alternating. Then this supplied sequence extends to a finite flype equivalence of those two connected-sum diagrams, including their chosen endpoint cut charts.

First extend the Q sequence across R. For each move the cut point can be kept off its finite flyping circle and crossing-motion locations by a short crossing-free edge isotopy. Make the whole isolated R factor disk sufficiently small about that cut window. It is then wholly in one of the two tangle disks: leave it fixed if that disk is fixed, or carry it together with the moving tangle if that disk moves. This produces exactly the original Q move after forgetting R. Its effect on R is either the identity or the same marked horizontal turnover h; every other R detail and its orientations are carried in that factor chart. Sphere planar isotopies extend over the same factor disk and connector collars. Thus the final diagram has Q′ literally as its closed Q factor, R as either R or Rʰ, and two possibly displaced cut points. The attaching component of Q′ is the required one by the supplied tracking assumption. No other component is chosen by an unlabelled isomorphism.

Move the isolated R factor along that Q′ component to the desired Q′ cut edge, using the explicit local factor-pass flype once per crossing encounter and crossing-free edge isotopies between them. This changes only R's turnover flag and the cut point on Q′; the closed Q′ diagram remains fixed. Next move the isolated Q′ factor along the attaching component of the current R diagram to the desired ABSTRACT R cut edge. This changes only Q′'s turnover flag and the cut point on R. The abstract labels of all crossing-free edges are retained under h, so the requested R edge makes sense for both flags. The first corrected Q′ cut edge is unchanged as an abstract factor edge by this second transport. These are finite routes.

At this point the two closed factors are hᵃQ′ and hᵇR, with the two desired abstract cut edges, for a,b∈{0,1}. Their flags must be equal. Indeed on an oriented component of a connected alternating factor the crossing encounters are even and alternate. Its proper strand obtained by cutting an edge has a well-defined first O/U phase and the opposite last phase. A turnover reverses each O/U height and preserves the oriented encounter order, so it toggles that first phase. For the target connected sum the two unturned first phases agree; this is exactly alternation at its two crossing-free connector arcs. For the current connected sum, which is still alternating after actual flypes, the phases are toggled by a and b. They can agree only when a=b. If both flags are zero, the diagram is the target after the disk/connector planar isotopy. If both are one, one entire flype toggles both and gives that same target. The oriented closed components and all supplied component labels have been transported throughout.

If one factor is the crossingless unknot, its sum is the other factor and the assertion is immediate; no undefined first phase is used. Otherwise the selected component in either connected projection has crossings, so every phase and route above exists. This proves actual attachment congruence for a supplied component-tracked equivalence, rather than silently promoting an unlabelled prime theorem to a marked one. It also shows why the component-tracking hypothesis is the exact remaining issue for substituting a prime-factor equivalence into an attachment-preserving decomposition proof.


## A white internal guide cannot return to the same global common arc

Here retain the reduced marked reconstruction of the actual essential pair (F,W), and assume that the direct white return-bigon cancellations have finished. Suppose an internal white push guide has its two endpoints on the SAME proper common arc u, even though its endpoints lie on distinct white core traces. Its companion arc in the upper F disk lies in one component of F cut along its common arcs; the white guide lies in one component of W cut along those arcs. Those components are the actual disk regions of the reconstructed checkerboards. In a reduced diagram a region does not contain both sides of the same crossing-band cocore: repeated opposite sectors give a nugatory crossing. Thus each of these two region disks contains the relevant side of u as a single boundary interval. The subinterval δ of u between the chosen endpoints is consequently a literal boundary subinterval of each disk. The white guide and δ bound a disk Q in the W region, whose interior misses every common arc. No continuation of u can enter Q at one of the endpoints: it would then have to leave Q through a common arc or through the guide, or end in its interior, all impossible for this proper disjoint arc system. Thus Q is an actual empty common-arc bigon.

The guide misses all reference axes. If δ meets an unused axis, every axis component in Q has both endpoints on δ; common axes are disjoint from Q. An outermost such component cuts off the white return bigon already eliminated. If δ misses all axes, the two guide endpoints lie on one and the same white core trace. This contradicts the push-through requirement that its two endpoint trace intervals be distinct and not incident to one common-face arc. Both possibilities are excluded. Therefore every remaining internal white guide joins two distinct GLOBAL common arcs. The distinct-arc saddle calculation above applies to these guides; identifying core trace components alone would not have justified that conclusion.

This argument concerns internal white guides only. It makes no assertion about white guides having one or two link-tube endpoints. In particular the prime-scoped endpoint assertion in Kindred Proposition3.11 is not imported: his proof that a guide with two tube endpoints lies on one reference edge uses primeness. Those endpoint configurations still require their own full trace before a composite normalizer may be called nine-good.


## Attachment-preserving comparison of two supplied prime sphere systems

The following is a proposed full comparison construction, not an import of the knot-only Schubert Statement and not yet an accepted source. Its inputs are finite twice-punctured sphere systems for the SAME nonsplit oriented link, with actual component labels, smooth cap-ball parameters and prime capped factors. A prime factor here is nontrivial and has no twice-punctured sphere decomposition with two nontrivial capped factors. Keep ALL other boundary caps whenever one capped region is examined. Besides changes of cap parameters and component-preserving isotopies of capped factors, the claimed output moves are transfers of whole attached subtrees along their attaching global component and interchange of successive subtrees on that component. These latter diagram operations have the explicit factor-pass constructions already given; no reassignment of attaching components is allowed.

First make the systems S,T transverse away from their marks. On a sphere of T intersecting S choose an innermost intersection disk D containing at most one mark. Existence is finite: one intersection circle has two disk sides and one has at most one mark; otherwise the circle nesting tree has at least two disjoint leaf disks, and at most two marks in total. The interior of the chosen D misses S. Its boundary lies on one sphere A of S and its interior lies in one region R of S. The actual collar of A together with a thickening of D is a sphere with three holes: surgery replaces A by the two disks of A capped by parallel copies of D. Every other boundary cap of R is retained in its existing parameter. On the opposite region this only replaces its A cap by the same short endpoint-paired cap pieces. This is the actual local model, not a purported isotopy of the original marked sphere.

If D has no mark, componentwise parity puts both marks of A on one side of its boundary. One new sphere is unmarked. In capped R it misses the whole capped link. That capped link is nonsplit by the earlier cigar argument, so the unmarked sphere has an empty ball side. Shrink the other cap balls about their cap arcs, relative the capped link, until they miss this sphere. None can lie in its empty side, because its cap arc would then be part of the capped link in that side. Consequently pulling back the empty ball and deleting it removes neither an attachment ball nor any substituted subtree. Retain the twice-marked surgery sphere. All capped factors and their component/attachment data are unchanged up to the supplied collar isotopies.

If D has one mark, the earlier componentwise parity calculation puts that mark and both old A marks on the SAME global component C, with one A mark on each surgery disk. In the A cap choose a boundary-parallel disk with boundary ∂D and a trivial cap arc crossing it once; its explicit strip construction in the existing knot cut-and-paste proof is valid here since all other cap balls are disjoint. Joining that disk to D gives a twice-punctured sphere of capped R. Since its capped factor P is prime, one new factor is P and the other is the one-component unknot. All other global component labels of P lie in the P side. The cap balls lying in the trivial new region are not deleted. Its only capped component is C, so every one of those cap balls is attached to C. The ordered-bead construction above replaces that trivial region by its retained oriented chain of these entire parameterized balls.

Suppress the boundary sphere next to the old opposite neighbour Q. This inserts the ordered chain into Q's C component. It leaves the closed capped factor Q unchanged, and it leaves P unchanged; each transferred branch is still attached along global C. If that chain is instead placed beside its old position in P, the two descriptions differ by moving the WHOLE intervening branch factor along C through Q or P, and by reassociating the crossing-free connector intervals. A branch is the capped link in an actual ball with one proper attaching arc and any closed components, so the factor-pass construction applies to the entire branch, including every nested attachment. It never asks to interchange branches on different components. Thus the one-mark surgery has an explicit attachment-preserving transfer description, rather than preservation merely of an unordered list of unmarked factors.

Push the two surgery copies off T inside its product collar. Their new D portions miss T; their remaining intersection circles are exactly those on the two old A disks other than ∂D. Hence their total intersection count with T is the old A count minus one. The subsequent deletion of a sphere only decreases this count further. The finite nonnegative total number of S/T intersection circles therefore decreases strictly at each replacement. This yields disjoint systems after finitely many of the stated transfers. No separate genus bound or assertion that a replaced sphere is isotopic to its original is used.

For the disjoint systems use their common union. Adding a disjoint twice-punctured sphere to a capped prime region replaces its factor P by P and an unknot; the component and ordered-attachment analysis is exactly the preceding one-mark analysis without an intersection disk. Adding one to a capped unknot region gives two unknots. Here a knot-only argument is legitimate because that region has EXACTLY ONE capped component: take its spanning disk, make it transverse to the new sphere, remove intersection circles by replacing a subdisk by an innermost sphere disk and pushing slightly, and retain the unique intersection arc with endpoints at the two punctures. Cutting the spanning disk along this arc gives boundary-parallelism disks for the two proper arcs in the two sphere balls. Their capped knots are therefore unknots. All retained attachment balls again occur on that one global component.

It follows, by adding the finitely many union spheres, that each old prime region contains exactly one nontrivial prime region of the common refinement; everything else inside it is a tree of trivial one-component connectors. Collapsing those connectors by their ordered-bead parameters recovers the original capped factor, with its ACTUAL global component labels and its incident branches. Doing this from S or from T gives a common collection of prime factors and a finite sequence of the explicit same-component branch transfers relating their attachment descriptions. This is stronger than equality of unordered factor isotopy classes. A trivial connector may have more than two incident caps: the ordered chain, rather than a valence-two suppression assumption, is what accounts for all of them.

To turn this comparison into a diagram argument, a prime factor replacement must still have a flype sequence carrying the intended component labels. Given such sequences, their extension across all incident branches follows by applying attachment congruence successively, rooting the finite tree at that factor. At each extension all previously attached branches are part of the existing diagram, and the next branch is one whole isolated one-string factor. The extended sequence retains the global component labels of both. Prescribed positions on the same component may be corrected by factor transport and successive-branch interchange; positions on different components are never exchanged. Thus no extra multi-attachment invariance is assumed. The still separate prime-stage obligation is to establish the needed component-tracked sequences by the actual geometric proof, not by citing its unlabelled Statement.


## Component markings in the actual prime pair-uniqueness argument

This is an audit of the ACTUAL proof, not an invocation of an unlabelled theorem with extra hypotheses. For prime L, the generic-intersection argument proving Proposition6.11 may be run with these labels. Its four event types can be checked individually. ## Prime white-guide exclusions after the direct early normalization

This paragraph is PRIME-scoped and will only be used for the prime stages of the attachment comparison. The actual prime parallel-arc argument gives the needed obstruction without enlarging the scope of any source: for an essential opposite-definite pair spanning a prime link, two parallel arcs on its cut-open disk regions must have endpoints on the same common arc and be parallel into that arc. Indeed different common arcs would give two differently coloured regions of the prime reconstructed sphere with two shared crossing vertices. Those regions share at most one edge, since two distinct shared edges bound a nontrivial two-point diagrammatic sum circle. Along their one shared edge the two arcs make the local meridian of the actual link tube. Its mod-two linking with that component is one, whereas a parallelism disk in the exterior would give zero. This contradiction leaves only the same-common-arc case, where the disk-region boundary interval gives the two parallelisms.

An internal white guide and its companion arc on the upper F disk are such a parallel pair. The same-global-arc return argument above then gives an empty u/v bigon, unless the guide endpoints belong to the same white core trace. The former has already been eliminated and the latter violates the push-through nonincidence condition. Therefore there is no internal white push in the prime case. Notice the order: the prime parallel-arc obstruction supplies the common-arc identification; it is not inferred merely from two core trace components.

For a guide with one tube endpoint, perform its formal boundary finger move temporarily. It adds two boundary-intersection points x+,x− of opposite signs. The arc ω with endpoint x− is nonstandard. In the prime essential setting the actual nonstandard-arc lemma makes ω boundary-parallel on W. Let W0 be its disk, with other endpoint z. The nonincidence condition forces W0 to meet an unused reference axis; otherwise the guide endpoints would be incident to the same common-face trace. Choose an outermost axis subdisk W1 not containing x− on its boundary. Its common-arc side cannot be an old u side, since the early normalization removed all one-u/one-v bigons and triangles. Hence it is one of the two NEW sides produced by the finger move. The only new endpoint lying there is x+, so z=x+. But then the new arc ω joins the two new points to one another, and tracing the inverse finger move makes it a circle of the old F∩W. That old pair has no circle. This excludes the one-tube-end case. The temporary nonstandard-arc argument is used ONLY with prime L.

For two tube endpoints, the same prime nonstandard-arc lemma makes the guide's white-core interval boundary-parallel on W. Its disk contains no reference axis: any such axis would have both endpoints on the disk's boundary interval in ∂W and would itself be boundary-parallel on W, contrary to reducedness. The boundary interval therefore contains no crossing-band endpoint, so both tube ends belong to the same reference edge. At this point the ordinary edge uniqueness argument is needed, rather than a sign claim about arbitrary two-end guides. Its full disk argument is as follows.

If a component γ of F∩S± meets that edge in two distinct tube intervals, choose an edge-strip arc joining those intervals. Essentiality of F makes it parallel in the edge torus to an interval η of ∂F. If η crosses just one checkerboard colour, list its 2m common-face intersections in order. Their common-face traces are distinct, since a repeated trace would give the removed boundary-cap move. Join successive intersection points by arcs in their incident upper and lower F disks. If that disk misses W, a B trace in it must enter and leave through the same common-face trace, contradicting the distinct list. If it misses B on its boundary, take an innermost B circle in it. Its F disk meets the reference axes, since otherwise its boundary would be a forbidden closed common-face trace; an outermost axis gives one of the removed u/v bigons. Both possibilities are impossible. Thus η crosses both colours. In the torus parallelism disk, an outermost W subarc then has two boundary intersections of opposite sign on η, contrary to all-positive F/W boundary intersections. Hence γ has only one tube interval on that edge. A two-tube-end white guide would therefore have both endpoints on that one interval, violating the defining distinct-end-interval condition. This completes the prime white-guide exclusions.

Together with the existing finite black-push procedure, these exclusions give an ACTUAL finite prime nine-good normalization. ## The (B+,W+) lower-endpoint row: explicit collar construction

Here is a direct construction for the ordinary-edge row of the table, retaining the possible intervening common-band chain in δ. Write p=δ− on ∂B and q=δ+ on ∂W. Let βp be the common black trace incident to p on γ0, and let λq be the upper link-tube interval of γ0 incident to q. Use first the portion of the latter on the ordinary edge E adjacent to q. The distinct-endpoint condition holds because one interval is a common-face interval and the other a tube interval; the only common-face interval which could meet both is βp itself. The absence of black type8 hence forces that incidence. Denote r=βp∩λq, the B endpoint of λq. It remains to justify that this endpoint is still on E. An upper tube interval can traverse at most one overpass before stopping: alternation makes the next pass an underpass, which is not part of this upper tube region. If λq traversed that overpass, its two incident black sectors would be the opposite black sectors of that crossing. The black core containing βp would then have to occupy both sectors, since it is the black region on the E side of the test chord and also contains βp at r. Reducedness excludes repeated opposite sectors. Thus λq does not traverse a pass, and r is on E. This establishes the ordinary-edge assertion from the forced incidence; it is not assumed for an arbitrary upper interval.

The lower circle Γ containing δ also contains βp: at p a common-face trace is incident to both upper and lower pieces. Thus Γ contains the connected path q→δ→p→βp→r. Let ωq be its white common-face interval at q, and let μr be its lower tube interval at r. In particular ωq and μr lie on the SAME lower circle; this is obtained by the displayed actual path and not assumed from their common projection. The edge interval λq lies between q and r. In the adjacent white face take a sufficiently thin collar of the closed E segment from just beyond q to just beyond r. Its interior misses every F trace: along the open q-to-r segment the only boundary-graph portion is λq, so there is no other F/W endpoint; finitely many compact common-face arcs stay a positive distance from a sufficiently thin closed subcollar. At q use the empty endpoint sector between ωq and this edge collar. A curve through that sector and collar, followed by the small lower-tube tail just chosen, gives an embedded white guide joining an interior point of ωq to an interior point of μr. Its interior misses F. Its common-face intersection is one interval. Its opposite-side projected boundary avoidance holds on the core automatically, and on the tail because its edge parameter lies beyond r, while the upper interval ended at r; take the tail sufficiently short to miss all other upper intervals and crossing windows.

Then ωq would be a whole proper F/W arc in the white core with both ends on one boundary edge interval of that disk. Only the immediate q-to-r UPPER interval is used as an ordinary edge here. The earlier path δ from p to q is allowed to traverse its full lower common-band chain, and is used solely to certify membership in Γ. Thus this proof does not make the invalid reduction of all of δ to one edge. The crossing-endpoint rows (U,B−) and (S,B−) remain separate and have not been inferred from this ordinary-edge row.


## Crossing-endpoint rows: use the underpass interval and nearest saddle

The later δ is still allowed to traverse common-band boundary chains. It is used only as a connected path on its lower circle; none of those later portions is silently identified with ∂F.

Use the actual crossing-ball quarter-disk normal charts. In each chosen side of the upper overpass, the upper boundary arcs of the finite family of saddle disks are disjoint, linearly ordered arcs between the black and white sides of that quarter disk. ## Rooted lower-disk normalization and the one-attachment property

The lower-disk choice in the prism construction can be made explicit. Make the permitted reference-book choice a height-graph choice first. Near a vertical reference axis, its two matched link-tube end disks are graphs z=z−(x,y) and z=z+(x,y), with z−<0<z+. Retain those actual graphs on an inner transverse disk. On an outer annulus interpolate them to the two profiles ±sqrt(ε²−x²−y²), with cutoff flat at both annulus boundaries and with the strict signs retained. The resulting upper and lower profiles are single-valued, have the common equatorial boundary z=0, and agree with the matched tube windows where required. Their region is a genuine small regular axis book. The common-band and saddle straightening is performed in a still thinner such book, so the previously supplied arc/page data persist. This explicitly arranges the projection property instead of assuming it for an arbitrary warped book.

Use the actual reference lower chamber as a height subgraph in the supplied projection collar: its boundary is the lower envelope of the reference hemispheres and link half-tubes, agreeing with the projection sphere off those windows. The monotone fiber maps and individual inward roundings established at the start provide the actual ball applications. On the reference cornered boundary, projection is a homeomorphism to the projection sphere. Finite one-sided smooth approximations of its explicit height profiles may be used for an individual disk application, retaining the chosen surface-circle incidence data. Thus projected lower boundary circles are a disjoint finite family of simple circles; no arbitrary TOP boundary parametrization is being assumed smooth.

For an arc δ of lower boundary in int(T−), its parallelism to the black-core boundary gives a literal empty ear Eδ in T− bounded by δ and an interval of the boundary of T−. The black part of this ear has no F trace because it lies in int(T+) after projection. Its remaining tube strip has no other link-boundary trace with the same longitudinal parameters, by the meridian-graph condition. At common-band windows use the matched positive band collar, which gives the same product strip and no additional trace. Thus the ear is empty of the whole lower boundary family, not merely empty of S0 traces. Distinct such ears have disjoint interiors: overlapping ears for disjoint proper arcs in a disk are nested, and the outer one would then contain the other arc, contrary to emptiness. Lower circles wholly contained in T− bound their ordinary projected disk sides inside T−; the outermost of these disks are disjoint from the ear interiors, since an empty ear contains no such circle and a disk compactly inside T− cannot contain an ear's boundary interval on ∂T−.

Choose a point t in the complementary central region, outside all ears and all these outermost inner-circle disks. Such a point exists near an open boundary interval between the finitely many ear endpoints: none of the compact inner-circle disks reaches that boundary interval, and the ear interiors are disjoint. For EVERY lower boundary circle Γ, choose the sphere disk ΔΓ with boundary π(Γ) which does not contain t. These disks form a nested/disjoint finite family, rooted at t. Their intersections with π(T−) consist solely of the empty ear disks just described, or of disks bounded by inner circles. In particular every connected component of ΔΓ∩π(T−) is a disk, and meets ∂π(T−) in at most one interval. It cannot contain the central region because it avoids t. This last choice is essential: an arbitrary choice of the other sphere side could contain the central region and would not give the needed one-attachment conclusion.

Realize this chosen family by actual lower disks. For one circle, the proper-disk comparison in the actual lower ball identifies its old disk, relative its boundary, with a disk close to the chosen boundary-side disk ΔΓ. For the finite family, process innermost disks in the rooted boundary-circle tree. A disk with an innermost boundary bounds a ball with its chosen boundary-side disk; every other disk has its boundary outside that side and hence lies on the other side, since the disks are disjoint and connected. Its collar-fixed disk sweep can consequently fix those other disks. Having placed it, use the same argument in the complementary cut ball and proceed. Nested target disks are placed at distinct small negative height levels, outer ones below inner ones, matching their fixed boundary collars. Finite height separation on each compact overlap keeps the actual disks disjoint. The original disk-window proof and isotopy extension supply an ambient isotopy relative all boundary circles, not just an abstract correspondence between disk families.

Each resulting lower disk projects injectively onto its selected ΔΓ. At the finite corner collars move the disk with the same actual rounding profile; the incidence topology is unchanged. It follows that each piece X of a lower disk inside the inner height-cylinder Y1 is a disk and that its boundary meets the outer wall ∂Y1 in at most one interval. Pieces lying wholly inside have no such interval. This is the actual one-attachment property needed to attach the lower cap strips without creating a handle. It is obtained from a rooted finite disk construction and the empty ears, rather than an unexplained assertion that all lower disks have a convenient projection.


## Actual meridian and crossing-cup parameters for the local strips

In the unit crossing-ball model take ℓo(a)=(sin a,0,cos a) for the overpass and ℓu(b)=(0,sin b,−cos b) for the underpass, with parameters between −π/2 and π/2 on the protected semicircles. Their unit tangents are To=(cos a,0,−sin a), Tu=(0,cos b,sin b). Positive normal frames are (ey,Ro=ℓo) and (ex,Ru=ℓu), because To×ey=Ro and Tu×ex=Ru. For a sufficiently small actual tube radius ε its boundary maps are

 Xo(a,θ)=(1+ε sinθ)ℓo(a)+ε cosθ ey,
 Xu(b,θ)=(1+ε sinθ)ℓu(b)+ε cosθ ex.

In both cases |X|²=1+2ε sinθ+ε². Consequently the inner crossing cup is EXACTLY sinθ≤−ε/2, with seams θ=π+arcsin(ε/2) and θ=2π−arcsin(ε/2). The complementary cup is outside the unit ball. Its overpass portion is on the upper side and its underpass portion on the lower side. The inner radial value θ=3π/2 at a=b=0 gives the two actual vertical-axis endpoints (0,0,±(1−ε)). Thus θ is a supplied genuine meridian coordinate, and a continuous lift of θ computes the winding of a peripheral boundary word; these are actual cup seams, rather than two informally marked peripheral points.

If the actual component orientation reverses the chosen longitudinal parameter, reverse the first normal vector to retain a positive meridian frame. ## Repeated upper arcs of one saddle: the homology and framing test

There is a useful direct exclusion for the potentially hidden repeated-crossing case: the TWO upper boundary arcs of one transverse saddle disk cannot both belong to the innermost circle γ0. Here the graph placement of the upper disk Fγ0 is used only as an allowed ambient normalization, retaining its boundary. This is a local exclusion in the continuing proof, not a declaration that every other case of Lemma4.1(A) has been checked.

For the residual case below, suppose these are the only two arcs of γ0 in this upper crossing disk, and that its overpass interval does not belong to γ0. In the upper crossing disk they cut off the two opposite corner caps; the middle region contains the top of the overpass. If the innermost side T+ contains that middle region, the upper overpass interval of ∂F must either lie in its interior or belong to γ0. The interior alternative contradicts innermostness. The boundary alternative is the separate overpass-plus-saddle guide configuration. Thus the case still requiring attention is precisely the one in which T+ contains the two corner caps and avoids the middle region. The two corner caps are joined through T+ outside this crossing disk. Choose a simple arc in that outside portion, extended through the two caps to interior points of the indicated saddle arcs. Its lift α+ in the graph disk Fγ0 has no intersection with W, since its interior lies in H+.

In the saddle choose an arc αs joining those same points, crossing its single W trace once and otherwise missing that trace. This is possible in the supplied four-page saddle chart: the W trace separates the two upper boundary arcs. The projection of αs can be chosen simple in the crossing disk, and the projection of α+ outside that disk, apart from the two short corner-cap approaches. Choose those approaches and the saddle arc together, so their projections form one simple closed curve. The smooth saddle graph chart is legitimate here: a transverse saddle, unlike a crossing band containing the vertical axis, is a graph over its disk shadow. For example a small chart z=κxy−εx²+εy² has the required alternating upper/lower sectors, lies below the upper x-pass and above the lower y-pass, and meets the vertical axis transversely. The normal-chart straightening transports this graph and its four protected boundary collars to the actual saddle. Across each chosen upper seam the lifted outside arc and inside saddle arc occupy opposite projected sides, so their annular surface neighbourhood also has injective projection near the seam; it is not a folded double covering of the shadow.

After rounding these two seams let τ=α+∪αs. A neighbourhood of τ in F has vertical transverse normal field, and its projection is an embedded annular neighbourhood of a simple plane curve. Its surface framing is therefore zero: the two normal lifts are homologous to constant small vertical translates, whose linking with τ is zero. Thus the Gordon–Litherland self-pairing of [τ] is zero. On the other hand τ has mod-two intersection ONE with the proper arc system F∩W: its upper-disk portion misses W and its saddle portion has the single transverse intersection just specified. A null-homologous closed curve in a surface has zero mod-two intersection with every relative one-cycle, so [τ] is nonzero in H1(F;Z/2), hence nonzero integrally as well (a compact surface with boundary has free integral H1 and mod-two reduction detects this class). This contradicts positive definiteness. Consequently this residual single-saddle repetition is excluded independently of the Figure26 guide. The reduction of a general repeated-crossing pattern to either a valid white guide or this residual pattern must still retain the actual quarter ordering and the nonincidence condition; it is not inferred just from the drawing.


## Crossing-disk regions reduce repetition to an empty guide or the residual saddle

The useful finite order check for (A) is on REGIONS, rather than on an arbitrary selected pair of γ0 arcs. In the simultaneous normal saddle chart, the upper hemisphere cut along the top-overpass rectangle has two disk quarters. In each quarter the upper saddle arcs are a linearly ordered parallel family. The ordering of the paired arcs in the other quarter is the same when both are read from the overpass side toward the outer corner. This pairing is part of the actual saddle chart, not an arbitrary permutation of endpoints: write the disjoint saddle graphs as z=f(x,y)+c_j with c_1<⋯<c_k, with the common collar adaptations. In either positive saddle sector the intersection with the upper hemisphere is the level f(x,y)+c_j=z_+(x,y); on an ordered transverse interval the same strict level order is read in the two sectors. Thus the outermost arc in both quarters belongs to the SAME extremal saddle. The finite relative chart straightening retains this order and the overpass collar.

Each complementary region of this upper crossing disk is therefore either a strip between successive saddle arcs in one quarter, an outer corner cap, or the central region adjoining the top overpass. The innermost side T+ has no F trace in its interior. Consequently a region which it contains has all its saddle-boundary intervals on γ0; one cannot cross a further saddle interval and continue through a region on the other side without putting an F trace inside T+.

If T+ contains a strip between successive upper saddle arcs, that strip is EMPTY of all upper F traces, not just γ0 traces. Its white-end collar gives a guide joining the incident white common-face traces on γ0. These are distinct: if a single white face trace joined these two saddle endpoints, it would be a return to the same crossing band in that white disk. The disk between it and that band's boundary interval has no other reference axis. Completing it across the two saddle sections in W gives a u/v return disk; an outermost such disk is one of the removed early bigons. Thus no such return is present. With distinct common-face endpoint intervals the guide's nonincidence condition is automatic. Its interior lies in the empty white corner strip, and it has no tube tail and hence no opposite-boundary projection obstruction. This is an actual internal upper white push, already excluded in the prime normalizer.

If T+ contains the central region, its upper ∂F overpass interval cannot lie in its interior. That interval therefore belongs to γ0. There is at most one such interval in this overpass, by the meridian-graph condition. A first saddle arc bounding the central region and that overpass interval bound an empty white corner strip. Its guide joins the saddle's white common-face interval to an interior point of the overpass tube interval. If those intervals were incident, their common corner would be one of the early white triangle regions. They are therefore nonincident. The guide's only tube portion is on the protected upper overpass; there is no lower ∂F trace in that crossing window, since any F boundary in a crossing ball is its single crossing-band boundary, and the presence of a transverse saddle excludes a crossing band. Thus the guide satisfies the projection-avoidance condition as well. It is an excluded one-tube-end white push.

We are left with T+ meeting only outer corner caps, with no central region and no intervening strips. A γ0 saddle arc must then be the outermost saddle arc of its quarter; there is at most one per quarter. If both quarters occur, their two arcs belong to the same extremal saddle by the paired level order above. The preceding zero-framing/nonzero-homology argument excludes exactly this residual case. Hence only one quarter can occur: γ0 has precisely its indicated saddle arc in C_t^+, and no overpass interval. This supplies (A) by finite region analysis. The key missing case in a mere 'draw a guide between two arcs' argument was the pair of outer caps, which need not have an empty strip between them; it has been handled separately by definiteness.


## The actual tube ends and neighbouring common band at a selected saddle

With the crossing-disk exclusion (A), the remaining (B)–(D) assertions can be traced without suppressing the face arcs. First consider a face arc η⊂γ0 with an endpoint on this saddle crossing and suppose that its other endpoint were on another crossing ball. Orient its face disk by the sphere orientation. At every port of one fixed colour, the direction along the face boundary toward the overpass is uniform; at the opposite colour it is reversed. This is the local alternating four-port rule, read with the fixed B-positive/W-negative convention. It uses face-boundary orientation, not an arbitrary orientation of a link component.

Orient η from its first port to its second. The direction along the incident face boundary at these two ends reverses relative to the normal side of η: at one end η enters the face and at the other it exits. Therefore the T+ side faces the overpass at one of these two ports. The whole overpass interval there is then on the T+ side unless another γ0 crossing arc separates it. The former puts an F trace inside the innermost disk; the latter contradicts (A). Thus η cannot have two crossing-ball ends. Applying this to the incident black and white arcs β and ω proves that their other ends lie on the actual link tube.
