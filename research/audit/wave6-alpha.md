## A3 Alpha concurrence

1. `lem-limsup-monotone-comparison` — **concur; `ai-altered`, `established-knowledge`.** Eventual pointwise order passes to every tail supremum and infimum, hence to extended `limsup` and `liminf`; this is the standard monotonicity result and needs no boundedness hypothesis.
2. `lem-nth-root-of-n-tends-to-one` — **concur; `ai-altered`, `established-knowledge`.** The limit `n^(1/n) -> 1` is classical, and the displayed `1 + 2/sqrt(n)` upper bound is an elementary quantitative weakening obtained from the standard binomial/AM-GM estimate.
3. `lem-power-over-geometric-null` — **concur; `ai-altered`, `established-knowledge`.** A fixed positive real power, including the stated positive-rational exponent case, is dominated by every geometric denominator with base greater than one.
4. `lem-factorial-beats-geometric` — **concur; `ai-altered`, `established-knowledge`.** For fixed real `x`, the absolute term ratio is eventually bounded by a constant below one, giving the classical limit `x^k/k! -> 0`.
5. `fs-limsup-additive` — **concur; `ai-altered`, `established-knowledge`.** `limsup` is subadditive rather than additive in general, and the standard anti-phase pair `(-1)^k` and `-(-1)^k` makes the sum identically zero while both individual `limsup`s equal one.
6. `ex-subsequential-limits-fill-unit-interval` — **concur; `ai-altered`, `established-knowledge`.** Successive meshes `{j/m : 1 <= j <= m}` are a standard dense-block construction: every term lies in `[0,1]`, and choosing a nearest mesh point in each growing block gives a subsequence to every point of the interval.
7. `ex-ratio-fails-root-succeeds` — **concur; `ai-altered`, `established-knowledge`.** For `a_k = 2^(-k+(-1)^k)`, exponent differences alternate between `-3` and `1`, so ratios are `1/8` and `2`, while division of the bounded exponent perturbation by the root index leaves the root limit `1/2`; this is the standard ratio-versus-root counterexample pattern.
8. `ex-closed-interval-is-perfect` — **concur; `ai-altered`, `established-knowledge`.** Every nondegenerate closed real interval is closed and each of its points, endpoints included, is approached by another point of the interval, so it is the canonical nonempty perfect-set example and is uncountable.
9. `cex-closed-bounded-in-q-not-compact` — **concur; `ai-altered`, `established-knowledge`.** The rational lower cut at the missing point `sqrt(2)` is bounded and relatively closed in `Q` but admits the standard increasing open cover with no finite subcover, canonically exhibiting the failure of Heine–Borel without completeness.
10. `cex-rationals-in-an-interval-are-disconnected` — **concur; `ai-altered`, `established-knowledge`.** Splitting `Q ∩ [0,2]` at the absent irrational `sqrt(2)` gives two nonempty separated pieces, the textbook demonstration that rational order-convexity does not imply connectedness in the real subspace topology.
11. `cex-unbounded-closed-set-not-compact` — **concur; `ai-altered`, `established-knowledge`.** `Z` is the standard closed unbounded noncompact subset of `R`, and `(0,1)` the standard bounded nonclosed noncompact subset, separately showing that neither Heine–Borel hypothesis may be dropped.
