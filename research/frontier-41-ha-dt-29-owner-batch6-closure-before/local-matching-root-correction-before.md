## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Consider a finite broken trajectory with autonomous Morse--Smale metric-gradient pieces and one regular continuation middle, or one augmented-regular compact parameter family of continuation middles. The ends of each continuation window are fixed independently of the parameter. At a configuration with $N$ autonomous breaks, the geometric compactification has a neighbourhood parametrized by
$$[0,\delta)^N\times B,$$
where $B$ is a sufficiently small chart of the broken stratum. The positive neck coordinates are $\rho_j=1/T_j$, where $T_j$ is the actual passage time between fixed local entry and exit sections at the $j$th intermediate critical point. Zero neck coordinates retain the corresponding break. The parametrization is a homeomorphism onto a neighbourhood, is smooth on each stratum, and its interior parametrizes precisely the unbroken solutions nearby. The continuation middle stays at its fixed time origin. Changes of the chosen critical charts and sections are smooth in these corner coordinates, with positive normal derivatives at the corresponding faces.

The same local matching construction applies to two regular continuation pieces joined through a Morse--Smale intermediate pair, when the two finite windows are placed on opposite sides of a variable autonomous plateau. The plateau length is the extra parameter; at infinite length its broken stratum is the product of the two regular continuation spaces. No global compactness statement is included.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite broken configuration and the stated ordinary or augmented transversality.

[F1] Actual metric stable/unstable disks are graphs tangent to the hyperbolic Hessian spectral subspaces; their flow transports are immersion charts. For smooth data the graph contraction bootstraps to smooth disks as explained in the finite-window description of a continuation datum ([[thm-local-stable-unstable-manifolds-for-hyperbolic-gradient-critical-points]], [[def-regular-continuation-datum-between-morse-smale-pairs]]).

[F2] In smooth coordinates with invariant stable and unstable axes, the mixed boundary problem has unique solutions in a fixed small ball, uniformly exponentially small endpoint maps and all their mixed derivatives. Those maps extend smoothly and flatly at $\rho=1/T=0$ ([[lem-mixed-boundary-hyperbolic-passage-has-uniform-endpoint-derivative-bounds]]).

[F3] A regular continuation space is a transverse finite-window fibre product. An augmented-regular family is the analogous transverse fibre product including its parameter, even when a vertical member is not regular. Autonomous Morse--Smale intersections are transverse ([[def-regular-continuation-datum-between-morse-smale-pairs]], [[def-two-parameter-continuation-homotopy]], [[def-morse-smale-pair]]).

[F4] Finite-time evolution and its inverses are smooth; a smooth equation with invertible derivative in its normal variables has a unique nearby solution depending smoothly on its remaining parameters ([[thm-time-dependent-vector-fields-have-local-smooth-evolution-operators]], [[thm-euclidean-implicit-function-theorem]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct, by exact finite-dimensional matching.

1.1 At every intermediate critical point, use [F1] to write the local stable disk as a graph over its stable spectral space and the unstable disk as a graph over its unstable spectral space. The map sending the two spectral coordinates to the sum of their two graph parametrizations has derivative the identity at the critical point, so its inverse gives smooth coordinates in which the disks are the coordinate axes. Invariance makes the vector field $s'=A_ss+R_s(s,u)$, $u'=A_uu+R_u(s,u)$ with $R_s(0,u)=R_u(s,0)=0$ and $DR(0)=0$. Shrink the ball so that $\langle s,s'\rangle<0$ and $\langle u,u'\rangle>0$ for nonzero components, by the spectral gaps and the small diagonal derivatives of $R$. Fix entry $|s|=r$ and exit $|u|=r$. The boundary data of [F2] permit these sections because its boundary-data domain has radius $2r$. Every trajectory entering and leaving the small box crosses each of these sections exactly once. [F1, F2, F4, given, construct]

2.1 Cut each broken piece at these sections, and fix one representative of each autonomous orbit by its section crossing. Its remaining exterior part has finite length in time, so [F4] gives a smooth exterior endpoint map. For a continuation piece its actual time origin is retained: transport its stable or unstable endpoint sheet across its fixed window and along the finite exterior segments. When a section is used instead of the fixed window endpoint, include the exterior phase as an extra variable. At a rigid negative-end connector, for example, the sheet $P=\Psi_{S,-S}^{-1}W^s_+(q)$ is transverse to $W^u_-(a)$; its autonomous phase saturation meets the exit section transversely to the unstable sphere. Its dimension is $\dim P+1-1=\dim P$, not $\dim P-1$. The phase is unique locally because the flow vector belongs to $TW^u_-(a)$ and does not belong to $TP$ in the rigid case. In positive-dimensional cases retain the intersection variables as coordinates on the stratum and choose a complement to the same transverse-normal map. This is exactly the section version of the fibre-product transversality in [F3]. [F3, F4, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Express the exterior matching equations in coordinates $(\xi,\eta)$: $\xi$ parametrizes the product of the broken piece spaces, including the common parameter for an augmented middle, and $\eta$ comprises their transverse normal variables and their section phase variables. At zero necks these are precisely the independent equations defining the broken piece spaces. Their derivatives are the normal quotient maps of [F3]; each is onto. Choose normal complements, on which these maps are invertible, and solve the section phase equation using its nonzero flow derivative. Gaussian elimination first removes these phase variables and then the chosen normal complements. The remaining diagonal blocks are the invertible transverse-normal blocks of the individual piece spaces; the free variables are $\xi$. Thus, after this coordinate choice, the zero-neck matching equations have the form $G(0,\xi,\eta)=\eta$, with $\eta=0$ parametrizing the given broken stratum. For an augmented family the complement is chosen for the full derivative including the parameter, not for a possibly singular vertical derivative. For two continuation pieces the zero-neck equations are the two independent regular fibre products, so their normal blocks form a direct sum. [F3, F4, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 For positive necks, replace each pair of critical-axis endpoints in these equations by the exact passage of [F2]: an entry point is $(a,\alpha_{T}(a,b))$ and its exit is $(\zeta_{T}(a,b),b)$, with $a$ and $b$ constrained to their section spheres. The exterior equations are unchanged. Consequently $G(\rho,\xi,\eta)-G(0,\xi,\eta)$ is a sum of smooth substitutions of endpoint maps that are flat at the relevant zero necks. It is smooth jointly in all neck and stratum coordinates, also when only some necks vanish. Extend each flat endpoint map by zero to negative $\rho$ for the local calculation. By [F4], the invertible $\eta$ derivative of step 3.1 gives a unique smooth $\eta=h(\rho,\xi)$ for small neck coordinates and $\xi$ in a smaller chart. At positive necks every segment is an exact solution and the exterior equations match it exactly, so this constructs actual trajectories without an approximate-zero or infinite-dimensional inverse argument. At zero necks the construction is the corresponding broken product. [F2, F4, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 The component estimates of [F2] imply that on a passage of length $T$, $|s(t)|\le C e^{-\beta t}$ and $|u(t)|\le C e^{-\beta(T-t)}$. Thus on fixed intervals after entry or before exit the passage converges to its stable or unstable axis orbit by finite-time uniqueness and continuous dependence; in the region far from both ends both components are small. The exterior matching variables converge to the broken variables of step 3.1. This proves geometric convergence as any neck tends to zero, with the continuation window unshifted. Conversely, a trajectory geometrically close to the chosen broken configuration meets the chosen entry and exit sections on the chosen immersion branches. Step 1.1 gives unique crossing times and hence unique $T_j$. If a breaking sequence had bounded $T_j$, finite-time continuous dependence would connect a nonconstant stable entry to an unstable exit through the critical point in finite time, contradicting uniqueness. Hence $T_j$ tends to infinity at exactly the breaks. These crossings and the exterior representatives recover $\rho$ and $\xi$; uniqueness in step 4.1 recovers the same trajectory. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, step 4.1]

6.1 Shrink the geometric transversal neighbourhood so that all recovered exterior variables lie in the charts of step 3.1 and all passage times are above the threshold of step 4.1. Steps 4.1–5.1 show that this neighbourhood is exactly the image of the matching chart. The inverse crossing coordinates are continuous, including at a zero neck by the preceding divergence argument; the direct map is continuous by step 5.1. Thus the chart is a homeomorphism onto a neighbourhood and has the claimed stratumwise smoothness and inverse coverage. Partial broken configurations are included by setting the corresponding necks to zero; uniqueness of the same equations makes these face parametrizations compatible. [F2, F4, step 4.1, step 5.1]

7.1 Changing a section or a critical coordinate chart changes a passage time by the two finite exterior crossing times. These are smooth functions of the section endpoints near the fixed stable and unstable crossings, since the crossings are transverse. Their limiting sum is a smooth function $c(\xi)$ of the broken variables; their difference from that limit is flat in the affected neck variable by [F2]. Therefore $T'=T+c(\xi)+e(\rho,\xi)$ with $e$ smooth and flat at $\rho=0$, and $\rho'=\rho/(1+c(\xi)\rho+e(\rho,\xi)\rho)$. This is smooth with normal derivative $1$ at the face. Exterior stratum coordinates change smoothly by [F4] and the unique matching equations. Apply this argument separately to the finitely many necks; it proves compatibility at higher corners as well as at one break. The same exterior equation argument for the two-window plateau proves the final assertion of the statement. [F2, F4, step 4.1, step 6.1, algebra] ∎
