## Mathematical synthesis and source-specific proof recipes

### Knot groups, meridians, and peripheral structure

No selected complete source gives a general diagram-to-Wirtinger-presentation proof together with the peripheral longitude. Conway’s section 2(a) (pp. 3–4) gives the presentation of a closed-braid complement as generators for puncture meridians with relations x_i = x_i^β; Morton (pp. 4–6) gives a related presentation for the closed braid together with its axis. These are braid-complement presentations, not a Wirtinger theorem for every regular link diagram, and the braid automorphism conventions differ across the sources.

For a proposed local route, define the compact exterior E_L = S^3 \\ int ν(L) for a tame smooth oriented link and a peripheral system consisting of the conjugacy classes of the boundary-torus subgroups together with positive meridians and zero-linking longitudes. For a knot, H_1(E_K) ≅ Z is generated by the meridian, and the preferred longitude is the primitive boundary slope mapping to zero in H_1(E_K). Build the Wirtinger presentation from a regular projection using one generator per diagram arc and one crossing relation; prove correctness by cutting the complement into crossing balls and applying van Kampen, then prove invariance under diagram moves by explicit Tietze transformations or by a separate Reidemeister-to-exterior argument. State basepoint changes as conjugation. The algorithm terminates because the diagram has finitely many arcs/crossings; the proof of correctness is a separate result. No local item found supplies this knot-specific package. The existing punctured-disk Artin theorem is useful but does not supply the knot-complement presentation or longitude.

The peripheral classification boundary must be explicit. The full group with peripheral data determines the complement by a geometric theorem; the statement that the group alone classifies knots is false in general. A path from an isomorphism of peripheral systems to an ambient equivalence uses 3-manifold topology beyond the local Artin automorphism theorem. All current link definitions are smooth links in S^3; they do not cover wild embeddings, arbitrary 3-manifolds, or locally flat topological knots.

### Alexander modules, Fox calculus, and normalization

The current local convention is important:

- def-one-variable-alexander-module-of-an-oriented-link defines A_L = H_1(\\widetilde X_L; Z) for the infinite cyclic cover classified by the total-linking map π_1(X_L) → H_1(X_L) ≅ Z^r → Z, with every positive meridian sent to 1. The deck generator acts as t, so the coefficient ring is Λ = Z[t^{±1}].
- def-alexander-polynomial-from-the-first-elementary-ideal takes the gcd of E_0(A_L) and hence records Δ_L up to the unit ±t^k; it names D_L = Δ_L for r>1 and D_L = Δ_L/(1-t) for a knot. It also records Δ_K(1)=±1 and square Seifert-matrix presentations as literature inputs, not local proofs.
- For a multicomponent link, the full abelian cover uses H_1(X_L) ≅ Z^r and Z[t_1^{±1},…,t_r^{±1}]. This is different from the one-variable total-linking cover. Morton’s and Conway’s D/torsion conventions are multivariable invariants; their equal-variable specialization must not be identified with the local one-variable absolute-homology Δ_L without the explicit factor and convention.

Morton, section 2 (printed pp. 2–6), is the strongest complete short source for the multivariable braid computation. It labels strands by their meridian variables, attaches to each crossing the variable of the undercrossing strand, and multiplies the corresponding colored Burau blocks. Its Theorem 1 proves that the Alexander polynomial of the closed braid together with its axis is det(I − x B_β) after identifying variables on strands joined by the braid permutation. Its Remark (2), pp. 3–4, suppresses the axis by the Torres–Fox formula and gives D^{mv}_{\\hat β} ≍ det(I−B_β)/(1−∏ t_i); for equal labels it yields the classical one-variable formula Δ_{\\hat β}(t) ≍ (1−t) det(I−B_β(t))/(1−t^n). The proof on pp. 4–6 uses a free-group presentation for the punctured disk, Fox derivatives of the braid action, the Fox chain rule, and a deleted-column determinant. The standard Fox quotient and Torres–Fox identity are stated as known inputs rather than proved from first principles.

Conway, sections 2(b)–2(d) and 3(c), supplies the most useful twisted recipe. For a finite CW complex X, a representation ρ: π_1(X)→GL_k(R) over an integral domain and an epimorphism ψ: π_1(X)→H to a finite-rank free abelian group, form C_*^{ρ⊗ψ}(X;Q(H)^k). The torsion is zero if this complex is not acyclic; if it is acyclic, it is defined from based chain bases and is ambiguous by ± det(ρ(π_1(X))) h, h∈H. Conway’s Lemma 2.6 proves acyclicity when the twisted Alexander module in degree one is torsion, using the 2-complex homotopy type of a link exterior, vanishing degree 3, and Euler characteristic. The Fox derivative rules give the evaluated boundary matrix. For a deficiency-one presentation, deleting a generator column with nonzero det((ρ⊗ψ)(x_j−1)) gives Wada’s quotient. The paper reports Wada’s quotient equals twisted Reidemeister torsion by citing Wada/Kitano; it does not prove invariance of Wada’s quotient under all Tietze moves. Theorem 3.15, pp. 16–17, proves the twisted reduced-Burau determinant identity by adding the braid axis, applying van Kampen and a short-exact-sequence torsion product, and then using Fox calculus. It requires a representation of the free group that extends over the closed-braid complement; the proof separates the torsion-module case from the non-torsion case. Conway explicitly notes that for a one-component link (t−1)τ = Δ(t), while for at least two components the full-abelianization torsion is the multivariable Alexander polynomial.

The local Fox lemma is published but proof_strategy is literature-input: it quotes Morton’s rule and does not derive the chain-level Fox matrix or the denominator. A full local proof should establish the Fox differential identities, show that the evaluated Fox Jacobian is the boundary map in the abelian/twisted cover chain complex, identify d_1 with the row of φ(g_j)-1, prove the deleted-minor quotient equals the relevant order/torsion, and check invariance under presentation changes. For genuinely noncommutative coefficient rings, ordinary determinants are unavailable; one must specify an Ore localization/Dieudonné determinant or a Whitehead-torsion target and prove the hypotheses. Neither the Fox item nor Conway’s commutative R[H] construction establishes a noncommutative order theorem.

### Seifert forms, signatures, and branched covers

Livingston’s complete 25-page survey is explicitly about classical knot concordance (not link concordance) and focuses on smooth concordance while separating locally flat topological results. Section 2, pp. 4–5, defines a Seifert pairing from positive push-off linking and states that V−V^T is the unimodular intersection form. Theorem 2.6 gives the slice-implies-metabolic argument through a 3-manifold bounded by the Seifert surface and slice disk, but leaves the construction/duality step compressed. Section 3.1, p. 7, reviews Levine–Tristram signatures; §3.3, p. 7, states the determinant polynomial and its ±t^k ambiguity, and that a metabolic form yields a norm factorization. These are survey-level accounts, not complete proofs of S-equivalence of all Seifert matrices, signature invariance at singular unit roots, or the algebraic concordance classification.

The source’s signature formula has a notational defect: the extracted text displays the same (1−ω) coefficient on V and V^T, which is not Hermitian for a general unit ω; the standard Hermitian convention uses the conjugate coefficient on the transpose term. Przytycki–Yasuhara’s p. 2 Hermitian-matrix display also appears typographically inconsistent with its immediately following factorization. A new local statement must choose and prove one convention consistently; neither printed expression should be copied without repair and an exact sign check.

Livingston’s six-page “Seifert Forms and Concordance” gives two useful complete short results. Theorem 3.1, pp. 4–5, gives the order formula for H_1 of a finite cyclic branched cover in terms of Alexander-polynomial evaluations at roots of unity; Corollary 3.2 proves that prime-power cyclic branched covers are rational homology spheres using Δ_K(1)=±1. Theorem 1.2, pp. 2 and 5–6, characterizes when all prime-power covers are homology spheres in terms of cyclotomic factors. The product/order formula should be stated with absolute value when interpreting it as a finite group order. The article’s Casson–Gordon construction is not a locally complete proof; it imports Litherland/Casson–Gordon theory and supplies a sketch of the signature estimates.

Przytycki–Yasuhara, full 24 pages, provides the strongest local proof route for branched-cover linking computations. Their Goeritz form for nonorientable spanning surfaces and Seifert matrix for orientable surfaces feed rational linking formulas. Proposition 2.1 proves basis and S*-move invariance of V G^{-1} V^T; Theorem 2.3 derives double-branched-cover linking of preimage components from a surgery-link matrix. For an oriented surface disjoint from the auxiliary link, Proposition 3.1 states invariance under Seifert S-equivalence. The p-fold cover is presented by the block matrix M_{p,α} built from M_α and M_α^T (pp. 2–3); Theorem 3.2 obtains preimage-linking numbers from M_{p,α}^{-1}. Section 4 defines the infinite-cyclic-cover linking pairing and proves Lemma 4.6 / Theorem 4.1: with G_α(t)=tM_α−M_α^T, the pairing is computed by inverse G_α(t), and \\widetilde{lk}_{X_∞}(K_1^k,K_2^k)−lk(K_1,K_2)=(1−t)λ_F(K_1,K_2)(t) under the stated zero-linking and disjoint-Seifert-surface hypotheses. Its surgery-linking matrix lemma is a reusable proof module; the existence of the branched-cover surgery presentation is attributed to Akbulut–Kirby and is not reproved in this article.

### Reidemeister torsion and satellite behavior

Ranicki’s 10-page notes (pp. 3–10) define torsion for finite based acyclic free chain complexes over a commutative ring, display its basis-unit ambiguity, and explain the quotient by units induced from Z[π_1]. Theorem 15 expresses torsion through an internal basis. Example 17, pp. 9–10, identifies the knot-exterior torsion over F(t) with (1−t)/p(t), where p is the characteristic polynomial of the deck action on first homology, so it recovers the Alexander polynomial up to the standard units. The note emphasizes that ordinary R-torsion is not a homotopy invariant before the relevant unit quotient; the noncommutative extension is Whitehead torsion. It is an introductory 2001 note, not a proof of twisted knot torsion or Wada’s theorem.

Livingston §5.1 and Theorem 5.1 (2004, printed pp. 11–13) describe a Casson–Gordon signature formula for satellite infection: when the infection axis has linking number zero, the new invariant is the old one plus a sum of signatures of the companion knot at characters on lifts. The proof is explicitly a sketch referencing Litherland and Gilmer. Conway’s introduction and bibliography identify twisted torsion and its satellite/concordance applications, but do not supply a general satellite formula proof. None of the selected sources gives a full local proof of the ordinary satellite formula Δ_{P(K)}(t) ≍ Δ_P(t)Δ_K(t^w) from the satellite exterior decomposition. This is a separate source/proof obligation if that result is in the frontier.

## Explicit proof modules still needed for this lane

1. **Wirtinger and peripheral data.** Define the regular link diagram input, arc and crossing conventions, a finite Wirtinger-presentation algorithm, and prove its group output by a precise van Kampen cover. Give an independent Tietze/Reidemeister invariance proof. Define each boundary torus, meridian, preferred longitude, and peripheral system; prove the abelianized longitude is zero-linking. If a category claims knot recovery from the peripheral group, isolate the 3-manifold rigidity/Gordon–Luecke input and its exact category hypotheses. Punctured-disk Artin suppliers do not discharge this.

2. **Fox/Alexander matrix.** Keep the current module construction and isotopy invariance. Add the missing Fox chain rule and cellular boundary proof, then prove the deleted-minor quotient with the denominator and normalization from the relevant d_1 map. State exactly which presentations and homomorphisms are admissible, how generator and relation changes affect the determinant, and which ideal/order is meant. Either prove the general free-abelian specialization rule locally or narrow its contract to the full abelianization and clearly specified specializations.

3. **Seifert-form bridge.** Define a Seifert surface and signed push-off form; prove V−V^T is the intersection form; prove existence from diagrams; define basis changes and S-equivalence; show det(V−tV^T) agrees with the local Alexander module polynomial under the chosen transpose/unit convention. Prove S-equivalence for isotopies, handle stabilizations, and basis changes rather than recording it solely as literature. Define the Levine–Tristram Hermitian matrix and prove signature invariance, including the averaged value at singular unit roots.

4. **Cyclic branched covers.** Define the connected p-fold cyclic branched cover of a tame knot in S^3, its deck action and category; prove the Seifert block presentation for H_1, the root-of-unity order formula, the rational-homology-sphere criterion, and inverse-matrix linking forms with all nonsingularity hypotheses. For concordance, use the existing smooth slice-disk double-cover lemma, and state separately what changes for locally flat topological disks.

5. **Multivariable and satellite invariants.** Define the full abelianization and multivariable order/torsion normalization separately from the one-variable total-linking module. Prove or import with an explicit exact supplier the Torres deletion formula, the satellite/winding-number formula, connected-sum formula, and orientation/mirror transformations. A colored braid determinant is an efficient computational route for braid closures, not a substitute for the general definition.

6. **Twisted/reidemeister torsion.** Reuse Conway’s chain-level setup, then add a local proof of the claimed Wada/Kitano quotient-to-torsion equality and invariance. For noncommutative coefficients, state the Ore and determinant framework explicitly or keep the theorem out of scope; ordinary determinants over a noncommutative group ring are not defined. Do not inflate Conway’s complete colored-braid theorem into a general knot classification or a general noncommutative theorem.

No date-sensitive claim about the 2026 frontier is certified by this source set. In particular, the open-problem lists in the 2004/2002/2001 texts are historical statements and must be rechecked against current sources before any op- design or status claim.
