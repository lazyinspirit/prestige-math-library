# Reader 5: concordance, 4-manifolds, and satellite operations

This report covers classical oriented knots in the fixed standard (S^3): smooth and locally flat topological concordance, slice genus, Seifert/Witt algebraic concordance, signatures and Arf/Casson–Gordon obstructions, satellite operations, and the relevant smooth/topological 4-manifold inputs. It does not treat knots in arbitrary 3-manifolds as part of the same category. “Topologically slice” below always means bounded by a **locally flat** proper topological disk; wild disks are outside the concordance definitions used by the sources.

2. **Danica Kosanović, Patrick Orson, Mark Powell, Arunima Ray, and Benjamin Matthias Ruppik, “Topological 4-manifolds: The disc embedding theorem and beyond,” Tech Topology Summer School notes, dated 27 August 2021** ([author-hosted PDF](https://aru-ray.github.io/documents/ttss-lectures.pdf)). They explicitly label the disk-embedding proof an outline: the Casson-handle/skyscraper construction and Freedman’s theorem that skyscrapers are standard are decisive cited inputs, not proved here. The Alexander-polynomial-one knot application on PDF p. 37 is only a brief sketch.

3. **Lisa Piccirillo, “The Conway knot is not slice,” arXiv:1808.02923v1, submitted 8 August 2018** ([versioned PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.02923v1)). I read PDF pages 1–9, §§1–4 and references; I also inspected the mathematical figures on pp. 3–4 and 8–9, including the full handle-calculus sequence. The paper defines the smooth 0-trace (X(K)), proves that smooth sliceness is equivalent to a smooth embedding of (X(K)) in (S^4), constructs (K') with (X(K')cong X(C)) for the Conway knot (C), and obtains (s(K')=2) from the Lee spectral sequence and a computed Khovanov table. Thus (C) is not smoothly slice although it is topologically slice by Freedman’s theorem for Alexander-polynomial-one knots. The local proof route still needs the dualizable-pattern handle calculus and the Khovanov/Lee/(s)-invariant foundations; reader 6 owns the latter material. The table is reported from a computer routine and is not accompanied by a standalone hand calculation.

4. **Tim D. Cochran, Christopher W. Davis, and Arunima Ray, “Injectivity of satellite operators in knot concordance,” arXiv:1205.5058v3, revised 16 July 2013; journal version, *Journal of Topology* 7 (2014), 948–964** ([versioned PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1205.5058v3)). I read PDF pages 1–16, §§1–7, all theorem proofs and references. The paper defines ordinary winding number and strong winding number, distinguishes smooth (C), topological (C^{top}), exotic-smooth (C^{ex}), and (mathbb Z[1/n])-homology concordance, then proves injectivity results by comparing zero-surgery manifolds through homology cobordisms. The exact theorem distinctions are recorded below. Its topological argument uses Freedman’s topological Poincaré theorem; smooth injectivity on ordinary (C) is conditional on the smooth 4-dimensional Poincaré conjecture. The problem list is from 2013 and is not treated as a current status report.

5. **Jae Choon Cha and Taehee Kim, “Iterated satellite operators on the knot concordance group,” arXiv:2402.04629v2, revised 4 March 2025; final journal version, *Advances in Mathematics* (2025), DOI 10.1016/j.aim.2025.110203** ([versioned PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.04629v2)). I read PDF pages 1–34, §§1–6, Appendix A, all references, and the author page. The paper proves infinite-rank results for winding-zero satellite filtrations under a nonzero Blanchfield self-pairing condition, proves the relevant iterates are not homomorphisms, and separates operators under coprime Alexander-polynomial hypotheses. The proofs use higher-order (L^2)-signatures, integral solvability, amenable-signature vanishing, and Blanchfield bordism. These foundational inputs are stated and used but not proved from first principles in the paper. Remark 1.1 asks whether the smooth graded quotients have infinite rank for every winding-zero operator whose image consists of topologically slice knots. That question also appears in the 2025 final publication. A targeted search on 18 October 2026 found no later resolution; this is a scoped status check, not an exhaustive literature review.

6. **Simon K. Donaldson, “An application of gauge theory to four-dimensional topology,” *Journal of Differential Geometry* 18 (1983), no. 2, 279–315** ([author-hosted reprint](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/donaldson.pdf)). ## Mathematical findings and exact scope

For oriented smooth knots (K_0,K_1subset S^3), smooth concordance is an oriented smooth annulus properly embedded in (S^3	imes[0,1]), with boundary (-K_0) and (K_1) in the end slices. A smooth slice knot bounds a smooth proper disk in (B^4). The topological definitions replace “smooth” with “topological” and require the annulus/disk to be locally flat; the ambient product/ball is in the topological category. The CDR paper also studies concordance in a smooth manifold merely homeomorphic to (S^3	imes I) and smooth (mathbb Z[1/n])-homology concordance. These equivalence relations are distinct. A citation to an abstract smooth cobordism definition is not a definition of an embedded annulus in a fixed product.

If (Fsubset S^3) is an oriented Seifert surface for a classical knot and (i_+) is the positive normal push-off, its Seifert pairing is (V(x,y)=operatorname{lk}(x,i_+y)) on (H_1(F;mathbb Z)). The skew part (V-V^T) is the unimodular intersection pairing on the surface. Livingston’s slice-disk argument shows that a slice knot gives a metabolic Seifert form, and concordant knots have algebraically concordant forms; this yields Levine’s surjective homomorphism from geometric concordance to algebraic concordance. It is not a classification of geometric concordance. The source treats signatures, Arf and Alexander-polynomial conditions as obstructions. At a unit complex number (omega) away from the Alexander roots, the Hermitian form built from (V) and (omega) gives the Levine–Tristram signature; at singular values use the two-sided limiting average. The Arf invariant is the (mathbb Z_2)-valued quadratic-form invariant of (V) modulo 2. For slice knots the relevant signatures and Arf invariant vanish, and the normalized Alexander polynomial has Fox–Milnor factorization. These invariants are not sufficient for smooth or topological sliceness.

The library’s current Alexander-polynomial definition is based on the zeroth elementary ideal of the absolute one-variable Alexander module and separately defines (D_L=Delta_L/(1-t)) for knots. Livingston’s (det(V-tV^T)) uses a standard Seifert-matrix convention with a different normalization. A new proof must establish the determinant/module comparison and align unit and ((1-t)) conventions before stating that an Alexander polynomial is (1). Do not identify similarly named polynomial symbols without this bridge.

For a prime-power branched cover (M_q) of a slice knot, the Casson–Gordon theorem supplies a metabolizer (L) for the torsion linking form on (H_1(M_q)) so that the associated Casson–Gordon Witt classes (	au(M_q,chi_x)) vanish for the permitted prime-power characters. The survey explains how satellite companionship converts these invariants into signature constraints on knots tied into bands. It gives definitions and theorem statements, but not a complete derivation of the full Casson–Gordon apparatus. Its COT section is an overview, and explicitly omits the (n+	frac12) definitions and the higher-order obstruction proofs.

Freedman’s theorem for classical knots is that the standard knot Alexander polynomial (det(V-tV^T)), considered up to (pm t^k), being the unit (1) implies topological sliceness by a locally flat disk. It makes no smooth-sliceness claim. The repository names its Alexander module polynomial and knot-normalized (D_L) separately, so a local item must prove the conversion instead of identifying symbols. The topological 4-manifold notes explain the proof architecture: produce framed immersed Whitney disks, construct Casson handles/skyscrapers, then use the disk-embedding/detection theorem that each skyscraper is topologically standard. The local-flat disk inherits a product neighborhood. The knot application still needs the detailed surgery/disk-embedding argument and the exact 4-dimensional Poincaré/cobordism inputs. A smooth slice disk is also locally flat, but a locally flat disk need not be smoothable relative to its boundary; the Conway knot is the explicit separation in Piccirillo’s theorem.

The exact Donaldson primary theorem read here is for a **closed, smooth, simply connected, oriented** 4-manifold with positive-definite integral intersection form: the form is equivalent over (mathbb Z) to the standard diagonal form. Livingston’s survey states a broader-looking (H_1(X;mathbb Z_2)=0) hypothesis in its compressed summary; the primary theorem used here has the simply-connected hypothesis. Keep the stronger exact contract unless a precise broader theorem is sourced and checked. A knot nonsliceness application must first prove that sliceness would produce a closed simply connected smooth definite 4-manifold with a forbidden lattice; Donaldson’s theorem alone does not construct that manifold.

For a pattern (Psubset S^1	imes D^2), (P(K)) is formed by inserting the pattern into the zero-framed tubular neighborhood of (K), and the winding number is algebraic intersection with a meridional disk. CDR’s **strong winding number (pm1)** means that the solid-torus meridian normally generates (pi_1(S^3setminus P(U))); ordinary winding number (pm1) is weaker unless (P(U)) is the unknot. Their theorem is:

- for nonzero winding number (n), (P) is injective on the paper’s (mathbb Z[1/n])-homology concordance classes;
- for strong winding number (pm1), (P) is injective on exotic-smooth and locally flat topological concordance classes;
- on the ordinary smooth concordance group, the same conclusion is conditional on the smooth 4-dimensional Poincaré conjecture.

The proof reduces injectivity to weak injectivity using (0)-surgery and an auxiliary satellite pattern, with the normal-generation hypothesis carried through the cobordisms. This is category-specific and cannot be restated as unconditional smooth injectivity.

Cha–Kim’s 2025 theorem uses the (P)-filtration (langle P^n(mathcal C)angle) of the concordance group. If (P) has winding number zero and its axis (eta) has nonzero self-pairing (Bell(eta,eta)) under the Blanchfield form of (P(U)), then every graded quotient (langle P^n(mathcal C)angle/langle P^{n+1}(mathcal C)angle) has infinite rank, in the topological category and hence in the smooth category. Under the same condition (P^n) is not a homomorphism for (nge1); their Theorem C also separates certain winding-zero operators with relatively prime Alexander polynomials. This does not settle the general smooth question for operators whose entire image is topologically slice. The proof’s local dependencies include integral solvability, higher-order Blanchfield forms/bordisms, Strebel group classes, Cheeger–Gromov bounds, Novikov additivity, and the amenable (L^2)-signature theorem.
