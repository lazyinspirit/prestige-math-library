## Exact theorem contracts and proof ingredients

### Exteriors, peripheral slopes, and decompositions

Use a compact exterior E_K = S^3 minus the interior of a closed tubular neighborhood N(K), with its boundary torus and a chosen oriented meridian–longitude basis (μ, λ). A slope is a primitive class pμ+qλ up to sign, conventionally p/q with meridional coefficient p; the longitude is 0. This convention must be stated in every surgery and boundary-slope item. It is distinct from the open complement S^3\K.

Hatcher, §1.2, Theorem 1.9 (statement PDF p. 15, printed p. 14; uniqueness proof PDF pp. 22–24, printed pp. 21–23) states: for a compact, irreducible, orientable 3-manifold M, there is a collection of disjoint incompressible tori such that cutting along them gives atoroidal or Seifert-fibered pieces, and a minimal such collection is unique up to isotopy. Hatcher’s atoroidal condition is that every incompressible torus is boundary-parallel. The proof gives a finite bound on essential tori by normalizing intersections with a triangulation and using mod-2 homology, then proves uniqueness by minimal-intersection cut-and-paste and Seifert-piece analysis. It does not show that every atoroidal piece is hyperbolic; it is not geometrization. Scope is compact orientable irreducible manifolds, and nonorientable cases require allowing Klein-bottle splittings.

Hatcher §1.1 proves prime decomposition for compact orientable 3-manifolds and records irreducible/prime distinctions. These theorems can support a later knot-exterior module only after the local definitions and proof links are supplied. Do not infer that the knot exterior is irreducible or boundary-incompressible without proving the knot-specific hypotheses.

### Hyperbolic structures, Dehn filling, and volume

Thurston Ch. 2 gives the standard models and hyperbolic trigonometry. Ch. 3 defines geometric structures through developing maps and holonomy and gives triangulated examples, including the figure-eight complement. Ch. 4 §§4.1–4.4 gives ideal-tetrahedron shape and edge-gluing equations: for each edge the product of complex shape parameters is 1 in the lifted logarithmic coordinate, with dihedral-angle sum 2π; the cusp link must have a compatible similarity structure. This is a construction for specified ideal triangulations, not a recognition theorem that an arbitrary knot exterior is hyperbolic.

Ch. 4 §4.5 defines generalized filling parameters by peripheral holonomy logarithms, with equation α_i H~(a_i)+β_i H~(b_i)=±2πi. Integer primitive slopes correspond to smooth filled manifolds; general complex parameters may produce cone/orbifold structures. Ch. 4 §4.6 treats the figure-eight fillings in detail; Theorem 4.7 is a knot-specific calculation, not the general hyperbolic Dehn filling theorem.

Thurston Ch. 5, Theorem 5.8.2, gives the local filling theorem: if M is a complete finite-volume cusped hyperbolic 3-manifold with k torus ends, a neighborhood of (∞,…,∞) in the product of k surgery-parameter spheres consists of fillings admitting hyperbolic structures. In particular, all but finitely many primitive integral filling slopes at each cusp give smooth hyperbolic manifolds. The presented argument truncates cusps, invokes a k-complex-dimensional deformation space and Mostow uniqueness, and uses holonomy trace coordinates to show the surgery parameters vary over a neighborhood. The proof relies on earlier deformation theory (Theorem 5.6), Mostow rigidity and a local openness result cited incompletely in the notes. The notes are valuable proof scaffolding but are not a modern self-contained proof module. No inference from “atoroidal” to “hyperbolic” is licensed here.

Ch. 5 §§5.11–5.12 covers the Margulis thin part and Jørgensen compactness for finite-volume hyperbolic manifolds. Ch. 6 §6.2 gives simplicial volume and the hyperbolic volume equality for closed oriented hyperbolic manifolds; §6.5.4 discusses the torus-boundary relative version and §6.5.6 strict volume loss under hyperbolic Dehn filling. These later arguments are sketches and depend on straightening, smearing and cusp truncation details. Ch. 7 §7.2 gives the ideal-tetrahedron volume formula Λ(α)+Λ(β)+Λ(γ), with angle sum π, and the maximal volume at the regular ideal tetrahedron (about 1.01494); it supplies useful computations, not general knot-volume classification. Ch. 5’s Mostow discussion is incomplete as a proof; it should not be cited as a local complete rigidity derivation.

Thurston Ch. 8 addresses Kleinian groups, convex cores, geometric finiteness, pleated surfaces, bending and cusps. Ch. 9 is historically important for algebraic/geometric convergence and ending laminations, but the source omits §§9.2 and 9.8; its tame-end and strong-convergence results are conditional, and it does not prove the later general tameness theorem. Ch. 11 §11.1 is a partial extension-of-vector-fields note. Avoid importing these notes as proofs of modern tameness/ending-lamination results.

### Normal-surface unknot decision (for cross-lane reuse)

Hass–Lagarias–Pippenger, §4 Lemmas 4.1–4.2 (PDF pp. 9–10) connect an essential disk in the compact knot exterior to unknotting. Their §5 Theorem 5.4 (p. 13) cites Haken’s fundamental-normal-surface theorem; Theorem 5.6 (p. 14) cites Jaco–Tollefson’s vertex-surface theorem. Theorem 8.1 (p. 22) states the exact decision bounds for an n-crossing diagram on a Turing machine: Haken search time O(exp(c n²)), space O(n² log n); Jaco–Tollefson vertex search time O(exp(c′ n)), same space. The good exterior triangulation has t=O(n) tetrahedra (the paper gives a concrete linear bound); search enumerates a finite coordinate box or extremal rays and tests normality, connectivity/disk topology, and nontrivial mod-2 boundary class. Theorem 1.1 says unknot recognition is in NP using a polynomial certificate.

This is an authoritative complete reading of the HLP paper, not a complete proof of the algorithm’s deepest ingredients. The paper’s proof of Theorem 5.4 is literally a citation to Haken/Schubert/Jaco–Rubinstein/Jaco–Oertel/Jaco–Tollefson, and its proof of Theorem 5.6 is one sentence referring to a Jaco–Tollefson corollary. Lemma 4.1’s “essential disk gives ambient isotopy to the unknot” is also only a short assertion. A rigorous local module plan must separately prove: (i) the PL good-triangulation construction and peripheral marking; (ii) normal coordinates, matching equations and quadrilateral admissibility; (iii) a minimal essential disk can be normalized without losing essentiality; (iv) fundamental/vertex existence and explicit finite coordinate bounds; (v) finite enumeration and polynomial-coordinate verification; (vi) essential-disk iff unknot by using the longitude kernel in H1 of the exterior, capping with the annulus in N(K), and an explicit PL disk/Schoenflies argument. Keep HLP’s bounds as theorem contracts until these proofs or a fully read source are supplied. Reidemeister enumeration is only a semidecision for “isotopic to the unknot”; it is not this finite decision algorithm.

### Character varieties, A-polynomial, and slopes

Baldwin–Sivek, §2.3 and §4, use diagonal restrictions of SL(2,C) representations to the marked peripheral group to define the A-polynomial; the abelian component contributes L−1. Their enhanced polynomial discards the reducible-representation component, so do not conflate the ordinary and enhanced polynomials. Edges of the Newton polygon record strict boundary slopes via imported Culler–Shalen theory.

Bénard–Florens–Rodau define a slope rational function from the kernel of H1(∂E_K;Ad ρ) → H1(E_K;Ad ρ) for generic admissible representations, with a separate treatment for boundary-parabolic representations. Proposition 1.2 makes this a rational function on each relevant irreducible character component. Theorem 4.1 (journal p. 578; PDF p. 18) states, in the paper’s variable order A=A_K(L,M), that on an irreducible character component X whose restriction image has dimension 1,
s_K(χ) = −(M/L)(∂_M A_K(L,M)/∂_L A_K(L,M)),
as a rational identity on the component (evaluate where defined). This is the negative logarithmic Gauss map. Theorem 5.2 (PDF p. 21) says at an ideal point of a one-dimensional A-polynomial component this slope value is minus the associated Culler–Shalen boundary slope, equivalently minus the corresponding Newton-polygon side slope, under the paper’s convention that aμ+bλ has slope b/a. These conclusions are not defined for every character and depend on the peripheral marking and dimension-one restriction hypothesis. Fox calculus in §3.5 provides a computational method, not a classification of all incompressible surfaces.

Baldwin–Sivek, Mathematische Annalen 394 (2026), Theorem 1.1: a knot in S3 has infinitely many rational SL(2,C)-abelian Dehn surgeries iff it is a torus knot. Theorem 1.2: its enhanced A-polynomial Newton polygon is contained in a line iff the knot is a torus knot. Corollaries 1.3–1.4 classify when the enhanced polynomial alone identifies a specific torus knot and show that the enhanced polynomial plus Alexander-polynomial degree identifies every torus knot. Theorem 1.7 gives a partial result for a nontrivial fibered non-torus knot with exactly two boundary slopes: it has a connected separating incompressible slope-zero surface or at least three boundary slopes. Proof route: thinness excludes hyperbolicity by imported results, connects to infinitely many SU(2)-abelian surgeries and instanton L-space knot constraints, rules out satellite knots through Alexander polynomial restrictions, then uses the JSJ-style trichotomy “torus, satellite or hyperbolic” with appropriate imported knot results. The argument is substantially gauge-theoretic, not a geometric proof of the classification. The paper explicitly leaves open Question 1.5 (whether the ordinary A-polynomial detects torus knots from all other knots), Question 1.6 (the two-boundary-slope characterization), and Question 1.8 (generalization to knots in other 3-manifolds). Keep those as terminal open-problem designs, never as prerequisites.

## Bounded recent frontier and evidence limits

The bounded search was targeted at 2023–2026 geometric Dehn-surgery/character-variety work. Two complete papers are incorporated above: the 2024 NYJM slope-invariant paper and Baldwin–Sivek’s 2026 journal article, whose accepted preprint v2 was posted 28 Nov 2025. This establishes recent activity at the interface of A-polynomials, twisted homology, boundary slopes, and surgery representation spaces. Baldwin–Sivek cite 2023–2024 work on small surgery and SU(2) (including BLSY24, Geom. Topol. 28(4), 1891–1922) and build on 2023 instanton L-space results, but I did not full-read those sources in this lane.

The search also surfaced the 2024 trace-fields/hyperbolic-Dehn-filling line (Garoufalidis et al., preprint arXiv:2103.00767, journal-version lead) and a 2024 preprint on the volume conjecture for hyperbolic Dehn-filled twist knots (arXiv:2410.20489; later Quantum Topology lead). These were not full-read here, and their titles/search metadata are leads only, not theorem evidence. This is a bounded search, not an exhaustive survey. Do not assert either lead’s theorem without obtaining and reading its exact version.

## Local proof obligations / suggested order

1. Define the categories precisely: smooth links versus PL knot diagrams; compact exterior E_K; meridian/zero-longitude conventions; peripheral slope and p/q filling; boundary, incompressibility, irreducibility, atoroidal, Seifert-fibered, satellite pattern and companion. Prove the tubular-neighborhood boundary is a torus and define the marking.
2. Prove the knot-specific homology/irreducibility facts used later: H1(E_K)=Z generated by the meridian, the zero-linking longitude is the primitive boundary kernel, and a nontrivial knot exterior is irreducible and boundary-incompressible (or state and prove the exact weaker hypotheses needed). Keep smooth/PL and locally flat assumptions explicit.
3. Give prime decomposition and Hatcher’s JSJ theorem as local theorem modules with the full proof dependencies and exact orientable/irreducible/compact hypotheses. Prove the satellite/JSJ trichotomy in S3 as a bridge. Do not turn “atoroidal” into “hyperbolic” without a separately stated hyperbolization theorem.
4. If the track needs hyperbolic classification, state the exact Haken hyperbolization contract for irreducible, atoroidal, boundary-incompressible compact 3-manifolds with torus boundary and its exclusions; then author a complete proof module or cite a complete accessible proof with all prerequisites. Geometrization/hyperbolization is a major proof obligation; no automatic inheritance from the supplied local items.
5. Define complete finite-volume hyperbolic structures, cusps, developing map/holonomy, ideal triangulations and shape equations. Prove the gluing equations suffice for a complete structure only with the vertex-link/completeness conditions.
6. Define Dehn filling along primitive slopes and prove group/fundamental-group quotient behavior separately. State hyperbolic Dehn surgery with exact cusped hypotheses. For a full proof, localize the deformation-space theorem, peripheral holonomy parameters, completeness criterion, trace-map openness, and Mostow rigidity; Thurston Ch. 5 alone leaves dependencies/sketches. Distinguish hyperbolic fillings from arbitrary topological fillings and orbifold/cone parameters.
7. Define volume and prove the ideal-tetrahedron formula, additivity under a complete ideal triangulation, volume/simplicial-volume equality with cusp-boundary hypotheses, and strict volume decrease under hyperbolic filling. Do not make a volume computation into an existence/classification argument.
8. Define representation and character varieties, the peripheral restriction map, ordinary/enhanced A-polynomial, variable order, Newton polygon, boundary slope and Culler–Shalen ideal point. For the BFR slope invariant, provide the twisted-homology definitions, admissibility locus, rationality proof, Fox-calculus derivation, and dimension/denominator conditions. Treat the cited research classification theorems as imported until their independent proof obligations are met.
9. For an algorithmic normal-surface unknot theorem, keep the separate PL lane: good triangulation from an n-crossing diagram; fundamental versus vertex normal surfaces; explicit finite bounds; completeness/correctness and Turing-model complexity; essential disk iff unknot. HLP supplies an exact complexity theorem and an honest module map, but not the complete proofs of Haken/Jaco–Tollefson. Do not confuse this with enumerating Reidemeister moves, which only semidecides positive instances.
