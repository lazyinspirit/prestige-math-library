## Mathematical contracts and source-specific proof recipes

### Ordinary Khovanov complex

For an oriented link diagram (D) with (n) ordered crossings, resolve every crossing by 0 or 1 smoothing. The vertices (vin{0,1}^n) form the cube; an edge changes one 0 to 1 and is labelled by the corresponding saddle cobordism. Apply the rank-two Frobenius algebra
(A=mathbb Z[X]/(X^2)), with (deg(1)=1,deg(X)=-1), to the circles of each smoothing. The multiplication and comultiplication are
(m(1otimes1)=1, m(1otimes X)=m(Xotimes1)=X, m(Xotimes X)=0), and
(Delta(1)=1otimes X+Xotimes1, Delta(X)=Xotimes X).
The differential is the signed sum of edge maps, with the cube sign on the edge changing coordinate (j) equal to ((-1)^{sum_{i<j}v_i}). Disjoint saddles commute before signs, so each square anticommutes and (d^2=0). Use the source’s writhe-dependent shifts to make (d) bidegree ((1,0)); Bar-Natan’s convention is (Kh(T)^r=[T]^r{r+n_+-n_-}), with homological index (r-n_-). The homology is an oriented-link invariant; the graded Euler characteristic is the Jones polynomial in the source’s normalization.

Khovanov’s 1999 paper proves Reidemeister invariance of the homology and discusses cobordism maps, but states movie/isotopy coherence of those maps as Conjecture 1 (up to sign). Bar-Natan 2005 resolves that conjecture in the stated up-to-sign sense: the formal bracket is invariant up to chain homotopy and induces a canopoly functor from isotopy classes of generic link/tangle cobordisms to formal complexes modulo homotopy and overall sign. His proof checks Carter–Saito movie moves. Applying a 1+1-dimensional TQFT gives the usual homology maps up to sign. This proves only up-to-sign functoriality; do not promote it to strict sign-free functoriality of ordinary even Khovanov homology. Include a separate sign-refinement source if strict functoriality is claimed.

Proof recipe: define the Frobenius algebra and shifts; check local saddle maps and anticommuting cube squares; prove the three Reidemeister chain-homotopy equivalences, including explicit cancellation for R1/R2 and cone/retract argument for R3; then show the Frobenius TQFT respects the local cobordism relations. For cobordism functoriality, define the movie category and prove the movie-move relations modulo homotopy/sign. Khovanov’s source has the complex/invariance proof; Bar-Natan supplies the missing movie-move proof. Do not infer ordinary Khovanov results from the existing Khovanov–Rozansky HOMFLYPT item.

### Lee deformation and Rasmussen (s)

Lee replaces the Frobenius algebra by the filtered deformation (A'=mathbb Q[X]/(X^2-1)). The induced filtration produces a spectral sequence whose (E_2) page is Khovanov homology and whose limit has rank (2^c) for a (c)-component link, with canonical classes indexed by orientations. For a knot, the two surviving filtration levels differ by 2; (s(K)) is their midpoint (equivalently (s_{max}-1=s_{min}+1)) and is even. Filtered cobordism maps and the canonical classes give (|s(K)|le 2g_4(K)); positive torus knots attain the bound. The proof requires Lee’s algebra/deformation, filtered spectral-sequence behavior, and the canonical-generator/cobordism argument, not merely the generic spectral-sequence supplier.

The equality (s(K)=2\tau(K)) for all knots is not open: Hedden–Ording prove it false. Their 2-twisted positive Whitehead double of the right-handed trefoil satisfies (	au=0) and (s=2) (Theorem 1.1). Equality does hold in several special classes listed in that paper, including alternating and positive/quasipositive knots; state the class precisely if used.

### Knot Floer, (	au), (epsilon), and (Upsilon)

For a doubly pointed Heegaard diagram of a null-homologous knot, (widehat{CFK}) is generated by intersection tuples and its differential counts Maslov-index-one holomorphic disks with multiplicities at the two basepoints. The two basepoints induce the Maslov and Alexander gradings/filtration. The minus/infinity versions retain the (U)-action and filtered chain-homotopy type. (widehat{HFK}) has graded Euler characteristic the Alexander polynomial; for a knot in (S^3), the top Alexander grading equals Seifert genus. A major analytic prerequisite is invariance under admissible Heegaard moves and invariance of holomorphic-disk counts. Manolescu’s full survey explains the construction but explicitly relies on those foundations rather than reproving them.

Ozsváth–Szabó define (	au(K)=min{m:H_*(F(K,m))\to\widehat{HF}(S^3)=\mathbb Z\text{ is nonzero}}), where (F(K,m)) is the subcomplex at Alexander filtration at most (m). It is additive, changes sign under mirror, and is a smooth-concordance homomorphism. Their Theorem 1.1 states: if (W) is smooth oriented with (partial W=S^3), (b_1(W)=b_2^+(W)=0), and a smooth properly embedded oriented surface (Sigmasubset W) has boundary (K), then
(2\tau(K)+\|[\Sigma]\|_1+[\Sigma]\cdot[\Sigma]\le 2g(\Sigma)).
Here (|[\Sigma]|_1) is the sum of absolute coefficients in the diagonal basis of the negative-definite intersection form used in the paper. Taking (W=B^4) gives (|\tau(K)|le g_4(K)). The proof route uses large surgery, Heegaard Floer cobordism maps, Donaldson diagonalization, and an adjunction inequality; these are not locally supplied as a knot-specific proof module. The PDF has an apparent factor-of-two typo in the derivation on PDF p.17; Theorem 1.1 on printed p.617 and the subsequent arithmetic/corollary state the consistent factor (2g(\Sigma)).

Hom’s complete 2017 survey defines (epsilon(K)in{-1,0,1}) via the filtered complex and (	au,
u); (epsilon(K)=0) implies (	au(K)=0), but the converse fails. It records additivity/mirror behavior and the relation to (
u^-) and the (V_s) sequence. Do not claim (epsilon) detects concordance-triviality.

Ozsváth–Stipsicz–Szabó define the (t)-modified complex from (CFK^-) over a long-power-series valuation ring and define (Upsilon_K(t)) as the maximal grading of a homogeneous non-torsion class. The construction is functorial under filtered chain maps/homotopies; (Upsilon) is piecewise linear, (Upsilon(0)=0), its initial slope is (-\tau), it is additive under connected sum, changes sign under mirror, and (|Upsilon_K(t)|\le t g_4(K)) for (0\le t\le1). Their Conjectures 1.23 and 1.24 ask whether the sequence of derivative-jump values at rational (t\in(0,1)) surjects onto a direct sum of countably many copies of (mathbb Z), respectively from all smooth concordance classes and from the subgroup of topologically slice knots. This source’s conjectures remain conjectures in that 2017 text; the bounded 2023–2026 search here did not verify a later resolution, so do not present either surjectivity as proved or definitively label its present-day status without further review.

### Grid computations and fiberedness

A grid diagram of size (n) has one (O) and one (X) in each row and column. Grid states are permutations (there are (n!)); the mod-2 differential sums empty rectangles containing no (X)- or (O)-markings. The Maslov grading and Alexander filtration are combinatorial, and the grid complex computes (widehat{HFK}(K)\otimes V^{n-1}); its filtered minus version recovers (CFK^-) up to the stated extra factors. Hence this gives a finite exact algorithm from a grid: enumerate states/empty rectangles, form the matrix over (mathbb F_2), and compute homology and filtration. It is not a polynomial-time claim: the displayed state set has size (n!). Manolescu–Ozsváth–Sarkar’s full 2007 paper identifies the grid differential with holomorphic disk counts and imports multi-pointed Heegaard invariance. It cites the detailed grid commutation/stabilization invariance proof to MOST07 rather than proving that combinatorial move theory in the paper. That missing source is an explicit prerequisite if the category authors a grid-move invariance theorem.

Ni’s Theorem 1.1 is exact: for a null-homologous knot (K) in a closed, oriented, connected 3-manifold (Y), with irreducible exterior (Y\setminus K), if
(widehat{HFK}(Y,K,[F],g(F))\cong\mathbb Z)
for a genus-(g) Seifert surface (F), then (K) is fibered and (F) is a fiber. In (S^3), the complement is irreducible, so this yields the top-rank-one fiberedness criterion. The converse that a fibered knot has rank-one top HFK is supplied by earlier Ozsváth–Szabó results, not reproved by Ni. Ni’s proof is genuinely substantial: sutured Floer/HFK decomposition formulas, Gabai taut decompositions and foliations, a characteristic product region/JSJ input, and a holomorphic-disk argument. Treat the exact theorem as importable only with those proof suppliers/citations scoped.

Kubota 2024 proves that the grid-defined (Upsilon) agrees with the original Ozsváth–Stipsicz–Szabó invariant: quotient the grid complex by identifying all (U_i), use a filtered chain-homotopy equivalence after tensoring with the extra (W)-factors, and pass to the (t)-modified complexes. This answers the equivalence problem addressed in that paper; it does not improve the (n!) state-count complexity.

### Two distinct Khovanov spectral sequences

1. Ozsváth–Szabó’s branched-cover spectral sequence (2003 preprint / 2005 publication): for (L\subset S^3), (E_2=\widetilde{Kh}(\overline L;\mathbb F_2)) and it converges to (widehat{HF}(\Sigma(L);\mathbb F_2)), where (Sigma(L)) is the branched double cover. The proof specializes the link-surgery spectral sequence and identifies the cube differential with reduced Khovanov via a TQFT. The paper states that invariance of the intermediate spectral sequence under filtered quasi-isomorphism was expected, not proved there (PDF p.4). This is not a spectral sequence to knot Floer homology of (K).

2. Kronheimer–Mrowka’s instanton spectral sequence (2010): with integral coefficients, (E_2=\widetilde{Kh}(\overline K;\mathbb Z)) and it converges to reduced singular instanton knot homology (I^\natural(K)). The reduced Khovanov unknot detector follows from this, the excision isomorphism (I^\natural(K;\mathbb Q)\cong KHI(K;\mathbb Q)), and the earlier theorem that sutured instanton (KHI) detects the unknot. The latter is cited to their separate “Knots, sutures and excision” paper, not proved here. The source proves substantial singular-ASD/Floer machinery but does not make those analytic prerequisites elementary or local.

The local filtered-complex supplier constructs the exact couple without convergence assumptions; the local bounded-filtration theorem gives pointwise convergence when each chain degree has a finite filtration (no uniform bound over degrees required). These are appropriate generic tools after the knot-specific filtration and first page have been defined. They do not construct the link-surgery or instanton spectral sequences.

## Computability, complexity, and recognition boundary

For an (n)-crossing diagram, the Khovanov cube has (2^n) vertices; a direct boundary matrix computation terminates but grows exponentially. Grid Floer computation has (n!) generators for grid size (n), before rectangle enumeration. These are effective finite invariant algorithms, not polynomial-time results. Khovanov’s unknot-detection theorem turns exact reduced-Kh computation into a decision procedure for the unknot, with no polynomial complexity bound supplied by the detector theorem. Equality of a general invariant on two knots is not an isotopy decision procedure unless a completeness theorem is separately established.

Reader 4 fully read the journal HLP source and reports: input a link diagram; build a good PL triangulation of its exterior with (t=O(n)) tetrahedra; enumerate Haken fundamental normal-coordinate vectors or Jaco–Tollefson minimal vertex solutions, test matching/quadrilateral constraints and the essential-disk conditions, and return yes iff the knot is unknotted. The source’s bounds are Haken (O(2^{O(n^2)})) time or Jaco–Tollefson (O(2^{O(n)})) time, both (O(n^2\log n)) space, plus polynomially verifiable NP certificates. The deep normal-surface minimum-genus/Jaco–Tollefson results are cited rather than proved. Use reader-4.md/json as the exact primary-source record; I did not read or independently verify HLP. Keep this diagram-to-normal-surface contract distinct from exponential Kh/grid invariant computation. Do not assert these historical upper bounds are current optimal complexity results.

## Proof prerequisites and source status for the scaffold

- **Ordinary Khovanov/Jones:** local Frobenius algebra, cube signs, grading conventions, and Reidemeister chain homotopies are required; generic chain-complex and homotopy items can be reused. Jones/TL/Markov suppliers can support decategorification, but the categorical bridge is knot-specific.
- **Khovanov cobordism maps:** use Bar-Natan’s movie-move result modulo homotopy/sign. A stricter sign-free target needs a separately read sign-refinement source.
- **(s):** Lee deformation, spectral sequence, canonical generators, and filtered cobordism/genus estimate. General filtered-complex items supply only abstract machinery.
- **Knot Floer/grid:** define admissible doubly pointed Heegaard diagrams or a grid model, chain complexes, differential, gradings and filtration; supply analytic Heegaard-move invariance, or import the full grid combinatorial invariance proof. A survey citation is not itself a proof.
- **(	au,epsilon,Upsilon):** define the filtered-complex constructions and derive algebraic properties from a proved filtered chain-homotopy/concordance interface. (	au)'s four-ball estimate additionally uses Floer cobordism/surgery and smooth 4-manifold inputs.
- **Fiberedness:** Ni’s exact theorem is a mature import, but its theorem assumptions and sutured/HFK decomposition prerequisites must accompany it.
- **Khovanov unknot detection:** Kronheimer–Mrowka’s theorem is established, but its conclusion rests on an analytic singular-instanton theory and a cited sutured-instanton unknot detector. Identify those as prerequisite modules, not as routine consequences of Kh.
- **Branched cover:** keep the target (widehat{HF}(Sigma(K))) distinct from knot Floer (widehat{HFK}(K)) and from instanton (I^\natural(K)).
