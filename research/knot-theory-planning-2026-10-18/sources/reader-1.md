# Reader 1: classical foundations, diagrams, and framed knots

## Lane and source coverage

This dossier covers the elementary classical layer: tame smooth and PL knots and links, regular projections, crossing conventions and Reidemeister moves, orientations, elementary link operations, crossing/linking numbers, Seifert surfaces and genus, framings, and small knot-table examples. The strongest existing local coverage is the published smooth oriented-link/Reidemeister/Yamada–Vogel/Alexander chain in Braid Groups. The new scaffold should take that chain as external prerequisite context and add only the missing knot-specific definitions and bridges.

I retrieved and read all 48 pages of Danny Calegari, *Introduction to Knot Theory*, University of Chicago course notes dated 6 May 2025. Full coverage: §1.1–1.3 (pp. 1–8: smooth and polygonal models, diagrams, Reidemeister theorem, crossing number and colorings); §2.1–2.2 (pp. 9–20: unknot/disk characterization, polygonal Jordan theorem, compact surface classification); §3.1–3.3 (pp. 21–29: Seifert surfaces, genus, connected sum and genus additivity, linking number and intersection); §4.1–4.3 (pp. 30–37: colorings, knot group and Alexander polynomial); §5.1–5.3 (pp. 38–47: Kauffman bracket, writhe normalization, alternating diagrams and HOMFLY); acknowledgments and references (p. 48). The full PDF and page-preserving `pdftotext -layout` extraction are in the ignored reader-1 directory.

I read all 11 pages of Hoel Queffelec, “Reidemeister’s theorem using transversality,” arXiv:2406.18203v1 (26 June 2024): §1 and Theorem 1 (pp. 1–3); multijet transversality setup and Theorem 2 (pp. 3–5); the complete written body of §3.1–3.6 (pp. 5–10); §4 and references (pp. 10–11). I read all 21 pages of Mohamed Elhamdadi, Mustafa Hajij, and Kyle Istvan, “Framed Knots,” arXiv:1910.10257v6 (23 May 2020): §§1–7.1 and all references (pp. 1–21). These are complete readings of the posted versions, not claims about later editions.

As a focused comparison, I read the complete available Gardner/ETSU course-note PDFs based on Charles Livingston’s 1993 *Knot Theory*: §2.1 (3 pp.), §2.2 (2 pp.), §2.3 (2 pp.), §2.4 (4 pp.), §2.5 (3 pp.), §3.1 (3 pp.), §4.3 (3 pp.), and §4.5 (5 pp.), plus the two-page §4.3 and §4.5 proof supplements. `pdftotext` preserves the 25 main-note pages; the supplements are image/vector Beamer printouts for which `pdftotext` returned no text, so I rendered and visually read every page. The two supplements state the Seifert-surface existence proof (Theorem 4.3.7) and genus-additivity/prime-decomposition-existence sketches (Theorems 4.5.10 and 4.5.9). ## Mathematical synthesis and proof routes

The appropriate primary model for the new track is the existing smooth model: a finite disjoint union of smoothly embedded oriented circles in $S^3$, considered up to ambient isotopy. It is essential to keep the category visible. Wild topological embeddings are excluded; a continuous family of pointwise smooth embeddings is weaker than ambient isotopy unless isotopy extension is supplied; smooth concordance in $B^4$ is a different relation from link isotopy in $S^3$. The local definition fixes precisely this smooth oriented $S^3$ contract. Calegari gives both smooth and polygonal models and proposes their equivalence, but explicitly says that the approximation paragraph establishing their agreement is not rigorously proved. A new tame/PL bridge needs its own proof: round finitely many polygonal corners in disjoint balls, approximate a compact smooth curve by a sufficiently fine polygonal normal graph, and perform both changes by isotopies through embeddings; for equivalence, carry the same construction relative to the ends of the isotopy trace. Do not silently identify arbitrary topological embeddings with these tame models.

For regular oriented diagrams, the local library already fixes the projection, over/under data, orientations, finiteness and transverse-double-point hypotheses. It also proves the full smooth oriented Reidemeister theorem. Queffelec gives a useful proof map: multijet transversality isolates projected cusp, double-tangency and triple-point events; the local models are R1, R2 and R3. His §3.1–3.6 is not a full proof of all three local models: §3.3 works out R1, while §§3.4-3.5 explicitly leave the R3 and R2 local analyses informal. The local library is therefore the rigorous logical supplier for the smooth theorem; Queffelec is source context and motivation, not a replacement proof. A PL version still needs a bridge from PL diagram movies to the existing smooth theorem or its own finite polygonal proof.

The principal missing diagram convention is a definition of crossing sign. The local diagram definition records the overpassing branch and the oriented strands; the Reidemeister-move definition then refers to positive/negative crossings, and the braid/writhe consumers count signs, but no base item defines the sign. A suitable contract chooses the projection-plane orientation from the ambient orientation and the positive viewing normal, then defines sign(c) = sgn det(v_over, v_under), where v_over and v_under are tangent vectors following the over- and under-strand orientations. The determinant uses that oriented plane. Calibrate the standard positive Artin half twist as +1 against the existing geometric-braid convention; if a diagram chart reverses the induced plane orientation, the sign reverses. Then define diagram writhe by summing all crossing signs and link linking number for components K_i,K_j by half the sum over their mixed crossings. Calegari also gives the equivalent convention using the signed crossings where one fixed component passes over; Elhamdadi et al. use the half-sum convention. The local statement should prove the equivalence under its sign convention rather than silently switch formulas. The local proof is finite: R1 creates only a self-crossing; R2 creates/removes two opposite signs; R3 preserves the three signs. Reversing one component reverses linking number, reversing both does not, and interchanging component labels leaves it unchanged. An independent proof route identifies linking number with the signed intersection of one component with an oriented Seifert surface for the other; Elhamdadi et al. §3.1 develops this through Alexander duality and homology, and Calegari §3.3 gives a diagrammatic additive-invariant proof.

For a diagram D, the crossing count c(D) is finite and directly computable. The crossing number c(K) = min{c(D) : D represents K} is a minimum over the Reidemeister class, not an algorithm supplied by the theorem. A particular diagram proves only an upper bound. Calegari §1.3 shows how colorings yield lower bounds; his §2.2 enumerates diagrams to prove that no 1- or 2-crossing knots exist and that the 3-crossing knot types are the two mirror trefoils. That finite low-crossing census is appropriate material for examples, provided each row distinguishes a displayed diagram’s crossing count from a proved minimal crossing number. The local Braid Groups example already computes all closures of the two-strand words sigma_1^m as T(2,m) torus links and covers m = 0, +/-1, +/-3; the Yamada–Vogel example already runs on 5_2. Reuse those examples instead of recreating braid calculations.

The classical operation most clearly missing locally is oriented connected sum. Calegari §3.1 defines it by deleting short arcs and joining the ends; his §3.1 Theorem 3.7 gives the standard genus-additivity proof route. Its upper bound glues minimal-genus Seifert surfaces by a rectangle. For the lower bound, make a minimal surface transverse to the decomposing sphere, remove innermost circle intersections by compression while tracking the closed sphere components, then cut along the single remaining spanning arc to obtain surfaces for the two summands. Gardner’s §4.5 notes give the same picture-driven argument; they explicitly do not give a uniqueness proof for prime decomposition. Do not infer prime-decomposition uniqueness from those notes.

The Seifert algorithm is a finite procedure on a regular oriented link diagram: smooth each crossing in the unique orientation-preserving way; span the resulting Seifert circles by disks, placed at separated heights to respect nesting; attach one half-twisted band in the small crossing ball for each original crossing, with sign and endpoints matching the crossing. Correctness requires a separate theorem: each local band restores exactly the original crossing and orientation, the finite disks and bands form an embedded orientable surface, and its boundary is the original link. Starting with s(D) disks and attaching c(D) bands gives Euler characteristic chi = s(D) - c(D). For a connected genus-g surface with m boundary components, chi = 2 - 2g - m; for a knot this gives the canonical upper bound g(K) <= (c(D) - s(D) + 1)/2. The Calegari and Gardner sources explain the construction, but their proofs are diagrammatic and leave orientation/embeddedness details; the local library currently supplies Seifert smoothing and the much deeper Seifert-circle height/Yamada–Vogel machinery, not the existence theorem, the surface definition, or knot genus. Existing topology suppliers give the closed-surface classification and a published compact-boundary-surface interface with chi = 2 - 2g - b (lem-finite-chart-surface-normal-forms-supply-jordan-disks-and-torsion-free-groups).

A framed knot should be stated as an oriented or unoriented embedded circle together with a homotopy class of nonzero normal vector fields (equivalently, a ribbon annulus with the knot as a core/boundary, once the precise model is fixed). In an oriented 3-manifold the normal bundle of an oriented circle is an oriented rank-two bundle; choosing a reference framing makes other framing classes a Z-torsor, since their relative difference is a loop in SO(2), which is a circle. The integer is not absolute before a reference is selected. A Seifert surface provides the zero framing; the self-linking number of a framed knot is the linking number with a small framed push-off. For a blackboard framing, that self-linking number equals the diagram writhe. An R1 kink changes the blackboard framing by one; R2/R3 preserve it. Framed diagram equivalence therefore needs its own theorem or modified R1 move, and must not identify a single framed kink with the unframed R1 move. Elhamdadi et al. §§3–6 cover these interfaces; their paper also relates a framing to a longitude on a tubular torus and to integer surgery, but surgery is not needed in this lane.

## Local supplier inventory and boundaries

The main reusable suppliers, their current full-file hashes, and the exact interfaces inspected are in `reader-1.json`. The key chain is:

- def-oriented-link-in-s-three-and-ambient-isotopy fixes smooth oriented links in S^3 and orientation-preserving ambient isotopy; it intentionally does not define polygonal links.
- `def-regular-oriented-link-diagram`, `def-planar-isotopy-of-link-diagrams`, `def-oriented-reidemeister-moves`, and `thm-oriented-reidemeister-equivalence-theorem` — published smooth diagram conventions and the complete oriented Reidemeister statement. `lem-every-oriented-link-admits-a-regular-projection`, `lem-a-smooth-isotopy-of-links-can-be-put-in-general-position`, `lem-a-generic-isotopy-of-links-has-only-reidemeister-singular-times`, and `lem-each-oriented-reidemeister-move-is-realized-by-ambient-isotopy` are the local proof modules; their transversality/isotopy-extension dependencies must stay explicit.
- `def-seifert-smoothing-and-seifert-circles-of-an-oriented-link-diagram`, `def-coherence-of-seifert-circles-and-the-height-of-a-diagram`, `def-reducing-arc-and-yamada-vogel-reducing-move`, `lem-yamada-vogel-reducing-moves-lower-bad-seifert-circle-complexity`, `lem-a-height-zero-diagram-represents-a-closed-braid`, and `thm-alexanders-closed-braid-theorem` — published smoothing, height reduction and Alexander closed-braid theorem. The Yamada–Vogel height is an auxiliary braid-conversion complexity, not the Seifert genus or crossing number.
- `def-the-framed-oriented-tangle-category` gives a precise framed tangle category, fixed boundary collars, framed isotopy and blackboard-framed generators. `def-framing-of-a-normal-bundle` gives actual normal-bundle trivializations. Neither currently states the classical knot framing integer, the zero Seifert framing, or blackboard writhe/self-linking equality.
- `lem-a-smooth-isotopy-of-compact-embedded-submanifolds-extends-to-an-ambient-isotopy` supplies the compact smooth isotopy-extension theorem. `thm-classification-of-compact-connected-surfaces` handles closed surfaces; the C² boundary-surface Euler/genus interface is `lem-finite-chart-surface-normal-forms-supply-jordan-disks-and-torsion-free-groups`.
- The Braid Groups pages already include `ex-torus-links-as-closures-of-two-strand-braids`, `ex-alexanders-braiding-algorithm-on-a-small-diagram`, and `def-exponent-sum-and-writhe-of-a-braid`. The last is a braid-group exponent-sum homomorphism, not the missing crossing-sign definition or writhe of a general oriented link diagram.

The smallest missing contracts are a PL/tame model and its equivalence bridge to the smooth local model; a crossing-sign definition calibrated to the Artin generators; diagram crossing number and writhe; oriented connected sum; a linking-number definition with its Reidemeister/intersection proof; Seifert-surface and knot-genus definitions; a finite Seifert-surface algorithm plus separate correctness and Euler-characteristic results; and framed-knot, framing-integer, self-linking, blackboard-framing and Seifert-framing statements with the framed diagram theorem. B-page examples should include the unknot, Hopf link/unlink, the trefoil and its mirror, the figure-eight knot, and low-crossing table rows; use the existing torus-braid and 5_2 examples as prerequisites/context.

No finite diagram census proves a general minimum-crossing algorithm. A chosen diagram gives a checkable upper bound; the lower-bound/classification proof is a separate obligation. No new open problem is needed for these classical definitions. If the wider track mentions Jones-polynomial unknot detection, it must be a terminal, date-checked `op-` item with an authoritative status source, never a supplier; I did not perform a separate status review of that frontier question for this lane. I also did not audit the full transitive proofs of every cited local supplier, the whole braid category, or all of the Calegari/Elhamdadi bibliographies.
