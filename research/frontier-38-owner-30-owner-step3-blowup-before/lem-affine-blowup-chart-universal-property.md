## Statement

Let $\varphi\colon A\to B$ be a ring map, $I\subseteq A$ an ideal and $a\in I$;
suppose the image $b=\varphi(a)$ is a nonzerodivisor in $B$ and
$IB=bB$. Then there is a unique $A$-algebra homomorphism
$A[I/a]\to B$ sending $x/a^n$ to the unique $y\in B$ with $x=b^ny$
($x\in I^n$); equivalently, $\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A[I/a]$
is the unique $A$-morphism into the chart $\operatorname{Spec}A[I/a]$ along
which the image of $a$ generates $I\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Spec}B}$. The
chart $A[I/a]$ itself satisfies the hypothesis with $b$ the image of $a$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A commutative ring $A$, an ideal $I\subseteq A$, an element $a\in I$, a ring map $\varphi\colon A\to B$ whose image $b=\varphi(a)$ is a nonzerodivisor in $B$ and satisfies $IB=bB$, and the affine blowup algebra $A[I/a]$ of ([[lem-affine-blowup-algebra-properties]]) built from the Rees algebra $R(I)=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}I^nt^n$ of ([[def-rees-algebra-and-rees-module]]).

[F1] [[lem-affine-blowup-algebra-properties]]: For a commutative ring $A$, an ideal $I\subseteq A$ and $a\in I$, the affine blowup algebra $A[I/a]:=(R(I))_{(a)}$ is the degree-zero part of the localisation of the Rees algebra $R(I)$ at the multiplicative set generated by $at$; the image of $a$ in $A[I/a]$ is a nonzerodivisor and $IA[I/a]=aA[I/a]$.

[F2] [[def-rees-algebra-and-rees-module]]: For a commutative ring $R$ and an ideal $I\subset R$, the Rees algebra is the graded subring $R(I)=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}I^nt^n\subset R[t]$; equivalently, it is the graded ring whose degree-$n$ piece is $I^n$.

[F3] [[def-multiplicative-subset-and-localisation]]: For a commutative ring $R$ and a multiplicative subset $S\subseteq R$, the localisation $S^{-1}R$ has elements written $r/s$ with $r/s=r'/s'$ if and only if $u(rs'-r's)=0$ for some $u\in S$; the operations are $r/s+r'/s'=(rs'+r's)/(ss')$ and $(r/s)(r'/s')=rr'/(ss')$; every $s\in S$ maps to a unit.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** Represent elements of the chart by fractions in the localised Rees algebra, define the map on those fractions, and verify well-definedness and uniqueness on the standard generators.

1.1 An element of the degree-zero part $C:=A[I/a]$ of $R(I)_{at}$ is a finite sum of classes $b\,t^n/(at)^n$ with $n\ge0$ and $b\in I^n$: a class in the localisation can be written $z/(at)^n$ with $z\in R(I)$, its degree-zero part is obtained from the degree-$n$ piece of $z$, and by [F2] that piece is of the form $b\,t^n$ with $b\in I^n$. Moreover, for $b\in I^n$ and $c\in I^m$ one has $b\,t^n/(at)^n=c\,t^m/(at)^m$ in $C$ if and only if $a^k(a^mb-a^nc)=0$ for some $k\ge0$: by [F3] applied to $R(I)$ and its multiplicative set, equality holds if and only if $(at)^k\bigl((at)^mb\,t^n-(at)^nc\,t^m\bigr)=0$ for some $k$, that is $t^{k+m+n}a^k(a^mb-a^nc)=0$, and the powers of $t$ form an $A$-basis of the polynomial ring $A[t]\supseteq R(I)$, so this is equivalent to $a^k(a^mb-a^nc)=0$. [F1, F2, F3]

1.2 For every $n\ge0$ and $x\in I^n$ there is a unique $y\in B$ with $\varphi(x)=b^ny$. Indeed $I^nB=(IB)^n=(bB)^n=b^nB$ and $\varphi(x)\in I^nB$, so such a $y$ exists; if $b^ny=b^ny'$ then $b^n(y-y')=0$, and $b$ is a nonzerodivisor, so $y=y'$. [given]

2.1 The assignment $\psi$ sending the class of $b\,t^n/(at)^n$ ($b\in I^n$) to the unique $y\in B$ of step 1.2 with $\varphi(b)=b^ny$ is a well-defined function on $C$: if $b\,t^n/(at)^n=c\,t^m/(at)^m$ for $b\in I^n$, $c\in I^m$, then $a^k(a^mb-a^nc)=0$ for some $k$ by step 1.1, so applying $\varphi$ gives $b^k\bigl(b^m\varphi(b)-b^n\varphi(c)\bigr)=0$; writing $\varphi(b)=b^ny$ and $\varphi(c)=b^my'$ with $y,y'$ as in step 1.2, this is $b^{k+m+n}(y-y')=0$, hence $y=y'$ because $b$ is a nonzerodivisor. [step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 The function $\psi$ is a unital $A$-algebra homomorphism $C\to B$. It respects addition because $b\,t^n/(at)^n+c\,t^m/(at)^m=(a^mb+a^nc)t^{n+m}/(at)^{n+m}$ and $\varphi(a^mb+a^nc)=b^m\varphi(b)+b^n\varphi(c)=b^{n+m}(y+y')$, so the image of the sum is $y+y'$ by the uniqueness in step 1.2; it respects multiplication because the product is $bc\,t^{n+m}/(at)^{n+m}$ and $\varphi(bc)=b^{n+m}yy'$; the unit class $a/a$ maps to $1$ since $\varphi(a)=b=b\cdot1$; and an element $\lambda\in A=I^0$ maps to $\varphi(\lambda)$, so $\psi$ is $A$-linear and unital. [step 2.1]

4.1 Every class $b\,t^n/(at)^n$ with $b\in I^n$ is a sum of products of the elements $x/a=\bigl(x\,t/(at)\bigr)$, $x\in I$, together with coefficients in $A$: writing $b$ as a finite sum of $n$-fold products of elements of $I$, the class $b\,t^n/(at)^n$ is the corresponding sum of $n$-fold products of such generators, with the case $n=0$ giving the classes of elements of $A$. Hence any $A$-algebra homomorphism $\chi\colon C\to B$ that sends each $x/a^n$ to the element prescribed in the statement is determined on these generators, and therefore equals $\psi$; so $\psi$ is the unique such homomorphism. [F2, step 3.1]

5.1 By [F1] the image of $a$ in $C=A[I/a]$ is a nonzerodivisor satisfying $IC=aC$, so the chart $C$ itself satisfies the hypothesis of the statement with $b$ the image of $a$. Finally, giving an $A$-algebra homomorphism $C\to B$ is the same as giving an $A$-morphism $\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}C$, and the hypothesis $IB=bB$, i.e. $b$ generating the pullback $I\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Spec}B}$, is exactly the condition named in the geometric formulation; the unique homomorphism $\psi$ therefore corresponds to the unique such $A$-morphism into the chart. [F1, step 4.1] ∎
