## Statement

Let $S$ be a regular surface over a field $k$ and let $C\subseteq S$ be a reduced curve (an effective Cartier divisor) with a closed point $p$ at which the multiplicity $m=\operatorname{mult}_p(C)$ of a local equation is finite and positive. Let $\pi\colon S'\to S$ be the blowup of the point $p$ with exceptional curve $E$ and let $C'$ be the strict transform of $C$. Then $\pi^*C=C'+mE$ as effective Cartier divisors on $S'$; equivalently, the strict transform is defined by dividing a local equation of the total transform by the $m$-th power of an exceptional equation on each chart, and $C'$ meets $E$ in the $0$-cycle of degree $m$ cut out by the degree-$m$ leading form of a local equation of $C$ at $p$ (its degree over $\kappa(p)$ is $m$; its degree over $k$ is $m[\kappa(p):k]$ when this residue degree is finite).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A regular surface $S$ over $k$, a reduced curve $C\subseteq S$ that is an effective Cartier divisor, a closed point $p\in S$ at which a local equation $f$ of $C$ has finite positive multiplicity, the blowup $\pi\colon S'\to S$ of $p$, the exceptional curve $E$, and the strict transform $C'$ of $C$.

[A1] **Choice.** The Axiom of Choice is assumed as inherited from the blowup and associated-graded constructions used below.

[F1] [[def-total-transform-divisor]]: The total transform of an effective Cartier divisor $D$ under a blowup is the pullback $\pi^*D$, and when $D$ is effective it is written $\pi^*D=D'+mE$ with $D'$ the strict transform and $m$ the multiplicity along the center, the displayed identity being a theorem proved here rather than a definitional convention.

[F2] [[def-strict-transform-closed-subscheme]]: The strict transform of a closed subscheme is the scheme-theoretic closure of the inverse image minus $E$; when the ideal of $E$ is invertible on a chart, it is the closed subscheme defined by the saturation of the inverse-image ideal by the ideal of $E$.

[F3] [[thm-pullback-center-ideal-invertible]]: The inverse image ideal of the blowup is invertible, and $E=V(\mathcal I\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Bl}})$ is an effective Cartier divisor cut locally by a generator of that ideal.

[F4] [[lem-affine-blowup-algebra-properties]]: For a domain $A$ and $a\ne0$, the affine blowup algebra $A[I/a]$ is a domain with $I\,A[I/a]=a\,A[I/a]$.

[F5] [[thm-associated-graded-ring-of-a-regular-local-ring]]: At the regular local ring $A=\mathcal O_{S,p}$ of dimension two with regular parameters $x,y$, the associated graded ring is $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m}A=\kappa(p)[X,Y]$ with $X,Y$ the initial classes of $x,y$.

[F6] [[lem-cartier-divisor-local-equation-equivalence]]: Two effective Cartier divisors agree where their local equations differ by a unit; effective Cartier data are exactly principal ideals generated by regular sections.

[F7] [[thm-affine-blowup-standard-charts]]: The blowup at $p$ has the two charts $\operatorname{Spec}A[y/x]$ and $\operatorname{Spec}A[x/y]$, glued by inverting the ratio.

[F8] [[thm-blowup-regular-surface-closed-point-regular]]: The point blowup of a regular surface is regular of pure dimension two with these charts, and its exceptional curve is an effective Cartier divisor.

## Proof

1.1 Work over $A=\mathcal O_{S,p}$, a regular local ring of dimension two with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m=(x,y)$. Let $f\in A$ be a local equation of $C$ at $p$ and let $m\ge1$ be its multiplicity: $f\in\mathfrak m^m\setminus\mathfrak m^{m+1}$, so the initial form $f_m\in\operatorname{gr}_mA=\kappa(p)[X,Y]$ is nonzero by [F5]. In the first chart $A[y/x]$ of [F7], write $T=y/x$ and $f=x^mg$ with $g=\sum_{i+j=m}c_{ij}T^j\in A[T]=A[y/x]$; reducing modulo $x$ gives $\bar g=f_m(1,T)\ne0$ because the dehomogenization of a nonzero form is nonzero, and $A[y/x]$ is a domain with $x$ a nonzerodivisor by [F4]. [F4, F5, F7]

2.1 The exceptional curve in this chart is cut by $x$, a local equation of the invertible ideal $\mathcal I\mathcal O_{S'}$ by [F3] and [F8]; by [F2] the strict transform of $C$ in this chart is cut by the saturation $(f):x^\infty$. Since $(f)=(x^m)\cdot(g)$ and $x\nmid g$ in the domain $A[y/x]$ (step 1.1), $(f):x^\infty=(g)$; hence the total transform factors in this chart as $\pi^*C=(f)=(x^m)(g)=mE+C'$, with $C'$ cut by $g$. [F2, F3, F8, step 1.1]

3.1 In the second chart $A[x/y]$ the same computation with the roles of $x$ and $y$ interchanged writes $f=y^mh$ with $h\bmod y=f_m(T',1)\ne0$, so the strict transform there is cut by $h$ and the total transform is $mE+C'$. On the overlap the two exceptional equations $x$ and $y=xT$ differ by the unit $T$, and the two strict-transform equations $g$ and $h$ differ by a unit: both cut the closure of the dense open part of $C$ away from $E$ inside the respective charts, so they agree after the transition identification. By [F6] the local data $(x^m g)$, $(y^mh)$ glue to the identity of Cartier divisors $\pi^*C=C'+mE$ on $S'$, the terms being the effective Cartier divisors $C'$ and $E$ of [F8], which is the identity $\pi^*C=C'+mE$ recorded in [F1]. [F1, F3, F6, F8, step 2.1]

4.1 The intersection of $C'$ with $E$ is computed in the first chart: $E\cap\operatorname{Spec}A[y/x]=V(x)\cong\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(p)[T]$ and $C'$ is cut by $g$, so $C'\cap E$ is cut by $g\bmod x=f_m(1,T)$, the degree-$m$ leading form of $f$ at $p$; this is a nonzero polynomial of degree $m$, hence cuts a $0$-cycle of degree $m$ over $\kappa(p)$, with point multiplicities and residue degrees, and its degree over $k$ is $m[\kappa(p):k]$ whenever this residue degree is finite. This proves the stated equality and the description of the intersection cycle. [F5, step 2.1] ∎
