## Statement refuted

**False claim:** for every closed point $p$ of a surface $S$ over a field $k$, the blowup of $p$ has regular total space and smooth exceptional divisor.

The curve $y^3-x^5=0$ in the affine plane supplies the counterexample: its blowup at the singular origin is not regular in one chart and its exceptional divisor is a nonreduced point of length three, hence not smooth.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$ of characteristic different from $2$ and $3$, the ring $A=k[x,y]/(y^3-x^5)$, $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$, its singular point the origin $p=V(x,y)$, and the blowup $\pi\colon X'\to X$ of $p$.

[A1] **Choice.** The Axiom of Choice is assumed as inherited from the blowup and associated-graded constructions used below.

[F1] [[thm-affine-blowup-standard-charts]]: The blowup of $\operatorname{Spec}A$ along $I=(x,y)$ has the two charts $\operatorname{Spec}A[I/x]$ and $\operatorname{Spec}A[I/y]$, glued by inverting the ratio.

[F2] [[lem-affine-blowup-algebra-properties]]: $A[I/a]$ is the affine blowup algebra with image of $a$ a nonzerodivisor and $I\,A[I/a]=a\,A[I/a]$; for a domain $A$ and $a\ne0$, $A[I/a]$ is a domain.

[F3] [[lem-tangent-cone-initial-ideal-presentation]]: For $I=(f)\subseteq k[t_1,\dots,t_n]$ with $f\ne0$, the associated graded ring of the local ring of $X=V(I)$ at the origin is $k[t_1,\dots,t_n]/(f_{\min})$, the quotient by the lowest nonzero homogeneous part of $f$.

[F4] [[thm-associated-graded-ring-of-a-regular-local-ring]]: If $(R,\mathfrak m,k)$ is regular local of dimension $d$, then $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m}R\cong k[X_1,\dots,X_d]$ is a polynomial ring.

[F5] [[thm-exceptional-divisor-normal-cone-proj]]: The exceptional subscheme of the blowup is canonically $\operatorname{Proj}_Z(\operatorname{gr}_{\mathcal I}\mathcal O_X)$, the projectivized normal cone.

[F6] [[cor-minimal-prime-over-a-nonzerodivisor-has-height-one]]: In a Noetherian commutative ring, a prime minimal over a principal ideal generated by a nonzerodivisor has height one.

[F7] [[cor-height-plus-quotient-dimension-affine-domain]]: In a finite-type $k$-domain, $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)+\dim(A/\mathfrak p)=\dim A$ for every prime.

[F8] [[def-smooth-morphism-schemes]]: A morphism smooth at a point has geometrically regular fibre there; a regular local ring is a domain, so a nonreduced fibre is not geometrically regular and the morphism is not smooth there.

[F9] [[cor-exceptional-divisor-smooth-center-normal-bundle]]: For a regular center on a surface the exceptional divisor is a projective line, in particular smooth over the residue field.

## Counterexample

1.1 The first chart is $A[I/x]=A[y/x]$: putting $s=y/x$, the relation $y^3-x^5=0$ becomes $x^3(s^3-x^2)=0$ in $A_x$, and since $A[I/x]$ is the $A$-subalgebra of $A_x$ generated by $s$ (by [F2] applied to $a=x$), it is the domain $k[x,s]/(s^3-x^2)$; its local ring at the origin $(x,s)=(0,0)$ is $R=k[x,s]_{(x,s)}/(s^3-x^2)$. The second chart is computed with $t=x/y$ and $u=yt^3$: from $y^3=x^5$ one gets $t^5y^2=1$, hence $u^2=y^2t^6=t$ and $y=u/t^3=u^{-5}$, so $A[I/y]=k[t,y]=k[u,u^{-1}]$, a localization of the polynomial ring $k[u]$, all of whose local rings are fields or discrete valuation rings and hence regular. [F1, F2, given]

2.1 The local ring $R$ is one-dimensional: $(s^3-x^2)$ is a principal ideal generated by the nonzerodivisor $s^3-x^2$ in the two-dimensional local ring $k[x,s]_{(x,s)}$, so its minimal primes have height one by [F6], and $\dim R=1$ since $k[x,s]_{(x,s)}$ has dimension two; alternatively [F7]. Its associated graded ring is $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m}R\cong k[X,S]/(X^2)$ by [F3], since the lowest homogeneous part of $s^3-x^2$ is $x^2$; this graded ring is not a domain, because $X\ne0$ while $X^2=0$. Were $R$ regular local, [F4] would make its associated graded ring a polynomial ring, in particular a domain; hence $R$ is not regular, and the total space $X'$ is not regular. [F3, F4, F6, F7, step 1.1]

3.1 The exceptional divisor is $\operatorname{Proj}_{\kappa(p)}(\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m}\mathcal O_{X,p})$ by [F5], where $\mathfrak m=(x,y)\subseteq A$; by [F3] its associated graded ring is $k[X,Y]/(Y^3)$, the lowest homogeneous part of $y^3-x^5$ being $y^3$. Hence $E=\operatorname{Proj}k[X,Y]/(Y^3)$: a single closed point, corresponding to the prime $(Y)$ of the graded ring $k[X,Y]/(Y^3)$, whose local ring has length three and nonzero nilpotents. Thus $E$ is a nonreduced subscheme of $\mathbb P^1_k$ and is not smooth over $k$, by [F8]. [F3, F5, F8, step 2.1]

4.1 This contrasts with the regular-center case [F9], where the exceptional divisor of a point blowup on a surface is a projective line and hence smooth; the hypothesis that the center is regular is therefore essential, and blowing up a nonregular center need neither make the total space regular nor produce a smooth or reduced exceptional divisor. [F9, step 2.1, step 3.1] ∎
