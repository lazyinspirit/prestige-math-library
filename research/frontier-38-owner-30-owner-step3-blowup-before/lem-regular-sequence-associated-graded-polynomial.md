## Statement

Let $R$ be a commutative ring and let $f_1,\dots,f_c$ be an $R$-regular
sequence ([[def-regular-sequence-on-a-module]]), with
$J=(f_1,\dots,f_c)$ ([[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]) and associated
graded ring $\operatorname{gr}_JR=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}J^n/J^{n+1}$
([[def-associated-graded-ring-and-module]]). The canonical graded
homomorphism

$$(R/J)[X_1,\dots,X_c]\longrightarrow\operatorname{gr}_JR,\qquad X_i\longmapsto f_i\bmod J^2,$$

is an isomorphism. In particular $J/J^2$ is free over $R/J$ on the classes of
$f_1,\dots,f_c$, and
$\operatorname{Sym}_{R/J}(J/J^2)=\operatorname{gr}_JR$. No Noetherian or
domain hypothesis is required.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A commutative ring $R$, an $R$-regular sequence $f_1,\dots,f_c\in R$ ([[def-regular-sequence-on-a-module]]) and the ideal $J=(f_1,\dots,f_c)$ ([[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]) with its associated graded ring $\operatorname{gr}_JR$ ([[def-associated-graded-ring-and-module]]).

[F1] [[def-regular-sequence-on-a-module]]: A finite ordered sequence $f_1,\dots,f_c$ in $R$ is $R$-regular when $R/(f_1,\dots,f_{i-1})\neq0$ and multiplication by $f_i$ is injective on $R/(f_1,\dots,f_{i-1})$ for every $i$, and $R/(f_1,\dots,f_c)\neq0$. In particular the truncated sequence $f_1,\dots,f_{c-1}$ is likewise $R$-regular, and $f_c$ is a nonzerodivisor on $R/J'$ for $J'=(f_1,\dots,f_{c-1})$.

[F2] [[def-associated-graded-ring-and-module]]: For an ideal $J\subseteq R$, the associated graded ring is $\operatorname{gr}_JR=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}J^n/J^{n+1}$ with multiplication $(a+J^{m+1})(b+J^{n+1})=ab+J^{m+n+1}$; in particular it is commutative and generated in degree one.

[F3] [[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]: $J=(f_1,\dots,f_c)$ is the ideal generated by the $f_i$: it consists of the finite sums $\sum_ic_if_i$ with $c_i\in R$, and each $f_i\in J$.


## Proof

1.1 Every element of $J$ is an $R$-linear combination of the $f_i$, so every element of $J^n$ is a sum of $n$-fold products of such combinations, hence is an $R$-linear combination of the monomials $f^{I}=f_1^{i_1}\cdots f_c^{i_c}$ with $|I|=i_1+\cdots+i_c=n$; therefore those monomials generate $J^n$ as an $R$-module and their images generate $J^n/J^{n+1}$ over $R/J$, so the canonical map $\varphi\colon(R/J)[X_1,\dots,X_c]\to\operatorname{gr}_JR$ is surjective in every degree. Consequently $\varphi$ is an isomorphism if and only if for every $n\ge0$ and all $a_I\in R$ the relation $\sum_{|I|=n}a_If^{I}=0$ forces all $a_I\in J$: a general element of the kernel of $\varphi_n$ has $\sum a_If^{I}\in J^{n+1}$, and every element of $J^{n+1}$ is a sum $\sum_{|I|=n}b_If^{I}$ with $b_I\in J$ by the monomial generation just proved, so subtracting it makes the relation vanish without changing the classes of the coefficients modulo $J$. [F2, F3]

2.1 Let $J'=(f_1,\dots,f_{c-1})$ and fix $n$. Grouping a homogeneous relation $\sum_{|I|=n}a_If^{I}=0$ according to the exponent of $f_c$ writes it as $\sum_{e=0}^{n}H_ef_c^e=0$ with $H_e=\sum_{|I'|=n-e}a_{I',e}f^{I'}\in(J')^{n-e}$, the primed multi-indices involving only $f_1,\dots,f_{c-1}$. We prove by induction on $l\in\{0,\dots,n\}$: if $\sum_{e=0}^{l}H_ef_c^e=0$ for elements $H_e\in(J')^{n-e}$, then all coefficients of $H_0,\dots,H_l$ lie in $J$. Since $(J')^{n-e}\subseteq(J')^{n-l+1}$ for $e\le l-1$, reducing the relation modulo $(J')^{n-l+1}$ gives $f_c^lH_l\in(J')^{n-l+1}$. [F3, step 1.1]

3.1 The truncated sequence $f_1,\dots,f_{c-1}$ is again $R$-regular, so the inductive hypothesis on $c$ gives, in the coefficient form recorded in step 1.1 for the ideal $J'$, that a degree-$(n-l)$ monomial expression $\sum_{|I'|=n-l}c_{I'}f^{I'}$ lying in $(J')^{n-l+1}$ has all $c_{I'}\in J'$. Applying this to the expression $\sum_{|I'|=n-l}f_c^la_{I',l}f^{I'}$, which equals $f_c^lH_l\in(J')^{n-l+1}$ by step 2.1, gives $f_c^la_{I',l}\in J'$ for every $I'$. Since $f_c$ is a nonzerodivisor on $R/J'$ by regularity, this forces $a_{I',l}\in J'$, and $J'\subseteq J$. [F1, step 2.1]

4.1 Since $a_{I',l}\in J'$ the element $\sum_{|I'|=n-l}a_{I',l}f^{I'}$ lies in $(J')^{n-l+1}$ and so can be written as a degree-$(n-l+1)$ monomial expression $\sum_{|I''|=n-l+1}b_{I'',l-1}f^{I''}$. Absorbing $f_c$ times this expression into the coefficient of $f_c^{l-1}$ gives a relation $\sum_{e=0}^{l-1}\tilde H_ef_c^e=0$ whose coefficients are $\tilde a_{I'',l-1}=a_{I'',l-1}+f_cb_{I'',l-1}$ in degree $n-l+1$ and $\tilde a_{I',e}=a_{I',e}$ for $e\le l-2$, because $\bigl(\sum_{I''}b_{I'',l-1}f^{I''}\bigr)f_c^l$ equals the term $H_lf_c^l$ that is being moved. By the induction hypothesis on $l$ all coefficients of $\tilde H_0,\dots,\tilde H_{l-1}$ lie in $J$; hence the coefficients $a_{I',e}$ for $e\le l-2$ lie in $J$ directly, and $a_{I'',l-1}=\tilde a_{I'',l-1}-f_cb_{I'',l-1}$ lies in $J$ as well, since $f_cb_{I'',l-1}\in J$. Together with $a_{I',l}\in J'$ this completes the induction on $l$. [F1, F3, step 3.1]

5.1 Taking $l=n$ in step 2.1, every coefficient $a_I$ of the original homogeneous relation lies in $J$; by step 1.1 the map $\varphi$ is therefore injective, hence an isomorphism of graded $R/J$-algebras. In degree one it identifies $(R/J)^c$ with $J/J^2$, so $J/J^2$ is free over $R/J$ on the classes of $f_1,\dots,f_c$; and since $\varphi$ maps the polynomial algebra onto the graded algebra generated by $J/J^2$, it identifies $\operatorname{Sym}_{R/J}(J/J^2)$ with $\operatorname{gr}_JR$. The only properties of the sequence used were the truncation regularity, the nonzerodivisor condition on $f_c$ modulo $J'$, and the monomial generation of the powers of $J$; no Noetherian or domain hypothesis entered. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1] ∎

## Remarks

The double induction is on the length $c$ of the sequence (step 3.1) and,
inside a fixed length, on the highest power $l$ of $f_c$ occurring in the
relation (step 4.1). The lemma is the algebraic input to the computation of
the normal cone of a regular immersion and to the identification of the
associated graded algebras $\operatorname{gr}_{J}R$ used in the deformation
$\delta_k(C')=\delta_k(C)-r\binom m2$ of the resolution theorem.
