## Statement refuted

Assume Dependent Choice ([[def-dependent-choice]]). Let $X=\mathbb C^2$ over the complex field ([[def-complex-numbers-and-arithmetic]]) have the maximum product norm
$$\|(z_1,z_2)\|_\infty=\max(|z_1|,|z_2|)$$
([[def-product-norms-on-finitely-many-normed-spaces]], [[rem-real-and-complex-normed-space-convention]]); this finite-dimensional normed space is Banach ([[cor-finite-dimensional-normed-spaces-are-banach]]). Let $A\in\mathcal B(X)$ ([[def-bounded-linear-operator]]) be the matrix
$$A=\begin{pmatrix}-1&4\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad D(A)=X.$$
For $M=25/16$ and $\omega=0$, every $\lambda>0$ lies in $\rho(A)$ and the first resolvent estimate
$$\|R(\lambda,A)\|_\infty\le \frac{M}{\lambda}$$
holds ([[def-resolvent-of-a-closed-operator]], [[def-operator-norm]]). But the $n=2$ Hille--Yosida estimate required by [[thm-hille-yosida-generation-theorem]] fails at $\lambda=3$:
$$\|R(3,A)^2\|_\infty=\frac{3}{16}>\frac{25}{144}=\frac{M}{3^2}.$$
The bounded-operator exponential semigroup generated by $A$ ([[lem-exponential-series-of-a-bounded-operator]]) is
$$e^{tA}=e^{-t}\begin{pmatrix}1&4t\\0&1\end{pmatrix},$$
and
$$\|e^{A/2}\|_\infty=3e^{-1/2}>\frac{25}{16}.$$
Thus a first-power resolvent estimate alone does not ensure the semigroup bound with the same $M>1$ and $\omega=0$.

**Refuted claim.** For every $M\ge1$, a closed densely defined operator whose positive real resolvent set contains $(0,\infty)$ and satisfies only $\|R(\lambda,A)\|\le M/\lambda$ for all $\lambda>0$ necessarily generates a strongly continuous semigroup bounded by $M$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Dependent Choice; $X=\mathbb C^2$ with the maximum product norm, $A=\begin{pmatrix}-1&4\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}$ on $D(A)=X$, and $M=25/16$, $\omega=0$.

[F1] The maximum product norm on $\mathbb C^2$ is $\|(z_1,z_2)\|_\infty=\max(|z_1|,|z_2|)$, with complex scalar homogeneity read using the complex modulus ([[def-product-norms-on-finitely-many-normed-spaces]], [[rem-real-and-complex-normed-space-convention]]). The complex scalar field is $\mathbb C$ ([[def-complex-numbers-and-arithmetic]]).

[F2] Every finite-dimensional normed space over $\mathbb C$ is Banach ([[cor-finite-dimensional-normed-spaces-are-banach]]).

[F3] The operator norm is the least constant $C$ such that $\|Bz\|_\infty\le C\|z\|_\infty$ for every $z\in X$ ([[def-operator-norm]]). For a matrix with nonnegative entries, the upper bound by its maximum row sum is attained on $z=(1,1)$ whenever the largest row sum is positive.

[F4] For $\lambda\in\rho(A)$, the resolvent is $R(\lambda,A)=(\lambda I-A)^{-1}$ ([[def-resolvent-of-a-closed-operator]]).

[F5] If a closed densely defined operator generates a semigroup with $\|T(t)\|\le Me^{\omega t}$, then every $\lambda>\omega$ satisfies $\|R(\lambda,A)^n\|\le M(\lambda-\omega)^{-n}$ for every integer $n\ge1$ ([[thm-hille-yosida-generation-theorem]]).

[F6] A bounded operator $A$ on a Banach space generates the strongly continuous exponential semigroup $e^{tA}$ ([[lem-exponential-series-of-a-bounded-operator]]).

[F7] The real exponential constant satisfies $e<3$ ([[cor-two-less-than-e-less-than-three]]).

[F8] Matrix multiplication is given by the finite coordinate sums of [[def-matrix-product-and-identity-matrix]].



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct matrix calculations.

1.1 Let $N=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$. Then $N^2=0$ and $A=-I+4N$ by matrix multiplication ([[def-matrix-product-and-identity-matrix]]). The space $X$ is Banach by [F1, F2]. The maximum-row-sum estimate gives $\|Az\|_\infty\le5\|z\|_\infty$, so $A$ is bounded; its domain is all of $X$, hence dense, and continuity shows its graph is closed. [F1, F2, F3, given]

1.2 Since $A=-I+4N$, the two summands commute and $N^2=0$. The exponential series gives $$e^{tA}=e^{-t}e^{4tN}=e^{-t}(I+4tN)=e^{-t}\begin{pmatrix}1&4t\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.$$ For $t\ge0$, its maximum row sum is $(1+4t)e^{-t}$. At $t=1/2$ this is $3e^{-1/2}=3/\sqrt e$. By [F7], $e<3$, hence $3/\sqrt e>3/\sqrt3=\sqrt3$; moreover $\sqrt3>25/16$ because $3>(25/16)^2$. Thus $\|e^{A/2}\|_\infty>25/16=M$. [F3, F6, F7, F8, algebra]

2.1 For every $\lambda>0$, $$\lambda I-A=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda+1&-4\\0&\lambda+1\end{pmatrix},\qquad R(\lambda,A)=\begin{pmatrix}(\lambda+1)^{-1}&4(\lambda+1)^{-2}\\0&(\lambda+1)^{-1}\end{pmatrix}$$ Their product is $I$, so every such $\lambda$ is in $\rho(A)$. For a vector $z$ with $\|z\|_\infty\le1$, the first row of $Rz$ has modulus at most $(\lambda+1)^{-1}+4(\lambda+1)^{-2}$ and the second at most $(\lambda+1)^{-1}$; the vector $(1,1)$ attains the first row sum. Thus $$\|R(\lambda,A)\|_\infty=\frac1{\lambda+1}+\frac4{(\lambda+1)^2}.$$ Writing $x=\lambda/(\lambda+1)\in(0,1)$ gives $$\lambda\|R(\lambda,A)\|_\infty=x+4x(1-x)=5x-4x^2=\frac{25}{16}-4\left(x-\frac58\right)^2\le\frac{25}{16}.$$ This proves the first-power estimate for every $\lambda>0$. [F3, F4, F8, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 At $\lambda=3$, $$R(3,A)=\begin{pmatrix}1/4&1/4\\0&1/4\end{pmatrix},\qquad R(3,A)^2=\begin{pmatrix}1/16&1/8\\0&1/16\end{pmatrix}.$$ The maximum row sum of the square is $3/16$, attained on $(1,1)$, so $$\|R(3,A)^2\|_\infty=\frac3{16}=\frac{27}{144}>\frac{25}{144}=\frac{M}{3^2}.$$ Therefore the required second-power estimate fails. [F3, F5, F8, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The direct second-power failure in step 3.1 also rules out a semigroup with generator $A$ and the prescribed bound by [F5]. The example therefore shows that the first-power resolvent estimate alone is insufficient for a general bound with $M>1$. [F5, step 3.1, step 1.2] ∎
