## Statement

Consider a finite recursively closed distance-three stabilizer-code simulation with the following **checked local gadget conditions**. These are hypotheses about elementary gadgets, rather than a hypothesis of exRec correctness or of a witness reduction. Use a complete stabilizer Pauli recovery, with one chosen Pauli correction for every syndrome, including uncorrectable syndromes. Let $D$ be this recovery followed by ideal decoding; require the zero-fault EC $E_0$ to obey $D E_0=D$ on every input. Let $P_s$, $s=0,1$, project onto the span of weight-at-most-$s$ Pauli errors applied to codewords. Equalities below hold as channel equalities on the indicated subspaces, with arbitrary finite reference systems. They must also hold when a faulty location interacts with an auxiliary memory; the memory is untouched at nonfaulty locations.

For multiple blocks, the indicated error sector is the joint linear span of tensor Pauli errors with the indicated weight in each block, tensored with an unchanged external reference. Arbitrary coherent errors and correlations between data blocks are allowed; no product noise or product state is required.

An EC with $f\le1$ faults maps every input into $P_f$. For $s+f\le1$, it preserves decoded recovery on $P_s$: $D E_f P_s=D P_s$, where a projection in a channel identity denotes restriction to its range. A gate gadget $G_f$, with ideal logical unitary $g$, on inputs in $\bigotimes_i P_{s_i}$, where $f+\sum_i s_i\le1$, has each output in $P_{f+\sum_i s_i}$ and satisfies $D^{\otimes\mathrm{out}}G_f=gD^{\otimes\mathrm{in}}$ on those inputs. A preparation with $f\le1$ faults has output in $P_f$ and decoded output its prescribed ideal state, independent of a reference or memory. A destructive measurement on $P_s$ with $s+f\le1$ has precisely the ideal logical instrument after $D$, including its retained classical outcome. Classical processing of the final outcomes is reliable. Idle gadgets satisfy the gate conditions. All scheduled operations, ancilla preparations, syndrome processing and measurements belong to these finite gadgets. Unknown unencoded inputs are not covered: initial inputs are encoded or are the prescribed preparations.

A rectangle is a gate gadget followed by output ECs; its extended rectangle (exRec) also includes its input ECs. Preparations have no input EC; destructive measurements have no output EC. Shared ECs are the only overlaps at a fixed scale. Use the following truncation convention recursively: when a later bad exRec claims an input EC, erase that shared EC from the earlier exRec, **including every elementary location belonging to the recursively expanded later exRec**. Here the expanded support is defined by the recursive input-collar construction in the proof: start with the ordinary recursively replaced gadget cores, and adjoin the leading lower-scale ECs of opening child roots, descending one scale at a time. Thus an erased EC also erases those opening lower-level ECs. Complete erased output ECs by ideal recovery ECs, starting at the lowest scale. Assume $b\ge2$ uniformly bounds the number of child-location roots in any complete one-scale exRec template, including its leading ECs; erased roots retain their template labels. Every recursive path has the declared full depth.

Scan roots in reverse circuit order. At scale zero a retained location is bad exactly when faulty. At scale $r\ge1$, perform the scale-$(r-1)$ scan inside the retained exRec, completing erased ECs ideally. Call the retained exRec bad if at least two bad child roots survive this scan, and good otherwise. A bad root claims its entire retained exRec; an earlier root loses any locations so claimed. A good root removes only its rectangle during decoding, leaving its leading ECs for the earlier scan. Use any fixed reverse topological order when roots are incomparable.

Then every good complete or truncated exRec is correct: its retained rectangle followed by decoded recovery equals decoded recovery at its input followed by the ideal logical operation, on the state produced by its retained leading ECs. The scan reduces the circuit exactly to its ideal circuit with faults only at selected bad roots. Each selected bad level-$r$ root has a full binary witness tree of depth $r$, with distinct faulty elementary leaves; trees of jointly selected bad roots have pairwise disjoint leaves. A fixed root has at most $A^{2^r-1}$ possible labelled witness trees, where $A=\binom b2$. Consequently, for any specified $k$ roots which are required to be jointly selected bad at level $r$, the independent elementary fault model gives
$$
\mathbb P(\text{all specified roots are selected bad})\le \bigl(A^{2^r-1}\eta^{2^r}\bigr)^k.
$$
This bound includes the dependence of truncation on the fault pattern; it does not assert independence of the selected bad-root events.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite recursive schedule, checked local gadget identities, decoder, truncation rule, full-depth hierarchy, bound $b$, and reverse scan in the statement. Prove correctness, disjoint witnesses, and the probability bound.

[F1] [[def-qc-noise-channel-model]]: For any fixed set of $h$ distinct elementary locations, the probability they are all faulty is $\eta^h$; other locations are unrestricted.

[F2] [[thm-finite-union-bound]] bounds the probability of a finite union by the sum of the probabilities.

[A1] The elementary EC, gate, preparation and measurement identities in the statement hold for every allowed fault map, with references and auxiliary memory retained.

[A2] The recursive templates, full-depth replacement, shared-EC overlap rule and bound $b$ are those in the statement. Truncation erases the full elementary support of a claimed later exRec, at every nested scale.

## Authoring status

The local exRec calculation and fixed disjoint-tree counting below do not complete the recursive overlap proof. In candidate step 1.4, factoring a final recursive decoder does not put its $D_j$ factor immediately after an internal amputated EC: retained gates may intervene. A valid proof needs an already established decoder-sweep invariant placing that cut decoder there before invoking $D_j\mathcal E_j=D_j$. The current induction does not supply this invariant without assuming the very higher-level exRec correctness being proved. This defect remains unresolved after two review/repair rounds. No local proof or logical-supplier use of this lemma is claimed.

## Candidate argument (incomplete)

1.1 First consider a complete level-one gate exRec containing at most one fault. Let $s_i$ count the faults in its input ECs, $f$ those in the gate, and $t_j$ those in its output ECs. Put $s=\sum_i s_i$. The total $s+f+\sum_jt_j$ is at most one. The first EC condition puts input block $i$ in $P_{s_i}$, even for arbitrary incoming states. The gate condition puts every output block in $P_{s+f}$ and identifies its decoded logical action with $g$ on the decoded EC outputs. Since $s+f+t_j\le1$, the decoded-recovery identity for output EC $j$ removes that EC without altering this logical action. Hence $D^{\otimes\mathrm{out}}(\bigotimes_jE_{t_j})G_f(\bigotimes_iE_{s_i})=gD^{\otimes\mathrm{in}}(\bigotimes_iE_{s_i})$. The same reasoning uses the stated preparation identity when there is no input and the measurement identity when there is no quantum output. All these are channel or instrument identities, so apply to entangled inputs and references. [given, A1, algebra]

1.2 Specify the elementary supports without treating a large gadget as an indivisible interval. A *core* is its ordinary recursive replacement circuit: each primitive root is replaced by its rectangle, without adjoining that root's leading ECs a second time. Give each core its schedule address (the enclosing root addresses followed by the child-slot address); shared ECs have one physical address, regardless of which exRec names them. The expanded support of an exRec is obtained from its gate and input/output EC cores as follows. At the first child layer, adjoin the leading EC core of each opening child root whose input wire enters those cores; repeat on the opening roots of the newly adjoined cores, at successively smaller scales, stopping at elementary locations. This is a finite descending-scale input collar. Whenever a leading $j$-EC is adjoined across a core boundary, its core is exactly the trailing $j$-EC of the preceding $j$-rectangle on that wire: ordinary rectangles consist of a gate followed by ECs, and adjoining leading ECs never adjoins a preceding gate. At preparation boundaries there is no preceding EC. This proves, by descending induction on the collar scale, that its extension outside the starting cores consists solely of complete EC cores, possibly with further smaller input collars; it never removes a proper gate core. Shared physical addresses are included once. [given, A2, construct, induction]

1.3 Now subtract a later claimed expanded exRec from its earlier neighbor. At the outer scale this removes the shared output EC cores. At the next scale it also removes exactly the leading EC cores adjoined to opening roots of those removed cores; those are output EC cores of earlier child rectangles by step 1.2. Continue at each smaller scale. Assign a deleted location to the largest deleted core containing it; collar cores external to that core remain separate pieces. This makes the deleted pieces disjoint. Consequently every newly exposed face is an output face of an EC at its own scale, although it can lie inside an earlier larger gate gadget. Multiple later claims take the union of these collars; an EC already deleted is omitted, and its smaller external collar is still included. This argument applies to that union as well. Thus the retained earlier circuit has no arbitrary holes: its missing pieces are output ECs at a finite set of nested decoder cuts. [A2, step 1.2, construct, induction]

1.4 Here is the algebra for restoring those cuts. Write $D_0=\operatorname{id}$ and $D_j=D\circ D_{j-1}^{\otimes m}$ on a level-$j$ block, where $m$ is the fixed block size. A cut in this decoder tree is an antichain of nodes meeting every path under consideration. Repeated substitution of this displayed definition writes $D_k$ as the decoders of those nodes followed by the remaining upper decoder tree. If $\mathcal E_j$ is an ideal recovery EC for $D_j$, then $D_j\mathcal E_j=D_j$ on all inputs; for $j=1$ this is the stipulated identity $D E_0=D$, and at larger scales it is the defining recovery identity for the concatenated decoder. The inserted $\mathcal E_j$ is an ideal recovery operation, not an assertion that an arbitrary incomplete physical recovery gadget is fault-free. Starting with the smallest exposed pieces in step 1.3, the decoder immediately beyond each such piece is its $D_j$ factor. For the retained prefix $T$ and remaining upper decoder $D_{\mathrm{up}}$, the exact identity is $D_{\mathrm{up}}(D_j\otimes D_{\mathrm{rest}})T=D_{\mathrm{up}}(D_j\otimes D_{\mathrm{rest}})(\mathcal E_j\otimes\operatorname{id})T$. Restore that piece using this identity. The smaller cuts under a restored larger EC are now internal to its completed core, so replace them by the single parent cut and continue upwards. This induction restores all missing cores of step 1.3, including the lower-scale pieces inside earlier gate gadgets. On scale reduction each inserted ideal recovery is the fault-free recovery at that scale and supplies no bad child or faulty elementary witness. At level one this completes the proof of truncated exRec correctness from step 1.1. At higher levels the same restoration is used within the simultaneous induction below; the algebra depends only on recursive decoder factorization, not on an assumption of exRec correctness. [given, A1, A2, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, construct, induction]

2.1 To move past bad gadgets without losing syndrome information, choose for each stabilizer block an invertible syndrome-retaining decoder $U:H_P\longrightarrow H_L\otimes H_S$. Such a decoder exists directly: the stabilizer joint eigenspaces have the same dimension as the code, choose one Pauli representative for each syndrome, and identify that eigenspace with the code using the representative. For correctable syndromes choose the representative used in the declared recovery; for other syndromes choose its declared Pauli recovery. This requires the declared ideal recovery to be this complete stabilizer recovery. Then $D=\operatorname{Tr}_S\operatorname{Ad}_U$. A bad retained gadget $B$ becomes exactly the CPTP operation $\operatorname{Ad}_{U_{\rm out}}B\operatorname{Ad}_{U_{\rm in}^{-1}}$ on logical registers and syndrome registers. There is no claim that it is a channel on logical registers alone. Its syndrome registers are an auxiliary memory, so the elementary identities [A1] remain applicable to subsequent simulations. [given, A1, construct]

2.2 Good unitary gadgets transmit the logical system independently of this memory. Here is the needed channel fact rather than an assumption about discarded syndromes. If a channel on $L\otimes S$ has logical marginal $g\operatorname{Tr}_S$ for every input and reference, conjugate by $g^{-1}$ and take its Choi matrix. Its marginal on output-$L$ and input-$L$ is supported on the one-dimensional maximally entangled subspace. Positivity implies that the whole Choi matrix is supported on that subspace tensor the remaining registers: a vector in the kernel of a positive marginal has zero quadratic form, hence is annihilated by the positive matrix. Thus the Choi matrix factors as the identity-channel Choi matrix tensor a positive matrix on the syndrome registers; trace preservation makes the latter a channel. Conjugating back gives $\operatorname{Ad}_g\otimes T_S$. At level one apply this argument on the entire filter sector fixed by the leading-EC fault counts, rather than just the image of one input state: under the stabilizer decoder this sector is the full logical space tensored with the span of its allowed syndrome basis vectors. The gate and trailing-EC identities in [A1] hold throughout that sector. This gives the required factorization on every reachable input, including its correlations with external registers. Preparations are independent of memory by hypothesis. For a final destructive measurement its full instrument identity already identifies the outcome law; subsequent classical processing is reliable. Therefore good replacements preserve the logical circuit even while all syndromes are retained. [given, A1, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

3.1 Induct on scale, simultaneously for complete and truncated exRecs. Scale one is steps 1.1–1.4. Suppose the assertion holds at scale $r$. Split a level-$(r+1)$ decoder into level-$r$ subblock decoders followed by a level-one decoder, and sweep the subblock decoders backwards through the retained exRec. A good child is replaced by its ideal operation by the induction hypothesis. A bad child is replaced by the exact operation of step 2.1, claiming its retained leading ECs as well as its rectangle. This removes the shared ECs from every earlier neighbor, precisely as specified in [A2]. Complete each newly truncated earlier child ideally using steps 1.2–1.4, and apply the same induction hypothesis to it. Incomparables act on different circuit wires at this boundary, so a fixed reverse topological order suffices. Because the schedule is finite, this removes all child roots. The resulting one-scale exRec has a fault exactly at each selected bad child root; all other child operations are ideal on logical registers, with harmless syndrome processing by step 2.2. [A1, A2, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 1.4, step 2.1, step 2.2, induction, construct]

3.2 If the parent was good, that one-scale exRec has at most one fault by the definition of the scan. Step 1.1 therefore moves the final level-one decoder across its rectangle. To obtain the decoder on the original parent-rectangle input, reverse the subblock sweep through the leading parent ECs, using exactly the channel identities used on the forward sweep, and reunite the subblock decoders with the level-one decoder. This proves parent exRec correctness on the image of its leading ECs. If output ECs were truncated, restore them from lowest scale upwards before this argument and remove them afterwards by steps 1.2–1.4. If the parent was bad, step 2.1 instead supplies its exact simulated fault. This proves the simultaneous induction and the stated circuit reduction; prescribed preparations and final measurements close its input and output boundaries. In particular, when there are no selected bad roots the output instrument is exactly ideal. [given, A1, A2, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 1.4, step 3.1, induction]

4.1 A selected bad level-$r$ root, $r\ge1$, contains at least two selected bad child roots in its retained scan. Choose the first two in the fixed order, and repeat inside each of them down to scale zero. The result is a full binary tree of depth $r$. A later selected bad child claimed every location of its retained exRec before the earlier child was processed. Hence the two child supports are disjoint. This remains true at every nested boundary because [A2] erases all overlapping elementary locations, not just the nominal outer EC. Induction down the tree gives $2^r$ distinct faulty elementary leaves. The same argument gives disjoint leaves for trees from jointly selected roots. Ideal completions create no faulty leaves. [given, A2, step 3.1, construct, induction]

4.2 Count the trees before considering probabilities. At each internal vertex its two child roots have labels in its fixed complete exRec template, with at most $b$ possibilities; hence their unordered pair has at most $A=\binom b2$ possibilities. Order that pair by the fixed template order. A full depth-$r$ binary tree has $2^r-1$ internal vertices, so it has at most $A^{2^r-1}$ labelings. A label sequence fixes every elementary leaf's physical location in the original schedule, regardless of which ECs the scan erased: truncation deletes locations and never changes a root's template address. Thus no extra count of truncation patterns is needed. Some labelings cannot survive a scan or repeat a leaf; discard them. Every actual witness is among the remaining predetermined labelings, by step 4.1. [given, A2, step 4.1, algebra]

5.1 For $k$ specified selected bad roots, choose one of these labelled witness trees for each root. There are at most $A^{k(2^r-1)}$ such tuples. Keep only tuples with $k2^r$ distinct elementary leaves. For a fixed retained tuple the probability all its leaves fault is $\eta^{k2^r}$ by [F1]; no restriction on other locations, erasure decisions, or additional faults is imposed. Step 4.1 makes the event of interest a subset of the union of these fixed leaf-fault events. Applying [F2] proves $\mathbb P(\text{all specified roots selected bad})\le A^{k(2^r-1)}\eta^{k2^r}$. This proof uses independence only for fixed elementary locations and therefore remains valid for fault-dependent truncations. [F1, F2, step 4.1, step 4.2, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The candidate recursive reduction above is not an established proof. The local gadget conditions are the $t=1$ specialization of the filter conditions in Section 9 of Aliferis–Gottesman–Preskill; the syndrome-retaining decoder and nested amputations are the devices in Sections 5.1–5.2. Checking these conditions for a concrete universal family is a separate obligation. Merely bounding spread, or checking the action on codewords without arbitrary input EC behavior, does not discharge it.
