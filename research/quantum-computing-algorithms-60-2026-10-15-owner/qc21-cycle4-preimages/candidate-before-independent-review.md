## Statement

Consider a finite recursively closed distance-three stabilizer-code simulation with the following **checked local gadget conditions**. These are hypotheses about elementary gadgets, rather than a hypothesis of exRec correctness or of a witness reduction. Use a complete stabilizer Pauli recovery, with one chosen Pauli correction for every syndrome, including uncorrectable syndromes. Let $D$ be this recovery followed by ideal decoding; require the zero-fault EC $E_0$ to obey $D E_0=D$ on every input. Let $P_s$, $s=0,1$, project onto the span of weight-at-most-$s$ Pauli errors applied to codewords. Equalities below hold as channel equalities on the indicated subspaces, with arbitrary finite reference systems. They must also hold when a faulty location interacts with an auxiliary memory; the memory is untouched at nonfaulty locations.

For multiple blocks, the indicated error sector is the joint linear span of tensor Pauli errors with the indicated weight in each block, tensored with an unchanged external reference. Arbitrary coherent errors and correlations between data blocks are allowed; no product noise or product state is required.

An EC with $f\le1$ faults maps every input into $P_f$. For $s+f\le1$, it preserves decoded recovery on $P_s$: $D E_f P_s=D P_s$, where a projection in a channel identity denotes restriction to its range. A gate gadget $G_f$, with ideal logical unitary $g$, on inputs in $\bigotimes_i P_{s_i}$, where $f+\sum_i s_i\le1$, has each output in $P_{f+\sum_i s_i}$ and satisfies $D^{\otimes\mathrm{out}}G_f=gD^{\otimes\mathrm{in}}$ on those inputs. A preparation with $f\le1$ faults has output in $P_f$ and decoded output its prescribed ideal state, independent of a reference or memory. A destructive measurement on $P_s$ with $s+f\le1$ has precisely the ideal logical instrument after $D$, including its retained classical outcome. Classical processing of the final outcomes is reliable. Idle gadgets satisfy the gate conditions. All scheduled operations, ancilla preparations, syndrome processing and measurements belong to these finite gadgets. Unknown unencoded inputs are not covered: initial inputs are encoded or are the prescribed preparations.

A rectangle is a gate gadget followed by output ECs; its extended rectangle (exRec) also includes its input ECs. Preparations have no input EC; destructive measurements have no output EC. Shared ECs are the only overlaps at a fixed scale. Use the following truncation convention recursively: when a later bad exRec claims an input EC, erase that shared EC from the earlier exRec, **including every elementary location belonging to the recursively expanded later exRec**. Here the expanded support is defined by the recursive input-collar construction in the proof: start with the ordinary recursively replaced gadget cores, and adjoin the leading lower-scale ECs of opening child roots, descending one scale at a time. Thus an erased EC also erases those opening lower-level ECs. Complete erased output ECs by ideal recovery ECs, starting at the lowest scale. Assume $b\ge2$ uniformly bounds the number of child-location roots in any complete one-scale exRec template, including its leading ECs; erased roots retain their template labels. Every recursive path has the declared full depth.

Scan roots in reverse circuit order. At scale zero a retained location is bad exactly when faulty. At scale $r\ge1$, perform the scale-$(r-1)$ scan inside the retained exRec, completing erased ECs ideally. Call the retained exRec bad if at least two bad child roots survive this scan, and good otherwise. A bad root claims its entire retained exRec; an earlier root loses any locations so claimed. A good root removes only its rectangle during decoding, leaving its leading ECs for the earlier scan. Use any fixed reverse topological order when roots are incomparable.

Then every good complete or truncated exRec is correct: its retained rectangle followed by decoded recovery equals decoded recovery at its input followed by the ideal logical operation, on the state produced by its retained leading ECs. The scan reduces the circuit exactly to its ideal circuit with faults only at selected bad roots. Each selected bad level-$r$ root has a full binary witness tree of depth $r$, with distinct faulty elementary leaves; trees of jointly selected bad roots have pairwise disjoint leaves. A fixed root has at most $A^{2^r-1}$ possible labelled witness trees, where $A=\binom b2$. Consequently, for any specified $k$ roots which are required to be jointly selected bad at level $r$, the independent elementary fault model gives
$$
\mathbb P(\text{all specified roots are selected bad})\le \bigl(A^{2^r-1}\eta^{2^r}\bigr)^k.
$$
This bound includes the dependence of truncation on the fault pattern; it does not assert independence of the selected bad-root events.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite recursive schedule, checked local gadget identities, decoder, truncation rule, full-depth hierarchy, bound $b$, and reverse scan in the statement. Prove correctness, disjoint witnesses, and the probability bound.

[F1] [[def-qc-noise-channel-model]]: For any fixed set of $h$ distinct elementary locations, the probability they are all faulty is $\eta^h$; other locations are unrestricted.

[F2] [[thm-finite-union-bound]] bounds the probability of a finite union by the sum of the probabilities.

[A1] The elementary EC, gate, preparation and measurement identities in the statement hold for every allowed fault map, with references and auxiliary memory retained.

[A2] The recursive templates, full-depth replacement, shared-EC overlap rule and bound $b$ are those in the statement. Truncation erases the full elementary support of a claimed later exRec, at every nested scale.

## Proof

1.1 Choose a complete stabilizer syndrome decoder $U:H_P\to H_L\otimes H_S$, using the stipulated Pauli recovery representative for each syndrome. The stabilizer eigenspaces are mutually orthogonal copies of the code, so this map is unitary. Write $\mathcal U=\operatorname{Ad}_U$ and $D=\operatorname{Tr}_S\mathcal U$. Recursively, $U_j$ first applies $U_{j-1}$ to each subblock and then $U$ to the resulting logical coordinates, retaining every syndrome register. Thus $H_{P,j}=H_L\otimes H_{S,j}$ after applying $U_j$. A weight-filter sector becomes $H_L\otimes S_0$ or $H_L\otimes S_1$, where $S_s$ is the span of syndrome basis vectors of errors of weight at most $s$. For a degenerate code, errors with the same syndrome give the same copy of the logical space, so this assertion remains true. All tensor products below include the external reference unchanged. [given, A1, construct]

1.2 At scale one let $s_i$ count faults in the leading ECs, $f$ those in the gate, and $t_a$ those in the output ECs which are present. If their total is at most one, the leading ECs put the input in $\bigotimes_i P_{s_i}$. The gate takes this entire sector into $P_{s+f}$ on each output, where $s=\sum_i s_i$, and has decoded logical action $g$. Every present output EC preserves that action because $s+f+t_a\le1$. If an output EC is absent, the decoded gate identity already gives precisely the same conclusion on that output; no EC is inserted. This proves, simultaneously for every subset $J$ of output ECs retained, $D_{\rm out}E_JG_f|_{\bigotimes_i P_{s_i}}=gD_{\rm in}|_{\bigotimes_i P_{s_i}}$. The corresponding preparation and destructive-measurement identities are exactly those in [A1]. This establishes the base assertion for complete and truncated rectangles together. [given, A1, step 1.1, algebra]

1.3 We will retain the syndromes in that identity. If a CPTP map $M$ on $H_L\otimes K$ has logical marginal $g\operatorname{Tr}_K$ for every input, its conjugation by $g^{-1}$ has identity logical marginal. Its Choi matrix has marginal supported on the one-dimensional maximally entangled logical subspace. Positivity forces the entire Choi matrix to have support in that subspace tensored with the remaining registers; hence it factors as the identity-channel Choi matrix times the Choi matrix of a CPTP map on $K$. Consequently $M=\operatorname{Ad}_g\otimes T$. Apply this to the entire filter sector in step 1.2, which by step 1.1 is the full logical space tensored with an allowed syndrome subspace. It gives an exact syndrome-retaining identity on that sector, including all input correlations. Extend $T$ to the remaining syndrome space by first projecting onto this sector and its orthogonal complement and using any fixed channel on the complement. Only the restriction is used on reachable states. For preparations the prescribed logical output is independent of memory by [A1]; for final destructive measurements retain the instrument's outcome. [given, A1, step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra]

2.1 Here are the actual circuit boundaries used below. An ordinary gadget core is its recursive replacement circuit, before adjoining leading ECs of its opening child roots. Give every child slot its schedule address and every shared EC one physical address. To expand an exRec, adjoin those opening leading EC cores, then their opening leading EC cores at the next lower scale, and continue downwards. An added leading $j$-EC is the output $j$-EC of the previous $j$-rectangle on precisely that wire; it does not include the previous gate. Thus after a later bad root claims its expanded support, its earlier neighbors lose only output ECs at the outer scale and at successively lower scales. Each affected lower rectangle still has the full input and output registers of its own gate. Its missing EC wires are connected directly from the last retained operation to the first operation owned by the later root. Register identity along that connection does not assert equality of their states after any recovery. This is a boundary in the actual retained directed circuit, not a factor of a remotely placed final decoder. Gates on other wires may lie between its endpoints in a linear schedule; none lies on that wire between these endpoints, by the definition of the preceding rectangle. [given, A2, construct, induction]

2.2 Apply step 2.1 recursively to every missing EC. At every scale the ordinary rectangles are disjoint cores. Assign a shared EC to its preceding rectangle unless the reverse scan has assigned it to the following selected bad root, in which case assign it there. Descend through the opening collars, making the same assignment for each smaller shared EC. Distinct actual elementary operations receive exactly one owner: a second mention of an already owned physical address is erased. Interfaces between owners are full register bundles of the relevant lower-scale gate or EC; when a larger interface is staggered, use its child interfaces until it becomes a full bundle. This recursion terminates at elementary wires. A reverse topological ordering of the owned directed subcircuits is the specified root order with the recursively prescribed collar order. Indeed the ownership only moves an output EC to the immediately following exRec on that wire; it never moves a gate past another gate or cuts a multi-register elementary operation. This proves both that the quotient network is causal and that its boundary addresses are the addresses in the given reverse scan. [A2, step 2.1, construct, induction]

2.3 There is an exact change of coordinates at each of these *live* boundaries. If an owned subcircuit $B$ has input and output block bundles with decoders $U_{\rm in}$ and $U_{\rm out}$, define $\widehat B=\mathcal U_{\rm out}B\mathcal U_{\rm in}^{-1}$. Then $\mathcal U_{\rm out}B=\widehat B\mathcal U_{\rm in}$ exactly. Insert $\mathcal U^{-1}\mathcal U$ on every internal bundle of the causal quotient network of step 2.2. The adjacent inverse pairs cancel when the network is composed, so the whole transformed network is exactly its decoded original network. At a staggered interface use the lower-scale bundles specified in step 2.2 and retain their upper decoder as a gate on their logical outputs. No decoder is moved across an intervening gate, and no $U_j\mathcal E_j=U_j$ identity is used. In particular, at a bad owner $\widehat B$ is the conjugation of the ORIGINAL retained physical channel; it retains its incoming syndrome and all original memory. Its logical support is just its own gate's input/output blocks, since the claimed ECs and their collars act only on those blocks and internal ancillas. Other blocks are external references. This proves localization without enlarging its elementary support. [given, A1, A2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

2.4 The ideal completions in the scan serve only to compute goodness; the exact network in step 2.3 contains no completion channel. Their relation is as follows. Restore deleted collars from the lowest scale upward, using fresh ideal operations at every deleted physical address. An opening child exRec of a restored core has all its leading smaller ECs in that core or in its restored input collar. Hence its entire recursively expanded support is ideal. By induction down to the elementary flags every root wholly added in such a restoration is good and claims no real location. A retained child keeps its original address and original faults; its newly restored output EC pieces contain no faults and introduce no selected bad root. Induction on the child scale therefore identifies its selected bad roots with those obtained by treating its missing output ECs as absent. At an interface already owned by a later bad root, neither calculation reuses its real elementary addresses: the virtual addresses carry ideal operations, and the original addresses remain owned by the later root. Thus the bad selected children in the completed scan are precisely the bad owners in the retained quotient network; completions contribute only good bookkeeping nodes. No channel equality between an original EC and its ideal replacement is asserted. [given, A2, step 2.1, step 2.2, construct, induction]

3.1 We prove by induction on $j$ the following strengthened assertion for every retained complete or truncated level-$j$ root, and for their finite causal networks: the exact conjugated network of step 2.3 has ideal logical operations at good roots and arbitrary operations on the declared logical support and syndrome memory at selected bad roots. For a good root, its identity is valid on the entire admitted input syndrome sector, not merely on the image of one fixed input state. The admitted sector is specified by the leading-EC fault counts at every exposed lower-scale boundary. Each of its defining conditions is a weight filter on the logical coordinates of that scale, with the smaller syndromes unrestricted or subject to earlier such conditions. By step 1.1 each filter constrains a syndrome register in the recursive decoder tree and acts as identity on the final logical coordinate. Their simultaneous range is therefore $H_L\otimes K$ for an allowed syndrome subspace $K$. The sector can include arbitrary correlations among these syndrome registers, logical blocks, and references. At $j=1$ the assertion is steps 1.2–1.3, while a bad root is always the exact conjugation in step 2.3. [given, A1, A2, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.3, construct, induction]

3.2 Suppose the assertion holds at every scale at most $j$. Decompose a retained level-$(j+1)$ exRec at its actual level-$j$ interfaces using step 2.2; use lower interfaces wherever a collar is staggered. All these components have scale at most $j$, so the induction hypothesis applies without invoking level-$(j+1)$ correctness. Replace each good component in the exact coordinate network by its logical ideal factor and its syndrome channel. Replace each bad component by its original conjugated map. Its syndrome and all smaller syndromes become auxiliary memory. Syndrome-only channels commute with logical ideal channels on disjoint factors; collect them into the intervening memory evolution, or into the next bad component. They do not create logical fault locations. Step 2.4 identifies the number and template addresses of the remaining bad components with the stated child scan. Thus a good parent gives the primitive one-scale exRec network with at most one effective faulty location, some output ECs possibly absent, and arbitrary syndrome memory interacting with the logical data only at that faulty location. [given, A1, A2, step 2.2, step 2.3, step 2.4, step 3.1, induction, construct]

3.3 Apply the local filter conditions [A1] to this network. If $s_i$ effective faults are in leading EC $i$, those ECs put the resulting logical block coordinates in $P_{s_i}$ even for arbitrary input, and the smaller syndrome registers are retained as reference or allowed fault memory. The total leading, gate, and present trailing fault count is at most one. Step 1.2 therefore proves the decoded logical identity for the parent, with every missing output EC simply absent. The entire admitted sector described in step 3.1 obeys this calculation, because that sector imposes precisely the lower-boundary filters used in step 3.2 and the just obtained outer filter conditions. Step 1.3 then gives its syndrome-retaining good factor. A bad parent remains the exact conjugation of its own retained channel, as in step 2.3. This completes the simultaneous induction for all truncations and finite networks. It uses only strictly smaller-scale identities in step 3.2 and elementary identities in this step. At the circuit boundaries the prescribed preparations or encoded inputs supply the admitted sectors, and final destructive measurement instruments supply the stated ideal output law when every selected top root is good. [given, A1, A2, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.3, step 3.1, step 3.2, induction]

4.1 A selected bad level-$r$ root, $r\ge1$, contains at least two selected bad child roots in its retained scan. Choose the first two in the fixed order, and repeat inside each of them down to scale zero. The result is a full binary tree of depth $r$. A later selected bad child claimed every location of its retained exRec before the earlier child was processed. Hence the two child supports are disjoint. This remains true at every nested boundary because [A2] erases all overlapping elementary locations, not just the nominal outer EC. Induction down the tree gives $2^r$ distinct faulty elementary leaves. The same argument gives disjoint leaves for trees from jointly selected roots. Ideal completions create no faulty leaves. [given, A2, step 2.4, step 3.3, construct, induction]

4.2 Count the trees before considering probabilities. At each internal vertex its two child roots have labels in its fixed complete exRec template, with at most $b$ possibilities; hence their unordered pair has at most $A=\binom b2$ possibilities. Order that pair by the fixed template order. A full depth-$r$ binary tree has $2^r-1$ internal vertices, so it has at most $A^{2^r-1}$ labelings. A label sequence fixes every elementary leaf's physical location in the original schedule, regardless of which ECs the scan erased: truncation deletes locations and never changes a root's template address. Thus no extra count of truncation patterns is needed. Some labelings cannot survive a scan or repeat a leaf; discard them. Every actual witness is among the remaining predetermined labelings, by step 4.1. [given, A2, step 4.1, algebra]

5.1 For $k$ specified selected bad roots, choose one of these labelled witness trees for each root. There are at most $A^{k(2^r-1)}$ such tuples. Keep only tuples with $k2^r$ distinct elementary leaves. For a fixed retained tuple the probability all its leaves fault is $\eta^{k2^r}$ by [F1]; no restriction on other locations, erasure decisions, or additional faults is imposed. Step 4.1 makes the event of interest a subset of the union of these fixed leaf-fault events. Applying [F2] proves $\mathbb P(\text{all specified roots selected bad})\le A^{k(2^r-1)}\eta^{k2^r}$. This proof uses independence only for fixed elementary locations and therefore remains valid for fault-dependent truncations. [F1, F2, step 4.1, step 4.2, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The exact coordinate network uses the original retained channels throughout. Ideal completions are used only for the scan's fault bookkeeping; no physical recovery replaces a syndrome-retaining boundary channel. The local conditions are the $t=1$ filter conditions of Section 9 of Aliferis–Gottesman–Preskill, and the syndrome-memory treatment follows Sections 5.1–5.2. Checking those local conditions for a concrete universal family is a separate obligation.
