## Definition

Input a finite Hermitian loss matrix $H$, a certified spectral interval, sparse-entry and sparse-sum access, a costed reference-state preparation, a rational error budget, and a finite polynomial/phase certificate. The output is either the heralded normalized Gibbs purification or an explicit FAIL; no matrix exponential, QSVT phase list, state preparation, inverse, postselection, or sparse-sum oracle is implicit. For arbitrary dimension carry the supplied reference preparation cost $C_\Phi$.

The instructions below define the finite smooth-Fourier matrix-function approximation, coherent spectral minimum estimate, and flagged Gibbs preparation used in this algorithm. Each named circuit and arithmetic loop is part of the definition; correctness, termination under numerical certificates, and resource bounds are separate claims.

## OP0 — Numerical arithmetic, roots, coefficient generation and finite amplification

**Certified elementary evaluation.** For rational x and error 2^-b, evaluate exp, sin and cos by their rational Taylor sums until the absolute remainder bound `3^ceil(|x|+1)|x|^(N+1)/(N+1)!` is below the error budget; sine/cosine may first reduce the argument by a certified interval for π. Compute π by the arctangent identity `π=16 arctan(1/5)−4 arctan(1/239)` with alternating-series bounds. For x≥0 compute square roots by rational bisection between 0 and `max(1,x)`; x=0 returns0 and x<0 returns INVALID in the real routine. Complex algebraic roots use ROOTS. For x>0 compute log x with `2Σ_(j=0)^(N−1)y^(2j+1)/(2j+1)`, y=(x−1)/(x+1), and tail `2|y|^(2N+1)/((2N+1)(1−y²))`. Each loop increases N or b until its explicit rational remainder test passes. Store the interval, not an uncertified floating result. Complex exponentials use the resulting sine/cosine intervals. For atan2(y,x), the zero vector returns INVALID; otherwise select its quadrant by certified signs (exact zero coordinates use their explicit axis angles), and bisect an angle interval in that quadrant using monotonic sine/cosine comparisons with the normalized vector until its width meets the requested error. If an interval straddles an equality, retain the adjoining angle intervals rather than request a real zero test. Return the midpoint plus its error interval. Whenever a numerical logarithm/angle appears in a ceiling or resource cutoff, compute an upper rational enclosure of width≤1/4 and take its ceiling; this defines a terminating conservative integer rule even at exact integer boundaries. Norms are square roots of explicit sums of squared coordinates; a zero norm takes the separately stated zero-output/INVALID branch before division. Bounds on the number/size of these operations are separate classical bit-cost obligations.

**ROOTS(p,b).** Input a nonzero polynomial with rational or exact-algebraic complex coefficients and b≥1. A nonzero constant polynomial returns the empty root list immediately; the zero polynomial returns INVALID. All candidate rational cuts/displacements are enumerated first by increasing power-of-two denominator, then increasing numerator, restricted to the stated middle-third interval. Remove repeated factors by the Euclidean gcd of p and p′, retaining multiplicities. Compute a rational Cauchy root bound `B>1+max_(j<d)|a_j/a_d|`. Enclose the square-free roots in the square `[-B,B]+i[-B,B]`. Subdivide rational rectangles. Before using a boundary, restrict p to each directed edge, and use the real polynomial gcd/Sturm signed-remainder sequence to certify that its real and imaginary parts never vanish simultaneously on that edge; if they do, move that cut to the first dyadic rational in the middle third that passes the check. Count the roots in a rectangle by its boundary winding number: isolate the finitely many zeros of the imaginary edge polynomial by Sturm bisection, retain crossings with positive real part, and add +1 for a negative-to-positive imaginary crossing, −1 for the reverse, and 0 for even contact. Boundaries are traversed counterclockwise. Constant zero imaginary edge polynomials are handled by the first displacement in that deterministic enumeration for which the entire new directed boundary is certified root-free; simultaneous complex zeros are already excluded. Also require that the imaginary value at every boundary vertex is nonzero and no edge imaginary restriction is identically zero, selecting the first enumerated displacement that certifies all these conditions. This removes vertex/corner crossing ambiguity; retain a nested partition and the certified outer root bound. Retain rectangles with positive winding count, discard zero-count rectangles, and subdivide until each retained rectangle has count one and diameter≤2^-b. Return the polynomial, disjoint isolating rectangles and original multiplicities. A Sturm sequence is formed by p,p′ and successive **negative** Euclidean remainders; endpoint sign variation differences count real roots after removing endpoint factors. The exact-algebraic variant uses the same finite field arithmetic, with algebraic values represented by their defining polynomial and isolator. Refinement selects the appropriate root after resultant field operations.

This defines the needed root isolation and selection operations. Their winding/Sturm correctness and bit bounds must be authored locally; they are not granted by a root count or source title. Refinement has finite termination on a nonzero finite-degree polynomial because distinct roots have positive separation and only finitely many forbidden cuts occur. ROOTS may take many refinements; no bound `poly(d,log(1/error))` is inferred without its separate certified-arithmetic proof.

**RawMean(V,M).** M=2^m≥4. V is a supplied coherent finite sampler, and the value circuit computes v(x)∈[0,1] to its allocated precision. Append a flag and rotate it to `sqrt(1−v(x))|0>+sqrt(v(x))|1>`, then uncompute the value scratch. Use the resulting preparation, its inverse and the flag reflection in the exact QA13 repeated-controlled-Q estimator. Output sin²(πy/M), or a certified rational enclosure when classical processing needs bits. `Mean(V,M,ρ)` repeats this circuit an odd r times, where r is the least positive odd integer at least `ln(1/ρ)/(2(8/π²−1/2)²)`, with an upper interval for the logarithm, and returns the median. The same scalar v is estimated each time; the output is not a median of unrelated spectral samples.

**RelProb(V,p_min,c,ρ).** Input 0<p_min≤1, 0<c≤1/100, and promise that V's designated good-or-unfinished probability p is either zero or at least p_min. Set `J=ceil(log₂(1024/sqrt(p_min)))`; for j=2,…,J estimate p with `Mean(V,2^j,ρ/(2(J+1)))`. Stop the rough loop at the first estimate with `(2^j)^2 estimate≥1024`; if none, test once more at 2^J and return ZERO if that estimate lies below `256/4^J`, otherwise retain J. With the retained scale M, run Mean at the least dyadic scale at least `256πM/c`, with failure ρ/2, and return its rational enclosure. If the intervals cannot certify a branch within the allocated numerical error, refine to error `min(c p_min/100,2^-j/100)` and use lower endpoint for the declared comparison. This is a bounded schedule; ZERO or a bad probability estimate can occur on a statistical failure branch. The relative-probability/zero-detection proof is a separate obligation. Count each repeated controlled Q and all confidence/bit costs, not merely the number of phase registers.

**CapAmplify(V,p_low,ρ).** A preparation V, its inverse and an ordinary or coherent verifier are explicit inputs. If p_low≥1/2, use direct samples; otherwise, in every round first make one direct verified sample and then, if it failed, use the finite QA11 rejection-sampled random-iteration rule with K the least positive integer satisfying p_low K²≥1. Run `ceil(16 ln(1/ρ))+16` fresh rounds; return the first verified good output and its success bit, or FAIL after the cap. Unknown actual success probability is not used to select an exact Grover peak. Output states are conditioned on the designated projector; classical output requires a diagonal costed predicate. This procedure is finite on every execution branch. Its success bound needs the declared lower-bound promise and the separate amplification results.


**SmoothFourier(f,x₀,r,Δ,B,e).** Input Taylor coefficients `f(x₀+x)=Σ a_k x^k`, with a proved convergent absolute bound `Σ |a_k|(r+Δ)^k≤B`; its coefficient evaluation circuit and uniform precision are explicit. Let ν=Δ/(r+Δ). Choose `K=ceil(ν^-1 ln(16B/e))+2`; compute b_k=a_k(r+Δ)^k for k=0,…,K−1. Let `L=ceil(ν^-2 ln(32B/e))+2` and `M′=ceil(ν^-1 ln(32B/e))+2`. Compute the finite arcsine coefficients `u_(2j)=0`, `u_(2j+1)=(2/π) binom(2j,j)/(4^j(2j+1))` up to L. Form coefficient arrays u^(0)=(1,0,…), and u^(k+1)_ℓ=Σ_(s=0)^ℓ u^(k)_s u_(ℓ−s), truncating above L. For every k<K,ℓ≤L and j=0,…,ℓ with `|j−ℓ/2|≤M′`, add `b_k u^(k)_ℓ (i/2)^ℓ (−1)^j binom(ℓ,j)` to coefficient c_(2j−ℓ). Multiply c_s by `exp(−iπs x₀/(2(r+Δ)))`. Round finite coefficient arrays so their total error≤e/16; zero coefficients are omitted. The resulting SELECT applies `exp(iπsH/(2(r+Δ)))`, controlled on its finite signed index. PREPARE, SELECT, PREPARE† give the usual LCU block, followed by a clean flag dilution α/B_upper if α=Σ|c_s|≤B_upper, where B_upper is an explicitly rounded upper bound on B plus coefficient error. c=0 returns the zero block by flipping a zero flag. This is source Lemma37's arcsine/convolution/binomial-tail construction and Theorem40's Fourier LCU, with concrete enlarged cutoffs; no undefined Fourier coefficients or matrix-function oracle remain.

**ShiftMin(H,K).** Given ||H||≤K and dimension d, if K=0 return λ_tilde=0. Otherwise prepare the costed |Φ_d> and run the finite QPE eigenvalue sampler on one half, with injective evolution exp(iH/(2K+1)), so its eigenvalue distribution is uniform including multiplicities. Use a dyadic phase scale at least `128π(2K+1)`. On each eigencomponent coherently repeat this QPE on separate phase registers an odd number at least `8 ln(1/ν_bad)+8`, compute the median reversibly, and decode eigenvalues to ±K to additive1/8. Here `ν_bad=ρ_call²/(10^12d(M_cap+1)²)` and M_cap is the declared total sampler-call cap including confidence trials. Retain all phase work; controlled simulations/inverses and numerical errors use this same budget. This coherent repetition keeps one eigencomponent, rather than repreparing an unrelated mixed spectral sample. Feed this coherent sampler into Min below with budget `100000 ceil(sqrt(d))`, target cumulative mass1/d and enough repetitions to make its failure≤ρ_call. Return λ_tilde rounded downward by1/8. On the good event it is within1/2 of λ_min; otherwise its error is included in the failure ledger. No diagonal-entry minimization substitutes for spectral minimum finding.

**Min(V,N,ρ_call).** V is a costed coherent sampler of a finite binary score and label, with a declared exact transitive total comparison (lexical ties allowed); an angular score may be a rational vector compared by half-plane and determinant signs rather than a rounded arctangent, with the minimum/desired quantile having cumulative probability≥1/N. Set the incumbent to the output of one fresh sample. Maintain a counter of actual V,V† calls with total cap `100000 ceil(sqrt(N))` per trial. For the predicate “encoded score<incumbent”, run a BBHT schedule with ranges `ceil((3/2)^j)`, j=0,1,…, using fresh V preparations, a uniformly drawn integer iteration count, one success-projector reflection and an initial-state reflection each iterate. Verify each measured candidate by that same encoded comparison; if smaller, replace the incumbent and restart j at0. Before a batch whose declared calls exceed the remaining budget, stop and return the incumbent. Stop immediately on a zero remaining budget. Run `ceil(20 ln(1/ρ_call))+20` fresh trials and return the smallest measured score, with lexical label ties. This is an explicit capped form of the source AppendixC generalized minimum finder. If the true minimum has less mass than1/N, no such minimum guarantee is claimed; the quantile version remains valid at its specified mass. For coherent approximate-score samplers the result is the corresponding robust “no label has every good estimate smaller” output, with all estimation and cap failure included in its separate theorem.

**GibbsFlag(H,η,e).** Given finite Hermitian H, sparse-sum access, bound ||ηH||≤K and η≥0, use ShiftMin on ηH to obtain λ_tilde. Set G=ηH−(λ_tilde−3/2)I; on the good event I≤G and its lowest eigenvalue≤2. Supply the costed reference-state preparation |Φ_d>. Set K_G to a certified upper norm bound/2, x₀=K_G+1/2,r=K_G,Δ=1/2, and use SmoothFourier for `f(x)=exp(−x/2)`, whose Taylor coefficients are `a_k=exp(−x₀/2)(−1/2)^k/k!` and absolute B=1. Apply its block circuit to the first half of |Φ_d>. The zero-work flag has subnormalized vector `(exp(−G/2)⊗I)|Φ_d>/B_upper`, with success probability `Tr(exp(−G))/(d B_upper²)`. This flag circuit and inverse are usable **without postselection**. To output a normalized purification, use CapAmplify with lower floor1/(64d), e≤ε_out/(10000sqrt(d)), and the declared confidence cap; measure its success projector and return the conditional state or FAIL. The energy shift changes no normalized Gibbs state. For η=0 prepare |Φ_d> directly. A known lower partition-function bound can replace the conservative floor and reduces the charged amplification.



The power-of-two reference state uses exactly the Hadamard/CNOT circuit in [[def-qa-30-sdp-reference-state-access]]. For other dimensions the procedure carries $C_\Phi$ as an input. The algorithm returns the normalized purification only on a verified amplification success branch; otherwise it returns FAIL.
