# Root scalar-collar construction: exact bounded audit

This is a constructive proposal for root adjudication, not a canonical item edit or acceptance. Native batch-31 writing may be active. The source audit and original fatal findings remain preserved.

## The exact reusable scalar adapter

Let K be an actual compact disk with corners, with one connected interval incoming face, one connected interval outgoing face, and two trajectory sides. Let Y be a C1 nonzero field tangent to the sides and transverse to the incoming/outgoing faces. Suppose every trajectory crosses from incoming to outgoing in finite time, and a C1 function g satisfies dg(Y)>0 throughout K. Let c be a C2 scalar defined on an open collar of the ENTIRE boundary, with dc(Y)>0 there. Suppose c is constant A and B on the two respective faces. Then A<B, and there is a C2 noncritical scalar v on K equal to c on a smaller open collar of the entire boundary.

1. The compact positive minimum of dg(Y), together with bounded g, bounds every transit time; the face transversality and C1 inverse theorem make exit time a C1 function. Normalizing transit time gives a C1 product G:[0,1]×[0,1]→K. Choose the transverse coordinate y so that y=0 and y=1 are the two trajectory sides. Since Y is unchanged on a neighborhood of the sides, shrinking in y gives invariant side strips inside the prescribed boundary collar. The pullback c∘G is defined on these entire strips and near both face ends.

2. On all fibers choose an endpoint cutoff η(t), equal to one sufficiently near t=0,1, whose support is uniformly short. Put d0=∂t(c∘G) on those end neighborhoods. It is positive and continuous. By compactness, choose η so that E(y)=∫ηd0 dt<B−A for all y. Extend ηd0 by zero across its middle gap; no undefined value of c is used there. With J=∫(1−η)dt>0, set h(y)=(B−A−E(y))/J and q0(t,y)=η(t)d0(t,y)+(1−η(t))h(y). The density q0 is positive, equals d0 near the two ends, and integrates to B−A.

3. On the invariant side strips put qs=∂t(c∘G)>0. Its integral is also B−A, by the constant endpoint values. Choose a transverse cutoff λ(y) supported in these strips and equal to one on smaller side strips. Set q=(1−λ)q0+λqs, interpreting the second term only on its support. The density remains positive, has integral B−A on every fiber, and equals qs on the smaller side strips. Define w(t,y)=A+∫0^t q(r,y)dr. It equals c∘G near t=0 because it has the same initial value and derivative; near t=1 it equals c∘G because it has the same terminal value B and derivative. It equals c∘G on the entire smaller side strips. Thus w∘G^{-1} agrees with c on the full boundary collar, not merely at its faces.

4. The C1-flow regularity closes by integration by parts, without assuming mixed second derivatives. Write F=c∘G on the end neighborhoods. The primitive of η∂tF is

    I(t,y)=η(t)F(t,y)−η(0)A−∫0^t η'(r)F(r,y)dr.

    Every nonzero term uses F only in its actual end-neighborhood domain. Since F is C1 and η is smooth, this expression is C1 jointly. In particular E(y)=I(1,y), h(y), and w0(t,y)=A+I(t,y)+h(y)∫0^t(1−η(r))dr are C1. On the side strips the primitive of qs is exactly F(t,y)−A, which is C1. Equivalently define w=(1−λ)w0+λF there, and w=w0 elsewhere. Thus w is C1 with ∂tw=q>0 even though ∂tF need only be continuous. Composition with the C1 inverse G^{-1} gives a C1 scalar. This supplies the transverse differentiability that a direct unsupported differentiation of ∂tF would miss.
5. Once that C1 scalar exists, the final C2 approximation is elementary and exact. Let v1 be the C1 scalar, already equal to C2 c near the boundary. Its Euclidean gradient has a positive minimum m on compact K. Take a C2 cutoff χ supported in the interior, equal to one away from the boundary collar, and with its transition region contained where v1=c. Extend v1 to a slightly larger neighborhood and convolve to vε, choosing C1 error less than m/[2(1+||∇χ||∞)]. The scalar (1−χ)v1+χvε is C2: v1 is only used without convolution on the region where it equals c. Its gradient differs from ∇v1 by less than m/2. It is noncritical and remains exactly c on the full boundary collar. The same finite-chart argument applies if rounded corners have already been placed inside a regular collar.

## What the actual lobe must supply

The root's proposed minimum raising is geometrically sound provided the actual first integral used is Morse at the center. On the center's nested-circle component, choose a strictly increasing reparameterization which raises its minimum toward the saddle value and is the identity near that value. It changes no regular circles, preserves critical inertia, and leaves the exterior branch unchanged. Pick a between the value at a short exterior exit section and the raised minimum; pick b just above the saddle. The prospective disk triad is the exterior incoming strip, a zero handle at the center, and the one handle at the saddle joining that disk to the strip. Its regular sides must be constructed as actual original-field trajectories and remain inside the domain of the fixed cap product. No external circle or closed face is an actual face of this source block.

The existing cancellation theorem is formulated for compact two-face smooth slabs. Its proof localizes field modifications away from the boundary. An interval-face version therefore needs an explicit local adapter: unchanged trajectory sides, support compactly inside the disk triad, local smooth Morse/connecting-orbit construction, and proof every modified trajectory reaches the two faces. A C1 gradient-like field with a C1 scalar having uniformly positive derivative on the compact triad would give the required finite transit bound. These are not obtained simply by renaming an interval a circle.

For the cap boundary germ, a global relation c=φ(u0) is unnecessary. Because dc annihilates each connected interval face, c is constant on that face. Along the original side trajectories dc(Y)>0, hence the outgoing constant exceeds the incoming one. Direct density construction preserves c even when different disconnected level components have different one-variable reparameterizations.

Near the saddle, the genuine cap section c=t_C has a nondegenerate Hessian if its differential is the pullback foliation one-form multiplied by a NONZERO factor. Differentiating at the characteristic zero then gives D(f*ω)=c0 Hess(t_C) with c0≠0. Merely having the same cooriented regular leaves is insufficient: g and g³ have the same regular leaves but different Hessian nondegeneracy. Extending the inner-annulus relation through the center by composing with u0 needs the same nondegeneracy/nonzero-factor property for u0 at p, or a separate actual local Morse-center construction and regular-circle gluing.

On a regular annulus of full connected circle leaves, both actual primitives and u0 factor through its regular quotient interval. Their one-variable transition functions have positive derivative on that REGULAR interval; no endpoint differentiability in u0(p) is needed. Adjust the positive affine amplitude and offset of H and interpolate the two positive derivative functions across a compact middle quotient interval, choosing its integral to give the desired endpoint difference. The resulting primitive is C2 because it agrees with actual H near p, actual T near the outer annulus/q, and is composed with regular u0 only in the intervening annuli. It has only the center and saddle. Its gradient trajectories agree with those of u0 up to positive reparameterization on regular points.

Raise the minimum only on the inner circle component with a strictly increasing reparameterization, identity on a neighborhood of the frontier value zero; the exterior negative branch is unchanged. Choose a<new minimum<0<b with a reached in the short exterior branch tube. In local saddle coordinates, take two regular incoming endpoints on opposite sides of that branch. The enclosed source region is the initial interval strip with the center disk and saddle band attached; the saddle band attaches once to the disk and once to the strip, so its outgoing face is a single interval and the region is a disk. Corner rounding is performed only in this regular boundary collar and must preserve its scalar germ.

This describes an actual disk-with-interval-faces construction rather than replacing its faces by fictitious circles. The local cancellation normal-form construction must be adapted explicitly to the interior of this disk, where the scalar and field can be smoothed; outside the compact smoothing/cancellation region the original C1 field and C2 scalar remain fixed.
