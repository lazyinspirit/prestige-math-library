## Statement

Assume AC; let $f_X$ have a fixed real Gaussian-process prior with mean $m$ and symmetric positive-semidefinite covariance matrix on a nonempty finite ordered candidate set $X$. Let the observation errors be independent $N(0,\sigma^2)$ variables, independent of the entire process. The initial observation sites, if any, are fixed before their observations and their values are generated by $y_i=f(x_i)+\xi_i$ under this same model, without an additional informative inclusion event. Use the exact least-index maximizer, or a deterministic approximate selector that is measurable with respect to the observed history and supplies its verified error $\delta_t$.

Let $H_t$ contain the initial data, selected sites, successful observation values, and earlier availability flags, where $t$ is the current number of observations. Before each reached adaptive call, write $A_{t+1}=1$ when the oracle returns $Y_{t+1}=f(x_{t+1})+\xi_{t+1}$ and $A_{t+1}=0$ when it returns `observation-unavailable`. Assume a measurable $q_t(H_t)\in[0,1]$ satisfies
$$P(A_{t+1}=1\mid H_t,f_X,\xi_{t+1},\ldots,\xi_N)=q_t(H_t)$$
almost surely. This is conditional independence of availability from the joint latent vector and all unused observation errors given the observed past. Other computation and status decisions are measurable functions of the observed history and fixed inputs. Availability may depend on past values and the selected site; $q_t=1$ includes always available observations.

At every valid history length $t$, assume $C_t=K_t+\sigma^2 I$ is positive definite when $t\ge1$ (which holds for every possibly singular $K_t$ when $\sigma^2>0$ by [[lem-positive-noise-regularizes-observed-covariance]], and holds for zero noise when $K_t$ itself is positive definite). Then whenever [[alg-finite-candidate-gp-acquisition-search]] computes a posterior state, a version of the conditional law of $f_X$ given the full observed history is the Gaussian kernel supplied by [[thm-finite-gaussian-process-posterior-and-predictive-laws]], evaluated on the actually selected observation list. This includes successful availability flags and the updated state at the final horizon. If the next call is unavailable, the retained current state remains the conditional law after learning that flag. Conditional-law assertions hold almost surely for realized histories; the fixed-list formulas provide an all-data-vector version and make no claim about uniquely determined laws on impossible histories. A failed solve after appending an observation leaves only an older stored state, whose assertion concerns its earlier history. This theorem proves a sequential posterior update, not a regret or global-search guarantee.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; the fixed GP prior and finite candidate set; independent centered Gaussian observation errors; the fixed initial sites without informative inclusion; the history-measurable acquisition and computation rules; conditionally noninformative availability as in the Statement; and positive definiteness of each observed covariance at a successful stage.

[A1] AC is part of the Gaussian-process construction and posterior interfaces used here. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[A2] Observation errors are independent of the process and of one another. [[def-independent-random-elements]]

[A3] At each reached call, conditional on $H_t$, the availability flag is independent of $(f_X,\xi_{t+1},\ldots,\xi_N)$, with success probability $q_t(H_t)$ as specified in the Statement.

[F1] For a fixed finite observation list with observed covariance positive definite, the posterior law is a Gaussian kernel defined for every data vector; repeated sites are allowed and posterior covariance may be singular. [[thm-finite-gaussian-process-posterior-and-predictive-laws]]

[F2] The posterior mean and covariance are the Schur-complement formulas, and its inverse systems can be evaluated by Cholesky plus triangular solves. [[thm-finite-gaussian-process-posterior-and-predictive-laws]]

[F3] The exact least-index maximizer is a measurable function of the observed history: on each fixed finite site-history, the posterior mean is affine in the observations, the posterior variance is fixed, and the finite comparisons defining each least-index maximizer are Borel sets. Any approximate selector is required to be deterministic and history-measurable and to return its verified error $\delta_t$. [[alg-finite-candidate-gp-acquisition-search]]

## Proof

1.1 At the initial history, if the supplied fixed list is empty, the conditional law is the given GP prior; otherwise [F1] gives the fixed-list posterior when its observed covariance is positive definite. The initial data are not selected by an informative inclusion event. By [A2], all unused errors are still independent Gaussian variables, jointly independent of the latent vector and initial history. Use the stronger induction assertion that, conditional on the full current history, the latent vector has the stated Gaussian posterior and all unused errors retain their product Gaussian law independently of it. The algorithm computes its initial state before any acquisition. [given, A1, A2, F1, base]

2.1 Assume the stronger induction assertion at a reached history $H_t$. By [F3], the next site is determined by $H_t$; observing its selection adds no information. The other computation and status decisions are also history-measurable. By the induction assertion, conditional on $H_t$ the next potential observation $Y=f(x_{t+1})+\xi_{t+1}$ is a Gaussian coordinate of the current latent vector plus independent Gaussian noise, and all subsequent unused errors are jointly independent of $(f_X,Y)$. This argument conditions on the past before treating the selected site as fixed; it does not assume that the adaptively selected full observation vector is unconditionally Gaussian. [given, A2, F3, step 1.1, IH]

2.2 For any bounded measurable function $g$ of $(f_X,\xi_{t+1},\ldots,\xi_N)$, [A3] and conditional expectation give $E[g\mathbf{1}_{\{A_{t+1}=1\}}\mid H_t]=q_t(H_t)E[g\mid H_t]$, and the corresponding identity for $A_{t+1}=0$ uses $1-q_t(H_t)$. On either branch of positive conditional probability, division by that probability shows that the joint latent and unused-error law is unchanged by learning the flag. Hence an unavailable call preserves the current posterior and terminates, and a successful flag leaves the Gaussian pair $(f_X,Y)$ from step 2.1 unchanged before $Y$ is revealed. Branches with zero conditional probability require no assertion about realized histories. [A3, step 2.1, algebra]

3.1 On success, write the current latent mean and covariance as $\mu_t,V_t$, let $x=x_{t+1}$, $w=V_t(:,x)$ and $d=V_t(x,x)+\sigma^2$. The positive-definite augmented observed covariance makes $d>0$: it is its scalar Schur complement when $t\ge1$, and its sole entry when $t=0$. Applying [F1] to the conditional Gaussian vector from steps 2.1 and 2.2 gives the updated mean $\mu_t+w d^{-1}(Y-\mu_t(x))$ and covariance $V_t-ww^T/d$. The remaining unused errors retain their independent Gaussian law independently of the updated latent vector: before conditioning on $Y$ their joint law factors from $(f_X,Y)$, so the same factorization remains after conditioning. This proves the stronger induction assertion after the new observation. [given, A2, F1, step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra]

3.2 These updates equal the batch formulas on the actual appended site list. Indeed, for $t\ge1$ write its observed covariance as $C_{t+1}=\begin{pmatrix}C_t&c\\c^T&a\end{pmatrix}$, where $c=k_t(x)$, $a=k(x,x)+\sigma^2$, and $d=a-c^TC_t^{-1}c$. Direct multiplication gives $C_{t+1}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}C_t^{-1}+C_t^{-1}cc^TC_t^{-1}/d&-C_t^{-1}c/d\\-c^TC_t^{-1}/d&1/d\end{pmatrix}$. Substitution in the batch mean and covariance gives exactly step 3.1, with $w=k(X,x)-K_{X,X_t}C_t^{-1}c$. For $t=0$, the same formulas reduce directly to the one-observation conditioning formula. Repeated sites introduce no change to these identities; only observed positive definiteness is required. [F1, F2, step 3.1, algebra]

4.1 Induction proves the full-history posterior assertion at every computed state, including the final horizon. Step 2.2 also proves that the current state remains correct after an unavailable flag, even when its probability depends on past observations. If an appended observation is followed by a failed solve, no new state is asserted: the stored earlier state describes only its earlier history. All formulas define versions on fixed site-history strata; their conditional-law meaning is almost sure on realized histories. No argument requires a nonsingular latent covariance, a pseudoinverse, a regret bound, or a global-optimality claim. [given, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 3.2, induction, discharge-induction: step 2.1] ∎
