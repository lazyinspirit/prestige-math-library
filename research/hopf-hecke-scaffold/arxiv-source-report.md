## 1. Semisimple Hopf structure: q-alg/9712033v1

**Reading evidence.** Section 1, pp.1–4, constructs the route through the Drinfeld double, modularity (Lemma 1.1), Verlinde eigenvalues and algebraic integrality (Lemma 1.2), divisibility for double modules (Theorem 1.4), and Frobenius type for quasitriangular Hopf algebras (Theorem 1.5). Section 2, pp.4–6, proves the order-two Drinfeld element lemma, removes its parity factor from the triangular structure, and invokes Deligne's theorem to reconstruct a twisted group algebra. Remarks 2.3–2.4 distinguish categorical, quantum and ordinary dimensions, and explain the supervector-space symmetric structure. Page 7 completes the references.

**Usable verified routes.** Under the paper's algebraically closed characteristic-zero semisimplicity hypotheses, centrality of the Drinfeld element follows from the antipode-square identity plus the Larson–Radford theorem. Lemma 2.1 uses equality of traces of `u` and `u^{-1}` on simple modules and scalar action; characteristic zero is essential when dividing by simple-module dimension. For Theorem 1.4, the algebraic-integrality inference is sound: if the square of the rational number `dim(H)/dim(V)` is integral over the integers, so is the number itself (transitivity of integrality, or a monic even polynomial); a rational algebraic integer is an integer. This elementary number-theory lemma must be supplied, not merely asserted.

**Closure required.** Finite-dimensional algebra semisimplicity and Wedderburn decomposition; Schur's lemma over an algebraically closed field; antipode anti-algebra/anti-coalgebra identities; dual Hopf algebra; Drinfeld double construction and its dimension; semisimplicity of the double; factorizability and its restriction to character/center algebras; character rings and central primitive idempotents; rigid braided/ribbon categories and their trace conventions; modularity and Verlinde formula; algebraic integers. Theorem 1.7 additionally imports Nichols–Zoeller freeness and Zhu's semisimplicity argument. Theorem 2.2 imports Deligne's Tannakian theorem, natural transformations of forgetful functors, and reconstruction. These are major theorems, not consequences of the definitions alone.

**Boundary.** The article does not prove Frobenius type for every finite-dimensional semisimple Hopf algebra: Theorem 1.5 assumes quasitriangularity. Theorem 1.7 is presented in the standing characteristic-zero setting. Do not remove either hypothesis. Twisting preserves the underlying algebra, but claims about symmetric structures require attention to the Drinfeld element/parity modification.

## 2. Twists and triangular classification: math/9905168v2

**Reading evidence.** Section 2, pp.2–3, fixes the convention `Delta^J(a)=J^{-1}Delta(a)J` and `R^J=J_{21}^{-1}RJ`. Section 3, pp.3–4, proves gauge/isomorphism uniqueness through fiber-functor uniqueness and symmetric twists. Section 4, pp.4–5, constructs a twist from a square-dimension projective representation via the dual matrix coalgebra and proves invertibility by a matrix-algebra bimodule argument. Section 5, pp.6–7, gives characteristic-zero triples/quadruples classification and the minimality criterion. Section 6, pp.7–8, discusses positive-characteristic lifting; Section 7, pp.8–9, derives solvability and grouplike existence using central-type groups; Section 8, pp.9–10, computes the bijective-one-cocycle example through an explicit matrix-algebra action. Page 11 includes the added correction reference.

**Independently checked normalization omission.** The displayed gauge formula `J^x=Delta(x)J(x^{-1} tensor x^{-1})` for invertible `x` satisfies `(epsilon tensor id)(J^x)=epsilon(x)^{-1} 1`. Thus a *normalized counital* twist remains normalized only when `epsilon(x)=1`. Invertibility gives `epsilon(x) != 0`, so rescaling `x` makes this possible. The scaffold must impose normalized gauge transformations or explicitly normalize; the article's terse wording “invertible element” hides this condition.

**Usable routes and missing steps.** The twist cocycle is justified by expanding coassociativity of `Delta^J`: compare the two conjugating tensors, then apply the displayed cocycle equation. Counitality follows from the two normalizations. Invertibility of `J` is separate and indispensable; a coalgebra structure alone proves the cocycle equation, not invertibility. In Section 8, projective representations, semidirect products, character duality of finite abelian groups and Fourier invertibility are prerequisites for verifying the explicit matrix-algebra action; the normalizing `|A|^{-1}` requires invertibility of the group order.

**Closure required.** The previous paper's triangular-to-group-twist theorem; uniqueness of symmetric fiber functors; natural tensor transformations; projective representations and normalized group two-cocycles; twisted group algebras; Skolem–Noether for the matrix-algebra action; regular representation calculations; semisimple/cosemisimple duality and antipode-square criterion; Movshev's minimal-twist/simple-coalgebra theorem; finite group central extensions and central-type groups. Solvability imports Howlett–Isaacs, whose proof uses classification of finite simple groups. Positive characteristic imports Witt-vector lifting and faithfulness of descent.

**Critical source caveat.** Theorem 6.1 on p.7 has an explicit footnote: **“The proof is incomplete but a complete proof is given in [E, Remark 4.4].”** Reference [E] on p.11 is Etingof, *On faithfulness of the lifting for Hopf algebras and fusion categories*, [1704.07855](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.07855). That repair paper was not read in this assignment. Therefore the positive-characteristic classification proof is not supplied by the five-paper corpus, and may not be certified from this paper alone. The characteristic-zero classification already requires deep supplied Tannakian/Movshev theory.

## 3. Completion of triangular classification: math/0202258v3

**Reading evidence.** Section 2, pp.2–3, defines affine supergroups by parity-preserving functors of points and finite supergroup algebras `k[G] semidirect Lambda(V)`. Section 3, p.3, states Deligne's exponential-length theorem and finiteness consequences. Section 4, pp.4–6, applies it to modified supergroups, Chevalley property, septuple classification, antipode powers and cotriangular comodules. Section 5, pp.6–7, treats finite tensor categories, the projective-cover correction (Remark 5.3), and positive-characteristic nonzero-global-dimension fusion categories (Theorem 5.4). Pages 7–8 list references.

**Closure and hidden hypotheses.** Supervector spaces, Koszul signs, exterior algebras, parity-preserving algebra maps, affine group functors, finite duality, rigid symmetric tensor categories, finite length, tensor exactness, projective covers, Jacobson radicals and Hopf ideals; the equivalence between Chevalley property and radical being a Hopf ideal; Deligne super-Tannakian reconstruction; earlier twist/supergroup correspondence and septuple classification. The fusion application also needs fusion/global dimension definitions, Witt vectors, extension of scalars and the Etingof–Nikshych–Ostrik lifting/uniqueness theorem. Algebraic closedness and characteristic zero are the standing assumptions through §4 and the first part of §5.

**Boundaries extracted from the full text.** Remark 5.3 says that projective covers were missing in earlier category questions; `Rep(G_a)` supplies a counterexample if finiteness is weakened to finitely many simple objects. Theorem 4.7 gives equivalence of cotriangular comodule categories with supergroup representations, **not** isomorphism of the infinite-dimensional coalgebras: the important remark pp.5–6 explains why vector-space dimensions need not be preserved. Theorem 5.4 needs nonzero global dimension in positive characteristic; Remark 5.5 explicitly supplies the failure when this is omitted. These results belong to a fully supplied advanced branch; they cannot justify a short foundational jump.

## 4. Cyclotomic Hecke/KLR structure: 0808.2032v4

**Reading evidence.** Introduction pp.1–4 gives the quiver (including the double-edge `e=2` case), presentation (1.6)–(1.15), grading and connection to Ariki's theorem. Section 2, pp.5–6, treats divided differences, commuting nilpotents, power-series evaluation, nilpotency propagation and finite dimensionality. Section 3, pp.6–19, develops the degenerate affine/cyclotomic algebra, weight idempotents, intertwiners, new KLR generators, rank-three relation checks, inverse Hecke generators and mutually inverse maps. Section 4, pp.19–27, repeats this in the nondegenerate case. Section 5, pp.27–28, specializes to Young seminormal form. Section 6, pp.29–31, proves base change/integral lattices and character-preserving reduction; pp.31–32 finish references.

**Verified foundation route.** For `partial_r(f)=(s_r f-f)/(y_r-y_{r+1})`, substitution `y_r=y_{r+1}` makes the numerator zero, so the polynomial factor theorem proves divisibility over any field; expand products to prove the twisted Leibniz rule (2.2). For commuting nilpotents `a_r` with `a_r^{N_r}=0`, every sufficiently high-total-degree monomial vanishes (degree exceeding `sum(N_r-1)`); this proves well-defined finite evaluation of formal series. A series with nonzero constant term evaluates to a unit by a finite geometric series. Variable permutation and divided differences must be applied to a **specified series representative before evaluation**; they need not descend to arbitrary quotient-algebra elements (§2.1 explicitly warns about this). These elementary suppliers are suitable early scaffold content.

**Prerequisite closure, including externally imported facts.** Symmetric-group adjacent-transposition presentation/reduced words; quotient algebras and universal properties; polynomial/Laurent polynomial rings; nilpotent geometric-series inversion; generalized simultaneous eigenspace decomposition for commuting operators; finite-dimensional algebra idempotents and blocks; Chinese-remainder spectral projectors and proof that they lie in the polynomial subalgebra. The cyclotomic PBW basis and `l^d d!` dimension are imported from Kleshchev/Ariki–Koike (§§3.1,4.1). Residue-spectrum support is imported from Kleshchev/Grojnowski, not proved by the quotient relation on the first generator alone. The assertion that residue sums are *primitive central* idempotents imports Brundan and Lyle–Mathas block classifications; centrality alone is weaker. Absolute irreducibility (§6 Corollary 6.2), character injectivity on the Grothendieck group, and field-independence of Specht formal characters also need suppliers. Integral-lattice arguments require finitely generated torsion-free modules over the PID integers and scalar-extension exactness.

**Proof obligations not silently closed.** Several all-equal-residue rank-three braid cases are left as exercises: Theorem 3.2 p.13, Theorem 3.3 p.18, Theorem 4.2 p.24, Theorem 4.3 p.27. Full scaffold proofs must either carry out these calculations or name exact proved suppliers. Corollary 2.2's “easy application” to spanning normal forms additionally needs a terminating rewriting argument. Distinguish `q=1` degenerate affine Hecke algebra from simply specializing all generators in the nondegenerate presentation; the latter's polynomial variables require a limiting/rescaling construction, not a name change. The type-A `e=2` double edge cannot use the single-edge KLR formulas. The Main Theorem is an algebra isomorphism/induced grading; the introduction explicitly says it does not immediately prove the full graded categorification conjecture.

## 5. Hecke/KL positivity: 1212.0791v2

**Reading evidence.** Section 1, pp.2–11, gives the three-way induction (Soergel character, hard Lefschetz, Hodge–Riemann), local/global form embedding, deformation and use of Rouquier complexes. Section 2, pp.11–14, proves the Lefschetz linear-algebra lemmas. Section 3, pp.14–23, gives Coxeter/Hecke conventions, graded bimodules, Bott–Samelson trace forms, Soergel character/hom formula and induced forms. Section 4, pp.23–25, proves the embedding theorem. Section 5, pp.25–28, uses the block Gram matrix and large-parameter deformation. Section 6, pp.28–43, develops minimal complexes, perverse filtrations, Rouquier linearity/positivity, Lefschetz factorization and the separate positive/zero-parameter hard-Lefschetz cases. Pages 43–45 finish references.

**Usable foundation and normalization.** Section 3.2 uses `H_s^2=1+(v^{-1}-v)H_s`, bar sends `v` to `v^{-1}` and `H_w` to `H_{w^{-1}}^{-1}`, with KL basis characterized by bar invariance and `H_w + sum_{y<w} v Z[v] H_y`. Remark 3.2 translates to the original convention: `v=q^{-1/2}`, `H_w=v^{length(w)}T_w`. Fix this convention before importing calculations. The elementary canonical-basis existence/uniqueness proof requires finite Bruhat intervals, triangular bar action and the Laurent decomposition into positive/negative powers; positivity is a distinct deep theorem.

**Useful verified small proof route.** Lemma 2.3 p.13 is an elementary substitute for weak Lefschetz: injective degree-one map `phi:V -> W(1)`, compatible Lefschetz forms, and Hodge–Riemann on `W` imply injectivity of `L^i:V^{-i}->V^i`. If `L^i phi(alpha)` is nonzero the claim follows by commutation; otherwise `phi(alpha)` is primitive and definiteness makes its self-pairing ` <alpha,L^i alpha>` nonzero. Symmetric graded dimensions then turn injectivity into isomorphism. This is a sound intermediate supplier once all its definitions are in place.

**Full prerequisite closure.** Coxeter exchange/deletion, Matsumoto and Bruhat/root positivity; a finite-dimensional **real reflection-faithful realization** with independent roots/coroots and dominant regular `rho` (§3.1); polynomial invariant-ring rank-two decomposition; graded tensor bimodules and shifts; additive idempotent completion and Krull–Schmidt; duality and graded Nakayama; supports/twisted-graph filtrations (these can be defined algebraically via annihilators, avoiding an otherwise silent sheaf prerequisite); Soergel classification, character/categorification and hom formula (Theorems 3.5–3.6 import [S4]); self-duality; symmetric bilinear forms, signature, primitive decompositions and continuity of eigenvalues; complexes, homotopies, radical quotient categories, cancellation of contractible summands and distinguished triangles; Rouquier inverse/braid invariance [Ro1] and Δ-splitting [LW]; descent of `B_x` to `(R,R^s)` bimodules [W] in Theorem 6.19; elementary `sl_2` Lefschetz-string representation theory.

**Boundaries and omitted steps.** Lemma 2.2 p.13 is explicitly a sketch requiring the reader's proof; supply the primitive decomposition argument. The positivity theorem is proved over real characteristic zero with the specified realization; do not claim it transfers unchanged to modular indecomposable characters. The categorical radical is not just the Jacobson radical of one ring without justification. A bilinear form on a free module over a polynomial ring is “non-degenerate” here when it induces an isomorphism to the dual, stronger than injectivity (§3.3). Embedded bimodules throughout mean *direct summands*, not arbitrary submodules. Continuity of signatures requires nondegeneracy throughout the deformation interval. All three inductive statements must be advanced together to avoid circular use of Soergel's conjecture to establish its own form uniqueness.

## Scaffold integration recommendation

Build the elementary common language first: tensor-product universal property and coherence maps; algebras and coalgebras by maps; bialgebra compatibility derived from tensor representations; convolution, its unit/associativity and antipode as inverse; group/function/enveloping/finite noncommutative examples with relations checked; modules, comodules, duality and finite-dimensional restrictions. On the Hecke branch supply symmetric-group/Coxeter word theory, rank-one deformation, basis/associativity proof, specialization, trace and representations before affine/cyclotomic or KL constructions. Join the branches by an **explicit** Hecke-satisfying braiding and its tensor action, not a claim that every Hecke algebra is naturally Hopf or every quasitriangular Hopf braiding is Hecke.
