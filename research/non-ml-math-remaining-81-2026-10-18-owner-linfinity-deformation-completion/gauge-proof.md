# Complete filtered gauge paths and actual deformation equivalences

Isolated proof proposal while batch43 is live. No canonical output is edited here. This supplies the gauge construction, rather than declaring an arbitrary equivalence relation on a solution set. Combine with `taylor/proof.md` and `exp-log/proof.md` for the full correspondence.

## 1. Filtration, coefficient convention and the exact gauge relation

Use the existing shifted convention W=V[1], |q_n|=1, characteristic zero, and additive positive filtration. If the given complete W has F¹W≠W, restrict to L=F¹W with its induced positive filtration: it is closed and complete, because any compatible cosets in L/F^p are compatible cosets in W/F^p whose F¹-coset is zero. The unique W-limit is therefore in F¹. All operations preserve L. Thus we may write L=F¹L without strengthening the original definition.

For a filtered vector space L define the completed polynomial coefficients and forms by

    L[t]^ = lim_p (L/F^pL)⊗k[t],
    L⊗^Ω = lim_p (L/F^pL)⊗k[t,dt], |t|=0, |dt|=1.

They are compatible polynomials modulo every quotient; their degrees need not have a common bound. Algebraic integration from0 to t is defined by ∫t^j dt=t^(j+1)/(j+1) in each quotient. Characteristic zero makes all denominators invertible, and integration commutes with reduction. Evaluation at0 or1 and differentiation are likewise quotientwise operations. No real interval, analytic convergence, or globally polynomial solution is asserted.

Put C(w)=Σ_{n≥1}q_n(w^n)/n! and q_1^w(a)=Σ_{n≥0}q_{n+1}(w^n,a)/n!, for w∈L⁰ and a∈L⁻¹. These are convergent by filtration. An elementary gauge path is a pair

    w(t)∈L⁰[t]^, a(t)∈L⁻¹[t]^,
    C(w(t))=0,  w'(t)=q_1^{w(t)}(a(t)).

Gauge equivalence is the equivalence relation generated by the endpoints of these pairs. This is also π₀ of the Maurer–Cartan polynomial-form construction, at the level of its1-skeleton; no Kan or rectification theorem is needed for that statement. It does not claim the concatenation of two polynomial paths is globally polynomial. Reflexivity has a=0; reversal is w(1−t),−a(1−t); finite chains give transitivity. The full operations and this explicit path construction, not just the zero locus C(w)=0, specify the relation.

## 2. Curved translation identities, proved locally

Work in the total-weight completed coaugmented symmetric coalgebra of `taylor/proof.md`. For w∈L⁰ the exponential e^w converges, is group-like, and multiplication M_w:x↦e^w x is a continuous coalgebra automorphism, since Δ(e^w x)=(e^w⊗e^w)Δx; its inverse is multiplication by e^(−w). It need not preserve the coaugmentation, which is precisely why its conjugated coderivation can have a constant term. This is an intermediate calculation, not a change to the uncurved L∞ definition.

Set Q^w=M_(−w) Q M_w. Then (Q^w)²=0. Expanding the unshuffle formula and selecting the distinguished q-block gives the actual Taylor components

    q_0^w=C(w),
    q_n^w(v_1,…,v_n)=Σ_{j≥0}q_{n+j}(w^j,v_1,…,v_n)/j!, n≥1.

For fixed positive-filtration inputs these sums converge; modulo F^p the input arity and every j are finite. The coefficient1/j! counts precisely the indistinguishable degree-zero w inputs. In particular Q^w(1)=C(w), while

    Q^w(a)=C(w)a+q_1^w(a).

Applying Q^w again and projecting to length one gives the fully typed curved identity

    (q_1^w)²(a)+q_2^w(C(w),a)=0.

Indeed the length-two term C(w)a contributes q_2^w(C(w),a), and the length-one term contributes q_1^w(q_1^w(a)); all remaining terms have length≥2 after projection. Applying (Q^w)² to1 similarly gives q_1^w(C(w))=0. These identities follow from an actual coderivation calculation rather than citing the unproved part of Dolgushev–Rogers Proposition2.1.

For a completed polynomial w(t), termwise differentiation modulo each F^p gives

    dC(w(t))/dt = q_1^{w(t)}(w'(t)).

There is no sign because |w|=|w'|=0. Thus a solution of w'=q_1^w(a) satisfies

    C(w)'=−q_2^w(C(w),a).

## 3. Existence and uniqueness of the filtered gauge flow

Given any w₀∈MC(L) and any prescribed a(t)∈L⁻¹[t]^, define

    w_0(t)=w₀,
    w_{j+1}(t)=w₀+∫_0^t q_1^{w_j(u)}(a(u))du.

The first difference lies in F¹. If x−y∈F^r, the difference q_1^x(a)−q_1^y(a) lies in F^(r+1): the q₁(a) terms cancel, and every remaining multilinear telescoping term contains x−y and a, with all other arguments in F¹. Therefore w_{j+1}−w_j∈F^(j+1). Each quotient stabilizes after finitely many iterations and is a genuine polynomial there, so completeness gives a unique compatible limit w(t) in L⁰[t]^ with w(0)=w₀. Differentiation/integration commute with the quotient maps, proving the ODE. Any other solution has the same integral equation; their difference starts in F¹ and is forced successively into every F^r by the same estimate, hence is zero by separation.

It remains to prove that this flow stays on the MC locus; that assertion is not built into the iteration. Let E(t)=C(w(t)). The previous section gives E'=−q_2^w(E,a), E(0)=0. Consequently

    E(t)=−∫_0^t q_2^{w(u)}(E(u),a(u))du.

Initially E∈F¹. If E∈F^r, the right side lies in F^(r+1), because a∈F¹. Induction gives E∈∩_rF^r=0. Thus w(t) is an elementary gauge path for every prescribed parameter, and every path is recovered by this unique flow from its own parameter and initial value. The filtration-raising estimate is the termination/convergence proof in each nilpotent quotient.

The exact form interpretation uses right-placed forms and z=w(t)−a(t)dt. With scalar tensor signs fixed in `taylor/proof.md`,

    C_Ω(z)=C(w)+(w'−q_1^w(a))dt.

Hence gauge paths are exactly MC elements over the completed polynomial de Rham interval. The Taylor theorem then proves every filtered L∞ morphism carries paths to paths, preserves their endpoints, and descends functorially to these gauge-equivalence classes.

## 4. The DGLA specialization with the exact shift signs

Let (g,d,[−,−]) be a complete filtered cohomological dg Lie algebra with d preserving filtration and brackets additive in filtration. Put L=g[1], with s:g→g[1] of degree−1, and define

    q_1(sx)=−s(dx),
    q_2(sx,sy)=(−1)^|x|s[x,y],
    q_n=0 for n≥3.

The displayed q₂ is graded symmetric: substituting Lie skewsymmetry gives q₂(sx,sy)=(−1)^((|x|−1)(|y|−1))q₂(sy,sx). The arity-one identity is d²=0. For arity two the three terms are

    q₁q₂(sx,sy)= (−1)^(|x|+1)s[dx,y]−s[x,dy],
    q₂(q₁sx,sy)= (−1)^|x|s[dx,y],
    (−1)^(|x|−1)q₂(sx,q₁sy)=s[x,dy],

so they cancel. For arity three, expanding the three unshuffles and the displayed q₂ sign gives the graded Lie Jacobi identity after a common invertible sign; equivalently substitute each q₂ formula and use [x,[y,z]]=[[x,y],z]+(−1)^(|x||y|)[y,[x,z]]. There are no terms in higher arity. Thus this is a filtered shifted L∞ algebra with no sign convention imported silently.

For R∈F¹g¹ and ξ∈F¹g⁰, w=sR and a=sξ have degrees0 and−1 and

    C(sR)=−s(dR+[R,R]/2),
    q_1^{sR}(sξ)=s(−dξ+[ξ,R]).

The shifted gauge-flow equation is exactly R'=−dξ+[ξ,R]. This fixes the sign needed to compare with conjugation of D+R in the concrete deformation problem.

## 5. Variable-parameter paths equal actual coalgebra-automorphism orbits

Use the complete coefficient-filtered endomorphism algebra E, its coderivations g, and fixed D of `exp-log/proof.md`; d=[D,−]. For a completed polynomial ξ(t)∈F¹g⁰[t]^, solve

    U'=ξ(t)U, U(0)=identity,
    V'=−Vξ(t), V(0)=identity

by the same integral Picard iteration in E[t]^, starting at identity. Multiplication by ξ raises filtration, so successive differences gain one coefficient order and every quotient is polynomial after finitely many iterations. Differentiating VU gives0, and similarly uniqueness or the same equation gives UV=identity. Thus U(t) is invertible with inverse V(t).

Let B(t)=ΔU(t)−(U(t)⊗U(t))Δ. Using the coderivation equation for ξ(t) and the product rule, in the explicitly completed tensor target one obtains

    B'=(ξ⊗1+1⊗ξ)B, B(0)=0.

Its coefficient-order induction forces B=0. The equations U(1_C)=1_C and εU=ε follow in the same way from ξ(1_C)=0 and εξ=0. Hence every U(t), including U(1), is an actual continuous coaugmented coalgebra automorphism congruent to identity modulo the coefficient ideal; there is no appeal to commutative exponential identities for this time-ordered solution.

The conjugate K(t)=U(t)(D+R₀)U(t)^−1 satisfies K'=[ξ,K]. Therefore R(t)=K(t)−D satisfies R'=−dξ+[ξ,R] and its initial value is R₀. Uniqueness of the filtered gauge-flow equation identifies it with every DGLA gauge path having the same parameter. Thus endpoints of all such paths are genuinely conjugate, and finite chains remain conjugate by composing the allowed automorphisms.

Conversely let an allowed coalgebra automorphism G carry D+R₀ to D+R₁. The noncommutative logarithm proof gives ξ=log G∈F¹g⁰. The actual path U(t)=exp(tξ) is polynomial modulo every coefficient quotient, is a coalgebra automorphism, and has endpoints identity,G. Its conjugation path has parameter ξ and joins R₀ to R₁. Thus actual deformation equivalence, independently defined as coalgebra conjugacy, is exactly the gauge relation proved above. Combined with (D+R)²=dR+[R,R]/2, this gives the promised non-tautological MC/deformation correspondence, including every allowed equivalence, not merely those guessed to exponentiate.
