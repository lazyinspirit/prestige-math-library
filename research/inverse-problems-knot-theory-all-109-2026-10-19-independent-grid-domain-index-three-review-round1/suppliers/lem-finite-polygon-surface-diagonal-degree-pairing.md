## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Supply a closed oriented smooth surface $\Sigma$ with an orientation-compatible finite polygon presentation whose paired boundary word is $\prod_{r=1}^h A_rB_rA_r^{-1}B_r^{-1}$, or the sphere presentation when $h=0$. This presentation includes its quotient map and finite subdivision into smooth face pieces; no surface classification theorem is assumed. Let $\Delta\subset\Sigma\times\Sigma$ be the complex diagonal for any compatible Riemann surface structure, and let $\mathcal O(\Delta)$ have its usual coordinate-difference section.

There are continuous circle-valued maps $a_r,b_r:\Sigma\to S^1$ with edge degrees dual to the ordered side loops. Write the same letters for their pulled-back integral degree-one circle classes, and $\omega$ for the positive degree-two face class. Their products satisfy $a_r\smile b_s=\delta_{rs}\omega$, $b_r\smile a_s=-\delta_{rs}\omega$, and all $a_ra_s,b_rb_s$ products vanish. For every pair of smooth maps $f,g:C\to\Sigma$ from a closed oriented surface with supplied finite triangle data,
$$\deg((f,g)^*\mathcal O(\Delta))=\deg f+\deg g-\sum_{r=1}^h\left\langle f^*a_r\smile g^*b_r-f^*b_r\smile g^*a_r,[C]\right\rangle.$$
Degrees of line bundles use finite face winding; map degree uses the positive polygon face generator, or equivalently signed inverse images of a regular value. In particular
$$\deg(f^*T\Sigma)=(2-2h)\deg f.$$
The formula permits $f=g$ or constant maps and does not interpret a nonisolated coincidence set as a finite transverse count. The supplied polygon is essential input to this choice-safe statement. No arbitrary-surface polygonal normal form, universal coefficient theorem or general Kunneth theorem is imported.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual finite polygon, its oriented edge labels and face, the complex diagonal line, and the two maps from the supplied finite source.

[F1] Finite relative-source cellular approximation and cellular homotopy require no choice. Only that finite clause is used here. ([[thm-cellular-approximation-for-maps-of-cw-pairs]])

[F2] Finite simplicial approximation after subdivision is proved by finite star choices and common-carrier homotopies. ([[thm-finite-simplicial-approximation-for-maps-of-pairs]])

[F3] Cup pullback is natural; cup product is graded commutative on classes without AC. Their proofs use explicit cochain formulas and a prescribed diagonal homotopy. ([[prop-cup-product-is-natural-unital-and-associative]], [[thm-singular-cohomology-is-graded-commutative]])

[F4] Finite graph sections and face windings define line degree, with tensor addition, frame independence and the signed isolated-zero interpretation. The ordinary diagonal line is locally defined by $z_1-z_2$. ([[lem-symmetric-product-quotient-determinant-has-vandermonde-ramification]])

## Proof

1.1 Give each chosen side loop its usual circle coordinate. Map one positive side loop once around $S^1$ and all other loops constantly to one, using the reverse parametrization on its paired negative side. This defines a map of the edge graph to $S^1$. Its composite with the polygon attaching boundary has winding zero, because every side occurs once in each direction. Its argument therefore has a continuous periodic real lift along that boundary: subdivide into finitely many proper circle arcs and concatenate their arguments. Extend that real function over the polygon, for example on a radial collar followed by a constant center, and exponentiate it. This extends the graph map to $\Sigma$. Carry this out for the $2h$ edges to obtain $a_r,b_r$. No infinite selection is used. A circle class can be represented explicitly by assigning to any singular path its lifted argument increment minus the difference of the principal arguments of its endpoints, divided by $2\pi$. The path lift is constructed by finite short-arc subdivisions; refinements give the same lift. On a singular triangle its boundary winding is zero, since the triangle contracts, so this cochain is closed. Thus these maps supply actual integral singular cocycles, without invoking UCT to assert their existence. [given, construct, algebra]

1.2 We give the finite comparison test only for the supplied polygon and its finite products, not an arbitrary finite CW complex. These targets admit compatible finite triangulations. Around the quotient polygon vertex choose a small disk formed by its cyclic corner sectors; along each remaining paired side choose a thin rectangular band; the remaining face is a disk. Subdivide the disk, band and face boundaries at their finitely many common endpoints and triangulate them with matching boundary subdivisions. This gives a finite triangle presentation; a further subdivision separates repeated edge endpoints. Products have finite triangulations by subdividing each product of ordered simplices by its monotone lattice paths. The original vertex, edge and face skeleta remain subpolyhedra. On their edge graph choose a nonzero line section by finite vertex/edge interpolation and frame its line on every characteristic two-disk. Its boundary winding is an integer $c(e^2)$. Extend that graph section to the refined edge graph, still nonzero; the finite triangle windings then sum to $c(e^2)$ on every original face, by cancellation on internal edges. A graph-section change changes these integers by its edge argument coboundary; a disk-frame change contributes zero. This gives the actual line-degree cochain on the refined two-skeleton. Here is the needed comparison with cup cochains, with no UCT. If a second integer two-cochain has the same total on every original two-cell, their difference $t$ has zero total on each disk. Set a one-cochain $b$ to zero on the original edge graph. In a triangulated two-disk, the dual graph of the triangles joined across interior edges is connected. Choose a finite spanning tree, root it at one triangle, and eliminate leaves: assign $b$ on the edge dual to the leaf-parent edge so that its coboundary cancels the leaf's remaining $t$ value. Continue toward the root. The root value is zero because the total of $t$ is zero; the assignments therefore give $\delta b=t$, with $b$ zero on the disk boundary. Distinct cells' assignments glue since their boundary values are zero. For a continuous finite surface map, [F1] moves its graph into the original one-skeleton and its faces into the original two-skeleton. Use [F2] on that finite polyhedral pair to obtain a finite simplicial map, with its graph still in the one-skeleton. Pullback preserves the cochain coboundary; its evaluation on the closed source cycle is zero. Hence cochains with the same original face totals have identical pullback evaluations. Line degree equals the evaluation of the triangle winding cochain: choose the target graph section for its pullback on the source graph and sum the signed triangle windings, cancelling all interior edges. Homotopies preserve it by finite disk-times-interval frames. Cup cochains below are actual singular cochains restricted to these finite characteristic triangles, so this finite correction applies to them as well. No global singular-cycle splitting or assertion that every finite CW complex is triangulable enters this test. [F1, F2, F4, construct, algebra]

2.1 The finite fan computes their products and its signs. Triangulate the polygon from its center. On the block $A_r,B_r,A_r^{-1},B_r^{-1}$, for any two degree-one cocycles $u,v$, let their edge periods be $u_A,u_B,v_A,v_B$. If $t$ is the value of $u$ on the initial radial edge, the cocycle equation gives successive radial values $t,t+u_A,t+u_A+u_B,t+u_B,t$. The front/back cup formula on the four oriented fan triangles consequently contributes $tv_A+(t+u_A)v_B-(t+u_B)v_A-tv_B=u_Av_B-u_Bv_A$. Adjacent radial terms cancel and every paired side is the same singular edge traversed with opposite signs, so the signed fan is a genuine surface cycle. Summing the blocks gives the displayed standard cup matrix. For the needed pullback evaluations, agreement on this positive face suffices by the explicit triangle-cochain correction of step1.2: a difference with zero face total is a coboundary on its finite two-skeleton, with zero boundary-edge primitive. Thus the displayed cup values and their pullbacks are established by actual finite cocycles rather than an asserted singular/cellular comparison or UCT splitting. The sphere has no one-edges and its single positive face has the same degree-two normalization. [F1, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Apply this test to the finite product CW structure on $\Sigma\times\Sigma$. Its degree-two cells are the two faces $e^2\times v,v\times e^2$ and the $2h\cdot2h$ products of oriented side edges. The cellular boundary is zero in every degree: the surface edge and face boundaries are zero, and the oriented product boundary is $\partial(P\times Q)=\partial P\times Q+(-1)^{\dim P}P\times\partial Q$. This formula follows by orienting each boundary face of its finite characteristic product disks; there is no Kunneth argument. These product disks have finite triangulations, so the test of step1.2 applies. In particular a two-cochain here is completely determined by its values on those finitely many generators. The classes $p_1^*\omega,p_2^*\omega$ evaluate as one on their own face and zero on the other generators. The products $p_1^*u\smile p_2^*v$ evaluate on an edge product $E\times F$ as $u(E)v(F)$: subdivide its oriented square by one diagonal and apply the front/back formula, with the constant vertex terms zero. They vanish on the two face generators. These computations describe the entire degree-two test without a general cross-product isomorphism. [F3, step 2.1, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

4.1 The diagonal line has value one on each face generator. Indeed restrict it to $\Sigma\times\{q\}$ for an interior point $q$, moving the fixed vertex to $q$ by a path if necessary. The section has one simple positive zero at $q$, so [F4] gives degree one; the other factor is identical. For the product of two side loops choose their usual smooth representatives in the polygon handle bands. The $A_r,B_r$ representatives meet once positively in their common handle; different handle bands are disjoint, and like curves have disjoint parallel representatives. These representatives are obtained by joining the paired side midpoints through the polygon bands and rounding their finitely many corners. Moving a representative slightly in its collar gives transverse intersections without changing the loop class. On a product of parameter circles, the diagonal section is locally $z(E(s))-z(F(t))$, with derivative columns $E'(s),-F'(t)$. Hence its signed zero sum is minus the oriented crossing count of the two curves. The values on $A_r\times B_s$ and $B_r\times A_s$ are therefore $-\delta_{rs}$ and $+\delta_{rs}$, and all like-edge products give zero. This computes all the product generators of step3.1, including the case of the same loop, where parallel representatives give zero. [F4, step 2.1, step 1.2, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Steps3.1–4.1 identify the diagonal obstruction cochain with the degree-two class $$p_1^*\omega+p_2^*\omega-\sum_r(p_1^*a_r\smile p_2^*b_r-p_1^*b_r\smile p_2^*a_r).$$ This can also be read purely as equality of its values on the finite two-cell list; step1.2 then proves its pullback degree equality. Pull back by $(f,g)$, use cup naturality [F3], and evaluate on the oriented source cycle. The two face terms are exactly $\deg f,\deg g$ by the signed-face definition of degree and the same finite evaluation test. This gives the asserted formula for arbitrary smooth $f,g$, without requiring their coincidence set to be transverse or finite. [F3, step 1.2, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Restrict the diagonal line to the diagonal itself. Its normal complex line is canonically $T\Sigma$: in a surface chart the normal coordinate is $z_1-z_2$, and under $w=h(z)$ its first normal derivative on the diagonal is $h'(z)$, precisely the tangent transition. Thus the derivative of the canonical diagonal section identifies its restricted line with $T\Sigma$. Set $g=f$ in step5.1. By step2.1, naturality and graded commutativity, each pair contributes $\langle f^*(a_r\smile b_r-b_r\smile a_r),[C]\rangle=2\deg f$. Hence its degree is $(2-2h)\deg f$, as claimed. Constant maps have degree zero and zero pulled-back products; $h=0$ is the empty sum; identical maps were treated by a bundle restriction, not an invented finite coincidence count. The only polygon choice was the supplied finite presentation, and all comparisons and extensions used finite cells. This completes the choice-safe diagonal and tangent degree calculation with its stated input scope. [F3, F4, step 2.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
