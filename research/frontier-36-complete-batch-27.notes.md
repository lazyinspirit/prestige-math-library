## Mathematical contracts and dependency audit

The page uses `n(r,a)` for closed discs, counts multiplicity, and regularizes a central a-point by `n(0,a) log r`. For the proximity it fixes the diameter-one chordal formula `δ(w,a)=|w-a|/(sqrt(1+|w|²)sqrt(1+|a|²))` and the analogous value at infinity. The published `def-chordal-metric-riemann-sphere` uses chord length on a **unit** sphere, hence diameter two. Our formula is one half of that chord length and is defined algebraically in the local item; no theorem from the unlisted sphere page is used as a premise. This convention is load-bearing in the exact First Main Theorem constant. Boundary a-points and poles give integrable angular logarithms, and `N` is continuous across their moduli. The Poisson–Jensen theorem starts at regular radii; the radial-limit clause is proved from local logarithmic integrability. Its divisor signs and multiplicities follow from the explicit disc Green factor. At the centre, a Laurent coefficient `c_a`, not `f(0)-a`, handles both a zero and a pole. For finite `a`, the scaffold derives the exact identity `m(r,a)+N(r,a)=T(r,f)+½log(1+|a|²)-log|c_a|`; at infinity it is the definition of `T`. Thus no `a=∞` analogy hides a missing proof.

I inspected the statements and relevant proofs of the published divisor and analytic suppliers, including `thm-isolated-zeros-holomorphic-function`, `thm-zero-order-factorization-holomorphic-function`, `thm-pole-characterizations`, `thm-poles-meromorphic-function-are-discrete-and-countable`, `thm-laurent-expansion-annulus`, `thm-poisson-representation-for-disc-harmonic-functions`, `thm-poisson-integral-solves-the-disc-dirichlet-problem`, and `cor-cauchy-estimates-taylor-coefficients`.
