
ITEM def-qa-37-certified-matrix-input


## Definition

Fix $d\in\{2,5,8\}$ and put
$$SU(d)=\{T\in M_d(\mathbb C):T^*T=I_d,\ \det T=1\}.$$
A **certified $SU(d)$ matrix input** is an exact target $T\in SU(d)$ together
with a deterministic Cauchy-name procedure which, on unary input $1^b$ for
$b\ge1$, returns a rational complex matrix $Q_b$ such that each real and
imaginary coordinate of each entry differs from the corresponding coordinate
of $T$ by at most $2^{-b}$. The interface also specifies a worst-case bit-time
bound $t(b)$ for the query. A **polynomial-time name** has
$t(b)\le(b+1)^c$ for some fixed integer $c\ge1$. The target is the matrix
specified by the entire Cauchy name; if two targets satisfy the same bounds,
every coordinate difference is at most $2^{1-b}$ for all $b$, hence is zero.
No individual $Q_b$ is required to be unitary or special unitary. A requested tolerance is a positive binary
rational and its numerator/denominator encoding length is recorded
separately. For $d=2$ this specializes the unary-name convention of
[[def-qc-effective-unitary-matrix-input]]. For $d=5,8$ it is the same
entrywise interface in the fixed block dimensions; no dimension is input to
an algorithm as an unbounded parameter.

ITEM lem-qa-37-product-error


## Statement

Let $U_1,\ldots,U_m$ and $V_1,\ldots,V_m$ be unitary operators on the same
finite-dimensional Hilbert space, and suppose
$\|U_j-V_j\|_{\rm op}\le\epsilon_j$ with $\epsilon_j\ge0$. With empty
products equal to the identity and empty sums equal to zero,
$$\|U_m\cdots U_1-V_m\cdots V_1\|_{\rm op} \le\sum_{j=1}^m\epsilon_j.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Two finite lists of unitaries on one finite-dimensional Hilbert
space and their individual error bounds.

[F1] The operator norm is induced by the Hilbert-space norm, and a unitary
preserves that vector norm.
[[def-operator-norm-on-a-finite-dimensional-inner-product-space]]
[[def-linear-isometry-and-orthogonal-or-unitary-operator]]

[F2] The operator norm is submultiplicative. For a unitary $Q$, its norm and
the norm of $Q^*$ are both $1$ by [F1], so submultiplicativity gives
$\|QX\|_{\rm op}=\|X\|_{\rm op}=\|XQ\|_{\rm op}$ by applying the same
upper bound to $Q$ and the reverse bound to $Q^*$.
[[cor-operator-norm-submultiplicative-and-t-star-t-identity]]

## Proof

1.1 If $m=0$, both products are $I$, so their difference is zero and the empty sum is zero. This is the claimed bound in the empty case. [given, algebra]

2.1 For $m\ge1$, insert and subtract mixed products to obtain $U_m\cdots U_1-V_m\cdots V_1 =\sum_{j=1}^m U_m\cdots U_{j+1}(U_j-V_j)V_{j-1}\cdots V_1.$ Expansion cancels adjacent terms and leaves exactly the left side. Each outer product is unitary and has norm one by [F1]; [F2] and the unitary invariance derived there show that each summand has norm at most $\epsilon_j$. The operator-norm triangle inequality then gives the stated sum. [F1, F2, given, algebra] ∎

ITEM thm-qa-37-output-distribution-error


## Statement

Let $U,V$ be unitary operators on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, let
$\rho$ be any density operator, and let $\mathsf M$ be any finite POVM. If
$\|U-V\|_{\rm op}\le\epsilon$, then the outcome distributions obtained by
measuring $U\rho U^*$ and $V\rho V^*$ have total-variation distance at most
$\epsilon$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Finite-dimensional unitaries $U,V$, a density operator $\rho$, a
finite POVM, and $\|U-V\|_{\rm op}\le\epsilon$.

[F1] For unit vectors $u,v$, pure-state trace distance is at most
$\|u-v\|$. [[lem-qa-21-pure-state-trace-distance-from-vector-error]]

[F2] Trace distance is one half the trace norm of the density-operator
difference. [[def-qc-trace-distance]]

[F3] The trace norm obeys the triangle inequality and positive homogeneity.
[[thm-qc-trace-norm-duality-and-norm-properties]]

[F4] A finite POVM's total-variation change is bounded by trace distance.
[[thm-qc-measurement-variation-at-most-trace-distance]]

[F5] Every finite-dimensional density operator has an orthonormal spectral
decomposition; its eigenvalues are nonnegative and sum to one.
[[thm-complex-spectral-theorem-for-normal-endomorphisms]]
[[def-qc-density-operator]]

[F6] The operator norm is induced by the Hilbert-space norm, and a unitary
preserves that vector norm.
[[def-operator-norm-on-a-finite-dimensional-inner-product-space]]
[[def-linear-isometry-and-orthogonal-or-unitary-operator]]

## Proof

1.1 First let $\rho=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|$ be pure with $\|\psi\|=1$. Then $U\psi,V\psi$ are unit vectors by [F6] and $\|U\psi-V\psi\|=\|(U-V)\psi\|\le\epsilon$ by the operator-norm definition in [F6]. By [F2] and the pure-state estimate in [F1], the output trace distance is at most $\epsilon$. [F1, F2, F6, given, algebra]

2.1 For a general density operator, [F5] gives $\rho=\sum_jp_j|\psi_j\rangle\langle\psi_j|$ with $p_j\ge0$ and $\sum_jp_j=1$. Unitary conjugation preserves this mixture. The trace-norm triangle inequality and homogeneity [F3], applied to the sum of output projector differences, bound the output trace distance by $\sum_jp_j\epsilon=\epsilon$ using step 1.1. [F2, F3, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Apply the POVM variation bound [F4] to the two output density operators from step 2.1. Their total variation distance is at most their trace distance, hence at most $\epsilon$. [F4, step 2.1] ∎

ITEM def-qa-37-anyon-task-and-promise


## Definition

Work in a fixed unitary braided fusion category $\mathcal C$ with the
representative set $\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal C)$ of
[[def-qc-tqc-fusion-tree-basis]]. Fix ordered inputs
$a_1,\ldots,a_n\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal C)$, $n\ge1$, a
parenthesized fusion tree, and a total charge
$c\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal C)$ for which the fusion space is nonzero. Simple charges, multiplicity bases, and
admissible fusion-tree vectors have the conventions of
[[def-qc-tqc-fusion-tree-basis]]. An encoded input subspace is specified by a
fixed-charge isometry as in [[def-qc-tqc-fixed-charge-encoding]]. Each task
instance supplies either an initial unit vector in that subspace as an abstract
quantum input, or a finite state-preparation procedure whose specified output
is that vector and whose preparation operations and ancillas are recorded.
The instance also gives a nonnegative rational error threshold and specifies
the correctness relation and metric to which it applies.

A **finite anyon decision task** is a promise pair $(Y,N)$ of disjoint sets of
binary strings with domain $D=Y\cup N$, together with a finite encoding of
admissible fusion-tree input labels; a solver must answer yes on $Y$ and no on
$N$, with no obligation outside $D$. A **finite anyon search task** is a domain
$D$ and a finite-string witness relation, with a valid witness required only
for encoded inputs in $D$. The input record states the number and order of
strands, the finite alphabet of positive and inverse Artin generators
available to the solver, any prescribed finite braid word, and the finite set
of adjacent fusion-measurement locations.
For distinct input charges, an adjacent exchange has the typed unitary
F/R action from the current ordered-list fusion space to the space for the
list with those charges exchanged; track that list after every generator.
These colored maps and their Artin relations are supplied by
[[thm-qc-tqc-gauge-covariance]]. If a single-space $B_n$ representation is
required for the physical-interface record
[[def-qc-tqc-physical-braid-interface]], use the finite orthogonal direct
sum of fusion spaces over all distinct orderings of the fixed input
multiset, with the same total $c$. Each generator maps each summand to its
exchanged-list summand by the typed unitary; its inverse reverses that
map. The colored Artin relations on each summand give the relations on
the direct sum, hence a unitary $B_n$ action. Record the occupied summand
and interpret measurements in its declared fusion-tree basis. Identical
charges give the usual single fixed-space action. The physical-interface
record specifies the mathematical action and separately states its
physical realization hypotheses. The
state-preparation field is an abstract task input and does not assert that a
physical device prepares the state or realizes the braid. Input length and
correctness-on-promises follow [[def-qa-task-and-promise-domain]].

ITEM def-qa-37-fusion-input-encoding


## Definition

In the unitary braided fusion category $\mathcal C$ fixed by
[[def-qa-37-anyon-task-and-promise]], use its fixed representative set
$\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal C)$. Fix $n\ge1$, ordered inputs
$a_1,\ldots,a_n\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal C)$, a total charge
$c\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal C)$ with nonzero fusion space, and
one parenthesized binary tree. Every charge label below belongs to this
same representative set. An **encoded fusion input** consists of the
finite tree shape, a simple-charge label on each internal edge, and a
multiplicity-basis index at each trivalent vertex. A label is **admissible**
exactly when every chosen vertex index belongs to the specified basis of its
multiplicity space $V_{xy}^z$; the allowed local vertex data and resulting
orthonormal fusion-tree vectors are those of
[[def-qc-tqc-fusion-tree-basis]]. The input encoding uses fixed self-delimiting
binary codes for charge labels and nonnegative multiplicity indices, with
out-of-range labels rejected as invalid encodings. It also records the total
charge and the selected computational subspace isometry required by
[[def-qa-37-anyon-task-and-promise]], as in
[[def-qc-tqc-fixed-charge-encoding]]. The tree labels and isometry are finite
classical data; they do not themselves specify or prepare the initial encoded
quantum state, which is the separate state/preparation field in the task
record.

ITEM def-qa-37-resource-cost-model


## Definition

For a finite anyon solver instance and one run, record separately the tuple
$$\bigl(L_{\rm in},\ N_{\rm any},\ |b|,\ N_{\rm prep},\ N_{\rm anc},\ N_{\rm meas},\ B_{\rm out},\ b_{\max},\ t_{\rm cl},\ T_H,\ \epsilon_{\rm op},\epsilon_{\rm tr},\epsilon_{\rm meas},\ell_{\rm leak},\ p_{\rm leak},\ N_{\rm phase},\ t_{\rm phase}\bigr).$$
Here $L_{\rm in}$ is the complete finite classical input-description bit
length, including the declared task, state/preparation descriptions when
classically encoded, and tolerance descriptions; a directly supplied quantum
input is not assumed to have a finite classical coordinate description;
$N_{\rm any}$ is the number of ordered simple-charge strand labels in the
fusion-space instance; and $|b|$ is the number of braid-generator occurrences
(inverse generators count as one). $N_{\rm prep}$ counts elementary
operations in the declared preparation procedure, while $N_{\rm anc}$ counts
the ancillary anyons or other physical ancilla systems used by preparation or
computation. If the initial state is supplied directly as an abstract quantum
input with no preparation interface, $N_{\rm prep}$ is “not applicable”, not
silently set to zero; $N_{\rm anc}$ still records any declared solver ancillas
(zero if none, or “not applicable” if no physical ancilla model is supplied).
$N_{\rm meas}$ is the number of fusion measurements,
and $B_{\rm out}$ is the total bit length of their classical outcome record
under its declared finite encoding (zero when no measurement occurs). The
largest unary precision query to a certified matrix name is $b_{\max}$; $t_{\rm cl}$
is the classical bit time used by search and postprocessing. $T_H$ is the total
Hamiltonian evolution time over the run in the declared physical time unit;
it is “not applicable” for an abstract braid-only model without Hamiltonian
evolution. The error coordinates $\epsilon_{\rm op},\epsilon_{\rm tr},
\epsilon_{\rm meas},\ell_{\rm leak},p_{\rm leak}$ are respectively a stated
operator-norm approximation bound, an independent in-band transport bound,
an implementation-to-ideal-measurement total-variation bound, and leakage
amplitude and probability bounds. $N_{\rm phase},t_{\rm phase}$ count phase
corrections and their classical control cost. Circuit description and
elementary wire costs follow [[def-qc-circuit-cost-model]], while classical
input bit cost follows [[def-qa-classical-bit-cost]] and finite nonoracle
gates follow [[def-qa-nonoracle-gate-cost]]. An inapplicable coordinate is
marked “not applicable”; it is not assigned value zero. Unless an interface
explicitly states a bound, no braid length, preparation operation, ancilla,
matrix precision, measurement, Hamiltonian time, search step, or phase
correction is free. The tuple is a bookkeeping convention; it does not assert
any of its error or runtime bounds.
