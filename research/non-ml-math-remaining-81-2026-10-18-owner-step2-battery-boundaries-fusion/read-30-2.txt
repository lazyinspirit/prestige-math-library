
ITEM def-qc-tqc-toric-excitations


## Definition

For a nonzero vector $\psi$ that is a simultaneous eigenvector of all toric
checks, its **e syndrome** is the set of vertices $q$ for which
$A_q\psi=-\psi$, and its **m syndrome** is the set of faces $f$ for which
$B_f\psi=-\psi$. A violated star is called an e defect and a violated
plaquette is called an m defect. These are finite stabilizer-syndrome labels;
the names do not assume that a material contains localized physical
quasiparticles.

The word **syndrome** follows the check-eigenvalue convention of
[[def-qc-stabilizer-syndrome-and-logical-operators]].

The checks are Hermitian involutions, as verified below; thus their
eigenvalues on any nonzero simultaneous eigenvector are indeed signs.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The toric checks on the finite edge register and their simultaneous-eigenvalue convention.

[F1] [[def-qc-tqc-toric-checks]]: every toric check is a Hermitian involution.

[F2] [[def-qc-stabilizer-syndrome-and-logical-operators]]: a syndrome bit is $0$ for commutation sign $+1$ and $1$ for sign $-1$.

## Verification

1.1 A Hermitian involution has only eigenvalues $+1$ and $-1$. Therefore each check eigenvalue on the given nonzero simultaneous eigenvector is one of these two signs, so the two sets in the Definition are well-defined finite subsets of vertices and faces. [given, F1, algebra]

2.1 The listed negative-check sets use exactly the same sign-to-bit convention as [F2], with the vertex and face families kept separate. The words e and m are names for these finite syndrome labels; the Definition explicitly makes no material-quasiparticle assumption. [F2, step 1.1] ∎


ITEM lem-qc-tqc-toric-check-commutation


## Statement

All star and plaquette checks of [[def-qc-tqc-toric-checks]] commute. The
product of all stars is the identity, and the product of all plaquettes is
the identity.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The periodic $L\times L$ torus with $L\ge3$ and its star and plaquette checks.

[F1] [[def-qc-tqc-toric-checks]]: $A_{i,j}$ is $X$ on the four edges incident to $q_{i,j}$, and $B_{i,j}$ is $Z$ on the four edges of $f_{i,j}$.

[F2] [[thm-qc-pauli-commutator-is-symplectic-pairing]]: two Pauli operators commute when the mod-two symplectic pairing of their binary supports is zero; for an $X$-support and a $Z$-support, this pairing is their overlap parity.

[F3] [[def-qc-tqc-periodic-square-cellulation]]: every edge has two distinct endpoints and borders two distinct faces, with all incidences read modulo $L$.

## Proof

1.1 For a fixed vertex $q_{i,j}$, the only faces sharing its incident edges are $f_{i,j}, f_{i-1,j}, f_{i,j-1}, f_{i-1,j-1}$. Their boundaries overlap the star support respectively in $\{h_{i,j},v_{i,j}\}$, $\{h_{i-1,j},v_{i,j}\}$, $\{h_{i,j},v_{i,j-1}\}$, and $\{h_{i-1,j},v_{i,j-1}\}$; every other face has empty overlap. Hence every star-plaquette overlap has even size. By [F2], each star commutes with each plaquette. Two stars commute because all their nonidentity factors are $X$; two plaquettes commute because all their nonidentity factors are $Z$. [given, F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 By [F3], every edge has exactly two endpoints, so in the product of all stars its $X$ factor occurs twice. Since $X^2=I$, the total product is $I$. Every edge borders exactly two faces, so the same argument with $Z^2=I$ gives $\prod_{i,j}B_{i,j}=I$. The first total product is also $\prod_{i,j}A_{i,j}=I$. [F1, F3, algebra] ∎


ITEM lem-qc-tqc-toric-cycle-quotient


## Statement

Let $Z_1$ be the space of primal edge chains with even incidence at every
vertex, and let $B_1$ be the span of the face boundaries. Then $B_1\subseteq
Z_1$, the quotient $Z_1/B_1$ has dimension two, and the two winding
parities give coordinates on that quotient. The same statement holds for
dual edge chains, with dual vertex boundaries in place of face boundaries.
The horizontal loop $\gamma_h=\sum_i h_{i,0}$ has winding pair $(1,0)$,
and the vertical loop $\gamma_v=\sum_j v_{0,j}$ has pair $(0,1)$; their dual
counterparts give the corresponding basis of the dual quotient.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The periodic square cellulation for $L\ge3$. Here $\mathbb F_2=\{0,1\}$ with addition modulo two, and $\mathbb F_2^S$ is the coordinate vector space on a finite set $S$.

[F1] [[def-qc-tqc-periodic-square-cellulation]]: there are $L^2$ vertices and faces and $2L^2$ edges, with horizontal and vertical edges indexed periodically.

[F2] [[lem-qc-tqc-toric-incidence-ranks]]: the span of face boundaries has dimension $L^2-1$, and the vertex-edge and dual incidence graphs are connected.

## Proof

1.1 The primal vertex-edge graph has the explicit spanning tree $T=\{h_{i,0}:0\le i<L-1\}\cup\{v_{i,j}:i\in\mathbb Z_L,\ 0\le j<L-1\}$. The horizontal edges form a path through row zero, and each other row is attached to that path by one vertical column path; hence $T$ is connected and has no cycle. For each edge $e\notin T$, add $e$ to the unique tree path between its endpoints. This gives a cycle $\gamma_e$ containing $e$ and no other edge outside $T$. These cycles are independent because the coefficient of each non-tree edge in a linear combination is its own coefficient. They span $Z_1$: subtract from any cycle the $\gamma_e$ corresponding to its non-tree edges; the remainder is an even-incidence chain supported on the tree, and a nonempty finite forest has a leaf of odd degree, so the remainder is zero. Thus $\dim Z_1=|E|-(|V|-1)=L^2+1$. [given, F1, construct, algebra]

2.1 Every face boundary has even incidence at each vertex, so $B_1\subseteq Z_1$. By [F2], $\dim B_1=L^2-1$. For a cycle $c$, define $w_h^k(c)=\sum_j c(h_{k,j})$ and $w_v^\ell(c)=\sum_i c(v_{i,\ell})$, with all sums in $\mathbb F_2$. Summing the even-incidence equations over column $k$ shows $w_h^k=w_h^{k-1}$; summing over row $\ell$ shows $w_v^\ell=w_v^{\ell-1}$. Thus the two parities $w_h,w_v$ are independent of the chosen cuts. Each face boundary crosses every such cut zero or two times, so both parities vanish on $B_1$. [given, F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The horizontal fundamental loop $\gamma_h=\sum_i h_{i,0}$ has $(w_h,w_v)=(1,0)$, and the vertical fundamental loop $\gamma_v=\sum_j v_{0,j}$ has $(w_h,w_v)=(0,1)$. Therefore no nonzero combination of these loops lies in $B_1$. A basis of $B_1$ together with $\gamma_h,\gamma_v$ is an independent family of $(L^2-1)+2=L^2+1$ cycles, which is the dimension of $Z_1$ from step 1.1. It is therefore a basis of $Z_1$, proving that the winding map identifies $Z_1/B_1$ with $\mathbb F_2^2$. The dual square cellulation has the same coordinate graph and incidence counts, so the identical argument proves its two winding coordinates. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎


ITEM def-qc-tqc-toric-hamiltonian


## Definition

For $L\ge3$ and couplings $J_e,J_m>0$, the finite toric-code Hamiltonian on
$\mathcal H_L$ is

$$H_L=-J_e\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb Z_L}A_{i,j}-J_m\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb Z_L}B_{i,j}.$$

The energy unit is fixed by $J_e,J_m$. This is a finite-volume operator
defined from the stabilizer checks; its definition makes no claim about a
material realization or a thermodynamic phase.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite register and commuting checks from [[def-qc-tqc-toric-checks]].

[F1] [[def-qc-tqc-toric-checks]]: every star and plaquette is a Hermitian involution supported on four edges.

[F2] [[lem-qc-tqc-toric-check-commutation]]: all checks commute.

## Verification

1.1 Each coefficient $-J_e$ and $-J_m$ is real, and each summand is self-adjoint by [F1]. Their finite sum is therefore self-adjoint. [given, F1, algebra]

2.1 The terms commute pairwise by [F2], so $H_L$ is a finite sum of commuting local check operators. Its locality follows from the four-edge supports in [F1]. [F1, F2, step 1.1] ∎


ITEM lem-qc-tqc-toric-joint-check-eigenspaces


## Statement

Delete the star $A_{0,0}$ and plaquette $B_{0,0}$ from the full check list,
and call the remaining $r=2L^2-2$ checks $g_1,\ldots,g_r$. They are
independent commuting Hermitian involutions. For each sign string
$s\in\mathbb F_2^r$, the operator

$$P_s=\prod_{a=1}^r\frac{I+(-1)^{s_a}g_a}{2}$$

is the orthogonal projection onto the corresponding joint eigenspace; the
$P_s$ are pairwise orthogonal and sum to the identity. The omitted checks
are determined by the retained ones, so every nonzero syndrome sector has
an even number of e defects and an even number of m defects.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The periodic toric checks for $L\ge3$ and the physical Hilbert space on $2L^2$ qubits.

[F1] [[def-qc-tqc-toric-checks]]: every check is a Hermitian involution supported on four distinct edge qubits.

[F2] [[lem-qc-tqc-toric-check-commutation]]: all checks commute, and the product of all stars and the product of all plaquettes are both $I$.

[F3] [[lem-qc-tqc-toric-incidence-ranks]]: after deleting one fixed star and one fixed plaquette, the remaining star and face-boundary supports are independent within their respective X and Z coordinates.

[F4] [[def-qc-tqc-toric-excitations]]: e and m defects are exactly the negative star and plaquette checks, respectively.

## Proof

1.1 A product of retained checks with identity binary support must have zero X support and zero Z support separately. By [F3], this forces the selected star subset and plaquette subset both to be empty, so the retained checks are independent. By [F1] and [F2] they are commuting Hermitian involutions. For a retained check $g_a$ and sign $s_a$, put $p_{a,s_a}=(I+(-1)^{s_a}g_a)/2$. Direct expansion using $g_a^*=g_a$ and $g_a^2=I$ gives $p_{a,s_a}^*=p_{a,s_a}$ and $p_{a,s_a}^2=p_{a,s_a}$. The checks commute, so their factors commute; hence $P_s$ is a self-adjoint idempotent, thus an orthogonal projection. [F1, F2, F3, given, algebra]

2.1 If $s\ne t$, choose an index $a$ with $s_a\ne t_a$. Then $p_{a,s_a}p_{a,t_a}=(I-g_a^2)/4=0$, so $P_sP_t=0$. Summing over all sign strings and distributing the finite product gives $\sum_sP_s=\prod_a(p_{a,0}+p_{a,1})=I$. Thus the ranges are pairwise orthogonal and their direct sum is the whole Hilbert space; zero projections are allowed. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 By [F2], the omitted star is the product of all retained stars, so on $\operatorname{ran}P_s$ its eigenvalue is the product of their signs; the product of the eigenvalues over all stars is therefore $+1$. A finite list of signs in $\{+1,-1\}$ has product $+1$ exactly when it contains an even number of minus signs. The same argument for the omitted plaquette gives even parity for the negative plaquette checks. By [F4], these are the e and m defect parities. The projectors therefore decompose the register into the stated full-check syndrome sectors. [F2, F4, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
