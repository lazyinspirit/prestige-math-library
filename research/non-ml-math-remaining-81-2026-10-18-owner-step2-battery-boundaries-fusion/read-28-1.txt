
ITEM cex-qc-tqc-algebraic-braid-not-unitary


## Statement refuted

The claim refuted is that an algebraic Yang–Baxter or Hecke relation by itself
guarantees that the resulting braid-group representation is unitary for some
positive-definite inner product.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the monoidal category of finite-dimensional complex vector spaces
with its usual tensor product, $X=\mathbb C$, $R=2\operatorname{id}_{X\otimes X}$,
and the three-strand braid group $B_3$.

[F1] [[def-yang-baxter-operator-on-an-object]]: a Yang–Baxter operator is an
invertible map $R:X\otimes X\to X\otimes X$ satisfying the cubic braid equation
on $X^{\otimes3}$.

[F2] [[thm-a-yang-baxter-operator-gives-braid-group-representations]]: such an
operator gives a homomorphism $\rho_n:B_n\to\operatorname{Aut}(X^{\otimes n})$
with $\rho_n(\sigma_i)=R_i$.

[F3] [[def-braid-group-by-the-artin-presentation]]: $B_3$ has generators
$\sigma_1,\sigma_2$ and relation $\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1=
\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2$.

[F4] [[def-generic-type-a-hecke-algebra]]: in the root normalization the
specialized Hecke relation is $(T_i-v)(T_i+1)=0$.

[F5] [[def-linear-isometry-and-orthogonal-or-unitary-operator]]: a unitary
operator on a finite-dimensional complex inner-product space is an invertible
linear isometry, hence preserves the induced norm.

[F6] [[def-real-and-complex-inner-product-space]]: the inner product is
linear in the first variable, conjugate-linear in the second, and positive
definite; these axioms give the scalar norm calculation used below.

## Counterexample
1.1 The map $R=2\operatorname{id}_{X\otimes X}$ is invertible. In the $\mathbb C$-linear strictification used by [F1], the transported map is $2\operatorname{id}$ on $X'\otimes X'$. Hence both local operators on $X'^{\otimes3}$ are $2\operatorname{id}$, and each side of the Yang–Baxter equation is $8\operatorname{id}$. Thus [F1] applies without identifying the tensor power with the original object $\mathbb C$. [given, F1, algebra]

2.1 By [F2] and [F3], $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$ act by $2I$ and their braid relator holds, since both sides are $8I$. At the root Hecke parameter $v=2$, the valid specialization $\mathbb Z[v^{\pm1}]\to\mathbb C$ sends each $T_i$ to $2$ and satisfies the root quadratic equation $(2-2)(2+1)=0$. Thus the scalar action also satisfies the Hecke quadratic relation in the stated normalization. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For any positive-definite inner product and any nonzero $z\in\mathbb C$, [F6] and nonnegativity of the norm give $\|2z\|=2\|z\|\ne\|z\|$. By [F5], the generator $2I$ is not unitary for any such inner product. Therefore these algebraic braid and Hecke relations do not force unitarity. [F5, F6, given, algebra] ∎
## Remarks

This example uses the repository's $v$-normalization $(T_i-v)(T_i+1)=0$.
It does not rule out unitary representations at other parameters or after a
positive dagger structure has been separately specified. The compact-group
averaging lemma [[lem-averaging-makes-a-finite-dimensional-representation-unitary]]
does not apply to the discrete infinite group $B_3$; the exponent-sum map
[[def-exponent-sum-and-writhe-of-a-braid]] maps it onto $\mathbb Z$.

ITEM def-qc-tqc-unitary-fusion-category


## Definition

Let $\mathcal C$ be a fusion category over $\mathbb C$ in the sense of
[[def-fusion-and-multifusion-category]]. A **dagger structure** assigns to every
morphism $f:X\to Y$ a morphism $f^\dagger:Y\to X$, conjugate-linearly, with
$(g\circ f)^\dagger=f^\dagger\circ g^\dagger$ and
$(f^\dagger)^\dagger=f$. A **unitary fusion category** is such a fusion
category equipped with this dagger so that every morphism space is a
finite-dimensional normed space, composition is submultiplicative,
$\|f^\dagger\|=\|f\|$, and $\|f^\dagger f\|=\|f\|^2$. Thus each
$\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal C}(X)$ is a finite-dimensional $C^*$-algebra.
Positive endomorphisms are the elements of its usual $C^*$-positive cone
(equivalently $u^\dagger u$ for an endomorphism $u$); in particular, if $c$ is
simple and $\operatorname{End}(c)=\mathbb C\operatorname{id}_c$, then
$\lambda\operatorname{id}_c$ is positive exactly when $\lambda\ge0$. For any
morphism $f:X\to Y$, $f^\dagger f$ is positive in $\operatorname{End}(X)$;
also require $f^\dagger f=0$ to imply $f=0$. Semisimple direct-sum
decompositions can be chosen orthogonally:
their inclusions $i_j$ satisfy $i_j^\dagger i_k=0$ for $j\ne k$,
$i_j^\dagger i_j=\operatorname{id}$, and $\sum_j i_j i_j^\dagger=\operatorname{id}$.
Tensor product preserves daggers:
$(f\otimes g)^\dagger=f^\dagger\otimes g^\dagger$. Its associator and unit
constraints are **unitary**, meaning $\alpha^\dagger=\alpha^{-1}$,
$\lambda^\dagger=\lambda^{-1}$, and $\rho^\dagger=\rho^{-1}$.

The underlying fusion category is rigid, so its evaluation and coevaluation
morphisms satisfy the duality zig-zag identities. Those morphisms are not
required to be unitary isomorphisms. A braiding is additional structure; when
it is specified as a **unitary braiding**, each component satisfies
$c_{X,Y}^\dagger=c_{X,Y}^{-1}$. Positivity and unitarity are extra structure,
not consequences of the fusion rules or of the braid relations alone.


ITEM def-qc-tqc-fusion-multiplicity-space


## Definition

Let $a,b,c$ be simple objects of a unitary fusion category
$\mathcal C$ ([[def-qc-tqc-unitary-fusion-category]]). The **fusion
multiplicity** is

$$N_{ab}^c:=\dim_{\mathbb C}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal C}(a\otimes b,c),$$

and the corresponding **fusion multiplicity space** is

$$V_{ab}^c:=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal C}(a\otimes b,c).$$

Since $c$ is absolutely simple
([[def-absolutely-simple-object]]), each $f,g\in V_{ab}^c$ has a unique scalar
$\langle f,g\rangle$ satisfying
$f\circ g^\dagger=\langle f,g\rangle\operatorname{id}_c$. This fixes the
inner-product convention to be linear in the first variable. The next local
theorem proves that this pairing is positive definite. No multiplicity-free
assumption is made: $N_{ab}^c$ may be any nonnegative integer.


ITEM def-qc-tqc-ribbon-modular-data


## Definition

A **unitary ribbon fusion category** is a unitary fusion category with a
unitary braiding and a ribbon structure in the sense of
[[def-twist-and-ribbon-structure]]. Thus the twist $\theta$ is a natural
automorphism of the identity satisfying the balancing identity and the
dual-compatibility condition of that root definition, and each component is
unitary: $\theta_X^\dagger=\theta_X^{-1}$. A ribbon structure is additional
data; it is not supplied by fusion rules or by a braid-group representation
alone.


ITEM def-qc-tqc-modularity


## Definition

Let $\mathcal C$ be a unitary ribbon fusion category. An object $x$ is
**transparent** if for every simple object $y$ the double braiding

$$c_{y,x}\circ c_{x,y}:x\otimes y\longrightarrow x\otimes y$$

is the identity. By naturality and additivity, the double braiding on a finite
direct sum is block diagonal with the double braidings on its simple summands;
therefore an object is transparent exactly when each simple summand is
transparent. Finite direct sums include the empty sum, so the zero object is
transparent. The category is **modular**, or **nondegenerate**, when its
transparent simple objects consist only of the tensor unit, equivalently when
its transparent objects are exactly the finite direct sums of the tensor unit.
This condition is additional to the existence of fusion spaces and a unitary
braid action.


ITEM def-qc-tqc-topological-spin


## Definition

For a simple charge $a$ in a unitary ribbon fusion category, the twist
component is an endomorphism $\theta_a\in\operatorname{End}(a)$. Since
$\operatorname{End}(a)=\mathbb C\operatorname{id}_a$, there is a unique scalar
$\vartheta_a\in\mathbb C$ such that

$$\theta_a=\vartheta_a\operatorname{id}_a.$$

The scalar $\vartheta_a$ is the **topological spin** of $a$. Unitarity of the
twist gives
$|\vartheta_a|^2\operatorname{id}_a=\theta_a^\dagger\theta_a=\operatorname{id}_a$;
since $\operatorname{id}_a\ne0$, $|\vartheta_a|=1$.


ITEM thm-qc-tqc-fusion-multiplicity-space-is-hilbert


## Statement

Let $a,b,c$ be simple objects in a unitary fusion category. The pairing on
$V_{ab}^c=\operatorname{Hom}(a\otimes b,c)$ defined by
$f\circ g^\dagger=\langle f,g\rangle\operatorname{id}_c$ is a
finite-dimensional positive-definite Hermitian inner product, linear in the
first variable and conjugate-linear in the second.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** a unitary fusion category $\mathcal C$ and simple objects $a,b,c$.

[F1] [[def-qc-tqc-unitary-fusion-category]]: morphism adjoints are conjugate-linear, reverse composition, and are involutive; each $f^\dagger f$ is $C^*$-positive; a positive scalar endomorphism of a simple object is a nonnegative real multiple of its identity; $f^\dagger f=0$ implies $f=0$; and morphism spaces are finite-dimensional.

[F2] [[def-absolutely-simple-object]]: for a simple charge $c$ in this complex fusion category, $\operatorname{End}(c)=\mathbb C\operatorname{id}_c$ and $\operatorname{id}_c\ne0$.

[F3] [[def-qc-tqc-fusion-multiplicity-space]]: $V_{ab}^c=\operatorname{Hom}(a\otimes b,c)$ and $\langle f,g\rangle$ is defined by $f\circ g^\dagger=\langle f,g\rangle\operatorname{id}_c$.

## Proof

**Given:** $\mathcal C,a,b,c$ as above.

1.1 The category is finite and semisimple, so $V_{ab}^c$ is a finite-dimensional complex vector space by [F1]. By [F2], every $f\circ g^\dagger\in\operatorname{End}(c)$ is a unique scalar multiple of the nonzero identity; hence the scalar pairing in [F3] exists and is unique. [F1, F2, F3, given]

1.2 For scalars $\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb C$ and $f,h,g\in V_{ab}^c$, linearity of composition gives $(\lambda f+\mu h)g^\dagger=\lambda fg^\dagger+\mu hg^\dagger$, while conjugate-linearity of dagger gives $f(\lambda g+\mu h)^\dagger=\bar\lambda fg^\dagger+\bar\mu fh^\dagger$. Taking the unique scalar coefficients from [F2] proves linearity in the first argument and conjugate-linearity in the second. Taking daggers of $fg^\dagger=\langle f,g\rangle\operatorname{id}_c$ gives $gf^\dagger=\overline{\langle f,g\rangle}\operatorname{id}_c$, so $\langle g,f\rangle=\overline{\langle f,g\rangle}$. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

1.3 For each $f\in V_{ab}^c$, $ff^\dagger=(f^\dagger)^\dagger\circ f^\dagger$ is positive in $\operatorname{End}(c)$ by [F1], so its scalar coefficient $\langle f,f\rangle$ is real and nonnegative. If $\langle f,f\rangle=0$, then $(f^\dagger)^\dagger\circ f^\dagger=0$; the nondegeneracy clause of [F1], applied to the morphism $f^\dagger$, gives $f^\dagger=0$ and then $f=0$. Thus $\langle f,f\rangle>0$ whenever $f\ne0$. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 The pairing is finite-dimensional by step 1.1, sesquilinear and Hermitian by step 1.2, and positive definite by step 1.3. If $V_{ab}^c=0$, scalar uniqueness in step 1.1 still holds and positive definiteness is vacuous because there are no nonzero vectors. These are exactly the inner-product axioms, with the stated linear-first convention. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3] ∎

ITEM thm-qc-tqc-ribbon-link-invariant


## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$
([[def-countable-choice]]). Let $\mathcal C$ be a unitary ribbon fusion
category, let $X$ be a simple color, and let $\beta\in B_n$ and
$\beta'\in B_m$ with $n,m\ge1$. If their blackboard-framed, $X$-colored
closures are isotopic as framed oriented links, then their root ribbon
evaluations satisfy $t_n(\beta)=t_m(\beta')$. No modularity assumption is
needed. Ordinary Markov stabilization is not a framed isotopy because it
changes the framing.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, a unitary ribbon fusion category $\mathcal C$,
a simple object $X$, and framed-isotopic blackboard closures of $\beta$ and
$\beta'$.

[A1] [[def-countable-choice]]: $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ asserts that every
countable family of nonempty sets has a choice function.

[F1] [[def-qc-tqc-ribbon-modular-data]]: the underlying category has a ribbon
structure, so it is a ribbon category in the root sense.

[F2] [[thm-ribbon-evaluation-is-an-invariant-of-framed-colored-links]]: under
$\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, in any ribbon category, framed-isotopic blackboard
closures of braids with the same color have equal root ribbon evaluations.

[F3] [[def-ribbon-evaluation-of-an-x-colored-closed-braid]]: for $n\ge1$,
$t_n(\beta)$ is the left categorical trace of the pivotal comparison composed
with the braid action; this is the root ribbon-evaluation notation.

## Proof
1.1 By [F1], $\mathcal C$ is a ribbon category with the same root duality, braiding, and twist structure required by [F2]. The color $X$ and the braids $\beta,\beta'$ meet [F2]'s common-color and positive-strand hypotheses. [F1, F2, given]

2.1 The assumed $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ supplies [F2]'s choice hypothesis by [A1]. Since the blackboard-framed closures are framed-isotopic, [F2] gives $t_n(\beta)=t_m(\beta')$; by [F3] these are exactly the root ribbon evaluations named in the Statement. The same root theorem states that ordinary Markov stabilization changes framing, and it uses no modularity hypothesis. [A1, F2, F3, given, step 1.1] ∎
