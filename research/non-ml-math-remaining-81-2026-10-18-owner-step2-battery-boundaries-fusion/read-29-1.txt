
ITEM def-qc-tqc-physical-braid-interface


## Definition

Let $V$ be a nonzero finite-dimensional complex inner-product space and let
$\rho:B_n\to U(V)$ be a fixed mathematical braid representation. A **physical
implementation record** for a braid $b\in B_n$ consists of all of the following
data and hypotheses:

1. a finite-dimensional physical state space identified with $V$ on a specified
   low-energy sector;
2. a $C^2$ path $H(s)$ of Hermitian endomorphisms, $0\le s\le1$, and a
   norm-continuous constant-rank orthogonal spectral projection $P(s)$ that
   is a coherently followed band in the sense of
   [[def-qa-36-finite-adiabatic-path-model]], including its local-contour
   continuation, and is separated from its complement by a uniform positive
   full gap;
3. an explicit initial preparation isometry into $\operatorname{ran}P(0)$,
   its declared preparation cost, and a stated slow-time schedule $T>0;$
4. a separate identification of the band's transported action for the chosen
   exchange path with $\rho(b)$, up to a stated scalar phase.

The first three clauses form an instance of the finite adiabatic path model
[[def-qa-36-finite-adiabatic-path-model]]. Under those hypotheses,
[[thm-qa-36-jrs-finite-adiabatic-bound]] bounds the deviation from ideal
adiabatic band following. That estimate does not establish that a material
realizes the fusion category, that the exchange path has the prescribed
holonomy, or that the initial state can be prepared at no cost. Locality,
temperature, quasiparticle separation, stray excitations, and tunnelling
require their own physical error model.

ITEM lem-qc-tqc-special-unitary-connected


## Statement

For every integer $n\ge1$, the matrix group
$$SU(n)=\{A\in M_n(\mathbb C):A^*A=I,\ \det A=1\}$$
is path connected.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $n\ge1$ and a matrix $A\in SU(n)$.

[F1] A normal complex matrix has an orthonormal eigenbasis.
[[thm-complex-spectral-theorem-for-normal-endomorphisms]]

[F2] For real $\alpha$, $|e^{i\alpha}|=1$, and
$e^{i\alpha}=e^{i\beta}$ exactly when $\alpha-\beta\in2\pi\mathbb Z$.
[[cor-complex-exponential-cartesian-form-modulus-and-eulers-identity]]
[[thm-kernel-and-fibres-of-complex-exponential]]

[F3] The complex exponential is entire, hence continuous.
[[thm-complex-exponential-is-entire-with-derivative-itself]]

## Proof

1.1 The matrix $A$ is unitary and therefore normal. By [F1], choose a unitary matrix $Q$ and real phases $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n$ such that $A=Q\operatorname{diag}(e^{i\alpha_1},\ldots,e^{i\alpha_n})Q^*$. Since $\det A=1$, [F2] gives $\sum_j\alpha_j=2\pi k$ for some integer $k$. Replace $\alpha_1$ by $\alpha_1-2\pi k$; the eigenvalue is unchanged and the sum of phases is now zero. This uses only finitely many eigenvalues. [given, F1, F2, choose, algebra]

2.1 Define $A(t)=Q\operatorname{diag}(e^{it\alpha_1},\ldots,e^{it\alpha_n})Q^*$ for $0\le t\le1$. Each $A(t)$ is unitary by [F2], and $\det A(t)=e^{it\sum_j\alpha_j}=1$, so $A(t)\in SU(n)$. The path is continuous by [F3], $A(0)=I$, and $A(1)=A$. Thus every element is joined to the identity, proving path connectedness. [given, F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra] ∎

ITEM def-qc-tqc-fixed-charge-encoding


## Definition

Fix a unitary fusion category $\mathcal D$ and the representative set
$\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal D)$ of [[def-qc-tqc-fusion-tree-basis]].
For an ordered list $x_1,\ldots,x_n\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal D)$,
$n\ge1$, a total charge $c\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal D)$, and a
parenthesized input tensor $T$, let $V=\operatorname{Hom}(T,c)\ne0$,
equipped with that definition's orthonormal fusion-tree inner product.
The identical-input case $x_1=\cdots=x_n=x$ retains the notation
$V=V(x^n\to c)$. Let
$\mathcal C=\mathbb C^k$ with its ordered orthonormal computational basis,
where $k\ge1$. A **fixed-charge encoding** is an isometry
$J:\mathcal C\to V$, so $J^*J=I_{\mathcal C}$. Its code subspace is
$J\mathcal C$, and its code projector is
$$P:=JJ^*:V\to V.$$
An encoding records a chosen subspace of a fusion space; it does not identify
that subspace with an object of the fusion category.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite-dimensional fusion space $V$, the computational space
$\mathcal C$, and an isometry $J:\mathcal C\to V$.

[F1] An isometry satisfies $J^*J=I_{\mathcal C}$.
[[def-linear-isometry-and-orthogonal-or-unitary-operator]]

[F2] The adjoint satisfies $(AB)^*=B^*A^*$ and $(J^*)^*=J$.
[[def-adjoint-of-a-linear-map-between-inner-product-spaces]]



## Proof

1.1 By [F2], $P^*=(JJ^*)^*=(J^*)^*J^*=JJ^*=P$. Also $P^2=JJ^*JJ^*=J(J^*J)J^*=JI_{\mathcal C}J^*=P$ by [F1]. Hence $P$ is an orthogonal projection. [given, F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 For every $u\in\mathcal C$, $P(Ju)=JJ^*Ju=Ju$ by [F1], so $J\mathcal C\subseteq\operatorname{ran}P$. Conversely $Pv=J(J^*v)$ lies in $J\mathcal C$ for every $v\in V$, so $\operatorname{ran}P\subseteq J\mathcal C$. Therefore $\operatorname{ran}P=J\mathcal C$. [given, F1, step 1.1, algebra] ∎

ITEM def-qc-tqc-logical-error-and-leakage


## Definition

Let $J:\mathcal C\to V$ be a fixed-charge encoding, $P=JJ^*$ its code
projector, $U\in U(\mathcal C)$ a logical target, and $W\in U(V)$ an ambient
unitary. Its zero-on-the-complement target extension is
$\widetilde U:=JUJ^*:V\to V$. Define the **logical block error** and
**leakage amplitude** by
$$\epsilon_{\mathrm{log}}(W,U):=\|PWP-\widetilde U\|_{\mathrm{op}}, \qquad \epsilon_{\mathrm{leak}}(W):=\|(I-P)WP\|_{\mathrm{op}}.$$
Both are finite operator norms. The first measures the compressed block
against the target; the second measures transfer from the code into its
orthogonal complement. They diagnose different errors.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite-dimensional encoding $J:\mathcal C\to V$, its projector
$P$, a logical unitary $U$, and an ambient unitary $W$.

[F1] $P$ is the orthogonal projection onto $J\mathcal C$.
[[def-qc-tqc-fixed-charge-encoding]]

[F2] The operator norm is finite for every linear map between
finite-dimensional inner-product spaces.
[[def-operator-norm-on-a-finite-dimensional-inner-product-space]]

## Proof

1.1 The maps $PWP-\widetilde U$ and $(I-P)WP$ are endomorphisms of $V$. Because $V$ is finite dimensional, [F2] assigns a finite operator norm to each map; the two quantities are therefore well defined. [given, F1, F2]

2.1 For a zero-leakage but logically incorrect boundary case, take $V=\mathcal C=\mathbb C^2$, $J=P=I$, $W=I$, and $U=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$. Then $\epsilon_{\mathrm{leak}}=0$ and $\epsilon_{\mathrm{log}}=\|I-U\|_{\mathrm{op}}=2$. This proves the two quantities are not interchangeable. [given, F1, F2, algebra] ∎

ITEM def-qc-tqc-braid-action-from-fusion-data


## Definition

Fix a unitary braided fusion category $\mathcal C$ and the simple-object
representative set $\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal C)$ used in
[[def-qc-tqc-fusion-tree-basis]]. Let $x,c\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal C)$,
let $n\ge2$, and suppose $V(x^n\to c)\ne0$. Use the left-associated fusion-tree basis. For the Artin
generator $\sigma_i$ that exchanges the $i$th and $(i+1)$st strands, let
$T_i$ be the specified sequence of $F$-moves that changes from the fixed tree
basis to a basis where those two charges fuse first, and let $R_i$ apply the
corresponding $R$-symbol on each pair-charge channel. Define
$$\rho_c(\sigma_i):=T_i^{-1}R_iT_i.$$
This defines the generator assignment. The next theorem proves that the
assignment descends from words to a well-defined group homomorphism. Word
products use matrix multiplication acting right-to-left on column vectors.
The abstract group in this definition is the Artin group
[[def-braid-group-by-the-artin-presentation]]; the positive crossing is the
generator convention used for the displayed $R$-symbols. If a motion is
described chronologically, its matrix product is written in reverse temporal
order.


## Positive crossing convention

The diagram depicts the positive adjacent generator on two identical charges.
Time runs from top to bottom; the continuous left-input strand passes over the
broken right-input strand. The crossing represents $c_{x,x}$, while its
pullback on a fusion space uses the inverse in the formula above. This fixes
the $R$-matrix orientation; the proof of the Artin relations is categorical.

[diagram omitted from reading extract]

## Remarks

The geometric configuration convention in
[[thm-geometric-braids-are-the-fundamental-group-of-unordered-configurations]]
uses first-loop-then-second multiplication, while raw slicing reverses
stacking. This definition fixes the algebraic Artin action directly and makes
no extra assertion that the library's geometric group is identified with the
Artin presentation under a stronger axiom branch.

ITEM lem-qc-tqc-zero-logical-error-forces-zero-leakage


## Statement

Let $J:\mathcal C\to V$ be an isometric encoding with $P=JJ^*$, let $U$ be
unitary on $\mathcal C$, and let $W$ be unitary on $V$. If the logical block
error $\|PWP-JUJ^*\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ is zero, then the leakage amplitude
$\|(I-P)WP\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ is zero.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Finite-dimensional inner-product spaces $\mathcal C,V$, an
isometry $J:\mathcal C\to V$, a unitary $U$ on $\mathcal C$, a unitary $W$ on
$V$, and zero logical block error.

[F1] The logical block error is $\|PWP-JUJ^*\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ and the leakage
amplitude is $\|(I-P)WP\|_{\mathrm{op}}$; $P=JJ^*$ is the orthogonal
projection onto $J\mathcal C$.
[[def-qc-tqc-logical-error-and-leakage]]

[F2] Isometries preserve inner-product norm, and orthogonal projection splits
a vector into orthogonal code and complement components.
[[def-linear-isometry-and-orthogonal-or-unitary-operator]]
[[def-inner-product-norm]]

## Proof

1.1 Zero block error means $PWP=JUJ^*$ as operators on $V$. For every $x\in\mathcal C$, $J^*J=I_{\mathcal C}$ gives $PWJx=PWPJx=JUJ^*Jx=JUx$. By [F2], $\|WJx\|=\|x\|$ and $\|PWJx\|=\|JUx\|=\|Ux\|=\|x\|$. [given, F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 The decomposition $WJx=PWJx+(I-P)WJx$ is orthogonal by [F1]. Its Pythagorean identity and step 1.1 give $\|(I-P)WJx\|^2=\|WJx\|^2-\|PWJx\|^2=0$ for every $x$. Hence $(I-P)WJ=0$, and since $P=JJ^*$, $(I-P)WP=0$; its operator norm is zero. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra] ∎

ITEM thm-qc-tqc-block-approximation-error


## Statement

Let $V=P V\oplus(I-P)V$ be an orthogonal decomposition of a finite-dimensional
Hilbert space, let $U$ and $W$ be unitary operators on the two summands, and
let $T:=U\oplus W$. If an ambient unitary $G$ satisfies
$\|G-T\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\delta$, then the logical block error of $G$ relative
to $U$ and its leakage amplitude both are at most $\delta$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The orthogonal projection $P$, the block-unitary target $T$, the
ambient unitary $G$, and $\delta\ge0$.

[F1] Logical block error and leakage are
$\|PGP-U P\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ and $\|(I-P)GP\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ after identifying
$U$ with its zero-on-the-complement extension.
[[def-qc-tqc-logical-error-and-leakage]]

[F2] The operator norm is the maximum of $\|Tx\|$ over unit vectors $x$.
[[def-operator-norm-on-a-finite-dimensional-inner-product-space]]

## Proof

1.1 Since $T$ preserves both summands, $PTP=UP$ on $PV$ and $(I-P)TP=0$. Thus $PGP-UP=P(G-T)P$. For any $y\in V$, the decomposition $y=Py+(I-P)y$ is orthogonal, so the Pythagorean identity gives $\|Py\|\le\|y\|$ and $\|(I-P)y\|\le\|y\|$. Applying the first inequality to $y=(G-T)Px$ and to $x$, for every unit vector $x$, yields $\|P(G-T)Px\|\le\|(G-T)Px\|\le\|G-T\|_{\mathrm{op}}$. Taking the maximum in [F2] proves the norm is at most $\delta$. This is the logical block error bound. [given, F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 Similarly $(I-P)GP=(I-P)(G-T)P$ because $(I-P)TP=0$. Use the second contraction proved in step 1.1: for every unit $x$, $\|(I-P)(G-T)Px\|\le\|(G-T)Px\|\le\|G-T\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\delta$. Taking the maximum gives the leakage bound. [given, F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
