
ITEM def-anyonic-fusion-path-space


## Definition

Let $\mathcal C$ be a fusion category over $\mathbb C$, so it is finite semisimple, rigid, monoidal, and has simple unit [[def-fusion-and-multifusion-category]]. Let $a,b,c$ be simple objects and let $n\ge0$. Fix one representative of each simple isomorphism class for the internal path labels; the external objects $a,b,c$ need not equal their representatives. Set $T_0=b$ and recursively fix the left-associated parenthesization $T_{j+1}:=T_j\otimes a$. The **fixed-boundary anyonic path space** is

$$H_{a,n;b,c}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal C}(c,T_n).$$

For simple labels $x,y$, define the split multiplicity space $W_{x,a}^y:=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal C}(y,x\otimes a)$. For $n\ge1$, a fusion path is a sequence of simple labels $x_0=b,x_1,\ldots,x_n=c$, with $x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1}$ drawn from the fixed representative set, together with, for every $1\le j\le n$, one basis index $\mu_j$ in a chosen basis $B_{x_{j-1},a}^{x_j}$ of $W_{x_{j-1},a}^{x_j}$. The basis index is retained even when distinct indices have the same adjacent labels. Fusion rules are the nonnegative integers $N_{x a}^{y}$ in $[x][a]=\sum_yN_{x a}^{y}[y]$ [[def-fusion-rules]].

For $n=0$, there is no path if $b\not\cong c$. If $b\cong c$, fix an isomorphism $u_{c,b}:c\to b$ and take it as the single empty-path vector (use $u_{b,b}=\operatorname{id}_b$ when the objects coincide). In the unitary setting choose this isomorphism unitary.

For $n\ge1$, if $i_j\in B_{x_{j-1},a}^{x_j}$ is the selected inclusion $x_j\to x_{j-1}\otimes a$, define the path composite by $\gamma_0:=\operatorname{id}_b$ and $\gamma_j:=(\gamma_{j-1}\otimes\operatorname{id}_a)\circ i_j:x_j\to T_j$. For $x_n=c$, $\gamma_n$ is a vector in $H_{a,n;b,c}$. The next lemma proves these composites form a basis and computes the dimension. No Hilbert-space or physical Hamiltonian structure is part of this definition.

ITEM def-categorical-object-and-operator-invertibility


## Definition

Let $\mathcal C$ be a fusion category with unit $\mathbf1$. Its Grothendieck fusion ring $K_0(\mathcal C)$ is the free abelian group on simple isomorphism classes, with multiplication induced by tensor product and coefficients given by the fusion rules of [[def-fusion-rules]].

- An object $X$ is **tensor-invertible** if there is an object $Y$ with $X\otimes Y\cong\mathbf1$ and $Y\otimes X\cong\mathbf1$.
- A class $[X]$ is a **fusion-ring unit** if it has a two-sided multiplicative inverse in $K_0(\mathcal C)$; that inverse may have negative integer coefficients in the simple-object basis.
- A linear operator is **invertible** only relative to a specified vector space $H$ and operator $T:H\to H$: it must have a two-sided inverse $S:H\to H$ with $ST=TS=I_H$.

These definitions concern different objects: categorical tensor product, a ring with virtual classes, and a chosen representation on a vector space.

ITEM def-anyonic-frobenius-schur-sign


## Definition

Let $\mathcal C$ be a unitary spherical fusion category over $\mathbb C$, with pivotal structure and spherical dimension as in [[def-pivotal-structure]], [[def-spherical-structure]], and [[def-the-dimension-of-an-object-relative-to-a-pivotal-structure]]. Let $a$ be a self-dual simple object with $N_{aa}^{\mathbf1}=1$. Choose a unit vector $v:\mathbf1\to a\otimes a$ spanning the one-dimensional vacuum split space and let $d_a>0$ be its positive spherical dimension.

A **normalized vacuum-channel self-duality datum** is a supplied pair of maps $\operatorname{coev}:\mathbf1\to a\otimes a$ and $\operatorname{ev}:a\otimes a\to\mathbf1$ of the form

$$\operatorname{coev}=\sqrt{d_a}\,v,\qquad \operatorname{ev}=\kappa_a\sqrt{d_a}\,v^\dagger,$$

where $\kappa_a\in\{+1,-1\}$ and the two duality zig-zag identities hold after the self-duality identification, using the left-dual convention of [[def-left-dual-and-right-dual-object]]. We call $\kappa_a$ the **Frobenius–Schur sign in this normalized cup/cap convention**. It is not a braiding or $R$-symbol phase.

This item records supplied normalized duality data; it does not assert that every self-dual simple object admits the displayed gauge or prove a trace, ribbon, or category-equivalence theorem. An example must supply the datum or compute the sign from coherent normalized F-data; fusion rules alone do not specify it.

ITEM def-anyonic-local-channel-projector


## Definition

Fix a fusion-path space with repeated simple site charge $a$, a position $i$ with two adjacent $a$-labels, and a simple output $s$ of $a\otimes a$, chosen from the fixed internal representative set of [[def-anyonic-fusion-path-space]]. Choose bases in the split spaces $\operatorname{Hom}(z,x\otimes y)$ at every vertex of the trees used below, retaining the fixed path vertex bases on the fixed tree. In the tree basis where this adjacent pair is fused first, let $\Pi_s$ be the projection onto the direct summand with pair output $s$, acting as the identity on every multiplicity index inside that channel and as zero on all other pair outputs. Let $A_i:T_{\mathrm{fixed}}\to T_{\mathrm{pair}}$ be a typed composite of associators and their inverses carrying the fixed path tree to this pair-first tree, without permuting the ordered inputs (including the boundary charge). In the split-tree bases of $\operatorname{Hom}(c,T)$, let $F_i$ be the matrix of $h\mapsto A_i\circ h$, with target coordinates as rows and source coordinates as columns. The proof below constructs these bases and proves invertibility in any complex fusion category. Define the **local channel projector** in the fixed path basis by

$$P_{i,s}:=F_i^{-1}\Pi_sF_i.$$

This is an idempotent for any fusion category because $F_i$ is invertible. In a unitary fusion category with orthonormal tree bases, $F_i$ is unitary and $P_{i,s}$ is an orthogonal projection. The channel projection includes every multiplicity coordinate; the definition has no multiplicity-free restriction. The split Hom convention here is $\operatorname{Hom}(c,T)$, as in [[def-anyonic-fusion-path-space]]. In the unitary case with adjoint vertex bases, each local left-to-right split recoupling matrix is the entrywise conjugate of the QC25 inverse-precomposition F matrix, as proved below; they agree for the displayed real Fibonacci and Ising blocks.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The complex fusion category, simple external objects, internal representative labels, finite trees, vertex bases, and channel projection in the Definition. Unitarity is assumed only for the unitary clauses.

[F1] [[def-fusion-and-multifusion-category]]: every object is a finite direct sum of simple objects, and tensor product is bilinear.

[F2] [[def-absolutely-simple-object]]: simple endomorphisms are scalar, and Hom spaces between distinct simple representatives vanish.

[F3] [[def-monoidal-category]]: the associator is an isomorphism; tensor product is a bifunctor.

[F4] [[def-qc-tqc-unitary-fusion-category]]: in the unitary case orthogonal decompositions exist, tensor product preserves dagger, and associators are unitary. The split path inner product is specified in [[thm-anyonic-path-space-is-hilbert-under-unitarity]].

[F5] [[def-qc-tqc-f-symbol]]: in the unitary fusion Hom convention the local associator matrix satisfies $f_j\circ\alpha^{-1}=\sum_kF_{kj}g_k$.

## Proof

**Given:** The Definition's split-tree coordinate data, with no dagger or positivity assumed in steps 1.1–2.1.

1.1 A semisimple decomposition of an object $X$ has inclusions $i_{z,\mu}:z\to X$ and retractions $p_{z,\mu}:X\to z$, with $p_{z,\mu}i_{z,\nu}=\delta_{\mu\nu}\operatorname{id}_z$, zero cross-composites for distinct representatives, and $\sum_{z,\mu}i_{z,\mu}p_{z,\mu}=\operatorname{id}_X$. By [F2], its inclusions for each $z$ form a basis of $\operatorname{Hom}(z,X)$. Any other basis is obtained by an invertible column change; multiplying the retractions by the inverse matrix retains both identities. Thus any specified split basis can be the inclusion basis of such a decomposition. A simple leaf itself has the one-term decomposition with identity inclusion, even when it is an external object different from its representative. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Build up a finite binary tree from its leaves. Suppose its child objects $T_L,T_R$ have decompositions with inclusion bases $(i_{x,\mu})$ and $(j_{y,\nu})$ as in step 1.1. Tensoring their decompositions gives inclusions $i_{x,\mu}\otimes j_{y,\nu}$ of $x\otimes y$ into $T_L\otimes T_R$, with tensor retractions whose composites and sum satisfy the same identities by bifunctoriality. Therefore, for any simple output $z$, composition gives the isomorphism $\bigoplus_{x,y,\mu,\nu}\operatorname{Hom}(z,x\otimes y)\to\operatorname{Hom}(z,T_L\otimes T_R)$; its inverse applies those tensor retractions. Inserting the specified vertex basis in each summand produces exactly all split-tree composites for output $z$, and proves they are a basis. For a nonroot subtree take $z$ once over the internal representative set and apply step 1.1 to obtain its decomposition. At the root use the actual external output $c$ and its specified vertex bases directly. This finite recursion proves the basis assertion for arbitrary external simple objects, without requiring arbitrary endpoint bases to be transported copies of representative bases. Every internal simple label and every multiplicity index is retained. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Postcomposition by $A_i$ is linear and invertible, with inverse postcomposition by $A_i^{-1}$. Step 2.1 supplies bases on both sides, hence its matrix $F_i$ is invertible. The pair-first basis is partitioned by the pair output $s$, retaining every coordinate within that channel, so $\Pi_s^2=\Pi_s$. Consequently $P_{i,s}^2=F_i^{-1}\Pi_s^2F_i=P_{i,s}$, proving the general algebraic assertion without unitarity. [F3, step 2.1, given, algebra]

4.1 In the unitary case choose orthonormal split vertex bases. Start step 1.1 with an orthogonal decomposition; any orthonormal change of its inclusion basis is unitary and retains $p_{z,\mu}=i_{z,\mu}^\dagger$. Tensor compatibility and composition make the decompositions in step 2.1 orthogonal. Thus its tree inclusions are orthonormal for $g^\dagger h=\langle h,g\rangle\operatorname{id}_c$. For unitary $A_i$ one has $(A_i g)^\dagger(A_i h)=g^\dagger A_i^\dagger A_i h=g^\dagger h$. Its matrix $F_i$ is therefore unitary. The coordinate projection $\Pi_s$ is self-adjoint in the orthonormal pair-first basis, so $P_{i,s}=F_i^\dagger\Pi_sF_i$ is self-adjoint as well as idempotent. [F2, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 To compare Hom conventions, take daggers in [F5]. Unitarity of the associator gives $\alpha\circ f_j^\dagger=\sum_k\overline{F_{kj}}g_k^\dagger$. The maps $f_j^\dagger,g_k^\dagger$ are exactly the corresponding adjoint split-tree bases. Hence the split postcomposition matrix for this local move is $\overline F$, while the QC25 fusion Hom matrix is $F$. This proves the claimed convention conversion and completes the well-definedness and unitary assertions, including zero-dimensional spaces with their unique empty coordinate maps. [F4, F5, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎

ITEM thm-anyonic-channel-projectors-are-reparenthesization-independent


## Statement

For fixed ordered tensor inputs and fixed source and target parenthesizations, any two sequences of canonical associator F-moves between them induce the same coordinate map. Consequently, conjugating a fixed pair-channel projection by either sequence gives the same operator on the fusion-path space.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A monoidal category, a fixed ordered list of simple input charges, two parenthesizations, and the fusion-tree bases.

[F1] The proof in [[def-anyonic-local-channel-projector]] supplies split-tree bases in any complex fusion category and invertible coordinate matrices of $h\mapsto A\circ h$. More generally, in any monoidal category where the Hom spaces and fixed bases in the Given data are available, this same postcomposition rule defines the coordinate map without a semisimplicity or unitarity hypothesis; all intermediate basis indices are retained.

[F2] [[thm-mac-lane-coherence-in-the-canonical-map-form]]: any two canonical morphisms between fixed parenthesized words on the same ordered inputs are equal.

[F3] [[def-anyonic-local-channel-projector]]: a local channel projector is conjugation of the channel projection by the chosen F-move composite.

## Proof

**Given:** The typed parenthesizations, associators, and channel projection in the Statement.

1.1 For a canonical associator map $A:T\to T'$, [F1] defines the split Hom map $A_*:h\mapsto A\circ h$. If $B:T'\to T''$, then $B_*(A_*(h))=(B\circ A)\circ h$. Thus each finite F-move sequence is the matrix of postcomposition by its canonical composite, including tensor products with identities and inverse associators. This argument applies in any monoidal category with the fixed coordinate spaces and bases posited in the Given data. [F1, construct]

2.1 The two sequences in the Statement have the same source and target parenthesized words, so their canonical morphisms are equal by [F2]. Coordinates of equal morphisms in the fixed fusion-tree bases are equal; hence the two matrix composites agree. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Conjugating the same channel projection by equal coordinate maps gives equal operators, since matrix multiplication preserves equality and the maps are invertible. This is exactly the definition in [F3]. [F3, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
