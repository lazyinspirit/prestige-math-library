
ITEM thm-qc-tqc-hexagon-coordinates


## Statement

Let $\mathcal C$ be a unitary braided fusion category with associator $\alpha$ and
braiding $c$. For all objects $X,Y,Z$, its two hexagons are

$$\alpha_{Y,Z,X}\circ c_{X,Y\otimes Z}\circ\alpha_{X,Y,Z} =(\operatorname{id}_Y\otimes c_{X,Z})\circ\alpha_{Y,X,Z} \circ(c_{X,Y}\otimes\operatorname{id}_Z),$$

$$\alpha_{Z,X,Y}^{-1}\circ c_{X\otimes Y,Z}\circ\alpha_{X,Y,Z}^{-1} =(c_{X,Z}\otimes\operatorname{id}_Y)\circ\alpha_{X,Z,Y}^{-1} \circ(\operatorname{id}_X\otimes c_{Y,Z}).$$

For simple input charges and a fixed simple total charge, the two route matrices
in each equation are equal in fusion-tree coordinates. Each entry is the sum
over all intermediate simple charges and multiplicity indices of the products
of the corresponding F- and R-block entries.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** a braided fusion category, simple input charges, a simple total
charge, and the orthonormal fusion-tree bases.

[F1] [[def-braiding]]: the braiding is a natural isomorphism and satisfies the
two hexagon equations displayed in the Statement.

[F2] [[def-qc-tqc-f-symbol]]: each associator change between tree bases is
represented by the corresponding F block, with all multiplicity indices.

[F3] [[def-qc-tqc-r-symbol]]: each braiding exchange on a channel is represented
by the corresponding typed R block.

[F4] [[lem-qc-tqc-fusion-tree-dimension]]: each intermediate tree space has a
complete basis indexed by all admissible simple labels and vertex multiplicity
indices.

## Proof
1.1 On a fixed fusion-tree basis, the matrix of each associator factor is the appropriate F block by [F2], and the matrix of each channel exchange is the appropriate R block by [F3]. An identity factor preserves all untouched labels and multiplicity indices. If a braiding input is a tensor product rather than a simple charge, decompose it into its finite simple summands; its matrix is the resulting finite block matrix, with the summand and multiplicity indices kept. Thus every factor in either hexagon composite has a specified finite matrix. [F2, F3, F4, construct]

2.1 For the first hexagon, let $M_1,M_2,M_3$ be the matrices of $\alpha_{X,Y,Z}$, $c_{X,Y\otimes Z}$, and $\alpha_{Y,Z,X}$ in order along its left route, and let $N_1,N_2,N_3$ be those of $c_{X,Y}\otimes\operatorname{id}_Z$, $\alpha_{Y,X,Z}$, and $\operatorname{id}_Y\otimes c_{X,Z}$ along its right route. For fixed endpoint indices $x,z$, the entries are respectively $\sum_{u,v}(M_3)_{z,v}(M_2)_{v,u}(M_1)_{u,x}$ and $\sum_{u',v'}(N_3)_{z,v'}(N_2)_{v',u'}(N_1)_{u',x}$. Every intermediate index is the full tuple of simple charges and vertex multiplicity indices for that tree. The two structural maps are equal by the first hexagon [F1], so their coordinate entries and these finite sums are equal. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 For the second hexagon, let $P_1,P_2,P_3$ be the matrices of $\alpha_{X,Y,Z}^{-1}$, $c_{X\otimes Y,Z}$, and $\alpha_{Z,X,Y}^{-1}$ along its left route; let $Q_1,Q_2,Q_3$ be those of $\operatorname{id}_X\otimes c_{Y,Z}$, $\alpha_{X,Z,Y}^{-1}$, and $c_{X,Z}\otimes\operatorname{id}_Y$ along its right route. The entries at fixed endpoints are $\sum_{u,v}(P_3)_{z,v}(P_2)_{v,u}(P_1)_{u,x}$ and $\sum_{u',v'}(Q_3)_{z,v'}(Q_2)_{v',u'}(Q_1)_{u',x}$. The indices again range over every simple intermediate charge and multiplicity coordinate. The second root hexagon [F1] makes the structural maps equal, so the two finite sums agree. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The two matrix equalities in steps 2.1 and 2.2 are the coordinate forms of the first and second hexagons, respectively, and each includes all admissible intermediate labels and multiplicities. [step 2.1, step 2.2] ∎

ITEM thm-qc-tqc-pentagon-coordinates


## Statement

Let $a,b,c,d$ be four simple input charges and $t$ a simple total charge. The
root associator identity

$$\alpha_{a,b,c\otimes d}\circ\alpha_{a\otimes b,c,d} =(\operatorname{id}_a\otimes\alpha_{b,c,d})\circ\alpha_{a,b\otimes c,d} \circ(\alpha_{a,b,c}\otimes\operatorname{id}_d)$$

has equal matrices along its two routes in the fusion-tree bases. If $A_1,A_2,A_3$
are the three successive matrices on the right and $A_4,A_5$ the two successive
matrices on the left, then

$$A_3A_2A_1=A_5A_4.$$

Each matrix coefficient sums over every intermediate simple charge and every
multiplicity-basis index.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** a unitary fusion category, simple objects $a,b,c,d$, and a simple
total charge $t$.

[F1] [[def-monoidal-category]]: the associator satisfies the pentagon equation
displayed in the Statement for all objects, including tensor products that are
not simple.

[F2] [[def-qc-tqc-f-symbol]]: an associator move between two fusion-tree bases
has matrix $F$, with the row/column convention and all vertex multiplicity
indices specified there.

[F3] [[lem-qc-tqc-fusion-tree-dimension]]: each parenthesized tree basis is a
complete orthonormal basis indexed by all admissible internal charges and
vertex multiplicity indices.

## Proof
1.1 Fix the source and target parenthesized tensor products in the pentagon. For each intermediate parenthesization, decompose every tensor product into simple summands and choose the tree basis of [F3]. Each associator in [F1] acts on these coordinates by the appropriate direct sum of F blocks from [F2], with identity factors on vertices it does not change. Thus the two sides of [F1] have coordinate matrices $A_3A_2A_1$ and $A_5A_4$, respectively. [F1, F2, F3, construct]

2.1 For fixed endpoint basis indices $x$ and $z$, let $u,v$ range over the complete composite indices of the two intermediate trees on the three-move route, and let $w$ range over the complete composite indices of the intermediate tree on the two-move route. Matrix multiplication gives $$\sum_{u,v}(A_3)_{z,v}(A_2)_{v,u}(A_1)_{u,x} =\sum_w(A_5)_{z,w}(A_4)_{w,x}.$$ Here each composite index consists of every simple internal charge together with every multiplicity-basis index at its vertices. Equality follows because the morphisms in [F1] are equal and [F3] supplies bases of the same Hom space; coordinates in a basis are unique. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The endpoint indices $x,z$ were arbitrary, so all entries of the two route matrices agree, proving $A_3A_2A_1=A_5A_4$ with all intermediate charge and multiplicity sums included. All sums are finite by finite semisimplicity. [F3, step 2.1] ∎
## Pentagon diagram

The vertices are the five parenthesizations of $a\otimes b\otimes c\otimes d$;
each arrow is the indicated associator. The diagram encodes exactly the two
composites stated and proved above; the proof uses the equations and coordinate
expansion, not the drawing.

ITEM thm-qc-tqc-gauge-covariance


## Statement

Let $\mathcal C$ be a unitary braided fusion category. A finite list of simple
charges and total charge determines a fusion space in each parenthesization.
For a crossing word with underlying object map $b_\beta:P_L\to P_{L'}$, define
its action on the corresponding Hom spaces by
$f\mapsto f\circ b_\beta^{-1}$, from $\operatorname{Hom}(P_L,d)$ to
$\operatorname{Hom}(P_{L'},d)$. Let $U_\beta$ be its matrix in chosen
fusion-tree bases. If each multiplicity basis is changed by the unitary
gauge matrices of [[def-qc-tqc-gauge-transform]], and $G_L,G_{L'}$ are the
induced source and target tree-basis unitaries, then

$$U_\beta'=G_{L'}^{-1}U_\beta G_L.$$

In particular, when $n\ge2$ and all $n$ strand charges are the same simple object $a$,
the adjacent exchanges act on the same fixed-total fusion space
$V=\operatorname{Hom}(a^{\otimes n},d)$, satisfy the Artin relations, and
define a unitary representation $\rho:B_n\to U(V)$, where $U(V)$ is the group
of invertible linear maps preserving the inner product on $V$.
If $V=0$, this is the unique action on the zero-dimensional space.
In that case $\rho'(\beta)=G_L^{-1}\rho(\beta)G_L$ for every braid $\beta$.
Thus basis-dependent matrix entries change, while the braid action up to
unitary equivalence and consistently transformed expectation values do not.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** a unitary braided fusion category, finite simple-charge lists, a
fixed simple total charge, and unitary multiplicity-basis changes.

[F1] [[def-monoidal-category]]: tensor product is a bifunctor, and the
associator satisfies the pentagon axiom.

[F2] [[def-braiding]]: each $c_{X,Y}$ is a natural isomorphism and satisfies
both hexagon identities.

[F3] [[thm-qc-tqc-pentagon-coordinates]] and
[[thm-qc-tqc-hexagon-coordinates]]: the associator and braiding coherence
identities hold in the full fusion-tree matrices, including all multiplicities.

[F4] [[def-qc-tqc-gauge-transform]] and
[[def-qc-tqc-fusion-tree-basis]]: local basis changes are unitary, and the
induced tree-basis change is the direct sum of the corresponding tensor
products at the vertices.

[F5] [[def-braid-group-by-the-artin-presentation]] and [[thm-von-dyck]]: the
generators and Artin relations present $B_n$, and generator maps satisfying
those relators extend uniquely to a group homomorphism.

[F6] [[def-qc-tqc-unitary-fusion-category]],
[[thm-qc-tqc-f-symbol-unitary]], and [[thm-qc-tqc-r-symbol-unitary]]: the
braiding and every F/R change of orthonormal fusion basis are unitary maps.

[F7] [[thm-mac-lane-coherence-in-the-canonical-map-form]]: any two canonical
rebracketing maps between the same parenthesized tensor words on the same
ordered factors are equal.

## Proof
1.1 For three consecutive charges $A,B,C$, naturality of the braiding in its first variable, applied to $c_{A,B}:A\otimes B\to B\otimes A$, gives $$c_{B\otimes A,C}\circ(c_{A,B}\otimes\operatorname{id}_C) =(\operatorname{id}_C\otimes c_{A,B})\circ c_{A\otimes B,C}.$$ This is an equality from $((A\otimes B)\otimes C)$ to $C\otimes(B\otimes A)$. Reassociating the target by $\alpha_{C,B,A}^{-1}$ and expanding each $c_{X\otimes Y,Z}$ by the second hexagon in [F2] gives the two adjacent Artin composites $$\begin{aligned} L&=(c_{B,C}\otimes\operatorname{id}_A)\circ\alpha_{B,C,A}^{-1} \circ(\operatorname{id}_B\otimes c_{A,C})\circ\alpha_{B,A,C} \circ(c_{A,B}\otimes\operatorname{id}_C),\\ R&=\alpha_{C,B,A}^{-1}\circ(\operatorname{id}_C\otimes c_{A,B}) \circ\alpha_{C,A,B}\circ(c_{A,C}\otimes\operatorname{id}_B) \circ\alpha_{A,C,B}^{-1}\circ(\operatorname{id}_A\otimes c_{B,C}) \circ\alpha_{A,B,C}. \end{aligned}$$ The cancelled factor is $\alpha_{C,B,A}^{-1}\circ\alpha_{C,B,A}=\operatorname{id}$; the remaining factors are exactly the displayed $L$ and $R$. Hence the adjacent braid relation holds as a typed equality of maps. Coherence [F7] identifies the canonical rebracketings of these three factors when they sit inside a longer tree; [F3] records the resulting equality in every fusion-tree block. [F1, F2, F3, F7, given, algebra]

1.2 If two crossings involve disjoint adjacent pairs, reparenthesize the ordered factors as $W_L\otimes(A\otimes B)\otimes M\otimes(C\otimes D)\otimes W_R$, where $W_L,W_R$ collect the outer factors and $M$ is the (possibly empty) intervening tensor product. Let $g:A\otimes B\to B\otimes A$ and $h:C\otimes D\to D\otimes C$ be the two crossings. Suppressing only the canonical parentheses, bifunctoriality gives the typed identity $$(\operatorname{id}_{B\otimes A}\otimes\operatorname{id}_M\otimes h)\circ(g\otimes\operatorname{id}_M\otimes\operatorname{id}_{C\otimes D}) =g\otimes\operatorname{id}_M\otimes h =(g\otimes\operatorname{id}_M\otimes\operatorname{id}_{D\otimes C})\circ(\operatorname{id}_{A\otimes B}\otimes\operatorname{id}_M\otimes h).$$ Tensoring with the outer identities gives the same equality on the full word. Coherence [F7] transports it back to the chosen tree, so distant braid generators commute, including when one or more intervening factors are present. [F1, F7, algebra]

2.1 For $n$ identical charges $a$, reassociate each object-level crossing $b_i$ to act on factors $i,i+1$, and let its fusion-space operator be $B_i(f)=f\circ b_i^{-1}$. The identity $B_{h\circ g}=B_h\circ B_g$ follows by composition: $B_h(B_g(f))=(f\circ g^{-1})\circ h^{-1}=f\circ(h\circ g)^{-1}$. Consequently the object-level relators proved in steps 1.1–1.2 hold for the $B_i$ as well. By [F5], the generator assignment extends uniquely to $\rho:B_n\to U(V)$. Reassociations are unitary by [F6], and the local exchange matrices are products of unitary F/R blocks from [F6]; hence each $B_i$ and every braid word is unitary. [F5, F6, step 1.1, step 1.2, construct]

2.2 In any parenthesized tree, let $G_T$ be the direct sum over admissible internal labels of the tensor products of the vertex gauge matrices. It is unitary because each factor is unitary and the tree basis is orthonormal by [F4]. The formulas in [F4] show that every F/R factor in a crossing matrix changes by inserting the inverse gauge at its target and the gauge at its source. Along a composite word, adjacent intermediate factors cancel, leaving $U_\beta'=G_{L'}^{-1}U_\beta G_L$. This also holds for colored words whose source and target lists differ. [F3, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 For identical charges, $L'=L$ and the formula of step 2.2 is conjugation by the single unitary $G_L$, so $\rho'(\beta)=G_L^{-1}\rho(\beta)G_L$ for every $\beta\in B_n$. If $\psi'=G_L^{-1}\psi$ and $M'=G_L^{-1}MG_L$, then $\langle\psi',M'\psi'\rangle=\langle G_L^{-1}\psi,G_L^{-1}M\psi\rangle =\langle\psi,M\psi\rangle$ because $G_L$ is unitary. Hence all expectation values, including measurement probabilities represented this way, are unchanged. [F4, step 2.2, algebra] ∎

ITEM cex-qc-tqc-hecke-not-positive


## Statement refuted

The claim refuted is that the algebraic type-A Hecke relations at every
parameter automatically supply a positive-definite inner product making the
resulting module action unitary.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the root type-A Hecke algebra in its $v$-normalization, specialized
to $v=2$, and the one-dimensional complex vector space $V=\mathbb C$.

[F1] [[def-generic-type-a-hecke-algebra]]: at a unit specialization $v_0$,
the type-A generators satisfy $(T_i-v_0)(T_i+1)=0$ and the Artin braid and
distant-commutativity relations.

[F2] [[def-real-and-complex-inner-product-space]]: a positive-definite complex
inner product has induced norm $\|z\|=\sqrt{\langle z,z\rangle}$, with
$\|z\|>0$ for $z\ne0$ and $\|\lambda z\|=|\lambda|\,\|z\|$.

[F3] [[def-linear-isometry-and-orthogonal-or-unitary-operator]]: a unitary
operator is an invertible linear isometry and therefore preserves this norm.

## Counterexample
1.1 The specialization $v\mapsto2$ is valid because $2\in\mathbb C^\times$. Assign $T_1\mapsto2I_V$ and $T_2\mapsto2I_V$. The quadratic relator maps to $(2-2)(2+1)I_V=0$. The adjacent braid relator maps to $2I_V\,2I_V\,2I_V=8I_V$ on both sides; there is no distant pair for $n=3$. Thus every defining relator vanishes, so the assignment from the free algebra descends to a one-dimensional $H_2(3)$-module. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Let $z\ne0$. For any positive-definite Hermitian form on $V$, $\|T_i z\|=\|2z\|=2\|z\|\ne\|z\|$ by [F2]. Therefore $T_i=2I_V$ is not an isometry and cannot be unitary by [F3], for any choice of positive inner product. [F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The specialization satisfies the algebraic Hecke and braid relations but its one-dimensional module has no positive inner product making its generators unitary. Hence an algebraic Hecke parameter alone is not a positivity or unitarity certificate. This witness says nothing about other parameters or about modules supplied with a separately verified dagger structure. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
