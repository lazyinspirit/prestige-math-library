
ITEM lem-qc-tqc-b6-joint-su5-su8-density


## Statement

Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$. For the normalized simultaneous six-$\tau$
Fibonacci B6 representation on
$V(\tau^6\to1)\oplus V(\tau^6\to\tau)\cong\mathbb C^5\oplus\mathbb C^8$,
the closure in $U(5)\times U(8)$ contains $SU(5)\times SU(8)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The simultaneous normalized matrices
$B_i^{(5)}\oplus B_i^{(8)}$, their common braid word $C$, and
$\mathrm{AC}_\omega$.

[F1] The five-dimensional proof constructs 24 independent Lie words from
$X=C_5-C_5^{-1}$ and records determinant character $r^3$; the
eight-dimensional proof constructs 63 independent Lie words from
$X=C_8-C_8^{-1}$ and records determinant character $r^5$.
[[thm-qc-tqc-b6-su5-density]] [[thm-qc-tqc-b6-su8-density]]

[F2] Closed subgroups are Lie subgroups under $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, and their
Lie algebras are bracket closed.
[[thm-cartans-closed-subgroup-theorem]]
[[prop-the-lie-algebra-of-a-lie-subgroup-is-a-lie-subalgebra]]

[F3] $\mathfrak{su}(n)$ consists of the trace-zero skew-Hermitian matrices.
A real basis is given by $i(E_{jj}-E_{nn})$ for $j<n$ and, for each $j<k$,
$E_{jk}-E_{kj}$ and $i(E_{jk}+E_{kj})$. Thus its real dimension is
$(n-1)+2\binom n2=n^2-1$, giving dimensions $24$ and $63$ for $n=5,8$.
[[ex-unitary-and-special-unitary-lie-groups]]

[F4] Both $SU(5)$ and $SU(8)$ are connected.
[[lem-qc-tqc-special-unitary-connected]]

[A1] Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$. [[def-countable-choice]]

[A2] Assume the fixed Fibonacci path convention and $F/R$ data stated in
[[lem-qc-tqc-fibonacci-b6-path-projectors-and-artin]].

## Proof

1.1 The common Fibonacci data in [A2] give the same normalized two-dimensional three-strand block for $C=B_1B_2^{-1}$ in both total-charge spaces. Direct multiplication gives determinant $1$, trace $\phi^{-2}$, and eigenvalues $e^{i\theta},e^{-i\theta}$ with $2\cos\theta=\phi^{-2}$ on that factor; the remaining factors contribute eigenvalue $1$. [A2, algebra]

1.2 For $n\ge2$, the complex Lie algebra $\mathfrak{sl}_n(\mathbb C)$ is simple. Let $I$ be a nonzero ideal. If some $X\in I$ has an off-diagonal entry $X_{ij}\ne0$, choose a traceless diagonal matrix $D$ whose ordered differences $\lambda_p-\lambda_q$ ($p\ne q$) are nonzero and pairwise distinct. Such a $D$ exists because each forbidden equality is a proper linear hyperplane in the trace-zero diagonal subspace, and finitely many proper hyperplanes do not cover a vector space over the infinite field $\mathbb C$. The map $\operatorname{ad}_D$ has eigenvectors $E_{pq}$ with these eigenvalues and is zero on diagonal matrices. Lagrange interpolation therefore supplies a polynomial in $\operatorname{ad}_D$ that sends $X$ to $X_{ij}E_{ij}$; since $I$ is an ideal, $E_{ij}\in I$. If instead a nonzero $X\in I$ is diagonal, it is nonscalar because it is traceless. Choose $i\ne j$ with $X_{ii}\ne X_{jj}$; then $[E_{ij},X]=(X_{jj}-X_{ii})E_{ij}\in I$, again giving a matrix unit. From $E_{ij}\in I$, bracketing with $E_{ji}$ gives $E_{ii}-E_{jj}\in I$, and bracketing that diagonal matrix with $E_{ji}$ gives $E_{ji}\in I$. For each $k\notin\{i,j\}$, $[E_{ki},E_{ij}]=E_{kj}$ and $[E_{jk},E_{ij}]=-E_{ik}$, so these units lie in $I$. The additional unit $E_{jk}$ follows explicitly from $[E_{ji},E_{ik}]=E_{ji}E_{ik}-E_{ik}E_{ji}=E_{jk}-0=E_{jk}$, since $i,j,k$ are distinct. We now have $E_{kj}$ and $E_{jl}$ for every $k,l\ne j$; for $k\ne l$, $[E_{kj},E_{jl}]=E_{kl}$. Thus every off-diagonal matrix unit lies in $I$, and their brackets give all trace-zero diagonal matrices. Hence $I$ is the whole algebra. If $n=2$, there is no $k\notin\{i,j\}$; the already obtained $E_{ij},E_{ji}$ and $E_{ii}-E_{jj}$ span $\mathfrak{sl}_2$, so this case is complete as well. The complexification of $\mathfrak{su}(n)$ is $\mathfrak{sl}_n(\mathbb C)$: every traceless complex matrix is uniquely a sum $A+iB$ of traceless skew-Hermitian matrices. A nonzero real ideal of $\mathfrak{su}(n)$ would complexify to a nonzero ideal of $\mathfrak{sl}_n(\mathbb C)$, hence to all of it; equality of dimensions then makes the real ideal all of $\mathfrak{su}(n)$. Therefore $\mathfrak{su}(5)$ and $\mathfrak{su}(8)$ are simple. Their dimensions $24$ and $63$ from [F3] show they are not isomorphic. [F3, algebra]

2.1 The algebraic number $\phi^{-2}$ has minimal polynomial $x^2-3x+1$, whose other root is $\phi^2>2$. If $e^{i\theta}$ were a root of unity, every algebraic conjugate of $e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta}$ would be a sum of two roots of unity and have absolute value at most $2$. The conjugate $\phi^2>2$ contradicts this, so $\theta/(2\pi)$ is irrational. [given, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 The 24 independent Lie words in the five-dimensional proof and the deterministically selected 63 independent Lie words in the eight-dimensional proof are each formed from the initial tangent $X$ by conjugations by the same generators and Lie brackets. Applying those same word operations to $(X_5,X_8)$ gives elements of $\mathfrak g$ whose first projections span $\mathfrak{su}(5)$ and whose second projections span $\mathfrak{su}(8)$, respectively, by [F1]. Therefore both coordinate projections of $\mathfrak g$ are surjective. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Each normalized generator has determinant pair $(r^3,r^5)$ by the rank calculations in [F1]. The joint determinant image is therefore finite, so the identity component of the closure lies in $SU(5)\times SU(8)$ and $\mathfrak g\subseteq\mathfrak{su}(5)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(8)$. With both projections surjective by step 2.2, $\mathfrak g$ is a subdirect Lie subalgebra of that product. [F1, F2, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 Define the kernel ideals $K_5=\{x\in\mathfrak{su}(5):(x,0)\in\mathfrak g\}$ and $K_8=\{y\in\mathfrak{su}(8):(0,y)\in\mathfrak g\}$. They are ideals because $\mathfrak g$ projects onto both factors and is closed under brackets. Simplicity says each kernel is either zero or the whole factor. If either is the whole factor, surjectivity of the other projection implies $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{su}(5)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(8)$. If both are zero, each projection restricts to an isomorphism from $\mathfrak g$ to its factor, yielding an isomorphism $\mathfrak{su}(5)\cong\mathfrak{su}(8)$, contrary to their different dimensions. Thus $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{su}(5)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(8)$. [F2, F3, step 3.1, step 1.2, algebra]

5.1 By [F2], the identity component of the joint closure is an embedded Lie subgroup of $SU(5)\times SU(8)$ with this full Lie algebra. It has full dimension and is open. The product is connected by [F4], so that open subgroup is the entire product. Therefore the joint closure contains $SU(5)\times SU(8)$. The only choice assumption is [A1]. [A1, F2, F4, step 4.1, algebra] ∎

ITEM thm-qc-tqc-fibonacci-single-qubit-density


## Statement

Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$. Let $B_1,B_2$ be the projective Fibonacci generators on the two-dimensional
three-$\tau$, total-$\tau$ fusion space from
[[ex-qc-tqc-fibonacci-qubit-matrices]]. The subgroup they generate is dense
in $PU(2)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $u=\phi^{-1}$, $v=\phi^{-1/2}$,
$r=e^{-3\pi i/5}$, $F=\begin{pmatrix}u&v\\v&-u\end{pmatrix}$,
$B_1=\operatorname{diag}(1,r)$, and $B_2=FB_1F$.

[F1] The matrix formulas, unitarity, Artin relation, and noncommutation are
verified directly in [[ex-qc-tqc-fibonacci-qubit-matrices]].

[F5] A complex normal endomorphism of a finite-dimensional inner-product
space has an orthonormal eigenbasis.
[[thm-complex-spectral-theorem-for-normal-endomorphisms]]

[F2] If $\alpha/(2\pi)$ is irrational, the powers of the circle rotation
$e^{i\alpha}$ are dense in the unit circle.
[[lem-qc-irrational-rotation-dense-on-circle]]

[F3] A closed subgroup of a finite-dimensional real Lie group is an embedded
Lie subgroup under ACω; its tangent space is bracket closed.
[[thm-cartans-closed-subgroup-theorem]]
[[prop-the-lie-algebra-of-a-lie-subgroup-is-a-lie-subalgebra]]

[F4] The quotient map $q:SU(2)\to PU(2)$ is a two-sheeted covering, and
$PU(2)$ is topologically isomorphic as a group to $SO(3)$.
[[lem-qc-tqc-pu2-is-so3]]

[F6] Quaternionic conjugation $\rho:SU(2)\to SO(3)$ is a smooth local
diffeomorphism, as shown by its explicit coordinate formula and differential.
[[ex-su-two-to-so-three-as-a-covering-homomorphism]]

[A1] Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, exactly the hypothesis of [F3] and [F4].
[[def-countable-choice]]

## Proof

1.1 Put $C=B_1B_2^{-1}$. Using $F^2=I$, $v^2=u$, and $r+r^{-1}=-u$, direct multiplication gives $\det C=1$ and $\operatorname{tr}C=r+r^{-1}+(2-r-r^{-1})u^2=u^2$; the last equality uses $u^2+u=1$, since $(2+u)u^2=1$. Thus the eigenvalues of the unitary $C$ are $e^{i\theta}$ and $e^{-i\theta}$, with $2\cos\theta=u^2=\phi^{-2}$. [F1, F5, algebra]

2.1 The number $\phi^{-2}$ has minimal polynomial $x^2-3x+1$, whose other root is $\phi^2>2$. If $e^{i\theta}$ were a root of unity, then $e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta}$ would have all algebraic conjugates of absolute value at most $2$, since every such conjugate is a sum of two roots of unity. The conjugate $\phi^2>2$ contradicts this. Hence $\theta/(2\pi)$ is irrational. [given, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Let $\bar\rho:PU(2)\to SO(3)$ be the topological group isomorphism supplied by [F4], and transport the standard smooth Lie-group structure of $SO(3)$ to $PU(2)$ along $\bar\rho$. This transported structure has exactly the quotient-metric topology. The covering map $\rho:SU(2)\to SO(3)$ from [F6] satisfies $\rho=\bar\rho\circ q$ and is a smooth local diffeomorphism; hence $q=\bar\rho^{-1}\circ\rho$ is a smooth local diffeomorphism too, so $dq_I$ is an isomorphism on tangent spaces. Since $\theta/\pi$ is irrational by step 2.1, [F2] shows that the projective powers of $C$ are dense in the smooth circle $T=\{[\operatorname{diag}(e^{it},e^{-it})]:t\in\mathbb R\}\subset PU(2)$ after choosing an eigenbasis; under $\bar\rho$ this is the ordinary rotation circle about an axis. Thus $T\subseteq H$, the closure of the projective braid image, and $H$ is closed in the transported Lie group. By [A1] and [F3], $H$ is an embedded Lie subgroup. In the eigenbasis, $C-C^{-1}=\operatorname{diag}(2i\sin\theta,-2i\sin\theta)$ is a nonzero tangent vector to the corresponding circle in $SU(2)$, so $dq_I(C-C^{-1})$ is a nonzero tangent to $T$ and lies in $\operatorname{Lie}(H)$. [A1, F2, F3, F4, F6, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 Write $X=dq(C-C^{-1})\in\operatorname{Lie}(H)$. Conjugation by $[B_1]$ sends $T$ to a circle with tangent $\operatorname{Ad}_{[B_1]}X$. If these circles had the same axis, the rotation in $SO(3)$ induced by $[B_1]$ would either preserve or reverse that axis. A reversing rotation is a half-turn, but this projective generator has angle $3\pi/5\ne\pi$. If it preserved the oriented axis, it would commute projectively with $C$. Choose a determinant-one lift of $[B_1]$ to $SU(2)$, using [F4]. Its conjugate of $C$ would then be either $C$ or $-C$, because the kernel of $q$ is $\{\pm I\}$. The second case contradicts invariance of trace and $\operatorname{tr}C=\phi^{-2}\ne0$, so $B_1$ would commute with $C$, and hence with $B_2$, contrary to [F1]. Therefore the two axes are nonparallel. Under the standard identification of $\mathfrak{so}(3)$ with $\mathbb R^3$, the Lie bracket is the cross product up to a nonzero scalar; the bracket of two nonparallel axes is nonzero and independent of both. Thus $\operatorname{Lie}(H)=\mathfrak{so}(3)$ by [F3]. [A1, F1, F3, F4, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 The identity component $H^0$ has the full dimension of connected $SO(3)$, so it is an open subgroup. A connected group has no proper open subgroup; therefore $H^0=SO(3)$ and $H=SO(3)$. By [F4], the projective braid image is dense in $PU(2)$. The only choice assumption is [A1], and the conclusion is projective; no efficient braid-length bound follows. [A1, F3, F4, step 4.1, algebra] ∎

ITEM lem-qc-tqc-su2-determinant-lifts-dense


## Statement

Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$. Let $q:SU(2)\to PU(2)$ be the quotient by
$\{\pm I\}$, and let $\Gamma\le PU(2)$ be dense. Then the full inverse image
$q^{-1}(\Gamma)$ is dense in $SU(2)$. Thus projective density gives
determinant-one lifts arbitrarily close to any $SU(2)$ target, while the
choice of a lift can differ by the central sign.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The two-sheeted covering homomorphism $q:SU(2)\to PU(2)$ and a
dense subgroup $\Gamma\le PU(2)$.

[F1] $q:SU(2)\to PU(2)$ is a two-sheeted covering with kernel
$\{\pm I\}$. [[lem-qc-tqc-pu2-is-so3]]

[F2] $\Gamma$ meets every nonempty open subset of $PU(2)$.
[[thm-qc-tqc-fibonacci-single-qubit-density]]

[A1] $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ is the axiom assumed by the two cited suppliers.
[[def-countable-choice]]

## Proof

1.1 Let $O$ be any nonempty open subset of $SU(2)$ and choose $u\in O$. By [F1], there is an evenly covered neighborhood $N_0$ of $q(u)$ and a sheet $S_0$ containing $u$ such that $q|_{S_0}:S_0\to N_0$ is a homeomorphism. Set $S=S_0\cap O$ and $N=q(S)$. Since $S$ is open in $S_0$, $N$ is open in $N_0$; the restriction $q|_S:S\to N$ remains a homeomorphism. [given, F1, choose]

2.1 The open set $N$ meets $\Gamma$ by [F2]; choose $g\in N\cap\Gamma$. Its unique lift $v\in S$ lies in $O$ and satisfies $q(v)=g$, hence $v\in q^{-1}(\Gamma)$. Thus every nonempty open $O$ meets $q^{-1}(\Gamma)$, which is exactly density in $SU(2)$. The only stated axiom is [A1]. [A1, F1, F2, step 1.1, choose] ∎
