## Statement

Let $M$ be a compact connected orientable PL three-manifold with a given finite compatible triangulation. Let $G$ be a boundary component, and let $F\subset G$ be a connected compact PL subsurface with nonempty boundary. Put $E=\overline{G\setminus F}$. Assume that each component of $E$ induces an injective fundamental-group map into $M$.

Let $k\subset F$ be a properly embedded polygonal arc, with distinct endpoints in $\partial F$, which is essential in $F$: it does not cobound an embedded disk in $F$ with an arc in $\partial F$. Suppose $k$ is homotopic in $M$, with its two endpoints fixed, to a path in $E$. The latter path need not be embedded. Then there is a properly embedded PL disk $D\subset M$ whose boundary is $k\cup l$, where $l\subset E$ is an embedded polygonal arc, $k\cap l=\partial k=\partial l$, and $l\setminus\partial l\subset\operatorname{int}E$.

The disk has exactly the prescribed arc $k$ as its trace in $F$. Neither irreducibility of $M$ nor injectivity of $\pi_1(F)\to\pi_1(M)$ is required. All PL structures and finite subdivisions are supplied or constructed by finite operations; no triangulation or smoothing theorem for arbitrary topological three-manifolds is assumed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite PL data, the essential arc and the fixed-endpoint homotopy in the Statement.

[F1] In a compact orientable finite PL three-manifold, a disk map with boundary in an open marked boundary subsurface and outside a prescribed normal subgroup gives a proper embedded PL disk whose boundary remains outside that subgroup. [[thm-loop-theorem-for-finitely-triangulated-orientable-pl-three-manifolds]]

[F2] A nullhomotopic embedded polygonal circle in the interior of a compact oriented finite PL surface bounds a PL disk region. The finite dual-tree argument in its proof constructs a graph spine of any connected surface with boundary, together with a PL identification with its graph thickening; a simply connected such surface is therefore a disk. [[lem-nullhomotopic-circles-on-finite-oriented-pl-surfaces-bound-pl-disks]]

[F3] Compact locally collared PL subpolyhedra have PL collars. Finite polyhedral maps admit compatible subdivisions and simplicial approximations as maps of pairs. [[lem-compact-locally-collared-pl-subpolyhedron-has-pl-collar]], [[thm-finite-simplicial-approximation-for-maps-of-pairs]]

[F4] A simple plane polygon bounds a PL disk, prescribed PL boundary maps extend across polygonal disks, and finite embedded arcs have disk neighborhoods. [[lem-finite-polygonal-disk-and-collar-surgery]]

## Proof

1.1 Form $W=M_+\cup_E M_-$ from two copies of $M$, with opposite orientations, by identifying their copies of $E$ pointwise. This is a compact orientable finite PL three-manifold, allowing corner straightening: away from $\partial E$ two boundary half-balls glue to a ball; along $\partial E$, a transverse two-dimensional section consists of two half-disks glued along one boundary half-radius, a polygonal half-disk after subdividing its sectors and mapping their rays in order. Taking its product with the edge coordinate gives the boundary chart. Compatible subdivisions of the finitely many original simplices and these sector charts supply a finite PL triangulation. The identified copy of $E$ is a properly embedded two-sided surface in $W$. The two copies of $F$ form the closed oriented boundary component $S=F_+\cup_{\partial F}F_-$. There is a continuous folding map $r:W\to M$, equal to the identity copy-map on both halves. Consequently each component of $E$ is $\pi_1$-injective in $W$: a loop nullhomotopic in $W$ folds to a nullhomotopy in $M$, where the assumed injection applies. This uses no irreducibility or amalgam normal-form assertion. [given, F3, construct]

2.1 The doubled arc $c=k_+\cup k_-\subset S$ is a simple polygonal circle and is essential in $S$. Here is a finite surface verification of the last assertion. If cutting $F$ along $k$ leaves a connected surface, its two copies in $S\setminus c$ are joined along the remaining nonempty boundary intervals; hence $S\setminus c$ is connected. By [F2], a nullhomotopic simple circle bounds a disk region and separates, so $c$ is not nullhomotopic. If the cut separates $F$ into $F_1,F_2$, neither is a disk: a disk side would be exactly a disk cobounded by $k$ and an arc of $\partial F$, excluded by essentiality. The two complementary closures $T_1,T_2$ of $c$ in $S$ are made by doubling the corresponding $F_i$ along their old boundary portions, leaving the two copies of $k$ as their single boundary circle $c$. Each $T_i$ folds onto $F_i$. If a $T_i$ were a disk, this retraction would make $F_i$ simply connected and hence a disk by the graph-spine statement of [F2], a contradiction. Were $c$ nullhomotopic, [F2] would make one of its complementary closures a disk. Thus this case too gives an essential $c$. These descriptions follow by cutting the finitely triangulated surface along the actual arc; no homology criterion for a separating arc is used. [F2, step 1.1, given, construct]

3.1 The supplied arc homotopy is a disk map into $M$ with boundary the concatenation of $k$ and a path in $E$. Glue two copies of its parameter disk along their boundary intervals mapping to that path, using the two copies of $M$ in $W$. The resulting parameter space is a disk and the resulting continuous disk map has boundary exactly $c$. Take a small closed PL annular neighborhood $A$ of $c$ in $S$. Apply [F1] with marked boundary subsurface $A$ and normal subgroup $\{1\}\trianglelefteq\pi_1(A)$. Its disk-map boundary is the annular core, hence outside that subgroup. We obtain a proper embedded PL disk $B\subset W$ with essential simple boundary in $\operatorname{int}A$. An essential simple circle in an annulus is isotopic to its core, with a finite PL construction as follows. Realize the annulus as a plane polygonal disk minus an interior polygonal disk. The circle bounds a polygonal disk by [F4]. That disk must contain the inner missing disk: otherwise it contracts the circle in the annulus. The region between these two nested disks, and likewise the region toward the outer boundary, is a polygonal annulus. To check the last identification, join their boundary polygons by a simple polygonal bridge in the region; deleting a narrow open bridge strip leaves a simple polygonal disk, so [F4] parametrizes it by a rectangle with the two bridge shores marked. Reattaching the strip identifies the two marked shores and gives an actual annular parametrization. These parametrizations on either side show that the embedded circle has one passage around the annulus, rather than a nonprimitive power. Put it and the chosen core in transverse position; in this rectangle model each returning pair of intersections cuts off a disk bigon. An innermost such bigon has interior disjoint from both circles; a polygonal disk move across it, supported in a small disk neighborhood, removes that pair. Finite repetition makes the circles disjoint, after which their intervening annular rectangle gives the sliding isotopy. The disk moves use the boundary extensions in [F4] and coning their finite subdivisions. Thus a PL isotopy of the annulus, fixed at its boundary, carries the disk boundary onto $c$. Extend this isotopy over a boundary collar of $W$ by [F3], and take its image of $B$. Thus $B$ may be required to have boundary exactly $c$, pointwise as a subset; no preservation of the original singular disk map is claimed. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, given, construct]

4.1 Put the interior of this embedded disk in finite PL general position with $E$, leaving its boundary $c$ fixed. At the two points $\partial k=c\cap\partial E$, prescribe the half-plane transverse model first. In finitely many interior ball charts, sufficiently small vertex shifts and coning across their stars make disk and $E$ faces transverse, avoid vertices of either sheet lying on the other sheet, and avoid edge-edge intersections after subdivision; transverse edge-face intersections are permitted. At one sheet edge the two incident face-intersection segments join with valence two, since the other sheet crosses that edge transversely; at either fixed endpoint the prescribed boundary half-plane model gives one incident intersection segment. Thus the intersections have the required local one-manifold models; each forbidden coincidence is a proper affine determinant condition, so a finite union cannot fill the allowed open parameter boxes. These shifts are ambient PL moves and preserve the disk's embeddedness, and the collar keeps its boundary fixed. The intersection $B\cap E$ is consequently a finite disjoint union of polygonal circles and proper arcs. Exactly two endpoints occur, namely $\partial k$; hence there is exactly one arc, besides any circles. Each intersection circle is nullhomotopic in $W$, since it lies on the disk $B$, and therefore nullhomotopic in its component of $E$ by step 1.1. By [F2] each bounds a PL disk in $E$. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 Remove the circle intersections by finite disk exchanges. Among the disk regions in $E$ bounded by the intersection circles, choose an inclusion-minimal one, $Q$. Its interior misses every intersection circle. It also misses the intersection arc: that arc has endpoints on $\partial E$, disjoint from $Q\subset\operatorname{int}E$, so it could enter $Q$ only by crossing its boundary circle, contrary to disjointness of the intersection curves. Thus $\operatorname{int}Q\cap B=\varnothing$. Replace the subdisk of $B$ bounded by $\partial Q$ by $Q$, pushing the replacement slightly to the side of $E$ occupied by the retained outside part of $B$ along that circle. The transverse local model has the outside annular collar on one fixed side, so a PL collar of $E$ supplies this push; round the resulting finite polygonal crease in its sector charts. Since the interior of $Q$ misses $B$, a sufficiently small push is disjoint from the retained compact disk, except at its attaching boundary before rounding. The new disk is properly embedded, has the same boundary $c$, and has strictly fewer circle intersections and no new ones. The unique intersection arc is retained because it cannot lie in the removed interior subdisk, which has no point of $\partial B$. Repeating terminates at a disk $B'$ whose intersection with $E$ is precisely one proper embedded arc $l$. [F2, F3, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 Cutting $B'$ along $l$ gives two PL disks, with boundaries $k_+\cup l$ and $k_-\cup l$. Each disk interior misses $E$ and hence lies in one of the two open sides $M_+\setminus E$ or $M_-\setminus E$. The side adjoining $k_+$ is $M_+$, since the original disk is proper in $W$ and $k_+\subset\partial M_+\setminus E$; connectedness places its whole interior there. Therefore that half, viewed in $M$, is the required proper embedded disk $D$. Its only boundary part in $F$ is exactly $k$, and the other boundary arc is $l\subset E$ with its interior in $\operatorname{int}E$. All normal-subgroup, circle-removal and boundary-trace requirements have been justified on actual finite PL objects. [step 1.1, step 5.1, construct] ∎

## Scope

This supplies the prescribed essential-arc disk operation, including a possibly singular comparison path in the incompressible boundary complement. It does not assert simultaneous disjointness for an entire cutting-arc system, manufacture the embedded essential annuli used in the closed-surface case, or prove the product-recognition and marking-homeomorphism theorems. Those require their actual subsequent arguments. The full Waldhausen paper was read as extracted text, including the proof of Lemma 5.2; the doubling proof above replaces its appeal to a special feature of the Loop-theorem construction rather than treating that appeal as a proved interface.
