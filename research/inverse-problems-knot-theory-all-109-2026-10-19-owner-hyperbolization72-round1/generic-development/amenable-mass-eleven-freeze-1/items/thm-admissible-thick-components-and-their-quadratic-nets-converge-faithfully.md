## Statement

Assume AC, hence Countable Choice. Let $X_n=H^2/\Gamma_n$ be pointed at $x_n=[i]$ with $\operatorname{inj}_{X_n}(x_n)\ge\eta>0$ and $\operatorname{area}(X_n)\le A<\infty$. Fix a thick-pointed limit $X=H^2/\Gamma$ of the actual atlas theorem, pointed at $x=[i]$. Call a number
$$0<\varepsilon<\min(\eta,\eta_*/2)$$
admissible if $2\varepsilon$ is not the translation length of ANY nonidentity loxodromic element of $\Gamma$. Here $\eta_*$ is the local displacement threshold. Admissible numbers exist in every subinterval of this displayed interval, since the excluded set is countable.

Let $Z$ and $Z_n$ be the connected components containing the distinguished points of the respective closed sets $\{\operatorname{inj}\ge\varepsilon\}$. For every admissible $\varepsilon$, they are eventually smoothly diffeomorphic by actual embeddings $P_n:Z\to X_n$ with image EXACTLY $Z_n$, extending to open neighborhoods and converging in all derivatives to identity in the limiting quotient charts. This is faithful convergence of the entire components, not merely forward embeddings of their compact subsets.

If $q_n\to q$ locally in all derivatives in those charts, then
$$\int_{Z_n}|q_n|\longrightarrow\int_Z|q|.$$
If $q\ne0$, for every $\delta>0$ the finite equal flat net from [[lem-compact-surface-regions-admit-finite-zero-free-quadratic-nets-carrying-their-monodromy]] on $Z$ transports to nets $U_n\subset\operatorname{int}Z_n$ with the same finite intersection graph, exact common disc area, uniformly positive lens fractions, and
$$\limsup_n\int_{Z_n\setminus U_n}|q_n|<\delta.$$
Their inclusions surject onto $\pi_1(Z_n)$, under the marking $P_n$; the finite monodromy labels agree under that marking. No subgroup-expansion or final amenable-mass conclusion is inferred from this analytic transport alone.

## Proof

1.1 The finite-area limit is nonelementary by [F2]. In particular $\Gamma$ contains a nonidentity element. On every fixed compact lifted set $B\subset H^2$, the injectivity functions are minima of a FIXED FINITE family of approximating displacement functions for all sufficiently large $n$. Here is the full compactness argument. One fixed nonidentity $u\in\Gamma$ and its approximants bound $\operatorname{inj}_{X_n}([z])$ above by one common finite $M$ on $B$, and also bound the limiting radius. Every minimizing matrix then moves some $z\in B$ by at most $2M$. Compact point-frame fibers put all such matrices in a fixed compact subset of the matrix group. Every finite limit lies in $\Gamma$, and no nonidentity sequence can tend to identity by [F1]. There are only finitely many possible nonidentity limits there, since $\Gamma$ is discrete. Choose their approximants. If infinitely many minimizing matrices were not among them, compactness would give a convergent subsequence to one of those limits, contradicting eventual uniqueness of approximants. The limiting minima likewise belong to that same finite set, after enlarging the compact matrix bound slightly if necessary. Thus on $B$ $$\operatorname{inj}_{X_n}([z])=\min_{u\in F}\tfrac12d(z,u_nz),\qquad \operatorname{inj}_X([z])=\min_{u\in F}\tfrac12d(z,uz)$$ for one finite $F\subset\Gamma\setminus\{1\}$. Each displayed function converges smoothly on $B$, so their finite minima converge uniformly. This proves local uniform injectivity convergence with all competing matrices accounted for. [A1, F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Near any point of the limiting level $\operatorname{inj}=\varepsilon$, the minimum is locally one smooth primitive displacement function, up to its inverse giving the same value. All elements of displacement at most $2\varepsilon<\eta_*$ there belong to a single real cyclic stabilizer by [F2]. A primitive generator strictly minimizes over its nontrivial powers; the other finitely many competitors from step 1.1 have a strict gap. Thus on a neighborhood the limit and late $n$ minima are respectively $\tfrac12d(z,uz)$ and $\tfrac12d(z,u_nz)$ for that generator and its unique approximants. This remains true even if one worries about a new root in $\Gamma_n$: its smaller displacement would be another minimizer from the finite family and would contradict the strict limiting gap. The resulting functions converge in every derivative. In a parabolic coordinate the function is $\operatorname{arsinh}(\ell/(2y))$, with nonzero gradient. In axis-normal loxodromic coordinates it is $\operatorname{arsinh}(\sinh(L/2)\cosh r)$, whose gradient vanishes exactly on $r=0$ at value $L/2$. Admissibility excludes that value. Hence the limiting level is regular and its nearby varying levels converge smoothly. [F1, F2, step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 The level set is compact and has finitely many smooth circles by [F2]. The group $\Gamma$ is countable because it is discrete in a second-countable matrix group. Its loxodromic translation lengths therefore exclude only countably many thresholds. To exhibit an admissible threshold in any desired open subinterval, enumerate those excluded numbers and choose successively nested closed rational subintervals, each inside the preceding interior, avoiding the next number and with lengths tending to zero. Their unique common point avoids every excluded number and remains in the original subinterval. This uses [A1] at most. Around each level circle choose a small smooth collar whose transverse coordinate is $t=\operatorname{inj}-\varepsilon$. Such a collar exists directly from the explicit cusp height or axis-normal coordinate in step 2.1, restricted where its derivative is nonzero; the cyclic period makes it a global circle collar. There are finitely many disjoint collars, and their widths can be chosen with a common positive lower bound. [A1, F2, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Choose a compact smooth cusp truncation $K\subset X$ containing the entire limiting $\varepsilon$-thick set and all these collars in its interior, and also any fixed bounded quotient ball required below. Its boundary, when nonempty, can be chosen at injectivity radius less than $\varepsilon/4$, by the explicit cusp formula in [F2]. If $X$ is closed, take $K=X$. Take [F1]'s smooth embeddings $F_n$ on a neighborhood of a slightly larger compact truncation. The functions $f_n=\operatorname{inj}_{X_n}\circ F_n$ converge uniformly on $K$ to $f=\operatorname{inj}_X$ by step 1.1, and smoothly on the finite level collars by step 2.1 and the smooth coordinate convergence of $F_n$. On a collar $(s,t)$ the limit function is $\varepsilon+t$. For large $n$, $\partial_t f_n$ is positive and close to one. The equation $f_n(s,t)=\varepsilon$ therefore has a unique small solution $t=t_n(s)$, smooth with all derivatives tending to zero: use the inverse theorem [F3] for $(s,t)\mapsto(s,f_n(s,t)-\varepsilon)$ and uniqueness on overlaps. Signs on the two fixed collar rims supply existence throughout the circle. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 On those disjoint collars define $G_n(s,t)=(s,t+\chi(t)t_n(s))$, where a fixed smooth cutoff is one near zero and zero near each collar rim, and extend by identity outside. Its derivative tends to identity, the transverse derivative is positive, and it fixes the collar rims; hence it is a global smooth diffeomorphism of a neighborhood of $K$, tending to identity in every derivative. It sends each limiting level circle to the actual $f_n=\varepsilon$ circle. Inside the collars it preserves the indicated thick side, by monotonicity. Outside them $|f-\varepsilon|$ has a positive minimum on the remaining compact set, and uniform convergence makes the signs agree. Consequently $$G_n(\{f\ge\varepsilon\}\cap K)=\{f_n\ge\varepsilon\}\cap K.$$ Define $P_n=F_nG_n$. This is the claimed smooth comparison with EXACT level-set matching inside the comparison compact region. [F1, F3, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 We prove that the matched base component is the WHOLE $Z_n$, not just a subset. There are two explicit capture checks. First, when $\partial K\ne\varnothing$, step 4.1 and uniform convergence put $\operatorname{inj}_{X_n}<\varepsilon/2$ on $F_n(\partial K)$. A connected thick component meeting $F_n(\operatorname{int}K)$ cannot leave it without meeting that boundary; connectedness and the separating boundary of an embedded compact region suffice. Thus no thick connection can escape through this comparison boundary. When $X$ is closed, $F_n(X)$ is compact and open in connected $X_n$, hence is all of $X_n$, so capture is immediate. Independently, [F1]'s area/r-net bound puts every base thick component in a common radius-$R$ quotient ball. Every point of such a ball lifts to the FIXED compact model ball $\overline B(i,R)$; finitely many eventual injective limit charts cover it. This is the actual converse chart capture proved in the unscaled mass theorem. Taking $K$ larger if needed, the corresponding chart images lie in $F_n(\operatorname{int}K)$, by local real inverse estimates and a finite compact cover. Thus the full $Z_n$ is captured even without inferring exhaustion from forward embeddings alone. [F1, F2, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 The distinguished point $F_n(x)$ need not be exactly $x_n$, but it converges to it in their common base chart. Both points and the short connecting chart segment lie in $\{\operatorname{inj}>\varepsilon\}$ for large $n$, since the base radius has the strict margin $\eta-\varepsilon>0$. The same holds after the vanishing perturbation $G_n$; the source basepoint lies away from the level collars. Thus $P_n(Z)$ meets the correct component $Z_n$. By step 5.1 it is a connected component of the matched thick set inside $F_n(K)$. Step 6.1 shows that the actual base component is entirely captured there and has no connection to an outside component. It follows that $P_n(Z)=Z_n$. The maps extend to neighborhoods and converge smoothly by construction. This establishes faithful convergence of the entire components including their smooth boundaries. [F1, F2, step 5.1, step 6.1, given, algebra]

8.1 Suppose now the quadratics converge locally in all derivatives. On each fixed source coordinate compact set, pullback of their mass by $P_n$ has density $$|q_n(P_n(z))|\,|\det DP_n(z)|,$$ where $q_n(P_n(z))$ denotes its coefficient in the corresponding target chart. It tends uniformly to the source coefficient's modulus. A finite coordinate partition of unity over compact $Z$ and measurable change of variables [F5] give $$\int_{Z_n}|q_n|=\int_ZP_n^*|q_n|\longrightarrow\int_Z|q|.$$ No holomorphicity of $P_n$ was assumed and no global quadratic norm-continuity theorem was used; this is a fixed compact-region Jacobian calculation after actual whole-component capture. [F5, step 7.1, given, construct, algebra]

9.1 If $q\ne0$, choose [F4]'s finite flat net $U$ inside $\operatorname{int}Z$ with omitted mass less than $\delta$. Its closed discs and extended neighborhoods are compactly contained in that interior. Transport them by the actual flat-chart construction, obtaining $U_n$. Local convergence and the real inverse comparison imply $U_n\subset\operatorname{int}Z_n$ for large $n$: their pulled-back closures under $P_n$ stay in a fixed compact neighborhood of the source discs, still inside $Z$. The same compact-interior argument shows that any fixed compact subset of $U$ lies in $P_n^{-1}(U_n)$ eventually. To see this uniformly, cover it by finitely many smaller closed subdiscs lying inside individual net discs; smooth convergence of the exact flat chart inverses preserves that strict interior inclusion. The net has the same finite intersection graph and positive uniform lens constants by [F4], since it has no boundary-only contacts. Finite-union mass convergence there and step 8.1 give $$\int_{Z_n\setminus U_n}|q_n|\longrightarrow\int_{Z\setminus U}|q|<\delta.$$ [F4, F5, step 7.1, step 8.1, given, construct, algebra]

10.1 Finally choose finitely many based loops in $U$ whose images generate $\pi_1(Z)$; [F4]'s finite net graph supplies them. Their compact union lies in $P_n^{-1}(U_n)$ for large $n$ by step 9.1. Its image under the diffeomorphism $P_n:Z\to Z_n$ therefore proves that $\pi_1(U_n)\to\pi_1(Z_n)$ is onto. The actual transported center-edge paths can be chosen homotopic to these marked paths in their fixed overlapping compact charts: local convergence places them in the same zero-free disc and lens neighborhoods, where path endpoints can be joined and disc contractions supply the homotopy. Equivalently their finite deck-transition words are the unique approximants in [F1], exactly as proved in [F4]. Thus the finite graph monodromy labels agree under the marking $P_n$. This completes all whole-component, mass and net transport claims. It does not turn local graph convergence into a global expansion assertion. [F1, F4, step 7.1, step 9.1, given, algebra] ∎
