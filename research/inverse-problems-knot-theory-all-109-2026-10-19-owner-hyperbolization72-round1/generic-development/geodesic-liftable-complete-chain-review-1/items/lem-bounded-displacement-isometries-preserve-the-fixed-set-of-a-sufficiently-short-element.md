## Statement

Assume AC. For every $C\ge0$ there is $\delta(C)>0$ such that the following holds uniformly. Let $\Gamma\le\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)$ be discrete torsion-free, let $p\in H^3$, and let $1\ne\gamma\in\Gamma$ satisfy $d(p,\gamma p)<\delta(C)$. Then EVERY $h\in\Gamma$ with $d(p,hp)\le C$ preserves the ideal fixed set of $\gamma$ and belongs to its full abelian elementary stabilizer in $\Gamma$. That stabilizer is cyclic loxodromic if $\gamma$ is loxodromic, or a rank-one or rank-two translation lattice if $\gamma$ is parabolic.

In particular, if $h_1,h_2\in\Gamma$ do not commute and both move $p$ by at most $C$, then every nonidentity element moves $p$ by at least $\delta(C)$. More generally, if two such elements move a point $q$ by at most $C$ and $d(p,q)\le D$, then every nonidentity element moves $p$ by at least $\delta(C+2D)$. No finite generation, geometric finiteness or finite-volume hypothesis is imposed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the discrete torsion-free group, the point and the displacement bound.

[A1] Full choice is [[def-axiom-of-choice]].

[F1] There is a universal $\eta_*>0$ for which the subgroup generated by the elements moving any point by at most $\eta_*$ is trivial or abelian elementary. Nontrivial elementary fixed-set stabilizers have exactly the cyclic loxodromic or parabolic translation-lattice forms in the Statement. The proof includes elliptic exclusion, the order-two nature of an ideal-pair interchange, and the exclusion of a loxodromic sharing a parabolic point in a discrete group containing that parabolic. [[lem-local-thin-components-of-torsion-free-kleinian-quotients]]

[F2] The upper-half-space and Hermitian models give the $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)$ action, the stabilizer $\mathrm{PSU}(2)$ of $o=(0,1)$ and the displacement formula $$\cosh d(o,(w,t))=\frac{|w|^2+t^2+1}{2t}.$$ Matrix action is continuous, and model isometries act transitively. [[lem-complete-curvature-minus-one-manifolds-are-kleinian-quotients]]

## Proof

1.1 Normalize $p=o$ by an isometry. For an isometry $g$ taking $o$ to $(w,t)$ use the determinant-one matrix $$T(w,t)=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt t&w/\sqrt t\\0&1/\sqrt t\end{pmatrix},$$ which takes $o$ to that same point. A determinant-one lift $G$ of $g$ is therefore $T(w,t)U$ with $U\in\mathrm{SU}(2)$, by [F2]. If $d(o,go)\le d\le1$, then $e^{-d}\le t\le e^d$ and $$|w|/\sqrt t\le\sqrt{2\cosh d-2}=2\sinh(d/2).$$ The diagonal entries differ from one by at most $e^{d/2}-1$. Thus for the operator norm one has $\|T-I\|\le Kd$ with, for example, $K=10$, and consequently $$\|G-U\|\le Kd,\qquad\|G\|\le1+Kd.$$ For an isometry $h$ moving $o$ by at most $C$, the same formula bounds both a lift $H$ and its inverse by $B_C=4e^{(C+1)/2}$: the squared Frobenius norm of its triangular factor is $t+t^{-1}+|w|^2/t=2\cosh d(o,ho)\le2\cosh C$, and the inverse triangular factor has the same bound. Multiplication by a unitary does not change operator norms. These are explicit uniform bounds, not compactness of a varying family of groups. [F2, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 Every $U\in\mathrm{SU}(2)$ has eigenvalues $e^{i\theta},e^{-i\theta}$ and an orthonormal eigenbasis. Indeed one complex eigenvalue exists from its quadratic characteristic polynomial, its modulus is one since $U$ preserves norm, and its orthogonal complement is invariant and has reciprocal eigenvalue by determinant one. For any integer $N\ge2$, place the $N+1$ points $0,\theta,\ldots,N\theta$ on the circle divided into $N$ equal half-open arcs. Two lie in one arc; their difference gives an integer $1\le k\le N$ with distance of $k\theta$ from $2\pi\mathbb Z$ at most $2\pi/N$. In the orthonormal eigenbasis this yields $$\|U^k-I\|\le2\pi/N.$$ Thus a bounded positive power is near identity uniformly over the entire point stabilizer, including rotations with no small angle at the first power. [F2, construct, algebra]

2.1 Fix $C$ and put $M_C=B_C^2$. By continuity in [F2], choose $a>0$ such that $\|A-I\|<a$ for a determinant-one matrix implies $d(o,Ao)<\eta_*$. Put $\rho=a/(2M_C)$. Choose an integer $N\ge2$ with $2\pi/N<\rho/2$, and then choose $$0<\delta(C)<\min\{1,\rho/(4NK e^{NK})\}.$$ For a lift $G$ of an element of displacement $d<\delta(C)$, take the unitary $U$ in step 1.1 and its power $k\le N$ in step 1.2. The telescoping identity for matrix powers gives $$\|G^k-U^k\|\le k(1+Kd)^{k-1}Kd\le NKd e^{NKd}<\rho/4.$$ Hence $\|G^k-I\|<\rho$. For every lift $H$ of an element of displacement at most $C$, $$\|HG^kH^{-1}-I\|\le M_C\|G^k-I\|<a/2.$$ Both $G^k$ and its conjugate therefore move $o$ by less than $\eta_*$. The choices $a,N,\delta(C)$ depend only on $C$ and the fixed model/classifier; no choice depends on $\Gamma,\gamma$ or $h$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 Apply [F1] to $\gamma^k$ and $h\gamma^kh^{-1}$. They lie in the same abelian elementary subgroup, and $\gamma^k$ is nonidentity because the group is torsion-free. Powers of a nontrivial parabolic have the same unique fixed point; powers of a loxodromic have the same two fixed points. Thus the two displayed elements have the same fixed set, and $h$ preserves the fixed set of $\gamma$. If it is a pair, $h$ cannot interchange its two points, since an interchange has order two. In a coordinate putting them at zero and infinity, it is diagonal and belongs to the full cyclic loxodromic stabilizer in [F1]. If it is a single point, put it at infinity and write $\gamma(z)=z+v$, $h(z)=az+b$. If $|a|\ne1$, conjugating that nontrivial translation by the suitable powers of $h$ gives translations tending to identity, contradicting discreteness. If $|a|=1$ and $a\ne1$, the affine map has a finite fixed point and is elliptic, excluded by [F1]. Hence $a=1$. All such elements are translations and belong to the full rank-one or rank-two abelian lattice. This proves the asserted fixed-set and stabilizer conclusion for EVERY bounded-displacement $h$. [F1, step 2.1, given, construct, algebra]

4.1 If two bounded-displacement elements $h_1,h_2$ do not commute, the existence of a nonidentity $\delta(C)$-short $\gamma$ would put both in the same abelian stabilizer by step 3.1, a contradiction. Thus no such $\gamma$ exists. Finally $$d(p,h_ip)\le2d(p,q)+d(q,h_iq)\le C+2D.$$ Apply the same result at $p$ with this bound. This proves the last assertion and all stated uniformity, including arbitrary infinitely generated groups and all parabolic ranks. The lemma supplies a separation operation once the actual two short noncommuting loops are constructed; it does not manufacture those loops or comparison charts from an unproved dome assertion. [step 3.1, given, algebra] ∎
