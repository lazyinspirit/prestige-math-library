## Statement

Assume AC. Let $\Gamma_n\le\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb R)$ be arbitrary discrete torsion-free groups, let $z_n\in H$, and put $X_n=H/\Gamma_n$, $x_n=[z_n]$. Suppose $a_n=\operatorname{inj}_{X_n}(x_n)>0$ tends to zero. After a subsequence and real Möbius changes of the lifted models, there are actual holomorphic embeddings of every fixed relatively compact domain in the cylinder $C=\mathbb C/\mathbb Z$ into $X_n$, carrying $[0]$ to $x_n$, compatible on their domains, whose pulled-back metrics $a_n^{-2}g_{X_n}$ converge in every $C^k$ norm to $4|dw|^2$. More precisely the domains can be an increasing exhaustion by cylinder bands. They give a convergent connected holomorphic atlas with overlap convergence and asymptotic separation as in [[thm-convergent-holomorphic-atlases-support-projective-quadratic-compactness]]. The cylinder has its usual inherited complex orientation; the choice of positive generator determines its real direction.

For arbitrary supplied $L_n\le\Gamma_n$, pointed by $[z_n]$ in $Y_n=H/L_n$, a further subsequence has simultaneous local covering limit $C_m\to C$ for a fixed $m\in\{1,2,\ldots\}\cup\{\infty\}$. Here $C_m=\mathbb C/(m\mathbb Z)$ for finite $m$, $C_\infty=\mathbb C$, and the map is induced by the identity on $\mathbb C$. The lifted metrics, scaled by the BASE $a_n^{-2}$, converge to $4|dw|^2$ on compact sets of $C_m$. The covering limit need not have finite degree and the original covers need not be regular.

When the $X_n$ also are compactifications of a fixed genus with a fixed finite number of marked punctures, any sequence of nonzero simple-pole quadratic differentials on $X_n$ consequently has a nonzero holomorphic projective limit on this connected cylinder by the cited analytic theorem. No integrability of that projective limit, concentration of quadratic mass, extension of this local limit over a nodal surface, or identification of amenable components is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the Fuchsian groups, basepoints with positive injectivity radii tending to zero, and the extra subgroups or fixed finite-type data only for their corresponding conclusions.

[A1] Choice is [[def-axiom-of-choice]].

[F1] The actual arbitrary-quotient thin classifier gives a universal $\eta_*>0$. At any fixed displacement level $0<\eta<\eta_*$, each thin component in $H^3$ is a precisely invariant horoball with full translation stabilizer of rank one or two, or a finite-radius tube with cyclic loxodromic stabilizer. Its closed stabilizer quotient embeds in the full quotient; different closed components are separated. Injectivity radius equals half the least nontrivial displacement and the quotient is proper. No finite-volume hypothesis occurs. [[lem-local-thin-components-of-torsion-free-kleinian-quotients]]

[F2] The real invariant hyperbolic plane has metric $(dx^2+dy^2)/y^2$. Its model distance satisfies $\cosh d(z,z')=1+|z-z'|^2/(2\operatorname{Im}z\operatorname{Im}z')$, from the actual Hermitian/hyperboloid distance formula. Real Möbius maps are its orientation-preserving isometries. [[lem-complete-curvature-minus-one-manifolds-are-kleinian-quotients]]

[F3] On a supplied connected convergent holomorphic atlas with actual overlap convergence and asymptotic separation, fixed-genus fixed-puncture nonzero simple-pole quadratics have a nonzero holomorphic projective subsequential limit. No integrability assertion is part of this conclusion. [[thm-convergent-holomorphic-atlases-support-projective-quadratic-compactness]]

## Proof

1.1 Fix $0<\eta<\eta_*$ and discard finitely many terms so $2a_n<\eta$. Regard $\Gamma_n$ as preserving the real plane in $H^3$. The point $z_n$ lies in a thin component $E_n$ from [F1]. Its stabilizer $A_n$ is cyclic. Indeed a parabolic stabilizer fixing a real point is conjugate by a real Möbius map to real translations $z\mapsto z+b$, $b\in\mathbb R$, so discreteness makes it rank one. A nontrivial elementary group preserving an ideal pair is cyclic loxodromic by [F1]. Thus no rank-two case occurs in the real subgroup. The intersection $E_n\cap H$ is a horodisc or a connected two-sided strip about the real loxodromic axis. Its quotient by $A_n$ embeds in $X_n$: any two of its points identified by $\Gamma_n$ must differ by the full stabilizer, by precise invariance of $E_n$. The least displacement at $z_n$ is attained, since model point stabilizers are compact and $\Gamma_n$ is discrete. Its minimizing element moves $z_n$ by $2a_n<\eta$ and belongs to $A_n$. Passing to a subsequence, all $A_n$ are parabolic or all loxodromic. We treat both explicitly. [A1, F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 In the parabolic case conjugate the fixed point to infinity and choose the generator $T_n(z)=z+\ell_n$ with $\ell_n>0$. Write the conjugated basepoint $z_n=b_n+iY_n$. By [F2], $$\sinh\bigl(d(z,T_n^kz)/2\bigr)=\frac{|k|\ell_n}{2\operatorname{Im}z}.$$ Among nonzero powers the generator minimizes displacement. As the minimizing element of step 1.1 belongs to $A_n$, $$\sinh a_n=\frac{\ell_n}{2Y_n},\qquad \frac{Y_n}{\ell_n}\longrightarrow\infty.$$ Use the holomorphic coordinate $z=z_n+\ell_n w$, where $w\mapsto w+1$ is the generator. It is defined on $\operatorname{Im}w>-Y_n/\ell_n$. At $|\operatorname{Im}w|\le R$ its generator half-displacement satisfies $$\sinh\bigl(d(z,T_nz)/2\bigr)=\frac{\sinh a_n}{1+(\ell_n/Y_n)\operatorname{Im}w}\longrightarrow0$$ uniformly. For large $n$ the whole band therefore lies in $E_n\cap H$. Its quotient by $\mathbb Z$ embeds in $X_n$ by step 1.1. The pulled-back scaled metric is $$a_n^{-2}g_{X_n}=\frac{(\ell_n/Y_n)^2}{a_n^2}\frac{|dw|^2}{(1+(\ell_n/Y_n)\operatorname{Im}w)^2}\longrightarrow4|dw|^2.$$ The convergence is $C^k$ for every $k$ on each fixed band: $\ell_n/Y_n=2\sinh a_n\to0$, its ratio to $a_n$ tends to two, and every positive derivative of the denominator contributes a power of this vanishing number. This also records the exact normalization constant. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 In the loxodromic case conjugate the endpoints to zero and infinity and choose the generator $T_n(z)=e^{\ell_n}z$ with $\ell_n>0$. The branch $\zeta=\log z=u+iv$, $0<v<\pi$, is holomorphic on $H$. Write $\log z_n=u_n+iv_n$. The distance formula [F2] gives $$\sinh\bigl(d(z,T_n^kz)/2\bigr)=\frac{\sinh(|k|\ell_n/2)}{\sin v}.$$ Thus the generator again minimizes among nonzero powers, and $$\sinh a_n=\frac{\sinh(\ell_n/2)}{\sin v_n}.$$ In particular $\ell_n\le2a_n\to0$ and $$\frac{\ell_n}{a_n\sin v_n}=2\frac{\ell_n/2}{\sinh(\ell_n/2)}\frac{\sinh a_n}{a_n}\longrightarrow2,\qquad \frac{\ell_n}{\sin v_n}\longrightarrow0.$$ Since $v_n\ge\sin v_n$ and $\pi-v_n\ge\sin v_n$, both $v_n/\ell_n$ and $(\pi-v_n)/\ell_n$ tend to infinity. The holomorphic coordinate $\log z=u_n+iv_n+\ell_n w$ is therefore defined on bands exhausting $\mathbb C$, and its generator is again $w\mapsto w+1$. [F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 On every fixed band $|\operatorname{Im}w|\le R$ the sine quotient $$\frac{\sin(v_n+\ell_n\operatorname{Im}w)}{\sin v_n}=\cos(\ell_n\operatorname{Im}w)+\cot v_n\sin(\ell_n\operatorname{Im}w)$$ tends to one in every $C^k$ norm. Indeed $\ell_n|\cot v_n|\le\ell_n/\sin v_n\to0$; the first derivative of the second term is bounded by that quantity, and each further derivative supplies another factor $\ell_n$. The zero-order estimate follows from $|\sin s|\le|s|$, while cosine derivatives are immediate. Consequently the generator displacement on this band tends uniformly to zero by step 2.2, so the band lies in $E_n\cap H$ for large $n$. Quotienting by its generator gives an actual embedded cylinder band in $X_n$. Since the upper-half-plane metric in the log coordinate is $|d\zeta|^2/\sin^2(\operatorname{Im}\zeta)$, the scaled metric is $$a_n^{-2}g_{X_n}=\frac{\ell_n^2}{a_n^2\sin^2 v_n}\left(\frac{\sin v_n}{\sin(v_n+\ell_n\operatorname{Im}w)}\right)^2|dw|^2\longrightarrow4|dw|^2$$ in every $C^k$ norm. This proves the asserted cylinder limit in both cases. Only explicit generator-displacement estimates on each band were used; no assertion that a large metric ball is contained in a thin component is needed. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

4.1 Choose a countable atlas of small Euclidean discs in $\mathbb C$ on which projection to $C=\mathbb C/\mathbb Z$ is injective on closures and a neighborhood. On each fixed finite collection, the preceding band embeddings exist for large $n$, and their coordinate transitions are exactly integer translations, unchanged with $n$. Closed coordinate subdiscs having disjoint cylinder images continue to have disjoint images in $X_n$: the whole containing band embeds. These are exactly overlap convergence and asymptotic separation, including finite simultaneous disjointness, required by [F3]. The domains exhaust $C$ and their embeddings agree on all common domains because they come from the same explicit coordinate formula. The basepoint is $w=0$ throughout. This verifies the atlas statement rather than merely formal convergence of the metric coefficients. [A1, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

4.2 Now let $L_n\le\Gamma_n$. The cyclic subgroup $L_n\cap A_n$ is $\langle T_n^{m_n}\rangle$ for one $m_n\in\{1,2,\ldots\}\cup\{\infty\}$, where $m_n=\infty$ denotes the trivial subgroup. This follows directly from division with remainder in $\mathbb Z$. Passing to a subsequence, either $m_n$ is a fixed finite $m$, or $m_n\to\infty$, with the infinity convention allowed. The same bands in the lifted plane embed modulo this subgroup in $Y_n$: if two of their points are identified by $L_n$, they are identified by $\Gamma_n$ inside $E_n$, hence by $L_n\cap A_n$. For fixed finite $m$, their quotients are bands of $C_m$, and the cover to the base cylinder band is exactly $w\bmod m\mathbb Z\mapsto w\bmod\mathbb Z$. If $m_n\to\infty$, every fixed compact rectangle in $\mathbb C$ projects injectively modulo $m_n\mathbb Z$ for late $n$, since the real width is eventually smaller than $m_n$. It also lies in one of the bands where all the preceding metric estimates hold. Thus such rectangles give actual plane charts for $Y_n$, with the same $C^k$ scaled metric limit $4|dw|^2$, and the limiting covering map to $C$ is the identity modulo $\mathbb Z$. No normality of $L_n$ is used. [A1, F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 These cover charts also have actual overlap convergence and asymptotic separation. For finite $m$ use small discs modulo $m\mathbb Z$ and the embedded bands; their overlap translations and cover expressions are fixed. For $m=\infty$ take any finite collection of compact plane discs. Their mutual real coordinate differences are bounded, so no nonzero multiple of $m_n$ can identify two of their points for large $n$; inside $E_n$ all possible identifications were already reduced to those multiples in step 4.2. Hence disjoint limit compact discs remain disjoint. Within a source chart and any target chart containing its image, the covering expression is the same integer translation used in the limiting map, thus is holomorphic and converges exactly. The pointed cover limit is connected, either $C_m$ or the plane; no other sheets or global mass are asserted captured. [F3, step 4.1, step 4.2, construct, algebra]

6.1 Finally, if fixed genus and puncture data and nonzero simple-pole quadratics on $X_n$ are supplied, the actual cylinder atlas of step 4.1 satisfies all hypotheses of [F3]. Its projective quadratic conclusion applies on this connected limit. This analytic operation uses only the convergent holomorphic atlases, not any erroneous inference that rescaling hyperbolic metric changes the underlying quadratic differential or preserves its global $L^1$ norm. In particular nonzero projective normalization alone does not prove integrability or faithful mass capture. All claimed thin surface, cover and optional projective conclusions follow. [F3, step 4.1, given, algebra] ∎
