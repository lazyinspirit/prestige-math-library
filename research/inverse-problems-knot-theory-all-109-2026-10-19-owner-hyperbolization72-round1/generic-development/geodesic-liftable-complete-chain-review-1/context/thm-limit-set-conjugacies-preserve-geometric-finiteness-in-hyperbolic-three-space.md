## Statement

Assume AC. Let $\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2\le\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)$ be discrete torsion-free nonelementary groups, with limit sets $\Lambda_1,\Lambda_2$. Suppose $\theta:\Gamma_1\to\Gamma_2$ is an isomorphism and $h:\Lambda_1\to\Lambda_2$ is a homeomorphism satisfying $h(\gamma p)=\theta(\gamma)h(p)$. Then $\Gamma_1$ is geometrically finite if and only if $\Gamma_2$ is, where geometric finiteness means finite volume of some positive-radius neighborhood of the convex core. In fact each group then has finite volume for every fixed-radius convex neighborhood, compact convex truncation, finitely many parabolic orbits, and both parabolic ranks as in the explicit criterion theorem.

The conjugacy sends conical limit points to conical limit points and bounded-parabolic points to bounded-parabolic points. The conical criterion here is intrinsic to the action on the limit set: $p\in\Lambda$ is conical if and only if there are distinct $g_j\in\Gamma$ and distinct $u,v\in\Lambda$ such that
$$g_jp\longrightarrow u,\qquad g_jq\longrightarrow v\quad\text{for every }q\in\Lambda\setminus\{p\}.$$
For the geometric-to-boundary implication the latter convergence is locally uniform on the entire sphere minus $p$.

In particular, any sphere homeomorphism conjugating these two Möbius groups sends their limit sets onto one another and preserves geometric finiteness. This applies to quasiconformal sphere conjugacies, without assuming finite generation, connected conformal boundary, absence of rank-one cusps or a quasiconformal extension to the interior. Both criterion directions, rather than only boundary dynamics, are part of the proof chain.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, both discrete torsion-free nonelementary groups and the supplied equivariant limit-set homeomorphism; in the last assertion a whole-sphere conjugacy is supplied instead.

[A1] Full choice is [[def-axiom-of-choice]].

[F1] The Hermitian hyperboloid realizes the model with $g:X\mapsto gXg^*$; its point stabilizer is compact, bounded-displacement isometry sets have compact closure, model closed balls are compact, and Möbius maps act by their isometric extensions. The upper half-space distance and geodesic formulas hold. [F2] Discrete torsion-free groups act freely and properly, with complete proper quotient; the local thin classifier proves the required elementary matrix and compact-fiber assertions without finite volume. [[lem-local-thin-components-of-torsion-free-kleinian-quotients]]

[F3] Finite positive-radius convex-neighborhood volume implies every limit point is conical or bounded parabolic. Conversely that boundary-point condition implies compact convex truncation, finitely many uniformly separated cusp orbits of ranks one or two, and finite volume of every fixed-radius convex neighborhood. ## Proof

1.1 We first give the matrix convergence fact used to recognize the limit set under a sphere conjugacy. For any sequence of distinct elements $g_j$ of a discrete group, choose determinant-one lifts $G_j$ and pass to a subsequence such that $G_j/\|G_j\|_{HS}$ converges to a norm-one matrix $A$. Their norms tend to infinity along a subsequence, since bounded lifts lie in a compact matrix set and a discrete subgroup meets such a set in finitely many elements. Thus $\det A=0$ and $A$ has rank one. Its kernel and image are projective points $b,a$. Projectivizing the normalized matrices shows $g_j\to a$ locally uniformly outside $b$: on a compact set of projective vectors away from the kernel the norm of $Av$ has a positive lower bound, so ordinary uniform matrix convergence can be divided by that norm. The adjugate matrices give the corresponding inverse convergence away from $a$ toward $b$. Also $g_jo\to a$ and $g_j^{-1}o\to b$ in the closed-ball compactification, for any model $o$. For $o=I$, the trace-normalized Hermitian matrix $G_jG_j^*/\operatorname{tr}(G_jG_j^*)$ tends to $AA^*/\operatorname{tr}(AA^*)$, the rank-one positive matrix representing the ideal point $a$; the inverse gives $b$. Replacing $o$ by another positive Hermitian model point gives the same image line and kernel conclusion. Consequently both $a,b$ lie in the limit set. The limit set is closed and invariant: its orbit-accumulation definition in the compact closed ball is closed on the ideal sphere, and applying an isometry to an accumulating orbit sequence gives an accumulating orbit sequence; changing the orbit basepoint does not change its ideal accumulation points, by bounded model distance and the distance formula in [F1]. [A1, F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Let $p$ be geometrically conical. Normalize $p=\infty$ and a ray to $p$ as $\{(b,Y):Y\ge Y_0\}$. Conicality gives distinct $\gamma_j$ and heights $Y_j\to\infty$ with $d(\gamma_jo,(b,Y_j))\le B$. The isometries $a_j:w\mapsto(w-b)/Y_j$ send these ray points to $(0,1)$, so $a_j\gamma_j o$ lies in a compact ball. The compact frame fiber of [F1] lets us pass to $a_j\gamma_j\to A\in\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)$. Put $g_j=\gamma_j^{-1}=(a_j\gamma_j)^{-1}a_j$. On the boundary, $a_jp=p=\infty$ and $a_jq\to0$ locally uniformly on the finite plane. Thus $g_jp\to A^{-1}\infty=u$ while $g_jq\to A^{-1}0=v$ locally uniformly for $q\ne p$. These are distinct. Since the limit set is closed and invariant by step 1.1, and it contains $p$ and some other point, both $u,v$ belong to it. Undoing the fixed normalization proves the geometric-to-intrinsic direction. [F1, F2, step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

2.2 Conversely suppose the displayed boundary condition holds for distinct $g_j$ and $u\ne v$. Choose two distinct points $q,r\in\Lambda\setminus\{p\}$, possible because the group is nonelementary. The geodesics $g_j[p,q]$ and $g_j[p,r]$ each have endpoints tending to the distinct pair $u,v$, so their distances from a fixed model point $o$ are uniformly bounded. Here is an explicit justification of that geometric bound. After a fixed Möbius coordinate sends $(u,v)$ to $(0,\infty)$, write the varying endpoints as $u_j\to0,v_j\to\infty$. The maps $F_j(z)=(z-u_j)/(1-z/v_j)$, with $1/v_j=0$ when $v_j=\infty$, tend to identity and send the pair to $(0,\infty)$. Their isometric extensions send each varying geodesic to the fixed vertical axis; their values on $o$ stay bounded, proving the asserted bound. It follows that $g_j^{-1}o$ stays at bounded distance from both complete geodesics $[p,q]$ and $[p,r]$. These orbit points escape every compact model set by properness in [F2]. A bounded-radius neighborhood of a complete geodesic has only its two endpoints as ideal accumulation points: normalize that geodesic to the vertical axis through zero, where bounded distance implies $|z|/Y$ bounded by the distance formula, so a finite ideal accumulation point must be zero. Every ideal accumulation point of this orbit sequence must therefore lie in $\{p,q\}\cap\{p,r\}=\{p\}$. Compactness of the closed ball shows $g_j^{-1}o\to p$. The same bounded-distance estimate now holds for a ray of $[p,q]$ tending to $p$: nearest points on the complete line cannot escape toward $q$, because bounded distance would then give ideal limit $q$, and cannot stay in a compact segment because the orbit escapes. Thus this is a conical orbit sequence. This proves the full intrinsic equivalence, using only two other points for this direction. [F1, F2, step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 A limit-set conjugacy therefore preserves conicality. If $g_jp\to u$ and $g_jq\to v$ for every $q\ne p$, continuity and equivariance give $\theta(g_j)h(p)\to h(u)$ and $\theta(g_j)h(q)\to h(v)$, with $h(u)\ne h(v)$. Surjectivity of $h$ and step 2.2 give conicality at $h(p)$. The inverse conjugacy proves the converse. It also preserves parabolic type. A nonidentity group matrix is unipotent or loxodromic by [F2]'s elementary classification; its fixed points on the ideal sphere are respectively one or two. All those fixed points belong to the limit set, since positive and, in the loxodromic case, negative iterates of an interior point converge to them by the translation and diagonal formulas. Thus the number of fixed points on the limit set detects parabolic type, and equivariance of $h$ preserves that number. In particular $\theta(\operatorname{Stab}_{\Gamma_1}p)=\operatorname{Stab}_{\Gamma_2}h(p)$. The restriction of $h$ off $p$ induces a homeomorphism of the stabilizer quotients: the quotient-map definition gives continuity of the induced map and of its induced inverse. Compactness of one such quotient is therefore equivalent to compactness of the other. This proves preservation of bounded-parabolic points. [F1, F2, step 2.1, step 2.2, given, algebra]

4.1 If $\Gamma_1$ has finite-volume positive-radius convex neighborhood, the reverse criterion in [F3] gives the conical-or-bounded-parabolic condition on $\Lambda_1$. Step 3.1 transfers it to $\Lambda_2$. The forward criterion in [F3] gives compact convex truncation, finitely many cusp orbits and finite volume of every fixed-radius convex neighborhood for $\Gamma_2$. Applying the same arguments to $h^{-1}$ proves the equivalence and all the stated geometric conclusions for both groups. This implication invokes actual local proofs of both parabolic criterion directions, not an alternative definition silently substituted for geometric finiteness. [F3, step 3.1, given, algebra]

5.1 Finally a sphere conjugacy indeed restricts to a limit-set conjugacy, without an interior extension assumption. For a discrete nonelementary Möbius group, its domain of discontinuity is exactly the maximal open subset on which the sphere action is properly discontinuous. To verify this characterization, let $K$ be a compact subset of the complement of the limit set. If infinitely many distinct $g_j$ satisfy $g_jK\cap K\ne\varnothing$, apply step 1.1: the exceptional point $b$ and attracting point $a$ belong to the limit set, so $K$ avoids both; local uniform convergence sends $K$ into an arbitrarily small neighborhood of $a$ disjoint from $K$, a contradiction. Conversely if $p$ lies in the limit set and $V$ is any open neighborhood of it, choose distinct $g_j$ with $g_jo\to p$ by the orbit definition. Step 1.1 supplies a subsequence converging on the sphere off one point to the constant $p$. Choose a small closed boundary disc $K\subset V$ containing $p$ in its interior and at least one point $q$ different from that exceptional point. Then $g_jq\in K$ eventually, so infinitely many $g_jK$ meet $K$. Thus no open neighborhood of $p$ supports a properly discontinuous action. Proper discontinuity in this compact-set sense is preserved under homeomorphic conjugacy, so a sphere conjugacy sends the two discontinuity domains onto one another and their complementary limit sets onto one another. Restrict it and apply step 4.1. In particular a quasiconformal sphere conjugacy satisfies this interface. No interior quasiconformal extension, convex-core assertion from conjugacy alone, or missing source equivalence has been used. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 4.1, given, construct, algebra] ∎

## Actual source reading and scope

The actual complete Bowditch Chapter 4 and Sections 3.1–3.4, and the actual complete Kapovich Section 4.9, Sections 4.14–4.18 and Section 8.3, were read. The local thin classifier and both forward and reverse criterion proofs were read in full during their development before this composition. Consequently the geometric-finiteness conclusion includes the rank-one and rank-two parabolic cases omitted in the partial book equivalence proof. No Maskit combination theorem, general Ahlfors–Bers parameterization or statement about global three-manifold hyperbolization is included.
