## Statement

Let $M$ be a compact connected orientable PL three-manifold with a supplied finite compatible triangulation. Let $F_0,F_1\subset\partial M$ be disjoint connected compact PL subsurfaces, each inducing an injective map of fundamental groups into $M$. Suppose there is a continuous map $f:S^1\times[0,1]\to M$ with $f(S^1\times\{j\})\subset\operatorname{int}F_j$ for $j=0,1$, whose core loop has infinite order in $\pi_1(M)$. Then there is a proper embedded PL annulus $A\subset M$ with one boundary circle in $\operatorname{int}F_0$ and the other in $\operatorname{int}F_1$, both nontrivial in $\pi_1(M)$.

If $F_0$ is a small annular neighborhood of a prescribed essential simple circle $k$, the first boundary circle can be required to be exactly $k$. No irreducibility, boundary irreducibility outside the two marked subsurfaces, finite-volume geometry or topological triangulation-existence theorem is assumed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The supplied finite oriented PL manifold, the disjoint injective marked boundary subsurfaces and the annulus map with infinite-order core.

[F1] Finite polyhedral pairs admit compatible subdivisions and simplicial approximations as maps of pairs. Finite image subpolyhedra have compact PL manifold neighborhoods retracting onto them with boundary trace contained in prescribed open marked subsurfaces. Compact PL boundaries have collars. [[thm-finite-simplicial-approximation-for-maps-of-pairs]], [[lem-finite-subpolyhedra-have-compact-pl-manifold-neighborhoods]], [[lem-compact-locally-collared-pl-subpolyhedron-has-pl-collar]]

[F2] A map lifts to a connected covering whenever its fundamental-group image is contained in that covering's subgroup. In particular a twofold edge-cocycle cover admits a lift of an annulus map when that cocycle evaluates to zero on its core. [[thm-covering-space-lifting-criterion]]

[F3] The actual finite proof of the covering-tower endpoint constructs twofold covers from simplicial one-cocycles, identifies the transpose boundary matrices with the reversed relative dual-cell chain complex, and computes closed orientable boundary components by finite polygon reduction. These constructions are finite and apply before setting their first-cohomology dimension equal to zero. [[lem-no-twofold-cover-forces-spherical-boundary-in-finite-pl-three-manifold]]

[F4] A compact oriented finite PL surface with boundary has a finite embedded graph spine and a PL graph-thickening presentation; a simply connected such surface is a disk. A nullhomotopic embedded interior circle bounds a PL disk. Polygonal disks admit prescribed PL boundary extensions, and finite polygonal arcs have strip neighborhoods. [[lem-nullhomotopic-circles-on-finite-oriented-pl-surfaces-bound-pl-disks]], [[lem-finite-polygonal-disk-and-collar-surgery]]

[F5] Free groups are torsion-free. Amalgam normal form embeds the factors and makes positive-length reduced words nontrivial; van Kampen identifies the surface seam decomposition with that amalgam. Cellular and relative cellular chains compute singular homology and its pair exact sequence. [[thm-free-groups-are-torsion-free]], [[thm-normal-form-for-free-products-with-amalgamation]], [[thm-seifert-van-kampen]], [[thm-cellular-homology-computes-singular-homology]], [[thm-relative-cellular-homology-computes-relative-singular-homology]], [[thm-long-exact-sequence-of-a-pair-in-singular-homology]]

[F6] For two disjoint proper preimage arcs in a disk, the two local double-arc resolutions give disks respectively by joining the two outer pieces and by joining all three pieces. Their finite polygonal constructions use each retained old piece at most once. [[lem-two-sheeted-pl-disk-immersion-has-essential-embedded-disk]]

## Proof

1.1 By [F1], make $f$ PL as a map of pairs, keeping its two boundary images inside the open marked surfaces, and push its interior off $\partial M$ using a boundary collar with a parameter positive precisely on the interior of the parameter annulus. Its core remains infinite order. Fix a finite triangulation of the parameter annulus making this proper map simplicial and let $D_0$ be its image. Take a compact connected PL manifold neighborhood $V_0$ retracting onto $D_0$, with its two boundary traces near the two image circles contained in $\operatorname{int}F_0$ and $\operatorname{int}F_1$. Write $a_0\in\pi_1(V_0)$ for its core class. All later maps to $M$ send this class to the original infinite-order class. [F1, given, construct]

1.2 The groups of the marked surfaces are torsion-free. With boundary, the graph spine in [F4] gives a finite-rank free group, so [F5] applies. For a closed surface the finite oriented polygon reduction in [F3] gives a sphere, a square torus or a product of at least two commutator blocks. The sphere group is trivial and the torus square presentation is $\mathbb Z^2$. In higher genus cut the commutator polygon along the separating circle between one handle and the remaining handles. Its two boundary surfaces have free groups, and their shared boundary has infinite order by [F4]. Van Kampen and [F5] give their amalgam over this injected cyclic group. Every element is conjugate into a factor or to a cyclically reduced alternating word: successively conjugate away two end syllables in the same factor until the first and last factors differ. A positive power of the latter word remains reduced and nonempty, hence nontrivial by [F5]. Finite-order elements must therefore lie in a torsion-free factor. This proves the assertion in every case, by finite surface and word operations. Consequently any nontrivial loop lying in either $F_j$ has infinite-order image in $M$, by the assumed injection. [F3, F4, F5, given, algebra]

2.1 At a stage $V_i$, with lifted annulus image $D_i$ and core $a_i$, take any nonzero simplicial one-cohomology class over $\mathbb F_2$ which evaluates to zero on $a_i$, if one exists. The explicit edge-label construction in [F3] gives a connected twofold cover: vertices are $(v,e)$, a lifted edge $vw$ changes the label by the cocycle value on $vw$, and the triangle cocycle identity supplies compatible simplex lifts. A nonzero class gives a connected cover, since a disconnected cover would give a vertex label whose coboundary was that cocycle. By [F2] lift the annulus map, take its image $D_{i+1}$, and then its compact retracting manifold neighborhood $V_{i+1}$. The traces remain in the lifted original open marked subsurfaces. Repeat. This process is finite: give image simplices the structure lifted from the fixed original image complex. Their number is bounded by the finite number of parameter simplices. The inverse image of $D_i$ is $D_{i+1}\cup tD_{i+1}$ for the deck involution $t$, and is connected because the connected cover retracts onto it. Thus these two finite image complexes meet. A common simplex and its distinct deck mate show that projection identifies distinct simplices of $D_{i+1}$, so its simplex count is strictly greater than that of $D_i$. The bounded strict increases terminate. No finite generation of an infinite cover is used. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 At the last stage $V$, evaluation on the core is injective on the finite vector space $H^1(V;\mathbb F_2)$, by construction; hence its dimension $d$ is at most one. The finite chain calculations detailed next, using the cellular and pair suppliers in [F5], give $$\dim H_1(V;\mathbb F_2)=d,\qquad\dim H_2(V,\partial V;\mathbb F_2)=d.$$ Explicitly, the first equality is equality of ranks of a finite boundary matrix and its transpose. For the second, the barycentric block dual to a $k$-simplex is the cone on its sphere or ball link; modulo its boundary side, its facets are exactly the blocks of immediate cofaces, each once. Thus cochains in degree $k$ identify with relative dual-cell chains in degree $3-k$, with the same incidence matrices. The pair exact sequence therefore bounds $\dim H_1(\partial V;\mathbb F_2)\le2d\le2$. The finite orientable polygon reduction of [F3] gives dimension $2g$ for each genus-$g$ boundary component. Consequently every boundary component is a sphere, except possibly one torus. These formulas are the actual general finite matrix argument, not the invalid inference that the zero-dimensional endpoint Statement alone covers $d=1$. [F3, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The two boundary loops of the final lifted annulus remain nontrivial in $V$, since their images in $M$ have infinite order. A boundary sphere cannot contain such a loop, so both loops lie on the unique torus boundary component $T$. Each is contained in its respective open marked trace, and those traces are disjoint. In each trace choose a compact connected surface neighborhood of that loop, inside the trace, and use the graph spine from [F4]. A spanning tree in its spine gives generators represented, up to basepaths, by simple embedded graph cycles; pushing the cycles slightly into the thickened graph gives embedded polygonal circles. At least one generator has nontrivial image in $V$, since otherwise the original trace loop would have trivial image. Thus we obtain disjoint simple circles $c_0,c_1\subset T$, in the two respective traces, each nontrivial in $V$. They are essential in $T$. Two disjoint essential circles on a torus cobound an embedded annulus: cutting at one gives the rectangle model with its paired sides, and the second essential circle is a core in that annulus by the polygonal bridge-and-bigon argument of [F4]. One of the intervening annuli has boundary $c_0\cup c_1$. Push its interior slightly into $V$ using the boundary collar. This gives a proper embedded annulus with infinite-order core: each marked boundary loop maps into $F_j$, whose group injects into $M$ and is torsion-free by its finite graph/polygon surface presentation. Its nontrivial image therefore has infinite order. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 We record the annular descent operation completely. Project a proper embedded annulus in a connected twofold cover to the stage below and perturb it into regular PL position relative to its deck translate. Paired disjoint coordinate balls, finite vertex-star moves and their translated mates preserve embeddedness upstairs. Avoid vertices on the other sheet and edge-edge intersections; allow transverse edge-face intersections, whose two incident face segments join with valence two. The projection is a proper oriented annulus immersion with only regular double circles and double arcs and at most two preimages of any value. Boundary curves remain in the two disjoint original marked traces. Orientability of domain and target gives trivial product-cross monodromy about each double circle: exchanging the two oriented sheet coorientations would reverse the induced orientation of their intersection line, impossible around that circle. The interval strips of each individual oriented sheet likewise return without reversing. Thus each double circle has two disjoint preimage circles and the crossing resolution can be performed consistently along the whole circle. [F1, F4, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 Suppose first there is a double circle. Its preimage circles are either both contractible in the parameter annulus or both essential. They cannot have different types: an essential annular circle maps to a nonzero power of the infinite-order core, whereas a contractible one maps to the identity. In the contractible case the two preimages bound disks, nested or disjoint. If nested, replace the larger disk map by the smaller one; if disjoint, interchange their disk maps, matching points with the same image at the crossing circle. Resolve the crossing by connecting adjacent rather than opposite arms in a sufficiently small polygonal product-cross neighborhood. The domain is still an annulus, its external boundary maps unchanged, and each retained old sheet piece is used at most once. In the essential case the two preimage circles cut the annulus into three consecutive annuli. Discard the middle annulus, and join the two outer annuli along their corresponding common image circle, using the adjacent-arm resolution. The result is again an annulus with the same two external boundary maps. The polygonal parametrizations and their boundary extensions in [F4] realize these operations. They remove the chosen double circle, introduce no new intersections and retain the infinite-order core through the unchanged boundary maps. Repeat finitely to remove all double circles. [F4, step 5.1, construct]

7.1 Consider a double arc with both endpoints on the same marked surface, say $F_0$. Its two disjoint preimage arcs have endpoints on the $0$-boundary of the parameter annulus. Cap the other boundary by a FORMAL parameter disk, solely to describe the finite cut-and-paste domains; no map on this cap is used. The two arcs cut that parameter disk into three consecutive disks. By the actual ordinary double-arc disk operation [F6], one adjacent-arm resolution joins the two outer pieces, and the other joins all three pieces in the opposite order; both domains are disks. If the formal cap is in an outer piece, the first resolution retains it; if it is in the middle piece, use the second resolution. Remove the formal cap afterward. The retained domain is an annulus, its $F_1$ boundary unchanged, and its other boundary still lies in $F_0$. The resolution along the proper image arc and its endpoint half-plane models is performed in a small product strip by two disjoint bent polygonal arcs, fixed near the strip frontier. It uses no old piece twice, so introduces no new double curve or triple value. The unchanged $F_1$ boundary retains the infinite-order core. The same construction applies with the labels reversed. [F4, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct]

8.1 If the double arc has one endpoint in each marked surface, both of its preimage arcs span the parameter annulus. Cutting along them gives two rectangles. For each rectangle, identify its two cut shores according to their common image-arc parameter and resolve to adjacent arms in the product-cross strip. This gives two candidate annulus immersions, using respectively the two old rectangle pieces and no others. Their boundary circles remain in the respective marked surfaces, and the chosen double arc disappears without new intersections. At the $F_0$ endpoint, the original boundary loop is the product of the two candidate boundary loops, up to conjugating joining paths in $F_0$: cutting the original boundary at the two preimage endpoints leaves its two successive paths, and the short resolution joins close each path separately. If both candidate boundary loops were trivial in $\pi_1(M)$, their product would be trivial there, contrary to the original infinite-order boundary. Retain a candidate whose boundary is nontrivial in $M$. Because it lies in the injective surface $F_0$, its nontrivial class has infinite order. Thus the retained annulus again has infinite-order core. These two arc cases exhaust all possibilities because the two marked surfaces are disjoint. [F4, step 7.1, construct]

9.1 The number of regular double curves strictly decreases in each of steps 6.1–8.1. All product-strip widths are chosen smaller than the finitely many distances to other double-value curves and nonincident sheet pieces. Outside those strips each retained old piece is used at most once. Thus the at-most-two-sheet condition and regularity persist, and there are no newly introduced double values. Finite repetition gives a proper PL annulus immersion with no double values, hence an embedding by compactness and the Hausdorff target. Its two boundaries lie in the respective marked subsurfaces and retain infinite order. This gives the required descent through ONE twofold covering, without assuming that intermediate resolved annuli still lift to embeddings. At the end of each descent the resulting embedded annulus is the fresh input for the next cover. Repeat down the finite tower from step 2.1 to obtain a proper embedded annulus in the original $M$. [step 2.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, step 8.1, construct]

10.1 Its boundary images remain in $\operatorname{int}F_j$ throughout the marked neighborhoods and local boundary moves. If $F_0$ is an annular neighborhood of $k$, its boundary there is an essential embedded annular circle and is therefore PL isotopic to $k$. The finite rectangle and innermost-bigon construction in step 4.1 gives this isotopy inside $\operatorname{int}F_0$. Extend it over a boundary collar of $M$, fixed away from that collar, and apply it to the final annulus. Its first boundary becomes exactly $k$ and its other boundary remains in $F_1$. This proves the stated full annular conclusion. No product-recognition or general marking-homeomorphism theorem has entered the construction. [F1, F4, step 9.1, construct] ∎
