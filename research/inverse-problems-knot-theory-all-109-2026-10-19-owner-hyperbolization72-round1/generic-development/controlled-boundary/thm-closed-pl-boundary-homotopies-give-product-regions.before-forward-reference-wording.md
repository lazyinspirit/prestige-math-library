## Statement

Let $M$ be a compact connected orientable irreducible PL three-manifold, equipped with a finite compatible triangulation. Let $F$ be a closed connected boundary component of positive genus inducing an injective fundamental-group map into $M$. Let $F'\subset\partial M\setminus F$ be a connected compact PL subsurface, also inducing an injective fundamental-group map into $M$.

Suppose that for every loop $k$ in $F$, some nonzero integral power of $k$ is freely homotopic in $M$ to a loop in $F'$. Then $F'$ is a whole closed boundary component, and there is a PL homeomorphism $F\times[0,1]\to M$ carrying $F\times\{0\}$ to $F$ and $F\times\{1\}$ to $F'$. The bottom restriction can be required to be the specified identification with the original $F$.

Irreducibility here means that every embedded PL two-sphere in the interior of $M$ bounds a PL three-ball. No general topological triangulation or smoothing theorem, no general Haken marking theorem and no preexisting product-region assumption is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite oriented PL manifold, the two injective boundary surfaces and all-loop power-homotopy hypothesis in the Statement.

[F1] An annulus map with infinite-order core between disjoint injective compact boundary subsurfaces gives a proper embedded PL annulus. If its first subsurface is an annular neighborhood of a prescribed simple essential circle, the first boundary can equal that circle exactly. Its proof also supplies the finite two-sheeted annulus circle and arc resolutions preserving an unchanged infinite-order boundary. [[thm-incompressible-pl-boundary-surfaces-with-common-loops-have-embedded-annuli]]

[F2] A nullhomotopic embedded polygonal circle in a finite oriented PL surface bounds a PL disk. Compact boundary surfaces have embedded finite graph spines and actual graph-thickening presentations. Polygonal disk boundary maps extend by finite subdivision and coning, and finite arcs have polygonal strip neighborhoods. [[lem-nullhomotopic-circles-on-finite-oriented-pl-surfaces-bound-pl-disks]], [[lem-finite-polygonal-disk-and-collar-surgery]]

[F3] Compact PL boundary pieces admit PL collars. Finite oriented polygon reduction constructs the standard closed genus model from a given finite triangulation. [[lem-compact-locally-collared-pl-subpolyhedron-has-pl-collar]], [[lem-no-twofold-cover-forces-spherical-boundary-in-finite-pl-three-manifold]]

## Proof

1.1 Write the genus of $F$ as $g\ge1$. Choose the standard chain of $2g$ embedded polygonal circles $k_1,\ldots,k_{2g}$ on its finite oriented genus model: successive circles meet transversely once, nonsuccessive circles are disjoint, and their union $K$ has a closed regular neighborhood $S\subset F$ which is a genus-$g$ surface with one boundary circle and disk complement. This can be constructed without a mapping-class theorem. Plumb $2g$ oriented annular strips in a linear chain, crossing the core of each new strip once with the preceding core. After the first pair their neighborhood is a punctured torus; adjoining the next two strips adds one handle and retains one boundary circle. Finite induction gives the punctured genus-$g$ model; cap its boundary with a polygonal disk. The oriented polygon reduction in [F3] identifies this capped finite model with $F$. Parametrize the model so that its bottom copy is the given $F$. Each $k_j$ is essential and has infinite order in $\pi_1(M)$, since the surface group is torsion-free, as proved directly in [F1], and $F$ is injective. Apply the given homotopy to a nonzero power of each $k_j$, and apply [F1] with its annular neighborhood and $F'$. This gives proper embedded annuli $A_j$ with first boundary exactly $k_j$ and other boundary in $\operatorname{int}F'$. [F1, F3, given, construct]

2.1 Put the finite union of annuli in regular PL position, fixing all $k_j$, allowing their other boundaries to move inside $\operatorname{int}F'$. Finite vertex-star moves in coordinate balls and boundary half-balls achieve only transverse double curves, isolated transverse triple points and no fourfold values. Avoid sheet vertices on the other sheets and edge-edge intersections, and allow transverse edge-face intersections, where the two neighboring segments join with valence two. At the specified first-boundary intersections prescribe the ordinary two-half-plane model first. No three $k_j$ meet. For a fixed pair $A_i,A_j$, intersection arcs have endpoints on $F$ or $F'$. For $|i-j|=1$ exactly one endpoint lies in $F$; it belongs to a unique arc running from that endpoint to $F'$, called the distinguished arc. All other intersection arcs have both endpoints on $F'$. For nonsuccessive indices there is no distinguished arc. A double circle is essential in one annulus if and only if it is essential in the other: an essential annular loop represents a nonzero power of its infinite-order core, whereas a contractible one is trivial in $M$. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Maintain the following priority invariant: EVERY annulus is embedded immediately before a distinct-label operation. It holds initially by step 1.1 and the ambient perturbations of step 2.1. Perform just one distinct-label operation, and then clean all self intersections of its outputs by step 4.1 before any next distinct-label operation. Remove an undesired double circle between distinct annuli by explicit cut-and-paste. If its two preimages bound disks, interchange their disk pieces and resolve the crossing to adjacent arms in a small polygonal product-cross strip. Each resulting annulus retains its prescribed $k_i$ or $k_j$ boundary. If the circles are both essential, cut both annuli there and exchange their $F'$-side annular halves, joining each $F$-side half to the other $F'$-side half by the adjacent-arm resolution. This again gives two annuli, each with its prescribed first boundary. Remove an undesired arc with both endpoints on $F'$ in the same fashion: such a proper arc in an annulus cuts off a disk cap adjoining its $F'$ boundary and avoiding its $F$ boundary. Exchange the two disk caps, attaching each to the other annular remainder and resolving to adjacent arms along the image arc. Both resulting domains are annuli. The polygonal extensions in [F2] make these actual finite maps and the half-plane endpoint moves remain in $F'$. Their first boundaries are unchanged, so their cores still have infinite order. Pieces used in either output are disjoint parameter pieces, each used once in the two outputs together. Since both input annuli were embedded, each output has at most TWO preimages at any self-double value: its pieces came from only those two embedded inputs, each once. Three distinct old labels at a triple can therefore acquire at most two copies of one output label, never three copies of that label. [F1, F2, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 Immediately after that one distinct-label operation, clean ALL self intersections of its two outputs before mixing labels again. A former intersection of the two embedded input annuli may now be a self intersection of one output, but each individual output is at most two-to-one by step 3.1. Other annuli remain individually embedded. Thus the disjoint-preimage two-sheet domain hypotheses of the self-annulus operations in [F1] actually hold; no unrestricted many-sheet annulus is an input. Remove such a self double circle by the contractible/essential two-preimage annulus operations in [F1]. Remove a self double arc by its same-boundary operation: all its endpoints lie on $F'$, since its first boundary is the embedded $k_j$. Fill the first annular hole by a formal parameter cap, perform one of the two disk double-arc resolutions retaining that cap, and remove the cap again. The result is an annulus with the identical first boundary. No map on the formal cap is used. Thus every intermediate domain remains an annulus labeled by its fixed first boundary. All local resolutions are supported in small product strips and retain or discard old sheet pieces without duplicating them. This includes nested disk caps and circles, using the disk-boundary extensions of [F2]. Cleanup changes only ONE label at a time and uses its old pieces at most once; its at-most-two self-preimage bound persists, while all other labels are unchanged. A third, different labeled sheet may cross a chosen self-double curve. In its three-coordinate-plane model resolve the two sheets of the cleaned label to disjoint adjacent arms; the third sheet meets them in separated ordinary double segments, so that triple disappears and no new triple is created. Apply the global decrease in step 5.1 to each cleanup operation. Finite cleanup restores embeddedness of every annulus, and only then permits the next distinct-label swap. [F1, F2, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 The finite process terminates. Count $t$, the number of triple values, and $d$, the number of double-curve components, including self double curves and the distinguished arcs. Resolve an entire undesired double-curve component at each operation. If that component contains a triple point, the two resolved sheets become disjoint in its strip, so each such triple point disappears. A third transverse sheet intersects the two new sheets in separate ordinary double segments; no new triple is created there. Outside the strip all old sheet germs are unchanged, merely reassigned or discarded, and every retained sheet piece occurs once, so no new triple appears elsewhere. Thus $t$ strictly decreases. If the chosen component contains no triple, choose its strip disjoint from all other double curves and sheet pieces except its two crossing sheets; then $t$ is unchanged and exactly that double component disappears, so $d$ decreases. Any harmless further PL subdivision changes neither count. Consequently the lexicographically ordered pair $(t,d)\in\mathbb N^2$ strictly decreases. At every stage the first boundaries stay fixed, so each successive pair still has exactly one distinguished arc and no other first-boundary intersections. Choose operations with the priority just proved: at an embedded stage, perform one undesired distinct-label swap if any remains, then the forced self-cleanup. Both the swap and every cleanup strictly decrease this SAME global pair $(t,d)$, even when a different third label crosses the chosen curve. Thus this priority algorithm terminates. At termination no undesired component remains: all annuli are embedded, successive annuli meet in precisely their distinguished spanning arc, and all nonsuccessive annuli are disjoint. No triple remains, since three distinct labels cannot have pairwise successive indices, and a repeated label would be an excluded self intersection. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 The resulting union $\bigcup A_j$ is the product $K\times[0,1]$, with its lower graph equal to $K\subset F$ and upper graph $K'\subset\operatorname{int}F'$. To check the assertion rather than assuming it from its homotopy type, cut every annulus at its intersection arcs and at one auxiliary spanning arc where needed. The complementary pieces are rectangles. A neighborhood of a distinguished arc is the product of a planar cross with an interval; a neighborhood of an annular rectangle edge is the corresponding strip product. Their attaching parameters agree after subdividing the finitely many intervals, and the cyclic order of the four cross arms is unchanged along the interval. The ribbon neighborhood of $K$ in $F$ therefore extends through these product boxes to a PL embedded $S\times[0,1]$, with $S\times\{1\}$ a regular neighborhood $S'$ of $K'$ in $F'$. In particular $S'$ is a punctured genus-$g$ surface with one boundary circle. The matching of finite vertex disks and edge bands gives this actual product with its marked ends; a deformation-retraction argument alone would not suffice. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 5.1, construct]

7.1 Adjoin a small collar of all of $F$ to this product neighborhood, and round its finite PL corners. The result is a compact embedded submanifold $V\subset M$ homeomorphic to $F\times[0,1]$, meeting $F'$ in $S'$, whose remaining frontier is a single proper PL disk $D$ with boundary $\partial S'$. Here is the finite neighborhood calculation. Put $B=\overline{F\setminus S}$, the disk from step 1.1. A short whole-$F$ collar together with the longer $S$-product has its upper boundary made of $S'$ and the disk consisting of the short upper copy of $B$ plus its vertical boundary cylinder. In the boundary collar of $S$, replace the right-angle step between the two collar heights by a linear polygonal profile. The corresponding region in every transverse rectangle is again a rectangle, with its four marked edges, by polygonal disk extension [F2]. Taking products over the finitely many edge intervals and matching their endpoint boxes gives a PL homeomorphism to the ordinary whole-$F$ collar; its bottom restriction is identity. This proves the asserted $V$ and its disk frontier. The construction uses exactly the product boxes from step 6.1 and the given collars, with no general regular-neighborhood uniqueness theorem. [F2, F3, step 6.1, construct]

8.1 The circle $\partial D=\partial S'$ is nullhomotopic in $M$, so injectivity of $F'$ makes it nullhomotopic in $F'$. By [F2] it bounds a PL disk $B'\subset F'$. This is the side complementary to $S'$: the latter has positive genus and hence cannot be that disk. A simple circle has just two complementary sides in its connected surface, and its disk region is the entire component on that side by [F2]. The other side is exactly $S'$, since $S'$ has that single frontier circle and is a compact subsurface lying in the interior of $F'$. Therefore $F'=S'\cup B'$ is a whole closed boundary component. The disk $D$ and the boundary disk $B'$ together give a sphere after pushing the latter slightly into the boundary collar and matching their edge collars. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 7.1, construct]

9.1 Irreducibility supplies a PL ball on the complementary side of this sphere. More explicitly the pushed sphere separates a small collar of $B'$ from the disk $D$; pull its ball back through that collar to obtain a ball bounded by $D\cup B'$, with $B'$ on the manifold boundary. This ball is disjoint from $\operatorname{int}V$. Indeed $\operatorname{int}V$ is connected and abuts the boundary component $F$, whereas the interior of an irreducibility ball is contained in $\operatorname{int}M$ and cannot contain $F$; hence it lies on the other side of the sphere. The local disk collar specifies the same side at every point. Adjoin this ball to $V$ along its disk frontier $D$. Its new boundary is exactly $F\cup F'$, with no frontier in $\operatorname{int}M$. It is thus both open and closed relative to connected $M$, so it equals $M$. Attaching a ball along this disk simply fills the missing top disk of the product collar in step 7.1. A prescribed PL map of that disk extends across its ball by coning a common finite subdivision of the boundary sphere; consequently the product homeomorphism extends over it and is identity on the bottom $F$. This proves the claimed PL product and both marked boundary identifications. [F2, F3, step 7.1, step 8.1, given, construct] ∎
