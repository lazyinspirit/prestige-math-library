## Statement

Assume full AC. Let $A,B$ be compact connected oriented surfaces with supplied finite PL triangulations, let $\pi_1(A)\ne\{1\}$, and let $f:(A,\partial A)\to(B,\partial B)$ be a PL map inducing an injective fundamental-group map. Then $f$ is homotopic as a map of pairs either to a finite-sheeted PL covering $A\to B$, or to a map with image in $\partial B$; in the latter alternative $A$ is an annulus.

If the restriction $f|_{\partial A}$ is a local homeomorphism, the homotopy can be chosen constant on $\partial A$. The alternatives retain all orientation signs and all possible finite covering degrees; no assumption that $A$ or $B$ is closed, hyperbolic, or of negative Euler characteristic is imposed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the finite oriented surfaces and the injective PL map of pairs.

[F1] Essential freely homotopic simple circles on finite oriented PL surfaces are ambient isotopic; disjoint ones cobound a PL annulus. Its actual proof supplies compact injected cores in noncompact covering surfaces, including the plane and cyclic-cover annulus descriptions, by finite surface neighborhoods, filling compact complementary components, joining multiple interfaces by bands, and amalgam normal form. [[lem-finite-pl-peripheral-multicurves-admit-minimal-annular-position]]

[F2] Nullhomotopic polygonal simple circles bound PL disks. A compact surface with boundary has an actual finite graph-thickening spine; a simply connected such surface is a disk, and a rank-one oriented graph thickening is an annulus. Finite polygonal disk boundary maps extend across disks, and finite arcs have strip neighborhoods. Boundary PL collars exist. [[lem-nullhomotopic-circles-on-finite-oriented-pl-surfaces-bound-pl-disks]], [[lem-finite-polygonal-disk-and-collar-surgery]], [[lem-compact-locally-collared-pl-subpolyhedron-has-pl-collar]]

[F3] Surface universal covers and their subgroup quotients exist; maps lift under the image-subgroup criterion and coverings inject groups. Van Kampen and injective boundary amalgam normal form compute surface gluings. [[thm-universal-cover-existence]], [[lem-subgroup-quotient-of-universal-cover]], [[thm-covering-space-lifting-criterion]], [[thm-covering-maps-inject-fundamental-groups]], [[thm-seifert-van-kampen]], [[thm-normal-form-for-free-products-with-amalgamation]]

[F4] Cellular and relative cellular chains compute singular homology and its pair sequence. The two-sphere is simply connected. Cyclic reduction gives the usual finite reduced-word model and conjugacy reductions in finite-rank free groups. [[thm-cellular-homology-computes-singular-homology]], [[thm-relative-cellular-homology-computes-relative-singular-homology]], [[thm-long-exact-sequence-of-a-pair-in-singular-homology]], [[thm-higher-dimensional-spheres-are-simply-connected]], [[lem-cyclic-reduction-normal-form]]

## Proof

1.1 We make explicit the compact-core operation needed for image-subgroup covers, without assuming the surface-map theorem. In a connected noncompact triangulated oriented surface $Y$, take a finite connected subsurface neighborhood $N$ of given compact data and given finitely many group generators. Keep any specified actual boundary circles of $Y$ as boundary circles of $N$. Such a neighborhood is made from finitely many chart disks, edge bands and triangle patches; its corners have finite PL sector charts. Fill all compact complementary components. If a remaining noncompact component meets two boundary interfaces of $N$, add a thin band along an embedded arc joining them and again fill any resulting compact complementary components. Each band reduces the total finite interface count, so the process stops. Every remaining complementary component is noncompact with a single interface circle. That circle is infinite order on the complementary side: a nullhomotopy of a positive power would lie in a finite compact subsurface with that boundary, which [F2] makes impossible except for a disk; such a disk would be the whole boundary-side component, contrary to noncompactness. On the $N$ side its circle is likewise injected unless $N$ is a disk. Van Kampen and [F3] over the finite star of complementary components inject $\pi_1(N)$ into $\pi_1(Y)$; the disk case is trivial. Since $N$ contains the supplied generators, this map is an isomorphism. This construction contains any prescribed compact image, not merely a generating graph. For compact $Y$ take $N=Y$. [F1, F2, F3, given, construct]

1.2 Two further finite surface facts will be used. An essential simple circle is never a proper power in its oriented surface group. If its class were $g^m$, $|m|>1$, lift it to the subgroup cover for $\langle g\rangle$. It is still an embedded circle. This cover has infinite cyclic group and is not a closed compact oriented surface, whose polygon presentation has abelianization of even rank. Apply step 1.1 with that circle and a generator. Its injected compact core has rank one and is an annulus by [F2]. But an essential embedded annular circle has winding number $\pm1$, as follows by cutting its nested planar polygonal collars along a bridge and parametrizing the resulting disk. This contradicts winding $m$. Also two DISTINCT boundary circles of a compact boundary surface cannot have conjugate nonzero powers unless that surface is an annulus. Its group is free by [F2]. Cyclically reduce their words. Equality of conjugate powers gives the same periodic cyclic word; its primitive period is unique, by repeatedly deleting the shorter prefix from the longer period. The preceding nonpower fact therefore makes the original circles freely homotopic up to reversal. By [F1] they cobound an annulus. Since both boundaries are actual boundary components, that annulus has no frontier inside the connected surface and is the entire surface. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

1.3 A surface with boundary or a noncompact surface has $\pi_2=0$: each sphere map has compact image inside a compact finite subsurface with boundary, and that subsurface retracts onto a graph by [F2]. A sphere map into a graph lifts to its tree cover, which contracts by deleting successive finite terminal edges on any compact image, so it is nullhomotopic. For a closed positive-genus surface, its polygon group is infinite. Its universal cover is noncompact, since a compact covering space would have a finite discrete fiber and thus finite degree. A sphere map lifts by [F3,F4] and the preceding argument makes it nullhomotopic there. Thus these closed surfaces also have $\pi_2=0$. We also need that no closed surface with nontrivial group admits a $\pi_1$-injective map into a graph. Put such a map in finite PL position relative to the midpoints of all edges. Its preimages are disjoint circles, each nullhomotopic in the surface by injectivity. An innermost such circle bounds a disk whose interior misses all those preimages; its image lies in a tree vertex-star. Contracting that tree patch and pushing the disk image past the midpoint removes the circle by a homotopy. Repetition leaves the entire image inside one vertex-star, making the induced group trivial, a contradiction. [F2, F3, F4, construct]

2.1 Take the connected cover $p:Y\to B$ corresponding to $f_*\pi_1(A)$. The lifted map $\widehat f:A\to Y$ induces an isomorphism of fundamental groups. The cover inherits its locally finite PL triangulation, orientation and boundary. If $A$ is closed and $Y$ has boundary or is noncompact, apply step 1.1 to a compact image and generators, obtaining a compact surface with boundary and graph spine through which $\widehat f$ factors. The resulting map to that graph remains injective on groups, contradicting step 1.3. Therefore the closed-source case has $Y$ compact and closed. If $A$ has boundary, each boundary loop has infinite order by [F2], since $A$ is not a disk. Its image is a nonzero-degree loop in one boundary circle of $B$, and its lift lies in a CIRCLE component of $\partial Y$: a nonzero-degree circle map cannot lift to the line cover, by its winding number. If the original boundary map is not locally a homeomorphism, homotope each such circle map to its nonzero-degree PL covering, using its real lift and linear interpolation of its periodic part; extend over a source boundary collar. If it already is a local homeomorphism, change nothing there. Thus all subsequent homotopies can fix these boundary maps. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 1.3, given, construct]

3.1 Suppose $\partial A\ne\varnothing$ and either $A$ is not an annulus or its two boundary images lie on different circles of $\partial Y$. In the nonannulus case all its boundary images lie on DIFFERENT circles of $\partial Y$: otherwise their nonzero powers would be conjugate in $\pi_1(Y)$, hence in $\pi_1(A)$ by the isomorphism, contrary to step 1.2. Take a compact core $N\subset Y$ from step 1.1 containing $\widehat f(A)$ and its generators, keeping all those actual boundary circles. Regard $\widehat f$ as a map of compact pairs $(A,\partial A)\to(N,\partial N)$. For a connected compact oriented triangulated surface, a relative two-cycle has equal coefficients on adjacent triangles, since each interior edge occurs twice with opposite orientation. Connectedness of the triangle adjacency graph makes that coefficient a single integer $d$. Thus $H_2(N,\partial N;\mathbb Z)$ is generated by its orientation chain, and its connecting boundary is the sum of ALL oriented boundary circles. Naturality of the pair sequence [F4] gives $$d\sum_{c\subset\partial N}[c]=\widehat f_*[\partial A].$$ Each image circle on the right has a nonzero coefficient and no cancellation with another source boundary. Hence $d\ne0$. Any artificial boundary circle of $N$ in the interior of $Y$ has coefficient zero on the right, impossible. Therefore there is no such circle, and $N$ is open and closed relative to connected $Y$, giving $N=Y$. In particular $Y$ is compact. This is an actual finite orientation-chain argument, not an assumed finiteness theorem for the subgroup cover. [F2, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 In this case every lifted boundary degree is $\pm1$. Indeed a source boundary class is not a proper power by step 1.2; its image under the group isomorphism is a power of the target boundary class, which also is primitive by that step. The exponent therefore has absolute value one. The nonzero coefficient $d$ of step 3.1 shows that every target boundary circle is hit and that its oriented degree is the same $d=\pm1$. Thus the fixed lifted boundary maps are homeomorphisms, bijecting all boundary components. Choose a disjoint essential proper arc system in $Y$ cutting it to a disk, by taking co-cores of the edges outside a spanning tree in the finite graph thickening [F2]. Make $\widehat f$ transverse to these arcs, relative to its already fixed boundary maps. Every target arc has exactly two boundary preimages, one at each endpoint; hence its preimage has exactly one proper arc, plus possibly circles. A circle component maps into an interval and is nullhomotopic in $A$ by injectivity. An innermost one bounds a disk in $A$; using $\pi_2(Y)=0$ from step 1.3, replace that disk map, relative to a slightly larger outer boundary, by a filling just off the relevant cut-arc face. Its boundary loop has degree zero in that interval, so this filling exists in its small contractible strip. The old and new disk maps have the same outer boundary, and their glued sphere is nullhomotopic; hence they are homotopic relative to it. This removes the circle and creates no other preimage component. Repeat finitely. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 Straighten each remaining preimage arc map to the homeomorphism of its target interval fixing its two endpoints. On a thin strip use the transverse signed normal coordinate and interpolate it to a nonzero linear normal parameter; this keeps its inverse image exactly that arc. The fixed source boundary degrees all have the same orientation sign $d$, so the corner half-strip models agree with those homeomorphisms on the original boundary. Cutting along the source and target arc systems now gives maps whose boundary maps are local homeomorphisms of polygonal circles. Each target polygon edge, including the two shores of each cut arc, has exactly one source occurrence. Consequently there is exactly one source boundary circle, mapping with degree $d$ to the target circle. Every cut-source component has boundary. Each is simply connected: its inclusion into $A$ is injective, by constructing graph spines in the cut pieces and reattaching the arc bands, while its image lies in the target disk; injectivity of $\widehat f_*$ forces its group to be trivial. Hence [F2] makes each component a disk, and the single boundary circle makes there be just one. Extend its boundary homeomorphism by polygonal disk extension. The original disk map is homotopic relative to the boundary to that extension by linear interpolation after parametrizing the target disk by a convex polygon. Reattach all paired shores. This proves that $\widehat f$ is homotopic, relative to its fixed boundary maps, to a PL homeomorphism $A\to Y$. [F2, step 1.3, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 The remaining boundary case is that $A$ is an annulus and both boundaries map to the same actual circle $c\subset\partial Y$. Here $\pi_1(Y)\cong\mathbb Z$. Step 1.1 gives a compact injected core containing $\widehat f(A)$, the whole circle $c$ and a generator. Its oriented graph thickening has rank one and is an annulus; $c$ is one of its actual boundary circles. Radially retract that annulus onto $c$ while fixing $c$. Composing gives a homotopy of $\widehat f$ into $c$, constant on both source boundaries. This is the annular-collapse alternative. Cases with different boundary images were already handled in steps 3.1–5.1. [F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct]

7.1 It remains to treat $A,Y$ closed. Their groups are nontrivial, and $\widehat f_*$ is an isomorphism by step 2.1. Choose an essential NONSEPARATING polygonal circle $c\subset Y$ on its positive-genus polygon model. Put $\widehat f$ in PL transverse position relative to $c$. Each contractible preimage circle can be removed: its image degree in $c$ is zero by group injectivity, so fill it into $c$ and use $\pi_2(Y)=0$ from step 1.3 to replace its disk map by one on the appropriate side, relative to an outer disk collar. Innermost choices and finite strip widths remove circles without new ones. For an essential preimage circle $a_i$, its degree to $c$ is nonzero by injectivity and has absolute value one: both simple circle classes are primitive by step 1.2 and the map on groups is an isomorphism. Orient all $a_i$ so they map to the same oriented $c$. The group isomorphism then makes the $a_i$ freely homotopic as ORIENTED curves. By [F1], their disjoint underlying circles are parallel; the chosen orientations agree under their annular parametrizations. Before proceeding, homotope each degree-$\pm1$ circle map to a PL homeomorphism and extend this homotopy across its disjoint transverse collar, replacing its signed normal coordinate by a linear one. The endpoint map is PL after finite strip subdivision and has exactly the same preimage circles, now with boundary homeomorphisms on both shores of every adjacent annulus. They are not empty: if all preimages disappeared, the image would lie in the cut surface $Y\setminus c$, a surface with boundary and graph spine, contradicting step 1.3. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.1, construct]

8.1 These parallel circles are nonseparating, and the sum of their signed transverse crossings is $\pm1$. Here is a direct group argument avoiding an unproved degree formula. A normal collar of the nonseparating $c$ defines a map $Y\to S^1$: its normal coordinate runs once around the circle and the map is $1$ outside the collar. The induced winding homomorphism $\chi_c:\pi_1(Y)\to\mathbb Z$ is onto, since a dual polygonal loop crosses $c$ once. Shrink the target collar so its full inverse image is contained in disjoint collars of the finitely many $a_i$, which is possible by compactness away from them. Their transverse signs are $s_i\in\{1,-1\}$. If their common parallel circle were separating, every loop would have zero signed crossing with it, contradicting surjectivity of $\chi_c\widehat f_*$. Thus it is nonseparating. Calling its corresponding onto character $\chi_a$, the actual collar crossing count gives $$\chi_c\widehat f_*=(\sum_i s_i)\chi_a.$$ The left side is onto because $\widehat f_*$ is onto; hence $\sum_i s_i=\pm1$. If there are more than one preimage circle, there are adjacent parallel ones with opposite signs. [F1, F2, step 2.1, step 7.1, construct, algebra]

9.1 Let $R$ be the annulus between such an adjacent opposite-sign pair. Its interior maps into $Y\setminus c$, and the two boundary curves map to the SAME boundary face of the compact surface obtained by cutting $Y$ at $c$, because their inward transverse signs are opposite. The restriction $R\to Y\mathbin{\|}c$ is injective on groups: its core maps to the essential boundary class, which has infinite order. Apply the already proved boundary-source result, steps 2.1–6.1, to this actual annulus map. A finite annular covering cannot send both of its boundary components to the same target boundary component; and a finite cover of a negative-Euler cut surface cannot be an annulus, as finite triangle counts multiply Euler characteristic. Therefore the restriction is homotopic relative to its two boundary homeomorphisms into that boundary face. With this collapse in place, push its normal coordinate to the common outside side across a thin strip, supported in $R$ and slightly larger collars of its two boundaries. This removes precisely those two preimage circles. In collar parameters this is replacing the normal function with two opposite-sign zeros by a function of the outside sign, keeping the two outer collar endpoints fixed; the angular map is the actual annular-collapse map. No new zero is created. Repetition terminates with exactly one preimage circle, since step 8.1 fixes the signed sum as $\pm1$. [F1, F2, step 6.1, step 7.1, step 8.1, construct]

10.1 Homotope the map on that remaining circle to its degree-$\pm1$ PL homeomorphism and straighten its transverse collar, preserving that sole preimage. Cut both closed surfaces there. The source cut surface is connected because its circle is nonseparating by step 8.1. The induced map of connected compact surfaces with boundary is injective on groups: for a nonseparating essential cut circle, form the infinite chain of copies of the cut surface, gluing alternate labeled circle faces in order. This is a covering of the original surface by its collar charts. Every finite chain has injective piece groups by [F3] and the infinite cyclic boundary injections in [F2]. A compact nullhomotopy lies in a finite chain, so the cut-piece group injects in that covering and then in the original by covering injectivity. Apply this to both source and target, and use injectivity of the original map. Its two boundary circles map homeomorphically to the TWO DIFFERENT boundary faces of the target cut surface. Apply the proved boundary-source classification once more. The annular-collapse alternative is impossible because the fixed boundary images are different faces; its finite covering degree must be one, since every target boundary point has exactly one preimage under its fixed boundary homeomorphism. It is therefore homotopic relative to both cut boundaries to a PL homeomorphism. Gluing their matched faces gives the required closed-surface homeomorphism, and their relative homotopies glue as well. [F1, F2, F3, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 8.1, step 9.1, construct]

11.1 In all noncollapse cases the original subgroup cover $Y\to B$ is finite-sheeted: $Y$ is compact and each fiber is closed discrete, hence finite, with a common cardinality by path lifting. Compose its PL covering with the homeomorphism just obtained to give the required finite PL covering $A\to B$. All boundary homotopies were held fixed whenever the initial boundary maps were local homeomorphisms; otherwise only the initial circle-normalization homotopy changed them. The annular collapse projects into $\partial B$. Every remaining closed or boundary possibility has been exhausted, proving the Statement. [F3, step 2.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 10.1, construct] ∎
