## Statement

Assume AC. Fix a discrete torsion-free cocompact group $\Gamma\le\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb R)$, a finite symmetric generating set $\mathcal S$, and a nonnegative integer $R$. There is a number $\gamma=\gamma(\Gamma,\mathcal S,R)>0$ such that every infinite-index subgroup $H\le\Gamma$ generated by at most $R$ elements has vertex expansion
$$|b_{\mathcal S}(V)|\ge\gamma|V|$$
for every finite vertex set $V$ of the right-coset Schreier graph $H\backslash\Gamma$. Here $b_{\mathcal S}(V)$ is the set of vertices outside $V$ joined to it by a generator edge. The same constant works for every such subgroup and every actual covering with this coset monodromy. No uniformity over different base metrics or unbounded subgroup ranks is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the fixed compact Fuchsian quotient, its finite symmetric generating set, the integer $R$, and an infinite-index subgroup with that generator bound.

[A1] Full choice is [[def-axiom-of-choice]].

[F1] The right-coset graph has edges $Hg\longrightarrow Hgs$. [[def-labeled-schreier-coset-graph]]

[F2] For a nonelementary finitely generated infinite-index subgroup of this compact Fuchsian group, its quotient has a compact convex core with finitely many geodesic boundary circles, area $2\pi(h-1)$ and free rank $h\le R$. The complement is the disjoint union of the actual funnels $r>0$, $t\bmod L$, with metric $dr^2+\cosh^2r\,dt^2$. [[thm-noncompact-finitely-generated-fuchsian-surfaces-have-finite-convex-cores-and-standard-ends]]

[F3] The cyclic and trivial subgroups of the same fixed group already have a common positive expansion bound for every fixed generating set. Its proof also constructs a finite compact Dirichlet polygon and proves its tiling and generating-set comparison directly. [[thm-cyclic-and-trivial-surface-cover-subgroups-have-common-positive-expansion]]

[F4] The smooth Riemannian divergence theorem holds under countable choice, and in coordinates the divergence is $(\det g)^{-1/2}\partial_i((\det g)^{1/2}X^i)$. [[thm-riemannian-divergence-theorem]], [[prop-coordinate-formula-for-riemannian-divergence]]

## Proof

1.1 Fix a center $o\in H^2$ and the finite compact Dirichlet polygon $D$ of [F3]. Write its area as $a>0$, let $k$ be its number of sides, and choose $m>0$ bounding their lengths. These choices depend only on $\Gamma$. More explicitly, compactness of the base bounds $d(z,\Gamma o)$ by some $B$; the Dirichlet comparisons put $D$ in $\overline B(o,B)$. The finitely many comparisons with $d(o,go)\le2B+2$ already exclude the radius-$B+1$ sphere, hence bound their convex intersection; all further comparisons are strict on that intersection. Properness makes this a finite polygon. Its translates tile with disjoint interiors, and the finite symmetric set $\mathcal T$ of side neighbors generates $\Gamma$ by following a generic compact path across finitely many tile sides. Thus the quotient tiles in $Y=H^2/H$ are indexed by $H\backslash\Gamma$, each of area $a$, even when sides or vertices of a tile are identified. [A1, F1, F3, given, construct]

1.2 First suppose $H$ is nonelementary, and let $K\subset Y$ be the compact core of [F2]. Its area is at most $c=2\pi R$. Define a field to be zero on $K$ and on each funnel to be $$X=\tanh r\,\partial_r.$$ It has norm at most one, agrees continuously with zero at the rim, and is smooth on each of those pieces. In funnel coordinates [F4] gives $$\operatorname{div}X=\frac1{\cosh r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}(\cosh r\tanh r)=1.$$ The normal flux at $r=0$ is zero. This last property is essential: it gives no singular flux supported on the core boundary. We justify the needed integral identity piece by piece rather than invoke an undeclared divergence theorem for nonsmooth fields. [F2, F4, construct, algebra]

2.1 For any finite tile collection $V$, lift its individual tile parametrizations to $H^2$ and split each tile along the lifts of the finitely many geodesic core rims meeting it. Only finitely many occur, by local finiteness of the geodesic boundary lifts and compactness of the tile. Intersections are finite geodesic segments, with coincident sides simply treated as boundary segments. On every funnel-side piece the field has a smooth extension across its rim in the Fermi coordinates. Apply [F4] there, rounding its finitely many corners and then passing to the limit: removed areas and inserted arc lengths tend to zero, and the smooth field and its divergence are bounded on the fixed compact piece. On core-side pieces the field is zero. Summing cancels every internal split-side flux, because the field has identical boundary value zero at a rim. Summing over the tiles then cancels every paired tile-side flux, including self-pairings and repeated neighbors. Consequently the total outer flux equals the area of the collection outside $K$, which is at least $a|V|-c$. No manifold-boundary assertion about the whole tile union is needed; identities were applied separately to its finite pieces. [F2, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 Each remaining tile side contributes outward flux at most its length $m$, since $|X|\le1$. A neighboring outside tile accounts for at most $k$ sides, with multiplicity. Hence $$a|V|-c\le mk\,|b_{\mathcal T}(V)|.$$ When $|V|\ge 2c/a$ this implies $|b_{\mathcal T}(V)|\ge a|V|/(2mk)$. To handle all smaller nonempty sets without any unjustified absorption, put $N=\max(1,\lceil2c/a\rceil)$. The coset graph is infinite because $H$ has infinite index and is connected because $\mathcal T$ generates $\Gamma$. Every nonempty finite set therefore has at least one outside neighbor: a path to any vertex outside it has a first exit. If $|V|<N$ this yields $|b_{\mathcal T}(V)|\ge |V|/N$. Thus $$\gamma_0=\min\{a/(2mk),1/N\}>0$$ works in both cases. Empty $V$ satisfies the inequality as well. All constants are independent of the position, lengths, genus and number of funnels of $H$; only the uniform core area bound was used. [F1, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, given, algebra]

4.1 Compare $\mathcal T$ to the specified $\mathcal S$. Write every member of $\mathcal T$ as a word of length at most $L\ge1$ in $\mathcal S$ and put $d=|\mathcal S|$. A vertex of $b_{\mathcal T}(V)$ is reached by such a word from $V$. Its path first exits $V$ at a vertex of $b_{\mathcal S}(V)$, followed by at most $L$ edges. A degree-at-most-$d$ graph has at most $(d+1)^{L+1}$ vertices at that distance from one vertex. Therefore $$|b_{\mathcal T}(V)|\le(d+1)^{L+1}|b_{\mathcal S}(V)|,$$ and $\gamma_0/(d+1)^{L+1}$ is a common $\mathcal S$ bound for all the nonelementary groups in question. This comparison retains loops and multiple edges and counts outside vertices throughout. [F1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 A torsion-free discrete elementary subgroup of this real compact-base group is trivial or cyclic, as in the classifier used in [F3]. Those cases have a common positive $\mathcal S$ bound $\gamma_{\mathrm{el}}$ by [F3]. Take the minimum of that bound and the one in step 4.1. This also handles $R=0$ or $R=1$, when no nonelementary subgroup generated by at most $R$ elements occurs. Finally a covering with the given coset monodromy has exactly the same labeled graph by lifting the specified generating loops, so relabeling its fiber or conjugating $H$ changes no border count. This proves all the stated uniformity, without assuming that every geometric subsurface intersection has the required index or rank. Those identifications remain separate application hypotheses. [F1, F3, step 4.1, given, algebra] ∎
