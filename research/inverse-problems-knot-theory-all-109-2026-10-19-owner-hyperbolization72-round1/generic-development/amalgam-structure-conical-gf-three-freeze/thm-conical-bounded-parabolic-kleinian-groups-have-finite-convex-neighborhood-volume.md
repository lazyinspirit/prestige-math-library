## Statement

Assume AC. Let $\Gamma\le\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)$ be a discrete torsion-free nonelementary Kleinian group, with limit set $\Lambda\subset\widehat{\mathbb C}$, and let $C=C(\Lambda)$ be its closed hyperbolic convex hull, the intersection of closed half-spaces whose ideal closures contain $\Lambda$. A point $p\in\Lambda$ is conical if, for some $o\in\mathbb H^3$ and some geodesic ray tending to $p$, a sequence of distinct orbit points $\gamma_j o$ tends to $p$ at bounded distance from that ray. A parabolic fixed point $p$ is bounded if $(\Lambda\setminus\{p\})/\operatorname{Stab}_\Gamma(p)$ is compact.

Suppose every point of $\Lambda$ is conical or bounded parabolic. Then all parabolic stabilizers are translation lattices of rank one or two in their normalized upper half-space coordinates. There is a $\Gamma$-equivariant collection of open horoballs $H_p$, one at each parabolic fixed point, whose distinct members are uniformly positively separated and which are precisely invariant under their stabilizers. There are only finitely many parabolic fixed-point orbits, and
$$\bigl(C\setminus\bigcup_p H_p\bigr)/\Gamma$$
is compact. For every $\epsilon>0$, the open and closed $\epsilon$-neighborhoods of $C/\Gamma$ have finite hyperbolic volume. Thus the group is geometrically finite in the convex-core-neighborhood-volume definition.

More explicitly, set $\delta_0=2\operatorname{arsinh}(1/4)$ and $\delta=2\operatorname{arsinh}(1/8)$. In a coordinate taking $p$ to infinity, if $\ell_p$ is the shortest nonzero stabilizer translation length, take
$$H_p=\{Y>\ell_p/(2\sinh(\delta/2))\}.$$
Distinct such horoballs have distance at least $2\log2$. The statement includes groups with no parabolics, both parabolic ranks, and any number of conformal-boundary components. It assumes no finite generation, compact conformal boundary, finite-sided fundamental polyhedron or pre-existing geometric-finiteness equivalence.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the discrete torsion-free nonelementary group and the conical-or-bounded-parabolic hypothesis in the Statement.

[A1] Full AC is [[def-axiom-of-choice]]. It supplies the selections of representatives and the finite covering arguments below.

[F1] In upper half-space, $ds^2=Y^{-2}(|dz|^2+dY^2)$ and $\cosh d((z,Y),(w,V))=1+(|z-w|^2+(Y-V)^2)/(2YV)$. The future hyperboloid is represented by positive Hermitian determinant-one matrices; a matrix $g$ acts by $X\mapsto gXg^*$. In particular $2\cosh d(I,gI)=\operatorname{tr}(gg^*)$. Closed bounded hyperbolic balls are compact, and Möbius maps extend isometrically with their usual boundary actions. These formulas and models are proved in the actual model suppliers. [[lem-complete-curvature-minus-one-manifolds-are-kleinian-quotients]], [[lem-hyperbolic-three-space-quasi-isometry-has-quasiconformal-boundary]]

[F2] Compact truncation of a convex hull with finitely many bounded-parabolic cusp orbits of translation rank one or two implies finite volume of every fixed-radius convex neighborhood. Its rank-one proof uses the transverse-strip bound and its rank-two proof the translation covolume. [[lem-compact-bounded-parabolic-truncations-have-finite-convex-neighborhood-volume]]

## Proof

1.1 A discrete subgroup acts properly on the model. To justify this directly, the set of model isometries carrying one fixed point into a compact set is compact: in the Hermitian model it is the product of a compact set of positive square roots with the compact stabilizer $\mathrm{SU}(2)$. Thus a compact set meets only finitely many translates of itself, since a discrete subgroup meets a compact subset of the matrix group in a finite set. A discrete subgroup is closed: if its elements accumulate at any matrix, quotients of two distinct nearby elements would tend to identity. The action is free because an isometry fixing an interior point is conjugate into the compact stabilizer; its powers either include identity or accumulate at identity, so discreteness and torsion-freeness exclude every nonidentity such isometry. Hence the quotient is a Riemannian covering quotient. These arguments also show that an affine boundary map of unit-modulus multiplier other than one cannot belong to a discrete torsion-free subgroup: it is conjugate to a rotation fixing an interior point. [A1, F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 If $p$ is parabolic, normalize it to infinity and choose one nontrivial translation $T_a:z\mapsto z+a$ in its stabilizer, where $a\ne0$. Any other stabilizer element is affine $z\mapsto uz+v$. If $|u|\ne1$, conjugating $T_a$ by suitable positive or negative powers gives distinct translations tending to identity, contrary to discreteness. If $|u|=1$ and $u\ne1$, the map has a finite fixed point and is a rotation about the vertical line through it, excluded by step 1.1. Thus the entire stabilizer acts by translations. Its lengths have a positive minimum $\ell$, since a discrete subset of a bounded Euclidean disc is finite. Rotate so a shortest translation is the positive real vector $\ell$. If every vector is real, division by $\ell$ leaves a remainder of absolute value less than $\ell$, forcing the group to be $\ell\mathbb Z$. Otherwise choose a vector of positive imaginary part and reduce its real part modulo $\ell$ to $[0,\ell)$. Among such vectors with imaginary part no greater than this candidate, one has a smallest positive imaginary part, since the corresponding closed rectangle meets the discrete translation group in a finite set. Call that vector $v$. Subtracting integer multiples of $v$ and then of $\ell$ reduces any lattice vector to that rectangle with imaginary part strictly smaller than $\operatorname{Im}v$. Minimality makes the imaginary part zero and the shortest-vector property makes the real remainder zero. Thus the stabilizer is the rank-two lattice $\ell\mathbb Z+v\mathbb Z$. This proves the full rank classification needed here without a Bieberbach import. [step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

2.2 We prove the complex Shimizu bound used to separate all cusps. Suppose a discrete group contains $T:z\mapsto z+1$ and $h=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with $c\ne0$. Then $|c|\ge1$. Otherwise set $h_0=h$ and $h_{j+1}=h_j T h_j^{-1}$. Matrix multiplication gives $c_{j+1}=-c_j^2$, $a_{j+1}=1-a_jc_j$, $b_{j+1}=a_j^2$ and $d_{j+1}=1+a_jc_j$. Writing $s=|c_0|<1$ gives $|c_j|=s^{2^j}$ and $|a_j|\le M$ for $M=\max(|a_0|,(1-s)^{-1})$. Hence $a_j\to1$, $b_j\to1$, $d_j\to1$ and $c_j\to0$. The elements $h_j$ are distinct because their nonzero lower-left entries have strictly decreasing absolute values. They converge to $T$, contradicting discreteness. For a group containing $z\mapsto z+\ell$ instead, dilation of the horizontal coordinate by $1/\ell$ replaces $c$ by $\ell c$. Thus $|\ell c|\ge1$ for every matrix not fixing infinity. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.3 Fix $o\in\mathbb H^3$ and its closed Dirichlet domain $D=\{x:d(x,o)\le d(x,\gamma o)\text{ for every }\gamma\in\Gamma\}$. Its translates cover the whole model: the distance of any fixed point to the discrete orbit $\Gamma o$ attains its minimum, since a closed bounded ball is compact and contains only finitely many orbit points. Each defining comparison is a closed hyperbolic half-space. For example in the hyperboloid model $\cosh d(x,o)$ and $\cosh d(x,\gamma o)$ are Lorentz linear functions of $x$; their comparison is the side of their bisecting hyperplane. Hence $D$ is convex and contains $o$. If an ideal point $p$ lies in its closure, the geodesic ray from $o$ to $p$ is contained in $D$, by taking limits of its segments from $o$. Such a $p$ cannot be conical. Indeed changing the orbit basepoint changes all orbit distances by one fixed constant, and any two rays tending to $p$ have tails at bounded Hausdorff distance: normalize $p=\infty$, when they are vertical rays, and use the distance formula in [F1] at equal heights. Thus conicality would give points $x_j$ on the ray from $o$, going to infinity, and orbit points $\gamma_j o$ with $d(x_j,\gamma_j o)\le B$ for one fixed $B$. The Dirichlet inequality would then give $d(x_j,o)\le B$, a contradiction. [F1, step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 The closed set of points displaced by at most $t>0$ by some nonidentity element of a parabolic stabilizer is the horoball $\{Y\ge\ell_p/(2\sinh(t/2))\}$. Indeed for a translation vector $v$, [F1] gives $2\sinh(d(x,T_vx)/2)=|v|/Y$, minimized when $|v|=\ell_p$. This description is independent of the normalized coordinate, so these horoballs form a $\Gamma$-equivariant family. For $t=\delta_0$, distinct cusp horoballs are disjoint. Suppose otherwise and take a common point $x$. Normalize the first cusp at infinity with shortest translation $\ell$, and write $x=(z,Y)$. Choose a shortest parabolic generator $h$ for the other cusp; its displacement at $x$ is at most $\delta_0$. Its matrix has $c_h\ne0$, and a lift has trace two. After conjugation by $w\mapsto(w-z)/Y$, its trace is still two and its lower-left entry is $Yc_h$. Writing that conjugated matrix as $I+N$, where $\operatorname{tr}N=0$, [F1] gives $2\cosh d(x,hx)=2+\|N\|_{HS}^2$. Therefore $|Yc_h|\le\|N\|_{HS}=2\sinh(d(x,hx)/2)\le2\sinh(\delta_0/2)$. The first cusp's displacement bound gives $\ell/Y\le2\sinh(\delta_0/2)$. Multiplying yields $|\ell c_h|\le4\sinh^2(\delta_0/2)=1/4$, contradicting step 2.2. [F1, step 2.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

3.2 The ideal boundary of $C$ is contained in $\Lambda$. If $q\notin\Lambda$, a sufficiently small round boundary disc about $q$ has closure disjoint from $\Lambda$. The complement of the hyperbolic half-space above that disc is a closed half-space whose ideal closure contains $\Lambda$ but excludes a neighborhood of $q$. By the stipulated convex-hull definition it contains $C$, excluding $q$ from its ideal closure. Also, for any distinct $p,q\in\Lambda$, their complete geodesic lies in $C$: every half-space in the defining intersection contains that geodesic by convexity and passage to limits of its endpoints. These elementary half-space assertions follow in the ball or upper half-space models of [F1]. Consequently every ideal accumulation point of $D\cap C$ belongs to $\Lambda$ and is not conical by step 2.3. By hypothesis it is a bounded parabolic fixed point. [F1, step 2.3, given, algebra]

3.3 We examine $D\cap C$ near any such point $p$, using the coordinate $p=\infty$. Write $o=(z_0,Y_0)$. For every stabilizer translation $v$, the Dirichlet inequalities and [F1] give $|z-z_0|^2\le|z-z_0-v|^2$, equivalently $2\operatorname{Re}((z-z_0)\overline v)\le|v|^2$. For rank two, applying this to both signs of two real-linearly independent lattice vectors bounds both horizontal coordinates of $z$. For rank one it bounds the coordinate along the generator. The other horizontal coordinate is bounded on $C$ by bounded-parabolicity: after rotating the generator to the real axis, the continuous imaginary-coordinate function descends to the compact quotient $(\Lambda\setminus\{\infty\})/A_p$, so $\Lambda$ lies in a bounded horizontal strip; its two vertical bounding half-spaces contain $C$. This is the strip argument proved explicitly in [F2]. Therefore in either rank $|z|$ is bounded on $D\cap C$. Outside $H_p$, the height is also bounded above. Thus $(D\cap C)\setminus H_p$ cannot accumulate at the ideal point infinity, because bounded horizontal coordinates and bounded height form a bounded Euclidean set and infinity is the point compactifying the whole Euclidean upper half-space and its finite boundary plane. [F1, F2, step 2.1, step 2.3, given, algebra]

4.1 Now take the open displacement horoballs at the smaller constant $\delta$. Their heights in each normalized coordinate are twice the $\delta_0$ heights. The distance from one smaller horoball to the complement of the larger horoball is at least $\log2$, by the inequality $|d\log Y|\le ds$; this value is approached on a vertical geodesic. Every path between two distinct smaller horoballs must exit one larger horoball and enter the other, since the larger closed horoballs are disjoint by step 3.1. Its length is therefore at least $2\log2$. Thus the stated family $\{H_p\}$ is uniformly separated, and equivariance makes each $H_p$ precisely invariant under its stabilizer. No finite list of cusp orbits has been assumed in this construction. [F1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 No parabolic fixed point is conical. Fix a model point $o$. Because the horoballs from step 4.1 are disjoint, $o$ belongs to at most one of them. If $\gamma o\in H_p$, then $o\in H_{\gamma^{-1}p}$ by equivariance, so the latter horoball must be that single fixed one, if it exists. Depth in a horoball is distance to its boundary and is isometry invariant. Consequently the depths of all points $\gamma o$ in $H_p$ are bounded by that one fixed depth of $o$; if $o$ lies in no horoball the orbit does not enter $H_p$. A geodesic ray to $p$ has depth tending to infinity, as is explicit from its vertical form after $p=\infty$ normalization. Points at bounded distance from this ray would also have unbounded depth, by the height inequality in [F1]. This is impossible for the orbit. Changing the orbit basepoint changes the distances by the fixed distance between the two basepoints, so it cannot alter this conclusion. Thus parabolic points are not conical; the hypothesis in the Statement implies that every parabolic fixed point is bounded. [F1, step 4.1, given, algebra]

5.2 Let $C_0=C\setminus\bigcup_p H_p$ and $D_0=D\cap C_0$. This is closed in $\mathbb H^3$. It has no ideal accumulation point: such a point would lie in the ideal closure of $D\cap C$, hence be bounded parabolic by step 3.2, whereas step 3.3 excludes accumulation there for the subset outside its particular horoball. Its closure in the compact closed ball model is therefore a compact subset lying wholly in $\mathbb H^3$. It follows that $D_0$ is compact. Since $C_0$ is $\Gamma$-invariant and translates of $D$ cover the model, the image of $D_0$ is exactly $C_0/\Gamma$. This quotient is thus compact. In particular compact truncation has been proved from the stated boundary criterion, not assumed as extra data. [F1, step 4.1, step 3.2, step 3.3, construct, algebra]

6.1 There are only finitely many parabolic orbits. For each parabolic point $p$, choose $q\in\Lambda\setminus\{p\}$. Their complete geodesic lies in $C$ by step 3.2. Its end at $p$ enters $H_p$, and its other end escapes $H_p$, so it crosses $\partial H_p$ at a point $x_p\in C$. This point lies outside every other cusp horoball by step 4.1, and therefore projects into the compact quotient $C_0/\Gamma$. Points chosen for different cusp orbits have quotient distance at least $2\log2$: every lift of one is on a different horoball from every lift of the other, and step 4.1 bounds all such distances. A compact metric space has only finitely many points separated by this positive constant, since finitely many balls of radius less than one-third of it cover the space. Therefore the orbit collection is finite. Choose its finite representatives. They satisfy all the hypotheses of [F2] by steps 2.1, 4.1 and 5.2 and the bounded-parabolic input. It follows that every fixed-radius convex neighborhood has finite hyperbolic volume, in both parabolic ranks. With no parabolics step 5.2 gives a compact convex quotient and [F2]'s empty-cusp case applies. This proves every conclusion in the Statement. [A1, F2, step 2.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 3.2, step 5.2, given, construct, algebra] ∎

## Actual source reading and missing reverse implication

The entire actual Bowditch Chapter 4 (PDF pp. 34–47), complete Sections 3.1–3.4 (pp. 18–24), and the full Dirichlet discussion pp. 31–34 were read, along with the Margulis summary pp. 16–17 and introductory pp. 1–5. This is not a claim to have read the whole paper. The direct proof here replaces the general-dimensional standard-cusp projection construction with an explicit complex Shimizu estimate and dimension-three translation coordinates. Its cusp separation and finite orbit count are proved even before finite generation is known.

The converse, finite convex-neighborhood volume implying the conical-or-bounded-parabolic criterion, requires an honest local thin-component classifier and packing argument. It is not asserted here, and no source theorem with an unproved parabolic case is used as a premise. Quasiconformal preservation of the criterion additionally needs its intrinsic boundary characterization; neither that separate interface nor a Maskit combination theorem is assumed here.
