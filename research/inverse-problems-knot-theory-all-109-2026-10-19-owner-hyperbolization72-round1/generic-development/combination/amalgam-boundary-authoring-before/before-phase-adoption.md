## Statement

Assume AC. Let $G_1,G_2$ be discrete torsion-free subgroups of $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)$ with a common proper subgroup $J$. Assume $J$ is elementary or nonelementary geometrically finite. Supply a Jordan circle $S$ dividing the sphere into closed Jordan discs $B_1,B_2$, and write $U_i=\operatorname{int}B_i$. Require that $J$ preserves both discs, that $\Lambda_J\subset S$, and, for $i=1,2$, that
$$B_i\cap\Lambda_{G_i}=\Lambda_J,$$
and $B_i\setminus\Lambda_J$ is precisely invariant under $J$ in $G_i$. Thus its translates by elements outside $J$ are disjoint from it. Assume the open interactive pair $(U_1,U_2)$ is proper: $U_2\setminus G_1U_1$ or $U_1\setminus G_2U_2$ is nonempty. At each rank-one parabolic point of $J$ require that $S$ has bounded transverse coordinate after normalizing its generator to a real translation; no such condition is imposed at rank two. Contacts of translated circles at limit points are allowed.

Put $G=\langle G_1,G_2\rangle$ and
$$R_i=\Omega(G_i)\setminus\bigcup_{h\in G_i}hU_i,\qquad S^\circ=S\setminus\Lambda_J.$$
Then $G$ is a discrete torsion-free amalgam, $S\cap\Omega(G)=S^\circ$, and this regular part of $S$ is precisely invariant under $J$ in $G$. All right-coset translates of $S$ form a null family: for pairwise distinct $g_nJ$, $\operatorname{diam}_{\rm sph}g_nS\to0$. Distinct translated circles do not cross; any contact between them belongs to $\Lambda(G)$.

The discontinuity set is exactly $G(R_1\cup R_2)$. Its quotient is obtained from $R_1/G_1$ and $R_2/G_2$ by identifying their copies of $S^\circ/J$ by the identity. This is an equality of the quotient topological spaces, with their actual conformal structures on the interiors and the conformal seam charts inherited from the original sphere. It is valid for disconnected pieces and empty seams. A point outside $G(\Lambda_{G_1}\cup\Lambda_{G_2})$ lies in $\Lambda(G)$ if and only if it is enclosed by an infinite nonbacktracking chain of the gluing discs; the discs in every such chain shrink to that point.

There is no assumption that $G_1,G_2$ are geometrically finite, that the circles are round, or that closed translated discs are disjoint. This theorem does not assert geometrical finiteness of the resulting group.

## Proof

1.1 Precise invariance of $B_i\setminus\Lambda_J$ implies $hU_i\cap U_i=\varnothing$ for $h\in G_i\setminus J$. Its open image therefore lies in $U_{3-i}$: it cannot meet $S$ since a neighborhood of a point of $S$ meets $U_i$. Consequently $hB_i\subset B_{3-i}$. The given properness and [F1] give $G=G_1*_JG_2$, discreteness and torsion-freeness. Distinct right-coset translates of $U_i$ in $G_i$ have disjoint interiors, and intersections of the corresponding closed discs can occur only in $\Lambda_{G_i}$: outside that set, both points in such an intersection are points of the precisely invariant set. [A1, F1, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 We record two elementary compactness details. For matrices escaping compact subsets of a discrete Möbius group, divide determinant-one representatives by their Euclidean matrix norm and take a subsequence. The nonzero limit has rank one; otherwise the original projective matrices would have a convergent invertible subsequence, violating discreteness. Its projective maps converge uniformly off the one kernel point to the image point. The Hermitian action on any interior model point tends to that same image point, which is thus in the actual group limit set. Next, if Jordan circles have spherical diameters tending to zero, each bounds a small disc of diameter tending to zero and its large complementary disc. Indeed a circle of diameter $\delta<\pi/4$ lies in a spherical $\delta$-ball, whose connected exterior lies in one complementary component. Its other component is contained in that ball. Among pairwise interior-disjoint Jordan discs with these boundaries, at most one eventually can be the large choice, since two complements of such small caps intersect in an open set. The same conclusion holds along any decreasing sequence of Jordan discs with these boundaries: every disc omits the fixed nonempty open complement of the first disc, whereas the complement of a large choice is contained in a cap of diameter tending to zero. Thus such a decreasing sequence has disc diameters tending to zero. [F3, F4, construct, algebra]

2.1 Apply [F2] inside $G_i$ to $S$, whose limit-set intersection and clearance are given. Therefore the circles $hS$, over distinct $hJ$, shrink. Step 1.2 and the disjoint interiors from step 1.1 imply the same shrinking for the discs $hB_i$. These discs are locally finite off $\Lambda_{G_i}$. Otherwise choose distinct cosets with discs meeting every neighborhood of some $x\notin\Lambda_{G_i}$. Their diameters shrink, so the images of every one of three fixed distinct points of $S$ tend to $x$. Step 1.2 applied to their matrices leaves at most one exceptional source point, and forces $x\in\Lambda_{G_i}$, a contradiction. In particular, at $x\in S^\circ$ no disc $hB_i$, $h\notin J$, contains or accumulates at $x$: containment is excluded by precise invariance and accumulation by local finiteness. [F2, step 1.1, step 1.2, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 Fix $x\in S^\circ$. It is regular for both factors by the given intersection equality. Choose a small open neighborhood $W$ of $x$ disjoint from every $hB_i$ with $h\in G_i\setminus J$, for both $i$, using step 2.1. Shrink it so that $hW\cap W=\varnothing$ for every nonidentity $h$ in either factor, using properness at $x$ in [F3]. For $h\in G_i\setminus J$, disjointness of $W$ from $h^{-1}B_i$ gives $hW\subset U_{3-i}$. Applying an alternating reduced word of length at least two to $W$, its last-applied letter $h\in G_i\setminus J$ therefore leaves the whole image in $hU_i\subset hB_i$, which is disjoint from $W$. Words of length one and elements of $J$ were already excluded. Thus $gW\cap W=\varnothing$ for every $g\ne1$ in $G$, so $x\in\Omega(G)$. Conversely $\Lambda_J\subset\Lambda_G$. We have proved $S\cap\Omega(G)=S^\circ$. The same word argument, beginning at a point of $S^\circ$ rather than its neighborhood, shows that no reduced word outside $J$ sends it to another point of $S^\circ$: a length-one such letter sends it strictly into the opposite open disc, and subsequent letters keep it in an outermost translated disc disjoint from $S^\circ$. Hence $S^\circ$ is precisely invariant under $J$. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, given, construct, algebra]

3.2 Now apply [F2] with ambient group $G$ and compact invariant set $S$. Step 3.1 supplies exactly $S\cap\Lambda_G=\Lambda_J$; the rank-one clearance of $S$ is unchanged. Therefore the circles $gS$ shrink over distinct right cosets $gJ$. No finite-generation or geometric-finiteness property of $G$ is used in this application. [F2, step 3.1, given, algebra]

4.1 The actual coset graph has vertices $G/G_1$ and $G/G_2$, edges $G/J$, with $gJ$ joining $gG_1$ to $gG_2$. It is connected by writing a finite factor word. It is a tree: removing immediate reversals from a closed edge path would give a nonempty alternating word outside $J$ equal to an element of $J$, contradicting [F1] after absorbing that last element. Its edge $gJ$ carries the circle $gS$; the half-space pointing from $gG_i$ towards $gG_{3-i}$ is $gB_i$. At a vertex $gG_i$, the outgoing discs are $ghB_i$, $hJ\in G_i/J$. Their interiors are disjoint, their boundary intersections are in $g\Lambda_{G_i}$, and their complement within $g\Omega(G_i)$ is $gR_i$. Along a nonbacktracking path these forward closed discs are nested. For two successive edges this is $hB_i\subset B_{3-i}$ from step 1.1 after changing coordinates by the initial prefix; induction proves the assertion along the path. This also shows that translated circles do not cross: connect their edges by the unique finite path and use its nested half-spaces. If two translated circles meet at a regular point for $G$, translate one to $S^\circ$ and use its precise invariance from step 3.1; the cosets must agree. Thus every contact of distinct circles lies in $\Lambda_G$. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 A point of $R_i$ on one of the outgoing circles $hS$ is regular for $G$ by step 3.1, since it is regular for $G_i$ and hence not in $h\Lambda_J$. A point of $R_i$ on none of those circles has a neighborhood $W$ disjoint from all $hB_i$, by step 2.1, and with disjoint nonidentity $G_i$ translates. Any reduced word outside $G_i$ is written with a possible left prefix in $G_i$, followed by a letter in $G_{3-i}\setminus J$, and a possible right suffix in $G_i$. The right suffix preserves $R_i$ and its neighborhood's disjointness from the outgoing discs. The first letter outside $G_i$ sends that neighborhood into $U_i$; further alternating letters and the left prefix put it in an outgoing $G_i$ disc, disjoint from $W$. To make the neighborhood assertion uniform over the suffix, take $\mathcal W=G_iW$: it is disjoint from all $hB_i$, is contained in $U_{3-i}$, and its image under every letter of $G_{3-i}\setminus J$ is contained in $U_i$. The same reduced-word induction applies to $\mathcal W$. Thus no word outside $G_i$ maps $W$ back into itself. This proves $R_i\subset\Omega(G)$ in all cases. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Take a point $x$ outside $G(R_1\cup R_2)$ and outside $G(\Lambda_{G_1}\cup\Lambda_{G_2})$. If it lies on an edge circle, it lies in that edge's regular seam and hence in both adjacent retained regions, a contradiction. At an initial vertex it therefore belongs to the interior of exactly one outgoing disc: otherwise it is in that vertex's retained region. Move across that edge and repeat. The reverse edge is not chosen, since its forward disc is the complementary half-space and $x$ is in the previous forward disc's interior. This constructs an infinite nonbacktracking path. Its edges are distinct cosets, its forward discs are nested, and step 3.2 makes their boundaries shrink. Step 1.2 makes their discs shrink, so their intersection is exactly $\{x\}$. Write their edge representatives $g_n$. The images of three fixed distinct points of $S$ tend to $x$; the rank-one matrix argument of step 1.2 implies $x\in\Lambda_G$. Conversely if a point is enclosed by such an infinite path, the same argument proves it is in $\Lambda_G$. Together with step 5.1 this proves both the asserted nested-disc characterization outside the factor limit sets and $\Omega(G)=G(R_1\cup R_2)$. [F3, step 1.2, step 3.2, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 It remains to check the quotient including its topology. In the tree description a regular point belongs to at most two vertex retained regions. If two vertices are separated by two or more edges, the path's first two half-spaces force a point common to their regions onto their edge-circle contact; step 4.1 excludes such contacts in $\Omega(G)$. The regions of two adjacent vertices meet exactly in that edge's regular seam, and its stabilizer is precisely the conjugate of $J$ by step 3.1. Every nonempty retained region contains an interior point: this is immediate off its outgoing circles, and follows from the two-sided neighborhood in step 3.1 at a regular seam. For a nonempty vertex retained region its stabilizer is its factor group: a word leaving that vertex puts any nonseam regular point in an outgoing disc as in step 5.1; a seam point can return to that same vertex only through its factor's existing seam translates. Thus all orbit identifications between $R_1$ and $R_2$ are exactly the stated factor identifications and the common seam identification. Locally away from seams, step 5.1 gives an ordinary factor-quotient chart. At a seam point, the neighborhood $W$ from step 3.1 has disjoint nonidentity $G$ translates; its two closed sides $W\cap B_1,W\cap B_2$ are precisely the two incident retained pieces, because $W$ avoids every other outgoing disc. Gluing these sides by the identity recovers $W$ with its original topology and conformal sphere chart. These local descriptions prove that the induced bijection from the stated glued quotient to $\Omega(G)/G$ is a homeomorphism, and has the specified conformal structure. They also cover empty seams and disconnected regions. No conical classification of the new nested points, cusp-rank computation or geometrical-finiteness conclusion has entered the proof. [F3, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, algebra] ∎

## Actual source reading and scope

The actual published Space I paper was read through PDF pp. 2–39, printed pp. 1097–1134, including all main combination proofs. The author reread the actual printed pp. 1114–1121 while developing this local block/tree proof. Its PDF SHA256 is 9a90e6bb93ea5d579c5a2cb62fdb2341539e4542a3ae4d9f03ee63c962b0b873. The earlier arXiv theorem has different geometric-finiteness hypotheses and is not substituted for the published theorem. Actual complete algebraic-amalgam and null-coset supplier proofs were read. The proof does not assume the resulting group is geometrically finite in order to obtain its shrinking edge-circle family: regularity at the original seam is proved first.

The full geometric-finiteness combination theorem additionally needs the conical behavior of the new nested points and the strong-block cusp compatibility, particularly when new parabolics or parabolic rank enlargement occur. Those operations, and the original generic hyperbolization theorem, remain separate obligations.
