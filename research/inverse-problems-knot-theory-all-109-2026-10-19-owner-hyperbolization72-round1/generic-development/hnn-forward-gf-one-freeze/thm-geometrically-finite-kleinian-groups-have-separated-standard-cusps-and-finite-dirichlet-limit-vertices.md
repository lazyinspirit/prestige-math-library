## Statement

Assume AC. Let $\Gamma\le\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)$ be discrete, torsion-free and nonelementary, let $\Lambda$ be its limit set and $C=C(\Lambda)$ its closed hyperbolic convex hull, and assume some positive-radius neighborhood of $C/\Gamma$ has finite volume. For each parabolic point $p$, its full stabilizer $A_p$ is a translation lattice of rank one or two and $p$ is bounded parabolic.

There is a $\Gamma$-equivariant family of standard open parabolic regions $E_p\subset\mathbb H^3$ with distinct closures separated by at least $2\log2$. They are precisely invariant under $A_p$. In upper half-space coordinates with $p=\infty$ and shortest translation length $\ell_p>0$, their forms are
$$E_p=\{(z,Y):(\operatorname{Im}z)^2+Y^2>S_p^2\}\quad\text{in rank one},\qquad E_p=\{Y>S_p\}\quad\text{in rank two}.$$
For rank one rotate the generator to the real direction. There is a finite $R_p\ge0$ with $\Lambda\setminus\{p\}\subset\{|\operatorname{Im}z|\le R_p\}$, and one may choose $S_p=2(R_p+2\ell_p)$. In rank two one may choose $S_p=4\ell_p$. The rank-one ideal peak domain $\{|\operatorname{Im}z|>S_p\}$ is contained in the discontinuity domain and precisely invariant under $A_p$.

Choose the center $o\in C$ for the closed Dirichlet domain $D=D(o)$. Then
$$V_D=\overline D\cap\Lambda$$
is a finite set of parabolic points, where the closure is in the closed-ball compactification. The following compactified truncation is compact and disjoint from $\Lambda$: remove from $\overline D$, for every $p\in V_D$, the open finite-coordinate extended region specified above, its strict ideal peak when the rank is one, and the point $p$ itself. Boundary points at equality with the displayed threshold are retained. Also $D\cap(C\setminus\bigcup_pE_p)$ is compact in $\mathbb H^3$.

Thus the compactified Dirichlet domain has only finitely many limit vertices and has a standard-cusp truncation avoiding the limit set. Its remaining ideal points may lie in the discontinuity domain; no compactness of an untruncated hyperbolic Dirichlet domain is asserted. Both parabolic ranks and the case of no parabolics are included.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the geometrically finite torsion-free nonelementary group, its convex hull, and the Dirichlet center in that hull.

[A1] Full choice is [[def-axiom-of-choice]]. It permits choices of parabolic-orbit representatives and normalizing coordinates.

[F1] The arbitrary-quotient local classifier gives properness, freeness, compact matrix sets over compact model sets, and full parabolic stabilizers that are translation lattices of rank one or two. [[lem-local-thin-components-of-torsion-free-kleinian-quotients]]

[F2] Finite positive-radius convex-neighborhood volume implies every limit point is conical or bounded parabolic, and every parabolic fixed point is bounded. [[thm-finite-convex-neighborhood-volume-implies-conical-bounded-parabolic-limit-set]]

[F3] The upper half-space metric is $Y^{-2}(|dz|^2+dY^2)$ and its isometries have the Möbius action and Hermitian model $X\mapsto gXg^*$. Closed model balls are compact, the closed-ball ideal boundary is the projective rank-one Hermitian boundary, and the usual geodesic and distance formulas hold. [[lem-complete-curvature-minus-one-manifolds-are-kleinian-quotients]], [[lem-hyperbolic-three-space-quasi-isometry-has-quasiconformal-boundary]]

## Proof

1.1 For each parabolic orbit choose a normalization and then transport it by $\Gamma$. By [F1] its stabilizer is a lattice. In rank one choose a shortest generator along the positive real axis; in rank two choose any shortest nonzero translation and rotate it to be positive real. Write its length as $\ell_p$. In rank one the imaginary coordinate descends continuously to the compact quotient $(\Lambda\setminus\{p\})/A_p$ from [F2], so it is bounded in absolute value by some $R_p<\infty$. Define $\rho_p(z,Y)=\sqrt{(\operatorname{Im}z)^2+Y^2}$ and $b_p=R_p$ in rank one; in rank two put $\rho_p=Y$ and $b_p=0$. First construct the larger regions $E_p^0=\{\rho_p>b_p+2\ell_p\}$. The definitions are invariant under the normalized stabilizer: real translations preserve the rank-one expression, and all horizontal translations preserve $Y$ in rank two. Hence transporting the representative region by different choices of an element carrying that representative to $p$ gives the same region. This defines a $\Gamma$-equivariant family before any disjointness assertion. [A1, F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 The translates of $D=D(o)$ cover the model: properness [F1] implies that the distance to the orbit $\Gamma o$ attains its minimum, which gives a Dirichlet representative. The domain is convex and contains $o$, since its defining comparisons are half-spaces; in the hyperboloid model $\cosh d(x,o)\le\cosh d(x,\gamma o)$ is a Lorentz-linear half-space inequality. Thus if $q\in\overline D\cap\partial\mathbb H^3$, the ray from $o$ to $q$ lies in $D$ by taking limits of finite segments. It cannot be conical: conicality, changing its orbit basepoint to $o$ and replacing its ray by this asymptotic ray, would give points $x_n$ on that ray tending to infinity with $d(x_n,\gamma_n o)\le L$ for a fixed $L$. The Dirichlet inequality then forces $d(x_n,o)\le L$, a contradiction. Rays to the same ideal point are at bounded distance on tails, directly from [F3] after that point is placed at infinity. Consequently every point of $\overline D\cap\Lambda$ is bounded parabolic by [F2]. Also the ideal boundary of $C$ is contained in $\Lambda$: a small round ideal disc about a point outside that closed limit set cuts off a half-space excluded from the defining convex-hull intersection. [F1, F2, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 We give the matrix bound for comparing different cusp coordinates. A discrete group containing $T:z\mapsto z+1$ has $|c|\ge1$ for every determinant-one matrix $h=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with $c\ne0$. Otherwise let $h_{n+1}=h_nTh_n^{-1}$. Direct multiplication gives $c_{n+1}=-c_n^2$, $a_{n+1}=1-a_nc_n$, $b_{n+1}=a_n^2$ and $d_{n+1}=1+a_nc_n$. For $s=|c_0|<1$, $|c_n|=s^{2^n}$ and $|a_n|\le\max(|a_0|,(1-s)^{-1})$. Thus $h_n\to T$ while their nonzero lower-left entries have strictly decreasing absolute values, contradicting discreteness. Scaling gives $|\ell c|\ge1$ when $T$ has translation length $\ell$. Now normalize two distinct parabolic points $p_i,p_j$ by coordinate maps $h_i,h_j$, and let $g=h_i h_j^{-1}$ have lower-left entry $c\ne0$. The lower-left entry of $gT_{\ell_j}g^{-1}$ is $-\ell_jc^2$. It lies in $h_i\Gamma h_i^{-1}$, which contains $T_{\ell_i}$. Applying the just-proved bound gives $|c|^2\ell_i\ell_j\ge1$. No assertion that the transition $g$ itself belongs to either coordinate group is needed. [F1, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 We need exact control outside each individual standard region. At a normalized parabolic point infinity write $o=(z_0,Y_0)$. Comparing its distance with the translate $o+v$ in the Dirichlet inequalities and using [F3] gives $2\operatorname{Re}((z-z_0)\bar v)\le|v|^2$. Applying this to both signs of a generator bounds the horizontal coordinate parallel to it. In rank two use two independent generators, bounding both horizontal coordinates on $D$. In rank one the other horizontal coordinate is bounded on $C$: its two vertical bounding half-spaces $|\operatorname{Im}z|\le b_p$ contain $\Lambda$ in their ideal closures, hence contain $C$. Thus $D\cap C$ has bounded horizontal coordinates in either rank. Outside $E_p$ its height is bounded as well, so $(D\cap C)\setminus E_p$ cannot accumulate at the ideal point infinity. For the full $D$ without intersecting $C$, rank one outside its extended region still has bounded imaginary coordinate and height, while the Dirichlet inequality bounds the real coordinate. Rank two again has both horizontal coordinates bounded and its outside-region height bounded. Therefore the portion of $D$ outside the finite-coordinate extended region and strict ideal peak has no accumulation at that parabolic point either, including sequences in the ideal boundary. [F3, step 1.1, step 1.2, given, algebra]

3.1 In these two coordinate frames $g\in\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)$ with $c\ne0$ has the exact upper half-space expression $Y'=Y/(|c|^2(|z+d/c|^2+Y^2))$ and $z'=a/c-\overline{z+d/c}/(c^2(|z+d/c|^2+Y^2))$. This follows by multiplying the Hermitian model matrix $Y^{-1}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}|z|^2+Y^2&z\\\bar z&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ by $g$ and $g^*$ in [F3]. In particular $|z'-a/c|^2+(Y')^2=1/(|c|^4(|z+d/c|^2+Y^2))$. Both $g\infty=h_i(p_j)$ and $g^{-1}\infty=h_j(p_i)$ are in the respective limit sets. If the source has rank one, its strip bound gives $|\operatorname{Im}(-d/c)|\le b_j$, and $\sqrt{|z+d/c|^2+Y^2}\ge\rho_j(z,Y)-b_j$. In rank two the same lower bound holds because $b_j=0$ and the square root is at least $Y=\rho_j$. At the target, if the rank is one, $|\operatorname{Im}(a/c)|\le b_i$ and the displayed norm identity gives $\rho_i(z',Y')\le b_i+1/(|c|^2\sqrt{|z+d/c|^2+Y^2})$. In rank two this inequality holds with $b_i=0$ since $\rho_i=Y'$. Hence a point with $\rho_j\ge b_j+2\ell_j$ has $\rho_i(z',Y')\le b_i+\ell_i/2<b_i+2\ell_i$, by step 2.1. The same calculation applies to finite ideal points with $Y=0$, and the source point infinity maps to $a/c$, whose target transverse coordinate is at most $b_i$ in rank one and whose height is zero in rank two. Thus even the closures of $E_{p_i}^0$ and $E_{p_j}^0$ in the ideal compactification are disjoint. This verifies disjointness for every pair of points, including different cusp orbits and both ranks. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Set $S_p=2(b_p+2\ell_p)$ and $E_p=\{\rho_p>S_p\}$ as in the Statement. The hyperbolic dual norm of $d\log\rho_p$ is at most one: in rank one it is $Y/\sqrt{(\operatorname{Im}z)^2+Y^2}\le1$, and in rank two it is one. Therefore every path from $\overline E_p$ to the complement of $E_p^0$ has length at least $\log2$. The larger closed regions are disjoint by step 3.1, so any path between distinct smaller closed regions has length at least $2\log2$. Equivariance gives precise invariance under the full stabilizer. In rank one the strict ideal peak lies in the discontinuity domain, since the limit set is contained in the smaller strip $|\operatorname{Im}z|\le b_p<S_p$. Its translates are disjoint by the same ideal disjointness calculation, and its stabilizer preserves it, so it is precisely invariant as stated. [F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Put $D_C^0=D\cap(C\setminus\bigcup_pE_p)$. It is closed in $\mathbb H^3$. If it had an ideal accumulation point, that point would lie in $\overline D\cap\Lambda$ by step 1.2 and hence be bounded parabolic. But $D_C^0$ lies outside that point's particular region, so step 2.2 excludes the accumulation. Compactness of the closed-ball compactification makes $D_C^0$ a compact subset of $\mathbb H^3$. Now take any $p\in\overline D\cap\Lambda$. The ray from $o$ to $p$ lies in $D\cap C$: it lies in $D$ by step 1.2, and in $C$ by convexity, $o\in C$ and passage to its ideal endpoint in $\Lambda$. It eventually enters $E_p$, as its normalized form is a vertical ray to infinity. Unless $o\in\overline E_p$, it therefore crosses $\partial E_p$ at $x_p\in D_C^0$; the point is outside every other region by step 4.1. At most one region closure contains $o$. Points $x_p$ for distinct remaining $p$ are separated by at least $2\log2$, again by step 4.1. A compact metric set has only finitely many points with this separation, since finitely many balls of radius less than $\log2$ cover it. This proves the finiteness of $V_D$, not merely finiteness of cusp orbits. [A1, F3, step 4.1, step 1.2, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

6.1 Perform the specified compactified truncation at those finitely many points of $V_D$. The remaining set has no limit point in any removed finite-coordinate open region or strict ideal peak, since the relevant strict inequalities define open sets there. Nor can it accumulate at a removed parabolic point, by the full-$D$ bound in step 2.2. It is therefore a closed subset of the compact closed-ball model, hence compact. Its intersection with $\Lambda$ is empty: all points of $\overline D\cap\Lambda$ are exactly the points of $V_D$, and all were removed. The no-parabolic case has $V_D=\varnothing$ by step 1.2 and gives the same assertion directly. This proves both the standard-region and Dirichlet conclusions without importing the omitted parabolic equivalence or a finite-sided-polyhedron theorem. [step 4.1, step 1.2, step 2.2, step 5.1, algebra] ∎
