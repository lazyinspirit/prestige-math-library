## 1. Closed witnesses for Borel sets

Assume AC. Put W=N^N with the usual complete metric determined by the first differing coordinate and the countable cylinder basis. Completeness follows because a Cauchy sequence eventually fixes each finite prefix; the stabilized coordinates give its limit. The eventually-zero sequences are a countable dense family. Coordinate pairing identifies W^N homeomorphically with W, using a fixed bijection N^2 to N.

In any Polish space P, call a subset closed-coded when it is the projection of a closed subset of P times W. Closed sets are closed-coded using their product with W. The class is closed under countable unions: if F_n are closed witnesses, use the first coordinate of a W witness to choose n and its tail for the F_n witness. The resulting subset is closed because the first coordinate is discrete. It is closed under countable intersections: code the witnesses for all F_n with coordinate pairing, impose all their closed conditions, and use AC to choose witnesses for the reverse inclusion. Every metric open set is a countable union of closed sets, using the positive distance to its closed complement; the whole space is handled separately. Thus both an open and its complement are closed-coded.

The class of sets whose members and complements are both closed-coded is a sigma-algebra: complement swaps the two witnesses; countable unions use the union coding for the sets and intersection coding for their complements. It contains the opens, so every Borel subset of P has a closed witness. Apply this to P=X times Y for Polish presentations of standard-Borel X and Y. A Borel relation R has a closed F contained in X times Y times W whose projection onto X times Y is exactly R. No Borel parametrization theorem or global Borel selector was imported.

## 2. Closed projections are measured Souslin sets

First let mu be a finite Borel measure on a Polish X. For any E contained in X set a(E)=inf{mu(B): B Borel and E contained in B}. Countable choice gives B_n containing E with measures tending to a(E). Their intersection H is Borel, contains E, and has measure a(E): it is contained in every B_n and has measure at least the infimum. If D is any Borel superset of E, then H intersect D also contains E, so its measure is at least a(E)=mu(H). Finite additivity therefore gives mu(H minus D)=0. This is the needed envelope property; no topological regularity, Lebesgue hull theorem, or subtraction of infinite measures is used.

Let (A_s), indexed by finite words in N, be a decreasing Borel scheme, including its root. Its branch set is S=union_f intersection_n A_(f restricted to n). Define E_s to be the branch union over branches extending s. Then E_s is contained in A_s and E_s=union_k E_(s followed by k). Choose a Borel envelope H_s for each E_s. Put B_s=A_s intersect intersection_(t prefix of s) H_t. These sets are Borel, decreasing, contain E_s and are contained in H_s. They retain the envelope property: a Borel superset D of E_s has B_s minus D contained in the null Borel set H_s minus D.

The Borel set C_s=B_s minus union_k B_(s followed by k) is null, since that union contains E_s. The union C of all C_s is Borel and null. If x belongs to B_root minus C, recursively choosing the least child retaining x produces a branch with x in every A along it. Conversely S is contained in B_root. Consequently B_root minus C is contained in S, which is contained in B_root. S is measurable for the completion of mu and agrees with the Borel B_root off C. This proves the measured Souslin assertion for arbitrary finite Borel mu.

Now take closed F contained in X times Z, with Z Polish. Fix a complete bounded compatible metric on Z and a countable dense family. Build countable open ball covers along a tree: the root is Z, each node's children cover its open set, their closed balls lie inside it, and their diameters tend to zero with depth. Such children exist by dense-center rational balls, shrinking the radius to stay inside the parent. Put A_s equal to the closure in X of the projection of F intersect (X times U_s). These are closed and decreasing.

The projection of F is exactly the Souslin branch set of A. A point (x,z) in F chooses a branch of covers containing z, so x lies in every A on that branch. Conversely, if x lies in those closures, choose x_n within 1/n of x and z_n in U_(branch restricted to n) with (x_n,z_n) in F. The nested sets and shrinking diameters make z_n Cauchy; completeness gives z. Closedness of F implies (x,z) in F. AC licenses these countably many witnesses. Thus the projection has the completion-measurability and Borel-version conclusion just proved.

For a Borel subset of X times Z, first use section 1 to obtain a closed witness in X times Z times W; project the latter onto X and apply the same argument to the Polish space Z times W. Therefore every such Borel projection is measurable in the completion of every finite Borel measure on X.

For sigma-finite mu, choose a countable disjoint Borel partition (D_j) into finite-measure sets. Give mu on D_j the positive weight 2^(-j-1)/(1+mu(D_j)). The resulting finite Borel measure has exactly the same null sets as mu. Its completion and that of mu coincide, since both consist of a Borel set changed on a subset of a Borel null set.
