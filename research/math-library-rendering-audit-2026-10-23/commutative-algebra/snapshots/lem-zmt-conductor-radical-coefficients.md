## Statement

Let $\varphi:R[x]\to S$ be a unital ring map of commutative rings that is
**finite**, that is, $S$ is finitely generated as an $R[x]$-module
([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]), and let
$J=\{g\in S: gS\subseteq\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)\}$ be the conductor of the
image of $\varphi$. Assume the following **integral-image condition**: every element
of $S$ integral over the ring map $R\to S$ in the sense of
[[def-integral-subalgebra-of-an-arbitrary-ring-map]] lies in
$\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)$.

Then $J$ is an ideal of $S$
([[def-radical-of-an-ideal]]) and the following hold, for $u\in S$ and
$P=a_0+a_1x+\cdots+a_kx^k\in R[x]$ with $k\ge0$
([[def-polynomial-ring-over-a-commutative-ring]]):

1. if $u\,\varphi(P)\in J$, then $u\,\varphi(a_k)^m\in J$ for some integer
   $m\ge0$;
2. if $u\,\varphi(P)\in\sqrt J$, then $u\,\varphi(a_i)\in\sqrt J$ for every
   $i\in\{0,\ldots,k\}$.

No hypothesis is imposed on the leading coefficient: it may be zero, a zero
divisor or a nilpotent, and $\varphi$ need not be injective.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite unital ring map $\varphi:R[x]\to S$ such that every element of $S$ integral over $R$ lies in $\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)$, the conductor $J=\{g\in S:gS\subseteq\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)\}$, an element $u\in S$ and a polynomial $P=a_0+a_1x+\cdots+a_kx^k\in R[x]$ with $k\ge0$.

[L1] For a unital ring map $R[x]\to S$ the set of elements of $S$ integral over the map is a subring of $S$ containing the image of $R[x]$; an element of $S$ integral over $R$ is in particular integral over the ring map $R[x]\to S$, and the separate integral-image condition in the Statement places each $R$-integral element in $\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)$ ([[def-integral-subalgebra-of-an-arbitrary-ring-map]]).

[L2] A commutative algebra is module-finite over its base ring when it is finitely generated as a module; in particular the finite map $\varphi$ exhibits $S$ as generated by finitely many elements $t_1,\ldots,t_n$ as an $R[x]$-module ([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]).

[L3] Let $A\subseteq B$ be commutative rings with $A\ne0$ and $b\in B$. Then $b$ is integral over $A$ if and only if $A[b]$ is finitely generated as an $A$-module; for that reason every element of a module-finite $A$-algebra $B$ is integral over $A$ ([[thm-integrality-and-finite-module-equivalences]]).

[L4] Let $\varphi:R[x]\to S$ be a unital ring map, let $t\in S$ be integral over $\varphi(R[x])$ and let $p\in R[x]$, $p=c_0+\cdots+c_kx^k$, satisfy $t\varphi(p)\in\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)$. Then there are $q\in R[x]$ and $n\ge0$ with $\varphi(c_k)^nt-\varphi(q)$ integral over $R$ ([[lem-zmt-one-variable-integral-correction]]).

[L5] For an ideal $I$ of a commutative ring the radical is $\sqrt I=\{x:x^n\in I\text{ for some }n\ge1\}$, and $\sqrt I$ is an ideal containing $I$ ([[def-radical-of-an-ideal]], [[lem-radical-is-an-ideal]]).

[L6] Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $f,g\in R[x]$ nonzero. Then the coefficient of $x^{\deg f+\deg g}$ in $fg$ is $\operatorname{lc}(f)\operatorname{lc}(g)$, and the coefficients of a product of polynomials are polynomial expressions in the coefficients of the factors ([[prop-polynomial-degree-laws-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The conductor $J$ is an ideal of $S$. It contains $0$ because $0\cdot S=0\subseteq\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)$; it is closed under addition, since $(g+g')S\subseteq gS+g'S\subseteq\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)$ for $g,g'\in J$; and it is closed under multiplication by $S$, since $(sg)S=g(sS)\subseteq gS\subseteq\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)$ for $g\in J$ and $s\in S$. If $S=0$, then $J=S=\{0\}$ and both assertions of the Statement hold trivially; hence we may assume from now on that $S\ne0$ and, by [L2], that $S=R[x]t_1+\cdots+R[x]t_n$ for finitely many $t_j\in S$. [given, L2]

2.1 For $g\in S$ one has $g\in J$ if and only if $gt_j\in\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)$ for all $j$: one direction is immediate from $t_j\in S$, and conversely $gS\subseteq\sum_jgR[x]t_j\subseteq\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)$ because $gt_j\in\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)$ for the finitely many module generators $t_j$ of [L2] and $\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)$ is closed under multiplication by elements of $\varphi(R[x])$. [given, step 1.1, L2]

3.1 Assume now that $u\varphi(P)\in J$ with $u\in S$ and $P=\sum_{i=0}^ka_ix^i$. For each $j$ the element $ut_j\in S$ is integral over $R[x]$ by [L3], because $S$ is module-finite over $R[x]$ by [L2]; and $u\varphi(P)t_j\in\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)$ by step 2.1. Applying [L4] with $t=ut_j$ and $p=P$ provides $q_j\in R[x]$ and an integer $n_j\ge0$ such that $\varphi(a_k)^{n_j}ut_j-\varphi(q_j)$ is integral over $R$; this element lies in $\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)$ by the integral-image condition of the Statement. Hence $\varphi(a_k)^{n_j}ut_j\in\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)$ for every $j$. [given, step 1.1, step 2.1, L1, L2, L3, L4]

4.1 Put $m=\max_jn_j\ge0$. For every $j$, $u\varphi(a_k)^mt_j=\varphi(a_k)^{m-n_j}\bigl(\varphi(a_k)^{n_j}ut_j\bigr)\in\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)$, because $\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)$ is closed under multiplication by elements of $\varphi(R[x])$ and $m-n_j\ge0$. By the criterion of step 2.1 this says $u\varphi(a_k)^m\in J$, which is assertion 1. [given, step 2.1, step 3.1, L2]

5.1 Now assume $u\varphi(P)\in\sqrt J$. By [L5] there is $N\ge1$ with $(u\varphi(P))^N\in J$. Since $\varphi$ is a ring homomorphism, $(u\varphi(P))^N=u^N\varphi(P^N)$. The coefficient of $x^{Nk}$ in $P^N$ is $a_k^N$ by polynomial multiplication, even if this coefficient is zero and the degree is less than $Nk$. Applying assertion 1 in the form of step 4.1 to $u^N$ and $P^N$ gives $m\ge0$ with $u^N\varphi(a_k)^{Nm}\in J$. If $m\ge1$, multiply by $u^{N(m-1)}$ to get $(u\varphi(a_k))^{Nm}\in J$; if $m=0$, multiply $u^N\in J$ by $\varphi(a_k)^N$ to get $(u\varphi(a_k))^N\in J$. In either case a positive power of $u\varphi(a_k)$ lies in $J$, so $u\varphi(a_k)\in\sqrt J$ by [L5]. [given, step 4.1, L5, L6]

6.1 Since $\sqrt J$ is an ideal of $S$ by [L5], the element $u\varphi(a_kx^k)=u\varphi(a_k)\varphi(x)^k$ lies in $\sqrt J$, and therefore $u\varphi(P-a_kx^k)=u\varphi(P)-u\varphi(a_kx^k)\in\sqrt J$; here $P-a_kx^k=a_0+a_1x+\cdots+a_{k-1}x^{k-1}$ has degree $\le k-1$. Induction on $k\ge0$ now gives $u\varphi(a_i)\in\sqrt J$ for every $i\in\{0,\ldots,k\}$: the case $k=0$ is the hypothesis itself, and the induction step is the reduction just performed together with step 5.1. [given, step 5.1, L5]

7.1 Steps 4.1 and 6.1 prove assertions 1 and 2 for every $u\in S$ and every $P\in R[x]$; the proof uses only finite choice (the finitely many module generators $t_j$ and the finite maximum $m$) and is therefore choice-free. ∎ [step 4.1, step 6.1]
