## Statement

Let $k$ be any field, let $t$ be an indeterminate and let
$A:=k[t^{2},t^{3}]\subseteq k[t]$ be the $k$-subalgebra generated by $t^{2}$
and $t^{3}$. Then the integral closure of $A$ in the rational function field
$k(t)$ is exactly $k[t]$, and $k[t]=A+At$ is a finite $A$-module.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** a field $k$ and the $k$-subalgebras $A=k[t^{2},t^{3}]\subseteq k[t]\subseteq k(t)$ of the polynomial ring in one indeterminate and its fraction field.

[L1] For a field $k$ and a set $S$ of elements of a $k$-algebra, the subalgebra generated by finitely many elements is written $k[s_1,\ldots,s_r]$, the smallest subring containing $k$ and the $s_i$; $\operatorname{Frac}(D)$ denotes the fraction field of a domain $D$ ([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]], [[def-field-extension-generated-subfields-and-simple-extension]], [[def-field-of-fractions]]).

[L2] The polynomial ring $k[t]$ over the field $k$ is an integral domain, and its fraction field is the rational function field $k(t)$ ([[cor-polynomial-ring-over-a-domain-is-a-domain]], [[def-zero-divisor-and-integral-domain]], [[def-field-of-fractions]]).

[L3] For every field $F$ and every finite $d\ge0$ the polynomial ring $F[y_1,\ldots,y_d]$ is an integrally closed domain ([[lem-polynomial-algebras-over-fields-are-integrally-closed]], [[def-integral-closure-and-integrally-closed-domain]]).

[L4] An element is integral over a subring when it is a root of a monic polynomial over that subring; integrality is transitive along domain inclusions; elements integral over a subring form a subring; an integrally closed domain contains every element of its fraction field integral over it ([[def-integral-element-and-algebraic-integer]], [[thm-transitivity-of-integrality]], [[cor-integral-elements-form-a-subring]], [[def-integral-closure-and-integrally-closed-domain]]).

[L5] In a domain, $a\ne0$ implies $ab=ac$ if and only if $b=c$, so a nonzero element of an integral domain is not a zero divisor ([[def-zero-divisor-and-integral-domain]], [[def-divisibility-and-associates-in-a-domain]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The element $t$ is nonzero in the domain $k[t]$, and then $t^{2}\ne0$ because a nonzero element of an integral domain is not a zero divisor by [L5]; so $t=t^{3}/t^{2}$ is a quotient of two elements of $A$ with nonzero denominator, and $t\in\operatorname{Frac}(A)$ by [L1]. Consequently $k[t]=A[t]\subseteq\operatorname{Frac}(A)$, and since $A\subseteq k[t]\subseteq\operatorname{Frac}(A)$ with $\operatorname{Frac}(A)$ a field, $k(t)=\operatorname{Frac}(k[t])\subseteq\operatorname{Frac}(A)$ by [L2]; the reverse inclusion is $A\subseteq k[t]$. Hence $\operatorname{Frac}(A)=k(t)$. [L1, L2, L5, given]

1.2 The element $t$ is a root of the monic polynomial $T^{2}-t^{2}\in A[T]$, so $t$ is integral over $A$ by [L4]. Every element of $k[t]$ is a finite sum $\sum_{n}a_{n}t^{n}$ with $a_n\in k$, and reducing exponents modulo $2$ via $t^{2}=t^{2}\in A$ expresses it as an $A$-linear combination of $1$ and $t$: that is, $k[t]=A+At$ is generated as an $A$-module by the two elements $1,t$. Being a finite $A$-module generated by integral elements it is integral over $A$, so every element of $k[t]$ is integral over $A$. [L4, given]

2.1 Suppose $z\in k(t)=\operatorname{Frac}(A)$ is integral over $A$. A monic equation for $z$ over $A$ has coefficients in $A\subseteq k[t]$, so $z$ is integral over $k[t]$; since $k[t]$ is integrally closed in its fraction field $k(t)$ by [L3] and [L2], such a $z$ lies in $k[t]$. Conversely every element of $k[t]$ is integral over $A$ by step 1.2. Therefore the integral closure of $A=k[t^{2},t^{3}]$ in $k(t)$ is exactly $k[t]$, which is the finite $A$-module $A+At$. [L2, L3, L4, step 1.1, step 1.2] ∎
