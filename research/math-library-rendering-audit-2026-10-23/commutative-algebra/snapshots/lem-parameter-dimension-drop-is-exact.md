## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice.

Let $(R,\mathfrak m)$ be a Noetherian local ring of positive dimension $d$, and let $(x,x_2,\ldots,x_d)$ be a system of parameters. Then
$$ \dim(R/(x))=d-1. $$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, a Noetherian local ring $(R,\mathfrak m)$ with $d=\dim R>0$, and a system of parameters $(x,x_2,\ldots,x_d)$.

[L1] Since $(x,x_2,\ldots,x_d)$ is a system of parameters, its ideal is $\mathfrak m$-primary. Hence the images of $x_2,\ldots,x_d$ generate an $\mathfrak m/(x)$-primary ideal in $R/(x)$; no dimension of the quotient is assumed here ([[def-system-of-parameters-and-parameter-ideal]], [[lem-parameter-ideal-equivalent-m-primary]]).

[L2] Under the Axiom of Choice, a prime minimal over an ideal generated by $r\ge1$ elements has height at most $r$, and conversely a prime of finite height $r$ is locally minimal over $r$ generators ([[thm-krull-height-theorem]], [[thm-krull-height-theorem-converse]]).

[L3] Prime ideals of $R/(x)$ correspond to prime ideals of $R$ that contain $(x)$ ([[thm-prime-spectrum-of-a-quotient-bijection]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [L1], the maximal ideal of $R/(x)$ is minimal over an ideal generated by $d-1$ elements. If $d=1$, that ideal is zero, so the maximal ideal is a minimal prime and the quotient has dimension zero. If $d\ge2$, apply the height theorem to its $d-1\ge1$ generators. In either case $\dim(R/(x))\le d-1$. [L1, L2, L3, given]

2.1 Suppose instead that $\dim(R/(x))\le d-2$. The quotient is a nonzero local ring, so this is impossible when $d=1$. For $d\ge2$, [L2] applied in the local ring $R/(x)$ provides an ideal generated by at most $d-2$ elements whose radical is the maximal ideal $\mathfrak m/(x)$. Lifting those generators to $R$ and adjoining $x$, we obtain an ideal of $R$ generated by at most $d-1$ elements with radical $\mathfrak m$. Applying the height theorem to the maximal ideal of $R$ would then give $d=\dim R\le d-1$, contradiction. [L2, L3, step 1.1]

3.1 Therefore $\dim(R/(x))=d-1$. [step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
