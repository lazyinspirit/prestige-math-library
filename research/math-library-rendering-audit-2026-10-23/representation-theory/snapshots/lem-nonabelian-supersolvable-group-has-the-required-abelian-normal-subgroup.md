## Statement

Let $G$ be a finite group and suppose $G$ has a series
$$1=G_0\triangleleft G_1\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft G_r=G$$
in which every term $G_i$ is normal in the whole group $G$ and every factor
$G_i/G_{i-1}$ has prime order; that is, $G$ is supersolvable in the sense of
([[def-supersolvable-groups-and-monomial-characters]]) with all series terms
normal in $G$.

If $G$ is nonabelian, then $G$ contains an abelian normal subgroup
$A\trianglelefteq G$ with $A\nsubseteq Z(G)$. Moreover, if
$K\trianglelefteq G$ is such that the quotient $G/K$ is nonabelian, then
$G/K$ contains an abelian normal subgroup that is not contained in $Z(G/K)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite group $G$ with a series $1=G_0\triangleleft G_1\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft G_r=G$ of subgroups normal in $G$ and prime-order factors, and a normal subgroup $K\trianglelefteq G$ such that $G/K$ is nonabelian; the first assertion assumes in addition that $G$ is nonabelian.

[F1] Along such a series each factor $G_i/G_{i-1}$ has prime order, in particular $G_i/G_{i-1}$ is cyclic and nontrivial for $i\ge1$. ([[def-supersolvable-groups-and-monomial-characters]]).

[F2] For a subgroup $N\le G$, the product $NK=\{nk:n\in N,k\in K\}$ of normal subgroups $N,K\trianglelefteq G$ is a normal subgroup of $G$ containing both. ([[def-normal-subgroup]]).

[F3] In the quotient $G/K$ the elements are the cosets $gK$, the quotient map $\pi:G\to G/K$, $g\mapsto gK$, is a surjective homomorphism, and $\pi(N)=NK/K$ for a subgroup $N\le G$. ([[def-quotient-group]]).

[F4] $Z(G)=\{z\in G:zg=gz\text{ for all }g\in G\}$, and $G$ is abelian precisely when $G=Z(G)$. ([[def-center-of-a-group]]).

[F5] First isomorphism theorem: for a group homomorphism $\varphi$ with kernel $L$, the induced map $G/L\to\operatorname{im}\varphi$ is an isomorphism. ([[thm-first-isomorphism-theorem-groups]]).

[A1] For any subgroup $G_{i-1}\trianglelefteq G_i$ of prime index $p$ and any $x\in G_i\setminus G_{i-1}$, the subgroup generated by $G_{i-1}$ and $x$ equals $G_i$, so every element of $G_i$ has the form $gx^{k}$ with $g\in G_{i-1}$ and $k\in\mathbb Z$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $K\trianglelefteq G$ and put $\bar G:=G/K$, $\bar G_j:=G_jK/K$ for $j=0,\dots,r$. Each $\bar G_j$ is a subgroup of $\bar G$ containing $\bar G_{j-1}$ and is normal in $\bar G$, because $G_j\trianglelefteq G$ and $K\trianglelefteq G$ ([F2], [F3]); moreover $\bar G_0=K/K=1$ and $\bar G_r=G/K=\bar G$. For each $j$ the map $\varphi:\bar G_j\to G_jK/G_{j-1}K$, $gK\mapsto gG_{j-1}K$, is well defined and a surjective homomorphism with kernel $\bar G_{j-1}=G_{j-1}K/K$, so by [F5] the quotient $\bar G_j/\bar G_{j-1}$ is isomorphic to $G_jK/G_{j-1}K$; that group is in turn a quotient of the prime-order group $G_j/G_{j-1}$, since $G_j\to G_jK/G_{j-1}K$ is surjective with $G_{j-1}$ in its kernel. Hence every factor $\bar G_j/\bar G_{j-1}$ has order $1$ or prime. [F2, F3, F5, given, algebra]

1.2 Now assume that $G$ is nonabelian, so $G=G_r\nsubseteq Z(G)$ by [F4], while $G_0=1\subseteq Z(G)$. Let $i\in\{1,\dots,r\}$ be the least index with $G_i\nsubseteq Z(G)$; such an index exists because $G_r\nsubseteq Z(G)$, and $i\ge1$ because $G_0=1$ is central. By minimality $G_{i-1}\subseteq Z(G)$. [F4, given, algebra]

2.1 By step 1.2 the subgroup $G_{i-1}$ is contained in $Z(G)$, hence in $Z(G_i)$; by [F1] the factor $G_i/G_{i-1}$ has prime order, so $G_i=G_{i-1}\langle x\rangle$ for any $x\in G_i\setminus G_{i-1}$ by [A1], and every element of $G_i$ is of the form $gx^{k}$ with $g\in G_{i-1}$. Two such elements $gx^{k}$ and $g'x^{k'}$ commute, because $g,g'\in G_{i-1}\subseteq Z(G_i)$ and powers of $x$ commute with each other. Hence $G_i$ is abelian. [A1, F1, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Thus $A:=G_i$ is abelian by step 2.1, it is normal in $G$ by hypothesis, and $A=G_i\nsubseteq Z(G)$ by the choice of $i$ in step 1.2. This is the asserted abelian normal subgroup of $G$ and proves the first assertion. [step 1.2, step 2.1, given]

4.1 Finally assume that $\bar G=G/K$ is nonabelian, so $\bar G=\bar G_r\nsubseteq Z(\bar G)$ by [F4] while $\bar G_0=1\subseteq Z(\bar G)$. Let $j$ be the least index with $\bar G_j\nsubseteq Z(\bar G)$; then $j\ge1$, $\bar G_{j-1}\subseteq Z(\bar G)$, and $\bar G_j\ne\bar G_{j-1}$, so by step 1.1 the factor $\bar G_j/\bar G_{j-1}$ has prime order. The computation of step 2.1, applied with the pair $(G_i,G_{i-1})$ replaced by the pair $(\bar G_j,\bar G_{j-1})$ inside $\bar G$, shows in the same way that $\bar G_j$ is abelian, while $\bar G_j\nsubseteq Z(\bar G)$ by the choice of $j$. Hence the nonabelian quotient $\bar G$ also contains an abelian normal subgroup that is not contained in its center, which is the second assertion. [F4, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
