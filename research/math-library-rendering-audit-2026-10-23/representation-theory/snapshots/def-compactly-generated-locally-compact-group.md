## Definition

Let $G$ be a locally compact Hausdorff topological group ([[def-topological-group]], [[def-locally-compact-space]], [[def-hausdorff-space]]). It is **compactly generated** if some compact subset $K\subseteq G$ ([[def-compact-space]]) generates $G$ as an abstract group, that is, $\langle K\rangle=G$ ([[def-generated-subgroup]]). Equivalently, there is a compact subset $Q\subseteq G$ with $e\in Q$ and $Q=Q^{-1}$ such that
$$G=\bigcup_{n\ge1}Q^n,\qquad Q^n=\{q_1\cdots q_n:q_1,\ldots,q_n\in Q\}.$$

## Remarks

For the equivalence, given a compact generating set $K$, take $Q=K\cup K^{-1}\cup\{e\}$. Inversion is a homeomorphism, so $K^{-1}$ is compact; a finite union of compact subsets is compact by the open-cover definition. The set $Q$ is symmetric and contains $e$. Its positive finite products form a subgroup: they contain $e$, are closed under concatenation, and are closed under inverses by symmetry. This subgroup contains $K$ and is contained in every subgroup containing $K$, so it is exactly $\langle K\rangle$; the copies of $e$ pad shorter words to any larger exponent. Conversely, if a symmetric identity-containing $Q$ has $G=\bigcup_{n\ge1}Q^n$, then every element of $G$ belongs to the subgroup generated by $Q$.

Every compact group is compactly generated, by taking $K=G$. More generally, every connected locally compact Hausdorff group is compactly generated, so in particular this holds for the connected $\sigma$-compact case. Indeed, choose a compact neighbourhood $K$ of the identity ([[def-neighbourhood-top]]) and let $H=\langle K\rangle$. Since $K$ contains an open neighbourhood $U$ of the identity, $H$ contains $U$ and is open: for each $h\in H$, the open set $hU$ lies in $H$. Every coset of an open subgroup is open, so the complement of $H$ is open as well. Thus $H$ is clopen, and connectedness forces $H=G$ ([[def-connected-space]]). No choice principle is used.
