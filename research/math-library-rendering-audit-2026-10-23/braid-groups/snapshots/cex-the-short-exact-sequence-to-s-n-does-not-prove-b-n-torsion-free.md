## Statement refuted

Let $1\to K\to G\to Q\to1$ be a short exact sequence of groups with $K$
torsion-free and $Q$ finite. Then $G$ is torsion-free. In particular the
configuration braid short exact sequence
$1\to PB_n\to B_n^{\mathrm{conf}}\to S_n\to1$, whose kernel $PB_n$ is
torsion-free and whose quotient $S_n$ is finite, would by itself show that
$B_n^{\mathrm{conf}}$ is torsion-free.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the Axiom of Choice, an integer $n\ge0$, the groups $(\mathbb Z,+,0)$ and $(\mathbb Z/2,+)$, and their external direct product $\mathbb Z\times(\mathbb Z/2)$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice holds ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F1] Assume AC. Then $PB_n$ is torsion-free for every $n\ge0$: if $g\in PB_n$ and $g^m=e$ for some $m\ge1$, then $g=e$ ([[thm-pure-braid-groups-are-torsion-free]]). AC is used only for this citation ([[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]]).

[F2] For every $n\ge0$ the sequence $1\to PB_n\xrightarrow{p_*}B_n^{\mathrm{conf}}\xrightarrow{\pi}S_n\to1$ of the configuration groups is short exact: $p_*$ is injective, $\pi$ is surjective and $\operatorname{im}p_*=\ker\pi$ ([[thm-configuration-braid-pure-braid-short-exact-sequence]]); moreover $|S_n|=n!$, so $S_n$ is finite ([[cor-symmetric-group-has-factorial-cardinality-again]]).

[F3] $(\mathbb Z,+,\cdot,0,1)$ is a commutative ring and a totally ordered ring: the order is total and compatible with addition, so $x\le y$ implies $x+z\le y+z$, and exactly one of $x<y$, $x=y$, $y<x$ holds for all integers ([[thm-int-comm-ring]], [[thm-int-ordered-ring]]).

[F4] $(\mathbb Z/2,+,[0]_2)$ is an abelian group with $[a]_2+[b]_2=[a+b]_2$ ([[thm-integers-modulo-n-basic-algebra]]). Its exactly two classes are $[0]_2$ and $[1]_2$ ([[thm-standard-representatives-modulo-n]], [[def-integers-modulo-n]]), so $[1]_2\ne[0]_2$ and $[1]_2+[1]_2=[2]_2=[0]_2$; hence it has order $2$ and is generated by $[1]_2$.

[F5] For any groups $G,H$ the componentwise operation $(g,h)(g',h')=(gg',hh')$ makes $G\times H$ a group with identity $(e_G,e_H)$, and the coordinate projections are group homomorphisms ([[def-external-direct-product-of-groups]], [[thm-external-direct-product-is-a-group]]).

[F6] In a group, $g^0=e$ and $g^{k+1}=g^k\cdot g$ for natural powers, and $g$ has finite order $k\ge1$ exactly when $k$ is the least positive integer with $g^k=e$ ([[def-group-power]], [[def-order-in-a-group]]).

## Counterexample

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **The two factors.** By [F3] the additive group $(\mathbb Z,+,0)$ is a group, and by [F4] $(\mathbb Z/2,+)$ is an abelian group of order $2$ with $[1]_2\ne[0]_2$ and $[1]_2+[1]_2=[2]_2=[0]_2$. [F3, F4]

1.2 **$\mathbb Z$ is torsion-free.** Let $a\in\mathbb Z$ with $a\ne0$ and let $m\ge1$. By trichotomy [F3] either $a>0$ or $-a>0$; assume $a>0$, the other case being symmetric with $m(-a)=-(ma)$. Since $0<a$ and the order is compatible with addition, $a<a+a=2a$ and inductively $ka<(k+1)a$ for every $k\ge1$: if $ka>0$ then $(k+1)a=ka+a>ka>0$. Hence $ma>0$ for every $m\ge1$, so $ma\ne0$; an element $a$ of finite additive order would have $ma=0$ for some $m\ge1$, so no nonzero element of $\mathbb Z$ has finite order and $(\mathbb Z,+,0)$ is torsion-free. [F3, F6]

2.1 **The sequence $1\to\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z\times(\mathbb Z/2)\to\mathbb Z/2\to1$.** By [F5] the set $G:=\mathbb Z\times(\mathbb Z/2)$ with componentwise addition is a group with identity $e_G=(0,[0]_2)$, and the second-coordinate projection $\pi:G\to\mathbb Z/2$ is a group homomorphism; it is surjective because $\pi(a,[b]_2)=[b]_2$ for every $[b]_2$. Its kernel is $K:=\{(a,[0]_2):a\in\mathbb Z\}$, and the first-coordinate map $K\to\mathbb Z$, $(a,[0]_2)\mapsto a$, is a group isomorphism; hence $K$ is torsion-free by step 1.2, and $G/K\cong\mathbb Z/2$ has order $2$ by [F4], so the quotient is finite. Thus $1\to K\to G\xrightarrow{\pi}\mathbb Z/2\to1$ is a short exact sequence with torsion-free kernel and finite quotient. [step 1.2, F4, F5]

3.1 **The middle group has an element of order two.** Put $g:=(0,[1]_2)\in G$. Using the componentwise operation of [F5] and the addition of [F4], $g^2=(0+0,\,[1]_2+[1]_2)=(0,[2]_2)=(0,[0]_2)=e_G$, while $g\ne e_G$ because $[1]_2\ne[0]_2$. By [F6] the element $g$ has order $2$, so $G$ is not torsion-free although $K$ is torsion-free and $G/K$ is finite. This refutes the general implication of the statement: a torsion-free kernel and a finite quotient do not force the middle group to be torsion-free. [step 2.1, F4, F5, F6]

4.1 **The braid reading.** Under the standing assumption [A1], which discharges the Axiom-of-Choice hypothesis that [F1] attaches to its own citation, the configuration braid sequence of [F2] has the two abstract features used above: its kernel $PB_n$ is torsion-free by [F1], and its quotient $S_n$ is finite by [F2]. Since the general implication fails already for the infinite cyclic kernel $\mathbb Z$ and the two-element quotient $\mathbb Z/2$ of step 2.1, those two features alone cannot establish torsion-freeness of $B_n^{\mathrm{conf}}$; only a finer argument about the specific groups can do that. This counterexample makes no claim that $B_n^{\mathrm{conf}}$ itself has torsion. [A1, step 2.1, step 3.1, F1, F2]

∎
