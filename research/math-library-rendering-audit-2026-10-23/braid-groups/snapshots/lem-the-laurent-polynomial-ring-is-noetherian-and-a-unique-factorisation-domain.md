## Statement

The Laurent polynomial ring $\Lambda=\mathbb Z[t^{\pm1}]$ of
[[def-the-laurent-polynomial-ring]] is Noetherian, hence every ideal of
$\Lambda$ is finitely generated, and $\Lambda$ is a unique factorisation
domain; its units are $\pm t^k$, $k\in\mathbb Z$, and $t$ is prime in $\mathbb Z[t]$ but becomes a unit in $\Lambda$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The ring $\Lambda=\mathbb Z[t^{\pm1}]$, described in [[def-the-laurent-polynomial-ring]] as the principal localisation $\mathbb Z[t]_S$ at $S=\{t^k:k\ge0\}$. No choice principle is used.

[F1] $\Lambda$ is commutative with unit, $t$ is a unit with inverse $t^{-1}$, and every element has a finite representative $\sum_k a_kt^k$; the localisation map $\mathbb Z[t]\to\Lambda$ is a unital ring homomorphism ([[def-the-laurent-polynomial-ring]], [[def-multiplicative-subset-and-localisation]], [[def-principal-localisation]]); the units of $\Lambda$ are exactly $\pm t^k$ ([[lem-units-and-powers-of-the-laurent-polynomial-ring]]).

[F2] $\mathbb Z$ is a Noetherian ring and the polynomial ring in finitely many variables over a Noetherian ring is Noetherian; in particular $\mathbb Z[t]$ is Noetherian ([[def-noetherian-ring]], [[cor-finite-variable-polynomial-ring-noetherian]], [[def-polynomial-ring-over-a-commutative-ring]]). Quotients and localisations of Noetherian rings are Noetherian ([[thm-noetherian-ring-quotients-and-localisations]]).

[F3] $\mathbb Z$ is a UFD ([[thm-fundamental-theorem-of-arithmetic]], [[def-unique-factorisation-domain]]). Gauss' lemma says that products of primitive polynomials are primitive, and that a primitive positive-degree integer polynomial is irreducible over $\mathbb Z$ exactly when irreducible over $\mathbb Q$ ([[lem-gauss-lemma-over-a-ufd]]). The polynomial ring $\mathbb Q[t]$ is a UFD ([[thm-polynomial-ring-over-a-field-is-a-ufd]]). The integer-polynomial UFD assertion needed below is derived from these claims, not quoted as a stronger Gauss-lemma statement.

[F4] For a multiplicative subset the localisation $A_S$ has the universal property of [[def-principal-localisation]] and [[def-multiplicative-subset-and-localisation]]: ring homomorphisms from $A_S$ correspond to ring homomorphisms from $A$ sending $S$ to units, and the image of $s\in S$ is a unit of $A_S$.

## Proof


1.1 **Noetherian.** Every nonzero ideal of $\mathbb Z$ is generated by its least positive element, by integer division, and the zero ideal is generated by $0$; thus $\mathbb Z$ is Noetherian. By [F2] the polynomial ring $\mathbb Z[t]$ is Noetherian, and $\Lambda=\mathbb Z[t]_S$ is a localisation of it; localisations of Noetherian rings are Noetherian by [F2]. Hence every ideal of $\Lambda$ is finitely generated. In $\mathbb Z[t]$, if $t$ divides $fg$, evaluation at zero gives $f(0)g(0)=0$ in the domain $\mathbb Z$, so one constant term vanishes and $t$ divides that factor. Since $t$ is nonzero and a nonunit there, it is prime; localization then makes it a unit by [F1]. [F1, F2, F3]

1.2 *The integer-polynomial UFD.* Write any nonzero integer polynomial as its integer content times a primitive polynomial. Factor the latter over $\mathbb Q[t]$ by [F3], and clear denominators and contents in each nonconstant factor to obtain primitive integer factors. Their product is primitive by Gauss' lemma. Two primitive integer polynomials related by a nonzero rational scalar differ only by sign: a reduced denominator would divide every coefficient of the first, and the scalar's numerator would divide every coefficient of the second. Hence the primitive polynomial is, up to sign, the product of these primitive factors, which are irreducible over $\mathbb Z$ by Gauss' lemma. Integer prime factors of the content complete the factorization. Uniqueness follows by comparing integer contents using integer factorization, then comparing the remaining factors in the UFD $\mathbb Q[t]$; primitive rational associates are integer associates by the same scalar argument. Thus $\mathbb Z[t]$ is a UFD, and its irreducibles are prime. [F3, algebra]

2.1 **Surviving irreducibles.** An irreducible $p\in\mathbb Z[t]$ becomes a unit in $\Lambda$ exactly when it divides a power of $t$: a relation $(p/1)(a/t^N)=1$ is equivalent, by injectivity of the localisation map, to $pa=t^N$, and the converse gives an inverse. Suppose $p$ remains a nonunit and $p=xy$ in $\Lambda$. Write $x=a/t^N$ and $y=b/t^M$. Then $pt^{N+M}=ab$ in $\mathbb Z[t]$. The UFD property of step 1.2 makes $p$ prime, so, after interchanging $x,y$, write $a=pa'$. Cancellation gives $a'b=t^{N+M}$, hence $y(a'/t^N)=1$. Thus $p$ is irreducible in $\Lambda$. [F1, F4, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 **Existence and uniqueness.** Every nonzero nonunit $x\in\Lambda$ has the form $a/t^N$ with $0\ne a\in\mathbb Z[t]$. Factor $a$ using step 1.2 and absorb all factors associated to $t$ into a Laurent unit. Step 2.1 shows that all remaining factors are irreducible in $\Lambda$, giving existence. In particular every irreducible of $\Lambda$ is associate there to one of these surviving integer-polynomial primes: its factorization can contain only one nonunit factor. To compare two Laurent factorizations, replace their factors by these integer-polynomial primes and absorb their Laurent units, which are $\pm t^k$ by [F1]. Clearing powers of $t$ gives equality in $\mathbb Z[t]$. Uniqueness there matches all primes not associated to $t$ on the two sides, hence matches the original Laurent factors up to permutation and Laurent units. Together with step 1.1, this proves the statement. [F1, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
