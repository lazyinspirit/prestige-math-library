## Statement

Fix $m\ge1$ and let $A_m$ be the Khovanov–Seidel type A algebra with vertex
projectives $P_i=A_me_i$, $0\le i\le m$
([[def-graded-khovanov-seidel-module-category-and-projectives]],
[[def-vertex-khovanov-seidel-modules]]). Every finitely generated graded
projective left $A_m$-module $P$ is isomorphic, as a graded module, to a finite
direct sum
$$P\cong\bigoplus_{i=0}^{m}\ \bigoplus_{r\in\mathbb Z}P_i\{r\}^{\oplus a_{i,r}}$$
of internal shifts of the vertex projectives, with multiplicities
$a_{i,r}\in\mathbb N$ zero for all but finitely many pairs $(i,r)$; the
multiplicities are uniquely determined by $P$. Equivalently, the shifted modules $P_i\{r\}$ are the indecomposable objects,
and their classes $[P_i\{r\}]$, $0\le i\le m$, $r\in\mathbb Z$, form a free
abelian basis of the split Grothendieck group of finite graded projectives,
with no relations among distinct pairs.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** An integer $m\ge1$, the algebra $A_m$ with vertex idempotents $e_i$, its path ideal $J$ generated by the arrows, the vertex projectives $P_i=A_me_i$ with internal shift $\{r\}$, and a finitely generated graded projective left $A_m$-module $P$.

[L1] $J$ is homogeneous, $J^3=0$, $J$ is spanned by the paths of length at least one, and the quotient satisfies $A_m/J\cong\mathbb Z^{m+1}$ with the classes of the vertex idempotents as basis ([[def-khovanov-seidel-path-ideal]]).

[L2] A graded left $A_m$-module is finite graded projective exactly when it is a degree-zero direct summand of a finite direct sum of internal shifts of the regular module, $P\oplus Q\cong A_m\{r_1\}\oplus\cdots\oplus A_m\{r_n\}$, with degree-zero inclusion and projection ([[thm-finite-graded-projectives-are-summands-of-finite-graded-free-modules]]).

[L3] Every submodule of a finite free $\mathbb Z$-module is free, hence torsion-free and finitely generated if the ambient module is; a graded subgroup of a graded free abelian group that is a direct summand is again a graded free abelian group, and it has a homogeneous $\mathbb Z$-basis ([[cor-submodules-of-finite-free-pid-modules-are-free]]).

[L4] $A_m=\bigoplus_{i=0}^mP_i$ as a left module, $P_i\{r\}$ is the shift with $(P_i\{r\})_d=(P_i)_{d-r}$, the shift is an automorphism of the category, and a direct summand of a projective object is projective; a direct summand of a finitely generated module is finitely generated ([[def-graded-khovanov-seidel-module-category-and-projectives]], [[thm-a-direct-summand-of-a-projective-is-projective]]).

[L5] $A_m$ has the $\mathbb Z$-basis of $4m+1$ classes of vertices, arrows and returns, so $A_m/J$ kills exactly the non-vertex basis paths and the vertices $e_0,\dots,e_m$ are pairwise orthogonal idempotents with sum $1$ ([[lem-the-khovanov-seidel-algebra-has-the-four-m-plus-one-path-basis]], [[def-khovanov-seidel-path-ideal]]).





## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 *$P/JP$ is a finite free graded abelian group.* By [L2] there is a degree-zero isomorphism $P\oplus Q\cong F:=A_m\{r_1\}\oplus\cdots\oplus A_m\{r_n\}$ for some finitely generated graded projective $Q$. Applying $(A_m/J)\otimes_{A_m}-=\mathbb Z^{m+1}\otimes_{A_m}-$ degreewise gives a degree-zero isomorphism of graded $\mathbb Z^{m+1}$-modules $P/JP\oplus Q/JQ\cong F/JF\cong(\mathbb Z^{m+1})\{r_1\}\oplus\cdots\oplus(\mathbb Z^{m+1})\{r_n\}$; in particular $P/JP$ is a direct summand, as a graded abelian group, of the finite free graded abelian group $F/JF$ whose homogeneous components are finite free $\mathbb Z$-modules. By [L3] each graded component $(P/JP)_d$ is a finitely generated torsion-free, hence free, $\mathbb Z$-module, and $P/JP$ has a homogeneous $\mathbb Z$-basis. [L1, L2, L3]

2.1 *The vertex decomposition of $P/JP$.* Since $J$ acts as $0$ on $P/JP$, the latter is a graded module over $A_m/J\cong\bigoplus_i\mathbb Ze_i$: the projections $e_i$ are orthogonal idempotents with sum $1$ [L5], so as a graded abelian group $P/JP=\bigoplus_i e_i(P/JP)$, and a homogeneous basis of $P/JP$ is the disjoint union of homogeneous bases of the graded free abelian groups $e_i(P/JP)$. Write $a_{i,r}$ for the rank of the degree-$r$ part $(e_i(P/JP))_r$ of the $i$-th summand; the $a_{i,r}$ are the multiplicities of the Cartesian basis of [L3] and are zero for all but finitely many $(i,r)$. [step 1.1, L1, L5]

3.1 *A surjection from the proposed direct sum.* Choose, for every $i$ and $r$, a homogeneous $\mathbb Z$-basis $\{[x_{i,r,s}]\}_{s=1}^{a_{i,r}}$ of the degree-$r$ part of $e_i(P/JP)$ and lift each $x_{i,r,s}$ to a homogeneous element $\widehat x_{i,r,s}\in P$ of degree $r$ that lies in $e_iP$; since $P=\bigoplus_ie_iP$ this is possible component by component. The lifts assemble into a degree-zero $A_m$-linear map $\varphi\colon F':=\bigoplus_{i,r}P_i\{r\}^{\oplus a_{i,r}}\to P$ with $\varphi(e_i\otimes 1_{r,s})=\widehat x_{i,r,s}$, the map on a summand being $ae_i\mapsto a\widehat x_{i,r,s}$. This is well defined because $\widehat x_{i,r,s}=e_i\widehat x_{i,r,s}$, so $ae_i=0$ implies $a\widehat x_{i,r,s}=0$. Reducing modulo $J$ gives the isomorphism $F'/JF'\to P/JP$ determined by the chosen bases, because $P_i/JP_i\cong\mathbb Z$ is concentrated at the vertex $i$ and $J$ kills the generators; hence $\varphi$ is surjective by the nilpotent Nakayama argument: if $C:=\operatorname{coker}\varphi$ then $C=JC$, so $C=J^3C=0$ because $J^3=0$. [step 2.1, L1, L4, L5]

3.2 *Uniqueness of the multiplicities.* Suppose $\bigoplus_{i,r}P_i\{r\}^{a_{i,r}}\cong\bigoplus_{i,r}P_i\{r\}^{b_{i,r}}$, and reduce modulo $J$: since $P_i/JP_i\cong\mathbb Z$ placed at internal degree $0$ with the vertex $i$ acting by $1$, an isomorphism of the direct sums induces, for every $i$ and $r$, an isomorphism of graded abelian groups between the degree-$r$ parts of the $i$-th vertex components, which are the free abelian groups of ranks $a_{i,r}$ and $b_{i,r}$; hence $a_{i,r}=b_{i,r}$. [step 2.1, L1, L5]

4.1 *The surjection splits, and is an isomorphism.* The module $P$ is projective, so the surjection $\varphi$ splits: there is a degree-zero $A_m$-linear $\psi:P\to F'$ with $\varphi\psi=\mathrm{id}_P$, and then $F'\cong P\oplus K$ with $K:=\ker\varphi$ a direct summand of $F'$, hence finitely generated graded projective by [L4]. Applying $(A_m/J)\otimes_{A_m}-$ to $F'\cong P\oplus K$ and comparing with the isomorphism $F'/JF'\cong P/JP$ of step 3.1 gives $K/JK=0$, so $K=JK=J^2K=J^3K=0$; hence $\varphi$ is injective and therefore an isomorphism $F'\cong P$. [step 3.1, L1, L4]

5.1 *Conclusion.* Every finitely generated graded projective $P$ is isomorphic to $\bigoplus_{i,r}P_i\{r\}^{\oplus a_{i,r}}$ by step 4.1, the multiplicities are finite in number by step 2.1 and unique by step 3.2. Equivalently, the comparison with the split Grothendieck group sends the class of such a sum to the finite sum $\sum a_{i,r}[P_i\{r\}]$, so the classes $[P_i\{r\}]$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb Z$ by step 3.2, and each $P_i$ is indecomposable: for $i=0$ its endomorphism ring is $e_0A_me_0=\mathbb Z e_0$, whose only idempotents are $0,1$. For $1\le i\le m$ the corner is $e_iA_me_i=\mathbb Ze_i\oplus\mathbb Z(i|i-1|i)$ with $(i|i-1|i)^2=0$, an idempotent $a+br$ satisfies $a^2=a$ and $b(2a-1)=0$ in $\mathbb Z$, so only $a=b=0$ and $a=1,b=0$ occur, and a decomposition $P_i=X\oplus Y$ would give a nontrivial idempotent. No choice principle is used: all bases are finite and chosen explicitly from the finitely many graded pieces. [step 4.1, step 3.2] ∎
