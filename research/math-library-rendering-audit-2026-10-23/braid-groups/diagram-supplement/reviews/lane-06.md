## Diagram conventions to preserve

- The source defines `sigma_k` by strands `m_k + rho` and `m_k - rho`, where `rho(1/2) = (0,-h)`. Thus the strand labelled `k` takes the negative-transverse side at the crossing and `k+1` the positive-transverse side in the source's plane coordinates. A drawing should label its transverse viewing direction or state this convention explicitly, since over/under appearance depends on the projection.
- In `gamma star beta`, `beta` occupies the lower half of the height interval and `gamma` the upper half. Read a product diagram bottom-to-top from its rightmost factor. Therefore the left panel is bottom-to-top `sigma_i`, `sigma_{i+1}`, `sigma_i`; the right is bottom-to-top `sigma_{i+1}`, `sigma_i`, `sigma_{i+1}`.
- For either three-factor word, the endpoint permutation is `(i i+2)`: labels `i` and `i+2` exchange, `i+1` stays put, and all other strands stay fixed. Both diagrams have the same labelled bottom and top endpoints. The endpoint set is fixed, but the outer strand labels are not pointwise fixed at the top.
- The source's common comparison braid is the rotation of the triple by angle `pi*u` about `q_{i+1}`, ending with the same outer-label exchange. The reflection about the middle point together with relabelling the outer strands identifies the opposite bracketing with this rotation. Any schematic should not imply that the picture alone proves the source's collision-free interpolation.
