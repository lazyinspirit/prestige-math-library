## Statement

**False claim.** If two probability measures agree on any family that generates the sigma-algebra, then they agree everywhere. The valid theorem [[thm-measure-uniqueness-on-a-sigma-finite-pi-system]] requires the generating family to be a pi-system.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The set $X=\{NW,NE,SW,SE\}$ and the family $\mathcal G$ of its north row, south row, west column, and east column.

[L1] A sigma-algebra generated by $\mathcal G$ is the smallest sigma-algebra containing $\mathcal G$ ([[def-generated-sigma-algebra]]).

[L2] A probability measure is a measure of total mass $1$ ([[def-probability-measure]]).

[L3] A pi-system is nonempty and closed under binary intersections ([[def-pi-system]]), and agreement on a generating pi-system with the stated finite exhaustion determines a measure ([[thm-measure-uniqueness-on-a-sigma-finite-pi-system]]).

## Refutation

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Define $\mu$ by masses $1/2$ at $NW,SE$ and $0$ at the other points, and define $\nu$ by masses $1/2$ at $NE,SW$ and $0$ at the other points. Finite summation over subsets makes both probability measures on $\mathcal P(X)$. [given, L2]

1.2 Intersections of a row and a column give every singleton, so $\sigma_X(\mathcal G)=\mathcal P(X)$; but those singleton intersections are not themselves in $\mathcal G$, so $\mathcal G$ is not a pi-system. [given, L1, L3]

2.1 Each row and each column contains one point of mass $1/2$ for each measure, so $\mu$ and $\nu$ agree on every member of $\mathcal G$. [given, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The measures differ, for example $\mu(\{NW\})=1/2$ while $\nu(\{NW\})=0$. Together with steps 2.1 and 1.2 this refutes the claim and identifies the missing intersection-closure hypothesis. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 1.2, L3] ∎
