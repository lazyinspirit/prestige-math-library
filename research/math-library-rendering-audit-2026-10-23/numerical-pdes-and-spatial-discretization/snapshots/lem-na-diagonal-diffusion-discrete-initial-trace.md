## Statement

Let $u_0$ be continuous and periodic, and use the explicit diagonal-diffusion
scheme of [[alg-na-explicit-diagonal-diffusion-hjb-grid-step]] with mesh
widths $h_{k,j}\to0$, time steps $\tau_k\to0$, and its diffusion-dominance
and CFL inequalities at every row. Then for every sequence of integers
$n_k\ge0$ with $t_k=n_k\tau_k\to0$ and $t_k$ within the computed horizon,
$$\sup_i|U_i^{n_k}-u_0(x_i)|\longrightarrow0.$$
The estimate is uniform over all finite control choices generated by the
maximization in the scheme.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The grid scheme, bounded coefficients of
[[def-na-periodic-diagonal-controlled-diffusion-hjb-problem]], continuous
periodic initial data, and row conditions from the statement. For any finite
transition tree, expectation means the finite sum of endpoint values weighted
by products of row coefficients.

[F1] The update expands using the central second difference and
sign-selected one-sided drift; its row conditions are
$|b_j^a|h_j\le\alpha_j^a$ and
$\tau_k\sum_j(2\alpha_j^a/h_j^2+|b_j^a|/h_j)\le1$
([[alg-na-explicit-diagonal-diffusion-hjb-grid-step]]).

[F2] There are finite constants $B$, $F$, and $A_*$ with
$|b^a(x,t)|\le B$, $|f^a(x,t)|\le F$, and
$\alpha_j^a(x,t)\le A_*$ for every control and point; $u_0$ is continuous
and periodic ([[def-na-periodic-diagonal-controlled-diffusion-hjb-problem]]).

[F3] The periodic lift of $u_0$ is uniformly continuous on every compact
cube; finitely many adjacent periodic lifts therefore share a modulus
$\omega_0(\delta)\to0$
([[thm-heine-borel-rn]], [[thm-heine-cantor-metric]],
[[def-na-periodic-grid-and-tensor-multilinear-interpolation]]).

[F4] The nodes and coordinate neighbors use the periodic Cartesian convention
of [[def-na-periodic-grid-and-tensor-multilinear-interpolation]].

## Proof

1.1 Fix one control row and abbreviate $\alpha=\alpha_j^a$, $b=b_j^a$, $h=h_j$. If $b\ge0$, the two neighbor transition weights in coordinate $j$ are $\tau(\alpha/h^2-b/h)$ at $x_i-h e_j$ and $\tau\alpha/h^2$ at $x_i+h e_j$. If $b<0$, they are $\tau\alpha/h^2$ at $x_i-h e_j$ and $\tau(\alpha/h^2-|b|/h)$ at $x_i+h e_j$. Their sum is $\tau(2\alpha/h^2-|b|/h)$ and their signed first moment is $\tau b$. The diffusion-dominance inequality in [F1] makes both neighbor coefficients nonnegative. The center coefficient is $1-\tau\sum_j(2\alpha_j^a/h_j^2-|b_j^a|/h_j)$, which is nonnegative by the CFL inequality in [F1]. Including the center, the row weights sum to one. Summing over coordinates gives mean displacement $\tau b^a(x_i,t_n)$. The raw squared displacement is at most $$\tau\sum_j\left(2\alpha_j^a(x_i,t_n)-|b_j^a(x_i,t_n)|h_j\right) \le2\tau dA_*.$$ Subtracting the squared mean cannot increase the second moment. [F1, F2, F4, given, algebra]

2.1 Unroll $n_k$ updates as a finite transition tree. At a node of the tree, the maximum in the scheme chooses one of finitely many control rows; that row is a finite probability distribution by step 1.1. The terminal value is thus a maximum of finite weighted averages of $u_0(X_{n_k})$ plus accumulated rewards. On every branch the sum of the drift means has magnitude at most $Bt_k$. The centered increments have conditional weighted mean zero. For two different times, the weighted cross term vanishes by first summing the later centered increment over all branches extending a fixed earlier path prefix. Expanding the square and using step 1.1 gives, uniformly over all finite policies, $$\mathbb E|X_{n_k}-x_i|^2\le2B^2t_k^2+4dA_*t_k.$$ The accumulated reward has magnitude at most $Ft_k$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Put $M_0=\|u_0\|_\infty$. For any $0<\delta<1$, split each finite endpoint average according to $|X_{n_k}-x_i|\le\delta$. On that part the change in $u_0$ is at most $\omega_0(\delta)$ by [F3]; on the complement it is at most $2M_0$. The complement weight is at most $\mathbb E|X_{n_k}-x_i|^2/\delta^2$. Therefore every policy average differs from $u_0(x_i)$ by at most $$\omega_0(\delta)+\frac{2M_0}{\delta^2} \left(2B^2t_k^2+4dA_*t_k\right)+Ft_k.$$ This bound is independent of $i$ and of the policy; taking a finite maximum preserves it. First let $k\to\infty$ and then $\delta\downarrow0$ to obtain the claimed uniform initial trace. [F2, F3, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
