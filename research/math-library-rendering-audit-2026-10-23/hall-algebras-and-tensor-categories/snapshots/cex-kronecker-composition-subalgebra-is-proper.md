## Statement

For the two-arrow Kronecker quiver, the degree $(1,1)$ Hall component has
$q+2$ basis classes, whereas its simple-generated composition subalgebra has
dimension at most two in that degree. Thus the full Hall algebra is strictly
larger than the composition subalgebra.

```tikzcd
1 \arrow[r, shift left=0.7ex, "a"]
  \arrow[r, shift right=0.7ex, swap, "b"] & 2
```

The diagram denotes two distinct parallel arrows from vertex $1$ to vertex
$2$. A representation assigns a scalar to each arrow when both vertex spaces
are one-dimensional.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $Q$ is the quiver displayed above over $\mathbb F_q$.

[F1] Its finite-dimensional representation category is an acyclic
finite-field hereditary Hall category, and dimension vector grades its Hall
algebra ([[lem-finite-acyclic-quiver-hall-category-is-hereditary-finitary]]).

[F2] The composition subalgebra in this example means the subalgebra of the
twisted Hall algebra generated by the vertex-simple classes.

[F3] The underlying untwisted Hall product is associative
([[thm-untwisted-hall-algebra-is-associative-and-unital]]).

[F4] Every finite field has cardinality $q\ge2$.

## Proof



**Proof technique:** classify the degree component.

1.1 The acyclic Euler form is $\chi(\alpha,\beta)=\alpha_1\beta_1+\alpha_2\beta_2-2\alpha_1\beta_2$, which is bilinear by [F1]. Define this example’s product by $[M]\star[N]=\nu^{\chi(\deg M,\deg N)}[M]*[N]$, where $*$ is the untwisted Hall product of [F3]. This twist preserves associativity, since $\chi(\alpha,\beta)+\chi(\alpha+\beta,\gamma)= \chi(\beta,\gamma)+\chi(\alpha,\beta+\gamma)$. Thus the composition subalgebra is defined for this quiver. [F1, F3, algebra]

1.2 A representation of dimension $(1,1)$ is a pair $(x,y)\in\mathbb F_q^2$ of arrow scalars. An isomorphism is a pair of nonzero endpoint scalars $(g_1,g_2)$, acting by common multiplication by $g_2/g_1$. The zero pair is one orbit and gives $S_1\oplus S_2$. Each nonzero orbit is a line in $\mathbb F_q^2$, giving $q+1$ further orbits. Thus the full Hall component has $q+2$ independent isomorphism-class basis vectors. [F1, given, algebra]

2.1 A product of simples of total degree $(1,1)$ must use $S_1$ and $S_2$ once each. There are only two possible words, $[S_1]\star[S_2]$ and $[S_2]\star[S_1]$, so the degree component generated by simples has dimension at most two. [F2, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Since $q\ge2$ by [F4], $q+2>2$. The two subspaces have different dimensions, proving strict containment. [step 1.2, step 2.1, F4, algebra] ∎
