## Definition

Let $(X,\mathcal U)$ be a uniform space. Give $[0,1]$ the subspace metric $d_{[0,1]}(s,t):=|s-t|$ obtained by restricting the usual real metric of [[lem-real-line-is-a-metric-space]], as licensed by [[def-isometry-and-metric-embedding]], and equip it with the uniformity generated by that metric ([[lem-metric-uniformity-dictionary]]). Let $\mathcal F_{\mathcal U}$ be the family of all uniformly continuous maps $f:X\to[0,1]$ ([[def-uniformly-continuous-map]]). For $f\in\mathcal F_{\mathcal U}$ put
$$ p_f(x,y):=|f(x)-f(y)|. $$
The **Samuel uniformity** $\mathcal U_S$ is the uniformity generated by the gauge $(p_f)_{f\in\mathcal F_{\mathcal U}}$ in the sense of [[def-gauge-of-pseudometrics]]. Thus a base consists of the sets
$$ E(F,\varepsilon)=\{(x,y):p_f(x,y)<\varepsilon\text{ for every }f\in F\}, $$
where $F\subseteq\mathcal F_{\mathcal U}$ is finite and $\varepsilon>0$.

The well-definedness of this gauge and its relation to $\mathcal U$ are proved in [[lem-samuel-function-pseudometrics-and-coarsening]]. The use of $[0,1]$ rather than arbitrary bounded real-valued functions is equivalent by affine rescaling, also proved there.
