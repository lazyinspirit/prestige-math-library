## Statement

Let $A$ be a commutative ring, let $I\subseteq A$ be an ideal and let
$a\in I$. Write $R(I)=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}I^nt^n\subseteq A[t]$ for the Rees
algebra of $I$, graded by the degree of $t$
([[def-rees-algebra-and-rees-module]],
[[def-graded-ring-and-graded-module]]). The **affine blowup algebra** is

$$A[I/a]:=\bigl(R(I)\bigr)_{(a)},$$

the degree-zero part of the localization of $R(I)$ at the multiplicative set
generated by the degree-one element $at\in It$
([[def-multiplicative-subset-and-localisation]]). Then:

1. the image of $a$ in $A[I/a]$ is a nonzerodivisor,
   $IA[I/a]=aA[I/a]$, and $\bigl(A[I/a]\bigr)_a=A_a$;
2. if $I=(a_0,\dots,a_r)$ with $a=a_0$
   ([[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]), the homomorphism
   $$A[x_1,\dots,x_r]/(ax_i-a_i)\longrightarrow A[I/a]$$ sending
   $x_i\mapsto a_i/a$ is surjective with kernel the $a$-power torsion;
3. if $A$ is reduced then $A[I/a]$ is reduced; if $A$ is a domain and
   $a\neq0$ then $A[I/a]$ is a domain;
4. the construction is independent of the generating set and of the
   representative used for the homogeneous localization, up to canonical
   $A$-algebra isomorphism.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A commutative ring $A$, an ideal $I\subseteq A$ and an element $a\in I$; the Rees algebra $R(I)=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}I^nt^n\subseteq A[t]$ of ([[def-rees-algebra-and-rees-module]]), localized at the multiplicative set generated by the degree-one element $at$ ([[def-multiplicative-subset-and-localisation]]), with grades read in the sense of ([[def-graded-ring-and-graded-module]]).

[F1] [[def-rees-algebra-and-rees-module]]: For an ideal $I$ of a commutative ring $A$, the Rees algebra is the graded subring $R(I)=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}I^nt^n\subseteq A[t]$, whose degree-$n$ piece is $I^n$ (so $R(I)_n=I^nt^n$), with $I^0=A$.

[F2] [[def-multiplicative-subset-and-localisation]]: For a commutative ring $R$ and a multiplicative subset $S\subseteq R$, the localization $S^{-1}R$ has elements written $r/s$ with $(r,s)\sim(r',s')$ iff $u(rs'-r's)=0$ for some $u\in S$; the operations are $r/s+r'/s'=(rs'+r's)/(ss')$ and $(r/s)(r'/s')=rr'/(ss')$; every $s\in S$ maps to a unit; if $0\in S$ the localization is the zero ring.

[F3] [[def-graded-ring-and-graded-module]]: A nonnegatively graded ring is a commutative ring $S=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}S_n$ with $S_nS_m\subseteq S_{n+m}$; an element of $S_n$ is homogeneous of degree $n$. In particular the degree-zero part of a $\mathbb Z$-graded ring is closed under addition, multiplication and contains the unit class of the localization, and products of homogeneous elements add degrees.

[F4] [[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]: If $I=(a_0,\dots,a_r)$ then every element of $I$ is a finite sum $\sum_ic_ia_i$ with $c_i\in A$, and conversely each $a_i$ lies in $I$.

## Proof

1.1 An element of the localization $R(I)_{at}$ is a class $z/(at)^n$ with $z\in R(I)$ and $n\ge0$; writing $z=\sum_ib_it^i$, its degree-zero component is $b_nt^n/(at)^n$ with $b_n\in I^n$. Hence the degree-zero part $D=(R(I))_{(a)}$ consists of the classes of $b\,t^n/(at)^n$ with $n\ge0$ and $b\in I^n$, so that $A[I/a]=D$; for $b\in I^n$, $c\in I^m$ one has $b\,t^n/(at)^n=c\,t^m/(at)^m$ in $D$ if and only if $a^k(a^mb-a^nc)=0$ for some $k\ge0$, because the defining relation is $(at)^k\bigl((at)^mb\,t^n-(at)^nc\,t^m\bigr)=0$ and $t$ is a nonzerodivisor of $A[t]$. [F1, F2, F3]

2.1 The assignment $\iota\colon D\to A_a$, $b\,t^n/(at)^n\mapsto b/a^n$, is a well-defined injective ring homomorphism: well-defined and injective because $b/a^n=c/a^m$ in $A_a$ holds exactly when $a^k(a^mb-a^nc)=0$ for some $k\ge0$, i.e. exactly under the equality criterion of step 1.1; compatible with addition because $(b\,t^n)/(at)^n+(c\,t^m)/(at)^m=(a^mb+a^nc)t^{n+m}/(at)^{n+m}$ and with multiplication because the product is $bc\,t^{n+m}/(at)^{n+m}$. Its image is the $A$-subalgebra $B=A[b/a:b\in I]\subseteq A_a$ generated by the fractions $b/a$: every $b/a$ is the image of $b\,t/(at)$, and conversely every $b\in I^n$ is a finite sum of products of $n$ elements of $I$, so $b/a^n$ is a sum of products of these generators (with coefficients from $A$). Hence $A[I/a]\cong B$ as $A$-algebras and all computations may be performed in $B\subseteq A_a$. [F1, F2, step 1.1]

3.1 The element $a$ is invertible in $A_a$, hence a nonzerodivisor on $A_a$, hence a nonzerodivisor on the subring $B$; in particular the image of $a$ in $A[I/a]$ is a nonzerodivisor. Moreover $aB\subseteq IB$ because $a\in I$, and $IB\subseteq aB$ because for $b\in I$ and $c/a^n\in B$ with $c\in I^n$ one has $b\cdot c/a^n=a\cdot bc/a^{n+1}$ with $bc\in I^{n+1}$; hence $IA[I/a]=aA[I/a]$. [F1, step 2.1]

3.2 The image of $A$ lies in $B$, since $c=ac/a$ for $c\in A$ and $ac\in I$; thus $A_a=A[1/a]\subseteq B_a$, and the inclusion $B\subseteq A_a$ induces $B_a\subseteq(A_a)_a=A_a$. Hence $B_a=A_a$, that is, $\bigl(A[I/a]\bigr)_a=A_a$. [F2, step 2.1]

3.3 Let $\varphi\colon C=A[x_1,\dots,x_r]/(ax_i-a_i)\to B$ send $x_i$ to $a_i/a$. Its image contains $A$ and every $a_i/a$; since $I$ is generated by the $a_i$, every $b\in I$ is a finite sum $\sum_ic_ia_i$, whence $b/a=\sum_ic_i(a_i/a)$ lies in the image; as the image is an $A$-subalgebra of $A_a$ containing all $b/a$ it contains $B$. So $\varphi$ is surjective. [F4, step 2.1]

3.4 If $A$ is reduced, then so is the localization $A_a$, and $B\subseteq A_a$ is a subring of a reduced ring, hence reduced: if $x^N=0$ in $B$ then $x=0$ already in $A_a$. If $A$ is a domain and $a\neq0$, then $A_a\subseteq\operatorname{Frac}(A)$ is a nonzero subring of a field (nonzero because $a\cdot a^{-1}=1$ with $a\neq0$), hence a domain, and so is its subring $B$. [F2, step 2.1]

4.1 Localizing $C$ at the multiplicative set generated by $a$ gives $C_a=A_a[x_1,\dots,x_r]/(ax_i-a_i)$ in which $a$ is a unit and hence $x_i=a_ia^{-1}$; the $A_a$-algebra homomorphism $C_a\to A_a$ sending $x_i\mapsto a_i/a$ is therefore surjective and has zero kernel, because evaluation at $x_i=a_i/a$ identifies the quotient $A_a[x_i]/(x_i-a_i/a)$ with $A_a$. Since the image of $C$ in $A_a$ is $B$ by step 3.3, $\ker(C\to B)=\ker(C\to C_a)$, and by the defining relation of the localization an element $f\in C$ lies in this kernel exactly when $a^Nf=0$ for some $N\ge0$, i.e. exactly when $f$ is $a$-power torsion. [F2, step 3.3]

5.1 The description $B=\{b/a^n:n\ge0,\ b\in I^n\}$ of step 2.1 uses only the pair $(I,a)$: no generating family is chosen, and a class in $D$ is compared with another by the equality criterion of step 1.1, so the use of a particular fraction representative is immaterial. A finite generating family $I=(a_0,\dots,a_r)$ with $a=a_0$ only produces the presentation $C$ of step 3.3, whose kernel is described in step 4.1; the image $B$ and hence the algebra are the same for every such family, with the identity of $B$ as the canonical isomorphism. Finally, for $a'\in I$ with $a'\neq a$ the algebras $B\subseteq A_a$ and $B'\subseteq A_{a'}$ lie in different localizations of $A$; the statement asserts no identification between them, and the transition maps between the corresponding charts are supplied separately by the standard-chart theorem. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.3, step 4.1] ∎

## Remarks

The vanishing of $A[I/a]$ is allowed: if $a=0$ then $0$ lies in the multiplicative set generated by $at$ and the localization, and hence $A[I/a]$, is the zero ring, consistently with $A_a=0$; all four assertions then hold trivially. The description $B=\{b/a^n\}$ is the normal form used in the chart computations of the blowup.
