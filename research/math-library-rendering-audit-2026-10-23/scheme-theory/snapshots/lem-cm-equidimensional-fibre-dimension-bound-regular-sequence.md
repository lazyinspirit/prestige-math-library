## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $k$ be a field and let $A$
be a finite-type $k$-algebra that is Cohen–Macaulay and
equidimensional of dimension $d$. Let $f_1,\ldots,f_i\in A$.
If $\dim A/(f_1,\ldots,f_i)\le d-i$, then the tuple is a
regular sequence in $A_{\mathfrak q}$ for every prime
$\mathfrak q$ containing the $f_j$. When its common zero set
is empty the conclusion is vacuous. This is the regularity
conclusion of Stacks Lemma 10.129.1.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The field algebra, Cohen–Macaulay and dimension conditions, and the indicated tuple.

[F1] A maximal ideal of a finite-type domain over a field has height equal to that domain's dimension. Consequently, if $A$ is equidimensional of dimension $d$, every maximal localization $A_{\mathfrak n}$ has dimension $d$: choose a minimal prime $\mathfrak a\subseteq\mathfrak n$, lift a length-$d$ chain from $(A/\mathfrak a)_{\mathfrak n}$, and use $\dim A_{\mathfrak n}\le\dim A=d$ ([[cor-affine-domain-maximal-ideal-height-equals-dimension]], [[cor-localisation-dimension-does-not-increase]]).

[F2] Cohen–Macaulayness localizes, and every system of parameters in a Cohen–Macaulay local ring is regular ([[cor-cohen-macaulayness-localises]], [[thm-parameters-and-regular-sequences-in-cohen-macaulay-modules]]).

[F3] A nonzero Noetherian local ring of dimension $e$ has a system of parameters of length $e$. The Krull height theorem says an ideal generated by fewer than the local dimension cannot be maximal-primary ([[thm-dimension-and-parameters-for-modules]], [[thm-krull-height-theorem]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** extend the equations by quotient parameters at a maximal ideal and use the height theorem to show that the resulting list has the full local dimension.

1.1 If $A=0$ or $V(f_1,\ldots,f_i)=\varnothing$, the assertion has no primes to test. Otherwise choose a maximal ideal $\mathfrak n$ containing all the $f_j$ and set $C=A_{\mathfrak n}$ and $J=(f_1,\ldots,f_i)C$. By [F1], $\dim C=d$, and by [F2], $C$ is Cohen–Macaulay. The nonzero local quotient $C/J$ has dimension $e\le\dim A/(f_1,\ldots,f_i)\le d-i$, where the first inequality is localization's dimension bound. [F1, F2]

2.1 Choose a system of parameters $\overline y_1,\ldots,\overline y_e$ for $C/J$ by [F3] and lift it to $y_1,\ldots,y_e\in C$. Then $(f_1,\ldots,f_i,y_1,\ldots,y_e)C$ is maximal-primary. It has $i+e\le d$ generators. The height theorem in [F3] forces $d\le i+e$, so $e=d-i$ and this tuple of $d$ elements is a system of parameters of $C$. By [F2] it is regular, hence its initial segment $f_1,\ldots,f_i$ is regular in $C$. [F2, F3, step 1.1]

3.1 Every prime $\mathfrak q\supseteq(f_1,\ldots,f_i)$ lies in some maximal $\mathfrak n$ of the same kind. The sequence is regular in $A_{\mathfrak n}$ by step 2.1; localizing further to $A_{\mathfrak q}$ preserves injectivity of each multiplication map and the required nonzero successive quotients because the $f_j$ all lie in $\mathfrak qA_{\mathfrak q}$. Thus it is regular at $\mathfrak q$. AC covers the selections of maximal ideals, components and parameters used above. [F2, step 2.1] ∎
