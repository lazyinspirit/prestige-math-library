## Example

Assume the inherited Axiom of Countable Choice $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$. Fix
$k\in\mathbb R$ and $n\ge2$, and let $M^n_k$ be the complete, simply connected
$n$-dimensional space form of constant sectional curvature $k$, with
two-dimensional model $M^2_k$ and distance $d_k$
([[def-constant-sectional-curvature-and-space-form]]). The five comparison
theorems of this pair degenerate to equalities in these models, and the strict
signs of their inequality directions are read off the explicit formulas for
$\operatorname{sn}_k$, $\operatorname{ct}_k$ and the model density:

1. **Equality in the constant-$k$ model.** In $M^n_k$ the normal Jacobi field
   with $J(0)=0$, $D_tJ(0)=E$ is $J(t)=\operatorname{sn}_k(t)P_tE$, so the
   Rauch comparison is an equality; the Hessian and Laplace–Beltrami
   comparison bounds are attained with equality,
   $\operatorname{Hess}r=\operatorname{ct}_k(t_0)(g-dr\otimes dr)$ and
   $\Delta_gr=(n-1)\operatorname{ct}_k(t_0)$; the Bishop–Gromov ratio equals
   $1$; and in $M^2_k$ every admissible hinge has opposite side exactly
   $c_k(a,b,\theta)$ while every admissible triangle is isometric to its own
   comparison triangle, so the hinge and triangle comparisons are equalities.
2. **Strict signs of the flat-versus-curved models.** For fixed admissible
   data and $k_1<k_2$, the model quantities are strictly ordered at every
   time and radius in their common positive domains:
$$\operatorname{sn}_{k_2}(t)<\operatorname{sn}_{k_1}(t),\qquad \operatorname{ct}_{k_2}(t)<\operatorname{ct}_{k_1}(t),\qquad V^\star_{k_2}(r)<V^\star_{k_1}(r),$$
   and the model angle opposite a fixed side is strictly larger,
   $\alpha_{k_2}(a,b,c)>\alpha_{k_1}(a,b,c)$, equivalently the model opposite
   side is strictly shorter, $c_{k_2}(a,b,\theta)<c_{k_1}(a,b,\theta)$ for
   $\theta\in(0,\pi)$. Thus against the flat model $k=0$ the positive model
   lies strictly below it for the Jacobi, Hessian, Laplacian and volume
   quantities and strictly above it for the Toponogov angle, and the negative
   model reverses each strict sign: exactly the directions in which the
   comparison theorems of this pair read "more curvature means shorter Jacobi
   fields, smaller distance Hessian and Laplacian, smaller volume, fatter
   triangles".

The computation is a diagnostic for the direction conventions of
[[thm-rauch-comparison-theorem-first-form]],
[[thm-hessian-comparison-for-distance-under-sectional-curvature-bounds]],
[[thm-laplacian-comparison-for-distance-under-a-ricci-lower-bound]],
[[thm-bishop-gromov-volume-comparison]],
[[thm-toponogov-hinge-comparison]] and
[[thm-toponogov-triangle-comparison]]; it supplies no later proof and is never
a dependency. For strict Jacobi-field inequalities the matched initial derivative $E$
is nonzero; if $E=0$, both fields vanish identically at every curvature.
All approximations, limits and strict inequalities below are
proved, not estimated numerically.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The inherited $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ of [A1]; the curvature $k\in\mathbb R$ and dimension $n\ge2$; the model functions $\operatorname{sn}_k,\operatorname{cs}_k,\operatorname{ct}_k$ of [F1] with their ODE and Wronskian identities [F2]; for [F4] an admissible side datum $(a,b,c)$ in the sense of the comparison-triangle definition, its comparison angle $\theta\in(0,\pi)$ opposite the side $c$ between the sides $a$ and $b$, and the halves $s:=(a+b)/2$, $u:=(a-b)/2$, $v:=c/2$; for the hinge form a unit-speed model hinge with sides $a,b>0$ and included angle $\theta$; a point $p$ in the space form, a point $q$ off $p$ and off its cut locus with $t_0:=d_g(p,q)>0$, and a radius $r>0$.

[A1] The countable-choice premise is the inherited $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ ([[def-countable-choice]]), carried by the comparison suppliers of this page. This example selects no family of objects: the model geodesics, Jacobi fields and comparison triangles it mentions are supplied by the cited definitions and theorems, and every computation below is performed on the explicit formulas.

[F1] Model functions ([[def-comparison-sine-cosine-and-cotangent-functions]]): $\operatorname{sn}_0(t)=t$, $\operatorname{sn}_k(t)=\sin(\sqrt k\,t)/\sqrt k$ for $k>0$ and $\operatorname{sn}_k(t)=\sinh(\sqrt{-k}\,t)/\sqrt{-k}$ for $k<0$; $\operatorname{cs}_k=\operatorname{sn}_k'$ is $1$, $\cos(\sqrt k\,t)$, $\cosh(\sqrt{-k}\,t)$ in the same three cases; $\operatorname{ct}_k=\operatorname{cs}_k/\operatorname{sn}_k$; $\operatorname{sn}_k$ is odd and $\operatorname{cs}_k$ is even. On the positive domain of $\operatorname{sn}_k$, $\operatorname{sn}_k>0$; $\operatorname{ct}_k>0$ for $k\le0$, while for $k>0$ it changes sign at $\pi/(2\sqrt k)$ within $(0,\pi/\sqrt k)$.

[F2] Model ODE and Wronskian ([[prop-model-functions-solve-the-constant-curvature-jacobi-equation]]): $\operatorname{sn}_k''+k\operatorname{sn}_k=0$, $\operatorname{cs}_k''+k\operatorname{cs}_k=0$, $\operatorname{cs}_k^2+k\operatorname{sn}_k^2=1$ on $\mathbb R$, with $\operatorname{sn}_k(0)=0$, $\operatorname{cs}_k(0)=1$, $\operatorname{sn}_k'(0)=1$, $\operatorname{cs}_k'(0)=0$.

[F3] Addition formulas ([[cor-complex-trigonometric-and-hyperbolic-addition-formulas]], [[thm-hyperbolic-identities-and-derivatives]]): the sine, cosine, hyperbolic sine and hyperbolic cosine addition formulas hold; consequently, for all real $x,y$ and all $k$, $$\operatorname{sn}_k(x+y)=\operatorname{sn}_k(x)\operatorname{cs}_k(y)+\operatorname{cs}_k(x)\operatorname{sn}_k(y),\qquad \operatorname{cs}_k(x\pm y)=\operatorname{cs}_k(x)\operatorname{cs}_k(y)\mp k\operatorname{sn}_k(x)\operatorname{sn}_k(y),$$ and $\operatorname{sn}_k(2x)=2\operatorname{sn}_k(x)\operatorname{cs}_k(x)$, $\operatorname{cs}_k(2x)=1-2k\operatorname{sn}_k(x)^2$. Moreover $(\cot x)'=-\csc^2x$ and $(\coth y)'=-\operatorname{csch}^2y$ on their domains ([[thm-tangent-cotangent-secant-cosecant-derivatives-and-periods]], the hyperbolic identities and derivatives theorem).

[F4] Model cosine law and comparison triangle ([[def-comparison-triangle-in-the-two-dimensional-space-form]]): for an admissible side datum $(a,b,c)$ in $M^2_k$ there is a comparison triangle, unique up to isometry of $M^2_k$, and its angle $\theta$ opposite the side $c$ between the sides $a$ and $b$ is determined by the model cosine law, which in the unified notation is $$\cos\theta=\frac{\operatorname{cs}_k(c)-\operatorname{cs}_k(a)\operatorname{cs}_k(b)}{k\operatorname{sn}_k(a)\operatorname{sn}_k(b)}$$ for $k\ne0$ and $\cos\theta=(a^2+b^2-c^2)/(2ab)$ for $k=0$. Admissibility means $a,b,c>0$, the strict triangle inequalities, and when $k>0$ also $a,b,c<\pi/\sqrt k$ and $a+b+c<2\pi/\sqrt k$; the angle lies in $(0,\pi)$.

[F5] Model Jacobi fields and Rauch comparison ([[ex-model-jacobi-fields-in-positive-zero-and-negative-curvature]], [[thm-rauch-comparison-theorem-first-form]]): in a space form of curvature $k$ the normal Jacobi field with $J(0)=0$, $D_tJ(0)=E$ is $J(t)=\operatorname{sn}_k(t)P_tE$; Rauch's theorem compares two manifolds with $\sec_{M_1}\ge\sec_{M_2}$ and gives $|J_1|\le|J_2|$ for matched initial data up to the first conjugate instant of $\gamma_1$.

[F6] Hessian comparison ([[thm-hessian-comparison-for-distance-under-sectional-curvature-bounds]]): at $q$ off $p$ and the cut locus, with $t_0<\pi/\sqrt k$ when $k>0$, the curvature bound $K\ge k$ along the minimizing geodesic gives $\operatorname{Hess}r\le\operatorname{ct}_k(t_0)g$ on the normal hyperplane, and the reverse curvature bound gives the reverse inequality.

[F7] Laplacian comparison ([[thm-laplacian-comparison-for-distance-under-a-ricci-lower-bound]]): $\operatorname{Ric}\ge(n-1)k\,g$ gives $\Delta_gr(q)\le(n-1)\operatorname{ct}_k(t_0)$.

[F8] Space-form equalities ([[ex-distance-hessian-and-laplacian-in-space-forms]]): in the space form of curvature $k$, $\operatorname{Hess}r=\operatorname{ct}_k(t_0)(g-dr\otimes dr)$ on all of $T_qM$ and $\Delta_gr(q)=(n-1)\operatorname{ct}_k(t_0)$; both comparison hypotheses of [F6] and [F7] hold with equality there.

[F9] Bishop–Gromov comparison and model volumes ([[thm-bishop-gromov-volume-comparison]], [[def-model-space-radial-area-and-ball-volume]], [[ex-bishop-gromov-ratio-is-constant-in-the-model-space]]): for $\operatorname{Ric}\ge(n-1)k\,g$ the ratio $R_p(r)=\operatorname{vol}_g(B(p,r))/V^\star_k(r)$ is nonincreasing with $\lim_{r\downarrow0}R_p(r)=1$; the model radial area is $A_k(t)=\omega_{n-1}\operatorname{sn}_k(t)^{n-1}$, the model ball volume is $V_k(r)=\int_0^rA_k$, and in the space form of curvature $k$, $\operatorname{vol}_g(B(p,r))=V^\star_k(r)$ for every $r>0$.

[F10] Toponogov hinge and triangle comparison ([[thm-toponogov-hinge-comparison]], [[thm-toponogov-triangle-comparison]]): $K\ge k$ gives $c\le c_k(a,b,\theta)$ for every admissible minimizing hinge, where $c_k(a,b,\theta)$ is the opposite side of the model hinge in $M^2_k$ determined by the cosine law of [F4]; and every actual angle of an admissible minimizing geodesic triangle is at least its comparison angle.

[F11] Taylor–Peano ([[thm-taylor-peano-remainder]]): a function that is $m$ times differentiable on an open neighborhood of $0$ has its order-$m$ Taylor expansion with remainder $o(x^m)$ there. In particular the smooth sine and hyperbolic sine satisfy $\sin x=x-x^3/6+o(x^3)$ and $\sinh x=x+x^3/6+o(x^3)$.

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct. The comparison coefficients of the five theorems are explicit functions of $k$, so equality in the model and the strict signs are computed from the displayed formulas. The only non-elementary point is the strict monotonicity of the model angle in $k$: differentiating the half-angle form of the model cosine law writes the derivative of the angle as a positive multiple of $\cos^2(\theta/2)G(u)+\sin^2(\theta/2)G(s)-G(v)$, where $G(t)=\operatorname{sn}_k(t)m_k(t)$ with $m_k(t)=\int_0^t x\operatorname{sn}_k(x)\,dx$, and this expression is positive because $t\mapsto G(t)$ is, as a function of $h=\operatorname{sn}_k^{\,2}$, a strictly convex function.

1.1 Elementary scalar estimates. For $x>0$ one has $\sin x<x$: the function $x-\sin x$ vanishes at $0$ and has derivative $1-\cos x\ge0$, which is positive except at multiples of $2\pi$, so it is strictly increasing on $[0,2\pi]$ and then visibly positive; for $x>0$ one has $\sinh x>x$, since $\sinh x-x$ vanishes at $0$ and has derivative $\cosh x-1\ge0$, positive for $x>0$. For $0<x<\pi$ one has $\cot x<1/x$: for $0<x\le\pi/2$ the function $\sin x-x\cos x$ vanishes at $0$ and has derivative $x\sin x>0$, so $\sin x>x\cos x$ and $\cot x=\cos x/\sin x<1/x$; for $\pi/2<x<\pi$ one has $\cot x<0<1/x$. For $y>0$ one has $\coth y>1/y$: $y\cosh y-\sinh y$ vanishes at $0$ and has derivative $y\sinh y>0$, so $y\cosh y>\sinh y$ and $\coth y=\cosh y/\sinh y>1/y$. Also $\cos x\le1$ and $\cosh y\ge1$, with the latter inequality strict for $y>0$. [F3]

1.2 Equality in the constant-$k$ model. The five comparisons are equalities in $M^n_k$ and $M^2_k$: - Jacobi: by [F5] the matched normal Jacobi field of the model is $J(t)=\operatorname{sn}_k(t)P_tE$, so the Rauch bound is attained at every $t$ of the domain. - Hessian and Laplacian: by [F8], $\operatorname{Hess}r$ and $\Delta_gr$ equal the comparison expressions of [F6] and [F7] exactly, and the two curvature hypotheses hold with equality. - Volume: by [F9], $\operatorname{vol}_g(B(p,r))=V^\star_k(r)$, so the Bishop–Gromov ratio is identically $1$. - Toponogov: by [F4] a comparison triangle in $M^2_k$ is unique up to isometry, so the comparison triangle of an admissible model triangle is congruent to the triangle itself and every model angle equals its comparison angle; in the hinge form the model opposite side is by definition $c_k(a,b,\theta)$ [F10], so both comparisons hold with equality. In each case the bounding quantity is exactly the model quantity, so no strictness remains inside a fixed model; strictness can only appear between models of different curvature, which is what steps 2.1, 2.2, 3.1 and 5.1 compute. [F4, F5, F8, F9, F10]

1.3 The primitives $m$, $G$ and $\rho$. For the rest of the computation fix $k$ and write $m(t):=m_k(t)=\int_0^t x\operatorname{sn}_k(x)\,dx$ on the positive domain, $h(t):=\operatorname{sn}_k(t)^2$, $G(t):=\operatorname{sn}_k(t)m(t)=h(t)\rho(t)$ with $\rho(t):=m(t)/\operatorname{sn}_k(t)$, and $\tan_k(t):=\operatorname{sn}_k(t)/\operatorname{cs}_k(t)$. Then $m(0)=0$, $m'=t\operatorname{sn}_k(t)$, $m$ is odd and $m>0$ on $(0,D_k)$; $h$ is even and strictly increasing on $(0,D_k/2)$ (for $k>0$ its restriction is $\sin^2(\sqrt k\,t)/k$, for $k\le0$ it is $t^2$ or $\sinh^2(\sqrt{-k}\,t)/(-k)$), with $h(t)>0$ for $0<t<D_k$; $G$ is even and $G>0$ on $(0,D_k)$; and on $(0,D_k/2)$ one has $\operatorname{cs}_k>0$, hence $\tan_k>0$. [F1]

2.1 The model Jacobi field is strictly shorter at larger curvature. For fixed $t>0$ put $$m_k(t):=\int_0^t x\,\operatorname{sn}_k(x)\,dx .$$ Since $\operatorname{sn}_k>0$ on $(0,D_k)$, where $D_k:=\pi/\sqrt k$ for $k>0$ and $D_k:=+\infty$ for $k\le0$, one has $m_k(t)>0$ for $0<t<D_k$. From the explicit formulas, for $k\ne0$, $$\partial_k\operatorname{sn}_k(t)=\frac{t\operatorname{cs}_k(t)-\operatorname{sn}_k(t)}{2k}=-\frac{m_k(t)}{2},$$ the first equality following by differentiating the displayed quotient $\sin(\sqrt k\,t)/\sqrt k$ (respectively $\sinh(\sqrt{-k}\,t)/\sqrt{-k}$) in $k$, and the second from $t\operatorname{cs}_k-\operatorname{sn}_k=(t\operatorname{cs}_k-\operatorname{sn}_k)(0)+\int_0^t(-kx\operatorname{sn}_k(x))\,dx=-k\,m_k(t)$, which uses $(\operatorname{cs}_k)'=-k\operatorname{sn}_k$ [F2]. At $k=0$, [F11] gives $\operatorname{sn}_k(t)=t-kt^3/6+o(k)$ from either side, so $\partial_k\operatorname{sn}_k(t)|_{k=0}=-t^3/6=-m_0(t)/2$. The derivative formula extends continuously across $k=0$ since the explicit model functions converge uniformly on the finite interval $[0,t]$. Hence $\partial_k\operatorname{sn}_k(t)<0$: for fixed $t>0$ the function $k\mapsto\operatorname{sn}_k(t)$ is continuously differentiable and strictly decreasing on $(-\infty,\pi^2/t^2)$. In particular $\operatorname{sn}_k(t)<t$ for $0<k<\pi^2/t^2$, $\operatorname{sn}_0(t)=t$ and $\operatorname{sn}_k(t)>t$ for $k<0$; by [F5] the same comparison is the strict sign of the Rauch comparison between the space forms of curvatures $k_1<k_2$: for matched initial derivatives of the same norm $|E|>0$, one has $|J_{k_2}(t)|=\operatorname{sn}_{k_2}(t)|E|<\operatorname{sn}_{k_1}(t)|E|=|J_{k_1}(t)|$. If $E=0$, both fields are identically zero. [F1, F2, F5, F11, step 1.1]

2.2 The model cotangent is strictly smaller at larger curvature. From the explicit formulas, $\operatorname{ct}_0(t)=1/t$, $\operatorname{ct}_k(t)=\sqrt k\cot(\sqrt k\,t)$ for $k>0$ and $\operatorname{ct}_k(t)=\sqrt{-k}\coth(\sqrt{-k}\,t)$ for $k<0$, and $\operatorname{ct}_k(t)\to1/t$ as $k\to0$ in either sign. For $k>0$, with $x=\sqrt k\,t\in(0,\pi)$, $$\frac{d}{dk}\operatorname{ct}_k(t)=\frac{\sin x\cos x-x}{2\sqrt k\,\sin^2x}<0,$$ because $\sin x\cos x\le\sin x<x$ for $x>0$ by step 1.1 (for $x\le\pi/2$ use $\sin x<x$ and $\cos x\le1$; for $x>\pi/2$, $\sin x\cos x<0<x$). For $k<0$, with $y=\sqrt{-k}\,t>0$, $$\frac{d}{dk}\operatorname{ct}_k(t)=-\frac{\sinh y\cosh y-y}{2\sqrt{-k}\,\sinh^2y}<0,$$ because $y<\sinh y\cosh y$ by step 1.1 applied to $\sinh(2y)>2y$. Hence for fixed $t>0$ the function $k\mapsto\operatorname{ct}_k(t)$ is strictly decreasing on $(-\infty,\pi^2/t^2)$, and $\operatorname{ct}_k(t)<1/t$ for $k>0$, $=1/t$ for $k=0$, $>1/t$ for $k<0$. By [F8] this is exactly the strict sign of the Hessian and Laplacian comparisons between the model of curvature $k$ and the flat model: the curvature bound $K\ge k'$ holds in $M^n_k$ whenever $k\ge k'$ [F6, F7], and the attained model value $\operatorname{ct}_k$ is strictly below $\operatorname{ct}_{k'}$ when $k>k'$. [F1, F3, F6, F7, F8, step 1.1]

2.3 Half-angle form of the model cosine law. Let $(a,b,c)$ be admissible with angle $\theta\in(0,\pi)$ opposite $c$ as in [F4], and put $s=(a+b)/2$, $u=(a-b)/2$, $v=c/2$. Then $$\sin^2\frac\theta2=\frac{h(v)-h(u)}{\operatorname{sn}_k(a)\operatorname{sn}_k(b)},\qquad \cos^2\frac\theta2=\frac{h(s)-h(v)}{\operatorname{sn}_k(a)\operatorname{sn}_k(b)},$$ and consequently the two identities $$h(s)-h(u)=\operatorname{sn}_k(a)\operatorname{sn}_k(b),\qquad h(v)=\cos^2\frac\theta2\,h(u)+\sin^2\frac\theta2\,h(s).$$ For $k\ne0$ this is a computation from the cosine law of [F4]: the addition formulas of [F3] give $\operatorname{cs}_k(a\pm b)=\operatorname{cs}_k(a)\operatorname{cs}_k(b)\mp k\operatorname{sn}_k(a)\operatorname{sn}_k(b)$, so $$1-\cos\theta=\frac{\operatorname{cs}_k(a-b)-\operatorname{cs}_k(c)}{k\operatorname{sn}_k(a)\operatorname{sn}_k(b)},\qquad 1+\cos\theta=\frac{\operatorname{cs}_k(c)-\operatorname{cs}_k(a+b)}{k\operatorname{sn}_k(a)\operatorname{sn}_k(b)},$$ and $\operatorname{cs}_k(2x)=1-2k\operatorname{sn}_k(x)^2$ converts the right hand sides into the two displayed expressions, whose sum is $1$ and gives the third identity; the fourth follows by substituting the third into the first. For $k=0$ the same identities are the elementary Euclidean computation: $\sin^2(\theta/2)=(v^2-u^2)/(ab)$, $\cos^2(\theta/2)=(s^2-v^2)/(ab)$, $s^2-u^2=ab$ and $v^2=\cos^2(\theta/2)u^2+\sin^2(\theta/2)s^2$ follow from $c^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos\theta$. Also, the map $\theta\mapsto c_k(a,b,\theta)$ is strictly increasing on $(0,\pi)$: the first identity writes $h(c_k(a,b,\theta)/2)=h(u)+\operatorname{sn}_k(a)\operatorname{sn}_k(b)\sin^2(\theta/2)$ whose right-hand side varies strictly increasingly in $\theta$. For $k>0$ the model opposite side is less than $D_k$, so its half lies in $(0,D_k/2)$, where $h$ is strictly increasing; for $k\le0$ the same holds for every positive half-side. Hence $c_k(a,b,\theta)$ and $\theta$ determine each other strictly increasingly. [F3, F4, step 1.3]

2.4 The ratio $\rho$ is strictly increasing. In the three cases, using the explicit forms of $\operatorname{sn}_k$ and the identity $m(t)=(\operatorname{sn}_k(t)-t\operatorname{cs}_k(t))/k$ for $k\ne0$ (both sides vanish at $0$ and have derivative $t\operatorname{sn}_k(t)$): - $k>0$: with $x=\sqrt k\,t$, $\rho(t)=(1-x\cot x)/k$ and $\rho'(t)=(x-\sin x\cos x)/(\sqrt k\,\sin^2x)>0$ by step 1.1; - $k=0$: $\rho(t)=t^2/3$, so $\rho'(t)=2t/3>0$; - $k<0$: with $y=\sqrt{-k}\,t$, $\rho(t)=(y\coth y-1)/(-k)$ and $\rho'(t)=(\sinh y\cosh y-y)/(\sqrt{-k}\,\sinh^2y)>0$ by step 1.1. Thus $\rho$ is strictly increasing on $(0,D_k)$. No monotonicity of $G=h\rho$ on the whole positive domain is asserted. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 1.3]

2.5 The addition identity for $G$. For all $a,b>0$, with $s=(a+b)/2$ and $u=(a-b)/2$, $$\operatorname{sn}_k(u)m(u)+\tfrac12\bigl[m(a)\operatorname{sn}_k(b)+\operatorname{sn}_k(a)m(b)\bigr]=\operatorname{sn}_k(s)m(s).$$ For $k\ne0$, multiply the difference of the two sides by $k$ and use $m(t)=(\operatorname{sn}_k(t)-t\operatorname{cs}_k(t))/k$ together with the product-to-sum consequences of [F3]: $\operatorname{sn}_k(x)\operatorname{cs}_k(x)=\operatorname{sn}_k(2x)/2$, $\operatorname{sn}_k(x)\operatorname{sn}_k(y)=(\operatorname{cs}_k(x-y)-\operatorname{cs}_k(x+y))/(2k)$, $\operatorname{cs}_k(x)\operatorname{cs}_k(y)=(\operatorname{cs}_k(x-y)+\operatorname{cs}_k(x+y))/2$, $\operatorname{sn}_k(x)\operatorname{cs}_k(y)=(\operatorname{sn}_k(x+y)+\operatorname{sn}_k(x-y))/2$; then the difference equals $$\bigl[h(u)-h(s)+\operatorname{sn}_k(a)\operatorname{sn}_k(b)\bigr] +\Bigl[-\tfrac{u}{2}\operatorname{sn}_k(2u)-\tfrac12\bigl(a\operatorname{cs}_k(a)\operatorname{sn}_k(b)+b\operatorname{sn}_k(a)\operatorname{cs}_k(b)\bigr)+\tfrac{s}{2}\operatorname{sn}_k(2s)\Bigr],$$ whose first bracket is zero by the addition formula for $\operatorname{cs}_k(2\cdot)$ and whose second bracket is zero because $a\operatorname{cs}_k(a)\operatorname{sn}_k(b)+b\operatorname{sn}_k(a)\operatorname{cs}_k(b)=s\operatorname{sn}_k(2s)-u\operatorname{sn}_k(2u)$; hence the difference is zero. For $k=0$ the identity reads $u^4/3+(a^3b+ab^3)/6=s^4/3$ and follows from $s^4-u^4=(s-u)(s+u)(s^2+u^2)=ab(a^2+b^2)/2$. [F2, F3, step 1.3]

3.1 The saturated model volume is strictly smaller at larger curvature. [F1, F9, step 2.1] For $k_1<k_2$, put $D_i:=D_{k_i}$, with $D_i=+\infty$ when $k_i\le0$. Then $D_2\le D_1$, and step 2.1 gives $A_{k_2}(t)<A_{k_1}(t)$ for $0<t<D_2$. For every $r>0$, $$V^\star_{k_i}(r)=\int_0^{\min\{r,D_i\}}A_{k_i}(t)\,dt.$$ The $k_1$ integral covers at least $(0,\min\{r,D_2\})$, on which its density is strictly larger. Hence $$V^\star_{k_2}(r)<V^\star_{k_1}(r)\qquad(r>0).$$ Thus the larger-curvature model has strictly smaller saturated balls, the strict form of the flat-versus-curved Bishop–Gromov comparison. [F1, F9, step 2.1]

3.2 The conjugate function $\Gamma$ is strictly convex. Let $\Gamma$ be defined on the set of values of $h$ by $\Gamma(h(t)):=G(t)$ for $t\in(0,D_k/2)$; this is well defined because $h$ is a strictly increasing bijection from $(0,D_k/2)$ onto its image. The formulas $G(t)\to0$ and $h(t)\to0$ as $t\downarrow0$ extend $\Gamma$ continuously by $\Gamma(0)=0$; when $k>0$, the explicit model formulas likewise extend it continuously to $h(D_k/2)$. Then $$\frac{d\Gamma}{dh}=\frac{\rho(t)}2+\frac{t}2\tan_k(t),\qquad \frac{d^2\Gamma}{dh^2}=\frac{\rho'(t)+\tan_k(t)+t/\operatorname{cs}_k(t)^2}{4\operatorname{sn}_k(t)\operatorname{cs}_k(t)}>0$$ at $h=h(t)$, $t\in(0,D_k/2)$. Indeed, $dh/dt=2\operatorname{sn}_k\operatorname{cs}_k$ and $dG/dt=\operatorname{cs}_k\,m+\operatorname{sn}_k\,m'=\operatorname{sn}_k(\operatorname{cs}_k\rho+t\operatorname{sn}_k)$, so $\frac{d\Gamma}{dh}=(dG/dt)/(dh/dt)=\rho/2+(t/2)\tan_k$; differentiating the last expression with respect to $t$ and dividing by $2\operatorname{sn}_k\operatorname{cs}_k$ gives the displayed second derivative, whose numerator is positive because $\rho'>0$ (step 2.4), $\tan_k>0$, $t>0$ and $\operatorname{sn}_k,\operatorname{cs}_k>0$ on $(0,D_k/2)$, while $(\tan_k)'=(1+k\tan_k^2)=\operatorname{cs}_k^{-2}$ by the Wronskian identity [F2]. Hence $\Gamma$ is strictly convex on the interior and, by its continuous endpoint extension, on every closed subinterval of the extended domain. [F2, step 1.3, step 2.4]

4.1 The half-angle numerator is positive. Let $(a,b,c)$ be admissible, $\theta\in(0,\pi)$ its angle at the vertex between the sides $a$ and $b$, and set $$M_1:=\cos^2\frac\theta2\,G(u)+\sin^2\frac\theta2\,G(s)-G(v), \qquad s=\frac{a+b}2,\quad u=\frac{a-b}2,\quad v=\frac c2 .$$ Then $M_1>0$. To see this, put $t_s:=s$ if $k\le0$ or $s\le D_k/2$, and $t_s:=D_k-s$ if $k>0$ and $s>D_k/2$. Then $t_s\in(0,D_k/2]$ and $h(t_s)=h(s)$. Also $|u|<v<t_s$: when $t_s=s$ this follows from the strict triangle inequality, and when $s>D_k/2$ it follows from $a+b+c<2D_k$. Since $\rho$ is strictly increasing and $h(s)=h(t_s)>0$, $G(t_s)\le G(s)$, strictly if $t_s<s$. Thus $|u|<v<t_s\le D_k/2$, and $h(u),h(v),h(t_s)$ lie in the increasing range of $h$; the fourth identity of step 2.3 makes $h(v)$ a strict convex combination of $h(u)$ and $h(t_s)$ with weights $\cos^2(\theta/2)$ and $\sin^2(\theta/2)$, both in $(0,1)$. Strict convexity of $\Gamma$ (step 3.2) on the closed interval between $h(u)$ and $h(t_s)$ gives $$G(v)=\Gamma(h(v))<\cos^2\tfrac\theta2\,\Gamma(h(u))+\sin^2\tfrac\theta2\,\Gamma(h(t_s)) =\cos^2\tfrac\theta2\,G(u)+\sin^2\tfrac\theta2\,G(t_s)\le\cos^2\tfrac\theta2\,G(u)+\sin^2\tfrac\theta2\,G(s),$$ so $M_1>0$; here $G(u)=G(|u|)$ because $G$ is even. This is the only place where the saturation $s>D_k/2$ of the positive-curvature model is handled. [step 1.3, step 2.3, step 2.4, step 2.5, step 3.2]

5.1 The model angle is strictly increasing in the curvature. Fix an admissible side datum $(a,b,c)$ and let $\theta(k)$ be the model angle opposite $c$ between the sides $a$ and $b$ in $M^2_k$, as $k$ ranges over the open admissible interval $(-\infty,k_{\max})$ (where $k_{\max}:=\min\{(\pi/\max\{a,b,c\})^2,(2\pi/(a+b+c))^2\}$); on this interval the datum stays nondegenerate and $\theta(k)\in(0,\pi)$. Then $$\theta'(k)=\frac{2M_1(k)}{\operatorname{sn}_k(a)\operatorname{sn}_k(b)\sin\theta(k)}>0,$$ where $M_1(k)$ is the positive quantity of step 4.1 computed with the model functions at $k$. Indeed, differentiating the fourth identity of step 2.3 in $k$ gives $$-G(v)=\frac{d}{dk}\Bigl[\cos^2\tfrac\theta2\Bigr](h(u)-h(s))-\cos^2\tfrac\theta2\,G(u)-\sin^2\tfrac\theta2\,G(s),$$ because at each fixed argument $t$, $\partial_k h(t)=2\operatorname{sn}_k(t)\partial_k\operatorname{sn}_k(t)=-G(t)$ by step 2.1; since $\frac{d}{dk}\cos^2(\theta/2)=-\tfrac12\sin\theta\,\theta'$ and $h(u)-h(s)=-\operatorname{sn}_k(a)\operatorname{sn}_k(b)$ by step 2.3, this rearranges to the displayed formula. Its right-hand side is positive because $M_1(k)>0$, $\operatorname{sn}_k(a),\operatorname{sn}_k(b)>0$ (the datum is admissible at $k$) and $\sin\theta(k)>0$. Therefore $\theta$ is strictly increasing on the admissible interval: for $k_1<k_2$ with the datum admissible at both, $\alpha_{k_2}(a,b,c)=\theta(k_2)>\theta(k_1)=\alpha_{k_1}(a,b,c)$. For fixed $\theta\in(0,\pi)$, if $c_{k_2}(a,b,\theta)\ge c_{k_1}(a,b,\theta)$, strict increase of the angle at fixed side lengths in $k$ and of the model side at fixed $k$ in $\theta$ would give an angle greater than $\theta$ at $k_2$ for its own side length, a contradiction. Thus $c_{k_2}(a,b,\theta)<c_{k_1}(a,b,\theta)$. [F1, F4, step 2.1, step 2.3, step 4.1]

6.1 The five strict signs and their directions. Assemble the computations. For $k_1<k_2$ with the relevant data admissible at both curvatures: - Rauch: for matched initial derivatives with common norm $|E|>0$, the model Jacobi field at $k_2$ is strictly shorter than at $k_1$ at every positive time in their common domain (step 2.1); in particular, against the flat model, positive curvature gives strictly shorter fields and negative curvature strictly longer fields, while inside a fixed model the bound is an equality (step 1.2). This is consistent with [F5]: in $M^n_{k_2}$ one has $\sec=k_2\ge k_1$, so Rauch's inequality $|J_{k_2}|\le|J_{k_1}|$ is strict for $E\ne0$; for $E=0$ both sides are zero. - Hessian and Laplacian: $\operatorname{ct}_{k_2}(t)<\operatorname{ct}_{k_1}(t)$ for $t>0$ in the common domain (step 2.2), so the space-form values $\operatorname{ct}_k$ of [F8] give strict inequalities relative to the comparison values of [F6], [F7]: the flat-versus-positive sign is $\operatorname{ct}_k(t)<1/t$ and the flat-versus-negative sign is $\operatorname{ct}_k(t)>1/t$. The directions match $\operatorname{Hess}r\le\operatorname{ct}_k\,g$ and $\Delta_gr\le(n-1)\operatorname{ct}_k$ under $K\ge k$, $\operatorname{Ric}\ge(n-1)k\,g$. - Volume: $V^\star_{k_2}(r)<V^\star_{k_1}(r)$ for every $r>0$ (step 3.1): the model of larger curvature has strictly smaller balls, matching the Bishop–Gromov upper bound, with equality inside a fixed model (step 1.2). - Toponogov: $\alpha_{k_2}>\alpha_{k_1}$ and $c_{k_2}(a,b,\theta)<c_{k_1}(a,b,\theta)$ for nondegenerate data (step 5.1): the model of larger curvature has strictly fatter triangles and strictly shorter opposite sides, matching the hinge inequality $c\le c_k(a,b,\theta)$ and the angle inequality $\alpha\ge\bar\alpha$ under $K\ge k$ [F10], with equality inside a fixed model (step 1.2). All equalities inside a fixed model are those of step 1.2. The example is a direction diagnostic only: every statement above is about the explicitly displayed model functions, no item of either page lists this example among its dependencies, and no later proof uses it. [F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, F10, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 1.2, step 5.1]

7.1 Audit of hypotheses, degenerate cases and choice. The zero initial derivative $E=0$ gives the zero Jacobi field at every curvature; it retains model equality and is excluded only from the strict field comparison. The strict monotonicity of step 5.1 is asserted for nondegenerate admissible data only: $\theta\in(0,\pi)$ and the strict triangle inequalities are used through the hypothesis $\theta\in(0,\pi)$ and through $|u|<v$, and the degenerate limits $\theta=0$ and $\theta=\pi$ are excluded, in agreement with the admissibility convention of [F4] and the strict inequalities of [F10]. The boundary case $k=0$ is treated throughout: the model functions have their $k=0$ values, the half-angle identities of step 2.3 reduce to the Euclidean cosine law and are proved there directly, the primitive $m$ is $t^3/3$ and the addition identity of step 2.5 is proved separately for $k=0$; the formula of step 5.1 uses only the identity of step 2.3, not a division by $k$. The case $s>D_k/2$ of the positive-curvature model, where the sine is decreasing in its argument, is handled in step 4.1 through the reflection $t_s=D_k-s$; for $k\le0$ no such case occurs since $D_k=+\infty$. The isosceles case $a=b$, i.e. $u=0$, is included: then $h(u)=0$ and the strict convexity inequality of step 4.1 still separates $h(u)$ from $h(s)$ because $s>0$. The endpoint radius and time values of the comparison theorems ($p$, the cut locus, $r=0$, $t_0=\pi/\sqrt k$) are excluded exactly as in [F6]–[F9] and are never substituted into a formula; the saturated model volume $V^\star_k$ handles $r\ge\pi/\sqrt k$ without evaluating the density at the spherical endpoint. No family of objects is selected: the model geodesics, triangles and Jacobi fields are supplied by the cited model definitions, so the inherited $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ of [A1] suffices and no further choice is used. No converse implication is asserted and no statement of this example is used by any other item; the diagnostic claims of steps 1.3 and 6.1 are consistent with, and independent of, the comparison theorems they test. [A1, F1, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.3, step 4.1, step 5.1] ∎

## Source locator

The equality case and the model computations are classical: the model solutions $\operatorname{sn}_k,\operatorname{cs}_k,\operatorname{ct}_k$ and their comparison roles are Datar, *Lectures on Riemannian Geometry*, §24.1 (printed pp.173–176) and Eschenburg, *Comparison Theorems in Riemannian Geometry*, §2 (printed pp.6–11); the space-form equalities in the Hessian, Laplacian and volume comparisons are Eschenburg §§3–5 (printed pp.11–20) and Datar §§26–27; the Toponogov comparison with its equality case in constant curvature is Eschenburg §6 (printed pp.21–25) and Lang, Chapter 5, Theorem 5.15 (printed pp.70–71, PDF pp.73–74). The half-angle law of cosines used in step 2.3 is Lang, *Riemannian and Metric Geometry*, Chapter 5, Lemma 5.1, and the strict monotonicity of the model opposite side in the included angle used there is the same chapter's Lemma 5.2 (printed pp.64–65); the strict monotonicity of the model angle in the curvature proved in step 5.1 is the classical comparison quantity monotonicity that underlies the space-form ordering, and it is derived here from the displayed formulas rather than quoted.
