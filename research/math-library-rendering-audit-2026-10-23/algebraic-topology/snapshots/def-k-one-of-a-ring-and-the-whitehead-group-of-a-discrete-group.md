## Definition

Let $R$ be an associative unital ring and let $\mathrm{GL}(R)\supseteq\mathrm E(R)$ be its stable groups ([[def-stable-general-linear-group-and-elementary-subgroup-of-a-ring]]). By [[lem-the-stable-elementary-subgroup-is-normal-and-contains-the-commutator-subgroup]], $\mathrm E(R)$ is normal in $\mathrm{GL}(R)$ and equals the commutator subgroup, so the quotient
$$K_1(R):=\mathrm{GL}(R)/\mathrm E(R)$$
is an abelian group. This group is written **additively**: for $A\in\mathrm{GL}_n(R)$ the symbol $[A]$ denotes the class of the stabilized matrix $A$ in $K_1(R)$, and the group law is the one induced by matrix multiplication, so
$$[AB]=[A]+[B],\qquad [I_n]=0,\qquad [A^{-1}]=-[A]$$
for compatible $A,B$; the class $[A]$ is unchanged by stabilization and equals the class of $\operatorname{diag}(A,I_m)$ for every $m$. The **reduced group**
$$\tilde K_1(R):=K_1(R)/\langle[-1]\rangle$$
is the quotient of $K_1(R)$ by the subgroup generated by the class of the $1\times1$ matrix $-1$ ([[def-generated-subgroup]]); in $\tilde K_1(R)$ one has $[-A]=[A]$ for every invertible $A$.

Now let $\pi$ be a discrete group, with integral group ring $\mathbb Z[\pi]$, a unital ring with basis the classes $[g]$ of the group elements ([[def-group-ring]], [[thm-group-ring-is-a-unital-algebra-with-basis-g]]). The **Whitehead group** of $\pi$ is
$$\mathrm{Wh}(\pi):=K_1(\mathbb Z[\pi])\big/\langle[\pm g]:g\in\pi\rangle,$$
the quotient by the subgroup generated by the classes of the $1\times1$ unit matrices $(\pm g)$. Reading the generators in the order $[-1]$, then $[g]=[-1]+[-g]$, exhibits the same subgroup as $\langle[-1]\rangle+\langle[g]:g\in\pi\rangle$, so there is a natural identification
$$\mathrm{Wh}(\pi)=\tilde K_1(\mathbb Z[\pi])\big/\langle[g]:g\in\pi\rangle .$$

**Functoriality and well-definedness.** A unital ring homomorphism $R\to R'$ carries invertible matrices to invertible matrices and elementary matrices to elementary matrices, hence induces $K_1(R)\to K_1(R')$ and $\tilde K_1(R)\to\tilde K_1(R')$; a group homomorphism $\varphi:\pi\to\pi'$ induces the unital ring homomorphism $\mathbb Z[\pi]\to\mathbb Z[\pi']$, $[g]\mapsto[\varphi(g)]$, which sends $[\pm g]$ to $[\pm\varphi(g)]$ and therefore descends to a homomorphism $\mathrm{Wh}(\pi)\to\mathrm{Wh}(\pi')$. These assignments are compatible with composition and preserve identities. If $\varphi$ is an inner automorphism of $\pi$, given by $c_g(h)=ghg^{-1}$, then the induced ring automorphism of $\mathbb Z[\pi]$ is conjugation by the unit $[g]$, so on $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb Z[\pi])$ it acts as $A\mapsto D_gAD_g^{-1}$ with $D_g$ the scalar matrix $g I_n$; conjugation by a fixed invertible matrix is the identity on the quotient $K_1$, because $[D_gAD_g^{-1}]=[D_g]+[A]-[D_g]=[A]$. Hence inner automorphisms of $\pi$ induce the identity on $\mathrm{Wh}(\pi)$.
