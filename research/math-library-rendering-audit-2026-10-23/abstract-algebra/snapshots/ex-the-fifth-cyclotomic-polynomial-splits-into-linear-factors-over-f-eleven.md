## Example

Over $\mathbb F_{11}$ the fifth cyclotomic polynomial

$$\Phi_5(t)=t^{4}+t^{3}+t^{2}+t+1$$

splits into four distinct linear factors:

$$\Phi_5(t)=(t-3)(t-4)(t-5)(t-9).$$

The four roots $3,4,5,9$ are exactly the primitive fifth roots of unity in
$\mathbb F_{11}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The field $\mathbb F_{11}$ and the polynomial $\Phi_5(t)=t^{4}+t^{3}+t^{2}+t+1$.

[L1] If $\gcd(n,q)=1$, the reduction of $\Phi_n$ in $\mathbb F_q[t]$ is a product of distinct monic irreducibles, each of degree the order of $[q]$ modulo $n$ ([[thm-factorisation-of-the-cyclotomic-polynomial-over-a-finite-field]]).

[L2] For $\gcd(n,q)=1$, the image of $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb F_q(\mu_n)/\mathbb F_q)$ in $(\mathbb Z/n)^\times$ is generated by $[q]$, so the extension degree is the order of $[q]$ modulo $n$ ([[thm-the-cyclotomic-galois-group-over-a-finite-field]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 In $(\mathbb Z/5)^\times$ one has $[11]=[1]$, so the order of $[11]$ modulo $5$ is $1$. Hence [L1] and [L2] say every irreducible factor of $\Phi_5$ over $\mathbb F_{11}$ is linear, and the factors are distinct. [L1, L2, algebra]

1.2 The powers of $3$ in $\mathbb F_{11}^\times$ are $3^{2}=9$, $3^{3}=5$, $3^{4}=4$ and $3^{5}=1$, so the four nontrivial fifth roots of unity in $\mathbb F_{11}$ are $3,9,5,4$. [given, algebra]

2.1 Each of $3,4,5,9$ is therefore a root of $t^{5}-1$ and is not $1$, so each is a root of $\Phi_5(t)=(t^{5}-1)/(t-1)$. Since $\Phi_5$ is monic of degree $4$, it follows that $$\Phi_5(t)=(t-3)(t-4)(t-5)(t-9).$$ [step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 The roots $3,4,5,9$ all have multiplicative order $5$ by step 1.2, so they are exactly the primitive fifth roots of unity in $\mathbb F_{11}$. [step 1.2, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

- **This is the order-one case of the finite-field factorisation theorem.** When $[q]$ has order one modulo $n$, every irreducible factor has degree one and the whole cyclotomic polynomial splits over the base field.
