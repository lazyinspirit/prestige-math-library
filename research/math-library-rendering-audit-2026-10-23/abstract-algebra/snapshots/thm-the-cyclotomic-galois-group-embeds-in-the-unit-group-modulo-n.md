## Statement

Let $K$ be a field and $n\ge1$ with $\operatorname{char}K\nmid n$
([[def-ring-characteristic]]), and let $E=K(\mu_n)$
([[def-cyclotomic-extension]]). Then $E/K$ is a finite Galois extension, and
there is an injective group homomorphism

$$\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)\longrightarrow(\mathbb Z/n)^{\times},\qquad \sigma\longmapsto[a_\sigma]_n,$$

where $a_\sigma$ is any integer with $\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^{a_\sigma}$ for a
primitive $n$-th root of unity $\zeta\in E$. The class $[a_\sigma]_n$ does not
depend on which primitive $n$-th root of unity is used, and $\sigma(x)=x^{a_\sigma}$
holds for every $x\in\mu_n(E)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $K$, an integer $n\ge1$ with $\operatorname{char}K\nmid n$, the extension $E=K(\mu_n)$ ([[def-cyclotomic-extension]]), and a primitive $n$-th root of unity $\zeta\in E$ ([[def-roots-of-unity-in-a-field]]).

[L1] For $n\ge1$, $t^{n}-1$ is separable over $K$ when $\operatorname{char}K\nmid n$; in a splitting field $E$ the group $\mu_n(E)$ is then cyclic of order $n$, has $\varphi(n)$ primitive $n$-th roots of unity, and $E=K(\zeta)$ for every primitive $n$-th root of unity $\zeta\in E$ ([[thm-separability-of-x-n-minus-one-and-the-order-of-the-group-of-roots-of-unity]]).

[L2] For a finite extension $L/F$ with $G=\operatorname{Aut}(L/F)$, the conditions "$L/F$ is Galois", "$L$ is the splitting field over $F$ of a separable polynomial", "$|G|=[L:F]$" and "$L^{G}=F$" are equivalent ([[thm-finite-galois-extension-characterizations]], [[def-finite-galois-extension-and-galois-group]], [[def-relative-field-automorphism-group]]).

[L3] $\mu_n(E)$ is a finite cyclic subgroup of $E^{\times}$ of order dividing $n$, and when its order is $n$ its generators are exactly the primitive $n$-th roots of unity ([[prop-the-roots-of-unity-in-a-field-form-a-finite-cyclic-group]]).

[L4] In a cyclic group $\langle g\rangle$ of finite order $m$, $g^{a}$ generates the group if and only if $\gcd(a,m)=1$ ([[lem-a-finite-cyclic-group-has-euler-totient-many-generators]]).

[L5] For an element $x$ of finite order $m$: $x^{k}=e$ if and only if $m\mid k$ ([[lem-order-characterisation]]).

[L6] For $n\ge1$ and $a\in\mathbb Z$, the class $[a]_n\in\mathbb Z/n$ ([[def-integers-modulo-n]]) is a unit of $\mathbb Z/n$ if and only if $\gcd(a,n)=1$ ([[thm-unit-criterion-modulo-n]]); the units form the group $(\mathbb Z/n)^{\times}$ of order $\varphi(n)$ ([[def-unit-group-modulo-n-and-euler-totient]]).

[L7] If $a$ is algebraic over a field $K$, then the simple extension $K(a)/K$ is finite, with degree equal to the degree of the minimal polynomial of $a$ ([[thm-simple-algebraic-extension-quotient-power-basis-and-degree]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [L1] the polynomial $t^{n}-1$ is separable over $K$, the group $\mu_n(E)$ is cyclic of order $n$ generated by $\zeta$, and $E=K(\zeta)$. The element $\zeta$ is algebraic because it is a root of $t^n-1$, so [L7] makes $E/K$ finite. Since $E$ is the splitting field of the separable polynomial $t^n-1$, [L2] now makes $E/K$ finite Galois. [L1, L2, L3, L7, given]

2.1 Each $\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)$ maps $\mu_n(E)$ into itself, since $\sigma(x)^{n}=\sigma(x^{n})=\sigma(1)=1$; being injective on the finite set $\mu_n(E)$ it restricts to a bijection, and it is multiplicative, so it restricts to a group automorphism of $\mu_n(E)$. [step 1.1, given]

3.1 Hence $\sigma(\zeta)$ generates $\mu_n(E)=\langle\zeta\rangle$, so $\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^{a}$ for some integer $a$, and [L4] gives $\gcd(a,n)=1$, so $[a]_n\in(\mathbb Z/n)^{\times}$ by [L6]. The class is well defined: $\zeta^{a}=\zeta^{b}$ means $\zeta^{a-b}=1$, which by [L5] and $\operatorname{ord}(\zeta)=n$ says $n\mid a-b$, that is $[a]_n=[b]_n$. Write $[a_\sigma]_n$ for this class. [step 1.1, step 2.1, L4, L5, L6]

4.1 For every $x\in\mu_n(E)$ one has $x=\zeta^{j}$ for some $j$, so $\sigma(x)=\sigma(\zeta)^{j}=\zeta^{a_\sigma j}=x^{a_\sigma}$. [step 1.1, step 3.1]

4.2 The map $\sigma\mapsto[a_\sigma]_n$ is a group homomorphism: $(\sigma\tau)(\zeta)=\sigma(\zeta^{a_\tau})=\sigma(\zeta)^{a_\tau}=\zeta^{a_\sigma a_\tau}$, so $[a_{\sigma\tau}]_n=[a_\sigma]_n[a_\tau]_n$ by the well-definedness of step 3.1. [step 3.1, L6]

4.3 It is injective: if $[a_\sigma]_n=[1]_n$ then $\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta$ by step 3.1, and since $E=K(\zeta)$ and $\sigma$ fixes $K$ pointwise, $\sigma$ is the identity on $E$. [step 1.1, step 3.1]

5.1 The class is independent of the chosen primitive $n$-th root of unity: any other one is a generator $\zeta'$ of $\mu_n(E)$ by [L3], so $\zeta'=\zeta^{b}$ for some $b$, and $\sigma(\zeta')=\sigma(\zeta)^{b}=\zeta^{a_\sigma b}=(\zeta^{b})^{a_\sigma}=(\zeta')^{a_\sigma}$, which exhibits the same exponent class. With steps 4.1, 4.2 and 4.3 this proves the theorem. [step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 4.2, step 4.3, L3] ∎

## Remarks

- **The embedding need not be onto.** Surjectivity is exactly irreducibility of the $n$-th cyclotomic polynomial over $K$ ([[prop-irreducibility-of-the-cyclotomic-polynomial-characterises-the-full-galois-group]]), and it fails over many base fields: over $\mathbb F_q$ the image is the cyclic subgroup generated by the class of $q$ ([[thm-the-cyclotomic-galois-group-over-a-finite-field]]).
