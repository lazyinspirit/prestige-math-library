## Statement refuted

Every localisation map $R\to S^{-1}R$ is injective.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The quotient ring $R=\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z$ and the subset $S=\{1,3\}$.

[F1] The kernel of a localisation map consists of the elements annihilated by some denominator ([[prop-localisation-zero-equality-and-kernel-criteria]]).

[F2] The ring $\mathbb Z/6$ is $\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z$ with congruence-class arithmetic ([[prop-integers-modulo-n-as-a-quotient-ring]]).

## Counterexample

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 In $R=\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z$, let $S=\{1,3\}$, the multiplicative set generated by the class of $3$ because $3^2=3$. The class of $2$ is nonzero, while $3\cdot2=0$. [F2, algebra]

2.1 Since the denominator $3$ annihilates $2$, [F1] gives $2/1=0$ in $S^{-1}R$. Thus the localisation map kills a nonzero element and is not injective. [F1, step 1.1] ∎
