## Statement

Let $E/F$ be finite Galois and let $L/F$ be a field extension inside a common overfield. Then $EL/L$ is finite Galois, and restriction gives

$$\operatorname{Gal}(EL/L)\cong\operatorname{Gal}(E/E\cap L).$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The compositum $EL$ and intersection $D:=E\cap L$ inside the common overfield.

[L1] A finite extension is Galois if and only if it is the splitting field of a separable polynomial, and for a finite Galois extension the fixed field of the full Galois group is the base field ([[thm-finite-galois-extension-characterizations]]).

[L2] If $H$ is a finite group of automorphisms of a field $K$, then $[K:K^H]=|H|$ and $\operatorname{Aut}(K/K^H)=H$ ([[thm-artin-fixed-field-degree-theorem]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [L1], $E$ is the splitting field over $F$ of a separable polynomial $f$. The same polynomial over $L$ remains separable and has splitting field $EL$, so [L1] makes $EL/L$ finite Galois. [L1]

2.1 An element of $\operatorname{Gal}(EL/L)$ permutes the roots of $f$, hence preserves $E$, and its restriction to $E$ fixes $D=E\cap L$. Restriction therefore defines a homomorphism into $\operatorname{Gal}(E/D)$; it is injective because $EL$ is generated by $E$ and $L$. [step 1.1]

3.1 Let $H$ be the restriction image. By [L1], an element of $E$ is fixed by $H$ exactly when it is fixed by every automorphism of $EL/L$, exactly when it lies in $L$; thus $E^H=E\cap L=D$. By [L2], $H=\operatorname{Aut}(E/E^H)=\operatorname{Gal}(E/D)$, so restriction is surjective and hence an isomorphism. If $E\subseteq L$, both groups are trivial; if $L=F$ or $D=F$, the displayed formula specializes directly. [step 2.1, L1, L2] ∎
