## Statement

Let $S,T\subseteq R$ be multiplicative, let $\overline T$ be the image of $T$ in $S^{-1}R$, and let $U\subseteq R$ be the multiplicative subset generated by $S\cup T$. Then there is a unique $R$-algebra isomorphism
$$ \overline T^{-1}(S^{-1}R)\cong U^{-1}R. $$
In particular, $(R_f)_g\cong R_{fg}$ for $f,g\in R$, where $g$ on the left denotes its image in $R_f$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Multiplicative subsets $S,T\subseteq R$, their generated multiplicative set $U$, and the image $\overline T\subseteq S^{-1}R$.

[F1] A homomorphism out of a localisation is uniquely determined by a map from the original ring that sends the denominator set to units ([[thm-universal-property-of-localisation]]).

[F2] Two objects with the same localisation universal property are uniquely isomorphic over the original ring ([[cor-localisation-is-unique-up-to-unique-isomorphism]]).

[F3] The principal localisation $R_f$ inverts the powers of $f$ ([[def-principal-localisation]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct universal-property argument.

1.1 A map $h:R\to A$ extends to $\overline T^{-1}(S^{-1}R)$ exactly when it sends $S$ to units and, after the first extension, sends every $t/1$ with $t\in T$ to a unit. [F1]

2.1 The image of $t/1$ is $h(t)$, so the condition in step 1.1 is exactly that $h$ send every member of $S\cup T$ to a unit, equivalently every element of the generated set $U$ to a unit. [step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Hence $\overline T^{-1}(S^{-1}R)$ and $U^{-1}R$ have the same universal property over $R$, so [F2] gives the unique displayed $R$-algebra isomorphism. [F1, F2, step 2.1]

4.1 For $S=\{f^n:n\in\mathbb N\}$ and $T=\{g^n:n\in\mathbb N\}$, the set $U$ is generated by $f$ and $g$. A map inverts both precisely when it inverts $fg$: if $fg$ is a unit, then $f(g(fg)^{-1})=1$ and $g(f(fg)^{-1})=1$. Thus [F1] identifies this localisation with $R_{fg}$, including $f=0$ or $g=0$. [F1, F3, algebra] ∎
