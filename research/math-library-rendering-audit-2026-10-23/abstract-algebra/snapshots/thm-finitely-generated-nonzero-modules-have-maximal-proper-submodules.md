## Statement

Assuming the Axiom of Choice, every finitely generated nonzero module has a maximal proper submodule. See [[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]].

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The hypotheses and objects in the Statement.

[L1] Let $M$ be a left $R$-module and $S\subseteq M$. The submodule generated by $S$ is $$\langle S\rangle_R:=\bigcap\{N\le M:S\subseteq N\}.$$ The family is nonempty because $M\le M$, and its intersection is a submodule by lem-submodule-criterion-sums-and-intersections. Thus $\langle S\rangle_R$ is the smallest submodule of $M$ containing $S$, just as def-generated-subgroup defines a generated subgroup. ([[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]]).

[L2] Let $M$ be a left $R$-module. A subset $N\subseteq M$ is a submodule when it is a subgroup of the additive group of $M$ and is closed under scalars: $$r\in R,\ n\in N\quad\Longrightarrow\quad rn\in N.$$ The operations on $N$ are the restrictions of those of $M$. Write $N\le M$ when the ring and module are understood. ([[def-submodule]]).

[L3] Assume the Axiom of Choice (def-axiom-of-choice). Let $(P, \le)$ be a nonempty poset in which every chain has an upper bound. Then $P$ has a maximal element (def-maximal-element). Note the hypothesis asks only for an upper bound, not a least upper bound, and the conclusion asserts only that a maximal element exists, never that a greatest one does. ([[thm-zorn]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $M\ne0$ be generated over $R$ by $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ [L1] and let $\mathcal P$ be the set of proper submodules of $M$ [L2], ordered by inclusion. It is nonempty, since $0$ is a proper submodule of the nonzero module $M$. [L1, L2, given, algebra]

2.1 Let $\mathcal C\subseteq\mathcal P$ be a chain. If $\mathcal C$ is empty, $0\in\mathcal P$ is an upper bound. Otherwise put $U=\bigcup\mathcal C$; any two elements of $U$ lie in a common member of the chain, so $U$ is a submodule. If $U=M$, each generator $x_i$ would lie in some member of $\mathcal C$, and the largest of those finitely many members would contain every $x_i$ and hence equal $M$, contradicting properness. So $U\in\mathcal P$ and is an upper bound for $\mathcal C$. [step 1.1, given, algebra]

3.1 Every chain in the nonempty poset $\mathcal P$ therefore has an upper bound in $\mathcal P$, so Zorn's lemma [L3] — which assumes the Axiom of Choice, as the Statement does — gives a maximal element of $\mathcal P$, that is, a maximal proper submodule of $M$. [L3, step 2.1, given]

4.1 Both hypotheses are load-bearing above: nonzeroness is what makes the poset of step 1.1 nonempty, and finite generation is what makes the union of a chain proper in step 2.1. This proves the stated claim. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, given] ∎
