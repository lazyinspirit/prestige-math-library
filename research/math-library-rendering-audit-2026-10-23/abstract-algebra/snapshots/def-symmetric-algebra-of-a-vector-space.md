## Definition

For a vector space $V$, let $J$ be the two-sided ideal of $T(V)$ generated by

$$v\otimes w-w\otimes v\qquad(v,w\in V).$$

The **symmetric algebra** of $V$ is the commutative graded algebra

$$S(V)=T(V)/J.$$

The quotient is commutative because its degree-one generators commute, and
all elements are sums of products of those generators. Since the relators are
homogeneous of degree two, $J$ is homogeneous. In degree $n$, the relation
space is generated by adjacent-transposition differences: an arbitrary
permutation difference is a telescoping sum along adjacent transpositions,
and each adjacent difference is a degree-$n$ multiple of a generator of $J$.
Thus the degree-$n$ piece is the quotient of $V^{\otimes n}$ by the span of
$t-\sigma(t)$ for $t\in V^{\otimes n}$ and $\sigma\in S_n$. The degree-zero
unit is the image of $1\in k$.
