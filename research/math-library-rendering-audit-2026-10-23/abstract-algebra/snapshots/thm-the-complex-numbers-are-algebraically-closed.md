## Statement

The field $\mathbb C$ is algebraically closed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A nonconstant polynomial $g \in \mathbb C[x]$.

[L1] If every nonconstant polynomial in $\mathbb R[x]$ splits over $\mathbb C$, then $\mathbb C$ is algebraically closed ([[lem-real-polynomial-splitting-suffices-for-the-fundamental-theorem-of-algebra]]).

[L2] Every finite family of nonzero polynomials has a splitting field ([[cor-splitting-fields-exist-for-finite-families]]).

[L3] An algebraic extension that is a splitting field of a polynomial is normal ([[prop-algebraic-splitting-extensions-are-normal]]).

[L4] Every field of characteristic zero is perfect, and every algebraic extension of a perfect field is separable ([[cor-fields-of-characteristic-zero-and-finite-fields-are-perfect]], [[cor-algebraic-extensions-of-perfect-fields-are-separable]]).

[L5] For a finite Galois extension $K/F$ with group $G$, the maps $H \mapsto K^H$ and $E \mapsto \operatorname{Gal}(K/E)$ are inverse inclusion-reversing bijections, and $$ [K:K^H]=|H|,\qquad [K^H:F]=[G:H]. $$ ([[thm-fundamental-theorem-of-finite-galois-theory]])

[L6] Degrees multiply in a finite tower ([[thm-tower-law-for-finite-field-extensions]]).

[L7] Every finite group has a Sylow $2$-subgroup ([[thm-sylow-first-theorem]]).

[L8] Every nontrivial finite $p$-group has a subgroup of index $p$ ([[thm-index-p-subgroup-existence-in-finite-p-groups]]).

[L9] Every complex number has a square root in $\mathbb C$ ([[thm-every-complex-number-has-a-square-root]]).

[L10] A square root of $-1$ in a real field extension determines a unique real-field embedding of $\mathbb C$ into that extension ([[cor-universal-property-of-the-complex-numbers]]).

[L11] A quadratic extension in characteristic not $2$ is obtained by adjoining a square root ([[lem-quadratic-extension-in-characteristic-not-two-is-generated-by-a-square-root]]).

[L12] An irreducible polynomial over $\mathbb R$ has degree $1$ or an even degree ([[cor-irreducible-real-polynomial-has-degree-one-or-even-degree]]).

[L13] A field generated by finitely many algebraic elements over a base field is finite over that base ([[thm-finitely-generated-algebraic-extensions-are-finite]]).

[L14] If $\alpha$ is algebraic over a field $F$, then the degree of its minimal polynomial equals $[F(\alpha):F]$ ([[cor-element-algebraic-iff-simple-extension-finite]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [L1], it is enough to prove that every nonconstant polynomial in $\mathbb R[x]$ splits over $\mathbb C$. Fix such a polynomial $f \in \mathbb R[x]$. [L1, suffices: split every nonconstant real polynomial over C]

1.2 Put $h:=f(x)(x^2+1)$. By [L2], choose a splitting field $E/\mathbb R$ of $h$. The roots of $h$ are algebraic over $\mathbb R$, and $E$ is generated by finitely many of them, so [L13] makes $E/\mathbb R$ finite. Because $E$ is a splitting field of $h$, [L3] makes it normal, and [L4] makes it separable. Thus $E/\mathbb R$ is finite Galois. [L2, L3, L4, L13, choose]

1.3 Let $G:=\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb R)$. By [L7], choose a Sylow $2$-subgroup $H \le G$, and put $M:=E^H$. Then [L5] gives $$ [M:\mathbb R]=[G:H], $$ which is odd by the definition of a Sylow $2$-subgroup. [L5, L7, choose]

2.1 Since $x^2+1$ splits in $E$, choose $j \in E$ with $j^2=-1$. By [L10], there is a unique real-field embedding $\iota:\mathbb C \to E$ sending $i$ to $j$. Identify $\mathbb C$ with its image, so that $$ \mathbb R \subseteq \mathbb C \subseteq E. $$ [L10, step 1.2, choose]

2.2 Let $\alpha \in M$. Since $\mathbb R \subseteq \mathbb R(\alpha) \subseteq M$, the tower law [L6] shows that $[\mathbb R(\alpha):\mathbb R]$ divides $[M:\mathbb R]$, so $[\mathbb R(\alpha):\mathbb R]$ is odd. Because $\alpha \in E$ and step 1.2 made $E/\mathbb R$ finite, the element $\alpha$ is algebraic over $\mathbb R$. Fact [L14] therefore makes the minimal-polynomial degree of $\alpha$ over $\mathbb R$ equal to the same odd number $[\mathbb R(\alpha):\mathbb R]$; by [L12] it must have degree $1$. Hence $\alpha \in \mathbb R$. Since every element of $M$ lies in $\mathbb R$, we get $M=\mathbb R$. [L6, L12, L14, step 1.2, step 1.3, algebra]

3.1 Because $M=E^H=\mathbb R=E^G$, the bijection in [L5] forces $H=G$. Therefore $G$ is a finite $2$-group. [L5, step 2.2]

4.1 Put $J:=\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb C)$. Then $J \le G$, so step 3.1 makes $J$ a finite $2$-group. Suppose $J$ is nontrivial. [step 2.1, step 3.1, assume-contra]

5.1 By [L8], the nontrivial $2$-group $J$ has a subgroup $K$ of index $2$. Put $L:=E^K$. Then $E^J=\mathbb C$ by [L5], and [L5] together with [L6] gives $$ [L:\mathbb C]=[J:K]=2. $$ [L5, L6, L8, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 The field $\mathbb C$ has characteristic zero, hence not $2$, so [L11] makes the quadratic extension $L/\mathbb C$ equal to $\mathbb C(\sqrt d)$ for some $d \in \mathbb C$. By [L9], choose $e \in \mathbb C$ with $e^2=d$. Then $$ L=\mathbb C(\sqrt d)=\mathbb C(e)=\mathbb C, $$ contradicting step 5.1. Therefore $J$ is trivial. [L9, L11, step 5.1, discharge-contradiction]

7.1 Since $\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb C)=1$, the fixed field of the trivial subgroup is $E$; by [L5], that fixed field is also $\mathbb C$. Thus $E=\mathbb C$. Because $E$ is a splitting field of $h$ and $f$ divides $h$, the polynomial $f$ splits over $\mathbb C$. By step 1.1 and [L1], this proves that $\mathbb C$ is algebraically closed. [L1, L5, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 6.1] ∎
