## Statement

On a nonzero finite-dimensional space, the largest invariant factor is the minimal polynomial. On the zero space the invariant-factor list is empty and the minimal polynomial is $1$, so there is no largest invariant factor.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A nonzero finite-dimensional space with invariant factors $f_1\mid\cdots\mid f_r$ supplied by [[thm-rational-canonical-form-existence-and-uniqueness]].

[L1] The annihilator of $V_T$ is generated by the minimal polynomial $\mu_T$ ([[prop-polynomial-module-is-finitely-generated-torsion-with-minimal-annihilator]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 A polynomial $p$ annihilates $\bigoplus_iF[x]/(f_i)$ exactly when $f_i\mid p$ for every $i$. Since $f_1\mid\cdots\mid f_r$, this is equivalent to $f_r\mid p$, so the module annihilator is $(f_r)$. [given, algebra]

2.1 By [L1], the same annihilator ideal is $(\mu_T)$. Both generators are monic, so $f_r=\mu_T$. The nonzero-space hypothesis ensures $r\ge1$; the zero-space convention is as stated. [step 1.1, L1] ∎
