## Statement

Let $F=\mathbb Q(q)$, $N\ge1$, and $n\ge0$. Let $H_n(F,q)$ be the type-A Hecke algebra generated by $t_1,\ldots,t_{n-1}$ with braid relations and $(t_i-q)(t_i+q^{-1})=0$; put $H_0=H_1=F$. On $V_N^{\otimes n}$ define $T_i$ by applying the operator of [[def-hh-hecke-symmetry-and-ordered-basis-operator]] in positions $i,i+1$. There is a unital algebra homomorphism $\rho_n:H_n(F,q)\to\operatorname{End}_F(V_N^{\otimes n})$ with $\rho_n(t_i)=T_i$. Its matrices are regular at $q=1$, where $T_i$ is the transposition of tensor positions $i,i+1$. This is an action into an endomorphism algebra; it makes no assertion that $\rho_n$ is injective.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $F=\mathbb Q(q)$, $N\ge1$, $n\ge0$, and the normalized type-A Hecke presentation.

[F1] The presentation of $H_n$ is the specialization of the universal Coxeter Hecke presentation in [[def-hh-universal-coxeter-hecke-parameters-and-presentation]] with type $A_{n-1}$ and common parameter $q$.

[F2] The universal presentation has the usual quotient universal property; [[thm-hh-generic-coxeter-hecke-standard-basis]] proves its standard basis and its base-change compatibility.

[F3] The operator of [[lem-hh-type-a-tensor-operator-is-a-hecke-symmetry]] satisfies the quadratic and braid relations, and at $q=1$ it is the flip.

## Proof

**Given:** The data in [F1]–[F3].

1.1 For $n\ge2$, each $T_i$ satisfies $(T_i-qI)(T_i+q^{-1}I)=0$ by [F3]. Adjacent operators satisfy the braid relation because, on the three positions they meet, this is exactly the identity $T_1T_2T_1=T_2T_1T_2$ proved in [F3]; tensoring with identities on the other positions preserves that equality. If $|i-j|>1$, then $T_i$ and $T_j$ act on disjoint tensor factors, so on every elementary tensor each changes only its own pair and the two orders of application give the same tensor and coefficient. Thus the family $(T_i)$ satisfies every defining relation in [F1]. [given, F1, F3, algebra]

2.1 By the quotient universal property in [F2], the assignment $t_i\mapsto T_i$ extends uniquely to a unital $F$-algebra homomorphism $\rho_n$. For $n=0,1$ the algebra and endomorphism image are the scalar algebra and the unique unital map supplies the same conclusion. Each matrix entry of every $T_i$ is a Laurent polynomial in $q$, so it is regular at $q=1$; [F3] identifies the specialized operators as adjacent flips. The standard-basis theorem in [F2] concerns the abstract algebra and does not identify it with its image under $\rho_n$. [step 1.1, F2, F3] ∎
