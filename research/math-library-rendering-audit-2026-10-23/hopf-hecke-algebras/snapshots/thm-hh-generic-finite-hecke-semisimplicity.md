## Statement

Let $W$ be a finite Coxeter group with finite generator set $S$, let $k$ be a field of characteristic zero, and form $R_k=k[v_C^{\pm1}:C\in\mathcal C]$ and $H_{R_k}(W)$ as in [[def-hh-specialization-and-regular-trace-determinant]]. Let $F:=\operatorname{Frac}(R_k)$ be the fraction field and let $D_W\in R_k$ be the regular-trace determinant. Then $D_W\ne0$ and the generic algebra $F\otimes_{R_k}H_{R_k}(W)$ is semisimple as a left regular module. More generally, for any ring homomorphism to a field $\phi:R_k\to K$, if $\phi(D_W)\ne0$, then $H_\phi$ is semisimple. No splitness claim is made.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite Coxeter group $W$ with finite generator set $S$, a characteristic-zero field $k$, the universal Laurent parameter ring $R_k$, and the finite free Hecke algebra $H_{R_k}(W)$ with basis $(T_w)_{w\in W}$.

[F1] The presented Coxeter group is the quotient with relators $s^2$ and $(st)^{m(s,t)}=1$ for finite $m(s,t)$, and any assignment of generators killing those relators extends uniquely to a group homomorphism ([[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]).

[F2] The generic Hecke algebra is presented by the quadratic relations $(T_s-v_s)(T_s+v_s^{-1})=0$ and the finite alternating braid relations; a family satisfying them in a unital algebra gives a unique algebra homomorphism ([[def-hh-universal-coxeter-hecke-parameters-and-presentation]]).

[F3] Presentation base change identifies $S\otimes_R(R\langle X\rangle/I)$ with the quotient by the image ideal generated by the specialized relations ([[lem-hh-universal-presentations-and-base-change]]).

[F4] Every commutative specialization is free with basis $(1\otimes T_w)_{w\in W}$ ([[thm-hh-generic-coxeter-hecke-standard-basis]]).

[F5] A Laurent polynomial ring over an integral domain is an integral domain, and its fraction field contains it injectively ([[lem-hh-finite-polynomial-and-localization-constructions]]).

[F6] A nondegenerate regular-trace pairing on a finite-dimensional field algebra forces its left regular module to be semisimple ([[lem-hh-regular-trace-criterion-in-characteristic-zero]]).

[F7] The specialized regular-trace Gram determinant is the image of $D_W$ under the coefficient homomorphism ([[def-hh-specialization-and-regular-trace-determinant]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Specialize every parameter $v_C$ to $1$ and call the resulting algebra $H_1$. By [F2], each specialized generator $T_s$ is an involution and the alternating braid relations hold. For involutions $a,b$, put $g:=ab$, so $ba=g^{-1}$. If $m=2r$, the braid relation is $g^r=g^{-r}$, equivalent to $g^{2r}=1$. If $m=2r+1$, its two sides are $g^ra$ and $(ba)^rb$; right multiplication by $a$ makes equality equivalent to $g^r=(ba)^{r+1}=g^{-(r+1)}$, which is equivalent to $g^{2r+1}=1$. Thus, for every finite braid label, braid and Coxeter relator are equivalent in the presence of the involution relations. The specialized generators satisfy exactly [F1]. [given, F1, F2, algebra]

1.2 If $\phi:R_k\to K$ is a ring homomorphism to a field with $\phi(D_W)\ne0$, [F7] says that the regular-trace Gram matrix of $H_\phi$ has nonzero determinant and is therefore nondegenerate. Applying [F6] shows that $H_\phi$ is semisimple. This criterion does not infer splitness and makes no claim when $\phi(D_W)=0$. [given, F6, F7, algebra]

2.1 By the group universal property in [F1], $s\mapsto T_s$ extends to $W\to H_1^\times$, and linear extension gives a $k$-algebra map $kW\to H_1$. Conversely, the group generators in $kW$ satisfy the specialized quadratic and braid relations, so the quotient presentation in [F3] gives a $k$-algebra map $H_1\to kW$. The composites are identity on their algebra generators, hence $H_1\cong kW$ and $T_w$ corresponds to $w$. By [F4], $(T_w)_{w\in W}$ is a basis of $H_1$, so $\dim_kH_1=|W|$. [step 1.1, F1, F3, F4, algebra]

3.1 For $g\in W$, $L_g$ permutes the group basis by $w\mapsto gw$. If $g=1$, all $|W|$ basis vectors are fixed; if $g\ne1$, $gw=w$ would imply $g=1$ by right cancellation, so none is fixed. Thus $\operatorname{Tr}_k(L_g)=|W|$ for $g=1$ and $0$ otherwise. The regular-trace Gram matrix at $v_C=1$ therefore has entries $|W|\delta_{y,x^{-1}}$; it is $|W|$ times a permutation matrix and has determinant $\pm|W|^{|W|}\ne0$ in characteristic zero. [step 2.1, F4, F7, given, algebra]

4.1 By [F7], this determinant is $D_W$ evaluated at $v_C=1$, so $D_W$ is not the zero Laurent polynomial. Since $k$ is a field, [F5] makes $R_k$ an integral domain and embeds it into its fraction field $F$; therefore $D_W$ remains nonzero in $F$. The regular-trace Gram matrix over $F$ is invertible, and [F6] proves that $F\otimes_{R_k}H_{R_k}(W)$ is semisimple. [step 3.1, F5, F6, F7, algebra]

5.1 When $|W|=1$, the matrix in step 3.1 is $[1]$, so the argument includes the trivial Coxeter group. All bases are finite and specified; no form of Choice is used. [step 3.1, given] ∎
