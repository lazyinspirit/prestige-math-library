## Statement

Let $G$ be a finitely generated group, and let $S$ and $T$ be finite generating
sets. Then the growth functions $\beta_{G,S}$ and $\beta_{G,T}$ are equivalent
under $\simeq$. Hence the growth type of a finitely generated group does not
depend on the chosen finite generating set.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finitely generated group $G$ and finite generating sets $S$ and $T$.

[L1] The growth function $\beta_{G,S}(n)$ counts the elements with $|g|_S \le n$, and $\beta_{G,T}(n)$ is defined similarly ([[def-growth-function-of-a-finitely-generated-group]]).

[L2] The relation $\simeq$ is the mutual comparison relation generated by $f(n) \le C\, g(Cn+C)+C$ for some natural number $C \ge 1$ ([[def-growth-comparison-and-growth-type]]).

[L3] The identity map between the two word metrics is a bilipschitz equivalence ([[thm-two-finite-generating-sets-of-a-group-give-bilipschitz-equivalent-word-metrics]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [L3], choose a natural number $C \ge 1$ such that $d_T(g,h) \le C\, d_S(g,h)$ and $d_S(g,h) \le C\, d_T(g,h)$ for all $g,h \in G$. [L3, choose]

2.1 If $|g|_S \le n$, then step 1.1 gives $|g|_T \le Cn$. So every element counted by $\beta_{G,S}(n)$ is also counted by $\beta_{G,T}(Cn)$, and therefore $\beta_{G,S}(n) \le \beta_{G,T}(Cn)$. Exchanging $S$ and $T$ yields the reverse inequality. [L1, step 1.1]

3.1 Because $C \ge 1$ and the growth functions are nondecreasing, step 2.1 implies $$\beta_{G,S}(n) \le \beta_{G,T}(Cn) \le C\,\beta_{G,T}(Cn+C)+C,$$ and likewise with $S$ and $T$ interchanged. Thus [L2] gives $\beta_{G,S} \preccurlyeq \beta_{G,T}$ and $\beta_{G,T} \preccurlyeq \beta_{G,S}$. Hence $\beta_{G,S} \simeq \beta_{G,T}$, and the growth type is independent of the finite generating set. [L2, step 2.1] ∎
