## Example

The integral Heisenberg group has homogeneous dimension $4$, and therefore its
growth function is equivalent to $m^4$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The integral Heisenberg group $H$.

[L1] The homogeneous dimension is $D(H)=\sum i \cdot \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z}(\gamma_i(H)/\gamma_{i+1}(H))$ ([[def-homogeneous-dimension-of-a-finitely-generated-nilpotent-group]]).

[L2] The growth function counts word-metric balls ([[def-growth-function-of-a-finitely-generated-group]]), and its equivalence class is independent of the finite generating set ([[thm-growth-type-is-independent-of-the-finite-generating-set]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Write $H=\mathbb Z^3$ with multiplication $(a,b,c)(a',b',c')=(a+a',\,b+b',\,c+c'+ab')$. A direct commutator calculation gives $[(a,b,c),(a',b',c')]=(0,0,ab'-a'b)$, so $\gamma_2(H)=[H,H]=\{(0,0,c):c \in \mathbb Z\}$ and $\gamma_3(H)=1$. [given, algebra]

2.1 The quotient $\gamma_1(H)/\gamma_2(H)$ is generated by the images of $(1,0,0)$ and $(0,1,0)$ and is isomorphic to $\mathbb Z^2$, while $\gamma_2(H)/\gamma_3(H) \cong \mathbb Z$. Therefore [L1] gives $D(H)=1 \cdot 2 + 2 \cdot 1 = 4$. [L1, step 1.1]

2.2 Put $x=(1,0,0)$, $y=(0,1,0)$ and $z=[x,y]=(0,0,1)$, using $[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$. Every element has the unique form $x^a y^b z^d=(a,b,ab+d)$, so $x,y$ generate $H$. A word of length at most $m$ in $x^{\pm1},y^{\pm1}$ has $|a|,|b|\le m$ and $|c|\le m^2$: multiplication by $x^{\pm1}$ changes only $a$, and multiplication by $y^{\pm1}$ changes $b$ by $\pm1$ and $c$ by $\pm a$. Thus its ball has at most $(2m+1)^2(2m^2+1)$ elements. [L2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For an integer $d>0$, put $q=\lfloor\sqrt d\rfloor\ge1$ and write $d=q\ell+r$ with $0\le r<q$. Then $\ell\le q+2$ and $z^d=[x^q,y^\ell][x^r,y]$, a word of length at most $2q+2\ell+2r+2\le6q+6$. For $d<0$ invert a word for $z^{-d}$, and for $d=0$ use the empty word. Hence, for every integer $k\ge1$, all the distinct elements $x^a y^b z^d$ with $|a|,|b|\le k$ and $|d|\le k^2$ lie in the ball of radius $8k+6$. That ball has at least $(2k+1)^2(2k^2+1)$ elements. [step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 The upper bound in step 2.2 is $O(m^4)$ for $m\ge1$. For $m\ge28$, take $k=\lfloor(m-6)/8\rfloor\ge m/16$ in step 3.1 to obtain a lower bound by a positive constant times $m^4$. These bounds prove equivalence with $m^4$ for $\{x,y\}$, and [L2] transfers that growth type to every finite generating set. Together with step 2.1 this proves the example. [L2, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1] ∎
