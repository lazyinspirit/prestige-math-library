## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $K$ be a number
field of degree $n$ and signature $(r_1,r_2)$, and let
$M_K=(4/\pi)^{r_2}(n!/n^n)\sqrt{|d_K|}$. Then
$\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_K)$ is generated by the classes of the nonzero
prime ideals $\mathfrak p\subseteq\mathcal O_K$ with $N\mathfrak p\le M_K$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, a number field $K$, its ring of integers
$\mathcal O_K$, and a class $[J]\in\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_K)$.

[F1] Under the Axiom of Choice $\mathcal O_K$ is a Dedekind domain
([[cor-ring-of-integers-is-a-dedekind-domain]]).

[F2] Minkowski bound: every class in $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_K)$ contains
an integral ideal $\mathfrak b$ with $N\mathfrak b\le M_K$
([[thm-minkowski-bound-for-ideal-classes]],
[[def-ideal-class-group-of-a-domain]]).

[F3] Every nonzero fractional ideal of a Dedekind domain factors uniquely as a
product of powers of nonzero prime ideals, with only finitely many nonzero
exponents and with nonnegative exponents on integral ideals
([[thm-unique-factorisation-of-ideals-in-dedekind-domains]]).

[F4] For nonzero integral ideals $\mathfrak a,\mathfrak b$ one has
$N(\mathfrak a\mathfrak b)=N\mathfrak a\,N\mathfrak b$
([[thm-ideal-norm-is-multiplicative]]), and for a nonzero prime ideal
$\mathfrak p$ there is a rational prime $p$ with $N\mathfrak p=p^f\ge2$
([[cor-norm-of-a-prime-ideal]]).

[F5] Multiplication of classes is well defined on
$\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_K)$ and is compatible with products of
fractional ideals
([[def-ideal-class-group-of-a-domain]],
[[lem-ideal-class-group-well-defined]]).

## Proof

1.1 Let $[J]\in\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_K)$. By [F2] there is an integral ideal $\mathfrak b$ with $[\mathfrak b]=[J]$ and $N\mathfrak b\le M_K$; in particular $\mathfrak b$ is nonzero. [F2, given]

2.1 By [F1] and [F3], the nonzero integral ideal $\mathfrak b$ has a unique factorisation $\mathfrak b=\mathfrak p_1^{e_1}\cdots\mathfrak p_r^{e_r}$ with the $\mathfrak p_i$ nonzero prime ideals of $\mathcal O_K$ and integers $e_i\ge1$. [F1, F3, step 1.1]

3.1 By [F4], $N\mathfrak b=\prod_{i=1}^r(N\mathfrak p_i)^{e_i}$ with each $N\mathfrak p_i\ge2$; since every factor of the positive integer $N\mathfrak b$ is at most $N\mathfrak b$, each $N\mathfrak p_i\le N\mathfrak b\le M_K$. [F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 By [F5] the class of the product is the product of the classes: $[J]=[\mathfrak b]=\prod_{i=1}^r[\mathfrak p_i]^{e_i}$ in $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_K)$. [F5, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 Steps 3.1 and 3.2 exhibit every class $[J]$ as a product of classes of nonzero prime ideals $\mathfrak p$ with $N\mathfrak p\le M_K$; hence those classes generate $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_K)$. [step 3.1, step 3.2] ∎

## Remarks

Finite generation alone does not imply finiteness of a group. In the preceding
[[thm-finiteness-of-the-number-field-class-group]], [F2] supplies an integral
ideal of norm at most $M_K$ representing every class. The finite set of these
bounded-norm ideals therefore surjects onto $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_K)$,
which proves finiteness.
