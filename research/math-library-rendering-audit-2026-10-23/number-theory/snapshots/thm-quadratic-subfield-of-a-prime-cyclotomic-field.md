## Statement

For an odd prime $p$, the unique intermediate field
$\mathbb Q\subseteq F\subseteq\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)$ with $[F:\mathbb Q]=2$ is
$$\mathbb Q(\tau_p)=\mathbb Q\bigl(\sqrt{p^{*}}\bigr),\qquad p^{*}=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}p,$$
where $\tau_p$ is the quadratic Gauss sum attached to a chosen primitive $p$-th
root of unity $\zeta_p$. At $p=3$ the field $\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)$ itself has
degree two over $\mathbb Q$, so the intermediate field of degree two may equal
the full cyclotomic field.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An odd prime $p$, a fixed primitive $p$-th root of unity $\zeta$ in a
fixed algebraic closure of $\mathbb Q$, and the Gauss sum
$\tau:=\tau_p=\sum_{a\bmod p}(a/p)\zeta^{a}$.

[F1] $\tau=\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}(a/p)\zeta^{a}\in\mathbb Z[\zeta]\subseteq\mathbb Q(\zeta)$,
the term $a=0$ vanishing, and the chosen root $\zeta$ is part of the data
([[def-quadratic-gauss-sum-in-a-cyclotomic-field]], [[def-legendre-symbol]]).

[F2] $\tau^{2}=p^{*}=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}p$ ([[thm-quadratic-gauss-sum-square]]).

[F3] For every integer $b$ not divisible by $p$, the automorphism
$\sigma_b(\zeta)=\zeta^{b}$ satisfies $\sigma_b(\tau)=(b/p)\tau$; consequently
the Gauss sum built from another primitive $p$-th root $\zeta^{b}$ equals
$(b/p)\tau$ and generates the same field as $\tau$
([[lem-galois-action-on-the-quadratic-gauss-sum]]).

[F4] $\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)$ is a finite Galois extension of $\mathbb Q$ with
group $G:=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)/\mathbb Q)$; the embedding
$G\to(\mathbb Z/p)^{\times}$ taking $\sigma$ with $\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^{b}$ to
$b$ is an isomorphism, and $[\mathbb Q(\zeta_p):\mathbb Q]=p-1$. The group
$(\mathbb Z/p)^{\times}$ is cyclic of order $p-1$, so $G$ is cyclic of order
$p-1$
([[cor-the-galois-group-of-a-rational-cyclotomic-field]],
[[cor-unit-group-modulo-prime-is-cyclic]],
[[def-cyclotomic-extension]]).

[F5] In a finite cyclic group of order $n$, every positive divisor $d$ of $n$
is the order of exactly one subgroup, and every subgroup is the unique subgroup
of its own order; in particular the cyclic group $G$ of [F4] has exactly one
subgroup of order $(p-1)/2$
([[lem-subgroup-lattice-of-a-finite-cyclic-group]]).

[F6] Fundamental theorem of finite Galois theory: for a finite Galois extension
$K/F$ with group $G$, the maps $H\mapsto K^{H}$ and
$E\mapsto\operatorname{Gal}(K/E)$ are mutually inverse bijections between
subgroups of $G$ and intermediate fields, and $[K^{H}:F]=[G:H]$; in particular
intermediate fields of degree two over $F$ correspond bijectively to subgroups
of index two ([[thm-fundamental-theorem-of-finite-galois-theory]]).

[F7] Lagrange's theorem: for a finite group $G$ and a subgroup $H\le G$ one has
$|G|=[G:H]\,|H|$ ([[thm-lagrange]]).

[F8] The Legendre symbol on $(\mathbb Z/p)^{\times}$ is a homomorphism onto
$\{\pm1\}$, so $(b/p)^{-1}=(b/p)$ for every nonzero class $b$
([[prop-legendre-symbol-on-units-is-homomorphism]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The sum $\tau$ is an element of $\mathbb Z[\zeta]$, hence of $\mathbb Q(\zeta)$, and it satisfies $\tau^{2}=p^{*}=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}p$. [F1, F2]

1.2 If $\zeta'=\zeta^{b}$ is any primitive $p$-th root of unity, then the Gauss sum built from $\zeta'$ is $\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}(a/p)\zeta^{ab}=\sigma_b(\tau)=(b/p)\tau$, a nonzero multiple of $\tau$; hence it generates the same subfield $\mathbb Q(\tau)$. [F1, F3, F8]

1.3 The rational number $p^{*}=\pm p$ is not a square in $\mathbb Q$: if $x=a/b$ in lowest terms had $x^{2}=p^{*}$, then $a^{2}=p^{*}b^{2}$; for $p^{*}=p$ the exponent of $p$ on the left side of $a^{2}=pb^{2}$ is $2v_p(a)$, while on the right it is $1+2v_p(b)$, which is odd, a contradiction, and for $p^{*}=-p$ the left side $a^{2}/b^{2}$ is positive while $-p$ is negative. [F2]

2.1 Since $\tau^{2}=p^{*}\ne0$, we have $\tau\ne0$, and $\tau\notin\mathbb Q$ by step 1.3; as $\tau$ is a root of the degree-two polynomial $X^{2}-p^{*}\in\mathbb Q[X]$, the degree $[\mathbb Q(\tau):\mathbb Q]$ divides $2$ and is not $1$, so it equals $2$, and $\mathbb Q(\tau)\subseteq\mathbb Q(\zeta)$ by step 1.1. Moreover $\tau$ is a square root of $p^{*}$, so $\tau=\pm\sqrt{p^{*}}$ and $\mathbb Q(\tau)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{p^{*}})$. [step 1.1, step 1.3]

3.1 Let $F\subseteq\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)$ be any intermediate field with $[F:\mathbb Q]=2$ and put $H:=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)/F)$. By [F6] the subgroup $H$ has index $[G:H]=[F:\mathbb Q]=2$ in $G$, so [F7] together with $|G|=p-1$ from [F4] gives $|H|=(p-1)/2$. As $(p-1)/2$ is a positive divisor of $p-1$, [F5] shows that the cyclic group $G$ has exactly one subgroup of order $(p-1)/2$, so $H$ is that same subgroup for every such $F$; the bijection [F6] then gives $F=\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)^{H}$, the same field for every such $F$. Since step 2.1 exhibits $\mathbb Q(\tau)$ as one of them, $\mathbb Q(\tau)$ is the unique degree-two intermediate field. [F4, F5, F6, F7, step 2.1]

4.1 Combining steps 1.2 and 3.1 with the identification of step 2.1: the unique degree-two intermediate field is $\mathbb Q(\tau_p)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{p^{*}})$, independently of the chosen primitive $p$-th root, and for $p=3$ one has $[\mathbb Q(\zeta_3):\mathbb Q]=p-1=2$ by [F4], so the unique degree-two intermediate field there is $\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)$ itself. [F4, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1] ∎

## Remarks

- **Nonvanishing is the decisive input.** Without $\tau_p^{2}=p^{*}\ne0$ the
  element $\tau_p$ might generate only $\mathbb Q$; the square computation of
  [[thm-quadratic-gauss-sum-square]] is what makes the generated field
  quadratic.
- **The sign of $\tau_p$ is not needed.** Replacing $\zeta_p$ by $\zeta_p^{\,b}$
  multiplies $\tau_p$ by the Legendre sign $(b/p)$, which leaves both the square
  and the generated field unchanged; this is the content of the sign
  counterexample on the companion examples page.
