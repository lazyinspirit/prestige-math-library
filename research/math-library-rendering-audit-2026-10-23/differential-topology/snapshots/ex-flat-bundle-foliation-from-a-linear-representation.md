## Example

Assume Countable Choice $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ ([[def-countable-choice]]). Let
$A\in\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb R)$ and let $M_A=(\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^n)/\mathbb Z$
with $k\cdot(t,v)=(t+k,A^kv)$, a suspension of the linear representation
$\rho(k)=A^k$ over $B=S^1$; let $F_A$ be the suspension foliation of $M_A$. Then:

1. $M_A\to S^1$, $[t,v]\mapsto[t]$, is a smooth fibre bundle with fibre
   $\mathbb R^n$ and locally constant transition functions (a flat vector
   bundle);
2. if $Av=v$, then the leaf through $[0,v]$ is a circle and the leaf loop
   $a(t)=[(t,v)]$, $t\in[0,1]$, has holonomy germ the germ of $A^{-1}$ at $v$;
   for $A$ diagonal with all eigenvalues different from $1$, the leaf through
   the origin is a circle whose holonomy group is generated by the germ of
   $A^{-1}$ at $0$ and is nontrivial whenever $A\neq I$;
3. the foliation $F_A$ is transverse to the fibres of the bundle projection.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A matrix $A\in\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb R)$ , the quotient $M_A=(\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^n)/\mathbb Z$ for $k\cdot(t,v)=(t+k,A^kv)$, and the suspension foliation $F_A$ of $\rho(k)=A^k$ ([[def-suspension-foliation-of-a-group-action]]).

[F1] In the suspension the leaf through $v$ is $\mathbb R/K_v$ with $K_v=\{k:A^kv=v\}$, $\pi_1(L_v)\cong K_v$, and the holonomy representation is $k\mapsto\operatorname{germ}_v(A^k)$, while forward-path holonomy is $\operatorname{germ}_v(A^{-k})$ ([[prop-suspension-holonomy-is-the-germ-of-the-monodromy-action]]).

[F2] The holonomy group of a leaf is the image of its holonomy representation ([[prop-isotropy-of-the-holonomy-groupoid-is-the-leaf-holonomy-group]]).

[F3] A smooth fibre bundle is a surjective submersion locally trivialized over a cover of the base, with transition functions between local trivializations that are smooth and compatible on overlaps ([[def-smooth-fibre-bundle-and-local-trivialization]]).

[F4] The circle is the quotient $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$; the arc trivializations used below are derived directly from its quotient relation ([[def-circle-as-real-line-mod-integers]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **The bundle structure.** Take arcs $U_i$ of $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ which are the images of $I_1=(-1/4,3/4)$ and $I_2=(1/4,5/4)$. The quotient map restricts injectively on each interval and is open, since the saturation of an open set is the union of its integer translates. Thus each arc has a unique smooth interval representative $t_i$. Define $\Phi_i:U_i\times\mathbb R^n\to M_A$ by $\Phi_i([t],v)=[t_i,v]$. Every point over $U_i$ has exactly one such representative, and local quotient charts of the suspension make $\Phi_i$ and its inverse smooth. On each overlap component $t_2-t_1$ is a constant integer (here $0$ or $1$), so the change of fibre coordinate is a constant power of $A$, hence linear and smooth. These trivializations cover $M_A$ and make its projection locally the product submersion. Their locally constant linear transitions supply the asserted flat vector bundle. [F3, F4, construct]


1.2 **The leaf through a fixed vector.** If $Av=v$, then $A^kv=v$ for every $k$, so $K_v=\mathbb Z$ and by [F1] the leaf through $[0,v]$ is $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$, a circle; the leaf loop $a(t)=[(t,v)]$, $t\in[0,1]$, corresponds to the generator of $\mathbb Z$, and its holonomy germ is the germ of $A^{-1}$ at $v$. [F1]

1.3 **The leaf through the origin.** Let $A$ be diagonal with all eigenvalues different from $1$. Then $A^k0=0$ for every $k$, so $K_0=\mathbb Z$ and the leaf through $[0,0]$ is a circle. By [F1] its holonomy representation is $k\mapsto\operatorname{germ}_0(A^k)$, whose image is the cyclic group generated by the germ of $A^{-1}$ at $0$; by [F2] that image is the holonomy group. If $A\neq I$, then $A^{-1}\neq I$; since $A^{-1}$ is linear, it cannot agree with the identity on a neighbourhood of $0$ without being the identity, so the germ of $A^{-1}$ at $0$ is not the identity germ and the holonomy group is nontrivial. If $A=I$ the holonomy is trivial. [F1, F2]

2.1 **Transversality to the fibres.** The leaves of $F_A$ are locally the images of the $\mathbb R$-directions $t\mapsto(t,v)$ and the fibres of $M_A\to S^1$ are locally the images of the $\mathbb R^n$-directions $v\mapsto(t,v)$; these two directions are complementary in $T_{[t,v]}M_A$, of dimensions $1$ and $n$. Hence $F_A$ is transverse to the fibres at every point ([[def-local-transversal-to-a-regular-foliation]]). [F3, step 1.2]

3.1 **Conclusion.** Steps 1.1, 1.2, 1.3 and 2.1 establish claims 1, 2 and 3. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.1] ∎
