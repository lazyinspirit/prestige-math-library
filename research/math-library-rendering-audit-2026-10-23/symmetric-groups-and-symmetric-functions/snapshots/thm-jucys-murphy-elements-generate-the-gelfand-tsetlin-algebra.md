## Statement

Let $n\ge1$. Then
$$\mathrm{GZ}(n)=\mathbb C[X_1,\dots,X_n],$$
the unital subalgebra generated by the Jucys-Murphy elements; this algebra is
the diagonal algebra in the Young basis and is a maximal commutative
subalgebra of $\mathbb C[S_n]$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Gelfand-Tsetlin algebra $\mathrm{GZ}(n)=\langle Z(\mathbb C[S_1]),\dots,Z(\mathbb C[S_n])\rangle$ and the Jucys-Murphy elements $X_1=0$, $X_k=\sum_{j<k}(j\ k)$ for $2\le k\le n$ ([[def-gelfand-tsetlin-algebra-for-the-symmetric-group-chain]], [[def-jucys-murphy-elements-of-the-symmetric-group-algebra]], [[def-center-of-the-group-algebra]]).

[F1] $\mathrm{GZ}(n)=\bigoplus_T\mathbb C P_T$ over the standard tableaux $T$ of size $n$, where the $P_T$ are nonzero pairwise orthogonal idempotents with $\sum_TP_T=1$ projecting onto the Young lines; $\mathrm{GZ}(n)$ is a maximal commutative subalgebra of $\mathbb C[S_n]$ and is the diagonal algebra in the Young basis ([[thm-gelfand-tsetlin-algebra-is-the-diagonal-algebra-in-the-young-basis]]).

[F2] Every $P_T$ is a polynomial in $X_1,\dots,X_n$ with rational coefficients, by the interpolation recursion $P_T=P_{T\downarrow[n-1]}\prod_{c\in A(\mu),\,c\ne c_T(n)}(X_n-c)/(c_T(n)-c)$ ([[thm-primitive-tableau-idempotents-by-jucys-murphy-interpolation]]).

[F3] $X_k=T_k-T_{k-1}$ for $2\le k\le n$, where $T_m=\sum_{1\le i<j\le m}(i\ j)$ is the sum of all transpositions of $S_m$; $T_m$ is a class sum, hence central in $\mathbb C[S_m]$, and $T_1=0$ ([[def-jucys-murphy-elements-of-the-symmetric-group-algebra]], [[thm-class-sums-form-a-basis-of-the-center-of-k-g]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Inclusion $\mathrm{GZ}(n)\subseteq\mathbb C[X_1,\dots,X_n]$. By [F2] every $P_T$ lies in $\mathbb C[X_1,\dots,X_n]$, and by [F1] the $P_T$ span $\mathrm{GZ}(n)$; hence every element of $\mathrm{GZ}(n)$ is a polynomial in the $X_k$. [F1, F2, algebra]

1.2 Inclusion $\mathbb C[X_1,\dots,X_n]\subseteq\mathrm{GZ}(n)$. The element $X_1=0$ lies in $\mathrm{GZ}(n)$; for $k\ge2$, [F3] writes $X_k=T_k-T_{k-1}$ as the difference of a central element of $\mathbb C[S_k]$ and a central element of $\mathbb C[S_{k-1}]$, both of which lie in the generating centres of $\mathrm{GZ}(n)$. Since $\mathrm{GZ}(n)$ is a subalgebra, it contains every polynomial in the $X_k$. [F1, F3, given, algebra]

2.1 The two inclusions give $\mathrm{GZ}(n)=\mathbb C[X_1,\dots,X_n]$, the algebra generated by the Jucys-Murphy elements; by [F1] this algebra equals $\bigoplus_T\mathbb C P_T$, the diagonal algebra in the Young basis, and is maximal commutative in $\mathbb C[S_n]$. Finally $\mathbb C[X_1,\dots,X_n]$ is reduced and finite-dimensional: it is the algebra of functions on the finitely many content vectors of the standard tableaux, of dimension the number of standard tableaux of size $n$. [F1, step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

- **Where maximality comes from.** The maximal-commutativity assertion is inherited from the diagonal-algebra theorem and not reproved here; the content of the theorem is the equality $\mathrm{GZ}(n)=\mathbb C[X_1,\dots,X_n]$, i.e. that the chain of centres and the commuting family of Jucys-Murphy elements generate the same algebra.

- **No centre-generation input.** The inclusion $\subseteq$ uses the interpolation formula for the path idempotents rather than the classical generation of the centre by one-cycle class sums; the inclusion $\supseteq$ uses only $X_k=T_k-T_{k-1}$ and the centrality of the transposition sums. No symmetric-function input and no choice principle is used.
