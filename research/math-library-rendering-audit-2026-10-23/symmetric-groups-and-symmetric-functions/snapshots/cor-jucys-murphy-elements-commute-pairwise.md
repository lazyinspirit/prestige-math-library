## Statement

For all $1\le i,j\le n$ one has $X_iX_j=X_jX_i$ in $\mathbb Z[S_n]$, hence in
$R[S_n]$ for every commutative ring $R$; that is, the Jucys-Murphy elements
commute pairwise, and the subalgebra they generate is commutative.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $n\ge1$, the Jucys-Murphy elements $X_1,\dots,X_n\in\mathbb Z[S_n]$, and for each $k$ the transposition sum $T_k=\sum_{1\le i<j\le k}(i\ j)$ ([[def-jucys-murphy-elements-of-the-symmetric-group-algebra]]).

[F1] $X_1=0$, $X_k=T_k-T_{k-1}$ for $2\le k\le n$, and therefore $\sum_{k=2}^nX_k=T_n-T_1=T_n$; for $m\le n$ the element $X_k$ of $\mathbb Z[S_m]$ maps to $X_k$ under the inclusion $\mathbb Z[S_m]\hookrightarrow\mathbb Z[S_n]$, and the identity $\mathbb Z[S_n]\to R[S_n]$ of the base change is a unital ring homomorphism sending $X_k$ to $X_k$ ([[def-jucys-murphy-elements-of-the-symmetric-group-algebra]]).

[F2] For $h\in S_n$ and distinct $i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}$ one has $h\,(i\ j)\,h^{-1}=(h(i)\ h(j))$ ([[lem-conjugating-a-cycle-relabels-its-entries]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** induction on $n$.

1.1 Base case. For $n=1$ the only element is $X_1=0$; for $n=2$ the elements are $X_1=0$ and $X_2=(1\ 2)$. In both cases every pair among $X_1,\dots,X_n$ consists of two commuting elements, namely $0$ or the single element $X_2$. [given, base]

1.2 Induction hypothesis. Let $n\ge3$ and assume that $X_2,\dots,X_{n-1}$ commute pairwise in $\mathbb Z[S_{n-1}]$. By [F1] the inclusion of group rings is a unital ring homomorphism carrying these elements to the corresponding elements of $\mathbb Z[S_n]$, so $X_2,\dots,X_{n-1}$ commute pairwise in $\mathbb Z[S_n]$ as well. [given, ih, F1]

1.3 The element $T_n=\sum_{1\le i<j\le n}(i\ j)$ is central in $\mathbb Z[S_n]$. Indeed, for $h\in S_n$, [F2] gives $h\,(i\ j)\,h^{-1}=(h(i)\ h(j))$ for every pair $i<j$, so $hT_nh^{-1}=\sum_{i<j}(h(i)\ h(j))=\sum_{i<j}(i\ j)=T_n$, because $\{i,j\}\mapsto\{h(i),h(j)\}$ is a bijection of the set of $2$-element subsets of $\{1,\dots,n\}$. Thus $hT_n=T_nh$ for every $h\in S_n$, and extending by linearity over the basis $S_n$ gives $zT_n=T_nz$ for every $z\in\mathbb Z[S_n]$. [F2, given, algebra]

2.1 For $2\le j\le n-1$ compare the two expansions of $X_jT_n$. On the one hand $X_jT_n=T_nX_j$ by step 1.3; on the other hand, using [F1] and the induction hypothesis of step 1.2, $X_jT_n=X_j\bigl(\sum_{k=2}^{n-1}X_k+X_n\bigr)=\sum_{k=2}^{n-1}X_jX_k+X_jX_n=\sum_{k=2}^{n-1}X_kX_j+X_jX_n$, while $T_nX_j=\bigl(\sum_{k=2}^{n-1}X_k+X_n\bigr)X_j=\sum_{k=2}^{n-1}X_kX_j+X_nX_j$. Subtracting the common term $\sum_{k=2}^{n-1}X_kX_j$ gives $X_jX_n=X_nX_j$. Together with the induction hypothesis and the base case this covers every pair, so all of $X_1,\dots,X_n$ commute pairwise in $\mathbb Z[S_n]$. [step 1.2, step 1.3, F1, algebra]

3.1 Finally let $R$ be a commutative ring. The base change $\mathbb Z[S_n]\to R[S_n]$ is a unital ring homomorphism and carries $X_k$ to $X_k$ for every $k$ by [F1]; applying it to the identity $X_iX_j=X_jX_i$ of step 2.1 gives $X_iX_j=X_jX_i$ in $R[S_n]$. Hence the subalgebra generated by the $X_k$ is commutative over any commutative ring. [step 2.1, F1, discharge-induction: step 1.1] ∎

## Remarks

- **Integrality and no choice.** The argument takes place entirely in $\mathbb Z[S_n]$ and uses only bilinear expansion in the group basis and the bijection $\{i,j\}\mapsto\{h(i),h(j)\}$ on two-element subsets. No characteristic is inverted, no module is selected and no choice principle is used; the base-change sentence is the only place a general ring appears.

- **The centrality of $T_k$ in the subgroup.** The same computation with $n$ replaced by $k$ shows that each $T_k$ is central in $\mathbb Z[S_k]$, since conjugation by $S_k$ permutes the transpositions of $S_k$. This is the only property of $T_n$ used above.
