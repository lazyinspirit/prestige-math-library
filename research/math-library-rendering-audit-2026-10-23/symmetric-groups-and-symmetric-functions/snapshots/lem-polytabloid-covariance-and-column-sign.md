## Statement

For every $\lambda\vdash n$, $n\ge0$, $\lambda$-tableau $t$, and
$\sigma\in S_n$, one has
$$\kappa_{\sigma\cdot t}=\sigma\kappa_t\sigma^{-1},\qquad e_{\sigma\cdot t}=\sigma\cdot e_t.$$
For every $\gamma\in C_t$,
$$\gamma\cdot e_t=\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)e_t.$$
Consequently $S^\lambda$ is an $S_n$-submodule of $M^\lambda$ and is
generated by any one $e_t$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $n\ge0$, $\lambda\vdash n$, a $\lambda$-tableau $t$,
$\sigma\in S_n$, and $\gamma\in C_t$.

[F1] The column antisymmetrizer is the finite group-algebra sum
$\kappa_t=\sum_{c\in C_t}\operatorname{sgn}(c)c$
([[def-column-antisymmetrizer-polytabloid-and-specht-module]]).

[F2] The polytabloid is $e_t=\kappa_t\cdot\{t\}$
([[def-column-antisymmetrizer-polytabloid-and-specht-module]]).

[F3] $S^\lambda$ is the complex span of all $e_s$ for $\lambda$-tableaux $s$
([[def-column-antisymmetrizer-polytabloid-and-specht-module]]).

[F4] $C_t$ is the direct product of the symmetric groups on its pairwise
disjoint column sets ([[def-row-and-column-stabilizers-of-a-tableau]]).

[F5] $C_{\sigma\cdot t}=\sigma C_t\sigma^{-1}$
([[lem-tableau-stabilizers-transform-by-conjugation]]).

[F6] Sign is a group homomorphism
([[thm-sign-is-a-homomorphism]]).

[F7] A $\lambda$-tableau is a bijection from $[\lambda]$ to
$\{1,\ldots,n\}$ ([[def-young-tableau-standard-tableau-and-shape]]).

[F8] The tabloid module has the tabloids as basis and carries the linear
extension of their left $S_n$-action
([[def-young-subgroup-tabloid-and-permutation-module]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Write $B_j$ for the set of labels in column $j$ and $H_j=S(B_j)$. By [F4], the $H_j$ have pairwise disjoint supports and every $c\in C_t$ has a unique factorization $c=c_1\cdots c_r$ with $c_j\in H_j$. The product $\prod_{j=1}^r\kappa_{B_j}$ expands over these tuples, and by [F6] the coefficient at $c_1\cdots c_r$ is $\prod_j\operatorname{sgn}(c_j)=\operatorname{sgn}(c)$. Hence $\kappa_t=\prod_{j=1}^r\kappa_{B_j}$, where $\kappa_{B_j}=\sum_{h\in H_j}\operatorname{sgn}(h)h$. If $n=0$, both sides are the empty product $1$. [given, F1, F4, F6, algebra]

1.2 For every $c\in C_t$, [F6] gives $\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma c\sigma^{-1})=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\operatorname{sgn}(c)\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma^{-1})=\operatorname{sgn}(c)$, since the homomorphism property makes $\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma^{-1})=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)^{-1}$. [given, F6, algebra]

1.3 For $\gamma\in C_t$, reindex [F1] by $d=\gamma c$. Since $\operatorname{sgn}(c)=\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma^{-1}d)=\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)\operatorname{sgn}(d)$, this gives $\gamma\kappa_t=\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)\kappa_t$. Applying both sides to $\{t\}$ and using [F2] proves $\gamma\cdot e_t=\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)e_t$. [given, F1, F2, F6, algebra]

2.1 By [F5], conjugation by $\sigma$ bijects $C_t$ with $C_{\sigma\cdot t}$. Reindexing [F1] by $d=\sigma c\sigma^{-1}$ and using step 1.2 gives $\kappa_{\sigma\cdot t}=\sigma\kappa_t\sigma^{-1}$. [given, F1, F5, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 From [F2], step 2.1, and the left module action [F8], $e_{\sigma\cdot t}=\kappa_{\sigma\cdot t}\cdot\{\sigma\cdot t\}=\sigma\kappa_t\sigma^{-1}\cdot(\sigma\cdot\{t\})=\sigma\cdot(\kappa_t\cdot\{t\})=\sigma\cdot e_t$. [given, F2, F8, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Every spanning vector of $S^\lambda$ has the form $e_s$ by [F3], and step 3.1 sends it under $\sigma$ to $e_{\sigma\cdot s}$, which is again a spanning vector. Thus $S^\lambda$ is an $S_n$-submodule of $M^\lambda$. [given, F3, F8, step 3.1]

5.1 Fix any $\lambda$-tableau $t$ and let $s$ be any other one. By [F7], the rule $\sigma(t(i,j))=s(i,j)$ defines a unique permutation $\sigma\in S_n$; for $n=0$ it is the identity. Then $s=\sigma\cdot t$ and step 3.1 gives $e_s=\sigma\cdot e_t$. Therefore the orbit of $e_t$ spans all the generators of $S^\lambda$, so $e_t$ generates $S^\lambda$ as an $S_n$-module. This holds for every choice of $t$. [given, F3, F7, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
