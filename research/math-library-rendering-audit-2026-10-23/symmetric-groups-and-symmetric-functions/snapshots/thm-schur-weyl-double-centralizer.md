## Statement

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional complex vector space, let $n\ge0$, and let
$E:=V^{\otimes n}$ carry the left place action of $S_n$
([[def-commuting-symmetric-and-linear-actions-on-tensor-power]]). Let $A$ be
the image of the algebra homomorphism $\mathbb C[S_n]\to\operatorname{End}(E)$
extending the place action, and let
$B:=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{g^{\otimes n}:g\in\operatorname{GL}(V)\}$
be the linear span of the diagonal operators
$g^{\otimes n}(v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes v_n)=gv_1\otimes\cdots\otimes gv_n$.
Then:

1. **(Mutual centralizers.)** $B=\{F\in\operatorname{End}(E):F a=aF\text{ for
   all }a\in A\}$ and $A=\{F\in\operatorname{End}(E):F b=bF\text{ for all
   }b\in B\}$.
2. **(Uniqueness of the identification.)** $B$ is a unital
   $\mathbb C$-subalgebra of $\operatorname{End}(E)$ and equals the image of
   the diagonal action of the universal enveloping algebra
   $U(\mathfrak{gl}(V))$, that is the unital subalgebra generated by the
   operators $\Delta(T)=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes T\otimes
   \mathbf 1^{\otimes(n-i)}$, $T\in\operatorname{End}(V)$
   ([[lem-tensor-place-operators-span-the-symmetric-centralizer]]).

All statements include $n=0$, where $E=\mathbb C$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** a finite-dimensional complex vector space $V$, an integer $n\ge0$, $E=V^{\otimes n}$ with its left $S_n$-action, the algebra $A$ and the space $B$ of the Statement.

[F1] The left place action of $S_n$ on $E$ and the diagonal action of $g\in\operatorname{GL}(V)$ commute, and $\Delta(T)=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes T\otimes\mathbf 1^{\otimes(n-i)}$ is linear in $T$ ([[def-commuting-symmetric-and-linear-actions-on-tensor-power]]). It is a Lie algebra homomorphism: operators in distinct tensor positions commute, and in each position the commutator is $[T,U]=TU-UT$, so $[\Delta(T),\Delta(U)]=\Delta([T,U])$.

[F2] The map $\Psi:({\operatorname{End}V})^{\otimes n}\to\operatorname{End}(E)$ is a linear isomorphism, equivariant for the place action and conjugation, and $$\operatorname{End}_{S_n}(E)=\Psi\bigl(\operatorname{span}\{T^{\otimes n}:T\in\operatorname{End}(V)\}\bigr)=\Psi\bigl(\operatorname{span}\{g^{\otimes n}:g\in\operatorname{GL}(V)\}\bigr)=A_n,$$ where $\operatorname{End}_{S_n}(E)$ is the centralizer of the place action and $A_n$ is the unital subalgebra generated by the $\Delta(T)$, which is the image of the diagonal action of $U(\mathfrak{gl}(V))$ ([[lem-tensor-place-operators-span-the-symmetric-centralizer]]).

[F3] Every finite-dimensional $\mathbb C[S_n]$-module is completely reducible ([[thm-maschkes-theorem-for-finite-groups-over-fields-whose-characteristic-does-not-divide-the-group-order]]).

[F4] The modules $\{S^\lambda:\lambda\vdash n\}$ form a complete irredundant list of the finite-dimensional irreducible complex $S_n$-representations ([[thm-complex-irreducibles-of-symmetric-groups-are-specht-modules]]).

[F5] A nonzero intertwiner between irreducible representations is an isomorphism, and every endomorphism of an irreducible representation over an algebraically closed field is scalar ([[cor-schurs-lemma-for-irreducible-representations]], [[cor-endomorphisms-of-an-irreducible-over-an-algebraically-closed-field-are-scalars]]).

[F6] For a finite-dimensional completely reducible representation of a group the isotypic decomposition into the sums of copies of the distinct irreducible subrepresentations is unique; and for an irreducible $S$ and an $S$-isotypical module $U$, evaluation $S\otimes\operatorname{Hom}_{S_n}(S,U)\to U$, $s\otimes f\mapsto f(s)$, is an isomorphism under which every $S_n$-map $U\to U'$ is uniquely of the form $E_{U'}(1_S\otimes a)E_U^{-1}$ for a linear $a:\operatorname{Hom}_{S_n}(S,U)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{S_n}(S,U')$, with $\operatorname{Hom}$-spaces carrying the trivial action and composition preserved ([[thm-isotypic-decomposition-of-a-completely-reducible-representation-is-unique]], [[lem-isotypical-evaluation-and-subspaces-of-multiplicity-spaces]]).

[F7] $\mathbb C[S_n]\cong\prod_{i=1}^rM_{n_i}(\mathbb C)$ for positive integers $n_i$, and the simple left modules over such a product are the column modules $\mathbb C^{n_i}$, one isomorphism class per factor, each supported on exactly one factor ([[thm-group-algebra-decomposes-as-a-product-of-matrix-algebras-over-an-algebraically-closed-field]], [[thm-simple-modules-over-semisimple-rings]]).

[F8] Under the correspondence between $k$-linear $G$-actions and compatible left $k[G]$-module structures, the subrepresentations of a representation $V$ are exactly the $k[G]$-submodules and $V$ is irreducible if and only if it is simple as a $k[G]$-module; $G$-equivariant maps are exactly the $k[G]$-module homomorphisms ([[thm-group-actions-and-group-ring-modules-correspond]], [[cor-subrepresentations-correspond-to-submodules-and-irreducibility-to-simplicity]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 [construct] Recall that $A$ is a unital subalgebra of $\operatorname{End}(E)$, because it is the image of a unital algebra homomorphism, and that $B$ is a unital subalgebra, because $g^{\otimes n}h^{\otimes n}=(gh)^{\otimes n}$ for $g,h\in\operatorname{GL}(V)$; both contain $\mathbf 1_E$ (for $B$ take $g=1_V$, and for $n=0$ note $g^{\otimes0}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb C}$ for every $g$ and $\operatorname{GL}(V)$ is nonempty). [F1, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 By [F3] the $\mathbb C[S_n]$-module $E$ is completely reducible, so by [F4] and the uniqueness of the isotypic decomposition in [F6] there are subspaces $E_\lambda$ for $\lambda$ in the finite set $\Lambda:=\{\lambda\vdash n:\operatorname{Hom}_{S_n}(S^\lambda,E)\ne0\}$ with $E=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}E_\lambda$, each $E_\lambda$ is $\lambda$-isotypical, and the evaluation maps of [F6] give $S_n$-isomorphisms $E_\lambda\cong S^\lambda\otimes M_\lambda$ with $M_\lambda:=\operatorname{Hom}_{S_n}(S^\lambda,E)$ carrying the trivial action; consequently the action of $\sigma\in S_n$ on $E_\lambda$ corresponds to $\rho_\lambda(\sigma)\otimes\mathbf 1_{M_\lambda}$, where $\rho_\lambda$ is the action on the irreducible $S^\lambda$. [F3, F4, F6, construct, algebra]

1.3 The simple left $\mathbb C[S_n]$-modules are, by [F7], the column modules of the factors of a decomposition $\mathbb C[S_n]\cong\prod_{i=1}^rM_{n_i}(\mathbb C)$, one class per factor and each supported on one factor; by [F8] the irreducible complex representations of the group $S_n$ are exactly the simple left $\mathbb C[S_n]$-modules, so by [F4] the isomorphism classes of simple left $\mathbb C[S_n]$-modules are exactly the classes $[S^\lambda]$, $\lambda\vdash n$. Hence the factors are indexed by the partitions of $n$, with $n_i=\dim_{\mathbb C}S^\lambda$ for the factor attached to $\lambda$, and $\dim_{\mathbb C}\mathbb C[S_n]=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}(\dim_{\mathbb C}S^\lambda)^2$. [F4, F7, F8, algebra]

1.4 An endomorphism $T\in\operatorname{End}(S\otimes M)$ of a tensor product, $S,M$ finite-dimensional over $\mathbb C$ with $M\ne0$, commuting with $\mathbf 1_S\otimes b$ for all $b\in\operatorname{End}(M)$ is of the form $T=x\otimes\mathbf 1_M$ for a unique $x\in\operatorname{End}(S)$: choose bases $s_1,\ldots,s_p$ of $S$ and $m_1,\ldots,m_q$ of $M$ (both finite, with $q\ge1$), write $T(s\otimes m_1)=\sum_{j=1}^qx_j(s)\otimes m_j$ with well-defined linear maps $x_j:S\to S$; for each $j$ let $b_j\in\operatorname{End}(M)$ be given on the basis by $b_j(m_1)=m_j$ and $b_j(m_i)=0$ for $i\ge2$; then $T(s\otimes m_j)=T((\mathbf 1\otimes b_j)(s\otimes m_1))=(\mathbf 1\otimes b_j)T(s\otimes m_1)=x_1(s)\otimes m_j$, so $T=x_1\otimes\mathbf 1_M$ on all basis vectors, and conversely every $x\otimes\mathbf 1_M$ commutes with the operators $\mathbf 1\otimes b$. [construct, algebra]

2.1 The first displayed identity of claim 1 holds: a map $F\in\operatorname{End}(E)$ commutes with every $a\in A$ exactly when it commutes with $\sigma$ for every $\sigma\in S_n$, because $A$ is the linear span of the place operators $\sigma$; hence the centralizer of $A$ is $\operatorname{End}_{S_n}(E)$, which equals $B$ by [F2]. The same fact of [F2] identifies $B$ with the image of the diagonal action of $U(\mathfrak{gl}(V))$, so claim 2 holds; in particular $B$ is a subalgebra, in agreement with step 1.1. [F2, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 The canonical algebra homomorphism $\Phi:\mathbb C[S_n]\to\prod_{\lambda\vdash n}\operatorname{End}(S^\lambda)$, $\Phi(x)=(\rho_\lambda(x))_{\lambda}$, is an isomorphism. It is injective: if $x$ acts as $0$ on every $S^\lambda$, then under the product decomposition of step 1.3 the element $x$ has a component in each factor which annihilates that factor's own column module, and so is $0$, so $x=0$; here [F8] identifies the column module of the factor attached to $\lambda$ with the simple module $S^\lambda$, on which $x$ acts as $\rho_\lambda(x)$ by definition of $\Phi$. It is then bijective because, by step 1.3 and $\dim_{\mathbb C}\operatorname{End}(S^\lambda)=(\dim_{\mathbb C}S^\lambda)^2$, source and target have the same finite dimension $\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}(\dim_{\mathbb C}S^\lambda)^2$. [F7, F8, step 1.3, construct, algebra]

3.1 The algebra $\operatorname{End}_{S_n}(E)$ is $\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\operatorname{End}(M_\lambda)$: an $S_n$-endomorphism of $E$ maps each isotypic component $E_\lambda$ into itself, since the image of a copy of $S^\lambda$ is $0$ or a copy of $S^\lambda$ by [F5] and [F4]; on $E_\lambda$ it is, by [F6] and step 1.2, exactly the operator induced by a unique $a_\lambda\in\operatorname{End}(M_\lambda)$ on the second tensor factor; maps between distinct components $E_\lambda\to E_\mu$ are zero by [F5] and [F4] because $S^\lambda$ and $S^\mu$ are non-isomorphic for $\lambda\ne\mu$; and the identifications preserve composition, so this is an algebra isomorphism under which $a_\lambda$ corresponds to the operator $\mathbf 1_{S^\lambda}\otimes a_\lambda$ on $E_\lambda$. In particular, by step 2.1, $B$ is exactly the set of operators $\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}(\mathbf 1_{S^\lambda}\otimes b_\lambda)$ with $b_\lambda\in\operatorname{End}(M_\lambda)$. [F4, F5, F6, step 2.1, step 1.2, algebra]

3.2 Under the isomorphism $\Phi$ of step 2.2 and the isomorphisms $E_\lambda\cong S^\lambda\otimes M_\lambda$ of step 1.2, the action of $x\in\mathbb C[S_n]$ on $E=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}E_\lambda$ is $\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}(\rho_\lambda(x)\otimes\mathbf 1_{M_\lambda})$, the factors with $\lambda\notin\Lambda$ acting on no summand; hence the image $A$ of this action map is exactly $$A=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\bigl(\operatorname{End}(S^\lambda)\otimes\mathbf 1_{M_\lambda}\bigr)\subseteq\operatorname{End}(E),$$ the direct sum of the indicated block subalgebras indexed by $\Lambda$: indeed the projection $\prod_{\lambda\vdash n}\operatorname{End}(S^\lambda)\to\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\operatorname{End}(S^\lambda)$ is surjective and $\Phi$ is an isomorphism. [step 1.2, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 Claim 1's second identity holds. Let $F\in\operatorname{End}(E)$ commute with every element of $B$; decompose $F$ as a block matrix $(F_{\mu\lambda})_{\mu,\lambda\in\Lambda}$ with $F_{\mu\lambda}\in\operatorname{Hom}(S^\lambda\otimes M_\lambda,S^\mu\otimes M_\mu)$ using the decomposition of step 1.2. Commuting with the operator $\mathbf 1_{S^\lambda}\otimes b_\lambda$ on the $\lambda$-block and $\mathbf 1_{S^\mu}\otimes b_\mu$ on the $\mu$-block gives $F_{\mu\lambda}(\mathbf 1_{S^\lambda}\otimes b_\lambda)=(\mathbf 1_{S^\mu}\otimes b_\mu)F_{\mu\lambda}$. If $\mu\ne\lambda$, use $b_\lambda=\mathbf 1_{M_\lambda}$, $b_\mu=0$ (available in $B$ by step 3.1 and $\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda$), obtaining $F_{\mu\lambda}=0$. Thus every off-diagonal block vanishes. For each $\lambda$, the diagonal block $F_{\lambda\lambda}$ commutes with $\mathbf 1_{S^\lambda}\otimes b$ for every $b\in\operatorname{End}(M_\lambda)$, since $B$ contains the operators supported on that single block by step 3.1. [F6, step 1.2, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 By steps 4.1 and 1.4 the centralizer of $B$ consists exactly of the elements $\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}(x_\lambda\otimes\mathbf 1_{M_\lambda})$ with $x_\lambda\in\operatorname{End}(S^\lambda)$, which is exactly $A$ by step 3.2; this proves the second identity of claim 1, and claim 2 was proved in step 2.1. [step 3.2, step 4.1, step 1.4, algebra]

6.1 Boundary and choice audit. For $n=0$ one has $E=V^{\otimes0}=\mathbb C$, $A=B=\mathbb C\,\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb C}=\operatorname{End}(E)$ and both centralizers equal the whole of $\operatorname{End}(E)$, in agreement with claims 1 and 2; the case is covered by the argument with $\Lambda=\{(\varnothing)\}$ when $\operatorname{Hom}_{S_0}(S^\varnothing,\mathbb C)=\mathbb C$. For $V=0$ and $n\ge1$ one has $E=0$, so $\operatorname{End}(E)=0=A=B$ by [F2] and all assertions hold; here $\Lambda=\varnothing$, the products over $\Lambda$ are the zero algebra, and steps 4.1 and 1.4 are vacuous. For $V\ne0$ and $n\ge1$, every $\lambda\in\Lambda$ has $M_\lambda\ne0$ by definition, so step 1.4 applies with $M=M_\lambda$. All bases and decompositions used are attached to finite-dimensional spaces and to the finitely many partitions of $n$; the decomposition of $E$ and the factors of $\mathbb C[S_n]$ are canonical, and no choice principle is invoked. [F2, F6, step 1.2, step 4.1, step 1.4, given] ∎

## Remarks

- **Two halves, two mechanisms.** The identity $\operatorname{End}_A(E)=B$ is the polarization lemma ([[lem-tensor-place-operators-span-the-symmetric-centralizer]]), a direct computation with symmetric tensors. The reverse identity $\operatorname{End}_B(E)=A$ goes through the semisimple structure of $\mathbb C[S_n]$: the isotypic decomposition of $E$ makes both centralizers products of full matrix algebras, and the two products are exchanged by the evaluation isomorphism ([[lem-isotypical-evaluation-and-subspaces-of-multiplicity-spaces]]).

- **No Lie machinery is imported.** The only Lie-theoretic input is the identification of $B$ with the image of the diagonal $U(\mathfrak{gl}(V))$-action, which is proved inside [[lem-tensor-place-operators-span-the-symmetric-centralizer]] by Newton identities; no classification or highest-weight theory is used here.

- **What the theorem does not say.** Nothing is asserted about the multiplicity spaces $M_\lambda$ beyond their occurrence: their irreducibility, pairwise inequivalence and highest weights are proved via the length cutoff and the local highest-weight computation later on this page.
