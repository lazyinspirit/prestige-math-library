## Statement

Let $n\ge1$, let $\lambda\vdash n$, let $F$ be any field, and let
$r_1<r_2<\cdots<r_m$ be the rows of the removable corners of $\lambda$, so
that $\lambda^{(i)}$ and the deletion map
$\theta_i:M^\lambda_F\to M^{\lambda^{(i)}}_F$ are as in
[[def-corner-order-and-specht-deletion-map]]. Let
$0=V_0\subseteq V_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq V_m=S^\lambda_F$ be the
$S_{n-1}$-stable subspaces of $S^\lambda_F$ of
[[lem-specht-branching-subspaces-are-invariant]], so that $V_i$ is spanned by
the standard polytabloids $e_t$ whose tableau $t$ carries the entry $n$ in one
of the rows $r_1,\dots,r_i$. For a standard $\lambda$-tableau $t$ whose entry
$n$ lies in row $r_i$, let $\bar t$ denote the tableau of shape
$\lambda^{(i)}$ obtained from $t$ by deleting the box of $n$ (all other
entries unchanged), which is again standard. Then for every $1\le i\le m$:

1. **(Values on the standard basis.)** One has $\theta_i(e_t)=e_{\bar t}$ for
   every standard $\lambda$-tableau $t$ with $n$ in row $r_i$, and
   $\theta_i(e_t)=0$ for every standard $\lambda$-tableau $t$ whose entry $n$
   lies in a row $r_l$ with $l<i$.
2. **(Image and kernel.)** $\theta_i$ restricts to a surjection
   $\theta_i|_{V_i}:V_i\to S^{\lambda^{(i)}}_F$ with kernel $V_{i-1}$.
3. **(The quotient.)** $\theta_i|_{V_i}$ induces an isomorphism of
   $S_{n-1}$-modules $V_i/V_{i-1}\cong S^{\lambda^{(i)}}_F$, where
   $S^{\lambda^{(i)}}_F$ carries its natural $S_{n-1}$-action on the labels
   $1,\dots,n-1$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** an integer $n\ge1$, a partition $\lambda\vdash n$, a field $F$, the removable rows $r_1<\cdots<r_m$ of $\lambda$, the partitions $\lambda^{(i)}$ and maps $\theta_i$, the subspaces $V_i$ of the Statement, and the standard polytabloids of the various shapes.

[F1] $M^\lambda_F$ is free with the $\lambda$-tabloids as basis, the left action satisfies $\sigma\cdot\{t\}=\{\sigma\cdot t\}$, $e_t=\kappa_t\cdot\{t\}=\sum_{\gamma\in C_t}\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)\,\{\gamma\cdot t\}$, $S^\lambda_F$ is the $F$-span of all polytabloids, $C_t\cap R_t=\{1\}$, $e_t\ne0$, $\gamma\cdot e_t=\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)e_t$ for $\gamma\in C_t$, and $e_{\sigma\cdot t}=\sigma\cdot e_t$, so that $S^\lambda_F$ is an $S_n$-submodule generated by any one $e_t$; the sign is read in $F$ through $\pm1$ ([[def-polytabloid-specht-module-over-an-arbitrary-field]], [[lem-polytabloid-covariance-and-column-sign]]).

[F2] For every field $F$ the standard polytabloids $\{e_t:t\text{ standard}\}$ are an $F$-basis of $S^\lambda_F$; consequently they are linearly independent and $\dim_F S^\lambda_F=f^\lambda$, the number of standard $\lambda$-tableaux ([[lem-integral-specht-garnir-straightening-and-field-basis]], claim 3).

[F3] $\theta_i:M^\lambda_F\to M^{\lambda^{(i)}}_F$ is $F$-linear and $S_{n-1}$-linear; on a tabloid $\{u\}$ it equals the tabloid obtained by deleting the label $n$ when $n$ lies in row $r_i$ of $\{u\}$, and equals $0$ otherwise; in particular $\theta_i(\gamma\cdot\{u\})=\gamma\cdot\theta_i(\{u\})$ for $\gamma\in S_{n-1}$ ([[def-corner-order-and-specht-deletion-map]]).

[F4] $V_i=\operatorname{span}_F\{e_t:t\text{ standard},\ n\text{ lies in one of the rows }r_1,\dots,r_i\}$, $V_0=0$, $V_0\subseteq V_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq V_m=S^\lambda_F$, and each $V_i$ is stable under the action of $S_{n-1}$ ([[lem-specht-branching-subspaces-are-invariant]]).

[F5] The box occupied by $n$ in a standard $\lambda$-tableau is removable, and deleting it leaves a standard tableau of size $n-1$ ([[lem-largest-entry-of-a-standard-tableau-is-removable]]).

[F6] The removable nodes of $\lambda$ are exactly the nodes $(j,\lambda_j)$ with $\lambda_j>\lambda_{j+1}$ (where $\lambda_{k+1}:=0$), the removable rows are $r_1<\cdots<r_m$, and $\lambda^{(i)}$ is $\lambda$ with the corner $x_i=(r_i,\lambda_{r_i})$ deleted; a node $(j,\ell)$ belongs to the diagram of $\lambda$ if and only if $\ell\le\lambda_j$, equivalently $j\le\lambda'_\ell$, and column $\ell$ has height $\lambda'_\ell$; also $\ell(\lambda)=\lambda'_1\ge\lambda'_2\ge\cdots$ ([[def-removable-and-addable-nodes-of-a-partition]], [[def-corner-order-and-specht-deletion-map]], [[def-partition-young-diagram-and-conjugate-partition]]).

[F7] A $\lambda$-tableau is a bijection from the boxes of the diagram of $\lambda$ to $\{1,\dots,n\}$, standard when its entries strictly increase along rows and down columns, and the left action is $(\sigma\cdot t)(i,j)=\sigma(t(i,j))$; the row stabilizer $R_t$ and column stabilizer $C_t=\prod_jS(B_j)$ are built from the row sets $A_i$ and the column sets $B_j=\{t(i,j):1\le i\le\lambda'_j\}$ ([[def-young-tableau-standard-tableau-and-shape]], [[def-row-and-column-stabilizers-of-a-tableau]]).

[F8] $\operatorname{sgn}$ is a homomorphism, and for a permutation of $\{1,\dots,n-1\}$ extended to $\{1,\dots,n\}$ by fixing $n$, the inversion pairs are the same in both groups, so its sign is unchanged ([[thm-sign-is-a-homomorphism]], [[def-inversions-inversion-number-and-sign]]).

[F9] A linear map $T:U\to W$ induces a linear isomorphism $U/\ker T\to\operatorname{im}T$ ([[thm-first-isomorphism-theorem-for-vector-spaces]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** constructive.

1.1 [construct] For a $\lambda$-tableau $u$ let $\rho(u)$ be the row of the entry $n$ in $u$, so that by [F3] $\theta_i(\{u\})\ne0$ if and only if $\rho(u)=r_i$, in which case $\theta_i(\{u\})$ is $\{u\}$ with $n$ deleted. Put $W_i:=\operatorname{span}_F\{e_t:t\text{ standard},\ \rho(t)=r_i\}\subseteq V_i$, so that $V_i=V_{i-1}+W_i$ by [F4]; and for a standard $t$ with $\rho(t)=r_i$ let $\bar t$ be the tableau of shape $\lambda^{(i)}$ obtained from $t$ by deleting the box of $n$. [given, F1, F3, F4, construct]

1.2 Let $t$ be standard with $\rho(t)=r_l$, and let $c$ be the column of $n$ in $t$. Since $n$ is the largest entry of $t$, it is the last entry of its row and the bottom entry of its column, so $n$ occupies the box $(r_l,\lambda_{r_l})$ and $c=\lambda_{r_l}$; by [F5] and [F6] this box is removable, so $\lambda_{r_l}>\lambda_{r_l+1}$, and column $c$ has boxes in exactly the rows $1,\dots,r_l$. Indeed $\lambda'_c$ counts the rows $j$ with $\lambda_j\ge c=\lambda_{r_l}$, and by weak decrease and $\lambda_{r_l}>\lambda_{r_l+1}$ these are exactly $j\le r_l$. In particular $\lambda'_c=r_l$ and the column set $B_c=\{t(1,c),\dots,t(r_l,c)\}$ has $n$ as its largest element. [F5, F6, F7, given, algebra]

2.1 For every standard $\lambda$-tableau $t$ the row $\rho(t)$ is one of the removable rows $r_1,\dots,r_m$: the box of $n$ is removable by [F5], and the removable nodes are the corners $(j,\lambda_j)$ by [F6]. Hence $V_i=\operatorname{span}_F\{e_t:t\text{ standard},\ \rho(t)\in\{r_1,\dots,r_i\}\}$, the polytabloids $e_t$ with $\rho(t)=r_i$ are linearly independent and form an $F$-basis of $W_i$, and the sets $\{t:\rho(t)=r_l,\ l\le i-1\}$ and $\{t:\rho(t)=r_i\}$ are disjoint. [F2, F4, F5, F6, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Let $t$ be standard with $\rho(t)=r_l$ and column $c$ of $n$, and let $\gamma\in C_t$. Since $\gamma$ preserves every column set by [F7], $\gamma^{-1}(n)\in B_c$; and the row of $n$ in the tabloid $\gamma\cdot\{t\}=\{\gamma\cdot t\}$ is the row of $\gamma^{-1}(n)$ in $t$, so by [F3] $\theta_i(\gamma\cdot\{t\})\ne0$ if and only if $\gamma^{-1}(n)\in A_{r_i}\cap B_c$, where $A_{r_i}$ is the entry set of row $r_i$ of $t$. Now $A_{r_i}\cap B_c$ is the set of entries in the box $(r_i,c)$, which by [F6] is a single label when $r_i\le\lambda'_c$ and is empty otherwise. If $l=i$ then $r_i=r_l=\lambda'_c$ by step 1.2, the box $(r_i,c)$ exists and contains $n$, so $A_{r_i}\cap B_c=\{n\}$; if $l<i$ then $r_i>r_l=\lambda'_c$, the box $(r_i,c)$ does not exist, and $A_{r_i}\cap B_c=\varnothing$. [F3, F6, F7, step 1.2, algebra]

2.3 For each $i$ the assignment $t\mapsto\bar t$ is a bijection from the set of standard $\lambda$-tableaux with $\rho(t)=r_i$ onto the set of standard $\lambda^{(i)}$-tableaux. It is well defined: by step 1.2 such a $t$ carries $n$ in the removable box $x_i=(r_i,\lambda_{r_i})$, and deleting that box leaves a standard tableau of shape $\lambda^{(i)}$ by [F5] and [F6]. Conversely, given a standard $\lambda^{(i)}$-tableau $u$, insert the label $n$ into the box $x_i$; since $x_i$ is the last box of row $r_i$ and the bottom box of its column in the diagram of $\lambda$ (the column height is $\lambda'_{\lambda_{r_i}}=r_i$, as the argument of step 1.2 shows for the corner), appending the largest label $n$ preserves both monotonicities of [F7], so the result is a standard $\lambda$-tableau with $\rho(t)=r_i$, and the two constructions are inverse to each other. [F5, F6, F7, step 1.1, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 Let $t$ be standard with $\rho(t)=r_i$. By step 2.2 the only $\gamma\in C_t$ with $\theta_i(\gamma\cdot\{t\})\ne0$ are those with $\gamma^{-1}(n)=n$, that is $\gamma\in C_t\cap S_{n-1}$; for such $\gamma$ one has $\theta_i(\gamma\cdot\{t\})=\gamma\cdot\theta_i(\{t\})=\gamma\cdot\{\bar t\}$ by the $S_{n-1}$-linearity of $\theta_i$ in [F3]. Hence $\theta_i(e_t)=\sum_{\gamma\in C_t\cap S_{n-1}}\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)\,\gamma\cdot\{\bar t\}$. Now $C_t\cap S_{n-1}=C_{\bar t}$: by [F7] $C_t=\prod_jS(B_j)$ for the column sets $B_j$ of $t$, so its elements fixing $n$ are the products of permutations of the $B_j$ with $j\ne c$ and of a permutation of $B_c\setminus\{n\}$, and $B_c\setminus\{n\}$ is the column set of $\bar t$ in column $c$ because the deleted box is the bottom box of column $c$ in $t$ (step 1.2), the other column sets being unchanged. By [F8] the sign of such a $\gamma$ is the same computed in $S_{n-1}$, so $\theta_i(e_t)=\sum_{\gamma\in C_{\bar t}}\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)\,\gamma\cdot\{\bar t\}=\kappa_{\bar t}\cdot\{\bar t\}=e_{\bar t}$ in $M^{\lambda^{(i)}}_F$, which is the first assertion of claim 1. [F1, F3, F7, F8, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.2, algebra]

3.2 Let $t$ be standard with $\rho(t)=r_l$ and $l<i$. By step 2.2 every $\gamma\in C_t$ satisfies $\theta_i(\gamma\cdot\{t\})=0$, so all terms of $\theta_i(e_t)=\sum_{\gamma\in C_t}\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)\theta_i(\gamma\cdot\{t\})$ vanish and $\theta_i(e_t)=0$, which is the second assertion of claim 1. [F1, F3, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 By steps 3.1, 3.2 and 2.3, $\theta_i$ annihilates $V_{i-1}$ and sends the basis $\{e_t:\rho(t)=r_i\}$ of $W_i$ onto the set of standard polytabloids $e_u$ of shape $\lambda^{(i)}$, which by [F2] is an $F$-basis of $S^{\lambda^{(i)}}_F$; hence $\theta_i(W_i)=S^{\lambda^{(i)}}_F$. Since $V_i=V_{i-1}+W_i$ (step 1.1) and $\theta_i(V_{i-1})=0$ by step 3.2, also $\theta_i(V_i)=S^{\lambda^{(i)}}_F$: the restriction $\theta_i|_{V_i}$ is surjective. [F2, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, step 2.3, algebra]

4.2 The map $\theta_i|_{W_i}:W_i\to S^{\lambda^{(i)}}_F$ carries the $F$-basis $\{e_t:\rho(t)=r_i\}$ of $W_i$ (step 2.1) onto the $F$-basis $\{e_u:u\text{ standard of shape }\lambda^{(i)}\}$ of $S^{\lambda^{(i)}}_F$ (steps 3.1 and 2.3 and [F2]), so it is an isomorphism of $F$-vector spaces; in particular $W_i\cap\ker\theta_i=0$. [F2, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 2.3, algebra]

5.1 Let $v\in V_i$ with $\theta_i(v)=0$. By step 1.1 write $v=v'+w$ with $v'\in V_{i-1}$ and $w\in W_i$. Since $\theta_i(v')=0$ by step 3.2, linearity gives $\theta_i(w)=0$, so $w=0$ by step 4.2 and $v=v'\in V_{i-1}$. Conversely $V_{i-1}\subseteq\ker\theta_i|_{V_i}$ by step 3.2, so the kernel is exactly $V_{i-1}$; with the surjectivity of step 4.1 this proves claim 2. [step 1.1, step 3.2, step 4.1, step 4.2, algebra]

6.1 The restriction $\theta_i|_{V_i}:V_i\to S^{\lambda^{(i)}}_F$ is a linear map with kernel $V_{i-1}$ and image $S^{\lambda^{(i)}}_F$ (claim 2), so by [F9] it induces an $F$-linear isomorphism $V_i/V_{i-1}\to S^{\lambda^{(i)}}_F$. This isomorphism is $S_{n-1}$-equivariant: $\theta_i$ is $S_{n-1}$-linear by [F3], and $V_i$, $V_{i-1}$ are $S_{n-1}$-stable by [F4], so the induced map on the quotient intertwines the quotient action with the natural $S_{n-1}$-action on $S^{\lambda^{(i)}}_F$. This proves claim 3. [F3, F4, F9, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 Boundary and choice audit. For $n\ge1$ there is at least one removable row by [F6], so $m\ge1$ and the list $r_1<\cdots<r_m$ is a finite nonempty list; for $n=1$ one has $\lambda=(1)$, $m=1$, $r_1=1$, $\lambda^{(1)}=\varnothing$, the unique standard tableau $t$ has $n=1$ in row $r_1$ and $C_t=C_{\bar t}=\{1\}$, and claim 1 reads $\theta_1(e_t)=e_{\bar t}$, the standard basis vector $e_{\bar t}=1$ of $S^\varnothing_F=F$, so that $V_1=S^{(1)}_F\to F$ is an isomorphism with kernel $V_0=0$, in agreement with claims 2 and 3. The argument is uniform in the field: it uses the standard basis on both sides, available over any $F$ by [F2] with no division and no characteristic hypothesis (in particular it covers $F$ of characteristic $2$), together with the finite groups $C_t$ and the explicit deletion and insertion of the largest label, so no choice principle is invoked. This proves claims 1, 2 and 3. [F2, F3, F4, F6, step 2.3, step 4.1, step 5.1, discharge-construct] ∎

## Remarks

- **Where the corner condition is used.** Both values in claim 1 come from the single observation of step 2.2: a column permutation can move a label into the box of $n$ only from the same column, and the corner column of a standard tableau has height equal to the row of $n$, so the box of $n$ is met only by the label $n$ itself when $n$ sits in row $r_i$, and by no label at all from row $r_i$ when $n$ sits in an earlier removable row $r_l$ with $l<i$.

- **No splitting is claimed.** The lemma identifies the successive quotients $V_i/V_{i-1}$ of the restricted module $S^\lambda_F$; it does not assert that the filtration splits, and this is exactly the point that fails in characteristic $p\le n$ for some shapes (see the companion examples page).

- **Field-uniformity.** Both bases used are the standard-polytabloid bases of [[lem-integral-specht-garnir-straightening-and-field-basis]], so the statement holds over every field, including characteristic $2$; no RSK correspondence or dimension count over $\mathbb C$ is used.
