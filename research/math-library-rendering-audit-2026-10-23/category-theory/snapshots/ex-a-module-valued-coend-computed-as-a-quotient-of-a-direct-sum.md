## Example

Let $\mathcal C$ be the walking arrow with objects $0$ and $1$ and one
non-identity morphism $u:0\to1$, and work in $\mathbb Z\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$.
Define a functor $T:\mathcal C^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal C\to\mathbb Z\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$ by

$$T(0,0)=T(1,1)=T(1,0)=\mathbb Z,\qquad T(0,1)=0,$$

with $T(u,1_0):\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z$ multiplication by $2$,
$T(1_1,u):\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z$ multiplication by $3$, and every map whose
codomain is $0$ the zero homomorphism.

Then the coend is computed by one generator:

$$\int^{c}T(c,c)\;\cong\;(\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z)\big/\langle(2,-3)\rangle\;\cong\;\mathbb Z,$$

and under this identification the two cowedge components are multiplication by
$3$ from $T(0,0)$ and multiplication by $2$ from $T(1,1)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The walking-arrow index category and the functor $T$ displayed above.

[L1] A module-valued coend is the direct sum of the diagonal values modulo the dinaturality submodule ([[thm-a-module-valued-coend-is-a-quotient-of-a-direct-sum-by-the-dinaturality-submodule]]).

[F1] A direct sum is formed with coordinate inclusions, and the empty direct sum is the zero module ([[def-direct-sum-of-a-family-of-modules]]).

[F2] For a submodule $N\le M$, the quotient module $M/N$ has cosets as elements and scalar multiplication $r(m+N):=rm+N$ ([[def-quotient-module]]).

[F3] For a fixed ring $R$, left $R$-modules and module homomorphisms form a large locally small category $R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$ ([[prop-modules-and-homomorphisms-form-category-rmod]]).

[F4] An end is a terminal wedge and a coend an initial cowedge ([[def-end-and-coend]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The displayed data define a functor into $\mathbb Z$-modules: the only non-identity square in $\mathcal C^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal C$ with nontrivial source factors through the slot $(1,0)$, and its codomain is $(0,1)$, where every structure map is the zero homomorphism, so the square commutes automatically. [F3, given]

2.1 By [L1] the coend is the direct sum of the two diagonal values modulo the submodule generated by the dinaturality differences coming from the off-diagonal value $T(1,0)=\mathbb Z$. Here the direct sum is $\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z$, and for $n\in\mathbb Z$ the generator contributed by $u$ is $\jmath_0(T(u,1_0)(n))-\jmath_1(T(1_1,u)(n))=(2n,-3n)$; the identity morphisms contribute only $(0,0)$. So the coend is $Q:=(\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z)/\langle(2,-3)\rangle$. [L1, F1, step 1.1]

3.1 The homomorphism $q:\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z$, $(a,b)\mapsto3a+2b$, kills $(2,-3)$ and so descends, by [F2], to a homomorphism $\bar q:Q\to\mathbb Z$. If $3a+2b=0$, then $3a=-2b$, so $3$ divides $b$ and $2$ divides $a$; writing $a=2k$ and $b=-3k$ shows $\ker q=\langle(2,-3)\rangle$. Hence $\bar q$ is an isomorphism, and under it the two quotient cowedge components are multiplication by $3$ and by $2$. [L1, F2, step 2.1]

4.1 On the nontrivial test module $\mathbb Z$, a cowedge $\lambda_0,\lambda_1:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z$ is determined by the integers $a:=\lambda_0(1)$ and $b:=\lambda_1(1)$, and the cowedge equation at $u$ is $2a=3b$. So $a=3t$ and $b=2t$ for a unique $t\in\mathbb Z$, and multiplication by $t$ on $Q\cong\mathbb Z$ is the unique homomorphism factoring the cowedge through the displayed quotient. This is exactly the initial-cowedge property on that test module. [F4, step 3.1] ∎

## Remarks

Richter's module example is general; this one chooses the maps $2$ and $3$ so that the quotient can be identified explicitly with $\mathbb Z$. The point is not the particular integers but the shape of the computation: the coend is a quotient of a direct sum by the dinaturality submodule.

The off-diagonal zero slot keeps the functoriality check finite. It makes every square landing in $(0,1)$ trivial, so the whole example reduces to one off-diagonal module and one generator of the relation.
