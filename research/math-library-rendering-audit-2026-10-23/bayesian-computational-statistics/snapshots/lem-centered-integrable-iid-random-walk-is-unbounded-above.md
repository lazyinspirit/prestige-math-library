## Statement

Let $(X_n)_{n\ge1}$ be IID real random variables with $\mathbb E|X_1|<\infty$, $\mathbb EX_1=0$, and $\mathbb P(X_1=0)<1$. Put $S_0=0$ and $S_n=\sum_{i=1}^nX_i$. Then
$$\mathbb P\left(\sup_{n\ge0}S_n=+\infty\right)=1.$$
In particular, $\limsup_{n\to\infty}S_n=+\infty$ almost surely.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The IID sequence, integrability, centering, and nondegeneracy in the statement.

[F1] IID means mutual independence and identical laws. [[def-identically-distributed-and-iid-random-variables]]

[F2] A tail event for an independent sequence has probability zero or one. [[thm-kolmogorov-zero-one-law]]

[F3] Sigma-algebras generated by disjoint groups of independent coordinates are independent. [[thm-grouping-independent-sigma-algebras]]

[F4] Dominated convergence permits passage through expectation under an integrable majorant. [[thm-dominated-convergence]]

[F5] A characteristic function is continuous and equals one at zero. [[lem-basic-properties-of-characteristic-functions]]

[F6] The characteristic function of a sum of independent real variables is the product of their characteristic functions. [[lem-characteristic-functions-under-affine-maps-and-independent-sums]]

[F7] Expectations are linear for integrable real variables. [[thm-linearity-of-the-lebesgue-integral-on-l-one]]

[F8] A nonnegative measurable function with integral zero vanishes almost everywhere. [[thm-nonnegative-integral-zero-iff-zero-almost-everywhere]]

## Proof

1.1 Let $M=\sup_{n\ge0}S_n$, a measurable $[0,+\infty]$-valued random variable. For each $m\ge1$, removing the first $m$ increments changes later partial sums only by the finite number $S_m$, and the removed partial sums form a finite set. Consequently $\{M=+\infty\}=\{\sup_{k\ge0}\sum_{i=m+1}^{m+k}X_i=+\infty\}$ belongs to $\sigma(X_{m+1},X_{m+2},\ldots)$. It is a tail event, so its probability is zero or one. [given, F1, F2]

2.1 Suppose its probability were zero. Then $M$ is finite almost surely. Define $M'=\sup_{k\ge0}\sum_{i=2}^{k+1}X_i$, which is also finite almost surely and has the same law as $M$: for every real $b$, the event that the supremum is at most $b$ is the decreasing intersection of the corresponding finite-prefix events, whose probabilities agree by the IID hypothesis. The variable $M'$ is independent of $X_1$ by independence of their coordinate sigma-algebras. Decomposing the supremum according to whether the empty partial sum is used gives $M=\max(0,X_1+M')$ almost surely. On null exceptional sets replace $M$ and $M'$ by zero if needed, without changing their laws or these almost-sure assertions. [step 1.1, F1, F3]

3.1 Put $A=\max(0,M'+X_1)$ and $B=M'$, so $A$ and $B$ are finite nonnegative variables with the same law. The map $u\mapsto\max(0,u)$ is one-Lipschitz and $B\ge0$, hence $|A-B|\le|X_1|$. For each positive integer $K$, equality in law gives $\mathbb E[\min(A,K)-\min(B,K)]=0$. The difference converges almost surely to $A-B$ and has absolute value at most $|A-B|\le|X_1|$. Dominated convergence therefore yields $\mathbb E(A-B)=0$, without requiring either $A$ or $B$ to have finite expectation. [step 2.1, F4, F7]

4.1 The identity $A-B=X_1+R$, where $R=(-B-X_1)^+$ and $u^+=\max(u,0)$, gives $0\le R\le|X_1|$. Centering and step 3.1 imply $\mathbb ER=0$, so $R=0$ almost surely. Thus $B+X_1=A$ almost surely and $B+X_1$ has the same law as $B$. Independence now gives $\varphi_B(t)\varphi_{X_1}(t)=\varphi_B(t)$ for every real $t$. [step 2.1, step 3.1, F6, F7, F8, given]

5.1 Continuity and $\varphi_B(0)=1$ supply $\delta>0$ such that $\varphi_B(t)\ne0$ for $|t|<\delta$. Step 4.1 then gives $\varphi_{X_1}(t)=1$ on this interval. Choose a positive integer $q$ with $1/q<\delta$. For every integer $k\ge q$, taking real parts gives $\mathbb E[1-\cos(X_1/k)]=0$, so $\cos(X_1/k)=1$ almost surely. Intersecting these countably many probability-one events, fix a resulting value $x$ of $X_1$. If $x\ne0$, some $k\ge q$ has $0<|x|/k<2\pi$, contradicting $\cos(x/k)=1$. Therefore $X_1=0$ almost surely, contrary to the hypothesis. [step 4.1, F5, F8, given]

6.1 The zero-probability alternative in step 1.1 is impossible, so $M=+\infty$ almost surely. Each finite prefix has finite maximum, and hence removing it preserves an infinite supremum. Thus the limsup of the partial sums is also $+\infty$ almost surely. [step 1.1, step 5.1] ∎
