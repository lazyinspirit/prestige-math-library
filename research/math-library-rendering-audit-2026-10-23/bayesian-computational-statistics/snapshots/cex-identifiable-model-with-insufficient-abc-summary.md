## Statement refuted

Full-model identifiability alone does not make an ABC summary target equal the full-data posterior. In the binary experiment below the sampling laws are distinct, while a constant summary leaves every tolerance target equal to the prior.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite parameter and data space, the proper prior-simulator and summary-target conventions in [[def-bayesian-model-as-prior-and-sampling-kernel]] and [[def-approximate-bayesian-computation]].

[F1] Model identifiability means that distinct parameters have distinct sampling laws. [[def-identifiability-of-an-estimand]], [[def-parametric-statistical-model]], [[def-observational-equivalence]], [[def-estimand-on-a-statistical-model]]

[F2] Sufficiency requires one parameter-independent conditional-data kernel given the summary. [[def-sufficient-statistic]]

[F3] The summary map is a measurable statistic. [[def-statistic-and-estimator]]

[F4] A Polish presentation is separable and has a compatible complete metric; the Borel sigma-algebra is generated by its open sets. [[def-standard-borel-space]], [[def-polish-space]], [[def-separable-space]], [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]

[F5] The usual metric on $\mathbb R$ is complete, the rationals are countable and dense, and its metric balls generate the usual topology. [[thm-reals-cauchy-complete]], [[thm-rationals-countable]], [[lem-rat-embeds-dense]], [[lem-metrics-on-rn]], [[def-metric-ball]], [[def-metric-topology]]

[F6] A positive-probability ABC target is the prior-predictive law conditioned on its tolerance event. If proposal-simulator pairs form an IID sequence with the prior-simulator joint law, the first accepted rejection output has that target law. [[def-approximate-bayesian-computation]], [[thm-abc-rejection-target-is-tolerance-conditioned-posterior]]

[F7] A posterior kernel satisfies the conditional-law event identities, and total variation on probability laws is the supremum of event-probability differences. [[def-bayesian-posterior-as-regular-conditional-law]], [[def-total-variation-distance-for-probability-laws]]

## Counterexample

**Given:** Let $\Theta=\{0,1\}$ and $\mathsf Y=\{0,1\}$, both with all subsets measurable. Put prior mass $1/2$ at each parameter and use the deterministic simulator $K(\theta,\{\theta\})=1$. Let $S(y)=0\in\mathbb R$, and observe $y_0=0$.

1.1 On either finite set, the discrete metric has values $0$ and $1$. Since Cauchy means that tail distances are eventually below every positive radius, a Cauchy sequence is eventually constant at radius $1/2$, so the metric is complete. Each finite set is countable and dense in itself, and all singletons are open; thus its Borel sigma-algebra is the power set. The identity is a Borel isomorphism to this Polish presentation, so [F4] shows both spaces are standard Borel. By [F5], the usual metric on $\mathbb R$ is complete and separable and induces its usual Borel sigma-algebra, so the summary target is standard Borel. The constant map $S$ is measurable. [given, F3, F4, F5, construct]

2.1 The sampling laws are $P_0=\delta_0$ and $P_1=\delta_1$, hence $P_0\ne P_1$. By [F1], the identity parameter is identifiable. The marginal probability of the observation $y_0=0$ is $1/2$; its prior-weighted likelihoods are $1/2$ at parameter $0$ and $0$ at parameter $1$. Therefore the full-data conditional law is $\delta_0$, satisfying the posterior event identity [F7]. [given, F1, F7, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For every $\epsilon\ge0$, the event $|S(Y)-0|\le\epsilon$ is the whole data space. It has probability one, so the ABC target is the unchanged prior, with mass $1/2$ at each parameter. For rejection sampling supplied with the IID pairs of [F6], the first proposal is accepted and its output has this same law. The target's total-variation distance from the full-data posterior is at least the discrepancy on $\{0\}$, namely $|1/2-1|=1/2$, by [F7]. [given, F6, F7, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 The statistic is not sufficient. If a common kernel $Q$ existed, taking summary event $C=\{0\}$ in [F2]'s identity would force $K(\theta,D)=Q(0,D)$ for every data event $D$ and both parameters. For $D=\{0\}$ this would require both $Q(0,\{0\})=1$ and $Q(0,\{0\})=0$, a contradiction. Thus identifiability does not prevent persistent distortion from an insufficient summary. [given, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
