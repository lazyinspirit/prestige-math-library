## Example

Let $T=\{a,b\}$, let $m(a)=m(b)=0$, and set $k(s,t)=1$ if $s=t$ and $0$
otherwise. Observe only $f_a$ with independent noise variance one and predict
$f_b$. The posterior-kernel law of $f_b$ is $N(0,1)$ for every observation
value, so observing the independent coordinate does not reduce uncertainty at
$b$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; the two-index equality kernel; and an independent
$N(0,1)$ observation-noise variable.

[A1] AC is required by the finite Gaussian-process and multivariate-normal
constructions. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] Any symmetric positive-semidefinite kernel on an arbitrary set defines a
canonical cylinder-space Gaussian process under AC.
[[thm-gaussian-process-exists-from-mean-and-positive-semidefinite-covariance-kernel]]

[F2] Under AC, every finite symmetric positive-semidefinite covariance matrix
defines a possibly singular Gaussian law. [[def-multivariate-normal-law]]

[F3] A finite product of sigma-finite measures exists and has the rectangle
product formula. [[thm-sigma-finite-product-measure-exists-is-rectangular-and-is-unique]]

[F4] Product-coordinate random elements are independent when their rectangle
probabilities factor. [[def-independent-random-elements]]
[[thm-rectangle-criterion-for-independent-random-elements]]

[F5] Coordinates on the cylinder sigma-algebra are measurable, and a finite
product sigma-algebra is generated by its coordinate sets.
[[def-coordinate-maps-and-cylinder-sigma-algebra]]
[[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]]

[F6] With positive-definite observed covariance, the finite posterior law is
Gaussian with the stated mean and covariance. [[thm-finite-gaussian-process-posterior-and-predictive-laws]]

## Verification

**Given:** The two-index model and one noisy observation in the Example.

1.1 For any finite list of indices, group coefficients at repeated sites. The equality kernel's quadratic form is the sum, over distinct sites $t$, of $(\sum_{i:t_i=t}a_i)^2$, hence is nonnegative. By [F1] the canonical GP law exists and [F2] supplies the $N(0,1)$ noise law. Their product probability exists by [F3]; its process and noise random elements are measurable by [F5] and independent by [F4]. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 Here $K_{XX}=1$, $K_{**}=1$, $K_{*X}=K_{X*}=0$, and the noise covariance is $R=1$. Thus $C=K_{XX}+R=2>0$, so [F6] applies. [given, F6, algebra]

2.1 The formula in [F6] gives posterior mean $0+0\cdot 2^{-1}(y-0)=0$ and variance $1-0\cdot2^{-1}\cdot0=1$ for every $y\in\mathbb R$. Hence the posterior version at the unobserved independent index is its original $N(0,1)$ law. [F6, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
