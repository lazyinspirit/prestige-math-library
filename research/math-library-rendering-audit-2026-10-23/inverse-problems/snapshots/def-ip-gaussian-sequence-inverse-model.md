## Definition

Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice. Let $X$ and $Y$ be real Hilbert spaces and let $K:X\to Y$ be a compact linear operator of infinite rank, viewed as an inverse model with exact datum $Kx$. Here compact means that the image of every bounded subset has compact closure. Write $N(K)=\{x\in X:Kx=0\}$ and $H_0=\overline{\operatorname{ran}K}$. Fix a positive singular system $(s_j,v_j,u_j)_{j\in\mathbb N_{>0}}$ for $K$, where $\mathbb N_{>0}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}$, $s_1\ge s_2\ge\cdots>0$, $s_j\to0$, $Kv_j=s_ju_j$, $(v_j)$ is a complete orthonormal basis of $N(K)^\perp$, and $(u_j)$ is a complete orthonormal basis of $H_0$. A complete orthonormal basis is an orthonormal family whose closed linear span is the whole indicated subspace. These are the compact SVD conventions; in particular, this model concerns minimum-norm representatives in $N(K)^\perp$ and does not estimate a kernel component.

For a truth $x\in N(K)^\perp$, let $\theta_j=\langle x,v_j\rangle_X$. The complete-basis coefficient map identifies $N(K)^\perp$ bijectively and isometrically with $\ell^2(\mathbb N_{>0},\mathbb R)$, so every $\theta\in\ell^2(\mathbb N_{>0},\mathbb R)$ is a model parameter. For $\epsilon>0$, take the functional white-noise experiment on the separable Hilbert space $H_0$ with observations $Y_x(g)=\langle Kx,g\rangle_Y+\epsilon W(g)$, where $W$ is centered, has covariance $\mathbb E[W(g)W(h)]=\langle g,h\rangle_Y$, and has independent standard-normal values on every finite orthonormal tuple. The countable complete basis makes $H_0$ separable. Define $\xi_j=W(u_j)$ and $Z_j=Y_x(u_j)/s_j$. The sequence sample space is $\mathbb R^{\mathbb N_{>0}}$ with the product Borel sigma-algebra, meaning the sigma-algebra generated by its coordinate projections. The coordinate law is the pushforward of the functional experiment under $y\mapsto(y(u_j)/s_j)_{j\in\mathbb N_{>0}}$; this map is measurable because each coordinate is a cylinder evaluation followed by scalar multiplication. The compact SVD expansion gives $\langle Kx,u_j\rangle_Y=s_j\theta_j$, and the positive-noise white-noise coordinate theorem gives the independent standard-normal coordinates $\xi_j$. Hence the normalized coordinates satisfy $Z_j=\theta_j+\epsilon s_j^{-1}\xi_j,\qquad j\in\mathbb N_{>0}.$

For the zero-noise boundary $\epsilon=0$, define the sequence experiment separately by the point mass at $\theta$, so $Z_j=\theta_j$. This boundary is not an instance of the positive-noise functional experiment.

## Coordinate diagram

The deterministic square commutes: $\Phi_X(x)=(\langle x,v_j\rangle)_j$ and $\Phi_{H_0}(y)=(\langle y,u_j\rangle)_j$ are the coefficient isometries, and $D_s(\theta)=(s_j\theta_j)_j$ is well defined because $s_j\le s_1$. The compact SVD expansion gives $\Phi_{H_0}(Kx)=D_s(\Phi_X(x))$. The noisy coordinates add $\epsilon\xi_j$ before division by $s_j$; the diagram is not a claim that white noise is an $H_0$-valued sample and is not used as a proof.

H_0 \arrow[r, "\Phi_{H_0}"'] & \ell^2(\mathbb{N}_{>0},\mathbb{R})
