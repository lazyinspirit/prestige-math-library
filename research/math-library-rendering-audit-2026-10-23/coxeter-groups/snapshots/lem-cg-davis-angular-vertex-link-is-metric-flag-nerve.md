## Statement

Let $(S,m)$ be a Coxeter matrix with $S$ finite, $W$ the presented group with length function $\ell$ ([[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]), $V=\mathbb R^S$ carrying the Coxeter form $B$ with $B(e_s,e_s)=1$, $B(e_s,e_t)=-\cos(\pi/m(s,t))$ for $m(s,t)<\infty$ and $B(e_s,e_t)=-1$ for $m(s,t)=\infty$, and the canonical reflection representation $\rho$ ([[def-cg-real-coxeter-form-and-reflection]] (1), [[def-cg-canonical-reflection-homomorphism]]). Let $\mathbb S$ be the set of spherical subsets with nerve $L$ ([[def-cg-spherical-nerve-coset-poset-and-davis-realization]] (1)), let $\Sigma$ be the Davis realization with its cellulation by the cells $wW_T$ ($T\in\mathbb S$) and its chain metric $d$ ([[thm-cg-davis-complex-cell-incidence-and-stabilizers]] (1),(2)), and let $C_T=\operatorname{conv}(W_Tx_T)$, $x_T=\sum_{s\in T}d_sv_s^{(T)}$, with its faces and face isometries be the Coxeter cell of [[lem-cg-finite-coxeter-orbit-polytopes-and-face-metrics]] for a fixed tuple of positive numbers $(d_s)_{s\in S}$. **Assume the Axiom of Choice** ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]); it is used in the proof only through [[lem-cg-spherical-simplex-existence-and-link-gram-formula]] (iii) and [[thm-cg-large-metric-flag-complexes-are-cat-one]].

**(1) Edge directions at a vertex.** Let $T\in\mathbb S$ and let $wW_T$ be a cell, identified with $C_T$ so that the $0$-cell $wW_\emptyset=\{w\}$ corresponds to $x_T$. For every $s\in T$ the only $1$-face of $C_T$ through $x_T$ with type $\{s\}$ is the segment $[x_T,sx_T]$, and $$sx_T-x_T=-2B(x_T,e_s)\,e_s=-2d_s\,e_s ,$$ because $x_T=\sum_{t\in T}d_tv_t^{(T)}$ and $v_t^{(T)}$ is the $B$-dual basis of $V_T$ ([[lem-cg-finite-coxeter-orbit-polytopes-and-face-metrics]] (1),(2), [[lem-cg-canonical-cell-exposed-faces-and-normal-cones]] (5)). The tangent cone $T_{x_T}C_T$ (the cone of inward directions at the vertex, in the sense of [[def-cg-spherical-gram-simplex-and-angular-link]]) is the simplicial cone generated by the vectors $-e_s$, $s\in T$: it equals $\{y\in V_T:B(v_s^{(T)},y)\le0\text{ for all }s\in T\}$, cut out by the facets through the vertex, and each $-e_s$ is an extreme ray, since a nonnegative combination $\sum_{t\ne s}\lambda_t(-e_t)$ has $B(\cdot,e_s)$-pairing $\ge0>-1=B(-e_s,e_s)$ and cannot equal $-e_s$ ([[lem-cg-spherical-simplex-existence-and-link-gram-formula]] (v); $V_T$ carries the inner product $B_T$, [[def-real-and-complex-inner-product-space]], [[def-metric-space]]).

**(2) Edge lengths.** For distinct $s,t\in T$ the subgroup $W_{\{s,t\}}$ is finite because it is contained in the finite group $W_T$, so $m(s,t)<\infty$. In the spherical simplex $\Sigma(B_T)$ of (3), the edge joining the directions $\mathbb R_{\ge0}(-e_s)$ and $\mathbb R_{\ge0}(-e_t)$ has its prescribed spherical length $$\arccos B(e_s,e_t)=\arccos\bigl(-\cos(\pi/m(s,t))\bigr)=\pi-\pi/m(s,t)\ \ge\ \pi/2$$ ([[def-cg-real-coxeter-form-and-reflection]], [[def-principal-inverse-sine-and-cosine]], [[thm-sine-cosine-signs-monotonicity-and-ranges]]). This is the local edge-cell length measured in that spherical simplex; no global shortest-path distance between these vertices in the whole link is asserted ([[def-cg-abstract-isometric-polyhedral-gluing-and-chain-metric]]).

**(3) The vertex link is the nerve.** For every spherical $T$ the link $\operatorname{Lk}(x_T,C_T)$ of the vertex is isometric to the spherical simplex $\Sigma(B_T)$ with vertex set $\{-e_s:s\in T\}$ and cosine matrix $B_T=B|_{V_T\times V_T}$, realized by the Gram construction of [[lem-cg-spherical-simplex-existence-and-link-gram-formula]] (i),(ii) and [[def-cg-spherical-gram-simplex-and-angular-link]], and for $U\subseteq T$ the face of $\Sigma(B_T)$ belonging to the face $\operatorname{conv}(W_Ux_T)$ is $\Sigma(B_U)$, the vertex map being $-e_s\mapsto-e_s$. Gluing over $T\in\mathbb S$ along these faces, the angular link $\operatorname{Lk}_\Sigma(w)$ of any $0$-cell $\{w\}$, with its truncated angular metric, is canonically isometric to the finite spherical complex $X:=|L|_B$ built on the nerve $L$ with the Gram matrices $B_T$ ([[lem-cg-spherical-simplex-existence-and-link-gram-formula]] (iii),(v),(vi), [[def-cg-abstract-isometric-polyhedral-gluing-and-chain-metric]]); the direction of the $1$-cell $wW_{\{s\}}$ corresponds to the vertex $s$ of $L$, and the identification preserves the weak topologies and the truncated angular metrics.

**(4) Large metric flag.** $X=|L|_B$ is a finite large metric flag complex in the sense of [[def-cg-large-spherical-metric-flag-and-almost-negative-matrix]]: by (2) every edge has length $\pi-\pi/m(s,t)\ge\pi/2$. If $T$ is a pairwise adjacent set of vertices of $L$, its cosine matrix $C_T$ is $B_T$, since each edge entry is the local length from (2). The parabolic presentation theorem identifies $(W_T,T)$ with the Coxeter system for the restricted matrix $m|_T$, which is the induced labelled subdiagram ([[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]] (2), [[def-cg-coxeter-diagram-components-and-finite-type]]). Hence the finite-type criterion gives $B_T$ positive definite exactly when $W_T$ is finite ([[thm-cg-finite-type-positive-definite-criterion]] (1), [[def-definiteness-inertia-and-signature-data-over-the-reals]]). By the definition of $L$, this is exactly when $T$ spans a simplex. The associated almost-negative matrix has entry $-1$ on non-edges and cosine of the prescribed edge length on edges, so it is exactly $B$: a pair is an edge precisely when $m(s,t)<\infty$, and then $\cos(\pi-\pi/m(s,t))=B(e_s,e_t)$; for a non-edge $m(s,t)=\infty$ and $B(e_s,e_t)=-1$.

**(5) Higher links.** For every spherical $U$ and every $w\in W$ the angular link $\operatorname{Lk}_\Sigma(wW_U)$ of the cell $wW_U$ is canonically isometric to the link $\operatorname{Lk}_X(U)$ of the face $U$ in $X$: its vertices are exactly the $s\notin U$ for which $U\cup\{s\}$ is spherical, its cells are the sets $T'$ with $U\cup T'$ spherical, and its Gram matrices are the iterated Schur complements of $B$ along $U$ ([[lem-cg-spherical-simplex-existence-and-link-gram-formula]] (iv)). The direct Schur-complement argument in step 4.1 shows that every such link is again a finite large metric flag complex. For every point $p$ of the relative interior of $wW_U$, some metric ball $B(p,\varepsilon)$ is isometric to the ball of radius $\varepsilon$ about $(0,o)$ in $\mathbb R^{|U|}\times C(\operatorname{Lk}_\Sigma(wW_U))$ ([[thm-cg-cone-join-metric-and-local-product-chart]]).

**(6) CAT(1) links.** Every vertex link $\operatorname{Lk}_\Sigma(w)=X$ and, by (5), every link $\operatorname{Lk}_\Sigma(wW_U)$ is CAT(1) for its truncated angular metric ([[thm-cg-large-metric-flag-complexes-are-cat-one]], [[def-cg-cat-zero-cat-one-and-local-geodesic]] (3),(4)); truncation means $d_\pi=\min\{\pi,d_{\mathrm{path}}\}$, including distance $\pi$ across distinct components or when no path exists ([[def-cg-euclidean-cone-and-spherical-join-metrics]] (2),(4)).

**(7) Abstentions.** Nothing is asserted here about CAT(0)-ness, contractibility, properness of the action or fixed points of finite subgroups; those are proved in the later items of this page. The link computation is independent of the choice of the distances $d_s$; the cell metrics of $\Sigma$ are not, and no claim is made here about them beyond [[thm-cg-davis-complex-cell-incidence-and-stabilizers]]. No Choice is used in (1)-(5) beyond the referenced construction of the spherical complex.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, a finite Coxeter matrix $(S,m)$, the presented group $W$ with length $\ell$, the Coxeter form $B$ on $V=\mathbb R^S$, the canonical reflection representation $\rho$, a fixed tuple $(d_s)_{s\in S}$ of positive real numbers, and the associated cells $C_T$, $T\in\mathbb S$.

[F1] The Coxeter form satisfies $B(e_s,e_s)=1$, $B(e_s,e_t)=-\cos(\pi/m(s,t))$ for finite $m(s,t)$ and $B(e_s,e_t)=-1$ for $m(s,t)=\infty$, and each generator acts by the reflection $\rho(s)=r_{e_s}$, $\rho(s)v=v-2B(v,e_s)e_s$; the map $\rho$ is a homomorphism ([[def-cg-real-coxeter-form-and-reflection]], [[def-cg-canonical-reflection-homomorphism]]). Its $B$-invariance is the local reflection-form calculation in step 1.3, followed by composition along a word for $u$.

[F2] $W$ is the group presented by $(S,m)$; for $T\subseteq S$ the standard parabolic is $W_T=\langle s:s\in T\rangle$, and $T$ is spherical exactly when $W_T$ is finite, so that the simplices of the nerve $L$ are the nonempty spherical subsets and $L$ is finite ([[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]], [[def-cg-spherical-nerve-coset-poset-and-davis-realization]] (1)).

[F3] Restricting the Coxeter matrix to $T\subseteq S$ gives the induced labelled subdiagram; finite type means that the Coxeter group of the indicated system is finite ([[def-cg-coxeter-diagram-components-and-finite-type]]).

[F4] For spherical $T$ the restriction $B_T$ is an inner product on $V_T$, the vectors $v_s^{(T)}$ ($s\in T$) form the $B$-dual basis, $C_T=\operatorname{conv}(W_Tx_T)$ is a compact convex polyhedral cell of dimension $|T|$ with $0$ in its interior, and its nonempty faces are exactly the sets $\operatorname{conv}(uW_Ux_T)$, $u\in W_T$, $U\subseteq T$, each occurring for exactly one coset $uW_U$, with $\dim\operatorname{conv}(uW_Ux_T)=|U|$ ([[lem-cg-finite-coxeter-orbit-polytopes-and-face-metrics]]).

[F5] Applied to the finite-type system $(W_T,T)$ and the point $x_T$ with $c_s:=B(v_s^{(T)},x_T)>0$ (where $d_s=B(e_s,x_T)$ denotes the mirror distance): every point of $W_Tx_T$ is a vertex of $C_T$; $\operatorname{conv}(wW_Ux_T)\subseteq\operatorname{conv}(w'W_{U'}x_T)$ holds exactly when $wW_U\subseteq w'W_{U'}$; and $C_T=\{v\in V_T:B(wv_s^{(T)},v)\le c_s\text{ for all }w\in W_T,\ s\in T\}$ with $0$ in the interior ([[lem-cg-canonical-cell-exposed-faces-and-normal-cones]] (4),(5)).

[F6] For a compact convex polyhedral cell with direction space $V$ and a face $F$, the tangent cone is $T_FC=\{\xi\in V:\langle\xi,n_j\rangle\ge0\text{ for all facets }j\text{ containing }F\}$, equivalently the closure of $\{\lambda(x-p):x\in C,\ \lambda\ge0\}$, and, with $U(F)=\operatorname{span}(F-F)$, its normal face link $\operatorname{Lk}_C(F)=(T_FC\cap U(F)^\perp)\cap S(V)$ carries the angular distance $d_{\mathrm{ang}}(\xi,\eta)=\arccos\langle\xi,\eta\rangle$; for a vertex $F$, $U(F)=\{0\}$ and this link is $T_FC\cap S(V)$; the spherical simplex $\Sigma(C)$ of a positive-definite Gram matrix $C$ with diagonal $1$ is built from the cone on the unit vertices realizing $C$ ([[def-cg-spherical-gram-simplex-and-angular-link]]).

[F7] For each $T\subseteq S$, [[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]] (2) identifies $(W_T,T)$ with the Coxeter system for the restricted matrix $m|_T$, whose Coxeter form is $B_T$; [[thm-cg-finite-type-positive-definite-criterion]] (1) therefore gives $B_T$ positive definite if and only if $W_T$ is finite. The Davis complex $\Sigma$ has one cell $wW_T$ for each $w\in W$ and $T\in\mathbb S$, its $0$-cells are the elements $w$, and the cell $wW_T$ is identified with $C_T$ so that the $0$-cell $wW_\emptyset=\{w\}$ corresponds to $x_T$ ([[thm-cg-davis-complex-cell-incidence-and-stabilizers]] (1),(2)).

[F8] The spherical simplex $\Sigma(C)$ is realized by the Gram construction, uniquely up to a linear isometry ([[lem-cg-spherical-simplex-existence-and-link-gram-formula]] (i),(ii)).

[F9] An isometric polyhedral gluing is the quotient of the disjoint union of its cells along face isometries satisfying the cocycle condition, and it carries the chain metric and its angular links ([[def-cg-abstract-isometric-polyhedral-gluing-and-chain-metric]]).

[F10] A finite spherical complex is large when all edge lengths are at least $\pi/2$; for a large complex the associated almost-negative matrix has off-diagonal entries the cosines of the edge lengths for adjacent vertices and $-1$ for non-adjacent ones; the complex is metric flag when a pairwise adjacent set of vertices spans a simplex exactly when its cosine matrix is positive definite; and the link of a face carries the iterated Schur-complement Gram matrices ([[def-cg-large-spherical-metric-flag-and-almost-negative-matrix]] (1)-(4), [[def-definiteness-inertia-and-signature-data-over-the-reals]]).

[F11] Under AC, every finite large metric flag complex is CAT(1) for its truncated angular metric. The proof uses compact geodesic components with their untruncated intrinsic spherical length metrics, then transfers the short comparison tests to the truncation ([[thm-cg-large-metric-flag-complexes-are-cat-one]]).

[F12] At a point $p$ in the relative interior of a face $F$, for some $\varepsilon>0$ the metric ball $B(p,\varepsilon)$ is isometric, preserving intrinsic lengths, to the ball of radius $\varepsilon$ about $(0,o)$ in $\mathbb R^{\dim F}\times C(\operatorname{Lk}_X(F))$ ([[thm-cg-cone-join-metric-and-local-product-chart]] (4)).

[F13] The face link has angular metric $d_\pi=\min\{\pi,d_{\mathrm{path}}\}$, with $d_{\mathrm{path}}=+\infty$ across components ([[def-cg-euclidean-cone-and-spherical-join-metrics]] (2),(4)). CAT(1) requires geodesics only for pairs at distance $<\pi$ and spherical comparison only for geodesic triangles of perimeter $<2\pi$ ([[def-cg-cat-zero-cat-one-and-local-geodesic]] (3)); all sides of such a triangle are $<\pi$, by the triangle inequality.

[F14] The principal inverse cosine satisfies $\arccos(-\cos(\pi/m))=\pi-\pi/m$ for every integer $m\ge2$, and $\cos$ is strictly decreasing on $[0,\pi]$ ([[def-principal-inverse-sine-and-cosine]], [[thm-sine-cosine-signs-monotonicity-and-ranges]]).

[F15] The inner product $B_T$ determines the distances on $V_T$ ([[def-real-and-complex-inner-product-space]]); a metric space and its isometries are as in the metric conventions, so a distance-preserving identification of two angular links is an isometry ([[def-metric-space]], [[def-isometry-and-metric-embedding]]).

[F16] The Axiom of Choice: every family of nonempty sets has a choice function ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F17] Each connected component of a finite spherical complex, with its chain metric, is a compact proper complete length space whose metric topology is the weak topology; under AC, every two points in a component are joined by a minimizing geodesic. Between distinct components the path distance is $+\infty$, an auxiliary value replaced by $\pi$ in the truncated angular metric ([[lem-cg-spherical-simplex-existence-and-link-gram-formula]] (iii),(vi)).

[F18] Face links of spherical simplices are given by iterated normalized Schur complements; their angular metrics are the intrinsic round metrics, their face identifications are canonical, and distinct components use the auxiliary infinite path-distance convention before truncation ([[lem-cg-spherical-simplex-existence-and-link-gram-formula]] (iv)-(vi)).

## Proof

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, a finite Coxeter matrix $(S,m)$, the presented group $W$ with length $\ell$, the Coxeter form $B$ and the representation $\rho$ on $V=\mathbb R^S$, a tuple $(d_s)_{s\in S}$ of positive real numbers, and the spherical subsets $\mathbb S$ with their cells $C_T$.

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Fix $T\in\mathbb S$ and $s\in T$. The vectors $v_t^{(T)}$ ($t\in T$) are the $B$-dual basis of $V_T$ and $B_T$ is an inner product, so $B(x_T,e_s)=\sum_{t\in T}d_tB(v_t^{(T)},e_s)=d_s$; the reflection formula of [F1] gives $\rho(s)x_T=x_T-2B(x_T,e_s)e_s$, that is $sx_T-x_T=-2d_se_s\ne0$. By [F4] the point $x_T$ is a vertex of the compact convex polyhedral cell $C_T$ of dimension $|T|$, and the nonempty faces of $C_T$ are exactly the sets $\operatorname{conv}(uW_Ux_T)$, one for each coset $uW_U$ ($u\in W_T$, $U\subseteq T$). [F1, F4]

1.2 By the inclusion criterion of [F5] a face $\operatorname{conv}(uW_Ux_T)$ contains $x_T$ exactly when $\{x_T\}=\operatorname{conv}(W_\emptyset x_T)\subseteq\operatorname{conv}(uW_Ux_T)$, that is exactly when $W_\emptyset\subseteq uW_U$, i.e. $1\in uW_U$, which forces $uW_U=W_U$. Hence the faces of $C_T$ through $x_T$ are precisely the sets $\operatorname{conv}(W_Ux_T)$, $U\subseteq T$, so the faces of type $\{s\}$ through $x_T$ are precisely the segments $\operatorname{conv}(W_{\{s\}}x_T)=[x_T,\rho(s)x_T]$, the straight segment from $x_T$ to $\rho(s)x_T$ because $\rho(s)x_T-x_T=-2d_se_s$. In particular $[x_T,sx_T]$ is the only $1$-face of $C_T$ through $x_T$ of type $\{s\}$, as asserted in (1). [F4, F5]

1.3 The reflection formula in [F1] gives, for $x,y\in V$ and $a=B(x,e_r)$, $b=B(y,e_r)$, $B(\rho(r)x,\rho(r)y)=B(x-2ae_r,y-2be_r)=B(x,y)-2ab-2ab+4ab=B(x,y)$, since $B(e_r,e_r)=1$. Every $u\in W$ is a finite word in the generators and $\rho$ is a homomorphism, so composition proves $B(\rho(u)x,\rho(u)y)=B(x,y)$. The facets of $C_T$ through $x_T$ are the faces $F_s:=\operatorname{conv}(W_{T\setminus\{s\}}x_T)$ for $s\in T$, by 1.1, 1.2 and the dimension statement in [F4]. Fix $s\in T$. Each generator $r\in T\setminus\{s\}$ fixes the quotient of $V_T$ by $V_{T\setminus\{s\}}$: the reflection formula gives $\rho(r)e_s-e_s\in V_{T\setminus\{s\}}$ and $\rho(r)V_{T\setminus\{s\}}=V_{T\setminus\{s\}}$. Thus for every $u\in W_{T\setminus\{s\}}$, $\rho(u)^{-1}e_s\in e_s+V_{T\setminus\{s\}}$ and $\rho(u)^{-1}e_t\in V_{T\setminus\{s\}}$ for $t\ne s$. Pairing with the dual basis gives $B(\rho(u)v_s^{(T)},e_t)=B(v_s^{(T)},\rho(u)^{-1}e_t)=\delta_{st}$ for every $t\in T$, so nondegeneracy of $B_T$ implies $\rho(u)v_s^{(T)}=v_s^{(T)}$. Consequently $B(v_s^{(T)},ux_T)=B(v_s^{(T)},x_T)=c_s$ for every such $u$, and $F_s$ lies in the supporting hyperplane $B(v_s^{(T)},y)=c_s$; [F5] puts $C_T$ in the half-space $B(v_s^{(T)},y)\le c_s$. Since $F_s$ has dimension $|T|-1$, it is exactly the equality face, and its inward normal is proportional to $-v_s^{(T)}$. By [F6], $T_{x_T}C_T=\{\xi\in V_T:B(v_s^{(T)},\xi)\le0\text{ for every }s\in T\}$. Writing $\xi=\sum_{t\in T}a_te_t$ gives $B(v_s^{(T)},\xi)=a_s$, so this is $\operatorname{cone}\{-e_s:s\in T\}$; these generators are extreme because each nonnegative combination $\sum_{t\ne s}\lambda_t(-e_t)$ has $B(\cdot,e_s)$-pairing $\sum_{t\ne s}\lambda_t(-B(e_t,e_s))\ge0$, whereas $B(-e_s,e_s)=-1$. [F1, F4, F5, F6]

1.4 If distinct $s,t\in T$, then $W_{\{s,t\}}\le W_T$ is finite, so the order $m(s,t)$ of $st$ is finite. The unit vectors $-e_s,-e_t$ in the positive-definite plane $V_{\{s,t\}}\subseteq V_T$ have inner product $B(e_s,e_t)=-\cos(\pi/m(s,t))$. Their local spherical edge length is therefore $\arccos(-\cos(\pi/m(s,t)))=\pi-\pi/m(s,t)\ge\pi/2$, by [F1], [F2], [F6], [F14]. [F1, F2, F6, F14]

1.5 For spherical $T$, $B_T$ is an inner product by [F7], and the cell $wW_T$ of the Davis complex is identified with $C_T$ so that $wW_\emptyset=\{w\}$ corresponds to $x_T$ and the $1$-cell $wW_{\{s\}}$, $s\in T$, corresponds to the unique $1$-face of type $\{s\}$ through the vertex; the direction of this $1$-cell at $w$ is therefore the ray $\mathbb R_{\ge0}(\rho(s)x_T-x_T)=\mathbb R_{\ge0}(-e_s)$ by 1.1. [F7]

1.6 By [F6] the angular link of the vertex is $\operatorname{Lk}_{C_T}(x_T)=T_{x_T}C_T\cap S(V_T)$, the unit sphere in $(V_T,B_T)$ ([[def-real-and-complex-inner-product-space]]); by 1.3 and 1.4 its vertices are the unit vectors $-e_s$ with pairwise products $B(-e_s,-e_t)=B(e_s,e_t)$, that is with Gram matrix $B_T$. By the Gram construction [F8] the spherical simplex $\Sigma(B_T)$ built on the unit vertices realizing $B_T$ is unique up to a linear isometry of its ambient inner product space; a linear isometry carrying its vertices to the $-e_s$ maps it onto $\operatorname{Lk}_{C_T}(x_T)$, and it preserves the angular distances $\arccos\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$, so it is an isometry of angular links [F15]. Moreover, for $U\subseteq T$ the face $\operatorname{conv}(W_Ux_T)$ has vertex $x_T$, and by the same computation applied to the finite-type system $(W_U,U)$ its link at $x_T$ is $\Sigma(B_U)$, the face of $\Sigma(B_T)$ spanned by the vertices $-e_s$, $s\in U$, with the vertex map $-e_s\mapsto-e_s$. 2.1 For distinct $s,t\in S$, every word in $s,t$ reduces using $s^2=t^2=1$ to an alternating word; if $m(s,t)<\infty$, each such word represents one of at most $2m(s,t)$ elements of the form $(st)^k$ or $s(st)^k$, so $W_{\{s,t\}}$ is finite. If $m(s,t)=\infty$, the element $st$ has infinite order by the Coxeter-matrix convention, so $W_{\{s,t\}}$ is infinite. Hence $\{s,t\}$ is an edge of $L$ exactly when $m(s,t)<\infty$. In that case its local edge-cell length is the value in 1.4; the direction of the $1$-cell $wW_{\{s\}}$ at $w$ is $\mathbb R_{\ge0}(-e_s)$ by 1.5. These rays and their local edge lengths are independent of the positive tuple $(d_s)$. No global shortest-path equality in the whole link is used. [F1, F2, step 1.4, step 1.5]

2.2 Fix $w\in W$. The cells of $\Sigma$ containing $w$ are the cells $wW_T$, $T\in\mathbb S$, and the link of $w$ in $wW_T$ is $\operatorname{Lk}_{C_T}(x_T)$, identified with $\Sigma(B_T)$ in 1.6; for $U\subseteq T$ the face $wW_U$ corresponds to the face $\operatorname{conv}(W_Ux_T)$ and its direction face at $w$ is $\Sigma(B_U)$. The cellulation glues these spherical simplices along those faces by the identity vertex maps $-e_s\mapsto-e_s$, compatible for all inclusions; [F17] gives a chain metric on each connected component, with the weak topology, and auxiliary path distance $+\infty$ between components. Truncating at $\pi$ gives a metric on the whole link with the same weak topology: balls of radius less than $\pi$ are exactly the componentwise intrinsic balls. The angular-link gluing of [F9] identifies this glued link with $\operatorname{Lk}_\Sigma(w)$. By the cell statement in [F7], its cells are exactly the spherical subsets $T\in\mathbb S$, with Gram matrices $B_T$, glued along their common faces, so it is the finite spherical complex $X=|L|_B$. The direction of $wW_{\{s\}}$ corresponds to vertex $s$ of $L$, and the cellwise link isometries induce the isometry of the glued polyhedral complexes and their truncated angular metrics by [F18]. [F7, F9, F15, F16, F17, F18, step 1.6]

3.1 By 2.1 every edge of $X$ has local length $\pi-\pi/m(s,t)\ge\pi/2$, so $X$ is large. For a pairwise adjacent set $T$ of vertices of $L$, the cosine test matrix in [F10] is $B_T$, including the empty matrix when $T=\emptyset$. By [F7], $B_T$ is positive definite exactly when $W_T$ is finite, and by [F2] that is exactly when $T$ spans a simplex of $L$. Thus $X$ is metric flag. The almost-negative matrix of [F10] equals $B$: on edges its entry is $\cos(\pi-\pi/m(s,t))=B(e_s,e_t)$ by 2.1 and [F1], while on non-edges $m(s,t)=\infty$ by 2.1 and the entry is $B(e_s,e_t)=-1$. Therefore $X$ is a finite large metric flag complex with associated matrix $B$. [F1, F2, F3, F7, F10, step 2.1, step 2.2]

4.1 Let $U\in\mathbb S$ and $w\in W$. For a spherical $T\supseteq U$, use the $w$-chart of its cell $wW_T$, so $wW_U$ corresponds to $F_U:=\operatorname{conv}(W_Ux_T)$. Its direction space is $V_U:=\operatorname{span}\{e_u:u\in U\}$: orbit differences lie in $V_U$ by the reflection formula, and the differences $ux_T-x_T=-2d_ue_u$ span it. The facets containing $F_U$ are exactly the $F_s$ of step 1.3 with $s\notin U$, by the face-inclusion criterion [F5]. Thus at a relative interior point of $F_U$, [F6] gives $T_{F_U}C_T=\{\xi:B(v_s^{(T)},\xi)\le0\ (s\in T\setminus U)\}=V_U+\operatorname{cone}\{-e_s:s\in T\setminus U\}$. Let $P_U$ be the $B_T$-orthogonal projection onto $V_U$. Intersecting this cone with $V_U^\perp$ gives exactly the cone generated by $-(e_s-P_Ue_s)$: projection gives one inclusion, and subtracting the $V_U$ component of any such nonnegative combination gives the other. Their normalized Gram matrix is the Schur complement along $U$ from [F18], exactly the normal link of the face $U$ in $\Sigma(B_T)$. In these $w$-charts the face maps of [[lem-cg-finite-coxeter-orbit-polytopes-and-face-metrics]] (3),(4) are $v\mapsto v+z_{T',T}$ for $U\subseteq T\subseteq T'$; their derivatives are inclusions, and the projection onto $V_U$ agrees on $V_T$. Thus they preserve the projected rays, and their cocycle makes the identifications agree on common cofaces. Consequently the link of $wW_U$ is obtained by gluing these spherical face links for $T\supseteq U$; its vertices are exactly the $s\notin U$ for which $U\cup\{s\}$ is spherical, its cells are the $T'$ with $U\cup T'$ spherical, and its simplex Gram matrices are the iterated normalized Schur complements of $B$ along $U$, by [F10] and [F18]. This is the face link $\operatorname{Lk}_X(U)$. For $U=\emptyset$ it is $X$, already finite large metric flag by step 3.1. For nonempty $U$, each link simplex Gram matrix is positive definite by the Schur-complement identity. Its off-diagonal entries are nonpositive: eliminating one vertex $v$ from a current positive-definite Gram matrix with nonpositive off-diagonal entries replaces an off-diagonal entry $c_{st}$ by $c_{st}-c_{sv}c_{tv}\le0$; its new diagonal entries $1-c_{sv}^2$ are positive by positive definiteness, and normalization by their positive square roots preserves signs. Repeating over the vertices of $U$ proves largeness of every link. To check metric flagness, let $Q$ be any pairwise adjacent set in $\operatorname{Lk}_X(U)$. Then $U\cup Q$ is pairwise adjacent in $X$. The link cosine matrix on $Q$ is the positive-diagonal normalization of the Schur complement of $B_U$ in the matrix $B_{U\cup Q}$; this follows entrywise from orthogonal projection off $V_U$ and the link formula [F18]. Since $B_U$ is positive definite, the block Schur-complement identity makes this link matrix positive definite exactly when $B_{U\cup Q}$ is. By step 3.1 the latter is positive definite exactly when $U\cup Q$ spans a simplex of $X$, which is exactly when $Q$ spans a simplex of the link. The empty set passes the empty-matrix convention in [F10]. Thus every cell link is again finite large metric flag. Finally, [F12] identifies a sufficiently small ball at a point in the relative interior of $wW_U$ with the corresponding ball about $(0,o)$ in $\mathbb R^{|U|}\times C(\operatorname{Lk}_\Sigma(wW_U))$; the link computation is independent of $(d_s)$ because its directions are the rays in step 2.1. 5.1 By step 3.1 and step 4.1, $X$ and every face link are finite large metric flag complexes. Under the stated AC assumption, [F11] therefore makes all these links CAT(1). Its compact short-loop argument uses the untruncated intrinsic geodesic metrics of the components supplied by [F17]. A pair at angular distance $<\pi$ and every triangle of perimeter $<2\pi$ lie in one such component; all triangle sides are $<\pi$ by [F13]. Distances between side points are at most half that perimeter, hence also $<\pi$, so the spherical comparison tests agree before and after truncation. This reconciles clause (6) with the completed supplier. AC is used only through [F17] and [F11]; the direct calculations make no further choice selections. Together with the preceding calculations this proves clauses (1)–(6), with the scope of (7). [F11, F13, F16, F17, step 3.1, step 4.1] ∎
