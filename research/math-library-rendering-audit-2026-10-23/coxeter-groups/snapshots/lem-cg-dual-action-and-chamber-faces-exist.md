## Statement

With the notation of [[def-cg-dual-chambers-and-reflection-hyperplanes]]:

**(1) The dual action is an action by linear maps.** For all $w_1,w_2\in W$ and $f\in V^*$ one has $\mathrm{id}\cdot f=f$ and $w_1\cdot(w_2\cdot f)=(w_1w_2)\cdot f$, and for each $w$ the map $f\mapsto w\cdot f$ is linear.

**(2) The faces are nonempty.** Let $f_s\in V^*$ be the dual basis functionals of the basis $(e_s)$ of $V$, i.e. $f_s(e_t)=\delta_{st}$ ([[def-dual-family-associated-to-a-basis]], [[thm-dual-family-is-a-basis-in-finite-dimension]]). For $I\subseteq S$ the functional $f_I:=\sum_{s\notin I}f_s$ satisfies $f_I(e_s)=1$ for $s\notin I$ and $f_I(e_s)=0$ for $s\in I$; hence $f_I\in C_I$ and every face is nonempty. In particular $C^\circ\ni\sum_{s\in S}f_s$ is nonempty and $C_S=\{0\}$.

**(3) Rank two.** Let $s\ne t$ in $S$ and put $P:=\mathbb Re_s+\mathbb Re_t$. Let $P^*:=\mathrm{Hom}(P,\mathbb R)$ be the algebraic dual space of the $2$-dimensional space $P$ ([[def-algebraic-dual-and-linear-functional]]), with the dual basis $f_s,f_t$ of the basis $(e_s,e_t)$ of $P$; functionals on $P$ are the restrictions of functionals on $V$. Let $W_{s,t}:=\langle\rho(s),\rho(t)\rangle\subseteq\mathrm{GL}(V)$ be the matrix subgroup generated by the two reflections. It preserves $P$. For $g\in W_{s,t}$ and $\varphi\in P^*$ define $(g\cdot\varphi)(v):=\varphi(g^{-1}v)$ for $v\in P$; this is a left action of $W_{s,t}$ on $P^*$ by linear maps, the contragredient of its restriction to $P$. The abstract subgroup $\langle s,t\rangle\le W$ acts through its image $W_{s,t}$; no action of all of $W$ on $P^*$ is asserted. Writing $c:=c(s,t)$ (so $c=1$ for $m(s,t)=\infty$) and coordinates $(y_s,y_t)$ on $P^*$, so that $\varphi=y_sf_s+y_tf_t$ and $\varphi(e_s)=y_s$, $\varphi(e_t)=y_t$, the dual generators act by $$\rho(s)^*(y_s,y_t)=(-y_s,\ 2cy_s+y_t),\qquad \rho(t)^*(y_s,y_t)=(y_s+2cy_t,\ -y_t);$$ each is an involution fixing the line $\{\varphi:\varphi(e_s)=0\}=\{y_s=0\}$ pointwise, respectively $\{\varphi:\varphi(e_t)=0\}=\{y_t=0\}$, and the functional $\delta(\varphi):=\varphi(e_s+e_t)$ is preserved by $W_{s,t}$ when $m(s,t)=\infty$. Put $\Phi_P:=W_{s,t}\{e_s,e_t\}\subset P$, the rank-two root system. For $\beta\in\Phi_P$ the rank-two root hyperplane is $H_\beta\cap P^*:=\{\varphi\in P^*:\varphi(\beta)=0\}$.

(i) If $m:=m(s,t)<\infty$: $W_{s,t}$ is a dihedral group of order $2m$, and the $2m$ chambers $wC_P$ ($w\in W_{s,t}$), where $C_P:=\{f\in P^*:f(e_s)\ge0,\ f(e_t)\ge0\}$, are exactly the $2m$ closed sectors cut out in $P^*$ by the $m$ root hyperplanes $H_\beta\cap P^*$ $(\beta\in\Phi_P)$, $\Phi_P:=W_{s,t}\{e_s,e_t\}$. They have pairwise disjoint interiors, their union is all of $P^*$, $W_{s,t}$ acts simply transitively on them, and any two distinct chambers are separated by at least one root hyperplane, i.e. there is $\beta\in\Phi_P$ with the two interiors in opposite open half-planes of $H_\beta$.

(ii) If $m(s,t)=\infty$: the chambers $wC_P$ ($w\in W_{s,t}$) have pairwise disjoint interiors and their union is $\{\varphi\in P^*:\varphi(e_s+e_t)>0\}\cup\{0\}$, that is, the closed half-plane $\{\varphi:\varphi(e_s+e_t)\ge0\}$ with the nonzero points of its boundary line removed; any two distinct chambers are separated by at least one root hyperplane $H_\beta\cap P^*$ $(\beta\in\Phi_P)$, and the traces $H_\beta\cap\{\varphi:\varphi(e_s+e_t)=1\}$ are exactly the integers in the coordinate $y_s$ on that affine line.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** a finite set $S$, a Coxeter matrix $m$, the space $V=\mathbb R^S$, the Coxeter form $B$, the reflections $r_a$, the presented group $W$, the canonical homomorphism $\rho:W\to\mathrm{GL}(V)$ with roots $\Phi$, and the dual action, chambers, faces and root hyperplanes on $V^*$ of [[def-cg-dual-chambers-and-reflection-hyperplanes]]; for distinct $s,t\in S$ the plane $P=\mathbb Re_s+\mathbb Re_t$ and the number $c=c(s,t)$.

[F1] $B(e_s,e_s)=1$ and $B(e_s,e_t)=-c$ for all $s,t$; for $B(a,a)\ne0$ the map $r_a$ is a linear involution preserving $B$ with $r_a(a)=-a$ and $r_a$ the identity on $\ker B(-,a)$; for $a=e_s$ this gives $r_s(e_s)=-e_s$ and $r_s(e_t)=e_t+2ce_s$, and symmetrically $r_t(e_t)=-e_t$, $r_t(e_s)=e_s+2ce_t$; and for finite $m(s,t)$ the restriction $B|_P$ is positive definite ([[def-cg-real-coxeter-form-and-reflection]], [[lem-cg-reflection-form-invariance-and-rank-two-orders]], clauses (2) and (3)(i)–(iv), and Proof step 2.1). In particular, $r_sr_t$ has exact order $m$ on $P$ for finite $m$, while for $m=\infty$ its restriction is $I_2+N$ with $N=\begin{pmatrix}2&-2\\2&-2\end{pmatrix}$ and $(r_sr_t)^k|_P=I_2+kN$.

[F2] $\rho:W\to\mathrm{GL}(V)$ is the unique homomorphism with $\rho(s)=r_s$ for $s\in S$, it satisfies $\rho(gh)=\rho(g)\rho(h)$ and $\rho(g^{-1})=\rho(g)^{-1}$, every $\rho(w)$ preserves $B$, every root has $B$-norm $1$, and $\rho(wsw^{-1})=r_{\rho(w)e_s}$ for all $w\in W$, $s\in S$, so all reflections of $W$ act as the reflections in the roots $\rho(w)e_s\in\Phi$ ([[def-cg-canonical-reflection-homomorphism]], [[lem-cg-reflection-representation-descends-and-root-norms]], clauses (1)–(4)).

[F3] The dual action is $(w\cdot f)(v)=f(\rho(w)^{-1}v)$; the closed chamber is $C=\{f\in V^*:f(e_s)\ge0\text{ for all }s\in S\}$, its interior is $C^\circ=\{f:f(e_s)>0\text{ for all }s\}$, the face of type $I$ is $C_I=\{f:f(e_s)=0\text{ for }s\in I,\ f(e_s)>0\text{ for }s\notin I\}$, and $H_\alpha=\{f\in V^*:f(\alpha)=0\}$ for $\alpha\in\Phi$ ([[def-cg-dual-chambers-and-reflection-hyperplanes]]).

[F4] For a finite-dimensional space the dual space, the dual family of a basis and its basis property are as recorded in the algebraic-dual and dual-basis items; in particular $f_s(e_t)=\delta_{st}$ defines unique elements $f_s$ of the dual of $P$ ([[def-algebraic-dual-and-linear-functional]], [[def-dual-family-associated-to-a-basis]], [[thm-dual-family-is-a-basis-in-finite-dimension]]).

[F5] $V=\mathbb R^S$ consists of the functions $S\to\mathbb R$ with pointwise operations, the functions $e_s$ form a basis of $V$, and $u=\sum_{s\in S}u(s)e_s$ for $u\in V$ ([[def-function-space]], [[lem-cg-reflection-form-invariance-and-rank-two-orders]], clause (1)).

[F6] Trigonometric facts: the addition formulas; $\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1$; $\sin x=0$ if and only if $x\in\pi\mathbb Z$, and $\cos x=0$ if and only if $x=(k+\frac12)\pi$ for some $k\in\mathbb Z$; $\cos(x+\pi)=-\cos x$, $\cos\pi=-1$; $\cos 2x=\cos^2x-\sin^2x$ and $\sin 2x=2\sin x\cos x$ follow from the addition formulas ([[thm-sine-and-cosine-addition-formulas]], [[cor-trigonometric-parity-and-pythagorean-identity]], [[thm-sine-cosine-zero-sets-and-fundamental-period]], [[thm-quarter-turn-values-and-shift-formulas]]).

[F7] $\pi>0$ and $\mathbb R$ is a totally ordered field, so $0<\pi/m\le\pi/2$ for $m\ge2$ and the order arithmetic below is the order of $\mathbb R$ ([[def-pi-via-first-positive-cosine-zero]], [[thm-reals-ordered-field]]).

[F8] Every real $\tau$ has an integer $j$ with $j\le\tau<j+1$ ([[lem-integer-part]]).

[F9] A nonzero linear map from a finite-dimensional real vector space to $\mathbb R$ is onto, because any nonzero value can be scaled to any prescribed real value. Its kernel therefore has codimension one by [[thm-rank-nullity]].

## Proof

**Proof technique:** first the action and face identities, then the explicit two-dimensional model, then the finite and infinite chamber computations.

1.1 The dual action is an action by linear maps. For $w\in W$ and $f\in V^*$ the formula $(w\cdot f)(v)=f(\rho(w)^{-1}v)$ defines an element of $V^*$ because a composite of linear maps is linear, and $f\mapsto w\cdot f$ is linear because evaluation at $v$ is linear in $f$. Moreover $\mathrm{id}\cdot f=f$, since $\rho(\mathrm{id})=\mathrm{id}$, and $$w_1\cdot(w_2\cdot f)=f\circ\rho(w_2)^{-1}\circ\rho(w_1)^{-1}=f\circ\rho(w_1w_2)^{-1}=(w_1w_2)\cdot f,$$ using $\rho(w_1w_2)=\rho(w_1)\rho(w_2)$ and hence $\rho(w_1w_2)^{-1}=\rho(w_2)^{-1}\rho(w_1)^{-1}$. [given, F2, F3, F4]

1.2 Generators on the dual plane. The formulas in [F1] show $r_s(P)=P=r_t(P)$, since each map preserves $P$ and is its own inverse. Hence every $g\in W_{s,t}=\langle r_s,r_t\rangle$ and its inverse preserve $P$. The restriction $g\mapsto g|_P$ is a homomorphism into $\mathrm{GL}(P)$, so $(g\cdot\varphi)(v)=\varphi(g^{-1}v)$ is well-defined on $P^*$ and is a left action: $(gh)\cdot\varphi=\varphi\circ h^{-1}\circ g^{-1}=g\cdot(h\cdot\varphi)$, and the identity acts trivially. Here $s\cdot\varphi$ and $t\cdot\varphi$ denote the actions of $r_s$ and $r_t$ respectively. The dual basis vectors satisfy $(s\cdot f_s)(e_s)=f_s(\rho(s)^{-1}e_s)=f_s(-e_s)=-1$ and $(s\cdot f_s)(e_t)=f_s(\rho(s)e_t)=f_s(e_t+2ce_s)=2c$, while $(s\cdot f_t)(e_s)=f_t(-e_s)=0$ and $(s\cdot f_t)(e_t)=f_t(e_t+2ce_s)=1$; hence in coordinates $s\cdot(y_sf_s+y_tf_t)=(-y_s)f_s+(2cy_s+y_t)f_t$, that is $$\rho(s)^*(y_s,y_t)=(-y_s,\ 2cy_s+y_t).$$ The same computation with $s$ replaced by $t$ gives $\rho(t)^*(y_s,y_t)=(y_s+2cy_t,\ -y_t)$. Both displayed maps are involutions: applying the first twice returns $(y_s,y_t)$, and applying the second twice returns $(y_s,y_t)$. The first fixes the line $\{\varphi:\varphi(e_s)=0\}=\{y_s=0\}$ pointwise and the second fixes $\{\varphi:\varphi(e_t)=0\}=\{y_t=0\}$ pointwise, since these are exactly the vectors whose coordinate in question vanishes. For the functional $\delta(\varphi)=\varphi(e_s+e_t)=y_s+y_t$ one has $\delta(s\cdot\varphi)=(2c-1)y_s+y_t$ and $\delta(t\cdot\varphi)=y_s+(2c-1)y_t$, so each generator preserves $\delta$ exactly when $c=1$, i.e. when $m(s,t)=\infty$; in that case $W_{s,t}$ preserves $\delta$ because it is generated by $r_s$ and $r_t$. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, algebra]

1.3 Faces. Let $I\subseteq S$ and put $f_I:=\sum_{s\notin I}f_s\in V^*$. By the dual-basis property $f_I(e_s)=1$ for $s\notin I$ and $f_I(e_s)=0$ for $s\in I$; hence $f_I\in C_I$ and every face is nonempty. In particular $I=\varnothing$ gives $\sum_{s\in S}f_s\in C^\circ$, so the interior is nonempty, and $I=S$ gives $C_S=\{f:f(e_s)=0\text{ for all }s\in S\}=\{0\}$, since a functional vanishing on the basis $(e_s)$ of $V$ is zero. For a root $\alpha$, $B(\alpha,\alpha)=1$ makes $\alpha\ne0$, so some coordinate $\alpha(s)\ne0$ and $f_s(\alpha)=\alpha(s)\ne0$. Thus $\mathrm{ev}_\alpha:V^*\to\mathbb R$ is a nonzero linear map and $H_\alpha$ is a hyperplane by [F9]. [given, F2, F4, F5, F9]

1.4 The finite rank-two model. Assume $m<\infty$ and put $\theta:=\pi/m$ and $c=\cos\theta$. Then $0<\theta\le\pi/2$, $\sin\theta\ne0$ and $1-c^2=\sin^2\theta$. On $P$ put $u:=e_s$ and $v:=(e_t+ce_s)/\sin\theta$. Then $B(u,u)=B(e_s,e_s)=1$, $B(u,v)=(B(e_s,e_t)+cB(e_s,e_s))/\sin\theta=(-c+c)/\sin\theta=0$ and $B(v,v)=(B(e_t,e_t)+2cB(e_s,e_t)+c^2B(e_s,e_s))/\sin^2\theta=(1-2c^2+c^2)/\sin^2\theta=(1-c^2)/\sin^2\theta=1$: thus $(u,v)$ is a $B|_P$-orthonormal basis of $P$ and $e_s=u$, $e_t=-cu+\sin\theta\,v$. Since $r_s$ fixes $\ker B(-,e_s)\cap P=\mathbb Rv$ pointwise and sends $u=e_s$ to $-u$, in the coordinates $(x_u,x_v)$ of the basis $(u,v)$ it is $r_s(x_u,x_v)=(-x_u,x_v)$; since $\ker B(-,e_t)\cap P$ is spanned by $(\sin\theta,c)$ and $r_t(e_t)=-e_t$, the map $r_t$ is the reflection in the line $\mathbb R(\sin\theta,c)$. Consequently the composite $A:=r_sr_t$ satisfies $A(u)=r_s(e_s+2ce_t)=r_s\bigl((1-2c^2)u+2c\sin\theta\,v\bigr)=(2c^2-1)u+2c\sin\theta\,v=(\cos2\theta)u+(\sin2\theta)v$ and $A(v)=-\sin2\theta\,u+\cos2\theta\,v$, so in the orthonormal coordinates $$A(x_u,x_v)=(x_u\cos2\theta-x_v\sin2\theta,\ x_u\sin2\theta+x_v\cos2\theta),$$ the rotation through $2\theta$, whose order is exactly $m$. [given, F1, F2, F5, F6, F7, algebra]

2.1 The rank-two roots. Assume $m<\infty$ and keep the orthonormal coordinates $(u,v)$ of 1.4, in which $A$ is the rotation through $2\theta$. Then $A^k e_s=(\cos2k\theta)u+(\sin2k\theta)v$ for every $k\in\mathbb Z$, and $r_sA^ke_s=(-\cos2k\theta)u+(\sin2k\theta)v=(\cos(m-2k)\theta)u+(\sin(m-2k)\theta)v$ because $\pi=m\theta$ and $\cos(\pi-\alpha)=-\cos\alpha$, $\sin(\pi-\alpha)=\sin\alpha$. Moreover $e_t=-\cos\theta\,u+\sin\theta\,v=(\cos(m-1)\theta)u+(\sin(m-1)\theta)v$, and the same two formulas applied to $e_t$ give $A^ke_t$ and $r_sA^ke_t$ at angles $(m-1+2k)\theta$ and $(1-2k)\theta$. As $k$ varies over $\mathbb Z$ the angles $2k\theta$ are exactly the even multiples of $\theta$ and the angles $(1-2k)\theta$ are exactly the odd multiples, so every element of $S:=\{(\cos j\theta)u+(\sin j\theta)v:j=0,1,\dots,2m-1\}$ is one of the four displayed families and $S\subseteq\Phi_P$; conversely $S$ contains $e_s$ and $e_t$ and is stable under $A$ (which adds $2\theta$ to an angle) and under $r_s$ (which sends an angle $\varphi$ to $\pi-\varphi=(m\theta-\varphi)$), hence stable under $W_{s,t}=\langle\rho(s),\rho(t)\rangle=\langle\rho(s),A\rangle$, so $\Phi_P\subseteq S$. Therefore $$\Phi_P=W_{s,t}\{e_s,e_t\}=\{(\cos j\theta)u+(\sin j\theta)v:\ j=0,1,\dots,2m-1\},$$ a set of exactly $2m$ unit vectors, and $\Phi_P$ is stable under $\beta\mapsto-\beta$. [given, F1, F2, F6, step 1.4]

2.2 The infinite case. Assume $m=\infty$, so $c=1$ and by 1.2 the generators act by $s\cdot(y_s,y_t)=(-y_s,2y_s+y_t)$ and $t\cdot(y_s,y_t)=(y_s+2y_t,-y_t)$ and preserve $\delta=y_s+y_t$. On the affine line $\{\delta=1\}$ parametrized by $\tau=y_s$ these maps read $\tau\mapsto-\tau$ and $\tau\mapsto2-\tau$, the two reflections of the line about $0$ and $1$. Every word in the two involutions rewrites to $A^k$ or $A^kr_s$ with $k\in\mathbb Z$, where $A=r_sr_t$; their actions on this affine line are respectively $\tau\mapsto\tau-2k$ and $\tau\mapsto-\tau-2k$. These are pairwise distinct, so restriction to $P$ is faithful on $W_{s,t}$. The interval images are $A^k[0,1]=[-2k,1-2k]$ and $A^kr_s[0,1]=[-1-2k,-2k]$, so they are exactly $[j,j+1]$ for $j\in\mathbb Z$, with distinct elements giving distinct intervals. These cover the affine line by [F8] and have pairwise disjoint interiors. Since $C_P$ is the cone on $[0,1]$ in the half-plane $\{\delta\ge0\}$ and the action is linear, the chamber $wC_P$ is the cone on the interval corresponding to $w$; these cones have pairwise disjoint interiors and their union is $\{\varphi:\delta(\varphi)>0\}\cup\{0\}$, because every point with $\delta>0$ is a positive multiple of a unique point of $\{\delta=1\}$ lying in a unit interval, while every point of a cone on a unit interval has $\delta\ge0$ and only $0$ has $\delta=0$. By [F1] and the normal forms above, the roots are precisely the four families $A^ke_s=(1+2k,2k)$, $A^ke_t=(-2k,1-2k)$, $A^kr_se_s=(-1-2k,-2k)$ and $A^kr_se_t=(2+2k,1+2k)$ for $k\in\mathbb Z$. Equivalently $\Phi_P=\{(j+1,j),(j,j+1):j\in\mathbb Z\}$, since the first and fourth families cover the even and odd values of $j$ in the first pair, and the second and third do so in the second pair. On $\{\delta=1\}$ the equation $\varphi(ae_s+be_t)=0$ is $(a-b)y_s+b=0$, so the two root families give wall traces $-j$ and $j+1$, exactly the integers. If two chamber intervals are $[j,j+1]$ and $[l,l+1]$ with $j<l$, the wall whose trace is $j+1$ places their interiors in opposite open half-planes, because its defining linear form on $\{\delta>0\}$ has the sign of $y_s/\delta-(j+1)$ up to a fixed nonzero factor. [given, F1, F2, F8, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 The rank-two walls. Assume $m<\infty$. The map $\flat:P\to P^*$, $x\mapsto B(x,\cdot)$, is an isomorphism: in the orthonormal basis $(u,v)$ of 1.4 its inverse sends $\varphi$ to $\varphi(u)u+\varphi(v)v$. Moreover it intertwines the two actions, since $(g\cdot\flat(x))(z)=B(x,g^{-1}z)=B(gx,z)=\flat(gx)(z)$ for $g\in W_{s,t}$. For $\beta\in P$ one has $\flat(\beta^\perp)=H_\beta\cap P^*$, where $\beta^\perp=\{x\in P:B(x,\beta)=0\}$: indeed $\flat(x)(\beta)=B(x,\beta)$ vanishes exactly when $x\in\beta^\perp$. By 2.1 each $\beta\in\Phi_P$ is a unit vector at an angle $j\theta$, so $\beta^\perp$ is the line at angle $j\theta+\pi/2$, and the lines $H_\beta\cap P^*$ depend only on the pair $\{\beta,-\beta\}$: there are exactly $m$ of them, at angles $\pi/2+j\theta$, $j=0,\dots,m-1$, equally spaced by $\theta$. The chamber $C_P=\{\varphi\in P^*:\varphi(e_s)\ge0,\ \varphi(e_t)\ge0\}$ corresponds under $\flat$ to $\{x\in P:B(x,e_s)\ge0,\ B(x,e_t)\ge0\}$, whose boundary lines are $e_s^\perp$ (the line at angle $\pi/2$) and $e_t^\perp$ (the line at angle $(m-1)\theta+\pi/2$, i.e. $\pi/2-\theta$): so $C_P=\flat$ of the closed sector between these two consecutive walls, of angle $\theta$, and no root hyperplane line meets $C_P^\circ$ because the $m$ walls are equally spaced by $\theta$ and contain both boundary lines. [given, F1, F2, F3, F6, step 1.4, step 2.1]

4.1 The finite chamber tiling. Assume $m<\infty$. Put $a:=r_s$, $b:=r_t$, and $A:=ab$. Since $a^2=b^2=1$ and $aAa=A^{-1}$, every word in $a,b$ rewrites to $A^k$ or $A^ka$. The finite relation $A^m=1$ therefore gives at most $2m$ elements, represented by $0\le k<m$. Their restrictions to $P$ are all distinct: the rotations $A^k|_P$ are distinct by their exact order $m$, the maps $A^ka|_P$ are distinct after cancellation of $a|_P$, and a rotation cannot equal a reflection because their determinants on $P$ are $1$ and $-1$. Thus restriction to $P$ is faithful on this matrix subgroup, $|W_{s,t}|=2m$, and it is the dihedral group of order $2m$. Its contragredient action on $P^*$ has the same distinct elements. The action maps the set of $m$ walls to itself, because $g\cdot(H_\beta\cap P^*)=H_{g\beta}\cap P^*$ for $g\in W_{s,t}$ and $\Phi_P$ is $W_{s,t}$-stable, so it permutes the $2m$ closed sectors; it maps the sector $C_P$ to the sectors obtained by successive rotations through $2\theta$ and by the reflection $\rho(s)$, which realizes all $2m$ of them: under $\flat^{-1}$, $C_P$ has angular interval $[\pi/2-\theta,\pi/2]$ and $r_sC_P$ has interval $[\pi/2,\pi/2+\theta]$; their $A^k$ images add $2k\theta$, giving all consecutive sectors modulo $2\pi$. Thus the orbit of $C_P$ is exactly the set of closed sectors cut out by the $m$ root hyperplanes. Since $|W_{s,t}|=2m$ equals the number of sectors, the action on the orbit is simply transitive, and the sectors have pairwise disjoint interiors and union $P^*$ as the $2m$ sectors of $m$ distinct lines through the origin. Finally, a sign choice for each of the $m$ wall forms defines an intersection of open half-planes. If nonempty it is convex, by linearity of those forms, and so lies in a single sector: the segment joining any two of its points meets no wall. Thus distinct sector interiors have different signs for some wall form, which supplies a root hyperplane separating their entire interiors. ## Remarks

Davis, Example D.2.1(i), printed p. 442, describes the infinite-dihedral Tits cone as the closed half-plane. For the literal union of the chamber images, step 2.2 shows that every nonzero point of its boundary is absent; the closed half-plane is the closure of that union.
