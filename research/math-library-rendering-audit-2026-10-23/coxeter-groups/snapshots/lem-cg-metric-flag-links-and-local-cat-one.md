## Statement

Let $X$ be a finite large metric flag complex ([[def-cg-large-spherical-metric-flag-and-almost-negative-matrix]]).

All CAT(1) assertions for a possibly disconnected complex use the truncated angular metric $d_\pi$; local balls of radius less than $\pi/2$ use the componentwise chain metric, which agrees with $d_\pi$ there.

**(i) Links stay large and metric flag.** For every face $F$ of $X$, $\operatorname{Lk}_X(F)$ is a finite large metric flag complex; for $F=\emptyset$ this is $X$. For nonempty $F$, the face-link entries are obtained by deleting the vertices of $F$ one at a time using [[lem-cg-spherical-simplex-existence-and-link-gram-formula]](iv). At each deletion of a vertex $v$, the normalized off-diagonal entry becomes $(c_{st}-c_{sv}c_{tv})/\sqrt{(1-c_{sv}^{2})(1-c_{tv}^{2})}\le0$, because the current entries $c_{sv},c_{tv}$ are nonpositive and the denominator is positive. Thus every simplex link matrix is positive definite with diagonal $1$ and nonpositive off-diagonal entries. If $T$ is pairwise adjacent in the link, then $F\cup T$ is pairwise adjacent in $X$, and the link cosine matrix on $T$ is the diagonally normalized Schur complement of $C_F$ in the principal cosine matrix $C_{F\cup T}$. Since $C_F$ is positive definite, the block Schur-complement identity gives that this link matrix is positive definite exactly when $C_{F\cup T}$ is; metric flagness of $X$ makes the latter equivalent to $F\cup T$ being a simplex, which is equivalent to $T$ being a simplex of the link ([[def-definiteness-inertia-and-signature-data-over-the-reals]]).

**(ii) Local models.** Let $F$ be a nonempty face, put $k:=\dim F$, and let $p\in\operatorname{relint}F$. The unit directions in the tangent cone at $p$ form the spherical join $\operatorname{Lk}_X(p)=S^{k-1}*\operatorname{Lk}_X(F)$, where $S^{k-1}$ is the round sphere of unit directions tangent to $F$, $\operatorname{Lk}_X(F)$ consists of the normal directions, and $S^{-1}=\emptyset$ when $F$ is a vertex ([[def-cg-euclidean-cone-and-spherical-join-metrics]](5), [[thm-cg-cone-join-metric-and-local-product-chart]](3)). There is $\varepsilon>0$ such that the cellwise radial map identifies the open ball $B(p,\varepsilon)$ isometrically with the open ball of radius $\varepsilon$ about the pole in the spherical cone $\{p\}*\operatorname{Lk}_X(p)$. For $x\ne p$ in this ball the radial coordinate is the chain distance $\theta=d(p,x)$ and the direction $u\in\operatorname{Lk}_X(p)$ is unique; at $\theta=0$ all directions represent the pole. In a cell containing $p$, the map is the spherical polar chart and its distance formula is $\cos d_\sigma(x,y)=\cos\theta\cos\theta'+\sin\theta\sin\theta'\cos d_{\operatorname{Lk}_\sigma(p)}(u,u')$. For a vertex $v$, cellwise radial coordinates are defined throughout $\operatorname{St}(v)$, and $B(v,\pi/2)$ is exactly the open radial cone with $0\le\theta<\pi/2$ on $\operatorname{Lk}_X(v)$. If $\operatorname{Lk}_X(F)$ is CAT(1) ([[def-cg-cat-zero-cat-one-and-local-geodesic]](3)), then $\operatorname{Lk}_X(p)$ and its spherical cone are CAT(1) ([[lem-cg-cat-zero-products-and-cat-one-joins]](ii),(iii)). For every $0<r<\min\{\varepsilon,\pi/2\}$, the closed ball $\bar B(p,r)$ is isometric to the closed radius-$r$ ball in that cone, hence is convex and CAT(1) with its induced metric ([[def-metric-ball]], [[lem-cg-comparison-convexity-and-model-spaces]](v)).

**(iii) The inductive local-curvature implication.** Assume that every finite large metric flag complex of dimension $<d$ is CAT(1). Then every $d$-dimensional finite large metric flag complex $X$ is locally CAT(1): for each nonempty face $F$, $\operatorname{Lk}_X(F)$ is a finite large metric flag complex of dimension at most $d-\dim F-1<d$ by (i), with the empty link assigned dimension $-1$; it is CAT(1) by hypothesis or by (iv). Clause (ii) then applies at every point because the relative interiors of the nonempty faces partition $X$. This is the induction step only; it is not an unconditional lemma about $X$.

**(iv) Dimension zero and truncation.** A $0$-dimensional finite large metric flag complex is a finite set of isolated vertices; with the truncated metric distinct vertices are at distance $\pi$, there are no distinct pairs at distance $<\pi$ and every triangle of perimeter $<2\pi$ is constant, so the CAT(1) tests hold vacuously, and the empty link is CAT(1) vacuously with one-point cone ([[def-cg-euclidean-cone-and-spherical-join-metrics]](3)). More generally, every $d_\pi$-triangle of perimeter $<2\pi$ in a finite spherical complex has all three sides $<\pi$ and lies in one component, where the sides equal the componentwise intrinsic path distances; its comparison test is therefore unchanged by truncation ([[thm-cg-cone-join-metric-and-local-product-chart]](1), [[def-cg-cat-zero-cat-one-and-local-geodesic]](4)).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite large metric flag complex $X$ with its vertex complex $K$ ([[def-cg-large-spherical-metric-flag-and-almost-negative-matrix]]), a face $F$ of $X$, and, in clause (iii), a dimension bound $d$.

[F1] Every simplex Gram matrix is positive definite with diagonal $1$; largeness means its off-diagonal entries are nonpositive; metric flagness says a pairwise adjacent vertex set spans a simplex exactly when its cosine matrix is positive definite; and the cells of $\operatorname{Lk}_X(F)$ are the sets $T$ for which $F\cup T$ is a simplex. The empty matrix is positive definite by convention. ([[def-cg-large-spherical-metric-flag-and-almost-negative-matrix]])

[F2] The link Gram matrix of a spherical simplex is obtained by the normalized Schur complement, is positive definite, and iteration over a face gives the face link and agrees with orthogonal projection off the linear span of that face; spherical simplices are convex and have unique barycentric ray coordinates. ([[lem-cg-spherical-simplex-existence-and-link-gram-formula]](ii),(iv))

[F3] A symmetric matrix is positive definite when its quadratic form is positive on every nonzero vector. ([[def-definiteness-inertia-and-signature-data-over-the-reals]])

[F4] A spherical simplex is a convex subset of a unit sphere; its cell distance is the round distance, its short radial geodesics satisfy the spherical cosine formula, and its face-link cells are the normal direction sections given by the Schur-complement projection in [F2]. The finite complex is glued along isometric faces and its componentwise chain distance is the infimum of finite cell-chain lengths. ([[lem-cg-spherical-simplex-existence-and-link-gram-formula]](ii),(iv), [[def-cg-large-spherical-metric-flag-and-almost-negative-matrix]](1), [[def-simplicial-subcomplex-star-closure-and-link]])

[F5] CAT(1) uses geodesics for pairs at distance $<\pi$ and spherical comparison for geodesic triangles of perimeter $<2\pi$; local CAT(1) means every point has a CAT(1) closed ball. The truncated angular metric is $d_\pi=\min\{\pi,d\}$, with infinite componentwise distances replaced by $\pi$. ([[def-cg-cat-zero-cat-one-and-local-geodesic]](3),(4), [[def-cg-large-spherical-metric-flag-and-almost-negative-matrix]](1))

[F6] Every round sphere $S^k$ is CAT(1) with diameter at most $\pi$; the join of CAT(1) spaces of diameter at most $\pi$ is CAT(1), and the one-point spherical cone $\{p\}*L$ is CAT(1) when $L$ is CAT(1). These are the claims of [[lem-cg-cat-zero-products-and-cat-one-joins]](ii),(iii); the consumer uses are verified in steps 3.2 and 4.1.

[F7] The truncated angular metric is $d_\pi=\min\{\pi,d_{\mathrm{path}}\}$, and the spherical join distance is given by $\cos d=\cos\theta\cos\theta'\cos d^1_\pi+\sin\theta\sin\theta'\cos d^2_\pi$, with $L*\emptyset=L$; the product-cone isometry identifies the join metric on joined cells with the intrinsic face metric. ([[def-cg-euclidean-cone-and-spherical-join-metrics]](2),(5), [[thm-cg-cone-join-metric-and-local-product-chart]](3))

[F8] In a CAT(1) space every closed ball of radius $<\pi/2$ is convex, and a convex subset with its induced metric is CAT(1). ([[lem-cg-comparison-convexity-and-model-spaces]](v), [[lem-cg-cat-zero-products-and-cat-one-joins]](iii))

[F9] Every closed ball in a round sphere of radius less than $\pi/2$ is geodesically convex. ([[lem-cg-comparison-convexity-and-model-spaces]](ii))

[F10] $B(p,r)$ denotes the open metric ball of radius $r$ and $\bar B(p,r)$ the closed ball. ([[def-metric-ball]])

## Proof
1.1 If $M$ is positive definite with diagonal $1$ and off-diagonal entries at most $0$, deleting its vertex $0$ gives the Schur complement $m'_{ij}=m_{ij}-m_{i0}m_{j0}$. For any nonzero vector $x$ on the remaining indices, the vector $w=(-\sum_{i\ne0}m_{0i}x_i,x)$ is nonzero and satisfies $w^{\mathsf T}Mw=x^{\mathsf T}M'x>0$; hence $M'$ is positive definite. In particular, testing a coordinate vector gives $1-m_{i0}^2>0$ on each diagonal. The off-diagonal entries are nonpositive because $m_{i0}m_{j0}\ge0$; normalizing by $D=\operatorname{diag}((1-m_{i0}^2)^{-1/2})$ preserves positive definiteness and gives diagonal $1$ with off-diagonal entries $m'_{ij}/\sqrt{(1-m_{i0}^2)(1-m_{j0}^2)}\le0$. [F3, algebra]

1.2 For a symmetric block matrix $A=\begin{pmatrix}P&Q\\Q^{\mathsf T}&R\end{pmatrix}$ with $P$ positive definite, $P$ is invertible: $Pv=0$ for nonzero $v$ would contradict $v^{\mathsf T}Pv>0$. Put $S=R-Q^{\mathsf T}P^{-1}Q$. For $x=(y,z)$, $x^{\mathsf T}Ax=(y+P^{-1}Qz)^{\mathsf T}P(y+P^{-1}Qz)+z^{\mathsf T}Sz$. Thus $A$ is positive definite if $S$ is, and if $A$ is positive definite then testing $y=-P^{-1}Qz$ for each nonzero $z$ shows that $S$ is positive definite. [F3, algebra]

1.3 Fix a spherical simplex $\sigma$ and $p$ in the relative interior of its face $F$. In the barycentric ray coordinates of [F2], the local inequalities for $\sigma$ at $p$ are $\dot\lambda_j\ge0$ for vertices $j\notin F$ and no sign restriction on the face-coordinate variations, subject to $\sum_i\dot\lambda_i=0$. The radial normalization chart and its inverse $y\mapsto y/\varphi(y)$ are smooth, so their differentials identify these coordinate tangent cones with the tangent cone in the sphere; its lineality is exactly the subspace with all $\dot\lambda_j=0$ outside $F$, namely $U:=T_pF$. Since $U$ and $-U$ lie in the convex cone $T_p\sigma$, subtracting the orthogonal projection onto $U$ shows $T_p\sigma=U\oplus(T_p\sigma\cap U^\perp)$. [F2, algebra]

1.4 Work in the component of $p$, with its componentwise chain metric. Let $\operatorname{St}(p)$ be the finite union of cells containing $p$. For each such spherical simplex $\sigma$, every face $\tau$ not containing $p$ is compact and disjoint from $p$, so the minimum of $d_\sigma(p,y)$ over $y\in\tau$ is positive. Choose $\delta>0$ below all these finitely many positive minima and with $\delta<\pi/2$; if there are no such faces, choose any $0<\delta<\pi/2$. On $\operatorname{St}(p)$ the cellwise radial function $r(x)=d_\sigma(p,x)$ is well defined and is $1$-Lipschitz on every incident cell. A chain from $p$ that leaves $\operatorname{St}(p)$ first reaches a face $\tau$ of an incident cell not containing $p$; at that point its prefix has length at least the cellwise radius, which is at least $\delta$. Thus if $d_X(p,x)<\delta$, every cell containing $x$ contains $p$. [F2, F4, algebra]

2.1 Iterating step 1.1 over the vertices of any nonempty face $F$ shows that every simplex of its face link has a positive-definite Gram matrix with diagonal $1$ and nonpositive off-diagonal entries. The link is finite, so it is a finite large spherical complex. [F1, F2, step 1.1]

2.2 In a simplex $\sigma\supseteq F$, the unit directions in $U=T_pF$ form $S^{k-1}$, while the directions in $T_p\sigma\cap U^\perp$ are the normal directions of $F$; their face-link Gram matrix is the Schur complement from [F2]. The orthogonal splitting of step 1.3 expresses each cell of the point link as the join of $S^{k-1}$ with the corresponding face-link cell. On each such cell, the inner-product formula is the spherical-join formula [F7], and the product-cone isometry identifies the induced face metrics with the intrinsic join metric [F7]. The identifications agree on common faces, so $\operatorname{Lk}_X(p)=S^{k-1}*\operatorname{Lk}_X(F)$ with the join metric. For a vertex $F$, the first factor is empty and the point link is the face link. [F2, F4, F7, step 1.3, algebra]

2.3 Choose $0<\varepsilon<\min\{\delta/4,\pi/4\}$. Map a cone point $(\theta,u)$ to the point at distance $\theta$ on the cellwise spherical geodesic from $p$ in direction $u$; common-face identifications make this map $\Phi$ well defined. The radial path has length $\theta$, while every chain staying in $\operatorname{St}(p)$ has length at least the variation of $r$, and every chain leaving it has length at least $\delta$. Hence the radial coordinate equals $d_X(p,x)$ for $\theta<\varepsilon$, and $\Phi$ is a bijection from the cone ball onto $B(p,\varepsilon)$, with all directions collapsed at the pole. If $d_\pi(u,u')<\pi$ and $\theta,\theta'>0$, then for every $\eta>0$ small enough there is a finite chain of link directions with consecutive directions in one link cell and total angle $\alpha<d_{\mathrm{path}}(u,u')+\eta<\pi$. Unfold its spherical sectors about their common radial boundaries into a unit-sphere sector of angle $\alpha$. Each sector map is an isometry by the spherical cosine formula, and the great-circle segment between radii $\theta,\theta'<\varepsilon$ stays in the radius-$\varepsilon$ cap by [F9]; subdividing at its finitely many sector crossings and mapping the pieces back gives a path in $X$ of length $\arccos(\cos\theta\cos\theta'+\sin\theta\sin\theta'\cos\alpha)$. Letting $\eta\downarrow0$ gives the upper distance bound by the cone formula. If either radius is $0$, the radial segment gives equality; if $d_\pi(u,u')=\pi$, the path through $p$ has length $\theta+\theta'$, the cone distance. Conversely, let a chain from $x$ to $y$ have length $L$. If $L\ge2\varepsilon$, then the cone distance is at most $\theta+\theta'\le2\varepsilon\le L$. If $L<2\varepsilon$, every point of the chain has distance from $p$ less than $3\varepsilon<\delta$, so each common cell contains $p$; in that cell the two directions lie in one link cell. Their global truncated link distance is at most their spherical distance in this cell, since the cell segment is an admissible link chain. The cone cosine formula is nondecreasing in that angle, so $\Phi^{-1}$ does not increase this chain step. No global isometry of a link cell is assumed. The cone distance is therefore at most $L$. Taking infima proves the isometry of the open balls. [F4, F7, F9, step 1.4, algebra]

3.1 If $F=\emptyset$, the link claim is the hypothesis on $X$; take $F$ nonempty. Let $T$ be pairwise adjacent in $\operatorname{Lk}_X(F)$. Then $F\cup T$ is pairwise adjacent in $X$. The diagonal link entries are $1$, and each off-diagonal link entry for $s,t\in T$ is the Schur-complement entry from the simplex $F\cup\{s,t\}$; therefore the full link cosine matrix on $T$ is the diagonally normalized Schur complement of $C_F$ in the principal cosine matrix $C_{F\cup T}$. Positive diagonal normalization preserves positive definiteness. Since $C_F$ is positive definite, step 1.2 says this link matrix is positive definite exactly when $C_{F\cup T}$ is. Metric flagness of $X$ makes that equivalent to $F\cup T$ being a simplex, which is equivalent to $T$ being a simplex in the link. The empty set is covered by the empty-matrix convention in [F1], and simplex link matrices are positive definite by step 2.1; hence the link is metric flag and clause (i) follows. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.2, step 2.1]

3.2 If $\operatorname{Lk}_X(F)$ is CAT(1), step 2.2 and [F6] show that $\operatorname{Lk}_X(p)$ is CAT(1), and the one-point join $\{p\}*\operatorname{Lk}_X(p)$ is CAT(1) as well. For $0<r<\min\{\varepsilon,\pi/2\}$, step 2.3 identifies $\bar B_X(p,r)$ with the closed radius-$r$ ball about the pole in that cone; [F8] makes this ball convex and CAT(1) with its induced metric. This proves the local CAT(1) conclusion at $p$. [F6, F8, F10, step 2.2, step 2.3]

3.3 Let $v$ be a vertex. For a point $x$ in a simplex containing $v$, its cellwise radial distance $\theta=d_\sigma(v,x)$ and direction are defined throughout that simplex. If $y$ lies in the opposite face $\tau$ not containing $v$, its barycentric ray coordinates involve only vertices $t\ne v$, so $\langle v,y\rangle$ has the sign of $\sum_{t\ne v}\lambda_t c_{vt}\le0$; hence $d_\sigma(v,y)\ge\pi/2$. The radial function is $1$-Lipschitz on every cell containing $v$, so every chain leaving $\operatorname{St}(v)$ has length at least $\pi/2$. For $x\in\operatorname{St}(v)$ with cellwise radius $\theta<\pi/2$, chains staying in the star have length at least $\theta$, chains leaving have length at least $\pi/2$, and the radial segment has length $\theta$; thus $d_X(v,x)=\theta$. If $x\in\operatorname{St}(v)$ has cellwise radius $\theta\ge\pi/2$, chains staying in the star have length at least $\theta$ and chains leaving have length at least $\pi/2$, so $d_X(v,x)\ge\pi/2$; points outside the star also have distance at least $\pi/2$. Conversely, every unit link direction has a representation $\eta=\sum_{t\ne v}b_t(u_t-c_{vt}v)$ with $b_t\ge0$ in an incident simplex. Its radial ray is $\cos\theta\,v+\sin\theta\,\eta=(\cos\theta-\sin\theta\sum_{t\ne v}b_tc_{vt})v+\sin\theta\sum_{t\ne v}b_tu_t$; all coefficients are nonnegative for $0\le\theta\le\pi/2$. Hence every direction extends through that radius within the star, and $B(v,\pi/2)$ is exactly the open radial cone on $\operatorname{Lk}_X(v)$. [F1, F2, F4, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 Suppose every finite large metric flag complex of dimension less than $d$ is CAT(1), and let $X$ have dimension $d$. For each nonempty face $F$, clause (i) gives a finite large metric flag link with $\dim\operatorname{Lk}_X(F)\le d-\dim F-1<d$; a nonempty link is CAT(1) by the hypothesis, and an empty link satisfies the CAT(1) tests vacuously by [F5]. Clause (ii), proved in step 3.2, then gives a CAT(1) neighborhood at every point of $\operatorname{relint}F$. These relative interiors partition $X$, so $X$ is locally CAT(1), proving clause (iii). [F1, F5, step 3.1, step 3.2]

5.1 A zero-dimensional finite large metric flag complex is a finite set of vertices with distinct vertices at truncated distance $\pi$. There are no distinct pairs at distance less than $\pi$, and every triangle with at least two distinct vertices has perimeter at least $2\pi$, so the CAT(1) tests hold; the empty link is CAT(1) vacuously. More generally, if a triangle in a finite spherical complex has $d_\pi$-perimeter less than $2\pi$, none of its sides can equal $\pi$ because the triangle inequality would force the other two sides to sum to at least $\pi$. Each side is therefore the componentwise intrinsic distance, and all vertices lie in one component, so its comparison is unchanged when using the truncated metric. This proves clause (iv). [F5, F7, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
