## Statement

In Blass's parameter-HOD model $N$, every ultrafilter on every set is
principal.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Work over the countable transitive $M\models\mathrm{ZF}+V=L$ and its Cohen extension $M[G]$ from the Blass construction. The target argument inside $N$ is choice-free. The ground $M$ and every finite-coordinate intermediate extension satisfy ZFC: $V=L$ supplies ground Choice, and forcing preserves it. Choice is used only for the finite-coordinate cardinal and ultrapower arguments in steps 7.1--8.1, not in the tail-flip, least-partition, parameter-coding, or $W$-rank arguments.

[F1] [[def-blass-finite-modification-classes-and-parameter-hod-model]] defines $P$, the coordinate reals $a_n$, their finite-modification classes, $f$, the finite parameter reservoir $S$, and the hereditary parameter-HOD class $N$.

[F2] [[def-ultrafilter]] defines principal and free ultrafilters and requires filters to be proper.

[F3] [[thm-ultrafilter-characterisation]] gives complement decision for an ultrafilter and the equivalent finite-intersection and upward-closure laws.

[F4] [[thm-small-forcing-does-not-create-measurable-cardinals]] locally defines an uncountable measurable cardinal by a nonprincipal ultrafilter on $\kappa$ closed under intersections of length below $\kappa$, proves that small forcing cannot create one, and supplies both the normal ultrapower embedding and seed-measure directions needed in step 8.1.

[F5] [[lem-feferman-tail-complement-automorphism]] gives the finite-condition calculation for complementing the unused tail of one Cohen coordinate.

[F6] [[lem-forcing-truth-lemma]] supplies a condition in the actual generic filter forcing any true fixed formula with the displayed parameters.

[F7] [[lem-forcing-monotonicity-density-and-decision]] supplies persistence, density closure, and decision density for the tail forcing.

[F8] [[lem-symmetry-lemma-for-forcing-automorphisms]] transports forcing statements under the finite-bit and infinite-tail automorphisms.

[F9] [[thm-hod-is-an-inner-model-containing-l]] proves the HOD axiom checks from finite definition-code composition; the same checks will be relativized below to $f$ and finitely many members of $S$.

[F10] [[thm-constructibility-is-absolute-and-l-is-minimal]] makes $L$ absolute between transitive ZF inner models with the same ordinals.

[F11] [[def-axiom-of-choice]] records the Choice assumption used in the ground, finite-coordinate forcing extensions, cardinal comparisons, and normal-measure ultrapowers.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** eliminate free ultrafilters first on $\omega$, then on ordinals, identify the model with the hierarchy generated by well-ordered unions, and finish by induction on that hierarchy.

1.1 Let $D$ be the class of sets uniquely definable in $M[G]$ from $f$, finitely many members of $S\setminus\{f\}$, and finitely many ordinals, so that $N=\{x:\operatorname{TC}(\{x\})\subseteq D\}$. Since $S$ is definable from $f$, $D$ and $N$ are definable from $f$. Finite lists of definition parameters concatenate, exactly as in F9, so $D$ is closed under every fixed uniquely defined operation on finitely many members of $D$. The hereditary clause gives transitivity and all ordinals. Pairing, Union, Infinity, Extensionality and Foundation follow as in the ordinary HOD proof. For a fixed formula, Separation in $N$ is obtained by defining the required subset after relativizing quantifiers to the definable class $N$; internal Power Set is $\mathcal P^{M[G]}(x)\cap N$; and Replacement is the set of uniquely specified $N$-outputs. Substitution of the finite definitions of the parameters puts each resulting set in $D$, while transitivity puts all its descendants in $D$. Thus $N$ is a transitive ZF inner model of $M[G]$. No well-order of $S$ and no Choice in $N$ was used. [F1, F9]

2.1 Suppose toward a contradiction that $U\in N$ is a free ultrafilter on $\omega$. By F3, a finite set in $U$ would put one of its singleton pieces in $U$, making $U$ principal; hence $U$ omits every finite set and contains every cofinite set. By F1, $U$ has a unique definition from $f$, ordinals, and finitely many reals $s_1,\ldots,s_t\in S\setminus\{f\}$. Each $s_i$ is a finite modification either of $a_{m_i}$ or of its complement. Choose $k=1+\max(\{m_1,\ldots,m_t\}\cup\{0\})$, so every coordinate occurring among the real parameters is below $k$. Let $A$ be whichever of $a_k$ and $\omega\setminus a_k$ belongs to $U$, as supplied by F3. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, assume-contra]

3.1 By F6, some finite $p\in G$ forces the unique defining formula for $U$ together with $A\in U$. Apply F5 with $n=k-1$: flip precisely the bits of coordinate $k$ above the finite domain of $p$. Since every $m_i<k$, this fixes $p$, every $s_i$, and all ordinals. It fixes $f$ because it interchanges the two finite-modification classes in $f(k)$ and fixes every other value. Its image $A'$ is equal modulo a finite set to $\omega\setminus A$. F8 therefore makes the same $p$ force $A'\in U$. In $M[G]$, both $A$ and $A'$ belong to $U$, so their finite intersection belongs to $U$, contradicting step 2.1. The involution treats the two possible choices of $A$ identically. Consequently every ultrafilter on $\omega$ in $N$ is principal. [F5, F6, F8, step 2.1, discharge-contradiction]

4.1 Suppose now that some ordinal carries a free ultrafilter in $N$, and let $\kappa$ be the least such ordinal with witness $U$. Step 3.1 and transport along a bijection show that $\kappa$ is uncountable. Let $\gamma$ be the least ordinal for which there is a partition $\langle A_\xi:\xi<\gamma\rangle$ of $\kappa$ with every $A_\xi\notin U$; it exists with $\gamma\leq\kappa$ by the singleton partition. If $\gamma<\kappa$, the least-piece map $h:\kappa\to\gamma$ pushes $U$ to an ultrafilter $h_*U$ on $\gamma$. It cannot be principal, since $\{\xi\}\in h_*U$ would say $A_\xi\in U$. This contradicts the minimality of $\kappa$, so $\gamma=\kappa$. The same pushforward along a hypothetical bijection from $\kappa$ to a smaller ordinal shows that $\kappa$ is a cardinal. [F2, F3, step 3.1, assume-contra]

5.1 The ultrafilter $U$ is uniform: if $B\in U$ had cardinality $\lambda<\kappa$, restricting $U$ to $B$ and transporting it along a bijection $B\to\lambda$ would give a free ultrafilter below $\kappa$. It is also $\kappa$-complete. Otherwise choose $\eta<\kappa$ and $B_\xi\in U$ for $\xi<\eta$ with $C=\bigcap_{\xi<\eta}B_\xi\notin U$. The sets consisting of points whose least failed membership test is $\xi$, together with $C$, partition $\kappa$ into $\eta+1<\kappa$ many $U$-small pieces: the $\xi$-piece is contained in $\kappa\setminus B_\xi$, and $C$ is small by assumption. This contradicts step 4.1. The empty intersection is $\kappa\in U$, so the argument includes $\eta=0$. [F3, F4, step 4.1]

6.1 Fix a finite list of $S$-real and ordinal parameters uniquely defining $U$, and let $K\subseteq\omega$ be the finite set of their Cohen-coordinate indices. Put $V_0=M[G\mathbin{\upharpoonright}(K\times\omega)]$ and factor the remaining forcing as $Q=\operatorname{Fn}((\omega\setminus K)\times\omega,2)$. Since $M=L$, every member of the finite-real extension $V_0=L[\langle a_k:k\in K\rangle]$ is hereditarily definable from those finitely many permitted reals and ordinals, so $V_0\subseteq N$. Let "$A\in\dot U$" abbreviate the forcing-language assertion that $A$ belongs to the unique object satisfying the fixed definition of $U$; this avoids choosing a noncanonical name. If $q,r\in Q$, flip the finitely many bits on which their common domains disagree; the image of $q$ is compatible with $r$. Such a flip fixes every ground name from $V_0$, fixes the defining real parameters, and fixes $f$ because finite changes preserve every $\delta$-class. Thus F8, persistence and density closure show that every assertion "$A\in\dot U$" with $A\in\mathcal P(\kappa)^{V_0}$ is decided by the top condition of $Q$. [F1, F7, F8, step 5.1]

7.1 In $V_0$ define $$U_0=\{A\in\mathcal P(\kappa)^{V_0}:1_Q\Vdash_Q A\in\dot U\}.$$ The forcing relation is definable there, and F6 together with the homogeneity calculation in step 6.1 gives $U_0=U\cap\mathcal P(\kappa)^{V_0}$. Hence F3 transfers properness, complement decision, and nonprincipality to $U_0$. If $\eta<\kappa$ and a sequence $\langle A_\xi:\xi<\eta\rangle\in V_0$ consists of members of $U_0$, then the sequence belongs to $N$ because $V_0\subseteq N$; step 5.1 puts its intersection in $U$, and that intersection is computed in $V_0$. It therefore belongs to $U_0$. Thus $V_0$ regards $U_0$ as a nonprincipal $\kappa$-complete ultrafilter and regards $\kappa$ as measurable. [F3, F4, F6, step 5.1, step 6.1]

8.1 The forcing from $M$ to $V_0$ is trivial when $K=\varnothing$ and otherwise countable. Any ground bijection witnessing that $\kappa$ was countable or was not a cardinal would remain a witness in $V_0$, so step 7.1 implies that $M$ already regards $\kappa$ as an uncountable cardinal; the forcing size is therefore below $\kappa$. F4 says that $M$ already has a measurable cardinal. Internally choose its least measurable cardinal $\lambda$ and use the auxiliary ultrapower construction in F4 to obtain a normal-measure embedding $j:M\to Q_0$ with critical point $\lambda$. Elementarity gives $Q_0\models V=L$. The transitive target contains every ordinal: for each ordinal $\alpha$, $j(\alpha)$ is an ordinal at least $\alpha$, so transitivity puts $\alpha$ in $Q_0$. F10 now gives $L^{Q_0}=L^M=M$, whence $Q_0=M$. But $\lambda$ is parameter-free definable as the least measurable cardinal, so elementarity and $Q_0=M$ give $j(\lambda)=\lambda$, contradicting that $\lambda$ is the critical point. This discharges the supposition in step 4.1: every ultrafilter on every ordinal in $N$ is principal. [F4, F10, F11, step 7.1, discharge-contradiction: step 4.1]

9.1 Work henceforth inside the ZF model $N$. Let $W$ be the least class containing every singleton and closed under unions indexed by ordinals: equivalently, start with the empty set and all singletons and at each successor stage add every $\bigcup_{\alpha<\theta}X_\alpha$ whose pieces appeared earlier, taking unions at limit stages. The least construction stage is the $W$-rank. Thus every nonsingleton $X\in W$ has a presentation as a well-ordered union of sets of strictly smaller $W$-rank. This hierarchy is a definable class in $N$; it does not select presentations simultaneously. [F1, step 8.1]

10.1 Induction on $W$-rank proves three closure facts, with ranks no larger than those generated in the induction. If $B\subseteq\bigcup_{\alpha<\theta}X_\alpha$, then $B=\bigcup_{\alpha<\theta}(B\cap X_\alpha)$, and the induction hypothesis applies to each intersection; the empty and singleton bases are immediate. If $h:X\twoheadrightarrow Y$, then $Y=\bigcup_{\alpha<\theta}h[X_\alpha]$, giving closure under surjective images by the same induction. A double induction gives finite products: distribute $X\times Y$ over a well-ordered-union presentation in either coordinate, with singleton and empty products as bases. Consequently finite sequences from a $W$-set, graphs and relations cut out as subsets of finite products, and well-ordered unions of these objects also lie in $W$. [step 9.1]

11.1 For each fixed $n$, the map sending a finite subset $z\subseteq\omega$ to $a_n\mathbin\triangle z$ enumerates $\delta(a_n)$; the analogous map enumerates $\delta(\omega\setminus a_n)$. These maps exist in $N$ using the single permitted parameter $a_n$ and the canonical well-order of the finite subsets of $\omega$. Hence each class is well-orderable and lies in $W$, without choosing representatives for all classes at once. The pair $C_n=\delta(a_n)\cup\delta(\omega\setminus a_n)$ lies in $W$, and the sequence $n\mapsto C_n$ is definable from $f$. Therefore $$S=\{f\}\cup\bigcup_{n<\omega}C_n\in W.$$ [F1, step 10.1]

12.1 We have $W\subseteq N$ because $N$ is transitive, contains all its singletons, and is internally closed under well-ordered unions. Conversely fix $x\in N$. For each $y\in x$, let $\theta_y$ be the least ambient hierarchy bound at which some formula, finite ordinal tuple, and finite tuple from $S$ uniquely define $y$ from $f$; the set-level satisfaction coding used in F9 makes this a set-theoretic predicate. Replacement bounds the $\theta_y$ by one ordinal $\theta$. Let $D$ be the set of all bounded definition codes whose unique output belongs to $x$. Substituting the fixed finite-parameter definition of $x$ shows that $D$ and its evaluation map are in $N$; no code was chosen separately for each $y$. The code space is a subset of a finite product and a well-ordered union of $\theta^{<\omega}$, $\omega$, and $S^{<\omega}$, so steps 10.1--11.1 put $D$ in $W$. Evaluation maps $D$ onto $x$, and closure under surjective images puts $x$ in $W$. Thus $N=W$. [F1, F9, step 1.1, step 10.1, step 11.1]

13.1 Induct on the $W$-rank of a carrier $X$. There is no proper ultrafilter on $\varnothing$, and every ultrafilter on a singleton is principal. Otherwise use step 9.1 to write $X=\bigcup_{\alpha<\theta}X_\alpha$ with lower-rank pieces and refine it to the disjoint partition $Y_\alpha=X_\alpha\setminus\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}X_\beta$; step 10.1 keeps every $Y_\alpha$ at lower rank. For an ultrafilter $U$ on $X$, the least-piece map $h:X\to\theta$ pushes $U$ to an ultrafilter on the ordinal $\theta$. By step 8.1 it is principal, say at $\alpha_0$, so $Y_{\alpha_0}=h^{-1}(\{\alpha_0\})\in U$ and is nonempty. The restriction of $U$ to $Y_{\alpha_0}$ is an ultrafilter there and is principal at some $y$ by induction. For every $B\subseteq X$, upward closure and intersection give $$B\in U\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad B\cap Y_{\alpha_0}\in U\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y\in B.$$ Hence $U$ is principal at $y$. Step 12.1 says every carrier in $N$ has a $W$-rank, so this proves the theorem for every set in $N$. [F2, F3, step 8.1, step 9.1, step 10.1, step 12.1] ∎
