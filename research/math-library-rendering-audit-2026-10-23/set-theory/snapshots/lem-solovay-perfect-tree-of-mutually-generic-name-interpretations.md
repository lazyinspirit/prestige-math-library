## Statement

Let $N=V[G_\xi]$ be a bounded intermediate model in the Solovay collapse, let $Q$ be the interval collapse from $\xi$ to some $\eta<\kappa$, and let $p\in Q$ and $\dot\tau\in N$ be a $Q$-name for a real.  Suppose that

$$\nexists q\le p\ \nexists y\in\mathbb R^N\quad q\Vdash_Q\dot\tau=\check y.$$

In $V[G]$ there is a perfect binary tree of $Q$-generics containing $p$, mutually generic in every pair of distinct branches, whose $\dot\tau$-interpretations are pairwise distinct and vary continuously.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $N,Q,p,\dot\tau$ as in the statement and the final collapse extension $V[G]$.

[F1] [[def-solovay-levy-collapse-setup]], [[lem-lc-inaccessible-size-and-rank-bounds]], and [[thm-collapse-and-levy-collapse-effects]]: $N$ and $Q$ arise from forcing of size below the inaccessible $\kappa$.  The families $\mathcal P(Q)^N$ and $\mathcal P(Q^2)^N$ have ambient cardinal below $\kappa$ and are countable after the remaining Lévy collapse.

[F2] [[thm-forcing-theorem]] and [[lem-forcing-monotonicity-density-and-decision]]: the forcing predicate is definable, and for each fixed bit formula the conditions deciding it are dense. Iterating this density finitely many times gives a dense set of conditions deciding any prescribed finite initial segment of a real name.

[F3] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: ambient AC enumerates dense sets of $Q$ and $Q^2$.

## Proof

1.1 The initial forcing producing $N$ and the interval forcing $Q$ both have size below $\kappa$.  Nice names for subsets of $Q$ and $Q^2$ are coded by subsets of a ground set of size below $\kappa$; strong inaccessibility bounds the collection of those codes below $\kappa$.  The remaining Lévy collapse therefore makes $\mathcal P(Q)^N$ and $\mathcal P(Q^2)^N$ countable in $V[G]$, as asserted in F1. Use F3 to enumerate their dense members and replace each by its downward closure. Recursively assign $p_s\le p$ for $s\in2^{<\omega}$. At stage $n$, extend every node into the first $n$ one-coordinate open dense sets and every ordered pair of distinct nodes into the first $n$ product open dense sets. There are only finitely many requirements at a level: process them successively, strengthening the affected coordinates each time. Downward closure preserves all requirements already met. [F1, F3]

2.1 Below every $q\le p$ there are two extensions forcing incompatible initial segments of $\dot\tau$. Otherwise, for some $q$, any two decided strings would be compatible. For each length $k$, density of deciding conditions then gives one unique string $u_k\in2^k$ that can be forced below $q$; least-string selection makes the sequence $(u_k)$ definable in $N$ from $q$ and $\dot\tau$. Its union is a real $u\in N$, and density closure gives $q\Vdash\dot\tau=\check u$, contradicting the displayed hypothesis, since $q\le p$. Apply this splitting below each node to make siblings $p_{s0},p_{s1}$ decide incompatible strings of length at least $|s|+1$, preserving all earlier finite requirements. [F2, step 1.1]

3.1 For $z\in2^\omega$, the filter generated by the branch is $g_z=\{q\in Q:(\exists n)\ p_{z\restriction n}\le q\}$, the upward closure in the convention that smaller conditions are stronger. It meets every enumerated dense set and so is $N$-generic. Distinct $z,w$ give an $N$-generic pair by the product requirements, and the incompatible decisions make $\dot\tau_{g_z}\ne\dot\tau_{g_w}$. Agreement through level $n$ fixes an output prefix of length $n$, so $z\mapsto\dot\tau_{g_z}$ is continuous. Compactness makes its injective image perfect and nonempty. [step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
