## Statement

For an endomorphism $T$ of a finite-dimensional $F$-vector space, the evaluation map induces an $F$-algebra isomorphism

$$F[x]/(\mu_T)\cong F[T]:=\{p(T):p\in F[x]\}.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An endomorphism $T$ and polynomial evaluation $\operatorname{ev}_T:F[x]\to\mathcal L(V,V)$.

[L1] Polynomial evaluation sends $p$ to $p(T)=\sum a_kT^k$ ([[def-polynomial-evaluation-at-an-endomorphism]]).

[L2] Its annihilating polynomials form the ideal $(\mu_T)$ ([[thm-minimal-polynomial-is-well-defined-and-controls-annihilators]]).

[L3] The first isomorphism theorem for rings gives $R/\ker f\cong\operatorname{im}f$ ([[thm-first-isomorphism-theorem-rings]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The finite-sum definition in [L1] gives $(p+q)(T)=p(T)+q(T)$, $(pq)(T)=p(T)q(T)$, and $1(T)=I$, so evaluation is an $F$-algebra homomorphism. Its image is $F[T]$ by definition, and [L2] identifies its kernel with $(\mu_T)$. [L1, L2, algebra]

2.1 Apply [L3] to step 1.1. On the zero space $\mu_T=1$, and both $F[x]/(1)$ and the zero endomorphism algebra are the one-element ring. [step 1.1, L3] ∎
