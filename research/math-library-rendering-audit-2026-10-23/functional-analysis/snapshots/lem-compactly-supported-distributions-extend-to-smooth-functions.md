## Statement

Give $C^\infty(\Omega)$ the topology generated by $q_{K,m}(f)=\max_{|\alpha|\le m}\sup_K|\partial^\alpha f|$ for compact $K\subseteq\Omega$ and $m\ge0$. A compactly supported distribution $u$ has a unique continuous complex-linear extension to this space, given by
$$\widetilde u(f)=u(\chi f),$$
where $\chi\in\mathcal D(\Omega)$ is any cutoff equal to one near $\operatorname{supp}u$. The result is independent of $\chi$. The extension vanishes on every smooth function zero near the support. No Hahn–Banach or choice axiom is needed.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] A compactly supported distribution has a finite-order bound on one compact neighborhood for all tests ([[thm-compactly-supported-distributions-have-global-finite-order]]).

[F2] Tests vanishing near the support have zero pairing; the empty-support distribution is zero ([[def-support-of-a-distribution]]).

[F3] Compact subsets admit smooth compact cutoffs equal to one on a neighborhood ([[lem-test-function-cutoffs-and-euclidean-localization]]).

## Proof

**Given:** a compactly supported distribution $u$, with support $S$.

1.1 Take $\chi$ from F3. For any smooth $f$, $\chi f$ is a test. If $\chi'$ is another such cutoff, $(\chi-\chi')f$ is a test vanishing near $S$, so F2 gives equal pairings. Thus the formula is well-defined, linear, and agrees with $u$ on tests because $(1-\chi)\varphi$ vanishes near $S$. If $f$ vanishes near $S$, so does $\chi f$, proving the additional vanishing assertion. [given, F2, F3]

2.1 Apply F1 to $\chi f$. Let $m,C,L$ be its fixed estimate and put $L'=L\cup\operatorname{supp}\chi$, compactly inside $\Omega$. The finite product formula gives $q_{L,m}(\chi f)\le2^m A q_{L',m}(f)$, where $A=\max_{|\beta|\le m}\sup_{L'}|\partial^\beta\chi|<\infty$. Hence $|\widetilde u(f)|\le C2^m A q_{L',m}(f)$, proving continuity in the stated smooth topology. [step 1.1, F1, algebra]

3.1 Tests are dense in that topology. Indeed a basic neighborhood of a smooth $f$ imposes finitely many derivative bounds on compacts $K_1,\ldots,K_s$. Use F3 on their finite union to take $\eta=1$ near that union. Then the test $\eta f$ has exactly the same derivatives as $f$ on all these compacts, so it lies in the neighborhood. If there are no constraints, the zero test suffices. If two continuous linear extensions agree on tests, their difference has a closed zero kernel (the scalar target is Hausdorff) containing this dense set, hence vanishes on all smooth functions. This proves uniqueness without selecting a sequence of cutoffs. For empty $S$ take the zero extension; empty $\Omega$ has only the zero function. $\square$ [step 2.1, F2, F3]
