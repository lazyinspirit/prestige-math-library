## Definition

An **ordered tensor register** consists of a finite ordered list of
finite-dimensional complex inner-product spaces $H_0,\ldots,H_{n-1}$, each
equipped with a specified ordered orthonormal basis. Its space is
$H_0\otimes\cdots\otimes H_{n-1}$, with factors retained in the displayed
order. The product basis is ordered lexicographically by its tuple of factor
indices. The empty tensor product is $\mathbb C$ with basis $\{1\}$. A
zero-dimensional factor is allowed in this mathematical construction and makes
the tensor space zero; it is not a register under
[[def-qc-register-and-computational-basis]].

The inner product is linear in the first argument and conjugate-linear in the
second, and on elementary tensors it is

$$\left\langle\bigotimes_{k<n}x_k,\bigotimes_{k<n}y_k\right\rangle_{\otimes}:=\prod_{k<n}\langle x_k,y_k\rangle_{{\rm root},k}.$$ 

For the empty tensor, the right side is the empty product $1$, giving the
standard inner product on $\mathbb C$.

For a permutation $\sigma$ of the $n$ factor positions, the **wire permutation**
$P_\sigma$ maps the product basis vector indexed by
$(i_0,\ldots,i_{n-1})$ to the product basis vector whose output factor order is
$(\sigma(0),\ldots,\sigma(n-1))$. It is extended linearly; for $n=0$ it is the
identity on $\mathbb C$. The extension is a linear map in the sense of
[[def-linear-map]].

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite ordered list of finite-dimensional complex inner-product
spaces with specified ordered orthonormal bases.

[F1] For free modules with ordered bases, the elementary tensors of basis
vectors form a basis, including an empty basis in either factor
([[thm-tensor-product-basis-from-bases]]).

[F2] Finite iterated tensor products represent multilinear maps, with the
empty tensor product equal to the scalar module and the one-factor case
unchanged ([[cor-finite-iterated-tensor-products-represent-multilinear-maps]]).

[F3] The standard coordinate pairing on a finite complex coordinate space is
an inner product, linear in the first argument
([[prop-standard-coordinate-inner-products]]).

[F4] Each factor pairing is linear in its first argument and
conjugate-linear in its second ([[def-real-and-complex-inner-product-space]]).

[F5] Coordinates in a finite ordered basis are unique
([[thm-unique-coordinates-with-respect-to-an-ordered-basis]]).

[F6] The module tensor product is generated by elementary tensors, which are
multilinear in their factors; over $\mathbb C$ it has its stated module
structure and the empty tensor unit is $\mathbb C$
([[def-tensor-product-of-modules-by-generators-and-relations]],
[[thm-commutative-ring-module-structure-on-a-tensor-product]],
[[cor-finite-iterated-tensor-products-represent-multilinear-maps]]).

[F7] A linear map preserves finite linear combinations
([[def-linear-map]]).

## Proof

1.1 For $n=0$, the declared space is $\mathbb C$ with its usual pairing and basis $\{1\}$. For $n>0$, repeated use of [F1] and the associativity identifications in [F2] shows that the lexicographically ordered elementary product vectors form a basis of the tensor space. If one factor basis is empty, the product basis is empty and the tensor space is zero. [given, F1, F2, F6, algebra]

2.1 Use the coordinates in this product basis to define the pairing by the standard coordinate formula of [F3]. The basis gives unique coordinates by [F5], so this is a well-defined sesquilinear positive-definite pairing. It is linear first and conjugate-linear second by [F3] and [F4]. [step 1.1, F3, F4, F5]

3.1 Write $x_k=\sum_{i_k}x_{k,i_k}e_{k,i_k}$ and $y_k=\sum_{i_k}y_{k,i_k}e_{k,i_k}$ in the supplied bases, using their unique coordinates [F5]. Multilinearity of elementary tensors [F6], applied one factor at a time, gives the coefficient $\prod_k x_{k,i_k}$ of each product-basis vector in $\bigotimes_k x_k$, and similarly $\prod_k y_{k,i_k}$ for $\bigotimes_k y_k$. The standard coordinate formula [F3] therefore gives $\sum_{(i_0,\ldots,i_{n-1})}\prod_k x_{k,i_k}\overline{y_{k,i_k}}$. Repeated finite distributivity factors this as $\prod_k\sum_{i_k}x_{k,i_k}\overline{y_{k,i_k}}$, which is the displayed product formula. For $n=0$ both expressions are $1$; if a factor has dimension $0$, the product basis is empty and both the tensor pairing and product over factor pairings are $0$. [step 2.1, F3, F5, F6, algebra]

4.1 The product formula depends only on the factor pairings. Any other supplied orthonormal bases therefore give the same pairing on elementary tensors; elementary tensors span by [F1], so sesquilinearity makes the pairings equal everywhere. [step 3.1, F1, F4, F6]

5.1 The factor permutation in the Definition sends the product basis bijectionally to the product basis in the reordered factors. Unique coordinates [F5] make its linear extension well-defined, and [F7] makes that extension linear; applying the inverse permutation to basis vectors gives the inverse linear map. For the empty list the definition is already the identity. [step 1.1, F1, F2, F5, F7, algebra] ∎
