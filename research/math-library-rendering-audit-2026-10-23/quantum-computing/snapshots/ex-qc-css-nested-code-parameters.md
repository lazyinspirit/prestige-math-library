## Example

Let $n=4$,
$C_2=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_2}\{1111\}$, and
$C_1=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_2}\{1111,1100\}$. A generator matrix
for $C_1$ has columns $1111,1100$, and a parity-check matrix has rows
$1100,0011$. The CSS code has $k=\dim C_1-\dim C_2=1$ and encoded states

$$|0_L\rangle=2^{-1/2}(|0000\rangle+|1111\rangle),\qquad |1_L\rangle=2^{-1/2}(|1100\rangle+|0011\rangle).$$

The X checks are generated by $X^{1111}$ and the Z checks by
$Z^{1100},Z^{0011}$. Logical operators can be chosen as
$X_L=X^{1100}$ and $Z_L=Z^{1010}$. This example has
$C_2^\perp\not\subseteq C_1$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The binary dot product, the nested-code CSS convention, and the displayed generators and parity checks.

[F1] [[thm-qc-css-encoding-dimension-and-commutation]]: $C_2\subseteq C_1$ encodes $\dim C_1-\dim C_2$ qubits, and $X$ checks from $C_2$ commute with $Z$ checks from $C_1^\perp$.

[F2] [[def-qc-dual-of-a-finite-field-linear-code]]: $C^\perp$ consists of vectors having dot product zero with every vector of $C$.

[F3] [[def-qc-stabilizer-syndrome-and-logical-operators]]: normalizer classes outside scalar phases and stabilizers act as nontrivial logical Paulis.

## Verification

1.1 The displayed generator rows of $C_1$ are independent, and the single generator of $C_2$ is nonzero and lies in $C_1$. Thus $\dim C_1=2$, $\dim C_2=1$, $k=1$, and the two displayed cosets are $C_2$ and $1100+C_2$. Their supports are disjoint, so [F1] gives the orthonormal logical basis shown. [F1, construct]

2.1 A vector $y=(y_1,y_2,y_3,y_4)$ lies in $C_1^\perp$ exactly when $y_1+y_2+y_3+y_4=0$ and $y_1+y_2=0$. These equations are equivalent to $y_1=y_2$ and $y_3=y_4$, so $C_1^\perp=\operatorname{span}\{1100,0011\}$. Both generators have even dot product with $1111$, so the X and Z checks commute. The vector $1010$ has even weight and hence lies in $C_2^\perp$, but it is not one of $0000,1111,1100,0011$, so it is not in $C_1$. This verifies $C_2^\perp\not\subseteq C_1$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Translation by $1100$ exchanges the two displayed logical coset states, so $X^{1100}$ acts as logical bit flip. The phase $(-1)^{1010\cdot y}$ is $+1$ for $y=0000,1111$ and $-1$ for $y=1100,0011$, so $Z^{1010}$ acts as logical phase flip. Their dot product is $1100\cdot1010=1$ in $\mathbb F_2$, hence the logical operators anticommute. [F1, F3, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
