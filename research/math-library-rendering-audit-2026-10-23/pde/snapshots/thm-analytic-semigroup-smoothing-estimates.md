## Statement

Assume Dependent Choice ([[def-dependent-choice]]) for the cited integral and semigroup suppliers.

Let $A$ be sectorial of angle $\delta\in(0,\pi/2]$ with vertex $0$ on a complex
Banach space $X$
([[def-sectorial-operator-with-the-semigroup-sign-convention]]) and let
$(T(t))_{t\ge0}$ be the contour semigroup of
[[lem-dunford-contour-construction-satisfies-the-semigroup-law]]. Then for
every $t>0$ and every $m\ge1$:

1. $T(t)X\subseteq D(A^m)$ and $\|A^mT(t)\|\le C_mt^{-m}$, where $C_m$ depends
   only on $m$, $\delta$ and the sectoriality constants $M_\varepsilon$;
2. $T\in C^\infty((0,\infty),\mathcal B(X))$ in the operator norm
   ([[def-operator-norm]]) and $\frac{d^m}{dt^m}T(t)=A^mT(t)$ for every $m\ge1$.

No choice principle beyond Dependent Choice is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A sectorial operator $A$ of angle $\delta$ with vertex $0$ on a complex Banach space $X$ and its contour semigroup $T$, with $\|R(\lambda,A)\|\le M_\varepsilon/|\lambda|$ on $|\arg\lambda|<\pi/2+\delta-\varepsilon$; a contour $\Gamma=\Gamma(r,\theta)$ with $r>0$, $\theta=\pi/2+\delta/2$, and $\varepsilon=\delta/4$ (so $\theta<\pi/2+\delta-\varepsilon$); a fixed $t>0$ and $m\ge1$.

[L1] For a fixed $\Gamma(r,\theta)$, the representation $T(z)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\Gamma e^{\lambda z}R(\lambda,A)\,d\lambda$ converges absolutely and locally uniformly on compact subsets of $\Sigma_{\theta-\pi/2}$. For general $z\in\Sigma_\delta$ the angle must satisfy $\theta>\pi/2+|\arg z|$. The resulting $T$ is norm-holomorphic on $\Sigma_\delta$ and independent of the radius and admissible angle ([[lem-contour-definition-of-an-analytic-semigroup]], [[lem-dunford-contour-construction-satisfies-the-semigroup-law]]); convergence of the polynomial-weighted integrals at positive real times is proved in step 1.1 below.

[L2] $A$ is closed, and for $\lambda\in\rho(A)$ one has $R(\lambda,A)X=D(A)$ and $AR(\lambda,A)=\lambda R(\lambda,A)-I$ on $X$ ([[def-sectorial-operator-with-the-semigroup-sign-convention]], [[def-resolvent-of-a-closed-operator]]).

[L3] For the curve integral, $\|\int_\gamma f\|\le\int_\gamma\|f\|$ and the integral is linear ([[lem-bochner-integral-norm-inequality]], [[lem-linearity-of-the-bochner-integral]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The differentiated contour formula. For $t>0$ and every $k\ge0$ the integral $\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\Gamma\lambda^k e^{\lambda t}R(\lambda,A)\,d\lambda$ converges absolutely, because on the rays $\|\lambda^k e^{\lambda t}R(\lambda,A)\|\le M_\varepsilon s^{k-1}e^{-cst}$ with $c>0$ (the admissible angle gives $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda t)\le-cst$) and $\int_r^\infty s^{k-1}e^{-cst}ds<\infty$ for $t>0$; differentiating $k$ times under the integral sign is justified by the same integrable majorant on compact time intervals bounded away from $0$, so $\frac{d^k}{dt^k}T(t)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\Gamma\lambda^k e^{\lambda t}R(\lambda,A)\,d\lambda$ and $T\in C^\infty((0,\infty),\mathcal B(X))$ in operator norm. [L1, L3, given, algebra]

1.2 The truncated integral lies in the domain. For $k\ge0$ the truncated integral $I_{k,R}:=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma_R}\lambda^k e^{\lambda t}R(\lambda,A)\,d\lambda$ is the norm limit of Riemann sums of elements of $D(A)$, and by [L2] $A$ of each such sum equals the corresponding sum of $\lambda^{k+1}e^{\lambda t}R(\lambda,A)-\lambda^k e^{\lambda t}I$; since $A$ is closed, the limit $I_{k,R}$ lies in $D(A)$ with $AI_{k,R}=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma_R}\lambda^{k+1}e^{\lambda t}R(\lambda,A)\,d\lambda-\frac1{2\pi i}\bigl(\int_{\Gamma_R}\lambda^k e^{\lambda t}d\lambda\bigr)I$. [L1, L2, L3, given, algebra]

1.3 The scalar contour integrals vanish. For every $k\ge0$ one has $\int_\Gamma\lambda^k e^{\lambda t}d\lambda=0$: close the truncated contour by the arc at radius $R$ through the left, on which $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda t)\le-cRt$; the integral of the entire function $\lambda^ke^{\lambda t}$ over the closed truncated curve vanishes (it has the global primitive $\int_0^\lambda w^ke^{wt}dw$), while the closing arc contribution is at most $2\pi R^{k+1}e^{-cRt}\to0$. [L1, given, algebra]

2.1 First order: $AT(t)=T'(t)$. Apply [step 1.2] with $k=0$: the truncated integral $I_{0,R}$ converges in operator norm to $T(t)$, while its $A$-image is $I_{1,R}-\frac1{2\pi i}(\int_{\Gamma_R}e^{\lambda t}d\lambda)I$. The first term converges in operator norm to $T'(t)$ by [step 1.1], and the scalar term tends to zero by [step 1.3]. Since $A$ is closed by [L2], for each $x\in X$ the convergence of $I_{0,R}x$ and $AI_{0,R}x$ gives $T(t)x\in D(A)$ and $AT(t)x=T'(t)x$. Thus $T(t)X\subseteq D(A)$ and $AT(t)=T'(t)$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, L2, given, algebra]

3.1 Higher orders by the same argument. Suppose $T(t)X\subseteq D(A^m)$ and $A^mT(t)=T^{(m)}(t)$. Apply [step 1.2] with $k=m$ to the truncated contour integral $I_{m,R}$ for $T^{(m)}$: its $A$-image is $I_{m+1,R}$ minus the scalar term in [step 1.2]. As $R\to\infty$, $I_{m,R}\to T^{(m)}(t)=A^mT(t)$ and $I_{m+1,R}\to T^{(m+1)}(t)$ in operator norm by [step 1.1], while the scalar term tends to zero by [step 1.3]. Closedness of $A$ then gives $A^{m+1}T(t)x=T^{(m+1)}(t)x$ for every $x\in X$, and $T(t)X\subseteq D(A^{m+1})$. Induction proves the claim for every $m\ge1$. [step 2.1, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, L2, given, algebra]

4.1 The bound. By the independence of the inner radius [L1], compute $T^{(m)}(t)$ with $r=1/t$. On the rays, $\|\lambda^me^{\lambda t}R(\lambda,A)\|\le M_\varepsilon s^{m-1}e^{-cst}$ and $\int_{1/t}^\infty s^{m-1}e^{-cst}ds=t^{-m}\int_1^\infty\sigma^{m-1}e^{-c\sigma}d\sigma$ after $\sigma=st$; on the arc $|\lambda|=1/t$ one has $|\lambda|^m=t^{-m}$, length at most $2\theta/t$, $|e^{\lambda t}|\le e$ and $\|R(\lambda,A)\|\le M_\varepsilon t$, so the arc contributes at most $2\theta eM_\varepsilon t^{-m}$. Hence $\|A^mT(t)\|=\|T^{(m)}(t)\|\le C_mt^{-m}$ with $C_m$ depending only on $m,\delta,M_{\delta/4}$ through the prescribed $\theta=\pi/2+\delta/2$ and $c=-\cos\theta$, and [step 3.1] supplies the domain membership and the derivative identity for every $m$. [step 1.1, step 3.1, L1, L3, given, algebra] ∎
