## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ be open, $n\ge1$,
let $1\le p\le\infty$, and let $p'$ be the conjugate exponent, with
$1'=\infty$ and $\infty'=1$. Take complex-valued (or real-valued)
$u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)$ and $v\in W^{1,p'}(\Omega)$. Say that an a.e.
class $f$ is compactly supported in $\Omega$ if some compact $K\subseteq\Omega$
satisfies $f=0$ almost everywhere on $\Omega\setminus K$. If at least one of
$u,v$ is compactly supported in this sense, then for every
$i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$,
$$\int_\Omega u\,D_i v\,dx=-\int_\Omega v\,D_i u\,dx.$$
The integrals are bilinear, without conjugation, and are absolutely
convergent by Hölder's inequality, including at $p=1$ and $p=\infty$. The
proof assumes only Countable Choice, exactly the strength needed by its supplier interfaces. Full AC is a stronger sufficient alternative: step 6.1 shows that it implies Countable Choice.
If $\Omega=\varnothing$, both sides are zero.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice, an open $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$, $n\ge1$, conjugate exponents $p,p'\in[1,\infty]$, the indicated Sobolev classes, and the compact-support condition in the Statement.

[F1] The classes $W^{1,r}$ consist of $L^r$ a.e. classes with weak first derivatives in $L^r$, and the test pairing is bilinear ([[def-sobolev-space-wkp-and-its-norm]]).

[F2] A weak derivative $D_i g$ satisfies the signed test identity $\int g\,\partial_i\phi=-\int (D_i g)\phi$ for every test function ([[def-weak-derivative-of-a-locally-integrable-function]]).

[F3] Complex Hölder gives $|\int fg|\le\|f\|_r\|g\|_{r'}$ for every conjugate pair, including $(1,\infty)$ and $(\infty,1)$ ([[thm-complex-holder-minkowski-and-the-quotient-norm]]).

[F4] Weak derivatives restrict to open subsets ([[lem-weak-derivative-linearity-locality-and-commutation]]).

[F5] A locally integrable weak derivative is unique as an a.e. class under Countable Choice ([[lem-weak-derivatives-are-unique-almost-everywhere]]).

[F6] For compact $K\subseteq\Omega$ there is a smooth compactly supported cutoff in $\Omega$ equal to one on a neighborhood of $K$; this construction is in ZF ([[lem-test-function-cutoffs-and-euclidean-localization]]).

[F7] There is an explicit real $\eta\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$ with $0\le\eta\le1$, equal to one on the unit ball and zero outside the radius-two ball ([[lem-schwartz-cutoffs-from-the-standard-smooth-step]]).

[F8] Under Countable Choice, compact sets have finite Lebesgue measure ([[prop-lebesgue-measure-is-sigma-finite-and-finite-on-bounded-sets]]).

[F9] Under Countable Choice, boxes have their product volume, so every nondegenerate box has positive measure ([[thm-lebesgue-measure-of-a-box-of-every-kind]]).

[F10] Under Countable Choice, rescaled integrable unit-mass kernels converge to the identity in every finite $L^r$, $1\le r<\infty$ ([[lem-complex-translation-and-approximate-identity-interfaces]]).

[F11] For a real unit-mass smooth compactly supported kernel, convolution is smooth, and compactly supported input gives compactly supported output ([[lem-complex-translation-and-approximate-identity-interfaces]]).

[F12] A mollifier generated by a unit-mass smooth bump is the rescaling $\eta_\varepsilon(x)=\varepsilon^{-n}\eta(x/\varepsilon)$ ([[def-mollifier-family-generated-by-a-unit-mass-smooth-bump]]).

[F13] For differentiable real-valued functions the derivative of a product is $(fg)'=f'g+fg'$; applying this coordinatewise to real and imaginary parts gives the smooth complex product rule ([[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]]).

[F14] Countable Choice says that every natural-number-indexed family of nonempty sets has a choice function ([[def-countable-choice]]).

[F15] The full Axiom of Choice gives a choice function for every family of nonempty sets, so it implies Countable Choice but is stronger than this proof requires ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

## Proof

1.1 The four factors in the asserted products lie in conjugate $L^p$ spaces: $u,D_i v\in L^p\times L^{p'}$ and $v,D_i u\in L^{p'}\times L^p$. By [F3] each product is integrable, with the endpoint pairs covered by the same inequality. The Sobolev test pairing in [F1] is bilinear, so no conjugation enters the desired identity. [F1, F3, given]

1.2 If $u=0$ as an a.e. class, then $D_i u=0$ by uniqueness of the weak derivative; both sides are zero. The same argument applies if $v=0$. [F1, F2, F5, given]

1.3 Let $g\in W^{1,r}(\Omega)$, $1\le r\le\infty$, vanish almost everywhere outside a compact $K\subseteq\Omega$. On the open set $V=\Omega\setminus K$, $g=0$ as a class. By restriction in [F4], $D_i g|_V$ is a weak derivative of zero there; zero is also such a derivative, so uniqueness in [F5] gives $D_i g=0$ almost everywhere on $V$. If $K=\varnothing$, this proves that $g=D_i g=0$ as classes, and any asserted pairing involving this compact factor vanishes. [F1, F2, F4, F5, given]

2.1 First take $1\le r<\infty$. Choose $\chi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ equal to one on a neighborhood of $K$ by [F6]. Extend representatives of $g$ and $D_i g$ by zero to $G$ and $H$ on $\mathbb R^n$. For every $\phi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$, the functions $g\,\partial_i\phi$ and $g\,\partial_i(\chi\phi)$ agree almost everywhere: they agree on a neighborhood of $K$, and $g=0$ off $K$. Likewise $D_i g\,\phi$ and $D_i g\,\chi\phi$ agree almost everywhere by step 1.3. The weak identity [F2] for $g$ applied to $\chi\phi$ therefore gives $$\int_{\mathbb R^n}G\,\partial_i\phi=\int_\Omega g\,\partial_i(\chi\phi)=-\int_\Omega D_i g\,\chi\phi=-\int_{\mathbb R^n}H\phi.$$ Thus $H$ is the weak derivative of $G$ on $\mathbb R^n$, and $G,H\in L^r$. [F1, F2, F5, F6, given, step 1.3]

3.1 The function $\eta$ from [F7] is integrable: it is bounded and supported on a compact set of finite measure by [F8]. Its integral $c$ is positive: the box $Q=[-1/(2\sqrt n),1/(2\sqrt n)]^n$ lies in the unit ball, since for $x\in Q$ one has $|x|^2\le n(1/(2\sqrt n))^2=1/4<1$. By [F9], $\lambda_n(Q)=(1/\sqrt n)^n>0$; as $\eta=1$ on $Q$ and $\eta\ge0$, its integral is at least $\lambda_n(Q)$. Put $\rho=\eta/c$ and use the family $\rho_\varepsilon(x)=\varepsilon^{-n}\rho(x/\varepsilon)$ from [F12]. By step 1.3, if $K=\varnothing$, then $G$ and $H$ are zero classes and $G_\varepsilon=0$ for every \varepsilon, so support and convergence are immediate. Assume $K$ is nonempty below. For $G_\varepsilon=\rho_\varepsilon*G$, [F11] gives smoothness and compact support. The integral defining $G_\varepsilon(x)$ vanishes whenever $\operatorname{dist}(x,K)>2\varepsilon$, because $G=0$ off $K$ and $\operatorname{supp}\rho_\varepsilon\subseteq\overline B_{2\varepsilon}(0)$. For each $x\in K$, openness gives a ball $B(x,r_x)\subseteq\Omega$. Compactness supplies finitely many half-radius balls covering $K$. Let $\delta$ be one quarter of the least radius in this finite list. For every $0<\varepsilon<\delta$, the closed $2\varepsilon$-neighborhood of $K$ lies in the union of the corresponding $B(x,r_x)$: every point $y$ in the neighborhood has some $z\in K$ with $|y-z|\le2\varepsilon$; a covering half-radius ball $B(x,r_x/2)$ contains $z$, so $|y-x|<r_x/2+2\varepsilon<r_x$. The neighborhood is compact, being $K+ \overline B_{2\varepsilon}(0)$, and hence lies in $\Omega$. Therefore $G_\varepsilon$ vanishes off a compact subset of $\Omega$, so $G_\varepsilon\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$. Since $G,H$ are supported in $K$, they are in $L^1(\mathbb R^n)$: for $r=1$ this is immediate, and for $1<r<\infty$ it follows from finite measure of $K$ [F8] and Hölder [F3]. Thus the convolutions in this step are defined. [F3, F7, F8, F9, F11, F12, given, step 1.3, step 2.1]

4.1 At every $x$, the weak identity [F2] for $G$ may be tested with the compactly supported smooth function $y\mapsto\rho_\varepsilon(x-y)$. Since $\partial_{y_i}\rho_\varepsilon(x-y)=-\partial_{x_i}\rho_\varepsilon(x-y)$, it gives $$\partial_iG_\varepsilon(x)=\int G(y)\,\partial_{x_i}\rho_\varepsilon(x-y)\,dy=\int H(y)\,\rho_\varepsilon(x-y)\,dy=(\rho_\varepsilon*H)(x).$$ By the finite-$L^r$ convergence in [F10], applied to $G$ and $H$, $$\|G_\varepsilon-G\|_{L^r(\mathbb R^n)}+\|\partial_iG_\varepsilon-H\|_{L^r(\mathbb R^n)}\longrightarrow0.$$ Consequently, taking $\varepsilon_j=\delta/(j+1)$ gives restrictions in $C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ whose classical $i$th derivatives converge to $D_i g$ along with convergence to $g$ in $L^r$. [F1, F2, F10, F11, F12, given, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 Suppose first that $u$ is compactly supported and $p<\infty$. Apply steps 1.3–4.1 to $g=u$, $r=p$, obtaining $u_j\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ with $u_j\to u$ and $\partial_i u_j\to D_i u$ in $L^p$. The weak identity [F2] for $v$ with weak derivative $D_i v$, tested against $u_j$, says $$\int_\Omega u_jD_i v=-\int_\Omega v\partial_i u_j.$$ By [F3], the differences between the left sides and $\int uD_i v$ are at most $\|u_j-u\|_p\|D_i v\|_{p'}$, while the differences between the right sides and $-\int vD_i u$ are at most $\|v\|_{p'}\|\partial_i u_j-D_i u\|_p$. Both tend to zero, proving the claim in this case. If instead $v$ is compactly supported and $p'<\infty$, apply the same approximation to $v$ in $L^{p'}$ and test the weak identity for $u$ with derivative $D_i u$; Hölder gives the two corresponding limits. This covers every case with a compactly supported factor of finite exponent, including $p=1$ when $u$ is compact and $p=\infty$ when $v$ is compact. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.3, step 4.1, given]

5.2 It remains only the case in which the compact factor has exponent $\infty$ and its partner has exponent $1$. Write the compact factor as $g\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)$, the other as $h\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)$, and let $K$ contain the essential support of $g$. The case $K=\varnothing$ is already settled by step 1.3. Choose $\chi$ as in [F6] with $\chi=1$ on a neighborhood of $K$, and put $w=\chi h$. For any $\phi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$, [F13] and the weak identity [F2] for $h$, tested with $\chi\phi$, give $$\int_\Omega w\,\partial_i\phi=\int_\Omega h\,\partial_i(\chi\phi)-\int_\Omega h(\partial_i\chi)\phi=-\int_\Omega\bigl(\chi D_i h+h\partial_i\chi\bigr)\phi.$$ For each coordinate $j$, the same test calculation gives the weak derivative $D_jw=\chi D_jh+h\, \partial_j\chi$. Each displayed candidate and $w$ are in $L^1$ and compactly supported, so the definition [F1] gives $w\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)$. By steps 1.3–4.1, choose $w_j\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ with $w_j\to w$ and $\partial_iw_j\to D_iw$ in $L^1$. Test the weak identity [F2] for $g$, whose weak derivative is $D_i g$, with $w_j$: $$\int_\Omega g\partial_iw_j=-\int_\Omega (D_i g)w_j.$$ The limits pass by $|\int g(\partial_iw_j-D_iw)|\le\|g\|_\infty\|\partial_iw_j-D_iw\|_1$ and the analogous estimate using $D_i g\in L^\infty$. On a neighborhood of $K$, $w=h$ as a.e. classes, so restriction [F4] and uniqueness [F5] give $D_iw=D_i h$ there. Off $K$, both $g$ and $D_i g$ vanish almost everywhere by step 1.3. Thus the limit is $$\int_\Omega gD_i h=-\int_\Omega hD_i g.$$ It gives the required orientation when $g=u,h=v$; when $g=v,h=u$, rearrange the same equality. This handles the remaining endpoint cases without any strong approximation in $W^{1,\infty}$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F13, step 1.3, step 4.1]

6.1 If $p=1$, a compactly supported $u$ is handled by step 5.1; if only $v\in W^{1,\infty}$ is compactly supported, step 5.2 applies. If $p=\infty$, a compactly supported $v\in W^{1,1}$ is handled by step 5.1; if only $u\in W^{1,\infty}$ is compactly supported, step 5.2 applies. For $1<p<\infty$, both exponents are finite, so step 5.1 applies whichever factor is compact. The zero-factor and empty-domain cases were addressed in the Statement and steps 1.2–1.3. If one starts instead from the stronger full Axiom of Choice [F15], then for each countable family of nonempty sets $(X_m)_{m\in\mathbb{N}}$ AC supplies a choice function on its range; composing with $m\mapsto X_m$ gives a choice function for the indexed family. Thus AC implies Countable Choice [F14], which is the only role of full AC and licenses exactly the Wkp, weak-derivative uniqueness, finite-measure, and mollification interfaces used in [F1], [F5], [F8], [F9], [F10], and [F11]. Under the Statement's Countable Choice hypothesis, no stronger choice principle is used. [F1, F5, F8, F9, F10, F11, F14, F15, given, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 5.1, step 5.2] ∎
