## Statement

Assume Dependent Choice ([[def-dependent-choice]]) for the cited integral and semigroup suppliers.

Assume Countable Choice. For the bounded-domain compact-resolvent and eigenvalue-attainment clause in (2), additionally assume the Axiom of Choice.

Let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ be nonempty and open, $n\ge1$, and let
$$a_0(u,v)=\int_\Omega\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla v}\,dx$$
on $H^1_0(\Omega)$ be the principal Dirichlet form ([[def-ltwo-operator-associated-with-a-symmetric-elliptic-form]], [[def-wkp-zero-as-a-sobolev-closure]]). Let $A$ be the $L^2(\Omega)$ operator associated with $a_0$ in the $e^{tA}$ convention,
$$D(A)=\{u\in H^1_0(\Omega):\exists f\in L^2(\Omega)\text{ with }a_0(u,v)=-(f,v)_{L^2}\ \forall v\in H^1_0(\Omega)\},\qquad Au=f.$$
Then:

(1) $A$ is densely defined, self-adjoint and satisfies $\langle Au,u\rangle=-a_0(u,u)\le0$; hence $A=\Delta_D$ and generates a contraction analytic semigroup $(T(t))_{t\ge0}$ of maximal allowed angle $\pi/2$, with $\|T(t)\|\le1$;

(2) if $a_0$ is coercive on $H^1_0(\Omega)$, the Rayleigh infimum
$$\lambda_*:=\inf_{0\ne u\in H^1_0(\Omega)}\frac{a_0(u,u)}{\|u\|_2^2}$$
is positive and $\|T(t)\|\le e^{-\lambda_*t}$. A sufficient condition for coercivity is that $\Omega$ lie in a slab of finite width, by zero extension and the one-dimensional Poincare inequality. If in addition $\Omega$ is bounded and the Axiom of Choice holds, compact resolvent makes $\lambda_*$ an attained first Dirichlet eigenvalue; for unbounded $\Omega$, the infimum need not be attained.

(3) If $\Omega$ is a bounded $C^2$ domain, then $D(A)=H^2(\Omega)\cap H^1_0(\Omega)$ and $Au=\Delta u$ for $u\in D(A)$, with graph norm equivalent to the $H^2$ norm ([[thm-global-h-two-dirichlet-regularity]]);

(4) for $m\ge1$ the domain $D(A^m)$ is the recursive graph domain $\{u\in D(A^{m-1}):Au\in D(A^{m-1})\}$ and $T(t)L^2\subseteq D(A^m)$ for $t>0$; if $\Omega$ is bounded $C^{2m}$, then $D(A^m)=\{u\in H^{2m}(\Omega):\Delta^ju\in H^1_0(\Omega),\ 0\le j<m\}$, but no such spatial identification may be asserted without the boundary compatibility ([[thm-higher-order-boundary-regularity-for-dirichlet-problems]]). Dependent Choice is assumed throughout for the semigroup suppliers; Countable Choice is assumed for self-adjointness, and the elliptic-regularity suppliers. The Axiom of Choice is used additionally only for the bounded-domain compact-resolvent and eigenvalue-attainment clause in (2); the unbounded-domain generation claim in (1) is preserved.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice; and, only when the bounded-domain compact-resolvent/eigenvalue-attainment clause is invoked, the Axiom of Choice; a nonempty open set $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ with $n\ge1$; the complex Hilbert space $L^2(\Omega)$ with its inner product $(\cdot,\cdot)_{L^2}$; the principal Dirichlet form $a_0(u,v)=\int_\Omega\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla v}\,dx$ on $H^1_0(\Omega)$, i.e. the symmetric divergence-form case with $a^{ij}=\delta^{ij}$, $b=0$, $c=0$; the Rayleigh infimum $\lambda_*:=\inf\{a_0(u,u)/\|u\|_{L^2}^2:0\ne u\in H^1_0(\Omega)\}$; the operator $A$ of the statement, defined by the weak identity $a_0(u,v)=-(Au,v)_{L^2}$ for all $v\in H^1_0(\Omega)$; the form operator $L$ of [L1] with $a_0(u,v)=(Lu,v)_{L^2}$; the iterated graph domains $D(A^0):=L^2(\Omega)$ and $D(A^m):=\{u\in D(A^{m-1}):Au\in D(A^{m-1})\}$ for $m\ge1$; and the semigroup $T$ of assertion (1).

[L1] The symmetric-case operator of [[def-ltwo-operator-associated-with-a-symmetric-elliptic-form]] on the Hilbert space $L^2(\Omega)$ ([[def-hilbert-space]]) is $D(L)=\{u\in H^1_0(\Omega):\exists f\in L^2(\Omega)\text{ with }a_0(u,v)=(f,v)_{L^2}\ \forall v\in H^1_0(\Omega)\}$ with $Lu:=f$, well defined and linear, and $H^1_0(\Omega)=W^{1,2}_0(\Omega)$ is the closure of $C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ in $W^{1,2}(\Omega)$ ([[def-wkp-zero-as-a-sobolev-closure]]).

[L2] In this symmetric case with $a^{ij}=\delta^{ij}$, $b=0$, $c=0$, the domain $D(L)$ is dense in $L^2(\Omega)$, $L$ is symmetric, and $(Lu,u)_{L^2}=a_0(u,u)\ge0$ for every $u\in D(L)$ ([[lem-associated-elliptic-operator-is-densely-defined-symmetric-and-lower-bounded]]).

[L3] In the complex scalar-field case $\mathbb K=\mathbb C$ of [[thm-symmetric-elliptic-form-operator-is-self-adjoint-with-compact-resolvent]], Countable Choice gives $L=L^*$ on $L^2(\Omega;\mathbb C)$; its real-case complexification branch is not needed here.

[L4] For a densely defined linear operator $T$ the adjoint $T^*$ is well defined with $D(T^*)=\{y:x\mapsto\langle Tx,y\rangle\text{ bounded}\}$ and $\langle Tx,y\rangle=\langle x,T^*y\rangle$ for $x\in D(T)$, $y\in D(T^*)$; $T^*$ is closed and reverses inclusions ([[def-adjoint-of-a-densely-defined-unbounded-operator]], [[lem-unbounded-adjoint-is-well-defined-and-closed]]).

[L5] A self-adjoint densely defined operator $A$ with $\langle Au,u\rangle\le0$ for every $u\in D(A)$ is sectorial of angle $\pi/2$ with vertex $0$ in the $e^{tA}$ convention ([[def-sectorial-operator-with-the-semigroup-sign-convention]]) and generates a bounded analytic semigroup of angle $\pi/2$ ([[def-complex-sector-and-bounded-analytic-semigroup]]) which is contractive on $[0,\infty)$ ([[thm-self-adjoint-nonpositive-operators-generate-bounded-analytic-semigroups]]).

[L6] A bounded analytic semigroup of angle $\delta\in(0,\pi/2]$ is a family $(T(z))_{z\in\Sigma_\delta\cup\{0\}}$ with $T(0)=I$, the functional equation, operator-norm holomorphy on $\Sigma_\delta$, strong continuity at the vertex and uniform boundedness on every strictly smaller sector; its generator is the generator of $(T(t))_{t\ge0}$, and its angle is the supremum of the $\delta$ for which such a family exists and extends the given one ([[def-complex-sector-and-bounded-analytic-semigroup]]).

[L7] A self-adjoint densely defined $A$ with $\langle Au,u\rangle\le-\lambda\|u\|^2$ for real $\lambda$ generates a holomorphic semigroup family with $\|T(z)\|\le e^{-\lambda\operatorname{Re}z}$ on $\Sigma_{\pi/2}$, in particular $\|T(t)\|\le e^{-\lambda t}$ for $t\ge0$; when $\lambda\ge0$ the family is a bounded analytic semigroup of angle $\pi/2$, and when $\lambda>0$ it decays exponentially ([[cor-spectral-gap-gives-exponential-decay-of-a-self-adjoint-parabolic-semigroup]]).

[L8] In the sign convention of this track the symbol $\Delta_D$ denotes the $L^2$ operator associated with the Dirichlet energy form for the equation $u'=Au$, i.e. the operator whose weak identity reads $a_0(u,v)=-(Au,v)_{L^2}$; it is not $-\Delta_D$ that generates the heat flow ([[rem-semigroup-sign-and-generator-conventions]]).

[L9] If $\Omega$ is open and there are a unit vector $e$ and $a<b$ with $a<x\cdot e<b$ for every $x\in\Omega$, then $\|u\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\le C(2)(b-a)\|Du\|_{L^2(\Omega)}$ for every $u\in W^{1,2}_0(\Omega)$, with a positive constant $C(2)$; Countable Choice is assumed ([[thm-poincare-inequality-for-w-one-p-zero]]).

[L10] If $\Omega$ is nonempty bounded open, then the first Dirichlet eigenvalue satisfies $\lambda_1>0$, $\lambda_1=\min_{0\ne u\in H^1_0(\Omega)}\|Du\|_{L^2}^2/\|u\|_{L^2}^2$ with the minimum attained exactly at the nonzero first eigenfunctions, and $\lambda_1$ is the smallest eigenvalue of an orthonormal eigenbasis $(e_j)_{j\ge1}\subseteq H^1_0(\Omega)$ of $L^2(\Omega)$ with $\lambda_j\to+\infty$; these suppliers assume the Axiom of Choice and Countable Choice ([[cor-poincare-constant-and-first-dirichlet-eigenvalue]], [[thm-rayleigh-principle-for-the-first-dirichlet-eigenvalue]], [[thm-discrete-spectrum-of-a-symmetric-elliptic-dirichlet-operator]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-countable-choice]]).

[L11] On an interval $I=(0,L)$ one has $\|u\|_{L^2(I)}\le(L/\pi)\|u'\|_{L^2(I)}$ for every $u\in H^1_0(I)$, with equality for $\phi(x)=\sin(\pi x/L)$, and $\int_I\phi'\overline{v'}\,dx=(\pi/L)^2\int_I\phi\overline v\,dx$ for every $v\in H^1_0(I)$ ([[lem-sharp-dirichlet-poincare-inequality-on-an-interval]]).

[L12] Global $H^2$ Dirichlet regularity, under Countable Choice: for a bounded $C^2$ domain $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ with $n\ge2$, if $u\in H^1_0(\Omega)$ weakly solves $Lu=f$ with $f\in L^2(\Omega)$, then $u\in H^2(\Omega)$ and $\|u\|_{H^2(\Omega)}\le C(\|f\|_{L^2(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^2(\Omega)})$ ([[thm-global-h-two-dirichlet-regularity]]).

[L13] Higher-order boundary regularity, under Countable Choice: for a bounded $C^{k+2}$ domain $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ with $n\ge2$, if $u\in H^1_0(\Omega)$ weakly solves $Lu=f$ with $f\in H^k(\Omega)$, then $u\in H^{k+2}(\Omega)$ with $\|u\|_{H^{k+2}(\Omega)}\le C(\|f\|_{H^k(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^2(\Omega)})$ ([[thm-higher-order-boundary-regularity-for-dirichlet-problems]]).

[L14] In dimension $n=1$ the bounded sets satisfying the local one-sided condition of [[def-bounded-c-k-domain-and-boundary-charts]] are finite disjoint unions of bounded open intervals.

[L15] For a sectorial operator $A$ of angle $\delta\in(0,\pi/2]$ with vertex $0$, the generated semigroup satisfies $T(t)X\subseteq D(A^m)$ for every $t>0$, $m\ge1$, and the contour semigroup is the unique exponentially bounded strongly continuous semigroup with generator $A$ ([[thm-analytic-semigroup-smoothing-estimates]], [[lem-generator-of-the-contour-semigroup-is-the-sectorial-operator]]).

[L16] The regularity conclusions of [L12] and [L13] assume that the given weak solution already belongs to $H^1_0(\Omega)$ and that the domain and forcing have the stated regularity. Thus they do not by themselves prove existence or remove these hypotheses ([[thm-global-h-two-dirichlet-regularity]], [[thm-higher-order-boundary-regularity-for-dirichlet-problems]]).

[L17] If $\Omega$ is bounded and the Axiom of Choice holds, then the shifted solution operator $K_\mu$ is compact, so $L$ has compact resolvent; the Axiom of Choice is used additionally only for this bounded-domain compactness clause ([[thm-symmetric-elliptic-form-operator-is-self-adjoint-with-compact-resolvent]]).

[L18] $W^{k,p}$ membership means that for every multi-index $\alpha$ with $|\alpha|\le k$ there is an $L^p$ class $D^\alpha u$ satisfying the weak-derivative identity against every test function, and the test pairing is bilinear, without conjugation ([[def-sobolev-space-wkp-and-its-norm]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The operator $A$ is well defined with $D(A)=D(L)$ and $Au=-Lu$: by [L1], the condition $a_0(u,v)=-(f,v)_{L^2}$ for all $v\in H^1_0(\Omega)$ says exactly that the pair $(u,g)$ with $g:=-f$ satisfies $a_0(u,v)=(g,v)_{L^2}$ for all $v$, which is the defining identity of membership in $D(L)$ with $Lu=g$; conversely, if $u\in D(L)$ with datum $g$, then $a_0(u,v)=(g,v)_{L^2}=(-(-g),v)_{L^2}$ for all $v$, so $u\in D(A)$ with the datum $f=-g$; hence $u\in D(A)$ if and only if $u\in D(L)$ and $Au=-Lu$, the datum being unique in both definitions, so $D(A)$ is dense in $L^2(\Omega)$ by [L2] and $\langle Au,u\rangle=-(Lu,u)_{L^2}=-a_0(u,u)\le0$ for every $u\in D(A)$ again by [L2]. [L1, L2, given, algebra]

1.2 The slab criterion. If there are a unit vector $e$ and $a<b$ with $a<x\cdot e<b$ for every $x\in\Omega$, then [L9] at $p=2$ gives $\|u\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\le C(2)(b-a)\|Du\|_{L^2(\Omega)}$ for every $u\in H^1_0(\Omega)=W^{1,2}_0(\Omega)$ by [L1], hence $a_0(u,u)=\|Du\|_{L^2}^2\ge(C(2)(b-a))^{-2}\|u\|_{L^2}^2$, and $\|u\|_{H^1}^2\le(1+C(2)^2(b-a)^2)\|Du\|_2^2$, so $a_0$ is coercive on $H^1_0$; this is the zero-extension, one-coordinate-dimension reduction performed by [L9], applied directly on $\Omega$. [L1, L9, given, algebra]

1.3 The bounded case under Axiom of Choice. Assume $\Omega$ is bounded and the Axiom of Choice holds; then the resolvent of $L$ is compact by [L17], and [L10] supplies $\lambda_1>0$ with $\lambda_1=\min_{0\ne u\in H^1_0(\Omega)}\|Du\|_{L^2}^2/\|u\|_{L^2}^2$ attained exactly at the nonzero first eigenfunctions and with $\lambda_1$ the smallest element of the eigenvalue list of an orthonormal eigenbasis $(e_j)\subseteq H^1_0(\Omega)$ of $L^2(\Omega)$, so $\lambda_*=\lambda_1$ is positive and attained by a first eigenfunction; Countable Choice enters through [L10], and Axiom of Choice is used for the compact-resolvent/spectral branch through [L17]. [L10, L17, given]

1.4 Unbounded domains need not attain. Fix $L>0$, put $L_k:=Lk/(k+1)$, $I_k:=(k(L+1),k(L+1)+L_k)$ and $\Omega:=\bigcup_{k\ge1}I_k$; since $L_k<L$, successive intervals have gap $(L+1)-L_k>1$, so the components are disjoint. Every $u\in H^1_0(\Omega)$ satisfies $\int_\Omega|u|^2=\sum_k\int_{I_k}|u|^2$ and $\int_\Omega|u'|^2=\sum_k\int_{I_k}|u'|^2$, and the restriction $u|_{I_k}$ lies in $H^1_0(I_k)$, because restriction to a component carries $C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ into $C_c^\infty(I_k)$ with classical derivatives restricting to classical derivatives and is bounded for the $W^{1,2}$ norm, hence carries the closure of the test functions into the closure; on each $I_k$ the sharp inequality [L11] gives $\int_{I_k}|u'|^2\ge(\pi/L_k)^2\int_{I_k}|u|^2\ge(\pi/L)^2\int_{I_k}|u|^2$ because $L_k<L$, so $a_0(u,u)\ge(\pi/L)^2\|u\|_{L^2}^2$ and $\lambda_*\ge(\pi/L)^2$, while the functions $\phi_k(x):=\sin(\pi(x-k(L+1))/L_k)$ on $I_k$, extended by zero, lie in $H^1_0(\Omega)$ because they lie in $H^1_0(I_k)$ by [L11] and are limits of compactly supported smooth functions on $I_k$ zero-extended to $\Omega$, and have Rayleigh quotient $(\pi/L_k)^2$, so $\lambda_*\le\inf_k(\pi/L_k)^2=(\pi/L)^2$; hence $\lambda_*=(\pi/L)^2$ is positive and $a_0$ is coercive, but the infimum is not attained: otherwise $\sum_k\int_{I_k}(|u'|^2-\lambda_*|u|^2)=0$ with every summand at least $((\pi/L_k)^2-(\pi/L)^2)\int_{I_k}|u|^2\ge0$ and every coefficient strictly positive, forcing $\int_{I_k}|u|^2=0$ for all $k$ and hence $u=0$, which is excluded from the Rayleigh quotient. [L1, L11, given, algebra]

2.1 Self-adjointness of $A$. By [L3] $L=L^*$ with $D(L^*)=D(L)$; since $D(A)=D(L)$ and $A=-L$ by [step 1.1], the adjoint description of [L4] gives $D(A^*)=D((-L)^*)=\{y:x\mapsto\langle -Lx,y\rangle\text{ bounded}\}=\{y:x\mapsto\langle Lx,y\rangle\text{ bounded}\}=D(L^*)=D(L)=D(A)$ and $\langle x,A^*y\rangle=\langle Ax,y\rangle=-\langle Lx,y\rangle=-\langle x,Ly\rangle=\langle x,-Ly\rangle$ for all $x\in D(A)$, so $A^*y=-Ly=Ay$ for every $y\in D(A)$ and $A$ is self-adjoint; with [step 1.1] $A$ is densely defined and satisfies $\langle Au,u\rangle\le0$. [step 1.1, L3, L4, algebra]

2.2 Identification with the Dirichlet Laplacian. By [L8] the symbol $\Delta_D$ denotes the $L^2$ operator associated with the Dirichlet energy form in the $u'=Au$ convention, whose defining weak identity is exactly $a_0(u,v)=-(Au,v)_{L^2}$ for all $v\in H^1_0(\Omega)$; [step 1.1] shows that $A$ has this defining identity, so $A=\Delta_D$. [step 1.1, L8, given]

2.3 Domain identification for $n\ge2$. Assume $n\ge2$, $\Omega$ a bounded $C^2$ domain, and let $u\in D(A)=D(L)$ with datum $f=Lu\in L^2(\Omega)$; [L12] applied to the weak equation $Lu=f$ gives $u\in H^2(\Omega)$ with $\|u\|_{H^2(\Omega)}\le C(\|Lu\|_{L^2}+\|u\|_{L^2})=C(\|Au\|_{L^2}+\|u\|_{L^2})$; conversely, if $u\in H^2(\Omega)\cap H^1_0(\Omega)$, then for every $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ the weak-derivative identities of [L18] applied to $\partial_ju\in H^1(\Omega)$ with test function $\overline{\varphi}$ give $a_0(u,\varphi)=\sum_j\int_\Omega\partial_ju\,\overline{\partial_j\varphi}=-\sum_j\int_\Omega\partial_j^2u\,\overline{\varphi}=-(\Delta u,\varphi)_{L^2}$, and because $C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ is dense in $H^1_0(\Omega)$ by [L1] while $v\mapsto a_0(u,v)$ and $v\mapsto(\Delta u,v)_{L^2}$ are both continuous on $H^1_0(\Omega)$, the identity $a_0(u,v)=-(\Delta u,v)_{L^2}$ holds for every $v\in H^1_0(\Omega)$; hence $u\in D(L)$ with $Lu=-\Delta u$, so $u\in D(A)$ with $Au=\Delta u$; therefore $D(A)=H^2(\Omega)\cap H^1_0(\Omega)$ and $Au=\Delta u$ on $D(A)$, and the estimate above together with $\|\Delta u\|_{L^2}\le n\|u\|_{H^2(\Omega)}$ shows that the graph norm $\|u\|_{L^2}+\|Au\|_{L^2}$ is equivalent to $\|u\|_{H^2(\Omega)}$. [step 1.1, L1, L12, L18, algebra]

2.4 Domain identification for $n=1$. Assume $n=1$; by [L14] a bounded $C^2$ domain is then a finite disjoint union of bounded open intervals $I_k$, and the following argument applies to each component and each $u\in D(A)=D(L)$: for $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(I_k)$, extended by zero to $\Omega$, the identity $a_0(u,\varphi)=(f,\varphi)_{L^2}$ with datum $f=Lu$ reads $\int_{I_k}u'\overline{\varphi'}=\int_{I_k}f\overline{\varphi}$, and replacing $\varphi$ by $\overline{\varphi}$ in the bilinear weak-derivative convention of [L18] shows that $-f$ is the weak derivative of the class $u'\in L^2(I_k)$; hence $u'$ has an $L^2$ weak derivative, $u\in W^{2,2}(I_k)=H^2(I_k)$, and $Lu=f=-u''$; conversely, if $u\in H^2(\Omega)\cap H^1_0(\Omega)$, then $u''\in L^2(\Omega)$ and the same weak-derivative identity gives $a_0(u,\varphi)=(-u'',\varphi)_{L^2}$ for every $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$, which extends to every $v\in H^1_0(\Omega)$ by density by [L1]; hence $u\in D(A)$ with $Au=\Delta u=u''$; consequently $D(A)=H^2(\Omega)\cap H^1_0(\Omega)$ and $Au=\Delta u$ in dimension one as well, and the graph norm is equivalent to the $H^2$ norm, since $\|u'\|_{L^2}^2=-(u'',u)_{L^2}\le\|u''\|_{L^2}\|u\|_{L^2}$ by [step 1.1] gives $\|u\|_{H^2(\Omega)}^2=\|u\|_{L^2}^2+\|u'\|_{L^2}^2+\|u''\|_{L^2}^2\le(\|u\|_{L^2}+\|u''\|_{L^2})^2\le2(\|u\|_{L^2}+\|Au\|_{L^2})^2$ while $\|u\|_{L^2}+\|Au\|_{L^2}\le2\|u\|_{H^2(\Omega)}$. [step 1.1, L1, L14, L18, given, algebra]

3.1 Generation and maximal angle. Applying [L5] to the self-adjoint operator $A$ of [step 2.1] with its quadratic bound of [step 1.1] yields that $A$ is sectorial of angle $\pi/2$ with vertex $0$ in the $e^{tA}$ convention and generates a bounded analytic semigroup $(T(z))_{z\in\Sigma_{\pi/2}\cup\{0\}}$ of angle $\pi/2$ with generator $A$, contractive on $[0,\infty)$; by the definition of the analytic-semigroup angle in [L6] the admissible angles are the $\delta\in(0,\pi/2]$ for which such a family exists, so the exhibited family realizes the maximal allowed angle $\pi/2$, and $\|T(t)\|\le1$ for $t\ge0$. [step 2.1, L5, L6, algebra]

3.2 Higher-order domains for $n\ge2$. Assume $n\ge2$ and $\Omega$ a bounded $C^{2m}$ domain, and let $E_m:=\{u\in H^{2m}(\Omega):\Delta^ju\in H^1_0(\Omega),\ 0\le j<m\}$; the claim is $D(A^m)=E_m$ for every $m\ge1$, and $E_1=D(A)$ with $Au=\Delta u$ is [step 2.3]; if $u\in D(A^m)$ with $m\ge2$, then $u\in D(A^{m-1})$ and $Au\in D(A^{m-1})$, so the induction hypothesis gives $u\in H^{2m-2}(\Omega)$ with $\Delta^ju\in H^1_0(\Omega)$ for $j<m-1$, and gives $Au\in H^{2m-2}(\Omega)$ with $\Delta^j(Au)\in H^1_0(\Omega)$ for $j<m-1$; since $Au=\Delta u$ by [step 2.3], the datum $f=Lu=-Au$ lies in $H^{2m-2}(\Omega)$, so [L13] with $k=2m-2$ applied to the weak solution $u\in H^1_0(\Omega)$ upgrades $u$ to $H^{2m}(\Omega)$, and $\Delta^ju\in H^1_0(\Omega)$ for $j<m$ holds by the induction hypothesis and $\Delta^j(Au)\in H^1_0(\Omega)$; hence $D(A^m)\subseteq E_m$; conversely, if $u\in E_m$ with $m\ge2$, then $u\in E_1=D(A)$ with $Au=\Delta u$, and $\Delta u\in H^{2m-2}(\Omega)$ has $\Delta^j(\Delta u)=\Delta^{j+1}u\in H^1_0(\Omega)$ for $j<m-1$, so $\Delta u\in E_{m-1}=D(A^{m-1})$ by the induction hypothesis, and $u\in D(A)$ with $Au=\Delta u\in D(A^{m-1})$ gives $u\in D(A^m)$ by the recursive definition; hence $E_m\subseteq D(A^m)$ and $D(A^m)=E_m$ for all $m\ge1$. [step 2.3, L13, given, algebra]

3.3 Higher-order domains for $n=1$. If $n=1$, then $\Omega$ is a finite disjoint union of bounded open intervals by [L14], and the weak-derivative argument of [step 2.4] gives, by induction on $m$, the description $D(A^m)=\{u\in H^{2m}(\Omega):\Delta^ju\in H^1_0(\Omega),\ 0\le j<m\}$: the case $m=1$ is [step 2.4]; if $u\in D(A^m)$ with $m\ge2$, then $u\in D(A^{m-1})$ and $Au\in D(A^{m-1})$, so the induction hypothesis applied to $u$ gives $u\in H^{2m-2}(\Omega)$ with $u^{(2j)}\in H^1_0(\Omega)$ for $j<m-1$ and applied to $Au=u''$ gives $u''\in H^{2m-2}(\Omega)$ with $u^{(2j+2)}\in H^1_0(\Omega)$ for $j<m-1$, hence $u\in H^{2m}(\Omega)$ and all $u^{(2j)}$, $0\le j<m$, lie in $H^1_0(\Omega)$; conversely, if $u\in H^{2m}(\Omega)$ has $u^{(2j)}\in H^1_0(\Omega)$ for $j<m$, then $u\in D(A)$ with $Au=u''$ by [step 2.4] and $u''\in H^{2m-2}(\Omega)$ satisfies $(u'')^{(2j)}=u^{(2j+2)}\in H^1_0(\Omega)$ for $j<m-1$, so $u''\in D(A^{m-1})$ by the induction hypothesis and $u\in D(A^m)$; in dimension one no boundary-regularity hypothesis is needed, because the weak-derivative identity of [L18] is available on every open set. [step 2.4, L14, L18, algebra]

4.1 Exponential decay under coercivity. If $a_0(u,u)\ge\alpha\|u\|_{L^2}^2$ for some $\alpha>0$ and all $u\in H^1_0(\Omega)$, then $\lambda_*\ge\alpha>0$, and [step 1.1] gives $\langle Au,u\rangle=-a_0(u,u)\le-\lambda_*\|u\|^2$ for every $u\in D(A)$, so the $\lambda_* >0$ branch of [L7] produces a bounded analytic semigroup of angle $\pi/2$ generated by $A$ with $\|T(z)\|\le e^{-\lambda_*\operatorname{Re}z}$ on $\Sigma_{\pi/2}$; by [L15] the contour semigroup is the unique exponentially bounded strongly continuous semigroup with generator $A$, and both the family of [L7] and the family $T$ of [step 3.1] are exponentially bounded and have generator $A$, so they coincide and $\|T(t)\|\le e^{-\lambda_*t}$ for $t\ge0$. [step 1.1, step 3.1, L7, L15, given, algebra]

4.2 Positive-time smoothing. By [L15] applied to the sectorial operator $A$ of [step 3.1], the semigroup $T$ generated by $A$ satisfies $T(t)L^2(\Omega)\subseteq D(A^m)$ for every $t>0$ and $m\ge1$, where the graph domains are the recursive domains of the statement: $D(A^m)=\{u\in D(A^{m-1}):Au\in D(A^{m-1})\}$ is equivalent to $\{u\in D(A^{m-1}):A^{m-1}u\in D(A)\}$ by induction on $m$, and $D(A^0)=L^2(\Omega)$. [step 3.1, L15, algebra]

5.1 The boundary-compatibility caveat. The spatial identifications in steps 3.2 and 3.3 retain $\Delta^ju\in H^1_0(\Omega)$ for every $0\le j<m$: these conditions follow from the recursive operator domain and cannot be discarded merely because $u\in H^{2m}(\Omega)$. For example, on a nonempty bounded smooth domain the constant function $1$ belongs to every $H^{2m}$ but not to $H^1_0$, since [L9] would give $\|1\|_2\le C\|\nabla1\|_2=0$ if it did. Moreover [L16] shows that the regularity suppliers for $n\ge2$ apply only under their boundary and data hypotheses. Thus the abstract smoothing of step 4.2 supplies the recursive graph domain on general open sets; the stated spatial conclusions require their additional hypotheses. This reasoning uses neither a trace lifting nor any additional choice assumption. [step 3.2, step 3.3, step 4.2, L9, L16, given, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The generation asserted in (1) uses the quadratic bound of [step 1.1] and [L5], with Countable Choice entering through the self-adjointness supplier [L3]; the elliptic estimates [L12] and [L13] also assume Countable Choice. Axiom of Choice is used additionally only in the bounded-domain compact-resolvent/spectral branch [step 1.3] through [L10] and [L17]; no step uses any further choice principle. The maximal angle $\pi/2$ of (1) is the largest angle the definition of a bounded analytic semigroup admits, and no claim is made that the semigroup is bounded on any larger sector, nor that the unshifted semigroup of a general coercive form is bounded without the self-adjointness used here.
