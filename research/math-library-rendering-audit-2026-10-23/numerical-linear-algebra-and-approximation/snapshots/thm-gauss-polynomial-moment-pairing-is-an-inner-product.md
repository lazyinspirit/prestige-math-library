## Statement

Let $(a,b,m,r,\mu)$ be moment data as in
[[def-gauss-moment-data]]. For $0\le k\le m$ with $2k\le r$, the pairing
[[def-gauss-polynomial-moment-inner-product]] is a well-defined real inner
product on $\mathbb R^{k+1}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Moment data $(a,b,m,r,\mu)$, an integer $k$ with $0\le k\le m$ and
$2k\le r$, and coefficient vectors $c,d\in\mathbb R^{k+1}$.

[F1] [[def-gauss-moment-data]]: $\mu$ is a finite positive Borel measure; its relative support contains at least $m+1$ points; and $|x|^j$ is integrable for every $j\le r$.

[F2] [[def-gauss-polynomial-moment-inner-product]]: $P_c(x)=\sum_{i=0}^k c_ix^i$ and $\langle c,d\rangle_\mu=\int P_cP_d\,d\mu$.

[F3] [[thm-nonzero-real-polynomial-has-at-most-degree-many-distinct-roots]]: a nonzero real polynomial of degree at most $k$ has at most $k$ distinct real roots.

[F4] [[prop-order-and-scalar-rules-for-the-nonnegative-integral]], [[prop-the-nonnegative-integral-agrees-with-the-simple-integral]], and [[def-integral-of-a-nonnegative-simple-function]]: the nonnegative integral is monotone and positively homogeneous, and $\int\mathbf1_U\,d\mu=\mu(U)$ for a Borel set $U$.

[F5] [[thm-linearity-of-the-lebesgue-integral-on-l-one]]: finite sums and real scalar multiples of integrable functions are integrable and their integrals obey the corresponding linearity rules.

[F6] [[thm-algebra-of-continuous-functions]]: every polynomial function is continuous; the relative Borel sigma-algebra is generated by the relative open sets ([[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]).

[F7] [[thm-reals-field]]: real addition and multiplication are distributive and multiplication is commutative.

[F8] [[def-inner-product-space]]: a real inner product is bilinear, symmetric, nonnegative on the diagonal, and definite.

## Proof

1.1 Each monomial $x^j$ with $j\le 2k$ is integrable by [F1]. Expanding the finite coefficient sums in [F2] expresses $P_cP_d$ as a finite real linear combination of such monomials, so [F5] makes the product integrable. Distributivity and [F5] then give bilinearity in each coefficient vector. [F1, F2, F5, algebra]

1.2 Real multiplication is commutative by [F7], so $P_cP_d=P_dP_c$ pointwise and the pairing is symmetric. [F2, F7, algebra]

1.3 If $c\ne0$, its coefficient list defines a nonzero formal polynomial $P_c$ of degree at most $k$. By [F3], its zero set has at most $k$ points. Since the support in [F1] has at least $m+1\ge k+1$ distinct points, choose a support point $s$ with $P_c(s)\ne0$. [F1, F2, F3, choose]

2.1 Put $\alpha=P_c(s)^2>0$. Continuity in [F6] gives a relative open neighborhood $U$ of $s$ on which $P_c(x)^2>\alpha/2$. This set is Borel and has $\mu(U)>0$ by the support definition. Monotonicity, homogeneity and the indicator identity in [F4] imply $\langle c,c\rangle_\mu=\int P_c^2\,d\mu\ge \frac{\alpha}{2}\mu(U)>0.$ [F1, F2, F4, F6, step 1.3, choose, algebra]

3.1 The pairing is well-defined by step 1.1, real-bilinear and symmetric by steps 1.1–1.2, nonnegative on the diagonal, and zero on the diagonal only for $c=0$ by step 2.1. These are the real inner product axioms in [F8]. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, F8] ∎
