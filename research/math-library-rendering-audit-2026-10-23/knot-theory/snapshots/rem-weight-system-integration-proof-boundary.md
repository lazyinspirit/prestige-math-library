## Remark

The converse integration theorem is a separate established result: over $\mathbb Q$, every unframed weight system is the top symbol of a finite-type knot invariant, by the Kontsevich integral. For every characteristic-zero field $k$, the same existence follows from the rational theorem by the finite-dimensional scalar-extension argument below. The Kontsevich integration proof itself belongs to the later authoring pair KT-35 and is not supplied here. This converse is not a premise for the KT-34 symbol theorem or its local relations, and it is an established theorem rather than an open problem.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A degree $n\ge0$, a characteristic-zero field $k$, and an unframed $k$-valued weight system on degree-$n$ chord diagrams modulo 4T and 1T.

[F1] [[def-framed-chord-diagram-and-four-term-relations]]: in degree $n$ the unframed symbol module is generated by finitely many chord diagrams modulo 4T and 1T relations, each an integer-coefficient linear combination of diagrams; a weight system is a linear functional on this quotient.

[F2] [Chmutov and Lando, “Mutant knots and intersection graphs,” §2, PDF pp. 2–3](https://msp.org/agt/2007/7-3/agt-v7-n3-p20-p.pdf): over $\mathbb Q$, the Kontsevich invariant integrates every unframed rational weight system to the top symbol of a finite-type knot invariant.

## Proof

1.1 A degree-$n$ chord diagram is a pairing of $2n$ cyclically ordered endpoints, so only finitely many such diagrams exist. A 4T relation is determined by a background diagram and finitely many endpoint positions, and a 1T relation by one diagram; hence there are finitely many relation vectors. By [F1], each has integer coefficients, so they form the rows of a finite integer matrix $A$. Write $A_n(F):=F^{C_n}/\operatorname{rowspan}_F(A)$ and $W_n(F):=\operatorname{Hom}_F(A_n(F),F)$. Since $k$ has characteristic zero, $\mathbb Q$ embeds in $k$. The rank of $A$ is unchanged by this field extension: a nonzero maximal minor over $\mathbb Q$ remains nonzero in $k$, while every larger minor that vanishes over $\mathbb Q$ still vanishes in $k$. Thus the row space over $k$ is the scalar extension of the rational row space, and $A_n(k)\cong A_n(\mathbb Q)\otimes_{\mathbb Q}k$. [given, F1, algebra]

2.1 The quotient is finite-dimensional, so extending a rational basis and its dual basis gives $W_n(k)\cong W_n(\mathbb Q)\otimes_{\mathbb Q}k$. If $n=0$, the sole diagram is the empty diagram and any weight system is its value $c\in k$; the constant invariant $V(K)=c$ has top symbol $c$. For $n>0$, choose a finite $\mathbb Q$-basis $\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_d$ of $W_n(\mathbb Q)$; finite-dimensional row reduction chooses this basis without a choice axiom. By [F2], for each $i$ there is a rational-valued finite-type invariant $V_i$ with top symbol $\phi_i$. Write the given weight system as $W=\sum_{i=1}^d a_i\phi_i$ with $a_i\in k$, and define $V=\sum_{i=1}^d a_iV_i$, viewing each rational value in $k$. Linearity of the finite-difference operator gives top symbol $W$. If $W_n(\mathbb Q)=0$, the sum is empty and the zero invariant supplies the zero weight system. [given, F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
