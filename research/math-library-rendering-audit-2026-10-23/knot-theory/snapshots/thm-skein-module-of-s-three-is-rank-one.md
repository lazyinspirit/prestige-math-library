## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice and let $R=\mathbb Z[A^{\pm1}]$ and
$\delta=-A^2-A^{-2}$. The Kauffman bracket skein module
$\mathcal K_A(S^3)$ is freely generated as an $R$-module by the empty link.
In particular, a zero-framed trivial circle represents
$\delta[\varnothing]$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Countable Choice and the Kauffman bracket skein module of the oriented three-sphere.

[A1] The Axiom of Countable Choice is the assumption $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ ([[def-countable-choice]]).

[F1] The module is generated by framed-link isotopy classes modulo the crossing relation $[L_\times]=A[L_A]+A^{-1}[L_B]$ and the zero-framed trivial-circle relation $[L\sqcup O]=\delta[L]$ ([[def-kauffman-bracket-skein-module-of-a-three-manifold]]).

[F2] Under $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, every framed link in $S^3$ has a regular planar diagram, and framed isotopies are represented by finite diagram moves ([[lem-framed-link-diagram-calculus]]).

[F3] Adding one positive or negative full twist to one component multiplies its skein class by $-A^3$ or $-A^{-3}$, respectively ([[lem-kauffman-bracket-module-framing-twist-factor]]).

[F4] Framed bracket evaluation is well-defined on $\mathcal K_A(S^3)$, is $R$-linear, and sends $[\varnothing]$ to $1$ ([[lem-kauffman-bracket-evaluation-is-invariant-for-framed-links-in-s-three]]).

## Proof


1.1 We prove by induction on the number of crossings of a regular diagram for a blackboard-framed link that its class is a scalar multiple of $[\varnothing]$. If the diagram has a crossing, the crossing relation in [F1] expresses its class as the sum of the two smoothing classes with coefficients $A$ and $A^{-1}$. The skein convention gives each smoothing its blackboard framing, and each has one fewer crossing, so the induction applies. A crossingless diagram is a finite disjoint union of planar circles. Choose an innermost circle; its planar disk has no other component in its interior and is disjoint from the remaining link. Its blackboard framing is the zero framing, so [F1] removes it with factor $\delta$. Remove this component and repeat; the crossingless link reduces to a scalar multiple of the empty link. For a general framed link, [F2] supplies a diagram and [F3], applied finitely many times to its framing-correction integer, reduces its class to a scalar multiple of the corresponding blackboard-framed diagram. Hence every class lies in $R[\varnothing]$. [A1, F1, F2, F3, given, induction, algebra]

2.1 Define $\phi:R\to\mathcal K_A(S^3)$ by $\phi(r)=r[\varnothing]$. Step 1.1 makes $\phi$ surjective. If $\phi(r)=0$, $R$-linearity and $E([\varnothing])=1$ give $r=E(\phi(r))=E(0)=0$. Thus $\phi$ is injective as well as surjective, so the empty class is a free $R$-module generator. [F4, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The trivial-circle relation in [F1], with $L=\varnothing$, gives $[O]=\delta[\varnothing]$, as claimed. [F1, algebra] ∎
