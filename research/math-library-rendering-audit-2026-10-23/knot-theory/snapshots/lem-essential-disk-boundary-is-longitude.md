## Statement

Let $E$ be the compact exterior of a smooth knot with supplied product boundary torus $T=S^1\times S^1$, fibre meridian $\mu$, and unique degree-one zero-homology longitude class $[\lambda]$ from the product data. Let $D\subset E$ be a properly embedded piecewise-linear disk, meaning an embedded copy of the closed disk with $D\cap T=\partial D$. If $\partial D$ is not null-homotopic in $T$, then $[\partial D]=[\lambda]$ or $-[\lambda]$ in $H_1(T;\mathbb Z)$, and its mod-two intersection number with $\mu$ is $1$.

## Statement references

[[lem-knot-exterior-homology-and-longitude-via-mayer-vietoris]] — the kernel of boundary-to-exterior homology is generated by the degree-one longitude, and meridian/longitude form a basis.

[[lem-essential-simple-curve-on-torus-is-primitive]] — a non-null-homotopic simple curve on the torus represents a primitive class.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $E,T,\mu,\lambda$ and the properly embedded disk $D$ in the statement.

[F1] [[lem-knot-exterior-homology-and-longitude-via-mayer-vietoris]]: $H_1(T;\mathbb Z)$ has basis $([\mu],[\lambda])$, the class $[\lambda]$ generates the kernel of $i_*:H_1(T)\to H_1(E)$, and product projection sends $[\mu]$ to $0$ and $[\lambda]$ to $1$.

[F2] [[lem-essential-simple-curve-on-torus-is-primitive]]: every non-null-homotopic embedded PL simple closed curve on a product torus has a nonzero primitive homology class.

## Proof

1.1 The boundary $\partial D$ is an embedded PL simple closed curve in $T$. Since it bounds the disk $D$ in $E$, its image under inclusion $i:T\hookrightarrow E$ is zero. By [F1], the kernel is $\mathbb Z[\lambda]$, so $[\partial D]=k[\lambda]$ for an integer $k$. [given, F1, construct]

2.1 The hypothesis and [F2] say $[\partial D]$ is nonzero and primitive. Since $[\lambda]$ is a basis vector by [F1], the multiple $k[\lambda]$ is primitive only if $k=1$ or $k=-1$. Hence $[\partial D]=\pm[\lambda]$. [given, F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Put the boundary curve in PL transverse position to the fibre $\mu=p^{-1}(s_0)$, where $p:T\to S^1$ is product projection. Its intersection points are exactly the preimages of $s_0$ under $p|_{\partial D}$. A lift of this PL circle map to $\mathbb R$ changes by its degree, which is the product-projection coefficient of $[\partial D]$, namely $+1$ or $-1$ by step 2.1. For a regular value, the signed crossing count of the lift through the integer translates of that value equals this degree; reducing modulo $2$ removes the signs, so the number of preimages is odd. Thus $\partial D$ has mod-two intersection $1$ with $\mu$. [F1, step 2.1, algebra, construct]

4.1 Steps 2.1 and 3.1 give the longitude class and the asserted parity. [step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
