## Statement

Let $\ell\ge3$, $q=e^{\pi i/\ell}$, $\delta=q+q^{-1}>0$, $k=\ell-2$, and let $[a]=\sin(a\pi/\ell)/\sin(\pi/\ell)$. For the normalized closure trace $\tau_n$, let $J_n$ be its trace-pairing radical and put $Q_n=\mathrm{TL}_n(\mathbb C,\delta)/J_n$. Then $J_n$ is exactly the endomorphism component of the negligible tensor ideal generated by the boundary Jones–Wenzl projector $p_{\ell-1}$. The $Q_n$ form the actual $A_{\ell-1}$ path-space matrix tower of [[def-temperley-lieb-type-a-path-spaces-and-matrix-unit-tower]], with paths starting at $0$ and staying in $\{0,\ldots,k\}$. Their simple modules are precisely the nonzero endpoint path spaces, and restriction branches with multiplicity one over adjacent predecessor vertices.

Reflection gives a finite-dimensional $C^*$-structure on $Q_n$ in which the normalized closure trace is faithful positive. The strand inclusions are injective unital $*$-maps and normalized right closure descends to the positive trace-preserving conditional expectation $\bar E_n:Q_{n+1}\to Q_n$ for $n\ge1$. More explicitly, choose the normalized path matrix units $F_{p,q}$ whose prefix inclusions satisfy $F_{p,q}\mapsto\sum_c F_{pc,qc}$. If $pc,qc$ end at $c$, with predecessor endpoints $a,b$, then
$$\bar E_n(F_{pc,qc})=\begin{cases}\dfrac{[c+1]}{\delta[a+1]}F_{p,q},&a=b,\\0,&a\ne b.\end{cases}$$
Here the extensions and matrix units exist only for valid adjacent retained labels. The diagonal trace is $\tau_n(F_{p,p})=\delta^{-n}[a+1]$ when $p$ ends at $a$. These assertions hold at every finite rank, including the $\ell=3$ boundary; no extra quotient/path identification is assumed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The stated positive root parameters and the closure/reflection convention.

[F1] Inclusion and normalized right closure preserve the trace radicals, are bimodular and trace-preserving, and satisfy $E_n\iota_n=\mathrm{id}$. ([[lem-temperley-lieb-right-closure-preserves-trace-radical]], [[lem-temperley-lieb-diagram-tower-conditional-expectation-identities]])

[F2] The retained path inclusions and retractions are recursively constructed by the JW up/down splitting, with complete path matrix units and diagonal unnormalized trace $[a+1]$. At endpoint $a\ge1$ the supplier's down maps are the cup inclusion $\alpha_a$ and cap map $\lambda_a^{-1}\beta_a$, where $\lambda_a=[a+1]/[a]>0$; it proves $\beta_a\alpha_a=\lambda_a p_{a-1}$ but does not assert $\beta_a=\alpha_a^*$. The adjoint relation and orthogonal normalization used below are proved locally. ([[lem-temperley-lieb-root-fusion-paths-have-nonzero-pairings]], [[lem-temperley-lieb-jones-wenzl-partial-trace-and-uniqueness]])

[F3] The fixed embedded boundary ideal is exactly $J_n$, its quotient is the full retained path matrix algebra, and categorical negligibility has these exact endomorphism components. ([[thm-temperley-lieb-root-negligible-quotient-is-semisimple]])

[F4] The abstract endpoint path tower uses prefix extension, and its restricted endpoint module branches once over each adjacent predecessor. ([[def-temperley-lieb-type-a-path-spaces-and-matrix-unit-tower]], [[thm-temperley-lieb-type-a-path-space-tower-branching]])

[F5] At this positive root, $[a]>0$ for $1\le a<\ell$, $[\ell]=0$, and $[a]+[a+2]=\delta[a+1]$. ([[def-temperley-lieb-quantum-integers-and-quantum-characteristic]])

[F6] Every normal endomorphism of a finite-dimensional complex inner-product space has an orthonormal eigenbasis; applied to a self-adjoint matrix, its eigenvalues are real and its negative spectral projection is an element of that same matrix block. ([[thm-complex-spectral-theorem-for-normal-endomorphisms]])

[F7] At the positive root, $\delta$ is real, the diagram involution is conjugate-linear horizontal reflection (fixing $1$ and each $U_i$ and exchanging cups with caps), and the normalized closure trace has diagram value $\tau_n(D)=\delta^{c(D)-n}$. Reflection preserves the number $c(D)$ of components after closure, and cyclicity is supplied by the closure-trace argument used in [F1]. ([[lem-temperley-lieb-diagram-tower-conditional-expectation-identities]], [[lem-temperley-lieb-right-closure-preserves-trace-radical]])

## Proof

**Given:** The hypotheses and [F1]–[F5].

1.1 The quantum-integer recurrence with real $\delta$ makes each $[r]$ real, and [F5] gives $[r]>0$ for $1\le r<\ell$. Starting with $p_0=p_1=1$, induction in the JW recursion shows $p_r^*=p_r$ through $r=\ell-1$: reflection fixes $P_r=p_r\otimes1$ and $U_r$, while the coefficient $[r]/[r+1]$ is real whenever this recursion is used. In particular the boundary projector $p_{\ell-1}$ is self-adjoint. For $n<\ell-1$, the generated ideal component $I_n$ is zero and is reflection-stable; for $n\ge\ell-1$, its fixed embedded generator $p_{\ell-1}\otimes1$ is self-adjoint, so $I_n=\mathrm{TL}_n(p_{\ell-1}\otimes1)\mathrm{TL}_n$ is reflection-stable because $(x(p_{\ell-1}\otimes1)y)^*=y^*(p_{\ell-1}\otimes1)x^*$. By [F3], these are the endomorphism components of the generated negligible tensor ideal. Also [F7] gives $\tau_n(D^*)=\overline{\tau_n(D)}$ on diagrams and hence on all elements. If $x\in J_n$, then for every $y$ cyclicity yields $\tau_n(yx^*)=\overline{\tau_n(xy^*)}=\overline{\tau_n(y^*x)}=0$; thus the trace radical is reflection-stable as well. Since [F3] identifies it with $I_n$, the quotient involution is well defined. [F2, F3, F5, F7, given, algebra]

2.1 Fix a retained endpoint $1\le a\le k$, write $f_a=p_a\otimes1$, and use the supplier's maps $\alpha_a=f_a(1_{a-1}\otimes\mathrm{cup})p_{a-1}$ and $\beta_a=p_{a-1}(1_{a-1}\otimes\mathrm{cap})f_a$. Horizontal reflection reverses composition and exchanges the cup and cap; step 1.1 fixes $f_a$ and $p_{a-1}$, so $\alpha_a^*=\beta_a$. The supplier identity $\beta_a\alpha_a=\lambda_a p_{a-1}$ therefore gives $\alpha_a^*\alpha_a=\lambda_a p_{a-1}$. Set $v_a=\lambda_a^{-1/2}\alpha_a$. Then $v_a^*v_a=p_{a-1}$ and $v_av_a^*=\lambda_a^{-1}\alpha_a\beta_a$, the down projection in the JW split. For $a<k$, the complementary up projection is $p_{a+1}$; it is self-adjoint by step 1.1 and orthogonal to the down projection by the JW turnback relations in [F2]. At $a=k$, the quotient kills $p_{k+1}=p_{\ell-1}$ before normalization, so only the down projection remains. The up inclusion and retraction are adjoints, as are $v_a$ and $v_a^*$. Composing these isometric branch maps along retained paths gives path maps $I_p$ with mutually orthogonal ranges and $\sum_p I_pI_p^*=1_n$. Hence $F_{p,q}=I_pI_q^*$ are complete matrix units and $F_{p,q}^*=F_{q,p}$. Their diagonal units equal the supplier's path projections (the reciprocal branch normalizations cancel), so $\operatorname{Tr}_n(F_{p,q})=\delta_{p,q}[a+1]$ by [F2]. Thus the quotient with its inherited reflection is a direct sum of full complex matrix algebras with the usual adjoint, hence a finite-dimensional $C^*$-algebra. Since $\tau_n(F_{p,p})=\delta^{-n}[a+1]>0$, its normalized closure trace is faithful positive. [F2, F3, F5, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Tensoring an endpoint path map with a strand and resolving its endpoint identity by the same isometric up/down splitting gives $F_{p,q}\otimes1=\sum_c F_{pc,qc}$. The coefficients are exactly one because the inclusion and retraction at each common endpoint use reciprocal normalizations. At $a=k$ delete $p_{k+1}$ before splitting; only its down term remains, as in [F2]. Thus the actual quotient inclusions are exactly the prefix maps of [F4]; no compatibility is imposed as an additional hypothesis. Transporting the endpoint action along these matrix units and applying [F4] gives the asserted multiplicity-one branching, omitting zero path spaces and invalid boundary labels. [F2, F3, F4, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 [F1] defines $\bar\iota_n([x])=[\iota_n(x)]$ and $\bar E_n([z])=[E_n(z)]$ independently of representatives. Bimodularity, trace preservation and $\bar E_n\bar\iota_n=\mathrm{id}$ pass to these quotients, so inclusion is injective. Its compatibility with reflection follows from real $\delta$. Closure also commutes with reflection: the two finite matching graphs obtained by reflecting before or after closing have the same components and the real factor $\delta^{-1}$. Thus $\bar E_n$ is star-preserving. For $z\ge0$ and $b\in Q_n$, bimodularity gives $\tau_n(b^*\bar E_n(z)b)=\tau_{n+1}(\bar\iota_n(b)^*z\bar\iota_n(b))\ge0$. If the self-adjoint $\bar E_n(z)$ had a negative spectral subspace in one of the finite matrix blocks from step 2.1, its nonzero spectral projection would contradict this inequality and faithfulness. Hence $\bar E_n$ is positive, unital, idempotent, bimodular and trace-preserving, the claimed conditional expectation. [F1, F6, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Bimodularity with the endpoint central projections shows that $\bar E_n(F_{pc,qc})=0$ if the predecessor endpoints $a,b$ differ. If they agree, bimodularity with the individual predecessor matrix units shows that its value is a single scalar $t_{a,c}F_{p,q}$ independent of $p,q$: multiplying on the left and right by predecessor diagonal units isolates that matrix entry, and multiplying by predecessor off-diagonal units identifies their coefficients. Trace preservation on a diagonal unit now gives $t_{a,c}\delta^{-n}[a+1]=\delta^{-(n+1)}[c+1]$, so $t_{a,c}=[c+1]/(\delta[a+1])$. The coefficients are positive and sum to one over valid neighbours by [F5]; at the upper boundary the omitted term is $[\ell]=0$, and at $a=0$ the nonexistent down term is $[0]=0$. For $\ell=3$ every vertex has one retained neighbour and this coefficient is one. Rank zero and rank one have their unique scalar path algebra and the unital inclusion between them. This proves every assertion at the stated finite ranks. [F1, F4, F5, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
