## Statement

For $V^k(\mathfrak g)$ from [[def-universal-affine-vacuum-module]], multiplication induces a vector-space isomorphism
$$U(\mathfrak g\otimes t^{-1}\mathbb C[t^{-1}])\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }V^k(\mathfrak g),\qquad u\longmapsto u\mathbf1.$$
In particular, $\mathbf1\ne0$ and the ordered monomials $x^1_{-n_1}\cdots x^r_{-n_r}\mathbf1$ ($n_i\ge1$), for a fixed ordered basis of negative current modes, form a basis.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite-dimensional complex simple Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, its affine central extension, and $k\in\mathbb C$.

[F1] [[def-untwisted-affine-central-extension]]: $\widehat{\mathfrak g}=\mathfrak g\otimes\mathbb C[t,t^{-1}]\oplus\mathbb CK$ and $[x_m,y_n]=[x,y]_{m+n}+m\delta_{m,-n}B(x,y)K$.

[F2] [[def-universal-enveloping-algebra-as-a-tensor-quotient]]: $U(\mathfrak a)=T(\mathfrak a)/I$, where $I$ is generated by $xy-yx-[x,y]$ for every complex Lie algebra $\mathfrak a$; this definition applies to infinite-dimensional $\mathfrak a$.

[F3] [[def-universal-affine-vacuum-module]]: $V^k(\mathfrak g)=U(\widehat{\mathfrak g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak p)}\mathbb C_k$, where $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak g[t]\oplus\mathbb CK$ and $\mathbb C_k$ is a representation.

[F4] [[lem-affine-vacuum-character-at-level-k]]: $\mathfrak p$ is a Lie subalgebra, $\mathfrak g[t]$ acts by zero on $\mathbb C_k$, and $K$ acts by $k$.

## Proof

1.1 Fix a finite ordered basis $e_1,\ldots,e_d$ of $\mathfrak g$ and the countable basis $e_{i,n}=e_i\otimes t^n$ of the loop modes, together with $K$. Fix a total order on this basis that places every mode with $n<0$ before every basis element of $\mathfrak p$; within each part use any fixed enumeration. For basis letters $b_j>b_i$, apply the defining rewrite $b_jb_i\mapsto b_ib_j+[b_j,b_i]$. Order words by length and then inversion number. The swapped word has one fewer inversion and every bracket term has smaller length; each bracket is a finite linear combination of basis letters. Thus every reduction tree is finitely branching and strictly decreases this well-founded pair, so it terminates in a finite linear combination of ordered words. [F1, F2, given, construct]

1.2 Reductions at disjoint adjacent pairs commute. The only overlapping reductions occur in a descending triple $xyz$ of basis letters with $x>y>z$. Reducing the left pair first and then swapping the remaining inversions gives
$$xyz\longrightarrow zyx+[y,z]x+y[x,z]+[x,y]z,$$
whereas reducing the right pair first gives
$$xyz\longrightarrow zyx+z[x,y]+[x,z]y+x[y,z].$$
Their difference is $([y,z]x-x[y,z])+(y[x,z]-[x,z]y)+([x,y]z-z[x,y])$. By bilinearity, the two-letter defining relations reduce these three differences respectively to $[\,[y,z],x\,]$, $[y,[x,z]]$, and $[\,[x,y],z\,]$; their sum is zero by Jacobi. Each bracket expands into finitely many basis letters, and every comparison term has smaller word length than the original triple. Induction on (length, inversion number) therefore joins every overlapping reduction, also inside any fixed left and right word context, so all reduction paths from each word agree. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

2.1 The resulting linear normal-form map fixes every ordered word and sends each contextual relation $u(b_jb_i-b_ib_j-[b_j,b_i])v$ to zero, by the uniqueness in step 1.2. This checks all basis pairs: a reversed pair is the negative of its oriented relation by antisymmetry, and an equal pair has bracket zero. Bilinearity then gives the same for arbitrary $x,y\in\widehat{\mathfrak g}$, so the map kills the full two-sided ideal $I$ of [F2]. Conversely each reduction changes a word by an element of $I$. Hence ordered monomials span the quotient, and a linear combination of distinct ordered monomials in $I$ would be fixed by the normal-form map and also sent to zero; it must be zero. Ordered monomials are therefore a basis of $U(\widehat{\mathfrak g})$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 The negative-mode span $\mathfrak n_-:=\mathfrak g\otimes t^{-1}\mathbb C[t^{-1}]$ is a Lie subalgebra because sums of two negative indices stay negative and [F1] has no central term for them. The subalgebra $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak g[t]\oplus\mathbb CK$ is established in [F4]. With the order fixed in step 1.1, each PBW word is a negative-mode monomial followed by a $U(\mathfrak p)$-monomial. Thus multiplication identifies $U(\mathfrak n_-)\otimes U(\mathfrak p)$ with $U(\widehat{\mathfrak g})$ as a vector space and as a right $U(\mathfrak p)$-module. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Tensor the right-module decomposition in step 3.1 over $U(\mathfrak p)$ with $\mathbb C_k$. It identifies [F3] with $U(\mathfrak n_-)\otimes\mathbb C_k$; the negative-mode PBW monomials tensor the nonzero generator of $\mathbb C_k$ to a basis. The empty monomial is nonzero, proving the claimed basis and vacuum assertion. [F3, F4, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
