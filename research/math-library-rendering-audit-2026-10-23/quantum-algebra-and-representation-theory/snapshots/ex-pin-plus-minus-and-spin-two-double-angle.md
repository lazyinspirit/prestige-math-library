## Example

In $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,0}$ the lift of the nonidentity reflection in $O(1)$ squares to $+1$, while in $\mathrm{Cl}_{0,1}$ it squares to $-1$. In positive two-dimensional signature, $\operatorname{Spin}(2)$ is the unit circle in the even Clifford algebra and its map to $SO(2)$ doubles the angle.

An explicit complex Clifford spinor module is $S=\mathbb C^2$, of complex dimension $2$, with positive-square generators acting by
$$c(e_1)=A:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad c(e_2)=B:=\begin{pmatrix}0&i\\-i&0\end{pmatrix}.$$
It is irreducible as a complex Clifford module. For $E=e_1e_2$, the positive-square chirality $\omega=iE$ acts as $\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$. Its half-spin spaces $S^+=\mathbb C(1,0)$ and $S^-=\mathbb C(0,1)$ are inequivalent irreducible complex Spin$(2)$ modules, each of complex dimension $1$. Their characters on $a+bE$, where $a^2+b^2=1$, are
$$\chi_+(a+bE)=a-ib,\qquad\chi_-(a+bE)=a+ib.$$
Thus the lift $g_\theta=\cos(\theta/2)-E\sin(\theta/2)$ of rotation by $\theta$ acts on $S^\pm$ by $\cos(\theta/2)\pm i\sin(\theta/2)$, respectively. The $2\pi$ lift acts by $-1$ on both lines, whereas the $4\pi$ lift acts by $1$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The conventions Pin$^+(n)\subset\mathrm{Cl}_{n,0}$, Pin$^-(n)\subset\mathrm{Cl}_{0,n}$ and the full-even Spin definition.

[F1] Pin is generated by vectors with $|Q(u)|=1$, full Spin is its even subgroup, and the Clifford norm on vector products is $\prod(-Q(u_j))$ ([[def-real-clifford-group-and-normalized-pin-spin]], [[lem-normalized-pin-spin-product-and-norm-laws]]).

[F2] The twisted action of an anisotropic vector is its hyperplane reflection ([[lem-twisted-clifford-adjoint-reflection-and-scalar-kernel]]).

[F3] Ordered square-free monomials form a basis of the Clifford algebra ([[thm-clifford-pbw-basis-and-associated-graded]]).

[F4] Over $\mathbb C$, a complex-linear map from a quadratic space to a unital associative complex algebra satisfying $c(v)^2=Q(v)I$ extends uniquely to a unital Clifford algebra homomorphism ([[thm-universal-property-of-clifford-algebra]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 In $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,0}$, $e^2=1$ and the unit-vector-generated Pin group is $\{1,-1,e,-e\}$; its reflection lift $e$ has square $+1$. In $\mathrm{Cl}_{0,1}$, $f^2=-1$ and Pin is $\{1,-1,f,-f\}\cong C_4$; its reflection lift has square $-1$. In both cases the even subgroup is $\{1,-1\}$. [given, F1, algebra]

1.2 In $\mathrm{Cl}_{2,0}$ put $E=e_1e_2$. Then $E^2=-1$, and by [F3] the even algebra is exactly $\mathbb R\oplus\mathbb R E\cong\mathbb C$. For $a^2+b^2=1$, $a+bE$ is a product of two unit vectors: $e_1(ae_1+be_2)=a+bE$. Thus every unit $a+bE$ belongs to Spin, and conversely every even unit product has norm one, so Spin$(2)=\{a+bE:a^2+b^2=1\}$. [F1, F3, algebra]

1.3 The matrices in the Example satisfy $A^2=B^2=I$ and $AB=-BA=\operatorname{diag}(-i,i)$. Hence $(z_1A+z_2B)^2=(z_1^2+z_2^2)I$ for all $z_1,z_2\in\mathbb C$. Applying [F4] to the complexification of the positive plane defines a unital complex Clifford action on $S$. Its image contains $P_+=(I+iAB)/2=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$ and $P_-=(I-iAB)/2=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)$, as well as $P_+A$ and $P_-A$, which are the other two matrix units. The image is therefore all of $M_2(\mathbb C)$; [F3] gives complex dimension $4$ for the source, so this map is an algebra isomorphism. Every nonzero invariant subspace contains both coordinate vectors by applying these matrix units to a nonzero vector, proving irreducibility of the complex dimension-$2$ Clifford module. [F3, F4, construct, algebra]

2.1 For $\theta\in\mathbb R$, let $g_\theta=\cos(\theta/2)-E\sin(\theta/2)$. Since $g_\theta^{-1}=\cos(\theta/2)+E\sin(\theta/2)$, direct multiplication using $Ee_1=-e_2$ and $e_1E=e_2$ gives $\rho_{g_\theta}(e_1)=\cos\theta\,e_1+\sin\theta\,e_2$ and $\rho_{g_\theta}(e_2)=-\sin\theta\,e_1+\cos\theta\,e_2$. Therefore the angle doubles; $g_{2\pi}=-1$ and $g_{4\pi}=1$. [F2, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Since $c(E)=AB=\operatorname{diag}(-i,i)$, the operator $c(\omega)=iAB$ has the displayed eigenspaces $S^\pm$, each a complex line. Restricting the Clifford action to the circle in step 1.2 gives on these lines the scalars $a-ib$ and $a+ib$, of absolute value one. The scalar functions are continuous in $(a,b)$, and multiplicativity of the Clifford action makes them group characters; the one-dimensional modules are irreducible. They are inequivalent because the Spin element $E$ acts by $-i$ on $S^+$ and $i$ on $S^-$. Substituting $a=\cos(\theta/2)$ and $b=-\sin(\theta/2)$ yields the two asserted half-angle actions. In particular the kernel element $g_{2\pi}=-1$ acts by $-1$ and $g_{4\pi}=1$ acts by $1$, consistently with the vector double-angle map in step 2.1. [step 1.2, step 2.1, step 1.3, algebra] ∎
