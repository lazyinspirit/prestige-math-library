## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

Let $k$ be algebraically closed of characteristic zero. For the full orthogonal group $O(V,B)$ of a nondegenerate symmetric $m$-dimensional space, the invariant ring $k[V^t]^{O(V,B)}$ is generated by $q_{ij}=B(v_i,v_j)$ and the kernel of $k[z_{ij}:i\le j]\to k[V^t]$, $z_{ij}\mapsto q_{ij}$, is the ideal of all $(m+1)$-minors of the generic symmetric Gram matrix. For the full symplectic group $Sp(V,\omega)$ of a nondegenerate alternating $2n$-dimensional space, $k[V^t]^{Sp(V,\omega)}$ is generated by $q_{ij}=\omega(v_i,v_j)$ for $i<j$, and the kernel of $k[a_{ij}:i<j]\to k[V^t]$ is generated by all principal Pfaffians of size $2n+2$ of the generic alternating Gram matrix. In both branches the claims concern the algebraic-group coaction invariants defined in [[def-polynomial-invariants-for-a-rational-linear-action]], not invariants under a finite subgroup of $G(k)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice and the algebraic-group invariant convention in [[def-polynomial-invariants-for-a-rational-linear-action]], the Gram-map range in [[lem-gram-map-fibers-and-rank-strata]], and the local rank-ring and torsor/descent obligations in [[lem-symmetric-and-alternating-rank-coordinate-rings]] and [[lem-gram-map-torsor-descent-and-codimension-extension]].

[F1] [[lem-symmetric-and-alternating-rank-coordinate-rings]]: the Gram rank varieties have precisely the stated prime, normal minor/Pfaffian coordinate rings.

[F2] [[lem-gram-map-fibers-and-rank-strata]]: the Gram map has image exactly the stated rank-bounded variety.

[F3] [[lem-gram-map-torsor-descent-and-codimension-extension]]: full-rank invariant regular functions descend and extend uniquely.

[F4] [[cor-strong-nullstellensatz-two-inclusions]]: under AC, for an ideal $I$ of a finite-variable polynomial ring over algebraically closed $k$, its closed-point vanishing ideal is $I(V(I))=\sqrt I$.

## Proof

1.1 If $V=0$, its tuple space has coordinate ring $k$ and both groups are trivial. The kernel of the Gram-coordinate map is the ideal of all entries, namely the $1$-minor ideal in the symmetric case and the $2$-Pfaffian ideal in the alternating case, so the result holds directly. Assume now $m=\dim V>0$ and first take $t\ge m+1$. Every Gram coordinate is invariant under the full isometry group, by its defining equation. Conversely an invariant polynomial $f$ on $V^t$ restricts to an invariant regular function on the open set of spanning tuples. The Gram-torsor descent in [F3] gives a regular function on the rank-$m$ Gram locus; [F1] and the codimension-extension assertion of [F3] extend it to a unique element $h$ of the Gram rank ring. Choose a polynomial representative $H$ for $h$. Then $f=H(q_{ij})$ on the spanning-tuples open set. This open is nonempty and dense in the affine space $V^t$ (some $m$ columns can be chosen as a basis), so the two polynomials agree everywhere. This proves generation in this range, by regular descent and extension rather than orbit separation alone. [given, F1, F3, algebra]

2.1 For $0\le t<m+1$, add $m+1-t$ extra vector slots and view $f$ as a polynomial independent of them. It is still invariant for the diagonal action on the enlarged tuple. By step 1.1 it is a polynomial in all enlarged Gram coordinates. Set the extra vectors equal to zero. Every Gram coordinate using an added slot vanishes, and the remaining ones are the original $q_{ij}$, proving generation for the original $t$. This includes $t=0$, when the result is the scalar ring. [step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 A polynomial in formal Gram coordinates pulls back to zero exactly when it vanishes on all Gram matrices: surjectivity onto the rank-bounded set is proved in [[lem-gram-map-fibers-and-rank-strata]]. By [F1] that set has the displayed prime minor or Pfaffian ideal. The strong Nullstellensatz in [F4] therefore makes its vanishing ideal that very ideal, since a prime ideal is radical. Thus the kernel is precisely the asserted ideal. When the rank bound exceeds the matrix size this is the zero ideal, as [F1] states. The descent in step 1.1 used the algebraic-group invariance equation, so the conclusion has exactly the coaction convention in the Statement. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
