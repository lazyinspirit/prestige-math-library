## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

Let $k$ be algebraically closed of characteristic zero, and let $V$ be as in [[lem-brauer-contraction-functor-and-bending]]: dimension $m\ge1$ with a nondegenerate symmetric form and $G=O(V)$, or dimension $2n\ge2$ with a nondegenerate alternating form and $G=Sp(V)$. Use the full-group contraction functor from that item. For all nonnegative integers $a,b$, its image is $\operatorname{Hom}_G(V^{\otimes a},V^{\otimes b})$. Its kernel is the subspace obtained by bending the multilinear determinant relations of size $m+1$ in the orthogonal case, or the multilinear Pfaffian relations of size $2n+2$ in the symplectic case. In particular, the endomorphism map $B_r(\epsilon m)\to\operatorname{End}_G(V^{\otimes r})$ is surjective for every $r\ge0$ and is injective exactly when $r\le m$ for $O(V)$ or $r\le n$ for $Sp(V)$. The kernel in each fixed algebra is the span of all bent relations in that rank. The tensor ideal generated by these relations means the family of subspaces in all morphism spaces closed under composition on either side and tensoring by arbitrary diagrams. No reverse-commutant theorem is asserted here.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice and the full-group multilinear FFT/SFT and the Brauer contraction/bending maps.

[F1] [[thm-orthogonal-and-symplectic-multilinear-fft-sft]]: multilinear contractions span, with the exact determinant/Pfaffian relation spans and stable ranges.

[F2] [[lem-brauer-contraction-functor-and-bending]]: Brauer diagrams act by invariant contractions, and bending identifies intertwiners with invariant tensors.

## Proof

1.1 Put $t=a+b$. The snake identities in [F2] bend all input factors into output factors, giving inverse isomorphisms on diagram morphism spaces and on equivariant linear-map spaces. Identify invariant tensors with invariant multilinear forms by the nondegenerate form on each factor. Each bent basis diagram is then its matching contraction times a sign in $\{1,-1\}$, because crossings act by $\epsilon$ times the flip and caps by the form. The signs are units and the matching assignment is a bijection: bending changes boundary labels and introduces no closed component. Thus the linear map from the free matching space to the invariant forms is exactly the map of [F1], after invertible diagonal sign and boundary-order changes. [given, F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 If $t$ is odd the diagram space is zero, and [F1] makes the invariant target zero as well. If $t$ is even, [F1] says that every invariant form is a sum of matching contractions. Bend their diagrams back using step 1.1 to obtain a preimage of every intertwiner. A linear combination maps to zero exactly when its bent contraction combination belongs to the determinant/Pfaffian relation span in [F1]. Undoing the same bend gives exactly the kernel asserted in the Statement. This holds for all $a,b$, including $a=0$ or $b=0$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 At $a=b=r$, the map is an algebra map because [F2] is a functor. The first isomorphism theorem gives $B_r(\epsilon m)/\ker\rho_r\cong\operatorname{End}_G(V^{\otimes r})$. Injectivity is equivalent, by the invertible bends in step 1.1, to independence of the contractions on $2r$ labels. By [F1] this holds exactly for $r\le m$ in the orthogonal branch and $r\le n$ in the symplectic branch. The empty case $r=0$ is the scalar identity. The kernels in all boundary types form a tensor ideal since the functor sends a composite or tensor product containing a zero image to zero. Within each fixed algebra the described span is therefore automatically a two-sided ideal. [F1, F2, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
