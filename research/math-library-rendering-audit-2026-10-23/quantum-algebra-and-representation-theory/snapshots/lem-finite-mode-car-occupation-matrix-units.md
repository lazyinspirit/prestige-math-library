## Statement

For $n\ge0$, let $\mathcal P_n$ be the set of subsets of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$, and let $\mathcal F_n=\ell^2(\mathcal P_n)$ with orthonormal basis $e_S$ for $S\in\mathcal P_n$. For $1\le j\le n$ define operators on this finite-dimensional Hilbert space by

$$ c_j e_S=\begin{cases}0&j\notin S,\\(-1)^{|\{s\in S:s<j\}|}e_{S\setminus\{j\}}&j\in S,\end{cases}\qquad c_j^\dagger e_S=\begin{cases}(-1)^{|\{s\in S:s<j\}|}e_{S\cup\{j\}}&j\notin S,\\0&j\in S.\end{cases} $$

Then $c_j^\dagger$ is the Hilbert-space adjoint of $c_j$, and $a_j\mapsto c_j$, $a_j^\dagger\mapsto c_j^\dagger$ defines a $*$-representation $\pi_n:\mathsf{CAR}^{\mathrm{alg}}_n\to\operatorname{End}(\mathcal F_n)$. Put $N_j=c_j^\dagger c_j$ and $P_\varnothing=\prod_{j=1}^n(I-N_j)$, with the empty product equal to $I$. For $S=\{i_1<\cdots<i_p\}$ and $T=\{j_1<\cdots<j_q\}$, set

$$ E_{S,T}=c_{i_1}^\dagger\cdots c_{i_p}^\dagger P_\varnothing c_{j_q}\cdots c_{j_1}, $$

with empty operator products equal to $I$. Then $E_{S,T}e_U=\delta_{T,U}e_S$. Consequently the image of $\pi_n$ is all of $\operatorname{End}(\mathcal F_n)\cong M_{2^n}(\mathbb C)$.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** An integer $n\ge0$, the finite-mode algebra $\mathsf{CAR}^{\mathrm{alg}}_n$, and the finite set $\mathcal P_n$.

[F1] The algebra $\mathsf{CAR}^{\mathrm{alg}}_n$ is generated by $a_j,a_j^\dagger$ subject to $\{a_i,a_j\}=0$, $\{a_i^\dagger,a_j^\dagger\}=0$, and $\{a_i,a_j^\dagger\}=\delta_{ij}1$ ([[def-finite-mode-car-algebra]]).

## Proof

1.1 On basis vectors, $c_j^\dagger$ reverses exactly the same basis transition as $c_j$ and has the same real sign. Hence its matrix is the transpose-conjugate matrix of $c_j$. Each of $c_j^2$ and $(c_j^\dagger)^2$ vanishes because after one allowed toggle, the second toggle of the same type is forbidden. On a basis vector containing $j$, $c_j^\dagger c_j$ is the identity and $c_jc_j^\dagger$ is zero; when $j$ is absent these roles reverse. Thus $\{c_j,c_j^\dagger\}=I$. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Fix $j<k$. Whether the $j$-toggle is allowed depends only on membership of $j$, and whether the $k$-toggle is allowed depends only on membership of $k$. Therefore, if either toggle is forbidden, both orders of the two distinct toggles give zero. If both are allowed, performing the $j$-toggle first changes the number of occupied indices below $k$ by one, while the $k$-toggle does not change the number below $j$. The signs in the displayed formulas therefore differ by $-1$ when the order is reversed. This proves every distinct-mode anticommutator among $c_j,c_j^\dagger,c_k,c_k^\dagger$ is zero. Along with step 1.1 these are exactly [F1], so the specified map extends through the quotient to a $*$-representation $\pi_n$. [given, F1, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 The operator $N_j$ is diagonal in the basis $e_S$, with eigenvalue $1$ when $j\in S$ and $0$ otherwise. Hence the commuting product $P_\varnothing$ is the projection onto $\mathbb C e_\varnothing$. For $T=\{j_1<\cdots<j_q\}$, the right-hand product $c_{j_q}\cdots c_{j_1}$ removes the elements of $T$ in increasing order; on $e_T$ each sign is $+1$, and on $e_U$ with $T\subsetneq U$ its result is a scalar multiple of $e_{U\setminus T}$. If $T\not\subseteq U$ it gives zero. The middle projection therefore kills every $e_U$ except $e_T$, and sends $e_T$ to $e_\varnothing$. The left-hand creation product sends $e_\varnothing$ to $e_S$, again with sign $+1$ because its indices are applied from largest to smallest. Thus $E_{S,T}e_U=\delta_{T,U}e_S$. [given, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 The operators $E_{S,T}$ are all standard matrix units relative to the basis $(e_S)_{S\in\mathcal P_n}$, so their linear span is $\operatorname{End}(\mathcal F_n)$. Each lies in the $*$-algebra generated by the $c_j,c_j^\dagger$, proving surjectivity. For $n=0$, the sole basis vector is $e_\varnothing$, $P_\varnothing=I$, and $E_{\varnothing,\varnothing}=I$, so the same conclusion holds. [step 2.2, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The construction uses only the finite occupation basis. It does not import an infinite-particle-domain argument or a choice principle.
