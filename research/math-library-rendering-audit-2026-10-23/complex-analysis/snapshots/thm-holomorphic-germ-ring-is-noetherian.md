## Statement

For every integer $m\ge1$, the holomorphic germ ring $\mathcal O_{m,0}$ is a
Noetherian commutative ring.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A fixed dimension $m\ge1$.

[L1] A commutative ring is Noetherian exactly when every ideal is finitely generated ([[def-noetherian-ring-and-module]]).

[L2] A finite module over a Noetherian ring is Noetherian ([[lem-finite-modules-over-noetherian-rings-are-noetherian]]).

[L3] Quotienting by a Weierstrass polynomial yields a finite module over the smaller germ ring ([[lem-weierstrass-quotient-is-a-finite-module]]).

[L4] A nonzero germ becomes regular after a linear coordinate change, and a regular germ admits Weierstrass preparation ([[lem-generic-linear-coordinate-makes-a-holomorphic-germ-regular]], [[thm-weierstrass-preparation-theorem]]).

[L5] One-variable holomorphic functions factor by their zero order, and units in the germ ring are exactly the nonvanishing germs ([[thm-zero-order-factorization-holomorphic-function]], [[prop-units-in-the-holomorphic-germ-ring]]).

[L6] Weierstrass division gives a quotient and remainder modulo the prepared polynomial ([[thm-weierstrass-division-theorem]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The proof is by induction on $m$. For $m=1$, let $I\trianglelefteq \mathcal O_{1,0}$ be a nonzero proper ideal. Choose $f\in I$ of minimal zero order $d$. By [L5], $f=z_1^d u$ with $u$ a unit. If $g\in I$, then $\operatorname{ord}_0(g)\ge d$, so again by [L5] one has $g=z_1^d h$ for some holomorphic germ $h$. Since $u$ is a unit, $z_1^d=u^{-1}f\in I$, so $g=h z_1^d\in(f)$. Thus every ideal is principal, hence finitely generated. The zero ideal and whole ring are generated by $0$ and $1$. Therefore [L1] makes $\mathcal O_{1,0}$ Noetherian. [L1, L5]

2.1 Assume $m>1$ and that $\mathcal O_{m-1,0}$ is Noetherian. Let $I\trianglelefteq\mathcal O_{m,0}$ be a proper nonzero ideal. Choose nonzero $f\in I$. By [L4], after a complex-linear coordinate change we may assume that $f$ is regular in $z_m$; this replaces $I$ by an isomorphic ideal under a ring automorphism, so finite generation is unaffected. By [L4] and [L5], write $f=uW$ with $u$ a unit and $W$ a Weierstrass polynomial. Since $I$ is an ideal and $u^{-1}$ exists, $W=u^{-1}f$ also lies in $I$. [step 1.1, L4, L5]

3.1 Let $\pi:\mathcal O_{m,0}\to\mathcal O_{m,0}/(W)$ be the quotient map. By [L3], the quotient is a finite $\mathcal O_{m-1,0}$-module, so [L2] and the induction hypothesis make it a Noetherian $\mathcal O_{m-1,0}$-module. Hence the submodule $\pi(I)$ is generated by finitely many classes $\pi(g_1),\dots,\pi(g_s)$ with $g_i\in I$. [step 2.1, L2, L3, choose]

4.1 Let $g\in I$. Since $\pi(g)\in\pi(I)$, there are $a_1,\dots,a_s\in\mathcal O_{m-1,0}$ with $$\pi(g)=a_1\pi(g_1)+\cdots+a_s\pi(g_s).$$ Thus $$g-(a_1g_1+\cdots+a_sg_s)\in\ker\pi=(W)\subseteq I,$$ so $g$ lies in the ideal generated by $W,g_1,\dots,g_s$. Therefore $I$ is finitely generated. By [L1], $\mathcal O_{m,0}$ is Noetherian. [step 3.1, L1, L6, algebra] ∎
