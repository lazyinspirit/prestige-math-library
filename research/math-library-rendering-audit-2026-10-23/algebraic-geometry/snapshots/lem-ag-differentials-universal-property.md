## Statement

Let $A\to B$ be a ring homomorphism of commutative rings and let
$\Omega_{B/A}$ with its universal derivation $\mathrm{d}$ be as in
[[def-ag-universal-algebraic-differentials]]. For every $B$-module $M$ the
assignment $g\mapsto g\circ\mathrm{d}$ is a $B$-module isomorphism

$$\operatorname{Hom}_B(\Omega_{B/A},M)\;\cong\;\operatorname{Der}_A(B,M),$$

natural in $M$: for every $B$-linear $h\colon M\to N$ the diagram of the two
assignment maps commutes, and the isomorphism is additive in $g$ and $D$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A ring homomorphism $A\to B$ of commutative rings, the presented
module $\Omega_{B/A}=F/R$ with universal derivation $\mathrm{d}$, and a
$B$-module $M$.

[F1] [[def-ag-universal-algebraic-differentials]]: $\Omega_{B/A}=F/R$, where $F$ is the free $B$-module on the basis symbols $[b]$, $b\in B$, and $R$ is generated by $[b+b']-[b]-[b']$, $[bb']-b[b']-b'[b]$ and $[\varphi(a)]$; $\mathrm{d}b=[b]+R$; an $A$-derivation into a $B$-module $M$ is a map $D$ that is additive, $A$-constant and satisfies the Leibniz rule.

## Proof

1.1 Every $g\in\operatorname{Hom}_B(\Omega_{B/A},M)$ gives $g\circ\mathrm{d}\in\operatorname{Der}_A(B,M)$: additivity, $A$-constancy and the Leibniz rule for $g\circ\mathrm{d}$ are those of $\mathrm{d}$ transported by the additive map $g$, and $\mathrm{d}$ has them by construction. The assignment is additive and $B$-linear: $(g_1+g_2)\circ\mathrm{d}=g_1\circ\mathrm{d}+g_2\circ\mathrm{d}$ and $(bg)\circ\mathrm{d}=b\,(g\circ\mathrm{d})$ pointwise. It is injective, because the classes $\mathrm{d}b$ generate $\Omega_{B/A}=F/R$ as a $B$-module, so $g\circ\mathrm{d}=0$ forces $g=0$. [F1, given]

2.1 Conversely let $D\in\operatorname{Der}_A(B,M)$. Since $F$ is free on the $[b]$, there is a unique $B$-linear $\widetilde{D}\colon F\to M$ with $\widetilde{D}([b])=D(b)$. Each relator is killed: $\widetilde{D}([b+b']-[b]-[b'])=D(b+b')-D(b)-D(b')=0$ by additivity of $D$, similarly the Leibniz relator, and $[\varphi(a)]\mapsto D(\varphi(a))=0$ by $A$-constancy. Hence $\widetilde{D}$ factors through $F/R=\Omega_{B/A}$, giving a $B$-linear $g_D\colon\Omega_{B/A}\to M$ with $g_D(\mathrm{d}b)=D(b)$, that is, $g_D\circ\mathrm{d}=D$. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The two assignments are mutually inverse: the derivation attached to $g_D$ is $g_D\circ\mathrm{d}=D$, and the homomorphism attached to $g\circ\mathrm{d}$ agrees with $g$ on every generator $\mathrm{d}b$, hence equals $g$ because those generators span. Each assignment is additive, so the bijection is a $B$-module isomorphism; and for $B$-linear $h\colon M\to N$ one has $h\circ(g\circ\mathrm{d})=(h\circ g)\circ\mathrm{d}$, which is exactly naturality in $M$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
