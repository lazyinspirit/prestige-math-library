## Statement refuted

False claim: for every ring map $B\to C$ over a base ring $A$ the canonical map
$C\otimes_B\Omega_{B/A}\to\Omega_{C/A}$ of
([[lem-ag-differentials-localization-base-change]]) is an isomorphism. With
$A=k$ a field, $B=k[x]$ and $C=k$ the map $x\mapsto0$, the source is the
one-dimensional $k$-vector space $k\otimes_{k[x]}\Omega_{k[x]/k}\cong k\cdot
\mathrm dx$, while the target is $\Omega_{k/k}=0$; the canonical map is the zero
map, so it is neither injective nor an isomorphism.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$, the $k$-algebras $B=k[x]$ and $C=k$ with the $k$-algebra map $\varepsilon\colon k[x]\to k$ sending $x$ to $0$.

[F1] [[def-ag-universal-algebraic-differentials]]: for a ring map $A\to B$, $\Omega_{B/A}$ is the $B$-module generated by the symbols $\mathrm db$ subject to additivity, the Leibniz rule, and $\mathrm da=0$ for $a$ in the image of $A$; when $A=B$ the base elements are all elements of the ring, so all generators vanish.

[F2] [[lem-ag-polynomial-quotient-differentials]]: for $A=k$ and $P=k[x]$ the module $\Omega_{P/k}$ is free with basis $\mathrm dx$, so $\Omega_{k[x]/k}\cong k[x]$ as a $k[x]$-module and $\mathrm dx\ne0$.

[F3] [[lem-ag-differentials-localization-base-change]]: for an arbitrary $A$-algebra map $B\to C$ there is a canonical $C$-linear map $C\otimes_B\Omega_{B/A}\to\Omega_{C/A}$, and for a general algebra map it is neither asserted injective nor asserted an isomorphism.

[F4] [[thm-right-exactness-of-tensor-products]]: tensoring is right exact and $(B/I)\otimes_BC\cong C/IC$; in particular $k\otimes_{k[x]}k[x]\cong k$ for the quotient $k=k[x]/(x)$.

## Counterexample

1.1 The source. By [F2] the module $\Omega_{k[x]/k}=k[x]\cdot\mathrm dx$ is free of rank one, so $k\otimes_{k[x]}\Omega_{k[x]/k}\cong k\otimes_{k[x]}k[x]\cong k$ by [F4], a one-dimensional $k$-vector space with basis $1\otimes\mathrm dx$; in particular $1\otimes\mathrm dx\ne0$. [F2, F4, algebra]

2.1 The target and the map. The map $\varepsilon$ exhibits $k$ as a $k$-algebra over itself, so every element of $k$ lies in the image of the base ring and [F1] gives $\Omega_{k/k}=0$. The canonical map of [F3] sends $1\otimes\mathrm dx$ to $\mathrm d\varepsilon(x)=\mathrm d0=0$, hence is the zero map from a one-dimensional space to the zero space: not injective, and not an isomorphism. The hypothesis of a general algebra map in the base-change statement is therefore essential. [F1, F3, step 1.1] ∎
