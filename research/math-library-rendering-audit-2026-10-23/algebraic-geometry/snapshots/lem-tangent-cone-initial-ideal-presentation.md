## Statement

Let $k$ be any field, $n\in\mathbb N$, $P=k[t_1,\ldots,t_n]$, and
$\mathfrak q=(t_1,\ldots,t_n)$. Let $I\subseteq\mathfrak q$,
$A=P/I$, $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$, and let $x$ be the rational origin, so
$\mathfrak m=\mathfrak q/I$ and $A/\mathfrak m=k$. For a nonzero polynomial
$f=\sum_d f_d$ decomposed into total-degree homogeneous parts, let $f_{\min}$
be its lowest nonzero homogeneous part, and let
$\operatorname{in}_{\mathfrak q}(I)$ be the ideal generated by all
$f_{\min}$ with $0\ne f\in I$. The canonical graded $k$-algebra map
$$P/\operatorname{in}_{\mathfrak q}(I)\longrightarrow\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m_x}\mathcal O_{X,x}$$
sending each variable to its degree-one initial class, is an isomorphism.
Consequently
$$\operatorname{Cone}_x(X)\cong\operatorname{Spec}\bigl(P/\operatorname{in}_{\mathfrak q}(I)\bigr).$$
If $I=(f)$ with $f\ne0$, then
$\operatorname{in}_{\mathfrak q}(I)=(f_{\min})$. The initial forms of a
chosen generating set for $I$ need not generate
$\operatorname{in}_{\mathfrak q}(I)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$, a finite $n\in\mathbb N$, and an ideal $I\subseteq\mathfrak q=(t_1,\ldots,t_n)$ of $P=k[t_1,\ldots,t_n]$.

[F1] [[def-tangent-cone-point]]: for a locally Noetherian scheme and a point $x$, the scheme-theoretic tangent cone is $\operatorname{Spec}(\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m_x}\mathcal O_{X,x})$ over $\kappa(x)$.

[F2] [[lem-cotangent-localization-at-rational-point]]: when $A/\mathfrak m=k$, localization induces an isomorphism $\mathfrak m^r/\mathfrak m^{r+1}\to(\mathfrak m A_{\mathfrak m})^r/(\mathfrak m A_{\mathfrak m})^{r+1}$ for every $r\in\mathbb N$.

[F3] [[def-associated-graded-ring-and-module]]: $\operatorname{gr}_J(R)=\bigoplus_{r\ge0}J^r/J^{r+1}$, with multiplication induced by multiplication in $R$.

[F4] [[lem-finite-variable-polynomial-algebras-over-fields-are-noetherian-direct]]: for a field $k$ and finite $n$, every ideal of $P=k[t_1,\ldots,t_n]$ has a finite generating list.

[F5] [[def-noetherian-ring]]: a ring is left Noetherian when its left regular module is Noetherian.

[F6] [[def-noetherian-module]]: every submodule of a Noetherian module is finitely generated.

[F7] [[def-left-right-and-two-sided-ideal]]: an additive subgroup of the left regular module is a left ideal exactly when it is closed under left multiplication; in a commutative ring the left, right and two-sided ideals agree.

[F8] [[def-submodule]]: a subset of a module is a submodule when it is an additive subgroup closed under scalar multiplication.

[F9] [[def-sum-and-product-of-ideals]]: products of ideals consist of finite sums of products of their elements.

[F10] [[def-quotient-ring]]: $P/I$ consists of additive cosets with $(a+I)(b+I)=ab+I$.

[F11] [[prop-canonical-quotient-ring-map]]: the canonical projection $P\to P/I$ is a surjective ring homomorphism with kernel $I$.

[F12] [[thm-quotient-is-field-iff-ideal-maximal]]: $R/M$ is a field if and only if $M$ is a maximal ideal.

[F13] [[cor-maximal-ideals-are-prime]]: every maximal ideal of a commutative ring is prime.

[F14] [[thm-stalk-structure-sheaf-prime-localization]]: for $\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}R$, $\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Spec}R,\mathfrak p}\cong R_{\mathfrak p}$.

[F15] [[thm-localisation-at-a-prime-is-local]]: $R_{\mathfrak p}$ is a nonzero local ring with unique maximal ideal $\mathfrak pR_{\mathfrak p}$.

[F16] [[def-monomials-multidegree-and-total-degree]]: each polynomial in finitely many variables has a unique finite monomial expansion, and each monomial has its total degree.

[F17] [[def-homogeneous-polynomial-and-homogeneous-ideal]]: a polynomial is homogeneous of degree $d$ when every occurring monomial has total degree $d$.

[F18] [[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]: the ideal generated by a subset is the smallest ideal containing that subset; the ideal generated by $f$ is written $(f)$.

[F19] [[thm-generated-ideal-description-in-a-commutative-ring]]: in a commutative ring, an ideal generated by a set consists of finite sums of ring multiples of its generators; in particular, elements of $(f)$ are of the form $gf$.

[F20] [[lem-field-is-a-commutative-ring]]: every field is an integral domain.

[F21] [[cor-multivariate-polynomial-ring-over-a-domain-is-a-domain]]: a polynomial ring in finitely many variables over an integral domain is an integral domain.

[F22] [[def-locally-noetherian-and-noetherian-scheme]]: a scheme is locally Noetherian if it has an affine open cover by spectra of Noetherian rings.

[F23] [[def-left-and-right-modules]]: the left regular module has scalar action $r\cdot a=ra$, given by ring multiplication.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Noetherian affine scheme. The finite-generation theorem [F4] says every ideal of $P$ is finitely generated. The left regular modules ${}_P P$ and ${}_A A$ have scalar action by multiplication [F23]; hence [F7] and [F8] identify their submodules with left ideals, which are ideals because both rings are commutative. Thus every submodule of ${}_P P$ is finitely generated; [F6] makes this module Noetherian and [F5] makes $P$ a Noetherian ring. Put $A=P/I$ and let $\pi:P\to A$ be the quotient map [F10, F11]. For an ideal $K\subseteq A$, its preimage $J=\pi^{-1}(K)$ is an ideal of $P$: additivity and closure under multiplication follow by applying the homomorphism $\pi$ and the ideal property of $K$. Since $P$ is Noetherian, [F7, F8] identify $J$ with a submodule of ${}_P P$, and [F6] gives a finite generating list $p_1,\ldots,p_s$ for $J$. Every $a\in K$ has a representative $p\in P$ by [F11], and then $p\in J$; writing $p=\sum_i c_i p_i$ shows $a=\sum_i\pi(c_i)\pi(p_i)$. Each $\pi(p_i)$ lies in $K$, so they generate $K$. Thus every ideal of $A$ is finitely generated. By [F7, F8, F23], every submodule of ${}_A A$ is an ideal; it too is finitely generated, so [F6] and [F5] show that $A$ is Noetherian. The affine cover $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$ itself makes $X$ locally Noetherian by [F22]. Each argument fixes one ideal or submodule and establishes existence of a finite generating list for it; no simultaneous family of lists is chosen, and no choice principle is used. [F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F10, F11, F22, F23, given, algebra]

1.2 The polynomial associated graded ring. Let $P_d$ be the homogeneous polynomials of total degree $d$. By [F16, F17], every polynomial is a finite sum of homogeneous parts. For every $r\ge0$, $\mathfrak q^r=\bigoplus_{d\ge r}P_d$: a product of $r$ elements of $\mathfrak q$ has no term of degree below $r$, while every monomial of degree at least $r$ factors as $r$ variables times a monomial; the finite-sum description of ideal products [F9] gives the two inclusions. Thus the degree-$d$ map $P_d\to\mathfrak q^d/\mathfrak q^{d+1}$ is an isomorphism. Since multiplication in the associated graded ring is induced from $P$ [F3], these maps identify $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak q}P$ with $P$ as graded rings. In degree zero this is the identification $P_0=k$. [F3, F9, F16, F17, given, algebra]

1.3 The principal case. Suppose $I=(f)$ for $0\ne f\in P$. By [F20, F21], $P$ is a domain. If $0\ne h\in I$, [F19] writes $h=gf$; since $h\ne0$, $g\ne0$. Let $d$ and $e$ be the lowest degrees of $f$ and $g$. The lowest-degree part of $gf$ is $g_{\min}f_{\min}$: all other products of homogeneous parts have degree greater than $d+e$, and this product is nonzero in the domain $P$. Thus every lowest form of a nonzero element of $I$ lies in $(f_{\min})$, while $f_{\min}$ is itself one of those forms. By [F18], $\operatorname{in}_{\mathfrak q}(I)=(f_{\min})$. [F18, F19, F20, F21, given, algebra]

2.1 The rational stalk. Evaluation at the origin sends every $t_i$ to zero and each constant to itself. By the unique monomial expansion [F16], its kernel in $P$ is $\mathfrak q$. As $I\subseteq\mathfrak q$, evaluation factors through $A$ with kernel $\mathfrak m=\mathfrak q/I$ and quotient $A/\mathfrak m\cong k$; [F12] makes $\mathfrak m$ maximal, and [F13] makes it prime. The stalk formula [F14] gives $\mathcal O_{X,x}\cong A_{\mathfrak m}$, whose unique maximal ideal is $\mathfrak n=\mathfrak m A_{\mathfrak m}$ by [F15]. By [F1] and step 1.1, $\operatorname{Cone}_x(X)=\operatorname{Spec}(\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak n}A_{\mathfrak m})$. [F1, F10, F11, F12, F13, F14, F15, F16, step 1.1, given, algebra]

2.2 Quotient filtration and its kernel. The quotient operations [F10, F11] give $\mathfrak m^r=(\mathfrak q^r+I)/I$ for every $r$, by induction on $r$. Projection therefore defines a graded ring map $\phi:\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak q}P\to\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m}A$, which is surjective in each degree because every class in $\mathfrak m^r/\mathfrak m^{r+1}$ is represented by an element of $\mathfrak q^r$. Use step 1.2 to identify its source with $P$. For a homogeneous $h\in P_d$, its class lies in the degree-$d$ kernel exactly when $h\in I+\mathfrak q^{d+1}$. Write $h=g+u$ with $g\in I$ and $u\in\mathfrak q^{d+1}$. If $h\ne0$, then $g=h-u$ has no terms below degree $d$ and its degree-$d$ part is $h$, so $g_{\min}=h$. Conversely, if $0\ne g\in I$ has lowest part $g_{\min}$ of degree $d$, then $g-g_{\min}\in\mathfrak q^{d+1}$, so $g_{\min}$ lies in the degree-$d$ kernel. The kernel is a homogeneous ideal; it contains every lowest form, and each of its homogeneous elements is itself a lowest form. It is therefore exactly $\operatorname{in}_{\mathfrak q}(I)$, including when $I=0$. [F3, F9, F10, F11, F16, F17, F18, step 1.2, given, algebra]

3.1 The local tangent cone. The localization maps of [F2] give an isomorphism in every degree from $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m}A$ to $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak n}A_{\mathfrak m}$. They preserve multiplication because each degree map is induced by the ring map $A\to A_{\mathfrak m}$ and the products in both associated graded rings are induced by ring multiplication [F3]. Thus step 2.2 yields the canonical graded $k$-algebra isomorphism $P/\operatorname{in}_{\mathfrak q}(I)\cong\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m_x}\mathcal O_{X,x}$, sending each variable to its degree-one initial class. By [F1] and step 2.1, its spectrum is the scheme-theoretic tangent cone at $x$. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, step 2.2]

4.1 A generating list need not suffice, and boundary cases. For $P=k[X,Y,Z]$, set $f_1=XY$, $f_2=XZ+Z(Y^2-Z^2)$, and $I=(f_1,f_2)\subseteq(X,Y,Z)$, so the origin belongs to $X=\operatorname{Spec}(P/I)$. The initial forms of this generating list are $XY$ and $XZ$, which generate an ideal contained in $(X)$. But $h=YZ(Y^2-Z^2)=Yf_2-Zf_1$ is a nonzero homogeneous element of $I$, so it is its own lowest form and belongs to $\operatorname{in}_{(X,Y,Z)}(I)$. Since $(XY,XZ)\subseteq(X)$ while $h\notin(X)$ (its image modulo $(X)$ is the same nonzero polynomial), the two displayed initial forms do not generate the initial ideal. This witness works in every characteristic because the distinct monomials $Y^3Z$ and $YZ^3$ cannot cancel. For $n=0$, $\mathfrak q=0$ forces $I=0$ and $P=A=k$, so the graded ring is $k$ in degree zero and the initial ideal is zero; the nonzero principal case is excluded. For $n=1$, each $\mathfrak q^r/\mathfrak q^{r+1}$ is generated by $t_1^r$, consistent with steps 1.2 and 2.2. Degree zero has $P_0=k$ and $I\subseteq\mathfrak q$ contains no nonzero constant; degree one is covered by the same kernel calculation with $\mathfrak q^2$. If $I=0$, the set of nonzero elements of $I$ is empty and its generated initial ideal is zero. The condition $I\subseteq\mathfrak q$ implies $I\ne P$, so the origin exists and $X$ is not empty. The lemma asserts no iff, so there are no converse directions to check. [F9, F16, F17, F18, F19, step 1.2, step 2.2, given, algebra] ∎
