## Statement refuted

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). The claim refuted: on the
real Neron-Severi space $\operatorname{N}^1_{\mathbb R}(X)$ of an integral
smooth projective surface $X$ over a field $k$
([[def-numerical-equivalence-and-neron-severi-space]]), the intersection form
is negative semidefinite, or negative definite, on the whole space, so that
every divisor class has nonpositive self-intersection.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** a field $k$, an integral smooth projective surface $X$ over $k$, an ample invertible sheaf $H$ on $X$, and the real Neron-Severi space $\operatorname{N}^1_{\mathbb R}(X)$ with its intersection form.

[F1] Ample classes on a projective surface exist: the H-projective structure of $X$ gives a closed immersion $i:X\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^n_k$ with $\mathcal O_X(1)=i^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^n}(1)$ closed H-very ample relative to $\operatorname{Spec}k$ ([[def-projective-morphism-pre-proj]], [[def-very-ample-invertible-sheaf-relative]]); such a class is H-very ample and ample ([[lem-very-ample-implies-ample]], [[def-ample-invertible-sheaf]]).

[F2] Positivity: for every ample $H$ one has $H\cdot H>0$ ([[lem-ample-divisor-positive-intersection-on-smooth-projective-surface]], case (2)); the intersection product is symmetric and $\mathbb Z$-bilinear ([[thm-surface-intersection-product-bilinear-and-symmetric]]).

[F3] Numerical equivalence: a class $[M]\in\operatorname{N}^1_{\mathbb R}(X)$ is zero exactly when $M$ is numerically trivial, i.e. $M\cdot N=0$ for every invertible $\mathcal N$; thus $[H]\ne0$ whenever $H\cdot H>0$ ([[def-numerical-equivalence-and-neron-severi-space]], [[def-invertible-sheaf]]).

[F4] A real form is negative semidefinite when $q(v)\le0$ for all $v$, and negative definite when $q(v)<0$ for all $v\ne0$; a single nonzero class with positive square refutes both properties ([[def-numerical-equivalence-and-neron-severi-space]] for the self-intersection form).

[F5] The Axiom of Choice is inherited from the ample-positivity and embedding suppliers of [F1]–[F2]; the sheaf $H$ is a single given object.



## Counterexample
**Given:** a field $k$, an integral smooth projective surface $X$ over $k$, and an ample invertible sheaf $H$ (for $X=\mathbb P^2_k$ take $H=\mathcal O(1)$, which is closed H-very ample via the identity embedding).

1.1 An ample class has positive square. By [F2] the self-intersection of the class $[H]$ is $[H]\cdot[H]=H\cdot H>0$; by [F3] the class $[H]$ is nonzero, since an ample class with $H\cdot H>0$ is not numerically trivial. Therefore the intersection form is neither negative semidefinite nor negative definite on $\operatorname{N}^1_{\mathbb R}(X)$: the vector $[H]\ne0$ has positive square, contradicting both definiteness conditions [F4]. [F2, F3, F4]

2.1 The plane as explicit witness. For $X=\mathbb P^2_k$ the twisting sheaf $\mathcal O(1)$ is closed H-very ample relative to $\operatorname{Spec}k$ via the identity closed immersion $X\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^2_k$ and hence ample [F1]; by step 1.1 its class has positive self-intersection, so on $\operatorname{N}^1_{\mathbb R}(\mathbb P^2_k)$ the form fails to be negative (semi)definite. More generally the computation shows that for any integral smooth projective surface the positive line $\mathbb R[H]$ generated by an ample class is a positive-definite one-dimensional subspace, so the Hodge index theorem [[thm-hodge-index-theorem-for-smooth-projective-surfaces]] needs the hypothesis $H\cdot H>0$ together with the restriction to the primitive part $H^{\perp}$ and cannot be strengthened to a statement about the whole space. [F1, F5, step 1.1] ∎
