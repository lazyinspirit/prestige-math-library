## Statement

For every field $k$, $X(D_k(M))=M$ via $m\mapsto e_m$, and $\operatorname{Hom}(D_k(M),D_k(N))=\operatorname{Hom}(N,M)$ naturally. Moreover $k[M]$ is finitely generated as an algebra if and only if $M$ is finitely generated. For $M\cong\mathbb Z^r\oplus\bigoplus_i\mathbb Z/n_i\mathbb Z$, one has $D_k(M)\cong\mathbf G_m^r\times\prod_i\mu_{n_i}$, where $\mu_n=\operatorname{Spec}k[t]/(t^n-1)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] The monomial basis, characters, and Hopf correspondence are in [[def-diagonalizable-group-and-character-module]].

## Proof

**Given:** Abelian groups $M,N$ and a field $k$.

1.1 If $a=\sum_m a_m e_m$ is group-like, coefficient comparison in $\Delta(a)=a\otimes a$ gives $a_m a_n=0$ when $m\ne n$ and $a_m=a_m^2$. Over a field at most one coefficient is nonzero; the counit makes exactly one coefficient nonzero, and it is $1$. Thus the group-like elements are precisely $e_m$. A Hopf map $k[N]\to k[M]$ must send $e_n$ to $e_{f(n)}$, with $f(n+n')=f(n)+f(n')$. Every such $f$ gives a Hopf map, proving both natural identifications. [F1, algebra]

2.1 Finite generators of $M$, together with their negatives, give finite algebra generators of $k[M]$. Conversely, take the finite union $T$ of the supports of finite algebra generators. Every product and sum has support in the submonoid generated by $T$, so all $e_m$ can occur only if that monoid is $M$; in particular $T$ generates $M$ as a group. Direct sums become tensor products of group algebras. The algebras for $\mathbb Z$ and $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$ are respectively $k[t,t^{-1}]$ and $k[t]/(t^n-1)$, which proves the product formula. [F1, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
