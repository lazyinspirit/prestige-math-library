## Statement

Assume AC ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

Let $(\mathcal I,E,\mu)$ be a marked ideal of maximal order whose support $\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal I,E,\mu)$ has a component of codimension one in the smooth $K$-scheme $X$ ([[def-maximal-order-and-tangent-directions]]).
Every codimension-one component $C$ of the support is regular and isolated from its other components. The labelled Cartier blowup at $C$ has $y^{-\mu}\sigma^*\mathcal I=\mathcal O_{X'}$ near its exceptional divisor. Whenever $C$ has SNC with $E$, this division is the controlled transform of an admissible multiple test blowup, and its support does not meet the exceptional divisor. Without that SNC hypothesis only the stated ideal-division calculation is asserted.
More precisely, at a general point of $C$ one has $\mathcal I=(u^{\mu})$ for a local equation $u$ of $C$, and if $\sigma\colon X'\to X$ is the blowup of $C$ with exceptional divisor $D$ given by $y$, then, on a neighbourhood of the inverse image of $C$, $\sigma^*\mathcal I=y^{\mu}\mathcal O_{X'}$ and hence the divided ideal is the unit ideal. No unit-ideal conclusion is asserted away from that neighbourhood.
Blowing up a codimension-one regular center is an isomorphism, but it is a nontrivial transformation of the marked ideal because its pulled-back ideal is divided by the $\mu$-th power of the exceptional ideal while the marking $\mu$ is unchanged.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Assume AC. Let $(\mathcal I,E,\mu)$ be a marked ideal of maximal order whose support has a codimension-one component $C$, and let $\sigma\colon X'\to X$ be the blowup of $C$.

[A1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: AC is used through the regular-local UFD supplier [F1].

[F1] [[thm-nonaffine-regular-local-ring-is-ufd]]: every local ring of the smooth scheme $X$ is a UFD under AC. Thus the height-one component prime at a point of $C$ is generated by a prime element $u$.

[F2] [[cor-localisations-of-regular-local-rings-are-regular]], [[thm-one-dimensional-regular-local-rings-are-dvrs]]: the localization of a regular local ring at a height-one prime is a one-dimensional regular local ring, hence a DVR; its maximal ideal is generated by the local parameter $u$.

[F3] [[def-maximal-order-and-tangent-directions]], [[def-order-of-an-ideal-sheaf-at-a-point]]: at a point of the marked support the order is at least $\mu$, while maximal order gives an upper bound $\mu$.

[F4] [[def-blowup-scheme-along-ideal]], [[def-effective-cartier-divisor]]: blowing up a Cartier divisor is an isomorphism and its exceptional ideal is generated locally by its equation $y$. If the center has SNC with $E$, [[def-multiple-test-blowup-and-controlled-transform]] makes the division $y^{-\mu}\sigma^*\mathcal I$ the controlled transform of the marked ideal; otherwise we use this expression only as a local ideal quotient.

[F5] [[def-embedding-dimension-and-regular-local-ring]], [[lem-regular-local-quotient-by-parameter-is-regular]]: in a regular local ring, an element of order one is a regular parameter and its quotient is regular.
## Proof

1.1 The local shape along the component. Fix $x\in C$, put $R=\mathcal O_{X,x}$, and let $P=I(C)_x$ be the height-one component prime. By [F1], $P=(u)$ for a prime element $u$. Since $C$ lies in the marked support and $\mu\ge1$, $\mathcal I_x\subseteq P$. Let $\eta$ be the generic point of $C$. By [F2], $R_P$ is a DVR with uniformizer $u$; since $\eta$ lies in the support and $\mathcal I$ has maximal order, [F3] gives $\operatorname{ord}_{R_P}(\mathcal I_P)=\mu$, so $\mathcal I_P=(u^\mu)$. For any $f\in\mathcal I_x$, this gives $sf=u^\mu g$ for some $s\notin P$ and $g\in R$. The element $u$ is prime and does not divide $s$, so repeated cancellation gives $f\in(u^\mu)$. Hence $\mathcal I_x=(u^\mu)J$ for an ideal $J\subseteq R$. If $\operatorname{ord}_x(u)\ge2$, then $\mathcal I_x\subseteq\mathfrak m_x^{2\mu}\subseteq\mathfrak m_x^{\mu+1}$, contradicting maximal order at $x$. Thus $\operatorname{ord}_x(u)=1$. If $J$ were proper, then $J\subseteq\mathfrak m_x$ and $\mathcal I_x\subseteq\mathfrak m_x^{\mu+1}$, again a contradiction. Therefore $\mathcal I_x=(u^\mu)$. By [F5], $u$ is a regular parameter and $R/(u)$ is regular, so $C$ is regular at $x$. Since $x$ was arbitrary, $C$ is regular, and locally along it the support is exactly $V(u)$; hence no other support component meets $C$. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F5]

2.1 The blowup is an isomorphism and resolves along the component. The regular component $C$ is an effective Cartier divisor by step 1.1, so its blowup is an isomorphism. Locally along $C$, write $y=u$ for its equation. Step 1.1 gives $\mathcal I=(u^\mu)$, hence $\sigma^*\mathcal I=(y^\mu)$ and $y^{-\mu}\sigma^*\mathcal I=\mathcal O_{X'}$ along the exceptional divisor. If $C$ has SNC with $E$, this is an admissible marked-ideal transformation by [F4], so its controlled-transform support misses the divisor. The local quotient calculation itself needs no boundary hypothesis. [F4, step 1.1] ∎
