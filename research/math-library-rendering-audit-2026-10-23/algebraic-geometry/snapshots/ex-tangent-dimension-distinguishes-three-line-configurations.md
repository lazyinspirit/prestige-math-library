## Example

Let $k$ be a field, and let
$$ V:=\operatorname{Spec}\bigl(k[x,y,z]/(xy,yz,zx)\bigr), $$
whose $k$-rational points are exactly the points of the three coordinate
axes of $\mathbb A^3_k$, with origin $o=(0,0,0)$. Then:

- $o$ is a $k$-rational point of $V$, and $T_oV\cong k^3$ is three-dimensional;
- for every finite list $g_1,\ldots,g_r\in k[u,v]$, at every $k$-rational point
  $q$ of $W:=\operatorname{Spec}(k[u,v]/(g_1,\ldots,g_r))$ the tangent space
  $T_qW$ is isomorphic to a linear subspace of $k^2$, so
  $\dim_kT_qW\le2$;
- consequently $V$ is not isomorphic over $k$ to any such $W$: an isomorphism
  would carry $T_oV$ isomorphically onto the tangent space of $W$ at the image
  of $o$, forcing the impossible inequality $3\le2$;
- in particular, taking the single equation $g_1=uv(u-v)$ gives Milne's
  configuration $W=\operatorname{Spec}(k[u,v]/(uv(u-v)))$, the union of the
  three distinct lines $u=0$, $v=0$ and $u=v$ through the origin of
  $\mathbb A^2_k$; so the coordinate axes of $\mathbb A^3_k$ are not
  isomorphic over $k$ to this union of three concurrent lines of the plane.

The three-line configuration is thus distinguished from the three axes by a
single numerical invariant, the dimension of the tangent space at the
distinguished point: three for the axes in space, at most two for anything
cut out in a plane.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$, the polynomial rings $k[x,y,z]$ and $k[u,v]$, the ideal
$(xy,yz,zx)\subseteq k[x,y,z]$, the affine $k$-scheme
$V=\operatorname{Spec}(k[x,y,z]/(xy,yz,zx))$, a finite list
$g_1,\ldots,g_r\in k[u,v]$, the ideal $I=(g_1,\ldots,g_r)\subseteq k[u,v]$, the
affine $k$-scheme $W=\operatorname{Spec}(k[u,v]/I)$, the origin
$o=(0,0,0)\in k^3$, and the particular list $uv(u-v)$ for the three-line
configuration.

[F1] [[def-polynomial-ring-over-a-commutative-ring]]: $k[x]$ is the set of
finitely supported coefficient functions $\mathbb N\to k$ with pointwise
addition and convolution product, and the commutative-ring axioms hold, so
$k[x,y,z]$ and $k[u,v]$ are commutative rings of the displayed polynomials
and a ring map out of them is multiplicative.

[F2] [[thm-universal-property-of-a-polynomial-ring-on-a-family]]: for
commutative rings $R,S$, a ring homomorphism $\varphi:R\to S$ and a family
$(s_i)$ in $S$ there is a unique ring homomorphism $R[x_i]\to S$ restricting
to $\varphi$ and sending $x_i\mapsto s_i$; over $k$ this identifies the
$k$-algebra maps $k[x,y,z]\to k$ with the triples $(a,b,c)$ via evaluation,
and the $k$-algebra maps $k[u,v]\to k$ with the pairs $(a,b)$ similarly.

[F3] [[def-homogeneous-polynomial-and-homogeneous-ideal]]: a polynomial is
homogeneous of degree $d$ when each occurring monomial has total degree $d$;
in particular a linear form is homogeneous of degree $1$, so every monomial
occurring in it has total degree $1$ and no constant term occurs.

[F4] [[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]: $(S)$ is the intersection of all
two-sided ideals containing $S$, and $(a)$ denotes the principal ideal
generated by $a$.

[F5] [[def-jacobian-matrix-affine-algebraic-set]]: for an ideal with a
specified finite generating list and a point $a$ with $f(a)=0$ for all $f$ in
the ideal, the Jacobian matrix at $a$ has rows
$(\partial f_i/\partial t_j(a))$, the formal monomial derivatives being read
in $k$, and it uses the actual ideal with the given list.

[F6] [[thm-zariski-tangent-space-jacobian-kernel]]: for any field, ideal
$I\subseteq k[t_1,\ldots,t_n]$, $X=\operatorname{Spec}(k[t]/I)$, rational
point $a\in X(k)$ and any finite generating list of $I$, the
coordinate-velocity map gives a canonical $k$-linear isomorphism
$T_aX\cong\ker J(a)\subseteq k^n$.

[F7] [[lem-evaluation-ideal-is-maximal]]: for $a\in k^n$ the evaluation map
$k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\to k$ has kernel $(x_1-a_1,\ldots,x_n-a_n)$, which is a
maximal ideal.

[F8] [[thm-quotient-ring-universal-property]]: a ring homomorphism whose
kernel contains a two-sided ideal factors uniquely through the quotient ring.

[F9] [[thm-affine-scheme-ring-anti-equivalence]]: ring maps $A\to B$ correspond
contravariantly to morphisms $\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A$, so
$k$-algebra homomorphisms $A\to k$ are the $k$-rational points of
$\operatorname{Spec}A$.

[F10] [[def-scheme-over-base]]: an $S$-scheme is a scheme with a morphism to
$S$, an $S$-morphism commutes with the structure maps, and $\mathbb A^n_k$
is the relative affine space over $\operatorname{Spec}k$.

[F11] [[def-morphism-of-schemes]]: scheme morphisms are morphisms of locally
ringed spaces and they compose.

[F12] [[lem-tangent-space-functoriality-classical]]: for a $k$-morphism of
$k$-schemes and $k$-rational points $x\mapsto y$, the differential
$d_xf:T_xX\to T_yY$ is $k$-linear and satisfies the identity and chain rules;
no Axiom of Choice is assumed or used.

[F13] [[def-dimension]]: a vector space with a finite basis $B\approx n$ is
$n$-dimensional, and isomorphic vector spaces have equal dimension because
the image of a basis under an isomorphism is a basis.

[F14] [[lem-standard-basis-of-f-n]]: the standard unit vectors form a basis of
$F^n$ with $n$ elements, so $\dim_FF^n=n$.

[F15] [[def-linear-subspace]]: a subset of a vector space is a linear subspace
when it contains $0$ and is closed under addition and scalar multiplication.

[F16] [[thm-dimension-of-a-linear-subspace]]: a linear subspace $U$ of a
finite-dimensional vector space $V$ is finite-dimensional with
$\dim_FU\le\dim_FV$.

[F17] [[lem-field-is-a-commutative-ring]]: every field is a commutative ring
with $1\ne0$ and is an integral domain.




## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The rings $A_V=k[x,y,z]/(xy,yz,zx)$ and $A_W=k[u,v]/I$ are commutative $k$-algebras presented as quotients of polynomial rings by the ideals of [F4], and $V=\operatorname{Spec}A_V$ and $W=\operatorname{Spec}A_W$ are affine $k$-schemes with structure maps to $\operatorname{Spec}k$ by [F10]; the two ideals are the actual ideals of the presentation, not a replacement of them. [given, F1, F4, F10]

2.1 The $k$-rational points of $V$: for $(a,b,c)\in k^3$ the evaluation homomorphism at $(a,b,c)$ is the unique $k$-algebra map with $x\mapsto a$, $y\mapsto b$, $z\mapsto c$ by [F2], its kernel $(x-a,y-b,z-c)$ is maximal by [F7], and it kills $(xy,yz,zx)$ exactly when $ab=bc=ca=0$ because a ring map is multiplicative [F1]; then [F8] factors it through a $k$-algebra map $A_V\to k$, which by [F9] is a $k$-rational point of $V$, and conversely every $k$-rational point arises from such a triple by [F9] and [F2]. If $ab=bc=ca=0$ and $a\ne0$, then $b=a^{-1}(ab)=0$ and $c=a^{-1}(ca)=0$ by [F17], and symmetrically; hence at most one coordinate is nonzero, and the $k$-rational points of $V$ are exactly the origin together with the points of the three coordinate axes. [step 1.1, F1, F2, F7, F8, F9, F17, algebra]

2.2 The $k$-rational points of $W$: by [F9] and [F2] a $k$-rational point $q$ of $W$ corresponds to a pair $(a,b)\in k^2$ whose evaluation map $k[u,v]\to k$ has $g_i(a,b)=0$ for every $i$, because [F8] factors this evaluation through $A_W$ exactly when it kills $I=(g_1,\ldots,g_r)$ as a multiplicative map [F1]; for the particular list $(uv(u-v))$ the condition reads $ab(a-b)=0$, which holds exactly when $a=0$ or $b=0$ or $a=b$ by [F17], so the $k$-rational points of that $W$ are exactly the points of the three lines $u=0$, $v=0$ and $u=v$. [step 1.1, F1, F2, F8, F9, F17, algebra]

2.3 The tangent bound in the plane: for every $k$-rational point $q$ of $W$ the ideal $I$ has the finite generating list $(g_1,\ldots,g_r)$, so [F6] gives a canonical $k$-linear isomorphism $T_qW\cong\ker J(q)$ with $J(q)$ the $r\times2$ matrix of [F5]; the kernel of the $k$-linear map $J(q)$ is a linear subspace of $k^2$ by [F15], whence $\dim_k\ker J(q)\le\dim_kk^2=2$ by [F16] and [F14]; therefore $\dim_kT_qW\le2$ at every $k$-rational point of $W$, independently of the list and of the field. [step 1.1, F5, F6, F14, F15, F16, algebra]

3.1 The tangent space of $V$ at the origin: the Jacobian matrix of the list $(xy,yz,zx)$ at $o$ is the $3\times3$ matrix with rows $(y,x,0)$, $(0,z,y)$, $(z,0,x)$ by [F5], and every entry is $0$ at the origin, so $J(o)$ is the zero matrix; by [F6] the tangent space is $T_oV\cong\ker(0:k^3\to k^3)=k^3$, and $\dim_kT_oV=3$ by [F13] and [F14], since the standard basis of $k^3$ has three elements and $\dim_kk^3=3$. [step 2.1, F5, F6, F13, F14, algebra]

4.1 No isomorphism: suppose $\varphi:V\to W$ is an isomorphism of $k$-schemes over $k$; its inverse $\varphi^{-1}$ is again a $k$-morphism by [F10], and $q:=\varphi(o)$ is a $k$-rational point of $W$ because $o$ is a $k$-rational point of $V$ and $\varphi$ is a $k$-morphism, composition of morphisms being [F11]; applying the identity and chain rules of [F12] to $\varphi^{-1}\circ\varphi=\operatorname{id}_V$ and $\varphi\circ\varphi^{-1}=\operatorname{id}_W$ at the $k$-rational points $o$ and $q$ gives $d_q\varphi^{-1}\circ d_o\varphi=\operatorname{id}_{T_oV}$ and $d_o\varphi\circ d_q\varphi^{-1}=\operatorname{id}_{T_qW}$, so $d_o\varphi:T_oV\to T_qW$ is a $k$-linear isomorphism; hence $\dim_kT_qW=\dim_kT_oV=3$ by [F13] and step 3.1, contradicting $\dim_kT_qW\le2$ from step 2.3. Thus there is no isomorphism $V\to W$ over $k$. [step 3.1, step 2.3, F10, F11, F12, F13, algebra]

5.1 Milne's three-line configuration: take the single equation $g_1=uv(u-v)$, so $r=1$ and $W=\operatorname{Spec}(k[u,v]/(uv(u-v)))$; the three factors $u$, $v$ and $u-v$ are linear forms, hence homogeneous of degree $1$ with no constant term by [F3], so each of the three lines $u=0$, $v=0$, $u=v$ passes through the origin; the lines are pairwise distinct, witnessed by the $k$-rational points of step 2.2 that lie on one line and on no other: $(0,1)$ has $u=0$ while $v=1$ and $u-v=-1$, $(1,0)$ has $v=0$ while $u=1$ and $u-v=1$, and $(1,1)$ has $u=v$ while $u=1$ and $v=1$, all these values being nonzero by [F17]; so $W$ is the union of the three distinct lines $u=0$, $v=0$ and $u=v$ through the origin, and step 4.1 shows that $V$ is not isomorphic to it over $k$. [step 2.2, step 4.1, step 2.3, F3, F17, algebra]

6.1 Boundary and scope dispositions: $V$ and $W$ are nonempty, the origin being a $k$-rational point of both by steps 2.1 and 2.2, and the particular three-line $W$ of step 5.1 contains the three lines; the zero cases are the zero Jacobian matrix of step 3.1 with kernel $k^3$, the zero vector of each tangent space, the empty list $r=0$ for which $W=\mathbb A^2_k$ and the bound of step 2.3 still holds, and the vanishing coordinate cases $a=0$, $b=0$, $a=b$ of step 2.2; there is one equation $uv(u-v)$ and one Jacobian row in the configuration of step 5.1, the general bound of step 2.3 using the displayed finite list; the degenerate configurations, such as a repeated line or the whole plane, are not excluded but only strengthen the bound, which uses no distinctness of the lines, distinctness being verified separately in step 5.1 for the three-line instance; the endpoints of the comparison are the two ambient dimensions three and two, realized as $\dim_kT_oV=3$ in step 3.1 and as the bound $\dim_kT_qW\le2$ in step 2.3; no Axiom of Choice or dependent choice is used, since the finite lists, the evaluation points, the matrices and the isomorphism are single given data and every cited supplier, including the functoriality lemma [F12] and the Jacobian kernel theorem [F6], is choice-free; and no biconditional is asserted as a claim, the only equivalences used being the correspondence between $k$-rational points and $k$-algebra maps of [F9] and the one-directional contradiction argument of step 4.1. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 2.2, step 2.3, step 4.1, step 5.1, F3, F6, F9, F12, F14, F17, algebra] ∎



## Source qualification

Milne, *Algebraic Geometry* v6.10, Example 4.43 (printed pp. 96–97) asks
whether the union $V$ of the coordinate axes in $\mathbb A^3$, presented as
$V(XY,YZ,XZ)$, is isomorphic to the zero set $W$ of $XY(X-Y)$ in
$\mathbb A^2$, and answers that it is not: the origin is the only singular
point of each, an isomorphism would have to match the singular points and
their tangent spaces, but $\dim T_o(V)=3$ while $\dim T_o(W)=2$. The item
proves the non-isomorphism without the singular-locus classification: it
computes the tangent space of $V$ at its $k$-rational origin as $k^3$ from
the zero Jacobian matrix of $(xy,yz,zx)$, and on the other side bounds the
tangent space of every $k$-rational point of any affine equation list in two
variables by the dimension of $k^2$, so that the comparison needs only
functoriality of the tangent space under an isomorphism. The source's
presentation language is kept: $V$'s $k$-rational points are the three
coordinate axes and $W$'s are the three lines $u=0$, $v=0$, $u=v$, the
identification being proved at the level of $k$-rational points, while the
scheme structures are the quotients by the displayed ideals. The source's
book-wide convention is that $k$ is algebraically closed; the computations
here use no algebraic closedness, no perfectness and no characteristic
hypothesis, and the three lines are pairwise distinct for every field because
their distinguishing points $(0,1)$, $(1,0)$ and $(1,1)$ use only $1\ne0$.
