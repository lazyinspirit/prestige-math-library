## Statement

Let $k$ be a field and let $R$ be a Noetherian local $k$-algebra with maximal
ideal $\mathfrak m$ and residue field $\kappa=R/\mathfrak m$. Assume that
$\kappa$ is finitely generated and separably generated over $k$ in the sense of
[[def-ag-separating-transcendence-basis]]. Then
$$0\longrightarrow\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^{2}\longrightarrow\Omega_{R/k}\otimes_R\kappa\longrightarrow\Omega_{\kappa/k}\longrightarrow0$$
is a short exact sequence, the first map sending the class of $x\in\mathfrak m$
to $\mathrm{d}x\otimes1$. If in addition $\kappa/k$ is finite separable, then
$\Omega_{\kappa/k}=0$, so the first map is an isomorphism
$\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^{2}\cong\Omega_{R/k}\otimes_R\kappa$; this applies in
particular at a closed point of a finite-type $k$-algebra whose residue field
is a finite separable extension of $k$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$, a Noetherian local $k$-algebra $(R,\mathfrak m,\kappa)$ whose residue field is finitely generated and separably generated over $k$, and, for the final clause, $\kappa/k$ finite separable.

[F1] [[lem-ag-differentials-universal-property]]: for a ring map $A\to B$ and every $B$-module $M$, composition with $\mathrm{d}$ is an isomorphism $\operatorname{Hom}_B(\Omega_{B/A},M)\cong\operatorname{Der}_A(B,M)$, naturally in $M$.

[F2] [[def-ag-separating-transcendence-basis]]: a finitely generated extension that is separably generated has algebraically independent elements $t_1,\dots,t_r$ over the base whose residual extension is finite separable; so $\kappa=k(t_1,\dots,t_r)(\alpha)=k(T)(\alpha)$ with $\kappa/k(T)$ finite separable.

[F3] [[thm-primitive-element-theorem-for-finite-separable-extensions]]: every finite separable extension is simple; this is applied both to $\kappa/k(T)$ and, in the final clause, to $\kappa/k$.

[F4] [[thm-evaluation-kernel-and-minimal-polynomial]]: for $\alpha$ algebraic over a field $F$ the evaluation map $F[X]\to F(\alpha)$ has kernel generated by a monic irreducible $P$, so $F(\alpha)\cong F[X]/(P)$ and $f(\alpha)=0$ implies $P\mid f$.

[F5] [[def-separable-elements-and-separable-extensions]]: $\alpha$ is separable over $F$ when it is algebraic over $F$ and its minimal polynomial over $F$ is separable.

[F6] [[thm-polynomial-is-separable-iff-coprime-to-its-derivative]]: for $0\ne f$ over a field, $f$ is separable if and only if $\gcd(f,f')=1$.

## Proof

1.1 A conormal computation. Let $S$ be any commutative $k$-algebra, let $I\subseteq S$ be an ideal and $B=S/I$. Then $I/I^{2}\to B\otimes_S\Omega_{S/k}\to\Omega_{B/k}\to0$ is exact, the first map sending the class of $x\in I$ to $1\otimes\mathrm{d}x$. The second map exists by [F1] applied to $S\to B$, and it is surjective because the elements $\mathrm{d}b$ generate $\Omega_{B/k}$; the composite is zero because $x\in I$ maps to $\mathrm{d}(x+I)=0$. The first map is well defined because for $x,y\in I$ one has $\mathrm{d}(xy)=x\,\mathrm{d}y+y\,\mathrm{d}x$, which lies in the image of $I\otimes_S\Omega_{S/k}$ in $\Omega_{S/k}$, so the classes of $x y$ and of $0$ in $I/I^{2}$ have the same image. Let $Q$ be the cokernel of the first map. A $B$-linear map $Q\to M$ is the same thing as a $k$-derivation $D\colon S\to M$ with $D(x)=0$ for all $x\in I$, because $B$-linear maps out of $B\otimes_S\Omega_{S/k}$ correspond by [F1] to $k$-derivations of $S$, and the quotient imposes exactly the vanishing on the classes $\mathrm{d}x$, $x\in I$. Such a $D$ factors through a $k$-derivation $B\to M$: it is constant on cosets, since $D(x+b)=D(b)$ for $x\in I$, and it satisfies Leibniz on classes, since for $x\in I$ the identity $D\bigl((x+b)b'\bigr)=xD(b')+b'D(x)+bD(b')=bD(b')+b'D(b)$ holds because $xM=0$ for the $B$-module $M$. Conversely every $k$-derivation of $B$ pulled back along $S\to B$ is such a $D$. By [F1] the functor $M\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_B(Q,M)$ is therefore isomorphic to $M\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_B(\Omega_{B/k},M)$, so the canonical map $Q\to\Omega_{B/k}$ is an isomorphism. [F1, algebra]

1.2 Preparation of the section. Write $\kappa=k(T)(\alpha)$ with $T=(t_1,\dots,t_r)$ as in [F2] and [F3], and let $P\in k(T)[X]$ be the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $k(T)$; by [F4] and [F5] the polynomial $P$ is monic, irreducible and separable, so $P'(\alpha)\ne0$ by [F6], since $P'$ is nonzero and $\gcd(P,P')=1$ makes its class a unit of $\kappa=k(T)[X]/(P)$. Put $\overline R:=R/\mathfrak m^{2}$, a local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^{2}$ and residue field $\kappa$, and write $\pi\colon\overline R\to\kappa$ for the quotient map. Choose $y_1,\dots,y_r,a\in\overline R$ with $\pi(y_i)=t_i$ and $\pi(a)=\alpha$, and let $\varphi_0\colon k[T]\to\overline R$ be the $k$-algebra map with $T_i\mapsto y_i$. It is injective because the $t_i$ are algebraically independent over $k$, so $k[y_1,\dots,y_r]$ is a polynomial ring and every nonzero element $q(y)$ of it is a unit of the local ring $\overline R$, since $\pi(q(y))=q(t)\ne0$ places it outside the maximal ideal. By the universal property of localisation, $\varphi_0$ extends to a $k$-algebra map $\varphi\colon k(T)\to\overline R$, and $\pi\circ\varphi$ is the inclusion $k(T)\hookrightarrow\kappa$ because they agree on the generators $T_i$. [F2, F3, F4, F5, F6]

1.3 Finite separable residue. If $\kappa/k$ is finite separable, then by [F3] there is $\alpha\in\kappa$ with $\kappa=k(\alpha)$; its minimal polynomial $P\in k[X]$ is separable by [F5], so $P'(\alpha)\ne0$ by [F4] and [F6]. Every $k$-derivation $D\colon\kappa\to M$ into a $\kappa$-module satisfies $0=D(P(\alpha))=\sum_iD(c_i)\alpha^{i}+P'(\alpha)D(\alpha)=P'(\alpha)D(\alpha)$ with $c_i\in k$ and $D(c_i)=0$, so $D(\alpha)=0$ because $\kappa$ is a field, and then $D=0$ on $\kappa=k[\alpha]$. By [F1] this forces $\Omega_{\kappa/k}=0$. [F1, F3, F4, F5, F6]

2.1 Applying step 1.1 with $S=R$, $I=\mathfrak m$ and $B=\kappa$ gives exactness of $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^{2}\to\Omega_{R/k}\otimes_R\kappa\to\Omega_{\kappa/k}\to0$; it remains to prove that the first map is injective. [step 1.1]

2.2 Correction of the lift. With the notation of step 1.2 form $\delta:=\varphi(P)(a)\in\overline R$, where $\varphi(P)\in\overline R[X]$ is $P$ with coefficients transported by $\varphi$. Then $\pi(\delta)=P(\alpha)=0$, so $\delta\in\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^{2}$, and $(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^{2})^{2}=0$ in $\overline R$ because $\mathfrak m\cdot\mathfrak m\subseteq\mathfrak m^{2}$. Also $\pi(\varphi(P')(a))=P'(\alpha)\ne0$, so $\varphi(P')(a)$ is a unit of $\overline R$. Set $a':=a-\varphi(P')(a)^{-1}\delta$, an element of $\overline R$ with the same residue $\pi(a')=\pi(a)=\alpha$. Taylor expansion in the commutative ring $\overline R$ terminates after the linear term because $a'-a\in\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^{2}$ has square zero, so $\varphi(P)(a')=\varphi(P)(a)+\varphi(P')(a)(a'-a)=\delta-\delta=0$. Hence the $k$-algebra map $k(T)[X]\to\overline R$ with $T_i\mapsto y_i$ and $X\mapsto a'$ kills $P$, and by [F4] it descends along $\kappa\cong k(T)[X]/(P)$ to a $k$-algebra map $s\colon\kappa\to\overline R$ with $\pi\circ s=\operatorname{id}_\kappa$. [step 1.2, F4, algebra]

3.1 A derivation inverse to $\mathrm{d}$. Define $D:=\operatorname{id}_{\overline R}-s\circ\pi\colon\overline R\to\overline R$; its image lies in $\ker\pi=\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^{2}$, and $D(x)=x$ for $x\in\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^{2}$ because $\pi(x)=0$. For $u,v\in\overline R$ write $u=s(\pi(u))+D(u)$ and $v=s(\pi(v))+D(v)$; since $\pi$ is a ring map, $D(u)$ and $D(v)$ have square zero and their product with any element of $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^{2}$ vanishes, so expanding gives $uv=s(\pi(uv))+u\,D(v)+v\,D(u)$ and hence $D(uv)=uD(v)+vD(u)$. Thus $D$ is a $k$-derivation of $\overline R$ into the $\overline R$-module $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^{2}$, and composing with $R\to\overline R$ gives a $k$-derivation $R\to\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^{2}$ whose value at $x\in\mathfrak m$ is the class of $x$ modulo $\mathfrak m^{2}$. [step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 Conclusion. By [F1] the derivation of step 3.1 corresponds to an $R$-linear map $\Omega_{R/k}\to\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^{2}$, which factors through $\Omega_{R/k}\otimes_R\kappa\to\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^{2}$ because the target is annihilated by $\mathfrak m$, giving a $\kappa$-linear $\theta$ with $\theta(1\otimes\mathrm{d}x)=x\bmod\mathfrak m^{2}$ for $x\in\mathfrak m$. The first map of step 2.1 sends the class of $x\in\mathfrak m$ to $1\otimes\mathrm{d}x$, so $\theta$ is a left inverse of it and that map is injective; with step 2.1, the displayed sequence is exact. [step 2.1, step 3.1, F1]

5.1 Finite separable residue concluded. By step 1.3 the hypothesis of the final clause gives $\Omega_{\kappa/k}=0$, so the exact sequence of step 4.1 reads $0\to\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^{2}\to\Omega_{R/k}\otimes_R\kappa\to0$, that is, $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^{2}\cong\Omega_{R/k}\otimes_R\kappa$ via the first map. [step 1.3, step 4.1] ∎
