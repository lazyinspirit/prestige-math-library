## Statement

Let $k$ be a perfect field and let $K/k$ be a finitely generated field
extension. Then $K$ has a separating transcendence basis over $k$
([[def-ag-separating-transcendence-basis]]): there are
$t_1,\dots,t_r\in K$, algebraically independent over $k$, such that
$K/k(t_1,\dots,t_r)$ is finite separable.

The argument in characteristic $p$ uses $p$-power linear independence and
exchanges of finitely many generators; it does not infer that
$k(t_1,\dots,t_r)$ is perfect, and no perfectness of any intermediate field is
asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A perfect field $k$ and a finitely generated field extension $K/k$, say $K=k(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n)$.

[F1] [[def-ag-separating-transcendence-basis]]: a finite tuple is a separating transcendence basis when its entries are algebraically independent over $k$ and the residual extension is finite separable, and then $r$ is the common cardinality of all transcendence bases.

[F2] [[def-algebraic-and-transcendental-elements]]: $a\in K$ is algebraic over a subfield $F$ when it satisfies a nonzero polynomial equation over $F$, and transcendental otherwise; $K/F$ is algebraic when every element of $K$ is algebraic over $F$.

[F3] [[thm-finitely-generated-algebraic-extensions-are-finite]]: if $a_1,\dots,a_s$ are algebraic over $F$, then $F(a_1,\dots,a_s)/F$ is finite.

[F4] [[thm-perfect-field-characterizations]]: $k$ is perfect if and only if $\operatorname{char}k=0$, or $\operatorname{char}k=p>0$ and the Frobenius map $a\mapsto a^p$ of $k$ is surjective.

[F5] [[cor-algebraic-extensions-of-perfect-fields-are-separable]]: every algebraic extension of a perfect field is separable.

[F6] [[def-separable-elements-and-separable-extensions]]: an extension is separable when each of its elements has separable minimal polynomial.

[F7] [[def-inseparable-degree]]: for a finite extension $K/F$, $[K:F]_i:=[K:F]/[K:F]_s$.

[F8] [[thm-multiplicativity-of-separable-degree]]: $[L:F]_s=[L:K]_s[K:F]_s$ for a finite tower $F\subseteq K\subseteq L$.

[F9] [[thm-tower-law-for-finite-field-extensions]]: $[L:F]=[L:K][K:F]$ for a finite tower.

[F10] [[thm-finite-extension-is-separable-iff-separable-degree-is-full]]: a finite extension is separable exactly when its separable degree equals its degree, so by [F7] a finite extension is separable exactly when its inseparable degree is $1$.

[F11] [[thm-evaluation-kernel-and-minimal-polynomial]]: for algebraic $a$ over $F$, $f(a)=0$ implies $m_a\mid f$, where $m_a$ is the monic irreducible minimal polynomial; and $m_a$ generates the evaluation kernel.

[F12] [[lem-gauss-lemma-over-a-ufd]] with [[thm-polynomial-ring-over-a-field-is-a-ufd]]: a polynomial that is irreducible and of positive degree in one variable over a polynomial ring over a field remains irreducible over the fraction field, and a polynomial ring over a field is a unique factorisation domain.

[F13] [[thm-polynomial-is-separable-iff-coprime-to-its-derivative]]: for $0\ne f$ over a field, $f$ is separable if and only if $\gcd(f,f')=1$.

[F14] [[thm-extension-generated-by-separable-elements-is-separable]]: an algebraic extension generated by separable elements is separable.

[F15] [[lem-transcendence-basis-exchange]]: given transcendence bases $S,T$ of $K/k$ and $s\in S$ there is $t\in T$ with $(T\smallsetminus\{t\})\cup\{s\}$ a transcendence basis; consequently two transcendence bases of a finitely generated extension have the same finite cardinality.

[F16] [[thm-primitive-element-theorem-for-finite-separable-extensions]]: a finite extension generated by elements all but possibly one of which are separable is simple.

[F17] [[thm-binomial-theorem-over-a-commutative-ring]], [[lem-prime-divides-intermediate-binomial-coefficients]]: the binomial expansion holds in every commutative ring, and $p$ divides its intermediate coefficients for exponent $p$. Hence $(a+b)^p=a^p+b^p$ in characteristic $p$.

## Proof

1.1 Running through the finite list $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n$ and adjoining an element exactly when it is transcendental over the field generated by the elements already adjoined produces, after finitely many steps, an algebraically independent $S\subseteq K$ over which every $\alpha_i$ is algebraic; then $K$ is algebraic over $k(S)$ and finitely generated over it, hence finite over $k(S)$ by [F3], so $S$ is a transcendence basis and $[K:k(S)]_i$ is defined by [F7]. The set of values $[K:k(T)]_i$, taken over the finite, algebraically independent $T\subseteq K$ with $K/k(T)$ finite, is a nonempty set of natural numbers and therefore has a least element; fix $T=\{x_1,\dots,x_d\}$ attaining it, and note that each such $T$ is a transcendence basis of $K/k$ by [F2]. If $\operatorname{char}k=0$, then $k(T)$ also has characteristic zero, so is perfect by [F4], and $K/k(T)$ is separable by [F5], so $T$ is already a separating transcendence basis. Henceforth assume $\operatorname{char}k=p>0$. Then Frobenius is surjective on $k$ by [F4], and $k$-linearly independent elements $a_1,\dots,a_m\in K$ have $k$-linearly independent $p$-th powers: from $\sum_ic_ia_i^p=0$ with $c_i\in k$ and $b_i^p=c_i$ one gets $\sum_i(b_ia_i)^p=0$, hence $\sum_ib_ia_i=0$ because $x\mapsto x^p$ is additive by [F17] and injective on the field $K$, so all $b_i$, hence all $c_i$, vanish. [F2, F3, F4, F5, F7, F17, given, F1]

2.1 Suppose $K/k(T)$ is not separable. By [F6] there is $x_{d+1}:=x\in K$ that is not separable over $K':=k(x_1,\dots,x_d)$, and $x$ is algebraic over $K'$ by [F2] since $K/K'$ is algebraic. Choose $F\in k[X_1,\dots,X_{d+1}]$ nonzero of least total degree with $F(x_1,\dots,x_{d+1})=0$. Then $F$ is irreducible: a factorisation $F=GH$ with $G,H$ nonconstant satisfies $\deg F=\deg G+\deg H$, and either $G$ or $H$ vanishes at $(x_1,\dots,x_{d+1})$ with strictly smaller total degree, contradicting minimality. [F2, F6, step 1.1]

3.1 Suppose every monomial exponent of $F$ were divisible by $p$. Since $k$ is perfect, write each coefficient $\lambda_\alpha=b_\alpha^p$ using [F4]. Then $F=G^p$ for the nonzero polynomial $G=\sum_\alpha b_\alpha X^{\alpha/p}$, by Frobenius additivity [F17]. Evaluation gives $G(x_1,\dots,x_{d+1})^p=0$ in the field $K$, hence $G(x_1,\dots,x_{d+1})=0$, contradicting minimality because $\deg G=\deg F/p<\deg F$. Thus some variable $X_j$ occurs with an exponent not divisible by $p$; fix such $j$. [F4, F17, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Set $L:=k(x_i:1\le i\le d+1,\ i\ne j)$ and $T'':=\{x_i:i\ne j\}$. Write $F=\sum_{\nu=0}^e C_\nu(X_i:i\ne j)X_j^\nu$ with $e\ge1$ and $C_e\ne0$. The total degree of $C_e$ is strictly less than that of $F$, so $C_e(x_i:i\ne j)\ne0$ by the minimality in step 2.1. Thus $P(T):=F(x_i:i\ne j;T)$ is a nonconstant polynomial in $L[T]$ vanishing at $x_j$, which proves $x_j$ algebraic over $L$. This makes $T''$ a transcendence basis of $K/k$: if $T''$ were dependent, a maximal independent subset $S\subsetneq T''$ would be a transcendence basis of $L$ over $k$, and since $L(x_j)=k(x_1,\dots,x_{d+1})$ is algebraic over $L$ by the preceding coefficient argument, the same finite set $S$ would be a transcendence basis of $k(x_1,\dots,x_{d+1})$ over $k$ with $|S|<d=|\{x_1,\dots,x_d\}|$, contradicting that $\{x_1,\dots,x_d\}$ is a transcendence basis of that field and that all transcendence bases of a finitely generated extension have the same cardinality [F15]; so $T''$ is independent, $K/L$ is algebraic, and $L$ is a rational function field over $k$ in the variables $x_i$, $i\ne j$. Consequently $P(T):=F(x_i:i\ne j;T)\in L[T]$ is the image of the polynomial $F\in k[X_i:i\ne j][X_j]$, which has positive $X_j$-degree and is irreducible in $k[X_i:i\ne j][X_j]=k[X_1,\dots,X_{d+1}]$; its coefficients are primitive, since a nonunit common factor would factor the irreducible $F$ of positive $X_j$-degree. Thus Gauss' lemma [F12] makes its image irreducible in $L[T]$. Since some $X_j$-exponent of $F$ is not divisible by $p$, the same exponent occurs in $P$, so $P'\ne0$ and $\gcd(P,P')=1$ because $P$ is irreducible and $\deg P'<\deg P$; thus $P$ is separable by [F13]. As $P(x_j)=0$ and $P$ is irreducible, $P$ is a nonzero scalar multiple of the monic minimal polynomial of $x_j$ over $L$ by [F11], so $x_j$ is separable over $L$ and $L(x_j)/L$ is separable by [F14]. [F11, F12, F13, F14, F15, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 By [F8] and [F9] the inseparable degree is multiplicative, $[E:F]_i=[E:M]_i[M:F]_i$ for finite $F\subseteq M\subseteq E$; and $[L(x_j):L]_i=1$ by [F10]. With $K'=k(T)$ and $L(x_j)=K'(x_{d+1})$ we get $[K:L]_i=[K:L(x_j)]_i\cdot[L(x_j):L]_i=[K:L(x_j)]_i$ and $[K:K']_i=[K:L(x_j)]_i\cdot[L(x_j):K']_i$, while $[L(x_j):K']_i>1$ by [F10] because $K'(x_{d+1})/K'$ is not separable. Therefore $[K:L]_i<[K:K']_i$, and $T''$ is a transcendence basis of $K/k$ by step 4.1 with a strictly smaller inseparable degree than $T$, contradicting the minimality of step 1.1. Hence $K/k(T)$ is separable and $T$ is a separating transcendence basis, which proves the theorem; moreover, by [F16] the residual finite separable extension is simple, so $K=k(T)(\gamma)$ for a single element $\gamma$ separable over $k(T)$. [F8, F9, F10, F16, step 1.1, step 4.1] ∎
