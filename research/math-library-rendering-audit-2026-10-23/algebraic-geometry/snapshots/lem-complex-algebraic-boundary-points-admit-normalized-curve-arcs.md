## Statement

Assume AC. Let $V$ be an irreducible complex algebraic variety of positive
dimension ([[def-classical-algebraic-prevariety-regular-maps-and-varieties]]),
let $O\subseteq V$ be a nonempty Zariski open subset and let $z\in V$. There is
an irreducible algebraic curve $C\subseteq V$ through $z$ not contained in
$V\setminus O$. There is also a holomorphic map $\gamma:\Delta\to V$ from a
sufficiently small complex disk, with $\gamma(0)=z$ and $\gamma(s)\in O$ for
all $s\ne0$; here $\Delta$ is a disk centred at $0$ and a map into $V$ is
holomorphic when its coordinate functions in an affine model containing its
image are holomorphic. The arc can be obtained by analytically parametrizing a
point over $z$ in the finite normalization of $C$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, an irreducible complex algebraic variety $V$ of dimension $\ge1$, a nonempty Zariski open subset $O\subseteq V$, and a point $z\in V$.

[F1] A finite morphism is closed, and for a finite ring map $R\to B$ the image of $V(J)$ is $V(\ker(R\to B/J))$; primes lie over primes, so a finite morphism of spectra is surjective onto its image ([[thm-finite-morphism-integral-closed]], [[thm-lying-over]]).

[F2] If $A$ is a nonzero finite-type algebra over a field $k$, there are algebraically independent elements $z_1,\ldots,z_d\in A$ such that $A$ is module-finite over $k[z_1,\ldots,z_d]$ ([[cor-noether-normalisation-module-finiteness]]).

[F3] For an irreducible classical variety $X$ one has $\dim X=\operatorname{trdeg}_k k(X)$; for a finite-type $k$-domain $A$ and a prime $\mathfrak p$ one has $\dim A=\operatorname{trdeg}_k\operatorname{Frac}(A)$ and $\dim A/\mathfrak p=\dim A-\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak p$, and a prime minimal over an ideal generated by $r$ elements has height at most $r$; every nonempty open subset of an irreducible variety has the same dimension, and every proper closed subvariety has strictly smaller dimension ([[thm-dimension-equals-transcendence-degree]], [[thm-affine-domain-dimension-transcendence-degree]], [[thm-dimension-formula-for-affine-domains]], [[thm-krull-height-theorem]], [[lem-dimension-nonempty-open-subset]]).

[F4] A reduced finite-type $\mathbb C$-scheme of pure dimension one admits a finite normalization $\nu:C^{\mathrm{nu}}\to C$ that is an isomorphism over the regular locus and birational on each irreducible component, with $C^{\mathrm{nu}}$ regular of dimension one; over the perfect field $\mathbb C$ regularity of a finite-type scheme is equivalent to smoothness ([[thm-normalization-reduced-curve-exists-finite]], [[thm-regular-equals-smooth-over-perfect-field]]).

[F5] If a curve $C\subseteq\mathbb C^M$ agrees near a point $p$ with the common zero set of $M-1$ holomorphic functions whose Jacobian matrix has rank $M-1$ at $p$, then near $p$ the curve agrees with a holomorphic graph over one ambient coordinate, and the graph projection is a homeomorphism onto a plane domain whose inverse is holomorphic ([[lem-nonsingular-complex-algebraic-curve-holomorphic-charts]], [[thm-holomorphic-implicit-function-theorem]]).

[F6] For $X=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb C[t_1,\ldots,t_M]/I)$ and $a\in X(\mathbb C)$, the tangent space satisfies $T_aX\cong\ker J(a)$ for every finite generating list of $I$; also $\dim_{\mathbb C}T_aX=\operatorname{edim}\mathcal O_{X,a}$, and $X$ is regular at $a$ exactly when $\operatorname{edim}\mathcal O_{X,a}=\dim\mathcal O_{X,a}$; every nonzero Noetherian local ring satisfies $\dim R\le\operatorname{edim}R$, and every regular local ring is a domain ([[thm-zariski-tangent-space-jacobian-kernel]], [[def-regular-local-ring-geometric-point]], [[thm-dimension-at-most-embedding-dimension]], [[thm-regular-local-rings-are-domains-and-cohen-macaulay]]).

[F7] Ideals of $\mathbb C[t_1,\ldots,t_M]$ have finite generating lists, and every classical variety is Noetherian with finitely many irreducible components ([[thm-hilbert-basis-theorem]], [[lem-classical-variety-noetherian-components]]).

[A1] AC is inherited from the suppliers above; it is used for the normalisation, lying-over and height-theorem steps and nothing else ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Choose an affine open neighbourhood $V_0$ of $z$ in $V$. Then $V_0$ is irreducible of dimension $d=\dim V\ge1$, and $V_0\cap O\ne\varnothing$ because two nonempty open subsets of the irreducible $V$ meet ([[def-irreducible-topological-space-and-subset]]). Put $B=V_0\setminus O$; it is closed in $V_0$ and proper. [F3, given, choose]

2.1 By [F2] applied to the coordinate ring $A$ of $V_0$, choose a finite morphism $h:V_0\to\mathbb A^d$ induced by an injection $\mathbb C[t_1,\ldots,t_d]\hookrightarrow A$ with $A$ module-finite over the image; the integer $d$ is $\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb C}\operatorname{Frac}(A)=\dim V_0$ by [F3]. For every irreducible component $W$ of $B$ the image $h(W)$ is closed by [F1], and the induced map $\mathbb C[t]/I(h(W))\hookrightarrow A/I(W)$ is finite and injective; hence $\operatorname{Frac}(A/I(W))$ is algebraic over $\operatorname{Frac}(\mathbb C[t]/I(h(W)))$, the two function fields have the same transcendence degree, and $\dim W=\dim h(W)$ by [F3]. A finite union of closed sets has dimension the maximum of the dimensions of its irreducible components, so $\dim h(B)=\dim B<d$, the inequality by [F3] since $B$ is a proper closed subset of the irreducible $V_0$. Thus $h(B)$ is a proper closed subset of $\mathbb A^d$ and there is a nonzero polynomial $P$ vanishing on it, or $P=1$ if $B=\varnothing$. [F1, F2, F3, A1, step 1.1]

3.1 Write $a=h(z)$. The Taylor expansion of a nonzero polynomial $P$ at $a$ has a nonzero homogeneous part, and a nonzero polynomial on $\mathbb C^d$ is not identically zero (induction on $d$ using that a nonzero one-variable polynomial has finitely many roots), so there is $w\ne0$ with $P(a+tw)$ not the zero polynomial in $t$; if $B=\varnothing$ take any $w\ne0$. The affine line $\ell=\{a+tw:t\in\mathbb C\}$ is therefore not contained in $h(B)$. [step 2.1, algebra, choose, A1]

4.1 Let $g_1,\ldots,g_{d-1}$ be linear forms cutting out $\ell$ in $\mathbb A^d$ (no forms occur when $d=1$), and let $C_0$ be an irreducible component of the closed subset $h^{-1}(\ell)\subseteq V_0$ containing $z$; such a component exists because $h(z)=a\in\ell$. Every component of $h^{-1}(\ell)$ has dimension at least $1$: it is a component of the zero set of the $d-1$ pulled-back forms, so its defining prime is minimal over an ideal generated by $d-1$ elements, has height at most $d-1$ by [F3], and $\dim=\dim A-\operatorname{ht}\ge d-(d-1)=1$; for $d=1$ this reads $\dim V_0=1$. Every component has dimension at most $1$ because it maps finitely into the curve $\ell$, so its dimension equals that of its closed image by the dimension comparison of step 2.1. Hence every component is a curve, and $C_0$ is one of them. [F1, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, A1]

5.1 The curve $C_0$ is not contained in $B$: otherwise $h(C_0)\subseteq h(B)$, while $h(C_0)$ is closed in $\ell$, irreducible, and of dimension $1=\dim C_0$ by step 4.1, hence equal to $\ell$, contradicting $\ell\not\subseteq h(B)$ from step 3.1. Therefore $C_0\cap O=C_0\setminus B\ne\varnothing$. The closure $C:=\overline{C_0}$ in $V$ is an irreducible curve through $z$ meeting $O$, so $C\not\subseteq V\setminus O$. [F3, step 3.1, step 4.1]

6.1 View $C_0$ with its reduced induced structure: it is a reduced finite-type $\mathbb C$-scheme of pure dimension one, so by [F4] it has a finite normalization $\nu:C_0^{\mathrm{nu}}\to C_0$, which is surjective by [F1]; choose $z_0\in C_0^{\mathrm{nu}}$ with $\nu(z_0)=z$. Then $C_0^{\mathrm{nu}}$ is regular of dimension one and, over $\mathbb C$, smooth. [F1, F4, step 5.1, choose]

7.1 Take an affine chart $\operatorname{Spec}A_0$ of $C_0^{\mathrm{nu}}$ containing $z_0$ and identify it with a closed algebraic set $X=V(I)\subseteq\mathbb C^M$, $I\subseteq\mathbb C[t_1,\ldots,t_M]$. Since $\mathcal O_{X,z_0}$ is a regular local ring of dimension one, [F6] gives $\dim_{\mathbb C}T_{z_0}X=1$; for a finite generating list $f_1,\ldots,f_r$ of $I$ this tangent space is the kernel of the $r\times M$ Jacobian matrix at $z_0$, so that matrix has rank $M-1$, and some $M-1$ of the $f_i$, say $f_1,\ldots,f_{M-1}$, have an invertible $(M-1)\times(M-1)$ minor at $z_0$. Put $Z=V(f_1,\ldots,f_{M-1})$ and let $J'$ be their Jacobian. Then $\dim\mathcal O_{Z,z_0}\le\operatorname{edim}\mathcal O_{Z,z_0}=\dim\ker J'(z_0)=M-(M-1)=1$ by [F6], while $\dim\mathcal O_{Z,z_0}\ge\dim\mathcal O_{X,z_0}=1$ because $X\subseteq Z$ induces a surjection of local rings and dimensions do not increase under quotients. Hence $\dim\mathcal O_{Z,z_0}=1$ and it is regular, therefore a local domain. [F3, F6, step 6.1, construct]

8.1 The inclusion $X\subseteq Z$ induces a surjection of one-dimensional local rings $\mathcal O_{Z,z_0}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal O_{X,z_0}$. Its kernel is a prime ideal, and since the source is a one-dimensional local domain, a nonzero kernel would be the maximal ideal with a zero-dimensional quotient, contradicting $\dim\mathcal O_{X,z_0}=1$; so the kernel is zero and $\mathcal O_{X,z_0}\cong\mathcal O_{Z,z_0}$. Write $I(X)=(g_1,\ldots,g_k)$ by [F7]. Each $g_i$ maps to zero in $\mathcal O_{Z,z_0}$, so for each $i$ there is $h_i\notin\mathfrak m_{z_0}$ with $h_ig_i$ vanishing on $Z$; for $h=h_1\cdots h_k\notin\mathfrak m_{z_0}$ all the $g_i$ vanish on $Z\cap\{h\ne0\}$, and since $X\subseteq Z$ we get $X\cap\{h\ne0\}=Z\cap\{h\ne0\}$. [F6, F7, step 7.1]

9.1 If $M=1$, step 8.1 identifies $X$ with an open neighbourhood of $z_0$ in $Z=\mathbb C$, so translation is the required holomorphic chart. For $M>1$, applying [F5] to $Z$ at $z_0$, whose $M-1$ defining functions have rank $M-1$ at $z_0$ by step 7.1, yields a plane domain $A'\subseteq\mathbb C$ and a holomorphic map $\gamma_0:A'\to\mathbb C^M$ whose image is a neighbourhood of $z_0$ in $Z$, with $\gamma_0$ injective and inverse holomorphic; let $c$ be the value of the free coordinate at $z_0$ and replace $\gamma_0$ by $s\mapsto\gamma_0(c+s)$ on a disk $\Delta\subseteq A'-c$ centred at $0$, so that $\gamma_0(0)=z_0$. Shrinking $\Delta$ and using step 8.1, we may assume $\gamma_0(\Delta)\subseteq X$. Composing $\gamma_0$ with the inclusion $X\subseteq C_0^{\mathrm{nu}}$ and with $\nu$ gives an arc into $C$; it is nonconstant and holomorphic, because every coordinate function of the affine model $V_0$ pulls back to a regular function on the affine set $X$, is there represented by a polynomial in $t_1,\ldots,t_M$, and substitution of the holomorphic graph coordinates gives a holomorphic function of $s$. The finite normalization of $C$ restricts over the open curve $C_0=C\cap V_0$ to the normalization of $C_0$: both affine descriptions use the same integral closure, localized at the defining open. Thus this parametrizes a point in the normalization of $C$ as stated. [F5, step 7.1, step 8.1, construct]

10.1 It remains to make the punctured arc avoid $V\setminus O$. Since $C_0\not\subseteq B$ and $B$ is closed in the irreducible curve $C_0$, the set $C_0\setminus O=C_0\cap B$ has dimension $0$ by [F3] and is therefore finite: a classical variety is Noetherian with finitely many irreducible components, and a zero-dimensional irreducible component is a single point. Because $\nu$ is finite, each of its fibres is finite: on an affine chart the coordinate ring is a finitely generated module over the base with finite-dimensional reduction modulo a maximal ideal. Hence $F:=\nu^{-1}(C_0\setminus O)$ is finite. The chart $\gamma_0$ of step 9.1 is injective, so $\gamma_0^{-1}(F)$ is finite with at most $|F|$ elements; replacing the disk by a smaller concentric disk, we may assume no nonzero parameter in $\Delta$ has $\gamma_0(s)\in F$ (if $z\in O$, the parameter $0$ is not in this preimage either). Then for every $s\ne0$ the point $\nu(\gamma_0(s))$ lies in $C_0\setminus\nu(F)=C_0\cap O\subseteq O$. Thus $\gamma:=\nu\circ\gamma_0:\Delta\to V$ is holomorphic with $\gamma(0)=z$ and $\gamma(s)\in O$ for all $s\ne0$, and the arc was obtained by parametrizing the point $z_0$ over $z$ in the finite normalization $C_0^{\mathrm{nu}}$. [F4, F7, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 9.1] ∎
