## Statement

Let $k\subseteq K$ be a finitely generated field extension that is separably
generated over $k$ by $t_1,\dots,t_r$
([[def-ag-separating-transcendence-basis]]). Then
$\mathrm{d}t_1,\dots,\mathrm{d}t_r$ are a $K$-basis of $\Omega_{K/k}$; in
particular $\Omega_{K/k}$ is a free $K$-module of rank $r$.

Assume moreover the Axiom of Choice. Let $\operatorname{char}k=0$ and let
$k\subseteq K\subseteq L$ be a tower of fields with $K/k$ finitely generated.
Then the natural map
$$L\otimes_K\Omega_{K/k}\longrightarrow\Omega_{L/k}$$
induced by $K\to L$ is injective. The Axiom of Choice is used exactly to choose
maximal algebraically independent subsets, that is transcendence bases, of $L$
over $K$ and of $K$ over $k$; every subsequent step is choice-free. The
assumption is declared as [[def-axiom-of-choice]] and is inherited by the
consumers of this theorem.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finitely generated field extension $k\subseteq K$ separably generated by $t_1,\dots,t_r$, so that $K/k(T)$ is finite separable for $T=\{t_1,\dots,t_r\}$; and, for the second part, an extension $k\subseteq K\subseteq L$ with $\operatorname{char}k=0$, $K/k$ finitely generated, together with the Axiom of Choice.

[F1] [[def-ag-separating-transcendence-basis]]: $T=(t_1,\dots,t_r)$ is algebraically independent over $k$ and the residual extension $K/k(T)$ is finite separable; every finitely generated extension is separably generated exactly when it possesses such a tuple.

[F2] [[thm-primitive-element-theorem-for-finite-separable-extensions]]: a finite extension generated by elements all but possibly one of which are separable over the base is simple; in particular every finite separable extension is simple, so $K=k(T)(\alpha)$ for an element $\alpha$ separable over $k(T)$.

[F3] [[thm-evaluation-kernel-and-minimal-polynomial]]: for $\alpha$ algebraic over a field $F$ there is a unique monic irreducible minimal polynomial $m_\alpha\in F[x]$ with $\ker(\operatorname{ev}_\alpha)=(m_\alpha)$, and $f(\alpha)=0$ holds if and only if $m_\alpha\mid f$.

[F4] [[thm-polynomial-is-separable-iff-coprime-to-its-derivative]]: for $0\ne f$ over a field, $f$ is separable over that field if and only if $\gcd(f,f')=1$.

[F5] [[lem-ag-polynomial-quotient-differentials]]: for a commutative ring $A$ and $n\ge0$ the module $\Omega_{A[x_1,\dots,x_n]/A}$ is free with basis $\mathrm{d}x_1,\dots,\mathrm{d}x_n$.

[F6] [[lem-ag-differentials-localization-base-change]]: for a multiplicative set $U$ inside an $A$-algebra $B$ the canonical map $U^{-1}\Omega_{B/A}\to\Omega_{U^{-1}B/A}$ is an isomorphism, with inverse sending $\mathrm{d}(b/u)$ to $u^{-1}\mathrm{d}b-bu^{-2}\mathrm{d}u$; and for every $A$-algebra map $B\to C$ there is a natural $C$-linear map $C\otimes_B\Omega_{B/A}\to\Omega_{C/A}$.

[F7] [[lem-ag-differentials-transitivity]]: for $A\to B\to C$ the sequence $C\otimes_B\Omega_{B/A}\to\Omega_{C/A}\to\Omega_{C/B}\to0$ is exact.

[F8] [[lem-ag-differentials-universal-property]]: for every $B$-module $M$, composition with $\mathrm{d}$ is an isomorphism $\operatorname{Hom}_B(\Omega_{B/A},M)\cong\operatorname{Der}_A(B,M)$, naturally in $M$.

[F9] [[thm-perfect-field-characterizations]]: a field is perfect if and only if its characteristic is $0$, or its characteristic is $p>0$ and its Frobenius map is surjective.

[F10] [[cor-algebraic-extensions-of-perfect-fields-are-separable]]: every algebraic extension of a perfect field is separable.

[F11] [[lem-maximal-algebraically-independent-subset-is-a-transcendence-basis]]: if $S\subseteq L$ is maximal for inclusion among the subsets of $L$ algebraically independent over a subfield $K$, then every element of $L$ is algebraic over $K(S)$.

[F12] [[thm-finitely-generated-algebraic-extensions-are-finite]]: if $a_1,\dots,a_s$ are algebraic over a field $F$, then $F(a_1,\dots,a_s)/F$ is finite.

[F13] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: every family of nonempty sets has a choice function; this is what licenses the maximal algebraically independent subsets chosen below.

## Proof

1.1 Set $K':=k(T)=k(t_1,\dots,t_r)$ and let $\alpha\in K$ with $K=K'(\alpha)$ be separable over $K'$, as supplied by [F2]. Let $P\in K'[X]$ be the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $K'$; by [F3] it is monic and irreducible, and by [F1] and the definition of a separable element $P$ is separable over $K'$. Hence $\gcd(P,P')=1$ by [F4], $P'\ne0$, and the class of $P'$ is invertible modulo $P$, so $P'(\alpha)\ne0$ in the field $K=K'[X]/(P)$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, given]

1.2 $\Omega_{K'/k}$ has $K'$-basis $\mathrm{d}t_1,\dots,\mathrm{d}t_r$. Indeed $k[T]\to K'$, $T_i\mapsto t_i$, identifies $k[T]$ with the polynomial ring on the algebraically independent elements $t_i$ by [F1], so by [F5] (with $A=k$ and $n=r$) the module $\Omega_{k[T]/k}$ is free on $\mathrm{d}T_1,\dots,\mathrm{d}T_r$; since a nonzero element of $k[T]$ maps to a nonzero element of $K'=k(T)$, the localisation isomorphism of [F6] applies with $U=k[T]\smallsetminus\{0\}$ and exhibits $\Omega_{K'/k}\cong K'\otimes_{k[T]}\Omega_{k[T]/k}$ with the images of $\mathrm{d}T_1,\dots,\mathrm{d}T_r$ as a $K'$-basis, and those images are exactly $\mathrm{d}t_1,\dots,\mathrm{d}t_r$. [F1, F5, F6]

2.1 $\Omega_{K/K'}=0$: for every $K$-module $M$ and every $K'$-derivation $D\colon K\to M$ we have $D(\alpha)=0$, because $0=D(P(\alpha))=\sum_iD(c_i)\alpha^i+P'(\alpha)D(\alpha)=P'(\alpha)D(\alpha)$ with $c_i\in K'$ the coefficients of $P$ and $D(c_i)=0$, and $P'(\alpha)\ne0$ by step 1.1; then $D=0$ on all of $K=K'[\alpha]$, since a derivation vanishing on $K'$ and on $\alpha$ vanishes on every polynomial in $\alpha$. By [F8] this says $\operatorname{Hom}_K(\Omega_{K/K'},M)=0$ for all $M$, hence $\Omega_{K/K'}=0$. Applying the transitivity sequence of [F7] to $k\to K'\to K$, the first map $\tau\colon K\otimes_{K'}\Omega_{K'/k}\to\Omega_{K/k}$ is therefore surjective, and by step 1.2 the elements $\tau(1\otimes\mathrm{d}t_i)=\mathrm{d}t_i$ generate $\Omega_{K/k}$ as a $K$-module. [step 1.1, step 1.2, F7, F8]

3.1 Independence of the generators. For each $j$ the $K'$-linear functional on $\Omega_{K'/k}$ with $\mathrm{d}t_i\mapsto\delta_{ij}$ exists by step 1.2 and corresponds by [F8] to a $k$-derivation $\partial_j\colon K'\to K'$ with $\partial_j(t_i)=\delta_{ij}$. Write $P=\sum_ic_iX^i$ and put $\beta_j:=-\bigl(\sum_i\partial_j(c_i)\alpha^i\bigr)P'(\alpha)^{-1}\in K$, which is defined by step 1.1. On the polynomial ring $K'[X]$ define $T_j(F):=\sum_i\partial_j(b_i)\alpha^i+F'(\alpha)\beta_j$ for $F=\sum_ib_iX^i$, so that $T_j(cF)=c\,T_j(F)+\partial_j(c)F(\alpha)$ and $T_j(FG)=F(\alpha)T_j(G)+G(\alpha)T_j(F)$ for all $c\in K'$ and $F,G\in K'[X]$: these identities follow from additivity of $\partial_j$ and the product rules for $\partial_j$ and for the formal derivative, and they say that $T_j$ is a derivation along the evaluation $K'[X]\to K$, $X\mapsto\alpha$, with $T_j(X)=\beta_j$. Moreover $T_j(P)=\sum_i\partial_j(c_i)\alpha^i+P'(\alpha)\beta_j=0$, so $T_j$ vanishes on the ideal $(P)$; since $K=K'[X]/(P)$ by [F3], $T_j$ descends to a well-defined $k$-derivation $D_j\colon K\to K$ extending $\partial_j$, and by [F8] to a $K$-linear map $\Omega_{K/k}\to K$ sending $\mathrm{d}t_i$ to $D_j(t_i)=\partial_j(t_i)=\delta_{ij}$. If now $\sum_ic_i\,\mathrm{d}t_i=0$ with $c_i\in K$, applying that map gives $c_j=0$ for each $j$, so $\mathrm{d}t_1,\dots,\mathrm{d}t_r$ are linearly independent over $K$ and, with step 2.1, form a $K$-basis of $\Omega_{K/k}$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, F3, F8]

4.1 The second part. Assume $\operatorname{char}k=0$ and choose, using [F13], a maximal algebraically independent subset $T_0\subseteq K$ over $k$ and a maximal algebraically independent subset $B\subseteq L$ over $K$. By [F11] the extensions $K/k(T_0)$ and $L/K(B)$ are algebraic; by [F9] every field of characteristic $0$ is perfect and by [F10] every algebraic extension of a perfect field is separable, so $K/k(T_0)$ and $L/K(B)$ are separable algebraic. Because $K/k$ is finitely generated, [F12] makes $K/k(T_0)$ finite, so $T_0$ is a separating transcendence basis of $K/k$ in the sense of [F1], and step 3.1 exhibits a finite $K$-basis $\mathrm{d}t_1,\dots,\mathrm{d}t_r$ of $\Omega_{K/k}$. [F1, F9, F10, F11, F12, F13, step 3.1]

5.1 Extension of derivations. Let $D\colon K\to L$ be a $k$-derivation. First extend $D$ to $K(B)$: each element of $K(B)$ lies in $K(B_0)$ for some finite $B_0\subseteq B$, and on the polynomial ring $K[B_0]$, for which $\Omega_{K[B_0]/K}$ is free on the $\mathrm{d}b$, $b\in B_0$, by [F5], the prescription $b\mapsto0$ for $b\in B_0$, together with $D$ on $K$, defines a unique $k$-derivation of $K[B_0]$ extending $D$; it extends uniquely to the fraction field $K(B_0)$ by the quotient rule $\mathrm{d}(c/d)=(d\,\mathrm{d}c-c\,\mathrm{d}d)/d^2$ of [F6], and for $B_0\subseteq B_1$ the extension on $K(B_1)$ restricts to the one on $K(B_0)$ by uniqueness, so a derivation $D_1\colon K(B)\to L$ extending $D$ is well defined on the union of the fields $K(B_0)$. Next let $x\in L$; then $K(B)(x)/K(B)$ is finite separable by step 4.1, hence simple, with minimal polynomial $Q$ of $x$ over $K(B)$ that is separable by [F10], so $Q'(x)\ne0$ by [F3] and [F4]. Substituting $K(B)$ for $K'$, $K(B)(x)$ for $K$ and $Q$ for $P$ in the construction of step 3.1 produces an extension of $D_1$ to $K(B)(x)$, and any two extensions of $D_1$ to $K(B)(x)$ agree, because their difference vanishes on $K(B)$ and takes at $x$ a value killed by $Q'(x)\ne0$. Declaring the value at $x$ to be that unique value defines $D_2(x)$ for every $x\in L$; it is a derivation because for $x,y\in L$ the field $K(B)(x,y)$ is finite separable over $K(B)$ by [F10] and [F12], carries an extension of $D_1$ by the same construction, and on it the derivation laws hold while its restrictions to $K(B)(x)$ and $K(B)(y)$ agree with the unique extensions, so the values $D_2$ assigns are additive and satisfy Leibniz. [step 3.1, step 4.1, F3, F4, F5, F6, F8, F10, F12]

6.1 Injectivity. Keep the notation of step 4.1, let $\rho\colon L\otimes_K\Omega_{K/k}\to\Omega_{L/k}$ be the natural map of [F6], and let $x=\sum_ic_i(1\otimes\mathrm{d}t_i)\in\ker\rho$, the elements $\mathrm{d}t_i$ being a $K$-basis of $\Omega_{K/k}$ by step 4.1. For each $j$ the functional $\mathrm{d}t_i\mapsto\delta_{ij}$ is a $K$-linear map $\Omega_{K/k}\to L$, hence by [F8] equals $E_j\circ\mathrm{d}$ for a $k$-derivation $E_j\colon K\to L$; by step 5.1 there is a $k$-derivation $\widetilde E_j\colon L\to L$ extending $E_j$, and by [F8] it induces an $L$-linear $\varphi_j\colon\Omega_{L/k}\to L$ with $\varphi_j(\rho(1\otimes\mathrm{d}t_i))=\widetilde E_j(t_i)=E_j(t_i)=\delta_{ij}$. Then $0=\varphi_j(\rho(x))=c_j$ for every $j$, so $x=0$. Hence $\rho$ is injective, which is the second assertion, and the theorem is proved. [step 4.1, step 5.1, F6, F8] ∎
