## Example

Let $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ be an IID sample from a law $P$ on $\mathbb R$ with $P|X|<\infty$. For a realized sample $x_1,\ldots,x_n$, let $x_{(1)}\le\cdots\le x_{(n)}$ be the order statistics and maximize
$$P_nm_\theta=-\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n|x_i-\theta|,\qquad \theta\in\mathbb R.$$
If $n=2k+1$, the unique maximizer is $x_{(k+1)}$. If $n=2k$, the full maximizing set is $[x_{(k)},x_{(k+1)}]$. Choosing the middle order statistic for odd $n$ and the midpoint of the two middle order statistics for even $n$ gives a measurable exact M-estimator.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite nonempty real sample from an IID law $P$ with $P|X|<\infty$, and $\Theta=\mathbb R$ with its usual metric.

[F1] [[def-m-estimator]]: An M-estimator is a measurable exact or approximate maximizer or minimizer of the empirical criterion $P_nm_\theta$.

[F2] [[def-estimator-sequence-in-iid-experiments]]: An estimator sequence is a sequence of measurable maps on the finite IID product sample spaces.

[F3] [[lem-real-line-is-a-metric-space]]: The usual metric on $\mathbb R$ is $d(x,y)=|x-y|$.

[F4] [[def-identically-distributed-and-iid-random-variables]]: IID means a common law and mutual independence of the whole family.

[A1] The sample space is $\mathbb R^n$ with product topology and Borel sigma-algebra generated by open sets. For finite $n$, rational open boxes form a countable basis, so this Borel sigma-algebra is $\mathcal B(\mathbb R)^{\otimes n}$. The criterion is $m_\theta(x)=-|x-\theta|$.

## Verification

1.1 The IID data context is as in [F4], and the candidate set is a metric space by [F3]. Each order statistic has the finite min-max representation $x_{(j)}=\min_{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\},\,|S|=j}\max_{i\in S}x_i$: every j-element subset contains a value at least $x_{(j)}$, while the subset of the first j ordered entries has maximum $x_{(j)}$. Coordinate projections are continuous. For finite vectors $a,b$, the maxima differ by at most $\max_i|a_i-b_i|$: each $a_i\le b_i+\delta$ for $\delta=\max_i|a_i-b_i|$, and the reverse inequality follows by swapping $a,b$; minima satisfy the same bound by negating the vectors. Thus finite maxima and minima, each order statistic, and the stated middle-point selection are continuous, hence Borel measurable under [A1]. The selection is an estimator sequence by [F2]. [A1, F2, F3, F4, given, algebra]

1.2 Put $Q(\theta)=\sum_i|x_i-\theta|$. For $n=2k+1$ and $\theta<x_{(k+1)}$, let $\delta=x_{(k+1)}-\theta>0$. Moving to $x_{(k+1)}$ decreases each of the k+1 distances for indices $i\ge k+1$ by exactly $\delta$; each of the other k distances can increase by at most $\delta$ by the triangle inequality. Therefore $Q(x_{(k+1)})-Q(\theta)\le k\delta-(k+1)\delta=-\delta<0$. For $\theta>x_{(k+1)}$, the k+1 distances for indices $i\le k+1$ decrease by $\delta=\theta-x_{(k+1)}$, while each of the other k distances can increase by at most $\delta$, giving the same strict inequality. Thus the odd-sample median uniquely minimizes $Q$. [A1, given, algebra]

2.1 For $n=2k$ and $\theta\in[x_{(k)},x_{(k+1)}]$, the first k observations lie at or below $\theta$ and the last k lie at or above it, so $Q(\theta)=k\theta-\sum_{i=1}^k x_{(i)}+\sum_{i=k+1}^{2k}x_{(i)}-k\theta$ is constant on this interval. If $\theta<x_{(k)}$, moving to $x_{(k)}$ decreases the k+1 distances for $i\ge k$ by $\delta=x_{(k)}-\theta$, while the other k-1 distances can increase by at most $\delta$; hence $Q(x_{(k)})-Q(\theta)\le(k-1)\delta-(k+1)\delta=-2\delta<0$. If $\theta>x_{(k+1)}$, moving to $x_{(k+1)}$ decreases the k+1 distances for $i\le k+1$ by $\delta=\theta-x_{(k+1)}$, while the other k-1 distances can increase by at most $\delta$, so $Q(x_{(k+1)})-Q(\theta)\le-2\delta<0$. Therefore the interval is exactly the minimizing set of $Q$ and the maximizing set of $P_nm_\theta$ by [F1]. [A1, F1, step 1.1, given, algebra] ∎
