## Definition

Let $\mathsf P:\Theta\to\mathcal P(\mathcal X,\mathcal A)$ be a parametric model on a statistical experiment, and let $\psi:\Theta\to\mathcal T$ be an estimand whose target set $\mathcal T$ is equipped with a metric $d$ ([[def-statistical-experiment]], [[def-parametric-statistical-model]], [[def-estimand-on-a-statistical-model]], [[def-metric-space]]). For $n\ge1$, the IID sample-size-$n$ experiment has sample space $\mathcal X^n$, product sigma-algebra $\mathcal A^{\otimes n}$, and sampling law $P_\theta^{\otimes n}$ at parameter $\theta$ ([[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]]). The product law is defined recursively from $P_\theta$; each factor is a probability measure and hence sigma-finite, so the finite product theorem supplies its existence, rectangle formula, and uniqueness ([[thm-sigma-finite-product-measure-exists-is-rectangular-and-is-unique]]).

A **nonrandomized estimator sequence** of $\psi$ is a sequence of measurable maps, in the sense of [[def-measurable-function-between-measurable-spaces]],
$$T_n:(\mathcal X^n,\mathcal A^{\otimes n})\to(\mathcal T,\mathcal B(\mathcal T)),\qquad n\ge1,$$
where $\mathcal B(\mathcal T)$ is the sigma-algebra generated by the open sets of the metric $d$, that is, the Borel sigma-algebra of its metric topology ([[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]). Its sampling distribution at $\theta$ and sample size $n$ is the pushforward of $P_\theta^{\otimes n}$ by $T_n$.

This definition fixes IID repeated sampling and data-measurable estimators. It imposes no measurability on the parameter map $\theta\mapsto P_\theta$ or on $\psi$.
