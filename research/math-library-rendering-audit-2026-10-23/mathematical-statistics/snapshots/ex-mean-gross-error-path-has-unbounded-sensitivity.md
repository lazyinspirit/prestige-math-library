## Example

On $\mathbb R$ with its Borel sigma-algebra, for $F=\delta_0$ and $H=\delta_a$, the mean functional $T(G)=\int x\,dG(x)$ has $T(F_{\epsilon,H})=\epsilon a$ and right derivative $a$ at $\epsilon=0$. Each path law has finite support and hence a finite first moment. Taking the supremum of the absolute derivatives over $a\in\mathbb R$ gives $+\infty$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The gross-error path and point masses $F=\delta_0$, $H=\delta_a$.

[F1] [[def-gross-error-path]]: $F_{\epsilon,H}=(1-\epsilon)F+\epsilon H$ for $0\le\epsilon\le1$.

[F2] [[def-dirac-measure]] and [[prop-dirac-measure-is-a-probability-measure]]: $\delta_b$ is a probability law concentrated at $b$.

[F3] [[cor-expectation-agrees-with-the-finite-weighted-sum]]: on a finite probability space, expectation is the finite weighted sum of attained values.

[F4] [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]: the real line carries the sigma-algebra generated by its open sets, so the identity function is measurable.

## Verification

1.1 By [F1] and [F2], the path law places weight $1-\epsilon$ at $0$ and weight $\epsilon$ at $a$; this also covers $a=0$, when both atoms coincide. [F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 The identity function is measurable for the Borel sigma-algebra because each Borel set is its own preimage under the identity. Let $S=\{0,a\}$ with its full power-set sigma-algebra and weights $w(b)=F_{\epsilon,H}(\{b\})$. Since the complement of $S$ has path-law measure zero, the integral of the identity is the integral of its restriction to this finite probability space: both are the same finite sum over the distinct atoms (apply the simple-function integral separately to the positive and negative parts). Thus [F3] gives $T(F_{\epsilon,H})=(1-\epsilon)0+\epsilon a=\epsilon a$, while $T(F)=0$. [F3, F4, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For $\epsilon>0$, the difference quotient is $(T(F_{\epsilon,H})-T(F))/\epsilon=a$, so the right derivative at zero is $a$. Since $a$ ranges over all real numbers, the supremum of its absolute value is $+\infty$. [step 2.1, algebra] ∎
