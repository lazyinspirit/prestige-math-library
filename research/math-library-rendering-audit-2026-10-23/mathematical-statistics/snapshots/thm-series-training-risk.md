## Statement

Let $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)$ be a probability space, let $n,K\ge1$, and let $Z_i=(X_i,Y_i)$, $1\le i\le n$, be iid random elements of $([a,b]\times\mathbb R,\mathcal B([a,b])\otimes\mathcal B(\mathbb R))$. Write $P_X$ for the law of $X_1$ and suppose the one-observation law is $P_X(dx)\kappa(x,dy)$ for a supplied Borel probability kernel $\kappa$ from $([a,b],\mathcal B([a,b]))$ to $(\mathbb R,\mathcal B(\mathbb R))$. Assume a Borel function $g_0:[a,b]\to\mathbb R$ is supplied such that, for $P_X$-almost every $x$, $\int |y|\,\kappa(x,dy)<\infty$, $g_0(x)=\int y\,\kappa(x,dy)$, and $\int (y-g_0(x))^2\,\kappa(x,dy)\le\Delta$ for a finite $\Delta\ge0$. Let $p_K:[a,b]\to\mathbb R^K$ be a fixed Borel $K$-dimensional spline basis and assume a deterministic $\bar\beta_K\in\mathbb R^K$, a constant $C\ge0$, and $\gamma>0$ satisfy
$$\int_{[a,b]}\{g_0(x)-p_K(x)^T\bar\beta_K\}^2\,P_X(dx)\le C^2K^{-2\gamma}.$$
Then, with the fitted training vector $\widehat{\mathbf g}_K$ from [[def-series-spline-least-squares]] and $\mathbf g_0=(g_0(X_1),\ldots,g_0(X_n))^T$,
$$E\left[\frac1n\|\widehat{\mathbf g}_K-\mathbf g_0\|_2^2\right]\le\frac{\Delta K}{n}+C^2K^{-2\gamma}.$$
This is expected empirical training-design loss.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The iid pairs, conditional-law kernel and moment bound in the statement; a fixed $K$-dimensional spline basis; and the deterministic approximation vector $\bar\beta_K$.

[A1] On the common probability space from the Statement, the pairs $(X_i,Y_i)$ are iid with one-observation law $P_X(dx)\kappa(x,dy)$, where $\kappa$ is a supplied Borel probability kernel from the Borel space $[a,b]$ to the Borel space $\mathbb R$; no kernel is selected from a family.

[A2] A Borel function $g_0$ is supplied, and for $P_X$-almost every $x$ its conditional first absolute moment is finite, its conditional mean is $g_0(x)$, and its conditional squared residual moment is at most $\Delta$.

[A3] $p_K:[a,b]\to\mathbb R^K$ is a fixed Borel $K$-dimensional spline basis, $D$ is the matrix with rows $p_K(X_i)^T$, and $\int_{[a,b]}\{g_0(x)-p_K(x)^T\bar\beta_K\}^2P_X(dx)\le C^2K^{-2\gamma}$ for the stated deterministic coefficient vector.

[F1] For the very spline basis $p_K$ specified in the Statement and [A3], the fitted training vector is the orthogonal projection of $\mathbf Y$ onto $\operatorname{col}(D)$ and agrees with every least-squares fitted vector ([[def-series-spline-least-squares]]); no different basis or spline space is used.

[F2] Real random variables are measurable maps, and their laws are pushforward probability measures ([[def-random-element-and-real-random-variable]], [[def-law-or-distribution-of-a-random-element]]).

[F3] The observations are independent random elements; their joint law is the product of their marginal laws ([[def-independent-random-elements]], [[thm-independent-random-elements-have-product-joint-law]]).

[F4] A probability kernel assigns a probability measure to each input and has measurable event-evaluation maps ([[def-measure-kernel-and-probability-kernel]]).

[F5] Finite product sigma-algebras are generated by measurable rectangles ([[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]]).

[F6] Orthogonal projection is the unique nearest point in the subspace ([[thm-orthogonal-projection-is-the-unique-nearest-point]]).

[F7] A finite independent list can be orthonormalized without changing its successive spans ([[thm-gram-schmidt-orthonormalisation]]).

[F8] For a fixed realized design matrix, its least-squares fitted vector is the orthogonal projection of the response onto the design column space, including when the design is rank deficient ([[def-fixed-design-projection-estimator]]).

[F9] The orthogonal projection is the unique vector in the subspace whose residual is orthogonal to that subspace ([[def-orthogonal-projection]]).

[F10] For two sigma-finite factor measures, the product measure exists and has the rectangle formula; finite probability products are built by repeated application ([[thm-sigma-finite-product-measure-exists-is-rectangular-and-is-unique]]).

[F11] A lambda-system containing a pi-system contains the sigma-algebra generated by that pi-system ([[thm-dynkin-pi-lambda]]).

[F12] If $f$ is a nonnegative measurable function, then $A\mapsto\int_A f\,d\mu$ is a measure ([[thm-indefinite-integral-of-a-nonnegative-function-is-a-measure]]).

[F13] For nonnegative product-measurable functions, integration over a finite product of sigma-finite spaces can be iterated in each coordinate ([[thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces]]).

[F14] Increasing nonnegative measurable functions on a measure space have integrals increasing to the integral of their pointwise limit ([[thm-monotone-convergence-for-the-integral]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Write $\mathbf X=(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$ and $\epsilon_i=Y_i-g_0(X_i)$. For fixed $\mathbf x$, set $\kappa_1=\kappa(x_1,\cdot)$ and, for $1\le m<n$, apply [F10] to $\kappa_m$ and $\kappa(x_{m+1},\cdot)$ to obtain $\kappa_{m+1}$; both factors are probability measures, so the rectangle formula gives total mass one at each stage. Induction gives the finite product probability measure $\kappa_n(\mathbf x,\cdot)$ with rectangle values $\kappa_n(\mathbf x,\prod_iB_i)=\prod_i\kappa(x_i,B_i)$. The response rectangles form a pi-system because intersections of rectangles are rectangles, and [F5] says they generate the response product sigma-algebra. On response rectangles the evaluation is Borel in $\mathbf x$ by [F4] and [F10]. Because $\kappa_n(\mathbf x,\cdot)$ is a probability measure, the class of sets with Borel evaluation contains the whole space, is closed under complements by subtraction from $1$, and is closed under disjoint countable unions by limits of partial sums; [F11] therefore gives measurability for every response Borel set. For rectangles $A=\prod_iA_i$ and $B=\prod_iB_i$, iid sampling gives $P(\mathbf X\in A,\mathbf Y\in B)=\prod_i\int_{A_i}\kappa(x,B_i)P_X(dx)$, and repeated Tonelli [F13] gives $\int_A\kappa_n(\mathbf x,B)P_X^{\otimes n}(d\mathbf x)=\prod_i\int_{A_i}\kappa(x,B_i)P_X(dx).$ For each fixed response rectangle $B$, the left side of the target identity is a finite measure in $A$; the right side is also a measure by [F12], applied to the nonnegative measurable function $\mathbf x\mapsto\kappa_n(\mathbf x,B)$. Design rectangles form a pi-system because intersections are rectangles and generate the design product sigma-algebra by [F5], so [F11] extends the equality to every design Borel $A$. For each such $A$, both sides are finite measures in $B$: countable additivity on the right follows from countable additivity of $\kappa_n(\mathbf x,\cdot)$ and monotone convergence [F14], and their common total mass is $P_X^{\otimes n}(A)$ because the joint design marginal is $P_X^{\otimes n}$ and $\kappa_n$ is a probability kernel. Thus the response sets $B$ for which the identity holds for every $A$ form a Dynkin system (complements use the common total mass); they contain the response rectangles, so [F11] gives equality for every response Borel set. Hence $\kappa_n$ is a conditional law of $\mathbf Y$ given $\mathbf X$. Under this product law the residuals are conditionally independent, have conditional means zero by [A2], and conditional second moments at most $\Delta$. Hence $E[\epsilon_i\epsilon_j\mid\mathbf X]=0$ for $i\ne j$ and $E[\epsilon_i^2\mid\mathbf X]\le\Delta$. [A1, A2, F2, F3, F4, F5, F10, F11, F12, F13, F14, given, algebra, construct]

2.1 Let $Q=\Pi_{\operatorname{col}(D)}$. The matrix $Q$ is a Borel function of $D$: partition the finite-dimensional matrix space by rank and, within each positive-rank stratum, by the lexicographically first nonzero maximal minor; on each resulting Borel piece, the corresponding independent columns form a matrix $C$. The Gram matrix $C^TC$ is positive definite, and $C(C^TC)^{-1}C^T$ is symmetric, idempotent, and has image $\operatorname{col}(C)=\operatorname{col}(D)$, so it is the orthogonal projection; at rank zero put $Q=0$. These finitely many Borel formulas show that $Q$ is measurable with respect to $\mathbf X$. Its rank $r$ is at most $K$. Scanning the finitely many columns and retaining a column precisely when it is not in the span of those already retained gives a basis of $\operatorname{col}(D)$; applying Gram–Schmidt [F7] gives an $n\times r$ matrix $U$ with orthonormal columns spanning that subspace. For any $v$, $UU^Tv$ belongs to $\operatorname{col}(D)$ and $U^T(v-UU^Tv)=0$, so its residual is orthogonal to the columns of $U$ and hence to $\operatorname{col}(D)$. By the uniqueness in [F9], $Q=UU^T$; therefore $\operatorname{tr}(Q)=\operatorname{tr}(U^TU)=r\le K$. Since $Q_{ii}=\|Qe_i\|_2^2\ge0$, step 1.1 gives $E[\epsilon^TQ\epsilon\mid\mathbf X]=\sum_iQ_{ii}E[\epsilon_i^2\mid\mathbf X]\le\Delta\operatorname{tr}(Q)\le\Delta K.$ [A1, A3, F7, F9, step 1.1, algebra, construct]

3.1 Write $\mathbf g=(g_0(X_1),\ldots,g_0(X_n))^T$. From [F1] and [F8], $\widehat{\mathbf g}_K=Q\mathbf Y$, and $\mathbf Y=\mathbf g+\epsilon$. In the orthogonal decomposition $\mathbb R^n=\operatorname{col}(D)\oplus\operatorname{col}(D)^\perp$, the projection [F9] is identity on the first summand and zero on the second, so $Q^T=Q=Q^2$, $\|\widehat{\mathbf g}_K-\mathbf g\|_2^2=\|Q\epsilon-(I-Q)\mathbf g\|_2^2=\epsilon^TQ\epsilon+\|(I-Q)\mathbf g\|_2^2,$ because $Q(I-Q)=0$. For $\bar{\mathbf g}=D\bar\beta_K\in\operatorname{col}(D)$, $(I-Q)\bar{\mathbf g}=0$; the nearest-point property [F6] therefore gives $\|(I-Q)\mathbf g\|_2^2\le\|\mathbf g-\bar{\mathbf g}\|_2^2$. [A3, F1, F6, F8, F9, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Taking expectations in step 3.1, applying step 2.1 and then iid sampling with [A3] yields $E\|\widehat{\mathbf g}_K-\mathbf g\|_2^2\le\Delta K+E\sum_{i=1}^n\{g_0(X_i)-p_K(X_i)^T\bar\beta_K\}^2\le\Delta K+nC^2K^{-2\gamma}.$ Divide by $n$ to obtain the claimed expected empirical training-risk bound. [A3, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
## Scope note

The generic inequality uses no choice axiom: the one-observation conditional kernel is an explicit model input, and the product-kernel identity is proved in step 1.1. The MSF-25 regression-target definition is used by the separate Sobolev-rate corollary, which states its AC, integrability, and selected-version assumptions. The MSF-03 loss/risk page is a required page prerequisite, but its current definition is parameter/action risk; the empirical training-prediction criterion is defined here separately and is not identified with independent test risk.
