## Statement

Let $(\epsilon_t)_{t\in\mathbb Z}$ be iid Rademacher variables and let
$e(u)=1$ in $H=L^2([0,1],dt;\mathbb R)$. The centered process
$$X_t=(\epsilon_t+2\epsilon_{t-1})e$$
is a finite-dependent causal shift. Its physical-dependence coefficients
satisfy $\delta_1=2\sqrt2$ and $\delta_m=0$ for $m\ge2$, while its
long-run covariance is $\Gamma=9(e\otimes e)$. Under AC, the theorem
[[thm-hilbert-valued-clt-for-l2-m-approximable-bernoulli-shifts]] gives
$$n^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^nX_t\Rightarrow G,$$
where $G$ is centered Gaussian in $H$ with covariance $9(e\otimes e)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC and countably many iid Rademacher innovations.

[A1] AC supplies Countable Choice and the countable independent family used
here ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[cor-countable-independent-copies-exist]]).

[F1] The causal-shift definition specifies $X_{t,m}$ by retaining the last
$m$ original innovations and independently replacing the earlier tail;
the physical-dependence coefficient is the $L^2(H)$ norm of the difference
([[def-l2-m-approximable-causal-bernoulli-shift]]).

[F2] The stated two-point Rademacher law gives mean zero and variance one;
independence of random elements means independence of their generated
sigma-algebras, and expectations of independent integrable real products
factor ([[def-expectation-of-a-nonnegative-or-integrable-random-variable]],
[[def-independent-random-elements]],
[[thm-factorization-of-expectations-for-independent-variables]]).

[F3] Under AC, a centered $L^2$-approximable causal shift has the Hilbert
valued CLT with its long-run covariance operator
([[thm-hilbert-valued-clt-for-l2-m-approximable-bernoulli-shifts]]).

[F4] Independence of random elements is independence of their generated
sigma-algebras, and disjoint groups of an independent sigma-algebra family
generate independent sigma-algebras
([[def-independent-random-elements]],
[[def-independent-sigma-algebras-and-events]],
[[thm-grouping-independent-sigma-algebras]]).

## Proof

**Given:** The innovations and the process in the Statement.

1.1 The vector $e$ has norm one, so $X_t$ has mean zero and $\mathbb E\|X_t\|_H^2=\mathbb E(\epsilon_t+2\epsilon_{t-1})^2=5$ by [F2]. For $m=1$, the replacement changes only $\epsilon_{-1}$ in $X_0$, so $X_0-X_{0,1}=2(\epsilon_{-1}-\epsilon'_{0,1,-1})e$ and [F2] gives $\delta_1^2=4(1+1)=8$. For $m\ge2$, both innovations on which $X_0$ depends are retained, so $X_{0,m}=X_0$ and $\delta_m=0$. Hence $\sum_{m\ge1}\delta_m=2\sqrt2<\infty$. [A1, F1, F2, algebra]

1.2 The lag-zero covariance is $5(e\otimes e)$. At lag one, the only nonzero product in $(\epsilon_0+2\epsilon_{-1})(\epsilon_1+2\epsilon_0)$ is $2\epsilon_0^2$, so $\mathbb E_B[X_0\otimes X_1]=2(e\otimes e)$. Reversing the factors gives the same operator. At every lag $|j|\ge2$ the innovation sets are disjoint, so independence and centering make the covariance zero. Therefore the long-run covariance sum is $(5+2+2)(e\otimes e)=9(e\otimes e)$. [F2, algebra]

1.3 Fix a cut $a\in\mathbb Z$. The variables $(\epsilon_j)_{j\in\mathbb Z}$ are independent by the iid assumption, so their generated sigma-algebras form an independent family by [[def-independent-random-elements]] and [[def-independent-sigma-algebras-and-events]]. Each $X_s$ is measurable with respect to $\sigma(\epsilon_s,\epsilon_{s-1})$. Thus $\sigma(X_s:s\le a)\subseteq\sigma(\epsilon_j:j\le a)$, while $\sigma(X_s:s\ge a+2)\subseteq\sigma(\epsilon_j:j\ge a+1)$. By [F4] the two source sigma-algebras are independent; their sub-sigma-algebras generated by the process groups inherit independence. The process is therefore $1$-dependent under the definition in [[def-l2-m-approximable-causal-bernoulli-shift]]. [F1, F4, given, algebra]

2.1 The moment, centering and summability conditions were verified in step 1.1, and step 1.2 gives the covariance required by [F3]. Applying [F3] proves the asserted sample-sum convergence; the Gaussian limit is allowed to have finite rank, as it does here. This conclusion concerns the $H$-valued sample sums. [F3, step 1.1, step 1.2] ∎
