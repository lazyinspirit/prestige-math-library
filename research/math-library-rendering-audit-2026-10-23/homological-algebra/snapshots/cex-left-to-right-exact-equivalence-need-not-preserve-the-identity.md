## Statement refuted

The equivalence $\Gamma^{rl}:\operatorname{Lex}(\mathcal A,\mathcal A)\to\operatorname{Rex}(\mathcal A,\mathcal A)$ of [[prop-left-to-right-exact-equivalence-sends-identity-to-nakayama]] need not send the identity functor to a functor naturally isomorphic to the identity. Witness: let $A_0$ be the $k$-algebra with $k$-basis $e_1,e_2,u$ ([[def-field]], [[def-vector-space]], [[def-dimension]]), unit $1=e_1+e_2$ ([[def-algebra-over-a-commutative-ring]]), orthogonal idempotents $e_1^{2}=e_1$, $e_2^{2}=e_2$, products $e_1u=u=ue_2$, and all remaining products of basis elements zero; these are the upper triangular $2\times2$ matrices. Let $\mathcal A=A_0\text{-}\mathrm{mod}$. The Nakayama functor $N^{r}$ of [[def-left-and-right-nakayama-functors-by-finite-kernel-calculus]] satisfies $N^{r}\cong A_0^{*}\otimes_{A_0}-$ by [[lem-nakayama-kernels-give-well-defined-adjoint-functors]], and evaluating on the projective left module $A_0e_1$ ([[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]], [[def-left-and-right-modules]]) gives $\dim_kN^{r}(A_0e_1)=\dim_kA_0^{*}e_1=2$ while $\dim_kA_0e_1=1$; hence $N^{r}$ is not naturally isomorphic to the identity and the equivalence does not preserve the identity object ([[def-natural-isomorphism]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$, the $k$-algebra $A_0$ with $k$-basis $e_1,e_2,u$, unit $1=e_1+e_2$, $e_1^{2}=e_1$, $e_2^{2}=e_2$, $e_1u=u=ue_2$ and all remaining products of basis elements zero ([[def-field]], [[def-vector-space]], [[def-algebra-over-a-commutative-ring]]), the category $\mathcal A=A_0\text{-}\mathrm{mod}$ of finite-dimensional left $A_0$-modules, and the Nakayama functor $N^{r}=\Gamma^{rl}(1_{\mathcal A})\cong A_0^{*}\otimes_{A_0}-$ ([[def-left-and-right-nakayama-functors-by-finite-kernel-calculus]], [[lem-nakayama-kernels-give-well-defined-adjoint-functors]]).

[F1] A left $A_0$-module is an abelian group with a scalar action satisfying $r(m+n)=rm+rn$, $(r+s)m=rm+sm$, $(rs)m=r(sm)$ and $1m=m$; the submodule $A_0e_1$ is the image of the $A_0$-linear map $a\mapsto ae_1$, and $A_0=A_0e_1\oplus A_0e_2$ because $e_1+e_2=1$ and $e_1e_2=e_2e_1=0$ ([[def-left-and-right-modules]], [[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]], [[def-bimodule]]).

[F2] The $k$-dual $A_0^{*}=\operatorname{Hom}_k(A_0,k)$ is a right $A_0$-module under $(\lambda\cdot a)(x)=\lambda(ax)$, and $A_0^{*}e_1$ denotes the image of the right multiplication map $A_0^{*}\to A_0^{*}$, $\lambda\mapsto\lambda\cdot e_1$; on the free left module $A_0$ the tensor product $A_0^{*}\otimes_{A_0}A_0e_1$ is generated by elementary tensors subject to $\lambda a\otimes x=\lambda\otimes ax$ ([[def-linear-map]], [[def-bimodule]], [[thm-universal-property-of-module-tensor-products]], [[prop-functoriality-of-module-tensor-products]]).

[F3] For a finite-dimensional $k$-vector space the dimension is the cardinality of a basis, and a $k$-linear isomorphism preserves dimensions ([[def-dimension]], [[def-vector-space]], [[def-linear-map]]).

[F4] The functor $\Gamma^{rl}$ of the categorical Eilenberg–Watts triangle sends the identity functor regarded as left exact to $N^{r}=\Gamma^{rl}(1_{\mathcal A})$; a natural isomorphism $N^{r}\cong1_{\mathcal A}$ would give an isomorphism $N^{r}(A_0e_1)\cong A_0e_1$ of $k$-vector spaces ([[prop-left-to-right-exact-equivalence-sends-identity-to-nakayama]], [[def-natural-isomorphism]]).

## Counterexample

1.1 The algebra $A_0$ is well defined: the $k$-linear assignment $e_1\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$, $e_2\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$, $u\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$ identifies $A_0$ with the algebra of upper triangular $2\times2$ matrices, in which the listed products hold and multiplication is associative; consequently $e_1,e_2$ are orthogonal idempotents summing to $1$, $e_1u=u=ue_2$, $ue_1=0$, $e_2u=0$ and $u^{2}=0$. [given, F1]

2.1 The left ideal $A_0e_1$ equals the $k$-span of $e_1$: from $e_1e_1=e_1$, $e_2e_1=0$ and $ue_1=0$ one gets $ae_1=\kappa(a)e_1$ for the coefficient functional $\kappa$ of $e_1$, so $A_0e_1=\operatorname{span}\{e_1\}$ has $\dim_kA_0e_1=1$. It is moreover projective in the lifting sense of [[def-projective-module]]: by [F1] it is a direct summand of $A_0$ with projection $\pi:a\mapsto ae_1$, the free module $A_0$ has the lifting property because a $A_0$-linear map out of $A_0$ is determined by its value at the generator $1$, which can be lifted along any epimorphism, and restricting a lift of $f\circ\pi$ to $A_0e_1$ lifts a given $f:A_0e_1\to M$. [step 1.1, F1, F3]

2.2 The image $A_0^{*}e_1$ has dimension $2$: for $\lambda\in A_0^{*}$ one computes $(\lambda\cdot e_1)(x)=\lambda(e_1x)$, so $\lambda\cdot e_1=\lambda\circ\varphi$ for the $k$-linear map $\varphi:A_0\to A_0$, $\varphi(x)=e_1x$, whose image is $e_1A_0=\operatorname{span}\{e_1,u\}$ of dimension $2$ by step 1.1; the restriction map $A_0^{*}\to(e_1A_0)^{*}$, $\lambda\mapsto\lambda|_{e_1A_0}$, is surjective since a functional on the direct summand $e_1A_0$ extends by zero on $\operatorname{span}\{e_2\}$, and composition with the surjection $\varphi$ is injective, so $A_0^{*}e_1=\{\mu\circ\varphi:\mu\in(e_1A_0)^{*}\}\cong(e_1A_0)^{*}$ has dimension $2$. [step 1.1, F2, F3]

3.1 By [F2] the multiplication map $A_0^{*}\otimes_{A_0}A_0e_1\to A_0^{*}e_1$, $\lambda\otimes x\mapsto\lambda\cdot x$, is a well-defined surjection, and it is injective with inverse $\nu\mapsto\nu\otimes e_1$: indeed $(\lambda\cdot e_1)\otimes e_1=\lambda\otimes(e_1e_1)=\lambda\otimes e_1$, and for $x=ce_1\in A_0e_1$ one has $\lambda\cdot x=(\lambda\cdot c)\cdot e_1$ and $((\lambda\cdot c)\cdot e_1)\otimes e_1=(\lambda\cdot c)\otimes e_1=\lambda\otimes ce_1$. Hence $A_0^{*}\otimes_{A_0}A_0e_1\cong A_0^{*}e_1$, and since $N^{r}\cong A_0^{*}\otimes_{A_0}-$ by the given data, step 2.2 gives $\dim_kN^{r}(A_0e_1)=\dim_kA_0^{*}e_1=2$. [step 2.1, step 2.2, F2, given]

4.1 Since $\dim_kN^{r}(A_0e_1)=2$ while $\dim_kA_0e_1=1$, the vector spaces $N^{r}(A_0e_1)$ and $A_0e_1$ are not isomorphic, so by [F4] there is no natural isomorphism $N^{r}\cong1_{\mathcal A}$; equivalently $N^{r}$ is not naturally isomorphic to the identity functor. By [F4] the equivalence $\Gamma^{rl}$ sends the identity functor, regarded as left exact, to $N^{r}$, so it does not send the identity to a functor naturally isomorphic to the identity, and the equivalence does not preserve the identity object. [step 3.1, F3, F4] ∎
