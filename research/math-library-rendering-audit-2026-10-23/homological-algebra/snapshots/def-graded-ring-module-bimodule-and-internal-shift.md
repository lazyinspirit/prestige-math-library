## Definition

**Ground ring and graded algebras.** Fix a commutative ring $k$. A **graded
$k$-algebra** is a $k$-algebra $A$ in the sense of
[[def-algebra-over-a-commutative-ring]], together with a direct sum
decomposition of $k$-modules

$$A=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb Z}A_i$$

such that $A_iA_j\subseteq A_{i+j}$ for all $i,j\in\mathbb Z$ and
$1_A\in A_0$. It is **unital and associative** when its underlying ring is;
every graded algebra on this page is unital and associative. Since
$\eta_A(r)=r\,1_A$ and $A_0$ is a $k$-submodule containing $1_A$, the structure
map has image in $A_0$, so each $A_i$ is a $k$-submodule and the scalars act
centrally on $A$.

**Graded modules and bimodules.** A **graded left $A$-module** is a left
$A$-module $M$ with a decomposition $M=\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb Z}M_d$ of
$k$-modules such that

$$A_iM_d\subseteq M_{i+d}\qquad\text{for all }i,d\in\mathbb Z .$$

A **graded right $A$-module** is a right $A$-module with a decomposition
$M=\bigoplus_dM_d$ such that $M_dA_i\subseteq M_{d+i}$ for all $i,d$. A
**graded $(B,A)$-bimodule** is a $(B,A)$-bimodule ([[def-bimodule]]) that is
graded as a $k$-module and homogeneous under both actions, the two actions
continuing to commute. Both induced $k$-actions must agree with the given
$k$-module structure: $\eta_B(t)m=tm=m\eta_A(t)$ for $t\in k$ and $m\in M$. Elements of $M_d$ are **homogeneous of degree $d$**, and
the decomposition expresses every $m\in M$ uniquely as a finite sum of nonzero
homogeneous components.

**Degree-zero maps.** A map $f:M\to N$ of graded left $A$-modules is
**degree-zero**, or a **graded map**, when it is $A$-linear and
$f(M_d)\subseteq N_d$ for every $d$. The category $\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A)$
has the graded left $A$-modules as objects and the degree-zero maps as
morphisms, with composition of maps. Every $M$ is an object of
$\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A)$ by its own grading, and $A$ is a graded
$(A,A)$-bimodule under left and right multiplication.

**Graded submodules.** A **graded submodule** of a graded left $A$-module $M$ is
a submodule $S\le M$ with $S=\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb Z}(S\cap M_d)$. Such an $S$
is itself a graded left $A$-module with homogeneous pieces
$S_d:=S\cap M_d$, because $A_iS_d\subseteq A_iM_d\cap S\subseteq M_{i+d}\cap S=S_{i+d}$;
equivalently, $S$ is generated by its homogeneous elements. In particular a
graded submodule is determined by its homogeneous pieces: if
$S=\bigoplus_dS_d$ and $T=\bigoplus_dT_d$ are graded submodules with $S_d=T_d$
for every $d$, then $S=T$.

**Internal shift.** For $r\in\mathbb Z$ and a graded module $M$, the
**internal shift** $M\{r\}$ is the module with

$$(M\{r\})_d:=M_{d-r}\qquad(d\in\mathbb Z),$$

carrying the same scalar action as $M$. Its components are additive subgroups
of $M$ whose sum is direct, so $M\{r\}$ is a $k$-module; and $M\{r\}$ is
graded because

$$A_i(M\{r\})_d=A_iM_{d-r}\subseteq M_{i+d-r}=(M\{r\})_{i+d}$$

for all $i,d$. For a graded bimodule both actions are unchanged, remain
homogeneous and still commute, so $M\{r\}$ is again a graded bimodule. The
shift is invertible: $(M\{r\})\{-r\}=M$, and $M\{0\}=M$. For a graded algebra
$A$, the shifts $A\{r\}$ of the regular bimodule are the modules denoted
$A\{r\}$ elsewhere on this page.

**Dictionary with the published twist.** In the published nonnegative
commutative convention ([[def-graded-ring-and-graded-module]]) the twist $M(a)$
is the graded module with $M(a)_d=M_{d+a}$ for every integer $a$. Substituting
$a=-r$ gives

$$(M\{r\})_d=M_{d-r}=M(-r)_d ,$$

so $M\{r\}=M(-r)$ as graded modules: the internal shift **reverses** the sign of
the published twist parameter. For $r=0$ both conventions agree with $M$.

**No super signs.** The internal degree is a genuine $\mathbb Z$-grading, not a
$\mathbb Z/2$-parity. No sign is inserted into the multiplication, the scalar
action or a degree-zero map merely because elements have nonzero degree; the
balancing relation of the tensor product below is likewise signed by nothing.
These are the unsigned associative conventions for this page; no differential
or signed symmetry on tensor products is part of this definition. They do not
exclude Koszul sign conventions in other categories of graded objects.
