## Statement

Let $k$ be a field and $A$ a finite-dimensional unital $k$-algebra. For a
finite-dimensional projective left $A$-module $P$ and finite-dimensional left
$A$-module $M$, define the object-level value

$$h(P,M):=\dim_k\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,M).$$

If $A$ is $\mathbb Z$-graded and $P,M$ are finite-dimensional graded left $A$-modules,
with $P$ projective in the degree-zero graded category, define

$$h_{\mathrm{gr}}(P,M):=\sum_{d\in\mathbb Z}v^d\dim_k\operatorname{Hom}_{A,d}(P,M),$$

where $\operatorname{Hom}_{A,d}(P,M)$ consists of $A$-linear maps sending each
$P_i$ into $M_{i+d}$. These are candidate values on object isomorphism
classes; no descent to pairings on $K_0\times G_0$ is asserted here.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The field $k$, the finite-dimensional unital algebra $A$, and the finite-dimensional projective and module objects specified in the Statement. In the graded case, all module maps are $A$-linear and homogeneous when a degree is specified. No axiom of choice is used.

[F1] $G_0$ is generated by isomorphism classes of objects modulo the short-exact-sequence relations ([[def-grothendieck-group-of-an-essentially-small-abelian-category]]).

[F2] The split Grothendieck group is generated by isomorphism classes of projectives modulo direct-sum relations ([[def-split-grothendieck-group-of-an-additive-category]]).

[F3] The graded groups carry the Laurent action with $v^d[M]=[M\{d\}]$ and $v^d[P]=[P\{d\}]$ ([[def-graded-grothendieck-group-shift-module-and-cartan-map]]).

[F4] For graded modules, $\operatorname{Hom}_{A,d}(P,M)$ is the group of $A$-linear maps satisfying $f(P_i)\subseteq M_{i+d}$ for every $i$ ([[def-graded-balanced-tensor-product-and-homogeneous-hom]]).

[F5] The scalar action of $k$ on a graded $k$-algebra is central ([[def-graded-ring-module-bimodule-and-internal-shift]]).

[F6] $\mathcal L_k(V,W)$ is a $k$-vector space under pointwise addition and scalar multiplication ([[prop-linear-maps-form-a-vector-space]]).

[F7] If $V,W$ are finite-dimensional $k$-vector spaces, then $\mathcal L_k(V,W)$ is finite-dimensional, with dimension $(\dim_k V)(\dim_k W)$ ([[cor-dimensions-of-matrix-and-linear-map-spaces]]).

[F8] The homogeneous components of a graded left $A$-module are $k$-modules ([[def-graded-ring-module-bimodule-and-internal-shift]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Every $A$-linear map between left $A$-modules is $k$-linear: for $\lambda\in k$, centrality of the scalar action gives $f(\lambda p)=f((\lambda1_A)p)=(\lambda1_A)f(p)=\lambda f(p)$. Sums and scalar multiples of $A$-linear maps remain $A$-linear, so $\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,M)$ is a $k$-vector subspace of $\mathcal L_k(P,M)$. Since $P,M$ are finite-dimensional, [F7] makes the ambient linear-map space finite-dimensional, and hence $\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,M)$ is finite-dimensional. Thus $h(P,M)$ is defined in $\mathbb Z$. [F5, F6, F7, given, algebra]

1.2 Each $\operatorname{Hom}_{A,d}(P,M)$ is also a $k$-vector subspace of $\mathcal L_k(P,M)$: the $A$-linearity and degree-$d$ conditions are preserved by addition and scalar multiplication, using [F4], [F5], and [F8]. Therefore every such homogeneous Hom space is finite-dimensional by [F6] and [F7]. [F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, given, algebra]

2.1 The supports $I_P:=\{i:P_i\ne0\}$ and $I_M:=\{j:M_j\ne0\}$ are finite. Indeed, if a finite-dimensional graded space had more than $n$ nonzero components, where $n$ is its dimension, choosing one nonzero vector in each of $n+1$ distinct components would give $n+1$ linearly independent vectors; this is only a finite selection. If $\operatorname{Hom}_{A,d}(P,M)\ne0$, choose a nonzero map $f$ in it. Since $f$ is nonzero, some $p\in P$ has $f(p)\ne0$. Decompose $p$ into its finitely many homogeneous components. As $f$ is homogeneous and $f(p)\ne0$, at least one component $p_i\in P_i$ has $f(p_i)\ne0$. Then $i\in I_P$ and $i+d\in I_M$, so $d\in I_M-I_P$. This difference set is finite, hence only finitely many terms in $h_{\mathrm{gr}}(P,M)$ can be nonzero. The exponent is this map degree $d$, consistent with the internal-shift normalization $v^d[M]=[M\{d\}]$ in [F3]. The formula is therefore a Laurent polynomial in $\mathbb Z[v,v^{-1}]$. If either module is zero, all homogeneous Hom spaces vanish and the sum is zero. [F3, F4, step 1.2, given, choose, algebra]

2.2 If $\alpha:P\to P'$ and $\beta:M\to M'$ are module isomorphisms, then $f\mapsto\beta f\alpha^{-1}$ is a $k$-linear isomorphism $\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,M)\to\operatorname{Hom}_A(P',M')$. For graded isomorphisms of degree zero it restricts, for every $d$, to an isomorphism of $\operatorname{Hom}_{A,d}$ spaces, since degree-zero maps preserve each homogeneous component. Hence both candidate values depend only on the object isomorphism classes. [F4, step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 The formulas in the Statement thus give well-defined functions on pairs of object isomorphism classes, with values in $\mathbb Z$ and $\mathbb Z[v,v^{-1}]$, respectively. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 2.2, construct]

4.1 The groups in [F1] and [F2] impose additional short-exact-sequence and direct-sum relations. This Definition specifies only the object-level values; it makes no claim that they are additive for those relations or descend to $K_0\times G_0$. [F1, F2, given] ∎
