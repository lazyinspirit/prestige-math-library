## Statement

In $K_0^{\mathrm{tri}}(\mathcal T)$, $[0]=0$ and $[X[n]]=(-1)^n[X]$ for every integer $n$. An exact functor $F:\mathcal T\to\mathcal T'$ between essentially small triangulated categories induces a homomorphism $F_*$ sending $[X]$ to $[F(X)]$. Identity and composition are respected, and naturally isomorphic exact functors induce the same map. No coherence for a collection of functor isomorphisms is inferred from these group identities.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Essentially small triangulated categories $\mathcal T,\mathcal T'$ with translation $[1]$, and an exact functor $F:\mathcal T\to\mathcal T'$.

[F1] $K_0^{\mathrm{tri}}(\mathcal T)$ is the free abelian group on the set $\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal T)$ of isomorphism classes, modulo the subgroup generated by $[Y]-[X]-[Z]$ for the distinguished triangles $X\to Y\to Z\to X[1]$ ([[def-triangulated-grothendieck-group]]).

[F2] TR1 gives that $X\xrightarrow{1_X}X\to0\to X[1]$ is distinguished for every object $X$, and that every triangle isomorphic to a distinguished one is distinguished ([[def-triangulated-category-axiom-tr-one]]).

[F3] TR2 says that a triangle is distinguished if and only if its signed left rotation is distinguished, and the left rotation of $X\xrightarrow{f}Y\xrightarrow{g}Z\xrightarrow{h}X[1]$ ends with $-f[1]:X[1]\to Y[1]$ ([[def-triangulated-category-axiom-tr-two]], [[def-rotation-of-a-triangle]]).

[F4] An exact functor is additive, carries a specified natural isomorphism $\xi_X:F(X[1])\to F(X)[1]$, and sends every distinguished triangle to a distinguished triangle ([[def-exact-functor-between-triangulated-categories]]).

[F5] A natural isomorphism has an inverse natural transformation, so each of its components is an isomorphism ([[def-natural-isomorphism]]).

[F6] A function from a set to an abelian group extends uniquely to a homomorphism on the free abelian group, and a homomorphism killing a subgroup factors uniquely through the quotient ([[def-free-abelian-group]], [[thm-quotient-group-universal-property]]).

[F7] The published universal-property theorem factors class functions that are additive on short exact sequences, respectively biproducts, through $G_0$ and split $K_0$ by exactly this free-group and quotient argument ([[thm-grothendieck-group-universal-properties-and-functoriality]]).

[F8] A category with translation is additive and is equipped with a specified quasi-inverse $[-1]$ of $[1]$, with iterated translates formed using the chosen coherence isomorphisms ([[def-category-with-translation]], [[def-triangulated-category]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The identity triangle $X\xrightarrow{1_X}X\to0\to X[1]$ is distinguished by TR1, so its relation $[X]=[X]+[0]$ holds in $K_0^{\mathrm{tri}}(\mathcal T)$; subtracting $[X]$ gives $[0]=0$. [F1, F2, algebra]

1.2 Let $F:\mathcal T\to\mathcal T'$ be exact. The assignment $\varphi([X]):=[F(X)]$ is a function on $\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal T)$: a functor preserves isomorphisms, so isomorphic objects have isomorphic images. If $X\to Y\to Z\to X[1]$ is distinguished, exactness of $F$ makes $F(X)\to F(Y)\to F(Z)\to F(X)[1]$ distinguished, using the specified shift isomorphism, so $\varphi$ satisfies $\varphi([Y])=\varphi([X])+\varphi([Z])$. By the free-group and quotient universal properties of [F6], in the pattern recalled in [F7], $\varphi$ factors uniquely through a homomorphism $F_*:K_0^{\mathrm{tri}}(\mathcal T)\to K_0^{\mathrm{tri}}(\mathcal T')$ with $F_*([X])=[F(X)]$. [F1, F4, F6, F7, construct, algebra]

2.1 By TR2 the signed left rotation $X\to0\to X[1]\xrightarrow{-1_{X[1]}}X[1]$ of the identity triangle is distinguished, so $[0]=[X]+[X[1]]$; by step 1.1, $[X[1]]=-[X]$. Applying this to $X[-1]$, and using that $[-1]$ is a quasi-inverse of $[1]$ so that $(X[-1])[1]\cong X$, gives $[X]=-[X[-1]]$ and hence $[X[-1]]=-[X]$. [F1, F3, F8, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 For the identity functor $1_{\mathcal T}$ the class function $[X]\mapsto[1_{\mathcal T}(X)]$ is $[X]\mapsto[X]$, so $(1_{\mathcal T})_*=1$. If $F:\mathcal T\to\mathcal T'$ and $G:\mathcal T'\to\mathcal T''$ are exact, then $G\circ F$ is exact: additivity, the composite natural isomorphism $\xi^{G}_{F(X)}\circ G(\xi^{F}_X):GF(X[1])\to GF(X)[1]$, and preservation of distinguished triangles all compose. On every generator, $(G\circ F)_*([X])=[(G\circ F)(X)]=[G(F(X))]=G_*\bigl(F_*([X])\bigr)$, so $(G\circ F)_*=G_*\circ F_*$. [F1, F4, step 1.2, algebra]

2.3 Let $\eta:F\Rightarrow G$ be a natural isomorphism between exact functors. Each component $\eta_X:F(X)\to G(X)$ is an isomorphism by [F5], so $F(X)$ and $G(X)$ have the same isomorphism class in $\mathcal T'$ and hence $[F(X)]=[G(X)]$ in $K_0^{\mathrm{tri}}(\mathcal T')$. The two induced homomorphisms agree on every generator of the free group and therefore are equal. This is an equality of group homomorphisms only; no coherence for a collection of such natural isomorphisms, and no group-action data, is asserted or obtained. [F1, F5, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 For $n\ge0$, induction on $n$ using $[X[n]]=[(X[n-1])[1]]=-[X[n-1]]$ gives $[X[n]]=(-1)^n[X]$; the case $n=0$ uses $X[0]\cong X$ and $n=1$ is step 2.1. For $n<0$, apply the nonnegative case to $X[n]$: $[X]=[(X[n])[-n]]=(-1)^{-n}[X[n]]$, so $[X[n]]=(-1)^n[X]$. Every integer $n$ is covered by the two cases. [F1, F8, step 2.1, induction, cases, algebra] ∎
