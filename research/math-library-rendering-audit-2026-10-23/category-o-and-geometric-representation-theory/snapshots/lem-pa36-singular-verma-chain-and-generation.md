## Statement

Assume the complex flag-variety setup, including the Axiom of Choice, of
[[def-pa36-flag-tdo-and-module]]. Let $\kappa$ be antidominant, put
$$\Phi_\kappa=\{\beta\in\Phi:\langle\kappa,\beta^\vee\rangle=0\}, \qquad J=\Delta\cap\Phi_\kappa,$$
and assume $J$ is a simple system for the full zero-root subsystem
$\Phi_\kappa$. Choose a reduced expression
$w_J=s_{\alpha_N}\cdots s_{\alpha_1}$ for the longest element of $W_J$.
Set $\mu_0=\rho$, $\mu_j=s_{\alpha_j}\mu_{j-1}$, and
$$H_j=M(-\kappa-\rho+\mu_j),\qquad \mathcal E_j=\mathsf E^B_{\kappa+\rho}(H_j),$$
using the exact twisted associated-bundle functor in
[[lem-pa36-twisted-associated-bundle-and-parabolic-descent]]. The rational
$B$-action has highest-vector weight $\mu_j$. Then there are embeddings
$$H_N\subseteq\cdots\subseteq H_0,\qquad \mathcal E_N\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal E_0 =\mathcal D_\kappa\otimes_{\mathcal O_X}\mathcal O_X(\rho).$$
For each $j$, a coherent module $N_j$ over
$\mathcal D^{\alpha_j}_{\kappa-\rho}$ satisfies
$$\mathcal E_{j-1}/\mathcal E_j\cong \mathcal O_X(\rho)\otimes p_{\alpha_j}^\dagger N_j.$$
Furthermore $\mathcal E_N$ is generated as a $\mathcal D_\kappa$-module by
finitely many global sections: for a finite-dimensional rational irreducible
$G$-module $V$ of highest weight $\rho$ there is a surjection
$$\mathcal D_\kappa\otimes_{\mathbb C}V\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal E_N,$$
with horizontal constant $V$-factor. The sequence of roots depends only on
the chosen reduced expression in $W_J$, not on any later module to which
the chain is applied. When $J$ is empty, $N=0$ and the same assertions hold
with $H_N=H_0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Complex antidominant $\kappa$, the full zero-subsystem condition,
the chosen reduced expression, and the objects defined in the statement.

[F1] Finite Weyl groups have closed-chamber stabilizers generated by the
perpendicular roots; reduced expressions have positive prefix roots and
longest elements reverse all positive roots
([[lem-finite-weyl-closed-chambers-and-stabilizers]],
[[lem-finite-weyl-positive-roots-and-simple-reflections]],
[[lem-finite-weyl-strong-exchange-and-deletion]]).

[F2] Complex antidominance implies
$\kappa-w\kappa\notin Q_+\setminus\{0\}$ for every $w\in W$
([[lem-pa36-complex-weight-central-character-separation]]).

[F3] $V$ exists as a rational $G$-module by [[thm-borel-weil]]; its weights
are $W$-invariant, lie below $\rho$, and every extremal weight $w\rho$ has
multiplicity one
([[prop-weyl-orbit-of-the-highest-weight-gives-extremal-weights-with-multiplicity-one]]).
The Borel–Weil convention there uses the line with character $-\rho$;
its section module has highest weight $-w_0\rho=\rho$.

[F4] PBW gives exact induction from $\mathfrak b$ and identifies Verma
modules with negative-root monomials. The central character of
$M(\lambda)$ is $\chi_{\lambda+\rho}$, and two such shifted characters
coincide exactly on a Weyl orbit
([[def-verma-module]],
[[thm-pbw-ordered-monomial-basis-for-the-enveloping-algebra]],
[[thm-harish-chandra-isomorphism-for-the-center]]).

[F5] The exact associated-bundle functor, its PBW identification, the
finite-$G$ tensor identity with horizontal constant factor, and its
partial-parabolic descent are proved in
[[lem-pa36-twisted-associated-bundle-and-parabolic-descent]].

[F6] Positive root subgroups, their product factorization, and the
minimal-parabolic root subgroup are those of
[[lem-semisimple-borel-root-factorization]] and
[[lem-semisimple-minimal-parabolic-root-subgroup]]. Finite-dimensional
$\mathfrak{sl}_2$ modules of highest weight $m-1$ integrate to their
polynomial $\mathrm{SL}_2$ representations
([[thm-finite-dimensional-representations-of-sl-two]]).

[F7] The enveloping algebra has primitive coproduct
$\Delta(A)=A\otimes1+1\otimes A$ and antipode $S(A)=-A$, with both
antipode convolution identities proved on all words
([[rem-hopf-algebra-structure-on-the-enveloping-algebra]]).

## Proof

1.1 Write $\kappa=a+ib$ in the real root space. Choose a real number $t$ outside the following finite exceptional set: for each root, exclude a value making $\langle a+tb,\beta^\vee\rangle=0$ unless both pairings already vanish; for each Weyl element, exclude a value making $w(a+tb)=a+tb$ unless $wa=a$ and $wb=b$. Each nonidentical linear equation excludes at most one value. Then the real vector $c=a+tb$ has stabilizer $W_c=W_a\cap W_b=W_\kappa$, and its perpendicular roots are exactly $\Phi_\kappa$. The real closed-chamber stabilizer theorem [F1] therefore identifies $W_\kappa$ with the group generated by these root reflections. The assumed simple system gives $W_\kappa=W_J$. Reduced-word positivity in [F1] gives $m_j=\langle\mu_{j-1},\alpha_j^\vee\rangle\in\mathbb Z_{>0}$: the inverse prefix sends $\alpha_j$ to a positive root. For completeness, $s_i$ permutes the positive roots other than $\alpha_i$, so $s_i\rho=\rho-\alpha_i$ and $\langle\rho,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle=1$. Each positive coroot is a nonzero nonnegative integral sum of simple coroots (apply the positive-root statement to the dual root system), giving the asserted positive integral pairing. The final $\mu_N=w_J\rho$ pairs strictly negatively with every positive coroot of $\Phi_\kappa$. [F1, given, algebra]

2.1 Choose root generators $e_\alpha,f_\alpha,h_\alpha$ with $[e_\alpha,f_\alpha]=h_\alpha$. In $H_{j-1}$ its highest vector $v$ has $\alpha_j$-weight $m_j-1$, because $\langle\kappa,\alpha_j^\vee\rangle=0$ and $\langle\rho,\alpha_j^\vee\rangle=1$. The identity $e_\alpha f_\alpha^m v=m(a-m+1)f_\alpha^{m-1}v$ for a highest vector of weight $a$, proved by commuting $e_\alpha$ past the $m$ factors, shows that $f_{\alpha_j}^{m_j}v$ is killed by $e_{\alpha_j}$. Every other simple positive generator commutes with $f_{\alpha_j}$ and kills $v$. Thus this is a highest vector of weight $-\kappa-\rho+\mu_j$, defining $H_j\to H_{j-1}$. On the negative-root PBW model it is multiplication on the right by $f_{\alpha_j}^{m_j}$. The associated graded is multiplication by a nonzero monomial in the polynomial algebra $S(\mathfrak n^-)$, hence injective; taking the highest nonzero degree proves injectivity of the original map. Twisting the rational highest weight adds $\kappa+\rho$, so the same map is $B$-equivariant. This gives the fixed chain. [F4, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 In the highest-weight module $V$ of [F3] put $$V'=\bigoplus_{\mu_N-\xi\notin Q_+}V_\xi,\qquad V''=V/V'.$$ This is a $B$-submodule: raising a weight outside the downset of $\mu_N$ cannot enter that downset, since subtracting a positive root from an element of $Q_+$ stays incompatible with the assumed outside condition in the reverse direction. More explicitly, if $\mu_N-(\xi+\beta)\in Q_+$ for $\beta>0$, then $\mu_N-\xi\in Q_+$, a contradiction. All weights of $V''$ satisfy $\mu_N-\xi\in Q_+$. Its one-dimensional extremal weight line $V_{\mu_N}$ is a $B$-submodule because raising it leaves this downset. [F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Put $\alpha=\alpha_j$, $m=m_j$ and $C_j=H_{j-1}/H_j$. Let $\mathfrak u^-_\alpha$ be the sum of negative root spaces other than $\mathfrak g_{-\alpha}$. It is a Lie subalgebra stable under the $\alpha$-Levi. Ordering its basis before $f_\alpha$ in PBW gives $$C_j\cong U(\mathfrak u^-_\alpha)\otimes \operatorname{span}(v,f_\alpha v,\ldots,f_\alpha^{m-1}v).$$ Indeed $\operatorname{im}H_j$ has exactly the basis with final $f_\alpha$ exponent at least $m$. The last factor is the $m$-dimensional simple $\alpha$-module. Positive root spaces other than $\mathfrak g_\alpha$ kill this factor: commute them past powers of $f_\alpha$; all resulting roots stay positive and different from $\alpha$, or vanish. Therefore this basis identifies $C_j$ with the parabolic induction from that finite-dimensional $\mathfrak p_\alpha$-module. In particular $\pi(e_\alpha)$ and $\pi(f_\alpha)$ are locally nilpotent on $C_j$: the adjoint $\alpha$-action on each finite PBW degree of $U(\mathfrak u^-_\alpha)$ is locally finite, and its tensor with the last factor is a sum of finite-dimensional $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ modules. [F4, F6, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 Suppose a weight $\xi$ of $V''$ has $\chi_{-\kappa+\xi}=\chi_{-\kappa+\mu_N}$. By [F4] and the $W$-invariance of the sign change, $\kappa-\xi\in W(\kappa-\mu_N)=W(\kappa-\rho)$, where the last equality uses $w_J\kappa=\kappa$. Choose $w$ with $w(\kappa-\xi)=\kappa-\rho$. Then $$\kappa-w\kappa=\rho-w\xi\in Q_+,$$ since $w\xi$ is a weight of $V$ and lies below $\rho$ by [F3]. By [F2] the difference is zero, so $w\kappa=\kappa$ and $w\in W_\kappa=W_J$. Thus $\xi=w^{-1}\rho\in W_J\mu_N$. For a strictly antidominant weight $\mu_N$ in this subsystem, $v\mu_N-\mu_N\in Q_+$ for every $v\in W_J$: telescope a reduced word, whose simple-root pairings with $\mu_N$ are negative and whose prefix roots are positive by [F1]. Consequently $\xi-\mu_N\in Q_+$, whereas $\mu_N-\xi\in Q_+$ by step 2.2. The pointed root cone forces $\xi=\mu_N$. Thus all other weights of $V''$ have central characters different from the distinguished one. This is separation for arbitrary complex antidominant $\kappa$, using no real-chamber restriction. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 Remove the integral character $\rho$ from the rational $B$-action on $C_j$. Its infinitesimal action becomes $\pi+\kappa$, and all torus weights are integral, beginning with $\mu_{j-1}-\rho$. This action extends to a rational $P_\alpha$-action. Explicitly keep the torus weight action and the existing positive root groups, and define the additional negative root group by $x_{-\alpha}(t)=\exp(t\pi(f_\alpha))$. This exponential is a finite sum on each vector by step 3.1. On finite PBW-degree subspaces, the Levi $\mathrm{SL}_2$ relations hold by [F6]; the torus action agrees because its $\alpha$-weight is that of $\pi$, as $\kappa(h_\alpha)=0$. The other torus characters are integral and hence rational. The non-Levi positive nilradical acts locally nilpotently and its conjugation relations with the Levi follow by exponentiating the Lie brackets; the finite Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff sums agree with its root-group factorization [F6]. These actions are locally finite: commuting a positive non-Levi root generator through a PBW word never increases its $\mathfrak u^-_\alpha$ degree, and the Levi preserves that degree. Each bounded-degree tensor the last finite factor is finite-dimensional and stable under $\mathfrak p_\alpha$. Hence all the displayed matrix coefficients are regular and glue to the rational parabolic action. Covariance with $\pi(\mathfrak g)$ follows on the root generators by the same bracket/exponential calculation. This is a twisted $(\mathfrak g,P_\alpha)$-module of twist $\kappa$. [F4, F6, step 3.1, algebra]

4.2 Induce $\mathbb C_{-\kappa-\rho}\otimes V''$ from $\mathfrak b$. By exactness of induction, its distinguished weight line induces the submodule $H_N$. The quotient has a finite Verma filtration with one factor $M(-\kappa-\rho+\xi)$ for each other weight, counted with multiplicity: order the finitely many weight spaces from higher to lower and refine within each torus weight space; the positive nilradical raises weights and the torus acts diagonally. Choose a central element $z$ whose value $c_0$ on the distinguished character differs from its values $c_i$ on all the other characters. Such $z$ exists: each forbidden equality is a proper linear hyperplane in the center, and a finite union of proper linear subspaces cannot cover a complex vector space (restrict to the finite span of elements individually separating the finitely many pairs). Let $g(t)=\prod_i(t-c_i)$ with repetition along the quotient filtration. The product $g(z)$ annihilates that quotient, because each factor kills its corresponding successive Verma factor; all factors commute. On $H_N$ it is the nonzero scalar $g(c_0)$. Therefore $g(z)/g(c_0)$ maps the induced module onto $H_N$ and is the identity on $H_N$, giving a projection. It intertwines the rational twisted $B$-action because $z$ is invariant under the adjoint action of connected $B$. This is an actual module projection; no $\mathcal O_X$-linearity is asserted for central operators on arbitrary differential modules. [F4, step 2.2, step 3.2, algebra]

5.1 Apply [F5] to the exact chain in step 2.1. The PBW identity gives $\mathcal E_0=\mathcal D_\kappa\otimes\mathcal O_X(\rho)$. Descent of step 4.1 gives $\mathcal E_{j-1}/\mathcal E_j= \mathcal O_X(\rho)\otimes p_\alpha^\dagger N_j$, with $N_j=\mathsf E^{P_\alpha}_\kappa(C_j)$. These modules are coherent: $\mathcal E_0$ is locally a rank-one free differential module, and the PBW filtered-noetherian argument makes its differential submodules $\mathcal E_j$ and quotients coherent. Locally the quotient has the form $\mathcal O(-1)\boxtimes N_j$; finite differential generators on the two affine fiber charts involve finitely many coefficients in $N_j$. Their base-differential span must be $N_j$, since vertical differentiation creates no new coefficients. The partial TDO is locally noetherian by the same PBW argument, so $N_j$ is coherent. [F4, F5, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Composing induction of $V\twoheadrightarrow V''$ with the projection of step 4.2 gives a surjection of twisted modules $$M(-\kappa-\rho)\otimes V\twoheadrightarrow H_N.$$ Here the tensor identity is the map $u\otimes(c\otimes v)\mapsto \sum(u_{(1)}\otimes c)\otimes u_{(2)}v$, with inverse obtained by inserting the antipode in the second enveloping factor. The coproduct and antipode from [F7] check on generators that both maps respect the $\mathfrak b$ relations, are mutual inverses, and commute with the twisted rational $B$-actions. Apply the exact functor [F5]. Its PBW and tensor identities give $\mathcal D_\kappa\otimes_{\mathbb C}V\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal E_N$ with differential action only on $\mathcal D_\kappa$. The sections $1\otimes v_i$ for a finite basis of $V$ are global differential generators. This proves finite global generation and completes all chain assertions, including the empty-word case. Choice enters through the inherited highest-weight and Weyl suppliers and the permitted bases. [F4, F5, F7, step 4.2, algebra] ∎
