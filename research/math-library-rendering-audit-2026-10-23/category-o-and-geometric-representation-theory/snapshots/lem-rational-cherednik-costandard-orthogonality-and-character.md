## Statement

Let $D=S(\mathfrak h^*)\rtimes\mathbb CW$ and let $E$ be irreducible, with $S(\mathfrak h^*)_{>0}$ acting by zero. The Euler-locally-finite part $\nabla_c(E)$ of $\operatorname{Hom}_D(H_{1,c},E)$ lies in $\mathcal O_c$, has simple socle $L(E)$ once, and satisfies $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O}(\Delta(E),\nabla(F))=\delta_{EF}\mathbb C$ and $\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathcal O}(\Delta(E),\nabla(F))=0$. Moreover $[\nabla(E)]=[\Delta(E)]$ in $K_0(\mathcal O_c)$, without a real-reflection or self-duality assumption.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite complex reflection group $W$ on $\mathfrak h$ and an arbitrary conjugacy-invariant parameter $c$, with the current minus-sign Cherednik normalization.

[F1] PBW identifies $H$ as free over $D$ on the left and identifies standards with $S(\mathfrak h^*)\otimes E$ ([[thm-rational-cherednik-pbw-theorem]], [[def-rational-cherednik-standard-module]]).

[F2] The Euler/finite-length proof supplies $[\mathrm{eu},x]=x$, $[\mathrm{eu},y]=-y$, scalar $a_E$ on singular $E$, finite weight spaces for objects, and unique simple heads for standards indexed by irreducible $W$-modules ([[lem-rational-cherednik-category-o-has-finite-length]]).

## Proof

1.1 Write $H=H_{1,c}$, $X=S(\mathfrak h^*)$, $Y=S(\mathfrak h)$, $D=X\rtimes\mathbb CW$, and $\Sigma=\{a_F:F\in\operatorname{Irr}W\}$. Regard $E$ as a $D$-module with positive-degree $X$ acting by zero. The full coinduced module is $N(E)=\operatorname{Hom}_D(H,E)$, with $(hf)(u)=f(uh)$. PBW identifies it with the product over $d\ge0$ of $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb C}(S^d\mathfrak h,E)$. On the degree-$d$ piece, $\mathrm{eu}$ acts by $a_E+d$: for $u\in S^d\mathfrak h$, one has $u\mathrm{eu}=(\mathrm{eu}+d)u$; evaluating a $D$-linear map kills the left $x_i$ in $\sum_i x_i y_i$ and evaluates the central $z$ on $E$ as $a_E$. These eigenvalues are distinct as $d$ varies. Therefore the Euler-locally-finite part is exactly their direct sum, denoted $\nabla(E)$. It is $H$-stable: $y$ lowers degree, $x$ raises degree, and $W$ preserves degree. In particular $Y$ acts locally nilpotently. Its Euler-graded $W$-space in degree $a_E+d$ is $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb C}(S^d\mathfrak h,E)\cong S^d(\mathfrak h^*)\otimes E$, exactly that of $\Delta(E)$. [F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 We prove finite generation of $\nabla(E)$. Any nonzero module with locally nilpotent $Y$ and locally finite Euler action has a singular irreducible $W$-submodule: start with a finite $Y$-orbit, take the common kernel of the commuting nilpotents, and then an irreducible $W$-submodule of its singular space by finite-group averaging. Its Euler scalar lies in $\Sigma$. Consequently $d(M)=\sum_{\beta\in\Sigma}\dim M[\beta]$ is positive on every nonzero subquotient with finite weight spaces, and is additive in exact sequences; generalized Euler spaces are exact by taking the appropriate finite eigencomponent of a preimage. This integer is finite for $\nabla(E)$ by step 1.1. Thus every strict chain of its submodules has length at most $d(\nabla(E))$; taking maximal or minimal proper submodules and iterating gives a finite composition series. Every simple subquotient is generated by a singular irreducible $W$-module, so is a quotient of some standard and belongs to $\mathcal O_c$. An extension of finite $X$-modules is finite: lift finitely many quotient generators and add finitely many submodule generators. Induction along this composition series proves that $\nabla(E)$ is finite over $X$, so it belongs to $\mathcal O_c$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The arbitrary-algebra coinduction adjunction is explicit: maps $M\to\operatorname{Hom}_D(H,F)$ correspond by evaluation at $1$ to $D$-maps $M\to F$, with inverse $m\mapsto(u\mapsto f(um))$. Compute Ext using the finite Koszul resolution $P_i=H\otimes_{\mathbb CW}(\Lambda^i\mathfrak h\otimes E)$, augmented onto $\Delta(E)$, with differential $u\otimes(y_1\wedge\cdots\wedge y_i\otimes e)\mapsto\sum_j(-1)^{j-1}uy_j\otimes(y_1\wedge\cdots\widehat y_j\cdots\wedge y_i\otimes e)$. The commuting $y_j$ give differential square zero, and the formula is $W$-equivariant, so it is well-defined. Each $\Lambda^i\mathfrak h\otimes E$ is a summand of a finite free $\mathbb CW$-module by averaging, so $P_i$ is a finite projective $H$-module. PBW identifies its restriction to $D$ with $D\otimes_{\mathbb CW}(Y\otimes\Lambda^i\mathfrak h\otimes E)$, whose differential is the ordinary polynomial Koszul operator $d=\sum_j y_j\iota_j$. On polynomial degree $p$ and exterior degree $i$ with $p+i>0$, the operator $h=(\sum_j\varepsilon_j\partial_{y_j})/(p+i)$ satisfies $dh+hd=1$, by the identities between exterior multiplication $\varepsilon_j$ and contraction $\iota_j$. It is $W$-equivariant and extends $D$-linearly. In total degree zero, augmentation projects to $D\otimes_{\mathbb CW}E=\operatorname{Res}_D\Delta(E)$. Thus this augmented restricted complex is explicitly contractible, proving exactness of the finite $H$-projective resolution. Adjunction identifies its Hom complex into $N(F)$ with this $D$-Hom complex into $F$, so $\operatorname{Hom}_H(\Delta(E),N(F))=\operatorname{Hom}_W(E,F)=\delta_{EF}\mathbb C$ and $\operatorname{Ext}^1_H(\Delta(E),N(F))=0$. No infinite free module or arbitrary choice principle is used. Any map from an Euler-locally-finite module into $N(F)$ lands in $\nabla(F)$ by step 1.1. For an extension $0\to\nabla(F)\to U\to\Delta(E)\to0$ in $\mathcal O_c$, push out to $N(F)$. The vanishing gives a retraction $U\to N(F)$ extending the inclusion of $\nabla(F)$; its image is Euler-locally-finite and hence lies in $\nabla(F)$. This splits the original extension and proves the stated orthogonality in $\mathcal O_c$. [F1, F2, step 2.1, algebra, step 1.1]

4.1 A simple submodule $L(F)$ of $\nabla(E)$ gives a nonzero map $\Delta(F)\to\nabla(E)$, forcing $F=E$ by step 3.1. Finite length ensures a nonzero socle, and its multiplicity is at most $\dim\operatorname{Hom}(\Delta(E),\nabla(E))=1$. Thus its socle is $L(E)$ once. This argument uses neither a real reflection group nor a simple-preserving duality. [F1, F2, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Euler-graded $W$-classes are additive on exact sequences, and step 1.1 gives identical such characters for $\Delta(E)$ and $\nabla(E)$. The simple characters are linearly independent: in a finite nonzero integral relation choose a label $F$ of minimal $\operatorname{Re}(a_F)$ among the nonzero coefficients. At Euler weight $a_F$, labels with larger real part cannot contribute, labels with the same real part but a different imaginary part have no such weight, and the remaining lowest spaces are the distinct irreducible $W$-modules. Their classes are independent because averaging splits finite $W$-modules into their irreducibles. Thus the coefficient of each such lowest label must be zero, a contradiction. It follows that $[\nabla(E):L(F)]=[\Delta(E):L(F)]$ for every $F$, and hence $[\nabla(E)]=[\Delta(E)]$ in $K_0(\mathcal O_c)$. This asserts equality of composition multiplicities, not an isomorphism of these modules. [F1, F2, step 4.1, algebra, step 1.1] ∎
