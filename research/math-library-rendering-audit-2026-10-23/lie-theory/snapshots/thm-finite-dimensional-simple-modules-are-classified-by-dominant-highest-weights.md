## Statement

Every finite-dimensional simple $\mathfrak g$-module is a cyclic highest-weight module whose highest weight is dominant integral, and for each dominant integral weight

$$\lambda=\sum_{i=1}^r m_i\omega_i \qquad (m_i\in \mathbb N_0),$$

there exists, up to isomorphism, a unique finite-dimensional simple module $L(\lambda)$ with highest weight $\lambda$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A complex semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ with chosen simple roots $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_r$ and fundamental weights $\omega_1,\dots,\omega_r$.

[F1] [[lem-finite-semisimple-pbw-and-highest-weight-construction]] constructs the Verma module and its unique simple quotient $L(\lambda)$; for dominant integral $\lambda$ that quotient is finite-dimensional, and every finite-dimensional simple module occurs uniquely this way.

[F2] [[def-fundamental-weights-for-a-chosen-simple-root-system]] expresses a dominant integral weight uniquely as $\sum_i m_i\omega_i$ with $m_i\in\mathbb N_0$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The finite PBW and highest-weight construction in [F1] proves that every finite-dimensional simple $\mathfrak g$-module is generated by a highest-weight vector of a unique dominant integral weight $\lambda$, and is isomorphic to $L(\lambda)$. This is a cyclic highest-weight module in the stated sense. [F1, given]

2.1 By [F2], the weight from step 1.1 is exactly $\lambda=\sum_i m_i\omega_i$ with $m_i\in\mathbb N_0$. The rank-one integrality and nonnegativity needed for this assertion are established in [F1]. [F1, F2, step 1.1]

3.1 Conversely, for each $\lambda$ of the form in step 2.1, [F1] constructs a finite-dimensional simple $L(\lambda)$ and proves uniqueness up to isomorphism. Together with step 1.1 this gives both directions of the classification. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
