## Statement

For every finite GCM and $i\ne j$, the maximal-ideal quotient satisfies $(\operatorname{ad}e_i)^{1-a_{ij}}e_j=0$ and $(\operatorname{ad}f_i)^{1-a_{ij}}f_j=0$. This asserts vanishing, without yet asserting generation of the defining ideal.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The standard simple generators in g(A), and i≠j.

[F1] A negative vector killed by all opposite simple generators is zero. ([[lem-kac-moody-opposite-simple-centralizer-vanishes]]).

[F2] The algebra $\mathfrak g(A)$ is the quotient of $\widetilde{\mathfrak g}(A)$ by the largest Cartan-disjoint ideal and retains the generator names $h,e_i,f_i$. ([[def-kac-moody-algebra-associated-to-a-gcm]]).

[F3] Before the quotient, the generators satisfy $[h,h']=0$, $[h,e_i]=\alpha_i(h)e_i$, $[h,f_i]=-\alpha_i(h)f_i$, and $[e_i,f_j]=\delta_{ij}h_i$. ([[def-contragredient-lie-algebra-before-the-maximal-ideal-quotient]]).

## Proof

1.1 The relations of F3 descend through the quotient of F2. For operators $E,F,H$ with $[E,F]=H$ and $[H,F]=-2F$, the identity $[E,F^m]=mF^{m-1}(H-m+1)$ follows from $[E,F^{m+1}]=[E,F^m]F+F^mH$ and $HF=F(H-2)$, starting at $m=1$. Take the adjoint operators of $e_i,f_i,h_i$. On $v=f_j$, $Ev=0$ and $Hv=-a_{ij}v$. For $m=1-a_{ij}$, the identity gives $EF^m v=m(-a_{ij}-m+1)F^{m-1}v=0$. [F2, F3, given]

2.1 For $k\notin\{i,j\}$, $\operatorname{ad}e_k$ commutes with $\operatorname{ad}f_i$ and kills $f_j$, so kills $F^mv$. For $k=j$, it commutes with $F$ and sends $f_j$ to $h_j$, so sends $F^mv$ to $F^mh_j$. Now $Fh_j=a_{ji}f_i$ and $F^2h_j=0$. If $m\ge2$ this vanishes; if $m=1$, $a_{ij}=0$ and the symmetric-zero axiom gives $a_{ji}=0$. Thus all $e_k$ kill the negative Serre vector. [F2, F3, given, step 1.1]

3.1 F1 kills this vector in $\mathfrak n^-$. The sign-changing involution descending in F2 proves the positive relation with the same exponent. At no point has an ideal been asserted to be generated by these vectors. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
