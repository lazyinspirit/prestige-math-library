## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. The centralizer of every torus $S$ in a compact
connected Lie group $G$ is connected; in particular $C_G(T)=T$ for a maximal
torus $T$. Consequently the Weyl group $W(G,T)=N_G(T)/T$ is finite and acts
faithfully on $T$ and on its Lie algebra.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Assume the Axiom of Choice, a compact connected Lie group $G$ with Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, a torus $S\le G$, and a maximal torus $T\le G$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is [[def-axiom-of-choice]]; it enters through the Haar-based metric of [L3] and the structure theory of [L1].

[L1] Every element of a compact connected Lie group lies in a maximal torus, every torus lies in a maximal torus, and a compact connected abelian Lie group is isomorphic to $\mathfrak t/\Lambda\cong(S^1)^r$ with surjective exponential map ([[thm-every-element-of-a-compact-connected-lie-group-lies-in-a-maximal-torus]], [[thm-maximal-tori-exist-in-compact-lie-groups]], [[thm-structure-of-a-compact-connected-abelian-lie-group]]).

[L2] A closed subgroup of a finite-dimensional real Lie group is an embedded Lie subgroup with a Lie algebra, and its exponential map is a local diffeomorphism at zero; the closure of a subgroup is a subgroup, the closure of a connected set is connected, a closed subset of a compact space is compact, a compact subset of a Hausdorff space is closed, and a Lie group whose Lie algebra is zero is discrete ([[thm-cartans-closed-subgroup-theorem]], [[cor-the-exponential-map-is-a-local-diffeomorphism-at-zero]], [[thm-closure-of-a-connected-set]], [[lem-closed-subset-of-a-compact-space-is-compact]], [[thm-compact-subset-of-a-hausdorff-space-is-closed]]).

[L3] $G$ carries a bi-invariant Riemannian metric whose value at the identity is a positive-definite inner product on $\mathfrak g$ invariant under every $\operatorname{Ad}_g$; consequently $\langle[X,U],V\rangle=-\langle U,[X,V]\rangle$ for all $X,U,V\in\mathfrak g$ ([[prop-compact-lie-groups-admit-bi-invariant-riemannian-metrics]]).

[L4] If $H$ is a closed normal subgroup of a finite-dimensional real Lie group $K$, then $K/H$ is a Lie group with Lie algebra canonically $\operatorname{Lie}(K)/\operatorname{Lie}(H)$ ([[thm-quotient-by-a-closed-normal-subgroup-is-a-lie-group]]). For a closed subgroup $H\le K$, its Lie algebra is $$ \operatorname{Lie}(H)=\{X\in\operatorname{Lie}(K):\exp(tX)\in H\text{ for every }t\in\mathbb R\} $$ by the construction in [[thm-cartans-closed-subgroup-theorem]].

[L5] The rationals are countable and the reals uncountable ([[thm-rationals-countable]], [[thm-r-uncountable]]).

[L6] Exponentials commute with Lie-group homomorphisms, and the differential of the adjoint representation is $\operatorname{ad}$ ([[prop-exponential-map-is-natural-for-lie-group-homomorphisms]], [[thm-the-differential-of-adjoint-is-ad]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $g\in C_G(S)$ and let $A$ be the closure in $G$ of the subgroup generated by $S$ and $g$, that is, of $\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb Z}g^nS$. Then $A$ is closed by definition, compact because $G$ is compact, abelian and a subgroup by [L2], and hence by [L2] an embedded Lie subgroup: a compact abelian Lie group. Its identity component $A^0$ is a compact connected abelian Lie group, hence by [L1] a torus, and $A^0$ is open in $A$ because identity components of Lie groups are open. [L1, L2]

1.2 If $a\in G$ lies in a maximal torus $M$, then by the surjectivity of the exponential map of the torus $M$ in [L1] there is $X\in\operatorname{Lie}(M)\subseteq\mathfrak g$ with $a=\exp(X)$; in particular every element of $A$ has this form. [L1]

1.3 A torus $U$ has surjective $N$-th power maps: write $u=\exp V$ by [L1] and take $\exp(V/N)$. It also has a dense cyclic generator, as follows. Use [L1] to identify $U$ with $\mathbb R^r/\mathbb Z^r$. Choose $x_1,\ldots,x_r$ so that $1,x_1,\ldots,x_r$ are rationally independent: at each of the finitely many stages the rational span of the previous choices is countable (enumerate rational tuples), so [L5] supplies a real outside it. Let $H$ be the closure of the subgroup generated by $x+\mathbb Z^r$. If $H\ne U$, [L2, L4] make $U/H$ a nontrivial compact connected abelian Lie group, hence a positive-dimensional torus by [L1]. Projection to one circle factor gives a nontrivial smooth homomorphism $\chi:U\to\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ vanishing on $H$. By [L6], $\chi(v+\mathbb Z^r)=\ell(v)+\mathbb Z$ for the real-linear differential $\ell$; since each coordinate vector represents zero in $U$, its coefficients $m_j=\ell(e_j)$ are integers. At least one is nonzero, since otherwise naturality and surjectivity of the exponential would make $\chi$ trivial. But $\chi(x)=0$ says $\sum m_jx_j\in\mathbb Z$, contradicting the chosen independence. Thus $H=U$. For $r=0$ the identity generates $U$. [L1, L2, L4, L5, L6]

2.1 The set $\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb Z}g^nA^0$ is an open subgroup of $A$ containing $\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb Z}g^nS$, whose closure is $A$; an open subgroup is closed and its cosets partition $A$, so this union is all of $A$. Hence $A/A^0$ is generated by the coset $gA^0$, and being a discrete compact group it is finite, of some order $N$; consequently $g^N\in A^0$. [L2, step 1.1]

3.1 There exists $a\in A$ whose powers are dense in $A$: since $A^0$ is a torus, step 1.3 supplies an element $a_0$ with dense powers; let $b\in A$ represent a generator of the cyclic group $A/A^0$ of order $N$ from step 2.1; the $N$-th power map of the torus $A^0$ is surjective by step 1.3, so there is $c\in A^0$ with $c^N=b^{-N}a_0$; then $(bc)^N=b^Nc^N=a_0$, so the closure of the powers of $bc$ contains the dense powers of $a_0$ and hence $A^0$, and $(bc)^k\in b^kA^0$ for every $k$, so it contains a representative of each coset of $A/A^0$; therefore the closure of the cyclic subgroup generated by $a:=bc$ is all of $A$. [L1, step 2.1, step 1.3]

4.1 Write $a=\exp(X)$ by step 1.2 and let $S'$ be the closure of $\{\exp(tX):t\in\mathbb R\}$; this is a compact connected abelian subgroup of $G$ by [L2] and hence a torus, and it contains $A$: indeed $a^k=\exp(kX)$ for every integer $k$, so the cyclic subgroup generated by $a$ lies in $\{\exp(tX)\}$, and taking closures gives $A\subseteq S'$; in particular $S'$ contains $S$ and $g$. [L2, step 1.2, step 3.1]

5.1 If $x\in C_G(S)$ and $S'$ is constructed for $x$ as in step 4.1, then $S'$ is a torus containing $S$ and $x$; conversely every torus containing $S$ lies in $C_G(S)$, since it is abelian. Hence $C_G(S)=\bigcup\{S':S'\text{ a torus}, S\subseteq S'\}$, a union of connected sets all containing the nonempty connected set $S$, which is therefore connected. [L2, step 4.1]

5.2 For a maximal torus $T$, applying step 4.1 to $x\in C_G(T)$ produces a torus $S'$ containing $T$ and $x$; maximality of $T$ forces $S'=T$, so $x\in T$. Hence $C_G(T)=T$; in particular $\mathfrak c_{\mathfrak g}(\mathfrak t)=\mathfrak t$ for $\mathfrak t=\operatorname{Lie}(T)$, because if $X$ centralizes $\mathfrak t$, then $\operatorname{Ad}(\exp(tX))H=H$ for $H\in\mathfrak t$: by [L6] this curve satisfies the linear equation $v\prime=\operatorname{ad}_Xv$ with constant solution $H$. Naturality and the surjectivity of $\exp_T$ show $\exp(tX)\in C_G(T)=T$, so differentiating gives $X\in\mathfrak t$. [L1, L6, step 4.1]

6.1 The normalizer $N:=N_G(T)$ is closed, because it is the intersection, over $t\in T$, of the closed conditions $gtg^{-1}\in T$ and $g^{-1}tg\in T$; hence $N$ is a compact Lie subgroup by [L2] and contains $T$ as a closed normal subgroup, so $N/T$ is a compact Lie group by [L4]. Its Lie algebra is $\mathfrak n/\mathfrak t$, where $\mathfrak n=\operatorname{Lie}(N)$; for $X\in\mathfrak n$ and $H\in\mathfrak t$ the curve $t\mapsto\operatorname{Ad}(\exp tX)H$ lies in $\mathfrak t$, so differentiating at $0$ gives $[X,H]\in\mathfrak t$, and then for every $H'\in\mathfrak t$ the invariance identity of [L3] gives $\langle[X,H],H'\rangle=\langle X,[H,H']\rangle=0$ because $\mathfrak t$ is abelian; positive definiteness yields $[X,H]=0$, so $X\in\mathfrak c_{\mathfrak g}(\mathfrak t)=\mathfrak t$ by step 5.2. Hence $\mathfrak n=\mathfrak t$ and the Lie algebra of $N/T$ is zero; by [L2] the group $N/T$ is discrete, and being compact it is finite. Thus $W(G,T)=N_G(T)/T$ is finite. [L2, L3, L4, L6, step 5.2]

7.1 The action of $W(G,T)$ on $T$ has kernel $\{gT:g\in N,\ gtg^{-1}=t\text{ for all }t\in T\}=C_G(T)/T=T/T=\{T\}$ by step 5.2, so it is faithful; if $gT$ acts trivially on $\mathfrak t=\operatorname{Lie}(T)$ then $\operatorname{Ad}(g)$ fixes $\mathfrak t$ pointwise, hence $g\exp(X)g^{-1}=\exp(\operatorname{Ad}(g)X)=\exp(X)$ for all $X\in\mathfrak t$ and $g$ centralizes $T$, so $gT=T$; thus the action on the Lie algebra is faithful as well. The Axiom of Choice entered through the cited metric and structure theory. [A1, L1, L6, step 5.2, step 6.1] ∎
