## Statement

Let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra with Killing form $B$. A **Cartan subalgebra** here means a maximal toral subalgebra: a maximal abelian subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ all of whose adjoint operators are diagonalizable. Such subalgebras exist, and every one is self-centralizing. There is a decomposition
$$\mathfrak g=\mathfrak h\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi}\mathfrak g_\alpha,\qquad \mathfrak g_\alpha=\{x:[h,x]=\alpha(h)x\text{ for all }h\in\mathfrak h\},$$
where the nonzero weights $\Phi$ are finite, span $\mathfrak h^*$, and each root space has dimension one. The form is nondegenerate on $\mathfrak h$, pairs $\mathfrak g_\alpha$ perfectly with $\mathfrak g_{-\alpha}$, and all other weight-space pairings are zero. Define $H_\alpha\in\mathfrak h$ by $B(H_\alpha,h)=\alpha(h)$. Then $c_\alpha=\alpha(H_\alpha)\ne0$, and $h_\alpha=2H_\alpha/c_\alpha$ admits root vectors $e_\alpha,f_\alpha$ with $[e_\alpha,f_\alpha]=h_\alpha$, $[h_\alpha,e_\alpha]=2e_\alpha$, $[h_\alpha,f_\alpha]=-2f_\alpha$.

The real span $\mathfrak h_{\mathbb R}$ of the $h_\alpha$ is a real form of $\mathfrak h$ on which $B$ is positive definite. On $E=\mathfrak h_{\mathbb R}^*$ with the dual form, $\Phi$ is a finite reduced crystallographic root system and $h_\alpha$ is its coroot. In particular the preceding abstract finite-Weyl conventions apply. For nonproportional roots $\alpha,\beta$, the root spaces with roots $\beta+k\alpha$ form one simple rank-one module: the integers occurring are a consecutive interval, reflection in $\alpha$ reverses it, and raising or lowering between adjacent spaces is nonzero with the positive $fe,ef$ coefficients of $V_m$. The adjoint operators of all root vectors are nilpotent. For any positive system, normalized triples $e_i,f_i,h_i$ of its simple roots generate $\mathfrak g$, and the $h_i$ form a basis of $\mathfrak h$. All assertions include $\mathfrak g=0$ and use no AC.

**Given:** The Lie, solvability and Killing conventions of [[def-finite-semisimple-lie-algebra-and-symmetric-adjoint-action]]. A derivation $D$ satisfies $D[x,y]=[Dx,y]+[x,Dy]$.

[F1] Killing nondegeneracy, invariance, the nilpotent-operator Engel theorem and solvability conclusion are [[lem-finite-lie-engel-trace-criterion-and-killing-nondegeneracy]].

## Proof

1.1 The center of $\mathfrak g$ is an abelian ideal, so is zero. Thus $\operatorname{ad}:\mathfrak g\to\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak g)$ is injective. Let $\mathcal D$ be its finite-dimensional derivation algebra; $[D,\operatorname{ad}_x]=\operatorname{ad}_{Dx}$ makes $\operatorname{ad}\mathfrak g$ an ideal in it. The invariant trace form $(D,T)\mapsto\operatorname{tr}_{\mathfrak g}(DT)$ restricts to the nondegenerate Killing form there. Consequently $\mathcal D=\operatorname{ad}\mathfrak g\oplus J$ as vector spaces, where $J$ is the perpendicular complement. Trace cyclicity and the ideal property make $J$ an ideal as well. Two complementary ideals commute, since their bracket lies in their intersection. Hence $D\in J$ satisfies $\operatorname{ad}_{Dx}=[D,\operatorname{ad}_x]=0$ for every $x$, whence $D=0$. Every derivation is therefore inner. [F1, given, algebra]

1.2 Choose a toral subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ of maximal dimension; the zero subalgebra starts the finite dimension search. Commuting diagonalizable operators admit simultaneous eigenspaces: successively decompose by a finite basis of operators, each preserving the earlier eigenspaces. Write $\mathfrak g=\bigoplus_\lambda\mathfrak g_\lambda$ for these joint weights, with $\mathfrak g_0=C_{\mathfrak g}(\mathfrak h)$. Invariance gives $(\lambda(h)+\mu(h))B(x,y)=0$ for $x\in\mathfrak g_\lambda,y\in\mathfrak g_\mu$. Thus distinct opposite-weight pairs are the only possible nonzero pairings, and F1 makes $B|_{\mathfrak g_0}$ nondegenerate. [F1, F3, given, algebra]

2.1 For the generalized eigenspaces of $\operatorname{ad}_x$, the derivation identity gives $$(\operatorname{ad}_x-\lambda-\mu)^N[y,z]=\sum_{j=0}^N\binom Nj[(\operatorname{ad}_x-\lambda)^j y,(\operatorname{ad}_x-\mu)^{N-j}z].$$ If $y,z$ have generalized weights $\lambda,\mu$, a sufficiently large $N$ makes this zero. Thus their bracket has generalized weight $\lambda+\mu$. The operator $S$ acting by $\lambda$ on each such space is therefore a derivation. By step 1.1 it is $\operatorname{ad}_{x_s}$, and $x_n=x-x_s$ has nilpotent adjoint action and commutes with $x_s$. If $y$ commutes with $x$, its adjoint operator preserves the generalized eigenspaces and hence commutes with $S$; center-freeness gives $[y,x_s]=[y,x_n]=0$. This constructs internal semisimple and nilpotent parts and their centralizer property. [step 1.1, F3, given, algebra]

3.1 For $x\in\mathfrak g_0$, step 2.1 puts $x_s,x_n$ in $\mathfrak g_0$. The commuting diagonalizable operators of $\mathfrak h$ and $x_s$ are simultaneously diagonalizable, so maximality forces $x_s\in\mathfrak h$. Therefore $\operatorname{ad}_x|_{\mathfrak g_0}=\operatorname{ad}_{x_n}|_{\mathfrak g_0}$ is nilpotent. F1 makes the adjoint image of $\mathfrak g_0$ solvable, and its central kernel is abelian; the derived series then shows $\mathfrak g_0$ itself solvable. F2 triangularizes its representation on all of $\mathfrak g$. The commutators are strictly upper triangular, so $B([\mathfrak g_0,\mathfrak g_0],\mathfrak g_0)=0$. Nondegeneracy from step 1.2 gives $[\mathfrak g_0,\mathfrak g_0]=0$. Now $\operatorname{ad}_{x_n}$ commutes with every $\operatorname{ad}_y$, $y\in\mathfrak g_0$; their product is nilpotent since one factor is nilpotent. Hence $B(x_n,y)=0$ for all such $y$, and nondegeneracy forces $x_n=0$. Every $x\in\mathfrak g_0$ is thus in $\mathfrak h$, proving self-centralization. This applies to any maximal toral subalgebra. [step 2.1, step 1.2, F1, F2, algebra]

4.1 Call the nonzero weights $\Phi$. Step 1.2 and F1 show $-\alpha\in\Phi$ and give perfect opposite-root pairings, together with nondegeneracy on $\mathfrak h$. Jacobi gives $[\mathfrak g_\alpha,\mathfrak g_\beta]\subseteq\mathfrak g_{\alpha+\beta}$, interpreting absent weights as zero. For $e\in\mathfrak g_\alpha,f\in\mathfrak g_{-\alpha}$, invariance gives $[e,f]=B(e,f)H_\alpha$, since pairing with $h\in\mathfrak h$ gives $B(e,[f,h])=\alpha(h)B(e,f)$. Choose $e,f$ with $B(e,f)\ne0$. If $\alpha(H_\alpha)=0$, the nonzero element $z=[e,f]$ commutes with both $e$ and $f$. Its adjoint action is diagonalizable because $z\in\mathfrak h$. Each eigenvalue space of $\operatorname{ad}_z$ is preserved by $\operatorname{ad}_e,\operatorname{ad}_f$, and the trace of their commutator on that space is both zero and its dimension times the eigenvalue of $\operatorname{ad}_z$. Thus every eigenvalue is zero, contradicting center-freeness and $z\ne0$. Hence $c_\alpha\ne0$. Rescaling $f$ so $B(e,f)=2/c_\alpha$ gives exactly the stated rank-one relations. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 3.1, F1, given, algebra]

5.1 Apply F2 to the adjoint representation of each root triple. Every root value $\beta(h_\alpha)$ is an integer, and $\operatorname{ad}_{e_\alpha},\operatorname{ad}_{f_\alpha}$ are nilpotent. The subspace $$\mathbb C H_\alpha\oplus\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\},\ k\alpha\in\Phi}\mathfrak g_{k\alpha}$$ is stable under the triple: brackets shift $k$ by one, and at opposite weights step 4.1 puts the bracket in $\mathbb C H_\alpha$. Its $h_\alpha$-weights are even and its zero-weight space has dimension one. Every simple summand supplied by F2 therefore has even highest weight and contributes a zero-weight line. There is exactly one summand, and it contains the three-dimensional adjoint triple, which is a copy of $V_2$ by its relations. The entire space is that $V_2$. Thus $\dim\mathfrak g_\alpha=1$ and no integer multiple $k\alpha$ with $|k|>1$ is a root. If $\beta=c\alpha$ is any proportional root, then $h_\beta=h_\alpha/c$, so $2c$ and $2/c$ are integers. Their product is four, hence $c$ is one of $\pm1,\pm2,\pm\tfrac12$. Excluding integer doubles for either root leaves only $\pm1$. [step 4.1, F2, algebra]

6.1 For nonproportional $\alpha,\beta$, the sum of the spaces $\mathfrak g_{\beta+k\alpha}$ is stable under the root triple and has $h_\alpha$-weights $\beta(h_\alpha)+2k$, each of dimension one. By step 5.1 these weights are integers of one parity. In F2's decomposition, any two simple modules of the same parity have a common weight (zero for even parity and one for odd parity), which would give multiplicity at least two. Hence there is exactly one simple summand. Its weights form a consecutive step-two string symmetric about zero. Therefore the roots in this string form a consecutive interval of $k$, reflection $\beta\mapsto\beta-\beta(h_\alpha)\alpha$ reverses the interval, and all adjacent raising/lowering brackets and their positive compositions are exactly those of F2. For proportional roots the reflection interchanges $\alpha,-\alpha$ by step 5.1. [step 5.1, F2, algebra]

7.1 The roots span $\mathfrak h^*$: an element of $\mathfrak h$ annihilated by all roots commutes with every weight space, so is central and zero. Nondegeneracy of $B|_{\mathfrak h}$ implies the $H_\alpha$, and hence the $h_\alpha$, span $\mathfrak h$ over $\mathbb C$. On their real span $\mathfrak h_{\mathbb R}$ all roots take real values, by the integral pairings of step 5.1. If $u$ belongs to both this span and its multiple by $i$, every root value on $u$ is both real and purely imaginary, hence zero; therefore $u=0$. This proves $\mathfrak h=\mathfrak h_{\mathbb R}\oplus i\mathfrak h_{\mathbb R}$ over $\mathbb R$. For $u\in\mathfrak h_{\mathbb R}$ the weight decomposition gives $B(u,u)=\sum_{\beta\in\Phi}\beta(u)^2$, strictly positive for $u\ne0$, and polarization makes $B$ real there. Its real dual form identifies each $\alpha$ with $H_\alpha$ (the real solution of its defining linear equations), so $c_\alpha=(\alpha,\alpha)>0$. Thus $h_\alpha$ is the Euclidean coroot. Spanning, finiteness, reducedness, integrality and reflection stability proved above verify every axiom in F4. [step 1.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, F4, algebra]

8.1 Apply F4 to choose a simple basis $\alpha_i$ and its coroot basis $h_i$. Choose normalized simple root vectors $e_i,f_i$. If a positive root $\beta$ is not simple, its expansion $\beta=\sum n_i\alpha_i$ and positive norm imply $(\beta,\alpha_i)>0$ for some $i$ with $n_i>0$. It is not proportional to that simple root. In its string, the positive $h_i$-weight $\beta(h_i)>0$ cannot be the lowest weight, so $\beta-\alpha_i$ is a root. It remains positive: at least one other simple coefficient is positive and every root's coefficients have one sign. By step 6.1 the bracket $[e_i,\mathfrak g_{\beta-\alpha_i}]$ is the nonzero one-dimensional space $\mathfrak g_\beta$. Induction on positive-root height generates all positive spaces; the negative argument with the $f_i$ generates all negative spaces. The brackets $[e_i,f_i]=h_i$ span $\mathfrak h$. Thus the simple triples generate $\mathfrak g$. If $\mathfrak g=0$, take $\mathfrak h=0$, $\Phi=\varnothing$ and all assertions have their empty meanings. All vector-space choices and inductions above are finite, so AC is not used. [step 5.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, F4, given, algebra] ∎
