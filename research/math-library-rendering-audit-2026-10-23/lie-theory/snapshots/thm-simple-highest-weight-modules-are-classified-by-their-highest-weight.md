## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional complex
semisimple Lie algebra with Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ and a fixed
positive system. Then two finite-dimensional simple highest weight
$\mathfrak g$-modules for this positive system are isomorphic if and only if
their highest weights are equal.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, such $\mathfrak g,\mathfrak h$, a fixed positive system, and finite-dimensional simple highest weight modules $V,W$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed; it enters through the root-space theory used by the cited suppliers ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[L1] A finite-dimensional irreducible module $V\ne0$ has a highest weight $\lambda(V)$: it contains a highest weight vector, and all its weights are $\le\lambda(V)$ for every such highest weight; the module is generated by any of its highest weight vectors ([[lem-every-finite-dimensional-irreducible-representation-has-a-highest-weight-vector]], [[prop-a-finite-dimensional-irreducible-module-is-generated-by-any-highest-weight-vector]], [[lem-highest-weight-modules-have-weights-below-the-top-weight]], [[def-highest-weight-vector-and-highest-weight-module]]).

[L2] The highest weight $\lambda(V)$ is unique: if $\lambda$ and $\lambda'$ both occur as highest weights of $V$, then the relations of [L1] give $\lambda\le\lambda'$ and $\lambda'\le\lambda$, so $\lambda=\lambda'$ by antisymmetry of the root order ([[def-partial-order-on-weights]]).

[L3] Every highest weight of a finite-dimensional irreducible module is dominant integral ([[lem-highest-weight-of-a-finite-dimensional-module-is-dominant-integral]], [[def-integral-dominant-and-strictly-dominant-weights]]).

[L4] $L(\lambda)=M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)/N_\lambda$ is a finite-dimensional simple highest weight module of highest weight $\lambda$ for every dominant integral $\lambda$ ([[lem-a-dominant-cyclic-highest-weight-module-has-a-unique-simple-quotient]], [[thm-finite-dimensionality-of-lambda-highest-weight-simple-modules-for-dominant-integral-lambda]]).

[L5] A finite-dimensional highest weight module $V$ of highest weight $\lambda$ satisfies $f_i^{\,m_i+1}v_\lambda=0$ for $m_i=\langle\lambda,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle$ and its highest weight vector $v_\lambda$; these are exactly the relations defining $M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)$, so the map $M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)\to V$, $u+I_\lambda\mapsto u\cdot v_\lambda$, is a well-defined surjective module map ([[lem-integrability-relations-for-a-dominant-highest-weight]], [[def-dominant-integrable-highest-weight-cyclic-module]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Assume $V$ and $W$ have the same highest weight $\lambda$; then $\lambda$ is dominant integral by [L3], so $L(\lambda)$ exists and is finite dimensional by [L4]. [L3, L4, A1]

1.2 Choose a highest weight vector $v_\lambda$ of $V$; by [L5] the classification relations hold in $V$, so the map $\varphi:M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)\to V$ with $\varphi(u+I_\lambda)=u\cdot v_\lambda$ is a well-defined surjective module map; its kernel $K$ is a submodule of $M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)$ and is proper because $v_\lambda\ne0$. [L5]

1.3 Conversely an isomorphism $\psi:V\to W$ of $\mathfrak g$-modules carries weights to weights bijectively (it intertwines the $\mathfrak h$-action), so the set of weights of $W$ is the image of that of $V$; by [L1] and [L2] each of $V$ and $W$ has a unique highest weight, and uniqueness of the maximum of a finite weight set under the partial order gives $\lambda(V)=\lambda(W)$. [L1, L2]

2.1 The quotient $M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)/K\cong V$ is simple by hypothesis, so $K$ is a maximal proper submodule of $M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)$; by [L4] the unique maximal proper submodule is $N_\lambda$, so $K=N_\lambda$ and $V\cong L(\lambda)$. [L4, step 1.2]

3.1 Hence any two finite-dimensional simple highest weight modules of highest weight $\lambda$ are both isomorphic to $L(\lambda)$, so equal highest weights force isomorphism. [step 2.1]

4.1 Step 1.3 proves that an isomorphism forces equal highest weights and step 3.1 proves that equal highest weights force isomorphism, so the two directions of the equivalence are established. [step 1.3, step 3.1] ∎
