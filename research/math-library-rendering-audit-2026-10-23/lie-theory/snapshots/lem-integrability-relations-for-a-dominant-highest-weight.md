## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional complex
semisimple Lie algebra with Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ and a chosen
positive system with simple roots $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_r$, let
$\lambda$ be dominant integral with $m_i=\langle\lambda,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle$
([[def-integral-dominant-and-strictly-dominant-weights]]), and let $V$ be a
finite-dimensional highest weight module of highest weight $\lambda$ with
highest weight vector $v_\lambda$
([[def-highest-weight-vector-and-highest-weight-module]]). For every simple
root $\alpha_i$ and a lowering vector $f_i\in\mathfrak g_{-\alpha_i}$ with
$[e_i,f_i]=h_{\alpha_i}$ for a suitable $e_i$
([[thm-root-sl-two-triple]]),
$$f_i^{\,m_i+1}v_\lambda=0 .$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, such $\mathfrak g,\mathfrak h$, a dominant integral $\lambda$ with $m_i=\langle\lambda,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle$, a finite-dimensional highest weight module $V$ of highest weight $\lambda$, and for each $i$ a pair $e_i\in\mathfrak g_{\alpha_i}$, $f_i\in\mathfrak g_{-\alpha_i}$ forming, together with $h_{\alpha_i}$, a copy of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed; it enters through the root-space theory supplying [L1] and through [L3] ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[L1] For every $i$, $[e_i,f_i]=h_{\alpha_i}$, $[h_{\alpha_i},e_i]=2e_i$ and $[h_{\alpha_i},f_i]=-2f_i$ ([[thm-root-sl-two-triple]]).

[L2] $v_\lambda\ne0$ is killed by $\mathfrak n^+$ and satisfies $h_{\alpha_i}\cdot v_\lambda=\lambda(h_{\alpha_i})v_\lambda=m_iv_\lambda$ ([[def-highest-weight-vector-and-highest-weight-module]], [[def-integral-dominant-and-strictly-dominant-weights]]).

[L3] A finite-dimensional $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-module is a direct sum of irreducibles, and an irreducible submodule with highest weight $m\ge0$ has dimension $m+1$ and weights $m,m-2,\dots,-m$ ([[thm-finite-dimensional-representations-of-sl-two]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Fix $i$ and consider $W=U(\operatorname{span}\{e_i,f_i,h_{\alpha_i}\})v_\lambda$, the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-submodule of $V$ generated by $v_\lambda$; it is finite dimensional because $V$ is, and $e_i\cdot v_\lambda=0$ while $h_{\alpha_i}\cdot v_\lambda=m_iv_\lambda$ by [L1] and [L2]. [A1, L1, L2]

2.1 By [L3] write $W=\bigoplus_{a=1}^sW_a$ as a direct sum of irreducible $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-submodules, and write $v_\lambda=\sum_av_a$ with $v_a\in W_a$.  Because every $W_a$ is stable under $e_i$ and $h_{\alpha_i}$, uniqueness of the direct sum and step 1.1 give $e_i v_a=0$ and $h_{\alpha_i}v_a=m_iv_a$ for every $a$. [L3, step 1.1]

3.1 For every $a$ with $v_a\ne0$, step 2.1 makes $v_a$ a highest weight vector of the irreducible module $W_a$ with highest weight $m_i$.  By [L3], $f_i^{m_i+1}v_a=0$; the same equality is trivial when $v_a=0$.  Summing over $a$ gives $f_i^{m_i+1}v_\lambda=0$ in $W$. [L3, step 2.1]

4.1 Since $W\subseteq V$, the vanishing of step 3.1 holds in $V$; as $i$ was arbitrary, $f_i^{m_i+1}v_\lambda=0$ for every simple root, which is the assertion. [step 3.1] ∎
