## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\mathfrak g_0$ be a finite-dimensional real
semisimple Lie algebra and let $\theta$ be a Cartan involution of
$\mathfrak g_0$, which exists by
[[thm-existence-of-a-cartan-involution]]
([[def-cartan-involution-of-a-real-semisimple-lie-algebra]]). Then every
Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h_0$ of $\mathfrak g_0$
([[def-cartan-subalgebra-of-a-lie-algebra]]) is carried by an inner
automorphism of $\mathfrak g_0$ onto a $\theta$-stable Cartan subalgebra
([[def-theta-stable-cartan-subalgebra-and-compact-split-parts]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice; a finite-dimensional real semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak g_0$ with Cartan involution $\theta$ and Killing form $B_0$; a Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h_0$ of $\mathfrak g_0$; and the complexification $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak g_0\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C$ with its Killing form $B$, its canonical conjugation $\sigma$, and the subspace $\mathfrak h:=\mathfrak h_0\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C=\mathfrak h_0\oplus i\mathfrak h_0$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is [[def-axiom-of-choice]]; it enters through the existence of a compact real form ([[thm-existence-of-a-compact-real-form]]), through the conjugacy theorem for Cartan subalgebras of $\mathfrak g$ ([[thm-cartan-subalgebras-of-a-complex-semisimple-lie-algebra-are-conjugate]]) and through the conjugacy theorem for Cartan involutions of $\mathfrak g_0$ ([[thm-conjugacy-of-cartan-involutions]]).

[L1] The complexification $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak g_0\oplus i\mathfrak g_0$ is a complex semisimple Lie algebra whose Killing form $B$ is the complex bilinear extension of $B_0$; the canonical conjugation $\sigma(X+iY)=X-iY$ is a conjugate-linear involutive automorphism of $\mathfrak g$ with fixed locus $\mathfrak g_0$, and $\sigma(\mathfrak h)=\mathfrak h$; the complex-linear extension of $\theta$ to $\mathfrak g$ is an involutive automorphism commuting with $\sigma$ ([[def-complexification-of-a-real-lie-algebra]], [[prop-complexification-preserves-semisimplicity]], [[thm-real-forms-correspond-to-conjugate-linear-involutions]], [[def-killing-form-of-a-finite-dimensional-lie-algebra]]).

[L2] $\mathfrak g$ has a compact real form $\mathfrak u_0$: a real form whose Killing form is negative definite. Its conjugation $\tau$, characterized by $\tau|_{u_0}=\mathrm{id}$ and $\tau(Z)=-Z$ for $Z\in i\mathfrak u_0$, is a conjugate-linear involutive automorphism of $\mathfrak g$ with fixed locus $\mathfrak u_0$ and $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak u_0\oplus i\mathfrak u_0$ ([[thm-existence-of-a-compact-real-form]], [[def-compact-real-form-of-a-complex-semisimple-lie-algebra]], [[thm-real-forms-correspond-to-conjugate-linear-involutions]]).

[L3] Any two Cartan subalgebras of the complex semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ are carried to one another by an inner automorphism of $\mathfrak g$, that is, by an element of the image of the adjoint map of a connected Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ ([[thm-cartan-subalgebras-of-a-complex-semisimple-lie-algebra-are-conjugate]]).

[L4] Any two Cartan involutions of $\mathfrak g_0$ are conjugate by an inner automorphism: for Cartan involutions $\eta,\theta$ there is $Y\in\mathfrak g_0$ with $\theta=e^{\operatorname{ad}_Y}\eta\,e^{-\operatorname{ad}_Y}$ ([[thm-conjugacy-of-cartan-involutions]]).

[L5] The realification $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb R}$ of $\mathfrak g$ is semisimple: its Killing form is $B_{\mathbb R}(Z,W)=2\operatorname{Re}B(Z,W)$, which is nondegenerate: if $\operatorname{Re}B(Z,W)=0$ for every $W$, testing $iW$ also gives $\operatorname{Im}B(Z,W)=0$, so $Z=0$ by nondegeneracy of $B$, so $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb R}$ is semisimple by the Cartan criterion, and then $Z(\mathfrak g_{\mathbb R})=0$ and $\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak g_{\mathbb R})=\operatorname{ad}(\mathfrak g_{\mathbb R})$ with $\operatorname{ad}$ injective ([[thm-cartans-semisimplicity-criterion]], [[thm-every-derivation-of-a-semisimple-lie-algebra-is-inner]], [[cor-semisimple-lie-algebras-are-centerless-and-perfect]], [[prop-trace-forms-are-symmetric-and-invariant]]).

[L6] A self-adjoint endomorphism of a finite-dimensional real inner product space has an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors with real eigenvalues ([[cor-real-spectral-theorem-for-self-adjoint-endomorphisms]], [[def-self-adjoint-and-normal-endomorphism]]).

[L7] A Cartan subalgebra is a nilpotent subalgebra equal to its own normalizer ([[def-cartan-subalgebra-of-a-lie-algebra]], [[def-normalizer-of-a-lie-subalgebra]], [[def-lower-central-series-and-nilpotent-lie-algebra]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The subspace $\mathfrak h=\mathfrak h_0\oplus i\mathfrak h_0$ is a Cartan subalgebra of $\mathfrak g$, and for every conjugate-linear involutive automorphism $\rho$ of $\mathfrak g$ the image $\rho(\mathfrak h)$ is again a Cartan subalgebra of $\mathfrak g$. Indeed $\mathfrak h$ is the complexification of the nilpotent algebra $\mathfrak h_0$, so its lower central series is the complexification of that of $\mathfrak h_0$ and terminates, and $\mathfrak h$ is nilpotent; if $X\in\mathfrak g$ satisfies $[X,\mathfrak h]\subseteq\mathfrak h$, writing $X=X_1+iX_2$ with $X_j\in\mathfrak g_0$ and comparing the $\mathfrak g_0$- and $i\mathfrak g_0$-components of $[X,\mathfrak h_0]\subseteq\mathfrak h_0\oplus i\mathfrak h_0$ gives $[X_j,\mathfrak h_0]\subseteq\mathfrak h_0$, so $X_j\in N_{\mathfrak g_0}(\mathfrak h_0)=\mathfrak h_0$ and $X\in\mathfrak h$; hence $N_{\mathfrak g}(\mathfrak h)=\mathfrak h$ and $\mathfrak h$ is a Cartan subalgebra by [L7]. For the second claim, $\rho(\mathfrak h)$ is a complex subspace because $\rho(iH)=-i\rho(H)$, it is closed under brackets because $\rho$ preserves them, it is nilpotent because $\rho$ restricts to an isomorphism of real Lie algebras $\mathfrak h\to\rho(\mathfrak h)$, and $N_{\mathfrak g}(\rho\mathfrak h)=\rho(N_{\mathfrak g}\mathfrak h)=\rho(\mathfrak h)$: for $X=\rho(X')$ one has $[X,\rho H]=\rho[X',H]$, so $X$ normalizes $\rho(\mathfrak h)$ exactly when $X'$ normalizes $\mathfrak h$. [L1, L7, algebra]

1.2 For a compact real form $\mathfrak u$ of $\mathfrak g$ with conjugation $\rho$, the map $\rho$ is a Cartan involution of $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb R}$ and $B_\rho(Z,W):=-B_{\mathbb R}(Z,\rho W)$ is an inner product on $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb R}$: writing $Z=X+iY$, $W=X'+iY'$ with $X,Y,X',Y'\in\mathfrak u$ one has $B(Z,\rho W)=B(X,X')+B(Y,Y')+i(B(Y,X')-B(X,Y'))$, and hence $\operatorname{Re}B(Z,\rho W)=B(X,X')+B(Y,Y')$, so $B_\rho(Z,Z)=-2(B(X,X)+B(Y,Y))>0$ for $Z\ne0$ because the Killing form of $\mathfrak u$ is negative definite, and $B_\rho$ is symmetric and bilinear. [L2, L5, algebra]

2.1 Fix a compact real form $\mathfrak u_0$ of $\mathfrak g$ with conjugation $\tau$, as in [L2], choose a maximal abelian subspace $\mathfrak a$ of $\mathfrak u_0$ and put $\mathfrak h_c:=\mathfrak a\oplus i\mathfrak a$. Then $\mathfrak h_c$ is a Cartan subalgebra of $\mathfrak g$ with $\tau(\mathfrak h_c)=\mathfrak h_c$: it is abelian, its normalizer in $\mathfrak g$ is itself because an element $X=Y+iZ$ with $Y,Z\in\mathfrak u_0$ normalizing $\mathfrak h_c$ has $[Y,\mathfrak a]\subseteq\mathfrak a$ and $[Z,\mathfrak a]\subseteq\mathfrak a$, and for $U\in N_{\mathfrak u_0}(\mathfrak a)$ and $A,A'\in\mathfrak a$, invariance gives $B([U,A],A')=B(U,[A,A'])=0$. Since $[U,A]\in\mathfrak a$ and $B$ is negative definite there, $[U,A]=0$. The centralizer of $\mathfrak a$ is $\mathfrak a$, because adjoining any centralizing vector gives an abelian subspace and $\mathfrak a$ is maximal; so $\mathfrak h_c$ is nilpotent and self-normalizing, and $\tau$ fixes $\mathfrak u_0$ and hence $\mathfrak a$ and $\mathfrak h_c$. By step 1.1 both $\mathfrak h$ and $\mathfrak h_c$ are Cartan subalgebras of $\mathfrak g$, so by [L3] there is an inner automorphism $\varphi$ of $\mathfrak g$ with $\varphi(\mathfrak h)=\mathfrak h_c$. Then $\tau':=\varphi^{-1}\tau\varphi$ is a conjugate-linear involutive automorphism of $\mathfrak g$ whose fixed algebra $\mathfrak u_0':=\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak u_0)$ is again a compact real form of $\mathfrak g$, since automorphisms preserve the Killing form, and it satisfies $\tau'(\mathfrak h)=\varphi^{-1}(\tau(\mathfrak h_c))=\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak h_c)=\mathfrak h$. [step 1.1, L2, L3, L5, algebra]

3.1 Put $\omega:=\sigma\tau'$, a complex-linear automorphism of $\mathfrak g$ because $\sigma$ and $\tau'$ are conjugate-linear involutions; it satisfies $\omega\tau'=\tau'\omega^{-1}$ since both sides equal $\sigma$, and it preserves $\mathfrak h$, since $\sigma(\mathfrak h)=\mathfrak h$ by [L1] and $\tau'(\mathfrak h)=\mathfrak h$ by step 2.1. Put $\rho:=\omega^2$. Then $\rho$ is an automorphism of $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb R}$ with $\rho(\mathfrak h)\subseteq\mathfrak h$, it is self-adjoint and positive definite for the inner product $B_{\tau'}$ of step 1.2, and it satisfies $\rho\tau'=\tau'\rho^{-1}$: invariance of $B_{\mathbb R}$ under $\omega^{-1}$ and $\tau'$ gives $B_{\tau'}(\omega Z,W)=-B_{\mathbb R}(\omega Z,\tau'W)=-B_{\mathbb R}(Z,\omega^{-1}\tau'W)=-B_{\mathbb R}(Z,\tau'\omega W)=B_{\tau'}(Z,\omega W)$, so $\omega$ is self-adjoint, $\rho=\omega^2=\omega^{*}\omega$ satisfies $B_{\tau'}(\rho Z,Z)=B_{\tau'}(\omega Z,\omega Z)>0$ for $Z\ne0$, and $\rho\tau'=\omega^2\tau'=\omega(\omega\tau')=\omega(\tau'\omega^{-1})=(\omega\tau')\omega^{-1}=\tau'\omega^{-1}\omega^{-1}=\tau'\rho^{-1}$. [step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 By [L6] applied to the self-adjoint operator $\rho$ and the $\rho$-invariant subspace $\mathfrak h$ of step 3.1 (its orthogonal complement is then also $\rho$-invariant), choose a $B_{\tau'}$-orthonormal basis of $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb R}$ consisting of eigenvectors of $\rho$ and containing a basis of $\mathfrak h$; let $\rho^{r}$, $r\in\mathbb R$, act on the eigenspace of $\rho$ for the eigenvalue $\lambda>0$ by multiplication by $\lambda^{r}$. Then $\rho^{r}(\mathfrak h)\subseteq\mathfrak h$ for every real $r$, and $\rho^{r}$ is an automorphism of $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb R}$: if $X,Y$ are eigenvectors with eigenvalues $\lambda_i,\lambda_j$, then $\rho[X,Y]=[\rho X,\rho Y]=\lambda_i\lambda_j[X,Y]$, so $[X,Y]$ lies in the eigenspace for $\lambda_i\lambda_j$ (or is $0$) and $\rho^{r}[X,Y]=(\lambda_i\lambda_j)^{r}[X,Y]=[\rho^{r}X,\rho^{r}Y]$, which extends to all $X,Y$ by bilinearity; moreover $\rho^{r}$ commutes with $\rho$ and with $\omega$ because $\omega\rho=\rho\omega$ and $\omega$ preserves every $\rho$-eigenspace. [step 3.1, L6, algebra]

5.1 Let $D$ be the endomorphism acting as $\log\lambda$ on the eigenspace of $\rho$ for the eigenvalue $\lambda>0$, so that $e^{D}=\rho$; then $D$ is self-adjoint for $B_{\tau'}$, and $D$ is a derivation of $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb R}$, since for eigenvectors $X,Y$ as in step 4.1 one has $D[X,Y]=(\log\lambda_i+\log\lambda_j)[X,Y]=[DX,Y]+[X,DY]$ and the identity extends by bilinearity. By [L5] there is a unique $X\in\mathfrak g_{\mathbb R}$ with $D=\operatorname{ad}_X$, so $\rho=e^{\operatorname{ad}_X}$ and the automorphism $\varphi_0:=\rho^{1/4}=e^{\operatorname{ad}_{X/4}}$ satisfies $\varphi_0(\mathfrak h)\subseteq\mathfrak h$ by step 4.1. [step 4.1, L5, algebra]

6.1 Define $\psi:=\varphi_0\tau'\varphi_0^{-1}$. Then $\psi^2=\mathrm{id}$, and $\psi$ is a Cartan involution of $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb R}$, because $B_\psi(Z,W)=B_{\tau'}(\varphi_0^{-1}Z,\varphi_0^{-1}W)$ is positive definite by step 1.2; moreover $\psi(\mathfrak h)=\varphi_0\tau'\varphi_0^{-1}(\mathfrak h)=\mathfrak h$ because $\varphi_0^{\pm1}(\mathfrak h)\subseteq\mathfrak h$ and $\tau'(\mathfrak h)=\mathfrak h$. Finally $\psi$ commutes with $\sigma$: using $\tau'\sigma=\omega^{-1}$ and $\rho\tau'=\tau'\rho^{-1}$ from step 3.1, the identities $\rho^{s}\sigma=\sigma\rho^{-s}$ and $\tau'\rho^{-1/4}=\rho^{1/4}\tau'$ for all real $s$ (valid on the $\rho$-eigenspaces, on each of which $\rho^{s}\sigma=\sigma(\sigma\rho^{s}\sigma)=\sigma\rho^{-s}$ because $\sigma\rho\sigma^{-1}=\rho^{-1}$) give $\psi\sigma=\varphi_0\tau'\varphi_0^{-1}\sigma=\rho^{1/4}\tau'\rho^{-1/4}\sigma=\rho^{1/4}\rho^{1/4}\tau'\sigma=\rho^{1/2}\omega^{-1}=\rho^{-1/2}\omega=\omega\rho^{-1/2}=\rho^{-1/4}\omega\rho^{-1/4}=\rho^{-1/4}\sigma\tau'\rho^{-1/4}=\sigma\rho^{1/4}\tau'\rho^{-1/4}=\sigma\psi$. [step 1.2, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 Since $\psi$ commutes with $\sigma$ and $\mathfrak g_0$ is the fixed locus of $\sigma$ in $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb R}$ by [L1], $\psi$ preserves $\mathfrak g_0$: for $Z\in\mathfrak g_0$ one has $\sigma(\psi Z)=\psi(\sigma Z)=\psi Z$. Hence $\eta:=\psi|_{\mathfrak g_0}$ is an involutive automorphism of $\mathfrak g_0$, and it is a Cartan involution: for $X\in\mathfrak g_0\setminus\{0\}$ one has $(B_0)_\eta(X,X)=-B_0(X,\eta X)=-\tfrac12B_{\mathbb R}(X,\psi X)=\tfrac12B_\psi(X,X)>0$, because $B$ restricts to $B_0$ on $\mathfrak g_0$ and $B_{\mathbb R}(X,\psi X)=2\operatorname{Re}B(X,\psi X)=2B(X,\psi X)$ there. Moreover $\psi(\mathfrak h)=\mathfrak h$ with $\psi(\mathfrak g_0)=\mathfrak g_0$ gives $\eta(\mathfrak h_0)=\psi(\mathfrak h\cap\mathfrak g_0)=\psi(\mathfrak h)\cap\psi(\mathfrak g_0)=\mathfrak h\cap\mathfrak g_0=\mathfrak h_0$. [step 1.2, step 6.1, L1, algebra]

8.1 By [L4] applied to the two Cartan involutions $\eta$ and $\theta$ of $\mathfrak g_0$ there is $Y\in\mathfrak g_0$ with $\theta=e^{\operatorname{ad}_Y}\eta\,e^{-\operatorname{ad}_Y}$. Put $\chi:=e^{\operatorname{ad}_Y}$, an inner automorphism of $\mathfrak g_0$. Then $\theta(\chi(\mathfrak h_0))=\chi(\eta(\mathfrak h_0))=\chi(\mathfrak h_0)$ by step 7.1, so $\chi(\mathfrak h_0)$ is a $\theta$-stable subspace of $\mathfrak g_0$; being the image of a Cartan subalgebra under an automorphism it is again a Cartan subalgebra of $\mathfrak g_0$. Thus $\mathfrak h_0$ is conjugate by the inner automorphism $\chi$ to the $\theta$-stable Cartan subalgebra $\chi(\mathfrak h_0)$, and the theorem follows. [step 7.1, L4, A1, algebra] ∎
