## Example

Assume the Axiom of Choice. In $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb R)$ put

$$K=\operatorname{SO}(2)=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\ \sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}\right\},\qquad A=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\0&a^{-1}\end{pmatrix}:a>0\right\},\qquad N=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\0&1\end{pmatrix}:x\in\mathbb R\right\}.$$

Then every $g\in\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb R)$ has a unique factorization
$g=kan$ with $k\in K$, $a\in A$ and $n\in N$, and the multiplication map
$K\times A\times N\to\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb R)$ is a diffeomorphism: this
is the Iwasawa decomposition of $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb R)$
([[thm-global-iwasawa-decomposition]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice; the group $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb R)$ of real $2\times2$ matrices of determinant $1$; the Cartan involution $\theta(X)=-X^{\mathsf T}$ with $\mathfrak k_0=\mathfrak{so}(2)$ and $\mathfrak p_0$ the symmetric traceless matrices; and a matrix $g=\begin{pmatrix}p&r\\q&s\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb R)$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is [[def-axiom-of-choice]]; it enters only through the global Iwasawa theorem of [L4] and the smooth structure of the group.

[L1] $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb R)$ is an embedded Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb R)$ and $\operatorname{SO}(2)$ is a closed connected subgroup with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{so}(2)=\mathbb R\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ ([[ex-general-and-special-linear-lie-groups]], [[ex-orthogonal-and-special-orthogonal-lie-groups]]).

[L2] The Cartan decomposition $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb R)=\mathfrak k_0\oplus\mathfrak p_0$ has $\mathfrak k_0=\mathfrak{so}(2)$ and $\mathfrak p_0=\{X:X^{\mathsf T}=X,\ \operatorname{tr}X=0\}$; $\mathfrak p_0$ is spanned by $H=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}$ and $\sigma=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$, and $[H,\sigma]=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\-2&0\end{pmatrix}\ne0$, so $\mathbb RH$ is a maximal abelian subspace of $\mathfrak p_0$ ([[ex-cartan-involution-and-k-plus-p-for-sl-n-r]], [[ex-compact-and-split-cartan-subalgebras-of-sl-two-r]], [[def-cartan-decomposition-of-a-real-semisimple-lie-algebra]], [[def-theta-stable-cartan-subalgebra-and-compact-split-parts]]).

[L3] The matrix exponential is the Lie-group exponential of a matrix group, and $\exp(X)=I+X$ whenever $X^2=0$ ([[ex-matrix-exponential-as-the-lie-group-exponential]], [[def-exponential-map-of-a-lie-group]]).

[L4] In the global Cartan setup of a connected real semisimple Lie group $G$ with finite center, with $A=\exp(\mathfrak a)$ and $N$ the connected subgroup with Lie algebra the sum $\mathfrak n$ of the positive restricted root spaces, the multiplication map $K\times A\times N\to G$ is a diffeomorphism ([[thm-global-iwasawa-decomposition]]).





**Proof technique:** direct matrix computation.

1.1 Write $E=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$ and $H=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}$. Then $[H,E]=2E$, so $\mathfrak n=\mathbb RE$ is the positive restricted root space for the functional $2f_1$ with $f_1(H)=1$ on the maximal abelian $\mathfrak a=\mathbb RH$ of [L2], and $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb R)=\mathfrak k_0\oplus\mathfrak a\oplus\mathfrak n$ is the Iwasawa decomposition on the Lie-algebra level, since the three spaces have dimensions $1,1,1$ and the sum is direct. [given, L2, algebra]

1.2 The subgroups generated by $\mathfrak a$ and $\mathfrak n$ are the sets displayed: $\exp(tH)=\begin{pmatrix}e^t&0\\0&e^{-t}\end{pmatrix}$, so $\exp(\mathfrak a)=A$ with $a=e^t>0$, and $E^2=0$ gives $\exp(xE)=I+xE=\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ by [L3], so $\exp(\mathfrak n)=N$. [given, L3, algebra]

2.1 Existence of the factorization. Since $g$ is invertible, its first column $(p,q)$ is nonzero, so $a:=\sqrt{p^2+q^2}>0$ is well defined. Put $k:=\tfrac1a\begin{pmatrix}p&-q\\ q&p\end{pmatrix}$ and $x:=\tfrac{pr+qs}{a^2}$; then $k^{\mathsf T}k=I$ and $\det k=\tfrac{p^2+q^2}{a^2}=1$, so $k\in\operatorname{SO}(2)$, and a direct multiplication gives $k^{-1}g=\tfrac1a\begin{pmatrix}p&q\\ -q&p\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}p&r\\ q&s\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a&\tfrac{pr+qs}{a}\\ 0&\tfrac{ps-qr}{a}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a&ax\\ 0&a^{-1}\end{pmatrix}$, using $ps-qr=1$ and $a^2=p^2+q^2$. Hence $g=k\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\0&a^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ with all three factors in $K,A,N$. [given, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Uniqueness. Suppose $g=k_1a_1n_1=k_2a_2n_2$ with $k_i\in K$, $a_i=\operatorname{diag}(a_i,a_i^{-1})$ and $n_i\in N$. Applying both sides to the first standard basis vector and using $n_ie_1=e_1$ gives $k_1a_1e_1=k_2a_2e_1$, so taking norms and using that $k_1,k_2$ are orthogonal yields $a_1=a_2>0$; hence $k_1e_1=k_2e_1$ and therefore $k_1=k_2$, because a rotation of $\mathbb R^2$ fixing $e_1$ is the identity. Then $a_1n_1=a_2n_2$ forces $n_1=n_2$, so all three factors are unique. [given, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Define $\Theta(g)=(g^{\mathsf T})^{-1}$ on $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb R)$. The identities $\Theta(gh)=\Theta(g)\Theta(h)$ and $\Theta^2=\mathrm{id}$ make it an involutive Lie-group automorphism; its differential is $d\Theta_I(X)=-X^{\mathsf T}=\theta(X)$, and its fixed group is $\{g:g^{\mathsf T}g=I,\ \det g=1\}=SO(2)=K$. It fixes the center $\{\pm I\}$ pointwise. Thus the global Cartan setup required by [L4] holds for $G=\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb R)$: its Lie algebra is semisimple, its center is finite, it is connected, and by steps 1.1 and 1.2 the data $\mathfrak k_0,\mathfrak a,\mathfrak n$ are exactly those of the displayed $K,A,N$. Therefore $K\times A\times N\to\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb R)$, $(k,a,n)\mapsto kan$, is a diffeomorphism, and by step 2.1 it is the unique factorization of each element. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1, L4, A1, algebra]

5.1 Endpoints and scope: for $g=I$ the factorization is $k=a=n=I$ with $a=1$ and $x=0$, which is the endpoint $t=0$ of the positive parameter domain $a>0$; for $a\to0^+$ no element of $A$ is lost because $A$ is defined by positivity of $a$. The choice principle is inherited only from [L4], while the matrix computations use none. The diagonal factor is exactly $\exp(\mathfrak a)$ and the unipotent factor is exactly $\exp(\mathfrak n)$, so the group-level statement matches the Lie-algebra-level Iwasawa decomposition of step 1.1. [given, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
