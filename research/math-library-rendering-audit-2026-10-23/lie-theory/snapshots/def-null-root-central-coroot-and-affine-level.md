## Definition

Fix the finite Cartan $\mathfrak h$ supplied by [[lem-finite-semisimple-cartan-root-and-string-structure]]. In [[def-degree-derivation-and-full-untwisted-affine-algebra]] put
$$\widehat{\mathfrak h}=\mathfrak h\oplus\mathbb Cc\oplus\mathbb Cd.$$
It is abelian, and its centralizer is itself: commuting with $d$ forces all nonzero modes to vanish, and commuting with $\mathfrak h$ forces the degree-zero finite part into $\mathfrak h$, by the cited self-centralization result. Extend each finite root $\alpha$ to $\widehat{\mathfrak h}$ by $\alpha(c)=\alpha(d)=0$.

The **null root** is the unique functional $\delta\in\widehat{\mathfrak h}^*$ with $\delta|_{\mathfrak h\oplus\mathbb Cc}=0$ and $\delta(d)=1$. The chosen central generator $c$ is called the **affine central coroot** in the normalized loop convention. Its identification with the primitive central combination of the affine simple coroots belongs to the presentation comparison.

The **level** of a weight $\Lambda\in\widehat{\mathfrak h}^*$ is $\Lambda(c)$. A module has level $k$ when $c$ acts as $k\operatorname{id}$ on the whole module. In particular a module generated by a weight vector $v$ of weight $\Lambda$ has level $\Lambda(c)$: centrality gives $c(x_1\cdots x_mv)=x_1\cdots x_mcv=\Lambda(c)x_1\cdots x_mv$ on every finite word, and these words span. An arbitrary weight module may have several levels, so no single level is asserted for it. The zero module satisfies every scalar-action identity; it does not determine a unique scalar level.
