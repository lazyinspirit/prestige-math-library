## Example

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb C)$ and let
$\chi$ be a central character which is not the central character of any nonzero
finite-dimensional $U(\mathfrak g)$-module. Then the central reduction
$U_\chi$ is a simple ring, and the unique primitive ideal of $U(\mathfrak g)$
with central character $\chi$ is $U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi$: for every
$\lambda\in\mathfrak h^*$ with $\chi_\lambda=\chi$ the Verma module $M(\lambda)$
is simple and
$$\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak g)}M(\lambda)=U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi,$$ so
the annihilator is generated by the Casimir relation $\Omega-\chi(\Omega)$
alone. This is the smallest instance of a Duflo annihilator: over a generic
central character the primitive ideal is exactly the unavoidable central ideal.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb C)$, a central character $\chi$ that is not the central character of any nonzero finite-dimensional $U(\mathfrak g)$-module, and the central reduction $U_\chi=U(\mathfrak g)/U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi$.

[F1] Under AC, $U_\chi$ is simple if and only if $\chi$ is not a finite-dimensional central character; when $U_\chi$ is simple, every nonzero module with central character $\chi$ is faithful over $U_\chi$, every Verma module $M(\lambda)$ with $\chi_\lambda=\chi$ is simple, and $\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak g)}M(\lambda)=U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi$ ([[lem-the-central-reduction-of-usl2-is-simple-away-from-finite-dimensional-characters]], [[def-central-reduction-of-the-enveloping-algebra]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F2] For every primitive ideal $I$ with central character $\chi$ one has $I\cap Z(U(\mathfrak g))=\ker\chi$ and $U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi\subseteq I$ ([[prop-a-primitive-ideal-determines-a-central-character]], [[def-primitive-ideal-of-an-enveloping-algebra]], [[def-central-character-of-a-lie-algebra-module]]).

[F3] The two-sided ideals of $U_\chi$ correspond to the two-sided ideals of $U(\mathfrak g)$ containing $U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi$ ([[lem-the-central-reduction-of-usl2-is-simple-away-from-finite-dimensional-characters]], [[def-left-right-and-two-sided-ideal]]).

[F4] Put $\Omega=ef+fe+\tfrac12h^2$. Under AC the center of $U(\mathfrak g)$ is $\mathbb C[\Omega]$, so $\ker\chi=(\Omega-\chi(\Omega))$ and $U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi=(\Omega-\chi(\Omega))U(\mathfrak g)$; moreover $\Omega$ acts on $M(\lambda)$ by $\lambda(\lambda+2)/2$, so every $\lambda$ with $\chi_\lambda=\chi$ satisfies $\lambda(\lambda+2)=2\chi(\Omega)$, where $\lambda$ denotes $\lambda(h)$. Conversely, algebraic closure supplies a root $\lambda$ of this quadratic; the Casimir eigenvalue then equals $\chi(\Omega)$, and since $\Omega$ generates the center, $\chi_\lambda=\chi$ ([[thm-the-complex-numbers-are-algebraically-closed]]) ([[lem-the-central-reduction-of-usl2-is-simple-away-from-finite-dimensional-characters]], [[prop-casimir-eigenvalue-on-a-highest-weight-module]], [[def-verma-module]], [[def-special-linear-lie-algebra-sl-two]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Since $\chi$ is not a finite-dimensional central character, [F1] gives that $U_\chi$ is a simple ring with no nonzero proper two-sided ideal. [F1, given]

2.1 Let $I$ be a primitive ideal of $U(\mathfrak g)$ with central character $\chi$. By [F2] one has $U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi\subseteq I$; the image $\bar I$ of $I$ in $U_\chi$ is therefore a two-sided ideal, hence is $0$ or $U_\chi$ by step 1.1. If $\bar I=U_\chi$ then $I+U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi=U(\mathfrak g)$, so $I=U(\mathfrak g)$ by [F2], contradicting that a primitive ideal is proper; therefore $\bar I=0$, i.e. $I\subseteq U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi$. With the reverse inclusion from [F2], $I=U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi$: the central ideal is the only primitive ideal with central character $\chi$. [step 1.1, F2, F3, algebra]

3.1 By [F4] there exists a weight $\lambda$ with $\chi_\lambda=\chi$, so the primitive fibre is nonempty. For any such $\lambda$, by [F1] the Verma module $M(\lambda)$ is simple with $\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak g)}M(\lambda)=U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi$, so $U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi$ is primitive (indeed it is the annihilator of a simple module) and is the unique primitive ideal over $\chi$ by step 2.1. Since $\ker\chi=(\Omega-\chi(\Omega))$ in $Z(U(\mathfrak g))=\mathbb C[\Omega]$, the annihilator is the two-sided ideal generated by the single Casimir relation $\Omega-\chi(\Omega)$, so over a generic central character the primitive ideal is exactly the unavoidable central ideal. [step 2.1, F1, F4, algebra] ∎
