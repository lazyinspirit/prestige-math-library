## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice for the conditional-expectation, martingale and stochastic-integral interfaces, and Countable Choice and Dependent Choice for the independent input streams. Let $E$ be a finite nonempty state set, let a finite-channel reaction system be as in [[def-reaction-network-propensity-system-and-generator]], and set $\Lambda:=\max_{x\in E}\lambda(x)$. Let $X$ be the process generated by the unlimited-budget Gillespie method, using its equivalent dominating-thinning realization with candidate rate $\Lambda$ and the natural filtration of candidate times and marks. For each channel $j$, define
$$N_j(t):=\#\{s\le t:\text{a channel-}j\text{ reaction occurs at }s\},\quad A_j(t):=\int_0^t a_j(X_{s-})\,ds,\quad M_j(t):=N_j(t)-A_j(t).$$
Then each $N_j$ is a simple counting process, $A_j$ is a continuous predictable compensator with $\mathbb E A_j(T)<\infty$, and $M_j$ is a square-integrable martingale. Its optional bracket and continuous predictable bracket are $[M_j]_t=N_j(t)$ and $\langle M_j\rangle_t=A_j(t)$. For distinct channels $i\ne j$, $[M_i,M_j]_t=\langle M_i,M_j\rangle_t=0$.

For any finite family of predictable real processes $H_1,\ldots,H_R$ satisfying
$$\sum_{j=1}^R\mathbb E\int_0^T H_j(s)^2a_j(X_{s-})\,ds<\infty,$$
the $L^2$ integrals against each $M_j$ exist and satisfy
$$\mathbb E\left[\left(\sum_{j=1}^R\int_0^T H_j(s)\,dM_j(s)\right)^2\right]=\sum_{j=1}^R\mathbb E\int_0^T H_j(s)^2a_j(X_{s-})\,ds.$$
Zero-increment channels remain counted as marked events and obey these same formulas.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, Countable Choice, Dependent Choice, a finite-state reaction system, and the independent exact exponential and uniform input streams.

[F1] The propensity system has finite channel rates, admissible poststates, and total rate $\lambda(x)=\sum_j a_j(x)$. ([[def-reaction-network-propensity-system-and-generator]])

[F2] The finite-state Gillespie law is exactly the recursively specified event law; its bounded-rate realization agrees with the dominating-thinning path law. ([[thm-finite-state-gillespie-and-next-reaction-exactness]], [[thm-dominating-thinning-exactly-simulates-reaction-rates]])

[F3] Rate-$\Lambda$ candidates have a Poisson count with mean $\Lambda h$ on an interval of length $h$, ordered event-time density, and no simultaneous points; a Poisson variable of mean $\mu$ has first and second moments $\mu$ and $\mu+\mu^2$. ([[lem-homogeneous-poisson-ordered-event-time-density]], [[def-scalar-poisson-count-law]], [[lem-poisson-count-law-normalization-and-moments]])

[F4] Predictable processes are measurable for the sigma-algebra generated by $(s,t]\times B$ with $B\in\mathcal F_s$ and by the time-zero generators; that sigma-algebra is closed under pointwise limits. ([[def-progressively-measurable-and-predictable-process]])

[F5] A continuous-time filtration is an increasing family of sigma-algebras on the probability space, and the martingale identity is required for every pair $s\le t$. ([[def-continuous-time-filtration-and-all-pairs-martingale]])

[F6] The finite-horizon simple counting-process contract requires an adapted simple unit-jump count with finite terminal count, a continuous predictable finite-mean compensator, and a square-integrable martingale difference. ([[def-counting-process-intensity-and-compensator-contract]])

[F7] Under that contract, $[M_j]=N_j$ and $\langle M_j\rangle=A_j$. ([[thm-compensated-counting-process-brackets]])

[F8] Compensated simple counts with no simultaneous jumps have zero optional and predictable cross brackets. ([[thm-cross-brackets-vanish-under-no-simultaneous-jumps]])

[F9] For each count satisfying the contract, finite-energy predictable integrands have an $L^2$ martingale integral with isometry $\mathbb E(\int H\,dM)^2=\mathbb E\int H^2\,dA$. ([[thm-l2-counting-process-integral-isometry]])

[F10] Bounded predictable step processes are dense in the compensator $L^2$ norm. ([[lem-predictable-step-processes-dense-in-compensator-l2]])

[F11] For square-integrable $U,V$, Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\mathbb E[|UV|]\le\|U\|_2\|V\|_2$, so $UV$ is integrable. ([[cor-cauchy-schwarz-for-random-variables]])

[F12] Expectation is linear on integrable random variables, and for every integrable $Z$, $|\mathbb E[Z]|\le\mathbb E[|Z|]$. ([[cor-expectation-linearity-monotonicity-and-modulus-bound]])

[A1] Full AC is inherited by the conditional-expectation, continuous-time martingale and counting-process contract interfaces. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-continuous-time-adapted-process-and-martingale]])

[A2] Countable Choice is used for the exponential and uniform laws. ([[def-countable-choice]])

[A3] Dependent Choice is used for the countable product probability space of the input streams. ([[def-dependent-choice]], [[thm-countable-product-of-probability-spaces]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $\mathcal G_t$ be generated by candidate times and marks up to $t$; these sigma-algebras increase with $t$, so [F5] makes them a continuous-time filtration. If $\Lambda=0$, all rates and all counts vanish. Otherwise the thinning realization has a rate-$\Lambda$ candidate process, and [F2] gives the same reaction path law as Gillespie. By [F3], candidates have no ties and their number on $[0,T]$ is finite almost surely. Hence each $N_j$ is adapted, nondecreasing and integer-valued with unit jumps and finite terminal value; also $N_j(T)\le N^*(T)$ and $0\le A_j(T)\le\Lambda T$. The state path is piecewise constant with finitely many jumps. Its left-limit process $X_{s-}$ is a pointwise limit of left-grid processes taking the value $X_{k2^{-n}}$ on $(k2^{-n},(k+1)2^{-n}]$; each is measurable for the generators in [F4]. Thus $a_j(X_{s-})$ is predictable. Because the density is bounded and piecewise constant along the path, for fixed $t$ its time integral is the limit of left-grid Riemann sums of values known by time $t$, so $A_j(t)$ is $\mathcal G_t$-measurable. The integral is continuous and bounded by $\Lambda T$; continuous adapted processes are pointwise limits of their left-grid predictable approximations, so $A_j$ is predictable with finite mean. [A1, A2, A3, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, given, construct]

2.1 Fix deterministic $s$ and $0<h\le T-s$, and condition on $\mathcal G_s$ with $X_s=x$. The first accepted channel-$j$ event has density $a_j(x)e^{-\lambda(x)u}$ at elapsed time $u$, by [F2]. Its probability over $0<u\le h$ differs from $a_j(x)h$ by at most $a_j(x)\lambda(x)h^2/2\le\Lambda^2h^2/2$. Further channel-$j$ events require at least two candidates; if $N^*$ is their count and $\mu=\Lambda h$, then [F3] gives $\mathbb E(N^*1_{\{N^*\ge2\}})=\mu(1-e^{-\mu})\le\mu^2$. Therefore $\left|\mathbb E(N_j(s+h)-N_j(s)\mid\mathcal G_s)-a_j(x)h\right|\le\tfrac32\Lambda^2h^2.$ If no candidate occurs in $(s,s+h]$, the state stays at $x$ and the compensator increment equals $a_j(x)h$. Otherwise both that increment and $a_j(x)h$ lie between $0$ and $\Lambda h$, while the conditional probability of a candidate is at most $\Lambda h$. Therefore $\left|\mathbb E(A_j(s+h)-A_j(s)\mid\mathcal G_s)-a_j(x)h\right|\le\Lambda^2h^2,$ so $|\mathbb E(M_j(s+h)-M_j(s)\mid\mathcal G_s)|\le\tfrac52\Lambda^2h^2$. For $B\in\mathcal G_s$, partition $[s,t]$ into intervals of mesh at most $\delta$; conditional expectation on each left endpoint bounds $|\mathbb E(1_B(M_j(t)-M_j(s)))|$ by $\tfrac52\Lambda^2\delta(t-s)$. Letting $\delta\downarrow0$ gives zero for every $B\in\mathcal G_s$, which is the all-pairs martingale identity. Since $N_j(T)\le N^*(T)$ and $A_j(T)\le\Lambda T$, the bound $M_j(T)^2\le2N_j(T)^2+2A_j(T)^2$ and [F3] give $\mathbb E M_j(T)^2<\infty$; the same bound applies at every $t\le T$. Thus $M_j$ is square-integrable. [F2, F3, step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 Candidates are simple, and each candidate mark selects at most one reaction channel. Therefore no two distinct channel counts jump together. Steps 1.1–2.1 verify the simple-count, finite-mean continuous predictable compensator, and square-integrable martingale hypotheses of [F6]. Applying [F7] gives $[M_j]=N_j$ and $\langle M_j\rangle=A_j$; applying [F8] to each distinct pair gives $[M_i,M_j]=\langle M_i,M_j\rangle=0$. [F3, F6, F7, F8, step 1.1, step 2.1]

3.2 For bounded predictable step integrands $H_i,H_j$ on a common deterministic partition, expand $\mathbb E(I_iI_j)$, where $I_i=\int H_i\,dM_i$. Terms from distinct time intervals vanish by conditioning on the left endpoint of the later interval and using the martingale property from step 2.1. On an interval of length $h$, the same-interval product has conditional absolute expectation at most $5\Lambda^2h^2$: the product of distinct channel counts is zero unless at least two candidates occur, whose $N^{*2}$ expectation is $\mu^2+\mu(1-e^{-\mu})\le2\mu^2$; the two count-compensator terms are each at most $\Lambda h\,\mathbb E(N^*\mid\mathcal G_s)\le\Lambda^2h^2$, and the compensator product is at most $\Lambda^2h^2$. If the step coefficients are bounded in absolute value by $K$, multiplying by them gives a bound $5K^2\Lambda^2h^2$. Refining the common partition leaves each step integral unchanged and makes the sum of these bounds tend to zero, so $\mathbb E(I_iI_j)=0$. [F3, step 2.1, given, algebra]

4.1 For general $H_j$ in the stated finite-energy class, [F10] supplies bounded predictable step approximations in each compensator $L^2$ norm. By [F9], their individual integrals converge in $L^2$ to $I_j=\int_0^T H_j\,dM_j$ and satisfy $\mathbb E I_j^2=\mathbb E\int_0^T H_j^2\,dA_j$. For each $i\ne j$, use the linearity and modulus inequality in [F12], then apply [F11] to the expanded product difference, to obtain $|\mathbb E(I_i^{(n)}I_j^{(n)})-\mathbb E(I_iI_j)|\le\|I_i^{(n)}-I_i\|_2\|I_j^{(n)}\|_2+\|I_i\|_2\|I_j^{(n)}-I_j\|_2\to0$, since the isometry bounds the approximating norms. Step 3.2 therefore gives $\mathbb E(I_iI_j)=0$. Expanding the square of the finite sum now yields the displayed vector isometry because $dA_j=a_j(X_{s-})ds$. The proof uses AC only through [A1], the input-stream construction only through [A2]–[A3], and counts zero-increment reactions because they remain marked candidate acceptances. [A1, A2, A3, F9, F10, F11, F12, step 3.1, step 3.2, algebra] ∎
