## Statement

Let $(X_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}$ be an independent sequence of random elements, and
let $\mathcal T(X_n:n\in\mathbb N)$ be its tail sigma-algebra. Then every event
$A\in\mathcal T(X_n:n\in\mathbb N)$ satisfies
$$\mathbb P(A)\in\{0,1\}.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An independent sequence $(X_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}$ and a tail event $A\in\mathcal T(X_n:n\in\mathbb N)$.

[L1] Every tail event is independent of every finite initial sigma-algebra. ([[lem-tail-events-are-independent-of-every-finite-initial-sigma-algebra]])

[L2] A monotone class containing an algebra contains the sigma-algebra generated by that algebra. ([[thm-monotone-class]])

[L3] Probability measures are continuous from below and from above on monotone event sequences, and probabilities lie in $[0,1]$. ([[lem-probability-measure-basic-identities]])

[L4] The tail sigma-algebra is $$\mathcal T(X_n:n\in\mathbb N)=\bigcap_{m\in\mathbb N}\sigma(X_n:n\ge m),$$ so every tail event in particular lies in $\sigma(X_n:n\ge0)$. ([[def-tail-sigma-algebra-of-a-sequence]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 For $m\in\mathbb N$, let $\mathcal H_m:=\sigma(X_0,\dots,X_m)$ and put $\mathcal A:=\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb N}\mathcal H_m$. Since the family $(\mathcal H_m)$ is increasing, $\mathcal A$ is an algebra of events. Define $$\mathcal D:=\{B\in\sigma(\mathcal A):\mathbb P(A\cap B)=\mathbb P(A)\mathbb P(B)\}.$$ Using the continuity statements from [L3], the class $\mathcal D$ is closed under increasing unions and decreasing intersections, so it is a monotone class. [given, L3]

1.2 For each $m$, [L1] gives $\mathcal H_m\subseteq\mathcal D$. Hence the algebra $\mathcal A=\bigcup_m\mathcal H_m$ is contained in $\mathcal D$. [L1]

2.1 Because $\mathcal D$ is a monotone class containing the algebra $\mathcal A$, [L2] yields $\sigma(\mathcal A)\subseteq\mathcal D$. But $\sigma(\mathcal A)=\sigma(X_n:n\ge0)$, and [L4] puts every tail event in this full-coordinate sigma-algebra. Therefore $A\in\mathcal D$, so $$\mathbb P(A)=\mathbb P(A\cap A)=\mathbb P(A)^2.$$ [step 1.1, step 1.2, L2, L4, algebra]

3.1 Step 2.1 and [L3] show that the probability $p=\mathbb P(A)$ satisfies $0\le p\le1$ and $p=p^2$, so $p\in\{0,1\}$. [step 2.1, L3, algebra] ∎
