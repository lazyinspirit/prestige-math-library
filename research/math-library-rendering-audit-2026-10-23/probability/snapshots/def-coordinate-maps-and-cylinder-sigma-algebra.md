## Definition

For measurable spaces $(E_i,\mathcal E_i)_{i\in I}$, put
$E=\prod_{i\in I}E_i$. For finite $F\subseteq I$, let
$\pi_F:E\to\prod_{i\in F}E_i$ be restriction to $F$ (the singleton empty
product when $F=\varnothing$). On $\prod_{i\in F}E_i$, write $q_i$ for the
$i$th coordinate map and define

$$\bigotimes_{i\in F}\mathcal E_i:=\sigma\{q_i^{-1}(A):i\in F,\ A\in\mathcal E_i\}.$$

For $F=\varnothing$ this is the two-set sigma-algebra on the singleton. If
$e:[n]\to F$ is any enumeration, coordinate relabelling identifies this
sigma-algebra with the recursively defined finite product sigma-algebra of
[[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]]: both are
generated by the same coordinate inverse images. Thus the notation is
independent of the enumeration.

A **finite-coordinate cylinder** is $\pi_F^{-1}(A)$ for
$A\in\bigotimes_{i\in F}\mathcal E_i$. The **cylinder sigma-algebra** is

$$\mathcal C_I:=\sigma\{\pi_F^{-1}(A):F\subseteq I\text{ finite},\ A\in\bigotimes_{i\in F}\mathcal E_i\}.$$

This is the product sigma-algebra on the arbitrary product; the empty-support cylinder is either $\varnothing$ or $E$.
