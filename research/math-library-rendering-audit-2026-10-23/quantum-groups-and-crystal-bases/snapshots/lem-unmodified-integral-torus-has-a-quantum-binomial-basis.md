## Statement

Let $(I,A,D,P,P^{\vee},q)$ be a finite symmetrizable Cartan datum
([[def-symmetrizable-cartan-datum-for-a-quantum-group]]), put
$A=\mathbb Z[q,q^{-1}]$ ([[def-the-laurent-polynomial-ring]]), let
$L:=\sum_{i\in I}\mathbb Z(d_ih_i)\subseteq P^{\vee}$, and let $U_A^0$ be the
$A$-subalgebra of $U_q^0$ generated by the $K_h^{\pm1}$ and the toral quantum
binomials $T_{i,c,t}$ ([[def-lusztig-divided-power-integral-form]]).

(a) **One simple torus.** Fix $i\in I$ and write $z:=q_i$, $K:=K_i$,
$T_t:=T_{i,0,t}$ for $t\ge0$. Then for all $r,s\ge0$, $c\in\mathbb Z$ and
$t\ge1$,
$$T_rT_s=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(r,s)}z^{rs}K^{-j}\binom{r+s-j}{r}_z\binom{r}{j}_zT_{r+s-j},\qquad T_{c+1,t}=z^tT_{c,t}+K^{-1}z^{-c+t-1}T_{c,t-1},$$
where $\binom{m}{r}_z$ is the Gaussian binomial of
[[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]; all
coefficients lie in $A$. Moreover the $A$-subalgebra of $U_q^0$ generated by
$K^{\pm1}$ and all $T_{c,t}$ equals the $A$-span of
$\{K^{\delta}T_t:\delta\in\{0,1\},\,t\ge0\}$, and that set is an $A$-basis.

(b) **Full torus.** Fix a $\mathbb Z$-basis of $P^{\vee}$, let $J$ be the
finite set of coordinates occurring in the finitely many vectors $d_ih_i$, and
let $D:=\bigoplus_{j\in J}\mathbb Z e_j$ be the corresponding coordinate direct
summand. For every coset of $L$ in $P^{\vee}$ choose the representative $c$
whose $D$-component has minimal sum of coordinate absolute values, ties broken
lexicographically, and whose complementary component is the coset's common
complementary component. Then
$$U_A^0=\bigoplus_{c}K_c\cdot A\text{-span}\Bigl\{\prod_{i\in I}K_i^{\delta_i}T_{i,0,t_i}:\delta_i\in\{0,1\},\,t_i\ge0\Bigr\},$$
the sum over the chosen representatives, so the displayed elements form an
$A$-basis of $U_A^0$.

Both statements are proved directly on the unmodified generators over the
non-PID ring $A$: no integral PBW or canonical-basis input, no inference from
torsion-freeness to freeness, and no modified-${}_A\dot U$ result is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Cartan datum with finitely many indices, the Laurent ring $A$, the subgroup $L=\sum_i\mathbb Z(d_ih_i)\subseteq P^{\vee}$, and the toral binomials $T_{i,c,t}=\prod_{s=1}^{t}(K_iq_i^{c-s+1}-K_i^{-1}q_i^{-c+s-1})/(q_i^{s}-q_i^{-s})$ for $i\in I$, $c\in\mathbb Z$, $t\ge0$.

[F1] $A=\mathbb Z[q,q^{-1}]$ is a subring of $\mathbb Q(q)$ containing $z^{\pm m}$ and $\binom{m}{r}_z$ for all $m,r\ge0$: the Gaussian binomials are Laurent polynomials in $q_i$ with integer coefficients ([[def-the-laurent-polynomial-ring]], [[lem-quantum-pascal-recurrence-and-gaussian-integrality]] (iv)), and $q_i^{\pm1}\in A$ ([[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]).

[F2] Each $T_{i,c,t}$ is a Laurent polynomial in $K_i^{\pm1}$ with coefficients in $\mathbb Q(q)$, all $T_{i,c,t}$ commute with one another and with every $K_h$, $T_{i,c,0}=1$, $T_{i,c,1}=(K_iq_i^{c}-K_i^{-1}q_i^{-c})/(q_i-q_i^{-1})$, and $T_{i,c,t}=T_{i,c,t-1}(K_iq_i^{c-t+1}-K_i^{-1}q_i^{-c+t-1})/(q_i^{t}-q_i^{-t})$ ([[def-lusztig-divided-power-integral-form]], [[lem-divided-power-and-toral-quantum-binomial-reordering-identities]] (ii)).

[F3] $U_q^0\cong\mathbb Q(q)[P^{\vee}]$ as algebras, with $K_hK_{h'}=K_{h+h'}$ and $K_0=1$; the $K_h$ for $h$ in a $\mathbb Z$-basis of $P^{\vee}$ are algebraically independent, and $U_q^0$ is commutative ([[thm-triangular-decomposition-of-a-quantized-enveloping-algebra]] (ii)).

[F4] The simple coroots $h_i$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb Q$ in $P^{\vee}\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q$, and all $d_i>0$; hence $L=\bigoplus_i\mathbb Z(d_ih_i)\cong\mathbb Z^{n}$ and each $d_ih_i$ has finite support in the fixed basis of $P^{\vee}$, so the set $J$ of coordinates occurring in the $d_ih_i$ is finite and $L\subseteq D$ ([[def-symmetrizable-cartan-datum-for-a-quantum-group]], [[def-free-abelian-group]]).

[F5] For every nonzero integer $t$, $q^t\ne1$: after replacing $t$ by $|t|$, equality would make $q$ a root of $T^{|t|}-1$. Thus $z^s-z^{-s}\ne0$ for $s\ge1$ ([[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]).





## Verification

**Proof technique:** evaluate both sides of the product identity at the infinitely many toral points $K=z^n$, using the Gaussian Vandermonde identity proved from the weighted-subset model of the ordinary Gaussian binomial; deduce the shift and stability identities by direct factorization; then span all shifts by the stable pair $\{K^{\delta}T_t\}$ and read off independence from the extremal exponents.

1.1 The simple torus and its evaluation. Fix $i$ and write $z=q_i$, $K=K_i$, $T_t=T_{i,0,t}$. By [F2], $T_t=\prod_{s=1}^{t}(Kz^{1-s}-K^{-1}z^{s-1})/(z^{s}-z^{-s})$, a Laurent polynomial in $K$ whose support is contained in $[-t,t]$: indeed each factor equals $Kz^{1-s}(1-K^{-2}z^{2s-2})$, so $T_t=K^{t}\prod_{s=1}^{t}z^{1-s}(1-K^{-2}z^{2s-2})/\prod_{s=1}^{t}(z^{s}-z^{-s})$, a $\mathbb Q(q)$-combination of $K^{t-2l}$ for $0\le l\le t$, and the two extreme coefficients (of $K^{t}$ and of $K^{-t}$) are nonzero by [F5]. Evaluating at $K=z^n$ for an integer $n\ge0$ gives $T_t(z^n)=\prod_{s=1}^{t}[n-s+1]_z/[s]_z$ with $[m]_z=(z^{m}-z^{-m})/(z-z^{-1})$, which equals the Gaussian binomial $\binom{n}{t}_z$ when $t\le n$, and equals $0$ when $t>n$ (the factor $s=n+1$ has numerator $z^{0}-z^{0}=0$); accordingly we use the convention $\binom{n}{t}_z=0$ for $t>n$ or $t<0$. Finally, if a Laurent polynomial in $K$ with coefficients in $\mathbb Q(q)$ vanishes at $K=z^n$ for infinitely many $n$, it is zero: after multiplying by a power of $K$ it becomes a polynomial whose infinitely many pairwise distinct roots $z^n$ force all coefficients to vanish (the $z^n$ are distinct because $q$ is transcendental by [F5]). [F2, F5, algebra]

1.2 The Gaussian Vandermonde. For an indeterminate $Q$ and $m,r\ge0$ put $[m]_Q:=1+Q+\cdots+Q^{m-1}$ and $B(m,r):=[m]_Q!/([r]_Q![m-r]_Q!)$, so that with $Q=z^2$ one has $B(m,r)=\binom{m}{r}_{z^2}$ in the notation of [F1]. We first record the weighted-subset model: if $\mathrm{inv}(S):=\#\{(a,b):a<b,\ a\notin S,\ b\in S\}$ for $S\subseteq\{1,\dots,m\}$, then $B(m,r)=\sum_{S\subseteq\{1,\dots,m\},\,|S|=r}Q^{\mathrm{inv}(S)}$. Indeed both sides equal $1$ for $m=r=0$ and $0$ for $r<0$ or $r>m$; splitting the subsets according to whether $m$ lies in $S$ gives the recurrence $B(m,r)=B(m-1,r)+Q^{m-r}B(m-1,r-1)$ for the right-hand side, and the same recurrence holds for the left-hand side because $B(m-1,r)+Q^{m-r}B(m-1,r-1)=\frac{[m-1]_Q!}{[r]_Q![m-r]_Q!}([m-r]_Q+Q^{m-r}[r]_Q)=\frac{[m-1]_Q![m]_Q}{[r]_Q![m-r]_Q!}=B(m,r)$, using $[m-r]_Q+Q^{m-r}[r]_Q=[m]_Q$. Splitting a subset $S\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}$ with $|S|=s$ into $A:=S\cap\{1,\dots,r\}$, $|A|=j$, and $B':=S\cap\{r+1,\dots,n\}$, the inversion count splits as $\mathrm{inv}(S)=\mathrm{inv}(A)+\mathrm{inv}(B')+(r-j)(s-j)$, because a crossing inversion $a<b$ with $a\in\{1,\dots,r\}\setminus A$, $b\in B'$ can always be realized, while pairs $(b,a)$ with $b\in\{r+1,\dots,n\}$, $a\le r$ are never inversions; hence for $0\le r\le n$, $B(n,s)=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(r,s)}Q^{(r-j)(s-j)}B(r,j)B(n-r,s-j).$ [F1, algebra]

1.3 The shift and stability identities. Put $N_s(c)=Kz^{c-s+1}-K^{-1}z^{-c+s-1}$. Since $N_s(c+1)=N_{s-1}(c)$ for $s\ge2$, factoring the first $t-1$ common factors reduces the shift to $N_1(c+1)-z^tN_t(c)=K^{-1}z^{-c+t-1}(z^t-z^{-t})$, proving $T_{c+1,t}=z^tT_{c,t}+K^{-1}z^{-c+t-1}T_{c,t-1}$. The product definition also gives $(z^{t+1}-z^{-t-1})T_{t+1}=T_t(Kz^{-t}-K^{-1}z^t)$. Rearranging yields $K^{-1}T_t=z^{-2t}KT_t-z^{-t}(z^{t+1}-z^{-t-1})T_{t+1}$; multiplying by $Kz^{2t}$ yields $K^2T_t=z^{2t}T_t+z^t(z^{t+1}-z^{-t-1})KT_{t+1}$. Every coefficient is in $A$. [F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 Normalization to the symmetric Gaussian binomials. By the identification of [[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]], $\binom{m}{r}_z=z^{-r(m-r)}\binom{m}{r}_{z^2}=z^{-r(m-r)}B(m,r)$ for $0\le r\le m$. First multiply step 1.2 by $B(n,r)$ and use the factorial identity $B(n,r)B(n-r,s-j)=B(n,r+s-j)B(r+s-j,r)$. Then multiply by $z^{-r(n-r)}z^{-s(n-s)}$ and substitute $B(m,r)=z^{r(m-r)}\binom{m}{r}_z$ in each term, the powers of $z$ on the right-hand side combine to $2(r-j)(s-j)+(r+s-j)(n-r-s+j)+r(s-j)+j(r-j)-r(n-r)-s(n-s)=rs-jn,$ as a direct expansion shows; in the resulting identity $\binom{n}{r}_z\binom{n}{s}_z=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(r,s)}z^{rs-jn}\binom{n}{r+s-j}_z\binom{r+s-j}{r}_z\binom{r}{j}_z$ both sides are zero when $n<r$ or $n<s$ (then $r+s-j\ge\max(r,s)>n$ for every $j$, so the factor $\binom{n}{r+s-j}_z$ vanishes), and the identity therefore holds for all $n,r,s\ge0$ under the convention of step 1.1. [step 1.2, F1, algebra]

3.1 The product identity. With notation as in step 1.1, we claim $T_rT_s=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(r,s)}z^{rs}K^{-j}\binom{r+s-j}{r}_z\binom{r}{j}_zT_{r+s-j}$ for all $r,s\ge0$. Both sides lie in $\mathbb Q(q)[K^{\pm1}]$: the left side by [F2], the right side because the coefficients are in $A\subseteq\mathbb Q(q)$ and $T_{r+s-j}$ is a Laurent polynomial in $K$ [F2]. Evaluating at $K=z^n$ and using step 1.1, the left side becomes $\binom{n}{r}_z\binom{n}{s}_z$ and the right side becomes $\sum_jz^{rs-jn}\binom{r+s-j}{r}_z\binom{r}{j}_z\binom{n}{r+s-j}_z$, and these agree for every $n\ge0$ by step 2.1. By the evaluation principle of step 1.1 the two Laurent polynomials coincide, proving the identity; its coefficients lie in $A$ by [F1]. [step 1.1, step 2.1, F1, F2, algebra]

4.1 Spanning and the simple-torus basis. Let $M:=A\text{-span}\{K^{\delta}T_t:\delta\in\{0,1\},\,t\ge0\}$. First, $K^{m}T_t\in M$ for all $m\in\mathbb Z$, $t\ge0$: for $m\ge2$, multiply the second identity of step 1.3(b) by $K^{m-2}$ to get $K^{m}T_t=z^{2t}K^{m-2}T_t+z^{t}(z^{t+1}-z^{-t-1})K^{m-1}T_{t+1}$, which reduces $|m|$ and lies in $M$ by induction on $|m|$ from the base $m=0,1$; for $m=-1$ use the first identity of step 1.3(b) directly; for $m\le-2$, multiply it by $K^{m+1}$ to get $K^{m}T_t=z^{-2t}K^{m+2}T_t-z^{-t}(z^{t+1}-z^{-t-1})K^{m+1}T_{t+1}$, again reducing $|m|$. Second, all $T_{c,t}\in M$ by induction on $t$ (the case $t=0$ gives $T_{c,0}=1$): for $c\ge0$ the shift identity $T_{c+1,t}=z^tT_{c,t}+K^{-1}z^{-c+t-1}T_{c,t-1}$ of step 1.3 (with unit coefficient $z^t\in A^\times$) iterates upward from $T_{0,t}=T_t\in M$, and for $c\le-1$ its other solution $T_{c,t}=z^{-t}T_{c+1,t}-K^{-1}z^{-c-1}T_{c,t-1}$ iterates downward from $c=0$. Third, $M$ is an $A$-subalgebra: $(K^{m}T_r)(K^{n}T_s)=K^{m+n}T_rT_s$ expands by step 3.1 into the terms $z^{rs}\binom{r+s-j}{r}_z\binom{r}{j}_zK^{m+n-j}T_{r+s-j}$, which lie in $M$ by the first part. Since $M$ contains $K^{\pm1}$ and every $T_{c,t}$, it contains the generated subalgebra; conversely $K^{\delta}T_t$ is a product of the generators $K$ and $T_{0,t}$, so $M$ equals the $A$-subalgebra generated by $K^{\pm1}$ and all $T_{c,t}$, which is the simple-torus part of $U_A^0$. Fourth, $\{K^{\delta}T_t\}$ is $A$-independent: if a finite combination with coefficients $(a_{\delta,t})$ is zero and not all coefficients vanish, let $N:=\max\{t:a_{0,t}\ne0\text{ or }a_{1,t}\ne0\}$; the exponent $N+1$ of $K$ occurs only in $KT_N$ (each $T_t$ has support in $[-t,t]$ by step 1.1, and $K^{\delta}T_t$ with $t\le N$ can reach exponent $N+1$ only for $(\delta,t)=(1,N)$), and its coefficient of $K^{N+1}$ is the nonzero extreme coefficient of $T_N$, so $a_{1,N}=0$; then the exponent $-N$ occurs only in $T_N$ ($K^{1}T_t$ has support in $[1-t,1+t]$ with $1-t>-N$ for $t\le N$), forcing $a_{0,N}=0$; descending on $N$ gives all coefficients zero. Hence $\{K^{\delta}T_t\}$ is an $A$-basis of $M$, as claimed in (a). [step 3.1, step 1.3, F1, F2, algebra]

5.1 The full torus. By [F3], $U_q^0=\mathbb Q(q)[P^{\vee}]$ with basis-like multiplication $K_hK_{h'}=K_{h+h'}$; by [F4], $L=\bigoplus_i\mathbb Z(d_ih_i)$ is free of rank $|I|$ and the $K_{d_ih_i}=K_i$ are algebraically independent. Let $B$ be the $A$-subalgebra of $\mathbb Q(q)[L]$ generated by the $K_i^{\pm1}$ and all $T_{i,c,t}$; since $\mathbb Q(q)[L]=\bigotimes_i\mathbb Q(q)[K_i^{\pm1}]$ and the generators with distinct $i$ lie in distinct tensor factors, step 4.1 applied in each factor gives $B=A\text{-span}\{\prod_iK_i^{\delta_i}T_{i,0,t_i}:\delta_i\in\{0,1\},t_i\ge0\}$ with the displayed products forming an $A$-basis. In $\mathbb Q(q)[P^{\vee}]=\bigoplus_{c}K_c\,\mathbb Q(q)[L]$, the direct sum running over the chosen representatives (whose existence and explicitness: the $D$-components of a fixed coset form a coset of $L$ in the finitely generated free group $D$, and the elements of minimal coordinate absolute-value sum are finitely many by boundedness of the coordinates, so the lexicographic tie-break is a finite comparison; the complementary component is determined by the coset because $L\subseteq D$ by [F4]), every product of generators of $U_A^0$ lies in $\bigoplus_cK_cB$ because $K_{h_1}\cdots K_{h_m}=K_{\sum h_l}$ and the $T$-products lie in $B$; conversely $K_c\in U_A^0$ and $B\subseteq U_A^0$ (each $K_i^{\delta_i}T_{i,0,t_i}$ is a product of generators). Hence $U_A^0=\bigoplus_cK_cB$, and the union of $K_c$ times the $A$-basis of $B$ over the representatives is an $A$-basis of $U_A^0$, proving (b). The construction uses only finitely many explicit coordinate comparisons per coset and no choice function on an infinite family; no integral PBW basis, canonical basis or modified-algebra input is used, and no freeness is inferred from torsion-freeness over the non-PID ring $A$. [step 4.1, F3, F4, algebra] ∎
