## Example

Let $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ be the rank-one Cartan datum with $\lambda(h_1)=m\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$,
and let $L(m)$ be the explicit simple module with basis $v_0,\dots,v_m$,
$Kv_t=q^{m-2t}v_t$, $Fv_t=[t+1]_qv_{t+1}$,
$Ev_t=[m-t+1]_qv_{t-1}$
([[ex-quantum-verma-and-simple-modules-for-sl-two]],
[[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]). Let
$A=\mathbb Z[q,q^{-1}]$ and let
$V_A=\sum_{t=0}^mAF^{(t)}v_0\subseteq L(m)$ be the lattice generated by the
highest weight vector under the divided powers of the integral form $U_A$
([[def-lusztig-divided-power-integral-form]]). Let $k$ be a field of
characteristic $0$, made an $A$-algebra by $q\mapsto1$, and put
$\bar V=V_A\otimes_Ak$.

Then $V_A$ is free over $A$ with basis $F^{(t)}v_0$ ($0\le t\le m$), and
$\bar V$ is a $k$-vector space of dimension $m+1$ with basis the images
$\bar v_t$ on which
$$\bar K=1,\qquad \bar F\bar v_t=(t+1)\bar v_{t+1},\qquad \bar E\bar v_t=(m-t+1)\bar v_{t-1},\qquad \bar T_{0,1}\bar v_t=(m-2t)\bar v_t,$$
with $\bar v_{-1}=\bar v_{m+1}=0$. This is exactly the classical
$(m+1)$-dimensional simple representation of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ on the coroot
basis: $\bar T_{0,1}$ is the Cartan generator $h$ acting by the weight $m-2t$,
and $\bar E,\bar F$ are the classical raising and lowering matrices, so the
weight multiplicities are $\dim_k\bar V_\mu=1$ for $\mu=m,m-2,\dots,-m$ and $0$
otherwise. For $m=2$ the specialized matrices are
$\bar E\bar v_0=0$, $\bar E\bar v_1=2\bar v_0$, $\bar E\bar v_2=\bar v_1$ and
$\bar F\bar v_0=\bar v_1$, $\bar F\bar v_1=2\bar v_2$, $\bar F\bar v_2=0$, with
$\bar T_{0,1}=\operatorname{diag}(2,0,-2)$, the standard adjoint-type triple.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The rank-one datum, the simple module $L(m)$ with its string basis, the lattice $V_A$, and the specialization $A\to k$, $q\mapsto1$.

[F1] $F^{(t)}v_0=\frac{1}{[t]!}F^tv_0$, the vectors $F^tv_0$ ($0\le t\le m$) are $\mathbb Q(q)$-independent, and the action on the string is $Kv_t=q^{m-2t}v_t$, $Fv_t=[t+1]_qv_{t+1}$, $Ev_t=[m-t+1]_qv_{t-1}$ ([[ex-quantum-verma-and-simple-modules-for-sl-two]], [[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]).

[F2] Gaussian binomials with nonnegative upper entry lie in $A$ ([[lem-quantum-pascal-recurrence-and-gaussian-integrality]]). Also $[t]!\in A$ and $[t]!\ne0$; the divided powers $E^{(r)},F^{(r)}$, the $K^{\pm1}$ and the $T_{c,t}$ generate $U_A$; on a weight vector of weight $\mu$ the toral binomial $T_{c,t}$ acts by the Gaussian value $\binom{\mu(h)+c}{t}_q$, preserving any stable lattice to which that supplier applies ([[def-lusztig-divided-power-integral-form]], [[lem-q-one-toral-specialization-identifies-the-classical-cartan-generators]] (b)).

[F3] On a $U_A$-stable label-graded lattice the specialization has $\bar K=\mathrm{id}$, $\bar T_{i,c,t}$ acting by the integer binomials and the classical Cartan commutators; the specialization is defined by base change of the integral form, never by substituting into rational expressions ([[lem-q-one-toral-specialization-identifies-the-classical-cartan-generators]] (b), [[prop-specialization-at-q-one-recovers-the-classical-enveloping-relations]] (c),(d)).

[F4] The local deformation lemma [[lem-local-q-one-deformation-proves-integrable-highest-weight-universality]] (a) gives equality of quantum and classical highest-weight characters. Its part (c) identifies the $q=1$ fibre of the specified ordinary-word lattice $V_R=\sum_w R F_wv_0$, over $R=\mathbb Q[q]_{(q-1)}$, with the classical simple module. In rank one the quantum string is the $(m+1)$-dimensional module of [[ex-quantum-verma-and-simple-modules-for-sl-two]] (a); the actual lattice comparison is checked below.



## Verification

**Proof technique:** check the lattice is preserved by the integral generators with A-valued matrices, tensor with k, and compare the resulting matrices with the classical rank-one simple representation.

1.1 The lattice. Put $v_t=F^{(t)}v_0$, $0\le t\le m$. These vectors are $\mathbb Q(q)$-independent by [F1], so $V_A=\bigoplus_{t=0}^mAv_t$ is free. For $r\ge0$, repeated application of the string formulas and division by $[r]!$ give $F^{(r)}v_t=\binom{t+r}{r}_qv_{t+r}$ if $t+r\le m$, and $0$ otherwise; likewise $E^{(r)}v_t=\binom{m-t+r}{r}_qv_{t-r}$ if $r\le t$, and $0$ otherwise. The coefficients are Laurent integral by the Gaussian identities. Also $K^{\pm1}v_t=q^{\pm(m-2t)}v_t$, and $T_{c,s}v_t=\binom{m-2t+c}{s}_qv_t\in Av_t$, including negative upper labels by the generalized-binomial identity of [F3]'s supplier. Thus every generator of $U_A$ preserves $V_A$. Each $v_t$ is obtained by a divided power from $v_0$, so $V_A=U_Av_0$. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

1.2 Specialization. Base change of the explicit free basis to $k$ gives $\bar V=V_A\otimes_Ak$ has the $k$-basis $\bar v_0,\dots,\bar v_m$ and dimension $m+1$. Substituting $q=1$ is legitimate here because the action matrices in the basis $F^{(t)}v_0$ are known explicitly with entries in $A$: $\bar K=\mathrm{id}$, $\bar F\bar v_t=(t+1)\bar v_{t+1}$, $\bar E\bar v_t=(m-t+1)\bar v_{t-1}$ and $\bar T_{c,s}\bar v_t=\binom{m-2t+c}{s}\bar v_t$, in particular $\bar T_{0,1}\bar v_t=(m-2t)\bar v_t$; this is the operator form of [F3] on the graded lattice. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 Comparison with the classical module. The displayed operators satisfy $[\bar E,\bar F]=\bar T_{0,1}$, $[\bar T_{0,1},\bar E]=2\bar E$ and $[\bar T_{0,1},\bar F]=-2\bar F$, including the string ends, so they define a classical $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-module. It is simple: if $x=\sum_{t=0}^T c_t\bar v_t\ne0$ with $c_T\ne0$, then $\bar E^T x=c_T\prod_{j=1}^T(m-j+1)\bar v_0\ne0$ in characteristic zero, and $\bar F^t\bar v_0=t!\bar v_t$ generates every basis vector. The empty product covers $T=0$ and $m=0$. This is the simple highest-weight module of weight $m$. To apply [F4](c) to this lattice, note that $[t]!$ is an $R$-unit since its value at $q=1$ is $t!\ne0$. Thus $R\otimes_A V_A=\sum_{t=0}^m R F^tv_0=V_R$. Since $A\to k$ factors through $R$, base change of its specified fibre gives precisely $\bar V$; [F4](a) separately gives the character comparison. The $m=2$ matrices follow from step 1.2. Freeness here is proved by the explicit string basis, without global freeness or choice. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
