## Definition

Let $(I,A,D,P,P^{\vee},q)$ be a symmetrizable Cartan datum for a quantum
group with $a_{ii}=2$ for all $i$
([[def-symmetrizable-cartan-datum-for-a-quantum-group]]), let
$U_q(\mathfrak g)$ be its Drinfeld–Jimbo algebra
([[def-drinfeld-jimbo-quantized-enveloping-algebra]]) with $q_i=q^{d_i}$ and
$K_i=K_{d_ih_i}$, and set

$$A:=\mathbb Z[q,q^{-1}]\ \subseteq\ \mathbb Q(q),$$

the image of the Laurent polynomial ring $\Lambda_1=\mathbb Z[t]_{S}$ of
[[def-the-laurent-polynomial-ring]] under the unital ring homomorphism sending
$t$ to the indeterminate $q$; this homomorphism is injective and $A$ is a
subring of $\mathbb Q(q)$ (Verification, step 1.1). We work over this
commutative domain $A$ inside the field $\mathbb Q(q)$.

For $i\in I$, $c\in\mathbb Z$ and $t\ge0$ define the **toral quantum binomial**

$$T_{i,c,t}:=\begin{bmatrix}K_i;c\\ t\end{bmatrix}:=\prod_{s=1}^{t}\frac{K_iq_i^{\,c-s+1}-K_i^{-1}q_i^{\,-c+s-1}}{q_i^{\,s}-q_i^{\,-s}}\in U_q(\mathfrak g),$$

the empty product being $T_{i,c,0}:=1$. Each factor is defined in
$U_q(\mathfrak g)$ because $q_i^{\,s}-q_i^{\,-s}\ne0$ for $s\ge1$
([[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]), so
$T_{i,c,t}$ is a polynomial in $K_i^{\pm1}$ with coefficients in $\mathbb Q(q)$;
in particular all $T_{i,c,t}$ commute with the $K_h$ and with one another
(Verification, step 2.1).

The **Lusztig divided-power integral form** is the $A$-subalgebra

$$U_A:=\bigl\langle E_i^{(r)},\ F_i^{(r)},\ K_h^{\pm1},\ T_{i,c,t}\ \colon\ i\in I,\ r,t\ge0,\ c\in\mathbb Z,\ h\in P^{\vee}\bigr\rangle\ \subseteq\ U_q(\mathfrak g),$$

where $x^{(r)}=x^r/[r]_i!$ are the divided powers of
[[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]. Write $U_A^\pm$
for the $A$-subalgebras generated by the $E_i^{(r)}$ and by the $F_i^{(r)}$,
and $U_A^0$ for the one generated by the $K_h^{\pm1}$ and the $T_{i,c,t}$.

Then:

(i) $U_A$ contains $E_i=E_i^{(1)}$, $F_i=F_i^{(1)}$ and all $K_h$;

(ii) $U_A$ is a root-graded $A$-subalgebra of the root-graded
$\mathbb Q(q)$-algebra $U_q(\mathfrak g)$ ([[def-positive-negative-and-toral-quantum-subalgebras]]):
with $U_A[\beta]:=U_A\cap U_q(\mathfrak g)[\beta]$ one has
$U_A=\bigoplus_{\beta\in Q}U_A[\beta]$, each $U_A[\beta]$ is an $A$-submodule,
and $U_A^\pm,U_A^0$ are graded in the same way;

(iii) $U_A\otimes_A\mathbb Q(q)\cong U_q(\mathfrak g)$ canonically, i.e. the
$\mathbb Q(q)$-span of $U_A$ is all of $U_q(\mathfrak g)$.

The shifted binomials $T_{i,c,t}$ with $c\ne0$ are included because the
divided-power reordering identity of
[[lem-divided-power-and-toral-quantum-binomial-reordering-identities]] has
coefficients $T_{i,2t-a-b,t}$ with a nonconstant index; the specializations at
$q=1$ of these elements are computed in
[[lem-q-one-toral-specialization-identifies-the-classical-cartan-generators]],
and the integral triangular decomposition and the specialization are treated
only after this definition.

No freeness of $U_A$ or of its parts over $A$, no independence of the listed
generators, and no triangularity of the products of the three parts is asserted
here. In particular $A$ is not a principal ideal domain (Verification, step
7.1), so no torsion-free-to-free inference over $A$ is permitted anywhere on
this page.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A symmetrizable Cartan datum with $a_{ii}=2$; the algebra $U_q(\mathfrak g)$ with its root grading and subalgebras $U_q^-,U_q^0,U_q^+$; the Laurent polynomial ring $\Lambda_1$ and the indeterminate $q$ over $\mathbb Q$; and the divided powers $x^{(r)}=x^r/[r]_i!$ of [[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]].

[F1] $\Lambda_1=\mathbb Z[t]_S$ ($S=\{t^k:k\ge0\}$) has the universal property: for every ring with unit $B$ and every unit $u\in B$ there is a unique unital ring homomorphism $\Lambda_1\to B$ with $t\mapsto u$; every element of $\Lambda_1$ has a unique finite expansion $\sum_ka_kt^k$ with $a_k\in\mathbb Z$; and the sum-of-coefficients map $\varepsilon\colon\Lambda_1\to\mathbb Z$ is a unital ring homomorphism ([[def-the-laurent-polynomial-ring]]).

[F2] $q$ is an indeterminate over $\mathbb Q$: $\mathbb Q(q)$ is the fraction field of $\mathbb Q[q]$, and no nonzero polynomial over $\mathbb Q$ vanishes at $q$; $q_i=q^{d_i}$ with $d_i>0$ ([[def-symmetrizable-cartan-datum-for-a-quantum-group]]).

[F3] $U_q(\mathfrak g)$ is graded by $Q=\bigoplus_i\mathbb Z\alpha_i$ with $\deg E_i=\alpha_i$, $\deg F_i=-\alpha_i$, $\deg K_h=0$, and $U_q(\mathfrak g)=\bigoplus_\beta U_q(\mathfrak g)[\beta]$ is a direct sum; $U_q^0$ is a quotient of the commutative group algebra $\mathbb Q(q)[P^{\vee}]$, so $U_q^0$ is commutative and $K_hK_{h'}=K_{h+h'}$, $K_0=1$; and $U_q(\mathfrak g)[\beta]$ is a $\mathbb Q(q)$-subspace for every $\beta$ ([[def-drinfeld-jimbo-quantized-enveloping-algebra]], [[def-positive-negative-and-toral-quantum-subalgebras]]).

[F4] $[m]_i!=\prod_{k=1}^m[k]_i\ne0$ for every $m\ge0$, $x^{(1)}=x$ and $x^{(0)}=1$, and $[m]_i=(q_i^m-q_i^{-m})/(q_i-q_i^{-1})$ ([[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]).

[F5] The subalgebras $U_q^-,U_q^0,U_q^+$ are generated by the $F_i$, the $K_h$, and the $E_i$ respectively, and these elements together generate $U_q(\mathfrak g)$ as a $\mathbb Q(q)$-algebra; the rank-one subalgebra generated by $E_i,F_i,K_i^{\pm1}$ is described by [[thm-quantized-sl-two-string-formulas]] (used only as the published divided-power convention reference) ([[def-positive-negative-and-toral-quantum-subalgebras]], [[def-drinfeld-jimbo-quantized-enveloping-algebra]]).

[F6] A subring of a field is a domain; if $M$ is an $A$-submodule of a $\mathbb Q(q)$-vector space and $0\ne d\in A$, then $m\mapsto m\otimes d^{-1}$ identifies $M$ with its image modulo the evident relation, and the canonical map $M\otimes_A\mathbb Q(q)\to U_q(\mathfrak g)$, $\sum_im_i\otimes s_i\mapsto\sum_is_im_i$, is injective: writing $s_i=a_id^{-1}$ with $a_i\in A$ and $0\ne d\in A$, the relation $\sum_ia_im_i=0$ follows from the vanishing of $\sum_is_im_i$ by multiplying by $d$ ([[def-the-laurent-polynomial-ring]] for the coefficient calculus).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** identify $A$ inside $\mathbb Q(q)$ by the universal property of $\Lambda_1$ and uniqueness of Laurent coefficients; compute the binomials inside the commutative toral subalgebra; then read off homogeneity, the grading, the generation statement and the change-of-rings isomorphism.

1.1 The assignment $t\mapsto q$ defines a unital ring homomorphism $\Lambda_1\to\mathbb Q(q)$ by the universal property of [F1], since $q$ is a unit of the field $\mathbb Q(q)$. It is injective: if $\sum_ka_kq^k=0$ with finitely many $a_k\ne0$, choose $N\ge0$ so that every $k+N\ge0$; then the polynomial $\sum_ka_kT^{k+N}\in\mathbb Q[T]$ vanishes at the indeterminate $q$, so by [F2] it is the zero polynomial and all $a_k=0$. Hence its image $A$ is a subring of $\mathbb Q(q)$, and in particular $A$ is a commutative domain. [given, F1, F2, construct]

2.1 Each factor $N_{i,c,s}:=K_iq_i^{\,c-s+1}-K_i^{-1}q_i^{\,-c+s-1}$ is a $\mathbb Q(q)$-linear combination of the toral elements $K_{d_ih_i}$ and $K_{-d_ih_i}$, and the denominator $q_i^{\,s}-q_i^{\,-s}=q_i^{\,-s}(q_i^{\,2s}-1)$ is nonzero for $s\ge1$: $q_i^{\,2s}=1$ would make the indeterminate $q$ a root of the nonzero polynomial $T^{2d_is}-1$ by [F2]. Hence every factor, and therefore $T_{i,c,t}$, is a well-defined element of $U_q^0$; expanding the product and using $K_hK_{h'}=K_{h+h'}$ and $K_iK_i^{-1}=1$ of [F3], each $T_{i,c,t}$ is a finite $\mathbb Q(q)$-linear combination of the toral elements $K_{md_ih_i}$ ($m\in\mathbb Z$), that is, a polynomial in $K_i^{\pm1}$. Since $U_q^0$ is commutative by [F3], all $T_{i,c,t}$ commute with every $K_h$ and with one another. [step 1.1, F2, F3, algebra]

3.1 The listed generators are homogeneous for the root grading of [F3]: $E_i^{(r)}=E_i^r/[r]_i!$ has degree $r\alpha_i$ because $\deg E_i=\alpha_i$ and the grading is multiplicative with $[r]_i!\ne0$ by [F4]; likewise $F_i^{(r)}$ has degree $-r\alpha_i$; and $K_h^{\pm1}$ and, by step 2.1, $T_{i,c,t}$ have degree $0$. [step 2.1, F3, F4, algebra]

4.1 By definition $U_A$ consists of the finite $A$-linear combinations of finite products of the listed generators, the empty product $1$ included. Every finite product of homogeneous elements is homogeneous of the sum of their degrees (the grading of [F3] is multiplicative), so every element of $U_A$ is a finite sum of homogeneous elements of $U_A$. Since $U_q(\mathfrak g)=\bigoplus_\beta U_q(\mathfrak g)[\beta]$ is a direct sum, this decomposition is unique, so $U_A=\bigoplus_\beta U_A[\beta]$ with $U_A[\beta]=U_A\cap U_q(\mathfrak g)[\beta]$; the same argument applies to the generated $A$-subalgebras $U_A^\pm$ (generators of degrees $\pm r\alpha_i$) and $U_A^0$ (degree-$0$ generators), which are therefore graded in the same way. Each $U_A[\beta]$, being the intersection of the $A$-submodule $U_A$ with a $\mathbb Q(q)$-subspace, is an $A$-submodule. [step 3.1, F3, algebra]

5.1 Since $E_i=E_i^{(1)}$, $F_i=F_i^{(1)}$ by [F4] and the $K_h$ are among the listed generators, $U_A$ contains $E_i$, $F_i$ and $K_h$; consequently its $\mathbb Q(q)$-span contains all generators of $U_q(\mathfrak g)$, so by [F5] the $\mathbb Q(q)$-span of $U_A$ is all of $U_q(\mathfrak g)$. [step 4.1, F4, F5, algebra]

6.1 The canonical multiplication map $\Phi\colon U_A\otimes_A\mathbb Q(q)\to U_q(\mathfrak g)$, $\sum_iu_i\otimes s_i\mapsto\sum_is_iu_i$ is surjective by step 5.1. It is injective by the argument of [F6]: given $\sum_iu_i\otimes s_i$ with $s_i=a_id^{-1}$, $a_i\in A$, $0\ne d\in A$, one has $\sum_iu_i\otimes s_i=\bigl(\sum_ia_iu_i\bigr)\otimes d^{-1}$, and $\Phi(\sum_iu_i\otimes s_i)=d^{-1}\sum_ia_iu_i=0$ forces $\sum_ia_iu_i=0$ in the $\mathbb Q(q)$-vector space $U_q(\mathfrak g)$ because $d\ne0$; hence $\sum_iu_i\otimes s_i=0$. Thus $\Phi$ is an isomorphism, which is claim (iii). [step 5.1, F6, algebra]

7.1 Finally, $A$ is not a principal ideal domain: the ideal $I=(2,q-1)\subseteq A$ is proper, since a relation $1=2a+(q-1)b$ would give $1=2\varepsilon(a)$ under the ring homomorphism $\varepsilon\colon A\to\mathbb Z$ with $q\mapsto1$ of [F1], in which $2a$ contributes an even integer. If $I=(d)$ were principal, then from $d\mid2$ and the domain $A$ one gets $2=de$, and reducing modulo the ring homomorphism $A\to\mathbb F_2[q,q^{-1}]$, $q\mapsto q$ (again [F1]) gives $0=\bar d\,\bar e$; if $\bar d=0$ then $q-1=df$ reduces to $q+1=0$, a contradiction, so $\bar d\ne0$ and hence $\bar e=0$, that is, $e=2e'$ for some $e'\in A$. Then $2=2de'$ and therefore $de'=1$ because $A$ is a domain with $2\ne0$, so $d$ is a unit and $1\in I$, contradicting the properness of $I$. Hence no principal ideal equals $I$. This is why no freeness, independence or triangularity statement is attached to $U_A$ in this definition; those are separate theorems with their own hypotheses. [step 1.1, step 6.1, F1, algebra] ∎
