## Definition

Let $(\mathcal X,\Sigma)$ be a measurable instance space, let $\{0,1\}$ have its full discrete sigma-algebra, and give $\mathcal Z:=\mathcal X\times\{0,1\}$ the product sigma-algebra. For each $k\in\mathbb N$, give $\mathcal Z^k$ its finite product sigma-algebra, with $\mathcal Z^0=\{()\}$. Let $\mathcal H$ be a nonempty class of measurable functions $c:\mathcal X\to\{0,1\}$. For $m,d\in\mathbb N$, write $[m]:=\{j\in\mathbb N:1\le j\le m\}$, so $[0]=\varnothing$, and give
$$\mathcal I_{m,d}:=\{I\subseteq[m]:|I|\le d\}$$
its discrete sigma-algebra. For $d\in\mathbb N$, a **deterministic sample-compression scheme of size at most $d$** consists of a measurable compression map $\kappa_m:\mathcal Z^m\to\mathcal I_{m,d}$ for every $m\in\mathbb N$, and fixed reconstruction maps $G_k:\mathcal Z^k\times\mathcal X\to\{0,1\}$ for $1\le k\le d$ and $G_0:\mathcal X\to\{0,1\}$, all measurable for the stated product and discrete sigma-algebras. If $S=(z_1,\ldots,z_m)$ and $I=\kappa_m(S)=\{i_1<\cdots<i_k\}$, write $S_I=(z_{i_1},\ldots,z_{i_k})$ and define $g(S_I)(x):=G_k(S_I,x)$; for $I=\varnothing$, use $G_0$. The scheme is **consistent** if, for every $c\in\mathcal H$, every $m\in\mathbb N$, and every $S=((x_1,c(x_1)),\ldots,(x_m,c(x_m)))$, this reconstruction satisfies $g(S_I)(x_j)=c(x_j)$ for every $j\in[m]$. The reconstruction map uses only the ordered selected labeled entries and no other side information; the reconstructed classifier need not belong to $\mathcal H$.
