## Definition

Let $(\mathsf X,\Sigma_X)$, $(\mathsf Y,\Sigma_Y)$, and $(\mathsf A,\Sigma_A)$ be nonempty measurable spaces. A **source/target pair of joint laws** consists of probability measures $P_S$ and $P_T$ on $(\mathsf X\times\mathsf Y,\Sigma_X\otimes\Sigma_Y)$. For $D\in\{S,T\}$, its input marginal is the probability measure $\mu_D$ on $(\mathsf X,\Sigma_X)$ defined by $\mu_D(A)=P_D(A\times\mathsf Y)$. If $h:\mathsf X\to\mathsf A$ is measurable and $\ell:\mathsf A\times\mathsf Y\to[0,+\infty]$ is product-measurable, then $(x,y)\mapsto(h(x),y)$ is measurable: the preimage of a measurable rectangle $U\times V$ is $h^{-1}(U)\times V$, and such rectangles generate the product sigma-algebra. Taking preimages once more shows that $(x,y)\mapsto\ell(h(x),y)$ is measurable. The domain risk is the extended nonnegative integral
$$R_D(h):=\int_{\mathsf X\times\mathsf Y}\ell(h(x),y)\,P_D(d(x,y)).$$

A **conditional label kernel** for $P_D$ is a specified probability kernel $K_D$ from $(\mathsf X,\Sigma_X)$ to $(\mathsf Y,\Sigma_Y)$ such that, for every nonnegative product-measurable $g:\mathsf X\times\mathsf Y\to[0,+\infty]$,
$$\int g(x,y)\,P_D(d(x,y))=\int_{\mathsf X}\left(\int_{\mathsf Y}g(x,y)\,K_D(x,dy)\right)\mu_D(dx).$$
The pair is under **covariate shift** when conditional label kernels $K_S,K_T$ are supplied, there is a measurable set $N$ with $\mu_T(N)=0$ such that $K_S(x,\cdot)=K_T(x,\cdot)$ as probability measures for every $x\notin N$, and $\mu_T\ll\mu_S$. No existence of conditional kernels is asserted by this definition.
