## Definition

Fix integers $n\ge1$, $k>1$, and $M>1$. Write $[r]=\{1,\ldots,r\}$ for a positive integer $r$, and let
$$
\Delta_k=\left\{p\in[0,1]^k:\sum_{i=1}^k p_i=1\right\},\qquad \mathcal E=\Delta_k^M.
$$
An oblivious contextual bandit instance consists of reward vectors $x_t\in[0,1]^k$ and advice matrices $E(t)\in\mathcal E$, for $t\in[n]$, all fixed before the learner's private randomization. The matrix $E(t)$ is revealed before the round-$t$ action; its row $E(t)_m=(E(t)_{m,1},\ldots,E(t)_{m,k})$ is expert $m$'s probability distribution over the $k$ actions. The expected reward of expert $m$ on round $t$ is $\langle E(t)_m,x_t\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^k E(t)_{m,i}x_{t,i}$.

At round $t$, the learner's pre-action history consists of its earlier actions and observed rewards. Given that history and the revealed advice, it selects a distribution $P_t\in\Delta_k$, draws a measurable finite-valued action $A_t\in[k]$ with conditional probabilities $\mathbb P(A_t=i\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})=P_{t,i}$, and observes only $X_t=x_{t,A_t}$. Here $\mathcal F_{t-1}$ is the sigma-algebra generated by the pre-action history. For every fixed expert sequence and reward sequence, the **expected regret against the best fixed expert** is
$$
R_n=\mathbb E\left[\max_{m\in[M]}\sum_{t=1}^n\langle E(t)_m,x_t\rangle-\sum_{t=1}^n X_t\right],
$$
where expectation is over the learner's action draws. The regret random variable is bounded, so this expectation is finite. This protocol makes no stochastic generalization claim and imposes no shared-structure condition on the experts; it does not cover advice or rewards selected in response to the learner's current action.
