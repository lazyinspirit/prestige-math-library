## Definition

Fix integers $k\ge1$ and $1\le p\le k$, and put $[k]=\{1,\ldots,k\}$. Let $\mathbb F_2=\{0,1\}$ with addition and multiplication modulo $2$. For a factor subset $G\subseteq[k]$, its incidence vector $1_G\in\mathbb F_2^k$ has coordinate $1$ at $j$ exactly when $j\in G$. Choose $p$ subsets $G_1,\ldots,G_p$ whose incidence vectors are linearly independent over $\mathbb F_2$, and choose signs $s_i\in\{-1,+1\}$. Using the run space and product columns of [[def-two-level-full-factorial-design]], define the **regular fraction**

$$\mathcal F=\{x\in\{-1,+1\}^{[k]}:x_{G_i}=s_i\text{ for }1\le i\le p\},\qquad x_G=\prod_{j\in G}x_j.$$

The corresponding design is called a regular $2^{k-p}$ fractional factorial; [[thm-regular-fractional-factorial-aliasing-criterion]] proves that $\mathcal F$ has exactly $2^{k-p}$ runs, including the case $p=k$ with one run. The choices $s_i=-1$ are alternate fractions and are part of the definition.

A **signed word** is a pair $(\epsilon,A)$ with $\epsilon\in\{-1,+1\}$ and $A\subseteq[k]$. It evaluates at $x$ as $\epsilon x_A$ and multiplies by

$$(\epsilon,A)(\delta,B)=(\epsilon\delta,A\triangle B),\qquad A\triangle B=(A\setminus B)\cup(B\setminus A).$$

The identity word is $(+1,\varnothing)$. The signed generator $h_i=(s_i,G_i)$ evaluates to $+1$ on $\mathcal F$. The **defining subgroup** $H$ is generated by $h_1,\ldots,h_p$; the alias theorem proves it is exactly the subgroup of signed words that evaluate to $+1$ on every run in $\mathcal F$.

For $A,B\subseteq[k]$, their restricted sign columns are **equal-column aliases** when $x_A=x_B$ for every $x\in\mathcal F$. They are **signed aliases with sign $\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}$** when $x_A=\sigma x_B$ on every run; sign $+1$ is the equal-column case and sign $-1$ records opposite columns. The alias theorem gives the exact criterion for each sign.

The **word length** of $(\epsilon,A)$ is $|A|$, the number of factor letters, independent of $\epsilon$. The **resolution** is the minimum $|A|$ among nonidentity words $(\epsilon,A)\in H$; it is written as a Roman numeral. Since $p\ge1$ and the generators are independent, $H$ has a nonidentity word of nonzero length.

## Remarks

- Equal or opposite design columns make the corresponding unrestricted linear-model coefficients nonseparable; see [[thm-factorial-column-aliases-prevent-separate-identification]]. A hierarchy or sparsity premise is an additional modeling assumption, not a consequence of the fraction.
- The resolution records only the shortest defining word. It does not describe the complete alias structure; that is given by the whole subgroup $H$.
