## Statement

Let $M$ be a compact smooth three-manifold, possibly nonorientable and with boundary, and let $f:F\to M$ be a smooth embedding of a compact smooth surface with $f^{-1}(\partial M)=\partial F$. Transversality to $\partial M$ is not assumed. Given an open neighborhood $U$ of $C=f(\partial F)$, there is a jointly smooth map $(x,t)\mapsto f_t(x)$ such that each $f_t$ is an embedding, $f_0=f$, every $f_t$ agrees with $f$ on $\partial F$ and outside $f^{-1}(U)$, and $f_t$ is neatly proper for every $t>0$. Time may be reparametrized to be stationary near both endpoints.

There is also a jointly continuous ambient isotopy of homeomorphisms $H_t:M\to M$, starting at the identity, supported in $U$, fixing $\partial M$ pointwise, and satisfying $H_t f=f_t$. The construction makes $H_t$ jointly smooth on $(M\setminus C)\times[0,1]$. If $f$ is already neat, the same $f_t$ alternatively admits a jointly smooth ambient extension fixing $\partial M$. No smooth ambient extension at an original tangency is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The compact smooth input manifolds, boundary-preimage embedding and prescribed neighborhood.

[F1] The Euclidean inverse theorem gives local smooth inverses for maps of full rank. Local vector-field flows exist uniquely and depend smoothly on initial points and parameters. [[thm-euclidean-inverse-function-theorem]] [[thm-picard-lindelof-local-existence-and-uniqueness]] [[thm-smooth-dependence-of-ode-solutions-on-parameters]]

[F2] The compact-source isotopy-extension construction uses finite graph charts, normalized finite smooth bumps and a finite compact-time flow continuation. Its same finite estimates apply to the boundary-supported fields below. [[lem-choice-free-isotopy-extension-for-a-compact-smooth-source]]

## Proof

1.1 If $\partial F$ is empty, take $f_t=f$ and $H_t=\mathrm{id}$. Otherwise $C$ is a finite disjoint union of smooth circles: a compact one-manifold is covered by finitely many connected interval charts, each contained in one component. We detail the finite differential data used below. On the compact $\partial M$, choose finitely many smaller coordinate charts whose closures lie in larger charts, with the smaller charts covering it. The ordinary Euclidean bump on each larger chart, positive on its smaller closure, has a positive finite sum. Divide by that sum to obtain smooth weights, and sum the coordinate Euclidean quadratic forms with those weights. This is a positive definite smooth metric, since at every point at least one summand is positive definite. Construct an inward field on $\partial M$ similarly from finitely many boundary-coordinate inward vectors. Its inward normal derivative is positive, since it is a positive weighted sum of positive numbers. Extend the finite coefficients to slightly larger half-charts. The flows in [F1] give a collar $c_M:\partial M\times[0,\delta)\to M$: at time zero its derivative is the direct sum of the boundary tangent map and that inward vector, hence invertible. Finite inverse neighborhoods and compact separation give one common positive width and injectivity. Precisely, close initial points lie in a common shrunk local inverse chart; initial points outside those chart neighborhoods form a compact set of distinct pairs with a positive separation of their images at time zero, and uniform small flow displacement retains that separation. The same finite inward-field construction supplies a source collar of $\partial F$. There is no choice of a countable cover here. [F1, F2, given, construct]

2.1 Let $L$ be the metric normal line bundle of $C$ in $\partial M$. It has a smooth metric and local unit frames, whose transitions are signs. There is a smooth normal tube $E$ from $|u|<\delta$ in $L$ into $\partial M$, with $E(s,0)=s$ and differential at zero equal to the inclusion of $T_sC\oplus L_s$. Here is a construction with the necessary uniformity. In the finite metric charts of step 1.1, the coefficients of the geodesic equation are the smooth functions obtained from the inverse metric matrix and its first derivatives. Apply [F1] to this finite system with initial point $s$ and initial velocity $u\in L_s$, for time one and uniformly small $|u|$; zero initial velocity is the constant solution, so finite compact bounds give this time interval. Uniqueness makes the local solutions agree in overlapping charts. Differentiating at $u=0$ gives $dE(v,w)=v+w$, which is invertible. The finite inverse-chart and separated-basepoint argument of step 1.1 makes $E$ injective on one uniform disk bundle. For clarity, shrink finitely many base inverse charts and take a positive Lebesgue number for that finite cover of $C$. Two sufficiently close basepoints with sufficiently small normal vectors belong to one inverse neighborhood. Pairs of basepoints farther apart form a compact set with positive ambient separation, which sufficiently small normal displacements cannot erase. This proves global injectivity, including between different components. Compose $E$ with $c_M$ to obtain normal half-plane bundle coordinates $\Psi(s,u,q)=c_M(E(s,u),q)$, $q\ge0$, near $C$. Reducing the common width makes their image lie in $U$. [F1, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 In the source collar, express $f$ in those coordinates as $(\beta(p,r),u(p,r),q(p,r))$. At $r=0$, $\beta$ is the boundary identification, and $u=q=0$. After identifying the source boundary with $C$, its derivative in the boundary direction is the identity. For one common smaller $r$ interval, $\beta_r:C\to C$ is a diffeomorphism. Indeed in arclength coordinates its derivative is uniformly positive and close to one; it is homotopic to the identity and has a lift $b_r(s)$ with $b_r(s+1)=b_r(s)+1$ and positive derivative, hence is globally one-to-one and onto on each circle. Smooth local inverses supplied by [F1] agree uniquely and give a smooth inverse family including $r=0$. Reparametrize the source collar by these inverses, so the first coordinate of $f$ is exactly $s$. The remaining normal vector is $(u(s,r),q(s,r))$, with $q(s,r)>0$ for $r>0$ by the boundary-preimage hypothesis. Its inward derivative at zero is nonzero: otherwise the two surface derivatives would both be tangent to $C$, contradicting the immersion rank of $f$. In local unit frames write $(u,q)=r\,v(s,r)$, where $v(s,r)=\int_0^1\partial_r(u,q)(s,\tau r)\,d\tau$. This vector is smooth up to $r=0$. Compactness gives $|v|\ge m>0$ on a smaller common collar. Thus the radius $R=|(u,q)|=r|v(s,r)|$ is smooth, and $\partial_rR(s,0)=|v(s,0)|\ge m$. Reduce the width so $\partial_rR\ge m/2$. It follows that a common $R$ interval has a unique inverse $r=r(s,R)$ smooth jointly through $R=0$: extend the smooth expression $r|v(s,r)|$ to negative $r$ in the local boundary chart and apply [F1], then use uniqueness to paste. This avoids differentiating an unsigned norm at zero. [F1, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 In these radius source coordinates the direction is $v/|v|$, smooth through zero. In a unit frame of $L$, write it as $(\cos\theta(s,R),\sin\theta(s,R))$. For $R>0$, $0<\theta<\pi$, while at zero the value may be either endpoint. The angle is smooth through zero: the usual smooth local argument function is defined on a neighborhood of each nonzero unit direction, including the two horizontal endpoint directions, and its value in $[0,\pi]$ is uniquely fixed by $q\ge0$. Changing the unit frame replaces $\theta$ by $\pi-\theta$. Choose a smooth radial cutoff $\chi$ equal to one near zero and zero before the outer radius of this tube, with values in $[0,1]$, and choose $0<\varepsilon<1$. Set $\theta_t=(1-t\varepsilon\chi(R))\theta+t\varepsilon\chi(R)\pi/2$. Replacing the direction of $f$ by $(\cos\theta_t,\sin\theta_t)$, with its basepoint and radius unchanged, defines $f_t$ on this collar, and keep $f$ elsewhere. The formulas are smooth through $R=0$, in $t$, and at the cutoff seam. They are equivariant under frame reversal. At zero, $\theta_t$ lies strictly in $(0,\pi)$ for $t>0$, so the derivative of the new inward normal coordinate is $\sin\theta_t(s,0)>0$: the new surface is neat. At positive radius the direction remains strictly inside that interval, so there are no new boundary points. The surface collar is embedded since projection onto $(s,R)$ is its inverse. Choose the original tube small enough that it misses the compact image of the complement of a slightly larger source collar; compactness and injectivity of $f$ give that separation. The modification is supported in a still smaller radial tube, so it cannot meet that unchanged image or the unchanged outer collar. Thus every $f_t$ is a global smooth embedding. [F1, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 There is an explicit topological ambient extension. At a point of positive normal radius $R$, let $\alpha\in[0,\pi]$ be its angle and put $k(s,R,t)=\tan(\theta_t(s,R)/2)/\tan(\theta(s,R)/2)>0$. Map its angle by $\tan(\alpha'/2)=k\tan(\alpha/2)$, fixing $0,\pi$, and preserve $s,R$. This is a strictly increasing interval homeomorphism taking $\theta$ to $\theta_t$. The half-angle formula defines it smoothly near zero; near $\pi$ use the reciprocal cotangent formula. For every $R>0$ the coefficient $k$ is finite, positive and smooth in $(s,R,t)$, including $t=0$, so the ambient map and inverse are jointly smooth there. Set $H_t(s,0)=s$. Radius preservation gives continuity jointly at $R=0$ independently of any blowup of $k$: every image and inverse image is at the same radius, and their distance from $s$ tends uniformly to zero on the compact base. The inverse has exactly the same construction with $k$ replaced by $1/k$. At time zero $k=1$, so $H_0$ is the identity. Outside the cutoff region $k=1$ as well, allowing extension by the identity to all of $M$. The endpoints $\alpha=0,\pi$ are fixed, so this extension fixes the whole ambient boundary pointwise. Frame reversal sends $\alpha,\theta,\theta_t$ to $\pi-\alpha,\pi-\theta,\pi-\theta_t$ and $k$ to $1/k$; the half-angle transformation is equivariant under exactly this replacement. Hence these formulas define a global isotopy even for a twisted normal line bundle and a nonorientable ambient manifold. They satisfy $H_tf=f_t$ by construction. [step 2.1, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 If $f$ was already neat, the angles at zero are bounded away from $0,\pi$ on the compact boundary. The whole track $f_t$ is then uniformly neat, including $t=0$. Apply the finite graph-field construction [F2] to its prescribed velocity. It can be made zero on $\partial M$: in a neat chart with ambient boundary coordinate $q$, the velocity on the surface vanishes at $q=0$, and is therefore $q$ times a smooth vector coefficient, by $w(s,q)=q\int_0^1\partial_qw(s,\tau q)\,d\tau$. Extend that coefficient off the surface in its transverse graph coordinate and retain the factor $q$. Finite normalized bump sums retain its specified values on the track and its zero boundary values. Compact source and time give a finite cover with one common field bound and local existence interval; finitely many successive flow intervals, as in [F2], produce the smooth ambient extension fixing $\partial M$ and supported in $U$. This is an alternative to the explicit angular homeomorphisms, which need not be smooth at $C$. For a tangent input no such smooth extension is asserted: a boundary-preserving diffeomorphism preserves equality of the surface tangent plane with the ambient boundary plane, so it cannot remove that tangency. [F1, F2, step 4.1, construct]

7.1 Reparametrize time by a smooth nondecreasing function constant near its two endpoints to make the asserted isotopies stationary there, if desired. In that version neatness is asserted after the initial stationary interval, rather than during it; equivalently the un-reparametrized family is neat at every positive time. Every metric, field, collar, angle chart, bound and lift used above was selected from finite compact data, and all transitions were explicitly checked. The ambient maps preserve all topological embedded-disk and product-neighborhood properties, since they are homeomorphisms fixing the boundary. This last statement is topological; no automatic preservation of a separately required smooth compression witness at the original tangencies is inferred. [F1, F2, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct] ∎
