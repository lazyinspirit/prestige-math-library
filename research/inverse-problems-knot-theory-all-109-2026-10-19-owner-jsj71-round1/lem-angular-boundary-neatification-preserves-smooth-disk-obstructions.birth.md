## Statement

Let $F_0=f(F)$ be a compact two-sided smoothly embedded surface in a compact smooth three-manifold, with $f^{-1}(\partial M)=\partial F$, and let $F_1,H_1$ be its angular neatification from [[lem-compact-smooth-boundary-preimage-surfaces-admit-neat-embedding-isotopies]]. The ambient manifold may be nonorientable. If $F_1$ has a smooth compressing disk or smooth boundary-compressing disk, then $F_0$ has a disk of the same type. Consequently absence of either kind of smooth disk passes from $F_0$ to $F_1$.

Here the boundary-compressing witness has one boundary arc in the surface and the other in $\partial M$, its interior missing both. The construction allows the broader convention of ordinary smooth disk embeddings with tangential endpoint contact, and also gives smooth corner-disk witnesses with transverse surface arcs when that convention is used. No smooth ambient removal of an original surface tangency is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The two-sided boundary-preimage surface, its explicit angular neatification and a smooth disk witness for the neat final surface.

[F1] The angular neatification is a smooth embedding isotopy, with a boundary-fixing topological ambient extension that is jointly smooth off $C=\partial F_0$. In normal coordinates it preserves the base coordinate $s$ and normal radius $R$. The final surface is neat. [[lem-compact-smooth-boundary-preimage-surfaces-admit-neat-embedding-isotopies]]

[F2] Two neat smooth spanning arcs in a parametrized annulus with the same endpoints and winding number admit a smooth ambient strip isotopy, fixed on collars where already matched. Its proof supplies the finite bigon and lens moves. [[lem-choice-free-smooth-torus-curves-and-annulus-strips]]

[F3] Finite graph embedding isotopies extend by compactly supported smooth fields and flows; the Euclidean inverse theorem gives the graph and collar coordinates used here. [[lem-choice-free-isotopy-extension-for-a-compact-smooth-source]] [[thm-euclidean-inverse-function-theorem]]

## Proof

1.1 First suppose $F_1$ has a compressing disk. Since $F_1$ is neat, choose a smooth inward field on $\partial M$ tangent to $F_1$ wherever the two meet, and extend it tangent to $F_1$ near that boundary. In adapted neat pair charts the inward coordinate vector has exactly these properties; a finite positive bump sum retains them, and charts away from $F_1$ have supports disjoint from it. Its small positive flow is a smooth embedding of $M$ into $M$, sends its boundary strictly inward, and preserves $F_1$. Uniqueness of the extended local flows shows that a trajectory initially outside $F_1$ cannot enter it. Apply this flow to the disk if necessary. Its boundary now lies in $\operatorname{Int}F_1$ and its whole image in $\operatorname{Int}M$. Its essential boundary remains essential: the surface flow only removes a smaller outer collar. A disk in $F_1$ bounded by the moved circle cannot contain any of those deleted collars, since each reaches an actual boundary circle of $F_1$ and misses the moved circle; hence such a disk could be pulled back to the old surface core. The disk is now a compact subset of $M\setminus C$. Pull it back by $H_1^{-1}$, which is a smooth diffeomorphism there by [F1]. The resulting smooth disk misses $F_0$ in its interior and has essential boundary on it. This proves the compression assertion without changing tangency jets by an ambient diffeomorphism at $C$. [F1, F3, given, construct]

2.1 For a boundary-compressing disk $D_1$, the pullback $D=H_1^{-1}(D_1)$ is an embedded disk smooth away from precisely its two boundary endpoints $p\in C$. Its surface boundary arc and ambient-boundary arc are smooth away from those endpoints, and the surface arc is essential because $H_1$ identifies the abstract surfaces. Choose disjoint small endpoint charts. We give a local replacement at one endpoint, leaving the disk and both boundary arcs unchanged outside that chart. If $F_0$ is neat there, its surface and ambient-boundary faces have ordinary quadrant coordinates. A smooth local disk patch exists in that quadrant, including the ordinary smooth-boundary convention: with $a(s)>0$ for $s<0$, $a=0$ for $s\ge0$, and $b(s)=a(-s)$, choose $a$ smooth and flat at zero, and use $(s,t)\mapsto(s,t+a(s),t+b(s))$, $t\ge0$, in the two positive face coordinates. Its boundary lies alternately on the two faces and its interior misses both; its derivatives at the endpoint are the independent vectors $(1,0,0),(0,1,1)$. A standard quadrant disk can instead be used for a disk-with-corners convention. The matching to the old disk away from the endpoint is proved in steps 5.1–6.1 below. [F1, F3, construct]

3.1 At a tangency, $T_pF_0=T_p\partial M$. In a normal half-plane chart $(s,u,q)$ of [F1], $q\ge0$, the inverse theorem therefore writes the surface as $q=h(s,u)$ for $u\ge0$ near that point, after changing the sign of $u$ if needed. Here $h(s,0)=0$ and $h(s,u)>0$ for $u>0$, with $h$ smooth up to the edge. Hadamard's integral formula gives $h(s,u)=u\,k(s,u)$ with smooth $k(s,u)=\int_0^1h_u(s,\tau u)\,d\tau$; it is positive for $u>0$. Also $h_u(s,0)\ge0$. Let $C_0$ bound $|h_{uu}|$ on a compact chart and choose $L>C_0$. Smooth quadrant patches on the two possible sides of this surface are
$\ P_-(s,r)=(s,r+s,r\,k(s,r+s))$
and
$\ P_+(s,r)=(s,r-s,h(s,r)+Lsr)$,
for $s,r\ge0$ small. At $s=0$ both match the surface ray, and at $r=0$ they lie in the ambient boundary on its respective sides $u>0,u<0$. Both are full-rank graph embeddings, including the endpoint. For $P_-$ its interior has $0<q<h(s,u)$, since $r/(r+s)<1$. For $P_+$ the interior has $q>0$ and, when $u=r-s\ge0$, the estimate $h_u(s,\xi)\ge-C_0r$ for $0\le\xi\le r$ gives $h(s,r)-h(s,r-s)+Lsr\ge(L-C_0)sr>0$. Where $u<0$ the surface is absent. Thus the patches lie on the desired side and meet neither face in their interiors. They are transverse to the two faces along their open boundary arcs, away from the endpoint. [F3, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 For an ordinary smooth disk rather than a corner disk, a smooth boundary join at the endpoint is also explicit. Choose the flat functions $a,b$ of step 2.1, put $r=t+a(s)$ and $v=b(s)+t^2+t a(s)$ for $t\ge0$, and replace the preceding two patches by
$\ Q_-(s,t)=(s,r+v,r\,k(s,r+v))$
and
$\ Q_+(s,t)=(s,r-v,h(s,r)+Lrv)$.
For $t=0,s<0$ these lie on $F_0$; for $t=0,s>0$ they lie in $\partial M$. Their boundary curves join smoothly through the endpoint with tangent the $s$ direction, because $a,b$ are flat. At that endpoint the inward derivative has $u$ component one and $q$ component $h_u(p)=0$, hence is independent of the boundary tangent. In a small common chart $\partial_t(r+v)>0$ and $\partial_t(r-v)=1-2t-a(s)>0$, so projection onto $(s,u)$ proves immersion and injectivity on the entire patch. In the interior $r>0,v>0$, and exactly the estimates of step 3.1 prove $0<q<h(s,u)$ below, or $q>h(s,u)$ above wherever that graph exists. Along the open surface boundary arc, $v_t=a(s)>0$; along the other open boundary arc $q_t>0$. These give transversality to the respective faces away from the endpoint. The new surface arc may approach $C$ tangentially in this ordinary smooth-boundary version; it still has the same essential relative arc class. The quadrant version of step 3.1 gives a transverse surface arc when required instead. [F3, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 We verify that these local patches can actually replace the old endpoint disk; existence of an unrelated model disk would not suffice. Choose $s=0$ at $p$ in the normal half-plane coordinates of [F1], and use the frontier $s^2+R^2=\delta^2$, $q\ge0$, for small $\delta$. It meets $C$ in two poles. Since $H_1$ preserves $s,R$, it preserves this frontier. The original smooth embedded $D_1$ meets it near its endpoint in one arc for every sufficiently small $\delta$. Indeed its local parametrization has injective linear derivative, and on each source radial ray the derivative of the ambient distance from its endpoint is the norm of that linear derivative plus a uniformly small error; it is positive on the compact source semicircle or quadrant. Thus each such ray meets the frontier once, while compact separation excludes other disk portions. The same argument applies to each smooth model patch. Their frontier arcs and endpoints avoid the two poles, so they are smooth in the pullback chart. They span the two edges of the same frontier region: below $F_0$ this region has explicit smooth strip coordinates $-\delta<s<\delta$, $0\le w=\alpha/\theta(s,R)\le1$; above use $w=(\alpha-\theta)/(\pi-\theta)$. Here $R=(\delta^2-s^2)^{1/2}>0$, and $\theta$ is the surface angle of [F1]. Thus no disk parameter or smoothing has been inferred at the poles. If the old arc is tangent to a strip edge, first perturb $D_1$ near that frontier endpoint, away from $C$, to make it transverse to its corresponding face: add $\varepsilon$ times the inward source-boundary coordinate to the nonnegative face coordinate, with a small finite cutoff. This is an arbitrarily small $C^1$ perturbation of an embedding, remains on the same face side, and leaves its boundary on that face. The graph projection lower bound on a finite compact chart cover, together with compact separation of distant source points, proves embedding stability for this perturbation. It makes both old frontier ends neat. [F1, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 Now move the frontier endpoints along the two strip edges to match those of the model, using finitely many compactly supported smooth edge flows and their graph extensions. The two resulting neat spanning arcs are contained in a compact rectangle of the strip. Embed that rectangle in an annulus; their relative winding is zero, since both lie in that one rectangle. The isotopy [F2] matches them, with chosen smaller endpoint collars. All these strip moves stay away from the poles. They extend into a frontier collar by its coordinates $(s,w,\rho)$, where $\rho=(s^2+R^2)^{1/2}$: multiply their velocity fields by a radial cutoff. The faces are $w=0,1$, so the extensions preserve them and still avoid $C$. After the arcs and their parameters match, glue the inner model subdisk to the old outer disk along that frontier arc. In a smaller collar each has a graph in a transverse frontier coordinate over the common arc parameter and $\rho$. Their values agree at the seam. Smooth the two graph functions across it by a cutoff convolution, fixed at the outer collar ends; this retains an embedded graph. At the two boundary endpoints use the already matched face collars, where the same graph interpolation retains the appropriate face. Compact positive separation from the other face off those collars ensures that the interior remains in the strict same-side region. This produces a smooth embedded disk, not just a homeomorphic image: it is the original smooth disk off the finitely many endpoint replacements, an explicit full-rank smooth graph patch at each replaced point, and an explicit smooth graph across each seam. Its surface boundary arc only changed within half-disk source collar neighborhoods of its two endpoints, so it remains in the original essential relative arc class. Its other boundary arc remains in $\partial M$, and the interior misses both faces. [F2, F3, step 5.1, construct]

7.1 Apply this replacement at the two endpoints in disjoint charts, choosing the above- or below-face patch according to the side occupied by the old disk. This gives a smooth boundary-compressing disk for $F_0$, with the ordinary smooth-boundary or smooth-corner interpretation specified in steps 3.1–4.1. Together with step 1.1 it proves both asserted implications. No step smoothed an arbitrary locally flat topological disk: the only nonsmooth points of the pulled-back witness were two known endpoints, the angular map was smooth everywhere else, and full-rank replacement patches and their finite frontier matches were supplied explicitly. All choices and replacements were finite, and nonorientability changes none of these local charts. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 6.1, construct] ∎
