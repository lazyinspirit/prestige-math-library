## Statement

Assume the full Axiom of Choice. Let $p,q$ be coprime nonzero integers, let $K=T(p,q)$ be the oriented primitive slope curve in the standard Heegaard torus, and let $\rho_t(z_1,z_2)=(e^{2\pi i p t}z_1,e^{2\pi i q t}z_2)$ be the weighted circle action on the unit $S^3\subset\mathbb C^2$. Choose $N(K)$ to be a sufficiently small closed metric tube around the orbit $K$ for the round metric; this tube is invariant because $\rho_t$ is isometric and preserves $K$. Set $E_K=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}N(K)$. Let $x$ and $y$ be based generators of the fundamental groups of the two solid-torus pieces obtained by cutting $E_K$ along the annulus left on the Heegaard torus, oriented so that the annulus core maps to $x^p$ and $y^q$. Then

$$\pi_1(E_K)=\langle x,y\mid x^p=y^q\rangle.$$

The common element $h=x^p=y^q$ is central and represents a regular fiber of a Seifert fibration of $E_K$ over a disk. The fibration has two distinguished core fibers of multiplicities $|p|$ and $|q|$; a fiber of multiplicity one is regular, and a fiber of multiplicity greater than one is exceptional. Thus there are exactly two exceptional fibers when $|p|,|q|>1$, and one is regular when one absolute parameter is $1$.

For any integers $\alpha,\beta$ with $p\alpha+q\beta=1$, compatible based paths identify the positive normal-disk meridian and preferred longitude in the peripheral system as

$$\mu=y^\alpha x^\beta,\qquad \lambda=h\mu^{-pq}.$$

Here the positive meridian is the oriented boundary of a normal disk, and the preferred longitude has degree one around $K$ and maps to zero in $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)$. Their homology classes form a basis of $H_1(\partial E_K;\mathbb Z)$, so both classes are primitive.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Full AC, coprime nonzero integers $p,q$, the chosen knot exterior and based paths, and the specified Heegaard torus.

[A1] Full AC is the axiom that every family of nonempty sets has a choice function ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[L1] Full AC implies the Axiom of Countable Choice, which is the hypothesis of the exterior and peripheral-system definitions ([[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]], [[def-countable-choice]]).

[F1] [[def-coprime-torus-knot]] identifies $K$ with the oriented primitive slope $p\alpha+q\beta$ on the standard unknotted Heegaard torus.

[F2] [[def-knot-exterior]] sets $E_K=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}N(K)$ with its boundary torus, and [[def-knot-meridian-and-preferred-longitude]] defines the positive meridian by the normal-disk boundary and the preferred longitude by degree one and zero exterior homology.

[F3] Under full AC, [[thm-knot-exterior-homology-and-longitude]] proves $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$ generated by the positive meridian, proves existence and uniqueness of the preferred-longitude class, and proves that their boundary classes form a basis of $H_1(\partial E_K;\mathbb Z)$.

[F4] [[thm-seifert-van-kampen]] identifies the fundamental group of a union from a path-connected open cover with the pushout of the two inclusion-induced maps from the intersection.

## Proof


**Given:** The knot, exterior, Heegaard splitting and AC hypotheses in the Statement.

1.1 The primitive slope $p\alpha+q\beta$ is essential on the Heegaard torus. Removing a small annular neighborhood of $K$ from that torus leaves a closed annulus $A$. On either side, removing the corresponding half of the tube from a solid torus only removes a boundary collar, so the two pieces retract to solid tori $W_x,W_y$ and their overlap retracts to $A$. Thicken the pieces by half-collars to obtain path-connected open sets covering $E_K$ whose intersection retracts to $A$. The core of $A$ winds $p$ times around the core generator $x$ on one side and $q$ times around $y$ on the other. Full AC [A1] supplies the countable-choice hypothesis of [F2] by [L1]. [given, A1, F1, F2, L1, construct]

1.2 The action $\rho_t$ is isometric for the round metric. Its generic orbits have slope $(p,q)$ and trivial stabilizer because $\gcd(p,q)=1$; hence the metric tube chosen in the Statement is invariant. The coordinate circles $z_1=0$ and $z_2=0$ have stabilizer orders $|q|$ and $|p|$ respectively. The orbit space is a sphere: on each torus $|z_1|=r$ with $0<r<1$, quotienting by the primitive circle orbit leaves a circle, and the two end circles collapse to the two coordinate-orbit points. Removing the invariant tube removes a disk from the orbit sphere, so the exterior is fibered over a disk with the stated multiplicities. Multiplicity one is the regular local model; multiplicity greater than one is exceptional. [F1, given, construct]

2.1 The three fundamental groups in this cover are $\pi_1(W_x)=\langle x\rangle\cong\mathbb Z$, $\pi_1(W_y)=\langle y\rangle\cong\mathbb Z$, and $\pi_1(A)=\langle a\rangle\cong\mathbb Z$, with inclusion maps $a\mapsto x^p$ and $a\mapsto y^q$. By [F4], $\pi_1(E_K)$ is their pushout. A group receiving maps from the two cyclic factors is determined by images of $x,y$; the maps agree on the intersection exactly when $x^p=y^q$. Hence the pushout has presentation $\langle x,y\mid x^p=y^q\rangle$. [F4, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 A regular orbit parallel to $K$ represents the annulus core $x^p=y^q=h$. This element commutes with $x$ because it is $x^p$, and commutes with $y$ because it is $y^q$; as $x,y$ generate the group from step 2.1, $h$ is central. [step 2.1, step 1.2, algebra]

3.2 Choose integers $c,d$ with $pd-qc=1$ and let $L=c\alpha+d\beta$ be the corresponding simple curve on the Heegaard torus; its algebraic intersection with $K$ is one. At the intersection, the boundary $m$ of a small normal disk splits into arcs $\gamma_x,\gamma_y$ on the two solid-torus sides, while $L$ splits into its crossing arc and complementary arc $L_1$. Inserting $L_1L_1^{-1}$ into $m=\gamma_x\gamma_y$ gives $m=(\gamma_xL_1)(L_1^{-1}\gamma_y)$. The first factor winds $c$ times around the $x$ core and the second winds $-d$ times around the $y$ core, hence $m=x^c y^{-d}$. Its abelianization under $x\mapsto q$, $y\mapsto p$ is $cq-dp=-1$, so it is opposite the positive normal-disk meridian, whose class is the positive generator by [F3]. Thus $\mu=m^{-1}=y^d x^{-c}$. Setting $\alpha=d$ and $\beta=-c$ gives $p\alpha+q\beta=1$ and the stated word; another Bezout solution changes the exponents by $(qk,-pk)$, so the word is unchanged because $y^{qk}=h^k=x^{pk}$ and $h$ is central. [A1, L1, F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, algebra, construct]

4.1 Abelianizing the presentation in step 2.1 gives the relation $p[x]=q[y]$. The map $x\mapsto q$, $y\mapsto p$ is surjective because $\gcd(p,q)=1$, and its kernel in $\mathbb Z^2$ is generated by $(p,-q)$, exactly the relation vector; hence it identifies this quotient with $\mathbb Z$. Step 3.2 sends the positive meridian to $1$, so by [F3] it is the positive generator of $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)$. Thus $[h]=p[x]=pq[\mu]$. The regular orbit $h$ is a degree-one parallel of $K$ on $\partial E_K$, and $\mu$ has degree zero there, so $\lambda=h\mu^{-pq}$ has degree one and zero image in $H_1(E_K)$. Uniqueness in [F3] identifies it with the preferred longitude; [F3] also proves that $[\mu],[\lambda]$ form a basis of $H_1(\partial E_K;\mathbb Z)$, so each is primitive. [L1, F2, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, algebra] ∎
