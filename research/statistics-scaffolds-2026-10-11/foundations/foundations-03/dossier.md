## Core claims and proof evidence

### Asymptotic scale and the delta method

Use \(X_n=O_P(1)\) to mean bounded in probability and \(X_n=o_P(1)\) to mean convergence to zero in probability. The existing library has convergence-in-probability and weak-convergence definitions, real-valued Slutsky, vector CMT, scalar and vector CLTs, but no \(O_P/o_P\) notation or general vector Slutsky item. The notation should be defined by tail probabilities, not assumed to be self-explanatory.

For finite-dimensional random vectors \(T_n\in\mathbb R^d\), \(r_n\to\infty\), and \(r_n(T_n-\theta)\Rightarrow Z\), differentiability of \(g:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^k\) at \(\theta\) gives
\[
r_n\{g(T_n)-g(\theta)\}=Dg(\theta)\,r_n(T_n-\theta)+o_P(1)\Rightarrow Dg(\theta)Z.
\]
The deterministic Taylor remainder is \(o(\|T_n-\theta\|)\); weak convergence makes \(r_n(T_n-\theta)\) tight, so scaling the remainder by \(r_n\) gives \(o_P(1)\). CMT and Slutsky then give the limit. Bartlett, Lecture 16, theorem/proof pp. 10–12, states the linearization and proves it from the Taylor remainder; Duchi, Lecture 2, theorem/proof pp. 2–3, independently gives the same argument. Bartlett’s statement is Euclidean vector-valued. The standard second-order scalar variant is justified when \(g\) is twice differentiable and \(Dg(\theta)=0\): \(r_n^2(g(T_n)-g(\theta))\Rightarrow \tfrac12 Z^T D^2g(\theta)Z\). Duchi, Lecture 2, p. 3, proves this and gives a Bernoulli/KL example; it also warns that a vanishing first derivative makes the first-order limit degenerate.

**Hypotheses that must remain visible:** the input limit is distributional and has a tight limit; \(g\) is differentiable at the target for first order; second order requires a second-order expansion and zero first derivative. A delta-method result cannot infer asymptotic normality when the Jacobian annihilates the Gaussian directions. For vector conclusions, the proof needs vector Slutsky/joint convergence, which the present library does not yet supply as a named theorem.

### M- and Z-estimators and consistency

For IID observations with law \(P\), a criterion \(m_\theta(x)\), and parameter set \(\Theta\), define
\[
M_n(\theta)=P_nm_\theta=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n m_\theta(X_i),\qquad M(\theta)=Pm_\theta.
\]
An M-estimator is a measurable exact or approximate maximizer of \(M_n\); its target is a separated maximizer \(\theta_0\) of \(M\). A Z-estimator is a measurable exact or approximate root of
\[
\Psi_n(\theta)=P_n\psi_\theta,\qquad \Psi(\theta)=P\psi_\theta.
\]
A differentiable interior M-estimator may induce a Z-estimating equation by setting its derivative to zero. The notions should remain separate: non-smooth M-estimators need not have a natural score/root equation. Bartlett, Lecture 15, pp. 2–6, gives these definitions and the Uniform[0, θ] example where the likelihood changes support and the MLE is a sample maximum without a natural Z equation.

A useful conditional consistency theorem is:

- M case: if \(\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}|M_n(\theta)-M(\theta)|=o_P(1)\); for every \(\varepsilon>0\), \(\sup_{d(\theta,\theta_0)\ge\varepsilon}M(\theta)<M(\theta_0)\); and the selected estimator satisfies \(M_n(\hat\theta_n)\ge M_n(\theta_0)-o_P(1)\), then \(\hat\theta_n\to_P\theta_0\).
- Z case: if \(\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\|\Psi_n(\theta)-\Psi(\theta)\|=o_P(1)\); \(\Psi(\theta_0)=0\) is separated by \(\inf_{d(\theta,\theta_0)\ge\varepsilon}\|\Psi(\theta)\|>0\); and \(\Psi_n(\hat\theta_n)=o_P(1)\), then \(\hat\theta_n\to_P\theta_0\).

Bartlett, Lecture 15, theorem and proof pp. 7–10, proves the M statement by splitting the criterion gap into the two uniform approximation errors and the approximate-maximization error; it reduces Z consistency to the M theorem using \(M_n=-\|\Psi_n\|\). van der Vaart, Lecture 5 §5.3, Theorems 5.8 and 5.10, pp. 54–56, independently proves almost-sure versions under a \(P\)-Glivenko–Cantelli class, separated population maximum/zero, and exact maximum/root. Chen, UW STAT 581, lines 100–118, explicitly identifies these with van der Vaart Theorems 5.7 and 5.9 and gives approximate-maximizer/root versions.

**Proof dependency:** uniform convergence is the mathematical workhorse, not a consequence of a pointwise law of large numbers for an arbitrary parameter-indexed family. van der Vaart derives concrete uniform convergence from Glivenko–Cantelli classes and entropy conditions. The current library has ordinary SLLNs, weak laws, and empirical-measure weak convergence, but no uniform law/Glivenko–Cantelli supplier. State the theorem conditionally or use a separate foundations-04 supplier placed before it; do not hide a ULLN inside “identifiability.” For finite parameter sets, the SLLN suffices and Stanford Lecture 3 p. 4 proves this simple case.

### Asymptotic normality of estimating equations

A high-level multivariate statement with proof is van der Vaart’s Theorem 6.19, Lecture 6 §6.5, pp. 73–74. Let \(X_i\) be IID \(P\), \(\psi_\theta:X\to\mathbb R^d\), and suppose:

1. \(P\psi_{\theta_0}=0\) and \(\hat\theta_n\to_P\theta_0\), with \(P_n\psi_{\hat\theta_n}=0\) (or a root residual \(o_P(n^{-1/2})\));
2. \(\theta\mapsto P\psi_\theta\) is differentiable at \(\theta_0\) with nonsingular derivative \(V_{\theta_0}\);
3. \(\theta\mapsto\psi_\theta\) is continuous in \(L^2(P)\) at \(\theta_0\), and the class \(\{\psi_\theta:\theta\) near \(\theta_0\}\) is \(P\)-Donsker (or an explicit local stochastic-equicontinuity condition gives \(G_n(\psi_{\hat\theta_n}-\psi_{\theta_0})=o_P(1)\));
4. \(P\|\psi_{\theta_0}\|^2<\infty\).

Then
\[
\sqrt n(\hat\theta_n-\theta_0)=-V_{\theta_0}^{-1}G_n\psi_{\theta_0}+o_P(1),
\]
so the limit is normal with covariance \(V^{-1}\operatorname{Var}_P(\psi_{\theta_0})V^{-T}\). The proof uses consistency plus Donsker equicontinuity to replace the random index by \(\theta_0\), expands \(P\psi_{\hat\theta_n}\) at \(\theta_0\), obtains root-\(n\) consistency by absorbing the differentiability remainder, and applies the empirical-process CLT and matrix inversion. The scalar version in Bartlett, Lecture 16 p. 13, derives a Taylor identity and then applies the CLT, LLN and Slutsky; it states sufficient assumptions \(P\psi_{\theta_0}^2<\infty\), \(P\dot\psi_{\theta_0}\ne0\), and a bounded-in-probability second derivative along the segment. This is an independent, more elementary treatment.

For a smooth M-estimator, take \(\psi_\theta=\nabla m_\theta\) at an interior optimum; then M-estimator asymptotic normality is a Z-estimator result, provided the root equation and empirical-process conditions hold. For non-smooth criteria, do not differentiate the objective where it has no derivative. The sample median is a useful worked example: \(\psi_\theta(x)=\operatorname{sign}(x-\theta)\), with a unique median and a positive continuous density \(p(\theta_0)>0\), gives \(\sqrt n(\hat\theta_n-\theta_0)\Rightarrow N(0,[4p(\theta_0)^2]^{-1})\). Chen, lines 132–140, supplies the calculation; the population derivative is \(2p(\theta_0)\) and the score variance is 1.

**Gap/caveat:** The Donsker condition is sufficient and can be stronger than needed. Do not state a general argmax M-estimator normality theorem without the local quadratic criterion expansion and stochastic equicontinuity/tightness that justify it. The UW note’s Theorem 9 (van der Vaart 5.23) states a general M-estimator expansion under a square-integrable local Lipschitz envelope, a nonsingular population Hessian, and consistency, but it does not reproduce a complete proof. Its displayed sketch for the classical MLE also compresses the step establishing a root-\(n\) rate. Use van der Vaart Theorem 6.19 for the fully stated Z-estimator proof route; prove any M-to-Z specialization or state its sufficient assumptions explicitly.

### Likelihood, Fisher information, QMD, and LAN

For a dominated parametric family \(p_\theta\), the log-likelihood for IID observations is \(\ell_n(\theta)=\sum_i\log p_\theta(X_i)\). At a regular interior true value, the score is \(s_\theta=\nabla_\theta\log p_\theta\), the mean score is zero, and the Fisher information is \(I_\theta=E_\theta[s_\theta s_\theta^T]\). Under interchange/differentiation conditions the information identity gives \(E_\theta[\nabla^2\log p_\theta]=-I_\theta\). Bartlett, Lecture 22 pp. 6–8, gives the score and information identity with a direct density-normalization derivation; Duchi, Lecture 3 pp. 5–6, independently derives the same identity. These are correct-model identities and should not be used under misspecification.

Quadratic mean differentiability at an interior \(\theta\) means, for a common dominating measure \(\mu\),
\[
\int\left(\sqrt{p_{\theta+h}}-\sqrt{p_\theta}-\tfrac12h^Ts_\theta\sqrt{p_\theta}\right)^2d\mu=o(\|h\|^2).
\]
QMD yields \(E_\theta s_\theta=0\), finite \(I_\theta=E_\theta s_\theta s_\theta^T\), and the local likelihood expansion, for \(h_n\to h\),
\[
\log\frac{dP_{\theta+h_n/\sqrt n}^{\,n}}{dP_\theta^{\,n}}=h^T\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^ns_\theta(X_i)-\tfrac12h^TI_\theta h+o_{P_\theta}(1).
\]
By the multivariate iid CLT, the central score tends \(N(0,I_\theta)\). This is LAN with local information \(I_\theta\). Bartlett, Lecture 22 Theorem p. 11 and Lectures 23 pp. 2–4 state the result; Stanford Lecture 19 §3 and Duchi’s 2021 notes §2, pp. 9–11, independently derive it. Duchi’s note uses \(W_{n,i}=2(\sqrt{p_n/p}-1)\), expands \(\log(1+W_{n,i}/2)\), controls the quadratic term by QMD and the remainder by \(\max_i|W_{n,i}|=o_P(1)\), and obtains the LAN expansion. Its local-uniform convention is for bounded local \(h\); a pointwise fixed-\(h\) theorem should not be silently promoted to uniform LAN.

A regular MLE theorem needs more than formal differentiation. One supported sufficient package is: \(\theta_0\) interior of an open parameter set; QMD at \(\theta_0\); nonsingular \(I_{\theta_0}\); a square-integrable local Lipschitz envelope for \(\theta\mapsto\log p_\theta\); and a consistent measurable MLE. Then
\[
\sqrt n(\hat\theta_n-\theta_0)=I_{\theta_0}^{-1}\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^ns_{\theta_0}(X_i)+o_P(1)\Rightarrow N(0,I_{\theta_0}^{-1}).
\]
Bartlett, Lecture 23 p. 15, states this as van der Vaart 5.39; Chen, lines 181–193, gives the same assumptions and an argument reducing QMD to the population likelihood curvature condition in the general M-estimator expansion. Duchi, Lecture 3 pp. 5–6, supplies the Taylor/CLT proof in a classical smooth case. The joint proof route is: QMD implies \(E s=0\) and the information identity; consistency localizes the MLE; Taylor-expand the score (or use LAN and local argmax conditions), apply the score CLT and Hessian LLN, invert the nonsingular information. If relying on the UW note’s displayed Taylor sketch, make the absorption that establishes root-\(n\) tightness explicit.

**Wilks theorem hypotheses:** for \(\Theta_0\subset\Theta\subset\mathbb R^d\), assume QMD with nonsingular information at \(\theta_0\in\Theta_0\), consistent constrained and unconstrained MLEs, a local square-integrable Lipschitz bound for log-likelihoods, and convergence of the local parameter sets \(H_n=\sqrt n(\Theta-\theta_0)\), \(H_{n,0}=\sqrt n(\Theta_0-\theta_0)\) to \(H,H_0\). If \(\theta_0\) is interior to \(\Theta\), \(H=\mathbb R^d\); if \(H_0\) is a linear subspace of dimension \(l\), then \(2\log LR_n\Rightarrow\chi^2_{d-l}\). Bartlett, Lecture 26 pp. 11–17, gives this local parameter-space theorem and proves it by replacing the likelihood-ratio process with the limiting Gaussian shift and identifying the difference of maximized quadratic forms as a squared distance. Stanford Lecture 6 pp. 1–2 gives an independent point-null Taylor proof but abbreviates regularity as “typical conditions”; prefer Bartlett’s more explicit hypotheses for the scaffold.

### Contiguity and Le Cam’s lemmas

For sequences of probability measures \(P_n,Q_n\), \(Q_n\triangleleft P_n\) means \(P_n(A_n)\to0\Rightarrow Q_n(A_n)\to0\) for every measurable sequence \(A_n\); mutual contiguity requires both directions. Suppose \(Q_n\ll P_n\), let \(L_n=dQ_n/dP_n\), and assume under \(P_n\),
\[
(T_n,\log(dQ_n/dP_n))\Rightarrow N\left(\begin{pmatrix}\mu\\-\sigma^2/2\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\Sigma&\tau\\\tau^T&\sigma^2\end{pmatrix}\right),
\]
then \(Q_n\triangleleft P_n\) and under \(Q_n\), \(T_n\Rightarrow N(\mu+\tau,\Sigma)\). The third-lemma proof is exact: the limiting law is tilted by \(e^Z\); its characteristic function is the joint Gaussian characteristic function evaluated at \((t,-i)\), yielding mean shift \(\tau\) without changing \(\Sigma\). If the reverse local likelihood ratios satisfy the corresponding mean-one lognormal limit, then the reverse contiguity follows by the same first-lemma argument. Bartlett, Lecture 21 Theorem/proof pp. 20–24; van der Vaart, Lecture 4 Theorem 4.4 and Example 4.5, pp. 40–41; and Duchi, 2021 §1.3, pp. 5–6, are independent treatments. Stanford Lecture 18 pp. 3–5 also states the results and gives the Gaussian special case.

**Le Cam’s first lemma:** the full equivalence among contiguity, positivity of inverse-likelihood-ratio subsequential limits, mean-one forward likelihood-ratio limits, and preservation of convergence in probability is stated in van der Vaart Lemma 4.2 p. 39, Berkeley Lecture 21 p. 13, and Duchi 2021 Lemma 1.1 pp. 3–4. The source notes omit a full proof of all equivalences (Duchi proves the defining convergence-in-probability characterization). A direct proof of the needed local-alternative consequence is available in the common-support case: if \(L_n=dQ_n/dP_n\ge0\), \(E_{P_n}L_n=1\), \(L_n\Rightarrow L\), and \(EL=1\), nonnegative convergence with matching means gives uniform integrability; hence for \(P_n(A_n)\to0\), \(Q_n(A_n)=E_{P_n}[L_n1_{A_n}]\to0\). Under LAN the likelihood-ratio limit is lognormal with mean 1. Le Cam III gives the reverse log-ratio limit under \(Q_n\), and the same argument applied to \(1/L_n\) gives reverse contiguity. If support may change, this common-support proof does not apply without separately controlling the singular part; use the full first-lemma theorem with its hypotheses rather than silently assuming absolute continuity. Do not claim the full four-way characterization is proved by the read notes.

### Asymptotic efficiency

LAN with nonsingular information \(K\) makes the local experiments \(P_{\theta_0+h/\sqrt n,n}\) asymptotically behave as observing a Gaussian shift \(Z\sim N(h,K^{-1})\). Le Cam III gives the shift under local alternatives. Duchi, 2021 §2.2 p. 12, shows \(K^{-1}\Delta_n\Rightarrow N(h,K^{-1})\) under those alternatives; Stanford Lecture 20 p. 1 gives the same local experiment.

The Bayesian local asymptotic minimax theorem requires a LAN experiment at \(\theta_0\) with nonsingular precision \(K\), and a bounded, symmetric, quasi-convex loss \(L\). It states
\[
\liminf_{c\to\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{\hat\theta_n}\sup_{\|\theta-\theta_0\|\le c/\sqrt n}E_{\theta,n}L(\sqrt n(\hat\theta_n-\theta))\ge E L(N(0,K^{-1})).
\]
Duchi, 2021 §4.1 pp. 17–18, proves this by averaging over a truncated Gaussian prior on the local parameter, replacing the posterior with its Gaussian LAN limit in total variation, and applying Anderson’s lemma. Duchi, Stanford Lecture 20 Theorem 3.1 and proof, pp. 2–4, gives an independent proof with the same bounded symmetric quasi-convex loss and LAN hypotheses. van der Vaart, Lecture 4 Theorems 4.12 and 4.14, pp. 44–49, independently proves convolution and local minimax results in a more general asymptotically differentiable experiment framework. The convolution theorem’s message is that a regular limit contains the Gaussian information-bound component plus independent extra noise; the minimax result is the loss-based form.

An estimator is efficient only relative to an explicitly stated class (in particular, regular/local estimators) and loss/variance criterion. The local minimax theorem is the safe foundation statement. A pointwise asymptotic variance claim alone does not establish local uniform optimality. In a correctly specified regular parametric family, the MLE expansion attains \(I^{-1}\); under misspecification the same inverse-information formula is generally wrong. For tests, Bartlett Lectures 24–25 pp. 10–22 give local limiting power and Pitman efficiency under \(n^{-1/2}\) alternatives; this should be a downstream pair after the exact testing conventions of foundations-02.

### Boundary, support, and misspecification failures

**Changing support / nonregular rate.** For \(X_i\stackrel{iid}{\sim}\operatorname{Unif}[0,\theta]\), \(\theta>0\), the likelihood MLE is \(X_{(n)}=\max_iX_i\). For fixed \(t\ge0\) and \(n>t/\theta\),
\[
P\{n(\theta-X_{(n)})>t\}=\left(1-\frac{t}{n\theta}\right)^n\longrightarrow e^{-t/\theta},
\]
so the rate is \(n\) with an exponential limit, not \(\sqrt n\) with a normal limit. The new support interval \((\theta,\theta+h]\) has mass \(h/(\theta+h)=O(h)\), which violates the \(o(h^2)\) QMD remainder. Bartlett, Lecture 15 pp. 3–4, identifies the MLE; Lecture 22 p. 14 proves non-QMD from the gained-support integral; Lecture 25 p. 17 gives the Hellinger rate. Chen, lines 165–170, independently computes the \(O(h)\) obstruction. The displayed order-statistic limit is an elementary derivation from the exact CDF.

**Boundary likelihood-ratio law.** Under \(X_i\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)\) with known \(\sigma\), test \(H_0:\mu\le0\) at the boundary \(\mu=0\) against \(\mu>0\). The unconstrained MLE is \(\bar X\), the constrained null MLE is \(\min(0,\bar X)\), and the LR statistic is \(n(\bar X_+)^2/\sigma^2=(Z\vee0)^2\), \(Z\sim N(0,1)\), hence it has mass \(1/2\) at 0 and mass \(1/2\) on \(\chi_1^2\). Bartlett, Lecture 26 p. 20, derives this local cone limit. Ordinary Wilks \(\chi^2\) requires an interior regular point and a linear local null space; the example shows why that hypothesis matters.

**Misspecified likelihood.** If data have law \(P\) outside \(\{p_\theta\}\), define the population target \(\theta^*=\arg\max_\theta E_P\log p_\theta(X)\), assuming it exists uniquely and is interior. A uniform law plus separation gives \(\hat\theta_n\to_P\theta^*\). Under a nonsingular population Hessian \(H=E_P\nabla^2\log p_{\theta^*}\), square-integrable score \(S=\nabla\log p_{\theta^*}\), and differentiability/integrability that justify the score Taylor expansion,
\[
\sqrt n(\hat\theta_n-\theta^*)=-H^{-1}\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^nS(X_i)+o_P(1),\qquad \operatorname{avar}=H^{-1}JH^{-T},\quad J=E_P[SS^T].
\]
Yen-Chi Chen, UW STAT 581 lines 28–62, states the pseudo-true target, a sufficient compactness/uniqueness/smoothness package, and the sandwich limit with a score-Taylor, CLT and Hessian-LLN proof sketch; lines 214–222 state that the inverse-information equality belongs to correct specification. The general Z theorem in van der Vaart, Lecture 6 §6.5, pp. 73–74, supplies an independent estimating-equation proof route under a \(P\)-Donsker condition. A concrete exact example is fitting the location family \(N(\theta,1)\) to IID data from \(N(\mu,\tau^2)\), with \(\tau^2\ne1\): \(\theta^*=\mu\), the MLE is \(\bar X\), and its asymptotic variance is \(\tau^2\), while the model-based Fisher inverse is 1. This follows from the sample-mean CLT.

The UW STAT 581 note also contains a high-dimensional missing-data failure at lines 229–245. It was read but is excluded from the proposed foundation-03 inventory because it would introduce a model-specific dependency from the later models category. The regularity failures retained here are the boundary/support and misspecification examples assigned to this reader.

## Smallest missing prerequisites and integration constraints

1. Add \(O_P/o_P\) notation and short vector/matrix stochastic-order calculus before proofs that use Taylor remainders, random Hessians, or root-\(n\) tightness. Existing real convergence definitions do not define these stochastic-order symbols.
2. Add vector Slutsky/joint convergence, or prove the constant-limit product special case inside delta and estimating-equation theorems. The current library’s Slutsky theorem is real-only, while score, Hessian and parameter vectors are multivariate.
3. Add or import from foundations-04 a ULLN/Glivenko–Cantelli theorem as an earlier supplier. M/Z consistency may be stated conditionally on ULLN; any entropy/GC proof must be a separate, fully hypothesized result, not an implicit fallback.
4. Foundations-01 must supply reusable statistical setup (experiment/model, parameter, estimand, estimator, likelihood and sampling law) before these definitions. Foundations-02 owns testing and finite-sample inference conventions used by local-power/Wilks examples. These are same-category-1 prerequisites; they must not import models/experiments or later Bayesian/computational/ML suppliers.
5. Efficient-estimator statements require a definition of regularity under contiguous \(n^{-1/2}\)-local alternatives and an explicit loss/variance criterion. The lower bound is for local risk in a LAN experiment, not a universal finite-sample inequality.
6. Correct-model MLE results and misspecified M-estimator results must be separate items. The identity \(E_\theta[-\nabla^2\ell_\theta]=E_\theta[s_\theta s_\theta^T]\) requires correct specification plus differentiation conditions; the sandwich theorem uses the true sampling law in H and J.
7. Boundary/support failures must sit adjacent to regular LAN/Wilks statements and say which regular hypothesis fails (common local \(L^2\) differentiability/interior tangent space). No boundary or misspecification example is an unproved substitute for a failed theorem.
