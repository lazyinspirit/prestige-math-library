# Foundations 04 source dossier: concentration, empirical processes, and minimax risk

**Status:** source-based scaffold proposal only. This dossier does not author library items or imply that any proposed item has passed an authoring or validation gate.

The smallest coherent sequence is seven A/B pairs. It starts with concentration and symmetrization, then adds entropy/chaining, uniform laws, and functional limits. It closes with minimax lower bounds and finite-sample risk upper bounds. The statistical experiment, decision rule, loss, risk, and minimax-risk interface is the foundations-01 proposal (`def-statistical-experiment-and-identifiability`, `def-estimator-and-randomized-decision-rule`, `def-loss-risk-and-decision-criteria`). Pair 1 and the empirical-process core can be used without that statistical interface. No pair depends on models/experiments, Bayesian/computational statistics, or machine-learning/adaptive-decision pages. Pair 4 uses ordinary empirical loss minimization as a general theorem about risks; pair 7 uses a fixed-design Gaussian/sub-Gaussian experiment stated inside the example, not a later model family.

## Proposed pair sequence

### Pair 1 — concentration and finite-dimensional reduction

**A — `def-subgaussian-subexponential-and-tail-proxies`.** Define centered sub-Gaussian and sub-exponential variables using either their MGF bounds or equivalent Orlicz norms, with the chosen norm convention and universal conversion constants stated. Record that an MGF proxy is an upper bound, not necessarily the variance. Give the corresponding tail and moment consequences. Include the vector convention used later: ε is sub-Gaussian with proxy σ² when every linear functional ⟨u,ε⟩ has MGF at most \(\mathbb E e^{\lambda\langle u,\varepsilon\rangle}\le e^{\sigma^2\lambda^2\|u\|_2^2/2}\). Distinguish independent summands from a sub-Gaussian vector; the latter need not have independent coordinates.

**B — `thm-hoeffding-bernstein-and-finite-max-concentration`.** State scalar independent-sum concentration, finite maxima, and the ε-net reduction as separate clauses with their hypotheses. A safe Hoeffding form is
\[
\Pr\{|\sum_i(X_i-\mathbb EX_i)|\ge t\}\le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{2t^2}{\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2}\right)
\]
for independent \(X_i\in[a_i,b_i]\). State Bernstein either for bounded centered variables with total variance \(v\) and bound \(b\), or for sub-exponential norms, keeping the chosen version's constants and assumptions together. For \(N\) possibly dependent sub-Gaussian variables, the union-bound tail for the maximum and its expectation bound have order \(\sigma\sqrt{\log(2N)}\); independence is not needed for this maximum bound. An ε-net of the Euclidean unit sphere has size at most \((1+2/\epsilon)^d\) (or the source's looser \((3/\epsilon)^d\) for \(0<\epsilon\le1\)); use it to bound a vector norm from linear functionals and make matrix/operator-norm reductions only when a separate matrix theorem is supplied.

**Proof route.** Chernoff's inequality plus an MGF bound gives the tail; optimize the exponential parameter. Hoeffding's lemma gives the bounded-summand MGF; the Bernstein MGF expansion separates the quadratic and linear regimes. For the finite maximum, union bound the individual tails and integrate. For an ε-net, approximate a maximizing unit vector and absorb the approximation error by (1/(1-\epsilon)). The route makes clear which conclusions use independence (sum) and which do not (finite maximum/net).

**B examples and boundary checks.** Rademacher sums and bounded sample means; maximum of \(N\) Gaussian coordinates; a sub-Gaussian vector norm via a net. Include a heavy-tailed finite-variance counterexample to explain why the same exponential bound does not follow from variance alone. A matrix Bernstein result may be an advanced sub-item only if its self-adjointness, independence, dimension factor, and operator-norm variance proxy are all explicit.

**Evidence.** Rigollet–Hütter, Chapter 1, PDF pp. 14–46: Definition 1.2 and Lemma 1.3 (sub-Gaussian MGF and Chernoff tail); Lemma 1.8 and Theorem 1.9 (Hoeffding lemma/inequality); Lemmas 1.10 and 1.12 and Theorem 1.13 (tail/moment conversion, squared sub-Gaussian is sub-exponential, Bernstein); Theorem 1.14 (maximum of finitely many sub-Gaussian variables); Lemma 1.18 and Theorem 1.19 (ε-net and vector norm); Theorem 1.27 (matrix Bernstein, optional). Pollard, *Probability tools*, Chapter 10, §10.5–10.6, provides the Orlicz/maximal-inequality and entropy-chain context. Wellner, Chapter 1 §3, provides an independent treatment of maximal inequalities and Bennett/Bernstein bounds.

### Pair 2 — empirical and Rademacher processes; symmetrization

**A — `def-empirical-process-and-rademacher-process`.** Define \(P_nf=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n f(Z_i)\), the centered process \(\mathbb G_n(f)=\sqrt n(P_n-P)f\), and, more generally, a centered sum of independent indexed processes \(\sum_i(X_i(t)-\mathbb EX_i(t))\). Define the Rademacher process \(R_n(t)=\sum_i\epsilon_iX_i(t)\) on an auxiliary product space, with independent uniform signs. State the index-class measurability contract at the outset: use a countable/densely separable measurable class, or state outer expectation/probability and outer weak convergence explicitly. Pointwise SLLN does not define or bound an uncountable supremum.

**B — `thm-independent-process-symmetrization-and-contraction`.** Give the independent-copy symmetrization inequality for a convex increasing Φ and the countable/separable index set: the Φ-moment of the centered supremum is bounded by the corresponding expectation over signs of the symmetrized supremum, with the factor 2 placed inside Φ under the selected convention. Derive the expected-supremum form as a corollary. State a separate tail form with its variance/slack condition rather than claiming that expected symmetrization alone implies a tail inequality. Add the Rademacher contraction result only under its actual zero-at-zero, Lipschitz, and symmetry/absolute-value conventions; do not silently omit its factor of 2 or the requirement that a zero function (or an equivalent centering) is available.

**Proof route.** On a product space, add an independent copy (X'_i). Jensen moves the copy expectation outside the convex functional; each difference (X_i-X'_i) is symmetric, so insert independent signs; the triangle inequality and convexity split the two copies and produce the 2 factor. The tail version first compares the original centered process to an independent-copy difference, using a one-index witness and an event on which the copy is small. Contraction follows by replacing one coordinate at a time and using the Lipschitz bound in the two sign states.

**B examples and traps.** Derive the Rademacher bound for a sample mean and use the empirical CDF as a class-indexed illustration. Contrast with the local library's scalar independent-series symmetrization: it does not prove a supremum-over-functions inequality. Include an uncountable-class measurability warning and explain the outer-probability/separable-version remedy.

**Evidence.** Pollard 1990, Theorem 2.2, gives convex-process symmetrization for independent process sums; §5, especially Theorem 5.7, treats contraction. Wellner 2005, Chapter 1 §5, gives independent-copy moment and probability symmetrization. Pollard 2026, Chapter 13 §§13.2–13.6, derives product-space and tail versions, Rademacher symmetrization, and oscillation control. The three expositions agree on the independent-copy/sign route; their measurability conventions differ and must not be collapsed.

### Pair 3 — covering, bracketing, VC entropy, and chaining

**A — `def-covering-packing-bracketing-and-metric-entropy`.** Define covering and packing numbers for a pseudometric/semimetric class, metric entropy \(\log N(\epsilon,\mathcal F,d)\), \(L^r(Q)\) class metrics, and \(L^r(P)\) brackets. State the packing/covering comparison with the radius convention made explicit. Keep sample-space (L^\infty(P)) equivalence classes separate from the process-space (\ell^\infty(T)) of bounded functions indexed by (T).

**B — `thm-dudley-chaining-and-vc-class-entropy`.** First state the classical entropy-chain upper bound. If the separable process is sub-Gaussian in increments for (d(s,t)=\|X_s-X_t\|_{\psi_2}), (T) is totally bounded, and the entropy integral is finite, then
\[
\mathbb E\sup_{t\in T}|X_t-X_{t_0}|\ \lesssim\ \int_0^{\operatorname{diam}(T)}\sqrt{\log N(\epsilon,T,d)}\,d\epsilon,
\]
with the constant depending only on the norm convention. State a corresponding probability/Orlicz form only with the source's explicit increment and measurability assumptions. Next state the polynomial uniform covering bound for VC sets and VC-subgraph function classes in (L^r(Q)), with the VC index, (Q), envelope, and (r\) range specified. This entropy estimate is the reusable bridge to pairs 4 and 5.

**Proof route.** Choose successively finer finite nets/partitions, telescope (X_t-X_{t_0}) across scales, and bound each scale's maximum of sub-Gaussian increments by its diameter times the square root of the logarithm of the net size. Summing dyadic scales yields the entropy integral. For VC classes, use Sauer–Shelah growth bounds on finite traces, then sample a finite cover from an arbitrary (Q); the sharpened Haussler argument removes the extra logarithmic factor for set classes. Function-class bounds require the VC-subgraph reduction and envelope scaling.

**B examples and limits.** Brownian motion illustrates a finite entropy integral after dyadic chaining. Half-spaces or threshold indicators illustrate VC dimension and polynomial covering growth. Smooth/Hölder classes illustrate that entropy exponents can make the integral finite or divergent. State explicitly that a finite-dimensional covering bound is not itself a Donsker theorem.

**Advanced extension, not a prerequisite.** The Pollard 2026 chapters also develop weighted partitions, majorizing measures, admissible partitions, and the Gaussian generic-chaining lower/upper comparison. They can motivate an optional `thm-majorizing-measure-and-generic-chaining-for-gaussian-processes`, but the exact admissibility and anchored-supremum conventions need a second full proof source before authors rely on it. Pollard flags §12.4 as a proof he would be surprised to be error-free. The classical Dudley/entropy supplier above has independent complete proof treatments and suffices for this scaffold's core dependency graph.

**Evidence.** Pollard 1990 §§3–4, Theorems 3.5 and 4.7–4.10; Wellner 2005 §§3–4 and §8, Theorems 3.1, 8.1–8.2; Pollard 2026 Chapters 10–12 and 14, especially §§10.3–10.7, 11.7–11.10, 12.1–12.4, 14.2–14.5. The VC growth bound also has the existing local Sauer–Shelah supplier, but that body does not imply the metric-entropy result.

### Pair 4 — uniform laws and risk control from uniform convergence

**A — `thm-glivenko-cantelli-by-bracketing-or-vc-entropy`.** Define a \(P\)-Glivenko–Cantelli class by \(\sup_{f\in\mathcal F}|P_nf-Pf|\to0\) in probability (or almost surely when the theorem says so). Give one primary sufficient criterion with its integrable envelope and measurability assumptions, preferably finite \(L^1(P)\) bracketing entropy at every positive scale; give the VC-subgraph/VC-set criterion as a corollary under an integrable envelope. If a random-entropy criterion is used, retain Pollard's independent-row formulation and state its outer-probability assumptions rather than replacing it with a deterministic entropy integral. A pointwise SLLN is not a ULLN.

**B — `thm-empirical-risk-minimizer-oracle-bound-under-uniform-deviation`.** For an integrable loss class \(\mathcal L\), let \(\mathcal R(f)=P\ell_f\), \(\mathcal R_n(f)=P_n\ell_f\), and assume \(\hat f\) is an \(\eta\)-approximate empirical minimizer. On the event \(\sup_{f\in\mathcal L}|P_n\ell_f-P\ell_f|\le\epsilon\), prove
\[
\mathcal R(\hat f)\le \inf_{f\in\mathcal L}\mathcal R(f)+2\epsilon+\eta.
\]
This is a deterministic comparison conditional on the deviation event; the theorem supplying that event is Pair 4A. The statistical vocabulary consumes foundations-01's risk interface, not a machine-learning supplier.

**Proof route.** For 4A, finite brackets reduce a supremum to finitely many endpoint averages plus an \(L^1(P)\) bracket width; apply the scalar strong/weak law and shrink the width. For VC classes, polynomial trace growth plus symmetrization and finite-class concentration gives uniform convergence. For 4B, add and subtract \(P_n\ell_{\hat f}\) and \(P_n\ell_f\), apply the uniform deviation twice and the η-minimization inequality.

**B examples and counterexample.** Threshold sets give a classical GC example. The class of all finite subsets of \([0,1]\) under continuous uniform \(P\) is not GC: for every realized finite sample (A_n=\{Z_1,\ldots,Z_n\}\), (P_n(A_n)=1) but (P(A_n)=0), so the supremum deviation is 1. This demonstrates why pointwise SLLN is insufficient.

**Evidence.** Pollard 1990, §8 Theorems 8.2–8.3, gives weak and strong uniform laws under random entropy/manageability assumptions. Wellner 2005, §6 Theorems 6.1–6.4, gives bracketing and VC criteria. Pollard 2026, Chapter 13 Example 8, shows the symmetrization/trace-growth route in the original VC argument. The ERM comparison is elementary once the loss/risk definition from foundations-01 is available.

### Pair 5 — empirical-process functional CLT and Donsker classes

**A — `def-donsker-class-and-asymptotic-equicontinuity`.** Define a \(P\)-Donsker class as one for which \(\mathbb G_n=\sqrt n(P_n-P)\) converges weakly in \(\ell^\infty(\mathcal F)\) to a tight centered Gaussian process with covariance
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbb G_P f,\mathbb G_P g)=P[(f-Pf)(g-Pg)].
\]
Define finite-dimensional convergence and asymptotic equicontinuity in a totally bounded semimetric, and state whether convergence is Borel or outer weak convergence. Distinguish the finite-vector iid CLT from the function-indexed CLT.

**B — `thm-functional-empirical-process-clt-by-entropy-and-equicontinuity`.** Give (i) the process-space convergence criterion: finite-dimensional convergence to a tight limit plus asymptotic equicontinuity is equivalent to weak convergence in \(\ell^\infty(T)\), under the stated total-boundedness/separability conditions; and (ii) the iid Donsker sufficient criterion: \(P\)-measurability, envelope \(F\) with \(PF^2<\infty\), and the uniform \(L^2(Q)\) entropy integral
\[
\int_0^1\sup_Q\sqrt{\log N(\epsilon\|F\|_{Q,2},\mathcal F,L^2(Q))}\,d\epsilon<\infty,
\]
plus the source's measurability/squared-class conditions. The entropy integral is sufficient, not necessary in every setting. State the bracketing-integral alternative separately if included. For triangular arrays, require covariance convergence, finite-dimensional Lindeberg, and a process-level asymptotic-equicontinuity condition; do not infer a functional CLT from finite-dimensional convergence alone.

**Proof route.** Establish finite-dimensional convergence by the ordinary multivariate/triangular-array CLT. Symmetrization reduces local increments to a Rademacher process; the entropy chain gives asymptotic equicontinuity. The empirical envelope/square class law controls the random local metric. Invoke the process convergence criterion only after tightness and the limit's uniformly continuous paths are established.

**B examples and traps.** For threshold indicators on the real line, the empirical CDF process converges to a Brownian bridge under the standard separability convention. Give a finite-dimensional Gaussian limit with no tightness as the counterexample pattern. Note that local empirical-measure weak convergence in (\mathbb R^d) is not a UCLT.

**Evidence.** Pollard 1990, §§9–10: Definition 9.1 and Representation Theorem 9.4, then Theorems 10.2 and 10.6. Wellner 2005, §§2 and 7, Theorem 2.1 and Theorems 7.1, 7.4, 7.7. Their finite-dimensional-plus-equicontinuity criteria independently support the structure; Pollard's outer-convergence treatment and Wellner's entropy-integral theorem supply complementary measurability/entropy details.

### Pair 6 — minimax lower bounds by testing, packing, and hypercubes

**A — `thm-minimax-lower-bounds-by-le-cam-fano-and-assouad`.** Use only foundations-01's experiment, estimator/decision-rule, loss, and minimax-risk definitions. Define binary testing as a two-action decision rule within this item, and define total variation and Kullback–Leibler divergence with conventions. State three reusable reductions:

1. **Two-point/Le Cam.** If (d(\theta_0,\theta_1)=\Delta), nearest-neighbor testing gives (\inf_{\hat\theta}\max_{j=0,1}\Pr_{\theta_j}\{d(\hat\theta,\theta_j)\ge\Delta/2\}\ge(1-\operatorname{TV}(P_0,P_1))/2). For squared metric loss this yields maximum expected risk at least (\Delta^2(1-\operatorname{TV})/8).
2. **Fano.** For \(M\ge2\) hypotheses separated by \(\Delta\), the nearest-hypothesis test and the KL information bound give error probability at least (1-(I(J;X)+\log2)/\log M) for a uniform hypothesis index \(J\); provide an average-KL-to-reference corollary with its absolute-continuity hypotheses. The loss lower bound follows by multiplying the error probability by the squared separation threshold.
3. **Assouad.** For a hypercube \(\{\theta_\omega:\omega\in\{0,1\}^d\}\), Hamming loss is bounded below by a constant times (d(1-\max_i\operatorname{TV}(P_{\omega},P_{\omega^{(i)}}))) after averaging over neighboring pairs. Translate to squared parameter loss only after the coordinate separation and loss geometry are stated.

Do not conflate the KL/Fano and total-variation/Le Cam routes. If Pinsker is used, state its precise convention and direction. Tensor-product KL additivity is a separate lemma with product-law assumptions.

**B examples.** In the Gaussian sequence experiment \(Y=\theta+\xi\), \(\xi\sim N(0,\sigma^2 I_d/n)\), a hypercube/Assouad construction gives the dense squared-ℓ2 rate (\sigma^2d/n). A Varshamov–Gilbert sparse packing with Fano gives (\sigma^2 k\log(ed/k)/n) over (k)-sparse vectors (state the range such as (1\le k\le d/2), and choose amplitude so pairwise KL is a small fraction of log packing size). These are experiment-specific lower bounds, not consequences of a combinatorial count alone.

**Proof route.** Map any estimator to the closest hypothesis. A wrong decision forces parameter error at least half the packing separation. Bound testing error by Le Cam/TV, Fano/KL or coordinatewise Assouad. For Gaussian sequence laws compute KL exactly and use product additivity; Varshamov–Gilbert supplies a large separated code. Check that the separation and divergence constraints are simultaneously satisfiable before claiming a rate.

**Evidence.** Rigollet–Hütter, Chapter 4, PDF pp. 99–127: Definitions 4.1–4.2; §4.2 nearest-packing reduction; Lemma 4.3/Proposition 4.4 (Neyman–Pearson/TV); Proposition 4.6 (KL and product additivity); Lemma 4.8 (Pinsker); Theorems 4.9–4.11; Lemma 4.12 and Corollaries 4.13–4.16. Duchi, Chapter 8 §§8.1–8.5 and §8.7, PDF pp. 179–199 and 207–210: risk definitions, packing reduction, Le Cam, Fano (including distance-based forms), Assouad, and global Fano. Duchi provides an independent general treatment of the lower-bound methods.

### Pair 7 — finite-sample risk upper bounds and high-dimensional rates

**A — `thm-projection-and-sparse-least-squares-risk-upper-bounds`.** For fixed design \(Y=X\theta^*+\varepsilon\), assume the zero-mean noise vector is sub-Gaussian with proxy \(\sigma^2\) in the linear-functional sense and \(r=\operatorname{rank}(X)\). For fitted-mean loss \(n^{-1}\|X\hat\theta-X\theta^*\|_2^2\), least squares has expected risk at most \(C\sigma^2r/n\) and, with probability at least \(1-\delta\), risk at most \(C\sigma^2(r+\log(1/\delta))/n\). State fitted-mean risk rather than coefficient risk unless a lower singular-value/design condition makes coefficients identifiable. If \(\theta^*\) is \(k\)-sparse, constrained \(\ell_0\) least squares has expected and high-probability risk of order \(\sigma^2 k\log(ed/k)/n\) (include the theorem's \(k\le d/2\) and confidence terms). This latter guarantee needs no design incoherence, but the estimator searches supports and is not an efficient algorithmic guarantee.

**B — `ex-high-dimensional-sparse-regression-upper-and-matching-lower-rates`.** Compare three explicit assumptions rather than asserting a universal sparse rate: (a) the constrained ℓ\(_0\) estimator above; (b) BIC/penalized subset selection, which achieves a sparse oracle-type fitted-risk bound without a design condition but is combinatorial; (c) Lasso, whose fast (k\sigma^2\log(2d/\delta)/n) bound in the read notes requires fixed-design column normalization, (k)-sparse truth and the stated incoherence condition (or a verified restricted-eigenvalue substitute). Also give the weaker ℓ\(_1\)-radius/slow-rate Lasso statement when no fast-rate design condition is available. Explain that the constrained-ℓ\(_0\) upper bound matches the sparse Gaussian-sequence minimax lower rate under an orthogonal design; the Lasso rate has a different logarithmic term and stronger design hypotheses. Do not imply that statistical risk bounds prove computational tractability or model validity.

**Proof route.** Least squares: compare fitted residual sums, reduce the random inner product to the norm of the projection of ε onto the column space, then apply the sub-Gaussian vector bound/net theorem. Sparse least squares: union bound those projection bounds over \(\binom d{2k}\) possible difference supports and integrate the tail for expected risk. Lasso: basic inequality gives the cone condition; incoherence/restricted eigenvalue controls the prediction norm on that cone. The BIC proof uses a complexity penalty to pay for the support count. Show which step fails without the relevant design or penalty assumption.

**B examples and caveats.** Orthogonal Gaussian sequence is the clean matching-rate case. Include a collinear design where coefficient error is unidentifiable even though fitted-value risk remains controlled. Distinguish statistical optimality from the computational cost of subset search. Keep signal sparsity, column norms, noise proxy, confidence parameter, and design compatibility adjacent to each rate.

**Evidence.** Rigollet–Hütter, Chapter 2, PDF pp. 47–78, Theorems 2.2, 2.4, 2.6/Corollaries 2.8–2.9, 2.14–2.18; Chapter 3, pp. 79–98, Theorems 3.3–3.7 for oracle comparisons; Chapter 4, pp. 99–127, lower bounds above. The notes themselves warn that constrained subset estimators can require exponentially many regressions. Chapter 1's concentration and finite-max lemmas are prerequisites within the same source.
