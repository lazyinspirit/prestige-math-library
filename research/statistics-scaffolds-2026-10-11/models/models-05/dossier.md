## Scope and category boundary

The scaffold covers second-order and strict stationarity, dependent sample means, autocovariance and spectral measures/estimators, linear and ARMA processes, finite-history prediction, state-space/HMM definitions, random fields, covariance kernels and variograms, spatial estimators and kriging, and spatial and temporal point-process models and likelihoods. State-space/Kalman and particle filtering are excluded to category 3. Other estimation/model procedures are considered only when a separate definition and source-backed guarantee are in scope. A model's fit to a physical system is an empirical claim, not a mathematical consequence of the model definition.

The proposed category-2 A items depend only on inspected probability, measure, Hilbert-space, linear-algebra, and harmonic-analysis suppliers, plus category-1 interfaces explicitly labelled planned below. No category-3 Bayesian/computational-statistics or category-4 machine-learning supplier is needed. The transitive dependency closure of the selected existing mathematical suppliers was inspected; no category-3/4 dependency is used. Claims needing dependent-data CLTs, functional spectral limits, or point-process asymptotics are omitted where the source proofs or hypotheses are incomplete.

## Source evidence and proof ledger

### Time series and stationary/dependent data

- **Definitions and covariance algebra.** Meinshausen [ETH notes](https://www.stat.ethz.ch/~nicolai/timeseries/TimeSeries2022b.pdf), complete pp. 1-57, especially pp. 2-9 and 13-14, gives strict/weak stationarity, γ(h), sample ACVF, and the dependent sample-mean variance calculation. Wang [STAT 720 notes](https://people.stat.sc.edu/wang528/Stat%20720/STAT720%20Notes.pdf), complete focused Sections 2.1-5.7 (PDF pp. 14-89), independently treats stationarity and ACVF. Wang Theorems 2.2-2.3 (PDF pp. 16-17) give ACVF properties and the positive-definite covariance-sequence characterization. The proof route for γ(h)=Cov(X(t+h),X(t)) being even and Toeplitz-positive-semidefinite is covariance symmetry and Var(∑a_jX(t_j))≥0. Exact sample-mean variance is $n^{-2}\sum_{|h|<n}(n-|h|)\gamma(h)$; if \(\sum_h|\gamma(h)|<\infty\), it is at most $n^{-1}\sum_h|\gamma(h)|$, yielding L2 consistency without a mixing or CLT assumption. Under strict stationarity and ergodicity, the existing Birkhoff supplier gives a different, almost-sure/L1 conclusion for integrable observations.
- **Sample autocovariance.** Wang Lemma 2.3 (PDF page 47 in the focused source) proves finite-sample algebraic properties of the $1/n$-normalized sample ACVF. The $1/n$ version is a Gram/cross-correlation sequence and is positive semidefinite. The unbiased $1/(n-|h|)$ rescaling is not guaranteed positive semidefinite; it should not be presented as a valid covariance sequence without a check. Centering at the sample mean introduces bias. Wang states sample-ACF asymptotic normality on pp. 47-50 without a complete proof/hypothesis set; ETH similarly summarizes periodogram limits. Those limits and confidence intervals are not theorem dependencies.
- **Spectral representation.** Wang Theorem 4.2 (PDF p. 63) states Herglotz representation and Corollary 4.2 gives the absolutely summable Fourier-series density. The proof supplier is already in the library: apply Bochner's theorem to the discrete LCA group \(\mathbb Z\), whose dual is the circle. Thus every positive-definite covariance sequence has a finite positive spectral measure; a density exists only when that measure is absolutely continuous. If \(\sum_h|\gamma(h)|<\infty\), then $f(\lambda)=(2\pi)^{-1}\sum_h\gamma(h)e^{-ih\lambda}\ge0\), under the frequency convention \([-\pi,\pi]\). A random sinusoid/periodic component may give spectral atoms and no density.
- **Periodogram.** Define a Fourier periodogram with a fixed centering and normalization convention, e.g. $I_n(\lambda)=(2\pi n)^{-1}|\sum_{t=1}^n(X_t-\mu)e^{-it\lambda}|^2$ when the mean is known. Its expected value is a Fejér-kernel convolution of the spectral measure/density; this follows by expanding the finite squared Fourier sum and grouping terms by lag. A smoothed periodogram is a definition/algorithm. Its asymptotic bias can be shown by the approximate-identity argument under continuity of $f$, nonnegative normalized kernels, and bandwidth conditions. Spodarev's corresponding estimator unit (PDF pp. 16-25, Lemma 2.10) proves a smoothing-bias result for regular-grid random fields. Neither source read here proves the raw periodogram's asymptotic exponential law, a spectral CLT, or standard-error coverage with a sufficient general set of assumptions. Do not assert these.

### Linear processes, ARMA, and prediction

- **Causal/invertible root criteria.** Wang Theorems 3.1-3.2 (PDF pp. 52-55) give proofs for the causal and invertible ARMA root conditions. With normalized, coprime polynomials $\phi$ and $\theta$ and white noise of finite nonzero variance, the causal representation with absolutely summable coefficients exists exactly when $\phi(z)$ has no zero in $|z|\le1$; its generating series is $\theta(z)/\phi(z)$. The analogous inverse representation is governed by $\theta(z)$ and $\phi(z)/\theta(z)$. The proof route is analytic power-series expansion beyond the unit disk for the sufficient direction, and coefficient comparison against uncorrelated innovations for necessity. Coprimality is needed for the necessity/identifiability wording: common factors must be cancelled before reading root conditions. Wang's Theorem 3.2 proof has a printed polynomial-symbol typo in its converse; use the corrected coefficient comparison, not the typo. Its Theorem 2.6 also misstates the general invertibility root condition; the corrected closed-unit-disk form above is independently proved in Theorem 3.2 and is the only form proposed.
- **Stationary ARMA spectrum.** Wang Theorem 4.4 (PDF pp. 65-66) proves that a stationary ARMA solution with φ nonzero on the unit circle, white-noise variance \(\sigma^2\), and coprime numerator/denominator has $f(\lambda)=\sigma^2(2\pi)^{-1}|\theta(e^{-i\lambda})|^2/|\phi(e^{-i\lambda})|^2$. It is proved by a two-sided absolutely summable representation and the linear-filter spectral identity. It does not require the representation to be causal or invertible. If a unit-circle zero occurs, this density formula is not supported as stated. Do not silently treat white noise as iid; iid/Gaussian noise requires that extra assumption.
- **Best linear prediction.** Wang Section 5 (PDF pp. 67-75) derives finite-history normal equations and prediction-error variance. The stronger exact existing item `thm-normal-equations-for-best-affine-l2-prediction` gives existence and uniqueness of the predictor as an almost-sure (L^2) class even if the covariance matrix is singular; coefficient vectors may then be nonunique. For a nonsingular history covariance matrix, solve $\Gamma a=\gamma_h$ and use intercept $\mu(1-\sum_i a_i)$; MSE is $\gamma(0)-a^T\gamma_h$. A Gaussian-process assumption upgrades the best affine predictor to $E[X_{n+h}\mid X_1,\dots,X_n]$; otherwise conditional expectation can be nonlinear. The proof is finite-dimensional projection plus the Gaussian regression property. Dewei's discussion has an incomplete uniqueness exercise, which the existing normal-equations item resolves.
- **Prediction recursion.** If an innovations/Durbin-Levinson recursion is included as an algorithm, define its covariance input, initialization and update equations separately from its guarantee. The guarantee should be the induction that its coefficients solve the finite Toeplitz normal equations and its error variance equals the corresponding projection residual; require positive-definite leading covariance blocks for unique scalar recursion coefficients. Wang's Section 5.3-5.7 supplies the derivation. A recursion is not a filtering algorithm and does not authorize a state-space filter. The scaffold may also omit this optional recursion and use the direct finite linear system.

### Spatial random fields, covariance, and variograms

- **Field/covariance definitions and construction.** Spodarev's [Spatial Statistics notes](https://www.uni-ulm.de/fileadmin/website_uni_ulm/mawi.inst.110/lehre/ss24/SpatialStatistics_2024.pdf), Section 1.1-1.2 (PDF pp. 3-8), and [Random Fields I draft](https://www.uni-ulm.de/fileadmin/website_uni_ulm/allgemein/zuloeschen/Random_Field.pdf), Sections 1.3-1.4 (PDF pp. 14-20), define random fields, strict/wide stationarity, isotropy, intrinsic stationarity and variograms. A field's covariance kernel is symmetric positive semidefinite because each finite quadratic form is the variance of a linear combination. Given a symmetric positive-semidefinite kernel and mean, finite-dimensional Gaussian laws are consistent; the existing multivariate-normal and Kolmogorov-extension suppliers yield a Gaussian field on the cylinder sigma-algebra. This construction gives no sample continuity or joint measurability without a further theorem.
- **Bochner and variogram proofs.** Spodarev's 2009 draft Theorem 2.1.1 (PDF p. 35) gives Bochner-Khinchin for continuous positive-definite functions on \(\mathbb R^d\); continuity/mean-square continuity must be stated. The library's stronger general LCA Bochner theorem can supply this clause. Proposition 2.2.1 (PDF pp. 43-44) proves that a second-order variogram is even, nonnegative, and conditionally negative definite; for zero-sum real coefficients the quadratic form reduces to minus the variance of a finite linear combination of increments. The draft prints complex weights without conjugation, so use real weights or the corrected Hermitian form. The proposition's Schoenberg characterization $e^{-\lambda\gamma}$ positive definite for all $\lambda$ is cited to Schoenberg, not proved there; leave it out unless another complete proof supplier is added. The 2009 draft's Theorem 2.4.1 (PDF pp. 57-61) is a separate Karhunen factorization representation: the covariance factors through $L^2(E,\nu)$; a representing random measure may require extending the probability space if the feature functions are not complete. Do not conflate this with the stationary Fourier spectral measure.
- **Statistical field estimates.** Spodarev 2024 Theorem 2.5 (PDF p. 17) proves L2 consistency of the spatial mean over a Van Hove sequence when the field is stationary, has finite second moment, and its covariance is integrable; its proof is the exact covariance double integral divided by squared window volume and a boundary/Van-Hove limit. Lemma 2.2 gives unbiasedness. Lemma 2.8 (PDF p. 19) derives asymptotic unbiasedness of sample covariance/variogram under the stated mean-estimator consistency; it is not an exact unbiasedness claim. The periodogram/Fejér identity and Lemma 2.10 (PDF p. 20) support expected-spectrum and smoothed-bias statements on a regular grid. Variance/weak-consistency claims in this unit are cited or only sketched; exclude them as guaranteed inference. A sample covariance estimate need not itself be a valid positive-semidefinite covariance model, as the Stanford covariance-estimation lecture illustrates.

### Kriging

Spodarev 2024 Sections 3.1-3.2 (PDF pp. 26-48) and Sun [Stanford Lecture 5](https://web.stanford.edu/class/stats253/lectures/lect5.pdf), read in full, independently cover best linear prediction and simple/ordinary/universal kriging. The latter is a compact slide derivation, not a full proof source; the longer Spodarev section supplies the quadratic optimization route.

- **Simple kriging:** for square-integrable observations, known mean function, and invertible observation covariance matrix Σ, weights are λ=Σ⁻¹c₀ and the predictor is μ(s₀)+λᵀ(Z-μ); its MSE is C(0)-c₀ᵀΣ⁻¹c₀. The normal equations prove best affine/linear prediction. For a Gaussian field the predictor is the conditional mean. State invertibility explicitly; a merely nonnegative covariance kernel may give a singular matrix.
- **Ordinary kriging:** with an unknown constant mean, unbiasedness imposes \(\sum_j\lambda_j=1\). Minimize the quadratic prediction error subject to that constraint; Lagrange multipliers give the augmented system. State nonsingularity/conditional-definiteness of the constrained system. Do not import simple-kriging variance shrinkage to ordinary kriging; Spodarev explicitly notes that it need not hold.
- **Universal kriging:** for trend \(\mu(s)=p(s)^T\beta\), unbiasedness imposes $P^T\lambda=p(s_0)$. The augmented covariance/trend system is uniquely solvable only with the needed covariance and full-column-rank design conditions. Spodarev Lemma 3.28 (PDF p. 46) incorrectly infers finite-site design-matrix rank from orthonormality of functions in a function space; do not reuse that proof. Estimated covariance/drift weights are plug-in estimates and are not the oracle optimum; no optimality or coverage claim follows without a separate result.

### Spatial and temporal point processes

- **Point process/Poisson/Cox.** Last and Penrose [Lectures on the Poisson Process](https://stoch.math.kit.edu/img/Last/lastpenrose2017.pdf), selected complete Chapters 1-4 (PDF pp. 13-49), rigorously treats point processes as random counting measures, intensity and Campbell formula (Proposition 2.7), Laplace functionals/law equality (Proposition 2.10), Poisson existence (Theorem 3.6), Laplace functional (Theorem 3.9) and Mecke's equation/converse (Theorem 4.1). The proof routes are simple-function/monotone convergence, independent counts, and the Poisson Laplace functional. Chapter 13 (PDF pp. 139-147) defines random measures/Cox processes; Proposition 13.6 proves the conditional-Poisson variance decomposition and Theorem 13.8 proves a Cox-Mecke characterization. Theorem 13.7 states that the Cox law determines the directing random-measure law; its proof reaches Proposition B.5 in Appendix B.1 (PDF p. 275), which was also read, but that proposition itself cites an external reference for analytic uniqueness. The final uniqueness step is therefore not a complete proof within the read source and is not used as a proved dependency. Appendix B.2 (PDF pp. 276-278) was read through the full proof of Theorem B.11, which supplies the input used by Chapter 8 Theorem 8.14. Chapter 13 Theorem 13.11 specifically invokes unread Chapter 6 Proposition 6.9 and Theorem 6.11; neither is used as a dependency. Baddeley independently supplies applied definitions and finite-window Poisson examples, but labels his notes minimum-detail.
- **Spatial stationary summaries/Palm.** Last–Penrose Chapter 8 (PDF pp. 81-93) gives stationarity, intensity, second factorial/reduced moment measures and Theorem 8.14: for stationary finite-intensity η on \(\mathbb R^d\), compact convex W with nonempty interior and dilations (W_n=a_nW), counts divided by volume converge in $L^1$ to conditional intensity given the invariant sigma-field. Under ergodicity this is the constant intensity. It gives no rate or CLT. Chapter 9 (PDF pp. 94-103) proves the refined Campbell/Palm formula (Theorem 9.1) and Poisson Slivnyak result (Theorem 9.4); Theorem 9.1 uses Proposition 8.7 (read in Chapter 8) and Proposition 6.3, whose proof-supporting Chapter 6 Section 6.1 pages 57-60 were also read. Baddeley Lecture 2 (pp. 26-42) independently defines second/factorial moments, pair correlation, K-function and edge-corrected estimators; Lecture 3 (pp. 43-56) introduces Palm and Campbell-Mecke. Define pair correlation only when the second factorial moment measure has the required density; it need not exist. Interpret K/Palm formulas under their stationarity/intensity assumptions, not by conditioning naively on an event of probability zero.
- **Spatial finite-window likelihoods.** Baddeley Lecture 4 (pp. 57-73) defines finite-window Poisson, hard-core and Strauss/Gibbs models, likelihood ratios relative to a Poisson reference, and pseudolikelihood. A direct proposed theorem can derive the inhomogeneous Poisson log likelihood $\sum_{x\in\eta\cap W}\log\lambda_\theta(x)-\int_W\lambda_\theta(x)dx$ for a stated observation window/reference measure, and $\hat\rho=N(W)/|W|$ for a homogeneous model (with the zero-count boundary case stated separately). A Gibbs density relative to a unit-rate Poisson reference must include a finite positive normalizing constant and support; pseudolikelihood is an approximation/estimating procedure, not the exact likelihood and not a proved consistency guarantee in these notes. No general Gibbs existence, mixing, or asymptotic inference claim is proposed.
- **Temporal conditional intensity.** Rasmussen's complete 19-page [Aalborg/arXiv notes](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.00221) prove Proposition 2.1 (conditional intensity to inter-event survival) and Proposition 2.2 (to conditional event density/distribution), and Proposition 3.1 (finite-window likelihood). The exact proof hypotheses include nonnegative predictable/local-integrable intensity and a non-explosion/infinite cumulative-hazard condition when requiring a next event almost surely; terminating processes instead need a mass at infinity/survival term. Likelihood is a product of event intensities times $\exp(-\int_0^T\lambda^*(u)du)$, under the stated simple-process/observation conditions; marks require their own conditional mark factor. The homogeneous Poisson MLE follows by differentiating the resulting log likelihood and checking the zero-count boundary. Hawkes/self-correcting formulas are examples of history dependence; the notes do not prove every parameterized intensity specification has a nonexplosive stationary realization.

## Proposed dependency-ordered A/B pairs

### TS01 — Stationarity, dependence, and sample autocovariance

- **A proposals:** `def-strict-and-weak-stationarity-for-time-series`; `def-autocovariance-and-sample-autocovariance`; `thm-sample-acvf-properties-and-summable-covariance-mean-consistency`.
- **B proposal:** `ex-stationary-dependent-series-and-nonergodic-mean`.
- **Content/assumptions:** Define strict stationarity by translation invariance of all FDDs on ℤ; weak stationarity by finite second moments, constant mean and lag-only covariance. Strict stationarity does not imply weak stationarity without moments; weak and strict stationarity coincide for Gaussian processes with finite second moments. State covariance identities and positive-definiteness. Define known-mean and sample-mean-centered ACVF with its denominator convention. Prove the exact finite-sample expectation factor $1-|h|/n$ for known-mean covariance, explain centering bias, prove positive semidefiniteness of the $1/n$ sample ACVF via a Gram sum, and show absolute covariance summability gives the displayed L2 sample-mean consistency bound. Separately allow the existing Birkhoff theorem for strict stationary ergodic integrable sequences.
- **Proof route and limit:** Group the double covariance sum by lag for the mean variance; use the covariance quadratic form for PSD; expand each sample lag sum for its expectation. B illustrates a random constant process: strictly stationary but nonergodic, with sample mean equal to the random level rather than its ensemble mean. It can also use the centered sample (1,0,0): the $1/(n-|h|)$ ACVF values $(2/9,-1/18,-2/9)$ give a Toeplitz matrix with eigenvalue proportional to $4-2\sqrt6<0$, so that rescaling is not always positive semidefinite. Do not claim an iid/dependent CLT or sample-ACF limit from stationarity/summability alone.
- **Suppliers/sources:** Existing covariance and Birkhoff suppliers in the audit. ETH pp. 2-9, 13-14; Wang Sections 2.1-2.2 and Lemma 2.3; [Wang](https://people.stat.sc.edu/wang528/Stat%20720/STAT720%20Notes.pdf) independently supplies covariance statements/proofs.

### TS02 — Spectral measures and periodogram inference

- **A proposals:** `def-time-series-spectral-measure-and-periodogram`; `thm-herglotz-spectrum-and-spectral-density`; `thm-periodogram-fejer-expectation-and-smoothing-bias`.
- **B proposal:** `ex-atomic-spectrum-versus-continuous-spectral-density`.
- **Content/assumptions:** Fix the ([-\pi,\pi]) Fourier convention. State γ(h)=∫ e^{ihλ}F(dλ), with finite positive F and F(total)=γ(0); identify a density only when F is absolutely continuous. Under ∑|γ(h)|<∞, derive the nonnegative Fourier density. Define the mean-corrected/known-mean periodogram normalization. Prove the finite-sample expected-periodogram Fejér convolution. Define kernel-smoothed spectral estimation separately and prove asymptotic bias under continuity of f and approximate-identity/bandwidth assumptions.
- **Proof route and limit:** Reuse the existing full LCA Bochner theorem for Herglotz. Expand the periodogram as a finite double sum, use covariance stationarity, then identify Fejér weights; the smoothing-bias proof is the approximate-identity argument. B compares an atomic sinusoidal spectrum with an AR(1) density.
