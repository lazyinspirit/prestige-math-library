## Scope and interface

The two strands are event-time inference and scientific measurement. The core reusable guarantees are definitions and likelihood calculations under explicit observation models, risk-set algorithms, and inference only under stated sampling, censoring, regularity, and measurement assumptions. Applicability of those assumptions to a clinical, engineering, or laboratory process is empirical and cannot be proved by the mathematical scaffold.

No models-04 pair should depend on Bayesian or computational-statistics category 3 or machine-learning category 4. The 2026 GUM amendment mentions Monte Carlo evaluation for significant nonlinearity; that is not imported here. The proposed uncertainty pair uses first-order Taylor propagation and labels its approximation. If that approximation is inadequate, the scaffold records higher-order Taylor treatment as a limit, not an unproved fallback.

## Planned category-1 interface requests

These are the smallest mathematical prerequisites missing from the inspected library. Models-04 definitions and results may depend on these requests only after category-1 planning supplies them; they are not existing suppliers.

1. **CT counting-process compensator and predictable integral.** Define a counting process relative to a filtration, intensity/compensator, and predictable integral; prove the intensity criterion giving M=N-A an all-pairs martingale, its predictable bracket, square-integrability/isometry, and orthogonality when two counting processes cannot jump together. At-risk processes must be predictable and the assumptions for ties, jumps, and finite horizons stated. Existing continuous-time definitions are reusable; discrete compensators and Brownian Ito integration are not substitutes.
2. **Counting-process martingale functional CLT.** On a finite horizon, prove a suitable martingale functional CLT under predictable-bracket convergence and a no-large-jumps/Lindeberg condition, with the exact path topology and tightness conditions. A scalar terminal martingale CLT alone does not justify bands or process limits for KM/Cox curves. Source stanford_unit11_martingale_clt (STAT331 Unit 13) states a counting-process version on pp. 10-12 but refers the proof to Fleming–Harrington Chapter 5. If the track supplies only pointwise limits, models-04 must keep the conclusions pointwise and not state functional convergence.
3. **Finite-dimensional delta method.** State the usual differentiability, normalization, weak-limit and covariance hypotheses and prove the first-order normal/covariance propagation result from Taylor expansion and Slutsky. The existing Taylor and iid-CLT items are useful inputs but no delta-method theorem was located.

No dependency on future categories 3 or 4 is needed. The general survival likelihood, risk-set algorithms, and descriptive measurement procedures belong in category 2; the three requests above are category-1 mathematical interfaces.

### 1. Observation schemes, censoring, and truncation

**A proposal:** def-event-time-observation-schemes and thm-likelihood-contributions-under-censoring-and-delayed-entry. Define latent event time T; right censoring U=min(T,C), Delta=1(T<=C); left, interval (L,R], and current-status censoring; and delayed entry at L, where only L<T subjects enter. Keep censoring (coarsening after sampling) distinct from truncation (selection into the sample). Give likelihood contributions f(U)^Delta S(U)^(1-Delta) for right censoring under T independent of C, F(R)-F(L) for interval observation, and event/censor contribution divided by S(L) for left-truncated right-censored data under independent entry and the stated observation model. A conditional version may state T independent of C given Z via existing conditional independence; do not infer that assumption from covariate adjustment alone.

**B proposal:** ex-event-time-observation-and-selection-bias. Hand-compute the observation contributions and contrast independent administrative censoring with outcome-dependent dropout and left truncation. Use a small selection counterexample to show that conditioning on L<T changes the observed lifetime distribution.

**Proof route and limits:** Factor the joint event/censor density under independence and drop the censoring factor only when it contains no event-model parameter. For delayed entry, condition the event-time law on T>L, giving denominator S(L); Eric Slud's two-page handout specifically corrects an omitted denominator in a classroom derivation. These calculations do not prove that an empirical censoring or entry mechanism is independent. General interval-censored NPMLEs are out of scope here and must not be implied by the right-censoring formulas.

**Suppliers/sources:** Existing def-cumulative-distribution-function-of-a-random-variable, def-independent-random-elements, def-conditional-independence-given-a-sigma-algebra, thm-taking-out-what-is-known (probability, independence, Markov-kernels, conditional-expectation pages). Sources: binghamton_qiqing_yu_survival_notes pp. 1-11; stanford_unit02_censoring_likelihood pp. 1-4; maryland_slud_left_trunc_right_cens_handout pp. 1-2.

### 2. Survival functions and reliability models

**A proposal:** def-survival-function-hazard-and-reliability-models, with separate thm-censored-exponential-likelihood-and-mle. Define S(t)=P(T>t), reliability R(t)=S(t) for a nonrepairable lifetime, hazard as the conditional instantaneous event rate where the limit/density exists, and cumulative hazard. Distinguish the continuous-hazard identity S(t)=exp{-integral from 0 to t of lambda(u)du} from jump/discrete hazards. Define exponential and Weibull families; do not describe the empirical bathtub curve as a theorem. For iid right-censored exponential data with independent censoring and positive total observed exposure, likelihood L(lambda) proportional to lambda^d exp(-lambda sum_i U_i) gives lambda-hat=d/sum_i U_i when d>0; if d=0, the likelihood supremum is at boundary lambda=0, so a positive interior MLE does not exist.

**B proposal:** ex-reliability-lifetimes-and-censoring. Contrast exponential constant hazard with increasing/decreasing Weibull hazard and show right-censored equipment observation contributes exposure time but no event factor.

**Proof route and limits:** Differentiate the log likelihood d log(lambda)-lambda sum U_i; separately check the boundary d=0. Parametric fit and a reliability curve describe a model only to the extent its assumptions fit the physical process. Repairable-system failure counts are a recurrent-event process, not a single lifetime with reliability S.

**Suppliers/sources:** Existing def-cumulative-distribution-function-of-a-random-variable, thm-probability-law-and-distribution-function-correspondence, thm-lebesgue-stieltjes-correspondence-with-distribution-functions. Sources: nist_sematech_reliability_chapter8 pp. 4-19, 155-163; stanford_unit02_censoring_likelihood pp. 1-7; binghamton_qiqing_yu_survival_notes pp. 1-11.

### 3. Counting processes, at-risk sets, and Nelson–Aalen

**A proposal:** def-survival-counting-and-at-risk-processes, def-nelson-aalen-estimator, and result thm-counting-process-compensator-and-nelson-aalen-properties. For right-censored subjects let N_i(t)=1(U_i<=t,Delta_i=1), Y_i(t)=1(U_i>=t); with delayed entry use Y_i(t)=1(L_i<t<=U_i), with endpoint convention fixed. Write N-dot=sum_i N_i and Y-dot=sum_i Y_i. For absolutely continuous hazard and an independent observation mechanism, the proposed intensity is Y_i(t)lambda(t given Z_i); define A_i(t)=integral from 0 to t of Y_i(u)lambda(u given Z_i)du. The estimator algorithm is Lambda-hat(t)=integral from 0 to t of 1{Y-dot(u)>0}Y-dot(u)^(-1)dN-dot(u), with 0/0=0. Keep the algorithm separate from the Cat-1 result that N_i-A_i is a martingale.

**B proposal:** ex-risk-sets-compensators-and-nelson-aalen. Work a small right-censored/delayed-entry table by hand and compare Y, event counts, and estimated cumulative hazard.

**Proof route and limits:** The one-event proof conditions on being at risk at time t and equates expected event increments with expected integrated hazard. For several subjects, sum the martingales and apply the bounded predictable integral result only after the planned counting-process interface is available. The right-continuous-at-event convention and continuous-hazard proof do not automatically cover tied jumps or arbitrary censoring. Mai Zhou's notes are explicitly an intuitive supplement, not a rigorous general martingale proof.

### 4. Kaplan–Meier product limit and uncertainty

**A proposal:** def-kaplan-meier-product-limit-estimator and distinct results thm-finite-support-kaplan-meier-consistency, thm-kaplan-meier-pointwise-limit-and-greenwood-estimator. At ordered event times t_j, define S-hat(t)=product over t_j<=t of (1-d_j/Y_j), where d_j events occur among Y_j at risk just before t_j. Define Greenwood's estimated variance as S-hat(t)^2 times the sum over t_j<=t of d_j/[Y_j(Y_j-d_j)] on ranges where denominators are nonzero. State that it estimates asymptotic variance under the model; it is not an exact finite-sample variance identity. State consistency only on horizons with positive event-survival and censoring-survival probabilities. A pointwise normal limit requires the counting-process martingale CLT and delta method; process-level bands require the requested functional CLT.

**B proposal:** ex-kaplan-meier-risk-set-calculation. Calculate a product limit and Greenwood estimate for hand data; include the boundary case where the last observed subject is censored and the curve is not identified past last adequate follow-up.

**Proof route and limits:** For finite support, derive conditional event probabilities in each risk set and use the strong law, then induct over ordered support points; Yu's notes prove this special case and state a general Yu–Li consistency result. For continuous times, express Lambda-hat-Lambda as a predictable integral of counting-process martingales plus a remainder that vanishes when both S_T and S_C stay positive on a fixed horizon; apply the martingale functional CLT, then the exponential/product-limit expansion. Stanford's course notes give that route and asymptotic result, but refer the general CLT and several process inequalities to Fleming–Harrington/Andersen et al. Greenwood follows a first-order log-product/delta calculation. Do not claim inference beyond the observation-overlap support.

**Suppliers/sources:** Existing distribution/CDF, conditional independence, martingale definitions, Taylor and Slutsky suppliers listed above; Cat-1 counting-process, functional-CLT and delta-method interfaces are required. Sources: stanford_unit03_kaplan_meier pp. 1-12; stanford_unit12_revisit_km pp. 2-14; independent mathematical cross-check binghamton_qiqing_yu_survival_notes pp. 30-38; algorithm cross-check nist_sematech_reliability_chapter8 pp. 103-104. ### 5. Logrank and weighted logrank procedures

**A proposal:** def-logrank-risk-set-score-and-test, separate from thm-logrank-null-limit. At event time t_j in a two-group study, define O_j, E_j=d_jY_1(t_j)/Y(t_j), and the hypergeometric conditional variance V_j; use Z=(sum_j(O_j-E_j))/sqrt(sum_jV_j). Define a weighted procedure by a stated predictable/time weight; do not call every weighting choice equally powerful or exactly calibrated. State the null as equal hazards/survival distributions and specify independent subject sampling, censoring independent of event time conditional on group (or modeled covariates), nondegenerate allocation/risk sets, horizon and the no-large-jump conditions for a normal limit. A Cox score equivalence at beta=0 is a separate result with the same risk-set assumptions.

**B proposal:** ex-logrank-with-crossing-hazards. Hand-calculate O-E,V; use crossing hazards/survival curves as a counterexample to reading a nonsignificant global logrank test as equality at every time.

**Proof route and limits:** Under the null, conditional allocation of failures across group risk sets is hypergeometric, yielding the score and variance. The counting-process representation writes the numerator as a sum of bounded predictable integrals against group martingales; the planned Cat-1 functional/terminal martingale CLT gives the limit under bracket and jump conditions. Stanford Unit 6 derives the finite risk-set statistic; its large-sample revisit supplies a martingale route but relies on external general CLT proofs. Weights change the alternative sensitivity; they do not repair informative censoring or prove proportional hazards.

**Suppliers/sources:** Existing conditional expectation/independence and continuous-time definitions; the Cat-1 counting-process and CLT interfaces are required. ### 6. Cox proportional hazards regression

**A proposal:** def-cox-proportional-hazards-model-and-partial-likelihood, separate thm-cox-score-limit-and-estimator-linearization. Define lambda(t given Z)=lambda_0(t)exp(beta transpose Z); under the model the hazard ratio for contrast z_1-z_0 is exp(beta transpose (z_1-z_0)), not a risk ratio. With no tied event times define L_p(beta) as the product over events j of exp(beta transpose Z_(j)) divided by the sum over subjects ell in R(t_j) of exp(beta transpose Z_ell). Give exact conditional risk-set forms for ties separately from Breslow/Efron approximations. State independent subjects, T independent of C given Z, correct proportional-hazards specification, predictable/appropriately observed covariates, boundedness or suitable moment conditions, positive risk-set denominators, and nonsingular limiting information for asymptotic normal inference. Make clear partial likelihood is not generally the full likelihood for censored observations.

**B proposal:** ex-cox-risk-set-likelihood-and-model-checks. Compute one partial-likelihood contribution by hand and contrast a constant hazard ratio with a time-varying/crossing effect. Diagnostics and subject-matter plausibility are empirical checks, not proofs of model assumptions or causal interpretation.

**Proof route and limits:** Differentiate log L_p for the score and Hessian. Under PH and conditional independent censoring, represent the score as sum_i integral of (Z_i-Z-bar(beta,t)) dM_i(t); bounded/predictable integrands and bracket convergence plus the planned martingale CLT give the score limit. A Taylor expansion around the true parameter and Slutsky yield the estimator limit if limiting information is positive definite. Stanford Unit 16 sketches consistency and this linearization; the MCLT proof is external. Yu pp. 58-63 independently derives the partial likelihood and warns that for discrete event times proportional hazards and proportional integrated hazards differ unless the baseline survival is continuous. Preserve that caveat. No causal claim follows from a fitted association.

### 7. Competing risks

**A proposal:** def-competing-risk-data-cause-specific-hazards-and-cumulative-incidence, separate thm-cause-specific-cumulative-incidence-decomposition. Define observed (T,D), cause-specific hazards lambda_k, all-cause survival S(t)=P(T>t), and cumulative incidence F_k(t)=P(T<=t,D=k)=integral over [0,t] of S(u-)dLambda_k(u); under continuous hazards this is integral from 0 to t of S(u)lambda_k(u)du. Define the Aalen–Johansen plug-in algorithm using all-cause S-hat and cause-k Nelson–Aalen increments. Separate cause-specific hazard regression from CIF regression.

**B proposal:** ex-competing-event-risk-versus-cause-incidence. Use a two-cause death example to show why a lower cancer-specific cumulative incidence can result from higher cardiovascular mortality and why treating competing failures as censoring in 1-S-hat_k does not estimate the observed CIF.

**Proof route and limits:** The CIF identity follows by decomposing the event probability over disjoint cause-specific increments and multiplying by survival from all causes immediately before the increment. Estimate it by the corresponding product-limit/compensator increments. The Stanford slides state these formulas and explicitly show that latent cause-specific failure-time joint laws are not identified from observed (T,D); no independence of latent times is assumed. Treating other causes as censored can support cause-specific hazard estimation under the censoring/model assumptions, but it does not make a competing event independent censoring for marginal CIF interpretation. The inspected slides do not prove the Aalen–Johansen limit theory; that requires the Cat-1 counting-process/functional-CLT interface.

**Suppliers/sources:** Existing law/CDF and planned counting-process suppliers only. Source: stanford_competing_risks_slides pp. 2-12. This is a slide-level source; no second complete proof treatment was obtained, so the planned result must carry the proof route and limitation above.

### 8. Measurement models, calibration, and uncertainty

**A proposal:** def-measurand-calibration-and-measurement-uncertainty, separate thm-first-order-measurement-uncertainty-propagation. Define a measurand, measurement model Y=f(X_1,...,X_m), corrections for recognized biases, reference-standard traceability, input estimates and standard uncertainties. Type A and Type B classify evaluation methods, not random versus systematic error. An error is not the same quantity as uncertainty. With input covariance matrix Sigma, first-order propagation is u_c^2(Y) approximately equal to grad(f)^T Sigma grad(f), derived by Taylor expansion and covariance bilinearity. Carry calibration coefficient covariance and future reading noise through the prediction; state approximation and correlation assumptions. Expanded uncertainty U=ku_c is not automatically a confidence interval: any coverage interpretation requires the distribution and effective-degrees-of-freedom conditions.

**B proposal:** ex-calibration-curve-reference-uncertainty-and-batch-drift. A calibration example propagates coefficient/reference uncertainty and a future measurement contribution. A day-varying slope example contrasts a per-batch curve with a pooled curve.

**Proof route and limits:** The local propagation expression is the covariance of the first-order Taylor linearization. If nonlinear remainder is not negligible, this approximation is not established by the first-order argument; the 2026 GUM amendment recommends higher-order Taylor or a Monte Carlo method. This scaffold may propose higher-order Taylor from the existing Taylor supplier; do not import Monte Carlo implementation. NIST's calibration chapter describes reference standards treated as exact for curve fitting while separately requiring their uncertainty in the result; it does not provide an errors-in-variables theorem. Its examples of per-batch curves and changing day slopes are process evidence, not universal laws. Measurement uncertainty applies to a stated measurand and configuration only to the extent the model and study represent that process.

**Suppliers/sources:** Existing def-moments-variance-and-covariance, lem-variance-and-covariance-identities-for-random-variables, thm-covariance-bilinearity-and-symmetry, thm-multivariable-taylor-formula-with-lagrange-remainder, cor-multivariable-taylor-formula-with-peano-remainder, and the planned Cat-1 delta-method interface. ### 10. Agreement between measurement methods

**A proposal:** def-method-agreement-and-limits, separate thm-normal-difference-agreement-limits. For paired differences D_i=A_i-B_i, define bias D-bar, sample spread s_D, and estimated limits D-bar plus/minus 1.96s_D. Under an approximately normal population of independent paired differences, the corresponding population limits mu_D plus/minus 1.96sigma_D contain approximately 95% of individual differences. They estimate agreement range and are distinct from confidence intervals for either limit. If differences vary with magnitude, a log ratio analysis may be suitable under positive values and its own multiplicative model. Acceptability margins must be set for the intended use before looking at the plot. Correlation alone is not agreement and agreement is not calibration against a known truth.

**B proposal:** ex-correlation-without-agreement. Give a perfectly correlated but offset or clinically too-dispersed pair of methods; then calculate the bias and limits. Include the primary paper's repeated-measures caveat only with its replication assumptions.

**Proof route and limits:** For normal D, standardize (D-mu_D)/sigma_D and use the standard normal 2.5%/97.5% quantiles (approximately plus/minus 1.96); replacing population parameters by D-bar,s_D yields estimated limits, not exact finite-sample coverage. Bland–Altman explicitly separates estimates from the population limits, recommends separate confidence intervals for endpoints, and says acceptable tolerances are application-specific. Its illustrative example is not evidence that two instruments agree.

**Suppliers/sources:** Existing def-moments-variance-and-covariance, covariance bilinearity and def-standard-normal-and-normal-laws. Source: bland_altman_1986_agreement pp. 1-9 (complete York-hosted reproduction; original Lancet pagination 307-310). No second full proof source was located; the paper itself is the methodological primary source.

### 11. Batch effects and measurement-process variation

Define batch/day/operator/gauge effects as named sources of measurement-process variation; distinguish within-run repeatability, between-run reproducibility and temporal stability. Decide fixed versus random effects from the target population and estimand, not convenience. A calibration curve pooled across batches is valid only under an explicit stable shared-curve model.

**B proposal:** ex-batch-drift-invalidates-pooled-calibration. Work the NIST example where day-specific slope changes make an averaged curve misleading; propose a check-standard or stratified calibration design and identify what each design can detect.

**Proof route and limits:** For a stated linear variance-components model, derive variance by independence/covariance algebra and estimate only with the model's replication/nesting design and assumptions. Without that model, retain batch comparisons as descriptive evidence and do not claim a universal correction, variance decomposition, or guarantee. NIST documents measurement-study designs and empirical drift examples; it warns the uncertainty is specific to a result and process representation.

## Reading audit, cross-checks, and unresolved gaps

The course units for censoring likelihood (Stanford Unit 2), KM (Unit 3), logrank (Unit 6), Cox (Units 7 and 9), counting-process/martingale development (Units 10-13), asymptotic KM/logrank/Cox (Units 15, 19, 20, 21), and competing risks (slides 1-12) were read across the full stated units. The unused Stanford Cox examples handout and continued two-sample-tests handout were completely extracted but not read or relied upon. The unit naming in the cached filenames is normalized; the document header titles are preserved in sources.json.

Qiqing Yu's full 87-page notes were extracted; only pp. 1-11, 30-38 and 58-63 were read. The pp. 30-38 finite-support Kaplan–Meier consistency proof independently cross-checks Stanford's product-limit derivation, and pp. 58-63 independently derive Cox's risk-set likelihood while flagging the discrete-time PH versus proportional-integrated-hazard distinction. The notes state a general Yu–Li consistency result but the proof read here is the finite-support induction plus finite-dimensional delta-method calculation; no claim is made that those pages prove Stanford's full functional CLT.

Mai Zhou's full 35-page notes were extracted; pp. 1-7 and 29-35 were read. The small-interval intensity/time-change derivation and left-entry risk indicator are useful cross-checks, but the notes explicitly say they supplement Fleming–Harrington and are not intended as a rigorous treatment of counting-process martingales. Eric Slud's complete two-page Maryland note supports the delayed-entry likelihood and Nelson–Aalen piecewise-hazard calculation, not general asymptotics.

The full Stanford process sequence includes a direct one-subject N-A proof under continuous event times and independent censoring, then states Doob–Meyer, bracket, stochastic-integral and martingale CLT results. The logrank finite risk-set derivation and later martingale argument are two units by the same author/course, not an independent proof; no second complete independent logrank proof was obtained. The Cox partial-likelihood and finite-support KM derivations do have the separate Yu cross-checks recorded above. Some general proofs are explicitly omitted or referred to Fleming–Harrington, Andersen–Borgan–Gill–Keiding, or Rogers–Williams. The finite-library martingale CLT proof is discrete time, so those omitted extensions remain Cat-1 requests. KM and Cox limits are therefore proposed only with the conditions and dependency interfaces written above.

For measurement, the complete main clauses 1-8 of the GUM were read (PDF pp. 13-39), the complete five-page 2026 amendment was read, and NIST Chapter 2 sections were read in the ranges above. These support model-based first-order uncertainty propagation, covariance accounting, calibration and process-characterization limitations. Neither a named uncertainty evaluation nor a gauge study proves that the physical process is stable or representative. Detection-limit treatment is narrower: the official 399-page NIST SP 958 was completely extracted but only PDF pp. 177-179 (printed pp. 164-166) were read. The IUPAC Currie PDF was attempted at its official URL and returned HTTP 403, so only NIST's historical summary is relied on for the listed formulas. Bland and Altman's full nine-page author-hosted reproduction was read; no second complete treatment was obtained.
