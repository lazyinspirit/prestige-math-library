## Fixed category, stacks, coefficients and conventions

Let Q be a finite acyclic quiver, k=F_q a finite field, A=Rep_k(Q) its essentially small category of finite-dimensional representations with a fixed skeleton, N=Z^{Q_0}, N_+=N^{Q_0}. Take K=Q(t), specialized at t=sqrt(q)>0 for the counting theorem; alternatively use K=Q(sqrt(q)) throughout that theorem. Distinguish the variable t used in the rational A2 identity from a fixed field cardinality. Set

χ(d,e)=Σ_i d_i e_i−Σ_{a:i→j}d_i e_j, and ⟨d,e⟩=χ(d,e)−χ(e,d).

The Hall convention is **subobject first**: (f*g)(M)=Σ_{U⊂M}f(U)g(M/U). Translate the upstream Hall page's convention explicitly if it uses quotient first. Use δ_M for the isoclass indicator, a_M=|Aut M|, κ_M=a_M δ_M. Complete by dimension: Hhat=∏_{d∈N_+}H_d, where H_d consists of functions on isoclasses of dimension d. Each H_d is finite dimensional since Rep_d(Q)(F_q) is finite. Degree-zero unit is δ_0. Use the total-dimension filtration; all infinite products/sums below are interpreted modulo degrees >D and then inverse-limited. No analytic convergence assertion.

For arbitrary z_i in the open upper half-plane, set Z(d)=Σ_i d_i z_i, φ(M)=arg Z(dim M)/π. Semistable means φ(U)≤φ(M) for every nonzero subobject U. Equal-phase zero objects are included in each semistable subcategory. The stack of degree-d semistables is [Rep_d(Q)^{ss}/G_d], G_d=∏_i GL_{d_i}. Its geometric definition tests subrepresentations over algebraic closures. 2. **HN existence, uniqueness, and rationality lemma.** For every M there is a unique filtration with nonzero semistable factors in strictly decreasing phase. In a fixed M, only finitely many dimension vectors occur. Choose a subobject of maximal phase, then maximal total dimension among those. The elementary argument-of-a-sum inequality implies this subobject is semistable. If a quotient subobject had phase at least that maximal phase, its inverse image would have larger phase or the same phase and greater dimension, contradiction. Repeat on the quotient; dimension strictly decreases. For uniqueness, first prove Hom(A,B)=0 for semistable A,B with φ(A)>φ(B): the image is both a quotient of A of phase at least φ(A), and a subobject of B of phase at most φ(B). Compare two filtrations using this vanishing to identify their first pieces, then induct. One can equivalently prove that sums of maximum-phase subobjects again have maximum phase, obtaining the unique largest destabilizing subobject directly. Over algebraic closures, uniqueness makes the filtration Frobenius invariant; each invariant subspace is defined over F_q (prove by unique reduced row-echelon equations and Frobenius). Thus semistability/HN for F_q representations agrees with the geometric stack condition. This detail is required to connect stack notation with finite counts.

3. **Extension-count normalization lemma.** For all U,V,

κ_U*κ_V=Σ_M |Ext1(V,U)_M|/|Hom(V,U)| ·κ_M.

Do not cite an exercise as a proof. Count exact sequences 0→U→M→V→0 in two ways. For fixed M their number is F_{U,V}^M a_U a_V, where F counts the indicated subobjects. Aut(M) acts on these sequences; its orbits are extension classes with middle term M, and each stabilizer is the subgroup 1+i h p, h∈Hom(V,U), of size |Hom(V,U)|. Orbit–stabilizer gives F_{U,V}^M a_U a_V/a_M=|Ext1(V,U)_M|/|Hom(V,U)|. All sets here are finite.

A_all=∏_{φ decreasing}S_Z(φ).
