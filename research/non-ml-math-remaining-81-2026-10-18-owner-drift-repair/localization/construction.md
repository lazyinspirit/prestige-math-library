## Exact convention and theorem

Work over C, with G connected simply connected semisimple, B a Borel, X=G/B, h=b/[b,b], and positive roots those in b. Carry the Axiom of Choice inherited from the actual invariant-theory and algebraic-geometry suppliers. Rank-zero is the identity equivalence on vector spaces. Do not silently claim arbitrary fields from this complex source chain.

Use Kashiwara's parameter kappa and write O_X(eta)=G×^B C_eta. For eta integral this line bundle has fiber character eta; O_X(eta) is ample when eta is strictly negative on positive simple coroots. Let rho be half the sum of positive roots. Define the sheaf of action algebras U_X(g)=O_X#U(g), with [xi,f]=xi_X(f), the isotropy ideal b_X=ker(O_X⊗g→T_X), and

`D_kappa = U_X(g)/(xi-(kappa+rho)(xi) : xi in b_X)`.

Here the isotropy is a sheaf, not the fixed subalgebra b inside U(g); the latter quotient is not an algebra. A TDO is an O_X-ring (the image of O_X need not be central) with its order filtration, F_0=O_X and gr D=Sym T_X, whose first-order commutator is the anchor. All D-modules below are left modules quasicoherent over O_X. For integral kappa, D_kappa is the sheaf of differential operators on O_X(kappa+rho); hence D_{-rho}=D_X. The Harish–Chandra character is chi_kappa: if z projects to f_z in U(h) along U(g)n, then chi_kappa(z)=f_z(kappa-rho). It is W-invariant in kappa. The action on D_kappa is via chi_kappa (check right Verma fibers and the principal antiautomorphism, rather than guessing the sign).

Parameter conversion: Sun's L_lambda has fiber character -lambda, Sun's D^lambda = D_{-lambda}, and Sun's chi_{-lambda} is our chi_kappa. MIT Lecture 29's unshifted line-bundle twist eta equals kappa+rho. Thus its U_eta has central character chi_{eta-rho}=chi_kappa. These conversions remove the apparent discrepancy between dominant and antidominant formulations.

For an arbitrary complex kappa, **antidominant** means <kappa,alpha^vee> is not a positive integer for every positive root alpha. **Regular** means <kappa,alpha^vee> is nonzero for every root. These are separate conditions: positivity/order inequalities on arbitrary complex numbers are meaningless. For real weights in the closed negative chamber the first condition holds; strictly negative chamber weights satisfy both. Let A_kappa=U(g)/U(g)ker chi_kappa. The local theorem must prove:

1. For every kappa, A_kappa→Gamma(X,D_kappa) is a filtered algebra isomorphism.
2. For antidominant kappa, H^i(X,M)=0 for every quasicoherent D_kappa-module M and i>0.
3. For regular antidominant kappa, Gamma is conservative, and Loc_kappa=D_kappa⊗_{A_kappa}(-) and Gamma are inverse equivalences between all A_kappa-modules and quasicoherent D_kappa-modules. They restrict to finitely generated algebra modules and coherent D-modules. Regularity of an infinitesimal character alone does not license Gamma for a fixed non-antidominant twist. One may choose an antidominant W-representative of a regular character: write kappa=a+i b, move b to a closed chamber, and use its parabolic Weyl stabilizer to move the component of a in the perpendicular root subsystem to the negative chamber. Roots outside that subsystem have nonzero imaginary pairing; positive roots inside it have nonpositive real pairing. This proves antidominance, and regularity is W-invariant. Include these finite-chamber steps locally.

The exact complex-weight formulation and full equivalence proof are Kashiwara §§5.2, 6.1–6.4, printed pp. 89–96. Sun's word “regular” in Corollary 4.12 means strictly dominant, as defined in his §3.4; it is not bare W-regularity. His subsequent chamber remark only discusses real weights and must not replace the complex-weight statement above.

**(L3) Normality and Springer functions.** Supply `lem-pa36-nilpotent-normality-and-springer-functions`. Identify T*X with G×^B n via the Killing form and the moment map with (b,x)↦x. Properness is the closed incidence embedding in X×N followed by projective projection. Surjectivity follows because every nilpotent is in some n_b. Unique Borel over e can be proved directly with Bruhat: if e is in Ad(g)b, write g=b_1 w b_2; b_1^{-1}e still has every simple coefficient nonzero, so membership in Ad(w)b implies w^{-1} sends every positive simple root to a positive root. Hence w=1. This works after any algebraically closed field extension of C, so with generic smoothness in characteristic zero the map is birational. A point-count alone in positive characteristic would not suffice.

Prove normality without Sun's problematic base-change shortcut or an unproved finiteness-of-nilpotent-orbits premise. For each simple alpha_i let n_i be the sum of all positive root spaces except g_{alpha_i}. It is the nilradical of the minimal parabolic P_i, hence P_i-stable. The complement of G.e in N is contained in the union of the images G×^{P_i}n_i: conjugate a nilpotent into n; if every simple coefficient is nonzero, (L2) puts it in G.e, otherwise it belongs to n_i. Each such associated bundle has dimension (dim X-1)+(dim n-1)=dim N-2. Thus the complement has codimension at least two. The invariant Jacobian is full rank at e by the existing central-fiber lemma and hence on G.e. The coordinate ring is a complete intersection, so S_2, and is regular in codimension one, so `thm-serre-normality-criterion` gives normality. This argument needs the already proved root-group/minimal-parabolic constructions and Bruhat decomposition, e.g. `thm-bruhat-decomposition-for-split-reductive-group`; these are suppliers, not consequences of localization.

Finally prove the elementary proper-birational-normal lemma locally: for a normal affine open Spec R in N, proper pushforward of O is a finite R-module (`thm-proper-pushforward-coherent`); birationality embeds its section algebra into Frac R; each element is integral over R by finiteness, hence belongs to R. Therefore mu_*O_{T*X}=O_N and C[N]→Gamma(T*X,O) is an isomorphism. This avoids treating connected fibers as a proof that a rational function is regular.
