## Contract BELL: `MA_{<p}(σ-centered)`

**Exact statement.** Let `P` be a σ-centered poset with the convention that smaller conditions are stronger. Let `D={D_ξ:ξ<κ}` be dense subsets, with `κ<p`. There is a directed filter `G⊆P` meeting every `D_ξ`. There is no cardinality restriction on `P`, no regularity assumption on `p`, and `D_ξ` may first be replaced by its open dense downward closure. This is the direction required in Shelah Lemma 1.8(3) and in the short-right-side argument of Theorem 1.12.

**Dependencies.** The definition of `p` as the least size of an SFIP family without a pseudointersection, countable coding of finite sequences, elementary cardinal arithmetic under AC, and the σ-centered forcing definition. Bell's source writes `P(c)` for the assertion that every centered family of fewer than `c` infinite subsets of `ω` has a pseudointersection. The proof of §1 uses the assertion only for the particular family sizes below the number of dense requirements; replace `c` throughout that section by the threshold `p` and apply the defining `<p` pseudointersection property. The continuum-regularity argument Bell mentions later on p. 153 belongs to the Dowker-space section, not this implication.

**Verified primary-proof reconstruction, Bell pp. 150–152.** Write `F=ω^{<ω}` and `F_n=ω^n`. Throughout, `q≤p` means `q` is stronger. The following is the exact parameterized version of Bell Lemma 1.1 and Theorem 1.2, with `χ<p` in place of `χ<c`.

1. **The `BB(<p)` input (Bell Lemma 1.1, p. 150).** Given `{h_α:F→ω:α<χ}`, first replace each map by its maximum over the finitely many strings preceding its argument in a fixed order `≤₀` of type `ω`; then `s≤₀t ⇒ h_α(s)≤h_α(t)`. On the countable set `F×ω`, form the centered family consisting of `E_α={(s,n):h_α(s)≤n}` for each `α` and every order-tail `{t:s≤₀t}×ω`. Its size is `χ+ℵ₀<p`, so it has an infinite pseudointersection `B`. Every order tail contains all but finitely many points of `B`, forcing `B` to meet arbitrarily late fibres `{t}×ω`. Let `H(s)` be the first `t≥₀s` whose fibre meets `B`, and recursively let `f(n)` be the least `k` with `(H(f↾n),k)∈B`. For fixed `α`, only finitely many points of `B` miss `E_α`; the distinct prefixes `f↾n` have unbounded `≤₀` ranks, so the selected fibres `H(f↾n)≥₀f↾n` eventually avoid all those exceptions. Hence for all large `n`, `h_α(f↾n)≤h_α(H(f↾n))≤f(n)`. This proves the exact prefix-bounding principle needed below; ordinary eventual domination alone would not suffice.
2. **Small subposet and directedness (Bell p. 151).** For `χ<p` dense open `D_α`, close a set of `max(χ,ℵ₀)` conditions under chosen dense extensions and finite compatibility witnesses for countably many rounds. The resulting subposet has size `<p`, is still covered by countably many centered pieces, and preserves the required density and compatibility tests. Bell's pairwise-compatible-selector formulation adds, for each pair `a,b` of conditions, the dense set `D_{ab}={q:q is incompatible with a or b} ∪ {q:q≤a and q≤b}`. There are `<p` such tests. A selector meeting them is pairwise compatible and has common refinements sufficient to generate a directed filter meeting the original `D_α`. No step uses `cf(p)=p`: a countable closure of a fixed cardinal `<p` retains that cardinality.
3. **Centered index sets (Bell p. 151).** Write `P=⋃_{m<ω}P_m` with each `P_m` centered and nonempty; discard empty pieces, then repeat each remaining piece infinitely often. For `α<χ` and `q∈P`, set `B_α(q)={m<ω: (∃d∈D_α∩P_m) d≤q}` and `B_α={m:D_α∩P_m≠∅}`. For fixed `m`, the family `{B_α(q):α<χ,q∈P_m}` is centered: for finitely many `q_i∈P_m`, take a common stronger condition using centeredness, then successively strengthen into the finitely many open dense `D_{α_i}`; the resulting condition lies in some `P_j`, so `j` belongs to every requested `B_{α_i}(q_i)`. Repetition makes every nonempty finite intersection of these index sets infinite, giving the SFIP required by the definition of `p`. Apply the `<p` pseudointersection property to obtain an infinite `A_m⊆ω` with `A_m⊆*B_α(q)` for all `α,q∈P_m`; enumerate it increasingly as `a_m(r)` (`r<ω`). In particular, `A_0⊆*B_α` for all `α`.
4. **Exact `d_α` recursion (Bell pp. 151–152).** Define `g:F→ω` by `g(∅)=0` and `g(s⌢⟨r⟩)=a_{g(s)}(r)`. For each fixed `α<χ`, recursively define a threshold `t_α(s)∈ω` and conditions `d_α(s,r)` for **every** `s∈F,r∈ω`, maintaining (i) `d_α(s,r)∈P_{g(s⌢r)}` always, and (ii) for `r≥t_α(s)`, `d_α(s,r)∈D_α` and, if `s` is nonempty, `d_α(s,r)≤d_α(s↾(|s|-1),s(|s|-1))`. At `s=∅`, `g(⟨r⟩)=a_0(r)` lies in `B_α` for all sufficiently large `r`, giving (ii). For any `s≠∅`, the parent condition `q=d_α(s↾(|s|-1),s(|s|-1))` exists even if its own index was below its threshold, and by (i) lies in `P_{g(s)}`. Since `A_{g(s)}⊆*B_α(q)`, every sufficiently late `g(s⌢r)=a_{g(s)}(r)` admits a `d∈D_α∩P_{g(s⌢r)}` stronger than `q`. Choose it as `d_α(s,r)` above the new threshold; for finitely many smaller `r`, choose an arbitrary member of the nonempty block `P_{g(s⌢r)}` to preserve (i). This is Bell's full recursion invariant; defining `d_α` only above threshold would leave some child-parent cases undefined.
5. **One branch meets all dense sets compatibly (Bell p. 152).** Apply `BB(<p)` to `{t_α:α<χ}`. Obtain `f∈ω^ω` and, for each `α`, `N_α` such that `t_α(f↾n)≤f(n)` for all `n≥N_α`. Select `q_α=d_α(f↾N_α,f(N_α))∈D_α`. For `α,β` with `N_α≤N_β`, induction (ii) makes `d_α(f↾N_β,f(N_β))≤q_α`. Both this later `d_α` and `q_β` belong to the centered block `P_{g(f↾(N_β+1))}`, so they have a common stronger condition. Therefore `q_α,q_β` are compatible. The compatibility-test reduction in step 2 yields a directed filter meeting every original dense set.

**Audit status.** The primary BELL recursion and its `<p` localization are verified at scaffold level, including the all-indices `d_α` invariant, each index and order direction. A future authored item must spell out the small-subposet closure and the elementary compatibility-test argument in its own proof; this evidence draft does not itself create that item. Bell's p. 153 statement about regularity of the continuum is not used.

## Contract TOWER-BOUND: `t≤b`

**Exact statement.** In ZFC, a tower of cardinality at most the bounding number exists. Equivalently, if `b<t`, an unbounded family of size `b` leads to a contradiction. This is used in Shelah Lemma 1.5 and Proposition 1.11 and Malliaris–Shelah Claim 14.7.

**Dependencies.** Earlier definitions of `b`, `t`, almost inclusion and increasing enumeration of an infinite subset of `ω`; AC for a length-`b` recursion and witness choices. No category-ideal theorem or `p=t` is used.

**Complete direct reconstruction.** Suppose `b<t` and choose an unbounded family `{f_α:α<b}` of increasing functions, replacing each arbitrary function by an increasing envelope if necessary. By recursion on `α<b`, since the preceding almost-decreasing chain has length `<t`, choose an infinite pseudointersection `C_α` of it. Thin `C_α` to an infinite `A_α` whose increasing enumeration `e_{A_α}` satisfies `e_{A_α}(n)≥f_α(2n)` for every `n`. Then `{A_α:α<b}` is almost decreasing. If it had an infinite pseudointersection `B`, fix `α` and let `k_α=|B\setminus A_α|`. For `n≥2k_α`, order statistics give `e_B(n)≥e_{A_α}(n-k_α)≥f_α(2(n-k_α))≥f_α(n)`. Thus `e_B` eventually dominates every `f_α`, contradicting unboundedness. Therefore the chain is a tower of size at most `b`. The factor `2n` is essential: without it, the finite index shift from almost inclusion would not justify domination.

**Historical-source limit.** The proposition and Rothberger attribution are explicit in Malliaris–Shelah Fact 14.2 and Shelah p. 304; the original Rothberger scan was obtained but its exact historical notation-to-statement proof passage remains unidentified. The direct proof above closes the mathematical contract independently and should be attributed as a reconstruction, not quoted as Rothberger's written proof.

## Contract P-REG: `cf(p)=p`

**Exact statement.** The pseudointersection number is a regular uncountable cardinal. Its regularity is required because the right cofinality in Shelah's `(κ,p)` peculiar cut and the corresponding CSP cut must be regular. Monk Proposition 57 gives a full proof; Shelah p. 304 and Malliaris–Shelah Fact 14.2 cite the historical Szymański result.

**Dependencies.** `p>ℵ₀`, AC, the definition of `p`, and the finite-block preservation lemma below. The argument does not use `p=t` or Bell's theorem.

**Proof skeleton, Monk printed pp. 22–24.**

1. Proposition 56: if `A,C⊆[ω]^ω`, `|A|+|C|<p`, and every finite intersection from `C` meets every `Y∈A` infinitely, then `C` has an infinite pseudointersection `X` that still meets every `Y∈A` infinitely. Work on the countable set `[ω]^{<ω}`. For `C∈C` use the set of finite subsets of `C`; for `Y∈A,n∈ω` use finite subsets of `ω\setminus n` meeting `Y`. These sets have the SFIP by the hypothesis. A pseudointersection `I` exists because there are `<p` requirements; `X=⋃I` gives both conclusions. The union is infinite because an infinite family of distinct finite subsets cannot all lie in one finite set.
2. Suppose `p` singular and let `F⊆[ω]^ω` be an SFIP family of size `p` with no pseudointersection, closed under finite intersections. Write `F=⋃_{ξ<μ}B_ξ`, `μ=cf(p)<p`, with increasing `B_ξ` each of size `<p` and closed under finite intersections. If `C` has size `<p` and finite intersections from `C` meet all `Y∈F` infinitely, then for each `ξ` the SFIP family `C∪B_ξ` has a pseudointersection `Z_ξ`. Apply Proposition 56 to `C` and `{Z_ξ:ξ<μ}`; obtain `X` pseudo of `C` and meeting every `Z_ξ` infinitely. Since `Z_ξ⊆*Y` for each `Y∈B_ξ`, `X` meets all `Y∈F` infinitely. This is the preservation step that a pointwise pseudointersection choice alone cannot give.
3. Recursively build infinite `C_ξ` for `ξ≤μ`, descending modulo finite and meeting every `Y∈F` infinitely. At stage `ξ<μ`, apply the preservation step to the earlier `C_η` together with `B_ξ`. Finite intersections remain positive against each `Y∈F` because the latest earlier `C_η` is positive and the chain is almost decreasing. At the terminal stage `μ`, apply it to the earlier `C_η` **without** a nonexistent `B_μ`. The resulting `C_μ` is almost below every earlier `C_ξ`, hence almost below each `Y∈B_ξ` and therefore every `Y∈F`, contradicting the choice of `F`.

**Textual corrections and historical limit.** Monk's Proposition 57 writes `A_α` where its setup defines `B_α`, and at the terminal index its displayed recursion cannot literally include `B_μ`; the corrected terminal step above is needed. The full repaired proof is mathematically coherent. It is an independent authored exposition citing Blass 7.15, not the historical Szymański primary proof; that original passage remains unverified and should not be claimed read.

## Contract SHELAH: `p<t ⇒ (κ,p)` peculiar cut

**Exact statement.** If `p<t`, there is a regular `κ` with `ℵ₁≤κ<p` and sequences of functions giving a `(κ,p)` peculiar cut in `ω^ω` (Shelah Definition 1.10 and Theorem 1.12). The right side has cofinality `p`, which is regular by P-REG. Use Shelah's strict eventual `<*` convention and prove both no-interpolation clauses; a mere pre-cut is insufficient for CSP Claim 14.13.

**Dependency spine from the primary paper.** Proposition 1.3 (short decreasing obstruction) → Lemma 1.5 (refinement, spending `t≤b`) → Definition 1.6/Observation 1.7 (block approximation codes) → Lemma 1.8(1)–(3) (upper bounds, spending Bell for part 3) → Lemma 1.9 (length-`p` approximation sequence) → Definition 1.10, Proposition 1.11 and Proposition 2.1 → Theorem 1.12. Bell supplies the `<p` σ-centered filter, TOWER-BOUND supplies `t≤b`, and P-REG supplies regularity of the right cofinality. The published `p≤t` bound is also required.

**Source-faithful proof skeleton, Shelah printed pp. 304–311.**

1. If `p<t`, enumerate an SFIP family `B={B_α:α<p}` closed under finite intersections. Proposition 1.3 tries to build `A_α` descending modulo finite while every `A_α` meets each `B∈B` infinitely and successor `A_{α+1}⊆B_α`. Success through `p` would be a tower of length `p<t`; at the first failed limit stage `i<p`, a cofinal sequence of length regular `κ=cf(i)<p` gives `A_j (j<κ)` with the stated obstruction: any pseudointersection is almost disjoint from some `B∈B`.
2. Lemma 1.5 uses `t≤b` to dominate `<t` functions recording successive hits of `A_i∩B_n`, then forms a diagonal infinite `B*` almost contained in every `B_n`. The family `A_i∩B*` is almost decreasing by hypothesis; since its length is `<t`, it has a pseudointersection. Lemma 1.8(2) applies this to finite binary block codes. Lemma 1.8(3) defines a σ-centered poset of finite block assignments plus finite bookkeeping sets of code indices and `A_i` indices; its `<p` dense sets demand arbitrarily many blocks meeting each requirement. Bell's contract gives a directed filter and a code upper bound inside `S_A`.
3. Lemma 1.9 recursively builds codes `η_α` for `α≤p`. The `<p` limit steps use Lemma 1.8(3); the final stage `α=p<t` uses Lemma 1.8(2). At successors, a pseudointersection of the obstruction chain together with three relevant `B` sets allows block extensions satisfying membership and pairwise first-hit tests. The paper's details are in clauses (c)–(e) on p. 307.
4. On `A*=dom(η_p)`, define `f_i(n)` as the first position where the final code has a 1 outside `A_i` (or ends), and `f^α(n)` as one past the first 1 inside `B_α` (or the end), exactly as Theorem 1.12 pp. 308–309 states. The `f_i` are eventually nonincreasing; the obstruction from Proposition 1.3 forces an eventually strictly decreasing cofinal subsequence. Lemma 1.9's pair tests make the `f^α` eventually linearly ordered; take a strictly increasing cofinal subsequence of length `ε*≤p`.
5. The two no-interpolant arguments on printed pp. 310–311 turn a hypothetical middle function into an infinite set almost contained in every `A_i` yet meeting every `B_α` infinitely, contradicting Proposition 1.3(b). In the argument using an infinite `A**⊆A*`, its existence comes from the `<t` almost-decreasing family `{n∈A*:f(n)<f_i(n)}` indexed by `κ<t`; this should be written explicitly in a local proof.
6. If `ε*<p`, use the actual σ-centered forcing construction of Proposition 2.1 with dense requirements for all `κ` left-side and `ε*` right-side functions. Their total is `<p`; Bell yields a filter, and its union produces a function violating the two no-interpolant clauses. Proposition 2.1 is stated for `κ₁≤κ₂`, but its forcing proof gives this **specific short-both-sides contradiction** without needing `κ≤ε*`; do not quote the stated proposition outside its hypothesis. Thus `ε*=p`. Proposition 1.11 excludes `κ=ℵ₀` because `p<t≤b`, giving `ℵ₁≤κ<p`.

**Exact source ambiguities that must be resolved in authored text.** On printed p. 310 Shelah labels both `(⊛)5` and `(⊛)6` as Definition 1.10 clause (e); `(⊛)5` is the **lower-bound/no-interpolant clause (d)**, while `(⊛)6` is clause (e). Lemma 1.8(3)'s order clause writes `min(u^q\setminus u^p)` although a bookkeeping-only extension may add no coordinate; the local poset definition must treat an empty new-coordinate set as vacuous. The source describes the density of its `I_{α,i}^m` sets as “plain”; a local proof must verify the finite-code/finite-`A_i` compatibility before applying Bell. The short-right-side use of Proposition 2.1 should be proved from its construction as in step 6, since its printed theorem hypothesis `κ₁≤κ₂` need not be known for `ε*`. These are targeted authoring obligations, not grounds to reject the primary theorem.

### Detailed Shelah interface check

**Lemma 1.8(2), printed p. 306.** For a `<t` increasing code sequence `η_α`, choose `A*` almost contained in every domain `dom(η_α)`. Regard finite binary strings based at points of `A*` as one countable set. Let `B_m` consist of strings whose base point is at least `m`, and let `E_α` consist of strings extending some block of `η_α`. Each `E_α∩B_m` is infinite. If `α<β`, eventual extension of `η_α` by `η_β` gives **some** `m₀` with `E_β∩B_{m₀}⊆E_α∩B_{m₀}`; the finitely many exceptional base points each carry infinitely many extensions, so inclusion modulo finite at every `m` would be false. This is exactly Lemma 1.5(iv). Lemma 1.5 produces an infinite set `A` almost contained in every `E_α` and every `B_m`. Its base points are unbounded and form an infinite `A'`; from each base point choose one string in `A∩(B_m\B_{m+1})`. For each fixed `α`, all but finitely many chosen strings extend the corresponding `η_α` block. Their sequence is the required `≤*` upper bound in `S`; nonempty 1-support is required only for `S_A`, not for this lemma's conclusion. This explains the paper's “plainly” at the final selection.

**Lemma 1.8(3), printed pp. 306–307.** A condition is `(ν,u,w,a)`: finitely many nonempty-support binary blocks `ν_n` indexed by finite `u⊂ω`, finite `w⊂δ` listing code requirements, and finite `a⊂i*` listing almost-decreasing sets `A_i`. A proper extension adds at least one new base point beyond all old ones, keeps old blocks, and at each new point `n` **strictly** extends every `η_α,n` for `α∈w` while putting every new 1 in each `A_i` for `i∈a`. This makes the source's `min(u^q\u^p)` clause defined; reflexivity is the equality case. To extend, choose the largest code index in finite `w`. At all sufficiently large points of its domain it extends every earlier `η_α` block; because it lies in `S_A`, its 1-support is almost contained in every member of finite `a`. Append a zero to its nonempty block to make the extension strict without changing 1-support; this witnesses a legal new coordinate. If `w=∅`, choose a base point `n` and then a 1-position `k≥n` in the infinite finite intersection of the `A_i` in `a`, using the block `[n,k+1)`. Conditions with the same finite `(ν,u)` form a centered set: merge finitely many bookkeeping sets and add a new legal coordinate. There are countably many `(ν,u)`, so the poset is σ-centered. For each code index `α<δ`, set index `i<i*`, and `m<ω`, the dense requirement `I^m_{α,i}` is met by extending with enough legal new coordinates and then adding `α,i` to the bookkeeping; future extensions enforce them. More precisely, the code index is `α<δ`, while the set index is `i<i*`. The total number of such requirements is `|δ|+|i*|+ℵ₀<p`, so BELL applies. The directed union is an infinite block sequence in `S_A` above every `η_α`. This verifies the finite-compatibility assertion that Shelah calls “plain.”

**Lemma 1.9, printed p. 307.** At the zero stage use Lemma 1.8(1) to choose a code in `S_A`. At a limit `α<p`, the preceding sequence has cardinality `<p`, so Lemma 1.8(3) gives an upper bound still in `S_A`; this does not require regularity of `p`. At successor `α+1`, let `(β,γ)=pr^{-1}(α)`. Since `B` is closed under finite intersections and each `A_i∩B` is infinite, the family `{A_i:i<κ}∪{B_α∩B_β∩B_γ}` has the SFIP and size `<p`. Choose an infinite pseudointersection and intersect it with the three `B` sets to obtain `B'_α⊆B_α∩B_β∩B_γ` almost contained in each `A_i`. Extend each surviving `η_α,n` by a new 1 at a sufficiently large point of `B'_α`; all new 1s remain almost inside each `A_i`, so the result lies in `S_A`. There are only finitely many first-hit comparisons for clauses (d),(e), including substitutions of `A_β/A_γ` when the indices are below `κ`; thin the infinite domain until each comparison has constant truth value. Finally, at `α=p<t`, use Lemma 1.8(2) in the larger space `S` (the final code need not be in `S_A`). This supplies every induction endpoint, including the source's terse zero and final stages.

**The two cut clauses of Theorem 1.12, printed pp. 308–311.** For each `n∈A*=dom(η_p)`, let `f_i(n)` be the first 1-position outside `A_i` (or the block end), and let `f^α(n)` be one past the first 1-position in `B_α`. Eventual inclusion `A_j⊆*A_i` makes the `f_i` nonincreasing. The paper's Proposition 1.3(b) argument shows there are cofinally many strict drops. If for one `i*` no later `f_j<*f_{i*}`, the sets `E_j={n∈A*:f_j(n)≥f_{i*}(n)}` are infinite and almost decreasing; since `κ<t`, choose an infinite pseudointersection `E`. On `E`, eventually `f_j=f_{i*}` for each `j>i*`. The union of final-code 1-positions before offset `f_{i*}(n)` over `n∈E` is infinite (the initial `η_0∈S_A` supplies a 1 in each late block), almost contained in every `A_j`, and meets every `B_α` infinitely (the `η_{α+1}` stage inserts such a 1 before the first outside-`A_{i*}` point). This contradicts Proposition 1.3(b). Thin to a strictly decreasing left side. Lemma 1.9's pair tests make the `f^α` linearly ordered modulo finite; select a strictly increasing cofinal subsequence. For fixed `α,i`, the single `η_{α+1}` stage yields `f^α≤*f_i`; **strict** `f^α<*f_i` uses any later successor extension whose newly inserted 1 is still in `A_i` and occurs after the first `B_α` hit. This extra-stage argument must be stated locally.

For the lower-bound clause (Definition 1.10(d)), suppose `h≤*f_i` for every `i` but `h` is not `≤*f^α` for any selected right function. Let `X` be the union, over all final-code blocks, of 1-positions before offset `h(n)`. The inequalities `h≤*f_i` make `X⊆*A_i` for every `i`. For each `B_β`, choose a selected right function eventually above `f^β`; infinitely many `h` values exceed that selected function, so `X∩B_β` is infinite. This contradicts Proposition 1.3(b). For the upper-bound clause (Definition 1.10(e)), suppose every selected `f^α≤*h` but no `f_i≤*h`. For each `i`, `E_i={n∈A*:h(n)<f_i(n)}` is infinite, and the `E_i` form an almost-decreasing sequence because the `f_i` do. Since `κ<t`, choose an infinite pseudointersection `A**` of the `E_i`. Form the same union `X` of 1-positions before `h(n)`, now using only `n∈A**`. It is almost inside every `A_i`, while the cofinal right sequence and `f^α≤*h` make it meet every `B_β` infinitely. Again Proposition 1.3(b) is contradicted. Thus the paper's two labels “(e)” are a typo: its `(⊛)5` proves (d), and `(⊛)6` proves (e).
