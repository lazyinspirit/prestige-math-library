## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Supply a stable finite-type reference Riemann surface $X=\overline X\setminus P$, of genus $g$ with $n$ labeled punctures and $2g-2+n>0$, together with a torsion-free cofinite disk Fuchsian model, its holomorphic quotient cover, and standard labeled cusp compactifications on both sides. Supply the complete correspondence between punctures and maximal parabolic conjugacy classes with primitive peripheral generators. The case $n=0$ is included.

A marked surface is a pair $(Y,m)$, where $Y=\overline Y\setminus P_Y$ is a compact Riemann surface with the same labeled points removed, and $m:\overline X\to\overline Y$ is an orientation-preserving quasiconformal homeomorphism preserving those labels. Two pairs are equivalent if a label-preserving biholomorphism $a:Y\to Z$ has $a m$ homotopic to the other marking through maps of punctured surfaces extending jointly continuously to the compactifications with all puncture labels fixed. Write $\mathcal T(X)$ for these classes. For $x=[Y,m]$ and $y=[Z,n]$, define
$$d_T(x,y)=\frac12\inf_h\log K_h,$$
where $h:Y\to Z$ ranges over orientation-preserving quasiconformal homeomorphisms extending to the labeled compactifications and satisfying $h m\simeq n$ in this puncture-relative sense. Here $K_h=(1+\|\mu_h\|_\infty)/(1-\|\mu_h\|_\infty)$ is the least quasiconformal constant. This also gives the usual full infimum if one initially asks only for a puncture-preserving quasiconformal map of the punctured surfaces: each such map extends quasiconformally across the punctures.

Then $d_T$ is a finite complete metric. Identify $\mathcal T(X)$ with the actual marked coefficient quotient of the local Bers theorem at the supplied reference model, and equip it with that quotient topology. The metric topology equals this topology. Every marked surface in $\mathcal T(X)$ has a cofinite disk model with the full labeled standard cusp data. Repeating the local Bers construction at any such marked base gives a chart on an open neighborhood of that point, and all these charts have holomorphic transitions. They therefore make $\mathcal T(X)$ a Hausdorff complex manifold of dimension $3g-3+n$. This includes the zero-dimensional stable case.

The coefficient change between two fixed reference surfaces is explicit. If $k:Y_a\to Y_b$ is their fixed quasiconformal change of marking with coefficient $\mu_0$, then the coefficient $R_k(\mu)$ on $Y_b$ is given in conformal coordinates, almost everywhere, by
$$R_k(\mu)(k(z))=\frac{k_z(z)}{\overline{k_z(z)}}\frac{\mu(z)-\mu_0(z)}{1-\overline{\mu_0(z)}\mu(z)}.$$
This is a biholomorphic map between the full open unit balls of bounded Beltrami sections. It induces the change of actual marked quotients. At $\mu_0$ its derivative is
$$D R_k(\mu_0)[\eta](k(z))=\frac{k_z(z)}{\overline{k_z(z)}}\frac{\eta(z)}{1-|\mu_0(z)|^2}.$$
For local sections $\sigma_a$ of the Bers maps $\beta_a$, the chart transition is $\beta_b\circ R_k\circ\sigma_a$ wherever the two charts overlap. Consequently the corresponding coefficient differential kernels are carried to one another by $D R_k$ and the chain rule. No assertion about extremal maps or a tangent norm is needed here.

[F3] Bounded measurable sphere coefficients of norm less than one have quasiconformal solutions. ## Proof

1.1 First, isolated marked points do not restrict the infimum. A puncture-preserving homeomorphism of the punctured compact surfaces extends homeomorphically over their finitely many ends: the inverse image of a compact core is compact, so each sufficiently small connected end neighborhood maps into a single prescribed end neighborhood, and the same holds for its inverse. If it is quasiconformal, extend its local bounded coefficient across the one missing point arbitrarily, cut it off outside a coordinate disc, and solve it there using [F3]. Composition with this local solution's inverse cancels its coefficient away from the center. The resulting punctured-disc biholomorphism has a continuous finite limit at the center in the target puncture coordinate. It extends holomorphically: its negative Laurent coefficients are bounded by $M r^j$ and vanish as $r\to0$. Apply the same argument to its inverse. Both extensions compose to identity on a punctured neighborhood and hence at the center by continuity. Thus it is a local biholomorphism across the center. Composing back with the quasiconformal local solution gives the claimed quasiconformal extension. This reasoning also shows that every label-preserving biholomorphism used in the equivalence extends biholomorphically to the compactifications. [F3, F4, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 For precision, bounded Beltrami sections on a fixed compact reference surface form a complex Banach space: in conformal charts the coordinate changes multiply the coefficient by a smooth modulus-one factor. A finite cover gives its essential norm and completeness by taking compatible chartwise $L^\infty$ limits. Explicitly, from an essential-norm Cauchy sequence take a subsequence with successive differences bounded by $2^{-j}$ outside a common null set in each of the finitely many charts; the representatives converge uniformly off those null sets to bounded compatible sections. The Cauchy property then gives essential-norm convergence of the whole sequence. Values at finitely many punctures are immaterial. Pullback under a supplied holomorphic disk cover is a complex-linear isometric identification with the invariant coefficient space: sheet charts give both directions, and a countable collection of sheet charts transports null sets. That cover is countable because the disk has a countable base and its fibers are discrete. By [F1], the coefficient quotient at $X$ is bijective with the actual classes just defined, not merely with unmarked targets. Every admissible marked biholomorphism or marked homotopy in one description is the same one in the other. This identifies the topology specified in the Statement with the local Bers quotient topology. [F1, A1, given, construct, algebra]

1.3 We supply the local-to-global integration needed for completeness. Let $\lambda$ be a bounded Beltrami section on a compact Riemann surface $\overline Y$ with $\|\lambda\|_\infty\le\kappa<1$. Choose finitely many coordinate discs $U_i$ and smaller discs $V_i$ with closures in $U_i$, covering the compact surface. In the $i$th coordinate extend the coefficient on $U_i$ by zero outside that coordinate domain; its norm is still at most $\kappa$. By [F3] integrate it on the sphere, obtaining a quasiconformal homeomorphism $u_i$. On $V_i$ use $u_i\circ z_i$ as the new coordinate, with the original underlying topology. On an overlap both coordinate maps have the same coefficient $\lambda$ in the old source coordinate. Their transition and its inverse are conformal by coefficient cancellation in [F3]. They form a Riemann surface atlas on the original compact topological surface. The identity map $F_\lambda$ from the old surface to the new one is quasiconformal with coefficient $\lambda$ and constant at most $(1+\kappa)/(1-\kappa)$, directly in these coordinate charts. The labeled points are the same underlying points. This constructs a compact marked limit surface, not just a structure on the punctured part, and requires no global surface uniformization theorem. [F3, F4, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The equivalence on marked pairs is an equivalence relation: use identity and the constant homotopy, reverse the homotopy and postcompose by the inverse biholomorphism for symmetry, and concatenate homotopies after conformal postcomposition for transitivity. These operations retain joint continuity at every labeled point. Admissible maps exist, since $n m^{-1}:Y\to Z$ is quasiconformal by [F3] and has the exact required marking. If equivalent representatives are substituted, pre- and postcomposing $h$ with their biholomorphisms gives a bijection of the admissible map classes and preserves $K_h$ by the coefficient formula. Thus $d_T$ is well-defined and finite on classes. It is nonnegative, vanishes on the diagonal, and is symmetric by admissible inversion and $K_{h^{-1}}=K_h$. Given admissible maps for $x\to y$ and $y\to z$, their composition is admissible and its log dilatation is at most the sum. Choosing each map within any positive error of its respective infimum and letting that error tend to zero proves the triangle inequality. This establishes a finite pseudometric without assuming infima are attained. [F3, F4, step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

2.2 We prove the coefficient rebasing formula and its full topological meaning. Fix any quasiconformal change of reference $k:Y_a\to Y_b$ with coefficient $\mu_0$ and let $f$ be a marked target map from $Y_a$ with coefficient $\mu$. By [F3], solving the composition formula for the coefficient of $f k^{-1}$ gives the displayed expression $R_k(\mu)$. The factors are defined almost everywhere because $k_z\ne0$ almost everywhere by the null-set transport in [F3]. Coordinate changes on source and target have the correct Beltrami phase, so the formula defines a section on $Y_b$. Pullback by $k$ identifies essentially bounded functions modulo null sets isometrically, by the null-set properties of $k$ and $k^{-1}$. Multiplication by its fixed phase is also an isometry. Pointwise disk automorphisms give $|R_k(\mu)|\le(\|\mu\|_\infty+\|\mu_0\|_\infty)/(1+\|\mu\|_\infty\|\mu_0\|_\infty)<1$. Thus $R_k$ maps the whole unit ball to the whole target coefficient ball. Its inverse is $R_{k^{-1}}$, since these are the coefficients of the successive reference changes; algebraically, with $\alpha=(\overline{k_z}/k_z)(\nu\circ k)$, it is $\mu=(\mu_0+\alpha)/(1+\overline{\mu_0}\alpha)$. Neumann expansion of the bounded multiplier $(1-\overline{\mu_0}\mu)^{-1}$, locally uniformly on coefficient subballs, proves complex Fréchet holomorphy. The same argument proves holomorphy of the inverse. Differentiation gives $D R_k(\mu)[\eta]\circ k=(k_z/\overline{k_z})(1-|\mu_0|^2)\eta/(1-\overline{\mu_0}\mu)^2$, and in particular the asserted formula at $\mu_0$. [F3, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 Every point $x=[Y,m]$ admits the full model needed to apply [F1] again. Apply [F2] to $m:X\to Y$. The model has all labeled parabolic cusps and compact complement, and the primitive generators and labels are unchanged. Reflection $z\mapsto1/\overline z$ gives its other disk quotient and conjugate standard cusp coordinates; the hyperbolic length element becomes $|d\zeta|/(|\zeta|\log(1/|\zeta|))$. Thus [F1] applies with $Y$ as new reference. Changing the marking of a target from $r:X\to Z$ to $r m^{-1}:Y\to Z$ gives a bijection of actual marked classes in both directions. Composition with $m$ or $m^{-1}$ transports each puncture-relative homotopy, so the bijection respects exactly our equivalence. It leaves the admissible maps between targets unchanged and is a pseudometric isometry. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.2 We give a useful metric estimate without assuming separation. For coefficients $\alpha,\beta$ on one reference, their target models have the admissible map $f^\beta(f^\alpha)^{-1}$. Its coefficient, pulled back by $f^\alpha$, has modulus $|\beta-\alpha|/|1-\overline\alpha\beta|$ by [F3]. Consequently, if $\|\alpha\|_\infty,\|\beta\|_\infty\le\kappa<1$ and $e=\|\beta-\alpha\|_\infty/(1-\kappa^2)<1$, then $d_T([\alpha],[\beta])\le\operatorname{arctanh} e$. At the zero coefficient the sharper estimate is $d_T([0],[\alpha])\le\operatorname{arctanh}\|\alpha\|_\infty$. Thus the coefficient projection is continuous into the pseudometric topology on every coefficient ball, and a holomorphic local section is continuous into that topology. [F3, F4, step 2.1, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.3 Let $(x_j)$ be a $d_T$-Cauchy sequence. Choose a subsequence $(x_{j_r})$ with $d_T(x_{j_r},x_{j_{r+1}})<2^{-r-3}$ for $r\ge1$. Choose representatives $(Y_r,m_r)$ of these classes and admissible quasiconformal maps $\phi_r:Y_r\to Y_{r+1}$ with $\tfrac12\log K_{\phi_r}<2^{-r-2}$, using the infimum and [A1]. Put $F_1=\mathrm{id}_{Y_1}$ and $F_r=\phi_{r-1}\cdots\phi_1:Y_1\to Y_r$. They extend to compact labeled surfaces. Induction using the admissible homotopies gives $F_r m_1\simeq m_r$ puncture-relatively, so they represent exactly the chosen subsequence. Let $S=\sum_{r\ge1}\log K_{\phi_r}<\infty$. Then $K_{F_r}\le e^S$, hence their Beltrami sections $\lambda_r$ on $\overline Y_1$ satisfy $\|\lambda_r\|_\infty\le\kappa=(e^S-1)/(e^S+1)<1$. These sections are compared on one fixed compact reference, so no varying-target coordinate norm has entered the estimate. [A1, F3, F4, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Every coefficient in step 2.2 is realized by [F1] at its corresponding reference, by step 3.1. The maps $f$ and $f k^{-1}$ mark the same target after the change of reference. Hence $R_k$ takes the exact marked fibers onto the exact rebased marked fibers, in both directions. The commuting diagrams with the quotient projections therefore induce mutually inverse continuous quotient maps: their compositions with the quotient projections are continuous by step 2.2, which is precisely the quotient-topology criterion. They are homeomorphisms. Thus the coefficient quotient topology is independent of the chosen marked cofinite reference, and all local Bers charts based at arbitrary marked points are charts on this same topological space. [F1, step 3.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

4.2 The pseudometric separates actual marked classes. Rebase at $x=[Y,m]$ using step 3.1, so $x$ is the zero class. If $d_T(x,y)=0$, choose admissible maps $h_j:Y\to Z$ representing the fixed class $y$ with $\tfrac12\log K_{h_j}\to0$. Their coefficients $\alpha_j$ on $Y$ tend to zero in essential norm by [F4]. Every $\alpha_j$ represents $y$, by admissibility and [F1]'s actual fiber description. Hence $\beta_Y(\alpha_j)$ is one fixed vector, independent of $j$. Holomorphy and continuity at zero imply that this fixed vector is $\beta_Y(0)=0$. The full-ball fiber theorem [F1] says that $y=x$. Thus $d_T$ is a metric, using neither an extremal map nor a compactness theorem for minimizing maps. [A1, F1, F4, step 3.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra]

4.3 The coefficient sequence in step 3.3 is Cauchy in essential norm. Indeed the composition formula gives, almost everywhere on $Y_1$, $\frac{|\lambda_{r+1}-\lambda_r|}{|1-\overline{\lambda_r}\lambda_{r+1}|}=|\mu_{\phi_r}\circ F_r|\le k_r,\qquad k_r=\tanh\bigl(\tfrac12\log K_{\phi_r}\bigr)$. The denominator on the left is at most $2$, so $\|\lambda_{r+1}-\lambda_r\|_\infty\le2k_r\le\log K_{\phi_r}$. The right sides are summable. Completeness of the section space in step 1.2 gives a limit $\lambda_\infty$ with $\|\lambda_\infty\|_\infty\le\kappa<1$. At the finitely many marked points choose arbitrary values. The bound and all compatibility laws are preserved by essential-norm convergence. [F3, F4, step 1.2, step 3.3, algebra]

5.1 The metric topology equals the quotient topology. First the coefficient projections are metric-continuous by step 3.2. Therefore each metric-open set has open preimage under a quotient projection and is quotient-open. Conversely fix any point $x$ and a quotient-open neighborhood $O$. Rebase at $x$ by step 4.1. Its local Bers chart identifies a neighborhood with an open $W\subset Q(Y^-)$, taking $x$ to zero. There is an open neighborhood $V$ of zero in $W$ whose inverse image is contained in $O$. By continuity of $\beta_Y$ at zero choose $0<\epsilon<1$ so that $\|\alpha\|_\infty<\epsilon$ implies $\beta_Y(\alpha)\in V$. If $d_T(x,y)<\operatorname{arctanh}\epsilon$, the definition of the infimum gives an admissible map from the base representative of $x$ to a representative of $y$ with coefficient norm less than $\epsilon$. By the full fiber theorem it represents $y$, so $y$ belongs to the inverse chart image of $V$, hence to $O$. Thus a metric ball about each point of $O$ lies in $O$. This proves the reverse inclusion of topologies and the claimed agreement at every base point. [F1, F4, step 4.1, step 3.2, step 4.2, construct, algebra]

5.2 Apply step 1.3 on $\overline Y_1$ to obtain a compact Riemann surface $\overline Y_\infty$ and a quasiconformal marking map $F_\infty$ with coefficient $\lambda_\infty$. Remove the same labeled points and mark the result by $m_\infty=F_\infty m_1$. This is an object of $\mathcal T(X)$. The maps $G_r=F_r F_\infty^{-1}:Y_\infty\to Y_r$ are admissible because $G_r m_\infty=F_r m_1\simeq m_r$. Their coefficients, pulled back by $F_\infty$, have modulus at most $\|\lambda_r-\lambda_\infty\|_\infty/(1-\kappa^2)\to0$, by the inverse-composition formula. Therefore $d_T([Y_\infty,m_\infty],x_{j_r})\le\tfrac12\log K_{G_r}\to0$. The triangle inequality and the Cauchy property give convergence of the whole original sequence to the same point. This proves metric completeness, including all marked punctures. [F3, F4, step 1.3, step 3.3, step 4.3, construct, algebra]

6.1 Finally we prove holomorphic chart compatibility. Let $x_a=[Y_a,m_a]$ and $x_b=[Y_b,m_b]$ be fixed bases with their cofinite models and their local Bers data $(\beta_a,\sigma_a,W_a)$ and $(\beta_b,\sigma_b,W_b)$. Put $k=m_b m_a^{-1}:Y_a\to Y_b$. A point with coordinate $q\in W_a$ is represented by the marking of the target associated with $\sigma_a(q)$. Changing the reference to $Y_b$ replaces that marking by its composition with $k^{-1}$, so its coefficient is $R_k(\sigma_a(q))$. Thus on the open overlap the other coordinate is exactly $\beta_b R_k\sigma_a(q)$. Each factor is a holomorphic Banach-space map by [F1] and step 2.2, and hence the transition is holomorphic. Swapping the two bases proves its inverse holomorphic. Metric separation in step 4.2 makes the space Hausdorff; its charts cover every point by steps 3.1 and 4.1, and their topology is the metric topology by step 5.1. Their common dimension is $3g-3+n$ by [F1]. These facts give a Hausdorff locally finite-dimensional holomorphic atlas; second countability is proved next. In the zero-dimensional case all coordinate spaces and transitions are the unique zero-dimensional ones. [F1, step 3.1, step 2.2, step 4.1, step 4.2, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 We verify the global countability condition, rather than making a convention about manifolds. The coefficient ball is convex, and its projection is continuous by step 3.2 and surjective by step 1.2. Thus the metric space is path connected, using projected straight coefficient segments. It is locally compact by its charts and [F5]: the inverse image of a sufficiently small closed coordinate ball is compact and contains an open neighborhood of its center. Hence every point has a compact closed metric ball of positive radius. Let $s(x)=\sup\{r\in(0,1]:\overline B(x,r)\text{ is compact}\}$ and put $r(x)=s(x)/10$. This is positive, and $s$ is $1$-Lipschitz: if a closed ball at $x$ of radius $a$ is compact and $d(x,y)<a$, every closed ball at $y$ of radius less than $a-d(x,y)$ is a closed subset of that compact ball. Taking suprema gives $s(y)\ge s(x)-d(x,y)$ and the reverse inequality follows by symmetry. Every closed ball of radius strictly less than $s(x)$ is compact by this definition and [F5]. Start with a compact closed ball $K_0$ with nonempty interior. Given compact $K_j$, take a finite covering by balls $B(x_i,r(x_i))$ centered in $K_j$, and let $K_{j+1}$ be the union of the closed balls $\overline B(x_i,2r(x_i))$. They are compact and $K_j\subset\operatorname{int}K_{j+1}$. The union $V=\bigcup_jK_j$ is open. It is closed too: if $x\in\overline V$, choose $y\in K_j$ with $\delta=d(x,y)<r(x)/4$ and a covering center $x_i$ with $d(y,x_i)<r(x_i)$. The Lipschitz bound gives $r(x)\le r(x_i)+(r(x_i)+\delta)/10$, hence $r(x_i)>\delta$ and $d(x,x_i)<2r(x_i)$. Thus $x\in K_{j+1}\subset V$. Connectedness implies $V=\mathcal T(X)$. Each compact metric $K_j$ has a finite $1/l$-net for every positive integer $l$, by its ball-cover compactness. AC chooses these countably many finite nets, whose union is countable and dense in $V$. Balls of positive rational radius centered on this dense set form a countable base: inside any prescribed open neighborhood choose such a center sufficiently close and such a radius smaller than the distance to the neighborhood's complement. The space is therefore second countable. Together with step 6.1 this proves the complex-manifold assertion with the usual countability axiom. The same argument covers dimension zero; alternatively a connected discrete space is a single point. [A1, F5, step 1.2, step 3.2, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 The differential compatibility also has its exact local meaning. On the open coefficient preimage of an overlap, the fiber theorem gives $\beta_b R_k=\Psi_{ba}\beta_a$, where $\Psi_{ba}$ is the transition just proved. Differentiate this identity at any coefficient in that open set. Since $D\Psi_{ba}$ is invertible, it follows that $D R_k$ carries $\ker D\beta_a$ bijectively onto $\ker D\beta_b$ at the corresponding coefficient. The explicit derivative of step 2.2 specializes at $\mu_0$ to the formula in the Statement. All proofs used actual puncture-relative marked equivalence and full coefficient balls; metric completeness and topology were established before the holomorphic atlas was declared. No extremal representative, pre-existing Teichmüller manifold, global Bers theorem or tangent-norm formula has been assumed. [step 2.2, step 6.1, step 7.1, algebra] ∎

## Actual reading and precise scope

The complete current local Bers theorem was authored and checked before this proof. The full seven-step cofinite Fuchsian-model supplier, the actual sphere mapping theorem's Statement, Facts and entire proof, the full quasiconformal composition/inverse proof and the actual maximal-dilatation definition were read. The actual Heine–Borel, closed-compact-subspace and continuous-compact-image supplier proofs were read in full for the global countability argument. The Laurent supplier was read in full during the Bers development. Kapovich Sections 5.4 and 7.1 were read in full from the primary cached PDF via OCR during that development; no book completeness theorem is used as a premise here.

The limit Riemann surface is constructed explicitly from finitely many local measurable-mapping charts on the compact reference. The proof establishes the complete metric, equality with every rebased Bers quotient topology, and a Hausdorff holomorphic finite-dimensional atlas. It also proves second countability by an explicit compact exhaustion. It does not establish extremal-map existence, a tangent norm formula, or any skinning or hyperbolization conclusion.
