## 1. Path polynomials and invariant generators

Define P_ij;x^(r) by summing paths of r Kazhdan degree as in the actual source9.5: factors have col(start)≤col(end); consecutive factors share the intervening row; the intervening row≤x contributes a minus sign and requires col(end)≥col(next start), while row>x contributes a plus sign and requires the strict reverse inequality. Start/end rows are i,j. These auxiliary P's are not all W elements. Cutting a path at its first/last visit to a changed sign interval gives Gauss factorization identities: the nonexceptional cuts occur once, and the exceptional cuts pair with opposite signs by moving the cut to its first/last sign-interval visit. This is the explicit sign-cut involution in source9.2. Therefore the appropriate P_ii;i−1, P_i,i+1;i and P_i+1,i;i give the D,E,F Gauss generators; no arbitrary P_ij;0 is silently identified with a shifted-Yangian T generator.

Remove the rightmost column of height t and use the source10.1–10.3 recursions. ## 2. Complete PBW and the quotient, with an explicit independence calculation

Projection to the column Levi is an algebra map on U(p). On the graded path polynomials it gives the Gauss decomposition of product_c(I+z X_c), with X_c supported on the rows present in column c. This full product need not satisfy unshifted RTT; only its allowed shifted Gauss coefficients are used. Their algebra relations follow by the right/left baby recursions: substitute the displayed D/E/F corrections into the finite parabolic relations, use the tensor-factor commutation and [E_ij,E_hk]=delta_jh E_ik−delta_ki E_hj, and group terms by the evaluation-factor degree0,1,2. The degree0 group is the smaller shifted relation; the degree1 group is its D-weight identity; the degree2 group is the displayed matrix-unit bracket. Adjacent triple relations reduce to these groups and Jacobi, as in the actually read BK parabolic2×2/3×3 proof. This gives a local coefficient verification, not an imported PBW theorem.

The quotient by D_1^(r), r>p_1, is spanned by the finite allowed ordered Gauss monomials. The source6.5–6.6 formulas express each higher E/F through the preceding D and lowest E/F, then each higher D through their commutator; induction on row block and mode gives the ranges s_ij<r≤s_ij+p_min(i,j). In particular higher D_i are polynomial expressions, not all zero.

For independence and generation in W, specialize X_c to pairwise distinct scalar matrices h_c I on its column block and compute the Jacobian of the allowed Gauss coefficients. If p_i=m≤p_j=M in a left-justified pyramid, the off-diagonal derivative for the cth common column is z product_(h≤M,h≠c)(1+h_h z). Dividing by the diagonal factor Q_i gives

dE_ij(z)=z product_(h=m+1)^M(1+h_h z)/(1+h_c z),
dF_ji(z)=z/(1+h_c z).

For E the allowed modes M−m+1,…,M have coefficient matrix (-h_c)^(k−1) product_(h=m+1)^M(h_h−h_c), 1≤k,c≤m. For F the allowed modes1,…,m give (-h_c)^(k−1). These are Vandermonde matrices with nonzero column factors. Diagonal D modes give the elementary-symmetric Jacobian on its p_i columns, also Vandermonde. Each ordered row pair uses its own matrix-entry variables, so the complete Jacobian is block diagonal and invertible. For the mirror, interchange E/F and the extra left/right columns. The same calculation applies. Thus all allowed graded generator images are algebraically independent, by the minimal-polynomial-relation/differentiation argument in the companion RTT proof.

The companion GG proof gives gr_K W as a polynomial slice algebra with precisely these degrees (the homogeneous centralizer basis has s_ij<r≤s_ij+p_min). ## 3. Genuine right model and head/tail shift

Reflect the pyramid and transpose matrix units with its column-renumbering permutation. This is an anti-isomorphism of U(g). Reversing each path preserves its degree, sign and cut inequalities and sends P_ij;x to P'_ji;x term by term. The shift matrix is transposed. The preceding reflected generator theorem therefore identifies the reflected W and the transpose images of the original generators. Applying this anti-isomorphism to its left reduction condition turns the left ideal into the right ideal and its tail shift into the original head shift. Consequently the SAME original W subalgebra equals {u:[eta_head(u),m]⊂I_chi U(g)}. Numerically eta_head−eta_tail=−tr_m ad on each diagonal matrix; this equality is explained by the column counts but is not used as a substitute for the path-transpose proof.

## 4. Free/cofree pairing and the tensor matrices

The GG product decomposition gives right W-free Q with lifts of the homogeneous exponential coordinates on M=exp(m). Choose projection p:Q→W onto the coordinate-constant summand. The map Q⊗_W L→E_L, q⊗l↦(u↦p(uq)l), is an m-module isomorphism. Its highest-filtration map is the perfect Taylor pairing of polynomial exponential coordinates and PBW distributions at1. These pairings are perfect in every finite weighted order; all weights are positive, so finite jet order and weighted order are cofinal. Subtracting the highest term successively proves both surjectivity and injectivity of the actual filtered map, including finite-generator filtrations on L and then filtered unions. This replaces the Skryabin free/cofree assertion used in source8.1.

For a finite g-module X with coefficients c_ij(u), evaluation at1 identifies Wh(E⊗X) with X: its inverse is the function u↦c(u*)v, where x*=chi(x)−x. For a sufficiently large power of I_chi, its star image is a product of nilpotent m operators on X, so the function is continuous. Therefore the unique matrix X_ij in U(p) satisfying p(X_ij 1_chi)=delta_ij and the left chi-equations gives Wh((Q⊗L)⊗X)=L⊗X. In an Engel basis it is unitriangular, hence invertible; Y=X^−1 gives the inverse of the natural p-module tensor map.
