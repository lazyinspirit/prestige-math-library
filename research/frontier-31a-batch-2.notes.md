## Fixed conventions, decisions, and proof routes

- The published `def-absolutely-continuous-function` is authoritative: for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $\delta>0$ so every finite family of subintervals with pairwise disjoint open interiors and total length below $\delta$ has total absolute endpoint increment below $\varepsilon$.  Degenerate intervals are included.  `rem-absolute-continuity-conventions-and-hierarchy-agreement` quotes this exact formulation and cites `thm-c1-lipschitz-ac-bv-hierarchy`; it does not redefine AC.
- Property (N) always concerns **images**: $\lambda(E)=0\Rightarrow\lambda(F(E))=0$, never preimages.  All main statements are real-valued compact-interval statements; endpoint derivatives are irrelevant to each a.e. assertion.
- The sharp FTC is deliberately divided into the $L^1$-indefinite-integral AC corollary, an AC-derivative-integrability theorem, zero-derivative uniqueness through Vitali, and the iff theorem.  Its proof combines those results and the already-published a.e.-differentiation theorem, rather than duplicating it.
- Banach--Zarecki's hard direction is planned as: property (N) plus the Growth-Lemma estimate on the differentiability set, then the null exceptional set, then the absolute-continuity criterion for an indefinite integral.  This discharges `rem-banach-zarecki`.
- Integration by parts uses the product-AC lemma, the sharp FTC, and the designated Fubini route.  The increasing change-of-variables theorem is separate from the nonmonotone theorem; the latter retains the hypothesis that the relevant primitive composed with $g$ is AC.  This avoids the known false unrestricted statement.
- The three composition items are distinct: $\mathrm{Lip}\circ AC$ is AC; $AC\circ\mathrm{Lip}$ is AC when the inner Lipschitz map is monotone; two arbitrary AC functions need not have AC composition.  No B-page witness is a dependency of an A item.
- The HK/Lebesgue item is this page's one seam with `the-gauge-integral-and-cousins-lemma`: on a compact interval it proves $f\in L^1$ iff both $f$ and $|f|$ are HK integrable, with equal values for $f$; hence a nonnegative HK-integrable function is Lebesgue integrable, while HK is strictly non-absolute.  The noncompact wording must use the published compact-truncation convention when it is authored.  It does not re-mint gauges, Cousin's lemma, or the unconditional HK FTC.
- Design repair: its final proposed false statement, “a differentiable function with bounded derivative on $[a,b]$ need not be AC,” is false for everywhere differentiability.  `cor-bounded-derivative-implies-lipschitz` and the published hierarchy prove the converse.  `rem-bounded-derivative-design-correction` records that correction; the genuine a.e.-differentiability trap remains `fs-continuity-almost-everywhere-differentiability-and-an-integrable-derivative-imply-newton-leibniz`.
