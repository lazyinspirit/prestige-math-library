## Conventions and dependency rationale

- An attachment is the adjunction-space pushout (X\cup_fD^n); for (n=0),
  (S^{-1}=\varnothing). The image of the disk is a closed cell, and only its
  interior is asserted to embed as an open cell. This prevents accidentally
  assuming a regular CW complex.
- A CW complex explicitly carries closure finiteness and the weak topology.
  Skeleta are the unions of cells of dimension at most (n), and all
  compact-domain arguments pass through the finite subcomplex generated by the
  finite cell support.
- Cellular chains use (C_n^{\mathrm{cell}}(X;G)=H_n(X^n,X^{n-1};G)). An
  orientation of each characteristic disk identifies its summand with the
  coefficient group. The boundary is the connecting map for three consecutive
  skeleta followed by the quotient map. An integral incidence number is defined
  directly from the induced endomorphism of oriented reduced sphere homology,
  including reduced (H_0(S^0;\mathbb Z)); it does not depend on the general
  sphere-degree definition planned for the next A page.
- The comparison theorem first establishes concentration of the consecutive
  relative groups, stabilization away from the attaching dimension, and the
  finite-support colimit lemma. The latter is mandatory for an infinite CW
  complex: neither the proof nor any calculation assumes finite dimension.
- Euler characteristic is restricted to finite CW complexes. Its product proof
  uses product cells and finite double sums, not the later Kunneth theorem.
