## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $(\Sigma,j)$ be a closed Riemann surface with positive area form $\eta$, and put $X=\operatorname{Sym}^m(\Sigma)$, $m\ge1$, with its induced complex structure $J_0$. Let $q:\Sigma^m\to X$ be the quotient, $\Delta\subset X$ its repeated-point locus, and $\Omega=\sum_{l=1}^m\operatorname{pr}_l^*\eta$. Off $\Delta$ the permutation-invariant form $\Omega$ descends to a smooth form $\omega$ on $X\setminus\Delta$. Equip $X$ with any smooth Riemannian metric $g$.

Let $L_\alpha,L_\beta$ be product tori formed from two finite systems of pairwise disjoint attaching curves, with transverse intersections between the systems. Suppose a smooth path $J_t$ agrees with $J_0$ on a neighborhood $V$ of $\Delta$ and tames $\omega$ on $X\setminus V$. Let $u:\mathbb R\times[0,1]\to X$ be a smooth $J_t$-holomorphic strip, with boundary on these tori, finite $g$-Dirichlet energy, and uniform limits at both ends to intersection generators. For each complementary region $R$ of the attaching curves write $n_R(u)$ for the signed intersection multiplicity of $u$ with $\{p\}+\operatorname{Sym}^{m-1}(\Sigma)$ at a generic $p\in R$.

There is a constant $C$ depending only on the fixed geometric data and the path, independent of $u$, such that
$$E_g(u)=\frac12\int_{\mathbb R\times[0,1]}(|u_s|_g^2+|u_t|_g^2)\,dsdt\le C\sum_R n_R(u)\int_R\eta.$$
In particular energies of all such representatives with one fixed domain are uniformly bounded. The finite-energy hypothesis here is explicit; no assertion that arbitrary asymptotically convergent smooth solutions automatically have finite energy is included.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice and the geometric data, path, and finite-energy strip in the statement.

[F1] A nonzero scalar holomorphic germ has isolated zeros and a finite-order factorization, by [[thm-zero-order-factorization-holomorphic-function]]. Simple roots vary holomorphically by [[thm-holomorphic-implicit-function-theorem]], and bounded holomorphic functions on a punctured disk extend over its center by [[thm-removable-singularity-characterizations]].

[F2] For a proper smooth map of connected oriented surfaces without boundary, integrating a compactly supported target two-form multiplies its integral by the degree, and the degree is the finite signed count over any supplied regular value, by [[lem-degree-is-well-defined-and-independent-of-the-normalized-top-form]] and [[thm-regular-value-formula-for-compact-support-degree]]. Generic regular values exist by applying [[thm-morse-sard-for-euclidean-maps]] in countably many charts; Countable Choice suffices for the countable null unions used here.

## Proof

1.1 The tori and their generators avoid $\Delta$: distinct coordinates of a tuple on a given product torus lie on distinct disjoint attaching curves. The set $u^{-1}(\Delta)$ is a finite set of interior points. Indeed, in local symmetric-function coordinates the repeated-root condition is the vanishing of the polynomial discriminant, a holomorphic defining function, possibly with singular zero set. Near each inverse image of $\Delta$, the equation is the ordinary $J_0$ holomorphic equation because $J_t=J_0$ on $V$. Its discriminant composition is therefore holomorphic. By [F1], its zeros are isolated unless it vanishes on a neighborhood. The set of strip-interior points having a neighborhood mapped into $\Delta$ is open and closed: at a limit point a local discriminant function and [F1] force local vanishing. Connectedness and the boundary condition exclude that possibility. Thus all inverse images are isolated. Uniform end limits exclude sufficiently long strip ends, and compactness of the remaining boundary images excludes a neighborhood of the two strip edges. A closed isolated subset of the resulting compact interior rectangle is finite. [F1, given, construct]

2.1 Over the complement of these finitely many points, pull back the $d=m!$ sheeted covering $q:\Sigma^m\setminus q^{-1}(\Delta)\to X\setminus\Delta$. This gives $p:F^\circ\to\mathbb R\times[0,1]$ and a smooth ordered-tuple map $\widetilde u$ with $q\widetilde u=up$. Give $F^\circ$ the complex structure for which $p$ is locally biholomorphic. Complete it across each missing interior point by the following explicit local construction. A finite covering of a punctured disk is described by its monodromy permutation: lift a circle and follow its finitely many sheet labels, splitting them into cycles. A cycle of length $r$ is filled by one disk on which $p(\zeta)=\zeta^r$. Near such a point the original map $u$ is $J_0$ holomorphic. In disjoint coordinate disks about its limiting tuple entries, the lifted coordinates are holomorphic roots off the center, by [F1]; they remain in those bounded disks and extend holomorphically by [F1]. Their symmetric functions continue to equal those of $up$ by continuity. This constructs a smooth branched cover $p:F\to\mathbb R\times[0,1]$ of degree $d$, with a smooth lift $\widetilde u:F\to\Sigma^m$, holomorphic over $u^{-1}(V)$. It also extends continuously at the finitely many ends, since the limiting tuples are off $\Delta$. The monodromy description follows by lifting radial segments after cutting a punctured disk along one radius; the only identification of the resulting sheet copies is the finite permutation across the cut. No compactness theorem for holomorphic maps is used. [F1, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Choose a smaller neighborhood $V_0$ of $\Delta$ with $\overline V_0\subset V$. If $\Delta$ is empty take $V_0=\varnothing$. On the compact set $X\setminus V_0$ the path uniformly tames $\omega$: this is given outside $V$, and on $V\setminus\Delta$ it follows from $J_t=J_0$ and the positive product form. Compactness of the unit tangent bundle over that set, together with compactness of the parameter interval, supplies $c>0$ such that for every relevant tangent vector $a$, $\frac12(|a|_g^2+|J_ta|_g^2)\le c\omega(a,J_ta)$. Consequently the energy integral of $u$ on $u^{-1}(X\setminus V_0)$ is bounded by $c/d$ times the integral of $\widetilde u^*\Omega$ over its preimage in $F$, by the covering change of variables. The equality disregards only finitely many branch points, which have measure zero. [step 1.1, step 2.1, given, algebra]

4.1 On $p^{-1}(u^{-1}(V_0))$ the map $\widetilde u$ is holomorphic for the product structure. Give $\Sigma^m$ its product Hermitian metric from $(j,\eta)$. Its holomorphic-map energy density equals $\widetilde u^*\Omega$. The smooth map $q$ on compact $\Sigma^m$ has bounded differential into $(X,g)$, so $E_g(q\widetilde u)$ on this part is bounded by a fixed constant times the product energy of $\widetilde u$ there. Conformal invariance of two-dimensional Dirichlet energy gives $E_g(up)=dE_g(u)$ on inverse images of subsets: off the branch points this is the ordinary change of variables with the scalar conformal factor of $p$, which cancels between the inverse metric and the area element. Thus this part has the same type of bound as step 3.1. Both lifted area integrands are nonnegative, by holomorphicity here and by tameness in step 3.1. Combining them gives $$E_g(u)\le\frac{C}{d}\int_F\widetilde u^*\Omega.$$ The lifted map has finite energy: on the tails and away from branch neighborhoods the inverse sheets of $q$ have uniformly bounded differentials, while on finitely many compact branch neighborhoods the constructed lift is smooth. Thus the individual signed area integrals below are absolutely integrable. [step 2.1, step 3.1, given, algebra]

5.1 Write $f_l=\operatorname{pr}_l\widetilde u$. For each region $R$, $f_l:f_l^{-1}(R)\to R$ is proper: its boundary and limiting end values lie on attaching curves, so inverse images of compact subsets of $R$ stay in a compact subset of the completed source away from its boundary and ends. Each component of its domain is a surface without boundary, and the restriction to that component is proper as well. For any compactly supported two-form on $R$, its inverse image is compact and hence meets only finitely many of these open components. Apply [F2] to those components and sum their degree formulas. All nonzero-degree components are among finitely many components: each contains a preimage of any common regular value, since its nonzero signed count is independent of that value, and a proper regular fibre is finite. Therefore there is one integer $d_{l,R}$, the total signed count at a regular value, such that $\int_{f_l^{-1}(R)}f_l^*\beta=d_{l,R}\int_R\beta$ for all compactly supported two-forms $\beta$ on $R$. Exhaust $R$ by compactly supported cutoffs in $\eta$. Absolute integrability from step 4.1 permits passage to the limit, yielding $$\int_{f_l^{-1}(R)}f_l^*\eta=d_{l,R}\int_R\eta.$$ [F2, step 2.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Choose a common regular point $p\in R$ for the finitely many coordinate maps, avoiding the finitely many tuple entries at branch values. At an intersection of $u$ with $E_p=\{p\}+\operatorname{Sym}^{m-1}(\Sigma)$ the tuple is then off $\Delta$, so precisely one of its entries equals $p$. Each of its $d$ ordered lifts contributes one preimage of $p$ among the $f_l$, with the same orientation sign: $q$ is a local holomorphic diffeomorphism off $\Delta$, and the normal coordinate to $E_p$ is exactly that entry's local complex coordinate. Consequently $$\sum_l d_{l,R}=d\,n_R(u).$$ In particular this signed multiplicity is independent of the generic point within $R$, and agrees with the disk-domain intersection convention. The inverse image of the attaching curves contributes zero to the integral of each $f_l^*\eta$: at points where $df_l$ has rank below two the pulled-back two-form vanishes, while at rank-two points the inverse function theorem identifies this inverse image locally with a finite union of smooth arcs and their intersection points, a measure-zero set. A countable chart cover retains measure zero. Summing the region formulas in step 5.1 therefore gives $$\int_F\widetilde u^*\Omega=\sum_{l,R}d_{l,R}\int_R\eta=d\sum_R n_R(u)\int_R\eta.$$ Combine this with step 4.1 to obtain the asserted energy bound, whose constant depends only on the fixed metrics, taming bounds, and path. Fixing the domain fixes its right-hand side and proves the final assertion. [F2, step 2.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra] ∎
