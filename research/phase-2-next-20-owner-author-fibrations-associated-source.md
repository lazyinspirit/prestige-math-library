## Complete ordinary-space candidate

**Conventions and data.** Let G be a topological group with identity 1, and let π:P→B be a right principal G-bundle: its continuous right action preserves π, and for every point of B there is an open neighborhood U and an equivariant homeomorphism θ:π⁻¹(U)→U×G over U, where (b,g)h=(b,gh). Let F have a continuous left action, with 1x=x and g(hx)=(gh)x. Give all products the ordinary product topology and subsets the ordinary subspace topology.

1. Define a right G-action on P×F by (p,x)·g=(pg,g⁻¹x). The identity and composition rules follow from the two action laws and (gh)⁻¹=h⁻¹g⁻¹. Each orbit is precisely the equivalence class generated by (pg,x)∼(p,gx): each such generator is an orbit relation, and (p,x)·g=(pg,g⁻¹x) is a generating relation. Thus equal classes may be tested using a single orbit element. Write Q=(P×F)/G and q:P×F→Q for the quotient. Since π(pg)=π(p), the continuous map (p,x)↦π(p) is constant on q-fibers. It descends by `thm-quotient-universal-property` to a unique continuous r:Q→B with r[p,x]=π(p).

2. Here is the restricted-quotient fact needed later, proved locally rather than assumed. If q:X→Y is a quotient map and V⊆Y is open, then q_V:q⁻¹(V)→V is a quotient map. It is continuous and surjective. If W⊆V has open inverse image under q_V, that inverse image is open in X because q⁻¹(V) is open in X. It equals q⁻¹(W), so W is open in Y by the quotient property and therefore in V. Conversely, if W is open in V then W is open in Y and q⁻¹(W) is open in q⁻¹(V). These are both directions. Apply this with V=r⁻¹(U), so q⁻¹(V)=π⁻¹(U)×F. The needed openness follows from Step 1, not from any presumed bundle property of r.

3. Fix one principal chart θ over U, write θ(p)=(π(p),a(p)), and put s(b)=θ⁻¹(b,1). The functions a and s are continuous by product and subspace properties. Equivariance gives a(pg)=a(p)g and p=s(π(p))a(p). The map t:π⁻¹(U)×F→U×F given by t(p,x)=(π(p),a(p)x) is continuous by the continuous action and the finite product property. It is constant on orbits: a(pg)(g⁻¹x)=a(p)gg⁻¹x=a(p)x. Step 2 and the quotient factorization property therefore give a continuous map χ:r⁻¹(U)→U×F, χ[p,x]=(π(p),a(p)x).

4. Define ψ:U×F→r⁻¹(U) by ψ(b,y)=[s(b),y]. The map to P×F is continuous, so its composite with q is continuous into Q; its image lies in r⁻¹(U), so the subspace characteristic property proves continuity into that subspace. Now χψ(b,y)=(b,1y)=(b,y), and ψχ[p,x]=[s(π(p)),a(p)x]=[s(π(p))a(p),x]=[p,x]. These computations prove both inverse identities on all points. Hence χ is a homeomorphism over U. These U cover B, which proves local triviality with fiber F.

5. For completeness the charts also give continuous transition functions. If s_i and s_j are the local sections and s_j(b)=s_i(b)c_ij(b), then c_ij(b)=a_i(s_j(b)) is continuous on U_i∩U_j. The associated transition χ_i ψ_j is (b,y)↦(b,c_ij(b)y). Uniqueness of principal coordinates gives c_ii=1, c_ji=c_ij⁻¹ and c_ik=c_ij c_jk. The action law proves the same cocycle identities for the fiber transitions. This uses no faithfulness of the action on F; different group elements may induce the same fiber map without affecting the conclusion.

6. Let h:A→B be continuous. Define h*P={(a,p):h(a)=π(p)}⊆A×P with projection to A and right action (a,p)g=(a,pg). It is continuous as a subspace-valued map, since its ambient coordinate maps are continuous and the equality h(a)=π(pg) remains true. Over V=h⁻¹(U), the map (a,p)↦(a,a_U(p)) is an equivariant homeomorphism (h*P)|V→V×G, with inverse (a,g)↦(a,θ_U⁻¹(h(a),g)). Continuity of both maps follows from finite products and the subspace property. The open sets V cover A. Thus h*P is itself a principal bundle and Steps 1–4 apply to it.

7. Let D=(h*P)×_G F and C=h*Q={(a,z):h(a)=r(z)}. Define Φ:D→C by Φ([(a,p),x])=(a,[p,x]). The prequotient function is continuous into A×Q and lands in C, so is continuous into C. It is constant on G-orbits, since [(pg),g⁻¹x]=[p,x]. Quotient descent therefore proves Φ continuous and well-defined. It respects the projections to A.

8. Over V=h⁻¹(U), the associated chart for D is χ_D([(a,p),x])=(a,a_U(p)x). The chart for C is χ_C(a,[p,x])=(a,a_U(p)x). For C its inverse is (a,y)↦(a,[s_U(h(a)),y]); product and subspace properties establish both continuities exactly as in Steps 3–4 (or restrict the already continuous chart χ_U on Q). These formulas give χ_C Φ χ_D⁻¹=id_(V×F). Thus Φ restricts to a homeomorphism on each member of the open cover of the base. In particular Φ is globally bijective: surjectivity and injectivity of any relevant fiber follow in any one chart containing its base point. Its inverse is continuous on the open sets C|V, so it is globally continuous by `lem-continuity-is-local-and-pastes`, Statement 2. Hence Φ is a bundle isomorphism. This proves the advertised isomorphism in either direction, without asserting that products preserve general quotient maps.

9. Naturality for successive pullbacks is literal on coordinates after the canonical parenthesis identifications. For k:T→A the canonical principal-bundle identification k*(h*P)→(h∘k)*P sends (t,(a,p)) to (t,p), where necessarily a=k(t); its inverse is (t,p)↦(t,(k(t),p)). These are continuous by product/subspace properties and G-equivariant. The analogous identification for Q is (t,(a,z))↦(t,z). Starting with [ (t,(k(t),p)),x ], either apply the associated comparison twice and then this identification, or identify the principal pullbacks first and apply the comparison for h∘k. Both results are (t,[p,x]). All points have that form, so the diagram commutes. For h=id_B the corresponding maps are (b,p)↦p and (b,z)↦z; the same computation proves the identity compatibility. This establishes the specified functoriality, including threefold compositions because every parenthesization forgets only the same redundant base coordinates.

## Cases, confidence and remaining integration work

Empty B forces P empty; the unique map A→B then forces A empty, and all spaces/maps in the comparison are empty. If A is empty with B nonempty, both pullback total spaces are empty and the comparison is the empty homeomorphism. If F is empty, Q and both associated total spaces are empty; the charts are homeomorphisms between empty spaces. This is compatible with a bundle definition permitting an empty fixed fiber; if that definition requires surjectivity, add F≠∅ explicitly instead of silently excluding this case. Singleton F gives the base bundle, with the same formulas. The trivial group case reduces to ordinary products; the proofs do not require G to have more than its identity. There are no numeric zero or endpoint parameters and no iff theorem beyond the restricted-quotient argument in Step 2, where both directions are given. No based points, higher homotopy groups, or low-degree actions are involved.

The proof is choice-free. Every local chart is taken one at a time to establish a local assertion; no simultaneous selection of charts, representatives, or lifts occurs. The unique principal coordinate is a function supplied by the chart, not a choice function. The global comparison is defined on equivalence classes before continuity is checked.

1. The local partition definition requires support, not just cozero, containment. Its exact formula is `supp(φ_s):=closure(coz(φ_s))`, and the preceding sentence requires a cover member to contain that support. Thus if α(v) is outside U_i, the open complement of supp(ρ_i) gives an evaluation neighborhood on which the corresponding segment minimum is zero. This proves supp(λ_T)⊆V_T by neighborhoods, valid without sequential assumptions or Hausdorffness of B. Normalization by a continuous positive G preserves the cozero set and hence the support; 0≤γ_T≤λ_T gives the other support inclusion.

2. Minima exist because each J_j is a nonempty compact closed real interval and ρ_i∘α is continuous. `cor-heine-borel-in-the-product-topology`, Statement 2 for n=1, and `thm-compactness-under-continuous-maps`, Statement 2 / proof steps 5.2–6.1, directly provide these hypotheses and attainment. A positive function on a compact interval has positive attained minimum, not merely nonnegative infimum. To make continuity of the minimum fully explicit, cover J_j by finitely many closed relative interval neighborhoods K_l on which f=ρ_i∘α varies from a chosen central value c_l by <ε/3. Require β(K_l)⊆ρ_i⁻¹((c_l−2ε/3,c_l+2ε/3)). These compact-open conditions contain α and ensure |ρ_iβ(v)−ρ_iα(v)|<ε on J_j. Consequently the two minima differ by ≤ε; beginning with any smaller error tolerance gives strict neighborhood control. This is the candidate's argument with all constants fixed.

3. For fixed n, take an open neighborhood O of α(I) meeting only finitely many cozero ρ_i. Such O exists by compactness of α(I) and local finiteness. On the compact-open neighborhood W(I,O), any word having a letter outside that finite set has λ_T identically zero (each segment is nonempty). Only finitely many words of the prescribed length remain. This proves the required family local finiteness, not just pointwise finiteness.

4. The shorter-word sum is continuous since it is a finite union, by word length, of locally finite families, using `lem-locally-finite-sums-are-continuous`, proof steps 1.1–3.1. For a word S with λ_S(α)>0 choose c>0 below that value and restrict to λ_S>c. For n>|S| and nc≥1, γ_T=max(0,λ_T−nΣ_short λ)=0 since λ_T≤1. Thus all sufficiently long words vanish on one neighborhood. The remaining finitely many lengths can be made locally finite by a finite neighborhood intersection. Minimal positive word length supplies γ_T>0 at every path, so G>0 everywhere; division by G is justified by `lem-algebra-of-continuous-real-maps-on-a-space`, proof step 3.2. Subfamilies indexed by S<T remain locally finite, hence the cumulative endpoints a_T and b_T are continuous as claimed.

5. The finite transport composition has a useful invariant to state in the final authored proof. For fixed s≤t, put u_j=min(t,max(s,j/n)) for 0≤j≤n. Then u_0=s, u_n=t, and after chart j the current point lies over α(u_j). Before it lies over α(u_(j−1)). On the overlap case s≤j/n and t≥(j−1)/n, these endpoints are respectively max(s,(j−1)/n) and min(t,j/n), both in J_j. The indicated chart formula is therefore defined on its entire closed case. On t≤(j−1)/n or s≥j/n, u_(j−1)=u_j and the identity is defined without needing the current point inside that chart. On intersections of cases the endpoints coincide at a chart endpoint and the chart formula is exactly the identity. These three closed cases cover s≤t. Finite closed pasting and induction prove joint continuity in (α,e,s,t) on the correct fiber-product domain, with no limit or metrizability assumption on F.

Their clipped ordered intervals fit end to start. The composition is finite and continuous, and inserting a zero-weight word inserts a literal identity.
