## Statement

Let $n\ge2$, $R_0=\mathbb Z[Q,Q^{-1}]$, $A=R_0[X_1^{\pm1},\ldots,X_n^{\pm1}]$, and $H_n^{\mathrm{aff}}$ be the algebra of [[def-hh-type-a-affine-bernstein-presentation]]. For $\lambda\in\mathbb Z^n$ and $w\in S_n$, write $X^\lambda=X_1^{\lambda_1}\cdots X_n^{\lambda_n}$ and let $T_w$ be the finite Hecke product along a reduced expression of $w$. Then the coefficient map $A\to H_n^{\mathrm{aff}}$ is injective and
$$
\{X^\lambda T_w:\lambda\in\mathbb Z^n,\ w\in S_n\}
$$
is an $R_0$-basis of $H_n^{\mathrm{aff}}$. For every commutative $R_0$-algebra $R'$, the scalar extension $R'\otimes_{R_0}H_n^{\mathrm{aff}}$ has basis $(1\otimes X^\lambda T_w)_{\lambda,w}$ and is the affine presentation with its relations mapped to $R'$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The universal affine presentation, the Laurent monomial algebra $A$, the finite type-A Coxeter group, and the polynomial-module homomorphism.

[F1] [[def-hh-type-a-affine-bernstein-presentation]]: $H_n^{\mathrm{aff}}$ is generated by $X_j^{\pm1}$ and $T_i$, with the stated finite Hecke, braid, and Bernstein relations.

[F2] [[lem-hh-type-a-finite-hecke-basis-in-multiplicative-normalization]]: the finite algebra on the $T_i$ with $(T_i-Q)(T_i+1)=0$ and type-A braids has basis $(T_w)_{w\in S_n}$ over $R_0$.

[F3] [[lem-hh-demazure-lusztig-operators-satisfy-affine-relations]]: the presented algebra maps to $K\rtimes S_n$; each operator is $c_i[1]+d_i[s_i]$ with $d_i\ne0$, has the full Bernstein relation, and preserves $A$.

[F4] [[def-hh-type-a-weight-lattice-laurent-ring-and-divided-differences]]: the monomials $(X^\lambda)_{\lambda\in\mathbb Z^n}$ are an $R_0$-basis of $A$ and $A$ embeds in its fraction field $K$.

[F5] [[lem-hh-universal-presentations-and-base-change]]: a presented algebra base-changes to the quotient by the images of its relations; tensoring a free module with a basis preserves the basis over every commutative specialization.

[F6] [[lem-hh-free-associative-ring-and-relations-descent]]: the free associative algebra is the free module on finite generator words, so every element of its quotient has a finite expression as a sum of generator words.

## Proof

**Given:** The data of the Statement.

1.1 The images of the Laurent generators commute and are units by [F1], so their finite Laurent monomials define the coefficient map from $A$. Every element of the presented algebra is represented by a finite linear combination of words in $T_i$ and $X_j^{\pm1}$ by [F6]. To show spanning, take such a word and count its pairs in which a $T_i$ occurs to the left of an $X_j^{\pm1}$. If the count is positive, an adjacent out-of-order pair can be rewritten by the Bernstein relation of [F3] as a Laurent term with that pair reversed plus a finite Laurent sum with one fewer $T$; the second assertion uses that $D_i$ maps $A$ to $A$. Induction first on the number of $T$ factors and then on this pair count moves every Laurent factor to the left. The finite $T_i$ generators in [F1] satisfy the presentation of $H_{n,Q}^{\mathrm{fin}}$, so its universal property maps $H_{n,Q}^{\mathrm{fin}}$ to $H_n^{\mathrm{aff}}$; its basis in [F2] then expresses the remaining finite word as an $R_0$-linear combination of $T_w$. Thus the displayed family spans. [F1, F2, F3, F6, given, construct]

1.2 For a reduced expression $w=s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_m}$, expand the image of $T_w$ under [F3] as a product of $m$ factors $c_{i_k}[1]+d_{i_k}[s_{i_k}]$. Each term is supported at a permutation expressed using at most $m$ adjacent transpositions, so its Coxeter length is at most $m$. The coefficient of $[w]$ can only come from choosing every reflection term: omitting one leaves fewer than $m=\ell(w)$ factors. Thus that coefficient is $b_w=\prod_{k=1}^{m}(s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_{k-1}})(d_{i_k})$, with the empty prefix acting as the identity. Every $d_{i_k}$ is nonzero by [F3] and each prefix acts by a field automorphism, so $b_w\ne0$ in $K$. [F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 Suppose $\sum_{w}f_wT_w=0$ with $f_w\in A$ and finite support. If any coefficient is nonzero, choose the maximum length $L$ of a $w$ with $f_w\ne0$. Apply the homomorphism of [F3] and compare the coefficient of $[w]$ for each such $w$ with $\ell(w)=L$. Terms indexed by $v$ with $\ell(v)<L$ cannot contribute to $[w]$ by step 1.2; terms with $\ell(v)=L$ contribute to $[w]$ only when $v=w$, since a length-$L$ permutation in the expansion requires choosing all $L$ reflection terms and then equals $v$. Thus the coefficient of $[w]$ is $f_wb_w=0$ in $K$. Since $b_w\ne0$ and $A\hookrightarrow K$ by [F4], $f_w=0$, a contradiction. Hence the family $(T_w)$ is $A$-linearly independent. [F3, F4, step 1.2]

3.1 Every $h\in H_n^{\mathrm{aff}}$ has a finite expansion $\sum_w f_wT_w$ by step 1.1; step 2.1 makes that expansion unique. Expanding each $f_w$ in the Laurent monomial basis of [F4] proves that $(X^\lambda T_w)_{\lambda,w}$ is an $R_0$-basis. In particular, the coefficient map $A\to H_n^{\mathrm{aff}}$ is injective, since a finite relation among its Laurent monomials would be an $R_0$-linear relation among the basis elements with $w=1$. [F4, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 For a commutative $R_0$-algebra $R'$, [F5] identifies $R'\otimes_{R_0}H_n^{\mathrm{aff}}$ with the quotient of the free associative $R'$-algebra by the images of the defining relations, and carries the $R_0$-basis of step 3.1 to the stated $R'$-basis. No generic-faithfulness argument after specialization is used; no flatness assumption on $R'$ is needed. [F1, F5, step 3.1]

5.1 The preceding steps prove spanning, independence over the universal domain, the faithful Laurent coefficient map, and transport of the explicit basis to every commutative base change. The proof is choice-free: it selects only a maximum from a finite support and uses the explicit monomial and operator bases. [F2, F3, F4, F5, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1] ∎
