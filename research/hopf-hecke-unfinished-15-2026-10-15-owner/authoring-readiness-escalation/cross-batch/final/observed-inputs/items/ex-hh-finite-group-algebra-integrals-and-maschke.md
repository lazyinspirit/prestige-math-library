## Example

Let $G$ be a finite group and $k$ a field. In the group Hopf algebra $kG$, the left and right integral spaces are both generated by $\Lambda_G=\sum_{g\in G}g$, and $\varepsilon(\Lambda_G)=|G|\,1_k$. Consequently $kG$ is semisimple exactly when $\operatorname{char}(k)=0$ or $\operatorname{char}(k)=p>0$ does not divide $|G|$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite group $G$, its group Hopf algebra $kG$, and a field $k$.

[F1] [[lem-hh-group-and-polynomial-hopf-algebras-exist]]: the group elements form a basis of $kG$, with $\Delta(g)=g\otimes g$, $\varepsilon(g)=1$, and multiplication induced by the group product.

[F2] [[def-hh-left-right-integrals-and-integral-functionals]]: $t$ is a left integral when $ht=\varepsilon(h)t$ for all $h$; the right-integral equation is $th=\varepsilon(h)t$.

[F3] [[thm-hh-hopf-maschke-integral-averaging]]: a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra over a field is semisimple iff it has a left integral with nonzero counit.

## Verification

1.1 For each $h\in G$, left multiplication permutes the group basis, so $h\Lambda_G=\sum_g hg=\sum_g g=\Lambda_G$; similarly $\Lambda_Gh=\Lambda_G$. Since $\varepsilon(h)=1$, these identities show $\Lambda_G$ is both a left and right integral. [F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 Let $t=\sum_g a_g g$ be a left integral. For any $h,u\in G$, comparison of the coefficient of $u$ in $ht=t$ gives $a_{h^{-1}u}=a_u$. For arbitrary $u,v\in G$, taking $h=uv^{-1}$ yields $a_v=a_u$, so all coefficients are equal and every left integral is a scalar multiple of $\Lambda_G$. If instead $t=\sum_g a_g g$ is a right integral, the coefficient of $u$ in $th=t$ gives $a_{u h^{-1}}=a_u$ for every $h,u\in G$; choosing $h=v^{-1}u$ gives $a_v=a_u$ for arbitrary $u,v$. Thus every right integral is also a scalar multiple of $\Lambda_G$, and both integral spaces are $k\Lambda_G$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Applying the counit gives $\varepsilon(\Lambda_G)=\sum_g1_k=|G|\,1_k$. Hence a left integral has nonzero counit exactly when this field element is nonzero: this always holds in characteristic zero, and in positive characteristic $p$ it holds exactly when $p\nmid |G|$. By [F3], this is equivalent to semisimplicity of $kG$. [F3, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
