## Statement

Let $R$ be a unital ring, $M$ a right $R$-module, $N$ a left $R$-module, and

$$\tau:M\times N\longrightarrow M\otimes_RN,\qquad \tau(m,n)=m\otimes n.$$

The map $\tau$ is balanced ([[def-balanced-and-bilinear-maps]]). For every abelian group $A$ and every balanced map $b:M\times N\to A$, there is a unique group homomorphism

$$\overline b:M\otimes_RN\longrightarrow A$$

such that $\overline b(m\otimes n)=b(m,n)$ for all $m,n$. Consequently composition with $\tau$ is a bijection

$$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Ab}}(M\otimes_RN,A)\cong\operatorname{Bal}_R(M,N;A).$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A unital ring $R$, a right $R$-module $M$, a left $R$-module $N$, an abelian group $A$, and a balanced map $b:M\times N\to A$.

[L1] The tensor product is $F/H$, where $F=\mathbb Z^{(M\times N)}$, $H$ is generated by the additivity and balance relations, and $m\otimes n=e_{(m,n)}+H$ ([[def-tensor-product-of-modules-by-generators-and-relations]]).

[L2] A balanced map is additive in each variable and satisfies $b(mr,n)=b(m,rn)$ ([[def-balanced-and-bilinear-maps]]).

[L3] Every element of $\mathbb Z^{(X)}$ has a unique finite expression $\sum_{x\in E}k_xe_x$ with $k_x\in\mathbb Z$ ([[def-free-module-on-a-set-and-standard-basis]]).

[L4] Every set map $u:X\to P$ into a left $S$-module extends uniquely to an $S$-module homomorphism $S^{(X)}\to P$ taking $e_x$ to $u(x)$ ([[thm-universal-property-of-free-modules]]).

[L5] If a group homomorphism $f:G\to K$ kills a normal subgroup $H$, then it factors uniquely through a group homomorphism $G/H\to K$ ([[thm-quotient-group-universal-property]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Regard $A$ as a $\mathbb Z$-module by integer multiplication in its additive group. A group homomorphism between abelian groups is automatically $\mathbb Z$-linear: additivity gives $f(na)=nf(a)$ for $n\ge0$, and $f(-a)=-f(a)$ gives the formula for negative integers. Thus $\mathbb Z$-module homomorphisms and group homomorphisms between these underlying additive groups are the same maps. [given, algebra]

1.2 If $h:M\otimes_RN\to A$ is a group homomorphism, then $(m,n)\mapsto h(m\otimes n)$ is balanced because the elementary tensors satisfy all three relations in [L1]. [L1, algebra]

2.1 Apply [L4] at $S=\mathbb Z$ and $X=M\times N$ to extend the set map $b$ uniquely to a $\mathbb Z$-linear map $\widetilde b:F\to A$ satisfying $\widetilde b(e_{(m,n)})=b(m,n)$. [L3, L4, step 1.1]

3.1 By [L2], $\widetilde b$ sends each generator of $H$ to zero: the two additivity generators map respectively to $b(m+m',n)-b(m,n)-b(m',n)$ and $b(m,n+n')-b(m,n)-b(m,n')$, while the balance generator maps to $b(mr,n)-b(m,rn)$. Hence $H\subseteq\ker\widetilde b$. [L1, L2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 By [L5], $\widetilde b$ factors uniquely through a group homomorphism $\overline b:F/H=M\otimes_RN\to A$, and $\overline b(m\otimes n)=\widetilde b(e_{(m,n)})=b(m,n)$. [L1, L5, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 The operations in steps 4.1 and 1.2 are inverse: starting from $b$ recovers its values on every pair, while starting from $h$ produces a homomorphism agreeing with $h$ on every elementary tensor, and those tensors generate $M\otimes_RN$. This proves both the asserted uniqueness and the displayed bijection. [step 4.1, step 1.2, L1]

6.1 No selection is made in the construction. If $M=0$ or $N=0$, every balanced map out of $M\times N$ is zero and the relations make every elementary tensor zero, so the same proof gives $M\otimes_RN=0$; the zero ring is covered as well. Since a module contains its zero element, $M\times N$ is never empty, so there is no separate empty-domain case. [L1, L2, step 5.1] ∎
