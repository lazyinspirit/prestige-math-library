## Definition

Let $n\ge0$ be an integer, with $S_0=\{1\}$ as in
[[def-partition-young-diagram-and-conjugate-partition]]. Let $A:=\mathbb Z[v^{\pm1}]$ be the ring of Laurent polynomials in one
indeterminate $v$ over $\mathbb Z$. For $n\ge2$ the **generic type-A Hecke
algebra** $H_v(n)$ is the associative unital $A$-algebra presented by generators
$T_1,\dots,T_{n-1}$ and the relations
$$T_i^2=(v-1)T_i+v\quad(1\le i<n),\qquad T_iT_{i+1}T_i=T_{i+1}T_iT_{i+1}\quad(1\le i\le n-2),\qquad T_iT_j=T_jT_i\quad(1\le i,j<n,\ |i-j|>1),$$
that is, the quotient of the free unital associative $A$-algebra on
$T_1,\dots,T_{n-1}$ by the two-sided ideal generated by these relators. The
first relation is written in the **RG-13 quadratic normalization**
$(T_i-v)(T_i+1)=0$, equivalent to the displayed form over $A$. For $n\le1$ there
are no generators and we set $H_v(n):=A$.

**Standard basis elements.** Let $S_n=\operatorname{Sym}(\{1,\dots,n\})$ with
simple transpositions $s_i=(i\ i+1)$
([[def-symmetric-group]]), and let $w\in S_n$ have inversion length $\ell(w)=\ell$
([[def-weyl-group-and-length-for-finite-gl-n]]). Choose a reduced expression
$w=s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_\ell}$, that is, a word of the minimal length $\ell(w)$
representing $w$, and put
$$T_w:=T_{i_1}\cdots T_{i_\ell}\in H_v(n),$$
the empty product when $w$ is the identity of $S_n$, so that $T_w=1$ for that
element. That $T_w$ is independent of the chosen reduced expression, and that
the elements $T_w$ form an $A$-basis of $H_v(n)$, is proved in the
standard-basis theorem below
([[thm-standard-basis-of-the-generic-type-a-hecke-algebra]]); the notation
distinguishes the generator $T_i$ with $1\le i\le n-1$ from $T_w$ for the group
element $w$.

**Specializations.** Let $R$ be a commutative $A$-algebra with structure map
$\mathbb Z[v^{\pm1}]\to R$, $v\mapsto v_0$, and suppose $v_0\in R^\times$. The
**specialization** of $H_v(n)$ at $v_0$ is the $R$-algebra
$R\otimes_AH_v(n)$; it is presented over $R$ by the images of $T_1,\dots,T_{n-1}$
subject to the same relations with $v$ replaced by $v_0$, and we write $H_{v_0}(n)$
for it. Two specializations are used on this page: $v\mapsto1$, where the
quadratic relation becomes $T_i^2=1$ together with the braid and commutation
relations, and $v\mapsto q$, where $q$ is the prime power defining
$\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb F_q)$; the latter specializes the generic algebra to
the Hecke algebra attached to the finite general linear group. The condition
$v_0\in R^\times$ is part of the definition of a specialization and, for a nonzero coefficient ring $R$, excludes $v_0=0$. The zero ring
is allowed: its unique element is a unit and its specialization is the zero algebra.

## Remarks

**Dictionary to the Soergel normalization.** The type-A Soergel item
`def-type-a-hecke-algebra-in-soergel-normalization`, homed later in the reading
order, uses $A_S=\mathbb Z[v_S^{\pm1}]$ and $q=v_S^{-2}$. Its algebra is the
base change $A_S\otimes_{A,\,v\mapsto v_S^{-2}}H_v(n)$: substitution makes
its quadratic, braid and commutation relations identical to the ones here.
This coefficient map is not a Laurent-ring isomorphism; its image is
$\mathbb Z[v_S^{\pm2}]$, and $A_S$ is free of rank two over that image with
basis $1,v_S$. Numerical specializations must therefore satisfy
$v_{\text{here}}=v_S^{-2}$. This comparison is orientation only and is not
a premise of the definition or its standard-basis proof.
