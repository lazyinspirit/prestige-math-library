## Statement

Let $H$ be a Hopf algebra over a field $k$ and let $R\in H\otimes H$ be an algebraic quasitriangular structure. For any left $H$-modules $V,W$, define
$c_{V,W}(v\otimes w)=\tau_{V,W}\big((\rho_V\otimes\rho_W)(R)(v\otimes w)\big)=\sum_i b_iw\otimes a_iv$
when $R=\sum_i a_i\otimes b_i$. These maps form a braiding on the category of **all** left $H$-modules. The associated operator $\check R_V=c_{V,V}$ satisfies the Yang–Baxter braid relation on $V^{\otimes3}$. The braiding is symmetric exactly when $R_{21}R=1\otimes1$. Conversely, every braiding on the category of all left $H$-modules uniquely determines an algebraic quasitriangular element $R\in H\otimes H$ by
$R=\tau_{H,H}\big(c_{H,H}(1_H\otimes1_H)\big).$
This converse uses naturality on all modules, including the regular module $H$, and does not assume finite dimensionality.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$, a Hopf algebra $H$ over $k$, and its category of all left modules with tensor action. In the forward direction, an invertible quasitriangular $R\in H\otimes H$ is specified; in the converse direction, a braiding on all these modules is specified.

[F1] [[def-hh-leg-notation-and-quasitriangular-hopf-algebra]]: tensor products are algebraic, the componentwise tensor-product algebra is used, and the leg embeddings, $R_{21}$, and $\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}$ have their stated meanings for any $R\in H\otimes H$. These definitions do not assume the quasitriangular axioms in the converse direction.

[F2] [[def-hh-braiding-and-triangular-structure]]: a braiding is natural, consists of $H$-linear isomorphisms on all pairs of modules, and satisfies the two hexagons; triangular means $R_{21}R=1\otimes1$.

[F3] [[def-hh-module-category-and-tensor-action]]: $h$ acts on $V\otimes W$ by $\sum h_{(1)}v\otimes h_{(2)}w$, and every left-module map intertwines that action.

[F4] [[thm-hh-bialgebra-module-tensor-coherence]]: the canonical associator is an $H$-linear map and the module tensor action is coassociative, so the parenthesized hexagon composites are module maps.

[F5] [[lem-hh-regular-module-detects-linear-and-tensor-identities]]: on the left regular module, $a\cdot1_H=a$ for every $a\in H$, so the regular module is generated by $1_H$.

[F6] [[lem-hh-quasitriangular-counit-and-yang-baxter-identities]]: every quasitriangular $R$ satisfies $R_{12}R_{13}R_{23}=R_{23}R_{13}R_{12}$.

[F7] [[lem-hh-tensor-algebra-regular-detection]]: left multiplication by an element of $H\otimes H$ or $H^{\otimes3}$ is determined by its value at the corresponding unit tensor.

[A1] Forward direction only: $R$ is invertible and satisfies $R\Delta(h)=\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}(h)R$, $(\Delta\otimes\operatorname{id})R=R_{13}R_{23}$, and $\alpha^{-1}(\operatorname{id}\otimes\Delta)R=R_{13}R_{12}$.

[A2] Converse direction only: a braiding on all left $H$-modules is given.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Write $R=\sum_i a_i\otimes b_i$. The left action of $H\otimes H$ on $V\otimes W$ sends $(a\otimes b)(v\otimes w)$ to $av\otimes bw$; therefore the finite tensor $R$ defines the linear map in the Statement, independently of its chosen sum representation. Its inverse is $(\rho_V\otimes\rho_W)(R^{-1})\circ\tau_{W,V}$, so $c_{V,W}$ is an isomorphism. [given, A1, F1, F3, construct]

1.2 Now suppose a braiding is given on all left $H$-modules. For $v\in V$, the map $f_v:H\to V$, $x\mapsto xv$, is $H$-linear because module associativity gives $f_v(hx)=(hx)v=h(xv)$, and [F5] gives $f_v(1_H)=v$. Set $R=\tau_{H,H}c_{H,H}(1_H\otimes1_H)$. Naturality for $f_v$ and $f_w$ then gives $c_{V,W}(v\otimes w)=\sum_i b_iw\otimes a_iv$ when $R=\sum_i a_i\otimes b_i$. [A2, F2, F3, F5, construct]

2.1 For $h\in H$, the diagonal tensor action and the quasitriangular relation give $c_{V,W}\rho_{V\otimes W}(h)=\tau_{V,W}(\rho_V\otimes\rho_W)(R\Delta(h)) =\tau_{V,W}(\rho_V\otimes\rho_W)(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}(h)R) =\rho_{W\otimes V}(h)c_{V,W},$ where the last equality is the elementary flip identity $\tau\rho_{V,W}(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}(h))=\rho_{W,V}(h)\tau$. Hence $c_{V,W}$ is $H$-linear. [given, A1, F1, F3, algebra, step 1.1]

2.2 If $f:V\to V'$ and $g:W\to W'$ are $H$-linear, then $(g\otimes f)c_{V,W}$ and $c_{V',W'}(f\otimes g)$ both send $v\otimes w$ to $\sum_i b_i g(w)\otimes a_i f(v)$, using the $H$-linearity of $f,g$. Thus the family is natural. [given, F1, F3, algebra, step 1.1]

2.3 On $u\otimes v\otimes w$, the map $c_{U,V\otimes W}$ gives $\sum_i (b_i)_{(1)}v\otimes(b_i)_{(2)}w\otimes a_i u$. The second hexagon identity rewrites this coefficient tensor as $R_{13}R_{12}=\sum_{j,k}a_ja_k\otimes b_k\otimes b_j$, so the result is $\sum_{j,k}b_kv\otimes b_jw\otimes a_ja_ku$, exactly $(\operatorname{id}_V\otimes c_{U,W})(c_{U,V}\otimes\operatorname{id}_W)(u\otimes v\otimes w)$. Equality on spanning pure tensors proves the first hexagon. [given, A1, F1, F3, F4, algebra, step 1.1]

2.4 On $u\otimes v\otimes w$, the map $c_{U\otimes V,W}$ gives $\sum_i b_iw\otimes(a_i)_{(1)}u\otimes(a_i)_{(2)}v$. The first hexagon identity rewrites the coefficient tensor as $R_{13}R_{23}=\sum_{j,k}a_j\otimes a_k\otimes b_jb_k$, so the result is $\sum_{j,k}b_jb_kw\otimes a_ju\otimes a_kv$, exactly $(c_{U,W}\otimes\operatorname{id}_V)(\operatorname{id}_U\otimes c_{V,W})(u\otimes v\otimes w)$. Equality on pure tensors proves the second hexagon. [given, A1, F1, F3, F4, algebra, step 1.1]

2.5 For $V^{\otimes3}$, write $c_1=\tau_{12}R_{12}$ and $c_2=\tau_{23}R_{23}$ for their induced operators. Moving the flips past the leg operators by conjugating the affected tensor factors gives $c_1c_2c_1=\tau_{12}\tau_{23}\tau_{12}R_{23}R_{13}R_{12}$ and $c_2c_1c_2=\tau_{23}\tau_{12}\tau_{23}R_{12}R_{13}R_{23}$. The two flip products agree on every triple pure tensor, and [F6] makes the two R-products equal, so $\check R_V$ satisfies the Yang–Baxter braid relation. [given, A1, F1, F6, algebra, step 1.1]

2.6 In the composite $c_{W,V}c_{V,W}$, conjugating the action of $R$ across the flip changes $R$ to $R_{21}$, so the composite acts on $V\otimes W$ by $R_{21}R$. If $R_{21}R=1\otimes1$, the composite is the identity, proving symmetry. [given, F1, F2, algebra, step 1.1]

2.7 Applying the formula of step 1.2 to $V=W=H$ yields $c_{H,H}=\tau_{H,H}\circ L_R$, where $L_R$ is left multiplication by $R$ on the componentwise algebra $H\otimes H$. Since $c_{H,H}$ and $\tau$ are bijective, $L_R$ is bijective. Surjectivity supplies $X$ with $RX=1$; then $R(XR-1)=0$, and injectivity of $L_R$ gives $XR=1$. Thus $R$ is invertible. [A2, step 1.2, F1, choose]

3.1 The $H$-linearity equation for $c_{H,H}=\tau L_R$ is $\tau L_RL_{\Delta(h)}=L_{\Delta(h)}\tau L_R$. Conjugating the right-hand action by the vector-space flip gives $L_RL_{\Delta(h)}=L_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}(h)}L_R$, hence $L_{R\Delta(h)}=L_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}(h)R}$. The conjugation identity holds on elementary tensors by the definition of the flip and extends linearly. [F7] evaluates the equal left-multiplication maps at $1_H\otimes1_H$, yielding $R\Delta(h)=\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}(h)R$. [A2, step 1.2, step 2.7, F1, F3, F7]

3.2 Apply the two hexagons of the given braiding to the regular modules $H,H,H$. By the formula in step 1.2, the first hexagon's two sides are the same output flip composed with left multiplication on $H^{\otimes3}$ by $\alpha^{-1}(\operatorname{id}\otimes\Delta)R$ and by $R_{13}R_{12}$, respectively. Cancel the common flip. Equality of the resulting maps and [F7] give the second quasitriangular hexagon. The other hexagon gives, in the same way, left multiplication by $(\Delta\otimes\operatorname{id})R$ and by $R_{13}R_{23}$; [F7] gives the first hexagon. [A2, step 1.2, F1, F2, F4, F7, step 2.7]

4.1 The formula in step 1.2 uniquely determines every $c_{V,W}$ from $R$. The preceding converse steps prove that the recovered $R$ is invertible and satisfies every quasitriangular axiom. If the braiding is symmetric, then $c_{H,H}c_{H,H}=\operatorname{id}$. Using $c_{H,H}=\tau L_R$ from step 2.7, the composite is left multiplication by $R_{21}R$; [F7] therefore gives $R_{21}R=1\otimes1$. Together with step 2.6 this proves the triangular/symmetric equivalence. [A2, step 2.6, step 1.2, step 2.7, step 3.1, step 3.2, F1, F7, algebra] ∎
