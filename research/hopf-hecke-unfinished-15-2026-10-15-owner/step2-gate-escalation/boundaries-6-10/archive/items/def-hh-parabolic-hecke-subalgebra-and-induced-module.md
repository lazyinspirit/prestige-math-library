## Definition

Let $(S,m)$ be a finite Coxeter matrix, $W$ its presented group with length $\ell$ and standard parabolic subgroup $W_J=\langle J\rangle$ for $J\subseteq S$ ([[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]). Let $R$, $H$, and the standard basis $\{T_w:w\in W\}$ be those of [[def-hh-universal-coxeter-hecke-parameters-and-presentation]] and [[thm-hh-generic-coxeter-hecke-standard-basis]]. Use the inherited parameters $v_j$ on the restricted Coxeter system $W_J$; distinct odd-edge components of $J$ may therefore carry the same parameter when they lie in one odd-edge component of $S$.

**Parabolic Hecke subalgebra.** Define the candidate $R$-submodule
$$
H_J:=\bigoplus_{u\in W_J}R T_u\subseteq H.
$$
The justifying theorem [[thm-hh-parabolic-hecke-freeness-and-induced-bases]] proves it is a subalgebra, is generated by $T_j$ for $j\in J$, and is the embedded image of the restricted-system Hecke algebra over $R$. The same theorem proves the left and right freeness assertions needed by the induced-module construction below.

For a left $H_J$-module $M$, define its **parabolic induction** to be the balanced tensor product
$$
\operatorname{Ind}_{J}^{W}(M):=H\otimes_{H_J}M,
$$
formed from the right $H_J$-module $H$ and left $H_J$-module $M$ ([[def-tensor-product-of-modules-by-generators-and-relations]], [[thm-universal-property-of-module-tensor-products]]). Thus $(h k)\otimes m=h\otimes(km)$ for $h\in H$, $k\in H_J$, and $m\in M$. The left $H$-action is $a\cdot(h\otimes m)=(ah)\otimes m$; it respects balancing because $(ahk)\otimes m=(ah)\otimes(km)$, and its module laws follow from associativity and the unit of $H$.

For a left $H$-module $N$, define **parabolic restriction** $\operatorname{Res}_{J}^{W}(N)$ to be its underlying $R$-module with the $H_J$-action obtained from the inclusion $H_J\hookrightarrow H$ proved by the same justifier. These constructions use left modules throughout; the theorem below also records the opposite right-module decomposition of $H$ so the two coset conventions remain explicit.
