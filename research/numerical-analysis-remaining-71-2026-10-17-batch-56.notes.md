## Mathematical scope and axioms

The one-dimensional branch fixes a uniform interval mesh, constant `epsilon > 0`, `b != 0`, homogeneous boundary data for stability, and the exact cell parameter. The separate layer example uses nonzero endpoint data and checks nodal fitting against the within-cell limiting error. The multidimensional P1 branch assumes a finite matching shape-regular affine simplicial mesh, `b in C1`, `c,f` continuous, `c - div(b)/2 >= omega > 0`, a C2 exact solution with zero boundary data, and the explicitly bounded residual parameter. It makes no result for weaker regularity or unrestricted multidimensional parameters.

The PSPG theorem specializes to stationary Stokes on a connected conforming simplicial mesh with continuous equal-order P1 spaces, zero-boundary velocity, mean-zero pressure, `nu > 0`, `tau_K > 0`, and `gamma >= 0`. The quadratic form controls velocity gradients, grad-div and the cellwise pressure-gradient stabilization. The checkerboard and grad-div examples are exact finite Q1 calculations and do not assert a general inf-sup theorem. The VMS result defines every finite-dimensional block map and norm, assumes exact fine-block and approximate Schur invertibility, and bounds the effect of replacing `A_ff^{-1}` by a residual closure `T`.

Countable Choice is used only where explicitly declared in the two error branches and only through their named imported suppliers. All other examples and finite-dimensional arguments are choice-free.
