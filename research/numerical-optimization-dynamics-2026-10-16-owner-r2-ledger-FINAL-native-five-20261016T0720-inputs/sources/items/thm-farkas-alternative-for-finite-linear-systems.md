## Statement

Let $A\in\mathbb R^{r\times n}$ and $b\in\mathbb R^r$. Exactly one of the
following holds:

1. There is $x\in\mathbb R^n$ with $x\ge0$ and $Ax=b$.
2. There is $y\in\mathbb R^r$ with $A^Ty\ge0$ and $b^Ty<0$.

Inequalities between vectors are coordinatewise.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The matrix and vector in the Statement and the standard Euclidean
inner product.

[F1] The cone generated by any finite list of Euclidean vectors is closed
([[lem-finitely-generated-euclidean-cones-are-closed]]).

[F2] Every point has a unique nearest point in a nonempty closed convex
Euclidean set ([[thm-nearest-point-in-a-nonempty-closed-convex-set]]).

[F3] If $p$ is the nearest point to $b$ in a closed convex set $C$, then
$\langle b-p,c-p\rangle\le0$ for every $c\in C$
([[lem-projection-onto-a-closed-convex-set-has-the-variational-inequality]]).

## Proof

1.1 Let $C=\{\sum_{j=1}^n x_j A_j:x_j\ge0\}$, where $A_j$ are the columns of $A$. This is a nonempty convex cone and is closed by [F1]. If $b\in C$, its nonnegative coefficients give alternative 1. If $b\notin C$, let $p$ be its nearest point in $C$ by [F2] and put $z=b-p\ne0$. [F1, F2, given, construct]

2.1 Applying [F3] with $c=0$ and $c=2p$ gives both $\langle z,p\rangle\ge0$ and $\langle z,p\rangle\le0$, hence $\langle z,p\rangle=0$. Applying [F3] to arbitrary $c\in C$ then gives $\langle z,c\rangle\le0$. In particular $A_j^Tz\le0$ for every column, and $\langle z,b\rangle=\langle z,p+z\rangle=\|z\|_2^2>0$. Thus $y=-z$ satisfies $A^Ty\ge0$ and $b^Ty=-\|z\|_2^2<0$, which is alternative 2. [F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The alternatives cannot both hold: if $Ax=b$ with $x\ge0$ and $A^Ty\ge0$, then $b^Ty=x^TA^Ty\ge0$, contradicting $b^Ty<0$. Step 1.1 produces one alternative in every case, so exactly one holds. [step 1.1, algebra] ∎
