LARS DIENING, JOHANNES STORN, AND TABEA TSCHERPEL
Abstract. We introduce a Scott–Zhang type projection operator mapping
to Lagrange elements for arbitrary polynomial order. In addition to the usual
properties, this operator is compatible with duals of ﬁrst order Sobolev spaces.
More speciﬁcally, it is stable in the corresponding negative norms and allows
for optimal rates of convergence. We discuss alternative operators with similar
properties. As applications of the operator we prove interpolation error esti-
mates for parabolic problems and smoothen rough right-hand sides in a least
squares ﬁnite element method.
1. Introduction
Interpolation operators are an important tool in numerical analysis and thus have
been studied intensively [Cl´e75; SZ90; Car99; Mel05; DR07; EG17]. While the focus
of these works lies primarily on error control in W s,p(Ω) for non-negative orders
s ∈N0, less is known for error control in negative Sobolev spaces W −1,p(Ω) with
p ∈(1, ∞). Such results are, however, needed for recent developments including
• time-stepping and space-time ﬁnite element methods for parabolic prob-
lems, where the time-derivative can only be controlled in this norm (cf. [Ste15;
TV16; SW21]),
• multilevel decompositions (cf. [BPV00; WZ17; F¨uh22]) and operator pre-
conditioning (cf. [SV20; SV21]),
• regularizations for PDEs with rough right-hand side, in particular for meth-
ods that require the right-hand side to be in Lp(Ω) like some DG, DPG, non-
conforming, or least squares schemes (cf. [VZ18a; VZ18b; VZ19; FHK22]),
• a posteriori error control for rough right-hand sides (cf. [KV21]).
This paper addresses this issue by designing a local Scott–Zhang type projection
operator Π0 (resp. Π) onto the Lagrange ﬁnite element space of arbitrary polynomial
degree k ∈N. The operator is stable and achieves optimal rates of convergence with
respect to Sobolev norms of order larger or equal to minus one as stated in our main
result in Theorem 1.
The construction of the operator and its analysis is carried out in Section 2 and
generalizes Tantardini’s approach for the lowest-order case k = 1 [Tan13, Sec. 7.6].
We modify the classical design of the celebrated Scott–Zhang projection operator
[SZ90] by using inner products with weights suited for functions in negative norm
Sobolev spaces. In particular, our weights are continuous, which allows testing with

Remark 4 (Other approaches). Classical Scott–Zhang type operators, such as the
one in [SZ90], use discontinuous weight functions and hence are not deﬁned on
W −1,2(Ω). In addition their adjoint operators do not preserve constants on interior
simplices as in (5), which is a key in the proof of optimal convergence rates in
Section 2.4 below. Operators that are better suited for approximation in W −1,2(Ω)
include the following.
• In [TV16, Thm. 5.2] Tantardini and Veeser design for arbitrary polyno-
mial degree k ∈N an operator with W −1,2(Ω)-approximability for W s,2(Ω)-
functions with s = 0, . . . , k + 1. However, the operator is not well-deﬁned
on W −1,2(Ω).
• In [Tan13, Sec. 7.6] Tantardini introduces an operator for k = 1 deﬁned
on W −1,2(Ω) with W −1,2-approximability for functions in W −1,2(Ω). This
operator is well-deﬁned on W −1,2(Ω) and coincides with our lowest-order
operator. Indeed, the reformulation of this operator inspires our general-
ization to k ≥1, see Remark 6.
• In [F¨uh22] F¨uhrer introduces several operators for the analysis of multilevel
preconditioning.
One of them is a projection onto the space L0
0(T ) and
allows for W −1,2(Ω)-approximability of functions in L2(Ω), see [F¨uh22,
Thm. 8]. This operator is W −1,2(Ω)-stable, but not W 1,2(Ω)-stable.
• In [SV20] Stevenson and van Veneti¨e develop an alternative projection for
the analysis of operator preconditioning. Their operator satisﬁes approx-
imation and stability estimates in W −1,2(Ω), but has a slightly increased
support. For a more detailed comparison see Remark 22.
• In [KV21] Kreuzer and Veeser introduce an operator that is locally W −1,2-
stable with local W −1,2-approximability.
The operator is a projection to
piecewise constant functions enriched by the span of face-supported Dirac
distributions.
Thus, it does not map to a polynomial space but to true
functionals (not representable by L2-densities).
A possible alternative technique to derive similar localized estimates as in Theorem 1
is discussed in [Vee19, Thm. 3].

Remark 9 (Preservation of constants by the adjoint). To conclude optimal con-
vergence rates in dual Sobolev spaces we have to take advantage of approximation
properties of the adjoint operator. Such properties would automatically be satisﬁed
by self-adjoint projection operators. However, in Section 3.1 below we argue that
our operator cannot be self-adjoint. The weaker condition (28) cures this lack of
self-adjointness. This weaker condition has been introduced for the lowest order
case in [Tan13, Prop. 7.22] and has also been employed by Tantardini and Veeser
in [TV16, Sec. 5]. Therein, they introduce for all polynomial degrees k ∈N a local
projection with optimal approximability in W −1,2(Ω) for functions in L2(Ω). How-
ever, they use discontinuous weight functions and consequently their operator does
not extend to W −1,2(Ω)-functions.

3.3. Locality and projection. Our operators Π0 and Π as well as further Scott–
Zhang type operators like the ones in [SZ90; TV16] are local projections that are
not self-adjoint. While the latter operators use discontinuous polynomial weights,
we use continuous weight functions to ensure that the operator is well-deﬁned on
functionals in W −1,2(Ω). Yet, the polynomial degree 3k of our weights is much
higher than the polynomial degree k of the discontinuous weights. A natural ques-
tion to ask is whether it is possible to reduce the polynomial order of the continuous
weight functions and obtain a local projection with properties as in Theorem 1. In
the following we argue that we need L1
k+1,0(T ) weights to ensure locality of the
projection. Furthermore, we illustrate that projections with similar locality and
W −1,2-approximation properties as Π0 require even larger degrees than k + 1. In
particular, we show that the degree 3k is optimal for k = 1 = d.

4.2. Interpolation on tensor meshes in space-time domains. So-called space-
