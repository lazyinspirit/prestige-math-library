## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\Gamma\le\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb R)$ be a supplied discrete torsion-free finite-coarea Fuchsian group. Write $H^+=\{\operatorname{Im}z>0\}$, $H^-=\{\operatorname{Im}z<0\}$ and $R=\mathbb R\cup\{\infty\}$. The reference Riemann surfaces and their holomorphic universal coverings are $X^\pm=H^\pm/\Gamma$ and $\pi^\pm:H^\pm\to X^\pm$. Supply their finite-type puncture compactifications and matching cusp labels. More explicitly, each puncture label $j$ specifies a conjugacy class of maximal parabolic subgroups $P_j=\langle\gamma_j\rangle$, its fixed point $\xi_j\in R$ up to $\Gamma$, and its puncture on each $X^\pm$; these are all the parabolic classes. Choose $\gamma_j$ positively around the upper puncture. The positive lower puncture loop then represents $\gamma_j^{-1}$. This cusp identification is part of the supplied marked reference model.

Let $Y^\pm$ be compact Riemann surfaces with finitely many labeled points removed, and let $m^\pm:X^\pm\to Y^\pm$ be supplied orientation-preserving quasiconformal homeomorphisms of finite maximal dilatations $K_\pm$. Require their extensions to the puncture compactifications to send every label to its corresponding label. The lower marking preserves the complex orientation of $H^-$; under reflection $H^+\to H^-$ it is the oppositely oriented surface marking.

There exist a normalized orientation-preserving quasiconformal sphere homeomorphism $f$ and a discrete faithful representation $\rho:\Gamma\to\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)$ such that $\rho(\gamma)=f\gamma f^{-1}$ and $K_f\le\max\{K_+,K_-\}$. The limit set of $G=\rho(\Gamma)$ is the quasicircle $C=f(R)$, and its full domain of discontinuity is the union of the two invariant simply connected domains $\Omega^\pm=f(H^\pm)$. Thus $G$ is quasifuchsian in this explicit sense, as well as being a quasiconformal deformation of the supplied cofinite Fuchsian group.

The induced quotient homeomorphisms $\overline f^\pm:X^\pm\to\Omega^\pm/G$ satisfy
$$I^\pm:=m^\pm\circ(\overline f^\pm)^{-1}:\Omega^\pm/G\longrightarrow Y^\pm,$$
where both $I^\pm$ are biholomorphic. In particular the two prescribed marked conformal boundary structures are realized simultaneously, with the exact markings $I^\pm\overline f^\pm=m^\pm$.

Parabolic elements are preserved in both directions. Each labeled maximal subgroup $P_j$ becomes the maximal parabolic subgroup $\rho(P_j)$ fixing $f(\xi_j)$, and its primitive marked generator is exactly $\rho(\gamma_j)$; the positive lower puncture generator remains its inverse. Under $I^\pm$ the puncture labels agree with the supplied labels on $Y^\pm$. No assertion of uniqueness on Teichmüller classes, holomorphic dependence, a skinning-map fixed point, or hyperbolization is included.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Full AC, the supplied cofinite torsion-free Fuchsian model with holomorphic quotient coverings and labeled cusp compactifications, and the two finite-dilatation quasiconformal markings extending the puncture labels.

[A1] AC supplies Countable Choice for the coefficient and quasiconformal suppliers and the countable chart localizations below. [[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]]

[F1] A Beltrami coefficient changes under a holomorphic coordinate map $\psi$ by $\mu(\psi z)\overline{\psi'(z)}/\psi'(z)$; its essential norm is unchanged. Sphere coefficients are the compatible almost-everywhere chart classes, with a strict norm bound below one. [[def-measurable-beltrami-coefficient]]

[F2] A bounded sphere Beltrami coefficient invariant under a discrete Möbius group has a normalized quasiconformal solution $f$ conjugating that group faithfully to a discrete Möbius group, preserving element orders and with $K_f\le(1+\|\mu\|_\infty)/(1-\|\mu\|_\infty)$. [[lem-invariant-beltrami-coefficients-deform-kleinian-groups]]

[F3] Quasiconformal inverses and compositions obey the almost-everywhere coefficient formulas, including exceptional-set transport. If two quasiconformal homeomorphisms on the same domain have the same coefficient, composing one with the other's inverse has zero coefficient and is $1$-quasiconformal; hence it is biholomorphic locally. [[thm-composition-and-inverse-quasiconformal]], [[thm-one-quasiconformal-is-conformal]]

## Proof

1.1 Let $\nu^\pm$ be the coefficient fields of $m^\pm$ on $X^\pm$, obtained in holomorphic source and target charts. A holomorphic change of target chart does not change the coefficient, and a change of source chart obeys [F1], so these are well-defined fields. Put $k_\pm=(K_\pm-1)/(K_\pm+1)$ and $k=\max\{k_+,k_-\}<1$. Pull $\nu^\pm$ back under the local holomorphic covering charts of $\pi^\pm$ to get $\widetilde\nu^\pm$ on $H^\pm$. On a sheet this is exactly the coefficient of $m^\pm\pi^\pm$. The formula is compatible on sheet intersections by [F1]; every local essential bound is $k_\pm$. Countable chart subcovers of the half-planes assemble the measurable fields and their exceptional null sets. For each $\gamma\in\Gamma$, $\pi^\pm\gamma=\pi^\pm$ gives $(\widetilde\nu^\pm\circ\gamma)\overline{\gamma'}/\gamma'=\widetilde\nu^\pm$ as coefficient classes. This is checked separately for each $\gamma$ and requires no simultaneous uncountable exceptional-set assertion. [A1, F1, F3, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 We give the group-action details that identify the full domain of discontinuity. A discrete subgroup of the finite-dimensional matrix group is closed: if distinct elements converged to any matrix-group point, quotients of consecutive convergent elements would be nonidentity elements tending to the identity, contradicting discreteness. Its intersection with a compact set is finite by the same subsequence argument. On the upper half-plane, a matrix sending $i$ to $x+iy$ is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\sqrt y&x/\sqrt y\\0&1/\sqrt y\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, up to the compact stabilizer $\mathrm{PSO}(2)$ of $i$. This exhibits the properness of the model action over compact subsets and shows that the orbit $\Gamma i$ has no accumulation in $H^+$. Its boundary accumulation set $\Lambda\subseteq R$ in the closed-disc compactification is closed and $\Gamma$-invariant. For any sequence of distinct elements, choose determinant-one real lifts $A_n$. Their matrix norms tend to infinity after passing to a subsequence, since a norm bound gives a compact set of determinant-one matrices. Normalize and pass to $A_n/\|A_n\|\to A\ne0$; its determinant is zero, so it has rank one. On the sphere the corresponding Möbius maps tend locally uniformly to the image line $a$ off the kernel line $b$. Both lines are real. Evaluating at $i$ gives $a\in\Lambda$, and applying the same calculation to inverses gives $b\in\Lambda$; for a two-by-two rank-one limit the inverse's image is the original kernel. Conversely every point of $\Lambda$ is an attracting line of such a subsequence. These elementary matrix limits fix the limit-set convention being used. [A1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Define a finite-chart coefficient $\mu$ by $\mu=\widetilde\nu^+$ on $H^+$, $\mu=\widetilde\nu^-$ on $H^-$ and $\mu=0$ on the real axis. The axis is area-null. Use the inversion pullback law in [F1] to define its infinity-chart class; the value at infinity is immaterial. Thus it is a sphere coefficient with $\|\mu\|_\infty\le k$. Every $\gamma\in\Gamma$ preserves both half-planes and their common real circle, so step 1.1 proves the required sphere invariance. Apply [F2] to obtain $f$ fixing $0,1,\infty$ and the faithful discrete representation $\rho(\gamma)=f\gamma f^{-1}$. Its image is torsion-free because element orders are preserved. The bound in [F2] gives $K_f\le(1+k)/(1-k)=\max\{K_+,K_-\}$. [A1, F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 Finite coarea forces $\Lambda=R$. First, if $\Lambda$ is empty, the proper orbit in step 1.2 is finite, because an infinite subset of the compact closed disc has an accumulation point. Its stabilizer lies in the compact rotation group and is finite by discreteness; torsion-freeness makes that stabilizer trivial. Thus $\Gamma$ is finite and hence trivial, whose hyperbolic-plane quotient has infinite area. If $\Lambda$ is one point, conjugate it to infinity. All group elements then have the form $z\mapsto az+b$ with $a>0$, $b$ real. If an element has $a\ne1$, it has a second, finite fixed point $c$. Any element not fixing $c$ has a nonidentity translation as its commutator with this element, since affine multipliers commute and affine maps with the same finite fixed point commute. Conjugating that translation by powers of the dilation shrinks it to the identity, impossible for a discrete group. Hence all elements would fix $c$; powers of the dilation then make $c$ a second orbit accumulation point, another contradiction. Therefore all elements are translations. Their discrete translation lengths form a cyclic additive subgroup of $\mathbb R$ (or zero); the quotient has infinite area, since the area integral on a fixed-width vertical strip is $\int_0^1y^{-2}dy=\infty$. These cases are excluded. Now suppose $\Lambda$ is a proper subset with at least two points. Choose a component interval $J$ of $R\setminus\Lambda$, and let $V_J$ be the open hyperbolic half-plane bounded by the geodesic joining its endpoints and facing $J$. Distinct translates of $J$ are distinct complementary intervals, so the corresponding open half-planes are disjoint. Consequently $V_J/\Gamma_J$ embeds in $H^+/\Gamma$, where $\Gamma_J$ stabilizes $J$. A real orientation-preserving Möbius map stabilizing this interval fixes its endpoints individually. Conjugate them to $0,\infty$, with $J$ the positive real interval. Then $V_J=\{\operatorname{Re}z>0,\operatorname{Im}z>0\}$ and $\Gamma_J$ consists of positive dilations. Discreteness makes it cyclic or trivial. In coordinates $z=r e^{i\theta}$ the area is $(dr/r)d\theta/\sin^2\theta$. A radial interval $1\le r\le a$ is a fundamental interval for a nontrivial dilation $r\mapsto ar$, and its area for $0<\theta<\pi/2$ is infinite; in the trivial case the subregion $1\le r\le2$ already has infinite area. This embedded infinite-area region contradicts finite coarea. All proper $\Lambda$ are excluded. [step 1.2, given, cases, construct, algebra]

3.1 Put $\Omega^\pm=f(H^\pm)$ and $C=f(R)$. These are two simply connected domains separated by the Jordan curve $C$, because $f$ is a sphere homeomorphism; $C$ is a quasicircle by its displayed quasiconformal parametrization. Conjugacy makes both domains invariant. The original deck actions on $H^\pm$ are free and properly discontinuous, and their conjugates have the same properties on $\Omega^\pm$. The quotient maps $q^\pm:\Omega^\pm\to\Omega^\pm/G$ are therefore unbranched coverings. Their Riemann surface charts are the charts of any sufficiently small injective sheet, with transition maps in $G$, hence holomorphic. The maps $f|_{H^\pm}$ descend to homeomorphisms $\overline f^\pm:X^\pm\to\Omega^\pm/G$ satisfying $q^\pm f=\overline f^\pm\pi^\pm$. No extension across the real circle is used in constructing these quotient complex structures. [step 2.1, given, construct, algebra]

4.1 The map $I^\pm=m^\pm(\overline f^\pm)^{-1}$ is a homeomorphism. On a small sheet its lift to $\Omega^\pm$ is $m^\pm\pi^\pm f^{-1}$. Restrict $\pi^\pm$ to an injective sheet and choose a target chart for $m^\pm$ there. The two local quasiconformal homeomorphisms $m^\pm\pi^\pm$ and $f$ on this source sheet have the same coefficient, namely $\widetilde\nu^\pm$, by steps 1.1 and 2.1. Their composition with $f^{-1}$ is therefore $1$-quasiconformal by [F3]. The same supplier makes it biholomorphic in these charts, including its inverse. As this is true around every point, $I^\pm$ are biholomorphic globally. Their definition gives the exact marked identities in the Statement. This argument uses local equal-coefficient cancellation, not a claimed global uniqueness theorem for maps on punctured surfaces. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

4.2 The boundary convergence limit set of $G$ is exactly $f(\Lambda)=C$. Indeed a rank-one subsequential convergence $\gamma_n\to a$ off $b$ from step 1.2 becomes $f\gamma_nf^{-1}\to f(a)$ locally uniformly off $f(b)$, by uniform continuity of sphere homeomorphisms on compact sets. Conversely, for any sequence of distinct elements in $G$, apply step 1.2 to its preimages and pass to that subsequence. Since the conjugates are Möbius, their own normalized complex matrix limits have rank one; testing at a point different from the two exceptional kernel points identifies their attracting line with $f(a)$. Thus there are no other attracting limits, and each point of $C$ occurs, by step 2.2. This is also the usual hyperbolic-orbit limit set: if $B_n/\|B_n\|\to B$ has rank one and $P$ is a positive Hermitian determinant-one matrix representing a point of $\mathbb H^3$, then the projective limit of $B_nPB_n^*$ is the nonzero rank-one matrix $BPB^*$, whose image is the attracting line. This calculation is the positive-Hermitian model of the Möbius action and shows equivalence of the two definitions here. Off $C$, the action is properly discontinuous by step 3.1. At $x\in C$, choose an attracting sequence with attracting point $x$ and exceptional point $y$. Every open neighborhood $U$ of $x$ contains a point $z\ne y$; eventually the images of $z$ lie in $U$, so infinitely many group elements have $gU\cap U\ne\varnothing$. Thus no point of $C$ belongs to the domain of discontinuity. The full domain is exactly $\Omega^+\sqcup\Omega^-$, as claimed. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 1.2, step 2.2, algebra]

5.1 For each $\gamma\ne1$, conjugation by a sphere homeomorphism identifies its fixed-point set exactly with the fixed-point set of $\rho(\gamma)$. A nonidentity Möbius transformation with one fixed point is parabolic: after moving that point to infinity, its formula is $z\mapsto az+b$, and having no finite fixed point forces $a=1$, $b\ne0$. Hence parabolics are preserved in both directions, without assuming differentiability at the boundary fixed point. A two-fixed-point nonidentity element of a discrete torsion-free Möbius group is loxodromic. After moving its fixed points to $0,\infty$, it is $z\mapsto az$; if $|a|=1$, then $a$ is either a root of unity, contrary to torsion-freeness, or its powers have a subsequence tending to one, contrary to discreteness. Thus no nonperipheral hyperbolic element becomes an accidental parabolic or an infinite-order elliptic. At a parabolic point normalized to infinity, a stabilizer element is an affine map $z\mapsto az+b$; here the original stabilizer is real, so $a>0$ and $a\ne1$ implies $|a|\ne1$. In that case, choose the sign of the powers so that $a^n\to0$; conjugating a fixed nontrivial cusp translation by those powers gives nonidentity translations tending to the identity. This is impossible by discreteness. Thus the original stabilizer consists of translations, and discreteness makes it cyclic; maximality of $P_j$ makes it the entire stabilizer. Conjugation identifies that whole stabilizer with the stabilizer of $f(\xi_j)$ in $G$, so $\rho(P_j)$ is the corresponding maximal cyclic parabolic subgroup and $\rho(\gamma_j)$ remains its primitive generator. [step 2.1, step 4.2, given, algebra]

6.1 Finally we verify the end markings, including the sign on the other side. In the supplied cusp normalization a positive upper generator is $z\mapsto z+1$. The upper puncture coordinate is $q_+=e^{2\pi iz}$ for $\operatorname{Im}z$ large positive; the lower coordinate is $q_-=e^{-2\pi iz}$ for $\operatorname{Im}z$ large negative. Increasing $\operatorname{Re}z$ by one gives winding $+1$ in $q_+$ and $-1$ in $q_-$. Thus positive loops on the two punctures represent $\gamma_j$ and $\gamma_j^{-1}$, respectively. The quotient homeomorphism $\overline f^\pm$ preserves the given deck marking because $f\gamma=\rho(\gamma)f$, and the equality $I^\pm\overline f^\pm=m^\pm$ from step 4.1 preserves each supplied puncture label and each of these signed peripheral classes. Transporting the finite puncture compactification of $X^\pm$ by $\overline f^\pm$ describes the end compactification of $\Omega^\pm/G$. The biholomorphism $I^\pm$ identifies each such end with the specified punctured coordinate disc on $Y^\pm$, and extends to its labeled missing point by the supplied extension of $m^\pm$. Hence no puncture is changed to a boundary circle or permuted, and no primitive cusp class is replaced by a multiple. This proves the exact puncture and parabolic assertions and completes the existence theorem. [step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, given, algebra] ∎

The actual Poincaré surface-model definition and closed-hyperbolic-surface deck-model theorem were also read; neither is used to generate a new cofinite group or the supplied puncture model. That source's existence construction is supplied locally here. Its stronger Bers identification and its Sullivan-rigidity, continuity and holomorphic-dependence claims are not used.

This supplies simultaneous uniformization existence for the two supplied quasiconformal marked structures, with exact peripheral marking. It proves neither the general pared-manifold deformation theorem nor a combination theorem, bounded-image theorem, skinning-map fixed point or generic Haken hyperbolization. The full generic hyperbolization proof still requires those separate established prerequisites to be authored and checked locally.
