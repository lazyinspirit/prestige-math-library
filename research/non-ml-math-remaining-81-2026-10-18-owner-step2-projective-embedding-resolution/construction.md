## Genuine scheme bridge for openness

The published embedding theorem proves a classical coordinate inclusion. Its homogeneous coordinate ring is not silently treated as a scheme closed-immersion theorem. This supplies the precise additional existing prerequisite for the draft openness proof.

For Plücker, work in a chart where a pivot p_I is invertible and normalize it to1. Define A from the signed one-outside Plücker coordinates. Apply a relation with tuples J minus an outside index j and I together with j. The coefficient of p_J is ±p_I=±1, and the other terms have fewer outside indices times a one-outside coordinate. Induction expresses every p_J as the corresponding minor of (I_r|A) **in the quotient ring**. Conversely all those minors satisfy the same relations as polynomial identities, over any coefficient algebra. Thus the maps from the free polynomial ring in A to the quotient chart ring and back by minors are inverse on their generators. This proves an actual isomorphism of rings, not a point bijection or a reduction argument. Pivot changes are the same localized invertible-minor changes of basis, so the charts glue to the Grassmannian scheme with its tautological subbundle; r=0,n gives a point.

For Segre, on the chart z_i0j0=1 set u_i=z_ij0 and v_j=z_i0j, normalizing the pivot coordinates to1. The minors give z_ij=u_i v_j inside the quotient ring. Substitution of products satisfies every minor, and extraction recovers the free u/v generators, so both maps are inverse. The homogeneous-ideal theorem therefore realizes this product as a genuine closed subscheme of projective space. Iteration gives the finite product of Grassmannians used in openness. These polynomial identities hold over any bar-k-algebra; in particular they persist over the affine representation coordinate ring. The bundle-map zero equations then define the closed incidence subscheme.
