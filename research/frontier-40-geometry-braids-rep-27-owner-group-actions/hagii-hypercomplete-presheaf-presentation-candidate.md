## 1. Exact underlying premises for the general local model

The full presheaf model requires the following non-additive simplicial-set foundation, denoted **KS**:

1. The Kan–Quillen model on all simplicial sets: cofibrations are monomorphisms; fibrations are horn maps; weak equivalences are homotopy-group isomorphisms after Kan replacement; a horn fibration is weak iff it has boundary lifting.
2. Ex∞ is a functorial Kan replacement with weak unit, preserves finite limits and filtered colimits, and every simplex/homotopy in Ex∞Y depends on finitely many original simplices of Y (Ex uses finite subdivisions, followed by a sequential union).
3. Weak equivalences are stable under pushout along a monomorphism and under transfinite monomorphic unions. The simplicial Cartesian corner/pushout-product axioms hold. Products/homotopy limits between Kan complexes have the corresponding derived mapping properties.

The existing packet proves additive analogues and the elementary product-horn combinatorics. It does not by itself prove KS1–KS3 for **arbitrary** simplicial sets. The exact source boundaries above also do not contain the recursively complete Kan–Quillen/Ex∞/left-properness proof. Therefore KS must either be supplied by an exact proved library/local supplier or remain an explicit foundational hold. Everything conditional on KS below is labelled accordingly. The ordinary stalk and hypercover-map statements for additive/Kan objects have their direct proofs and do not require the general model existence theorem.

## 2. Stalks, local homotopy sheaves and local finite liftings

For a simplicial presheaf F and x∈X, F_x=colim_{x∈U}F(U), degreewise. Finite limits and maps from finite simplicial sets commute with this filtered colimit: a finite compatible set of germs is represented on one common neighborhood, and equality of finitely many germs is enforced after one more shrinking.

If F is objectwise Kan, F_x is Kan: a horn uses finitely many simplices and equations, so represent it on a neighborhood and fill it there. For a basepoint germ represented by a vertex a∈F(U), maps from a finite simplicial sphere and their simplicial homotopies likewise descend to one neighborhood. Thus

π_n(F_x,a_x)=colim_{x∈V⊂U}π_n(F(V),a|V),

including π0. The homotopy sheaf on U has exactly this stalk. A map of sheaves of sets/groups is an isomorphism iff all stalk maps are: surjectivity is local representation of a germ and injectivity is local equality of two sections, after which the ordinary sheaf gluing axiom gives the inverse. Consequently for objectwise Kan presheaves, local homotopy-sheaf equivalence is exactly stalkwise weak equivalence. For arbitrary presheaves the same assertion follows under KS2, since Ex∞ commutes with the stalk colimit and its unit does not change the homotopy groups. In particular for all the simplicial ring/module objects of the previous packets, underlying additive Kan objects already make the first proof applicable.

A map p:F->G has local horn (respectively boundary) lifting iff each p_x has actual horn (respectively boundary) lifting. In one direction represent the finite stalk square on a neighborhood, enforce its commutativity there, and use the local lift. In the other direction, fix a section-level square on U; at every x∈U a stalk lift is represented on some V_x⊂U, and finitely many equations enforce the lift after shrinking. The V_x cover U and give the required local lifts, with no compatibility between different V_x asserted. Under KS1 it follows that local boundary lifting is exactly local horn lifting plus stalkwise weak equivalence. This is the enough-points version of DI7.2, with the proof given here rather than imported for all sites.

## 3. Hypercover maps are stalkwise trivial and admit bounded refinements

Let U•->h_W be a hypercover whose levels are coproducts of represented opens: each augmented matching map U_n->M_n(U/h_W) is a local epimorphism. The matching object uses only a finite number of faces in each fixed degree. Therefore its stalk equals the matching object of the stalk simplicial set. A local epimorphism of presheaves of sets gives a surjection on stalks by the local-section definition. Thus

U_x -> (h_W)_x

has boundary lifting in every degree. If x∉W both sides are empty; if x∈W the target is a point and U_x is a contractible trivial Kan fiber by the ordinary packet's explicit section/homotopy argument. This proves a hypercover map is stalkwise weak without an imported local model theorem. The same proof covers the generalized matching formulation of a hypercover.

Here is the matching construction behind DHI5.1/5.5, expanded in the present setting. If q:E->T has local boundary lifting and a hypercover U->h_W maps to T, construct a split hypercover V refining U with a compatible map V->E. Assume degrees below n already built. Local boundary lifting makes

E_n -> M_nE ×_{M_nT} T_n

a local epimorphism. Hypercover matching for U likewise supplies local degree-n choices over the chosen lower V data. Pull back these two matching maps to M_n(V/h_W), obtaining a presheaf Z with a local epimorphism Z->M_n(V/h_W) whose sections specify **both** a compatible U_n lift and an E_n lift. Cover each represented matching section by opens where those lifts exist. Take their coproduct as the new nondegenerate summands, and adjoin the already fixed latching/degenerate summands. Face maps are the prescribed matching data and degeneracies the latching inclusions. The matching equations ensure all simplicial identities; this is exactly the finite matching universal property. Iterate n. This gives a strict map V->E and V->U, and all matching maps of V are local epimorphisms.

For a finite monomorphism K⊂L, the cotensor corner of a map with local boundary lifting again has local boundary lifting: at each stalk it is a boundary map, since the transposed lifting inclusion is the product pushout of two monomorphisms and the ordinary boundary packet gives lifting against every monomorphism. Finite K,L permit passage through stalks. Consequently the preceding construction also makes a lift over a hypercover after refinement for any square K⊗U->F,L⊗U->G with p:F->G locally boundary-trivial. This is the exact finite hypercover-lifting result used below.

The class of hypercovers has a **set** of bounded split refinements. Choose an uncountable regular λ larger than the cardinalities of the site and its point set; increase it so every represented open has fewer than λ section simplices in the site. If V has fewer than λ summands through degree n, every section of its next matching presheaf is a finite tuple of those summands' sections, so there are fewer than λ such sections over all represented opens. Each can be covered by fewer than λ open arrows (a covering family is a subset of the fixed site's arrow set), and choose a lift for each. Thus the matching construction uses fewer than λ new summands. Latching pieces have the same bound. After countably many degrees the total remains below λ by uncountable regularity. Apply this to the local-boundary hypercover map U->h_W itself to obtain a bounded split V refining U. Up to fixed ordinal labels for the summand sets, all such data form a set. This proves density as well as sethood, including the cardinal step that DHI6.6 abbreviates. It uses AC for simultaneous choices. No localization-existence theorem is used.

## 4. Conditional existence of the local injective presheaf model

Under KS there is a cofibrantly generated model on sPre(Open X) with W=stalkwise weak equivalences and Cof=all monomorphisms. Its fibrations are the maps with RLP against the monomorphisms in W. These fibrations are global lifting maps; they are **not merely the stalkwise/local horn maps**.

We give the recognition details to make the local existence route explicit. Limits/colimits are pointwise. Every object is small for sufficiently large regular filtered colimits: choose a cardinal larger than the total number of its section simplices, the site's arrows and their operator relations; a natural transformation uses only that many choices/equalities, which settle at one stage. A monomorphism A⊂B is assembled from a set I of bounded monomorphisms. For one missing section simplex of B, close it under simplicial operators and restrictions, producing a bounded subpresheaf C. Its inclusion A∩C->C is bounded. Pushout adjoins A∪C inside B. Well-order missing section simplices and iterate; this writes A⊂B as an I-cell map. Choose isomorphism representatives of bounded maps for I; their total data form a set. Domains are small.

W has retract and two-out-of-three closure from its stalk definition. I-inj implies RLP against all monomorphisms, in particular h_U×∂Δ[n]->h_U×Δ[n]; by evaluation/adjunction it is objectwise boundary-trivial and hence stalkwise weak by KS. W∩Cof is stable under pushout and transfinite composition: stalks preserve these colimits and monomorphisms, and KS3 supplies the corresponding simplicial-set assertions.

Here is the solution-set condition, rather than an unproved appeal to accessibility. Given an I-square into a weak map f:F->G, start bounded subpresheaves F0,G0 containing its images and closed under f, restrictions and operators. Enlarge them to a bounded subarrow f∞ still stalkwise weak. At each stage and each point/basepoint, include witnesses for surjectivity of π0 and all πn of the current subarrow, and for injectivity whenever a current class maps to zero or two components become equal. The weak map on the **full** stalks supplies those witnesses. Each loop/path/nullhomotopy in Ex∞F or Ex∞G uses finitely many original simplices by KS2, represented on one neighborhood; adjoin those original simplices and their closures. There are fewer than λ witnesses per stage: the point set, dimensions and finite simplicial diagrams in the bounded subpresheaves all have that bound. Repeat countably. Every sphere, relation or homotopy in the union occurs at a finite stage (including a finite Ex iteration), so the next stages supplied its witness. Thus f∞ is weak on every stalk. Its total size is bounded. Isomorphism representatives of all such bounded weak subarrows form a set, and the original I-square factors through one of them. This proves the required solution set.

Apply the following explicit Smith construction, whose complete proof was read in Beke1.7–1.9. For every I-square into one of the solution-set weak maps w, push out I to form its corner and I-factor that corner as cell(I) followed by I-inj. The composite from the original source into that intermediate object is a monomorphism and is weak by two-out-of-three, since the last map is I-inj and the original w is weak. Let J be the **set** of those composites. Every square from I into W factors through J. Starting with any weak f, at successive stages attach the J-maps interpolating all I-squares into the remaining weak map; pushout/transfinite closure preserves weakness of the initial composite, so the remaining map stays weak. After a regular smallness stage it is I-inj. A weak cofibration is consequently a retract of a J-cell map. Hence cof(J)=Cof∩W. Small-object factorizations for I and J, the retract argument and two-out-of-three now give all model axioms. This proof uses no general accessible-category or Bousfield-localization theorem beyond the explicit size/solution-set arguments just supplied.

The analogous objectwise injective model is obtained by the same argument with section evaluations instead of stalks in the witness enlargement. The site is small, so the same cardinal bounds work. It is used for the hypercover fibrancy comparison below. Thus its existence is also conditional only on KS, not an additional unproved diagram-model theorem.

The local model is simplicial. The corner proof transposes mapping-space horn/boundary problems to pushout products. A mono with a horn gives an objectwise anodyne inclusion by the finite product-horn proof already supplied, hence an objectwise weak map under KS1 and therefore a local weak mono. If one of the monos is locally weak, the boundary-product map is stalkwise weak by the Cartesian corner property in KS3. Global fibration lifting against these local weak monos proves SM7. This is exactly where the non-additive Cartesian weak-equivalence part of KS3, beyond the earlier horn combinatorics, is used.

## 5. Conditional hypercover characterization, with the recognition proof

Under the local/objectwise models just constructed, the locally fibrant objects are precisely the **objectwise injective-fibrant** presheaves F satisfying

F(W) ≃ holim_{[n]∈Δ} ∏_{α} F(U_{n,α})

for every hypercover U->h_W (or the dense bounded split set of Section3). The product is over its represented summands. Using the local projective presentation instead changes “injective-fibrant” to “objectwise Kan”; it does not make these two fibrancy conditions identical.

For the forward direction, a hypercover map U->h_W is local weak by Section3. All objects are cofibrant in the injective model, so maps into a fibrant F give Map(h_W,F)≃Map(U,F). The right side is the displayed homotopy totalization: write U as the realization of its simplicial diagram of discrete levels. Its latching inclusion is objectwise a monomorphism (the degenerate simplices), hence that diagram is Reedy cofibrant. The tensor/coend adjunction identifies its mapping object with totalization, and SM7 makes the corresponding cosimplicial mapping object Reedy fibrant. Thus this is its derived limit. In particular dense refinements suffice by the same realization and weak-map argument.

Here is a converse proof avoiding Hirschhorn's localization-existence import. Suppose F is injective-fibrant and has all hypercover descent. Factor F->* in the local model as a local weak mono F->HF followed by a local fibration. HF is also injective-fibrant (objectwise weak monos are among local weak monos) and has hypercover descent by the forward implication. Factor F->HF in the **objectwise injective** model as an objectwise weak mono F->Z and an injective fibration p:Z->HF. Z is injective-fibrant, is locally weakly equivalent to HF and has hypercover descent, since F->Z is objectwise weak and homotopy totalization preserves equivalences between the corresponding Kan diagrams.

For an arbitrary boundary square for p over a represented W, p is a local horn map and stalkwise weak, hence locally boundary-trivial by Section2. Section3 produces a hypercover U->h_W on which that square has an actual lift. The cotensor corner

Z^{Δ[n]} -> (HF)^{Δ[n]}×_{(HF)^{∂Δ[n]}}Z^{∂Δ[n]}

is an injective fibration, with both ends injective-fibrant. Both ends also have hypercover descent: cotensors are homotopy right adjoints, and the displayed pullback is a homotopy pullback because the corner factors are fibrations; homotopy limits commute with these constructions. Mapping h_W and U into this corner gives a square of Kan spaces whose vertical maps are fibrations and whose horizontal maps are equivalences by descent. The specified lower-left vertex has a lift on the right. Equivalence on components shows some vertex in its component lifts on the left; a Kan fibration lifts edges, so the specified vertex itself lifts. This fills the original boundary square. Thus p is objectwise boundary-trivial and F->HF is objectwise weak by two-out-of-three.

The latter map is also a mono, so it is an objectwise acyclic cofibration. Injective fibrancy of F gives a retraction HF->F. Consequently F is a retract of the locally fibrant HF and is locally fibrant. This proves the converse and the exact characterization. The mapping-corner component argument is DHI6.4, whose full source proof was read; the refinements are constructed explicitly in Section3.

Calling this model “hypercomplete” means that all homotopy-sheaf equivalences are inverted and fibrant objects satisfy **all hypercover** descent. It is not enough to check Čech descent or only a finite principal-cover complex for arbitrary space-valued sheaves. DHI's appendix explicitly distinguishes these assertions. This packet makes no additional assertion that an arbitrary ordinary presheaf equals the inverse limit of its sectionwise Postnikov truncations after sheafification.

## 6. Conditional sheaf presentation and comparison with the cotangent candidate

Degreewise sheafification a is left exact and left adjoint to inclusion i. The unit F->iaF is an isomorphism on every degreewise stalk: germs of sheafified sections are represented locally by original sections, and equality of germs is local equality. Therefore it is a local weak map. The counit aiE=E is strict.

Under KS the same recognition proof yields a model on simplicial sheaves with weak maps detected on stalks and cofibrations all sheaf monomorphisms. Generate them by a(I): an inclusion of sheaves has an underlying presheaf mono; its bounded presheaf-cell decomposition sheafifies to the original inclusion. Sheafification preserves monos and all colimits. Smallness follows by choosing regular cardinals above the site and covering-data size: such filtered colimits commute with the sheaf equalizer/products defining every covering condition and are computed sectionwise, so Hom from a bounded generating presheaf stabilizes by adjunction. The presheaf bounded-witness solution set sheafifies to a solution set of weak maps; sheafification has identical stalks and so preserves/reflection of weakness. The same Smith proof applies. No assertion about all topoi or absence of enough points is needed.

The adjunction a⊣i is then enriched Quillen because a preserves monos and local weak monos. Its unit is local weak on all objects and its counit is identity. Consequently it is a Quillen equivalence, with actual derived mapping-space comparison after model replacements by SM7. This proves a local simplicial-presheaf/sheaf presentation **conditional on KS**. It does not say i(E) is already injective/local fibrant just because E is a degreewise sheaf.

The strict cotangent sheaf K in `hagii-localization-cotangent-gluing-candidate.md` has the calculated stalks and affine comparisons. Its presheaf and sheaf versions consequently represent the same **underlying local homotopy object** under this conditional presentation: sheafification's unit is stalkwise weak and the cotangent/germ comparison is stalkwise weak. This closes the underlying-space comparison if KS and the chosen strict chart presentation are supplied.

It is not yet the needed global derived **ring/module** adjunction. A model on simplicial presheaves/sheaves of sets does not automatically transfer to sheaves of simplicial commutative rings or modules over the variable sheaf O_X. Global fibrancy means compatible lifting/descent, while additive Kan fibrancy of each stalk only gives local lifts. Thus the affine path-transfer argument cannot simply be reused with “all global objects fibrant.” One must construct the local ring/module models and their derived enriched adjunctions, or another exact sheaf-mapping/totalization construction for the chosen cotangent resolution. Local weak equivalences must be detected through that transfer, and derived coefficient extension and square-zero extension must satisfy the required Quillen compatibility. Only then does the strict sheaf universal differential adjunction compute global derived derivations. Stalkwise cofibrancy of P or K does not prove global cofibrancy or global representability.

## Additional commissioned full-module interface

The cotangent K constructed in the affine and strict-sheaf candidates is a simplicial module, hence a **connective** module object. Its underlying local space-valued sheaf and its connective stalk comparisons are not yet its realization in the commissioned **full stable derived sheaf-module framework**. The latter admits arbitrary degree shifts and nonconnective test modules; HAGII's sheaf-level cotangent discussion uses stable module categories (Sp(A-Mod)). The present space-valued local model does not construct that stable category, its coefficient/sheaf pullback, or the functor embedding connective simplicial modules with the correct mapping spectra.

A completed route must either (a) construct the stabilized local O_X-module model, prove its connective inclusion/realization is compatible with the chosen affine models and coefficients, and prove the full stable derived sheaf derivation adjunction; or (b) give a fully proved comparison with an explicitly constructed unbounded derived sheaf-module category, including the variable simplicial ring action and its tensor/mapping compatibility. Additive Dold–Kan for a fixed ordinary ring is insufficient for (b), and a nonnegative simplicial module is not by itself an unbounded dg module over an arbitrary variable simplicial ring. Sheaf homotopy groups or stalk weak equivalences do not supply either comparison.

Accordingly the precise target-specific hold includes **realization of K in the full stable sheaf-module category and its universal property for that category's test modules**, in addition to KS/local presentation and global ring/module mapping transfer. Nothing in the conditional presheaf/sheaf-space Quillen comparison above clears this stable realization premise.
