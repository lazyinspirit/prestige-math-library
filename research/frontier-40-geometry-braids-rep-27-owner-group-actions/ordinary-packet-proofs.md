## def-simplicial-set-homotopy-and-trivial-kan-fibration

### Statement

A simplicial set is a contravariant functor from the simplex category $\Delta$ to sets ([[def-simplicial-object-and-simplicial-commutative-ring]], [[def-functor-and-contravariant-functor]]). Put $\Delta[n]_k=\operatorname{Hom}_\Delta([k],[n])$. Its boundary $\partial\Delta[n]$ consists of the non-surjective maps $[k]\to[n]$; $\partial\Delta[0]$ is empty. Products and pullbacks of simplicial sets are computed degreewise. A simplicial homotopy from $f$ to $g$ is a map $H:X\times\Delta[1]\to Y$ restricting to $f,g$ at the two vertices. A simplicial set is contractible here when it is homotopy equivalent in this sense to the one-point constant simplicial set. A map $p:X\to Y$ is a trivial Kan fibration when every square with left side $\partial\Delta[n]\hookrightarrow\Delta[n]$, $n\ge0$, admits a diagonal lift; in degree zero this means $p_0$ is surjective. Thus this term specifies boundary lifting, not merely a quasi-isomorphism of complexes.

### Proof / construction

These are the exact Stacks definitions: representable simplices and their boundaries, simplicial-set homotopy, and Definition14.30.1. Face and degeneracy maps are obtained from the elementary order maps in the existing simplicial-object definition. The equality of the boundary with non-surjective maps follows by factorizing a non-surjective order map through a proper face; a surjective map contains the distinguished nondegenerate n-simplex and is outside the boundary. No AC is required for these definitions.

## lem-trivial-simplicial-fibration-fibres-products-and-contraction

### Statement

Assume AC ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). A trivial Kan fibration of simplicial sets ([[def-simplicial-set-homotopy-and-trivial-kan-fibration]]) lifts every degreewise injective map. It is stable under pullback and set-indexed products. Every fibre over a vertex of a constant target is nonempty and contractible, and every set-indexed product of such fibres is nonempty and contractible. In particular, a trivial Kan fibration is a simplicial homotopy equivalence.

### Proof / construction

Proof. Every simplex has a unique nondegenerate ancestor: if $x=\alpha^*y=\beta^*z$ with alpha,beta surjective and y,z nondegenerate, take an order-preserving section xi of beta. Nondegeneracy forces alpha xi surjective, so dim(z)>=dim(y); reversing the roles gives equality. Thus alpha xi is the identity for every section xi of beta, forcing alpha=beta and y=z. Existence follows by removing degeneracies until dimension decreases to a nondegenerate simplex. Consequently adjoining a missing simplex of smallest dimension together with its degeneracies is precisely the pushout of a simplex along its boundary. For a lifting problem Z subset W, well-order the missing nondegenerate simplices by dimension and then within each dimension, using AC, and successively choose a boundary lift. At limit stages take the union. Unique nondegenerate ancestors ensure no conflicting degeneracy assignments. This proves lifting against every monomorphism. Pullback stability is the fibre-product universal property. For products solve the boundary problem coordinatewise and use AC to choose the set-indexed family of lifts. The empty product is the point. Lifting the empty subobject into Y produces a section q:Y->X. Lift $X\times\partial\Delta[1]\subseteq X\times\Delta[1]$ over Y with endpoint maps id_X and qp; the resulting H gives a homotopy from id_X to qp, while pq=id_Y. Thus p is a homotopy equivalence. A fibre is the pullback of p along a point, hence a trivial fibration over a point, and its products are likewise trivial fibrations over a point. This proves the contraction assertion. AC is used exactly in the well-order/transfinite lift selections and arbitrary product lift selection; no model-category theorem is invoked.

## lem-simplicial-normalization-prism-and-trivial-fibration-criterion

### Statement

For a simplicial abelian group M, put $s(M)_n=M_n$, $d=\sum_{i=0}^n(-1)^id_i$, with differential zero out of degree zero; put $N(M)_n=\bigcap_{i<n}\ker d_i$ and differential $(-1)^nd_n$ ([[def-chain-complex-in-an-abelian-category]], [[def-simplicial-object-and-simplicial-commutative-ring]]). Inclusion $N(M)\to s(M)$ is a natural chain homotopy equivalence. A simplicial-set homotopy induces a chain homotopy on free abelian or free R-module chains. If a homomorphism of simplicial abelian groups is a homotopy equivalence of underlying simplicial sets, its associated chain map is a quasi-isomorphism. A termwise surjective homomorphism inducing a quasi-isomorphism of associated complexes is a trivial Kan fibration ([[def-simplicial-set-homotopy-and-trivial-kan-fibration]]).

### Proof / construction

Proof. Define $p_{n,0}=1$, $p_{n,i}=(1-s_{i-1}d_{i-1})\cdots(1-s_0d_0)$ for 1<=i<=n, and $p_n=p_{n,n}$. Applying the factors successively kills d_0,...,d_{n-1}; the earlier killed faces remain zero by the simplicial identities. Thus p_n maps to N(M)_n and is the identity there. The same identities give a chain projection p and, for $H_n=\sum_{i=0}^n(-1)^i s_i p_{n,i}$, the identity $dH+Hd=1-\iota p$. To verify it, expand each differential into its faces; the pairs at positions i and i+1 cancel using d_i s_i=d_{i+1}s_i=1, the faces before i commute to the earlier projection factors, and the faces after i commute past s_i. The surviving sum telescopes to $1-p_{n,n}$. This proves normalization and also its functoriality; hence N is exact, since its natural direct-summand projection commutes with every simplicial homomorphism and gives lifts for any degreewise epimorphism. For a homotopy of simplicial sets, the prism maps $h_i(x)=H_{n+1}(s_i x,(0,...,0,1,...,1))$, with i+1 zeros, give $\sum_i(-1)^ih_i$ on free chains. Expanding its boundary, the internal faces cancel in adjacent prism terms and the endpoint faces give g-f. Thus free chains preserve homotopy equivalences. To pass from underlying-set homotopy equivalence f:M->M' to group homology, compare the free abelian simplicial groups Z[M] and Z[M'] with their evaluation maps to M,M'. Every normalized cycle x lifts to the normalized free cycle [x]-[0]. If f(x) bounds, a normalized bounding y in M' lifts to [y]-[0] whose boundary, up to the displayed normalization sign, is [f(x)]-[0]. The free map is a homology isomorphism by the prism argument. Lifting a free cycle proves surjectivity on group homology; a lifted cycle mapping to this free boundary proves injectivity. For the final lifting criterion, let f:M->M' be termwise epic with acyclic kernel K. A boundary-lifting problem in degree n first lifts its target simplex and subtracts that lift from the prescribed faces, reducing to K->0. In degree zero choose a lift directly. For n>=1 fill the first n faces inductively: begin u=s_0x_0 and replace u by $u+s_r(x_r-d_ru)$ for r=1,...,n-1. The identities and face compatibility preserve the previously filled faces. The remaining discrepancy z=x_n-d_nu has every face zero, hence is a normalized cycle in K_{n-1}. Normalization and acyclicity give w in N(K)_n with $(-1)^nd_nw=z$. Then u+(-1)^nw fills the last face while leaving the others fixed. This supplies every boundary lift, proving the exact trivial-Kan criterion without an ambient model category.

## lem-standard-polynomial-resolution-admissibility

### Statement

Let A->B be a map of commutative unital rings and $P_n=(A[-]\,U)^{n+1}(B)$ its standard polynomial simplicial resolution ([[def-standard-resolution-of-a-ring-map]]), where U forgets the algebra. Its augmentation P->B is termwise surjective, a homotopy equivalence of underlying simplicial sets over the constant set B, and a trivial Kan fibration. Its associated A-module complex is a free resolution of B, so it is an admissible polynomial resolution for computing cotangent complexes.

### Proof / construction

Proof. The free-forgetful unit sends an underlying element x to its polynomial variable [x], and the counit evaluates variables. On the augmented underlying simplicial set, define the extra degeneracy t:P_n->P_{n+1} by x->[x], including B->P_0. The adjunction triangle and naturality give $d_0t=1$, $d_{i+1}t=td_i$, $s_{i+1}t=ts_i$ and $s_0t=tt$, with their augmented interpretations. These identities can also be checked directly on the nested polynomial expressions defining P. For an order map alpha:[n]->[1] with r initial zeros, use $t^r d_0^r$ (using augmentation if r=n+1). These maps commute with every order map by the four identities, and define a homotopy over B between the constant section b->[...[b]...] and id_P. Augmentation followed by that section is therefore homotopic to identity; the other composite is identity. Every augmentation is surjective since nested variables lift every b. Apply [[lem-simplicial-normalization-prism-and-trivial-fibration-criterion]]: underlying-set homotopy equivalence gives a quasi-isomorphism on associated abelian-group complexes, and termwise surjectivity then gives a trivial Kan fibration. Each P_n is a free A-module on its monomials, so the associated complex, cohomologically reindexed in nonpositive degrees, is the claimed free resolution. The extra degeneracy is a set map, not an algebra-linear chain contraction; the normalization/prism lemma is what justifies the passage to module homology.

## def-polynomial-factorization-category-and-cotangent-diagram

### Statement

Assume AC for the size bounds and transported polynomial presentations ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Fix a map A->B of commutative unital rings and an infinite cardinal kappa at least the sizes of A,B and of the variable sets in the specified countable polynomial resolutions. The small category $C_{B/A}^{\kappa}$ is opposite to the category whose objects are polynomial presentations $A[E]\to B$ with |E|<=kappa (using ordinal representatives and transported specified presentations) and whose arrows are A-algebra maps commuting with augmentation ([[def-polynomial-ring-on-a-family-of-indeterminates]], [[def-category]]). Its cotangent diagram is the contravariant functor to B-modules $F_{B/A}(P\to B)=\Omega_{P/A}\otimes_PB$ ([[def-kahler-differentials-algebra]], [[def-functor-and-contravariant-functor]]). A simplicial polynomial resolution yields a cosimplicial object of this opposite category. A commutative ring square with A',B' gives the functor $P\mapsto P\otimes_AA'$ to the corresponding bounded category over A'->B', after taking a common bound.

### Proof / construction

Use the actual polynomial-factorization category of Stacks92.4, with a small bounded presentation model. There is a set of objects: ordinals lambda<=kappa, polynomial rings on lambda, and all functions lambda->B specifying their augmentations. Each hom collection is a set of assignments of the source variables to target polynomials satisfying the augmentation condition. Enlarge by the specified countable presentations or transport them degreewise to these models; conjugating all face/degeneracy maps by those algebra isomorphisms preserves the simplicial identities. A suitable infinite kappa exists under AC by bounding the countable union of the given variable sets and A,B. Since a polynomial ring on <=kappa variables over a ring of size <=kappa has size <=kappa, the standard resolution also fits at every stage. This introduces no universe axiom. Differential functoriality gives the stated contravariant module diagram and polynomial differential modules are free. Tensoring a polynomial presentation changes its coefficient ring and preserves the same variable set. Independence of further bound enlargement follows from the coefficient/category-change lemma below when a common standard resolution computes both bounded categories; no unbounded category colimit is being assumed.

## lem-projective-representables-and-derived-colimits-of-module-diagrams

### Statement

Assume AC. For a small category C and commutative ring R, the category $\mathcal A_R(C)=\operatorname{Fun}(C^{op},R\text{-Mod})$ is abelian with pointwise exactness. The diagrams $R_U=R[\operatorname{Hom}_C(-,U)]$ are projective and every diagram has a canonical epimorphism from a direct sum of them. Bounded-above projective replacements can therefore be supplied in this category, and $L\operatorname{colim}_{C^{op}}$ exists there using the published supplied-replacement derived-functor interfaces. Moreover $\operatorname{colim}R_U=R$, and for a diagram F in degree zero its derived colimit is computed by the bar complex $K_n(F)=\bigoplus_{U_n\to...\to U_0}F(U_0)$ with alternating face differential (last face uses restriction of coefficients).

### Proof / construction

Proof. Kernels, cokernels, finite sums and images are computed objectwise; their structure maps are induced by those of the diagrams, proving all abelian axioms and pointwise exactness. Sending a natural map R_U->F to the image of [id_U] gives Hom(R_U,F)=F(U), with inverse [a:V->U]->F(a)(s). Evaluation is exact, so R_U is projective. A direct sum of these objects is projective under AC by choosing lifts of all component maps. The epimorphism $\bigoplus_{U,s\in F(U)}R_U\to F$ sends the basis a to F(a)(s); at V, its summand (V,s) and id_V lift every s, proving epimorphy. Repeating this construction on successive kernels supplies projective resolutions. For bounded-above complexes, apply [[lem-bounded-above-complexes-admit-projective-replacements]] with these specified representable-sum epimorphisms; AC supplies its DC/lifting assumptions. The hypotheses of [[thm-projective-complexes-model-the-bounded-above-derived-category]] and [[thm-existence-of-the-bounded-above-left-total-derived-functor]] are thereby instantiated, rather than assumed for the diagram category. Apply colimit to the supplied projective models. Each generator [a:V->U] in R_U identifies in its colimit with [id_U], while the augmentation sending every [a] to 1 shows this generator has no extra relation; hence colim R_U=R. Finally K is an exact functor from diagrams to chain complexes, since each degree is a direct sum of evaluations. For R_U its basis is a chain U_n->...->U_0 together with a map U_0->U. Appending that final map and U gives an extra degeneracy; with sign (-1)^{n+1} it contracts the augmented complex onto R. Thus K(P) has no higher homology for every projective representable sum P. For a projective resolution G_p->F, the first-quadrant double complex K_q(G_p) augments in one direction to K(F), with exact rows by pointwise exactness, and in the other to colim G, with exact columns by the contraction. Finite-diagonal totalization therefore makes both maps quasi-isomorphisms: kill a cycle's component of largest positive row/column index by an augmented exactness lift and descend the index until it is zero. This proves K(F) computes the derived colimit canonically and functorially. The bar description also gives the canonical change-of-category map by applying a functor to each chain. The representable-sum epimorphism at every replacement stage is defined from all elements of the required diagram; this supplies a uniform objectwise replacement assignment and does not assume a choice function on a proper class. Homotopy-class comparison arrows between its models are supplied by projective lifting under AC and uniquely determined in the localized category.

## lem-contractible-cosimplicial-evaluation-computes-derived-colimit

### Statement

Assume AC. Let C be small, R commutative, and U:Delta->C cosimplicial. Suppose each simplicial set $n\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_C(U_n,V)$, V in C, is contractible ([[def-simplicial-set-homotopy-and-trivial-kan-fibration]]). Then, for every contravariant R-module diagram F, the simplicial module chain complex F(U_bullet) is canonically isomorphic to $L\operatorname{colim}_{C^{op}}F$ in D(R). This is functorial in F. The isomorphism is a canonical derived-category roof from projective resolutions; contraction choices are only used to prove its arrows are quasi-isomorphisms.

### Proof / construction

Proof. Use the supplied representable-sum projective resolution G_p->F of [[lem-projective-representables-and-derived-colimits-of-module-diagrams]], and form the first-quadrant chain double complex A_{p,q}=G_p(U_q), signed on one differential so the total differential squares to zero. For each q, evaluation is exact, so G_p(U_q)->F(U_q) is an augmented exact row. For each p, write G_p as a direct sum of R_V. Then G_p(U_bullet) is a direct sum of the free R-module chains on Hom_C(U_bullet,V); every summand augments to R and is contractible on chains by the prism argument of [[lem-simplicial-normalization-prism-and-trivial-fibration-criterion]]. Its degree-zero augmentation is the map to colim R_V=R, so G_p(U_bullet)->colim G_p is an augmented exact column. Both augmentations give a roof $F(U_\bullet)\leftarrow\operatorname{Tot}A\to\operatorname{colim}G_\bullet$. The maps are quasi-isomorphisms by finite-diagonal elimination: in the augmented cone, the component of a cycle at the largest positive column (respectively row) index is a cycle for the other differential; lift it by exactness, subtract its total boundary, and repeat down to zero. Every total degree has finitely many indices, so the process terminates; no convergence assumption is imported. The right-hand complex computes the derived colimit by the preceding lemma. Comparison lifts between projective resolutions preserve the augmentation roof and are unique up to homotopy; the supplied projective-model theorem makes this roof independent of replacement choices. Naturality in F follows from those comparison lifts. Thus the isomorphism is canonical in D(R), even though choices of contractions were used to prove exactness.

## lem-derived-colimit-coefficient-and-category-change

### Statement

Assume AC. For small C and a commutative ring map R->R', there is a canonical isomorphism $L\operatorname{colim}_{C^{op}}F\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}R'\cong L\operatorname{colim}_{C^{op}}(F\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}R')$ for bounded-above R-module diagram complexes F, where the right-hand scalar extension is computed pointwise. For a degree-zero pointwise-flat diagram it is ordinary pointwise tensor. If u:D->C is a functor and W_bullet is cosimplicial in D such that Hom_D(W_bullet,V) and Hom_C(uW_bullet,U) are contractible for all V,U, then the canonical change-of-category map $L\operatorname{colim}_{D^{op}}u^*F\to L\operatorname{colim}_{C^{op}}F$ is an isomorphism for every degree-zero contravariant R-module diagram F.

### Proof / construction

Proof. Choose the representable-sum projective replacement G of F from [[lem-projective-representables-and-derived-colimits-of-module-diagrams]]. Each G^n evaluates to free R-modules, G tensor_R R' computes the derived scalar extension objectwise, and its terms are sums of the R'-representables. Thus the right-hand derived colimit is colim(G tensor_R R'), equal to (colim G) tensor_R R' because colimits commute with coefficient tensor. Every term of colim G is free R, since colim R_U=R, so the latter tensor also computes the left-hand derived scalar extension. This proves the canonical isomorphism on the actual common projective model; bounded-above flat tensor invariance ([[lem-bounded-above-flat-tensor-complexes-preserve-quasi-isomorphisms]]) proves independence of the model. If F is pointwise flat, objectwise tensor of its projective resolution is exact, so derived scalar extension agrees with ordinary tensor. For the category part, apply [[lem-contractible-cosimplicial-evaluation-computes-derived-colimit]] first to W in D and then to uW in C. Both derived colimits are computed by the same complex F(uW_bullet). The comparison is the identity on this complex: the canonical comparison is obtained by applying u to chains in the bar description of the preceding projective lemma, and the projective/evaluation augmentation roofs commute with those maps. Equivalently lift a projective resolution of u^*F to the restricted projective resolution of F, apply the two augmentation roofs, and use uniqueness up to homotopy to obtain that identity. This proves the comparison is an isomorphism canonically, not an arbitrary existence of isomorphic objects. All tensors here are ordinary module-diagram derived tensors; no simplicial-ring model structure or Quillen adjunction is assumed.

## lem-cotangent-complex-resolution-independence

### Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice for the published derived-tensor and resolution-comparison suppliers ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $A\to B$ be a map of commutative unital rings. Let $P_\bullet\to B$ and $Q_\bullet\to B$ be polynomial simplicial $A$-algebra resolutions with augmentations that are trivial Kan fibrations of simplicial sets, in the sense of [[def-simplicial-set-homotopy-and-trivial-kan-fibration]] and Stacks92.5.5; the standard resolution is one such resolution ([[def-standard-resolution-of-a-ring-map]], [[def-simplicial-object-and-simplicial-commutative-ring]]). Their complexes $\Omega_{P_\bullet/A}\otimes_{P_\bullet}B$ and $\Omega_{Q_\bullet/A}\otimes_{Q_\bullet}B$ have canonical identifications with $L_{B/A}$ in $D(B)$ ([[def-cotangent-complex-of-a-ring-map]], [[def-derived-category-of-an-abelian-category]], [[def-quasi-isomorphism]]); hence they are canonically isomorphic there. The canonical comparison is in the derived category and need not be a distinguished direct chain map between the two chosen complexes. For any commutative square of ordinary ring maps $A\to B$, $A'\to B'$, $A\to A'$ and $B\to B'$, functoriality gives a canonical comparison $L_{B/A}\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}B'\to L_{B'/A'}$. If the square induces a quasi-isomorphism $B\otimes_A^{\mathbf L}A'\to B'$, this comparison is an isomorphism in $D(B')$. Equivalently, for the ordinary pushout $B'=B\otimes_AA'$, it suffices that $\operatorname{Tor}_i^A(B,A')=0$ for all $i>0$; in particular flat $A\to A'$ suffices. If one writes $B'=B\otimes_A^{\mathbf L}A'$, this ordinary-ring statement applies when that derived tensor product is concentrated in degree zero. There is also the valid same-target special case: for composable maps $A\to A'\to B$ such that the canonical map $B\otimes_A^{\mathbf L}A'\to B$ is a quasi-isomorphism, $L_{B/A}\cong L_{B/A'}$ in $D(B)$, for example for a localization $A'=S^{-1}A$ through which $A\to B$ factors. Cohomology and degree shifts are those of [[def-homology-object-of-a-chain-complex]] and [[def-shift-of-a-chain-complex]]. Derived module tensor and its Tor test use [[def-derived-tensor-product-in-the-bounded-above-setting]] and [[prop-homology-of-the-derived-tensor-product-is-tor]]; AC implies the latter supplier's Dependent Choice assumption.

### Proof / construction

Full local proof, under the stated AC. 1. Choose a bounded small polynomial-factorization category [[def-polynomial-factorization-category-and-cotangent-diagram]] containing the standard resolution and the specified P,Q. For every object A[E]->B, maps into an admissible resolution are precisely the product, over e in E, of its augmentation fibers over the images of e. These fibers and their arbitrary products are contractible by [[lem-trivial-simplicial-fibration-fibres-products-and-contraction]]. Hence the hypotheses of [[lem-contractible-cosimplicial-evaluation-computes-derived-colimit]] hold for each of the three resolutions. Apply that lemma to the cotangent diagram F(P->B)=Omega_P/A tensor_P B. Evaluation on the standard resolution is the definition of L_B/A, and evaluation on P or Q is their stated complex. The three canonical augmentation roofs into the same diagram derived colimit give the required canonical isomorphisms in D(B). The standard resolution is genuinely admissible by [[lem-standard-polynomial-resolution-admissibility]], whose underlying contraction is passed to module homology by [[lem-simplicial-normalization-prism-and-trivial-fibration-criterion]]. Enlargement of the bound is harmless by the category-change lemma with the shared standard resolution. 2. For a commutative ordinary ring square, use the functor u:P->P tensor_A A' between the bounded factorization categories. Standard-resolution functoriality sends a nested variable [p] to the nested variable of its image, giving the canonical map of standard simplicial algebras and hence the canonical cotangent comparison. By [[lem-derived-colimit-coefficient-and-category-change]], coefficient extension changes Lcolim_B F into Lcolim_B'(F tensor_B^L B'). Every value of F is a free B-module, since polynomial differentials are free, so this coefficient tensor is ordinary. Differential base change identifies it with u^*F_{B'/A'} ([[lem-differentials-base-change]]). 3. Assume the canonical derived tensor B tensor_A^L A'->B' is a quasi-isomorphism. The associated A-module complex of the standard resolution is bounded above and free by the admissibility lemma, so its tensor with A' computes this derived tensor using the exact published bounded-above projective/tensor suppliers. Therefore P_bullet tensor_A A'->B' is a quasi-isomorphism. It is termwise surjective: its augmentation in degree zero surjects onto B tensor_A A', whose canonical map to B' is an isomorphism on H0, and degeneracies give the higher surjections. The normalized abelian lifting criterion makes it a trivial Kan fibration. Consequently both the source standard resolution and its image under u satisfy the contractible-cosimplicial hypothesis. The category-change half of the preceding lemma identifies Lcolim_old u^*F_new with Lcolim_new F_new. Composing these coefficient and category comparisons is exactly the canonical cotangent map, so it is an isomorphism. 4. In an ordinary pushout, the derived tensor map is a quasi-isomorphism precisely when higher Tor vanishes, by the published Tor identification under AC implying DC. Flat A->A' implies that vanishing. For A->A'->B and the same-target canonical quasi-isomorphism, take B'=B and the identity on B in the preceding square; this gives the retained localization comparison L_B/A=L_B/A'. Every required ordinary-ring prerequisite is now a supplied local helper or exact published module/differential supplier. This proof makes no claim that the genuine derived-ring/Quillen/module-descent foundations for the separate derived-scheme item are supplied. An augmentation to constant B which is a weak equivalence in the stated simplicial-ring sense is automatically termwise surjective: H0 is B, so P0->B is surjective, and degeneracies give the remaining surjections. The normalized lifting criterion therefore makes the resolution convention in the cotangent definition admissible for this comparison.

## lem-cotangent-complex-h0-and-polynomial-case

### Statement

Assume AC for the resolution-comparison supplier ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). (1) For every homomorphism $A\to B$ of commutative unital rings one has $H^0(L_{B/A})\cong\Omega_{B/A}$ ([[def-cotangent-complex-of-a-ring-map]], [[def-kahler-differentials-algebra]], [[thm-kahler-differentials-existence-presentation]], [[def-homology-object-of-a-chain-complex]]). (2) If $B$ is a polynomial $A$-algebra, then $L_{B/A}$ is quasi-isomorphic to $\Omega_{B/A}$ placed in degree $0$ ([[def-quasi-isomorphism]]).

### Proof / construction

Full proof. Write P0=A[B], with symbols [b], and P1=A[P0]. The cokernel of the two face maps on differentials is the free B-module on d[b] modulo d(p)-d[epsilon(p)] for every polynomial p in P0: the two faces send the outer variable [p] to p and to [epsilon(p)]. Taking p=a, [b]+[c] and [b][c] forces d[a]=0, additivity and the Leibniz relation. Conversely those derivation relations imply d(p)=d[epsilon(p)] for every polynomial p, by induction on sums/products. Thus this cokernel represents A-derivations of B, hence is Omega_B/A by the published differential presentation; it is H0 of the standard simplicial differential complex. For polynomial B, the constant simplicial A-algebra B with identity augmentation is a polynomial resolution and identity is a trivial Kan fibration. Apply [[lem-cotangent-complex-resolution-independence]] to compare it canonically with the standard resolution. Its associated differential complex is the constant simplicial module Omega_B/A: the alternating differential is identity in positive even chain degrees and zero in odd degrees. Pair consecutive positive degrees to contract them, leaving Omega_B/A in degree zero. This proves the polynomial clause, without importing an unspecified acyclicity theorem. The full sources are Stacks92.4.5-92.4.7 (08QF-08QH); AC enters only through the newly supplied comparison route.
