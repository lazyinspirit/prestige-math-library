## 1. An explicit finite complex construction

The following arguments are algebraic, with no use of general strictification. Fix a nonpositive commutative dg ring R and finitely many closed degree-zero elements f₁,…,fᵣ whose classes generate the unit ideal in H⁰(R). Ordinary rings are a special case. Write R_S=R[(∏_{i∈S}fᵢ)⁻¹] for each nonempty subset S of {1,…,r}. Let P be the finite poset of these subsets, ordered by inclusion. All modules below may be unbounded cochain dg modules. A localization is computed degreewise and is exact, so Hᵏ(M⊗R R_S)=Hᵏ(M) localized at f_S. This follows directly because localization preserves kernels and images: clear finitely many denominators in each finite relation.

These localizations are jointly conservative. If Hᵏ(M) localizes to zero at every fᵢ, then for each x in that module there are positive integers nᵢ with fᵢⁿⁱx=0. The ideal (f₁ⁿ¹,…,fᵣⁿʳ) has unit radical since (f₁,…,fᵣ) does; therefore it contains 1 and x=0. The same argument applied to the cone detects quasi-isomorphisms. The unit relation is used in H⁰(R), which acts on every Hᵏ(M); an equality modulo dg boundaries suffices.

For a **strict** diagram F:P→dgMod_R define Bᵖ(F)=∏_{S₀<⋯<Sₚ}F(Sₚ), with Bᵖ=0 for p≥r. Its horizontal differential on a chain S₀<⋯<Sₚ₊₁ is

δc = Σ_{i=0}^{p}(−1)ⁱc_{S₀<⋯<Ŝᵢ<⋯<Sₚ₊₁} +(−1)^{p+1}F(Sₚ⊂Sₚ₊₁)c_{S₀<⋯<Sₚ}.

The terms correspond to deleting a vertex, transporting the coefficient along the last edge when that vertex was terminal. Deleting two vertices in the two orders gives the same coefficient with opposite signs; for two terminal deletions this uses strict composition. Thus δ²=0. δ commutes with the internal differential. Define T(F) in total degree k by ∏_{p=0}^{r−1}Bᵖ(F)^{k−p}, with differential δ+(−1)ᵖd_F on column p. The dg R-action on column p is a·c=(−1)^{p|a|}ac; this is the usual shift action and makes that differential R-linear with the Leibniz sign. There are only finitely many columns and entries. Hence T preserves objectwise quasi-isomorphisms: filter by column, use exactness of finite products and the finite successive cone sequences. It also commutes with every R_S localization. No infinite-totalization convergence assertion is involved.

Two elementary bar identities used below have direct chain homotopies. A natural transformation u⇒v of functors of indexing posets induces a homotopy between the corresponding restriction maps: on a chain insert the transformation edge at each position and sum with sign (−1)ʲ, deleting zero/repeated edges. In δh+hδ the deletions other than the inserted edge cancel in pairs; the two uncancelled end faces give the two restriction maps. This is the ordinary prism calculation and works with the transported terminal coefficients because the naturality squares commute. If a poset Q has initial object q, augment T(F|Q) by F(q) through its transition maps. Insert q at the beginning of each chain (zero on chains already beginning there). The same deletion calculation contracts the augmented complex horizontally; the sign (−1)ᵖ on its internal differential makes the total contraction valid. Thus F(q)→T(F|Q) is a quasi-isomorphism, indeed an augmented bar contraction. These formulas also prove the natural-transformation identities without appealing to a homotopy-cofinality theorem.

## 2. Effective descent of strict cartesian diagrams

A strict cartesian localization diagram E consists of an R_S-module E_S for each S and strict R-linear maps E_S→E_T for S⊂T, satisfying composition, such that E_S⊗R_S R_T→E_T is a quasi-isomorphism. Regard E as a strict R-module diagram and set M=T(E).

For each S put P_S={T:T⊃S}, and K_S=T(E|P_S). Each K_S is an R_S-module. Restrictions to P_T⊂P_S give a **strict** diagram K. The augmentation E_S→K_S is a quasi-isomorphism since S is initial in P_S; these augmentations form a strict natural transformation E→K. Restricting the bar complex from P to P_S gives a strict natural map M⊗R R_S→K_S.

This last map is a quasi-isomorphism. After localization at f_S, define u:P→P_S by u(T)=T∪S. The cartesian maps E_T⊗R R_S→E_{T∪S} are objectwise quasi-isomorphisms; finite bar exactness transfers this to T. For the inclusion i:P_S→P we have ui=id and the natural transformation id_P⇒iu. The prism calculation in §1 shows that restriction to P_S and pullback along u are mutually inverse up to bar chain homotopy for the diagram E∘iu. Therefore T(E)⊗R R_S→T(E|P_S)=K_S is a quasi-isomorphism. We have explicitly exhibited the strict zigzag

E → K ← (S↦M⊗R R_S)

of objectwise quasi-isomorphisms. This proves effectivity for **strict cartesian diagrams**, including all unbounded modules, on a finite principal cover.

For an arbitrary global module N, let E_S=N⊗R R_S. The natural augmentation N→T(E) becomes a quasi-isomorphism after every R_{i} localization: after localization, the union-with-{i} prism argument reduces the bar complex to the subposet with initial {i}, whose augmentation is contracted. Joint conservativity therefore makes N→T(E) a quasi-isomorphism globally. This proves recovery as well as existence. The construction uses the finite subset-intersection diagram; it does not assert that an arbitrary cosimplicial hypercover can be replaced by this diagram without a separate comparison.

## 3. Mapping-complex compatibility, without a localization theorem

Here is a direct resolution construction. Every dg R-module N has a semifree resolution Q→N. At stage zero adjoin, in their degrees, free generators with zero differential mapping to chosen cocycles representing all Hᵏ(N). At each subsequent stage, for every cocycle z in the current free module whose image is a boundary in N, adjoin a generator e one degree below z, set de=z, and send e to a chosen primitive of the image of z. Keep all prior generators. Every kernel class appears at a finite stage and is killed at the next; every target class was reached at stage zero. Filtered colimits of modules are exact, so the union Q→N is a quasi-isomorphism. The differential of each new generator involves only earlier generators, hence this is semifree. Simultaneous choices use AC.

For any acyclic module V, Hom_R(Q,V) is acyclic. Given a closed homogeneous map a of degree n, construct a map b of degree n−1 recursively on the ordered free generators, requiring db−(−1)^{n−1}bd=a. When all earlier values have been chosen, the required value of d_Vb(e) is a(e)+(−1)^{n−1}b(de). Its differential is zero by the closedness equation for a, d²e=0 and the already satisfied equation on de. Choose a primitive in V. This completes the recursion, including infinite unions, and proves every Hom cocycle is a boundary. Thus Q is K-projective. Its localization remains semifree and hence K-projective by exactly the same argument. Different resolutions compare by lifting against acyclic cones; the same generator recursion shows lifting and uniqueness up to homotopy, so the resulting derived Hom complexes are independent up to quasi-isomorphism.

For any Q and strict E, the elementary extension/restriction adjunction gives, chainwise,

Hom_R(Q,E_S)=Hom_{R_S}(Q⊗R R_S,E_S).

Hom_R(Q,−) commutes with the finite products and shifts defining T. Consequently for a semifree Q and M=T(E),

Hom_R(Q,M)=T(S↦Hom_{R_S}(Q⊗R R_S,E_S)).

Apply this to E_S=N⊗R R_S and the proved quasi-isomorphism N→T(E). K-projectivity yields the natural derived mapping-complex equivalence

RHom_R(N₁,N₂) ≃ T(S↦RHom_{R_S}((N₁)_S,(N₂)_S)).

This holds for arbitrary unbounded modules, without perfection or a compactness assumption on N₁. In particular it identifies all Ext groups and all chain homotopies. It avoids the incorrect shortcut of claiming localization commutes with RHom for an arbitrary source: the adjunction here retains its R_S-linear target, and the finite bar limit is taken before any such assertion. Applying the explicit Dold–Kan inverse to the nonpositive smart truncation of these mapping complexes gives the usual additive mapping simplicial groups. Identifying that enriched construction with a Dwyer–Kan coherent localization, or proving that its coherent diagram category is represented by the strict diagrams of §2, is a further theorem, **not proved by this mapping-complex calculation**.

## 4. A complete tower convergence calculation and its limit

For a connective cochain dg R-module C (Hᵏ(C)=0 for k>0), the smart truncations C_n=τ_{≥−n}C form an inverse tower. Since R is nonpositive these are dg R-modules: quotient by the differential-stable submodule consisting of all degrees below −n and the boundaries in degree −n. Their transition maps are degreewise surjective. In each fixed degree, the tower eventually equals C in that degree with its differential. Thus lim_n C_n=C degreewise.

Define the tower homotopy-limit complex explicitly by Cone(1−shift:∏C_n→∏C_n)[−1]. The map 1−shift is surjective degreewise: given y_n choose x₀, then recursively lift x_n−y_n through the surjection C_{n+1}→C_n. Its kernel is lim C_n. The short exact sequence therefore identifies the displayed cone with C up to quasi-isomorphism. Products of modules are exact (componentwise surjectivity uses AC). This proves C≃holim_n C_n, without an unbounded spectral-sequence argument. Degreewise localization commutes with smart truncation because it is exact; hence cartesian localization diagrams remain cartesian after truncation. The finite T construction commutes with this tower homotopy limit, since it uses finite products, shifts, and an explicit differential. This proves that the strict finite-cover gluing above is compatible with Postnikov truncations and their reconstruction.

It does **not** prove the same completeness statement for arbitrary sheaves on an arbitrary site. Evaluating sheaf homotopy truncations on an open need not equal truncating the original section complex. Nor does it construct a tower of simplicial commutative **algebras** with the required multiplicative k-invariants; the module quotient calculation does not prove algebra Postnikov gluing. These are exact boundaries of this result.

## 6. Narrower route for the actual commissioned cotangent claim

The sole reviewer correctly distinguishes existence/gluing of the **specific cotangent modules** from arbitrary coherent-module effective descent. The commissioned item asserts that L_X/Y is obtained by gluing affine representatives and represents relative derived derivations; it does not commission the general HAGII QCoh-stack theorem. Thus general strictification need not be an unavoidable prerequisite if one constructs this particular module strictly and proves its required mapping property directly. The following is a concrete alternate route, with its unresolved steps stated rather than promoted to proofs.

Let A=f⁻¹O_Y and B=O_X be sheaves of simplicial rings on X. The relative free-algebra/underlying-set adjunction exists degreewise for sheaves: apply the polynomial algebra construction to the underlying presheaf, then sheafify. Sheafification of sets has the following explicit germ description: two sections have the same germ iff they agree on some smaller neighborhood, and each germ is represented locally. Finite algebraic expressions therefore give (A[S])_x=A_x[S_x]. Kernels, cokernels and finite algebraic relations commute with taking stalks, since filtered colimits of modules are exact. Likewise degreewise sheafification followed by normalization has the same normalized stalk complex as normalization followed by taking stalks, since normalized degree n uses only finitely many kernel conditions. Consequently a strict functorial polynomial-resolution construction compatible with germs would produce a **single** global simplicial module

K=B⊗_P Ω_{P/A},

whose stalk at x is B_x⊗_{P_x}Ω_{P_x/A_x}. This removes the need to strictify an arbitrary collection of overlap equivalences. The sheaf differential, face maps, degeneracies and module action are actual maps before localization; their identities follow from their algebraic definitions and persist under sheafification.

One can make the ordinary relative free comonad resolution strictly natural in the entire arrow A→B: a square (A→B)→(A′→B′) sends a polynomial coefficient a to its image in A′ and a generator [b] to [image(b)]. Iterate the free comonad; its counit and comultiplication are natural under these squares, so the resulting resolution and Ω construction have strict coefficient-square maps and strict composition. For simplicial input this produces a bisimplicial object, followed by its diagonal. The augmentation has a degreewise underlying-set extra degeneracy in the resolution direction. Turning that contraction into a weak equivalence of diagonals, and proving this chosen diagonal is a valid cofibrant resolution for the variable simplicial base, still requires the proved bisimplicial/diagonal and model hypotheses. Degreewise polynomiality alone does not establish those facts. Alternatively the small-object construction using **all** lifting squares can be made coefficient-square natural if its relative generating objects and every attachment map commute with changing A; fixed-base functorial factorization does not prove this stronger naturality by itself.

To identify this global K with the affine modules and prove quasi-coherence, the needed local theorem is

L_{B/A}⊗_B^L B_q ≃ L_{B_q/A_p}

for the local coefficient rings at f(x),x, and its naturality for chart refinements. It combines derived localization on the source algebra with vanishing of the cotangent complex of localization on the base. The base-change theorem for a single homotopy pushout is only part of this: when both affine charts shrink, one must also prove relative localization invariance (or the relevant transitivity triangle and vanishing). Once that theorem and its simplicial-module version are proved, the stalkwise comparison identifies K on each affine chart with the sheafified L_{B/A}; localization of its homotopy modules then proves quasi-coherence on the ordinary truncation. In the dg localization model, §§1–3 give the strict effective module gluing and mapping-complex calculation needed for this chart step. They do not prove the variable simplicial-ring comparison.

Finally, the local algebraic derivation adjunction does not by itself prove **global derived** derivation representability. One must construct the sheaf module/algebra derived mapping models, or show that their mapping spaces are computed by the specific affine-basis totalizations used here and respect the strict sheafification comparison. A degreewise sheafified Hom presheaf gives ordinary local maps; its global sections need not equal derived global maps. Exact sheafification and stalk detection prove quasi-isomorphism of sheaves, but do not supply higher derived global sections or homotopy-coherent compositions. This is the remaining target-specific mapping prerequisite. If it is proved directly for the strict cotangent construction, arbitrary QCoh descent and general HS18.6 need not be imported at all.

Accordingly the minimal remaining list for this narrower route is: valid strictly coefficient-square-natural resolutions compatible with germs; derived localization/transitivity and local cotangent vanishing; the variable simplicial module mapping/sheaf comparison; and the global sheaf-derived derivation adjunction. General arbitrary-module hyperdescent is a possible route, not an irreducible commissioned claim. The current packet supplies an explicit finite-cover algebraic mapping calculation toward that narrower route and retains the hold on its exact unresolved interfaces.
