## Supplier proof and premise verification

Read the published distribution-injectivity theorem in full. It is published, assumes Countable Choice, and injects complex L1_loc classes modulo a.e. equality into distributions. CC is already declared by H(div); real test identities extend to complex tests by splitting real and imaginary parts. For two divergence representatives g1,g2, h=g1-g2 is L2, and for every compact C in D, finite Lebesgue measure and Cauchy–Schwarz give integral_C |h| <= |C|^(1/2) ||h||_2 < infinity. Thus h is L1_loc. Subtracting divergence identities gives the zero regular distribution, and injectivity gives h=0 a.e. This is precisely the missing argument; uniqueness of one function's individual weak derivatives did not establish it.

Exact published suppliers added to `def-na-hdiv-space` and to the second occurrence in RT0 conformity:

- `thm-locally-integrable-functions-embed-in-distributions`: injectivity under CC (the smallest missing uniqueness theorem).
- `cor-cauchy-schwarz-inequality-for-l-two`: the compact L1 estimate.
- `prop-lebesgue-measure-is-sigma-finite-and-finite-on-bounded-sets`: compact finite measure under CC.

The old direct `lem-weak-derivatives-are-unique-almost-everywhere` dependency is replaced at those two occurrences. It remains a legitimate transitive Sobolev supplier. The real/complex class convention introduces no stronger choice assumption. No published supplier needed repair.

The general-D weak trace proof was read fully before consumer checks. The published H1 interface uses real L2 weak derivatives and genuine Sobolev norm under CC; the published H1_0 definition is the norm closure of real compact smooth tests on every open set, with no boundary regularity requirement. These two definitions and published Cauchy–Schwarz are exactly the needed premises. For L_v(w)=integral(v dot grad w + div(v) w), the two L2 pairings and finite-dimensional Cauchy–Schwarz give |L_v(w)| <= ||v||_Hdiv ||w||_H1. Distributional divergence makes L_v zero on compact smooth tests; continuity makes it zero on their H1 closure. Hence it descends to the quotient and infimizing over representatives gives the stated norm bound, including independence and linearity. H1_0 is closed by definition, so its quotient norm is definite. No Lipschitz-boundary, boundary-measure, trace-extension or divergence-density theorem is silently needed. The empty domain is the zero-space case.

The bounded-open zero-mean proof was read fully. Bounded measurable D has finite measure by the published finite-measure supplier. Therefore 1 is L2; its weak gradient is zero, so 1 is H1(D), and [1] is in T_D. Holder gives div(v) in L1 from its L2 membership. Evaluating the proved general-D Green functional at [1] gives integral_D div(v); its vanishing for v in the trace kernel gives zero. Neither connectedness nor Lipschitz boundary enters. The only local repair here is the dual-type sentence: gamma_n v is an element of T_D' acting on [1] in T_D.

RT0 conformity had a second instance of the uniqueness mistake not listed in the native findings: step 3.2 identifies the global L2 divergence with each cell's polynomial divergence. It now subtracts the two test identities on K, verifies the difference is L2(K), hence L1_loc(K) by the explicit compact estimate, and invokes regular-distribution injectivity. F7 and its exact dependencies were repaired accordingly. Its Statement and every other proof step are retained.
