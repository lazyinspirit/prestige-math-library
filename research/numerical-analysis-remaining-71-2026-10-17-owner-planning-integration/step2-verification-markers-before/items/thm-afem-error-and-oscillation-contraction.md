## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and Countable Choice for the weak elliptic existence suppliers used to instantiate either model branch.

Let $(\mathcal T_k,U_k)$ be generated by exact P1 Galerkin solves and the three-generation algorithm with $\theta_E,\theta_O\in(0,1]$, and assume every per-call bisection budget is sufficient for a complete refinement. For the estimator and oscillation of [[def-afem-p1-residual-estimator-and-data-oscillation]], write $e_k=\|\nabla(u-U_k)\|_2$, $\eta_k=\eta(U_k,\mathcal T_k)$ and $\operatorname{osc}_k=\operatorname{osc}(U_k,\mathcal T_k)$. On the symmetric branch $b=0$, no initial-mesh smallness is needed. On the constant-convection branch assume the uniform dual-pickup bound of [[lem-afem-convection-quasi-orthogonality-under-fine-mesh]] and set $\mu=C_Dh_0^s|b|$. Define
$$\kappa=\min\left\{\frac12,\frac{\theta_E}{C_{\rm eff}C_{\rm rel}}\right\},\qquad \nu=\begin{cases}0,&b=0,\\ \mu/(1-\mu),&b\ne0.\end{cases}$$
In the convection branch assume $\mu<1$, so that $\nu$ is defined and the quasi-orthogonality estimate applies, and assume $\nu<\kappa$. Then there are $\beta>0$ and $0<\alpha<1$, depending only on the displayed constants and marking parameters, such that
$$e_{k+1}^2+\beta\operatorname{osc}_{k+1}^2 \le\alpha\big(e_k^2+\beta\operatorname{osc}_{k}^2\big).$$

Moreover, after possibly reducing a positive weight $\sigma$, there are $\beta'>0$ and $0<\alpha'<1$ such that
$$e_{k+1}^2+\beta'\operatorname{osc}_{k+1}^2+\sigma\eta_{k+1}^2 \le\alpha'\big(e_k^2+\beta'\operatorname{osc}_{k}^2+\sigma\eta_k^2\big).$$
This second assertion records the residual-estimator reduction as part of the usual AFEM quasi-error. Constants and smallness hypotheses belong to the chosen marking and the stated dual-pickup bound.

## Facts & Assumptions

[A1] The Axiom of Choice and Countable Choice are assumed for the weak-solution suppliers and Sobolev spaces ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-countable-choice]]).

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, Countable Choice, exact weak and Galerkin solutions, reliability and marked local efficiency, bulk estimator and oscillation sets, three-generation refinement, and in the convection case the stated dual-pickup bound.

[F1] Reliability gives $e_k^2\le C_{\rm rel}\eta_k^2$, and marked local efficiency gives $\eta_k(M_E)^2\le C_{\rm eff}(\|\nabla(U_{k+1}-U_k)\|_2^2+\operatorname{osc}_k^2)$ ([[thm-afem-p1-residual-reliability-and-local-efficiency]]).

[F2] The energy error satisfies exact symmetric orthogonality for $b=0$ and the stated quasi-orthogonality inequality for constant $b$ ([[lem-afem-convection-quasi-orthogonality-under-fine-mesh]]).

[F3] Data oscillation contracts by $\operatorname{osc}_{k+1}^2\le\gamma_O\operatorname{osc}_k^2$, where $\gamma_O=1-\frac34\theta_O<1$ ([[lem-afem-data-oscillation-reduction-under-marked-refinement]]).

[F4] The residual estimator satisfies the reduction inequality after marked refinement, with a correction term proportional to $\|\nabla(U_{k+1}-U_k)\|_2^2$ ([[lem-afem-residual-estimator-reduction]]).

[F5] Under the stated choice assumptions, weak Dirichlet Poisson has a unique solution ([[thm-existence-and-uniqueness-for-the-weak-dirichlet-poisson-problem]]).

[F6] Constant-convection weak Dirichlet Poisson has a unique weak solution under the same choice assumptions ([[cor-afem-constant-convection-weak-wellposedness]]).

[F7] Under Countable Choice, every finite-dimensional conforming subspace has a unique exact Galerkin solution for the bounded coercive form ([[thm-cea-quasi-optimality-for-conforming-galerkin-approximations]]).

## Proof

1.1 By [A1, F5] or [A1, F6], as appropriate to the chosen branch, the weak solution exists uniquely under the stated choice assumptions. By [F7], the finite-dimensional P1 Galerkin solution on each mesh also exists uniquely. Write $d_k=U_{k+1}-U_k$. By [F1] and the Dörfler property, $\theta_E\eta_k^2\le\eta_k(M_E)^2\le C_{\rm eff}(\|\nabla d_k\|_2^2+\operatorname{osc}_k^2),\qquad \eta_k^2\ge e_k^2/C_{\rm rel}.$ Thus $\|\nabla d_k\|_2^2\ge\kappa e_k^2-\operatorname{osc}_k^2$. By [F2], $e_{k+1}^2\le(1+\nu)e_k^2-\|\nabla d_k\|_2^2$, where $\nu=0$ in the symmetric case and $\nu=\mu/(1-\mu)$ otherwise. Consequently $e_{k+1}^2\le(1-\delta)e_k^2+\operatorname{osc}_k^2,\qquad \delta=\kappa-\nu>0.$ [A1, F1, F2, F5, F6, F7, given, algebra]

2.1 Combine step 1.1 with [F3]: $e_{k+1}^2+\beta\operatorname{osc}_{k+1}^2\le(1-\delta)e_k^2+(1+\beta\gamma_O)\operatorname{osc}_k^2.$ Choose $\beta>1/(1-\gamma_O)$. Then both coefficients $1-\delta$ and $\gamma_O+1/\beta$ are strictly less than one. Their maximum is a valid $\alpha<1$, proving the first contraction. [A1, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Set $\varepsilon=\theta_E/4$ in [F4]. Dörfler marking gives $\eta_{k+1}^2\le\rho_E\eta_k^2+C_E\|\nabla d_k\|_2^2,\qquad \rho_E=1+\varepsilon-\tfrac12\theta_E=1-\tfrac14\theta_E<1,$ where $C_E$ is finite. Also [F2] implies $\|\nabla d_k\|_2^2\le(1+\nu)e_k^2$. Choose $\sigma>0$ so $\sigma C_E(1+\nu)<\delta/2$. Adding $\sigma$ times this estimator inequality to the error and oscillation bounds gives coefficients strictly below one on $e_k^2$ and $\eta_k^2$. Choose $\beta'$ large enough that the coefficient on $\operatorname{osc}_k^2$, divided by $\beta'$, is below one; this is possible by $\gamma_O<1$. The maximum of the three resulting coefficients is $\alpha'<1$, which proves the second inequality. The case of zero error or zero oscillation satisfies the same inequalities without division. [A1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
