## Statement

Assume AC. Let $\Gamma\le\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)$ be discrete, torsion-free, nonelementary and geometrically finite in the finite-positive-radius-convex-neighborhood-volume sense. Put $\Omega=\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus\Lambda_\Gamma$. Then $X=\Omega/\Gamma$ is a finite disjoint union of compact Riemann surfaces with finitely many points removed. Each orbit of rank-one parabolic points gives exactly TWO such punctures, each with primitive monodromy in the full rank-one parabolic stabilizer. Their oriented puncture generators are mutually inverse under the common cusp normalization. Rank-two cusps give no puncture of $X$. Empty $\Omega$ gives the empty union.

Let $X_j$ be a component, $D\subset\Omega$ a lift and $H=\operatorname{Stab}_\Gamma D$. Suppose the monodromy $\pi_1(X_j)\to\Gamma$ is injective and $H$ is nonelementary. Then $D$ is simply connected, has a conformal disk uniformizer, and that uniformizer realizes $X_j$ as a complete finite-area hyperbolic surface with a cofinite Fuchsian deck group $H_0$. Its parabolic classes are exactly the nonzero powers of its puncture classes. Under $H_0\cong H$, every such class is parabolic in $\Gamma$ and every puncture generator gives the full primitive ambient rank-one stabilizer.

The converse concerning ALL parabolics of $H$ is not included: an ambient parabolic may still be an accidental parabolic for the uniformizing action unless a separate boundary-to-paring condition excludes it. Nor is geometric finiteness of $H$, a Jordan boundary of $D$, or an opposite quasidisk asserted here.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The group and its geometric-finiteness hypothesis, and for the second conclusion the component, injective monodromy and nonelementary stabilizer.

[F1] The actual full standard-cusp theorem gives equivariant separated extended regions, rank-one peak half-planes $\{\pm\operatorname{Im}z>S_p\}\subset\Omega$ for a real primitive translation $z\mapsto z+\ell_p$, and a compactified Dirichlet truncation disjoint from the limit set after its finitely many parabolic ideal vertices and their strict peaks are removed. The closures of peaks belonging to distinct parabolic points are disjoint in $\Omega$, by its Proof3.1–4.1. Its Proof2.1 also proves the matrix bound $|\ell c|\ge1$ for ANY discrete group containing $z\mapsto z+\ell$ and any determinant-one matrix with lower-left entry $c\ne0$; that calculation has no geometric-finiteness premise. [[thm-geometrically-finite-kleinian-groups-have-separated-standard-cusps-and-finite-dirichlet-limit-vertices]]

[F2] Discrete torsion-free Möbius groups have no nonidentity elliptics; elementary groups and full parabolic stabilizers have the explicit normal forms of [[lem-local-thin-components-of-torsion-free-kleinian-quotients]]. The Hermitian/ideal model is in [[lem-complete-curvature-minus-one-manifolds-are-kleinian-quotients]].

[F3] Covering homotopies lift uniquely by finite local strips, in [[lem-covering-homotopies-lift-by-finite-local-strips]]. A map from a simply connected locally path-connected source lifts through a covering after a starting point is prescribed, by the zero-subgroup case of [[thm-covering-space-lifting-criterion]]. Under AC a proper homologically simply connected plane domain is conformally a disk by [[thm-riemann-mapping-theorem]]. Disk automorphisms are Möbius by [[thm-disc-automorphisms-are-rotated-blaschke-factors]].

[F4] Schwarz–Pick gives the derivative inequality, hence contraction of the metric $2|dz|/(1-|z|^2)$ by disk holomorphic maps. It gives equality for disk automorphisms by applying the inequality also to their inverses. [[thm-schwarz-pick-lemma-on-the-unit-disc]]

[F5] Closed compact oriented surfaces are the genus models in [[thm-classification-of-compact-connected-surfaces]]. Removing finitely many disks or points gives Euler count $2-2g-n$; in the nonnegative cases the oriented finite-type surface is a sphere, plane, cylinder or torus, with respectively trivial, trivial, cyclic or rank-two abelian fundamental group, directly from their disk, cylinder and polygon models.

## Proof

1.1 The action on $\Omega$ is free and properly discontinuous. Freeness follows from [F2], since every fixed point of a nonidentity parabolic or loxodromic lies in the limit set. To prove properness on a compact $K\subset\Omega$, suppose infinitely many distinct elements have $\gamma K\cap K\ne\varnothing$. Choose distinct such elements and normalize determinant-one matrices by their Euclidean matrix norms. Properness/discreteness in the matrix group forces a subsequence of norms to infinity; a unit-matrix subsequence converges to a nonzero rank-one matrix $A$. Its image point $q$ and kernel point $p$ lie in $\Lambda_\Gamma$: apply the Hermitian orbit formula to a fixed positive matrix and to the inverses, whose normalized adjugates have the opposite rank-one limit. Off $p$ the Möbius maps converge uniformly on compact sets to $q$. Since $K$ misses both points, eventually $\gamma K$ misses $K$, a contradiction. For a free proper action, an evenly covered neighborhood is obtained by first taking a compact small neighborhood, then excluding its finitely many nonidentity intersections with translates. Quotient charts are holomorphic, the quotient is Hausdorff by the same finite compact-neighborhood exclusion, and its countable base is the image of a countable base of $\Omega$ under the open quotient map. Thus $X$ is a Riemann surface, possibly disconnected, and $D/H$ is its corresponding connected component with the stated monodromy. [A1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Every point of $\Omega$ has a representative in the ideal boundary of the closed Dirichlet domain $D_o$. Here is the boundary attainment check, so finite-point Dirichlet coverage is not silently substituted. Move a chosen ordinary point to infinity. On the discrete orbit $\Gamma o$, its height function has a maximum: any orbit sequence leaving compact subsets has only limit-set ideal accumulation, whereas infinity is ordinary, so a sequence of heights bounded away from zero can neither escape horizontally nor tend upward to infinity. Hence a height maximizing sequence lies in a compact model set and meets only finitely many orbit points. If $\gamma o$ has largest height, the limiting distance comparison along a ray to infinity is the Dirichlet comparison based at $\gamma o$. Equivalently, in the ball model those comparisons are closed Lorentz half-space inequalities; their ideal limits are the height comparisons just obtained. Thus infinity belongs to $\gamma\overline D_o$, and transporting back proves the assertion. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 There are finitely many cusp orbits. The compact quotient of the truncated convex hull in [F1] contains a point on each standard cusp boundary: a ray in the hull to its parabolic vertex eventually enters that cusp. Points from different cusp orbits have quotient distance bounded below by the fixed separation of standard regions. A compact set admits only finitely many such separated points. In rank one the TWO ideal peaks at a cusp are preserved separately by its full real translation stabilizer, and every element outside that stabilizer has disjoint peak closures by [F1]. Peaks from distinct cusps have the same disjointness. Their quotient images are therefore embedded disjoint punctured disks, with embedded compact rim circles at equality. Explicitly their coordinates are $w=\exp(2\pi iz/\ell_p)$ on the upper peak and $w=\exp(-2\pi iz/\ell_p)$ on the lower peak; their images are $0<|w|<\exp(-2\pi S_p/\ell_p)$. Each map has exactly the full translation fibers, so its positively oriented puncture generator is $T_{\ell_p}$ above and $T_{\ell_p}^{-1}$ below. No element can identify these two peaks in the quotient: an element taking one to the other must fix their unique parabolic vertex, but its translation stabilizer preserves their signs. Rank-two standard regions have no ideal peak in $\Omega$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Remove all these open peak quotients from $X$. Its remainder has a compact set of representatives: by step2.1 every representative can first be moved into $\overline D_o$, and a point outside ALL peaks stays outside those of its finitely many Dirichlet ideal vertices. The ideal part of the compactified truncation supplied by [F1] is a compact subset of $\Omega$ containing all such representatives. Thus the remainder is a closed subset of its compact quotient image and is compact. Its boundary is exactly the finite disjoint collection of embedded smooth peak rims, so it is a compact surface with boundary. It has finitely many connected components, since a compact set of ordinary surface points is covered by finitely many connected coordinate disks and boundary half-disks. Every removed punctured disk attaches along one of these rims. Adding its center using the displayed $w$ coordinate gives a compact Riemann surface at that end; holomorphic compatibility on overlaps is the same $w$ coordinate before its center is added. This gives precisely a finite union of compact Riemann surfaces minus finitely many points. If there are no ordinary points all these assertions mean the empty union. In particular no finite-area conclusion has yet been inferred from a topological Euler count. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

4.1 In the component with injective monodromy, a loop in $D$ projects to a loop in $X_j$ with trivial monodromy, hence a nullhomotopic loop there. Lift its nullhomotopy by [F3]; the prescribed initial lift is the given loop, and the final constant loop lifts to a constant loop. Thus $D$ is simply connected. The image of monodromy is exactly $H$: a path in connected $D$ from a point to its translate projects to a loop realizing any chosen element of $H$, and every lifted loop stays in $D$. It is therefore an isomorphism $\pi_1(X_j)\cong H$. Since a discrete torsion-free abelian Möbius group is elementary by [F2], $H$ is nonabelian. The four nonnegative Euler cases in [F5] are excluded, so $X_j$ has negative Euler characteristic. The domain $D$ misses the ambient limit set, which has at least three points. Moving one missed point to infinity makes it a proper plane domain. Simply connectedness makes every complex cycle have zero index at every omitted point: decompose a finite cycle into finitely many closed loops and contract them in the domain, which leaves the index unchanged. Thus it is homologically simply connected, and [F3] gives a conformal map $\phi:\mathbb D\to D$. The deck action transported through $\phi$ is a free properly discontinuous group of disk Möbius maps, hence a discrete torsion-free Fuchsian group $H_0$. Its quotient is biholomorphic to $X_j$. [F2, F3, F5, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 The disk metric in [F4] descends to a smooth curvature-minus-one metric on $X_j$; invariance follows by applying Schwarz–Pick in both directions to each deck map. Its geodesics are complete, since their local lifts are model geodesics and can be extended for every real time before projecting. For each puncture coordinate $0<|w|<R$, the inclusion of that punctured disk into $X_j$ lifts after precomposition with its simply connected upper-half-plane cover to a holomorphic map into the universal disk, by [F3]. Schwarz–Pick [F4], after Cayley coordinates, makes its pulled-back density at most $|dw|/(|w|\log(R/|w|))$. Its area on $0<|w|<R_0<R$ is consequently at most $2\pi/\log(R/R_0)<\infty$. The remaining compact core has finite area by smoothness and a finite chart cover. This proves completeness and finite TOTAL area, so $H_0$ is cofinite. The lengths of its puncture circles tend to zero, bounded above by $2\pi/\log(R/r)$. Their monodromy in $H$ is the nontrivial full translation generator already computed in step2.2, so their corresponding elements of $H_0$ are nontrivial. A hyperbolic disk element has positive translation length, a lower bound for every loop representing it; therefore these shrinking circles force their elements to be parabolic. [F3, F4, step 2.2, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Conversely, take any parabolic element of $H_0$ and its full cyclic stabilizer. Normalize a primitive generator to $z\mapsto z+\ell$ in the upper half-plane. Its stabilizer is rank one since it is a discrete real translation subgroup; [F2] supplies this classification. The matrix bound in [F1] implies that $\{Y>T\}$ is precisely invariant when $T>\ell$: an element not fixing infinity satisfies $Y'\le1/(|c|^2Y)\le\ell^2/Y<T$. Hence its quotient is an embedded cusp cylinder in $X_j$. On every compact subset of $X_j$ the injectivity radius has a positive lower bound, by finitely many evenly covered metric disks. The horocycles in this cylinder have length $\ell/Y$, so the entire sufficiently high tail misses any prescribed compact subset. By step3.1 the connected tail is therefore in one of the actual punctured-disk ends. Its primitive loop $a$ is a power $b^m$ of that end's puncture generator $b$. By step5.1, $b$ is parabolic; the equality makes it fix the same point as $a$, so $b=a^n$ for an integer $n$ in the full stabilizer of $a$. Thus $a=a^{nm}$, giving $n=m=1$ or $n=m=-1$. The full primitive cusp class and the primitive puncture class agree up to sign. All parabolic powers are therefore exactly puncture powers. This converse concerns the uniformizing group $H_0$, not every ambient parabolic of $H$. [F1, F2, step 3.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 The primitive puncture monodromies of step2.2 belong to the full ambient rank-one stabilizers, and those translations preserve the containing component $D$, so those full groups are contained in $H$. The isomorphism in step4.1 therefore sends every parabolic puncture power of $H_0$ to its corresponding actual parabolic power in $H$. An additional ambient parabolic might correspond under that isomorphism to a nonperipheral loop; none of the proof identified it with a uniformizing parabolic. Excluding precisely that possibility is the separate boundary-to-paring operation in the Statement. The proof has supplied finite conformal type and cofinite incompressible uniformizers, not a geometric-finiteness proof for $H$ or a quasidisk/opposite-domain claim. [F2, step 2.2, step 4.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra] ∎
