## Statement

Assume AC. Let $H$ be a discrete torsion-free subgroup of $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)$, with ordinary domain $\Omega_H$, and write $W=(\mathbb H^3\cup\Omega_H)/H$. Let $C\subset W$ be a compact properly embedded locally flat two-sided annulus, with supplied product and boundary quadrant collars. Its two rims lie in $\partial W$, represent an infinite-order class in $\pi_1(W)=H$, and are not freely homotopic within $\partial W$.

Suppose a component $U$ of $W\setminus C$ induces a surjection $\pi_1(U)\to\pi_1(W)$. Then $C$ separates $W$ into two sides. For the closure $T$ of the side other than $U$, inclusion $\pi_1(C)\to\pi_1(T)$ is an isomorphism. Its core is parabolic. The ordinary boundary of $T$ consists of exactly two annular puncture neighborhoods, with their finite rim circles included. They give two distinct isolated conformal puncture ends of $\partial W$. Their peripheral elements generate one full maximal parabolic stabilizer of $H$, up to the base-path conjugacies. In particular the generator is primitive.

No third distinct conformal puncture end of $\partial W$ can have a nontrivial peripheral element conjugate to a power of this generator. Consequently no second annulus satisfying these hypotheses can pair either of these two punctures with a third puncture. Geometric finiteness, a three-manifold compact core and a product classification of $T$ are not assumptions or conclusions.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual Kleinian quotient, locally flat compact annulus with collars, infinite-order core, nonhomotopic ordinary rims and surjective outside component.

[F1] Collared van Kampen gives amalgamated products for separating cuts and the crossing-generator presentation for a nonseparating cut. Amalgam normal form embeds both factors and makes their intersection exactly the edge subgroup. [[thm-seifert-van-kampen]] [[thm-normal-form-for-free-products-with-amalgamation]]

[F2] A map into a cover lifts under the fundamental-group subgroup condition. [[thm-covering-space-lifting-criterion]]

[F3] The actual discrete torsion-free matrix computations classify nontrivial elements as parabolic or loxodromic. A full stabilizer containing a parabolic is a rank-one or rank-two translation group after normalization, without a finite-volume hypothesis. The upper half-space and ordinary compactifications have their explicit Möbius action. [[lem-local-thin-components-of-torsion-free-kleinian-quotients]] [[lem-complete-curvature-minus-one-manifolds-are-kleinian-quotients]]

[F4] Jordan curves bound topological disks with prescribed boundary extensions. Essential freely homotopic finite PL surface circles admit ambient PL isotopies, and disjoint ones have annular strip parameters. [F5] A bounded holomorphic function on a punctured disk has a removable singularity. [[thm-removable-singularity-characterizations]]

## Proof

1.1 The annulus is injective because its cyclic group generator has infinite-order image. If it did not separate, its normal collar would give a crossing-number homomorphism $\pi_1(W)\to\mathbb Z$: assign zero to paths staying in the cut piece and one to a chosen crossing generator. The collared van Kampen relations preserve this number, and the crossing generator maps to one. Every loop in $U$ has number zero, contradicting the assumed surjection. Thus $C$ separates. It has exactly two sides: its connected two-sided collar meets just two complementary components, and every component meets that collar, since $W$ is connected. Let $A=\pi_1(\overline U)$, $B=\pi_1(T)$ and $Z=\pi_1(C)$. Face maps are injective since their composites to $H$ are. Van Kampen and [F1] give $H=A*_ZB$. The first factor surjects to $H$, and both factors inject. Since their intersection is exactly $Z$, every element of $B$ must belong to the image of $Z$. Hence $Z\to B$ is an isomorphism. Write its infinite-order image as $\langle a\rangle\le H$. [A1, F1, given, construct, contradiction]

2.1 Use [F2] to lift the actual inclusion of $T$ to the cyclic cover $W_a=(\mathbb H^3\cup\Omega_H)/\langle a\rangle$. The lift is injective, since its composite to $W$ is the inclusion of $T$; it is the inverse of the covering restriction over its image. Its only interior frontier is ONE lift $C_0$ of $C$, because $\pi_1(C)\to\pi_1(T)$ is an isomorphism. Thus its interior is a full component of $(\mathbb H^3/\langle a\rangle)\setminus C_0$, not a region bounded by additional unnoticed lifts. Enlarge only the ORDINARY boundary to the explicit elementary compactification $V_a=(\mathbb H^3\cup\Omega_{\langle a\rangle})/\langle a\rangle$. The annulus $C_0$ remains compact proper locally flat with the same collars. Its core generates $\pi_1(V_a)=\langle a\rangle$. It separates $V_a$: otherwise the same crossing-number presentation as in step 1.1 would give an epimorphism to $\mathbb Z$ vanishing on the rim generator, which can be pushed into a boundary collar away from $C_0$. This contradicts generation of $\pi_1(V_a)$ by that rim. There are exactly two components, by connectedness of its collar as above. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, contradiction]

3.1 Record the elementary BOUNDARY topology without classifying either three-dimensional side. For a loxodromic $a:z\mapsto bz$, $|b|>1$, logarithmic coordinates identify $\partial V_a$ with $\mathbb C/(2\pi i\mathbb Z+\log b\mathbb Z)$, a torus. Its meridian-longitude map to $\pi_1(V_a)$ kills the first generator and sends the second to $a$. Each rim of $C_0$ therefore has homology $(p,\pm1)$. The two disjoint essential rims are parallel and cut this torus into two annuli. Here is a finite category justification even if the original rims are only locally flat. Their supplied surface strips allow a finite cyclic subdivision into small coordinate disks; inside disjoint smaller strip rectangles replace successive arcs by polygonal arcs, retaining their cyclic order and endpoints. Jordan disk extensions in [F4], applied to the two shores of each rectangle, realize these replacements by surface isotopies. A finite refinement and smaller strip widths keep all nonadjacent arcs disjoint. This puts both curves in a finite PL subdivision of the flat torus. Their nonzero longitude and disjointness give parallel primitive slopes; [F4]'s annular placement gives the two strips, and undoing those finite topological isotopies gives the annuli for the original curves. For parabolic $a:z\mapsto z+1$, $\partial V_a=\mathbb C/\mathbb Z=S^1\times\mathbb R$. The same finite strip argument, now in a compact subannulus containing both rims, gives a middle compact annulus $R_0$ and two outer half-infinite annuli $R_+,R_-$. Their lifts contain respectively $\{\operatorname{Im}z>M\}$ and $\{\operatorname{Im}z<-M\}$ for some finite $M$, because each lifted rim has a compact period segment and hence bounded imaginary coordinate. At either rim the boundary quadrant collar puts its two adjacent ordinary regions on opposite sides of $C_0$. Thus in the torus case the two annuli belong to different sides; in the cylinder case $R_0$ belongs to one side and BOTH $R_+,R_-$ to the other. No solid-torus annulus-product theorem, smoothing of a PL marking or proper-plane theorem is needed. [F3, F4, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Every ordinary boundary region on the lifted $T$ side in step 3.1 lies in $\Omega_H/\langle a\rangle$. Indeed suppose a lift $\zeta$ of one of its points lay in $\Lambda_H$. Choose an interior basepoint of $\overline U$ away from $C$, and a lift $o\in\mathbb H^3$. There are elements $h_j\in H$ with $h_jo\to\zeta$, by the definition of the limit set. Since $\zeta$ is ordinary for $\langle a\rangle$, a compact elementary boundary chart at its cyclic-quotient image has a one-sided interior neighborhood wholly in the chosen $T$ component: $C_0$ is compact and misses this point, and the quadrant incidence identifies this component there. Eventually the images of $h_jo$ in $\mathbb H^3/\langle a\rangle$ lie in that component. Their images in $W$ are all the chosen basepoint outside $T$, a contradiction. This proves the claimed inclusion. The resulting ordinary region is actually in the lifted copy of $T$: it has adjacent interior in that copy, and $T$ is closed with boundary $C$. Its projection to $W$ is therefore injective. If $a$ were loxodromic, step 3.1 gives an ordinary annulus joining the two rims on this side, now entirely in $\partial W$. Its parameter gives a free homotopy there, contrary to the hypothesis. Hence $a$ is parabolic. The same contradiction excludes the side with middle annulus $R_0$ in the parabolic case. The ordinary boundary of the lifted $T$ is thus exactly $R_+\cup R_-$, including their finite rims. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, given, construct, contradiction]

5.1 Normalize $a:z\mapsto z+1$ and use the two half-planes in step 3.1. Their quotients by $\langle a\rangle$ are punctured disks by $\exp(2\pi iz)$ on the upper half-plane and $\exp(-2\pi iz)$ on the lower one. Projection to $\partial W$ is injective there by step 4.1. They are disjoint and have compact finite boundary circles, so they define two distinct isolated conformal puncture ends. More explicitly, each sufficiently small punctured disk has just that boundary circle as its frontier in $\partial W$; deleting these two disjoint frontier circles isolates its two punctured components. A sequence tending to either missing tip cannot accumulate at an ordinary point of $\partial W$: disjoint evenly covered ordinary charts would then force a nonproper translate-identification of those punctured disk neighborhoods, contrary to their injection. Equivalently the loop around such a tip is the nontrivial parabolic $a$, while an ordinary filled disk has trivial monodromy. By [F3], any element of the full $H$ stabilizer of infinity is a translation $z\mapsto z+c$. It carries a sufficiently high upper half-plane to an overlapping upper half-plane. Injection of that punctured neighborhood into $\partial W$ forces this translation to lie in $\langle a\rangle$. Thus the full stabilizer is exactly $\langle a\rangle$, excluding both a proper root and an independent second translation. The finite annular regions from step 4.1 retain each original rim as the boundary of its corresponding puncture neighborhood. [F3, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Suppose a third distinct conformal puncture had a peripheral element conjugate to $a^r$, $r\ne0$. Choose its punctured-disk neighborhood disjoint from the two sufficiently far tails just constructed, and conjugate a lift so its monodromy is $a^r$. The covering restriction over that punctured disk has trivial kernel on fundamental groups, because $a$ has infinite order. Its universal lift is therefore a univalent holomorphic map $u:\mathbb H\to\Omega_H$ with $u(w+1)=u(w)+r$. Since the punctured neighborhood downstairs is disjoint from BOTH tails, its full lift avoids their translates and in particular avoids $\operatorname{Im}z>M$ and $\operatorname{Im}z<-M$. Hence $|\operatorname{Im}u(w)|\le M$. The function $\exp(2\pi i u(w)/r)$ is invariant under $w\mapsto w+1$ and descends, via $q=\exp(2\pi iw)$, to a holomorphic function $F$ on the punctured unit disk. Both $F$ and $1/F$ are bounded by $\exp(2\pi M/|r|)$. By [F5] both extend holomorphically across zero, and their product remains one, so $F(0)\ne0$. On a small disk its values are close to $F(0)$ and the convergent power series for $\log(1+z)$ supplies a holomorphic logarithm of $F/F(0)$. Thus its winding around a small circle is zero. But the lift of that circle is a horizontal segment $w\to w+1$, on which the logarithm $2\pi i u(w)/r$ changes by $2\pi i$. Its winding is one, a contradiction. There is no third such puncture. Any other annulus satisfying the hypotheses and sharing one of the first punctures has conjugate full primitive parabolic stabilizer by step 5.1, so its other puncture cannot be a third. This completes all claims without geometric finiteness or an Ahlfors-finiteness import. [F2, F5, step 4.1, step 5.1, given, construct, contradiction] ∎
