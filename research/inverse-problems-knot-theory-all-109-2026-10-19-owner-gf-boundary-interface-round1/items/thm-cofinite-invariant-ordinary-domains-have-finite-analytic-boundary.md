## Statement

Assume AC. Let $H\le\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)$ be discrete torsion-free and nonelementary. Suppose a simply connected component $D$ of its actual ordinary domain $\Omega_H$ is $H$-invariant, and $S=D/H$ is a complete finite-area hyperbolic surface of finite analytic type, of genus $g$ with $n$ punctures. Its compact truncation and standard cusp collars may be retained as marking data.

Every component of $\Omega_H$ is simply connected. The quotient $\Omega_H/H$ is a FINITE disjoint union of compact Riemann surfaces with finitely many punctures, each carrying its complete finite-area disk-uniformizing metric. Every component stabilizer is nonelementary and finitely generated. There are at most $1+2(3g-3+2n)$ components in the quotient, including $S$.

If, additionally, every parabolic element of $H$ acts parabolically in the disk uniformization of $D$, then EVERY other ordinary component is itself $H$-invariant. Its punctures correspond bijectively to those of $S$, with their primitive peripheral conjugacy classes, up to inversion. This conditional conclusion does not assert that another ordinary component exists.

This establishes the actual analytic-finiteness step of the invariant-boundary cylinder argument. It does not assert geometric finiteness, a Jordan boundary, a full peripheral marking or a product realization of the three-manifold.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual invariant simply connected ordinary component and its supplied finite-type cofinite quotient, in the analytic Kleinian category.

[F3] The actual surface-core proof constructs embedded disks for nullhomotopic simple circles, fills null boundary circles and proves injectivity of compact essential surface regions by their proper boundary lifts. These local arguments do not use surjectivity onto the WHOLE surface except to choose its generator list. Finite compact surfaces have finite graph/polygon presentations. [F6] Constant-rank coordinates identify a locally constant-rank map with $(x,y)\mapsto(x,0)$. [[thm-euclidean-constant-rank-normal-form]]

[F7] Finite smooth chart bumps exist, and regular values common to the interior and boundary restrictions are dense. Thus a sphere-loxodromic element cannot act parabolically on a simply connected invariant ordinary domain: conjugating a putative disk parabolic to $w\mapsto w+1$ would give such a map on the entire upper half-plane, contradicting the bound as $T\to\infty$. For two distinct actual simply connected ordinary components, their stabilizer intersection is root-closed inside either stabilizer. ## Proof

1.1 First $\partial D=\Lambda_H$. The boundary is contained in $\Lambda_H$ because $D$ is an actual ordinary component. Conversely choose any orbit sequence tending to $\xi\in\Lambda_H$ and normalize determinant-one matrices to Euclidean matrix norm one. Discreteness makes the original norms unbounded; a convergent subsequence has rank-one limit, since the normalized determinants tend to zero. Projective values therefore converge to $\xi$ away from its one kernel point. Choose a point of the closed invariant set $\partial D$ different from that point; such a choice is possible since $H$ is nonelementary. Its images converge to $\xi$, proving the other inclusion. Every other ordinary component $E$ is simply connected. Indeed a Jordan loop in $E$ has the connected $D$ on one side; the opposite open Jordan disk misses $D$ and therefore misses $\partial D$, since each of its points has a neighborhood missing $D$. Its closed disk consequently lies in $\Omega_H$ and, by connectedness, in $E$. Arbitrary loops in $E$ are approximated by finite polygonal loops in its planar coordinate and split at their finitely many crossings into Jordan loops, so they contract. Thus $E$ has a disk uniformizer and $Y=E/H_E$ has injective group $\pi_1(Y)=H_E\le H$, where $H_E$ is the FULL stabilizer. The same holds for $S$, whose group is all of $H$. The radial ball contraction contracts $\mathbb H^3\cup\Omega_H$, so these are also the actual group maps into $W=(\mathbb H^3\cup\Omega_H)/H$. [A1, F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Any finite disjoint family of smooth essential circles in the other $Y$'s admits pairwise disjoint compact locally flat annuli to $S$, with those exact first rims. Induct using [F2]'s cut comparison. In the current cut piece the corresponding cut surface of $S$ is group-isomorphic; it has a compact core because it is obtained by finitely many cuts of the given finite-type $S$. Its generator graph can be chosen smooth in its open interior, and the disk-filling and boundary-lift argument of [F3] gives a compact smoothly bounded injective core there. A loop on that core represents the next boundary circle, yielding an actual annulus map. Push its interior off all boundary faces by the supplied collars, preserving its two boundary loops. The compact image and its two compact marked boundary subsurfaces now lie away from all OLD cut faces, in an open smooth submanifold of the original analytic $W$. Choose finitely many relatively compact coordinate balls and half-balls covering this data and a smooth finite bump sum equal to one near it. A common interior/boundary regular level in $(0,1)$ of that sum gives a compact manifold $V$ with corners: the gradients of the level and the ambient boundary coordinate are independent at their intersection. Choose its connected component containing the map. Both marked subsurfaces are in its original boundary trace, away from these new corners. By [F7], triangulate its boundary faces relative to their intersections and the finitely many marked boundary circles, then extend the admissible boundary triangulation over $V$. This is a GIVEN compatible finite PL target, with injective marked surface groups because their composites into the current cut piece are injective. Apply the compact annulus theorem [F2]. Its interior misses $\partial V$, so after regluing it misses every old annulus; its new core is injective because its first rim has infinite order in $H$. Its boundary parameters are finite whole-star piecewise smooth in the original analytic boundary, exactly as required in the cyclic-frontier theorem. Continue the finite induction. No global triangulation or smoothing of $W$, no compact core of $W$ and no noncompact annulus theorem has been inserted. [F2, F3, F7, step 1.1, construct, induction]

3.1 Set $B=3g-3+2n$. At most $B$ pairwise disjoint nontrivial unoriented free-homotopy classes occur on $S$, counting puncture classes. For nonperipheral curves, cutting the compact puncture truncation gives components of negative Euler characteristic, after omitting annular collars of repeated or peripheral curves. If their total nonperipheral count is $c$, their boundary count is $2c+n$ and is at most three times their negative Euler characteristic: a genus-$a$, $b$-boundary component has $b=2-2a-\chi\le-3\chi$ when $\chi\le-1$. Hence $2c+n\le3(2g-2+n)$ and $c\le3g-3+n$; at most $n$ puncture classes remain. Now take any finite family of disjoint pairwise nonhomotopic essential circles in the other $Y$'s. Their annular partners in $S$ from step 2.1 are disjoint. If two partners are parallel on $S$, join their two annuli through that intervening surface annulus and push its middle off $S$ using its collar. This gives a compact locally flat annulus between the two original circles, disjoint from $S$; its outside component contains $S$ and therefore surjects to $H$. The original circles are not homotopic in the ordinary boundary, so [F2] makes them distinct paired puncture rims. A third original circle with the same $S$ class would pair one of these punctures to a third puncture, forbidden by the same theorem. Thus each $S$ class occurs at most twice. With $Q=2B$, EVERY such finite family across the whole other boundary has size at most $Q$. Piecewise smooth intermediate rims cause no category change: the finite graph rounding in the cyclic-frontier proof applies in their actual analytic boundary charts. [F2, F3, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 This curve bound implies finite generation of EVERY other component group; it does not yet assert finite area. If $Y$ is compact this follows from its finite surface triangulation. Otherwise, if its group is nontrivial, take a compact connected smooth regular neighborhood of finitely many nontrivial loops and fill all nullhomotopic boundary circles on the outside by [F3]'s embedded disks. The resulting compact essential subsurface $K$ injects into $Y$ by that supplier's proper-boundary-lift argument. Its genus is at most $Q$: the usual disjoint handle meridians have dual curves of intersection one in $K$, so remain pairwise nonparallel and nontrivial in $Y$. Each ambient parallel class occurs at most twice among $\partial K$. Indeed between consecutive parallel boundaries there is an annulus by [F3]'s elementary cyclic-cover strip argument, which does not use finite generation. If such an annulus is inside connected $K$, then $K$ is that annulus and has only two boundaries. Otherwise it is outside $K$; three parallel boundaries would put both sides of the middle one outside $K$, contradicting its boundary collar. Hence $b(K)\le2Q$ and its free rank $h(K)=2\operatorname{genus}(K)+b(K)-1\le4Q-1$. Choose such a $K$ maximizing the bounded integer pair $(h(K),\operatorname{genus}(K))$ in that order. Any additional loop is contained, together with $K$, in a larger compact essential subsurface $K'$ obtained by the same finite graph-neighborhood and disk filling. In $K'\setminus\operatorname{int}K$, every component is attached along old boundary circles. A disk cap is excluded by their essentiality. A component of negative Euler characteristic increases $h$; an annulus joining TWO old boundaries preserves $h$ but increases genus. The only remaining components are annular collars with one old and one new boundary, which do not change the group. Maximality thus makes $\pi_1(K)\to\pi_1(K')$ surjective. Every loop of $Y$ is in some such $K'$, so $\pi_1(Y)$ is finitely generated. If it was trivial, $Y$ is the disk and is kept as a separate case, not treated as a cofinite surface. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 We give the actual analytic exclusion of disk and bordered-end cases. Since $H=\pi_1(S)$ is finitely generated, fix $r$ generators and take an integer $N>6r$. First suppose an ordinary component $E$ has trivial stabilizer. In disk coordinates choose $N$ independent smooth real functions $v_j$ supported in one proper open circle arc, and put $h_b(\theta)=\theta+\sum_{j=1}^N b_jv_j(\theta)$ for $b$ in a sufficiently small real ball. Its derivative is positive, and $\phi_b(re^{i\theta})=re^{ih_b(\theta)}$ is a uniformly bilipschitz, hence quasiconformal, disk homeomorphism; its boundary values determine $b$ and it fixes the complementary open arc. Direct polar differentiation gives $\mu_b=e^{2i\theta}(1-h_b')/(1+h_b')$ almost everywhere. Thus the coefficients depend real analytically in the $L^\infty$ norm, with a holomorphic complexification on a small parameter ball. Pull these coefficients to $E$ by its disk uniformizer and extend $H$-invariantly to all its translates, putting zero on the remaining sphere. There is no consistency obstruction because the stabilizer is trivial. The translates are a countable disjoint family, since $H$ is finitely generated, so the extension is measurable and retains the same norm and parameter estimates. Let $W_b$ be the normalized sphere solution. By [F4] the $r$ conjugated generator matrices form a real-analytic map from the parameter ball to the real $6r$-dimensional manifold $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)^r$. At a point of maximum differential rank, a nonzero maximal minor remains nonzero locally and no larger rank occurs; [F6] therefore gives a NONCONSTANT parameter arc with all these matrices fixed. The arc lies in one small chart and its parameters remain distinct. [F1, F4, F6, step 1.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Here is the rigidity calculation on that fiber, with no prime-end theorem assumed. Write its base parameter as $b_0$, its common represented group as $H'$, and $g_b=W_bW_{b_0}^{-1}$. Each $g_b$ centralizes $H'$ and fixes its limit set POINTWISE. To verify the last assertion, a commuting sphere homeomorphism fixes every attracting fixed point of every loxodromic element by its iterates. These points are dense: conjugate one fixed loxodromic by a sequence of group matrices tending to any prescribed limit point, and choose one of its two endpoints different from the kernel of the normalized rank-one limit; invert the loxodromic if necessary. Continuity finishes the claim. The continuous arc $g_b$, starting at identity, preserves each ordinary component of $H'$, since the orbit of an ordinary point is a path in the fixed open ordinary set and cannot change its component. Thus $W_bE$ is one fixed domain $E'$. If $u:\mathbb D\to E$ is its original disk map, equal-coefficient cancellation [F4] makes $F_b=W_bu\phi_b^{-1}$ a conformal map onto $E'$. Put $F=F_{b_0}$; then $F_b=F A_b$ for disk Möbius maps $A_b$, varying continuously by evaluation at interior points. Consequently $$F^{-1}g_bF=A_b\phi_b\phi_{b_0}^{-1}.$$ In this disk case the last two factors are radial conings of smooth circle diffeomorphisms. Thus this expression has explicit continuous boundary values, and sends each radius nontangentially to its boundary endpoint, because $A_b$ is Möbius. No assertion about arbitrary quasiconformal boundary limits is needed. [F1, F4, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Postcompose $F$ with a fixed sphere Möbius coordinate making it bounded; this is possible because the disjoint nonempty domain $W_{b_0}D$ contains a disk. For almost every $\theta$ its nontangential limit $a$ exists by [F5] and belongs to $\partial E'\subset\Lambda_{H'}$. Write $\psi_b$ for the explicit circle value of $A_b\phi_b\phi_{b_0}^{-1}$. The identity in step 6.1 and pointwise fixation of $\Lambda_{H'}$ imply $F(A_b\phi_b\phi_{b_0}^{-1}(re^{i\theta}))\to a$. If $F$ has a nontangential limit at $\psi_b(\theta)$, that limit is therefore $a$. If the continuous path $b\mapsto\psi_b(\theta)$ were nonconstant, its image would contain an open circle arc. At almost every angle in that arc [F5] supplies the limit, which would equal this SAME $a$. Positive-measure Hardy uniqueness applied to $F-a$ would make $F$ constant, a contradiction. Hence the path is constant for almost every $\theta$, and circle continuity makes $\psi_b$ identity for all $\theta$. On the fixed complementary arc of the coned families this says $A_b$ is identity; a disk Möbius map fixing an arc is identity. Thus the boundary functions of $\phi_b$ equal those of $\phi_{b_0}$, forcing $b=b_0$, contrary to the nonconstant fiber arc. There are no trivial-stabilizer disk components. This argument explicitly uses nontangential access only for the chosen radial conings, not for arbitrary quasiconformal maps. [F4, F5, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, contradiction]

8.1 After step 7.1 excludes trivial stabilizers, consider a component with nontrivial stabilizer $H_E$. It is finitely generated by step 4.1. Uniformize $E$ by $u:\mathbb D\to E$ and write $L=u^{-1}H_Eu\le\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb D)$. If its quotient is compact it already has finite area. Otherwise [F3] gives finitely many standard cusp and funnel ends when $L$ is nonelementary. Suppose a funnel exists. In its Fermi coordinate $(s,t)$, $t\bmod\ell$, the metric is $ds^2+\cosh^2s\,dt^2$. The conformal coordinate $t+iv$, $v=\int_0^s\operatorname{sech}q\,dq$, is a finite-height cylinder, $0<v<\pi/2$. Choose a cutoff $\eta(v)$ zero near its inner edge and one near $\pi/2$, and $N$ independent smooth periodic functions $v_j(t)$ supported in a proper subarc of one period. The maps $(t,v)\mapsto(t+\eta(v)\sum b_jv_j(t),v)$ are diffeomorphisms for small real $b$, are identity near the inner edge, and extend by identity over the rest of $Y$. Their derivatives and inverses have uniform bounds in this conformal cylinder, so they are uniformly quasiconformal. Their coefficient is a quotient of linear expressions in $b$ with denominator bounded away from zero; hence it has the same holomorphic $L^\infty$ complexification as before. Lift the identity-based isotopy to $\mathbb D$. Its lift $\phi_b$ commutes with $L$. On each ideal boundary interval of a lifted funnel it extends smoothly with nonzero boundary derivative, preserves that interval, and fixes an open subinterval, by the explicit finite-height coordinate. Its boundary values on ONE such interval already determine $b$. These observations follow in the upper-half-plane axis model: the coordinate $t+iv$ extends analytically across every compact subarc of that ideal interval; only its two axis endpoints are excluded. [F1, F3, F4, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 7.1, construct, algebra]

9.1 Pull these lifted coefficients to $E$ and extend over its $H$ orbit. Commutation with $L$ makes the coefficient $H_E$-invariant, so this is well-defined even when several group elements name the same component. Repeating step 5.1 gives a nonconstant constant-representation arc, and step 6.1 gives the SAME identity $F^{-1}g_bF=A_b\phi_b\phi_{b_0}^{-1}$. Here $A_b$ centralizes $L$, because both conformal maps $F_b,F$ intertwine $L$ with the SAME represented subgroup. If $L$ is nonelementary, this Möbius centralizer is identity by two loxodromics with different fixed sets. If $L$ is cyclic loxodromic it fixes its two endpoints separately and preserves each interval between them; if cyclic parabolic it fixes its sole endpoint and preserves its complementary interval. Thus in each case the chosen ideal interval $I$ is preserved throughout the arc. The expression is smooth at every point of $I$, carries radii nontangentially there, and has a common open subinterval where both $\phi_b$ and $\phi_{b_0}$ are identity. The Fatou/Hardy argument of step 7.1, now only on $I$, forces its boundary values to be identity on $I$. On the common fixed subinterval this forces $A_b$ to be identity; the injective boundary parameter family on $I$ then forces $b=b_0$, the same contradiction. Hence no funnel exists. For cyclic groups the explicit loxodromic cylinder has two such finite-height ends, and the parabolic cylinder has the finite-height end $0<\operatorname{Im}z<T$ in $\mathbb H/\langle z\mapsto z+\ell\rangle$. The identical periodic collar deformation applies to either. Thus nontrivial cyclic component stabilizers are excluded as well; they are not mislabeled cofinite hyperbolic annuli. [F1, F3, F4, F5, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, step 8.1, construct, contradiction]

10.1 Every remaining component stabilizer is consequently nonelementary and finitely generated. A noncompact quotient has no funnels, so [F3]'s COMPLETE end description consists of its compact core and finitely many standard cusps, with finite area. Compact quotients are finite-area closed surfaces. Each such surface has at least one essential simple circle. Choosing one from each of any finite collection of the other components gives pairwise nonhomotopic disjoint circles in the ordinary boundary; step 3.1 bounds their number by $Q$. Thus there are at most $Q$ other components, not an unexamined infinite family of disks or triply punctured spheres. Adding the given $S$ gives the stated $1+Q$ bound. Every cusp is conformally a puncture in the explicit standard model, so these are exactly compact Riemann surfaces with finitely many punctures. No geometric-finiteness, Jordan-boundary or relative-product conclusion has been inferred. This proves the asserted actual analytic-finiteness operation. [F3, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 7.1, step 9.1, construct, algebra]


11.1 Assume now the additional no-accidental-parabolic hypothesis, and fix another component $E$ with $K=H_E$. Put $Y=E/K$. It is cofinite and of finite type by step 10.1. Each puncture generator of $Y$ is parabolic in its disk action. It cannot be sphere-loxodromic by [F8], so it is sphere-parabolic. The added hypothesis makes it parabolic also on $D$. It is primitive in $K$: a root in $K$ acts on $E$ with the same parabolic fixed point, and the discrete disk stabilizer of a cusp is generated by its primitive peripheral element. Distinct punctures of $Y$ give distinct maximal-parabolic conjugacy classes in $K$, since in the disk uniformization these are precisely the distinct cusp orbits. These are assertions about the SAME group $K$, so their distinctness persists in its action on $D$. Uniformize $D$ and consider the Fuchsian surface $Z=D/K$. Its group is finitely generated. If it is noncompact, [F3] supplies a compact core $F$ with finitely many cusp or funnel boundary circles and free fundamental group. Every puncture class just identified is consequently a distinct primitive cusp boundary class of $F$, up to inversion. [F1, F3, F8, step 10.1, given, construct, algebra]

12.1 Suppose first $Y$ has $m>0$ punctures. Its oriented compact truncation has the relation $sum_{j=1}^m[c_j]=0$ in the abelianization of $K$. If $Z$ is compact it has no parabolics, already a contradiction. Otherwise transport this relation into $H_1(F;mathbb Z)$. Each $c_j$ is one DISTINCT boundary class of $F$ with coefficient $+1$ or $-1$, by step 11.1. In a connected oriented genus-$a$ surface with $b>0$ boundary circles, the ONLY relation among those boundary classes is their total oriented sum. Indeed its polygon presentation has handle generators and boundary generators $d_1,ldots,d_b$, with the one relator $prod_{
u=1}^a[x_

u]d_1cdots d_b$; abelianizing leaves exactly $d_1+cdots+d_b=0$, with the handle classes independent. This also covers $b=1$, whose single boundary class is zero. The nonzero coefficient vector given by the $m$ distinct classes can therefore be a relation only if it uses ALL $b$ boundary circles, with the same coefficient after orientation. Thus there are no funnel boundaries and no unused cusp boundaries. By [F3], $Z$ has finite area. If $Y$ is closed instead, $K$ is a closed aspherical surface group. It cannot be the free group of a noncompact $Z$: a finite graph and $Y$ would both be classifying spaces for $K$, but their second integral homology groups are respectively zero and $mathbb Z$. Explicitly maps inducing the group isomorphism are built on edges, extended over the surface relator by its null word, and their composites are homotopic to identity by lifting cell homotopies to the contractible disk or tree; hence they would induce isomorphic homology groups. This contradiction makes $Z$ compact in this case. Again it has finite area. [F3, step 11.1, construct, contradiction]

13.1 The covering $D/K	o D/H=S$ is a local hyperbolic isometry. Choose an embedded metric disk in $S$ with positive area. Its inverse image consists of $[H:K]$ disjoint isometric disks, one for each sheet. Since $D/K$ has finite area by step 12.1, the index is finite. In the closed case both surfaces have no punctures. Actual basepoint paths realizing these conjugacies may be retained as tracks; no prescribed transverse annulus track, full boundary homeomorphism or three-dimensional product has been inferred.
