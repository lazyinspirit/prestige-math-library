## Statement

Assume AC. Let $\Gamma\le\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)$ be discrete, torsion-free, nonelementary and geometrically finite. Let $D$ be a component of its ordinary domain, and $H=\operatorname{Stab}_\Gamma(D)$. Suppose the conformal boundary component $D/H\to(\mathbb H^3\cup\Omega_\Gamma)/\Gamma$ is fundamental-group injective. Suppose, additionally, that every parabolic element of $H$ acts parabolically in the disk uniformization of $D$.

Then $H$ is geometrically finite, its limit set is a quasicircle, and $D$ and the opposite component $D^*$ of its sphere complement are quasidisks with one finite common quasidisk constant for this fixed model. Both are $H$-invariant. Their quotients are finite-type complete finite-area hyperbolic surfaces. Their punctures correspond bijectively by full primitive maximal-parabolic conjugacy classes, with the actual conjugating paths retained. There are no additional components of $\Omega_H$.

The no-accidental-parabolic condition is not inferred from incompressibility alone. It follows, in particular, from the following ACTUAL realized pared marking data. Let $M$ be compact connected oriented, smooth or a GIVEN finite PL three-manifold, and $P$ a finite union of injective collared annuli and entire torus boundary components, with every torus boundary component included. Supply an isomorphism $\rho:\pi_1(M)\to\Gamma$ sending the peripheral subgroups to parabolics and accounting for every parabolic conjugacy class. Let $F$ be a component of $\partial M\setminus\operatorname{int}P$ with an actual boundary marking inducing an isomorphism $\pi_1(F)\to H$ equal to its inclusion followed by $\rho$. A componentwise homotopy equivalence in an incompressible realization supplies precisely this group datum. Suppose EVERY essential SIMPLE circle in $\operatorname{int}F$ homotopic through $M$ into $P$ is homotopic through $\partial M$ into $P$. Then the no-accidental condition above holds, and all the stated geometric conclusions follow. No all-loops version of that simple-circle condition is assumed. Both given categories and all peripheral tracks are retained. Elementary, empty-ordinary-domain and torus-boundary branches are not silently included in its negative-Euler-characteristic surface input. No initial hyperbolic representation or model for an original topological manifold is constructed here.

## Proof

1.1 By [F1], $D$ is simply connected, $S=D/H$ is finite-type cofinite, and $D$ is a FULL ordinary component for $H$. In particular $H$ is nonelementary. If $H=\Gamma$, it is already GF; only the final hull comparison will be needed in this branch. Otherwise choose $g\in\Gamma\setminus H$. The distinct ambient ordinary component $gD$ is disjoint from $D$. Since $\Omega_\Gamma\subset\Omega_H$, it lies in another actual component $E$ of $\Omega_H$. By [F2], $E$ is simply connected and $H$-invariant, $Y=E/H$ is cofinite finite-type, and $S,Y$ have the same primitive cusp classes. Both inclusions into $W=(\mathbb H^3\cup\Omega_H)/H$ induce group isomorphisms. Their compact cusp truncations have the same genus: for noncompact surfaces use the free rank $2g+n-1$ and the cusp bijection; for closed surfaces use the rank $2g$ of the abelianization. They have negative Euler characteristic. [A1, F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 If there are punctures, choose one smooth cusp rim in $Y$ for each of its finitely many punctures. Apply [F2]'s simultaneous annulus construction to obtain disjoint annuli to $S$. Each partner on $S$ has the corresponding peripheral class, by the primitive cusp bijection. The finitely many distinct peripheral partners can be made the rims of disjoint cusp truncation collars on $S$ by their surface annulus parameters; extend those finite collar adjustments to the annuli. In the finite cut comparison, cutting $S$ at such a peripheral rim leaves its cusp-tail annulus and its core side, whose group is ALL of $H$. The corresponding outside cut piece therefore surjects to $H$. The two rims are on different conformal boundary components and are not homotopic there. Thus [F3] applies to every annulus. It gives the two actual puncture tails and the canonical tube for each primitive class. Choose the finite family of tubes disjoint and sufficiently far into their paired sides. The original cylinders' compact initial parts do not change their labels. Write $S_0,Y_0$ for the compact surfaces left after removing these canonical ordinary tails, and $A_i$ for the tube-frontier annuli. If there are no punctures put $S_0=S$, $Y_0=Y$ and use no tubes. Let $W'$ be what remains of $W$ after deleting the open ends of these canonical tubes, retaining $A_i$. It is a connected manifold with corners. The polar tube formula gives a strong deformation retraction $W\to W'$ by $r\mapsto(1-s)r+sR$, fixed at $r=R$ and outside the tubes. Consequently $\pi_1(W')=H$ and its universal cover is contractible: lift this deformation to the contractible cover $\mathbb H^3\cup\Omega_H$. No compactness of $W'$ has yet been assumed. [F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 We construct the FULL boundary marking, including the transverse paths. In the punctured case choose $s_0\in\partial S_0$, its corresponding $y_0\in\partial Y_0$, and let $\tau:s_0\to y_0$ be the actual first tube-frontier path. For each other rim choose $s_i$, its corresponding $y_i$, a path $\sigma_i:s_0\to s_i$ in $S_0$, and its actual frontier path $\delta_i:s_i\to y_i$. Use the tube parameter to prescribe the circle homeomorphism $b_i:\partial_iS_0\to\partial_iY_0$, retaining its actual sign. Inclusion identifies both surface groups with $H$. Thus it gives an isomorphism $\alpha:\pi_1(S_0,s_0)\to\pi_1(Y_0,y_0)$ characterized by $i_Y\alpha(\ell)=\tau^{-1}i_S(\ell)\tau$. Since $i_Y$ is SURJECTIVE, choose a path $\eta_i:y_0\to y_i$ in $Y_0$ whose ambient relative-endpoint class is that of $\tau^{-1}\sigma_i\delta_i$: start with any path in $Y_0$ and correct it by a based loop realizing the discrepancy in $H$. For the distinguished rim use constant paths. The actual annulus identity $c\delta_i\simeq\delta_i b_i(c)$ then gives precisely $\alpha(\sigma_i c\sigma_i^{-1})=\eta_i b_i(c)\eta_i^{-1}$. Apply [F4] to obtain a homeomorphism $h:S_0\to Y_0$ equal to every $b_i$ and realizing EVERY $\eta_i$. All surfaces and these finite parameters carry compatible finite PL structures from their smooth collars, as required by that supplier. In the closed case choose any $\tau$ and construct a map realizing the same isomorphism $\alpha$ on edges and the surface relation. Relative approximation and [F4]'s closed surface classification give a degree-one covering $h$, homotopic to that map; hence it is a homeomorphism with the specified based marking. [F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Put $F=S_0$ and $X=F\times[0,1]$. On its bottom boundary use the inclusion of $S_0$, on its top use $h$ into $Y_0$, and on every side annulus use the actual polar frontier $A_i$, with its prescribed circle endpoints. These maps paste to a homeomorphism $b$ from $\partial X$ onto their compact union $B\subset\partial W'$. This is a union of entire boundary components: at the rim seams the supplied quadrant charts give full boundary neighborhoods, while away from seams the surface or annulus charts do; hence the image is open as well as compact closed in $\partial W'$. It is not assumed to be ALL of that boundary. Its induced group data agree with the projection $X\to F$ and inclusion of $F$ into $W'$. Indeed the bottom and top loops agree by $\alpha$, and each additional connector loop has ambient class $\sigma_i\delta_i(h\sigma_i)^{-1}\tau^{-1}=1$ by the exact paths in step 3.1. These generate the boundary group together with its two surface groups, as follows by cutting the finitely many side annuli along their transverse paths and applying van Kampen. For closed $F$, the two source boundary components have the same compatible group map with connecting path $\tau$. Prescribe also an entire small source collar of $\partial X$ to map by $(x,t)\mapsto(b(x),t)$ into the target boundary collar. This does not change any group or path condition, since the collars retract to their boundaries. Apply [F5] to this finite subpair of $X$ and the contractible-cover target $W'$, obtaining an actual continuous $f:X\to W'$ equal to this product on the collar. [F3, F4, F5, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Push any remaining interior contacts with $\partial W'$ inward, keeping a smaller prescribed source collar fixed. This is an explicit target-collar operation: in its height coordinate replace height by its maximum with a small positive function on source interior, zero on the fixed boundary, and taper the change before the target collar's outer edge. Only finitely many boundary charts are needed on the compact image. The resulting map sends $\operatorname{int}X$ into $\operatorname{int}W'$. Push forward the finite oriented fundamental chain of $X$. Its boundary lies in $B$, so it defines local integral degrees at every point of $\operatorname{int}W'$. These degrees are locally constant: in a coordinate ball missing the pushed boundary chain, excision and the ball orientation trivialization [F6] identify all point restrictions of the same relative cycle with one integer. Overlapping balls and connectedness propagate that integer. It is $+1$ near a chosen bottom boundary point, after orienting $X$ there to match $W'$: the product collar is its only sheet sufficiently near that point. The compact image of the remaining source complement misses this boundary point, so shrinking its target neighborhood excludes any additional sheet. Therefore the local degree is one throughout the connected interior. Every interior point must lie in the compact image $f(X)$, since a missing point has zero local degree. Every boundary point is a limit of interior points and $f(X)$ is closed; thus $W'=f(X)$ is COMPACT. Moreover no boundary point can lie outside $B$: only source boundary maps to target boundary, so such a point could not be in $f(X)$. We have proved the actual entire-boundary identification and compactness, not assumed a compact core or a punctured-product theorem. [F5, F6, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 This compact truncation proves that $H$ is GF directly. In each of its finitely many canonical cusp frames the paired tails give $\Lambda_H\setminus\{\infty\}\subset\{|y|\le M\}$. The convex hull lies between the vertical geodesic planes $y=\pm M$. For a point $(x+iy,t)$ at hyperbolic distance at most $a$ from this slab, the plane-distance formula gives $|y|\le M+t\sinh a$. Hence its volume in the unit-translation cusp quotient above $t=T>0$ is at most $$\int_T^\infty\frac{2M+2t\sinh a}{t^3}\,dt<\infty.$$ On the canonical tube $\sqrt{y^2+t^2}\ge R$, that same inequality makes $t$ bounded below away from zero when $R>M$; the remaining bounded-height strip has finite volume as well. Outside the tubes, the closed $a$-neighborhood of the hull is compact in the HYPERBOLIC interior of the compact $W'$: it has no ordinary-boundary accumulation, because the ideal boundary of a fixed-distance neighborhood of a hyperbolic hull is exactly its limit set, whereas those boundary charts lie in $\Omega_H$. This last assertion follows directly in the ball model: bounded hyperbolic distance makes two sequences approaching the sphere have the same Euclidean limit, and the hull's ideal boundary is $\Lambda_H$. Compactness now gives finite volume there. Every fixed-radius hull neighborhood has finite quotient volume, which is the GF volume criterion. There is no rank-two cusp hidden here, since the actual paired-tail theorem gives FULL rank-one stabilizers. If there were no punctures, the compact $W'$ alone proves the same conclusion. [F3, step 2.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Finally let $L$ be the cofinite Fuchsian uniformizing group of $D/H$. Its hull is the full hyperbolic plane: its complete end classification has no funnels, so no ideal interval can be omitted from its limit circle. Its plane-neighborhood quotient has finite volume by the Fermi metric $dr^2+\cosh^2r\,ds_{\mathbb H^2}^2$, with factor $\int_{-a}^a\cosh^2r\,dr$ times the finite surface area. Thus $L$ is GF. The isomorphism $L\to H$ is type-preserving in BOTH directions: the stated no-accidental condition is one direction; the converse is the arbitrary-height univalent-strip exclusion in [F7], since a disk parabolic cannot become sphere-loxodromic. Apply [F7] to the two ACTUAL GF groups, using step 6.1 or the $H=\Gamma$ branch of step 1.1. It gives a continuous equivariant plane-to-hull quasi-isometry. The actual quasicircle theorem then makes $\Lambda_H$ a quasicircle with a finite common bound for both complementary quasidisks. Since $D$ is a full component, it is one of them; the other is $D^*$ and there are no additional components. Each is $H$-invariant, since $H$ preserves $D$ and hence also its complement. Both quotients are finite-type cofinite by [F2], including the primitive puncture bijection and the paths already constructed. This proves all geometric outputs for the stated genuine input, without asserting the missing original topological model or deriving its no-accidental condition by fiat. [F1, F2, F7, step 1.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]


8.1 After step 7.1 proves the geometric conclusion with its stated input, we verify that input for the additional pared-marking implication without strengthening its SIMPLE-circle hypothesis. Write $J=i_*\pi_1(F)\le\pi_1(M)$; the supplied marking identifies $J$ isomorphically with $H$. For any nontrivial parabolic $c\in H$, peripheral accounting expresses its inverse image, with a suitable base path, as $g a^r g^{-1}$, where $r\ne0$ and $a$ is a primitive element of an annular or torus peripheral group. Such an $a$ is its annular core or a primitive torus slope: divide the two integral torus coordinates by their greatest common divisor. The element $\gamma=\rho(gag^{-1})$ is parabolic and satisfies $\gamma^r=c\in H$. By [F8]'s ONE-component parabolic root theorem, $\gamma\in H$; no second component or no-accidental input is needed. Take the subgroup cover $M_J\to M$. The chosen lift $F_J$ is HOMEOMORPHIC to $F$, because its full group lies in $J$, and $\pi_1(F_J)\to\pi_1(M_J)=J$ is an isomorphism. The path $g$ lifts from its basepoint and the loop $a$ closes on the corresponding lift of $P$. This lifted peripheral component has a genuine compact annular neighborhood of that loop. For annular $P$ its whole group is in $J$. For torus $P$, its intersection with $J$ is cyclic: after disk uniformization every commuting nontrivial subgroup of the discrete torsion-free disk group has cyclic centralizer, as seen by normalizing its hyperbolic axis or parabolic fixed point and using discreteness of real translations. The intersection contains the primitive torus slope $a$, so is exactly $\langle a\rangle$; a cyclic lattice subgroup containing a primitive lattice vector cannot be a larger cyclic subgroup. Thus this torus lift is the actual cylinder cover, and $a$ is its embedded core. No claim that an arbitrary subgroup is root-closed is being used. [F1, F7, F8, step 7.1, given, construct, algebra]

9.1 Push $F_J$ slightly inward from its surface boundary collar to a compact core $F_1\subset\operatorname{int}F_J$, with the same group, and choose an annular boundary neighborhood $F_0$ of the just constructed peripheral core, disjoint from $F_1$. Surjectivity of $\pi_1(F_1)\to J$ supplies an annulus map from that core to a loop in $F_1$. In the PL case the cover has the induced GIVEN locally finite triangulation, and [F8]'s finite-star neighborhood construction supplies an actual compact finite PL target containing the map and both marked subsurfaces, with the required injective groups. In the smooth case triangulate the compact pair first, relative to the finite paring rims, using [F8], and use the same cover construction. Apply the actual compact annulus theorem with FIRST rim fixed at $a$. Its other rim is an essential SIMPLE circle $\beta_J\subset\operatorname{int}F_J$. It represents $a$ up to conjugacy in the cover group $J$, regardless of which transverse conjugator the theorem retained. The homeomorphism $F_J\to F$ projects it to an essential simple circle $\beta\subset\operatorname{int}F$. The original parabolic $c$ is conjugate in $H$ to $\rho(\beta)^r$, because ALL these conjugacies took place in $M_J$ and its group is $J$, not the larger ambient group. The projected embedded annulus gives a homotopy of $\beta$ through $M$ into $P$. Apply the stated SIMPLE-circle extra to this actual simple $\beta$; it gives a homotopy through $\partial M$. By [F8]'s surface-only boundary-to-paring theorem, $\beta$ is peripheral inside $F$. Here a whole torus in $P$ cannot be the endpoint on the boundary component containing $F$; such a component has empty complement. On that actual boundary component the paring is a union of annuli, exactly as that supplier requires. Since $\pi_1(F)=J$, the original $c$ consequently pulls back to a peripheral power INSIDE $F$. Thus every $H$-parabolic word is $F$-peripheral. We did not apply the simple-circle extra to an arbitrary pulled-back word, nor claim that the downstairs annulus theorem preserves a prescribed conjugator. [F8, step 8.1, given, construct, algebra]

10.1 It remains to transfer this peripheral information to the ACTUAL disk metric on $S=D/H$; an unmarked free-group isomorphism alone is insufficient. By [F1], $S$ is cofinite finite-type. Its disk cusp generators are sphere-parabolic by [F7]'s strip exclusion, so step 9.1 makes each one a power of a boundary circle of $F$. Both cusp generators and essential surface boundary circles are primitive in their surface groups: lift a hypothetical proper power of an embedded essential circle to its cyclic subgroup cover, whose compact annular core has embedded-circle winding $\pm1$. Thus these powers are $\pm1$. Distinct $S$ cusp classes give distinct boundary circles of $F$, since distinct boundary circles of a negative-Euler-characteristic surface cannot have conjugate nonzero powers; a cyclic-cover annular strip would otherwise make the connected surface an annulus. If $S$ has punctures, $F$ cannot be closed: a closed aspherical surface group cannot be the free group of $S$, by the disk/tree classifying-space and second-homology argument. The oriented relation summing ALL cusp classes of $S$ to zero therefore gives a relation among distinct boundary classes of $F$, with coefficients $\pm1$. The finite polygon presentation of $F$ has only ONE boundary-class relation, their total oriented sum; it follows that these are ALL its boundary circles. Hence every peripheral class of $F$ acts disk-parabolically on $S$. Step 9.1 now proves the same for EVERY parabolic of $H$, which is the desired no-accidental condition. If $S$ is closed, $F$ must instead be closed by the same closed-surface/free-group distinction. An $H$-parabolic would then, by step 9.1, be a nonzero power of a nonexistent boundary circle, a contradiction. This proves that case as well. The negative-Euler-characteristic qualifications used here follow from the group isomorphism to the nonelementary disk group: sphere, disk, annulus and torus groups are trivial or abelian and cannot occur. We have now derived the exact input of the first part from the stated realized pared data and its SIMPLE-circle extra, so that part gives all its quasifuchsian boundary outputs in both given categories. Constructing such a realized model or proving those actual pared/hierarchy inputs for original72 remains a separate obligation. [F1, F7, F8, step 8.1, step 9.1, construct, algebra] ∎
