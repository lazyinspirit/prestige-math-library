## Statement

Assume AC and fix integers $g,n\ge0$ with $2g-2+n>0$. For each positive integer $j$ supply a discrete torsion-free group $\Gamma_j\le\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)$, an ordinary component $D_j$ whose boundary is a Jordan curve, and its full stabilizer $H_j$. Suppose $D_j/H_j$ is a compact genus-$g$ Riemann surface minus $n$ punctures, with its complete finite-area disk metric and compact standard cusp truncations. Supply another simply connected ordinary component $E_j\ne D_j$, its full stabilizer $K_j$, a loxodromic element $\gamma_j\in K_j\setminus H_j$, and a point $x_j\in E_j$ such that

$$d_{E_j}(x_j,\gamma_jx_j)\longrightarrow0.$$

Then $\Lambda H_j=\partial D_j$, $H_j$ is geometrically finite with finite volume for every fixed-radius convex neighborhood, and the other component $D_j^*$ of $\Omega H_j$ contains $E_j$. Both conformal quotients and both intrinsic convex-hull-facing surfaces have compact cusp truncations of type $(g,n)$ and area $2\pi(2g-2+n)$.

There are Möbius coordinates $Q_j$ and a number $c>0$ depending only on $(g,n)$, such that, for all sufficiently large $j$, the normalized data satisfy

$$Q_j\gamma_jQ_j^{-1}\longrightarrow G(z)=\frac{z}{1+cz},\qquad Q_jx_j\longrightarrow0.$$

The convergence is in the Möbius group, $G$ is a nonidentity parabolic fixing zero, and $\infty\in Q_j\Lambda H_j$. Writing $\Delta_r=\{z:|z|<r\}$, the closed disk $\overline\Delta_2$ is contained in $Q_jD_j^*$, and its projection into $Q_jD_j^*/(Q_jH_jQ_j^{-1})$ is injective. Moreover $(Q_j\gamma_jQ_j^{-1})(\Delta_{3/2})\subset\Delta_2$. The projected disk is an actual univalent chart in the normalized sphere coordinate. These conclusions are uniform over all supplied data of the fixed surface type.

The Jordan-boundary model is an explicit hypothesis. This result does not construct an initial geometrically finite realization, prove Jordan boundary from incompressibility alone, or infer that an abstract subgroup is an actual ordinary-component stabilizer.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The exact geometric components, full stabilizers, cofinite boundary data, nonliftable elements and displacement sequence in the Statement.

[F1] For an invariant Jordan domain its disk uniformizer extends equivariantly to the Jordan circle, its conjugated disk group is discrete and torsion-free, and parabolic/loxodromic types correspond exactly. [[lem-jordan-invariant-domains-exclude-accidental-parabolics]]

[F2] An equivariant homeomorphism of actual limit sets preserves geometric finiteness, including finite volume of every fixed-radius convex neighborhood. Its full proof supplies the normalized rank-one matrix convergence and recognition of ordinary orbits used below. [[thm-limit-set-conjugacies-preserve-geometric-finiteness-in-hyperbolic-three-space]]

[F3] Actual geometrically finite groups have finite conformal type and compact standard conformal cusp truncations for injective ordinary-component monodromy. Simply connected ordinary components automatically have this injective monodromy. Actual domes have their complete hyperbolic metric, proper surjective quotient map inducing the identity on the deck group, and exact cusp-end/primitive-peripheral correspondence when the conformal quotient is cofinite. [[lem-standard-gf-truncations-give-finite-conformal-boundaries-and-cofinite-incompressible-uniformizers]], [[lem-simply-connected-domes-have-complete-hyperbolic-metrics-and-uniform-short-loop-carriers]]

[F4] The finite-type intrinsic area is computed by [[cor-gauss-bonnet-for-compact-oriented-surfaces-with-smooth-boundary]]. Truncating a cusp $\{y>T\}/(z\mapsto z+\ell)$ removes area $\ell/T$, and the signed boundary curvature on the retained core is $-1$, hence its boundary integral is $-\ell/T$. Thus that formula gives total area $-2\pi\chi$ exactly after restoring the cusp tails. An intrinsic hyperbolic surface of area $A$ has, at every point, a nontrivial based loop of length at most $L=2\operatorname{arcosh}(1+(A+1)/(2\pi))+1$: otherwise its embedded disk of radius $(L-1)/2$ would have area $2\pi(\cosh((L-1)/2)-1)=A+1$. Lift a segment to a nearest nontrivial translate, or approximate the displacement infimum, to obtain the loop. This argument uses the complete disk covering metric from [F3].

[F5] For every bound $C$ there is $\delta(C)>0$ such that a nonidentity $\delta(C)$-short element and an element moving the same point by at most $C$ belong to the same full abelian elementary stabilizer. This includes bounded screw rotations, handled by its actual compact-unitary bounded-power proof. The local thin classifier supplies precisely invariant finite-radius loxodromic displacement tubes and the full cyclic loxodromic stabilizer. [[lem-bounded-displacement-isometries-preserve-the-fixed-set-of-a-sufficiently-short-element]], [[lem-local-thin-components-of-torsion-free-kleinian-quotients]]

[F6] For the actual distinct simply connected ordinary components in the Statement, $H_j\cap K_j$ is root-closed inside $K_j$. Consequently a nontrivial element of $H_j$ cannot have the same loxodromic fixed pair as $\gamma_j\notin H_j$: their full ambient fixed-pair stabilizer is cyclic, so a nonzero power of $\gamma_j$ would belong to $H_j$. [[lem-two-simply-connected-ordinary-components-have-root-closed-stabilizer-intersections]]

[F7] A univalent equivariant strip of height $T$ and period $L$ has a logarithmic multiplier increment satisfying $|\lambda|\le2\pi L/T$. For a hyperbolic disk action of translation length $L$ mapping to an ambient loxodromic, its branch satisfies $|\lambda|\le2L$. [[lem-equivariant-univalent-strips-bound-loxodromic-log-multipliers]]

[F8] After a complementary point is put at infinity, the quarter-disk theorem gives $\rho_E(z)\ge1/(2\delta_E(z))$ on a simply connected domain. In particular if zero and infinity are outside $E$, then $\rho_E(z)\ge1/(2|z|)\ge\sigma(z)/2$, where $\sigma(z)=2/(1+|z|^2)$ is spherical length density. [[thm-koebe-one-quarter-theorem]]

[A1] Full choice is [[def-axiom-of-choice]].

## Proof

1.1 First derive the geometric model consequences used later, rather than adding a finite-volume assumption on $H_j$. By [F1] a disk uniformizer of $D_j$ extends to its Jordan boundary, conjugating $H_j$ to a cofinite disk group $L_j$. Its disk limit set is the whole circle. Indeed any nonempty complementary gap interval would determine an exterior hyperbolic half-plane. Distinct gap translates have disjoint half-planes, so the stabilizer quotient embeds in the disk quotient. The gap stabilizer fixes its two endpoints and is trivial or cyclic hyperbolic. The half-plane has infinite area if the stabilizer is trivial; in the cyclic case Fermi coordinates about the bounding axis give area $\ell\int_0^\infty\cosh t\,dt=\infty$. Either contradicts cofiniteness. The boundary extension therefore identifies the circle with $\Lambda H_j$. To verify the latter equality with the three-dimensional orbit definition, take a point in $D_j$. The rank-one convergence proof in [F2] says every divergent group subsequence converges on compact sphere sets off one kernel point to its interior-orbit limit point; the kernel belongs to $\Lambda\Gamma_j$ and hence is outside $D_j$. Thus ordinary-point orbit accumulation and interior-orbit accumulation coincide. Equivariance and the closed-disk homeomorphism now give $\Lambda H_j=\partial D_j$. For $L_j$, the convex hull is its invariant hyperbolic plane. Its radius-$a$ neighborhood has quotient volume $\operatorname{area}(\mathbb D/L_j)\int_{-a}^a\cosh^2t\,dt<\infty$: in the hyperboloid parametrization $(\cosh t\,y,\sinh t)$ its metric is $dt^2+\cosh^2t\,ds_{H^2}^2$. Apply [F2] to the actual circle conjugacy to conclude geometric finiteness of $H_j$ and finite volume of every radius-$a$ convex neighborhood. Since the Jordan circle has exactly two complementary disks, write its other disk as $D_j^*$. The component $E_j$ lies in it, because it is connected, misses $D_j$, and lies outside $\Lambda H_j\subset\Lambda\Gamma_j$. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 Put $\varepsilon_j=d_{E_j}(x_j,\gamma_jx_j)\to0$. The disk action of $\gamma_j$ on $E_j$ is hyperbolic. If it were parabolic, put that disk action at $w\mapsto w+1$ on the upper half-plane and sphere-conjugate $\gamma_j$ to multiplication by its expanding multiplier $a_j$, $|a_j|>1$. The composed uniformizer is a univalent map $F:H^+\to\mathbb C^*$ with $F(w+1)=a_jF(w)$. Its holomorphic logarithm on the half-plane has one fixed period increment $\lambda$ with real part $\log|a_j|>0$. Applying [F7] on the nested strips $0<\operatorname{Im}w<T$ gives $|\lambda|\le2\pi/T$ for every $T>0$, a contradiction. The action cannot be elliptic because it is free and properly discontinuous on $E_j$. Thus its disk translation length $L_j$ is positive and at most $\varepsilon_j$. The hyperbolic consequence of [F7] gives a branch $\lambda_j$ of its expanding sphere multiplier satisfying $\operatorname{Re}\lambda_j>0$ and $|\lambda_j|\le2L_j\le2\varepsilon_j$. In particular $\lambda_j\to0$. This supplies the complex multiplier control, rather than inferring matrix compactness from small real length alone. [F1, F7, given, construct, contradiction]

2.1 Apply [F3] to $H_j$ and its two simply connected ordinary components. Both disk quotients have compact standard cusp truncations. Parabolic conjugacy classes of the common abstract group correspond exactly to punctures on either quotient: this is the primitive puncture classification in [F3] together with the boundary type correspondence [F1]. They therefore have the same number $n$ of punctures. Their genera agree as well: the rank of their abelianized fundamental group is $2g$ for $n=0$ and $2g+n-1$ for $n>0$, by the usual finite surface polygon cell count, while the group is the same $H_j$. Thus both have type $(g,n)$. By the dome assertion of [F3], both facing intrinsic boundary quotients have this same group and number of cusp ends, hence the same genus by that count. Formula [F4] gives their area $A=2\pi(2g-2+n)$. These facing surfaces cover ALL the ordinary boundary of $C_j=\operatorname{CH}(\Lambda H_j)$: every supporting plane has a nonempty exterior ideal round disk in one of the two components of $\Omega H_j$, and its ideal contact face is exactly the corresponding dome face by [F3]. If $C_j$ is planar the two facing copies are the same plane with opposite normal sides; they still provide the same loop bound. Consequently through EVERY point of $\partial C_j$ there is a nontrivial $H_j$-loop of intrinsic, and hence ambient, length at most the common $L=L(A)$ from [F4]. This assertion is about the entire boundary, not only the original $D_j$-facing part. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 Choose a hyperbolic radius $R$ with $\sinh R>100$. We will normalize with a fixed small displacement $d_0>0$, depending only on $(g,n)$. Let $P_{d_0}$ be the matrix $\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\2\sinh(d_0/2)&1\end{pmatrix}$. It tends to the identity as $d_0\downarrow0$. Choose $d_0<\min\{\delta(10),1/20\}$ so small that every point of the compact hyperbolic ball $\overline B_R(o)$ is moved by $P_{d_0}$ less than $\eta/2$. This is possible by continuity of the explicit matrix action, uniformly on that compact ball. Let $\alpha_j,\beta_j$ be the repeller and attractor of $\gamma_j$. Choose $q_j\in\Lambda H_j\setminus\{\alpha_j,\beta_j\}$, possible since its limit set is a Jordan circle. First send $\alpha_j$ to zero and $q_j$ to infinity. A determinant-one lift of the resulting map is $\begin{pmatrix}e^{\lambda_j/2}&0\\b_j&e^{-\lambda_j/2}\end{pmatrix}$ with $b_j\ne0$, since infinity was not its other fixed point. Conjugation by $z\mapsto t_j e^{i\theta_j}z$ can make its lower-left coefficient the positive real number $$c_j=\sqrt{2\cosh d_0-|e^{\lambda_j/2}|^2-|e^{-\lambda_j/2}|^2}.$$ This is positive for all sufficiently large $j$, since $\operatorname{Re}\lambda_j\to0<d_0$. Explicitly use $t_j=|b_j|/c_j$ and choose $e^{i\theta_j}=b_j/|b_j|$. Call the resulting full coordinate map $Q_j$. The displacement formula $2\cosh d(o,Go)=\|G\|_{HS}^2$ gives $d(o,Q_j\gamma_jQ_j^{-1}o)=d_0$. Its matrices converge to $P_{d_0}$, with $c=2\sinh(d_0/2)>0$. Thus the limiting map is exactly $G(z)=z/(1+cz)$, a nonidentity parabolic fixing zero. This construction keeps $\infty\in Q_j\Lambda H_j$ throughout. [F5, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 Fix $0<\eta<\min\{\eta_*/2,\delta(L)/2\}$, where $\eta_*$ is the local thin threshold in [F5]. For any loxodromic $\gamma\in K_j\setminus H_j$ let $T_\gamma=\{p:d(p,\gamma p)<\eta\}$. It is empty or a connected finite-radius axis tube. It misses $\partial C_j$: otherwise the $L$-short nontrivial $h\in H_j$ at that point from step 2.1 would, by [F5], preserve $\gamma$'s fixed pair, contradicting [F6]. A nonempty $T_\gamma$ is then wholly inside or wholly outside $C_j$, because it is connected and misses its boundary. The inside alternative is impossible. Its quotient projection into $\mathbb H^3/H_j$ is injective: if $hT_\gamma$ meets $T_\gamma$, the two short elements $h\gamma h^{-1}$ and $\gamma$ have the same elementary fixed set by [F5], so $h$ belongs to the full cyclic fixed-pair stabilizer; [F6] makes $h=1$. The unquotiented tube has infinite volume, by its positive-radius circular cross-section and unbounded longitudinal coordinate. If it lay in $C_j$, its injective local-isometric image would give infinite volume in a fixed-radius convex neighborhood quotient of $C_j$, contradicting step 1.1. Thus $C_j\cap T_\gamma=\varnothing$. This excludes hull interior points as well as boundary points without assuming a uniform injectivity bound throughout the interior of a quasifuchsian convex core. [F5, F6, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, contradiction]

4.1 For large $j$, the normalized $\gamma_j$ moves every point of $\overline B_R(o)$ by less than $\eta$, by step 2.2 and uniform continuity of the model action on that compact set. Thus this ball lies in its displacement tube, and step 3.1 implies it misses the normalized convex hull. Every finite $\xi\in Q_j\Lambda H_j$ lies on the ideal vertical geodesic joining $\xi$ to infinity, wholly contained in that hull because both endpoints belong to its ideal set. The distance from $o=(0,1)$ to that vertical line is $\operatorname{arsinh}|\xi|$: minimizing $\cosh d(o,(\xi,y))=(|\xi|^2+y^2+1)/(2y)$ gives $y=\sqrt{1+|\xi|^2}$ and $\cosh d=\sqrt{1+|\xi|^2}$. Therefore $|\xi|\ge\sinh R>100$. In particular zero is not in the normalized limit set. It belongs to the opposite domain: zero is the repeller of $\gamma_j\in K_j$, so belongs to $\overline{Q_jE_j}$ by negative iteration of any point there; that component lies in $Q_jD_j^*$ by step 1.1, and a small ordinary neighborhood of zero meeting it lies in the same component. The connected disk $\overline\Delta_{100}$ avoids the limit set and contains zero, so it is contained in $Q_jD_j^*$. This gives actual disk containment in the normalized sphere coordinate. [step 1.1, step 3.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

5.1 We prove the needed quotient injection for the whole fixed disk, including thin points. Suppress the normalization from notation and suppose $1\ne h\in H_j$ has $h\overline\Delta_2\cap\overline\Delta_2\ne\varnothing$. Its pole $h^{-1}\infty$ belongs to $\Lambda H_j$ and is outside $\Delta_{100}$, so write $h(z)=(az+b)/(cz+1)$, with $|c|\le1/100$. Both fixed points of $h$, with repetition for a parabolic, also lie in that limit set. If $c\ne0$ and these finite fixed points are $u,v$, their equation is $cz^2+(1-a)z-b=0$, giving $|(1-a)/b|=|1/u+1/v|\le2/100$. If $c=0$ the same bound follows from the finite affine fixed point, or from $a=1$ for a translation. A nonidentity map cannot have $b=0$, since it would fix the ordinary point zero. Thus $|a-1|\le2|b|/100$. Taking $|z|,|w|\le2$ with $w=h(z)$ gives $$|b|\le2(1+2/100)+2|a|\le4+4/100+2|a-1|,$$ so $|b|<6$, $|a-1|<12/100$, and $|(a-bc)-1|<18/100$. Dividing its matrix by a square root of its determinant therefore gives a determinant-one lift with Hilbert–Schmidt norm less than $10$, and hence $d(o,ho)<10$ by the displacement formula. Step5.1 has $d(o,\gamma_jo)=d_0<\delta(10)$, so [F5] forces $h$ to preserve the fixed pair of $\gamma_j$. That puts its repeller zero in the limit set of $H_j$, since a nonidentity such $h$ is loxodromic with that pair, contradicting step 4.1. Hence no such nonidentity $h$ exists. This proves injectivity on $\overline\Delta_2$; its interior projection is a local holomorphic covering chart and is therefore the required univalent comparison embedding. [F5, step 2.2, step 4.1, construct, algebra, contradiction]

6.1 Finally zero and infinity are outside $Q_jE_j$: zero is a fixed point of $\gamma_j$, and infinity belongs to $Q_j\Lambda H_j\subset Q_j\Lambda\Gamma_j$. The quarter-disk lower bound [F8] gives $\rho_{Q_jE_j}\ge\sigma/2$. Therefore the small displacement assumption implies $d_{\mathrm{sph}}(Q_jx_j,(Q_j\gamma_jQ_j^{-1})Q_jx_j)\le2\varepsilon_j\to0$. Any convergent spherical subsequence of the normalized points must have limit fixed by the uniform Möbius limit $G$ from step 2.2. Its only fixed point is zero, so compactness of the sphere gives $Q_jx_j\to0$. The coefficient $c=2\sinh(d_0/2)<1/10$ has $G(\overline\Delta_{3/2})\subset\Delta_2$, since $|G(z)|\le(3/2)/(1-3c/2)<2$. Uniform convergence on that compact disk, which avoids the limiting pole, gives the same inclusion for all sufficiently large $j$. Thus the exact common comparison disk, quotient injection, nonidentity parabolic limit, centers and local invariance are all proved from the supplied geometric model. No arbitrary annular subgroup, initial realization or Jordan-boundary conclusion has been inserted as an unstated premise. [F8, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.2, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
