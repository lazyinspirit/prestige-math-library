## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Use the actual balanced round-component integrable model and the protected autonomous Hilbert family $J(a)$ of the proper-unit-domain generic regularity theorem, with finitely many fixed node values in its working exact charts. There are at least two attaching-system cut components. Supply finite smooth triangulations of their planar closures as in the integrable planar index theorem.

After shrinking its parameter ball, EVERY actual disk of a proper unit component domain, at any of those nodes and any parameter, has compact node-fixed $H^3\to H^2$ Fredholm index two. Its weighted node-fixed index is also two at every integer order and weight $0<\delta<\pi$. For every parameter at which all these disks are regular, each domain and node has finitely many positive-affine orbits. Such parameters include a dense residual subset of the ball.

For any two such regular parameters, the orbit counts modulo two agree separately for every prescribed node and every proper unit component domain. More precisely there is an actual smooth path between them, staying in this one ball, whose parametrized disk orbit spaces are compact smooth one-manifolds with boundary the two endpoint orbit sets.

This defines and proves local deformation invariance of the actual counts. It does not compute their value for a component with several boundary circles, assert existence of its disks, classify higher domains, or give disk attachment or target degeneration gluing.

## Proof

1.1 First shrink the family to ensure index two without presuming a perturbed index-domain formula. Otherwise Countable Choice selects $a_n\to0$ and disks of non-two index. Pass to one of the finitely many domains and nodes. By [F2], mark-fixing affine reparametrizations make a subsequence converge globally smoothly to a nonconstant standard integrable disk of that same domain and node. In the actual local-addition charts of [F4], these disks and structures give operators on fixed Hilbert spaces converging in $H^3\to H^2$ operator norm: the coefficient formula and the finite compact-source product estimates bound that difference by finitely many coefficient and map derivative differences, which tend to zero. Node and boundary constraints identify by these actual charts. Fredholm index is locally constant here by a direct finite block argument. Split the limiting operator domain into its finite kernel and its orthogonal complement, and its target into its closed range and finite cokernel. Its range block has a bounded inverse by [F4]; the corresponding block of every sufficiently close operator remains invertible by a Neumann series. Eliminate it to a finite map from that fixed kernel to that fixed cokernel. The nearby index is their dimension difference, independent of the finite map. Thus the indices converge to, and eventually equal, the index two supplied by [F1], a contradiction. Source reparametrization conjugates the actual operators by bounded isomorphisms and cannot change index. This gives one neighborhood for the finite nodes and domains. Shrink the Hilbert ball into it using [F3]'s smooth coefficient continuity. The compact/weighted comparisons in [F4] give index two in every asserted weighted realization. [F1, F2, F3, F4, given, construct, contradiction]

2.1 At a parameter where all these disks are regular, [F2] now gives finitely many orbits separately for each domain and node. The parameter set contains the residual set in [F3]. We construct a genuine regular connecting path between any two such parameters $a_0,a_1$. Choose smooth real functions $h,\beta$ on $[0,1]$ with $h(0)=0$, $h(1)=1$, $0\le h\le1$, and $\beta>0$ on $(0,1)$ with $\beta(0)=\beta(1)=0$. Put $$a_b(t)=a_0+h(t)(a_1-a_0)+\beta(t)b.$$ The segment between $a_0,a_1$ has positive distance from the complement of the open Hilbert ball, by compactness; hence all sufficiently small $b$ keep the entire path inside that ball. For $0<t<1$, varying $b$ spans every original parameter tangent because $\beta(t)>0$. Therefore [F3] makes the actual universal derivative in the disk and $b$ variables onto at every proper unit-domain zero. At the endpoints it is onto because the fixed endpoint disk operators are onto, by their chosen regularity. [F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Apply the actual zero-chart construction in [F4] to this universal path equation in variables $(u,t,b)$, on its open universal regular locus; the integer domain is locally constant there, by the actual local-addition homotopy and divisor degrees. The projection from the interior zero locus to $b$ is Fredholm of index three. Indeed its fixed $(u,t)$ derivative is $P\xi+c\tau$, where $P$ has index two and $c$ is the one smooth path-derivative forcing. Appending the one-dimensional domain variable raises index by one, as follows by the same finite range/kernel block elimination in step1.1. Universal surjectivity makes the projection kernel and cokernel those of this fixed derivative, by its tangent equation, exactly as in [F3]. A bounded right inverse of the universal derivative is obtained by finitely many lifts of its finite range quotient and the kernel-complement inverse of $P$, as in [F3]. The actual split zero theorem therefore applies. The map charts are second-countable by [F4]. Apply the CC Sard--Smale theorem to this smooth interior projection, separately for the finite nodes and domains, and intersect their residual sets. Choose a regular $b$ in the small open ball. At $t=0,1$, surjectivity already holds independently of $b$, so no boundary version of Sard--Smale is needed. The fixed-$b$ parametrized zero spaces are consequently smooth three-manifolds with boundary precisely the endpoint zero spaces. Their finite-dimensional tangent spaces are the kernels of $(P,c)$, including at the endpoints; at an endpoint $P$ onto makes the $t$ coordinate a submersion, giving the claimed boundary charts. [F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 The positive affine action on each such parametrized zero space is free and proper. Freeness and the sequential properness proof are exactly those in [F4], with $t_n$ retained as an additional compact interval coordinate: convergence of the maps in $H^3$ is uniform, and divergent affine parameters force a constant pointwise limit, contradicting the nonconstant unit domain. The action is smooth on this finite-dimensional solution space. To justify this without assuming a smooth action on all rough $H^3$ maps, repeat [F4]'s higher-order zero-chart argument for $(P,c)$: its kernel consists of smooth sections with one smooth forcing $c\tau$, the higher range quotients identify by elliptic regularity, and onto-ness at $H^3\to H^2$ implies onto-ness at every higher order. Its higher zero charts therefore include into the $H^3$ zero chart with the identity on their common finite kernel; the finite-dimensional inverse theorem makes them local diffeomorphisms. Source composition can then be differentiated any fixed number of times after passing to a sufficiently high Sobolev order. The actual two orbit directions are independent by [F4]. A complement to them and the finite inverse theorem give smooth local quotient slices of dimension one, also at the boundary. Properness excludes additional distant returns and makes the quotient Hausdorff. It is second-countable because the action quotient map is open and the original space is second-countable. Its boundary is exactly the endpoint orbit sets, since the action fixes $t$. [F2, F4, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 These one-dimensional quotients are compact. For any sequence of their points, choose representatives and pass to $t_n\to t$ by compactness of the interval. The structures then converge smoothly, so [F2] gives mark-fixing affine reparametrizations and global smooth convergence to a disk of the same node and domain for that limiting parameter. The corresponding quotient points converge in a local quotient chart. Thus the quotient is sequentially compact. It is second-countable, and hence compact under Countable Choice: a countable basis gives a countable subcover of any open cover; if its first $n$ members never cover, choose a point outside their union for every $n$. A convergent subsequence contradicts the eventual inclusion in one covering member of its limit. Such a countable subcover is obtained by choosing, for each basic open set lying in a cover member, one such member. This uses only Countable Choice. In particular there are no additional boundary strata, since the convergence in [F2] is global smooth modulo precisely the permitted affine maps. [F2, F4, step 4.1, construct, contradiction]

6.1 Finally a compact smooth one-manifold has an even number of boundary points by the following finite argument. Choose finitely many relatively open interval or half-interval cores covering it, whose closures lie in manifold charts. Their frontiers, together with the actual boundary points in those charts, form a finite set $F$; the boundary itself is finite because it is discrete and covered by finitely many boundary charts. Every component of the complement of $F$ lies in one chosen core: membership in that core is locally constant on the complement of its frontier, and some core contains each point. Cutting each of the finitely many interval cores at $F$ shows that there are only finitely many such components; each is an open interval, and its closure joins two points of $F$, possibly the same point. A circle component with $F$ empty can be given one extra chart point and cut there, without changing the boundary. The resulting finite graph has valence two at each interior vertex and valence one at every actual boundary vertex. Its degree sum equals twice its edge count, so the number of boundary vertices is even. Apply this to each compact quotient in step5.1. Its boundary consists of exactly the finite endpoint orbit sets, by step4.1 and step2.1. Their cardinalities therefore agree modulo two. The same chosen path is regular simultaneously for every prescribed node and unit component domain by step3.1. No value for this common parity or existence of a disk has been inferred. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
