## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Use the balanced round-component integrable model with $m$ disjoint attaching circles, product torus $L$, at least two cut components, and a fixed node tuple $x\in L$. Let $A$ be a proper planar cut component with a supplied finite smooth triangulation. Suppose NO attaching circle has both of its boundary copies on $A$. Thus its $e\ge1$ boundary circles have distinct labels, and every other labelled circle is disjoint from $\overline A$.

For the standard integrable structure, the actual smooth disks of unit component domain $A$, with boundary on $L$ and the prescribed boundary node $x$, form exactly ONE orbit under the positive affine automorphisms fixing that node. They exist, and their actual compact node-fixed $H^3\to H^2$ linearizations are onto of real index two, with kernel precisely the two affine directions. The same regularity and index hold in every higher and actual weighted realization with $0<\delta<\pi$.

For the actual protected nearly symmetric Hilbert family based at this integrable model, every parameter in a sufficiently small ball likewise has exactly one such disk orbit, regular of index two. Its mod-two count is therefore one. A common ball works for finitely many nodes and components satisfying the stated no-internal-pair condition.

This treats any number of external boundary circles. It does not remove an internal paired attaching circle, compute the count for a component with such a pair, or supply target-neck or boundary-attachment gluing.

## Proof

1.1 Choose a Möbius sphere coordinate putting one deleted face on the unbounded side. The actual $A$ becomes a bounded round disk with disjoint round closed holes removed, so [F1] applies at the prescribed node points $x_j$ on its $e$ boundary circles. Let $H>0$ be its normalized positive zero-period harmonic function. The closed differential $\alpha=H_y\,dx-H_x\,dy$ has zero integral on every closed path. Here is the finite planar justification. Approximate any piecewise smooth closed path by a polygonal one through a subdivision in sufficiently small convex disks inside $A$; local primitives, obtained by integrating the closed differential along radial segments in those disks, show that the integrals agree. Perturb the finitely many polygon vertices slightly so its crossings are transverse and finite. Splitting at crossings decomposes its oriented edge cycle into finitely many simple polygon cycles, with immediate backtracks contributing zero. By [F1] each simple polygon bounds its bounded planar region. Because its edges avoid the disjoint round holes, each hole lies entirely inside or outside it. Its bounded region also lies inside the convex outer disk. Apply the plane Green identity to that polygonal region with the enclosed holes removed; it expresses the closed-form integral as the sum of the enclosed boundary periods, all zero. Thus all closed-path integrals vanish. Define $G$ by the integral of $\alpha$ from a fixed point to a variable point; path independence just proved makes it single valued, and local differentiation gives $G_x=H_y$, $G_y=-H_x$. Consequently $$p=G+iH:A\longrightarrow\mathbb H$$ is holomorphic. Its additive real constant is arbitrary. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Near $x_j$ the kernel expansion in [F1] and the local Cauchy--Riemann equations give $$p=-c_j/w_j+q_j,$$ where $c_j>0$ and $q_j$ is holomorphic and real on that boundary arc. Indeed the imaginary part of the difference is a smooth harmonic zero-edge remainder, which has a local conjugate; two holomorphic functions with the same imaginary part differ by a real constant. Away from the $x_j$, odd reflection of $H$ from [F1] makes $p$ holomorphic across the circle and real there. Hopf's strict normal derivative from that proof makes its tangential derivative nonzero. At $x_j$ the displayed simple pole gives a smooth nonzero derivative into the real projective target circle, using $1/p$. Thus each boundary restriction is a local diffeomorphism to $\mathbb R\cup\{\infty\}$, with its only preimage of infinity at $x_j$, and hence has degree one and is a diffeomorphism. The map $p$ is proper over $\mathbb H$: a sequence whose image stays in a compact subset cannot approach its boundary, where values tend to the real circle or infinity. Its nonempty image is open and closed in the connected half-plane, and is therefore all of it. Each sufficiently large imaginary target value has one preimage near each simple pole and none on the remaining compact source, so its degree is $e$. The usual local power model in [F3] then gives exactly $e$ roots with multiplicities over every interior target point. [F1, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Construct a symmetric-product disk by taking, for each $z\in\mathbb H$, the unordered fiber $p^{-1}(z)$ in $A$ and adjoining the fixed node coordinates on all inactive labels. The latter circles are a positive distance from $\overline A$ by the hypothesis. This fiber map is holomorphic including branch values. In fact near a point of local degree $b$ the source coordinate in [F3] makes $p=z_0+w^b$. In that target surface coordinate its local unordered roots have elementary symmetric coefficients those of $w^b-(z-z_0)$, which are holomorphic. Distinct inverse points have disjoint coordinate neighborhoods; their finitely many clusters combine into the actual symmetric-product chart. At a boundary value every root is regular by step2.1, so the roots extend smoothly across the boundary circle by their simple inverses. At infinity the same holds in the coordinate $1/p$, and all those roots tend to their respective prescribed $x_j$. The resulting closed disk has boundary in $L$ and node $x$. Each point $y\in A$ appears in exactly the fiber over $p(y)$, with actual divisor multiplicity one: in the local power model its fiber polynomial evaluated at the coordinate of $y$ is a unit times $p(y)-z$. Points outside $\overline A$ are never active roots. This proves that its actual domain is precisely the unit domain $A$. Conversely [F2] identifies every standard disk of that domain with such a proper map $p:A\to\mathbb H$, with those simple boundary poles. Its positive imaginary part and single-valued conjugate have zero periods, so [F1] makes it a positive scalar multiple of $H$. Thus every such $p$ differs from the constructed one by a positive affine target map. Inverse fibers change by precomposition by its inverse source automorphism. This proves existence and the single affine orbit of actual disks. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Compute the actual kernel, rather than infer regularity from orbit uniqueness. Its sections are smooth and holomorphic in integrable target charts by [F3]. Inactive roots lie in disjoint constant coordinate disks. Each inactive component of a kernel section is a holomorphic scalar with a fixed real boundary line, hence constant: its imaginary part is zero on the circle and the weak maximum principle makes it zero inside, and Cauchy--Riemann then makes its real part constant. Its fixed-node value is zero, so it vanishes. For active roots, away from branch points a section gives a root displacement $\xi(w)$; define the infinitesimal base-map variation $$\dot p(w)=-dp(w)\xi(w).$$ It extends holomorphically through every branch. To check this exactly, in the local model $p(w)=z_0+w^b$ a holomorphic symmetric-product tangent section is the coefficient variation $\dot F(w,z)=\sum_{j=0}^{b-1}a_j(z)w^j$ of $F=w^b-(z-z_0)$. Differentiating $F=0$ gives $bw^{b-1}\xi=-\dot F$, hence $\dot p(w)=\dot F(w,p(w))$, a holomorphic function. These expressions agree with the definition off the branch and therefore on overlaps. They also show injectivity: if $\dot p=0$, the displacements and thus the coefficient section vanish off finitely many branch values and hence everywhere. [F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 On the boundary away from the poles, $dp$ sends the tangent line of its actual circle to the real line, so $\dot p$ has real boundary values. At $x_j$ use the source node coordinate $\zeta=-1/z$. The active inverse root has $w_j$ proportional to $\zeta$ to first order by step2.1. A smooth holomorphic node-fixed section is $O(\zeta)=O(w_j)$, while $dp=O(w_j^{-2})$. Therefore $\dot p$ has at most a simple pole there. Reflection across its real analytic boundary makes its leading coefficient real. Its imaginary part has exactly the real-residue local type in [F1] and has zero periods because $\dot p$ itself is single valued. By [F1], $\operatorname{Im}\dot p=\lambda H$ for a real $\lambda$, and the real-valued holomorphic difference is constant. Thus $$\dot p=\lambda p+\tau,\qquad \lambda,\tau\in\mathbb R.$$ The kernel injection of step4.1 consequently bounds its real dimension by two. The two actual affine sections supplied by [F3] are independent and belong to it, so they are its full kernel. The actual node-fixed Fredholm index is two by [F2], with the supplied finite smooth triangulation. Hence its cokernel is zero. [F3] transfers onto-ness, index and the same kernel to all claimed higher and weighted realizations. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 We prove persistence for every small protected parameter. Fix one standard representative $u_0$ and two independent affine kernel sections $\xi_1,\xi_2$. In its actual local-addition chart from [F3] define two compact-source phases by pairing its chart section with $\rho\xi_j$, where $\rho$ is a nonnegative smooth interior cutoff chosen so the resulting two-by-two Gram matrix is positive definite. Such a cutoff exists because the two affine sections are independent on every nonempty open set: on the nonempty immersed root patches their factors are $1$ and $z$, real-linearly independent when $\operatorname{Im}z>0$. On nearby maps the phase under affine precomposition is smooth jointly in the group parameter and the actual $H^3$ map variable. Change integration variables to put all source diffeomorphism derivatives on the smooth compact weight and reference $u_0$ in the local-addition inverse; the remaining dependence on the map is an actual smooth compact-source composition as in [F3]. This avoids differentiating composition on the full rough mapping space. The phase derivative on the affine kernel is invertible. Augmenting the actual Cauchy--Riemann section with these two phases therefore gives a bounded invertible map derivative at $u_0$, by step5.1. [F4] yields a unique nearby phase-zero solution for every sufficiently small parameter, smooth in $H^3$. Neumann inverses preserve regularity, and the finite Fredholm block argument preserves index two. The finite phase implicit equation places every nearby solution in that one affine orbit. Local-addition homotopies fix the node and boundary and retain its integer domain. [F3, F4, step 3.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 There are no additional orbits for parameters tending to zero. Otherwise Countable Choice selects such a sequence and one representative of a different orbit at each parameter. By [F4] its subsequence, after exact node-fixing affine maps, converges globally smoothly to a standard disk of domain $A$ and node $x$. Step3.1 identifies that orbit with $u_0$; one additional fixed affine map makes the convergence to that chosen representative. All sufficiently late maps then lie in the local neighborhood of step6.1 and belong to its unique orbit, a contradiction. Hence one smaller parameter ball contains exactly one regular orbit at every parameter, giving actual mod-two count one. For a finite list intersect the finitely many balls. This proves the many-external-boundary case with no internal paired circle; the harmonic and root constructions above have neither removed such a pair nor established any target-neck gluing. [F3, F4, step 3.1, step 6.1, construct, contradiction] ∎
