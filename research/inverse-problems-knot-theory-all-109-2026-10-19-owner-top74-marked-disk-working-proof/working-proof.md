### 5.1 Relative two-complex extension across the upper three-handles

### 5.3 Explicit height reparametrization — bounded end control

Here is the actual scalar estimate used to compress all the preceding height bounds; its proof is independent of a citation to a controlled product theorem.     a(gamma_n)<1/n,
    gamma_n<b(gamma_(n-1)),
    c(gamma_n)<gamma_(n-1).

These choices are possible at every finite step. Choose a starting h(gamma_1) larger than d^(-1)(1), then define for n≥1

    h(gamma_(n+1)) = max{ d^(-1)(1/n),

The sequence is strictly decreasing. ### 5.4 Actual finite cyclic Wall-matrix reduction

The following algebraic reduction is proved here; it is NOT being imported as an unexplained assertion that Wh(Z)=0. Put S=Z[t] and Lambda=Z[t,t^(-1)]. Every invertible matrix over Lambda becomes the identity after stabilization, elementary row/column operations and multiplication of basis vectors by units ±t^j. All operations in this argument are finite. A controlled geometric realization must still keep their handle slides in the chosen units; no geometric support assertion is inferred solely from the algebra.

The finite integer freeness fact used below is also explicit: for a finitely generated torsion-free abelian group A, form rational fractions (a,n), with (a,n) equivalent to (b,m) exactly when ma=nb. This embeds A into its rationalization. A finite generating list spans a finite-dimensional rational vector space; successive finite independence tests choose a basis. Clearing the finitely many denominators of the original generators embeds A in a scaled Z^r. The coordinate-projection induction then proves freeness. This uses neither an infinite basis nor a PID theorem with unstated choice hypotheses.

First prove the polynomial assertion. If A∈GL_n(S), its constant A₀ is an integral unimodular matrix. Multiply by A₀^(-1), leaving constant term I. Suppose the resulting polynomial matrix has degree d≥2, and write A=A_<d+A_d t^d. The matrix

    D = [ A_<d       A_d t   ]
        [ -t^(d-1)I    I     ]

is elementary equivalent to diag(A,I): subtract A_d t times the lower block row, then add t^(d-1) times the second block column to the first. The resulting off-diagonal blocks vanish. Conversely D is invertible, has constant term I, and has degree at most d−1. Repeating reduces degree to one, after finite stabilizations, giving I−tN with N an integral matrix. Its determinant is the polynomial unit1; hence det(lambda I−N)=lambda^m. The elementary determinant-adjugate proof of Cayley–Hamilton gives N^m=0.

Every kernel ker N^j is a saturated subgroup of Z^m, since a nonzero integer multiple of N^j x can vanish only when N^j x vanishes. Subgroups of finite free abelian groups are free by the projection-to-one-coordinate induction: take the least positive element of the coordinate image, choose one preimage, and split off that generator; the remaining subgroup lies in one fewer coordinates. A saturated inclusion has a torsion-free finitely generated quotient, which is free by the same integer argument. Thus choose bases successively extending along

    0 ⊂ ker N ⊂ ker N² ⊂ ... ⊂ Z^m.

In this integral basis N strictly lowers the filtration and is strictly block upper triangular. Therefore I−tN is upper unitriangular and is reduced to I by finitely many elementary operations. Integral change-of-basis matrices themselves reduce by the Euclidean algorithm to diagonal ±1 and permutations, also generated by elementary operations and signs. This proves that every polynomial-invertible matrix is stably elementary modulo constant integral units. No polynomial projective-module theorem has been assumed.

Now let A∈GL_n(Lambda). Multiplying by t^l I for sufficiently large l gives B∈Mat_n(S); the permitted diagonal units record this change. Since B is Laurent-invertible, det B=±t^k for k≥0: the only units in Lambda are ±t^j, as comparison of lowest and highest exponents of a product shows. The map B:S^n→S^n is injective, by multiplying with its adjugate in the integral domain S. Let P=coker B. The adjugate identity gives t^k P=0. Thus P is finitely generated as an abelian group by the finitely many classes t^j e_i with 0≤j<k.

P is torsion-free over Z. Indeed suppose a v=Bw for a nonzero integer a. Then B^(-1)v=w/a. The left side, computed as ±t^(-k)adj(B)v, is an integral Laurent vector; the right side is a rational POLYNOMIAL vector. Its negative coefficients vanish, and all remaining coefficients must be integers. Hence B^(-1)v∈S^n and v∈im B. This proves saturation. By the preceding finite integer argument P≅Z^r. Multiplication by t on P is a nilpotent integral matrix N, since t^k P=0.

There is a second injective free presentation of this SAME S-module:

    0 → S^r --C=tI−N→ S^r → P → 0.

The last map evaluates a vector polynomial at N. It is onto because the chosen integral basis generates P; polynomial division by the monic matrix tI−N gives its kernel im C explicitly. Injectivity follows from its leading coefficient I. The same saturated-kernel basis argument triangularizes N. Over Lambda, C then becomes upper triangular with diagonal t, and elementary operations remove its off-diagonal entries. Thus C has the required unit reduction.

For completeness compare the two presentations explicitly, rather than invoking an unproved classification of presentations. Choose lifts u:S^n→S^r and v:S^r→S^n of the identity on P. Since both presentations are injective, there are unique polynomial matrices a,b,h,j satisfying

    uB=Ca,   vC=Bb,   vu−I=Bh,   uv−I=Cj.

The fiber product Q={(x,y): [x]=[y] in P} has two free parametrizations:

    (x,z) ↦ (x, ux+Cz),
    (y,w) ↦ (vy+Bw, y).

Their change matrix, and its inverse, are

    F = [ u   C ] ,     F^(-1) = [ v   B ].
        [-h  -b ]                  [-j  -a ]

These inverse identities follow directly by recovering the same pair (x,y) and using the injectivity of B and C; hence F is polynomial-invertible. The polynomial assertion already proved reduces F to elementary operations and constant integral units.

In the first parametrization the inclusion of the kernel of Q→P, with coordinates (r,s)↦(Br,Cs), is

    D_B = [ B   0 ] ;
          [-a   I ]

in the second it is

    D_C = [ 0   C ] .
          [ I  -b ]

Thus F D_B=D_C. Elementary block operations reduce D_B to diag(B,I) and D_C, after a permitted block permutation, to diag(C,I). Over Lambda all these matrices are invertible. Since F has the polynomial reduction and C the unit reduction, the identity gives the asserted finite reduction of B and hence A. The rank-zero case P=0 reduces directly to the polynomial assertion.

This proves the actual finite cyclic stable intersection-matrix reduction needed in a fixed unit, including the integer-lattice issue; no Laurent PID assumption is made. It does NOT yet prove the controlled global assembly assertion that all elementary reductions can be arranged over an arbitrary control-space cover, nor the vanishing of every higher/negative controlled obstruction term. ### 5.5 Reduced K₀ and negative local cyclic coefficients, with finite-resolution proof

Here full AC remains explicit. The following supplies the other LOCAL algebraic coefficients, rather than assuming that the K₁ calculation alone kills a controlled obstruction over a two-dimensional base.

A subgroup of an arbitrary free abelian group is free: well-order its basis, intersect the subgroup with successive initial spans, and at a successor take the least positive generator in the image of the new coordinate when this image is nonzero. Its chosen preimage splits that successive quotient. Transfinite unions give a basis; the choices here are allowed by full AC. Consequently every abelian group has a free resolution of length at most one.

For a ring R of global projective dimension at most d, R[u] has global projective dimension at most d+1. For an R[u]-module M use the exact sequence

    0 → R[u]⊗_R M --(u⊗1−1⊗u_M)→ R[u]⊗_R M → M → 0.

Injectivity follows by inspecting the highest nonzero polynomial coefficient; the quotient map evaluates u by its given action on M. An R-projective resolution of M of length d induces one for each of the first two modules; lifting the displayed map between those resolutions and taking its cone gives a projective resolution of M of length at most d+1. Localization preserves these resolutions because localization is exact and carries projectives to projectives. Thus Z[u_1,...,u_s,u_1^(-1),...,u_r^(-1)] is regular of finite global projective dimension, at most s+1. The argument does not assert that all its projectives are free.

These rings are Noetherian. For one polynomial variable this follows directly from the leading-coefficient ideals: their ascending chain stabilizes, select finitely many polynomials realizing generators of the finitely many relevant ideals, and subtract their shifted leading terms to lower degree. Induct on the number of variables; localization preserves finite generation of ideals. Over a Noetherian ring of global dimension d, a finitely generated module has a FINITE resolution by finitely generated projectives: start with finite free covers through degree d; their kernels remain finitely generated, and dimension shifting makes the last kernel projective. The comparison/lifting proof for projective resolutions gives that the alternating projective class is independent of the chosen resolution. Indeed the cone of a comparison equivalence is a finite exact projective complex; split it successively from its last nonzero term to see its alternating class is zero. Hence K₀, defined by finitely generated projectives and direct-sum relations, equals G₀, defined by finitely generated modules and exact-sequence relations, for each of these rings.

Now compute G₀(Lambda), Lambda=Z[t,t^(-1)], directly. Every finitely generated module has a finite prime filtration. To justify this, choose a maximal annihilator among nonzero elements in a nonzero Noetherian module; it is prime (if ab kills x but b does not, the annihilator of bx would be strictly larger unless a already kills x). This gives a cyclic prime submodule. Repeat in the quotient. The growing submodules cannot form an infinite strictly increasing chain, so the process terminates.

The prime ideals of Lambda have these forms:

* (0);
* a principal height-one prime (f), including a rational prime (p);
* (p,F), where p is a rational prime and the reduction of F is irreducible in F_p[t,t^(-1)].

Here is the classification needed, not an unsupported dimension claim. A prime contracting to0 in Z localizes to either0 or a principal prime in Q[t,t^(-1)]; choose a primitive integral Laurent generator f. The content/Gauss calculation shows contraction of that principal ideal is (f). A prime contracting to(p) is the inverse image of a prime in the PID F_p[t,t^(-1)], giving exactly the other two possibilities. The Gauss content identity follows by reducing primitive factors modulo a prime which would divide all product coefficients; their nonzero reductions have nonzero product in F_p[t,t^(-1)].

For a nonzero principal prime the exact sequence

    0 → Lambda --f→ Lambda → Lambda/(f) → 0

gives class zero in G₀. For (p,F), the regular sequence p,F has the explicit exact Koszul resolution

    0 → Lambda --(-F,p)→ Lambda² --(p,F)→ Lambda → Lambda/(p,F) → 0.

To check its middle exactness, if pa+Fb=0, reduce modulo p. Since F is nonzero there, b is divisible by p; substitution gives the indicated generator. Thus its class is also zero. The prime filtration therefore expresses every module class as an integer multiple of [Lambda]; rank over Q(t) shows this multiple is unique. Consequently K₀(Lambda)=Z by rank and reduced K₀(Lambda)=0. In particular every finitely generated projective is stably free: equality [P]=r[Lambda] gives P⊕Q≅Lambda^r⊕Q; add a projective complement of Q in a finite free module to obtain an actual stable free isomorphism.

Finally the NEGATIVE coefficient vanishing does not need a claim about multivariable projectives being free. For any of the regular Noetherian rings R above, the map

    G₀(R[u]) → G₀(R[u,u^(-1)])

is surjective. Every finitely presented Laurent module is the localization of a finitely presented polynomial module: choose its finite generators and relation matrix and multiply each relation by a sufficiently large unit power of u to clear negative exponents. Apply K₀=G₀ for the two regular rings. Thus K₀(R[u])→K₀(R[u,u^(-1)]) is surjective. In the defining Bass cokernel

    K_(-1)(R)=coker( K₀(R[u])⊕K₀(R[u^(-1)]) → K₀(R[u,u^(-1)]) )

this proves K_(-1)(R)=0. Iterate the same definition: all polynomial/Laurent rings encountered remain regular Noetherian of finite global dimension, so all their K_(-1) groups vanish, then all K_(-2), and inductively every negative group vanishes. This proves the local negative cyclic coefficients actually needed, without an unexplained appeal to a general negative-K vanishing theorem.

### 5.8 Cyclic-times-torus algebra required by the actual torus trick

The original controlled geometric-module proof uses Wh(pi×Z^j), not just Wh(pi). Here pi=Z, so the needed rings are multivariable integral Laurent rings. The following extends the preceding finite algebra without assuming that arbitrary projectives over such rings are free.

For a regular Noetherian ring R of finite global dimension, with the commutative integral domains used here, polynomial homotopy invariance of K₀ has this constructive proof. Give a finitely generated R[u]-module M the degree filtration from a finite generating set. Its associated graded module has a finite graded-projective resolution: choose graded finite free covers, and stop after the finite global-dimension bound. The last kernel is projective; an ungraded lift to a graded surjection may be replaced by its degree-zero component, so it is graded-projective as well. Every finite graded-projective module P over R[u] is induced from a finite graded R-projective Q=P/uP. Indeed choose an R-linear homogeneous section of P→Q, and map R[u]⊗Q to P. The map is onto by descending leading degree. Since P is projective, its kernel remains exact after reduction modulo u; that kernel has quotient modulo u equal to zero. It is bounded below and finitely generated, so descending leading degree makes it zero. Thus the map is an isomorphism.

Lift this graded resolution to a filtered resolution of M. For an induced graded-projective generator Q in a particular degree, the map Q→gr M lifts to that filtration step of M by R-projectivity. Its induced filtered map is strictly onto, by successive cancellation of leading terms. The kernel therefore has associated graded equal to the graded kernel. Repeat; at the last step an associated-graded isomorphism lifts to an isomorphism because the filtration is exhaustive and bounded below, and every leading-degree descent is finite. This gives a finite resolution of M by modules induced from R-projectives. Its Euler class shows K₀(R)→K₀(R[u]) is onto; evaluation at u=0 is its left inverse, hence it is an isomorphism.

The map G₀(R[u])→G₀(R[u,u^(-1)]) is also an isomorphism. Surjectivity was proved in5.5. Its kernel is generated by u-power-torsion modules: compatible finitely generated polynomial lattices lift an exact sequence of Laurent modules to an exact sequence of polynomial modules, and any two lattices have a common sum whose quotient differences are u-power torsion. Kernels of localization may first be discarded; they too are u-power torsion, since all modules are finitely generated. A u-power-torsion module has a finite filtration whose quotients N have u acting by zero. Each such N has zero class in G₀(R[u]) by the exact sequence

    0 → R[u]⊗_R N --u→ R[u]⊗_R N → N → 0.

The first map is injective by polynomial coefficients, with no R-flatness requirement on N. This proves injectivity. Using K₀=G₀, we obtain K₀(R[u,u^(-1)])=K₀(R). Starting with Z gives rank K₀=Z for every integral polynomial/Laurent ring in finitely many variables. In particular their finitely generated projectives are stably free, with the actual complement argument in5.5.

Next prove the nilpotent K₁ operation over any of these rings R. Consider finite R-modules with a nilpotent endomorphism N, and the subcategory whose underlying R-module is projective. Every object has a finite resolution by objects of that subcategory: if N^m=0, cover it by a finite free module over B=R[z]/(z^m). This is free as an R-module. The kernel is again finite over R and has the same nilpotence bound. Repeat; projective dimension over R decreases until the last kernel is R-projective. Euler classes of these resolutions are independent of the chosen covers. For two B-free covers, their fiber product splits over each cover by B-projectivity and gives the Schanuel relation; iterating gives independence. A short exact sequence is handled by the same horseshoe construction. If a module was already R-projective, each resulting kernel is R-projective and the defining exact-sequence relation supplies the same Euler class. Thus this resolution identifies the Grothendieck group of the projective-underlying category with that of all finite nilpotent modules.

On a projective-underlying object define tau(P,N) to be the stable K₁ class of I−uN on R[u]⊗P, taking a finite projective complement with zero endomorphism to represent it on a free module. Complement choices agree after stabilization. Exact sequences split as R-modules; their endomorphism matrices are block triangular, so elementary operations prove additivity of tau. But in the category of all finite nilpotent modules the filtration by kernels of powers of N has quotients with zero endomorphism. Their Euler resolutions can be taken with zero endomorphisms as well, so tau is zero on them. Consequently tau(P,N)=0 for every nilpotent N. Higman linearization from5.4 now proves K₁(R[u])=K₁(R): a polynomial invertible matrix of constant term I linearizes to I−uN, and nilpotence follows by Cayley–Hamilton in the integral domain R.

For Laurent matrices the cokernel argument extends with one important replacement: the polynomial cokernel P is R-PROJECTIVE, not merely torsion-free. If B∈Mat_n(R[u]) has Laurent inverse, its determinant is a unit of R times u^k. For EVERY R-module L, B:L[u]^n→L[u]^n is injective: multiply by the adjugate and use that multiplication by u^k is injective on polynomial vectors over any module. The flat R-presentation by the two polynomial free modules therefore shows Tor₁^R(P,L)=0 for every L. The module P is finite over R because u^kP=0, and R is Noetherian, so P is finitely presented and flat, hence projective. Here is the finite splitting argument for that last assertion. For a finite presentation 0→J→R^q→P→0, flatness makes J→R^q pure. If j_1,...,j_s generate J, the simultaneous finite linear equations asking for a map R^q→J which fixes every j_l have a solution in R^q (the identity). Purity implies they have a solution in J: tensor the equation's cokernel with J and R^q to see this implication. The resulting map is a retraction onto J, so P is projective.

Since K₀(R)=Z, add a finite free R summand to P to make it free. On its presentation this amounts to adding a block uI, an allowed unit block over the Laurent ring. Multiplication by u on the new free cokernel is nilpotent N. The explicit fiber-product presentation comparison in5.4 works unchanged over R. Its comparison matrix is polynomial-invertible, hence has K₁ class from R by the preceding nilpotent argument. The standard presentation uI−N is u times I−u^(-1)N; the latter has trivial class by the same polynomial result in u^(-1). Thus K₁(R[u,u^(-1)]) is generated by K₁(R) and units u. Induction from Z shows that every stable invertible matrix over Z[t_1^(±1),...,t_m^(±1)] reduces by elementary operations and diagonal monomial units. This proves Wh(Z^m)=0 for every finite m, exactly the cyclic-times-torus condition, not just the one-variable special case.

This is a finite algebraic proof with explicit regular-resolution and nilpotent operations. It supplies the ring-theoretic premise of the torus construction; it does not claim that its geometric truncation, flux cancellation, handle realization or relative product has been completed.

### 6.2 The finite controlled homology-to-homotopy operation

Here is the finite operation needed between handle exchanges; it is not an appeal to uncontrolled acyclicity. Work over a compact buffered subset of N with a metric. A sequence of pairs (A_i,B_i) maps to N and its successive maps commute with these projections. Assume both A_i→A_(i+1) and B_i→B_(i+1) have the following small relative-two-complex lift property: a finite relative two-complex diagram over N has a lift agreeing with its supplied part, with base error below delta. Assume also that every relative singular j-cycle, j≤k, supported over a closed set C becomes a boundary over the delta-neighborhood of C in the next pair. All complexes and chains used in a particular application are finite. For each eps>0 and k there is a delta>0 such that a composition of at most T(k) such maps pushes every finite relative k-complex into the final B, fixed on its prescribed part, through base tracks of diameter less than eps. One may use T(0)=2, T(1)=4, T(2)=11 and T(k)=2T(k−1)+2 for k≥3; in particular T(k)≤2^(k+2)−2.

For k=0 lift its finitely many vertices to B at the next stage over their prescribed base points. Two sufficiently close finite base vertex maps are joined by small paths in coordinate balls of N. The relative-one-complex diagram consisting of these paths lifts at the next stage and provides the required small homotopy. For k=1 first do vertices and then repeat for the finitely many edges. The relative-two-complex property lifts the edge homotopies, with all endpoint tracks already fixed. Tolerances are chosen so that the sum of these two errors and the coordinate-ball homotopies is below eps.

For k≥2 first apply the induction to the (k−1)-skeleton with bound gamma. Subdivide the remaining k-cells until their projected images have diameter below gamma; the earlier skeleton homotopy makes each remaining cell map have diameter below3gamma. Fix one such cell (D^k,S^(k−1)). The homological-zero hypothesis supplies a finite singular (k+1)-chain bounding its relative fundamental cycle in the next pair, with its support over the delta-enlargement of that same small image set. Glue the finitely many parameter simplices of this chain along their cancelling faces, retaining the given disk and its boundary faces; the resulting finite pair (L,Q) maps to the next (A,B), and the disk's relative class is zero in H_k(L,Q). This is merely a finite realization of the supplied singular boundary equation; no assertion that the glued object is a manifold is needed.

Map the cone on the finite one-skeleton of L to N, extending its given base map. A sufficiently small neighborhood of the old cell image lies in a coordinate ball; contract that ball to construct the cone map, with an arbitrarily prescribed small enlargement rho of its diameter. Attach that cone to L. The relative-two-complex lifting property extends the existing map of L across the cone at the next stage, because the cone has dimension at most two. ### 6.3 Removing low handles without losing their control

The construction here is in a genuinely smooth n-manifold, n≥6. Smooth relative handle decompositions can be made arbitrarily small over N: take finitely many smooth coordinate boxes over a compact base buffer, subdivide them until their images have the desired diameter, and take handles dual to their finite compatible smooth cellulation, with a retained collar on the prescribed boundary. Small perturbations make attaching maps transverse to earlier handles. The construction is locally finite on an exhaustion. No triangulation or smoothing of the TOP control manifold N is assumed in this step.

For zero-handles, choose small paths from them to the incoming collar using the supplied relative-zero-complex contractions. Push those paths into the zero/one-handle skeleton. Order the finitely many zero-handles. If one old zero-handle is used m times, replace it by m consecutive zero-handles and m−1 connecting one-handles, namely its product with a subdivided interval; make the analogous replacement of a one-handle traversed several times by parallel one-handles joined by cancelling two-handles. The inherited ordering of visits makes the chosen paths disjoint after these subdivisions. Move other attaching maps slightly off these paths. Each chosen chain is now a ball meeting the collar in one boundary ball; absorb it into the collar. With sufficiently small original handles, its projected diameter is bounded by the original path diameter plus a fixed multiple of the handle mesh, independent of how many other chains there are.

Suppose handles below k have been removed and the actual pair is relatively k-connected through the small diagram homotopies. For each k-handle take its core disk with boundary on the incoming level. The given contraction of that finite relative disk, pushed into the handle skeleton, supplies a small relative (k+1)-disk in the union of the next level and incoming collar. When 2k+1<n, general position makes it miss the k-handle cores. In the remaining range k≤n−3, pass instead to the corresponding relative homology class in the small local simply connected lifting region. Excision removes the k-handle interiors; the attached boundary pieces have no cells below k, so the same relative class is represented at the next level. The finite controlled implication6.2 turns its small homological filling into the required small homotopy, retaining the already specified boundary. Thus no unrestricted large nullhomotopy is substituted.

Make a small disk patch of each such (k+1)-disk embedded at the next boundary level and join all its visits to next-level handles by disjoint arcs in its parameter disk. General position embeds these arcs in the boundary because their dimensions sum below n−1. The boundary spheres at their ends are homotopic, within the same small track image, to spheres meeting exactly the associated k-handle belt sphere once and the other belts not at all. For k=1,2 the needed ambient move is the smooth Whitney operation: its double-point loops are filled in the supplied small simply connected region; its two-dimensional Whitney disks can be made embedded and disjoint in boundary dimension n−1≥5, since 2+2<n−1. Boundary twists correct the framing, with the extra pairs removed by a parallel move in that same coordinate buffer. For higher k this sentence alone would be false: arbitrary homotopic high-dimensional embedded spheres need not be isotopic. The exact relative engulfing operation in Quinn6.1, printed312 (the Stallings/Wall geometric-connectivity move), must instead supply the ambient isotopy of these specially constructed patches, supported near the actual two-complex of their homotopies. That operation is being reconstructed below and is not yet supplied by the ordinary two-dimensional Whitney count. AFTER that operation, introduce the cancelling (k+1,k+2)-pairs on the embedded patches and perform its isotopies. Each old k-handle now has a cancelling (k+1)-handle meeting it in one transverse disk. Absorb each pair. All isotopies are supported in the small homotopy neighborhoods and change nothing on the protected product. The output has no handles of index at most k, with a bound which is a fixed multiple of the original finite track bound. Choose that input bound before running the finite operation.

The dual argument removes high handles when connectivity is supplied from the other end. In particular, a smooth thin h-cobordism with these actual small homotopies is reduced to two adjacent middle handle indices. Its handle boundary matrix and a small inverse come from the two homotopies: subdivide their finite track prisms and intersect them with the dual cells; the equation dh+hd=I gives the matrix inverse in the two-term complex, with propagation bounded by the track diameter plus the cell mesh. This is the actual geometric matrix, not an abstract inverse chosen after forgetting positions.

### 6.4 The control-space issue in geometric matrix splitting

Section5.9 is written for a finite PL control manifold. N in the present application is only TOP and must not be assigned a PL structure while proving product structure. The algebraic splitting can nevertheless be used on each compact metric base buffer as follows. Embed that compact metric space C into the Hilbert cube by its normalized distances to a countable dense sequence, with summably decreasing coordinate weights. Choose q so the tail diameter is below a prescribed eta. In R^q choose a compact smooth regular neighborhood U of the projected C, with its neighborhood width also below eta. Every point of U times the Hilbert-cube tail is then within3eta of C: compare its first q coordinates to a projected point of C and use the tail bound. The neighborhood U admits an ordinary finite smooth cellulation.

Project each existing geometric basis point to U. Apply the finite matrix construction5.9 there. For each of the FINITELY many auxiliary bases introduced by that construction choose a nearby point of C and lift its position back; no continuous section of the projection is needed. Every nonzero matrix entry and every finite deformation track changes its base position by at most6eta. Since geometric algebra records only finitely many basis locations, this yields the same algebraic identities and the required control in C after initially reserving this error budget. Relative buffered subsets are treated with two nested projected neighborhoods: the construction is identity on the larger protected buffer, and eta is chosen below the distance to its smaller retained subset. The lift therefore leaves every actual protected basis and its matrices unchanged. This is an algebraic reduction of the control SPACE, not a claim of a topological manifold approximation of N. Its input uses a smooth regular neighborhood of the projected compactum; such a neighborhood is obtained from a finite union of small Euclidean boxes by smoothing its finite boundary corners and taking a regular sublevel of that finite smoothing.

The resulting small elementary operations are realized by cancelling handle stabilizations, slides along their small coordinate paths and sign changes. The relative-two-complex lifts from6.1 supply the actual small path relations needed for those slides. Coefficientwise cancelling intersections are removed by the smooth Whitney construction of6.3. Thus a matrix reduced to a geometric basis bijection gives actual cancelling handle pairs, with the old relative collar fixed. At this point the remaining end construction must manufacture the finite projective-kernel presentation for each neighborhood and apply this operation to it; that presentation and the successive small-shell choices are the next step, not an inference from the final algebraic rank alone.
