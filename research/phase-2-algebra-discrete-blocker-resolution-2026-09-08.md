## Batch 3 — modular traces and low-degree group homology

### Proof and source evidence

The relevant complete passages were read in Webb §10.1 through Lemma 10.1.1
and the Brauer-character definition and §10.2 Theorem 10.2.2; Pound/Martin
Lemma 6.6; *Yanqi Lake Lectures on Algebra I* §§11.1--11.2 through Theorem
11.2.3; Halle §5.3; Stacks Project §10.153 Definition 10.153.1 and complete
proofs of Lemmas 10.153.2 and 10.153.9; Löh Definition 1.7.12 through Theorem
1.7.15 and Theorem 3.2.18 with proof; Weibel §§6.4, 6.7, and 6.8.3--6.8.8;
and Dekimpe--Hartl--Wauters complete §§2--5 and §10.2. The latter reading
includes the extension, partial-splitting, coefficient-pushout, pullback,
normalizer-quotient, exactness, and cocycle computations. DHW §9 is merely a
comparison with the spectral-sequence transgression and is not used by the
owned direct construction.

Webb's Lemma 10.1.1 is under splitting-modular-system assumptions and does not
alone prove the arbitrary complete-DVR statement. The corrected route uses
completeness to obtain Henselianity by the Stacks Newton proof. For
`T^m-1`, `p` not dividing `m` makes the derivative a unit at a residue root;
uniqueness of the lift makes the prime-to-`p` lift multiplicative.

The bar comparison is now a local proof, not a citation-shaped gap. Give the
homogeneous bar complex the right `ZG` action `b*g = g^(-1)b`; the balanced
tensor product maps to diagonal coinvariants, with the displayed inverse, and
balancing, every face, and both composites are checked before composing with
the published bar-resolution computation.

The low-degree route likewise avoids an unbuilt spectral-sequence supplier.
The increasing row filtration of `Tot_n` is finite for `n <= 3`. Successively
eliminating components identifies the obstruction in `E^2_(2,0)`, its
degree `(-2,1)` zigzag to `E^2_(0,1)`, the edge lift, and the reverse
obstructions; the two composites vanish and the reverse calculations give
exactness. Finite associated gradeds suffice.

For the free presentation, `C_q=(B_q(F))_R` is free over `ZG`.
Horizontal homology of `B^right(G) tensor_ZG C_q` vanishes in positive degree
and has zero row `(B_q(F))_F`; vertical homology is
`B^right_p(G) tensor_ZG H_q(R;Z)`. This proves the required low-degree
abutment without inventing a strict `G`-action on the bar complex of `R`.

## Batch 13 — finite automata and the Turing boundary

### Proof and source evidence

The complete used ranges were read in Gallier §§3.3--3.5 through Definitions
3.5--3.7 and the determinisation invariant; Aho Lecture 3 §§1--2; Savage
§5.1 through Definition 5.1.1; Watrous Lecture 12 §12.1 through Definition
12.2; and Sipser/MIT 18.404J Lecture 5 slides 8--10. All five retained
sources are fetch-verified.

Finite epsilon saturation stabilizes by stage `|Q|`. Word transition is
ordinary natural-number recursion on `N x P(Q)` with the input position carried
explicitly and frozen past the word length. The subset construction starts at
`ECl({q0})`, so its induction invariant also covers the empty word. The full
power set is finite and nonempty and includes the absorbing empty subset.

The Turing interface is deliberately definitional: finite state/input/tape
alphabets, distinct start/accept/reject states, total transition on nonhalting
states, right-infinite finite-support tape, and a clamped-left boundary. Blank
is excluded from the input alphabet, so initial support is exactly the input
domain, including empty input. One/two halt states, one/two-sided tapes, and
halt-transition conventions are recorded as model variants, not unsupported
equivalence theorems.
