## Mathematics requiring owner reconciliation

The controlling plan's surrogate-regret inequality is not sound with its stated constant and tie convention. The plan and plan-spec otherwise agree on finite-label calibration, the AC/RCD Bayes branch, the item interfaces, and the calibration-estimator limit order; the plan controls the corrected local draft below.

1. The plan assumes `|eta(x)-1/2|^s <= c [L_Phi(x,0)-L_Phi(x,h*_Phi(x))]` and concludes `2c [L_Phi(h)-L*_Phi]^(1/s)`. Mohri et al., Theorem 4.7, instead assumes the right side is `c^s [L_Phi(x,0)-L_Phi(x,h*_Phi(x))]` to obtain coefficient `2c`. Under the plan's coefficient, the pointwise/Jensen argument yields `2 c^(1/s)`. A counterexample to the planned constant is the one-point law `Y=+1`, logistic margin loss `Phi(t)=ln(1+e^t)`, `s=2`, `c=1/2`, and score `h=-0.1`: the extended conditional minimizer is `+infinity`, the conditional gap is `ln 2`, and `|eta-1/2|^2=1/4 <= (1/2)ln 2`; yet `R_01(h)-R*_01=1` and the planned right side is `sqrt(ln(1+e^0.1))<1`.
2. The plan defines `R_01(h)=P(Yh(X)<=0)`, so zero scores are errors for both labels. At `eta=1/2`, logistic loss has conditional minimizer `0`; the margin premise is `0<=0`, and `h=0` has classification excess `1/2` but surrogate excess zero. Thus the planned zero-remainder bound fails. Mohri's §4.7 predicts `+1` at zero; Lemma 4.5's displayed equality using `1_{h h*<=0}` overcounts when `h=0, eta>1/2`, though the resulting upper inequality remains valid. The batch-8 proof avoids that equality and gives the exact zero-is-error risk formula plus the sound correction `E[min(eta,1-eta) 1_{h=0}]`, with scale factor `2c^(1/s)`. This theorem remains `escalated` for owner reconciliation; the planned false formula is not asserted.

Proposed owner choice: either use the source margin parameterization `c^s` and source tie convention (`h=0` predicts `+1`), proving the clean `2c` bound with the corrected upper-risk argument; or retain the plan's coefficient and zero-is-error convention with the locally proved `2c^(1/s)` factor and explicit zero-score risk term.
