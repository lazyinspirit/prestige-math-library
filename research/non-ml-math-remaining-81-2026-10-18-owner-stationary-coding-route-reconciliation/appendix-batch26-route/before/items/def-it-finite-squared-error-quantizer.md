## Definition

Let $d,k\ge1$, let $X\subseteq\mathbb R^d$ be Borel, and give $X$ the relative Borel sigma algebra $\mathcal B(X)=\{X\cap B:B\text{ is in the sigma algebra generated by Euclidean open sets in }\mathbb R^d\}$. Let $\mu$ be a probability measure on $\mathcal B(X)$ with $\int_X\|x\|_2^2\,d\mu(x)<\infty$, and let $c_1,\ldots,c_k\in\mathbb R^d$ be reproduction points, where $\|x\|_2^2:=\sum_{\ell=1}^d x_\ell^2$. A finite squared-error quantizer is a measurable map $Q:X\to\{1,\ldots,k\}$, with the finite index set given its discrete sigma algebra; its reproduction at $x$ is $c_{Q(x)}$ and its distortion is

$$D_\mu(Q;c_1,\ldots,c_k):=\int_{\mathbb R^d}\|x-c_{Q(x)}\|_2^2\,d\mu(x).$$

This is the expected single-letter distortion of [[def-it-single-letter-distortion-and-block-distortion]] for the measurable distortion $d(x,c)=\|x-c\|_2^2$. For a fixed ordered center tuple $c=(c_1,\ldots,c_k)$, the nearest-center rule assigns $x$ to the least index minimizing $\|x-c_j\|_2^2$. Its cell at index $i$ is

$$C_i(c):=\bigcap_{j<i}\{x:\|x-c_i\|_2^2<\|x-c_j\|_2^2\}\cap\bigcap_{j>i}\{x:\|x-c_i\|_2^2\le\|x-c_j\|_2^2\}.$$

The convention assigns every tie to the smallest index. A general finite measurable quantizer need not use this nearest-center partition; in particular, a compander partition below is specified by its own interval boundaries. When $X=\mathbb R^d$, this is the ordinary full-domain nearest-center quantizer.
