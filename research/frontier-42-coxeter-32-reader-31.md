## Mathematical assessment

- The reversed-product lemma proves the moved-space identity by telescoping fixed-vector differences and independence, then obtains reflection length by a rank bound; faithfulness is unnecessary for this bound.
- The root suppliers provide exactly the reverse-product face criterion, independent normals, common-face cone intersections, simple systems spanning moved spaces, convex positive-root cones, and moved-space rigidity under a common upper bound needed by the lattice proof. The meet uses the span of the common complex, not an unjustified equality with the intersection of moved spaces. The empty intersection and rank-one cases are separate, and joins follow by meeting the finite nonempty upper-bound set.
- The Kreweras argument proves closure, bijectivity, order reversal, length complementation and the square formula. The type-A proof includes both directions of the noncrossing criterion, singleton removal, interval-block contraction, refinement in both directions, and the white-region successor formula. Its final meet/join claims follow from crossing chords and transitivity of the long cycle.
- The S4 and S3 products, interval sizes, crossing obstruction, and dihedral normal forms and reflection lengths agree with independent calculations.
