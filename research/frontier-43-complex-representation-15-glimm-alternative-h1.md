## Corrected local CAR-subquotient component

The following argument supplies a genuine component from the hypothesis that an irreducible image has no compacts. It does **not** turn the CAR trace into a non-type-I factor representation of the ambient algebra.

Let D ⊆ B(H) be irreducible with D ∩ K(H)=0. Every nonzero hereditary subalgebra E of D is irreducible on its support Hilbert space and still has no nonzero compacts; consequently E is nonabelian. Indeed an abelian irreducible algebra acts on a one-dimensional support, giving a nonzero finite-rank operator in D. The hereditary irreducibility assertion can be supplied by the restriction/corner proof already identified in Blackadar II.6.1.9 or by the complete finite-vector approximation in Farah Corollary 3.4.6. In a nonabelian irreducible image, choose a self-adjoint b with two distinct spectral values, disjoint nonzero continuous spectral cutoffs f(b),g(b), and y with f(b)yg(b)≠0. Such y exists because otherwise the closed invariant subspaces generated by the two cutoffs would contradict irreducibility. Then x=f(b)yg(b) is nonzero and x²=0.

A necessary normalization in the recursion is stronger than merely ∥x∥=1. Write x=u|x| in the bidual, and choose a continuous function r with r(0)=0, 0≤r≤1, and r(t)=1 on a nonempty interval near ∥x∥. Set w=u r(|x|). This belongs to C*(x): r(t)/t extends continuously at zero after choosing r zero near zero. It satisfies w²=0, ∥w∥=1, and the corner

`D_{≪w*w} = {a ∈ D : (w*w)a = a(w*w) = a}`

is nonzero, since it contains a nonzero continuous spectral function supported inside the plateau of r(|x|). It is hereditary. Mere normalization without a plateau need not produce a nonzero exact-eigenvalue-one corner.

For a=w*w-fixed on both sides, Φ_w(a)=a+waw* is a *-monomorphism; its inverse on its range is compression to the initial support of w. Here w*w and ww* have orthogonal supports because w²=0. Direct multiplication gives Φ_w(a)Φ_w(b)=Φ_w(ab), and wΦ_w(a)=Φ_w(a)w, with the same equality for w*. These are the calculations omitted in Farah Lemma 3.7.3.

Starting with D, choose plateau-normalized square-zero contractions w_n recursively in the successive nonzero hereditary initial corners. Replicate through Φ_{w_0},…,Φ_{w_{n-1}} to obtain v_n∈D. This yields pairwise commuting **self-adjoint generated algebras** C*(v_n); not merely the weaker equations v_i v_j=v_j v_i. Put h_n=v_n*v_n+v_n v_n*. Each h_n is central in C*(v_n), is a positive contraction of norm one, and for i<n the construction gives

`h_i v_n = v_n h_i = v_n`, hence `h_i h_n = h_n`.

These identities follow first for one replication, where w*w fixes the initial corner and ww* fixes its replicated range, and then inductively under the *-monomorphisms Φ. Let B=C*(v_n:n≥0). All h_n are central in B. There is a character χ on the unital abelian algebra Z=C*(1,h_n:n≥0) with χ(h_n)=1 for every n. For a finite initial segment, h_N has norm one and h_i h_N=h_N for i<N, so a character attaining h_N=1 has every earlier h_i=1. Compactness of the character space gives χ for all n. Extend χ to a pure state of the unitization of B: the extension states form a nonempty compact face, and an extreme point of that face is pure. In its irreducible GNS representation ρ, Schur's lemma and the central values give ρ(h_n)=I.

Consequently ρ(v_n)²=0 and ρ(v_n)*ρ(v_n)+ρ(v_n)ρ(v_n)*=I. Multiplying this last equality by ρ(v_n)*ρ(v_n) shows that the two positive summands are complementary projections. Thus ρ(v_n) is a matrix unit linking them, and C*(ρ(v_n)) is a unital M₂. These M₂ algebras commute. The induced unital map M₂^{⊗m}→B(H_ρ) is injective because its finite-dimensional domain is simple. The maps are compatible with a↦a⊗I, and their norm closure is precisely the CAR algebra. Hence ρ(B)≅CAR and B has a CAR quotient.

For an arbitrary A with an irreducible π such that π(A)∩K=0, apply this to D=π(A) and take the inverse image of B under π. This gives a subalgebra of A with a CAR quotient. It does **not** require lifting the nilpotents or asserting that an infinite tensor product of cones embeds into A. The printed final proof of Farah Theorem 3.7.2 passes to an image quotient; the inverse-image step above makes the subquotient conclusion explicit. The local proof claims only this conclusion, not the stronger tensor-subalgebra formulation.

## CAR trace is a factor, but arbitrary extension fails

Let C=lim M₂^{⊗n} be CAR and τ the compatible normalized matrix trace. On H=L²(C,τ), left multiplication gives the GNS representation and ξ=1 is cyclic. Right multiplication commutes with the left image and ξ is cyclic for the right image too. Therefore ξ is separating for M=C'', and its normal vector trace is faithful. Traciality extends by bounded strong approximation (Farah Lemma 4.2.5 gives the source argument).

For z∈Z(M), let E_n be the orthogonal projection in L² onto M₂^{⊗n}. Left and right multiplication by that finite matrix algebra leave its L²-subspace invariant and commute with E_n; hence E_n(z) is central in the finite matrix algebra and equals τ(z)1. The finite matrix algebras have dense union in L², so E_n(z)→z in L² and z=τ(z)1. Thus M is a finite factor. It contains unital M₂^{⊗n} for every n, so it is infinite-dimensional; a finite type-I factor is a finite matrix algebra. Therefore M is type II₁. This proves the CAR factor supplier in full, subject only to the ordinary tracial GNS/finite-factor definitions.

It is false that an arbitrary state extension of τ to a larger separable C*-algebra retains this factor type. In the same representation set A₀=C*(C,K(H))⊆B(H). This algebra is separable. The vector state φ(a)=⟨aξ,ξ⟩ extends τ. Because K(H)⊆A₀, ξ is cyclic for A₀ and A₀ acts irreducibly. Thus its GNS von Neumann algebra is B(H), type I, whereas the restricted CAR cyclic representation has the type-II₁ closure just proved. The ambient A₀ may have other non-type-I representations; the example refutes the proposed inference from **this** trace extension. No CAR-subquotient theorem alone justifies a factor-preserving extension.

## The strengthened compression and exact remaining hold

Farah Exercise 3.10.35 asserts B⊆A and q∈B'∩A** with qAq=Bq≅CAR. The equation qAq=Bq is substantially stronger than the CAR subquotient above: it controls compression of every element of A. The displayed construction does not impose this equation for a dense enumeration of A. The source gives no proof of the needed additional recursion.

Even after proving such an equation, a trace state on qAq must be realized by an A-state whose normal extension is supported on q. This does not follow for an arbitrary projection q merely by defining φ(a)=τ(qaq). For example, let A=C([0,1]) and let q∈A** be the supremum of the mutually orthogonal support projections of evaluations at points of a countable dense proper subset D. Then qAq≅C([0,1]) faithfully. Evaluation at x∉D gives a state on qAq, but the associated state on A has normal extension supported on the x-evaluation projection and assigns q value zero. This is a concrete obstruction to an unsupported normal-extension claim. A proof needs a suitable closed/compact support projection, a directly verified supported extension, or a different construction giving the same interface.

Here is the complete **conditional** deduction once that precise support interface is actually proved. Suppose φ is an A-state with normal extension φ̃(q)=1, qAq=Bq≅CAR, q commutes with B, and φ(a)=τ(qaq), where τ is the CAR trace. In its GNS representation π, put e=π**(q) and M=π(A)''. Then eξ=ξ. The e-compressed cyclic subspace is the CAR tracial GNS space because eπ(a)ξ=π**(qaq)ξ and these compressed elements are exactly Bq. Thus eMe is the type-II₁ factor above. The central support of e in M is I: any central projection annihilating e annihilates ξ and consequently the dense subspace Mξ. If z∈Z(M), then eze is scalar in eMe, and full central support implies z itself is scalar. Therefore M is a factor. It cannot be type I since a nonzero corner of a type-I factor is type I. Since it has a nonzero finite full corner, it is type II. No faithful/cofinal requirement is needed for this implication.

Accordingly the minimal H1 interface sufficient for **factor type I ⇒ GCR** is: every nonzero separable antiliminal primitive quotient admits some non-type-I factor representation. One does not need to construct a faithful factor or one nonzero on every cofinal essential ideal for this particular implication: factor-type-I passes to quotients by precomposition. If A fails GCR, an irreducible quotient with no compacts has no nonzero liminal ideal (restriction to such an ideal would provide compacts), so that interface contradicts factor-type-I. This simplification preserves the claim; it changes only the strength of an intermediate construction.
