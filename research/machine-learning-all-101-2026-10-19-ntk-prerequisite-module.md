## Retained exact contract

Assume AC, inherited from the existing Gaussian/Hermite suppliers (and hence their countable/dependent choice uses). Let input dimension `n0 >= 1`, finite depth `L >= 2`, and bias scale `beta > 0`. Use Jacot–Gabriel–Hongler's parametrization `W alpha / sqrt(width) + beta b`, independent standard Gaussian weight and bias entries at initialization, linear output layer, and sequential hidden-width limit. Let `sigma:R->R` be globally Lipschitz and nonpolynomial as a function on R. No C² or bounded-second-derivative assumption is needed for **this positivity proposition**; those belong to the separate time-dependent lazy-limit theorem.

Set `Sigma^1(x,y)=x·y/n0+beta²`, and recursively `Sigma^(l+1)(x,y)=E[sigma(U)sigma(V)]+beta²`, where `(U,V)` is the centered Gaussian with covariance from `Sigma^l`. Put `Theta^1=Sigma^1` and `Theta^(l+1)=Theta^l dotSigma^(l+1)+Sigma^(l+1)` with entrywise kernel product and `dotSigma^(l+1)=E[sigma'(U)sigma'(V)]`. Choose a bounded measurable representative of the a.e. derivative; the positive marginal variances make its null-set choices irrelevant.

The scalar limiting kernel `Theta^L` is **strictly** positive definite on the entire unit sphere `S^(n0-1)`: for every finite collection of pairwise distinct sphere points and every nonzero real coefficient vector its Gram quadratic form is positive. This includes `n0=1` (the two-point sphere) and data containing antipodal pairs. There is no non-antipodal, angular separation, minimum distance, analyticity, or activation-parity hypothesis. Repeated observations are handled as a function on distinct inputs/quotient evaluations; an observation-index Gram matrix with duplicate rows need not be invertible. The vector-output kernel is `Theta^L tensor I`, with the corresponding block positive definiteness. The positive-bias condition is part of the original source architecture, not a new restriction: removing it would invalidate this proof and the source's claimed unrestricted sphere conclusion for some activations.

### 1. `lem-gaussian-ltwo-hermite-completeness`

**Statement to author.** For standard Gaussian measure gamma and `h_m=H_m/sqrt(m!)`, the h_m form an orthonormal basis of real `L²(gamma)`. Every f has norm-convergent Hermite expansion and Parseval identity. A continuous nonpolynomial f in this space has unbounded support of nonzero Hermite coefficients. In particular this holds for every rescaled nonpolynomial Lipschitz activation.

**Existing exact inputs.** `def-monic-probabilists-hermite-polynomials` and `lem-hermite-orthogonality-and-monomial-expansion` give convention, triangular polynomial span, and orthogonality, not completeness. Add `cor-uniqueness-of-the-l-one-fourier-transform`, `lem-complex-lp-completeness-density-and-inner-product`, `thm-dominated-convergence`, `def-standard-normal-and-normal-laws`, `def-complex-exponential`, and `def-axiom-of-choice` for the following short direct proof. These Fourier/Lp pages are already in the current transitive page closure (orders 550 and 460); this adds explicit item obligations, not a missing backward page edge.

**Complete proof route to implement.** If f is orthogonal to all H_m, triangularity gives all Gaussian moments `integral f(x)x^m gamma(dx)=0`. The Lebesgue function `v=f phi`, with `phi=(2pi)^(-1/2) exp(-x²/2)`, is integrable by Cauchy–Schwarz. For every finite a, `integral |f| exp(a|x|) dgamma` is finite by Cauchy–Schwarz and completing the square in `exp(2a|x|)phi(x)`. Taylor partial sums for `exp(-2pi i xi x)` are dominated by `exp(2pi |xi||x|)`. Dominated convergence therefore gives zero Fourier transform of v, because each Taylor term integrates to zero. Fourier uniqueness yields v=0 a.e.; phi>0 yields f=0 gamma-a.e. Finite orthogonality gives Bessel's bound; L² completeness makes partial sums converge; the residual is orthogonal to every H_m and so zero. This proves expansion and Parseval. Finite Hermite support would make f equal to a polynomial a.e.; continuity plus positivity of gamma on every nonempty interval upgrades this to equality everywhere. Lipschitz growth guarantees L² integrability. This proves the nonpolynomial tail implication without assuming it from orthogonality.

The published `ex-hermite-functions-as-fourier-eigenfunctions` actually contains a complete Fourier-uniqueness proof of an unweighted Hermite-function basis. It is homed on `schwartz-space-and-the-plancherel-theorem-examples`, a B page; do not use that example as a cross-page logical supplier. The A-supplier proof just specified is independent of it and uses no Plancherel or Hermite completeness circularity.

### 2. `lem-gaussian-hermite-covariance-expansion`

**Statement to author.** If f,g are real L²(gamma), with normalized Hermite coefficients a_m,b_m, and `(X,Y)` is a centered Gaussian pair with unit variances and correlation rho in `[-1,1]`, then `E[f(X)g(Y)]=sum_m a_m b_m rho^m`. The series is absolutely convergent even at the endpoints. In particular the dual kernel of f is `sum_m a_m² rho^m` and `sum_m a_m²=||f||²`.

**Dependencies.** Supplier 1; the Hermite definition/orthogonality; `def-multivariate-normal-law`, `def-standard-normal-and-normal-laws`, `thm-factorization-of-expectations-for-independent-variables`, `thm-dominated-convergence`, and the L²/Cauchy–Schwarz interface. Gaussian laws are finite-dimensional only; no infinite-index GP existence is needed.

**Complete proof route to implement.** Prove locally the generating identity `exp(tx-t²/2)=sum H_m(x)t^m/m!` using the explicit polynomial coefficients in the Hermite definition (absolutely convergent exponential products). Real Gaussian exponential moments follow directly by completing the square in the normal density, including zero variance. Applied to Gaussian projections, they give `E[exp(sX-s²/2)exp(tY-t²/2)]=exp(rho st)`. Differentiate finitely many times at (0,0): polynomial times `exp(C(|X|+|Y|))` is integrable, by Cauchy–Schwarz, Gaussian square-completion and finite Gaussian moments; this justifies derivatives through expectation. Coefficient comparison yields `E[h_m(X)h_n(Y)]=delta_mn rho^m`. At rho=±1 also verify directly `Y=±X` a.s. and Hermite parity. Apply this identity to the finite partial sums of f,g. Their marginals are gamma; Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the product-expectation error by the L² approximation errors independently of rho. Finally `sum |a_m b_m rho^m| <= ||a||_2 ||b||_2`, proving all endpoint/interchange claims. No Mehler formula is imported unproved.

### 3. `lem-biased-hermite-kernel-is-strictly-positive-definite`

**Statement to author.** Let mu be continuous, nonpolynomial and in `L²(gamma)`; let `0<alpha<1`, `delta=1-alpha`, and `c>=0`. Then `k(x,y)=c+sum_{r>=0} a_r² (alpha x·y+delta)^r` is strictly positive definite on every finite-dimensional unit sphere, where a_r are mu's normalized Hermite coefficients. The original kernel uses `q=1/n0+beta²`, `mu(z)=sigma(sqrt(q)z)`, `alpha=1/(1+n0 beta²)`, `delta=n0 beta²/(1+n0 beta²)`, and `c=beta²`.

**Dependencies.** Suppliers 1–2, `thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces` (or its nonnegative-series specialization), ordinary binomial formula and finite Euclidean inner products. Prove tensor/Gram positivity and polynomial separation locally, rather than depending on a quantum-algorithm Schur-product page or a general sphere theorem.

**Complete proof route to implement.** Binomial expansion gives `k(t)=sum_{m>=0} d_m t^m`, with `d_m=1_(m=0)c+alpha^m sum_{r>=m} a_r² binom(r,m)delta^(r-m)`. Tonelli gives `sum_m d_m=c+sum_r a_r²<infinity` by alpha+delta=1. Consequently each d_m is finite, the expansion is absolutely/uniformly convergent on `[-1,1]`, and rearrangement there is valid even when alpha t+delta is negative. Unbounded nonzero a_r and alpha,delta>0 show **every** d_m>0.

For finitely many unit points x_i, realize `(x_i·x_j)^m` as the Gram matrix of coordinate tensors `x_i^(tensor m)` in `R^(n0^m)` (m=0 means the scalar 1). Thus the quadratic form is `sum_m d_m ||sum_i c_i x_i^(tensor m)||²`. Absolute convergence permits the finite-sum interchange. If it vanishes, every tensor moment vanishes. For each index j the explicit polynomial
`p_j(x)=product_(i!=j) [(x-x_i)·(x_j-x_i)/||x_j-x_i||²]`
is defined for pairwise distinct points, equals 1 at x_j and 0 at all other points. Each of its homogeneous parts is a linear functional of a tensor power, so the vanishing moments imply `0=sum_i c_i p_j(x_i)=c_j`. This proves strictness, including antipodal points and the two-point sphere. For one point the empty product is 1. No quantitative separation bound is required, and this establishes a sufficient criterion rather than claiming the full Schoenberg equivalence.

### 4. `lem-gaussian-activation-covariance-strictness-and-ntk-recursion`

**Statement to author.** For a continuous nonconstant Lipschitz sigma, strict positive definiteness of a covariance kernel on each finite distinct-input set implies strict positive definiteness of its next activation covariance; covariance and derivative-expectation Gram matrices are PSD, and the displayed NTK recursion preserves a strictly positive additive covariance term.

**Dependencies.** `def-multivariate-normal-law`, `def-standard-normal-and-normal-laws`, `thm-c1-lipschitz-ac-bv-hierarchy`, `thm-fundamental-theorem-of-calculus-for-absolutely-continuous-functions`, finite-dimensional spectral/square-root interfaces already used by the Gaussian definition, expectation linearity/Cauchy–Schwarz, and AC. The absolute-continuity pages (orders 244/466) are in the existing closure. A general Rademacher theorem is unnecessary: on each compact interval Lipschitz implies AC, so the sharp FTC gives a.e. differentiability; countable union covers R, difference quotients bound the derivative, and their measurable limits give a representative. State this short regularity argument explicitly.

**Complete proof route to implement.** On a finite input set, a positive-definite covariance C gives `Z=C^(1/2)G` with invertible square root and independent standard normal coordinates. Every nonempty open box has positive product-normal probability. Continuity/invertibility therefore give full support of Z without importing a density theorem from another page. If `sum c_i sigma(Z_i)=0` a.s., continuity and full support imply the same identity at every vector in R^N. Holding all but coordinate j fixed and choosing two values where sigma differs forces c_j=0. Hence `E[(sum c_i sigma(Z_i))²]>0` for c nonzero; adding `beta² (sum c_i)²` preserves strictness. Lipschitz growth ensures finite second moments. Derivative features are bounded, so their finite Gram quadratic forms are expectations of squares. Prove the finite Schur-product fact locally: PSD matrices have finite Gram factorizations, and entrywise product is the Gram of coordinatewise tensor products. Thus the `Theta^l dotSigma^(l+1)` term is PSD, and adding strict `Sigma^(l+1)` is strict. Positive variances are at least beta², so derivative null-set choices are irrelevant. No global Gaussian-process or RKHS existence theorem enters this argument.
