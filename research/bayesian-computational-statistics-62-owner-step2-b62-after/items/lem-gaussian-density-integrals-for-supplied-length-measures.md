## Statement

For $d\in\{1,2\}$, suppose a countably additive measure $\lambda_d$ on $\mathcal B(\mathbb R^d)$ is supplied and satisfies
$$\lambda_d\!\left(\prod_{j=1}^d(a_j,b_j]\right)=\prod_{j=1}^d(b_j-a_j)\qquad(a_j<b_j).$$
No assertion about constructing these measures is made.

1. If $Q$ is a nondegenerate compact box and $h:Q\to\mathbb R$ is bounded, Borel measurable and Riemann integrable, then its integral against the supplied measure equals its proper Riemann integral. Nonnegative Borel functions on the whole space are computed from increasing compact-box cutoffs by monotone convergence.
2. For the supplied $\lambda_1$, every $m\in\mathbb R$, $v>0$ and
$$g_{m,v}(x)=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi v}}e^{-(x-m)^2/(2v)}$$
satisfy
$$\int g_{m,v}\,d\lambda_1=1,\qquad \int xg_{m,v}(x)\,d\lambda_1(x)=m,\qquad \int(x-m)^2g_{m,v}(x)\,d\lambda_1(x)=v.$$
The signed first moment is absolutely integrable. These are density-integral statements and require no choice axiom in addition to the supplied measures.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The supplied Borel measures and their half-open-box volumes in the Statement.

[F1] Borel sets form the sigma-algebra generated by open sets. A continuous real function is Borel measurable: its inverse images of open sets are open, and the sets whose inverse images are Borel form a sigma-algebra. [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]

[F2] Measures are monotone; nonnegative simple integrals are finite sums of coefficients times cell measures and agree with the nonnegative integral. [[prop-measure-monotonicity]], [[def-integral-of-a-nonnegative-simple-function]], [[prop-the-nonnegative-integral-agrees-with-the-simple-integral]]

[F3] Nonnegative integrals are monotone and homogeneous, and integrals are linear on absolutely integrable functions. [[prop-order-and-scalar-rules-for-the-nonnegative-integral]], [[thm-linearity-of-the-lebesgue-integral-on-l-one]]

[F4] A bounded Riemann integrand has equal upper and lower Darboux integrals; continuous one-variable functions on compact intervals are Riemann integrable. [[thm-multidimensional-darboux-equals-riemann]], [[thm-continuous-implies-integrable]]

[F5] Monotone convergence and dominated convergence apply on a supplied measure space. [[thm-monotone-convergence-for-the-integral]], [[thm-dominated-convergence]]

[F6] The improper Riemann Gaussian integral is $\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-t^2}dt=\sqrt\pi$; proper substitution and integration by parts hold on compact intervals. [[thm-gaussian-integral]], [[thm-substitution]], [[thm-integration-by-parts]]

[F7] The exponential is continuously differentiable with derivative itself, and the chain rule applies. Exponential growth dominates each fixed polynomial, and a positive real has a positive square root. [[thm-derivative-of-exponential]], [[thm-chain-rule]], [[thm-exponential-beats-every-polynomial]], [[thm-of-square-roots]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** finite Darboux bounds followed by explicit cutoffs.

1.1 Every compact box has finite supplied measure, since it is contained in a larger half-open box. A bounded coordinate face has measure zero: enclose it in a half-open slab of arbitrary positive thickness $\eta$, with the other side lengths fixed and positive. Its measure is at most $C\eta$ by the given volume formula and [F2], and so must be zero. In dimension one this says that singletons are null. Consequently compact boxes have their usual volumes, and disjointifying a finite grid of closed cells only removes null faces. [given, F2, algebra]

1.2 For a nonnegative bounded Borel Riemann integrand $h$ on $Q$, take the infimum and supremum of $h$ on each cell of any finite grid. The corresponding disjoint-cell simple functions bound $h$, and [F2] and step 1.1 make their integrals exactly the lower and upper Darboux sums. By [F3] and [F4], the integral of $h$ is squeezed to its Riemann value. For signed bounded $h$, add a finite constant to make it nonnegative and subtract that constant's finite integral using [F3]. This proves the compact comparison. For nonnegative Borel $H$ on the whole space, $H\mathbf1_{[-n,n]^d}\uparrow H$, so [F5] gives the cutoff assertion. The grids, extrema, and cutoff sequence are finite or explicitly specified; no countable selection is used. [step 1.1, F2, F3, F4, F5, construct]

2.1 Put $\phi(t)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-t^2/2}$. It is continuous and Borel by [F1] and [F7]. On $[-n,n]$, proper substitution $t=\sqrt2u$ in [F6] computes its Riemann integral. As $n\to\infty$, [F6]'s improper Gaussian value makes this tend to one. Step 1.2 identifies each compact integral with its supplied-measure integral, and [F5] gives $\int\phi\,d\lambda_1=1$. [F1, F4, F5, F6, F7, step 1.2]

2.2 Since $\phi'=-t\phi$ by [F7], compact integration by parts [F6] gives
$$\int_{-n}^nt^2\phi(t)dt=\int_{-n}^n\phi(t)dt-2n\phi(n).$$
All functions and derivatives here are continuous, so the proper integral hypotheses hold by [F4]. Also $n\phi(n)\to0$: for $n\ge1$, $n^2/2\ge n/2$, hence $0\le ne^{-n^2/2}\le ne^{-n/2}\to0$ by [F7]. Apply step 1.2 and monotone convergence [F5] to the nonnegative $t^2\phi(t)$ cutoffs. Step 2.1 then gives $\int t^2\phi(t)\,d\lambda_1=1$. [F4, F5, F6, F7, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra]

2.3 The bound $|t|\le1+t^2$ and steps 2.1–2.2 make $|t|\phi(t)$ integrable. On each symmetric compact interval, the Riemann integral of $t\phi(t)$ is zero by oddness, using the proper substitution $t=-u$. Step 1.2 transfers these compact integrals to the supplied measure. Dominated convergence [F5], with majorant $|t|\phi(t)$, yields $\int t\phi(t)\,d\lambda_1=0$. [F3, F5, F6, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra]

3.1 Fix $m\in\mathbb R$ and $\sigma=\sqrt v>0$. On the explicitly specified intervals $[m-\sigma n,m+\sigma n]$, proper substitution $x=m+\sigma t$ gives compact integrals of $g_{m,v}$, $(x-m)^2g_{m,v}$ and $(x-m)g_{m,v}$ equal to the corresponding integrals of $\phi$, $\sigma^2t^2\phi$ and $\sigma t\phi$ on $[-n,n]$. Step 1.2 and monotone convergence give mass one and centered second moment $\sigma^2=v$. Since $|x-m|\le1+(x-m)^2$, the centered first moment is absolutely integrable, and the same dominated-cutoff argument as step 2.3 gives centered mean zero. Linearity then gives raw mean $m$. This proves both claims for the supplied measures without constructing any new measure or invoking a choice-dependent normal-law supplier. [F3, F5, F6, F7, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 2.3, construct, algebra] ∎
