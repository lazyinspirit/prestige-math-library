A representation of sl2(C) on a complex vector space V is a linear mapping
⇢: sl2 ! EndC(V )
that is compatible with taking commutators; the representation is of course deter-
mined by the three linear operators ⇢(H), ⇢(E), and ⇢(F). We usually suppress ⇢
and denote these operators simply by H, E, and F.
A representation is called irreducible if it does not contain any nontrivial sub-
space that is invariant under the action by H, E, and F. A basic fact is that every
ﬁnite-dimensional representation of sl2(C) is a direct sum of irreducible representa-
tions. The fancy reason is that sl2(C) is the complexiﬁcation of the real Lie algebra
su2, and that every ﬁnite-dimensional representation of su2 lifts to a representation
of the Lie group SU2 (which, as a manifold, is just S3). Because SU2 is compact,
one can then average over the group to obtain an invariant inner product, which can
be used to decompose V into irreducible representations. So much for the general
theory; we won’t need it, because we can easily prove all these facts by hand.
Because we are really interested in An+⇤(M), we are only going to consider
representations V with the following property: for every v 2 V , one has Env = 0
and F nv = 0 for n ≫0. This happens for example when V is ﬁnite-dimensional;
our representation is not ﬁnite-dimensional, but it still has this property. For the
sake of today’s discussion, let’s call such representations bounded.

Operations on representations. We also need to know a little bit about direct
sums and tensor products of representations.
Suppose that V and V 0 are two
representations of sl2(C). The direct sum V ⊕V 0 is also a representation in the
obvious way. More interesting is the case of the tensor product V ⌦V 0. Here E,
F, and H act in the following way:
E(v ⌦v0) = Ev ⌦v0 + v ⌦Ev0
F(v ⌦v0) = Fv ⌦v0 + v ⌦Fv0
H(v ⌦v0) = Hv ⌦v0 + v ⌦Hv0
This makes sense if we think of the action by a Lie algebra as the derivative of the
action by the corresponding Lie group. Correspondingly, the Weyl elements acts as
w(v ⌦v0) = w(v) ⌦w(v0).

tion of sl2(C) by setting
(E · f)(v) = Ef(v) −f(Ev),
(F · f)(v) = Ff(v) −f(Fv),

map f : V ! V 0 is a morphism of sl2(C)-representations exactly when the element
f 2 Hom(V, V 0) belongs to ker H \ ker E \ ker F.
