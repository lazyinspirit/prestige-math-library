## Statement

Assume AC, hence Countable Choice. Let $H\le\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb R)$ be a nontrivial discrete torsion-free group generated by at most $R<\infty$ elements, and suppose $Y=H^2/H$ is noncompact. There is a compact connected smoothly bounded surface $N\subset Y$ with nonempty boundary such that inclusion induces an isomorphism $\pi_1(N)\cong\pi_1(Y)=H$. Every boundary circle is essential in $Y$. Its full inverse image $\widetilde N\subset H^2$ is connected; the interior is simply connected and its boundary consists of a locally finite disjoint family of proper embedded lines, each invariant under the nontrivial boundary element.

The group $H$ is consequently free of some rank $h\le R$, and $\chi(N)=1-h$. If $h=1$, $N$ is an annulus. The statement allows parabolics; the lifted proper lines may have the same ideal point at both ends. It does not yet assert geodesic boundaries, a funnel or cusp decomposition, or equality with the convex core.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the finite generator list, nontriviality, discreteness, torsion-freeness and noncompact quotient.

[A1] Choice and Countable Choice are [[def-axiom-of-choice]] and [[def-countable-choice]].

[F1] The model plane is simply connected and complete; its discrete torsion-free real group acts freely and properly with a complete proper covering quotient. Nonidentity elements are parabolic or loxodromic, their powers leave compact sets, and a compact model set meets only finitely many translates of another compact set. Quotient distance and the real hyperbolic model are supplied locally. [[lem-local-thin-components-of-torsion-free-kleinian-quotients]], [[lem-complete-curvature-minus-one-manifolds-are-kleinian-quotients]]

[F2] Under AC, every compact Jordan region in the plane is a closed topological disc. The usual bounded and unbounded complementary components and separation by the Jordan curve are part of the same actual theorem. [[lem-jordan-schoenflies-extension-for-plane-curves]]

[F3] Smooth boundary collars exist under Countable Choice. [[thm-collar-neighborhood-theorem]] Compact smoothly bounded metric-extended surface regions have finite relative curvilinear triangulations. Capping their finitely many boundary polygons adds one to Euler characteristic per cap, and a closed connected orientable cap has Euler characteristic $2-2g$. [[thm-finite-geodesic-triangulation-of-a-compact-riemannian-surface]], [[cor-orientable-compact-surface-has-euler-characteristic-two-minus-two-g]]

[F4] Freely reduced words form the free group, with identity exactly the word reduced to empty. [[thm-reduced-words-form-the-free-group]]

## Proof

1.1 Choose based loops representing the supplied generators in $Y$. They may be replaced, within their based homotopy classes, by finite geodesic polygons. Indeed finitely many small convex quotient charts cover their compact images; subdivide each parameter interval until each subarc lies in one such chart and replace it by the unique chart geodesic with the same endpoints, using geodesic interpolation there for the homotopy. The union is a finite embedded graph after subdivision at crossings and endpoints of overlaps. To verify finiteness rather than assume general position, lift every one of the finitely many compact geodesic arcs to the model. Only finitely many deck matrices can cause one such compact lift to meet another, by [F1]. Two model geodesic segments either have at most one crossing or share one segment; combine common segments and subdivide their finitely many endpoints. This gives the claimed finite graph, connected because all loops share the basepoint. Choose small disjoint smooth vertex discs and thin edge rectangles, using the finite positive separation of nonincident pieces, then smooth their finitely many corners inside coordinate discs. Their union is a compact connected smooth regular neighborhood $N_0$ containing the graph. Its inclusion into $Y$ surjects onto $H$, since it contains the generator loops. Its boundary is nonempty, since a compact boundaryless same-dimensional embedded region would be open and closed in connected $Y$ and would make $Y$ compact. [A1, F1, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 Every nullhomotopic embedded circle $C\subset Y$ bounds an EMBEDDED compact disc. Lift its nullhomotopic parametrization to a closed embedded circle $\widetilde C\subset H^2$; it is embedded because its projection is the embedding $C$. By [F2] its bounded region $D$ is a compact disc. Distinct deck translates of its boundary are disjoint: they are distinct lifts of the same embedded circle, and a nonidentity element cannot preserve the compact lifted circle because its powers would remain in a compact set, contrary to [F1]. Disjoint Jordan boundaries have disjoint interiors or one of their discs contains the other, by separation. Strict containment $gD\subset D$ or $D\subset gD$ is impossible, since iterating $g$ or $g^{-1}$ would give an infinite orbit contained in compact $D$. Equality is excluded for the same reason. Thus $D$ has disjoint deck translates and projects injectively to an embedded compact disc with boundary $C$. This supplies the disc theorem needed here from the actual planar Jordan result. [F1, F2, construct, algebra]

2.1 Fill every boundary circle of $N_0$ that is nullhomotopic in $Y$ by its disc from step 1.2, on the complementary side. The disc cannot be on the $N_0$ side: connectedness would then put all of $N_0$ in that disc, making its entire fundamental-group image trivial, contrary to the nontrivial surjective image $H$. Different such filling discs are disjoint. Otherwise their disjoint boundary circles force nesting, which would put one boundary circle of $N_0$ inside a disc whose interior lies on the complementary side of $N_0$, impossible. There are finitely many boundary circles, so this gives a compact connected smoothly bounded region $N$. Smoothness follows in ambient surface charts: a filled boundary disappears and all remaining boundaries are the old smooth curves. The induced image still is all of $H$, because it contains the old generators and the fillings are already discs in $Y$. Every remaining boundary is essential. The boundary cannot become empty, since that would make the noncompact connected $Y$ equal to compact $N$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 Every lift of a remaining boundary circle is a proper embedded line. Its monodromy element $u\in H$ is nontrivial by essentiality and has infinite order by [F1]. One compact lifted period arc and its translates $u^k$ give the whole lifted curve. Properness follows because a compact model set meets only finitely many of these compact translated arcs, again by [F1]. The full family of boundary lifts is locally finite by the same compact-translate argument applied to finitely many period arcs. Distinct lifts are disjoint, since the projected boundary circles are embedded and pairwise disjoint. These lines are genuinely unbounded in the model plane; in the parabolic case no assertion of two distinct ideal endpoints is made. [F1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Each component $W$ of the inverse image of $\operatorname{int}N$ is simply connected. A Jordan loop in $W$ bounds a compact Jordan disc $D$ in $H^2$ by [F2]. No boundary line from step 3.1 can enter $D$: a proper line has points outside every compact set, and a path from a point inside $D$ to one outside would cross its boundary loop, whereas that loop is disjoint from every boundary lift. Thus $D$ misses every boundary line. Its connected interior consequently lies in the same component $W$, since the complement of those lines is partitioned by the inverse images of $\operatorname{int}N$ and its outside components. More generally any loop in $W$ is homotopic to a finite polygonal loop in $W$, using finitely many small Euclidean discs covering its image. Perturb finitely many polygon vertices slightly inside those discs to obtain finitely many transverse crossings and no overlapping segments. Splitting at its finitely many crossings decomposes it, up to edge-path insertion and cancellation, into finitely many Jordan loops. Each contracts through its disc in $W$ as just proved, so the original loop contracts in $W$. This proves simple connectedness without assuming a topological classification of noncompact surfaces. [F1, F2, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 It follows that $\pi_1(N)\to H$ is injective: push a loop into the interior using a finite boundary collar, supplied by [F3]; if it is nullhomotopic in $Y$, its lift to the model closes and lies in one $W$ from step 4.1, where it contracts. Projecting that contraction contracts the loop in $N$. Surjectivity was proved in step 2.1, so inclusion is an isomorphism. The full inverse image of $N$ is connected. Explicitly, given two lifts of points of $N$, a path in $N$ joins their projected points; lift it from the first point. Its endpoint differs from the desired second lift by a deck element. Since every deck element is the image of a based loop in $N$, append such a loop to correct the endpoint and obtain a path in the preimage between the two lifts. Thus the connected preimage has precisely the boundary family already described in step 3.1. [F1, step 2.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Finally apply [F3] to $N$. The finite face-dual graph, augmented by an outside vertex incident to boundary edges, is connected: a path across face interiors to the nonempty boundary crosses a finite sequence of edge-adjacent faces. Choose a spanning tree rooted at that outside vertex. Remove its faces with parents before children, collapsing each triangle across its parent edge, which is free after the parent has gone. In the reference triangle $x,y\ge0$, $x+y\le1$, with free edge $x+y=1$, the deformation $H_t(x,y)=(x-tm,y-tm)$, $m=\min(x,y)$, fixes the two retained sides and ends on their union. Thus each removal is an actual deformation retraction of the remaining complex. This leaves a connected finite graph spine, so a spanning tree and non-tree-edge loops give a free basis for $\pi_1(N)$ by tree-path insertion and freely reduced edge cancellation [F4]. Its rank is $h=1-\chi(N)$, since every triangle/edge collapse preserves Euler characteristic. Since $H$ is generated by at most $R$ elements, $h\le R$: exponent sums onto the free basis give a rational vector space of dimension $h$, which the images of those $R$ generators must span. [F3, F4, step 2.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 If $h=1$, cap the $b>0$ boundary circles of $N$. Its closed orientable cap has genus $g_N\ge0$ by [F3], so $\chi(N)=2-2g_N-b$. Combining with $\chi(N)=0$ gives $2g_N+b=2$ and thus $g_N=0,b=2$. The finite orientable polygonal model, or cutting the two capped discs along one joining arc in the sphere and applying [F2], identifies $N$ with an annulus. This completes the stated compact incompressible-core and rank conclusions. No geodesic straightening or classification of its complement has yet been used. [F2, F3, step 6.1, given, algebra] ∎
