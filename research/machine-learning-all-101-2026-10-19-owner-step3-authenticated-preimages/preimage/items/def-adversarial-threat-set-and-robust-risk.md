## Definition

Let $(\mathcal X,\Sigma_X)$ and $(\mathcal Y,\Sigma_Y)$ be measurable spaces, let $\mathcal A$ be a measurable action space, and let $P$ be a probability measure on $(\mathcal X\times\mathcal Y,\Sigma_X\otimes\Sigma_Y)$. A threat correspondence is a map $\Delta:\mathcal X\to\mathcal P(\mathcal X)$ with $\Delta(x)\ne\varnothing$ for every $x$. It is measurable when its graph $\{(x,x')\in\mathcal X^2:x'\in\Delta(x)\}$ belongs to $\Sigma_X\otimes\Sigma_X$. For a measurable predictor $f:\mathcal X\to\mathcal A$ and a nonnegative measurable loss $\ell:\mathcal A\times\mathcal Y\to[0,+\infty]$, the pointwise robust loss is
$$L_{\Delta,f}(x,y)=\sup_{x'\in\Delta(x)}\ell(f(x'),y).$$
Its robust risk is the outer expectation
$$R^{\mathrm{rob}}_{P,\Delta}(f)=\mathbb E_P^*[L_{\Delta,f}(X,Y)] :=\inf\{\mathbb E_P[g(X,Y)]:g\ge L_{\Delta,f}\text{ and }g\text{ is nonnegative measurable}\}.$$
If $L_{\Delta,f}$ is measurable, this is its ordinary expectation. The supremum need not be attained. For a metric $\rho$ on $\mathcal X$ and a radius $\epsilon\ge0$, the closed-ball threat is $\Delta_\epsilon(x)=\{x'\in\mathcal X:\rho(x,x')\le\epsilon\}$. On $\mathbb R^d$, $\|u\|_\infty:=\max_{1\le j\le d}|u_j|$ and the $\ell_\infty$ threat is $\Delta_\epsilon(x)=\{x+\delta:\|\delta\|_\infty\le\epsilon\}$. For a binary classifier $f:\mathcal X\to\{-1,+1\}$, the robust zero-one loss is $\mathbf1\{\exists x'\in\Delta_\epsilon(x):f(x')\ne y\}$.
