## Example

Fix $0<\alpha\le1/4$, $b\in\mathbb R$, $\sigma\ge0$, and $x_0\in\mathbb R$. For every integer horizon $N\ge1$, take the finite probability space $\{-1,1\}^N$ with each sign vector assigned mass $2^{-N}$, let $\xi_0,\ldots,\xi_{N-1}$ be its coordinate signs, set $f(x)=x^2/2$, and run $x_{t+1}=x_t-\alpha(x_t+b+\sigma\xi_t)$. Write $q=1-\alpha$. For every $0\le t\le N$, the horizon-consistent moment is
$$\mathbb E[x_t^2]=\bigl(q^t x_0-b(1-q^t)\bigr)^2+\frac{\alpha\sigma^2}{2-\alpha}(1-q^{2t}),$$
so these moments converge as $t\to\infty$ to $b^2+\alpha\sigma^2/(2-\alpha)$. In the unbiased case $b=0$ with $\sigma>0$, the fixed-step expected squared gradient therefore has a positive noise floor.

Under AC, two published finite-horizon bounds also apply. For $b=0$, if $R_N$ is chosen independently and uniformly from $\{0,\ldots,N-1\}$ on a finite extension, then $\mathbb E|x_{R_N}|^2\le x_0^2/(\alpha N)+\alpha\sigma^2$. For general $b$, the uniform average satisfies $N^{-1}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\mathbb E[x_t^2]\le x_0^2/(\alpha N)+(5/2)b^2+\alpha\sigma^2$.

## Verification

1.1 For a fixed horizon $N$, let $\mathcal F_t$ be generated by $\xi_0,\ldots,\xi_{t-1}$, with $\mathcal F_0$ trivial. For every fixed sign prefix of length $t$, exactly half its extensions have $\xi_t=1$ and half have $\xi_t=-1$, so $\mathbb E[\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_t]=0$ and $\operatorname{Var}(\xi_t\mid\mathcal F_t)=1$. Since $x_t$ is $\mathcal F_t$-measurable and $f'(x)=x$, the direction $G_t=x_t+b+\sigma\xi_t$ has conditional mean $x_t+b$ and conditional variance $\sigma^2$; it is unbiased when $b=0$ and has bias $b$ otherwise. All variables have finite moments because the sample space is finite. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F11, given, algebra]

1.2 Put $m_t=\mathbb E[x_t]$. The update is $x_{t+1}=qx_t-\alpha b-\alpha\sigma\xi_t$, hence $m_{t+1}=qm_t-\alpha b$ with $m_0=x_0$. Induction gives $m_t=q^t x_0-b(1-q^t)$ for every $t\le N$. [F1, given, induction, algebra]

2.1 Let $V_t=\mathbb E[(x_t-m_t)^2]$. Because $x_t-m_t$ is $\mathcal F_t$-measurable and $\xi_t$ has conditional mean zero and conditional second moment one, the cross term vanishes and $V_{t+1}=q^2V_t+\alpha^2\sigma^2$, with $V_0=0$. Thus $V_t=\alpha^2\sigma^2\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}q^{2j}=\frac{\alpha\sigma^2}{2-\alpha}(1-q^{2t})$, since $1-q^2=\alpha(2-\alpha)$. Adding $m_t^2$ proves the displayed moment formula, and it is independent of the chosen horizon whenever $N\ge t$. [F2, F4, F11, step 1.2, given, algebra]

2.2 Now assume AC for the published bounds. In the unbiased case, $L=1$, $f_{\inf}=0$, $\Delta=x_0^2/2$, and step 1.1 verifies conditional unbiasedness and variance $\sigma^2$; $\alpha\le1/4\le1/L$. Use [F8] with $p_k=1/N$ and apply [F9] to obtain $\mathbb E|x_{R_N}|^2\le x_0^2/(\alpha N)+\alpha\sigma^2$. [A1, F8, F9, step 1.1, given, algebra]

3.1 Here $q=1-\alpha\in[3/4,1)$, so [F7] gives $q^t\to0$. The formula in step 2.1 therefore yields $\mathbb E[x_t^2]\to b^2+\alpha\sigma^2/(2-\alpha)$; if $b=0<\sigma$, this limit is strictly positive. This proves the exact constant-step noise-floor claim without using AC. [F7, step 2.1, given, algebra]

4.1 For general $b$, step 1.1 verifies conditional mean $f'(x_t)+b$, bias bound $\beta=|b|$, variance bound $\sigma^2$, and $f(x)\ge0$. The step restriction is $\alpha\le1/(4L)$ because $L=1$. Apply [F10] with $\Delta=x_0^2/2$ to obtain $N^{-1}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\mathbb E[x_t^2]\le x_0^2/(\alpha N)+(5/2)b^2+\alpha\sigma^2$. The exact limiting floor from step 3.1 is at most $b^2+\alpha\sigma^2$, since $2-\alpha\ge1$, and is therefore consistent with this bound. [A1, F10, step 1.1, step 3.1, given, algebra] ∎
