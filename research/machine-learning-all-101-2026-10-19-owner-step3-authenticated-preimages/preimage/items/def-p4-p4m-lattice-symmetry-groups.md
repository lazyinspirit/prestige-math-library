## Definition

Let $\rho=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ be the quarter-turn matrix and $\sigma=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}$ a reflection matrix. Let $C_4=\{\rho^j:j=0,1,2,3\}$ and $D_4=\{\rho^j,\rho^j\sigma:j=0,1,2,3\}$, with matrix multiplication. For $A=C_4$ or $D_4$, define $P_A=\mathbb Z^2\times A$ with multiplication

$$
(t,M)(s,N)=(t+Ms,MN).
$$

Each pair $(t,M)$ acts on lattice sites by $x\mapsto Mx+t$. The p4 lattice-symmetry group is $P_{C_4}$, generated by integer translations and quarter-turns. The p4m lattice-symmetry group is $P_{D_4}$, generated by integer translations, quarter-turns, and reflections. Their orientation subgroups have respectively four and eight elements, but both full groups are infinite because they contain every integer translation.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The matrices $\rho,\sigma$, the sets $C_4,D_4$, and the pair multiplication displayed in the Definition. Prove that these are groups acting on $\mathbb Z^2$, and that the full groups are infinite.

[F1] [[def-group]]: A group operation is associative, has an identity, and every element has a two-sided inverse.

[F2] [[def-group-action]]: A left action satisfies $e\cdot x=x$ and $(gh)\cdot x=g\cdot(h\cdot x)$ for every $g,h\in G$ and $x$ in the acted-on set.

## Proof

1.1 The matrices satisfy $\rho^4=I$, $\sigma^2=I$, and $\sigma\rho\sigma=\rho^{-1}$. The rotation matrices are $I,\rho,-I,-\rho$, hence distinct; their products add exponents modulo $4$, and $(\rho^j)^{-1}=\rho^{-j}$. The four matrices $\sigma,\rho\sigma,-\sigma,-\rho\sigma$ are distinct, have determinant $-1$, and differ from the rotations, which have determinant $1$. Thus $C_4$ has four elements and $D_4$ has eight. Writing elements of $D_4$ as $\rho^j\sigma^\epsilon$ with $\epsilon\in\{0,1\}$, the relation $\sigma\rho^j=\rho^{-j}\sigma$ shows products stay in $D_4$, and $(\rho^j\sigma^\epsilon)^{-1}=\rho^{-(-1)^\epsilon j}\sigma^\epsilon$ also lies in $D_4$. Both matrix groups preserve $\mathbb Z^2$ because $\rho$ and $\sigma$ are signed permutation matrices. [given, F1, algebra]

2.1 For either matrix group $A$, the pair multiplication is closed on $\mathbb Z^2\times A$. It is associative because both parenthesizations of three factors $(t,M),(s,N),(r,Q)$ equal $(t+Ms+MN r,MNQ)$. Its identity is $(0,I)$. For every pair $(t,M)$, the pair $(-M^{-1}t,M^{-1})$ belongs to the same set by 1.1, and $(t,M)(-M^{-1}t,M^{-1})=(0,I)=(-M^{-1}t,M^{-1})(t,M)$. Thus $P_{C_4}$ and $P_{D_4}$ satisfy the group axioms in [F1]. [given, F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The identity pair acts identically because $(0,I)\cdot x=x$. The affine maps respect pair multiplication because $M(Nx+s)+t=MNx+Ms+t$, so $((t,M)(s,N))\cdot x=(t,M)\cdot((s,N)\cdot x)$ and [F2] holds. The translations $(t,I)$ are distinct as $t$ ranges over $\mathbb Z^2$, so each full group is infinite. [given, F2, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
