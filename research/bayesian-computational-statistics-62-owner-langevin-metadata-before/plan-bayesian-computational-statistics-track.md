# Bayesian and Computational Statistics — prose scaffold

**Status: all six source dossiers integrated.** This is Category 3. Pair and
item identifiers below are proposed interfaces, not authored library content.
The proposed category directory is
`bayesian-computational-statistics/`; every B page is an examples companion
and never supplies another pair.

This category may use existing mathematics, independent probability/numerical
suppliers, and Categories 1–2. It has no direct or transitive dependency on
Category 4. Bayesian learning applications may be consumed by Category 4 after
their Category 3 interfaces are stable, but Category 3 proofs and algorithms
must stand without them. Category 1 (`MSF-*`) and Category 2 (`SME-*`) are
earlier draft interfaces; proposed dependencies on them are provisional until
the corresponding plans are reconciled. In particular, BCS owns the generic
optimization and Monte Carlo machinery needed for its own computation. It may
reuse the already published matrix factorizations and conditioning results.

Algorithms are proposed as `kind: algorithm` items with `alg-` IDs and a
`## Definition` body. Correctness, convergence, bias, and error claims are
separate result items with their own hypotheses. Statistical assumptions are
contracts, not conclusions: identifiability, correct specification, tail
conditions, mixing, and regularity must appear where used. Posterior
probability, algorithmic approximation, numerical stability, and frequentist
coverage are distinct claims.

**Notation:** `AC` means the Axiom of Choice only where it is explicitly
inherited from a local supplier's proof. Absolute continuity is written
`\ll`; it is a separate measure-theoretic hypothesis.

### BCS-01 — Bayesian models, priors, and posterior laws
**Pages:** bayesian-models-priors-and-posterior-laws / bayesian-models-priors-and-posterior-laws-examples. **Scope:** A Bayesian model consists of a measurable parameter space $(\Theta,\mathcal T)$, prior probability $\Pi$, measurable observation space $(\mathsf Y,\mathcal Y)$, and probability kernel $K(\theta,\cdot)$ with $\theta\mapsto K(\theta,B)$ measurable for every $B\in\mathcal Y$. The generic MSF model is only an indexed family; use the new `MSF-01 def-parameter-measurable-statistical-model-as-markov-kernel` when that family is to be mixed. Form joint law $\Pi(d\theta)K(\theta,dy)$ by kernel integration. For a measurable observation random element $Y$, define posterior initially as an $\Omega$-indexed RCD of $\theta$ given $\sigma(Y)$; its existence requires standard-Borel $\Theta$ and the inspected RCD theorem (AC here means Axiom of Choice). This is not yet a kernel indexed by every $y\in\mathsf Y$. When $\mathsf Y$ is standard Borel, factor the RCD through $Y$ with the inspected factorization lemma/disintegration theorem to obtain a measurable posterior kernel $y\mapsto\Pi_y$; on null conditioning values its version is not uniquely determined. For a dominated model, BCS-02's jointly measurable likelihood-density formula separately yields a data-indexed kernel on valid evidence fibers with the stated filler elsewhere, without using that standard-Borel observation-space route. Distinguish RCD, data-indexed posterior kernel, density formula, and null-fiber versions. **Proof route:** Product-kernel construction and RCD existence; when claiming a $y$-indexed kernel from an RCD, use `lem-regular-conditional-kernels-factor-through-a-standard-borel-conditioning-variable` or `thm-disintegration-of-a-joint-law-on-standard-borel-spaces` with both spaces standard Borel; carry the supplier's Axiom-of-Choice premise. **Examples:** finite parameter Bayes update; RCD as an $\Omega$-kernel; standard-Borel observation posterior kernel; dominated formula for a non-standard-Borel observation space. **Requires:** `MSF-01` `def-statistical-experiment`, `def-parametric-statistical-model`, `def-parameter-measurable-statistical-model-as-markov-kernel`; `def-probability-measure`, `def-measure-kernel-and-probability-kernel`, `def-conditional-law-given-a-random-element`, `thm-existence-of-regular-conditional-distributions-for-standard-borel-targets`; additionally `lem-regular-conditional-kernels-factor-through-a-standard-borel-conditioning-variable` and `thm-disintegration-of-a-joint-law-on-standard-borel-spaces` only for the data-indexed RCD case. **Sources:** Gelman et al., *Bayesian Data Analysis*, §§1.1–1.3; Johnson, Ott & Dogucu, *Bayes Rules!*, §§2.1.1–2.1.4, 2.3.1–2.3.6. Reuse the inspected local RCD and probability-kernel items; source hashes and exact extraction ranges are in the Bayesian-01 manifest.

### BCS-02 — Posterior propriety and dominated Bayes formula
**Pages:** posterior-propriety-and-dominated-bayes-formula / posterior-propriety-and-dominated-bayes-formula-examples. **Scope:** For a prior measure $\Pi$ on $(\Theta,\mathcal T)$ and nonnegative measurable likelihood section $L_y$, define $Z(y)=\int L_y(\theta)\Pi(d\theta)$. On a fixed observation fibre the normalized measure is a probability exactly when $0<Z(y)<\infty$. For a proper prior, the almost-everywhere result additionally requires that $(\theta,y)\mapsto L_y(\theta)$ be jointly measurable and be a normalized sampling-kernel density against a sigma-finite data measure $\nu$; separate RN versions indexed by $\theta$ do not by themselves imply joint measurability. Then Tonelli gives $\int Z(y)\nu(dy)=1$, so normalization holds on the prior-predictive full-measure set where $Z>0$; zero/infinite fibres receive only the existing RCD filler, not a pointwise learned posterior. For a sigma-finite improper prior, prove the finite-positive normalizer statement locally and distinguish it from a prior-predictive probability. Give flat-prior known-variance Normal mean posterior $N(\bar y,\sigma^2/n)$ for $n\ge1$; show improper Beta$(0,0)$ Binomial posterior diverges at an endpoint when $y=0$ or $y=n$. **Proof route:** Reuse the existing Bayes-kernel theorem for proper priors, Tonelli for almost-everywhere normalization, and explicit integration/completing the square for examples. Never form a quotient at zero/infinite evidence. **Examples:** valid finite evidence, prior-predictive null fiber, improper flat prior yielding proper posterior, improper Beta endpoint divergence. **Requires:** BCS-01; `MSF-04` `def-dominated-statistical-experiment`, `def-likelihood-density`; `thm-conditional-density-formula`, `thm-bayes-formula-for-dominated-kernels`, `thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces`. **Sources:** Gelman et al., *Bayesian Data Analysis*, §§1.3, 2.8; Johnson, Ott & Dogucu, *Bayes Rules!*, §§2.1.3–2.1.4, 2.3.4–2.3.6. Do not duplicate the generic proper-prior posterior-kernel theorem.

### BCS-03 — Conjugate families and Gaussian conditioning
**Pages:** conjugate-families-and-posterior-prediction / conjugate-families-and-posterior-prediction-examples. **Scope:** State the sufficient exponential-family update direction: for IID data with likelihood density $h(y)\exp\{\eta(\theta)^TT(y)-A(\theta)\}$ against a declared data measure and prior density proportional to $\exp\{\eta(\theta)^T\chi-\nu A(\theta)\}$ against a declared parameter measure, update $\chi$ by $\sum_iT(y_i)$ and $\nu$ by $n$, only when the prior and posterior normalizers are finite positive. Define the proper laws used in the examples: Beta$(\alpha,\beta)$ has density $p^{\alpha-1}(1-p)^{\beta-1}/B(\alpha,\beta)$ on $(0,1)$; Gamma$(a,b)$ is shape-rate with density $b^a\lambda^{a-1}e^{-b\lambda}/\Gamma(a)$ on $(0,\infty)$; Poisson$(e\lambda)$ has mass $e^{-e\lambda}(e\lambda)^y/y!$ for $y\in\mathbb N_0$, with $e\ge0,\lambda>0$ (when $e=0$ it is concentrated at zero); Dirichlet$(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k)$ has density $\Gamma(\sum_j\alpha_j)\prod_jp_j^{\alpha_j-1}/\prod_j\Gamma(\alpha_j)$ relative to Lebesgue measure $dp_1\cdots dp_{k-1}$ on $p_j>0,\sum_{j<k}p_j<1$, with $p_k=1-\sum_{j<k}p_j$; and Multinomial$(n,p)$ has mass $n!\prod_jp_j^{y_j}/\prod_jy_j!$ on integer counts $y_j\ge0,\sum_jy_j=n$. Require $\alpha,\beta>0$, Gamma shape/rate positive, and every Dirichlet coordinate positive. Then give exact Beta–Binomial update $\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha+y,\beta+n-y)$ for integer $0\le y\le n$; exposure-adjusted Gamma–Poisson update $\mathrm{Gamma}(a+\sum_i y_i,b+\sum_i e_i)$ for independent counts and proper prior; Normal–Normal update for independent $Y_i\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)$, known $\sigma^2>0$, prior $\mu\sim N(\mu_0,\tau_0^2)$ with $\tau_0^2>0$, variance $V_n=(\tau_0^{-2}+n\sigma^{-2})^{-1}$ and mean $V_n(\mu_0/\tau_0^2+n\bar y/\sigma^2)$; and Dirichlet–multinomial update $\alpha_j+y_j$. Prove Dirichlet normalization by iterated Beta integrals; do not claim the converse characterization of regular exponential families. Define the reusable Gaussian conditioner: for $X\in\mathbb R^d,Y\in\mathbb R^m$ jointly Gaussian with mean $(a,b)$ and PSD block covariance $\begin{pmatrix}A&C\\C^T&B\end{pmatrix}$, if $B\succ0$ the everywhere-defined Gaussian version of $\mathcal L(X\mid Y=y)$ has mean $a+CB^{-1}(y-b)$ and PSD covariance $A-CB^{-1}C^T$; $A$ and the conditional covariance may be singular. Exclude singular-$B$ pseudoinverse formulas. **Proof route:** Likelihood-kernel multiplication, positive-parameter Beta/Gamma integrals, iterative Beta normalization, and the residual $X-a-CB^{-1}(Y-b)$, whose zero covariance with $Y$ gives independence for a jointly Gaussian vector; prove the Schur-complement covariance is PSD. **Examples:** Beta–Binomial, exposure-adjusted Gamma–Poisson, normal precision pooling, multinomial Dirichlet, Gaussian conditioning with singular state covariance and SPD observed block. **Requires:** BCS-01, BCS-02; `MSF-04` `def-likelihood-density`; `MSF-05` `def-exponential-family`; `def-real-beta-integral`, `thm-real-beta-gamma-identity`, `thm-change-of-variables-for-expectation`, `thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces`, `def-multivariate-normal-law`, `lem-characteristic-function-of-a-multivariate-normal-law`. **Sources:** Gelman et al., *Bayesian Data Analysis*, 3rd ed., §§2.1, 2.4–2.6, 3.3–3.6, App. A; Johnson, Ott & Dogucu, *Bayes Rules!*, Ch. 3 §§3.1–3.5, Ch. 5 §§5.2.1–5.2.4/5.3.1–5.3.4; Bayesian-04 Särkkä App. A. The normal-model example with a textbook arithmetic typo is not reused.

### BCS-04 — Posterior prediction, credible sets, and Bayes actions
**Pages:** posterior-prediction-and-bayesian-decision / posterior-prediction-and-bayesian-decision-examples. **Scope:** When the posterior is supplied as a measurable kernel $y\mapsto\Pi_y$ by the standard-Borel disintegration route in BCS-01 or the dominated formula in BCS-02, define posterior predictive $Q_y^{pred}(A)=\int Q_\theta(A)\Pi_y(d\theta)$ for future probability kernel $Q_\theta$. Under finite second moments prove $E(\tilde Y|y)=E_{\Pi_y}E(\tilde Y|\theta)$ and $\operatorname{Var}(\tilde Y|y)=E_{\Pi_y}\operatorname{Var}(\tilde Y|\theta)+\operatorname{Var}_{\Pi_y}E(\tilde Y|\theta)$. Define a credible set as a measurable set $C(y)$ with $\Pi_y(C(y))\ge1-\alpha$; central quantile intervals are one choice, while HPD sets require a density and may be disconnected/nonunique. Given a measurable action space and loss $L(\theta,a)$, define posterior risk only when the integral is finite; call $a^*(y)$ a Bayes action only when it is a measurable minimizer and the minimum is attained. Under $E_{\Pi_y}\theta^2<\infty$, squared loss uniquely gives the posterior mean; under $E_{\Pi_y}|\theta|<\infty$, absolute loss is minimized by any posterior median; finite-class zero–one loss is minimized by a class of maximal posterior mass. The squared-loss proof inherits the Axiom-of-Choice assumption from the published L2-projection supplier. Do not assert pointwise zero–one MAP optimality for a continuous parameter. Posterior credibility is not a frequentist coverage theorem. **Proof route:** Tonelli/conditional integration for prediction; expand conditional second moments; squared-loss posterior mean follows from the checked L2 projection theorem; median and finite-class actions follow by direct loss comparison; use MSF-03 only under its measurable attained-minimizer contract. **Examples:** predictive variance decomposition, asymmetric loss, nonunique posterior median, continuous density mode without point mass, credible interval with nonnominal frequentist coverage. **Requires:** BCS-01, BCS-02; `MSF-03` `def-loss-function-and-risk`; `thm-conditional-expectation-is-the-l2-orthogonal-projection`, `thm-conditional-integration-through-a-regular-conditional-law`, `thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces`. **Sources:** Gelman et al., *Bayesian Data Analysis*, §§1.3, 2.5, 9.1; Johnson, Ott & Dogucu, *Bayes Rules!*, Ch. 8 §§8.1–8.3, §16.5. Neither source gives generic arbitrary-loss minimizer existence.

### BCS-05 — Hierarchical Bayes and partial pooling
**Pages:** hierarchical-bayesian-models-and-partial-pooling / hierarchical-bayesian-models-and-partial-pooling-examples. **Scope:** On standard-Borel group and parameter spaces, define a jointly measurable hyperparameter kernel $\eta\mapsto G_\eta$ and conditional observation kernels. Given a proper hyperprior $\Pi_\eta$ and conditional independence, form $\Pi_\eta(d\eta)\prod_jG_\eta(d\theta_j)f_j(y_j\mid\theta_j)$; call its normalization a joint posterior only on an observation fibre with positive finite evidence. For known-variance normal random effects with $s_j^2>0$ and $\tau^2>0$, derive $\theta_j\mid\mu,\tau,y_j\sim N(\hat\theta_j,V_j)$ where $\hat\theta_j=(\bar y_j/s_j^2+\mu/\tau^2)/(1/s_j^2+1/\tau^2)$ and $V_j=(1/s_j^2+1/\tau^2)^{-1}$. This is conditional shrinkage; when hyperparameters are unknown, integrate their joint posterior rather than plug them in silently. Define current-group prediction by integrating over that group’s posterior and new-group prediction by first drawing $\theta_{new}\sim G_\eta$ and integrating over the posterior of $(\eta,\theta_{new})$. Finite exchangeability is a modeling assumption and does not imply a mixture-of-iid representation; no de Finetti theorem is claimed here. **Proof route:** Product-kernel factorization and BCS-03 Normal–Normal update conditional on hyperparameters; check posterior normalizer. **Examples:** complete/no/partial pooling; full posterior versus plug-in hyperparameters; improper variance hyperprior causing impropriety. **Requires:** BCS-01, BCS-02, BCS-03; `MSF-01` `def-parametric-statistical-model`, `def-parameter-measurable-statistical-model-as-markov-kernel`; `def-conditional-independence-given-a-sigma-algebra`, `thm-independent-random-elements-have-product-joint-law`, `thm-existence-of-regular-conditional-distributions-for-standard-borel-targets`, `thm-conditional-integration-through-a-regular-conditional-law`. **Sources:** Gelman et al., *Bayesian Data Analysis*, §§5.1–5.4, 5.7; Johnson, Ott & Dogucu, *Bayes Rules!*, Chs. 15–16. Improper scale-prior examples are warnings, not generic prior recommendations.

### BCS-06 — Marginal likelihoods, Bayes factors, and empirical Bayes
**Pages:** marginal-likelihoods-bayes-factors-and-empirical-bayes / marginal-likelihoods-bayes-factors-and-empirical-bayes-examples. **Scope:** For each model with proper parameter prior define evidence $m_i(y)=\int f_i(y\mid\theta_i)\Pi_i(d\theta_i)$ using a jointly measurable likelihood kernel and a common data dominating measure. For finite model space and positive prior model weights $w_i$ summing to one, when every evidence is finite positive prove $P(M_i\mid y)=w_im_i(y)/\sum_jw_jm_j(y)$ and posterior odds equal prior odds times $BF_{21}=m_2/m_1$. A point null needs positive prior mass/explicit model atom; a purely continuous prior assigns it zero. Arbitrarily scaled improper priors do not define generic evidence or Bayes factors. Define Type-II maximum marginal likelihood $\hat\eta(y)\in\arg\max_\eta m(y\mid\eta)$ only when that maximizer exists, and define the plug-in posterior $\Pi(d\theta\mid y,\hat\eta(y))$; contrast with full Bayes retaining hyperparameter uncertainty/cross-group dependence. State no generic EB risk, consistency, dominance or credible-coverage guarantee. For the balanced one-way normal random-effects model $Y_{ij}=\mu+a_i+\epsilon_{ij}$, $i=1,\ldots,k$, $j=1,\ldots,r$, with $k,r\ge2$, independent $a_i\sim N(0,\tau^2)$ and $\epsilon_{ij}\sim N(0,\sigma^2)$, define $MSB=\frac{r}{k-1}\sum_i(\bar Y_{i\cdot}-\bar Y_{\cdot\cdot})^2$ and $MSW=\frac1{k(r-1)}\sum_{i,j}(Y_{ij}-\bar Y_{i\cdot})^2$. Then $E(MSB)=\sigma^2+r\tau^2$, $E(MSW)=\sigma^2$, so the method-of-moments estimate is $(MSB-MSW)/r$; it may be negative, and truncating at zero changes the method and does not prove a structural zero. **Proof route:** Integrate under proper within-model prior and apply finite Bayes to model indicator; derive the ANOVA expectations under the stated normal model; use BCS-05 hierarchical posterior for full Bayes and distinguish plug-in. Normalize every evidence first. **Examples:** point-null model indicator; diffuse-prior sensitivity; improper-prior arbitrary constant; negative ANOVA moment estimate. **Requires:** BCS-02, BCS-05; `MSF-01` `def-statistical-experiment`; `MSF-04` `def-dominated-statistical-experiment`, `def-likelihood-density`; `thm-bayes-formula-for-dominated-kernels`; `thm-finite-bayes-theorem`. **Sources:** Gelman et al., *Bayesian Data Analysis*, §7.4, §§5.1, 5.3–5.4; Johnson, Ott & Dogucu, *Bayes Rules!*, Ch. 8 §§8.2.1–8.2.2. No numerical evidence algorithm is claimed.

### BCS-07 — Hellinger geometry and posterior consistency
**Pages:** posterior-consistency-and-tests / posterior-consistency-and-tests-examples. **Scope:** For densities against a common dominating measure define root-L2 Hellinger distance $h^2(P,Q)=\int(\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2d\mu$, prove invariance under change of common dominating measure, and prove $\|P-Q\|_{TV}\le h(P,Q)$ and $h(P,Q)^2\le2\|P-Q\|_{TV}$ for the library's $\sup_A|P(A)-Q(A)$ convention. For a well-specified dominated IID model with truth $P_0$, state the Schwartz consistency result on a metric-separated complement: every KL neighborhood $\{P:KL(P_0\|P)<\epsilon\}$ has positive prior mass and, for each $\eta>0$, tests of $P_0^n$ against $\{P:d(P,P_0)>\eta\}$ have summable type-I and uniform type-II errors (exponential errors are a sufficient contract); then posterior mass outside that neighborhood tends to zero almost surely. State any in-probability variant with its own test/denominator rates instead of conflating the modes. **Proof route:** Hellinger factorization and Cauchy–Schwarz; lower-bound the posterior denominator over the KL set, split the tested numerator, then use Fubini and Borel–Cantelli under the stated summability. **Examples:** identifiable finite model; identifiability without testing/support; translate an L1-distance claim by its factor of two. **Requires:** BCS-01, BCS-02; `MSF-04` `def-dominated-statistical-experiment`, `def-likelihood-density`; `MSF-21` `def-kullback-leibler-divergence`, `thm-pinsker`; `def-total-variation-distance-for-probability-laws`, `thm-bayes-formula-for-dominated-kernels`, `thm-kolmogorov-iid-l1-strong-law`. **Sources:** Ghosal–Ghosh–van der Vaart (2000), §§1–2, 7–8, Theorems 2.1, 2.4, 7.1 and 7.3 and their denominator/test proofs; Kleijn–van der Vaart (2012), §4, Lemma 4.1 and Corollary 4.2. ### BCS-55 — Multilevel Monte Carlo and cost theorem
**Pages:** multilevel-monte-carlo / multilevel-monte-carlo-examples. **Scope:** Define geometrically refined levels $h_l=M^{-l}T$, coupled corrections and telescoping expectation. This direct finite-$L^2$ proof is a separate local route and is not inferred from Higham–Mao–Stuart. Their source theorem supports the same rate under its exact autonomous contract: $C^1$ drift/diffusion, one-sided Lipschitz drift, globally Lipschitz diffusion, polynomial local-Lipschitz drift, and uniform moments of every order for the exact solution, step process, and continuous interpolation; the globally Lipschitz/linear-growth specialization obtains these moments from an all-moment initial condition. For weak order one, require the separate P-NUM-SDE-02 bounded-test/backward-Kolmogorov contract: $u(t,x)=E[\varphi(X_T^{t,x})]$ solves the backward equation, lies in $C^{2,4}$ with time derivatives through order two and space derivatives through order four bounded by $C(1+\|x\|^q)$, and exact/Euler processes have uniform moments of order $p\ge2q+8$. The local one-step expected Taylor remainder is $O(h^2)(1+\|x\|^{q+4})$; telescoping gives bias $O(h_L)$, so $\alpha=1$. When this payoff is also Lipschitz, $\beta=1$ remains and the same logarithmic complexity follows. Lipschitz payoff alone does not give weak order one. **Proof route:** Prove the abstract theorem by telescoping, variance allocation $N_l\propto\sqrt{V_l/C_l}$, bias/variance budgeting and geometric sums. For the finite-$L^2$ Euler route, prove the strong interpolation estimate locally from the P-NUM-SDE-01 assumptions with Itô isometry, BDG and Grönwall, then use shared increments for the coupled correction bound. Separately, reproduce or explicitly import H-M-S under its exact autonomous all-moment hypotheses; do not transfer those source hypotheses to the finite-$L^2$ route without proof. For weak order, prove the one-step backward-Kolmogorov cancellation and Gaussian Taylor remainder $O(h^2)(1+\|x\|^{q+4})$ under the stated $C^{2,4}$ growth and $p\ge2q+8$ moment hypotheses; deriving that solution regularity from coefficient/payoff assumptions is a separate obligation. Neither Euler rate is part of the abstract MLMC proof, and adaptive bias stopping remains heuristic. **Examples:** the conditional Lipschitz-payoff Euler case $(\alpha,\beta)=(1/2,1)$; the smoother weak-order-one Euler case $(1,1)$; a discontinuous payoff with $\beta=1/2$ and $\alpha=1$; failure when either rate is absent. **Requires:** BCS-25, BCS-54 and standard IID/moment suppliers for the abstract theorem; P-NUM-SDE-01 — Euler–Maruyama scheme and coefficient/moment setup, and P-NUM-SDE-02 — strong/weak rate contracts, only for the conditional Euler cases. These are independent pre-category designs, not completed library proofs; P-NUM-SDE-02 inherits P-SDE-01's strong-solution contract. No Category 4 supplier is used. **Sources:** Giles (2008), “Multilevel Monte Carlo Path Simulation,” Theorem 3.1 and full proof, *Operations Research* 56(3), 607–617, PDF pp. 1–11. ### BCS-57 — Randomized quasi-Monte Carlo and replicate error
**Pages:** randomized-quasi-monte-carlo-and-replicates / randomized-quasi-monte-carlo-and-replicates-examples. **Scope:** Define an RQMC set $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ on $[0,1]^d$ by marginal uniformity $X_i\sim U([0,1]^d)$ (the points need not be independent) and a separate low-discrepancy property. For integrable $f$, the single-rule estimator $\hat I=n^{-1}\sum_i f(X_i)$ is unbiased. For $R\ge2$ independent full-set randomizations with estimates $\hat I_1,\ldots,\hat I_R$, the replicate variance estimator is $[R(R-1)]^{-1}\sum_{r=1}^R(\hat I_r-\bar I_R)^2$; it estimates randomization variance, and a Student interval requires additional replicate-distribution assumptions. Keep point count $n$ separate from randomization count $R$. For a nested uniform scramble of a $(0,m,d)$-net in base $b\ge\max(d,2)$, and $f\in L^2([0,1]^d)$ with variance $\sigma^2$, state Owen's exact bound $\operatorname{Var}(\hat I)\le (b/(b-1))^{\min(m,d-1)}\sigma^2/n\le e\sigma^2/n$. For a nested uniform scramble of a $(\lambda,t,m,d)$-net with $n=\lambda b^m$, $1\le\lambda<b$, also state the source's faster bound $\operatorname{Var}(\hat I)=O(n^{-3}(\log n)^{d-1})$ only when all mixed partial derivatives using each of the $d$ distinct coordinates once are continuous on $[0,1]^d$. Do not promise a general CLT; small $R$ may give poor interval coverage. **Proof route:** Direct expectation and independent-replicate variance; the two scrambled-net bounds require the stated ANOVA/scrambling results and their local proof or exact proof suppliers. MLQMC needs a separate coupling/levelwise proof. **Examples:** one scramble versus independent replicates; digital shift versus nested scramble; smooth/singular integrands; skewed finite-$R$ interval. **Requires:** BCS-54, BCS-56; `def-moments-variance-and-covariance`, `thm-lindeberg-levy-iid-central-limit-theorem`, `def-weak-convergence-of-borel-probability-measures`, `thm-slutsky-for-real-random-variables`. **Sources:** Owen (2019), Chapter 17 §§17.1–17.14, printed pp. 89–137/PDF pp. 91–140, Theorems 17.3 and 17.5 (17.3's and 17.5's full variance proofs refer to external papers).
