## Statement

Let $A=A_{\bar0}\oplus A_{\bar1}$ be a unital associative superalgebra over $\mathbb C$, and let $S$ be a set of homogeneous elements of $A$. The two-sided ideal $I=(S)$ generated by $S$ is graded, meaning $I=(I\cap A_{\bar0})\oplus(I\cap A_{\bar1})$. Consequently the quotient $A/I$ inherits a $\mathbb Z/2$-grading compatible with multiplication.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A unital associative superalgebra $A=A_{\bar0}\oplus A_{\bar1}$ and a set $S$ of homogeneous elements.

[F1] [[def-associative-superalgebra-over-complex-numbers]]: multiplication is associative and sends $A_{\bar i}A_{\bar j}$ into $A_{\overline{i+j}}$; the unit belongs to $A_{\bar0}$.

[F2] [[def-left-right-and-two-sided-ideal]]: a two-sided ideal is an additive subgroup stable under left and right multiplication by every element of the ring.

[F3] [[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]] and [[lem-ideal-criterion-and-intersections]]: the generated ideal is the intersection of the nonempty family of all two-sided ideals containing $S$, and every such intersection is a two-sided ideal.

## Proof

1.1 Let $J$ consist of all finite sums $\sum_{k=1}^m a_ks_kb_k$, with $a_k,b_k\in A$ and $s_k\in S$, including the empty sum $0$. Concatenating sums and replacing $a_k$ by $-a_k$ show that $J$ is an additive subgroup; multiplying a sum on the left or right absorbs that factor into $a_k$ or $b_k$, using associativity from [F1], so [F2] makes $J$ a two-sided ideal. It contains every $s\in S$ as $1s1$. Every two-sided ideal containing $S$ contains each $a_k s_k b_k$ and every such finite sum. Thus $J$ is the smallest two-sided ideal containing $S$, and [F3] gives $I=J$. [given, F1, F2, F3, construct]

2.1 For each summand $asb$ in a finite expression defining $J$, write $a=a_{\bar0}+a_{\bar1}$ and $b=b_{\bar0}+b_{\bar1}$. Then $asb=\sum_{i,j\in\{0,1\}}a_{\bar i}s b_{\bar j}$; each term is homogeneous by [F1] and lies in $J$. Therefore every homogeneous component of every element of $J$ lies in $J$, proving $I=(I\cap A_{\bar0})\oplus(I\cap A_{\bar1})$. Every coset in $A/I$ decomposes into its even and odd component classes; if a class lies in their intersection, its two homogeneous representatives differ by an element of the graded ideal $I$, so both representatives lie in $I$ and the class is zero. The quotient grading is direct, and the product respects it because the product in $A$ does. [F1, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
