
CONSUMER topological-braid-gates-encodings-and-universality

This page develops unitary braid actions on fixed-total-charge fusion spaces, then separates projective phase, logical block error, and leakage. It verifies the three-anyon Ising matrices directly and proves the multi-anyon Majorana Clifford restriction under an explicit channel-identification interface. For the fixed Fibonacci F/R convention, it verifies the three-anyon matrices and exact joint SU(5) × SU(8) density of the two six-strand path-matrix blocks using finite algebraic certificates. Triple-anyon encodings extend these conditional gates to arbitrary finite registers with explicit operator-norm, logical-error, and leakage bounds. The physical interface is conditional on a realized fusion space and its specified exchange action. No exact leakage-free entangler or useful joint braid-length bound is asserted; common braid phases must be tracked under coherent control. The QC25 Fibonacci data carrier supplies the coherent fixed-chirality model through an explicit exact verification of all simple-input pentagon and hexagon sectors; pair-charge and triple codes are compared inside that category without identifying their projectors. The Ising data carrier supplies only the three-anyon sector; the all-m Majorana channel identification is an explicit interface hypothesis, not a consequence claimed from that carrier.

CONSUMER def-qc-tqc-fixed-charge-encoding

## Definition

Let $V=V(x^n\to c)\ne0$ be the finite-dimensional fixed-total-charge fusion
space for $n$ identical charges $x$, equipped with the unitary fusion-tree
inner product from [[def-qc-tqc-fusion-tree-basis]]. Let
$\mathcal C=\mathbb C^k$ with its ordered orthonormal computational basis,
where $k\ge1$. A **fixed-charge encoding** is an isometry
$J:\mathcal C\to V$, so $J^*J=I_{\mathcal C}$. Its code subspace is
$J\mathcal C$, and its code projector is
$$P:=JJ^*:V\to V.$$
An encoding records a chosen subspace of a fusion space; it does not identify
that subspace with an object of the fusion category.

Fix an ordered list of simple charges $a_1,\ldots,a_n$, a simple total charge

incoming charges $x,y$ and outgoing charge $z$, choose one vector from an

CONSUMER def-qc-tqc-braid-action-from-fusion-data

## Definition

Let $x$ be a simple charge in a unitary braided fusion category, let $n\ge2$,
and fix a total charge $c$ with nonzero multiplicity space
$V(x^n\to c)$. Use the left-associated fusion-tree basis. For the Artin
generator $\sigma_i$ that exchanges the $i$th and $(i+1)$st strands, let
$T_i$ be the specified sequence of $F$-moves that changes from the fixed tree
basis to a basis where those two charges fuse first, and let $R_i$ apply the
corresponding $R$-symbol on each pair-charge channel. Define
$$\rho_c(\sigma_i):=T_i^{-1}R_iT_i.$$
This defines the generator assignment. The next theorem proves that the
assignment descends from words to a well-defined group homomorphism. Word
products use matrix multiplication acting right-to-left on column vectors.
The abstract group in this definition is the Artin group
[[def-braid-group-by-the-artin-presentation]]; the positive crossing is the
generator convention used for the displayed $R$-symbols. If a motion is
described chronologically, its matrix product is written in reverse temporal
order.

SUPPLIER def-qc-tqc-unitary-fusion-category

## Definition

Let $\mathcal C$ be a fusion category over $\mathbb C$ in the sense of
[[def-fusion-and-multifusion-category]]. A **dagger structure** assigns to every
morphism $f:X\to Y$ a morphism $f^\dagger:Y\to X$, conjugate-linearly, with
$(g\circ f)^\dagger=f^\dagger\circ g^\dagger$ and
$(f^\dagger)^\dagger=f$. A **unitary fusion category** is such a fusion
category equipped with this dagger so that every morphism space is a
finite-dimensional normed space, composition is submultiplicative,
$\|f^\dagger\|=\|f\|$, and $\|f^\dagger f\|=\|f\|^2$. Thus each
$\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal C}(X)$ is a finite-dimensional $C^*$-algebra.
Positive endomorphisms are the elements of its usual $C^*$-positive cone
(equivalently $u^\dagger u$ for an endomorphism $u$); in particular, if $c$ is
simple and $\operatorname{End}(c)=\mathbb C\operatorname{id}_c$, then
$\lambda\operatorname{id}_c$ is positive exactly when $\lambda\ge0$. For any
morphism $f:X\to Y$, $f^\dagger f$ is positive in $\operatorname{End}(X)$;
also require $f^\dagger f=0$ to imply $f=0$. Semisimple direct-sum
decompositions can be chosen orthogonally:
their inclusions $i_j$ satisfy $i_j^\dagger i_k=0$ for $j\ne k$,
$i_j^\dagger i_j=\operatorname{id}$, and $\sum_j i_j i_j^\dagger=\operatorname{id}$.
Tensor product preserves daggers:
$(f\otimes g)^\dagger=f^\dagger\otimes g^\dagger$. Its associator and unit
constraints are **unitary**, meaning $\alpha^\dagger=\alpha^{-1}$,
$\lambda^\dagger=\lambda^{-1}$, and $\rho^\dagger=\rho^{-1}$.

The underlying fusion category is rigid, so its evaluation and coevaluation
morphisms satisfy the duality zig-zag identities. Those morphisms are not
required to be unitary isomorphisms. A braiding is additional structure; when
it is specified as a **unitary braiding**, each component satisfies
$c_{X,Y}^\dagger=c_{X,Y}^{-1}$. Positivity and unitarity are extra structure,
not consequences of the fusion rules or of the braid relations alone.

SUPPLIER def-qc-tqc-f-symbol

## Definition

Let $a,b,c,d$ be simple charges in a unitary fusion category, and put
$P_L=((a\otimes b)\otimes c)$ and $P_R=a\otimes(b\otimes c)$. The local
fusion-tree basis result [[lem-qc-tqc-fusion-tree-dimension]] identifies

$$\mathcal H_L:=\bigoplus_e V_{ab}^e\otimes V_{ec}^d \quad\text{with}\quad\operatorname{Hom}(P_L,d),$$

and

$$\mathcal H_R:=\bigoplus_f V_{bc}^f\otimes V_{af}^d \quad\text{with}\quad\operatorname{Hom}(P_R,d),$$

by the corresponding tree compositions. These direct sums carry the
orthonormal left- and right-tree bases. If $\mu:a\otimes b\to e$ and
$\nu:e\otimes c\to d$ are vertex basis maps, write
$L_{e,\mu,\nu}:=\nu\circ(\mu\otimes\operatorname{id}_c):P_L\to d$. If
$\rho:b\otimes c\to f$ and $\sigma:a\otimes f\to d$ are vertex basis maps,
write $R_{f,\rho,\sigma}:=\sigma\circ(\operatorname{id}_a\otimes\rho):P_R\to d$.
Let $\alpha_{a,b,c}:P_L\to P_R$ be the associator. Define the **F-symbol
matrix** $F_d^{abc}$ by

$$L_{e,\mu,\nu}\circ\alpha_{a,b,c}^{-1} =\sum_{f,\rho,\sigma}F_d^{abc}[(f,\rho,\sigma),(e,\mu,\nu)]R_{f,\rho,\sigma}.$$

Rows index target-tree labels and multiplicity indices; columns index
source-tree labels and multiplicity indices. The sums retain every fusion
channel and every multiplicity coordinate.
If the fixed-total fusion space is zero-dimensional, the F-symbol is the unique
empty $0\times0$ matrix.

SUPPLIER def-qc-tqc-r-symbol

## Definition

Let $a,b,c$ be simple objects in a unitary braided fusion category, with
braiding $c_{a,b}:a\otimes b\to b\otimes a$ as in [[def-braiding]]. The typed
channel-exchange map on $V_{ab}^c=\operatorname{Hom}(a\otimes b,c)$ is the
pullback

$$\mathcal R_{ab}^c(f):=f\circ c_{a,b}^{-1}\in V_{ba}^c.$$

Choose orthonormal bases in the two multiplicity spaces. The matrix of
$\mathcal R_{ab}^c$ in these bases is the **R-symbol** $R_{ab}^c$. This
pullback convention fixes the orientation of the coordinate exchange; reversing
the braiding orientation replaces it by the inverse convention. If the spaces
are zero-dimensional, the braiding isomorphism identifies both with $0$ and
the R-symbol is the unique empty $0\times0$ matrix.

CONSUMER def-qc-tqc-ising-majorana-interface

## Definition

Let $m\ge2$ and let $\mathcal H=(\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes m}$. A **Majorana
system of length $2m$** is a family of operators
$\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_{2m}\in\operatorname{End}(\mathcal H)$ satisfying
$$\gamma_j^*=\gamma_j,\qquad \gamma_j^2=I,\qquad \gamma_j\gamma_k=-\gamma_k\gamma_j\quad(j\ne k).$$
Its parity operator is $\Pi=i^m\gamma_1\cdots\gamma_{2m}$. For each adjacent
pair, put
$$Q_1(j)=\frac{I-i\gamma_j\gamma_{j+1}}2,\qquad Q_\psi(j)=\frac{I+i\gamma_j\gamma_{j+1}}2,\qquad U_j=\exp\!\left(-\frac\pi4\gamma_j\gamma_{j+1}\right).$$

A **Majorana–Ising exchange interface** on a fixed parity sector consists of
such a system, a choice $\eta\in\{1,-1\}$ and the sector
$\mathcal H_\eta=\ker(\Pi-\eta I)$, together with the following explicit
model-identification hypotheses for every $1\le j<2m$: the Ising pair-charge
projections are identified with $Q_1(j),Q_\psi(j)$, and the exchange is
$R_1Q_1(j)+R_\psi Q_\psi(j)$ with the same relative phase
$R_\psi/R_1=i$. The last identity is required for all adjacent pairs as part
of the interface; the three-anyon supplier below establishes it only in its
stated sector and does not prove this all-$m$ identification.

CONSUMER def-qc-tqc-six-anyon-encoded-subspace

## Definition

Assume the Fibonacci fusion rules
$1\otimes x=x\otimes1=x$ and $\tau\otimes\tau=1\oplus\tau$, with each
listed summand of multiplicity one. For six ordered $\tau$ charges, let
$W_a$ be the fixed-input, fixed-total fusion space of total charge
$a\in\{1,\tau\}$ in the tree that first fuses leaves $1,2,3$ and $4,5,6$
separately, then fuses those triple outputs. Let
$V_a$ be the same fixed-charge space in the left-associated tree.

To identify these spaces, write
$A=((\tau_1\otimes\tau_2)\otimes\tau_3)$. Use the object associator
composite
$$g=(\alpha^{-1}_{A\otimes\tau_4,\tau_5,\tau_6}) \circ\alpha^{-1}_{A,\tau_4,\tau_5\otimes\tau_6} \circ(\operatorname{id}_A\otimes\alpha_{\tau_4,\tau_5,\tau_6})$$
from the triple-grouped input tree to the left-associated tree. On a fusion
morphism $f$ into $a$, define $G_a(f)=f\circ g^{-1}:V_a$; this is a unitary
change of fusion-tree coordinates.

For one triple of total charge $\tau$, its two orthonormal path vectors are
indexed by the charge $x\in\{1,\tau\}$ after the first two anyons: both
$x\otimes\tau\to\tau$ channels have multiplicity one. Write these vectors as
$|x\rangle$. For $x,y\in\{1,\tau\}$ and $a\in\{1,\tau\}$, let $|x,y;a\rangle$
be the grouped-tree vector with first triple path $x$, first triple total
$\tau$, second triple path $y$, second triple total $\tau$, and final vertex
channel $\tau\otimes\tau\to a$. The last channel has multiplicity one. Define
$$\mathcal C_a:=G_a\bigl(\operatorname{span}\{|x,y;a\rangle: x,y\in\{1,\tau\}\}\bigr)\subseteq V_a.$$
In the ordered basis
$(G_a|1,1;a\rangle,G_a|1,\tau;a\rangle,
G_a|\tau,1;a\rangle,G_a|\tau,\tau;a\rangle)$, the inclusion
$J_a:\mathbb C^4\to V_a$ is an isometry; its code projector is $P_a=J_aJ_a^*$.
The same ordered local triple paths are used for $a=1$ and $a=\tau$; only
the final pair-charge coordinate differs. The orthogonal complements are
called leakage spaces.

SUPPLIER lem-qc-tqc-fusion-tree-dimension

## Statement

Let $a_1,\ldots,a_n$ be simple charges, let $d$ be a simple total charge, and
fix any parenthesized binary tree $T$ on the ordered input list. The fusion-tree
vectors of [[def-qc-tqc-fusion-tree-basis]] form an orthonormal basis of
$\operatorname{Hom}(T,d)$. Their number is

$$m_T(d)=\sum_{\ell\,\mathrm{admissible}}\;\prod_{v\,\mathrm{internal}}N_{\ell(v_L)\,\ell(v_R)}^{\ell(v)},$$

where the leaves carry the inputs, the root carries $d$, and each internal edge
is labeled by a simple charge. For the left-associated tree on $n$ inputs this
gives $m_0(d)=1$ if $d=\mathbf1$ and $0$ otherwise,
$m_1(d)=1$ if $a_1\cong d$ and $0$ otherwise, and

$$m_n(d)=\sum_e m_{n-1}(e)N_{e a_n}^d\qquad(n\ge2).$$

USE
[F2] The fusion-tree lemma proves that these path vectors form a complete
orthonormal basis and counts them by the products of the vertex
multiplicities. [[lem-qc-tqc-fusion-tree-dimension]]

1.1 The fusion rule gives $\dim V(\tau^2\to1)=\dim V(\tau^2\to\tau)=1$ and therefore $\dim V(\tau^3\to1)=1$, $\dim V(\tau^3\to\tau)=2$. Applying the fusion-tree count [F2] to the grouped tree, total charge $1$ receives one basis vector from the $(1,1)$ triple-output pair and four from $(\tau,\tau)$, so $\dim W_1=1+2\cdot2=5$. Total charge $\tau$ receives two vectors from $(1,\tau)$, two from $(\tau,1)$, and four from $(\tau,\tau)$, so $\dim W_\tau=2+2+4=8$. The map $G_a$ in [F4] is unitary, hence $\dim V_1=5$ and $\dim V_\tau=8$. [A1, F2, F4, algebra]

1.2 Each selected triple-total-$\tau$ factor has its two paths $|1\rangle$ and $|\tau\rangle$, and the final $\tau\otimes\tau\to a$ vertex has multiplicity one. Thus the four grouped-tree vectors $|x,y;a\rangle$ are admissible tree vectors by [F1] and form an orthonormal family by [F2]. Applying the unitary $G_a$ preserves orthonormality, so they define an isometric inclusion $J_a$; [F3] gives the projector $P_a=J_aJ_a^*$ and $\dim\mathcal C_a=4$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, given, algebra]

morphism $f:X\to Y$ a morphism $f^\dagger:Y\to X$, conjugate-linearly, with

SUPPLIER ex-qc-tqc-fibonacci-data

## Example

Take the skeletal complex-linear category whose simple labels are
$\mathbf1,\tau$ and whose objects are finite direct sums of them, including
the empty direct sum $0$. For objects
$A=\bigoplus_s s^{\oplus m_s}$ and $B=\bigoplus_s s^{\oplus n_s}$, set
$\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)=\bigoplus_s\operatorname{Mat}_{n_s\times m_s}(\mathbb C)$;
composition is block-matrix multiplication, dagger is conjugate transpose,
and the norm is the maximum of the operator norms of its blocks. Thus direct
sums are orthogonal. Define tensor product on simple labels by the fusion rules
$\mathbf1\otimes a=a\otimes\mathbf1=a$ and $\tau\otimes\tau=\mathbf1\oplus\tau$,
and extend it distributively to direct sums. For each allowed simple fusion
channel, use the standard unit-vector inclusion as the orthonormal basis of
its one-dimensional multiplicity space. On simple-label blocks, tensor a pair
of morphism matrices by their Kronecker product on multiplicity coordinates
and by the identity on each simple fusion channel; extend over direct sums.
This defines a bifunctor compatible with dagger. Use the strict unit gauge:
every F or R coordinate involving $\mathbf1$ is the identity. Put

$$\phi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2},\qquad u=\phi^{-1},\qquad v=\phi^{-1/2}.$$

The only nontrivial associator blocks are

$$F^{\tau\tau\tau}_{\mathbf1}=[1],\qquad F^{\tau\tau\tau}_{\tau}=\begin{pmatrix}u&v\\v&-u\end{pmatrix},$$

in the intermediate-channel order $(\mathbf1,\tau)$. In the pullback R
convention of [[def-qc-tqc-r-symbol]], the exchange blocks are

$$R^{\tau\tau}_{\mathbf1}=x=e^{-4\pi i/5},\qquad R^{\tau\tau}_{\tau}=y=e^{3\pi i/5}.$$

Because these are pullback symbols, define the object-level braiding
$c_{\tau,\tau}$ on the $\mathbf1$ and $\tau$ summands by $x^{-1}$ and
$y^{-1}$, respectively. Every braiding with a unit input is the strict
identity. These fixed chiral data define the Fibonacci unitary braided fusion
category in this gauge; the verification checks every nontrivial pentagon and
hexagon sector.

## Statement

SUPPLIER def-qc-tqc-unitary-fusion-category

## Definition

Let $\mathcal C$ be a fusion category over $\mathbb C$ in the sense of
[[def-fusion-and-multifusion-category]]. A **dagger structure** assigns to every
morphism $f:X\to Y$ a morphism $f^\dagger:Y\to X$, conjugate-linearly, with
$(g\circ f)^\dagger=f^\dagger\circ g^\dagger$ and
$(f^\dagger)^\dagger=f$. A **unitary fusion category** is such a fusion
category equipped with this dagger so that every morphism space is a
finite-dimensional normed space, composition is submultiplicative,
$\|f^\dagger\|=\|f\|$, and $\|f^\dagger f\|=\|f\|^2$. Thus each
$\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal C}(X)$ is a finite-dimensional $C^*$-algebra.
Positive endomorphisms are the elements of its usual $C^*$-positive cone
(equivalently $u^\dagger u$ for an endomorphism $u$); in particular, if $c$ is
simple and $\operatorname{End}(c)=\mathbb C\operatorname{id}_c$, then
$\lambda\operatorname{id}_c$ is positive exactly when $\lambda\ge0$. For any
morphism $f:X\to Y$, $f^\dagger f$ is positive in $\operatorname{End}(X)$;
also require $f^\dagger f=0$ to imply $f=0$. Semisimple direct-sum
decompositions can be chosen orthogonally:
their inclusions $i_j$ satisfy $i_j^\dagger i_k=0$ for $j\ne k$,
$i_j^\dagger i_j=\operatorname{id}$, and $\sum_j i_j i_j^\dagger=\operatorname{id}$.
Tensor product preserves daggers:
$(f\otimes g)^\dagger=f^\dagger\otimes g^\dagger$. Its associator and unit
constraints are **unitary**, meaning $\alpha^\dagger=\alpha^{-1}$,
$\lambda^\dagger=\lambda^{-1}$, and $\rho^\dagger=\rho^{-1}$.

The underlying fusion category is rigid, so its evaluation and coevaluation
morphisms satisfy the duality zig-zag identities. Those morphisms are not
required to be unitary isomorphisms. A braiding is additional structure; when
it is specified as a **unitary braiding**, each component satisfies
$c_{X,Y}^\dagger=c_{X,Y}^{-1}$. Positivity and unitarity are extra structure,
not consequences of the fusion rules or of the braid relations alone.

spaces, unitary associators and braidings, and unitary F/R coordinates; their
pentagon and hexagon coordinate identities match the categorical coherence
maps. The fusion-tree dimension lemma supplies complete bases.

## Statement

Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$. In the normalized Fibonacci B6 representation on
$V(\tau^6\to1)\cong\mathbb C^5$, the closure of the braid image in $U(5)$
contains $SU(5)$.

SUPPLIER ex-qc-tqc-fibonacci-data

## Example

Take the skeletal complex-linear category whose simple labels are
$\mathbf1,\tau$ and whose objects are finite direct sums of them, including
the empty direct sum $0$. For objects
$A=\bigoplus_s s^{\oplus m_s}$ and $B=\bigoplus_s s^{\oplus n_s}$, set
$\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)=\bigoplus_s\operatorname{Mat}_{n_s\times m_s}(\mathbb C)$;
composition is block-matrix multiplication, dagger is conjugate transpose,
and the norm is the maximum of the operator norms of its blocks. Thus direct
sums are orthogonal. Define tensor product on simple labels by the fusion rules
$\mathbf1\otimes a=a\otimes\mathbf1=a$ and $\tau\otimes\tau=\mathbf1\oplus\tau$,
and extend it distributively to direct sums. For each allowed simple fusion
channel, use the standard unit-vector inclusion as the orthonormal basis of
its one-dimensional multiplicity space. On simple-label blocks, tensor a pair
of morphism matrices by their Kronecker product on multiplicity coordinates
and by the identity on each simple fusion channel; extend over direct sums.
This defines a bifunctor compatible with dagger. Use the strict unit gauge:
every F or R coordinate involving $\mathbf1$ is the identity. Put

$$\phi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2},\qquad u=\phi^{-1},\qquad v=\phi^{-1/2}.$$

The only nontrivial associator blocks are

$$F^{\tau\tau\tau}_{\mathbf1}=[1],\qquad F^{\tau\tau\tau}_{\tau}=\begin{pmatrix}u&v\\v&-u\end{pmatrix},$$

in the intermediate-channel order $(\mathbf1,\tau)$. In the pullback R
convention of [[def-qc-tqc-r-symbol]], the exchange blocks are

$$R^{\tau\tau}_{\mathbf1}=x=e^{-4\pi i/5},\qquad R^{\tau\tau}_{\tau}=y=e^{3\pi i/5}.$$

Because these are pullback symbols, define the object-level braiding
$c_{\tau,\tau}$ on the $\mathbf1$ and $\tau$ summands by $x^{-1}$ and
$y^{-1}$, respectively. Every braiding with a unit input is the strict
identity. These fixed chiral data define the Fibonacci unitary braided fusion
category in this gauge; the verification checks every nontrivial pentagon and
hexagon sector.

[F8] The current Fibonacci data carrier states $\tau\otimes\tau=1\oplus\tau$
and fixes $R_\tau/R_1=e^{-3\pi i/5}$ and the displayed unitary $F$ block in
the stated gauge. [[ex-qc-tqc-fibonacci-data]]

1.1 Write $u=\phi^{-1}$ and $r=R_\tau/R_1=e^{-3\pi i/5}$ by [F8]. ## Statement

charges and total charge determines a fusion space in each parenthesization.

of invertible linear maps preserving the inner product on $V$.

unitary equivalence and consistently transformed expectation values do not.

SUPPLIER def-qc-tqc-fusion-tree-basis

## Definition

Fix an ordered list of simple charges $a_1,\ldots,a_n$, a simple total charge
$d$, and a parenthesized binary fusion tree. At each internal vertex with
incoming charges $x,y$ and outgoing charge $z$, choose one vector from an
orthonormal basis of $V_{xy}^z$. An **admissible labeling** assigns a simple
charge to each internal edge so that each chosen vertex basis is nonempty; a
separate multiplicity-basis index is retained at every vertex. Compose the
vertex morphisms, inserting the associators required by the chosen tree, to
obtain a morphism from the parenthesized input tensor to $d$. These composite
morphisms are the **fusion-tree vectors**. The family is called a
**fusion-tree basis** when it is an orthonormal basis of the fixed-input,
fixed-total fusion space. For the empty input list the tensor is $\mathbf1$
and its empty tree vector is $\operatorname{id}_{\mathbf1}$ when $d=\mathbf1$;
for one input $a_1$, the only tree vector is $\operatorname{id}_{a_1}$ when
$d=a_1$. The next local lemma proves the basis property for every finite input
list and includes the dimension recursion ([[lem-qc-tqc-fusion-tree-dimension]]).

gives $m_0(d)=1$ if $d=\mathbf1$ and $0$ otherwise,
$m_1(d)=1$ if $a_1\cong d$ and $0$ otherwise, and

$$m_n(d)=\sum_e m_{n-1}(e)N_{e a_n}^d\qquad(n\ge2).$$

SUPPLIER def-qc-tqc-unitary-fusion-category

## Definition

Let $\mathcal C$ be a fusion category over $\mathbb C$ in the sense of
[[def-fusion-and-multifusion-category]]. A **dagger structure** assigns to every
morphism $f:X\to Y$ a morphism $f^\dagger:Y\to X$, conjugate-linearly, with
$(g\circ f)^\dagger=f^\dagger\circ g^\dagger$ and
$(f^\dagger)^\dagger=f$. A **unitary fusion category** is such a fusion
category equipped with this dagger so that every morphism space is a
finite-dimensional normed space, composition is submultiplicative,
$\|f^\dagger\|=\|f\|$, and $\|f^\dagger f\|=\|f\|^2$. Thus each
$\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal C}(X)$ is a finite-dimensional $C^*$-algebra.
Positive endomorphisms are the elements of its usual $C^*$-positive cone
(equivalently $u^\dagger u$ for an endomorphism $u$); in particular, if $c$ is
simple and $\operatorname{End}(c)=\mathbb C\operatorname{id}_c$, then
$\lambda\operatorname{id}_c$ is positive exactly when $\lambda\ge0$. For any
morphism $f:X\to Y$, $f^\dagger f$ is positive in $\operatorname{End}(X)$;
also require $f^\dagger f=0$ to imply $f=0$. Semisimple direct-sum
decompositions can be chosen orthogonally:
their inclusions $i_j$ satisfy $i_j^\dagger i_k=0$ for $j\ne k$,
$i_j^\dagger i_j=\operatorname{id}$, and $\sum_j i_j i_j^\dagger=\operatorname{id}$.
Tensor product preserves daggers:
$(f\otimes g)^\dagger=f^\dagger\otimes g^\dagger$. Its associator and unit
constraints are **unitary**, meaning $\alpha^\dagger=\alpha^{-1}$,
$\lambda^\dagger=\lambda^{-1}$, and $\rho^\dagger=\rho^{-1}$.

The underlying fusion category is rigid, so its evaluation and coevaluation
morphisms satisfy the duality zig-zag identities. Those morphisms are not
required to be unitary isomorphisms. A braiding is additional structure; when
it is specified as a **unitary braiding**, each component satisfies
$c_{X,Y}^\dagger=c_{X,Y}^{-1}$. Positivity and unitarity are extra structure,
not consequences of the fusion rules or of the braid relations alone.

## Statement

Let $\mathcal C$ be a unitary braided fusion category. A finite list of simple
charges and total charge determines a fusion space in each parenthesization.
For a crossing word with underlying object map $b_\beta:P_L\to P_{L'}$, define
its action on the corresponding Hom spaces by
$f\mapsto f\circ b_\beta^{-1}$, from $\operatorname{Hom}(P_L,d)$ to
$\operatorname{Hom}(P_{L'},d)$. Let $U_\beta$ be its matrix in chosen
fusion-tree bases. If each multiplicity basis is changed by the unitary
gauge matrices of [[def-qc-tqc-gauge-transform]], and $G_L,G_{L'}$ are the
induced source and target tree-basis unitaries, then

$$U_\beta'=G_{L'}^{-1}U_\beta G_L.$$

In particular, when $n\ge2$ and all $n$ strand charges are the same simple object $a$,
the adjacent exchanges act on the same fixed-total fusion space
$V=\operatorname{Hom}(a^{\otimes n},d)$, satisfy the Artin relations, and
define a unitary representation $\rho:B_n\to U(V)$, where $U(V)$ is the group
of invertible linear maps preserving the inner product on $V$.
If $V=0$, this is the unique action on the zero-dimensional space.
In that case $\rho'(\beta)=G_L^{-1}\rho(\beta)G_L$ for every braid $\beta$.
Thus basis-dependent matrix entries change, while the braid action up to
unitary equivalence and consistently transformed expectation values do not.
