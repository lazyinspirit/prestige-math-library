CONSUMER lem-relative-one-parameter-morse-path-with-finite-events

## Statement

Assume full AC. Let $W$ be a compact smooth $n$-manifold, $n\ge1$, with fixed product collars, and let $f_0,f_1:W\to[0,1]$ be excellent adapted Morse functions equal to the same noncritical product height on those collars. There is a smooth path $F_s$ joining them, stationary near its parameter endpoints and fixed on smaller boundary collars, with finitely many exceptional parameters. Each exceptional parameter contains exactly one of: a corank-one interior birth/death with nonzero kernel cubic and nonzero parameter unfolding, or a transverse crossing of two nondegenerate critical values. All other critical points are nondegenerate, all other critical values are distinct, no two events share a parameter, and a birth value differs from every other critical value. The critical locus is a compact smooth curve whose parameter projection has only nondegenerate folds. Each fold creates or destroys two critical points of adjacent indices with distinct critical values. By a homotopy supported near each fold, preserving this event list and the endpoints, the path can further be put near that fold in smooth parameter-dependent spatial coordinates into the form

$$F_s=a(s)+b(s)u+c_0u^3+Q(y),\qquad b(0)=0,\quad b'(0)\ne0,\quad c_0>0,$$

where $Q$ is a fixed nondegenerate quadratic form in $n-1$ variables. Empty ends, disconnected $W$, and index $0/n$ pairs are allowed. This is a function-path statement with fixed product height; it has no boundary critical events and asserts no global descending-field or surgery-move theorem.

SUPPLIER thm-parametric-transversality

## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ ([[def-countable-choice]]). Let $\mathcal F:M\times S\to N$ be a smooth family of maps and let $Z\subseteq N$
be an embedded submanifold. If $\mathcal F\pitchfork Z$, then the set of
parameters $s\in S$ for which the slice $\mathcal F_s:M\to N$ fails to be
transverse to $Z$ is a null subset of $S$.

USE
[F1] For a finite-dimensional smooth evaluation family transverse to an embedded submanifold, the bad slice parameters are null; finite unions of such exceptional sets are null and their complement meets every parameter ball ([[thm-parametric-transversality]], [[prop-countable-unions-and-subsets-of-manifold-null-sets-are-null]], [[prop-a-null-set-has-dense-complement-in-a-positive-dimensional-manifold]]).

3.1 **Application of the actual finite-dimensional transversality theorem.** For each row and each relevant $k$, the source is the smooth manifold $\{s:\beta(s)>0\}\times\operatorname{Conf}_k(\{x:\gamma(x)>0\}),$ of dimension $kn+1$. The target is the product spatial 3-jet bundle over that source. The evaluation map also depending on $z$ is a submersion in every vertical jet direction by step 1.1, and covers the identity on the spatial base; hence it is transverse to each displayed stratum. Apply [F1] to each of these finitely many maps/strata. The union of their exceptional parameter subsets is null, so its complement meets every parameter ball. Pick one $z$ in that complement and set $F_s=H_{s,z}$. If a target stratum has codimension greater than its configuration source dimension, its transverse inverse image is empty: at a point of the inverse image the differential would have to surject onto a normal space of strictly larger dimension. Thus all the high-codimension rows are avoided. The $B_1$ inverse image and the Morse equal-value-pair inverse image are zero-dimensional smooth submanifolds. This is an application of a supplied finite-dimensional theorem to explicit submersive families, not an application of an unproved infinite-dimensional or multijet version. At a $B_1$ point choose coordinates with Hessian kernel spanned by $u$ and invertible Hessian block in $y$. Let $b=\partial_s d_xF$ and $c=\partial_u^3F$. To see exactly what transversality says, project the linearization of the gradient onto its kernel covector: its spatial columns vanish there and its parameter column is $b(u)$. In the remaining $n-1$ gradient directions the Hessian is invertible. The differential of the corank equation in the remaining kernel spatial direction is $c$ (terms from differentiating the kernel vector vanish because $Au=0$). Therefore the determinant of the normal derivative to $B_1$ is, up to a nonzero factor, $b(u)c$. Its invertibility implies $b(u)\ne0$ and $c\ne0$. The separate avoidance of $B_{1,0}$ is compatible with, and corroborates, this calculation. At an equal-value pair with invertible Hessians, eliminate the spatial normal directions using the two Hessian blocks. The remaining derivative is the derivative of the difference of the two critical values along the parameter. Thus transversality is exactly nonzero crossing speed. The three- and four-point exclusions ensure that there is only one pair at a crossing parameter. The two-$B_1$ and $B_1$-plus-pair exclusions ensure that no birth coincides with another birth or crossing. The $B_1$-plus-equal-value exclusion ensures that a birth value is distinct from every other critical value. [given, F1, F2, F3, step 2.1]

CONSUMER lem-relative-descending-path-with-finite-same-index-passages

## Statement

Assume full AC. Let $W$ be a compact smooth $d$-dimensional collared bordism, $d\ge1$, and let $f_s$ be a path supplied by [[lem-relative-one-parameter-morse-path-with-finite-events]], with the given noncritical height fixed on the spatial collars. Prescribe descending endpoint fields, stationary near the parameter ends, standard near their Morse critical points, and having no trajectories from index $i$ to index $j\ge i$. Morse–Smale endpoint fields suffice. Then there is a smooth descending family $X_s$ with those endpoint and collar values such that:

1. Near every fold it is the negative Euclidean gradient of the exact cubic model. At every regular critical point it has a smooth hyperbolic descending model. Near each same-index passage it may be taken to have the normalized linear Morse model at the destination.
2. No ordinary trajectory increases index. The entire family has finitely many same-index connecting trajectories; each occurs at a distinct parameter away from births and critical-value crossings. Each is a transverse parameter passage: on an incoming section at its destination, the $i-1$ source-sphere tangent directions together with the parameter direction map isomorphically onto the $i$-dimensional normal space of the stable section.
3. A sufficiently small parameter neighborhood of each birth contains no same-index trajectory. Between its two newborn critical points there is exactly the standard cancelling trajectory.
4. In successive one-critical elementary bands, the full framed attaching embeddings and actual outgoing boundary maps vary smoothly on each regular function chamber, including through positive-index-drop tangencies. The existing elementary birth and critical-value-crossing comparisons retain their actual outgoing maps, and their composition retains the terminal boundary parametrization.

Disconnected $W$, empty ends, and index $0/d$ births are allowed. This is an elementary-band assertion: it neither asserts slice-wise Morse–Smale transversality at positive index drops nor supplies parameterwise grouping of arbitrary based handle presentations by index or a global Kirby completeness theorem.

They replace an anonymous infinite-dimensional residuality assertion. Fix a relatively compact piece of one of the above incidence domains and an admissible family $X$. Its zero set in the compact closure is compact. At each zero choose the finite shear directions from phase 2, and use a finite subcover of that zero set. Taking the union of these finitely many directions produces ONE finite-dimensional perturbation family whose universal incidence is transverse near that compact zero set. Starting from $X^{(0)}$, at stage $n$ use finite Sard to achieve condition $n$ and preserve conditions $1,\ldots,n-1$; choose the change smaller than half a previously reserved openness radius for each of these finitely many conditions. Also choose its $C^n$ norm below $2^{-n}$ and small enough to preserve descending margins on its compact support. Reserve positive tail budgets for all achieved conditions: after stage $n$, require the sum of all later $C^{r_n}$ changes to be smaller than half its openness radius. This can be enforced by choosing every later change below the minimum of finitely many geometric budgets. The sequence converges in every $C^r$ norm to one smooth field family. Every achieved condition survives its total tail. Protected regions stay exactly fixed. A point outside the protected critical regions lies in some compact exhaustion piece with a descending margin; its margin is protected once that piece enters the list. Equivalently one can bound each field change pointwise by a fixed small positive multiple of the original $-df(X)$ on the permitted region. Hence the limit remains strictly descending everywhere away from the critical set. The list comprises: ordinary single incidences with $i\le j$; all ordinary double same-index incidences with distinct trajectories; all nonpositive-drop incidences at each fixed critical-value-crossing parameter; and the fixed-birth incidence problems in phase 6. Parameter-endpoint problems are already satisfied by the prescribed endpoint fields and need no change. Positive-drop single incidences are not imposed, so even non-Morse–Smale endpoint fields satisfying the stated nonpositive-incidence hypothesis cause no difficulty. No claim of a single bounded polynomial parameter family for arbitrarily long trajectories is made: each compact finite-time problem has a finite family, and the displayed convergent construction handles the countable exhaustion. Only zeros satisfying this condition require the shears above; their neighborhoods can be chosen in the interior of $W$. Compactness controls all such zeros for nearby fields too. Possible ambient trajectories outside $W$ are not included and acquire no genericity claim. This resolves domain changes during the successive small perturbations. For the resulting family, dimension gives no index-increasing ordinary trajectory and locally isolated equal-index incidences. It also excludes simultaneous distinct equal-index trajectories, including pairs joined end-to-end at the same parameter. The derivative at an equal-index incidence is an isomorphism after quotienting the flow direction. On an incoming stable section this says exactly that its $i-1$ source-sphere directions and its one parameter direction span the $i$ normal unstable directions. ## Definition

Fix an integer $\ell\ge3$ and set $q=e^{\pi i/\ell}$, so the multiplicative order of $q^2$ is $\ell$. Use the divided-power integral form of [[def-drinfeld-jimbo-quantized-enveloping-algebra]] from [[def-lusztig-divided-power-integral-form]], specialized by $v\mapsto q$ to $\mathbb C$. In type $A_1$, write $K=K_h$ and $E,F$ for the rank-one generators. The convention is

$$KEK^{-1}=q^2E,\qquad KFK^{-1}=q^{-2}F,\qquad [E,F]=\frac{K-K^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}},$$

$$\Delta(K)=K\otimes K,\qquad \Delta(E)=E\otimes K+1\otimes E,\qquad \Delta(F)=F\otimes1+K^{-1}\otimes F.$$

This is the Sawin coproduct, rather than the inverse-$K$ coproduct in [[thm-the-drinfeld-jimbo-formulas-define-a-hopf-algebra]]. Its counit is $\varepsilon(K)=1$, $\varepsilon(E)=\varepsilon(F)=0$ and its antipode is $S(E)=-EK^{-1}$, $S(F)=-KF$, $S(K)=K^{-1}$. The exact integral transport is from the library algebra with opposite coproduct, by $E_{\rm target}=KE_{\rm lib}$, $F_{\rm target}=F_{\rm lib}K^{-1}$ and $K_{\rm target}=K_{\rm lib}$. Substituting these generators gives the displayed coproduct and unchanged mixed relation. On divided powers the formulas are $E_{\rm target}^{(r)}=v^{-r(r-1)}K^rE_{\rm lib}^{(r)}$ and $F_{\rm target}^{(r)}=v^{r(r-1)}F_{\rm lib}^{(r)}K^{-r}$; the inverse formulas also have Laurent-monomial coefficients, so the integral lattice and toral generators are preserved.

The Lusztig specialization includes all divided powers $E^{(r)},F^{(r)}$ and the toral quantum binomials; this is the divided-power form, not the small quantum group. A finite-dimensional module is **type 1** when it carries its integral-weight grading $M=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb Z}M_m$ with $K|_{M_m}=q^m$, the full toral character $T_{c,t}|_{M_m}=\begin{bmatrix}m+c\\t\end{bmatrix}_q I$ for every $c\in\mathbb Z$ and $t\ge0$, and $E^{(r)}M_m\subseteq M_{m+2r}$, $F^{(r)}M_m\subseteq M_{m-2r}$. The binomial is its Laurent-polynomial specialization, including negative upper indices. The grading is part of the data and is not collapsed when distinct weights have the same $K$ eigenvalue. Every module intertwiner preserves it: for $m<n$, $T_{-m,n-m}$ has eigenvalues $0$ and $1$ on weights $m$ and $n$.

For $a\ge0$, the Weyl module $\Delta(a)$ has basis $v_0,\ldots,v_a$ and actions

$$Kv_j=q^{a-2j}v_j,\qquad E^{(r)}v_j=\begin{bmatrix}a-j+r\\r\end{bmatrix}_qv_{j-r},\qquad F^{(r)}v_j=\begin{bmatrix}j+r\\r\end{bmatrix}_qv_{j+r},$$

where the Gaussian binomials are the Laurent-polynomial specializations of [[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]], and $v_j=0$ outside $0\le j\le a$. Its dual is the dual Weyl module $\nabla(a)=\Delta(a)^\vee$. A **Weyl filtration** (respectively dual-Weyl filtration) is a finite submodule filtration whose successive quotients are modules $\Delta(a)$ (respectively $\nabla(a)$). A **tilting module** is a module with both filtrations.

Take $s=e^{\pi i/(2\ell)}$, so $s^2=q$. The ribbon category is the full weight-lattice extension $\mathcal U_s^\dagger(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ of this Lusztig form, not its root-lattice restriction. Its toral factor is the completed function algebra on the weight lattice $P=\mathbb Z\omega$, acting on each weight space by its weight character. Before specialization, the rank-one universal $R$-matrix is the Sawin weight-lattice expression

$R=D\sum_{r\ge0}q^{r(r+1)/2}(1-q^{-2})^r[r]_q!E^{(r)}\otimes F^{(r)},\qquad D|_{M_m\otimes N_n}=s^{mn}.$

interpreted in the completed tensor product; at the root it means the image of the integral completed form, not termwise division by a vanishing $[r]_q!$. In ordinary powers the coefficient is $q^{r(r-1)/2}(q-q^{-1})^r/[r]_q!$; changing to two divided powers multiplies that coefficient by $([r]_q!)^2$. Thus the displayed divided-power coefficient is Laurent integral and vanishes for $r\ge\ell$ after specialization. The toral factor is the weight pairing, fixed explicitly by $D$ above. The pivotal map is $j_M(x)(\varphi)=\varphi(Kx)$ and the twist is $\theta=u^{-1}j$ for the Drinfeld map $u$. The object class of this page is the full subcategory of finite-dimensional integrable type-1 tilting modules for this weight-lattice ribbon extension. No assertion about semisimplicity or the trace radical is part of this definition.
