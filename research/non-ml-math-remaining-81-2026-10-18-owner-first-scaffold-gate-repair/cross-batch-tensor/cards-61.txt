
CONSUMER lem-temperley-lieb-critical-standard-modules-are-simple

## Statement

Let $q\in\mathbb C^\times$, $\delta=q+q^{-1}$, $n\ge0$, and $0\le p\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor$. Put $t=n-2p$, let $\kappa=\operatorname{ord}(q^2)$, and let $\phi_{n,t}$ be the link-state capping form on $W_{n,t}(\mathbb C,\delta)$. If $(n,p)$ is critical in the sense of [[def-temperley-lieb-complex-critical-labels-and-reflection]], then $\phi_{n,t}$ is nondegenerate and $W_{n,t}(\mathbb C,\delta)$ is simple.

SUPPLIER def-temperley-lieb-quantum-integers-and-quantum-characteristic

## Definition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring with unit and $\delta\in R$. Define the quantum integers by

$$[0]_\delta=0,\qquad [1]_\delta=1,\qquad [r+1]_\delta=\delta[r]_\delta-[r-1]_\delta\quad(r\ge1).$$

The **quantum characteristic** of $(R,\delta)$ is the least positive integer $\ell$ with $[\ell]_\delta=0$, if one exists, and $\ell=\infty$ otherwise. No element of $R$ is inverted in this definition.

For $q\in\mathbb C^\times$ and $\delta=q+q^{-1}$, write $[r]_q=[r]_\delta$. If $q\ne\pm1$, this agrees with $(q^r-q^{-r})/(q-q^{-1})$; at $q=\pm1$ the recurrence gives $[r]_q=rq^{r-1}$. For complex-parameter classification, write $\kappa=\operatorname{ord}(q^2)$, with $\kappa=\infty$ when $q^2$ has infinite order. If $q\ne\pm1$, the quantum characteristic equals $\kappa$ when $\kappa<\infty$ and is $\infty$ otherwise. At $q=\pm1$, $\kappa=1$ but the complex quantum characteristic is $\infty$, because $[r]_q=rq^{r-1}\ne0$ for every positive $r$.

For a field $K$ of positive characteristic $p$, set $p(0)=1$ and, when $\ell<\infty$, set $p(i)=\ell p^{i-1}$ for $i\ge1$. In characteristic zero and finite $\ell$, the finite digit scale is $p(1)=\ell$; the separate characteristic-zero criterion below uses $a\ell-1$ for arbitrary positive integers $a$. If $\ell=\infty$, every finite index is already in the range $n<\ell$.

USE
[F1] The complex quantum integers satisfy $[r]_q=(q^r-q^{-r})/(q-q^{-1})$ for $q\ne\pm1$, and $[r]_q=rq^{r-1}$ for $q=\pm1$; the recurrence quantum characteristic is distinct from $\kappa$ at $q=\pm1$ ([[def-temperley-lieb-quantum-integers-and-quantum-characteristic]]).

1.1 If $\kappa=1$, then $q=\pm1$. By [F1], every positive quantum integer is nonzero, so each factor $[t+1+j]_q/[j]_q$ in [F3] is nonzero. Therefore $\det G_{n,p}\ne0$ in this case. [F1, F3, F5, given, algebra]


CONSUMER lem-temperley-lieb-zero-loop-perfect-matching-standard-module-is-simple

## Statement

Let $p\ge1$, $q\in\{i,-i\}$, $\delta=q+q^{-1}=0$, and $n=2p$. Then the right Temperley–Lieb standard module $W_{2p,0}(\mathbb C,0)$, whose basis consists of noncrossing perfect matchings of $2p$ sites, is simple. This conclusion concerns the actual module; its ordinary link-state form is identically zero and its zero form quotient is not used.

SUPPLIER def-temperley-lieb-link-state-standard-modules

## Definition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring with unit, let $\delta\in R$, and let $n\ge0$. Let $t$ be an integer with $0\le t\le n$ and $n-t$ even. The diagram module $M_R(n,t)$ has $n$ bottom boundary points and $t$ top boundary points, with stacking and loop removal as in [[def-temperley-lieb-diagram-category]]. A component of a basis diagram is **propagating** if it meets both the bottom and top boundaries. Write $\operatorname{prop}(D)$ for the number of propagating components of a diagram $D:n\to t$.

Let $M_R^{<t}(n,t)$ be the $R$-span of the basis diagrams $D:n\to t$ with $\operatorname{prop}(D)<t$. Define the **Temperley–Lieb link-state standard module**

$$W_{n,t}(R,\delta):=M_R(n,t)/M_R^{<t}(n,t).$$

Write $T_{n,t}$ for the set of diagrams in $\mathcal D(n,t)$ with exactly $t$ propagating components. The cosets of these diagrams form an $R$-basis of $W_{n,t}$. Identifying each diagram with its bottom arcs and defects gives a partial matching of the $n$ ordered bottom sites, with the additional condition that the $t$ unpaired sites are joined to the ordered top sites by noncrossing propagating strands. In particular, no defect lies inside an arc. The unpaired sites are called **defects**. Thus $t$ is the defect or through-line number; in sources using $p$ for the number of arcs, $p=(n-t)/2$.

The intended right action of $\mathrm{TL}_n(R,\delta)$ is $x\cdot a=[x\circ a]$, where $x\in M_R(n,t)$, $a\in M_R(n,n)$, and $x\circ a$ stacks $x$ above $a$. The submodule $M_R^{<t}(n,t)$ is stable under this action, so it descends to $W_{n,t}$; the complete well-definedness argument is [[lem-temperley-lieb-link-state-module-action-is-well-defined]].

USE
[F1] The link-state basis consists of noncrossing partial matchings with $p=(n-t)/2$ arcs and $t$ defects ([[def-temperley-lieb-link-state-standard-modules]]).

SUPPLIER lem-temperley-lieb-link-state-gram-form-is-invariant

## Statement

Let $R$, $\delta$, $n$, $t$ and $W_{n,t}(R,\delta)$ be as in [[def-temperley-lieb-link-state-standard-modules]]. For link states $x,y$, let $x^*$ denote reflection across a horizontal line. The diagram $y\circ x^*:t\to t$ is either $\delta^c1_t$ for some $c\ge0$ or has fewer than $t$ propagating components. Define $\phi_{n,t}(x,y)=\delta^c$ in the first case and $0$ in the second, and extend $R$-bilinearly. Then $\phi_{n,t}$ is symmetric and satisfies

$$\phi_{n,t}(x\cdot a,y)=\phi_{n,t}(x,y\cdot a^*)$$

for all $x,y\in W_{n,t}(R,\delta)$ and $a\in\mathrm{TL}_n(R,\delta)$. Here $a^*$ is reflection across a horizontal line. The matrix of $\phi_{n,t}$ in the link-state basis is the Gram matrix obtained by reflecting and capping two link states, with one factor $\delta$ for each closed loop.

USE
[F3] The capping form on basis states is $\delta^c$ when all defect paths propagate and zero otherwise ([[lem-temperley-lieb-link-state-gram-form-is-invariant]]).


CONSUMER lem-temperley-lieb-annular-central-element-and-reflection-maps

## Statement

Work over $\mathbb C$, with $q\ne0$, $\delta=q+q^{-1}$. Write $A_n=\mathrm{TL}_n(\mathbb C,\delta)$ and $V_{n,p}=W_{n,n-2p}(\mathbb C,\delta)$. There is a central, reflection-invariant element $F_n\in A_n$ acting on $V_{n,p}$ by
$$f_{n,p}=q^{n-2p+1}+q^{-(n-2p+1)}.$$
When $q\ne\pm1$ and $(n,p)$ is noncritical, restriction splits as $V_{n,p}\downarrow=V_{n-1,p}\oplus V_{n-1,p-1}$. Except at $(n,p,\delta)=(2,1,0)$, induction has a natural exact sequence
$$0\longrightarrow V_{n+1,p+1}\longrightarrow V_{n,p}\uparrow\longrightarrow V_{n+1,p}\longrightarrow0,$$
which splits when $(n,p)$ is noncritical. Here induction of a right module means $V\otimes_{A_n}A_{n+1}$.

If $\kappa=\operatorname{ord}(q^2)\ge2$ and $p<p'$ are a pair reflected in a single intervening critical line, meaning $0<p'-p<\kappa$ and $p=p'+r(n,p')-\kappa$, then
$$\operatorname{Hom}_{A_n}(V_{n,p},V_{n,p'})\ne0.$$
Out-of-range standard modules are zero. Statements about a splitting omit zero summands.

SUPPLIER lem-temperley-lieb-link-state-module-action-is-well-defined

## Statement

For $R$, $\delta$, $n$, $t$ and $W_{n,t}(R,\delta)$ as in [[def-temperley-lieb-link-state-standard-modules]], the rule $x\cdot a=[x\circ a]$ defines a unital right $\mathrm{TL}_n(R,\delta)$-module structure on $W_{n,t}(R,\delta)$.

USE
[F2] The link-state action and rightmost-site restriction sequence are [[lem-temperley-lieb-link-state-module-action-is-well-defined]] and [[lem-temperley-lieb-link-state-restriction-filtration]].

4.1 On the submodule and quotient in [F2], $F_{n-1}$ acts by $f_{n-1,p}$ and $f_{n-1,p-1}$. Their difference is $-(q-q^{-1})(q^{n-2p+1}-q^{-(n-2p+1)})$. Under the noncritical and $q\ne\pm1$ hypotheses it is nonzero. The operator on the restriction satisfies $(F_{n-1}-f_{n-1,p})(F_{n-1}-f_{n-1,p-1})=0$, because each factor kills its respective subquotient. Its two spectral projections therefore give an invariant direct sum splitting the restriction sequence. [F2, F4, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Append an adjacent arc at sites $n+1,n+2$ to $d$, obtaining $\bar d\in V_{n+2,p+1}$. The map $\Phi(u\otimes d)=u\bar d$ into $V_{n+2,p+1}\downarrow$ is balanced and $A_{n+1}$-linear: elements of $A_n$ act only on the first $n$ sites. Its image of $h_r\otimes d$ has its rightmost adjacent arc at $r$; deleting that arc recovers $d$. Conversely every $(n+2,p+1)$-state has a rightmost adjacent arc; delete it at position $r$, and the remaining state is $r$-admissible. These two operations are inverse, so the spanning tensors in step 1.2 map bijectively to the link-state basis. They are therefore a basis and $\Phi$ is an isomorphism, except in the stated exceptional case. Applying the restriction sequence [F2] to $V_{n+2,p+1}$ now gives the asserted induction sequence. Applying step 4.1 at rank $n+2$ splits it whenever $(n,p)$ is noncritical, since $n+2-2(p+1)+1=n-2p+1$. [F1, F2, step 4.1, step 1.2, algebra]

SUPPLIER lem-temperley-lieb-link-state-gram-determinant

## Statement

Let $q\in\mathbb C^\times$, $\delta=q+q^{-1}$, $n\ge0$, and $0\le p\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor$. Put $t=n-2p$, and let $G_{n,p}$ be the Gram matrix of the link-state form on $W_{n,t}(\mathbb C,\delta)$ in its link-state basis. Set

$$d_{n,r}=\binom nr-\binom n{r-1},\qquad \binom n{-1}=0.$$

Then

$$\det G_{n,p}=\prod_{j=1}^{p}\left(\frac{[n-2p+1+j]_q}{[j]_q}\right)^{d_{n,p-j}}.$$

The empty product for $p=0$ is $1$. The right side is interpreted as its polynomial continuation in $\delta$ at parameter values where an individual displayed quotient has a removable zero-over-zero form.

USE
[F3] For generic $q$ the link-state Gram matrix is invertible, by [[lem-temperley-lieb-link-state-gram-determinant]].

3.1 Compute $F_n$ on the state $z_p$ with adjacent arcs $(1,2),(3,4),\ldots,(2p-1,2p)$ and all remaining sites defects. At a cup, expansion of the two adjacent crossings uses the identities $d_ig_{i+1}g_i=-d_id_{i+1}$ and $g_ig_{i+1}d_i=-d_{i+1}d_i$; for example the first is $qd_i-d_id_{i+1}-\delta d_i+q^{-1}d_id_{i+1}d_i=-d_id_{i+1}$. The two minus signs on the two sides of each cup cancel. Thus each initial cup can be removed without changing the coefficient of $z_p$. For the $t=n-2p$ defect strands, expand the auxiliary strand successively from its first crossing. A smoothing connecting two defects vanishes in the standard quotient. There are two remaining continuations: following the auxiliary strand on its two sides gives respectively $q^t\delta-q^{t-1}=q^{t+1}$ and $q^{-t}\delta-q^{-t+1}=q^{-(t+1)}$. The factors arise by choosing the identity smoothing at each subsequent crossing until the final cap; that cap either closes a loop or chooses the second smoothing, giving the displayed two terms on each side. Hence the coefficient of $z_p$ is $f_{n,p}$. For generic $q$, [F3] makes the form nondegenerate. Capping $z$ against $y$ and then attaching $x$ gives the rank-one diagram operator $z\mapsto\phi(y,z)x$, because lower-propagating terms vanish. Thus every nonzero vector generates the standard, which is simple. Centrality now makes $F_n$ scalar on the entire standard: over $\mathbb C$ a central operator has an eigenvalue, whose nonzero eigenspace is the simple module. Since the action matrices and $f_{n,p}$ are Laurent polynomial in $q$, this scalar identity extends to every $q\ne0$. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

SUPPLIER thm-temperley-lieb-diagram-generators-span

## Statement

For every $n\ge0$, each basis diagram in $\mathcal D(n,n)$ is a finite product of the elementary diagrams $d_1,\ldots,d_{n-1}$ from [[def-temperley-lieb-diagram-category]]. The product can be chosen so that no intermediate stack creates a closed cycle. Therefore the set of diagrams lies in the unital subalgebra of $\mathrm{TL}_n(R,\delta)$ generated by these diagrams.

USE
[F1] Diagram stacking is associative, reflection is an anti-involution, the elementary diagrams $d_i$ generate, and $d_i^2=\delta d_i$, $d_id_{i\pm1}d_i=d_i$, $d_id_j=d_jd_i$ for $|i-j|>1$ ([[thm-temperley-lieb-diagram-category-laws]], [[thm-temperley-lieb-diagram-generators-span]], [[lem-temperley-lieb-generators-satisfy-local-relations]]). The spanning Jones normal forms are [[thm-temperley-lieb-generator-normal-form]].

1.1 It suffices to perform computations for left modules: send a right module to the left module with $a\cdot v=v\cdot a^*$, where $*$ is diagram reflection. This is an exact equivalence preserving Hom direction, induction and restriction. Fix a square root of $q$ and put $g_i=q^{1/2}1-q^{-1/2}d_i$. Expanding with [F1] gives $g_i^{-1}=q^{-1/2}1-q^{1/2}d_i$ and $g_ig_{i+1}g_i=g_{i+1}g_ig_{i+1}$. Let $T_n:A_{n+1}\to A_n$ close the last top endpoint to the last bottom endpoint externally on the right, removing any resulting loop with factor $\delta$. This is an $A_n$-bimodule map by the matching rule. Define $M_n=g_n\cdots g_2g_1^2g_2\cdots g_n$ and $F_n=T_n(M_n)$; set $F_0=\delta$. Every summand of $M_n$ has an even total number of square-root factors, so $F_n$ has coefficients in $\mathbb Z[q,q^{-1}]$, independent of the chosen square root. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 For clarity give a basis proof of the induction sequence. In left-module notation put $h_r=d_rd_{r+1}\cdots d_n$ for $1\le r\le n$, and $h_{n+1}=1$. A state $d$ is $r$-admissible when it has no adjacent arc at an index $i\ge r$. The tensors $h_r\otimes d$ with $d$ $r$-admissible, including all $1\otimes d$, span $A_{n+1}\otimes_{A_n}V_{n,p}$. First, a diagram word is a sum of $h_ru$ with $u\in A_n$: reverse a spanning Jones normal form from [F1]. Its initial ascending block is either $h_r$ or contains no $d_n$, and every later block lies in $A_n$. The surjection from the presented algebra to the diagram algebra is supplied by the local relations, so this spanning assertion applies to diagram words. Next, if $d$ has its largest adjacent arc at $s\ge r$, write $d=d_sf$. For $n\ge3$ this can be done without division: move the endpoint immediately outside that adjacent arc through the arc, so applying $d_s$ restores $d$ and creates no loop. If $s=1$ use site $3$; otherwise use site $s-1$. The outside site is either a defect or an endpoint of another arc, and reconnecting its former partner supplies the inverse state $f$. Now the relations give $h_r\otimes d=h_{s+2}\otimes d_r\cdots d_{s-1}d$. The latter state is $(s+2)$-admissible: the adjacent arc at $s$ has moved to $r$, all intervening endpoints shift by two, and the tail beyond $s+1$ still has no adjacent arc. Repetition, increasing $r$, proves spanning. At $n=1,2$ the same conclusion is checked on the at most two states, except $n=2,p=1,\delta=0$, when the needed factorization $d=d_1f$ fails. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

SUPPLIER lem-temperley-lieb-generators-satisfy-local-relations

## Statement

Fix $n\ge0$, a commutative ring $R$, and $\delta\in R$. For $1\le i<n$, let $d_i\in\mathrm{TL}_n(R,\delta)$ be the elementary cup-cap diagram of [[def-temperley-lieb-diagram-category]]. Then

$$d_i^2=\delta d_i,\qquad d_i d_{i+1}d_i=d_i,\qquad d_{i+1}d_i d_{i+1}=d_{i+1},\qquad d_i d_j=d_jd_i\quad(|i-j|>1).$$

Consequently the assignment $u_i\mapsto d_i$ extends to a unital $R$-algebra homomorphism

$$\pi_n:P_n(R,\delta)\longrightarrow\mathrm{TL}_n(R,\delta).$$

## Statement

1.1 First work at generic $q$. The rightmost-defect submodule has nondegenerate form by [F2], so its orthogonal complement is invariant and maps isomorphically to the restriction quotient. The smaller standards are simple by their rank-one diagram operators; an invariant form on such a standard is a scalar multiple of its nondegenerate standard form, because their associated maps into the dual differ by an endomorphism, and an endomorphism of a finite-dimensional complex simple module is scalar (take any eigenvalue). Subtracting the submodule projection from quotient basis lifts gives a unitriangular Gram splitting $G_{n,p}=U^{\mathsf T}(G_{n-1,p}\oplus\alpha_{n,p}G_{n-1,p-1})U$. Compute its scalar as follows. Group the link states into the four types: $n,n-1$ both defects; $n$ a defect and $n-1$ on an earlier arc; $(n-1,n)$ an arc; and $n$ on an arc ending earlier than $n-1$. Put $A=G_{n-2,p}$, $B=G_{n-2,p-1}$ and write the smaller-rank matrix as $G_{n-1,p}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&AV_0\\V_0^{\mathsf T}A&V_0^{\mathsf T}AV_0+\alpha_{n-1,p}B\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. In the current off-diagonal Gram block the first column block is $(0,B)^{\mathsf T}$: types one and three have unmatched defects, while cutting the terminal arc in types two and three removes no closed component. The type-three diagonal block is $\delta B$, since the shared terminal arc closes one loop. If $(X,Y)^{\mathsf T}$ is the first column block of the projection coefficient matrix, multiplication by $G_{n-1,p}$ gives $AX+AV_0Y=0$ and $V_0^{\mathsf T}AX+(V_0^{\mathsf T}AV_0+\alpha_{n-1,p}B)Y=B$. Thus $X=-V_0Y$ and $Y=\alpha_{n-1,p}^{-1}I$. Its quadratic contribution is $\alpha_{n-1,p}^{-1}B$ after the four $V_0^{\mathsf T}AV_0$ terms cancel with coefficients $1-1-1+1$. Comparing the type-three block gives $\alpha_{n,p}=\delta-\alpha_{n-1,p}^{-1}$. At $n=2p$, deleting the terminal site closes exactly one additional component, so $G_{2p,p}=\delta G_{2p-1,p-1}$ and $\alpha_{2p,p}=\delta$. Since $F_{n-1}^*=F_{n-1}$ and the eigenvalues differ, the two summands are orthogonal. For generic $q$ this is exactly the orthogonal splitting just computed, since the submodule form is nondegenerate and its orthogonal complement is unique. Thus this Gram matrix identity is an identity of rational matrices regular at every noncritical root, and specializes there. This continuity argument applies to the entire matrix identity, rather than inferring a rank from a specialized determinant. SUPPLIER def-temperley-lieb-quantum-integers-and-quantum-characteristic

## Definition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring with unit and $\delta\in R$. Define the quantum integers by

$$[0]_\delta=0,\qquad [1]_\delta=1,\qquad [r+1]_\delta=\delta[r]_\delta-[r-1]_\delta\quad(r\ge1).$$

The **quantum characteristic** of $(R,\delta)$ is the least positive integer $\ell$ with $[\ell]_\delta=0$, if one exists, and $\ell=\infty$ otherwise. No element of $R$ is inverted in this definition.

For $q\in\mathbb C^\times$ and $\delta=q+q^{-1}$, write $[r]_q=[r]_\delta$. If $q\ne\pm1$, this agrees with $(q^r-q^{-r})/(q-q^{-1})$; at $q=\pm1$ the recurrence gives $[r]_q=rq^{r-1}$. For complex-parameter classification, write $\kappa=\operatorname{ord}(q^2)$, with $\kappa=\infty$ when $q^2$ has infinite order. If $q\ne\pm1$, the quantum characteristic equals $\kappa$ when $\kappa<\infty$ and is $\infty$ otherwise. At $q=\pm1$, $\kappa=1$ but the complex quantum characteristic is $\infty$, because $[r]_q=rq^{r-1}\ne0$ for every positive $r$.

For a field $K$ of positive characteristic $p$, set $p(0)=1$ and, when $\ell<\infty$, set $p(i)=\ell p^{i-1}$ for $i\ge1$. In characteristic zero and finite $\ell$, the finite digit scale is $p(1)=\ell$; the separate characteristic-zero criterion below uses $a\ell-1$ for arbitrary positive integers $a$. If $\ell=\infty$, every finite index is already in the range $n<\ell$.

USE
[F5] The quantum integers satisfy $[a+1]_q=\delta[a]_q-[a-1]_q$ and vanish, for $q\ne\pm1$, precisely when $q^{2a}=1$ ([[def-temperley-lieb-quantum-integers-and-quantum-characteristic]]).

## Definition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring with unit ([[def-algebra-over-a-commutative-ring]]) and let $\delta\in R$. The object labels are the nonnegative integers $n=0,1,2,\ldots$. For labels $m,n$, place bottom points $b_1,\ldots,b_m$ and top points $t_1,\ldots,t_n$ along the rectangle from left to right on their respective edges. Their cyclic order is $t_1,\ldots,t_n,b_m,\ldots,b_1$. When $m=n=0$, the empty matching is included.

An $(m,n)$ **diagram** is a perfect matching of these $m+n$ boundary labels which is noncrossing: there are no four labels $a,b,c,d$ in cyclic order whose matched pairs are $\{a,c\}$ and $\{b,d\}$. A picture consists of disjoint arcs realizing the matching; equality is equality of the matching, so planar deformation that fixes the boundary does not change a diagram. If $m+n$ is odd, there are no such diagrams. Write $\mathcal D(m,n)$ for this set and define the $R$-module

$$M_R(m,n):=R^{(\mathcal D(m,n))},$$

the free $R$-module on the set of diagrams ([[def-free-module-on-a-set-and-standard-basis]]). In particular, its diagram basis is empty when $m+n$ is odd.

For basis diagrams $D:m\to n$ and $E:n\to p$, stack $E$ above $D$ and identify the $n$ matching boundary points. The resulting finite graph has degree two at each identified point, where degree means the number of incident arcs. Each component is a path joining two exterior boundary points or a closed cycle. Remove every cycle and multiply the remaining exterior matching by $\delta^c$, where $c$ is the number of removed cycles. Denote this output by $E\circ D$ and extend the rule $R$-bilinearly to a map $M_R(n,p)\times M_R(m,n)\to M_R(m,p)$. A following result proves closure of these operations on the diagram modules and the category axioms.

The matching $1_n:n\to n$ pairs each bottom point to the top point directly above it. Horizontal juxtaposition, with the left diagram placed first, defines $m\otimes m'=m+m'$ on object labels and $D\otimes D'$ on basis diagrams; extend it $R$-bilinearly to the modules. A following result proves that these operations give a strict monoidal category.

For $1\le i<n$, let $d_i\in M_R(n,n)$ be the diagram with a cap joining top points $i,i+1$, a cup joining bottom points $i,i+1$, and vertical through strands at every other position. Define $\mathrm{TL}_n(R,\delta)$ to be the $R$-module $M_R(n,n)$ with bilinear multiplication $xy:=x\circ y$ and distinguished element $1_n$. A following result proves that it is a unital associative $R$-algebra and identifies it with the endomorphism algebra at object $n$.

The left factor is stacked above the right factor. The loop value is $\delta$; no invertibility or nonzero condition on $\delta$ is part of these data.

## Definition

For labels $m,n$, place bottom points $b_1,\ldots,b_m$ and top points $t_1,\ldots,t_n$ along the rectangle from left to right on their respective edges. Their cyclic order is $t_1,\ldots,t_n,b_m,\ldots,b_1$. When $m=n=0$, the empty matching is included.

An $(m,n)$ **diagram** is a perfect matching of these $m+n$ boundary labels which is noncrossing: there are no four labels $a,b,c,d$ in cyclic order whose matched pairs are $\{a,c\}$ and $\{b,d\}$. A picture consists of disjoint arcs realizing the matching; equality is equality of the matching, so planar deformation that fixes the boundary does not change a diagram. If $m+n$ is odd, there are no such diagrams. Write $\mathcal D(m,n)$ for this set and define the $R$-module

$$M_R(m,n):=R^{(\mathcal D(m,n))},$$

the free $R$-module on the set of diagrams ([[def-free-module-on-a-set-and-standard-basis]]). In particular, its diagram basis is empty when $m+n$ is odd.

For basis diagrams $D:m\to n$ and $E:n\to p$, stack $E$ above $D$ and identify the $n$ matching boundary points. The resulting finite graph has degree two at each identified point, where degree means the number of incident arcs. Each component is a path joining two exterior boundary points or a closed cycle. Remove every cycle and multiply the remaining exterior matching by $\delta^c$, where $c$ is the number of removed cycles. Denote this output by $E\circ D$ and extend the rule $R$-bilinearly to a map $M_R(n,p)\times M_R(m,n)\to M_R(m,p)$. A following result proves closure of these operations on the diagram modules and the category axioms.

The matching $1_n:n\to n$ pairs each bottom point to the top point directly above it. Horizontal juxtaposition, with the left diagram placed first, defines $m\otimes m'=m+m'$ on object labels and $D\otimes D'$ on basis diagrams; extend it $R$-bilinearly to the modules. A following result proves that these operations give a strict monoidal category.

For $1\le i<n$, let $d_i\in M_R(n,n)$ be the diagram with a cap joining top points $i,i+1$, a cup joining bottom points $i,i+1$, and vertical through strands at every other position. Define $\mathrm{TL}_n(R,\delta)$ to be the $R$-module $M_R(n,n)$ with bilinear multiplication $xy:=x\circ y$ and distinguished element $1_n$. A following result proves that it is a unital associative $R$-algebra and identifies it with the endomorphism algebra at object $n$.

The left factor is stacked above the right factor. The loop value is $\delta$; no invertibility or nonzero condition on $\delta$ is part of these data.

Thus the linear equations $d_ie=0$, $\varepsilon(e)=1$ imply $ae=\varepsilon(a)e$ for every $a$, and in particular $e^2=e$. Horizontal reflection defines an anti-involution $*$ fixing generators and preserving $\varepsilon$. The element $f=ee^*$ is killed by the generators on both sides and has augmentation $1$. Right annihilation gives $fe=f$, while left annihilation of $e$ gives $fe=e$; hence $e=f$ is two-sided. For two such elements $e,e'$, the same argument gives $ee'=e$ by right annihilation of $e$ and $ee'=e'$ by left annihilation of $e'$. They are equal. These arguments use no division and work over every commutative coefficient ring. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

SUPPLIER def-temperley-lieb-diagram-category

## Definition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring with unit ([[def-algebra-over-a-commutative-ring]]) and let $\delta\in R$. The object labels are the nonnegative integers $n=0,1,2,\ldots$. For labels $m,n$, place bottom points $b_1,\ldots,b_m$ and top points $t_1,\ldots,t_n$ along the rectangle from left to right on their respective edges. Their cyclic order is $t_1,\ldots,t_n,b_m,\ldots,b_1$. When $m=n=0$, the empty matching is included.

An $(m,n)$ **diagram** is a perfect matching of these $m+n$ boundary labels which is noncrossing: there are no four labels $a,b,c,d$ in cyclic order whose matched pairs are $\{a,c\}$ and $\{b,d\}$. A picture consists of disjoint arcs realizing the matching; equality is equality of the matching, so planar deformation that fixes the boundary does not change a diagram. If $m+n$ is odd, there are no such diagrams. Write $\mathcal D(m,n)$ for this set and define the $R$-module

$$M_R(m,n):=R^{(\mathcal D(m,n))},$$

the free $R$-module on the set of diagrams ([[def-free-module-on-a-set-and-standard-basis]]). In particular, its diagram basis is empty when $m+n$ is odd.

For basis diagrams $D:m\to n$ and $E:n\to p$, stack $E$ above $D$ and identify the $n$ matching boundary points. The resulting finite graph has degree two at each identified point, where degree means the number of incident arcs. Each component is a path joining two exterior boundary points or a closed cycle. Remove every cycle and multiply the remaining exterior matching by $\delta^c$, where $c$ is the number of removed cycles. Denote this output by $E\circ D$ and extend the rule $R$-bilinearly to a map $M_R(n,p)\times M_R(m,n)\to M_R(m,p)$. A following result proves closure of these operations on the diagram modules and the category axioms.

The matching $1_n:n\to n$ pairs each bottom point to the top point directly above it. Horizontal juxtaposition, with the left diagram placed first, defines $m\otimes m'=m+m'$ on object labels and $D\otimes D'$ on basis diagrams; extend it $R$-bilinearly to the modules. A following result proves that these operations give a strict monoidal category.

For $1\le i<n$, let $d_i\in M_R(n,n)$ be the diagram with a cap joining top points $i,i+1$, a cup joining bottom points $i,i+1$, and vertical through strands at every other position. Define $\mathrm{TL}_n(R,\delta)$ to be the $R$-module $M_R(n,n)$ with bilinear multiplication $xy:=x\circ y$ and distinguished element $1_n$. A following result proves that it is a unital associative $R$-algebra and identifies it with the endomorphism algebra at object $n$.

The left factor is stacked above the right factor. The loop value is $\delta$; no invertibility or nonzero condition on $\delta$ is part of these data.

## Statement

Let $q=e^{\pi i/\ell}$, $\ell\ge3$, $\delta=q+q^{-1}$ and $\tau_n=\delta^{-n}\operatorname{Tr}_n$. Put

$$\operatorname{Rad}(\tau_n)=\{x\in\mathrm{TL}_n:\tau_n(xy)=0\text{ for every }y\in\mathrm{TL}_n\}.$$

Let $I_n$ be the two-sided ideal generated by $p_{\ell-1}\otimes1_{n-\ell+1}$ for $n\ge\ell-1$, and $I_n=0$ below that rank. Then $I_n=\operatorname{Rad}(\tau_n)$, and $\mathrm{TL}_n/I_n$ is the direct sum of full matrix algebras with multiplicities given by length-$n$ paths from $0$ of heights $0,\ldots,\ell-2$. The additive idempotent-complete negligible quotient has simple labels $0,\ldots,\ell-2$, represented by $p_a$, and

$$a\otimes b\cong\bigoplus_{\substack{|a-b|\le c\le\min(a+b,2\ell-4-a-b)\\c\equiv a+b\ (2)}}c.$$

At $n=\ell-1$ the unreduced algebra is semisimple, while its trace radical is the nonzero one-dimensional block generated by $p_{\ell-1}$. Trace radical and Jacobson radical are therefore distinct at this first boundary.

SUPPLIER def-temperley-lieb-quantum-integers-and-quantum-characteristic

## Definition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring with unit and $\delta\in R$. Define the quantum integers by

$$[0]_\delta=0,\qquad [1]_\delta=1,\qquad [r+1]_\delta=\delta[r]_\delta-[r-1]_\delta\quad(r\ge1).$$

The **quantum characteristic** of $(R,\delta)$ is the least positive integer $\ell$ with $[\ell]_\delta=0$, if one exists, and $\ell=\infty$ otherwise. No element of $R$ is inverted in this definition.

For $q\in\mathbb C^\times$ and $\delta=q+q^{-1}$, write $[r]_q=[r]_\delta$. If $q\ne\pm1$, this agrees with $(q^r-q^{-r})/(q-q^{-1})$; at $q=\pm1$ the recurrence gives $[r]_q=rq^{r-1}$. For complex-parameter classification, write $\kappa=\operatorname{ord}(q^2)$, with $\kappa=\infty$ when $q^2$ has infinite order. If $q\ne\pm1$, the quantum characteristic equals $\kappa$ when $\kappa<\infty$ and is $\infty$ otherwise. At $q=\pm1$, $\kappa=1$ but the complex quantum characteristic is $\infty$, because $[r]_q=rq^{r-1}\ne0$ for every positive $r$.

For a field $K$ of positive characteristic $p$, set $p(0)=1$ and, when $\ell<\infty$, set $p(i)=\ell p^{i-1}$ for $i\ge1$. In characteristic zero and finite $\ell$, the finite digit scale is $p(1)=\ell$; the separate characteristic-zero criterion below uses $a\ell-1$ for arbitrary positive integers $a$. If $\ell=\infty$, every finite index is already in the range $n<\ell$.
