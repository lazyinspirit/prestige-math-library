## Definition

Fix $m\ge1$ and let $A_m$ be the Khovanov–Seidel type A algebra of
[[def-khovanov-seidel-type-a-quiver-algebra]], graded by internal degree, with
the vertex idempotents $e_i:=(i)$ and the generators $u_i:=(i|i+1)$,
$d_i:=(i+1|i)$.

**The finite module category.** $A_m\text{-mod}$ is the category whose objects
are the **finitely generated graded left $A_m$-modules** and whose morphisms are
the **degree-zero $A_m$-linear maps** $f:M\to N$, that is
$f(M_d)\subseteq N_d$ for every $d$, with composition of maps. Throughout this
page every left module, right module and bimodule over $A_m$ is graded and every
homomorphism is degree-zero unless stated otherwise. Finite generation is in the
sense of [[def-finitely-generated-graded-projective-module]], and graded
modules, degree-zero maps and internal shifts are those of
[[def-graded-ring-module-bimodule-and-internal-shift]].

**Internal shift.** For $r\in\mathbb Z$ and a graded left $A_m$-module $M$ the
**internal shift** $M\{r\}$ is the graded module with
$$(M\{r\})_d=M_{d-r}\qquad(d\in\mathbb Z),$$
carrying the same left $A_m$-action as $M$. The shift $\{1\}$ moves the grading
upwards by one: an element of $M_d$ is an element of $(M\{1\})_{d+1}$.

**Vertex projectives.** Put
$$P_i:=A_me_i,\qquad {}_iP:=e_iA_m\qquad(0\le i\le m),$$
equipped with the grading in which a homogeneous element of $A_m$ of degree $d$
lies in degree $d$. Because paths are written left to right and the product is
concatenation, $P_i$ consists of the classes of the paths **ending** at $i$ and
${}_iP$ of the classes of the paths **beginning** at $i$. The algebra splits as
$$A_m=\bigoplus_{i=0}^mP_i=\bigoplus_{i=0}^m{}_iP$$
as a left, respectively right, module over itself; the decomposition into vertex
projectives is displayed as proved below.

**Claims proved below.** $A_m\text{-mod}$ is an abelian category; the internal
shift $\{r\}$ is an automorphism of $A_m\text{-mod}$; each $P_i$ is a finite
graded projective left $A_m$-module and each ${}_iP$ a finite graded projective
right $A_m$-module, generated by $e_i$.

**Notation.** The source writes $P_i=A_m(i)$ for the summand $A_me_i$, and
$\{r\}$ for the internal shift; the parentheses in $A_m(i)$ are part of that
summand notation and never denote a shift. On this page only the braces $\{r\}$
denote the internal shift, and only the brackets $[r]$ of
[[def-bounded-projective-homotopy-category-for-a-m]] denote the homological
shift, which is not defined yet.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $m\ge1$, the algebra $A_m$ with its internal degree, the vertex idempotents $e_i=(i)$ and the vertex projectives $P_i=A_me_i$ and ${}_iP=e_iA_m$.

[F1] $A_m=\mathbb Z\Gamma_m/I_m$ is a ring, graded by internal degree with $A_m=\bigoplus_{d\ge0}(A_m)_d$, the classes $e_i=(i)$ and $u_i=(i|i+1)$ are homogeneous of degree $0$ and $d_i=(i+1|i)$ is homogeneous of degree $1$, and the generators of $I_m$ vanish in $A_m$ ([[def-khovanov-seidel-type-a-quiver-algebra]]).

[F2] In $\mathbb Z\Gamma_m$ the vertex paths are orthogonal idempotents, $(u)(v)=\delta_{uv}(u)$, with $\sum_{j=0}^m(j)=1$, and the endpoint formulas $(u)p=p$ for $s(p)=u$ and $p(v)=p$ for $t(p)=v$ hold, the products being $0$ otherwise; passing to the quotient $A_m$, the images $e_j$ satisfy the same identities ([[def-path-ring-of-a-finite-quiver-over-the-integers]]).

[L3] $A_m$ is a free abelian group of rank $4m+1$ with basis the classes of the $m+1$ vertices, the $2m$ arrows and the $m$ returns $(i|i-1|i)$, $1\le i\le m$ ([[lem-the-khovanov-seidel-algebra-has-the-four-m-plus-one-path-basis]]).

[L4] A graded left $A$-module $M$ is an $A$-module with $M=\bigoplus_dM_d$ and $A_iM_d\subseteq M_{i+d}$; a degree-zero map is $A$-linear with $f(M_d)\subseteq N_d$; $\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A)$ is the category of graded left $A$-modules and degree-zero maps; the internal shift $M\{r\}$ has $(M\{r\})_d=M_{d-r}$ and is again graded; a graded submodule is a submodule with $S=\bigoplus_d(S\cap M_d)$ ([[def-graded-ring-module-bimodule-and-internal-shift]]).

[L5] A graded left $A$-module $P$ is graded projective when every degree-zero epimorphism $q:E\twoheadrightarrow M$ and every degree-zero $f:P\to M$ admit a degree-zero lift $\widetilde f$ with $q\widetilde f=f$; $P$ is finitely generated when $P=Ap_1+\cdots+Ap_n$ for finitely many homogeneous $p_j$, equivalently when the underlying module is finitely generated; $P$ is finite graded projective when it is both ([[def-finitely-generated-graded-projective-module]]).

[L6] For every unital associative $\mathbb Z$-graded algebra $A$ the category $\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A)$ is abelian; kernels, images, cokernels and finite biproducts are computed in each homogeneous degree; a sequence is exact precisely when it is exact degreewise ([[lem-graded-module-kernels-cokernels-and-biproducts-are-degreewise]]).

[L7] A graded left $A$-module $P$ is finite graded projective if and only if $P$ is a degree-zero direct summand of a finite direct sum of internal shifts $A\{r_1\}\oplus\cdots\oplus A\{r_n\}$; in particular every finite direct sum of shifts of $A$ is a projective object of $\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A)$, and no arbitrary-index choice is used ([[thm-finite-graded-projectives-are-summands-of-finite-graded-free-modules]]).

[F8] $\mathbb Z$ is a principal ideal domain and hence Noetherian ([[cor-principal-ideal-domains-are-noetherian]]).

[L9] Every finitely generated left module over a left Noetherian ring is Noetherian, that is, each of its submodules is finitely generated ([[thm-finitely-generated-modules-over-noetherian-rings-are-noetherian]]).

[L10] A unital ring $R$ is left Noetherian when its left regular module ${}_RR$ is Noetherian, and a module is Noetherian when each of its submodules is finitely generated; the submodules of ${}_RR$ are exactly the left ideals ([[def-noetherian-ring]], [[def-noetherian-module]]).





## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 *$A_m$ is left and right Noetherian.* By [L3] the abelian group $A_m$ is free of rank $4m+1$ over $\mathbb Z$, hence finitely generated as a $\mathbb Z$-module; $\mathbb Z$ is Noetherian by [F8], so [L9] makes $A_m$ Noetherian as a left $\mathbb Z$-module, that is, every $\mathbb Z$-submodule of $A_m$ is finitely generated as a $\mathbb Z$-module. A left ideal of $A_m$ is a $\mathbb Z$-submodule, so it is generated as a $\mathbb Z$-module, hence as a left ideal, by finitely many elements, and [L10] gives that $A_m$ is left Noetherian; the same computation with right ideals, $A_m$ being finitely generated as a right $\mathbb Z$-module as well, makes $A_m$ right Noetherian. [L3, F8, L9, L10]

1.2 *The category and its ambient category.* By [L4] and [L5] the objects of $A_m\text{-mod}$ are the graded left $A_m$-modules that are finitely generated over $A_m$, and its morphisms are the degree-zero $A_m$-linear maps, so $A_m\text{-mod}$ is the full subcategory of $\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A_m)$ on the finitely generated objects; by [L6] the ambient category $\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A_m)$ is abelian, with degreewise kernels, cokernels and finite biproducts. [L4, L5, L6]

1.3 *$P_i$ is a degree-zero direct summand of $A_m$.* By [F2] the identities $(u)(v)=\delta_{uv}(u)$ and $\sum_j(j)=1$ hold in $A_m$, so with $e_j=(j)$ one has $A_m=\bigoplus_jA_me_j$: every $x\in A_m$ equals $x\cdot1=\sum_jxe_j$ with $xe_j\in A_me_j$, and if $\sum_jx_j=0$ with $x_j\in A_me_j$ then $x_i=x_ie_i=\bigl(\sum_jx_j\bigr)e_i=0$. The inclusion $\iota_i:P_i\to A_m$ and the map $p_i(x):=xe_i$ are $A_m$-linear, and $p_i$ is degree-zero because $e_i$ is homogeneous of degree $0$ by [F1]; as $p_i\iota_i=\mathrm{id}_{P_i}$ and $\iota_ip_i$ is the projection onto the $i$-th summand, $P_i$ is a degree-zero direct summand of $A_m=A_m\{0\}$, a finite direct sum of zero shifts of $A_m$, and $P_i$ is generated over $A_m$ by $e_i$. [F1, F2, L4]

1.4 *The internal shift is an automorphism of $A_m\text{-mod}$.* Let $M$ be a finitely generated graded left $A_m$-module and $r\in\mathbb Z$. By [L4] the shift $M\{r\}$ has $(M\{r\})_d=M_{d-r}$ and is a graded left $A_m$-module; if $M=Ax_1+\cdots+Ax_n$ with homogeneous $x_j$, then each $x_j$ lies in $(M\{r\})_{d_j+r}$ for its degree $d_j$ in $M$ and $M\{r\}=Ax_1+\cdots+Ax_n$, so $M\{r\}$ is an object of $A_m\text{-mod}$. A degree-zero map $f:M\to N$ satisfies $f(M_{d-r})\subseteq N_{d-r}$ for every $d$, so the same underlying map is a degree-zero map $M\{r\}\to N\{r\}$: the shift is a functor, additive because addition of maps is unchanged, and $\{r\}\{s\}=\{r+s\}$ on objects and on morphisms, so $\{r\}$ has the two-sided inverse $\{-r\}$ and is an automorphism of $A_m\text{-mod}$. [L4]

2.1 *Closure of the finitely generated objects under kernels, cokernels and finite sums.* Let $f:M\to N$ be a degree-zero map of finitely generated graded left $A_m$-modules. By [L6] the kernel, image and cokernel of $f$ are graded modules, with $\ker f$ a graded submodule of $M$; since $M$ is a finitely generated left module over the left Noetherian ring $A_m$ of step 1.1, [L9] makes $\ker f$ finitely generated. The image $\operatorname{im}f\cong M/\ker f$ and the cokernel $\operatorname{coker}f=N/\operatorname{im}f$ are quotients of finitely generated modules, hence finitely generated, and the direct sum $M\oplus N$ is generated by the union of finite homogeneous generating families of $M$ and $N$, while the zero module is generated by the empty family. [step 1.1, L6, L9]

2.2 *$P_i$ is finite graded projective.* By step 1.3 the module $P_i$ is a degree-zero direct summand of $A_m=A_m\{0\}$, a finite direct sum of internal shifts of $A_m$, and $P_i$ is generated by the single element $e_i$; [L7] therefore makes $P_i$ a finite graded projective left $A_m$-module in the sense of [L5]. [step 1.3, L5, L7]

2.3 *${}_iP$ is finite graded projective on the right.* The opposite ring $A_m^{\mathrm{op}}$ is a graded $\mathbb Z$-algebra with the same homogeneous pieces $(A_m^{\mathrm{op}})_i=(A_m)_i$, because the graded algebra axiom $A_j^{\mathrm{op}}A_i^{\mathrm{op}}\subseteq(A_m^{\mathrm{op}})_{i+j}$ reads $A_iA_j\subseteq(A_m)_{i+j}$ in $A_m$; a graded right $A_m$-module is precisely a graded left $A_m^{\mathrm{op}}$-module with the same degree-zero maps, and the mirrored computation of step 1.3, using [F2] and the right module structure, gives $A_m=\bigoplus_je_jA_m$ as a direct sum of right submodules, so ${}_iP=e_iA_m$ is a degree-zero direct summand of $A_m^{\mathrm{op}}\{0\}$, generated by $e_i$. Applying [L7] to the graded algebra $A_m^{\mathrm{op}}$ makes ${}_iP$ a finite graded projective right $A_m$-module in the sense of [L5]. [step 1.3, F2, L5, L7]

3.1 *$A_m\text{-mod}$ is abelian.* By steps 1.2 and 2.1 the full subcategory $A_m\text{-mod}$ of the abelian category $\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A_m)$ contains a zero object, is closed under binary biproducts, and contains a kernel and a cokernel of each of its morphisms: if $fg=0$ for a morphism $g$ of the subcategory, the factorization of $g$ through $\ker f$ asserted by [L6] has codomain the finitely generated object $\ker f$, so it is a morphism of the subcategory and exhibits $\ker f\to M$ as a kernel in $A_m\text{-mod}$, and dually $\operatorname{coker}f$ is a cokernel in $A_m\text{-mod}$. Hom-sets are abelian groups under addition of maps and composition is additive, so $A_m\text{-mod}$ is additive; and the canonical comparison $\operatorname{coim}f\to\operatorname{im}f$, an isomorphism in $\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A_m)$ by [L6], is a morphism of $A_m\text{-mod}$ because the subcategory is full. Hence $A_m\text{-mod}$ is abelian. [step 1.2, step 2.1, L6]

4.1 *Conclusion.* $A_m\text{-mod}$ is the abelian category of the finitely generated graded left $A_m$-modules and degree-zero maps (steps 1.2 and 3.1); the internal shift $\{r\}$ is an automorphism of it (step 1.4); $P_i=A_me_i$ is a finite graded projective left $A_m$-module and ${}_iP=e_iA_m$ is a finite graded projective right $A_m$-module, each generated by the homogeneous idempotent $e_i$ (steps 2.2 and 2.3); and $A_m$ decomposes as $\bigoplus_iP_i$ on the left and as $\bigoplus_i{}_iP$ on the right (steps 1.3 and 2.3). The source's notation $P_i=A_m(i)$ refers to this summand; the internal shift of this page is written $\{r\}$, so no claim about a shift is being made by that notation. [step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.2, step 2.3, step 1.4, step 3.1] ∎
