## Statement

Let $\Theta\subset S^1$ be a nonempty open set of unit line normals and let $f\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^2;\mathbb C)$. If the line integral $Rf(\theta,s)$ is zero for every $\theta\in\Theta$ and $s\in\mathbb R$, then $f$ is zero everywhere. The assertion holds without Countable Choice.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A nonempty open set $\Theta\subset S^1$, a compactly supported smooth complex function $f$, and the hypothesis $Rf(\theta,s)=0$ for every measured normal and offset.

[F1] For this proof, put $W_\Theta=\{\sigma\theta:\theta\in\Theta,\ \sigma\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}\}$. Since $\Theta$ is nonempty and open, it contains an open arc $J$, and the positive-radius sector $\{r\theta:r>0,\ \theta\in J\}$ is a nonempty open subset of $W_\Theta$.

[F2] [[thm-ip-fourier-slice-identity]]: for compactly supported smooth input and the line-normal convention, $\widehat{Rf}(\theta,\sigma)=\widehat f(\sigma\theta)$ for all real $\sigma$; the supplier proof uses no Countable Choice.

[F3] [[lem-ip-compact-support-fourier-laplace-is-entire]]: the compact-support Riemann Fourier-Laplace transform of a smooth compactly supported function is entire in each coordinate, with coordinate derivatives given by integrals with polynomial factors; the proof is choice-free.

[F4] [[thm-multidimensional-integral-properties]]: the real Riemann integral is linear, monotone, and bounded by volume times a uniform absolute bound; apply it componentwise to complex functions.

[F5] [[thm-identity-theorem-holomorphic-functions]]: a holomorphic function on a connected domain that vanishes on a set with an accumulation point in the domain vanishes everywhere.

[F6] [[thm-real-stone-weierstrass-for-compact-metric-spaces]]: real polynomials on a compact subset of $\mathbb R^2$ are dense in its real continuous functions because they form a unital point-separating algebra.

## Proof

1.1 The hypothesis and the compact-support Fourier-slice identity [F2] give $\widehat f(\sigma\theta)=0$ for every $\theta\in\Theta$ and $\sigma\in\mathbb R$. By [F1], this includes the nonempty open positive-radius sector generated by $J$; choose a product of open real intervals $I_1\times I_2$ inside that sector. For each real $\xi_2\in I_2$, the entire function $z_1\mapsto\widehat f(z_1,\xi_2)$ vanishes on $I_1$, so [F5] makes it zero for every complex $z_1$. Then for every real $\xi_1$ the entire second-coordinate slice vanishes on $I_2$, and [F5] makes it zero everywhere. Thus $\widehat f$ vanishes on all of $\mathbb R^2$. [F1, F2, F3, F5, given, choose]

2.1 Let $K=\operatorname{supp}f$. If $K=\varnothing$, then $f=0$. Otherwise, fix a rectangle $B$ whose interior contains $K$ and interpret all integrals below as compact-support Riemann integrals over $B$. Repeated use of the coordinate derivative formula in [F3] shows $\int_B x^\alpha f(x)\,dx=0$ for every multi-index $\alpha$, since all real-frequency derivatives of $\widehat f$ vanish at the origin. The real polynomials restricted to $K$ form a unital subalgebra separating points, because the coordinate functions distinguish distinct points. For an arbitrary $\varepsilon>0$, apply [F6] separately to $\operatorname{Re}\overline f$ and $\operatorname{Im}\overline f$ with error $\varepsilon/2$ and combine the two real polynomials into one complex polynomial $p$ with $\|p-\overline f\|_{C(K)}<\varepsilon$. Every moment is zero, so $\int_Bfp=0$. Since $f=0$ outside $K$, the real Riemann integral bound [F4] on both components gives $0\le\int_B|f|^2=\left|\int_Bf(\overline f-p)\right|\le\sqrt2\,\operatorname{vol}(B)\|f\|_{C(B)}\varepsilon$. As this holds for every $\varepsilon>0$, $\int_B|f|^2=0$; no sequence of approximants is selected. If $f$ were nonzero at a point, continuity would bound $|f|^2$ below by a positive number on a nondegenerate box inside $B$, forcing its nonnegative Riemann integral to be positive. Thus $f=0$ everywhere. This proof uses neither Fourier inversion nor Countable Choice. [F3, F4, F6, given, step 1.1] ∎
