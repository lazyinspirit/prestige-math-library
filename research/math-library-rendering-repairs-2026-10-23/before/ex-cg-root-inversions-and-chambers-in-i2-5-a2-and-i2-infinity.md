## Example

Let $S=\{s,t\}$ with $m:=m(s,t)\in\{3,5,\infty\}$, $V=\mathbb R^S$, and let $B$ be the Coxeter form with $c=\cos(\pi/m)$ for $m<\infty$ and $c=1$ for $m=\infty$, so that $B(e_s,e_s)=B(e_t,e_t)=1$ and $B(e_s,e_t)=-c$ ([[def-cg-real-coxeter-form-and-reflection]]). Let $\rho,\Phi,\Phi_\pm,N,C^\circ,W,\ell$ be as on this page ([[def-cg-canonical-reflection-homomorphism]], [[thm-cg-root-sign-and-simple-reflection-positivity]], [[def-cg-geometric-inversion-set]], [[thm-cg-root-inversion-formulas-and-strong-exchange]]), and let $u_k$ denote the alternating word $s\,t\,s\cdots$ of $k$ letters. The following data are computed and verified.

**(i) $A_2=I_2(3)$.** $c=\tfrac12$ and $\Phi_+=\{e_s,\ e_t,\ e_s+e_t\}$. For the six elements,
$$N(1)=\emptyset,\quad N(s)=\{e_s\},\quad N(t)=\{e_t\},\quad N(st)=\{e_t,\,e_s+e_t\},\quad N(ts)=\{e_s,\,e_s+e_t\},\quad N(sts)=\Phi_+,$$
each of cardinality $\ell(w)$. In the dual plane $V^*$ the three walls $H_{e_s},H_{e_t},H_{e_s+e_t}$ cut out six sectors, and the six chambers $wC^\circ$ occur in the cyclic order $C^\circ,\ sC^\circ,\ stC^\circ,\ stsC^\circ,\ tsC^\circ,\ tC^\circ$, with $stsC^\circ=-C^\circ$; explicitly $H_{e_s}$ separates $C^\circ$ from $u_1C^\circ,\dots,u_3C^\circ$ and $H_{e_t}$ from $u_3C^\circ,\dots,u_5C^\circ$.

**(ii) $I_2(5)$.** $2c=\phi=\tfrac{1+\sqrt5}{2}$, $4c^2-1=\phi$, and
$$\Phi_+=\{e_s,\ e_t,\ e_t+\phi e_s,\ e_s+\phi e_t,\ \phi(e_s+e_t)\},$$
five roots, each of $B$-norm one. With $\ell(u_k)=\min(k,10-k)$ for $0\le k<10$, for $1\le k\le5$
$$N(u_1)=\{e_s\},\quad N(u_2)=\{e_t,\,e_s+\phi e_t\},\quad N(u_3)=\{e_s,\,e_t+\phi e_s,\,\phi(e_s+e_t)\},$$
$$N(u_4)=\{e_t,\,e_s+\phi e_t,\,\phi(e_s+e_t),\,\phi e_s+e_t\},\quad N(u_5)=\Phi_+,$$
and for $5\le k<10$ one has $N(u_k)=\Phi_+\setminus N(u_{k-5}')$, where $u_j'$ is the alternating word of length $j$ beginning with $t$; for instance $N(u_7)=\{e_t,\,e_s+\phi e_t,\,\phi(e_s+e_t)\}$, of cardinality $3=\ell(u_7)$. The ten chambers $u_kC^\circ$ are the sectors cut out by the five root lines; the chambers on the negative side of $H_{e_s}$ are exactly $u_1C^\circ,\dots,u_5C^\circ$, and those on the negative side of $H_{e_t}$ are exactly $u_5C^\circ,\dots,u_9C^\circ$.

**(iii) $I_2(\infty)$.** $c=1$. Put $u:=e_s+e_t$; then $B(u,\cdot)=0$ on $\mathbb Re_s+\mathbb Re_t$, and
$$\Phi_+=\{e_s+ku:k\ge0\}\cup\{e_t+ku:k\ge0\}$$
is infinite. For every $k\ge0$,
$$N((st)^k)=\{ju+e_t:0\le j\le 2k-1\},\qquad N((st)^ks)=\{ju+e_s:0\le j\le 2k\},$$
of cardinalities $2k=\ell((st)^k)$ and $2k+1=\ell((st)^ks)$. The chambers $wC^\circ$ are the cones over the intervals $(j,j+1)$ of the affine line $\{f:f(e_s)+f(e_t)=1\}$, and the walls $H_{e_s},H_{e_t}$ meet that line in the points $0$ and $1$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** a two-element set $S=\{s,t\}$ with $m:=m(s,t)\in\{3,5,\infty\}$, the space $V=\mathbb R^S$ with basis vectors $e_s,e_t$, the Coxeter form $B$ with constant $c$ ($c=\cos(\pi/m)$ for $m<\infty$, $c=1$ for $m=\infty$), the presented Coxeter group $W$ with length function $\ell$, the canonical reflection homomorphism $\rho:W\to\mathrm{GL}(V)$ with root system $\Phi$, the partition $\Phi=\Phi_+\sqcup\Phi_-$, the inversion sets $N(w)$ of [[def-cg-geometric-inversion-set]], and the alternating words $u_k=s\,t\,s\cdots$ of $k$ letters beginning with $s$.

[F1] $B(e_s,e_s)=B(e_t,e_t)=1$ and $B(e_s,e_t)=-c$; the reflection $r_a$ with normal $a$, $B(a,a)\ne0$, is $r_a(v)=v-\frac{2B(v,a)}{B(a,a)}a$, so that $r_s(e_s)=-e_s$, $r_s(e_t)=e_t+2ce_s$ and $r_t(e_t)=-e_t$, $r_t(e_s)=e_s+2ce_t$ ([[def-cg-real-coxeter-form-and-reflection]], [[lem-cg-reflection-form-invariance-and-rank-two-orders]]).

[F2] $\rho$ is a homomorphism with $\rho(s)=r_s$, $\rho(t)=r_t$, so $\rho(uv)=\rho(u)\rho(v)$ and $\rho(1)=\mathrm{id}_V$; the root system is $\Phi=\{\rho(w)e_r:w\in W,\ r\in\{s,t\}\}$, every root has $B$-norm one, and $\Phi$ is invariant under every $\rho(w)$; moreover $\Phi_+=\Phi\cap V_+$ and $\Phi_-=\Phi\cap(-V_+)=-\Phi_+$ with $\Phi=\Phi_+\sqcup\Phi_-$, $V_+=\{\lambda e_s+\mu e_t:\lambda,\mu\ge0\}$ and $V_+\cap(-V_+)=\{0\}$ ([[def-cg-canonical-reflection-homomorphism]], [[lem-cg-reflection-representation-descends-and-root-norms]], [[thm-cg-root-sign-and-simple-reflection-positivity]] (1), (2)).

[F3] $N(w)=\{\alpha\in\Phi_+:\rho(w)\alpha\in\Phi_-\}$ for $w\in W$; $N(1)=\emptyset$; and for $u\in W$, $r\in\{s,t\}$ with $\ell(ur)>\ell(u)$ one has $N(ur)=\{e_r\}\sqcup\rho(r)N(u)$, while for $\ell(ur)<\ell(u)$ one has $N(ur)=\rho(r)(N(u)\setminus\{e_r\})$, where $\rho(r)N(u)=\{\rho(r)\beta:\beta\in N(u)\}$ ([[def-cg-geometric-inversion-set]] (1), (2)).

[F4] $|N(w)|=\ell(w)$ for every $w\in W$, and the map $\alpha\mapsto t_\alpha$ is a bijection from $\Phi_+$ onto the reflection set $T$ ([[thm-cg-root-inversion-formulas-and-strong-exchange]] (1), (2)).

[F5] In $W$ one has $s^2=t^2=1$ and, when $m<\infty$, $(st)^m=1$; if $m<\infty$ the elements $u_0,\dots,u_{2m-1}$ exhaust $W$ and $\ell(u_k)=\min(k,2m-k)$; if $m=\infty$ the elements $u_k$ are pairwise distinct with $\ell(u_k)=k$ ([[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]], [[lem-cg-rank-two-prefix-and-chamber-length-induction]] (1)).

[F6] The dual action is $(w\cdot f)(v)=f(\rho(w)^{-1}v)$, and $f\in wC^\circ$ if and only if $f(\rho(w)e_s)>0$ and $f(\rho(w)e_t)>0$; for every $w\in W$ and $r\in\{s,t\}$ one has $wC^\circ\subseteq rB_r$ if and only if $\ell(rw)<\ell(w)$. If $m<\infty$, the $2m$ chambers $wC^\circ$ $(w\in W)$ are exactly the $2m$ sectors cut out by the $m$ root hyperplanes $H_\beta$ $(\beta\in\Phi)$, with pairwise disjoint interiors; the corresponding closed sectors cover $V^*$, while the open sectors cover its complement of the root lines. If $m=\infty$, the chambers have pairwise disjoint interiors, the corresponding closed chambers have union $\{f\in V^*:f(e_s+e_t)>0\}\cup\{0\}$, distinct chambers are separated by a root hyperplane, and the traces $H_\beta\cap\{f:f(e_s)+f(e_t)=1\}$ are exactly the integers in the coordinate $y_s=f(e_s)$ on that affine line ([[def-cg-dual-chambers-and-reflection-hyperplanes]], [[lem-cg-dual-action-and-chamber-faces-exist]] (3), [[lem-cg-rank-two-prefix-and-chamber-length-induction]] (1), (4)).

[F7] Addition formulas: $\sin(x+y)=\sin x\cos y+\cos x\sin y$ and $\cos(x+y)=\cos x\cos y-\sin x\sin y$; $\cos(-x)=\cos x$ and $\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1$; $\cos(x+\pi)=-\cos x$, $\cos(\pi/2)=0$ and $\cos\pi=-1$; cosine is strictly decreasing on $[0,\pi]$; $\pi>0$ and $\pi/2$ is the smallest positive zero of cosine; every $a\ge0$ has a unique nonnegative square root $\sqrt a$, and $\sqrt5>1$; a product of two real numbers is zero only if one factor is zero ([[thm-sine-and-cosine-addition-formulas]], [[cor-trigonometric-parity-and-pythagorean-identity]], [[thm-quarter-turn-values-and-shift-formulas]], [[thm-sine-cosine-signs-monotonicity-and-ranges]], [[def-pi-via-first-positive-cosine-zero]], [[thm-of-square-roots]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **The case $A_2$: the constant.** Put $x:=\pi/3$, $q:=\cos x$ and $r:=\sin x$. The addition formulas give $\cos 2x=\cos^2x-\sin^2x=2q^2-1$ and $\sin 2x=2qr$, hence $\cos 3x=\cos(2x+x)=(2q^2-1)q-(2qr)r=2q^3-q-2q(1-q^2)=4q^3-3q$; since $3x=\pi$ and $\cos\pi=-1$, we get $4q^3-3q+1=0$, that is $(q+1)(2q-1)^2=0$. Now $0<\pi/3<\pi/2<\pi$ and cosine is strictly decreasing on $[0,\pi]$ with $\cos 0=1$ and $\cos(\pi/2)=0$, so $0<q<1$; hence $q\ne-1$ and $q=1/2$. Thus in this case $c=1/2$, and the reflection formulas of [F1] give $\rho(s)e_t=e_t+e_s$ and $\rho(t)e_s=e_s+e_t$. [F1, F7, algebra]

1.2 **The case $I_2(5)$: the chambers.** By [F6] the ten chambers $u_kC^\circ$ $(0\le k<10)$ are exactly the ten sectors cut out by the five root lines. By [F5], $(st)^5=1$ together with $s^2=t^2=1$ gives $su_k=u_{1-k}$ and $tu_k=u_{-1-k}$ with indices modulo $10$; combined with $\ell(u_k)=\min(k,10-k)$ this gives $\ell(su_k)=\ell(u_k)-1$ exactly for $1\le k\le5$, and $\ell(tu_k)=\ell(u_k)-1$ exactly for $5\le k\le9$. By the shortening equivalence of [F6], the chambers on the negative side of $H_{e_s}$ are exactly $u_1C^\circ,\dots,u_5C^\circ$ and those on the negative side of $H_{e_t}$ exactly $u_5C^\circ,\dots,u_9C^\circ$. [F5, F6, algebra]

1.3 **The case $I_2(\infty)$: the invariant functional direction.** Here $c=1$, so $B(u,u)=B(e_s,e_s)+2B(e_s,e_t)+B(e_t,e_t)=1-2+1=0$, $B(u,e_s)=B(e_s,e_s)+B(e_t,e_s)=1-1=0$ and $B(u,e_t)=B(e_s,e_t)+B(e_t,e_t)=-1+1=0$, where $u:=e_s+e_t$; by bilinearity $B(u,\cdot)=0$ on $\mathbb Re_s+\mathbb Re_t$. Moreover $\rho(s)u=\rho(s)e_s+\rho(s)e_t=-e_s+(e_t+2e_s)=u$ and $\rho(t)u=(e_s+2e_t)-e_t=u$, so $\rho(w)u=u$ for every $w\in W$, because $W$ is generated by $s,t$ and $\rho$ is a homomorphism. [F1, F2, F5, algebra]

2.1 **The case $A_2$: the positive roots.** By 1.1, the three vectors $e_s=\rho(1)e_s$, $e_t=\rho(1)e_t$ and $e_s+e_t=\rho(t)e_s$ are roots; they lie in $V_+\setminus\{0\}$, hence in $\Phi_+$, and they are pairwise distinct as functions on $\{s,t\}$, with values $(1,0)$, $(0,1)$, $(1,1)$. By [F6] the six chambers are cut out by the three root hyperplanes $H_\beta$, $\beta\in\Phi$; the map $\alpha\mapsto H_\alpha$ from $\Phi_+$ onto these lines is injective, because two roots on one line are proportional and both have $B$-norm one by [F2], forcing the proportionality factor to be $\pm1$, and it is surjective because every $H_\beta$ equals $H_{-\beta}$ with exactly one of $\pm\beta$ in $\Phi_+$. Hence $|\Phi_+|=3$ and $\Phi_+=\{e_s,\,e_t,\,e_s+e_t\}$. [F2, F6, step 1.1]

2.2 **The case $A_2$: the chambers.** Write $y_s:=f(e_s)$ and $y_t:=f(e_t)$ for $f\in V^*$, so that $f$ is described by the pair $(y_s,y_t)$. By [F6], $f\in wC^\circ$ if and only if $f(\rho(w)e_s)>0$ and $f(\rho(w)e_t)>0$. Using the products $\rho(s)e_s=-e_s$, $\rho(s)e_t=e_s+e_t$, $\rho(t)e_t=-e_t$, $\rho(t)e_s=e_s+e_t$ from [F1] and 1.1, together with $\rho(st)e_s=e_t$, $\rho(st)e_t=-e_s-e_t$, $\rho(ts)e_s=-e_s-e_t$, $\rho(ts)e_t=e_s$, $\rho(sts)e_s=-e_t$, $\rho(sts)e_t=-e_s$ (computed from $\rho(uv)=\rho(u)\rho(v)$), this gives $C^\circ=\{y_s>0,\ y_t>0\}$, $sC^\circ=\{y_s<0,\ y_s+y_t>0\}$, $stC^\circ=\{y_t>0,\ y_s+y_t<0\}$, $stsC^\circ=\{y_s<0,\ y_t<0\}=-C^\circ$, $tsC^\circ=\{y_s>0,\ y_s+y_t<0\}$ and $tC^\circ=\{y_t<0,\ y_s+y_t>0\}$. On each of these six sets the signs of $(y_s,\,y_t,\,y_s+y_t)$ are the six distinct patterns $(+,+,+)$, $(-,+,+)$, $(-,+,-)$, $(-,-,-)$, $(+,-,-)$, $(+,-,+)$; these are exactly the six combinations not excluded by one of the three equations $y_s=0$, $y_t=0$, $y_s+y_t=0$, so the six chambers are the six sectors, in the stated cyclic order. By [F5], $s^2=t^2=1$ and $(st)^3=1$ give $s(st)^j=(ts)^js$ and $t(st)^j=(ts)^jt$ for all $j\ge0$ and $(ts)^j=(st)^{-j}$, whence $su_k=u_{1-k}$ and $tu_k=u_{-1-k}$ with indices read modulo $6$; combined with $\ell(u_k)=\min(k,6-k)$ this gives $\ell(su_k)=\ell(u_k)-1$ exactly for $k\in\{1,2,3\}$ and $\ell(tu_k)=\ell(u_k)-1$ exactly for $k\in\{3,4,5\}$. By the shortening equivalence of [F6], $H_{e_s}$ separates $C^\circ$ from $u_1C^\circ,u_2C^\circ,u_3C^\circ$ and $H_{e_t}$ from $u_3C^\circ,u_4C^\circ,u_5C^\circ$. [F5, F6, step 1.1, algebra]

2.3 **The case $I_2(5)$: the constant.** Put $c:=\cos(\pi/5)$ (the constant of the form at $m=5$) and $q:=\cos(2\pi/5)$. By 1.1, $\cos(\pi/3)=1/2$; since $0<\pi/5<\pi/3<\pi$ and cosine is strictly decreasing on $[0,\pi]$, we have $c>1/2>0$. The double-angle formula gives $q=2c^2-1$, and since $4\pi/5=\pi-\pi/5$ with $\cos(\pi-x)=\cos((-x)+\pi)=-\cos(-x)=-\cos x$, we get $\cos(4\pi/5)=-c$; on the other hand $\cos(4\pi/5)=2q^2-1=2(2c^2-1)^2-1$. Hence $2(2c^2-1)^2-1+c=0$, that is $8c^4-8c^2+c+1=0$, and because $8c^4-8c^2+c+1=(2c-1)(c+1)(4c^2-2c-1)$ we obtain $4c^2-2c-1=0$, as $c>1/2$ and $c\ne-1$. Finally $4X^2-2X-1=4(X-\tfrac{1+\sqrt5}{4})(X-\tfrac{1-\sqrt5}{4})$ by expansion, so $c$ is one of the two numbers $\tfrac{1\pm\sqrt5}{4}$; since $\sqrt5>1$ gives $\tfrac{1-\sqrt5}{4}<0<c$, we conclude $c=\tfrac{1+\sqrt5}{4}=\phi/2$ with $\phi:=\tfrac{1+\sqrt5}{2}$, and then $2c=\phi$ and $4c^2-1=2c+1-1=2c=\phi$. [F7, step 1.1, algebra]

2.4 **The case $I_2(\infty)$: the positive roots.** Put $\Psi:=\{e_s+ku:k\ge0\}\cup\{e_t+ku:k\ge0\}$. First, $\Psi\subseteq\Phi_+$: clearly $e_s,e_t\in\Psi\cap\Phi_+$, and for $k\ge1$ the identities $e_s+ku=\rho(s)(e_t+(k-1)u)$ and $e_t+ku=\rho(t)(e_s+(k-1)u)$ hold by 1.3, so induction on $k$ shows that every element of $\Psi$ is a root; every element of $\Psi$ has nonnegative coefficients in $e_s,e_t$, so $\Psi\subseteq\Phi\cap V_+\setminus\{0\}=\Phi_+$. Conversely, $\Psi\cup(-\Psi)$ is stable under $\rho(s)$ and $\rho(t)$: by 1.3 one has $\rho(s)(e_s+ku)=-e_s+ku$, which is $e_t+(k-1)u$ for $k\ge1$ and $-e_s$ for $k=0$; $\rho(s)(e_t+ku)=(e_t+2e_s)+ku=e_s+(k+1)u$; $\rho(t)(e_t+ku)=-e_t+ku$, which is $e_s+(k-1)u$ for $k\ge1$ and $-e_t$ for $k=0$; and $\rho(t)(e_s+ku)=(e_s+2e_t)+ku=e_t+(k+1)u$. Since $W$ is generated by $s,t$ and $\rho$ is a homomorphism, $\Psi\cup(-\Psi)$ is stable under every $\rho(w)$; it contains $e_s,e_t$, so it contains the orbit $\Phi$. Hence $\Phi\subseteq\Psi\cup(-\Psi)$, and an element of $\Phi_+$ lies in $\Psi$: it lies in $\Psi\cup(-\Psi)$, and no element of $-\Psi$ has all coefficients nonnegative, while the elements of $V_+$ do. Therefore $\Phi_+=\Psi$; finally the two families $e_s+ku$ and $e_t+ku$ are disjoint and each is injective in $k$, because $u\ne0$ while evaluating $e_s+ku=e_s+k'u$ at $s$ gives $k=k'$, and $e_s+ku=e_t+k'u$ would give $1+k=k'$ at $s$ and $k=1+k'$ at $t$, hence $k=k+2$, a contradiction; so $\Phi_+$ is infinite. [F1, F2, step 1.3, algebra]

2.5 **The case $I_2(\infty)$: the inversion sets.** By [F5] the $u_k$ are pairwise distinct with $\ell(u_k)=k$, and $u_{2k}=(st)^k$, $u_{2k+1}=(st)^ks$. We prove by induction on $k\ge0$ the two formulas $N((st)^k)=\{ju+e_t:0\le j\le 2k-1\}$ and $N((st)^ks)=\{ju+e_s:0\le j\le 2k\}$. For $k=0$ the first is $N(1)=\emptyset$ and the second is $N(s)=\{e_s\}$. Assume the second formula for some $k\ge0$. Since $\ell((st)^{k+1})=2k+2>2k+1=\ell((st)^ks)$, the recursion of [F3] gives $N((st)^{k+1})=\{e_t\}\sqcup\rho(t)N((st)^ks)$; by 1.3, $\rho(t)e_s=e_s+2e_t=u+e_t$, so $\rho(t)\{ju+e_s:0\le j\le2k\}=\{ju+u+e_t:0\le j\le2k\}=\{(j+1)u+e_t:0\le j\le2k\}$ and therefore $N((st)^{k+1})=\{ju+e_t:0\le j\le2k+1\}$, which is the first formula with $k+1$. Since $\ell((st)^{k+1}s)=2k+3>2k+2=\ell((st)^{k+1})$, again, but with $\rho(s)e_t=e_t+2e_s=u+e_s$ from 1.3, $N((st)^{k+1}s)=\{e_s\}\sqcup\rho(s)\{ju+e_t:0\le j\le2k+1\}=\{ju+e_s:0\le j\le2k+2\}$, the second formula with $k+1$. So both formulas hold for all $k\ge0$. Their elements are pairwise distinct because $u\ne0$, so the two sets have cardinalities $2k$ and $2k+1$, equal to $\ell((st)^k)=2k$ and $\ell((st)^ks)=2k+1$ by [F5], in agreement with [F4]. [F3, F4, F5, step 1.3, algebra]

3.1 **The case $A_2$: the inversion sets.** By [F3] and 1.1, and since $\ell(u_0)=0<\ell(u_1)=1<\ell(u_2)=2<\ell(u_3)=3$ by [F5], one has $N(s)=\{e_s\}\sqcup\rho(s)N(1)=\{e_s\}$, $N(t)=\{e_t\}$, $N(st)=\{e_t\}\sqcup\rho(t)N(s)=\{e_t,\,e_s+e_t\}$, $N(ts)=\{e_s\}\sqcup\rho(s)N(t)=\{e_s,\,e_s+e_t\}$ and $N(sts)=\{e_s\}\sqcup\rho(s)N(st)=\{e_s,\,e_s+e_t,\,\rho(s)(e_s+e_t)\}=\{e_s,\,e_s+e_t,\,e_t\}=\Phi_+$, where we used $\rho(s)e_t=e_s+e_t$ and $\rho(s)(e_s+e_t)=\rho(s)e_s+\rho(s)e_t=-e_s+(e_s+e_t)=e_t$; also $N(1)=\emptyset$. The cardinalities $0,1,1,2,2,3$ equal $\ell(1),\ell(s),\ell(t),\ell(st),\ell(ts),\ell(sts)$ by [F5], as asserted. [F3, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 **The case $I_2(5)$: the positive roots.** By 2.3 and [F1], $\rho(s)e_t=e_t+\phi e_s$ and $\rho(t)e_s=e_s+\phi e_t$, and using $\rho(uv)=\rho(u)\rho(v)$ and $\phi^2=\phi+1$ one computes $\rho(st)e_s=\rho(s)(e_s+\phi e_t)=-e_s+\phi(e_t+\phi e_s)=(\phi^2-1)e_s+\phi e_t=\phi(e_s+e_t)$. Hence the five vectors $e_s=\rho(1)e_s$, $e_t=\rho(1)e_t$, $e_t+\phi e_s=\rho(s)e_t$, $e_s+\phi e_t=\rho(t)e_s$ and $\phi(e_s+e_t)=\rho(st)e_s$ are roots; each lies in $V_+\setminus\{0\}$, so all five lie in $\Phi_+$, and they are pairwise distinct as functions on $\{s,t\}$, with values $(1,0)$, $(0,1)$, $(\phi,1)$, $(1,\phi)$, $(\phi,\phi)$. By [F6] the ten chambers are cut out by the five root hyperplanes $H_\beta$, $\beta\in\Phi$, and as in 2.1 the map $\alpha\mapsto H_\alpha$ is a bijection from $\Phi_+$ onto these lines, so $|\Phi_+|=5$ and the five listed vectors exhaust $\Phi_+$; each of them has $B$-norm one because it is a root, by [F2]. 4.1 **The case $I_2(5)$: the inversion sets $N(u_k)$, $1\le k\le5$.** By [F5], $\ell(u_k)=k$ for $1\le k\le5$, so each step below is a shortening-free step of the recursion of [F3] and the union is disjoint. Starting from $N(1)=\emptyset$ and using the reflection values $\rho(t)e_s=e_s+\phi e_t$, $\rho(s)e_t=e_t+\phi e_s$, $\rho(s)(e_s+\phi e_t)=\phi(e_s+e_t)$, $\rho(t)(e_t+\phi e_s)=\phi(e_s+e_t)$, $\rho(t)\phi(e_s+e_t)=\phi e_s+e_t$ and $\rho(s)\phi(e_s+e_t)=e_s+\phi e_t$ (all from [F1], $\rho(uv)=\rho(u)\rho(v)$ and $\phi^2=\phi+1$) one computes $N(u_1)=\{e_s\}$, $N(u_2)=\{e_t\}\sqcup\rho(t)N(u_1)=\{e_t,\ e_s+\phi e_t\}$, $N(u_3)=\{e_s\}\sqcup\rho(s)N(u_2)=\{e_s,\ e_t+\phi e_s,\ \phi(e_s+e_t)\}$, $N(u_4)=\{e_t\}\sqcup\rho(t)N(u_3)=\{e_t,\ e_s+\phi e_t,\ \phi(e_s+e_t),\ e_t+\phi e_s\}$ and $N(u_5)=\{e_s\}\sqcup\rho(s)N(u_4)=\{e_s,\ e_t+\phi e_s,\ \phi(e_s+e_t),\ e_s+\phi e_t,\ e_t\}=\Phi_+$, the last equality by 3.2. This gives the displayed sets $N(u_1),\dots,N(u_5)$, of cardinalities $k=|N(u_k)|=\ell(u_k)$ as required by [F4]. [F3, F4, F5, step 3.2, algebra]

5.1 **The case $I_2(5)$: the inversion sets $N(u_k)$, $5\le k<10$.** Since $N(u_5)=\Phi_+$ by 4.1, the reflection $\rho(u_5)$ maps $\Phi_+$ bijectively onto $\Phi_-$ (as $N(u_5)=\Phi_+$ means $\rho(u_5)\Phi_+\subseteq\Phi_-$, and $\rho(u_5)$ induces a bijection of $\Phi$); hence for $\alpha\in\Phi_+$: $\alpha\in N(u_5w)$ if and only if $\rho(w)\alpha\in\Phi_+$, because $\rho(u_5)$ maps $\Phi_+$ onto $\Phi_-$ and $\Phi_-$ onto $\Phi_+$. Comparing with the definition of $N(w)$ this gives $N(u_5w)=\Phi_+\setminus N(w)$ for every $w\in W$. For $5\le k<10$ one has $u_k=u_5\,u_{k-5}'$, where $u_j'$ is the alternating word of length $j$ beginning with $t$; hence $N(u_k)=\Phi_+\setminus N(u_{k-5}')$. In particular $N(u_6)=\Phi_+\setminus\{e_t\}$ and, using the recursion of [F3] once more for $u_2'=ts$ (read with the roles of $s$ and $t$ exchanged, $\ell(u_2')=2>\ell(t)=1$), $N(u_2')=\{e_s\}\sqcup\rho(s)N(u_1')=\{e_s,\ e_t+\phi e_s\}$, so that $N(u_7)=\Phi_+\setminus N(u_2')=\{e_t,\ e_s+\phi e_t,\ \phi(e_s+e_t)\}$, of cardinality $3=\ell(u_7)$. 6.1 **The case $I_2(\infty)$: the chambers.** Let $\delta(f):=f(e_s)+f(e_t)$ for $f\in V^*$, so that $\delta(f)=f(u)$; since $\rho(w)u=u$ for all $w$ by 1.3, the value $\delta(f)$ is preserved by the dual action: $\delta(w\cdot f)=(w\cdot f)(u)=f(\rho(w)^{-1}u)=f(u)=\delta(f)$. On $L:=\{f:\delta(f)=1\}$ with coordinate $y_s=f(e_s)$, $C^\circ$ has trace $(0,1)$. By [F1] and the dual action in [F6], $s$ acts by $y_s\mapsto-y_s$ and $t$ by $y_s\mapsto2-y_s$; hence $st$ acts by translation $y_s\mapsto y_s-2$. It follows that $(st)^kC^\circ\cap L=(-2k,1-2k)$ and $(st)^ksC^\circ\cap L=(-2k-1,-2k)$ for every integer $k$. These are all intervals $(j,j+1)$, and all group elements are alternating words by [F5] (cancelling adjacent equal letters); therefore these are exactly the open chamber traces, and no integer point belongs to one. Every chamber is a nonempty cone contained in $\{\delta>0\}$, since this holds for $C^\circ$ and $\delta$ is invariant. Each chamber $K$ equals the cone $\{\lambda x:\lambda>0,\ x\in K\cap L\}$ over its trace, because for $f\in K$ its normalisation $f/\delta(f)$ lies in $K\cap L$ and $f=\delta(f)\,(f/\delta(f))$ with $\delta(f)>0$; hence the chambers are exactly the cones over the intervals $(j,j+1)$. Finally $H_{e_s}\cap L$ is the point with $y_s=0$, and $H_{e_t}\cap L$ is the point with $y_s=1$, because $y_t=1-y_s$ on $L$. Together with 1.1-1.3, 2.1-2.5, 3.1-3.2, 4.1 and 5.1 this verifies all the claims of (i), (ii) and (iii). [F1, F5, F6, step 1.3, algebra] ∎
