## Statement

Let $i\in I$, write $z=q_i$, and let $V(a)$, $V(b)$ be the simple rank-one modules of nonnegative integral highest weights $a,b$ for the subalgebra generated by $E_i,F_i,K_i^{\pm1}$, with divided bases $v_j=F_i^{(j)}v_0$ $(0\le j\le a)$ and $w_k=F_i^{(k)}w_0$ $(0\le k\le b)$ ([[thm-quantized-sl-two-string-formulas]]). Consider the tensor product with the coproduct $\Delta E_i=E_i\otimes K_i^{-1}+1\otimes E_i$, $\Delta F_i=F_i\otimes1+K_i\otimes F_i$ ([[thm-the-drinfeld-jimbo-formulas-define-a-hopf-algebra]]), its Kashiwara operators $\tilde e_i,\tilde f_i$ ([[thm-unique-kashiwara-i-string-decomposition]]) and the $\mathbb Z$-graded $\mathbb Q(q)$-vector space $V(a)\otimes V(b)$ with the tensor lattice
$$L:=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{a}\bigoplus_{k=0}^{b}A_0\,(v_j\otimes w_k)$$
over the local ring $A_0$ ([[def-the-local-ring-at-q-zero]]). Then:

(i) $L$ is a crystal lattice: it is a free $A_0$-module generating $V(a)\otimes V(b)$ over $\mathbb Q(q)$, it respects the weight grading, and $\tilde e_iL\subseteq L$, $\tilde f_iL\subseteq L$.

(ii) Modulo $q$ one has, for all $0\le j\le a$, $0\le k\le b$,
$$\tilde f_i(v_j\otimes w_k)=\begin{cases}v_{j+1}\otimes w_k & \text{if } a-j>k,\\ v_j\otimes w_{k+1} & \text{if } a-j\le k,\end{cases}$$
and
$$\tilde e_i(v_j\otimes w_k)=\begin{cases}v_{j-1}\otimes w_k & \text{if } a-j\ge k,\\ v_j\otimes w_{k-1} & \text{if } a-j<k,\end{cases}$$
where terms leaving the divided-basis ranges are $0$. In particular $\tilde f_ib=b'$ if and only if $b=\tilde e_ib'$ for residues $b,b'$ of the product basis (the partial-inverse property).

(iii) The nonzero residue strings are the chains $(0,t),(1,t),\dots,(a-t,t),(a-t,t+1),\dots,(a-t,b)$, $0\le t\le\min(a,b)$, and they partition the rectangle $0\le j\le a$, $0\le k\le b$.

This is a finite, choice-free calculation, independent of the grand loop and of global integral freeness.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The index $i$, $z=q_i$, nonnegative integers $a,b$, the rank-one modules $V(a),V(b)$ with divided bases, and the tensor product $V(a)\otimes V(b)$ with the coproduct of the statement.

[F1] $V(a)$ has basis $v_0,\dots,v_a$ with $K_iv_j=z^{a-2j}v_j$, $F_iv_j=[j+1]_zv_{j+1}$, $E_iv_j=[a-j+1]_zv_{j-1}$, and divided powers act by $F_i^{(m)}v_j=\binom{j+m}{m}_zv_{j+m}$, $E_i^{(m)}v_j=\binom{a-j+m}{m}_zv_{j-m}$ ([[thm-quantized-sl-two-string-formulas]], parts (i)-(iii)); the same holds for $V(b)$ with basis $w_k=F_i^{(k)}w_0$.

[F2] $\Delta E_i=E_i\otimes K_i^{-1}+1\otimes E_i$ and $\Delta F_i=F_i\otimes1+K_i\otimes F_i$ define the Hopf coproduct, so the tensor product is a module with $\Delta E_i,\Delta F_i$ nilpotent and $K_i$ diagonalisable with the sum of the two weights ([[thm-the-drinfeld-jimbo-formulas-define-a-hopf-algebra]]).

[F3] On each $V(a)$ there is a symmetric bilinear $\sigma$-contravariant form $B_a$, normalized by $B_a(v_0,v_0)=1$, with the divided-string norms $B_a(F_i^{(r)}v_0,F_i^{(r)}v_0)=z^{r(a-r)}\binom{a}{r}_z=:d(a,r)$ for $0\le r\le a$, and $B_a(F_i^{(n)}h,F_i^{(n)}h)=d(m,n)B_a(h,h)$ for every highest vector $h$ of highest weight $m$ ([[lem-cartan-projection-constructs-the-quantum-verma-contravariant-form]]).

[F4] $\operatorname{ord}_z[m]_z=1-m$ for $m\ge1$, and $\operatorname{ord}_z\binom{N}{R}_z=-R(N-R)$ with leading coefficient $1$ for $0\le R\le N$; elements of $\mathbb Q(q)$ of nonnegative order lie in $A_0$, and $d(N,R)=z^{R(N-R)}\binom{N}{R}_z$ lies in $1+qA_0$, hence is a unit of $A_0$ ([[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]], [[lem-quantum-pascal-recurrence-and-gaussian-integrality]], [[def-the-local-ring-at-q-zero]]).

[F5] Every element of the tensor product has a unique admissible $i$-string decomposition, and the Kashiwara operators $\tilde e_i,\tilde f_i$ act on the string of a highest vector $h$ of length $A$ by $\tilde e_iF_i^{(n)}h=F_i^{(n-1)}h$ and $\tilde f_iF_i^{(n)}h=F_i^{(n+1)}h$, with $F_i^{(-1)}h=F_i^{(A+1)}h=0$; $M=\bigoplus$ of its string spaces ([[thm-unique-kashiwara-i-string-decomposition]]).

[F6] Define the symmetric bilinear product form $B(x\otimes y,x'\otimes y'):=B_a(x,x')B_b(y,y')$. The single-factor forms of [F3] ([[lem-cartan-projection-constructs-the-quantum-verma-contravariant-form]]) make the product basis orthogonal with diagonal Gram entries $d(a,j)d(b,k)$. Its tensor contravariance is established locally in step 1.1 from the generator values $\sigma(K_i)=K_i$, $\sigma(E_i)=zF_iK_i^{-1}$, $\sigma(F_i)=z^{-1}K_iE_i$ and the coproduct [F2] ([[thm-the-drinfeld-jimbo-formulas-define-a-hopf-algebra]]); it is not asserted by the single-module Cartan-projection lemma.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct. Construct the rank-one highest vectors of the tensor product by an explicit recurrence, use the $\mathbb Q$ positivity of the residue form to identify the tensor lattice, compute the residues of the divided strings and read off the crystal rule; all steps are finite and choice-free.

1.1 Tensor contravariance and divided coproduct. Since $\Delta$ is an algebra map, $\Delta(\sigma(E_i))=z\Delta(F_i)\Delta(K_i^{-1})=zF_iK_i^{-1}\otimes K_i^{-1}+1\otimes zF_iK_i^{-1}=(\sigma\otimes\sigma)\Delta(E_i)$, and $\Delta(\sigma(F_i))=z^{-1}\Delta(K_i)\Delta(E_i)=z^{-1}K_iE_i\otimes1+z^{-1}K_i\otimes K_iE_i=(\sigma\otimes\sigma)\Delta(F_i)$; the equality for $K_i^{\pm1}$ is immediate. Both $\Delta\sigma$ and $(\sigma\otimes\sigma)\Delta$ reverse products, so equality on generators proves equality on the rank-one algebra. Factor contravariance then gives $B(\Delta(X)(x\otimes y),x'\otimes y')=B(x\otimes y,(\sigma\otimes\sigma)\Delta(X)(x'\otimes y'))=B(x\otimes y,\Delta(\sigma(X))(x'\otimes y'))$ for all $X$; extend by bilinearity to all tensor vectors. This proves the tensor contravariance of the product form. From $\Delta F_i=F_i\otimes1+K_i\otimes F_i$ and the $q$-commutation $(K_i\otimes F_i)(F_i\otimes1)=z^{-2}(F_i\otimes1)(K_i\otimes F_i)$, expanding the $n$-th power gives $\Delta F_i^{(n)}=\sum_{s=0}^{n}z^{-s(n-s)}F_i^{(n-s)}K_i^s\otimes F_i^{(s)}$: the coefficient of $(F_i\otimes1)^{n-s}(K_i\otimes F_i)^s$ in $(\Delta F_i)^n$ is $z^{-s(n-s)}\binom{n}{s}_z$, and division by $[n]_z!$ cancels the Gaussian binomials as $[n]_z!=\binom{n}{s}_z[s]_z![n-s]_z!$. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

1.2 The highest vectors. For $0\le t\le\min(a,b)$ define $c_0=1$ and $c_{l+1}=-c_lz^{b-2t+2l+2}\frac{[b-t+l+1]_z}{[a-l]_z}$ for $0\le l<t$; all denominators are nonzero since $l<t\le a$, and set $h_t:=\sum_{l=0}^{t}c_l\,v_l\otimes w_{t-l}$. Substituting $\Delta E_i$ and comparing the coefficient of $v_{l-1}\otimes w_{t-l}$ for $1\le l\le t$ gives $c_l[a-l+1]_zz^{2(t-l)-b}+c_{l-1}[b-t+l]_z$, which is zero by the recurrence; the coefficients of $v_{-1}\otimes w_t$ and $v_t\otimes w_{-1}$ vanish because those basis vectors do not exist. Hence $\Delta E_ih_t=0$, and $h_t$ has weight $a+b-2t$ since $K_i$ acts on $v_l\otimes w_{t-l}$ by $z^{a-2l}z^{b-2(t-l)}=z^{a+b-2t}$. [F1, F2, algebra]

1.3 The norm criterion. For $x=\sum_{j,k}\alpha_{j,k}v_j\otimes w_k$ one has $B(x,x)=\sum_{j,k}\alpha_{j,k}^2d(a,j)d(b,k)$, so $x\in L$ implies $B(x,x)\in A_0$ because $d(a,j)d(b,k)\in A_0$; conversely, if $B(x,x)\in A_0$ but $x\notin L$, take the minimal $N\ge1$ with $q^Nx\in L$ and let $\overline{x}\neq0$ be its residue in $L/qL$. Then $B(q^Nx,q^Nx)=q^{2N}B(x,x)\in qA_0$, while on residues the form $\overline B$ is diagonal with all entries congruent to $1$ modulo $q$, so $\overline B(\overline x,\overline x)$ is the sum of the squares of the rational residues of the coordinates of $\overline x$, a positive rational, contradicting $\overline B(\overline x,\overline x)=0$. Hence $L=\{x:B(x,x)\in A_0\}$. [F4, F6, algebra]

2.1 Valuations. By [F4], $\operatorname{ord}_zc_{l+1}-\operatorname{ord}_zc_l=(b-2t+2l+2)+(1-(b-t+l+1))-(1-(a-l))=a-t+1$ for every $l$, so the orders telescope to $\operatorname{ord}_zc_l=l(a-t+1)>0$ for $l\ge1$, with $\operatorname{ord}_zc_0=0$; hence $c_l\in A_0$, $c_0=1$ and $c_l\in qA_0$ for $l>0$. Therefore $h_t\equiv v_0\otimes w_t\pmod{qL}$, and by [F6] $B(h_t,h_t)=\sum_lc_l^2d(a,l)d(b,t-l)\equiv d(b,t)\equiv1\pmod q$, since $d(a,l)\in1+qA_0$ and the $l>0$ terms lie in $qA_0$. [F4, F6, step 1.2]

2.2 Coefficients. Put $u_{t,n}:=F_i^{(n)}h_t$ and write $u_{t,n}=\sum_{j,k}\gamma_{j,k}\,v_j\otimes w_k$; by weight, $\gamma_{j,k}=0$ unless $j+k=t+n$. Applying $\Delta F_i^{(n)}=\sum_sz^{-s(n-s)}F_i^{(n-s)}K_i^s\otimes F_i^{(s)}$ of step 1.1 to $h_t$ and using [F1] gives the finite sum $\gamma_{j,k}=\sum_{l}c_l\,z^{-s(n-s)+s(a-2l)}\binom{j}{l}_z\binom{k}{t-l}_z$ with $s=k-t+l$, over the integers $l$ with $0\le l\le\min(t,j)$ and $0\le s\le n$ (the symmetry of the Gaussian binomials rewrites the divided-power coefficients). [F1, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 The tensored strings lie in $L$. Let $A:=a+b-2t$ and $u_{t,n}:=F_i^{(n)}h_t$ for $0\le n\le A$. By [F5](ii), $F_i^{(n)}h_t\ne0$ for $0\le n\le A$; the span of these $A+1$ vectors is generated by $h_t$ and stable under $E_i,F_i,K_i$, so it is the simple rank-one module of highest weight $A$ by [F1], and the form restricted to it is a nonzero contravariant form, hence by [F3] a multiple of the normalized form, with factor $B(h_t,h_t)$; hence $B(u_{t,n},u_{t,n})=d(A,n)B(h_t,h_t)\in A_0$ by step 2.1 and [F4]. The norm criterion of step 1.3 therefore gives $u_{t,n}\in L$. [F3, F4, F5, step 2.1, step 1.3]

4.1 Integrality of the residue. Every summand in step 2.2 is an integer Laurent series in $z$: its factors $c_l$, $[m]_z$ and $\binom{N}{R}_z$ are quotients of Laurent polynomials whose denominator factors $[m]_z$ have leading coefficient $\pm1$, so their inverses are Laurent series with integer coefficients with finitely many negative terms. Since $u_{t,n}\in L$ by step 3.1, each $\gamma_{j,k}$ lies in $A_0$; an element of $A_0$ that is also an integer Laurent series has an integer residue, and the sum of the squares of the residues is $1$ because $B(u_{t,n},u_{t,n})\equiv1\pmod q$ by step 2.1 and [F4], while the Gram entries are congruent to $1$. [step 3.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, F4, algebra]

5.1 Distinguished coefficient. If $n\le a-t$, take $(j,k)=(n,t)$. The summand with $l=0$ is $c_0z^0\binom{n}{0}_z\binom{t}{t}_z=1$; a summand with $l\ge1$ has $s=l$ and valuation $l(a-t+1)+l(a-2l)-l(n-l)-l(t-l)-l(n-l)=l(2a-2n-2t+1)+l^2\ge l+l^2>0$ by the additivity of $\operatorname{ord}_z$ in [F4] (here $a-t-n\ge0$). Hence $\gamma_{n,t}\equiv1\pmod q$. [F4, step 2.2, step 4.1]

5.2 Second distinguished coefficient. If $n\ge a-t$, take $(j,k)=(a-t,\,n-a+2t)$. The summand with $l=0$ is $z^{t(n-a+t)}\binom{n-a+2t}{t}_z$, of valuation $t(n-a+t)-t(n-a+t)=0$ and leading coefficient $1$ by [F4]; a summand with $l\ge1$ and $\binom{j}{l}_z\neq0$, so $l\le a-t$, has $s=n-a+t+l$ and valuation $l(a-t+1)-s(n-s)+s(a-2l)-l(j-l)-(t-l)(k-t+l)=l+l^2>0$, where the exponent identity uses $k-t+l=s$. Hence $\gamma_{a-t,\,n-a+2t}\equiv1\pmod q$. [F4, step 2.2, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 The residues are the chain positions. By steps 5.1-5.2 the distinguished coefficient of $u_{t,n}$ has residue $1$. Since all residues are integers and their squares sum to $1$ by step 4.1, every other coefficient has residue $0$. Hence the residue of $u_{t,n}$ is the single basis element $v_j\otimes w_k$ with $(j,k)=(n,t)$ when $n\le a-t$ and $(j,k)=(a-t,n-a+2t)$ when $n\ge a-t$. [step 4.1, step 5.1, step 5.2]

7.1 Partition into chains. Fixing $t$ and letting $n$ run over $0,\dots,a+b-2t$, the positions of step 6.1 are $(0,t),(1,t),\dots,(a-t,t),(a-t,t+1),\dots,(a-t,b)$. These are the chains of the statement, and they partition the rectangle $0\le j\le a$, $0\le k\le b$: a cell $(j,k)$ lies on the chain of $t=\min(k,a-j)$, and then it is the position $n=j$ if $k=t$ and $n=a-t+k-t$ if $k>t$ (then $j=a-t$), which determines the position uniquely. The total number of vectors $u_{t,n}$ is $\sum_{t=0}^{\min(a,b)}(a+b-2t+1)=(a+1)(b+1)$. [step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 $A_0$-basis. The matrix expressing the family $\{u_{t,n}\}$ in the product basis reduces modulo $q$ to a permutation matrix by steps 6.1-7.1, hence has determinant congruent to $\pm1$ modulo $q$, a unit of $A_0$; the adjugate identity over $A_0$ makes the matrix invertible over $A_0$. Since $\{u_{t,n}\}\subseteq L$ by step 3.1 and both families have $(a+1)(b+1)$ members, $\{u_{t,n}\}$ is an $A_0$-basis of $L$. [step 3.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 The crystal rule. By [F5] the operators act on the strings by $\tilde e_iF_i^{(n)}h_t=F_i^{(n-1)}h_t$ and $\tilde f_iF_i^{(n)}h_t=F_i^{(n+1)}h_t$, with the endpoint terms zero; since the strings form the $A_0$-basis of $L$ by step 8.1, both operators preserve $L$ and shift the residues along the chains of step 7.1. Reading off which tensor factor changes gives the two displayed rules: along $(n,t)\mapsto(n+1,t)$ the first factor moves exactly when $a-(n)>\ t$, that is $a-j>k$, and along $(a-t,k)\mapsto(a-t,k+1)$ the second factor moves, which is the case $a-j\le k$; the $\tilde e_i$-rule is read in the reverse direction, where the first factor moves exactly when $a-j\ge k$. Terms leaving the ranges are $0$. For residues $b=v_j\otimes w_k$ and $b'$: if $a-j>k$ then $\tilde f_ib=v_{j+1}\otimes w_k$ and $a-(j+1)\ge k$, so $\tilde e_ib'=b$; if $a-j\le k$ then $\tilde f_ib=v_j\otimes w_{k+1}$ and $a-j<k+1$, so $\tilde e_ib'=b$; the converse implications follow by the same two evaluations of the displayed rules. Hence $\tilde f_ib=b'$ if and only if $b=\tilde e_ib'$. No choice is used, and neither the grand loop nor any freeness over $\mathbb Z[q,q^{-1}]$ enters. [step 7.1, step 8.1, F5, algebra] ∎
