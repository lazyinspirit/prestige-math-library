## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice for the faithful triangular embedding and the affine kernel/image quotient suppliers ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

Let $k$ be a field and let $G$ be an affine unipotent algebraic group over $k$ ([[def-affine-scheme]], [[def-group-scheme-over-a-field]]). Then there is a central series
$$G=G_0\supseteq G_1\supseteq\dots\supseteq G_r=1$$
of closed subgroup schemes of $G$ whose successive quotients are isomorphic to closed subgroup schemes of $\mathbf G_a$. In particular every unipotent algebraic group is nilpotent and hence solvable.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** AC, a field $k$ and an affine unipotent algebraic group $G$ over $k$.

[F1] Assume AC. $G$ is isomorphic to a closed subgroup scheme of $U_n$ for some $n\ge1$ (the triangular criterion), and the image of any closed subscheme under this inclusion is a closed subscheme of $U_n$. ([[thm-unipotent-group-triangular-criterion]])

[F2] $U_n$ has a central series $U_n=U_n^{(0)}\supseteq\dots\supseteq U_n^{(m)}=1$ of closed subgroup schemes with successive quotients canonically isomorphic to $\mathbf G_a$. ([[lem-upper-unitriangular-central-series]])

[F3] The cited subgroup-commutator definition concerns abstract groups. Here its scheme-theoretic extension is used explicitly: for closed subgroup schemes $A,B$ of an affine group scheme $G$, define $[A,B]$ as the smallest closed subgroup scheme of $G$ through which the commutator morphism $A\times B\to G$ factors. It exists by schematic intersection of all such closed subgroup schemes (on coordinate rings, the sum of their Hopf ideals). Put $\gamma_1G=G$ and $\gamma_{j+1}G=[G,\gamma_jG]$; call $G$ nilpotent if some $\gamma_jG=1$. A descending series is central when each commutator morphism $G\times G_i\to G$ factors through $G_{i+1}$. The required lower-central-series containment is proved in step 3.1, rather than inferred from abstract-group nilpotence. ([[def-subgroup-commutator-and-lower-central-series]], [[def-derived-subgroup-and-solvable-algebraic-group]])

[F4] Under AC, a homomorphism of affine finite-type groups has a closed scheme-theoretic image and identifies the quotient by its scheme kernel with that image. Quotients by closed normal affine subgroups are represented affine fppf quotients. ([[lem-nonaffine-group-image-exact-quotient-properties]], [[thm-nonaffine-affine-normal-group-quotient-affine]])

[F5] The derived subgroup is generated by commutators and the derived series terminates exactly for solvable groups. ([[def-derived-subgroup-and-solvable-algebraic-group]])

## Proof

**Given:** AC, a field $k$ and an affine unipotent algebraic group $G$ over $k$.

1.1 By [F1] fix a closed embedding $j:G\hookrightarrow U_n$. Put $G_i=G\cap U_n^{(i)}$ for $0\le i\le m$, where $U_n^{(i)}$ is the central series of [F2]; the $G_i$ are closed subgroup schemes of $G$ with $G_0=G$ and $G_m=1$. [F1, F2]

2.1 The series is central scheme-theoretically. For every commutative $k$-algebra $R$, the commutator of $g\in G(R)$ and $h\in G_i(R)$ lies in $U_n^{(i+1)}(R)$ by [F2], and it lies in $G(R)$. Hence it lies in $G_{i+1}(R)$, the fibre-product intersection. These factorizations are natural in $R$, so the commutator morphism $G\times G_i\to G$ factors through $G_{i+1}$. The same argument gives conjugation stability of each $G_i$, since $ghg^{-1}=[g,h]h\in G_i(R)$ for every $R$. Thus the terms are closed normal subgroup schemes and $[G,G_i]\subseteq G_{i+1}$ by the minimality definition in [F3]. [F2, F3, step 1.1]

2.2 Restrict to $G_i$ the homomorphism $U_n^{(i)}\to U_n^{(i)}/U_n^{(i+1)}\cong\mathbf G_a$. Its scheme kernel is exactly $G\cap U_n^{(i+1)}=G_{i+1}$. By [F4] its represented quotient $G_i/G_{i+1}$ is the closed image in $\mathbf G_a$. This proves the successive-quotient assertion for nonreduced groups as well. [F2, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 By induction $\gamma_{i+1}G\subseteq G_i$: the case $i=0$ is equality, and if $\gamma_{i+1}G\subseteq G_i$, step 2.1 makes the commutator morphism $G\times\gamma_{i+1}G\to G$ factor through $G_{i+1}$, so its smallest closed subgroup image $\gamma_{i+2}G$ lies there by [F3]. Hence $\gamma_{m+1}G=1$ and $G$ is nilpotent. Likewise $D^0G=G_0$, and if $D^iG\subseteq G_i$, the commutator morphism on $D^iG\times D^iG$ factors through $G_{i+1}$ by step 2.1; the definition of the derived subgroup [F5] gives $D^{i+1}G\subseteq G_{i+1}$. Thus $D^mG=1$, proving solvability. All factorizations were checked on every base algebra, so no smoothness or reducedness is needed. [F3, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
