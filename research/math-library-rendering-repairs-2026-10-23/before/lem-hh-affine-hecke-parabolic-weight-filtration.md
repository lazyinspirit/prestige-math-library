## Statement

Use the definitions of [[def-hh-affine-hecke-parabolic-subalgebras-and-central-characters]] and [[def-hh-affine-hecke-weight-temperedness-and-positive-position]]. Then $H$ is right free over $H_P$ with basis $N_w$, $w\in W^P$, and $1\otimes V$ is an injected copy of every $H_P$-module $V$ in $I_P(V)$. For finite dimensional $V$, induction has the following multiset of generalized $A$-weights, with multiplicities:
$$
\operatorname{Wt}(I_P(V))=\coprod_{w\in W^P}w\operatorname{Wt}(V).
$$
Every irreducible $H_P$-module has its weights in a single $W_P$-orbit. More generally the $H_P$-submodule generated by a generalized $t$-weight space has all weights in $W_Pt$. Temperedness of $V$ is equivalent to temperedness of $I_P(V)$. For $\nu\in E^P$, the rule $\theta_\ell\mapsto e^{\nu(\ell)}\theta_\ell$, $N_s\mapsto N_s$ ($s\in P$) is an automorphism $\psi_\nu$ of $H_P$. An essentially tempered module with exponent $\nu$ is uniquely of the form $\tau\circ\psi_\nu$ for a tempered $\tau$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The above affine Hecke algebra, $P\subseteq B$, and finite dimensional modules.

[F1] Bernstein PBW gives the bases $N_w\theta_\ell$ and $\theta_\ell N_w$ of $H$ and the finite Hecke relations; each simple cross-relation has $fN_s-N_s s(f)\in A$ for $f\in A$ ([[thm-hh-affine-hecke-general-root-data-bernstein-pbw]]).

[F2] Finite Weyl length equals inversion number, positive roots have nonnegative simple-root coordinates, and simple reflections generate parabolic Weyl groups ([[lem-root-datum-combinatorics]]).

[F3] The parabolic algebra, weights, cones and temperedness are as in [[def-hh-affine-hecke-parabolic-subalgebras-and-central-characters]] and [[def-hh-affine-hecke-weight-temperedness-and-positive-position]].

## Proof

**Given:** These data.

1.1 Every right coset of $W_P$ has a minimal-length representative $w$; minimality gives $w(P)\subset R_L^+$, since otherwise right multiplication by a corresponding simple reflection decreases its inversion number. Such a $w$ sends all positive subsystem roots to positive roots. Along a reduced word for $v\in W_P$, inversion numbers therefore increase at each step of $wv$, giving $\ell(wv)=\ell(w)+\ell(v)$. Conversely this equality shows $w$ is the unique minimum in its coset: if $u=wv$ were another minimum its length would be $\ell(w)+\ell(v)$, forcing $v=1$. Thus $W^P$ is exactly this set of representatives. Applying [F1] to finite products and the cross-relations shows $H_P$ has basis $N_v\theta_\ell$ for $v\in W_P$, $\ell\in L$: these elements span the subalgebra because cross-relations and finite Hecke multiplication keep all finite Weyl indices in $W_P$, and are independent as a subset of the PBW basis of $H$. The length-additive factorization $N_{wv}=N_wN_v$ now proves $H=\bigoplus_{w\in W^P}N_wH_P$. Tensoring gives $I_P(V)=\bigoplus_{w\in W^P}N_w\otimes V$, including the injected identity summand, which is $H_P$-stable. [F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 Repeatedly move $f\in A$ through a reduced word for $N_w$ using [F1]. The leading term is $N_w w^{-1}(f)$; each correction omits at least one generator, so after finite Hecke multiplication it is a linear combination of $N_vg_v$ with $\ell(v)<\ell(w)$ and $g_v\in A$. Factoring each $v=up$ with $u\in W^P$, $p\in W_P$, gives $\ell(u)\le\ell(v)<\ell(w)$. The subspaces of $I_P(V)$ spanned by summands with representative length at most $k$ are therefore $A$-stable. Their successive quotients are direct sums indexed by the representatives of length $k$, with $f$ acting on the $w$-summand as $w^{-1}(f)$ on $V$. Commuting invertible operators $\theta_\ell$ can be simultaneously decomposed into generalized eigenspaces: take one lattice basis, split into generalized eigenspaces of the first operator, and successively split each stable summand for the remaining finitely many operators. In an invariant-subspace exact sequence the generalized $t$-weight spaces form an exact sequence, since their projections are polynomials in these commuting operators (finite-dimensional polynomial interpolation). This proves the multiset formula, including multiplicities and exactness under submodules and quotients. [F1, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For $f\in A^{W_P}$ every simple cross-relation in $P$ has zero right side, so $f$ commutes with $A$ and the finite generators, and belongs to the center of $H_P$. Distinct finite $W_P$-orbits in $T$ are separated by such invariants. To see this explicitly for two orbits, on their finite union choose for each ordered pair of distinct points a monomial taking different values, and multiply normalized linear differences to obtain a Laurent polynomial equal to one at a chosen point and zero at all others. Sum these polynomials over the second orbit and average over $W_P$; the resulting invariant is zero on the first orbit and one on the second. On an irreducible finite-dimensional complex module a central operator is scalar: any one of its eigenspaces is a nonzero stable subspace and hence the whole module. The invariant separation just proved therefore forces all its weights into one orbit. Now let $U$ be generated by a generalized $t$-weight space of a module of dimension $d$, and choose a lattice basis $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_r$ of $L$. On that weight space the commuting operators $\theta_{\ell_i}$ are simultaneously upper-triangular with sole eigenvalues $t(\ell_i)$, by the definition of generalized weight space. Hence every $f\in A$ acts there with sole eigenvalue $f(t)$; $f-f(t)$ is nilpotent on a space of dimension at most $d$, so its $d$th power kills the initial weight space. For invariant $f$, centrality then makes the same power kill all of $U$. Any weight $t'$ of $U$ has $f(t')=f(t)$ for every invariant, forcing $t'\in W_Pt$ by separation. [F1, F3, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 If $V$ is tempered, every logarithmic weight belongs to $K_P$. For $w\in W^P$, $wK_P\subset K_B$ because $w(P)$ is positive. The multiset formula proves $I_P(V)$ tempered. Conversely the injected copy of $V$ has logarithmic weights in $K_B$ if $I_P(V)$ is tempered; this alone need not give $K_P$, so additionally apply the formula for the longest minimal representative $w_* = w_0w_{P,0}\in W^P$. Writing a logarithmic weight $\lambda$ as central part plus simple-coroot coefficients, the conditions $\lambda\in K_B$ and $w_*\lambda\in K_B$ imply $\lambda\in K_P$: pair $\lambda$ with every dominant vector $z\in E^P\cap C$, noting that $w_*z=w_0z$ is antidominant, to obtain both signs of the same pairing, and use also all global central vectors. More directly, $\lambda\in K_B$ gives nonpositive simple-coroot coefficients; for $z$ in the relative interior of $E^P\cap C$, $w_*^{-1}w_0z=w_{P,0}z=z$, so $(w_*\lambda,w_0z)=(\lambda,z)$. The left side is nonnegative because $w_*\lambda\in K_B$ and $w_0z$ is antidominant, while $(\lambda,z)\le0$. Equality forces every coefficient outside $P$ to vanish. Thus $\lambda\in K_P$. The existence of this $w_*$ and its positivity on $P$ follows because $w_{P,0}$ reverses subsystem positives and $w_0$ reverses all positives. [F2, F3, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 For $\nu\in E^P$ the character $e^\nu$ is fixed by $W_P$ and takes value one on every root $\beta\in P$. Consequently scaling $\theta_\ell$ by $e^{\nu(\ell)}$ preserves the lattice, finite Hecke, and both ordinary and doubled-root cross-relations: every coefficient root monomial in those relations is fixed, and the two numerator terms acquire the same scalar. Replacing $\nu$ by $-\nu$ is the inverse. Twisting a module by $\psi_\nu$ adds $\nu$ to all its logarithmic weights, so the last assertion follows from the definitions. [F1, F3, algebra] ∎
