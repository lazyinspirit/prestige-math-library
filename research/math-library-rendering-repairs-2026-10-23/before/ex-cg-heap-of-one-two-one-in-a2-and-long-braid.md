## Example

Let $S=\{s_1,s_2\}$ with $m(s_1,s_2)=3$, the Coxeter matrix of type $A_2$, let $W$ be the presented group and let $w_0:=s_1s_2s_1\in W$. Then:

**(1)** The heap $P$ of the word $(s_1,s_2,s_1)$ is the chain $1\prec2\prec3$ with labels $s_1,s_2,s_1$.

**(2)** $(1,2,3)$ is a convex chain of length $3=m(s_1,s_2)$ in $P$ whose labels alternate between $s_1$ and $s_2$, so condition (a) of [[thm-cg-fully-commutative-forbidden-chain-criterion]] (2) fails for $P$. Its covering pairs are $1\lessdot2$ and $2\lessdot3$, whose labels are distinct, so condition (b) holds: here it is the failure of (a), not of (b), that is relevant, and correspondingly the reduced word $s_1s_2s_1$ contains the contiguous braid factor $\langle s_1,s_2\rangle_3$, so clause (1) of the same theorem shows that $w_0$ is not fully commutative. In particular $P$ is not the heap of any fully commutative element.

**(3)** The reduced words of $w_0$ are exactly $s_1s_2s_1$ and $s_2s_1s_2$: both are reduced of length $3$, they are related by the braid relation, and they lie in different commutativity classes, since neither contains two adjacent commuting letters and thus $C(s_1s_2s_1)=\{s_1s_2s_1\}$ and $C(s_2s_1s_2)=\{s_2s_1s_2\}$. This exhibits explicitly the failure of full commutativity detected in (2).

**(4)** The heap $P$ has four order ideals: $\varnothing$, $\{1\}$, $\{1,2\}$, $\{1,2,3\}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Coxeter matrix of type $A_2$ on $S=\{s_1,s_2\}$, the presented group $W$, the word $s=(s_1,s_2,s_1)$ with heap $P=P_s$ and product $w_0=s_1s_2s_1$.

[F1] In the heap of a word, $i\prec j$ is generated by $i<j$ with equal or noncommuting labels; $C(q)$ is the set of words obtained from $q$ by finitely many interchanges of adjacent letters with $m=2$; and an element $x\in W$ is fully commutative when $\mathcal R(x)=C(u)$ for one of its reduced words $u$ ([[def-cg-labeled-word-heap-and-fully-commutative-element]], clauses (2), (5), (6)).

[F2] The relators include $s^2$ and $(st)^{m(s,t)}$ for $m(s,t)<\infty$, so $s_1s_2s_1=s_2s_1s_2$ in $W$ because $m(s_1,s_2)=3$; and if $m(s,t)=2$, then $s t=t s$ because $s^2=t^2=(st)^2=1$ gives $st=(st)^{-1}=ts$ ([[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]).

[F3] (i) Any two reduced expressions of the same element are braid-equivalent, braid moves being replacements of alternating subwords of length $m(x,y)<\infty$ by the other alternating word; (ii) the alternating words of length $q\le m$ in the dihedral subgroup generated by $s,t$ are reduced in $W$ ([[thm-hh-matsumoto-reduced-word-theorem]], clauses (1) and (3)).

[F4] For a word $s$ with heap $P_s$ and product $w$: $w$ is fully commutative if and only if no reduced word of $w$ contains $\langle u,v\rangle_{m(u,v)}$ as a contiguous factor for any distinct $u,v$ with $3\le m(u,v)<\infty$ (clause (1)); and conditions (a) and (b) of clause (2) together are equivalent to "$s$ is reduced and $w$ is fully commutative", conditions (a), (b) being the absence of convex alternating chains of length $m(u,v)$ and of covering pairs with equal labels ([[thm-cg-fully-commutative-forbidden-chain-criterion]]).

[F5] An element $y$ covers $x$ when $x\prec y$ and no $z$ satisfies $x\prec z\prec y$ ([[def-graded-poset-and-rank]]).

## Verification

**Given:** The type $A_2$ Coxeter matrix on $S=\{s_1,s_2\}$ and the word $s=(s_1,s_2,s_1)$ with product $w_0$.

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The heap relations are computed pair by pair from [F1]. Positions $1,2$ carry the noncommuting letters $s_1,s_2$ with $m(s_1,s_2)=3$, so $1\prec2$; positions $2,3$ carry the noncommuting letters $s_2,s_1$, so $2\prec3$; and positions $1,3$ carry the equal label $s_1$, so $1\prec3$ as well. Hence the generating relations are contained in the chain $1\prec2\prec3$, and the transitive closure of all three relations is exactly that chain; its labels are $s_1,s_2,s_1$. By [F5] the covering pairs are $1\lessdot2$ and $2\lessdot3$, since no element lies strictly between consecutive positions of the chain, and their labels $(s_1,s_2)$ and $(s_2,s_1)$ are distinct. [given, F1, F5]

1.2 Reducedness and the commutation classes. By [F3](ii) the alternating words of length $3=m(s_1,s_2)$ in $\langle s_1,s_2\rangle$ are reduced in $W$, so $s_1s_2s_1$ and $s_2s_1s_2$ are reduced of length $3=\ell(w_0)$, and by [F2] they represent the same element $w_0$. The only braid moves between words in $\{s_1,s_2\}$ replace an alternating subword of length $m(s_1,s_2)=3$ by the other alternating word, that is, they exchange the two displayed words; by [F3](i) every reduced word of $w_0$ is braid-equivalent to $s_1s_2s_1$, hence $\mathcal R(w_0)=\{s_1s_2s_1,\ s_2s_1s_2\}$. Neither word contains two adjacent commuting letters, since every adjacent pair is $\{s_1,s_2\}$ with $m(s_1,s_2)=3\ne2$; hence no commutation applies and $C(s_1s_2s_1)=\{s_1s_2s_1\}$, $C(s_2s_1s_2)=\{s_2s_1s_2\}$ are two distinct classes whose union is $\mathcal R(w_0)$. [given, F1, F2, F3]

1.3 The order ideals are the prefixes of the chain $1\prec2\prec3$: a subset $I$ is downward closed exactly when $2\in I\Rightarrow1\in I$ and $3\in I\Rightarrow2\in I$, which gives $\varnothing$, $\{1\}$, $\{1,2\}$, $\{1,2,3\}$ and no further subset. [given, F1]

2.1 The chain $(1,2,3)$ is convex in $P$: it exhausts the three elements of $P$, so there is no element outside it lying between two of its members. It has length $3=m(s_1,s_2)$ and its labels $s_1,s_2,s_1$ alternate between the distinct letters $s_1,s_2$, so it is a convex alternating chain of the forbidden length and condition (a) of [F4] clause (2) fails. Its covering pairs are $1\lessdot2$ and $2\lessdot3$ by 1.1, with labels $(s_1,s_2)$ and $(s_2,s_1)$ distinct, so condition (b) holds. [given, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 Since $s_1s_2s_1$ is a reduced word of $w_0$ by 1.2 and contains the contiguous factor $\langle s_1,s_2\rangle_3$ (its three letters), [F4] clause (1) shows that $w_0$ is not fully commutative. Moreover $P$ is not the heap of any fully commutative element: if $P\cong P_{s'}$ for some $s'\in\mathcal R(w')$ with $w'$ fully commutative, then by [F4] clause (2) applied to the reduced word $s'$, the heap $P_{s'}$ contains no convex alternating chain of length $m(u,v)$ with $3\le m(u,v)<\infty$, while $P$ contains the convex alternating chain exhibited in 2.1 and convexity, length and labels are preserved by the labeled isomorphism, a contradiction. [given, F1, F4, step 1.2, step 2.1] ∎
