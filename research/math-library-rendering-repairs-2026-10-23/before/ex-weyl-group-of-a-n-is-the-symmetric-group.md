## Example

For $n\ge 1$, $W(A_n)\cong S_{n+1}$, acting on the sum-zero subspace of
$\mathbb R^{n+1}$ by permuting coordinates.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $n\ge1$, the sum-zero subspace $E\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+1}$, and the model $A_n=\{\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j:i\ne j\}\subseteq E$.

[L1] The coordinate model of $A_n$ consists of the roots $\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j$ in the sum-zero subspace ([[ex-classical-root-systems-in-euclidean-coordinates]]).

[L2] The Weyl group is generated by the root reflections ([[def-weyl-group-of-a-root-system]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $\rho:S_{n+1}\to O(E)$ be the homomorphism obtained by restricting coordinate permutations to $E$. For $x\in E$, the reflection formula gives $s_{\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j}(x)=x-(x_i-x_j)(\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j)=\rho((ij))x$. Hence [L2] and the fact that the transpositions generate $S_{n+1}$ give $W(A_n)=\rho(S_{n+1})$. [L1, L2, algebra]

2.1 The homomorphism $\rho$ is injective. Indeed, if $\rho(\sigma)$ is the identity on $E$, then for every $i\ne j$ it fixes $\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j$, so $\varepsilon_{\sigma(i)}-\varepsilon_{\sigma(j)}=\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j$; uniqueness of the positive and negative coordinate positions gives $\sigma(i)=i$ and $\sigma(j)=j$. Thus $\sigma=1$, and step 1.1 yields $W(A_n)\cong S_{n+1}$ with the asserted action. [L1, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
