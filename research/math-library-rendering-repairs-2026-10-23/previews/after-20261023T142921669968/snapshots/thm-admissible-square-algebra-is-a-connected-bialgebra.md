## Statement

Assume AC. The mod-two algebra $A$ generated by the Steenrod squares is a connected nonnegatively graded bialgebra. Its coproduct is the algebra homomorphism $\Delta(\operatorname{Sq}^n)=\sum_{i+j=n}\operatorname{Sq}^i\otimes\operatorname{Sq}^j$, with counit $\varepsilon(\operatorname{Sq}^0)=1$ and $\varepsilon(\operatorname{Sq}^n)=0$ for $n>0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; the free associative graded algebra $T$ on the symbols $s_n$, $n>0$, with $s_0=1$; its quotient $\mathcal A_{\mathrm{Adem}}=\mathcal A$ by the two-sided Adem ideal $I$; and the tensor-product algebra structures with the Koszul sign rule (all signs trivial over $\mathbb F_2$).

[F1] The square algebra is the quotient of the free algebra by the homogeneous two-sided Adem ideal, and the quotient-to-operation map is well defined ([[def-mod-two-square-algebra-admissible-sequences-and-excess]], [[thm-adem-relations-for-steenrod-squares]]); external evaluation is faithful on the tensor product of square operations ([[lem-external-evaluation-detects-tensor-square-operations]]).

[F2] Quotients of vector spaces have well-defined linear operations and projections, tensor products decompose over bases and commute with direct sums, and the universal property of the tensor product makes balanced formulas well defined ([[def-quotient-vector-space-and-canonical-projection]], [[prop-quotient-vector-space-operations-and-projection]], [[thm-tensor-products-commute-with-arbitrary-direct-sums]], [[thm-tensor-product-basis-from-bases]], [[thm-unit-isomorphisms-for-module-tensor-products]], [[thm-universal-property-of-module-tensor-products]]); the Cartan formula computes the coproduct on generators ([[thm-cartan-formula-for-steenrod-squares]], [[thm-steenrod-squares-are-well-defined-and-natural]]).

[F3] The admissible-basis theorem gives $\mathcal A_0=\mathbb F_2\cdot1$ and $\mathcal A_i=0$ for $i<0$, and AC supplies the complementary subspace used in the kernel computation ([[thm-admissible-composites-present-the-mod-two-square-algebra]], [[cor-every-vector-space-has-a-basis]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $T$ be the free associative graded algebra on symbols $s_n$, $n>0$, with $s_0=1$. Define an algebra homomorphism $\widetilde\Delta:T\to T\otimes T$, $\widetilde\Delta(s_n)=\sum_{i+j=n}s_i\otimes s_j$, using the graded tensor-product multiplication; over $\mathbb F_2$ all Koszul signs equal $1$. Define $\widetilde\varepsilon(s_0)=1$ and $\widetilde\varepsilon(s_n)=0$ for $n>0$, extending multiplicatively. On generators, $\widetilde\Delta$ is coassociative because both iterates sum once over triples $i+j+k=n$. The two counit identities also hold on generators. Since all maps in these identities are algebra homomorphisms, the identities hold on $T$. [given, F2]

2.1 Let $R_0$ be the set of displayed Adem relation generators, and let $I=(R_0)$ be their two-sided ideal. The admissible-basis identification $T/I\to A$ identifies each $R\in R_0$ with the zero natural operation. For spaces $X,Y$ and classes $x\in H^*(X)$, $y\in H^*(Y)$, repeated application of the published Cartan formula gives $R(x\times y)=(q\otimes q)(\widetilde\Delta R)\cdot(x\otimes y)$, where $q:T\to A$ is the quotient map and the tensor action means external product after applying the two factors. The left side is zero because $R\in R_0$ is an Adem relation. Tensor faithfulness therefore gives $(q\otimes q)(\widetilde\Delta R)=0$. To compute the kernel, use AC and basis extension to choose a complement $C$ with $T=I\oplus C$. Distribution of tensor products over this finite direct sum gives $T\otimes T=(I\otimes I)\oplus(I\otimes C)\oplus(C\otimes I)\oplus(C\otimes C)$. [step 1.1, F1, F2]

3.1 The map $q\otimes q$ kills the first three summands and restricts to the isomorphism $C\otimes C\to(T/I)\otimes(T/I)$ on the last. Hence $\ker(q\otimes q)=I\otimes T+T\otimes I$; write $J$ for this kernel. Since $I$ is a two-sided ideal, $J$ is a two-sided ideal in $T\otimes T$. We have $\widetilde\Delta(R)\in J$ for each $R\in R_0$. Every element of $I$ is a finite sum of terms $xRy$ with $x,y\in T$ and $R\in R_0$, so multiplicativity gives $\widetilde\Delta(xRy)=\widetilde\Delta(x)\widetilde\Delta(R)\widetilde\Delta(y)\in J$. Therefore $\widetilde\Delta(I)\subset J$, and $\widetilde\Delta$ descends to $\Delta_A:A\to A\otimes A$. Also $\widetilde\varepsilon(R)=0$ for every $R\in R_0$ because each generator is homogeneous of positive degree. Since $\widetilde\varepsilon$ is multiplicative, it vanishes on all of $I$ and descends to $A$. The descended maps remain algebra homomorphisms, coassociative and counital. The basis theorem gives $A_0=\mathbb F_2\cdot1$ and $A_d=0$ for $d<0$. Thus $A$ is a connected nonnegatively graded bialgebra, with $\Delta_A(\operatorname{Sq}^n)=\sum_{i=0}^n\operatorname{Sq}^i\otimes\operatorname{Sq}^{n-i}$. No antipode is required by the Hopf-freeness supplier. [step 2.1, F1, F3] ∎
