## Statement

Let P be a pattern of strong winding number ±1. For every oriented knot K, regard P(K) as the pattern obtained by tying K into each strand of P in the companion solid torus. Then this new pattern P(K) also has strong winding number ±1. A pattern obtained from any strong-winding pattern by taking connected sum with a local knot also has strong winding number ±1.

## Statement references

[CDR, Proposition 2.5 and complete proof](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1205.5058v3)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A pattern P, its unknotted-solid-torus closure P(U), strong-winding meridian η, and a knot K inserted as companion.

[F1] Strong winding means the axis meridian normally generates the closure complement group ([[def-satellite-induced-concordance-operator]]).

[F2] The knot exterior has meridian $\mu_K$ and preferred longitude $\lambda_K$ on its boundary torus ([[def-knot-exterior]], [[def-knot-meridian-and-preferred-longitude]]).

[F3] The knot-group meridian normally generates $\pi_1(S^3\setminus K)$: meridional filling gives $S^3$, and van Kampen quotients by the normal closure of $\mu_K$ to $\pi_1(S^3)=1$ ([[def-knot-exterior]], [[def-knot-meridian-and-preferred-longitude]]).

[F4] Connected sum is formed by joining the two knot exteriors along a meridional annulus ([[def-knot-connected-sum-and-prime-knot]]).

## Proof

1.1 Let λ denote the longitude of the pattern solid torus. Filling the outer boundary of the pattern exterior along λ produces $S^3\setminus P(U)$, so the induced epimorphism $\pi_1(ST\setminus P)\to\pi_1(S^3\setminus P(U))$ has kernel normally generated by λ. By [F1], the target is normally generated by the image of η; hence $\pi_1(ST\setminus P)$ is normally generated by η and λ. [F1, given, algebra]

1.2 For a local connected sum with any knot J, the closure-complement group is the amalgamated product of the two knot groups along their common meridian, by cutting at the connected-sum sphere and applying van Kampen. Each knot-group factor is normally generated by its meridian. [F3, F4, algebra]

2.1 Decompose the complement of P(K) along the companion torus into $ST\setminus P$ and $S^3\setminus K$. Van Kampen gives a generating set normally contained in the images of these two groups. The first is normally generated by η and λ by step 1.1; the second is normally generated by $\mu_K$ by [F3]. [F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Under the torus gluing, λ is identified with $\lambda_K$ and η with $\mu_K$. Since $\mu_K$ normally generates the companion-knot group, λ becomes redundant, and η normally generates the entire satellite-complement group. The outer solid-torus meridian for the pattern P(K) is isotopic in the complement to η, so it too normally generates. [F1, F2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The solid-torus meridian normally generates the strong-winding factor by hypothesis; quotienting the amalgamated product by that meridian kills this factor and the common meridian. The other knot-group factor is then killed because its meridian normally generates it. Thus the whole closure-complement group is normally generated by the solid-torus meridian. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
