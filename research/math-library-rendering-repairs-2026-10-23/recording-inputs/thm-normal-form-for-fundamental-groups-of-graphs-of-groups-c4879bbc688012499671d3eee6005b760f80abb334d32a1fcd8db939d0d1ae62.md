## Statement

Fix a graph of groups $\mathcal G$ and a maximal subtree $T$. Every element of
$\pi_1(\mathcal G,T)$ is represented by a reduced closed graph-of-groups
word, and a reduced closed word of positive edge length is nonidentity. A
reduced closed word of edge length $0$ represents the identity exactly when
its unique coefficient is the identity of the corresponding vertex group.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A graph of groups $\mathcal G$ on a connected graph $X$ and a maximal subtree $T$.

[L1] The path group is generated by the vertex groups and oriented edges, subject to the reversal and edge-group conjugacy relations. ([[def-path-group-of-a-graph-of-groups]])

[L2] The relative fundamental group is obtained from the path group by killing the tree edges. ([[def-fundamental-group-of-a-graph-of-groups-relative-to-a-maximal-tree]])

[L3] A closed graph-of-groups word has a closed underlying edge path, and a reduced word forbids immediate backtracking across an edge unless the intermediate coefficient lies in the corresponding edge-group image. ([[def-reduced-graph-of-groups-word]])

[L4] In an amalgamated free product, every element has a unique reduced normal form, and a positive-length reduced word is nonidentity. ([[thm-normal-form-for-free-products-with-amalgamation]])

[L5] In an HNN extension, every Britton-reduced word is nonidentity and every element has Britton normal form. ([[thm-hnn-normal-form-theorem]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** induction.

1.1 If $X$ has no geometric edges, then $\pi_1(\mathcal G,T)$ is the unique vertex group, so every element already has edge length $0$ and the identity clause is immediate. If $X$ has one geometric edge, then either that edge lies in $T$, in which case [L1] and [L2] present $\pi_1(\mathcal G,T)$ as the corresponding amalgamated free product and [L4] gives the required normal form, or it lies outside $T$, in which case [L1] and [L2] present $\pi_1(\mathcal G,T)$ as the corresponding HNN extension and [L5] gives the required normal form. [L1, L2, L4, L5, base]

1.2 Assume the theorem holds for graphs of groups with at most $n$ geometric edges, and let $X$ have $n+1$. If $X$ has an edge $e$ outside $T$, remove that edge. The graph $X\setminus\{e\}$ stays connected because it still contains the spanning tree $T$, and [L1] and [L2] identify $\pi_1(\mathcal G,T)$ with the HNN extension of $\pi_1(\mathcal G|_{X\setminus\{e\}},T)$ having stable letter $e$ and associated subgroups the two images of $G_e$. Otherwise $X=T$ is a tree. Choose a leaf edge $e$ of $T$; removing it splits $X$ into connected components $X_1$ and $X_2$, and $T\setminus\{e\}$ restricts to maximal subtrees $T_1\subseteq X_1$ and $T_2\subseteq X_2$. Unwinding [L1] and [L2] then identifies $\pi_1(\mathcal G,T)$ with the amalgamated free product of $\pi_1(\mathcal G|_{X_1},T_1)$ and $\pi_1(\mathcal G|_{X_2},T_2)$ over $G_e$. [ih, L1, L2]

2.1 In each smaller graph-of-groups fundamental group, the induction hypothesis supplies reduced closed representatives. Applying the amalgam normal form [L4] or the HNN normal form [L5] to the decomposition from step 1.2 and then expanding the smaller representatives back into the original generators gives a closed graph-of-groups word in which the only forbidden simplifications are exactly the backtracking patterns ruled out by [L3]. Hence every element of $\pi_1(\mathcal G,T)$ has a reduced closed graph-of-groups representative. [L3, L4, L5, step 1.2]

3.1 The same normal-form theorems [L4] and [L5] say that a reduced amalgam word or Britton-reduced word is nonidentity whenever it has positive syllable length, and that syllable length $0$ gives the identity only when the remaining base-group coefficient is the identity. Under the identifications of step 1.2, this is exactly the statement that a reduced closed graph-of-groups word of positive edge length is nonidentity, and that an edge-length-$0$ reduced closed word is the identity only when its unique coefficient is the identity in the relevant vertex group. [L4, L5, step 2.1, discharge-induction] ∎

## Remarks

The following two-vertex example separates the two operations in step 1.2.
Orient the tree edge $f$ from $v$ to $w$ and the loop $e$ at $w$ as shown;
$T$ consists of the two vertices and the geometric edge underlying $f$.

The two spans below the graph show the injective edge-group maps; the two
copies of $G_w$ in the lower span denote the same vertex group.

```tikz
\begin{tikzpicture}[every node/.style={font=\large}]
\node[draw,circle] (v) at (0,0) {$v$};
\node[draw,circle] (w) at (6,0) {$w$};
\draw[->] (v) -- node[above] {$f\in T$} (w);
\draw[->] (w) to[out=45,in=135,looseness=6] node[above] {$e\notin T$} (w);
\node (gv) at (0,-2) {$G_v$};
\node (gf) at (3,-2) {$G_f$};
\node (gw) at (6,-2) {$G_w$};
\draw[->] (gf) -- node[above] {$\alpha_f$} (gv);
\draw[->] (gf) -- node[above] {$\alpha_{\bar f}$} (gw);
\node (gwl) at (0,-3.5) {$G_w$};
\node (ge) at (3,-3.5) {$G_e$};
\node (gwr) at (6,-3.5) {$G_w$};
\draw[->] (ge) -- node[above] {$\alpha_e$} (gwl);
\draw[->] (ge) -- node[above] {$\alpha_{\bar e}$} (gwr);
\end{tikzpicture}
```

Setting $f=1$ identifies $\alpha_f(a)$ with $\alpha_{\bar f}(a)$ for
$a\in G_f$, giving the amalgam $A=G_v\ast_{G_f}G_w$.
The remaining stable letter satisfies
$e\alpha_{\bar e}(b)e^{-1}=\alpha_e(b)$ for $b\in G_e$, with both images
viewed in $A$. Thus, in the HNN notation $t\alpha(b)t^{-1}=\beta(b)$,
one takes $t=e$, $\alpha=\alpha_{\bar e}$ and $\beta=\alpha_e$.
For any oriented edge $h$, the immediate reversal $h\,g\,\bar h$ is a
cancellation pattern precisely when
$g\in\alpha_{\bar h}(G_h)$; a reduced word excludes this pattern.
These pictures illustrate the defining relations and the decomposition;
the proof above is independent of them.
