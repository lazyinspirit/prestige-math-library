## Statement

Let $f_i:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}$ be convex and continuously differentiable with $L$-Lipschitz gradients, set $F=n^{-1}\sum_i f_i$, and let $x^*$ satisfy $\nabla F(x^*)=0$. Conditional on the current iterate $x_k$ and table $\alpha_i^k$, assume an independent uniform index $I_k$ and finite second moments. Put

$$S_k=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\|\alpha_i^k-\nabla f_i(x^*)\|_2^2,\quad \bar\alpha_k=\frac1n\sum_i\alpha_i^k,\quad v_k=\nabla f_{I_k}(x_k)-\alpha_{I_k}^k+\bar\alpha_k.$$

Then

$$\mathbb E[v_k\mid\mathcal F_k]=\nabla F(x_k),\qquad \mathbb E[\|v_k\|_2^2\mid\mathcal F_k]\le6L(F(x_k)-F(x^*))+6S_k,$$

and, for the SAGA table replacement in [[alg-saga-finite-sum-method]],

$$
\mathbb E[S_{k+1}\mid\mathcal F_k]\le(1-1/n)S_k+\frac{2L}{n}(F(x_k)-F(x^*)).
$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite-sum assumptions, $x^*$, AC, and the SAGA update order. As stipulated by conditioning on the current iterate and table, $\mathcal F_k$ is the history before drawing $I_k$ and contains the current state: $x_k$ and every $\alpha_i^k$ are $\mathcal F_k$-measurable. For the initialized SAGA process this is the history generated by the past indices $I_0,\ldots,I_{k-1}$, or any larger history containing that state from which $I_k$ is independent.

[F1] [[alg-saga-finite-sum-method]]: $v_k$ uses the old table; after the step, only $\alpha_{I_k}$ is replaced by $\nabla f_{I_k}(x_k)$.

[F2] [[lem-convex-smooth-component-gradient-cocoercivity]]: $n^{-1}\sum_i\|\nabla f_i(x)-\nabla f_i(y)\|_2^2\le2L(F(x)-F(y)-\langle\nabla F(y),x-y\rangle)$.

[F3] [[def-stochastic-gradient-oracle-and-unconstrained-update]]: an iterate process is coordinatewise measurable with respect to the current sigma-algebra; an oracle output is measurable at the next time. Table measurability here is part of the current-state conditioning above, not an assertion of this oracle definition.

[F4] [[def-conditional-expectation-as-an-ae-class]]: conditional expectations are almost-sure classes and assume AC for existence.

[F5] [[lem-conditioning-a-known-variable-and-an-independent-variable]]: an independent integrable index indicator has conditional expectation equal to its mean.

[F6] [[thm-taking-out-what-is-known]]: measurable integrable factors can be taken outside conditional expectation.

[F7] [[thm-basic-algebra-and-order-properties-of-conditional-expectation]]: conditional expectation is linear and preserves finite sums.

[F8] [[def-euclidean-inner-product]]: the inner product is bilinear and $\|z\|_2^2=\langle z,z\rangle$.

[F9] [[thm-cauchy-schwarz-and-the-euclidean-norm]]: $|\langle u,v\rangle|\le\|u\|_2\|v\|_2$.

[F10] [[lem-finite-sum-gradient-is-average]]: $\nabla F$ is the finite average of component gradients.

[F11] [[lem-finite-sum-laws]]: finite sums are additive, obey scaling and splitting, and are monotone termwise.

[A1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: assume AC for existence of the conditional-expectation classes used below.

## Proof

**Given:** The objects and hypotheses in the Statement.

1.1 At initialization the algorithm sets $\bar\alpha_0=n^{-1}\sum_i\alpha_i^0$. If $\bar\alpha_k=n^{-1}\sum_i\alpha_i^k$ and the sampled index is $i$, its one evaluation $g_k=\nabla f_i(x_k)$ and table-mean update give
$$\bar\alpha_{k+1} =\bar\alpha_k+\frac1n(\nabla f_i(x_k)-\alpha_i^k) =\frac1n\sum_{j\ne i}\alpha_j^k+\frac1n\nabla f_i(x_k) =\frac1n\sum_j\alpha_j^{k+1},$$
by the recursion and finite-sum algebra [F11]. Induction proves this maintained-mean identity pathwise at every finite $k$. [F1, F11, given, algebra, induction]

2.1 By [F1], $v_k=\nabla f_{I_k}(x_k)-\alpha_{I_k}^k+\bar\alpha_k$. Conditioned on $\mathcal F_k$, $x_k$ and every $\alpha_i^k$ are known, while each event $\{I_k=i\}$ has conditional probability $1/n$ by [F5]; taking known vectors out by [F6], linearity [F7], the maintained-mean identity [step 1.1], [F10] and [F11] give $\mathbb E[v_k\mid\mathcal F_k]=\nabla F(x_k)$. [A1, F1, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F10, F11, given, algebra]

2.2 Let $g_i=\nabla f_i(x_k)-\nabla f_i(x^*)$ and $h_i=\alpha_i^k-\nabla f_i(x^*)$, so $\bar h=n^{-1}\sum_i h_i=\bar\alpha_k$ by [F10], [F11], [step 1.1], and $\nabla F(x^*)=0$. Then $v_k=g_{I_k}-h_{I_k}+\bar h$. Expanding its square and applying [F9] and $2ab\le a^2+b^2$ gives $\|a+b+c\|_2^2\le3(\|a\|_2^2+\|b\|_2^2+\|c\|_2^2)$; averaging yields $\mathbb E[\|v_k\|_2^2\mid\mathcal F_k]\le3(n^{-1}\sum_i\|g_i\|_2^2+S_k+\|\bar h\|_2^2)$. Also, expanding the finite average and applying vector and scalar Cauchy–Schwarz from [F9] gives $\|\bar h\|_2^2=n^{-2}\sum_{i,j}\langle h_i,h_j\rangle\le n^{-2}(\sum_i\|h_i\|_2)^2\le n^{-1}\sum_i\|h_i\|_2^2=S_k$. The finite-sum expansions use [F11]. [A1, F1, F3, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, F10, F11, given, algebra]

3.1 Apply [F2] with $y=x^*$ to obtain $n^{-1}\sum_i\|g_i\|_2^2\le2L(F(x_k)-F(x^*))$. Substitution in step 2.2 proves $\mathbb E[\|v_k\|_2^2\mid\mathcal F_k]\le6L(F(x_k)-F(x^*))+6S_k$. [A1, F2, F11, step 2.2, given, algebra]

4.1 If $I_k=i$, the table update [F1] changes $S$ by $n^{-1}(\|g_i\|_2^2-\|h_i\|_2^2)$. Averaging over the $n$ equally likely indices gives $\mathbb E[S_{k+1}\mid\mathcal F_k]=(1-1/n)S_k+n^{-2}\sum_i\|g_i\|_2^2$; [F2] bounds the last term by $(2L/n)(F(x_k)-F(x^*))$. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F5, F6, F7, F11, step 3.1, given, algebra] ∎
