## Definition

For a fixed finite alphabet $\Sigma$, the **language-class ladder used on this page** records the
containments
$$ \{\text{regular languages over }\Sigma\}\subseteq\{\text{context-free languages over }\Sigma\}\subseteq\{\text{decidable languages over }\Sigma\}\subseteq\{\text{recognizable languages over }\Sigma\}. $$

Here:

- regular means recognized by some DFA, by
  [[def-regular-language-by-dfa-recognition]];
- context-free means generated by some CFG, by
  [[def-language-generated-by-a-cfg]];
- decidable means decided by some Turing machine, and
- computably enumerable means recognizable by some Turing machine, by
  [[def-language-recognized-and-decided]].

## Remarks

- On this page, "computably enumerable" and "recognizable" are synonymous.

- This four-class ladder is not the classical Chomsky hierarchy. The classical
  hierarchy inserts the context-sensitive languages as its type-$1$ class;
  decidable languages are used here instead as a broader comparison class.

- The hierarchy concerns classes of languages, not classes of machine
  descriptions.

The following diagram summarizes this page-specific comparison for the fixed
alphabet $\Sigma$. Each node is a class of languages over $\Sigma$; the arrows
record the non-strict containments in the Definition.

```tikz
\begin{tikzpicture}[every node/.style={font=\small}]
\node (r) at (0,0) {Regular};
\node (c) at (3,0) {Context-free};
\node (d) at (6,0) {Decidable};
\node (e) at (9,0) {Recognizable};
\draw[->] (r) -- node[above] {$\subseteq$} (c);
\draw[->] (c) -- node[above] {$\subseteq$} (d);
\draw[->] (d) -- node[above] {$\subseteq$} (e);
\end{tikzpicture}
```

The diagram asserts no strictness and does not insert the context-sensitive
class of the classical Chomsky hierarchy.
