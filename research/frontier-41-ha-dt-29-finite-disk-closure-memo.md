# Finite characteristic-disk induction: newly closed ports and exact limits

This is a new construction pass, not another review of the fixed-word infimum route. It changes no shared carrier or controller. The period-frontier item is an interface owned by the period-frontier author; the argument below is complete provided that interface supplies a connected finite frontier for the maximal nested-circle basin, including its regular-boundary alternative. In particular it does not manufacture compactness of a separatrix graph from finite singularity count.

## 1. A cap gives an actual globally compatible transverse product

The earlier local-center-saddle memo's instruction to keep the plaque coordinates of B(x) in every box is not literally a gluing proof: transverse chart transitions can change plaque coordinates. Equality of transverse holonomy values implies equality of leaf labels, not equality of points. Here is a repair.

For one C² cap B:W→L, choose a smooth positively transverse ambient vector field V on a neighborhood of the compact image. Finite local positive fields and a smooth partition of unity give V; positivity is an open convex condition. Its flow Φ_s is smooth. Choose finitely many boxes and a finite subdivision of W subordinate to their inverse images. Tree continuation gives a leaf label z_i(t) in each box. The finitely many remaining edge relations are identity holonomy germs because their B-images are nullhomotopic in L: they come from loops of the parameter disk. Shrink to one common interval where all relations agree as maps.

On each box solve

    z_i(Φ_s(B(x))) = z_i(t)

for the unique small s=s_i(x,t). Its left derivative with respect to s is nonzero. The C² inverse-function theorem and compactness give a uniform interval and a jointly C² solution. On overlaps the two equations specify the same leaf label on the same short V orbit; uniqueness gives exactly the same s. Thus P(x,t)=Φ_{s(x,t)}(B(x)) glues pointwise and in both derivatives. For fixed t its points lie in one leaf by the finite continuation relations, and ∂_tP is transverse. No immersion or injectivity of B is needed.

To obtain the exact old collar, first define B there by projecting f(x) along that same V orbit to the endpoint leaf label. This projection is C² by the identical scalar-root argument. Extend B across the source interior using the single fixed leafwise cap, flattening its seam in an open collar. On the source circuit Γ the section t_C is zero. Construct P on a fixed enlarged block before thinning the exterior collar; compactness then puts its actual section range inside P's interval. Uniqueness of the scalar root gives f(x)=P(x,t_C(x)) exactly on that thinner collar. This fixes both the old nonzero-range issue and the pointwise overlap issue. The statement needs the supplied projection collar; an arbitrary prechosen B and arbitrary nearby f need not lie on the same V orbits.

## 2. Null simple frontier gives the missing cancellation scalar

Let Ω be the nested-circle basin about p and let its frontier Γ be one simple homoclinic loop through q, with no other zero in its closed disk. Its source interior occupies one saddle quadrant: if it occupied three, the two unused characteristic separatrix half-rays would lie inside Ω, whereas every point of Ω other than p lies on a periodic orbit. Uniqueness excludes that possibility.

Suppose the rounded image of Γ is null in its leaf. Fix one leafwise cap. The ambient holonomy germ of Γ is therefore identity on both sides, since leafwise homotopic loops have equal holonomy and a null loop has identity holonomy. Cover q and the compact regular edge of Γ by finitely many boxes. In the q box use u=z∘f, with u(q)=0. Transport this coordinate along the edge by the transverse chart transitions. Identity holonomy makes the returned coordinate exactly u on a two-sided interval. Consequently the finitely many scalar expressions glue to a single C² first integral on an open neighborhood N of Γ, with du≠0 off q and the original nondegenerate saddle Hessian at q up to a nonzero factor.

Choose its sign so u<0 on the basin quadrant. On an inner periodic curve C contained in N, this scalar is a regular coordinate on the ordered period-annulus base. Extend it inward to the center by a strictly increasing C² coordinate on that base, matching u on a full collar of C and matching a positive affine change of the local center function near p. This extension is explicit: take two disjoint endpoint base collars, use the supplied C² period product to choose a scalar base coordinate on the compact interval between them, choose a positive C¹ derivative matching the prescribed endpoint derivatives, and adjust its integral by a positive bump to match the endpoint values. The center affine scale and offset are free, so the total value difference can first be chosen larger than the fixed endpoint-collar integrals. Integration gives the C² coordinate. All transitions are increasing C² diffeomorphisms. It extends the first integral over Ω, has precisely the minimum p, and preserves the first integral on N. In particular u(p)<u(q)=0. Take W to be Ω with a thin exterior regular neighborhood of Γ; it contains no other critical point.

For the *standard source Euclidean metric*, -∇u has two saddle unstable half-rays. Exactly one lies in the basin quadrant; the other lies in the opposite sign-negative quadrant outside Ω. The inside ray cannot leave Ω: u strictly decreases along it, while the whole frontier has value zero. Starting at any negative level it remains in a compact inner disk. If its limiting value were greater than u(p), a compact regular level band would have |∇u|≥m>0, contradicting d u/dτ=-|∇u|²≤-m². Hence its value tends to u(p). Any accumulation point then has value u(p), which occurs only at p, so the trajectory tends to p. The other ray is regular immediately after leaving a small saddle chart and crosses a short transverse exit section before reaching any other singularity. Thus the precise Euclidean-gradient hypothesis in the existing conditional cancellation item is genuinely supplied; an arbitrary characteristic vector field was never called a gradient.

The scalar just constructed and the cap-product section t_C may use different coordinates, but their differentials annihilate the same source line field on the connected regular outer collar. Their local increasing transition functions agree on overlaps by the same two-sided identity-return relation. Reparameterize u on that collar by this transition, then extend inward using the positive-derivative construction above. Near q use the corresponding increasing reparameterization of z∘f. The resulting scalar is exactly t_C on the old outer collar, not merely a first integral with the same level sets. The Euclidean-gradient branch conclusions are unchanged because a positive scalar reparameterization multiplies ∇u by a positive function.

The local-center-saddle memo now applies literally: smooth the inner scalar in C² by finite convolution, use its adapted field and auxiliary annular triad with one attaching/belt intersection, cancel with support around the chosen q-to-p arc inside W, blend back through a regular annulus, and compress the scalar range by an increasing map fixing the old collar range. The map P(x,v(x)) removes exactly p,q and equals f on an open outer collar. The final map need not be homotopic to the old interior map, which is immaterial: its original prescribed outer boundary remains fixed.

## 3. Actual C² saddle ports and the correct rounding statement

The saddle port issue does not require parameterizing hyperbolas through their singular limit. Choose short regular transverse sections through the two branches of a chosen passage, away from q. In the target foliation box their scalar z∘f restricted to each section has nonzero derivative. The C² scalar-root theorem therefore gives the endpoints as C² functions of t, including t=0. Along each compact regular edge, finite first-integral boxes produce a jointly C² strip: choose its source tangent parameter in the regular kernel direction and solve z∘f=t for its transverse coordinate. On overlaps use the exact coordinate transitions, and keep common open parameter collars. All port derivatives transverse to the ambient foliation are nonzero.

For a finite circuit itinerary, its corresponding nearby periodic orbits force the *one-sided* composite transverse return map to be identity on an interval. This is stronger than a converging sequence of unrelated identity germs. Each passage is replaced in the target plaque by a C² joining path, with a jointly C² correction on the two regular port collars. Explicitly, if E(θ) is the endpoint joining path and Y_±(θ,t) the prescribed collar traces, set

    A(θ,t)=E(θ)+χ_-(θ)(Y_-(θ,t)-Y_-(θ,0))
                    +χ_+(θ)(Y_+(θ,t)-Y_+(θ,0)).

Choose the two cutoffs supported in disjoint larger collars. For small t this stays in a convex plaque disk, agrees exactly on the smaller open collars, and has z=z(t) with z'(t)≠0. The patch is leafwise homotopic to the original saddle passage rel ports by convex interpolation. The return identity closes the complete trace. Different saddle visits use the C² nonzero-derivative transverse transition z_j(t); no unjustified common numerical chart coordinate is assumed.

A leafwise immersed endpoint representative can be obtained, when needed, by first making small leafwise perturbations of *all* endpoint regular arcs and corner paths inside finitely many plaque disks. A compact interval path in a two-dimensional plaque can be replaced by a finite polygonal path with nonzero edges and rounded corners with nonzero velocity; choose the finitely many vertices so adjacent directed edges are not opposite, preserving common collars chosen for this replacement. Make the same modification to each positive-t strip by its plaque coordinates. It preserves the leafwise classes and its transverse parameter. Immersion at t=0 persists for sufficiently small t by compactness and C¹ continuity. Exact equality to the raw f-images on the original regular arcs is unnecessary and is not asserted.

The current rounding Statement should be repaired from “family of prescribed characteristic level loops” to “family leafwise freely homotopic, for each positive parameter, to the prescribed characteristic level loops, obtained by these tracked plaque replacements.” A hyperbola family in the saddle model xy=t has no jointly differentiable parametrization through the original saddle corner with the required endpoint regularity. The true class-preserving trace is exactly what the vanishing-cycle definition and cap persistence consume. This is a local correction of an overly literal auxiliary formulation; the main vanishing-cycle theorems are preserved.

## 4. Finite selection and full disk theorem

Use the earlier disk-closure draft's three geometric additions: one-quadrant disk index count, strict inner-disk search for a nested two-loop frontier, and finite rank. Their role is now upgraded: each null simple endpoint supplies all ports of §2, rather than leaving the global scalar as an assumption.

The period-frontier interface yields the frontier of the maximal center annulus. Distinct singular ambient leaves prevent a connected characteristic frontier from containing different saddle vertices. A one-saddle graph has at most two homoclinic edges. The basin is an increasing union of its bounded periodic disks, hence a whole bounded complementary component of that graph. A side-by-side two-loop graph has separate bounded components, so one center basin has only one lobe as its frontier. A nested pair has a one-quadrant inner bounded disk K containing a center, by the inward boundary-degree calculation. Search from a center in K regardless of the two image lobe classes. Its trajectories cannot cross the invariant boundary. Any further nested pair has its saddle strictly inside K; its inner disk K' excludes that saddle and therefore has strictly fewer interior saddles. If a later frontier reaches ∂K it is that simple circuit. This is source topology and uses no inherited essential boundary class.

For a selected maximal annulus, near-center loops are null in a plaque. If any regular loop is essential, the first-essential-parameter lemma gives a vanishing cycle using the C² period trace. Otherwise all regular loops are null. An essential simple saddle endpoint gives a vanishing cycle using §3. A null simple endpoint gives one fixed cap and §2 cancellation. A null regular endpoint has trivial two-sided ambient holonomy; a regular source neighborhood then has a closed-orbit band across the endpoint, contradicting maximality. An essential regular endpoint gives the same first-essential trace. An outer transverse boundary cannot be the limit of periodic circles in its regular transverse collar. A regular leafwise boundary is the prescribed essential endpoint. An isolated outer center is impossible in a disk: a small punctured center neighborhood is foliated by circles, so joining this cap to the original center cap and intervening product annulus would form an open and closed compact surface without boundary inside the connected source disk. Equivalently their regular annulus has a circle at each end which is filled on opposite sides, producing a compact boundaryless two-dimensional submanifold of the interior disk; an inclusion is open locally, so compactness makes it closed, a contradiction.

Rank is (S,s(K)), lexicographically, where S is the total saddle count in the current full disk. Source descent lowers s(K). Null simple cancellation lowers S exactly by one, leaves the original outer collar fixed, and removes no other zero. Re-separate the finitely many remaining singular images rel that collar. At S=0 there is still a center by c-s=1, and the preceding regular/boundary alternatives yield an essential endpoint. Thus the full disk theorem follows in finitely many cancellations. Every regular annulus extension is absorbed into the *one maximal family* supplied by the frontier interface; no artificial iteration over ever smaller regular bands occurs.

Both wrappers now have short complete routes: an ambiently null closed transversal spans a relative C² characteristic disk, so apply the transverse-boundary disk alternative; a nonzero element of ker[π₁(L)→π₁(M)] is represented by a C² leaf loop spanning an ambient C² disk, so apply the essential-leafwise-boundary alternative. Neither ambient spanning disk is a leafwise cap.

## 5. Haefliger minimality is not a consequence of changing the disk

The actual minimal-cycle assertion concerns the cycles of the *original* characteristic disk. The above induction changes its interior map. A nonidentity cycle found in a replacement disk need not be a cycle of the original disk. Nor does an essential endpoint of the original center annulus automatically have nonidentity holonomy on its opposite side. It is therefore invalid to use finite cancellation to certify that unchanged assertion.

No new lower-bound proof for all original-disk nonidentity cycles is supplied by this pass. Keep that richer auxiliary Statement recorded with proof not-supplied, and remove its dependency edge from the nulltransversal wrapper, whose full conclusion is now supplied by the independent finite-disk induction. This is not weakening the main result or deleting the Haefliger claim. The exact missing original-disk minimality clause remains a source-cycle compactification/lower-bound theorem preserving a nonidentity germ at the limit. No repeated fixed-word/infimum research is proposed.

## 6. New geometric nested-cycle bound closes original-disk minimum selection

This section supersedes §5's provisional stop. It uses the *new* geometric interface sent by the period-frontier author: decreasing periodic Jordan curves inherit a one-intersection property on every sufficiently short everywhere-transverse section. It does not assume the earlier unproved arbitrary-chain bound.

First take a trajectory entering the transverse disk boundary, outside the stable separatrices of the finitely many saddles. Its omega limit cannot contain a center: a sufficiently small invariant center circle cannot be crossed. The local generalized Poincaré–Bendixson argument gives a periodic orbit or finite saddle graph. Separation of singular ambient leaves reduces a connected graph to one saddle. The return word along the orbit's eventual graph itinerary is nonidentity: if it were identity on a full interval, the actual characteristic return on the section would be identity and sufficiently late iterates of the approaching orbit would instead be periodic. For two homoclinic edges the word factors into the two lobe words; if both were identity their composite would be identity. Thus there is a simple regular or homoclinic source cycle with nonidentity ambient holonomy.

Let A be the infimum of areas of bounded domains of all such simple cycles in the *unchanged* source disk. There are only finitely many singular simple cycles: each is a homoclinic orbit based at one of finitely many saddles, and each saddle has four half-branches. If one of these attains A, use it. Otherwise choose regular cycles with area tending to A, using AC_ω. Each bounded regular-cycle disk contains a center by the ordinary boundary-degree index count. There are finitely many centers; an infinite subsequence contains the same center p. Two characteristic circles containing p are disjoint and nested, because trajectories do not cross and their bounded Jordan domains intersect. Extract a decreasing subsequence: the areas converge to the infimum, so if no minimum is already attained, there are arbitrarily later strictly smaller areas. Call the resulting closed disks K_n and their circles C_n.

A fixed sufficiently small center neighborhood is contained in every K_n: a C_n cannot cross its invariant circles and cannot lie entirely inside that neighborhood, since the image of each such small circle is plaque-null and has identity ambient holonomy. Hence K=intersection K_n has nonempty interior and positive area.

Obtain a Hausdorff subsequence of C_n by finite rational square covers of the compact disk and a deterministic diagonal selection of the finitely many occupied cells at each mesh; this requires only the already declared AC_ω and compact Euclidean completeness. Its limit G is invariant under the characteristic C¹ flow by uniform dependence on initial points on compact time intervals. It is connected. Every short transverse section in a regular flow box meets each C_n at most once: all oriented crossings have the same sign, whereas Jordan entry/exit crossings must alternate. At a regular limit point, the flow-box projection of approaching points gives a unique approaching section intersection, and hence exactly one local regular arc of G. The nested inside sides converge to the inside side of that arc; this identifies G with ∂K at every regular point. At a saddle, the local first-integral levels and the four port sections give only the incident separatrix half-branches; no other regular arc can accumulate there without meeting one of those sections. Center charts are excluded as above. Thus G=∂K.

The limit has no separate regular periodic component unless it is that whole periodic orbit: such a component is locally open in G by its flow-box circle cover and closed by compactness, whereas G is connected. A regular edge cannot have an additional nonsingular limiting component because each regular limit point has only the one flow-box arc; equivalently the local PB proof applies using the inherited one-section-point property. Therefore each nonperiodic edge has saddle alpha/omega endpoints. There are finitely many saddle half-branches, so G is a finite graph. Internal chain transitivity also follows directly: approximate two points of G by points on the same C_n, follow that periodic orbit in finitely many flow-time segments, and project its points to G with uniform flow dependence. The terminal-SCC trapping argument from the period author then makes G strongly connected. Separation reduces it to one saddle, with one or two homoclinic edges.

Now trim each regular edge and place one short transverse section at its midpoint. For large n, C_n hits every such section at least once by Hausdorff convergence and the local flow-box projection, and at most once by the Jordan crossing argument. It therefore traverses each edge exactly once. The finitely many saddle ports leave only finitely many circuit itineraries; take a subsequence with one itinerary. Cover the trimmed edges by fixed finite ambient plaque chains. At each saddle, its small source neighborhood maps into a single ambient foliation box, so replace the passage there by a reference plaque path with its two port endpoints. Every actual passage has that same plaque transverse label. Thus all C_n have, after their specified transversal identifications, the holonomy germs of *one* return word H at parameters t_n tending to zero. This proves the geometric word bound rather than assuming it. If H were identity at zero on an interval, it would be identity at all sufficiently late t_n, contradicting the nonidentity of C_n. Therefore H is nonidentity at zero.

If G is a regular circle or a simple homoclinic loop, it is the required limiting nonidentity cycle. If it has two edges, at least one simple lobe has nonidentity word. Its entire bounded Jordan domain is contained in each K_n: its boundary lies in K_n, while the connected unbounded exterior of K_n is disjoint from that boundary and lies on its unbounded side. This argument does not assume the lobe's image is null. The limiting domain has area at most lim area(K_n)=A; the infimum definition gives area at least A, so it attains A. For this area inequality no measure-continuity theorem is needed: a bounded Jordan domain contained in every K_n has its area bounded by each area(K_n). Every strictly interior nonidentity simple-cycle domain would have strictly smaller area, since the two distinct boundaries leave a nonempty open source region between them. Thus this cycle is inclusion-minimal among the original source cycles. There is no modification of the disk in this proof.

For regular cycles and one-sector bounded homoclinic cycles, minimality gives identity on the inward side without the false claim that all interior trajectories are closed. Suppose the inward return germ were nonidentity. Choose a nonfixed parameter in an interval of that nonidentity set. Orient time so the corresponding characteristic near-cycle drifts inward. Follow its one circuit and close the short transverse displacement by the section; smooth the two joins on the inward side to obtain a source trapping Jordan curve inside the selected cycle. Its field is tangent along the long arc and points strictly inward on the short closing section. Standard first-exit uniqueness therefore gives a compact positively invariant inner disk for that time orientation. Choose a point in the incoming section outside all stable separatrices of saddles for that orientation. Such exceptional points are countable: each of finitely many separatrix trajectories has discrete section intersections, coded by rational intervals. A real interval is not countable, so a nonexceptional point exists. Its omega limit cannot be a center or an isolated saddle; PB and the finite-graph/return-word argument from the initial existence step give a nonidentity simple cycle strictly inside the trapping disk. This contradicts minimality. Hence the inward germ is identity. Since the full germ is nonidentity, its opposite-side germ is nonidentity. This proves the inward-identity conclusion in those two cases, while permitting interior saddle/center dynamics as required by the existing countermodel.

The trapping construction uses the actually realized local return itinerary. At a homoclinic corner, the one-sector or multi-sector passage is taken through the relevant source saddle quadrants and then trimmed to regular ports; it is not an assertion that an arbitrary opposite-side holonomy map is the source return. On the bounded source-domain side the return is realized on exactly the inward displacement interval under discussion, and nonidentity supplies the strict transverse closing displacement. If that interval has no inward source passage, the ambient return must instead be tested by the finite plaque port fence; this is the exact realization clause needed for the last paragraph and should be stated explicitly in the Haefliger carrier.

## 8. The three-sector port closes by the global area minimum

This is the root-requested precise local construction and supersedes §7's residual stop. The selected source cycle P minimizes the area of its bounded Jordan domain among *all* source simple cycles with nonidentity full ambient holonomy. Thus a nonidentity cycle with a smaller domain is impossible even when it shares the boundary saddle q. This stronger fact is essential.

Suppose the bounded side of the simple homoclinic P occupies three saddle quadrants. Let b be the unused unstable half-trajectory from q on this bounded side. Source uniqueness keeps b inside the bounded domain. Its closure is compact. A center cannot be its omega limit or belong to that limit: a small periodic center circle is invariant, and a trajectory cannot cross it. A singleton saddle r different from q is impossible, since it would make b a q-to-r connection, contradicting distinct singular ambient leaves. A periodic omega orbit lies strictly inside P. Its attracting return germ is nonidentity: an identity return on a neighborhood would close b into a periodic orbit, contradicting its alpha limit q. This gives a smaller nonidentity cycle, contradicting global minimal area.

For the remaining nonsingleton omega alternative, generalized PB and the finite branch argument give a finite connected saddle graph. Distinct singular leaves give only one saddle r. If r≠q, the graph cannot touch P: a regular touch would include the trajectory P and hence q, while a singular touch is q itself. It is therefore strictly inside P. The orbit approaching the graph follows a finite saddle-port itinerary, with an actual characteristic return given by the ambient edge word and single-box saddle passages. If this word were identity on a neighborhood, the approaching orbit would be periodic. Thus the word is nonidentity; if there are two homoclinic edges at r, at least one lobe word is nonidentity. That simple cycle is strictly inside P and again has smaller area.

It remains to exclude a nonsingleton omega graph at q. There are only four half-branches. A graph containing the unused unstable branch contains a regular point of b. The regular-edge clause of generalized PB says that such an omega-graph trajectory has a saddle as its forward endpoint. Applied to b itself it would force b to tend that saddle, making omega(b) a singleton rather than the proposed graph. Consequently a nonsingleton graph at q can contain only the used branches, and is P itself.

But omega(b)=P is incompatible with the three-sector incidence. Choose fixed small incoming and outgoing saddle port sections in C¹ Morse coordinates u=xy, so P uses one adjacent incoming/outgoing pair and its bounded side is the three-quadrant side. A sequence of b-points approaching a regular point of the incoming P branch flows to the incoming port by regular compact-edge transport. On the bounded side its small nonzero u-level enters the adjacent *interior* quadrant; the hyperbola passage exits at the unused unstable port. Its exit point tends the regular b-port point as u tends zero (on x=δ the second coordinate is u/δ). Since omega sets are closed and invariant under passage with these compact ports, that b-port point belongs omega(b). It does not lie on P, a contradiction. The divergent passage time at q is harmless: the exit times tend to infinity and the exit points converge, exactly the omega-limit criterion. No ambient/source holonomy identification is used in this exclusion.

Therefore b tends q along a stable half-branch. It cannot tend q along the used stable branch, because that regular branch already belongs to the trajectory P; uniqueness would identify b with P although their unstable half-branches differ. It must return along the unused stable branch. Hence the two unused branches form a homoclinic loop Q wholly inside the bounded P-domain. The bounded Q-domain lies in the bounded P-domain by Jordan separation: the connected unbounded complement of the latter is disjoint from Q and lies on its unbounded side. Q's bounded side is its one-quadrant side; its three-quadrant side would contain the exterior quadrant of P, which is impossible for a contained domain. Q has strictly smaller area than P, since the domains are distinct and their difference contains a nonempty open region. Global minimality therefore implies that Q's *full two-sided* ambient holonomy germ is identity. This conclusion uses no leafwise nullity of Q.

Trim the two loops at the four fixed saddle ports. In the pinched region between P and Q the source hyperbola pairing connects a P-port to a Q-port and then the other Q-port back to the remaining P-port. The regular edge maps are the fixed C² holonomy continuations. Each local saddle passage stays in one ambient plaque and has the identity transverse map in that q-box coordinate. Thus the actual characteristic return on a regular P-edge section on its bounded side has word

    H_P composed with H_Q or H_Q^{-1},

up to the one fixed transversal conjugacy and order convention. Since H_Q is the identity on a full interval, this source return equals H_P on its inward interval. This is the missing realized-return port. It was proved by constructing and eliminating the auxiliary loop, rather than by directly calling the inward ambient P-word a source return.

Suppose H_P were nonidentity inward. Pick a sufficiently small nonfixed parameter in an open nonfixed interval, and orient time so its return displacement is toward the pinched interior. The corresponding one-circuit characteristic segment through the P-and-Q edge strips is simple: a self-intersection would make it periodic before its nonfixed return. Close its two distinct section endpoints by the short transverse section interval. No other intersection occurs with that interval after shrinking the finite graph strips and the section: the realized first-return segment has no intermediate section hit. The resulting piecewise-regular Jordan curve lies strictly inside the bounded P-domain. On its characteristic part the field is tangent; on the short closing section it points into its bounded disk for the chosen time orientation. At the two joins, round inside arbitrarily small regular flow boxes using curves whose inward field component is nonnegative. Uniqueness/first-exit then gives a compact positively invariant source disk strictly inside P. This disk does not contain q: q lies on the boundary of the larger P-domain, while a Jordan curve in its interior has its whole bounded domain there.

Choose an entering trajectory in this trapping disk which is not a saddle stable separatrix for the chosen time orientation. There are finitely many separatrices and each has discrete crossing times on its real time domain, hence countably many section intersection points, so such a point exists in the open nonfixed section interval. Center limits are excluded by the invariant-circle argument. PB yields a periodic orbit or finite one-saddle graph strictly inside P. Its approaching-return word is nonidentity as above; in the graph case at least one simple lobe has nonidentity word. This gives a smaller-area nonidentity simple cycle, contradicting the selected global minimum. Therefore H_P is identity on the inward half-transversal. Its full germ was nonidentity, so it is nonidentity on the opposite side.

Combined with the regular-cycle and one-sector case from §6, this proves the full original-disk Haefliger minimum one-sided-cycle conclusion. Interior centers and saddles may remain; the false all-interior-trajectories-closed assertion is neither stated nor used. All selections remain finite or countable under AC_ω. The three-sector construction uses global area minimality, source saddle pairing, PB regular-edge endpoints, and the full identity germ of the smaller auxiliary Q; it never needs a cap for Q.
