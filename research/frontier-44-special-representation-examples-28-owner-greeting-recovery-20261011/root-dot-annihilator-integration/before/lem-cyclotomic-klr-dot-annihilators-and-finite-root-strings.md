## Statement

Let $k$ be any field and let $Q$ satisfy (Q1)-(Q4) for a real symmetrizable
Cartan datum with finite color set. For monic $a_j(u)\in k[u]$ of degree
$\lambda_j\ge0$, impose $a_{\nu_1}(x_1)e(\nu)=0$ and write $R^a(\beta)$ for the
resulting ordinary quotient. Then: every dot has a scalar-coefficient monic
annihilator in each fixed block; every $R^a(\beta)$ is finite dimensional over
$k$; and, for fixed $\beta$ and $i$, $R^a(\beta+m\alpha_i)=0$ for all
sufficiently large $m$. Thus right-append induction has finite $i$-strings. In
the standard homogeneous specialization $a_j(u)=u^{\lambda_j}$, every dot is
nilpotent and these are statements about the induced graded algebra and graded
functors. For an inhomogeneous monic specialization the assertions are
ungraded; no graded highest-weight Grothendieck identification is included.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$, a KLR datum $Q$ for a real symmetrizable Cartan datum
with finite color set $I$, monic polynomials $a_j\in k[u]$ of degrees
$\lambda_j\ge0$, and the quotient $R^a(\beta)$ of
[[def-cyclotomic-klr-quotient-at-a-dominant-weight]] for the monic
specialization $(a_j)$.

[F1] The KLR algebra $R_n(Q)$ is generated by idempotents $e(\nu)$, dots
$x_a$ and crossings $\tau_a$ subject to relations (1)-(7) of
[[def-quiver-hecke-klr-algebra-for-a-symmetrizable-cartan-datum]]: in
particular $\tau_a^2e(\nu)=Q_{\nu_a\nu_{a+1}}(x_a,x_{a+1})e(\nu)$; for
$\nu_a=\nu_{a+1}$ one has $\tau_ax_a-x_{a+1}\tau_a=e(\nu)$ and
$\tau_ax_{a+1}-x_a\tau_a=-e(\nu)$; for $\nu_a\ne\nu_{a+1}$ one has
$\tau_ax_a=x_{a+1}\tau_a$ and $\tau_ax_{a+1}=x_a\tau_a$; the $x_a$ commute,
and the far commutation and braid relations hold. The polynomials $Q_{ij}$
are homogeneous of degree $-2b_{ij}$ for $\deg u=2d_i$, $\deg v=2d_j$, and
the two extremal coefficients of $Q_{ij}$ are nonzero (Q3)-(Q4)
([[def-quiver-hecke-klr-algebra-for-a-symmetrizable-cartan-datum]],
[[def-klr-grading-and-diagrammatic-composition]]).

[F2] $R(\beta)$ has the PBW basis of [[thm-klr-pbw-basis-theorem]]: for a
fixed reduced-expression system, the monomials $\tau_wx_1^{a_1}\cdots
x_n^{a_n}e(\nu)$ with $w\in S_n$, $a_r\ge0$, $\nu\in I^n$, and
$\sum_r\alpha_{\nu_r}=\beta$, form a $k$-basis of $R(\beta)$; equivalently the
monomials $x_1^{a_1}\cdots x_n^{a_n}\tau_we(\nu)$ form a basis.

[F3] For a single color $i$, the subalgebra $R(n\alpha_i)$ is the nil-Hecke
algebra generated by $k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ and $\tau_1,\dots,\tau_{n-1}$ with
$\tau_a^2=0$, the braid and far commutation relations and the nil-Hecke dot
moves; $\partial_{w_0}=\tau_{i_1}\cdots\tau_{i_{\binom n2}}$ for a reduced
expression of $w_0$ is independent of the reduced expression, the element
$x_1^{n-1}x_2^{n-2}\cdots x_{n-1}\partial_{w_0}$ is a nonzero idempotent, and
the monomials $x_1^{a_1}\cdots x_n^{a_n}\tau_w$ form a $k$-basis
([[def-divided-power-klr-idempotents-and-projectives]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** propagate one explicit first-strand annihilator to every
position by the equal-colour mixed relations and a determinant elimination over
$k[x]/g$, read finite-dimensionality off the PBW basis, then bound $i$-strings
by an induction on the number of non-$i$ letters.

1.1 The first-strand annihilator. Let $g_1:=\prod_{j\in I}a_j\in k[u]$, monic of degree $\Lambda:=\sum_{j\in I}\lambda_j$. For every $\nu$ and every block, $a_{\nu_1}(x_1)e(\nu)=0$ by definition of the quotient, and $g_1$ is divisible by $a_{\nu_1}$, so $g_1(x_1)e(\nu)=0$ for all $\nu$: the monic polynomial $g_1$ with scalar coefficients annihilates the first dot $x_1$ in every block, with the convention that a zero corner satisfies the identity trivially. [given, F1]

1.2 Equal-colour exchange. Suppose a one-variable polynomial $f$ satisfies $f(x_a)e(\nu)=0$ on a block with $\nu_a=\nu_{a+1}=j$; write $x=x_a$, $y=x_{a+1}$ and put $\kappa_a:=-\tau_a$. Then $\kappa_a^2=\tau_a^2=Q_{jj}(x,y)=0$ because $Q_{jj}:=0$ (Q1), and from the equal-colour dot moves $\kappa_ax=y\kappa_a-e$ and $\kappa_ay=x\kappa_a+e$ one computes $\kappa_af(x)=f(y)\kappa_a+\partial f$ and $\kappa_af(x)\kappa_a=f(y)\kappa_a^2+\partial f\kappa_a=\partial f\kappa_a$, where $\partial f:=(f(y)-f(x))/(x-y)\in k[x,y]$ is the divided difference and $f(y)$ is the same one-variable polynomial evaluated at $y$. Applying the identity $(x-y)\kappa_af\kappa_ae(\nu)=(s_af-f)\kappa_ae(\nu)=(\kappa_af-\partial f-f\kappa_a)e(\nu)$ to a module on which $f(x)e(\nu)$ acts as zero, the terms containing $f$ vanish and give $\partial f e(\nu)M=0$, whence $f(y)e(\nu)M=(f(x)+(y-x)\partial f)e(\nu)M=0$. Hence $f(x_{a+1})e(\nu)$ also annihilates the module, where $\partial f=(s_af-f)/(x-y)$ is the divided difference of [F3] with the sign convention of the polynomial representation. [F1, given, algebra]

1.3 Unequal-colour exchange. Suppose $f\in k[u]$ is monic of degree $d$ with $f(x_a)e(\nu)=0$ on a block with $\nu_a=j\ne l=\nu_{a+1}$; write $x=x_a$, $y=x_{a+1}$. Using the unequal-colour dot slides $\tau_ax=y\tau_a$, $\tau_ay=x\tau_a$ and $\tau_a^2=Q_{jl}(x,y)$, one has $\tau_af(x)\tau_ae(\nu)=f(y)Q_{jl}(x,y)e(\nu)$; since $f(x)e(\nu)=0$, the left side is zero and $f(y)Q_{jl}(x,y)$ annihilates the module. Let $\rho(y):=\operatorname{Res}_x\bigl(f(x),Q_{jl}(x,y)\bigr)\in k[y]$ be the resultant with respect to $x$; by the Bezout identity for the Sylvester matrix there are $A,B\in k[x,y]$ with $\rho(y)=A\,f(x)+B\,Q_{jl}(x,y)$, and by homogeneity (Q3) and the extremal condition (Q4) the coefficient of $x^{-a_{jl}}$ in $Q_{jl}$ is a nonzero scalar $c$, so the Sylvester determinant shows that $\rho\ne0$ has leading coefficient $c^d$ in $y$, of degree $d(-a_{lj})\ge0$. Multiplying $\rho=A f(x)+BQ_{jl}$ by $f(y)$ and dividing by $c^d$ gives a monic $h\in k[u]$ of degree $d(1-a_{lj})$ with $h(x_{a+1})e(\nu)= \frac{1}{c^d}\bigl(A(x,y)f(x)f(y)+B(x,y)f(y)Q_{jl}(x,y)\bigr)e(\nu)=0$, since both $f(x)e(\nu)$ and $f(y)Q_{jl}(x,y)e(\nu)$ annihilate the module. If $d=0$ the corner is already zero; if $a_{lj}=0$ then $Q_{jl}$ is the nonzero constant $c$ and $f(x_{a+1})e(\nu)$ itself vanishes. [F1, algebra]

2.1 Every dot has a monic annihilator. Induct on the position $a$: step 1.1 gives a monic annihilator in $k[u]$ for $x_1$ in every block; if $g_a$ annihilates $x_a$, then steps 1.2 and 3.1 applied to the adjacent pair $(a,a+1)$ produce a monic polynomial $g_{a+1}\in k[u]$ annihilating $x_{a+1}$, the degree changing only by the fixed factor $1-a_{lj}$ and by nothing else across the finitely many colour pairs. Hence in every block of every length each dot $x_a$ has a scalar-coefficient monic annihilator, and the degrees can be chosen from a finite set depending only on the Cartan datum and on $\Lambda$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3]

3.1 Finite-dimensionality. Fix $\beta$ with $|\beta|=n$ and let $g_a$ be a monic annihilator of $x_a$ on $R^a(\beta)$ as in step 2.1, of degree $d_a$. By the PBW basis [F2], each corner $e(\nu)R^a(\beta)e(\nu')$ is spanned by the classes of $\tau_wx_1^{m_1}\cdots x_n^{m_n}e(\nu)$; using $g_a(x_a)=0$, any exponent $m_a\ge d_a$ is reduced to a $k$-linear combination of smaller powers of $x_a$ (the $x$'s commute), so every element of the corner is a combination of the finitely many classes with $0\le m_a<d_a$. Since $I^{\beta}$ is finite and $S_n$ is finite, $R^a(\beta)$ is finite dimensional over $k$. [F2, step 2.1]

3.2 Nilpotence in the standard homogeneous case. Suppose $a_j(u)=u^{\mu_j}$ for monic powers, so $x_1$ is killed by $x_1^{\Lambda}$ with $\Lambda=\max_j\mu_j$. If $x_a^N=0$ on a block and the colours are equal, the computation of step 1.2 with $f(u)=u^N$ gives $x_{a+1}^N=0$ (both $f(x)$ and $\partial f=\frac{y^N-x^N}{x-y}=-\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}x^{N-1-r}y^r$ kill the module). If the colours differ $j\ne l$, write $Q_{jl}(x,y)=c\,y^{q}+xH(x,y)$ with $q:=-a_{lj}\ge0$ and $c\ne0$, using (Q3)-(Q4); the identity $\kappa_ax^N\kappa_a=y^NQ_{jl}(x,y)$ gives $c\,y^{N+q}=-x\,y^NH(x,y)$ on the module, and raising this identity of commuting polynomials in $x,y$ to the $N$th power kills the right side ($x^N=0$), so $y^{N(N+q)}=0$. Starting from $x_1^{\Lambda}=0$ and iterating over positions, every dot $x_a$ is nilpotent, with an exponent obtained by the finitely many displayed transformations. [F1, step 1.2, step 2.1]

3.3 All-$i$ tails vanish. Fix a prefix length $\ell$; for each of the finitely many prefixes $w\in I^{\ell}$ let $d_w$ be the degree of the monic annihilator $g_w\in k[u]$ of $x_{\ell+1}$ on the block $wi$ given by step 2.1, and put $d:=\max_wd_w$. Let $w\in I^{\ell}$ and consider the word $wi^{d_w+1}$. Right concatenation embeds $R(\ell+1)$ into $R(\ell+1+d_w+1)$ as the first $\ell+1$ strands and carries the cyclotomic ideal into the cyclotomic ideal, so $g_w(x_{\ell+1})e(wi^{d_w+1})=0$ in $R^a(\ell+1+d_w+1)$. Applying step 1.2 successively along the equal-$i$ pairs $(\ell+1,\ell+2),\dots,(\ell+d_w,\ell+d_w+1)$ gives $(\partial_{\ell+d_w}\cdots\partial_{\ell+1}g_w)(x_{\ell+1})e(wi^{d_w+1})=0$; by the classical computation of iterated divided differences (the composite acts on polynomials as the alternating operator $\sum_{\sigma\in S_{d_w+1}}(-1)^{\ell(\sigma)}\sigma/\prod_{r<s}(x_r-x_s)$, so a monic degree-$d_w$ polynomial in the first variable evaluates to $\pm1$ and every lower-degree monomial contributes $0$) this element equals $(-1)^{d_w}e(wi^{d_w+1})$. Hence $e(wi^{d_w+1})=0$, and since $d\ge d_w$ every word with $d+1$ consecutive $i$'s at positions $\ell+1,\dots,\ell+d+1$ is zero. [F3, step 1.2, step 2.1]

4.1 Finite $i$-strings. Fix $i$. For a word $w$ over $I$ let $\#_i(w)$ be the number of letters equal to $i$. Claim: for every $b\ge0$ there is an integer $L_b$ such that every word with at most $b$ letters different from $i$ and $|w|\ge b+L_b$ is zero in the quotient. For $b=0$: an all-$i$ word of length $\ge L_0:=d+1$, where $d$ is a uniform annihilator degree at prefix length $0$ from step 2.1, is zero by step 3.3. Assume $L_{b-1}$; put $k:=L_{b-1}$, $\ell:=b-1+k$, and let $m$ be an all-$i$ tail bound at prefix length $\ell$ obtained from step 3.3 (a finite maximum over the finitely many prefixes of length $\ell$). Set $L_b:=k+m-1$ and let $w$ have at most $b$ non-$i$ letters and $|w|\ge b+L_b=\ell+m$. If the prefix of length $\ell$ contains at least $k$ letters $i$ it has at most $b-1$ non-$i$ letters and length $\ell=(b-1)+L_{b-1}$, so it is zero by the induction hypothesis and $w=0$. Otherwise the prefix contains at most $k-1$ letters $i$, while $w$ contains at least $L_b=k+m-1$ of them, so the last $m$ positions are all $i$ and the tail is zero by step 3.3; in both cases $w=0$. [step 3.3]

5.1 Conclusion. Let $\beta$ be fixed, put $b:=\sum_{j\ne i}\beta_j$, and let $m:=L_b-b$ if positive. Every word in $R^a(\beta+m\alpha_i)$ has exactly $b$ non-$i$ letters and length $|\beta|+m=b+(\beta_i+m)\ge b+L_b$ once $\beta_i+m\ge L_b$, so it is zero by step 4.1 and $R^a(\beta+m\alpha_i)=0$. Thus right-append induction at colour $i$ has finite strings, and $R^a(\beta)$ is finite dimensional by step 3.1; in the standard homogeneous specialization the extra nilpotence of step 3.2 holds and all statements are graded statements about the induced algebra and functors, while the inhomogeneous case uses no grading. Every step is an ordinary finite computation in $k[x]$ and $k[x,y]$. [step 3.1, step 3.2, step 4.1] ∎
