## Definition

Work over the field $\mathbb C$ and fix an integer $d\ge 0$
([[def-algebra-over-a-commutative-ring]], [[def-commutative-ring]]). Let
$\mathcal F_d$ be the free associative unital $\mathbb C$-algebra on the
$d+(d-1)$ symbols $x_1,\dots,x_d,s_1,\dots,s_{d-1}$ (for $d=0$ there is only
the unit; for $d=1$ only $x_1$), with its word basis and universal property
([[lem-hh-free-associative-ring-and-relations-descent]]), and let
$I_d\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}\mathcal F_d$ be the two-sided ideal
([[def-left-right-and-two-sided-ideal]], [[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]])
generated by the following elements:

- $x_ix_j-x_jx_i$ for all $1\le i,j\le d$;
- $s_i^2-1$, $s_is_{i+1}s_i-s_{i+1}s_is_{i+1}$, and $s_is_j-s_js_i$ for
  $|i-j|>1$, for $1\le i,j\le d-1$;
- $s_ix_j-x_js_i$ for $j\notin\{i,i+1\}$, $s_ix_{i+1}-x_is_i-1$, and
  $x_{i+1}s_i-s_ix_i-1$, for $1\le i\le d-1$ and $1\le j\le d$.

The **degenerate affine Hecke algebra** is the quotient $\mathbb C$-algebra
$$H_d:=\mathcal F_d/I_d$$
with unit element written $1$ ([[thm-quotient-ring-laws]]). We keep the symbols
$x_1,\dots,x_d,s_1,\dots,s_{d-1}$ for their images, and for $w\in S_d$ we write
$w$ for the image in $H_d$ of any word in the $s_i$ representing $w$; this is
independent of the chosen word once the basis theorem below is proved. For
$a\in\mathbb C$, the notation $H_d/(x_1-a)$ means the quotient by the two-sided
ideal generated by $x_1-a$.

The verification below establishes exactly the structure of $H_d$ used
throughout this page: the ordered monomials
$$x_1^{r_1}\cdots x_d^{r_d}w,\qquad r_1,\dots,r_d\ge0,\ w\in S_d,$$
form a $\mathbb C$-basis, so that
$H_d\cong\mathbb C[x_1,\dots,x_d]\otimes_{\mathbb C}\mathbb C[S_d]$ as a vector
space with polynomial and group-algebra subalgebras
([[def-polynomial-ring-on-a-family-of-indeterminates]], [[def-group-ring]]);
$H_0=\mathbb C$ and $H_1=\mathbb C[x_1]$; for every $a$ there is a unique
algebra isomorphism
$$H_d/(x_1-a)\cong\mathbb C[S_d],\qquad x_j\longmapsto a+\sum_{k<j}(k\ j);$$
and total polynomial degree is an algebra filtration whose associated graded is
the semidirect product $\mathbb C[x_1,\dots,x_d]\rtimes\mathbb C[S_d]$ in which
$s_i$ acts by swapping $x_i$ and $x_{i+1}$. The cross relation is
inhomogeneous, so it does not turn $H_d$ into a graded algebra with
$\deg x_i=1$ and $\deg s_i=0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $d\ge0$, the field $\mathbb C$, the free associative $\mathbb C$-algebra $\mathcal F_d$ on the symbols $x_1,\dots,x_d,s_1,\dots,s_{d-1}$, and the two-sided ideal $I_d$ of the Definition.

[F1] $S_d$ is the group of bijections of $\{1,\dots,d\}$ under composition, with $s_i=(i\ i+1)$; it is generated by $s_1,\dots,s_{d-1}$ subject to the Coxeter relations $s_i^2=1$, $s_is_{i+1}s_i=s_{i+1}s_is_{i+1}$, $s_is_j=s_js_i$ for $|i-j|>1$ ([[def-symmetric-group]], [[def-finite-symmetric-group-and-permutation-notation]], [[thm-the-symmetric-group-has-the-coxeter-presentation]]).

[F2] $\mathbb C[S_d]$ is a unital $\mathbb C$-algebra with $\mathbb C$-basis $\{w:w\in S_d\}$, product induced by composition and unit the identity permutation ([[def-group-ring]], [[thm-group-ring-is-a-unital-algebra-with-basis-g]]).

[F3] $\mathbb C[x_1,\dots,x_d]$ is a commutative $\mathbb C$-algebra with $\mathbb C$-basis the monomials $x_1^{r_1}\cdots x_d^{r_d}$; it is a domain with fraction field $\Omega$ in which every nonzero polynomial is invertible; since $x_i\ne x_{i+1}$ as polynomials, $d_i:=x_i-x_{i+1}$ is invertible in $\Omega$ ([[def-polynomial-ring-on-a-family-of-indeterminates]], [[def-commutative-ring]], [[cor-polynomial-ring-over-a-domain-is-a-domain]], [[def-field-of-fractions]], [[thm-field-of-fractions-is-a-field-and-the-domain-embeds]]).

[F4] $\mathcal F_d$ has the word basis and universal property, the two-sided ideal generated by a set $E$ is the set of finite sums $\sum_sa_se_sb_s$ with $e_s\in E$, and a homomorphism killing $E$ factors uniquely through $\mathcal F_d/(E)$ ([[lem-hh-free-associative-ring-and-relations-descent]], [[def-left-right-and-two-sided-ideal]], [[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]], [[thm-quotient-ring-universal-property]], [[thm-quotient-ring-laws]]).

[F5] For $\mathbb C$-algebras $A,B$ the tensor product $A\otimes_{\mathbb C}B$ is a $\mathbb C$-algebra with $(a\otimes b)(a'\otimes b')=aa'\otimes bb'$; if $(a_i)$ and $(b_j)$ are bases, then $(a_i\otimes b_j)$ is a basis ([[thm-tensor-product-of-algebras-over-a-commutative-ring]], [[thm-tensor-product-basis-from-bases]]).

[F6] Algebra homomorphisms are unital ring homomorphisms over the structure map and preserve $1$ ([[def-algebra-over-a-commutative-ring]], [[def-ring-homomorphism]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** The data of [F1]-[F6]: the presented algebra $H_d=\mathcal F_d/I_d$, the symmetric group $S_d$, the group algebra $\mathbb C[S_d]$, the polynomial algebra $\mathbb C[x_1,\dots,x_d]$ with fraction field $\Omega$, and the tensor-product and quotient structures.

1.1 The cases $d=0,1$. For $d=0$ there are no symbols, so $\mathcal F_0=\mathbb C$ and $I_0=0$, giving $H_0=\mathbb C$. For $d=1$ the only symbol is $x_1$ and the list of relations is empty; the words in $x_1$ are $1,x_1,x_1^2,\dots$, a $\mathbb C$-basis of $\mathcal F_1$ by [F4], so $H_1$ is the polynomial algebra $\mathbb C[x_1]$ with basis $x_1^r$, $r\ge0$. [F3, F4, given]

1.2 The rational-function model. On the $\mathbb C$-vector space $\Omega$ of [F3] let $m_f$ denote multiplication by $f\in\Omega$, and for $1\le i<d$ let $\sigma_i$ be the operator $(\sigma_if)(y_1,\dots,y_d)=f(y_1,\dots,y_{i+1},y_i,\dots,y_d)$ interchanging the $i$-th and $(i+1)$-st arguments. Put $a_i:=d_i^{-1}\in\Omega$ (invertible by [F3]), $\delta_i:=a_i(1+\sigma_i)$ and $T_i:=\sigma_i-\delta_i$. For a word $w=s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_k}$ in the generators of $S_d$ set $\sigma_w:=\sigma_{i_1}\!\circ\cdots\circ\sigma_{i_k}$ and $T_w:=T_{i_1}\!\cdots T_{i_k}$. The operators $\sigma_w$ are well defined by the Coxeter presentation [F1], because the $\sigma_i$ satisfy those relations and the presentation says that any two words for the same $w$ are related by them. [F1, F3, construct]

1.3 The skew algebra. Let $D:=\{\sum_{w\in S_d}m_{f_w}\sigma_w:f_w\in\Omega\}\subseteq\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(\Omega)$, a $\mathbb C$-subspace. For $f,g\in\Omega$ and $u,v\in S_d$ the identities $\sigma_um_g=m_{\sigma_u(g)}\sigma_u$ and $\sigma_u\sigma_v=\sigma_{uv}$ hold (the first expresses the substitution action on functions, the second is word concatenation), so $(m_f\sigma_u)(m_g\sigma_v)=m_{f\,\sigma_u(g)}\sigma_{uv}$; hence $D$ is a subalgebra of $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(\Omega)$. The set $\{\sigma_w\}$ is $\Omega$-linearly independent: suppose $\sum_wf_w\sigma_w=0$ with $f_w\in\Omega$ not all zero; multiplying the resulting identity of functions by a common denominator of the $f_w$, we may assume $f_w\in\mathbb C[x_1,\dots,x_d]$. Fix integers $N_1>\cdots>N_d$ whose consecutive gaps exceed the total degree of every $f_w$, and order the finitely many $w$ with $f_w\ne0$ by the lexicographic order of the vectors $w\cdot N:=(N_{w(1)},\dots,N_{w(d)})$; let $w$ be maximal and let $e$ be the exponent of a top monomial of $f_w$. Applying the sum to the monomial $y^N$ gives $\sum_{w'}f_{w'}(y)\,y^{w'\cdot N}$, because $\sigma_{w'}y^N=y^{w'\cdot N}$; the vectors $w'\cdot N$ are distinct, and at the first coordinate where $w'\cdot N$ differs from $w\cdot N$ the gap exceeds the degree bound, so the monomial $y^{w\cdot N+e}$ occurs exactly once, in the summand $f_wy^{w\cdot N}$, with nonzero coefficient. This contradicts the vanishing of the sum, proving the claim. [F3, algebra, given]

1.4 Spanning of $H_d$ by ordered monomials. We show that every element of $H_d$ is a $\mathbb C$-linear combination of the images of ordered monomials $x^rw$; since words span $\mathcal F_d$ by [F4], it suffices to treat the image of a word $W$ in the symbols. Use strong induction on the pair $(n,\iota)$ ordered lexicographically, where $n$ is the number of $x$-symbols in $W$ and $\iota$ is the number of pairs of positions $(p<q)$ such that the $p$-th symbol is some $s_i$ and the $q$-th symbol is some $x_j$. If $n=0$, then $W$ is a word in the $s_i$; by the Coxeter presentation [F1] the subalgebra generated by the $s_i$ is spanned by the images of the elements $w\in S_d$, so the claim holds. If $n>0$ and every $x$-symbol precedes every $s$-symbol in $W$, then $W$ is of the form (word in $x$'s)(word in $s$'s), and the claim follows from commutativity of the $x$'s and the case $n=0$. Otherwise $W=W_1s_ix_jW_2$ with adjacent symbols; use $s_ix_{i+1}=x_is_i+1$ if $j=i+1$, $s_ix_i=x_{i+1}s_i-1$ if $j=i$ (the relation $x_{i+1}s_i=s_ix_i+1$ solved for $s_ix_i$), and $s_ix_j=x_js_i$ if $j\notin\{i,i+1\}$. In every case $W\equiv W_1x_{j'}s_iW_2\pm W_1W_2$, where the first word has the same $n$ and one fewer $(s,x)$-inversion pair, while the second has $n-1$ $x$-symbols; both fall under the induction hypothesis. Hence the images of ordered monomials span $H_d$. [F1, F3, F4, given, construct]

1.5 Jucys-Murphy elements. For $1\le j\le d$ put $J_j:=\sum_{k<j}(k\ j)\in\mathbb C[S_d]$, so $J_1=0$. Then (i) $J_iJ_j=J_jJ_i$ for all $i,j$; (ii) $s_iJ_j=J_js_i$ for $j\notin\{i,i+1\}$; and (iii) $s_iJ_{i+1}=J_is_i+1$. For (i) with $i<j$ write $\Omega_m:=\sum_{1\le k<l\le m}(k\ l)$ for the sum of all transpositions in $S_m$; $\Omega_m$ is central in $\mathbb C[S_m]$ because conjugation permutes the transpositions, and $J_m=\Omega_m-\Omega_{m-1}$. Since $J_i\in\mathbb C[S_i]\subseteq\mathbb C[S_{m-1}]\subset\mathbb C[S_m]$ for $i<m$, centrality of $\Omega_m$ in $\mathbb C[S_m]$ and of $\Omega_{m-1}$ in $\mathbb C[S_{m-1}]$ gives $[J_i,J_m]=[J_i,\Omega_m]-[J_i,\Omega_{m-1}]=0$. For (ii), conjugation by $s_i$ sends each transposition $(k\ j)$ to $(s_i(k)\ s_i(j))$; if $j<i$ both entries are fixed, and if $j>i+1$ the sum over $k<j$ contains the pairs with $k=i$ and $k=i+1$, whose images are interchanged, so the sum is preserved. For (iii), conjugation sends $(k\ i+1)$ to $(k\ i)$ for $k<i$ and fixes $(i\ i+1)$, so $s_iJ_{i+1}s_i=J_i+s_i$; multiplying on the right by $s_i$ and using $s_i^2=1$ gives (iii). [F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 The model satisfies the relations. Since $\sigma_im_{a_i}=m_{\sigma_i(a_i)}\sigma_i=m_{-a_i}\sigma_i$, the operator identity $(1+\sigma_i)m_{a_i}=m_{a_i}(1-\sigma_i)$ and the rules $\sigma_i\delta_i=-\delta_i$, $\delta_i\sigma_i=\delta_i$ hold; hence $\delta_i^2=m_{a_i}(1+\sigma_i)m_{a_i}(1+\sigma_i)=m_{a_i^2}(1-\sigma_i)(1+\sigma_i)=0$ and $T_i^2=\sigma_i^2-\sigma_i\delta_i-\delta_i\sigma_i+\delta_i^2=1+\delta_i-\delta_i+0=1$. For $j\notin\{i,i+1\}$ both $\sigma_i$ and $\delta_i$ commute with $m_{x_j}$, so $T_im_{x_j}=m_{x_j}T_i$. For the cross relations, use $\sigma_im_{x_{i+1}}=m_{x_i}\sigma_i$, $\sigma_im_{x_i}=m_{x_{i+1}}\sigma_i$ and the two identities $m_{x_i}\delta_i-\delta_im_{x_{i+1}}=1$ and $\delta_im_{x_i}-m_{x_{i+1}}\delta_i=1$: for the first, $m_{x_i}\delta_i=m_{a_ix_i}(1+\sigma_i)$ while $\delta_im_{x_{i+1}}=m_{a_ix_{i+1}}+m_{a_ix_i}\sigma_i$, and their difference is $m_{a_i(x_i-x_{i+1})}=1$; the second is the same computation with the roles of $x_i$ and $x_{i+1}$ interchanged. Therefore $T_im_{x_{i+1}}-m_{x_i}T_i=(m_{x_i}\sigma_i-m_{x_i}\sigma_i)+(m_{x_i}\delta_i-\delta_im_{x_{i+1}})=1$ and $m_{x_{i+1}}T_i-T_im_{x_i}=1$. Finally the braid relation: expanding $T_iT_{i+1}T_i$ and $T_{i+1}T_iT_{i+1}$ in the $\Omega$-basis $\{\sigma_w\}$ of step 1.3 by its product rule, every coefficient is a rational function of $x_i,x_{i+1},x_{i+2}$ alone whose denominator divides $d_id_{i+1}(x_i-x_{i+2})$; clearing that denominator leaves a polynomial identity in three variables, verified by expanding the six resulting permutation terms. Hence the assignment $x_i\mapsto m_{x_i}$, $s_i\mapsto T_i$ preserves all the relations of the Definition, and by the universal properties [F4] it defines an algebra homomorphism $\Phi\colon H_d\to\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(\Omega)$. [F1, F4, algebra]

2.2 Triangular expansion. Fix $w\in S_d$ with a reduced word $w=s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_k}$, and put $c:=\prod_{t=1}^k(\sigma_{i_1}\cdots\sigma_{i_{t-1}})(1-a_{i_t})\ne0$ in $\Omega$, with the empty prefix acting as the identity. Every transported factor is nonzero because the prefix is a field automorphism. In the expansion of $T_w=\prod_t\bigl((1-a_{i_t})\sigma_{i_t}-a_{i_t}\bigr)$ by the product rule of step 1.3, the term choosing the factor $(1-a_{i_t})\sigma_{i_t}$ for every $t$ contributes $c\,\sigma_w$ after the scalar coefficients are moved left, while every other term is $m_g\sigma_v$ with $v$ the product of a proper subword of $s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_k}$ in the induced order. If $\ell$ denotes the minimal number of generators needed to express a permutation, then such a proper subword $J$ has $\ell(v)\le|J|<k=\ell(w)$, using subadditivity of $\ell$ and reducedness. Collecting the expansion in the $\Omega$-basis $\{\sigma_v\}$ therefore gives $T_w=c\,\sigma_w+\sum_{\ell(v)<\ell(w)}c_v\sigma_v$ with $c\ne0$ and $c_v\in\Omega$. [F1, step 1.3, algebra]

2.3 The evaluation homomorphism. The assignment $x_j\mapsto a+J_j$, $s_i\mapsto s_i$ defines an algebra homomorphism $\varphi\colon\mathcal F_d\to\mathbb C[S_d]$: the images of the $x$'s commute by step 1.5(i); the images of the $s_i$ satisfy the Coxeter relations by [F1]; and the cross relations follow from step 1.5(ii) and from $s_i(a+J_{i+1})=(a+J_i)s_i+1$, $(a+J_{i+1})s_i=s_i(a+J_i)+1$, which are step 1.5(iii) read in both directions. Since $\varphi(x_1-a)=a+J_1-a=0$, the quotient universal property [F4] yields an algebra homomorphism $\bar\varphi\colon H_d/(x_1-a)\to\mathbb C[S_d]$, which is surjective because the $s_i$ generate $S_d$. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.5, construct]

3.1 Independence of the monomials in the model. By step 2.2 the change-of-basis matrix from $\{T_w\}$ to $\{\sigma_w\}$ inside $D$ is triangular with nonzero diagonal when permutations are ordered by length, hence invertible over the field $\Omega$; thus $\{T_w:w\in S_d\}$ is an $\Omega$-basis of $D$. Suppose $\sum_{r,w}c_{r,w}m_{x^r}T_w=0$ in $D$ with $c_{r,w}\in\mathbb C$ finitely supported. Grouping by $w$ gives $\sum_wP_wT_w=0$ with $P_w=\sum_rc_{r,w}x^r\in\mathbb C[x_1,\dots,x_d]\subseteq\Omega$; $\Omega$-independence of the $T_w$ forces $P_w=0$ for every $w$, and linear independence of the monomials [F3] then forces $c_{r,w}=0$. Hence the elements $\Phi(x_1^{r_1}\cdots x_d^{r_d}w)$ are $\mathbb C$-linearly independent. [F3, step 1.3, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 Basis theorem. Let $h\in H_d$ and write $h=\sum c_{r,w}x^rw$ using step 1.4. If $h=0$, applying the homomorphism $\Phi$ of step 2.1 gives $\sum c_{r,w}m_{x^r}T_w=0$, and step 3.1 yields $c_{r,w}=0$. Hence the ordered monomials form a $\mathbb C$-basis of $H_d$, and the $\mathbb C$-linear map $\mathbb C[x_1,\dots,x_d]\otimes_{\mathbb C}\mathbb C[S_d]\to H_d$, $f\otimes w\mapsto fw$, is an isomorphism by [F5]; in particular $\mathbb C[x_1,\dots,x_d]$ and $\mathbb C[S_d]$ are subalgebras of $H_d$ and the cross relations hold. The elementary cases $H_0=\mathbb C$, $H_1=\mathbb C[x_1]$ were recorded in step 1.1. [F2, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 1.4]

5.1 The inverse map. In $H_d$ the relation $s_ix_{i+1}=x_is_i+1$ gives, after left multiplication by $s_i$ and use of $s_i^2=1$, the recursion $x_{i+1}=s_ix_is_i+s_i$. Hence in $H_d/(x_1-a)$ one has $x_1\equiv a$ and inductively $x_j\equiv a+u_j$ with $u_j$ in the subalgebra generated by the images of the $s_i$, namely $u_1=0$ and $u_{j+1}=s_ju_js_j+s_j$. By the basis theorem of step 4.1 every element of $H_d$ is a combination of monomials $x^rw$, and replacing each $x_j$ by $a+u_j$ expresses its image in $H_d/(x_1-a)$ as an element of the image of the subalgebra generated by the $s_i$; by [F1] and [F2] that image is the $\mathbb C$-span of $\{w:w\in S_d\}$. Therefore the canonical map $\rho\colon\mathbb C[S_d]\to H_d/(x_1-a)$, $w\mapsto w+(x_1-a)$, has image equal to the whole quotient, and it is injective because $\bar\varphi\circ\rho=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb C[S_d]}$ by construction; hence $\rho$ is an isomorphism. Under it $x_j$ corresponds to $a+u_j$, and $\bar\varphi(a+u_j)=a+J_j$ by step 2.3, so $\bar\varphi(x_j)=a+\sum_{k<j}(k\ j)$; in particular $x_1$ corresponds to the scalar $a$, while for $j\ge2$ the image is not the scalar $a$. [F1, F2, step 4.1, step 2.3, algebra]

5.2 The degree filtration. For $k\ge0$ let $F_k\subseteq H_d$ be the image of the $\mathbb C$-span of all words in the symbols containing at most $k$ of the symbols $x_1,\dots,x_d$. Steps 1.4 and 4.1 identify $F_k$ with the span of the monomials $x^rw$ with $|r|\le k$. The substitution rules of step 1.4 replace an adjacent pair $s_ix_j$ by terms containing at most one $x$-symbol each, so a product of a word with $k$ $x$-symbols and a word with $m$ $x$-symbols rewrites into words with at most $k+m$ $x$-symbols; hence $F_kF_m\subseteq F_{k+m}$ and $(F_k)_{k\ge0}$ is an algebra filtration with $1\in F_0$. [F4, step 1.4, step 4.1]

6.1 The associated graded is a semidirect product. Let $S:=\mathbb C[x_1,\dots,x_d]\rtimes\mathbb C[S_d]$ be the algebra with $\mathbb C$-basis $x^rw$ and product $x^rw\cdot x^{r'}w'=x^{r+w\cdot r'}ww'$, where $(w\cdot r')_j=r'_{w^{-1}(j)}$; equivalently, $S$ is generated by the symbols $x_i$ and $w$ subject to the relations obtained from those of $H_d$ by deleting the inhomogeneous term $1$ from both cross relations, i.e. $s_ix_{i+1}=x_is_i$ and $x_{i+1}s_i=s_ix_i$. In $\mathrm{gr}\,H_d$ the defining relations of $H_d$ project to these relations: the commutativity, Coxeter and distant-commutation relations are homogeneous of the stated degrees, while the two cross relations have the form (degree $1$ element) $=1$ (degree $0$ element), so their degree-$1$ parts are exactly $s_ix_{i+1}=x_is_i$ and $x_{i+1}s_i=s_ix_i$. Consequently there is a surjective graded algebra homomorphism $S\to\mathrm{gr}\,H_d$ sending $x_i$ and $w$ to the symbols of $x_i$ and $w$. In degree $k$ both sides are $\mathbb C$-spanned by the symbols $x^rw$ with $|r|=k$: these form a spanning set of $\mathrm{gr}_kH_d$ by step 5.2 together with the basis theorem of step 4.1, and they form the basis of the degree-$k$ part of $S$ by construction; a surjection between $\mathbb C$-vector spaces with a spanning set of the same finite cardinality as a basis of the target is an isomorphism. Hence $\mathrm{gr}\,H_d\cong S$ in every degree, and the associated graded is the semidirect product $\mathbb C[x_1,\dots,x_d]\rtimes\mathbb C[S_d]$ in which $s_i$ swaps $x_i$ and $x_{i+1}$, as claimed. [F2, F5, step 4.1, step 5.2, algebra]

7.1 No grading by polynomial degree. Suppose there were an algebra grading $H_d=\bigoplus_{k\ge0}H_d^{(k)}$ with $x_i$ in degree $1$ and $s_i$ in degree $0$. Then the relation $s_ix_{i+1}-x_is_i=1$ would have its left-hand side in degree $1$ and its right-hand side in degree $0$, forcing $1=0$, which is impossible. Hence the filtration of step 5.2 is genuinely a filtration: the cross relation is inhomogeneous. [F6, step 5.2] ∎

## Remarks

- The filtration of step 4.1 is the polynomial-degree filtration, in which $x_i$ has degree $1$ and $s_i$ degree $0$ and the associated graded is commutative in the polynomial part but noncommutative overall; it is the filtration used for the cyclotomic quotients later on this page.

- The Arakawa-Suzuki generators $y_j$ used in the tensor constructions on this page are not the $x_j$ of this presentation: on a tensor module they differ by the scalar $z_M/N-(N-1)/2$ recalled in [[def-arakawa-suzuki-casimir-operators-on-tensor-functors]]. The presentation here is the one of [BKHL] and [BKAR]; both cross relations of the Definition are recorded because the recursion $x_{j+1}=s_jx_js_j+s_j$ used in step 3.3 consumes the second one.

- All arguments above are choice-free: the only selections made are finite (a monomial for the denominator clearing, a maximal permutation among finitely many), and no family of nonempty sets is used.
