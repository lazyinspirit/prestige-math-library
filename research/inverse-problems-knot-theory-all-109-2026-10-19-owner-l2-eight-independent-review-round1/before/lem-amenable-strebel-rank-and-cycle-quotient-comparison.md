## Statement

Work under the standing AC convention for half-solutions and von Neumann modules. Let $R=\mathbb Q$ or $\mathbb F_p$, let $G$ be countable, amenable and in $D(R)$, and put $A=\mathcal N G$. If $f:M\to(\mathbb ZG)^b$ is a right-module map with arbitrary domain, then
$$\dim_A\operatorname{im}(f\otimes A)\ge\dim_R\operatorname{im}(f\otimes R).$$
If $M$ is finite free, also $\dim_A\ker(f\otimes A)\le\dim_R\ker(f\otimes R)$. Let $C_*$ be a chain complex with $C_i$ and $C_{i-1}$ finite free, and let $x_1,\ldots,x_r\in C_i$ be cycles. Let $H_A$ and $H_R$ be the submodules of the corresponding degree-i homology generated by their coefficient images. Then
$$\dim_A H_i(C_*\otimes A)-\dim_A H_A\le\dim_R H_i(C_*\otimes R)-\dim_R H_R.$$
No freeness of $C_{i+1}$ is required for this inequality. The convention includes the empty cycle list, giving the ordinary Betti-number upper bound.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The modules, coefficients, cycle list and standing foundational convention of the Statement.

[F1] The class $D(R)$ detects injectivity between projective $RG$-modules from trivial-coefficient injectivity. [[def-integral-half-solutions-strebel-and-l2-signatures]]

[F2] Amenability makes all positive-degree Tor groups with $A$ dimension zero. [[thm-amenable-von-neumann-l2-flatness]]

[F3] Submodules and quotients inside finite projection modules have dimensions given by their Hilbert closure projections; matrix kernels and images satisfy trace rank-nullity; dimension-zero modules are closed under submodules and quotients. [[lem-finite-von-neumann-module-dimension-and-dense-quotients]]

## Proof

**Given:** The data in the Statement.

1.1 Let $d=\dim_R\operatorname{im}(f\otimes R)\le b$. This image is spanned by augmented vectors $f(m)\otimes1$ for $m\in M$, so choose $d$ of them forming a basis. Let $j:(\mathbb ZG)^d\to(\mathbb ZG)^b$ have the corresponding columns $f(m)$. Its augmentation is injective, so [F1] makes $j\otimes_{\mathbb Z}R$ injective over $RG$. It follows that $j$ is injective integrally: for $R=\mathbb Q$ clear denominators, and for $R=\mathbb F_p$ divide a putative integral kernel vector by the largest common power of $p$ in its finitely many coefficients, then reduce modulo $p$. Complexifying preserves injection because any finite complex coefficient relation splits, along a basis of their finite rational span, into rational relations that clear denominators. Apply [F2] to the cokernel presentation of this injection. The kernel of $j\otimes A$ has dimension zero; its projection is zero by faithfulness in [F3]. Thus its image has dimension $d$ and lies in $\operatorname{im}(f\otimes A)$, proving the image inequality. If the domain of $f$ is finite free, subtract this inequality from its common finite free rank and use rank-nullity in [F3] to obtain the kernel inequality. [F1, F2, F3, construct, algebra]

1.2 Put $Z=\ker(d_i\otimes A)\subseteq A^a$, where $a$ is the rank of $C_i$, and let $B=\operatorname{im}(d_{i+1}\otimes A)$. Let $P_Z,P_B$ be their Hilbert closure projections, so $P_B\le P_Z$. Then $H_i(C_*\otimes A)=Z/B$ has dimension $\tau(P_Z)-\tau(P_B)$ by [F3]. Let $L=B+\sum_jx_jA$ and write $P_L$ for its Hilbert closure projection. Orthogonally projecting off $\overline B$ shows that the Hilbert-closed span of the projected columns $(P_Z-P_B)x_j$ is the range of $P_L-P_B$: the closure of $L$ is the orthogonal sum of $\overline B$ and that closed projected span. Consequently the algebraic image $Q$ of this finite column matrix is projective and has dimension $\tau(P_L)-\tau(P_B)$ by [F3]. [F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The natural projection from $H_A=L/B$ onto $Q$ is surjective. Its kernel is a submodule of $P_BA^a/B$, which has dimension zero by [F3]. Since $Q$ is projective, this surjection splits, so $H_A\cong K\oplus Q$ with $\dim_A K=0$. This implies $\dim_A H_A=\dim_A Q$: the lower bound follows from the displayed summand; for the upper bound, project any finite projective $P\subseteq K\oplus Q$ onto $Q$. That map is a matrix between finite projection modules, whose kernel is a finite projection module contained in $K$. Its trace is zero, hence its projection is zero, so it is injective and $\dim_A P\le\dim_A Q$ by [F3]. Taking the defining supremum proves the equality. Therefore $\dim_A H_i-\dim_A H_A=\tau(P_Z)-\tau(P_L)$. [F3, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 Define $g:C_{i+1}\oplus(\mathbb ZG)^r\to C_i$ by $g(v,(a_j))=d_{i+1}v+\sum_jx_ja_j$. Its $A$-image is $L$. Apply step 1.1 to $g$ and to the finite-free-domain map $d_i:C_i\to C_{i-1}$. They give $\tau(P_L)\ge\dim_R\operatorname{im}(g\otimes R)$ and $\tau(P_Z)\le\dim_R\ker(d_i\otimes R)$. Subtracting and using step 2.1 yields the claimed inequality, since the difference of those two ordinary ranks is precisely $\dim_R H_i(C_*\otimes R)-\dim_R H_R$. With no cycles, $g=d_{i+1}$ and the same computation proves the Betti-number bound. All dimensions here are finite because the middle chain module has rank $a$, so no subtraction of infinite dimensions occurs. [step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
