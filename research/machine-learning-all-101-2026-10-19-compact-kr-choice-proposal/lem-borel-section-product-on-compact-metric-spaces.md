## Statement

Let $X,Y$ be nonempty compact metric spaces, let $\mu,\nu$ be countably additive Borel probability measures on $X,Y$, and give $X\times Y$ its product topology. For every Borel set $E\subseteq X\times Y$, its section $E_x:=\{y\in Y:(x,y)\in E\}$ is Borel in $Y$, and $x\mapsto\nu(E_x)$ is Borel measurable. The formula
$$\lambda(E):=\int_X\nu(E_x)\,d\mu(x)$$
defines a Borel probability measure on $X\times Y$ satisfying
$$\lambda(A\times B)=\mu(A)\nu(B)$$
for every Borel $A\subseteq X$ and $B\subseteq Y$. In particular its coordinate marginals are $\mu,\nu$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The compact metric spaces, probabilities, and product topology in the Statement.

[F1] Distance to a nonempty set is 1-Lipschitz. A closed subset of a compact metric space is compact. [[lem-distance-to-set-is-lipschitz]] [[lem-closed-subset-of-a-compact-space-is-compact]]

[F2] If an open set in $Y\times X$ contains $K\times\{x\}$ for compact $K\subseteq Y$, the tube lemma gives an open neighborhood $V$ of $x$ with $K\times V$ contained in that set. It uses an indexed family of all admissible pairs of neighborhoods and one finite subcover, without choice. [[lem-tube-lemma-for-a-compact-factor]]

[F3] Measures are continuous from below on increasing measurable sequences. [[thm-continuity-from-below-for-measures]]

[F4] A lambda-system containing a pi-system contains its generated sigma-algebra. [[thm-dynkin-pi-lambda]]

[F5] Nonnegative integrals are monotone, homogeneous and finitely additive, agree with the simple integral, and preserve increasing pointwise limits. [[def-nonnegative-lebesgue-integral]] [[prop-order-and-scalar-rules-for-the-nonnegative-integral]] [[cor-additivity-of-the-nonnegative-lebesgue-integral]] [[prop-the-nonnegative-integral-agrees-with-the-simple-integral]] [[thm-monotone-convergence-for-the-integral]]

[F6] A Borel sigma-algebra is generated by the open sets. Continuous maps pull Borel sets back to Borel sets, and the product topology has open rectangles as a basis. [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]] [[def-product-topology]] [[def-metric-topology]]

[F7] Finite sums and real scalar multiples of real Borel measurable functions are Borel measurable, as are increasing pointwise limits of nonnegative Borel functions. [[prop-closure-properties-of-measurable-functions-used-by-the-integral]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 For an open set $G\subseteq Y$ with $G\ne Y$, put $K_n:=\{y:d(y,Y\setminus G)\ge1/n\}$ for integers $n\ge1$. By [F1] these sets are closed and compact. They increase, lie in $G$, and exhaust $G$: if $y\in G$, some ball $B(y,r)$ lies in $G$, so $d(y,Y\setminus G)\ge r>0$ and $y\in K_n$ for some $n$. If $G=Y$, use $K_n=Y$. Thus [F3] gives $\nu(G)=\sup_n\nu(K_n)$ for a specified compact exhaustion of each open $G$, including $G=\varnothing$. [given, F1, F3, F6, construct]

2.1 Let $U\subseteq X\times Y$ be open. The map $y\mapsto(x,y)$ is continuous for fixed $x$, so each $U_x$ is open. Put $h_U(x)=\nu(U_x)$. If $h_U(x)>a$ for $a\ge0$, step 1.1 provides one compact $K\subseteq U_x$ with $\nu(K)>a$. The coordinate interchange $S:Y\times X\to X\times Y$, $S(y,z)=(z,y)$, is a homeomorphism: it and its inverse take basic open rectangles to basic open rectangles by [F6]. Thus $S^{-1}(U)$ is open and contains $K\times\{x\}$. Apply [F2] in $Y\times X$ to obtain an open neighborhood $V$ of $x$ with $K\times V\subseteq S^{-1}(U)$, or equivalently $V\times K\subseteq U$. For every $z\in V$, $K\subseteq U_z$ and $h_U(z)\ge\nu(K)>a$. Consequently $\{h_U>a\}$ is open for every real $a$ (for $a<0$ it is all of $X$). Hence $h_U$ is lower semicontinuous and Borel: the inverse images of real rays $(a,+\infty)$ are open, and these rays generate the real Borel sigma-algebra, as their rational subfamily generates all open intervals by countable unions and complements. [step 1.1, F2, F6, choose]

3.1 Every Borel $E\subseteq X\times Y$ has Borel sections because the continuous map $y\mapsto(x,y)$ has inverse image $E_x$. Let $\mathcal D$ be the family of Borel $E$ for which $h_E(x):=\nu(E_x)$ is Borel. It contains $X\times Y$, with $h=1$. If $E\in\mathcal D$, then $h_{E^c}=1-h_E$ is Borel by [F7]. For disjoint $E_n\in\mathcal D$, countable additivity of $\nu$ gives $h_{\bigcup_n E_n}=\sum_nh_{E_n}$, an increasing limit of Borel partial sums and therefore Borel by [F7]. Thus $\mathcal D$ is a lambda-system. All open sets belong to it by step 2.1; the open sets form a pi-system generating $\mathcal B(X\times Y)$. By [F4], $\mathcal D=\mathcal B(X\times Y)$. [step 2.1, F4, F6, F7, construct]

4.1 Define $\lambda(E)=\int h_E\,d\mu$, which is meaningful and lies in $[0,1]$ by step 3.1 and [F5]. It has $\lambda(\varnothing)=0$ and $\lambda(X\times Y)=1$. For disjoint Borel $E_n$, step 3.1 gives $h_{\bigcup_nE_n}=\lim_N\sum_{n=1}^Nh_{E_n}$ increasingly. Finite additivity and monotone convergence [F5] yield $\lambda(\bigcup_nE_n)=\lim_N\sum_{n=1}^N\lambda(E_n)=\sum_n\lambda(E_n)$. Hence $\lambda$ is a countably additive Borel probability. [step 3.1, F5, construct]

5.1 For Borel $A\subseteq X,B\subseteq Y$, the rectangle $A\times B$ is Borel as the intersection of the two coordinate inverse images. Its section measure is $h_{A\times B}(x)=\mathbf1_A(x)\nu(B)$. The simple-integral formula [F5] gives $\lambda(A\times B)=\mu(A)\nu(B)$. Taking $B=Y$ or $A=X$ proves the marginal identities. [step 4.1, F5, F6] ∎

## Remarks

This construction works on the full Borel sigma-algebra of the product topology. It neither asserts nor uses equality with the sigma-algebra generated by Borel rectangles. It requires no simultaneous selection of finite nets. The integral convergence is over specified sequences, and the compact exhaustion in step 1.1 is given by a formula. A general optimal-coupling attainment theorem is not part of this lemma.
