## Statement

Let $(X,d)$ be a nonempty compact metric space. The coupling cost $W_1^X$ is a finite metric on the set of countably additive Borel probability measures on $X$. In particular it is nonnegative and symmetric, $W_1^X(\mu,\nu)=0$ holds exactly when $\mu=\nu$, and for any third Borel probability $\rho$,
$$W_1^X(\mu,\nu)\le W_1^X(\mu,\rho)+W_1^X(\rho,\nu).$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The compact metric space and Borel probabilities in the Statement; fix one $x_0\in X$.

[F1] The coupling cost is defined on the product-topology Borel sigma-algebra. It is finite and equals the supremum of $\int f\,d\mu-\int f\,d\nu$ over 1-Lipschitz functions anchored at $x_0$. All these functions are bounded Borel and integrable. [[def-compact-metric-wasserstein-one]] [[thm-kantorovich-rubinstein-duality-on-compact-metric-spaces]]

[F2] Distance to a fixed nonempty subset is 1-Lipschitz. [[lem-distance-to-set-is-lipschitz]]

[F3] Nonnegative measurable functions increasing to a limit have increasing integrals converging to the integral of the limit. [[thm-monotone-convergence-for-the-integral]]

[F4] A lambda-system containing a pi-system contains the sigma-algebra it generates. Borel sets are generated by open sets, which form a pi-system. [[thm-dynkin-pi-lambda]] [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]

[F5] The integral is linear on bounded integrable real functions. [[thm-linearity-of-the-lebesgue-integral-on-l-one]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F1], all costs are finite. The zero function is admissible, so their dual values are nonnegative. The admissible class is closed under $f\mapsto-f$; by [F5] this exchanges the objectives for $(\mu,\nu)$ and $(\nu,\mu)$, proving symmetry. If $\mu=\nu$, every objective vanishes, so $W_1^X(\mu,\mu)=0$. For any admissible $f$, [F5] gives $\int f\,d\mu-\int f\,d\nu=(\int f\,d\mu-\int f\,d\rho)+(\int f\,d\rho-\int f\,d\nu)\le W_1^X(\mu,\rho)+W_1^X(\rho,\nu)$. Taking the supremum and using [F1] gives the triangle inequality. [given, F1, F5, algebra]

2.1 Suppose $W_1^X(\mu,\nu)=0$. For admissible $f$, both its objective and the objective of $-f$ are at most zero by [F1], hence $\int f\,d\mu=\int f\,d\nu$. If $g$ is any real $L$-Lipschitz function for $L>0$, then $f=(g-g(x_0))/L$ is admissible; boundedness and integrability follow from [F1], and [F5] and the equal total masses imply $\int g\,d\mu=\int g\,d\nu$. Constants have the same integrals as well. [given, F1, F5, algebra]

2.2 Let $G\subseteq X$ be open with $G\ne X$. Define $g_n(x)=\min\{1,n\,d(x,X\setminus G)\}$ for $n\ge1$. The complement is nonempty, and [F2] makes $g_n$ an $n$-Lipschitz function: multiplication by $n$ multiplies its bound and truncation by $1$ cannot increase absolute differences. These continuous Borel functions satisfy $0\le g_n\uparrow\mathbf1_G$: on the complement the distance is zero; at every point of $G$, an open ball contained in $G$ gives strictly positive distance to the complement. By step 2.1, $\int g_n\,d\mu=\int g_n\,d\nu$ for every $n$. Applying [F3] yields $\mu(G)=\nu(G)$. For $G=X$, this equality is the equality of total masses. [step 2.1, F2, F3, F4, construct]

3.1 The family of Borel sets $A$ satisfying $\mu(A)=\nu(A)$ is a lambda-system: total masses agree, complements preserve equality by subtraction from one, and countable disjoint unions preserve equality by countable additivity. Step 2.2 shows that it contains all opens. By [F4] it contains every Borel set. Thus $\mu=\nu$, establishing separation; together with step 1.1 this proves all metric axioms. [step 1.1, step 2.2, F4] ∎
