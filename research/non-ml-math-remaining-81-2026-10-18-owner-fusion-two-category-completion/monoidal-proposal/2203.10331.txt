The Relative Deligne Tensor Product over
Pointed Braided Fusion Categories
Thibault D. D´ecoppet
June 2022
Abstract
We give a formula for the relative Deligne tensor product of two in-
decomposable ﬁnite semisimple module categories over a pointed braided
fusion category over an algebraically closed ﬁeld.
Introduction
The relative Deligne tensor product of module categories is a central construc-
tion of the theory of ﬁnite tensor categories, which categoriﬁes the relative
tensor product of modules over an algebra. For instance, working over an al-
gebraically closed ﬁeld of characteristic zero, it is shown in [ENO09] that, for
a ﬁxed fusion category C, graded extensions of C are classiﬁed by maps into
the Brauer-Picard 3-groupoid BrPic(C) of invertible C-C-bimodule categories.
In this case, the relative Deligne tensor product over C provides the operation
of composition of 1-morphisms. Another example is given by considering B a
braided fusion category. Then, the relative Deligne tensor product over B of two
ﬁnite semisimple right B-module categories possesses a right action by B. Thus,
we can consider the Picard 3-groupoid Pic(B) of invertible right B-module cate-
gories, which was shown to classify crossed braided extensions of B in [ENO09].
The two preceding examples have been recently shown to hold more generally
for any (braided) ﬁnite tensor categories over an arbitrary algebraically closed
ﬁeld (see [DN22]). Now, by deﬁnition, the Brauer-Picard and Picard groupoids
only know about invertible (bi)module categories. But the relative Deligne ten-
sor product provides a natural composition operation for all exact bimodule
categories.
This can be used to construct a symmetric monoidal 3-category
whose objects are ﬁnite tensor categories, and 1-morphisms are exact bimod-
ule categories (see [DSPS21]). Via the cobordism hypothesis, the study of this
symmetric monoidal 3-category initiated in [DSPS21] has highlighted the link
between ﬁnite tensor categories and low dimensional topology. In a somehwat
diﬀerent direction, given C a multifusion category over an algebraically closed
ﬁeld of characteristic zero, we may form Mod(C) the 2-category of all ﬁnite
semisimple right C-module categories. The properties of these 2-categories were
1

axiomatized in [DR18] under the deﬁnition of ﬁnite semisimple 2-categories.
Further, if B is a braided fusion category, the 2-category Mod(B) inherits a
rigid monoidal structure given by the relative Deligne tensor product over B,
and so is a fusion 2-category (see [DR18] for the deﬁnition). In particular, the
maximal subgroupoid of Mod(B) is Pic(B). Analogously, for a general fusion
category C, we recover BrPic(C) as the maximal subgroupoid of Mod(Z(C)),
the fusion 2-category associated to the Drinfel’d center of C. These last consid-
erations continue to hold over an arbitrary algebraically closed ﬁeld as discussed
in [D´ec21b].
Due to their connections with graded extensions, the Brauer-Picard group
and the Picard group have received a lot of attention (see [ENO09], [DN13],
[BN14], [Mom14], [GJS15], [GS16], [GP17], [EM18], [MN18], and [NR18]). But,
relatively few explicit computations of the relative Deligne tensor product of
non-invertible exact (bi)module categories are known. For instance, the relative
Deligne tensor product over the symmetric fusion category of representations of
a ﬁnite group was computed in [Gre10]. Another example is given as follows.
Let A be a ﬁnite abelian group, and write VectA for the fusion category of
A-graded ﬁnite dimensional vector spaces. Working over an algebraically closed
ﬁeld of characteristic zero, a formula for the relative Deligne tensor product of
all ﬁnite semisimple module categories over VectA with the trivial symmetric
braiding was given in [ENO09]. In [D´ec22], we extended their computations to
obtain a formula for the relative Deligne tensor product over Vectβ
A, where β is
any braiding on VectA. In the present article, we generalize this result in two
directions. Firstly, we will allow our base ﬁled to be any algebraically closed
ﬁeld. Secondly, we will let the underlying fusion category carry any associator ω,
and compute the relative Deligne tensor product of all ﬁnite semisimple module
categories over Vect(ω,β)
A
, where β is a compatible braiding. As a consequence,
we get an explicit formula for the fusion rule of Mod(Vect(ω,β)
A
). Finally, we
give tables for the relative Deligne tensor product over Vect(ω,β)
A
, when A is
either Z/p, Z/p2, Z/2 ⊕Z/2, or Z/4 ⊕Z/2 with various choices of associator
and compatible braiding. We also compute the Picard groups of Z/p ⊕Z/p and
Z/2 ⊕Z/2 ⊕Z/2 with speciﬁc choices of associators and compatible braidings.
Let us conclude by mentioning that, for some speciﬁc groups and braidings, sur-
prisingly similar formulas have been independently obtained in [RSS22] through
high energy physics considerations. The precise relation with our present work
remains to be clariﬁed.

Preliminaries
Let k be an algebraically closed ﬁeld, and C be a fusion category over k. A right
C-module category is a category M over k together with a linear right action
M × C →M and natural isomorphisms
µM,C,D : M ⊗(C ⊗D) →(M ⊗C) ⊗D,
for every M ∈M and C, D ∈C that are suitably coherent. We call such a
module category indecomposable if it cannot be written as the direct sum of
two non-trivial module subcategories. Every ﬁnite semisimple right C-module
category can be written as a direct sum of indecomposable ﬁnite semisimple
right C-module categories (see [EGNO15]).
Thanks to a theorem of Ostrik (see [Ost03], and [DSPS19] for its general-
ization to arbitrary ﬁelds), every ﬁnite semisimple right C-module category M
is equivalent to the right C-module category ModC(R) of left R-modules in C
for some algebra R in C. As this will be important for us later, let us be more
precise. One can construct a bilinear functor Hom(−, −) : Mop × M →C,
which provides an enrichment of M over C, and ﬁts into an adjunction
HomM(M ⊗C, N) ∼= HomC(C, Hom(M, N)),
with C ∈C and M, N ∈M. Now, for a suﬃciently general choice of P in M,
the functor

non-zero object.
We will also be interested in the following special class of module categories.
We say that a ﬁnite semisimple right C-module category M ≃ModC(R) is

1.3
A Decomposition Principle
We now wish to prove that the relative Deligne tensor product over a pointed
braided fusion category can be computed “one prime at a time”. This justiﬁes
why all of the examples we will consider later are ﬁnite abelian p-groups for some
prime p. For completeness, we begin by showing that every pointed braided
fusion category can be split into a Deligne tensor product of pointed braided
fusion categories whose groups of equivalence classes of simple objects have
coprime order.
Lemma 1.13. Let A and B be two ﬁnite abelian groups whose orders are co-
prime. Any pointed braided fusion category whose group of equivalence classes
of invertible object is A×B is equivalent to Vectq1

Proof. By [Mac50], quadratic forms C →k× on a ﬁnite abelian group C are in

(0, 0, 1) in Z/2⊕3. Assume that char(k) ̸= 2, and let β : Z/2⊕3 × Z/2⊕3 →k×
be the bilinear form given by β(ei, ej) = −1 if j = i, i + 1 and β(ei, ej) = 1
otherwise. Setting C := Vectβ
A, we will now prove that Pic(C) is isomorphic to
S4, the symmetric group on four elements.
We begin by determining the number of simple objects of Mod(C), which
coincides with the number of non-equivalent indecomposable ﬁnite semisim-
ple right C-module categories by lemma 1.8. Using corollary 1.7, we ﬁnd that
Mod(C) has 1 simple object corresponding to the subgroup of A of order 1, and
7 simple objects corresponding to subgroups of order 2, 7·2 simple objects corre-
sponding to subgroups of order 4 together with a class in H2(Z/2⊕2, k×) ∼= Z/2,
and 8 simple objects corresponding to A together with a class in H2(Z/2⊕3, k×) ∼=
Z/2⊕3. This means that Mod(C) has 30 simple objects.
Now, let us write E12 for the subgroup of A spanned by e1 and e2. The
restriction of β to E12 corresponds to the quadratic form q2 of example 3.3.
Thus, the inclusion E12 ֒→A induces a braided monoidal functor F12 : E →C.
By proposition 3.3.4 of [D´ec21b], this induces a monoidal functor Mod(F12) :
Mod(E) →Mod(C).
Further, as F12 is faithful, Mod(F12) is injective on
simple objects, thence it induces an injective map Pic(E) →Pic(C). But we
have identiﬁed Pic(E) ∼= S3 in example 3.3. This means that Mod(F12)(⟨a⟩) =
⟨e1⟩, and Mod(F12)(⟨b⟩) = ⟨e2⟩are invertible objects of Mod(C) and satisfy

in Pic(C). Similarly, we can consider the subgroup E23 of E spanned by e2
and e3. As the restriction of β to E23 also corresponds to q3, we get an inclu-
sion Mod(F23) : Mod(E) →Mod(C). We have Mod(F23)(⟨a⟩) = ⟨e2⟩, and
Mod(F23)(⟨b⟩) = ⟨e3⟩, so that ⟨e3⟩is invertible in Mod(C) and satisfy
⟨e3⟩2 = ⟨0⟩, (⟨e2⟩· ⟨e3⟩)3 = ⟨0⟩
in Pic(C).
