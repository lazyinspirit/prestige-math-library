# Reader 6: learned and data-driven inverse problems

Read date: 2026-10-18 (Australia/Sydney). Lane: learned regularizers and unrolled methods; plug-and-play / RED; score and diffusion posterior methods; neural operators for PDE inverse maps; robustness and uncertainty limits.

## Full texts obtained and read

All four documents below were downloaded in full from the versioned arXiv PDF endpoints, extracted page-by-page with PyMuPDF (the environment has no `pdftotext` executable), and read from first page through the final page in bounded chunks. Page labels and page counts are preserved in the ignored extracted text files. Hashes and authoritative/version metadata are in the companion JSON. These notes are paraphrases, not copied passages.

1. **Daras et al., “A Survey on Diffusion Models for Inverse Problems,” arXiv:2410.00083v1, submitted 2024-09-30.** Full 38-page versioned preprint, including its theorem proofs/appendices and bibliography. This is a useful map of the field, explicitly a work in progress and non-exhaustive. The paper distinguishes four methodological families: explicit approximations to the time-dependent measurement score; variational posterior approximations; optimization over latent inputs to deterministic generators; and particle/MCMC methods intended to become exact in an infinite-computation limit. The key identity is the conditional-score split `∇ log p_t(x_t|y) = ∇ log p_t(x_t) + ∇ log p_t(y|x_t)`. Although the unconditional score is learned, the likelihood term is generally an intractable integral over `p(x_0|x_t)`. Common DPS, DDRM, DDNM and related updates replace that term by point, Gaussian, moment-matched, pseudo-inverse or proximal approximations; these are algorithms, not exact posterior samplers by definition. The appendix proves Tweedie’s formula for additive Gaussian corruption and the equality of the DSM and conditional-mean squared-error minimizers under the stated differentiability/support assumptions. It does not establish convergence or posterior correctness for each surveyed inverse solver. Its “asymptotically exact” label describes a limit claim under method-specific assumptions and unbounded computation; finite discretized samplers retain approximation error. The survey itself cautions that computation may not scale and reports the general intractability of posterior sampling even when prior sampling is fast.

2. **Xu, Sun, Liu, Wohlberg, and Kamilov, “Provable Convergence of Plug-and-Play Priors with MMSE denoisers,” arXiv:2005.07685v1, 2020-05-15.** Full 10-page letter and appendices. It studies the precise PnP-ISTA iteration `x_t = D_σ(x_{t-1} − γ∇g(x_{t-1}))`. For a non-degenerate prior density on `R^n`, exact AWGN MMSE denoiser `D_σ(z)=E[X|X+N(0,σ²I)=z]`, continuously differentiable `g` with `L`-Lipschitz gradient, and finite lower bound on the constructed objective, the paper constructs an (in general nonconvex) extended-valued regularizer `h_mmse` on the image of `D_σ`. For fixed `0<γ≤1/L`, its Theorem 1 proves monotone decrease of `g+h_mmse` and `∥∇(g+h_mmse)(x_t)∥→0`. The proof uses Tweedie’s identity to show the exact MMSE denoiser is the proximal minimizer for this possibly nonconvex penalty, then a quadratic majorizer and summability of surrogate decrease. It does **not** assume convexity or nonexpansiveness; the result is only stationarity, not global minimization, uniqueness, a rate, or posterior sampling. The paper’s assumptions and objective involve the exact MMSE for a specified prior/noise, whereas a trained CNN that empirically approximates MMSE is not covered by the theorem. The experiments are small Bernoulli–Gaussian sparse recovery and explicitly show suboptimality of this ISTA model relative to GAMP due to the false iterative-AWGN error model. There is a notational slip in the last displayed line of Appendix A.2: the residual-gradient norm is written as a residual-function norm; the preceding descent argument establishes the intended vanishing-gradient conclusion.

3. **Zheng et al., “InverseBench: Benchmarking Plug-and-Play Diffusion Priors for Inverse Problems in Physical Sciences,” ICLR 2025; arXiv:2503.11043v2, version dated 2025-09-30.** Full 29-page conference paper and appendices. It compares 14 plug-and-play diffusion methods and domain-specific baselines across first-Born linear optical scattering, multi-coil MRI, nonlinear black-hole interferometric imaging, full-waveform inversion, and 2D Navier–Stokes initial-vorticity inversion. It records nonlinear/nonclosed-form/PDE access constraints and counts forward/gradient evaluations. Its results are empirical on the authors’ selected datasets, training distributions, tuning budgets and small test sets. They find some methods with more forward-model queries improve accuracy on explicit forward maps, but can be expensive; this pattern breaks for PDE solvers. In their FWI/Navier–Stokes implementations, methods can be sensitive to numerical admissibility (including CFL constraints) and inner Langevin noise; a small step-size change may make a solver ignore observations or become unstable. The paper also exhibits prior-mismatch failures: reconstructions of out-of-distribution sources are biased toward trained prior features. Thus the work motivates constrained parameterizations, solver-aware updates, prior/forward mismatch studies, and multimodal posterior evaluation; it proves no convergence, robustness, calibration, or universal superiority theorem. The authors’ own experimental protocol includes a one-sample validation set for FWI and one-sample validation / ten-sample test for Navier–Stokes, so generalization claims should be scoped accordingly.

4. **de Hoop, Kovachki, Lassas, and Nelsen, “Extension and neural operator approximation of the electrical impedance tomography inverse map,” arXiv:2511.20361v1, 2025-11-25.** Full 80-page preprint, including technical appendices and experiment details. It is a formal neural-operator result, not merely an empirical FNO paper. For Calderón’s conductivity problem on a smooth bounded domain, conductivities are uniformly elliptic, equal the background near the boundary, and lie in a dimension-dependent bounded-variation class for `d=2` or bounded `C^{1,α}` class for `d≥3`. Its key inverse stability estimate is logarithmic in the DtN discrepancy, with an `L²` conductivity error. The authors transfer this to a Hilbert-space norm on (smoothed, when necessary) NtD integral kernels using smoothing and Hilbert–Schmidt inequalities. A Hilbert-space extension theorem then extends the inverse map to all kernel inputs while retaining the logarithmic modulus. Compactness of the admissible conductivity set and continuity of the forward map allow a generalized FNO to uniformly approximate this extension on compact sets. Theorem 4.8 gives, for compact noise set `K` and noise norm at most `δ`, an existence bound `sup ||γ−Ψ(κ_γ+η)||₂ ≤ ε + C ω(δ)`, with the inverse problem’s logarithmic modulus `ω`. Assumptions include a smooth unit-contained domain with bi-Lipschitz boundary charts, globally Lipschitz smooth non-polynomial activation, exact continuum PDE / operator definitions, bounded admissible conductivities, compact noise set and existence of a sufficiently expressive architecture. This is an **existence** theorem; it is not a constructive training guarantee, finite-sample generalization bound, solver-cost bound, calibration result, or proof that a particular trained model achieves the bound. Numerics are 2D unit-disk experiments with finite-element-generated NtD data, three chosen distributions and finite samples. The paper explicitly notes the logarithmic instability remains: obtaining `O(ε)` worst-case reconstruction error can require exponentially small measurement noise. It also states that the framework does not apply automatically to every inverse problem; for example, the smoothing argument used here can fail for hyperbolic PDE boundary maps.

## Cross-source mathematical conclusions and cautions

- A learned reconstruction can be supervised estimator, learned update rule, regularizer/denoiser, score field, or amortized inverse operator. These mathematical objects have different contracts. Unrolling a finite number of iterations inherits no convergence theorem unless the finite learned map is separately analyzed; training loss minimization is not evidence that an output solves the original inverse problem.
- In PnP/RED, replacing a proximal map by an arbitrary denoiser does not generally define minimization of a scalar objective. For a proper closed convex penalty, a proximal map must have structural properties (in finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces, in particular firm nonexpansiveness / cyclic monotonicity conditions); a generic CNN does not acquire them by being called a denoiser. Fixed-point convergence under an averaged/nonexpansive operator hypothesis is distinct from optimization optimality and from Bayesian posterior correctness.
- Score-based methods use learned approximations to time-indexed scores, numerical SDE/ODE discretization, and typically an approximate data-consistency/conditional-score term. Each source of error must be named. Tweedie identities are exact for the stated Gaussian corruption and exact conditional mean; substituting a learned network creates an approximation. Posterior sampling claims additionally require appropriate transition kernels, invariant target, and convergence/mixing arguments. “Diffusion prior” alone implies none of these.
- Identifiability and stability are separate. For a linear map `A`, any `v∈ker(A)` leaves noiseless data unchanged (`A(x+v)=Ax`); without structural restrictions or a prior that distinguishes these possibilities, unique recovery is impossible. Even uniqueness does not imply stability: Calderón/EIT provides a concrete example with logarithmic stability. Noise amplification or learned priors cannot remove this information-theoretic limit uniformly over unrestricted signal classes.
- Conditional expectation is optimal under squared-error risk, but a posterior mean may be visually implausible or outside a nonconvex signal class. MAP, posterior mean, perceptual/distortion objectives and posterior sampling are distinct targets; the survey reviews the perception–distortion obstruction but the underlying theorem was not independently full-text read in this lane.
- Distribution shift in the benchmark is direct evidence that strong learned priors can suppress rare but data-consistent solutions. Reported robustness is distribution- and operator-specific empirical evidence, not a guarantee under arbitrary prior mismatch, unknown forward model, measurement corruption or PDE parameter constraints.
- Uncertainty quantification from repeated generated outputs is not automatically calibrated posterior uncertainty. If approximate samplers or surrogate scores are used, posterior fidelity, coverage, calibration and mode capture each need explicit assumptions and verification. The benchmark identifies multimodality as an open research need; do not use such an open question as a logical supplier.

## Possible frontier structure and prerequisite gaps

The numerical inverse-problem and optimization material already supplies much of the deterministic foundation, so do not duplicate spectral filtering, convex proximal-gradient, generic adjoint differentiation, or existing particle/MCMC infrastructure. A learned/data-driven section can instead center on: (i) supervised estimator and unrolled-algorithm definitions, with risk/identifiability and stability tracked separately; (ii) learned denoiser/PnP and RED fixed-point procedures with the exact regularity/operator assumptions exposed; (iii) score/diffusion prior definitions and measurement-guided algorithms, with approximate-score/discretization terms separated from exact conditional posterior scores; (iv) amortized neural-operator approximation for a PDE inverse map, using Calderón as a proved example if the author can reproduce the stability/extension/compactness argument; and (v) open problems on calibrated posterior uncertainty under learned priors, robustness under forward/prior shift, and efficient stable solvers for nonlinear PDE operators. These are directions, not approved pair IDs.

Likely local prerequisites if exact existing claims cannot support the desired scope: a formal non-identifiability result for a measurement map with a nontrivial kernel; an inverse stability/modulus-of-continuity definition; posterior mean/MAP/posterior sampling target distinctions; a denoiser/proximal-map characterization; fixed-point convergence for averaged/nonexpansive maps; a learned reconstruction risk/deployment shift definition; and a rigorous data-consistency/solver-admissibility contract for PDE-constrained inverse procedures. A Calderón/FNO theorem is substantial enough to require its own intermediate modules for EIT forward map, inverse stability, Hilbert–Schmidt kernels, extension, compactness and operator approximation; a citation to the 80-page paper is not a local proof.

## Research horizons not consumed as theorem suppliers

1. Certified uncertainty for diffusion-based inverse solvers: calibration / coverage under model and score error, data mismatch, and finite computational budget.
2. Distributionally robust recovery with prior shift or unknown forward operators, while preserving data consistency and explicit stability bounds.
3. Stable learned inversion for PDE systems with admissibility constraints (CFL or positivity), differentiable/black-box solvers, and computationally realistic guarantees.
4. Finite-compute posterior sampling and multimodal mode coverage in high-dimensional nonlinear inverse problems; exactness only asymptotically is not a practical finite-compute certificate.
5. Sample complexity and generalization of neural operators for genuinely infinite-dimensional inverse maps, including noisy finite measurements and discretization transfer.

These are open directions. The full texts above support concrete hypotheses, examples and limits, but they do not establish universal or general-purpose learned inverse solvers, prior-shift robustness, calibrated diffusion uncertainty, or finite-compute posterior exactness.
