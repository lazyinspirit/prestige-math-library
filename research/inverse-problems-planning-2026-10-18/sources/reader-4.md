## Mathematical claims, hypotheses, and proof routes

### Radon/X-ray imaging

Salo §2.1 pp. 4–7 proves the Fourier slice theorem and, for compactly supported smooth functions on R² with all line data, injectivity and the exact normal-operator identity `R*R = 4π|D|⁻¹`; filtered backprojection gives `f=(4π)⁻¹R*|D_s|Rf`. The resulting Sobolev stability estimate is posed as Exercise 2.1, not proved in the notes. These statements require complete Euclidean data and do not imply stability for truncated or limited-angle data.

For a compact smooth Riemannian manifold with smooth boundary, Salo §2.2–2.4 pp. 7–19 assumes simplicity (strictly convex boundary, nontrapping, no conjugate points) for scalar geodesic X-ray injectivity. The normal operator is elliptic of order −1 in the interior under simplicity; that supplies microlocal recovery of singularities and a parametrix modulo smoothing, not by itself global injectivity. The proof route in dimension two reduces `If=0` to `VXu=0` for a compactly supported primitive on the unit sphere bundle, applies the Pestov identity, and uses positivity of the index form from no conjugate points. The printed reduction is explicitly simplified to 2D and `f∈C_c∞(M°)`; the theorem statement is broader and references the full proof in PSU23. Salo pp. 10–12 records counterexamples on trapped geometries and states open injectivity questions for general strictly convex nontrapping manifolds; higher-dimensional positive results require a strictly convex foliation. Do not claim universal geodesic injectivity.

The 2023 Mazzucchelli–Salo–Tzou article §§1, 4–6 (pp. 1–11, 19–27, 42–44) gives a more current microlocal distinction. For an analytic double-fibration transform, the Bolker condition means the left canonical-relation projection is an injective immersion. Under this condition, absence of analytic wavefront singularities in measured data implies absence at the corresponding object covector (Theorem 1.2). Analytic microlocal continuation yields local uniqueness only when the unknown vanishes on one side of an appropriate hypersurface and the conormal covector is visible to the transform (Theorem 1.3). For 2D analytic strictly convex nontrapping manifolds, a strictly convex foliation/layer-stripping argument yields global injectivity for analytic nonvanishing weights (Theorem 1.4). The Bolker condition fails in geometries with relevant conjugate points; restricted data can also remove visible covectors. In seismic settings failure is associated with artifacts in the normal operator rather than a universal reconstruction guarantee (article pp. 10–11).

### Wave-equation coefficient/source identification

Salo §3 pp. 20–39 models the hyperbolic DN map for `(∂²_t−Δ_g+q)u=0` on `M×(0,T)`, with zero initial or terminal Cauchy data and boundary Dirichlet-to-Neumann measurements. For real smooth compactly supported interior potentials `q_j`, equality of DN maps yields an integral identity by Green's formula. Geometric-optics solutions in a simple background, and Gaussian beams on a general compact background, concentrate solution products on light rays. Thus the data determine line integrals of `q_1−q_2` along nontrapped maximal geodesics of length `<T` (Theorem 3.1). To conclude `q_1=q_2`, one additionally needs simplicity/injectivity of the spatial geodesic X-ray transform and observation time exceeding the maximal geodesic length (Theorem 3.2). Finite propagation gives an immediate nonidentifiability region beyond distance `T/2` from the boundary (Remark 3.3). For time-dependent coefficients the recovered quantities are light-ray integrals, and injectivity is again conditional on geometry. The notes explicitly list general time-dependent and Lorentzian injectivity questions as open (pp. 22–23).

The Gaussian-beam proof route is constructive at the level of asymptotic solutions: solve the eikonal equation to infinite order along a null bicharacteristic, choose a complex quadratic phase whose Hessian solves a Riccati equation and has positive imaginary part, solve transport equations, and use Gaussian decay away from the ray. For simple geometry, real geometric optics instead solves the eikonal equation globally with polar distance. Neither construction supplies a noise-stability modulus for the coefficient inverse map.

### Calderón/EIT coefficient identification

Salo §4 pp. 39–57 distinguishes scalar conductivity, anisotropic conductivity, and Schrödinger potential formulations. In the Euclidean smooth scalar case, boundary determination recovers the full boundary Taylor series from the DN map (Theorem 4.3); the proof uses the Alessandrini identity and boundary-concentrating complex-phase quasimodes. For interior recovery in dimension at least three, the linear result shown is conditional on a transversally anisotropic product geometry `R×M₀` and injectivity of the geodesic X-ray transform on `M₀` (Theorem 4.1); the CGO solutions and a Carleman solvability estimate reduce the data identity to geodesic integrals. The notes explicitly mark general manifold uniqueness and partial-data uniqueness as open questions (p. 41). In 2D, the text points to separate Euclidean results and isothermal coordinates; do not conflate those with the dimension-at-least-three result.

For the semilinear equation `−Δ_g u+q u³=0`, small boundary data and a third Fréchet derivative produce an identity involving products of four harmonic functions; intersecting geodesic concentration then yields uniqueness on the same product geometry (Theorem 4.2). This nonlinear result does not resolve the corresponding linear problem in arbitrary geometry. The anisotropic conductivity problem has a diffeomorphism gauge: boundary-fixing changes of variables preserve the boundary data, so the metric is not generally identifiable as a tensor without quotienting by that gauge. The notes discuss the reduction to a metric equation but do not prove a general anisotropic stability theorem.

### Current analytic tomography frontier and limits

The 2023 Mazzucchelli–Salo–Tzou article proves recovery of analytic wavefront directions for analytic weighted transforms under the local Bolker condition (Theorem 1.2, proof §§5.1–5.6 pp. 27–42). The argument localizes to a conormal/Fourier-integral model, represents FBI transforms using Gaussian wave packets, reduces the kernel to an oscillatory integral, proves a unique nondegenerate complex critical point, and obtains the required positive quadratic imaginary phase by analytic stationary phase. Local uniqueness then combines this microlocal recovery with analytic continuation. For null-bicharacteristic/light-ray transforms, the result requires a nonconjugate segment and covectors transverse to the ray satisfying the paper's nonparallel condition; timelike/lightlike visibility is not interchangeable (Theorems 1.5–1.6 and §§4.4, 6). For analytic 2D geodesic transforms, global uniqueness needs strict convexity, nontrapping, analyticity, and layer stripping (Theorem 1.4). The paper also explains that when Bolker fails, seismic normal operators may generate artifact branches.

This work is a genuine contemporary theoretical frontier result, but its scope is analytic microlocal uniqueness/support, not a uniform quantitative stability estimate under noise, a practical reconstruction algorithm, or all smooth geometries. The lecture notes likewise give no general stability result for nonlinear PDE coefficient recovery. This reader therefore supplies precise conditional uniqueness and visibility material; quantitative stability, partial-data robustness, and reconstruction under realistic sampling/noise need separate sources/obligations.

**Proposed modules and contracts.**

1. `def-double-fibration-transform-and-canonical-relation`: For oriented manifolds `X,G` and embedded `Z⊂G×X` with both coordinate projections submersions and `dim(G)+dim(X)>dim(Z)>dim(G)≥dim(X)`, define the weighted fiber integral for a smooth nonvanishing weight and prove its conormal canonical relation `C=(N*Z\0)'`. MST §§3–4, pp. 16–27 proves the equivalences. For restricted/lower-dimensional curve families, do not replace the conditions by “no conjugate points” without checking the stated rank hypotheses.
3. `def-analytic-wavefront-fbi-transform` and `thm-analytic-stationary-phase-for-local-fio`: Define analytic wavefront regularity through exponential decay of a specified Gaussian/FBI packet transform. Prove a sufficient analytic stationary-phase estimate for a unique nondegenerate complex critical point, with the positive quadratic imaginary phase and exponential error control. MST cites Sjöstrand’s general FBI characterization and analytic stationary phase; it proves its tailored kernel reduction, critical-point analysis, and phase estimates in §§5.1–5.6, pp. 27–42. General analytic FBI/stationary-phase foundations are not reproved there. Either supply those foundations or prove a self-contained specialized lemma strong enough for the stated application.
4. `thm-analytic-singularity-recovery-under-bolker`: For an analytic double fibration transform and a point where Bolker holds, prove `(z,ζ)∉WF_a(Rf) ⇒ (x,η)∉WF_a(f)` for compactly supported distributions. Follow with a separate `thm-microlocal-analytic-continuation` contract: if a distribution vanishes on one side of a C² hypersurface and the relevant conormal analytic singularity is absent, then it vanishes locally. MST Theorem 1.3/§6 applies this cited continuation principle; the general principle is not proved from first principles in MST.

The article therefore proves the double-fibration kernel geometry, its Bolker criteria, the specialized analytic recovery argument, and conditional applications. It imports general distribution/FIO calculus, analytic FBI/stationary phase, and microlocal analytic continuation. The resulting uniqueness is qualitative and conditional on analyticity, visible covectors, and Bolker; it is not a quantitative stability result.

## Scaffold implications

Build a narrowly scoped PDE inverse track around distinct data maps and unknown classes: (i) scalar source recovery from elliptic/wave boundary or interior data, (ii) scalar Schrödinger/conductivity DN identification, (iii) geodesic Radon/X-ray and limited-data visibility, and (iv) inverse scattering as a separate model. State well-posedness/forward map before injectivity; state stability separately and quantitatively only where proved. Include counterexample/open-problem carriers for trapping/conjugate-point failure, finite-time invisible regions, partial-angle invisible wavefronts, anisotropic boundary-fixing diffeomorphism gauge, and the unresolved general-manifold/partial-data questions. For algorithms, specify acquisition geometry, discretized forward/adjoint maps, regularization and stopping criteria, and label a method's conditional convergence separately from the exact-data uniqueness theorem. The sources establish mathematical pathways but do not provide enough to assert robust numerical performance or universal stable inversion.
