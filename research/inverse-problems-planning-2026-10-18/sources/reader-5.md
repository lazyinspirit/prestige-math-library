## Full texts read

### Christian Clason, *Regularization of Inverse Problems: Lecture Notes*

- Original source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00617v2 and https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.00617v2
- Author: Christian Clason. Version 2, submitted 8 February 2021; PDF title page dated 9 February 2021. Retrieved 2026-10-18.
- Complete PDF: 137 pages. Read the complete extraction, pages 1–137, in bounded chunks, including the bibliography. PDF SHA-256 `696b4f580464ec0e1da6a1863b91d9142aa58b8b841b8ea437b5944152cd649e`. Extracted text SHA-256 `1fc2fc6b617668bd4e8fdd9811af7981bf6c3fff2fe7ffbc8a81b589ded1d38b`.
- Scope and caveat: the notes explicitly restrict their main theory to Hilbert spaces and describe the Bayesian/statistical portions as cursory. They give a strong, self-contained analytic account of nonlinear Hilbert-space regularization, but do not develop general Banach-space penalties, sparse recovery, TV/BV, or manifold-valued constraints.

Relevant verified results and proof ingredients:

- Chapters 9–10, pp. 81–99: local ill-posedness; Fréchet/Gâteaux differentiation; Lipschitz derivative gives the quadratic remainder `||F(x+h)-F(x)-F'(x)h|| ≤ L||h||²/2` (Lemma 9.5). The tangential cone condition (TCC) `||F(x+h)-F(x)-F'(x)h|| ≤ η||F(x+h)-F(x)||`, `η<1`, yields the two-sided residual comparison `(1-η)||F(x+h)-F(x)|| ≤ ||F'(x)h|| ≤ (1+η)||F(x+h)-F(x)||` (Theorem 9.8). Under the TCC and `x†-x0 ∈ N(F'(x†))⊥`, the exact solution is the unique local `x0`-minimum-norm solution (Theorem 9.9). The text cautions that these abstract nonlinear conditions are often difficult to verify and sometimes fail.
- Nonlinear Tikhonov, pp. 89–99: for weakly closed `F` with weakly closed domain, the direct method gives a minimizer of `0.5||F(x)-y||² + α/2||x-x0||²`; bounded minimizing sequences have weakly convergent subsequences and weak closedness identifies the limit (Theorem 10.1). Minimizers are not generally unique, so stability is via weak cluster points (Theorem 10.2). Under the paper's local differentiability, convex-domain and source/nonlinearity hypotheses, classical source conditions imply an `O(sqrt(δ))` estimate with `α ≍ δ`; the discrepancy principle requires extra conditions (Theorems 10.4–10.6). A variational source inequality with coefficient `β1<1` directly gives `O(sqrt(δ))` solution error and `O(δ)` residual for `α ≍ δ` (Theorem 10.7). A classical derivative source condition implies that VSC under either a Lipschitz derivative or TCC (Lemma 10.8). These are global-minimizer statements; the notes explicitly say numerical methods may find only local minima.
- Nonlinear Landweber, pp. 100–108: with continuously Fréchet differentiable `F`, a local TCC with `η<1/2`, `||F'(x)||≤1`, a ball containing an exact solution and an initial point in the smaller ball, the iteration `x_{n+1}=x_n+F'(x_n)^*(yδ-F(x_n))` is Fejér monotone while the residual exceeds `2(1+η)δ/(1-2η)`. Morozov stopping with `τ>2(1+η)/(1-2η)` gives a finite stopping index bounded by `Cδ⁻²`; the proof sums the decrease in squared error and residual. Exact-data convergence follows from square summability of residuals and a Cauchy argument; noisy-data convergence uses fixed-prefix stability (Theorems 11.4–11.6). Conditions are local and Hilbert-space-specific.
- Levenberg–Marquardt, pp. 109–115: each Newton correction is the unique minimizer of a Tikhonov-regularized linearized least-squares subproblem. A forcing rule sets the linearized residual to a fixed fraction `σ` of the nonlinear residual. A range approximation condition with `γ>1`, local starting-point condition and stronger product-type TCC yield Fejér decrease and a discrepancy stopping index `O(1+|log δ|)` (Theorems 11.8–11.13). The source represents an exact nonlinear Newton/LM scheme, not a universal guarantee for inexact linear solvers.
- Iteratively regularized Gauss–Newton (IRGNM), pp. 115–119: the update minimizes the linearized data term while penalizing distance of the new iterate to `x0`. The proof separates spectral Tikhonov bias, noisy-data error and nonlinear linearization error. Under compactness/continuous differentiability, Lipschitz derivative, source representation `x†-x0=|F'(x†)|^ν w`, `ν∈[1,2]`, small source norm, geometric parameter decrease `1<α_n/α_{n+1}≤q`, and a stopping/noise coupling `α_N^{(ν+1)/2}≤τδ≤α_n^{(ν+1)/2}` before stopping, it proves `||x_N^δ-x†||=O(δ^{ν/(ν+1)})` (Theorems 11.15–11.16). The notes point to literature for discrepancy versions and more general assumptions; do not silently broaden this rate.

### José A. Iglesias, Gwenael Mercier, Otmar Scherzer, *A note on convergence of solutions of total variation regularized linear inverse problems*

- Original source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.06495v3 and https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.06495v3
- Authors as verified on the title page: José A. Iglesias, Gwenael Mercier, Otmar Scherzer. arXiv v3 dated 26 March 2018; journal reference *Inverse Problems* 34(5), 055011 (2018), DOI 10.1088/1361-6420/aab92a. Retrieved 2026-10-18.
- Complete PDF: 26 pages. Read all 26 pages, including appendices, figure pages and bibliography. PDF SHA-256 `0c9f32f3fca0d0cf0faa151736ef50cfe21c3472da1dbf5e97e4919d2057c362`; extraction SHA-256 `eb8e8b837e90bcc59f6a0543f69bba60a06dbe54754d437dc28aa44eb33c2fe3`.
- Verified setting: linear bounded `A:L²(Ω)→L²(Σ)`; 2D functions and BV/finite-perimeter geometry; full space and bounded Lipschitz domains with Dirichlet/full-space/Neumann TV conventions. This is not a nonlinear-TV theorem or a Banach-space generalization.

Precise results checked:

- Primal functionals are `0.5||Au-f||² + α TV(u)` with separate boundary conventions. Existence uses bounded TV and L² control plus BV compactness/lower semicontinuity; the Neumann proof separately handles the constant nullspace (Propositions 1–2, pp. 3–5).
- The source condition is `R(A*) ∩ ∂TV(u†) ≠ ∅`, where `u†` is a minimum-TV solution. Fenchel duality identifies the dual variable constraint `A*p∈∂TV(0)`, and the source condition is equivalent to attainment of the limiting dual problem (Theorem 1, Lemma 2, pp. 5–7). Under this condition, the unique penalized dual maximizers converge strongly to the minimum-norm limiting dual certificate; conversely, bounded dual maximizers imply the source condition (Proposition 3, pp. 7–8).
- Audit-corrected 2026-10-18: for `α_n→0` and `||w_n||||A*||/α_n≤η`, with `η<2√π` in full-space/Dirichlet cases and `η<1/C(Ω)` in the Neumann case, after a subsequence and for almost every level the sets converge in measure and their boundaries converge in Hausdorff distance to those of a selected minimum-TV limit. Bounded Dirichlet domains additionally need the variational-curvature lower bound on the complement. The earlier summary “α=o(δ)” was incorrect (Theorem 2, PDF pp. 9–10, equations (19)–(22)); the read attestation above remains the reader’s original attestation, not a new full-text read by the audit agent. The proof links dual variables to generalized curvature, proves uniform density estimates for level sets, then uses compactness/coarea and finite-perimeter geometry (Theorems 2 and Section 4, pp. 8–19). The density argument changes by boundary condition; the Neumann case cannot use the ordinary whole-space isoperimetric inequality directly.
- Applications checked include circular Radon inversion and deblurring. Source conditions are explicit and not automatic: examples include mollified indicators and characteristic functions of bounded smooth sets. The numerical primal-dual method is reported as an implementation, not established here as a separate convergence theorem (Section 5, pp. 19–21). The appendix proves the Fenchel duality, 1-homogeneous subgradient identity, coarea and perimeter facts used by the main argument (pp. 21–24).

### Barbara Kaltenbacher, *Convergence rates under a range invariance condition with application to electrical impedance tomography*

- Original source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18704v1 and https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.18704v1
- Author: Barbara Kaltenbacher. arXiv v1 submitted 27 March 2024. Title page identifies *IMA Journal of Numerical Analysis* (2024), article/drad044; bibliography identifies the 2023 online publication. Retrieved 2026-10-18.
- Complete PDF: 27 pages. Read all 27 pages, including appendix and references. PDF SHA-256 `d9796ef6f95294c1416d54f1b6752d8ce1665e6757cd0510a57d368399bbf809`; extraction SHA-256 `ff3fe849185790ffc3db767d9978616f358eaab590fd0e76be2cae6e7e910608`.
- This is the contemporary research source for the lane. It works with Banach-space parameter and data maps, VSCs, range invariance, relaxed inverse formulations, PDE coefficient identification and EIT. It should not be conflated with reader 4's tomography/scattering uniqueness lane: the contribution reviewed here is rate analysis for regularized nonlinear optimization/iteration.

Precise claims and caveats:

- TCC is contrasted with differential range invariance `F(x)-F(x0)=K r(x)`, `K∈L(eX,Y)`. Range invariance can remain verifiable under partial/boundary observations where TCC is not known, but may require enlarging parameter or data spaces, creating nonidentifiability which a penalty must remove (pp. 2–5, 17–18).
- A VSC is imposed on the original unrelaxed problem: `-<ξ,x-x†> ≤ b Δ_Rξ(x,x†)+ψ(||F(x)-F(x†)||^p)`, `b<1`, over a regularizer sublevel. The index function must satisfy the paper's monotonicity, lower-rate and conjugate conditions; Hölder and logarithmic classes are treated (pp. 6–7).
- Joint variational regularization under range invariance proves rates for the data residual, Bregman distance, constraint penalty and consistency residual under the stated regularizer compatibility, Lipschitz `r`, parameter/noise coupling, and approximate-minimizer tolerance (Theorem 3, pp. 8–11). Split minimization needs an extra penalized regularizer component and a coupling inequality to control the split error; it does not inherit the joint theorem for free (Theorem 5, Corollary 6, pp. 12–14).
- A frozen Newton scheme requires decreasing `α_n`, tolerance bounded by the index-dependent noise scale, a discrepancy-like stopping index based on an a priori `α` sequence, a growth condition on consecutive conjugate index values, linked `β_n`, and a small Taylor remainder condition (2.36) with an explicit smallness bound. Under these, the stopped method obtains data residual and range/penalty consistency rates (Theorem 7, pp. 15–17). This is distinct from the ordinary LM/IRGNM recurrences in Clason.
- EIT applications are not abstract blanket examples: the paper spells out parameter/state/observation spaces, all-at-once versus reduced equations, relaxation operators, Taylor remainder bounds, source regularity classes and logarithmic rates for 3D conductivity recovery (Sections 3, pp. 19–24). The boundary-data/source regularity assumptions matter; claims should retain them.
- Appendix proves an unconditioned subsequential convergence result under explicit compactness/lower-semicontinuity topology assumptions and parameter scaling, while uniqueness upgrades it (Proposition 12, pp. 25–26). This is not a substitute for VSC rate hypotheses.

Thus the exact local carriers support generic optimization interfaces, but several local prerequisite modules must be proposed before the inverse-problems claims are authorable: (i) nonlinear forward maps between Banach/Hilbert spaces, Fréchet derivative/adjoint, weak closedness and direct-method existence; (ii) TCC and range-invariance definitions and their valid residual/linearization consequences; (iii) Bregman distance, convex subdifferential, variational source condition and index-function conjugate calculus; (iv) spatial BV, distributional gradient, TV/seminorm, finite-perimeter/coarea and domain/boundary TV conventions; (v) Banach-space penalty/source regularization including p-convexity and norm-to-Bregman conversion; (vi) the assumptions and stopping/parameter contracts for Landweber, LM and IRGNM as distinct regularization algorithms. Compressed sensing additionally needs exact restricted injectivity/nullspace or source-certificate hypotheses, and manifold constraints need a chosen metric/geometry plus retraction/projection existence and compatibility with the forward map. These are obligations for the integration author, not claims that every proposed result needs every module.

## Lane recommendations grounded in the texts

Prioritize a compact but linked inverse-problems scaffold around: nonlinear ill-posed model and local identifiability; TCC and alternative range invariance; nonlinear Tikhonov existence/stability plus classical and variational source conditions; nonlinear Landweber and discrepancy stop; regularized LM and IRGNM with their different parameter/stopping hypotheses; Bregman/TV regularization with source certificate, error measure and geometric level-set result; sparse penalty recovery certificates; and a constrained/manifold inverse model. Keep convergence, rates, minimizer existence, algorithm definition, numerical local convergence, model mismatch and identifiability as separate claims. State model mismatch separately (e.g. data generated outside `range(F)` or misspecified `F`); the cited exact-data rates do not establish robustness to arbitrary operator error. Use the published optimization carriers only for the exact finite-dimensional mechanics they establish.
