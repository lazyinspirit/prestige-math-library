## Clason: results, hypotheses, and proof architecture

**Ill-posedness, pseudoinverse, and singular systems.** Printed §§3.1–3.2, pp. 16–26 (PDF pp. 18–28), define the Moore–Penrose inverse by restricting a bounded Hilbert-space operator to `N(T)^⊥ → R(T)` and extending it to `D(T†)=R(T)⊕R(T)^⊥`. For data in this domain, it returns the unique minimum-norm least-squares solution; all least-squares solutions differ by the kernel. The normal equation is `T*T x=T*y`. Theorem 3.7 proves `T†` is continuous exactly when `R(T)` is closed, via the closed graph theorem in one direction and continuous extension/density in the other. Corollary 3.8: compact `K` with infinite-dimensional range has noncontinuous pseudoinverse; otherwise a bounded inverse on `N(K)^⊥` would make the identity on the infinite-dimensional range compact. This makes precise why finite-dimensional invertibility intuition fails.

Theorem 3.9 (pp. 21–23; PDF 25–27): for compact `K:X→Y` between Hilbert spaces, positive singular values decrease to zero (or finitely terminate), with orthonormal bases of `R(K)` and `R(K*)`; `Kv_n=σ_n u_n`, `K*u_n=σ_n v_n`, and `Kx=Σ σ_n<x,v_n>u_n`. Proof: apply the compact self-adjoint spectral theorem to `K*K`, set `σ_n=sqrt(λ_n)` and `u_n=Kv_n/σ_n`, prove orthonormality and completeness, then push finite partial sums through continuous `K`. Theorem 3.10 (pp. 23–26; PDF 27–30) gives the Picard range criterion: for `y∈R(K)`, `y∈R(K)` iff `Σ σ_n^{-2}|<y,u_n>|²<∞`; coefficients of `K†y` are `<y,u_n>/σ_n`. Essential caveats: a nullspace component is invisible, range may be dense but nonclosed, and square summability is the actual solvability condition.

**Regularization and optimality barrier.** Printed §4.1, pp. 30–35 (PDF 32–37), defines a regularization as bounded operators `R_α` converging pointwise to `T†` on its domain. For noisy data with deterministic norm error `≤δ`, the canonical split is propagated data error `≤δ||R_α||` plus approximation bias `||R_αy−T†y||`. Theorem 4.6 (p. 33/PDF 35) says for discontinuous `T†` an a-priori rule is convergent iff `α(δ)→0` and `δ||R_{α(δ)}||→0`. The Morozov rule sets the largest regularization parameter whose residual is at most `τδ`, `τ>1`; existence in Theorem 4.7 requires dense range and uniformly bounded `T R_α` (in addition to a strictly decreasing null parameter grid). The Bakushinskii veto (Theorem 4.8, pp. 34–35) rules out a universally convergent noise-level-free heuristic parameter rule when `T†` is discontinuous. The proof uses the hypothetical rule to construct continuity of `T†`; it does not say heuristics never work on restricted/noisy-data classes.

**Source conditions and minimax deterministic rate.** Printed §4.2, pp. 36–43 (PDF 38–45). For compact `K`, `X_{ν,ρ}={|K|^νw:||w||≤ρ}` is a power source set in `N(K)^⊥`; in singular coordinates this is the strengthened Picard condition `Σσ_n^{-2−2ν}|<y,u_n>|²<∞` (Lemma 4.13). Theorem 4.11 gives conditional modulus `ε(X_{ν,ρ},δ)≤δ^{ν/(ν+1)}ρ^{1/(ν+1)}` by spectral interpolation. Theorem 4.12 constructs singular-vector directions attaining equality along `δ_n=ρσ_n^{ν+1}`. Theorem 4.10 turns this modulus into a lower bound on any regularizer's worst-case error. Thus no method uniformly beats the exponent on the whole source ball. Theorem 4.14 states how order-optimality plus dense range yields convergence for a discrepancy-type rule with sufficiently large `τ`; the proof spectrally truncates the exact solution and balances truncation/data radii. General index functions extend powers; logarithmic source conditions are appropriate in exponentially ill-posed problems. These are conditional rates, not rates for arbitrary solutions.

**Filters, TSVD, Tikhonov, qualification, saturation.** Printed Chapter 5, pp. 44–57 (PDF 46–59). A filter family `φ_α:[0,||K||²]→R` is regularizing when it is bounded/piecewise continuous, tends pointwise to `1/λ` for `λ>0`, and `sup λ|φ_α(λ)|≤C`. Set `R_α=φ_α(K*K)K*`, residual filter `r_α(λ)=1−λφ_α(λ)`. Lemmas 5.3–5.5 bound `||KR_α||`, `||R_α||`, and express bias spectrally; Theorem 5.6 proves strong convergence on `D(K†)` by finite-head/tail splitting and bounded convergence. TSVD uses `φ_α(λ)=1/λ` above cutoff and zero below; it has infinite qualification. Tikhonov uses `1/(λ+α)`; Landweber uses `(1−(1−ωλ)^m)/λ` with a suitable step.

Theorem 5.9 (pp. 50–51/PDF 54–55): if `x†∈X_{ν,ρ}`, `α(δ)` is comparable to `(δ/ρ)^{2/(ν+1)}`, `sup|φ_α|=O(α^{-1})`, and the residual bound `sup λ^{ν/2}|r_α(λ)|=O(α^{ν/2})` holds, then the error is order `δ^{ν/(ν+1)}ρ^{1/(ν+1)}`. The two terms are respectively data amplification and approximation bias. Qualification is the largest range of powers for which this residual bound holds. Theorem 5.11 gives Morozov order optimality only up to qualification minus one, with `τ>sup_{α,λ}|r_α(λ)|`, dense range, and continuity/existence of the residual choice; finite qualification costs one derivative. For Tikhonov, Corollaries 6.1–6.2 (pp. 59–60/PDF 61–62) give qualification 2 and a-priori optimal rates through `ν≤2`, discrepancy rates through `ν≤1`. Theorem 6.4 (pp. 60–61/PDF 62–63) proves the a-priori `δ^(2/3)` saturation barrier for nonzero solutions when compact `K` has infinite-dimensional range, by injecting noise into a singular-vector coordinate. TSVD has no such finite qualification saturation. Theorem 6.5 identifies Tikhonov's unique solution with the minimizer of `0.5||Kx−y||²+0.5α||x||²` and normal equation `(K*K+αI)x=K*y`; proof is a positive quadratic difference identity. This variational equivalence is specific to quadratic Hilbert penalties.

**Landweber.** Printed Chapter 7, pp. 66–70 (PDF 68–72). Algorithm: `x_0=0`, `x_{m+1}=x_m+ωK*(y−Kx_m)` with `0<ω<||K||^{-2}` (Clason sometimes takes strict margin). Iterates are polynomials in `K*K`; filter is `(1−(1−ωλ)^m)/λ`. Early stopping is regularization: `m→∞` while data amplification remains controlled. Proofs use singular components, contraction/nonexpansiveness, and a bias/noise split. Residual norm decreases, and the discrepancy stopping rule chooses the first index with residual `≤τδ`, `τ>1`; a bound gives finite stopping `m=O(δ^{-2})`, improved to `O(δ^{-2/(ν+1)})` under a power source condition. The scheme can be slow; conjugate-gradient and accelerated methods are mentioned but not proved. Initial guess and step-size are mathematical inputs; nullspace components remain un-recovered.

**Discretization error is a separate regularization source.** Printed Chapter 8, pp. 72–79 (PDF 74–81). Domain-projection least squares can fail to converge without stability; Theorem 8.2 uses boundedness of an adjoint pseudoinverse sequence, and compactness then forces a source condition. Dual range projection has a clean characterization: `x_n=P_{X_n}x†`, `X_n=T*Y_n`, hence converges by density. Theorem 8.8 requires `n(δ)→∞` and `δ/μ_{n(δ)}→0`, where `μ_n` is the smallest retained singular value. Theorem 8.9 gives `μ_n≤σ_n`; Theorem 8.10 shows approximation operator error at least `σ_{n+1}`, equality for the singular-vector subspace. This explains balancing finite-dimensional discretization, noise amplification, and regularization parameter rather than treating discretization as harmless. A local scaffold should distinguish operator approximation error from the regularization bias/noise terms.

**Scope cross-checks from remaining chapters.** Chapters 9–11 (printed pp. 81–116/PDF 83–118) treat nonlinear problems separately: complete continuity implies compact derivatives; tangential cone/Lipschitz derivative assumptions are substantive and not automatic; nonlinear Tikhonov existence uses weak closedness and weak compactness; iterated Newton variants require their own local assumptions. These do not extend the linear claims automatically. Chapter 12 (pp. 118–126/PDF 120–128) changes deterministic bounded-noise error to expected squared risk under Gaussian white noise, which is generally a stochastic process rather than a Hilbert-space vector. In the sequence model, linear estimator risk is exactly bias squared plus variance `Σ[(1−γ_n)^2 x_n² + δ² γ_n²/σ_n²]`; decay `σ_n≈n^{-μ}` changes rates. Theorem 12.4 gives Pinsker's linear minimax estimator over ellipsoids, not a deterministic worst-case bound. Chapter 13 (pp. 127–131/PDF 129–133) is expressly a finite-dimensional Gaussian Bayesian sketch: MAP equals Tikhonov for that specific Gaussian prior/likelihood; posterior mean also equals MAP only in this Gaussian setting. It does not prove infinite-dimensional Bayesian well-posedness or computational posterior convergence.

## Recent source: Brandt–Horn composite operators

This preprint studies `A=G∘F` with two compact, simultaneously diagonalizable linear operators and composes regularizers `R^F_γ R^G_α`. Theorem 1 (pp. 3–4) first assumes compatible minimum-norm solutions and `A†=F†G†`; its key stability condition is that the intermediate regularization error multiplied by `||R^F_γ||` tends to zero. It explicitly acknowledges `A†` need not equal `F†G†` for general noninjective/non-surjective factors. Under a common power source condition and filter assumptions (bounded residual, `||R||=O(parameter^{-1/2})`, common qualification `ν0>1`), Theorem 6 (pp. 6–9) gives double-discrepancy convergence with rate stated as `O(δ^{(ν−1)/(ν+1)}ρ^{1/(ν+1)^2})` for `1<ν≤ν0−1`. Its proof combines the first stage's Morozov bound, interpolation, and a second residual test to control the second stage's norm growth. The paper also derives a-priori balancing, but reports a suboptimal exponent for the two-stage composition. Numerical MPI and convolution experiments (pp. 10–17) illustrate outcomes; they are not guarantees.

Limitations/reader concerns to preserve: (i) the abstract regularization theorem is only on a restricted data set and relies on factorization/compatible minimum norm solutions; it is not the generic identity `(G∘F)†=F†G†`; (ii) simultaneous diagonalization is a strong simplifying assumption, and does not cover general noncommuting factors; (iii) equal qualifications are imposed for exposition; (iv) BPF experiment uses a method outside the filters used in the proof, so empirical success is not covered by Theorem 6; (v) the conclusion explicitly leaves optimality of the double-discrepancy exponent open. There is a visible notation inconsistency: introduction p. 1 first writes `A=F∘G`, later p. 1 and the body use `A=G∘F`; Theorem 1's prose also has a stray `Z⊂Z`. The proof's composition order follows the body `G∘F`. These should be resolved against authors/version before using the claim. This paper is a research lead, not a logical supplier for core results.

## Suggested inverse lane coverage and boundaries

Keep the principal deterministic chain in Hilbert spaces: Hadamard/Nashed ill-posedness → compact spectral theorem/SVD and Picard criterion → generalized inverse and source sets → filter definition/residual and bias-noise decomposition → TSVD and Tikhonov (qualification, saturation) → Landweber early stopping → Morozov discrepancy → finite-dimensional discretization error and parameter coupling. Separate procedure definitions from convergence/rate theorems. For any spectral-filter family, state the filter bound, qualification, source exponent, noise model, and exact parameter rule; do not claim a universal rate from source conditions alone. Include at least one singular-coordinate example illustrating noise amplification and one discretization example as B leaves.

Potential genuinely new local obligations, if exact supplier interfaces do not already cover them: dense-range/range-closure characterization for compact infinite-rank ill-posedness; Picard range theorem for compact operators; generic filter regularization theorem and generic Morozov result (existing Tikhonov-specific suppliers do not automatically imply the family-level result); discretization/stability coupling; optimality lower bound on source balls. Source conditions are assumptions, not facts about arbitrary exact solutions. Finite qualification is method saturation; do not conflate it with a limit on truth smoothness or with numerical failure. Unknown-noise heuristics carry restricted-data assumptions and should not be given an unconditional deterministic convergence theorem.
