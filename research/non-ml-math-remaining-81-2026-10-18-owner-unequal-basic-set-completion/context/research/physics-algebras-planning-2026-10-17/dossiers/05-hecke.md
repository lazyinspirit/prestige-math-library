## Scope and mathematical findings

The library already has unusually broad Hecke coverage: a universal
unequal-parameter Coxeter presentation and standard-basis proof; type-A affine
and cyclotomic PBW; Murphy cellular bases; type-A Specht theory; generic
quantum Schur–Weyl duality; category-O/Kazhdan–Lusztig theory; type-A Soergel
and Rouquier categorification; KLR algebras and Ariki’s theorem; and
Markov-trace link invariants. The scaffold should reuse these as distinct
results with their actual parameter domains. The main gaps are broad
unequal-parameter cell representation theory, a full affine classification
outside type A, any local DAHA/rational-Cherednik theory, and a careful
explanatory bridge from algebraic Hecke actions to model-specific
integrability in physics.

### Conventions must be translated before comparing theorems

There is no universal q-normalization. At minimum, keep these conventions
visible and state the rescaling at every interface.

| Convention | Quadratic relation | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Generic Coxeter / Soergel-symmetric | \((T_s-v_s)(T_s+v_s^{-1})=0\), hence \(T_s^2=(v_s-v_s^{-1})T_s+1\) | Universal Laurent-parameter Hecke algebra, bar involution and KL basis. |
| Multiplicative | \((S_s-Q_s)(S_s+1)=0\), with \(S_s=v_sT_s,\ Q_s=v_s^2\) | Type-A affine/cyclotomic presentations in hopf-hecke-algebras. It is related to the preceding convention by the displayed rescaling, not by identifying generators. |
| Common type-A representation theory | \((T_i-\xi)(T_i+1)=0\), equivalently \(T_i^2=(\xi-1)T_i+\xi\) | Mathas/Kleshchev cyclotomic representation theory; at \(\xi=1\), the finite algebra is \(F\mathfrak S_n\). |
| Quantum tensor operator | \((T_i-q)(T_i+q^{-1})=0\) | The ordered-basis \(U_q(\mathfrak{gl}_N)\) tensor symmetry. The rescaled generator \(S_i=qT_i\) satisfies \((S_i-q^2)(S_i+1)=0\). |

Lusztig’s unequal-parameter convention has a weight function \(L:W\to\Gamma\),
with \(v_s=v^{L(s)}\) and \(L(s)=L(t)\) whenever s,t are conjugate in W
(equivalently, in an odd-labelled connected component of the Coxeter graph).
The local universal Hecke page allows independent units on these odd
classes; the specialization to the integral weight-function setting should
be stated when invoking Lusztig’s cell results. The bar is semilinear, sends
\(v\mapsto v^{-1}\) and \(T_s\mapsto T_s^{-1}\). KL bases depend on this
convention and on the associated v-shift. A cell theorem copied across
conventions without translating generator and parameter may be wrong by signs
and powers.

For type A at parameter \(\xi\), define quantum characteristic by

\[
e=\min\{m\ge 1:1+\xi+\cdots+\xi^{m-1}=0\},\qquad e=0\text{ if no such }m\text{ exists}.
\]

If \(\xi\ne1\), finite e is the multiplicative order of \(\xi\). If \(\xi=1\),
then e equals \(\operatorname{char}F\) in positive characteristic and is
zero in characteristic zero. Thus “generic,” “root of unity,” “parameter
one,” and “positive characteristic” are separate axes: at \(\xi=1\),
characteristic p is already the finite-e=p modular case. For affine Hecke
algebras of p-adic groups, the positive real parameters and residue-field
cardinalities in Solleveld’s classification are a different setup from a
quantum-characteristic specialization of the finite type-A algebra.

### Coxeter Hecke algebra, standard basis, bar and KL cells

For a Coxeter system \((W,S)\), the Hecke algebra is generated by the simple
reflections with Coxeter braid relations and a quadratic deformation. A
reduced word defines \(T_w\) only after using Matsumoto’s theorem;
independence of the \(T_w\) is a separate theorem. The local universal
presentation handles that separation correctly: reduced-word independence
and length multiplication give spanning, while a regular-module
representation proves the indexed elements independent. The result is a
free basis over the universal Laurent ring and survives arbitrary
commutative base change without a flatness assumption. This proves
freeness of the algebra, not semisimplicity of any specialized representation
category.

In equal or weighted Coxeter settings, the bar involution and
Bruhat-triangular bar-invariant KL basis provide algebraic input for cells.
The type-A local page proves the R-polynomial recursion, KL polynomial
recursion and inversion, and the left/right/two-sided preorders. It also
identifies type-A cells by Robinson–Schensted tableaux. That last
classification is type A; it does not establish arbitrary Coxeter or
unequal-parameter cell theory. A cell module is built from the span or
quotient associated to a cell preorder, and its representation-theoretic
behavior needs additional results; existence of the KL basis alone is not a
semisimplicity or simple-module classification.

Lusztig’s book supplies the standard algebraic unequal-parameter
construction: weight function, basis, bar, KL basis and left/right/two-sided
cells. Its P1–P15 statements are conjectures in that source’s general
framework, stated under the book’s boundedness assumption on (W,L); some
parameter/type cases have proofs and others require separate hypotheses. This dossier does **not** assert that all P1–P15 are currently
open, nor that they are all proved. The 2026 literature check was selective.
Geck’s modular notes illustrate the more focused finite-Weyl use of cells in
canonical basic sets and modular representation theory. Any extension in the
scaffold should name the precise weight-function regime and theorem rather
than say “unequal parameters are known.”

### Finite type A: cellularity, Specht modules and roots of unity

The cellular-algebra mechanism has four stages: a basis with a poset
triangularity condition and anti-involution; cell modules with an associative
form; their radicals; and classification of simples by the nonzero cell
heads. The type-A Hecke/Murphy basis fits this mechanism. Murphy’s cellular
Specht modules, Jucys–Murphy eigenvalues/contents and seminormal forms are
valuable both in generic representation theory and after specialization.
The convention warning is essential: the Murphy cell module at \(\lambda\)
can be dual/sign-conjugate to the ordinary Dipper–James Specht module of
label \(\lambda\); do not silently identify the label conventions. In a
separated/seminormal range, content denominators are invertible and
seminormal bases exist. This condition is stronger than merely having the
integral Murphy basis.

Over a field, quantum characteristic controls type-A simple labels: in the
usual level-one convention they are e-regular partitions; the cellular basis
is still present at nonsemisimple parameters, but the forms may degenerate.
A criterion based on the generic regular trace or Schur elements must be
applied to the actual specialization. Generic semisimplicity does not imply
semisimplicity at a root of unity, and nonvanishing of one chosen trace
determinant is a sufficient certificate in the local theorem rather than an
assertion that its zero locus is exactly the nonsemisimple locus.

At cyclotomic level, multipartitions, residue sequences and Jucys–Murphy
content data govern blocks and branching. The Brundan–Kleshchev blockwise
isomorphism gives each type-A cyclotomic Hecke block a sign-adjusted KLR
presentation and a nontrivial \(\mathbb Z\)-grading. In characteristic zero,
Ariki’s categorification identifies projective classes with canonical/global
basis vectors, while graded Specht decomposition polynomials are the
standard-to-simple transition coefficients. This requires the
characteristic-zero theorem and its exact cyclotomic type-A setting. Over
positive characteristic, the same canonical basis does not by itself give
the decomposition matrix: Brundan–Kleshchev define graded adjustment
polynomials and factor the matrix through them. This boundary is general,
while special cases and known adjustment results may be better behaved.
The local theorem does not say every positive-characteristic decomposition
number is unknown.

The degenerate Hecke algebra at \(\xi=1\) has polynomial generators and
additive cross-relations; its cyclotomic quotient has a PBW basis, but it is
not simply the multiplicative presentation with a singular parameter value.
The local Arakawa–Suzuki/higher-level Schur–Weyl material supplies this
degenerate branch and its category-O connections. Keep the finite-e
root-of-unity branch distinct from the generic-\(\xi\) nondegenerate branch.

### Quantum Schur–Weyl duality and Temperley–Lieb quotients

The quantum type-A braid operator gives a Hecke action on
\(V_N^{\otimes n}\) that commutes with \(U_q(\mathfrak{gl}_N)\). The library
proves the two mutual centralizer equalities over \(\mathbb Q(q)\), q
indeterminate, by a classical specialization and matrix-rank bounds. The
abstract Hecke action is faithful when \(N\ge n\); for \(N<n\) it can have
a kernel. In particular, this theorem makes no root-of-unity or
positive-characteristic centralizer claim. Do not extrapolate the generic
double-centralizer theorem through a nonsemisimple specialization without
another argument.

The \(N=2\) tensor representation factors through the Temperley–Lieb quotient
in its appropriate parameter convention. This is a quotient/image
statement; it does not say the Hecke algebra itself is Temperley–Lieb, nor
that every specialization has a semisimple TL category. The representation
map is the clean bridge to link topology: Hecke towers support Markov traces
and HOMFLYPT; the Jones polynomial is a parameter specialization through
the TL quotient. These are algebraic/topological theorems, not a claim
about a particular physical Hamiltonian.

### Affine, cyclotomic, DAHA and rational Cherednik branches

The affine type-A Bernstein presentation has Laurent polynomial generators
\(X_i^{\pm1}\), finite Hecke generators and crossing relations. The local
PBW theorem proves \(X^\lambda T_w\) is a basis and computes the center over
the universal coefficient ring; it is not a p-adic Iwahori decomposition.
For general root data, Solleveld’s survey gives a Bernstein presentation,
PBW, center, parabolic induction and a tempered/Langlands classification
for positive real parameters, with proofs frequently referred elsewhere.
Its analytic/classification assumptions must be retained.

The cyclotomic/Ariki–Koike quotient imposes a monic relation on the first
affine \(X\)-generator. PBW normal words have \(r^n n!\) elements. The
local proof of the basis uses integral spanning and generic tableau modules;
it does not infer \(p(X_i)=0\) for every Jucys–Murphy element. Generic
splitness belongs to the generic fraction field; the free basis survives
arbitrary specialization, including nonsemisimple ones.

The DAHA branch begins with Cherednik’s double-affine generators and
commuting difference operators/Macdonald theory. Rational Cherednik
algebras are rational degenerations with a PBW theorem under the
symplectic-reflection parameter condition; their category O and cyclotomic
quotients support further categorification. Etingof–Ginzburg is read through
the PBW theorem and rational-Cherednik setup, while Kirillov is read through
the DAHA/Macdonald lecture. The current library has a proven type-A affine
presentation and a degenerate affine presentation, but **no local DAHA or
rational-Cherednik definition/PBW/category-O proof page**. These branches
are substantive research directions, not reusable local theorem suppliers
today.

### Soergel, KLR and category-O categorification

Soergel bimodules categorify the equal-parameter Hecke algebra. The 2014
Elias–Williamson Hodge paper proves Soergel’s character conjecture and KL
positivity for any Coxeter system equipped with its reflection-faithful real
representation and positive dominant Lefschetz operator; the proof is
characteristic-zero/real Hodge theory. The local library deliberately proves
a restricted type-A version using fully supplied support filtrations,
invariant forms, local intersection forms, Rouquier complexes and
Hodge–Riemann signs. Do not replace that local proof graph with a title
citation to the general paper. In positive characteristic, the p-canonical
basis can differ; the ordinary characteristic-zero positivity theorem does
not settle that problem.

The 2013 Soergel Calculus article presents generators-and-relations
diagrammatics, light/double-leaf bases and cellularity, but states an H3
caveat. Hazi’s 2024 paper confirms that the H3 Zamolodchikov relation remains
incomplete in the literature it reviews and says the diagrammatic Hecke
category is not defined there for H3 parabolics. The 2013 calculus also
makes Demazure surjectivity explicit in its double-leaves/equivalence
conclusions; Hazi identifies \(2\in k^\times\) as a standard sufficient
condition, with the more precise realization conditions in that article.
The 2024 result settles a necessary two-colored Jones–Wenzl projector
criterion and the equivalence with Abe’s bimodule category under its
hypotheses, but does not fill the H3 relation. No search can certify that no
later 2025–26 resolution exists; none was found in the selective check
recorded here.

KLR algebras categorify the negative half of a quantum group by induction and
restriction functors; cyclotomic quotients categorify integrable
highest-weight modules. The type-A Brundan–Kleshchev bridge transports this
grading to cyclotomic Hecke blocks. Rouquier’s 2-Kac–Moody framework and
Khovanov–Lauda diagrammatics provide broader categorification context. The
local generic KLR page works for all fields and suitable graded parameters
for its PBW and quantum-Serre statements; its global-basis theorem has
stricter symmetric, characteristic-zero, finite-loopless-quiver hypotheses.
The cyclotomic page’s highest-weight results and its conditional
global-basis layer must likewise stay separate.

Category O enters through the Kazhdan–Lusztig character theorem, projective
functors/Soergel functor, parabolic and singular KL modules, and the
Arakawa–Suzuki/higher-level Schur–Weyl functor. The degenerate affine Hecke
action on tensor functors uses Casimir and place-permutation operators; its
cyclotomic quotient is tied to higher-level category O under the local
conditions. This is a distinct route from the nondegenerate root-of-unity
Ariki theorem.

### Physics, integrability and topology: the scope of the bridge

There are three mathematical routes to physical examples; none makes every
Hecke representation a physical model.

1. A constant braid/Yang–Baxter operator on \(V\otimes V\) gives local braid
   group representations. If its quadratic polynomial is Hecke, the braid
   representation factors through a Hecke algebra. Spectral-parameter
   Baxterization is an additional construction and convention check; the
   constant braid equation alone does not provide a Hamiltonian or
   transfer-matrix family.
2. Quantum-group tensor representations give commuting Hecke/quantum-group
   actions. At \(N=2\), the Hecke image is related to Temperley–Lieb, making
   link invariants accessible. To call a specific transfer matrix
   integrable, one still needs an \(R(u)\) or \(L\)-operator satisfying the
   spectral Yang–Baxter equation, boundary conditions and the relevant
   commuting transfer matrices.
3. In Povolotsky–Pyatov–Tribe–Westbury–Zaboronski, local stochastic
   two-site idempotents generate continuous-time particle-process
   generators, Hecke quotients occur for listed models, and duality
   functions are intertwiners to smaller Hecke representations. Their first
   classification assumes reflection-symmetric/equal left-right hopping
   rates; the second uses a matrix ansatz. Their Markov duality and
   Baxterization results are model-specific, including reactions that do
   not conserve particle number.

On the topology side, the Hecke Markov trace gives HOMFLYPT and its
Temperley–Lieb/Jones specialization gives Jones. The cyclotomic fusion
formula uses Jucys–Murphy contents and primitive idempotents under explicit
semisimplicity/content-separation hypotheses. Newer skein/DAHA results are
a research interface, not a consequence of the local Markov-trace theorem.

## Prerequisite closure and work still needed

Reuse the local Coxeter exchange/Matsumoto, tensor/base-change, cellular,
finite-field representation, quantum-group, graded-module, homological and
braid foundations. For any expanded Hecke page, the proof graph still needs
to spell out the following where applicable:

* A universal Coxeter Hecke page needs the Coxeter presentation,
  exchange/Matsumoto and coefficient-ring base change. The existing page
  already supplies these and proves the basis. Do not reopen them as gaps.
* A general KL/cell page beyond type A needs the chosen weight function,
  Bruhat order, bar convention, construction of the weighted KL basis, cell
  preorders, and proof of the claimed cell-module multiplication/closure. A
  source’s conjectural general cell property is not a local proof.
* A modular finite-Hecke theorem needs an actual specialization field and
  parameter, Schur-element/regular-trace conditions for semisimplicity, and
  cell/Specht form behavior after specialization. Cellular bases do not make
  cell forms nondegenerate.
* A root-of-unity/cyclotomic/KLR page needs residue quiver orientation,
  e-definition, multipartition charge, cyclotomic relation, block
  idempotents, explicit Hecke–KLR generator maps both directions, and the
  field/characteristic range. Ariki’s canonical-basis argument is
  characteristic zero; positive characteristic needs the adjustment layer.
* A Schur–Weyl/TL theorem needs the explicit tensor operator, Hecke relation,
  quantum-group action and commutation, centralizer proof, and dimension/range
  check. If specializing q, prove semisimplicity or the appropriate quotient
  statement separately.
* An affine representation classification needs root data and Bernstein PBW,
  center/central character, parabolic induction, and exact positivity and
  parameter assumptions used in tempered/Langlands statements. The
  DAHA/Cherednik branch additionally needs its own PBW and degeneration maps.
* A Soergel diagrammatic equivalence needs the realization, characteristic,
  Demazure-surjectivity/Abe conditions, local rank-two and rank-three
  relations, double-leaves spanning/independence, and the H3 relation if H3
  is included. Ordinary KL positivity and p-canonical behavior are distinct.
* A physical integrability example needs to identify the Hilbert/state space,
  local generator or transfer matrix, parameter reality/stochasticity and
  boundary conditions, then show that the Yang–Baxter/Hecke representation
  yields the claimed commuting observables or Markov duality. Citing a Hecke
  action alone is not enough.

No exact 2026 literature census was done. The dated selected sources verify
recent work on unequal-parameter affine geometry (Antor 2025), monodromic
Soergel calculus (Sandvik 2026), wreath-product cellularity (Hudak–Lai 2026),
affine standard modules over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\) (Dawydiak 2026),
and skein/DAHA representations (Matson 2025). These are research leads, not
interchangeable foundational proofs. Antor’s G2 classification assumes a
torsion-free subgroup generated by the two torus parameters and verifies
separate geometric conditions; it does not settle every unequal-parameter
affine classification. Hudak–Lai prove d=2, not all wreath parameters.
