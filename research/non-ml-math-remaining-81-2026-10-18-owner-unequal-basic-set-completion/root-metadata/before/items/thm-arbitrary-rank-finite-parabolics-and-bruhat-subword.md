## Statement

Let $S$ be any set with Coxeter matrix $m$, and let $W,\ell,T,\le$ be as in [[def-arbitrary-rank-coxeter-system-and-bruhat-order]]. For a finite $J\subseteq S$, let $W_J^*$ be the group presented by the restricted matrix $m|_J$, and let $\phi_J:W_J^*\to W$ send each generator to the same-named element.

1. The map $\phi_J$ is injective with image $\langle J\rangle$, and intrinsic length in $W_J^*$ equals ambient length in $W$ on this subgroup.
2. For $y,w\in W$, $y\le w$ if and only if a reduced expression of $y$ occurs as a subword of any fixed reduced expression of $w$. In particular, the interval $\{y:y\le w\}$ is finite and has at most $2^{\ell(w)}$ elements.
3. For every $s\in S$ and $w\in W$, $\ell(sw)=\ell(w)\pm1$ and $\ell(ws)=\ell(w)\pm1$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The arbitrary-rank Coxeter presentation, its length and reflection-chain order, a finite subset $J\subseteq S$, and a finite set of generators $K\subseteq S$ containing the supports used in a given argument.

[F1] [[def-arbitrary-rank-coxeter-system-and-bruhat-order]]: $W$ is the quotient of the free group on $S$ by the normal closure of the involution and finite braid relators; elements and relators have finite word support; $\ell$ is minimum word length; and $y\le w$ means there is a finite chain of length-increasing right multiplications by reflections.

[F2] [[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]]: for a finite Coxeter matrix on $K$ and $J\subseteq K$, the canonical presentation map from the Coxeter group on $J$ into the group on $K$ is injective, and intrinsic $J$-length equals ambient $K$-length on that subgroup.

[F3] [[thm-hh-coxeter-exchange-deletion-and-faithfulness]]: in a finite-generator Coxeter system, left and right multiplication by a simple generator change length by exactly $1$ in absolute value.

[F4] [[def-cg-bruhat-order-by-reflection-chains]]: Bruhat comparison is the transitive closure of length-increasing right multiplication by reflections.

[F5] [[thm-cg-bruhat-subword-characterization]]: in a finite-generator Coxeter system, $y\le w$ if and only if a reduced expression of $y$ is a subword of any fixed reduced expression of $w$.

## Proof

**Given:** The data in the Statement.

1.1 **Finite-generator parabolic embedding.** The image of $\phi_J$ is $\langle J\rangle$, since its image is generated by the named generators. To prove injectivity, let $g\in W_J^*$ map to $1$, and represent $g$ by a word $u$ in $J$. In the free group $F(S)$, the word $u$ lies in the normal closure of the Coxeter relators by [F1]. By the definition of a generated normal subgroup, $u$ is a finite product of conjugates of relators and their inverses. Let $K$ contain $J$ and every letter appearing in these finitely many conjugating words and relators. The same finite product identity holds in $F(K)$, so $u$ is the identity in the Coxeter group presented on $K$. Applying [F2] to the finite system on $K$ and its subset $J$ shows that the map from $W_J^*$ into this group is injective. Hence $g=1$. [F1, F2, construct]

2.1 **Intrinsic and ambient lengths.** Every $J$-word is an $S$-word, so ambient length is at most intrinsic $J$-length. Conversely, take $w\in W_J^*$ and a reduced ambient expression for $\phi_J(w)$, of length $r$. Let $K$ contain $J$ and the letters in that expression. By step 1.1 applied to $K$, the presentation on $K$ embeds in $W$; thus the equality between a $J$-word for $w$ and the chosen ambient word already holds in the group on $K$. By [F2], intrinsic $J$-length equals $K$-length on $W_J^*$. The chosen expression gives $K$-length at most $r$, while every $K$-word is also an $S$-word and so has length at least $r$. Hence both lengths equal $r$, proving intrinsic and ambient length equality. [F1, F2, step 1.1]

3.1 **Simple-generator length changes.** Fix $s\in S$ and $w\in W$. Choose a finite $K$ containing $s$ and the letters of a word for $w$. Steps 1.1 and 2.1 embed the group on $K$ and preserve lengths. Applying [F3] in that finite-generator system gives both asserted $\pm1$ formulas in $W$. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1]

3.2 **Bruhat comparison implies a subword.** Suppose $y\le w$, and fix a reduced expression of $w$. Choose a finite Bruhat chain from $y$ to $w$. Each element in this chain has finite word support, and each reflection used in an edge is a conjugate of a simple generator by a finite word. The union of these supports and the letters of the fixed expression of $w$ is contained in a finite $K\subseteq S$. By steps 1.1 and 2.1, the chain lies in the embedded finite-generator group on $K$ with all lengths preserved; its edges are still right multiplications by reflections. The finite-generator subword theorem [F5] now gives a reduced expression of $y$ as a subword of the fixed expression of $w$. [F1, F2, F4, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1]

3.3 **A reduced subword implies Bruhat comparison.** Suppose a reduced expression of $y$ occurs as a subword of a fixed reduced expression of $w$. Let $J$ be the finite support of that expression of $w$. The subword theorem [F5] gives $y\le w$ in the finite-generator Coxeter group on $J$. By steps 1.1 and 2.1 its presentation embeds in $W$ and preserves lengths; each reflection in the finite Bruhat chain maps to a reflection in $W$. The chain therefore gives $y\le w$ in the global reflection-chain order. [F1, F4, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 Every element below $w$ is represented by a subword of a fixed reduced expression of $w$, by step 3.2. There are at most $2^{\ell(w)}$ such subwords, so the lower interval is finite with the stated bound. A strict comparison has a nonempty length-increasing chain by [F1], hence $\ell(y)<\ell(w)$ when $y<w$. Steps 3.1–3.3 prove all remaining clauses. All supports, products in the normal closure, and chains used above are finite. [F1, step 3.1, step 3.2, step 3.3] ∎
