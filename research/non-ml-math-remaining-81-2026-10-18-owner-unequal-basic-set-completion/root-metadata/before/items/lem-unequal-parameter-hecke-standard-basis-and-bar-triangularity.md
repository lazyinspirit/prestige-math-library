## Statement

Let $S$ be any set with Coxeter group $W$, let $L:W\to\Gamma$ be a weight function as in [[def-unequal-parameter-weight-function-and-coefficient-order]], and let $H_A(W,L)$ be as in [[def-unequal-parameter-coxeter-hecke-algebra]]. Then:

1. For each $w$, the product $T_w$ along a reduced expression is independent of the chosen expression, and $\{T_w:w\in W\}$ is an $A$-basis of $H_A(W,L)$.
2. The assignments $\overline{v^\gamma}=v^{-\gamma}$ and $\overline{T_s}=T_s^{-1}$ extend to an involutive semilinear algebra automorphism of $H_A(W,L)$.
3. For every reduced expression of $w$, the expansion of $\overline{T_w}-T_w$ in the standard basis is supported on $T_y$ with $y<w$ in Bruhat order.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An arbitrary-rank Coxeter system, a weight function $L$, the group algebra $A$, and the Hecke presentation $H_A(W,L)$.

[F1] [[def-arbitrary-rank-coxeter-system-and-bruhat-order]]: every word uses finitely many generators; the group has the Coxeter presentation; and Bruhat order is the reflection-chain order.

[F2] [[def-unequal-parameter-weight-function-and-coefficient-order]] and [[lem-simple-generator-weights-extend-to-a-weight-function]]: $A=\mathbb Z[\Gamma]$, $\overline{v^\gamma}=v^{-\gamma}$, $L$ is additive on length-additive products, and its simple-generator values are equal along every finite odd-labelled edge.

[F3] [[def-unequal-parameter-coxeter-hecke-algebra]]: $H_A(W,L)$ is generated by $T_s$ with $(T_s-v^{L(s)})(T_s+v^{-L(s)})=0$ and the finite Coxeter braid relations; the quadratic relation gives $T_s^{-1}=T_s-(v^{L(s)}-v^{-L(s)})$.

[F4] [[thm-arbitrary-rank-finite-parabolics-and-bruhat-subword]]: every finite set of simple generators gives an embedded finite-generator Coxeter presentation with the same intrinsic and ambient lengths, and Bruhat comparisons in it are reduced-subword comparisons.

[F5] [[thm-hh-generic-coxeter-hecke-standard-basis]]: for a finite Coxeter generator set and parameters constant on odd-edge components, the generic Hecke algebra has standard basis $\{T_w\}$; that basis survives arbitrary commutative coefficient-ring base change without a flatness assumption.

[F6] [[thm-cg-bruhat-subword-characterization]]: in a finite Coxeter system, $y\le w$ exactly when a reduced expression of $y$ is a subword of a fixed reduced expression of $w$.

## Proof

**Given:** The data in the Statement.

1.1 **Finite-support spanning.** Fix a finite $J\subseteq S$. Its odd-edge components have a universal Laurent coefficient ring with one unit parameter for each component. By [F2], sending that parameter to $v^{L(s)}\in A^\times$ is independent of $s$ in the component, so it defines a coefficient-ring homomorphism. By [F4] and [F5], the finite Hecke algebra on $J$, after this base change, has basis $\{T_u:u\in W_J\}$. Every word representing an element of the global presented algebra uses some finite $J$, so applying this finite spanning statement to that word shows that the global elements $T_w$ span $H_A(W,L)$. [F2, F4, F5, construct]

1.2 **The bar automorphism.** The quadratic relation in [F3] gives $T_s^{-1}=T_s-(q_s-q_s^{-1})$, where $q_s=v^{L(s)}$. Define a semilinear multiplicative map on the free algebra by $v^\gamma\mapsto v^{-\gamma}$ and $T_s\mapsto T_s^{-1}$. The image of a quadratic relator is $(T_s^{-1}-q_s^{-1})(T_s^{-1}+q_s)$, which is zero because multiplying $T_s^2-(q_s-q_s^{-1})T_s-1=0$ by $T_s^{-2}$ gives that same identity. The image of a braid relation is the inverse of the reversed alternating relation; reversal of either alternating word is one of the two defining alternating words, so the relation is preserved. The map descends to $H_A(W,L)$. Its square fixes every coefficient and every $T_s$, hence is the identity. [F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 **Independence by finite support.** Suppose a finite sum $\sum_{w\in F}a_wT_w$ is zero in $H_A(W,L)$. In the free associative presentation, a representative belongs to the two-sided ideal generated by the quadratic and braid relations. Such membership is a finite sum of products of generators and relations, so only finitely many simple generators occur in that expression and in the reduced words for $w\in F$. Put them in a finite set $J$. The same relation then holds in the finite Hecke presentation on $J$, where [F5] gives independence of its standard basis. Thus every $a_w=0$. Together with step 1.1 this proves that $\{T_w\}$ is an $A$-basis and that the reduced product $T_w$ is independent of the chosen expression. [F1, F3, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 **Multiplication by a simple generator.** By [F4], exactly one of $\ell(sx)=\ell(x)+1$ and $\ell(sx)=\ell(x)-1$ holds. In the first case, a reduced word for $x$ prefixed by $s$ is reduced, so $T_sT_x=T_{sx}$. In the second, $x=s(sx)$ is reduced and the quadratic relation gives $T_sT_x=T_{sx}+(q_s-q_s^{-1})T_x$. Thus, when a simple letter is prepended to a standard-basis term, each output label is either the value with that letter prepended or the value with that letter omitted. [F1, F3, F4, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 **Bruhat support.** Write a reduced expression $w=s_1\cdots s_k$. Then $\overline{T_w}=T_{s_1}^{-1}\cdots T_{s_k}^{-1}$. Expanding each inverse as $T_{s_i}-(q_{s_i}-q_{s_i}^{-1})$ gives scalar multiples of products along subwords. Build each selected product from its rightmost letter toward its left; at each multiplication step 3.1 shows that its standard-basis labels are still values of subwords of the original reduced expression. By [F4] and [F6], each such label $y$ satisfies $y\le w$. A proper subword has length at most $k-1$, so it cannot equal $w$; the full reduced word contributes $T_w$ with coefficient one. Hence $\overline{T_w}-T_w$ is supported strictly below $w$. [F4, F6, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Steps 1.1 and 2.1 prove the standard-basis clause, step 1.2 constructs the involution, and step 4.1 proves its strict Bruhat support. All reductions take place in finite supports, so no finite-rank hypothesis or choice axiom is used. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 1.2, step 4.1] ∎
