## Confirmed findings and corrections

1. **Weak-solution lemma.** The old inner cutoff radius did not explicitly contain the curve/slit; its local argument skipped coincident endpoints, incorrectly required subdivision disks to meet the original divisor in at most one point, and did not establish cancellation of extra intermediate poles. The closed-form integral also needed its actual improper-integral meaning. The corrected construction contains the entire compact path image in the inner disk, uses the logarithm only on the annulus, treats equal local endpoints by the constant factor, and computes the integral by punctured-circle Stokes. It proves absolute convergence from the local O(1/r) bound, then cancels all intermediate endpoint powers in the finite product. Local primitive endpoint differences define continuous-path integration; a compact homotopy-square calculation gives the disk primitive. The full claim for every closed smooth complex one-form, arbitrary finite curve chain, and smooth-factor uniqueness is preserved, including a nonzero closed chain with zero boundary. The Statement now explicitly says the weak solution is nowhere zero off its divisor and interprets the general integral as the absolutely convergent improper integral; its holomorphic dbar integral remains an ordinary smooth integral. Read the complete relevant Forster 20.3/20.5 proof pages 160–163 from the fetched full PDF, and independently checked the boundary sign.

2. **Abel–Jacobi Definition.** The old assertion that a nonzero-degree divisor necessarily changes with any nonzero point shift was false for torsion shifts. The original exact formula is retained: the two classes differ by degree(D) times the point shift. They differ exactly when that multiple is nonzero; nonzero-degree torsion cancellation is explicit. Degree zero is always independent. The local-lift construction now directly uses the functional-valued primitive map in a quotient chart, rather than an unnecessary composition with a local inverse.

3. **Abel–Jacobi well-definedness lemma.** Its displayed local primitive lift omitted the fixed initial-path functional. The corrected lift includes that constant, so its projection is exactly the point map; the derivative and all Statement clauses are unchanged.

4. **Principal-divisor vanishing lemma.** The old proof silently replaced the given curve by its constructed radial curve, lifted an open interval from an undefined initial endpoint, asserted disjoint image subsets, and counted endpoint multiplicities using an embedded-arc assumption absent from the Statement. The corrected proof works for every given curve with interior avoiding the branch values: lift in both directions from an interior parameter, retain distinct values at each parameter with multiplicity, extend connected tails through finite local power neighborhoods, and count each endpoint using its nearby regular fibre. The trace integral is calculated over finite evenly covered subintervals and passed to endpoint limits using local primitives, without a global inverse branch along a self-intersecting path. The Statement and its possibly branched finite/infinite endpoints are preserved.

5. **Jacobi inversion.** The old proof omitted genus zero, used equality of Abel–Jacobi classes after adding a principal divisor without citing the result that establishes that equality, and claimed sharper surjectivity before proving divisor-map surjectivity. The corrected proof handles genus zero with empty tuples and the point Jacobian, cites the existing principal-divisor vanishing lemma, first proves division-by-N surjectivity, and then applies RR to an effective degree-g representative. The unrestricted claim to factor through any quotient that identifies permuted tuples was false when that quotient makes further unrelated identifications; it is corrected to the coordinate-permutation/symmetric-product quotient. Both approved surjectivity claims and the explicit functional representation are retained.

6. **Positive-genus embedding.** The old several-variable inverse-function map was dimensionally ill formed. The corrected proof chooses a component with nonzero derivative, uses its scalar holomorphic inverse, and writes the remaining components as a holomorphic graph. Compactness and injectivity exclude other branches of the global image in a smaller chart. Fact F7 now explicitly refers to the surjective degree-zero divisor extension, rather than suggesting surjectivity of the point map in every genus. All four claims remain.

7. **Pentagon example.** The old Galois argument wrongly listed only two of the four allowable nonconjugate eigenpairs, invoked an unproved Hodge splitting, and used injectivity of the homology-to-dual map where real spanning of the dual was needed. The corrected proof uses the cyclic integral homology module and its characteristic polynomial, the real-linear extension of the full period map, and equivariance to obtain each eigenvalue once. Every admissible pair becomes the required pair after exchanging eigenvectors and replacing the generator by a power. Finite spectral projections establish diagonalization. Trace vanishing rules out invariant forms; real spanning of period functionals proves the normalizing period is nonzero. The existing Picard/Jacobian corollary is now an explicit supplier for the final Picard claim. The displayed lattice, full-rank Vandermonde proof and all example claims are preserved.

8. **Base-point example.** Its old proof attributed a torus group-difference formula to a fact that supplied only pointwise nonzero evaluation. The corrected proof uses precisely that fact: the positive-genus point map is nonconstant and zero at its base point, so a suitable other base point has nonzero shift. This proves the original degree-one dependence claim without the unsupported torus interface.

9. **Abel-image example.** The old argument incorrectly treated a proper one-dimensional subtorus as impossible merely because its ambient dimension is larger. The corrected proof first establishes that the image generates the full Jacobian. If the image were a subtorus, it would already equal the subgroup it generates, contradicting its dimension-one graph/empty interior when g>1. The genus-one and genus-two models remain.
