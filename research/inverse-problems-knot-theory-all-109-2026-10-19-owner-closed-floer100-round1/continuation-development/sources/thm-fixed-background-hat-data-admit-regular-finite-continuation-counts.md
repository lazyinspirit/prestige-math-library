## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Fix a globally weakly admissible complete singly pointed diagram of ANY closed connected oriented smooth three-manifold, with one surface complex structure $j$, area form and protected nearly symmetric geometry. Use a small Cayley coefficient ball about the standard background, with common exact product generator charts. Let $J^-_t,J^+_t$ be two paths in that ball, constant on genuine endpoint time collars with the same endpoint values as the background. Suppose EVERY nonconstant zero-basepoint strip for either path is regular, in the actual fixed-end $H^1\to L^2$ realization. These are hypotheses about actual chosen data; no Spin-c or rational homology restriction is imposed.

There is a smooth source-dependent path $K(s,t)$ in the same protected coefficient neighborhood, equal to $J^-_t$ for all sufficiently negative $s$ and to $J^+_t$ for all sufficiently positive $s$, constant on genuine positive time collars at $t=0,1$, with the following properties. EVERY actual nonconstant finite-energy solution with uniform generator ends of
$$u_s+K(s,t,u)u_t=0,\qquad u(s,0)\in T_\alpha,\quad u(s,1)\in T_\beta,\qquad n_z(u)=0$$
has onto actual fixed-end operator, in every integer higher realization. Its constant solutions are the generator maps and have invertible index-zero operators. Across all endpoints and realized classes there are finitely many actual index-zero solutions. They are counted WITHOUT a translation quotient: the source transition is fixed. Thus their mod-two numbers define a finite matrix from the $J^-$ generator module to the $J^+$ generator module.

More generally, ANY fixed compact-transition structure in this same family, with these end paths and regular at EVERY zero-basepoint solution, has a finite actual index-zero solution set, by the same anchored argument. The construction proves that such choices exist.

This is regularity and finite continuation counts, not yet a chain-map, composition or chain-equivalence theorem. It compares only the specified common integrable background and protected small family. Changing $j$, comparing merely sector-admissible inputs, Heegaard moves, gradings and plus/infinity maps remain separate. Genus zero gives only the point solution and its identity one-by-one count matrix.

## Proof

1.1 In genus zero all maps go to a point and the stated conclusion is direct, so suppose the genus is positive. There is ONE energy bound $B$ for every actual zero-basepoint strip considered here, independent of its index and of its prescribed source dependence in the fixed small coefficient family. To see the domain assertion explicitly, let $L$ be the finite rational kernel of real periodic region chains with $n_z=0$. A nonzero nonnegative vector in it gives a rational such vector by approximation on its positive-coordinate face, and clearing denominators contradicts global admissibility. Thus $L\cap\mathbb R^r_{\ge0}=0$. For fixed endpoints the nonnegative continuous-class domains lie in one affine translate of $L$. If integral members were unbounded, their normalized vectors would have a nonnegative unit limit in $L$, by deterministic nested-cube subsequence extraction, a contradiction. Consequently there is a finite set $\mathcal D$ of nonnegative domains over all endpoint pairs, determined BEFORE regularity. The energy estimate still holds for prescribed $K(s,t)$: locally near the diagonal its fixed integrable coefficients give the same finite ordered-root covers as [F1]; away from it pointwise tameness bounds $|du|^2$ by the pulled-back product area. On the integrable part the bounded differential of the symmetric quotient gives that same bound on the cover. Integrating each root's oriented degree over the surface regions gives $d\sum_R n_R\int_R\eta$ on a degree-$d$ cover. Division by $d$ proves $E(u)\le C\sum_R n_R\int_R\eta$, with one $C$ in the small family. No derivative of $K$ occurs in this pointwise argument. Protected local positivity applies because $K=\operatorname{Sym}(j)$ throughout every chosen point-divisor neighborhood, independently of source. Take $B=\max(\{C\sum_R n_R(D)\int_R\eta:D\in\mathcal D\}\cup\{0\})$. All required solutions have energy at most $B$, including future limiting or homotopy solutions within this same geometry. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 Write the two end paths in the common small Cayley coordinates as $J^\pm=J_b(I+C^\pm)(I-C^\pm)^{-1}$. With a smooth longitudinal cutoff $\chi$ changing from zero to one on $[-1,1]$, interpolate the coefficients by $C^b(s,t)=(1-\chi(s))C^-(t)+\chi(s)C^+(t)$. The small coefficient ball retains tameness and all protected values throughout, so this gives a genuine compact-transition background $K^b$. The two end paths equal the same background value on a common positive time collar, hence so does this interpolation. For the auxiliary perturbations let $P_X$ be the union of the compact protected diagonal/divisor set $D$ and pairwise separated compact generator neighborhoods $A_x$, each inside a larger exact generator chart. These pieces are separated and $D\cap\bigcup A_x=\varnothing$. Use smaller working charts compactly inside $\operatorname{Int}(A_x)$. Enumerate smooth anti-$K^b$ matrix bumps $B_n$ with compact support in $\mathbb R\times(0,1)\times(X\setminus P_X)$ by rational source/target charts and projected coordinate matrices, as in [F3]. Their finite span is dense in each finite norm on smooth compactly supported allowed fields. Put $$c_n=\frac{2^{-n}}{1+\max_{j\le n}\|e^{n\sqrt{1+s^2}}B_n\|_{C^j}},\qquad A(a)=\sum_na_nc_nB_n,\quad a\in\ell^2.$$ Cauchy--Schwarz proves convergence in every $C^k$ and in every exponentially weighted source $C^k$ norm: for fixed $k,N$, the terms with $n\ge\max(k,N)$ have their weighted norm at most $2^{-n}|a_n|$, and the finitely many earlier terms have compact support. The sum is flat to all orders at $t=0,1$. The ordinary coefficient fundamental theorem identifies its derivative sums. Thus $K(a)=K^b(I+A(a))(I-A(a))^{-1}$ is a smooth Hilbert family into every coefficient norm, with unchanged protected values, flat time-endpoint jets and source tails converging rapidly to the EXACT specified end paths. Its actual tangent fiber map is the invertible Cayley derivative, so density transfers to anti-$K(a)$ fields. It need not have compact transition support or a time collar; it is explicitly auxiliary. [F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The actual source-dependent section is smooth in the map and Hilbert parameters in $H^3\cap X_0\to H^2$. Use [F3]'s boundary-preserving local-addition charts around smooth reference maps with generator offsets. Its formula consists of first derivatives of the chart section and smooth coefficient compositions with $K(a)(s,t,x)$. The $H^2$ multiplication estimate and the chain/product rules from [F3] apply term by term, with uniform source coefficient derivative bounds from step1.2. On every infinite generator tail the reference offset is constant and the remaining terms contain a derivative of the $H^3$ section or an exponentially decaying reference derivative; no nonintegrable coefficient-only term appears. Thus the same explicit estimates give a genuine jointly smooth section and its mixed parameter derivatives. The chart boundary subspace is independent of $a$ because the physical tori and time-endpoint structures are fixed. [F2] gives its actual Fredholm derivative at every zero, in both low and higher realizations. At a constant map the operator is exactly $D_0$ in the protected product chart, hence is invertible of index zero by [F4]. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 The universal section is onto at every nonconstant solution. Such a solution has a point mapping outside $P_X$: otherwise its connected extended image lies in the one separated generator component $A_x$ containing its negative end, so the whole map solves $D_0v=0$ in that chart; finite energy and slice Poincare give $v\in H^1$, and [F4]'s inverse makes it constant. The free inverse image is an open nonempty interior set. The map cannot be constant on an open set without being constant everywhere. Indeed its difference from that constant in a coordinate chart satisfies an actual smooth Cauchy--Riemann-type system; normalization of its smooth principal complex structure gives the unique-continuation equation in [F4], and continuation through overlapping charts propagates the constant. Therefore some free point has $u_t\ne0$, since the equation gives $u_s=-K u_t$. If an $L^2$ adjoint obstruction $\eta$ were nonzero, its interior regularity is the actual [F4] argument applied to this source-dependent principal symbol: in a local complex frame the symbol is $D_0$, its remaining coefficients are smooth, and its formal adjoint is reduced by source reflection and a smooth invertible coefficient multiplication to $D_0+$ zeroth order. The local energy identity, mollification and tangential bootstrap give smoothness. Unique continuation makes its zeros isolated. Choose the free point also with $\eta\ne0$. Prescribe an anti-$K$ endomorphism $Y$ there with $Yu_t=\eta$, extend it smoothly and project onto anti-$K$ fields, and multiply it by a nonnegative SOURCE-and-target bump in a sufficiently small neighborhood of $(s,t,u(s,t))$. Its full source preimage lies in the same positive-pairing source rectangle because the bump includes both source coordinates. Hence $\int\langle\eta,Y(s,t,u)u_t\rangle>0$. Finite actual parameter tangent combinations approximate this compactly supported $Y$ in $C^0$ by step1.2; the pairing error is at most $\|\eta\|_2\|u_t\|_2$ times that error. This contradicts annihilation. No target injective-point or time-slice theorem is needed for these FULL-source variations. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.2, step 2.1, construct, contradiction]

4.1 This is onto in the actual higher section as well. The low operator has a finite-dimensional cokernel; lift its quotient basis by finitely many parameter directions using step3.1, and solve the remaining range with the bounded inverse on its orthogonal kernel complement. The local higher estimates in [F4] make this inverse respect the $H^2$ target and $H^3$ domain. Thus the explicit universal inverse gives the actual split zero charts in [F5]. The zero manifold is second-countable in the separable configuration-parameter product. Its parameter projection has kernel the actual map-operator kernel and cokernel its cokernel; its Fredholm index is the geometric strip index from [F2]. Its derivative is onto precisely when that map operator is onto. Split the finite kernel and cokernel and eliminate the infinite invertible block to see that the projection-index strata are open. Apply [F5]'s Countable-Choice Sard theorem separately to each integer index and each of finitely many generator pairs, and take their countable residual intersection. Every nonconstant zero-basepoint solution for a parameter in it is regular. One may apply the argument to all classes, since nonconstant solutions form an open subset of the actual second-countable configuration space; alternatively zero degree is locally constant under its chart homotopies. Constant solutions were already regular for every parameter in step2.1. Fix one such auxiliary parameter. [F2, F4, F5, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Approximate this chosen auxiliary structure by actual compact-transition, time-collar structures. Multiply $A(a)$ in step1.2 by $\rho_R(s)\theta_\epsilon(t)$, with $\rho_R=1$ for $|s|\le R$, zero for $|s|\ge R+1$ and uniformly bounded derivatives, and $\theta_\epsilon$ zero on actual time collars and one off slightly larger ones. The source exponential estimates give convergence of the longitudinal truncations in every uniform $C^k$. Flat time jets and Taylor's integral remainder make time truncation converge in every $C^k$ too, despite derivatives $O(\epsilon^{-k})$ of that cutoff. The Cayley inverse preserves these convergences and small-family tameness. Choose $R\to\infty$ and $\epsilon\to0$ along a sequence. Each resulting $K_n$ is EXACTLY the prescribed $J^\pm$ outside its finite transition interval, has genuine positive time collars, and preserves all generator/protected values. Their approach to the two end paths is uniform in $n$, with every source derivative, by the same exponential bounds. Thus [F2]'s asymptotically autonomous hypothesis holds even though their compact transition widths increase. [F2, F3, step 1.2, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 All sufficiently late approximants are regular at EVERY zero-basepoint solution. If not, Countable Choice supplies nonregular solutions along a subsequence. Step1.1 bounds their energies by $B$, and [F2] supplies an anchored limit with no bubbles, a central solution for the chosen auxiliary path, and finitely many autonomous end levels. The central component is regular by step4.1, or is a constant with the invertible operator in step2.1. Every end level is regular by the stated hypotheses on $J^\pm$. Give these finitely many onto operators their bounded right inverses $Q_j$. Identify tangent bundles on fixed converging cores by [F3]'s boundary-preserving local addition, equal to the coordinate identity on matching generator ends. Their fixed-core operator errors tend to zero. All actual operators on the connecting necks and sufficiently far outer tails are EXACTLY $D_0$, because the maps lie in the common generator charts by [F2]. Choose longitudinal cutoffs $\eta_{j,n}$ equal one on their cores, with $\sum_j\eta_{j,n}^2=1$, changing in long gaps with derivative at most $C/R_n$, $R_n\to\infty$. If there is only the central level its cutoff is one everywhere. Forcing localization, actual translations of the autonomous levels and the fixed central core give $$Q_n^0f=\sum_j\eta_{j,n}T_{j,n}Q_j(T_{j,n}^{-1}(\eta_{j,n}f)),\qquad P_{u_n}Q_n^0=I+E_n,$$ with $\|E_n\|_{L^2\to L^2}\le C(\delta_n+R_n^{-1})\to0$. These estimates follow from the exact first-order Leibniz identity; infinite tails have no coefficient errors. The norm of $Q_n^0:L^2\to H^1$ is uniformly bounded by the finite component inverse norms. Neumann correction makes the actual operator onto, contradicting nonregularity. This uses compactness followed by an actual linear construction, not an unsupported assertion that regularity is open under every source truncation. Higher range agreement gives all integer realizations. Fix one genuine compact-transition approximant with this property. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, contradiction]

7.1 Finally the actual index-zero solutions for ANY fixed compact-transition structure in this family regular at every zero-basepoint solution are finite; the approximant just constructed is one such structure. Any sequence of them has [F2]'s bounded-energy anchored limit. Every nonconstant autonomous end level has index at least one, since it is regular and its nonzero translation derivative is a kernel vector. The central component is regular of nonnegative index, including the constant index-zero case. The exact index sum therefore permits NO end levels and makes the central index zero. There are no bubbles and no lost tail energy or diameter. This yields global $H^3$ convergence with no source translation: on each far tail the sequence and limit solve the same exact chart equation, so their difference $w_n$ satisfies $D_0(\chi w_n)=\chi'w_n$ for a fixed far-tail cutoff. The actual inverse and higher estimates give $\|\chi w_n\|_{H^3}\le C\|\chi'w_n\|_{H^2}\to0$ from smooth convergence on its fixed transition band. The compact core converges in its local-addition chart too. At the regular index-zero limit the actual zero chart has dimension zero and is a single point locally, so the sequence is eventually that solution. If there were infinitely many distinct solutions, Countable Choice selects ordered tuples of arbitrarily large finite size; flatten them, retain first occurrences and choose representatives to obtain a sequence of distinct solutions. The preceding compactness contradicts this. Thus all actual index-zero solutions, across endpoints and classes, are finite. Their empty or finite parities define the asserted matrix, without quotienting translation or asserting any chain identity. Genus zero was included at the start. [F2, F4, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 6.1, construct, contradiction] ∎
