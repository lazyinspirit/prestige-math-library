## Statement

Assume Countable Choice and the common fixed-background globally weak closed-diagram geometry of [[thm-fixed-background-hat-data-admit-regular-finite-continuation-counts]]. The following two actual gluing assertions hold, with zero basepoint degree throughout.

First let $q\in\{0,1\}$, let $a$ vary near an interior point $a_0\in\mathbb R^q$, and let $K_a(s,t)$ be a smooth family equal to fixed autonomous paths $J^\pm_t$ outside one common compact longitudinal interval. The time collars, protected values and exact generator charts are common. Let $u$ be an actual central solution at $a_0$, with index $-q$, such that its ACTUAL augmented derivative
$$\mathbb R^q\oplus H^3_u\longrightarrow H^2_u,\qquad(\dot a,\xi)\longmapsto P_u\xi+(\partial_aK_a[\dot a])(s,t,u)u_t$$
is onto. Thus it is an isomorphism, its index being zero. Let $v$ be an actual regular autonomous index-one strip on either end, with matching generator. Then that ordered central/end pair has an actual injective exhaustive half-interval gluing collar in the augmented family of total index $1-q$, with no longitudinal translation quotient of the central solution. Its interior consists of actual solutions regular in the stipulated AUGMENTED family, and every bounded-energy sequence converging to precisely that pair with no other components is eventually in this collar. The central family parameter is solved as part of gluing. For $q=0$ the collar lies in the fixed continuation moduli of index one; for $q=1$ it lies in the parameterized continuation moduli of index zero. Class and basepoint degree are the actual concatenated ones.

Second let $K^{01},K^{12}$ be two compact-transition choices whose EVERY zero-basepoint solution is regular, with common autonomous middle end $J^1$ and outer ends $J^0,J^2$, all of whose nonconstant zero-basepoint autonomous strips are regular. Translate their transition supports to centers $-R/2,+R/2$, and join them by their identical middle value $J^1$, obtaining a genuine smooth structure $K^R$ for every sufficiently large $R$. For all sufficiently large $R$, every zero-basepoint solution for $K^R$ is regular, its actual index-zero solution set is finite, and that set is in bijection with pairs of actual index-zero solutions for $K^{01},K^{12}$ having a common intermediate generator. The bijection is actual gluing, with no extra multiplicity and with each pair's continuous concatenated class. Consequently their finite mod-two index-zero matrices satisfy
$$\Phi_{K^R}=\Phi_{K^{12}}\Phi_{K^{01}}.$$
This assertion includes constant component solutions. It does not assert that the family of maps as $R$ varies is injective when both components are constant.

These are gluing/collar and finite composition-count statements. Chain-map and homotopy identities require the corresponding actual one-manifold compactifications; no polygon or Heegaard move map is asserted.

## Proof

1.1 Start with a central solution and a positive-end autonomous level; the negative-end construction is obtained by reversing the order of the two cores, with the same orientation-preserving strip charts. All end coordinate derivatives of both smooth maps decay like $C_ke^{-\gamma|s|}$ in the exact product chart at their common generator. Fix a compact-core scalar phase on $v$ nonzero on $v_s$ and normalize it to value one on that vector. Since $P_v$ is onto of index one, its kernel is precisely that translation line and its zero-phase restriction is an isomorphism; subtracting its kernel projection from a specified right inverse constructs its bounded inverse. The augmented central operator is an isomorphism by hypothesis and [F1]. Its parameter derivative has support inside the fixed transition interval: the end structures are independent of $a$. These are actual inverses in $H^3\to H^2$ by [F2], rather than formal index data. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 For the second assertion choose $S$ containing both transition supports. For $R>4S$ use $K^{01}(s+R/2,t)$ on $s\le0$ and $K^{12}(s-R/2,t)$ on $s\ge0$. On an open middle interval both are exactly $J^1$, so the definition is smooth and has the stated outer values. Every zero-basepoint solution has the common energy bound from [F1], independent of $R$. Its actual two-anchor compactness follows by the same proof in [F1], now retaining both anchors $-R/2,+R/2$ among the finite bad-window centers. After recentering about them the two coefficients converge on every fixed rectangle to the ACTUAL compact profiles $K^{01},K^{12}$. Every further escaping bounded-offset cluster lies to the left, in the middle, or to the right, and has coefficient limit $J^0,J^1,J^2$. The local bubble extraction, source-dependent small-window generator collapse, target divisor-curvature degree additivity and zero-mark bubble exclusion are exactly the local constructions in [F1]; none depends on there being only one anchor. Thus the limit consists of these two anchored central components, possibly constant, and finitely many autonomous levels between and outside them, with full energy, classes and index sum. The two anchors remain ordered, even if one or both have zero energy. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Leave the central map anchored and translate the autonomous map to distance $R$ to its right. On the overlap their coordinates are exponentially small. Combine them by real scalar cutoffs in that exact chart, equal to the appropriate original map on its core. The boundary planes are preserved by the real linear combination. The central profile is already autonomous on that end; near the displaced core the prescribed equation is exactly the autonomous end equation. Thus the resulting actual preglue $w_R$ solves the prescribed equation off its cutoff band, has the concatenated generator ends and has residual of $H^2$ norm at most $C e^{-\gamma R/2}$. Its continuous Whitney class is the concatenation: contract the same exact-chart neck to the generator by a real scalar homotopy. The actual section in [F2] has one quadratic Lipschitz bound independent of $R$: every nonlinear coefficient lies on one of the two fixed or translated nonflat cores; its derivatives and the reference-map derivative norms there are uniformly bounded. On the connecting generator region the section and local addition are exactly linear. Long constant regions contribute no coefficient-only forcing. This explicitly supplies the uniform nonlinear estimate even though a reference map's unrenormalized zeroth norm could depend on the separation. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Construct the actual right inverse at this preglue. Give its two cores squared cutoffs $\eta_0,\eta_1$ with $\eta_0^2+\eta_1^2=1$, changing only in a gap of width proportional to $R$ and with first derivative $O(R^{-1})$. Identify their tangent bundles by [F2], equal to the identity in the common generator chart. Localize the forcing, apply the augmented central inverse and the zero-phase autonomous inverse, multiply their section outputs by the corresponding cutoffs and sum. Retain the ONE central parameter output unchanged. Its forcing is supported where $\eta_0=1$, so it produces no unmatched parameter term on the other core or the neck. The exact first-order Leibniz identity and the squared partition give right-inverse error $O(R^{-1})$ on the $H^2$ target, with the derivative cutoffs through order three bounded accordingly. Neumann correction supplies an onto augmented operator with bounded right inverse uniformly in $R$. At the smooth preglue, the bundle-symbol theorem in [F1] still applies; its completed boundary phase is the concatenated class phase, whether or not the nonlinear section vanishes there. Thus the preglue's actual geometric index plus $q$ is one by [F1]'s class/index additivity. Its kernel therefore has dimension one. More concretely truncate the autonomous translation vector on its core and correct it by $(I-QP)$. Its phase tends to one and its core norm stays bounded below, since the cutoff error is $O(R^{-1})$. Thus the same translated phase is an isomorphism on the one-dimensional kernel. Subtract that kernel multiple from $Q$ to obtain the uniformly bounded inverse on zero-phase sections. This finite phase correction includes the central parameter component and uses no differentiability of translations on the entire Sobolev map space. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Apply actual Newton on that zero-phase augmented domain. Steps2.1 and3.1 give an exponentially small residual, uniformly bounded inverse and uniformly bounded quadratic constant. The corrected solution $(a_R,g_R)$ has $|a_R-a_0|+\|g_R-w_R\|_{H^3}\le C e^{-\gamma R/2}$, is regular with augmented index one, and has the same class and basepoint degree. The corrected operator remains onto by its actual Neumann inverse. Its full local zero chart is one-dimensional. Smoothness for each finite $R$ follows in fixed local charts: the preglue translates ONE fixed smooth exponentially decaying map, whose derivatives of every order are in the required Sobolev spaces. The source coefficient family is smooth. The unique zero-phase inverse is locally a Neumann inverse of a fixed inverse, so no operator-norm differentiability of arbitrary source translations is assumed. Its first parameter derivative is uniformly bounded by differentiating that inverse identity; the actual coefficient and phase derivatives are uniformly bounded on the fixed or translated cores. Differentiating the residual and Newton equation therefore makes the correction's first derivative exponentially small too. On the translated autonomous core the $R$ derivative tends to $-v_s$, whereas it tends to zero on the central core. It is nonzero, and so parameterizes an open interval of the actual augmented zero chart. For $q=1$, $a_R$ remains in the stipulated interior parameter neighborhood. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

4.2 All components of this limit are regular: by hypothesis for the two central profiles and the three autonomous paths, and by the exact $D_0$ inverse for any constant central component. If solutions for arbitrarily large $R$ had nononto operators, take their two-anchor limit and patch its finitely many actual component right inverses with squared longitudinal cutoffs. Each core error tends to zero and all gaps and outer tails are exactly $D_0$; hence the actual right-inverse error is $C(\delta_n+R_n^{-1})\to0$, exactly as in step 3.1 without its phase constraint. A Neumann correction contradicts nonregularity. Therefore every zero-basepoint solution for all sufficiently large $R$ is regular. Nonconstant autonomous levels have index at least one. Each central component has nonnegative index because it is regular, including index zero for a constant. For a sequence of actual index-zero solutions the index sum in step 1.2 thus excludes every nonconstant autonomous level and makes both central indices zero. Their actual index-zero sets are finite by [F1]'s anchored compactness and zero-chart argument, which depends only on regularity of all zero-marked solutions. It is not necessary to assume finiteness of arbitrary Whitney-class sets. [F1, F2, F3, step 3.1, step 1.2, construct, contradiction]

5.1 This interval exhausts the specified nodal limit. For any sequence with that two-component limit, use the compact-core phase on the escaping autonomous profile to fix its shift relative to $v$, giving its actual separation $R_n\to\infty$. The scalar phase inverse theorem applies to the one fixed smooth profile; the change-of-source-variable formula puts all phase derivatives on its compact test. The anchored map and the parameter converge to the specified central pair. All maps lie in the exact chart on the long gap and outer tails by the stipulated no-loss convergence and [F1]. Their difference from $w_{R_n}$ is individually in the augmented Sobolev domain. On every nonflat core it tends to zero smoothly. Its nonlinear remainder is supported on those cores and therefore tends to zero in $H^2$; on all long chart necks the section is exactly linear. Apply the uniformly bounded zero-phase inverse in step 3.1 to this equation and the exponentially small preglue residual. It gives GLOBAL augmented $H^3$ convergence to $w_{R_n}$, not just convergence on cores. Newton uniqueness makes the original sequence exactly $(a_{R_n},g_{R_n})$ eventually. To see global injectivity for sufficiently large $R$, choose $0<e<E(v)$ so the tail-energy function $G_v(c)=\int_{[c,\infty)\times[0,1]}|dv|^2$ has $G_v(c)=e$ and $G_v'(c)<0$. Such a regular value exists inside a source interval with positive energy density. The full glued map's tail energy is nonincreasing globally, and near the escaping core equals $G_v(s-R)+O(e^{-\gamma R/2})$, with the same first-derivative control in translated source and in $R$ by step 4.1. Its crossing of $e$ therefore occurs uniquely at $s=R+c+O(e^{-\gamma R/2})$, with derivative $1+O(e^{-\gamma R/2})$. It is strictly increasing in $R$. This distinguishes all large parameters. The central profile is anchored, so there is no global translation to divide out. Core-convergence and neck-collapse tests give the half-interval topology, and the proved exhaustion identifies its endpoint with precisely the specified broken pair. The negative-end case uses a decreasing rather than increasing source position and gives the same one-sided collar. [F1, F2, F3, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 For each matching pair of these finitely many rigid central solutions, translate them to the specified two anchors and preglue across their common generator exactly as in step 2.1. Both actual component operators are isomorphisms of index zero, so the squared-cutoff construction gives a uniformly bounded inverse directly, with NO phase or free central parameter. The actual sum index is zero by class concatenation. Uniform quadratic bounds and Newton give one actual regular solution for each large $R$, in that continuous class. The converse in step 5.1 simplifies: the two anchors fix both cores, and the uniform full inverse upgrades compact-core closeness and the exact-chart neck equation to global $H^3$ closeness. Newton uniqueness therefore captures every sequence with that pair as limit. Distinct pairs cannot produce the same solution for arbitrarily large $R$, since fixed-anchor smooth limits would identify their first and second components. Taking the maximum of finitely many thresholds makes these neighborhoods disjoint and ensures every index-zero solution is obtained: otherwise step 4.2 would extract an omitted pair and contradict its exhaustion. This proves the actual bijection, including a constant component or two constants. Each fixed sufficiently large $K^R$ has finite zero-index set by the bijection, and all its zero-marked operators are regular by step 4.2. Counting that finite bijection modulo two gives exactly $\Phi_{K^R}=\Phi_{K^{12}}\Phi_{K^{01}}$. No identity between a finite juxtaposition count and a composite was assumed before this neck argument, and no chain identity or move theorem has been inferred. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 5.1, step 4.2, construct, algebra] ∎
