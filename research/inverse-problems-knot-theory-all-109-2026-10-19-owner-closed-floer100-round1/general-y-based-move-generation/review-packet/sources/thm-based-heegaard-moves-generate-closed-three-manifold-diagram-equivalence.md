## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Any two actual pointed Heegaard diagrams of the same closed connected oriented smooth three-manifold are related by a finite sequence of attaching-curve isotopies and same-system handleslides avoiding the basepoint, genus stabilizations and destabilizations in basepoint-disjoint disks, and orientation-preserving embedded-diagram isomorphisms carrying the two handlebodies, surface, attaching systems and basepoint. Genus zero and empty attaching systems are included. The input compression witnesses may be proper in the broad smooth sense without boundary transversality.

Every intermediate diagram is a complete pointed Heegaard diagram of that same manifold: a genus-$g$ surface with $g$ disjoint compression disks cutting each handlebody into one ball. The theorem is purely geometric. It does not assert admissibility of every intermediate diagram, analytic maps, auxiliary-choice invariance, gradings or a comparison of complexes. No knot or extra basepoint is required in the input or retained in the conclusion.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] The closed pointed-diagram convention and its actual complete disk systems are supplied. An auxiliary local unknot through the basepoint gives two marked crossings, endpoint Morse pairs on the actual general manifold, and a common protected function and field germ, retaining the endpoint systems up to marked transports and isotopies. ([[def-hat-heegaard-floer-complex-of-a-closed-three-manifold]], [[lem-based-heegaard-splittings-admit-a-common-auxiliary-protected-unknot]])

[F2] On any compact closed smooth manifold the relative function construction protects fixed quadratic extrema by excising their balls, gives finitely many folds and ordinary value crossings, and keeps the remaining critical values in a uniform interior range. The actual general-manifold weak-field supplier then gives no index-increasing trajectory, finitely many isolated parameter-transverse same-index trajectories and product folds with whole event-free neighborhoods. The general-three-manifold grouping supplier retains these fields and the protected tube and separates indices at the middle level. ([[lem-relative-function-event-paths-preserve-fixed-extrema]], [[lem-cc-relative-descending-paths-have-finite-same-index-events]], [[lem-finite-weak-knot-morse-movies-admit-relative-middle-level-grouping]])

[F3] Ordinary clusters without connecting trajectories admit relative parameterized height rearrangements with the SAME field. Local quadratic handles and finite collar gluing retain their actual coordinate core and cocore and framed attaching maps. Product folds have their actual full crossing spheres meeting once. A single transverse framed cancelling pair can be removed relative to the incoming boundary, carrying subsequent attaching data. ([[lem-two-regular-critical-clusters-rearrange-parametrically-relative-a-knot]], [[lem-local-morse-sublevel-pair-is-a-handle-pair]], [[lem-gluing-handle-morse-models-along-collars]], [[lem-simple-function-births-admit-geometrically-cancelling-gradient-data]], [[thm-handle-cancellation]])

[F4] A smooth auxiliary Riemannian metric exists under Countable Choice. Parameterized local inverses, smooth Jordan-disk maps with prescribed boundary collars, supported neat proper isotopy extension, compactly supported flows and finite compact-set cutoffs are supplied. All applications to cut manifolds below follow finite rounding in product face collars. ([[cor-every-smooth-manifold-admits-a-riemannian-metric]], [[lem-choice-free-smooth-inverse-function-theorem-in-euclidean-space]], [[lem-smooth-jordan-disk-boundary-maps-extend-to-diffeomorphisms]], [[lem-supported-neat-proper-isotopies-extend-boundary-preservingly]], [[thm-compactly-supported-time-dependent-vector-fields-have-global-evolution-on-a-compact-time-interval]], [[lem-manifold-bump-for-a-compact-set-inside-an-open-set]])

[A1] Countable Choice is assumed as in the actual general-manifold suppliers. The additional disk, graph and event constructions are finite. No three-sphere move-generation or automatic-admissibility conclusion is a premise. ([[def-countable-choice]])

## Proof

**Given:** The data and Countable Choice in the Statement.

1.1 Apply [F1] to the two input diagrams. We obtain one auxiliary marked oriented circle $K$ with marks $w,z$ and endpoint Morse pairs on the actual $Y$, with precisely one protected minimum and maximum, and no other critical point on their common protected tube. The lower and upper systems are their actual stable and unstable systems up to the already specified transports and marked isotopies; the final tube blend was chosen to miss both compact systems. The endpoints have no same-index or index-increasing trajectory. Apply [F2]'s protected-extrema function path, its actual weak descending-field path and its grouping, in that order. These are genuinely general-manifold Statements. They give one finite movie fixed on a smaller common tube, stationary at the original endpoints. Its middle level is regular except at finitely many index-$(1,2)$ folds. Lower ordinary values are in separated ranges near $1/10,3/10$ and upper values near $7/10,9/10$; fold blocks have centers $1/5,1/2,4/5$. Near stationary endpoints the original grouped values are retained. No extra marked-pair insertion has occurred. [F1, F2, A1, given, construct]

2.1 Extract the actual disks on any regular slice with no same-index trajectory. Within the lower half reorder minima below the index-one points using [F3]: every inverted zero/one pair has no connection in either direction, since the only height-allowed direction would increase index. Finite adjacent exchanges decrease the inversion number. This is auxiliary bookkeeping with the SAME field, no change near the middle level or protected tube, and no change to stable sets. The upper procedure is dual. At a regular level between the zero- and one-handle groups, the sublevel is a finite union of standard quadratic minimum balls expanded through a critical-free collar. Each lower index-one unstable core has two distinct finite-time feet on these balls. An infinite transit would give a broken trajectory to another index-one point, prohibited here. Distinct cores cannot intersect by uniqueness of flow. The local handle comparison [F3] therefore gives the actual lower sublevel as those balls with framed one-handles at the feet. Its stable disks are the coordinate cocores $u=0$ continued to the middle level. Reverse hitting is finite: any break before that level could only be at another lower index-one point, because there is no lower index-two point. The cocores are disjoint, since a point has only one forward limit. Cutting them leaves the minimum balls with absorbable half-handle disk collars, hence actual standard balls. This is a finite handle description inside $Y$, not a claim about arbitrary embedded spheres in $Y$. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 The one protected lower minimum ball contains exactly the lower arc of $K$; every other ball is empty. In a small quadratic minimum sphere its two knot branches are antipodal radial rays. The finitely many pulled-back one-handle feet avoid those poles: a foot at a pole would be the same trajectory as a knot branch by uniqueness. Choose a meridian semicircle through the poles missing all foot centers, then take their foot disks small enough to miss this semicircle. Its radial half-disk is a bridge witness. Continue its boundary collar through the regular complement and absorb the cut half-handle caps; none meets that witness. The upper construction is the same with $1-f,-X$. Thus every slice gives extended systems with ONE occupied ball and possibly $e_0$ lower and $e_3$ upper empty balls. The lower zero/one graph is connected: all remaining handles have index at least two and connected attaching regions, so cannot join different components; the completed $Y$ is connected. The upper graph is connected by reversal. A spanning tree joins its balls into one ball; each remaining one-handle adds one to the boundary genus without disconnecting it. Consequently the middle level is connected and, with genus $g$, the extended system sizes are $g+e_0$ and $g+e_3$. Both sides are handlebodies. No homology assumption about $Y$ was used. [F3, F4, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Reduce an extended system to a complete system. Its finite connected dual graph has a distinguished occupied root. Choose a spanning tree rooted there and delete the disks corresponding to its edges. Regluing a leaf ball along its single joining disk absorbs a boundary disk collar, so a finite tree of balls is a ball. The retained system therefore has $g$ disks and cuts into ONE ball with the same protected arc and witness. For two trees, add an edge of the second absent from the first, remove an edge on its unique cycle absent from the second, and repeat; each exchange increases their shared edges. One exchange detaches an EMPTY tree component $E$. Glue its internal tree faces to obtain an empty ball. Its boundary contains the old joining face $a$, the new joining face $b$ and finitely many other retained faces. The complement of $a$ is a planar disk with these face holes and no marks. Slide the retained disk at $b$ over the other face holes successively, using thin bands in that planar region. Each band merges two face rims; after all mergers the new $b$ rim and $a$ cobound an empty annulus, giving a curve isotopy from the new disk boundary to $a$. The actual band sum is a parallel copy of the two proper disks joined by the band, pushed into this cut ball and rounded in its product collars. The remaining empty cap gives its proper-disk comparison to the $a$ face. A repeated retained label can have two faces in $E$; process them separately, always with the distinct anchor label $b$. No occupied arc or mark is in $E$. Thus a tree exchange is finite based disk slides and isotopies, not a homology computation. [F3, F4, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 This reduction also carries extended slides and empty cut-ball refinements to finite based slides. For an extended slide $D_b\mapsto D_b'$ over $D_c$, choose thin parallel disks and a band collar so the completed $D_b'$ is disjoint from the entire old family. Cutting along the old family, it is parallel to the boundary cap enclosing the two selected faces and their band; this cap contains no mark. Adding it splits off one empty ball, producing a common refinement of the old and slid systems. A tree reduction of either system lifts to a tree of this refinement by including the edge of the disk removed to recover it. The tree-exchange construction relates these reductions. For an insertion splitting a cut ball into an old ball and an empty companion, simply include the new edge in the lifted tree. Deletions reverse this operation. All these are statements about the ACTUAL CUT balls; no new disk is assumed boundary-parallel in the uncut handlebody. Empty or genus-zero complete systems cause no exception: their connected graph is a tree and deleting it leaves the single occupied ball. [F3, F4, step 4.1, construct]

5.2 On an event-free closed interval the actual middle levels and disks vary smoothly. Local critical models vary smoothly, and their compact crossing circles have uniformly bounded hitting times to the middle level: a divergent sequence would, by [F2]'s uniform broken-trajectory extraction, give a same-index connection at a limiting parameter. Scalar positive-speed regular models near a fold have the same invariant coordinate disks and uniform exponential hitting estimates on such a closed interval. Continue their cocores by those finite flows. Identify the levels by the supported field $V_t=-(\partial_tf_t)\operatorname{grad}f_t/|\operatorname{grad}f_t|^2$ near their compact regular union. It is zero near $K$, so the identification fixes $w,z$ and their knot germs. Transport a chosen spanning tree and its reductions. In the identified surface each resulting disjoint circle system is a smooth isotopy avoiding the marks. On a sufficiently small time subinterval its curves are graphs in disjoint annuli missing the marks; interpolate the graphs one at a time. A finite subcover gives finitely many allowed one-curve isotopies. Alternative endpoint trees are handled by the tree-exchange construction. No finiteness of other equal critical values introduced by grouping is needed. [F2, F3, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 Consider a single same-index event. Only indices one and two are possible, since a minimum has no outgoing sphere and a maximum no incoming sphere. For index one let $p\to q$ be its orbit, and use a small product block at $p$ with $f=f(p)-x^2+y^2+z^2$ and descending field $(2x,-2y,-2z)$. The event uses the positive $x$ branch. On the outgoing face $x=\eta$ the stable sheet of $q$, pulled back through a finite regular event tube, is a smooth curve $y=a(t,z)$ with $a(0,0)=0$ and $a_t(0,0)\ne0$. Write $a(t,z)=A(t)+zB(t,z)$. The section shear $(y,z)\mapsto(y-zB(t,z),z)$ straightens it to $y=A(t)$ and fixes zero. Its radial lift is smooth: for any such shear $H$, $$H(r\theta)=rL(r,\theta),\quad L=\int_0^1 DH(sr\theta)\theta\,ds,$$ so the lift is $(r,\theta)\mapsto(r|L|,L/|L|)$ on a smaller cylinder, with $L\ne0$. It changes the actual incoming collars only by supported smooth isotopies. Use $A$ as the parameter. The original quadratic flow has invariants $xy,xz$; its backward passage from $x=\eta$ to $|(y,z)|=\eta$ gives $x_{\rm in}=|(y,z)|$ and $v_{\rm in}=\eta v/|v|$. Hence the varying sheet is $xy=\eta t$, $x>0$. Opposite parameter signs round the same outgoing strip with complementary halves of the actual stable cocore $x=0$. The two quadrants have explicit smoothing graphs $|y|=\eta|t|/x$; fixing the outer collars and using monotone coordinate $x-|y|$ compares any two positive smoothing sizes without passing through $t=0$. [F2, F3, F4, step 5.2, construct, algebra]

6.2 For a fold choose a small regular-ended height band containing its two critical points and no old one; the numerical grouping ranges give this uniformly. Old handles outside the band have finite smooth collar transport. In its product model $f_t=g(u,t)-|y|^2+|z|^2$, after reflection $g_{uuu}>0$. The full local crossing spheres of [F3] lie in the block and meet once. Record a critical-free comparison, to retain the ACTUAL exterior attaching data. On a small fixed $[-r,r]$ choose $\rho=1$ near its ends and zero near zero, with $g_u>0$ on its support. Define $$c_t=\frac{g(r,t)-g(-r,t)-\int_{-r}^r\rho g_u}{\int_{-r}^r(1-\rho)},\qquad G_t(u)=g(-r,t)+\int_{-r}^u[\rho g_u+(1-\rho)c_t].$$ At the fold $c_t>0$, and it remains positive on a smaller parameter interval. Thus $G_t'>0$ and $G=g$ near both ends. With a transverse cutoff replace $g$ by $G$ on an inner box. No critical point appears in its annulus: choose transverse radius comparable to $r$; its signed-square gradient is bounded below by that radius, whereas the added derivative is $O(r^3)/r=O(r^2)$, uniformly for parameter size $O(r^2)$. On the unoccupied side the convex interpolation is also critical-free. Patch a descending field unchanged outside the block. Old same-index trajectories cannot enter and return from this modified height band, since their endpoint heights lie in one old index group disjoint from it; index increases remain excluded by group order. This gives a regular comparison with the unoccupied slice, retaining every exterior collar and knot germ. [F2, F3, F4, step 5.2, construct, algebra]

7.1 The local same-index calculation is an actual DISK band sum. Cut the stable disk of $p$ at the diameter where the event strip meets it, producing half-disks $D_p^+,D_p^-$. The continuing sheet toward $q$ is a rectangular band from this diameter to the surviving part of $D_q$. The two parameter signs cap that band by the two respective halves. Attach a parallel copy of the WHOLE $D_p$ to the first disk along a parallel band. Pair its $D_p^+$ half with the old cap across their product collar and flatten that collar; its remaining half is $D_p^-$. Thus the second disk is the slide of $D_q$ over $D_p$, followed by a proper-disk isotopy, with framing inherited from the actual product block and event tube. Outside this compact tube all hitting times stay bounded; a failure would yield a second same-index event by [F2]. Other lower disks and the upper system therefore undergo only isotopies. The tube and band miss $K$, since its trajectories go to protected extrema; compact separation allows them to miss every other disk. For index two reverse $f,X$. The common-refinement construction then turns the actual extended slide into finite based complete-system slides for arbitrary endpoint tree choices. No global feature of $S^3$ enters this coordinate and disk-cap proof. [F2, F3, F4, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct]

7.2 For an index-$(0,1)$ fold every OLD lower index-one value is above its entire band. Its stable disk, continued backwards upwards to the middle level, cannot enter that band, so old disks vary smoothly across the event. After cutting those old disks the pair lies in one old cut ball. The new one-handle has exactly one foot on the new minimum ball, because its unstable zero-sphere meets the full stable sphere of that minimum once; its other branch goes to the old side. No old uncut one-handle remains in this component. Cutting the new cocore therefore leaves a new minimum ball with one half-handle cap and the old ball with the other cap. The new ball is EMPTY: the protected knot halves descend to the unchanged protected minimum outside the fold. Both are actual balls by disk-collar absorption. This is exactly the empty cut-ball refinement just analyzed, even when there is only ONE protected minimum and even when the new empty ball is not a leaf in the uncut handle graph. It inserts no mark. The upper index-$(2,3)$ fold is dual. These extreme folds are thus erased by complete-system slides and isotopies; no analytic or geometric MOS insertion is being assumed. [F2, F3, F4, step 5.1, step 6.2, construct]

7.3 For an index-$(1,2)$ fold, its new stable and unstable circles $a,b$ on the middle level meet exactly once and bound their actual disks in the product block, with the product framings. Isolate them by the following finite cleaning. For each other lower curve meeting $b$, take an intersection nearest the crossing $a\cap b$ along a segment of $b$ having no intervening old intersection. Slide that lower disk over the new lower disk $a$ along a thin parallel band on this segment. The two contact arcs near the endpoint cancel after pushing the parallel band through its disk collar, removing that selected intersection with $b$; disjointness from other lower disks is retained by choosing the band on the cut side and nearest the crossing. Continue, decreasing the finite intersection number. Now clean upper curves meeting $a$ dually using $b$. The second cleaning does not restore the first, since the lower curves now miss the whole band neighborhood of $b$. All bands miss $K$ and the marks, since $a,b$ lie in its disjoint fold block. The regular neighborhood of the two new framed disks after cleaning is the standard cancellation region: its surface part is a punctured torus with meridian $a$ and longitude $b$, and its boundary misses every other disk. In local crossing coordinates the single attaching/belt intersection is the product one; [F3]'s framed cancellation fills it by a disk and absorbs its boundary collar, relative to the incoming band boundary. In the critical-free replacement's scalar model the filled level is precisely the graph $G_t(u)-|y|^2+|z|^2=1/2$, since $G_t'>0$. Thus the cancellation identifies the cleaned old attaching arcs with their graph projections, fixed on exterior collars. They are the actual replacement presentation, not just a presentation of a diffeomorphic ambient manifold. Conversely inserting this framed pair in that disk is the standard genus-one stabilization. For completeness its local ball has an explicit product description: thicken the two distinguished disks on their opposite sides and the single crossing box, using their product collars. Each remaining disk thickening is a ball attached to that box along a disk face; absorption of these disk collars gives a ball. Its surface trace is a disk crossing chart plus the two alternating edge bands, hence a once-punctured torus. Thinness and the cleaned boundary curves make its boundary miss all other disks. After cutting either distinguished disk its half of this ball is an empty boundary cap of the corresponding cut ball. Existing bridge witnesses can be chosen to miss this cap: push each auxiliary boundary subarc out of the empty cap across its disk boundary, then extend this finite interval isotopy through a thin inward collar fixed near the marked endpoints and actual knot arc. Round its finitely many corners first; [F4] supplies the supported extensions. Thus the cut-and-cap replacement retains the one occupied arc and its bridge witness, and the exterior tree reductions still apply. This construction is local and requires no Schoenflies theorem for $Y$. [F2, F3, F4, step 6.2, construct]

8.1 Choose disjoint closed event intervals about all folds and same-index trajectories and finitely many event-free intervals covering their complement. The regular-interval, isolated-trajectory and three fold arguments give the actual extended-system comparisons on these intervals. For a middle fold choose identical exterior trees outside its local cancellation region; its disk-collar filling retains those exterior cut balls. This yields precisely one genus change on their complete reductions, with the finite cleaning slides. For extreme folds use the empty-ball and common-refinement arguments. At common regular endpoints any tree discrepancy is finite slides by the tree-exchange construction. At the original endpoints there are no empty balls, so the complete reductions are exactly the endpoint systems constructed at the start, up to their already proved marked isotopies. Concatenate the finite comparisons. Every retained system has $g$ disks and one ball on each side by the cut-ball extraction and tree reduction; local genus changes adjust both disk counts by one. Curve perturbations against the other system can be made transverse at the finitely many resulting diagrams, away from both marks, by small surface graph perturbations; they are curve isotopies and do not alter completeness. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 5.2, step 7.1, step 7.2, step 7.3, construct]

9.1 Finally FORGET the auxiliary $K$ and $w$ throughout this comparison. Every curve isotopy, disk-slide band and genus-change disk avoided BOTH $w,z$, hence is an allowed move avoiding the original sole basepoint $z$. The ambient transports used at the endpoints and on regular levels carry all the original embedded-diagram objects, so remain the permitted isomorphisms after forgetting. No operation on a Floer complex accompanies this forgetting, and no tensor factor or marked-pair comparison is asserted. Reversing the endpoint transports recovers the two original diagrams, including different initial basepoint positions as allowed by the point-transport construction of [F1]. The genus-zero case is covered by the tree reduction when no retained disks remain. All additional choices are finite; Countable Choice was spent only at the stated local, function and weak-field suppliers. This proves the full geometric claim. [F1, F2, F3, F4, A1, step 1.1, step 8.1, construct] ∎
