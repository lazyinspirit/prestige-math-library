## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $\mathcal H=(\Sigma,\alpha,\beta,z)$ be a complete globally weakly admissible pointed Heegaard diagram for ANY closed connected oriented smooth three-manifold. Choose actual zero-basepoint regular data defining a finite chosen hat complex, with a fixed surface complex structure and area form and with $J_t=\operatorname{Sym}^g(j)$ on a neighborhood of the diagonal and each chosen region-point divisor. The path has genuine physical-time collars. Such data exist by the globally weak chosen-complex theorem.

There is an ordinary genus-one Heegaard stabilization in a disk in the $z$-region, disjoint from $z$ and all curves, together with actual allowed time-collar data on the stabilized diagram, for which every zero-basepoint holomorphic strip is EXACTLY an old strip times the unique new intersection point $c$. The stabilized diagram is globally weakly admissible. The actual linearization splits as the old linearization plus a bounded invertible real strip operator. Thus this bijection preserves actual indices, regularity, finite rigid counts and translation quotients.

The generator maps
$$I(x)=x\times c,\qquad P(x\times c)=x$$
are actual inverse chain isomorphisms of the hat complexes. In particular $PI=\mathrm{Id}$ and $IP=\mathrm{Id}$; their inverse chain homotopies can be taken to be zero. Destabilization uses P. This is a geometric stabilization move map for these actual chosen diagrams, not merely an auxiliary almost-complex comparison.

The target disk is chosen inside a neighborhood on whose ENTIRE point-divisor family the old structure is standard; the proof supplies a smooth global stabilized structure with holomorphic normal coordinates for every divisor over a separating circle. It does not assume that an arbitrary extension has that property or invoke an unproved target-degeneration compactness theorem. This is the general closed-manifold hat operation; positive-basepoint minus/infinity stabilization, arbitrary attaching-circle isotopy or handleslide maps and absolute gradings are not asserted here. Genus zero is included.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] A complete globally weak diagram admits actual regular finite zero-basepoint data and a square-zero chosen hat differential. The actual count convention uses index one before longitudinal translation. ([[thm-globally-weak-closed-heegaard-data-admit-a-finite-chosen-hat-complex]], [[def-hat-heegaard-floer-complex-of-a-closed-three-manifold]])

[F2] The point-divisor line and its relative degree are constructed even at repeated tuples. Moving the point inside one attaching-complement component preserves its relative degree. Proper holomorphic normal zeros count positively. ([[lem-symmetric-product-divisor-intersections-and-point-movement]])

[F3] Relative to a compatible standard reference, a tame structure has Cayley coefficient B of norm less than one; scaling B by a scalar in $[0,1]$ retains tameness and fixed standard values. The coefficient is zero wherever the structure is standard. The actual formula and its smooth finite patching are proved in [[lem-product-integrable-generator-neighborhoods-and-relative-tame-extension]].

[F4] The flat strip operator $D_0=\partial_s+i\partial_t$ with real and imaginary boundary lines is an actual bounded $H^1\to L^2$ isomorphism, with the indicated original boundary completion. ([[lem-transverse-linear-strip-cauchy-riemann-isomorphism]])

[A1] Countable Choice is assumed; target charts, collars and root splittings used below are finite. The old data and geometric Heegaard stabilization are those specified in the Statement. ([[def-countable-choice]])

## Proof

**Given:** The actual globally weak diagram, chosen regular hat data and Countable Choice.

1.1 Suppose first $g>0$. Let N be the old open neighborhood of $E_z=\{z\}+\operatorname{Sym}^{g-1}\Sigma$ on which EVERY $J_t$ is standard. There is a disk $D_0$ around a point $q\ne z$ in the $z$-region, disjoint from z and the curves, such that $E_{\overline D_0}\subset N$. Indeed the continuous addition map $(a,D)\mapsto a+D$ from $\Sigma\times\operatorname{Sym}^{g-1}\Sigma$ sends $\{z\}\times\operatorname{Sym}^{g-1}\Sigma$ into N; compactness of its second factor gives a whole point neighborhood of z with that property. Choose q and a sufficiently small closed holomorphic coordinate disk within that neighborhood but away from z. Consequently EVERY divisor $E_a$, $a\in D_0$, has the standard holomorphic normal equation on an old target neighborhood. Its degree on a zero-basepoint class is $n_a=n_z=0$ by [F2], since $a,z$ are in the same region. Every old zero-basepoint strip therefore avoids $E_a$ for EVERY such a: its isolated normal zeros are positive, while containment is excluded by its generator ends. Thus all its roots avoid the entire disk $D_0$, not just its center. [F1, F2, A1, given, construct]

1.2 Take the flat torus $T=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+i\mathbb Z)$, with horizontal and vertical meridians $\alpha_0,\beta_0$ meeting at the single point c. Choose a point a and a small flat disk about it away from these circles. In coordinates $\xi(q)=0$, $\eta(a)=0$ form the actual complex plumbing $\xi\eta=\epsilon$, with a sufficiently small positive $\epsilon$. This is an ordinary surface connected sum: the complex overlap is an annulus with a holomorphic nonzero derivative, so it supplies a genuine surface atlas. Fix a separating circle C given by $|\xi|=r$, with its buffered collar mapped inside $D_0$ in the old coordinate, and $|\eta|=\epsilon/r$ on the torus side. The two components of $\Sigma'\setminus C$ are holomorphically identified with $O=\Sigma\setminus\overline D_r$ and $T^\circ=T\setminus\overline D_{\epsilon/r}(a)$. The old curves and z lie in O and the new curves and c in $T^\circ$. Their product tori have exactly the generators $x\times c$. Choose a positive surface area form equal to the old form on O and the flat torus form away from the plumbing annulus; on the annulus interpolate positive densities in its complex coordinate. [A1, given, construct]

2.1 We construct the stabilized target structure globally, not merely on its product subdomain. On the component of configurations avoiding C with exactly ONE root on the torus side set $J'_t=J_t\times i$ under the actual holomorphic product identification $\operatorname{Sym}^g O\times T^\circ$. This identification is smooth even at old-root collisions because its root supports lie on disjoint surface subsets. Write its Cayley coefficient relative to the standard symmetric structure as $B_t\oplus0$. On configurations with zero torus roots and exactly one root in the buffered C collar, extend this coefficient as $0\oplus B_t$ on that root and its remaining g old roots, multiplying by a smooth collar cutoff which is one on a smaller collar of C and zero farther into the old side. The old coordinate embeds the collar across C into $D_0$, so any remaining collar root can be evaluated in the old symmetric product. If TWO roots lie in the buffered collar, that remaining coefficient is identically zero by $E_{D_0}\subset N$ in step 1.1. Thus different choices of a distinguished collar root agree as the ZERO coefficient on an open neighborhood of every overlap or collision there. On components with at least two torus roots use zero coefficient. When an old root approaches C from the one-torus component, its old coefficient is likewise zero on a whole neighborhood by step 1.1, and matches this standard extension. When its torus root approaches C, the displayed $0\oplus B_t$ formula is the SAME formula on both sides. These observations verify smooth matching across every root-crossing locus. Away from these neighborhoods use zero coefficient on the zero-torus component. The collar cutoff transitions have compact support inside the stated unique-root patches and hence extend smoothly by zero. This defines one smooth global coefficient in symmetric charts, including all repeated tuples. [F3, step 1.1, step 1.2, construct]

3.1 Each nonzero coefficient block in that construction is an old tame block and a zero root block, multiplied by a scalar in $[0,1]$. Where an old root lies in the new area-transition collar the coefficient is zero, so the possible change in its positive surface density is harmless. Elsewhere the old product form is unchanged. The coefficient norm is therefore less than one for the NEW descended product form, and [F3] gives a genuine smooth tame $J'_t$. It has the old time collars. Near any repeated-point tuple its coefficient vanishes: old collisions use the old diagonal neighborhood, torus collisions are in a standard component, and collar collisions were checked in step 2.1. Near each old chosen region-point divisor it also vanishes, since that point is outside the collar and lies in the old block, where the old structure is standard; all other blocks are standard. These neighborhoods are uniform in t by compactness. Thus $J'_t$ has the required protected nearly symmetric values and time collars. At an old generator times c it is exactly the stated product; old exact generator normal forms, when specified, extend by the flat new factor. [F1, F3, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 For EVERY point b on C the constructed structure has an actual holomorphic normal coordinate to $E_b$. At a simple collar root it is the corresponding surface coordinate: step 2.1 gives the exact split $j_{\mathrm{root}}\oplus J_{\mathrm{remaining}}$, so the root's equation is the ordinary scalar holomorphic equation, irrespective of the remaining block. At a repeated collar root the WHOLE structure is standard and the local defining polynomial of $E_b$ is holomorphic. These cases cover $E_b$, and their normal coordinates differ by nonzero complex units. A holomorphic strip with an open subset in $E_b$ would remain in it by the scalar identity theorem continued through those charts; its generator limits exclude this. Its intersections are consequently isolated and positive. The limits avoid the compact divisor, so their preimages lie in a bounded source core and there are finitely many. The divisor degree equals that positive sum by [F2]'s local winding formula. Full integrability of the REMAINING block was not assumed in this normal-coordinate argument. [F2, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 C lies in the SAME attaching-complement region as z on the stabilized surface. The torus complement of its two meridians is a connected disk; removing its small plumbing disk and joining it to the old z-region minus $D_r$ yields one connected region, while every other old region is unchanged. A disk removal inside a connected surface region preserves its connectedness by a boundary-circle detour for any path. Thus [F2] gives $n_b=n_z$ for every b on C. Any actual zero-basepoint new strip has zero degree at each $E_b$. The positivity in step 4.1 forces it to avoid ALL these divisors. Its configurations therefore never have a root on C. The number of torus-side roots is a continuous integer on the connected source and is one at both generator ends. Hence it is one everywhere. The entire strip lies in the actual product component of step 2.1 and splits as $(v,w)$ with $v$ an old $J_t$ strip on O and $w$ a flat torus strip on $T^\circ$, with the prescribed two boundary systems and uniform ends. Each factor has finite energy for THIS strip. Indeed its uniform ends give a continuous compactified-disk image. That compact image is disjoint from the closed set $E_C$, so it has positive distance from $E_C$. The product projection and all fixed-order coordinate derivatives are bounded on a neighborhood of that particular compact image, hence bound both factor energies by a finite constant times its given energy. No false uniform inverse bound at a collision of roots approaching C from opposite sides is used. [F2, step 1.2, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 The torus factor w is constant c. Here is a direct lift proof, so no higher-genus or target-neck theorem is imported. Its uniform ends compactify it to a continuous disk. Such a disk map to the flat torus has a continuous plane lift: subdivide each segment from one fixed source center to a variable point into the SAME sufficiently large finite number of pieces, using uniform continuity, and sum the unique torus difference vectors of length less than $1/4$; this depends continuously on the endpoint and telescopes to the map. The lift is smooth locally because the quotient is a local translation. Its two boundary arcs lie on one horizontal and one vertical integer-level line, which meet exactly once, so both lifted endpoints coincide. Subtract that intersection and call the lifted holomorphic strip W. Its boundary values lie in $\mathbb R,i\mathbb R$ and tend to zero at both ends. Transverse slice Poincare gives $\|W(s,\cdot)\|_2\le C\|W_t(s,\cdot)\|_2$: endpoint values on transverse lines are bounded by their difference, the integral of $W_t$. Finite energy thus makes $W\in H^1$. Conjugate reflection across $t=0$ and negative-conjugate reflection across $t=1$ give an $H^1$ extension, antiperiodic with period two. Even-kernel convolution followed by real longitudinal cutoffs supplies smooth approximants with the same two boundary planes. Thus W belongs to precisely the boundary completion in [F4]. The actual inverse [F4] applied to $D_0W=0$ makes W zero. [F4, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

6.2 Check global weak admissibility, rather than assuming it is preserved. Each new meridian has the SAME connected z-region on its two sides; that region was described in step 5.1. Every region two-chain is consequently the old coefficient vector with that one region replaced by its joined counterpart. For a periodic new chain, the coefficient jump across the new meridians is zero, so its boundary coefficient on each new full circle is zero. Across old edges its jumps and boundary condition are the old ones. Restriction therefore identifies its periodic domains with the old periodic domains, preserving every old coefficient and $n_z$. Conversely each old periodic chain extends with that same region coefficient, and is periodic. A nonzero normalized chain thus has both signs by the old global weakness. This proves the NEW diagram is globally weakly admissible, with no periodic-index or Spin-c formula assumed. [F1, step 1.2, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Conversely every old zero-basepoint strip v avoids $D_0$ by step 1.1, and hence lies on the old side of C. Its product with c is an actual $J'_t$ strip by step 2.1 and has zero basepoint degree. Steps 5.1--6.1 show that these two constructions are inverse on ALL actual zero-basepoint strips, not just on formal domains or extracted limits. Under the actual product target chart along $(v,c)$ its linearization and boundary spaces split as $$P_{(v,c)}=P_v\oplus D_0.$$ There are no coupling terms: on the whole product component the structure is exactly $J_t\times i$ and its old block is independent of the torus coordinate. The torus summand is the bounded isomorphism [F4]. Thus the actual index is unchanged, kernel and cokernel are those of $P_v$, and regularity is inherited. Translation acts only on v when it is nonconstant, so the rigid orbit bijection and all its finite counts follow. No regular perturbation of the split component is needed. [F1, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 The finite differential matrices are identical under $I(x)=x\times c$, by step 7.1 and the old actual count convention. Both count before quotient at index one and then divide by the same nonconstant translation action; no artificial factor comes from the constant torus component. Consequently $d'I=I\,d$, and $P$ is its literal inverse on the full finite generator module. Thus $Pd'=dP$, $PI=\mathrm{Id}$ and $IP=\mathrm{Id}$. Their inverse homotopies are zero. The new square-zero and finite-count properties follow from the identical OLD matrix, whose genuine count realization was just verified. This proves actual stabilization and destabilization maps for the chosen hat complexes. [F1, step 7.1, step 6.2, construct, algebra]

9.1 If $g=0$, the old target is a point with its empty generator. Its stabilization is the same one-intersection torus diagram. Choose its flat structure and a basepoint away from its two circles. There is one generator, and the lift and actual inverse proof of step 6.1 makes every finite-energy strip constant of index zero; its rigid differential is empty. Its sole complementary region gives no nonzero periodic domain with zero basepoint coefficient. Thus both diagrams have the one-dimensional zero-differential complex, and the same I,P give exact inverse chain maps. In all genera the argument retains arbitrary closed Y: it uses only the actual ordinary geometric stabilization and the local zero-basepoint splitting, not an $S^3$ knot-specialized disk theorem. It proves this move operation and does not infer the remaining isotopy/handleslide, positive-basepoint stabilization or grading claims. [F1, F4, A1, step 6.1, step 8.1, construct] ∎
