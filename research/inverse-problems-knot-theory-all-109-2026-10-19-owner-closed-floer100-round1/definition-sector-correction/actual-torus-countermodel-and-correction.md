# Necessary full-module admissibility correction

The exact preimage is `original-before.md` in this directory. No canonical Definition has yet been changed. The defect is the literal phrase “at every generator”: it makes admissibility vacuous when there are no generators, and misses a Spin-c sector when its generator set is empty. This is an actual counterexample to the current original invariance Statement, rather than an unproved classical step.

## Two actual diagrams of the same oriented manifold

Use the Heegaard torus `T=R²/Z²`, with alpha the circle `y=0`. Fill each side by a solid torus with its meridian in this horizontal slope. Gluing the two meridian disks and the annulus between their boundaries gives a two-sphere; the common longitude supplies its product circle. Equivalently the union is the double of `S¹×D²`, namely `S¹×S²`. The basepoint is `z=(0,1/2)`. Take `0<epsilon<1/10`.

First take beta to be `y=epsilon`. Its meridian is isotopic to alpha, it is a complete genus-one disk system, and opposite-system transversality is vacuous. There are no generators. The literal current Definition consequently imposes no normalized index-zero periodic test. Every actual count sum is empty, the candidate differential is zero and its homology is the zero vector space. These data satisfy all the current generator-quantified and finite-count clauses.

Now replace beta by the embedded essential graph `y=epsilon sin(2 pi x)`. The graph is isotopic to the preceding parallel circle while avoiding z, through `y=epsilon(1-t+t sin(2 pi x))`. There is one ordinary tangency and then two transverse crossings, at `(0,0)` and `(1/2,0)`. The two small lenses are

- `D_+`: `0<x<1/2`, `0<y<epsilon sin(2 pi x)`;
- `D_-`: `1/2<x<1`, `epsilon sin(2 pi x)<y<0`.

Both avoid z. The remaining region is connected. Every normalized formal periodic chain has boundary an integer multiple of beta minus alpha and is an integer multiple of `P=D_+-D_-`, up to the sign of the chosen full-circle orientation. To check this directly, its jumps across alpha and beta determine one integer a, the coefficient in the outside region is zero, and the two lens coefficients are a and minus a. Hence every nonzero normalized periodic chain has both signs: this diagram is globally weak.

## Actual counts, not a rank convention

Choose an integrable complex structure on this graph diagram by actual smooth transport from the two-circle genus-one model. The latter is made from two close equal-radius circles, with one plumbing hole in their common interior and the paired hole in their common exterior. Each circle is essential after the gluing, and the two complementary lenses are untouched. One circle is a smooth normal graph over the other, with exactly two simple zeros. A positive circle reparametrization sends these two zeros to `0,1/2`; multiplying the normal coordinate by the positive ratio of the sine graph and that normal graph extends smoothly across the two zeros. It gives a collar diffeomorphism sending the pair to the prescribed sine graph. Its collar extension over the cut annulus is the finite prescribed-collar surface construction. Transporting the complex structure by this diffeomorphism is an actual holomorphic identification of the two symmetric products; it does not use the symmetric power of a nonholomorphic map in a fixed complex structure.

Each lens is therefore an actual circular bigon in these transported holomorphic coordinates. The harmonic primitive with imaginary boundary values zero and one has no conjugate period on the simply connected lens. Its two logarithmic corner ends give a proper degree-one map to the strip, hence its inverse is the unique holomorphic bigon up to real translation. An actual homogeneous deformation becomes a bounded holomorphic function real on both boundary lines, hence is a real constant. The actual cap winding gives pretranslation index one, so its one-dimensional kernel proves surjectivity. Thus each actual rigid count is one. These are exactly the bigon proof and arbitrary-angle inverse in the current circular top-cycle source; no absolute grading or invariance theorem is being used.

Both positive bigons run from the same crossing `x+` to the other crossing `x-`. The difference of their realized classes is P and has index zero. Every zero-basepoint domain from `x+` to `x-` has coefficients `(1+a,-a,0)` for one integer a. Nonnegativity permits only `a=0,-1`, precisely the two lenses. The reverse reference has coefficients `(-1+a,-a,0)`, which are never both nonnegative. A normalized same-end periodic chain is a multiple of P and is nonnegative only when zero. Consequently the actual hat differential is `d x+=(1+1)x-=0`, `d x-=0`. Its actual homology is `F₂²`.

The two literal admissible inputs of the current Definition thus give dimensions zero and two for the same Y. This disproves the current generator-only admissibility convention as a sufficient input for the original full-module invariant Statement.

## The missing sector is explicit

On `S¹×S²`, identify the tangent bundle with the trivial oriented rank-three bundle by

`a ∂theta+v  ->  a p+v`, with `p in S²`, `v perpendicular to p`.

Its inverse is `w -> ((w dot p)∂theta,w-(w dot p)p)`. This is a smooth framing and gives a Spin structure and its Spin-c extension with trivial determinant line. The annulus between the disjoint original alpha and beta circles, normalized using z outside it, is a nonzero nonnegative periodic domain. Its caps form the sphere above, and its pairing with this zero-Chern sector is zero. Ordinary weak admissibility in this intended sector therefore FAILS even though the sector has no generators. This is exactly the missing test.

## Smallest honest correction under discussion

Define ordinary weak admissibility for a specified Spin-c structure s by ALL nonzero integral periodic domains P with `n_z(P)=0` and `c1(s)[H(P)]` **equal to zero**, requiring both signs. This predicate is imposed even if no generator has label s. The sector complex has only generators carrying s. A diagram advertised for the full single-diagram module must meet this condition for every intended sector, including the empty ones; the full group includes all Spin-c sectors. The realized index-zero condition is a consequence for actual classes after the index theorem, and is not substituted for the whole predicate before its formal/realized comparison is proved.

This preserves every closed oriented Y and the ordinary fixed-sector construction. For the full single-diagram module, the existence of a Spin/torsion Spin-c structure whose Chern class has zero pairing makes the all-sector predicate equivalent to global mixed signs. That consequence needs its actual topology supplier and must be stated openly; it is not a silent change of the original hypothesis. The current contrary sentence that global weakness is strictly stronger for the full module cannot remain.
