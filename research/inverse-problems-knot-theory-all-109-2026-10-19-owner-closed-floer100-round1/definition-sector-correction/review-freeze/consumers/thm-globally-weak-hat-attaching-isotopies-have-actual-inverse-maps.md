## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Suppose two complete globally weak pointed Heegaard diagrams for any closed connected oriented smooth three-manifold differ by a supplied attaching-circle isotopy avoiding the basepoint and the other circles of its own family. Their actual chosen hat complexes, with the auxiliary choices described below, have actual finite chain maps and inverse chain homotopies. The construction uses diagram transport on regular intervals and genuine triangle maps at finitely many births and deaths. The local inverse at a birth is an explicit unit-pair cancellation map composed with a finite action-triangular inverse. It does not assume that birth cancellation preserves counts or that arbitrary intermediate diagrams are admissible.

One fixed finite winding preparation makes the whole central isotopy globally weak. The preparation and its removal are themselves globally weak isotopies. All finite analytic comparisons are performed using the stated continuation suppliers and consistently retained actual generator charts. This proves the attaching-circle isotopy operation between globally weak hat diagrams for general Y, including both attaching families. It does not prove the sector-only input comparison, positive-basepoint minus invariance, absolute grading or naturality.

## Facts & Assumptions

## Proof

1.1 Make the compact isotopy generic by an actual finite-dimensional perturbation relative its endpoint collars and marked-point neighborhoods. In finitely many graph charts write intersection with the fixed opposite curve as $f(t,\theta)=0$. Take finite chart bump functions and their coordinate products to embed the compact parameterized circles: a positive bump identifies its local chart, its coordinate products recover the point, and their differentials recover each tangent vector. These finitely many functions, together with polynomials through degree five in those coordinates and finite chart cutoffs, separates two-jets at each point and one-jets at any two distinct points: at two distinct embedded coordinate vectors choose a linear function taking distinct values and multiply its cubic interpolation factors by the local degree-two monomials. The conditions $f=f_\theta=0$ are therefore transverse on the two-dimensional parameter cylinder. The three conditions adding either $f_t=0$ or $f_{\theta\theta}=0$ are excluded, and the simultaneous two-point tangency condition has four equations on the three-dimensional configuration $(t,\theta,\theta')$, so is excluded as well. Apply [F2] on its countably many coordinate patches and take one common arbitrarily small perturbation. Compactness makes the tangency set finite, with distinct event times. Smallness retains the actual embedded circle family, its own-family disjointness and its avoided marks. Taylor expansion and the scalar parameter Morse completion give exactly the local ordinary fold $y=x^2-t$. [F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Prepare one uniformly admissible movie, rather than assuming all the original intermediate diagrams are admissible. Wind the FIXED opposite system using [F1]. Its paired dual loops can avoid the finite region representatives at BOTH endpoints. Choose the winding test points in open central plateaus of those annuli, away from the endpoints and common regular values of all the finitely many compact moving-curve trace maps $(t,\theta)\mapsto\Sigma$. Sard [F2] supplies such points. Their trace preimages are finite, so the multiplicities of every partial moving trace there are bounded by one finite integer K, uniformly in t. Homology relations among full circles remain constant along the isotopy. Since each complete family has independent homology classes, the moving boundary coefficients are rational linear functions of the fixed-system boundary coefficients, on their relation space; use a nonzero maximal minor. The original periodic evaluations are likewise bounded linear functions of these fixed coefficients by [F1]'s injectivity. Thus one finite C bounds every intermediate test evaluation by C times the maximum fixed boundary coefficient. At a time when a moving curve passes a test point use the two one-sided region evaluations; the extra jump is bounded by the same coefficient norm. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Take one integer $N>C+1$. The exact opposite winding traces of [F1] add $+Nb_i$ and $-Nb_i$ to the paired evaluations of every periodic domain, where $b_i$ is its fixed-system boundary coefficient. A nonzero domain has some $|b_i|$ maximal and positive, so the two resulting evaluations have opposite signs, uniformly along the WHOLE central path. If a selected point lies momentarily on a moving curve, sufficiently close one-sided points give the same strict signs. Hence the wound central movie is globally weak at every regular slice. The winding paths at the two ends are globally weak too: their supports avoid the selected old region representatives, so the inverse-trace correspondence leaves ALL their old coefficients unchanged at those points during EVERY partial winding. Nonnegativity would give an old nonnegative periodic domain, hence zero, and the inverse trace then makes the new one zero. Finally perturb the finite movies generically while retaining these open sign neighborhoods. The original endpoints are thus joined by winding, the uniformly weak central path and unwinding, all with finitely many ordinary events. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 On a closed interval containing no event, transverse intersection paths are smooth. Construct a compact surface vector field carrying the moving curves and preserving the fixed opposite curves SETWISE. At an intersection add a tangential velocity along the moving curve so that its total velocity is tangent to the fixed curve; transversality gives a unique scalar adjustment. In its local transverse axes extend that common prescribed value by compatible smooth fields tangent to the fixed axis and with the required normal component on the moving axis. Outside these finitely many patches use ordinary curve collars; a finite partition retains both linear conditions. The compact flow fixes the marks and transports both actual systems. By [F3] it gives an actual chain isomorphism with inverse for transported data. This proves the regular-interval operation without presuming that its action on homology is the identity. [F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

4.2 At one birth use the scalar normal form $y=x^2-t$ in a disk containing no other intersections or marks. A sufficiently small Hamiltonian displacement of the opposite curve crosses this parabola once and creates exactly the same two new intersections. Its compensating zero-mean displacement can be supported in a second collar arc missing the moving family. The endpoint representatives are joined to those of the given fold by regular-interval isotopies, since no further tangency occurs after shrinking the two parameter intervals. Choose a positive area form annihilating all old periodic domains by the finite-simplex construction in [F4], and balance the small opposite-system displacement for this form. The old/new triple is globally weak: its periodic chains are old chains plus signed thin annuli; retained old-region points first kill the old part, and the two private lune signs kill each remaining coefficient. Thus [F5] supplies actual finite triangle maps and actual square-zero differentials for this local comparison. [F4, F5, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Denote the new two generators by a,b, with the positive small bigon directed $a\to b$. It has actual count one by [F4]. Any other such domain would have the same action difference, namely its tiny bigon area, since all periodic areas vanish. A non-small domain contains a fixed old-region core and has larger area, so cannot contribute. The reverse coefficient $b\to a$ and both diagonal coefficients vanish by their negative or zero area; a nonconstant zero-area solution is impossible by the actual energy bound. These assertions persist for small regular perturbations by the actual local inverse and zero-mark compactness in [F4]. The old-generator part O of the new complex therefore has its actual differential in the form $$do=Ao+a\lambda(o)+b\mu(o),\qquad da=u+b,\qquad db=v.$$ Here $u,v\in O$ and all maps are the actual finite counts, not an abstract presentation chosen by a basis change. [F4, F5, step 4.2, construct, algebra]

6.1 The identity $d^2=0$ implies $\mu(u)=\lambda(u)=0$, $Au=v$, and $\mu A=\lambda$. Put $D=A+u\mu$ on O. Define $$p(o)=o,\quad p(a)=0,\quad p(b)=u;\qquad i(o)=o+a\mu(o);\qquad h(b)=a,\quad h(a)=h(o)=0.$$ Direct multiplication gives $D^2=0$, $pd=Dp$, $di=iD$, $pi=I$, and $$ip+I=dh+hd.$$ For example on b the last equality is $u+b=da$, on a it is $a=h(u+b)$, and on O it is $a\mu(o)=hdo$. Thus these are explicit finite unit-pair cancellation maps and a chain homotopy, with no appeal to a formal invariance principle. [F5, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Let H be the actual small-isotopy triangle map from the old complex to the new one, using its two-lune top cycle. It is a chain map by [F5]. Set $T=pH$. Its nearest old-generator coefficient is one by [F4]'s actual triangle inverse. A triangle to b cannot have its entire domain inside the thin opposite-system collar: the isolated tangent component of the moving curve there contains no old crossing, whereas every other moving-curve collar component has only its one old crossing. Thus such a triangle contains an old-region core. Every other direct old-output triangle also contains one. The extra term $uH_b$ introduced by p has at least the same positive area cost, since u counts positive strips. With the bounded-density balanced area choice of [F4], total unsigned swept area is smaller than half that fixed core area. Old periodic areas vanish, so relative action on each finite old connectivity component is well defined. Therefore $T=I_0+N$, where $I_0$ is the nearest-generator bijection and $I_0^{-1}N$ strictly lowers finite action. It is nilpotent; the finite geometric series is a literal chain inverse of T. [F4, F5, step 4.2, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 The actual triangle map H has the explicit chain inverse $T^{-1}p$. Its left composite is literally $T^{-1}pH=I$. Put $X=HT^{-1}p$ on the new complex. Since $pHT^{-1}=I_O$, $pX=p$, and $ip+I=dh+hd$ from step 6.1 gives $$X+I=d(hX+h)+(hX+h)d.$$ Indeed $(ip+I)X+(ip+I)=X+I$, since $ipX=ip$; substitute the actual homotopy identity and use that X is a chain map. This proves both inverse assertions at the birth. A death uses these inverse maps and homotopies. All are finite actual matrices and actual triangle counts; no moving-boundary compactification has been substituted for this argument. [step 6.1, step 7.1, construct, algebra]

9.1 The finite concatenation can be made with actual analytic comparisons satisfying their stated hypotheses. First fix all local geometric intervals and their finitely many balanced area forms. At each regular interval transport the actual generator charts by the flow of step 4.1. At a birth its new normal-form patch misses every existing crossing, so its local complex model may be inserted while retaining all those chart germs; retain the new pair's charts in the next interval. Thus shared endpoint diagrams have common prescribed actual generator charts. Choose the finite regular perturbations close enough to their standard symmetric references that each is tame for ALL the finitely many positive area forms that will be used there; standard surface complex structures are compatible with every positive surface area form, and compactness gives one positive margin for this finite choice. At a shared endpoint first use the fixed-background full-boundary theorem in [F5] to align each actual boundary tensor with one common compatible tensor, retaining the prescribed right-angle charts of this external pair. Then its changing-background theorem compares the backgrounds with that boundary tensor, the common fixed area form and the retained charts. Internal auxiliary-circle pairs keep their arbitrary actual angles and require no such external chart change. No independence under a previously unsupported change of generator charts or arbitrary simultaneous change of area forms is asserted. [F3, F4, F5, step 4.1, step 4.2, step 8.1, construct, algebra]

10.1 Compose the finitely many regular-interval isomorphisms, birth/death equivalences and analytic comparisons. Compose their literal inverse maps in reverse order. For two consecutive maps the inverse homotopies are obtained by adding the first homotopy to the conjugate of the second by the intervening chain maps; direct expansion of $gf+I=dK+Kd$ verifies this rule, and finite induction gives the asserted global inverse homotopies. For the other attaching family conjugate the source and surface structures and interchange the families as in the actual handleslide proof [F4]. This preserves domains, operator indices, counts and homotopies and gives the other-family movie. Genus zero has no attaching-circle isotopy and gives the identity. This proves exactly the stated globally weak hat operation. It does not convert ordinary sector weakness into global weakness without an actual sector-relative comparison. [F4, F5, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 8.1, step 9.1, construct, algebra] ∎
