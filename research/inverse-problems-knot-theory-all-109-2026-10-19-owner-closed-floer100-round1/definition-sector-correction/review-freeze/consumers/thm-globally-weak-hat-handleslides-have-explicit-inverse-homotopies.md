## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $(\Sigma,\alpha,\beta,z)$ be a complete globally weakly admissible pointed Heegaard diagram for any closed connected oriented smooth three-manifold, and let a specified beta handleslide band avoid z and the remaining beta curves. There is a representative gamma of this actual slide, followed by pointed attaching-curve isotopies, and there are genuine allowed chosen hat data on both diagrams, such that the actual triangle map
$$F(x)=m_2(x,\Theta_{\beta\gamma})$$
is a chain homotopy equivalence. Its count is the finite actual zero-basepoint index-zero triangle count, with no triangle translation quotient. The proof gives an explicit finite inverse and inverse chain homotopies by actual quadrilateral counts. The diagram and the whole required finite multidiagram are globally weakly admissible; no rational-homology-sphere or torsion restriction is imposed.

The analogous alpha move follows by conjugating the source and surface complex structures and interchanging the two attaching families. Auxiliary chosen-data comparisons supplied by the named continuation theorem may be composed before and after these maps wherever its stated endpoint hypotheses apply. This theorem proves the globally weak hat handleslide operation. It does not replace an ordinary sector-admissibility hypothesis by global weakness, assert positive-basepoint minus invariance, or infer absolute grading or naturality.

## Proof

1.1 Choose one point in every original alpha-beta region. A sufficiently thin ribbon neighborhood of the two sliding beta circles and their band misses all those points and z. Realize the circular model inside it by its actual cut-disk and band coordinates: the two meridians and band form the same finite ribbon graph with the same cyclic orders, and [F3] matches the rounded vertex disks and edge collars. Its outer boundary is the specified band-sum curve, followed by the explicit graph isotopy of [F1]. Retain every other beta curve with a separated small two-intersection gamma copy. The common outside region contains z and all the retained old points. Extend the complex model over the complementary finite cut surface using the prescribed collars in [F3]. Choose delta as the sufficiently small circle copies of beta in [F2], and gamma-prime as its circle copies of gamma. Thus these are actual attaching systems of the same handlebody, not merely homologous families. Choose the finitely many model crossing points away from alpha. [F1, F2, F3, given, construct]

2.1 Prove global admissibility for the WHOLE finite list $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta,\gamma'$. The slide gives an actual integral unimodular matrix M with $[\gamma]=M[\beta]$. Any full-curve homology relation $\alpha a+\beta b+\gamma c=0$ therefore gives the old alpha-beta relation $\alpha a+\beta(b+Mc)=0$. A finite surface cellular boundary solution supplies its integral old domain; subtracting it leaves the model beta-gamma domain. Normalize both by zero at z. This decomposes a triple periodic domain integrally as $P_{\rm old}+P_{\rm model}$, without rational divisibility or a Spin-c premise. At each retained old-region point the model chain is zero. Nonnegativity would make every old coefficient nonnegative, so global weakness forces $P_{\rm old}=0$. The model coefficient tests of [F1] then force $P_{\rm model}=0$. Delta-beta and gamma-prime-gamma add only their signed thin-annular differences Q. First choose the old and model test points outside those thin collars. After the preceding two vanish, each remaining Q has private positive and negative thin-lune points off the OTHER collars and off all original crossings. Evaluating there forces its coefficient to vanish. This proves global weakness for the entire list and hence for every sub-list. Small generic parameter choices exclude all triple intersection points, retaining these open sign witnesses. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 There is a positive surface area form annihilating ALL these periodic chains, with uniformly small unsigned delta-beta and gamma-prime-gamma swept areas. For the original triple periodic real space V, $V\cap\mathbb R_{\ge0}^r=\{0\}$ by step2.1 and rational Gaussian elimination. Let q be orthogonal projection to $V^\perp$. The image under q of the coordinate simplex is compact convex and omits zero. Its minimum-norm vector a satisfies $\langle a,q e_j\rangle\ge|a|^2>0$, so every coordinate of a is positive and a annihilates V. A small positive baseline density plus one interior bump in every region realizes these areas. Now take literal delta and gamma-prime circle translates of size epsilon. Each signed Q lune has a private patch of area comparable to epsilon off all other Q supports. Increase the density on the lesser-area sign by a bounded multiple of a bump in that patch to make its signed Q area zero. These finitely many disjoint corrections have bounded density and alter old periodic areas by $O(\epsilon)$. Correct the latter with fixed region-interior bumps off the thin collars, using an invertible minor of the old periodic evaluation matrix. Their coefficients are $O(\epsilon)$ and preserve positivity without altering any Q. Thus the final forms have uniform positive bounds, every periodic area is zero, the unsigned swept area is $O(\epsilon)$, and every retained old region core has area at least one common $m_0>0$. [step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Equal signed swept area also gives an actual small Hamiltonian path from beta to delta, although literal circle translation need not be Hamiltonian for the chosen area form. In a normal collar write its endpoint as the graph f. For every $s\in[0,1]$ solve the scalar equation that the graph $sf+b(s)$ has signed swept area zero. Its derivative in b is strictly positive; the implicit inverse gives smooth b with $b(0)=b(1)=0$. The normal velocity has zero area mean, hence a single-valued periodic primitive, whose collar extension is the desired Hamiltonian. Do this independently on the disjoint circles, and likewise for gamma and gamma-prime. The endpoint circular domains used in [F1]–[F2] have not changed. At an alpha intersection prescribe a supported positive germ preserving its one model circle SETWISE and straightening alpha against it; supports miss all mutual model-circle crossings. The prescribed-germ construction in [F3] extends these finitely many germs. Pull the surface complex structure back by their composite. This is a holomorphic identification with the original complex model carrying every model circle to itself, so all its counts are preserved exactly. Mutual model-circle angles retain their actual values. [F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Choose the coherent data of [F4] sufficiently close to this integrable model on the model subdiagrams, with its protected structures and exact generator charts retained. The actual compactness and inverse arguments in [F1]–[F2] retain their top cycles and both products. Their differentials are zero, including the reversed pairs by the strip transformation $(s,t)\mapsto(-s,1-t)$. On every pair involving alpha the chosen actual hat differential and its square zero are those of [F4]. There is no appeal here to the invariance theorem being proved: all choices are made on this ONE globally weak finite multidiagram, and the maps and identities follow from its actual zero- and one-dimensional polygon spaces. [F1, F2, F4, step 2.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Consider the small-isotopy triangle map $H:CF(\alpha,\beta)\to CF(\alpha,\delta)$ with the top beta-delta input. Let $H_0$ be the nearest-generator bijection. There is one actual small triangle for each nearest generator, by the scalar triangle inverse and count in [F2]; all its root supports are disjoint and off the diagonal. A domain confined to the thin beta-delta collars can only be this nearest domain: each alpha arc there has its one original crossing, and its top beta-delta corner starts exactly one partial lune on the required side. Any other nonnegative domain contains a retained old-region core. For the small model its unique small-domain count persists by exactly [F2]'s compactness argument: no two-family strip domain can be supported inside the three-sided region, and zero-mark bubbles are excluded. Thus the coefficient of $H_0$ is one, and every other term has area at least $m_0-O(\epsilon)$. [F2, F4, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 This area estimate makes H an actual finite chain isomorphism. On a zero-mark connectivity component of the finite old generator set, assign relative action by the area of any realized smooth Whitney model from a chosen generator. Its ambiguity is an old periodic area and hence zero by step3.1. Gluing the nearest small triangle inversely identifies each target component with its old component; a triangle to any other generator gives such a smooth relative model, so different connectivity components do not mix. The area of its nearest small triangle is $O(\epsilon)$, uniformly over the finite set. By step6.1 every other term strictly lowers the resulting finite old action when epsilon is small enough. Therefore $K=H_0^{-1}(H-H_0)$ is nilpotent, and $$H^{-1}=(I+K+\cdots+K^{n-1})H_0^{-1}$$ for any n at least the number of generators. The actual triangle identity in [F4] makes H a chain map; its literal inverse is consequently a chain map as well. The same proof gives a small-isotopy chain isomorphism $H':CF(\alpha,\gamma)\to CF(\alpha,\gamma')$. This is finite action linear algebra, not a completion or an assumed Hamiltonian-isotopy invariance theorem. [F4, step 3.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 Write $A=CF(\alpha,\beta)$, $B=CF(\alpha,\gamma)$, $C=CF(\alpha,\delta)$ and $B'=CF(\alpha,\gamma')$. Define actual triangle chain maps $F:A\to B$, $G:B\to C$, and $F':C\to B'$ using respectively the top cycles for beta-gamma, gamma-delta and delta-gamma-prime. The quadrilateral count and the two products in [F2] give ACTUAL finite degree-one maps $Q:A\to C$ and $R:B\to B'$ satisfying $$GF+H=dQ+Qd,\qquad F'G+H'=dR+Rd.$$ The input-cycle differential terms vanish by step5.1. If the coherent endpoint triangles use a different sufficiently small regular choice from the model, the actual relative one-parameter homotopy in [F4] is added to Q or R. Their endpoints have the same literal count-one products by [F2], so this merely changes the displayed actual homotopy, not its conclusion. [F1, F2, F4, step 5.1, step 7.1, construct, algebra]

9.1 Define $L=H^{-1}G$, $N=FH^{-1}$, $M=(H')^{-1}F'$, $q=QH^{-1}$ and $r=(H')^{-1}R$. These are finite actual chain maps except for the stated degree-one homotopy maps. Multiplying the first identity of step8.1 by $H^{-1}$ on the left gives $$LF+I_A=d(H^{-1}Q)+(H^{-1}Q)d.$$ On the other sides the two identities give $GN+I_C=dq+qd$ and $MG+I_B=dr+rd$. Direct multiplication, over $\mathbb F_2$, yields $$M+N=d(Mq+rN)+(Mq+rN)d.$$ Multiplying this identity by G and adding the second of the preceding two gives $$FL+I_B=NG+I_B=d\big((Mq+rN)G+r\big)+\big((Mq+rN)G+r\big)d.$$ Thus L is an explicit chain homotopy inverse of the actual geometric handleslide map F, with both inverse homotopies specified. No inference from an abstract quasi-isomorphism or equality of homology ranks was used. [step 7.1, step 8.1, construct, algebra]

10.1 For a slide of the other attaching family use the actual conjugate diagram $(-\Sigma,\beta,\alpha,z)$. The identity from $(\Sigma,j)$ to $(-\Sigma,-j)$ is antiholomorphic; in symmetric coefficient charts it is coefficientwise conjugation, hence a smooth real diffeomorphism even at collisions. On strips combine it with the antiholomorphic source map $(s,t)\mapsto(s,1-t)$ and replace $J_t$ by $-J_{1-t}$. The two minus signs cancel in the Cauchy--Riemann equation, with the same input and output. The positive area form becomes minus the old one on the oppositely oriented surface. Domains and marked degrees retain their signs, since source and surface orientations are both reversed. On polygons conjugate the marked disk and reverse its boundary labels in the same way. These are real operator isomorphisms, so they retain actual indices, kernels, cokernels, counts and sewing homotopies. Applying the proved beta operation in this conjugate diagram and transporting back gives the alpha operation. Allowed chosen-data comparisons at either end are the actual chain equivalences of [F5], composed with the displayed inverse maps and homotopies. Only their stated endpoint hypotheses are used. All diagrams in this construction are globally weak by step2.1, for arbitrary closed Y. Ordinary sector-only diagrams and positive-basepoint polynomial complexes require their own admissibility and weighted comparison proofs; neither has been inferred from this operation. [F5, step 2.1, step 9.1, construct, algebra] ∎
