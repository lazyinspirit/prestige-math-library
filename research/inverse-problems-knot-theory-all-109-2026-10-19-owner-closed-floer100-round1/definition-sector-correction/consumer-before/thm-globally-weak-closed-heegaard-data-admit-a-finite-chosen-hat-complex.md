## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Give an actual complete singly pointed Heegaard diagram of ANY closed connected oriented smooth three-manifold $Y$, with transverse attaching systems, one-ball compression cuts, basepoint $z$, a reference orientation-compatible Riemann surface structure and a positive area form. Suppose it is GLOBALLY weakly admissible: every nonzero periodic region domain with $n_z=0$ has both signs. Broad smooth tangencies of auxiliary compression-disk witnesses are permitted.

There is a choice of surface complex structure, protected region and marked-divisor neighborhoods, common product-integrable generator charts and a nearly symmetric tame path constant on genuine endpoint time collars, for which EVERY nonconstant finite-energy strip with $n_z=0$ has onto actual fixed-end Cauchy--Riemann operator. There are finitely many actual index-one strip orbits with $n_z=0$, across all endpoints and Whitney classes, and their modulo-two count matrix satisfies $\widehat\partial^2=0$. These actual chosen counts therefore define a finite chain complex over $\mathbb F_2$.

Every complete pointed splitting can first be made globally weakly admissible by the finite basepoint-avoiding curve isotopies in [[thm-complete-pointed-heegaard-diagrams-admit-globally-weak-admissible-isotopies]]. Consequently a chosen-data complex of this kind can be obtained from every supplied complete splitting of general $Y$. This does not identify complexes before and after those isotopies. It neither imposes global admissibility on ALL inputs of the original sector-specific definition nor proves the construction for every merely sector-admissible diagram. Auxiliary-choice and Heegaard-move invariance, Spin-c decomposition, relative or absolute grading, plus/infinity and cobordism maps remain separate. In genus zero the empty generator gives the one-dimensional complex with zero differential; a possibly empty positive-genus generator set is not asserted to have nonzero homology.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual complete singly pointed splitting, its global mixed-sign admissibility, reference complex structure, positive area form and basepoint. No homology or Spin-c restriction on $Y$ is assumed.

[F1] Proper compression disks have same-boundary neat replacements and smooth cut-ball parametrizations after transporting an abstract handlebody to its standard embedded model. These data supply an actual finite oriented surface polygon. A prescribed positive local surface germ extends to a supported disk diffeomorphism with identity boundary collar. ([[lem-proper-compression-disks-admit-neat-witnesses-with-standard-cut-balls]], [[lem-neat-balanced-handlebody-cuts-supply-finite-surface-polygons]], [[lem-smooth-jordan-disk-boundary-maps-extend-to-diffeomorphisms]])

[F2] The rational finite-region proof of [[lem-pointed-heegaard-admissibility-finite-domain-slices]] uses only a rational periodic-domain matrix, the zero-mark mixed-sign property and fixed endpoint corners. Its finite-dimensional argument is repeated explicitly below for these general closed data. [[thm-complete-pointed-heegaard-diagrams-admit-globally-weak-admissible-isotopies]] supplies the actual basepoint-avoiding global admissibility isotopies when they are needed.

[F3] Continuous classes admit actual smooth fixed-end models, with homotopy-invariant additive actual index and additive point-divisor degrees. Differences of same-end smooth domains are full-curve periodic; equal domains give equal actual indices, with the supplied finite surface polygon. ([[lem-continuous-whitney-classes-admit-relative-smooth-fixed-end-models]], [[lem-smooth-relative-whitney-homotopies-preserve-strip-fredholm-index]], [[lem-symmetric-product-divisor-intersections-and-point-movement]], [[lem-equal-whitney-domains-have-equal-boundary-windings-and-fredholm-index]])

[F4] Protected point-divisor multiplicities are nonnegative by [[lem-pointed-heegaard-integrable-divisor-positivity]]. The domain-energy estimate is $E(u)\le C\sum_R n_R(u)\int_R\eta$, with one common $C$ on a sufficiently small fixed protected coefficient family. Auxiliary flat-endpoint-jet all-strip regular paths have actual constant-collar approximants regular on EVERY nonconstant zero-marked strip below any specified finite energy bound. The compact cut systems must each have a chosen zero mark in every cut component; here the single basepoint does so. ([[lem-nearly-symmetric-heegaard-strip-domain-energy-bound]], [[thm-countable-choice-flat-endpoint-jet-universal-strip-regularity]], [[lem-zero-marked-bounded-energy-strip-regularity-survives-collar-approximation]])

[F5] For a path regular on all zero-marked strips below $E_*$, the actual index-one orbits below that energy are finite. For each fixed zero-marked index-two class $\phi$, if $E_*>B_\phi=C\sum_R|n_R(\phi)|\int_R\eta$, the actual compactified one-manifold has finitely many broken rigid ends and gives their modulo-two cancellation. These results apply to general compact Heegaard data, without a torsion Spin-c or knot premise. ([[thm-finite-zero-marked-actual-index-one-strip-counts-under-an-energy-bound]], [[thm-fixed-zero-marked-whitney-class-index-two-compactification-and-mod-two-cancellation]])

## Proof

1.1 If the genus is zero, the target is a point and the stated one-generator zero differential follows directly; assume henceforth positive genus. Prepare the geometric data without a category narrowing. View either actual handlebody as an abstract regular neighborhood of its finite graph, transport it to the standard ball-with-handles model embedded in $S^3$, apply [F1]'s broad same-boundary compression preparation there and transport back. The surface, attaching circles and diagram in $Y$ are unchanged. The resulting one-ball cut supplies the actual finite polygon needed by [F3]. To choose genuine integrable crossing charts, at each of finitely many crossings take a positive smooth chart $\Psi$ straightening the two arcs and an old holomorphic chart $c$. The positive local germ $c^{-1}\Psi$ fixes that crossing. Extend it by [F1] to a diffeomorphism supported in a small crossing disk, identity near its boundary. Choose these disks disjoint and off all chosen region points and $z$. Their finite composite $F$ gives the actual integrable structure $j=F^*j_{\mathrm{ref}}$; the charts $cF=\Psi$ are holomorphic, with unchanged holomorphic transition maps. Their products give common $\mathbb R^h/i\mathbb R^h$ generator charts in $\operatorname{Sym}^h\Sigma$. Choose protected diagonal and region-divisor neighborhoods separated from the two compact tori and smaller generator charts, and use the constant standard background path. No interpolation of surface complex structures has been presumed integrable. [F1, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Prove domain finiteness independently of an index bound and BEFORE choosing regular counts. Number the regions and let $L\subset\mathbb R^r$ consist of real periodic chains with $n_z=0$. The finite integer boundary matrix, modulo the full-circle span, makes $L$ a rational linear kernel. It contains no nonzero nonnegative vector: if $v\ge0$ were nonzero, restrict to the face of its strictly positive coordinates. A rational basis of that restricted kernel and sufficiently close rational approximations to the finitely many coefficients of $v$ give a nonzero rational nonnegative vector. Clearing denominators contradicts the assumed integral mixed-sign condition. Fix endpoints $x,y$ and collect the nonnegative domains of ALL continuous zero-basepoint Whitney classes between them. If the collection is nonempty, choose one domain $D_0$. Differences have their endpoint corners cancel by [F3], so the collection lies in $(D_0+L)\cap\mathbb R^r_{\ge0}$. Its integral members are bounded: otherwise choose, using a fixed enumeration of $\mathbb Z^r$, integral members of norm exceeding each integer. Normalize them. A subsequence has a nonnegative unit-vector limit, by successive subdivision of the finite coordinate cube and retention of the first subcube containing infinitely many indices. Its difference from $D_0/\|D_n\|$ lies in the closed kernel $L$, so the limit is a forbidden nonzero nonnegative member of $L$. Thus there are only finitely many integral domains for this pair. This is the actual [F2] argument, requiring no rational-homology-sphere premise. [F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Take the finite union $\mathcal D$ of these finite sets over the finite generator pairs. Let $C$ be a common domain-energy constant on the small protected coefficient family in [F4] and put $$B=\max\left(\{C\sum_R n_R(D)\int_R\eta:D\in\mathcal D\}\cup\{0\}\right),\qquad E_*=B+1.$$ Every actual zero-basepoint holomorphic strip has a nonnegative region domain, by protected-divisor positivity, hence has energy at most $B$ by [F4]. More importantly, any concatenation of two such strips has a nonnegative domain, zero basepoint degree and a continuous Whitney class by [F3]; its domain is itself in $\mathcal D$. Its fixed-class bound $B_\phi=C\sum_R|n_R(\phi)|\int_R\eta$ is therefore at most $B$ too. This uniform bound uses all continuous nonnegative domains, not just eventually counted strips. There is no claim that two classes with the same domain are equal, or that all signed domains are bounded. [F3, F4, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Use the zero-mark set consisting ONLY of $z$; the additional protected region divisors are not required to have zero degree. Choose in this protected family an auxiliary all-strip regular flat-jet path from [F4]. Its genuine constant-collar approximants have the same surface, boundary structures, generator charts and common energy constant. The bounded-energy zero-marked openness theorem in [F4] makes every sufficiently late approximant regular on every nonconstant zero-basepoint strip of energy at most $E_*$. Fix one. Step 3.1 bounds ALL of its actual zero-basepoint strips by $B<E_*$, so it is regular for all of them, including every possible limiting level; no negative-index nonregular level is left uncontrolled. This also implies each nonconstant such strip has positive actual index, since its nonzero longitudinal derivative is a square-integrable kernel section by its exact end decay and onto-ness. Applying the actual finite-count assertion in [F5] now gives finitely many index-one orbits across every generator pair and represented class. Their modulo-two finite sums define a matrix on the finite vector space. [F4, F5, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Fix initial and final generators. A term in the coefficient of their differential square is an ordered pair of these finitely many rigid orbits. Its concatenated continuous class $\phi$ has zero basepoint degree and actual index two by [F3]. There are only finitely many such ordered pairs and hence finitely many such concatenated classes; distinct classes with identical domains are not identified. For EACH of these actual classes step 3.1 gives $B_\phi\le B<E_*$. The chosen path is regular on its entire required zero-basepoint energy range by step 4.1, so [F5]'s fixed-class compactification and parity theorem apply. Its boundary pairs are exactly all the rigid orbit pairs whose concatenated class is this $\phi$. Their number is even. Summing this identity over the finite list of actual concatenated classes exhausts the coefficient of the differential square and makes it zero in $\mathbb F_2$. No summation over merely formal domains, no finiteness assumption for arbitrary class sets, and no positive-mark boundary-degeneration theorem were used. [F3, F5, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 If the supplied complete splitting is not globally weakly admissible, first apply [F2]'s winding theorem. It leaves $Y$, the splitting surface and basepoint fixed, retains complete disk systems and ends with transverse curves satisfying exactly the global hypothesis used in step 2.1. The preceding construction applies to that diagram. No chain map comparing its counts to counts on the original diagram has been supplied by the winding theorem or by this proof. In particular the conclusion is existence of an actual finite chosen-data complex for general closed $Y$, not its topological invariance, a construction for every weaker sector-admissible input, or a grading assertion. Genus zero was settled in step 1.1; the empty-set cases in steps 2.1 and 3.1 give $B=0$ and a finite zero module if no positive-genus generators exist. [F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
