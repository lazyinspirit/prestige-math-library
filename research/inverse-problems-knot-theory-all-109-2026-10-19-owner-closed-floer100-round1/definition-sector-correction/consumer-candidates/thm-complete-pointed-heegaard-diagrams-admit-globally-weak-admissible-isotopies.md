## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Give any actual complete singly pointed Heegaard diagram of a closed connected oriented smooth three-manifold $Y$ as in [[def-hat-heegaard-floer-complex-of-a-closed-three-manifold]], with transverse attaching systems and basepoint $z$. There is a finite sequence of smooth isotopies of individual alpha circles, avoiding $z$ and the other alpha circles, after which opposite systems are again transverse and EVERY nonzero integral periodic region domain with $n_z=0$ has both positive and negative coefficients. The same splitting surface, manifold, basepoint and beta system are retained; the alpha system still bounds a complete compression-disk system.

Thus globally weakly admissible diagrams can be chosen for every such complete splitting, for general $Y$. Neither a rational homology sphere nor a torsion Spin-c premise is imposed. This is the global condition, stronger than ordinary weakness in ONE specified sector. The input here is an arbitrary complete geometric diagram; it is not asserted to satisfy that condition before the isotopies. The corrected full-module admissibility convention is equivalent to global weakness, whereas a specified-sector-only construction remains separate. No count invariance along the isotopies, sector-index theorem, strong Spin-c admissibility or Floer move map is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual complete diagram and its specified compression disks. A periodic domain here is ANY integral chain of complementary regions with boundary an integral sum of full attaching curves; it need not be known to represent a Whitney class.

[F1] Broad proper auxiliary compression disks admit same-boundary neat replacements with parametrized rounded cut balls by [[lem-proper-compression-disks-admit-neat-witnesses-with-standard-cut-balls]]. For an abstract handlebody apply it after carrying the regular neighborhood of its finite graph to the standard ball-with-handles model embedded in $S^3$, and carry the replacements back. This preparation leaves every attaching circle fixed.

[F2] [[lem-finite-planar-hole-boundary-maps-extend-with-prescribed-collars]] gives actual diffeomorphisms between the finite planar cut surfaces, with all prescribed rim and collar maps.

[F3] [[lem-choice-free-relative-transversality-for-a-compact-smooth-embedding]] supplies arbitrarily small ambient perturbation isotopies of a finite compact circle system transverse to a finite opposite circle system, with support in a prescribed neighborhood. It assumes no stronger choice axiom. Neighborhoods of points disjoint from the moving circles may be excluded from that support.

## Proof

1.1 Prepare either complete disk system by [F1], without changing its boundary. Its one cut ball has a parametrized sphere boundary with two collared disk faces for each attaching circle. Send that punctured sphere by [F2] to a sphere with paired round holes, one pair inside each of finitely many disjoint round cluster disks. Prescribe the map on one rim of each pair arbitrarily with the required orientation and on the other by composing with its actual original pairing, so the map descends across the signed rim collars. Inside each cluster join its two holes by an embedded arc whose endpoints are paired; in the quotient this gives a smooth simple loop meeting just that attaching circle once. The cluster disks are disjoint, so the loops are pairwise disjoint. The signed collars make their intersections transverse. They can avoid $z$: arrange its image outside the finitely many arcs, or move an arc off that one point by a small local detour. Pull back to obtain dual loops for either system. In particular both attaching systems admit loops crossing their individual circles once and missing all other circles of the SAME system. No condition on intersections with the opposite system has yet been imposed. For genus zero there are no curves, and connectedness of the surface already makes every periodic domain constant; the conclusion is immediate. Assume positive genus from now on. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Number the original regions and regard real periodic domains with $n_z=0$ as a real subspace $V\subset\mathbb R^r$. It is a rational kernel: subdivide the finite curve graph at its vertices, take the integer matrix sending region coefficients to oriented edge coefficients, require its image to lie in the rational span of full curve vectors, and append $n_z=0$. The coefficients $c_i(P)$ of the alpha circles in $\partial P$ are unique, since full alpha and beta edge vectors have disjoint sets of open edges and each individual circle has an open edge; they are rational linear functions on $V$. The map $c:V\to\mathbb R^g$ is injective. Indeed if its alpha part is zero, $\partial P=\sum_i b_i\beta_i$. Follow the beta-dual loop for $\beta_i$ from step1.1, perturbed to avoid all finite alpha-beta vertices and to cross other curve edges transversely. Across an alpha edge the coefficient jump is zero, and across its sole beta crossing it is $\pm b_i$. The total jump around a closed loop is zero, so $b_i=0$. Thus all boundary jumps vanish. A path on the connected surface joining two region interiors can avoid the finite vertices and cross edges transversely by finitely many small chart detours. It propagates coefficient equality through all regions. Therefore $P$ is constant, and $n_z=0$ makes $P=0$. [step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 Choose the alpha-dual loops $\gamma_i$ from step1.1 transverse to beta by [F3], keeping them disjoint, avoiding $z$ and retaining their single alpha crossings by making the perturbation small. Choose for each two disjoint parallel copies $\gamma_i^+,\gamma_i^-$, and mutually disjoint annular neighborhoods of all $2g$ copies, avoiding $z$. They meet only $\alpha_i$, in one radial arc. Actual smooth coordinates $(r,\theta)\in(-a,a)\times\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ on each annulus can be chosen with that arc $\theta=0$, by transporting its intersection point around a thin curve collar. Choose $w_i^\pm=(0,\theta_i^\pm)$ with $0<\theta_i^\pm<1$ off beta; there are such points since the central circle is transverse to beta in finitely many points. These points lie off the original curves too. Their region evaluations $n_{w_i^\pm}:V\to\mathbb R$ are rational linear functions. Rational Gaussian elimination and injectivity in step2.1 express each as $\sum_j L_{ij}^\pm c_j(P)$: restrict $c$ to a rational basis of $V$, invert a nonzero maximal minor and extend the resulting left inverse to $\mathbb R^g$. Consequently one finite $C\ge0$ satisfies $|n_{w_i^\pm}(P)|\le C\max_j|c_j(P)|$ for all $P\in V$. If $V=0$ no winding is needed. Otherwise choose an integer $N>C+1$. All this is finite-dimensional linear algebra, with no selection of a basis of an infinite-dimensional space. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Fix a smooth bump $h(r)$ supported in $(-a,a)$, equal to one near $r=0$, and with $0\le h\le1$. On each plus annulus use the isotopy $H_\tau(r,\theta)=(r,\theta-N\tau h(r))$; on each minus annulus use $(r,\theta+N\tau h(r))$, $0\le\tau\le1$. It is the identity near the annulus boundary and is a diffeomorphism for every $\tau$, with inverse obtained by reversing its angular shift. In particular this is an isotopy of the original circles, not a Dehn twist changing their isotopy classes. The supports are disjoint and meet only their prescribed alpha circle, so the operations can be performed one at a time. Orient the radial arc by increasing $r$ first. Its oriented trace has domain coordinates $(\tau,r)$, boundary the final arc minus the initial arc. At a plus testpoint its preimages solve $-N\tau=\theta_i^+\pmod1$ and there are EXACTLY $N$ solutions in $0<\tau<1$. The Jacobian to $(r,\theta)$ has determinant $+Nh(0)>0$, so each contributes $+1$. On the minus annulus the same computation gives determinant $-Nh(0)<0$ and $N$ preimages. Reversing the arc orientation reverses both trace and the convention for its boundary coefficient. Orient each $\alpha_i$ and its annular coordinate consistently so this calculation gives, for its full trace $T_i$, $$n_{w_i^+}(T_i)=N,\qquad n_{w_i^-}(T_i)=-N.$$ For any point off the two arc banks its multiplicity is explicitly the finite signed count of solutions of $\mp N	au h(r)=	heta\pmod1$; this count is locally constant off those banks and its jumps are the stated boundary. Thus the trace chain is defined even if an intermediate circle is tangent to beta. The trace is zero near $z$. This is a computation of an oriented two-chain multiplicity, including the full integer number of turns. [step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Let $\alpha_i'$ be the final circles before the final transversality perturbation. Their trace satisfies $\partial T_i=\alpha_i'-\alpha_i$. For a periodic domain $P\in V$, set $P'=P+\sum_i c_i(P)T_i$. This is an actual integer region chain when $P$ is integral: the trace's integer local degrees are constant away from the old and new alpha arcs; their boundary jumps are exactly the specified trace boundary. The old alpha jumps cancel in the displayed sum. Thus $\partial P'=\sum_i c_i(P)\alpha_i'+\sum_i b_i(P)\beta_i$, and $n_z(P')=0$. Reversing the isotopy gives the inverse chain operation, so ALL normalized periodic domains of the new system occur this way, with the same full-circle coefficients. At the selected points the supports are disjoint, hence $$n_{w_i^\pm}(P')=n_{w_i^\pm}(P)\pm Nc_i(P).$$ If $P\ne0$, pick an index with $|c_i(P)|=\max_j|c_j(P)|>0$. Step3.1 bounds the two old coefficients by $C|c_i(P)|$, strictly less than $N|c_i(P)|$. Thus these two new coefficients have opposite signs. This works for EVERY real nonzero periodic domain and therefore for every integral one, with one single finite $N$. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 The winding may leave tangencies to beta. The final circles miss the finitely many selected testpoints: on the center of each annulus $h=1$ and $N$ is integral, so their intersection there is still at $\theta=0$. Choose small closed testpoint neighborhoods missing all final alpha circles and $z$. Apply [F3] successively to each alpha circle, with arbitrarily small supports excluding those neighborhoods and $z$ and disjoint from all other alpha circles. Compact separation retains disjointness and previous transverse intersections. These final small isotopy traces have zero multiplicity at every testpoint and at $z$, so the inverse-trace bijection of step5.1 preserves its exact sign witnesses. Opposite systems are now transverse, and their intersections are finite by compactness and local transversality. Each surface isotopy of an alpha circle extends over its handlebody by a thin boundary collar: if $K_\tau$ is its supported surface ambient isotopy, the collar map $(p,r)\mapsto(K_{\chi(r)}p,r)$ is a diffeomorphism, identity near its inner boundary, with slice-wise inverse and a triangular derivative. Transport its complete disk system by this map. Thus the manifold and splitting did not change, and the resulting alpha curves still bound a complete system. The basepoint and beta curves never moved. Genus zero and $V=0$ were already included. The claimed global admissibility follows, without a Spin-c, analytic count or move-invariance inference. [F3, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
